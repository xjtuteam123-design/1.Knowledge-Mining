# Paper Metadata
- **Title:** Influence of Ni content on the oxidation behavior of alumina-forming Co-based oxide dispersion strengthened superalloys at 900 °C
- **Authors:** Hao Yu, Sosuke Kondo, Ryuta Kasada, Shigeharu Ukai
- **Journal:** Corrosion Science
- **Year:** 2023 (Volume 212, Article 110933; available online 23 December 2022)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.corsci.2022.110933
- **Alloy system studied:** Co-20Cr-15Al-xNi (at%) oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) superalloys with Y₂Hf₂O₇ oxide particles
- **Research objective:** To systematically investigate the effect of Ni additions (0, 10, 20, 30 at%) on the oxidation behavior of alumina-forming Co-based ODS superalloys at 900 °C in air
- **Material category:** Co-based ODS superalloy (alumina-forming); not a Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloy

# Alloy System and Research Object
This paper studies Co-based oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) superalloys, which are not refractory high-temperature alloys in the Nb-W/Mo-W/refractory HEA sense. However, the paper contains relevant knowledge on ODS alloy design, oxide dispersion, oxidation resistance mechanisms, and element-function relationships that are transferable to refractory alloy contexts.

Four grades of Co-based ODS superalloys with nominal chemical composition **Co-20Cr-15Al-xNi-0.4Y₂O₃-0.6Hf (at%)**, where x = 0, 10, 20, 30 at%, were investigated. The alloys contain a Co solid solution phase (FCC) and a CoAl phase (B2), with fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ oxide particles distributed in both phases.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Processing route:**
1. **Mechanical alloying (MA)** – to incorporate oxide dispersoids
2. **Spark plasma sintering (SPS)** at 1100 °C with 50 MPa
3. **Hot rolling** – deformation amount not explicitly stated
4. **Final annealing** at 1100 °C – time and cooling method not explicitly stated

**Sample preparation for oxidation testing:**
- Cut into 15 mm × 8 mm × 1 mm rectangular specimens
- Wet ground with SiC papers up to 2400-grit
- Polished with diamond paste down to 1 µm
- Ultrasonically cleaned in alcohol and dried in hot air

**Oxidation testing:**
- Muffle furnace in air at 900 °C
- Exposure times: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 49, 64, 81, 100, 225 h
- Weight changes measured after cooling to room temperature using Sartorius MC 5 precision electronic balance (1 µg resolution)
- Samples subjected to multiple oxidation/cooling cycles for intermittent mass gain measurement

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Matrix phase:** Co-based ODS superalloy with Co solid solution phase (FCC) and CoAl phase (B2, body-centered cubic) [from Introduction, citing refs 8-10]

**Oxide dispersoids:**
- Fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ oxide particles densely distributed in both FCC and B2 phases
- Particles are stable up to 1200 °C
- In the 0Ni sample after oxidation, Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles remain fine and uniformly distributed in the inner α-alumina layer
- In the 30Ni sample, coarsened HfO₂ particles (irregular shape) form in addition to Y₂Hf₂O₇

**Matrix precipitates induced by Ni addition:**
- Large Hf-rich precipitates (~200 nm) form in the substrate of 30Ni sample
- These precipitates are rich in Hf but not oxygen, i.e., not oxides
- Attributed to reduced Hf solubility in Ni-containing Co-based alloys (Ni-Hf solubility approaches 0 at% below 600 °C, vs. ~0.7 at% Hf solubility in Co)

**Oxide scale structure (after oxidation at 900 °C):**
- **Outer layer:** θ-alumina (monoclinic symmetry)
- **Inner layer:** α-alumina (hexagonal close-packed, HCP structure)
- **Interface between outer θ-alumina and inner α-alumina:** Cr₂O₃ layer forms in 30Ni sample
- **Inclusions in inner α-alumina:** Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles in 0Ni; Y₂Hf₂O₇ + coarse HfO₂ in 30Ni

**α-alumina grain size and coarsening:**
| Sample | 64 h | 225 h | Coarsening rate |
|--------|------|-------|-----------------|
| 0Ni | 0.30 µm | 0.51 µm | 1.3 × 10⁻³ µm·h⁻¹ |
| 30Ni | 0.21 µm | 0.48 µm | 1.7 × 10⁻³ µm·h⁻¹ |

**Strengthening and microstructural mechanisms discussed:**
- Oxide dispersion strengthening (Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles)
- Grain boundary pinning by oxide particles (restrains coarsening of alumina grains)
- The paper does not explicitly discuss solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, or grain-boundary strengthening of the alloy matrix itself

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

**Oxidation kinetics at 900 °C in air:**

| Sample | Parabolic rate constant, kₚ (mg²·cm⁻⁴·h⁻¹) |
|--------|---------------------------------------------|
| 0Ni | 8.70 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 10Ni | 8.19 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 20Ni | 3.42 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 30Ni | 2.40 × 10⁻⁴ |

- All samples followed parabolic oxidation kinetics for the first 100 h
- Weight gain after 225 h deviated from parabolic law, especially for Ni-containing samples
- Ni addition reduced oxidation rate (lower mass gain) with increasing Ni content; 30Ni exhibited the least mass gain
- No scale spallation was observed

**Oxide layer structure:**
- Duplex alumina scale: outer θ-alumina + inner α-alumina on all samples
- Oxide scale thickness decreases with increasing Ni content

**Oxide scale thickness (0Ni vs 30Ni):**
- At 64 h: 0Ni has thick outer θ-alumina and thin inner α-alumina; 30Ni has thin outer θ-alumina and relatively thick inner α-alumina
- At 225 h: dramatic increase in inner α-alumina thickness in 0Ni

**Elemental distribution in oxide scale (EPMA/TEM-EDS):**
- Alumina scale enriched in O and Al
- Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles incorporated in inner α-alumina layer (pre-existing in matrix)
- Cr enrichment at θ-/α-alumina interface in 30Ni (Cr₂O₃)
- HfO₂ formation in 30Ni inner α-alumina layer
- Hf depletion in substrate beneath inner α-alumina in 30Ni

**Effect of Ni on oxidation mechanisms:**
1. Ni promotes Cr diffusion → rapid initial Cr₂O₃ formation → nuclei for α-alumina → faster formation of continuous inner α-alumina → suppresses outward cation diffusion → inhibits θ-alumina growth
2. Cr₂O₃ layer at θ-/α-alumina interface also inhibits oxygen inward diffusion → slows α-alumina growth in late stage
3. Ni decreases Hf solubility in Co-based matrix → Hf precipitation in matrix → fewer fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles → larger α-alumina grain coarsening rate (1.7 × 10⁻³ vs 1.3 × 10⁻³ µm·h⁻¹)

**Oxygen diffusion considerations:**
- Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles in α-alumina may act as fast transport paths for oxygen
- YSZ oxygen diffusion coefficient: 7.0 × 10⁻¹³ m²·s⁻¹ (at relevant temperature)
- Alumina oxygen diffusion coefficient: 5.5 × 10⁻³¹ m²·s⁻¹ at 900 °C in air
- Fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles in 0Ni provide short inter-particle distance and high Y₂Hf₂O₇/alumina interface density → accelerated oxygen inward diffusion → faster α-alumina growth

## [Ni]

**Applicable Alloy System:** Co-20Cr-15Al-xNi ODS superalloys (x = 0-30 at%); Co-based ODS alloys; also relevant to Ni-containing Co-based alloys in general

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 0, 10, 20, 30 at% Ni

**Main Function:**
- FCC stabilizer (reduces brittle B2 CoAl phase formation)
- Improves ductility of alumina-forming Co-based ODS alloys
- Improves oxidation resistance by suppressing alumina scale growth
- Promotes Cr diffusion and Cr₂O₃ formation

**Microstructural Mechanism:**
- Increases Cr diffusion coefficient in Co-based alloys → rapid initial Cr₂O₃ formation at θ-/α-alumina interface → serves as nucleation sites for α-alumina
- Reduces Hf solubility in the Co-based matrix → Hf precipitation → fewer fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles
- Suppresses θ-alumina growth by promoting rapid inner α-alumina formation (blocks outward cation diffusion)

**Property Effect:**
- Reduces parabolic oxidation rate constant from 8.70 × 10⁻⁴ (0Ni) to 2.40 × 10⁻⁴ mg²·cm⁻⁴·h⁻¹ (30Ni) at 900 °C in air
- Suppresses growth of outer θ-alumina (thinner layer at 64 h and 225 h)
- Facilitates formation of continuous inner α-alumina
- α-alumina grain size at 64 h: 0.30 µm (0Ni) vs 0.21 µm (30Ni)
- Mechanical property improvement (ductility) referenced from literature but not measured in this study

**Potential Side Effects:**
- Decreases Hf solubility → Hf-rich precipitates in matrix → reduces number density of fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ dispersoids
- Coarsening of HfO₂ particles in the inner α-alumina layer
- Higher α-alumina grain coarsening rate (1.7 × 10⁻³ µm·h⁻¹ for 30Ni vs 1.3 × 10⁻³ µm·h⁻¹ for 0Ni)
- Deviation from parabolic oxidation law after 225 h

## [Hf]

**Applicable Alloy System:** Co-Cr-Al-Ni ODS superalloys; Y₂O₃-Hf-containing ODS alloys

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 0.6 at% Hf

**Main Function:**
- Forms Y₂Hf₂O₇ oxide dispersoids with Y₂O₃
- Reactive element effect in oxidation
- Contributes to oxide dispersion strengthening

**Microstructural Mechanism:**
- Combines with Y₂O₃ to form fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles, stable up to 1200 °C
- Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles act as reinforcing precipitates and pin alumina grain boundaries
- HfO₂ can form in the oxide scale via in situ oxidation of matrix-dissolved Hf by inward-diffusing oxygen

**Property Effect:**
- Contributes to excellent high-temperature strength (from prior work, not measured here)
- Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles in α-alumina provide grain boundary pinning, reducing α-alumina grain coarsening rate to 1.3 × 10⁻³ µm·h⁻¹ in 0Ni
- May accelerate oxygen inward diffusion through fast transport paths

**Potential Side Effects:**
- In 30Ni, reduced Hf solubility leads to coarse HfO₂ particles with irregular shape in the α-alumina layer
- Hf depletion in the substrate beneath the oxide layer in 30Ni
- Coarse Hf-rich precipitates in the 30Ni substrate may weaken the refining effect of Hf on dispersed oxide particles

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Oxide Dispersion Strengthening (ODS)

**Related Elements:** Y₂O₃, Hf, Y₂Hf₂O₇, YSZ, La₂O₃

**Typical Alloys:** Co-20Cr-15Al-xNi-0.4Y₂O₃-0.6Hf ODS superalloys; Co-based ODS alloys; FeCrAl ODS alloys

**Key Compositions:** 0.4 at% Y₂O₃ + 0.6 at% Hf forming Y₂Hf₂O₇

**Microstructural Changes:**
- Fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ oxide particles densely distributed in FCC and B2 matrix phases
- Particles stable up to 1200 °C
- During oxidation, particles incorporated into inner α-alumina layer
- Particle size and distribution affected by Ni content (Ni reduces Hf solubility → coarser particles)

**Property Improvements:**
- Excellent high-temperature strength (from prior work)
- Pinning effect restrains coarsening of α-alumina grains during oxidation
- 0Ni with fine Y₂Hf₂O₇: α-alumina grain coarsening rate 1.3 × 10⁻³ µm·h⁻¹

**Risks and Limitations:**
- Y₂Hf₂O₇/alumina interface may accelerate oxygen inward diffusion
- Higher density of fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ in α-alumina → faster α-alumina growth
- Ni addition reduces Y₂Hf₂O₇ refining effect due to Hf precipitation

**Original Evidence:** Introduction, Discussion Section 4.2, Conclusions (3)

**Related Elements:** Ni, Cr, Al, Hf, Y

**Typical Alloys:** Co-20Cr-15Al-xNi ODS (x = 0-30 at%)

**Key Compositions:** 15 at% Al (alumina formation), 20 at% Cr (Cr₂O₃ nucleation), 10-30 at% Ni (Cr diffusion enhancement)

# Key Conclusions

1. **Alumina scale formation:** All Co-20Cr-15Al-xNi-0.4Y₂O₃-0.6Hf (x = 0, 10, 20, 30 at%) ODS superalloys formed a duplex alumina scale at 900 °C in air, consisting of outer θ-alumina and inner α-alumina. No scale spallation occurred.

2. **Ni addition improves oxidation resistance:** Ni additions of 10-30 at% reduced the oxidation rate by suppressing alumina scale growth. The parabolic rate constant decreased from 8.70 × 10⁻⁴ (0Ni) to 2.40 × 10⁻⁴ mg²·cm⁻⁴·h⁻¹ (30Ni).

3. **Mechanism of Ni effect on θ-alumina:** Ni promotes Cr diffusion → rapid initial Cr₂O₃ formation → enhanced α-alumina nucleation → rapid formation of continuous inner α-alumina → suppresses outward cation diffusion → inhibits θ-alumina growth.

4. **Mechanism of Ni effect on α-alumina:** Cr₂O₃ layer at θ-/α-alumina interface also inhibits oxygen inward diffusion → slows α-alumina growth in late-stage oxidation.

5. **Y₂Hf₂O₇ dual role:** Fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles provide grain boundary pinning (reducing α-alumina grain coarsening) but may also provide fast oxygen transport paths (accelerating α-alumina growth). Ni addition reduces the number density of fine Y₂Hf₂O₇ due to Hf precipitation, shifting this balance.

6. **Oxidation kinetics deviation:** Weight gain after 225 h deviated from the parabolic law, especially for Ni-containing samples, attributed to α-alumina grain growth.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations explicitly noted in the paper:**
- The kₚ values only describe the oxidation behavior during the first 100 h; oxidation kinetics did not exactly follow parabolic law after longer exposure
- The oxygen diffusion coefficient of Y₂Hf₂O₇ particles is not yet available; effect of Y₂Hf₂O₇ distribution on α-Al₂O₃ growth requires future investigation
- Detailed solubility of Hf in Co-based ODS superalloys is not known; inference based on binary phase diagrams (Co-Hf, Ni-Hf)
- TEM observation of the most initial stage of inner α-alumina formation was not performed
- Whether Y₂Hf₂O₇ XRD peaks come from oxidation product or matrix cannot be determined from XRD alone (clarified via time-series XRD)
- Initial α-alumina formation mechanism in 30Ni is assumed ("it is reasonable to assume...")

**Applicable scope:**
- Results are specific to Co-based ODS superalloys at 900 °C in air
- Not directly applicable to Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloys without validation
- Thermodynamic calculations restricted to binary Co-Hf and Ni-Hf phase diagrams (SSOL5 database)
- Mechanical property characterization (tensile, creep, hardness) was not performed in this study
