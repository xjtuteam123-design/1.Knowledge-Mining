# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title**: Phase stability, mechanical properties, and ion irradiation effects in face-centered cubic CrFeMnNi compositionally complex solid-solution alloys at high temperatures
- **Authors**: Calvin Parkin, Michael Moorehead, Mohamed Elbakhshwan, Xuan Zhang, Pengyuan Xiu, Lingfeng He, Mukesh Bachhav, Kumar Sridharan, Adrien Couet
- **Journal**: Journal of Nuclear Materials
- **Year**: 2022
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnucmat.2022.153733
- **Volume/Article**: 565 (2022) 153733
- **Alloy system studied**: Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni face-centered cubic (FCC) compositionally complex solid-solution alloys (CSA), also described as FCC high-entropy alloys in the CrFeMnNi system
- **Research objective**: Evaluate phase stability, mechanical properties, and radiation damage effects from heavy ions in two FCC CrFeMnNi compositionally complex alloys for potential nuclear reactor applications
- **Material category**: Compositionally complex solid-solution alloys / high-entropy alloys (FCC, CrFeMnNi system). Note: This is not a refractory alloy system; it is a 3d transition metal-based FCC CSA.

# Alloy System and Research Object

This paper focuses on two face-centered cubic (FCC) compositionally complex solid-solution alloys (CSAs) in the Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni system:
- **Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈** (at%)
- **Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅** (at%)

A reference alloy, Model Alloy 709 (M709), with nominal composition Fe-bal, Cr₂₁Ni₂₄ (at%), was also studied for comparison. The alloys were selected based on CALPHAD thermodynamic calculations to explore the CrFeMnNi composition space, with the goal of identifying CSAs that maintain a single-phase FCC structure at reactor-relevant temperatures.

**Vacuum induction melting**: Used for large size samples to produce sheets for wire electrical discharge machining of miniature tensile specimens.

**Homogenization**: Annealed at 1200 °C for 2 days (48 h), followed by air-cooling in inert environment.

**Aging experiments**: Long-term thermal stability tested at 700 °C for 1, 5, and 40 days. Samples encapsulated in quartz with 13.3 Pa Ar, annealed in open-air cylindrical furnace.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## As-fabricated (post-homogenization)

- Both Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ and Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ exhibited single-phase polycrystalline FCC crystal structure.
- Grain size: on the order of hundreds of micrometers.
- EDS confirmed full chemical homogeneity.
- XRD confirmed no second phases present.
- Few dislocations, no precipitates in pre-irradiation condition.

## After aging at 700 °C

**Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈**:
- CALPHAD predicted phase separation below 780 °C.
- At 700 °C, predicted to have FCC + Cr₇₂Fe₁₀Mn₁₈ BCC (~4% volume fraction).
- Aging confirmed precipitation of a Cr-rich, Mn-containing, Fe/Ni-deficient BCC phase distributed evenly throughout the bulk.
- XRD confirmed BCC phase formation, agreeing with CALPHAD predictions.
- Experimental lattice parameters in good agreement with CALPHAD predictions (max overprediction of 0.12 Å for FCC phase).

**Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅**:
- CALPHAD predicted single-phase structure down to 600 °C.
- Post-aging EDS showed a relatively low volume fraction of Cr-rich clusters, precipitating most favorably at grain boundaries.
- XRD indicated a small volume fraction of a secondary phase.
- This represents a discrepancy with CALPHAD database predictions.

## Irradiated microstructure (500 °C, 75 dpa, Ni²⁺ ions)

**Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈**:
- Dislocation loops (undetermined type): average size 25 ± 8 nm, density 1.32 × 10²¹ m⁻³
- Faulted dislocation loops: average size 9 ± 2 nm, density 4.16 × 10²¹ m⁻³
- Voids: average size ~100 ± 32 nm, number density 1.2 × 10²⁰ m⁻³, swelling level 8.6% in plateau region
- Mn enrichment just before displacement peak; Fe and Ni showed opposite behavior; Cr relatively unaffected.
- Ni enrichment near voids (~10 at%).

**Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅**:
- Dislocation loops (undetermined type): average size 20 ± 12 nm, density 2.43 × 10²¹ m⁻³
- Faulted dislocation loops (small): average size 6.7 ± 1.2 nm, density 2.20 × 10²² m⁻³
- Faulted dislocation loops (large, sparse): average size 68 ± 34 nm, density 4.73 × 10¹⁹ m⁻³
- Voids: density 5.74 × 10²⁰ m⁻³, average size ~21 ± 8 nm, swelling level 0.40%, localized at depth ~1.5 μm (corresponding to ~125 dpa beyond damage peak)
- Some voids faceted, some round; transition at ~20 nm.
- Ni enrichment near voids; Mn enriched before displacement peak (less than 5% change).

**M709 (reference)**:
- No voids observed.
- Dislocation loops: density 6.49 × 10²¹ m⁻³, average size 13 ± 7 nm.
- Faulted dislocation loops: average size 15 ± 5 nm, density 7.99 × 10²¹ m⁻³.

## APT results after irradiation

Both CSAs showed homogeneous distribution of primary elements after 75 dpa at 500 °C, indicating no significant chemical separation or phase separation induced by irradiation in the bulk.

**Elemental segregation**: Ni enrichment near voids in both CSAs, similar to conventional austenitic FeNiCr alloys.

# Mechanical Properties

Tensile testing performed at room temperature and 500 °C in air, following ASTM E21. Strain rate: nominal 0.001/s. Sample geometry: dog-bone shaped, total length 25.4 mm, gage length 7.6 mm, gage width 1.52 mm, uniform thickness 0.77 mm.

| Property | Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ (RT) | Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ (500°C) | Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ (RT) | Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ (500°C) |
|----------|--------------------------|---------------------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| Yield strength | 155 MPa | 93 MPa | 151 MPa | 100 MPa |
| Elongation | ~70% (excellent ductility) | Lower than RT | Slightly less ductile than Cr₁₈ alloy | Gains ductility compared to RT |

**Fracture behavior**: Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ at RT showed necking and slip lines, typical of ductile fracture.

**Comparison to Kumar et al. [11]**: The alloys in this study showed higher ductility and lower strength compared to Kumar et al., who used cold-rolled samples with 86% thickness reduction. The cold work increased strength and reduced ductility in the reference study.

**Elastic modulus**: Not reported because mechanical properties of the entire loading train were not known at both temperatures.

**Note on thermal stability during tensile testing**: The precipitation reported in aging experiments (Section 3.1) is not expected to affect fracture behavior because tensile tests were performed within a few tens of minutes after temperature stabilization.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

The original paper does not focus on oxidation resistance.

**Thermal stability**: Both alloys experienced phase separation at 700 °C, which limits their applications in advanced reactor concepts. XRD data showed low intensity peaks from oxide phases induced by aging, present below 2θ = 5° and between 2θ = 6° and 11°.

**Irradiation stability**:
- Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈: experienced 8.6% void swelling after 75 dpa at 500 °C in the plateau region.
- Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅: experienced 0.40% void swelling near a depth of ~1.5 μm (beyond the damage peak, ~125 dpa), with no voids observed in the plateau region.
- M709 (reference): no void swelling observed at 75 dpa.
- No significant radiation-induced segregation or precipitation observed in the bulk (via APT).

**Mn depletion**: Mn somewhat depleted beyond the displacement peak in both alloys, indicating vacancy diffusion deeper into the material.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Card 1: Chromium (Cr)

**[Element]**: Chromium (Cr)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni FCC compositionally complex solid-solution alloys (this paper)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 15–18 at% (target compositions: Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ and Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅)

**[Main Function]**: Primary alloying element in FCC CSA; contributes to phase stability considerations; forms Cr-rich BCC precipitate phase at elevated temperature.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: At 700 °C, Cr-rich BCC phase precipitates in both alloys. In Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈, predicted BCC phase composition is Cr₇₂Fe₁₀Mn₁₈ (~4% volume fraction). The precipitate is Cr-rich, Mn-containing, and Fe/Ni-deficient. In Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅, Cr-rich clusters precipitate preferentially at grain boundaries with lower volume fraction.

**[Property Effect]**: The precipitation of Cr-rich BCC phase limits long-term applications of these CSAs at 700 °C. After irradiation, Cr remained relatively unaffected by the Mn/Fe/Ni redistribution near the displacement peak.

**[Potential Side Effects]**: Higher Cr content (Cr₁₈ vs Cr₁₅) correlates with stronger driving force for BCC phase precipitation at 700 °C, reducing thermal stability of the FCC solid solution.

**[Original Evidence]**: "Aging experiments at this temperature confirmed varying degrees of precipitation of a body-centered cubic phase in both Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ and Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅" (Abstract); "EDS shows this phase is distributed throughout the bulk of the aged Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈, but primarily only near the grain boundaries in aged Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅" (Section 3.1).

## Card 2: Iron (Fe)

**[Element]**: Iron (Fe)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni FCC compositionally complex solid-solution alloys (this paper)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 27–35 at% (target compositions: Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ and Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅)

**[Main Function]**: Major alloying element; balances composition with Ni to achieve single-phase FCC structure.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Fe is deficient in the Cr-rich BCC precipitate. During irradiation, Fe shows opposite behavior to Mn in depth profile (Mn enriched before displacement peak, Fe and Ni depleted; and vice versa on sides).

**[Property Effect]**: Higher Fe content in Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ (35 at% vs 27 at%) correlates with lower void swelling (0.40% vs 8.6%), though this is likely a combination of Fe, Mn, and Ni effects. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Fe's isolated effect on mechanical properties.

**[Potential Side Effects]**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**[Original Evidence]**: "Mn is enriched just before the displacement peak and slightly depleted on either side, with Fe and Ni showing the opposite behavior and Cr left relatively unaffected" (Section 3.3.1, Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ results).

## Card 3: Manganese (Mn)

**[Element]**: Manganese (Mn)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni FCC compositionally complex solid-solution alloys (this paper)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 15–27 at% (target compositions: Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ and Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅)

**[Main Function]**: Major alloying element; significantly affects vacancy mobility, stacking fault energy (SFE), and irradiation behavior.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Mn increases vacancy mobility and stacking fault energy for Mn compositions beyond ~15 wt%. Vacancies preferentially diffuse via Mn atoms. In Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ (27 at% Mn), Mn is enriched just before the displacement peak and depleted on either side, likely due to vacancies migrating down the concentration gradient exchanging primarily with Mn atoms. Lower Mn content in Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ (15 at% Mn) results in less vacancy diffusion down the concentration gradient.

**[Property Effect]**: Higher Mn content (Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈) showed 8.6% void swelling at 75 dpa/500 °C, while lower Mn content (Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅) showed only 0.40% swelling (though at different depths). Mn also affects the nucleation rate of interstitial loops, with Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ exhibiting higher density of smaller loops. Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ also lost ductility at 500 °C while Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ gained ductility, though the paper attributes this more to precipitation differences.

**[Potential Side Effects]**: Higher Mn may increase void swelling susceptibility by enhancing vacancy mobility and enabling deeper vacancy migration. It also can cause elemental redistribution during irradiation.

**[Original Evidence]**: "Mn increases the vacancy mobility and the stacking-fault energy (SFE) for Mn composition beyond ∼15 wt%"; "Since vacancies preferentially diffuse via Mn... there will be less diffusion down the vacancy concentration gradient for the lower Mn-content Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ relative to the Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ alloy" (Section 4.2).

## Card 4: Nickel (Ni)

**[Element]**: Nickel (Ni)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni FCC compositionally complex solid-solution alloys (this paper)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 27–35 at% (target compositions: Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ and Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅)

**[Main Function]**: Major alloying element; stabilizes FCC structure; contributes to solid solution.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Ni is depleted from the Cr-rich BCC precipitate phase. During irradiation, Ni shows enrichment near voids (approximately 10 at% near voids). Ni is also shown to be depleted where Mn is enriched before the displacement peak.

**[Property Effect]**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Ni's isolated effect on mechanical properties. Higher Ni content in Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ (35 at% vs 28 at%) correlates with lower void swelling.

**[Potential Side Effects]**: Ni enrichment near voids may be associated with radiation-induced segregation effects, but no bulk segregation was observed via APT.

**[Original Evidence]**: "EDS characterization of the voids showed some enrichment of Ni by about 10 at% near voids" (Conclusions); "Ni enrichment near voids in both CSAs, which has been observed previously in more conventional austenitic FeNiCr alloys" (Section 4.2).

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Card 1: Solid-Solution Strengthening

**[Mechanism]**: Solid-solution strengthening via compositional complexity

**[Related Elements]**: Cr, Fe, Mn, Ni (multi-principal element solid solution)

**[Typical Alloys]**: Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈, Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅

**[Key Compositions]**: 4 principal elements at 15–35 at% each

**[Microstructural Changes]**: Single-phase FCC solid solution with homogeneous distribution of elements on lattice sites.

**[Property Improvements]**: Yield strengths of 155 MPa (Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈) and 151 MPa (Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅) at room temperature, with excellent ductility (~70% elongation for Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈). The cold work likely increased the alloy strength and reduced ductility dramatically compared to this study" (Section 4.1).

## Card 3: Precipitation Strengthening (Unintentional at 700 °C)

**[Mechanism]**: BCC phase precipitation at 700 °C

**[Related Elements]**: Cr-rich, Mn-containing, Fe/Ni-deficient BCC phase

**[Typical Alloys]**: Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈, Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅

# Key Conclusions

1. **Phase stability**: Both Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ and Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ experienced phase separation at 700 °C, with varying degrees of BCC precipitation. Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ exhibited bulk precipitation while Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ showed grain boundary precipitation only. This limits high-temperature applications pending further optimization.

2. **CALPHAD validation**: CALPHAD predictions were confirmed for Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ (predicting BCC precipitation at 700 °C) but not for Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ (which was predicted to remain single-phase but still experienced minor precipitation).

3. **Mechanical properties**: Room-temperature yield strengths of 155 MPa and 151 MPa for Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ and Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ respectively, with excellent ductility (~70% for Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈). At 500 °C: 93 MPa and 100 MPa respectively. Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ gained ductility at 500 °C, while Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ did not.

4. **Irradiation response at 500 °C, 75 dpa (Ni²⁺ ions)**:
   - Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈: 8.6% void swelling in plateau region; voids ~100 nm.
   - Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅: 0.40% void swelling near ~1.5 μm depth (beyond damage peak, ~125 dpa); no voids in plateau region; voids ~21 nm.
   - M709 reference: no void swelling observed.
   - Both CSAs had similar dislocation loop sizes (20–25 nm) and densities (1.32–2.43 × 10²¹ m⁻³) compared to M709 (13 nm, 6.49 × 10²¹ m⁻³).
   - Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ had smaller faulted loops (6.7 ± 1.2 nm) at higher density (2.20 × 10²² m⁻³) than Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ (9 ± 2 nm, 4.16 × 10²¹ m⁻³).

5. **Chemical distribution under irradiation**: APT showed no significant bulk segregation or precipitation after 75 dpa at 500 °C. Ni enrichment near voids (~10 at%) consistent with conventional austenitic alloys. Mn redistribution (enrichment before displacement peak) observed, attributed to vacancy-Mn exchange.

6. **Mechanism for swelling differences**: The paper proposes that the higher Mn content in Cr₁₈Fe₂₇Mn₂₇Ni₂₈ enhances vacancy mobility, enabling deeper vacancy migration and earlier void nucleation. In Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅, the higher density of small sessile faulted loops increases sink strength, delaying void nucleation to higher dpa.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

## Limitations

1. **Alloy system**: This is a Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni FCC compositionally complex alloy system, not a refractory alloy system. It is not applicable to Nb-W, Mo-W, or refractory high-entropy alloy contexts.

2. **Phase stability limitation**: Both alloys underwent phase separation at 700 °C, limiting their high-temperature applicability in the current compositional form.

3. **CALPHAD database limitation**: The PanHEA2021 database predicted Cr₁₅Fe₃₅Mn₁₅Ni₃₅ to be single-phase at 700 °C, but experiments revealed minor precipitation, indicating a need for database improvement.

4. **Mechanical testing limitation**: Elastic modulus was not reported due to unknown loading train compliance.

5. **Irradiation temperature/dose scope**: Only investigated at 500 °C and 75 dpa (average plateau dose). Void swelling behavior may differ at other temperatures (e.g., 575 °C has been shown to promote more swelling) or doses.

6. **Irradiation particle**: Used Ni²⁺ heavy ions (3.7 MeV), not neutrons or other ion species.

7. **Thermal stability vs. irradiation duration**: The irradiation lasted only a few hours, so thermal precipitation effects (observed at 700 °C for 1 day or more) did not occur during irradiation at 500 °C.

## Applicable Scope

1. This paper is relevant to **3d transition metal FCC compositionally complex alloys** for potential nuclear reactor applications (fuel cladding, ducts).
2. The findings on **Mn content affecting vacancy mobility and void swelling** may inform alloy design in the Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni system.
3. The paper demonstrates the **importance of CALPHAD validation** with experimental aging studies.
4. The results are applicable to **sodium fast reactor** relevant temperatures (500–700 °C) and radiation damage levels (75 dpa).
5. The observation that **compositional complexity alone does not guarantee superior irradiation resistance** (M709 reference outperformed the CSAs in void swelling resistance) is an important finding.
6. The paper is **not directly applicable** to refractory alloy systems (Nb, W, Mo, Ta-based), ODS alloys, or non-FCC high-entropy alloys.
