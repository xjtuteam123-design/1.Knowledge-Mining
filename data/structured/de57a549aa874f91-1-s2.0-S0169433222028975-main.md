# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy family:** Iridium-based dual-phase γ/γ’ refractory superalloys
- **γ phase:** FCC Ir solid solution matrix

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

This is a theoretical structural study, not an experimental microstructure characterization.

- **Matrix phase (γ):** FCC Ir solid solution
- **Precipitate phase (γ’):** L1₂-ordered intermetallic Ir₃X and ternary Ir₃XₘVₙ
- **Structural stability conclusions from DFT:**
  - All calculated phases have no imaginary phonon frequencies → dynamically stable
  - Large negative cohesive energy (E_c) and formation energy (ΔH) values indicate exothermic formation and good chemical stability
- **Coherency (theoretical):**
  - Binary Ir₃Hf and Ir₃Zr have critical coherent diameter d_crit ≈ 20 nm → predicted to lose coherency at small precipitate sizes; experimental misfit dislocations observed previously in Ir/Ir₃Zr and Ir/Ir₃Hf confirm semicoherent nature

  - Ternary Ir₂₄V₅Nb₃: d_crit ≈ 85 nm at room temperature
  - Ternary Ir₂₄V₅Ta₃: d_crit ≈ 145 nm at room temperature

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [V (Vanadium) in Ir₃X ternary γ’ phases]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ir-based dual-phase γ/γ’ superalloys; specifically, ternary Ir₃XₘVₙ L1₂ phases where X = Ti, Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Ternary V additions were modeled in 32-atom supercells as Ir₂₄Ti₇V (1 V), Ir₂₄V₅Ta₃ (5 V), Ir₂₄V₅Nb₃ (5 V), Ir₂₄Hf₂V₆ (6 V), Ir₂₄Zr₂V₆ (6 V).
- **Main Function:** Reduce lattice misfit with the Ir matrix; improve interfacial coherency; increase critical coherent diameter; increase Debye temperature and bonding strength.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Ir₃V has a negative lattice misfit with Ir (room-temperature measured value −0.7%), while the other Ir₃X compounds have positive misfits. Mixing negative-misfit Ir₃V with positive-misfit Ir₃X results in cancellation of lattice distortion. Since the atomic radius of V (135 pm) is equal to Ir (135 pm), V substitution allows the γ’ phase lattice to shrink or cease expanding, offsetting the expansive effect of larger X atoms (Ti, Zr, Nb, Hf, Ta). The reduced misfit enlarges the critical coherent diameter for semicoherent transition.
- **Property Effect:**
  - Lattice misfit reduced from −0.6% to +2.4% (binary) to −0.8% to 0% (ternary) in 0–2000 K range
  - Critical coherent diameter d_crit of Ir₂₄Ti₇V, Ir₂₄Zr₂V₆, Ir₂₄Hf₂V₆: 400–7000 nm up to 2000 K
  - Debye temperature increased: e.g., Ir₃Ta 422.7 K → Ir₂₄V₅Ta₃ 453.3 K
  - Bulk modulus retained at 2000 K: 200–240 GPa for ternary alloys vs >160 GPa for binary alloys
- **Potential Side Effects:** The paper does not report experimental side effects. Theoretically, V-containing Ir₃V has negative β’ (concentration expansion coefficient) for certain systems, but the paper does not discuss degradation of ductility or workability.
- **Original Evidence:** Abstract; Section 3.2 (Lattice misfit and coherency); Table 2; Fig. 4(b); Fig. 5(b); Section 4 (Conclusion).

## [Ti (Titanium) in Ir-based γ/γ’ alloys]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ir/Ir₃Ti and Ir/Ir₃Ti–V ternary systems
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Ir₃Ti binary; Ir₂₄Ti₇V (Ti fraction of X+V = 7/8)
- **Main Function:** Form L1₂ γ’ phase with minimum lattice misfit among the five positive-misfit Ir₃X binaries; maintain high coherency with Ir matrix.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Ti atomic radius (140 pm) is closest to Ir (135 pm), resulting in minimal lattice constant expansion. Among binary Ir₃X, Ir₃Ti has the largest critical coherent diameter at elevated temperature (~350 nm). Mixing with V produces Ir₂₄Ti₇V with near-zero lattice misfit.
- **Property Effect:**
  - Room-temperature lattice misfit: +0.1% (experimental)
  - Ir₃Ti critical coherent diameter ~350 nm at elevated temperature
  - Ir₂₄Ti₇V predicted fully coherent with critical coherent diameter >400 nm up to 2000 K
  - Ir₂₄Ti₇V has the lowest volumetric thermal expansion coefficient among ternary alloys at high temperature
  - Cohesive energy 9.537 eV/atom, Debye temperature 422.4 K (binary Ir₃Ti)
- **Potential Side Effects:** Not stated in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.2; Fig. 4(a); Fig. 5(a); Table 1; Table 2; Fig. S2(a).

## [Hf (Hafnium) in Ir-based γ/γ’ alloys]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ir/Ir₃Hf and Ir/Ir₃Hf–V ternary systems
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Ir₃Hf binary; Ir₂₄Hf₂V₆ (Hf fraction of X+V = 1/4)
- **Main Function:** Form L1₂ γ’ phase with strong chemical bonding, but large lattice misfit problem requiring V co-alloying.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Hf atomic radius 155 pm, much larger than Ir (135 pm) → large positive lattice expansion. Binary Ir₃Hf has d_crit ≈ 20 nm → semicoherent. Adding V (Ir₂₄Hf₂V₆) yields near-zero misfit and excellent coherency.
- **Property Effect:**
  - Room-temperature lattice misfit: +2.4% (experimental)
  - Ir₃Hf d_crit ≈ 20 nm, weakly temperature dependent
  - Ir₂₄Hf₂V₆ d_crit 400–7000 nm up to 2000 K
  - Cohesive energy 9.579 eV/atom, Debye temperature 369.4 K (binary)
  - Bulk modulus >160 GPa at 2000 K (binary); ~200–240 GPa ternary
- **Potential Side Effects:** Large lattice misfit of binary Ir₃Hf adversely impacts coherency and may promote coarsening of γ’ precipitates.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.2; Table 1; Fig. 4(a); Fig. 5(a).

## [Zr (Zirconium) in Ir-based γ/γ’ alloys]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ir/Ir₃Zr and Ir/Ir₃Zr–V ternary systems
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Ir₃Zr binary; Ir₂₄Zr₂V₆ (Zr fraction of X+V = 1/4)
- **Main Function:** Form L1₂ γ’ phase; high-temperature stability demonstrated experimentally in Ir-Nb-Zr system up to 2373 K.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Zr atomic radius is 155 pm (same as Hf), causing a large positive lattice expansion in binary Ir₃Zr (measured misfit +2.7%). The solubility lines of Ir/Ir₃Zr in the phase diagram narrow near 1600 K, leading to a sharp decrease in lattice misfit at that temperature.
- **Property Effect:**
  - Room-temperature lattice misfit: +2.7% (experimental)
  - Ir₃Zr d_crit ≈ 20 nm → semicoherent; misfit dislocations observed experimentally
  - Ir₂₄Zr₂V₆ d_crit 400–7000 nm up to 2000 K (fully coherent)
  - Cohesive energy 9.715 eV/atom; Debye temperature 398.0 K (binary); 459.1 K (Ir₂₄Zr₂V₆)
  - Largest volumetric thermal expansion among ternary alloys at high temperature
- **Potential Side Effects:** Binary Ir₃Zr has the largest positive lattice misfit (+2.7%) among Ir₃X compounds, leading to rapid loss of coherency.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.2; Table 1; Fig. 4(a); Fig. 5(a); Fig. S3.

## [Nb (Niobium) in Ir-based γ/γ’ alloys]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ir/Ir₃Nb and Ir/Ir₃Nb–V ternary systems; also relevant to Ir-Nb-Zr ternary experimental system
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Ir₃Nb binary; Ir₂₄V₅Nb₃ (Nb fraction of X+V = 3/8)
- **Main Function:** Form L1₂ γ’ phase; contributes to solid-solution and precipitation strengthening.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Nb atomic radius is 145 pm vs Ir 135 pm → positive misfit of +1.4% experimentally. Nb and Zr are fully miscible in L1₂ phase (Yamabe et al. Ir-Nb-Zr ternary study). β’ value for Ir₃Nb: 0.040 (present) / 0.040 (previous literature).
- **Property Effect:**
  - Room-temperature lattice misfit: +1.4% (experimental); DFT value ~+0.75% (Yu et al. cited)
  - d_crit of Ir₃Nb increases above 1600 K
  - Ternary Ir₂₄V₅Nb₃: d_crit ≈ 85 nm at room temperature
  - Cohesive energy 10.311 eV/atom (highest among Ir₃X)
  - Debye temperature 449.7 K (calculated) / 458 K (experimental)
  - Bulk modulus >160 GPa at 2000 K
- **Potential Side Effects:** Relatively large positive lattice misfit in binary Ir₃Nb may reduce coherency compared to Ir₃Ti.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.2; Table 1; Table 2; Fig. 4(a); Fig. 5(a); Reference [18].

## [Ta (Tantalum) in Ir-based γ/γ’ alloys]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ir/Ir₃Ta and Ir/Ir₃Ta–V ternary systems
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Ir₃Ta binary; Ir₂₄V₅Ta₃ (Ta fraction of X+V = 3/8)
- **Main Function:** Form L1₂ γ’ phase with stable coherency at relatively small critical diameter.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Ta atomic radius 145 pm; Ir₃Ta has intermediate positive misfit. The Ir/Ir₃Ta phase diagram solubility lines are nearly vertical, meaning composition changes make negligible contribution to the temperature-dependent lattice misfit.
- **Property Effect:**
  - Room-temperature lattice misfit: +1.04% (experimental)
  - d_crit ≈ 40 nm, weakly temperature dependent
  - Ternary Ir₂₄V₅Ta₃: d_crit ≈ 145 nm at room temperature
  - Cohesive energy 9.972 eV/atom
  - Debye temperature 422.7 K (binary) → 453.3 K (ternary)
- **Potential Side Effects:** Larger misfit than Ir₃Ti; binary Ir₃Ta may be less coherent than Ir₃Ti.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.2; Table 1; Fig. 4(a); Fig. 5(a).

## Coherency Strengthening / Interface Coherency Optimization

- **Related elements:** V (as misfit-compensating element), combined with Ti, Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta.
- **Typical alloys:** Ir/Ir₂₄Ti₇V, Ir/Ir₂₄Zr₂V₆, Ir/Ir₂₄Hf₂V₆ (fully coherent); Ir/Ir₂₄V₅Nb₃ and Ir/Ir₂₄V₅Ta₃ (small misfit)
- **Key compositions:** Ir₃XₘVₙ ternary L1₂ phases with m + n = 1
- **Microstructural changes:** Reduction of lattice misfit between γ and γ’ phases from −0.6% to +2.4% (binary) to −0.8% to 0% (ternary) over 0–2000 K; substantial increase in critical coherent diameter from ~20 nm to ~400–7000 nm for optimized ternary alloys.
- **Property improvements:** Enhanced high-temperature structural stability; higher Debye temperature (indicating stronger bonding); bulk modulus retained at 2000 K (200–240 GPa); improved coherency implies better creep/thermal resistance (from the misfit-stability paradigm).
- **Risks and limitations:** The paper is computational; experimental validation is not provided. The critical diameter model is approximate (neglects elastic anisotropy; assumes spherical precipitates in the original formulation, though Eq. (5) includes elastic interaction). The paper also notes that the model derived by Liang et al. predicted that binary Ir₃Ti, Ir₃Zr, Ir₃Hf, and Ir₃Nb cannot form fully coherent interfaces, except Ir/Ir₃Ti; ternary strategy is needed to achieve full coherency.

## Precipitation Strengthening (Order Strengthening)

- **Related elements:** Hf, Nb, Ta, Ti, V, Zr (as γ’ formers)
- **Typical alloys:** Ir/Ir₃X and Ir/Ir₃XₘVₙ
- **Key compositions:** L1₂-structured Ir₃X precipitates
- **Microstructural changes:** Coherent precipitated γ’ particles embedded in FCC Ir (γ) matrix.
- **Property improvements:** The paper states that dual-phase Ir-based superalloys “exhibit remarkable mechanical properties, super-high melting temperatures (>2300 K), and corrosion resistance at elevated temperatures” and that “order and coherency strengthening” are among the strengthening mechanisms.
- **Risks and limitations:** Coarsening of γ’ phase caused by large misfit would adversely impact high-temperature mechanical properties; semicoherent transition at small precipitate sizes for large-misfit binaries.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Novel alloy design strategy validated:** Mixing Ir₃V (negative lattice misfit with Ir) with another Ir₃X binary (positive misfit) to form ternary Ir₃XₘVₙ γ’ phases effectively reduces the total lattice misfit with the Ir matrix to near zero over a wide temperature range (0–2000 K).

2. **Lattice misfit reduction:** From −0.6% to +2.4% for binary Ir/Ir₃X to −0.8% to 0% for ternary Ir/Ir₃XₘVₙ.

3. **Best-performing ternary alloys:** Ir₂₄Ti₇V, Ir₂₄Zr₂V₆, and Ir₂₄Hf₂V₆ exhibit fully coherent γ/γ’ interfaces with critical coherent diameters of 400–7000 nm even up to 2000 K — they should form stronger γ/γ’ composites.

4. **Thermophysical stability:** All new ternary phases are thermodynamically and dynamically stable (negative formation enthalpy, no imaginary phonon frequencies). Their bulk modulus remains high at 2000 K (~200–240 GPa).

5. **V addition effects:** Increases Debye temperature and bonding strength; reduces concentration expansion coefficients compared to binary alloys; mitigates the adverse effect of phase concentration variation on lattice misfit at elevated temperatures.

6. **Atomic radius explanation:** Near-zero misfit in Ti- and V-containing systems arises because Ti and V atomic radii are closest to Ir. Large atoms (Zr, Hf) expand the lattice; V contracts it; mixing these effects cancels misfit.

7. **Stability up to 2373 K:** Experimental evidence (Yamabe et al.) indicates that L1₂ Ir₃NbₘZrₙ phases are highly stable up to 2373 K, supporting the ternary design concept.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Computational nature:** This is purely a first-principles DFT study. No experimental alloys were fabricated or tested. All property values are theoretical predictions.
- **No mechanical testing:** Yield strength, tensile/compressive strength, elongation, fracture toughness, creep rupture life, hardness, and Young’s modulus were not calculated or measured.
- **No oxidation data:** Oxidation resistance, oxide layer composition, and oxidation kinetics were not addressed.
- **No microstructural characterization:** Grain size, grain boundaries, precipitate size distributions, and elemental segregation are not discussed.
- **Critical diameter model limitation:** The critical coherent diameter formula is an approximate model; it does not account for elastic anisotropy, precipitate shape evolution, or elastic interactions in a fully rigorous multi-particle framework.
- **DFT limitations:** GGA-PBE may underestimate band gaps (not relevant here) but is standard for metals; temperature effects are included via quasi-harmonic approximation; high-temperature anharmonic contributions beyond Grüneisen parameter treatment are approximate.
- **Scope of applicability:** The conclusions are specific to Ir-based dual-phase γ/γ’ superalloys. The alloy design principle (mixing negative- and positive-misfit constituents) may be transferable, but the paper does not validate this for other systems (e.g., Ni-based, Nb-W, Mo-W, refractory high-entropy alloys).
- **Experimental validation referenced:** Some values are compared with experimental data: room-temperature lattice misfits (Yamabe et al.), Debye temperatures, sound velocities, bulk moduli, and thermal expansion curves. Mean error of room-temperature misfit vs experiments: 0.135%.
