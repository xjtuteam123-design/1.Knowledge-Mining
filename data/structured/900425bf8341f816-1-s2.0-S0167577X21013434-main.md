# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title**: AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) high entropy alloy strengthened by a novel long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) structure and (Nb, M)C (M = Mo, Hf, Cr)
- **Authors**: Qilu Ye, Bin Yang, Jiqing Zhao, Gang Yang, Zhihua Gong, Li Zhang
- **Journal**: Materials Letters
- **Year**: 2021
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.matlet.2021.130646
- **Alloy system studied**: AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ eutectic high-entropy alloy modified with Mo, Nb, Hf, C
- **Research objective**: To improve the high-temperature and room-temperature properties of AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ HEA by adding Mo, Nb, Hf, C to produce multiple carbides, and to investigate the effect of annealing on microstructure stability and tensile properties, including the formation of a novel LPSO phase
- **Material category**: Refractory-element-containing high-entropy alloy (Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni base with Mo, Nb, Hf, C); not a classical Nb-W, Mo-W, or refractory high-entropy alloy, but contains Mo, Nb, Hf refractory elements

# Alloy System and Research Object

The base alloy is AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁, a eutectic high-entropy alloy with FCC/B2 microstructure, previously declared a promising superalloy. This work develops a modified alloy AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) by adding Mo, Nb, Hf, and C to produce multiple carbides and further improve properties. The research objective is two-fold: (1) to improve high-temperature strength via multiple carbide formation, and (2) to investigate the annealing-induced nano-scale phase (LPSO) and its effect on room-temperature and high-temperature tensile properties.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Melting**: Vacuum induction melting (VIM) of 5 kg ingot from 99.9% pure elements (Al, Co, Cr, Fe, Mo, Ni, Nb, Hf, C).
- **Starting condition**: As-cast.
- **Annealing treatment**: Box resistance furnace; samples cooled in air after holding at temperature.
  - Annealing temperature: 973 K
  - Annealing time: 2 h, 48 h (reported results for 2 h and 48 h; range stated as 2–48 h)
- **No forging, rolling, or other thermomechanical processing** reported in this paper.
- **No hot isostatic pressing, spark plasma sintering, or powder metallurgy** reported.
- **No additive manufacturing** used.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**As-cast microstructure**:
- **Dendrite phase**: L1₂-type FCC single solid solution rich in Co, Cr, Fe, Ni (confirmed by TEM SAED and EDS).
- **Interdendrite**: two BCC phases:
  - (Ni, Al)-rich BCC
  - (Cr, Mo)-rich BCC
- **Carbide phase**: (Nb, M)C (M = Mo, Hf, Cr) multiple carbide present at boundaries and in dendrites. Described as “almost a high entropy carbide” due to relatively high contents of Cr, Mo, Nb, Hf, and C (EDS: Nb 42.76 at.%, C 41.09 at.%, Mo 7.13 at.%, Hf 4.98 at.%, Cr 4.04 at.%).
- **Casting quality**: Excellent casting properties reported (Fig. 1a).

**Annealed microstructure (973 K for 2 h)**:
- A large number of nano-scale phases formed in the dendrite.
- SAED patterns show streaks (indicating stacking faults) and quarter points between bright spots, identifying the phase as a **long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase**.
- The LPSO phase is **rich in Ni and Al** (EDS: Ni 47.98 at.%, Al 22.56 at.%). This is described as a **novel (Ni, Al)-rich LPSO phase**, different from previously reported (Ni, Ti)-rich LPSO in HEAs.
- Stacking faults are directly visible in HRTEM lattice fringes (via FFT).

**Grain size**: Not explicitly reported in the original paper.

**Precipitate size**: LPSO phase is described as “nano-scale,” but specific size values are not provided.

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed**:
- Multiple carbide precipitation strengthening (from (Nb, M)C)
- Nano-scale phase (LPSO) precipitation strengthening
- The paper does not explicitly discuss solid-solution strengthening, grain-boundary strengthening, or ODS strengthening as separate mechanisms.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Card 1: Mo + Nb + Hf + C (combined addition)

**Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ eutectic high-entropy alloy (modified to AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C))

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Mo 3.8 at.%, Nb 1 at.%, Hf 0.2 at.%, C 1 at.% (nominal). Measured carbide composition: Nb 42.76 at.%, C 41.09 at.%, Mo 7.13 at.%, Hf 4.98 at.%, Cr 4.04 at.%

**Main Function**: Formation of multiple (high-entropy) carbide (Nb, M)C (M = Mo, Hf, Cr) to improve high-temperature strength without sacrificing ductility

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Precipitation of (Nb, M)C carbide at dendrite boundaries and within dendrites; the carbide is described as “almost a high entropy carbide” due to high contents of multiple metallic elements (Cr, Mo, Nb, Hf) plus carbon. High-entropy carbides are reported in the introduction to have greater stability and strengthening than single carbides.

**Property Effect**: 
- Tensile strength at 973 K increased from ~538 MPa (AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ baseline) to 771 MPa (modified alloy).
- Elongation at 973 K: 24.5% for modified alloy vs. ~22% for AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ (no decrease in ductility).
- 973 K tensile strength reached 90% of room-temperature tensile strength (858 MPa).

**Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper. The paper reports no decrease in ductility.

**Original Evidence**: Abstract: “a small amount of Mo, Nb, Hf, C elements were added to produce multiple carbides to improve further the properties of the alloy... Compared with AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ alloy, the tensile strength of the new AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) HEA at 973 K increased from 538 to 771 MPa without decreasing in ductility.” Section 3: “The participation in the boundaries and dendrite was multiple carbide (Nb, M)C (M = Mo, Hf, Cr), and it was almost a high entropy carbide due to the contents of Cr, Mo, Nb, Hf and C in the carbide were all relatively high.” Table 1.

## Card 2: NbC / (Nb, M)C carbide

**Applicable Alloy System**: High-entropy alloys (this paper: AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C)); also reported in other HEAs (per references cited)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Nb 1 at.% + C 1 at.% (nominal); carbide composition EDS: Nb 42.76 at.%, C 41.09 at.%, Mo 7.13 at.%, Hf 4.98 at.%, Cr 4.04 at.%

**Main Function**: High-temperature strengthening through carbide formation

**Microstructural Mechanism**: (Nb, M)C carbide precipitates at grain/dendrite boundaries and within dendrites; multiple elements incorporate into the carbide forming a high-entropy carbide with enhanced stability

**Property Effect**: 
- 973 K tensile strength: 771 MPa (vs. 538 MPa for base alloy without this addition). 
- Elongation at 973 K: 24.5% (no ductility loss).

**Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper. The paper reports good casting properties and no ductility decrease.

**Original Evidence**: Introduction: “NbC was reported to increase significantly the high temperature property in some HEAs [4–6].” Section 3 and Table 1.

## Card 3: Hf + C (hafnium carbide formation contribution)

**Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) high-entropy alloy

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Hf 0.2 at.% + C 1 at.% (nominal); Hf content in (Nb, M)C carbide: 4.98 at.%

**Main Function**: Participates in multiple carbide formation (Hf is incorporated into (Nb, M)C as M = Hf)

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Hf atoms substitute into the (Nb, M)C carbide lattice as one of the M elements, contributing to the formation of an “almost high entropy carbide”

**Property Effect**: The combined carbide contribution (including Hf) raised 973 K tensile strength from 538 to 771 MPa. The individual contribution of Hf is not isolated in the original paper.

**Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**Original Evidence**: Table 1 (Hf 4.98 at.% in (Nb, M)C phase). Section 3: “multiple carbide (Nb, M)C (M = Mo, Hf, Cr).”

## Card 4: Annealing-induced (Ni, Al)-rich LPSO phase

**Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) high-entropy alloy

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not an alloying element but a phase formed during annealing. Composition of LPSO: Ni 47.98 at.%, Al 22.56 at.%, Co 10.05 at.%, Fe 9.75 at.%, Cr 4.58 at.%, Mo 2.4 at.%, C 2.68 at.%

**Main Function**: Nano-scale precipitation strengthening at room temperature

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase forms in the dendrite (FCC) during annealing at 973 K. Identified by SAED streak patterns (indicating stacking faults) and quarter points between bright spots. Contains stacking faults visible in HRTEM lattice fringes. Similar LPSO phases in Mg alloys are known to increase yield strength without reducing ductility.

**Property Effect**: 
- After annealing at 973 K for 48 h: room-temperature yield strength increased from 498 to 1008 MPa; room-temperature tensile strength increased from 858 to 1242 MPa; no decrease in ductility.
- At 973 K (after 2 h annealing): no significant improvement observed. “The LPSO phase can significantly improve the tensile property of the alloy at room temperature, while no obvious improvement was found on the property at 973 K.”

**Potential Side Effects**: None noted at room temperature (no ductility decrease). However, the LPSO phase does not improve high-temperature (973 K) properties, suggesting it may not be stable or effective as a strengthening phase at elevated temperature.

**Original Evidence**: Section 3: “a large number of nano-scale phase was formed in the dendrite... the phase was a long-period stacking ordered phase (LPSO)... this LPSO phase was rich in Ni and Al.” Fig. 4(a): RT tensile; Fig. 4(b): 973 K tensile. Table 1 (LPSO composition).

## Card 5: Mo

**Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) high-entropy alloy

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Mo 3.8 at.% (nominal). Distribution: FCC dendrite 5.80 at.%; (Ni, Al)-rich BCC 0.91 at.%; (Cr, Mo)-rich BCC 9.75 at.%; (Nb, M)C 7.13 at.%; LPSO 2.4 at.%

**Main Function**: Participates in multiple carbide formation and solid-solution partitioning into (Cr, Mo)-rich BCC phase

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Mo partitions into both the (Cr, Mo)-rich BCC interdendritic phase and the (Nb, M)C carbide. It substitutes in the carbide lattice as one of the M elements.

**Property Effect**: The specific contribution of Mo alone is not isolated in the original paper. Combined with Nb, Hf, and C, it contributes to 973 K tensile strength of 771 MPa.

**Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**Original Evidence**: Table 1 (Mo distribution).

## Card 6: C (carbon)

**Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) high-entropy alloy

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: C 1 at.% (nominal). C content in carbide: 41.09 at.%; in FCC phase: 2.49 at.%; in BCC phases: 3.29 and 2.81 at.%

**Main Function**: Carbide formation with Nb, Mo, Hf, Cr to create high-entropy carbide precipitates

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Carbon combines with Nb, Mo, Hf, Cr to form (Nb, M)C multiple carbide at dendrite boundaries and within dendrites. C also appears in EDS measurements of FCC and BCC phases (2.49–3.29 at.%), suggesting some carbon is in solid solution or sub-microscopic precipitate form.

**Property Effect**: Contributes to 973 K tensile strength of 771 MPa. Specific carbon-only contribution not isolated.

**Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper. Good casting properties reported.

**Original Evidence**: Table 1; Section 3.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Mechanism 1: Multiple / high-entropy carbide precipitation strengthening

- **Related elements**: Mo, Nb, Hf, Cr, C
- **Typical alloys**: AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) EHEA; references cited include NbC-strengthened HEAs and high-entropy carbide literature
- **Key compositions**: Mo 3.8 at.%, Nb 1 at.%, Hf 0.2 at.%, C 1 at.% (nominal); carbide: Nb 42.76 at.%, C 41.09 at.%, Mo 7.13 at.%, Hf 4.98 at.%, Cr 4.04 at.%
- **Microstructural changes**: Formation of (Nb, M)C carbide at dendrite boundaries and within dendrites; carbide is “almost a high entropy carbide” because multiple metallic elements occupy the metal sublattice
- **Property improvements**: 973 K tensile strength increased from ~538 MPa (base alloy) to 771 MPa; elongation 24.5% (no decline from ~22% baseline)
- **Risks and limitations**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper. General risks of carbide additions in HEAs (e.g., reduced ductility, processing difficulty) are not discussed here.

## Mechanism 2: Nano-scale LPSO phase precipitation strengthening

- **Related elements**: Ni, Al (with Co, Fe, Cr, Mo, C also present in the phase)
- **Typical alloys**: AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) HEA (annealed); LPSO phases known in Mg-Gd/Zn and Mg-Y/Zn alloys
- **Key compositions**: LPSO phase: Ni 47.98 at.%, Al 22.56 at.%, Co 10.05 at.%, Fe 9.75 at.% (Table 1)
- **Microstructural changes**: Nano-scale LPSO phase forms in FCC dendrites during annealing at 973 K; characterized by stacking faults (SAED streaks, HRTEM lattice fringe evidence) and quarter-point superlattice reflections
- **Property improvements**: After 973 K / 48 h annealing: RT yield strength 498 → 1008 MPa; RT tensile strength 858 → 1242 MPa; no ductility reduction
- **Risks and limitations**: No improvement at 973 K (tensile properties of annealed alloy at 973 K were “virtually unchanged” vs. as-cast); LPSO may not be stable or effective at elevated temperature (973 K)

## Mechanism 3: Solid-solution strengthening

- **Related elements**: Mo, Co, Cr, Fe, Ni in FCC and BCC phases
- **Typical alloys**: AlCoCrFeNi₁.₉(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) HEA
- **Key compositions**: FCC dendrite: Co 16.23 at.%, Cr 17.48 at.%, Fe 22.49 at.%, Mo 5.80 at.%, Ni 25.19 at.%; BCC phases as in Table 1
- **Microstructural changes**: Multi-element solid solutions in L1₂ FCC dendrite and two BCC interdendritic phases
- **Property improvements**: Baseline strength values reflect solid-solution contribution but are not isolated in this paper. The paper does not quantify solid-solution strengthening effects separately.
- **Risks and limitations**: Not discussed in the original paper.

## Mechanism 4: Grain-boundary strengthening / grain refinement

- **Not discussed** in the original paper. No grain size data or grain-boundary strengthening analysis is presented.

## Mechanism 5: Oxide dispersion strengthening (ODS)

- **Not applicable** to this paper. No oxide dispersoids (Y₂O₃, La₂O₃, etc.) are used or discussed.

## Mechanism 6: Oxidation-resistant alloying

- **Not discussed** in the original paper. Al and Cr are present but their oxidation resistance role is not addressed.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Multiple carbide formation**: Adding Mo, Nb, Hf, and C to AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ produced a (Nb, M)C (M = Mo, Hf, Cr) multiple carbide, described as almost a high-entropy carbide, distributed at dendrite boundaries and within dendrites.

2. **High-temperature improvement**: The modified alloy achieved a tensile strength of 771 MPa at 973 K, up from 538 MPa for AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁, with elongation of 24.5% (vs. ~22% baseline). The 973 K tensile strength reached 90% of the room-temperature tensile strength (858 MPa).

3. **Novel LPSO phase**: Annealing at 973 K for 2 h produced a novel nano-scale (Ni, Al)-rich LPSO phase in the FCC dendrite, distinct from previously reported (Ni, Ti)-rich LPSO in HEAs. It contains stacking faults.

4. **Room-temperature strengthening by LPSO**: After annealing at 973 K for 48 h, room-temperature yield strength increased from 498 to 1008 MPa, and tensile strength from 858 to 1242 MPa, without loss of ductility.

5. **Limited high-temperature effect of LPSO**: The LPSO phase did not significantly improve 973 K tensile properties after annealing at 973 K for 2 h.

6. **Alloy casting behavior**: The new alloy exhibited excellent casting properties.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- The paper does not report **weight percentages** of alloying elements; only atomic ratios are given.
- **Grain size, precipitate size (specific dimensions), hardness, elastic modulus, density, fracture toughness, creep, and compressive properties** are not reported.
- **Oxidation resistance and high-temperature environmental stability** (e.g., oxidation weight gain, oxide layer type, oxygen/nitrogen embrittlement, grain-boundary oxidation) are not addressed.
- The paper reports **tensile tests at 973 K** but no tests at other elevated temperatures (e.g., 1073 K, 1273 K, 1473 K) despite the general refractory/alloy high-temperature focus.
- The high-temperature effect of the LPSO phase was tested only after **2 h annealing**; a longer annealed sample (48 h) was not tested at 973 K.
- The paper presents the authors’ speculation that high-entropy carbides have better stability and strengthening than single carbides (from cited references), but the direct comparison with single NbC in this specific alloy system is not experimentally verified.
- The role of individual elements (Mo vs. Nb vs. Hf) in carbide formation is not separated; property improvements are attributed to the combined carbide effect.
- The applicability of the LPSO strengthening concept to refractory-centric alloys (Nb-W, Mo-W, refractory HEAs) is not established; the LPSO phase here is based on a non-refractory Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni matrix.
- The paper is a short communication (Materials Letters) and does not include long-term thermal stability studies beyond 48 h at 973 K.
