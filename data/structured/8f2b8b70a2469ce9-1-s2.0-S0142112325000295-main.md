# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title**: Machine learning model for predicting the influence of crystallographic orientation on thermomechanical fatigue of Ni-base superalloys
- **Authors**: Rohan Acharya, Alexander N. Caputo, Richard W. Neu
- **Journal**: International Journal of Fatigue
- **Year**: 2025
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2025.108832
- **Alloy system studied**: Ni-base superalloys (single-crystal and directionally-solidified)
- **Research objective**: To extend a previously developed probabilistic physics-guided neural network (PPgNN) to predict thermomechanical fatigue (TMF) and creep-fatigue life across different crystallographic orientations by incorporating a normalized elastic modulus as a physics-informed input
- **Material category**: Ni-base superalloys — single-crystal (SX) and directionally-solidified (DS). This paper is not within the domain of Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. It is a machine learning modeling study focused on Ni-base superalloys.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Physics-informed input enables orientation generalization**: Incorporating a normalized elastic modulus (E_ratio = E_〈001〉/E_〈hkl〉) as a physics-informed input allows the PPgNN model to predict fatigue life across all crystallographic orientations using only sparse orientation-specific data.

2. **Orientation–life correlation**: The model predicts that the 〈001〉 orientation has the longest fatigue life while the 〈111〉 orientation has the shortest, consistent with the inverse relationship between fatigue life and elastic modulus.

3. **Elastic modulus magnitude and temperature dependence**: At room temperature, the Young's modulus in 〈111〉 is 2.5× that of 〈001〉; at 1100 °C, this difference increases to nearly 4×. The model uses the average ratio at T_max and T_min for TMF predictions.

4. **Model quality**: The PPgNN predictions fall within a factor of 3 of observed lives for most data points across all orientation types (〈001〉/L, T-oriented, non-〈001〉). The 95% confidence intervals successfully envelop most actual ground truth values.

5. **Off-axis orientation sensitivity**: For single crystals, an off-axis misorientation of 10° or less impacts life by less than a factor of 2, while 15° off-axis affects life by greater than a factor of 2.

6. **DS transverse orientation surrogate**: Using ϕ = 22.5° with θ = 90° as a surrogate for the T-orientation elastic modulus of DS alloys provides a reasonable estimate (E_L/E_T = 0.79 predicted vs. 0.80 experimental at 900 °C).

7. **TMF phasing effects**: IP TMF is generally more damaging than OP TMF, particularly with tensile dwells. For T_max = 950 °C and 20 min dwell, OP TMF with compressive dwell reduces life more compared to IF with 20 min compressive dwell.

8. **Limitation of scalar orientation input**: The model cannot capture grain-boundary-controlled creep rupture behavior under T-oriented IP TMF conditions at high strain ranges, as it has no information about grain boundaries or creep data.

9. **Physics-informed generalization advantage**: "a purely data-driven machine learning approach would require significantly more experimental fatigue data across intermediate orientations to achieve similar results," whereas the physics-guided input enables accurate predictions from sparse datasets.

10. **Model flexibility**: The PPgNN can predict life for any crystallographic orientation, not just the limited cases where experimental data exists, providing a unified framework for all orientations and test conditions.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Applicable scope:**
- This paper applies exclusively to **SX and DS Ni-base superalloys** used in gas turbine blades. It is not directly transferable to refractory high-temperature alloys (Nb-W, Mo-W, etc.) or additively manufactured refractory alloys without adaptation of the physics inputs.
- The model covers loading conditions from room temperature to 1100 °C, strain ratios R_ε = ∞ to 0, strain rates from 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻² 1/s, mechanical strain ranges from 0.3% to 2.0%, TMF T_max from 750 °C to 1050 °C, T_min from 100 °C to 600 °C, and dwell times up to 20 minutes.
- The dataset comprises 573 data points from open literature: 337 SX (274 〈001〉, 63 non-〈001〉) and 236 DS (177 L-oriented, 59 T-oriented).

**Limitations:**
- The PPgNN does not have information on grain boundaries, so it cannot predict grain-boundary-controlled fracture, such as the first-cycle creep rupture observed in T-oriented IP TMF at high strain range (0.7%) and T_max = 950 °C.
- The model uses a single scalar input (normalized elastic modulus) to represent orientation, which is a first-order approximation for DS alloys and does not capture the true grain texture variability.
- The model was validated on Ni-base superalloys only; its applicability to other alloy systems is not demonstrated.
- The model predictions are within a factor of 3 of observed life, which is typical engineering accuracy but may be insufficient for some design applications.
- The paper notes that the uncertainty of orientation is inherent in the training data because the actual crystallographic misorientation of test specimens is often not reported.
- The paper does not provide any information about alloy composition–property relationships, strengthening mechanisms, or oxidation behavior relevant to refractory alloy development. It is a computational/machine learning study, not an experimental materials science investigation.

**Not applicable to the scope of this literature analysis template**: The paper does not address Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. It provides no information about carbide precipitation, oxide dispersion strengthening, oxidation layer characterization, or refractory alloy composition design.
