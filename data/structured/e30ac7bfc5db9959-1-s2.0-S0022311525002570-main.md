# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Enhanced strength and thermal stability of tungsten alloys by the dispersion of nanoscale potassium bubbles and ZrC particles
- **Authors:** Run Yang, Zhuoming Xie, Rui Liu, Junfeng Yang, Xuebang Wu, Xianping Wang, Qianfeng Fang, Cheng Ye, Changsong Liu
- **Journal:** Journal of Nuclear Materials, Volume 613, 155863
- **Year:** 2025 (Available online 30 April 2025; received 14 December 2024; revised 19 April 2025; accepted 28 April 2025)
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnucmat.2025.155863
- **Alloy system studied:** W-based alloys: K-doped W (WK) and K-doped W with 0.5 wt.% ZrC (WZCK)
- **Research objective:** To enhance the strength, lower the ductile-to-brittle transition temperature (DBTT), and improve the thermal stability of tungsten alloys by the synergistic dispersion of nanoscale potassium bubbles and ZrC particles, with the goal of achieving high-performance plasma-facing materials (PFMs) for fusion reactors.
- **Material category:** W-based alloy (K-doped tungsten; carbide-dispersion-strengthened W alloy), powder-metallurgy + hot-rolled bulk tungsten alloy.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- The paper studies **tungsten-based alloys** for plasma-facing materials in fusion reactors.
- Two alloys are compared:
  - **WK:** K-doped W alloy (potassium content 76 wt. ppm in final alloy; nominal 105 wt. ppm in starting powder).
  - **WZCK:** K-doped W alloy with 0.5 wt.% ZrC addition (nominal Zr content 3700 wt. ppm; C 320 wt. ppm; O 718 wt. ppm in final alloy).
- Material category: W-based alloy; K-bubble-dispersion-strengthened W; ZrC-precipitate/K-bubble synergistically strengthened W alloy.
- Not a multi-principal element alloy or refractory high-entropy alloy.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Fabrication route:** Powder metallurgy.
- **Ball milling:** K-doped W powders and ZrC particles mixed and ball-milled in a planetary ball mill for 4 h in argon atmosphere, rotation speed 240 rpm, ball-to-powder weight ratio 8:1. WC/Co balls and mortars used to minimize impurity contamination.
- **Cold isostatic pressing (CIP):** ball-milled powders compacted into green compact.
- **Sintering:** 2050 °C for 4 h in flowing dry hydrogen.
- **Hot rolling:** sintered materials hot-rolled at 1600 °C with total thickness reduction of about 70%; final plate thickness ~6.0 mm.
- **Density:** relative density of WK = 99.9%; WZCK = 99.8% (theoretical density of W 19.3 g/cm³ and ZrC 6.73 g/cm³).
- **Annealing for thermal stability studies:** 1000 °C–1800 °C for 1 h in vacuum. Key conditions: 1000 °C/1 h and 1600 °C/1 h.
- The consolidation and hot rolling were carried out at ATTL Advanced Materials Co., Ltd.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Matrix phase
- BCC W matrix.

## WK (1000 °C annealed) — EBSD
- Elongated grains: ~75.1 μm in RD, ~36.2 μm in TD.
- Fraction of low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs, 2°–10°): 48.2%.
- Grain boundaries: high fraction of LAGBs indicating subgrain substructure.

## WZCK (1000 °C annealed) — EBSD
- Coarse and ultrafine grains present; fraction of LAGBs = 59.4% (higher than WK).
- LAGBs distributed in elongated mother grains; they interlock to form cellular grids.
- BSE shows elongated grains composed of fine equiaxed subgrains with average grain size ~2.6 μm; fine particles and pores observed.
- LAGBs represent grain boundaries of fine subgrains.

## After 1600 °C/1 h annealing
- LAGB fraction decreases to 6.5% (WK) and 7.2% (WZCK) → subgrain annihilation and recrystallization.
- Fully recrystallized, nearly equiaxed grains.
- **Grain size after 1600 °C:** WK = 157.8 μm; WZCK = 52.4 μm. ZrC particles suppress grain growth at elevated temperature.

## TEM — WK
- Nanoscale K bubbles, size 10–300 nm, distributed in grains and grain boundaries.
- More than 70% of K bubbles smaller than 40 nm.
- K bubbles pin grain boundaries and dislocations.

## TEM — WZCK
- Both ZrC nanoparticles and nanoscale K bubbles observed.
- Highly dispersed nanoparticles and K bubbles significantly refine grains.
- Smallest dispersoids (~10 nm) contain K and O signals: K bubbles, mainly in grain interior.
- Larger particles with Zr, C, O (40–70 nm) and Zr, O: Zr-C-O particles and larger ZrO₂ particles; attributed to reaction of ZrC nanoparticles with impurity O.
- A small fraction of dispersoids containing Zr, O, K observed near grain boundaries; possibly ZrC particles encapsulated in K bubbles during sintering.
- Precipitate type: ZrC, Zr-C-O, ZrO₂.
- K bubbles: 10–300 nm; >70% smaller than 40 nm.

## Strengthening mechanisms discussed
- **Solid-solution strengthening:** Not explicitly discussed.
- **Precipitation/dispersion strengthening:** ZrC nanoparticles (hard, coherent/semi-coherent interfaces with W).
- **K-bubble strengthening:** K bubbles pin dislocations and grain boundaries.
- **Grain-boundary strengthening / grain refinement:** ZrC and K bubbles refine grains; Hall-Petch relationship cited for recrystallized samples.
- **LAGB strengthening:** High fraction of LAGBs hinders dislocation motion while maintaining ductility.
- **Second-phase strengthening:** Synergy of K bubbles and ZrC particles.

## Reference data cited in the paper
- Hot rolled pure W plate (Shen et al.): UTS ~695 MPa at 200 °C; ~480 MPa at 500 °C.
- Hot rolled pure W (authors' previous work): UTS 644 MPa, TE 4.3% at 200 °C; UTS 339 MPa, TE 20.7% at 500 °C.
- As-rolled/stress-relieved carbide-dispersion-strengthened W (W-HfC, W-ZrC, W-TiC): tensile strength 450–680 MPa at 500 °C; decreases to below 320 MPa after annealing at 1600 °C.
- W-K alloy (Ref. [44]): high tensile strength 611 MPa; after annealing at 1600 °C for 0.5 h, strength decreased to 413 MPa.
- Hot rolled pure W after recrystallization: tensile strength decreases from 330 MPa to 170 MPa (Ref. [37]).

## Fracture behavior
### 1000 °C annealed
- WK at 150 °C: transgranular cleavage predominant.
- WZCK at 150 °C: transgranular cleavage predominant, but much finer lamellar texture.
- WK at 200 °C: transgranular cleavage predominant; more torn grains at 200, 300 °C; at 400 °C, layered fracture along grain boundaries.
- WZCK at 200–400 °C: few microcracks along grain boundaries; dense delamination with ductile avulsion governs fracture. Obvious plastic deformation of W grains implies good ductility.

### 1600 °C annealed
- Both samples show columnar-to-equiaxed grain transition.
- Fracture at 150–300 °C: mixture of intergranular and transgranular fracture; fraction of transgranular fracture in WK higher than WZCK.
- At 400 °C: transgranular fracture dominated; particles found on WZCK fracture surface attributed to ZrC particles.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Element or Element Combination: K (Potassium) — as nanoscale K bubbles

### Applicable Alloy System
- W-based alloys; K-doped W (WK); WZCK. Also historically K-doped W wires for filament lamps.

### Addition Amount or Composition Range
- Starting powder: 105 wt. ppm K; final WK alloy: 76 wt. ppm K; final WZCK alloy: 73 wt. ppm K.

### Main Function
- Improves high-temperature strength, raises recrystallization temperature (up to 1900 °C in K-doped W wires), lowers DBTT, and enhances thermal shock resistance and neutron-irradiation tolerance.

### Microstructural Mechanism
- Nanoscale K bubbles (10–300 nm; >70% smaller than 40 nm) pin grain boundaries and dislocations. K bubbles can deform plastically during high-temperature testing; they exist as liquid/gas phase at 63 °C–760 °C and can be stretched into rod-shaped bubbles. K bubbles inhibit lateral movement of grain boundaries during hot rolling, improving recrystallization resistance.

### Property Effect
- WK at 1000 °C annealed: brittle at 150 °C, ductile at 200 °C with UTS 702 MPa, TE 2.4%. At 500 °C UTS 516 MPa. WK hardness 456 HV after 1000 °C annealing. K-doped W with high tensile strength of 611 MPa has been reported in the literature (Ref. [44]). K-doped W shows DBTT ~300 °C after 1.135 dpa/600 °C neutron irradiation, lower than pure W (>580 °C).

### Potential Side Effects
- Bulk W-K alloys generally show much lower strength and higher DBTT than W-K wires. Strength decreases significantly after recrystallization.

## Element or Element Combination: ZrC (0.5 wt.%)

### Applicable Alloy System
- W-based alloys; W-ZrC; WZCK; carbide-dispersion-strengthened W.

### Addition Amount or Composition Range
- 0.5 wt.% ZrC addition (nominal). Final alloy composition: Zr = 3700 wt. ppm; C = 320 wt. ppm; O = 718 wt. ppm. Starting ZrC particle size ~50 nm.

### Main Function
- Increases tensile strength, lowers DBTT, refines grains, absorbs impurity oxygen, improves thermal stability and irradiation resistance.

### Microstructural Mechanism
- ZrC nanoparticles have high melting temperature (3540 °C), high hardness, and low lattice mismatch with W; they can form coherent or semi-coherent interfaces with W, inhibiting particle coarsening and enhancing interfacial bonding. ZrC acts as oxygen getter, reacting with impurity O to form Zr-C-O (40–70 nm) and larger ZrO₂ particles, suppressing brittle intergranular fracture and reducing DBTT. ZrC particles refine grains and pin grain boundaries.

### Property Effect
- WZCK at 150 °C (1000 °C annealed): ductile, UTS 960 MPa, TE 6.8%; WK brittle at 150 °C. At 200 °C: WZCK UTS 883 MPa, TE 13.4%. At 500 °C (1000 °C annealed): WZCK UTS 699 MPa vs. WK 516 MPa. After 1600 °C annealing: WZCK UTS 460 MPa at 500 °C vs. WK 319 MPa. WZCK hardness 467 HV after 1000 °C, 390 HV after 1600 °C. WZCK recrystallization temperature ~1600 °C; grain size after 1600 °C annealing 52.4 μm vs. 157.8 μm for WK. Literature: W-0.5wt%ZrC shows DBTT 300–500 °C after 1 dpa/600 °C neutron irradiation. As-rolled/stress-relieved W-ZrC/W-TiC/W-HfC tensile strength 450–680 MPa at 500 °C; decreases below 320 MPa after 1600 °C annealing.

### Potential Side Effects
- Increased O and C content in the alloy (Table 2); formation of Zr-C-O and ZrO₂ particles; still susceptible to strength decrease after prolonged high-temperature operation.

### Original Evidence
- Section 1, paragraph 2; Section 3.3, Figs. 6, 8; Table 2; Section 4.2, paragraph 2; Conclusions.

## Element or Element Combination: Zr + C + O (ZrC → Zr-C-O/ZrO₂ transformation)

### Applicable Alloy System
- W-ZrC; WZCK.

### Addition Amount or Composition Range
- Zr = 3700 wt. ppm; C = 320 wt. ppm; O = 718 wt. ppm in final WZCK alloy.

### Main Function
- Oxygen gettering; transformation of ZrC to Zr-C-O and ZrO₂; suppresses low-temperature brittleness; stabilizes precipitates at high temperature.

### Microstructural Mechanism
- ZrC nanoparticles react with impurity oxygen to form Zr-C-O particles (40–70 nm) and larger ZrO₂ particles. This reaction removes harmful oxygen from grain boundaries, reducing brittle intergranular fracture and DBTT. Some dispersoids containing Zr, O, and K near grain boundaries are possibly ZrC encapsulated in K bubbles during sintering.

### Property Effect
- Contributes to WZCK lower DBTT (100–150 °C vs. 150–200 °C for WK). Improved ductility at 150–200 °C. High thermal stability up to 1600 °C.

### Potential Side Effects
- O content increases to 718 wt. ppm; presence of ZrO₂ could be beneficial for oxide dispersion strengthening but specific quantitative effect not stated.

## Oxide dispersion strengthening
- **Related elements:** O, Zr; ZrO₂ particles.
- **Typical alloys:** WZCK; W-ZrC (literature).
- **Key compositions:** O = 718 wt. ppm; ZrO₂ particles formed from ZrC + O.
- **Microstructural changes:** ZrO₂ particles formed by reaction of ZrC with impurity O; suppresses brittle intergranular fracture; removes oxygen from grain boundaries.
- **Property improvements:** Reduced DBTT; improved ductility at low temperatures (WZCK ductile at 150 °C vs. WK brittle).

## Grain-boundary strengthening / grain refinement
- **Related elements:** ZrC; K bubbles; hot rolling; LAGBs.
- **Typical alloys:** WZCK; WK; W-ZrC.
- **Key compositions:** 0.5 wt.% ZrC; 73–76 wt. ppm K.
- **Microstructural changes:** ZrC and K bubbles pin grain boundaries; refine grains; fine equiaxed subgrains ~2.6 μm in 1000 °C annealed WZCK; LAGB fraction 59.4% in WZCK vs. 48.2% in WK. After 1600 °C annealing, WZCK grain size 52.4 μm vs. WK 157.8 μm.
- **Property improvements:** Hall-Petch strengthening; higher strength after recrystallization.
- **Risks and limitations:** LAGBs annihilate after 1600 °C annealing (LAGB fraction drops to 7.2%).

## Grain refinement/strengthening by hot rolling
- **Related elements:** Hot-rolling deformation; high fraction LAGBs.
- **Typical alloys:** WZCK; WK.
- **Key compositions:** 70% thickness reduction at 1600 °C.
- **Microstructural changes:** Introduces high fraction of LAGBs (59.4% in WZCK); fine subgrains; dislocation substructures.
- **Property improvements:** Higher strength while maintaining ductility due to LAGBs allowing dislocation transmission.
- **Risks and limitations:** Substructures annihilated at ≥1400–1600 °C annealing, reducing strength.

## Second-phase strengthening / dispersion strengthening (Synergistic)
- **Related elements:** K bubbles + ZrC particles.
- **Typical alloys:** WZCK.

# Key Conclusions

1. WZCK (K-doped W + 0.5 wt.% ZrC) fabricated by powder metallurgy and hot rolling exhibits high strength, low DBTT (100–150 °C), and high thermal stability.
2. WZCK is ductile at 150 °C with UTS 960 MPa and TE 6.8%, while WK is brittle at 150 °C. At 200 °C, WZCK UTS 883 MPa and TE 13.4%; WK UTS 702 MPa and TE 2.4%.
3. WZCK shows a recrystallization temperature of ~1600 °C and maintains fine grains and higher strength after 1600 °C annealing. At 500 °C after 1600 °C annealing, WZCK UTS = 460 MPa (44.2% higher than WK UTS = 319 MPa), TE = 26.2%.
4. The synergistic effects of nanoscale K bubbles, ZrC particles, and high fraction of LAGBs from hot rolling lead to high strength, low DBTT, and high thermal stability.
5. ZrC nanoparticles react with impurity oxygen to form Zr-C-O and ZrO₂ particles, which suppress low-temperature brittleness and reduce DBTT.
6. The dispersed K bubbles and ZrC particles significantly refine grains and improve strength and thermal stability of W alloys.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- The original paper does not include creep, fracture toughness, or fatigue properties.
- The paper does not report oxidation resistance data or oxidation layer composition.
- The paper does not investigate irradiation resistance of WZCK; irradiation resistance is only inferred from literature on W-ZrC and W-K alloys.
- The mechanism of ZrC encapsulated in K bubbles is based on TEM observation and is presented with caution (“It might be…”), not fully confirmed.
- All tensile tests were performed along the rolling direction (RD) only; anisotropy in mechanical properties was not assessed in detail.
- The study is limited to two alloys (WK and WZCK); no systematic variation of ZrC content or K content was performed.
- The paper focuses on bulk plasma-facing material applications in fusion reactors; extrapolation to other applications requires further validation.
- The experimental approach is conventional powder metallurgy + hot rolling; no additive manufacturing route was evaluated.
- The original paper does not explicitly state the exact phase identification (e.g., XRD) for Zr-C-O and ZrO₂ particles beyond EDS/TEM observations.
