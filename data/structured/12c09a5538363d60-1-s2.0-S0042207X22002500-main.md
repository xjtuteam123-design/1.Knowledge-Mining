# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Microstructure refinement and oxidation behavior improvement in Mo modified TiAl4822 alloy prepared by vacuum sintering
- **Authors**: Wenguang Zhu, Ye He, Xiangyong Cui, Yonggang He, Bo Li, Conghui Zhang
- **Journal**: Vacuum
- **Year**: 2022
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.vacuum.2022.111122
- **Alloy system studied**: TiAl4822 (Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr, nominal) and TiAl4822-4 wt% Mo
- **Research objective**: To investigate the effect of 4 wt% Mo addition on microstructure refinement, high-temperature compressive properties (800 °C), and high-temperature oxidation behavior (900 °C) of TiAl4822 alloy fabricated by mechanical milling and vacuum hot-pressing sintering.
- **Material category**: TiAl-based intermetallic alloy (TiAl4822), Mo-modified TiAl alloy. Note: This is **not** a Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloy; it is a γ-TiAl intermetallic alloy. The paper falls within the broader scope of high-temperature structural materials but not within refractory metal alloys.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- Pre-alloyed TiAl4822 powder (particle size 106–180 μm) produced by plasma rotating electrode process (PREP).
- High-purity Mo powder (99.9%, particle size 2.5–6 μm) produced by RO process.
- Two alloys studied:
  1. TiAl4822 (Mo-free reference)
  2. TiAl4822-4Mo (TiAl4822 + 4 wt% Mo)
- Both alloys fabricated by mechanical milling followed by cold isostatic pressing and vacuum hot-pressing sintering at 1200 °C/2 h.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Pre-alloy powder production**: Plasma rotating electrode process (PREP); TiAl4822 powder particle size 106–180 μm.
- **Mo powder production**: RO process; purity 99.9%; particle size 2.5–6 μm.
- **Mechanical milling**: Planetary ball mill (SPEX 8000 M), 3 h, rotation 300 r/min, argon gas protection.
  - Ball-milling tank: resin tank.
  - Grinding balls: hard corundum.
  - Ball-to-powder ratio: 4.
  - Ball diameter: 5 mm.
- **Cold isostatic pressing (CIP)**: 200 MPa.
- **Hot pressing sintering**: 1200 °C/2 h, heating rate 100 °C/min, applied pressure 10 kN, vacuum ~1.0 × 10³ Pa (note: original text shows “~1.0 × 10 3 Pa” which is 1.0 × 10³ Pa = 1000 Pa, a relatively coarse vacuum).
- **Billet size**: Ф 44 × 20 mm.
- **Cooling after sintering**: Furnace cooling to room temperature.
- **No subsequent heat treatment** (e.g., no separate homogenization, annealing, or aging) is described.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phases**: γ phase (L1₀ tetragonal structure) and α₂ phase (D0₁₉ ordered hexagonal structure).
- **Microstructure type**: Bi-modal structure consisting of:
  - Equiaxed γ phase
  - γ/α₂ lamella colonies
- **Formation mechanism described**:
  - During 1200 °C sintering, alloy is in γ + α two-phase region, promoting equiaxed γ phase formation.
  - During furnace cooling, α → α₂ + γ eutectoid reaction occurs, forming γ/α₂ lamella.
- **Lamella characteristics**: Multi-scaled α₂ lamella distribution:
  - Coarse α₂ lamella: ~0.5 μm width (micro scale)
  - Fine α₂ platelets: ~20 nm width (nano scale)
  - Both distributed in γ matrix.
- **Grain/colony size** (average of three SEM images):
  - TiAl4822:
    - Equiaxed γ grain: 20 μm
    - γ/α₂ colony: 25 μm
  - TiAl4822–4Mo:
    - Equiaxed γ grain: 12 μm
    - γ/α₂ colony: 17 μm
  - Conclusion: Mo addition results in grain refinement of both γ grains and γ/α₂ colonies.
- **Lattice parameters**:
  - TiAl4822 (Mo-free):
    - γ phase: (110) spacing = 0.274 nm; a = (010) = 0.387 nm; c = (100) = 0.400 nm; c/a = 1.033
    - α₂ phase: c = (0001) = 0.408 nm; (10 1̄ 0) = 0.205 nm
  - TiAl4822–4Mo:
    - γ phase: a = (100) = 0.394 nm → **1.8% lattice expansion along a direction**
    - α₂ phase: c = (0001) = 0.438 nm → **7.3% lattice expansion along [0001]**; (10 1̄ 0) = 0.218 nm → **6.3% lattice expansion along [10 1̄ 0]**
  - Mo causes lattice distortion in both γ and α₂ phases.
- **Dislocation substructure**: Equiaxed γ phase in TiAl4822–4Mo shows long straight dislocations, probably 1/2[1 1̄ 0] slip related to β → γ phase transformation. The paper notes that slip on (111)[0 1̄ 1] and (111)[1̄ 01] experiences strong P–N stress due to tetragonal structure and Ti–Al polarization, while slip on (111)[1 1̄ 0] is easier.
- **No precipitates** (e.g., carbides, borides, silicides, oxides) are reported in this paper. Oxide phases are observed only at the surface after oxidation testing (TiO₂, Al₂O₃), not in the microstructural phase constitution.

# Mechanical Properties

- **Compressive yield strength at 800 °C** (0.2% offset; strain rate 0.05 s⁻¹; tested on Gleeble 3500):
  - TiAl4822: 479.4 MPa (average of ≥3 samples)
  - TiAl4822–4Mo: 554.6 MPa (average of ≥3 samples)
  - Improvement: ~75.2 MPa increase (≈15.7% higher).
- **Microhardness (room temperature)**:
  - TiAl4822: 240.5 HV (average)
  - TiAl4822–4Mo: 297.8 HV (average)
  - Improvement: 23.8% higher than TiAl4822.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed**:
  - **Solid-solution strengthening**: Mo addition causes lattice misfit (ε_b) and modulus misfit (ε_G). Combined misfit ε_L expressed as: ε_L = √[(ε′_G)² + (αε_b)²], where ε′_G = ε_G/(1 + 0.5|ε_G|), α = 9–16. Using Labusch-Nabarro model, solid-solution strengthening H_i = 3G_i * ε_L_i^(4/3). Measured lattice misfits: 1.8% in γ phase; 6.3–7.3% in α₂ phase.
  - **Grain refinement (Hall-Petch)**: Smaller equiaxed γ grains and γ/α₂ colonies in Mo-containing alloy. Critical stress τ_c = √[2Gbτ*/(π(1−v)λ)] = k₀/√λ; yield strength proportional to λ^(−1/2).
- **Elongation/ductility**: Not explicitly reported for compressive tests. No tensile elongation, fracture toughness, or creep data are provided.
- **Tensile properties**: Not tested/reported.
- **Creep performance**: Not tested/reported.
- **Fracture toughness**: Not tested/reported.
- **Elastic modulus**: Not explicitly measured; shear modulus used in equations but no numerical value given.
- **Density**: Not reported.
- **Strain rate**: 0.05 s⁻¹ for compressive tests.
- **Testing atmosphere**: Not explicitly stated for compression testing; likely ambient based on Gleeble 3500 typical operation, but the paper does not specify.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxidation test conditions**: 900 °C, quartz tube furnace; durations: 50 h, 150 h, 250 h.
- **Weight gain (ΔM) after 900 °C/250 h**:
  - TiAl4822: 5.58 mg/cm²
  - TiAl4822–4Mo: 3.74 mg/cm²
  - Reduction: 32.97% lower than TiAl4822.
- **Oxidation rate (k) from parabolic fitting (ΔM² = k·t)**:
  - TiAl4822: 0.130 mg² cm⁻⁴ h⁻¹
  - TiAl4822–4Mo: 0.060 mg² cm⁻⁴ h⁻¹
  - Oxidation exponent n = 2 for both alloys, indicating diffusion-controlled oxide growth.
- **Oxide layer structure** (after 900 °C/100 h):
  - TiAl4822: Total oxide layer depth = 9.9 μm. Three layers:
    1. Outermost: TiO₂ (rough and porous surface layer)
    2. Sub-surface: Al₂O₃ (relative dense and uniform)
    3. Inner: TiO₂/Al₂O₃ mixture
  - TiAl4822–4Mo: Total oxide layer depth = 7.9 μm. Same three-layer structure.
- **Surface oxide grain size**:
  - TiAl4822: ~4 μm
  - TiAl4822–4Mo: ~2–3 μm
- **EDS surface composition** (Fig. 6):
  - TiAl4822 (Point A): Ti 31.8 at%, Al 0 at%, O 68.2 at%, Nb 0 at%, Cr 0 at%
  - TiAl4822–4Mo (Point B): Ti 32.0 at%, Al 4.7 at%, O 62.9 at%, Mo 0 at%, Nb 0.1 at%, Cr 0.3 at%
- **Elemental distribution in oxide layer** (TiAl4822–4Mo):
  - Mo and Nb: not present in surface layer; mainly distributed near the matrix (inner layer region). Mo enrichment near matrix.
  - Cr: distribution similar to O but at much lower density; dissolves in TiO₂, decreasing oxygen vacancy density and inhibiting oxide growth.
- **Oxidation mechanism discussion**:
  - Mo reduces oxygen solid solubility in matrix, promoting external Al₂O₃ formation and inhibiting internal oxidation.
  - Mo has higher valence electron than O, decreasing oxygen vacancy concentration and oxidation rate.
  - Mo may retard diffusion of Ti and Al, decelerating oxidation reaction.
  - Diffusion coefficients at 900 °C:
    - Al in γ: 2.41 × 10⁻¹⁸ m²/s; Al in α₂: 6.82 × 10⁻¹⁹ m²/s
    - Ti in γ: 1.06 × 10⁻¹⁷ m²/s; Ti in α₂: 3.18 × 10⁻¹⁸ m²/s
    - Ti diffusion is much faster than Al diffusion in both phases, accounting for rapid TiO₂ surface formation.
- **Thermodynamic driving forces**:
  - ΔG⁰_T,TiO₂ = −910000 + 173T
  - ΔG⁰_T,Al₂O₃ = −1676000 + 320T
  - (Note: Original text presents these as positive equations; the magnitudes indicate Al₂O₃ has larger driving force in magnitude, but the authors emphasizes that kinetics, not thermodynamics, governs phase distribution.)
- **High-temperature embrittlement or oxygen/nitrogen embrittlement**: Not discussed; the paper focuses on oxidation resistance improvement but does not address mechanical degradation after oxidation or ductility loss mechanisms.
- **Grain-boundary oxidation**: Not explicitly discussed; oxidation is described in terms of layered oxide formation rather than grain-boundary penetration.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Mo]

**Applicable Alloy System**: TiAl4822 (Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr, at.%), Mo-modified TiAl alloy prepared by vacuum hot-pressing sintering.

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 4 wt% Mo (sintered alloy composition: 4.03 wt% Mo).

**Main Function**:
- Solid-solution strengthening (lattice misfit in γ and α₂ phases)
- Grain refinement (equiaxed γ grains and γ/α₂ colonies)
- High-temperature compressive strength improvement at 800 °C
- Improvement of high-temperature oxidation resistance at 900 °C

**Microstructural Mechanism**:
- Mo dissolves in γ and α₂ phases, causing lattice distortion. SAED analysis shows 1.8% lattice expansion along a-axis of γ phase, 7.3% and 6.3% lattice expansion along c and a₁ directions of α₂ phase.
- Grain refinement is attributed to low diffusion rate of Mo in TiAl matrix during sintering.
- In oxide layer, Mo is enriched near the TiAl matrix (inner layer), reducing oxygen solid solubility in matrix, promoting external Al₂O₃ formation, inhibiting internal oxidation.
- Mo has higher valence electron than O, decreasing oxygen vacancy concentration in oxide layers.

**Property Effect**:
- Room-temperature microhardness: increases from 240.5 HV (TiAl4822) to 297.8 HV (TiAl4822–4Mo), 23.8% increase.
- Compressive yield strength at 800 °C: increases from 479.4 MPa (TiAl4822) to 554.6 MPa (TiAl4822–4Mo), ~15.7% increase.
- Oxidation weight gain after 900 °C/250 h: decreases from 5.58 mg/cm² (TiAl4822) to 3.74 mg/cm² (TiAl4822–4Mo), 32.97% reduction.
- Oxidation rate: decreases from 0.130 mg² cm⁻⁴ h⁻¹ (TiAl4822) to 0.060 mg² cm⁻⁴ h⁻¹ (TiAl4822–4Mo).
- Oxide layer thickness after 900 °C/100 h: decreases from 9.9 μm (TiAl4822) to 7.9 μm (TiAl4822–4Mo).

**Potential Side Effects**:
- The paper does not explicitly identify negative effects of Mo addition (e.g., density increase, ductility reduction, B2 phase formation) in this study. However, the introduction notes that Mo increases the fraction of B2 phase in some TiAl alloys (citing Li et al.), which is stated as beneficial for high-temperature compressive properties, but the present paper does not analyze B2 phase formation.
- The paper does not discuss hot cracking, workability, or room-temperature ductility effects for this specific alloy.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

**Related Elements**: Mo (primary), Nb, Cr (intrinsic to TiAl4822).

**Typical Alloys**: TiAl4822–4Mo (studied), with reference to Nb-modified TiAl alloys in literature.

**Key Compositions**: 4 wt% Mo; 4.95 wt% Nb; 2.27 wt% Cr.

**Microstructural Changes**:
- Mo causes lattice expansion: 1.8% in γ phase (along a); 7.3% (along [0001]) and 6.3% (along [10 1̄ 0]) in α₂ phase.
- Dislocation accumulation observed in equiaxed γ phase of Mo-containing alloy (long straight dislocations, probably 1/2[1 1̄ 0] slips).
- No precipitate formation associated with solid-solution strengthening in this paper.

**Property Improvements**:
- Compressive yield strength at 800 °C: 479.4 MPa → 554.6 MPa (+15.7%).
- Room-temperature microhardness: 240.5 HV → 297.8 HV (+23.8%).

**Risks and Limitations**:
- The paper uses Labusch-Nabarro and Hall-Petch models but does not quantify relative contributions of solid-solution vs. grain-boundary strengthening.
- Mo addition increases the fraction of B2 phase in some TiAl alloys (noted from literature), which can embrittle at low temperatures; not studied here.

## Grain Refinement / Grain-Boundary Strengthening

**Related Elements**: Mo (low diffusivity retards grain growth).

**Typical Alloys**: TiAl4822–4Mo prepared by powder metallurgy (mechanical milling + CIP + vacuum hot-pressing).

**Key Compositions**: 4 wt% Mo; grain sizes:
- Equiaxed γ grain: 20 μm → 12 μm (40% reduction)
- γ/α₂ colony: 25 μm → 17 μm (32% reduction)

**Microstructural Changes**:
- Reduced equiaxed γ grain size.
- Reduced γ/α₂ colony size.
- Multi-scaled α₂ lamella retained (coarse ~0.5 μm, fine ~20 nm).

**Property Improvements**:
- Higher compressive yield strength at 800 °C (554.6 MPa vs. 479.4 MPa).
- Higher microhardness (297.8 HV vs. 240.5 HV).

**Risks and Limitations**:
- Grain refinement may reduce creep resistance at very high temperatures (not tested in this paper).
- Hall-Petch analysis assumes dislocation pile-up at grain boundaries; applicability at 800 °C in TiAl is not critically examined.

## Oxidation-Resistant Alloying (Mo)

**Related Elements**: Mo, combined with inherent Nb and Cr in TiAl4822.

**Typical Alloys**: TiAl4822–4Mo, TiAl4822.

**Key Compositions**: 4 wt% Mo addition to TiAl4822 (4.03 wt% measured).

**Microstructural Changes**:
- Mo not present in outer TiO₂ layer; enriched near matrix in inner layer.
- Mo reduces oxygen solid solubility in matrix, promotes external Al₂O₃ formation, inhibits internal oxidation.
- Smaller oxide surface grain size (2–3 μm vs. 4 μm for TiAl4822).
- Shallower oxide layer thickness (7.9 μm vs. 9.9 μm after 100 h).

**Property Improvements**:
- Oxidation weight gain after 900 °C/250 h reduced from 5.58 to 3.74 mg/cm² (−32.97%).
- Oxidation rate reduced from 0.130 to 0.060 mg² cm⁻⁴ h⁻¹ (−53.8%).
- Oxide layer thickness reduced by ~20% after 100 h.

**Risks and Limitations**:
- The paper speculates that Mo reduces vacancy density and retards Ti/Al diffusion, but does not directly measure oxygen vacancy concentrations or diffusion coefficients in Mo-modified alloy.
- The exact mechanism (e.g., doping of TiO₂, Al₂O₃ stabilization) is inferred from valence-electron arguments and is not independently verified experimentally in this paper.

# Key Conclusions

1. Both TiAl4822 and TiAl4822–4Mo exhibit a bi-modal microstructure with multi-scaled α₂ lamella after vacuum hot-pressing sintering at 1200 °C/2 h.
2. Addition of 4 wt% Mo effectively refines equiaxed γ grain size (20 → 12 μm) and γ/α₂ colony size (25 → 17 μm).
3. Mo addition results in lattice misfit of 1.8% in γ phase and 6.3–7.3% in α₂ phase, confirmed by SAED analysis.
4. Mo addition increases room-temperature microhardness from 240.5 HV to 297.8 HV (+23.8%).
5. Mo addition increases compressive yield strength at 800 °C from 479.4 MPa to 554.6 MPa (+15.7%).
6. Strengthening originates from two contributions: solid-solution strengthening (lattice misfit) and grain refinement (Hall-Petch effect).
7. Mo addition improves oxidation resistance at 900 °C: weight gain after 250 h reduced from 5.58 to 3.74 mg/cm² (−32.97%); oxidation rate reduced from 0.130 to 0.060 mg² cm⁻⁴ h⁻¹.
8. Mo is mainly distributed near the alloy matrix in the oxide structure; because Mo has higher valence electron than O, it suppresses oxygen vacancy formation, retarding oxidation.
9. Both alloys form a multilayer oxide consisting of TiO₂ (outermost), Al₂O₃ (sub-surface), and TiO₂/Al₂O₃ mixed layer (inner).
10. Oxidation kinetics follow parabolic law (n = 2), indicating diffusion-controlled oxidation.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy system limitation**: This paper studies γ-TiAl intermetallic alloys (TiAl4822 and TiAl4822–4Mo), not refractory high-entropy alloys or Nb/W/Mo-based refractory alloys. If the intended application is refractory alloy databases, this paper is not directly applicable.
- **Temperature range**: Mechanical properties only tested at 800 °C (compression); oxidation tested at 900 °C. No data for >1000 °C or room-temperature tensile properties.
- **No high-temperature strength data beyond 800 °C** (e.g., 1000–1600 °C) are provided.
- **No tensile properties**: Only compressive yield strength and microhardness reported.
- **No creep, fracture toughness, or fatigue data**.
- **Density not reported**.
- **Mechanism uncertainty**: The oxidation mechanism of Mo is partly speculative; the authors infer reduced oxygen vacancy density from valence-electron arguments but do not directly measure vacancy concentrations or oxygen diffusion coefficients.
- **B2 phase consideration**: The introduction mentions that Mo can increase B2 phase fraction in TiAl alloys, which can be beneficial for high-temperature compressive properties but may reduce ductility. The present paper does not characterize B2 phase or discuss ductility effects.
- **Processing specificity**: Results apply to powder-metallurgy (mechanical milling + CIP + vacuum hot-pressing) TiAl4822 with 4 wt% Mo. Extrapolation to cast, forged, or additively manufactured TiAl alloys requires caution.
- **Lack of quantitative strengthening-model validation**: The Labusch-Nabarro and Hall-Petch treatments are presented without quantitative decomposition of strengthening contributions or experimental verification of model constants (e.g., shear modulus values are not given).
- **Oxidation only at one temperature (900 °C)**: No oxidation measurement at 800 °C or >900 °C; the paper mentions strength/oxidation degrade above 900 °C but does not present experimental data in that range.
- **No post-oxidation mechanical testing**: Effect of oxidation on subsequent mechanical properties is not evaluated.

It is included as a reference for Mo-modified TiAl intermetallic alloy behavior, which may be of peripheral interest for high-temperature aerospace structural materials. If the target domain is strictly refractory metal alloys (Nb/W/Mo/Ta-based), this paper should be tagged accordingly and may not contribute directly to that database.
