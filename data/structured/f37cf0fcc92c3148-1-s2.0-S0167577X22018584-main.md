# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Microstructure and high-temperature mechanical properties of new-type heat-resisting aluminum alloy Al6.5Cu2Ni0.5Zr0.3Ti0.25V under the T7 condition
- **Authors:** Hongbin Dai, Lang Wang, Bolun Dong, Jian Miao, Sanbao Lin, Hongtao Chen
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2023 (Volume 332, Article 133503; available online 14 November 2022)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.matlet.2022.133503
- **Alloy system studied:** Al-Cu-Ni-Zr-Ti-V cast heat-resistant aluminum alloy
- **Research objective:** To investigate the microstructure and high-temperature mechanical properties of a new-type heat-resisting aluminum alloy Al6.5Cu2Ni0.5Zr0.3Ti0.25V prepared by low-pressure sand mold casting and subjected to T7 heat treatment, with emphasis on the relationship between microstructure and 350 °C tensile performance.
- **Material category:** Cast heat-resistant aluminum alloy (Al-Cu based multi-microalloyed system). This is **not** a refractory high-temperature alloy (Nb-W, Mo-W, refractory high-entropy alloy, etc.).

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy designation:** Al6.5Cu2Ni0.5Zr0.3Ti0.25V (nominal composition in wt.% indicated by element numbers)
- **Alloy family:** Al-Cu cast aluminum alloy with transition metal microalloying (Ni, Zr, Ti, V, Mn, Cr)
- **Primary strengthening strategy:** Combination of θ′-Al2Cu nanoprecipitates, thermally stable Al3M (M = Ti, Zr, V) phase, and grain-boundary Al7Cu4Ni phase
- **Intended service temperature:** 350 °C (thermo-mechanical coupling environment for lightweight aero-engine components)

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase:** α-Al solid solution (face-centered cubic, Fm3m, a = 0.4049 nm)
- **Average grain size:** 150 μm (measured by EBSD; largest grain size 290 μm)
- **Grain orientation/texture:** Weak preferred orientation; {100} pole figure maximum intensity only 3.65; no obvious texture orientation
- **Grain boundary character:** 71.4% low-angle grain boundaries (<15°); grain boundaries less than 2° account for the largest proportion (61.7%)

**Second phases identified:**

| Phase | Crystal structure/character | Location | Morphology | Size |
|-------|---------------------------|----------|------------|------|
| θ-Al2Cu | Face-centered cubic, Fm3m, a = 0.4049 nm | Grain boundary | Fine bone-shaped/island phase | Not explicitly quantified for grain-boundary θ |
| θ′-Al2Cu | Metastable, coherent/semi-coherent | Within α-Al grains | Nanometer needle-like precipitates | Average length 55 nm |
| τ-Al7Cu4Ni | Ternary intermetallic | Grain boundary (completely or partially wets grain boundaries) | Reticular/island phase | Not explicitly quantified; described as "tiny reticular" |
| Al3M (M = Ti, Zr, V) | Thermally stable phase | Within α-Al matrix grains | Fine sheet-like | Not explicitly quantified |

- **Grain boundary wetting:** The paper explicitly discusses "complete and incomplete wetting of grain boundaries" by the second solid phase, with τ-Al7Cu4Ni and θ-Al2Cu exhibiting grain-boundary wetting equilibrium phenomena.
- **Elemental segregation:** Cu-rich and Ni-rich phases (τ-Al7Cu4Ni) form along grain boundaries of α-Al matrix.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed:** (1) Orowan/dislocation hindrance by θ′-Al2Cu nanoprecipitates at high temperature; (2) thermal stability enhancement by Al3M (M = Ti, Zr, V); (3) grain-boundary pinning/crack propagation resistance by τ-Al7Cu4Ni + θ-Al2Cu; (4) solute segregation for precipitate stabilization (referenced from prior work, not directly demonstrated in this paper).

**Note on thermal stability (microstructural, not oxidation):** The paper emphasizes thermal stability of the strengthening phases rather than oxidation resistance. The θ′-Al2Cu precipitates are metastable and coarsen/transform at temperatures above 250 °C in traditional Al-Cu alloys; microalloying with Zr, Ti, V, Ni is intended to stabilize precipitates and suppress coarsening/transformation at 350 °C. The Al3M (M = Ti, Zr, V) phase is described as a "thermal stable phase."

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

---

**[Cu]**

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Al-Cu-based cast heat-resistant aluminum alloys (Al6.5Cu2Ni0.5Zr0.3Ti0.25V)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 6.5 wt.% Cu
- **Main Function:** Primary precipitate-forming element; forms θ-Al2Cu equilibrium phase and θ′-Al2Cu metastable strengthening precipitates; provides the main high-temperature strengthening contribution through θ′ nanoprecipitates
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** (1) During T7 aging, nanometer needle-like θ′-Al2Cu precipitates (average length 55 nm) form within α-Al grains and hinder dislocation movement at high temperature; (2) at grain boundaries, equilibrium θ-Al2Cu (fine bone-shaped morphology) participates in grain-boundary wetting and contributes to grain-boundary strengthening in combination with τ-Al7Cu4Ni
- **Property Effect:** Contributes to room-temperature UTS of 330.8 MPa and 350 °C UTS of 127.5 MPa (combined with other phases). In the traditional Al-Cu system without microalloying, 350 °C tensile strength is only about 75 MPa, indicating the importance of Cu precipitates combined with stabilizing elements.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Metastable θ′-Al2Cu rapidly transforms to incoherent stable θ-Al2Cu above 250 °C without microalloying, losing coherency and weakening high-temperature performance; low room-temperature ductility (1.75% elongation) associated with brittle fracture mode at 25 °C
- **Original Evidence:** "The main strengthening phase in the traditional Al-Cu alloy is the θ-Al2Cu phase, but the metastable phase... rapidly changes to the non-coherent stable phase at a high temperature above 250 °C" (Introduction); "there are a large number of precipitated θ′-Al2Cu precipitates with nanometer needle-like distribution on the α-Al matrix, with an average length of 55 nm" (Section 3); Fig. 2(a), (e), (f)

---

**[Ni]**

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Al-Cu-Ni cast heat-resistant aluminum alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 2.0 wt.% Ni
- **Main Function:** Forms thermally stable ternary τ-Al7Cu4Ni intermetallic phase at grain boundaries; improves high-temperature performance and microstructural stability of the alloy
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Ni reacts with Cu and Al to form τ-Al7Cu4Ni, which distributes at grain boundaries (reticular/tiny island morphology). This phase completely or partially wets the α-Al grain boundaries, increasing grain-boundary binding strength and preventing crack propagation at high temperature.
- **Property Effect:** Contributes to high-temperature strength retention (127.5 MPa UTS at 350 °C); enables ductile fracture with tear dimples at 350 °C. Reference cited (Tiwary et al.): Al-Cu-Ni alloy with α-Al + Al2Cu + Al7Cu4Ni achieved 1 GPa fracture strength and 9% fracture strain at room temperature, maintaining high strength at 300 °C.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Formation of large grain-boundary second phases may contribute to brittle fracture at room temperature (elongation only 1.75% at 25 °C)
- **Original Evidence:** "When Ni element is added to Al-Cu alloy, Al7Cu4Ni is generated, which can greatly improve the high-temperature performance" (Introduction); "On the grain boundary, a large amount of island phase is τ-Al7Cu4Ni" (Section 3); "The combination of a tiny reticular τ-Al7Cu4Ni phase and a small amount of tiny bony θ-Al2Cu increases the binding strength of grain boundaries and prevents crack propagation" (Section 3); Fig. 2(d), (g), (h)

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

---

## Precipitation Strengthening (θ′-Al2Cu nanoprecipitates)

- **Related Elements:** Cu (with Ti, Zr, V for stabilization)
- **Typical Alloys:** Al6.5Cu2Ni0.5Zr0.3Ti0.25V; Al-Cu cast alloys
- **Key Compositions:** 6.5 wt.% Cu; T7 heat treatment (535 °C/30 h solution + 190 °C/12 h aging)
- **Microstructural Changes:** Nanometer needle-like θ′-Al2Cu precipitates with average length of 55 nm distributed on α-Al matrix; coherent/semi-coherent metastable phase (revealed by SADP)
- **Property Improvements:** Hinder dislocation movement at high temperature; contribute to UTS of 127.5 MPa and YS of 108 MPa at 350 °C; critical for maintaining strength above 250 °C where traditional θ-Al2Cu fails
- **Risks and Limitations:** Metastable θ′-Al2Cu transforms to incoherent stable θ-Al2Cu above 250 °C without microalloying, causing loss of high-temperature performance; low room-temperature elongation (1.75%) with brittle fracture at 25 °C suggests limited ductility at ambient temperature
- **Original Evidence:** "there are a large number of precipitated θ′-Al2Cu precipitates with nanometer needle-like distribution on the α-Al matrix, with an average length of 55 nm" (Section 3); "needle-like θ′-Al2Cu will hinder the dislocation movement in the grain at a high temperature" (Section 3)

## Intermetallic Phase Strengthening (Al3M and Al7Cu4Ni)

- **Related Elements:** Ti, Zr, V (Al3M); Ni, Cu (Al7Cu4Ni)
- **Typical Alloys:** Al6.5Cu2Ni0.5Zr0.3Ti0.25V
- **Key Compositions:** 0.5 wt.% Zr, 0.3 wt.% Ti, 0.25 wt.% V; 2.0 wt.% Ni, 6.5 wt.% Cu
- **Microstructural Changes:** (1) Fine sheet-like Al3M (M = Ti, Zr, V) within grains provides thermally stable phase that survives high-temperature exposure; (2) tiny reticular τ-Al7Cu4Ni phase and fine bony θ-Al2Cu at grain boundaries exhibit wetting equilibrium behavior
- **Property Improvements:** Combined effect yields 350 °C UTS of 127.5 MPa (vs. 75 MPa for traditional Al-Cu); grain-boundary phases increase binding strength and prevent crack propagation; enable ductile fracture behavior at 350 °C (16.11% elongation with tear dimples)
- **Risks and Limitations:** Extensive grain-boundary phase coverage may contribute to room-temperature brittleness (1.75% elongation, river patterns, cleavage steps at 25 °C)
- **Original Evidence:** "The thermal stable phase Al3M(M = Ti, Zr, V) and needle-like θ′-Al2Cu will hinder the dislocation movement in the grain at a high temperature. The combination of a tiny reticular τ-Al7Cu4Ni phase and a small amount of tiny bony θ-Al2Cu increases the binding strength of grain boundaries and prevents crack propagation" (Section 3)

## Grain-Boundary Wetting and Strengthening (Second Solid Phase Wetting)

- **Related Elements:** Cu, Ni (forming θ-Al2Cu and τ-Al7Cu4Ni)
- **Typical Alloys:** Al-Cu-Ni cast alloys
- **Key Compositions:** 6.5 wt.% Cu, 2.0 wt.% Ni
- **Microstructural Changes:** Second solid phase (τ-Al7Cu4Ni, θ-Al2Cu) completely or partially wets the α-Al grain boundaries; 71.4% low-angle grain boundaries (<15°) observed
- **Property Improvements:** Strengthens grain boundaries, prevents crack propagation at high temperature; supports ductile fracture at 350 °C
- **Risks and Limitations:** Grain-boundary wetting phenomenon may create continuous brittle networks potentially reducing low-temperature ductility
- **Original Evidence:** "This behavior is closely related to the so-called second solid phase complete and incomplete wetting of grain boundaries [11,12]" (Section 3); citations to Straumal et al. on grain boundary wetting

# Key Conclusions

1. The Al6.5Cu2Ni0.5Zr0.3Ti0.25V alloy was successfully prepared by low-pressure sand mold casting under T7 heat treatment conditions (535 ± 5 °C/30 h solution treatment, water quench at 80 °C, aging at 190 ± 5 °C for 12 h).

2. The average grain size of the α-Al matrix is 150 μm (maximum 290 μm), with 71.4% low-angle grain boundaries and weak crystallographic texture.

3. The microstructure consists of:
   - θ′-Al2Cu nanoprecipitates (needle-like, average length 55 nm) within α-Al grains
   - Thermally stable Al3M (M = Ti, Zr, V) fine sheet phase within grains
   - τ-Al7Cu4Ni (reticular/tiny island morphology) and θ-Al2Cu (fine bone morphology) at grain boundaries, exhibiting complete/incomplete grain-boundary wetting

4. Mechanical properties:
   - At 25 °C: UTS = 330.8 MPa, YS = 270 MPa, elongation = 1.75% (brittle fracture with river patterns and cleavage steps)
   - At 350 °C: UTS = 127.5 MPa, YS = 108 MPa, elongation = 16.11% (ductile fracture with tear dimples)

5. The 350 °C tensile strength of 127.5 MPa represents a significant improvement over traditional Al-Cu alloys (maximum ~75 MPa at 350 °C).

6. The high-temperature strengthening arises from combined effects: θ′-Al2Cu and Al3M hinder dislocation movement within grains, while grain-boundary τ-Al7Cu4Ni and θ-Al2Cu increase grain-boundary binding strength and prevent crack propagation.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy system limitation:** This paper addresses a cast heat-resistant **aluminum** alloy, not a refractory high-temperature alloy (Nb-W, Mo-W, W-based, refractory high-entropy alloy, or additively manufactured refractory alloy). The melting point, service temperature (350 °C), strengthening mechanisms, and property targets are fundamentally different from refractory alloy systems.

- **Scope of property data:** Only uniaxial tensile properties at 25 °C and 350 °C are reported. No creep, fatigue, fracture toughness, hardness, oxidation resistance, or long-term thermal stability data are provided.

- **Heat treatment condition:** Only the T7 (overaged) condition is studied. The effect of other temper conditions (e.g., T6, T5, as-cast) on microstructure and properties is not investigated.

- **Compositional scope:** Only one alloy composition (Al6.5Cu2Ni0.5Zr0.3Ti0.25V) is examined. No systematic compositional variation or comparison series is included.

- **Microstructural quantification:** While phases are identified, the volume fractions, size distributions, number densities, and spatial statistics of precipitates (θ′-Al2Cu, Al3M, Al7Cu4Ni, θ-Al2Cu) are not comprehensively quantified.

- **Deformation processing:** No wrought processing (extrusion, rolling, forging) is examined; applicability to wrought product forms is not established.

- **Atmosphere:** The testing atmosphere for elevated-temperature tensile tests is not explicitly stated.

- **Long-term stability:** While the paper claims improved thermal stability based on literature precedent, no direct long-term thermal exposure or coarsening kinetics study is performed in this work.

- **Applicable applications:** Lightweight aero-engine components operating in thermo-mechanical coupling environments up to approximately 350 °C where traditional Al-Cu alloys are insufficient.
