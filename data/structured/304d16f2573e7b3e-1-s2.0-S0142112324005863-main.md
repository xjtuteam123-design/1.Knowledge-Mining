# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title**: Temperature effects on low cycle fatigue deformation of powder metallurgy superalloy FGH95
- **Authors**: Yanjie Li, Yibin Liu, Kesi Wu, Jie Sun, Dahai Zhang, Qingguo Fei
- **Journal**: International Journal of Fatigue
- **Year**: 2025 (Volume 192, Article 108727; available online 20 November 2024)
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2024.108727
- **Alloy system studied**: FGH95 powder metallurgy nickel-based superalloy
- **Research objective**: To investigate the temperature effects on low cycle fatigue fracture modes and microstructure evolution mechanisms of FGH95 alloy at 420 °C, 510 °C, and 600 °C
- **Material category**: Powder metallurgy nickel-based superalloy (precipitation-hardened, γ/γ′ microstructure)

# Alloy System and Research Object

This paper studies the FGH95 powder metallurgy nickel-based superalloy, which is utilized in turbine disks of aero engines. The material exhibits a high volume fraction of γ′ precipitate phase with face-centered cubic (FCC) matrix structure. The research focuses on low cycle fatigue (LCF) deformation behavior and fracture mechanisms at three temperatures (420 °C, 510 °C, and 600 °C), specifically examining the transition from shear fracture to Mode I-type fracture with increasing temperature.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Face-centered cubic (FCC) γ phase. XRD confirmed that the phase structure remains FCC after LCF tests at T420, T510, and T600.
- **Precipitate phase**: γ′ phase (Ni₃(Al,Ti)-type), confirmed by TEM selected area diffraction (SAED). The γ′ precipitate is the primary strengthening phase.
- **Matrix composition**: The γ matrix phase comprises Co and Cr elements (identified via TEM-EDS).
- **Precipitate composition**: The γ′ precipitated phase comprises Ni, Al, and Ti elements (identified via TEM-EDS).
- **Precipitate morphology**: At T420 (low temperature), deformed γ′ precipitates appear as diamond shapes (due to dislocation shearing). At T600 (high temperature), undeformed γ′ precipitates remain as regular squares (due to dislocation bypassing).
- **Grain size**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Elemental segregation**: Not explicitly discussed as a primary finding, though TEM-EDS elemental mapping shows distinct partitioning of Co and Cr to the γ matrix and Ni, Al, and Ti to the γ′ precipitates.
- **Unmelted Zr**: Elemental analysis revealed the presence of unmelted Zr within the alloy, attributed to the powder metallurgy process.

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed**: The paper discusses precipitate strengthening (γ′ precipitation hardening) and how the relative strengths of the γ matrix and γ′ phase change with temperature, affecting dislocation motion.

# Mechanical Properties

**Low cycle fatigue testing conditions**:
- Test standard: Chinese standard GB/T 15248-2008
- Specimen gauge length: 5.5 mm
- Loading mode: Stress control, stress ratio R = 0.1
- Loading frequency: 5 Hz
- Test temperatures: 420 °C (T420), 510 °C (T510), 600 °C (T600)
- Maximum stress levels: 990 MPa, 1015 MPa, 1186 MPa, 1265 MPa
- At least 7 valid data points per stress level

**Fatigue life (S-N curve fitting)**:
- The double logarithmic Basquin equation (σ = aN_b^f) provides a superior fit to fatigue life data under all three test conditions.
- At high stress levels, fatigue life at T510 is shorter than at T420.
- At low stress levels, fatigue life at T510 is longer than at T420.
- The fitted curves for T420 and T510 intersect.
- At all stress levels, fatigue life at T600 is higher than at T420 and T510.
- Fatigue life increases with increasing temperature, a trend also observed in GH4049.

**Cyclic stress-strain behavior** (at peak stress of 1265 MPa):
- T420 (420 °C): Unambiguous cyclic hardening; stress rises rapidly and stabilizes during initial cycles, indicating strong resistance to plastic deformation.
- T510 (510 °C): Relatively lower stress peaks; slight softening trend with increasing cycles, indicating good cyclic stability.
- T600 (600 °C): Pronounced cyclic softening; peak stress gradually decreases with increasing cycles, indicating significantly enhanced plastic deformation capacity.

**Fracture characteristics**:
- T420: Fracture angle θ = 56° (shear fracture)
- T510 (referred to as 550 °C in one instance, likely a typo; original text states "420 °C and 550 °C" but elsewhere consistently uses T510): Fracture angle θ = 50° (shear fracture)
- T600: Fracture angle θ = 8° (Mode I-type fracture; flat fracture surface)

**Tensile strength, compressive strength, elongation, fracture toughness, creep performance, hardness, elastic modulus, density**: Not reported in this paper.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Precipitation Strengthening (γ′ Precipitation Hardening)

- **Related elements**: Ni, Al, Ti (and possibly Nb)
- **Typical alloys**: FGH95 powder metallurgy nickel-based superalloy
- **Key compositions**: Al: 3.46 wt.%, Ti: 2.56 wt.%, Nb: 3.56 wt.%, Ni: Balance
- **Microstructural changes**: High volume fraction of γ′ precipitates distributed in FCC γ matrix. At low temperature (420 °C), dislocations shear the γ′ precipitates, causing them to deform from square to diamond morphology. At high temperature (600 °C), γ′ phase strength increases relative to γ matrix, causing dislocations to bypass the precipitates via climbing, forming dislocation rings.
- **Property improvements**: The increasing strength of γ′ with temperature is responsible for the observed increase in fatigue life with temperature. At 1265 MPa peak stress, T600 shows highest fatigue life, followed by T510 and T420 at low stress levels.
- **Risks and limitations**: The change in dislocation-precipitate interaction with temperature leads to a change in fracture mode—from shear fracture at low temperatures to Mode I fracture at high temperatures.

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related elements**: Cr, Co, W, Mo (present in the alloy)
- **Typical alloys**: FGH95 powder metallurgy nickel-based superalloy (γ matrix)
- **Key compositions**: Cr: 13.13 wt.%, Co: 8.07 wt.%, W: 3.51 wt.%, Mo: 3.54 wt.%
- **Microstructural changes**: The γ matrix comprises primarily Co and Cr, with W and Mo presumably in solid solution. The paper notes that the γ matrix phase strength decreases at high temperatures while γ′ phase strength increases.
- **Property improvements**: Solid solution strengthening contributes to the overall alloy strength, but specific contributions are not quantified.
- **Risks and limitations**: At high temperatures, the γ matrix phase strength decreases, which affects overall alloy deformation behavior. Specific numerical values not provided.

## Temperature-Dependent Strengthening Mechanism Transition

- **Related elements**: γ′ forming elements (Ni, Al, Ti), γ matrix elements (Cr, Co)
- **Typical alloys**: FGH95, and precipitation-hardened nickel-based superalloys in general
- **Key compositions**: As listed in Table 1
- **Microstructural changes**: At low temperatures, the γ matrix phase is stronger than γ′ phase; dislocations shear both phases. At high temperatures, γ′ is stronger than γ matrix; dislocations climb and bypass γ′ precipitates.
- **Property improvements**: Fatigue life at T600 is consistently higher than at T420 and T510 at all stress levels. Cyclic softening at T600 indicates enhanced plastic deformation capacity.
- **Risks and limitations**: Shear fracture at low temperatures (T420, T510) vs. Mode I fracture at high temperature (T600). The fracture mode transition is primarily controlled by dislocation motion mechanism, not by oxidation or other environmental effects.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Fatigue life-temperature relationship is not strictly inverse**: Under certain loading conditions, fatigue life of FGH95 increases with temperature. At T600 (600 °C), fatigue life is markedly longer than at T420 and T510 at all stress levels tested.

2. **Two distinct fracture modes identified**:
   - At low temperatures (T420, T510): Shear fracture along crystal slip surfaces (fracture angles θ = 56° and 50°, respectively).
   - At high temperature (T600): Mode I-type fracture perpendicular to stress axis (fracture angle θ = 8°).

3. **Micro-mechanism of fracture mode transition**: The change in fracture mode is attributed to the temperature-dependent strength of γ′ precipitates relative to the γ matrix:
   - At low temperatures: γ matrix strength > γ′ strength; dislocations cut through both phases, leaving deformed diamond-shaped γ′ precipitates; cracks initiate along slip zones, leading to shear fracture.
   - At high temperatures: γ′ strength > γ matrix strength; dislocations bypass γ′ precipitates by climbing, forming dislocation rings; crack initiation occurs at phase interfaces, leading to Mode I fracture.

4. **Cyclic behavior evolution**:
   - T420: Cyclic hardening
   - T510: Slight cyclic softening with stable behavior
   - T600: Pronounced cyclic softening

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Alloy system specificity**: This paper focuses exclusively on the FGH95 powder metallurgy nickel-based superalloy. The findings regarding temperature-dependent fracture mode transitions may not directly apply to refractory high-entropy alloys, W-based, Mo-based, or Nb-W-based alloys.

2. **Fatigue testing parameters**: The study used stress-controlled LCF testing with a stress ratio of R = 0.1, loading frequency of 5 Hz, and temperatures of 420, 510, and 600 °C. Results may differ under strain-controlled conditions, different stress ratios, or higher temperatures.

3. **Microstructural limits**: The paper does not provide grain size data, precipitate size distribution, or detailed carbide characterization. These microstructural factors may influence fatigue behavior but are not quantified.

4. **Mechanical property data**: The paper does not report conventional mechanical properties such as tensile strength, yield strength, elongation, or hardness. Only fatigue life data and cyclic stress-strain behavior are presented.

5. **Temperature range**: The study is limited to 420-600 °C. The behavior at higher temperatures (>600 °C) or lower temperatures is not addressed.

6. **Oxidation effects**: While oxidation is cited as affecting fatigue life in other studies, the specific oxidation behavior of FGH95 at the test temperatures is not characterized in this paper.

7. **Unmelted Zr particles**: The paper notes the presence of unmelted Zr particles from the powder metallurgy process but does not investigate their effect on fatigue crack initiation or fatigue life. This remains an open question.

8. **Refractory alloy relevance**: This paper is not directly about refractory high-temperature alloys, Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, or refractory high-entropy alloys. It is a nickel-based superalloy study. The knowledge extracted is primarily relevant as a reference for understanding temperature-dependent strengthening mechanisms in precipitation-hardened alloys, but direct applicability to refractory alloy systems would require careful consideration of phase constitution differences.
