# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Vacuum brazing of Al0.3CoCrFeNi high-entropy alloys with CoCrFeNiNb0.5 eutectic filler metal for wide-temperature range application
- **Authors**: Y. Lei, Y.N. Li, Y.C. Cai, X.Y. Wang, S.P. Hu, X.G. Song, Z.B. Chen, H.C. Shi
- **Journal**: Vacuum
- **Year**: 2024
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.vacuum.2024.113670
- **Alloy system studied**: Al0.3CoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy (base metal) brazed with CoCrFeNiNb0.5 eutectic high-entropy alloy (filler metal)
- **Research objective**: To develop a multicomponent CoCrFeNiNb0.5 eutectic high-entropy alloy filler metal for vacuum brazing of Al0.3CoCrFeNi HEAs, aiming to achieve excellent metallurgical compatibility and mechanical properties over a wide temperature range (cryogenic to elevated temperature)
- **Material category**: High-entropy alloys (single-phase FCC-structured Al0.3CoCrFeNi base metal; eutectic high-entropy alloy CoCrFeNiNb0.5 filler metal with FCC + C14 Laves lamellar structure). Not a refractory HEA or Nb-W/Mo-W-based alloy system.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Base metal (BM)**: Al0.3CoCrFeNi single-phase FCC high-entropy alloy
- **Filler metal (FM)**: CoCrFeNiNb0.5 eutectic high-entropy alloy (nominal composition CoCrFeNiNb0.5, approximately 10 at.% Nb at eutectic point)
- **Joint structure**: Al0.3CoCrFeNi / FCC[Nb]ss diffusion layer / Laves + FCC eutectic + Nb(s,s) / FCC[Nb]ss diffusion layer / Al0.3CoCrFeNi
- **Material category**: High-entropy alloys; brazed joint

# Alloy Composition

## Base Metal: Al0.3CoCrFeNi
- Elements: Al, Co, Cr, Fe, Ni
- Al content: 0.3 (molar ratio, i.e., Al0.3CoCrFeNi)
- Exact at.% values: Not explicitly tabulated in the original paper. The paper states it is single-phase FCC structured.
- Impurities (O, N, H): Not explicitly stated for BM alone; joint contamination levels are discussed (see below).

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Filler Metal Preparation
- **Method**: Arc-melting high-purity Co, Cr, Fe, Ni, and Nb particles (>99.6 wt%)
- **Post-processing**: Wire-electrode cutting into ~300 μm-thick alloy pieces; hand-polished to 120 μm-thick slices (brazing foils)

## Brazing Process
- **Brazing method**: Vacuum brazing
- **Vacuum level**: 3 × 10⁻³ Pa
- **Brazing temperature**: 1280 °C
- **Heating rate**: 10 °C/min
- **Holding time**: 10 min
- **Cooling**: Furnace cooling to room temperature
- **Base metal dimensions**: 12 mm × 5 mm × 2.5 mm
- **Assembly configuration**: FM sandwiched between two Al0.3CoCrFeNi substrates

## Pre-brazing Surface Preparation
- BM polished with SiC papers
- Ultrasonic cleaning in acetone bath for 20 min

## DSC Characterization of FM
- **Melting temperatures**: TS = 1232 °C, TL = 1253 °C
- **Melting range (TL − TS)**: 21 °C
- **Heating rate**: 10 °C/min

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Filler Metal (as-cast, before brazing)
- **Structure**: Fine lamellar eutectic with average lamellae thickness ((λFCC + λLaves)/2) ≈ 502 nm, plus a few brighter dendrites
- **Phases**: C14 Laves phase (lamella/dendrite) + FCC matrix
- **Dendritic Laves formation indicates actual eutectic point of CoCrFeNi-Nb system may be lower than 10 at.% Nb**

## Brazed Joint (after brazing at 1280 °C/10 min)
- **Braze zone width**: ~129 ± 3 μm (only 9 μm wider than original joint clearance)
- **Joint structure**: Al0.3CoCrFeNi/FCC[Nb]ss diffusion layer/Laves + FCC eutectic + Nb(s,s)/FCC[Nb]ss diffusion layer/Al0.3CoCrFeNi
- **Diffusion zones**: Two ~37 ± 4 μm-thick diffusion zones formed due to Nb diffusion from molten FM to Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM
- **Eutectic morphology**: Chrysanthemum-like eutectic colonies; FCC/Laves lamellar structure
- **Phase A**: C14 Fe2Nb-type Laves phase (hexagonal crystal structure, confirmed by SAED from [010] zone axis)
- **Phase B**: FCC-structured solid solution (consistent with Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM)
- **Phase C**: Nb(s,s) at grain boundaries of eutectic colonies and near-intergranular region of Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM (contains 64.54 at.% Nb)
- **Phase D**: FCC phase in diffusion zone (contains 1.73 at.% Nb)
- **Lattice mismatch (δ) between Laves and FCC**: ~4.44% (angle θ between (101) Laves and (111) FCC = ~14.1°)
- **Interfacial dislocations**: High density of edge dislocations at Laves/FCC phase interface observed via HRTEM/IFFT; interfacial dislocation networks alleviate long-range internal stresses caused by lattice mismatch
- **Element segregation**: 
  - Laves phase richer in Nb (19.11 at.%) and depleted in Cr, Fe, Co relative to FCC matrix
  - Slight increase in Ni content in Laves phase; slight drop in Cr, Fe, and Co
  - The segregation is attributed to enthalpy of mixing (ΔHmix) differences between Nb and other alloying elements
- **Grain boundaries**: Minor white Nb(s,s) precipitates at GBs of eutectic colonies; no evident O segregation at grain boundaries or lamellar structure
- **Chemical compatibility**: Co, Cr, Fe, Ni concentrations in FCC matrix are close to those in Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM without notable concentration gradients

## Strengthening Mechanisms Discussed
- **Solid-solution strengthening**: Nb atoms diffusing into Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM form FCC[Nb]ss diffusion layer, providing solid-solution strengthening
- **Second-phase strengthening**: C14 Laves phase embedded in soft FCC matrix acts as strengthening agent; rule-of-mixtures composite behavior
- **Interfacial strengthening**: Low-energy FCC/Laves phase boundary (δ ≈ 4.44%) improves bonding strength
- **Dislocation strengthening**: Edge dislocation networks at Laves/FCC interface; cryogenic mechanical nano-twinning in FCC phases

# Mechanical Properties

## Shear Test Conditions
- **Sample bonding area**: 5 mm × 5 mm
- **Displacement speed**: 0.5 mm/min
- **Testing machine**: Instron 5967 (universal testing machine), with electric heater for high-temperature tests
- **Cryogenic test method**: Sample immersed in liquid nitrogen (−196 °C)
- **Atmosphere**: Not explicitly stated; room temperature and 700 °C tests presumably in air (not explicitly stated)
- **Number of specimens**: More than three per condition

## Shear Properties of Brazed Joints

| Testing Temperature | Shear Strength (MPa) | Shear Displacement (mm) | Fracture Location | Fracture Mode |
|---------------------|----------------------|------------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Room temperature (RT) | 461 | 1.35 | Nb diffusion zone adjacent to Laves/FCC eutectic | Multiple secondary cracks perpendicular to shear direction |
| −196 °C (liquid nitrogen) | 741 | 1.42 | Laves/FCC eutectic zone | Brittle quasi-cleavage fracture |
| 700 °C | 380 | 1.62 | Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM | Ductile fracture |

## Comparison Values (from references cited in the paper)
- **RT shear strength of Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM**: 461 MPa = 74% of BM strength
- **CoCrFeMnNi/CoCrFeMnNi joint brazed with 70Au-8Pd-22Ni**: 173 MPa (RT shear strength)
- **CoCrFeMnNi joint brazed with BNi-2**: 319 MPa
- **CoCrFeMnNi joint brazed with MBF601**: 321 MPa
- **Compressive stress of CoCrFeNiNb0.5 EHEAs**: 2321 MPa (from cited reference [26])
- **Maximum tensile strength of Al0.3CoCrFeNi HEAs**: ~850 MPa (from cited reference [45])

## Load Mode
- Shear test only; no tensile, compressive, creep, or fracture toughness data for the brazed joint are reported in this paper.

## Room-Temperature Shear Performance Analysis
- Crack nucleation: At Laves/FCC interfaces adjacent to Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM due to deformation ability difference
- Crack propagation: In highly deformed Nb diffusion zone

## Cryogenic (−196 °C) Performance Analysis
- Strength and displacement both improved relative to RT
- Mechanism: Reduced thermal energy increases Peierls-Nabarro barriers → harder dislocation movement → higher yield strength of FCC matrix, Al0.3CoCrFeNi, and Laves phase
- Additional mechanism: Mechanical nano-twinning activated in FCC matrix, FCC[Nb]ss diffusion zone, and Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM due to lower stacking fault energy at cryogenic temperature → sustaining strain-hardening effect → synergistic strength-ductility improvement
- Brittle Laves phase at −196 °C has inferior deformability → cracks initiate at Laves/FCC interface and propagate rapidly through brittle Laves phase

## 700 °C Performance Analysis
- FCC matrix, FCC[Nb]ss diffusion zone, and Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM soften
- Lamellar C14 Laves phase maintains high strength at 700 °C
- Second-phase hardening from C14 Laves phase accommodates plastic strain
- Joint ruptures in Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM (ductile fracture) because braze zone deformation strength is higher than BM strength

## Hardness, Elastic Modulus, Density, Fracture Toughness

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Niobium (Nb)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
CoCrFeNiNbₓ eutectic high-entropy alloys; Al0.3CoCrFeNi brazed joints (via CoCrFeNiNb0.5 filler metal)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
- Filler metal nominal: CoCrFeNiNb0.5 (~9.1 at.% Nb; eutectic point at ~10 at.% Nb)
- Laves phase: 19.11 at.% Nb
- FCC matrix: 1.08 at.% Nb
- Nb(s,s) precipitate: 64.54 at.% Nb
- Nb solid solution in Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM diffusion zone: ~1.36–1.73 at.%

**[Main Function]**
- Promotes Laves phase formation and eutectic reaction in CoCrFeNi-Nb system
- Lowers melting temperature of CoCrFeNi alloy from 1588 °C to 1253 °C at 0–10 at.% Nb
- Provides solid-solution strengthening in Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM via Nb diffusion
- Enables formation of FCC/Laves lamellar eutectic structure with superior mechanical properties

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Large atomic-sized Nb has more negative enthalpy of mixing (ΔHmix) with Co, Cr, Fe, Ni → destabilizes FCC phase → forms C14 Fe2Nb-type Laves phase
- Laves phase formation criterion: atomic size difference δ >3% and Allen electronegativity ΔxA >5.5%
- Nb diffusion into Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM creates FCC[Nb]ss diffusion layer with solid-solution strengthening
- During cooling, extra Nb atoms precipitate as Nb(s,s) at grain boundaries of eutectic colonies and near-intergranular region of Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM (decreased solid solubility)
- Segregation of Laves phase causes minor increase in Ni and slight drop in Cr, Fe, Co due to ΔHmix differences

**[Property Effect]**
- RT shear strength of joint: 461 MPa (74% of Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM)
- −196 °C shear strength: 741 MPa / displacement 1.42 mm
- 700 °C shear strength: 380 MPa / displacement 1.62 mm
- Braze zone strength estimated to be several times higher than Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM due to Laves phase strengthening (compressive strength of CoCrFeNiNb0.5 EHEAs = 2321 MPa vs. Al0.3CoCrFeNi max tensile strength ≈ 850 MPa; cited values)
- Lattice mismatch between Laves and FCC: δ ≈ 4.44% → low-energy phase boundary

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Excessive Nb content forms brittle Nb(s,s) at grain boundaries (although volume fraction in this joint is low, its influence on shear strength is negligible according to the paper)
- Brittle Laves phase has inferior deformability at cryogenic temperatures, causing quasi-cleavage fracture in eutectic zone at −196 °C
- Nb content reduction below 0.5 in CoCrFeNiNbₓ FM can reduce Laves volume fraction, increase lamellar spacing, and improve joint comprehensive properties (paper speculation, not tested in this work)
- Large deformation ability difference between braze zone (high strength) and Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM → crack nucleation at Laves/FCC interfaces

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening (Nb)

**Related Elements**: Nb

**Typical Alloys**: Al0.3CoCrFeNi joint brazed with CoCrFeNiNb0.5 FM; FCC[Nb]ss diffusion zone

**Key Compositions**: ~1.36–1.73 at.% Nb in Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM diffusion zone

**Microstructural Changes**: Nb atoms diffuse into Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM, forming ~37 ± 4 μm-thick FCC[Nb]ss diffusion layers on both sides of braze zone

**Property Improvements**: Diffusion zone formation is "favorable for joint strength via solid solution strengthening of Nb atoms" (Section 3.2). Specific numerical contribution not isolated from overall joint strength.

**Risks and Limitations**: Nb concentration gradient and deformation ability difference between braze zone and BM cause crack nucleation at Laves/FCC interfaces near BM during RT shear.

**Original Evidence**: Fig. 2(b), Section 3.2

## Second-Phase Strengthening (C14 Laves Phase)

**Related Elements**: Nb (primary Laves-forming element), Co, Cr, Fe, Ni (matrix-forming elements)

**Typical Alloys**: CoCrFeNiNb0.5 EHEA braze zone; Al0.3CoCrFeNi/CoCrFeNiNb0.5 brazed joint

**Key Compositions**: 19.11 at.% Nb in Laves phase; Laves/FCC lamellar thickness ~502 nm (in as-cast FM); eutectic colonies in braze zone ~129 μm wide

**Microstructural Changes**: FCC/Laves lamellar eutectic structure; C14 Fe2Nb-type Laves phase embedded in FCC matrix; low-energy phase boundary δ ≈ 4.44%; high density of edge dislocations at interfaces

**Property Improvements**:
- Braze zone strength higher than Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM (rule-of-mixtures, Eq. (4))
- Joint at RT: 461 MPa shear strength
- Joint at −196 °C: 741 MPa
- Joint at 700 °C: 380 MPa, with ductile fracture in BM (braze zone maintains higher strength than BM)

**Risks and Limitations**:
- Brittle Laves phase causes quasi-cleavage fracture in eutectic zone at −196 °C
- Large deformation ability difference between braze zone and BM leads to RT crack nucleation at Laves/FCC interfaces
- Laves phase shape and lamellar spacing need tailoring (reducing Nb content or post-weld heat treatment above 750 °C for spheroidization) to further optimize joint properties

**Original Evidence**: Table 1, Figs. 2, 4, 5; Section 4.2

## Cryogenic Strengthening (Mechanical Nano-Twinning)

**Related Elements**: None specific to a single element; related to FCC crystal structure of CoCrFeNi-based HEAs

**Typical Alloys**: FCC matrix in braze zone, FCC[Nb]ss diffusion zone, Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM

**Key Compositions**: FCC phases with similar chemical composition (Co ~23–25 at.%, Cr ~16.5–20 at.%, Fe ~16.5–21 at.%, Ni ~18.5–29 at.%, Al 2–4.5 at.%, Nb 0–2 at.%)

**Microstructural Changes**: At −196 °C, deformation mode transitions from planar-slip dislocations to mechanical nano-twinning due to lower stacking fault energy

**Property Improvements**:
- Joint shear strength increases from 461 MPa (RT) to 741 MPa (−196 °C)
- Shear displacement increases from 1.35 mm (RT) to 1.42 mm (−196 °C)
- Strength-ductility synergistic improvement attributed to sustaining strain-hardening from dislocation glide + nano-twinning

**Risks and Limitations**: Brittle Laves phase at −196 °C limits deformability; cracks propagate rapidly through Laves phase, causing joint failure in eutectic zone rather than BM

**Original Evidence**: Fig. 5(a), Section 4.2

# Key Conclusions

1. **Successful brazing**: Al0.3CoCrFeNi HEAs were successfully vacuum-brazed to itself using a self-prepared CoCrFeNiNb0.5 eutectic high-entropy alloy filler metal at 1280 °C for 10 min in vacuum of 3 × 10⁻³ Pa.

2. **Interfacial microstructure**: Joint structure is Al0.3CoCrFeNi/FCC[Nb]ss diffusion layer/Laves + FCC eutectic + Nb(s,s)/FCC[Nb]ss diffusion layer/Al0.3CoCrFeNi. Braze zone mainly consists of FCC + C14 Laves lamellar eutectic structure with superior metallurgical compatibility with Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM.

3. **Low-energy interface**: Lattice mismatch degree (δ) between Laves and FCC phases is ~4.44%, indicating a low-energy phase boundary.

4. **Shear properties**:
   - RT: 461 MPa / 1.35 mm shear displacement (74% of BM strength)
   - −196 °C: 741 MPa / 1.42 mm (improved due to mechanical nano-twinning and sustained strain-hardening)
   - 700 °C: 380 MPa / 1.62 mm (second-phase hardening from C14 Laves phase; joint fractures in BM with ductile mode)

5. **Strategy and potential**: Developing lower-melting-point eutectic HEAs filler metals (CoCrFeNiNb0.5 has TL = 1253 °C, narrow melting range of 21 °C) is an effective approach for high-quality brazing between HEAs, supporting wide-temperature range applications from −196 °C to 700 °C.

6. **Tunability**: Joint properties can be tailored by adjusting Nb content in the filler metal (reducing Nb to <0.5 reduces Laves volume fraction, increases lamellar spacing) or by post-weld heat treatment above 750 °C to spheroidize lamellar C14 Laves phase.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Not a refractory alloy paper**: This paper studies Al0.3CoCrFeNi and CoCrFeNiNb0.5 high-entropy alloys. It does not contain W, Mo, Ta, Zr, Hf, Ti, V, Re, or refractory HEAs.

2. **No tensile/compressive/creep/fracture toughness data**: Only shear strength and shear displacement are reported for the brazed joint. Mechanical data for base metal and filler metal are cited from other references (e.g., Al0.3CoCrFeNi tensile strength ≈ 850 MPa; CoCrFeNiNb0.5 compressive stress = 2321 MPa).

3. **No oxidation resistance data**: Oxidation weight gain, oxide layer constitution, and service stability under oxidative atmosphere are not measured or discussed. The statement about C14 Laves phase having high oxidation resistance is based on cited work, not original measurements.

4. **No hardness or density data**: Hardness, elastic modulus, and density of the joint or constituent phases are not reported.

5. **Post-weld heat treatment not experimentally validated**: The suggestion to reduce Nb content or apply post-weld heat treatment above 750 °C for Laves phase spheroidization is stated as speculation based on referenced literature [25]; it is noted as "currently under investigation in our research team."

6. **Brazing temperature relation to BM**: Brazing temperature of 1280 °C is 27 °C above FM melting temperature TL (1253 °C) and ~253 °C lower than Al0.3CoCrFeNi BM melting point; applicability to other BM systems not verified.

7. **Size limitation**: Joint strength and ductility depend on Laves phase shape and lamellar spacing; these are not independently varied in this study.

8. **Fracture mechanics analysis**: The paper presents fracture morphology analysis but no quantitative fracture toughness or crack propagation rate data.

9. **Specific temperature points**: Mechanical tests were conducted at only three temperatures: RT, −196 °C, and 700 °C. Intermediate temperatures (e.g., 300–600 °C) were not tested.
