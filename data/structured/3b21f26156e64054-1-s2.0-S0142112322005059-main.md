# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Fatigue life evaluation for notched single-crystal Ni-based superalloys considering inhomogeneous rafting microstructure
- **Authors:** Y.S. Fan, X.G. Yang, L. Tan, T.X. Sui, D.Q. Shi, H. Liu
- **Journal:** International Journal of Fatigue
- **Year:** 2023 (Volume 166, Article 107255)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2022.107255
- **Alloy system studied:** Single-crystal Ni-based superalloy (second-generation SC Ni-based superalloy)
- **Research objective:** To establish a strain energy density-based low cycle fatigue (LCF) life prediction model for notched single-crystal Ni-based superalloys that accounts for inhomogeneous γ'/γ rafting microstructure, and to evaluate the predicted lifetime uncertainty induced by microstructural statistical dispersion.
- **Material category:** Ni-based single-crystal superalloy

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper investigates a second-generation single-crystal Ni-based superalloy used for turbine blades and vanes in aero engines. The material contains a high volume fraction of L1₂-type γ' precipitates embedded in a γ matrix. The as-received alloy has a γ' volume fraction of 70%, with a mean γ' precipitate width of 480 nm and a mean γ matrix channel width of approximately 55 nm. The research focuses on center-hole specimens (Ø1 mm central hole) with gauge section dimensions of 2.5 mm × 6 mm × 15 mm, loaded parallel to the [001] crystal orientation (orientation deviation <7°).

A note on classification: This is a Ni-based superalloy, not a refractory high-temperature alloy (Nb-W, Mo-W, refractory high-entropy alloy, or additively manufactured refractory alloy). The extraction is performed because it is the paper in question; however, for retrieval systems targeting refractory alloys, this paper would be tagged as an out-of-domain or adjacent-knowledge reference.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

The as-received superalloy underwent a two-stage solution heat treatment:
- **First stage (solution):** 1290 °C for 1 h, then 1300 °C for 2 h, then 1315 °C for 4 h
- **Second stage (solution):** 1120 °C for 4 h
- **Aging treatment:** 870 °C for 32 h
- **Cooling:** All heat treatments were followed by air cooling.

After heat treatment, a central hole of Ø1 mm was fabricated by electrical discharge machining (EDM) on dog-bone-shaped specimens.

**Rafting treatments (pre-LCF microstructural degradation):**
Rafting treatments were conducted on a CTM304-A1 creep tester under five conditions:
1. 1000 °C / 30 h / 70 MPa
2. 1050 °C / 30 h / 50 MPa
3. 1050 °C / 50 h / 70 MPa
4. 1050 °C / 60 h / 100 MPa
5. 1050 °C / 100 h / 100 MPa

The notation "#°C/## h/### MPa" means the specimen was held for ## h under a given applied stress of ### MPa at # °C.

The paper does not report arc melting, vacuum melting, induction melting, powder metallurgy, mechanical alloying, hot pressing, spark plasma sintering, hot isostatic pressing, or additive manufacturing. Rolling and forging are not explicitly stated in the original paper.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase:** γ matrix (FCC Ni-based solid solution).
- **Precipitate phase:** L1₂-type γ' precipitates (Ni₃Al-based intermetallic).
- **Initial γ' volume fraction:** 70%
- **Mean γ' precipitate width (initial):** 480 nm
- **Mean γ matrix channel width (initial):** ~55 nm
- **Rafting morphology:** Under high-temperature tensile stress, N-type rafting occurs (γ' rafts align normal to the tensile stress axis), forming plate-like γ'/γ layered structures. P-type rafting occurs under compressive stress.
- **Microstructural degradation modes:** Stress-assisted rafting (directional coalescence) and stress-free isotropic coarsening (Ostwald ripening).
- **Grain boundary features:** Not explicitly discussed because the material is single-crystal (no grain boundaries). The paper focuses on γ'/γ interfaces.
- **Elemental segregation:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper. Not discussed in the context of carbide or oxide precipitates.
- **Carbides, oxides, borides, silicides:** Not explicitly discussed. The paper focuses on γ'/γ rafting rather than precipitation of secondary phases.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed in the paper:**
  - The paper does **not** discuss solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, dispersion strengthening, or ODS strengthening in the conventional sense.
  - The paper discusses the **Orowan mechanism** in the context of dislocation motion in γ channels (Table 2, equation T4: τOr = αμb/ω₀(1-ξ)).
  - The main microstructural mechanisms discussed are dislocation motion in the γ channel, dislocation wall formation (back stress), and the effect of γ channel widening on fatigue behavior.

- Widening of the γ channel promotes crack propagation along the γ channel.
- Plate-like γ'/γ morphology reduces resistance to crack opening.

# Key Conclusions

1. A modified strain energy density (MSED) is proposed that accounts for rafting by introducing a rafting state parameter ξ. The plastic part of MSED is determined by a microstructure-sensitive constitutive model, and the elastic part is modified by a (1+ξ) factor.

2. The proposed life model integrates three key components: strain energy gradient, effective damage zone size (L_eff), and weight function φ(ΔW^d_t, L).

3. The predicted fatigue life of center-hole specimens with different rafting treatments falls within ±2 scatter bands when both the effect of rafting on plastic deformation and the modification of positive elastic strain energy density are considered (Fig. 13c).

4. Without considering rafting, the predicted lifetime can be overestimated by up to ~9 times for heavily rafted conditions (e.g., 1050 °C/100 h/100 MPa).

5. Microstructural rafting near the central hole is most severe at the hole root and least severe at the top (Fig. 9). At initial rafting stages, spatial gradients are notable; with prolonged high-temperature exposure, stress-independent coarsening dominates and the microstructure tends to become homogeneous.

6. The uncertainty of predicted lifetimes decreases with rafting extent and fatigue life. This occurs because:
   - Heavily rafted specimens have less sensitivity of predicted error (P_e) to γ channel width (ω) variation.
   - At longer lifetimes, the proportion of plastic dissipation in MSED decreases, weakening the dependence of predictions on microstructural statistical characteristics.

7. The 5%–95% confidence interval of predicted lifetimes mostly falls within ±2 scatter bands, indicating good agreement with experimental data (Fig. 17).

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Material scope:** The paper specifically investigates a second-generation Ni-based single-crystal superalloy. Results and model parameters are composition-specific and may not be directly transferable to other Ni-based superalloys or refractory alloys without recalibration.

2. **Temperature range:** The rafting model and constitutive model are calibrated for the range of 980–1100 °C (rafting treatments at 1000 °C and 1050 °C, LCF tests at 980 °C). Applicability outside this temperature range is not established.

3. **Stress state:** The rafting model is based on the triaxial stress state parameter tr(σ)/(3σ_s). The model ignores the effect of inelastic deformation on rafting behavior (stated explicitly in Section 3.1).

4. **Fatigue loading:** LCF tests were performed at R = 0.1 with a frequency of 1 Hz. The model is not validated for other stress ratios, strain ratios, or loading frequencies.

5. **Specimen geometry:** The model is validated only for center-hole specimens with Ø1 mm hole and specific gauge section dimensions. The effective damage zone size L_eff = α·(N_f)^β was fitted from center-hole specimens; its transferability to other notch geometries is not confirmed.

6. **Microstructural uncertainty:** Uncertainty quantification is based on γ channel width dispersion alone. Other microstructural variabilities (e.g., γ' size, γ' volume fraction) are not included in the uncertainty assessment.

7. **Oxidation:** The paper does not consider oxidation during LCF testing or rafting treatments. The atmosphere during LCF testing is not specified.

8. **Mechanical properties characterization:** Direct tensile, compressive, hardness, and creep data are not provided. Only fatigue life and cyclic stress–strain responses are reported.

9. **Atmospheric conditions:** Not explicitly stated for LCF tests or the long-term rafting treatments. The influence of the environment is excluded from the analysis, although the paper mentions polishing to remove oxidation layers before LCF testing.
