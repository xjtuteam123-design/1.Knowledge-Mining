# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Additive Manufacturing Method
- **Process**: Electron Beam Powder Bed Fusion (EB-PBF) / Electron Beam Melting (EBM)
- **Machine**: Arcam Q10+ electron beam melting system
- **Chamber Vacuum**: 10⁻³ mbar, maintained through controlled bleed of high-purity helium
- **Build Substrate**: Ti-6Al-4V (Ti64) bar stock, 65 mm diameter, 40 mm thickness
- **Preheat Temperature**: Estimated between 1100 °C and 1500 °C based on two-color pyrometry measurements taken intermittently; intentionally varied between builds (higher vs. lower preheat energies tested)
- **Preheat Energy**: Varied from 735 J/mm² (higher preheat, longer duration and higher current) to 325 J/mm² (lower preheat, shorter duration and lower current)

## Melt Parameters
- **Beam Current**: 21 mA for all samples
- **Layer Thickness**: 70 μm (all builds)
- **Hatch Spacing**: 32.5 μm (all builds)
- **Beam Speed**: 
  - Build 1: 324–588 mm/s
  - Build 2: 456–720 mm/s
  - Build 3: identical parameters for all parts (specific speed not stated, but derived from Build 2 Part 3's parameters: largest spot size and slowest beam speed)
- **Focus Offset (FO)**: 
  - Build 1: 20–40 mA
  - Build 2: larger spot sizes (specific values in mA not stated for Builds 2 and 3, but increased relative to Build 1)
- **Speed Function (SF)**: Varied from 3 to 9
- **Scan Pattern**: Raster scan pattern for all builds
- **Beam Energy Density**: 750–2000 J/mm³ (approximately 100 times the theoretical volumetric energy required to heat and melt tungsten)
- **Ramp-up Strategy**: Beam energy density increased gradually during first several millimeters of each build by gradually decreasing beam speed from a speed suitable for the start plate material (Ti64) to the final beam speed for the tungsten part

## Post-Processing
No heat treatment, hot isostatic pressing, or mechanical post-processing was applied. Samples were characterized in the as-built condition.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Phase Constitution
- **Matrix Phase**: Not explicitly stated as BCC in this paper, but tungsten is intrinsically BCC. The paper discusses slip mechanisms in BCC metals.
- **Second Phases / Precipitates**: 
  - Tungsten oxides (WO₃) can form at grain boundaries due to oxygen segregation; WO₃ is stable up to 2000 °C at an oxygen partial pressure of 10⁻⁷ bar (the approximate oxygen level in the experiments)
  - No intentional carbides, borides, or ODS particles
- **Grain Structure**: Columnar grain boundaries are implied (cracks propagate along columnar grain boundaries)
- **Grain Size**: Not explicitly quantified in the original paper
- **Elemental Segregation**: Oxygen segregates to grain boundaries where it forms tungsten oxides, reducing grain boundary strength
- **Grain Boundary Features**: Grain boundaries are susceptible to oxygen embrittlement; cracks propagate preferentially along columnar grain boundaries

## Microstructural Observations
- **Support Structure**: A porous support region forms between the Ti64 base plate and the dense W region of interest (height varies from ~1.5 mm in Build 3 to ~7.5 mm in Build 2); chimney pores form in the support region and propagate upward
- **Dense Regions**: Build 2, Part 3 shows a large region of essentially full density at the top of the sample, atop a porous support structure
- **Crack Morphology**: 
  - Cracks are vertical (along build/Z direction), localized in XY plane
  - EBSD shows localized orientation gradients at crack edges, indicating plastic deformation preceding cracking (i.e., solid-state cracking)
  - Strings of pores along the build direction can transition into large cracks
  - Concentrated heavily cracked areas surrounded by mostly uncracked material suggest relatively rare crack nucleation event with easy propagation along Z direction and minimal in-plane propagation

## Defect Analysis
- **In-situ NIR Analysis**: Near-infrared imaging after melting and after raking of each layer used to identify pores and create 3D visualization; pixel resolution ≈ 40 μm
- **Detection Algorithm**: Four-step image processing flow: (1) image correction and NIR image mapping; (2) correction of temporally dependent emissivity; (3) detection of anomalies using spatial statistical analytics; (4) measurement of morphological properties such as center of mass, volume, orientation, eccentricity

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxidation Weight Gain**: Not quantified in this paper.
- **Oxidation Layer Type**: WO₃ is discussed; tungsten exposed to oxygen at room temperature develops a ~10 Å layer of WO₃ after just one hour (per Warren et al.).
- **Oxidation Temperature and Duration**: 
  - WO₃ is stable up to 2000 °C at an oxygen partial pressure of 10⁻⁷ bar (approximate oxygen level in EB-PBF chamber)
  - During the preheat cycle (temperatures below 2000 °C), the tungsten build is oxidizing
  - During melting (temperatures above 2000 °C), the tungsten build is reducing
- **Effects of Oxygen on Cracking**:
  - Oxygen reacts readily with tungsten at elevated temperatures
  - Oxygen has very low solubility in solid tungsten
  - Dissolved oxygen segregates to grain boundaries upon cooling, forming tungsten oxides that reduce grain boundary strength and promote intergranular cracking
  - Braun et al. (LPBF) showed that reducing oxygen in the build environment from 800 ppm to 20 ppm significantly reduced but did not eliminate cracking
  - Wang et al. proposed that excess oxygen beyond solubility limit forms nanopores at grain boundaries, which then nucleate cracks
- **Powder Oxygen Pickup**:
  - Virgin powder: < 250 ppm O
  - A 10 Å WO₃ layer on 90 μm diameter powder contributes only ~18 ppm additional oxygen (negligible)
  - For smaller powders: 45 μm powder → +35 ppm; 15 μm powder → +106 ppm; 5 μm powder → +318 ppm
  - Non-spherical powders have larger surface-area-to-volume ratio, increasing oxygen content further
  - EB-PBF typically uses larger-diameter powder, which may be advantageous for oxygen-sensitive materials

## High-Temperature Stability Issues
- **High-Temperature Embrittlement**: Oxygen embrittlement and grain-boundary oxidation are the primary concerns; tungsten oxides weaken grain boundaries
- **Oxygen Embrittlement**: Oxygen segregates to grain boundaries, forms brittle oxides, and promotes intergranular fracture
- **Nitrogen Embrittlement**: Not discussed in this paper.
- **Grain-Boundary Oxidation**: Discussed extensively; WO₃ forms at grain boundaries and reduces grain boundary strength

## [W] (Tungsten Base Metal)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Pure tungsten processed via electron beam powder bed fusion (EB-PBF)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
99.9% W, with < 250 ppm O (virgin powder)

**[Main Function]**
Base metal for plasma-facing components in fusion reactors; selected for high-temperature service, high sputtering threshold, low tritium retention, and good thermal conductivity

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- BCC crystal structure
- Thermally activated kink-pair generation in screw dislocations controls slip; at low temperature, slip occurs on a slip system with high Peierls stress, leading to brittle fracture before yielding
- At higher temperatures, slip systems with lower Peierls stresses but higher kink-pair activation energies become active, enabling plastic deformation

**[Property Effect]**
- DBTT: 0–600 °C depending on purity and processing history
- Extreme high-temperature service capability (fusion divertor application)
- High thermal conductivity (required for rapid heat removal from plasma-facing surface)
- The original paper does not provide specific mechanical property values for the EB-PBF processed tungsten.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- High DBTT (0–600 °C) makes room-temperature fabrication and handling difficult
- Susceptible to intragranular cracking
- Difficulty of joining to structural materials
- Oxygen embrittlement at grain boundaries
- Recrystallization and grain growth in heat-affected zones reduce ductility by creating long, straight crack paths along grain boundaries embrittled by re-segregated oxides

**[Original Evidence]**
Section 1 (Introduction): "tungsten is a notoriously difficult metal to fabricate, due to its high ductile-to-brittle transition temperature (DBTT, ≈ 0–600 °C [3]), susceptibility to intragranular cracking [4], and the difficulty of joining tungsten parts to other structural materials [5]."

## [O] (Oxygen)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Pure tungsten, additively manufactured by EB-PBF or LPBF

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
- Virgin powder: < 250 ppm O (manufacturer specification)
- Build chamber oxygen partial pressure: approximately 10⁻⁷ bar
- Braun et al. (LPBF): varied environment from 800 ppm to 20 ppm O

**[Main Function]**
Detrimental impurity; not an intentional alloying addition

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Oxygen reacts readily with tungsten at elevated temperatures
- Oxygen has very low solubility in solid tungsten
- Upon cooling, oxygen dissolved in the melt pool segregates to grain boundaries, forming tungsten oxides (e.g., WO₃)
- Oxides reduce grain boundary strength, leaving intergranular fracture as the dominant failure mode
- Excess oxygen beyond the solubility limit can form nanopores at grain boundaries, which nucleate cracks
- Powder surface oxidation: tungsten exposed to oxygen at room temperature develops a ~10 Å WO₃ layer after just one hour

**[Property Effect]**
- Promotes intergranular cracking
- Increases DBTT (with higher oxygen content)
- Reduces grain boundary cohesion
- Braun et al. found that decreasing O₂ in the build chamber from 800 ppm to 20 ppm significantly reduced but did not eliminate cracking in SLM tungsten
- WB-PBF at high vacuum with large-diameter powder (90 μm) limits oxygen pickup: ~18 ppm additional oxygen from a 10 Å WO₃ layer

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Oxygen embrittlement
- Intergranular crack initiation and propagation
- Nanopore formation at grain boundaries
- For finer powders (5 μm), a 10 Å WO₃ layer can contribute 318 ppm additional oxygen, significantly increasing crack susceptibility

**[Original Evidence]**
Section 4.2 (Cracking): "Oxygen is a particular problem: oxygen reacts readily with tungsten at high temperatures, but has very low solubility in solid tungsten. This means that oxygen dissolved in the melt pool at high temperatures will tend to segregate to grain boundaries, where it can form various species of tungsten oxides. These oxides then reduce grain boundary strength, leaving intergranular fracture as the dominant failure mode."

# Key Conclusions

1. **Process Feasibility**: Pure tungsten plasma-atomized powder (99.9% W, 90 μm diameter, < 250 ppm O) was successfully processed via EB-PBF using an Arcam Q10+ system, producing dense and microcrack-free regions (notably Build 2, Part 3).

2. **In-situ Defect Detection**: The first application of in-situ near-infrared (NIR) layer images for 3D build reconstruction and defect detection in refractory metals was demonstrated. Spatial statistical analytics were used to detect anomalies (pores) with morphological classification.

3. **Preheat Temperature Role**: Ambient temperature during build significantly influences cracking behavior. The powder bed temperature was estimated at 1100–1500 °C for all builds. Higher preheat energy (735 J/mm² vs. 325 J/mm²) was found to suppress cracking in builds with identical melt parameters.

4. **Cracking Mechanism**: Cracks in AM tungsten form via a **solid-state cracking mechanism**, evidenced by localized orientation gradients at crack edges indicating plastic deformation preceding cracking. Cracks nucleate from stress concentrations associated with pores, then propagate along columnar grain boundaries in the build (Z) direction. This is distinct from solidification cracking (hot tearing).

5. **Beam Overlap and Remelting**: The high beam powers available in EB-PBF enable approximately 14 times remelting per point (vs. ~2 times for typical LPBF), which reduces thermal gradients and cooling rates, lowering thermal and residual stresses. This is analogous to dual-beam welding, which is known to suppress cracking via thermal strain reduction.

6. **Oxygen Control**: Oxygen is a critical factor in cracking; oxygen segregates to grain boundaries and forms brittle oxides (WO₃), promoting intergranular fracture. EB-PBF advantages include vacuum environment, larger powder size (reduced oxygen pickup), and reducing conditions during melting (above 2000 °C) that can remove oxygen.

7. **Process Window**: A process map was constructed for additively manufactured tungsten showing that a minimum energy density of approximately 200 J/mm³ is required to produce dense tungsten, and ambient temperature above 1000 °C is of considerable assistance. The EB-PBF samples reported here occupy a distinct region of the process map at 750–2000 J/mm³.

8. **Limitations**: Pores below 40 μm (camera pixel resolution) cannot be detected by NIR. Even at temperatures well above DBTT (above 600 °C), cracking was not entirely eliminated across all samples, indicating that merely processing above DBTT is insufficient to prevent cracking; thermal and residual stress management is also critical.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

## Limitations
- **Mechanical Property Data**: The paper does not report any mechanical property testing (tensile, compressive, hardness, etc.) of the fabricated tungsten. All findings are based on defect analysis (porosity, cracks) and process-structure observations.
- **Crack Elimination**: Cracking was not entirely eliminated across all samples and process conditions; Build 2, Part 3 showed crack-free regions but not all samples were crack-free.
- **Pore Detection Resolution**: In-situ NIR detection is limited by camera pixel resolution (~40 μm); smaller pores cannot be detected.
- **Temperature Measurement**: The powder bed temperature was estimated using intermittent two-color pyrometry; precise continuous temperature monitoring was not possible.
- **Support Structure**: The porous support structure between the Ti64 base plate and dense W region is not fully characterized; it may contribute to cracking via pore nucleation sites.
- **Generalizability**: This is a single-machine study (Arcam Q10+); parameters may not transfer directly to other EB-PBF systems.

## Applicable Scope
- **Fusion Reactor Plasma-Facing Components**: Pure tungsten divertor components requiring high-temperature service, high sputtering threshold, low tritium retention, and good thermal conductivity
- **EB-PBF Processing of Refractory Metals**: The process observations (preheat temperature, beam overlap, oxygen control) are applicable to other refractory metals processed via EB-PBF
- **In-situ Defect Detection**: The NIR-based defect detection algorithm approach is applicable to other refractory metals and high-density materials
- **Not Directly Applicable To**: LPBF of tungsten (different thermal history and oxygen environment); multi-element tungsten alloys (the paper focuses on pure W); mechanical property qualification (no mechanical testing was performed)
