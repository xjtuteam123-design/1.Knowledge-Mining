# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** A single-phase Ti3Zr1.5NbVAl0.25 refractory high entropy alloy with excellent combination of strength and toughness
- **Authors:** Shuai Zeng, Yuhui Zhu, Wei Li, Hongwei Zhang, Haifeng Zhang, Zhengwang Zhu
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.matlet.2022.132548
- **Alloy system studied:** Ti-Zr-Nb-V-Al refractory high entropy alloy
- **Research objective:** To develop a ductile, low-density refractory high entropy alloy with balanced strength and toughness through hot forging and annealing; to evaluate both quasi-static tensile properties and dynamic impact toughness, and to investigate deformation and fracture mechanisms.
- **Material category:** Refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA); single-phase BCC solid solution; wrought (hot-forged and annealed) material.

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies a Ti3Zr1.5NbVAl0.25 refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) with the explicit design intent of combining intrinsically ductile, relatively low-density elements (Ti, Zr, Nb, V) with a small Al addition for solid-solution strengthening. The alloy is prepared by vacuum arc melting, then hot forged and annealed to produce a recrystallized, single-phase BCC structure with coarse equiaxed grains. The research objective is twofold: (1) to demonstrate an RHEA with superior room-temperature tensile ductility and toughness, and (2) to provide the first reported combination of high tensile strength and Charpy V-notch impact toughness in an RHEA. The material is categorized as a wrought (not additively manufactured or powder-metallurgy) refractory high-entropy alloy.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Melting:** Vacuum arc melting of mixtures of the corresponding elements (purities of Ti, Nb, V, Al, Zr all >99.5%).
- **Ingot dimensions after melting:** approximately φ 90 × 140 mm; weight ~5.5 kg.
- **Hot forging:** Ingot heated to 1323 K (1050 °C), held for 90 min, then hot forged with a total thickness reduction of 50%. Final forged dimensions approximately 45 × 45 × 390 mm.
- **Annealing:** A specimen of size 45 × 45 × 60 mm was cut and annealed at 1423 K (1150 °C) for 15 min, followed by water quenching.
- **Processing route category:** Vacuum arc melting → hot forging → recrystallization annealing → water quench. No powder metallurgy, mechanical alloying, SPS, HIP, or additive manufacturing is involved.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase:** Single-phase BCC (body-centered cubic), confirmed by XRD and TEM/SAED.
- **Second phases/precipitates:** None detected. The paper states that Al completely dissolved into the matrix with no segregation to form B2 phase. No carbides, oxides, borides, silicides, or intermetallics were observed.
- **Grain size:** Average grain size measured by EBSD is 265 μm after annealing. Grains are described as “coarse equiaxed grain.”
- **Grain boundary features:** TEM bright-field micrograph and SAED at the grain boundary region confirm single-phase structure without any precipitation at grain boundaries.
- **Elemental segregation:** None; TEM-EDS mapping demonstrates uniform distribution of all constituent elements.
- **Phase stability prediction:** The single BCC structure was also predicted by empirical criteria: mixing enthalpy 15 ≤ ΔH_mix ≤ 5 kJ/mol (actually stated as “15 ≤ ΔH_mix ≤ 5 kJ/mol” in the original, but typically this criterion is −15 ≤ ΔH_mix ≤ 5 kJ/mol; reproduced here as written), atomic size difference δ ≤ 6.6%, Ω ≥ 1.1, and VEC < 6.87. Calculated empirical parameters are in Table S1 of Supplementary data (not provided).
- **BCC stabilization mechanism:** High concentration of Nb and V (a total of 30 at.%) stabilized the β phase.

Strengthening mechanisms discussed:
- **Solid-solution strengthening:** Identified as the dominant strengthening mechanism. The calculated solid-solution strengthening contribution is ~709 MPa.
- Precipitation strengthening: Not applicable (no precipitates).
- ODS/dispersion strengthening: Not applicable.
- Grain-boundary strengthening/grain refinement: Not explicitly discussed as a strengthening mechanism; the grain size is coarse (265 μm), and the paper attributes toughness improvement to microstructure homogenization and defect elimination by forging/annealing rather than grain refinement.
- Second-phase strengthening: Not applicable (single-phase alloy).

## [Dislocation-Mediated Plasticity (Cross-Slip/Wavy Slip)]

Although not a classical “strengthening” mechanism, the paper presents dislocation behavior as the dominant plasticity mechanism. This card is added for completeness.

[Related Elements]
Not element-specific; general BCC crystal behavior.

[Typical Alloys]
Ti3Zr1.5NbVAl0.25 single-phase BCC RHEA.

[Key Compositions]
Same as above.

[Microstructural Changes]
- At 1.5% strain: coplanar slip of dislocations dominates, with occasional cross-slip.
- At 10% strain: massive straight planar slip bands (PSBs) observed; dislocations arranged in loops and bundles; frequent dislocation cross-slip occurs due to close-packed {110} and {112} slip planes.
- At fracture: wavy slip dominates; abundant cross-slip forms high-dense dislocation walls (HDDWs).
- No deformation twins detected.

[Property Improvements]
The multiple dislocation interactions and dislocation multiplication are the dominant mechanism for large plasticity (24.5% elongation).

[Risks and Limitations]
No deformation twinning observed, so plasticity relies entirely on dislocation slip and cross-slip.

[Original Evidence]
“Based on the above results, the plastic deformation in current RHEA appears to be accommodated mainly by dislocation activity, and the multiple dislocation interactions via dislocation multiplication are considered to be the dominant mechanism for the large plasticity of this RHEA.” Section 3, Fig. 4a–d.

# Key Conclusions

1. A Ti3Zr1.5NbVAl0.25 refractory high-entropy alloy was successfully prepared by vacuum arc melting, followed by hot forging at 1323 K for 90 min (50% thickness reduction) and annealing at 1423 K for 15 min with water quenching.
2. The alloy has a single-phase BCC structure with no precipitates or B2 phase; Al fully dissolves in the matrix; average grain size is 265 μm; density is ~5.76 g/cm³.
3. Room-temperature tensile yield strength is ~899 MPa; fracture elongation is ~24.5%.
4. Charpy V-notch impact toughness is 119.5 J/cm²; U-notch impact toughness is 131.3 J/cm². The paper states this V-notch toughness has not been previously reported for BCC RHEAs.
5. Solid-solution strengthening is the dominant strengthening mechanism, with an evaluated contribution of ~709 MPa.
6. The superior strength–toughness combination is attributed to (i) the single-phase BCC solid solution and (ii) hot forging plus annealing that homogenizes the microstructure and eliminates casting defects.
7. Plastic deformation is accommodated by dislocation activity: coplanar slip at low strain, planar slip bands with cross-slip at intermediate strain, and wavy slip with high-dense dislocation walls at fracture; no deformation twins are observed.
8. The alloy shows the best synergy of yield strength and impact toughness compared to several HEAs, Ti alloys, TWIP steels, and TRIP steels.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Composition scope:** Results are specific to a single alloy composition, Ti3Zr1.5NbVAl0.25, and should not be generalized to all RHEAs or BCC HEAs without caution.
- **Temperature range:** Only room-temperature mechanical properties are reported. No high-temperature strength, creep, or elevated-temperature ductility data are available, which limits assessment of service performance at high temperatures typical for refractory alloys.
- **Oxidation resistance:** Not studied. The paper provides no data on oxidation behavior, oxidation layers, or environmental stability, so no conclusions about high-temperature environmental resistance can be drawn.
- **Anisothermal/processing dependence:** The reported properties correspond to a specific processing route (vacuum arc melting → hot forging at 1323 K → annealing at 1423 K/15 min → water quench). Different thermomechanical treatments may yield different microstructures and properties.
- **Interstitial content:** No C, O, N, H analysis is provided, so the potential influence of interstitial contamination on mechanical behavior is unknown.
- **Grain size effect:** The alloy has relatively coarse grains (265 μm); grain refinement was not explored, so the influence of grain size on strength or toughness is not addressed.
- **Failure mechanism under impact:** The paper reports ductile dimple fracture with occasional quasi-cleavage facets during crack initiation and propagation, but no quantitative fracture-mechanics parameters (e.g., K_IC) are reported.
- **Comparison database:** The strength–toughness comparison in Fig. 2b draws on literature alloys; conclusions about superiority depend on selected references and test standards, which may differ between studies.
