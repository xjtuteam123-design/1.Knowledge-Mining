# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system studied**: MoNbTaTiCx refractory high-entropy alloy system (eutectic RHEA with carbide reinforcement)
- **Material category**: Refractory high-entropy alloy with in-situ carbide second phase (eutectic RHEA composite)
- **Research objective**: To design and prepare a novel eutectic MoNbTaTiCx refractory high-entropy alloy with a good combination of room-temperature fracture strain and high-temperature (1473 K) strength, and to investigate the microstructure evolution and mechanical properties as a function of C content.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Phase constitution**: All MNTTCx alloys (x = 0.1 to 0.6) are composed of:
  - **BCC matrix phase** (presumably Mo-Nb-Ta-Ti-rich solid solution)
  - **FCC MC-type carbide phase** (metal carbide with rock-salt structure)
- **XRD observations**: The peak intensity of the MC carbide phase increases with increasing C content, indicating increasing volume fraction of the MC phase.
- **Microstructure evolution with C content** (from SEM-BSE):
  - **x = 0.1, 0.2 (low C)**: Block-shaped carbides (light-contrast phase in BSE) are mainly dispersed at grain boundaries.
  - **x = 0.3, 0.4 (intermediate C)**: Hypoeutectic morphology with alternating lamellar layers in the interdendritic area (mixture of primary dendrites + eutectic).
  - **x = 0.5**: **Fully eutectic morphology** consisting of eutectic colonies where the two phases (BCC + FCC MC carbide) are formed alternately. Lamellar size is smaller than in other alloys.
  - **x = 0.6 (high C)**: Hypereutectic microstructure with primary carbide blocks.
- **Phase identification via BSE contrast**: Light-color phase = inferred BCC matrix; dark-color phase = inferred MC carbide (based on average Z-contrast).
- **Elemental segregation/partitioning (from EDS, Table S1)**:
  - **Mo**: distributed mainly in the matrix (BCC)
  - **Ti**: mainly in the carbides (MC)
  - **Nb and Ta**: relatively uniform distribution
  - With low C content, Ti is the dominant carbide-forming element; with higher C content, more Nb and Ta appear in the carbides (because NbC and TaC have high melting points and thermodynamic stability).
- **Grain size**: Not quantitatively reported in the main text. The paper states that the lamellar size in MNTTC₀.₅ (eutectic) is smaller than in other alloys, which is beneficial for mechanical performance.
- **Precipitate size and distribution**: 
  - Blocky carbides at grain boundaries for low C (x = 0.1–0.2)
  - Lamellar eutectic for x = 0.5
  - Coarse primary FCC carbides for x = 0.6
- **Grain boundary features**: Blocky carbides at grain boundaries in low-C alloys are detrimental to room-temperature deformation ability.
- **Is the paper discussing strengthening mechanisms?**: Yes. The paper explicitly mentions:
  - Second-phase strengthening (by carbide phase)
  - Grain refinement (eutectic structure)
  - Interface strengthening (inhibiting dislocation glide)
  - Solid-solution strengthening is mentioned as a baseline for RHEAs but noted to weaken rapidly with temperature; the paper's focus is on second-phase strengthening to compensate.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Carbon (C)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: MoNbTaTiCx refractory high-entropy eutectic alloy.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: x = 0 to 0.6 in molar ratio (i.e., 0 to 0.6 mol C per mol of equimolar MoNbTaTi). Carbon was introduced via TiC powder (purity > 99.9%).
- **Main Function**: 
  - Formation of FCC MC-type carbide phase as a second strengthening phase.
  - Controls the solidification morphology from hypoeutectic → eutectic → hypereutectic.
  - At the eutectic composition (x = 0.5), enables simultaneous grain refinement and second-phase strengthening.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: 
  - At low C content (x = 0.1–0.2), C reacts preferentially with Ti to form blocky Ti-rich MC carbides at grain boundaries.
  - At higher C content, more Nb and Ta participate in carbide formation (NbC and TaC with high melting points).
  - At x = 0.5, a fully eutectic lamellar structure of alternating BCC and FCC MC carbide forms, with refined lamellar spacing and high density of interfaces.
  - Interfaces inhibit dislocation gliding.
- **Property Effect**:
  - Room-temperature compression: as C increases from 0 to 0.6, yield strength increases progressively, while fracture strain decreases from ~20% (x = 0) to ~10% (x = 0.6).
  - At 1473 K: yield strength increases from the lowest value in carbon-free MoNbTaTi (specific values not given) to 496 MPa for MNTTC₀.₅ and a maximum of 510 MPa for MNTTC₀.₆.
  - MNTTC₀.₅ (eutectic) achieves a fracture strain of 13.4% at RT with a yield strength of 496 MPa at 1473 K, surpassing many reported RHEAs.
- **Potential Side Effects**: 
  - Blocky carbides at grain boundaries (low C, x = 0.1–0.2) are detrimental to room-temperature deformation ability.
  - Hypereutectic alloy (x = 0.6) forms coarse primary FCC carbides with poor deformation ability, causing a significant drop in room-temperature fracture strain.
  - Increasing C content generally reduces room-temperature fracture strain (trade-off between strength and ductility).
- **Original Evidence**: 
  - "The microstructures changed from hypoeutectic to eutectic and then to hypereutectic as the C content increased." (Conclusion)
  - "Blocky carbides at the interdendrite area and grain boundaries are detrimental for deformation ability especially at room temperature." (Section 3.2)
  - "In the hypereutectic alloy (x = 0.6), coarse primary FCC carbides with poor deformation ability are formed in the alloy, causing obvious drop of room-temperature fracture strain." (Section 3.2)
  - Fig. 1(b): XRD patterns show increasing MC peak intensity with C content.
  - Fig. 2(a-f): SEM-BSE micrographs showing microstructure evolution.

## Titanium (Ti)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: MoNbTaTiCx refractory high-entropy eutectic alloy.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Equimolar with Mo, Nb, Ta (1:1:1:1 molar ratio). Alloy base composition.
- **Main Function**: 
  - Primary carbide-forming element (strong affinity with C, most negative mixing enthalpy of −109 kJ/mol among Mo, Nb, Ta, Ti).

  - At higher C contents, more Nb and Ta participate in carbide formation.
- **Property Effect**: 
  - The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the effect of Ti alone on mechanical properties, as Ti was not varied independently.
  - Ti-based carbides contribute to second-phase strengthening.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not specifically discussed in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence**: 
  - "Mo is distributed mainly in the matrix while Ti mainly in the carbides." (Section 3.1)

  - "Ti will be the dominate element in the carbides when C content is low." (Section 3.1)

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: Mo, Nb, Ta, Ti in the BCC matrix.
- **Typical Alloys**: MoNbTaTi (carbon-free base alloy); all MNTTCx alloys.
- **Key Compositions**: Equimolar Mo:Nb:Ta:Ti (1:1:1:1 molar ratio).
- **Microstructural Changes**: Severe lattice distortion due to atomic size mismatch and chemical complexity in the BCC solid solution.
- **Property Improvements**: 
  - Provides baseline high-temperature strength for RHEAs.
  - For the MNTTCx series, solid-solution strengthening is the primary mechanism in the carbon-free MoNbTaTi alloy.
  - The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for solid-solution strengthening contribution alone.
- **Risks and Limitations**: 
  - "This strengthening effect rapidly weakened with increasing temperature." (Introduction, citing reference [1])
  - Insufficient for high-temperature applications alone; second-phase strengthening is needed as a complement.
- **Original Evidence**: 
  - "Solid solution strengthening provides the greatest contribution to superior performance at high temperatures due to severe lattice distortion. However, this strengthening effect rapidly weakened with increasing temperature." (Introduction)

## Carbide Precipitation / Second-Phase Strengthening (MC-type carbide)

- **Related Elements**: C, Ti, Nb, Ta; MC-type FCC carbides (TiC, NbC, TaC, and mixed carbides).
- **Typical Alloys**: MNTTCx (x = 0.1–0.6).
- **Key Compositions**: Eutectic composition MNTTC₀.₅ (MoNbTaTiC₀.₅); hypereutectic MNTTC₀.₆.
- **Microstructural Changes**: 
  - FCC MC-type carbide phase precipitates in the BCC matrix.
  - Morphology evolves from blocky grain-boundary carbides (low C) → eutectic lamellae (x = 0.5) → coarse primary carbides (x = 0.6).
  - Increasing C content increases MC carbide volume fraction.
- **Property Improvements**: 
  - Compressive yield strength increases monotonically with C content at both RT and 1473 K.
  - At 1473 K: yield strength reaches 496 MPa (x = 0.5) and 510 MPa (x = 0.6).
  - The MNTTCx alloys exhibit better high-temperature performance than MoNbTaTi and other typical RHEAs (including MoNbTaW-type).
- **Risks and Limitations**: 
  - Blocky carbides at grain boundaries (x = 0.1–0.4) reduce room-temperature fracture strain.
  - Coarse primary carbides (x = 0.6) cause significant drop in RT fracture strain.
  - General trade-off: strength increases but ductility decreases with C content.
- **Original Evidence**: 
  - "The enhancement of the strengths is mainly contributed by the more prominent second phase strengthening effect by the carbide phase." (Section 3.2)
  - "Second-phase strengthening is a method to compensate for the weakness of solid solution strengthening by introducing carbide, silicide, nitride, etc." (Introduction)

## Grain Refinement / Eutectic Interface Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: C (as a eutectic promoter with carbide-forming elements Ti, Nb, Ta).
- **Typical Alloys**: MNTTC₀.₅ (fully eutectic MoNbTaTiC₀.₅).
- **Key Compositions**: x = 0.5 molar ratio of C in MoNbTaTiCx.
- **Microstructural Changes**: 
  - Fully eutectic microstructure with alternating BCC + FCC MC carbide lamellae.
  - Lamellar size in MNTTC₀.₅ is smaller than in other alloys in the series.
  - High volume fraction of strengthening phase; high density of interfaces.
- **Property Improvements**: 
  - MNTTC₀.₅ achieves a good combination: 13.4% fracture strain at RT (similar to MNTTC₀.₄) and 496 MPa yield strength at 1473 K (higher than MNTTC₀.₄).
  - Enhanced ability to accommodate dislocations during deformation.
- **Risks and Limitations**: 
  - Requires precise control of C content to hit the eutectic composition.
  - Deviation to hypereutectic (x = 0.6) or hypoeutectic (x ≤ 0.4) reduces the balanced properties.
- **Original Evidence**: 
  - "Eutectic RHEAs are a potential candidate for combining deformation ability with high-temperature strength. This is due to grain refinement, a higher volume fraction of the strengthening phase, and stronger interface inhibiting dislocation gliding." (Introduction)
  - "Eutectic morphology with an alternate lamellar structure is proved to be an effective way to achieve good combination of ductility and strength at the same time, due to high volume fraction of strengthening phase, good interface bonding, and various interfaces which could accommodate more dislocations in the deformation process." (Section 3.2)
  - "MNTTC₀.₅ alloy with a fully eutectic microstructure has similar fracture strain compared with MNTTC₀.₄ alloy while owing higher strength." (Section 3.2)

# Key Conclusions

1. **Successful preparation**: MoNbTaTiCx (MNTTCx, x = 0–0.6) refractory high-entropy alloys were successfully prepared by vacuum arc melting with TiC as the carbon source.

2. **Phase constitution**: All alloys consist of a BCC matrix phase and an FCC MC-type carbide phase. The MC carbide volume fraction increases with C content (XRD peak intensity increase).

3. **Microstructure evolution**: 
   - The C content controls the solidification morphology: hypoeutectic (x = 0.1–0.4) → fully eutectic (x = 0.5) → hypereutectic (x = 0.6).
   - The CALPHAD-predicted eutectic point was at x = 0.4 (MoNbTaTiC₀.₄), but the experimental eutectic composition was found at x = 0.5 (MoNbTaTiC₀.₅).
   - Elemental partitioning: Mo partitions to the matrix; Ti partitions to carbides; Nb and Ta are relatively uniform, with more Nb/Ta entering carbides at higher C content.

4. **Strengthening mechanisms**: 
   - Carbide second-phase strengthening is the dominant strengthening mechanism.
   - The eutectic structure at x = 0.5 provides the optimal combination of strength and ductility through grain refinement, high strengthening-phase volume fraction, and interface-assisted dislocation accommodation.
   - Solid-solution strengthening weakens at high temperatures; carbide strengthening compensates for this.

5. **Optimal composition and properties**: 
   - **MNTTC₀.₅** (fully eutectic): 
     - Yield strength at 1473 K = **496 MPa**
     - Fracture strain at room temperature = **13.4%**
   - This combination surpasses many reported refractory high-entropy alloys, including MoNbTaTi (carbon-free) and MoNbTaW-type RHEAs.
   - Maximum high-temperature yield strength was 510 MPa in MNTTC₀.₆, but at the cost of reduced room-temperature ductility.

6. **Trade-off**: Increasing C content increases strength (RT and HT) but decreases room-temperature fracture strain; the eutectic microstructure at x = 0.5 mitigates this trade-off.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Testing method limitation**: Only compression tests were performed; no tensile tests, creep tests, or fracture toughness measurements are reported.
- **Temperature range**: Mechanical properties were tested only at room temperature and 1473 K (1200 °C). Performance at 800 °C, 1000 °C, 1300 °C, 1400 °C, or above 1473 K is not reported.
- **Oxidation behavior**: Not studied; oxidation resistance and high-temperature environmental stability are unknown for these alloys.
- **Microstructural quantification**: Grain size, lamellar spacing, precipitate size, and volume fraction are not quantitatively reported in the main text. No TEM analysis is reported.
- **Heat treatment**: No heat treatment (homogenization, annealing, aging) was applied; the alloys were tested in the as-cast condition. The effect of heat treatment on microstructure and properties is unknown.
- **Composition range limited**: Only equimolar MoNbTaTi base with varying C (0–0.6 molar ratio) was studied. The effects of varying the metal ratios (e.g., non-equimolar Mo/Nb/Ta/Ti) are not explored.
- **Elemental analysis limited**: EDS data for carbide composition are presented only in supplementary material (Table S1), not fully detailed in the main text.
- **Impurity content (O, N, H)**: Not reported.
- **Atmosphere for high-temperature compression**: Not explicitly stated in the main text.
- **Density and elastic modulus**: Not reported.
- **Applicability**: The findings are directly applicable to MoNbTaTiCx-type refractory high-entropy alloys prepared by vacuum arc melting. Extrapolation to other alloy systems (e.g., W-based, Mo-W-based, ODS alloys, additively manufactured refractory alloys) is not supported by this paper.
