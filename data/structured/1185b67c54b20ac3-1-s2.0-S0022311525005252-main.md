# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** Mechanical properties, 475 °C embrittlement and irradiation resistance of FeCrAl-Y₂O₃ ODS alloy with Ti and V alloying additions
- **Authors:** T. Stasiak, J.J. Jasiński, M. Zieliński, M. Wilczopolska, A. Kosińska, W. Chmurzyński, K. Mulewska, R. Diduszko, M. Chmielewski, M. Kowal, J. Rzempołuch, K. Ciporska, Ł. Kurpaska, J. Jagielski
- **Journal:** Journal of Nuclear Materials
- **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jnucmat.2025.156131
- **Alloy system studied:** FeCrAl-based oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) ferritic alloys with Y₂O₃, Ti, and V additions
- **Research objective:** To evaluate the mechanical properties at high temperatures (up to 800 °C), resistance to 475 °C embrittlement, and irradiation resistance of FeCrAl-Y₂O₃ ODS alloys with Ti and V additions, with particular emphasis on the impact of minor vanadium addition
- **Material category:** FeCrAl-based ODS ferritic alloy (not a refractory alloy; it is a ferritic ODS steel)

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies FeCrAl-based ODS alloys for Generation IV nuclear reactor applications, specifically as accident-tolerant fuel (ATF) claddings. Two alloy compositions are compared:

- **ODS-1-Ti:** Fe-12Cr-5Al-0.3Y₂O₃-1.0Ti (wt.%)
- **ODS-2-TiV:** Fe-12Cr-5Al-0.3Y₂O₃-1.0Ti-0.5V (wt.%)

The matrix is ferritic (BCC). The key research variables are:
1. Effect of vanadium addition (0.5 wt.%) on mechanical properties
2. Resistance to 475 °C embrittlement (α-α′ phase separation)
3. Ion irradiation resistance (self-ion Fe²⁺ irradiation at 300 °C)

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Mechanical Alloying (MA):**
- Retsch PM100 planetary ball mill
- 250 rpm for 50 hours
- Argon atmosphere
- Balls-to-powder ratio: 5:1
- Total powder weight per batch: ~50 g
- Milling jar: martensitic stainless steel

**Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS):**
- Device: Lukasiewicz Institute of Microelectronics and Photonics, Poland
- Graphite round die (Φ = 25 mm)
- Sample height: ~9 mm
- Temperature: 1050 °C
- Time: 10 min
- Uniaxial pressure: 40 MPa

**Homogenization Annealing:**
- Temperature: 1020 °C
- Time: 30 minutes
- Atmosphere: argon
- Cooling: in air

**475 °C Embrittlement Annealing:**
- Temperature: 475 °C
- Time: 1000 h
- Atmosphere: air
- Cooling: slow furnace cooling

**Ion Irradiation:**
- Ion type: Fe²⁺ (self-ion irradiation)
- Energy: 10 MeV
- Temperature: 300 °C
- Dose: ODS-1-Ti - 1 dpa (fluence 1 × 10¹⁵ ions/cm²); ODS-2-TiV - 5 dpa (fluence 5 × 10¹⁵ ions/cm²)
- Surface preparation: electropolished to obtain stress-free surface
- Mask covered one-half of sample for direct comparison

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Matrix phase:** Single BCC (space group: Im3̄m), ferritic

**Grain size:**
- ODS-1-Ti: 540 nm (as-sintered), increases to 680 nm after 475 °C/1000 h
- ODS-2-TiV: 600 nm (as-sintered), stable at 610 nm after 475 °C/1000 h
- Grain orientation is random, typical for SPS-prepared samples

**Nanometric precipitates:**
- Volume fraction: 2.02% (ODS-1-Ti) vs. 2.64% (ODS-2-TiV) in as-sintered state
- Average size: 26 nm (ODS-1-Ti) vs. 24 nm (ODS-2-TiV); size range between 10–50 nm in both samples
- Most oxide precipitates are aluminum-rich, common for FeCrAl ODS alloys
- Types of oxides mentioned in context: Y₂O₃, Y₂Zr₂O₇, Y₄Zr₃O₁₂, Y₂TiO₅, YAlO₃ (YAH/YAP), Y₄Al₂O₉ (YAM), Y₃Al₅O₁₂ (YAG), Y₂Ti₂O₇, and non-stoichiometric Y-Ti-O enriched clusters
- Carbide precipitates also present

**After 475 °C/1000 h annealing:**
- Precipitate volume fraction increases slightly: 2.02% → 2.21% (ODS-1-Ti); 2.64% → 2.80% (ODS-2-TiV)
- Significant increase in volume fraction of smallest precipitates (diameter below 15 nm), suggesting nucleation of additional precipitates during annealing (not growth of existing ones)

**After ion irradiation:**
- Precipitate volume fraction remains similar: ODS-1-Ti: 1.99% (after 1 dpa) vs. 2.02% (pristine); ODS-2-TiV: 2.66% (after 5 dpa) vs. 2.64% (pristine)
- No significant changes in precipitate size distribution

**Dislocation density:**
- ODS-1-Ti pristine: 2.68 × 10¹⁴ ± 0.2 × 10¹⁴ m⁻²
- ODS-1-Ti after 1 dpa irradiation: 4.01 × 10¹⁴ ± 0.4 × 10¹⁴ m⁻²
- ODS-2-TiV pristine: 3.10 × 10¹⁴ ± 0.6 × 10¹⁴ m⁻²
- ODS-2-TiV after 5 dpa irradiation: 5.16 × 10¹⁴ ± 0.4 × 10¹⁴ m⁻²

**Discussion of alpha-prime (α′) phase:**
- Chromium local micro- or nano-segregation (typically responsible for 475 °C embrittlement) was not found using STEM-EDS mapping
- Potential chromium segregation (α′ phase formation) could be below STEM-EDS resolution limit
- Atom probe tomography (APT) investigations are planned for future studies

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed:**
- Dispersion strengthening (nanometric oxide precipitates)
- Grain boundary strengthening (fine grains <1 μm)
- Solid-solution strengthening
- Dislocation strengthening
- Peierls-Nabarro stress

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

The paper does not directly test oxidation resistance but reviews relevant literature:
- Cr significantly improves corrosion resistance
- Al substantially improves corrosion and oxidation resistance by forming a dense protective layer on the surface
- However, Al can lead to formation of relatively large aluminum oxides, which are not beneficial for mechanical properties
- Al content >2 at.% can suppress or limit α′ phase formation; however, for alloys with lower Cr content, Al can promote embrittlement
- Chromium content between 10 and 12 wt.% seems optimal for sufficient oxidation resistance while preventing harmful embrittlement

**475 °C Embrittlement Resistance:**
- ODS-1-Ti (without V): total elongation decreases significantly from 13 ± 1% to 4 ± 1% after annealing at 475 °C/1000 h
- ODS-2-TiV (with V): total elongation shows almost no change (12 ± 2% as-sintered vs. 11 ± 3% after annealing)
- Hardness increase is limited in both samples (4.5% for ODS-1-Ti, 2.8% for ODS-2-TiV), compared to ~50% hardness increase reported by Capdevila et al. in another ODS alloy
- α′ phase was not observed in STEM-EDS mapping
- The better resistance of ODS alloys to 475 °C embrittlement is attributed to nanometric oxide particles affecting Cr diffusion pathways, acting as trap sites for point defects and decreasing diffusion rate

**Irradiation Resistance:**
- Tem of nanometric oxide precipitates shows stability after ion irradiation up to 5 dpa
- Precipitate volume fraction remains similar after irradiation
- Limited hardening (<10% increase) compared to ~20% in Fe-12Cr-6Al-0.4Zr-0.5Ti-0.5Y₂O₃ ODS and ~42–59% in Fe-12.5Cr-4.5Al-4Mo without oxide particles
- XRD shows single bcc phase in both pristine and irradiated areas
- Microstrain parameter (ε₀) increases by 67% after 1 dpa in ODS-1-Ti and by 345% after 5 dpa in ODS-2-TiV
- Unit cell constant decreases after ion implantation, attributed to formation of voids or vacancies

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

---

**[Vanadium (V)]**

- **Applicable Alloy System:** FeCrAl-Y₂O₃ ODS ferritic alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 0.5 wt.% (in ODS-2-TiV sample)
- **Main Function:** Increases mechanical strength at all testing temperatures up to 800 °C; significantly increases resistance to 475 °C embrittlement; increases ion irradiation resistance; prevents grain growth during annealing
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** 
  - Solid-solution strengthening (k_V = 4000)
  - Promotes nanometric precipitate formation, resulting in higher volume fraction of precipitates (2.64% vs. 2.02%)
  - Smaller average precipitate size (24 nm vs. 26 nm)
  - Prevents grain growth during annealing at 475 °C (grain size stable at 600–610 nm)
  - Higher volume fraction of precipitates acts as sinks for irradiation-induced defects
  - Nanometric precipitates affect Cr diffusion pathways, retarding α′ phase formation
- **Property Effect:** 
  - Room temperature YS: 893 ± 23 MPa (vs. 769 ± 24 MPa without V)
  - Room temperature UTS: 993 ± 10 MPa (vs. 902 ± 6 MPa without V)
  - 600 °C YS: 541 ± 18 MPa (vs. 490 ± 8 MPa without V)
  - After 475 °C/1000 h: total elongation remains at 11 ± 3% (vs. 12 ± 2% as-sintered), while without V it drops from 13 ± 1% to 4 ± 1%
  - Hardness: 353 ± 6 HV0.5 (vs. 330 ± 9 HV0.5 without V)
  - Irradiation hardening: 0.34 GPa after 5 dpa (vs. 0.38 GPa after 1 dpa without V)
- **Potential Side Effects:** Slightly lower room-temperature elongation (12 ± 2% vs. 13 ± 1% without V); not explicitly stated if other side effects exist
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.1: "the alloy with vanadium (ODS-2-TiV) has higher strength than those without vanadium (ODS-1-Ti) at all testing temperatures"; Section 3.2: "the addition of vanadium significantly increases the resistance to '475 °C embrittlement'"

---

**[Titanium (Ti)]**

- **Applicable Alloy System:** FeCrAl-Y₂O₃ ODS ferritic alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 1.0 wt.%
- **Main Function:** Beneficial in oxide formation in ODS alloys; forms complex oxides (Y₂TiO₅, Y₂Ti₂O₇, Y-Ti-O enriched clusters); contributes to dispersion strengthening
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Ti reacts with Y₂O₃ during powder metallurgy processing to form complex Y-Ti-O oxides; provides solid-solution strengthening (k_Ti = 4000); contributes to nanometric precipitate volume fraction
- **Property Effect:** Contributes to the overall strengthening; specific individual contribution to properties not separated from other elements; theoretical solid-solution strengthening contribution is included in the total σₛₛ calculation
- **Potential Side Effects:** Titanium-rich precipitates tend to be smaller than aluminum-rich oxides, so higher Ti content might improve ductility (per literature cited: "smaller average oxide particle size positively impacts the elongation"); the original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Ti-only effects
- **Original Evidence:** Introduction: "One of the alloying elements beneficial in oxide formation in ODS alloys is Ti"

---

**[Yttrium Oxide (Y₂O₃)]**

- **Applicable Alloy System:** FeCrAl-based ODS ferritic alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 0.3 wt.%
- **Main Function:** Source of nanometric oxide precipitates; provides dispersion strengthening; acts as sinks for irradiation-induced defects; retards α′ phase formation by affecting Cr diffusion
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** During powder metallurgy processing, Y₂O₃ reacts with alloying elements (Ti, Al, Zr, etc.) to form complex oxides; nanometric oxides block dislocation movement and act as trap sites for point defects
- **Property Effect:** Resulting nanometric precipitates contribute 236 MPa (ODS-1-Ti) and 286 MPa (ODS-2-TiV) of dispersion strengthening; irradiance-induced hardening is limited (<10%) compared to alloys without oxide particles
- **Potential Side Effects:** Formation of relatively large aluminum-rich oxides when Al content is high; yttrium-aluminum complex oxides (YAM, YAG) can form with varying mechanical effects; the original paper notes that most oxides are aluminum-rich, which tend to be larger than titanium-rich or zirconium-rich precipitates
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.2: "Nanometric oxides act as trap sites for point defects and therefore decrease diffusion rate. It retards the formation of the α′ phase"

---

**[Chromium (Cr)]**

- **Applicable Alloy System:** FeCrAl-based ODS ferritic alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 12.0 wt.%
- **Main Function:** Significantly improves corrosion resistance; essential for oxidation resistance in nuclear reactor applications
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Provides solid-solution strengthening (k_Cr = 1400); at 475 °C, can separate into Cr-rich α′ phase causing embrittlement; content between 10–12 wt.% is optimal for sufficient oxidation resistance while preventing harmful embrittlement
- **Property Effect:** Contributes 216–235 MPa of solid-solution strengthening (combined with other elements); corrosion resistance improves with Cr content
- **Potential Side Effects:** Higher Cr content (e.g., 20 wt.%) promotes α-α′ separation and 475 °C embrittlement (reference to Capdevila et al. study showing ~50% hardness increase); potential formation of Cr-rich α′ phase under irradiation
- **Original Evidence:** Introduction: "The presence of Cr significantly improves corrosion resistance"; "The chromium content between 10 and 12 wt.% seems optimal to have sufficient oxidation resistance while usually preventing harmful embrittlement"

---

**[Aluminum (Al)]**

- **Applicable Alloy System:** FeCrAl-based ODS ferritic alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 5.0 wt.%
- **Main Function:** Substantially improves corrosion and oxidation resistance by forming a dense protective layer on the surface; content >2 at.% can suppress or limit α′ phase formation
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Provides solid-solution strengthening (k_Al = 4000); forms aluminum-rich oxides during MA/SPS processing; Al-rich oxides tend to be larger than Ti-rich or Zr-rich precipitates
- **Property Effect:** Critical for oxidation resistance; potential trade-off with mechanical properties due to formation of relatively large aluminum oxides
- **Potential Side Effects:** Large aluminum oxides are not beneficial for mechanical properties; can promote embrittlement for alloys with lower Cr content (per literature cited); can lead to limited ductility
- **Original Evidence:** Introduction: "The content of Al in the matrix substantially improves the corrosion and oxidation resistance of ODS alloys by forming a dense protective layer on the surface"; "However, the presence of Al could lead to the formation of relatively large aluminum oxides, which are not beneficial in terms of mechanical properties"

**[Yttrium (Y)]**

- **Applicable Alloy System:** FeCrAl-based ODS ferritic alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 0.3 wt.% Y₂O₃
- **Main Function:** Source of oxide dispersion strengthening; forms stable nanometric precipitates contributing to high-temperature strength and irradiation resistance
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Forms Y-Ti-O complex oxides during mechanical alloying; nanometric Y-containing oxides act as sinks for irradiation-induced defects and block dislocation motion
- **Property Effect:** Dispersion strengthening of 236–286 MPa; stable oxide precipitates after irradiation up to 5 dpa
- **Potential Side Effects:** Not explicitly discussed in the paper
- **Original Evidence:** Section 4.3: "The nanometric precipitates could act as sinks for irradiation-induced defects, mitigating radiation damage"

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

---

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Cr (k=1400), Al (k=4000), Ti (k=4000), V (k=4000)
- **Typical Alloys:** FeCrAl-Y₂O₃ ODS alloys
- **Key Compositions:** 12Cr-5Al-1Ti-0.3Y₂O₃ (±0.5V) (wt.%)
- **Microstructural Changes:** Substitutional atoms in BCC Fe lattice create elastic strain fields that impede dislocation glide
- **Property Improvements:** Calculated contribution: 216 MPa (ODS-1-Ti), 235 MPa (ODS-2-TiV)
- **Risks and Limitations:** Interstitial elements (C, O) contribute negligible solid-solution strengthening since they are mostly in precipitates; only main substitutional elements considered

---

## Oxide Dispersion Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Y₂O₃, Ti, V (promotes precipitate formation), Al (forms aluminum-rich oxides)
- **Typical Alloys:** FeCrAl-Y₂O₃ ODS ferritic alloys
- **Key Compositions:** 0.3 wt.% Y₂O₃ + 1.0 wt.% Ti + 0.5 wt.% V
- **Microstructural Changes:** Nanometric complex oxide precipitates (10–50 nm; average 24–26 nm); volume fraction 2.02–2.64%; precipitates stable after 475 °C/1000 h annealing and 5 dpa ion irradiation
- **Property Improvements:** Dispersion strengthening contribution: 236 MPa (ODS-1-Ti), 286 MPa (ODS-2-TiV); high RT UTS of 902–993 MPa; good high-temperature strength (541 MPa at 600 °C for ODS-2-TiV)
- **Risks and Limitations:** Larger aluminum-rich oxides are less beneficial than Ti-rich or Zr-rich precipitates; slightly lower ductility compared to some other ODS alloys

---

## Grain Boundary Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** V (prevents grain growth)
- **Typical Alloys:** FeCrAl-Y₂O₃ ODS ferritic alloys
- **Key Compositions:** Ultrafine-grained BCC matrix (540–600 nm)
- **Microstructural Changes:** Fine grain size provides high density of grain boundaries acting as barriers to dislocation motion; V addition prevents grain growth during annealing (600→610 nm vs. 540→680 nm without V)
- **Property Improvements:** Grain boundary strengthening contribution: 204 MPa (ODS-1-Ti), 194 MPa (ODS-2-TiV)
- **Risks and Limitations:** Fine grains may contribute to limited ductility at lower temperatures

---

## Dislocation Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Carbon (forms carbides through MA)
- **Typical Alloys:** FeCrAl-Y₂O₃ ODS ferritic alloys
- **Key Compositions:** Dislocation density: 2.68 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² (ODS-1-Ti), 3.10 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² (ODS-2-TiV) in pristine state
- **Microstructural Changes:** High dislocation density from powder metallurgy processing; increases after ion irradiation (to 4.01 × 10¹⁴ and 5.16 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² for ODS-1-Ti and ODS-2-TiV, respectively)
- **Property Improvements:** Dislocation strengthening contribution: 335 MPa (ODS-1-Ti), 360 MPa (ODS-2-TiV)
- **Risks and Limitations:** Dislocation density varies significantly between grains, making representative measurement challenging

---

## Oxidation-Resistant Alloying

- **Related Elements:** Cr (12 wt.%), Al (5 wt.%)
- **Typical Alloys:** FeCrAl-based ODS ferritic alloys
- **Key Compositions:** 12Cr-5Al (wt.%)
- **Microstructural Changes:** Formation of dense protective oxide layer (Al₂O₃ and Cr₂O₃); Al-rich precipitates within matrix
- **Property Improvements:** Good corrosion and oxidation resistance for nuclear applications
- **Risks and Limitations:** Al can form large oxides detrimental to mechanical properties; trade-off between oxidation resistance and ductility

---

## 475 °C Embrittlement Resistance

- **Related Elements:** Cr (12 wt.%), Al (5 wt.%), V (0.5 wt.%), Y₂O₃ (0.3 wt.%), Ti (1 wt.%)
- **Typical Alloys:** FeCrAl-Y₂O₃ ODS alloys
- **Key Compositions:** 12Cr-5Al-0.3Y₂O₃-1Ti-0.5V (wt.%) for best embrittlement resistance
- **Microstructural Changes:** Nanometric precipitates act as trap sites for point defects, decreasing diffusion rate and retarding α′ phase formation; V addition limits grain growth and maintains precipitate stability
- **Property Improvements:** ODS-2-TiV (with V): no significant change in total elongation after 475 °C/1000 h (12 ± 2% vs. 11 ± 3%); hardness increase limited to 2.8%
- **Risks and Limitations:** Without V (ODS-1-Ti): total elongation drops from 13 ± 1% to 4 ± 1%; α′ phase below STEM-EDS resolution limit; APT needed for conclusive confirmation

# Key Conclusions

1. FeCrAl-Y₂O₃ ODS alloys with Ti and V additions show promising mechanical properties for Generation IV nuclear reactor applications:
   - Room temperature ultimate tensile strength up to 993 ± 10 MPa
   - Yield strength of 893 ± 23 MPa at room temperature
   - Good high-temperature strength (541 ± 18 MPa at 600 °C)

2. Vanadium addition (0.5 wt.%) significantly improves mechanical strength at all testing temperatures (up to 800 °C) through:
   - Higher volume fraction of nanometric precipitates (2.64% vs. 2.02%)
   - Smaller average precipitate size (24 nm vs. 26 nm)
   - Solid-solution strengthening

3. Vanadium addition significantly increases resistance to 475 °C embrittlement:
   - No significant change in total elongation after 1000 h annealing (12 ± 2% → 11 ± 3%)
   - Limited grain growth (600 → 610 nm) compared to vanadium-free sample (540 → 680 nm)

4. Both alloys show promising irradiation resistance:
   - Limited hardening (<10% increase)
   - Stable nanometric oxide precipitates after Fe²⁺ ion irradiation up to 5 dpa at 300 °C
   - Vanadium-containing alloy shows better resistance to irradiation-induced hardening

5. Theoretical yield strength calculations are in good agreement with experimental values:
   - ODS-2-TiV: 903 MPa calculated vs. 893 MPa measured
   - ODS-1-Ti: 844 MPa calculated vs. 769 MPa measured
   - Primary strengthening contributions from dislocation strengthening (335–360 MPa), dispersion strengthening (236–286 MPa), solid-solution strengthening (216–235 MPa), and grain boundary strengthening (194–204 MPa)

6. No voids after irradiation observed; α′ phase not detected by STEM-EDS (below resolution limit); atom probe tomography (APT) investigations planned for future studies.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations:**
1. The study does not directly test oxidation resistance; only reviews literature
2. STEM-EDS resolution limit may be insufficient to detect nanometric α′ phase formation; APT is needed for conclusive confirmation
3. Dislocation density measurements made on relatively small area (approximately 1.5 μm², six grains), which may not be fully representative
4. Dislocation density varies significantly between grains in powder metallurgy samples
5. Miniaturized tensile specimens may have higher uncertainty in total elongation measurements than standard specimens
6. Ion irradiation with Fe²⁺ self-ions is a proxy for neutron irradiation; actual neutron irradiation behavior may differ
7. Irradiation doses limited to 1 dpa (ODS-1-Ti) and 5 dpa (ODS-2-TiV), which are relatively low compared to reactor lifetime doses
8. Most observed oxides are aluminum-rich, which tend to be larger than titanium-rich or zirconium-rich precipitates, potentially limiting ductility
9. The specific individual contributions of Ti and V to mechanical properties are not separated

**Applicable Scope:**
- Generation IV nuclear reactor components, specifically accident-tolerant fuel (ATF) claddings
- Applications requiring high-temperature strength up to 800 °C
- Materials operating at temperatures near 475 °C, where embrittlement resistance is critical
- Powder metallurgy (mechanical alloying + SPS) FeCrAl-based ODS alloys
- Ferritic matrix ODS alloys for nuclear applications
