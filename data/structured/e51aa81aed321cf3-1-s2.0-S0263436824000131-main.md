# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Microstructure and mechanical properties of WMoCu alloy doped with different sized Mo particles
- **Authors**: Xu Wang, Kexing Song, Jiang Feng, Jiandong Xing, Tao Huang, Yiming Zhang
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2024
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2024.106565
- **Alloy System Studied**: W-Cu pseudo-alloy doped with Mo (WMoCu alloy), prepared by infiltration process with Ni as activating element
- **Research Objective**: To investigate the effects of nanoscale (80 nm) and microscale (3 μm) Mo particle doping on the microstructure and mechanical properties of WMoCu alloys at room temperature and elevated temperature; to analyze the formation mechanism of WMo solid solution/core-shell structure at the W/Cu interface; to study the strengthening mechanisms.
- **Material Category**: W-Cu infiltrated pseudo-alloy doped with Mo (WMoCu alloy, W60Mo25Cu15). Note: This is not a conventional refractory high-entropy alloy or multi-principal element alloy; it is a W-Cu infiltration composite/pseudo-alloy with Mo as a strengthening additive.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system**: W-Cu pseudo-alloy modified with Mo and activated by Ni (W-Mo-Cu-Ni system)
- **Materials studied**:
  1. W85Cu15 (reference alloy, contains 0.5 wt.% Ni as activating element)
  2. W60Mo25Cu15(M) — doped with 3 μm Mo particles
  3. W60Mo25Cu15(N) — doped with 80 nm Mo particles
- **Fabrication route**: Powder mixing → pressing → pre-sintering → liquid Cu infiltration
- **Application background**: High-voltage electrical contacts, arc extinguishers, electromagnetic railroad gun components, rocket engine nozzles; materials requiring high-temperature strength and arc erosion resistance

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Infiltration Process (as described in paper):

1. **Powder mixing**:
   - W, Mo, and Ni powders mixed in home-made powder mixer
   - Milling for 6 h at 60 rpm
   - Ball-to-powder ratio: 5:1

2. **Compaction**:
   - Mixed powders pressed into W-Mo skeletons using four-column hydraulic press
   - Pressure: 210 MPa for 1 min
   - Skeleton dimensions: 40 mm diameter × 11 mm length

3. **Pre-sintering**:
   - Temperature: 900 °C for 1.5 h
   - Atmosphere: Hydrogen (H₂)

4. **Infiltration**:
   - Oxygen-free Cu plate placed on pre-sintered W-Mo skeleton
   - Temperature: 1350 °C for 4 h
   - Atmosphere: Hydrogen (H₂)

5. **Cooling**: Cooled from 1350 °C through 1083 °C (Cu melting point) to room temperature. Not explicitly stated whether furnace cooling or controlled cooling rate.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Matrix Phases:
- **Cu-rich phase**: Cu-based solid solution containing dissolved W, Mo, and Ni
- **W-rich phase**: W-based solid solution containing dissolved Mo, Cu, and Ni
- **WMo solid solution**: Formed at interfaces between W and Cu due to interdiffusion; W and Mo are infinitely mutually soluble (both BCC structure, atomic radii W: 1.41 Å, Mo: 1.40 Å)

## Interface Features:
- **Core-shell structure**: Formed around W particles; approximate thickness ~2 μm
- **WMo transition layer**: Formed at the W/Cu interface, composed of a Cu-based solid solution and a W-based solid solution
- **Amorphous structure**: Present at the W/Cu interfaces in the WMoCu alloy, with good adhesion to the crystalline phases; the uneven amorphous-crystalline interface increases deformation resistance during interfacial sliding
- **WCu alloy interface (reference)**: Tightly bonded, no pores or defects, but atomic arrangement disordered due to lattice mismatch (lattice spacings: W (110) = 0.223 nm; Cu (100) = 0.186 nm)

## Elemental Distribution:
- In W60Mo25Cu15(M) (3 μm Mo): More uniform microstructure and Mo distribution; denser, fewer copper-rich areas
- In W60Mo25Cu15(N) (80 nm Mo): Copper-rich areas present; less uniform; nanoscale Mo particles tend to agglomerate/grow together with W particles during sintering due to high surface energy
- EDS of WMoCu(M) shows W:Mo atomic ratio of ~3:1 in WMo solid solution regions

## WMo Skeleton Observations (post-sintering at 1350 °C for 4 h):
- **WMo(M) skeleton**: Higher degree of particle boundary passivation, almost total rounding of particle boundaries; sintering necks formed between some W or Mo particles; some polyhedral particles remain; relatively uniform distribution with few pores
- **WMo(N) skeleton**: Nano-scale Mo particles absorbed onto W particles during milling, grow together with W; more thorough reaction during sintering; prone to closed pore formation and conspicuous pores → copper-rich areas after infiltration

## Element Diffusion Mechanism:
- Ni preferentially dissolves in Cu to form CuNi(L) liquid phase during infiltration
- CuNi(L) alloy melt wraps W and Mo particles, increasing surface energy and improving diffusion efficiency
- CuNi(L) acts as a bridge for diffusion channels among W-W, W-Mo, and Mo-Mo particles
- W and Mo atoms enter CuNi(L) liquid phase, diffuse through grain boundaries or bulk diffusion, form WMo solid solution
- During cooling (1350 °C → 1083 °C), dissolved W and Mo crystallize as Mo- or W-rich solid solution on W/Mo particle surfaces
- During cooling (1083 °C → RT), remaining liquid forms Cu-rich solid solution in the outer layer

## Phase Identification:
- XRD diffraction peaks of W and Mo shift and form double peaks, indicating mutual solid solution of W and Mo

# Mechanical Properties

## Room Temperature (25 °C):
| Alloy | Tensile Strength (MPa) | Elongation (%) |
|---|---|---|
| W85Cu15 | 640 | 2 |
| W60Mo25Cu15(M) (3 μm Mo) | 698 | 2 |
| W60Mo25Cu15(N) (80 nm Mo) | 539 | 0.49 |

- Tensile strength of WMoCu(M) is 29.49% higher than WMoCu(N) and 8.3% higher than WCu
- Elongation of WMoCu(M) is equivalent to WCu alloy (2%), which is 4 times that of WMoCu(N) (0.49%)
- Strain rate: 5 × 10⁻⁴ /s
- Testing machine: Instron 5565 and Instron 5582
- Each test repeated three times

## High Temperature (600 °C):
| Alloy | Tensile Strength (MPa) |
|---|---|
| W85Cu15 | 242 |
| W60Mo25Cu15(M) (3 μm Mo) | 381 |
| W60Mo25Cu15(N) (80 nm Mo) | 321 |

- Tensile strength of WMoCu(M) at 600 °C is 57.44% higher than WCu
- Tensile strength of WMoCu(N) at 600 °C is 32.64% higher than WCu

## Hardness:
- Measured using 320HBS-3000 Brinell hardness tester
- Load: 1000 kg for 15 s
- Numerical values: Not explicitly reported in the original paper

## Electrical Conductivity:
- Measured using Sigma 2008B1 digital conductivity meter
- Numerical values: Not explicitly reported in the original paper

## Relative Density:
- Measured using Archimedean method
- Numerical values: Not explicitly reported in the original paper

## Fracture Mechanism:
- **Room temperature**: Both WCu and WMoCu alloys show coexistence of intergranular fracture and transgranular cleavage fracture
  - Nearly equiaxed grains on fracture surface
  - Smooth and flat grain boundaries
  - River-like cleavage steps on W particles
  - Ductile tearing of Cu phase
  - Dimples around W and Mo particles in WMoCu(M)
  - No conspicuous cracks at interfaces (good phase interface bonding)
- **600 °C**: Severe oxidation at tensile fracture
  - Dense small particles distributed on WCu fracture surface
  - Coarse particles on WMoCu(M) and WMoCu(N) fracture surfaces
  - Thicker oxide layers on WMoCu alloys' fracture surfaces
  - Some W particle fracture observed

## Load Transfer:
- In WMoCu(M), elements evenly distributed on fracture surface with no obvious agglomeration
- Good load transfer from Cu phase to W phase contributes to improved mechanical properties

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

## High-Temperature Oxidation at 600 °C during Tensile Testing:
- Severe oxidation occurs at tensile fracture surfaces at 600 °C
- **WCu alloy**: Dense small particles distributed on fracture surface; W particles more easily exposed to air due to weak Cu/W interfacial strength, which prevents further oxidation of Cu
- **WMoCu(M) and WMoCu(N)**: Coarse particles on fracture surface; thicker oxide layer; more Cu elements adhere to W particle surfaces during fracture due to higher interfacial strength from WMo solid solution
- The thicker oxide layer in WMoCu alloys is attributed to better Cu/W interfacial bonding (more Cu adheres to W particles → more Cu oxidation)
- Fracture of some W particles observed in high-temperature longitudinal sections of WMoCu alloys

## Oxidation Mechanism Discussion:
- The paper does not provide quantitative oxidation weight gain measurements
- No specific oxide layer type (e.g., Al₂O₃, Cr₂O₃, SiO₂, HfO₂, ZrO₂) was identified for this system
- No apparent discussion of high-temperature embrittlement, oxygen embrittlement, nitrogen embrittlement, or grain-boundary oxidation beyond the observation of surface oxidation during tensile testing

## High-Temperature Stability of WMo Skeleton:
- WMo solid solution exhibits elevated thermal stability
- Effectively impedes grain growth and grain boundary migration in high-temperature environments
- Enhances resistance to deformation at high temperature
- Primary determinant of high-temperature strength (Cu phase melts or evaporates during arc erosion; W skeleton governs strength)

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Mo (Molybdenum) — Nano-scale vs. Micro-scale Particles]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
W-Cu infiltration pseudo-alloy (WMoCu); W60Mo25Cu15 alloy, 25 wt.% Mo

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
25 wt.% Mo; two particle sizes: 80 nm and 3 μm

**[Main Function]**
Forms WMo solid solution transition layer at W/Cu interface to improve interfacial bonding; enhances high-temperature tensile strength; promotes densification; increases thermal stability of the W skeleton.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
Mo is completely soluble in W in all compositions (both BCC, atomic radii W: 1.41 Å vs. Mo: 1.40 Å). During infiltration, the CuNi(L) liquid phase facilitates mutual diffusion of W and Mo atoms. WMo solid solution forms at interfaces, creating a core-shell structure (~2 μm thick) around W particles. The solid solution lattice distortion hinders dislocation sliding, increasing deformation resistance. The solid solution also exhibits high thermal stability, impeding grain growth and grain boundary migration at elevated temperatures.

**[Property Effect]**
- With 25 wt.% Mo (3 μm particles): Room temperature tensile strength = 698 MPa; elongation = 2%; 600 °C tensile strength = 381 MPa
- With 25 wt.% Mo (80 nm particles): Room temperature tensile strength = 539 MPa; elongation = 0.49%; 600 °C tensile strength = 321 MPa
- Compared to W85Cu15 (no Mo): RT tensile strength = 640 MPa; elongation = 2%; 600 °C tensile strength = 242 MPa
- Micro-scale Mo particles resulted in 29.49% higher RT tensile strength and 57.44% higher 600 °C tensile strength compared to nano-scale Mo
- Micro-scale Mo particles did not diminish elongation compared to WCu alloy; nano-scale Mo drastically reduced elongation to 0.49%

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Nano-scale Mo particles (80 nm) agglomerate and grow with W particles due to high surface energy, leading to non-uniform microstructure, copper-rich areas, closed pores, and coarse pores after infiltration → reduced overall mechanical properties
- Nano-scale Mo doping significantly reduces elongation (0.49% vs. 2%)
- Mo is immiscible with Cu and may slightly impair electrical conductivity (stated in introduction; specific values not provided)

**[Original Evidence]**
Table 1; Fig. 1–3, 5–6; Conclusions (1)–(3); Sections 3.2 and 3.3

## [Ni (Nickel) — Activating Element]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
W-Cu infiltration pseudo-alloy (WMoCu); W85Cu15 and W60Mo25Cu15 alloys

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
0.5 wt.%; particle size 1 μm

**[Main Function]**
Acts as an activating element to promote wetting of W/Mo particles by liquid Cu and enhance mutual diffusion among W, Mo, and Cu during infiltration.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
Ni preferentially dissolves into Cu during infiltration (above 1083 °C) to form CuNi(L) alloy liquid phase. This melt:
- Wraps W and Mo particles, increasing their surface energy
- Provides more disordered atomic arrangement with more vacancies and defects in the liquid phase
- Improves diffusion efficiency of W and Mo into the Cu melt
- Acts as a bridge for forming diffusion channels among W-W, W-Mo, and Mo-Mo particles
- Facilitates W or Mo atom diffusion into adjacent particles through grain boundaries or bulk diffusion

**[Property Effect]**
The specific numerical contribution of Ni is not separated from that of Mo. The paper states that Ni addition reduces the surface tension between W and liquid Cu (with reference to prior work by Wang [23] and Ahangarkani [24]). The Ni-activated infiltration process resulted in WMo solid solution formation and improved interfacial bonding.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
Not explicitly discussed in the original paper. The presence of Ni in the Cu phase may affect electrical conductivity; specific effects not quantified.

**[Original Evidence]**
Section 3.2, Fig. 5; Table 1; Introduction references [19,20,23,24]

## [W (Tungsten) — Dual Particle Size]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
W-Cu infiltration pseudo-alloy (WMoCu); W85Cu15 and W60Mo25Cu15 alloys

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
85 wt.% (WCu alloy) or 60 wt.% (WMoCu alloys); two particle sizes: 6 μm and 20 μm, mixed at 1:1 mass ratio

**[Main Function]**
Provides the primary refractory skeleton for high-temperature strength and arc erosion resistance; dual particle size mixture enhances packing density.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
The 6 μm:20 μm W particle mixture (1:1) was used to achieve high density of the WMoCu alloy skeleton. The W particles form a network that is infiltrated by liquid Cu. In WMoCu alloys, WMo solid solution forms around W particles, creating a core-shell structure.

**[Property Effect]**
- The resulting W particles appear as white areas with equiaxed grains in the final microstructure
- At room temperature, fracture mechanism includes transgranular cleavage (river-like cleavage steps on W particles) and intergranular fracture
- At 600 °C, W particles may fracture during tensile testing
- Specific numerical values: Not separately quantified for W particle size effects beyond overall alloy properties

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Non-uniform W particle size distribution may lead to non-uniform microstructure (in the absence of proper mixing)
- Weak Cu/W interfacial strength in WCu alloy (without Mo) leads to W particles being more easily exposed during high-temperature fracture

## [Mo + Ni Combination]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
W-Cu infiltration pseudo-alloy (WMoCu)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
25 wt.% Mo + 0.5 wt.% Ni (with 60 wt.% W and balance Cu)

**[Main Function]**
Synergistic effect: Ni activates wetting and diffusion, enabling Mo to form WMo solid solution at W/Cu interfaces, enhancing interfacial bonding and high-temperature strength.

- Nano-scale Mo + Ni combination may lead to closed pores and copper-rich areas

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening (WMo Solid Solution)

- **Related Elements**: W, Mo
- **Typical Alloys**: WMoCu(M), WMoCu(N)
- **Key Compositions**: 25 wt.% Mo, 60 wt.% W, balance Cu
- **Microstructural Changes**: Formation of WMo solid solution at W/Cu interfaces; core-shell structure (~2 μm thick) around W particles; amorphous structure at interfaces; WMo transition layer composed of W-rich and Cu-rich solid solutions
- **Property Improvements**: 
  - Room-temperature tensile strength improved from 640 MPa (WCu) to 698 MPa (WMoCu(M), 3 μm Mo)
  - High-temperature (600 °C) tensile strength improved from 242 MPa (WCu) to 381 MPa (WMoCu(M)) — 57.44% enhancement
  - Mechanism: Solute atom dissolution distorts solid solution lattice, hindering dislocation sliding; WMo solid solution has high thermal stability, impeding grain growth and grain boundary migration
- **Risks and Limitations**: 
  - Nano-scale Mo leads to agglomeration, non-uniform distribution, copper-rich areas, reduced elongation (0.49%), and closed pores
  - Mo-Cu immiscibility may slightly impair electrical conductivity

# Key Conclusions

1. **Mo particle size effect**: Micro-scale (3 μm) Mo particle doping produces a more uniform microstructure and Mo distribution in W60Mo25Cu15(M) alloy compared to nano-scale (80 nm) Mo doping in W60Mo25Cu15(N).

2. **Interface formation**: A WMo transition layer forms at the W/Cu interface, with a core-shell structure ~2 μm thick around W particles. The interface exhibits amorphous structure, which increases deformation resistance during interfacial sliding and improves bonding strength.

3. **Ni activation**: The addition of 0.5 wt.% Ni promotes mutual diffusion among W, Mo, and Cu. The CuNi(L) liquid phase acts as a bridge connecting diffusion channels between W and Mo phases, enabling WMo solid solution formation through grain boundary and bulk diffusion.

4. **Mechanical superiority**: W60Mo25Cu15(M) achieves superior tensile strength compared to W60Mo25Cu15(N) and W85Cu15 at both room temperature and 600 °C:
   - RT: 698 MPa (WMoCu(M)) vs. 640 MPa (WCu) vs. 539 MPa (WMoCu(N))
   - 600 °C: 381 MPa (WMoCu(M)) vs. 242 MPa (WCu) vs. 321 MPa (WMoCu(N))
   - The 600 °C tensile strength increase relative to WCu: +57.44% for WMoCu(M), +32.64% for WMoCu(N)

5. **Elongation preservation**: Micro-scale Mo doping (3 μm) does not reduce elongation compared to WCu alloy (both 2%), whereas nano-scale Mo doping (80 nm) drastically reduces elongation to 0.49%.

6. **High-temperature strength origin**: The high thermal stability of the WMo solid solution predominantly determines the high-temperature strength of the alloy, as the WMo skeleton governs the material's behavior when Cu softens or melts.

7. **Fracture mechanism**: WCu and WMoCu alloys exhibit coexisting intergranular fracture and transgranular cleavage fracture at room temperature, accompanied by ductile tearing of the Cu phase.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

## Limitations of the Study (as identifiable from the original paper):

1. **No quantitative hardness or conductivity values**: Although hardness and electrical conductivity were measured (using 320HBS-3000 Brinell tester and Sigma 2008B1 conductivity meter), specific numerical values are not reported in the main text.

2. **No quantitative density values**: Relative density was measured by Archimedean method, but numerical values are not explicitly reported.

3. **No quantitative oxidation data**: Oxidation at 600 °C is discussed qualitatively (thicker oxide layer, severe oxidation, coarse vs. dense particles), but no weight gain or oxide layer thickness measurements are provided.

4. **No oxide composition analysis**: No characterization of the specific oxide phases formed at high-temperature fracture surfaces.

5. **Limited temperature range**: Mechanical testing conducted only at room temperature (25 °C) and 600 °C. Intermediate and higher temperatures (800 °C, 1000 °C, 1200 °C, 1400 °C, etc.) are not evaluated.

6. **Single strain rate tested**: Only one strain rate (5 × 10⁻⁴ /s) used for tensile testing.

7. **No creep or fatigue data**: Long-term service stability assessment (creep, fatigue, rupture life) is absent.

8. **Ni content not systematically varied**: Only 0.5 wt.% Ni was used; effects of different Ni additions are not explored within this paper (though referenced from prior literature).

9. **Electrical conductivity impact of Mo not quantified**: The paper mentions Mo may slightly impair electrical conductivity (introduction), but no specific measurements are reported.

10. **Limited particle size range**: Only two Mo particle sizes (80 nm and 3 μm) are compared; no intermediate sizes investigated.

11. **No investigation of Cu content variation**: Only one W:Mo:Cu ratio (60:25:15) is studied for Mo-doped alloys.

## Applicable Scope:

- **Alloy system**: W-Cu infiltration pseudo-alloys with Mo additions; relevant to WCu, WMoCu, and MoCu systems prepared by activated infiltration
- **Applications**: High-voltage electrical contacts, arc extinguishers, electromagnetic railgun components, rocket engine nozzles
- **Fabrication route**: Infiltration method (powder mixing → pressing → pre-sintering → Cu infiltration) with Ni activation
- **Mo particle size range**: 80 nm to 3 μm
- **W content range**: 60–85 wt.%
