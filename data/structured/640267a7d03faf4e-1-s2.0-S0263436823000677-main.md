# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** High-temperature compression behavior of bimodal α-Mo structured Mo-Si-B alloy
- **Authors:** Rui Li, Xuan Chen, Juan Wang, Bin Li, Fuxue Yan, Guojun Zhang
- **Journal:** International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year:** 2023 (Volume 112, article 106167, available online 28 February 2023)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106167
- **Alloy system studied:** Mo-12Si-8.5B (at.%) with 0.57 wt% La₂O₃ — a Mo-Si-B multiphase refractory alloy
- **Research objective:** To investigate the effect of bimodal (micron/submicron) α-Mo structure on the high-temperature compressive deformation behavior and microstructural evolution of a fine-grained Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La₂O₃ alloy at 1000–1400 °C
- **Material category:** Mo-based refractory multiphase alloy (α-Mo + Mo₃Si + Mo₅SiB₂ with La₂O₃ oxide dispersion)

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper investigates two microstructural variants of the same nominal composition **Mo-12Si-8.5B (at.%) + 0.57 wt% La₂O₃**:

1. **Fine-grained (FG/unimodal) alloy** — all α-Mo grains in the submicron range, fabricated by high-energy ball milling (HEBM) followed by hot pressing (HP) sintering.
2. **Bimodal alloy** — α-Mo matrix with a bimodal micron/submicron grain structure (fine-grained regions + coarse-grained α-Mo regions of ~3.06 μm size, 16.8 vol%), fabricated by blending HEBMed FG Mo-Si-B-La₂O₃ powder (85 wt%) with unmilled coarse Mo-La₂O₃ powder (15 wt%), followed by HP sintering.

The three constituent phases are:
- α-Mo (BCC solid solution matrix)
- Mo₃Si (A15 intermetallic)
- Mo₅SiB₂ (T2 intermetallic)

The research objective is to understand how the bimodal α-Mo grain structure affects compressive strength, yield strength, dynamic softening resistance, deformation mechanisms, dynamic recovery/recrystallization behavior, and the strengthening role of La₂O₃ nanoparticles at 1000–1400 °C.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Fine-grained (FG/unimodal) alloy fabrication:**
1. Mo-La₂O₃, Si, and B powders weighed according to the alloy composition.
2. Powders mixed evenly.
3. High-energy ball milling (HEBM) in a planetary ball mill (PM 400, Retsch, Germany) under Ar atmosphere using WC balls (Φ 18.5 mm and Φ 9.6 mm), rotational speed 250 rpm, duration 20 h, ball-to-powder mass ratio 10:1.
4. Hot pressing (HP) sintering at 1600 °C for 3 h under 49 MPa pressure in vacuum (< 10⁻³ Pa) using a hot pressure sintering furnace (VHP 2000, Shenyang Weitai Technology Development Co., LTD, China).

**Bimodal alloy fabrication:**
1. Mo-La₂O₃ powder divided into two parts with mass ratio 85:15.
2. 85 wt% Mo-La₂O₃ powder mixed with Si and B powder, followed by HEBM (same conditions as above) to prepare FG Mo-Si-B-La₂O₃ powder.
3. The remaining 15 wt% Mo-La₂O₃ powder treated as CG powder (no HEBM).
4. Unmilled CG Mo-La₂O₃ powder mixed with HEBMed FG Mo-Si-B-La₂O₃ powder evenly.
5. HP sintering at 1600 °C for 3 h under 49 MPa in vacuum.

**Mixing process (below HEBM intensity):** planetary ball mill (QM3-sp2, Nanjing University Instrument Factory, China) under Ar atmosphere with agate balls (Φ 6 mm), 400 rpm for 6.5 h, ball-to-powder mass ratio 1:1.

**Post-processing heat treatment:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper. The alloys were tested in the as-HPed condition without apparent additional homogenization, annealing, or aging treatments.

**Compression test specimen preparation:** Cylindrical specimens 5.5 mm diameter × 11 mm height machined from as-HPed bulk by wire-cut electro-discharge machining.

**Compression testing conditions:**
- Equipment: Gleeble-1500D thermo-mechanical simulator (DSI, America)
- Temperatures: 1000 °C, 1200 °C, 1400 °C
- Strain rate: 10⁻³ s⁻¹
- True strain: 0.3 (30%)
- Heating rate: 10 °C/s
- Pre-test soaking: 5 min at test temperature for uniform temperature field
- Atmosphere: vacuum below 10 Pa
- Cooling after testing: immediate water quench to room temperature

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**As-HPed (initial) microstructures:**

*FG (unimodal) alloy:*
- Submicron-scale α-Mo matrix with submicron Mo₃Si/T2 particles dispersed within.
- α-Mo grain size: 0.51 μm
- Mo₃Si grain size: 0.42 μm
- T2 grain size: 0.40 μm
- All three phases have grain sizes below 2 μm.
- Volume fractions: α-Mo 46.3 vol%, Mo₃Si 28.4 vol%, T2 25.3 vol%
- La₂O₃ nanoparticles mainly distributed at grain boundaries, partially within grain interiors.

*Bimodal alloy:*
- α-Mo matrix with micrometer/submicrometer bimodal structure.
- Submicron Mo₃Si/T2 particles dispersed within the bimodal α-Mo matrix.
- FG α-Mo grain size: 0.62 μm
- CG α-Mo grain size: 3.06 μm (grains above 2 μm)
- Mo₃Si grain size: 0.54 μm
- T2 grain size: 0.52 μm
- CG α-Mo volume fraction: 16.8 vol%
- FG α-Mo volume fraction: 29.6 vol%
- Total α-Mo volume fraction: 46.4 vol% (similar to FG alloy)
- Volume fractions: Mo₃Si 29.1 vol%, T2 24.5 vol%
- Coarse equiaxial α-Mo grains in CG regions with some La₂O₃ nanoparticles inside CG grains
- Most nano-scale La₂O₃ particles at grain boundaries of FG regions, a small part in grain interiors of FG regions.

**La₂O₃ distribution:**
- Intragranular and intergranular nano-scale La₂O₃ particles present.
- In FG alloy: mainly at grain boundaries, partially within grain interiors.
- In bimodal alloy: some in CG α-Mo grain interiors, most at grain boundaries of FG regions.

**Deformed microstructures:**

At 1000 °C (~15% strain):
- All three phases in both alloys remain equiaxed grains with little grain size change.
- CG α-Mo grains in bimodal alloy show higher LAGB frequency (41.04%) compared to α-Mo in FG alloy (12.45%).
- Most CG α-Mo grains are deformed grains, some are substructured; FG α-Mo, Mo₃Si, T2 remain largely undeformed.
- Brittle fracture at ~20% strain for both alloys.

At 1200 °C (~30% strain):
- FG α-Mo, Mo₃Si, T2 grains remain equiaxed with little grain size change.
- CG α-Mo grains deform obviously, forming rod-like structures.
- LAGB frequency significantly increased in all phases compared to 1000 °C.
- CG α-Mo LAGB frequency (56.05%) higher than FG α-Mo (38.56%).
- Most rod-like CG α-Mo grains are substructured; small part deformed and recrystallized.
- TEM evidence: high density of closely spaced dislocations in CG α-Mo, dislocation tangles and networks; Mo₃Si shows short dislocations forming walls and subgrain boundaries (dynamic recovery); T2 shows neatly arranged short dislocations without walls.

At 1400 °C (~30% strain):
- FG α-Mo grains slight deformation only.
- CG α-Mo grains: part equiaxed, part elongated; grain size significantly increased, indicating recrystallization and grain growth.
- FG Mo₃Si and T2 remain equiaxed with little grain size change.
- CG α-Mo LAGB frequency 64.57% (highest recorded).
- CG α-Mo grains mainly deformed and substructured, less recrystallized grains.
- Evidence of secondary deformation of recrystallized CG α-Mo grains enhancing work hardening.

**EBSD classification (definitions used):**
- LAGBs: 2° ≤ θ < 15° misorientation
- HAGBs: θ ≥ 15° misorientation
- Recrystallized grains: adjacent-grain orientation difference < 1°
- Substructured grains: orientation difference 1–7.5°
- Deformed grains: orientation difference > 7.5°

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed:** grain-boundary strengthening (Hall-Petch type), solid-solution strengthening (noted for Si and B in α-Mo from literature), Orowan dispersion strengthening by La₂O₃ nanoparticles, work hardening, dynamic recovery/recrystallization-related softening resistance.

# Mechanical Properties

**Compressive properties at 1000–1400 °C (strain rate 10⁻³ s⁻¹):**

| Temperature | Sample | Compressive Strength | Yield Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 °C | FG | Higher than bimodal | Higher than bimodal | Both alloys brittle fracture at ~20% strain, peak stress at 14–15.4% strain |
| 1000 °C | Bimodal | Lower than FG | Lower than FG | CG α-Mo weakens grain boundary strengthening |
| 1200 °C | FG | Lower than bimodal | Lower than bimodal | Both alloys do not fracture up to 30% strain; peak stress at 8.2–8.3% strain |
| 1200 °C | Bimodal | Higher than FG | Higher than FG | Stronger work-hardening; stronger dynamic softening resistance |
| 1400 °C | FG | Lower than bimodal | Lower than bimodal | Peak stress at ~15% strain |
| 1400 °C | Bimodal | Higher than FG | Higher than FG | Peak stress at ~10% strain |

**Specific quantitative values for compressive strength and yield strength are presented only graphically in Fig. 4 of the original paper; exact numerical values are not extracted from the text.** However, the paper explicitly states:

> "Especially at 1400 °C, compared to the FG alloy, the compressive strength and yield strength of bimodal alloy are increased by 55.2% and 43.6%."

**Key property observations:**
- Flow stress increases initially, reaches peak, then gradually decreases to either fracture or steady state at all temperatures.
- At 1000 °C: brittle fracture at ~20% strain. Peak stress at 14–15.4% strain.
- At 1200 °C: peak stress at 8.2–8.3% strain, then gradual decrease. Bimodal alloy has lower stress-decreasing rate (stronger dynamic softening resistance).
- At 1400 °C: peak stress at ~15% (FG) and ~10% (bimodal) strain; peak stress maintained for longer strain before obvious decrease (good dynamic softening resistance). Bimodal alloy shows stronger work-hardening.
- With increasing deformation temperature, peak stress decreases due to higher softening rate.

**Room-temperature properties (from previous study, reference [13]):**
- Fracture toughness of bimodal alloys: 11.3–12.5 MPa·m^(1/2) (improved significantly vs. FG ~9 MPa·m^(1/2))
- Compression strength was not decreased dramatically.

**Other properties (not measured in this work):**
- Hardness, elastic modulus, density, creep rupture life: Not reported in the original paper.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

### Si + B (combined)

**Applicable Alloy System:** Mo-Si-B (Mo-12Si-8.5B at.%)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 12 at.% Si and 8.5 at.% B (nominal)

**Main Function:** Formation of Mo₃Si (A15) and Mo₅SiB₂ (T2) intermetallic phases that provide high-temperature strength, creep resistance, and oxidation resistance through formation of protective borosilicate scale.

**Microstructural Mechanism:** Si and B react with Mo to form Mo₃Si and T2 intermetallics distributed in α-Mo matrix. In the as-HPed state, these phases occupy 28.4–29.1 vol% (Mo₃Si) and 24.5–25.3 vol% (T2). The intermetallics provide physical obstruction to slip and increase flow stress. From cited literature (Jain et al.): solid solution of small levels of Si and B in Mo promotes compressive strength at 1000–1200 °C via solid solution strengthening. At 1000 °C both Mo₃Si and T2 remain largely undeformed; at 1200–1400 °C they begin to undergo deformation and dynamic recovery.

**Property Effect:** The three-phase composite (α-Mo + Mo₃Si + T2) provides high-temperature strength. The Mo₃Si and T2 phases show lower deformability than α-Mo; T2 does not form dislocation walls at 1200 °C while Mo₃Si undergoes dynamic recovery. Literature cited: T2 provides physical obstruction to slip, increasing flow stress of alloys at 1000–1400 °C.

**Potential Side Effects:** Mo₃Si and T2 intermetallics are inherently brittle at low temperatures; the overall alloy exhibits brittle fracture at 1000 °C at ~20% compression strain. The intermetallics limit dislocation storage capability in fine grains, reducing room-temperature ductility and fracture toughness (~9 MPa·m^(1/2) for FG alloy).

**Original Evidence:** Table 1 (phase volume fractions and grain sizes); Fig. 1 (phase distribution); Fig. 12 (Mo₃Si and T2 deformation at 1200 °C); Introduction paragraphs citing Jain et al. [14] and Alur et al. [15].

### La₂O₃ (La addition as oxide dispersion)

**Applicable Alloy System:** Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La₂O₃

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 0.57 wt% La₂O₃ (in final alloy); starting Mo-La₂O₃ powder contains 0.6 wt% La₂O₃

**Main Function:** Oxide dispersion strengthening (ODS) via nano-scale La₂O₃ particles that impede dislocation slip and pin grain boundaries. Improves high-temperature strength and reduces creep rate.

**Microstructural Mechanism:**
- **Intragranular La₂O₃ particles:** interact with dislocations via Orowan bypass mechanism, impeding dislocation slip effectively, increasing α-Mo matrix strength. Intragranular La₂O₃ particles in CG α-Mo regions provide more obvious Orowan strengthening than in FG α-Mo regions due to better dislocation storage ability and higher dislocation density in CG α-Mo.
- **Intergranular La₂O₃ particles:** pin grain boundaries, inhibiting grain boundary sliding deformation. The strengthening effect of La₂O₃ on grain boundaries is more obvious in FG alloy (higher grain boundary density) than in bimodal alloy.
- La₂O₃ also provides Orowan strengthening in Mo₃Si and T2 intermetallics, but this effect is weaker than in α-Mo.

**Property Effect:** Nano-scale La₂O₃ particles effectively strengthen the α-Mo matrix and reduce creep rate of the alloy. No specific numerical strengthening values are provided in the original paper; the effect is demonstrated qualitatively via TEM observation of dislocation-particle interactions (Figs. 12 and 13). Specific quantitative strengthening contribution: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**Potential Side Effects:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper. No adverse effects on workability, ductility, or fracture toughness are discussed. Literature reference [16] mentions Zr forming Mo₂Zr/ZrO₂ particles can strengthen α-Mo matrix by pinning dislocations; La₂O₃ is analogous.

**Original Evidence:** Figs. 2, 12, 13 (TEM images of La₂O₃-dislocation and La₂O₃-grain boundary interactions); Discussion section 4: "The strengthening effect of La₂O₃ particles on the Mo-Si-B alloy includes the interaction between La₂O₃ particles and dislocation as well as the interaction between La₂O₃ particles and grain boundaries."

### Bimodal α-Mo grain structure (microstructural design element, not elemental addition)

**Applicable Alloy System:** Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La₂O₃

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** CG α-Mo regions: 3.06 μm average grain size, 16.8 vol% of total alloy; FG α-Mo regions: 0.62 μm average grain size, 29.6 vol% of total alloy.

**Main Function:** Improve fracture toughness without dramatic strength loss at room temperature; provide high-temperature strengthening above 1200 °C by reducing grain boundary sliding and enhancing work hardening; improve dynamic softening resistance.

**Microstructural Mechanism:**
- At 1000 °C: CG α-Mo regions weaken grain boundary strengthening (fewer grain boundaries), lowering strength compared to FG alloy.
- At 1200–1400 °C: CG α-Mo regions exhibit less grain boundary sliding deformation than FG regions; when grain boundary sliding becomes the main deformation mode at high temperatures, the fewer boundaries in CG α-Mo provide relative strengthening.
- CG α-Mo regions preferentially undergo plastic deformation, dynamic recovery, and recrystallization, bearing the main plastic deformation and delaying dynamic softening of FG α-Mo, Mo₃Si, and T2.
- Large plastic deformation of CG α-Mo induces significant work hardening, further strengthening the alloy.
- At 1400 °C, recrystallized CG α-Mo grains can undergo secondary deformation, contributing to additional work hardening.

**Property Effect:** At 1400 °C, compressive strength increased by 55.2% and yield strength by 43.6% compared to FG alloy. At 1200–1400 °C, bimodal alloy shows stronger dynamic softening resistance and work-hardening capacity. Room-temperature fracture toughness improved to 11.3–12.5 MPa·m^(1/2) (from previous study [13]). At 1000 °C, strength is lower than FG alloy due to weakened grain boundary strengthening.

**Potential Side Effects:** At 1000 °C, compressive strength and yield strength are lower than FG alloy due to weakened grain boundary strengthening. Both alloys undergo brittle fracture at ~20% strain at 1000 °C.

**Original Evidence:** Table 1; Figs. 3, 4, 6–16; Conclusions 1–3; Discussion section 4.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

### 1. Grain-Boundary Strengthening (Hall-Petch type)

**Related Elements:** N/A (microstructural effect)

**Typical Alloys:** FG Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La₂O₃; bimodal variant with CG α-Mo regions

**Key Compositions:** α-Mo grain size 0.51 μm (FG) vs. 0.62 μm (FG regions) + 3.06 μm (CG regions) in bimodal alloy.

**Microstructural Changes:** FG alloy contains higher density of grain boundaries than bimodal alloy. At low temperature (1000 °C), grain boundaries impede dislocation slip effectively, strengthening the alloy.

**Property Improvements:** At 1000 °C, FG alloy exhibits higher compressive strength and yield strength than bimodal alloy due to more effective grain boundary strengthening.

**Risks and Limitations:** At 1200–1400 °C, thermal activation enhances grain boundary sliding; grain boundary strengthening effect disappears. FG alloy with more grain boundaries becomes weaker at high temperatures due to greater grain boundary sliding deformation. At 1000 °C, dislocation pile-up at grain boundaries causes stress concentration, leading to crack formation and brittle fracture at ~20% strain.

### 2. Work Hardening (dislocation accumulation)

**Related Elements:** N/A (strain-induced)

**Typical Alloys:** Bimodal Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La₂O₃

**Key Compositions:** CG α-Mo regions (3.06 μm, 16.8 vol%) capable of large plastic deformation.

**Microstructural Changes:** CG α-Mo grains undergo large plastic deformation with densely spaced dislocations, tangles, and networks. At 1400 °C, deformed CG α-Mo undergoes dynamic recovery/recrystallization, then secondary deformation further enhances work hardening.

**Property Improvements:** At 1200 °C and 1400 °C, bimodal alloy exhibits stronger work hardening than FG alloy. This contributes to higher compressive and yield strengths.

**Risks and Limitations:** Work hardening contribution is limited by dynamic recovery/recrystallization at high temperatures.

### 3. Oxide Dispersion Strengthening (ODS by La₂O₃)

**Related Elements:** La, O (as La₂O₃)

**Typical Alloys:** Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La₂O₃ (both FG and bimodal)

**Key Compositions:** 0.57 wt% La₂O₃; nano-scale particle size; distribution at grain boundaries and within grains.

**Microstructural Changes:** Intragranular La₂O₃ particles impede dislocation slip via Orowan mechanism; intergranular La₂O₃ particles pin grain boundaries, inhibiting grain boundary sliding.

**Property Improvements:** Strengthens α-Mo matrix and reduces creep rate. The Orowan strengthening effect is more obvious in CG α-Mo regions (better dislocation storage ability, higher dislocation density). The grain boundary pinning effect is more obvious in FG alloy (more grain boundaries). Quantitative values: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**Risks and Limitations:** The Orowan strengthening in Mo₃Si and T2 intermetallics is weaker than in α-Mo due to their lower dislocation densities.

### 4. Solid-Solution Strengthening (cited literature)

**Related Elements:** Si, B (in Mo matrix); Zr (cited from literature)

**Typical Alloys:** Mo-Si-B solid solution alloy; Mo-9Si-8B with Zr addition

**Key Compositions:** Small levels of Si and B in solid solution in α-Mo; 1 at.% Zr in Mo-9Si-8B

**Microstructural Changes:** Si and B in solid solution provide solid solution strengthening at 1000–1200 °C (Jain et al.). Zr prevents SiO₂ formation on grain boundaries, enhancing grain boundary strength, and forms Mo₂Zr/ZrO₂ nanoparticles that pin dislocations (Hochmuth et al.).

**Property Improvements:** Si and B increase compressive strength at 1000–1200 °C. Zr addition decreases creep rate of Mo-9Si-8B by one order of magnitude.

**Risks and Limitations:** Alloying with W did not achieve as good strengthening as Nb (Schneibel et al.). Zr addition may influence phase stability; specific risks for the Mo-Si-B-La₂O₃ system not discussed.

### 5. Grain Boundary Sliding Resistance (high-temperature strengthening)

**Related Elements:** N/A (microstructural effect)

**Typical Alloys:** Bimodal Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La₂O₃

**Key Compositions:** Bimodal α-Mo structure with CG regions (3.06 μm, 16.8 vol%) + FG regions (0.62 μm, 29.6 vol%).

**Microstructural Changes:** At 1200–1400 °C, grain boundary sliding becomes the main deformation mode due to increased thermal activation. CG α-Mo regions exhibit less grain boundary sliding than FG regions.

**Property Improvements:** Higher yield and compressive strength of bimodal alloy at 1200–1400 °C; increased by 55.2% (compressive strength) and 43.6% (yield strength) at 1400 °C relative to FG alloy.

**Risks and Limitations:** Effect is temperature-dependent; at 1000 °C where grain boundary sliding is not dominant, the benefits do not apply and the bimodal alloy is actually weaker.

### 6. Dynamic Softening Resistance (deformation/recrystallization-mediated)

**Related Elements:** N/A (microstructural effect)

**Typical Alloys:** Bimodal Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La₂O₃

**Key Compositions:** CG α-Mo regions serve as preferential deformation sites.

**Microstructural Changes:** CG α-Mo regions preferentially undergo plastic deformation, dynamic recovery, and dynamic recrystallization. This delays the occurrence of dynamic softening in FG α-Mo, Mo₃Si, and T2 regions.

**Property Improvements:** Bimodal alloy maintains stress for longer strain at 1200–1400 °C; lower decreasing rate of stress after peak at 1200 °C. Good dynamic softening resistance at 1200–1400 °C.

**Risks and Limitations:** As temperature increases from 1000 °C to 1400 °C, dynamic recovery and recrystallization of CG α-Mo becomes easier; more FG Mo₃Si and T2 grains undergo deformation and dynamic recovery.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Temperature-dependent strength inversion:** At 1000 °C, the FG alloy has higher compressive strength and yield strength than the bimodal alloy due to more effective grain boundary strengthening. At 1200 °C and 1400 °C, the bimodal alloy is stronger because CG α-Mo regions exhibit less grain boundary sliding deformation.

2. **Significant strength improvement at 1400 °C:** The bimodal alloy shows 55.2% higher compressive strength and 43.6% higher yield strength than the FG alloy at 1400 °C.

3. **Deformation sequence:** In the bimodal alloy at 1000–1400 °C, the CG α-Mo regions preferentially undergo plastic deformation, dynamic recovery, and recrystallization, bearing the main plastic deformation. The FG α-Mo regions are easier to deform than FG Mo₃Si and T2 regions.

4. **Work hardening contribution:** The large plastic deformation of CG α-Mo regions in the bimodal alloy provides additional work hardening at 1200–1400 °C, particularly evident at 1400 °C where secondary deformation of recrystallized CG α-Mo grains further enhances work hardening.

5. **La₂O₃ strengthening:** Nano-scale La₂O₃ particles strengthen the alloy by both impeding dislocation slip (Orowan mechanism) and pinning grain boundaries. Orowan strengthening is more effective in CG α-Mo regions; grain boundary pinning is more effective in FG alloys.

6. **Dynamic softening resistance:** The bimodal alloy exhibits good dynamic softening resistance at 1200–1400 °C because preferential deformation of CG α-Mo regions delays dynamic softening of FG α-Mo, Mo₃Si, and T2 regions.

7. **Temperature effect on intermetallics:** As temperature increases from 1000 °C to 1400 °C, FG Mo₃Si and T2 become easier to deform and undergo dynamic recovery. T2 does not form dislocation walls at 1200 °C while Mo₃Si does.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Single composition:** Only Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La₂O₃ is studied; the conclusions may not directly apply to other Mo-Si-B compositions or other refractory alloy systems.
- **Limited microstructural variants:** Only two microstructures (FG unimodal and one bimodal variant with 15 wt% unmilled powder) are compared. The effect of different CG α-Mo volume fractions or grain sizes is not explored.
- **Compression-only testing:** Results are limited to compressive deformation behavior. Tensile properties, creep life, fatigue behavior, and fracture toughness (at high temperature) are not measured.
- **Strain rate limitation:** Only one strain rate (10⁻³ s⁻¹) is investigated; the strain rate sensitivity of the bimodal structure effect is not addressed.
- **Short true strain:** Compression tests were terminated at εt = 0.3; long-term steady-state creep or complete fracture behavior is not captured.
- **Limited temperature range:** Tested at 1000, 1200, and 1400 °C; performance at lower (room temperature) or higher temperatures is inferred from literature or previous studies rather than directly tested.
- **Quantitative strength values:** The paper presents strength comparisons graphically (Fig. 4) but does not provide tabulated numerical values for all data points. Only percentage improvements at 1400 °C are explicitly stated.
- **Oxidation behavior:** Oxidation resistance is not systematically studied; only a thin surface scale is incidentally observed after 1400 °C testing. The high-temperature oxidation behavior of these specific alloys under service-like conditions is not characterized.
- **Atmosphere effects:** Compression tests performed in vacuum (<10 Pa), not in air or under service-like atmospheres; oxidation contribution during compression is minimized.
- **Elastic modulus, hardness, density, and fracture toughness (high-temperature):** Not reported in the original paper.
