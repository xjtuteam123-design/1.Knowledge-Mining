# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phases**: FCC (face-centered cubic) + B2 (ordered bcc, based on NiAl-type ordering) — dual-phase structure.
- **As-cast microstructure**: 
  - Lamellar regions (region A): repeating FCC (green in EBSD) and B2 (red in EBSD) phases.
  - Very fine clusters indexed as B2 (region B): finely intertwined mixture of B2 phases due to spinodal decomposition.
- **Cryo-rolled microstructure (65% reduction)**:
  - Nanocrystalline structure with elongated morphology.
  - Inclined shear bands and fragmented regions.
  - Dense dislocation structures.
  - Bundles of parallel nano-twins (average thickness ~ 10 nm).
  - Extensive stacking faults (SFs), confirming low stacking fault energy (SFE) of the FCC phase.
  - Nanoscale lamellar structure of the FCC subdivided by extended boundaries.
- **800 °C / 1 h annealed**:
  - Lamellar morphology retained.
  - B2 phase contains low-angle boundaries (LABs, 2° ≤ LABs ≤ 15°).
  - FCC phase contains high-angle boundaries (HABs > 15°) and annealing twin boundaries (TBs).
  - High KAM values indicate unrecrystallized state.
  - Grain size: 0.60 ± 0.1 µm.
  - Phase fractions: FCC 0.46 ± 0.03; B2 0.52 ± 0.03.
  - KAM average: 0.72; KAM ≤ 1° fraction: 0.79.
- **900 °C / 1 h annealed**:
  - Heterogeneous microstructure with surviving lamellar regions and transformed microduplex regions.
  - Lamellar region: grain size 0.84 µm; FCC 0.47; B2 0.52; KAM average 0.70; KAM ≤ 1° fraction 0.80.
  - Microduplex region: grain size 0.74 µm; FCC 0.35; B2 0.62; KAM average 0.83; KAM ≤ 1° fraction 0.66.
  - Overall: grain size 0.81 ± 0.10 µm; FCC 0.42 ± 0.04; B2 0.55 ± 0.01; KAM average 0.75; KAM ≤ 1° fraction 0.76.
  - Microduplex region shows smaller grain size and higher B2 fraction than the lamellar region.
  - Higher KAM in the microduplex region indicates higher defect density and internal misorientation.
- **1000 °C / 1 h annealed**:
  - Fully microduplex structure.
  - Predominantly low KAM areas.
  - Grain size: 0.98 ± 0.1 µm.
  - Phase fractions: FCC 0.54 ± 0.03; B2 0.42 ± 0.02.
  - KAM average: 0.60; KAM ≤ 1° fraction: 0.92.
- **Phase fraction trend with annealing temperature**:
  - FCC fraction decreases up to 900 °C but increases considerably at 1000 °C.
  - B2 fraction increases at lower temperatures but shows a reversal in trend beyond 900 °C.
  - This is attributed to the increased stability of the FCC phase at higher annealing temperatures.
- **Grain size trend**: Minor increase in grain size with increasing temperature.
- **Second phases / precipitates**: Not reported (no carbides, oxides, or intermetallic precipitates beyond the B2 phase).
- **Elemental segregation**: Not explicitly discussed; the B2 and FCC phases constitute the phase-separated structure.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed**: 
  - Heterogeneous microstructure strengthening (mechanical incompatibility between hard B2 and soft FCC domains).
  - Back-stress strengthening.
  - Strain-hardening enhancement via deformation twins.
  - Grain refinement / ultrafine-grained structure.
  - Not discussed: solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, dispersion strengthening, ODS strengthening, grain-boundary strengthening as a specifically named mechanism (grain refinement is implicit).

## Processing Route Card: Moderate Cryo-rolling + Annealing

[Element or Element Combination]
Not applicable — this is a processing route, not an element.

[Applicable Alloy System]
AlCrFe₂Ni₂ high entropy alloy (FCC + B2 dual-phase). Also applicable to other dual-phase HEAs with low-SFE FCC phases.

[Addition Amount or Composition Range]
65% cryo-rolling reduction followed by annealing at 800–1000 °C for 1 hour.

[Main Function]
Moderate cryo-rolling at cryogenic temperatures accelerates structural evolution (grain subdivision into nanocrystalline structure) compared to room-temperature rolling, enabling a nanocrystalline lamellar structure at only 65% reduction.

[Microstructural Mechanism]
- During cryo-rolling, the low SFE of the FCC phase restricts cross-slip, leading to faster generation and preservation of dislocations.
- This results in grain subdivision: accumulation of dislocations → systematic increment in misorientation → transformation of LABs into HABs.
- The nanocrystalline structure with profuse nucleation sites (HABs, shear bands, heterogeneities) ensures ultrafine microstructures after annealing.
- Differential strain distribution during cryo-rolling (due to softer FCC and harder B2) creates differential driving force for transformation during annealing.

[Property Effect]
- The 900 °C / 1 h annealed specimen achieves superior strength-ductility synergy: YS 738 MPa, TS 1153 MPa, elongation ~21%.
- This is superior to other cobalt-free HEAs (references [5–10]).
- Cryo-rolling increases strength but reduces ductility; annealing restores ductility but compromises strength. The intermediate 900 °C annealing achieves the best balance.

[Potential Side Effects]
Direct cryo-rolled material has reduced ductility (numerical values not provided).

[Original Evidence]
"The low SFE of the FCC phase (restricting cross-slip) and faster generation and preservation of dislocations result in an accelerated structural evolution so that a nanocrystalline structure is obtained at a moderate deformation during cryo-rolling." Also: "The remarkably heterogeneous microstructure at the intermediate annealing temperature consisted of a mix of lamellar and microduplex domains and showed strength-ductility synergy superior to other cobalt-free HEAs."

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Mechanism: Heterogeneous Microstructure Strengthening (Mechanical Incompatibility + Back-Stress)

- **Related elements**: Al, Ni (B2 phase); Fe, Ni (FCC phase). Not element-specific; the mechanism arises from the dual-phase structure.
- **Typical alloys**: AlCrFe₂Ni₂ (this study); AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ eutectic HEA (reference [4]); other dual-phase HEAs.
- **Key compositions**: AlCrFe₂Ni₂ with B2 fraction ~0.55 (overall) and ~0.62 (microduplex region) at 900 °C anneal.
- **Microstructural changes**: 
  - Heterogeneous microstructure with lamellar and microduplex domains having different phase fractions, grain sizes, deformation states, and hardness.
  - The microduplex region has a higher B2 fraction (0.62) and smaller grain size (0.74 µm) than the lamellar region (B2 0.52; grain size 0.84 µm).
  - Higher KAM in the microduplex region (0.83) than the lamellar region (0.70).
- **Property improvements**:
  - YS 738 MPa, TS 1153 MPa, elongation ~21% at 900 °C anneal.
  - Enhanced strain-hardening rate with multistage recovery.
  - Long-range back-stress resisting yielding of the softer phase.
- **Risks and limitations**:
  - Mechanical incompatibility between hard B2 and soft FCC domains creates strain gradients that must be accommodated at domain interfaces/boundaries.
  - The original paper does not mention specific risks beyond this.

## Mechanism: Grain Refinement / Ultrafine-Grained Structure via Cryo-Rolling and Annealing

- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; related to the low SFE of the FCC phase (Fe-Ni-rich).
- **Typical alloys**: AlCrFe₂Ni₂ (this study); AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ (reference [4]).
- **Key compositions**: 65% cryo-rolled AlCrFe₂Ni₂, annealed at 800–1000 °C.
- **Microstructural changes**:
  - Cryo-rolling produces a nanocrystalline lamellar structure with nano-twins (~10 nm thickness) and extensive stacking faults.
  - Annealing at 800 °C produces an ultrafine lamellar structure (grain size 0.60 ± 0.1 µm).
  - Annealing at 900 °C produces a heterogeneous ultrafine structure (grain size 0.74–0.84 µm).
  - Annealing at 1000 °C produces a fully microduplex ultrafine structure (grain size 0.98 ± 0.1 µm).
- **Property improvements**:
  - Cryo-rolling increases strength but reduces ductility.
  - Annealing restores ductility while retaining fine grain size.
  - The 900 °C annealed sample achieves the best strength-ductility combination (YS 738 MPa, TS 1153 MPa, elongation ~21%).
- **Risks and limitations**:
  - Direct cryo-rolled material has reduced ductility (numerical values not provided).
  - Prolonged or higher-temperature annealing leads to grain growth and loss of strength.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Cryo-rolling (65% reduction) of AlCrFe₂Ni₂ HEA** produces a nanocrystalline lamellar structure with profuse nano-twins (average thickness ~10 nm) and stacking faults, confirming the low stacking fault energy of the FCC phase.

2. **Progressive microstructural transformation with annealing**:
   - 800 °C / 1 h: lamellar structure retained, unrecrystallized (high KAM).
   - 900 °C / 1 h: heterogeneous microstructure with surviving lamellar and transformed microduplex regions.
   - 1000 °C / 1 h: fully microduplex structure.

3. **The 900 °C / 1 h annealed specimen exhibits superior strength-ductility synergy**:
   - Yield strength: 738 MPa
   - Ultimate tensile strength: 1153 MPa
   - Elongation: ~21%
   - This is superior to other cobalt-free HEAs and superior to the as-cast material (YS ~633 MPa, TS ~1092 MPa, elongation ~14%).

4. **Mechanism of superior properties**: The heterogeneous microstructure (mixed lamellar + microduplex domains with different phase fractions, grain sizes, deformation states, and hardness) leads to mechanical incompatibility, back-stress strengthening, and enhanced strain-hardening with multistage recovery.

5. **Moderate cryo-rolling is an effective processing route** for tailoring heterogeneous microstructure and mechanical properties in cobalt-free dual-phase HEAs.
