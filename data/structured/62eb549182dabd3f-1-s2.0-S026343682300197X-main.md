# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Thermal stability of (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 and (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 refractory high entropy alloys with a bcc + B2 structure
- **Authors:** E. Panina, N. Yurchenko, G. Salishchev, S. Zherebtsov, N. Stepanov
- **Journal:** International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106297
- **Alloy system studied:** (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 and (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs)
- **Material category:** Refractory high-entropy alloy / refractory multi-principal element alloy with bcc + B2 dual-phase structure
- **Research objective:** To examine the thermal stability (long-term annealing at 1000 °C for 700 h) of dual-phase bcc + B2 refractory high entropy alloys based on ductile B2 CoHf matrix reinforced by bcc NbMo particles.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system:** (HfCo)100-x(NbMo)x pseudo-binary refractory high-entropy alloy system. Two compositions studied:
  - (HfCo)90(NbMo)10: nominally 45 at.% Hf, 45 at.% Co, 5 at.% Nb, 5 at.% Mo
  - (HfCo)75(NbMo)25: nominally 37.5 at.% Hf, 37.5 at.% Co, 12.5 at.% Nb, 12.5 at.% Mo
- **Design concept:** Ductile B2 CoHf compound as matrix reinforced by hard bcc NbMo phase, rather than conventional approach where B2 is the brittle ordered phase and bcc is the matrix.
- **Material category:** Refractory high-entropy alloy with bcc + B2 dual-phase structure; not an ODS alloy; not additively manufactured.

- **Melting method:** Vacuum arc melting using pure elements
- **Initial condition:** As-cast
- **Heat treatment:** Long-term annealing at 1000 °C for 700 h
  - Samples encapsulated in vacuumed quartz ampules
  - Placed in pre-heated Nabertherm furnace
  - Air-cooled after annealing

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**As-cast microstructures:**
- **(HfCo)90(NbMo)10:** B2 matrix grains + bcc/B2 eutectic regions. B2:bcc ratio ≈ 8:1. Additional small amount (~2%) of Nb-rich bcc phase at B2 grains/eutectic interfaces.
- **(HfCo)75(NbMo)25:** Mostly bcc + B2 eutectic structure with some primary bcc particles. B2:bcc ratio ≈ 7:3. Nanoscale bcc/B2 precipitates already present in as-cast state. Some Nb-rich bcc particles detected.
- **Orientation relationship:** "Cube-on-cube" (110)bcc||(110)B2, [001]bcc||[001]B2

**After annealing at 1000 °C for 700 h:**

**(HfCo)90(NbMo)10:**
- No new phases detected by XRD. Only bcc and B2 phases remain.
- B2 matrix grains (bright in SEM-BSE) + bcc/B2 eutectic regions (bcc dark-grey, Nb/Mo-rich)
- Nb-rich bcc particles and Mo-rich areas observed
- Nanoscale precipitates formed:
  - bcc precipitates inside B2 matrix: size = 12 nm, volume fraction = 30%
  - B2 precipitates inside bcc phase: mostly finer, mean size ~4 nm
  - Coarser B2 precipitates (~70 nm) associated with Nb-enriched bcc phase
  - B2 precipitates in bcc phase are (Co, Hf)-rich
  - bcc precipitates in B2 matrix are (Nb, Mo)-rich

**(HfCo)75(NbMo)25:**
- Excellent eutectic microstructure stability; no new phases detected
- Separate Nb-rich and Mo-rich bcc areas observed
- Nanoscale precipitates:
  - bcc precipitates inside B2 phase: size = 15 nm, fraction increased from ~25% (as-cast) to 35% (annealed)
  - B2 precipitates inside bcc phase: size = 6 nm
  - Coarser B2 precipitates (~70 nm) in Nb-rich bcc phase

**Thermo-Calc predictions (TCHEA 4.0 database):**
- **(HfCo)90(NbMo)10:** Solidification starts with (Nb, Mo)-rich bcc at ≈2400 °C; terminates at ≈1620 °C with (Hf, Co)-rich B2. Expected B2:bcc ratio ≈ 9:1. Hf2Co compound predicted at T ≤ 730 °C with volume fraction < 1%.
- **(HfCo)75(NbMo)25:** Liquid phase separation at T ≥ 2450 °C. (Nb, Mo)-rich liquid solidifies into bcc; (Hf, Co)-rich liquid crystallizes into B2 at ≈1620 °C. Predicted B2:bcc ratio = 3:1. Hf2Co predicted at T ≤ 710 °C with insignificant fraction.

# Mechanical Properties

**Compression test conditions:**
- Uniaxial compression, gauge dimensions 5 × 3 × 3 mm³
- Temperatures: 22 °C and 800 °C
- Atmosphere: air
- Initial strain rate: 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹
- Tested to fracture or 50% height reduction

**Mechanical properties summary:**

| Alloy | Condition | Test temperature (°C) | Yield strength (MPa) | Peak stress (MPa) | Fracture strain (%) |
|-------|-----------|----------------------|---------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 | As-cast | 22 | 640 | 1165 | 15 |
| (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 | As-cast | 800 | 460 | – | >50 |
| (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 | Annealed 1000 °C/700 h | 22 | 1340 | 1490 | 4 |
| (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 | Annealed 1000 °C/700 h | 800 | 645 | – | >50 |
| (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 | As-cast | 22 | 1155 | 1620 | 9 |
| (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 | As-cast | 800 | 780 | – | >50 |
| (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 | Annealed 1000 °C/700 h | 22 | 1490 | 1845 | 4 |
| (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 | Annealed 1000 °C/700 h | 800 | 865 | – | >50 |

**Key mechanical behavior observations:**
- (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 is softer but more ductile than (HfCo)75(NbMo)25
- Increasing test temperature from 22 to 800 °C causes softening and increased ductility
- Both alloys show noticeable strain hardening at room temperature
- Weak hardening followed by near steady-state flow at 800 °C
- Annealing led to substantial strengthening and some decrease in ductility
- Most pronounced annealing effect: (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 at room temperature, YS increased from 640 to 1340 MPa (+700 MPa)
- (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 showed smaller strengthening effect from annealing because precipitates were already present in as-cast state

**Orowan strengthening calculation:**
- Δσ = 0.538·G·b·(√f/d)·ln(d/2b)
- G = 20 GPa (shear modulus, from B2 CoZr data; no CoHf data available)
- b = 0.224 nm (from bcc lattice parameter)
- f = 30%, d = 12 nm for bcc precipitates in B2 matrix of (HfCo)90(NbMo)10
- Calculated Δσ ≈ 615 MPa
- Experimental YS difference: 700 MPa
- Discrepancy attributed to B2 precipitates in bcc phase also contributing to strength
- Lower strengthening at 800 °C possibly due to dynamic coarsening of bcc precipitates

## Hf + Co (combined as CoHf B2 compound)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** (HfCo)100-x(NbMo)x refractory high-entropy alloys, (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 and (HfCo)75(NbMo)25
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Hf:Co atomic ratio ≈ 1:1. In (HfCo)90(NbMo)10: 45 at.% Hf + 45 at.% Co; in (HfCo)75(NbMo)25: 37.5 at.% Hf + 37.5 at.% Co
- **Main Function:** Forms ductile B2 CoHf ordered compound that serves as the matrix/framework phase
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Forms B2 ordered bcc phase. B2 has ordered bcc structure with low lattice misfit (~2%) relative to the (Nb, Mo)-rich bcc phase. Enables "cube-on-cube" (110)bcc||(110)B2, [001]bcc||[001]B2 orientation relationship. Dissolves limited amounts of Nb and Mo (~4 at.% Nb + Mo in as-cast non-equilibrium condition) that precipitate as bcc nanoparticles upon annealing.
- **Property Effect:** Provides matrix ductility. B2 matrix supports dislocation activity. Baseline YS of (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 as-cast = 640 MPa at RT, 460 MPa at 800 °C. (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 (higher bcc fraction) as-cast YS = 1155 MPa at RT, 780 MPa at 800 °C. Fracture strain of (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 as-cast = 15% at RT, >50% at 800 °C.
- **Potential Side Effects:** B2 contains dissolved Nb/Mo in non-equilibrium as-cast state; decomposition during annealing causes fine precipitation leading to reduced room-temperature ductility (15% → 4% for (HfCo)90(NbMo)10).
- **Original Evidence:** Abstract states alloys consist of "a mixture of (Nb, Mo)-rich bcc and (Hf, Co)-rich B2 phases". EDS maps (Fig. 2c, d; Fig. 3c, d) show Hf and Co enrichment in B2 precipitates and matrix. As-cast (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 B2 phase contains ~4 at.% Nb+Mo (ref. [23]).

## Nb + Mo (combined as addition)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** (HfCo)100-x(NbMo)x refractory high-entropy alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Total Nb+Mo = 10 at.% in (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 (5 at.% Nb + 5 at.% Mo); 25 at.% in (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 (12.5 at.% Nb + 12.5 at.% Mo)
- **Main Function:** Forms hard (Nb, Mo)-rich bcc phase particles that reinforce the B2 matrix; increases alloy strength
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Forms disordered bcc (A2) phase. In (HfCo)90(NbMo)10, forms bcc/B2 eutectic regions with B2 matrix grains and a small amount of Nb-rich bcc phase. In (HfCo)75(NbMo)25, forms mostly bcc + B2 eutectic structure with some primary bcc particles. During annealing, bcc nanoprecipitates (Nb, Mo-rich) form inside B2 phase and Nb-Mo partitioning leads to separate Nb-rich and Mo-rich bcc phases. bcc precipitates in B2 matrix: 12 nm size, 30 vol% (in (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 annealed).
- **Property Effect:** Higher Nb+Mo content increases yield strength: (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 (25% NbMo) shows much higher YS (1155 MPa RT, 780 MPa 800 °C) than (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 (640 MPa RT, 460 MPa 800 °C). bcc nanoprecipitates contribute Orowan strengthening Δσ ≈ 615 MPa (calculated), compared with experimental YS increase of 700 MPa in (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 after annealing.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Higher Nb+Mo content decreases ductility: fracture strain reduces from 15% to 9% at RT(from (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 to (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 as-cast). Unexpected Nb/Mo partitioning into separate Nb-rich and Mo-rich phases during annealing.
- **Original Evidence:** Fig. 2a3, 2a4, 3a3, 3a4 EDS maps show Nb-rich and Mo-rich areas. bcc precipitates inside B2 matrix are composed mainly of Nb and Mo (Fig. 2c). Table 2 shows YS data. "The as-cast alloys consisted of a mixture of (Nb, Mo)-rich bcc and (Hf, Co)-rich B2 phases" (Abstract).

## Nanoprecipitate bcc in B2 matrix (strengthening phase)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 and (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 RHEAs after annealing at 1000 °C/700 h
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** bcc nanoprecipitate size = 12 nm in (HfCo)90(NbMo)10, 15 nm in (HfCo)75(NbMo)25; volume fraction = 30% in annealed (HfCo)90(NbMo)10, 35% in annealed (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 (up from ~25% as-cast)
- **Main Function:** Precipitation strengthening of B2 matrix via Orowan mechanism
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** bcc precipitates (Nb, Mo-rich, almost without Co and Hf) form by decomposition of supersaturated B2 phase during annealing. Low lattice misfit (~2%) with B2 phase allows fine, coherent precipitate formation. Ashby-Orowan equation used: Δσ = 0.538·G·b·(√f/d)·ln(d/2b). Calculated Δσ ≈ 615 MPa using G = 20 GPa, b = 0.224 nm, f = 30%, d = 12 nm.
- **Property Effect:** RT YS of (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 increases from 640 MPa (as-cast) to 1340 MPa (annealed), ΔYS = 700 MPa. Compression RT PS increases from 1165 to 1490 MPa. Fracture strain decreases from 15% to 4%. At 800 °C, YS increases from 460 to 645 MPa (Δ = 185 MPa). Smaller effect in (HfCo)75(NbMo)25: YS 1155 → 1490 MPa RT, 780 → 865 MPa at 800 °C.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Decreased room-temperature ductility (15% → 4% in (HfCo)90(NbMo)10). Lower strengthening at 800 °C possibly due to dynamic coarsening of precipitates during testing.
- **Original Evidence:** "The bcc precipitates inside the B2 matrix had the size of 12 nm and volume fraction of 30%" for (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 annealed (Section 3). "The strength increment was mostly attributed to the Orowan hardening of the B2 phase by the bcc precipitates" (Conclusion 2). Ashby-Orowan calculation in Section 3. Fig. 2b shows dark-field TEM image of bcc precipitates in B2 matrix.

## B2 precipitates in bcc phase

- **Applicable Alloy System:** (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 and (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 RHEAs after annealing
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Size ~4 nm in (HfCo)90(NbMo)10, ~6 nm in (HfCo)75(NbMo)25; coarser B2 precipitates ~70 nm in Nb-rich bcc phase
- **Main Function:** Secondary strengthening phase; accompanies bcc precipitation in B2
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** (Co, Hf)-rich B2 precipitates form inside (Nb, Mo)-rich bcc phase, driven by excess Co+Hf in as-cast bcc (up to ~11 at.% Co+Hf in bcc per ref. [23]). Coarser B2 precipitates (~70 nm) in Nb-rich bcc regions attributed to larger lattice parameter of Nb-rich bcc increasing lattice misfit with B2.
- **Property Effect:** Contributes to overall strength but smaller effect than bcc precipitates in B2 matrix. Some discrepancy between calculated (Δσ ≈ 615 MPa) and actual (700 MPa) YS increase attributed to B2 precipitation in bcc phase.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Not explicitly stated in original paper.
- **Original Evidence:** Fig. 2d shows STEM-HAADF images with EDS maps confirming (Co, Hf)-rich B2 precipitates in bcc phase. "The B2 precipitates in the bcc phase were mostly finer and had the mean size of ~4 nm" (Section 3).

## Precipitation strengthening (Orowan mechanism)

- **Related Elements:** Nb, Mo (bcc precipitates); Co, Hf (B2 precipitates)
- **Typical Alloys:** (HfCo)90(NbMo)10, (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 RHEAs
- **Key Compositions:** bcc precipitates 12-15 nm, 30-35 vol% in B2 matrix after 700 h at 1000 °C
- **Microstructural Changes:** Nanoscale bcc precipitates (Nb, Mo-rich) form inside B2 matrix; B2 precipitates form inside bcc phase. Decomposition of supersaturated as-cast phases. Low lattice misfit (~2%) enables fine, coherent precipitation.
- **Property Improvements:** YS increase of 700 MPa at RT (from 640 to 1340 MPa) in (HfCo)90(NbMo)10; YS increase of 335 MPa at RT (from 1155 to 1490 MPa) in (HfCo)75(NbMo)25. At 800 °C, YS increases of 185 MPa and 85 MPa, respectively.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Ductility loss at RT (15% → 4%, 9% → 4%). Smaller enhancement at 800 °C due to dynamic coarsening. Process control challenges for obtaining consistent precipitate distributions. Inconsistency between predicted and actual B2:bcc ratios may be due to non-equilibrium solidification.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Phase stability:** The (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 and (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 alloys demonstrate reasonable phase stability during long-term annealing at 1000 °C for 700 h. No additional phases formed; the alloys remained bcc + B2 mixtures. This stability largely agrees with Thermo-Calc predictions.

2. **Precipitation response:** Annealing caused precipitation of nanoscale bcc/B2 particles inside the B2/bcc phases:
   - bcc precipitates in B2 matrix: 12 nm, 30 vol% in (HfCo)90(NbMo)10
   - bcc precipitates in B2 matrix: 15 nm, 35 vol% in (HfCo)75(NbMo)25
   - B2 precipitates in bcc phase: ~4-6 nm (coarser ~70 nm in Nb-rich bcc)
   - Precipitation driven by supersaturation of as-cast phases; as-cast B2 contained ~4 at.% Nb+Mo, as-cast bcc contained up to ~11 at.% Co+Hf

3. **Nb-Mo partitioning:** Unexpected formation of separate Nb-rich and Mo-rich bcc phases occurred during annealing, contradicting the expected unlimited mutual solubility of Nb and Mo per the Nb-Mo binary diagram. This finding suggests Co and/or Hf may limit Nb-Mo mutual solubility—though the authors note this is a hypothesis requiring future verification.

4. **Strengthening:** Annealing produced significant strengthening:
   - (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 RT YS: 640 → 1340 MPa (+700 MPa)
   - (HfCo)75(NbMo)25 RT YS: 1155 → 1490 MPa (+335 MPa)
   - Orowan strengthening calculation for bcc precipitates in B2 matrix yields Δσ ≈ 615 MPa, matching well with the 700 MPa experimental YS increase
   - Effect is larger in (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 because precipitates were not present in its as-cast state, while they were already present in (HfCo)75(NbMo)25

5. **Ductility trade-off:** Strengthening was accompanied by reduced room-temperature ductility:
   - (HfCo)90(NbMo)10: FS 15% → 4%
   - (HfCo)75(NbMo)25: FS 9% → 4%
   - Both alloys retain ductility >50% at 800 °C even after annealing

6. **Novel design route:** Results demonstrate potential for precipitation-hardened RHEAs based on stable bcc + B2 structures with ductile B2 CoHf matrix and hard bcc NbMo phase.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy system specificity:** Results are specific to (HfCo)90(NbMo)10 and (HfCo)75(NbMo)25. Extrapolation to other bcc + B2 RHEAs should be done with caution.
- **Shear modulus assumption:** Orowan strengthening calculation uses shear modulus G = 20 GPa from B2 CoZr compound due to lack of available data for CoHf. This assumption may introduce error in the calculated Δσ value.
- **Nb-Mo partitioning explanation is speculative:** The mechanism causing Nb/Mo partitioning is not conclusively established. The authors explicitly state "future work is required to verify this assumption" regarding the role of Co and/or Hf in limiting Nb-Mo mutual solubility.
- **Limited mechanical testing conditions:** Only compression tests at 22 °C and 800 °C were performed. No tensile tests, creep tests, fracture toughness, or tests at 1000 °C or above were reported.
- **No oxidation characterization:** Although compression tests were performed in air at 800 °C, no oxidation weight gain, oxide scale characterization, or oxidation kinetics were reported.
- **Composition verification:** Exact alloy compositions were measured in a prior study [23]; this paper does not report measured oxygen, nitrogen, or carbon impurity content—a critical consideration for refractory alloys.
- **No grain size measurement:** Grain size was not explicitly reported, so the contribution of grain-boundary strengthening or grain refinement cannot be assessed.
- **Short-term thermal stability only:** 700 h annealing covers a single temperature (1000 °C). Behavior at other temperatures or longer times is unknown. Thermo-Calc predicts Hf2Co phase at ≤730 °C (for (HfCo)90(NbMo)10) and ≤710 °C (for (HfCo)75(NbMo)25), which could potentially form at lower service temperatures; this was not experimentally verified.
- **Workability and processability:** The paper is based on cast alloys; no information is provided on hot working, machining, or manufacturing scalability.
- **High-density concern:** Hf and Nb are high-density elements. Density is not reported in this paper, but the use of Hf as a major constituent suggests these alloys may not be lightweight, potentially limiting specific-strength advantages.
