# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Nanocrystalline strengthened Mg-Li alloy with a bcc structure prepared via heat treatment and rolling
- **Authors:** Shun Zhang, Bin Sun, Ruizhi Wu, Ya Zhou, Qiang Wu
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.matlet.2022.131680
- **Alloy system studied:** Mg-14Li-0.5Ni (wt.%) alloy
- **Material category:** Mg-Li alloy with bcc (β-phase) structure. Note: This is NOT a refractory high-temperature alloy system (not Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, refractory high-entropy alloy, or additively manufactured refractory alloy). It is a superlight Mg-Li alloy.
- **Research objective:** To obtain a nanocrystalline strengthened β-phase Mg-Li alloy through homogenization treatment followed by room-temperature rolling, overcoming the difficulty of obtaining nanoscale grains in high-lithium Mg-Li alloys due to their low recrystallization temperature and rapid grain growth.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

1. **Melting and casting:**
   - Induction melting furnace under ambient argon gas
   - Melt poured into a steel mold to obtain as-cast specimens
   - Raw materials: pure Mg (99.95 wt.%), pure Li (99.90 wt.%), Mg-20 wt% Ni master alloy

2. **Homogenization:**
   - Temperature: 200 °C
   - Time: 20 h
   - Cooling: Quenched

3. **Rolling (both as-cast and homogenized samples):**
   - Rolling temperature: Room temperature
   - Rolling speed: 500 cm/min
   - Reduction per pass: 0.2 mm
   - Total reduction rate: 80%
   - Naming convention:
     - C-rolled: rolled specimen from as-cast alloy
     - H-rolled: rolled specimen from homogenized alloy

4. **No other heat treatments** (no annealing, aging, forging, HIP, or additive manufacturing) were reported.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

1. **As-cast alloy:** β-phase (bcc structure) matrix. Microstructural details not explicitly described beyond this.

2. **Homogenized alloy:**
   - Matrix: bcc β-phase
   - Second phase: Spherical α (hcp) particles with average size of 350 nm distributed in the β matrix
   - Orientation relationship between α and β: (0001)α // (1̄10)matrix
   - The α phase layers almost fully separate and surround the β phase grains
   - Contact angle between α phase and β phase is zero, indicating complete grain boundary wetting
   - The homogenization caused a phase transformation from β (bcc) to α (hcp), creating numerous α/β phase boundaries

3. **C-rolled alloy (rolled directly from as-cast):**
   - Complete dynamic recrystallization after room-temperature rolling
   - Average recrystallized grain size: approximately 0.4 μm

4. **H-rolled alloy (homogenized + rolled):**
   - Recrystallized grain size significantly reduced to 80 nm (nanocrystalline)
   - Grain size distribution shown in inset of Figure 2b
   - α phase with a size of 50 nm also detected (refined during rolling)
   - HRTEM confirms α phase viewed from <112̄0> zone axis
   - The grain boundary changed from complete wetting to incomplete wetting during rolling (transition from complete to incomplete wetting)
   - The continuous layer between α and β disappeared after rolling

**Comparison:** The recrystallized grain size of C-rolled alloy is 5 times that of H-rolled alloy, demonstrating that homogenization before rolling effectively refines the recrystallized grain size.

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed:**
- Grain-boundary strengthening (nanocrystalline grains of 80 nm)
- Phase-boundary strengthening (α/β boundaries hindering dislocation movement)
- Dislocation density increase during rolling
- Work hardening ability changes due to α phase introduction

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Li (Lithium)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Mg-Li binary and Mg-Li-Ni ternary alloys

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
14 wt.% Li in Mg-14Li-0.5Ni alloy

**[Main Function]**
- Reduces the c/a ratio of the Mg hcp lattice
- Activates <c+a> dislocation slip
- When Li content exceeds 10.3 wt.%, stabilizes the β phase with bcc crystal structure, which greatly improves plasticity
- Lowers recrystallization temperature

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Changes matrix from hcp to bcc phase when Li >10.3 wt.%
- Reduces recrystallization temperature, causing dynamically recrystallized grains to grow rapidly during deformation even at room temperature
- The β (bcc) phase provides higher plasticity compared to hcp Mg

**[Property Effect]**
- Mg-16Li alloys can achieve elongation of more than 35%
- For Mg-14Li-0.5Ni: as-cast fracture elongation is 30%; homogenized fracture elongation is 50%
- The Li atoms make the magnesium alloy soft (reduced strength)
- The original paper does not provide specific yield strength values for different Li contents beyond the above.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Softens the Mg alloy (reduces strength)
- Reduces recrystallization temperature, making nanograined Mg-Li alloys difficult to obtain by traditional processing
- Rapid grain growth of dynamically recrystallized grains even at room temperature
- Usually requires liquid nitrogen cooling conditions to obtain nanograins, increasing production cost and difficulty

**[Original Evidence]**
> "Li can reduce the value of c/a and activate <c+a> dislocation slip. When the Li content exceeds 10.3 wt%, the β phase with the crystal structure of bcc appears, which greatly improves the plasticity. For instance, the elongation of Mg-16Li alloys can reach more than 35%. However, the Li atoms make the magnesium alloy soft." (Introduction, Section 1)

> "The addition of Li reduces the recrystallization temperature, and the dynamically recrystallized grains grow rapidly during deformation even at room temperature. Nanograined Mg-Li alloys usually need to be obtained under the cooling conditions of liquid nitrogen, which increases production costs and difficulty." (Introduction, Section 1)

## [homogenization-induced α phase (hcp) in β (bcc) matrix]

*Note: This is not an element but a phase/processing combination concept central to the paper.*

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Mg-14Li-0.5Ni (wt.%) with bcc β-phase matrix

**[Composition Range]**
Spherical α phase with average size of 350 nm formed during homogenization at 200 °C for 20 h

**[Main Function]**
- Introduces numerous α/β phase boundaries that hinder dislocation movement, improving strength
- Creates conditions for dynamic recrystallization during subsequent rolling, enabling nanocrystalline grain refinement
- Improves work hardening ability
- Improves both strength and plasticity simultaneously

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Homogenization at 200 °C for 20 h causes transformation of bcc β phase to hcp α phase, forming spherical α particles (350 nm average size)
- Orientation relationship: (0001)α // (1̄10)matrix
- α phase layers almost fully separate and surround the β phase grains
- Contact angle between α and β is zero, indicating complete grain boundary wetting
- The α phase has a higher ability to produce dislocations than the β matrix
- The α/β boundaries provide a large number of nucleation sites for recrystallized grains
- Higher microstructure heterogeneity from α phases increases deformation strain during rolling, increasing dislocation density and promoting DRX
- During rolling, the α phase is also refined to approximately 50 nm
- The grain boundary transitions from complete to incomplete wetting during rolling

**[Property Effect]**
- After homogenization: UTS increases to 127 MPa; uniform elongation increases from 9% to 15%; fracture elongation increases from 30% to 50%
- After homogenization + rolling: recrystallized grain size reduced from 0.4 μm to 80 nm (5× refinement compared to C-rolled)
- H-rolled strength is 150% higher than as-cast alloy
- H-rolled strength is 1.5 times that of C-rolled alloy
- H-rolled loses work hardening ability (work hardening rate <0 after yielding)

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- After rolling, the continuous α/β layer disappears, and grain boundary becomes incompletely wetted, which is responsible for plastic loss in the H-rolled alloy
- The nanostructured sample loses work hardening ability
- The H-rolled alloy exhibits lower plasticity compared with the C-rolled alloy

**[Original Evidence]**
> "This indicates that the transition from β to α occurred during homogenization." (Results and Discussion, Section 3)

> "After homogenization, the ultimate tensile strength is increased to 127 MPa. It is worth noting that the increase in strength does not sacrifice plasticity. The uniform elongation is increased from 9% to 15%, and the fracture elongation is increased from 30% to 50%." (Results and Discussion, Section 3)

> "The recrystallized grain size of the C-rolled alloy is 5 times that of the H-rolled alloy, which indicates that homogenization before rolling can effectively refine the recrystallized grain size." (Results and Discussion, Section 3)

> "The recrystallized grain size is significantly reduced to 80 nm... In addition, an α phase with a size of 50 nm was also detected." (Results and Discussion, Section 3)

> "This transformation should be responsible for plastic loss." (Results and Discussion, Section 3)

## Work Hardening / Dislocation Strengthening

**[Related Elements]**
Mg, Li, Ni (alloy-dependent)

**[Typical Alloys]**
Mg-14Li-0.5Ni (wt.%)

**[Key Compositions]**
Pre-homogenization introduces α phase that enhances work hardening ability

**[Microstructural Changes]**
- α phase has higher dislocation production ability than β matrix
- Evenly distributed α phase disperses stress during plastic deformation, avoiding stress concentration
- Complete grain boundary wetting by α phase facilitates dislocation transfer and GB sliding
- During rolling, higher microstructure heterogeneity increases deformation strain and dislocation density

**[Property Improvements]**
- Homogenized alloy shows improved uniform elongation (15% vs 9%)
- Homogenized alloy shows improved fracture elongation (50% vs 30%)
- Homogenized alloy UTS = 127 MPa

**[Risks and Limitations]**
- After rolling to nanocrystalline state, work hardening ability is lost (rate <0 after yielding)
- C-rolled alloy retains work hardening (rate >0 after yielding)

**[Original Evidence]**
> "The α phase introduced by homogenization has a higher ability to produce dislocations than the matrix... the homogenized alloy can produce more dislocations to adapt continuous deformation." (Results and Discussion, Section 3)

> "The evenly distributed α phase disperses the stress of the matrix during plastic deformation, thereby avoiding the stress concentration of the coarse grains and improving the ductility." (Results and Discussion, Section 3)

# Key Conclusions

1. **Nanocrystalline strengthened Mg-14Li-0.5Ni alloy** with a density of 1.42 g/cm³ was successfully obtained by pre-homogenization (200 °C for 20 h) followed by room-temperature rolling (80% total reduction).

2. **Pre-homogenization induces β→α phase transformation**, creating spherical α particles (350 nm) in the β matrix. This improves both strength (UTS = 127 MPa) and plasticity (fracture elongation 50% vs 30%) simultaneously.

3. **Homogenization creates conditions for nanocrystal formation during rolling** by:
   - Introducing α/β boundaries that provide nucleation sites for dynamic recrystallization
   - Increasing microstructure heterogeneity and dislocation density during rolling
   - Enabling recrystallized grain refinement from 0.4 μm (without homogenization) to 80 nm (with homogenization)

4. **Nanocrystalline grain refinement provides significant strengthening**: H-rolled strength is 150% higher than as-cast alloy and 1.5 times that of C-rolled (ultrafine-grained) alloy.

5. **Trade-off**: The nanostructured sample loses work hardening ability and exhibits lower plasticity compared to the ultrafine-grained sample, due to suppression of dislocation nucleation and motion in nanograins and the loss of complete α/β grain boundary wetting during rolling.

6. **Novel strategy**: This paper provides a new method for manufacturing nanostructure-strengthened Mg-Li alloys at room temperature without requiring liquid nitrogen cooling.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Alloy system limitation:** This is a Mg-Li alloy, NOT a refractory high-temperature alloy. The knowledge extracted here is applicable to Mg-Li lightweight alloys and may have limited transferability to Nb-W, Mo-W, or refractory high-entropy alloy systems.

2. **Temperature range:** All experiments and property evaluations were conducted at room temperature. No high-temperature mechanical properties or oxidation resistance were evaluated.

3. **Specific numerical values lacking:**
   - Yield strength values are not explicitly provided for any condition
   - UTS of as-cast alloy is not explicitly stated (only relative comparison)
   - Specific UTS values for C-rolled and H-rolled alloys are not numerically stated in the text (only relative to as-cast and each other)
   - No hardness, compressive strength, or fracture toughness data reported

4. **Ni role not discussed:** The paper does not explain the function or role of the 0.5 wt.% Ni in the alloy.

5. **Microstructural characterization limited:** Only TEM and HRTEM were used. No XRD, EBSD, or SEM results are presented.

6. **Single composition studied:** Only one alloy composition (Mg-14Li-0.5Ni) was investigated. No compositional optimization or systematic study of Li or Ni effects is presented.

7. **The results are specific to Mg-Li alloys with high Li content** (>10.3 wt.% Li, bcc β-phase dominant). Extrapolation to other Mg alloys or other alloy systems requires caution.

8. **The paper is a short communication** (Materials Letters format), so the depth of analysis and supporting experiments is limited.
