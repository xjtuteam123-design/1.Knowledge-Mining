# Paper Metadata
- **Title**: Promoted high-temperature strength and room-temperature plasticity synergy by tuning dendrite segregation in NbMoTaW refractory high-entropy alloy
- **Authors**: Bo Sun, Qianqian Wang, Jinyong Mo, Bingjie Wang, Xiubing Liang, Baolong Shen
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2024 (Available online 10 November 2023)
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106469
- **Alloy system**: NbMoTaW refractory high-entropy alloy system (Nb-Mo-Ta-W quaternary system)
- **Material category**: Refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA), specifically NbMoTaW RHEA with modified Nb/Mo contents
- **Research objective**: To overcome the trade-off between high-temperature (HT) strength and room-temperature (RT) plasticity in NbMoTaW RHEA by tuning dendrite segregation to create a heterostructure

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Alloy system**: Nb-Mo-Ta-W refractory high-entropy alloy system
- **Parent alloy**: NbMoTaW (equiatomic Nb25Mo25Ta25W25, also denoted as NM25)
- **Modified alloys**: NbxMoxTa50-xW50-x (x = 5, 25, 45 at.%)
  - NM5: Nb5Mo5Ta45W45
  - NM25: Nb25Mo25Ta25W25 (equiatomic NbMoTaW)
  - NM45: Nb45Mo45Ta5W5
- **Material density**:
  - NbMoTaW (NM25): ~13.75 g/cm³ (reported in literature)
  - NM45: 10.35 g/cm³ (measured by Archimedes method)
- **Melting temperatures (theoretical, rule-of-mixture)**:
  - NM25 alloy: solidification at ~3100 K; annealing at 1873 K corresponds to 0.58 Tm (DR) and 0.61 Tm (ID)
  - NM45 alloy: solidification between Tm of Nb and Mo (~2880 K); annealing at 1873 K corresponds to 0.65 Tm (DR) and 0.66 Tm (ID)
- **Sample designations**:
  - NMx-AC: as-cast samples (x = 5, 25, 45)
  - NMx-AN: annealed samples at 1873 K for 500 min (x = 5, 25, 45)

# Alloy Composition
- **Alloy series**: NbxMoxTa50-xW50-x (x = 5, 25, 45 at.%)
- **NM5 (Nb5Mo5Ta45W45)**:
  - Nb: 5 at.%
  - Mo: 5 at.%
  - Ta: 45 at.%

- **NM25 (Nb25Mo25Ta25W25)**: Equiatomic NbMoTaW
  - Nb: 25 at.%
  - Mo: 25 at.%
  - Ta: 25 at.%
  - W: 25 at.%
- **NM45 (Nb45Mo45Ta5W5)**:
  - Nb: 45 at.%
  - Mo: 45 at.%
  - Ta: 5 at.%
  - W: 5 at.%
- **Raw material purity**: Bulk metals (Nb, Mo, Ta, W) with high purity ≥ 99.95 wt%
- **Interstitial impurities**: 
  - Oxygen (O) segregation at grain boundaries observed in all samples, forming Ta-Nb rich oxides
  - Specific composition at grain boundary oxides given in Table S2 (multi-component composition, discontinuously distributed)
  - C, N, H contents: Not explicitly stated in the original paper

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Preparation method**: Vacuum arc melting
- **Melting conditions**:
  - Melting current: ~300 A
  - Remelted at least six times to ensure chemical homogeneity
  - Pure Ti used as getter to remove residual oxygen in chamber
- **Annealing treatment**:
  - Temperature: 1873 K
  - Duration: 500 min
  - Heating rate: ~10 K/s
  - Cooling rate: ~10 K/s
- **Sample states studied**:
  - As-cast (AC)
  - Annealed (AN)
- **Post-deformation analysis**: Samples subjected to compression at 1873 K

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
## Phase Constitution
- **Matrix phase**: Single body-centered cubic (BCC) phase in all NMx alloys (NM5, NM25, NM45), confirmed by XRD
- **Phase stability after annealing**: Single BCC structure maintained after annealing at 1873 K for 500 min (NMx-AN samples)
- **Equilibrium phase diagram prediction**: Single BCC phase predicted for all NM5, NM25, NM45 alloys (Thermal-Calc with TCHEA database)

## Grain Structure
- **Grain morphology**: Nearly-equiaxed grains without obvious solidification microtexture
- **Average grain size**:
  - NM5-AC: 95 μm
  - NM25-AC: 104 μm
  - NM45-AC: 96 μm
- **Influence of Nb/Mo content on grain size**: Limited influence observed

## Dendrite Segregation
- **NM5-AC**: No obvious dendrite segregation inside grains; straight grain boundaries
- **NM25-AC**: Obvious dendrite segregation visible; serrated grain boundaries
- **NM45-AC**: Pronounced dendrite segregation visible; serrated grain boundaries
- **Segregation behavior**:
  - NM25-AC: W and Ta segregate in dendrites (K > 1); Mo segregation not in dendrites
  - NM45-AC: W, Ta, and Mo all segregate in dendrites (K > 1)
  - K = c(DR)/c(ID), where K > 1 indicates segregation in dendrite region
- **Interdendrite (ID) fraction**:
  - NM25: 13%
  - NM45: 11%
- **Dendrite (DR) and interdendrite (ID) mechanical heterogeneity**:
  - NM25-AC: DR hardness 6.84 GPa, ID hardness 7.35 GPa; DR reduced modulus 239.82 GPa, ID reduced modulus 234.91 GPa
  - NM45-AC: DR hardness 6.60 GPa, ID hardness 7.49 GPa; DR reduced modulus 235.95 GPa, ID reduced modulus 235.61 GPa
  - Average hardness: NM25-AC 7.01 GPa, NM45-AC 6.99 GPa
  - Average reduced modulus: NM25-AC 238.2 GPa, NM45-AC 235.8 GPa

## Grain Boundary Features
- **NM5-AC**: Straight grain boundaries
- **NM25-AC and NM45-AC**: Serrated grain boundaries
- **Grain boundary oxides**: O segregation at grain boundaries in all three samples; Ta-Nb rich oxides, discontinuously distributed with multi-component composition

## Precipitates
- **NM5-AN**: No visible precipitates after annealing
- **NM25-AN**: Small amount of precipitates appear in ID region after annealing
- **NM45-AN**: Large amount of precipitates visible in ID region after annealing; precipitates show hole-like appearance after etching
- **Precipitate morphology in NM45-AN**:
  - Particle-like precipitates: average diameter 102 nm
  - Rod-shaped precipitates: average width 105 nm
  - Rod-shaped precipitates intersect at specific angles, implying preferential orientations
  - Particle-like precipitates tend to separate
- **Precipitate structure**: Long-period stacking ordered structure decomposed from disordered BCC matrix along closely-packed {110}BCC planes
- **Precipitate-matrix interface**: Coherent phase boundary with BCC matrix
- **Precipitate composition**: Decomposition of Nb and Ta out from BCC matrix; O, Ta, Nb contents gradually increase while Mo and W contents decrease from matrix to rod-shaped to particle-like precipitates
- **Precipitate after HT deformation**: Nanoscale precipitates decompose out from BCC solid solution in ID region of NM45-AC after compression at 1873 K

## Structural Stability
- **NM5 and NM25**: Microstructure stable at 1873 K
- **NM45**: Low structural stability in ID region at 1873 K, promoting precipitation
- **Annealing homologous temperature**: 
  - NM25: 0.58 Tm (DR), 0.61 Tm (ID)
  - NM45: 0.65 Tm (DR), 0.66 Tm (ID)
- **Precipitation behavior**: Temperature-dependent and stress-promoted

## Testing Conditions
- **Strain rate**: 10⁻³ s⁻¹ (both RT and 1873 K)
- **Sample dimensions**: Cylinder Φ 2 mm × 4 mm cut from center of ingots
- **RT testing machine**: Instron-5982
- **HT testing machine**: Zwick-KAPPA100
- **Nanoindentation**: Maximum load 20 mN, dwell time 10 s, interval 30 μm, loading/unloading rates 0.5 mN/s
- **Elastic constants (first-principles calculation)**:
  - NM25: C11 = 385 GPa, C12 = 162 GPa, C44 = 76 GPa; B = 236 GPa, G = 89 GPa, E = 237 GPa, ν = 0.333, Pugh's ratio = 2.655
  - NM45: C11 = 377 GPa, C12 = 142 GPa, C44 = 58 GPa; B = 222 GPa, G = 74 GPa, E = 200 GPa, ν = 0.349, Pugh's ratio = 2.987

## Fracture Behavior
- **RT fracture surface (NMx-AC)**:
  - NM5-AC: Intergranular fracture
  - NM25-AC: Quasi-cleavage fracture
  - NM45-AC: Quasi-cleavage fracture; smaller fracture facet with denser quasi-cleavage steps; more resistant to quick crack propagation
- **1873 K fracture surface**: All samples show material spallation from side surface; sluggish failure corresponding to strain softening stage

## Deformation Behavior at 1873 K
- **All NMx samples**: Continuous drop of stress after yielding (strain softening behavior)
- **Softening mechanisms**: Dynamic recrystallization (evidenced by LAGBs at DR region), material spallation from side surface, coarse cracks
- **No grain boundary sliding**: Even at testing temperature up to 0.66 Tm for NM45 alloy
- **Hetero-deformation**: Dislocation glide in stable DR region; strengthened by nanoscale precipitates in unstable ID region
- **NM45-AN**: Most resistant to crack propagation due to large amount of precipitates at ID region; slower softening rate

## Strengthening Mechanisms Discussed
- **Solid-solution strengthening**: Atomic size misfit δr = 0.0524 (NM25), 0.0556 (NM45) — comparable
- **Grain-boundary strengthening**: Δσgb ≈ 8 MPa (neglectable); Hall-Petch coefficient ky = 1600 MPa·μm^1/2
- **Precipitation strengthening**: Not present in as-cast samples; present after annealing and after HT deformation
- **Dislocation strengthening**: Low dislocation density in as-cast sample; not significant
- **Heterogeneous strengthening (heterostructure)**: Primary mechanism for NM45 alloy's superior properties
- **Modulus misfit (for HT strength)**: δG = 1.0109 (NM25), 1.4596 (NM45)

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability
- **Grain boundary oxides**: Ta-Nb rich oxides present at grain boundaries in all as-cast samples (NM5, NM25, NM45)
- **Oxide composition**: Multi-component composition; discontinuously distributed
- **Oxide content trend**: Ta content in oxides is lower in NM45-AC than NM25-AC
- **Oxide-related embrittlement**: Ta-oxide phase transition from orthorhombic to tetragonal structure at ~1800 K may cause structural shrinkage and stress concentration at grain boundaries, making GBs more susceptible to cracking under tensile stress at HT
- **RT brittleness**: GB oxide segregation may not be responsive to intrinsic brittleness of NbMoTaW system RHEAs (NM45-AC fractured without intergranular failure at >6% strain)
- **Service temperature limitation**: 0.6 Tm generally considered as upper limitation of service temperature
- **Structural stability concerns**: 
  - NM5 and NM25 alloys stable at 1873 K
  - NM45 alloy shows low structural stability in ID region at 1873 K
- **Oxidation weight gain**: Not measured in this paper
- **Protective oxide scale**: Not discussed in this paper

## [Solid-Solution Strengthening]
- **Related Elements**: Nb, Mo, Ta, W
- **Typical Alloys**: NbxMoxTa50-xW50-x (x = 5, 25, 45)
- **Key Compositions**: NM25 (Nb25Mo25Ta25W25), NM45 (Nb45Mo45Ta5W5)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Atomic size misfit creates lattice strain field
- **Property Improvements**: Atomic size misfit δr = 0.0524 (NM25) and 0.0556 (NM45) — comparable contribution
- **Risks and Limitations**: Not the primary differentiator between NM25 and NM45 properties; comparable solid-solution strengthening in both alloys
- **Original Evidence**: Section 4.2; δr calculation using formula from reference [37]

## [Modulus Misfit Strengthening (High-Temperature Strength)]
- **Related Elements**: Nb, Mo (higher content in NM45)
- **Typical Alloys**: NbxMoxTa50-xW50-x (x = 5, 25, 45)
- **Key Compositions**: NM45 (Nb45Mo45Ta5W5) with δG = 1.4596
- **Microstructural Changes**: Modulus misfit between constituent elements resists elastic deformation
- **Property Improvements**: δG = 1.4596 (NM45) vs 1.0109 (NM25); NM45 more resistant to elastic deformation and should reach high yield strength
- **Risks and Limitations**: NM45 yield strength is slightly lower (~57 MPa) than NM25 at 1873 K due to higher homologous temperature; yield strength more temperature-dependent at ultrahigh temperature
- **Original Evidence**: Section 4.4, δG calculation using formula from reference [37]

## [Heterostructure Strengthening (Dendrite Heterogeneity)]
- **Related Elements**: Nb, Mo, W, Ta (segregation-driven)
- **Typical Alloys**: NbxMoxTa50-xW50-x (x = 5, 25, 45); particularly NM45
- **Key Compositions**: NM45 (Nb45Mo45Ta5W5) with pronounced dendrite heterogeneity
- **Microstructural Changes**: Dendrite (DR) and interdendrite (ID) regions with different composition and mechanical properties; hardness difference between DR and ID regions; serrated grain boundaries
- **Property Improvements**: NM45-AC achieves RT yield strength 1334 MPa and peak strain 6.1%; hardened ID regions contribute to heterogeneous strengthening effect; back stress and forward stress interactions improve plasticity
- **Risks and Limitations**: Pronounced heterogeneity may reduce structural stability at very high temperature; ID region prone to precipitation at 1873 K
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 5, Fig. 8, Section 4.2

## [Grain-Boundary Strengthening]
- **Related Elements**: Not element-specific; grain size effect
- **Typical Alloys**: NbxMoxTa50-xW50-x (x = 5, 25, 45)
- **Key Compositions**: Grain size 95–104 μm across all alloys
- **Microstructural Changes**: Grain size variation between NM25 (104 μm) and NM45 (96 μm)
- **Property Improvements**: Δσgb ≈ 8 MPa (neglectable contribution)
- **Risks and Limitations**: Not a significant contributor due to similar grain sizes
- **Original Evidence**: Section 4.2, Hall-Petch calculation with ky = 1600 MPa·μm^1/2

## [Precipitation Strengthening (Annealing-Induced)]
- **Related Elements**: Nb, Ta, O (precipitate composition)

# Key Conclusions
1. **Heterostructure strategy**: Tuning dendrite segregation in NbMoTaW RHEA by increasing Nb and Mo content creates a pronounced heterostructure between dendrite (DR) and interdendrite (ID) regions, enabling large RT plasticity without deterioration in HT strength.

2. **Optimal alloy**: Nb45Mo45Ta5W5 (NM45) achieves large RT plasticity of 6.1% and high yield strength of 440 MPa at 1873 K in as-cast condition.

3. **Annealing effect**: Annealing at 1873 K for 500 min causes nanoscale ordered precipitates to form in the interdendrite region, further increasing RT plasticity to 9.0% while maintaining comparable yield strength of 415 MPa at 1873 K.

4. **Density reduction**: NM45 has significantly reduced density of 10.35 g/cm³ compared to 13.75 g/cm³ for equiatomic NbMoTaW, providing better specific strength.

5. **Strength retention mechanism**: The strength retention up to 1873 K arises from the large modulus misfit of NM45 alloy and hetero-deformation between dendrite and interdendrite regions.

6. **Precipitate behavior**: Nanoscale precipitates in the interdendrite region are temperature-dependent and stress-promoted, contributing to work-hardening at RT and softening resistance at 1873 K.

7. **Deformation mechanism at 1873 K**: Hetero-deformation occurs with dislocation glide in the stable DR region and precipitate strengthening in the unstable ID region; no grain boundary sliding observed even at 0.66 Tm.

8. **Strain softening**: All NMx samples show strain softening after peak stress at 1873 K, attributed to dynamic recrystallization, surface spallation, and coarse crack propagation.

9. **Segregation behavior**: Increasing Nb and Mo content promotes dendrite segregation and formation of serrated grain boundaries, driven by constitutional undercooling effects.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Composition range**: The conclusions are limited to NbxMoxTa50-xW50-x (x = 5, 25, 45 at.%) alloys prepared by vacuum arc melting.
- **Structural stability**: NM45 alloy shows low structural stability in the ID region at 1873 K, which may limit long-term service at this temperature.
- **No oxidation resistance data**: The paper does not provide quantitative oxidation weight gain or oxidation layer analysis.
- **High-temperature embrittlement**: Ta-Nb rich oxides at grain boundaries may cause embrittlement at high temperature due to oxide phase transformation; removing GB oxides may be necessary for stable HT mechanical properties.
- **Yield strength limitation**: NM45 yield strength at 1873 K is slightly lower (~57 MPa) than NM25, indicating that yield strength at ultrahigh temperature remains temperature-dependent; further improvements may require retention of moderate amounts of more refractory elements (Ta, W) or additional structural design.
- **No long-term creep data**: Creep performance and rupture life are not reported.
- **Density consideration**: While NM45 has reduced density (10.35 g/cm³), it is still relatively high compared to some other structural materials.
- **Tensile stress limitation**: Surface spallation at 1873 K suggests NMx alloys cannot well resist localized tensile stress, limiting their applicability under tensile loading conditions at ultrahigh temperatures.
- **Not explicitly stated**: No fracture toughness measurements, no fatigue testing, no irradiation tolerance assessment.

The paper's dendritic heterostructure strategy is presented as a feasible method to toughen brittle RHEAs and provides a pathway for designing NbMoTaW-system RHEAs with combined high HT strength and large RT plasticity.
