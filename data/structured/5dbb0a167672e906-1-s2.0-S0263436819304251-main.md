# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Effect of processing parameters on the density, microstructure and strength of pure tungsten fabricated by selective electron beam melting
- **Authors**: Guangyu Yang, Pengwei Yang, Kun Yang, Nan Liu, Liang Jia, Jian Wang, Huiping Tang
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals & Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2019
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2019.105040
- **Alloy system studied**: Pure tungsten (W)
- **Material category**: Additively manufactured refractory metal (SEBM-fabricated pure tungsten; not an alloy)
- **Research objective**: To study the effect of SEBM processing parameters on the density, microstructure, and compression strength of pure tungsten; to determine a preliminary processing window for SEBM of pure tungsten; to investigate the influence of substrate preheating temperature on cracking control.

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

Pure tungsten fabricated by selective electron beam melting (SEBM). No intentional alloying elements were used. The material is commercially pure tungsten powder produced by a sintering-crushing method. This paper focuses on an additively manufactured single-phase BCC refractory metal rather than an alloy. The key research objects are:
1. Determination of a SEBM processing window for pure W.
2. Relationship between energy density and densification (porosity, balling, shrinkage holes).
3. Control of cracking through substrate preheating and in-situ heat accumulation.
4. Columnar grain formation and its effect on compressive mechanical properties.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Additive manufacturing method**: Selective electron beam melting (SEBM)
- **Equipment**: Sailong S2 SEBM system (Xi'an Sailong Metal Materials Co., Ltd., China)
- **Powder layer thickness (t)**: 50 μm (0.05 mm)
- **Hatch distance (h)**: 50 μm (0.05 mm; the original text states “50 mm,” but based on context and Figure 1b, this is likely a typographical error and should be interpreted as 50 μm; the energy density calculation in Table 2 supports 0.05 mm)
- **Accelerating voltage (U)**: 60 kV
- **Beam current (I)**: 9–20 mA (Table 2)
- **Scanning speed (v)**: 100–1500 mm/s (Table 2)
- **Energy density (E)**: 208–3840 J/mm³; calculated as E = (I × U)/(v × h × t)
- **Substrate**: Tungsten plate, size 100 × 100 × 10 mm
- **Vacuum pressure**: < 10⁻² Pa
- **Scan strategy**: Scan direction rotated by 90° between layers
- **Substrate preheating temperatures**: 200 °C, 400 °C, 600 °C (Table 3)
- **Observed in-situ substrate temperature during melting**: Stabilized at 800–950 °C after about 30 min, regardless of initial preheating temperature; temperature maintained a “zigzag” profile due to cyclic raking and melting
- **Heat treatment / post-processing**: None reported
- **Sample dimensions**: 10 mm × 10 mm × 4 mm; compression specimens Φ3.6 mm × 5.7 mm

No homogenization, rolling, forging, annealing, hot isostatic pressing, or aging was applied. The processing is solely as-built SEBM.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: BCC tungsten (single-phase pure W; no second phases mentioned)
- **Grain morphology**: Strong columnar grain structure along the build direction, formed by epitaxial growth due to heat conduction through the substrate and previously solidified layers.
- **Columnar grain width**: Approximately 50–100 μm.
- **Grain boundaries**: Coarse columnar grain boundaries; residual stress accumulates at grain boundaries during forming.
- **Microcracks**: No obvious macro- or micro-cracks on as-fabricated or as-polished surfaces; however, micro-cracks were observed along grain boundaries after etching, indicating residual stress accumulation.
- **Precipitates / second phases**: None reported in the original paper. No carbides, oxides, borides, silicides, or other precipitates are mentioned.
- **Segregation**: Not reported.
- **Melt pool features**: Overlapping scanning lines with approximately 50% overlap rate; melt pool stirring by electron beam causes obscure scanning lines at higher scanning speeds/currents.
- **Balling**: Occurs at high scanning speeds (e.g., 500–1500 mm/s) and at very high beam currents (e.g., 30 mA) due to poor beam focusing. Balling caused unfilled gaps on the surface.
- **Shrinkage holes**: Present in samples with relative density 70–90% (insufficient fusion region).
- **Porosity**: Relative density values:
  - Limited fusion region: < 70%
  - Insufficient fusion region: 70–90%
  - Proper fusion region: > 90%, up to 99.5% (at 100 mm/s, 16 mA)
- **Elemental discussion**: No alloying elements are discussed. The paper focuses on pure W, so no solid-solution strengthening or second-phase strengthening from alloying is discussed. The paper does discuss grain-boundary strengthening/refinement potential as a future route to improve strength.
- **Strengthening mechanisms mentioned in the paper**:
  - The authors mention that refinement of columnar grains and improvement of columnar grain boundary strengthening are expected to improve compression strength.
  - No classical precipitation, dispersion, ODS, or solid-solution strengthening is reported.

# Key Conclusions

1. A preliminary SEBM processing window for pure tungsten was established based on melt pool balling behavior. Pure tungsten samples with a relative density of 99.5% were fabricated without obvious pores and microcracks. The optimum density was achieved at a scanning speed of 100 mm/s and beam current of 16 mA (energy density ≈ 3840 J/mm³).
2. The process window was divided into four regions:
   - Limited fusion: relative density < 70% (energy density 208–312 J/mm³)
   - Insufficient fusion: relative density 70–90% (336–1320 J/mm³)
   - Proper fusion: relative density > 90% (1440–3840 J/mm³)
   - Excessive fusion: flanging and deformation (1440–2400 J/mm³ with high beam power)
3. Balling of the melt pool increased with increasing scanning speed at fixed current and with excessive beam current (e.g., 30 mA) due to poor electron beam focusing.
4. Despite low initial substrate preheating (200 °C), the substrate temperature stabilized at 800–950 °C after about 30 min because of efficient downward heat conduction from the melt pool. This enabled effective relaxation of thermal stress and controlled cracking. Microcracks were still observed along grain boundaries after etching, indicating residual stress.
5. As-built pure W showed strong columnar grain structures along the build direction, with columnar grains 50–100 μm wide, formed by epitaxial growth due to directional heat conduction.
6. Room-temperature compression strength along the build direction was 1560 MPa at a strain rate of 0.0001/s for a 99.5% dense sample. Failure occurred by decohesion along columnar grain boundaries and brittle transgranular fracture.
7. The mechanical properties of SEBM-fabricated W were higher than those reported for SLM-fabricated W (1000–1523 MPa) primarily due to higher density, but the coarse columnar grains limited the strength improvement. The authors suggested that refinement and strengthening of columnar grain boundaries could further improve the compression strength.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Material scope**: Pure tungsten only; no alloys or intentionally added elements were studied. Conclusions cannot be directly applied to W-based alloys, Nb-W alloys, Mo-W alloys, or refractory high-entropy alloys without experimental verification.
- **Mechanical testing scope**: Only room-temperature compression testing was performed. No tensile, creep, fracture toughness, hardness, or high-temperature mechanical properties are reported.
- **Oxidation scope**: No oxidation resistance data were reported. Service stability in oxidative or fusion-relevant environments is not covered.
- **Microstructural scope**: The paper reports columnar grains and grain-boundary microcracks, but no detailed EBSD, TEM, or crystallographic texture analysis is provided. No precipitate analysis is presented.
- **Compositional scope**: Impurity contents (O, C, Fe, Si, K, Na) are from the starting powder; their evolution during SEBM is not discussed. Vacuum level (< 10⁻² Pa) may influence oxygen pickup but is not analyzed.
- **Processing scope**: The SEBM processing window is equipment-specific (Sailong S2, 60 kV, 50 μm layer thickness, 50 μm hatch distance). The authors note that beam power (P) rather than energy density alone was the most effective factor in the excessive fusion region, so the energy density parameter alone does not fully capture process behavior.
- **Speculative statements**: The claim that grain boundary refinement and strengthening would improve compression strength is presented as an expectation, not an experimentally confirmed result in this paper.
- **Typographical ambiguities**: The hatch distance is stated as “50 mm” in the methods, but the energy density calculation and Figure 1 strongly suggest the intended value is 50 μm (0.05 mm). Readers should interpret it accordingly.
