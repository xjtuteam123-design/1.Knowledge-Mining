# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** Synthesis of Nb-based powder alloy by mechanical alloying and plasma spheroidization processes for additive manufacturing
- **Authors:** I.S. Goncharov, N.G. Razumov, A.O. Silin, N.E. Ozerskoi, A.I. Shamshurin, A. Kim, Q.S. Wang, A.A. Popovich
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2019
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matlet.2019.03.014
- **Alloy system studied:** Nb-Si based alloy (Nb-25Ti-2Cr-2Al-16Si, at.%)
- **Research objective:** To synthesize Nb-Si based alloy powder for additive manufacturing (selective laser melting and direct energy deposition) via mechanical alloying followed by plasma spheroidization, and to investigate the microstructure and phase evolution during these powder preparation processes.
- **Material category:** Nb-Si based alloy (refractory multi-phase intermetallic composite powder for additive manufacturing); powder metallurgy (mechanical alloying + plasma spheroidization). Not a Nb-W alloy, Mo-W alloy, or refractory high-entropy alloy per se, but a Nb silicide-based composite.

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

- The paper studies a Nb-Si based alloy powder with nominal composition **Nb-25Ti-2Cr-2Al-16Si (at.%)**.
- The powder is intended for use in additive manufacturing technologies such as **selective laser melting (SLM)** and **direct energy deposition (DED)**.
- The alloy belongs to the Nb-silicide-based composite family, which is a candidate for high-temperature structural applications to replace Ni-based superalloys.
- Stated advantages of Nb-Si based alloys from the introduction: high melting point (1750 °C), low density (6.6–7.2 g/cm³), good specific mechanical properties at elevated temperatures.
- Stated limitations motivating this work: poor room-temperature fracture toughness and insufficient high-temperature oxidation resistance.

# Alloy Composition

- **Nominal alloy composition (at.%):** Nb-25Ti-2Cr-2Al-16Si
  - Nb: balance (approximately 55 at.% if calculated as 100 − 25 − 2 − 2 − 16)
  - Ti: 25 at.%
  - Cr: 2 at.%
  - Al: 2 at.%
  - Si: 16 at.%
- The starting materials were blended elemental powders of Nb, Ti, Cr, Al, and Si, each of 99.9% purity.
- **Impurity discussed:** Fe contamination arises during mechanical alloying from steel grinding media. The Fe content increases drastically with prolonged milling time (notably after 300 min). The paper does not provide a quantitative Fe content in at.% or wt.%.
- No quantitative data on C, O, N, or H impurities are provided in the original paper.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Mechanical alloying:**
  - Equipment: attritor
  - Atmosphere: argon
  - Milling times investigated: 100 min, 250 min, 300 min, 350 min
  - Milling media: steel balls of 10 mm diameter
  - Ball-to-powder ratio: 20:1
- **Classification:**
  - After 300 min of mechanical alloying, the powder was classified to a particle size fraction of **45–71 µm** for further processing.
- **Plasma spheroidization:**
  - Equipment: Tekna TEK-15 plant
  - Plasma torch type: inductively coupled plasma (ICP)
  - Plasma torch power: 15 kW
  - Powder feeding rate: 30 g/min
- No conventional heat treatment, homogenization, rolling, forging, or annealing processes are reported in this paper.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **After mechanical alloying:**
  - Mechanical alloying leads to the formation of a **Nb solid solution**.
  - XRD peak broadening and reduced intensity of Nb diffraction peaks are attributed to grain refinement and lattice distortion.
  - Ti, Cr, Al, and Si enter the lattice interstices of Nb and form a solid solution, causing the lattice constant of Nb to increase and the diffraction peaks to shift toward lower angles.
  - After 100 and 250 min of milling, the powder shows significant inhomogeneity with layered structures and even residual particles of raw elemental powders.
  - After 300 min of milling, no separated elemental powder is observed; the particles consist of a layered structure of relatively uniformly distributed contrasting areas.
  - After 350 min, the microstructure becomes more homogeneous and the particle morphology is near-equiaxed.
  - EDS line scan analysis confirms lack of homogeneity after 100 and 250 min, and more uniform chemical element distribution after 300 and 350 min.
  - After 300 min, Fe contamination from grinding media becomes greatly increased; contamination increases with milling time.

- **After plasma spheroidization:**
  - The powder particle microstructure consists of three contrasting phases in BSE imaging: black, gray, and white.
  - According to XRD analysis:
    - **Black phase:** Nb₅Si₃
    - **Gray phase:** Nb₃Si
    - **White phase:** Nb solid solution (Nbₛₛ)
  - All phases are uniformly distributed across the powder particle and have sizes less than 1 µm.
  - During solidification, phases grow up to **5 µm** at the center of the particle.
  - The melting and rapid quenching process during plasma spheroidization tends to refine the phases and stabilize Nb silicides.
- The paper mentions grain refinement, lattice distortion, and solid-solution formation during mechanical alloying, and phase refinement during plasma spheroidization. Specific mechanisms such as solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, or dispersion strengthening are not quantitatively evaluated for this powder state.
- Grain size values of the final powder are not explicitly given. Phase size <1 µm uniformly, growing up to 5 µm at particle centers.

# Key Conclusions

1. A Nb-based composite powder with chemical composition **Nb-25Ti-2Cr-2Al-16Si (at.%)** was successfully synthesized by mechanical alloying followed by plasma spheroidization.
2. Mechanical alloying leads to the formation of a Nb solid solution, with reduced intensity and broadening of Nb diffraction peaks due to grain refinement and lattice distortion.
3. At **300 min** of milling in an attritor, a uniform distribution of chemical elements is achieved. Further increasing milling time leads to a drastic increase in Fe contamination from grinding media.
4. After plasma spheroidization, the powder microstructure consists of uniformly distributed **Nb₅Si₃**, **Nb₃Si**, and **Nb solid solution** phases. The phase size is generally less than 1 µm, with some growth up to 5 µm at particle centers.
5. The melting and rapid quenching process during plasma spheroidization tends to refine the phases and stabilize Nb silicides.
6. The powder with a fraction of 45–71 µm, high sphericity, and homogeneous phase distribution is considered suitable for additive manufacturing technologies such as selective laser melting and direct energy deposition.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Process-focused study:** The paper focuses only on powder synthesis (mechanical alloying and plasma spheroidization). No consolidated material, additively manufactured parts, or mechanical testing are reported.
- **No quantitative impurity analysis:** Fe contamination is discussed qualitatively, but no quantitative measurement of Fe content, or other impurities (C, O, N, H), is provided.
- **No oxidation testing:** The introduction identifies poor high-temperature oxidation resistance as a limitation of Nb-Si based alloys, but no oxidation experiments are performed.
- **No mechanical properties:** Strength, toughness, ductility, hardness, and creep behavior are not measured for the powder or any consolidated form.
- **Alloy composition fixed:** Only one nominal composition (Nb-25Ti-2Cr-2Al-16Si, at.%) is studied; no compositional variations or comparisons with other alloys are presented.
- **Mechanistic conclusions limited:** Solid-solution formation during mechanical alloying is inferred from XRD and EDS, but no quantitative lattice parameter measurement or dislocation density analysis is reported.
- **Applicability:** The conclusions are applicable to the preparation of spherical Nb-Si based alloy powders for additive manufacturing via mechanical alloying and plasma spheroidization. The general approach may extend to other refractory alloy powder systems, but this is not demonstrated in the paper.
