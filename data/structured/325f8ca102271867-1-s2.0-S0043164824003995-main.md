# Paper Metadata
- **Title**: Microstructure, mechanical properties and high-temperature sliding wear response of a new Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5 high-entropy alloy
- **Authors**: Xiaotian Wu, Lihong Su, Anh Kiet Tieu, Jun Cheng, Cuong Nguyen, Hongtao Zhu, Jun Yang, Guanyu Deng
- **Journal**: Wear
- **Year**: 2025 (Available online 14 November 2024)
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.wear.2024.205634
- **Volume/Pages**: Wear 562-563 (2025) 205634

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Alloy system studied**: Al-Cr-Fe-Ni-V high-entropy alloy system, specifically Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5 (Co-free HEA)
- **Material category**: High-entropy alloy (HEA), with dual-phase BCC + B2 microstructure; not a refractory high-entropy alloy in the conventional sense (no refractory elements such as W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf, Ti in the studied alloy). The studied alloy is based on Al-Cr-Fe-Ni-V, which is a transition metal HEA rather than a refractory HEA.
- **Research objective**: To develop a new V-containing, Co-free HEA (Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5) and investigate its phase constitution, microstructure, mechanical properties (hardness, compressive behavior from RT to 800 °C), and high-temperature sliding wear performance (RT to 800 °C) against Si3N4 ceramic balls. The work aims to understand the influence of V on microstructure, mechanical and tribological properties.

# Alloy Composition
- **Nominal composition**: Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5, atomic ratio Al:Cr:Fe:Ni:V = 1:2:2:2:1, i.e., Al-12.5 at.%, Cr-25 at.%, Fe-25 at.%, Ni-25 at.%, V-12.5 at.%.
- **Measured composition by SEM/EDS (average of as-cast HEA)**: Al-11.8 at.%, Cr-25.1 at.%, Fe-25.5 at.%, Ni-25.2 at.%, V-12.4 at.%.
- **Purity of starting materials**: Al, Cr, Fe, Ni, V pellets with purities of 99.5 wt.%.
- **Elements present**: Al, Cr, Fe, Ni, V. The alloy does **not** contain W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf, Ti, Re, C, B, Y, La, Si, or O as intentional alloying additions.
- **Impurities**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper regarding ppm-level impurities such as C, O, N, H.
- **Number of samples**: One bulk alloy sample (single composition) was fabricated and studied.

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Fabrication method**: Conventional arc melting.
- **Melting atmosphere**: Argon gas protecting atmosphere.
- **Ingot weight**: Approximately 70 g.
- **Number of remelting cycles**: At least five times to ensure chemical homogeneity.
- **Post-processing**: As-cast state; no homogenization, annealing, rolling, forging, or other heat treatments reported in the original paper.
- **Additive manufacturing**: Not used.
- **Powder metallurgy**: Not used.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
- **Phase constitution**: Dominant BCC phase with ordered B2 precipitates.
- **XRD results**: Three distinct diffraction peaks corresponding to (110), (200), and (211) crystal planes of BCC phase. The (110) peak is the strongest.
- **Phases identified by TEM**:
  - Main (Ni, Al)-rich spherical precipitates: ordered B2/BCC structure.
  - (V, Cr, Fe)-rich matrix: disordered BCC structure.
  - Minor (V, Cr)-rich strip-like phase: BCC structure; volume fraction ~4.3%.
- **Average grain size of spherical B2 precipitates**: ~190 nm.
- **Precipitate-matrix orientation relationship**: The B2 precipitate exhibits the same orientation as the BCC parent phase/matrix.
- **Elemental segregation**:
  - V-rich strip-like phase contains ~83.79 at.% V; V in matrix is ~10.51 at.%.
  - (Al, Ni)-rich phase contains high Al and Ni.
  - (V, Cr, Fe)-rich matrix contains high V, Cr, Fe.
- **Microstructural defects**: The as-cast HEA has no obvious porosity or micro-cracks (as confirmed by SEM).
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed in paper**:
  - Solid-solution strengthening: Not explicitly discussed as the primary mechanism, but the paper references the effect of V on lattice distortion/strengthening in the introduction (citing Yin et al.: V provides high strengthening effect due to atomic volume and high misfit volume in both FCC and BCC HEAs).
  - Precipitation strengthening: Formation of fine (Al, Ni)-rich B2 spherical precipitates (~190 nm) within the BCC matrix.
  - Grain-boundary strengthening: Not explicitly discussed.
  - Dispersion strengthening / ODS strengthening: Not applicable (no oxide dispersoids).
  - The paper emphasizes dual-phase (B2 + BCC) composite effects similar to those described for AlCrFeNi-based HEAs.

# Mechanical Properties
- **Average Vickers hardness**: 570.5 ± 28.8 HV (500 g load, 20 s dwell time; fifteen measurements; note: abstract mentions around 570.5 HV, while Section 3.2 mentions 15 measurements; text says 15 measurements for hardness, not ten).
- **Nanoindentation results**:
  - (Al, Ni)-rich phase: nano-hardness ~10.6 ± 0.7 GPa.
  - (V, Cr)-rich phase: nano-hardness ~14.9 ± 0.4 GPa.
  - (V, Cr)-rich phase has slightly higher resolved elastic modulus (Er) than (Al, Ni)-rich phase.
- **Room-temperature compressive properties**:
  - Yield strength: 2210.5 ± 36.2 MPa
  - Ultimate compressive strength: 2532.7 ± 40.8 MPa
  - Total plasticity/plastic strain to failure: 22.1 ± 1.8 %
  - Strain rate: 1 × 10⁻³ 1/s
- **High-temperature compressive properties (Gleeble tests, strain rate 1 × 10⁻³ 1/s)**:
  - At 600 °C: ultimate compressive strength = 1218.6 ± 29.3 MPa; strain to failure ~2.1 times that at RT
  - At 700 °C: ultimate compressive strength = 677.4 ± 14.2 MPa; strain to failure ~70%
  - At 800 °C: ultimate compressive strength = 338.5 ± 12.9 MPa
  - Strength drop from RT to 600 °C: ~51.9% decrease
  - The alloy exhibits good mechanical properties and thermal stability up to 700 °C.
- **Fracture characteristics at RT**: Brittle fracture, cleavage fracture features with river patterns. Fracture surface near Cr, Fe, Ni-rich region is relatively smooth; V, Cr-rich region shows river pattern.
- **Hardness of counterface material**: Si3N4 ball hardness ~1600 HV.
- **Elastic modulus, fracture toughness, creep, rupture life**: Not reported in the original paper.
- **Density**: Not reported in the original paper.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability
- **Oxidation during wear testing**: Localized surface oxidation was detected by SEM-EDS and XPS analyses.
- **Oxidation products**:
  - At RT to 300 °C: V2O5 is the primary wear-induced oxidation product (from Raman).
  - At 500–600 °C: NiO and FeO peaks; various coexisting oxides; vanadium oxide peaks absent at 500–600 °C.
  - At 700 °C: Cr2O5 (noted in paper as Cr2O5, likely Cr2O3 or chromium oxide) and Fe2O3/V2O5.
  - At 800 °C: β-V2O5, Fe2O3, V2O5, Cr2O3, Al2O3, NiO, Ni2O3, NiCr2O4.
- **XPS-detected oxides**:
  - Cr2O3 (binding energies: 576.6 eV 2p3/2, 586.4 eV 2p1/2)
  - Al2O3 (71–79 eV)
  - Fe2O3 (710.8 eV 2p3/2, 724.3 eV 2p1/2)
  - NiO, Ni2O3, NiCr2O4
  - VO2 (RT), V2O5 (800 °C)
- **Oxidation temperature range studied**: RT to 800 °C during sliding wear.
- **Effect of alloying elements on oxidation**: V2O5 has relatively low hardness and fails to provide adequate wear protection at 800 °C, leading to increased wear rate and friction. Cr and Al form denser protective oxide sublayers.
- **Issues**:
  - Thermal softening above 600 °C leads to reduced strength and increased wear.
  - Oxidation becomes significant above 600 °C; oxidative wear dominates at ≥600 °C.
  - Microcracks form in the friction layer at 500 °C and above.
  - The paper does not report static oxidation tests (no weight gain data). All oxidation information is from sliding wear conditions.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Element: V (Vanadium)]
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Al-Cr-Fe-Ni-V high-entropy alloy system; also relevant to other HEAs containing V.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Nominal 12.5 at.% V (measured 12.4 at.%) in Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5; V-rich phase contains ~83.79 at.% V; matrix contains ~10.51 at.% V.
- **Main Function**: Solid-solution strengthening, formation of (V, Cr)-rich BCC phase, influence on tribological properties via formation of V oxides (V2O5, VO2).
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: V partitions to the (V, Cr)-rich BCC matrix and forms a minor (V, Cr)-rich strip-like phase. According to literature cited in the paper (Yin et al., Acta Mater. 188 (2020) 486–491), V provides a high strengthening effect in both FCC and BCC HEAs due to atomic volume mismatch and high misfit volume.
- **Property Effect**:
  - The current Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5 HEA exhibits Vickers hardness ~570.5 HV, RT compressive strength ~2532.7 MPa, plasticity ~22.1%.
  - At 600 °C compressive strength is 1218.6 MPa.
  - V2O5 formation at high temperature (800 °C) has low hardness and fails to provide wear protection, leading to increased wear rate.
- **Potential Side Effects**: V promotes sigma phase formation in CoCrFeNi and CoCrFeNiMn HEAs (according to literature cited in paper, Salishchev et al.); V2O5 formation at high temperature can reduce wear resistance; V addition may decrease plastic strain in some HEAs (Dong et al. for AlCoCrFeNiVx).
- **Original Evidence**: XRD and TEM analyses in Figs. 1, 3–5; EDS point analysis showing V-rich phase with 83.79 at.% V; Raman and XPS analyses showing V2O5 and VO2 formation; nanoindentation showing (V, Cr)-rich phase has hardness ~14.9 GPa vs (Al, Ni)-rich phase ~10.6 GPa.

## [Element combination: Al + Ni]
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Al-Cr-Fe-Ni-V high-entropy alloy system; also relevant to Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni and Al-Cr-Fe-Ni HEAs.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Nominal Al 12.5 at.% + Ni 25 at.% in Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5; (Al, Ni)-rich spherical precipitates form with average grain size ~190 nm.
- **Main Function**: Formation of ordered B2 phase precipitates, precipitation strengthening, influence on mechanical properties.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: (Al, Ni)-rich spherical precipitates (~190 nm) form within the (V, Cr, Fe)-rich BCC matrix. The precipitates have ordered B2/BCC structure and share the same orientation as the BCC matrix. B2 ordered structure originates from transformation of disordered BCC phase (consistent with Al-Cr-Fe-Ni HEA literature, e.g., Jin et al., J. Alloy Compd. 770 (2019) 655–661; Yu et al., Mater. Lett. 138 (2015) 78–80).
- **Property Effect**:
  - (Al, Ni)-rich phase exhibits lower nano-hardness (~10.6 GPa) and greater maximum/residual nanoindentation depth than (V, Cr)-rich phase (~14.9 GPa).
  - The overall Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5 HEA shows good RT compressive strength and plasticity.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not specifically reported in the paper for this combination.
- **Original Evidence**: TEM and STEM analyses in Figs. 3–5; nanoindentation results in Fig. 6; HAADF-STEM and FFT analyses showing B2/BCC ordered structure with same orientation as BCC matrix.

## [Element combination: V + Cr]
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Al-Cr-Fe-Ni-V high-entropy alloy system; also relevant to V-Cr containing BCC HEAs.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Cr 25 at.% + V 12.5 at.% nominal; (V, Cr)-rich phase contains ~83.79 at.% V; (V, Cr)-rich matrix contains ~10.51 at.% V.
- **Main Function**: Formation of BCC phases, contribution to hardness and strength, influence on tribological oxidation products.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: The (V, Cr)-rich phase exhibits BCC crystal structure, consistent with theoretical calculation by Gao et al. and experimental observations by Eggert et al. (cited in paper). The (V, Cr)-rich matrix surrounds B2 precipitates. V and Cr partition together into the BCC phase.
- **Property Effect**:
  - (V, Cr)-rich phase nano-hardness ~14.9 ± 0.4 GPa, higher than (Al, Ni)-rich phase ~10.6 ± 0.7 GPa.
  - At high temperature, Cr oxides (Cr2O3) form and contribute to protective glaze layer; V forms V2O5 with low hardness, which reduces wear protection at 800 °C.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Cr oxide (Cr2O5/Cr2O3) and V oxide (V2O5) formation can influence wear; V2O5 is soft and leads to increased wear.
- **Original Evidence**: TEM and SAED analyses in Figs. 3–4; nanoindentation in Fig. 6; Raman and XPS analyses in Figs. 15–16 and Tables 3–4.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening
- **Related Elements**: V, Cr, Fe, Ni, Al in the studied alloy; V is highlighted in literature as a strong solid-solution strengthener in both FCC and BCC HEAs (Yin et al. cited in paper).
- **Typical Alloys**: Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5; AlCoCrFeNiVx; CoCrFeNiV; CoCrFeMnNiVx.
- **Key Compositions**: Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5 (Cr ~25 at.%, V ~12.4 at.%); AlCoCrFeNiV (V 16.7 at.% with lower plasticity of 0.7%).
- **Microstructural Changes**: Solid solution formation with lattice distortion due to atomic size mismatch; V has atomic volume large in FCC HEA and small in BCC HEA, high misfit volume.
- **Property Improvements**:
  - Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5: Ultimate compressive strength 2532.7 MPa at RT, 1218.6 MPa at 600 °C.
  - AlCoCrFeNiV0.2: Ultimate strength 3297.8 MPa, plasticity 26.8% (from Table 2, literature).
  - VCoNi: Ultimate strength 1300 MPa, plasticity 38% (Table 2, literature).
- **Risks and Limitations**: Solid-solution strengthening may reduce ductility; AlCoCrFeNiV (full V equiatomic) has plasticity only 0.7% (Table 2).
- **Original Evidence**: Introduction discussion citing Yin et al.; Table 2 comparison of V-containing HEAs; XRD and TEM showing BCC/B2 phases.

## Precipitation Strengthening
- **Related Elements**: Al and Ni (forming B2 phase); Cr and Fe also present in matrix.
- **Typical Alloys**: Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5; Al-Cr-Fe-Ni based HEAs such as CrFeNi2Al (Jin et al., cited in paper).
- **Key Compositions**: (Al, Ni)-rich B2 spherical precipitates of ~190 nm average size.
- **Microstructural Changes**: Fine spherical B2 precipitates form within the BCC matrix. The B2 precipitates have same orientation as the BCC parent phase. B2 forms by ordering transformation from disordered BCC.
- **Property Improvements**:
  - Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5: RT compressive strength 2532.7 MPa; hardness 570.5 HV.
  - CrFeNi2Al (literature): maximum mechanical strength 1357 MPa, total ductility 6.4% due to nano-lamellar microstructure.
- **Risks and Limitations**: B2/brittle phase may contribute to brittle fracture behavior; Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5 shows brittle cleavage fracture at RT.
- **Original Evidence**: TEM and HRTEM analyses in Figs. 3–5; SAED patterns showing BCC and B2 phases; HAADF-STEM images showing (Al, Ni)-rich spherical precipitates.

## Oxidation-Resistant Alloying
- **Related Elements**: Cr (forms Cr2O3), Al (forms Al2O3), V (forms V2O5/VO2), Ni (forms NiO/Ni2O3/NiCr2O4), Fe (forms Fe2O3/FeO).
- **Typical Alloys**: Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5 studied here.
- **Key Compositions**: Not specifically optimized for oxidation resistance; the paper discusses oxidation as a byproduct of high-temperature wear.
- **Microstructural Changes**: Formation of layered oxide tribolayers at high temperature: outermost Fe-rich friable layer, middle dense Cr/Al oxide protective sublayer, innermost Ni/Fe-rich plastically deformed sublayer. At 800 °C, the oxide layer consists of multiple sublayers.
- **Property Improvements**: At 600–800 °C, a compact glaze layer reduces friction coefficient; Cr2O3 and Al2O3 provide protective denser sublayer. Near-surface nano-hardness increases with test temperature: 11.2 ± 0.18 GPa at RT → 12.2 ± 0.21 GPa at 300 °C → 13.4 ± 0.23 GPa at 600 °C → 16.1 ± 0.26 GPa at 800 °C.
- **Risks and Limitations**: V2O5 is soft and reduces wear protection at 800 °C; oxidative wear dominates above 600 °C; microcracks in friction layers above 500 °C.
- **Original Evidence**: Raman analysis in Fig. 15; XPS analysis in Fig. 16 and Table 4; cross-section SEM-EDS in Figs. 17–18 and Table 5; nano-hardness profiles in Fig. 19.

# Key Conclusions
1. The as-cast Al0.5CrFeNiV0.5 HEA consists of three phases: main (Ni, Al)-rich B2 precipitates, (V, Cr, Fe)-rich BCC matrix, and a minor (V, Cr)-rich strip-like phase with volume fraction ~4.3%.
2. At RT, the alloy exhibits average Vickers hardness 570.5 ± 28.8 HV, ultimate compressive strength 2532.7 ± 40.8 MPa, and total plasticity 22.1 ± 1.8%.
3. The alloy maintains good thermal stability of mechanical strength up to 600 °C (ultimate compressive strength 1218.6 ± 29.3 MPa), but strength decreases significantly at 700 °C (677.4 ± 14.2 MPa) and 800 °C (338.5 ± 12.9 MPa).
4. The friction coefficient changes only slightly (0.4–0.5) between RT and 800 °C, but wear rate increases monotonically with temperature, particularly above 600 °C.
5. At low temperatures (RT–500 °C), abrasion is the primary wear mechanism; at ≥600 °C, adhesive and oxidative wear become dominant due to thermal softening and enhanced oxidation.
6. At 800 °C, the friction layer stratifies into multiple sublayers: an outermost Fe-rich friable layer, a middle dense Cr/Al oxide protective layer, and an innermost Ni/Fe-rich plastically deformed region.
7. Near-surface nano-hardness increases with wear testing temperature due to oxide formation (11.2 GPa at RT to 16.1 GPa at 800 °C), but the presence of soft V2O5 limits wear protection at 800 °C.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Reported Limitations**:
  - The nanoindentation results could not distinguish B2 and BCC phases due to small grain sizes and indenter tip dimension limitations; only average results for (Al, Ni)-rich and (V, Cr)-rich phases are reported.
  - Only one alloy composition was studied; no systematic composition variation was performed.
  - No static oxidation tests were performed; oxidation data are derived from sliding wear conditions.
  - The paper does not report tensile properties, fracture toughness, creep, or long-term high-temperature stability.
  - The as-cast state was studied; no heat treatments or thermomechanical processing were applied.
- **Applicable Scope**:
  - The findings are relevant to Co-free Al-Cr-Fe-Ni-V HEAs for potential structural and tribological applications up to ~700 °C.
  - The paper provides a database point for V-containing HEAs and their room- and high-temperature compressive properties, as summarized in Table 2.
  - The wear results apply to dry sliding conditions against Si3N4 balls under 20 N load, 1 Hz frequency, 20 min duration, at temperatures RT–800 °C.
  - The study is limited to one alloy composition; generalization to other Al-Cr-Fe-Ni-V compositions requires additional data.
- **Not Explicitly Stated in the Original Paper**:
  - ppm-level impurity concentrations (C, O, N, H) in the starting materials or final alloy.
  - Density and elastic modulus (other than resolved elastic modulus from nanoindentation) values.
  - Fracture toughness and creep properties.
  - Detailed atomic-scale mechanisms of B2 precipitate strengthening; the paper focuses on microstructural characterization and property measurements rather than quantitative strengthening model analysis.
