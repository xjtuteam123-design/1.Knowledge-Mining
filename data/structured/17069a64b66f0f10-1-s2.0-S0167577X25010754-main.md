# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title**: Synergistic enhancement of strength and ductility in laser direct energy deposited Ti-6Al-4V alloy via dual-microalloying with Cu and Nb
- **Authors**: Tao Kuang, Chi Zhang, Yang Liu, Mingyong Jia, Yaojun Lin, Wenze Li, Fei Chen
- **Journal**: Materials Letters
- **Year**: 2025
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.matlet.2025.139046
- **Alloy system studied**: Ti-6Al-4V (titanium alloy) with Cu and/or Nb microalloying additions, fabricated by laser-directed energy deposition (LDED)
- **Research objective**: To overcome the strength-ductility trade-off in LDED Ti-6Al-4V alloys through a dual-microalloying strategy using Cu and Nb, elucidating the individual and synergistic effects of Cu and Nb additions on phase composition, grain morphology, and mechanical performance.
- **Material category**: Additively manufactured titanium alloy (LDED Ti-6Al-4V with microalloying). Not a refractory high-temperature alloy system.

# Alloy System and Research Object

**Important note**: This paper is about Ti-6Al-4V titanium alloy, not a refractory Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloy system. The alloy contains Nb as a microalloying element, but the base alloy is Ti-based. The knowledge extracted below must be interpreted in the context of Ti-6Al-4V, not refractory alloys.

Four distinct Ti-6Al-4V variants fabricated via LDED:
1. Unmodified Ti-6Al-4V
2. 0.2 wt% Nb-modified Ti-6Al-4V
3. 1.0 wt% Cu-modified Ti-6Al-4V
4. Dual-modified (1.0 wt% Cu + 0.2 wt% Nb) Ti-6Al-4V

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Matrix phase:**
- α-Ti with hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structure (dominant matrix phase)
- β-Ti phase present at nanometer scale between α-phase grains; content gradually increases with Cu and Nb additions
- Needle-like martensitic structure observed in pure Ti-6Al-4V
- Basket-weave structure composed of lath-like grains in all four alloys

**Second phases / precipitates:**
- **Ti₂Cu intermetallic phase**: exhibits body-centered tetragonal (BCT) crystal structure; precipitated along β grain boundaries (intergranular precipitation); preferentially aligned along interlamellar regions
- **Nb-induced precipitates**: higher density of nanoscale precipitates compared to pure Ti-6Al-4V (nature of these precipitates not explicitly identified in the extracted text)

**Grain size and α-lath width:**
- Pure Ti-6Al-4V: average α-lath width ~257 nm (above 250 nm)
- Ti-6Al-4V + Nb: average α-lath width above 250 nm (negligible effect on α-lath width)
- Ti-6Al-4V + Cu: average α-lath width reduced to ~230 nm
- Ti-6Al-4V + Cu + Nb: average α-lath width reduced to ~229 nm
- α-lath refinement from 257 nm → 229 nm (with Cu addition)

**Dislocation density:**
- Average KAM value for pure Ti-6Al-4V: 0.38°
- Average KAM value for Cu-Nb dual-modified: 0.45°
- Both Cu and Nb additions elevate dislocation density; Cu demonstrates more pronounced effect via dislocation strengthening

**Elemental segregation:**
- EDS mapping indicates uniformly distributed elements in the Cu-Nb dual-modified alloy
- Line-scan elemental distribution shows depletion of Ti and Al alongside Cu and V enrichment along grain boundaries, indicating intergranular phase formation driven by elemental redistribution during LDED processing

**Microstructural features of dual Cu-Nb modified alloy:**
- Predominantly martensitic microstructure with network-like texture surrounding needle-like martensitic morphology
- Reticulated structure due to intergranular Ti₂Cu precipitation along β grain boundaries and solute segregation effect
- Ti₂Cu intermetallic phases preferentially aligned along interlamellar regions
- α-Ti grains display lamellar structure with heterogeneous size distribution

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed in the paper:**
- Solid-solution strengthening (Cu)
- Precipitation strengthening (Ti₂Cu intermetallic)
- Grain-boundary strengthening / boundary strengthening
- Dislocation strengthening (increased dislocation density)
- α-lath refinement / grain refinement
- Second-phase hardening (Ti₂Cu)
- β-phase stabilization (Nb-mediated)

**[Cu]**

- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Ti-6Al-4V titanium alloy with Cu microalloying (LDED-processed)

- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 1.0 wt% Cu (individual and in dual modification with Nb)

- **[Main Function]**: Strength enhancement via solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening (Ti₂Cu intermetallic formation), α-lath refinement, and dislocation density increase

- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**:
  - Cu addition significantly reduces average α-lath width from ~257 nm to ~230 nm
  - The significant undercooling effect induced by Cu broadens the constitutional supercooling zone, counteracts thermal gradients under high laser energy, and reduces α-lath width
  - Solid-solution strengthening effect of Cu and formation of precipitates lead to lattice distortion of α-Ti, hindering dislocation slip and increasing KAM values
  - Ti₂Cu intermetallic phases (BCT crystal structure) precipitated intergranularly along β grain boundaries and preferentially aligned along interlamellar regions
  - Cu demonstrates a more pronounced effect on dislocation strengthening than Nb

- **[Property Effect]**:
  - UTS: 975.1 MPa → 1079.6 MPa with 1.0 wt% Cu (+10.7% enhancement relative to base Ti-6Al-4V)
  - Elongation: 7.3% → 6.7% with 1.0 wt% Cu (−8.2% deterioration relative to base Ti-6Al-4V)
  - Average α-lath width reduced from ~257 nm to ~230 nm
  - Dislocation density (KAM value) increased compared to pure Ti-6Al-4V
  - Testing at room temperature

- **[Potential Side Effects]**:
  - Reduced ductility: elongation decreased from 7.3% to 6.7% (−8.2%) when Cu added individually
  - Ti₂Cu phase precipitation can degrade ductility
  - Fracture morphology shows both cleavage facets and dimples, indicating reduced ductility
  - This is consistent with prior literature: Li et al. demonstrated that introducing 1.0 wt% Cu into Ti-6Al-4V enhanced UTS by 30.9% while reducing ductility by 34.4%; with increasing Cu content, strength gradually increases whereas ductility shows a pronounced decline

- **[Original Evidence]**:

  - Section 3, Results and Discussion: "Cu significantly reduces the average width of α-laths to 230 nm"
  - "The individual addition of Cu elevates the UTS to 1079.6 MPa, whereas the elongation declines to 6.7%"

- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Ti-6Al-4V titanium alloy with Nb microalloying (LDED-processed)

- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 0.2 wt% Nb (individual and in dual modification with Cu)

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

---

**1. Solid-Solution Strengthening**

- **Related elements**: Cu
- **Typical alloys**: Cu-modified/Dual Cu-Nb modified Ti-6Al-4V (LDED)
- **Key compositions**: 1.0 wt% Cu
- **Microstructural changes**: Lattice distortion of α-Ti; increased dislocation density (KAM value)
- **Property improvements**: UTS increase from 975.1 to 1079.6 MPa (Cu alone) or 1182.3 MPa (Cu-Nb)
- **Risks and limitations**: Ductility reduction when Cu is added alone (elongation 7.3% → 6.7%); Ti₂Cu precipitation may dominate over solid-solution strengthening effects
- **Original evidence**: "The solid solution strengthening effect of Cu and the formation of precipitates lead to lattice distortion of α-Ti, which can hinder dislocation slip and increase KAM values" (Section 3)

---

**2. Precipitation Strengthening (Ti₂Cu / Intermetallic)**

- **Related elements**: Cu (forming Ti₂Cu); relative to Ti-Cu intermetallic systems
- **Typical alloys**: Cu-modified and Cu-Nb dual-modified Ti-6Al-4V (LDED)
- **Key compositions**: 1.0 wt% Cu
- **Microstructural changes**: Ti₂Cu intermetallic phases (BCT structure) precipitated intergranularly along β grain boundaries; preferentially aligned along interlamellar regions
- **Property improvements**: Boundary strengthening and second-phase hardening contribute to enhanced UTS
- **Risks and limitations**: Individual Cu addition degrades ductility via Ti₂Cu phase precipitation (cleavage facets observed); excessive Cu content gradually increases strength but pronouncedly decreases ductility
- **Original evidence**: "Ti₂Cu intermetallic phases are preferentially aligned along interlamellar regions. This specific architecture enhances strength through combined mechanisms of boundary strengthening and second-phase hardening" (Section 3)

---

**3. Grain-Boundary Strengthening / Boundary Strengthening**

- **Related elements**: Cu (Ti₂Cu at grain boundaries)
- **Typical alloys**: Cu-Nb dual-modified Ti-6Al-4V (LDED)
- **Key compositions**: 1.0 wt% Cu + 0.2 wt% Nb
- **Microstructural changes**: Intergranular Ti₂Cu precipitation along β grain boundaries; reticulated structure; network-like texture
- **Property improvements**: Contributes to 21.2% UTS enhancement and 17.8% elongation enhancement (room temperature)
- **Risks and limitations**: Intergranular precipitation when not balanced by β-phase stabilization (from Nb) can lead to ductility loss
- **Original evidence**: "The refinement of α-lath, coupled with the synergistic effect of Ti₂Cu precipitates at grain boundaries, is expected to simultaneously improve both strength and ductility" (Section 3)

# Key Conclusions

1. **Cu-Nb dual-microalloying overcomes the strength-ductility trade-off**: The simultaneous addition of 1.0 wt% Cu and 0.2 wt% Nb to Ti-6Al-4V achieves UTS of 1182.3 MPa (+21.2%) and elongation of 8.6% (+17.8%) relative to unmodified LDED Ti-6Al-4V (UTS: 975.1 MPa, elongation: 7.3%).

2. **Cu alone increases strength but reduces ductility**: 1.0 wt% Cu addition elevates UTS to 1079.6 MPa (+10.7%) but reduces elongation to 6.7% (−8.2%), consistent with prior literature showing Cu-induced strength-ductility trade-off.

3. **Nb alone modestly enhances both strength and ductility**: 0.2 wt% Nb addition yields UTS of 1017.3 MPa (+4.3%) and elongation of 8.0% (+9.6%), attributed to β-phase stabilization and nanoscale precipitate formation.

4. **Complementary mechanisms drive synergy**: α-lath refinement (257 nm → 229 nm), enhanced dislocation density (KAM: 0.38° → 0.45°), intergranular Ti₂Cu precipitation, β-phase stabilization, and network structure formation collectively contribute to the dual enhancement.

5. **Microstructural origin of synergy**: The reticulated structure formed by intergranular Ti₂Cu precipitation along β grain boundaries combined with the solute segregation effect enables simultaneous strength and ductility enhancement, providing a new paradigm for compositionally tailored in-situ precipitation engineering in additive manufacturing.

6. **Cu-induced undercooling and constitutional supercooling**: The significant undercooling effect from Cu broadens the constitutional supercooling zone, counteracts thermal gradients under high laser energy, and reduces α-lath width, demonstrating the role of Cu in refining the microstructure during LDED.

7. **Nb-mediated β-phase stabilization**: Nb addition effectively stabilizes the β phase, improving ductility through β-phase-mediated plastic deformation mechanisms, which counteracts the embrittling tendency of Ti₂Cu precipitation.

8. **Fracture morphology confirms synergy**: Pure Ti-6Al-4V and Nb-modified alloys show predominantly dimple features; Cu-modified alloy shows both cleavage facets and dimples (indicating reduced ductility); Cu-Nb dual-modified alloy shows predominantly dimple features, confirming restored ductility.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Not a refractory alloy study**: This paper investigates Ti-6Al-4V titanium alloy with Cu and Nb microalloying. It does not involve Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloy systems. Extracted element-function knowledge (especially for Nb) must be understood in the context of titanium alloy microalloying, not refractory alloy strengthening.

2. **Limited compositional range**: Only 0.2 wt% Nb and 1.0 wt% Cu additions are systematically studied. The joint optimization space for Cu and Nb compositions is not explored. The authors cite prior literature for higher Cu contents but do not independently test a wide composition range.

3. **Room-temperature tensile properties only**: Mechanical characterization is limited to room-temperature tensile testing. No high-temperature strength, creep, oxidation resistance, or fatigue properties are reported. The service stability of the Cu-Nb modified alloy at elevated temperatures or in oxidizing environments is not addressed.

4. **As-deposited condition only**: All microstructure and property data are for the LDED as-deposited condition. No heat treatment studies are reported; the stability of Ti₂Cu precipitates and microstructural evolution under heat treatment are not investigated.

5. **Single processing method**: Only LDED with one set of processing parameters (360 W laser power, 8.5 mm/s scanning speed) is studied. The transferability of the Cu-Nb dual-microalloying strategy to other AM methods (e.g., LPBF/SLM, EBM) or conventional processing is not addressed.

6. **Limited characterization of Nb-induced precipitates**: The paper mentions that Nb incorporation results in a higher density of nanoscale precipitates, but the exact nature (composition, crystal structure) of these Nb-induced precipitates is not explicitly identified in the extracted text.

7. **No oxidation or corrosion evaluation**: The oxygen content is controlled during deposition (below 20 ppm), but no oxidation resistance or corrosion behavior is characterized. The impact of Cu on titanium alloy corrosion resistance is not discussed.

8. **The paper does not explore** high-temperature embrittlement, oxygen/nitrogen embrittlement, or grain-boundary oxidation behavior, which are of particular interest in refractory alloy contexts but may be relevant to elevated-temperature applications of titanium alloys as well.
