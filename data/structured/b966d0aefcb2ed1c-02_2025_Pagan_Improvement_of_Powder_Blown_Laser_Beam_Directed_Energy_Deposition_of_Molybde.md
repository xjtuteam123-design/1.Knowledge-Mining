# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Improvement of Powder Blown Laser Beam-Directed Energy Deposition of Molybdenum Using Lanthanum Oxide Additions
- **Authors**: Michael Pagan, Andrew Hutchinson, Nathaniel J. Lies, Aaron P. Stebner
- **Journal**: JOM, Vol. 77, No. 10, 2025, pp. 7235–7246
- **Year**: 2025 (published online April 10, 2025)
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11837-025-07343-w
- **Alloy system studied**: Mo-based alloys with La₂O₃ additions (MoLa)
- **Research objective**: To investigate the use of La₂O₃ additions for improving the directed energy deposition laser beam blown powder (DED-LB-BP) additive manufacturing of molybdenum; to refine grain size, reduce porosity and cracking, and enable processing at lower laser powers (<2000 W) than previously reported (4500–9000 W)
- **Material category**: Additively manufactured Mo-based refractory alloy with lanthanum oxide dispersion (ODS-type Mo–La₂O₃)

# Alloy System and Research Object

- Base material: commercially pure Mo powder (Mo-102)
- Dispersoid addition: La₂O₃ (lanthana) nanoparticles
- Alloy system: Mo–La₂O₃; laser powder blown directed energy deposition (DED-LB-BP)
- Research aim: improvement of DED-LB-BP processability of Mo via La₂O₃ ceramic additions; study of grain refinement, porosity reduction, and crack mitigation

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Powder mixing**
- Mixing method: tumble mixing in aluminum bottle
- Mixing equipment: Accuris Instruments W3300-500 Compact Balance
- Rotation speed: 40 rpm, total time 6 h, spinning direction alternated every 2 h
- Batches of max 3 kg
- Resulting powder morphology: electrostatic adhesion of La₂O₃ to Mo particle surfaces (Fig. 3c)

**Powder characteristics**
- D10, D50, D90 for pure Mo: 48.8 ± 1.0 μm, 60.2 ± 0.9 μm, 73.7 ± 1.0 μm
- D10, D50, D90 for MoLa (2.1 wt.%): 43.1 ± 0.5 μm, 59.1 ± 0.5 μm, 80.1 ± 1.2 μm
- Hall flow rate pure Mo: 11.04 s (for 50.0 g)
- Hall flow rate MoLa: 14.29 s
- Powder feed rate (identical carrier gas): pure Mo 2.18 g/min/auger rpm; MoLa 1.54 g/min/auger rpm

**DED-LB-BP process**
- Equipment: Optomec LENS 860 hybrid manufacturing system
- Laser: nLight 1070-nm fiber laser, max power 2000 W
- Spot size: Gaussian, 600 μm diameter; focal length 200 μm
- Oxygen level: ≤45 ppm during manufacturing
- Nozzles: 4 discrete coaxial copper nozzles, aperture 1.3 mm
- Carrier gas flow: 5.1 LPM
- Center gas flow: 21.2 LPM
- Initial working distance: 5.715 mm
- Layer height: 0.3 mm
- Interlayer dwell: 1 s
- Scan strategy: longitudinal, +X 25.4 mm then –X 25.4 mm, 25 layers, 7.5 mm tall walls
- Build plate: Mo, 152.4 mm × 76.2 mm × 38.1 mm, sandblasted with aluminum oxide to reduce reflectivity
- Lead in/out distance with 2.54-mm z-step between layers

**Design of Experiments (DoE) parameters**
- Zone-1 (low / middle / high)
  - Laser power: 700 / 1100 / 1500 W
  - Scan speed: 4 / 5 / 6 mm/min (stated as mm/min in Table II; note laser speed in Table III is mm/s — values in Table III use 4, 6, etc. mm/s)
  - Mass flow: 6.48 / 7.48 / 8.48 g/min
- Zone-2 (low / middle / high)
  - Laser power: 1500 / 1700 / 1900 W
  - Scan speed: 3 / 4 / 5 mm/s
  - Mass flow: 5.48 / 6.48 / 7.48 g/min

**Best parameters identified**
- For Mo + 2.1 wt.% La₂O₃: (1500 W, 5.48 g/min, 3 mm/s) and (1700 W, 6.48 g/min, 4 mm/s) achieved >99% density
- For direct comparison, pure Mo was deposited using (1500 W, 5.48 g/min, 3 mm/s) yielding 95% density, and (1700 W, 6.48 g/min, 4 mm/s) yielding 96% density

**Substrate preparation**
- Machined Mo build plate, resurfaced by milling and sandblasting with Al₂O₃ particles

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Matrix phase**
- BCC Mo (not explicitly stated as BCC but implied for Mo)
- La₂O₃ remains as separated dispersed particles; no dissolution into Mo matrix confirmed by XRF (Fig. 3g, h)

**Pure Mo microstructure (no La₂O₃)**
- Very large grains: average 0.4 mm wide, extending several mm (entire build height)
- “Zig-zag” epitaxial grain growth due to alternating scan pattern
- Spherical pores inside grains, likely MoOₓ contamination that vaporizes upon melting
- Cracking along grain boundaries
- Density achieved: 95–96%

**MoLa (with La₂O₃) microstructure**
- Grain refinement for all La₂O₃ concentrations (0.7–3.5 wt.%)
- Minimum grain size <200 μm at 2.1 wt.% La₂O₃ (in XZ plane of wall)
- La₂O₃ particles preferentially located at grain boundaries, some in grain interiors (Fig. 3e, f)
- Continuous boundaries through microstructure; epitaxial-like growth (“frustrated epitaxy”) rather than equiaxed structure typical of inoculation
- Grain size decrease partially attributed to thinner deposits due to changed geometric fidelity (underbuilding)
- Printed densities >99% for MoLa at optimized parameters

**Grain refinement mechanism assessment**
- Authors conclude La₂O₃ acts primarily as grain boundary pinner, not inoculant
- Rationale: (i) La₂O₃ melting temperature lower than Mo, thus no solid La₂O₃–liquid Mo interface for heterogeneous nucleation; (ii) continuous boundaries and similar grain width per layer suggest dependence on previous layer; (iii) lack of clear inoculant nucleation events; (iv) refinement in build direction partly due to underbuilt thinner layers

**Distribution of La₂O₃**
- Primarily at Mo grain boundaries, also in grain interiors (Fig. 3e, f)
- XRF confirms strong La localization in spherical regions, proving no dissolution into Mo matrix

**Porosity**
- Pure Mo: significant porosity; spherical pores inside grains
- MoLa: limited porosity; >99% density at optimized parameters

**Cracking**
- Pure Mo: intergranular cracking along long vertical grain boundaries
- MoLa: reduced cracking

- No yield strength, tensile strength, compressive strength, elongation, or hardness values are provided.
- No high-temperature mechanical property testing is reported.

- The paper cites previous literature that La₂O₃ additions to Mo (MoLa) can increase mechanical and corrosive properties, recrystallization temperature, room-temperature and high-temperature mechanical strengthening, creep resistance, and fracture toughness (Refs. 26, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36, 37).

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

The original paper does not report oxidation testing of printed materials.

- No oxidation weight gain, oxide layer characterization, or oxidation temperature/duration reported.
- The paper cites previous literature that La₂O₃ absorbs impurities and oxides away from Mo during processing, traps oxygen, and prevents formation of brittle Mo oxides (Refs. 26, 30, 31, 32, 35, 51–53).
- The paper discusses the mechanism: La₂O₃ particles scavenge oxides/impurities away from Mo, and prefer to reside at grain boundaries, thus mitigating intergranular embrittlement caused by brittle molybdenum oxide and carbide formation.
- The paper also mentions pure Mo suffers from oxide embrittlement and intergranular brittleness due to impurity (C, N, O) enrichment at grain boundaries.

**[La₂O₃ (lanthana nanoparticles)]**

[Applicable Alloy System]
DED-LB-BP additively manufactured Mo; ODS-type Mo–La₂O₃

[Addition Amount or Composition Range]
0.7 wt.%, 1.4 wt.%, 2.1 wt.%, 2.8 wt.%, 3.5 wt.% La₂O₃ (corresponding to 1.09–5.42 vol.%); optimal grain refinement observed at 2.1 wt.% La₂O₃

[Main Function]
Grain refiner (grain boundary pinner) for molybdenum during DED-LB-BP; reduces porosity and intergranular cracking; scavenges impurities/oxides from Mo; improves final part density.

[Microstructural Mechanism]
- La₂O₃ particles electrostatically adhere to Mo powder surfaces during mixing, remain as separate dispersed particles in the melt and solidify preferentially at Mo grain boundaries.
- La₂O₃ acts as a grain boundary pinner inhibiting epitaxial growth across layers, not as an inoculant (La₂O₃ melting temperature lower than Mo; no solid–liquid interface for heterogeneous nucleation).
- Continuous boundaries and similar grain widths across layers indicate “frustrated epitaxy.”
- La₂O₃ traps oxygen and impurities, preventing formation of brittle Mo oxides/carbides at grain boundaries.
- Spherical pores in pure Mo are attributed to MoOₓ contamination vaporization; La₂O₃ mitigates this.

[Property Effect]
- Grain size: reduced from several millimeters (pure Mo) to <200 μm at 2.1 wt.% La₂O₃ (in build direction, XZ plane). Original grain size of pure Mo extends entire sample length; pure Mo average width 0.4 mm.
- Density: >99% for 2.1 wt.% MoLa at optimized parameters, vs. 95–96% for pure Mo under same parameters.
- Cracking: reduced with La₂O₃ additions.
- The original paper does not provide direct mechanical property values.

[Potential Side Effects]
- Reduced powder flowability (Hall flow from 11.04 s to 14.29 s; feed rate from 2.18 to 1.54 g/min/auger rpm)
- Reduced geometric fidelity: wavy and jagged walls, underbuilding, thinner layer deposits; wall waviness similar to pure Mo with too low laser power, suggesting La₂O₃ affects laser energy absorption
- Theoretical density decreases with increasing La₂O₃ (from 10.180 g/cm³ at 0.7 wt.% to 10.019 g/cm³ at 3.5 wt.%)

[Original Evidence]
Fig. 3c (electrostatic adhesion of La₂O₃ on Mo powder surface); Fig. 3e, f (particles at grain boundaries and grain interior); Fig. 3g, h (XRF confirming La localization and non-dissolution); Fig. 4 (grain refinement with La₂O₃ addition); Fig. 6 (grain size reduction); Section “Microstructure of Pure Molybdenum and MoLa Samples”; Table I (compositions and densities); Conclusion section.

# Key Conclusions

1. Pure Mo DED-LB-BP samples exhibit very large epitaxial grains (several millimeters, encompassing entire build height), average 0.4 mm wide, with spherical porosity and intergranular cracking.

2. Adding 0.7–3.5 wt.% La₂O₃ to Mo powder significantly decreases grain size, porosity, and cracking.

3. Minimum grain size (<200 μm) is achieved at 2.1 wt.% La₂O₃.

4. Mo + 2.1 wt.% La₂O₃ samples printed at optimized parameters (1500 W, 5.48 g/min, 3 mm/s; and 1700 W, 6.48 g/min, 4 mm/s) achieved >99% density, compared to 95% and 96% for pure Mo under identical parameters.

5. La₂O₃ particles remain as separated dispersed particles, preferentially at grain boundaries; they do not dissolve into the Mo matrix (confirmed by XRF).

6. The grain refinement mechanism is not inoculation but likely grain boundary pinning (“frustrated epitaxy”), since La₂O₃ has a lower melting temperature than Mo and no solid La₂O₃–liquid Mo interface exists during solidification.

7. The study demonstrates feasibility of DED-LB-BP of Mo at laser powers of 1500–1900 W, significantly lower than previous 4500–9000 W reports.

8. Trade-off: lower laser power approach has a lower deposition rate (108 mm³/min vs. 1397 mm³/min for 9000 W, 10.58 mm/s) and higher estimated laser energy consumption for equivalent volume, but achieves superior microstructure and density.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- The original paper does not report direct mechanical properties of printed materials. All property claims (e.g., strengthening, creep resistance, fracture toughness) for La₂O₃–Mo are from cited literature, not measured in this study.
- The paper does not measure oxidation resistance of the printed alloys directly.
- The CCD-RSM statistical optimization lacked replicates; AIC variable selection without replicates is acknowledged by authors as not statistically rigorous.
- Density comparisons between pure Mo and MoLa were made only at two parameter sets; the highest-density MoLa parameter set from the response surface was not retested as pure Mo.
- The grain size analysis was performed only in the XZ plane (plane of the wall) using line intercept; other planes were not characterized.
- Geometric fidelity of MoLa samples was lower (wavy/jagged walls); no quantitative measurement of dimensional accuracy is reported.
- The energy consumption comparison is rough, ignoring auxiliary systems, build overlap, powder waste, and motor power consumption.
- The paper does not provide direct evidence of La₂O₃ particle size distribution in the final microstructure beyond SEM/Z contrast and XRF mapping.
- The applicability is limited to DED-LB-BP of Mo thin-wall structures; bulk component behavior is not addressed.
- The paper does not explore how La₂O₃ affects the melting temperature or absorption mechanisms quantitatively; the statement that La₂O₃ affects laser energy absorption is inferred from waviness, not directly measured.
