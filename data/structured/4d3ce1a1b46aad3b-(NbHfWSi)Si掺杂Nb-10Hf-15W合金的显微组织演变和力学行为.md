# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Microstructural evolution and mechanical behaviour of Nb–10Hf–15W alloy doped with Si
- **Authors**: Junhua Liao, Fan Yu, Zhenhe Wei, Jiangbo Sha
- **Journal**: Materials Science and Technology
- **Year**: 2021
- **DOI**: 10.1080/02670836.2021.1987695
- **Alloy System Studied**: Nb–Hf–W–Si system
- **Research Objective**: To understand how Si additions affect the phase constitutions, microstructural evolution, and mechanical behaviour of a multi-component Nb–10Hf–15W alloy under as-cast and heat-treated conditions
- **Material Category**: Nb-W-based solid-solution alloy with silicide (Nb5Si3) reinforcement, dual-phase NbSS/Nb5Si3 microstructure

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper investigates Si-doped Nb–10Hf–15W alloys with Si contents of 0, 1, 3, and 5 at.%. The base alloy Nb–10Hf–15W is a Nb solid-solution alloy with moderate strength and ductility at room and high temperatures. Si was added to form a dual-phase NbSS/Nb5Si3 microstructure to strengthen the alloy without negatively impacting ductility and formability. The Si content was limited to ~5 at.% to control the phase fraction of the silicide.

# Alloy Composition

**Nominal compositions of alloys studied (in at.%):**
- **0Si**: Nb–10Hf–15W (no Si)
- **1Si**: Nb–10Hf–15W–1Si
- **3Si**: Nb–10Hf–15W–3Si
- **5Si**: Nb–10Hf–15W–5Si

**Actual measured chemical concentrations (at.%):**

| Alloy | Nb | Hf | W | Si |
|-------|------|------|------|------|
| 0Si | 75.33 | 9.53 | 15.14 | – |
| 1Si | 74.10 | 9.74 | 15.23 | 0.93 |
| 3Si | 72.12 | 9.85 | 15.12 | 2.91 |
| 5Si | 70.05 | 9.97 | 15.09 | 4.89 |

A small amount of weight loss of Hf and Si elements occurred during the six times remelting process.

**Impurity phases**: Less than 0.5% volume fraction of white particles identified as HfO2 is present in both as-cast and heat-treated samples. Purity of all raw elements was 99.95% (in mass) or higher. No specific C, O, N, or H ppm-level impurity concentrations are explicitly stated in the original paper.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Melting procedure:**
- Button ingots of Nb–10Hf–15W–xSi alloys (x = 0, 1, 3, 5 at.%), each weighing 150 g
- Prepared by vacuum arc melting (VAR)
- Remelted six times to homogenize the composition of the ingots

**Heat treatment:**
- Annealed at 1600 °C in vacuum for 100 h
- Followed by furnace cooling to ensure quasi-equilibrium microstructures

**Specimen preparation for mechanical testing:**
- Specimens were mechanically polished using SiC paper (1200-grit) with water as lubricant before testing
- For WD-XRF analysis, ingots were cut to a cylinder 10 mm in diameter and 3 mm in height, polished with SiC paper up to 3000 grit

**Additive manufacturing**: Not used in this paper.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## As-cast samples:
- **0Si and 1Si alloys**: Single Nb solid solution (NbSS) phase, all of Hf, W, and Si substitute into the NbSS matrix
- **3Si alloy**: Primary dendrite-like NbSS arms plus grey strips and islands distributed in interdendritic regions
- **5Si alloy**: NbSS matrix plus networks (less than 10 μm in width) of eutectic consisting of bright NbSS phase and grey Nb3Si phase in interdendritic regions

## Heat-treated samples (1600 °C/100 h):
- **0Si alloy**: Single NbSS microstructure
- **1Si alloy**: α-Nb5Si3 particles precipitate in the NbSS matrix
- **3Si alloy**: NbSS matrix with eutectoid NbSS/α-Nb5Si3 dots (2–7 μm in size) and strips (~5 μm in width); area fraction f ≈ 4.8%
- **5Si alloy**: Eutectoid NbSS/α-Nb5Si3 network (5–8 μm in width); area fraction f ≈ 11.5%; some smaller dot-like α-Nb5Si3 phase (~2 μm in size) emerge in the NbSS matrix

A eutectoid decomposition of Nb3Si → NbSS + α-Nb5Si3 takes place in the 3Si and 5Si alloys during heat treatment at 1600 °C for 100 h.

## Phase composition data:

**NbSS phase composition (range):** (73.18–75.06) Nb – (15.74–16.75) W – (8.91–9.71) Hf – (~0.91) Si

**α-Nb5Si3 phase in heat-treated samples:**
- In 1Si: 35.21 Nb, 27.51 Hf, 37.28 Si (at.%), no W
- In 3Si: 34.20 Nb, 29.03 Hf, 36.77 Si (at.%), no W
- In 5Si: 35.23 Nb, 25.98 Hf, 38.79 Si (at.%), no W

The atomic ratio of Nb+Hf to Si is close to 5:3, so Nb5Si3 can be regarded as a multiple silicide (Nb, Hf)5Si3 phase.

## Elemental segregation:
- In as-cast samples, W atoms partition to the primary NbSS phase rather than into the NbSS+Nb3Si eutectic
- Hf atoms partition preferentially to the eutectic; Hf concentration in the eutectic zone is twice that of the primary NbSS phase
- Si partitions to the eutectic rather than to the NbSS phase
- After heat treatment, Si concentration in the NbSS phase decreases due to diffusion from primary NbSS into the Nb5Si3 phase

## Grain size:
- NbSS grains in both as-cast and heat-treated samples are as large as a few hundred micrometres (up to 100 μm order)

## Strengthening mechanisms discussed:
- Solid-solution strengthening (W, Hf, Si in NbSS)
- Second-phase strengthening (α-Nb5Si3)
- Grain-boundary strengthening by silicide at high temperature
- Grain refinement discussed as a potential route to improve toughness (from cited literature)

# Mechanical Properties

## Vickers Hardness (Hv):

| Sample | As-cast Hv | Heat-treated Hv |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| 0Si | 440 ± 55 | 410 ± 31 |
| 1Si | 500 ± 48 | 465 ± 37 |
| 3Si | 545 ± 44 | 490 ± 54 |
| 5Si | 620 ± 66 | 565 ± 48 |

Hardness increases monotonously with increasing Si content, with an increment of 35–40% in Hv when Si increases from 0 to 5 at.%. For each composition, the as-cast sample has the highest Hv value and the heat-treated sample at 1600 °C is the lowest, by 10%.

Test conditions: Applied load of 4.9 N, loading time of 15 s, average of ten measurements.

## Fracture Toughness (KQ):

| Sample | As-cast KQ (MPa·m^1/2) | Heat-treated KQ (MPa·m^1/2) |
|--------|------------------------|-----------------------------|
| 0Si | 10.58 ± 1.12 | 7.83 ± 0.64 |
| 1Si | 8.76 ± 1.03 | 7.55 ± 0.77 |
| 3Si | 8.01 ± 0.75 | 7.89 ± 0.81 |
| 5Si | 7.72 ± 0.83 | 6.52 ± 0.71 |

Test method: Single-notched three-point-bending plates, notch up to a/w = 0.5, dimensions 30 mm × 6 mm × 3 mm, loading span of 24 mm, crosshead displacement rate of 0.1 mm min⁻¹, room temperature.

## Compressive Properties of Heat-Treated Alloys:

| Alloy | RT σ0.2 (MPa) | RT σmax (MPa) | RT εf (%) | 1250°C σ0.2 (MPa) | 1350°C σ0.2 (MPa) |
|-------|--------------|--------------|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 0Si | 800 | 1545 | 21.3 | 498 | 371 |
| 1Si | 1200 | 1583 | 17.5 | 575 | 465 |
| 3Si | 1260 | 1570 | 14.3 | 622 | 529 |
| 5Si | 1300 | 1698 | 9.1 | 677 | 543 |

Test conditions: Room-temperature compressive tests at strain rate of 3×10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ on Φ13 mm × 5 mm cylindrical samples. High-temperature compressive tests at 1250 and 1350 °C in argon atmosphere at strain rate of 3×10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ using Gleeble 1500. Compressive cylinders measured 6 mm in diameter and 9 mm in length.

At 1250 and 1350 °C, the Si-containing samples show strain-hardening stage after yielding up to a strain of 45%, while the 0Si sample shows flat or small increase in post-yield curve.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

**[Element or Element Combination]: W (Tungsten)**

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: Nb–Hf–W–Si solid-solution alloys with silicide reinforcement

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 15 at.% nominal; actual measured 15.09–15.23 at.% in bulk alloys; 15.74–16.75 at.% in NbSS phase

**[Main Function]**: Strong solid-solution strengthening element for Nb-based alloys

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Substitutes into the NbSS phase as a solute atom; partitions preferentially to the primary NbSS phase rather than the Nb3Si eutectic; W is completely absent from the α-Nb5Si3 phase

**[Property Effect]**: Hv of Nb increases from ~80 (pure Nb) to 230 (Nb–10W at.%) to 410 for the NbSS phase in Nb–10Hf–15W solid solution; contributes to room-temperature σ0.2 of 800 MPa and high-temperature strength of 498 MPa at 1250 °C and 371 MPa at 1350 °C in the Si-free alloy

**[Potential Side Effects]**: Larger amounts of W lead to increased density of Nb alloys, decreasing specific density, which is important for aeronautics and astronautics applications; raises the brittle-to-ductile transition temperature (BDTT) of Nb; increases Peierls–Nabarro barrier energy, reducing intrinsic toughness and ductility; promoted cleavage failure of the NbSS phase

**[Original Evidence]**: "W and Mo are the strongest solid-solution strengthening elements for Nb." "The increase of the P–N energy due to these solid-solution strengthening elements restricts the emission and movement of dislocations from the crack tip and thus decreases the intrinsic toughness and ductility of the Nb solid solution."

**[Element or Element Combination]: Hf (Hafnium)**

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: Nb–Hf–W–Si solid-solution alloys with silicide reinforcement

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 10 at.% nominal; actual measured 9.53–9.97 at.% in bulk alloys; 8.91–10.17 at.% in NbSS phase; 25.98–29.03 at.% in α-Nb5Si3 phase

**[Main Function]**: Solid-solution strengthening element; also partitions to the α-Nb5Si3 phase, forming (Nb, Hf)5Si3

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Substitutes into both the NbSS matrix and the Nb5Si3 lattice; partitions preferentially to the eutectic in as-cast condition, with Hf concentration in the eutectic zone twice that of the primary NbSS phase

**[Property Effect]**: The increased Hv from 230 (Nb–10W) to 410 for the nominal composition Nb–10Hf–15W solid solution results from the additional 8.91Hf + 6.03W solute atoms, contributing significant solid-solution hardening. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Hf-only strengthening.

**[Potential Side Effects]**: Formation of HfO2 impurity particles (<0.5% volume fraction) in both as-cast and heat-treated samples; Hf content lost during remelting process (from 10 at.% nominal to 9.53–9.97 at.% measured)

**[Original Evidence]**: "The combination of Hf and W creates a significant solid-solution hardening effect on NbSS." "Less than a 0.5% volume fraction of white particles is present in both the as-cast and the heat-treated samples, which was determined to be the HfO2."

**[Element or Element Combination]: Si (Silicon, forming Nb5Si3 silicide)**

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: Nb–Hf–W–Si dual-phase NbSS/Nb5Si3 microstructure

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 0–5 at.% nominal; actual measured 0.93–4.89 at.%; NbSS phase contains only 0.18–0.25 at.% Si after heat treatment

**[Main Function]**: Forms Nb5Si3 silicide phase for second-phase strengthening; strengthens NbSS grain boundaries at high temperature

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: 
- As-cast: forms Nb3Si + NbSS eutectic in interdendritic regions; Si partitions to eutectic rather than NbSS phase
- Heat-treated: Nb3Si undergoes eutectoid decomposition Nb3Si → NbSS + α-Nb5Si3; silicide forms network or discontinuous distribution along NbSS grain boundaries
- α-Nb5Si3 fraction increases with increasing Si content: 3.5% (3Si as-cast), 9.3% (5Si as-cast), 4.8% (3Si heat-treated), 11.5% (5Si heat-treated)

**[Property Effect]**: 
- Hv increases monotonously with increasing Si content (from 440 to 620 in as-cast; from 410 to 565 in heat-treated; 35–40% increment)
- Room-temperature σ0.2 increases from 800 to 1300 MPa, σmax from 1545 to 1698 MPa
- At 1250 °C, σ0.2 increases from 498 to 677 MPa; at 1350 °C from 371 to 543 MPa
- High-temperature strain hardening is maintained up to ~40–45% strain
- α-Nb5Si3 phase has compressive fracture strength of ~670 MPa even at 1500 °C (from cited literature)

**[Potential Side Effects]**: Decreases room-temperature fracture toughness (KQ from 10.58 to 7.72 MPa·m^1/2 in as-cast) and fracture strain (from 21.3% to 9.1%); silicide is preferred crack initiation and propagation site; increases stress triaxiality constraint on ductile NbSS phase

**[Original Evidence]**: "With the addition of Si, the compressive strength of the heat-treated alloys at 1250 and 1350 °C increases without reducing the ductility through strengthening the NbSS grain boundaries by the α-Nb5Si3 silicide."

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

---

**[Mechanism]: Solid-Solution Strengthening**

**[Related Elements]**: W, Hf, Si (in NbSS phase)

**[Typical Alloys]**: Nb–10Hf–15W–xSi (x = 0–5)

**[Key Compositions]**: NbSS phase contains 15.74–16.75 at.% W, 8.91–10.17 at.% Hf, 0.18–0.25 at.% Si (heat-treated)

**[Microstructural Changes]**: W, Hf, Si substitute into BCC Nb lattice; W partitions preferentially to primary NbSS; Si concentration in NbSS decreases after heat treatment

**[Property Improvements]**: 
- Hv of NbSS phase: 410–465 (vs. 80 for pure Nb, 230 for Nb–10W)
- Contributes to high-temperature strength: σ0.2 = 371–543 MPa at 1350 °C

**[Risks and Limitations]**: W and Si raise BDTT; increase Peierls–Nabarro barrier energy; restrict dislocation emission and movement; promote cleavage failure; increased density from W

---

**[Mechanism]: Second-Phase (Silicide) Strengthening**

**[Related Elements]**: Si, Hf (forming α-Nb5Si3 / (Nb,Hf)5Si3)

**[Typical Alloys]**: Nb–10Hf–15W–3Si, Nb–10Hf–15W–5Si

**[Key Compositions]**: 3–5 at.% Si; silicide fraction 4.8–11.5% after heat treatment

**[Microstructural Changes]**: 
- As-cast: Nb3Si + NbSS eutectic in interdendritic regions
- Heat-treated: eutectoid decomposition Nb3Si → NbSS + α-Nb5Si3; discontinuous network (3Si) or continuous network (5Si) along NbSS grain boundaries

**[Property Improvements]**: 
- Room-temperature σ0.2: 800 → 1300 MPa (0Si → 5Si)
- 1250 °C: 498 → 677 MPa

**[Risks and Limitations]**: Silicide is preferred crack initiation and propagation site; reduces room-temperature fracture toughness from 7.89 to 6.52 MPa·m^1/2; increases stress triaxiality constraint on ductile NbSS phase; reduces room-temperature fracture strain from 21.3% to 9.1%

**[Mechanism]: Grain-Boundary Strengthening by Silicide at High Temperature**

**[Related Elements]**: Si (forming α-Nb5Si3 network along boundaries)

**[Typical Alloys]**: Nb–10Hf–15W–3Si, Nb–10Hf–15W–5Si

**[Key Compositions]**: 3–5 at.% Si with silicide network along NbSS boundaries

**[Risks and Limitations]**: At room temperature, continuous eutectoid zones are preferred crack propagation pathways; silicide brittleness limits effectiveness at lower temperatures

# Key Conclusions

1. **Microstructure evolution**: As-cast 0Si and 1Si alloys contain a single NbSS phase, while 3Si and 5Si alloys consist of primary dendrite-like NbSS plus (NbSS+Nb3Si) eutectic. After heat treatment at 1600 °C for 100 h, the Nb3Si decomposition to NbSS+α-Nb5Si3 occurs in the 3Si and 5Si alloys. The α-Nb5Si3 silicide fraction increases with increasing nominal Si content.

2. **High-temperature strengthening**: The addition of Si increases the compressive strength of heat-treated alloys at 1250 and 1350 °C without reducing ductility through strengthening the NbSS grain boundaries by the α-Nb5Si3 silicide. As Si content increases up to 5 at.%, the bulk 0.2% yield strength increases from 498 to 677 MPa at 1250 °C and from 371 to 543 MPa at 1350 °C.

3. **Room-temperature embrittlement**: Si additions significantly decrease the fracture toughness of as-cast samples and the compressive fracture strain of heat-treated samples at room temperature. The fracture strain of heat-treated samples decreases from 21.3 to 9.1% as Si increases to 5 at.%; the KQ of as-cast samples decreases from 10.58 to 7.72 MPa·m^1/2. The lowest KQ value of 6.52 MPa·m^1/2 is obtained in the 5Si sample heat-treated at 1600 °C.

4. **Failure mechanisms**: For single NbSS microstructure, the crack propagates along the NbSS grain boundaries, with intergranular fracture as the dominant mode. For the NbSS/α-Nb5Si3 microstructure, the crack propagates through the brittle α-Nb5Si3 phase causing brittle failure in bending, while the NbSS phase fails in cleavage mode.

5. **Mechanism of room-temperature toughness degradation**: Three factors are identified: (1) W and Si substituting in NbSS embrittle Nb by raising BDTT and increasing Peierls–Nabarro barrier energy; (2) large grain size of NbSS phase (up to 100 μm) promotes cleavage failure; (3) increasing α-Nb5Si3 fraction reduces bulk toughness through rule-of-mixtures effect, preferred crack initiation sites, and increased stress triaxiality constraint on ductile NbSS phase.

6. **Formability promise**: At 1250 and 1350 °C, the NbSS/α-Nb5Si3 microstructure maintains strain hardening up to 40–45% strain, indicating excellent plastic deformation capability for thermomechanical processing such as hot extrusion, forging, or rolling.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Limited Si content range**: The study only investigates Si additions of 0–5 at.%; higher Si contents that may form continuous eutectic networks remain unexplored.

2. **No oxidation resistance data**: The paper does not provide systematic oxidation resistance testing, despite identifying HfO2 impurity particles in both as-cast and heat-treated samples.

3. **No creep or rupture performance**: Mechanical property characterization focuses on compressive strength and fracture toughness; no creep performance or rupture life data are presented.

4. **Coarse grain microstructure**: NbSS grain size remains up to 100 μm order after arc melting and heat treatment; the authors cite literature showing that grain refinement to 5–10 μm via SPS could significantly improve toughness.

5. **Single heat treatment condition**: Only one heat treatment schedule (1600 °C for 100 h with furnace cooling) is investigated; effects of different heat treatment temperatures or cooling rates are not explored.

6. **Small-scale samples**: Button ingots weighing 150 g were used; scaling effects on microstructural uniformity and mechanical properties for industrial-scale processing are not addressed.

7. **Room-temperature ductility penalty**: The Si addition reduces room-temperature ductility and toughness; practical applications at low temperatures require careful composition optimization balancing high-temperature strength with low-temperature damage tolerance.
