# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Fatigue behavior of Pt-modified aluminum coated single crystal superalloy at 900 ℃
- **Authors**: Jiawei Wang, Tao Guo, Kewei Gao, Xiaolu Pang
- **Journal**: International Journal of Fatigue
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2023.107596
- **Alloy system studied**: Ni-based second-generation single crystal superalloy (DD5) with Pt-modified aluminide bond coats
- **Research objective**: To investigate the effect of different diffusion-resistance bond coats (β-(Ni,Pt)Al coating with Re-base diffusion barrier and γ′ coating) on the high-cycle fatigue performance of a second-generation single crystal superalloy at 900 ℃
- **Material category**: Ni-based single crystal superalloy with Pt-modified aluminide coatings; not a refractory alloy (Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloy)

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Substrate**: Second-generation single crystal superalloy DD5
- **Coatings studied**:
  1. β-(Ni,Pt)Al coating with Re-base diffusion barrier (referred to as "β coating")
  2. γ′ coating (γ′-Ni3Al-based)

**Note on scope**: This paper does not concern Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. The paper is about a Ni-based single crystal superalloy with Pt-modified aluminide coatings, tested under high-cycle fatigue at 900 ℃. However, it does discuss refractory elements (Re, W, Mo, Ta, Cr) and their roles in the superalloy substrate and coating, which is relevant to precipitate formation (TCP phases) and solid-solution strengthening.

## Coatings
- **β coating**: Outer layer composed of β-NiAl phase; intermediate diffusion barrier composed of Re-rich σ phase (tetragonal, enriched with Re and Cr); interdiffusion zone (IDZ) containing TCP rods (enriched with Re, Cr, W, Mo)
- **γ′ coating**: γ′-Ni3Al with W- and Re-rich precipitated phases; alumina particles near the surface

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Substrate (DD5)
- Matrix: face-centered cubic (FCC) γ
- Precipitates: cuboidal γ′ (Ni3Al) with average size approximately 300 nm, 68.6% volume fraction
- After fatigue at 900 ℃: surface oxidation forms Al2O3, bulk AlN near surface, γ′-free zone (thickness 4 μm) due to Al depletion, surface recrystallization

## β Coating
- Three-layer structure: outer layer (β-NiAl), intermediate diffusion barrier (Re-rich σ phase, tetragonal crystal structure), interdiffusion zone (IDZ) containing white TCP rods
- Re-rich particles enriched with Re and Cr (σ phase)
- During fatigue at 900 ℃: tiny Re-rich particles coalesce, diffusion barrier thickness reduced from 12.1 μm to 7.0 μm
- Needle-like TCP phases in secondary reaction zone (SRZ) increase
- Microcracks observed in interdiffusion zone
- Dislocation pile-up at σ/β phase boundaries; stacking faults on (200) planes in σ phase; some σ phases break

## γ′ Coating
- γ′-Ni3Al matrix with W- and Re-rich precipitated phases (σ phase)
- Alumina particles near surface
- Average grain size reduced from 1.8 μm to 1.2 μm after fatigue; maximum grain size reduced from 4 μm to 2.6 μm
- During fatigue: σ phases dissolve as γ′ transforms to γ due to selective oxidation of Al; refractory elements (Re, Cr) dissolve homogeneously in γ phase
- Internal oxidation penetrates into substrate along crack paths

## Casting Defects
- Bare sample: casting pores observed; at 350 MPa, an internal casting pore with length 80 μm located 80 μm from surface
- γ′ coating sample at 362.5 MPa: casting pore and slip lines near crack source
- β coating sample at 340 MPa: casting pore with 73 μm length located 121 μm from surface

# Mechanical Properties

## High-Cycle Fatigue (HCF) at 900 ℃
- **Test conditions**: Rotating-bend fatigue, temperature 900 ℃, load ratio R = −1, frequency f = 80 Hz, sinusoidal waveform
- **Fatigue strength at 10^7 cycles**:
  - Bare DD5: 337.5 MPa
  - β coating sample: 325 MPa
  - γ′ coating sample: 350 MPa
- **Behavior by stress range**:
  - Above 375 MPa: bare sample and γ′ coating sample have similar fatigue life
  - Above 350 MPa: no significant difference in fatigue life between β coating and bare samples
  - Below 337.5 MPa: β coating has negative effect on fatigue life
  - Below 350 MPa: γ′ coating has positive effect on fatigue life
- **Hardness of substrate**: 550 Hv (from reference [19])

## Fracture Observations
- Bare sample at 350 MPa: fatal fatigue crack initiated from internal casting defect (80 μm pore at 80 μm from surface); crack on {111} crystallographic plane with step-like feature
- γ′ coating sample at 362.5 MPa: fatal crack initiated from surface due to bond coat cracking first; casting pore and slip lines also observed near crack source
- β coating sample at 375 MPa: crack initiated from bond coat; substrate oxidation near interface
- β coating sample at 340 MPa: multiple crack origins (surface and internal casting pore); casting pore 73 μm at 121 μm from surface

## Crack Initiation Analysis
- All fatigue cracks in bare samples initiated from casting defects
- Bond coats changed fatal defect from near-surface to internal; maximum stress intensity factor of fatal defect decreased
- Fatigue crack sources of coated samples located on surface

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxidation of bare DD5 at 900 ℃**: Surface oxidation produces stable continuous Al2O3 film; bulk AlN formed near surface (due to Cr content >5% and Al content)
- **γ′ coating oxidation**: Protective thermally grown oxide (TGO), predominantly α-Al2O3, forms on top of bond coat; TGO thickness increases with cycle number; oxidation occurs at grain boundaries of bond coat; internal oxidation penetrates into substrate
- **β coating oxidation**: Brittle aluminum oxide generated on surface and grain boundaries of bond coat; cracks easily under cyclic stress
- **Element interdiffusion**: β coating's Re-base diffusion barrier inhibits Al and Ni interdiffusion; γ′ coating shows no obvious interdiffusion with substrate
- **TCP phases in SRZ**: Needle-like TCP phases in secondary reaction zone decrease ductility and creep resistance; microcracks initiate at tips of brittle intermetallic precipitates

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Re (Rhenium)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-based single crystal superalloy (DD5); β coating diffusion barrier; γ′ coating precipitates
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 3.0 wt.% in DD5 substrate; forms Re-rich σ phase in diffusion barrier and coatings
- **Main Function**: Solid-solution strengthening; forms σ phase TCP precipitates; in β coating, serves as diffusion barrier to inhibit Al/Ni interdiffusion
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: In DD5 substrate, Re contributes to γ/γ′ microstructure stability and helps resist plastic deformation. In β coating, Re-rich σ phase particles (tetragonal) in the diffusion barrier coalesce at high temperature (900 ℃), reducing barrier thickness from 12.1 μm to 7.0 μm. Dislocations pile up at σ/β phase boundaries due to different Burgers vectors. In γ′ coating, σ phases dissolve as γ′ transforms to γ; Re dissolves homogeneously in γ phase, providing solid-solution strengthening.
- **Property Effect**: In γ′ coating, solution of Re in γ phase resists plastic deformation and prevents fatigue crack propagation. However, in β coating, Re-rich σ phase causes intergranular embrittlement, stress concentration, and microcrack formation. The β coating with Re-base diffusion barrier decreased fatigue strength of substrate from 337.5 MPa to 325 MPa at 10^7 cycles.
- **Potential Side Effects**: TCP σ phase is brittle; causes stress concentration; dislocation pile-up at σ/β boundaries; σ-phase cracking initiates fatigue cracks; Re-base diffusion barrier causes intergranular embrittlement and reduces fatigue life.
- **Original Evidence**: "The Re-rich particle belonged to σ phase with a tetragonal crystal structure [8]." "The Re-base diffusion barrier has a deleterious effect on mechanical properties by causing intergranular embrittlement." "Solution strengthening effect of these refractory elements is significant importance for the γ phase to resist plastic deformation and prevent fatigue crack propagation." (Fig. 9, Fig. 13, Section 4.1)

## Cr (Chromium)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-based single crystal superalloy DD5 (7.0 wt.%); β coating σ phase; γ′ coating precipitates
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 7.0 wt.% in substrate; enriched in Re-rich σ phase particles
- **Main Function**: Oxidation resistance (promotes Al2O3 formation); TCP phase constituent; solid-solution strengthening
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: In bare DD5, Cr content >5% with Al promotes formation of continuous Al2O3 scale. In β coating, Cr is enriched in σ phase particles. In γ′ coating, as γ′ transforms to γ due to Al selective oxidation, Cr dissolves homogeneously in γ phase, contributing to solid-solution strengthening.
- **Property Effect**: The σ phase dissolution in γ′ coating eliminates stress concentration, reducing fatigue crack initiation probability. In β coating, Cr-containing σ phase in diffusion barrier coalesces at 900 ℃, reducing barrier thickness.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Cr can form TCP phases when combined with Re, W, Mo; TCP phases are brittle and cause stress concentration.
- **Original Evidence**: "The presence of a certain amount of Cr in high temperature alloys with Al content higher than 5% results in the formation of a stable and continuous outer Al2O3 film." "Tetragonal σ-phases enriched in Re and Cr precipitated out during the preparation process due to their limited solubility in the intermetallic phases γ′ [31]." (Fig. 7, Fig. 9a)

## Al (Aluminum)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-based single crystal superalloy DD5 (6.2 wt.%); both β and γ′ coatings
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 6.2 wt.% in substrate; β-NiAl outer layer; γ′-Ni3Al coating
- **Main Function**: Forms γ′ phase (Ni3Al) and β phase (NiAl); promotes protective Al2O3 scale formation; oxidation resistance
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: In bare DD5, selective oxidation of Al forms continuous Al2O3 scale. Depletion of Al leads to γ′ decomposition and formation of γ′-free zone (4 μm). In γ′ coating, selective oxidation of Al causes γ′→γ transformation, allowing dissolution of refractory elements. In β coating, Al2O3 forms on surface and grain boundaries, and cracks under cyclic stress.
- **Property Effect**: Al depletion during fatigue leads to surface recrystallization and local plastic deformation, but these do not form fatal fatigue cracks. In γ′ coating, the aluminum content was sufficient to maintain selective oxidation during the short-duration fatigue test; long-term fatigue properties may be affected by low Al content.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Al depletion leads to γ′-free zone formation; selective oxidation consumes Al from coating; internal oxidation penetrates into substrate along crack paths.
- **Original Evidence**: "The presence of a certain amount of Cr in high temperature alloys with Al content higher than 5% results in the formation of a stable and continuous outer Al2O3 film." "Due to the depletion of Al elements, the γ′ phase decomposes and a γ′ free zone with a thickness of 4 μm is formed." "The γ′ phase in the bond coat transforms to γ phase due to the selective oxidation of aluminum." (Fig. 7b, Fig. 9a and b, Section 3.5)

## Pt (Platinum)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Pt-modified aluminide coatings on Ni-based single crystal superalloy
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Present in both β-(Ni,Pt)Al and γ′ coatings; specific Pt content not stated in this paper
- **Main Function**: Promotes selective oxidation of Al; improves oxidation resistance; enhances α-Al2O3 scale adhesion and stability
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Pt promotes selective oxidation of aluminum and maintenance of protective α-Al2O3 scale. In coatings, Pt is present in both β-NiAl and γ′-Ni3Al phases (not explicitly quantified).
- **Property Effect**: Pt-modified aluminide coatings possess superior oxidation resistance compared to unmodified coatings. Both β-(Ni,Pt)Al and γ′ coatings form α-Al2O3 TGO at 900 ℃.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not directly addressed in this paper; however, Pt addition may affect interdiffusion behavior (not explicitly stated).
- **Original Evidence**: "The presence of Pt in the coating can promote the selective oxidation and adhesion of α-Al2O3 scale [5], improve the stability of alumina and decrease the oxidation rate [6]." (Introduction, Section 4.1)

## W (Tungsten)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-based single crystal superalloy DD5 (5.0 wt.%); γ′ coating precipitates
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 5.0 wt.% in substrate; forms W-rich precipitates in γ′ coating
- **Main Function**: Solid-solution strengthening; TCP phase constituent; contributes to γ/γ′ stability
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: In γ′ coating, W-rich precipitated phases (σ phase) form during preparation. As γ′ transforms to γ due to Al selective oxidation, W dissolves into γ phase, contributing to solid-solution strengthening. In β coating, W is also present in TCP phases in interdiffusion zone.
- **Property Effect**: Solution of W in γ phase resists plastic deformation and prevents fatigue crack propagation in γ′ coating.
- **Potential Side Effects**: W contributes to TCP phase formation, which are brittle and cause stress concentration.
- **Original Evidence**: "The tracer EDS elemental mappings reveal that the white particles distributed in the γ′ matrix are the precipitated phases of W and Re." "TCP particles are composed of refractory elements, such as Re, Cr, W and Mo." (Fig. 2b, Section 4.1)

## Mo (Molybdenum)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-based single crystal superalloy DD5 (1.5 wt.%)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 1.5 wt.% in substrate
- **Main Function**: Solid-solution strengthening; TCP phase constituent
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Mo is found in TCP particles (along with Re, Cr, W) formed in the interdiffusion zone of β coating.
- **Property Effect**: TCP phase formation absorbs refractory elements from superalloy, weakening solid-solution strengthening of the substrate.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Contributes to brittle TCP phase formation; reduces substrate strength when absorbed into TCP.
- **Original Evidence**: "TCP particles are composed of refractory elements, such as Re, Cr, W and Mo. The formation of TCP phase absorbs a large amount of refractory elements in the superalloy, which greatly weakens the solid solution strengthening effect of these elements and leads to a reduction in the strength of the substrate." (Section 4.1)

## Ta (Tantalum)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-based single crystal superalloy DD5 (6.5 wt.%)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 6.5 wt.% in substrate
- **Main Function**: γ′ phase former; solid-solution strengthening; high-temperature strength
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Ta is a γ′ forming element in Ni-based superalloys (not discussed in detail in this paper).
- **Property Effect**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values or detailed mechanism for Ta.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

- **Main Function**: Grain boundary strengthening; improves coating adhesion; improves oxidation resistance (not discussed in detail in this paper)

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: Re, W, Mo, Cr, Ta (refractory elements)
- **Typical Alloys**: Ni-based single crystal superalloy DD5 and its coatings
- **Key Compositions**: Re 3.0 wt.%, W 5.0 wt.%, Mo 1.5 wt.%, Cr 7.0 wt.%, Ta 6.5 wt.% in DD5 substrate; Re and Cr enriched in σ phase of coatings
- **Microstructural Changes**: In γ′ coating, as γ′ transforms to γ due to Al selective oxidation, refractory elements (Re, Cr) dissolve homogeneously into γ phase; in β coating, TCP phase formation absorbs refractory elements, weakening solid-solution strengthening
- **Property Improvements**: In γ′ coating, solution of refractory elements in γ phase resists plastic deformation and prevents fatigue crack propagation; this is cited as a key reason for better fatigue performance of γ′ coating (350 MPa at 10^7 cycles vs. 325 MPa for β coating)
- **Risks and Limitations**: When refractory elements are consumed by TCP phase formation (as in β coating's interdiffusion zone), solid-solution strengthening is reduced; TCP phases are brittle and cause stress concentration and crack initiation
- **Original Evidence**: "Solution strengthening effect of these refractory elements is significant importance for the γ phase to resist plastic deformation and prevent fatigue crack propagation." "The formation of TCP phase absorbs a large amount of refractory elements in the superalloy, which greatly weakens the solid solution strengthening effect of these elements and leads to a reduction in the strength of the substrate." (Section 3.5, Section 4.1)

## Precipitation Hardening (γ′ Phase)

- **Related Elements**: Al, Ti (not present), Ta, Ni
- **Typical Alloys**: Ni-based single crystal superalloy DD5
- **Key Compositions**: 68.6% cuboidal γ′ precipitates embedded in γ matrix; γ′ phase based on Ni3Al
- **Microstructural Changes**: γ′ precipitates (~300 nm) provide high-temperature strength; after fatigue at 900 ℃, γ′ decomposes near surface due to Al depletion, forming γ′-free zone (4 μm); surface recrystallization occurs
- **Property Improvements**: γ′ precipitates ensure excellent mechanical properties of single crystal superalloys at high temperature
- **Risks and Limitations**: γ′-free zone formation near surface due to Al depletion; surface recrystallization caused by γ′ depletion provides recrystallized nuclei
- **Original Evidence**: "The γ′ precipitate is an intermetallic compound based on Ni3Al, formed by the eutectic reaction. It ensures excellent mechanical properties of single crystal superalloys at high temperature [23]." "Due to the depletion of Al elements, the γ′ phase decomposes and a γ′ free zone with a thickness of 4 μm is formed." (Fig. 7a, b, Section 3.4)

## Grain Boundary Strengthening (Coating Grain Boundaries)

- **Related Elements**: Pt, Al, oxide formation at grain boundaries
- **Typical Alloys**: Pt-modified aluminide coatings (both β and γ′)
- **Key Compositions**: β-NiAl outer layer; γ′-Ni3Al coating; Pt present in both
- **Microstructural Changes**: In γ′ coating, grain size reduced from 1.8 μm to 1.2 μm (average) and 4 μm to 2.6 μm (maximum) after fatigue; oxidation occurs at grain boundaries; KAM maps show misorientation along crack sides
- **Property Improvements**: Not directly stated as beneficial; grain boundary oxidation leads to crack paths
- **Risks and Limitations**: Oxidation at grain boundaries leads to brittleness and crack formation; grain boundary misorientation contributes to crack development
- **Original Evidence**: "Oxidation also tends to occur at the grain boundaries of the bond coat." "Average grain size reduced from 1.8 μm to 1.2 μm, maximum grain size reduced from 4 μm to 2.6 μm." (Fig. 9, Fig. 10, Section 3.5)

## Oxide Strengthening / Oxidation-Resistant Alloying

- **Related Elements**: Al, Cr, Pt (promotes selective Al oxidation)
- **Typical Alloys**: DD5 superalloy and Pt-modified aluminide coatings
- **Key Compositions**: Al 6.2 wt.%, Cr 7.0 wt.% in DD5; Pt-modified aluminides in coatings; TGO primarily α-Al2O3
- **Microstructural Changes**: Protective α-Al2O3 TGO forms on surface and at grain boundaries; γ′-free zone forms beneath surface due to Al depletion; internal oxidation penetrates into substrate
- **Property Improvements**: Oxidation resistance is improved by Pt-modified coatings; however, oxide scale cracking under cyclic stress initiates fatigue cracks
- **Risks and Limitations**: Brittle aluminum oxide cracks under cyclic stress; internal oxidation penetrates into substrate; Al depletion leads to γ′ decomposition; surface recrystallization occurs
- **Original Evidence**: "A protective thermally grown oxide (TGO), predominantly α-Al2O3, is seen on the top of the γ′ bond coat." "The brittle aluminum oxide was generated on the surface and grain boundaries of the bond coat, which could easily crack under cyclic stress." (Fig. 9, Fig. 11, Section 3.5)

# Key Conclusions

1. The β-(Ni,Pt)Al coating with Re-base diffusion barrier decreased the fatigue performance of the DD5 substrate (fatigue strength at 10^7 cycles: 325 MPa vs. 337.5 MPa for bare), while the γ′ coating improved fatigue performance (350 MPa at 10^7 cycles).
2. Casting pores are responsible for fatigue crack initiation in bare single crystal superalloys; bond coats changed the fatigue crack initiation mechanism.
3. Oxidation of the bond coats is the main factor causing failure under high-temperature cyclic loading; TGO α-Al2O3 cracks under cyclic stress.
4. The diffusion barrier and interdiffusion zone in β coating had negative effects on fatigue performance due to brittle TCP σ phase precipitation and dislocation pile-up at σ/β boundaries.
5. With selective oxidation of aluminum, solution of refractory elements (Re, Cr) into γ phase is of significant importance for the γ′ coating to resist plastic deformation and prevent fatigue crack propagation.
6. The β coating has a larger total crack path length (outer layer + diffusion barrier + interdiffusion zone) compared to γ′ coating (outer layer only), resulting in higher stress concentration factor.
7. Coating thickness and microstructure, rather than oxidation, determine the difference in fatigue performance between the two coatings due to the short duration of fatigue experiments and sufficient Al content.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Paper scope**: This paper focuses on high-cycle fatigue at a single temperature (900 ℃) of a single-crystal Ni-based superalloy (DD5) with two types of Pt-modified aluminide coatings. It does not cover refractory alloys (Nb-W, Mo-W, refractory HEAs) or additive manufacturing.
- **Not addressed**: High-temperature tensile strength, creep, fracture toughness of the substrate or coatings are not measured; only rotating-bend fatigue at 900 ℃ is reported.
- **Single-point fatigue data**: The S-N curve was determined by the single-point method; statistical scatter is not reported.
- **Short-term exposure**: The authors note that "the difference in aluminum content did not affect the oxidation behavior of the coating" due to short fatigue experiment duration. Long-term oxidation behavior may differ.
- **Ceramic top coat not considered**: The paper notes that for specimens with ceramic top coat (TBC), the effect of bond coat on fatigue performance may differ due to ceramic layer not deforming with bond coat.
- **Specific coating compositions**: Exact Pt and Al contents of the coatings are not provided; detailed preparation processes are referenced but not reproduced in this paper.
- **Not applicable to refractory alloy systems**: The mechanisms discussed (γ/γ′ microstructure, Pt-modified aluminide oxidation) are specific to Ni-based superalloy coatings and may not directly transfer to Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloy systems.
