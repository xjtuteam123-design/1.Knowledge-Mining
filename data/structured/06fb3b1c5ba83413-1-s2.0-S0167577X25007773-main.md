# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Thermodynamic-driven phase-field simulation of coarsening kinetics in Ni-Al-W superalloys at high temperature
- **Authors**: Ramdevsinh Jhala, Nagaraj Patil, Debasish Shit, V.K. Bupesh Raja, Abinash Mahapatro, Deepak Gupta
- **Journal**: Materials Letters
- **Year**: 2025
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.matlet.2025.138748
- **Alloy System Studied**: Ni-Al-W ternary superalloy (single-crystal)
- **Research Objective**: To investigate the interactive effects of aluminium and tungsten on the coarsening kinetics of γ′ precipitates in Ni-Al-W superalloys at high temperature using phase-field simulations
- **Material Category**: Ni-based single-crystal superalloy (ternary model system). This is not a refractory high-entropy alloy, Nb-W-based alloy, Mo-W-based alloy, or additively manufactured refractory alloy. It is a Ni-based superalloy with W as a refractory alloying element.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Matrix Phase**: γ (gamma) phase — Ni-based solid solution matrix
- **Strengthening Phase**: γ′ (gamma-prime) precipitates — Ni₃Al-type ordered phase
- **Modeling Temperature**: 1473 K (1200 °C), representing high-temperature service conditions such as overheating events in gas turbines or missile applications
- **Modeling Method**: Thermodynamically informed phase-field model using the Grand potential approach with Thermo-Calc thermodynamic data
- **Total Energy Decomposition**: Interfacial energy (f_int), chemical energy (f_chem), and elastic energy (f_elast)
- **Initial Microstructure**: Super-cooled γ phase with γ′ precipitates embedded semi-randomly at 70 nm spacing; γ′ phase homogeneously distributed at start

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix Phase**: γ (gamma) phase — disordered FCC Ni-based solid solution
- **Precipitate Phase**: γ′ (gamma-prime) — ordered L1₂ Ni₃Al-type phase
- **Precipitate Spacing**: Initial spacing 70 nm
- **Precipitate Morphology**: 
  - Near-zero misfit promotes isotropic γ′ particles with spherical morphologies
  - Transition from near-zero to negative misfit induces γ′ alignment along the 〈001〉 crystallographic direction
  - Negative misfit correlates with slower coarsening kinetics
- **Lattice Misfit Behavior**: 
  - As W content rises, lattice misfit shifts from positive to negative values
  - Higher Al content further reduces misfit across all W levels
  - The change is driven by redistribution of misfit strain and elastic energy, not by creation of a two-phase mismatch network
- **W Interface Segregation**: W accumulates at the γ/γ′ interface; ΔC_W (W-content variation at interface) rises significantly with higher concentrations of both Al and W
- **Interfacial Energy Evolution**: Interface energy density continuously decreases with aging time
- **Elastic Energy Evolution**: Elastic energy density increases during steady-state coarsening
- **Grain Size**: Not discussed in the original paper (single-crystal superalloy context, simulation is intragranular)
- **Strengthening Mechanism Discussions in Paper**: The paper discusses coarsening resistance mechanisms primarily through interface chemistry (diffusion barrier effect of W at γ/γ′ interface) and elastic energy effects (misfit-driven resistance to coarsening), rather than traditional solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, grain-boundary strengthening, or ODS strengthening

## Al (Aluminium)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-Al-W ternary single-crystal superalloy
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 15.0–18.0 at.%
- **Main Function**: Essential for γ′ (Ni₃Al) phase formation and stabilization; influences lattice misfit and interfacial chemistry

## W (Tungsten)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-Al-W ternary single-crystal superalloy
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 3.0–6.0 at.%
- **Main Function**: Influences lattice misfit and diffusion kinetics; acts as slow-diffusing element that partitions to γ phase and accumulates at γ/γ′ interface; acts as diffusion barrier slowing coarsening
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: W partitions to γ phase and accumulates at γ/γ′ interface, modifying interfacial energy and acting as a diffusion barrier; as W content rises, lattice misfit shifts from positive to negative; slow-diffusing species retards coarsening through ΔC_W at interface (K_sim ≈ 1/ΔC_W²)
- **Property Effect**:
  - At low Al content (15 at.%): increasing W suppresses coarsening rate while moderately increasing γ′ volume fraction
  - Higher W concentration increases ΔC_W at interface, reducing coarsening rate by impeding diffusion-driven processes
  - Transition from positive to negative misfit leads to more stable γ′ phase with enhanced resistance to coarsening
  - More negative misfit correlates with slower coarsening kinetics
  - The original paper does not provide specific numerical values of coarsening rates for individual W levels.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Elevated W levels promote non-spherical γ′ shapes (morphological anisotropy along 〈001〉 direction); excessive W may lead to more negative misfit which, while suppressing coarsening, induces microstructural anisotropy
- **Original Evidence**: "tungsten influences lattice misfit and diffusion kinetics" (Introduction); "at low Al content (15 at.%), increment of W levels suppress the coarsening rate while moderately increasing the γ′ volume fraction (Fig. 1b)"; "W, as a slow-diffusing element, partitions to the γ phase and accumulates at the γ/γ′ interface, modifying interfacial energy and acting as a diffusion barrier to slow coarsening"

# Key Conclusions

1. **Interactive Al-W effects**: Synergistic Al-W interactions in Ni-Al-W superalloys simultaneously inhibit γ′ coarsening and preserve high γ′ volume fractions at 1473 K.

2. **Composition-dependent behavior**:
   - In low-W alloys (3.0 at.% W), increasing Al enhances both γ′ volume fraction and coarsening rate.
   - At low Al content (15 at.%), increasing W suppresses coarsening while moderately increasing γ′ volume fraction.
   - In high-W alloys (6 at.% W), increasing Al slows coarsening, though less pronounced than W's effect in low-Al systems.

3. **Misfit-driven anisotropy**: Elevated W levels shift lattice misfit from positive to negative; near-zero misfit promotes spherical γ′ particles while negative misfit induces γ′ alignment along the 〈001〉 crystallographic direction.

4. **Energy interplay**: Interfacial energy density decreases with aging time while elastic energy density increases; effective energy factor (Z_eff = f_int/f_elast) decreases with increasing γ′ fraction and more negative misfit, indicating reduced driving force for coarsening.

5. **Interface chemistry controls kinetics**: W-content variation at γ/γ′ interface (ΔC_W) increases with W and Al concentrations; larger ΔC_W reduces coarsening rate via K_sim ≈ 1/ΔC_W². W's slowing effect is markedly stronger than Al's, while Al's impact saturates at high negative misfit.

6. **Optimal Al-W balance**: An optimal Al-W ratio stabilizes γ′ microstructures by regulating both interfacial chemistry and elastic energy, maximizing coarsening resistance.

7. **Generalizability**: Synergistic interactions between Al and slow-diffusing species (W, Mo, Ta, Re) suggest coarsening resistance arises from elemental interplay, not from any single element.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Computational simulation only**: This is a phase-field modeling study; no experimental validation or physical alloy fabrication is reported in this paper. All results are based on Grand potential approach with Thermo-Calc thermodynamic data.

2. **Modeling temperature fixed at 1473 K**: All simulations performed at a single temperature; no temperature-dependent coarsening behavior is investigated.

3. **Ternary simplification**: The system is simplified to Ni-Al-W only; complex commercial superalloys containing Cr, Co, Mo, Re, Ta, etc. are not modeled. The comparison to Ni-Al-Mo, Ni-Al-Re, and Ni-Al-Ta is qualitative and based on cited literature.

4. **Limited composition ranges**: Al restricted to 15–18 at.%, W restricted to 3–6 at.%; effects outside these ranges are not addressed.

5. **No mechanical property values reported**: The paper does not report yield strength, creep life, hardness, or other measurable mechanical properties; conclusions are limited to coarsening kinetics and microstructural evolution.

6. **No oxidation or environmental behavior**: High-temperature environmental degradation is not addressed.

7. **No quantitative optimal Al-W ratio provided**: While the concept of an optimal ratio is discussed, specific numerical values of the optimal composition are not reported.

8. **Applicability to refractory alloy systems**: While this paper concerns Ni-based superalloys and is not directly a refractory alloy study, the concepts of slow-diffusing element segregation at interfaces, misfit-driven coarsening resistance, and synergistic element interactions may be relevant to γ/γ′-type refractory systems or other two-phase refractory alloys, but such extrapolation is not made in the original paper.
