# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system studied:** AlNbTiVZr₀.₂₅ refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) with a multicomponent B2 matrix phase
- **Material category:** Refractory high-entropy alloy / B2-ordered intermetallic-based RHEA
- **Research objective:** To investigate the yield stress anomaly (YSA) in a B2-matrix AlNbTiVZr₀.₂₅ RHEA during compression tests at 22–900 °C, and to identify the deformation mechanisms responsible for the YSA, particularly the role of dissociated a⟨111⟩ superdislocations.
- **Key terminology used:** Refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA), B2-ordered phase, yield stress anomaly (YSA), superdislocations, antiphase boundary (APB)

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase:** Multicomponent B2 phase (CsCl-prototype; cP2; Pm-3m)
- **Second phases/precipitates:** (Zr, Al)-rich precipitates identified as Zr₅Al₃-type phase (Mn₅Si₃-prototype; hP16; P6₃/mcm)
- **Volume fraction of precipitates:** ~10%
- **Orientation relationship:** (1 0 0)B2 || (1 1 1)Zr₅Al₃, [0 0 1]B2 || [0 1 1]Zr₅Al₃
- **Grain size:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper text.
- **Grain boundary features:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper text.
- **Elemental segregation:** (Zr, Al)-rich precipitates indicate Zr and Al partitioning into the Zr₅Al₃ phase; the B2 matrix is multicomponent.
- **Precipitate size and distribution:** Zr₅Al₃ precipitates with ~10% volume fraction; after compression at 700 °C, “some coagulation/aggregation of the Zr₅Al₃ particles should be noted.”
- **Discussion of strengthening mechanisms:** The paper primarily discusses solid-solution strengthening and the order–disorder strengthening mechanism related to APB energy and superdislocation dissociation, not carbide/oxide dispersion or grain-boundary strengthening.
- **Dislocation structure at 700 °C, ε ≈ 1%:** Multiple dissociated dislocations in the B2 matrix. Dissociated a⟨111⟩ superdislocations comprising two a/2⟨111⟩ partial dislocations linked by an antiphase boundary (APB). Dissociation width r ≈ 10 nm observed at 700 °C. g·b = 0 invisibility analysis showed dislocations visible for g = 110, 200, 020, but invisible for g = 110, confirming Burgers vector b = a⟨111⟩ (paired a/2⟨111⟩ partials).

- **Applicable Alloy System:** AlNbTiVZr₀.₂₅ B2-ordered refractory high-entropy alloy

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related elements:** All principal elements (Al, Nb, Ti, V, Zr) contribute to the multicomponent B2 matrix as a high-entropy solid-solution phase.
- **Typical alloys:** AlNbTiVZr₀.₂₅ B2-ordered RHEA.
- **Key compositions:** Equiatomic AlNbTiV with Zr₀.₂₅.
- **Microstructural changes:** Single multicomponent B2 matrix phase with Zr₅Al₃ precipitates. High-entropy mixing stabilizes the B2 phase.
- **Property improvements:** High yield strength at room temperature (1385 MPa) and elevated temperatures (870 MPa at 600 °C; 1005 MPa at 700 °C peak).
- **Risks and limitations:** Brittleness (ε < 10% at 22–700 °C); the paper does not explicitly attribute the strengthening quantitatively to solid-solution effects alone.

## Order–Disorder Strengthening (Yield Stress Anomaly Mechanism)

- **Related elements:** Multicomponent B2 composition; specifically discussed in relation to B2 compounds such as FeCo-2V and Fe₂MnAl L2₁ compounds in the broader literature, and AlNbTiVZr₀.₂₅ in this paper.
- **Typical alloys:** AlNbTiVZr₀.₂₅ B2 RHEA; also relevant to binary and ternary B2 compounds such as FeCo-2V and Fe₂MnAl.
- **Key compositions:** B2-ordered AlNbTiVZr₀.₂₅ with Zr₅Al₃ precipitates.
- **Microstructural changes:** At 700 °C and ε ≈ 1%, dissociated a⟨111⟩ superdislocations comprising two a/2⟨111⟩ partial dislocations linked by an APB. Dissociation width r ≈ 10 nm. g·b invisibility analysis: visible for g = 110, 200, 020; invisible for g = 110, confirming Burgers vector b = a⟨111⟩ paired partials.
- **Property improvements:** Yield stress anomaly (YSA) with a stress peak of 1005 MPa at 700 °C. Strain rate insensitivity at 700 °C. The YSA represents a positive temperature dependence of strength, enhancing high-temperature strength relative to the decreasing trend at lower temperatures.
- **Risks and limitations:** The YSA mechanism is attributed to the glide of dissociated a⟨111⟩ superdislocations; the authors note that “additional experimental work is required to verify this suggestion.” The structure is relatively stable up to 700 °C, though some coagulation/aggregation of Zr₅Al₃ particles occurs. The YSA is not explained by phase decomposition, unlike the case of an Al₁₀Nb₁₅Ta₅Ti₃₀Zr₄₀ RHEA with a nanoscale bcc/B2 mixture [19].
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3 Results and discussion; Figures 2 and 3; references to Stoloff and Davies [16], Marcinkowski and Miller [23], Crawford and Ray [20], Liao and Baker [24].

# Key Conclusions

1. The AlNbTiVZr₀.₂₅ RHEA consists of a multicomponent B2 matrix (CsCl-prototype; cP2; Pm-3m) and (Zr, Al)-rich Zr₅Al₃-type precipitates (Mn₅Si₃-prototype; hP16; P6₃/mcm) with ~10% volume fraction.
2. Compression tests at 22–900 °C (initial strain rate 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹) revealed a conventional negative temperature dependence of yield stress at 22–600 °C and 800–900 °C, and a yield stress anomaly (YSA) at 600–800 °C with a stress peak at 700 °C (1005 MPa). Room-temperature YS is 1385 MPa; YS at 600 °C is 870 MPa.
3. The yield stress at 700 °C is insensitive to strain rate variations in the range 10⁻⁴ to 5 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹.
4. TEM analysis at 700 °C and ε ≈ 1% revealed multiple dissociated a⟨111⟩ superdislocations in the B2 matrix, each consisting of two a/2⟨111⟩ partial dislocations linked by an APB, with a dissociation width r ≈ 10 nm. g·b = 0 analysis (invisible for g = 110) confirmed b = a⟨111⟩ paired partials.
5. The YSA is attributed to the glide of dissociated a⟨111⟩ superdislocations, consistent with the order–disorder strengthening mechanism proposed by Stoloff and Davies [16], where APB energy depends on the degree of long-range order, S, and chemical composition. The observed dissociation width (~10 nm) correlates with the ~15 nm spacing associated with YSA in FeCo-2V.
6. The YSA cannot be explained by phase decomposition because the alloy's structure remained relatively stable at 700 °C, aside from some coagulation/aggregation of Zr₅Al₃ particles.
7. The authors suggest that controlling the degree of order (S) to tune superdislocation dissociation width may enable the design of multicomponent B2 intermetallics with room-temperature tensile ductility and promising high-temperature strength, but state that additional experimental work is required.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Limitations of the study:**
  1. The processing and heat treatment details are not described in this paper; they are only referenced to prior publications [17,18]. Therefore, the influence of processing on the observed YSA cannot be assessed from this paper alone.
  2. The exact chemical composition (at.%) is not provided in the main text; it is relegated to Table S1 of the Supplementary Material, which is not included in the reproduced text.
  3. Specific yield stress values at temperatures other than 22 °C, 600 °C, and 700 °C are not given in the main text; values for 500, 750, 800, and 900 °C are found in Table S2 (not reproduced), limiting the completeness of the extracted dataset.
  4. The attribution of the YSA to dissociated a⟨111⟩ superdislocation glide is presented as the “most probable reason,” and the authors explicitly acknowledge: “additional experimental work is required to verify this suggestion.” Speculative aspects (such as the proposal to tune S for room-temperature ductility and high-temperature strength) are not presented as confirmed conclusions.
  5. No oxidation resistance, creep, fracture toughness, or fatigue data are provided, limiting applicability to high-temperature service environments.
  6. Grain size and grain-boundary effects are not characterized, so potential grain-boundary strengthening contributions cannot be assessed.
  7. The study is limited to compression testing; no tensile properties are reported.
  8. The strain rate insensitivity was only tested at 700 °C, not across the full temperature range of the YSA.

- **Applicable scope:**
  - The findings are directly applicable to B2-ordered refractory high-entropy alloys, particularly those containing Al and early transition metals (Nb, Ti, V, Zr).
  - The yield stress anomaly mechanism (dissociated a⟨111⟩ superdislocation glide with APB-coupled partials) is relevant to understanding high-temperature deformation of B2 intermetallics and multicomponent B2 RHEAs.
  - The results may inform the design of B2-based RHEAs with tailored order parameter to balance room-temperature ductility and high-temperature strength, though this remains to be validated.
  - The paper does not address environmental effects (oxidation, embrittlement), so the conclusions should not be extrapolated to oxidizing or aggressive atmospheres without additional data.
