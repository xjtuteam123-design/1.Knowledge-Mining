# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** Effects of Nb addition and heat treatment on the microstructure, mechanical property and internal friction behavior of FeCrAlMo cladding alloys
- **Authors:** H. Wang, Y.X. Gao, M. Sun, G. Li, K. He, X.G. An, W.B. Jiang, Q.F. Fang, X.P. Wang
- **Journal:** Journal of Nuclear Materials
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnucmat.2022.154044
- **Alloy system studied:** FeCrAlMo-based alloys with Nb additions (Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-xNb, x = 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 wt%)
- **Research objective:** To investigate the influence of Nb addition and heat treatment on the evolution of dispersed Fe₂Nb precipitates, mechanical properties, and internal friction (IF) behavior of Nb-containing Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo alloys for accident tolerant fuel (ATF) cladding applications
- **Material category:** Ferritic FeCrAl-based ATF cladding alloy with Laves phase precipitation strengthening (not a refractory high-entropy alloy, Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory multi-principal element alloy, and not additively manufactured)

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies ferritic FeCrAl-based alloys designed for accident-tolerant fuel (ATF) cladding in light-water reactors. The base composition is Fe-13Cr-4.5Al (wt%), with 2 wt% Mo added for solid-solution strengthening and variable Nb additions (0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 wt%) for precipitation strengthening via Fe₂Nb Laves phase particles. This is not a refractory high-temperature alloy system in the conventional sense, but rather a ferritic steel-based cladding alloy for nuclear applications.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

The following processing route was used for all alloys:

1. **Raw materials:** 99.99 wt% purity
2. **Melting:** Vacuum induction melting
3. **Homogenization:** 1200 °C for 2 h
4. **Hot rolling:** 1200 °C, total thickness reduction of 90%
5. **Warm rolling:** 700 °C, total thickness reduction of 60%
6. **Cold rolling:** Room temperature, final thickness ~2 mm
7. **Heat treatment:** Annealing at 800 °C, 900 °C, 1000 °C, or 1100 °C for 1 h under Ar atmosphere, followed by water quenching

The paper notes that during warm rolling at 700 °C, Fe₂Nb Laves phase particles can precipitate, as a temperature range of 400 °C to 1000 °C can result in Fe₂Nb Laves phase precipitation in Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-1Nb alloy.

**Deformation amounts:**
- Hot rolling: 90% thickness reduction
- Warm rolling: 60% thickness reduction
- Cold rolling: final thickness ~2 mm (degree of reduction not explicitly stated)

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Matrix phase:** Ferritic BCC matrix (ferritic FeCrAl alloy)

**Second phases/precipitates:**
- **Fe₂Nb Laves phase particles** (confirmed by selected area diffraction pattern in TEM from previous study by the same group)
- Particle size: ~200 nm in N15 800 and N15 1000 samples
- Particle number density: 5 × 10¹⁵ m⁻² in N15 800; 6 × 10²¹ m⁻² in N15 1000
- At 1100 °C with 1.5 wt% Nb (N15 1100): particles agglomerate and grow to ~1 μm, number density decreases significantly
- At 1100 °C with 0.5 wt% Nb (N05 1100): Fe₂Nb particles dissolve back completely, no precipitates visible

**Precipitate distribution:**
- N05 800: small amount of nano-scale (~200 nm) precipitates at grain boundaries and inside grains
- N15 800: more Fe₂Nb particles with increasing Nb content; particle size 200 nm, number density 5 × 10¹⁵ m⁻²
- N15 1000: particle density increases substantially to 6 × 10²¹ m⁻², no significant increase in particle size
- N15 1100: particles agglomerate and coarsen to ~1 μm, density decreases

**Grain size:**
- N0 800: 80 ± 18 μm
- N0 1000: 250 ± 60 μm
- N0 1100: 400 ± 94 μm
- N05 1000: 180 ± 41 μm
- N05 1100: 250 ± 42 μm
- Grain size increases with annealing temperature
- Nb-containing alloys show grain refinement due to Fe₂Nb pinning of grain boundaries

**Grain boundary features:**
- Fe₂Nb Laves phase particles dispersed on grain boundaries have a pinning effect on grain boundary motion
- This prevents grain coarsening during warm rolling at 700 °C and heat treatment
- In N15 1100, grains remain elongated (not fully recrystallized), indicating Fe₂Nb particles stabilize the rolled microstructure

**Elemental segregation:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**Recrystallization behavior:**
- Fe₂Nb Laves particles shift recovery and recrystallization to higher temperatures
- Recrystallization temperature increases with increasing Nb content
- At 1100 °C and 1.5 wt% Nb, full recrystallization does not occur

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed:**
- Precipitation strengthening (dispersion strengthening) via Fe₂Nb nanoscale particles — identified as the main strengthening mechanism
- Grain refinement via Fe₂Nb pinning of grain boundaries
- Solid-solution strengthening via Mo (discussed only in the introduction, not experimentally investigated in this paper)
- Dislocation pinning by Fe₂Nb nanoscale particles

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

This paper does not experimentally investigate oxidation resistance. However, the introduction discusses that:

- Cr and Al contents are needed to form protective alumina scales on the surface for high-temperature oxidation resistance
- Cr content must be balanced: too high forms Cr-rich α′ precipitates causing radiation-induced embrittlement; too low (<12%) provides insufficient oxidation resistance
- Al content must be balanced: too high (>8%) causes welding and fabrication difficulties
- The base composition of Fe-13Cr-4.5Al was determined to balance oxidation resistance and radiation tolerance

**Applications relevance:**
- FeCrAl alloys have much higher oxidation resistance than zircaloy over a wide temperature range
- Large neutron absorption cross section of FeCrAl alloys limits tube wall thickness to 0.5 mm, requiring high-temperature strength improvement

**High-temperature embrittlement or related issues:** Not explicitly studied in this paper. The paper focuses on strength improvement rather than oxidation or embrittlement behavior.

FeCrAlMo-based ATF cladding alloys (Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-xNb system)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 wt% Nb in Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo alloys

Precipitation strengthening via formation of Fe₂Nb Laves phase nanoscale particles; grain refinement through grain boundary pinning; retardation of recovery and recrystallization

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Precipitation Strengthening / Dispersion Strengthening via Fe₂Nb Laves Phase

**Related elements:** Nb (with Fe)

**Typical alloys:** Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-xNb (x = 0.5-1.5 wt%)

**Key compositions:** Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-1.5Nb (wt%), annealed at 1000 °C for 1 h

**Microstructural changes:**
- Nanoscale Fe₂Nb Laves phase particles precipitated in ferritic matrix and at grain boundaries
- Particle size: ~200 nm
- Particle density: up to 6 × 10²¹ m⁻² at optimal conditions
- At suboptimal conditions (too high temperature, 1100 °C), particles coarsen to ~1 μm or dissolve back

**Property improvements:**
- UTS at RT increased from ~466 MPa (Nb-free) to 692 MPa (1.5 wt% Nb) at 1000 °C annealing: 48.2% increase
- UTS at 800 °C increased from ~55 MPa (Nb-free, calculated) to 95 MPa (1.5 wt% Nb at 1000 °C annealing): 58.3% increase
- Grain refinement: grain size reduced from 250 ±60 μm (N0 1000) to 180 ±41 μm (N05 1000)

**Risks and limitations:**
- Requires careful heat treatment temperature control: too low (800 °C) results in insufficient particle density; too high (1100 °C) results in particle coarsening or dissolution
- High Nb content with high annealing temperature leads to agglomeration and reduced strengthening
- May delay recrystallization significantly, affecting ductility and workability

## Grain Boundary Strengthening / Grain Refinement via Fe₂Nb Pinning

**Related elements:** Nb

**Typical alloys:** Nb-containing FeCrAlMo alloys (0.5-1.5 wt% Nb)

**Key compositions:** Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-xNb, annealed at 800-1100 °C

**Microstructural changes:**
- Fe₂Nb Laves phase particles dispersed at grain boundaries
- Grain growth retarded during annealing
- Grain size reduced compared to Nb-free alloys: 250 ±42 μm vs 400 ±94 μm at 1100 °C; 180 ±41 μm vs 250 ±60 μm at 1000 °C

**Risks and limitations:**
- Grain boundary pinning also affects recrystallization, potentially leading to elongated, un-recrystallized grains
- Excessive pinning may reduce ductility, though the paper states "acceptable ductility"

## Dislocation Pinning via Fe₂Nb Laves Phase Particles

**Related elements:** Nb

**Typical alloys:** Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-xNb (x = 0.5-1.5 wt%)

**Key compositions:** 0.5-1.5 wt% Nb; heat treatment at 800-1100 °C

**Microstructural changes:**
- High density nanoscale Fe₂Nb particles dispersed in matrix pin dislocations
- Recovery and recrystallization reduced and shifted to higher temperatures
- Elongated grains persisting even at 1100 °C with 1.5 wt% Nb

**Property improvements:**
- Internal friction P₂ peak (recrystallization peak, ~680 °C) disappears in Nb-containing alloys, indicating dislocation pinning
- Strengthening at elevated temperatures maintained due to stabilized dislocation structure

**Risks and limitations:**
- Incomplete recrystallization may affect other properties
- Not explicitly discussed in the paper

# Key Conclusions

1. **Optimal precipitate morphology:** High-density nanoscale Fe₂Nb Laves phase particles with a maximum density of 6 × 10²¹ m⁻² and particle size of 200 nm were achieved with 1.5 wt% Nb addition and annealing at 1000 °C.

2. **Optimal mechanical properties:** Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-1.5Nb (wt%) annealed at 1000 °C exhibited the highest tensile strength and acceptable ductility at all tested temperatures (RT, 300 °C, 500 °C, 800 °C). UTS at 800 °C reached 95 MPa, 58.3-73% higher than Nb-free FeCrAlMo alloy.

3. **Internal friction evidence for dislocation pinning:** The P₂ IF peak (~680 °C) corresponding to recovery and recrystallization disappeared in Nb-containing FeCrAlMo alloys, indicating that high-density nanoscale Fe₂Nb particles effectively pin dislocations and shift recovery/recrystallization to higher temperatures.

4. **Internal friction evidence for grain boundary pinning:** The P₃ IF peak (grain boundary peak, ~720 °C) disappeared in Nb-containing alloys, proving that Fe₂Nb particles pin grain boundaries and block grain boundary sliding.

5. **Main strengthening mechanism:** Dispersion strengthening via nanoscale Fe₂Nb precipitation is the primary mechanism for improving tensile strength of Nb-containing FeCrAlMo alloys.

6. **IF technique as a novel research method:** The internal friction technique can serve as a novel method to study strengthening mechanisms of FeCrAl-based alloys, providing experimental evidence for second-phase particle dispersion strengthening.

7. **Nb content and annealing temperature interplay:** Both factors are important for precipitate morphology. Higher Nb content increases precipitation density, while annealing temperature controls dissolution (low Nb) or agglomeration (high Nb). Particles with high density and small size exhibit the highest strength improvement.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations:**

1. **Alloy system specificity:** The findings apply to Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-xNb (wt%) ferritic alloys, not to Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloys. The strengthening mechanism of Fe₂Nb Laves phase precipitation is specific to Fe-based ferritic alloys.

2. **Nb content range:** Only Nb contents up to 1.5 wt% were studied. Effects of higher Nb contents are not covered.

3. **Annealing temperature range:** Only 800-1100 °C was studied during short annealing time (1 h). Longer annealing times and their effect on precipitates are not covered.

4. **Tensile testing atmosphere:** The atmosphere for high-temperature tensile testing is not explicitly stated, which may affect results.

5. **No oxidation resistance testing:** This paper does not experimentally test either high-temperature steam oxidation or air oxidation resistance, despite the alloy being designed for ATF cladding applications.

6. **No creep testing:** Creep behavior, which is critical for ATF cladding, is not studied in this paper.

7. **Discrepancy in reported values:** The paper states "73% higher" in the abstract but "58.3% higher" in the main text for UTS improvement of N15 1000 compared to N0 1000 at 800 °C. This discrepancy is noted in the original paper.

8. **Neutron irradiation effects:** Not tested in this paper. Radiation tolerance of Nb-containing FeCrAlMo alloys is not addressed.

9. **Welding and fabrication:** Though mentioned as potential issue for high Al content, no welding or fabrication tests were performed on the Nb-containing alloys.

10. **Not a refractory high-temperature alloy:** This paper does not study refractory high-entropy alloys, Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. It is included here for comparison purposes only.

**Applicable scope:**
The findings are applicable to nuclear-grade ferritic FeCrAlMo-based ATF cladding materials in the composition range Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-xNb (wt%) with x = 0-1.5 wt%, processed by vacuum induction melting, hot/warm/cold rolling, and annealed at 800-1100 °C.
