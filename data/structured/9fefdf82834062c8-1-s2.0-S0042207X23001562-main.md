# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** W–Ta–Nb solid solutions prepared by vacuum arc melting: Microstructure evolution and mechanical properties
- **Authors:** Wenyang Liu, Yufeng Huang, Yao Wang, Wensheng Liu, Yunzhu Ma
- **Journal:** Vacuum
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vacuum.2023.111959
- **Alloy system studied:** W–Ta–Nb ternary solid solution alloys (tungsten-based solid solutions)
- **Material category:** W-based solid solution alloy (W–Ta–Nb system)
- **Research objective:** To investigate the effect of low-content Nb addition on the microstructure evolution, strength, and toughness of W–30Ta solid solutions, with the goal of further improving strength and toughness while maintaining high specific gravity.

# Alloy System and Research Object

This paper investigates tungsten-based solid solution alloys in the W–Ta–Nb system, prepared by vacuum arc melting. The base composition is W–30Ta (wt.%), to which varying amounts of Nb (1, 3, and 5 wt.%) are added. The alloy system belongs to the category of tungsten-based solid solution materials, not a refractory high-entropy alloy or multi-principal element alloy, since it is based on a single BCC solid solution phase with W as the primary constituent.

The research object is the effect of small Nb additions on:
- Microstructure (grain size, lattice parameter, interplanar spacing, phase constitution)
- Densification behavior
- Hardness and elastic modulus
- Compressive strength and compressive strain
- Fracture mode and toughness

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Preparation route:**
1. **Powder mixing:** Raw powders weighed according to design, poured into mixing tank under argon protective gas, mixed using a three-dimensional motion mixer for 8 hours. Ball-to-material ratio: 1:1. Rotational rate: 12 r/min.
2. **Cold isostatic pressing (CIP):** Mixed W–Ta–Nb powder cold isostatically pressed into small cylindrical blocks at a pressure of 300 MPa. Die diameter: φ 38 mm, height: 70 mm.
3. **Vacuum arc melting:** The pressed block was smelted in an arc melting furnace, melting 10 times. The arc current was gradually increased until the block was melted for 5 minutes, then cooled with the furnace.

**Heat treatment:** No separate homogenization, annealing, or aging heat treatment is explicitly described beyond the arc melting process itself (10 remelting cycles with 5-minute melting duration and furnace cooling). The paper does not mention rolling, forging, hot pressing, spark plasma sintering, hot isostatic pressing, or additive manufacturing.

**Additive manufacturing:** Not applicable to this paper. The paper does not use LPBF, SLM, EBM, or DED. However, it cites related work on laser melting of W–Nb alloys (Ref. 19) and selective laser melting of tungsten (Refs. 20–22) for comparison.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Phase constitution:**
- Single-phase BCC W–Ta–Nb solid solution in all alloys studied.
- XRD showed no diffraction peaks of Nb, Ta, or Ta2O5; only tungsten-based solid solution peaks were detected.
- XRD peaks shifted to lower 2θ angles (leftward) with increasing Nb content, indicating increased lattice constant and interplanar spacing.
- TEM confirmed single-phase BCC structure without precipitates or phase transitions after deformation.

**Lattice parameters and interplanar spacing (Table 3):**

| Alloy | Theoretical r (Å) | Theoretical a (Å) | Theoretical d110 (Å) | Theoretical 2θ (°) | Experimental 2θ (°) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| W–30Ta | 1.402 | 3.238 | 2.289 | 39.344 | 39.900 |
| W–30Ta–1Nb | 1.402 | 3.239 | 2.290 | 39.334 | 39.850 |
| W–30Ta–3Nb | 1.403 | 3.240 | 2.291 | 39.314 | 39.750 |
| W–30Ta–5Nb | 1.404 | 3.242 | 2.292 | 39.295 | 39.660 |

- TEM high-resolution for W–30Ta–3Nb showed interplanar spacing of 2.284 Å in the [011] crystal orientation.

**Grain size:**
- Grain sizes decreased with increasing Nb content: 313 μm (0 wt.% Nb), 217 μm (1 wt.% Nb), 177 μm (3 wt.% Nb), 163 μm (5 wt.% Nb).
- When Nb content was 3 wt.%, minimum crystal grain size could reach 100 μm.
- Structure consisted mainly of equiaxed grains with a small amount of elongated grains.

**Grain boundary features:**
- W–30Ta (without Nb) showed small gaps between grains.
- With Nb addition, no obvious cracks between grains were observed.
- Nb was reported to suppress micro-cracks (citing Xue et al. on laser-melted W–Nb alloys).

**Elemental distribution:**
- EPMA and STEM-EDS mapping showed uniform distribution of W, Ta, and Nb without elemental enrichment or segregation.

**EDS matrix composition (Table 4, atom%):**

**Dislocation structure:**
- High density of dislocations observed within grains (both before and after deformation).
- After compression, ductile (edge) dislocations appeared in addition to screw dislocations, which is different from pure W where screw dislocation slip is the main deformation mechanism.

**Strengthening/softening mechanisms discussed:**
- Solid solution strengthening (Nb dissolved into W lattice)
- Grain boundary strengthening / grain refinement (Hall-Petch relationship)
- Solid solution softening (modulus decrease with Nb addition)
- Composite effect / compound effect (Nb is softer than W, reducing overall strength at high Nb content)

# Mechanical Properties

**Density (Fig. 8):**
- Actual density decreased linearly with increasing Nb content (because Nb density is lower than W density).
- Relative density increased gradually with increasing Nb content, reaching a maximum of 99.6%.
- Rate of density reduction decreased when Nb content reached 3 wt.% (attributed to promoting densification).

**Vickers hardness (Figs. 9 and 10):**
- Hardness increased first and then decreased with increasing Nb content.
- W–30Ta (no Nb): average hardness 8.3 GPa, elastic modulus 300 GPa.
- Maximum hardness of W–Ta–Nb solid solutions reached 10.9 GPa.
- The specific alloy achieving maximum hardness is not explicitly stated, but context suggests it is W–30Ta–1Nb or W–30Ta–3Nb.

**Elastic modulus (Fig. 10):**
- Elastic modulus of W–Ta–Nb solid solutions decreased with increasing Nb content.
- W–30Ta base: 300 GPa.
- The decrease in modulus is described as a "solid solution softening phenomenon."

**Compressive properties (Fig. 11):**
- Compressive strength increased first and then decreased with increasing Nb content.
- Maximum compressive strength: 1.7 GPa.
- Maximum compressive strain: 24%.
- The specific alloy achieving these maximum values is not explicitly stated in the text excerpt; it is described as occurring at an intermediate Nb content.
- Testing was performed at room temperature using Instron-3369 universal testing machine.
- Loading mode: compression.
- Strain rate: not explicitly stated.
- Atmosphere: not explicitly stated.

**Grain refinement contribution to strength (Hall-Petch calculation):**
- K value: 14 MPa·mm^(1/2)
- Calculated σd values: 25 MPa (0 wt.% Nb), 30 MPa (1 wt.% Nb), 33 MPa (3 wt.% Nb), 35 MPa (5 wt.% Nb)
- The contribution of grain refinement to strength was described as "much lower than the actual strength value" and "very limited."

**Fracture behavior (Fig. 12):**
- W–30Ta (no Nb): mainly intergranular fracture, smooth and flat section, weak grain boundary bonding.
- W–Ta–Nb with Nb addition: mixed intergranular and transgranular fracture; more resistance to crack propagation in the dissociation process; small dimples observed (local deformation in shear zone).
- W–30Ta–3Nb compared with W–30Ta–1Nb: transgranular dissociation fracture decreased with excessive Nb addition; large cracks between grains formed by crack propagation at grain boundaries.

**Ductility/toughness indicator:**
- The paper discusses the G/B ratio (shear modulus/bulk modulus) as an indicator of toughness.
- Previous computational results (Refs. 13, 14) showed that adding Nb decreases G/B ratio of W-based solid solutions, suggesting improved toughness.
- The paper states this is consistent with the improved strength and toughness observed in their study.

**Creep, fracture toughness, high-temperature strength:** Not tested or reported in this paper.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## W (Tungsten)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** W–Ta–Nb ternary solid solutions (base element)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 63.3–70.79 at.% (69.5–65.8 wt.% in Table 1)
- **Main Function:** Base element providing high density, high strength, high melting point, high hardness, and low coefficient of thermal expansion.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Forms BCC lattice as the matrix of the solid solution; screw dislocation slip is the primary intrinsic deformation mechanism; exhibits inherent brittleness with anti-Schmid's law behavior on the micro-scale; main fracture plane is (100) while close-packed plane is (110).
- **Property Effect:** Pure W has inherent brittleness. In W–30Ta (no Nb), compressive fracture is mainly intergranular; hardness 8.3 GPa; elastic modulus 300 GPa. Specific numerical values for pure elemental W are not provided in the original paper.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Inherent brittleness; coarse grains in melted condition; micro-cracks at grain boundaries without Nb addition.
- **Original Evidence:** "The inherent brittleness of pure tungsten greatly limits its further application." "The toughness of tungsten materials can be improved by solid solutions of other elements" (Introduction, p. 1). "The hardness of the sample was affected by solid solution strengthening, fine grain strengthening and composite effect" (Section 3.2, p. 8).

## Nb (Niobium)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** W–Ta–Nb ternary solid solutions (alloying addition to W–Ta base)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 1 wt.% (1.73 at.%), 3 wt.% (5.45 at.%), 5 wt.% (8.26 at.% in matrix EDS); Table 1 lists 3.23 wt.% and 4.98 wt.% Nb.
- **Main Function:** Improves toughness, refines grain size, promotes densification, suppresses micro-cracks, modifies deformation mechanism from screw to ductile (edge) dislocations.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** 
  - Nb atoms replace W atoms in the BCC lattice, increasing lattice constant and interplanar spacing (XRD peak shift to lower angles).
  - Increases liquid-solid semi-solid range during solidification, providing more nucleation media and preferential nucleation, leading to grain refinement.
  - Reduces elastic strain energy of edge dislocations (via reduced shear modulus), making initiation of ductile dislocations easier.
  - Decreases G/B ratio, indicating improved toughness.
- **Property Effect:** 
  - Grain size decreases from 313 μm (0% Nb) to 217 μm (1% Nb), 177 μm (3% Nb), 163 μm (5% Nb).
  - Relative density increases to 99.6%; actual density decreases.
  - Vickers hardness increases to a maximum of 10.9 GPa (from 8.3 GPa without Nb), then decreases at higher Nb content.
  - Elastic modulus decreases continuously with Nb addition (from 300 GPa baseline).
  - Maximum compressive strength reaches 1.7 GPa with strain up to 24% at intermediate Nb content.
  - Fracture mode changes from predominantly intergranular (no Nb) to mixed intergranular + transgranular with dimples (with Nb).
- **Potential Side Effects:** 
  - Nb is much softer than W and Ta; excessive Nb content reduces overall strength ("compound effect" weakness).
  - Lower melting point of Nb (2468 °C) compared to W (3410 °C) and Ta (2990 °C) may reduce alloy melting point and affect high-temperature mechanical properties.
  - Excessive Nb may lead to local content inhomogeneity, reducing performance.
- **Original Evidence:** "When the content of Nb was 3 wt%, the minimum size of the crystal grains can reach 100 μm" (Section 3.1, p. 5). "The addition of a small amount of Nb can effectively improve the strength and toughness of W–Ta–Nb solid solutions. The addition of a small amount of Nb reduced the energy required for the initiation of ductile dislocations in W–Ta–Nb solid solutions" (Abstract). "Compared with W and Ta, Nb is soft materials and its strength is much lower than that of W and Ta. The addition of excessively high content of Nb caused the mechanical properties of W–Ta–Nb solid solution materials to decrease" (Section 3.2, p. 8). "According to the simulation results, the addition of Nb atoms increases the ratio of bulk modulus to shear modulus (B/G), Poisson's ratio and Cauchy pressure, which means that the addition of Nb can modify the brittleness of pure tungsten" (Introduction, p. 1).

## Ta (Tantalum)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** W–Ta–Nb ternary solid solutions (co-alloying element with W)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 30 wt.% Ta in base alloy (28.6–30.1 wt.% in Table 1); 27.16–29.21 at.% in EDS
- **Main Function:** Changes the deformation mechanism of pure tungsten, increases dislocation density, introduces ductile dislocations in addition to screw dislocations.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Ta is completely miscible with W in BCC solid solution; solid solution alters deformation behavior.
- **Property Effect:** Not quantified separately in this paper. The paper notes that Ta changes the deformation mechanism but "does not contribute enough to the deformability of tungsten based solid solutions" (Introduction, p. 1), motivating the Nb addition study.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Insufficient improvement in deformability/toughness when used alone; high melting point complicates processing.
- **Original Evidence:** "The addition of tantalum changes the deformation mechanism of pure tungsten and increases the dislocation density compared with pure tungsten. On the basis of screw dislocations, ductile dislocations appear. However, tantalum does not contribute enough to the deformability of tungsten based solid solutions" (Introduction, p. 1).

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Nb (primary), Ta (co-alloying), W (base)
- **Typical Alloys:** W–30Ta, W–30Ta–1Nb, W–30Ta–3Nb, W–30Ta–5Nb
- **Key Compositions:** 0–5 wt.% Nb in W–30Ta base
- **Microstructural Changes:** Nb atoms replace W in BCC lattice, increasing lattice constant and interplanar spacing; XRD peaks shift to lower angles; no second phases or precipitates formed.
- **Property Improvements:** Vickers hardness increased from 8.3 GPa (no Nb) to maximum 10.9 GPa; compressive strength increased to 1.7 GPa maximum; solid solution strengthening was the dominant strengthening mechanism (grain refinement contribution was minimal at 25–35 MPa).
- **Risks and Limitations:** Solid solution softening occurs simultaneously (elastic modulus decreases from 300 GPa with Nb addition); excessive Nb content (5 wt.%) leads to strength decrease due to Nb's inherent softness and "compound effect."

## Grain Refinement / Grain Boundary Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Nb (grain refiner)
- **Typical Alloys:** W–Ta–Nb solid solutions
- **Key Compositions:** 1–5 wt.% Nb
- **Microstructural Changes:** Grain size decreases from 313 μm (0% Nb) to 217 μm (1% Nb), 177 μm (3% Nb), 163 μm (5% Nb); minimum grain size reaches 100 μm at 3 wt.% Nb; more nucleation media during solidification; suppression of grain-boundary micro-cracks.
- **Property Improvements:** Hall-Petch calculated grain-boundary strengthening contribution: 25 MPa (0%), 30 MPa (1%), 33 MPa (3%), 35 MPa (5%) — described as "very limited" compared to actual strength values; grain refinement reduces stress concentration tendency, making cracks difficult to produce and propagate.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Contribution to strength is much lower than solid solution strengthening; grain refinement alone cannot explain the observed strength.

## Solid Solution Softening

- **Related Elements:** Nb
- **Typical Alloys:** W–Ta–Nb solid solutions
- **Key Compositions:** Increased Nb content (1–5 wt.%)
- **Microstructural Changes:** Reduced shear modulus (G) and elastic modulus of the solid solution; reduced elastic strain energy of edge dislocations; easier initiation of ductile dislocations.
- **Property Improvements:** Increased ductility/toughness (compressive strain up to 24%); elastic modulus decreased from 300 GPa (W–30Ta) with increasing Nb; reduced G/B ratio (an indicator of toughness); improved plastic deformation ability.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Decreased modulus may be undesirable for high-stiffness applications; excessive softening may reduce overall mechanical performance.

## Ductile Dislocation Activation (Toughness Enhancement)

- **Related Elements:** Ta (activates ductile dislocations in W), Nb (reduces energy required for ductile dislocation initiation)
- **Typical Alloys:** W–Ta binary (Ta effect), W–Ta–Nb ternary (combined effect)
- **Key Compositions:** 30 wt.% Ta + 1–5 wt.% Nb
- **Microstructural Changes:** From predominantly screw dislocation slip (pure W) to appearance of ductile (edge) dislocations; increased dislocation mobility and density; edge dislocation energy lowered by Nb addition via reduced shear modulus.
- **Property Improvements:** Increased resistance to crack growth; improved plastic deformation ability; compressive strain up to 24%; mixed intergranular + transgranular fracture with dimples indicating improved toughness.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Ta alone insufficient for deformability; excessive Nb reduces overall strength; the paper does not provide quantitative fracture toughness values.

# Key Conclusions

1. W–Ta–Nb solid solution materials with uniform structure were successfully prepared by cold isostatic pressing (300 MPa) and vacuum arc melting (10 melting cycles, 5 min each time, furnace cooling). The main phase was single-phase BCC W–Ta–Nb solid solution.

2. Nb content in the solid solution increased with increasing Nb addition; lattice constant and interplanar spacing increased correspondingly (XRD peak shift to lower angles confirmed by TEM).

3. Nb has a significant grain-refining effect: grain size decreased from 313 μm (0% Nb) to 163 μm (5% Nb).

4. Relative density increased with Nb addition, reaching a maximum of 99.6% (Nb promotes densification and suppresses micro-cracks).

5. Vickers hardness increased first then decreased with Nb content: from 8.3 GPa without Nb to a maximum of 10.9 GPa.

6. Compressive strength increased first then decreased; maximum compressive strength was 1.7 GPa with strain up to 24% at an intermediate Nb content.

7. Small Nb additions effectively improved strength and toughness of W–Ta–Nb solid solutions by reducing the energy required for initiation of ductile dislocations; fracture mode changed from predominantly intergranular (W–30Ta) to mixed intergranular + transgranular with dimples.

8. The strengthening mechanisms include solid solution strengthening (dominant), grain refinement strengthening (limited contribution, 25–35 MPa), and composite effect (Nb softness causing strength decrease at high Nb content).

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations acknowledged in the paper:**
- The contribution of grain refinement to strength was calculated to be very low (25–35 MPa via Hall-Petch with K = 14 MPa·mm^(1/2)), indicating that the relationship between grain size and strength is not the dominant strengthening factor.
- Excessive Nb addition (5 wt.%) leads to decreased strength due to Nb's inherent softness and possible local inhomogeneity.
- The paper does not quantify the optimal Nb content precisely between 1 and 3 wt.% for maximum strength/toughness balance.

**Limitations not explicitly addressed in the original paper:**
- No high-temperature mechanical property testing (the paper discusses concerns about melting point reduction but provides no high-temperature data).
- No fracture toughness (KIC) testing or quantitative ductility measurement beyond compressive strain.
- No oxidation resistance characterization.
- No creep testing.
- No systematic strain rate sensitivity study.
- The full chemical composition of W–30Ta–1Nb alloy is not listed in Table 1 (only the name is mentioned).
- The specific alloy(s) achieving maximum hardness (10.9 GPa) and maximum compressive strength (1.7 GPa) are not explicitly identified in the text.
- The paper does not discuss the effect of interstitial impurities (C, O, N, H) on mechanical properties in detail, despite listing O and H contents in Table 1.
- Sample sizes and number of specimens tested for mechanical properties are not reported.
- No analysis of preferred crystallographic orientation or texture is provided.

**Applicable scope:**
- Tungsten-based solid solution alloys for aerospace, military, and advanced manufacturing applications requiring high strength, high density, high melting point, and improved toughness.
- The findings are specific to vacuum arc-melted W–30Ta alloys with small Nb additions (1–5 wt.%). The results may not be directly transferable to powder metallurgy, additive manufacturing, or other W-based alloy systems without additional validation.
