# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title**: Fabrication of Co-Cr-Ni-Ti medium entropy alloy via dual-wire arc additive manufacturing with excellent high-temperature mechanical properties
- **Authors**: Yuzeng Feng, Kang Peng, Shengguo Ma, Jun Cao, Tiehui Fang, Xizhang Chen
- **Journal**: Materials Letters
- **Year**: 2025
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.matlet.2025.139119
- **Alloy system studied**: Co-Cr-Ni-Ti medium entropy alloy (MEA)
- **Research objective**: To fabricate a Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA via dual-wire arc additive manufacturing (D-WAAM) and systematically investigate its microstructure and high-temperature mechanical properties
- **Material category**: Medium entropy alloy (MEA); additively manufactured (dual-wire arc additive manufacturing, D-WAAM)

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system**: Co-Cr-Ni-Ti medium entropy alloy with a face-centered cubic (FCC) matrix
- **Manufacturing route**: Dual-wire arc additive manufacturing (D-WAAM) with cold metal transfer (CMT) welding platform
- **Starting materials**: Custom-designed Co-Cr-Ni twisted wire (1.2 mm diameter) and pure Ti wire (0.8 mm diameter); the twisted wire was formed by twisting two Cr30Ni70 alloy wires and one pure Co wire, each with a diameter of 0.6 mm
- **Substrate**: 304 stainless steel plate
- **Key phases identified**: FCC matrix, L1₂ (spherical precipitates), η phase (blocky precipitates)

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Disordered FCC structure (confirmed by FFT pattern observed along the [011]FCC axis)
- **Second phases/precipitates**:
  - Blocky η phase in the interdendritic (ID) region (confirmed by FFT pattern)
  - Spherical L1₂ phase (confirmed by FFT pattern observed along the [001]FCC axis; the (110) diffraction point indicates superlattice ordering)
- **Microstructural morphology**: Characteristic dendritic (DR) microstructure
  - Dark gray areas: FCC phase (dendritic cores)
  - Bright white areas: η phase (interdendritic regions)
- **Elemental segregation**: Significant microsegregation due to non-equilibrium solidification from the rapid cooling rate of WAAM; Ni-Ti enrichment in the ID blocky η phase; Co-Cr segregation in the DR regions
- **Mixing enthalpy rationale**: The Ni-Ti atom pair exhibits a mixing enthalpy of −35 kJ/mol, which is more negative than other atom pairs, favoring the formation of Ni/Ti-rich intermetallic compounds
- **Precipitate size**: Not explicitly quantified in the original paper. The paper describes the L1₂ precipitates as “spherical” and smaller, while the η phase is “blocky” and larger.
- **Precipitate distribution**: Blocky η phase located in ID regions; spherical L1₂ precipitates observed among neighboring η phases
- **Grain size**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

# Mechanical Properties

All tensile tests were performed on a universal testing machine at a constant strain rate of 2 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹.

| Temperature | Yield Strength | Ultimate Tensile Strength | Elongation | Fracture Mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Room temperature (RT) | Not explicitly stated | Not explicitly provided as separate values | Not explicitly stated | Intergranular fracture |
| 500 °C | Not explicitly stated | Not explicitly stated | Higher than RT, specific value not stated | Ductile fracture |
| 600 °C | 547 MPa | 793 MPa | 16.7 % | Ductile fracture |
| 650 °C | Not explicitly stated | Not explicitly stated | ~5 % | Mixed ductile-brittle fracture |
| 700 °C | Not explicitly stated | ~699 MPa (ultimate tensile strength) | Not explicitly stated; described as brittle | Brittle fracture |

- **High-temperature strength retention**: The alloy retained an ultimate tensile strength of 699 MPa at 700 °C.
- **Strength trend**: Strength decreases at high temperatures, but the tensile strength reduction is “insignificant.”
- **Ductility anomaly**: The alloy exhibits higher ductility at 500 °C and 600 °C than at room temperature. Elongation decreases to ~5 % at 650 °C, and the alloy becomes brittle at 700 °C.
- **Ductility loss mechanism**: Attributed to the coarsening of the η phase at 700 °C, which leads to stress concentration and crack initiation, causing premature fracture.
- **Sawtooth flow behavior**: Clearly observable in the high-temperature tensile curves; attributed to dynamic strain aging (DSA) arising from interactions between moving dislocations and solute atoms.
- **Dislocation-precipitate interaction**:
  - Smaller spherical L1₂ precipitates: dislocations can shear through them
  - Larger blocky η phases: dislocations cannot directly cut through; dislocations form rings around them (Orowan bypass)
- **Comparison with other methods at 600 °C**: The D-WAAM-fabricated Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA exhibits better high-temperature tensile properties than alloys prepared by other methods (references [5,14,16–19] cited).

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Ti]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Co-Cr-Ni-Ti medium entropy alloy (FCC-based MEA) fabricated by D-WAAM
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Ti wire feeding speed of 1.5 m/min (pure Ti, 0.8 mm diameter); exact at.% not provided in the original paper
- **Main Function**: Formation of Ni-Ti-rich intermetallic compounds (η phase) and spherical L1₂ precipitates; contributes to high-temperature strength
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: The Ni-Ti atom pair has a mixing enthalpy of −35 kJ/mol, which is more negative than other atom pairs, favoring formation of Ni/Ti-rich intermetallic compounds in the interdendritic region during non-equilibrium solidification
- **Property Effect**: Contributes to ultimate tensile strength of 793 MPa at 600 °C and retention of 699 MPa at 700 °C; specific numerical values for Ti-only strengthening are not provided in the original paper
- **Potential Side Effects**: Coarsening of the η phase at 700 °C leads to stress concentration and crack initiation, causing premature fracture and loss of ductility (brittle at 700 °C, elongation ~5 % at 650 °C)
- **Original Evidence**: “The Ni-Ti atom pair (−35 kJ/mol) exhibits a more negative mixing enthalpy than other atom pairs [11], favoring the formation of Ni/Ti-rich intermetallic compounds.” (Section 3, Results and Discussion)

## [Co-Cr]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Co-Cr-Ni-Ti medium entropy alloy (FCC-based MEA) fabricated by D-WAAM
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Co-Cr-Ni twisted wire (1.2 mm diameter) formed by two Cr30Ni70 alloy wires and one pure Co wire, each 0.6 mm diameter; exact at.% not provided
- **Main Function**: Forms the dendritic FCC matrix phase
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Co-Cr segregates in the dendritic (DR) regions during non-equilibrium solidification, forming the disordered FCC matrix
- **Property Effect**: Provides the ductile FCC matrix that supports elongation of 16.7 % at 600 °C; specific numerical values for Co-Cr-only contribution are not provided in the original paper
- **Potential Side Effects**: None explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence**: “Co-Cr segregation dominates in the DR regions.” (Section 3, Results and Discussion; EDS mapping, Fig. 2c)

## [Ni+Ti]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Co-Cr-Ni-Ti medium entropy alloy (FCC-based MEA) fabricated by D-WAAM
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Exact at.% not provided
- **Main Function**: Formation of interdendritic blocky η phase
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: More negative mixing enthalpy (−35 kJ/mol) between Ni and Ti than other atom pairs favors the formation of Ni/Ti-rich intermetallic compounds under non-equilibrium solidification
- **Property Effect**: Produces the blocky η phase that impedes dislocation motion (Orowan bypass mechanism), thereby maintaining high-temperature strength
- **Potential Side Effects**: η phase coarsening at 700 °C causes premature fracture and brittleness
- **Original Evidence**: “Ni-Ti enrichment in the interdendritic (ID) blocky η phase, while Co-Cr segregation dominates in the DR regions.” (Section 3, Results and Discussion)

## [L1₂-forming elements (Ni, Ti)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Co-Cr-Ni-Ti medium entropy alloy (FCC-based MEA) fabricated by D-WAAM
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Exact composition not stated in the original paper
- **Main Function**: Formation of spherical L1₂ ordered precipitates
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: L1₂ phase formation is attributed to the in-situ heat treatment effect of additive manufacturing; FFT pattern along [001]FCC shows (110) diffraction point indicating superlattice ordering
- **Property Effect**: Smaller spherical L1₂ precipitates can be sheared by dislocations, contributing to strengthening while maintaining some ductility; contributes to the optimal balance of strength (793 MPa UTS at 600 °C) and plasticity (16.7 % elongation at 600 °C)
- **Potential Side Effects**: None explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence**: “The FFT pattern observed along the [001]FCC axis (Fig. 3(c₂)) reveals the spherical precipitates as L12 phase, where the (110) diffraction point indicates superlattice ordering. The formation of L12 phase can be attributed to the in-situ heat treatment effect of additive manufacturing [9,11].” (Section 3, Results and Discussion)

## Second-phase strengthening (dual-size precipitate interaction)

- **Related elements**: Ni, Ti (η and L1₂ phases)
- **Typical alloys**: Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA
- **Key compositions**: Two precipitate populations: smaller spherical L1₂ and larger blocky η
- **Microstructural changes**: Dual-size precipitates in the FCC matrix
- **Property improvements**: 
  - Smaller L1₂ precipitates are sheared by dislocations
  - Larger η precipitates are bypassed via Orowan looping
  - Combined mechanisms impede dislocation motion and promote solute aggregation, maintaining high-temperature strength and enhancing dynamic strain aging (DSA)
- **Risks and limitations**: η phase coarsening at elevated temperatures leads to brittleness
- **Original evidence**: “When the dislocation encounters the smaller spherical L12 precipitates, it can pass through the precipitates. Smaller spherical L12 phases allow dislocations to shear through, whereas larger blocky η phases cannot be directly cut, dislocations form rings around them to bypass.”

## Dynamic strain aging (DSA)

- **Related elements**: Solute atoms (not individually identified)
- **Typical alloys**: Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA
- **Key compositions**: Not applicable
- **Microstructural changes**: Precipitates impede dislocation motion and promote aggregation of solute atoms around moving dislocations
- **Property improvements**: Sawtooth flow behavior in high-temperature tensile curves; contributes to maintaining high-temperature strength
- **Risks and limitations**: DSA can be associated with reduced ductility and strain-rate sensitivity
- **Original evidence**: “It is noteworthy that a sawtooth flow behavior is clearly observable in the high temperatures tensile curve. This phenomenon is attributed to dynamic strain aging (DSA), arising from complex interactions between moving dislocations and solute atoms [14].”

## Additive manufacturing-oriented composition control

- **Related elements**: Ni, Ti (dual-wire approach)
- **Typical alloys**: Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA via D-WAAM
- **Key compositions**: Twisted Co-Cr-Ni wire (1.2 mm) + pure Ti wire (0.8 mm) at feed speeds of 7 m/min and 1.5 m/min, respectively
- **Microstructural changes**: In-situ alloying during deposition; in-situ heat treatment effect from repeated thermal cycling leads to L1₂ phase formation
- **Property improvements**: Enables fabrication of MEA with composition flexibility compared to single-wire WAAM; achieves UTS of 793 MPa and elongation of 16.7 % at 600 °C
- **Risks and limitations**: Non-equilibrium solidification causes microsegregation; η phase coarsening at elevated temperatures
- **Original evidence**: “The formation of L12 phase can be attributed to the in-situ heat treatment effect of additive manufacturing [9,11].”

# Key Conclusions

1. The Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA was successfully fabricated via dual-wire arc additive manufacturing (D-WAAM) using twisted wire and pure Ti wire.

2. The alloy exhibits a dendritic microstructure composed of three phases:
   - FCC matrix (dendritic regions)
   - Spherical L1₂ precipitates
   - Blocky η phase (interdendritic regions)

3. Significant microsegregation occurs due to non-equilibrium solidification from rapid cooling:
   - Ni-Ti enrichment in interdendritic η phase
   - Co-Cr segregation in dendritic FCC regions
   - The Ni-Ti atom pair's negative mixing enthalpy (−35 kJ/mol) favors Ni/Ti-rich intermetallic formation

4. The L1₂ phase formation is attributed to the in-situ heat treatment effect inherent to additive manufacturing.

5. Excellent high-temperature mechanical properties:
   - At 600 °C: YS = 547 MPa, UTS = 793 MPa, elongation = 16.7 % (optimal balance of strength and plasticity)
   - At 700 °C: UTS retains 699 MPa
   - Superior to alloys prepared by other methods at 600 °C

6. The dual-size precipitate strengthening mechanism:
   - Smaller spherical L1₂ precipitates are sheared by dislocations
   - Larger blocky η phases are bypassed via Orowan looping
   - Both mechanisms impede dislocation motion and promote dynamic strain aging

7. Ductility transition:
   - Higher ductility at 500 °C and 600 °C than at room temperature
   - Elongation drops to ~5 % at 650 °C
   - The alloy becomes brittle at 700 °C due to η phase coarsening, causing stress concentration and crack initiation

8. Fracture modes transition with temperature:
   - RT: intergranular fracture
   - 500 °C and 600 °C: ductile fracture
   - 650 °C: mixed ductile-brittle fracture
   - 700 °C: brittle fracture

9. The D-WAAM Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA is a promising candidate for high-temperature structural applications.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Composition quantification**: The exact atomic percentage or weight percentage composition of the deposited alloy is not provided in the original paper, limiting reproducibility of the exact alloy stoichiometry.
- **No post-deposition heat treatment**: Results pertain only to the as-deposited condition; the effect of post-deposition heat treatment on microstructure and mechanical properties is not explored.
- **Grain size not reported**: The paper does not provide grain size measurements, limiting complete characterization of the microstructure.
- **Limited temperature range for mechanical testing**: Tensile tests were performed at RT, 500 °C, 600 °C, 650 °C, and 700 °C; properties above 700 °C are not investigated.
- **No oxidation data**: No oxidation resistance testing was reported; the applicability of this alloy in oxidizing environments cannot be assessed from this paper.
- **No creep or long-term stability data**: Creep performance and long-term high-temperature service stability are not evaluated.
- **DSA effect**: The observed dynamic strain aging and sawtooth behavior may lead to reduced ductility and potentially negative effects on fatigue and strain-rate sensitivity, though these are not discussed.
- **η phase coarsening limitation**: The ductility loss at 700 °C due to η phase coarsening limits the maximum service temperature for applications requiring both strength and ductility.
- **In-situ heat treatment dependence**: The formation of L1₂ precipitates relies on the in-situ heat treatment effect of AM; this may vary with different deposition parameters or layer heights.
- **Applicable scope**: The findings are applicable to Co-Cr-Ni-Ti medium entropy alloys fabricated by dual-wire arc additive manufacturing under similar deposition parameters (twisted wire feed speed 7 m/min, Ti wire feed speed 1.5 m/min, travel speed 5 mm/s, voltage 21.3 V, current 187 A, argon shielding 15 L/min). Extrapolation to other material systems or processing routes should be made with caution.
