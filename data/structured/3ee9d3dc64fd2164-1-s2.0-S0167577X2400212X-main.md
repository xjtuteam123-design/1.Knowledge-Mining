# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies a newly designed Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ eutectic medium entropy alloy (EMEA) fabricated by powder plasma arc additive manufacturing (PPA-AM). The alloy is designed based on the Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni system, with Al and Cr contents fixed at 17 at.% each, and Co/Ni ratio adjusted to achieve a fully eutectic FCC/B2 (L1₂/B2) microstructure. The reference material is AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA), also fabricated by PPA-AM under identical processing conditions.

Alloy design rationale:
- Based on a literature survey of eutectic medium/high-entropy alloys (EM/HEAs) in the Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni system, the relationship between Al (or Cr) content and the (Co+Ni)/(Al+Cr) ratio was analyzed.
- The (Co+Ni)/(Al+Cr) ratio in eutectic alloys is concentrated between 1 and 2.5.
- The eutectic composition falls within a narrow range: Al content 15–21%, Cr content 10–26%.
- By setting Al = 17% and Cr = 17%, the composition Al₁₇Cr₁₇CoₓNi₆₆₋ₓ was obtained.
- JMatPro software (Ni-based database) was used to determine that x = 33 yields a complete FCC/B2 eutectic composition without a primary phase. The final designed alloy is Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃.
- Thermodynamic parameters were calculated to confirm MEA character: ΔS_conf = R·lnN (between R and 1.5R), ΔH_mix = −12.825 J/(K·mol), ΔS_mix = 11.093 J/(K·mol), δ = 5.33%, VEC = 7.8. These values comply with empirical rules for eutectic phase formation (ΔH_mix between −18 and −6 kJ/mol, VEC between 6 and 8.5, δ > 3%).

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Phase constitution
- **Matrix phases**: Ordered hard BCC-B2 phase + ordered soft FCC-L1₂ phase, arranged in an alternating lamellar eutectic structure.
- **XRD**: The Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ alloy shows more pronounced (111) and (220) FCC diffraction peaks than AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁, and additionally shows a (222) BCC/B2 diffraction peak.
- **SAED**: Confirms the presence of two alternating phases, L1₂ and B2.

## Elemental partitioning
- **L1₂ phase**: Rich in Co and Cr.
- **B2 phase**: Rich in Ni and Al.

## Phase fractions and microstructural metrics (measured by ImageJ software)
| Metric | Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ | AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ |
|--------|---------------------|----------------|
| FCC/L1₂ phase fraction | 42.41% | 39.40% |
| Regular eutectic region fraction | 42.95% | 39.22% |
| Irregular eutectic region fraction | 57.05% | 60.78% |

- The average lamellar thickness (both bright and dark layers) is significantly smaller in Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ than in AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁. Specific thickness values are not explicitly provided in the extracted text; only a qualitative statement is given.

## Dislocation observations
- TEM bright-field imaging shows dislocations accumulating near the L1₂/B2 phase boundaries, attributed to thermal residual stresses arising from rapid heating and cooling cycles during PPA-AM.

## Grain structure
- Both alloys exhibit a typical eutectic colony microstructure. Grain size data are not explicitly provided in the original paper.
- No specific precipitates (carbides, oxides, borides, silicides, etc.) are reported. The microstructure consists solely of the two-phase L1₂/B2 eutectic.

## Fractography
- Both alloys exhibit a mixed fracture mode.
- Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃: Significant necking in FCC/L1₂ phases; cleavage fracture in B2 phases. Fracture surface dominated by finer dimples, trench-like patterns, and some cleavage planes around grain boundaries, indicating good plasticity at room temperature.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Al]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Eutectic medium/high entropy alloys in the Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni system, particularly the Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ EMEA and AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ EHEA.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 17 at.% Al in the designed Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ alloy; literature survey indicates eutectic compositions have Al content between 15% and 21%.
- **Main Function**: Critical element for BCC/B2 eutectic phase formation; partitioning to the B2 phase.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Al partitions preferentially to the B2 phase, together with Ni. The Al content is crucial for achieving the FCC/B2 (L1₂/B2) complete eutectic microstructure without a primary phase, as confirmed by JMatPro solidification path calculations.
- **Property Effect**: Through the formation of a finer eutectic structure, Al contributes to higher microhardness (335.54 HV), ultimate tensile strength (1028 MPa), and elongation (19.09%) compared to the AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ reference (871.99 MPa, 14.40%). Specific Al-only property contributions are not isolated in the original paper.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly discussed in the original paper, but high Al content in Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni systems tends to promote B2/BCC phase formation, which can reduce ductility if the B2 volume fraction becomes excessive.
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 1(a) shows the relationship between Al(Cr) content and (Co+Ni)/(Al+Cr) ratio; Fig. 2 EDS maps show Al enrichment in the B2 phase; Fig. 1(b) JMatPro solidification path confirms no primary phase.

## [Cr]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Eutectic medium/high entropy alloys in the Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni system, particularly the Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ EMEA and AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ EHEA.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 17 at.% Cr in the designed Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ alloy; literature survey indicates eutectic compositions have Cr content between 10% and 26%.
- **Main Function**: L1₂/FCC phase former; partitioning to the L1₂ phase.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Cr is enriched in the L1₂ phase (together with Co). The Cr content is closely related to the critical Al content for BCC eutectic phase formation, as the Al and Cr contents are kept consistent in the design of the CoNi matrix.
- **Property Effect**: Not individually isolated in the original paper. The L1₂ phase volume fraction (42.41%) contributes to ductility improvement. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Cr-only property contributions.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly discussed in the original paper. Generally, high Cr content in Al-containing HEAs can promote brittle sigma phase formation, but this is not discussed in the paper.
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 2 EDS maps show Cr enrichment in the L1₂ phase; the design logic in Section 3 states that “Cr was closely related to the critical Al element for BCC eutectic phase formation.”

## [Co]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Eutectic medium/high entropy alloys in the Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni system, particularly the Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ EMEA and AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ EHEA.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## [Solid-Solution Strengthening]

- **Related Elements**: Al, Cr, Co, Ni (in the Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ MEA); Al, Cr, Co, Fe, Ni (in the AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ EHEA).
- **Typical Alloys**: Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃, AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁.
- **Key Compositions**: Multicomponent equiatomic or near-equiatomic mixtures producing medium/high configurational entropy.
- **Microstructural Changes**: Formation of single-phase or dual-phase solid solutions (L1₂ and B2 ordered phases). Both phases are solid solutions of multiple elements with elemental partitioning: L1₂ = Co, Cr-rich; B2 = Ni, Al-rich.
- **Property Improvements**: The phase composition with hard B2 and soft L1₂ provides a combination of strength and ductility. Specific solid solution strengthening contribution is not isolated. The overall UTS is 1028 MPa, yield strength 488 MPa, elongation 19.09%.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Not explicitly discussed. Thermal residual stresses from PPA-AM generate dislocations near phase boundaries, which interact with the solid solution strengthening.
- **Original Evidence**: XRD and SAED confirm L1₂/B2 phases; Fig. 2 TEM shows dislocations near phase boundaries.

## [Phase Interfacial Strengthening (L1₂/B2 Interface)]

- **Related Elements**: Al, Cr, Co, Ni.
- **Typical Alloys**: Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ eutectic medium entropy alloy.
- **Key Compositions**: 17 at.% Al, 17 at.% Cr, 33 at.% Co, 33 at.% Ni.
- **Microstructural Changes**: Fine alternating L1₂/B2 lamellar structure. The finer lamellar thickness in Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ compared to AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ produces a higher density of FCC/B2 interfaces. Dislocations accumulate near the L1₂/B2 phase boundaries due to thermal residual stresses from rapid heating/cooling cycles during PPA-AM.
- **Property Improvements**: Ultimate tensile strength 1028 MPa, elongation 19.09%, microhardness 335.54 HV. Compared to AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ UTS 871.99 MPa, elongation 14.40%, this is a 17.89% increase in UTS and 32.57% increase in elongation.
- **Risks and Limitations**: The B2 phase (hard, ordered BCC) undergoes cleavage fracture. Mixed fracture mode: necking in L1₂ phases, cleavage fracture in B2 phases. The lamellar structure also creates local stress concentrations at interfaces under thermal residual stress.
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 4(b) tensile curves; the paper states “the enhanced density of FCC/B2 interfaces induced by the finer eutectic spacing could remedy the ductility of the alloy.” Also “finer eutectic structure is the main reason for the alloy obtains higher microhardness.”

## [Eutectic Colony and Lamellar Refinement Strengthening]

- **Related Elements**: Al, Cr, Co, Ni.
- **Typical Alloys**: Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃, AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁.
- **Key Compositions**: Dual-phase L1₂/B2 eutectic.
- **Microstructural Changes**: The eutectic colony consists of regular and irregular layered regions. Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ has a higher regular region fraction (42.95% vs. 39.22%) and higher FCC/L1₂ phase fraction (42.41% vs. 39.40%). The average lamellar thickness is significantly smaller in Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ than in AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁.
- **Property Improvements**: Higher microhardness (335.54 HV vs. 278.35 HV), higher UTS (1028 MPa vs. 871.99 MPa), higher elongation (19.09% vs. 14.40%). The paper states: “The regular eutectic with higher fraction would lead to the higher strength for the alternating layers in EM/HEAs.”
- **Risks and Limitations**: The abrupt transition between regular and irregular layers may create heterogeneous deformation fields. Not explicitly quantified in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 3(c) and Fig. 3(d) show ImageJ analysis of phase fractions and layer thicknesses; Section 3 states the effect of finer eutectic structure.

# Key Conclusions

1. A novel Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ eutectic medium entropy alloy was successfully designed using thermodynamic calculations (JMatPro) and fabricated by powder plasma arc additive manufacturing (PPA-AM).
2. The PPA-AMed alloy consists of fine alternating ordered L1₂ (soft FCC) and B2 (hard BCC) phases, with no primary phase (complete eutectic solidification).
3. The Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ alloy exhibits a yield strength of 488 MPa, ultimate tensile strength of 1028 MPa, elongation of 19.09%, and microhardness of 335.54 HV at room temperature.
4. Compared to the PPA-AMed AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ reference alloy, the designed alloy shows a 17.89% increase in UTS and a 32.57% increase in elongation.
5. The superior mechanical properties are attributed to:
   - Higher proportion of regular eutectic regions (42.95% vs. 39.22%)
   - Higher FCC/L1₂ phase fraction (42.41% vs. 39.40%)
   - Finer lamellar eutectic structure with higher FCC/B2 interface density
   - Dislocations accumulated near L1₂/B2 phase boundaries due to thermal residual stresses from PPA-AM
6. The fracture mode of Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃ is mixed: significant necking in L1₂ phases and cleavage in B2 phases, with finer dimples, trench-like patterns, and some cleavage planes.

## Limitations inferred from the extracted content

2. **No oxidation resistance data**: No oxidation weight gain, oxide layer characterization, or long-term service stability tests are reported. The suitability of this alloy for high-temperature oxidizing environments is not addressed.
3. **No post-processing heat treatment**: The effect of heat treatment, annealing, HIP, or other post-AM treatments on microstructure and properties is not investigated.
4. **Limited composition range**: Only one composition (Al₁₇Cr₁₇Co₃₃Ni₃₃) is compared to one reference (AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁). No systematic composition variation study is performed.
5. **No creep or fatigue data**: The paper focuses on static tensile properties at room temperature only.
6. **Microstructural quantification is limited**: Some values are given (phase fractions, regular/irregular region fractions), but absolute lamellar thickness values are not explicitly stated in the extracted text (only qualitative comparison of “significantly smaller”).
7. **No detailed discussion of anisotropy**: As an additively manufactured material, the mechanical properties may be anisotropic; this is not addressed.
8. **Applicable scope**: The findings are applicable to eutectic medium entropy alloys in the Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni system fabricated by PPA-AM, particularly those with FCC/B2 (L1₂/B2) dual-phase structures. Extrapolation to other alloy systems or processing routes should be done with caution.

## Additional note on material classification
This alloy is not a refractory high-temperature alloy in the traditional sense (e.g., Nb-W, Mo-W, Ta-based, or refractory high-entropy alloys with melting points > 2000 °C). The Al-Co-Cr-Ni system has relatively low melting points and is primarily designed for moderate-temperature structural applications. This limitation should be considered when retrieving this knowledge for refractory alloy applications.
