# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Interfacial segregation enables exceptional high-temperature thermal stability in magnesium alloy
- **Authors**: Jianxin Zhou, Xiaojun Luo, Hong Yang, Yuyang Gao, Wenlong Xie, Zhihua Dong, Bin Jiang, Fusheng Pan
- **Journal**: Vacuum
- **Year**: 2025 (Volume 238, Article 114311)
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.vacuum.2025.114311
- **Alloy System Studied**: Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys (magnesium rare-earth alloys)
- **Research Objective**: To enhance the thermal stability of Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys by optimizing the distribution of solute atoms at grain boundaries and phase boundaries; specifically studying the effect of Gd/Y content variation on interfacial segregation and high-temperature mechanical properties
- **Material Category**: Wrought magnesium alloy (hot-extruded Mg-RE alloy) — **Note: This is NOT a refractory high-temperature alloy, Nb-W-based alloy, Mo-W-based alloy, refractory high-entropy alloy, or additively manufactured refractory alloy.**

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy System**: Mg-xGd-(10-x)Y-1.5Zn-1Mn (x = 2, 4, 6, 8 wt%)
- **Four alloy variants**:
  - 8Gd-2Y: Mg-8Gd-2Y-1.5Zn-1Mn (wt%)
  - 6Gd-4Y: Mg-6Gd-4Y-1.5Zn-1Mn (wt%)
  - 4Gd-6Y: Mg-4Gd-6Y-1.5Zn-1Mn (wt%)
  - 2Gd-8Y: Mg-2Gd-8Y-1.5Zn-1Mn (wt%)
- **Research Focus**: Interfacial segregation of Gd, Y, Zn, Mn at grain boundaries and α-Mn particle boundaries; its effect on thermal stability and high-temperature strength
- **Note**: This paper is about a **magnesium alloy system**, not a refractory alloy system. The knowledge extraction below is provided for completeness but falls outside the intended refractory alloy domain.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Melting**: Fabricated in a controlled atmosphere of SF₆ (1 vol%) and CO₂ (99 vol%) using pure Mg (99.99%), Mg-30Gd (wt%), Mg-30Y (wt%), Mg-5Mn (wt%) master alloys and pure Zn (99.99%)
- **Homogenization**: 520 °C for 14 h
- **Extrusion**: 420 °C with a ram velocity of 0.1 mm/s and an extrusion ratio of 11:1
- **Post-extrusion heat treatment**: Not stated
- **Thermal exposure test**: 4Gd-6Y alloy held at 300 °C for 100 h to evaluate grain size stability

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix**: α-Mg (hcp)
- **Secondary phases**:
  - Blocky LPSO (long-period stacking ordered) phase
  - Lamellar LPSO phase (18R and 14H type LPSO phases in Mg-Y-Zn system per cited reference [35])
  - Mg₅RE phase (in as-homogenized state; transforms with increasing Y content)
  - Mg₃RE phase (at grain boundaries in 8Gd-2Y alloy, before full transformation to LPSO)
  - RE-enriched phases
  - α-Mn particles (nanoscale, uniformly distributed)
- **Grain structure after extrusion**: Bimodal microstructure with fine dynamic recrystallized (DRXed) grains and coarse deformed grains
- **Average DRXed grain sizes**:
  - 8Gd-2Y: 1.65 μm
  - 6Gd-4Y: 1.64 μm
  - 4Gd-6Y: 1.33 μm (smallest)
  - 2Gd-8Y: 1.87 μm
- **Texture**: All alloys exhibit similar texture with [011̄0] direction parallel to extrusion direction (ED)
- **α-Mn particle characteristics**:
  - Volume fractions: 8Gd-2Y (0.97%), 6Gd-4Y (0.97%), 4Gd-6Y (2.17%), 2Gd-8Y (1.04%)
  - Average diameters: 8Gd-2Y (65.4 nm), 6Gd-4Y (62.8 nm), 4Gd-6Y (57.7 nm), 2Gd-8Y (63.3 nm)
  - 4Gd-6Y exhibits the highest number density and smallest size of α-Mn particles
- **Interfacial segregation**:
  - Gd, Y, Zn, Mn co-segregate at grain boundaries in all alloys
  - Zn and Y segregate at α-Mn particle interfaces
  - Gd preferentially accumulates in α-Mn particle interiors
  - Y concentration at α-Mn particle interface peaks at ~3.18% in 4Gd-6Y alloy
- **LPSO phase coarsening**: The 2Gd-8Y alloy exhibits significant coarsening of lamellar LPSO phase compared to other alloys

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxidation resistance**: Not addressed in this paper.
- **Service stability**: The 4Gd-6Y alloy demonstrated remarkable thermal stability; even after 100 h heating at 300 °C, grain size did not significantly increase.
- **High-temperature microstructural stability**: Post-tensile testing at 300 °C revealed α-Mn particles preserved their morphology and exhibited no coarsening, indicating exceptional high-temperature shear resistance and thermal stability.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Gd + Y (combined rare-earth)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Total RE (Gd+Y) fixed at 10 wt%; individual Gd/Y ratio varies from 8/2 to 2/8
- **Main Function**: Solid-solution strengthening; grain boundary segregation for thermal stability; phase boundary segregation control
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Gd and Y have larger atomic radii than Mg and induce positive misfits when occupying GB sites; they co-segregate with Zn and Mn at GBs to reduce interfacial energy. Gd preferentially accumulates in α-Mn particle interiors (negative formation entropy), while Y segregates at α-Mn interfaces (positive formation entropy).
- **Property Effect**: At 300 °C, 4Gd-6Y alloy achieved YS 266 MPa, UTS 296 MPa. The optimal Y/Gd ratio (4Gd-6Y) maximizes α-Mn particle number density (2.17 vol%) and minimizes α-Mn particle size (57.7 nm).
- **Potential Side Effects**: Excess Y (2Gd-8Y) leads to LPSO phase coarsening, which depletes Y/Zn from α-Mn interfaces and reduces α-Mn strengthening effectiveness. Not explicitly stated whether ductility is compromised at high Y content.
- **Original Evidence**: "The interfacial concentration of GB and α-Mn particles significantly influence grain size, α-Mn particle size, and number density, which in turn critically impact the differences in mechanical behavior among these alloys." (Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Section 3)

## [Zn]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 1.5 wt% (fixed)
- **Main Function**: LPSO phase formation; GB and α-Mn particle boundary segregation
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Zn has smaller atomic radius than Mg and creates negative misfits at GB sites, co-segregating with RE elements; Zn segregates at α-Mn particle interfaces, limiting Mn diffusion and suppressing particle coarsening
- **Property Effect**: Zn segregation at α-Mn interfaces contributes to high-temperature thermal stability of the α-Mn strengthening particles. Not explicitly stated with specific numerical values for Zn-only contribution.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Excess Y/Zn aggregation at stacking faults leads to LPSO phase coarsening via ledge mechanism, competing with α-Mn for Y/Zn elements
- **Original Evidence**: "The Y and Zn elements segregated at the α-Mn particle boundaries, significantly suppressing the growth of α-Mn particles and promoting the formation of more α-Mn particles." (Abstract, Fig. 3)

## [Mn]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 1 wt% (fixed)
- **Main Function**: Formation of α-Mn particles for Orowan strengthening; GB segregation
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Mn combines with the α-Mg matrix to form nanoscale α-Mn particles. Mn segregation at GBs contributes to thermal stability. The α-Mn particles serve as strong obstacles to dislocation motion, even at 300 °C.
- **Property Effect**: Orowan strengthening contribution for 4Gd-6Y alloy calculated at ~56.8 MPa. α-Mn particles resist shearing at 300 °C.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Without proper interfacial segregation, α-Mn particles may coarsen and lose Orowan strengthening effectiveness at elevated temperatures.
- **Original Evidence**: "The preserved morphology and absence of coarsening in α-Mn particles highlight their exceptional high-temperature shear resistance and thermal stability." (Fig. 5, Section 3)

## [α-Mn nanoparticles]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Volume fractions 0.97–2.17%; average diameters 57.7–65.4 nm
- **Main Function**: Orowan dispersion strengthening; high-temperature thermal stability when interface-stabilized
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: α-Mn nanoparticles impede dislocation motion (both ⟨a⟩ and ⟨c+a⟩ type dislocations) during high-temperature deformation; they resist shearing due to Y/Zn interfacial segregation
- **Property Effect**: In the 4Gd-6Y alloy, α-Mn particles contribute ~56.8 MPa strengthening via Orowan mechanism; particles remain stable after 300 °C deformation
- **Potential Side Effects**: Without sufficient Y/Zn interfacial segregation, α-Mn particles may coarsen at high temperature, diminishing Orowan strengthening effectiveness
- **Original Evidence**: "Post-tensile testing at 300 °C (Fig. 5(c and d)) reveals that dislocations, including ⟨a⟩ and ⟨c+a⟩ types, are effectively impeded by α-Mn particles." (Section 3)

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## [Grain-Boundary Strengthening]

- **Related Elements**: Gd, Y, Zn, Mn (co-segregating solutes)
- **Typical Alloys**: Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys
- **Key Compositions**: Mg-xGd-(10-x)Y-1.5Zn-1Mn (x = 2–8 wt%)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Solute segregation at GBs (Gibbs free energy of segregation ΔG_Seg for Gd: −7.7 kJ/mol, Y: −7.3 kJ/mol, Zn: −6.5 kJ/mol, Mn: −3.9 kJ/mol, all negative, favoring segregation). Fine DRXed grains formed during extrusion.
- **Property Improvements**: At 300 °C, grain size remains stable after 100 h; dislocations blocked at GBs during high-temperature tensile testing
- **Risks and Limitations**: As temperature rises, Hall-Petch k_y parameter decreases rapidly; "inverse" Hall-Petch phenomenon may occur if grain size falls below critical threshold; GB sliding and migration become problematic
- **Original Evidence**: "The solute segregation of Gd/Y/Zn/Mn elements at GBs effectively inhibits grain growth and obstructs dislocation movement, enabling the 4Gd-6Y alloy to maintain high strength at 300 °C."

## [Orowan Strengthening / Precipitation Strengthening]

- **Related Elements**: Mn (via α-Mn particles); Y and Zn (via interfacial segregation stabilizing particles)
- **Typical Alloys**: Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys
- **Key Compositions**: Mn content 1 wt% (fixed); optimum Gd/Y ratio 4:6
- **Microstructural Changes**: Nanoscale α-Mn particles uniformly distributed in matrix; 4Gd-6Y has highest volume fraction (2.17%) and smallest size (57.7 nm)
- **Property Improvements**: Orowan strengthening contribution calculated at ~56.8 MPa (4Gd-6Y alloy); α-Mn particles resist shearing at 300 °C, maintaining high thermal stability
- **Risks and Limitations**: Secondary phases are highly susceptible to shearing and exhibit inherent instability at phase boundaries at elevated temperatures; coarsening reduces strengthening effectiveness
- **Original Evidence**: "The higher concentration of Y/Zn elements at α-Mn particle interfaces contributes to the formation of high-density and small-sized α-Mn particles, while preventing their coarsening and shearing during high-temperature deformation." (Section 3)

## [Solid-Solution Strengthening]

- **Related Elements**: Gd and Y (RE elements with larger atomic radii than Mg)
- **Typical Alloys**: Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys
- **Key Compositions**: Total RE (Gd+Y) = 10 wt%
- **Microstructural Changes**: Gd and Y dissolved in α-Mg matrix; atomic size mismatch creates lattice distortion
- **Property Improvements**: Contributes to baseline strength; specific numerical contribution not separately calculated in this paper
- **Risks and Limitations**: Not explicitly discussed
- **Original Evidence**: Not explicitly quantified in this paper

## [Interfacial Segregation-Based Thermal Stabilization]

- **Related Elements**: Gd, Y, Zn, Mn (co-segregating at GBs); Y, Zn (segregating at α-Mn PBs)
- **Typical Alloys**: Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys
- **Key Compositions**: Optimal ratio Mg-4Gd-6Y-1.5Zn-1Mn
- **Microstructural Changes**: Reduced GB energy via Gibbs adsorption; Y/Zn segregation at α-Mn interfaces inhibits Mn diffusion into particles; reduced driving force for GB migration and particle coarsening
- **Property Improvements**: Exceptional thermal stability at 300 °C: grain size unchanged after 100 h; YS 266 MPa and UTS 296 MPa maintained at 300 °C
- **Risks and Limitations**: Competitive segregation between LPSO phase and α-Mn particles for Y/Zn elements can reduce interface stability of α-Mn particles
- **Original Evidence**: "In accordance with the Gibbs adsorption isotherm, the segregation of solute atoms at the interface mitigates interface energy, thereby diminishing the driving force and retarding interfacial migration."

# Key Conclusions

1. **Optimal Gd/Y ratio**: The Mg-4Gd-6Y-1.5Zn-1Mn alloy exhibits superior room-temperature and high-temperature mechanical properties compared to 8Gd-2Y, 6Gd-4Y, and 2Gd-8Y alloys.

2. **Yield strength at 300 °C**: The 4Gd-6Y alloy achieved a YS of 266 MPa and UTS of 296 MPa at 300 °C, outperforming previously documented Mg alloys.

3. **Grain boundary segregation**: Gd, Y, Zn, and Mn co-segregate at grain boundaries in all alloys. The negative Gibbs free energies of segregation (Gd: −7.7, Y: −7.3, Zn: −6.5, Mn: −3.9 kJ/mol) confirm thermodynamic favorability of segregation.

4. **α-Mn particle interface segregation**: Y and Zn segregate at α-Mn particle boundaries, suppressing particle growth and promoting formation of more α-Mn particles. The 4Gd-6Y alloy has the highest Y concentration at α-Mn interfaces (~3.18%).

5. **Competitive segregation**: Excess Y (2Gd-8Y) leads to LPSO phase coarsening through competitive consumption of Y/Zn, reducing α-Mn interface stability.

6. **Grain size stability**: The 4Gd-6Y alloy shows no significant grain growth after 100 h at 300 °C, demonstrating excellent thermal stability.

7. **Strengthening contributions**: GB strengthening (~155 MPa) and α-Mn particle Orowan strengthening (~56.8 MPa) are the primary strengthening contributors in the 4Gd-6Y alloy at room temperature.

8. **Design paradigm**: The study introduces an alloy design strategy based on optimizing solute distribution at GBs/PBs to enhance thermal stability.
