# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title**: Achieving a high-strength ZK60 alloy by introducing nano solute clusters
- **Authors**: Hang Zhang, Teng Li, Rongguang Li, Boshu Liu, Shanshan Li, Di Wu
- **Journal**: Materials Letters
- **Year**: 2024 (Available online 18 October 2023)
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.matlet.2023.135432
- **Alloy system studied**: ZK60 magnesium alloy (Mg-Zn-Zr system)
- **Research objective**: To fabricate a high-strength ZK60 alloy plate by introducing a microstructure with high-density nano solute clusters through low-temperature rolling at 100 °C, and to elucidate the formation mechanism of nano clusters and related strengthening mechanisms.
- **Material category**: Wrought magnesium alloy (ZK60 commercial Mg alloy). **Note**: This is NOT a refractory alloy. It does not belong to Nb-W, Mo-W, W-based, refractory high-entropy alloy, or additively manufactured refractory alloy categories. It is a Mg-Zn-Zr wrought alloy.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Starting material**: Commercial extruded ZK60 alloy bar
- **Sample dimensions**: 50 mm (length) × 20 mm (width) × 3 mm (thickness)
- **Homogenization**: 400 °C for 24 h
- **Rolling direction**: Parallel to extrusion direction (ED)
- **Preheating before rolling**:
  - R200 sample: preheated at 200 °C for 20 min, rolled with a thickness reduction of 70%
  - R100 sample: preheated at 100 °C for 20 min, rolled with a thickness reduction of 40%
- **Roller temperature**: Heated to the respective rolling temperatures (200 °C or 100 °C) to reduce temperature loss
- **Sample labeling**: R200 = rolled at 200 °C; R100 = rolled at 100 °C
- **Rationale for temperature selection**: Based on previous work, 200 °C was chosen to facilitate the formation of high-density solute clusters and precipitates, while 100 °C was chosen to facilitate the formation of high-density solute clusters specifically. (Note: the original text states 200 °C for "solute clusters and precipitates" and 100 °C for "solute clusters" — the wording regarding 200 °C/clusters appears in the original as: "the rolling temperature of 200 °C and 100 °C are chosen for facilitating the formation of high-density solute clusters and precipitates, respectively." This may contain an ambiguity; the actual results show precipitates dominant in R200 and clusters dominant in R100.)
- **Arc melting, vacuum melting, induction melting, powder metallurgy, mechanical alloying, hot pressing, SPS, HIP, AM**: Not used in this work.
- **Rolling temperature**: 100 °C and 200 °C
- **Deformation amounts**: 40% thickness reduction (R100), 70% thickness reduction (R200)
- **Post-rolling annealing or aging**: Not performed.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Mg (HCP)
- **Precipitates (R200 sample)**:
  - Average precipitate size: 53 nm
  - Phase identified by Fast Fourier Transform (FFT): [1 1 0]-oriented C14-type MgZn₂ Laves phase
  - Precipitates form mainly in elongated grains
- **Precipitates (R100 sample)**:
  - Average precipitate size: 24 nm
  - Phase identified by FFT: also [1 1 0]-oriented C14-type MgZn₂ Laves phase
  - Number density and size of precipitates in R100 are less than R200
- **Grain structure (R200 sample)**:
  - Volume fraction of dynamically recrystallized (DRXed) grains: 89%
  - DRXed grain size: 1.4 µm
  - Almost no twins observed
  - Low dislocation density (from KAM map)
  - Typical basal texture: maximum multiple of uniform distribution (MUD) = 10
- **Grain structure (R100 sample)**:
  - Volume fraction of DRXed grains: 13%
  - DRXed grain size: 0.5 µm
  - Twins observed in elongated grains
  - Higher dislocation density than R200 (from KAM map)
  - Basal texture: MUD = 12
- **Nano solute clusters (R100 sample only)**:
  - Zn atoms segregated on dislocations; Zr atoms show random distribution
  - Satellite reflection spots observed in diffraction pattern of elongated grains, indicating high-density Zn solute clusters
  - Satellite spots hard to observe in DRXed grains → clusters mainly formed in elongated grains
  - Rod-shaped clusters: average length = 10 nm, average width = 0.5 nm
  - Block-shaped clusters: average size = 2 nm, randomly distributed in elongated grains
- **Nano substructures (R100)**: Many nano substructures present (proven by diffraction rings)
- **Elemental segregation**: Zn enriches in precipitates and segregates on dislocations; Zr only enriches in precipitates
- **Discussed strengthening mechanisms**: Dislocation strengthening, nano substructure strengthening, nano cluster strengthening, grain boundary strengthening, precipitation strengthening, texture strengthening

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Zn (Zinc)

- **[Element]**: Zn
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: ZK60 (Mg-Zn-Zr) wrought magnesium alloy
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 5.86 wt.% Zn in ZK60 alloy
- **[Main Function]**: Formation of high-density nano solute clusters on dislocations and nano substructures during low-temperature rolling; provides strong dragging effect on dislocation motion, which significantly enhances strength.
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: During rolling at 100 °C, the inhibited recovery of dislocations and limited diffusion rate of Zn atoms cause Zn atoms to diffuse only to nearby dislocations and nano substructures, forming high-density rod-shaped (10 nm × 0.5 nm) and block-shaped (~2 nm) solute clusters. These clusters act as strong obstacles to dislocation motion.
- **[Property Effect]**: R100 sample with Zn clusters achieves YS = 336 MPa and UTS = 375 MPa, which are 129 MPa and 56 MPa higher than R200 sample (YS = 207 MPa, UTS = 319 MPa), respectively.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: Reduced room-temperature elongation (R100 elongation = 5% vs. R200 elongation = 17%).
- **[Original Evidence]**: Figure 3(j) shows Zn atoms enriched in precipitates and segregating on dislocations; Figure 4(d) shows satellite reflection spots in elongated grains, indicating high-density Zn solute clusters; Figure 4(f–k) shows rod-shape and block-shape clusters.

## Zr (Zirconium)

- **[Element]**: Zr
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: ZK60 (Mg-Zn-Zr) wrought magnesium alloy
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 0.54 wt.% Zr in ZK60 alloy
- **[Main Function]**: Grain refinement of Mg alloys (typically through Zr-induced nucleation); in this work, Zr is observed enriched only in precipitates and shows random distribution in the matrix.
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: EDS mapping shows Zr atoms are only enriched in the precipitates (Figure 3(k), Figure 4(c)). The original paper does not explicitly discuss the grain-refinement role of Zr in this specific work, though it is a well-known grain refiner in Mg alloys.
- **[Property Effect]**: Not explicitly quantified in the original paper with regard to Zr alone. The DRXed grain size in R100 is 0.5 µm and in R200 is 1.4 µm, but the contribution of Zr to these values is not isolated.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: Not discussed in the original paper.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Figure 3(k) and Figure 4(c): "The Zr atoms are only enriched in the precipitates" and "Zr atoms show random distribution."

## Zn + Zr (Combined)

- **[Element Combination]**: Zn + Zr
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: ZK60 (Mg-5.86Zn-0.54Zr, wt.%) wrought magnesium alloy
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 5.86 wt.% Zn + 0.54 wt.% Zr
- **[Main Function]**: Zn provides age-hardening and solute-cluster strengthening; Zr provides grain refinement. In this work, the combination with low-temperature rolling creates a synergistic strengthening from grain boundaries, precipitates, dislocations, nano substructures, and nano clusters.
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Low-temperature rolling inhibits recovery and DRX, creating high-density dislocations and nano substructures. Zn atoms segregate on these defects to form nano clusters. Zr-rich precipitates (C14-MgZn₂ Laves phase) form, with smaller precipitate size (24 nm) in R100 vs. 53 nm in R200.
- **[Property Effect]**: The combined effect of dislocations, nano substructures, and nano clusters compensates for the loss in grain-boundary strengthening and precipitation strengthening in R100, yielding 336 MPa YS and 375 MPa UTS.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: Lower ductility (5% elongation in R100 vs. 17% in R200).
- **[Original Evidence]**: Discussion section, paragraph 2: "the strong strengthening effect caused by high-density dislocations, nano substructures, and nano clusters can compensate the loss in strengthening effect from grain boundaries, and precipitates."

## C14-MgZn₂ Laves Phase

- **[Phase]**: C14-type MgZn₂ Laves phase
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: ZK60 (Mg-Zn-Zr) alloy
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: Not applicable (precipitate phase, not an added element)
- **[Main Function]**: Precipitation strengthening
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: [1 1 0]-oriented C14-type MgZn₂ Laves phase precipitates form in both R200 (average size 53 nm) and R100 (average size 24 nm) samples. Precipitates form mainly in elongated grains. Zr is enriched in these precipitates.
- **[Property Effect]**: Precipitation strengthening contributes to the strength of both samples. However, R200 has stronger precipitation strengthening than R100 due to larger precipitate density; yet R100 still has higher overall strength due to dominant nano cluster, dislocation, and nano substructure strengthening.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: Not discussed. The reduction in precipitation strengthening in R100 is compensated by other mechanisms.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Figure 3(c) and 3(g): FFT patterns identify the precipitates as [1 1 0]-oriented C14 type MgZn₂ Laves phase.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Nano Solute Cluster Strengthening

- **[Related Elements]**: Zn
- **[Typical Alloys]**: ZK60 rolled at low temperature (100 °C)
- **[Key Compositions]**: Mg-5.86Zn-0.54Zr (wt.%)
- **[Microstructural Changes]**: High-density Zn solute clusters form on dislocations and nano substructures in elongated grains. Rod-shaped clusters: 10 nm length, 0.5 nm width. Block-shaped clusters: ~2 nm. Identified by satellite reflection spots in diffraction patterns along <0001> zone axis.
- **[Property Improvements]**: YS increased from 207 MPa (R200) to 336 MPa (R100); UTS increased from 319 MPa to 375 MPa. The clusters provide strong dragging effect on dislocation motion, playing a crucial role in enhancing strength.
- **[Risks and Limitations]**: Elongation reduced from 17% (R200) to 5% (R100).
- **[Original Evidence]**: Discussion section: "the high-density solute clusters can provide strong dragging effect on the dislocation motion, which plays crucial role in enhancing the strength"; Figure 4(d) satellite reflection spots.

## Dislocation Strengthening

- **[Related Elements]**: Not element-specific; enabled by low-temperature rolling.
- **[Typical Alloys]**: ZK60 rolled at 100 °C
- **[Key Compositions]**: Mg-5.86Zn-0.54Zr (wt.%)
- **[Microstructural Changes]**: High-density dislocations in R100 sample, as shown by KAM maps (Figure 2(d) vs. 2(i)).
- **[Property Improvements]**: Contributes to the 129 MPa higher YS of R100 vs. R200.
- **[Risks and Limitations]**: May reduce ductility.
- **[Original Evidence]**: KAM map in Figure 2(i) shows higher dislocation density in R100 than R200.

## Nano Substructure Strengthening

- **[Related Elements]**: Not element-specific; induced by low-temperature rolling.
- **[Typical Alloys]**: ZK60 rolled at 100 °C
- **[Key Compositions]**: Mg-5.86Zn-0.54Zr (wt.%)
- **[Microstructural Changes]**: Many nano substructures exist in R100 sample, proven by diffraction rings in TEM (Figure 3(h)).
- **[Property Improvements]**: Contributes to high strength along with dislocations and nano clusters.
- **[Risks and Limitations]**: Not explicitly quantified as a separate contribution.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Figure 3(h): "The diffraction rings prove that many nano substructures exist in R100 sample."

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening

- **[Related Elements]**: Zr (grain refiner)
- **[Typical Alloys]**: ZK60 rolled at 200 °C (DRXed microstructure)
- **[Key Compositions]**: Mg-5.86Zn-0.54Zr (wt.%)
- **[Microstructural Changes]**: R200: 89% DRXed grains with average size 1.4 µm. R100: 13% DRXed grains with average size 0.5 µm.
- **[Property Improvements]**: R200 derives significant strengthening from the high fraction of fine DRXed grains; however, R100 has stronger overall strengthening from clusters/dislocations despite weaker grain-boundary strengthening.
- **[Risks and Limitations]**: Loss of grain-boundary strengthening in R100 is compensated by dislocation, nano substructure, and nano cluster strengthening.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Figure 2(a) and 2(f); Discussion section: "the strong strengthening effect caused by high-density dislocations, nano substructures, and nano clusters can compensate the loss in strengthening effect from grain boundaries."

## Texture Strengthening

- **[Related Elements]**: Not element-specific.
- **[Typical Alloys]**: ZK60 rolled at 100 °C and 200 °C
- **[Key Compositions]**: Mg-5.86Zn-0.54Zr (wt.%)
- **[Microstructural Changes]**: Both samples have basal texture. R200: MUD = 10; R100: MUD = 12.
- **[Property Improvements]**: Texture strengthening is similar between R100 and R200 because MUD values are close.
- **[Risks and Limitations]**: Not a dominant differentiator between the two samples.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Figure 2(e) and 2(j); Discussion section: "the texture strengthening effect should be similar."

## Precipitation Strengthening

- **[Related Elements]**: Zn, Zr
- **[Typical Alloys]**: ZK60 rolled at 200 °C and 100 °C
- **[Key Compositions]**: Mg-5.86Zn-0.54Zr (wt.%); precipitates are C14-type MgZn₂ Laves phase
- **[Microstructural Changes]**: R200: average precipitate size 53 nm, mainly in elongated grains; R100: average precipitate size 24 nm, fewer precipitates than R200.
- **[Property Improvements]**: Precipitation strengthening contributes to both samples but is more pronounced in R200.
- **[Risks and Limitations]**: In R100, reduced precipitation strengthening is compensated by nano clusters, dislocations, and nano substructures.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Figures 3(a–c) and 3(d–g); Discussion section.

# Key Conclusions

1. A high-strength ZK60 alloy plate with a high-density of nano Zn solute clusters was successfully fabricated by rolling at 100 °C.
2. R100 sample achieved a high yield strength of 336 MPa and ultimate tensile strength of 375 MPa, significantly superior to R200 sample (YS = 207 MPa, UTS = 319 MPa).
3. The high-density dislocations and nano substructures formed during low-temperature rolling at 100 °C facilitate the segregation of Zn atoms, leading to the formation of high-density nano solute clusters (rod-shaped: 10 nm × 0.5 nm; block-shaped: ~2 nm).
4. The strong strengthening effect caused by high-density dislocations, nano substructures, and nano clusters can compensate for the loss in strengthening from grain boundaries and precipitates in R100.
5. The finding of nano solute clusters sheds light on designing high-strength Mg alloys, and conventional low-temperature rolling can be readily implemented in industrial production.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy system limitation**: This paper studies ZK60 magnesium alloy, which is **not** a refractory high-temperature alloy. It does not involve Nb-W, Mo-W, W-based, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. All findings are specific to Mg-Zn-Zr wrought alloys processed by low-temperature rolling.
- **Testing scope**: Only room-temperature tensile properties were measured. No high-temperature mechanical properties, creep, oxidation resistance, or fracture toughness data are provided.
- **Processing scope**: Only conventional rolling at 100 °C and 200 °C was investigated. No arc melting, powder metallurgy, additive manufacturing, or other processing routes were used.
- **Mechanism quantification**: The individual contributions of dislocations, nano substructures, nano clusters, grain boundaries, precipitates, and texture to the total strengthening are discussed qualitatively but not quantitatively separated (e.g., no Taylor equation or superposition model calculations are provided).
- **Alloy composition**: Only one nominal composition (ZK60: Mg-5.86Zn-0.54Zr wt.%) was studied. The effect of varying Zn and Zr content on nano cluster formation was not explored.
- **Ductility trade-off**: The high strength in R100 is accompanied by reduced elongation (5% vs. 17% in R200), indicating a strength-ductility trade-off that may limit application in structural components requiring higher ductility.
- **Generalizability**: The formation mechanism of nano Zn solute clusters may or may not be transferable to other Mg alloy systems or to refractory alloys; no such transferability is claimed or demonstrated in this paper.
