# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Microstructure and mechanical property of a novel ReMoTaW high-entropy alloy with high density
- **Authors:** Qinqin Wei, Qiang Shen, Jian Zhang, Ben Chen, Guoqiang Luo, Lianmeng Zhang
- **Journal:** International Journal of Refractory Metals & Hard Materials
- **Year:** 2018
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2018.05.006
- **Alloy System Studied:** ReMoTaW refractory high-entropy alloy (equiatomic)
- **Material Category:** Refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA)
- **Research Objective:** To develop a high-density HEA by replacing Nb with Re in NbMoTaW, targeting density above 15 × 10³ kg/m³ for applications such as armor-piercing projectiles and density gradient materials, and to characterize its microstructure, density, and mechanical properties.

- **Research Object:** Investigate phase formation, segregation behavior, density, compressive mechanical properties, and strengthening mechanisms of the ReMoTaW HEA in the as-arc-melted condition.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Preparation Method:** Vacuum arc melting
- **Starting Materials:** Elemental powders of Re, Mo, Ta, and W with purities > 99.95%
- **Atmosphere:** Protective argon atmosphere
- **Crucible/Plate:** Water-cooled copper plate
- **Melting Details:** Re-melted five times; flipped for each melt; held in the liquid state for about 6 minutes during each melting event
- **Ingot Mass:** 20 g
- **Ingot Geometry:** Buttons ~6 mm thick, ~15–20 mm in diameter
- **Heat Treatment:** None reported — characterized in as-cast (as-arc-melted) condition. The paper states that “microstructure of this alloy in annealed condition will be studied in the future.” No homogenization, rolling, forging, aging, or additive manufacturing reported.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Overall Phase Constitution:** Three disordered solid solution phases observed:
  1. **Matrix:** Single BCC solid solution phase; lattice parameter a = 0.3176 nm.
  2. **Darker region (Re-rich phase):** HCP crystal structure; lattice parameters a = 0.2503 nm, c = 0.4678 nm.
  3. **Brighter region (Ta-rich phase):** Tetragonal structure; lattice parameters a = 1.0201 nm, c = 0.5011 nm; identified as solid solution based on β-Ta phase (referred to as β-Ta-rich phase).

- **Microstructural Features:**
  - **Matrix:** Coarse dendritic structure; average grain size ~182.76 μm.
  - **Particles:** Average grain size ~1.93 μm; distributed in grain interior of the matrix.
  - **Elemental Segregation:** Ta is rich in the particle phase (~86.45 at.% Ta by SEM-EDS); Re is enriched in the darker HCP phase; matrix composition is close to equiatomic.

- **VEC Analysis:** The valence electron concentration (VEC) for ReMoTaW alloy is determined as 6, which lies within the proposed range for BCC solid solution formation (VEC < 6.87 per Guo et al.).

- **Phase Segregation Explanation (as given in paper):**
  - Mo, Ta, and W are infinitely soluble with each other (per binary phase diagrams), while Re has limited but large solubility in the other elements.
  - The high entropy effect extends solubility limits, allowing most Re to remain in solid solution.
  - Due to limited solubility, a small amount of Re precipitates as a Re-rich phase.
  - The limited solid solution of Re in Ta further reduces the solubility of Ta in the matrix.
  - Ta has larger atomic size, causing serious lattice distortion and hindering atomic diffusion, thus forming a Ta-rich phase.
  - Melting point temperature differences: Re-rich phase (Tm* = 3432 K) solidifies first; β-Ta-rich phase (Tm* = 3281 K) solidifies later during non-equilibrium solidification.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Re (Rhenium)

**Element:** Re

**Applicable Alloy System:** ReMoTaW refractory high-entropy alloy; W-based and Mo-based systems (per cited references [12, 13])

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 25 at.% (equiatomic); Re-rich phase contains 81.04 at.% Re; matrix contains 23.45 at.% Re

**Main Function:** Increases alloy density; enhances room-temperature ductility of BCC elements (e.g., W and Mo); contributes to solid solution formation.

**Microstructural Mechanism:** Re has a large but limited solubility in Mo, Ta, W; high entropy effect extends solubility limits, so most Re is solid soluble in the BCC matrix; a small amount precipitates as an HCP Re-rich phase. Re has a smaller atomic size compared to Nb, reducing lattice distortion in the matrix relative to NbMoTaW.

**Property Effect:** Density of ReMoTaW is 16.69 × 10³ kg/m³ (significantly higher than NbMoTaW's 13.75 × 10³ kg/m³). The maximum compressive strength is 1451 MPa, and failure strain is 5.69% (both ~20% and ~170% higher than NbMoTaW, respectively). Hardness is 640 ± 23 HV. The original paper attributes the enhanced room-temperature plasticity to smaller lattice distortion from Re's smaller atomic size.

**Potential Side Effects:** Limited solubility in other elements leads to phase segregation (formation of HCP Re-rich phase). Increased density renders the alloy present if considering applications requiring moderate density, but no specific side effect reported in this paper.

**Original Evidence:** "Re, which has high density of 21.03 × 10³ kg/m³ and can enhance the ductility for several BCC elements such as W and Mo [12,13]" (Section 1, page 9). "This alloy shows the enhanced room-temperature plasticity, mainly because this alloy has smaller lattice distortion due to the smaller atomic size of Re than that of Nb [14]" (Section 3, page 10). "Due to the large solubility of Re in other elements and the high entropy effect which can extend the solubility limits, most of Re is solid soluble in the matrix. As the solubility of Re is limited, Re cannot be fully soluble in the BCC matrix and a small amount is precipitated as Re-rich phase" (Section 3, page 10). EDS data in Table 1 and TEM results in Fig. 2.

## Ta (Tantalum)

**Element:** Ta

**Applicable Alloy System:** ReMoTaW refractory high-entropy alloy; refractory HEAs in general

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 25 at.% (equiatomic); β-Ta-rich phase contains 86.77 at.% Ta; matrix contains 23.56 at.% Ta

**Main Function:** Contributes to density; forms β-Ta-rich second-phase particles that provide precipitation strengthening.

**Microstructural Mechanism:** Ta has limited solid solution in the matrix due to Re's limited solubility in Ta; Ta's larger atomic size causes serious lattice distortion, hindering atomic diffusion and promoting Ta-rich phase formation. β-Ta-rich phase (tetragonal, a = 1.0201 nm, c = 0.5011 nm) precipitates as relatively fine and homogeneously distributed particles (~1.93 μm average size) in the grain interior. Ta-rich phase solidifies later than Re-rich phase due to lower melting point.

**Property Effect:** Precipitation of fine β-Ta-rich particles impedes dislocation motion in the BCC matrix, increasing strength via precipitation strengthening. The maximum compressive strength is 1451 MPa. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the strengthening increment attributable solely to Ta-rich precipitates.

**Potential Side Effects:** Formation of a secondary phase (phase segregation) in an alloy nominally expected to form a single solid solution.

**Original Evidence:** "The composition analysis result obtained by EDS shows that element Ta is rich in the particle phase and the content is about 86.45 at.%" (Section 3, page 9). "The precipitation of relatively fine and homogeneously distributed β-Ta-rich particles are likely to impede the motion of dislocations in the BCC matrix and increase the strength of the alloy in the form of precipitation strengthening [20]" (Section 3, page 10). "As the melting point temperatures (Tm*) of the regions shown in Table 1..., Re-rich phase having high melting point will solidify first and β-Ta-rich phase having low melting point solidify later" (Section 3, page 10).

## Mo+Ta+W (Combination)

**Element Combination:** Mo + Ta + W

**Applicable Alloy System:** ReMoTaW refractory high-entropy alloy

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Mo 25 at.% + Ta 25 at.% + W 25 at.% (equiatomic total of 75 at.%)

**Main Function:** Forms the BCC matrix solid solution; provides density and solid solution strengthening.

**Microstructural Mechanism:** Mo, Ta, and W are infinitely soluble with each other per binary phase diagrams, forming a miscible BCC solid solution matrix. Ta has larger atomic size causing lattice distortion, while Mo and W distribute relatively smoothly in the matrix.

**Property Effect:** BCC matrix solid solution provides the primary load-bearing phase; the alloy achieves 1451 MPa maximum compressive strength and 640 ± 23 HV. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the individual contributions.

**Potential Side Effects:** Despite mutual solubility of Mo, Ta, and W, the presence of Re disrupts full mixing, resulting in three-phase segregation.

**Original Evidence:** "element Mo, Ta and W are infinitely soluble with each other while Re is limited but large soluble in other elements" (Section 3, page 10).

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid Solution Strengthening

**Mechanism:** Solid solution strengthening

**Related Elements:** Re, Mo, Ta, W in the BCC matrix

**Typical Alloys:** ReMoTaW high-entropy alloy

**Key Compositions:** Equiatomic ReMoTaW; BCC matrix with composition Re 23.45 at.%, Mo 26.52 at.%, Ta 23.56 at.%, W 26.37 at.% (by EDS)

**Microstructural Changes:** BCC solid solution formed with lattice parameter a = 0.3176 nm. The matrix composition is close to equiatomic despite segregation.

**Property Improvements:** Maximum compressive strength of 1451 MPa at room temperature. Enhanced room-temperature plasticity attributed to smaller lattice distortion (Re replacing Nb in NbMoTaW). Failure strain of 5.69% (~170% higher than NbMoTaW).

**Risks and Limitations:** Solid solution strengthening is limited by phase segregation; Ta's larger atomic size causes lattice distortion that hinders atomic diffusion, contributing to segregation rather than uniform solid solution strengthening.

**Original Evidence:** "the element compositions are close to equiatomic" (Section 3, page 9, Table 1). "This alloy shows the enhanced room-temperature plasticity, mainly because this alloy has smaller lattice distortion due to the smaller atomic size of Re than that of Nb" (Section 3, page 10).

## Precipitation Strengthening (Second-Phase Strengthening)

**Mechanism:** Carbide-free precipitation strengthening via β-Ta-rich intermetallic-like phase

**Related Elements:** Ta (primary precipitate former), Re (secondary HCP phase)

**Typical Alloys:** ReMoTaW high-entropy alloy

**Key Compositions:** β-Ta-rich phase with 86.77 at.% Ta; Re-rich HCP phase with 81.04 at.% Re

**Microstructural Changes:** Relatively fine and homogeneously distributed β-Ta-rich particles (~1.93 μm average size) embedded in BCC matrix grain interior; quasi-cleavage fracture surface shows fine second-phase particles.

**Property Improvements:** Precipitation strengthening contributes to both increased strength (1451 MPa, ~20% higher than NbMoTaW) and hardness (640 ± 23 HV, ~44% higher than NbMoTaW). The original paper does not provide the specific quantitative contribution of precipitation strengthening to these improvements.

**Risks and Limitations:** Presence of second phases may degrade ductility relative to a true single-phase HEA; the paper does not discuss whether the second phases cause premature failure.

**Original Evidence:** "the precipitation of relatively fine and homogeneously distributed β-Ta-rich particles are likely to impede the motion of dislocations in the BCC matrix and increase the strength of the alloy in the form of precipitation strengthening [20]" (Section 3, page 10).

## Cocktail Effect (High-Entropy Alloy Design Concept)

**Mechanism:** Cocktail effect (synergistic mixing of multiple elements)

**Related Elements:** Re, Mo, Ta, W

**Typical Alloys:** ReMoTaW high-entropy alloy

**Key Compositions:** Equiatomic ReMoTaW

**Microstructural Changes:** The alloy exhibits three disordered solid solution phases (BCC matrix, HCP Re-rich phase, tetragonal β-Ta-rich phase) rather than a single phase.

**Property Improvements:** Enhanced hardness of 640 ± 23 HV is attributed to the cocktail effect originating from the high hardness of Re, combined with precipitation strengthening. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values separating cocktail effect contribution from precipitation strengthening.

**Risks and Limitations:** The cocktail effect is qualitative; phase segregation indicates that the high-entropy mixing is incomplete in this system.

**Original Evidence:** "The enhanced hardness of the ReMoTaW alloy is caused by the cocktail effect, which is originated from the high hardness of Re, and precipitation strengthening of relatively fine and homogeneously distributed β-Ta-rich particles" (Section 3, page 10).

# Key Conclusions

1. A novel ReMoTaW high-entropy alloy with a high density of 16.69 ± 0.01 × 10³ kg/m³ was successfully synthesized by vacuum arc melting. This density surpasses the highest previously reported HEA density (NbMoTaW, 13.75 × 10³ kg/m³).

2. Despite the equiatomic nominal composition, the ReMoTaW alloy exhibits three disordered solid solution phases:
   - BCC matrix (a = 0.3176 nm)
   - HCP Re-rich phase (a = 0.2503 nm, c = 0.4678 nm)
   - Tetragonal β-Ta-rich phase (a = 1.0201 nm, c = 0.5011 nm)

3. Phase segregation is explained by differences in binary alloy solubility (Mo, Ta, W infinitely soluble; Re limited solubility) and melting point differences during non-equilibrium solidification (Re-rich phase solidifies first at Tm* = 3432 K; β-Ta-rich phase later at Tm* = 3281 K).

4. The maximum compressive strength is 1451 MPa, which is ≈20% higher than that of NbMoTaW. The failure strain is 5.69%, which is ≈170% higher than that of NbMoTaW.

5. The Vickers hardness is 640 ± 23 HV, ≈44% higher than that of NbMoTaW.

6. The enhanced room-temperature plasticity is attributed to smaller lattice distortion from Re's smaller atomic size compared to Nb. Enhanced strength and hardness are attributed to precipitation strengthening from fine β-Ta-rich particles and the cocktail effect.

7. The fracture mode is quasi-cleavage with river-pattern markings.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Applicable Scope:** This is a short communication focusing on the as-cast microstructure and room-temperature compressive mechanical properties of a novel ReMoTaW equiatomic HEA for high-density applications.
- **Limitations:**
  1. **No high-temperature mechanical testing:** The paper does not report elevated-temperature strength, creep, or oxidation resistance, which are critical for refractory alloys.
  2. **No heat treatment study:** Only as-cast condition was examined; the paper explicitly states that annealed microstructure will be studied in the future.
  3. **No phase stability investigation:** The stability of the three phases under thermal exposure is not addressed.
  4. **No impurity analysis:** Final oxygen, nitrogen, carbon, and hydrogen contents are not reported despite using powder raw materials.
  5. **Limited property characterization:** Only room-temperature compression and hardness were measured; no tensile properties, fracture toughness, or elastic modulus were reported.
  6. **No oxidation testing:** No oxidation resistance data are provided.
  7. **Strengthening attribution qualitative:** The relative contributions of solid solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, and cocktail effect are discussed qualitatively without quantitative separation.
  8. **Small specimen geometry:** Compression specimens were 4 mm height × 2 mm diameter (very small), which may affect measured property values.
  9. **Single composition only:** Only the equiatomic ReMoTaW composition was studied; no compositional variations were investigated.
  10. **Porosity concern:** Measured density is 0.48% lower than rule-of-mixtures value, attributed to porosity formed during melting; no porosity quantification or optimization reported.
