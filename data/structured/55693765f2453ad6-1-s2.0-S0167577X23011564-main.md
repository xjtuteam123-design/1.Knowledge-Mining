# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Effect of normalizing temperature on the strengthening mechanisms of the advanced Re-containing 10%Cr steel with low N and high B contents
- **Authors**: A. Fedoseeva, I. Brazhnikov
- **Journal**: Materials Letters
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.matlet.2023.134971
- **Alloy System Studied**: Re-containing 10%Cr-2%W-0.8%Cu martensitic steel (Fe-based, not a refractory alloy system)
- **Material Category**: 9-12% Cr martensitic heat-resistant steel (creep-resistant steel for fossil fuel power plants)

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Research Objective**: To investigate the effect of normalizing temperature (NT) ranging from 1050 °C to 1200 °C on the structure and tensile properties of a Re-containing 10%Cr-2%W-0.8%Cu steel, and to select the optimal NT for further creep tests.
- **Material Category**: Advanced 9-12% Cr martensitic steel (not a refractory high-temperature alloy; this is a heat-resistant steel for power plant applications).
- **Application Context**: Tubes, pipes, and blades for fossil fuel power plants operating at 600–620 °C and 25–30 MPa.

- **Refractory elements present**: W (2.1 wt.%), Mo (0.6 wt.%), Nb (0.05 wt.%), Re (0.17 wt.%).
- **Notable features**: Low N (0.002 wt.%) and high B (0.015 wt.%) contents; Re addition.
- **Impurity-level elements**: Not explicitly categorized as ppm-level impurities in the original paper, though N is very low at 0.002 wt.%.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Melting**: Vacuum-induction furnace melting.
- **Chemical composition control**: Optical emission spectrometer FOUNDRY-MASTER-UVR.
- **Normalizing (NT)**: Four different normalizing temperatures were investigated: 1050 °C, 1100 °C, 1150 °C, and 1200 °C, each for 1 h.
- **Tempering**: Following normalizing, tempering at 770 °C for 3 h, air cooling.
- **Thermo-Calc predicted phase regions**:
  - 1050–1100 °C: Austenite + MX + W₂B; carbon in martensite after cooling = 0.0975–0.0994 wt.%.
  - 1150–1200 °C: Austenite + W₂B; carbon in martensite = 0.1 wt.%.
  - Full dissolution of MX carbonitrides at NTs higher than 1110 °C.
  - Delta-ferrite formation temperature predicted as 1242 °C; experimentally, 5% delta-ferrite was found after 1200 °C.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Tempered martensite lath structure (TMLS) — BCC ferritic matrix with martensitic laths.
- **Second phases**:
  - M₂₃C₆ carbides (Cr-rich carbides).
  - MX carbonitrides:
    - Nb-rich MX (NbX) at normalizing temperatures of 1050 °C and 1100 °C (round particles).
    - (V,Nb)-rich MX at normalizing temperatures of 1150 °C and 1200 °C (plate-like particles).
  - W₂B phase (white particles in SEM), decreasing in amount with increasing NT, fully dissolved at 1200 °C.
  - Delta-ferrite: 0% at 1050 °C and 1100 °C; 0.3% at 1150 °C; 4.8% at 1200 °C.
- **Prior austenite grain (PAG) size**:
  - 1050 °C NT: 64 ± 5 μm
  - 1100 °C NT: 75 ± 5 μm
  - 1150 °C NT: 105 ± 10 μm
  - 1200 °C NT: 122 ± 10 μm
  - PAG size doubled when NT exceeded 1110 °C due to absence of Zener pinning force from full dissolution of MX carbonitrides.
- **Lath structure**:
  - Mean lath width: 320 ± 15 nm (regardless of NT; individual values: 330 ± 20 nm at 1050 °C, 323 ± 20 nm at 1100 °C, 335 ± 20 nm at 1150 °C, 310 ± 20 nm at 1200 °C).
  - Dislocation density inside laths (TEM): (2–3) × 10¹⁴ m⁻² (specific values: 2.1 ± 0.5 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² at 1050 °C, 2.2 ± 0.5 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² at 1100 °C, 2.8 ± 0.5 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² at 1150 °C and 1200 °C).
  - Dislocation density in lath boundaries (EBSD): 1.3 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² at 1050 °C, 1.8 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² at 1100 °C, 1.0 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² at 1150 °C, 1.3 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² at 1200 °C; maximal after 1100 °C.
  - Lath area per unit volume: 0.44 m⁻¹ at 1050 °C, 0.56 m⁻¹ at 1100 °C (+27% compared to 1050 °C), 0.32 m⁻¹ at 1150 °C, 0.42 m⁻¹ at 1200 °C.
- **Boundary features**:
  - Fraction of low-angle boundaries (LABs, 2–5° misorientation corresponding to martensitic laths) increased by 16% as NT increased from 1050 °C to 1200 °C.
  - Distance between high-angle boundaries (HABs) increased by 33% with NT increase from 1050 °C to 1200 °C.
  - Block size (h_block) ~ 0.025 × D_PAG.
  - Kernel average misorientation (local distortion): maximum 0.375 rad regardless of NT.
- **M₂₃C₆ carbide characteristics**:
  - Mean size: 72 ± 5 nm (1050 °C NT), 57 ± 5 nm (1100 °C NT), 118 ± 10 nm (1150 °C NT), 127 ± 10 nm (1200 °C NT).
  - Volume fraction: 0.018% (1050 °C and 1100 °C), 0.01% (1150 °C and 1200 °C).
  - At 1050–1100 °C NT: precipitated as dense chains along both HABs and LABs, with most M₂₃C₆ having orientation relationships (ORs) with martensitic laths.
  - At 1150–1200 °C NT: located along HABs and LABs as separate particles; ORs between carbides and matrix were not observed.
- **MX carbonitride characteristics**:
  - NbX: mean size 35 ± 3 nm (1050 °C NT), 25 ± 3 nm (1100 °C NT); volume fraction 0.00053% at both NTs; round morphology.
  - (V,Nb)X: mean size 34 ± 3 nm (1150 °C NT), 41 ± 3 nm (1200 °C NT); volume fraction 0.0025% at both NTs; plate-like morphology.
  - Volume fraction of (V,Nb)X was 5 times higher than that of NbX.
- **Matrix composition** (EDX, regardless of NT): 9.8 at.% Cr, 0.62% W, 0.35% Mo, 2.9% Co.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed in the paper**: Lath strengthening, dislocation strengthening, solid solution strengthening, particle strengthening (M₂₃C₆ and MX).

# Mechanical Properties

- **Room-temperature (20 °C) tensile properties**:
  - Yield strength (YS): +10% increase with NT up to 1100 °C compared to other NTs.
  - Ultimate tensile strength (UTS): +10% increase with NT up to 1100 °C compared to other NTs.
  - Increasing NT to 1200 °C provided the same level of tensile properties as 1050 °C.
- **High-temperature (650 °C) tensile properties**:
  - No increment in both YS and UTS was observed regardless of NT.
- **Strengthening contributions to 20 °C yield strength** (calculated via linear combination):
  - σ₀ (lattice friction stress): 17 MPa.
  - σ_disl (dislocation strengthening): 145–160 MPa (same regardless of NT).
  - σ_lath (subgrain boundary strengthening): 130 MPa (same regardless of NT).
  - σ_ss (solid solution strengthening): 140 MPa (same regardless of NT).
  - σ_part (particle strengthening): 143 MPa (1050 °C NT), 181 MPa (1100 °C NT), ~130 MPa (1150 °C and 1200 °C NT).
  - The +10% YS increase at 1100 °C NT is attributed to the +53 MPa increase in particle strengthening (from 143 MPa at 1050 °C to 181 MPa at 1100 °C).
  - Particle strengthening after 1150 °C and 1200 °C was about 130 MPa, with high fraction of (V,Nb)X partially compensating for the loss of M₂₃C₆ strengthening.
- **Creep properties (650 °C, initial stresses 100–200 MPa)**:
  - Short-term creep tests at 200 MPa and 180 MPa after 1050 °C and 1100 °C NT showed similar rupture time.
  - The paper suggests (but does not confirm) that increased particle strengthening due to refined M₂₃C₆ retaining ORs with laths and NbX may stabilize TMLS for longer time during creep and overcome creep strength breakdown.
- **Testing conditions**:
  - Flat specimens with cross section of 1.5 mm × 3 mm and 16 mm gauge length for tensile tests.
  - Instron 5882 testing machine.
  - Creep specimens with 25 mm gauge length and 7 mm × 3 mm cross section.
  - Test temperatures: 20 °C and 650 °C.
- **Hardness**: Not reported in the original paper.
- **Elastic modulus**: Not reported in the original paper.
- **Density**: Not reported in the original paper.
- **Fracture toughness**: Not reported in the original paper.

## [W (Tungsten)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: 10%Cr martensitic heat-resistant steel.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 2.1 wt.% W (in the present steel); previous works used 3 wt.% W.
- **Main Function**: Solid-solution strengthening of the matrix; W also participates in W₂B formation and Laves phase formation (in previous works with higher W content).
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: W contributes to solid solution strengthening (σ_ss, calculated as 140 MPa regardless of NT). W₂B particles form during normalizing and dissolve with increasing NT (fully dissolved at 1200 °C). W in matrix is 0.62% (by EDX) regardless of NT. The decrease in W content from 3% to 2.1% (compared to previous works [5–7]) is associated with modified creep resistance behavior.
- **Property Effect**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the effect of W alone on mechanical properties. Solid solution strengthening contribution from all alloying elements in matrix is 140 MPa at 20 °C.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly discussed in this paper; however, decrease in W content (from 3% to 2%) is noted in context of changing creep resistance behavior in previous works.
- **Original Evidence**: "The change in alloying of the Re-containing 10% Cr steel by decrease in W content and addition of Cu improves the short-term creep resistance, but this does not overcome the loss in creep resistance after 10,000 h [8]."

## [B (Boron)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: 10%Cr martensitic heat-resistant steel.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0.015 wt.% B (described as "high B contents").
- **Main Function**: Boron forms W₂B particles during normalizing; these particles are present as white particles in SEM images.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: W₂B phase acts as pinning particles. The amount of W₂B decreased with increasing normalizing temperature, reaching full dissolution at 1200 °C. The presence of W₂B affects the Zener pinning force, which contributes to controlling prior austenite grain size. The paper notes that the absence of pinning Zener force due to full dissolution of MX carbonitrides (not W₂B specifically) at NTs higher than 1110 °C causes the double increase in PAG size.
- **Property Effect**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the effect of B alone on mechanical properties.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not discussed in this paper.
- **Original Evidence**: "The amount of W₂B phase shown as white particles in Fig. 1c decreased to full their dissolution, as NT increased to 1200 °C."

## [Nb (Niobium)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: 10%Cr martensitic heat-resistant steel.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0.05 wt.% Nb.
- **Main Function**: Niobium forms Nb-rich MX carbonitrides (NbX) at normalizing temperatures of 1050–1100 °C; at higher NTs (1150–1200 °C), Nb combines with V to form (V,Nb)-rich MX carbonitrides.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: NbX particles provide Zener pinning force that controls prior austenite grain size during normalizing. Full dissolution of NbX occurs at NTs > 1110 °C, leading to abnormal grain growth (PAG size doubles). NbX also contributes to particle strengthening (σ_part). The morphology changes from round NbX (at 1050–1100 °C) to plate-like (V,Nb)X (at 1150–1200 °C). NbX is refined by 31% at 1100 °C compared to 1050 °C (from 35 ± 3 nm to 25 ± 3 nm).
- **Property Effect**: Particle strengthening contribution: 143 MPa (1050 °C NT, with NbX at 35 nm) and 181 MPa (1100 °C NT, with NbX refined to 25 nm). The +53 MPa increase in particle strengthening at 1100 °C is attributed to refinement of both M₂₃C₆ and NbX.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Full dissolution of NbX at NTs > 1110 °C leads to loss of Zener pinning force and doubling of PAG size.
- **Original Evidence**: "MX changed from round Nb-rich MX at NTs of 1050 °C and 1100 °C (Fig. 2b) to plate-like (V,Nb)-rich MX at NTs of 1150 °C and 1200 °C (Fig. 2c). NbX and (V,Nb)X had similar sizes, but volume fraction of (V,Nb)X was 5 times higher than that of NbX."

## [V (Vanadium)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: 10%Cr martensitic heat-resistant steel.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0.2 wt.% V.
- **Main Function**: Vanadium forms (V,Nb)-rich MX carbonitrides at high normalizing temperatures (1150–1200 °C).
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: At NTs of 1150 °C and 1200 °C, V combines with Nb to form plate-like (V,Nb)X carbonitrides. The volume fraction of (V,Nb)X is 5 times higher than that of NbX, which partially compensates for the loss of strengthening from coarsened M₂₃C₆ carbides. The (V,Nb)X particles form during tempering after normalizing when NbX has been dissolved.
- **Property Effect**: Particle strengthening from (V,Nb)X partially compensates the loss of M₂₃C₆ strengthening at high NTs, resulting in total particle strengthening of about 130 MPa after 1150 °C and 1200 °C NT.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not discussed.
- **Original Evidence**: "Opposite, formation of large M₂₃C₆ after NT higher than 1150 °C strongly decreased their contribution to particle strengthening, and high fraction of (V,Nb)X partially compensated the loss of strengthening due to M₂₃C₆ providing sum strengthening of about 130 MPa."

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: Cr, W, Mo, Co (matrix elements), Re.
- **Typical Alloys**: 10%Cr-2%W-0.8%Cu martensitic steel (present work).
- **Key Compositions**: Matrix contains 9.8 at.% Cr, 0.62% W, 0.35% Mo, 2.9% Co (EDX).
- **Microstructural Changes**: Tempering equalizes the matrix chemical composition regardless of normalizing temperature; matrix composition is the same across all NTs.
- **Property Improvements**: Solid solution strengthening contribution to 20 °C yield strength is 140 MPa for all NTs (no variation with NT).
- **Risks and Limitations**: Not discussed in this paper.

## Carbide and Carbonitride Precipitation Strengthening (Particle Strengthening)

- **Related Elements**: C, Cr, Nb, V, W, B.
- **Typical Alloys**: 10%Cr-2%W-0.8%Cu martensitic steel.
- **Key Compositions**: M₂₃C₆ carbides (57–127 nm); NbX carbonitrides (25–35 nm); (V,Nb)X carbonitrides (34–41 nm); W₂B borides.
- **Microstructural Changes**:
  - At 1050–1100 °C NT: M₂₃C₆ forms dense chains along HABs and LABs with orientation relationships (ORs) with martensitic laths; NbX is round.
  - At 1100 °C NT: M₂₃C₆ is refined by 20% (from 72 to 57 nm) and NbX by 31% (from 35 to 25 nm) compared to 1050 °C NT.
  - At 1150–1200 °C NT: M₂₃C₆ coarsens significantly (118–127 nm) and loses ORs with matrix; (V,Nb)X replaces NbX with 5× volume fraction.
- **Property Improvements**:
  - Particle strengthening contribution to 20 °C YS: 143 MPa (1050 °C), 181 MPa (1100 °C), ~130 MPa (1150–1200 °C).
  - The +10% improvement in YS and UTS at 1100 °C NT is attributed to +53 MPa increase in particle strengthening (from 143 to 181 MPa).
  - At 1150–1200 °C, (V,Nb)X partially compensates the loss of M₂₃C₆ strengthening.
- **Risks and Limitations**:
  - Coarsening of M₂₃C₆ at high NT (≥1150 °C) leads to loss of ORs and significant reduction in particle strengthening.
  - The paper notes that M₂₃C₆ with ORs helps stabilize the lath structure; loss of ORs is associated with creep strength breakdown in previous works.

## Grain Boundary Pinning (Zener Pinning)

- **Related Elements**: Nb, V (via MX carbonitrides), B (via W₂B), C, N.
- **Typical Alloys**: 10%Cr martensitic heat-resistant steel.
- **Key Compositions**: NbX carbonitrides at 0.00053 vol.% (1050–1100 °C); (V,Nb)X at 0.0025 vol.% (1150–1200 °C); W₂B decreasing with NT, fully dissolved at 1200 °C.
- **Microstructural Changes**:
  - At NTs ≤ 1100 °C, MX carbonitrides provide Zener pinning force that restricts PAG growth (PAG size 64–75 μm).
  - At NTs > 1110 °C, full dissolution of MX carbonitrides removes the Zener pinning force, causing PAG size to double (105–122 μm).
  - W₂B also dissolves progressively, with full dissolution at 1200 °C.
- **Property Improvements**: Controlled grain size via Zener pinning contributes to optimized microstructure for tensile and creep properties.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Loss of Zener pinning at high NTs leads to abnormal grain growth and potential loss of strengthening.

## Lath (Subgrain Boundary) Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: Not directly element-specific; depends on martensitic transformation.
- **Typical Alloys**: 10%Cr martensitic heat-resistant steel.
- **Key Compositions**: Mean lath width 320 ± 15 nm; lath area per unit volume 0.32–0.56 m⁻¹.
- **Microstructural Changes**: Tempering at 770 °C equalizes lath structure regardless of NT; lath width is similar across all NTs.
- **Property Improvements**: Lath strengthening contribution to 20 °C YS is 130 MPa for all NTs.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Not discussed in this paper.

## Dislocation Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: Not direct element-specific; affected by martensitic transformation and carbide pinning.
- **Typical Alloys**: 10%Cr martensitic heat-resistant steel.
- **Key Compositions**: Dislocation density inside laths: (2.1–2.8) × 10¹⁴ m⁻²; dislocation density in lath boundaries: (1.0–1.8) × 10¹⁴ m⁻².
- **Microstructural Changes**: Highest dislocation density in lath boundaries observed after 1100 °C NT (1.8 × 10¹⁴ m⁻²); tempering equalizes dislocation density regardless of NT.
- **Property Improvements**: Dislocation strengthening contribution to 20 °C YS is 145–160 MPa for all NTs.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Not discussed.

# Key Conclusions

1. An increase in normalizing temperature of the Re-containing 10%Cr steel to 1100 °C provides a +10% increase in both yield strength and ultimate tensile strength at 20 °C.
2. The improvement in tensile properties at 1100 °C is caused by improved particle strengthening due to refinement of M₂₃C₆ carbides (by 20%) and NbX carbonitrides (by 31%). This results in a +53 MPa increase in particle strengthening (from 143 MPa at 1050 °C to 181 MPa at 1100 °C).
3. Tempering at 770 °C equalizes strengthening contributions due to laths, dislocations, and alloying elements in the matrix after all normalizing temperatures. These contributions are: σ_disl = 145–160 MPa, σ_lath = 130 MPa, σ_ss = 140 MPa.
4. Further increase in normalizing temperature to 1150 °C and 1200 °C causes significant coarsening of M₂₃C₆ carbides (118–127 nm vs. 57–72 nm), loss of orientation relationships with the matrix, and formation of δ-ferrite (0.3% at 1150 °C, 4.8% at 1200 °C). These changes do not lead to positive effects on tensile properties.
5. At 650 °C, no increment in YS or UTS was observed regardless of normalizing temperature.
6. Short-term creep tests (200 and 180 MPa at 650 °C) after 1050 °C and 1100 °C NT showed similar rupture times. The paper suggests (but does not confirm) that increased particle strengthening due to refined M₂₃C₆ retaining ORs with laths and NbX may stabilize the tempered martensite lath structure for longer times during creep and overcome creep strength breakdown.
7. 1100 °C is identified as the optimal normalizing temperature for further creep investigations.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy System**: This paper studies a Fe-based 9-12% Cr martensitic steel, not a refractory high-temperature alloy (e.g., Nb-based, Mo-based, W-based alloys or refractory high-entropy alloys). The findings are specific to this steel class and should not be directly extrapolated to refractory alloy systems.
- **Temperature Range**: The service temperature for this steel is 600–620 °C, and testing was conducted at 20 °C and 650 °C. This is significantly lower than the typical operating temperatures of refractory alloys (1000 °C+).
- **No Oxidation Data**: Oxidation resistance is not discussed in this paper. Any conclusions about oxidation resistance for this alloy system cannot be drawn from this work.
- **Creep Data Limited**: Only short-term creep tests (rupture times at 200 and 180 MPa) are reported. Long-term creep behavior (>10,000 h) is referenced from previous works but not evaluated in this paper.
- **Strengthening Model**: The strengthening contribution calculations are based on models from the literature (references [10–15]), and the specific numerical values are derived from these models rather than direct experimental measurement of each contribution.
- **The paper notes** that the suggestion about improved long-term creep resistance at 1100 °C (due to particle refinement) is speculative and requires further creep testing to confirm.
- **Single Alloy Composition**: Only one alloy composition is studied (the Re-containing 10%Cr-2%W-0.8%Cu steel). The effects of individual alloying elements are inferred from previous works and are not isolated in this study.
- **Processing Route**: The alloy is produced by vacuum-induction melting with conventional normalizing and tempering heat treatment. This is not applicable to additive manufacturing or powder metallurgy routes for refractory alloys.
