# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Selective laser melting additive manufacturing of pure tungsten: Role of volumetric energy density on densification, microstructure and mechanical properties
- **Authors**: Meng Guo, Dongdong Gu, Lixia Xi, Hongmei Zhang, Jiayao Zhang, Jiankai Yang, Rui Wang
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals & Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2019
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2019.105025
- **Alloy system studied**: Pure tungsten (W)
- **Research objective**: To investigate the effects of volumetric energy density on densification behavior, microstructure evolution, and mechanical performance (compressive strength, hardness, wear resistance) of SLM-processed pure tungsten parts.
- **Material category**: Additively manufactured refractory metal (pure W, BCC)

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

This paper studies **pure tungsten** fabricated via selective laser melting (SLM), a powder-bed additive manufacturing technique. It is not an alloy study but a processing-parameter–microstructure–property relation study for a single-element BCC refractory metal. Tungsten is a refractory metal with a melting point of ~3420 °C, high thermal conductivity, high hardness, and high DBTT (ductile-to-brittle transition temperature, typically 200–400 °C).

The central variable is **volumetric energy density (E, J/mm³)**, defined as:

\[
E = \frac{P}{v \cdot h \cdot d}
\]

where \( P \) is laser power, \( v \) is scanning speed, \( h \) is hatch spacing (50 μm), and \( d \) is layer thickness (20 μm).

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## SLM equipment and parameters

- **Machine**: Self-developed SLM apparatus; YLR-500 Ytterbium fiber laser, maximum power 500 W, spot size 70 μm.
- **Atmosphere**: High-purity Ar; oxygen content kept below 100 ppm during processing.
- **Substrate**: Stainless steel; preheated to 200 °C.
- **Scanning strategy**: Chessboard strategy with adjacent layers rotated 67°.
- **Layer thickness (d)**: 20 μm (constant).
- **Hatch spacing (h)**: 50 μm (constant).
- **Laser power (P)**: 200–350 W.
- **Scanning speed (v)**: 200–400 mm/s.
- **Volumetric energy densities tested**: 500, 667, 833, 1000, and 1167 J/mm³.
- **Printing duration**: ~12 h.
- **Specimen geometries**: cubic samples 10 mm × 10 mm × 5 mm; cylindrical compression specimens 10 mm length, 8 mm diameter (L/D = 1.25, per GB/T 7314-2017).
- **Post-processing**: No post-SLM heat treatment or HIP is reported. Samples were cut from substrate by WEDM.
- **No arc melting, vacuum melting, induction melting, mechanical alloying, hot pressing, SPS, rolling, forging, annealing, or aging was used in this study.** All samples are as-built SLM material.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Phase constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Pure BCC W (JCPDS #04-0806). No secondary phases (no oxides, no carbides, no Laves, no B2) detected by XRD.
- No second-phase precipitates including ZrC, HfC, TiC, NbC, TaC, HfO₂, ZrO₂, Y₂O₃, La₂O₃, borides or silicides are present.

## Grain structure

- OM images show microstructures composed of **equiaxed columnar crystals**.
- Microcracks initiated at grain boundaries and propagated along them (Fig. 6d).
- Grain size values are **not explicitly quantified** in the paper.
- No elemental segregation is reported (single-element system).
- No precipitate size/distribution (no precipitates exist).
- No direct discussion of solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, ODS strengthening, or grain-boundary strengthening as intentional mechanisms. Densitification and residual stress control are the dominant microstructure-quality factors.

## Residual stress observations

- XRD peak shifts indicated residual stress evolution with energy density:
  - From 667 to 833 J/mm³, 2θ shifted to higher angles (increased residual stress).
  - From 833 to 1000 J/mm³, 2θ shifted to lower angles (stress relief attributed to thermal accumulation/annealing effect of successive layer melting).

## Defects/Porosity/Cracks

- At 500 J/mm³: mass of large pores with regular shapes; reticular microcracks; density 16.95 g/cm³ (87.8% theoretical).
- At 667 J/mm³: fewer large pores; some small regular pores; density 17.2 g/cm³ (89.1% theoretical).
- At 833 J/mm³: large pores almost disappeared; small pores remained; microcracks obvious.
- At 1000 J/mm³: pores completely disappeared; only microcracks remained; density 19.0 g/cm³ (98.4% theoretical).
- At 1167 J/mm³: small pores and microcracks reappeared; density 18.82 g/cm³ (97.5% theoretical).
- **Microcracks were found to be inevitable** in SLM-processed pure W across all energy densities, primarily thermal cracks caused by shrinkage and the high DBTT of W (200–400 °C).

- Theoretical density of W: 19.3 g/cm³ (98.4% corresponds to 19.0/19.3).

## Microhardness (Vickers, 0.3 kg load, 15 s dwell)

- **Peak**: 474 HV at 1000 J/mm³.
- Hardness decreased at both lower and higher energy densities due to densification collapse and thermal accumulation/coarsening, respectively.

## Compressive properties (room temperature, CMT5205, L/D = 1.25 per GB/T 7314-2017)

- **Ultimate compressive strength (UCS)**:
  - 902 MPa at 1000 J/mm³ (highest).
  - Increased with energy density from 500 to 1000 J/mm³, then decreased at 1167 J/mm³.
- **Fracture mode**: brittle fracture with less plastic deformation; intergranular fracture dominant; cleavage steps and facets observed.
- **No tensile data, no high-temperature strength data (800 °C, 1000 °C, 1200 °C, 1400 °C, 1600 °C), no elongation, no fracture toughness, no creep, no rupture life, no elastic modulus, no strain-rate-dependent properties are reported.**
- Comparison with conventional manufacturing (Table 2):
  - SLM (this work): 474 HV, 902 MPa UCS
  - CVD: 419 HV, 780 MPa
  - PM: 445 HV, 1000–1200 MPa
  - SPS: 302 HV, 980 MPa

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

This is a single-element study, so the element-function framework is limited. No alloying elements (Mo, Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf, Ti, V, Cr, Al, Si, Re, C, B, Y, La, O-dispersoids) are intentionally added. The only elements present in the system are W (base) and trace impurities (O, C, S).

## [W (Tungsten, base element)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Pure W, additively manufactured refractory metal; also relevant to W-based alloys for nuclear fusion, lighting, military, medical, electronics.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Balance of the material; 99.99 wt% purity class based on impurity levels (O 0.0016 wt%, C 0.0063 wt%, S 0.0022 wt%).
- **Main Function**: Base refractory metal providing high melting point (~3420 °C), high thermal conductivity, high hardness, and high compressive strength; serves as BCC matrix for SLM processing.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: BCC crystal structure; rapid solidification during SLM produces equiaxed columnar grains; high surface tension of liquid W and high viscosity affect wetting/spreading and balling; high DBTT (200–400 °C) and thermal shrinkage during fast cooling promote intergranular microcracking.
- **Property Effect**:
  - Vickers microhardness: 474 HV at optimal E = 1000 J/mm³.
  - Room-temperature ultimate compressive strength: 902 MPa at optimal E = 1000 J/mm³.
  - Density: 19.0 g/cm³ (98.4% theoretical) at optimal E = 1000 J/mm³.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Extremely high melting point makes full melting and consolidation difficult; high thermal conductivity accelerates heat extraction, causing thermal shrinkage stress and cracking; high DBTT (200–400 °C) makes material sensitive to brittle intergranular fracture at room temperature; balling tendency due to high liquid viscosity, surface tension, and short solidification time (46 μs) relative to droplet spreading time (86.3 μs).
- **Original Evidence**: “Tungsten (W) is a very promising material... such as high melting point (3420 °C), high thermal conductivity, high tensile strength and high hardness” (Section 1); “The microcracks initiated at the grain boundaries and propagated along the grain boundaries” (Section 3.3); “for tungsten, it is reported that the droplets spreading time is 86.3 μs whereas its solidification time is only 46 μs” (Section 3.2); Figs. 4–9; Tables 1–2.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

This paper does not study solid-solution strengthening, carbide/boride precipitation strengthening, oxide dispersion strengthening, grain-boundary strengthening in the sense of fine-grain Hall-Petch enhancement, or second-phase strengthening. The dominant mechanism governing mechanical properties is **densification and defect control**.

## [Densification-Dominated Mechanical Property Control (near-full density processing)]

- **Related Elements**: W (base only); no alloying elements used.
- **Typical Alloys**: Pure W, SLM-processed refractory metals without secondary phases.
- **Key Compositions**: Pure W with O ≤ 0.0016 wt%, C 0.0063 wt%, S 0.0022 wt%.
- **Microstructural Changes**: With increasing energy density from 500 to 1000 J/mm³, porosity decreased from large regular pores to complete elimination; balling was suppressed; microcracks from thermal shrinkage remained inevitable. At 1167 J/mm³, porosity increased again due to Marangoni-driven gas entrapment and melt instability/evaporation.
- **Property Improvements**:
  - Density: 16.95 → 19.0 g/cm³ (500 → 1000 J/mm³)
  - Microhardness: peak 474 HV at 1000 J/mm³
  - Room-temperature compressive strength: peak 902 MPa at 1000 J/mm³
  - COF: 0.95 (500 J/mm³) → 0.45 (1000 J/mm³)
  - Wear rate: 12 × 10⁻⁵ (500 J/mm³) → 1.3 × 10⁻⁵ mm³·N⁻¹·m⁻¹ (1000 J/mm³)
- **Risks and Limitations**: Microcracks remain inevitable in SLM-processed pure W; too high energy density (>1000 J/mm³) leads to grain coarsening, thermal accumulation, gas entrapment via intense Marangoni flow, and density reduction; high DBTT causes brittle intergranular fracture; no ductility data available.
- **Original Evidence**: Sections 3.1–3.4, Figs. 3–11. The authors state: “the maximum obtained density and relative density were 19.0 g/cm³ and 98.4%, respectively” (Section 3.2); “the ultimate compressive strength reached 902 MPa at the energy density of 1000 J/mm³, which was the highest among all samples” (Section 3.4).

# Key Conclusions

1. **Optimal energy density**: A volumetric energy density of 1000 J/mm³ (P = 300 W, v = 300 mm/s by inference) yielded a nearly fully dense pure W part: density 19.0 g/cm³, 98.4% of theoretical.

2. **Densification behavior**: Density increases with energy density up to 1000 J/mm³ due to complete powder melting and reduced balling; above this, density decreases due to intense Marangoni-driven gas migration, melt instability, and element evaporation/burning.

3. **Microcracks are inevitable**: Microcracks from thermal shrinkage and high DBTT were present at all energy densities; however, they had negligible effect on compressive strength and hardness compared to conventional methods.

4. **Mechanical performance**:
   - Peak microhardness: 474 HV.
   - Ultimate compressive strength at room temperature: 902 MPa.
   - Fracture was brittle, predominantly intergranular, with cleavage steps and facets.

5. **Wear performance**:
   - Optimal COF 0.45 and wear rate 1.3 × 10⁻⁵ mm³·N⁻¹·m⁻¹ at 1000 J/mm³.
   - Wear mechanism transitioned from exfoliative (667 J/mm³) → abrasive (833 J/mm³) → adhesion (1000 J/mm³).

6. **SLM viability for pure W**: SLM is a promising method for refractory metals; mechanical properties are comparable to or better than CVD and SPS, approaching PM values.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Not an alloy study**: The paper does not provide data on W-based alloys, Mo-W alloys, Nb-W alloys, or refractory high-entropy alloys. No alloying-element-function information beyond pure W is available.
- **No high-temperature mechanical data**: No tensile, creep, rupture, fracture toughness, or high-temperature (800–1600 °C) strength values are reported.
- **No oxidation-resistance data**: Oxidation weight gain, oxide layer composition, oxidation kinetics, and oxidation-induced embrittlement are not studied.
- **No direct ductility measurement**: Compressive plasticity/elongation values are not quantified; fracture is described qualitatively as brittle.
- **No heat treatment or HIP**: All data correspond to as-built SLM material; no post-process annealing, HIP, or other thermal treatments.
- **Grain size not quantified**: Although equiaxed columnar crystals are mentioned, no specific grain size (e.g., μm-scale values) is provided.
- **Microcrack mitigation not resolved**: The study acknowledges microcracks as inevitable but does not propose additive alloying, substrate temperature, scanning strategy, or composition modification to fully suppress them.
- **Single-parameter sweep**: Only volumetric energy density is varied; hatch spacing (50 μm) and layer thickness (20 μm) are held constant; scanning speed and power are not independently decoupled.
- **Applicability**: Results are most directly applicable to additive manufacturing of pure W and, cautiously, to refractory powder-bed AM parameter optimization studies where densification is the dominant mechanism. The findings are not designed to describe solid-solution, precipitation, or dispersion strengthening effects in multi-element refractory alloys.
