# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** Microstructure and mechanical properties of molybdenum-titanium-zirconium-carbon alloy TZM processed via laser powder-bed fusion
- **Authors:** L. Kaserer, J. Braun, J. Stajkovic, K.-H. Leitz, P. Singer, I. Letofsky-Papst, H. Kestler, G. Leichtfried
- **Journal:** International Journal of Refractory Metals & Hard Materials
- **Year:** 2020
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2020.105369
- **Alloy system studied:** Mo-Ti-Zr-C alloy (TZM)
- **Material category:** Additively manufactured refractory alloy; Mo-based alloy (TZM) processed via LPBF
- **Research objective:** To investigate for the first time the microstructure and mechanical properties of TZM processed via laser powder-bed fusion (LPBF), with focus on the role of Ti, Zr, and C alloying additions in suppressing oxygen-induced hot cracking.

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies the molybdenum alloy TZM processed by laser powder-bed fusion. TZM is a Mo-based alloy containing nominally 0.5 wt% Ti, 0.08 wt% Zr, and 0.01–0.04 wt% C. The study addresses the problem that pure Mo processed by LPBF is susceptible to hot cracking due to oxygen impurity segregation to grain boundaries and formation of MoO₂. The research object is to understand how Ti, Zr, and C additions influence crack suppression, precipitate formation, microstructure evolution, and resulting mechanical properties in the LPBF process.

# Alloy Composition

**TZM powder composition (pre-alloyed, gas-atomized):**
- Ti: 5045 ± 29 μg/g (≈0.5045 wt%)
- Zr: 861 ± 23 μg/g (≈0.0861 wt%)
- C: 257 ± 2 μg/g (≈0.0257 wt%)
- O: 388 ± 36 μg/g
- N: < 5 μg/g
- H: < 1 μg/g
- Metallic purity: > 99.9%
- Powder particle size: D10 = 9.1 μm, D50 = 20.9 μm, D90 = 42.3 μm
- Bulk density: 5.84 ± 0.12 g/cm³
- Tap density: 6.45 ± 0.09 g/cm³

**LPBF sample composition after processing:**
- Ti: 4650 ± 19 μg/g (8% reduction from powder)
- Zr: 885 ± 10 μg/g (essentially unchanged)
- C: 163 ± 29 μg/g (37% reduction from powder)
- O: 168 ± 16 μg/g (57% reduction from powder)
- N: < 5 μg/g
- H: < 1 μg/g

The Zr:O atomic ratio in the LPBF sample is calculated as 1:1.08, meaning Zr is present super-stoichiometrically relative to oxygen.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Manufacturing method:** Laser powder-bed fusion (LPBF) using a customized laboratory-scale machine equipped with a 400 W, 1064 nm Nd:YAG fiber laser with substrate plate heating.

**Process parameters:**
- Linear energy density (LED): 0.58 J/mm (LED = P/v, where P is laser power and v is scanning speed)
- Substrate plate temperature: 800 °C during the building process
- Oxygen level in building chamber: < 20 ppmv
- Shielding gas: Argon
- Layer height: 0.03 mm
- Hatch distance: 0.1 mm
- Scanning pattern: zig-zag island scanning, 67° layer rotation, 0.5 mm layer shift
- Substrate plate: pure molybdenum
- Cooldown: controlled at rate of 100 K/hour after build
- Overall build time: approximately 14 h including cooldown
- Sample separation: wire-cut electrical discharge machining (EDM)

**Heat treatment after LPBF:** No post-build stress-relief annealing or heat treatment is explicitly described. The controlled cooling at 100 K/h serves as a slow cooldown from the 800 °C substrate temperature. The paper states all samples showed brittle fracture without any ductility, and the as-built condition was tested.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Porosity and density:**
- Measured density: 10.13 ± 0.03 g/cm³
- Theoretical density of TZM: 10.16 g/cm³
- Relative density: 99.7 ± 0.3%
- SEM images show only a few spherical pores < 50 μm in diameter

**Grain structure:**
- Columnar grains elongated toward the build direction due to epitaxial grain growth
- Mean aspect ratio: 0.4 ± 0.17
- Area-weighted average grain size: 70 ± 45 μm (side view), 51 ± 31 μm (top view)
- Number-weighted average grain size: 11 ± 14 μm (side view), 13 ± 17 μm (top view)
- High number of small grains < 10 μm
- Grain boundary distribution: 61% high-angle grain boundaries, 18% low-angle boundaries 5°–15°, 21% between 2° and 5°
- Weak 〈111〉 fiber texture parallel to the build direction

**Precipitates:**
- Mo₂C spherical precipitates in grain interior, size < 50 nm
- ZrO₂ spherical precipitates in grain interior, size < 50 nm, identified by Zr- and O-rich energy-dispersive X-ray signal with absence of C
- Ternary molybdenum-titanium carbide (Mo,Ti)ₓCᵧ precipitates, non-spherical/elongated, containing Mo, C, Ti, and O; evidence for oxygen solubility in ternary Mo-Ti carbide found for the first time
- No precipitates found at grain boundaries
- No MoO₂ or TiO₂ observed at grain boundaries
- No titanium-containing precipitates except in complex carbides

**Dislocation structure:**
- Low-angle grain boundaries and numerous dislocations observed via TEM HAADF imaging, comparable to LPBF-Mo

# Mechanical Properties

**Three-point bending test (ambient temperature, DIN EN ISO 3327, N = 7 samples, load applied in build direction):**
- Mean bending strength (excluding samples 1 and 4 with defects): 591 ± 26 MPa
- Samples 1 and 4 with lack-of-fusion porosity ~400 μm: bending strength ~250 MPa
- Comparison: pure Mo LPBF samples with pores and cracks showed bending strength 267 ± 50 MPa
- Comparison: PM-TZM sheets in recrystallized state: 1150 MPa (Plansee internal database), LPBF-TZM ~50% lower

**Hardness:**
- Vickers hardness: 264 ± 19 HV10 (measured with 98.0665 N load)

**Fracture behavior:**
- All samples showed brittle fracture without any ductility
- Fracture mode: transgranular brittle cleavage with river patterns
- No intergranular portions observed on fracture surfaces
- Samples with bending strength ~550–630 MPa: spherical pores < 50 μm on fracture surface
- Samples with bending strength < 250 MPa: powder-filled lack-of-fusion porosity ~400 μm diameter
- Critical defect size for PM-TZM calculated using a_c = (1/π)(K_IC/Yσ)² with K_IC = 12.9 MPa·m¹ᐟ² for forged/recrystallized TZM, σ = 800 MPa, Y = 1, giving ~80 μm
- Critical defect size for LPBF-TZM expected to be lower due to higher dislocation density, lower dislocation mobility, and high inner stresses

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

The paper does not explicitly discuss high-temperature oxidation resistance in terms of oxidation weight gain, oxide scale formation, or oxidation kinetics. The relevant oxygen-related discussion concerns:

- Oxygen impurity in the feedstock (388 ± 36 μg/g in powder, reduced to 168 ± 16 μg/g in LPBF sample)
- Avoidance of MoO₂ formation at grain boundaries through ZrO₂ and ternary Mo-Ti carbide oxygen binding
- Carbothermic reduction: MoO₂ + 2 C → Mo + 2 CO, with CO outgassing accounting for reduced C and O contents
- The suppression of oxygen segregation to grain boundaries prevents hot cracking and produces purified grain boundaries

No data is provided for high-temperature oxidation testing, oxide layer formation, or long-term service stability at elevated temperature.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

---

**[Element] Zirconium (Zr)**

**[Applicable Alloy System]** Mo-based TZM alloy processed by LPBF and by welding/PM

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]** 861 ± 23 μg/g (0.086 wt%) in powder; 885 ± 10 μg/g (0.0885 wt%) in LPBF sample

**[Main Function]** Suppresses oxygen-induced hot cracking in LPBF-processed Mo-based alloys by binding oxygen as ZrO₂, thereby preventing MoO₂ formation at grain boundaries; contributes to precipitation strengthening through in-situ ZrO₂ particle formation

**[Microstructural Mechanism]** Zr has the lowest Gibbs free energy (ΔG) for oxide formation among Mo, Ti, and Zr, so it reacts preferentially with oxygen to form ZrO₂. Because ZrO₂ has a higher melting point than Mo, ZrO₂ particles solidify first and are distributed within the grain interior rather than at grain boundaries. In the LPBF sample, the Zr:O atomic ratio of 1:1.08 means Zr is present super-stoichiometrically relative to oxygen; a Zr content of 885 ± 10 μg/g is sufficient to bind all 168 ± 16 μg/g of oxygen as ZrO₂, leaving no unbound oxygen to form MoO₂ or TiO₂ at grain boundaries.

**[Property Effect]** Enables crack-free LPBF processing of TZM at 99.7 ± 0.3% relative density. The fracture mode is transgranular rather than intergranular, indicating purified grain boundaries. The original paper does not provide specific quantitative values for Zr's individual contribution to strength.

**[Potential Side Effects]** ZrO₂ particles remain as second-phase inclusions that can act as porosity nucleation sites if not fully outgassed; excess Zr beyond oxygen stoichiometry could form ZrC or other phases, though this is not discussed in detail in the original paper.

**[Original Evidence]** "Zirconium is the strongest oxide former in this alloy and therefore reacts with the oxygen forming ZrO₂ particles" (State of the art, p. 1). "Precipitates that are rich in zirconium and oxygen and that lack the carbon signal may be detected (see Fig. 5a)" (Section 3.2). "The atomic ratio of zirconium to oxygen content in the sample is 1:1.08. This means that zirconium is present super-stoichiometrically in relation to oxygen... No unbound oxygen would remain, and the formation of MoO₂ and TiO₂, which both have lower melting points than that of molybdenum and which would therefore segregate to high-angle grain boundaries, would be prevented."

**[Element] Carbon (C)**

**[Applicable Alloy System]** Mo-based TZM alloy processed by LPBF; also relevant for Mo-0.45 wt% C LPBF alloy

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]** 257 ± 2 μg/g (0.0257 wt%) in powder; 163 ± 29 μg/g (0.0163 wt%) in LPBF sample (37% reduction during LPBF)

**[Main Function]** Forms Mo₂C and ternary (Mo,Ti)ₓCᵧ precipitates; contributes to carbothermic reduction of oxygen (MoO₂ + 2C → Mo + 2CO) during melting, reducing both C and O contents in the final part

**[Microstructural Mechanism]** Mo₂C precipitates form within grain interiors (size < 50 nm, spherical). Ternary molybdenum-titanium carbides form as non-spherical precipitates that can dissolve oxygen, providing an additional mechanism for oxygen binding and grain-boundary purification. No precipitates are found at grain boundaries, in contrast to PM-TZM where carbides are located at grain and sub-grain boundaries.

**[Property Effect]** Enables crack-free processing through oxygen removal; contributes to precipitate strengthening. Specific quantitative strengthening contribution from carbides is not provided in the original paper.

**[Potential Side Effects]** Carbon loss during LPBF (37% reduction) means final carbon content deviates from nominal composition; carbothermic reduction produces CO, which must outgas—if trapped, it could contribute to pore formation.

**[Original Evidence]** "The carbothermic reduction MoO₂ + 2 C → Mo + 2 CO and the subsequent outgassing of CO may account for the reduced carbon and oxygen contents when comparing the LPBF sample with the powder" (Section 3.1). "Ternary molybdenum‑titanium carbides (Mo,Ti)xCy [2,3]... were previously reported in PM-TZM, but this is the first time that evidence for oxygen solubility in a ternary molybdenum‑titanium carbide has been found. The ability to dissolve oxygen in ternary molybdenum‑titanium carbide is a mechanism that assists in suppressing the segregation of oxygen and thus probably purifies the grain boundaries not only in LPBF-TZM but also in conventional PM-TZM" (Section 3.2).

**[Element] Titanium (Ti)**

**[Applicable Alloy System]** Mo-based TZM alloy processed by LPBF and PM

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]** 5045 ± 29 μg/g (0.5045 wt%) in powder; 4650 ± 19 μg/g (0.465 wt%) in LPBF sample (8% reduction)

**[Main Function]** Solid-solution strengthening of the Mo matrix; formation of ternary (Mo,Ti)ₓCᵧ carbides; contributes to oxygen binding through ternary carbide formation

**[Microstructural Mechanism]** Most Ti is dissolved in the Mo matrix, providing solid-solution hardening—the paper states "similar to PM-TZM [2], titanium in LPBF-TZM is almost exclusively dissolved in the molybdenum solid, its strength increasing through solid solution hardening." Some Ti participates in ternary molybdenum-titanium carbide precipitates, which can dissolve oxygen. No separate TiC precipitates were found in LPBF-TZM; no titanium-containing precipitates were found except in complex carbides.

**[Property Effect]** Contributes to overall alloy strength through solid-solution hardening. Specific quantitative strengthening contribution from Ti is not provided in the original paper.

**[Potential Side Effects]** Ti and titanium oxides (TiO, TiO₂) have lower boiling points (3287 °C, 3227 °C, 2970 °C) than Mo (4639 °C); these temperatures are "significantly exceeded locally in the melt pool," leading to Ti outgassing and 8% Ti loss during LPBF. Outgassing of titanium and titanium oxides may also contribute to pore formation.

**[Original Evidence]** "The remaining titanium forms ternary molybdenum‑titanium carbides [2–4] and titanium carbide precipitates [3–5]" (State of the art, p. 1). "Similar to PM-TZM [2], titanium in LPBF-TZM is almost exclusively dissolved in the molybdenum solid, its strength increasing through solid solution hardening" (Section 3.2). "The loss of titanium indicates the outgassing of titanium and titanium oxides, which—at 3287 °C (Ti), 3227 °C (TiO), and 2970 °C (TiO₂)—have lower boiling points than molybdenum (4639 °C)" (Section 3.1).

**[Element Combination] Ti + Zr + C (as a combined system in TZM)**

**[Applicable Alloy System]** Mo-based TZM alloy; specifically for LPBF processing where this combination enables crack-free fabrication

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]** Ti: 0.465–0.505 wt%, Zr: 0.086–0.089 wt%, C: 0.016–0.026 wt%

**[Main Function]** The combined Ti-Zr-C addition system prevents hot cracking in LPBF Mo; Zr binds O as ZrO₂, C enables carbothermic reduction of MoO₂ to remove oxygen as CO gas, Ti provides solid-solution strengthening and forms oxygen-dissolving ternary carbides. The synergistic effect purifies grain boundaries and prevents MoO₂ formation.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]** Three distinct mechanisms operate: (1) Zr reacts preferentially with oxygen to form ZrO₂ particles in grain interiors; (2) carbothermic reduction converts MoO₂ to Mo while releasing CO gas that outgasses; (3) Ti forms ternary (Mo,Ti)ₓCᵧ carbides that can dissolve oxygen. Together these mechanisms prevent oxygen segregation to grain boundaries during solidification, eliminating the MoO₂ liquid film that causes hot cracking in pure Mo LPBF.

**[Property Effect]** Crack-free samples with 99.7 ± 0.3% relative density were successfully produced. Bending strength of 591 ± 26 MPa for samples with only < 50 μm pores, significantly higher than cracked pure Mo LPBF at 267 ± 50 MPa.

**[Potential Side Effects]** O, Ti, and C loss during processing changes nominal composition; volatile species that do not outgas may produce spherical pores; final alloy still shows brittle transgranular fracture without ductility.

**[Original Evidence]** "The alloying elements suppress the segregation of oxygen to the grain boundaries so that crack-free samples with a density of 99.7 ± 0.3% may be produced" (Abstract). "Oxygen is not only bound by ZrO₂. Research has shown that the ternary molybdenum‑titanium carbide, which can be found in LPBF–TZM–as is the case with pressed/sintered TZM—can dissolve oxygen" (Abstract).

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

---

**[Mechanism] Solid-Solution Strengthening**

**[Related Elements]** Ti (dissolved in Mo matrix)

**[Typical Alloys]** TZM, PM-TZM, LPBF-TZM

**[Key Compositions]** 0.465–0.505 wt% Ti in Mo matrix

**[Microstructural Changes]** Ti atoms substitute into the BCC Mo lattice, creating lattice distortion and elastic strain fields that impede dislocation motion. In LPBF-TZM, almost all Ti is dissolved in the Mo solid solution, similar to PM-TZM. No separate TiC precipitates were found.

**[Property Improvements]** Increases alloy strength compared to pure Mo. The paper states Ti contributes to strength through solid-solution hardening but does not provide specific quantitative contribution values for the LPBF material. The paper references that PM-TZM achieves higher strength than pure Mo through this mechanism.

**[Risks and Limitations]** Ti loss through evaporation during LPBF (8% reduction) reduces the achievable solid-solution strengthening level. Excess Ti could form TiO₂ at grain boundaries if oxygen is not fully bound by Zr.

**[Original Evidence]** "Similar to PM-TZM [2], titanium in LPBF-TZM is almost exclusively dissolved in the molybdenum solid, its strength increasing through solid solution hardening" (Section 3.2).

---

**[Mechanism] Carbide Precipitation Strengthening (and Secondary Oxygen Dissolution)**

**[Related Elements]** Mo, C, Ti (forming Mo₂C and ternary (Mo,Ti)ₓCᵧ)

**[Typical Alloys]** TZM, Mo-0.45 wt% C LPBF alloy, PM-TZM

**[Key Compositions]** C: 0.016–0.026 wt%; additional Mo and Ti

**[Microstructural Changes]** Mo₂C spherical precipitates (< 50 nm) form in grain interiors; ternary Mo-Ti carbide precipitates of non-spherical morphology also form. No precipitates are located at grain boundaries in LPBF-TZM, in contrast to PM-TZM where precipitates are at grain and sub-grain boundaries. The ternary carbide shows a novel oxygen dissolution capacity.

**[Property Improvements]** Carbide precipitates contribute to strengthening. In conventional PM-TZM, carbides at grain boundaries retard grain and sub-grain migration, increasing recrystallization temperature and creep resistance. Specific quantitative values for the strengthening effect in LPBF-TZM are not provided.

**[Risks and Limitations]** Carbothermic reduction consumes C during processing (37% reduction), reducing the amount available for carbide formation. If CO outgassing is incomplete, it may contribute to porosity.

**[Original Evidence]** "Mo₂C and ZrO₂ particles with a size of < 50 nm are precipitated in the grain interior" (Abstract). "Ternary non-spherical oxygen enriched carbide was found... this is the first time that evidence for oxygen solubility in a ternary molybdenum‑titanium carbide has been found" (Section 3.2).

---

**[Mechanism] Oxide-Based Grain-Boundary Purification (Crack Suppression Mechanism)**

**[Related Elements]** Zr, O (forming ZrO₂); also involves C in carbothermic reduction

**[Typical Alloys]** LPBF-TZM, welded TZM

**[Key Compositions]** Zr: 0.086–0.089 wt%; O: 0.017–0.039 wt% in final part

**[Microstructural Changes]** ZrO₂ particles form in grain interiors (not at grain boundaries) due to high melting point causing early solidification. Grain boundaries are free of MoO₂ and TiO₂, and free of all precipitates. The Zr:O atomic ratio of 1:1.08 indicates stoichiometric excess of Zr sufficient to bind all oxygen as ZrO₂. Low-angle grain boundaries and dislocations are observed, comparable to LPBF-Mo, but thermal stresses are lower due to substrate plate heating.

**[Property Improvements]** Enables crack-free LPBF processing of Mo-based TZM at 99.7 ± 0.3% relative density. The fracture mode is fully transgranular (no intergranular portions observed), confirming effective grain-boundary purification. Crack-free samples significantly outperform cracked pure Mo LPBF (bending strength 591 MPa vs. 267 MPa for cracked material).

**[Risks and Limitations]** Residual ZrO₂ and other particles may act as pore nucleation sites; brittle fracture without ductility persists in the as-built state.

**[Original Evidence]** "The alloying elements suppress the segregation of oxygen to the grain boundaries so that crack-free samples with a density of 99.7 ± 0.3% may be produced" (Abstract). "The river patterns shown in Fig. 8 indicate a transgranular brittle cleavage fracture... the fracture surfaces do not show any intergranular portions. This indicates that the samples do not show weakened grain boundaries as they have been effectively purified from segregated oxygen" (Section 3.3).

---

**[Mechanism] Grain-Boundary Strengthening via Purification**

**[Related Elements]** Not element-specific; achieved through oxygen removal from grain boundaries

**[Typical Alloys]** LPBF-TZM, contrasted with pure Mo LPBF

**[Key Compositions]** Final O content: 168 ± 16 μg/g (reduced from 388 ± 36 μg/g in powder)

**[Microstructural Changes]** Grain boundaries are free of precipitates and oxygen segregation. In contrast, pure Mo LPBF shows oxygen segregation to grain boundaries with formation of MoO₂, leading to hot cracking along weakened grain boundaries. LPBF-TZM shows 61% high-angle grain boundaries, 18% low-angle boundaries 5°–15°, and 21% between 2° and 5°.

**[Property Improvements]** Transgranular rather than intergranular fracture; increased bending strength compared to cracked pure Mo; reduced susceptibility to hot cracking. The proportion of low-angle grain boundaries is reduced compared to pure Mo LPBF without substrate plate heating due to lower thermal stresses.

**[Risks and Limitations]** The transgranular brittle fracture mode (without ductility) still limits practical applications requiring toughness.

**[Original Evidence]** "The fracture mode is transgranular" (Abstract). "Compared to [20] where molybdenum was processed via LPBF without substrate plate heating, the proportion of low angle grain boundaries in LPBF TZM measured here is smaller due to lower thermal stresses" (Section 3.1).

# Key Conclusions

1. TZM was successfully produced via LPBF with a density of 99.7 ± 0.3% of theoretical density and a crack-free microstructure, in contrast to pure Mo which is susceptible to hot cracking.

2. The Ti-Zr-C alloying system suppresses oxygen segregation to grain boundaries through three mechanisms: (a) Zr binds O as ZrO₂; (b) carbothermic reduction removes oxygen as CO; (c) ternary Mo-Ti carbides dissolve oxygen. This results in grain boundaries free of MoO₂ and effective purification.

3. The as-built LPBF-TZM microstructure shows columnar grains elongated along the build direction (aspect ratio 0.4 ± 0.17), a weak 〈111〉 fiber texture parallel to the build direction, and grain sizes comparable to or finer than electron-beam-welded TZM.

4. Mo₂C, ZrO₂, and ternary Mo-Ti carbide precipitates (< 50 nm) are all located in the grain interior; no precipitates are found on grain boundaries.

5. Mechanical properties are limited by residual porosity: samples with only spherical pores < 50 μm achieve a bending strength of 591 ± 26 MPa, while samples with 400 μm lack-of-fusion porosity drop to ~250 MPa. Fracture mode is transgranular brittle cleavage in all cases.

6. The strength of LPBF-TZM in the as-built condition (~591 MPa) is approximately 50% lower than recrystallized PM-TZM sheets (1150 MPa), attributed to residual porosity, high dislocation density, and high internal stresses.

7. Residual porosity in LPBF-TZM is typically within the same size range as the calculated critical defect size (~80 μm for PM-TZM), indicating defect-limited mechanical behavior.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations explicitly acknowledged in the paper:**
- All mechanical testing was conducted at ambient temperature only; no elevated-temperature mechanical properties were measured for the LPBF-TZM material.
- Fracture toughness was not directly measured for LPBF-TZM; the critical defect size calculation relies on literature values for PM-TZM (K_IC = 12.9 MPa·m¹ᐟ²), which may not accurately represent the LPBF material.
- The paper does not report tensile properties (yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, elongation) due to the brittle nature of the material; three-point bending and hardness are the only mechanical characterization methods used.
- No systematic optimization study of process parameters is presented; the results are for a single optimized parameter set (LED = 0.58 J/mm, 800 °C substrate temperature).
- Ductility is not achieved in the as-built condition; all samples fracture in a brittle manner, which limits immediate engineering applicability without post-processing or further alloy development.
- The paper does not investigate the effect of post-build heat treatment on microstructure or mechanical properties.

**Applicable scope:**
- The findings are directly relevant to LPBF additive manufacturing of Mo-based alloys, particularly for near-net-shape fabrication of components that cannot be produced through conventional powder metallurgy.
- The crack-suppression mechanism (Zr-O binding, carbothermic reduction, ternary carbide oxygen dissolution) is relevant to welded Mo alloys and may inform alloy design for other refractory metal LPBF processes, such as W-based systems.
- The quantitative data relate to the specific TZM composition (approximately Mo-0.5Ti-0.08Zr-0.02C in wt%) processed with the specified LPBF parameters and powder characteristics. Extrapolation to significantly different compositions, parameter sets, or powder morphologies should be done with caution.
- The study demonstrates feasibility but does not establish a complete processing window or provide design allowables for load-bearing applications.
