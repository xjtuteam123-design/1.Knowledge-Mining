# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Comparing the hot corrosion of (100), (210) and (110) Ni-based superalloys exposed to the mixed salt of Na₂SO₄-NaCl at 750 °C: Experimental study and first-principles calculation

- **Alloy system studied:** Ni-based single-crystal (SC) superalloy DD10
- **Research objective:** To investigate the effect of crystal orientation—(100), (210), and (110)—on the hot corrosion behavior of Ni-based SC superalloys exposed to a mixed salt of 90 wt% Na₂SO₄ + 10 wt% NaCl at 750 °C for 200 h, and to explain the orientation dependence using first-principles calculations of atomic diffusion.
- **Material category:** Ni-based single-crystal superalloy (not a refractory alloy system).

**Note on scope:** This paper does **not** study Nb-W, Mo-W, Nb-W-Mo-Zr, Mo-based, W-based, refractory high-entropy alloys, ODS refractory alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys.   - Degreased ultrasonically in acetone, then dried in hot air.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

This paper focuses on **hot corrosion** in a mixed Na₂SO₄-NaCl environment, not pure oxidation. The following information is extracted:

## Hot corrosion conditions
- **Environment:** 90 wt% Na₂SO₄ + 10 wt% NaCl mixed salt.
- **Temperature:** 750 °C.
- **Duration:** 200 h.
- **Salt deposition method:** “Deposit recoat” method; specimens preheated to 350 °C, sprayed with salt to achieve a uniform coating of (1.0 ± 0.1) mg/cm²; re-sprayed every 20 h.
- **Cleaning between weighings:** Specimens suspended in boiling distilled water for 30 min, dried, and weighed.

## Mass gain results (after 200 h)
- **(210)-oriented specimen:** 0.59 ± 0.03 mg/cm² (lowest).
- **(100)-oriented specimen:** 0.61 ± 0.08 mg/cm².
- **(110)-oriented specimen:** 0.81 ± 0.02 mg/cm² (highest).
- **Order of mass gain:** (210) < (100) < (110).

## Hot corrosion kinetics
- Log(y) vs. log(t) curves were fitted using the relation yⁿ = kt.
- The slopes for (100)- and (210)-oriented specimens were almost similar.
- The slope for (110)-oriented specimen was larger, indicating different corrosion kinetics.

## Corrosion product constitution (XRD)
- Cr₂O₃, TiO₂, α-Al₂O₃ detected on all specimens.
- XRD also detected NiO, NiS, TiS₂ via XPS analysis.
- XPS detected NiO and NiS (Ni 2p), Cr₂O₃ and CrO₃ (Cr 2p), Al₂O₃ (Al 2p), TiO₂ and TiS₂ (Ti 2p).

## Corrosion product layer structure (cross-sectional SEM/EPMA)
- **Three-layered structure:**
  1. Outer layer: hot corrosion products (Cr₂O₃, TiO₂, α-Al₂O₃, NiO, NiS, TiS₂).
  2. Sub-outer layer: γ′-free zone (Al depletion; orthorhombic γ′ amount decreased).
  3. Innermost layer: matrix.
- **(100)-oriented specimen:** thin oxide scale, shallow internal corrosion.
- **(210)-oriented specimen:** almost no internal corrosion, very thin oxide scale, most complete and dense corrosion product layer.
- **(110)-oriented specimen:** thick oxide scale, deep internal corrosion, loose acicular oxide clusters with micropores; severe phase interface attack; W enrichment detected in corrosion scale indicating severe internal corrosion.

## Surface morphology observations
- **(100)-oriented specimen:** dense chromium-nickel-cobalt mixed film with cracks; spallation regions with acicular Ti-Cr oxides and spinel oxides.
- **(210)-oriented specimen:** dense chromium-cobalt-nickel mixed film.
- **(110)-oriented specimen:** many loose acicular oxides, easy to fall off, leading to spallation zone.

## Corrosion mechanisms
- **Chloride-induced corrosion (active oxidation):**
  - Cl₂ penetrates scale and attacks alloy, forming volatile metal chlorides (MClₓ).
  - Metal chlorides diffuse outward and react with O₂ to form MₓOᵧ, releasing Cl₂ to repeat the cycle (active oxidation).
  - Selectivity order based on ΔG°f at 750 °C: AlCl₃ < TiCl₄ < CrCl₂ < CrCl₃ < NiCl₂ (Al most susceptible to Cl₂ attack, then Ti, then Cr, then Ni).
- **Sulfate-induced corrosion:**
  - SO₄²⁻ dissociates to release S₂ and O₂.
  - Oxidation by SO₃: Al most affinitive to oxidation in SO₃ environment (2Al + SO₃ = Al₂O₃ + 1/2S₂, ΔG°f = −1063.259 kJ/mol SO₃); Cr second (2Cr + SO₃ = Cr₂O₃ + 1/2S₂, ΔG°f = −575.459 kJ/mol SO₃).
  - Sulfidation: Ti most affinitive to sulfur (Ti + S₂ = TiS₂, ΔG°f = −357.981 kJ/mol); Ni second (2Ni + S₂ = 2NiS, ΔG°f = −130.672 kJ/mol).
- **Phase interface attack:** Corrosion occurred mainly in γ′ phase and along γ/γ′ phase boundaries; dissolution and chemical reactions at phase interfaces led to enhanced attack.

## Effects of alloying elements on oxidation/hot corrosion resistance
- **Cr:** Forms Cr₂O₃ scale, which protects and acts as a barrier to mitigate corrosion. Higher Cr diffusivity on (210) face promotes protective Cr₂O₃ formation.
- **Al:** Forms α-Al₂O₃ at the alloy/scale interface; Al is preferentially attacked by Cl₂ and SO₃, leading to Al depletion and γ′-free zone formation.
- **Ti:** Forms TiO₂ (acicular oxides) and TiS₂; loose TiO₂ is detrimental to hot corrosion resistance.
- **W:** Enrichment in corrosion scale on (110) indicates severe internal corrosion; W is not specifically discussed as a protective or detrimental alloying element.
- **Mo, Ta:** Present in the alloy but not specifically discussed for hot corrosion behavior in this paper.

## High-temperature embrittlement, oxygen/nitrogen embrittlement, grain-boundary oxidation
- Not explicitly stated in the original paper. The paper focuses on hot corrosion, not mechanical embrittlement.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Ni

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ni-based single-crystal superalloy DD10 (γ/γ′ microstructure).
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Balance (Bal.) in wt.%.
- **Main Function:** Base matrix element of the superalloy; forms both γ and γ′ phases.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Ni is mainly distributed in γ′ phase; forms NiO and NiS as corrosion products.
- **Property Effect:** NiS and NiO are corrosion products formed during hot corrosion. The migration energy barrier for Ni diffusion in γ′ phase depends on crystal orientation: (100) = 3.335 eV, (110) = 0.996 eV, (210) = 5.078 eV.
- **Potential Side Effects:** NiS formation indicates sulfidation attack; NiCl₂ can form at high chlorine activity, contributing to active oxidation.
- **Original Evidence:** Table 1; Fig. 1 element maps; Fig. 5(b) XPS Ni 2p spectrum; Fig. 14(a).

## Cr

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ni-based single-crystal superalloy DD10.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 12.0 wt.%.
- **Main Function:** Forms protective Cr₂O₃ scale, which acts as a barrier to mitigate hot corrosion.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Cr is mainly distributed in γ phase; diffuses outward during hot corrosion to form Cr₂O₃; also forms volatile CrCl₂/CrCl₃ under chlorine attack.
- **Property Effect:** Cr₂O₃ scale formation is beneficial for hot corrosion resistance. The migration energy barrier for Cr diffusion in γ phase: (100) = 3.875 eV, (110) = 1.287 eV, (210) = 5.86 eV. Cr has the lowest migration energy among Ni, Al, Cr, Ti on the (210) crystal face, indicating that protective Cr₂O₃ is more easily formed on (210) orientation, contributing to superior hot corrosion resistance.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Cr₂O₃ can be converted to volatile CrCl₂/CrCl₃ under high chlorine activity, leading to scale degradation; CrO₃ detected by XPS indicates further oxidation.
- **Original Evidence:** Table 1; Figs. 3 and 4; Fig. 5(c) XPS Cr 2p spectrum; Table 2; Fig. 14(d); Fig. 15.

## Ti

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ni-based single-crystal superalloy DD10.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 4.0 wt.%.
- **Main Function:** Forms γ′ phase; contributes to precipitation strengthening in the original superalloy (not discussed in this paper). During hot corrosion, Ti forms TiO₂ and TiS₂.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Ti is mainly distributed in γ′ phase; Ti is the most affinitive to sulfur among Ni, Al, Cr, Ti, forming TiS₂ (ΔG°f = −357.981 kJ/mol); also forms acicular TiO₂ surface oxides. Ti can react with SO₃ via TiCl₄ + 2SO₃ = TiS₂ + 3O₂ + 2Cl₂.
- **Property Effect:** TiO₂ acicular oxides are loose and easy to fall off, leading to spallation; TiS₂ is a sulfidation product. High TiO₂ content in corrosion product films, especially on (210)-oriented specimen. Migration energy barriers for Ti diffusion in γ′ phase: (100) = 2.217 eV, (110) = 0.576 eV, (210) = 9.4 eV.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Loose TiO₂ acicular oxides are detrimental to hot corrosion resistance; TiS₂ formation indicates sulfidation attack.
- **Original Evidence:** Table 1; Fig. 1 element maps; Fig. 4; Fig. 5(e) XPS Ti 2p spectrum; Fig. 10; Fig. 11; Fig. 14(c).

## Al

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ni-based single-crystal superalloy DD10.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 3.6 wt.%.
- **Main Function:** Oxidized to form α-Al₂O₃ at the alloy/scale interface; preferential chloridation and sulfidation target during hot corrosion.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Al is mainly distributed in γ′ phase; Al is the most affinitive to Cl₂ (lowest ΔG°f for metal chloride formation at 750 °C); Al₂O₃ formation via 2Al + SO₃ = Al₂O₃ + 1/2S₂ (ΔG°f = −1063.259 kJ/mol SO₃). Al depletion leads to γ′-free zone formation.
- **Property Effect:** α-Al₂O₃ provides some protection at the alloy/scale interface, but AlCl₃ formation and diffusion lead to Al depletion and γ′-free zone. Migration energy barriers for Al diffusion in γ′ phase: (100) = 3.049 eV, (110) = 0.667 eV, (210) = 6.567 eV.
- **Potential Side Effects:** AlCl₃ formation and volatilization cause Al depletion near the interface, leading to γ′ dissolution and formation of a γ′-free zone (softened zone).
- **Original Evidence:** Table 1; Fig. 1 element maps; Table 3; Fig. 10; Fig. 11; Fig. 14(b).

# Key Conclusions

1. The three oriented specimens exhibited continuous mass gain during hot corrosion in 90 wt% Na₂SO₄ + 10 wt% NaCl at 750 °C for 200 h. The mass gain after 200 h was:
   - (210): 0.59 ± 0.03 mg/cm² (lowest)
   - (100): 0.61 ± 0.08 mg/cm²
   - (110): 0.81 ± 0.02 mg/cm² (highest)
   - Order: (210) < (100) < (110).

2. The (210)-oriented specimen exhibited superior hot corrosion resistance, with the least spallation, thinnest corrosion product scale, and shallowest internal attack. The (110)-oriented specimen exhibited the most severe corrosion, with many loose acicular oxide clusters on the surface, the deepest internal attacks, and phase interface attacks.

3. The main corrosion products on all specimens were Cr₂O₃, Al₂O₃, NiO, TiO₂, NiS, and TiS₂. The corrosion product scale on the (110)-oriented specimen was loose with micropores, unlike the denser scales on (100)- and (210)-oriented specimens.

4. The orientation-dependent hot corrosion behavior is attributed to crystal face anisotropy. First-principles CI-NEB calculations showed that the migration energy barriers for Ni, Al, Ti, Cr, and W in the three crystal faces follow the order (210) > (100) > (110). Consequently, diffusivities decrease in the order (110) > (100) > (210), consistent with the experimental mass gain results.

5. The atomic packing density (APD) model alone cannot explain the hot corrosion behavior of the (210)-oriented specimen. The APD values decrease in the order (100) > (110) > (210), but experimental results show the opposite trend for (210), indicating that the γ/γ′ phase boundary structure and diffusion anisotropy must be considered.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy system limitation:** This study is limited to a single Ni-based single-crystal superalloy (DD10). The results may not be directly transferable to polycrystalline Ni-based superalloys, Co-based superalloys, refractory alloys, or other alloy systems.
- **Orientation limitation:** Only (100), (210), and (110) crystallographic orientations were studied.
- **Environment limitation:** The hot corrosion tests were performed only in 90 wt% Na₂SO₄ + 10 wt% NaCl at 750 °C for 200 h with a specific “deposit recoat” method.
- **Mechanical property data:** The paper does not report any mechanical property data before or after corrosion. It cannot be used to quantify the mechanical degradation caused by hot corrosion.
- **DFT model scope:** The first-principles calculations considered only Ni, Al, Ti, Cr, and W diffusion in γ and γ′ phases. Mo and Ta diffusion were not studied, although they are present in the alloy.
- **No refractory-alloy content:** This paper contains no information on refractory high-entropy alloys, Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, ODS alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys.
