# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** Machine learning guided optimal composition selection of niobium alloys for high temperature applications
- **Authors:** Trupti Mohanty, K. S. Ravi Chandran, Taylor D. Sparks
- **Journal:** APL Machine Learning
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0129528
- **Alloy system studied:** Nb-based alloys (niobium alloys for high-temperature applications)
- **Research objective:** To use machine learning, specifically Bayesian optimization with Gradient Boost Regressor, to screen novel Nb-based quaternary and quinary alloy compositions with high yield strength and ultimate tensile strength over a temperature range from room temperature to 1300 °C for turbine applications.
- **Material category:** Nb-based refractory alloys (solid-solution strengthened alloys)

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system:** Niobium (Nb)-based alloys for high-temperature turbine applications
- **Base element:** Niobium (Nb)
- **Alloying elements considered in virtual design space (14 elements):** B, C, Co, Cr, Hf, Mo, N, Re, Si, Ta, Ti, V, W, Zr
- **Candidate alloy forms:**
  - Quaternary: Nb_a X_x Y_y Z_z
  - Quinary: Nb_a W_w X_x Y_y Z_z
- **Research objective:** To design Nb-based alloys that can withstand temperatures >1100 °C, exceeding the operating limit of Ni-based superalloys, with high yield and ultimate tensile strength for gas turbine applications.

# Alloy Composition

This paper is a machine-learning alloy design study rather than an experimental alloy characterization paper. It does not fabricate or test new alloys; rather, it uses existing experimental data to train a model and predict new candidate compositions.

## Training Dataset Composition

- The training dataset consists of **18 unique Nb alloys** with mechanical strength reported at temperatures from **24 to 1871 °C**.
- Total of **140 yield strength** and **144 ultimate tensile strength** data points.
- Specific alloy composition names mentioned in the paper:
  - **Nb 72.8 C 0.7 Mo 8.8 Ti 17.7** (missing yield strength values at 24 and 1260 °C)
  - **Nb 64.4 W 10.2 Ti 19.5 Mo 5.8** (missing yield strength values at 1260 and 1427 °C)
  - **Nb 87.7 W 11.2 Zr 1.1** (highest known room-temperature UTS: 1182 MPa)
- Full training dataset is from a compilation by K. S. Ravi Chandran (2021), not included in full in the paper body but available in supplementary material.

## Suggested Alloy Compositions (Virtual Candidates)

The paper reports several suggested compositions from Bayesian optimization. Key compositions explicitly mentioned:

- **Nb 87 Zr 1.1 Hf 0.6 W 11.3** (wt.%)
- **Nb 85 Zr 1.2 Ta 3.5 W 9.7 Re 0.6** (wt.%)

Other suggested candidates are listed in supplementary material tables TS5–TS8 and TS12–TS13 but are not explicitly given in the main text.

## Composition Restrictions

- Constraint: Nb weight percentage ≥ 50 wt.%
- Element concentration granularity: 1 wt.%
- B, C, N concentration range: 1–5 wt.%
- Other elements concentration range: 1–20 wt.%

# Key Conclusions

1. Machine learning combined with domain-knowledge-based feature selection is effective for predicting UTS and YS of Nb-based alloys using sparse experimental data. Domain knowledge-based custom features (15 descriptors) achieved higher prediction accuracy than simple atom percentage or extensive composition-based features.

2. Gradient Boost Regressor (GBR) with custom features outperformed other models:
   - UTS prediction: R² = 0.84, MAE = 57.42 MPa
   - YS prediction: R² = 0.77, MAE = 73.43 MPa

3. Temperature is the most important feature for predicting strength values (strength decreases with increasing temperature). Other important features include bulk electron concentration, Pauling electronegativity, and elastic modulus.

4. Bayesian optimization with GBR as surrogate model and expected improvement as utility function successfully screened novel quaternary and quinary Nb-based alloy candidates from a search space of ~1.6 × 10⁶ (quaternary) and ~60 × 10⁶ (quinary) candidates.

5. Top seven suggested quaternary candidates have predicted room-temperature UTS around 910 MPa (SD ~130 MPa), near the highest known value of 1182 MPa. These candidates may achieve strength values higher than 1182 MPa, but this needs experimental validation.

6. Key alloying elements in suggested compositions are W, Hf, Zr, Ta, and Re. These elements have high or complete solid solubility in Nb, likely forming single-phase BCC solid solutions.

7. CALPHAD calculations (pseudo-binary phase diagrams) for two representative suggested alloys (Nb 87 Zr 1.1 Hf 0.6 W 11.3 and Nb 85 Zr 1.2 Ta 3.5 W 9.7 Re 0.6) show single-phase BCC solid solution is thermodynamically stable from 550 °C to 2500 °C.

8. The approach demonstrates both exploitive capability (searching near existing high-strength alloys) and explorative capability (identifying alloys away from known high-strength clusters, e.g., Re-containing quinary alloys).

9. Machine learning guided alloy design can significantly reduce experimental effort and accelerate development of Nb-based alloys for turbine applications.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

## Limitations

1. **Small training dataset:** Only 18 unique Nb alloys with 140 YS and 144 UTS data points. Model prediction accuracy is low in the high-strength regime due to data sparsity.

2. **High uncertainty in predictions:** Standard deviations for top candidates are ~130 MPa, indicating significant prediction uncertainty.

3. **No experimental validation:** All suggested compositions are predictions; none have been experimentally fabricated or tested. The paper explicitly states that experimental validation is needed.

4. **Feature importance may change:** If new alloys are added to training data, feature importance and model performance may change.

5. **Thermodynamic evaluation limited:** CALPHAD analysis was performed only for two suggested alloys using pseudo-binary phase diagrams, not full multicomponent phase diagrams. The paper acknowledges that "we have not yet used thermodynamic modeling software to evaluate beyond the binary systems, and it remains a possibility that ternary or more complex alloys could form intermetallic multiphase composites."

6. **Composite target assumptions:** YS and UTS are linearly correlated in the dataset (r = 0.975), so the composite optimization may not provide significantly different insights than optimizing either property alone.

7. **Room-temperature screening assumption:** Candidates were screened at room temperature based on the hypothesis that alloys with high room-temperature strength are likely to have reasonable high-temperature strength. This hypothesis is not proven.

8. **Oxidation resistance not considered:** The design strategy does not incorporate oxidation resistance or other service-related properties.

9. **No mechanical property values at specific high temperatures provided for all suggested candidates:** The paper plots predicted strength vs temperature in figures (Fig. 5, Fig. S9) but does not provide detailed numerical tables in the main text.

## Applicable Scope

1. **Design of Nb-based alloy compositions:** The approach is specifically designed for Nb-based alloys with high-temperature strength requirements (gas turbine applications).

2. **Sparse data regimes:** The domain knowledge-based feature selection approach is applicable to other alloy systems with limited experimental data.

3. **High-throughput virtual screening:** The method can screen millions of candidate compositions efficiently.

4. **Active learning framework:** The approach is designed for iterative improvement, where new experimental data can be fed back to improve the model.

5. **Composition space:** Limited to quaternary (Nb-X-Y-Z) and quinary (Nb-W-X-Y-Z) alloys with 14 specified alloying elements (B, C, Co, Cr, Hf, Mo, N, Re, Si, Ta, Ti, V, W, Zr) and Nb weight percentage ≥ 50%.

6. **Property targets:** Yield strength and ultimate tensile strength (individually and as composite targets) over temperature range from room temperature to 1871 °C.

7. **Element concentration range:** B, C, N: 1–5 wt.%; other elements: 1–20 wt.%.

8. **Not applicable to:** Oxidation resistance prediction, grain-boundary features, microstructure prediction (beyond CALPHAD), creep performance, or fatigue properties—these are not included in the model.
