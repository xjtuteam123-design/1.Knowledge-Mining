# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy System Studied**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti alloy (dilute Mo-Ti alloy)
- **Material Category**: Mo-based alloy, powder metallurgy (PM) rolled sheet, surface-modified by internal nitriding
- **Research Objective**: To evaluate the effect of a novel two-step internal nitriding process on the ductile-brittle transition temperature (DBTT) of Mo–0.5 mass% Ti alloy, with the aim of suppressing recrystallization embrittlement and improving low-temperature ductility. The study focuses on preserving a heavily deformed surface microstructure via TiN precipitate pinning and assessing low-temperature (77–298 K) three-point bend behavior.

# Alloy Composition

- **Base Alloy**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti
- **Processing Method**: Powder metallurgy; rolled sheet approximately 1 mm thick
- **Alloying Elements**:
  - Ti: 0.5 mass% (Matrix: Mo)
- **Impurity Levels**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper for the Mo–0.5Ti alloy; C, O, N impurity levels are not reported.
- **Comparative Alloy Mentioned**: TZM alloy (Mo–0.5% Ti–0.08% Zr–0.03% C) appears in the Discussion section (cited from Kobayashi et al., reference 7), but was not the primary material in this study.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Primary Material**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti alloy produced by powder metallurgy, rolled to a sheet thickness of approximately 1 mm.
- **Specimen Preparation**: Rectangular specimens (2.5 mm wide × 25 mm long) were cut from the rolled sheet. Surface oxide layers were removed by emery paper polishing, followed by electropolishing, to prepare specimens for nitriding.

- **Internal Nitriding Treatment (Two-Step Process)**:
  - **Step 1 (Primary Nitriding)**: Performed at 1173–1223 K in 1 atm N₂ gas flow, below the recrystallization temperature of the alloy (approximately 1273 K).
    - Representative condition: (1173 K – 64 h) + (1223 K – 16 h)
  - **Step 2 (Secondary Nitriding)**: Performed at 1773 K for 24 h in N₂ gas, to coarsen the ultra-fine nitride particles precipitated during primary nitriding.
- **Recrystallized Comparison Specimen**: Mo–0.5Ti alloy heat-treated at 1773 K for 1 h in vacuum (1.3 × 10⁻⁴ Pa) to obtain recrystallized material.
- **Post-Recrystallization Nitrided Specimen**: Part of the recrystallized material was subsequently nitrided at 1773 K for 24 h (same as secondary nitriding temperature), to investigate the effect of nitriding after recrystallization.
- **Atmosphere**: N₂ gas at 1 atm for nitriding; vacuum (1.3 × 10⁻⁴ Pa) for recrystallization heat treatment.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix Phase**: BCC Mo matrix.
- **Second Phase**: TiN (titanium nitride) particles, rod-shaped (棒状), 50–120 nm in length. The majority of TiN precipitates were located within Mo grains (intragranular), not at grain boundaries.
- **Microstructure of As-Rolled Material**: Heavily deformed microstructure with fine grains in the surface region.
- **Microstructure of Recrystallized Material (1773 K, 1 h, vacuum)**: Equiaxed grains with average grain size of approximately 30 μm.
- **Microstructure of Two-Step Nitrided Material**:
  - Two-layer structure: Surface region approximately 130 μm deep retained a fine-grained deformed microstructure similar to the as-rolled material.
  - The inner region of the specimen recrystallized during secondary nitriding at 1773 K, and subsequent nitrogen diffusion caused internal nitriding in the recrystallized interior.
  - Recrystallization at the surface was significantly suppressed because ultra-fine TiN precipitates formed during primary nitriding grew during secondary nitriding and acted as effective pinning points for grain-boundary migration.
  - The thickness of the retained fine-grained surface layer depended strongly on the secondary nitriding conditions (referenced to Nagae et al., reference 5).
- **Hardness Distribution** (Vickers hardness):
  - Surface hardness of two-step nitrided specimen: approximately 360 HV.
  - Hardness of recrystallized Mo–0.5Ti alloy (dashed line in Fig. 2): lower than surface hardness of nitrided specimen; precipitation hardening from internal nitriding was confirmed.
  - At depths > 200 μm from the surface, hardness also increased due to internal nitriding after recrystallization, caused by nitrogen diffusion during the 1773 K secondary nitriding treatment.
- **Strengthening Mechanisms Discussed**:
  - Precipitation hardening (TiN precipitates; rod-shaped particles 50–120 nm in length; mainly intragranular).
  - Grain refinement/grain-boundary strengthening (retention of fine-grained deformed microstructure at the specimen surface via Zener pinning by TiN particles).
  - Grain-boundary pinning (Zener pinning) by TiN precipitates delays recrystallization.

# Mechanical Properties

- **Test Method**: Three-point bend test.
- **Test Temperatures**: 77 K to room temperature (298 K).
- **Apparent Strain Rate**: 1.2 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹.
- **Specimen Geometry**: 2.5 mm wide × 25 mm long, thickness approximately 1 mm (original sheet thickness).

- **Yield Strength at 243 K**:
  - Two-step nitrided specimen: **1620 MPa**
  - Recrystallized specimen: **970 MPa**
  - Ratio: Two-step nitrided specimen was approximately **1.7 times** the yield strength of the recrystallized specimen.
- **Bend Ductility at 243 K**:
  - Two-step nitrided specimen: Bent to more than 90 degrees without fracture.
  - Recrystallized specimen: Fractured after showing only slight ductility (brittle fracture after bending only a few degrees).
- **Post-Recrystallization Nitrided Specimen (1773 K – 24 h after recrystallization)**:
  - Tested at 243 K: Fractured in a brittle manner **before** showing yield strength (no plastic yielding observed).
  - This result indicates that nitriding after recrystallization severely embrittles the material, consistent with nitrogen segregation to grain boundaries causing grain-boundary embrittlement (cited from Hiraoka et al., reference 6, on Mo bicrystals).
- **Ductile-Brittle Transition Temperature (DBTT)**:
  - Two-step nitrided specimen: approximately **153 K**.
  - Recrystallized specimen: approximately **203 K** (cited from Yoshimura et al., reference 8).
  - Estimated from the temperature at which yield strength equals fracture strength, based on the temperature dependence of yield and fracture strengths from three-point bend tests.
- **Hardness**:
  - Surface hardness of two-step nitrided specimen: approximately **360 HV** (Vickers).
  - Exact hardness of recrystallized Mo–0.5Ti alloy: Not explicitly stated as a numerical value in the original text; shown as a dashed line in Fig. 2, lower than the surface hardness of the nitrided specimen.
- **Fracture behavior**:
  - Recrystallized specimen at 243 K: Brittle fracture after slight bending.
  - Two-step nitrided specimen at 243 K: Ductile bending beyond 90°.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxidation Resistance**: Not addressed in the original paper. Nitriding was performed in N₂ gas at 1 atm; no oxidation behavior was evaluated.
- **Service Stability**: The paper addresses low-temperature embrittlement (recrystallization embrittlement and DBTT), not high-temperature oxidation.
- **High-Temperature Embrittlement Issues Discussed**:
  - Molybdenum materials suffer from low-temperature brittleness due to weak grain boundaries common to BCC refractory metals with high melting points.
  - When Mo materials are used above 1273 K and undergo recrystallization, coarse equiaxed grains form, allowing cracks to propagate straight, causing DBTT to rise to near room temperature.
  - Nitrogen segregation to grain boundaries severely embrittles Mo grain boundaries (cited from Hiraoka et al., reference 6).
  - Internal nitriding of recrystallized TZM alloy increases hardness but reduces low-temperature ductility (cited from Kobayashi et al., reference 7).

## Ti (Titanium) in Mo–Ti Alloy

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Mo–Ti dilute alloy (Mo-based alloy)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0.5 mass% Ti
- **Main Function**: Provides the basis for internal nitriding; Ti reacts with nitrogen to form TiN precipitates following internal nitriding.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: During internal nitriding, nitrogen diffuses into the Mo–Ti alloy and reacts with dissolved Ti to form TiN precipitates. During primary nitriding (below recrystallization temperature), ultra-fine TiN particles precipitate in the deformed surface microstructure. These TiN particles then grow during secondary nitriding at 1773 K and act as grain-boundary pinning points (Zener pinning) that suppress recrystallization of the deformed surface microstructure.
- **Property Effect**: TiN precipitation hardening increases surface hardness to approximately 360 HV. The retention of fine-grained deformed microstructure at the surface (approximately 130 μm deep) improves low-temperature ductility and lowers DBTT from approximately 203 K (recrystallized) to 153 K (two-step nitrided). At 243 K, yield strength is 1620 MPa for the two-step nitrided specimen compared to 970 MPa for the recrystallized specimen.
- **Potential Side Effects**: TiN precipitation hardening can generally reduce low-temperature ductility if nitriding is performed after recrystallization, as shown by the post-recrystallization nitrided specimen, which fractured in a brittle manner before yielding at 243 K.
- **Original Evidence**: Abstract; Section 1 (Introduction); Section 3 (Results and Discussion, Figs. 1–6); Conclusion (1)–(3).

## TiN (Titanium Nitride Precipitate)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Mo–Ti alloy, internally nitrided
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not stated as a composition; TiN particles formed from 0.5 mass% Ti in Mo by internal nitriding.
- **Main Function**: Precipitation hardening; grain-boundary pinning (Zener pinning) to suppress recrystallization and preserve fine-grained deformed microstructure.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: TiN precipitates as rod-shaped particles 50–120 nm in length, mostly intragranular. Primary nitriding below the recrystallization temperature forms ultra-fine TiN precipitates in the deformed surface. During secondary nitriding, these particles grow and pin grain boundaries, preventing recrystallization of the surface deformed microstructure, resulting in a two-layer structure with approximately 130 μm of fine-grained deformed surface layer retained.
- **Property Effect**: Surface hardness approximately 360 HV; yield strength at 243 K approximately 1.7 times that of recrystallized material (1620 MPa vs 970 MPa); DBTT reduced from approximately 203 K to 153 K; bend ductility at 243 K exceeds 90° without fracture.
- **Potential Side Effects**: TiN precipitation hardening alone (when nitriding is performed after recrystallization) causes severe grain-boundary embrittlement and brittle fracture at 243 K.
- **Original Evidence**: Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4; Conclusion (1)–(3); Discussion Section.

## N (Nitrogen, in Nitriding Treatment)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Mo–Ti alloy (Mo-based alloy), internal nitriding process
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Introduced by 1 atm N₂ gas flow at 1173–1773 K (multi-step process: primary 1173–1223 K for 64 + 16 h; secondary 1773 K for 24 h).
- **Main Function**: Internal nitriding to form TiN precipitates in Mo–Ti alloy; the multi-step process controls the size and distribution of TiN, enabling grain-boundary pinning during secondary nitriding.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Nitrogen diffuses into the alloy from the surface. During primary nitriding below the recrystallization temperature, ultra-fine TiN precipitates form in the deformed surface. Secondary nitriding at 1773 K coarsens TiN precipitates, enabling grain-boundary pinning and suppression of recrystallization in the surface region. In deeper regions, internal nitriding occurs after recrystallization (due to nitrogen diffusion), causing precipitation hardening but also grain-boundary embrittlement.
- **Property Effect**: Two-step nitriding improves low-temperature ductility and lowers DBTT to 153 K, increases surface hardness to approximately 360 HV, and yield strength at 243 K to 1620 MPa. In contrast, nitriding after recrystallization causes brittle fracture at 243 K before yielding due to nitrogen segregation at grain boundaries.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Nitrogen segregation to grain boundaries can cause severe grain-boundary embrittlement, especially when nitriding is performed after recrystallization or without the two-step microstructure control.
- **Original Evidence**: Abstract; Section 2 (Experimental); Section 3 (Figs. 2, 5); Discussion (reference to Hiraoka et al., reference 6; Kobayashi et al., reference 7); Conclusion (1)–(3).

## Precipitation Hardening (TiN Precipitate Strengthening)

- **Related Elements**: Ti, N (N₂ gas nitriding)
- **Typical Alloys**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti internally nitrided; references to TZM (Mo–0.5Ti–0.08Zr–0.03C) internally nitrided (cited from Kobayashi et al.)
- **Key Compositions**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti; TiN precipitates 50–120 nm rod-shaped
- **Microstructural Changes**: Precipitates 50–120 nm rod-shaped, mostly intragranular. In the two-step nitrided specimen, TiN precipitates formed in the deformed surface during primary nitriding, then coarsened during secondary nitriding at 1773 K. The precipitates are located within Mo grains.
- **Property Improvements**: Surface hardness approximately 360 HV; hardening relative to recrystallized Mo–0.5Ti alloy is observed.
- **Risks and Limitations**: When nitriding is performed after recrystallization, TiN precipitation hardening causes severe loss of low-temperature ductility: the post-recrystallization nitrided specimen fractured in a brittle manner at 243 K before yielding. Precipitation hardening alone (without the retained fine-grained deformed microstructure) is not sufficient to improve DBTT.

## Grain-Boundary Pinning (Zener Pinning) by TiN Precipitates

- **Related Elements**: Ti, N (via TiN precipitates)
- **Typical Alloys**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti, two-step nitrided
- **Key Compositions**: TiN precipitates 50–120 nm
- **Microstructural Changes**: TiN precipitates at grain boundaries in the deformed surface microstructure effectively pin grain-boundary migration, delaying recrystallization. A fine-grained deformed surface layer approximately 130 μm deep persists after two-step nitriding. The inner region recrystallizes during secondary nitriding at 1773 K.
- **Property Improvements**: Retention of fine-grained surface layer improves low-temperature ductility (bend > 90° at 243 K) and lowers DBTT to approximately 153 K, compared to 203 K for fully recrystallized Mo–0.5Ti alloy. Yield strength at 243 K is 1620 MPa, approximately 1.7 times the recrystallized specimen (970 MPa).
- **Risks and Limitations**: The effect is surface-limited: only approximately 130 μm of the surface retains the fine-grained deformed microstructure; deeper regions recrystallize and are embrittled by internal nitriding after recrystallization.

## Grain Refinement / Fine-Grained Deformed Microstructure Retention

- **Related Elements**: Ti, N (via TiN precipitates acting as grain-boundary pinning points)
- **Typical Alloys**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti, two-step nitrided
- **Key Compositions**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti, rolled sheet with heavily deformed surface microstructure
- **Microstructural Changes**: The two-step nitriding process retains a fine-grained deformed microstructure in the surface region (approximately 130 μm) through TiN grain-boundary pinning, preventing recrystallization during high-temperature exposure (1773 K secondary nitriding).
- **Property Improvements**: Improved low-temperature bend ductility (bend > 90° at 243 K); DBTT reduced from approximately 203 K to 153 K; yield strength at 243 K increased to 1620 MPa (1.7× compared to recrystallized).
- **Risks and Limitations**: The retained fine-grained surface layer is limited to the near-surface region; interior regions recrystallize and are susceptible to nitrogen-induced grain-boundary embrittlement.

## Recrystallization Embrittlement Control

- **Related Elements**: Ti (and N via TiN precipitates)
- **Typical Alloys**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti (and general Mo-based alloys)
- **Key Compositions**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti
- **Microstructural Changes**: In Mo-based alloys, recrystallization above 1273 K causes coarse equiaxed grains, allowing straight crack propagation and raising DBTT to near room temperature. Two-step internal nitriding with TiN precipitates suppresses recrystallization at the surface via grain-boundary pinning, thereby avoiding coarse equiaxed grains and maintaining crack-arresting fine-grained surface microstructure.
- **Property Improvements**: DBTT lowered from approximately 203 K (recrystallized) to approximately 153 K (two-step nitrided); ductile bending > 90° at 243 K; suppression of recrystallization embrittlement in the surface layer.
- **Risks and Limitations**: The effect is limited to near-surface regions; bulk recrystallization in the interior still occurs during high-temperature exposure, and internal nitriding after recrystallization can embrittle the interior through nitrogen grain-boundary segregation.

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: TiN precipitates (grain-boundary pinning), N (segregation)
- **Typical Alloys**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti, two-step nitrided
- **Key Compositions**: Mo–0.5 mass% Ti with TiN precipitates
- **Microstructural Changes**: TiN precipitates strengthen grain boundaries by pinning grain-boundary migration (Zener pinning). In contrast, nitrogen segregation to grain boundaries severely embrittles Mo grain boundaries (cited from Hiraoka et al., reference 6).

- **Risks and Limitations**: Nitrogen segregation can cause grain-boundary embrittlement (post-recrystallization nitrided specimen fractured at 243 K before yielding). The fine-grained surface layer must be retained to avoid grain-boundary embrittlement.

# Key Conclusions

1. The two-step internal nitriding process (primary nitriding at 1173–1223 K below the recrystallization temperature, followed by secondary nitriding at 1773 K for 24 h) retains the heavily deformed fine-grained surface microstructure of Mo–0.5 mass% Ti rolled sheet to a depth of approximately 130 μm, through TiN grain-boundary pinning.

2. The two-step nitrided specimen exhibits a yield strength of 1620 MPa at 243 K, approximately 1.7 times that of the recrystallized specimen (970 MPa), while also showing excellent ductility (bending more than 90° at 243 K), despite the higher yield strength.

3. The DBTT of the two-step nitrided specimen is approximately 153 K, compared to approximately 203 K for the recrystallized Mo–0.5Ti alloy, demonstrating a significant improvement in low-temperature ductility.

4. The favorable low-temperature properties of the two-step nitrided specimen arise from the retention of the fine-grained deformed surface microstructure via TiN grain-boundary pinning, rather than from the mere presence of dispersed precipitates. Nitriding after recrystallization (same final nitriding temperature of 1773 K for 24 h) results in brittle fracture at 243 K before yielding, due to nitrogen grain-boundary segregation and loss of the fine-grained surface microstructure.

5. Two-step (multi-step) internal nitriding is considered highly effective for improving recrystallization embrittlement and associated low-temperature brittleness of molybdenum alloys.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy System Specificity**: The study is limited to Mo–0.5 mass% Ti produced by powder metallurgy and rolled to approximately 1 mm thickness. The general applicability to other Mo-based alloys was not directly investigated in this paper (except for comparison references to TZM).
- **Testing Temperature Range**: Mechanical testing was performed only at low temperatures (77 K to room temperature). High-temperature strength and long-term service stability were not evaluated in this study.
- **Microstructural Gradient**: The beneficial surface fine-grained microstructure is limited to a depth of approximately 130 μm after the specific two-step nitriding conditions. The interior region recrystallized and was embrittled by internal nitriding after recrystallization, as indicated by hardness increase at depths > 200 μm and degradation of low-temperature ductility when nitriding was performed after recrystallization.
- **Oxidation Behavior**: Not evaluated.
- **Strain Rate**: Bend tests were performed at a single apparent strain rate of 1.2 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹.
- **Quantification of Precipitate Volume Fraction, Size Distribution, and Spacing**: Not reported in this paper (precipitate length 50–120 nm was stated, but volume fraction, number density, and inter-particle spacing were not quantified).
- **Mechanism of Strength Increase**: The contribution of grain size (retained fine-grained surface microstructure) vs. precipitation hardening to the yield strength increase (1620 MPa vs 970 MPa) is described as a topic for future investigation.
- **Nitrogen Segregation Kinetics and Concentration Profiles**: The paper does not provide quantitative nitrogen concentration profiles; only hardness distributions are shown.
- **Scale-Up and Geometric Effects**: All experiments were performed on thin sheet specimens (approximately 1 mm thickness), which may influence internal nitriding depth and mechanical behavior; extension to bulk components was not addressed.
