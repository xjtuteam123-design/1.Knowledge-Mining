# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: A novel Nb-W-C alloy with special microstructure and excellent mechanical property
- **Authors**: Xiaohong Yuan, Yan Wei, Yong Han, Wanchun Zhao, Li Chen, Xian Wang, Changyi Hu, Jialin Chen, Shanju Zheng
- **Journal**: Journal of Alloys and Compounds
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jallcom.2023.172178
- **Alloy System Studied**: Nb-W-C alloy prepared by Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD)
- **Material Category**: Nb-W-based alloy produced by CVD (not powder metallurgy, not arc melting, not additive manufacturing in the LPBF/SLM/EBM/DED sense)
- **Research Objective**: To design and prepare a new kind of Nb-W-C alloy with special microstructure and excellent mechanical property using CVD combined with homogenization heat treatment; to clarify the strengthening control factors and microstructure evolution during heat treatment.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD)
- **Deposition method**: CVD on Mo substrate
- **Substrate shape**: Cylinder, Φ 30 mm × 30 mm
- **Raw materials**: Nb plates (≥99.99%), W plates (≥99.99%), Mo substrate; high purity carbon sheet placed below Mo substrate to provide carbon source
- **Chlorination temperature (Tc)**: 750 °C
- **Deposition temperature (Td)**: 1100 °C–1400 °C
- **Chlorine flow (F_Cl2)**: 50–300 ml/min
- **Hydrogen flow (F_H2)**: 300–800 ml/min
- **Deposition time**: 48 h
- **Deposition thickness**: 4 mm (original deposition thickness on substrate)
- **Sample extraction**: Test samples were taken from the middle of the deposited bulk; upper and lower parts cut off by 1 mm thickness each.

### CVD reactions
1. 2Nb(s) + 5Cl₂(g) ↔ 2NbCl₅(g) (chlorination of Nb at 300 °C)
2. 2NbCl₅(g) + 5H₂(g) ↔ 2Nb(s) + 10HCl(g)
3. W(s) + 3Cl₂(g) ↔ WCl₆(g) (chlorination of W above 700 °C)
4. WCl₆(g) + 3H₂(g) ↔ W(s) + 6HCl(g)
5. 2Nb(s) + C(g) ↔ Nb₂C(s) (C vaporized at 1000 °C)

## Homogenization Heat Treatment
- **Furnace**: High temperature vacuum furnace
- **Heating rate**: 10 °C/min
- **Cooling method**: Furnace cooling (cooled with furnace)
- **Temperatures and times**:
  - 1400 °C – 4 h
  - 1400 °C – 6 h
  - 1600 °C – 4 h
  - 1600 °C – 6 h
- **Atmosphere**: High temperature vacuum

## Sample Preparation for Tensile Testing
- Samples machined by wire cutting into tensile samples.
- Tensile sample thickness: 2 mm.
- Surface polished with 400# and 1200# sandpaper before testing to eliminate surface defect influence.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Matrix Phase
- **(Nb,W)ₛₛ solid solution**, BCC structure confirmed by SAED.
- Lattice constant a = 3.256 Å (between Nb a = 3.294 Å and W a = 3.191 Å), confirming substitutional solid solution formation.

## Second Phase
- **β-Nb₂C** hexagonal carbide, identified by SAED.
  - Crystal structure: hexagonal (β-Nb₂C), not orthorhombic α-Nb₂C, not γ-Nb₂C.
  - Lattice constants: a = 5.44 Å, c = 5.0 Å, a/c = 1.088.
  - Carbon is enriched in the second phase; W is depleted in the second phase (poor in W, rich in Nb and C according to STEM-EDS in Fig. 10).

## Grain Structure
- **As-deposited**: Columnar grains with average grain size D = 9.67 μm; average length = 15.53 μm; average width = 4.73 μm; grain aspect ratio (L/W) = 3.283.
- **1400 °C–4 h**: Average grain size D = 19.22 μm; average length = 21.63 μm; average width = 13.41 μm; L/W = 1.613.
- **1400 °C–6 h**: Average grain size D = 38.26 μm; average length = 41.91 μm; average width = 27.43 μm; L/W = 1.528.
- **1600 °C–4 h**: Average grain size D = 66.89 μm; average length = 68.41 μm; average width = 51.37 μm; L/W = 1.332.
- **1600 °C–6 h**: Average grain size D = 214.26 μm; average length = 209.32 μm; average width = 172.26 μm; L/W = 1.215.

Grain morphology evolves from columnar to equiaxed with increasing heat treatment temperature and time. Grain size increases to more than 20 times that of as-deposited after 1600 °C treatment.

## Layered Structure / Composition Segregation
- As-deposited and 1400 °C-treated samples exhibit a **layered structure** with alternating Nb-rich (dark) and W-rich (bright) layers.
- **Average layered structure thickness**: 5–15 μm.
- Layered structure is formed by competitive chlorination/reduction cycles of W and Nb during CVD deposition; W segregates first, then Nb adsorbs on W-rich layer.
- W-rich layers have higher atomic number contrast (bright in BSE/EPMA).
- Layered structure is perpendicular to deposition direction and runs through Nb-W grains.
- After 1600 °C heat treatment, the layered structure disappears due to elemental redistribution and homogenization.

## Nb₂C Distribution and Size
- As-deposited: Nb₂C mostly less than 2 μm in length; more than half <0.5 μm; ellipsoidal or short rod shape; discontinuously distributed at grain boundaries and within grains.
- 1400 °C–4 h: Nb₂C length concentrated in 1–5 μm range.
- 1600 °C–4 h: Nb₂C coarsens; number of Nb₂C >5 μm increases sharply; small-sized Nb₂C merges and grows along long axis.
- Nb₂C area fractions:
  - As-deposited: 1.385%
  - 1400 °C–4 h: 3.608%
  - 1600 °C–4 h: 3.65%

### Nb₂C SEM-EDS composition
- As-deposited: 70.21 wt.% Nb, 11.16 wt.% W, 18.63 wt.% C
- 1400 °C–4 h: 81.21 wt.% Nb, 4.46 wt.% W, 14.34 wt.% C
- 1600 °C–4 h: 84.7 wt.% Nb, 5.93 wt.% W, 9.37 wt.% C
- Note: authors caution that SEM-EDS spot diameter is larger than STEM-EDS, so matrix composition may be included.

## Grain Orientation
- As-deposited: grains inclined to <100> and <111> orientations.
- 1400 °C: columnar crystals along deposition direction, preferred orientation <100>.
- 1600 °C: equiaxed grains, preferred orientation <111>.

## Strengthening Mechanisms Discussed
- **Solid-solution strengthening** from W (due to high melting point and similar atomic radius of W and Nb).
- **Carbide precipitation strengthening** from Nb₂C (β-Nb₂C) — described by Ashby-Orowan mechanism.
- **Grain refinement strengthening** (Hall-Petch effect).
- **Layered structure effect**: separates Nb₂C and inhibits size coarsening during carbide formation.
- **Dispersion strengthening**: fine Nb₂C dispersion strengthens at 1400 °C.
- **Precipitation strengthening**: increase in Nb₂C area fraction after homogenization.

# Mechanical Properties

## Room-Temperature Tensile Properties
- **Alloy heat-treated at 1400 °C–6 h**: tensile strength reaches **670 MPa**.
- The strength of 1400 °C–6 h sample is **about 35–45% higher** than the as-deposited Nb-W-C alloy and the existing widely used Nb521 alloy produced by melting-forging-annealing technology.
- Reference: Nb521 room temperature strength is 400–600 MPa; Nb521 high temperature strength is higher at 1000–1400 °C.
- **Elastic modulus**: Of homogenizing treated alloy slightly higher than that of as-deposited alloy. (No specific numerical value provided for elastic modulus in the original paper.)
- **As 1400 °C increases to 1600 °C**: tensile strength of the alloy decreases by about 95% after holding for 6 h (exact wording: "the tensile strength of the alloy decreases by about 95% after holding for 6 h").

## Fracture Behavior
- As-deposited and 1400 °C samples: **cleavage fracture** with layered characteristics.
- 1600 °C samples: **intergranular fracture**; crack initiation at interface between carbides and (Nb,W)ₛₛ matrix, due to high hardness, brittleness and poor deformability of Nb₂C.

## Other Mechanical Properties
- **Hardness**: Not explicitly provided with values. Nb₂C is described as having high hardness.
- **Density**: Not explicitly provided for this alloy. Nb density cited as 8.57 g/cm³, melting point 2741 K.
- **High-temperature strength, elongation, compressive strength, fracture toughness, creep performance, rupture life, strain rate**: Not explicitly provided in the original paper.
- **Testing atmosphere**: Room-temperature uniaxial tensile tests on Shimadzu Tester Testing machine; atmosphere not explicitly stated.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

The original paper does not directly investigate oxidation resistance of the Nb-W-C alloy. No oxidation weight gain, oxidation layer type, oxidation temperature or duration are reported for this alloy.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## W (Tungsten)
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Nb-W-C CVD alloy (this paper)
- **Addition Amount**: 10 ± 0.08 wt.%
- **Main Function**: Solid-solution strengthening of Nb matrix; contributes to formation of layered structure during CVD deposition; improves room and high temperature strength of alloy matrix.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: W has high melting point and similar atomic radius to Nb, forming substitutional solid solution (Nb,W)ₛₛ with BCC structure. W has larger atomic number, providing Z-contrast in BSE/EPMA (bright W-rich layers). W segregation layers form during CVD due to competitive chlorination/reduction reactions.
- **Property Effect**: Room temperature tensile strength reaches 670 MPa after 1400 °C–6 h treatment; W contributes to solid-solution strengthening. Specific W-only contribution to strength is not quantified; the original paper does not provide isolated numerical values for W strengthening contribution.
- **Potential Side Effects**: W-rich layers segregate during CVD, creating inhomogeneous layered structure. High temperature heat treatment (1600 °C) causes W redistribution leading to disappearance of layered structure. Density increase is implied by W addition but not quantified.
- **Original Evidence**: Table 1 (10 ± 0.08 wt.% W); Fig. 6 (EPMA segregation of W, bright W-rich layers); "The addition of W and Mo elements in the alloy can form a replacement solid solution because of their high melting point and similar atomic radius, so as to improve the room and high temperature strength of the alloy matrix"; lattice constant a = 3.256 Å confirming (Nb,W)ₛₛ.

## C (Carbon)
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Nb-W-C CVD alloy (this paper)
- **Addition Amount**: 2.5 ± 0.02 wt.%
- **Main Function**: Carbide precipitation strengthening via Nb₂C formation; stabilizing fine carbides to effectively improve strength.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: C combines with Nb to form β-Nb₂C (hexagonal, a = 5.44 Å, c = 5.0 Å, a/c = 1.088). C is enriched in Nb₂C and depleted in W. Carbide area fraction increases from 1.385% (as-deposited) to 3.608% (1400 °C–4 h) and 3.65% (1600 °C–4 h). Fine Nb₂C particles act as dispersion strengthening phase and pin grain boundaries. Carbon atom vaporized at 1000 °C during CVD.
- **Property Effect**: Tensile strength of 1400 °C–6 h sample reaches 670 MPa, attributed in part to fine Nb₂C precipitation strengthening. The paper cites Ashby-Orowan mechanism for precipitation strengthening estimation.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Coarse Nb₂C at 1600 °C causes embrittlement and intergranular fracture. Nb₂C is brittle with high hardness and poor deformability; cracks initiate at carbide/matrix interface during tensile deformation. Excessive C in carbide coarsening weakens alloy.
- **Original Evidence**: Table 1 (2.5 ± 0.02 wt.% C); Eq. (5) 2Nb(s) + C(g) ↔ Nb₂C(s); Fig. 9 (SAED of Nb₂C); Fig. 10 (STEM-EDS shows Nb and C enrichment in second phase); Table 3 (Nb₂C area fraction and composition); Ashby-Orowan equation: Δσ = (5.9√f / d) ln(d / 2.5×10⁻⁴).

## Nb (Niobium)
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Nb-W-C CVD alloy (this paper)
- **Addition Amount**: Balance (Bal.)
- **Main Function**: Matrix element providing ductility, low density, and high melting point base.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Nb forms BCC (Nb,W)ₛₛ matrix (a = 3.256 Å) and reacts with C to form β-Nb₂C. Nb-rich layers in layered structure are where carbides preferentially concentrate.
- **Property Effect**: As matrix, contributes to overall strength; no isolated Nb contribution to strength is quantified in the original paper.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not discussed for this alloy in the paper.
- **Original Evidence**: Table 1 (Nb balance); Fig. 9 (BCC matrix diffraction patterns); Fig. 6 (EPMA Nb segregation, dark Nb-rich layers).

## Mo (Molybdenum) — substrate only
- **Applicable Alloy System**: CVD Nb-W-C alloy substrate (this paper)
- **Addition Amount**: Not an alloying element; used as substrate (Φ 30 mm × 30 mm cylinder). Not listed in Table 1 composition.
- **Main Function**: Substrate for CVD deposition, not an alloying element in this paper.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Not applicable as alloying element.
- **Property Effect**: Not applicable.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not discussed in the original paper. (Note: Mo is mentioned in Introduction as a strengthening element for Nb alloys such as Nb521 but is not part of this specific alloy.)
- **Original Evidence**: "The Nb-W-C alloy is deposited on Mo substrate via CVD." Table 1 lists only Nb, W, C.

## W + C Combination
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Nb-W-C CVD alloy (this paper)
- **Addition Amount**: W 10 ± 0.08 wt.% + C 2.5 ± 0.02 wt.%
- **Main Function**: Combined effects of solid-solution strengthening (W) and carbide precipitation strengthening (C → Nb₂C), with layered structure controlling carbide distribution.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: During CVD, W segregates into W-rich layers; Nb₂C precipitates preferentially in Nb-rich layers. Layered structure separates Nb₂C and inhibits carbide coarsening. At 1400 °C, layered structure persists and fine Nb₂C is retained. At 1600 °C, layered structure disappears, Nb₂C coarsens.
- **Property Effect**: Room temperature tensile strength of 670 MPa after 1400 °C–6 h, 35–45% higher than as-deposited Nb-W-C and Nb521. 1600 °C treatment decreases tensile strength by ~95% compared to 1400 °C.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Disappearance of layered structure at 1600 °C leads to carbide coarsening and severe grain growth, causing intergranular fracture and strength loss.
- **Original Evidence**: Conclusions (1)–(3); Table 2 grain data; Fig. 12 and Table 3 Nb₂C size and area fraction; Fig. 14 stress-strain curves.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening
- **Related Elements**: W (and Mo in reference alloys such as Nb521)
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb-W-C CVD alloy (this paper); Nb521 (Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr) mentioned as reference
- **Key Compositions**: 10 ± 0.08 wt.% W in Nb-W-C alloy
- **Microstructural Changes**: W dissolves in Nb matrix forming BCC (Nb,W)ₛₛ solid solution. Lattice constant a = 3.256 Å, between Nb (3.294 Å) and W (3.191 Å), confirming substitutional solid solution. W segregates in layered structure due to CVD deposition process.
- **Property Improvements**: Contributes to room and high temperature strength; final alloy strength after 1400 °C–6 h reaches 670 MPa. The original paper does not quantitatively separate W solid-solution strengthening contribution from other mechanisms.
- **Risks and Limitations**: W-rich segregation layers during CVD cause microstructural heterogeneity; W redistribution at 1600 °C homogenization eliminates layered structure; W increases alloy density. Quantitative W specific strengthening contribution not isolated in the original paper.

## Carbide Precipitation Strengthening (Nb₂C)
- **Related Elements**: C, Nb
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb-W-C CVD alloy (this paper); PWC-11 (Nb-1Zr-0.1C) mentioned as reference for carbide-strengthened Nb alloys
- **Key Compositions**: 2.5 ± 0.02 wt.% C forming β-Nb₂C (hexagonal, a = 5.44 Å, c = 5.0 Å, a/c = 1.088)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Needle-like / short rod-like β-Nb₂C precipitates form at grain boundaries and within grains. Area fraction: 1.385% (as-deposited) → 3.608% (1400 °C–4 h) → 3.65% (1600 °C–4 h). Carbide length distribution: as-deposited mostly <2 μm; 1400 °C–4 h concentrated 1–5 μm; 1600 °C >5 μm increases sharply.
- **Property Improvements**: Fine Nb₂C at 1400 °C strengthens alloy, achieving 670 MPa at 1400 °C–6 h. Ashby-Orowan mechanism used to estimate strengthening: Δσ = (5.9√f / d) ln(d / 2.5×10⁻⁴), where d is average particle size (μm), f is volume fraction.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Coarse Nb₂C at 1600 °C acts as brittle phase; crack initiation at carbide/matrix interface; intergranular fracture; tensile strength decreases by ~95% at 1600 °C–6 h compared to 1400 °C–6 h.

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening / Grain Refinement (Hall-Petch)
- **Related Elements**: Not element-specific; process-dependent
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb-W-C CVD alloy (this paper)
- **Key Compositions**: Not applicable; governed by heat treatment temperature/time
- **Microstructural Changes**: As-deposited grain size 9.67 μm; 1400 °C–4 h 19.22 μm; 1400 °C–6 h 38.26 μm; 1600 °C–4 h 66.89 μm; 1600 °C–6 h 214.26 μm (20× coarsening). Columnar grains transform to equiaxed with increasing temperature.
- **Property Improvements**: Fine grains at 1400 °C contribute to strength (670 MPa); Hall-Petch relationship cited as major strengthening contributor.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Severe grain coarsening at 1600 °C (20× increase) embrittles alloy and causes intergranular fracture; overshadows carbide strengthening benefits.

## Layered Structure / Composition Segregation (Unique to CVD)
- **Related Elements**: Nb, W (competitive chlorination/reduction cycles)
- **Typical Alloys**: CVD Nb-W-C alloy (this paper)
- **Key Compositions**: 10 wt.% W, 2.5 wt.% C
- **Microstructural Changes**: Layered structure with alternating Nb-rich and W-rich layers of 5–15 μm thickness; formed by cyclic competitive adsorption/reduction of WCl₆ and NbCl₅; Nb₂C precipitates preferentially in Nb-rich layers; layered structure separates Nb₂C.
- **Property Improvements**: Layered structure inhibits Nb₂C coarsening during carbide formation, helping maintain fine carbides and strength at 1400 °C.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Layered structure disappears at 1600 °C due to elemental homogenization, leading to Nb₂C merging/coarsening and rapid grain growth.

# Key Conclusions

1. A new kind of Nb-W-C alloy with special structure was prepared by CVD combined with homogenizing heat treatment. The microstructure is composed of columnar crystals with specific orientation, needle-like Nb₂C second phase, and layered structure with Nb/W composition segregation.

2. The layered structure effectively separates Nb₂C and inhibits size coarsening during carbide formation. As heat treatment temperature increases to 1600 °C, Nb₂C gradually merges and grows due to the disappearance of the layered structure.

3. The tensile strength of the sample reaches 670 MPa after homogenizing treatment at 1400 °C for 6 h, which is about 35–45% higher than the as-deposited Nb-W-C alloy and the existing widely used Nb521 alloy produced by melting-forging-annealing technology. Excessively high heat treatment temperature coarsens grains and carbides, changing fracture mode from cleavage to intergranular fracture.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Processing method limitation**: The alloy is prepared by CVD, which is distinct from conventional melting-based methods. The layered structure and columnar grain morphology are unique to CVD. Conclusions on strengthening mechanisms may not directly transfer to arc-melted or powder-metallurgy Nb alloys.

- **Composition scope**: Only one composition (10 wt.% W, 2.5 wt.% C) is studied. No composition optimization or comparison across multiple Nb-W-C compositions is provided. The original paper does not investigate effects of varying W or C content.

- **Mechanical testing scope**: Only room-temperature tensile tests are performed. High-temperature strength (despite being a refractory alloy), creep, fatigue, fracture toughness, and compressive properties are not reported. No strain rate effects are investigated.

- **Oxidation behavior**: Not investigated at all. The original paper does not provide oxidation weight gain, oxide layer analysis, or protective coating discussion for the Nb-W-C alloy.

- **Substrate influence**: The alloy is deposited on Mo substrate; the upper and lower parts of deposited bulk are cut off. However, the paper does not explicitly state whether Mo diffusion into the Nb-W-C alloy occurs during CVD or heat treatment. Mo is not listed in Table 1 chemical composition, so its presence is presumed negligible or undetected.

- **Elastic modulus and density**: Elastic modulus is described qualitatively as "slightly higher" after homogenization, but no numerical value is given. Density of the Nb-W-C alloy is not reported.

- **Quantitative separation of strengthening mechanisms**: The paper attributes strength to combined effects of solid-solution strengthening (W), carbide precipitation strengthening (Nb₂C), and grain refinement, but does not quantify individual contributions; only the Ashby-Orowan equation is cited without explicit calculated numerical values for Δσ in the extracted text.

- **Recrystallization temperature**: Estimated to be lower than 1400 °C according to 0.4 × melting point of the alloy, making the alloy prone to recrystallization at treatment temperatures. This limits the use of high-temperature homogenization.

- **Sample size**: Tensile sample thickness is 2 mm; the deposited bulk is 4 mm thick after cutting 1 mm from each side. This limits available material volume and may not represent bulk-scale mechanical behavior.
