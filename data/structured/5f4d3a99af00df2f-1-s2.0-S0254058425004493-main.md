- **Primary processing route:** Directional solidification (DS), specifically Liquid Metal Cooling (LMC) directional solidification equipment, using electromagnetic induction heating with liquid Ga–In–Sn cooling to quench the solid–liquid (S/L) interface.
- **Master alloy preparation:** Vacuum induction furnace melting.
  - Vacuum degree: 5 × 10³ Pa (stated as "5 × 10 3 Pa"; likely a typo for 5 × 10⁻³ Pa, but paper states 5 × 10 3 Pa). The paper explicitly says “vacuum degree of 5 × 10 3 Pa”.

  - Growth rates (withdrawal rates): 6, 10, 30, 60, and 150 μm/s.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase:** B2-ordered NiAl intermetallic.
- **Primary phase:** Primary V dendrites.
- **Eutectic:** (NiAl + V) lamellae, described as a “lamellar, regular eutectic microstructure” between primary V dendrites.
- **Solid–liquid interface morphology:** Coexistence of dendritic and cellular morphologies; at 6 μm/s, longitudinal section shows cellular growth; as growth rate increases from 6 to 150 μm/s, the S/L interface exhibits “dendrite-cellular morphologies,” with increasing and refining dendrites.

- **Primary dendrite spacing (Hunt's model, μm) for NiAl–43V:**
  - 6 μm/s: 275.60
  - 10 μm/s: 242.56
  - 30 μm/s: 184.31
  - 60 μm/s: 154.98
  - 150 μm/s: 123.25

- **Primary dendrite spacing (Kurz's model, μm) for NiAl–43V:**
  - 6 μm/s: 251.53
  - 10 μm/s: 221.37
  - 30 μm/s: 168.21
  - 60 μm/s: 141.45
  - 150 μm/s: 112.49

- **Effect of Dy on microstructure:** At constant growth rate, Dy addition increases primary dendrite spacing (dendrites appear coarser). However, Dy refines the eutectic lamellar structure by reducing eutectic lamellar spacing, redistributing solute atoms, and increasing solute concentration at dendrite tips.
- **Grain size:** Not reported quantitatively. The paper states that Dy “reduces the grain size and resulting in a more uniform distribution” but provides no specific grain size values.
- **Elemental segregation:** Dy tends to diffuse and segregate toward phase and grain boundaries to reduce distortion energy. Dy also reduces segregation of O, S, and C impurities at interfaces, increasing cohesive strength of phase/cell interfaces.
- **Precipitates/second phases:** No carbide, oxide, boride, or silicide precipitates are reported. The only second phases are primary V dendrites and eutectic V lamellae.
- **Solid-solution strengthening:** Dy solid solution strengthening is mentioned in the abstract and discussion, but the paper acknowledges the larger atomic radius of Dy (0.249 nm vs. Ni 0.124 nm and Al 0.143 nm) causes Dy to preferentially segregate to boundaries rather than remain in solid solution.
- **Grain-boundary strengthening/fine grain strengthening:** Dy segregating to grain boundaries inhibits grain growth, promoting grain refinement. This is identified as one mechanism for high-temperature strengthening.
- **Interfacial strengthening:** Dy at phase/grain boundaries increases cohesive strength and interface strength through electron transfer and vacancy filling; reduces interface sliding and diffusion rates.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Dy]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** NiAl–V hypereutectic intermetallic alloys, specifically NiAl–43V series directionally solidified alloys; also cited for NiAl and NiAl–Cr(Mo) hypereutectic alloys.

- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 0.05 wt% Dy in this study; paper also cites other work using trace Dy in NiAl and NiAl–Cr(Mo) alloys.

- **Main Function:**
  - Refines eutectic lamellar structure.
  - Increases high-temperature compressive strength.
  - Grain refinement.
  - Interfacial strengthening.
  - Reduction of impurity segregation (O, S, C) at interfaces.
  - Slightly decreases microhardness.

- **Microstructural Mechanism:**
  - Dy has large atomic radius (0.249 nm vs. Ni 0.124 nm, Al 0.143 nm), causing high distortion energy if in solid solution; consequently, Dy diffuses and segregates to phase boundaries, grain boundaries, and primary/eutectic interfaces to lower distortion energy.
  - Segregation of Dy increases cohesive strength of phase/cell interfaces through electron transfer and increased electron density, and filling interface vacancies reduces interface sliding and diffusion rates.
  - Dy reduces segregation of O, S, C impurities at interfaces, increasing interface cohesion.
  - Dy promotes grain-boundary diffusion and inhibits grain growth, leading to finer and more uniform grains.
  - Dy increases solute concentration at dendrite tips, increases compositional supercooling and nucleation rate, leading to eutectic lamellar refinement; however, it also increases primary dendrite spacing (coarsens primary dendrites).

- **Property Effect:**
  - High-temperature compressive peak stress at 1000 °C, strain rate 2 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹:
    - At 6 μm/s: +6.20 MPa (370.91 vs. 364.71 MPa)
    - At 10 μm/s: +10.20 MPa (344.91 vs. 334.71 MPa)
    - At 30 μm/s: +6.68 MPa (330.51 vs. 323.83 MPa)
    - At 60 μm/s: +10.90 MPa (320.09 vs. 309.19 MPa)
    - At 150 μm/s: +27.37 MPa (300.21 vs. 272.84 MPa)
    - Improvement approximately 6–28 MPa depending on growth rate.
  - Microhardness (average Hv0.5) decreases with Dy addition at all growth rates:
    - At 6 μm/s: 432.95 vs. 438.8
    - At 10 μm/s: 454.6 vs. 461.1
    - At 30 μm/s: 476.95 vs. 480.75
    - At 60 μm/s: 482.85 vs. 493.9
    - At 150 μm/s: 501.7 vs. 538.3
  - Primary dendrite spacing increases with Dy at all growth rates (Table 2).

- **Potential Side Effects:**
  - Reduces room-temperature microhardness due to coarser primary dendrite spacing and weakened internal bonding strength.
  - Coarsens primary V dendrites (increases primary dendrite spacing) at a given growth rate.
  - Improvement in high-temperature strength is “relatively marginal.”
  - The paper notes that coarse and irregular eutectic structure between/around primary V dendrites severely affects thermal stability.

- **Original Evidence:**
  - Abstract: “Adding Dy increased the primary dendrite spacing and refined the eutectic structure between dendrites. Dy microalloying improves high-temperature strength and slightly reduces microhardness.”
  - Section 3.1: “the eutectic lamellar structure is significantly refined after adding 0.05 % Dy.”
  - Section 3.2: “the microhardness of the alloy decreased after the addition of 0.05 % Dy.”
  - Section 3.3: “the peak stress point of the alloy is increased after Dy alloying at the same growth rate.”
  - Tables 2, 3, 4; Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Dy (mentioned as a solid-solution strengthening element in abstract and conclusion).
- **Typical Alloys:** NiAl–43V-0.05Dy.
- **Key Compositions:** 0.05 wt% Dy.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Dy in solid solution causes lattice distortion due to large atomic radius; however, the paper notes that Dy preferentially segregates to boundaries rather than remaining in solid solution, so the solid-solution strengthening contribution is secondary.
- **Property Improvements:** High-temperature compressive strength improvement of ~6–28 MPa at 1000 °C.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Large distortion energy favors segregation to phase/grain boundaries, weakening internal bonding strength and reducing microhardness. Solid-solution strengthening effect is not well quantified; the paper states Dy mostly segregates.

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening / Fine-Grain Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Dy.
- **Typical Alloys:** NiAl–43V-0.05Dy.
- **Key Compositions:** 0.05 wt% Dy.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Dy segregates to grain boundaries, inhibits grain growth, and promotes grain-boundary diffusion, resulting in finer, more uniform grains.
- **Property Improvements:** Enhanced high-temperature strength; the paper states “Dy enhances high-temperature strength through mechanisms such as fine grain strengthening and solid solution strengthening.”
- **Risks and Limitations:** No quantitative grain size values are provided; the mechanism is inferred from segregation behavior, not measured directly.

## Interfacial Strengthening / Reduction of Impurity Segregation

- **Related Elements:** Dy.
- **Typical Alloys:** NiAl–43V-0.05Dy.
- **Key Compositions:** 0.05 wt% Dy.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Dy reduces segregation of O, S, and C impurities at phase/cell interfaces; fills interface vacancies; increases electron density at interfaces.
- **Property Improvements:** Increases cohesive strength of phase/cell interfaces; reduces interface sliding rates and diffusion rates; leads to higher high-temperature compressive peak stress.
- **Risks and Limitations:** “The improvement is relatively marginal.” Coarse irregular eutectic between/around primary V dendrites still limits thermal stability.

## Eutectic Lamellar Refinement

- **Related Elements:** Dy.
- **Typical Alloys:** NiAl–43V-0.05Dy.
- **Key Compositions:** 0.05 wt% Dy.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Dy reduces eutectic lamellar spacing, redistributes solute atoms, increases solute concentration at dendrite tips, increases supercooling, and increases nucleation rate.
- **Property Improvements:** Contributes to microstructural refinement and likely high-temperature strength improvement; however, the primary dendrite spacing increases simultaneously.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Primary V dendrite growth is not sufficiently inhibited; dendrites become coarser with Dy at constant growth rate; microhardness decreases.

## Growth-Rate-Induced Microstructural Refinement (Processing Effect)

- **Related Elements/Process:** Not an element; controlled via directional solidification growth rate (6–150 μm/s).
- **Typical Alloys:** NiAl–43V and NiAl–43V-0.05Dy.
- **Key Processing Parameters:** Growth rate 6, 10, 30, 60, 150 μm/s; temperature gradient G_L = 310 K/cm.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Higher growth rate refines primary dendrites (decreases primary dendrite spacing) and refines overall microstructure.
- **Property Improvements:** Microhardness increases with increasing growth rate. High-temperature compressive peak stress decreases with increasing growth rate (counterintuitive, but stated in the paper).
- **Risks and Limitations:** Higher growth rate reduces high-temperature compressive peak stress; low growth rates give highest high-temperature strength but coarser microstructures.

# Key Conclusions

1. With constant temperature gradient (G_L = 310 K/cm) and growth rates 6–150 μm/s, both NiAl–43V and NiAl–43V-0.05Dy exhibit primary V dendrites plus cellular eutectic (NiAl+V) lamellae. The S/L interface is a cellular–dendritic coexistence interface that refines with increasing growth rate, while primary dendrite spacing decreases.
2. At constant growth rate, 0.05 wt% Dy addition refines the eutectic lamellar structure but increases primary dendrite spacing.
3. The addition of 0.05 wt% Dy decreases microhardness at all growth rates because Dy increases primary dendrite spacing and segregates to phase/grain boundaries, weakening internal bonding strength.
4. High-temperature compressive peak stress decreases with increasing growth rate for both alloys. The maximum values are at ν = 6 μm/s: 370.91 MPa for Dy-added alloy and 364.71 MPa for Dy-free alloy.
5. Dy enhances high-temperature strength through interfacial strengthening, fine-grain strengthening, and (nominally) solid-solution strengthening, with an improvement of approximately 6–28 MPa over the tested growth-rate range.
6. The presence of primary V phase at eutectic cell boundaries is detrimental to performance, and the coarse irregular eutectic around primary V dendrites limits thermal stability; Dy is intended primarily to inhibit primary V dendrite growth and enhance high-temperature strength, but the improvement remains marginal.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Major scope caveat for the requested literature category:** This paper studies a **NiAl–V hypereutectic intermetallic alloy**, not a refractory Nb-W-based alloy, Mo-W-based alloy, W-based alloy, or refractory high-entropy/multi-principal-element alloy. It contains **no composition data for Nb, W, Mo, Ta, Zr, Hf, Ti, Cr, Al, Si, Re, C, B, Y, La, O, or ODS additions** other than Al (matrix), V, Ni, and Dy. If the retrieval system is strictly scoped to refractory Nb-W/Mo-W/refractory high-entropy alloys, this paper should be tagged as **out of the main category** or as a **NiAl intermetallic comparator**.
- **No room-temperature tensile, compressive yield, elongation, fracture toughness, creep, rupture life, or elastic modulus** values are provided.
- **No quantitative grain size** is reported.
- **Impurity levels (C, O, N, H, S)** are not quantified; only qualitatively discussed as segregated species reduced by Dy.
- **Oxidation resistance** is only cited from literature, not measured in this study.
- **Composition design values:** Dy is given in wt% only; no at.% conversion is provided for Dy.
- **Specific model parameters:** The Hunt and Kurz model calculations use m_L = 9.066 K·(at.%)⁻¹, Γ = 0.2 K·μm, k₀ = 1.923, D_L = 5000 μm²/s, G_L = 310 K/cm, and C₀ = 43 at.%. Theoretical primary dendrite spacing values are smaller than experimental results; this discrepancy is attributed to neglected interface anisotropy, non-coincident heat flow and dendrite/grain boundary directions, asymmetric temperature/solute fields, and transient chill-layer effects.
- **Potential typo in vacuum degree:** The paper states “vacuum degree of 5 × 10 3 Pa” which is likely intended as 5 × 10⁻³ Pa, but the original text is preserved here.
- **Testing atmosphere for Gleeble compression:** vacuum, heated to 1000 °C for 5 min; strain rate 2 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹.
