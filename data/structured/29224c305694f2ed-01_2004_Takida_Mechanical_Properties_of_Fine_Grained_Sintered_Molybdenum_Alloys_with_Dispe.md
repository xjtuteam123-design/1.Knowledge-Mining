# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Mechanical Properties of Fine-Grained, Sintered Molybdenum Alloys with Dispersed Particles Developed by Mechanical Alloying
- **Authors**: Tomohiro Takida, Hiroaki Kurishita, Mamoru Mabuchi, Tadashi Igarashi, Yoshiharu Doi, Takekazu Nagae
- **Journal**: Materials Transactions, Vol. 45, No. 1
- **Year**: 2004
- **DOI**: Not provided in the paper text
- **Alloy system studied**: Mo-ZrC and Mo-TaC particle-dispersed molybdenum alloys
- **Material category**: Mo-based alloy, carbide particle-dispersed Mo alloy, fine-grained sintered Mo alloy
- **Research objective**: To develop Mo alloys with improved low-temperature toughness and room- and high-temperature strength without any plastic working after consolidation, by fabricating fine-grained, particle-dispersed Mo alloys via mechanical alloying followed by HIP or SPS.

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies molybdenum (Mo)-based alloys with dispersed transition metal carbide particles (ZrC or TaC). The material category is **Mo-based particle-dispersed alloys** fabricated by mechanical alloying followed by consolidation (HIP or SPS). The research focuses on the effects of fine grain size and carbide particle dispersion on low-temperature toughness (DBTT), room-temperature strength, high-temperature strength, and superplastic behavior.

Reference materials used for comparison:
- Mo(M/H): Pure Mo fabricated by MA and HIP
- Mo(R): Pure Mo sheet recrystallized at 2070 K
- TEM: Mo-0.3La₂O₃ sheet recrystallized at 2370 K (trade name)

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Mechanical alloying (MA):**
- Mixed powders in argon atmosphere
- Planetary type ball mill
- Duration: 108 ks (30 hours)
- Pots and balls: Cemented carbide (WC-Co)
- Ball-to-powder weight ratio: 6.5
- Disk rotation speed: 230 rpm
- Imposed energy: 5G
- No solvents or additives used

**Consolidation methods:**

*HIP (Hot Isostatic Pressing) - designated as (M/H):*
- Temperature: 1570 K (0.54 Tm)
- Pressure: 147 MPa
- Duration: 10.8 ks (3 hours)
- Atmosphere: Argon

*SPS (Spark Plasma Sintering) - designated as (M/S):*
- Temperature: 2070 K (0.72 Tm)
- Pressure: 74 MPa
- Duration: 0.3 ks (5 minutes)
- Atmosphere: Reduced pressure (10 Pa)

**Post-consolidation annealing:**
- Temperature range: 1870 to 2470 K
- Duration: 3.6 ks (1 hour)
- Vacuum: 2.7 × 10⁻⁴ Pa
- Purpose: To examine thermal stability of microstructures and control grain size

**Reference material processing:**
- Mo(R): Pure Mo sheet recrystallized at 2070 K
- TEM: Mo-La₂O₃ sheet recrystallized at 2370 K

**Notable aspect:** No plastic working (forging, rolling) was applied after consolidation—all properties were achieved in the as-consolidated state.

# Mechanical Properties

## Low-Temperature Toughness (Impact Bending)

Three-point impact bending tests at temperatures from 270 to 470 K, deformation rate 5 m/s, bend bar specimens 1 mm wide × 20 mm long × 1 mm thick, supporting span length 12.5 mm.

**Key DBTT results:**

*As-sintered condition:*
- TAC08(M/H): DBTT lower than TEM by approximately 30 K; upper shelf energy (USE) about twice that of TEM
- TAC08(M/S) and ZRC08(M/S): Slightly lower DBTT and higher USE than Mo(R); appreciably higher DBTT and higher USE than TEM
- TaC addition provides more beneficial effect on decreasing DBTT than ZrC addition
- ZRC08(M/H) has the highest DBTT among the carbide-added alloys

*Annealed condition (2070 K, 3.6 ks):*
- DBTT of TAC08(M/S) decreased by approximately 90 K
- DBTT of ZRC08(M/S) decreased by approximately 40 K
- No significant change in DBTT of TAC08(M/H) after annealing
- TAC08 (both M/H and M/S) shows lower DBTT than TEM
- All carbide-added alloys have much lower DBTT than Mo(R)

**Specific DBTT comparison (from Fig. 4):**
- HIPed TAC08 (TAC08(M/H)): DBTT lower by 30–40 K than recrystallized Mo-La₂O₃ (TEM)
- All carbide-added Mo alloys in annealed condition: much lower DBTT than Mo(R)

**Key room-temperature findings:**
- As-sintered ZRC08 tensile strength was 1.8 times higher than Mo(R), but no plastic deformation observed
- Annealed ZRC08 yield strength 3 times higher and tensile strength 2.3 times higher than Mo(R)
- TAC08(M/S) yield and tensile strengths about 1.5 times higher than Mo(R)
- ZRC08(M/S) annealed showed 9% elongation, significantly better than ZRC08(M/H) (0.6%)

## High-Temperature Tensile Properties

Test temperatures: 1170 to 1970 K (0.40–0.68 Tm)
Initial strain rates: 4.2 × 10⁻⁵ to 8.3 × 10⁻² s⁻¹
Atmosphere: Argon

**Key high-temperature findings:**
- ZRC08 and TAC08 maintain high levels of strength up to about 1800 K
- Tensile strengths 3 to 5 times higher than Mo(R) and TEM below 1470 K (= 0.51 Tm)
- Tensile strengths 1.5 to 2 times higher at 1770 K (= 0.61 Tm)
- At 1970 K (= 0.68 Tm), ZRC08 and TAC08 show slightly lower tensile strength than Mo(R) and TEM, probably due to grain boundary sliding
- Strength shows strong temperature dependence, attributed to decrease in grain-size strengthening effect with increasing temperature due to enhanced diffusion
- ZRC08(M/S) with grain size 3.0 μm showed elongation sharply increased above 1770 K, reaching 180% at 1970 K

## Superplastic Behavior

- ZRC08(M/H)* with grain size 0.3 μm deformed at 1770 K at 8.3 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹
- Large elongation of 551% attained with uniform elongation
- Strain-rate sensitivity (m value) at 1770 K: 0.41 at strain rates below 10⁻² s⁻¹
- Superplastic deformation attributed to fine grain structure (0.3 μm)

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

The paper does **not** directly address oxidation resistance or oxidation behavior. The focus is on mechanical properties, thermal stability of microstructure, and superplastic behavior.

**Thermal stability (microstructural stability):**
- No significant grain growth even after annealing at 2470 K for 3.6 ks
- Attributed to the pinning effect of dispersed particles (ZrC or TaC) against grain boundary migration
- ZRC08 exhibits particularly excellent microstructural stability

**High-temperature mechanical stability:**
- Strength maintained up to approximately 1800 K
- At 1970 K (0.68 Tm), softening occurs, attributed to grain boundary sliding
- Oxygen content of carbide-added alloys: approximately 0.09–0.14 mass%
- No discussion of oxidation weight gain, oxide layer formation, or oxidation mechanisms in this paper

## [Grain-Boundary Strengthening by Carbide Particles]

**Related Elements:** ZrC, TaC, TiC dispersed particles

**Typical Alloys:** Mo-0.8ZrC (ZRC08), Mo-0.8TaC (TAC08), Mo-TiC

**Key Compositions:** 0.8 mol% ZrC or TaC; 1 mol% TiC

**Microstructural Changes:**
- Fine dispersed carbide particles pin grain boundaries
- Grain growth suppressed up to 2470 K for 3.6 ks
- Fine-grained Mo matrix (0.3–11.2 μm) maintained
- Semicoherency between carbide particles and Mo matrix (from prior work)

**Property Improvements:**
- Low-temperature toughness: DBTT of TAC08(M/H) is 30–40 K lower than Mo-La₂O₃ (TEM)
- Room-temperature yield strength: 1015–1038 MPa (annealed ZRC08), 3 times higher than Mo(R)
- Room-temperature UTS: 1053–1077 MPa (annealed ZRC08), 2.3 times higher than Mo(R)
- High-temperature strength: 3–5 times higher than Mo(R) below 1470 K; 1.5–2 times higher at 1770 K

- At 1970 K (0.68 Tm), strength becomes slightly lower than Mo(R) and TEM due to grain boundary sliding
- Strong temperature dependence of strength due to decreased grain-size strengthening at high temperatures
- Mechanism of weak grain boundary strengthening by TaC and ZrC not fully understood (stated in paper as speculative)

**Original Evidence:**

## [Grain Refinement / Grain-Size Strengthening (Hall-Petch)]

**Related Elements:** All alloying additions contribute indirectly through particle-dispersed grain refinement

**Typical Alloys:** Mo-0.8ZrC, Mo-0.8TaC

**Key Compositions:** Fine grain sizes of 0.3–11.2 μm (vs. 53.8 μm for recrystallized pure Mo)

**Microstructural Changes:**
- Grain size reduction from 53.8 μm (Mo(R)) to 0.3–2.8 μm (ZRC08) or 10.9–11.2 μm (TAC08(M/S))
- Grain size maintained by dispersed particle pinning
- M/H specimens show smaller grain size than M/S specimens due to higher particle number density

**Property Improvements:**
- Room-temperature yield strength increase by ~3 times (ZRC08 annealed: 1015–1038 MPa vs. Mo(R): 344 MPa)
- Room-temperature UTS increase by ~2.3 times (ZRC08 annealed: 1053–1077 MPa vs. Mo(R): 469 MPa)
- Strength enhancement up to approximately 1800 K

**Risks and Limitations:**
- Grain-size strengthening effect decreases with increasing temperature due to enhanced diffusion
- At 1970 K, benefits of grain refinement are overcome by grain boundary sliding, causing strength drop below that of coarse-grained Mo(R) and TEM
- Reduced room-temperature ductility for very fine-grained alloys (ZRC08 as-sintered: 0% elongation)

**Original Evidence:**
- "Mo exhibits a strong grain size dependence of yield stress because of a high elastic modulus."
- "The achieved high strength of the Mo alloys mainly stems from grain refinement"
- "The observed strong temperature dependence of the strength is likely attributed to decrease in the grain-size strengthening effect with increasing temperature due to enhanced diffusion."
- Fig. 3 and Table 2 support these findings

## [Orowan Dislocation-Particle Interaction]

**Related Elements:** ZrC, TaC dispersed particles

**Typical Alloys:** Mo-0.8ZrC, Mo-0.8TaC

**Key Compositions:** 0.8 mol% carbide addition

**Microstructural Changes:**
- Fine dispersed particles act as obstacles to dislocation motion

# Key Conclusions

1. **Successful fabrication without plastic working:** Thick bulky sintered Mo alloys with high strength and high toughness were successfully fabricated by HIP or SPS using MA-processed powders of pure Mo with 0.8 mol% ZrC or TaC. Excellent mechanical properties were obtained for the first time in the as-consolidated state without subsequent plastic working.

2. **Microstructural thermal stability:** The fabricated alloys exhibited no significant grain growth even after annealing at 2470 K for 3.6 ks, due to the pinning effect of dispersed ZrC or TaC particles against grain boundary migration. ZRC08 showed particularly excellent microstructural stability.

3. **Low-temperature toughness:** DBTTs of all carbide-added Mo alloys were much lower than that of recrystallized pure Mo. TaC-added Mo alloy (TAC08) showed the lowest DBTT, considerably lower (by 30–40 K) than recrystallized Mo-La₂O₃ (TEM). TaC addition is more effective than ZrC addition in reducing DBTT.

4. **Room-temperature strength:** ZrC-added Mo alloys (ZRC08) showed the highest room-temperature strength: yield strength 1015–1038 MPa and UTS 1053–1077 MPa in annealed condition (approximately 3× and 2.3× that of Mo(R), respectively).

5. **High-temperature strength:** ZRC08 and TAC08 maintained high strength up to approximately 1800 K: 3–5 times higher than Mo(R) below 1470 K and 1.5–2 times higher at 1770 K. However, at 1970 K, strength fell slightly below that of Mo(R) and TEM due to grain boundary sliding.

6. **Superplastic behavior:** ZRC08(M/H) with 0.3 μm grain size exhibited 551% elongation at 1770 K with strain-rate sensitivity of 0.41, demonstrating superplasticity in fine-grained Mo alloys.

7. **Origin of superior properties:** The excellent mechanical properties are attributable to fine-grained microstructure and grain-boundary strengthening by fine particles.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations:**
1. The paper does not address oxidation resistance or oxidation behavior of the Mo alloys—a critical limitation for high-temperature structural applications.
2. The correlation between low-temperature toughness and microstructural parameters (relative density or grain size) is not clear. The paper acknowledges conflicting observations (e.g., TAC08(M/H) has the lowest DBTT but lowest relative density; ZRC08(M/H) has highest DBTT despite smallest grain size and higher density).
3. The mechanism by which TaC vs. ZrC additions differently affect weak grain boundary strengthening is speculative and not well understood.
4. High-temperature strength degrades significantly above 0.68 Tm due to grain boundary sliding, limiting the upper-use temperature.
5. Room-temperature ductility of annealed ZRC08(M/H) is very limited (0.6% elongation).
6. The paper does not provide detailed TEM characterization of particle-matrix interface coherency for TaC and ZrC (semicoherency was reported for TiC in prior work).
7. The observed large difference in annealing response between M/H and M/S specimens (particularly for TAC08) is attributed to insufficient precipitation during short SPS holding time, but this is inferred rather than directly confirmed by microstructural analysis.

**Applicable Scope:**
- Mo-based alloys for high-temperature structural applications requiring fine-grained microstructures without plastic working
- Mo alloys with dispersed ZrC or TaC particles fabricated by MA + HIP or MA + SPS
- Applications requiring thermal stability up to 2470 K
- Structural materials where bulk thickness is required (not limited to thin sheets or wires)
- The conclusions apply to Mo-0.8 mol% ZrC and Mo-0.8 mol% TaC compositions specifically, and may not directly transfer to other carbide types or concentrations
