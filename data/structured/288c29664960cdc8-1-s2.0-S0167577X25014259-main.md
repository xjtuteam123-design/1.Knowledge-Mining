# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Base alloy:** GH4099, a γ′ phase-reinforced nickel-based superalloy
- **Manufacturing route:** Selective Laser Melting (SLM), also referred to as laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) in related literature cited
- **Research focus:** Effect of two solution treatment temperatures (1080 °C and 1120 °C) followed by standard aging at 900 °C on microstructure and 900 °C tensile properties
- **Notable characteristic of GH4099:** The paper states that GH4099 has attracted widespread attention owing to its excellent SLM processability and high-temperature strength, in contrast to many other γ′ phase reinforced nickel-based superalloys which frequently exhibit micro-cracks during SLM processing.

## Heat Treatment Regimes
Two solution treatment schedules, both followed by the same aging treatment:

**SA1:** 1080 °C × 1 h / FC + 900 °C × 5 h / FC
- Solution treatment at 1080 °C for 1 hour, furnace cooling
- Aging at 900 °C for 5 hours, furnace cooling

**SA2:** 1120 °C × 1 h / FC + 900 °C × 5 h / FC
- Solution treatment at 1120 °C for 1 hour, furnace cooling
- Aging at 900 °C for 5 hours, furnace cooling

**Cooling method:** Furnace cooling (FC) for both solution and aging steps in both regimes.

## Microstructure Discussion Points
- Higher solution temperature (SA2) promotes recovery and grain growth.
- The higher percentage of HAGBs in SA2 is stated to enhance ductility by providing more effective barriers to dislocation movement and facilitating grain boundary sliding during deformation.
- The less elongated grain morphology in SA2 reduces mechanical anisotropy, potentially enabling more uniform plastic deformation.
- The 1080 °C solution treatment was insufficient to fully eliminate directional microstructural features and residual stresses inherited from the SLM process.
- The 1120 °C solution treatment resulted in more complete dissolution of pre-existing γ′ precipitates and other phases during solutioning, providing a more homogeneous supersaturated solid solution for uniform nucleation and isotropic growth during aging.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

## [Al (Aluminum)]

**Applicable Alloy System:** GH4099 Ni-based superalloy (SLM-fabricated)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 2.23 wt.%

**Main Function:** Primary γ′ phase-forming element (together with Ti). Al combines with Ni to form Ni₃(Al,Ti) γ′ precipitates, which are the main strengthening phase in this alloy.

**Microstructural Mechanism:** During solution treatment, γ′ precipitates dissolve to form a supersaturated solid solution; subsequent aging leads to reprecipitation. The solution temperature significantly affects γ′ reprecipitation: SA1 (1080 °C, insufficient dissolution) results in directionally-aligned, elongated γ′ precipitates influenced by residual strain fields; SA2 (1120 °C, more complete dissolution) leads to homogeneous supersaturated solid solution, promoting uniform nucleation and isotropic growth of spherical γ′ precipitates.

**Property Effect:** The smaller, spherical, uniformly distributed γ′ precipitates in SA2 provide more effective strengthening, correlating with yield strength of 450 MPa vs. 358 MPa at 900 °C (26% improvement).

**Potential Side Effects:** When γ′ precipitates are elongated and directionally aligned (SA1), they provide less effective strengthening and may contribute to mechanical anisotropy.

**Original Evidence:** "The higher solution temperature in SA-2 resulted in more complete dissolution of pre-existing γ′ precipitates and other phases during solutioning. This provides a more homogeneous supersaturated solid solution, promoting uniform nucleation and isotropic growth of precipitates during the subsequent aging treatment, resulting in the spherical morphology."

# Key Conclusions

1. Solution treatment temperature significantly influences precipitate morphology, grain boundary characteristics, and carbide evolution in SLM-fabricated GH4099 alloy, which collectively determine the mechanical performance at elevated temperatures.

2. The 1120 °C solution treatment (SA2) proved optimal compared to 1080 °C (SA1), producing a microstructure characterized by:
   - Uniformly distributed spherical γ′ precipitates (average size 107.9 nm vs. 133.2 nm)
   - More homogeneous carbide networks
   - Higher proportion of high-angle grain boundaries (45.1% vs. 38.3%)
   - Reduced crystallographic texture
   - Larger average grain size (92 μm vs. 72 μm)

3. At 900 °C tensile testing, SA2 demonstrated:
   - 26% higher yield strength (450 MPa vs. 358 MPa)
   - 17% higher ultimate tensile strength (454 MPa vs. 387 MPa)
   - 41% greater elongation (8.1% vs. 5.8%)

4. The superior strength of SA2 is attributed to smaller, more uniformly distributed spherical γ′ precipitates (vs. elongated, directionally-aligned precipitates in SA1).

5. The enhanced ductility of SA2 is attributed to the higher HAGB proportion and less elongated grain morphology, which facilitate grain boundary sliding and reduce mechanical anisotropy.

6. The 1080 °C solution treatment was insufficient to fully eliminate directional microstructural features and residual stresses inherited from the SLM process, resulting in elongated, directionally-aligned γ′ precipitates and discrete carbide morphologies that created stress concentration points.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Alloy system limitation:** This paper studies a Ni-based superalloy (GH4099), NOT a refractory high-temperature alloy, Nb-W-based alloy, Mo-W-based alloy, or refractory high-entropy alloy. The findings are not directly transferable to refractory alloy systems.

2. **Heat treatment scope:** Only two solution treatment temperatures (1080 °C and 1120 °C) were examined, both with the same aging treatment (900 °C × 5 h / FC). The full heat treatment parameter space (other temperatures, times, cooling rates) was not explored.

3. **Mechanical property testing:** Only high-temperature tensile testing at 900 °C was performed. Room-temperature mechanical properties, creep resistance, fatigue behavior, fracture toughness, and oxidation resistance were not evaluated.

4. **Oxidation resistance:** The paper does not include any oxidation testing or oxidation layer characterization, despite the high-temperature application context.

5. **Microstructure analysis depth:** The paper does not provide detailed transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis of γ′ precipitate structure, carbide crystallography, or dislocation-precipitate interactions. The M₂₃C₆ carbide identification is stated as "likely" based on compositional analysis, not confirmed by diffraction.

6. **Mo and W roles:** The specific contributions of Mo and W to solid-solution strengthening are not individually analyzed or quantified.

7. **Long-term service stability:** No long-term thermal exposure tests or microstructural stability assessments beyond the described heat treatments were performed.

8. **Data discrepancy note:** There appears to be an inconsistency between Table 1 in the original paper and the text regarding which value corresponds to yield strength vs. ultimate tensile strength. The text descriptions (SA1: YS 358 MPa, UTS 387 MPa; SA2: YS 450 MPa, UTS 454 MPa) have been used as the authoritative source in this extraction.

9. **Cooling rate effect:** The paper uses furnace cooling for both solution and aging steps. The effect of cooling rate on γ′ precipitate size and morphology was not investigated.

10. **Build orientation effects:** Tensile specimens were machined perpendicular to the build direction. The effect of build orientation on mechanical properties after heat treatment was not comprehensively evaluated.

11. **Atmosphere during testing:** The atmosphere (air, vacuum, inert gas) during high-temperature tensile testing at 900 °C is not explicitly stated in the original paper.

12. **Relevance to refractory alloys:** While this paper is outside the primary scope of refractory alloy literature, it provides useful methodological reference for how heat treatment parameters can be systematically investigated in additively manufactured high-temperature alloys, and how microstructure-property correlations can be established.
