# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Machine learning accelerated design of a family of AlxCrFeNi medium entropy alloys with superior high temperature mechanical and oxidation properties
- **Authors**: Ling Qiao, R.V. Ramanujan, Jingchuan Zhu
- **Journal**: Corrosion Science
- **Year**: 2023 (Available online 21 November 2022)
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.corsci.2022.110805
- **Alloy system studied**: AlxCrFeNi medium-entropy alloys (MEAs)
- **Research objective**: To use machine learning (GRNN model) to map the relationship between temperature, alloying elements, and yield strength, then develop AlxCrFeNi MEAs with superior high-temperature mechanical and oxidation properties.
- **Material category**: Medium-entropy alloy (MEA) / multi-principal element alloy (Co-free Al–Cr–Fe–Ni system)

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy family**: AlxCrFeNi medium-entropy alloys (Co-free)
- **Compositions studied**: x = 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2
- **Nominal composition**: Al–Cr–Fe–Ni system with near-equimolar ratio of Cr, Fe, Ni and varying Al content
- **Application target**: High-temperature structural applications (aerospace, gas-turbine engines, transport)
- **Key design goal**: Low density, high melting point, high specific yield strength at elevated temperatures, excellent oxidation resistance at 1000 °C

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Mixed BCC structure: disordered A2 phase (Fe–Cr rich) + ordered B2 phase (Ni–Al rich). The dual-phase structure formed via spinodal decomposition.
- **Second phases/precipitates**: None reported (no carbides, oxides, borides, silicides)
- **Grain size**: Not explicitly quantified in the original paper
- **Grain boundary features**: Typical grain boundaries observed in Al0.5CrFeNi; lamellar structures near grain boundaries
- **Elemental segregation**: 
  - Al–Ni enriched phases (B2 ordered) within grains
  - Fe–Cr enriched phases (A2 disordered) near grain boundaries / interdendritic regions
  - With x ≤ 0.3: dendritic structure; interdendritic regions depleted in Al
  - With x ≥ 0.5: lamellar and maze-shaped spinodal structures (alternating interconnected dual-phase)
- **Precipitate size and distribution**: Not reported (no precipitate phases)
- **Strengthening mechanism discussed**: Two-phase strengthening from ordered B2 (Ni–Al rich) and disordered A2 (Fe–Cr rich) phases via spinodal decomposition. The paper emphasizes this as the primary reason for excellent high-temperature mechanical properties.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

## Oxidation Test Conditions
- **Sample**: Rectangular, 10 mm × 10 mm × 2 mm
- **Grinding**: 1200 grit SiC abrasive paper
- **Temperature**: 1000 °C
- **Duration**: 24 h
- **Atmosphere**: Dry air
- **Heating rate**: 10 °C/min

## Oxidation Weight Gain (24 h at 1000 °C)

| Alloy | Weight Gain (mg/cm²) |
|-------|----------------------|
| Al0.8CrFeNi | ~1.4 |
| Al1.0CrFeNi (AlCrFeNi) | ~0.6 |
| Al1.2CrFeNi | ~1.9 |

- **Comparison benchmarks**: 
  - SUS 405 stainless steel: 6.45 mg/cm² at 1000 °C
  - Cr–Mn–Fe–Co–Ni HEA: 4.45 mg/cm² at 1000 °C
- **AlCrFeNi (x=1.0) exhibits best oxidation resistance among the three alloys tested.**

## Oxidation Rate Constants

| Alloy | Stage 1 (ΔG² = kt + C) | Stage 2 (ΔG² = kt + C) |
|-------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Al0.8CrFeNi | k = 0.16 | k = 0.026 |
| Al1.0CrFeNi | k = 0.014 | k = 0.014 |
| Al1.2CrFeNi | k = 0.24 | k = 0.1 |

## Oxidation Products
- **Major oxides**: Al₂O₃, Cr₂O₃
- **Minor oxides**: Fe₂O₃, FeCr₂O₄
- **Al0.8CrFeNi and AlCrFeNi**: Fe₂O₃ peaks near 49° and 64°
- **Al1.2CrFeNi**: Fe₂O₃ peak near 64° disappeared; small FeCr₂O₄ peaks near 30° and 36°

## Oxide Layer Characteristics
- Al₂O₃ forms first during initial oxidation
- Al₂O₃ shows poor adhesion to substrate → spallation observed
- Cr₂O₃ forms as oxidation proceeds (fast diffusion of Cr relative to Ni)
- Cr₂O₃ provides effective passivation and long-term protection above 1000 °C
- More Cr₂O₃ in AlCrFeNi alloy (x=1.0) → lowest oxidation rate
- Fe-oxides (Fe₂O₃, FeCr₂O₄) also contribute to oxidation resistance

## High-Temperature Service Issues Noted
- **Spallation of Al₂O₃**: Due to poor adhesion between Al₂O₃ and substrate; compressive stress from thermal expansion mismatch between substrate and oxide
- **No mention of high-temperature embrittlement, oxygen embrittlement, nitrogen embrittlement, or grain-boundary oxidation** in the original paper

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Al]

**Applicable Alloy System**: AlxCrFeNi medium-entropy alloys (Co-free Al–Cr–Fe–Ni system)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: x = 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2 (molar ratio)

**Main Function**: 
1. Enhances yield strength at elevated temperatures (strongest positive correlation among alloying elements based on Pearson coefficient analysis)
2. Reduces density (190 g/cm³ per unit x decrease; from 7.45 to 6.45 g/cm³)
3. Increases melting point (from 1385 °C at x=0.3 to 1430 °C at x=1.2)
4. Participates in B2 ordered phase formation (Ni–Al rich)
5. Improves oxidation resistance (forms Al₂O₃ protective oxide)

**Microstructural Mechanism**: 
- Al–Ni enrichment promotes formation of ordered B2 phase
- At x ≥ 0.5, induces spinodal decomposition into alternating lamellar/maze-shaped dual-phase structure
- Al₂O₃ forms first during oxidation, acting as initial protective layer

**Property Effect**:
- Yield strength: Al0.8CrFeNi reaches 2083.7 MPa fracture strength at 200 °C
- Density decreases from 7.45 g/cm³ (x=0.3) to 6.45 g/cm³ (x=1.2)
- Melting point increases: 1385 °C (x=0.3) → 1404 °C (x=0.8) → 1430 °C (x=1.2)
- At x > 1.0 (Al1.2), strength decreases slightly (1783.9 MPa at 200 °C), indicating optimal Al content around x=0.8–1.0

**Potential Side Effects**:
- Excessive Al (x=1.2) reduces fracture strength and plastic strain (22.85% at 200 °C)
- Al₂O₃ scale has poor adhesion → spallation
- Over addition leads to slight decrease in yield strength

**Original Evidence**: 
- "Al has the strongest positive association with the yield strength at elevated temperature" (Pearson coefficient, Fig. 3)
- "The addition of Al brought some increase to the yield strength of alloy at first, then led to a slight decrease with further increase of Al content" (Section 3.1)
- "A decrease in density (from 7.45 g/cm³ to 6.45 g/cm³) is observed for higher Al content" (Section 3.3)

---

## [Cr]

**Applicable Alloy System**: AlxCrFeNi medium-entropy alloys (Co-free Al–Cr–Fe–Ni system)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Equimolar ratio with Fe and Ni (1 molar ratio)

**Main Function**: 
1. Participates in A2 disordered phase formation (Fe–Cr rich)
2. Forms protective Cr₂O₃ oxide layer during high-temperature oxidation
3. Provides strong stability at high temperature (>1000 °C) for long-term oxidation protection

**Microstructural Mechanism**: 
- Fe–Cr enrichment favors formation of disordered A2 phase
- Cr diffuses faster than Ni to surface, enabling Cr₂O₃ formation
- Cr₂O₃ provides effective passivation layer against further oxidation

**Property Effect**:
- Higher Cr₂O₃ content in AlCrFeNi (x=1.0) corresponds to lowest oxidation rate (k = 0.014)
- AlCrFeNi exhibits best oxidation resistance (0.6 mg/cm² after 24 h at 1000 °C)

**Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper

**Original Evidence**: 
- "Fe–Cr enrichment is in favor of the formation of disordered solid solution A2 phase" (Section 3.2)
- "The fast diffusion of Cr onto the surface results in the formation of Cr₂O₃ as the oxidation process proceed which can slow down further oxidation" (Section 3.5.2)
- "The formation of more Cr₂O₃ were responsible for the reduction in the oxidation rates of AlCrFeNi alloy" (Conclusion point 5)

## [Ni]

**Applicable Alloy System**: AlxCrFeNi medium-entropy alloys (Co-free Al–Cr–Fe–Ni system)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Equimolar ratio with Cr and Fe (1 molar ratio)

**Main Function**: 
1. Participates in B2 ordered phase formation (Ni–Al rich)
2. The Ni–Al rich B2 phase contributes to high yield strength at elevated temperatures

**Microstructural Mechanism**: 
- Ni–Al enrichment tends to form ordered B2 phase
- Two-phase structure of B2 (Ni–Al rich) + A2 (Fe–Cr rich) produced by spinodal decomposition

**Property Effect**: 
- Strengthening by B2 and A2 phases leads to high yield strength at elevated temperature
- Specific yield strength comparable to Inconel 718

**Potential Side Effects**: 
- Ni has lower melting point (1453 °C) than Cr (1857 °C) and Fe (1538 °C); Ni–Al enriched grains have lower melting point than Fe–Cr enriched regions

**Original Evidence**: 
- "Ni–Al enrichment tends to form ordered B2 phase" (Section 3.2)
- "Strengthening by the Ni–Al rich B2 phase and Fe–Cr rich A2 phase leads to high yield strength at elevated temperature" (Section 3.4)

## [Fe]

**Applicable Alloy System**: AlxCrFeNi medium-entropy alloys (Co-free Al–Cr–Fe–Ni system)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Equimolar ratio with Cr and Ni (1 molar ratio)

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## 1. Two-Phase (Spinodal Decomposition) Strengthening

**Related Elements**: Al, Ni, Cr, Fe

**Typical Alloys**: AlxCrFeNi MEAs (x ≥ 0.5)

**Key Compositions**: Al0.5CrFeNi, Al0.8CrFeNi, Al1.0CrFeNi, Al1.2CrFeNi

**Microstructural Changes**: 
- Spinodal decomposition produces alternating interconnected dual-phase microstructure
- B2 ordered (Ni–Al rich) phases within grains
- A2 disordered (Fe–Cr rich) phases near grain boundaries/interdendritic regions
- Dense lamellar structure near grain boundaries
- Maze-shaped morphology within grains

**Property Improvements**: 
- Al0.8CrFeNi: fracture strength 2083.7 MPa at 200 °C, 2076.7 MPa at 400 °C
- Al0.5CrFeNi: fracture strength 1952.5 MPa at 200 °C, 1905.1 MPa at 400 °C
- Excellent plasticity at elevated temperatures (no fracture above 600 °C)
- High specific yield strength superior to refractory HEAs and comparable to Ni-based superalloys

**Risks and Limitations**: 
- At x = 1.2, strength decreases (1783.9 MPa at 200 °C), suggesting optimal two-phase balance may be lost with excessive Al
- The original paper does not provide quantitative phase volume fraction data

## 2. Solid-Solution Strengthening

Not explicitly discussed as a distinct mechanism. The primary strengthening mechanism identified is the two-phase (B2 + A2) microstructure, not conventional solid-solution strengthening.

## 3. Oxidation-Resistant Alloying (Al + Cr)

**Related Elements**: Al, Cr

**Typical Alloys**: Al0.8CrFeNi, Al1.0CrFeNi, Al1.2CrFeNi

**Key Compositions**: x = 0.8–1.2 Al with equimolar Cr

**Microstructural Changes**: 
- Formation of protective Al₂O₃ and Cr₂O₃ oxide layers
- Minor Fe₂O₃ and FeCr₂O₄ also present

**Property Improvements**: 
- AlCrFeNi (x=1.0): lowest oxidation rate constant (k = 0.014)
- 24 h weight gain: 0.6 mg/cm² (vs. 6.45 mg/cm² for SUS 405 stainless steel)
- All three alloys show superior oxidation resistance to SUS 405 steel and Cr–Mn–Fe–Co–Ni HEA

**Risks and Limitations**: 
- Al₂O₃ has poor adhesion to substrate → spallation
- Compressive stress from thermal expansion mismatch causes spalling
- Al1.2CrFeNi shows higher oxidation rate (0.24/0.1 mg/cm² stages) than Al1.0CrFeNi, indicating excessive Al does not always improve oxidation resistance

# Key Conclusions

1. **Machine learning model**: GRNN with K-fold cross-validation successfully predicts yield strength of multi-component alloys at elevated temperatures (R² = 0.9068 for training, 0.8421 for testing; final validation R² = 0.9036).

2. **Pearson correlation analysis**: Al exhibits strongest positive correlation with elevated-temperature yield strength; Cr, Co, Ni exhibit negative correlation.

3. **Microstructure**: All AlxCrFeNi MEAs consist of Al–Ni rich B2 (ordered) and Cr–Fe rich A2 (disordered) phases formed via spinodal decomposition. Phase transition absent below 1300 °C for x > 0.5.

4. **Melting point**: Increases with Al content; Al1.2CrFeNi reaches approximately 1430 °C (liquidus).

5. **Density**: Decreases from 7.45 g/cm³ (x=0.3) to 6.45 g/cm³ (x=1.2) with increasing Al.

6. **Mechanical properties**: 
   - Optimal Al content around x=0.8–1.0 for strength
   - Al0.8CrFeNi exhibits highest fracture strength (2083.7 MPa at 200 °C)
   - All alloys show no fracture at ≥ 600 °C, even after 75% height reduction
   - Specific yield strength superior to MoNbTaW, TaNbHfZrTi RHAs, Haynes 230; comparable to Inconel 718

7. **Oxidation resistance**: 
   - AlCrFeNi (x=1.0) has best oxidation resistance (0.6 mg/cm² after 24 h at 1000 °C)
   - Oxide scales: Al₂O₃ + Cr₂O₃ (major), Fe₂O₃ + FeCr₂O₄ (minor)
   - Cr₂O₃ provides most effective long-term protection
   - Al₂O₃ spallation due to poor adhesion

8. **Design principle**: Machine learning accelerates alloy design by mapping composition–temperature–property relationships, enabling rapid identification of promising alloy systems without extensive trial-and-error experiments.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations**:

1. **Dataset size**: The ML model was trained on a small dataset (Table 2 shows limited variables); K-fold cross-validation was necessary due to limited data. The original paper notes "Due to a limited dataset in present work, the K-fold cross-validation is an effective method."

2. **Mechanical testing**: Only compressive tests performed; no tensile tests, creep tests, or fracture toughness measurements.

3. **Temperature range**: Mechanical properties tested only up to 800 °C; oxidation tested at 1000 °C. Service behavior above 800 °C for mechanical loading not evaluated.

4. **As-cast condition only**: No heat treatment or thermo-mechanical processing was performed; effect of post-processing on microstructure and properties not investigated.

5. **Oxidation characterization**: Oxidation test limited to 24 h; long-term oxidation behavior not evaluated.

6. **Phase quantification**: Volume fractions of B2 and A2 phases not quantitatively reported.

7. **Grain size**: Not reported, limiting understanding of grain-boundary strengthening contribution.

8. **Composition deviations**: Actual vs. nominal compositions not reported; no elemental analysis (e.g., ICP-OES) of bulk ingots.

**Applicable Scope**:

- Co-free Al–Cr–Fe–Ni medium-entropy alloy system for high-temperature structural applications
- Applicable to alloy design guided by machine learning approaches
- Relevant to high-temperature oxidation-resistant structural materials
- Potentially useful for aerospace, gas-turbine engines, and transport applications where low density and high specific strength at elevated temperatures are required
- Not directly applicable to refractory Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or W-based alloys (this alloy system does not contain W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Hf, Zr, or Ti)
