# Alloy System and Research Object

**Research objective:** To evaluate He-induced damage evolution at elevated temperatures (750 °C and 850 °C) in Inconel 617 and Alloy 800H, with Hastelloy N as a reference alloy, for potential applications in higher-temperature Molten Salt Reactors (MSRs).

**Alloy systems studied:**
- Alloy 800H (Fe-Ni-Cr austenitic alloy)
- Inconel 617 (Ni-Cr-Co-Mo superalloy)
- Hastelloy N (Ni-Mo-Cr-Fe alloy)

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Material preparation:**
- Samples were cut from cold rolled and annealed bulk alloys
- Dimensions: 10 mm × 6.5 mm × 1 mm
- Mechanical polishing from 400-grit to 2000-grit SiC paper
- Final mirror-like surface achieved by mechanical polishing
- Electro-polished at 36 V using an electrolyte (50% H₂SO₄, 40% glycerol, 10% deionized water) at 0 °C for 10 s to remove surface stress layer

**Irradiation conditions:**
- He ion irradiation: 1 MeV He⁺ ions
- Temperatures: 750 °C and 850 °C
- Ion fluences:
  - Alloy 800H: 2.2 × 10¹⁶ ions/cm² (average He concentration: 0.13 at.%)
  - Inconel 617: 4.4 × 10¹⁶ ions/cm² (average He concentration: 0.25 at.%)
  - Hastelloy N: 5.9 × 10¹⁶ ions/cm² (average He concentration: 0.34 at.%)
- Irradiation facility: 4 MV pelletron accelerator at SINAP-CAS
- SRIM 2008 predictions: maximum He concentration at ~1700 nm; maximum damage at ~1600 nm; maximum irradiation depth ~2000 nm

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Pre-irradiation microstructure:**
- All three alloys used in the cold rolled and annealed condition (details of matrix phases not explicitly discussed for unirradiated state)

**He bubble characteristics at 750 °C:**
- Most He bubbles densely distributed at depths of 1500–2000 nm
- Alloy 800H: larger bubbles, smaller number density
- Inconel 617: highest number density among all alloys at both temperatures
- Hastelloy N: heterogeneous bubble nucleation, uneven size distribution; most bubbles spherical, few faceted

**He bubble characteristics at 850 °C:**
- Alloy 800H: bubble size increases, number density decreases
- Inconel 617: denser distribution with small average size; significant broader distribution beyond damage peak; precipitated clusters (embryo of M₆C and M₂₃C₆ carbides) act as nucleation sites
- Hastelloy N: all He bubbles faceted; bubble-loop complexes formed by dislocation loops decorating He bubbles

**Precipitated clusters in Inconel 617 (irradiated at 850 °C):**
- EDS mapping at ~1800 nm depth shows clusters enriched in C and Cr, depleted in Co, Fe, Ni, and Mo
- These clusters are likely the embryo/nucleation stage of M₆C and M₂₃C₆ carbides
- Irradiation time was short (2.83 h for Inconel 617)

**Frank loops (interstitial-type):**
- Mean sizes:
  - Alloy 800H: 8.3 nm (750 °C), 10.0 nm (850 °C)
  - Inconel 617: 12.7 nm (750 °C), 25.6 nm (850 °C)
  - Hastelloy N: 27.8 nm (750 °C), 30.0 nm (850 °C)
- Coarsening with temperature observed in all alloys
- Hastelloy N has the largest and densest Frank loops at both temperatures
- Frank loops in Hastelloy N at 850 °C show both length and width increase due to bubble-loop complex formation

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed:**
- Irradiation-induced defects (He bubbles and Frank loops) acting as obstacles to dislocation motion → irradiation hardening
- Dispersed barrier hardening (DBH) model applied to calculate yield strength increments

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

The original paper does not discuss oxidation resistance. The focus is on He irradiation-induced damage evolution:

**He swelling resistance (volume fraction of He bubbles):**
- Alloy 800H: smallest volume fraction (best swelling resistance)
- Inconel 617: intermediate
- Hastelloy N: largest volume fraction (worst)
- At higher temperature (850 °C): volume fraction decreases in Inconel 617 and Alloy 800H, but increases in Hastelloy N

**He embrittlement:** The paper notes that He embrittlement is a primary concern for integrity of nickel-based nuclear structural materials at high temperature, as He atoms precipitate as He bubbles and degrade mechanical performance.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

**C (Carbon)**

**Applicable Alloy System:** Inconel 617 (Ni-Cr-Co-Mo superalloy), general nickel-based alloys

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 0.06 wt.% in Inconel 617 (0.08 wt.% in Alloy 800H; 0.05 wt.% in Hastelloy N)

**Main Function:** Carbon accelerates the formation of M₆C and M₂₃C₆ carbides at elevated temperatures; these precipitates serve as nucleation sites for He bubbles during irradiation, increasing He bubble number density.

**Microstructural Mechanism:** At elevated temperatures (700–900 °C), C atoms diffuse in the matrix and grain boundaries, reacting with Mo and Cr to form carbides through reactions: 6M + C → M₆C and 23M + 6C → M₂₃C₆. Even in the early nucleation stage (short irradiation times), C-enriched clusters form and serve as heterogeneous nucleation sites for He bubbles. EDS mapping showed these clusters are enriched in C and Cr but depleted in Co, Fe, Ni, and Mo.

**Property Effect:** In Inconel 617 irradiated at 850 °C, the formation of C-Cr-rich precipitated clusters led to a denser and broader distribution of He bubbles beyond the damage peak region. This increased the swelling behavior. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the cluster size or density.

**Potential Side Effects:** C-enriched precipitates act as He bubble nucleation sites, increasing He bubble number density and leading to denser He bubble distributions. This may degrade irradiation resistance and potentially increase embrittlement. Additionally, the depletion of Ni and Mo atoms around clusters may contribute to significant coarsening of Frank loops.

**Original Evidence:** "Due to the small activation energy for M₆C and M₂₃C₆ carbides nucleation, the C atom accelerated by high temperature diffuse in matrix and also in the GBs, encounter Mo and Cr atoms to form precipitated through reactions: 6M + C → M₆C and 23M + 6C → M₂₃C₆" (Section 4.1). "The EDS mapping shows that these clusters are enriched in C and Cr, while Co, Fe, Ni and Mo are depleted" (Section 4.1, Fig. 12).

**Cr (Chromium)**

**Applicable Alloy System:** Inconel 617 (Ni-Cr-Co-Mo superalloy)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 22.00 wt.% in Inconel 617 (20.10 wt.% in Alloy 800H; 6.96 wt.% in Hastelloy N)

**Main Function:** Cr participates in the formation of M₂₃C₆ carbides at elevated temperatures, which serve as He bubble nucleation sites during irradiation.

**Microstructural Mechanism:** At elevated temperatures (700–900 °C), Cr reacts with C to form M₂₃C₆ carbides. During short irradiation times, Cr-C-rich precipitated clusters (embryo of M₂₃C₆ carbides) form and act as heterogeneous nucleation sites for He bubbles.

**Property Effect:** Cr participation in precipitate formation leads to an increased He bubble number density in Inconel 617 at 850 °C. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the property changes attributable solely to Cr.

**Potential Side Effects:** Cr-C-rich precipitate formation can act as He bubble nucleation sites, leading to denser He bubble distributions, which may increase swelling and irradiation-induced hardening.

**Original Evidence:** "These clusters are enriched in C and Cr, while Co, Fe, Ni and Mo are depleted" (Section 4.1, Fig. 12). "The volume fraction of precipices, such as M₂₃C₆, γ' phase, increases with increasing aging time and temperature from 700 °C to 900 °C" (Section 4.1).

**Mo (Molybdenum)**

**Applicable Alloy System:** Inconel 617, Hastelloy N

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 9.00 wt.% in Inconel 617; 16.50 wt.% in Hastelloy N

**Main Function:** Mo promotes carbide formation at elevated temperatures (M₆C), contributing to precipitate formation during irradiation.

**Microstructural Mechanism:** Mo reacts with C to form M₆C carbides at temperatures between 700–900 °C. In Inconel 617, Mo is depleted in the precipitated clusters while C and Cr are enriched, indicating Mo may be involved in carbide formation but is not concentrated in the initial cluster stage.

**Property Effect:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper with specific numerical values. The paper does not provide direct evidence of Mo's specific contribution to property changes.

**Potential Side Effects:** Depletion of Mo atoms around precipitated clusters may contribute to coarsening of Frank loops in Inconel 617 at 850 °C. This is stated as: "Moreover, the depleted atoms located at cluster initially, such as Ni and Mo atoms, lead to the significant coarsening of Frank loops" (Section 4.1).

**Original Evidence:** "The volume fraction of precipices, such as M₂₃C₆, γ' phase, increases with increasing aging time and temperature from 700 °C to 900 °C" (Section 4.1). "The EDS mapping shows that these clusters are enriched in C and Cr, while Co, Fe, Ni and Mo are depleted."

**He (Helium, as generated transmutation product)**

**Applicable Alloy System:** All nickel-based alloys under irradiation (Alloy 800H, Inconel 617, Hastelloy N)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range:** He concentration depends on Ni content and neutron dose. Values: 0.13 at.% (Alloy 800H), 0.25 at.% (Inconel 617), 0.34 at.% (Hastelloy N) corresponding to neutron dose of 1.5 × 10²³ n/cm²; SRIM-predicted peak He concentration at ~1700 nm.

**Main Function:** He atoms precipitate as He bubbles in nickel-based alloys under irradiation, leading to irradiation-induced hardening and swelling. He embrittlement is a primary concern for structural integrity at high temperature.

**Microstructural Mechanism:** He atoms produced by (n, α) reactions migrate and precipitate as He bubbles. At elevated temperatures (≥750 °C), He atoms are thermally dissociated from He nuclei and diffuse until trapped by sinks, other bubbles, or precipitated clusters. Bubble-loop complexes form when He bubbles interact with dislocation loops. In Hastelloy N at 850 °C, all He bubbles are faceted and decorated by dislocation loops, forming bubble-loop complexes.

**Property Effect:** He bubbles and bubble-loop complexes act as barriers to dislocation motion, leading to irradiation-induced hardening. The volume fraction of He bubbles determines the degree of swelling. In Alloy 800H, the smallest He bubble volume fraction correlates with best swelling resistance. Specific values: Alloy 800H showed lowest ΔH_e (0.94 GPa at 750 °C, 0.62 GPa at 850 °C), whereas Hastelloy N showed highest ΔH_e (1.28 GPa at 850 °C).

**Potential Side Effects:** He bubbles degrade mechanical performance by acting as obstacles to dislocation motion, causing irradiation hardening and embrittlement. Faceted He bubbles may act as stronger obstacles than equilibrium bubbles due to overpressure, leading to greater hardening than predicted by DBH model.

**Original Evidence:** "As a product of (n, α) nuclear transmutation reaction, He atoms easily precipitate as He bubbles, which further degrade the mechanical performance of alloys in nuclear reactors" (Section 1). "The blocking effect of He bubbles on the slip dislocation increases with their pressure increasing" (Section 4.2).

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

**Irradiation-Induced Defect Strengthening (He bubbles + Frank loops)**

**Related Elements:** He (transmutation product), C, Cr, Mo, Ni, Fe

**Typical Alloys:** Alloy 800H, Inconel 617, Hastelloy N under He ion irradiation at 750–850 °C

**Key Compositions:** He concentrations of 0.13 at.% (Alloy 800H), 0.25 at.% (Inconel 617), 0.34 at.% (Hastelloy N)

**Microstructural Changes:** He bubbles (spherical and faceted) form in the damage zone (1500–2000 nm depth). Frank loops (interstitial-type) coarsen with temperature. Bubble-loop complexes form in Hastelloy N at 850 °C where faceted He bubbles are decorated by dislocation loops. In Inconel 617 at 850 °C, C-Cr-rich precipitated clusters form and act as He bubble nucleation sites.

**Property Improvements:** Irradiation-induced hardening measured by nanoindentation. The degree of hardening (ΔH/H₀) at 850 °C follows: Alloy 800H < Inconel 617 < Hastelloy N.

**Risks and Limitations:** He bubbles and Frank loops pin dislocation lines, inhibiting free motion and increasing yield stress. The DBH model underestimates hardening in Inconel 617 due to precipitated cluster contributions and in Hastelloy N due to over-pressured faceted He bubbles having higher obstacle strength than the assumed 0.2. The model parameter selection (α = 0.27 for Frank loops) may not apply to bubble-loop complexes with expanded width.

**Precipitate-Assisted He Bubble Nucleation**

**Related Elements:** C, Cr, Mo (carbide formers)

**Typical Alloys:** Inconel 617

**Key Compositions:** Inconel 617 with 22.00 wt.% Cr, 9.00 wt.% Mo, 0.06 wt.% C

**Microstructural Changes:** At 850 °C irradiation (2.83 h), precipitated clusters (embryo of M₂₃C₆ and M₆C carbides) form. EDS mapping shows these clusters are enriched in C and Cr, depleted in Co, Fe, Ni, and Mo. These clusters serve as nucleation sites for He bubbles, leading to denser He bubble distribution.

**Property Improvements:** Not applicable—this mechanism increases damage rather than improving properties. It increases the He bubble number density, leading to broader distribution of He bubbles beyond the damage peak region.

**Risks and Limitations:** The increased He bubble number density may lead to increased swelling and may contribute to greater irradiation hardening than predicted by the DBH model. The original paper indicates that this mechanism causes deviation between experimental and calculated hardening values in Inconel 617 (ΔH_e = 1.26 GPa vs ΔH_c = 1.01 GPa at 850 °C).

**Original Evidence:** "These observed precipitated clusters, the embryo of precipitates, increase the number density of He bubbles" (Section 4.1). "Deviation between experimental and calculated values of hardness increment in Inconel 617 is caused by the formation of precipitated clusters" (Section 5, conclusion 5).

---

**Bubble-Loop Complex Strengthening**

**Related Elements:** He (transmutation product)

**Typical Alloys:** Hastelloy N

**Key Compositions:** Hastelloy N with 0.34 at.% He (fluence: 5.9 × 10¹⁶ ions/cm²) at 850 °C

**Microstructural Changes:** At 850 °C, all He bubbles in Hastelloy N are faceted and decorated by dislocation loops, forming bubble-loop complexes. The complex size increases with irradiation temperature. The complexes form through trapping of dislocation loops or self-interstitial atoms (SIA) by He bubbles, or through growth of He-V-SIA complexes by absorbing loops and He-V clusters at elevated temperatures.

**Property Improvements:** Not applicable—this mechanism degrades material performance.

**Risks and Limitations:** The volume fraction of He bubbles increases in Hastelloy N at higher temperature due to bubble-loop complex formation, increasing swelling. The DBH model underestimates hardening (ΔH_e = 1.28 GPa vs ΔH_c = 0.87 GPa at 850 °C) because faceted over-pressured He bubbles have higher obstacle strength than equilibrium bubbles. The obstacle strength α = 0.27 for Frank loops may not apply to bubble-loop complexes with expanded width.

**Original Evidence:** "The volume fraction increase in Hastelloy N alloy with increasing irradiation temperature is caused by the formation of bubble-loop complexes" (Section 5, conclusion 2). "The ΔH_c is much smaller than ΔH_e irradiated at 850 °C...Compared with the bubbles reaching equilibrium status, these faceted He bubbles acted as stronger obstacle to pin the movement of dislocation lines" (Section 4.2).

# Key Conclusions

1. With irradiation temperature increasing (750→850 °C):
   - He bubble size increases and number density decreases in Alloy 800H and Hastelloy N
   - Opposite evolution occurs in Inconel 617: denser and broader He bubble distribution
   - Frank loops coarsen in all investigated alloys

2. Alloy 800H has the smallest volume fraction of He bubbles, followed by Inconel 617 and Hastelloy N, demonstrating the best resistance to swelling above 750 °C. Notably, Hastelloy N's swelling worsens at 850 °C, while the other two alloys improve.

3. In Inconel 617 irradiated at 850 °C, large volume fractions of precipitated clusters (embryo of M₂₃C₆/M₆C carbides) form. These act as additional He bubble nucleation sites and, combined with thermal dissociation of He atoms, lead to denser and broader He bubble distribution.

4. Alloy 800H has the best resistance to irradiation-induced hardening at 850 °C, followed by Inconel 617 and Hastelloy N. At 750 °C, the three alloys show similar resistance.

5. Deviations between experimental and calculated hardening values arise from:
   - In Inconel 617: formation of precipitated clusters not accounted for in DBH model
   - In Hastelloy N: selection of inadequate obstacle strength (α values) for over-pressured faceted He bubbles and bubble-loop complexes

6. The comparison suggests Alloy 800H demonstrates greater potential as a structural material for higher-temperature MSRs due to its smaller He bubble volume fraction and better resistance to irradiation-induced hardening.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations acknowledged in the paper:**
- The Frank loops are assumed to be interstitial-type based on literature (Chen et al., 2016) but were not directly confirmed in this study using outside-inside method.
- The DBH model calculations use obstacle strengths (α = 0.2 for He bubbles, 0.27 for Frank loops) that may not adequately represent: (1) the additional obstacle contribution of precipitated clusters in Inconel 617, and (2) the stronger obstacle strength of over-pressured faceted He bubbles (compared to equilibrium bubbles) in Hastelloy N. The α = 0.27 for Frank loops may not apply to bubble-loop complexes with expanded width.
- The precipitated clusters in Inconel 617 at 850 °C cannot be counted directly due to their small size, so their contribution to hardening was not quantified.
- Irradiation times are short (e.g., 2.83 h for Inconel 617), so the precipitated clusters are only at the nucleation stage.

**Applicable scope:**
- The results apply specifically to He ion irradiation at 750–850 °C with fluences corresponding to He concentrations of 0.13–0.34 at.% (neutron dose equivalent ~1.5 × 10²³ n/cm²).
- The paper focuses on three nickel-based alloys: Alloy 800H, Inconel 617, and Hastelloy N.
- The results are relevant to higher-temperature Molten Salt Reactor applications where He embrittlement and irradiation-induced hardening are critical concerns.
