# Paper Metadata
- **Title**: Oxide dispersion strengthening mediated ultra-high strength at wide temperature range in NbTaTiV refractory high-entropy alloys
- **Authors**: Xin Zhang, Tiejun Li, Yuankui Cao, Tao Liao, Zhonghao Xie, Ao Fu, Jia Li, Qihong Fang, Zhen He, Bin Liu
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106352
- **Alloy system studied**: NbTaTiV-based refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) reinforced with Al₂O₃
- **Material category**: Oxide-dispersion-strengthened (ODS) refractory high-entropy alloy; powder metallurgy (mechanical alloying + spark plasma sintering / rapid hot-pressing)

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Research objective**: To design and synthesize an ultra-strong NbTaTiV RHEA reinforced with 0.35 wt% Al₂O₃, and to systematically investigate the characteristics of oxides and their effects on mechanical properties at room and elevated temperatures. The work aims to combine oxide dispersion strengthening and solid-solution strengthening to achieve high strength while maintaining room-temperature processability.
- **Alloy family**: Nb-Ta-Ti-V refractory high-entropy alloy system, equiatomic or near-equiatomic base composition.
- **Oxide addition**: 0.35 wt% nano-sized Al₂O₃ (30–50 nm initial particle size).

# Alloy Composition
- **Nominal alloy designation**: NbTaTiV (based on equiatomic Nb-Ta-Ti-V) + 0.35 wt% Al₂O₃
- **Starting raw powders**: Nb, Ta, Ti, V powders (<45 μm), purities above 99.5%; Al₂O₃ powder (30–50 nm).
- **Overall measured composition of the 1600 °C-sintered sample** (EDS, at.%):
  - Nb: 22.26 at.%
  - Ta: 23.42 at.%
  - Ti: 23.94 at.%
  - V: 24.02 at.%
  - Al: 0.88 at.%
  - O: 3.79 at.%
  - N: 1.69 at.%
- **Matrix phase composition (Point A)**, at.%:
  - Nb: 21.88
  - Ta: 23.41
  - Ti: 24.14
  - V: 20.83
  - Al: 0.52
  - O: 5.69
  - N: 3.52
- **Precipitate/particle composition (Point B)**, at.%:
  - Nb: 1.46
  - Ta: 0.78
  - Ti: 52.24
  - V: 2.51
  - Al: 0.03
  - O: 24.55
  - N: 18.44
- **Interstitial impurity levels**: The as-milled powders contain high contents of O and N introduced by powder metallurgy processing; specific ppm values are not stated in the original paper, but O and N are quantified in at.% by EDS (Table 1). The exact residual O and N of the milled powder before sintering is not explicitly stated in the original paper.

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Method**: Powder metallurgy route: mechanical alloying (MA) followed by rapid hot-pressing sintering (described as SPS-like consolidation).
- **Milling**: Planetary ball milling (MITR-YXQM-4L) at 250 r·min⁻¹ for 20 h; tungsten carbide vials and balls; ball-to-powder mass ratio 10:1. Atmosphere for milling not explicitly stated, but residual air is mentioned as a source of O and N; argon atmosphere is not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Consolidation**: Rapid hot-pressing sintering at 1400 °C, 1500 °C, and 1600 °C for 10 min under 50 MPa pressure. The sintering atmosphere is not explicitly stated in the original paper; high-temperature compression testing was performed in vacuum (5 × 10⁻³ Pa).
- **Deformation/thermomechanical processing**: None stated.
- **Heat treatment details**: No separate homogenization or aging; sintering temperature is the variable heat treatment parameter.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
- **Matrix phase**: Single BCC phase in the matrix. At 1400 °C, the material consists of a BCC phase plus a small amount of Al₂O₃; at 1600 °C, Al₂O₃ has fully reacted/disappeared per XRD.
- **Lattice parameter of BCC matrix**:
  - Milled powder: 3.243 Å (Debye-Scherrer calculation)
  - Sintered at 1400 °C: 3.242 Å
  - Sintered at 1600 °C: 3.238 Å
  - Theoretical NbTaTiV lattice parameter: 3.229 Å (reference [20] value) or 3.230 Å (text); TEM-measured matrix lattice parameter: 3.214 Å
- **Second phase / precipitates**: In-situ formed Ti-(O, N) submicron particles, identified by TEM/SAED as FCC structure with lattice parameter 4.194 Å, close to Ti-(O,N) phase in MoNbTaTiV (4.188 Å).
- **Average Ti-(O,N) particle diameter** (1600 °C sample): 358.55 nm; size range from tens of nanometers to ~1000 nm, majority below 600 nm.
- **Grain size (EBSD)**:
  - 1400 °C sintered: ~9.20 μm
  - 1500 °C sintered: ~15.67 μm
  - 1600 °C sintered: ~19.60 μm
  - Comparison: as-sintered NbTaTiV RHEA matrix without Al₂O₃ addition: 38 μm (reference [5])
- **Elemental segregation/distribution**: Nb, Ta, and V are uniformly distributed in the matrix; Ti, N, and O are concentrated in particle regions; Al is uniformly distributed in the matrix and depleted in most particle regions.
- **Grain boundary features**: Ti-(O,N) particles are reprecipitated and coarsened within grains and at grain boundaries with increasing sintering temperature.
- **Phase evolution pathway**: During milling, Al₂O₃ is evenly dispersed; Ti reacts with air to form Ti-(O,N) compounds that are refined and dissolved by repeated fracture and cold-welding. During sintering above 1400 °C, Al₂O₃ reacts with Ti: 3·Ti + y·Al₂O₃ → 3·TiₓO_y + 2y·Al. Al atoms dissolve into the matrix due to high vacancy concentration and high temperature; supersaturated interstitial atoms reprecipitate as Ti-(O,N) particles on TiO nuclei due to similar structure.

# Mechanical Properties
- **Room-temperature compression** (constant strain rate 0.001 s⁻¹, in air):
  - Yield strength:
    - 1400 °C sintered: 1888 MPa
    - 1500 °C sintered: 1911 MPa
    - 1600 °C sintered: 2075 MPa
  - Fracture/ultimate compressive strength (1600 °C sample): 2780 MPa
  - Plastic fracture strain: ~15% for all three sintering temperatures; total compressive strain above 20% (conclusion states moderate compressive ductility >20%)
  - Fracture mode: cleavage fracture with flabelliform river patterns, typical of BCC RHEAs.
- **High-temperature compression** (1600 °C-sintered sample, vacuum 5 × 10⁻³ Pa, strain rate 0.001 s⁻¹, heated at 5 °C·s⁻¹, soaked 2 min, compressed to 30%):
  - Yield strength at 700 °C: 1063 MPa
  - Yield strength at 800 °C: not explicitly stated as a single number in the text; yield strength decreases from 1063 MPa at 700 °C to 690 MPa at 1000 °C. Specific 800 °C and 900 °C values are not explicitly stated in the original paper.
  - Yield strength at 900 °C: not explicitly stated in the original paper.
  - Yield strength at 1000 °C: 690 MPa
  - Total compressive strain: above 30% at all high-temperature test conditions.
  - Work hardening observed at 700–900 °C, reaching maximum strength within initial 20% strain; strain softening at later stages. At 1000 °C, rapid yield stress decline and apparent softening.
- **Microhardness (Vickers)**:
  - 1400 °C sintered: not explicitly stated as a single number in the text; trend shows gradual increase with sintering temperature.
  - 1500 °C sintered: not explicitly stated in the original paper.
  - 1600 °C sintered: 559.9 HV
  - Comparisons: as-cast NbTaTiV: 298 HV [4]; P/M NbTaTiV without oxide addition: 427.9 HV [5].
- **Comparison with other alloys (room temperature)**:
  - NbTaTiV (MA + SPS, no oxide): 1108 MPa yield, 1821 MPa fracture, ~23% plastic strain
  - NbTaTiVAl (MA + SPS): 1450 MPa yield, 1619 MPa fracture, ~3% plastic strain
  - NbTaTiV/Ti-C-O (MA + SPS): 1510 MPa yield, ~2050 MPa fracture, ~10% plastic strain
  - NbTa₀.₅TiZrAl₀.₅ (MA + SPS): 1500 MPa yield, ~2100 MPa fracture, ~22% plastic strain
  - NbTaTiV (arc-melting): 965 MPa yield, ~1370 MPa fracture, 50% plastic strain
  - HfNbTaTiV (arc-melting): 1350 MPa yield, >45% plastic strain
  - MoNbTaTiV (arc-melting): 1400 MPa yield, 2450 MPa fracture, 30% plastic strain
  - NbTaTiVW (arc-melting): 1420 MPa yield, ~1880 MPa fracture, 20% plastic strain
- **Density, elastic modulus, fracture toughness, creep, rupture life**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability
- **Oxidation behavior**: Not directly tested. High-temperature compression tests were performed in vacuum (5 × 10⁻³ Pa), so oxidation resistance data are not provided in this paper.
- **Oxide layer analysis**: Not studied. No Al₂O₃, Cr₂O₃, SiO₂, HfO₂, or ZrO₂ protective scale characterization is reported.
- **Potential embrittlement issues**: The paper mentions that the inherent brittleness of the BCC crystal structure and the distribution of Ti-(O,N) particles at grain boundaries contribute to cleavage fracture at room temperature. High-temperature embrittlement, oxygen embrittlement, or nitrogen embrittlement are not specifically addressed beyond the role of interstitial O and N in strengthening.
- **Interstitial O and N effects**: O and N are deliberately incorporated from residual air during milling and from Al₂O₃ reaction; they contribute to solid-solution strengthening and Ti-(O,N) precipitation, improving strength while maintaining moderate ductility. No negative oxidation behavior is discussed.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Al₂O₃ (nano-alumina addition)

**[Element or Element Combination]**: Al₂O₃ (nano-sized, 30–50 nm initial particle size)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: NbTaTiV refractory high-entropy alloy (powder metallurgy route)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 0.35 wt% Al₂O₃ added to equiatomic NbTaTiV

**[Main Function]**: Acts as an oxygen/aluminum source for in-situ transformation during sintering. It is not retained as Al₂O₃ in the final 1600 °C-sintered material; instead it reacts with Ti to form TiO (subsequently Ti-(O,N) particles) and releases Al into solid solution.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: During milling, nano-Al₂O₃ is uniformly dispersed in the matrix. During sintering above 1400 °C, the reaction 3·Ti + y·Al₂O₃ → 3·TiₓO_y + 2y·Al occurs. High vacancy concentration at high temperature promotes Al ion diffusion into the matrix. The TiO particles serve as nucleation sites for Ti-(O,N) reprecipitation because of similar crystal structures. The overall O and N from residual air during milling also participate in Ti-(O,N) formation.

**[Property Effect]**: Yield strength increases from 1108 MPa (NbTaTiV without oxide, MA + SPS) to 2075 MPa (with 0.35 wt% Al₂O₃, sintered at 1600 °C) at room temperature, while plastic strain decreases from ~23% to ~15%. High-temperature yield strength at 1000 °C is 690 MPa, much higher than other RHEAs and Inconel-718 at the same temperature range.

**[Potential Side Effects]**: The original paper does not explicitly state negative side effects of Al₂O₃ addition. However, the resulting Ti-(O,N) particles at grain boundaries are associated with cleavage fracture and inherent brittleness of the BCC structure.

**[Original Evidence]**: Abstract; Section 3, XRD analysis (Fig. 2); Section 3, reaction schematic; Table 1; Fig. 6 element mapping.

## Ti-(O,N) (titanium oxynitride submicron particles)

**[Element or Element Combination]**: Ti-(O,N) / TiₓO_y with high N content

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: NbTaTiV-based refractory high-entropy alloys (and MoNbTaTiV, HfNbTaTiZrV systems per references)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: In-situ formed; measured precipitate composition in 1600 °C sample (at.%): Ti 52.24, O 24.55, N 18.44. Overall sample contains O 3.79 at.% and N 1.69 at.%. Particle average diameter 358.55 nm.

**[Main Function]**: Provides oxide-dispersion strengthening via submicron particle reinforcement.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Dislocation by-pass (Orowan-type) mechanism, because particles are above hundred-nanometer scale (average ~359 nm) and/or incoherent with the matrix. The authors state: “the submicron Ti-(O, N) particles are large enough (the average diameter is 358.55 nm), thereby mainly strengthening the RHEAs through the dislocation by-pass mechanism.” Ti-(O,N) particles form at grain interiors and grain boundaries during sintering.

**[Property Effect]**: Contributes to the ultra-high yield strength of 2075 MPa (room temperature) and 690 MPa (1000 °C). Room-temperature plastic strain remains ~15%.

**[Potential Side Effects]**: Ti-(O,N) particles at grain boundaries contribute to cleavage fracture and BCC brittleness at room temperature.

**[Original Evidence]**: Abstract; Section 3, Table 1; Fig. 5 TEM/SAED; Fig. 6 HAADF-EDS; Section 3 discussion of strengthening mechanisms.

## Al (aluminum solute)

**[Element or Element Combination]**: Al (solute dissolved from Al₂O₃ reaction)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: NbTaTiV RHEA with Al₂O₃ addition

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: Overall measured Al content: 0.88 at.% (1600 °C sample); matrix Al: 0.52 at.%

**[Main Function]**: Solid-solution strengthening by reducing lattice parameter and altering interplanar spacing.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Al atoms dissolve into the NbTaTiV BCC matrix during sintering due to high vacancy concentration. Lattice parameter decreases from 3.242 Å (1400 °C) to 3.238 Å (1600 °C) as more Al dissolves. Reduced interplanar spacing makes dislocation slip more difficult.

**[Property Effect]**: Contributes to yield strength improvement from 1108 MPa (base NbTaTiV P/M) to 2075 MPa (with Al₂O₃-derived Al). Room-temperature plastic strain ~15%.

**[Potential Side Effects]**: The original paper does not explicitly state that Al solute causes embrittlement in this specific system, but prior work on NbTaTiVAl (1450 MPa yield, ~3% plastic strain) indicates that higher Al contents can drastically reduce ductility. In this paper, Al content is low (0.88 at.%).

**[Original Evidence]**: Abstract; Section 3, XRD lattice parameter discussion; Table 1; Section 3 solid-solution strengthening discussion.

## Interstitial O and N (residual air-derived)

**[Element or Element Combination]**: O and N interstitials

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: Powder metallurgy NbTaTiV RHEAs, especially with Al₂O₃ addition

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: Overall O: 3.79 at.%, N: 1.69 at.% (1600 °C sample); matrix O: 5.69 at.%, N: 3.52 at.%

**[Main Function]**: Solid-solution strengthening and Ti-(O,N) precipitate formation.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Titanium's extremely high affinity for air promotes reaction with O and N during milling, forming Ti-(O,N) compounds. These are refined, dispersed, and partially dissolved during milling, then reprecipitated during sintering. O and N also occupy interstitial sites in the BCC lattice, decreasing lattice parameter and hindering dislocation motion.

**[Property Effect]**: Part of the combined strengthening that raises yield strength to 2075 MPa at room temperature and 690 MPa at 1000 °C.

**[Potential Side Effects]**: Interstitial O and N can cause embrittlement in BCC refractory alloys if uncontrolled; however, in this case, controlled Ti-(O,N) precipitation appears to preserve moderate ductility (~15%).

**[Original Evidence]**: Section 3, reaction description; Table 1; Section 3 strengthening discussion; reference [18] on Y₂O₃ addition also highlights O/N-Ti interaction.

## Ti (titanium in NbTaTiV)

**[Element or Element Combination]**: Ti

**[Applicable Alloy System]**: NbTaTiV RHEA

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: Matrix Ti: 24.14 at.%; precipitate Ti: 52.24 at.%; overall Ti: 23.94 at.%

**[Main Function]**: Reacts with O and N to form Ti-(O,N) precipitates; also reacts with Al₂O₃ to form TiO and release Al.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Ti's high affinity for oxygen and nitrogen drives Ti-(O,N) formation during milling and sintering. Ti also participates in the Al₂O₃ reduction reaction above 1400 °C.

**[Property Effect]**: Enables oxide-dispersion strengthening through Ti-(O,N) particles.

**[Potential Side Effects]**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**[Original Evidence]**: Section 3, reaction schematic; Table 1; Fig. 6 element mapping.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Oxide Dispersion Strengthening (via in-situ Ti-(O,N) particles)

**[Related Elements]**: Ti, O, N, Al (from Al₂O₃)

**[Typical Alloys]**: NbTaTiV + 0.35 wt% Al₂O₃; also reported in MoNbTaTiV, HfNbTaTiZrV with Al₂O₃ reinforcement (cited references)

**[Key Compositions]**: 0.35 wt% Al₂O₃; Ti-(O,N) particles with average diameter 358.55 nm; precipitate composition ~52 at.% Ti, ~25 at.% O, ~18 at.% N

**[Microstructural Changes]**: Nano-Al₂O₃ added during milling fully dissolves/reacts during sintering at 1600 °C, forming dispersed submicron Ti-(O,N) particles within grains and at grain boundaries. Grain size refines from 38 μm (base P/M NbTaTiV) to 19.60 μm at 1600 °C (or 9.20 μm at 1400 °C).

**[Property Improvements]**: Room-temperature yield strength 2075 MPa, fracture strength 2780 MPa; high-temperature yield strength 690 MPa at 1000 °C; microhardness 559.9 HV.

**[Risks and Limitations]**: Ti-(O,N) particles at grain boundaries contribute to cleavage fracture and BCC brittleness; ductility remains moderate (~15% plastic strain).

## Solid-Solution Strengthening (Al + interstitial O/N)

**[Related Elements]**: Al, O, N

**[Typical Alloys]**: NbTaTiV with Al₂O₃ addition

**[Key Compositions]**: Overall Al 0.88 at.%, O 3.79 at.%, N 1.69 at.%

**[Microstructural Changes]**: Al dissolves in BCC matrix; lattice parameter decreases from 3.242 Å to 3.238 Å. O and N occupy interstitial sites; interplanar spacing reduced, dislocation motion hindered.

**[Property Improvements]**: Significant contribution to yield strength 2075 MPa (room temperature) and high-temperature strength retention.

**[Risks and Limitations]**: The original paper does not explicitly state that the specific Al/O/N levels cause embrittlement; however, general knowledge from the field and cited Ref [23] suggests that higher Al leads to low ductility. The moderate Al content here appears balanced.

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening / Grain Refinement

**[Related Elements]**: Al₂O₃ addition, Ti-(O,N) pinning particles

**[Typical Alloys]**: NbTaTiV RHEA

**[Key Compositions]**: Not applicable (microstructural parameter, not composition)

**[Microstructural Changes]**: Al₂O₃/oxide-derived particles inhibit grain growth during sintering. Grain sizes: 9.20 μm (1400 °C), 15.67 μm (1500 °C), 19.60 μm (1600 °C), all smaller than 38 μm for base NbTaTiV.

**[Property Improvements]**: Grain refinement contributes partially to enhanced strength.

**[Risks and Limitations]**: Continual grain growth with increasing sintering temperature partially offsets refinement benefit at higher temperatures, although precipitate strengthening dominates.

## Particle (Dispersion) Strengthening via Dislocation By-pass

**[Related Elements]**: Ti-(O,N) submicron particles

**[Typical Alloys]**: NbTaTiV + Al₂O₃

**[Key Compositions]**: Particle average diameter 358.55 nm (above the threshold for Orowan by-pass)

**[Microstructural Changes]**: Large incoherent or semi-coherent Ti-(O,N) particles distributed in BCC matrix and at grain boundaries.

**[Property Improvements]**: Contributes to ultra-high strength at room and high temperatures.

**[Risks and Limitations]**: The by-pass mechanism is less effective than shearing for very fine coherent particles; the strengthening is mainly at room and moderate high temperatures. At 1000 °C, yield strength decreases to 690 MPa.

# Key Conclusions
1. A novel oxide-reinforced NbTaTiV RHEA with dispersive submicron Ti-(O,N) particles, dissolved Al, and interstitial O/N was successfully fabricated via mechanical alloying and rapid hot-pressing/sintering at 1600 °C from a mixture containing 0.35 wt% nano-Al₂O₃.
2. During high-temperature sintering, Al₂O₃ reacts with Ti to form TiO and release Al; the supersaturated interstitial atoms (O, N) then reprecipitate as Ti-(O,N) particles.
3. The oxide-reinforced NbTaTiV RHEA sintered at 1600 °C exhibits the highest room-temperature yield strength (2075 MPa), fracture strength (2780 MPa), and microhardness (559.9 HV), with ~15% compressive plastic strain.
4. High-temperature yield strength is 1063 MPa at 700 °C and 690 MPa at 1000 °C, with total compressive strain above 30%, showing superior strength retention compared to other RHEAs and Ni-based superalloys (e.g., Inconel-718 declines sharply around 700 °C).
5. The ultra-high strength is mainly attributed to (i) particle strengthening from highly dispersed Ti-(O,N) submicron particles via dislocation by-pass mechanism and (ii) solid-solution strengthening from dissolved Al and interstitial O/N atoms. Grain-boundary strengthening from refined grains also partially contributes.
6. The moderate plasticity is attributed to the high dispersion of Ti-(O,N) particles, the solid solution of Al and interstitials, and the refined grain structure.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Limited oxidation data**: The paper does not investigate oxidation resistance or oxide scale formation. High-temperature mechanical tests were performed in vacuum, so oxidation behavior under service conditions is unknown.
- **Single base alloy system**: Results apply to the equiatomic NbTaTiV RHEA with 0.35 wt% Al₂O₃. Applicability to other RHEA systems is inferred from references but not directly demonstrated here.
- **Processing-specific**: The high O and N contents, fine grain size, and Ti-(O,N) precipitates are consequences of the powder metallurgy route with residual air. The specific impurity levels depend on milling conditions and may not be directly reproducible with different processing parameters.
- **Mechanical testing conditions**: Only compression testing, not tensile, fracture toughness, or creep, was reported. Fracture toughness and creep performance are not addressed.
- **800 °C and 900 °C specific yield strength values**: Not explicitly stated in the text; only the range from 1063 MPa (700 °C) to 690 MPa (1000 °C) is given.
- **Microhardness values at 1400 °C and 1500 °C**: Only the trend is given; specific values are not stated in the original paper except 559.9 HV at 1600 °C.
- **Thermal stability**: Long-term thermal stability of Ti-(O,N) particles beyond the 10-min sintering treatment is not assessed.
- **Whether the material contains W, Mo, Hf, Zr, Cr, Si, Re, C, B, Y, or La**: No. The alloy system is Nb-Ta-Ti-V with Al from Al₂O₃ addition. Tungsten carbide from milling media is not reported as a significant impurity in the alloy composition analysis.
