# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** Solid solution softening or hardening induced by minor substitutional additions in a Hf20Nb31Ta31Ti18 refractory high entropy alloy
- **Authors:** Xiaolong Li, Lu Jin, Huahai Mao, Hideyuki Murakami, Sheng Guo
- **Journal:** AIP Advances
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0160762
- **Alloy system studied:** Hf-Nb-Ta-Ti refractory high entropy alloy with minor additions of Mn, Al, Cu, Fe
- **Research objective:** To investigate the effect of minor substitutional additions (Al, Cu, Mn, Fe) on room-temperature and elevated-temperature hardness of a single bcc phase Hf20Nb31Ta31Ti18 refractory high entropy alloy, and to explore the mechanism of solid solution softening/hardening for potential application to ductilize RHEAs with good high-temperature strength but poor room-temperature ductility.

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Material category:** Refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA), single bcc phase
- **Base alloy:** Hf20Nb31Ta31Ti18 (at.%), designated as HNTT
- **Derivative alloys:** HNTT-Mn, HNTT-Al, HNTT-Cu, HNTT-Fe
- **Doping level:** Nominal 2.5 at.% substitutional addition, replacing Nb and Ta proportionally (Hf20Nb29.75Ta29.75Ti18-X2.5)
- **Material form:** As-cast arc-melted buttons
- **Alloy system relevance:** Ductile RHEA system with no superior high-temperature strength, used as a model system to prove the concept of solid solution softening (SSS) for future application to high-strength but brittle RHEAs.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase:** Single BCC phase confirmed by XRD for all five alloys (HNTT, HNTT-Mn, HNTT-Al, HNTT-Cu, HNTT-Fe).
- **Second phases / precipitates:** None observed in XRD patterns. The single-phase BCC structure was intended by the authors for studying solid solution hardening/softening in isolation.
- **Grain size:** Not reported in the original paper.
- **Grain boundary features:** Dendritic and inter-dendritic features typically seen in directly cast HEAs were observed via BSE images.
- **Elemental segregation:**
  - Ta (high melting point) segregates to the dendritic region.
  - Ti and Al (relatively low melting points) segregate to the inter-dendritic region.
  - The same segregation pattern was observed in all tested alloys.
- **Precipitate size and distribution:** Not applicable (single-phase alloys).
- **Relevant strengthening mechanisms discussed:**
  - Solid solution hardening (SSH)
  - Solid solution softening (SSS)
  - Lattice distortion / misfit-induced elastic stress fields
  - Dislocation–solute interaction via double-kink nucleation and kink migration
  - No precipitation strengthening, dispersion strengthening, grain boundary strengthening, or second-phase strengthening in these alloys.

## Softening/Hardening Effects at RT (relative to HNTT)

- Mn addition (nominal 2.5 at.%, actual 0.2 at.%): softened HNTT by **20.0%** at RT
- Al addition (nominal 2.5 at.%, actual 1.9 at.%): softened HNTT by **18.1%** at RT
- Cu addition (nominal 2.5 at.%, actual 1.3 at.%): softened HNTT by **13.3%** at RT
- Fe addition (nominal 2.5 at.%, actual 2.4 at.%): hardened HNTT by **5.2%** at RT

## Softening/Hardening Effects at 600 °C (relative to HNTT)

- Mn: softened by ~14%
- Al: softened by ~17.4%
- Cu: softened by ~13.1%
- Fe: hardened by ~3.5% (from 226 to 234 HV)

## Hardness-Temperature Behavior (Three Stages)

1. **Below 300 °C (400 °C for HNTT-Fe):** Strong temperature dependence; hardness decreases with increasing temperature. The effect of solutes is most pronounced in this stage.
2. **300/400 °C to 800 °C:** Temperature-independent plateau (athermal range); hardness is virtually insensitive to temperature.
3. **Above 800 °C:** Rapid decrease in hardness with increasing temperature, attributed to diffusion-controlled deformation mechanisms.

## Notes on Mechanical Properties

- Tensile strength, compressive strength, elongation, fracture toughness, creep performance, strain rate, elastic modulus, and density are **not reported** in this paper.
- Testing atmosphere for hardness: High vacuum (down to 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ Pa).
- Hardness tester: Intesco-HTM 1400 high-temperature Vickers hardness tester.
- Number of indentations averaged at each temperature: ~5.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Mn]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Hf-Nb-Ta-Ti refractory high-entropy alloy (Hf20Nb31Ta31Ti18)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Nominal 2.5 at.%; actual measured ~0.2 at.% (significant loss during arc melting)
- **Main Function:** Solid solution softening; induces softening in the temperature range RT–1000 °C; selected based on first-principles calculations as a promising low-cost substitute for Re to improve low-temperature ductility of bcc refractory alloys
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Maintains single BCC phase; interacts with screw dislocation cores; promotes double-kink nucleation mechanism over kink migration at low temperatures; reduces the energy barrier for double-kink nucleation along an initially straight dislocation line (both attractive and repulsive interactions can reduce the nucleation barrier)
- **Property Effect:** 
  - RT hardness decreased from 294.4 (HNTT) to 235.5 HV (HNTT-Mn), a 20.0% softening
  - 600 °C hardness decreased by ~14% from 226 to 194 HV
  - Softening persists from RT to 1000 °C
- **Potential Side Effects:** 
  - High volatilization during arc melting causing composition control difficulties (lost ~92% of nominal Mn addition)
  - The original paper does not report a direct effect on tensile ductility or high-temperature strength retention; the concept was proposed but not experimentally verified for tensile properties
- **Original Evidence:** Table I and Table II; Figs. 3 and 4; Discussion section: "With 2.5 at. % nominal addition of Mn, Al, and Cu, the HNTT base alloy was softened by 20%, 18.1%, and 13.3%, respectively"; "the softening effect here is distinct especially by Mn"

## [Fe]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Hf-Nb-Ta-Ti refractory high-entropy alloy (Hf20Nb31Ta31Ti18)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Nominal 2.5 at.%; actual measured ~2.4 at.%
- **Main Function:** Solid solution hardening; consistent hardening effect from RT to 800 °C
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Maintains single BCC phase; the content of 2.5 at.% Fe is already above the crossover concentration, so kink migration dominates over double-kink nucleation at low temperatures; Fe atoms interact with dislocations along the dislocation line and prohibit the lateral motion of kinked screw dislocations; elastic stress field generated by atomic size mismatch or modulus misfit

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Softening (SSS)

- **Related Elements:** Mn, Al, Cu (and theoretical candidates such as Re, Os; first-principles-predicted alternatives such as Mn and Al for W-based alloys)
- **Typical Alloys:** Single-phase BCC refractory high-entropy alloys; Group VIA metals (Mo, W) ductilized by Re or Os
- **Key Compositions:** 2.5 at.% nominal Mn, Al, or Cu addition in Hf20Nb31Ta31Ti18
- **Microstructural Changes:** No phase transformation (single BCC retained); solute atoms interact with 1/2⟨111⟩ screw dislocation cores; promote double-kink nucleation as the dominant plastic deformation mechanism at low temperatures
- **Property Improvements:** 
  - RT hardness reduced by up to 20% (Mn), 18.1% (Al), 13.3% (Cu)
  - Softening persists from RT to 1000 °C
  - Intended application: induce room-temperature tensile ductility in RHEAs with excellent high-temperature strength but zero RT tensile ductility (concept proposed, not directly demonstrated here)
- **Risks and Limitations:**
  - The softening effect at high temperatures may slightly weaken the base alloy
  - Composition control is difficult for elements with high volatility (e.g., Mn) during arc melting
  - The original paper did not directly demonstrate tensile ductility improvement
  - Softening occurs only below a certain solute concentration (crossover concentration); above that, hardening (SSH) can occur

## Solid-Solution Hardening (SSH)

- **Related Elements:** Fe (and in RHEA context, Mo, W at high levels)
- **Typical Alloys:** Single-phase BCC RHEAs; HfNbTaTiZr family
- **Key Compositions:** 2.5 at.% nominal Fe addition in Hf20Nb31Ta31Ti18
- **Microstructural Changes:** Single BCC retained; Fe atoms interact with screw dislocations along the dislocation line; kink migration dominated deformation at low temperatures; elastic stress field from lattice misfit (atomic size and modulus difference)
- **Property Improvements:**
  - RT hardness increased by 5.2% (294.4 → 309.8 HV)
  - Hardening maintained from RT to 800 °C
  - Athermal plateau (300–800 °C) primarily controlled by SSH from lattice misfit effects
- **Risks and Limitations:**
  - Hardening effect is relatively small at this addition level
  - SSH effect disappears at 1000 °C (hardness of HNTT-Fe lower than HNTT at 1000 °C)
  - High-level Mo or W additions for SSH typically lead to room-temperature embrittlement
  - SSH alone cannot resolve the RT ductility vs. HT strength trade-off

## Dislocation Core Interaction Mechanism (Double-Kink Nucleation vs. Kink Migration)

- **Related Elements:** All substitutional solutes (Mn, Al, Cu, Fe)
- **Typical Alloys:** BCC structured alloys, including BCC RHEAs
- **Key Compositions:** 2.5 at.% nominal addition of each element
- **Microstructural Changes:** No microstructural change; mechanism of dislocation motion changes: double-kink nucleation (softening) vs. kink migration (hardening)
- **Property Improvements:**
  - Below 300 °C (400 °C for Fe): Mn, Al, Cu promote double-kink nucleation → softening; Fe promotes kink migration → hardening
  - 300–800 °C: athermal plateau governed by SSH from lattice misfit
  - Above 800 °C: diffusion-controlled processes dominate, hardness drops quickly
- **Risks and Limitations:**
  - Crossover concentration divides double-kink nucleation (below) and kink migration (above); the crossover concentration shifts to lower values with increasing temperature
  - Both attractive and repulsive solute-dislocation interactions can enhance double-kink nucleation
  - The mechanism is solute-type and temperature dependent, making alloy design non-trivial

# Key Conclusions

1. **Minor substitutional additions can cause either solid solution hardening or softening in a single BCC Hf20Nb31Ta31Ti18 RHEA, depending on the element type.**
   - 2.5 at.% nominal addition of Fe → **solid solution hardening** (5.2% RT hardness increase)
   - 2.5 at.% nominal addition of Mn, Al, Cu → **solid solution softening** (13.3–20.0% RT hardness decrease)

2. **The hardness-temperature behavior of all tested alloys follows a universal three-stage pattern:**
   - Stage I (below 300 °C, or 400 °C for Fe-added alloy): Strong temperature-dependent decrease in hardness; solutes have the most significant effect
   - Stage II (300/400–800 °C): Temperature-independent athermal plateau; controlled primarily by SSH from lattice misfit (atomic size and modulus differences)
   - Stage III (above 800 °C): Rapid hardness drop due to enhanced diffusion-controlled processes

3. **Solid solution softening is attributed to double-kink nucleation dominated plastic deformation mechanism**, where solutes reduce the double-kink nucleation barrier along an initially straight screw dislocation line.

4. **Solid solution hardening is attributed to kink migration dominated plastic deformation mechanism**, where solute atoms interact with dislocations along the line and impede lateral kink motion.

5. **The crossover from softening to hardening occurs at a critical solute concentration**, which shifts to lower concentrations with increasing temperature. This explains the element-type and temperature dependence of softening/hardening behavior.

6. **The strategy of using solid solution softening to induce RT ductility in high-temperature-strength but brittle BCC RHEAs is proposed as a future goal** based on the results; however, actual tensile ductility was not measured in this study.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **No tensile or compressive mechanical properties measured:** The paper only reports Vickers hardness from RT to 1000 °C. The claimed relevance to tensile ductility improvement is conceptual and not experimentally verified.
- **Only hardness measurements:** No yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, elongation, fracture toughness, or creep data are provided.
- **No homogenization or heat treatment:** All alloys are as-cast with dendritic segregation. The effect of a homogenized, equilibrated microstructure on softening/hardening behavior remains untested.
- **Composition control issues:** Mn experienced severe volatilization loss during arc melting, making the nominal and actual compositions significantly different (2.5 at.% nominal vs. 0.2 at.% measured). This complicates dose-dependent interpretation of the Mn results.
- **Limited composition range:** Only 2.5 at.% nominal addition was studied. The crossover concentration effect (transition from softening to hardening) was mentioned but not directly measured in this paper.
- **Narrow temperature range:** The upper testing temperature was 1000 °C, which is below the typical high-temperature target range of 1200–1600 °C for advanced RHEAs.
- **No oxidation or long-term stability data:** The service stability of these alloys under oxidative or aggressive environments was not addressed.
- **Applicable scope:** The findings are most directly applicable to single-phase BCC refractory high-entropy alloys with intrinsically ductile matrices, such as HfNbTaTiZr and its derivatives. Extrapolation to multi-phase RHEAs or other matrix structures should be done with caution.
- **Mechanism discussion based on established literature models:** The double-kink nucleation and kink migration model is well established for dilute BCC alloys; its direct applicability to concentrated HEA solid solutions is discussed based on analogy and prior literature, not direct in-situ verification in this work.
