- **Authors**: Jiayi Liu, Yufeng Huang, Wensheng Liu, Yao Wang, Yong Zhang, Yunzhu Ma

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system studied**: W-Ta-Re ternary alloy
- **Material category**: W-based alloy (solid-solution strengthened tungsten alloy)
- **Processing route**: Spark plasma sintering (SPS)
- **Research objective**: To study the effect of Ta concentration on the microstructure and mechanical properties of W-xTa-10wt%Re (x = 0, 10, 20, and 30 wt%) alloys fabricated by spark plasma sintering, and to understand the strengthening/toughening mechanisms of the alloys.

# Alloy Composition

- **Base system**: W-xTa-10 wt% Re (x = 0, 10, 20, and 30 wt%)

**Table 2 alloy designations and nominal compositions:**

| Alloy | W (wt%) | Ta (wt%) | Re (wt%) |
|-------|---------|----------|----------|
| Ta0 | 90 | 0 | 10 |
| Ta10 | 80 | 10 | 10 |
| Ta20 | 70 | 20 | 10 |
| Ta30 | 60 | 30 | 10 |

- **Alloying elements involved**: W (main), Ta, Re
- **Purity of raw powders**: W ≥99.8%, Ta ≥99.9%, Re ≥99.9%
- **Raw powder particle sizes**: W ~4.0 μm, Ta ~4.4 μm, Re ~5.0 μm
- **Impurity elements after sintering**: The Ta10 processed sample contained C, H, and O impurities. Fig. 6f provides impurity measurements, but numerical values are not explicitly given in the text; O is associated with Ta-oxide formation (Ta₂O₅). The paper notes that residual gas pollution during vacuum sintering leads to O absorption, and Ta has a high affinity for oxygen.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Mixing**: W, Ta, Re powders initially mixed by three-dimensional mixing machine for 10 h under argon atmosphere to prevent oxidation.
- **Pre-compaction**: Mixed powder placed in cylindrical graphite dies with a diameter of 15 mm for pre-pressing.
- **Consolidation method**: Spark plasma sintering (SPS), apparatus: LABOX-325R (Japan).
- **SPS parameters**:
  - Axial pressure: 40 MPa
  - Heating rate: 100 °C/min
  - Sintering temperature: 2000 °C
  - Holding time: 10 min
  - Atmosphere: vacuum
- **Specimen extraction**: Samples taken from the center of the sintered alloy to avoid surface carburization.
- **Post-sintering heat treatment**: None reported.
- **Deformation**: Room-temperature compressive deformation was applied for mechanical testing (not a processing step).

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Single BCC solid solution (W-Ta-Re disordered substitutional solid solution). XRD shows four main BCC peaks corresponding to (110), (200), (211), and (220).
- **Thermodynamic calculation parameters**:
  - ΔH_mix values: Ta10: 4.50 kJ/mol, Ta20: 6.98 kJ/mol, Ta30: 8.89 kJ/mol
  - ΔS_mix values: Ta10: 5.31 J·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹, Ta20: 6.67 J·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹, Ta30: 7.50 J·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹
  - Ω values: Ta10: 4.28, Ta20: 3.41, Ta30: 2.97
  - δ values: Ta10: 1.01%, Ta20: 1.29%, Ta30: 1.47%
  - VEC values: Ta10: 6.00, Ta20: 5.90, Ta30: 5.79
  - All alloys satisfy Ω ≥ 1.1 and δ ≤ 6.6%, and VEC < 6.87 ⇒ BCC is predicted stable.
- **Lattice parameter**: Measured from TEM: a = 3.243 Å for Ta10 matrix.
- **Interplanar spacing**: d-spacing of (110) in Ta10 matrix increases to 2.293 Å (pure W: 2.238 Å, pure Ta: 2.338 Å).
- **Second phases/oxide particles**: Ta₂O₅ particles (dark gray/black particles) distributed inside grains and at grain boundaries. Identified by SAED as Ta₂O₅ with a simple hexagonal structure. XRD does not clearly resolve oxide peaks because the volume fraction is small.
- **Volume fraction of oxides**: Increases with increasing Ta content; oxides tend to agglomerate at higher Ta concentration (especially at 30 wt% Ta).
- **Grain size**:
  - Ta0: 17.3 ± 5.5 μm
  - Ta10: 9.1 ± 2.8 μm
  - Ta20: 7.6 ± 2.2 μm
  - Ta30: 6.7 ± 2.5 μm
- **Grain morphology**: Equiaxed/quasi-equiaxed at low Ta; becomes irregular with increasing Ta content. After compression, grains flatten to elongated strip shape; average grain width in compressed Ta10 reduces to 3.7 ± 1.0 μm; some grains narrower than 1 μm.
- **Elemental segregation**: EDS-mapping of Ta10 shows uniform distribution of W, Ta, Re in the matrix; oxide particles are Ta+O rich.
- **Dislocation structure after deformation**:
  - Short, curved dislocations inside grains
  - High dislocation density
  - Dislocations terminate at grain boundaries
  - Tangled dislocations
  - Cellular dislocation structure (formed by cross-slip)
  - Screw dislocations slipping along (110) plane
- **Crystallographic texture after deformation**: Preferred orientation with <111> and <001> parallel to the compression direction.
- **Density**:
  - Theoretical density: Ta0: 19.51 g/cm³, Ta10: 19.19 g/cm³, Ta20: 18.89 g/cm³, Ta30: 18.60 g/cm³
  - Relative density: Ta0: 95.33%, Ta10: 94.81%, Ta20: 94.83%, Ta30: 95.63%

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxidation resistance testing**: Not performed in this paper.
- **Oxide formation during processing**: Ta₂O₅ oxide particles form inevitably during SPS due to the high affinity of Ta with oxygen and residual gas in the vacuum sintering environment.
- **Detrimental effects of oxides**: With ≥30 wt% Ta, hard oxide particles aggregate at grain boundaries and within grains, reducing intergranular/intragranular bonding strength, causing transgranular cleavage fracture, and decreasing compressive strain to failure.
- **Processing recommendation**: The paper states that it is necessary to maintain a high vacuum environment during sintering to minimize oxide formation.
- **High-temperature service stability / oxidation resistance**: Not studied.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [W (Tungsten) as Matrix Element]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: W-Ta-Re solid-solution alloy; also applicable to other W-based alloys.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 60–90 wt% W in this study.
- **Main Function**: Base matrix with high density, high strength, high hardness, good electrical/thermal conductivity, low sputtering yield, low neutron activation; suitable for plasma-facing wall materials.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: BCC crystal structure. Main slip direction of W is <111>, then <001>.
- **Property Effect**: Provides base strength but suffers from low-temperature brittleness, recrystallization brittleness, irradiation brittleness, and high DBTT. No specific W-only values in this paper.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Inherent brittleness limits demanding applications.
- **Original Evidence**: Introduction section; Fig. 10 discussion of slip directions: "The main slip direction of W is <111>, and then <001>".

## [Re (Rhenium)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: W-Re binary alloys; W-Ta-Re ternary alloys.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Fixed at 10 wt% Re in this study.
- **Main Function**: Improves ductility and fracture toughness; reduces DBTT; promotes solid-solution softening; improves dislocation mobility.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Transforms screw dislocations from symmetric to asymmetric structure; reduces Peierls stress and shear resistance (based on DFT); decreases the barrier of kink nucleation; increases dislocation mobility; promotes cross-slip.
- **Property Effect**: Kappacher et al. reported a decrease in activation energy in W-10wt%Re alloy from nanoindentation. Compared to pure W, W-Re alloys exhibit better ductility and fracture toughness. The paper cites that Re has a limited effect on improving strength.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Limited strengthening effect; does not significantly increase strength.
- **Original Evidence**: Introduction: "Romaner et al. also showed that the addition of Re can transform the screw dislocations from symmetrical to asymmetric structure, and then reduce the Peierls stress and shear resistance based on Density Functional Theory (DFT)." "Kappacher et al. reported a decrease in the activation energy in W-10 wt% Re alloy through nanoindentation experiments." "However, the addition of Re has a limited effect on improving the strength of materials."
- **Additional note**: In this study, Re is a fixed component (10 wt%) and its individual contribution was not varied. The discussion attributes enhanced cross-slip and screw dislocation activation to the combination of Ta and Re.

## [Ta (Tantalum)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: W-Ta binary alloys; W-Ta-Re ternary alloys; also reported in ZrNbMoTaW refractory high-entropy alloy.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0–30 wt% Ta in this study (in increments of 10 wt%); previous studies also examined various W-Ta compositions.
- **Main Function**: Grain refinement; solid-solution strengthening; increased hardness; increased yield strength;
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: 
  - Larger atomic radius (Ta: 1.43 Å, W: 1.39 Å) causes lattice distortion and increases dislocation density.
  - Increased lattice parameter (from 2.238 Å for pure W (110) d-spacing to 2.293 Å for Ta10).
  - Provides more nucleation sites during sintered neck formation because Ta has lower melting point and stronger atomic diffusion ability than W.
  - Solute atoms act as atom-scale obstacles to dislocation movement, interacting with elastic stress fields of dislocations.
  - Grain refinement (Hall-Petch behavior).
- **Property Effect**:
  - Yield strength: increased from 681.3 ± 10.9 MPa (0 wt% Ta) to 1248.8 ± 13.2 MPa (30 wt% Ta), an ~183% enhancement.
  - Hardness: increased from 354.4 ± 7.1 HV (0 wt% Ta) to 515.9 ± 6.8 HV (30 wt% Ta).
  - Grain size refinement: from 17.3 ± 5.5 μm to 6.7 ± 2.5 μm.
  - Ductility: maintained good compressive ductility (>42% strain, no fracture) at 10–20 wt% Ta; degraded at 30 wt% Ta.
- **Potential Side Effects**: 
  - High affinity with oxygen → forms Ta₂O₅ oxide particles during SPS.
  - At >30 wt% Ta, excessive hard oxide particles aggregate at grain boundaries and inside grains, reduce interfacial bonding strength, causing transgranular cleavage fracture and early cracking.
  - Reduced theoretical density (higher Ta content lowers overall density slightly: 19.51 → 18.60 g/cm³).
  - Slight decrease in relative density at 10–20 wt% Ta (94.81–94.83%) compared to Ta0 (95.33%), attributed to Ta oxides distributed along boundaries; improves slightly at 30 wt% Ta (95.63%) due to enhanced densification.
- **Original Evidence**: Abstract: "The average grain size reduces to 6.7 ± 2.5 μm after the addition of 30 wt% Ta, whereas it is 17.3 ± 5.5 μm for the W–Re matrix without the addition of Ta. In addition, its yield strength increased to 1248.8 ± 13.2 MPa, an enhancement by ~183%." Section 3.3: "The alloy with 30 wt% Ta content exhibited a yield strength of 1248.8 ± 13.2 MPa, increase by ~183% compared to that of Ta0 alloy, indicating that Ta has a certain strengthening effect on the materials, most probably attributing to the combined effect of lattice distortion and grain refinement while forming solid solution with more Ta atoms."

## [W + Re combined additions]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: W-Re alloys; W-Ta-Re ternary alloys.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 10 wt% Re with variable W.
- **Main Function**: Ductility improvement, DBTT reduction, softening, enhanced dislocation mobility.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: The addition of Re increases dislocation mobility of ½<111> type dislocations; transforms screw dislocations from symmetric to asymmetric structure; reduces Peierls stress and shear resistance; promotes screw dislocation and cross-slip.
- **Property Effect**: Compared to pure W, W-Re alloys exhibit better ductility and fracture toughness; small amount of Re significantly reduces DBTT. In this paper, W-Ta-Re alloys maintained good ductility at 10–20 wt% Ta without fracture after compression.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Re has limited effect on improving strength.
- **Original Evidence**: Section 3.4: "The barrier of kink nucleation decreases and the dislocation mobility increases after the solution of subgroup elements in BCC metal system. The addition of Ta and Re atoms alters the strength of the local interatomic chemical bonds, which is expected to reduce the Peierls stress and ultimately contribute to the observed screw dislocation and cross-slip."

## [W + Ta combined additions]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: W-Ta binary and W-Ta-Re ternary alloys.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0–30 wt% Ta in this study.
- **Main Function**: Effective grain refinement; strength enhancement; improved ductility at moderate Ta content.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: W and Ta form infinite solid solution with same BCC structure; Ta preferentially provides nucleation sites during sintering; lattice distortion increases dislocation density.
- **Property Effect**: Grain size refined from 17.3 μm (0 wt% Ta) to 6.7 μm (30 wt% Ta); yield strength increased by ~183% at 30 wt% Ta.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Excessive Ta leads to oxide formation and ductility reduction.
- **Original Evidence**: Conclusion 2: "The solid dissolved processes of Ta atoms cause lattice distortion. With increased Ta content, the strength and hardness of the alloys increase continuously affected by solution strengthening and grain refinement mechanism, and the strength is improved by ~183% compared without the addition of Ta. When the content of Ta exceeds 30 wt%, excessive hard oxides lead to the fracture of the alloy due to their poor interfacial bonding with the matrix."

## [Ta + O (oxygen)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: W-Ta-Re alloys fabricated by SPS.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Oxygen content not quantified in text; O is a residual impurity during sintering; Ta₂O₅ particles identified.
- **Main Function**: Unintentional second phase (oxide dispersion) — not an intentional addition.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Ta₂O₅ particles with simple hexagonal crystal structure form by reaction between Ta and oxygen during vacuum sintering; distributed inside grains and at grain boundaries; can pin grain boundaries and hinder grain growth.
- **Property Effect**: Increases hardness (partly); contributes to strength increase. However, excessive oxide agglomeration at ≥30 wt% Ta reduces bonding strength between particles and matrix, causing transgranular cleavage fracture, spallation of hard particles, and reduced compressive ductility.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Reduction in compressive strain to failure (~42% for Ta30); poor interfacial bonding; crack initiation sites.
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 7(b): SAED patterns of Ta₂O₅ (Area B) showing simple hexagonal structure. Section 3.3: "Large amounts of hard oxides reduced the intergranular or intragranular bonding strength, causing a rapid decline in compressive strain."

## [C, H, O (impurities)]

- **Applicable Alloy System**: W-Ta-Re alloys fabricated by SPS.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Measured in Ta10 processed sample (Fig. 6f), but specific values not explicitly stated in text.
- **Main Function**: Residual gas pollution; O forms oxides with Ta; C not discussed as carbide; H not discussed.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: O reacts with Ta to form Ta₂O₅.
- **Property Effect**: O reduces densification slightly; excess oxides cause embrittlement.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Embrittlement and reduction in ductility.
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 6f caption: "impurity composition measurements of Ta10 processed sample"; Section 3.2: "Attributing to the high affinity of Ta with oxygen, numerous fine oxide particles inevitably dispersed inside the grains or at the grain boundaries. Therefore, it is necessary to maintain a high vacuum environment in the process of sintering."

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related elements**: Ta, Re (substitutional solutes in W BCC matrix)
- **Typical alloys**: W-Ta-Re ternary alloys; W-Ta binary alloys; W-Re binary alloys
- **Key compositions**: 0–30 wt% Ta + 10 wt% Re, balance W
- **Microstructural changes**: Disordered substitutional solid solution; lattice distortion due to atomic radius mismatch (Ta: 1.43 Å, W: 1.39 Å, Re: 1.37 Å); increased lattice parameter (3.243 Å for Ta10); solute atoms generate stress fields interacting with dislocation elastic stress fields; solutes act as atom-scale obstacles to dislocation movement; increased dislocation density.
- **Property improvements**: Yield strength increased from 681.3 MPa (Ta0) to 1248.8 MPa (Ta30); hardness from 354.4 HV to 515.9 HV.
- **Risks and limitations**: Solid-solution strengthening by Re is limited; the main solid-solution strengthening effect comes from Ta. Excess Ta leads to oxide issues.
- **Original evidence**: Section 3.4: "The solid solution strengthening is most essential one... On the one hand, the atomic radius of Ta is larger than that of W and Re, resulting in lattice distortion and increasing the density of dislocations. On the other hand, the solute atoms generate the stress field, interacting with the elastic stress field around the dislocations."

## Grain Refinement / Grain-Boundary Strengthening

- **Related elements**: Ta (facilitates nucleation sites during SPS)
- **Typical alloys**: W-Ta-Re alloys fabricated by SPS
- **Key compositions**: 0–30 wt% Ta + 10 wt% Re
- **Microstructural changes**: Average grain size reduced from 17.3 ± 5.5 μm (Ta0) to 6.7 ± 2.5 μm (Ta30); grain boundaries act as barriers to dislocation slip; dislocations pile up at grain boundaries.
- **Property improvements**: Yield strength follows Hall-Petch relationship (Fig. 12); grain refinement contributes to the overall strength enhancement (yield strength increase of ~183% for Ta30).
- **Risks and limitations**: Grain refinement effect gradually diminishes as grain size approaches original powder particle size (~5 μm).
- **Original evidence**: Section 3.4: "Grain refinement strengthening is also an important mechanism... Grain boundary is the resistance of dislocation movement. The dislocations hardly slip cross grain boundaries into adjacent grains, and they form dislocation pile-ups at grain boundaries, causing stress concentration."

## Dislocation Strengthening / Work Hardening

- **Related elements**: Ta and Re (promote screw dislocation activation and cross-slip)
- **Typical alloys**: W-Ta-Re alloys (deformed by compression)
- **Key compositions**: Ta10 alloy analyzed after compression
- **Microstructural changes**: High dislocation density; short, curved dislocations; dislocations terminate at grain boundaries; tangles; cellular dislocation structures; screw dislocations along (110) plane; preferred orientation of <111> and <001>.
- **Property improvements**: High strain hardening rate; enhanced plastic deformation resistance; good ductility sustained during compression.
- **Risks and limitations**: Not explicitly discussed.
- **Original evidence**: Section 3.4: "Fig. 11b shows a lot of short, curved dislocations scattered inside the grains, and that the deformed matrix has a high dislocation density. Part of the dislocation lines terminate at the grain boundary, thus effectively blocking the dislocation slip."

## Oxide Dispersion (Unintentional)

- **Related elements**: Ta + O (Ta₂O₅ particles)
- **Typical alloys**: W-Ta-Re alloys fabricated by SPS with residual oxygen
- **Key compositions**: Ta₂O₅ as second phase; fraction increases with Ta content.
- **Microstructural changes**: Fine Ta₂O₅ oxide particles inside grains and at grain boundaries; can pin grain boundaries and hinder grain growth.
- **Property improvements**: Contribution to hardness increase; some grain boundary pinning contribution to grain refinement.
- **Risks and limitations**: At ≥30 wt% Ta, excessive oxide agglomeration causes transgranular cleavage fracture, reduces compressive strain to ~42%, and leads to early failure. Oxides reduce intergranular/intragranular bonding strength.
- **Original evidence**: Section 3.3: "Excessive Ta content will increase the number and aggregation of hard oxide particles in the alloy... The crack originated from the hard particle and the direction of the cleavage surface shifts obviously, generating a radial river pattern."

## Solid-Solution Softening / Ductility Enhancement

- **Related elements**: Re and Ta (subgroup elements in BCC W)
- **Typical alloys**: W-Re, W-Ta-Re
- **Key compositions**: 10 wt% Re fixed; 0–30 wt% Ta
- **Microstructural changes**: Decrease in kink nucleation barrier; increase in dislocation mobility; transformation of screw dislocations from symmetric to asymmetric; promotion of cross-slip and double-cross slip.
- **Property improvements**: Better ductility and compressive strain (>42% for Ta10 and Ta20 without fracture); higher fracture resistance.
- **Risks and limitations**: Excessive Ta causes hard oxide formation, counteracting the ductility benefit.
- **Original evidence**: Section 3.4: "The barrier of kink nucleation decreases and the dislocation mobility increases after the solution of subgroup elements in BCC metal system. The addition of Ta and Re atoms alters the strength of the local interatomic chemical bonds, which is expected to reduce the Peierls stress and ultimately contribute to the observed screw dislocation and cross-slip."

## Spark Plasma Sintering-Oriented Composition Control

- **Related elements**: Ta (improves densification via enhanced diffusion), O (residual impurity)
- **Typical alloys**: W-Ta-Re alloys
- **Key compositions**: 0–30 wt% Ta + 10 wt% Re, consolidated by SPS at 2000 °C, 40 MPa, 10 min
- **Microstructural changes**: Homogeneous BCC solid solution; refined grains; uniform elemental distribution.
- **Property improvements**: Relative density >94.8% for all alloys; homogeneous microstructure confirmed by small hardness error bars.
- **Risks and limitations**: Ta oxide formation due to residual oxygen; graphitic die can cause surface carburization; vacuum level needs to be high.
- **Original evidence**: Section 3.2: "Therefore, it is necessary to maintain a high vacuum environment in the process of sintering."

# Key Conclusions

1. W-xTa-10 wt% Re (x = 0, 10, 20, 30) alloys present a stable single-phase BCC structure, confirmed by both thermodynamic calculation (Ω ≥ 1.1, δ ≤ 6.6%, VEC < 6.87) and XRD experiments.
2. The addition of Ta significantly refines grain size because Ta has lower atomic diffusion activation energy than W and provides more nucleation sites during sintered neck formation.
3. Strength and hardness increase continuously with Ta content due to solution strengthening and grain refinement. Yield strength improved by ~183% from 681.3 ± 10.9 MPa (Ta0) to 1248.8 ± 13.2 MPa (Ta30).
4. At Ta content >30 wt%, excessive hard Ta₂O₅ oxides lead to transgranular cleavage fracture due to poor interfacial bonding with the matrix, reducing compressive ductility.
5. After deformation, the microstructure shows <111> and <001> preferred orientation; dislocation density and interactions increase; screw dislocations and cross-slip are activated, maintaining ductility.
6. The combined introduction of Ta and Re provides excellent mechanical properties by simultaneous strengthening and toughening mechanisms.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Testing limitations**: Only room-temperature compression tests were performed. High-temperature strength, creep, fatigue, and fracture toughness were not measured.
- **Oxidation**: High-temperature oxidation resistance was not evaluated.
- **Impurity quantification**: Specific numerical values of C, H, O impurities are not reported in the main text (only presented in Fig. 6f); relative density of Ta10 was slightly lower due to oxide formation.
- **Tensile properties**: Not measured; the mechanical property evaluation was solely compressive.
- **Strain rate**: The strain rate used in compression tests is not explicitly stated.
- **DBTT measurement**: Not directly measured in this study; the benefit of Re on DBTT reduction is cited from previous literature.
- **Scope**: The results are specific to SPS-fabricated W-Ta-Re alloys with a fixed 10 wt% Re and Ta content between 0 and 30 wt%. The conclusions should not be directly extrapolated to other refractory systems (Mo-based, Nb-based, HEAs) without validation.
- **Extrapolation caveat**: The beneficial effect of Ta on ductility is limited to ≤20 wt% in this study; at 30 wt% Ta, although strength is highest, ductility is compromised by hard oxide particles. The optimum composition for balanced strength and ductility appears to be 10–20 wt% Ta under the current processing conditions.
