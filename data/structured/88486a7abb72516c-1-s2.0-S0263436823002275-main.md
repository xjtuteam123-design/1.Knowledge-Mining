# Paper Metadata
- **Title:** Effect of Ge addition on microstructural evolution and mechanical properties of quinary Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy
- **Authors:** Zhaobo Li, Liangshun Luo, Binbin Wang, Baoxian Su, Lei Luo, Liang Wang, Yanqing Su, Jingjie Guo, Hengzhi Fu
- **Journal:** International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106327
- **Alloy system studied:** Nb-Si-Ti-Al-Cr-Ge (Nb-silicide based in-situ composite)
- **Research objective:** To explore in detail the effect of Ge addition (1–9 at.%) on microstructural evolution and mechanical properties (room-temperature fracture toughness, elastic modulus, nanohardness, high-temperature compression strength) of hypereutectic Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr (at.%) alloy.
- **Material category:** Nb-Si based in-situ composite (Nb-silicide based ultrahigh-temperature alloy); not a refractory high-entropy alloy, not a W-based or Mo-based alloy, not ODS, not additively manufactured.

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Base alloy:** Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr (at.%, nominal hypereutectic Nb-Si-based alloy)
- **Ge-added alloys:** Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr-xGe (x = 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 at.%), named 1Ge, 3Ge, 5Ge, 7Ge, 9Ge.
- **Material type:** In-situ composite of Nb-based solid solution (Nbss) and niobium silicides (mainly β-Nb5Si3).
- **Application context:** Candidate hot-end components for next-generation aero engines; target service range 1200–1400 °C.

# Alloy Composition
Elemental raw materials purities: Nb (99.9 wt%), Si (99.99 wt%), Ti (99.4 wt%), Al (99.9 wt%), Cr (99.4 wt%), Ge (99.9 wt%).

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Preparation method:** Vacuum non-consumable arc melting with tungsten electrode under argon atmosphere in a water-cooled copper crucible.
- **Ingot size:** 100 g button ingots.
- **Melting repetitions:** Five times per ingot to guarantee chemical homogeneity.
- **Specimen preparation:** Cuboid specimens 6 × 6 × 3 mm³ cut by electro-discharge machining (EDM) from the middle of button ingots; polished with SiC sandpapers for microstructure analysis.
- **Fracture toughness specimens:** 2 × 4 × 20 mm³ cuboids cut by EDM; surfaces polished to scratch-free condition.
- **High-temperature compression specimens:** Cylinders Φ6 mm × 9 mm; polished to remove EDM scales before testing; quenched into water promptly after high-temperature test to retain deformed microstructure.
- **No heat treatment described in the original paper.** The alloys were studied in as-cast condition and after high-temperature compression at 1200 °C/0.001 s⁻¹ with 20% strain.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
- **As-cast phase constitution (all Ge-added alloys):** Only Nbss (Nb-based solid solution) and β-Nb5Si3; no Nb3Si, no α-Nb5Si3 in as-cast condition. Ge intensively promotes formation of β-Nb5Si3 and restrains Nb3Si.
- **Base Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy (from prior study [14]):** Nbss + α-Nb5Si3 + few Nb3Si.
- **Primary phase:** β-Nb5Si3 in all Ge-added alloys (hypereutectic). Primary β-Nb5Si3 shows lath-shaped morphology in 1Ge; amount of lath-shaped decreases and polygonal primary β-Nb5Si3 increases with Ge content.
- **Eutectic structures:** Chaotic, irregular Nbss/β-Nb5Si3 eutectics. Classified as eutectics I (fine β-Nb5Si3 particles, usually around primary β-Nb5Si3 blocks) and eutectics II (big β-Nb5Si3 particles). Volume fractions of eutectics I and II decrease with increasing Ge; they almost disappear in 9Ge alloy.
- **Additional phase:** Some black Tiss (titanium-based solid solution) phases at Nbss/β-Nb5Si3 interfaces, rich in Ti. In 9Ge alloy, fine Nb5Ge3 particles (yellow in EBSD maps) exist among large β-Nb5Si3 phase.
- **Precipitate size and distribution (main phases):** β-Nb5Si3 average diameter increases with Ge (5.73 µm in 1Ge to 17.85 µm in 9Ge); Nbss average diameter decreases (7.74 µm in 1Ge to 5.77 µm in 9Ge). Primary β-Nb5Si3 aspect ratios range 1.57–2.54.
- **Elemental segregation:** Ge segregates into β-Nb5Si3 and Tiss; Ti segregates into Nbss and Tiss; Si content in Nbss varies (2.83 at.% in 1Ge; 2.11 at.% in 3Ge; 1.29 at.% in 5Ge; 2.79 at.% in 7Ge; 2.62 at.% in 9Ge). Ge in β-Nb5Si3 reaches ~13 at.% in 9Ge alloy.
- **Grain boundaries:** As-cast alloys dominated by lower-angle grain boundaries (<2°): Nbss 86.7% (1Ge) / 85.2% (9Ge); β-Nb5Si3 82.2% (1Ge) / 93.2% (9Ge); Nb5Ge3 93.1% (9Ge). After deformation: lower-angle boundaries decrease; LAGBs (2–15°) and HAGBs (≥15°) increase. Deformed 1Ge Nbss: 65.8% lower-angle, 32.3% LAGB, 1.9% HAGB. Deformed 9Ge Nbss: 34.8% lower-angle, 32.7% LAGB, 32.5% HAGB.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed in paper:** The paper primarily discusses the role of solid-solution effects (Ge strengthening bonding in constituent phases; Si solid solution in Nbss affecting toughness), phase volume fraction effects, ductile-phase toughening, and dynamic recrystallization during high-temperature deformation. It does not discuss ODS, carbide precipitation, or boride/silicide precipitation (beyond the intrinsic in-situ silicide phase) in detail.

# Mechanical Properties
Alloy compositions are Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr-xGe (x in at.%).

**Room-temperature fracture toughness (three-point bending, single-side notch cantilever beam method; loading rate 0.1 mm/min):**
- 1Ge: KQ = 18.47 MPa·m^1/2 (highest; 36.92% higher than base)
- 3Ge: 29.58% higher than base
- 5Ge: 15.12% higher than base
- 7Ge: 5.41% higher than base
- 9Ge: 36.18% lower than base (i.e., below 13.49 MPa·m^1/2; specific value not given)
- Base Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr: KQ = 13.49 MPa·m^1/2
- Note: All Ge-added alloys except 9Ge have higher toughness than base. Loading-displacement curves show linear increase to peak then rapid drop; no significant plastic deformation.

**Elastic modulus of constituent phases (nanoindentation, G200, Berkovich tip radius 20 nm):**
- Nbss elastic modulus: 1Ge baseline; 3Ge −0.20%; 5Ge +21.23%; 7Ge +27.30%; 9Ge +32.66% (relative to 1Ge)
- β-Nb5Si3 elastic modulus: 3Ge +1.50%; 5Ge +7.06%; 7Ge +12.62%; 9Ge +17.22% (relative to 1Ge)
- Specific reported values: Nbss in 5Ge = 185.6 GPa; Nbss in 7Ge = 194.9 GPa; Nbss in base Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr = 155.5 GPa (previous study [14]).

**Nanohardness (nanoindentation):**
- Nbss nanohardness increases until 7 at.% Ge and then decreases with 9 at.% Ge.
- β-Nb5Si3 nanohardness increases from 1 to 5 at.% Ge, then decreases slightly.
- Specific numerical nanohardness values not given in text.

**High-temperature compression strength (1200 °C, strain rate 0.001 s⁻¹):**
- 9Ge: highest compression strength = 425 MPa.
- Strength steadily increases with Ge content. Specific values for other Ge contents not provided.
- Test atmosphere: not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- Strain: 20% (deformation to 20% strain).
- Loading mode: uniaxial compression.

**Fracture behavior:** Brittle cleavage fracture in all alloys. 1Ge and 3Ge show tearing ridges and dimples (ductile failure in Nbss during rupture). 5Ge, 7Ge, 9Ge show increased river patterns, cleavage steps, and cracks; more primary β-Nb5Si3 cleavage.

**Elastic modulus of Nbss effect on toughness (Ashby et al. [48]):** Higher elastic modulus of Nbss correlates with higher fracture toughness.

**Density:** Not explicitly measured or stated.

**Hardness (macro):** Not stated. Only nanohardness of phases reported.

**Creep/rupture life:** Not studied.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability
The paper does not experimentally study oxidation resistance of the Ge-added alloys. Only background information from literature:

- Introduction notes that lower contents of Cr and Al (e.g., 2 at.%) can enhance oxidation resistance by forming compact Cr2O3 and Al2O3 scales, while controlled low additions avoid damage to toughness.
- Ti, Zr, Hf can reinforce oxidation resistance of Nb-Si-based alloys (from literature [8,9]).
- Ge addition is reported in literature [10] to improve high-temperature oxidation resistance of coatings on Nb-Si alloys via formation of GeO2·SiO2 glass layer and to improve “pesting” oxidation behavior at moderate temperatures.
- **No oxidation weight gain, oxide layer characterization, or oxidation test data are reported for the studied Ge-added bulk alloys.**

Service stability issues mentioned:
- β-Nb5Si3 → α-Nb5Si3 + Nbss eutectoid decomposition is sluggish and temperature-dependent. In the studied alloys, β→α transformation after hot compression at 1200 °C with 20% strain occurred only in 1Ge alloy.
- Cracks and holes form principally in primary silicides during high-temperature compression due to brittleness and large size.
- No discussion of oxygen embrittlement, nitrogen embrittlement, or grain-boundary oxidation in this paper.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Ge]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** Nb-Si-Ti-Al-Cr in-situ composite (Nb-silicide based alloys); Ge-added hypereutectic Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 at.% (nominal). In constituent phases: up to ~13 at.% Ge in β-Nb5Si3 (9Ge alloy); 1.40–4.02 at.% in Nbss; 2.50–15.27 at.% in Tiss.
- **Main Function:** Promotes formation of β-Nb5Si3; restrains formation of Nb3Si; elevates elastic modulus and nanohardness of constituent phases; elevates high-temperature compression strength; improves room-temperature fracture toughness up to ~1 at.% Ge (relative to base alloy); stabilizes β-Nb5Si3 at lower temperatures; promotes chaotic Nbss/β-Nb5Si3 eutectics.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Ge substitutes for Si in β-Nb5Si3 lattice; stabilizes W5Si3-type β-Nb5Si3. Ge solid solution in Nbss causes contraction of Nbss lattice because Ge (1.39 Å) is smaller than Nb (1.47 Å). Ge shifts alloy composition point in Nb-Si binary phase diagram toward higher effective Si content, promoting β-Nb5Si3. Ge strengthens bonding within constituent phases. In 9Ge, Ge segregation leads to simultaneous formation of Nb5Ge3 and β-Nb5Si3 with identical crystal orientation.
- **Property Effect:** 1Ge alloy has max fracture toughness 18.47 MPa·m^1/2 (36.92% higher than base 13.49 MPa·m^1/2). Toughness decreases with Ge ≥ 3 at.%; 9Ge toughness is lower than base. High-temperature compression strength at 1200 °C/0.001 s⁻¹ increases with Ge content; 9Ge shows 425 MPa (highest). Elastic modulus of Nbss increases by 21.23% (5Ge), 27.30% (7Ge), 32.66% (9Ge) relative to 1Ge; β-Nb5Si3 modulus increases by 7.06% (5Ge), 12.62% (7Ge), 17.22% (9Ge). Nanohardness of Nbss increases up to 7 at.% Ge then decreases; β-Nb5Si3 nanohardness increases up to 5 at.% Ge then slightly decreases.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Increasing Ge content increases volume fraction of brittle β-Nb5Si3 (from 43.48% at 1Ge to 70.51% at 9Ge) and decreases ductile Nbss (56.52% → 29.49%), reducing room-temperature fracture toughness. More primary β-Nb5Si3 promotes crack propagation. Large primary β-Nb5Si3 is prone to cracking and hole formation during high-temperature deformation. In 9Ge, Nb5Ge3 particles form.
- **Original Evidence:** “Ge intensively promoted the formation of β-Nb5Si3 and restrained the formation of Nb3Si.” “1Ge alloy exhibited the max KQ of 18.47 MPa·m^{1/2}.” “The 9Ge alloy exhibits the highest compression strength (425 MPa).” Tables 1–3, Figs. 1, 3, 6, 7.

## [Si]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** Nb-Si based in-situ composites; Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr(-xGe) alloys.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 16 at.% nominal in alloy; dissolved Si in Nbss ranges 1.29–2.83 at.% (Ge-added alloys); base alloy Nbss contains 4.13 at.% Si (from previous study [14]).
- **Main Function:** Forms silicide phases (β-Nb5Si3) that provide high-temperature strength and creep resistance; as a solid solution element in Nbss, Si strongly embrittles Nbss via Nb–Si covalent bonds.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Forms β-Nb5Si3 silicide phase; Si in solid solution in Nbss damages toughness through strong Nb–Si covalent bonding; interplanar spacing shifts in XRD follow Bragg equation due to Ti and Ge solution.
- **Property Effect:** Increasing effective Si content (via Ge addition) increases volume fraction of β-Nb5Si3, increasing high-temperature strength and decreasing room-temperature fracture toughness. Literature cited: Nb-xSi-27.63Ti-12.92Cr-2.07Al-1.12Hf (x=0, 0.5, 1, 2 at.%) toughness sharply decreased with minor Si. Directionally solidified Nb-xSi-22Ti-3Cr-3Al-2Hf (x=3, 9, 16 at.%): toughness 19.25, 18.91, 14.66 MPa·m^1/2, respectively.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Embrittlement of Nbss; formation of brittle silicides that crack during deformation.
- **Original Evidence:** “Si leads to huge damage on toughness of Nbss because of the strong Nb–Si covalent bond.” Section 4.2, references [43–47].

## [Ti]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** Nb-Si-Ti-Al-Cr(-Ge) alloys; established in literature for Nb-Si binary/ternary systems.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 22 at.% nominal; Ti in Nbss 7.98–22.03 at.%; Ti in Tiss 14.13–31.39 at.%; Ti in β-Nb5Si3 8.55–16.53 at.% (Ge-added alloys).
- **Main Function:** Improves room-temperature toughness and oxidation resistance; can change lattice parameter of Nbss; forms Tiss along interfaces; influences phase transformations.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Ti (1.45 Å radius) is almost same size as Nb (1.47 Å). Varying Ti content in Nbss changes Nb lattice contraction due to chemical bonding changes; lower Ti content leads to smaller contraction. Ti can decrease transformation temperature between α-Nb5Si3 and β-Nb5Si3 (from literature via first-principles). Tiss forms as black phase at Nbss/β-Nb5Si3 interfaces.
- **Property Effect:** Ti content variation in Nbss explains XRD peak shifts. The high Ti content in Nbss in 1Ge, 7Ge, 9Ge vs lower in 3Ge, 5Ge. Literature notes Ti improves toughness and oxidation resistance but excessive Ti (>24 at.%) decreases melting point and ductile-brittle transition temperature.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Excessive Ti decreases melting point and ductile-brittle transition temperature; should not exceed 24 at.%.
- **Original Evidence:** Table 1; “The solid solution contents of Ti in Nbss are, respectively, 11.11 and 7.98 at.% in 3Ge and 5Ge alloys.” Introduction section.

## [Al + Cr]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** Nb-Si-Ti-Al-Cr alloys (Nb-silicide based).
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 2 at.% Al + 2 at.% Cr (base composition). In phases: Al 0.71–2.32 at.% in silicides and Nbss; Cr 0.50–3.50 at.% in constituent phases.
- **Main Function:** Lower contents improve high-temperature oxidation resistance (Al forms Al2O3, Cr forms Cr2O3 protective scales); content controlled to 2 at.% each to avoid damage to toughness.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Formation of compact protective oxide layers (Al2O3, Cr2O3) based on literature background. In the present paper, Al and Cr partition among phases; no direct oxidation experiment.
- **Property Effect:** No specific property values from this paper’s experiments; oxidation improvement is literature-based.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Higher Cr and Al content can damage toughness; therefore minor contents (2 at.%) are controlled.
- **Original Evidence:** “The lower contents of Cr and Al can effectively enhance the oxidation resistance by forming compact Cr2O3, and Al2O3 scales, respectively, and minor contents of added Cr and Al such as 2 at.% are controlled to avoid the damage on toughness.”

## [Zr + Y2O3 / ODS-related] 
Not applicable to this paper. No Zr or Y2O3 used in this study.

## [Ge + Si combination]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ge-Nb-Si ternary; Nb-Si-Ti-Al-Cr-Ge quinary.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Ge 1–9 at.% with Si 16 at.%.
- **Main Function:** Ge behaves similarly to Si; shifts effective Si content; promotes β-Nb5Si3; Ge and Si can replace each other in W5Si3 structure.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Similar properties of Ge and Si shift composition point from lower Si to higher Si content zone in Nb–Si phase diagram, promoting β-Nb5Si3. Ge can stabilize β-Nb5Si3 at 1200 °C with content over ~12.4 at.% in silicide (from Utton et al. [38]).
- **Property Effect:** β-Nb5Si3 formation; high-temperature strength; no direct oxidation data in this paper.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Increase in brittle silicide volume fraction; possible Nb5Ge3 formation at higher Ge contents.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 4.1 discussion; “According to the experimental and thermodynamic assessment of the Ge-Nb-Si ternary phase diagram [38], lower amounts of Ge could stabilize β-Nb5Si3 (W5Si3 structure) such as 3.4 at.% at 1200 °C.”

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-solution strengthening
- **Related elements:** Ge, Ti, Al, Cr, Si in Nbss.
- **Typical alloys:** Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr-xGe (x=1–9).
- **Key compositions:** Ge in Nbss 1.40–4.02 at.%; Ti in Nbss 7.98–22.03 at.%; Si in Nbss 1.29–2.83 at.%.
- **Microstructural changes:** Ge and Ti dissolve in Nbss; lattice contraction due to Ge (1.39 Å) and Ti (1.45 Å) relative to Nb (1.47 Å); changes in chemical bonding. Ti content variation causes XRD peak shifts.
- **Property improvements:** Ge increases elastic modulus of Nbss by 21.23–32.66% (5–9Ge vs 1Ge) and nanohardness up to 7 at.% Ge; increases β-Nb5Si3 modulus by 7.06–17.22%.
- **Risks and limitations:** Si in Nbss severely embrittles via strong Nb–Si covalent bond; Ge addition simultaneously increases volume fraction of brittle β-Nb5Si3, reducing toughness.
- **Original evidence:** “The increase in the elastic modulus and nanohardness of constituent phases is possibly due to strengthening the bonding in the constituent phases by Ge.” Fig. 6, Table 1.

## Ductile-phase toughening (analogous to grain/grain-boundary and second-phase toughening)
- **Related elements/phases:** Ductile Nbss phase + brittle β-Nb5Si3.
- **Typical alloys:** Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr-xGe (x=1–9).
- **Key compositions:** Nbss volume fraction 56.52% (1Ge) → 29.49% (9Ge); β-Nb5Si3 43.48% (1Ge) → 70.51% (9Ge).
- **Microstructural changes:** Chaotic Nbss/β-Nb5Si3 eutectics (eutectics I fine, eutectics II coarse); eutectic fractions decrease with Ge.
- **Property improvements:** 1Ge alloy fracture toughness 18.47 MPa·m^1/2 (36.92% higher than base). Crack deflection, branching, bridging, and microcracks are effective toughening methods.
- **Risks and limitations:** More primary brittle β-Nb5Si3 and less Nbss reduce toughness. Chaotic eutectics almost disappear in 9Ge, contributing to low toughness.
- **Original evidence:** “The volume fractions of Nbss and β-Nb5Si3 play the pivotal role on toughening of Nb–Si alloy”; “chaotic eutectics of Nbss/β-Nb5Si3 are conducive to toughening.”

## Grain-boundary strengthening / Dynamic recrystallization during high-temperature deformation
- **Related elements:** Not element-specific; mechanism determined by phase type (ductile Nbss vs brittle silicides).
- **Typical alloys:** 1Ge and 9Ge deformed at 1200 °C/0.001 s⁻¹ to 20% strain.
- **Key compositions:** 1Ge (Nbss 56.52%, β-Nb5Si3 43.48%); 9Ge (Nbss 29.49%, β-Nb5Si3 70.51%).
- **Microstructural changes:** Dislocations generate and accumulate into LAGBs; LAGBs transform to HAGBs; dynamic recrystallization occurs. Nbss recrystallized fraction: 1Ge 1.7%→11.5%; 9Ge 9.8%→14.3%. Nb5Si3 recrystallized fraction: 1Ge 7.6%→20.7%; 9Ge 1.0%→6.3%.
- **Property improvements:** Flow softening during deformation; avoidance of cracks in fine recrystallized Nb5Si3 in 1Ge.
- **Risks and limitations:** Large primary β-Nb5Si3 in 9Ge undergoes cracking and hole formation; dynamic recrystallization limited at only 20% strain; brittle silicides show little deformation.
- **Original evidence:** Sections 3.4, 4.3; Figs. 10–15; Table 4.

# Key Conclusions
1. All Ge-added alloys (Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr-xGe, x = 1–9 at.%) consist of primary β-Nb5Si3 and chaotic Nbss/β-Nb5Si3 eutectics. Ge intensively promotes β-Nb5Si3 formation and suppresses Nb3Si; no metastable Nb3Si in Ge-added alloys.
2. Compared to base Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr (KQ = 13.49 MPa·m^1/2), fracture toughness increases when Ge < 9 at.%. The 1Ge alloy shows the highest KQ = 18.47 MPa·m^1/2 (36.92% higher), attributed to abundant chaotic Nbss/β-Nb5Si3 eutectics. With further Ge increase, toughness decreases due to higher volume fraction of brittle β-Nb5Si3.
3. Ge increases elastic modulus and nanohardness of both Nbss and β-Nb5Si3 via strengthening interatomic bonding.
4. High-temperature compression strength at 1200 °C/0.001 s⁻¹ increases with Ge content; 9Ge alloy shows maximum 425 MPa, primarily due to increased volume fraction of primary β-Nb5Si3.
5. Dynamic recrystallization is limited at 20% compression strain; ductile Nbss undergoes more recrystallization than brittle silicides; dynamic recrystallization occurs mainly in fine β-Nb5Si3 particles (eutectic/fragments).
6. β-Nb5Si3 transforms to α-Nb5Si3 after high-temperature compression (1200 °C, 20% strain) only in 1Ge alloy; other Ge-added alloys retain β-Nb5Si3.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- All studied alloys are in as-cast (arc-melted) condition; no wrought/PM/AM processing routes or heat treatments were studied.
- The paper focuses on Nb-Si-Ti-Al-Cr-Ge system; findings may not directly transfer to W-based, Mo-based, or refractory high-entropy alloys.
- Oxidation resistance was not experimentally measured; oxidation-related statements are based on literature background.
- High-temperature compression was conducted only at 1200 °C and 0.001 s⁻¹ (20% strain); behavior at other temperatures, strain rates, or service conditions is not covered.
- Fracture toughness values are reported as KQ, not valid K_IC; obtained from notched three-point bending specimens with limited plastic deformation.
- No tensile tests, creep tests, fatigue tests, or density/hardness (macro) measurements are reported.
- The effect of Ge on oxidation resistance of bulk alloys was not explored, despite being a known application goal.
- The research does not include quantitative nanoindentation hardness values (only trends reported).
