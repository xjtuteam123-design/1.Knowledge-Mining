# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Study on thermal fatigue stability in microstructure and mechanical property of Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 high-entropy alloy wire and pearlite wire at high/medium temperature environment
- **Authors:** Mengchuan Shi, Xinghui Pan, Dan Wu, Hengcheng Liao, Guangjing Li, Hongfang Liu, Weijun Zhu
- **Journal:** International Journal of Fatigue
- **Year:** 2024
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2024.108274
- **Alloy system studied:** Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 high-entropy alloy (HEA) wire, compared with pearlite (high-carbon steel) wire
- **Research objective:** To study the thermal fatigue stability in microstructure and mechanical property of Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wire and pearlite wire at 400–700 °C under low-cycle thermal fatigue conditions, with the goal of evaluating the potential of HEA wire to replace pearlite wire in medium/high temperature thermal fatigue environments.

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Material category:** High-entropy alloy (single-phase FCC) wire and pearlite (ferrite + cementite) steel wire.  
- **Alloy system:** Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 (nominal at.%); the actual composition in wt.% is given (see below). The paper is **not** about Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys.  
- **Comparative material:** Pearlite wire with a nominal composition of C 0.59–0.66 wt.%, Si 0.15–0.35 wt.%, Mn 0.30–0.60 wt.%, S 0.00–0.03 wt.%, P 0.00–0.03 wt.%, balance Fe.  
- **Research object:** Thermal fatigue (low-cycle, 400–700 °C, 60–300 cycles) stability of microstructure and mechanical properties of cold-drawn wires.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Pearlite wire processing
- Cold-drawn from a bar of diameter 5.5 mm to a wire of diameter 1.6 mm at room temperature.
- Subsequently annealed.
- Finally drawn into a wire of diameter 0.45 mm in a water tank.
- Accumulated drawing strain, ε = 2.54.

## Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wire processing
- Continuously cold-drawn from a bar of diameter 6.34 mm to a wire of diameter 0.45 mm at room temperature.
- Accumulated drawing strain, ε = 5.29.

## Thermal fatigue treatment (both wires)
- Sample length: 45 cm.
- Test temperatures: 400 °C, 500 °C, 600 °C, 700 °C.
- Heating time per cycle: 1 min in a muffle furnace (preheated and held for 30 min to ensure uniform temperature).
- Cooling time per cycle: 1 min air cooling outside the furnace.
- Constraint: no constrained ends (simplified unconstrained thermal cycling).
- Number of cycles: 60 cycles for most tests; additional tests at 500 °C and 600 °C for 180 or 300 cycles for HEA wire; 180 cycles at 600 °C for pearlite wire.

## Supplementary isothermal annealing treatment
- Temperature: 400–700 °C.
- Duration: 60 min.
- Performed on both pearlite wire and HEA wire with diameter 0.45 mm for comparison with thermal fatigue treatment.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Pearlite wire in as-drawn state
- Multi-layer structure parallel to the drawing direction, consisting of ferrite (bright) and cementite (narrow, grey) lamellae.
- Average pearlite lamellar spacing: ~25 nm.
- Average ferrite lamellar thickness: ~20 nm.
- Cementite lamella thickness: ~5 nm.
- Cementite lamellae are discontinuous and nano-crystallized due to extreme plastic deformation (nano-crystallization of cementite).
- High density of crystal defects (simple and entangled dislocations) in ferrite.
- Cementite may decompose during cold drawing; weak diffraction spots of cementite suggest partial decomposition.
- C atoms segregated in ferrite due to cementite decomposition; some diffraction spots of ferrite show tail fins/drag phenomena.
- SAED of ferrite: (0 1 1) plane spacing 0.203 nm, (0 0 2) 0.143 nm, (1 1 2) 0.117 nm; cementite (1 2 1) plane spacing 0.209 nm.
- Fe:C atom ratio in ferrite in as-drawn state: 84.50:15.50 (TEM-EDS), indicating severe supersaturation of C in ferrite.

## Pearlite wire after thermal fatigue
- **400 °C:** Multilayer structure faintly visible; ferrite/cementite interface no longer clear; nano-sized cementite particles formed at original cementite lamellae, larger than in as-drawn state.
- **500 °C:** Cementite particles spheroidize and grow along lamellae direction; bamboo-like recovery structure in some ferrite layers; local recovery.
- **600 °C:** More and larger spherical cementite particles; original ferrite multilayer structure almost disappears; replaced by equiaxed-grain structure.
- **700 °C:** Ferrite grain size increases significantly; polygonized ferrite; cementite particles larger.
- C atom distribution changes from fine strips (as-drawn) to spherical distribution at ferrite grain boundaries (after 700 °C thermal fatigue).

## Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wire in as-drawn state
- Single-phase FCC matrix.
- Multi-layer structure parallel to drawing direction.
- Fine nanoscale deformation twins between layers, formed to coordinate deformation between layers.
- SAED of FCC matrix: (1 1 1) plane spacing 0.207 nm, (0 0 2) 0.180 nm, (0 2 2) 0.127 nm, (1 1 3) 0.108 nm.

## Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wire after thermal fatigue
- **400 °C and 500 °C:** Multilayer structure does not change at all; no discernible microstructural evolution.
- **600 °C:** Local bamboo-like recovery structure appears in some layers; recovery starts.
- **700 °C:** Multilayer strip structure still discernible; some recrystallized grains have formed locally.
- Deformation twins persist after 400 °C and 500 °C thermal fatigue.
- Annealing twins observed after 600 °C and 700 °C thermal fatigue.
- No obvious element segregation before and after thermal fatigue (TEM-element mapping), attributed to sluggish diffusion effect.
- XRD (1 1 1) peak shift is negligible (from 43.457° to 43.510° after 700 °C thermal fatigue), indicating no phase transformation and only slight release of lattice distortion.

## Dislocation density (Williamson-Hall method from XRD)
- Pearlite wire as-drawn: 17.5 × 10¹³ m⁻².
- Pearlite wire after 700 °C thermal fatigue: 3.8 × 10¹³ m⁻² (decreased by 78.3 %).
- HEA wire as-drawn: 13.92 × 10¹³ m⁻².
- HEA wire after 700 °C thermal fatigue: 5.67 × 10¹³ m⁻² (decreased by 59.3 %).
- HEA wire dislocation density does not change below 500 °C thermal fatigue; starts to decrease at 600 °C.

## Phase constituents and stability
- Pearlite wire comprises ferrite (BCC) + cementite (Fe₃C); Fe₃C cannot be detected by XRD because Cu-Kα radiation was used.
- HEA wire is single-phase FCC; no phase transformation (no exothermic/endothermic peaks) in the range 50–800 °C by DSC.

# Mechanical Properties

## Tensile testing conditions
- Room-temperature tensile test.
- Constant displacement speed: 1 mm/min.
- Gauge length: 50 ± 0.5 mm (measured by vernier caliper).
- Extension measured by crosshead displacement; no extensometer due to small wire diameter (0.45 mm).
- Data are average of three samples.

## Pearlite wire
| State | UTS (MPa) | YS (MPa) |
|---|---|---|
| As-drawn | 1995 | 1816 |
| Thermal fatigue 400 °C (60 cycles) | 1834 | 1653 |
| Thermal fatigue 500 °C (60 cycles) | 1473 | 1369 |
| Thermal fatigue 600 °C (60 cycles) | 1011 | 1006 |
| Thermal fatigue 700 °C (60 cycles) | 646 | 606 |
| Isothermal annealing 400 °C (60 min) | 1749 | 1564 |
| Isothermal annealing 500 °C (60 min) | 1323 | 1233 |
| Isothermal annealing 600 °C (60 min) | 950 | 944 |
| Isothermal annealing 700 °C (60 min) | 632 | 583 |

- UTS decrease from as-drawn to 700 °C thermal fatigue: 67.6 %.
- YS decrease from as-drawn to 700 °C thermal fatigue: 66.6 %.
- Significant strength drop already observed after 400–500 °C thermal fatigue.
- Strain does not rise monotonously with thermal fatigue temperature; decreases at 500 °C and reaches trough at 600 °C (tempering brittleness-like behavior); result verified by repeated tests.

## Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wire
| State | UTS (MPa) | YS (MPa) |
|---|---|---|
| As-drawn | 1548 | 1410 |
| Thermal fatigue 400 °C (60 cycles) | 1581 | 1432 |
| Thermal fatigue 500 °C (60 cycles) | 1553 | 1407 |
| Thermal fatigue 600 °C (60 cycles) | 1313 | 1144 |
| Thermal fatigue 700 °C (60 cycles) | 681 | 553 |
| Isothermal annealing 400 °C (60 min) | 1562 | 1421 |
| Isothermal annealing 500 °C (60 min) | 1551 | 1417 |
| Isothermal annealing 600 °C (60 min) | 1202 | 1009 |
| Isothermal annealing 700 °C (60 min) | 672 | 506 |

- No strength decrease after thermal fatigue at 400 °C and 500 °C compared with as-drawn state.
- UTS decrease from as-drawn to 700 °C thermal fatigue: 56.0 %.
- YS decrease from as-drawn to 700 °C thermal fatigue: 60.8 %.
- At 700 °C thermal fatigue, decrement percentages are lower than those for pearlite wire.
- HEA wire UTS and YS after 500 °C and 600 °C thermal fatigue (60 cycles) are higher than those of pearlite wire, even though HEA wire as-drawn strength is much lower.

## Effect of cycle number
- HEA wire at 500 °C: UTS and YS change is negligible up to 300 cycles.
- Pearlite wire at 600 °C from 60 to 180 cycles: UTS decreases by 14.9 %, YS decreases by 15.6 %.
- HEA wire at 600 °C from 60 to 180 cycles: UTS decreases by 4.9 %, YS decreases by 3.8 %.

## Yield strength vs. dislocation density
- YS approximately linearly related to dislocation density (Bailey-Hirsch relationship qualitatively demonstrated).

## Creep, fracture toughness, hardness, elastic modulus, density
- Not reported in the original paper.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- The original paper does **not** study oxidation behavior, oxidation weight gain, oxide layer type, or oxidation temperature.
- The focus is on **thermal fatigue stability** (microstructural and mechanical stability during cyclic heating/cooling without external load) rather than oxidation.
- However, the following service stability issues are discussed:
  - Pearlite wire shows **high temperature sensitivity**: severe strength loss, cementite spheroidization, ferrite recovery/recrystallization, and C depletion from ferrite.
  - Pearlite wire exhibits **tempering brittleness** at 500 °C and 600 °C under both thermal fatigue and isothermal annealing; strain notably decreases at 500–600 °C.
  - HEA wire shows superior thermal stability at 400–500 °C with no strength loss and negligible microstructural change; at 600–700 °C recovery/recrystallization occurs but to a lesser extent than pearlite wire.
- High-temperature embrittlement, oxygen embrittlement, nitrogen embrittlement, grain-boundary oxidation: not explicitly stated in the original paper.

## C (Carbon) in Pearlite Wire

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Pearlite (high-carbon steel) wire
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 0.59–0.66 wt.% (nominal); Fe:C atom ratio in ferrite in as-drawn state: 84.50:15.50; after 700 °C thermal fatigue: 97.43:2.57
- **Main Function:** Forms cementite (Fe₃C) lamellae; provides precipitation strengthening and solid-solution strengthening in ferrite due to supersaturation.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** During cold drawing, cementite decomposes; C atoms dissolve into ferrite, creating severe supersaturation. Supersaturated C atoms form Cottrell atmospheres that pin dislocations and cause lattice distortion, leading to solid-solution strengthening. Upon thermal fatigue, C precipitates as carbide particles; cementite lamellae fragment, spheroidize, and coarsen.
- **Property Effect:** As-drawn UTS 1995 MPa, YS 1816 MPa. After 700 °C thermal fatigue, UTS 646 MPa, YS 606 MPa (decrease of 67.6 % and 66.6 %). C diffusion coefficient in ferrite: 6.0 × 10⁻¹³ m²/s at 400 °C; 4.2 × 10⁻¹² at 500 °C; 1.9 × 10⁻¹¹ at 600 °C; 6.2 × 10⁻¹¹ at 700 °C.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Severe supersaturation of C and high fraction of ferrite/cementite interface cause microstructural instability; leads to drastic strength loss during thermal fatigue; causes tempering brittleness at 500–600 °C.
- **Original Evidence:** Figs. 1–3, 13–14; discussion section; Table 1.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Fe, Ni, Cr, Mn in HEA; C in ferrite (pearlite wire).
- **Typical Alloys:** Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA; pearlite steel wire with supersaturated ferrite.
- **Key Compositions:** Fe 32.46 wt.%, Ni 37.82 wt.%, Cr 18.84 wt.%, Mn 10.36 wt.% (HEA); C up to ~15.5 at.% in ferrite in as-drawn pearlite (Fe:C ratio 84.50:15.50).
- **Microstructural Changes:** In HEA, multi-principal elements distributed randomly in a single-phase FCC lattice; large lattice distortion. In pearlite, severe supersaturation of C in ferrite due to cementite decomposition during cold drawing; lattice distortion of ferrite.
- **Property Improvements:** HEA as-drawn YS 1410 MPa, UTS 1548 MPa; pearlite as-drawn YS 1816 MPa, UTS 1995 MPa.
- **Risks and Limitations:** In pearlite, supersaturated C is thermodynamically unstable, leading to rapid strength loss during thermal fatigue; in HEA, lattice distortion is more stable due to sluggish diffusion.
- **Original Evidence:** Discussion; Figs. 12–15.

## Precipitation Strengthening / Carbide Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** C (cementite Fe₃C) in pearlite wire.
- **Typical Alloys:** Pearlite high-carbon steel wire.
- **Key Compositions:** C 0.59–0.66 wt.%; cementite lamellae thickness ~5 nm in as-drawn state.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Cementite lamellae fragment, spheroidize, and coarsen during thermal fatigue; nano-sized cementite particles form at 400 °C and grow further at higher temperatures.
- **Property Improvements:** As-drawn UTS 1995 MPa, YS 1816 MPa; cementite lamellae block dislocation motion.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Spheroidization and coarsening at 500–700 °C severely reduce strength; tempering brittleness at 500–600 °C.
- **Original Evidence:** Fig. 2; discussion.

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening / Layer-Boundary Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Not element-specific; structural feature of cold-drawn wires.
- **Typical Alloys:** Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wire and pearlite wire.
- **Key Compositions:** Pearlite lamellar spacing ~25 nm; ferrite lamellae thickness ~20 nm; HEA multi-layer structure thickness not explicitly stated.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Multi-layer structures with dense layer boundaries in both wires hinder dislocation movement; during thermal fatigue, recovery and recrystallization consume these boundaries, reducing strength.
- **Property Improvements:** High as-drawn strength (see above).
- **Risks and Limitations:** Layer boundaries are unstable at elevated temperatures; recrystallization leads to strength loss.
- **Original Evidence:** Discussion, Fig. 1, Fig. 4.

## Grain Refinement / Boundary Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Not element-specific; generated by heavy cold drawing.
- **Typical Alloys:** Both HEA and pearlite wires.
- **Key Compositions:** Pearlite lamellar spacing ~25 nm; ferrite thickness ~20 nm; HEA layer thickness not explicitly stated.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Heavy drawing creates nano-scale lamellar structures with high dislocation density; grain refinement contributes to Hall-Petch strengthening.
- **Property Improvements:** As-drawn pearlite UTS 1995 MPa, YS 1816 MPa; HEA UTS 1548 MPa, YS 1410 MPa.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Fine structures are thermodynamically unstable; coarsening at 600–700 °C reduces strength.
- **Original Evidence:** Introduction; Figs. 1, 2, 4; discussion.

## Deformation Twinning / Annealing Twinning

- **Related Elements:** FCC alloy feature; common in low stacking fault energy materials.
- **Typical Alloys:** Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wire.
- **Key Compositions:** Single-phase FCC matrix.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Nanoscale deformation twins form between layers during cold drawing; after 400–500 °C thermal fatigue, deformation twins persist; after 600–700 °C, annealing twins form.
- **Property Improvements:** Twin boundaries increase dislocation motion resistance and provide stable strain-hardening capability; contributes to maintaining high strength even after thermal fatigue.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence:** Fig. 1d; Fig. 17; discussion.

# Key Conclusions

1. Pearlite wire microstructure degrades progressively with thermal fatigue temperature: recovery at 400 °C, incomplete recrystallization at 500 °C, complete recrystallization at 600 °C, and grain coarsening at 700 °C.
2. Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wire shows excellent microstructural stability at 400 °C and 500 °C (no change in multilayer structure); recovery starts at 600 °C and local recrystallization at 700 °C.
3. Pearlite wire UTS/YS decrease significantly with thermal fatigue temperature; after 700 °C thermal fatigue, UTS drops by 67.6 % and YS by 66.6 %.
4. HEA wire retains full strength after 400 °C and 500 °C thermal fatigue; after 700 °C, UTS decreases by 56.0 % and YS by 60.8 %.
5. HEA wire after 500 °C and 600 °C thermal fatigue has higher UTS and YS than pearlite wire, despite lower as-drawn strength.
6. Increasing thermal fatigue cycles from 60 to 180 at 600 °C causes greater strength degradation in pearlite wire (UTS −14.9 %, YS −15.6 %) than in HEA wire (UTS −4.9 %, YS −3.8 %).
7. Both thermal fatigue and isothermal annealing at 500–600 °C induce obvious tempering brittleness in pearlite wire.
8. HEA wire exhibits superior phase stability due to sluggish diffusion and the absence of phase transformations (no DSC peaks between 50–800 °C), while pearlite wire shows C precipitation, cementite spheroidization, and phase transformation near 740–760 °C.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy system applicability:** The paper studies only Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wire and pearlite steel wire. Conclusions should not be extrapolated to refractory alloys or other HEA systems without verification.
- **Temperature range:** 400–700 °C; behavior below 400 °C or above 700 °C is not covered, except for DSC scans up to 800 °C.
- **Cyclic loading:** Thermal fatigue was performed without mechanical load and with no constrained ends; actual service conditions (combined thermal and mechanical fatigue, constrained ends) may differ.
- **Sample geometry:** Wires with a diameter of only 0.45 mm; results may not scale to bulk components.
- **Testing conditions:** Tensile tests were performed at room temperature after thermal fatigue; no high-temperature tensile tests were conducted.
- **Characterization limits:** Fe₃C cannot be detected by XRD with Cu-Kα radiation; C content determined by EDS has limited accuracy.
- **Oxidation resistance:** Not studied in this paper.
- **The tempering brittleness mechanism in pearlite wire at 500–600 °C is not explained; the paper states the reason “isn’t clear, needing further study.”**
