# Paper Metadata
- **Title**: Design and development of low density, high strength ZrNbAlVTi high entropy alloy for high temperature applications
- **Authors**: Poulami Chakraborty, Apu Sarkar, Kawsar Ali, Jyoti Jha, N. Jothilakshmi, Ashok Arya, Raghvendra Tewari
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106222
- **Alloy system studied**: Equiatomic ZrNbVTiAl high entropy alloy (refractory-based, low-density HEA)
- **Research objective**: Design, synthesize, and evaluate a low-density, high-strength, bcc-based refractory HEA for high-temperature nuclear structural applications (targeting Generation IV fission and fusion reactor conditions up to 1000 °C)
- **Material category**: Refractory high-entropy alloy (multiphase; primarily B2/bcc matrix with Al–Zr intermetallics)

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Target application**: Structural material for next-generation nuclear reactors (Generation IV fission and fusion), operating at temperatures up to 1000 °C; lightweight structural applications
- **Design strategy**: Equiatomic five-component HEA combining refractory elements (Nb, Zr, V, Ti) with light element Al to achieve low density, bcc-based structure, good high-temperature strength, low neutron absorption cross-section (Nb, Zr, Al), and satisfactory workability
- **Design tools**: CALPHAD (Thermo-Calc with TCHEA5 database) and DFT-based first-principles simulations (VASP, ATAT/SQS)

# Alloy Composition
- **Alloy name**: ZrNbVTiAl (also written as ZrNbAlVTi in title)
- **Nominal composition**: Equiatomic, i.e., 20 at.% each of Zr, Nb, V, Ti, Al
- **Weight percentage**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper (requires conversion)
- **Measured composition after synthesis**:
  - Dendritic regions (average): 22Nb–23Ti–22V–18Al–15Zr (at.%)
  - Interdendritic regions (average): 10Nb–8Ti–5V–36Al–41Zr (at.%)
- **Impurities**: Not explicitly reported; authors note that short melting time in levitation melting ensured negligible impurity pickup
- **Elements present**: Nb, V, Ti, Zr, Al (all five principal elements); no W, Mo, Ta, Hf, Cr, Si, C, B, Y, or La in the composition
- **Density (experimental)**: 5.76 g/cm³
- **DFT-calculated density**: 5.891 g/cm³

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Synthesis method**: Induction levitation melting (ILM)
  - 20 kW MOSFET-based induction heating unit
  - Operating frequency: ~170 kHz
  - Maximum output voltage: 1000 V
  - Water-cooled segmented copper crucible (8 fingers, 20 mm inside diameter) housed in quartz tube
  - Vacuum level: 10⁻³ mbar (rotary vacuum pump)
  - Initial charge: 27 g
  - Final button weight: 25.12 g
  - Melting temperature range: 2000–2400 °C
- **Excess Al addition**: 10% excess aluminum added to account for vaporization losses
- **Post-melting processing**: None (as-cast condition only)
- **Heat treatment**: None reported
- **Deformation processing**: None prior to testing (compression tests performed on as-cast material)
- **Sample preparation**: EDM cutting, metallographic grinding (SiC up to 1200 grit), diamond polishing, colloidal silica final polishing (1 μm)

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
- **Primary phase**: BCC (B2 ordered) phase, approximately 90% volume fraction (EBSD); described as “ordered bcc (B2) phase” in as-cast condition
- **Secondary phases**:
  - Zr₅Al₃ and ZrAl₂ intermetallics identified by XRD
  - Total intermetallic fraction: ~8% by Rietveld refinement (XRD) / ~8% by EBSD phase map
  - CALPHAD predicted ~4% intermetallics (EBSD measured); discrepancy attributed to non-equilibrium solidification during levitation melting
  - Additional Ti–Al type phases also detected in EBSD phase map
- **Grain size**:
  - As-cast: large grains with diameters close to 300 μm (EBSD)
  - As-cast dendritic grain size: ~60 μm
  - After DRX at 900 °C and 1000 °C: ~2 μm (recrystallized grains)
  - After DRX at 1000 °C: grain diameter approximately half of that obtained at 900 °C
- **Solidification structure**: Dendritic microstructure
  - Dendrite cores: enriched in Nb, Ti, V (composition: 22Nb–23Ti–22V–18Al–15Zr at.%)
  - Interdendritic regions: enriched in Al and Zr (composition: 10Nb–8Ti–5V–36Al–41Zr at.%)
  - Sub-micron sized interdendritic phases present
  - Intermetallic segregation attributed to delayed solidification of Zr–Al intermetallics (deep eutectic in Zr–Al binary system)
- **Phase stability (DFT)**:
  - Enthalpy of mixing (H_mix): −11.37 kJ/mol (negative)
  - Enthalpy of formation (H_form): −5.15 kJ/mol (negative)
  - Entropy of mixing (S_mix): 13.37 kJ/mol (satisfies high-entropy criterion)
  - Empirical parameter φ = 6.35 (indicates multiphase formation, consistent with CALPHAD and experiment)
- **Liquidus temperature (CALPHAD)**: Less than 2000 K (1727 °C)
- **Single BCC (B2) phase region (CALPHAD)**: Exists from liquidus down to ~1400 K (1127 °C); intermetallics form below this temperature under equilibrium conditions
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed in paper**:
  - Solid-solution strengthening (multi-component bcc matrix)
  - Precipitation/second-phase strengthening from Al–Zr and Al–Ti intermetallics
  - Dynamic recrystallization-induced grain refinement (from ~60 μm to ~2 μm)

# Mechanical Properties
- **Room temperature compression**:
  - Yield strength (YS): 1.045 GPa (1045 MPa)
  - Ultimate (peak) compressive strength: ~1.25 GPa (at ~14% strain; crack initiation began at this strain)
  - Strong strain hardening behavior
  - Satisfactory ductility (cracks initiated only at ~14% strain)
  - Strain rate: 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹
  - Loading mode: Compression (cylindrical specimens, 4 mm diameter × 6 mm height)
- **High-temperature compression** (true stress–true strain; strain rate 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹; vacuum ~5 × 10⁻⁵ mbar):
  - Tests at 800 °C, 900 °C, and 1000 °C
  - Uniform deformation without cracking at all three temperatures
  - Flow stress initially increases, reaches a peak, then decreases (characteristic of dynamic recrystallization)
  - Deformed to up to 50% reduction
- **Specific yield strength (YS/density)**:
  - Superior at 800 °C and 1000 °C compared to NbTiVZr, HfNbTaTiZr, and CrNbVTiZr alloys (per Fig. 12)
  - ~100 GPa·g/cm³ at 800 °C (units in original paper appear to be MPa·g/cm³; notation ambiguous—see original text)
  - At room temperature: can be computed as 1045 MPa / 5.76 g/cm³ ≈ 181.4 MPa·g/cm³ (not explicitly calculated in paper)
- **Strain rate sensitivity (m)**:
  - Tested at 800 °C via strain rate jump test (10⁻⁴, 10⁻³, 10⁻² s⁻¹)
  - m ≈ 0.3 (typical of dynamic recrystallization)
- **Constitutive parameters (from hot deformation analysis)**:
  - Stress exponent n = 3.3
  - Activation energy Q = 302 kJ/mol
  - Zener-Hollomon parameter fit well (linear in log-log scale)
  - Q close to self-diffusion activation energies of bcc elements (Nb ~397 kJ/mol, V ~270 kJ/mol); higher than β-Zr (~126 kJ/mol), β-Ti (~130 kJ/mol), fcc Al (~142 kJ/mol)
  - Suggests dislocation climb controlled by lattice diffusion of bcc elements
- **Hardness**:
  - Vickers microhardness: 576 HVN (5.7 GPa)
  - Load: 300 gf; dwell time: 12 s; average of 10+ readings
- **Elastic properties (DFT calculated)**:
  - Single crystal elastic constants: C₁₁ = 144.80 GPa, C₁₂ = 107.91 GPa, C₄₄ = 47.99 GPa
  - Bulk modulus (B): 120.21 GPa
  - Shear modulus (G): 36.17 GPa
  - Young's modulus (E): 98.62 GPa
  - Poisson's ratio (ν): 0.363
  - Mechanical stability confirmed (C₁₁ + 2C₁₂ > 0; C₁₁ − C₁₂ > 0; C₄₄ > 0)
- **Elastic properties (experimental, ultrasonic)**:
  - Bulk modulus (B): 125.0 GPa
  - Shear modulus (G): 46 GPa
  - Young's modulus (E): 122.92 GPa
  - Poisson's ratio (ν): 0.336
  - Longitudinal wave transducer: 15 MHz; shear wave transducer: 5 MHz
  - Average of 5 measurements
- **Agreement between DFT and experiment**:
  - Bulk modulus: within 4%
  - Shear modulus: ~21% difference
  - Young's modulus: ~19% difference
- **Testing atmosphere**: Room temperature compression in air; high-temperature compression in vacuum (~5 × 10⁻⁵ mbar)
- **Fracture toughness, creep, rupture life, elastic modulus at high temperature**: Not reported

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability
- The original paper does not report oxidation resistance data, oxidation weight gain, or oxide layer characterization.
- High-temperature compression tests were conducted in vacuum (~5 × 10⁻⁵ mbar), so resistance to oxidation was not evaluated under the testing conditions.
- The presence of Al and Zr-intermetallics is implied to contribute to high-temperature strength retention; however, oxidation behavior was not explicitly investigated.
- No discussion of oxygen embrittlement, nitrogen embrittlement, grain-boundary oxidation, or high-temperature embrittlement in the context of experimental evaluation was provided.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Aluminum (Al)
- **[Element or Element Combination]**: Al
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Equiatomic ZrNbVTiAl refractory HEA
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 20 at.% (equiatomic); 10% excess Al added during melting to compensate for vaporization losses; interdendritic regions contain 36 at.% Al
- **[Main Function]**: Reduces overall alloy density; promotes formation of light-weight intermetallic strengthening phases; specific strength enhancement
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Strong ordering tendency with refractory elements (especially Zr and Ti); formation of Al–Zr intermetallics (Zr₅Al₃, ZrAl₂) and Al–Ti phases in interdendritic regions; similar atomic radius (143 pm) to Ti, Nb, V promotes solid solution formation; Al–Zr binary system has deep eutectic leading to delayed solidification and interdendritic segregation
- **[Property Effect]**:
  - Density reduced to 5.76 g/cm³ (vs. stainless steel 7.8 g/cm³, Inconel 718 8.22 g/cm³; lower than most refractory HEAs ≥6 g/cm³)
  - Specific yield strength superior at 800 °C and 1000 °C compared to Al-free refractory HEAs
  - Room temperature yield strength: 1045 MPa
  - Hardness: 576 HVN
- **[Potential Side Effects]**:
  - Formation of brittle intermetallic phases (Zr₅Al₃, ZrAl₂)
  - Elemental segregation to interdendritic regions reduces microstructural homogeneity
  - Vapor pressure during melting requires excess addition
- **[Original Evidence]**: Abstract; Section 1; Section 3.2; Figs. 3, 4, 5; Fig. 12; References [29, 36]

  - Promotes formation of brittle Al–Zr intermetallics

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening
- **Related elements**: Nb, V, Ti, Zr, Al in bcc solid solution
- **Typical alloys**: Equiatomic ZrNbVTiAl HEA; other refractory bcc HEAs (AlNbTiV, NbTiVZr, HfNbTaTiZr)
- **Key compositions**: 20 at.% each of Zr, Nb, V, Ti, Al
- **Microstructural changes**: B2 ordered bcc matrix enriched in Nb, Ti, V; lattice distortion from multiple principal elements with different atomic sizes
- **Property improvements**: Room-temperature yield strength of 1.045 GPa (higher than austenitic stainless steels ~0.55 GPa and Inconel ~1.0 GPa in compression); high-temperature strength maintained up to 1000 °C
- **Risks and limitations**: Difficult to isolate contribution of solid-solution strengthening from precipitation strengthening due to coexistence of Al–Zr and Al–Ti intermetallics; B2 ordering may influence dislocation behavior

## Precipitation/Second-Phase Strengthening (Intermetallic Strengthening)
- **Related elements**: Al and Zr (primary), with some Ti–Al phases
- **Typical alloys**: ZrNbVTiAl; AlNbTiVZr₀.₅ (Stepanov et al.)
- **Key compositions**: ~8% intermetallic fraction; Zr₅Al₃ and ZrAl₂ identified; interdendritic regions 36Al–41Zr (at.%)
- **Microstructural changes**: Sub-micron intermetallic particles in interdendritic regions; intermetallic fraction lower than thermodynamic predictions (~4% by EBSD vs. CALPHAD prediction) due to non-equilibrium cooling
- **Property improvements**:
  - Superior specific yield strength at 800 °C and 1000 °C compared to intermetallic-free refractory HEAs
  - Intermetallics retain hardness at elevated temperatures and pin dislocation motion
- **Risks and limitations**:
  - Potential ductility reduction (room-temperature cracks initiated at ~14% strain)
  - Segregation leads to microstructural inhomogeneity
  - Brittle phases may cause cracking during fabrication

## Grain Boundary Strengthening / Grain Refinement via Dynamic Recrystallization
- **Related elements**: All refractory elements (Ti, Zr, Nb, V) enable DRX at high temperatures; intermetallics may pin boundaries
- **Typical alloys**: ZrNbVTiAl; MoNbHfZrTi; HfNbTaTiZr
- **Key compositions**: Equiatomic; DRX observed at 900 °C and 1000 °C
- **Microstructural changes**:
  - As-cast grain size: ~300 μm (EBSD) / ~60 μm (dendritic)
  - Recrystallized grain size: ~2 μm at 900 °C and 1000 °C
  - Grain size at 1000 °C approximately half that at 900 °C
  - Initial grain refinement processes observed at 800 °C (elongated grains, both from SEM and IPF maps)
- **Property improvements**:
  - Dynamic recrystallization suggests good hot workability/fabricability
  - Strain rate sensitivity m ≈ 0.3, characteristic of DRX
  - Stress exponent n = 3.3 and Q = 302 kJ/mol indicative of dislocation climb-controlled deformation
- **Risks and limitations**: Flow softening at high temperature; DRX grain refinement during high-temperature service may affect long-term creep performance (not addressed in the paper)

## Low-Density Alloying for High Specific Strength
- **Related elements**: Al (2.7 g/cm³, primary density reducer), Ti (4.5 g/cm³, secondary density reducer)
- **Typical alloys**: ZrNbVTiAl; AlNbTiV; AlNbTiVZr₀.₅; other low-density refractory HEAs
- **Key compositions**: 20 at.% Al; 20 at.% Ti
- **Microstructural changes**: Bcc (B2) solid-solution + Al–Zr intermetallic precipitates
- **Property improvements**:
  - Density: 5.76 g/cm³ (vs. stainless steels 7.8 g/cm³, Inconel 718 8.22 g/cm³)
  - Superior specific yield strength up to 1000 °C
  - Elastic modulus ~128 GPa (experimental ultrasonic)
- **Risks and limitations**: 
  - Al-induced intermetallic formation may reduce toughness
  - Density reduction limited by presence of heavy refractory elements (Nb, Zr, V)

## Thermodynamic Stability for High-Entropy Alloy Design
- **Related elements**: All five components
- **Typical alloys**: ZrNbVTiAl
- **Key compositions**: Equiatomic; S_mix = 13.37 kJ/mol; H_mix = −11.37 kJ/mol; H_form = −5.15 kJ/mol; φ = 6.35
- **Microstructural changes**: Formation of mature bcc-dominant (B2) multiphase microstructure; intermetallics due to strong ordering tendencies
- **Property improvements**: Thermodynamic stability supports prolonged high-temperature service
- **Risks and limitations**: Negative H_mix can promote intermetallic formation rather than single-phase random solution; φ parameter predicted multiphase microstructure, consistent with experiment

# Key Conclusions
1. A low-density (5.76 g/cm³), equiatomic ZrNbVTiAl HEA was successfully designed using combined CALPHAD and DFT approaches and synthesized via induction levitation melting.
2. The as-cast microstructure consists of ~90% B2 type bcc phase with ~8% intermetallic phases (primarily Zr₅Al₃ and ZrAl₂) concentrated in interdendritic regions; dendrite cores are enriched in Nb, Ti, and V, while interdendritic regions are enriched in Al and Zr.
3. The alloy exhibits a room-temperature compressive yield strength of 1045 MPa, peak strength of ~1.25 GPa, and a Vickers microhardness of 576 HVN.
4. The alloy shows superior specific yield strength at 800 °C and 1000 °C compared to established refractory HEAs (NbTiVZr, HfNbTaTiZr, CrNbVTiZr), attributed to low density (5.76 g/cm³) combined with retention of strength due to intermetallic formation.
5. Dynamic recrystallization occurs during compression testing at 900 °C and 1000 °C, resulting in grain refinement from ~300 μm (as-cast) to ~2 μm (recrystallized); this indicates excellent hot workability and fabricability.
6. Strain rate sensitivity (m ≈ 0.3) and constitutive parameters (n = 3.3, Q = 302 kJ/mol) are consistent with DRX and dislocation climb-controlled deformation during high-temperature loading.
7. The experimentally determined elastic modulus is approximately 123 GPa (Young's modulus), with DFT calculations within acceptable agreement (B within 4%, E within ~19%).
8. The alloy combines low density, high strength, good hot workability, and thermodynamic stability, making it a potential candidate for light-weight, high-temperature structural applications including advanced nuclear reactor components.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Oxidation resistance**: Not evaluated in this paper; high-temperature compression tests were conducted in vacuum (~5 × 10⁻⁵ mbar). Service performance in oxidizing or corrosive environments (e.g., molten salts, liquid metals, supercritical water) remains untested.
- **Irradiation resistance**: Although the alloy was designed for nuclear applications and the disordered B2 phase is expected to resist irradiation-induced disordering, no irradiation experiments were performed.
- **Creep performance**: Not evaluated. Long-term creep properties at high temperature are unknown and critical for structural nuclear applications.
- **Duplex microstructure effects**: The effects of multiphase microstructure (B2 matrix + Al–Zr/Al–Ti intermetallics) on long-term thermal stability, phase coarsening, and property degradation are not addressed.
- **Compositional homogeneity**: Elemental segregation (Nb/Ti/V in dendrites vs. Al/Zr in interdendritic regions) raises questions about uniformity of mechanical properties at larger scales.
- **Scalability**: Only 27 g buttons were melted; scalability to larger ingots or complex components was not demonstrated.
- **Heat treatment optimization**: No heat treatments or thermo-mechanical processing routes were optimized; only as-cast condition was characterized.
- **Density characterization method**: Water displacement (Archimedes) may under-report true density if closed porosity or trapped phases exist.
- **Notation in Fig. 12**: The units reported for specific yield strength in Fig. 12 (GPa·g/cm³) appear to be inconsistent; the exact numerical magnitude should be verified when citing.
- **Alloy categorization**: The authors explicitly note that the term “HEA” is used for simplicity, though the material is multiphase (more accurately a compositionally complex alloy).
- **Diffusion mechanism understanding**: The authors note that diffusion mechanisms in multicomponent refractory systems are not well understood; detailed deformation mechanism interpretation should be treated as preliminary.
- **Intermetallic evolution**: The role of Al–Zr and Al–Ti intermetallics in long-term stability (thermal, irradiation) and their evolution during thermomechanical processing was not investigated.
- **Room-temperature ductility**: The paper reports cracks initiated at ~14% strain in compression; tensile ductility and fracture toughness data at room temperature are not provided.
- **Testing conditions**: Mechanical properties were evaluated in compression only (monotonic loading); tensile, fatigue, and fracture toughness properties are not characterized.
- **Large deformation behavior**: High-temperature tests were carried out only up to 50% reduction; effects of larger strains on recrystallization and texture evolution were not investigated.
