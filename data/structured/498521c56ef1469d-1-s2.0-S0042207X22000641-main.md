# Paper Metadata
- **Title**: A two-phase ultrafine-grained NbMoTaWV refractory high entropy alloy with prominent compressive properties
- **Authors**: Haiyan Peng, Zhixin Kang, Yan Long, Li Zhou
- **Journal**: Vacuum
- **Year**: 2022
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vacuum.2022.110930
- **Alloy system studied**: NbMoTaWV equiatomic refractory high-entropy alloy
- **Research objective**: To fabricate a two-phase refractory NbMoTaWV HEA with excellent strength at room and elevated temperatures by mechanical alloying (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS), and to characterize its microstructure and mechanical properties.
- **Material category**: Refractory high-entropy alloy; additively manufactured refractory alloy (specifically, powder metallurgy route via mechanical alloying + spark plasma sintering)

# Alloy System and Research Object
The paper focuses on an equiatomic NbMoTaWV refractory high-entropy alloy fabricated by mechanical alloying (MA) followed by spark plasma sintering (SPS). The research objective is to produce a fine-grained, two-phase refractory HEA with enhanced room-temperature and elevated-temperature compressive properties compared with the same nominal alloy produced by vacuum arc melting (from prior literature, Ref. [3] in the paper, reportedly VNbMoTaW).

# Alloy Composition
- Alloy name: NbMoTaWV
- Nominal equiatomic composition: Nb:Mo:Ta:W:V = 1:1:1:1:1
- Equivalent nominal atomic percentage: 20 at.% Nb, 20 at.% Mo, 20 at.% Ta, 20 at.% W, 20 at.% V
- Starting materials: elemental powders of Nb, Mo, Ta, W, V with purity > 99.9 wt.% and particle sizes ≤ 48 μm
- Impurities: Oxygen is present in the final microstructure as impurity; oxygen was introduced during powder preparation and SPS process. Specific ppm-level O content is not stated, but EDS of the oxide phase indicated ~60.32 at.% O in the (Ta,V)O2 phase.
- The alloy contains W, Mo, Nb, Ta, V. It does not contain Zr, Hf, Ti, Cr, Al, Si, Re, C, B, Y, La as intentional additions. No specific C, N, H contents are reported.

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Mechanical alloying (MA)**: Elemental powders (Nb, Mo, Ta, W, V; purity > 99.9 wt.%; particle size ≤ 48 μm) in equiatomic composition were ball milled in a stainless steel vial with stainless steel balls. Milling was carried out inside a glove box to reduce oxidation of HEA powders. Milling times tested: 10, 20, 30, and 40 h. Final milling time used for consolidation: 40 h. Crystal size of BCC phase after 40 h milling: 10.8 nm (from Williamson-Hall formula).
- **Spark plasma sintering (SPS)**: Milled powders were loaded into a graphite die and compacted using a Dr. Sinter 825 apparatus. Sintering temperature: 1500 °C; sintering time: 10 min; applied stress: 30 MPa; vacuum pressure: < 8 Pa; heating rate: 100 °C/min. After SPS, cylindrical samples with diameter 20 mm and height 10 mm (Φ20 × 10 mm) were obtained.
- No post-sintering homogenization, rolling, forging, annealing, or aging treatments are reported.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
- **Matrix phase**: Body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution.
  - Average grain size of BCC matrix: ~2.7 μm (from TEM).
  - Lattice parameter of BCC phase: 3.316 Å (measured along [111] zone axis).
  - Composition of BCC matrix from EDS (region '1'): Nb ~25.38 at.%, Mo ~22.55 at.%, Ta ~15.26 at.%, W ~19.64 at.%, V ~18.63 at.%; close to nominal equiatomic composition.
- **Second phase**: Tetragonal (Ta,V)O2 oxide phase.
  - Lattice parameters: a = 4.709 Å; c = 3.067 Å.
  - Volume fraction: ~13.8%.
  - Average particle size: ~0.5 μm.
  - Distribution: homogeneously dispersed in the BCC matrix.
  - Composition of oxide phase from EDS (region '2'): Ta ~22.3 at.%, V ~14.95 at.%, O ~60.32 at.%; enriched in Ta, V, and O; hardly any Nb, Mo, W. The sum of Ta + V content is about half the O content, consistent with (Ta,V)O2.
  - The (Ta,V)O2 phase corresponds to TaO2-type structure with partial substitution of Ta by V.
- **Phase evolution**: After 40 h of MA, only a single BCC solid solution phase was detected. After SPS, the tetragonal (Ta,V)O2 phase precipitated from the primary BCC phase. The oxide is attributed to oxygen introduced during powder preparation and SPS; Ta and V exhibit higher oxygen affinity than Nb, Mo, W.
- **Density**: Bulk alloy density of 11.6 g/cm³.
- The paper discusses solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening (oxide dispersion strengthening), and grain-boundary strengthening.

# Mechanical Properties
Room-temperature compressive properties (cylindrical specimens Φ3 × 6 mm; strain rate 1 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹):
- Yield strength, σy: ~2785 MPa
- Compressive strength, σmax: ~3661 MPa
- Strain to failure, εf: ~16.2%
- Vickers hardness: ~774 HV (load 2.94 N)

Elevated-temperature compressive properties (cylindrical specimens Φ6 × 10 mm; strain rate 1 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹; compression):
- At 1000 °C: compressive strength σmax = 1978 MPa
- At 1200 °C: compressive strength σmax = 1433 MPa
- Yield strength at elevated temperatures: not explicitly reported.
- Elongation/plasticity at elevated temperature: The alloy became less ductile at elevated temperature; the authors attribute this to the second phase activating cracks along grain boundaries. No specific plastic strain values at elevated temperature are provided.

Comparison with vacuum arc melted, cast VNbMoTaW HEA from Ref. [3] (Table 1):
- Cast VNbMoTaW: σy = 1246 MPa, σmax = 1270 MPa, εf = 1.7%, hardness = 535 HV.
- Present SPS NbMoTaWV relative to cast VNbMoTaW: σy increased by 123.5%, σmax increased by 188.3%, εf increased by 864.7%, hardness increased by 47.4%; average grain size reduced from ~80 μm (cast) to 2.7 μm (SPS).

Other reference alloys in Table 1:
- AlCoCrFeNiTi0.5 (casting): σy = 2260 MPa, σmax = 3140 MPa, εf = 23.3%
- Co25Ni25Fe25Al7.5Cu17.5 (SPS): σy = 1795 MPa, σmax = 1936 MPa, εf = 10.6%, 454 HV
- Al0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 (SPS): σy = 2732 MPa, σmax = 3172 MPa, εf = 10.1%, 712 HV
- AlMo0.5NbTa0.5TiZr (casting): σy = 2000 MPa, σmax = 2368 MPa, εf = 10%, 580 HV
- HfMo0.5NbTiV0.5 (casting): σy = 1260 MPa, εf > 35%, 403 HV
- HfMo0.5NbTiV0.5Si0.7 (casting): σy = 1617 MPa, σmax = 2016 MPa, εf = 18.5%, 612 HV
- Al0.6CrNbTi2V0.5 (casting): σy = 1240 MPa, εf > 50%

Fracture toughness, creep performance, rupture life, elastic modulus: not reported in the original paper.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability
The paper does not directly measure oxidation weight gain, oxide layer thickness, or long-term oxidation resistance. However, the following can be extracted:
- The sintered alloy contains an in-situ precipitated oxide phase, tetragonal (Ta,V)O2, indicating that oxygen was picked up during powder processing and SPS despite vacuum conditions (< 8 Pa) and glove-box handling.
- The presence of oxygen led to formation of (Ta,V)O2, preferentially involving Ta and V due to their higher oxygen affinity compared with Nb, Mo, and W.
- Ductility loss at elevated temperature is attributed to the presence of the second phase activating cracks along grain boundaries; this suggests the oxide phase can act as crack initiation site at high temperature, i.e., a possible embrittlement mechanism. No quantitative grain-boundary oxidation data or oxidation kinetics are provided.
- No information is provided about Al2O3, Cr2O3, or SiO2 protective scales; no oxidation temperature or duration tests are reported.

## Ta (Tantalum)

**[Element or Element Combination]**  
Ta (Tantalum)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**  
NbMoTaWV equiatomic refractory high-entropy alloy (BCC matrix + tetragonal (Ta,V)O2 second phase), fabricated by MA + SPS.

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**  
Nominal equiatomic 20 at.% Ta. In BCC matrix after SPS, ~15.26 at.% Ta; in (Ta,V)O2 second phase, ~22.3 at.% Ta. Starting powder: elemental Ta, purity > 99.9 wt.%.

**[Main Function]**  
Participates in BCC solid solution strengthening and forms in-situ tetragonal (Ta,V)O2 oxide precipitate; Ta has high oxygen affinity, leading to oxide second-phase precipitation during SPS.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**  
During SPS, oxygen introduced from powder handling/sintering reacts preferentially with Ta (and V) due to higher oxygen affinity of Ta compared with Nb, Mo, W; this leads to precipitation of tetragonal (Ta,V)O2 (a = 4.709 Å, c = 3.067 Å) particles (~0.5 μm) homogenously dispersed in BCC matrix. These particles act as obstacles to dislocation motion and hinder grain boundary sliding at elevated temperature.

**[Property Effect]**  
The oxide precipitation strengthening from (Ta,V)O2 is cited as a major contributor to σy ~2785 MPa, σmax ~3661 MPa at room temperature, and elevated-temperature strength of 1978 MPa at 1000 °C and 1433 MPa at 1200 °C. Compared with cast VNbMoTaW (σy 1246 MPa, σmax 1270 MPa, εf 1.7%), the present alloy shows a 123.5% increase in σy, 188.3% increase in σmax, and 864.7% increase in εf.

**[Potential Side Effects]**  
Ta has high oxygen affinity leading to oxide formation; at elevated temperature, the presence of (Ta,V)O2 may activate cracks along grain boundaries and reduce ductility. This is explicitly suggested: “Nevertheless, the alloy became less ductile at elevated temperature, which was probably attributed to the presence of the second phase activating cracks along grain boundaries.”

**[Original Evidence]**  
“Combining with the XRD results, diffraction peaks of (Ta,V)O2 phase are close to those of TaO2, but part of Ta atom is replaced by V.” “It is well known that Ta and V exhibit considerably higher oxygen affinity than other three elements [14], hence the (Ta,V)O2 phase formed.” EDS region '2': Ta ~22.3 at.%, V ~14.95 at.%, O ~60.32 at.%.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related elements**: Nb, Mo, Ta, W, V (equiatomic)
- **Typical alloys**: NbMoTaWV (BCC refractory HEA)
- **Key compositions**: Nb 20 at.%, Mo 20 at.%, Ta 20 at.%, W 20 at.%, V 20 at.%
- **Microstructural changes**: Formation of single BCC solid solution phase after 40 h of MA (crystal size 10.8 nm); after SPS, BCC matrix remains with near-nominal composition (Nb ~25.38 at.%, Mo ~22.55 at.%, Ta ~15.26 at.%, W ~19.64 at.%, V ~18.63 at.%) and lattice parameter 3.316 Å.
- **Property improvements**: Contributes to overall high strength at room and elevated temperatures, but the paper does not isolate solid-solution strengthening contribution from precipitation and grain-boundary strengthening. Room-temperature σy ~2785 MPa, σmax ~3661 MPa, hardness 774 HV; 1000 °C σmax 1978 MPa; 1200 °C σmax 1433 MPa.
- **Risks and limitations**: Not explicitly discussed in the original paper; the single mechanism is not isolated.

## Oxide Precipitation / Dispersion Strengthening (in-situ (Ta,V)O2)

- **Related elements**: Ta, V, O
- **Typical alloys**: NbMoTaWV prepared by MA + SPS
- **Key compositions**: (Ta,V)O2 phase with Ta ~22.3 at.%, V ~14.95 at.%, O ~60.32 at.%; volume fraction ~13.8%; average particle size ~0.5 μm
- **Microstructural changes**: Tetragonal (Ta,V)O2 particles (a = 4.709 Å, c = 3.067 Å) homogeneously dispersed in BCC matrix; oxide precipitates from BCC during SPS due to oxygen pickup and high oxygen affinity of Ta and V.
- **Property improvements**: The paper attributes the excellent room and elevated-temperature strength primarily to fine (Ta,V)O2 particles dispersed in BCC matrix. They act as obstacles to dislocation motion at room temperature and hinder grain boundary sliding at elevated temperatures. Room-temperature σy ~2785 MPa vs cast reference 1246 MPa; 1000 °C σmax 1978 MPa vs cast reference ~1454 MPa; 1200 °C σmax 1433 MPa vs cast reference ~943 MPa.
- **Risks and limitations**: The second phase may activate cracks along grain boundaries at elevated temperature, reducing ductility. Oxygen contamination is an unintentional impurity; the oxide phase formation may not be a fully controllable strengthening route.

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening / Grain Refinement

- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; process-related (MA + SPS)
- **Typical alloys**: NbMoTaWV prepared by MA + SPS
- **Key compositions**: Equiatomic NbMoTaWV
- **Microstructural changes**: MA + SPS produces average BCC grain size of ~2.7 μm, compared with ~80 μm grain size in cast material from prior work (Ref. [3]).
- **Property improvements**: Grain boundary strengthening is identified by the paper as one of two significant strengthening mechanisms. Yield strength σy increased by 123.5%, σmax by 188.3%, εf by 864.7%, and hardness by 47.4% compared with cast alloy.
- **Risks and limitations**: Not explicitly discussed beyond benefits; fine grain size may affect elevated-temperature properties via grain boundary sliding, but second-phase particles hinder GB sliding.

## Additive Manufacturing-Oriented Composition Control (not explicitly discussed)

The paper does not discuss composition control for additive manufacturing; it uses powder metallurgy via MA + SPS, which is a solid-state powder consolidation route rather than a beam-based AM method. No explicit AM-oriented alloy design is presented.

# Key Conclusions

1. A refractory NbMoTaWV equiatomic HEA was successfully fabricated by mechanical alloying (40 h) and spark plasma sintering (1500 °C, 10 min, 30 MPa, vacuum < 8 Pa).
2. After 40 h of mechanical alloying, only a single BCC solid solution phase was formed; the BCC crystal size was 10.8 nm.
3. After SPS, a two-phase microstructure developed: a BCC matrix (average grain size ~2.7 μm; lattice parameter 3.316 Å) and a tetragonal (Ta,V)O2 oxide phase (a = 4.709 Å, c = 3.067 Å; average particle size ~0.5 μm; volume fraction ~13.8%) homogeneously dispersed in the matrix.
4. The (Ta,V)O2 phase formed because oxygen was introduced during powder preparation and SPS, and Ta and V have higher oxygen affinity than Nb, Mo, or W.
5. Room-temperature compressive properties: σy ~2785 MPa, σmax ~3661 MPa, εf ~16.2%, hardness ~774 HV.
6. High-temperature compressive strength: σmax = 1978 MPa at 1000 °C and 1433 MPa at 1200 °C.
7. Compared with a vacuum arc melted cast VNbMoTaW alloy from literature, the SPS NbMoTaWV alloy showed 123.5% higher σy, 188.3% higher σmax, 864.7% higher εf, and 47.4% higher hardness.
8. The notable strength improvement is attributed to (a) precipitation of fine (Ta,V)O2 particles, which hinder dislocation motion at room temperature and grain boundary sliding at elevated temperature, and (b) grain boundary strengthening from a much smaller BCC grain size (2.7 μm vs ~80 μm for cast).
9. At elevated temperature, ductility decreased, likely because the second phase activates cracks along grain boundaries.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- The paper reports only compression tests, not tensile tests; tensile strength, tensile elongation, fracture toughness, creep, and fatigue data are not provided.
- Elevated-temperature yield strength values are not explicitly given; only compressive strength (σmax) at 1000 °C and 1200 °C is reported.
- The exact oxygen impurity content in the bulk alloy is not quantified; the paper infers oxygen pickup from EDS of the oxide phase and processing conditions.
- No detailed characterization of oxide particle crystallography or orientation relationship with the matrix is provided beyond SAED lattice parameters.
- The study is limited to a single alloy composition (equiatomic NbMoTaWV); no compositional variations are explored.
- The observed oxide (Ta,V)O2 is an unintended product of oxygen contamination; reproducibility may depend on control of oxygen during powder handling and SPS, which is not fully characterized.
- The poor elevated-temperature ductility associated with the (Ta,V)O2 second phase suggests that the benefits of precipitation strengthening may be offset by embrittlement in service at high temperatures; further microstructural optimization may be required. This is an observation from the paper, but not fully resolved.
- The comparison with previous work is based on literature data (cast VNbMoTaW), not on a side-by-side experiment in the same study.
