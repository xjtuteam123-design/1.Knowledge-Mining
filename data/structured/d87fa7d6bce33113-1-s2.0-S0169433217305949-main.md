# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Microstructure of rapidly solidified Nb-based pre-alloyed powders for additive manufacturing
- **Authors**: Yueling Guo, Lina Jia, Bin Kong, Shengnan Zhang, Fengxiang Zhang, Hu Zhang
- **Journal**: Applied Surface Science
- **Year**: 2017
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.apsusc.2017.02.221
- **Alloy System Studied**: Nb-based alloy (Nb-Ti-Cr-Al-Si)
- **Material Category**: Nb-based alloy powder, pre-alloyed powder for powder-based additive manufacturing; Nb-Si-based composites referenced as relevant alloy family
- **Research Objective**: To systematically investigate the microstructure, surface oxidation, and microhardness of sphere-shaped Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si pre-alloyed powders prepared by plasma rotating electrode processing (PREP) for powder-based additive manufacturing.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy System**: Nb-based refractory alloy with composition Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si (at.%)
- **Material Category**: Nb-based alloy; pre-alloyed spherical powders for powder-based additive manufacturing; Nb-Si-based in situ composites are discussed as context but not directly studied
- **Research Objective**: Characterize the rapidly solidified microstructure of PREP-processed Nb-based pre-alloyed powders, including phase constitution, dendritic structure, secondary dendrite arm spacing (SDAS), surface oxide layer chemistry and thickness, and microhardness as a function of powder size. Also examine the effect of high-temperature heat treatment (1450 °C for 3 h) on microstructure and phase evolution.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Electrode Production**: Nb-based alloy disks produced via vacuum induction melting (VIM), used as electrodes for PREP.
- **Powder Production Method**: Plasma rotating electrode processing (PREP) in Ar atmosphere. The rotating Nb-based alloy electrode was melted by plasma arc; melt droplets formed and rapidly solidified during free fall in a containerless state.
- **Powder Size Classification**: Four size fractions: N1 (45–75 μm), N2 (75–180 μm), N3 (180–250 μm), N4 (250–380 μm).
- **Heat Treatment**: HT at 1450 °C for 3 h performed on N2 powders and VIM alloy in a vacuum furnace under high-purity argon atmosphere. N2 powders were sealed in a silica tube before HT to avoid oxidation.
- **Hot Isostatic Pressing**: Not mentioned.
- **Additive Manufacturing**: Not performed in this paper; powders were prepared for powder-based AM. The paper discusses SLM, EBM, and LMD as relevant AM technologies but does not conduct AM experiments.
- **Mechanical Alloying**: Not used.
- **Rolling/Forging**: Not used.
- **Homogenization**: Not explicitly stated.
- **Cooling Method**: Rapid solidification during free fall in PREP; containerless solidification.
- **Deformation Amount**: Not applicable for powders.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Phase Constitution
- **Matrix Phase**: Nb solid solution (Nbss), BCC structure (inferred from Nbss designation; not explicitly stated as BCC but Nb solid solution is conventionally bcc).
- **Second Phases**: Cr₂Nb Laves phase. Silicides were suggested as part of interdendritic precipitates (based on EPMA enrichment of Cr and Si, combined with TEM results from a previous work [28]).
- **Non-equilibrium Phases**: Not detected by XRD in PREP powders or VIM alloy.

## Microstructure of As-Fabricated Powders
- **Dendritic Structure**: Fine Nbss dendrites observed in all powder size fractions and VIM alloy. Surface of powders exhibited dendritic structures.
- **Grain Boundaries and Features**: Grain boundaries observed in TEM of N1 powders; some dislocations also observed.
- **Elemental Segregation**: Interdendritic segregation of Si, Ti, and Cr observed by EPMA. Si, Ti, Cr concentrated in interdendritic areas. Al was homogeneously distributed.
- **Secondary Dendrite Arm Spacing (SDAS)**:
  - N1: approximately 0.85 μm (lower end of range)
  - N4: approximately 5.14 μm (upper end of range)
  - VIM alloy: 76.57 μm
  - Overall average SDAS for powders ranged from 0.85 to 5.14 μm depending on powder size.
  - SDAS increased with increasing powder size.
- **Cr₂Nb Phase**:
  - Not observed by SEM in N1, N2, N3 powders; observed in N4 powders and VIM alloys.
  - TEM of N1 powders confirmed Cr₂Nb phases with length scale 300–500 nm.
  - Composition of Cr₂Nb by TEM-EDS: 20.15Nb-43.73Cr-23.60Ti-12.01Si-0.51Al (at.%).
  - Composition of Cr₂Nb by SEM-EDS (in VIM alloy): 26.28Nb-9.17Si-17.72Ti-45.78Cr-1.05Al (at.%).
  - Composition of Nbss by TEM-EDS: 44.75Nb-35.57Ti-16.41Cr-2.19Al-1.08Si (at.%).
- **Interdendritic Precipitates**: Enriched in Cr and Si, not Ti. Suggested to be Cr₂Nb and silicides based on EPMA and previous TEM work [28].

## Microstructure After Heat Treatment (1450 °C for 3 h)
- Dendritic structures transformed to large grains in both N2 powders and VIM alloy.
- Cr₂Nb phases precipitated at grain boundaries as continuous distributions, and also inside grains with scattered distribution.
- Some TiO₂ oxides observed after HT, attributed to reaction between Ti and residual oxygen.

## Strengthening Mechanisms Discussed
- **Second-phase hardening**: Precipitation of Cr₂Nb Laves phases impedes dislocation motion (discussed in relation to VIM alloy hardness).
- **Microstructure refinement hardening**: Finer dendritic structure (smaller SDAS) results in higher hardness, attributed to Hall-Petch-type relationship.
- The paper does not explicitly discuss solid-solution strengthening, carbide precipitation, oxide dispersion strengthening, or ODS mechanisms.

# Mechanical Properties

## Microhardness
- **Testing Method**: Vickers microhardness, 100 g load, 10 s dwell time. At least six measurements per sample. Errors <5%.
- **Hardness Order (average HV, high to low)**: N1 > N2 > N3 > N4
- **Comparison with VIM alloy**: VIM alloy hardness was lower than N3 powders but higher than N4 powders.
- **Hardness-SDAS Relationship**: Hardness increased with decreasing powder size (and decreasing SDAS), following Hall-Petch relationship. The paper describes that microhardness HV tends to linearly increase with the inverse square root of SDAS.
- **Abnormal Hardness of VIM Alloy**: The VIM alloy had higher hardness than N4 powders despite much larger SDAS, attributed to second-phase hardening from fibrous Laves Cr₂Nb precipitates.
- **Specific Numerical Hardness Values**: Not provided in the paper. The paper states relative ordering and trends but does not provide explicit HV values for each powder size.

## Tensile, Compressive, Creep, Fracture Toughness
- Not measured or reported in this paper.
- The paper focuses on powder microhardness as the mechanical property indicator.

## Testing Conditions
- **Atmosphere**: Not specified for microhardness testing.
- **Temperature**: Room temperature (implied; not explicitly stated).
- **Loading Mode**: Vickers indentation (quasi-static indentation).

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

## Surface Oxide of As-Fabricated Powders
- **Oxide Layer Thickness**: 9.39 nm (measured by AES depth profiling)
- **Oxide Types Identified by XPS**: Nb₂O₅, SiO₂, TiO₂, Cr₂O₃, Al₂O₃
- **XPS Oxide/Metal Ratios**:
  - Nb oxides: 84.5% of Nb signal (M_oxi/(M⁰ + M_oxi))
  - Ti oxides: 52.2% of Ti signal
  - Cr oxides: 71.9% of Cr signal
- **Surface Composition by AES**:
  - Outermost surface atomic concentration of Ti: up to 47.86%
  - Oxidized Ti on surface: 40.44 at.% (as fraction of total Nb+Ti+Al normalized signal)
  - Oxidized Nb on surface: 11.42 at.%
  - Oxidized Cr on surface: 21.76 at.%
  - Outermost surface is dominated by Ti-oxides, due to higher oxygen affinity of Ti compared to Cr and Nb.
- **Oxygen Depth Profile**: Oxygen concentration decreased exponentially with etch depth. Curve fit equation: y = 74.3 exp(−x/6.9) − 0.4, where y is oxygen concentration (wt.%) and x is etch depth (nm). Adj. R² = 0.986.
- **Clean Surface**: PREP produced a clean powder surface free of oxide particles; few oxide particles observed by SEM.
- **Effect of PREP on Composition**: No chemical loss from PREP; measured composition close to nominal.

## Oxidation-Related Issues in Additive Manufacturing
- **Oxide Contamination**: The paper discusses that oxide contamination tends to concentrate at melt pool edges during SLM, causing balling tendency (citing Zhou et al. [15]).
- **Degradation after HT**: TiO₂ oxides formed after HT at 1450 °C for 3 h due to reaction between Ti and residual oxygen.

## Oxidation Resistance at High Temperature
- Not directly tested in this paper. The paper focuses on surface oxidation of powders at room temperature after PREP, not high-temperature oxidation resistance.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Knowledge Card: Ti (Titanium)

- **Element**: Ti (Titanium)
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Nb-based alloys (Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 37 at.% nominal; 36.01 at.% measured (ICP-AES)
- **Main Function**: High oxygen affinity element that preferentially forms TiO₂ on powder surface; contributes to surface oxide layer composition.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Titanium is rejected to interdendritic areas during solidification (segregates to interdendritic regions). It forms TiO₂ oxides at high temperature (1450 °C HT) by reacting with residual oxygen.
- **Property Effect**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Ti's effect on mechanical properties. Ti dominates the surface oxide chemistry (up to 47.86 at.% on outermost surface).
- **Potential Side Effects**: Formation of TiO₂ oxides during high-temperature heat treatment due to high oxygen affinity; possible contribution to oxidation issues in service.
- **Original Evidence**: "on the surface of Nb-based alloy powder mainly covers the Ti-oxide, since the oxygen affinity of Ti is higher than that of Cr and Nb" (Section 3, page 368). "Some TiO₂ oxides are observed, resulting from the reaction between Ti and the residual oxygen, caused by its high affinity of oxygen" (Section 3, page 372). Fig. 2 shows Ti 2p3/2 XPS narrow scan with TiO₂ peak.

## Knowledge Card: Cr (Chromium)

- **Element**: Cr (Chromium)
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Nb-based alloys (Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 13 at.% nominal; 13.66 at.% measured (ICP-AES)
- **Main Function**: Forms Cr₂Nb Laves phase; Cr segregates to interdendritic areas.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Cr forms Cr₂Nb Laves phase with Nb. Cr₂Nb precipitates are found at grain boundaries (continuous distribution) and within grains (scattered) after HT at 1450 °C for 3 h. Cr₂Nb phase is enriched in Cr and Si, not Ti.
- **Property Effect**: Cr₂Nb Laves phase contributes to second-phase hardening, increasing microhardness by impeding dislocation motion. In the VIM alloy, precipitation of fibrous Cr₂Nb particles increased hardness above N4 powders despite a coarser dendritic structure.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated. However, Laves phase formation can affect ductility in other systems (not discussed in this paper).
- **Original Evidence**: "the Cr₂Nb phases are evident in N4 powders and VIM alloys" (Section 3, page 369–370). "The Cr₂Nb phase is determined by selected-area diffraction patterns (SADPs) as well as TEM-EDS, which is 20.15Nb-43.73Cr-23.60Ti-12.01Si-0.51Al (at.%)" (Section 3, page 371). "the precipitates are composed of Cr₂Nb and silicides" (Section 3, page 371). Fig. 4(j) shows interdendritic and intragranular Cr₂Nb phases in VIM alloy.

## Knowledge Card: Si (Silicon)

- **Element**: Si (Silicon)
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Nb-based alloys (Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 1 at.% nominal; 1.43 at.% measured (ICP-AES)
- **Main Function**: Forms silicides; segregates to interdendritic areas.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Si concentrates in interdendritic areas during solidification. Interdendritic precipitates are enriched in Cr and Si. In combination with TEM results from previous work [28], the precipitates are concluded to be Cr₂Nb and silicides. SiO₂ forms on powder surface.
- **Property Effect**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Si's effect on mechanical properties in this alloy.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in this paper. Silicon may contribute to silicide formation and associated brittleness in some Nb–Si systems, but this is not discussed here.
- **Original Evidence**: "Interdendritic precipitates are enriched in Cr and Si, rather than Ti" (Section 3, page 371). Fig. 5 shows EPMA distribution of Cr, Si, Ti on interdendritic areas. "In combination with the TEM result in our previous work [28], it could be concluded that the precipitates are composed of Cr₂Nb and silicides" (Section 3, page 371).

## Knowledge Card: Ti + Cr + Si Combination

- **Element Combination**: Ti + Cr + Si
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Nb-based alloy (Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Ti 37 at.%, Cr 13 at.%, Si 1 at.% (nominal)
- **Main Function**: These elements segregate to interdendritic areas during solidification, forming Cr₂Nb and silicide precipitates in the interdendritic regions.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: During solidification, Ti, Cr, and Si are rejected from the solidifying Nbss dendrites and concentrate in interdendritic regions. Cr₂Nb Laves phase and silicides form as interdendritic precipitates. Si and Cr are enriched in interdendritic precipitates, while Ti also concentrates in interdendritic areas.
- **Property Effect**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for mechanical property changes from this combination in powders. The VIM alloy with more developed interdendritic Cr₂Nb precipitates showed higher hardness than N4 powders.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Interdendritic segregation can contribute to chemical inhomogeneity; Cr₂Nb Laves phase formation may affect properties in additively manufactured components.
- **Original Evidence**: "The elements of Si, Ti, Cr concentrate on the interdendritic areas" (Section 3, page 371, Fig. 7). "Composition of the Cr₂Nb, measured by SEM-EDS, is 26.28Nb-9.17Si-17.72Ti-45.78Cr-1.05Al (at.%)" (Section 3, page 371).

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Mechanism: Second-Phase Hardening (Cr₂Nb Laves Phase)

- **Related Elements**: Cr, Nb, Si, Ti
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si; VIM alloy with Cr₂Nb precipitates
- **Key Compositions**: Cr₂Nb measured by TEM-EDS: 20.15Nb-43.73Cr-23.60Ti-12.01Si-0.51Al (at.%); by SEM-EDS: 26.28Nb-9.17Si-17.72Ti-45.78Cr-1.05Al (at.%)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Cr₂Nb Laves phase precipitates at grain boundaries (continuous distribution) and within grains (scattered) after HT at 1450 °C for 3 h. In as-fabricated powders, Cr₂Nb size 300–500 nm in N1 powders; larger and observable by SEM in N4 powders and VIM alloy. In VIM alloy, fibrous Cr₂Nb particles precipitate.
- **Property Improvements**: VIM alloy shows "abnormal" increase in microhardness compared to N4 powders despite coarser dendritic structure. The paper attributes this to second-phase hardening from fibrous Laves Cr₂Nb particles large enough to impede dislocation motion.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Laves phase formation may affect ductility and workability in other systems, though not discussed in this paper. The second-phase hardening effect can mask microstructure refinement hardening effects.

## Mechanism: Microstructure Refinement / Hall-Petch Hardening

- **Related Elements**: Not element-specific; related to powder size and solidification conditions
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si PREP powders
- **Key Compositions**: Same composition across all powder sizes; SDAS varies from 0.85 to 5.14 μm (PREP powders) vs. 76.57 μm (VIM alloy)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Finer dendritic structures (smaller SDAS) in smaller powder sizes due to higher undercooling during rapid solidification. N1 powders have fine Nbss dendrites with SDAS ~0.85 μm; N4 powders have coarser SDAS ~5.14 μm.
- **Property Improvements**: Microhardness increased with decreasing powder size (N1 > N2 > N3 > N4), following inverse square root of SDAS relationship (Hall-Petch). The paper states that smaller SDAS corresponds to more dendrite boundaries, limiting dislocation free path and increasing strain hardening.
- **Risks and Limitations**: The relationship does not hold when comparing VIM alloy to N4 powders due to masking by second-phase hardening from Cr₂Nb precipitates.

## Mechanism: Rapid Solidification / Dendritic Refinement

- **Related Elements**: Not element-specific
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si PREP powders
- **Key Compositions**: Same nominal composition
- **Microstructural Changes**: PREP rapid solidification produces much finer dendritic structures compared to VIM. SDAS in PREP powders (0.85–5.14 μm) is about 15 times smaller than VIM alloy (76.57 μm). Higher undercooling in smaller powders leads to solute trapping and refined dendrites.
- **Property Improvements**: Finer microstructure corresponds to higher microhardness (Hall-Petch).
- **Risks and Limitations**: Rapid solidification can suppress formation of observable Cr₂Nb precipitates due to short timescale; Cr₂Nb forms at sub-micrometer scale (300–500 nm) in N1 powders.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Pre-alloyed powder production**: Sphere-shaped Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si (at.%) pre-alloyed powders were successfully produced by PREP with no significant composition loss (measured 46.51Nb-36.01Ti-13.66Cr-2.39Al-1.43Si at.% vs. nominal).

2. **Phase constitution**: Main phases are Nb solid solution (Nbss) and Cr₂Nb Laves phase. Cr₂Nb was confirmed by TEM in N1 powders (300–500 nm size), even though not visible in SEM for smaller powder size fractions.

3. **Microstructure**: Fine dendritic structures in as-fabricated powders; SDAS increased with powder size (0.85–5.14 μm), and VIM alloy had much coarser SDAS (76.57 μm). Si, Ti, Cr segregate to interdendritic areas; Al is homogeneously distributed.

4. **Surface oxidation**: A 9.39 nm oxide layer formed on powder surface. Outermost surface is dominated by Ti-oxides (Ti at 47.86 at.%), due to higher oxygen affinity of Ti compared to Cr and Nb. Oxygen concentration decreased exponentially with etch depth: y = 74.3 exp(−x/6.9) − 0.4.

5. **Heat treatment effect**: After HT at 1450 °C for 3 h, dendrites transformed to large grains. Cr₂Nb phases precipitated at grain boundaries (continuously) and inside grains (scattered). TiO₂ oxides formed due to Ti reacting with residual oxygen.

6. **Microhardness**: Hardness order N1 > N2 > N3 > N4; hardness increased with decreasing powder size following Hall-Petch relationship. VIM alloy hardness was higher than N4 powders due to second-phase hardening from fibrous Cr₂Nb precipitates, masking refinement effects.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **No additively manufactured component tested**: The paper only studies powders; no AM processing was performed. The suitability of these powders for actual AM processing is discussed conceptually but not experimentally verified.
- **No quantitative mechanical properties beyond microhardness**: Tensile, compressive, creep, fracture toughness, and high-temperature strength were not measured. Only Vickers microhardness at presumably room temperature was evaluated.
- **No explicit numerical hardness values**: The paper presents relative hardness ordering but does not provide specific HV numbers for each powder size fraction.
- **No impure element quantification**: Bulk C, O, N, H concentrations are not provided as ppm values. Only surface oxygen is characterized by AES/XPS.
- **Oxidation resistance not tested at high temperature**: The paper focuses on surface oxide layer of as-fabricated powders, not high-temperature oxidation resistance.
- **Single alloy composition**: Only Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si is studied; results may not be fully generalizable to other Nb-based alloys or other additive manufacturing powders.
- **Temperature not explicitly stated for microhardness testing**: Assumed room temperature but not explicitly confirmed in the paper.
- **Strengthening mechanism discussion is limited**: The paper focuses on hardness trends, second-phase hardening, and Hall-Petch effect, but does not discuss solid-solution strengthening, oxide dispersion strengthening, or other mechanisms in detail.
