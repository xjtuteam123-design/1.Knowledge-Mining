# Alloy System and Research Object

This paper is **outside the scope of refractory high-temperature alloys, Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys**. It concerns the use of a CoCrFeNiMn high-entropy alloy thin film as an interlayer for vacuum diffusion welding between a near-α titanium alloy (TA15) and 304 austenitic stainless steel.

The CoCrFeNiMn alloy is a face-centered cubic (FCC) high-entropy alloy, commonly known as the Cantor alloy, and is not a refractory alloy system.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## HEAF Structure (Before Welding)
- **Crystal structure**: Face-centered cubic (FCC) single phase, CoCrFeNiMn (PDF#33-0945)
- **Characteristic diffraction peaks**: 43.77°, 50.99°, 75.00°
- **Crystallinity**: Increased with increasing sputtering temperature, as evidenced by increased XRD peak intensity
- **Peak positions**: Unchanged with sputtering temperature, confirming stable single-phase structure
- **Surface roughness (RMS)**: 
  - HEAF@25 °C: 4.77 nm
  - HEAF@200 °C: 6.47 nm
  - HEAF@400 °C: 25.2 nm
  - HEAF@600 °C: 135 nm
  - 304SS substrate: 9.13 nm
  - TA15 substrate: 5.86 nm
- **Surface morphology**: Dense and uniform at 25 °C; grains transform from island-shaped to cluster-shaped at 600 °C; increased porosity at 600 °C
- **Elemental uniformity**: Nearly uniform, homogeneous composition without segregation

## Joint Microstructure (After Welding)
### Joint region classification (from left to right):
- **Region I**: 304SS base metal
- **Region II**: High-temperature diffusion reaction zone near weld in 304SS
  - Cr content highest in this region: 26.14% (without interlayer) and 27.82% (with interlayer)
  - Cr enrichment attributed to uphill diffusion (active diffusion) characteristics
- **Region III**: Weld fusion zone (appears black under OM)
  - Without interlayer: Fe 31.08%, Ti 48.35%, Ni 5.59%, Cr 6.75%, Al 8.23% (at.%) — likely Fe₂Ti, FeTi, Cr₂Ti IMCs
  - With interlayer: Fe 14.57%, Ti 70.46%, Ni 8.03%, Cr 4.33%, Mn 2.61% (at.%) — smaller amount of Fe-Ti, Cr-Ti, Ni-Ti IMCs
- **Region IV**: Smooth, flat β-Ti zone
  - Widths: Sample 1: 29.17 μm, Sample 2: 33.76 μm, Sample 3: 30.04 μm, Sample 4: 39.60 μm, Sample 5: 34.76 μm
- **Region V**: Biphasic α-Ti + β-Ti coexistence zone
- **Region VI**: Initial equiaxed α-Ti phase of TA15 base material

### Key microstructural observations:
- **Near 304SS side**: Well-formed solid solution welding, no delamination
- **Near TA15 side**: Distinct reaction layer interfaces; IMC formation
- **Weld seam gap**: Narrow gap observed at 25 °C sputtering; disappeared at 400 °C; cracks reappeared at 600 °C due to high surface roughness causing inadequate contact
- **Compound phase layer width**: ~4.5 μm with interlayer; ~10 μm without interlayer
- **IMC types without interlayer**: Fe₂Ti, Cr₂Ti, FeTi, Cr₂Ti, σ-Fe, NiTi
- **IMC types with interlayer**: Small amounts of Fe₂Ti, brittle σ-Fe, Ni₃Ti, NiTi
- **Phases on fracture surfaces**:
  - 304SS without interlayer: σ-Fe, Fe₂Ti, Cr₂Ti
  - TA15 without interlayer: Fe₂Ti, Cr₂Ti, FeTi, σ-Fe, NiTi
  - 304SS with interlayer: FCC phase structures
  - TA15 with interlayer: Ductile FCC phase, Ni₃Ti, NiTi

### Element diffusion:
- Ti and Fe element contents highest at the joint; primary IMCs are Fe-Ti intermetallics
- With interlayer: Element diffusion more uniform, smoother profiles, smaller peak platforms (~4.5 μm)
- Without interlayer: Fe, Cr, Ni exhibit multiple distinct peaks, indicating intense chemical reactions forming Fe-Ti, Cr-Ti IMCs; gradient platform ~10 μm

### β-Ti zone
- Fe, Ni, Cr are β-Ti stabilizing elements; width of β-Ti region reflects elemental diffusion extent

# Mechanical Properties

## Shear Strength
| Sample | Sputtering Temp | Shear Strength (MPa) |
|--------|----------------|---------------------|
| Sample 5 (no interlayer) | N/A | ~86.5 MPa |
| Sample 1 | 25 °C | >100 MPa (exact value not given) |
| Sample 2 | 200 °C | ~152.5 MPa (highest) |
| Sample 3 | 400 °C | >100 MPa (exact value not given) |
| Sample 4 | 600 °C | >100 MPa (exact value not given) |

- **Strength improvement**: Sample 2 shows 76.3% increase compared to Sample 5 (no interlayer)
- **Testing geometry**: Compression shear test
- **Compression rate**: 1.11 mm/min
- **Test repetitions**: Three tests per sample, averaged

## Hardness
- **Weld seam hardness**:
  - Without interlayer: Peak hardness ~675 HV (due to Fe-Ti and Fe-Ti-Cr IMCs)
  - With interlayer: Maximum hardness ~600 HV (increases with sputtering temperature)
  - IMC layer in joint without interlayer has significantly higher peak hardness and broader affected region
- **Hardness measurement**: Multiple points, interval of 5 μm, perpendicular to the weld

## Fracture Behavior
- **Fracture type**: All samples exhibited **brittle fracture** characteristics
- Without interlayer: Flat fracture, distinct river patterns and cleavage steps on both sides
- With interlayer (25 °C): TA15 side — brittle cleavage steps; 304SS side — flat, intergranular fracture
- With interlayer (200 °C): TA15 side — uneven, ripple-like, fan-shaped cleavage steps; 304SS side — smooth fine plane, rock candy pattern, typical brittle intergranular fracture
- With interlayer (400 °C): Larger brittle planes and finer planes on both sides
- With interlayer (600 °C): TA15 side — small planes with few cleavage steps; 304SS side — smooth surface with flow pattern
- **Fracture mode**: Fundamentally brittle in all cases, regardless of sputtering temperature

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

**Not explicitly studied in this paper.** The paper focuses on microstructure and mechanical properties of welded joints. The thermal stability of the CoCrFeNiMn HEAF interlayer during welding is implicitly addressed through the sluggish diffusion effect, but explicit oxidation behavior or oxidation resistance testing was not reported.

The paper mentions that HEA interlayers exhibit "excellent high-temperature phase stability" as a general property (Introduction section), but no specific oxidation experiments, oxidation weight gain measurements, or oxide layer characterization are reported for the joints or the interlayer material.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [CoCrFeNiMn High-Entropy Alloy Thin Film (HEAF)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
- Dissimilar metal joining: TA15 titanium alloy / 304 stainless steel
- As an interlayer material in vacuum diffusion welding

**[Composition Range]**
- Target: Approximately equiatomic CoCrFeNiMn
- Actual film (at.%). Cr 16.57–17.52%, Mn 16.70–19.24%, Fe 18.48–20.73%, Co 20.08–21.79%, Ni 21.96–25.71%

**[Main Function]**
- Acts as an interlayer to suppress the formation of brittle Fe-Ti intermetallic compounds during diffusion welding
- Reduces atomic diffusion rate between base materials
- Slows down elemental interdiffusion (sluggish diffusion effect)

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- High entropy effect and sluggish diffusion effect inherent to HEAs reduce the diffusion rates of Fe, Cr, Ni elements
- Unique crystal structure and lattice distortion of HEAs effectively slow down atomic diffusion rate
- Retards formation of Fe-Ti, Cr-Ti, Ni-Ti IMCs
- Element diffusion at the joint interface is slower, contributing to stabilization and homogenization of the joint interface structure
- Reducing atomic diffusion also significantly decreases Kirkendall voids

**[Property Effect]**
- Joint shear strength: 86.5 MPa (without interlayer) → 152.5 MPa (with interlayer at 200 °C sputtering), a 76.3% improvement
- Peak weld hardness: 675 HV (without interlayer) → ~600 HV (with interlayer), suggesting reduced brittleness
- Brittle phase width: ~10 μm (without interlayer) → ~4.5 μm (with interlayer)
- No reduction in fracture mode: all joints still exhibit brittle fracture

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- At higher sputtering temperatures (600 °C), surface roughness increases (RMS 135 nm), causing incomplete interface contact, stress concentration, gap/crack formation in the weld seam
- At 25 °C sputtering, narrow gap observed in the weld seam
- Cannot fully eliminate brittle fracture characteristics
- The interlayer introduces additional processing complexity (magnetron sputtering)

**[Original Evidence]**
- Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6, Tables 5–6, Fig. 7, Fig. 8, Fig. 10
- Conclusion section: "The maximum shear strength of this joint reached approximately 152.5 MPa, marking an increase of about 76% compared to the 86.5 MPa of the joint without an interlayer."
- "Joints with interlayer exhibited superior mechanical properties compared to those without, owing to the synergistic effect of high entropy and sluggish diffusion, which slowed down element diffusion and effectively suppressed the formation of brittle phases in the weld seam."

## [Ni (as a constituent of CoCrFeNiMn interlayer)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
- CoCrFeNiMn HEAF interlayer for TA15/304SS diffusion welding

**[Composition Range]**
- 21.96–25.71 at.% in the film (highest among the five elements)

**[Main Function]**
- Forms Ni-Ti intermetallic compounds (NiTi, Ni₃Ti) that are more ductile than Fe-Ti and Fe-Ti-Cr IMCs
- Optimizes plasticity and toughness at the joint

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Ni diffuses through the interlayer and reacts with Ti from TA15
- Ni-Ti IMCs are more ductile than Fe-Ti and Fe-Ti-Cr IMCs
- Presence of appropriate Ni-Ti IMCs can enhance joint plasticity and toughness

**[Property Effect]**
- With interlayer: Ni content at IMC region is 8.03 at.% (vs. 5.59 at.% without interlayer)
- Fe content reduced from 31.08% to 14.57% at the IMC region, suggesting reduced Fe-Ti IMC formation
- Contributes to reduced peak hardness (675 HV → 600 HV)
- Contributes to improved shear strength (86.5 → 152.5 MPa)

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- At high concentrations, Ni can also form brittle IMCs with Ti
- Excessive Ni-Ti IMC formation is not beneficial

**[Original Evidence]**
- Table 6, EDS analysis; Discussion on Table 6: "Ni exhibits excellent corrosion resistance at high temperatures, and Ni-Ti IMCs are more ductile than Fe-Ti and Fe-Ti-Cr IMCs. Consequently, the presence of appropriate Ni-Ti IMCs can optimize the plasticity and toughness at the joint, thereby improving the mechanical properties of the joint."
- Fig. 10: NiTi and Ni₃Ti detected on TA15 fracture surface with interlayer

## [Cr (as a constituent of CoCrFeNiMn interlayer and 304SS substrate)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
- CoCrFeNiMn HEAF interlayer for TA15/304SS diffusion welding; also a major constituent of 304SS

**[Composition Range]**
- 16.57–17.52 at.% in HEAF; 22.50 wt.% in 304SS substrate

**[Main Function]**
- Forms Cr₂Ti IMCs that are brittle and detrimental to joint performance
- Cr shows uphill diffusion characteristics (active diffusion, not concentration-gradient diffusion)

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Cr enrichment observed at the initial contact area on the 304SS side (Region II), with Cr content reaching 26.14–27.82 at.%
- Cr enrichment related to uphill diffusion (active diffusion) phenomenon
- Cr reacts with Ti to form brittle Cr₂Ti IMCs when interlayer is absent

**[Property Effect]**
- Without interlayer: Cr₂Ti detected on both fracture surfaces
- With interlayer: Smaller Cr-Ti IMC content at the joint
- Cr content at IMC region: 6.75% (without interlayer) → 4.33% (with interlayer)

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Formation of Cr₂Ti significantly embrittles the joint
- Cr uphill diffusion complicates concentration profile across the interface

**[Original Evidence]**
- Table 5, Table 6, Fig. 10
- "The Cr enrichment phenomenon is closely related to its uphill diffusion characteristics, where Cr diffusion occurs via active diffusion rather than concentration gradient diffusion"

## [Fe (from 304SS substrate and CoCrFeNiMn interlayer)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
- 304SS substrate and CoCrFeNiMn HEAF interlayer

**[Composition Range]**
- 18.48–20.73 at.% in HEAF; Balance (approximately 67%) in 304SS

**[Main Function]**
- Major source of the problem in direct Ti/steel welding: forms FeTi, Fe₂Ti IMCs
- The CoCrFeNiMn interlayer reduces Fe diffusion to Ti, suppressing Fe-Ti IMC formation

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Without interlayer: Intense chemical reaction between Fe and Ti forms Fe₂Ti, FeTi, σ-Fe, and Cr₂Ti IMCs
- Fe-Ti IMCs have low ductility and critically impair joint service performance
- With interlayer: Fe diffuses more slowly through the high-entropy film; Fe content in IMC region drops from 31.08% to 14.57%

**[Property Effect]**
- Without interlayer: Shear strength 86.5 MPa, peak hardness 675 HV
- With interlayer: Shear strength up to 152.5 MPa, peak hardness ~600 HV
- Fe-Ti IMC layer width reduced from ~10 μm to ~4.5 μm

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Fe-Ti IMCs remain present even with interlayer, though in much smaller amounts
- Fe₂Ti and σ-Fe are still detected on fracture surfaces with interlayer

**[Original Evidence]**
- Fig. 10, Tables 5–6
- "Due to the low solubility of Fe atoms in Ti, brittle Fe-Ti intermetallic compounds (IMCs) such as FeTi and Fe₂Ti tend to form at the weld interface, significantly reducing the strength and ductility of the joint"

## [Sputtering Temperature (Processing Parameter)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
- CoCrFeNiMn HEAF deposition on TA15 and 304SS

**[Treatment Range]**
- 25 °C, 200 °C, 400 °C, 600 °C

**[Main Function]**
- Controls surface roughness, crystallinity, grain size, and porosity of the HEAF
- Influences joint interfacial contact quality and subsequent diffusion behavior

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Surface RMS roughness increases with temperature: 4.77 nm (25 °C) → 6.47 nm (200 °C) → 25.2 nm (400 °C) → 135 nm (600 °C)
- XRD peak intensity increases with temperature, indicating improved crystallinity
- Grain morphology: island-shaped (lower temp) → cluster-shaped (600 °C)
- Increased porosity at 600 °C
- Surface roughness affects atomic diffusion rates during welding: rougher surfaces accelerate atomic diffusion

**[Property Effect]**
- Optimal sputtering temperature for joint morphology: 200–400 °C
- Highest shear strength at 200 °C (152.5 MPa)
- At 600 °C: Stress concentration, gaps, cracks in weld seam, declining joint quality
- At 25 °C: Narrow gap in weld seam

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Excessive roughness (at 600 °C) leads to insufficient contact and weld defects
- Increased porosity at high temperatures

**[Original Evidence]**
- Fig. 2, Table 3, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Fig. 8
- "The optimal CoCrFeNiMn interlayer sputtering temperatures were 200–400 °C to realize the most ideal TA15/304SS vacuum diffusion welded joints morphology"

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## [High-Entropy Interlayer Mechanism (Sluggish Diffusion + High Entropy Effect)]

**Related Elements**:
- Co, Cr, Fe, Ni, Mn (CoCrFeNiMn high-entropy composition)

**Typical Alloys**:
- CoCrFeNiMn (Cantor alloy) as thin film interlayer

**Key Compositions**:
- Approximately equiatomic Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-Mn (actual: Cr 16.57–17.52%, Mn 16.70–19.24%, Fe 18.48–20.73%, Co 20.08–21.79%, Ni 21.96–25.71%)

**Microstructural Changes**:
- Interlayer material retains FCC single-phase structure
- Element diffusion through the interlayer is slowed by the sluggish diffusion effect
- Reduced compound phase layer width at the weld seam (~4.5 μm with interlayer vs. ~10 μm without)
- Reduced Kirkendall void formation
- Elemental concentration profiles are more uniform and smoother across the interface
- Smaller peak concentration gradients in line scans

**Property Improvements**:
- Shear strength: 86.5 MPa (without interlayer) → 152.5 MPa (200 °C interlayer), 76.3% increase
- Weld peak hardness: 675 HV → 600 HV
- Brittle IMC region width: ~10 μm → ~4.5 μm
- Reduced hardness broadening at the weld

**Risks and Limitations**:
- Fracture mode remains brittle regardless of interlayer
- Higher sputtering temperatures (600 °C) degrade joint quality due to excessive surface roughness
- Some Fe₂Ti and σ-Fe brittle phases still form even with interlayer
- Requires additional processing step (magnetron sputtering) before welding
- Interlayer optimum performance window is narrow (sputtering temperature 200–400 °C)

## [Sluggish Diffusion (High-Entropy Effect) Mechanism]

**Related Elements**:
- Co, Cr, Fe, Ni, Mn (multiple principal elements)

**Typical Alloys**:
- CoCrFeNiMn high-entropy alloy (Cantor alloy)

**Key Compositions**:
- Near-equiatomic ratios of five elements

**Microstructural Changes**:
- Self-diffusion coefficients of elements in HEAs are approximately an order of magnitude lower than in conventional alloys and pure metals
- Lattice distortion and unique crystal structure slow down atomic diffusion rate
- Suppresses intermetallic compound formation
- Reduces Kirkendall void formation (imbalance in elemental flux at the interface)
- Results in more uniform and smoother element concentration profiles

**Property Improvements**:
- Significant reduction in brittle IMC formation
- Weld seam width decreases
- Shear strength increases from 86.5 MPa to 152.5 MPa

**Risks and Limitations**:
- Sluggish diffusion cannot fully prevent brittle IMC formation
- Effective diffusion suppression depends on interlayer quality (surface roughness, porosity)

**Original Evidence**:
- "Compared to conventional alloys and pure metals, the self-diffusion coefficients of elements in HEAs are typically an order of magnitude lower, resulting in significantly slower diffusion rates"
- "Joints with interlayer exhibited superior mechanical properties compared to those without, owing to the synergistic effect of high entropy and sluggish diffusion, which slowed down element diffusion and effectively suppressed the formation of brittle phases in the weld seam"

## [Ni-Ti IMC Ductility Improvement Mechanism]

**Related Elements**:
- Ni (from interlayer) + Ti (from TA15)

**Typical Alloys**:
- CoCrFeNiMn HEA interlayer for Ti alloy/stainless steel joints

**Key Compositions**:
- Ni content at IMC region: 8.03 at.% (with interlayer) vs. 5.59 at.% (without interlayer)

**Microstructural Changes**:
- Ni-Ti IMCs (NiTi, Ni₃Ti) form at the weld seam
- Ni-Ti IMCs are more ductile than Fe-Ti and Fe-Ti-Cr IMCs
- FCC-phase (Fe,Ni) solid solution forms at the interlayer/304SS side

**Property Improvements**:
- Reduced overall weld seam hardness (675 HV → 600 HV)
- Improved shear strength
- Lower brittleness at the joint interface

**Risks and Limitations**:
- Ni-Ti IMCs still have limited ductility compared to ductile metallic bonds
- Excessive Ni-Ti IMC formation could also embrittle the joint
- Contribution is secondary to the primary sluggish diffusion effect

**Original Evidence**:
- Table 5, Table 6, Fig. 10
- "Ni exhibits excellent corrosion resistance at high temperatures, and Ni-Ti IMCs are more ductile than Fe-Ti and Fe-Ti-Cr IMCs. Consequently, the presence of appropriate Ni-Ti IMCs can optimize the plasticity and toughness at the joint, thereby improving the mechanical properties of the joint."

# Key Conclusions

1. CoCrFeNiMn high-entropy alloy thin films were successfully deposited on TA15 and 304SS substrates using magnetron sputtering, exhibiting an FCC single-phase structure with homogeneous elemental composition.

2. Increasing sputtering temperature from 25 °C to 600 °C increased surface roughness (RMS: 4.77 nm to 135 nm) and improved film crystallinity, but caused porosity at the highest temperature.

3. The optimal sputtering temperature range for the interlayer was 200–400 °C to achieve ideal joint morphology.

4. The highest shear strength of ~152.5 MPa was achieved at 200 °C sputtering temperature, representing a 76.3% improvement over the 86.5 MPa measured for the joint without an interlayer.

5. The high-entropy and sluggish diffusion effects of the CoCrFeNiMn interlayer reduced elemental diffusion rates, suppressed the formation of brittle Fe-Ti, Cr-Ti, and Ni-Ti IMCs, and reduced Kirkendall void formation.

6. The brittle IMC layer width was reduced from ~10 μm (without interlayer) to ~4.5 μm (with interlayer).

7. All joints exhibited brittle fracture characteristics regardless of interlayer use, though the fracture morphology varied with sputtering temperature.

8. The interlayer promoted the formation of more ductile NiTi and Ni₃Ti compounds, along with FCC-phase structures, instead of brittle Fe₂Ti, Cr₂Ti, and σ-Fe phases.

9. The introduction of CoCrFeNiMn HEAF interlayers provides an effective solution for improving dissimilar metal joining between titanium alloys and stainless steels.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations explicitly identified in the paper:**
- All joints still exhibit brittle fracture, regardless of interlayer use
- Spreading temperatures above 400 °C cause excessive surface roughness, surface porosity, and weld seam defects
- The optimum sputtering temperature window is narrow (200–400 °C)
- Some brittle IMCs (Fe₂Ti, σ-Fe) are still present even with the interlayer
- The optimum sputtering temperature for film deposition is around 200–400 °C; at 600 °C, surface roughness increases significantly (RMS 135 nm), causing concern for interface contact

**Limitations not discussed but evident:**
- Only shear strength was tested; no tensile strength, fatigue, or fracture toughness data were reported
- No high-temperature mechanical property testing was performed, despite the intended service conditions implying elevated temperatures
- No long-term service stability, oxidation, or corrosion testing was performed
- Only one welding parameter combination (950 °C, 170 min, 10 MPa) was investigated
- No cross-sectional TEM characterization was performed for detailed phase identification at the interface
- The paper does not investigate the mechanism of how the interlayer reduces Kirkendall porosity in detail

**Applicable Scope:**
- This paper is applicable to dissimilar metal joining of titanium alloys and stainless steels using CoCrFeNiMn high-entropy alloy interlayers
- Not applicable to refractory high-temperature alloy systems, Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, or refractory high-entropy alloys
- The findings are specific to vacuum diffusion welding at 950 °C with CoCrFeNiMn interlayers
- Results may not directly translate to other joining processes (e.g., laser welding, fusion welding) or other temperature regimes

**Relevance to Requested Scope:**
This paper is presented for completeness but does not fall within the specified domain of refractory high-temperature alloys, Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys.
