# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Ultrastrong 42CrMo/CoCrNi multilayer composites at cryogenic temperatures
- **Authors**: Yuchen Zhang, Bo Li, Baoxi Liu, Kaisheng Ming
- **Journal**: Materials Letters
- **Year**: 2025 (Volume 388, Article 138322)
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.matlet.2025.138322
- **Alloy System Studied**: 42CrMo steel / CoCrNi medium-entropy alloy (MEA) multilayer composite
- **Research Objective**: To fabricate a multilayer composite integrating high-strength 42CrMo steel with high-toughness CoCrNi MEA via hot-rolling, aiming to synergize high strength and enhanced ductility at cryogenic temperatures.
- **Material Category**: Laminated/multilayer composite comprising a low-alloy steel (42CrMo) and a medium-entropy alloy (CoCrNi). This is not a refractory alloy system.

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper investigates a **multilayer composite** consisting of alternating layers of **42CrMo steel** (a Cr-Mo low-alloy steel with BCC ferrite structure) and **CoCrNi medium-entropy alloy** (FCC austenite structure). The composite is fabricated by hot-rolling stacks of the two materials.

This is **not a refractory alloy system**. The paper falls outside the scope of Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. The information is extracted here for completeness based on the provided paper text, but relevant knowledge cards for refractory alloy design are largely not applicable.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Hot-Rolling Fabrication
- Two multilayer composites were fabricated:
  - **L₄/₁**: Layer thickness ratio of 42CrMo:CoCrNi = 4:1, using 2 mm-thick 42CrMo sheet and 0.5 mm-thick CoCrNi sheet.
  - **L₂/₁**: Layer thickness ratio of 42CrMo:CoCrNi = 2:1, using 2 mm-thick 42CrMo sheet and 0.5 mm-thick CoCrNi sheet.

- **Processing route for L₄/₁ and initial L₂/₁**:
  - 40-layer alternating 42CrMo/CoCrNi stacks
  - Hot-rolling at **1150 °C**
  - Total thickness reduction: **~90%**

- **Additional processing for final L₂/₁**:
  - The hot-rolled L₂/₁ sheet was cut into 10 small pieces.
  - Pieces were stacked together and hot-rolled again at **1150 °C** with **~90% thickness reduction**.
  - This second rolling cycle produced the finer layer thicknesses (5.5 μm / 3.4 μm) in the L₂/₁ sample.

- No other heat treatment (e.g., annealing, homogenization, aging) is mentioned for the composites after hot-rolling.

- The composite sheets were tested directly after hot-rolling.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Phase Constitution
- **CoCrNi layer**: Austenitic structure (FCC) in both samples (Fig. 2a, d).
- **42CrMo layer**: Ferritic structure (BCC) in both samples (Fig. 2a, d).
- After deformation at 93 K, localized **martensitic transformation from FCC to HCP** was observed in the CoCrNi layer (Fig. 4d).

## Layer Thickness and Microstructure
- **L₄/₁ sample**:
  - 42CrMo layer thickness: **119 μm**
  - CoCrNi layer thickness: **28 μm**
  - Interfaces relatively flat with slight undulations.

- **L₂/₁ sample**:
  - 42CrMo layer thickness: **5.5 μm**
  - CoCrNi layer thickness: **3.4 μm**
  - Wavy interfaces due to local shear deformation mismatch under multiple hot-rolling processes.

- Both composites exhibit excellent metallurgical bonding without visible pores or microcracks at interfaces.

## Grain Size
- **42CrMo layer**:
  - L₄/₁: average grain size **4.7 μm**
  - L₂/₁: bamboo-like grain morphology with average grain size **9.9 μm**; single grains span the entire layer thickness. This morphology likely results from dynamic recrystallization during hot-rolling.
- **CoCrNi layer**:
  - L₄/₁: approximately **1.2 μm**
  - L₂/₁: approximately **2.2 μm**

- Explanation for larger grain size in L₂/₁: The much smaller layer thickness in L₂/₁ causes layer interfaces to have a greater influence on grain growth, leading to interface-assisted grain growth during hot-rolling.

## Interfacial Features
- Cr-rich carbide particles (M₂₃C₆-type or similar, though not explicitly identified) present in the CoCrNi layer near interfaces.
- Carbon diffused from the 42CrMo layer into the CoCrNi layer during hot-rolling.
- Interlayer atomic interdiffusion supports strong metallurgical bonding.
- No oxides formed at interfaces despite slight oxygen enrichment.

## Deformation Microstructure (post-tensile at 93 K)
- **42CrMo layer**: Deforms primarily through dislocation activities (Fig. 4a).
- **CoCrNi layer**: Deformation accommodated by:
  - High-density stacking faults (SFs)
  - Extensive deformation twinning (nano-twins in fine grains)
  - Localized martensitic transformation from FCC to HCP
- KAM (Kernel Average Misorientation) values indicate higher geometrically necessary dislocation (GND) accumulation in the CoCrNi layer than in the 42CrMo layer for both samples.

## Grain Boundary and Precipitate Features
- Carbides (Cr-rich) are primarily located in the CoCrNi layer.
- Precipitate size and distribution are not quantified in the paper.
- No grain-boundary features beyond the bamboo-like morphology are discussed in detail.

# Mechanical Properties

## Tensile Properties at 298 K and 93 K
Strain rate: **10⁻³ s⁻¹**

| Sample | Temperature | Yield Strength (YS) | Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS) | Elongation |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|--------------------------------|------------|
| **L₄/₁ composite** | 298 K | Not explicitly stated at 298 K | Not explicitly stated at 298 K | Not explicitly stated at 298 K |
| **L₄/₁ composite** | 93 K | **1.61–1.62 GPa** (abstract: 1.61 GPa; text: 1.62 GPa) | **1.99 GPa** | **4.9%** |
| **L₂/₁ composite** | 93 K | **659 MPa** | **1.05 GPa** | **5.0%** |
| **42CrMo steel (monolithic)** | 93 K | Not explicitly stated | **>2.3 GPa** | Brittle fracture with minimal plastic deformation |
| **42CrMo steel (monolithic)** | 298 K | Not explicitly stated | Not explicitly stated | Not explicitly stated |

- With decreasing temperature from 298 K to 93 K, both composites show increased strength and decreased ductility.
- At 93 K, the L₄/₁ sample achieves a balanced combination of strength and ductility (YS 1.61 GPa, UTS 1.99 GPa, elongation 4.9%).
- The L₂/₁ sample has lower strength but slightly higher elongation (5.0%) due to its coarser bamboo-like grain structure promoting easier dislocation movement in the 42CrMo layer.

## Fracture Behavior at 93 K
- **42CrMo layer**: Brittle cleavage fracture with flat surfaces and no dimples (Fig. 3b, c).
- **CoCrNi layer**: Ductile fracture with many fine dimples (Fig. 3d, e).
- Cracks initiating in the 42CrMo layer are arrested by the ductile CoCrNi layer, improving overall ductility.

## Comparison with Other Composites
- Compared with maraging steel/CoCrNi and SUS304/Cr17 multilayer composites fabricated by hot-rolling, the 42CrMo/CoCrNi composites exhibit comparable tensile strength but much higher ductility at room temperature.

# Key Conclusions

1. A 42CrMo/CoCrNi multilayer composite was successfully fabricated via hot-rolling at 1150 °C with ~90% thickness reduction (40-layer stacks; L₂/₁ underwent an additional stacking and rolling cycle).

2. At 93 K, the L₄/₁ composite achieves an ultra-high yield strength of **1.61 GPa**, ultimate tensile strength of **1.99 GPa**, and elongation of **4.9%**.

3. The CoCrNi layer enhances ductility at cryogenic temperatures by activating extensive deformation twinning, high-density stacking faults, and localized FCC-to-HCP martensitic transformation.

4. These deformation mechanisms in the CoCrNi layer enhance strain-hardening, reduce localized stress concentrations around the brittle 42CrMo layers, delay crack propagation, and prevent premature failure.

5. Cracks initiating in the 42CrMo layer are effectively arrested by the ductile CoCrNi layer, contributing to improved composite ductility at 93 K.

6. The 42CrMo layer provides the high-strength BCC component; at 93 K, monolithic 42CrMo steel exhibits brittle fracture with strength >2.3 GPa and minimal plastic deformation.

7. Optimizing layer thickness and grain size is critical:
   - The L₄/₁ sample (119 μm / 28 μm layer thicknesses; 4.7 μm / 1.2 μm grain sizes) achieves 1.61 GPa YS, 1.99 GPa UTS, 4.9% elongation.
   - The L₂/₁ sample (5.5 μm / 3.4 μm layer thicknesses; 9.9 μm / 2.2 μm grain sizes) achieves only 659 MPa YS, 1.05 GPa UTS, 5.0% elongation.
   - The coarser bamboo-like grain structure in L₂/₁ promotes easier dislocation movement in the 42CrMo layer, reducing strength.

8. Cr-rich carbides (Cr = 61.5 ± 2.5 at.%, C = 28.3 ± 2.2 at.%) form in the CoCrNi layer due to carbon diffusion from the 42CrMo layer during hot-rolling, indicating strong metallurgical bonding.

9. The multilayer design strategy offers a promising approach to developing cryogenic materials with both high strength and ductility, addressing the inherent brittleness of high-strength BCC steels.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Not a refractory alloy system**: This paper studies a 42CrMo steel / CoCrNi MEA multilayer composite. It does not involve Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, refractory high-entropy alloys, refractory multi-principal element alloys, ODS alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. Therefore, knowledge extracted from this paper has limited direct applicability to refractory alloy database construction.

- **Composition information incomplete**: The nominal compositions of the 42CrMo steel (wt.% of C, Cr, Mo, Si, Mn, etc.) and the CoCrNi MEA (at.% of Co, Cr, Ni) are not provided. The original paper only states the material names and identifies the major elements via EPMA.

- **Heat treatment details incomplete**: The paper does not report any post-rolling heat treatment (e.g., annealing, tempering) for the composites. The effect of heat treatment on carbide evolution, grain size, and mechanical properties is not discussed.

- **Mechanical testing limited**: Tensile testing was conducted only at 298 K and 93 K. No intermediate cryogenic temperatures, no high-temperature testing, no creep testing, no fracture toughness testing, and no strain-rate variation studies are reported.

- **Oxidation resistance not addressed**: The paper does not investigate high-temperature oxidation resistance, oxidation weight gain, oxide layer formation, or long-term service stability.

- **No elemental mechanism quantification**: The paper does not quantify the contribution of individual elements (e.g., Mo, Cr, C) to solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, or other strengthening mechanisms. The roles of individual elements are not systematically analyzed.

- **Grain size data for L₂/₁**: The paper states that the 42CrMo layer in L₂/₁ has a bamboo-like morphology with an average grain size of 9.9 μm, but it is noted that this is larger than in L₄/₁ (4.7 μm) despite the smaller layer thickness. The authors attribute this to interface-assisted grain growth during multiple hot-rolling processes, but this conclusion is based on qualitative observation and is not further substantiated by quantitative analysis.

- **Comparison with other composites**: The paper compares its composites with maraging steel/CoCrNi and SUS304/Cr17 multilayer composites at room temperature, stating that the current composites exhibit "much higher ductility." However, specific numerical values for these comparisons are not provided in the text.

- **No long-term cryogenic service performance**: The paper only evaluates tensile properties at 93 K without assessing long-term exposure, fatigue, impact toughness, or environmental stability in cryogenic service conditions.
