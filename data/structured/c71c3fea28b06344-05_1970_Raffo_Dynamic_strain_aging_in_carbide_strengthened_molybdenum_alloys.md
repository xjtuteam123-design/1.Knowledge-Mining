# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Dynamic Strain Aging in Carbide Strengthened Molybdenum Alloys
- **Authors:** Peter L. Raffo
- **Journal:** NASA Technical Note TN D-5355
- **Year:** 1969 (published July 1969)
- **DOI:** Not provided in the original paper
- **Alloy system studied:** Mo-Ti-Zr-C and Mo-Zr-C alloys (carbide-strengthened molybdenum alloys)
- **Research objective:** To obtain data that would aid in determining a detailed mechanism of dynamic strain aging in carbide-strengthened molybdenum alloys, by studying the temperature and strain rate dependence of tensile stress-strain behavior of a solution-treated commercial Mo-Ti-Zr-C alloy (Mo-TZC) and two experimental Mo-Zr-C alloys
- **Material category:** Carbide-strengthened molybdenum-based alloy

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies dynamic strain aging processes in solution-treated molybdenum-titanium-zirconium-carbon (Mo-Ti-Zr-C) and molybdenum-zirconium-carbon (Mo-Zr-C) alloys. The alloys are strengthened by the addition of Group IVA carbide formers (Ti, Zr, or Hf) and carbon. Three specific alloy compositions were investigated: one commercial Mo-TZC alloy and two experimental Mo-Zr-C alloys.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Melting and Fabrication:**
- Mo-TZC alloy was obtained commercially as as-swaged arc-melted rod
- Mo-ZC-1 and Mo-ZC-2 alloys were arc-melted at the Lewis Research Center
- Ingots were extruded at 3200 °F (2033 K)
- Extruded material was swaged at 2000–2500 °F (1366–1644 K)

**Specimen Preparation:**
- Tensile specimens prepared from 0.25- or 0.13-inch (0.64- or 0.33-cm) diameter rod
- Gage length: 1 inch (2.54 cm)
- Gage diameter: 0.16- or 0.07-inch (0.41- or 0.18-cm)

**Heat Treatment (Solution Annealing):**
- All specimens were initially solution annealed at 4000 °F (2477 K) for 30 minutes
- Performed in an induction furnace with a tungsten susceptor
- Annealing performed at pressure less than 10⁻⁵ torr
- Mo₂C powder was placed in the crucible with specimens to minimize decarburization (maximum losses of 10% using this technique)
- After heat treatment, specimens were quenched by turning off power and introducing helium gas into the furnace

**No additional homogenization, rolling, forging, or aging treatments were explicitly described beyond the initial extrusion/swaging and solution annealing.**

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Matrix Phase:**
- Molybdenum-rich solid solution (BCC, implied by reference to "body centered cubic metals")

**Second Phases and Precipitates:**
- In as-solution-treated condition: Mo₂C platelets that precipitated during quench (complete suppression of precipitation during cooling is "virtually impossible" in this alloy)
- After deformation at 3000 °F (1922 K): General precipitation of (Ti, Zr)C face-centered cubic carbide replacing Mo₂C platelets
- Fine particles approximately 100 Å (10 nm) observed at 2700 °F (1755 K), aligned in arrays suggesting nucleation on dislocations
- Possibility of unresolvable precipitates or metal-carbon pairs/clusters at lower temperatures (e.g., Ti-C or Zr-C pairs or clusters)

**Grain Size:**
- Not explicitly stated in the original paper

**Dislocation Structure:**
- As-solution-treated: dislocation density is low; dislocations may have been generated by thermal stresses around carbides during quench
- Strained at 1200 °F and 2100 °F (922 K and 1422 K): considerable tangling around Mo₂C platelets
- Strained at 2700 °F and 3000 °F (1755 K and 1922 K): lower dislocation density; precipitation has advanced further
- At 3000 °F (1922 K): dislocation density quite low; Mo₂C platelets replaced by general precipitation of (Ti, Zr)C

**Nucleation Sites:**
- Dual nucleation sites for (Ti, Zr)C: on dislocations and in the matrix (consistent with Ryan and Martin's findings)

**Dislocation Density Measurements (Table II):**
- Measured dislocation densities ranged from 6.6 × 10⁸ cm⁻² to 68.1 × 10⁸ cm⁻²
- Rate of dislocation multiplication (Kd = p/ε) ranged from 1.5 × 10¹⁰ to 11.2 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻²

# Mechanical Properties

**Flow Stress Behavior:**
- Broad flow stress maximum observed in all three alloys
- Peak flow stress (~70 ksi, 483 MN/m²) occurs at approximately:
  - 2100 °F (1422 K) for Mo-TZC
  - 2700 °F (1755 K) for Mo-ZC-1 and Mo-ZC-2
- The flow stress peak is sharper in Mo-Zr-C alloys than in Mo-TZC
- Flow stress at 0.05 strain measured

**Strain Rate Sensitivity:**
- Negative strain-rate sensitivity from approximately 900 °F (755 K) to 2100 °F (1422 K) for Mo-TZC
- At 2100 °F (1422 K): negative strain-rate sensitivity over entire range of strain rates (0.002 to 2.0 min⁻¹)
- At 2700 °F (1755 K): positive strain-rate sensitivity
- Maximum flow stress decreased with increasing strain rate
- Temperature of flow stress maximum was independent of strain rate

**Work Hardening:**
- High work-hardening rate in vicinity of flow stress peak
- Flow stress peak results from greater rate of work hardening in dynamic strain aging region
- Proportional limit data show small peak at approximately 2100 °F (1422 K); peak height increases with increasing plastic strain

**Serrated Flow:**
- Serrated stress-strain curves observed in narrow temperature range just below flow stress peak temperature
- Serration amplitude: on the order of 1 ksi (7 MN/m²)
- Serrations generally larger in Mo-Zr-C alloys than in Mo-TZC
- Stress-strain curves became smooth above the flow stress peak

**Critical Strain (εC):**
- Critical strain for onset of serrations observed; temperature and strain-rate sensitive
- For Mo-ZC-2 (Mo-1.08Zr-0.74C):
  - At 1500 °F (1088 K): εC = Aε̇^m with m = -1
  - At 1800 °F (1255 K): m = -0.1
- Relation between critical stress and critical strain: σC = σ₀ + BεC^(1/2)

## [Ti (Titanium)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]:** Mo-Ti-Zr-C alloy (Mo-TZC)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]:** 2.40 at.% Ti in Mo-TZC

**[Main Function]:** Group IVA carbide former; forms TiC or (Ti, Zr)C precipitates for carbide strengthening

**[Microstructural Mechanism]:** Ti combines with carbon to form titanium carbide or a mixed (Ti, Zr)C face-centered cubic carbide. During plastic deformation at elevated temperatures, precipitation of (Ti, Zr)C occurs on dislocations and in the matrix. The precipitates and/or Ti-C pairs or clusters immobilize mobile dislocations during dynamic strain aging.

**[Property Effect]:** Contributes to peak flow stress of approximately 70 ksi (483 MN/m²) at 2100 °F (1422 K) in Mo-TZC. The peak in flow stress is broader in Mo-TZC compared to Mo-Zr-C alloys, suggesting Ti effects the dynamic strain aging mechanism differently than Zr alone.

**[Potential Side Effects]:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**[Original Evidence]:** Table I shows Ti composition; Abstract and Introduction identify Ti as Group IVA carbide former; Discussion section mentions "Ti (or Zr)-C pairs or clusters" as possible immobilizing species (p. 14); dual nucleation of (Ti, Zr)C observed at 3000 °F (1922 K).

## [C (Carbon)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]:** Mo-Ti-Zr-C and Mo-Zr-C alloys (carbide-strengthened molybdenum alloys)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]:**
- 0.18 at.% C in Mo-TZC
- 0.49 at.% C in Mo-ZC-1
- 0.74 at.% C in Mo-ZC-2
- Mo₂C powder added during annealing to minimize decarburization

**[Main Function]:** Forms carbides (Mo₂C, TiC, ZrC, (Ti, Zr)C) for precipitation strengthening and dynamic strain aging

**[Microstructural Mechanism]:** Carbon combines with Mo, Ti, and/or Zr to form carbides. During solution treatment and quenching, Mo₂C platelets form. During elevated-temperature deformation, (Ti, Zr)C precipitates form on dislocations and in the matrix. The immobile carbides pin dislocations, increasing dislocation multiplication rate and work-hardening rate during dynamic strain aging.

**[Property Effect]:** Peak flow stress approximately 70 ksi (483 MN/m²) in the temperature range 2100–2700 °F (1422–1755 K). Serrated flow curves observed in narrow temperature range below the flow stress peak.

**[Potential Side Effects]:** Carbon diffusion in molybdenum is predicted to cause dynamic strain aging in the range 850–1100 °F (727–866 K), the lower end of the observed dynamic strain aging range. Single interstitials are unlikely to contribute significantly due to low solubility of interstitials in molybdenum.

**[Original Evidence]:** Table I; Results section describing Mo₂C platelets (p. 9); Discussion section on carbon diffusion (p. 13–14); equation (4) relating strain rate and interstitial diffusion coefficient.

## [(Ti, Zr)C (Titanium-Zirconium Carbide)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]:** Mo-Ti-Zr-C alloy (Mo-TZC) at elevated aging/deformation temperatures

# Key Conclusions

1. **Dynamic strain aging characteristics** in carbide-strengthened molybdenum alloys include: (a) a peak in flow stress-temperature curve in the range 1500–2400 °F (1088–1588 K); (b) serrated stress-strain curves; (c) negative strain-rate sensitivity in the region of flow stress peak; and (d) rapid rate of work hardening in the vicinity of the flow stress peak.

2. **Dislocation multiplication and strengthening:** The flow stress during dynamic strain aging is directly associated with high dislocation density, following the relation τ = τ₀ + αμb√(ρ). The strengthening is a work-hardening effect from increased dislocation multiplication rate, not direct precipitate hardening.

3. **Immobilization mechanism:** Dynamic strain aging is believed to arise from immobilization of mobile dislocations by carbide precipitates or metal-carbon pairs or clusters. The exact atomic species could not be definitively identified. Ti-C or Zr-C pairs/clusters are suggested as possible immobilizing species at lower temperatures, while carbide precipitation occurs at higher temperatures.

4. **Critical strain for serrated flow:** A critical strain εC for the onset of serrated yielding exists and shows anomalous temperature and strain-rate dependence compared to conventional substitutional diffusion-controlled dynamic strain aging, suggesting that substitutional diffusion cannot solely explain the observed behavior.

5. **Mo₂C behavior:** Complete suppression of Mo₂C precipitation during cooling from solution treatment is virtually impossible, resulting in Mo₂C platelets in the "solution-treated" condition.

6. **Temperature dependence of peak stress:** The flow stress peak occurs at approximately 2100 °F (1422 K) for Mo-TZC and approximately 2700 °F (1755 K) for Mo-Zr-C alloys. The peak is sharper in Mo-Zr-C alloys than in Mo-TZC.

7. **Activation energy:** The temperature range where serrations disappeared was so narrow that no meaningful value of activation energy could be determined.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations:**

1. **Limited alloy compositions:** Only three alloys were studied (one commercial and two experimental), all based on Mo-Ti-Zr-C or Mo-Zr-C systems. Results may not directly apply to other carbide-strengthened molybdenum alloys (e.g., Mo-Hf-C) or other refractory alloy systems.

2. **Mechanism unresolved:** The paper explicitly states that the atomic species responsible for dislocation immobilization "is obscure." The question of whether actual precipitates or simpler metal-carbon clusters cause the immobilization "may have to be resolved by other experimental techniques."

3. **Temperature range limitation:** The dynamic strain aging behavior observed is specific to the temperature range 1500–2400 °F (1088–1588 K) and strain rates 0.002–2.0 min⁻¹. Behavior outside this range is not characterized.

4. **No oxidation resistance data:** The paper does not address oxidation resistance or environmental stability, which is a critical limitation for high-temperature applications of molybdenum alloys.

5. **Microstructural resolution limitations:** Unresolvable precipitates at lower straining temperatures could not be directly observed, and the possibility of their presence could not be eliminated.

6. **No activation energy determination:** The narrow temperature range of serration disappearance prevented meaningful activation energy determination.

7. **Date of publication (1969):** The experimental techniques available at that time (transmission electron microscopy at 100 kV, no advanced characterization methods) limited the ability to resolve fine-scale microstructural changes and identify nano-scale precipitates or clusters.

8. **Solution-treatment limitations:** Complete suppression of Mo₂C precipitation during cooling was impossible, complicating interpretation of the "solution-treated" condition.

**Applicable Scope:**
- Carbide-strengthened molybdenum alloys of the Mo-Ti-Zr-C and Mo-Zr-C type
- Solution-treated (quenched from 4000 °F/2477 K) rather than wrought conditions
- Tensile deformation at elevated temperatures (1200–3000 °F / 922–1922 K)
- Strain rates from 0.002 to 2.0 min⁻¹
- Vacuum or inert atmosphere conditions
