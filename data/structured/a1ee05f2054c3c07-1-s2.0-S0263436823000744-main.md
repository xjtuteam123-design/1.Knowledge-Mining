# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Molybdenum alloy Mo-Ti-Zr-C adapted for laser powder bed fusion with refined isotropic microstructure and excellent high temperature strength
- **Authors**: L. Kaserer, J. Braun, J. Stajkovic, K.-H. Leitz, P. Singer, I. Letofsky-Papst, H. Kestler, G. Leichtfried
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106174
- **Alloy system studied**: Mo-Ti-Zr-C (TZM) molybdenum alloy
- **Research objective**: To adapt the conventional TZM alloy composition to LPBF processing conditions by increasing carbon content, and to investigate the effects on microstructure and mechanical properties at room and elevated temperatures.
- **Material category**: Additively manufactured refractory alloy (LPBF-processed Mo-based TZM alloy)

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Base alloy**: TZM (titanium-zirconium-molybdenum), a powder-metallurgy Mo alloy for high-temperature applications.
- **Conventional TZM composition**: 0.5 wt% Ti, 0.08 wt% Zr, 0.01–0.04 wt% C (equivalent to ~1 at.% Ti, 0.084 at.% Zr, 0.008–0.032 at.% C).
- **Studied variants**:
  - TZM-0.5 at.% C: slightly increased carbon content (0.5 at.% C)
  - TZM-2.3 at.% C: substantially increased carbon content (2.3 at.% C)
- **Research goal**: Investigate the effect of increased carbon content in LPBF-manufactured TZM on grain refinement, texture, oxygen segregation, and mechanical properties (room temperature bending, tensile at 23 °C, 800 °C, 1200 °C).

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Powder production
- Pre-alloyed spherical powders produced by gas atomization.
- Sieved with mesh size of 63 μm.
- Particle size (Table 1):
  - TZM-0.5 at.% C: D10 = 11.5 μm, D50 = 24.1 μm, D90 = 49.8 μm
  - TZM-2.3 at.% C: D10 = 10.7 μm, D50 = 21.1 μm, D90 = 41.8 μm
- Bulk density: 5.6 ± 0.1 g/cm³ (TZM-0.5 at.% C), 5.4 ± 0.1 g/cm³ (TZM-2.3 at.% C)
- Tap density: 6.1 ± 0.1 g/cm³ for both

## LPBF parameters (Table 2)
- Equipment: AconityLAB with 400 W, 1064 nm Yb:YAG fiber laser
- Linear energy density (LED): 0.58 J/mm
- Substrate plate temperature: 800 °C
- Layer height: 0.03 mm
- Hatch distance: 0.1 mm
- Scan strategy: Island scanning, 5 mm × 5 mm islands, zig-zag, 67° layer-wise rotation, shift factor 10
- Building chamber oxygen level: < 20 ppmv
- Substrate material: pure molybdenum

## Post-processing heat treatment
- In-situ heat treatment after building: substrate plate temperature set to 1200 °C for 1 h, then controlled cooling at 10 K/min for stress relief.
- Purpose: prevent formation of cold cracks.
- Samples separated from substrate plate via wire-cut electrical discharge machining.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Grain morphology and texture

| Parameter | TZM-0.5 at.% C | TZM-2.3 at.% C |
|---|---|---|
| Grain morphology | Coarse, columnar grains elongated along building direction | Refined, nearly isotropic grains |
| Mean grain size | ~2× larger than TZM-2.3 at.% C | ~0.5× of TZM-0.5 at.% C (exact value not stated) |
| Texture | Mild texture along BD, prevalence 4.3 in ⟨001⟩, clustering in ⟨111⟩ | Nearly non-textured, prevalence 1.8 in ⟨111⟩ |
| Isotropic | No (side view and top view grain size distributions differ) | Yes (side view and top view grain size distributions are similar) |

## Cellular subgrain structure (TZM-2.3 at.% C)
- Cellular subgrain structure observed in TEM.
- Cells contain Mo with Ti in solid solution: (Mo, Ti) cells.
- Mean cell size: 0.4 ± 0.1 μm.
- Intercellular phase thickness: < 100 nm.
- Intercellular phase composition: Mo, Ti, C, O — ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide.
- Interface between cells and intercellular phase: semi-coherent.
- EFTEM elemental maps confirmed:
  - Carbide network contains C, Ti, O, and Mo.
  - Spherical particles rich in Zr and O (ZrO₂) preferentially located at the interface between cells and intercellular phase.
- FFT analysis: intercellular phase lattice spacing of 0.23–0.26 nm and 0.5 nm; cell phase periodicity of 0.22–0.23 nm.

## Phases identified
- Matrix: BCC Mo with Ti in solid solution
- Intercellular second phase: ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide with dissolved oxygen
- Nanoparticles: ZrO₂ (zirconium dioxide)
- No segregated oxygen impurities found on grain boundaries.

## Densities
- Both TZM-0.5 at.% C and TZM-2.3 at.% C: absolute density 10.1 ± 0.1 g/cm³.
- Relative density: 99.9 ± 0.1% (TZM-0.5 at.% C) and 99.8 ± 0.1% (TZM-2.3 at.% C).
- Residual pores: predominantly spherical, diameter < 50 μm; sporadic aspherical pores with extension < 100 μm.
- Porosity comparable between both materials.

## Strengthening mechanisms discussed
- Grain refinement via carbon-induced thermal supercooling.
- Grain boundary purification via oxygen gettering by (Mo, Ti) carbide and ZrO₂.
- Carbide network formation (second-phase strengthening).
- Solid-solution strengthening by Ti in Mo matrix.
- Precipitation/dispersion strengthening by ZrO₂ nanoparticles.
- Grain-boundary strengthening via oxygen segregation elimination.

# Mechanical Properties

## Room-temperature three-point bending strength (DIN EN ISO 3327)
| Material | Bending strength (MPa) |
|---|---|
| Unmodified LPBF TZM (0.1 at.% C, from ref. [12]) | 490 ± 170 |
| TZM-0.5 at.% C | 666 ± 360 |
| TZM-2.3 at.% C | 998 ± 287 |

- Increase from 0.5 at.% to 2.3 at.% C: **~50% increase** in bending strength.
- Both materials exhibited brittle fracture without plastic deformation at room temperature.

## Tensile properties — TZM-2.3 at.% C (DIN EN ISO 6892-1 Method B at 23 °C; DIN EN ISO 6892-2 at 800 °C and 1200 °C)

| Test temperature | Orientation | UTS (MPa) | YS (MPa) | Elongation at break A (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 23 °C | Vertical (parallel BD) | Not explicitly stated in original paper | Not explicitly stated in original paper | 0 (fracture in linear elastic range, no plastic deformation up to 450 MPa) |
| 23 °C | Horizontal (normal BD) | Not explicitly stated in original paper | Not explicitly stated in original paper | 0 (fracture in linear elastic range) |
| 800 °C | Combined (vertical + horizontal) | ~600 | ~450 | 4–7% |
| 1200 °C | Combined (vertical + horizontal) | ~450 | ~400 | 4–7% |

- At 23 °C, fracture occurred in the linear elastic range at a stress of up to 450 MPa.
- No significant differences between vertical and horizontal orientations at elevated temperatures.
- At room temperature, vertically oriented samples showed lower tensile strength than horizontally oriented samples, attributed to elongated lack-of-fusion pores transverse to the test direction.
- Elongation at break up to 9% mentioned in the summary, and 4–7% in the results section — the paper states both ranges.

| Room temperature | Lower by a factor of 2 |

- The high carbide content and carbide network enhanced elevated-temperature strength.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxidation resistance**: Not explicitly investigated in this paper.
- **Oxygen embrittlement**: Major issue discussed. Oxygen segregation to grain boundaries weakens them and causes intergranular cracking in Mo during LPBF.
- **Mechanisms for oxygen mitigation**:
  1. Oxygen dissolution in ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide network.
  2. ZrO₂ nanoparticle formation.
- **Grain boundary purification**: No segregated oxygen impurities found on grain boundaries in TZM-2.3 at.% C.
- **High-temperature embrittlement**: Not explicitly discussed; the paper focuses on LPBF process crack susceptibility and room-temperature brittleness due to low fracture toughness of Mo.
- **Nitrogen/hydrogen**: Low levels in specimens confirmed (H < 5 μg/g, N < 1 μg/g). No nitrogen embrittlement discussed.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Carbon (C)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Mo-Ti-Zr-C (TZM) LPBF molybdenum alloy

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
0.1 at.% (unmodified), 0.5 at.% (TZM-0.5 at.% C), 2.3 at.% (TZM-2.3 at.% C). Conventional TZM: 0.01–0.04 wt% C.

**[Main Function]**
1. Grain refinement during LPBF solidification.
2. Formation of ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide network.
3. Oxygen gettering and grain boundary purification.
4. High-temperature strength enhancement.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Carbon causes solute rejection at the solid/liquid interface during solidification, creating a lag in the growth rate, which increases thermal undercooling and promotes new grain formation (nucleation).
- Carbon forms a closed network of ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide surrounding (Mo, Ti) cells.
- The carbide network absorbs oxygen impurities, minimizing diffusion length for oxygen and preventing grain-boundary oxygen segregation.
- The closed narrow network (mean cell size 0.4 ± 0.1 μm) maximizes oxygen-gettering efficiency.

**[Property Effect]**
- Grain size reduced by a factor of 2 when increasing C from 0.5 to 2.3 at.%.
- Texture prevalence decreased from 4.3 (<001> for 0.5 at.% C) to 1.8 (<111> for 2.3 at.% C).
- Bending strength increased by 50% (666 ± 360 MPa → 998 ± 287 MPa).
- UTS at 800 °C was 24% higher, and at 1200 °C was 16% higher than conventional PM TZM.
- Crack-free, dense components (relative density > 99.8%).

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Room-temperature UTS lower by a factor of 2 compared to conventional PM TZM (due to residual porosity and low fracture toughness below DBTT).
- No explicit negative effects on workability or ductility reported.

**[Original Evidence]**
- "Increasing the carbon content to 2.3 at.% yielded a significantly refined and isotropic microstructure." (Abstract)
- "C triggers a lag in the growth rate during solidification, owing to solute rejection at the solid/liquid interface, thereby increasing the thermal undercooling of the melt and resulting in new grain formation." (Introduction)

## [Titanium (Ti)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Mo-Ti-Zr-C (TZM) LPBF molybdenum alloy

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
0.91–0.99 at.% (approximately 1 at.% Ti)

**[Main Function]**
1. Contributes to grain refinement (second-order effect).
2. Solid-solution strengthening of Mo matrix.
3. Participates in ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide formation.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Titanium is fully soluble in Mo solid, so its own solute rejection effect is minor.
- Titanium likely reduces the diffusion rate of Mo in the melt, increasing the lag of the S/L interface and increasing thermal supercooling.
- Presence of Ti atoms may impede diffusion of C and Zr in the melt, steepening concentration gradients of rejected solutes and increasing constitutional supercooling.
- Ti dissolves in Mo cells forming (Mo, Ti) solid solution.

**[Property Effect]**
- Contributes to the overall grain refinement observed in TZM-2.3 at.% C relative to pure Mo-C alloys at similar carbon content.
- Not quantified separately from carbon effects. The original paper states: "comparing the contributions of titanium and zirconium to grain refinement is not possible with the available data."

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**[Original Evidence]**
- "In the case of titanium, a substantial solute rejection effect is unlikely, because titanium is fully soluble in the molybdenum solid." (Discussion)
- "Titanium might have contributed to the observed grain refinement with a twofold effect." (Discussion)

## [Zirconium (Zr) and Oxygen (O)] — ZrO₂

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Mo-Ti-Zr-C (TZM) LPBF molybdenum alloy

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
- Zr: 0.098–0.149 at.%
- O in LPBF specimen: 0.011 at.% (TZM-0.5 at.% C) and 0.023 at.% (TZM-2.3 at.% C)

**[Main Function]**
1. Binding oxygen as nanometer-sized ZrO₂ particles.
2. Oxygen gettering and grain boundary purification.
3. Grain refinement contribution via solute rejection (similar to carbon).

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Zr forms nanometer-sized ZrO₂ particles preferentially at the interface between (Mo, Ti) cells and ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide intercellular network.
- ZrO₂ formation consumes oxygen impurities, preventing their segregation to grain boundaries.
- ZrO₂ particles distributed at carbide-cell interfaces act as additional oxygen traps.
- Zirconium may contribute to grain refinement through solute rejection effect similar to carbon.

**[Property Effect]**
- Grain boundary purification confirmed: no segregated oxygen impurities found on grain boundaries.
- Contribution to overall strength enhancement not separately quantified.
- Crack-free LPBF processing achieved partly due to oxygen mitigation.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**[Original Evidence]**
- "nanometer-sized particles rich in Zr and O located at or near the interface" (Results)
- "Two mechanisms ensure that the grain boundaries remain free of oxygen: the formation of ZrO₂ nanoparticles and the dissolution of oxygen in the ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide." (Discussion)

## [Ternary (Mo, Ti) Carbide]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Mo-Ti-Zr-C (TZM) LPBF molybdenum alloy

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
Volume fraction depends on C content: 0.5 at.% C vs 2.3 at.% C. Specific carbide volume fractions are not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**[Main Function]**
1. Oxygen gettering (oxygen dissolution).
2. Grain boundary purification.
3. Retention of refined grain structure at elevated temperature.
4. High-temperature strengthening.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Forms as intercellular phase surrounding (Mo, Ti) cells in a closed network when carbon content reaches 2.3 at.%.
- Semi-coherent interface with (Mo, Ti) cells.
- Dissolves oxygen impurities within the carbide lattice.
- Closed network with mean cell size 0.4 ± 0.1 μm minimizes oxygen diffusion distance.
- Carbides retard grain boundary migration at elevated temperatures.

**[Property Effect]**
- UTS at 800 °C exceeded conventional PM TZM by 24%; at 1200 °C by 16%.
- Yield strength at 800 °C: ~450 MPa; at 1200 °C: ~400 MPa.
- Grain boundary purity maintained, enabling transgranular fracture and enhanced strength.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Room temperature tensile strength lower than conventional PM TZM, attributed to porosity not carbide phase.
- Not explicitly stated in the original paper if carbides reduce ductility.

**[Original Evidence]**
- "The UTS of TZM-2.3 at.% C at 800 °C was 24%, and that at 1200 °C was 16% higher than those of conventionally produced TZM rods in the stress-relieved condition." (Discussion)
- "Carbides retard the grain boundary migration at elevated temperatures and increase the high-temperature strength of molybdenum and molybdenum alloys." (Introduction)

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## [Solid-Solution Strengthening]

**Related elements**: Titanium (Ti) in Mo matrix

**Typical alloys**: Mo-Ti-Zr-C (TZM) LPBF alloy

**Key compositions**: ~1 at.% Ti in solid solution in BCC Mo

**Microstructural changes**: Ti dissolved in BCC Mo lattice; (Mo, Ti) cells formed.

**Property improvements**: Contributes to base strength of TZM. Specific values not stated separately from other mechanisms.

**Risks and limitations**: Titanium is fully soluble in Mo, so its strengthening contribution is limited compared to carbide and grain-refinement mechanisms. No specific risks identified.

---

## [Grain Refinement via Carbon-Induced Constitutional Supercooling]

**Related elements**: Carbon (primary), Zr (secondary), Ti (indirect via diffusion impedance)

**Typical alloys**: Mo-C alloys, TZM (Mo-Ti-Zr-C);

**Key compositions**: 2.3 at.% C most effective in this study; 0.5 at.% C produces only minor grain refinement.

**Microstructural changes**:
- Columnar, epitaxially grown grains at 0.5 at.% C → refined, isotropic grains at 2.3 at.% C.
- Grain size halved.
- Texture prevalence reduced from 4.3 (<001>) to 1.8 (<111>).
- Formation of cellular subgrain structure.

**Property improvements**:
- Isotropic mechanical behavior (tensile properties similar in vertical and horizontal orientations at 800 °C and 1200 °C).
- 50% increase in bending strength (666 ± 360 MPa → 998 ± 287 MPa).

**Risks and limitations**:
- At room temperature, residual porosity still limits tensile strength; fracture in elastic range.
- Standard deviation in bending strength remains large (±287 MPa at N=7 samples) due to critical pore size effects.

---

## [Carbide Network Strengthening / Second-Phase Strengthening]

**Related elements**: Carbon, Titanium

**Typical alloys**: TZM (Mo-Ti-Zr-C) LPBF alloy with elevated C content (2.3 at.%)

**Key compositions**: 2.3 at.% C, 1 at.% Ti → ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide network with cell size 0.4 ± 0.1 μm

**Microstructural changes**: Closed cellular network of (Mo, Ti) carbide surrounding (Mo, Ti) cells; intercellular phase thickness < 100 nm.

**Property improvements**: Elevated-temperature strength superior to conventional PM TZM (UTS +24% at 800 °C, +16% at 1200 °C).

**Risks and limitations**: Room-temperature tensile strength remains lower than conventional PM TZM (factor of 2), attributed to residual porosity and low fracture toughness below DBTT.

---

## [Grain Boundary Purification / Oxygen Gettering]

**Related elements**: Carbon (via (Mo,Ti) carbide), Zirconium (via ZrO₂)

**Typical alloys**: TZM (Mo-Ti-Zr-C) LPBF alloy with C ≥ 0.5 at.%

**Key compositions**: 2.3 at.% C for maximal effect; Zr present at ~0.1–0.15 at.%

**Microstructural changes**:
- Grain boundaries free of segregated oxygen.
- Oxygen dissolved in ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide.
- Oxygen bound in nanometer-scale ZrO₂ particles.
- Semi-coherent interface between carbide and cells.

**Property improvements**: Enhanced grain boundary strength, transgranular fracture, increased overall strength (50% bending strength increase from 0.5 to 2.3 at.% C).

**Risks and limitations**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

## [Oxide Dispersion Strengthening (ZrO₂ Nanoparticles)]

**Related elements**: Zirconium, Oxygen

**Typical alloys**: TZM (Mo-Ti-Zr-C)

**Key compositions**: Zr ~0.1 at.% in alloy; O ~0.02 at.% in LPBF specimen

**Microstructural changes**: Nanometer-scale ZrO₂ particles at (Mo, Ti) carbide/cell interfaces and in regions close to the intercellular phase.

**Property improvements**: Contributes to oxygen gettering and grain boundary purification; specific contribution to strength not separately quantified.

**Risks and limitations**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the ZrO₂ strengthening contribution.

# Key Conclusions

1. **LPBF compatibility**: TZM alloys with carbon contents of 0.5 at.% and 2.3 at.% can be processed crack-free by LPBF with relative densities > 99.8%.

2. **Grain refinement**: A carbon content of 2.3 at.% transforms the coarse, columnar, textured microstructure (prevalence 4.3 in ⟨001⟩) into a refined, nearly isotropic microstructure (prevalence 1.8 in ⟨111⟩), with grain size reduced by a factor of 2.

3. **Mechanism**: Carbon promotes grain refinement by increasing thermal supercooling at the solid/liquid interface via the solute rejection effect.

4. **Cellular microstructure**: TZM-2.3 at.% C develops a cellular subgrain structure with (Mo, Ti) cells of 0.4 ± 0.1 μm mean size, surrounded by a closed network of ternary (Mo, Ti) carbide (< 100 nm thickness).

5. **Oxygen management**: Two complementary mechanisms purify the grain boundaries: oxygen dissolution in the (Mo, Ti) carbide network and ZrO₂ nanoparticle formation. No segregated oxygen at grain boundaries was detected.

6. **Mechanical properties**:
   - Bending strength increased by 50% (from 666 ± 360 MPa to 998 ± 287 MPa) when increasing carbon from 0.5 to 2.3 at.%.
   - TZM-2.3 at.% C exhibits nearly isotropic tensile behavior at 800 °C and 1200 °C.
   - UTS at 800 °C: ~600 MPa; at 1200 °C: ~450 MPa.
   - Elongation at break at elevated temperature: 4–7% (up to 9%).
   - UTS exceeded conventional PM TZM by 24% at 800 °C and by 16% at 1200 °C.

7. **Room-temperature limitation**: Tensile strength at 23 °C remains below conventional PM TZM (approximately factor of 2 lower), with fracture in the elastic range due to residual porosity and low fracture toughness below DBTT.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Composition limited to TZM**: The conclusions are specific to Mo-Ti-Zr-C alloy system and may not directly transfer to W-, Nb-, or multi-principal element refractory alloys.

2. **LPBF-specific results**: All microstructural and mechanical data were obtained under specific LPBF conditions (LED 0.58 J/mm, 800 °C substrate temperature, Mo substrate plate). Results may not apply to other additive manufacturing methods (EBM, DED, SLM) or conventional powder metallurgy.

3. **Residual porosity effects**: Room-temperature tensile properties are limited by residual porosity; the paper notes that critical defect size effects dominate below DBTT. Higher-density processing parameters may yield further improvement.

4. **Limited test temperature range**: High-temperature tensile tests only at 800 °C and 1200 °C. Behavior at intermediate or higher temperatures (e.g., 1400 °C, 1600 °C) is not assessed.

5. **No creep testing**: The paper references creep resistance improvement indirectly but does not provide creep data. High-temperature long-term stability beyond short-term tensile tests is not validated.

6. **Oxidation resistance not investigated**: The paper focuses on oxygen gettering during LPBF processing, not on oxidation behavior during service. No information on oxidation resistance of TZM-2.3 at.% C in air at elevated temperatures.

7. **No fracture toughness measurement**: Fracture toughness is mentioned as an inherent limitation of Mo alloys but not directly measured.

8. **Statistical variability**: Large standard deviations in bending strength (±287–360 MPa) limit precision of strength predictions.

9. **Element-function contributions not separately quantified**: The individual contributions of Ti, Zr, and C to grain refinement and strengthening cannot be separated with available data.

10. **In-situ heat treatment limitations**: The stress-relief heat treatment was constrained by equipment capabilities (maximum substrate plate temperature 1200 °C), which may not fully eliminate all residual stresses.
