# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Improvement in the oxidation resistance of Nb-Si based alloy by selective laser melting
- **Authors**: Yueling Guo, Lina Jia, Bin Kong, Fengxiang Zhang, Jinhui Liu, Hu Zhang
- **Journal**: Corrosion Science
- **Year**: 2017
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.corsci.2017.08.022
- **Alloy system studied**: Nb-Si based ultrahigh temperature alloy (Nb-Si-Ti-Cr-Al-Hf system)
- **Research objective**: To examine the oxidation behaviour of Nb-Si based alloy fabricated by selective laser melting (SLM) compared with conventional vacuum induction melting (VIM), with emphasis on the effect of microstructure refinement on oxidation resistance at 1250 °C for up to 50 h.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Material category**: Nb-Si based ultrahigh temperature alloy, specifically a Nb-Si-Ti-Cr-Al-Hf multicomponent system
- **Alloy designation**: Nb-18Si-24Ti-2Cr-2Al-2Hf (at.%) nominal composition
- **Core phase constituents**: Nb solid solution (Nbss), Nb5Si3 silicide, and Nb3Si phases

## Powder Preparation

- Powder morphology: irregular shapes

- **Laser system**: In-house developed SLM system equipped with an IPG YLR-500 Yb:YAG fiber laser
- **Laser wavelength**: 1060 nm
- **Maximum laser power**: 500 W (continuous mode)
- **Substrate material**: Titanium (selected because Ti has unlimited solid solubility in Nb)
- **Substrate preheating**: Preheated by rapid scanning of laser beam four times
  - Preheating laser power: 150 W
  - Preheating scanning speed: 1500 mm/s
  - Preheating hatching distance: 0.12 mm
- **Processing atmosphere**: Argon (Ar)
- **SLM printing parameters**:
  - Laser power: 375 W
  - Scanning speed: 1100 mm/s
  - Hatching distance (h): 0.10 mm
  - Layer thickness: 0.05 mm

## Conventional Comparison Process
- **Vacuum induction melting (VIM)**: Used to fabricate comparison Nb-Si based alloy samples

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Phase Constitution (XRD, both SLM and VIM alloys)
- **Main phases**: Nbss (Nb solid solution), Nb5Si3, and Nb3Si
- No significant difference in phase types between SLM and VIM alloys

## Microstructure of VIM Alloy
- Large-scale Nb5Si3 phases in dark-grey contrast
- Darker parts of Nb5Si3 typically contain higher content of Ti, suggesting chemical segregation during solidification
- Nbss phases in light-grey contrast

## Microstructure of SLM Alloy
- **Drastically refined microstructure** compared with VIM alloy, due to larger solidification rate during SLM
- **Spherical Nbss phases**: maximum diameter of **306 nm**
- **Strip-shaped Nb5Si3 particles**: up to **200 nm** in length, distributed between neighbouring Nbss spheres
- **HfO2 oxides** (white contrast) formed during SLM processing
- **Molten pool boundaries**: arc-shaped configuration caused by Gaussian energy distribution of laser beam
- Molten pool boundaries display coarser microstructure
- Solidification proceeds vertical to the tangential direction of molten pool boundary (adversely along heat flow direction)
- **Cracks**: Some cracks still found on top surface of as-built part, even with optimized processing parameters and substrate preheating

## Strengthening/Property-Related Mechanisms Discussed
- Microstructure refinement via rapid solidification
- Grain boundary and phase interface effects on diffusion

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

## Oxidation Testing Conditions
- **Temperature**: 1250 °C
- **Atmosphere**: Air (per discussion of oxidation behaviour)
- **Exposure times**: 5 min, 3 h, 7 h, 11 h, 23 h, 35 h, and 50 h
- **Measurement method**: Total mass gains (sample + spalled oxide scales) measured with precision analytical balance (accuracy 0.00001 g)

## Weight Gain Results
- **SLM alloy**: 96.48 mg/cm² after 50 h at 1250 °C
- **VIM alloy**: 198.91 mg/cm² after 50 h at 1250 °C
- **Parabolic rate constant (k_p)**:
  - SLM alloy: **0.22 × 10³ mg² cm⁻⁴ h⁻¹**
  - VIM alloy: **1.22 × 10³ mg² cm⁻⁴ h⁻¹**
- However, the paper later states in Section 4 Discussion that the SLM alloy exhibits a slightly higher k_p value (0.22 vs 1.22), which appears to be inconsistent in the original wording. The original text states both "0.22 × 10³, much lower than that of the VIM alloy (1.22 × 10³)" in one place and "slightly higher k_p" in the Discussion. The numerical values consistently show SLM alloy with the lower rate constant.

## Oxidation Kinetics
- Both SLM and VIM alloys exhibit **parabolic oxidation kinetics** (linear relationship between weight gain and square root of time)

## Oxide Scale Composition (XRD and TEM-EDS)
Oxides identified on both VIM and SLM alloys after oxidation at 1250 °C for 50 h:
- **Nb₂O₅** (main oxide)
- **TiO₂/CrNbO₄** (cannot be distinguished by XRD due to same crystal structures and similar lattice parameters)
- **TiNb₂O₇**
- **Ti₂Nb₁₀O₂₉**
- **SiO₂** (amorphous/glassy phase, identified only by TEM-SAD/EDS, not XRD)

## Oxide Scale Morphology and Structure

### Surface Evolution
- After 5 min oxidation: Microcrystallized outmost oxides (< 2 μm in size) on SLM alloy; Nbss and silicide clearly distinguished on VIM alloy surface
- After 11 h: Relatively intact oxide scale on SLM alloy; spallation observed on VIM alloy
- After 50 h: Oxide scale on SLM alloy remains intact with cracking; VIM alloy suffers severe spallation with thick platelet-like oxide scales

### Cross-sectional Structure
- **Three oxide layers** form on both alloys after 3 h oxidation:
  1. Outer oxide layer (completely oxidised)
  2. Transitional oxide layer (partially oxidised)
  3. Inner oxide layer (slightly oxidised)

- **SLM alloy characteristics**:
  - More compact oxide scale without spallation
  - Outer continuous Ti- and Cr-enriched oxide layer
  - Inner continuous SiO₂ layer at interface of outer and transitional oxide layers
  - Larger volume of Si-oxides in outer oxide layer
  - Nbss phases oxidised more slightly (even Nbss not seriously oxidised)
  - Layered distributions of alloying elements across oxide scale
  - Several holes generated after 50 h, but oxide scale still attached to alloy

- **VIM alloy characteristics**:
  - Severe spallation
  - Cracking of transitional oxide layer after 3 h
  - Large-scale Nbss phases suffer severe oxidation with needle-like oxides
  - Poor integrity with various holes
  - No distinguished layering of elements
  - Si only enriched in areas where silicides exist

### TiO₂ Location
- TiO₂ oxides located on phase boundary of Nbss/silicide in both alloys, indicating phase boundaries act as shortcuts for inward oxygen transport

## Oxidation Mechanism Analysis (Section 4 Discussion)
Three beneficial effects of ultra-refined microstructure on oxidation resistance:

1. **Lowering oxygen partial pressure in alloy**: Dispersed fine silicides increase specific surface area, lowering oxygen partial pressure at metal-oxide interfaces

2. **Improving scale adhesion with lower stress level**: Microcrystallized oxides reduce volume expansion and relieve growth stress via plastic deformation

3. **Facilitating formation of continuous protective oxide layers**: More short-circuit diffusion channels (phase interfaces and grain boundaries) accelerate diffusion of Si, Ti, and Cr, facilitating selective oxidation and formation of protective SiO₂, TiO₂, and Cr₂O₃/CrNbO₄ layers

## Pilling-Bedworth Ratio (PBR) Reference
- TiO₂: PBR = 1.70–1.78
- Nb₂O₅: PBR = 2.68

# Key Conclusions

1. **Phase constitution**: Nbss, Nb5Si3, and Nb3Si are the main phases of Nb-Si based alloys fabricated by either SLM or VIM. The SLM alloy is distinguished by an ultra-refined microstructure compared with the VIM alloy.

2. **Microstructure features of SLM alloy**: Spherical Nbss phases with maximum diameter of 306 nm and strip-shaped Nb5Si3 particles up to 200 nm in length; HfO2 oxides form during SLM; cracks remain on the top surface.

3. **Oxidation resistance improvement**: SLM alloy exhibited significantly better oxidation resistance. After oxidation at 1250 °C for 50 h, weight gains were 96.48 mg/cm² (SLM) and 198.91 mg/cm² (VIM), with parabolic rate constants of 0.22 × 10³ and 1.22 × 10³ mg² cm⁻⁴ h⁻¹, respectively.

4. **Oxide scale characteristics**: SLM alloy formed a more compact oxide scale without spallation, while VIM alloy suffered severe spallation. SLM alloy featured a continuous outer (Ti, Cr)-enriched oxide layer and an inner successive SiO₂ layer, which retarded inward oxygen diffusion and improved scale adhesion.

5. **Mechanism**: The improvement in oxidation resistance is attributed to microstructure refinement, which:
   - Reduces oxygen partial pressure at metal-oxide interfaces through dispersed fine silicides
   - Improves scale adhesion through reduced growth stress and microcrystallized oxides
   - Facilitates formation of continuous protective oxide layers through accelerated short-circuit diffusion of Si, Ti, and Cr

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Cracking issue**: Cracks remain on the SLM-processed Nb-Si based alloy even with optimized parameters and substrate preheating, which could compromise mechanical properties and potentially accelerate oxidation in uncracked regions, as noted by the authors ("it is reasonable to infer that the uncracked SLM alloy would exhibit a much more favourable oxidation resistance").

- **No mechanical property data**: This paper does not provide mechanical property data (yield strength, tensile strength, elongation, hardness, etc.) for the SLM or VIM alloys, preventing direct evaluation of the mechanical-property trade-off from grain refinement.

- **Limited oxidation duration**: Maximum 50 h oxidation exposure at a single temperature (1250 °C). Longer-term stability and cyclic oxidation behaviour are not investigated.

- **No detailed in-situ characterization**: The mechanisms discussed are inferred from microstructural characterization and EPMA mapping, not from in-situ oxidation experiments.

- **Weight gain inconsistency**: The paper mentions the SLM alloy exhibits "a slightly higher k_p" in the Discussion section, while elsewhere the k_p of SLM (0.22 × 10³) is said to be "much lower" than VIM (1.22 × 10³). This inconsistency may be a typographical error, as the numerical values and all other statements consistently indicate SLM has superior oxidation resistance.

- **Limited Al and Hf role discussion**: The paper does not individually discuss the contributions of Al and Hf to oxidation resistance, limiting element-function understanding for these components.

- **Applicable scope**: This work specifically applies to Nb-Si-Ti-Cr-Al-Hf based alloys processed by SLM, with the findings relevant to dense, non-porous components (cracks notwithstanding). The results may not directly extend to other refractory alloys or processing methods without additional validation.
