# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Excelling mechanical response of a powder metallurgy medium entropy alloy under extreme loading conditions
- **Authors:** Atif Muhammad, Yulong Li, Du Bing, Aamir Raza Muhammad, Zakir Sheikh Muhammad
- **Journal:** Powder Technology
- **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.powtec.2025.120737
- **Alloy System Studied:** CoCrNi medium entropy alloy (equiatomic)
- **Research Objective:** To fabricate an ultrafine-grained CoCrNi medium entropy alloy via powder metallurgy (mechanical alloying + spark plasma sintering) and investigate its dynamic mechanical response at room and elevated temperatures (300–900 °C) under high strain rates (2500/s and 5000/s).
- **Material Category:** Medium entropy alloy (MEA), FCC single-phase, powder metallurgy (PM) processed; not a refractory alloy system. This paper is included for comparative context regarding dynamic high-temperature performance versus refractory high-entropy alloys (specifically TaNbHfZrTi is cited as a comparison in Table 1).

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy System:** CoCrNi equiatomic medium entropy alloy (FCC crystal structure)
- **Material Category:** Medium entropy alloy with ultrafine-grained microstructure produced by powder metallurgy (mechanical alloying + spark plasma sintering + annealing)
- **Research Objective:** 
  1. Fabricate contamination-free, fine-grained FCC CoCrNi alloy using advanced powder metallurgy with alumina milling media.
  2. Investigate dynamic compression response at ambient and elevated temperatures (300 °C, 600 °C, 900 °C) under strain rates of 2500/s and 5000/s.
  3. Elucidate deformation mechanisms including twinning, stacking faults, and dislocation activity that sustain work hardening in an ultrafine-grained material.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Cast Reference Material
- **Method:** Vacuum induction levitation furnace melting
- **Grain Size After Casting:** ~90 μm (coarse-grained)

## Powder Metallurgy (PM) Material
- **Mechanical Alloying (High-Energy Ball Milling):**
  - Milling media: Alumina balls (diameter 2 mm and 3 mm) — used instead of traditional metallic/steel balls to mitigate contamination
  - Vials: Steel vials
  - Ball-to-powder ratio: 1:2
  - Atmosphere: Argon gas filled and sealed
  - Mill type: Planetary ball mill
  - Milling time: 10 h effective
  - Rotational speed: 300 r/min
  - Cooling interval: 5-min cool-down every hour to prevent abnormal temperature rise

## Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS)
- **Die:** Graphite die, diameter 50 mm
- **Heating Temperature:** 1000 °C
- **Pressure:** 40 MPa (constant)
- **Holding Time:** 15 min at constant pressure
- **Cooling:** Furnace-cooled to room temperature under vacuum
- **Density Results:**
  - True density: 8.28 g/cm³
  - Bulk density: 8.10 g/cm³
  - Porosity: 2.22%

## Post-Sintering Annealing
- **Designation:** "SA" (sintered-annealed)
- **Temperature:** 1000 °C
- **Time:** 12 h
- **Atmosphere:** Vacuum, in a glass tube furnace

## Specimen Preparation
- **Method:** Electrical Discharge Machining (EDM)
- **Dimensions:** Cylindrical, 4 mm height × 5 mm diameter
- **Surface Preparation:** Mechanically polished on parallel surfaces

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Phase Constitution
- **Matrix Phase:** Single-phase FCC
- **XRD Confirmation:** SA material peaks coincide well with cast material; no second phase, oxides, or precipitates detected
- **Peak Shift:** Slight peak shift for SA attributed to change in crystallite size and/or strain induced during milling

## Grain Size and Distribution
- **Cast Material:** Average grain size ~90 μm
- **SA Material:** Average grain size ~3 μm (ultrafine-grained); nano-grains present in range of ~50 to 250 nm
- **Grain Boundary Character:** Conventional high-angle grain boundaries (θ > 15°); random orientation without prominent texture

## Nanostructural Features in SA Material (Before Deformation)
- **Annealing Twins:** Abundant; clear peak at 60° misorientation; twin thickness ~40 nm; straight boundaries
- **Nano-grains:** Recrystallized nano-grains (~50–250 nm) predominantly along pre-existing grain boundaries and triple junctions
- **Stacking Faults:** Present along twin boundaries
- **Dislocations:** Present in milled/sintered material from deformation during high-energy milling and high-pressure sintering
- **Local Strain Gradients:** Revealed by Kernel Average Misorientation (KAM) maps; intragrain deformation gradients in sintered and SA materials, absent in cast material

## Microstructural Evolution After Room-Temperature Dynamic Compression
- **At 2500/s:** Single-variant deformation twins; dense dislocation structures in twin vicinity
- **At 5000/s:** Multi-order, intersecting deformation twins with thickness ≤15 nm; high density of stacking faults; Lomer-Cottrell locks; nano-grains; bent twin boundaries with high dislocation accumulation
- **Twin Characteristics After Deformation:** Finer structures (≤15 nm), bent boundaries, higher dislocation density compared to pre-deformation annealing twins

## Microstructural Evolution After Elevated-Temperature Dynamic Compression (5000/s)
- **At 300 °C:** Twins and high-density dislocation clusters present; twin/matrix lamellae formed by subdivision of primary annealing twins
- **At 600 °C:** Abundant very fine deformation twins; dislocations confined within twin boundaries; grain boundary bulging (indicative of dynamic recrystallization initiation); no softening or fracture
- **At 900 °C:** Pancake/lenticular grain morphology (typical of dynamic recovery); dislocations along grain boundaries; nano-grains along triple junctions; annealing twins; very fine deformation twins still present → nano-twins remain stable at 900 °C

## Deformation Mechanisms in Cast Material (For Comparison)
- **At 2500/s:** Abundant stacking faults and Lomer-Cottrell locks; planar dislocation glide; no deformation twins observed
- **At 5000/s:** Deformation twins present (thicker than in SA material); higher dislocation density; dislocation accumulation along grain and twin boundaries

# Mechanical Properties

## Room-Temperature Compressive Properties
- **Loading Mode:** Compression
- **Quasi-static Strain Rate:** 0.001/s (MTS universal testing machine)
- **Dynamic Strain Rates:** 2500/s and 5000/s (modified Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar)
- **Yield Strength Enhancement (SA vs. Cast):**
  - Quasi-static: 354% higher than cast
  - Dynamic (5000/s): ~300% higher than cast
- **Fracture:** No specimen fractured under any loading condition
- **Strain Rate Sensitivity (SRS) Parameter (m):**
  - SA material: m = 0.29 (at fixed strain of 0.1)
  - Cast material: m = 0.069
  - Comparison: m ~ 0.02 for coarse-grained and ultrafine-grained copper; m ~ 0.03 for CrMnFeCoNi HEA
- **Work Hardening Rate:**
  - SA material exhibited continued work hardening at room temperature despite ultrafine grains
  - Work hardening rate at 5000/s exceeded cast counterpart at start of deformation
  - No strain softening observed

## Elevated-Temperature Compressive Properties (SA Material at 5000/s)
- **Test Temperatures:** 300 °C, 600 °C, 900 °C
- **Flow Stress Values (at 5000/s dynamic compression):**
  - At 300 °C: ~1690 MPa
  - At 600 °C: ~1380 MPa
  - At 900 °C: ~1120 MPa
- **Work Hardening at Elevated Temperatures:** SA material exhibited prominent work hardening trend up to strain of ~0.25 at all test temperatures, followed by steady-state regime; minor softening only at highest test temperature (900 °C)
- **No Fracture:** No fracturing at any elevated temperature condition

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxygen Content After Sintering:** Reduced from 3.076 wt.% (milled powder) to 0.04255 wt.% (sintered pellet), indicating that SPS consolidation effectively reduced oxygen content.
- **Carbon Content After Sintering:** Increased from 0.0387 wt.% (milled powder) to 0.125 wt.% (sintered product), attributed to carbon diffusion from graphite dies and foils during elevated-temperature compaction.
- **Oxidation Layer Type:** Not explicitly studied in this paper.
- **High-Temperature Stability:**
  - SA material exhibited thermal stability at elevated temperatures without softening or fracturing at 300–900 °C under dynamic loading.
  - Nano-twins remained stable at 900 °C; stability attributed to their low boundary energy.
  - Grain boundary bulging observed at 600 °C and above, indicating early-stage dynamic recrystallization, but no fracture or catastrophic softening occurred.
- **Potential Issues:** Oxygen contamination during ball milling remains a challenge despite inert gas use, due to increased powder surface area and potential leaks. Carbon contamination from graphite tooling during SPS was observed.
- **Grain Growth Resistance:** SA material retained ultrafine grains after 12 h annealing at 1000 °C, attributed to sluggish diffusion effects inherent to high/medium entropy alloys.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Co–Cr–Ni Equiatomic Combination]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** CoCrNi medium entropy alloy (FCC)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Equiatomic (33.3 at.% each)
- **Main Function:** Provides low stacking fault energy (~18–22 mJ/m²) that enables twin and stacking fault formation; contributes to excellent ductility, fracture toughness, and cryogenic-temperature mechanical properties.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Low SFE favors dissociation of perfect dislocations into partial dislocations; promotes deformation twinning and stacking fault formation during high-strain-rate deformation; sluggish diffusion effect retards grain growth at high temperatures.
- **Property Effect:** Dynamic flow stress of 1690 MPa at 300 °C, 1380 MPa at 600 °C, 1120 MPa at 900 °C (at 5000/s). Room-temperature yield strength increased 354% (quasi-static) and ~300% (dynamic) compared to cast counterpart. No fracture under any loading condition. The original paper does not provide specific room-temperature yield strength numerical values in MPa.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Relatively low intrinsic yield strength in cast form (coarse-grained), requiring grain refinement for high-temperature applications.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 1, Introduction, paragraphs 1–3; Section 3.1, XRD and EDS results confirming single-phase FCC and equiatomic composition.

## [Carbon (C as Impurity)]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** CoCrNi PM alloy
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 0.0387 wt.% (milled powder), 0.125 wt.% (sintered product)
- **Main Function:** Not intentionally added; carbon is a contamination element introduced from graphite dies/foils during SPS.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Carbon diffuses into sintered compacts at elevated temperatures; no carbide precipitation was observed in the final SA microstructure (single-phase FCC).
- **Property Effect:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper regarding mechanical property effects.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Carbon contamination may alter interstitial content and potentially affect stacking fault energy or mechanical properties; considered a contamination concern in PM processing.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.1: “the use of graphite dies and foils during compaction can result in substantial carbon diffusion into the sintered products at elevated temperatures.”

## [Oxygen (O as Impurity)]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** CoCrNi PM alloy
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 3.076 wt.% (milled powder), 0.04255 wt.% (sintered pellet)
- **Main Function:** Not intentionally added; oxygen is a contamination element from air exposure during ball milling.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Oxygen contamination occurs due to increased powder surface area during milling and potential leaks/impurities in milling environment despite argon atmosphere. SPS consolidation significantly reduced oxygen content (from 3.076 to 0.04255 wt.%).
- **Property Effect:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper regarding mechanical property effects of residual 0.04255 wt.% oxygen.
- **Potential Side Effects:** High oxygen content in milled powder could promote oxide formation, but final sintered product was free of oxides per XRD and TEM analysis.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.1: “Oxygen contamination continues to pose a significant challenge during ball milling, despite the use of inert gas atmospheres, due to the increased surface area of the powders and the potential for leaks or impurities in the milling environment.”

## [Alumina (Al₂O₃ Milling Media Selection)]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** CoCrNi PM alloy (processing-related, not an alloying element)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Used as milling balls (2 mm and 3 mm diameter); ball-to-powder ratio 1:2
- **Main Function:** Replacement of traditional steel milling balls to minimize metallic contamination during mechanical alloying.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Low-density alumina balls reduce contamination from milling media; effective cold-welding of powder particles still achieved despite lower density, as confirmed by SEM of milled powder morphology.
- **Property Effect:** Enabled production of contamination-free single-phase FCC CoCrNi alloy. The original paper does not provide specific quantitative comparison of alumina vs. steel ball contamination levels.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Low-density balls exhibit reduced welding/mixing efficiency compared to heavy steel balls; requires careful selection of milling parameters to compensate.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.1: “using less-density alumina balls in the current study helped to mitigate the contamination problem from milling media”; Section 3.1, Fig. 2b caption.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## 1. Grain Boundary Strengthening (Hall-Petch)
- **Related Elements:** Not element-specific; applicable to CoCrNi
- **Typical Alloys:** CoCrNi, CrMnFeCoNi, other FCC HEAs/MEAs
- **Key Compositions:** Equiatomic CoCrNi
- **Microstructural Changes:** Grain refinement from ~90 μm (cast) to ~3 μm average (SA) with nano-grains (50–250 nm); high-angle grain boundaries (θ > 15°); nano-grains at triple junctions
- **Property Improvements:**
  - Yield strength increased 354% (quasi-static) and ~300% (dynamic at 5000/s) compared to cast
  - Dynamic flow stress: 1690 MPa (300 °C), 1380 MPa (600 °C), 1120 MPa (900 °C) at 5000/s
- **Risks and Limitations:** Ultrafine-grained materials typically exhibit reduced work-hardening capacity and susceptibility to microstructural instability at elevated temperatures; this study demonstrates that the synergistic presence of nano-twins and stacking faults counterbalances these effects.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.2: “The plausible reason for SA's remarkable YS and FS is primarily rooted in its fine grain. It is well known that grain refinement enhances strength in a classical Hall-Petch way.”

## 2. Twinning-Induced Strengthening (Dynamic)
- **Related Elements:** CoCrNi (low SFE alloy)
- **Typical Alloys:** CoCrNi, CrMnFeCoNi, TWIP steels
- **Key Compositions:** Equiatomic CoCrNi; SFE = 18 ± 4 or 22 ± 4 mJ/m²
- **Microstructural Changes:** 
  - Multi-variant deformation twins at 5000/s with thickness ≤15 nm
  - Second- and third-order twins comprising few atomic layers
  - Twin/matrix lamellae formed at 300 °C
  - Nano-twins retained at 900 °C
- **Property Improvements:** Twin boundaries act as barriers to dislocation motion; reduced twin spacing increases strength, SRS, work-hardening rate, and mobile dislocation density at twin boundaries; sustains work hardening in refined grains.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Twinning is challenging to initiate in ultrafine/nanocrystalline grains due to increased resolved shear stress; critical grain size below which twinning is entirely suppressed was not evaluated.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.3.2: “The stability of nano twins at elevated temperatures is rooted in their low energy boundaries”; Section 3.3.1: “The evolution of deformation twins under dynamic loading serves as secondary plasticity carriers.”

## 3. Stacking Fault Strengthening
- **Related Elements:** CoCrNi (low SFE)
- **Typical Alloys:** CoCrNi, other low-SFE FCC alloys
- **Key Compositions:** Equiatomic CoCrNi
- **Microstructural Changes:** Stacking faults throughout matrix forming Lomer-Cottrell locks at 5000/s; SFs formed by glide of partial dislocations; full dislocations dissociate into partials to accommodate plasticity in low-SFE fine-grained materials
- **Property Improvements:** SFs act as effective barriers to dislocation motion; facilitate dislocation emission; collectively enhance strength and work hardening.
- **Risks and Limitations:** None explicitly stated; SF formation is energetically favorable when twinning is suppressed in refined grains.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.3.1: “Stacking faults are present throughout the matrix and make Lomer-Cottrel locks... The SFs also act as an effective barrier to dislocation motion.”

## 4. Work Hardening Retention via Twin-Boundary-Mediated Dislocation Storage
- **Related Elements:** CoCrNi
- **Typical Alloys:** CoCrNi, other nanotwinned FCC metals
- **Key Compositions:** Equiatomic CoCrNi
- **Microstructural Changes:** Bent twin boundaries with accumulated dislocations; dislocations transmitted through twins at misoriented interfaces; stress fields around dislocation pile-ups at twin/SF boundaries
- **Property Improvements:** Continued work hardening at room temperature and elevated temperatures (up to strain ~0.25 at all test temperatures); absence of strain softening or premature failure; high SRS (m = 0.29)
- **Risks and Limitations:** Mechanism may not be applicable to alloys with high SFE or coarse-grained microstructures; requires nano-twin stability at service temperature.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.3.1: “In fact, the evolution of deformation twins under dynamic loading serves as secondary plasticity carriers. The deformation twins provide extra interfaces to hinder dislocation motion and increase the flow stress of the material.”

## 5. Sluggish Diffusion Effect (High-Temperature Stability)
- **Related Elements:** CoCrNi multicomponent composition
- **Typical Alloys:** HEAs and MEAs
- **Key Compositions:** Equiatomic CoCrNi
- **Microstructural Changes:** Retention of ultrafine grains after 12 h annealing at 1000 °C; nano-twins stable at 900 °C; grain boundary bulging but no catastrophic grain growth
- **Property Improvements:** High-temperature strength without softening or fracture; flow stress of 1120 MPa at 900 °C and 5000/s exceeding TaNbHfZrTi refractory HEA at 450 °C
- **Risks and Limitations:** Grain boundary bulging observed at 600 °C, suggesting incipient dynamic recrystallization; long-term thermal stability above 900 °C not evaluated.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.3.2: “The stability of nano twins at elevated temperatures is rooted in their low energy boundaries... Nano twins further subdivide the original ultrafine grains into nano-blocks, creating more interfaces to reduce the mean free path of dislocation.”

# Key Conclusions

1. Ultrafine-grained single-phase FCC CoCrNi medium entropy alloy was successfully fabricated via high-energy ball milling with alumina balls + spark plasma sintering at 1000 °C/40 MPa/15 min + annealing at 1000 °C/12 h vacuum, without second phases, oxides, precipitates, or metallic contamination.
2. The SA alloy exhibited 354% higher quasi-static yield strength and ~300% higher dynamic yield strength (at 5000/s) compared to cast counterpart; no fracture under any tested condition.
3. SA material demonstrated outstanding strain rate sensitivity (m = 0.29) and work hardening retention at room temperature despite ultrafine grain size, attributed to synergistic action of deformation twins, stacking faults, nano-grains, and dislocations.
4. Dynamic compression at 5000/s produced multivariant, intersectional deformation twins (≤15 nm thick) and high-density Lomer-Cottrell locks in SA material, serving as barriers to dislocation motion and enhancing strength.
5. At elevated temperatures (300–900 °C), SA material retained high flow stress (1690 MPa at 300 °C, 1380 MPa at 600 °C, 1120 MPa at 900 °C) at 5000/s without softening or fracture.
6. Nano-twins persisted at 900 °C due to their low-energy boundaries, contributing to elevated-temperature stability and work hardening; grain boundary bulging signified early-stage dynamic recrystallization without failure.
7. PM CoCrNi SA alloy outperformed TaNbHfZrTi refractory HEA (1000 MPa at 450 °C, 2600/s) with 1120 MPa at 900 °C and 5000/s; comparable to Ni-based superalloy GH4133B and LY100 steel at lower temperatures.
8. Key processing factors: use of alumina balls (avoid metallic contamination), 10 h milling at 300 r/min, 1:2 ball-to-powder ratio, argon atmosphere, SPS at 1000 °C/40 MPa/15 min, and furnace cooling to produce ultrafine grains with retained nano-twins.
9. The PM method is a viable route for producing ultrafine-grained MEA with dynamic high-temperature mechanical performance exceeding several conventional and refractory alloys.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Not a Refractory Alloy Study:** This paper focuses on CoCrNi medium entropy alloy; it does not study Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or other refractory alloy systems except for quoting one comparison data point for TaNbHfZrTi refractory HEA.
- **Compression-Only Loading:** All mechanical data are from compression tests; no tensile, flexural, or fracture toughness data are provided. Tensile ductility and tensile strength values are not available from this paper.
- **No Long-Term High-Temperature Exposure:** The elevated-temperature tests were dynamic compression tests at 300–900 °C; no long-term creep, oxidation, or thermal cycling data are presented. High-temperature stability beyond 900 °C was not evaluated.
- **Grain Size Effect on Twinning Suppression:** The critical grain size below which twinning is entirely suppressed in CoCrNi was not evaluated in this study; authors note this as a future research direction.
- **Porosity:** Bulk density (8.10 g/cm³) was slightly lower than true density (8.28 g/cm³), resulting in 2.22% porosity; the influence of this porosity on mechanical properties was not systematically addressed.
- **Contamination Control:** Despite use of alumina balls and argon atmosphere, oxygen contamination (3.076 wt.% in milled powder) and carbon diffusion (0.125 wt.% in sintered product) were observed; the impact of residual C (0.125 wt.%) on stacking fault energy or mechanical properties requires further investigation.
- **No Ductile-to-Brittle Transition Data:** Applicable scope does not cover cryogenic behavior of this PM alloy; the paper cites cryogenic properties of cast CoCrNi for context but does not test PM CoCrNi at cryogenic temperatures.
- **Strain Rate Range:** Dynamic tests were limited to 2500/s and 5000/s; quasi-static at 0.001/s. Ultra-high strain rates (>10⁴/s) and intermediate strain rates were not explored.
- **Comparison Basis:** The comparison in Table 1 is against literature data from different testing conditions (different strain rates and specimen geometries); direct quantitative comparison should be interpreted with caution.
