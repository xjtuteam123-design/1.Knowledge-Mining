# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title**: Gradient-structured high-entropy alloy with improved combination of strength and hydrogen embrittlement resistance
- **Authors**: Abbas Mohammadi, Marc Novelli, Makoto Arita, Jae Wung Bae, Hyoung Seop Kim, Thierry Grosdidier, Kaveh Edalati
- **Journal**: Corrosion Science
- **Year**: 2022
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.corsci.2022.110253
- **Alloy system studied**: CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy (Cantor alloy)
- **Research objective**: To introduce gradient nanostructures containing surface nanotwins in a CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy via surface mechanical attrition treatment (SMAT), and to compare hydrogen embrittlement resistance with coarse-grained and nanograined counterparts.
- **Material category**: High-entropy alloy (FCC structured, equiatomic CrMnFeCoNi / Cantor alloy). Note: This paper does NOT involve Nb-W, Mo-W, or refractory high-entropy alloy systems. However, its methodology of gradient-structured design and hydrogen embrittlement resistance may be conceptually transferable to other alloy systems.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system**: Equiatomic CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy (Cantor alloy), face-centered cubic (FCC) structure.
- **Research object**: Comparison of three microstructural conditions: (i) coarse-grained (homogenized), (ii) gradient-structured (SMAT-processed under soft and mild conditions), and (iii) nanograined (HPT-processed), with focus on tensile properties and hydrogen embrittlement resistance.

# Alloy Composition

- **Nominal composition**: Equiatomic CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy.
- **Individual elements**: Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni (five principal elements in equiatomic ratio; approximately 20 at.% each). Weight percentages are not explicitly given in the paper.
- **Raw material purity**: Pure metals with purities higher than 99.5 wt.%
- **Impurities/ppm-level elements**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper for the alloy itself. However, micro-pores formed during vacuum induction melting are mentioned as microstructural features.
- **Presence of W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf, Ti, V, Cr, Al, Si, Re, C, B, Y, La, O**: Cr is a principal element. No W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf, Ti, V, Al, Si, Re, C, B, Y, La, O additions are reported in this alloy system.
- **SMAT ball material**: 100C6 steel (Fe–1–1.4 Cr–0.3Mn–0.2Si, in wt%), hardness ~59 HRC. This is the processing tool material, not the alloy composition.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Melting method**: Vacuum induction melting under an argon atmosphere.
- **Homogenization**: 1273 K (1000 °C) for 1 h, followed by water quenching. Produced coarse-grained structure with average grain size of ~30 μm.
- **Surface mechanical attrition treatment (SMAT)**:
  - Ultrasonic SMAT method at room temperature, applied on four faces of tensile specimens.
  - Soft condition: ultrasonic amplitude 40 μm, 30 s per face.
  - Mild condition: ultrasonic amplitude 60 μm, 60 s per face.
  - Ball diameter: 2 mm.
  - Selected to introduce surface nanotwins while avoiding surface nanograins from intense SMAT.
- **High-pressure torsion (HPT)**:
  - Disks of 10 mm diameter and 0.8 mm thickness cut from homogenized sample.
  - Pressure: 6 GPa.
  - Turns: 10.
  - Temperature: room temperature.
  - Produced nanograined structure with average grain size of 25 nm.
- **Hydrogen charging**:
  - Electrochemical hydrogen charging for 48 h at room temperature (300 K).
  - Cathodic current density: 100 mA cm⁻².
  - Electrolyte: 3% NaCl aqueous solution containing 3 g L⁻¹ NH₄SCN.
  - Platinum wire as counter electrode.
- **Tensile specimen gauge dimensions**:
  - Homogenized and SMAT-processed: 1 × 1 × 4 mm³.
  - HPT-processed: 0.6 × 0.6 × 1.5 mm³, prepared at 2 mm away from the disc center.
- **No rolling, forging, annealing, or aging treatments** are used in this paper beyond the above processing routes.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Face-centered cubic (FCC), as confirmed by EBSD phase map for the homogenized sample. No BCC, B2, Laves, sigma, or other second phases are reported.
- **Coarse-grained (homogenized) sample**:
  - Equiaxed micrograins with average size of ~30 μm.
  - Annealing twins present within the microstructure.
  - Micro-pores present (formed during vacuum induction melting).
  - Uniform distribution of Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni (SEM-EDS).
- **Gradient-structured (SMAT-processed) samples**:
  - Deformed structure beneath the surfaces.
  - Thin refined or nanotwinned region just below the surface (particularly for mild SMAT).
  - Intragranular disorientations observed via EBSD; more significant for mild SMAT.
  - High density of planar defects including {111} 60° twins near the surface.
  - Gradient of microstructure from surface to interior (early stages of surface nanocrystallization).
- **Nanograined (HPT-processed) sample**:
  - Numerous nanograins with average size of 25 nm.
  - Weak crystallographic texture with random misorientation angles (ring SAED pattern).
  - Some regions less distorted; other regions distorted by grain boundaries or dislocations.
  - Grain-boundary mobility and dislocation activity limited by the five principal elements, resulting in finer grain size than typical HPT-processed simple metals/alloys.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed**:
  - Grain refinement strengthening and twin-boundary strengthening (surface nanotwins) in gradient-structured samples.
  - Grain-boundary strengthening in nanograined HPT sample.
  - The paper does NOT discuss solid-solution strengthening, carbide precipitation strengthening, oxide dispersion strengthening, or ODS strengthening in this alloy system.
  - Dislocation accumulation capability of the coarse-grained interior contributes to ductility.

- **Room-temperature yield strength of gradient-structured samples**: 500 MPa (soft SMAT) and 700 MPa (mild SMAT), which is 2–3 times higher than the yield stress of coarse-grained material (220 MPa).
- **Room-temperature ductility of gradient-structured samples under hydrogen**: 15–33%, with soft SMAT showing 22% uniform ductility.
- **Hardness, elastic modulus, fracture toughness, creep performance, rupture life**: Not reported in this paper.
- **Density**: Not reported.
- **Loading mode**: Tensile.
- **Atmosphere**: Not explicitly stated for tensile tests in air; hydrogen-charged tests were conducted after electrochemical charging.
- **Thermal desorption spectroscopy (TDS)**: Heating rate 400 K h⁻¹; temperature range 308–773 K.
- **Hydrogen content**: 3.4 wt. ppm (coarse-grained), 5.3 wt. ppm (soft SMAT), 6.1 wt. ppm (mild SMAT), 19.5 wt. ppm (HPT nanograined).

**Fracture behavior**:
- Homogenized uncharged: completely ductile with many dimples.
- Homogenized hydrogen-charged: ductile center with dimples; intergranular cracking at edge with slip traces (hydrogen-enhanced localized plasticity, HELP; possibly also adsorption-induced dislocation emission, AIDE).
- Soft SMAT uncharged: ductile with dimples at edge and center.
- Soft SMAT hydrogen-charged: ductile center; brittle edge with intergranular and transgranular cracking, multiple slip planes, flat surfaces. Transgranular cracking associated with surface nanotwins.
- Mild SMAT uncharged: ductile with dimples.
- Mild SMAT hydrogen-charged: ductile center; intergranular and transgranular cracking at edge; step-like ridge transgranular cracking resembling twin pattern; twin boundary cracking contributes.
- HPT uncharged: localized ductility with small dimples in center and edge.
- HPT hydrogen-charged: intergranular cracking at edge and center; grain boundaries visible; hydrogen-enhanced decohesion (HEDE) mechanism; failure in elastic region.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **This paper does not investigate oxidation resistance.** No oxidation weight gain, oxide layer characterization, or high-temperature oxidation behavior is reported.
- **Service stability issues discussed**: Hydrogen embrittlement, specifically:
  - Hydrogen diffusion from surface is enhanced by refined microstructure; nanograin boundaries can enhance hydrogen diffusion.
  - Coherent twin boundaries do not enhance hydrogen diffusion as significantly as grain boundaries, but twin boundaries on surface may act as crack initiation/propagation sites.
  - Hydrogen embrittlement mechanisms identified: HELP (coarse-grained, SMAT-processed), HEDE (nanograined HPT).
  - Critical hydrogen content for initiation/acceleration of HELP mechanism in this HEA: below approximately 6.1 wt. ppm (as measured in mild SMAT sample).
- **No high-temperature embrittlement, oxygen embrittlement, nitrogen embrittlement, or grain-boundary oxidation** are discussed.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni Equiatomic Combination]

**Applicable Alloy System**: CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy (Cantor alloy), FCC structured.

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Equiatomic (approximately 20 at.% each); raw materials with purity >99.5 wt.%.

**Main Function**: Formation of FCC high-entropy alloy matrix with good hydrogen embrittlement resistance and high thermodynamic stability.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: 
- High configurational entropy stabilizes single FCC phase.
- Presence of five principal elements limits dislocation activity and grain-boundary mobility, resulting in finer grain size after HPT (25 nm) than typical HPT-processed metals/simple alloys.
- The motion of dislocations locally accelerated by hydrogen is suppressed in the presence of five elements in solid solution form, increasing the threshold hydrogen content for HELP mechanism.

**Property Effect**: 
- Coarse-grained condition: yield strength ~220 MPa; 65% elongation after hydrogen charging; hydrogen content 3.4 wt. ppm.
- Gradient-structured condition: yield strength 500–700 MPa; 15–33% elongation under hydrogen.
- Nanograined condition: yield strength 1850 MPa uncharged; breaks in elastic region after hydrogen charging.
- Good hydrogen embrittlement resistance at hydrogen contents below ~6.1 wt. ppm.

**Potential Side Effects**: 
- Low yield strength in coarse-grained condition.
- High sensitivity of nanograined condition to hydrogen embrittlement (19.5 wt. ppm hydrogen, intergranular cracking, HEDE).
- Micro-pores from vacuum induction melting may remain after processing.

**Original Evidence**: 
- Fig. 1 (EDS elemental mappings show uniform distribution), Fig. 5 (TEM images of HPT-processed nanograins), Fig. 6 (TDS spectra and tensile stress-strain curves), Discussion section, paragraphs on FCC HEA hydrogen compatibility.

## [Nanotwins (introduced by SMAT on surface)]

**Applicable Alloy System**: CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy (surface layer of gradient-structured samples).

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not applicable (microstructural feature, not alloying element). Introduced by SMAT under soft (40 μm, 30 s) or mild (60 μm, 60 s) conditions.

**Main Function**: Improvement of strength-ductility combination and resistance to hydrogen embrittlement in surface layer.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: 
- Nanotwins act as barriers for dislocation motion and sites for dislocation nucleation, improving strength-ductility combination.
- Hydrogen diffusion through coherent twin boundaries is not as significant as through grain boundaries.
- Twin boundaries on surface may act as crack initiation and propagation sites.
- Nanotwin gradients can counteract material weakening in presence of hydrogen through enhanced localized strengthening via a self-accommodation mechanism.

**Property Effect**: 
- Soft SMAT: yield strength 500 MPa, uniform ductility 22% under hydrogen; hydrogen content 5.3 wt. ppm.
- Mild SMAT: yield strength 700 MPa; hydrogen content 6.1 wt. ppm.
- Transgranular cracking observed in hydrogen-charged SMAT samples, associated with surface nanotwins.

**Potential Side Effects**: 
- Twin boundaries on surface may act as crack initiation and propagation sites (Fig. 9f).
- More severe SMAT (intense conditions) could introduce surface nanograins, which enhance hydrogen diffusion and weaken hydrogen embrittlement resistance (intentionally avoided in this study).

**Original Evidence**: 
- Fig. 3 (EBSD IPF maps), Fig. 4 ({111} 60° twin boundary mapping, intragranular misorientation), Fig. 6a (tensile curves), Fig. 8f and 9f (fracture surfaces), Discussion section on gradient structures, nanotwins, and hydrogen diffusion.

## [Nanograin Boundaries (introduced by HPT)]

**Applicable Alloy System**: CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy, nanograined condition produced by HPT (6 GPa, 10 turns).

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not applicable (microstructural feature). Average nanograin size: 25 nm.

**Main Function**: Ultrahigh strength (yield strength 1850 MPa) in uncharged condition.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: 
- Numerous nanograins with average size 25 nm.
- Random misorientation angles, weak crystallographic texture.
- High density of grain boundaries.
- Nanograin boundaries enhance hydrogen diffusion from surface into bulk.

**Property Effect**: 
- Uncharged: yield strength 1850 MPa, low ductility.
- Hydrogen-charged: breaks in elastic region (no plastic yielding); hydrogen content 19.5 wt. ppm; intergranular cracking; HEDE mechanism.

**Potential Side Effects**: 
- Poor hydrogen embrittlement resistance due to enhanced hydrogen diffusion along nanograin boundaries.
- Catastrophic failure in elastic region after hydrogen charging.

**Original Evidence**: 
- Fig. 5 (TEM bright-field, dark-field, SAED, high-resolution images), Fig. 6 (tensile curves and TDS), Fig. 10 (fracture surfaces), Discussion section on hydrogen diffusion and grain boundaries.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Gradient-structured CrMnFeCoNi HEA** produced by SMAT under soft (40 μm, 30 s) or mild (60 μm, 60 s) conditions exhibits a good combination of high yield stress (500–700 MPa) and high ductility (15–33%) under hydrogen charging, compared with coarse-grained (~220 MPa, low yield) and nanograined (1850 MPa, but brittle under hydrogen) counterparts.
2. **Coarse-grained (homogenized) HEA** shows high ductility (65% elongation under hydrogen) but low yield strength (~220 MPa).
3. **Nanograined (HPT-processed) HEA** shows ultrahigh yield strength (1850 MPa) but poor hydrogen embrittlement resistance; after hydrogen charging, it breaks in the elastic region with intergranular cracking and HEDE mechanism; hydrogen content 19.5 wt. ppm.
4. **Hydrogen content increases with severity of deformation**: 3.4 wt. ppm (coarse-grained), 5.3 wt. ppm (soft SMAT), 6.1 wt. ppm (mild SMAT), 19.5 wt. ppm (HPT).
5. **Hydrogen diffusion is enhanced and hydrogen embrittlement resistance weakened with increasing intensity of deformation**; nanograin boundaries are particularly detrimental, while coherent twin boundaries are less damaging but can act as crack initiation/propagation sites.
6. **FCC HEAs with five principal elements exhibit improved resistance to hydrogen embrittlement** by increasing the threshold hydrogen content for HELP mechanism, due to suppressed dislocation motion in the multi-element solid solution.
7. **Gradient structures containing surface nanotwins** effectively control hydrogen content and hydrogen embrittlement, providing a pathway for extending the application range of HEAs in structural and hydrogen-related fields.
8. **Critical hydrogen content** for initiation/acceleration of HELP in this CrMnFeCoNi HEA is considered to be below approximately 6.1 wt. ppm (as measured in the mild SMAT sample).

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy system scope**: This paper specifically studies the CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy (Cantor alloy) with FCC structure. Findings may not be directly transferable to refractory high-temperature alloys (Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based), refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys, which typically have BCC matrices and different hydrogen behavior.
- **Strain rate sensitivity**: Only one initial strain rate of 5.6 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ was investigated. The paper notes that future studies at lower strain rates are needed to examine strain rate sensitivity effects on hydrogen embrittlement behavior of gradient-structured HEAs.
- **Hydrogen charging method**: Electrochemical charging used; will not represent gaseous hydrogen exposure conditions in all service environments.
- **Micro-pores**: Samples contain micro-pores from vacuum induction melting; although the paper states they do not significantly affect hydrogen embrittlement (based on 65% elongation of hydrogen-charged homogenized sample), their influence on other properties is not systematically quantified.
- **Oxidation resistance**: Not investigated in this paper.
- **Gradient structure optimization**: The paper states that future studies are required to optimize gradient nanostructures by considering threshold hydrogen content for different chemical compositions of HEAs.
- **Testing temperature**: Room temperature only (300 K); no high-temperature mechanical testing, creep, or oxidation testing is reported.
- **Grain-size range**: Coarse-grained (~30 μm), gradient-structured (refined/nanotwinned surface + coarse interior), nanograined (25 nm). No intermediate grain sizes are systematically studied.
