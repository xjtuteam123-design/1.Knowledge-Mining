# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Deformation**: The alloy was deformed by rolling to produce sheet. The rolling direction is a key reference for sample orientation (longitudinal, transversal, and 45° samples).
- **Initial state**: The alloy was studied in the "state of delivery" which corresponds to the deformed (rolled) condition, described as having a fibrous microstructure with equiaxial cells of 2–5 μm average diameter and high dislocation density inside them.
- **Annealing treatments**: The paper discusses annealing at various temperatures to study microstructural evolution:
  - Annealing at 1223 K: Considerable coarsening of grain due to disappearance of grain boundaries.
  - Annealing at 1423 K: Intensification of cell growth process; structural texture practically disappears; primary recrystallization is almost complete at 1523 K.
  - Annealing at 1523 K: Primary recrystallization practically completed; elongated grain structure in rolling direction still saved; grain size within limits from 4 to 10 μm (or 4 to [illegible] μm).

# Key Conclusions
1. Sheet molybdenum alloy CМ-10 in the deformed (rolled) state exhibits anisotropy of mechanical characteristics up to test temperature of 1273 K.
2. Maximum strength properties are reached on transversal samples; minimum strength properties are reached on samples cut at 45° to the rolling direction.
3. Maximum relative elongation corresponds to longitudinal samples.
4. Strength properties (ultimate strength and yield point) monotonically decrease with increasing test temperature throughout the entire tested range (298–1473 K).
5. Plastic characteristics decrease with increasing test temperature up to 1073–1273 K, then sharply increase, reaching 25–35% (or 32–37%) at 1473 K.
6. The deformation of the alloy is accompanied by dynamic return (500–800 K), polygonization (900–1300 K), and recrystallization (above 1300 K).
7. Annealing leads to grain coarsening and disappearance of cell structure; primary recrystallization is practically complete at 1523 K. Grain size increases from ~10 μm (at 1423 K) to ~800 μm (at 2223 K).
8. The mechanical anisotropy is attributed to structural texture (grain elongation in rolling direction, strong flattening along sheet width) and crystallographic texture ({001}<110>, {111}<112>).
