# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title**: Joining C/C composite in a few seconds using refractory high entropy alloy fillers via spark plasma sintering
- **Authors**: Xincheng Wang, Li Fu
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106398
- **Alloy system studied**: Refractory high entropy alloy fillers (ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa) used for joining C/C composites; resulting high entropy carbides (Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C and (Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C
- **Research objective**: To join C/C composites using single-phase ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa refractory high entropy alloy (RHEA) fillers via flash spark plasma sintering (SPS) within 16 seconds, and to investigate the resulting joint microstructure, room-temperature shear strength, and fracture behavior
- **Material category**: Refractory high-entropy alloy fillers; high entropy carbides formed in situ during joining

# Alloy System and Research Object

The research object is the joining of 2.5-dimensional C/C composites using two refractory high-entropy alloy fillers:
- **ZrHfNbTa** (equimolar refractory high entropy alloy, single BCC structure)
- **TiZrHfTa** (equimolar refractory high entropy alloy, single BCC structure)

During flash SPS joining, these RHEA fillers react with carbon from the C/C composite substrates to form high entropy carbides (HECs), serving as the joining interlayer. The paper investigates the phase transformation from RHEA (BCC) to HEC (FCC rock salt) and the resulting joint properties.

A pure Ti foil (100 μm thick) is used as a comparison group for shear strength testing.

# Alloy Composition

## Filler Alloys (as used)

**ZrHfNbTa filler:**
- Equimolar refractory high entropy alloy
- Foil thickness: 150–200 μm
- Single BCC crystal structure (as reported in the authors' previous work [18])
- Atomic radius of constituent elements: Zr (1.590 Å), Hf (1.564 Å), Nb (1.448 Å), Ta (1.430 Å)

**TiZrHfTa filler:**
- Equimolar refractory high entropy alloy
- Foil thickness: 150–200 μm
- Single BCC crystal structure (as reported in the authors' previous work [18])
- Atomic radius of constituent elements: Ti (1.429 Å), Zr (1.590 Å), Hf (1.564 Å), Ta (1.430 Å)

**Pure Ti filler (comparison group):**
- Pure Ti foil, 100 μm thick

## Resulting High Entropy Carbide Interlayers

**(Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C** (from ZrHfNbTa filler):
- Near-equimolar high entropy carbide
- FCC rock salt crystal structure
- Lattice parameter: 4.599 Å
- Predicted lattice parameter (Vegard's law): 4.563 Å

**(Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C** (from TiZrHfTa filler):
- Near-equimolar high entropy carbide
- FCC rock salt crystal structure
- Lattice parameter: 4.584 Å
- Predicted lattice parameter (Vegard's law): 4.527 Å

**Residual second phase in C/C-TiZrHfTa-C/C joint:**
- Ti–Ta rich solid solution phase with BCC structure
- Present in small quantities only in central regions of the brazed seam
- Attributed to incomplete reaction of TiZrHfTa filler with C/C composite substrates

## C/C Composite Substrate

- 2.5-dimensional C/C composite
- Density: 1.78 g/cm³
- Fabricated using needled felt and carbon fiber cloth

**Elements relevant to the study:** Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta, Ti, C (from substrate). No W, Mo, Cr, Al, Si, Re, B, Y, La, O, or N elements are mentioned in this study.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Joining Method: Flash Spark Plasma Sintering (FSPS)

- **Equipment**: SPS system (HPD 25/1, FCT systems, Germany)
- **Atmosphere**: Vacuum
- **Applied load**: 5 kN
- **Discharge treating time**: 16 seconds
- **Heating rate**: Over 100 °C/s (rapid heating)
- **Cooling rate**: Extremely rapid cooling
- **Final joining temperature**: Exceeding 2200 °C for ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa fillers

The flash joining process was controlled by setting the discharge treating time rather than precise temperature measurement, due to challenges in measuring the actual sample temperature precisely.

## Sample Assembly

- RHEA foil placed between two C/C samples as a sandwich structure
- Entire assembly wrapped with graphite felt for thermal insulation
- Substrate dimensions: 15 × 8 × 6 mm³
- Surfaces polished with SiC sandpaper and diamond suspension, ultrasonically cleaned in ethanol

## Temperature Details

- For RHEA fillers: Final joining temperature exceeded 2200 °C (greater than melting points of ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa, approximately 2000 °C)
- For pure Ti filler: Maximum temperature reached approximately 2170 °C (significantly higher than Ti melting point of 1678 °C)

No homogenization, rolling, forging, annealing, or aging treatments were performed beyond the flash SPS joining process.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Joint Morphology

- Both joints showed similar morphology with no observable defects such as voids or cracks at the joint interfaces
- Brazed seam thickness: 7–15 μm (significant shrinkage from original 150–200 μm foil thickness due to melt infiltration into C/C pores)
- Melted fillers infiltrated into pores in bundles and among pyro-carbon of the C/C substrate

## Phase Constitution

### C/C-ZrHfNbTa-C/C Joint
- **Matrix/Joining phase**: Single near-equimolar (Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C high entropy carbide
- **Crystal structure**: FCC rock salt
- **Lattice parameter**: 4.599 Å
- No second phase observed

### C/C-TiZrHfTa-C/C Joint
- **Main phase**: (Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C high entropy carbide
- **Crystal structure**: FCC rock salt
- **Lattice parameter**: 4.584 Å
- **Second phase**: Ti–Ta rich solid solution with BCC structure, present in small quantities in only a few central regions of the brazed seam
- STEM-EDS confirmed the second phase was abundant in Ti–Ta
- High-resolution STEM HAADF and FFT confirmed BCC structure of the second phase
- No significant atomic partition at the phase boundary

## Elemental Segregation and Composition Uniformity

- Zr and Hf have large negative energy of formation for their monocarbides
- This results in accumulation and formation of a Zr–Hf rich carbide at the C/C-filler interfaces during flash SPS joining
- Due to the short process time (16 s), the formed HEC interlayers showed slightly uneven composition, though this was challenging to detect in EDS mapping
- XRD test positions were near the carbide-C/C interface where more Zr and Hf content existed

## Lattice Parameter Analysis

- Both (Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C and (Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C XRD-measured lattice parameters (4.599 Å and 4.584 Å) were slightly higher than Vegard's law predictions (4.563 Å and 4.527 Å)
- Attributed to Zr and Hf having larger atomic radii (Zr: 1.590 Å, Hf: 1.564 Å) compared to Ti (1.429 Å), Nb (1.448 Å), and Ta (1.430 Å)
- The test regions contained more Zr and Hf

## Grain Size and Precipitate Size

Not explicitly stated in the original paper. The STEM analysis confirmed homogeneous solid solution phases but did not report grain size or precipitate size values.

# Mechanical Properties

## Room-Temperature Shear Strength

| Joint Type | Shear Strength |
|------------|----------------|
| C/C-ZrHfNbTa-C/C | 16.0 ± 2.4 MPa |
| C/C-TiZrHfTa-C/C | 14.1 ± 2.0 MPa |
| C/C-Ti-C/C (pure Ti foil comparison) | 19.8 ± 3.4 MPa |

- Test method: Single lap offset shear test
- Testing machine: Universal testing machine (INSTRON 3882)
- Testing speed: 0.5 mm/min
- Testing temperature: Room temperature
- Number of samples: 4 per condition

## Fracture Behavior

- **Fracture mode**: Predominantly adhesive failure at the C/C-filler interface, with partial cohesive failure within the C/C composite adjacent to the C/C-HEC interface
- Most HEC reaction interlayers and C/C composites remained intact after fracture
- Fractured carbon fibers were observed on HEC interlayer surfaces in both perpendicular and parallel directions to the joining interfaces
- Adhesive failure attributed to high residual stress from CTE mismatch between C/C composite and formed HECs due to extremely rapid cooling rate during flash SPS
- The formed HEC showed typical cleavage fracture characteristics

## High-Temperature Performance (Projected)

The paper notes that joints with RHEA fillers may demonstrate superior high-temperature strength compared to pure Ti filler joints because:
- HECs have higher melting points than TiC (TiC melting point: 3140 °C)
- HECs exhibit superior mechanical properties at high temperature compared to most binary or individual carbides

No specific high-temperature mechanical property values were measured in this study.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Zr + Hf (Combined)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Refractory high entropy alloys (ZrHfNbTa, TiZrHfTa) used for joining C/C composites, forming high entropy carbides
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Equimolar at 25 at.% each in ZrHfNbTa; equimolar at 25 at.% each in TiZrHfTa (for the 4-element equimolar compositions)
- **Main Function**: Preferential carbide formation at the C/C-filler interface; driving force for HEC formation during flash SPS joining
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Zr and Hf possess large negative energy of formation for their monocarbides, causing their segregation toward the filler-C/C interface to form Zr–Hf rich carbide at the beginning of the joining process. Zr and Hf have the largest atomic radii among the constituent elements (Zr: 1.590 Å, Hf: 1.564 Å), contributing to lattice parameter expansion of the resulting HEC.
- **Property Effect**: Facilitate complete transformation of the ZrHfNbTa filler to single-phase carbide with no residual second phase. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values relating Zr+Hf content to mechanical property changes beyond the overall joint shear strengths (ZrHfNbTa joint: 16.0 ± 2.4 MPa; TiZrHfTa joint: 14.1 ± 2.0 MPa).
- **Potential Side Effects**: Zr–Hf segregation during joining causes chemical inhomogeneity in the HEC interlayer due to insufficient homogenization time (16 s); residual Zr–Hf rich carbide at C/C-filler interfaces
- **Original Evidence**: “Zr and Hf have a large negative energy of the formation of their monocarbides, which therefore, results in the accumulation and formation of a Zr–Hf rich carbide at the C/C-filler interfaces during flash SPS joining.” (Section 3, Fig. 2 discussion)

## Ti

- **Applicable Alloy System**: TiZrHfTa refractory high entropy alloy filler for C/C composite joining; resulting (Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C high entropy carbide
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Equimolar at ~25 at.% in TiZrHfTa
- **Main Function**: Constituent of RHEA filler; contributes to HEC formation; its smaller atomic radius affects carbon diffusion in the resulting carbide
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Ti has the smallest atomic radius (1.429 Å) among Ti, Zr, Hf, Nb, and Ta. This results in a smaller lattice parameter of the (Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C carbide near the central area compared to (Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C, producing smaller tetrahedral sites. This affects the carbon self-diffusion process—where C atoms occupy empty tetrahedral sites before migrating into nearest-neighbor C-vacancies—making C diffusion into the central region of TiZrHfTa filler more difficult than into ZrHfNbTa filler.
- **Property Effect**: Introduction of Ti (replacing Nb) leads to incomplete carbonization of the TiZrHfTa filler during 16 s flash SPS, leaving a small amount of Ti–Ta rich BCC second phase in the joint center. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Ti-dependent property changes beyond the joint shear strength (TiZrHfTa joint: 14.1 ± 2.0 MPa vs ZrHfNbTa joint: 16.0 ± 2.4 MPa).
- **Potential Side Effects**: Residual unreacted Ti–Ta rich alloy phase in the joint center due to Ti's smaller atomic radius reducing carbon diffusion rate; slightly lower room-temperature shear strength compared to ZrHfNbTa filler
- **Original Evidence**: “the atomic radius of Ti (1.429 Å) is the smallest among these five elements, the lattice parameter of the carbide formed near the central area of the C/C-TiZrHfTa-C/C joint was smaller than that near the central area of the C/C-ZrHfNbTa-C/C joint, resulting in a smaller tetrahedral site... C diffused more readily into the central region of the ZrHfNbTa filler, avoiding the occurrence of a second phase.” (Section 3, Figs. 1–3 discussion)

## Ta

- **Applicable Alloy System**: ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa refractory high entropy alloy fillers for C/C composite joining
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Equimolar at ~25 at.% in both fillers
- **Main Function**: Constituent of RHEA filler; participates in HEC formation; forms residual Ti–Ta rich phase in TiZrHfTa system
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: In the TiZrHfTa system, Ta (along with Ti) remains in the unreacted Ti–Ta rich BCC solid solution phase in the joint center due to lower carbon affinity compared to Zr and Hf. STEM analysis confirmed uniform atomic dispersion in this second phase, indicating homogeneous solid solution formation.
- **Property Effect**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Ta-dependent property changes. The presence of residual Ti–Ta rich phase did not significantly alter joint shear strength (TiZrHfTa joint: 14.1 ± 2.0 MPa vs ZrHfNbTa joint: 16.0 ± 2.4 MPa, considered comparable).
- **Potential Side Effects**: In TiZrHfTa system, Ta segregates with Ti into unreacted second phase; this phase's minute quantity made its influence on strength and fracture behavior difficult to analyze
- **Original Evidence**: “the second phase was abundant in Ti–Ta... the second phase had a bcc structure... atoms were uniformly dispersed with no significant contrast variation, indicating the formation of a homogeneous solid solution.” (Section 3, Fig. 3 discussion)

## Nb

- **Applicable Alloy System**: ZrHfNbTa refractory high entropy alloy filler for C/C composite joining
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Equimolar at ~25 at.% in ZrHfNbTa
- **Main Function**: Constituent of RHEA filler; participates in HEC formation; contributes to complete carbonization without residual second phase
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Nb has a larger atomic radius (1.448 Å) than Ti (1.429 Å), which contributes to larger tetrahedral sites in the resulting (Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C carbide. This facilitates carbon self-diffusion into the central region of the ZrHfNbTa filler during flash SPS joining, enabling complete transformation to single-phase HEC without residual unreacted alloy.
- **Property Effect**: Complete transformation to single-phase (Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C interlayer without second phase; joint shear strength of 16.0 ± 2.4 MPa. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Nb-dependent property changes beyond this.
- **Potential Side Effects**: None identified in this paper
- **Original Evidence**: “no second phase was found in the C/C-ZrHfNbTa-C/C joint... C diffused more readily into the central region of the ZrHfNbTa filler, avoiding the occurrence of a second phase.” (Section 3, Fig. 1–3 discussion)

## Carbon (C) – from C/C Substrate

- **Applicable Alloy System**: C/C composite joining with ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa RHEA fillers
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: C/C composite substrate provides carbon through diffusion into the molten RHEA filler during flash SPS joining
- **Main Function**: Converts RHEA fillers from BCC structure to high entropy carbides (HECs) with FCC rock salt structure, forming the joining interlayer
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Carbon diffuses into the molten RHEA filler during flash SPS, forming carbides. The carbon self-diffusion process is a two-step mechanism: C atoms occupy empty tetrahedral sites before migrating into nearest-neighbor C-vacancies. The rate of C diffusion is influenced by the lattice parameter of the formed carbide, which depends on the atomic radii of the metallic constituents.
- **Property Effect**: Full or partial transformation to HEC interlayer. Complete transformation in ZrHfNbTa system (single-phase (Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C), partial transformation in TiZrHfTa system ((Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C + small residual Ti–Ta rich phase). The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for carbon-dependent property changes.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Incomplete carbonization can leave residual unreacted alloy second phase in the joint center
- **Original Evidence**: “the C self-diffusion rate is closely related to the formation of joints... The C self-diffusion process is thought to be a two-step process, where the C atom can occupy an empty tetrahedral site before migrating into a nearest neighbor C-vacancy.” (Section 3)

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## High Entropy Carbide Formation (Phase Transformation Joining)

- **Related Elements**: Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta, Ti, C
- **Typical Alloys**: ZrHfNbTa, TiZrHfTa RHEA fillers converting to (Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C and (Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C HECs
- **Key Compositions**: Equimolar RHEA fillers (e.g., 25 at.% each of Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta; or 25 at.% each of Ti, Zr, Hf, Ta)
- **Microstructural Changes**: BCC RHEA transforms to FCC rock salt HEC via reaction with carbon from C/C substrate during flash SPS at >2200 °C in 16 seconds; brazed seam shrinks from 150–200 μm foil thickness to 7–15 μm due to melt infiltration into C/C pores
- **Property Improvements**: High entropy carbide interlayer with high melting point (formed from RHEA with melting point ~2000 °C, producing carbide with superior high-temperature properties compared to binary or individual carbides); room-temperature joint shear strengths of 16.0 ± 2.4 MPa (ZrHfNbTa) and 14.1 ± 2.0 MPa (TiZrHfTa)
- **Risks and Limitations**: 
  - Insufficient homogenization time (16 s) leads to slightly uneven composition in HEC interlayer
  - Zr and Hf segregation to interfaces creates composition gradient
  - In TiZrHfTa system, incomplete carbonization leaves residual Ti–Ta rich BCC second phase
  - High residual stress from CTE mismatch between C/C and HEC, leading to predominantly adhesive failure mode
  - Room-temperature shear strength lower than that achieved with pure Ti filler (19.8 ± 3.4 MPa)

## Solid-Solution Strengthening (in RHEA Fillers)

- **Related Elements**: Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta, Ti (multi-principal element solid solution)
- **Typical Alloys**: ZrHfNbTa, TiZrHfTa refractory high entropy alloys
- **Key Compositions**: Equimolar compositions
- **Microstructural Changes**: Single BCC crystal structure, homogeneous solid solution with uniform atomic dispersion (confirmed by STEM HAADF analysis showing no significant contrast variation)
- **Property Improvements**: High structural stability at elevated temperatures; unique combination of high tensile strength and plasticity; suitable for high-temperature joining applications. The original paper does not provide specific mechanical property values for the RHEA fillers themselves.
- **Risks and Limitations**: 
  - RHEA fillers transform to HECs during joining, so the BCC solid solution is not present in the final joint
  - The paper does not measure mechanical properties of the RHEA fillers separately

## Flash SPS Rapid Joining Mechanism

- **Related Elements**: Not element-specific (process-based mechanism)
- **Typical Alloys**: Applicable to joining of high-temperature resistant materials using RHEA fillers
- **Key Compositions**: Any RHEA filler composition
- **Microstructural Changes**: Ultra-high heating rate (>100 °C/s), extremely rapid cooling; melted fillers infiltrate into C/C pores and among pyro-carbon; significant seam shrinkage (150–200 μm → 7–15 μm)
- **Property Improvements**: Achieves joining in 16 seconds compared to conventional brazing or diffusion bonding which require significantly longer processing times; electric field accelerates self-diffusion and ion migration through joining interface; novel, economical, and convenient joining method
- **Risks and Limitations**: 
  - Extremely rapid cooling creates high residual stress from CTE mismatch at joining interface
  - 16-second process time insufficient for complete homogenization of formed HEC
  - Incomplete carbonization possible for certain RHEA compositions (e.g., TiZrHfTa)
  - Lower room-temperature shear strength compared to pure Ti filler joints

# Key Conclusions

1. **Successful flash joining**: C/C composites were successfully joined in 16 seconds using ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa refractory high entropy alloy fillers via flash SPS with rapid heating (>100 °C/s) and cooling rates. The final joining temperature exceeded 2200 °C, above the melting points of the RHEA fillers (~2000 °C).

2. **Phase transformation**: The ZrHfNbTa filler completely transformed into a single-phase (Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C high entropy carbide with FCC rock salt structure. The TiZrHfTa filler transformed primarily into (Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C HEC, but with a very small amount of Ti–Ta rich BCC second phase remaining in the central area of the brazed seam due to incomplete carbonization.

3. **Lattice parameter expansion**: XRD-measured lattice parameters (4.599 Å for (Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta)C; 4.584 Å for (Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C) were slightly higher than Vegard's law predictions (4.563 Å and 4.527 Å respectively), attributed to Zr and Hf enrichment near the C/C-filler interfaces.

4. **Carbon diffusion mechanism**: The smaller atomic radius of Ti (1.429 Å) compared to Nb (1.448 Å) reduces the tetrahedral site size in the formed carbide, impeding carbon self-diffusion into the central region of TiZrHfTa filler and resulting in residual unreacted Ti–Ta rich phase.

5. **Shear strength**: Room-temperature shear strengths were comparable for both RHEA fillers: 16.0 ± 2.4 MPa (ZrHfNbTa) and 14.1 ± 2.0 MPa (TiZrHfTa). Both were slightly lower than pure Ti filler joints (19.8 ± 3.4 MPa).

6. **Fracture mode**: Fracture predominantly occurred at the C/C-filler interface via adhesive failure mode, driven by high residual stress from CTE mismatch during rapid cooling. Partial cohesive failure within the C/C composite was also observed.

7. **High-temperature potential**: Despite lower room-temperature strength than pure Ti filler, RHEA-based joints are expected to outperform at high temperatures due to higher melting points and superior high-temperature mechanical properties of HECs compared to TiC and most binary/individual carbides.

8. **Element segregation**: Zr and Hf, having large negative formation energies for monocarbides, segregate to the C/C-filler interface to form Zr–Hf rich carbide initially; the 16-second process is insufficient for full homogenization of the HEC interlayer.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Limited mechanical property data**: The study only measures room-temperature shear strength; no elevated-temperature mechanical testing is performed, despite claims of superior high-temperature performance potential.

2. **No oxidation resistance data**: The paper does not address the oxidation resistance or long-term service stability of the HEC-containing joints at elevated temperatures.

3. **Single joining condition**: Only one discharge treating time (16 s) is investigated; the influence of varying SPS parameters (time, pressure, heating rate) on joint quality is not explored.

4. **Lower room-temperature strength**: The RHEA-based joints exhibit slightly lower room-temperature shear strength (14.1–16.0 MPa) compared to pure Ti filler joints (19.8 MPa), indicating that the RHEA approach may be most advantageous for high-temperature applications rather than room-temperature ones.

5. **Second phase in TiZrHfTa system**: The residual Ti–Ta rich second phase in TiZrHfTa joints, while present in minute quantities, indicates incomplete reaction; its influence on long-term mechanical performance and high-temperature stability is not fully characterized.

6. **Residual stress issues**: The extremely rapid cooling inherent to flash SPS creates high residual stresses from CTE mismatch, leading to predominantly adhesive failure at the joint interface. This limits the current joint strength and remains an unresolved issue.

7. **Limited scope of alloy systems**: Only two equimolar RHEA compositions (ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa) are tested; the influence of non-equimolar compositions or additional alloying elements on joining behavior is not explored.

8. **No microstructural quantification**: Grain size, precipitate size, and microstructural feature dimensions of the HEC interlayers are not quantified.

9. **Element-function mechanisms are inferred**: The role of carbon diffusion rate based on atomic radius and tetrahedral site size is presented as an explanation but is not directly verified by diffusion experiments or atomistic simulations.

13. **Elastic modulus**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
