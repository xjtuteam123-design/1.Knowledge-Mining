# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** A lightweight Al0.8Nb0.5Ti2V2Zr0.5 refractory high entropy alloy with high specific yield strength
- **Authors:** Wentao Jiang, Xiaohong Wang, Siying Li, Tengfei Ma, Ye Wang, Dongdong Zhu
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.matlet.2022.133144
- **Alloy system studied:** Al-Nb-Ti-V-Zr refractory high entropy alloy (RHEA)
- **Material category:** Lightweight refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA)
- **Research objective:** To develop a novel low-density AlNbTiVZr refractory high-entropy alloy with high specific yield strength through composition optimization (reducing Al and Nb content, increasing Ti and V content) and to characterize its microstructure and compressive properties at room temperature and elevated temperatures.


# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Material category:** Lightweight refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA)
- **Alloy system:** Al-Nb-Ti-V-Zr quinary system
- **Alloy designation:** Al0.8Nb0.5Ti2V2Zr0.5 (non-equiatomic RHEA)
- **Research context:** The paper addresses the need to reduce the density of RHEAs by replacing high-density elements (Ta, W, Hf) with lighter elements (Al, Ti, V, Zr). The alloy composition is presented as a lightweight variant of previously reported AlNbTiVZr and CrNbTiVZr alloys.
- **Comparison references:** The paper compares specific yield strength against other RHEAs and Inconel 718 superalloy.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Matrix Phase
- **Structure:** BCC solid solution
- **Lattice parameter:** 0.322 nm (calculated from the strongest diffraction peak in the [110] crystal orientation)
- **XRD observation:** The XRD pattern shows only a single BCC phase.

## Second Phase
- **Type:** C14-Laves phase (hexagonal structure)
- **Detection:** Not detectable by XRD due to very low volume fraction; identified by SEM-BSE and TEM/SAED.
- **SAED confirmation:** Hexagonal structure with [−24−23] zone axis for the second phase; BCC structure with [−111] zone axis for the matrix.
- **Morphology:** The C14-Laves phase forms a relatively thick layer (of the order of several μm) that separates BCC matrix grains, creating a "grain boundary (GB) wetting by a second solid phase" phenomenon. Some grain boundaries are completely wetted, others are partially wetted.

## Elemental Partitioning (EDS mapping)
- **BCC matrix phase:** Enriched in Ti and V
- **C14-Laves phase:** Enriched in Al and Zr
- **Nb:** Evenly distributed in both phases

## Grain Size
- Not explicitly stated in the original paper. No quantitative grain size measurement is provided.

## Interface Characteristics
- **BCC/C14-Laves interface:** Semi-coherent interface, as revealed by HRTEM (interplanar distances between adjacent grains are quite different).
- **Lattice distortion:** IFFT analysis of the BCC matrix reveals a high density of lattice misfit, indicating severe lattice distortion due to the presence of a large number of Ti and V solute atoms.

## Strengthening Mechanisms Discussed in the Paper
The paper explicitly discusses three strengthening contributions (Fig. 2):
1. **Solid-solution strengthening:** Large quantities of Ti and V in the BCC matrix cause severe lattice distortion.
2. **Second-phase strengthening:** Hard and brittle C14-Laves phase (Al- and Zr-rich) distributed as a network at grain boundaries.
3. **Chemical bonding effect:** Al forms strong chemical bonds with the other four elements, all having negative mixing enthalpies with Al (Fig. 2d).
4. **Grain-boundary stabilization:** The incomplete GB wetting by C14-Laves phase stabilizes the grain structure and prevents grain growth at high temperatures.


# Mechanical Properties

## Density
- **Measured value:** 5.35 g/cm³
- **Method:** Archimedes method, 5 measurements averaged

## Hardness
- **Measured value:** 5.86 GPa
- **Method:** Ultra-micro dynamic hardness tester, 1000 mN load, 20 s dwell

## Compressive Properties (Strain rate 1 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹)

| Temperature | Yield Strength (σ_ys) | Peak Stress (σ_p) | Fracture Strain (ε) |
|-------------|----------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| 298 K | 1418 ± 35 MPa | 1617 MPa | 17.9% |
| 873 K | 931 ± 43 MPa | 1311 MPa | 21.8% |
| 1073 K | 760 ± 32 MPa | 824 MPa | >50% (no fracture) |

- **At 298 K:** The alloy fractures after reaching peak stress. Fracture strain ≈ 18%.
- **At 873 K:** Fracture strain ≈ 21.8%.
- **At 1073 K:** The alloy does not fracture; strain exceeds 50%.

## Specific Yield Strength (SYS = Yield Strength / Density)

| Temperature | SYS |
|-------------|-----|
| 298 K | 302 kPa·m³/kg |
| 873 K | 245 kPa·m³/kg |
| 1073 K | 154 kPa·m³/kg |

- The SYS at 298 K is approximately twice that of Inconel 718.
- The SYS at all tested temperatures is significantly higher than other reported RHEAs and Inconel 718.
- The paper notes that among referenced RHEAs, CrNbTiVZr has a density of 6.57 g/cm³ with σ_ys = 615 MPa at 1073 K; AlNbTiVZr has a density of 5.79 g/cm³ with σ_ys = 550 MPa at 1073 K.

## Element or Element Combination: Ti (Titanium)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Lightweight Al-Nb-Ti-V-Zr refractory high-entropy alloys.

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
Ti molar ratio = 2 (equivalent to ~34.5 at.% in the Al0.8Nb0.5Ti2V2Zr0.5 alloy).

**[Main Function]**
- Reduces density of the alloy (contributes to lightweight design).
- Major constituent of the BCC solid solution matrix phase.

- The alloy achieves σ_ys = 1418 MPa at 298 K, 931 MPa at 873 K, and 760 MPa at 1073 K with Ti as the dominant matrix constituent (along with V).
- The alloy density is reduced to 5.35 g/cm³.
- The original paper does not provide specific numerical values isolating the Ti contribution.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- Potential contribution to lattice distortion severe enough to reduce ductility (inferred from the general RHEA context, but not explicitly stated for Ti alone).

**[Original Evidence]**
- Section 3, Fig. 1h: "A density of lattice misfit is observed in the alloy, indicating that the presence of a large number of Ti and V solute atoms in the matrix will lead to serious lattice distortion."
- Fig. 1i EDS mapping: "BCC matrix phase enriched in Ti and V."

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards


## Mechanism: Solid-Solution Strengthening

**Related Elements**
Ti, V (primary solid-solution strengtheners in this alloy).

**Typical Alloys**
Al0.8Nb0.5Ti2V2Zr0.5 (present paper); other BCC RHEAs.

**Key Compositions**
Ti molar ratio = 2 (~34.5 at.%), V molar ratio = 2 (~34.5 at.%), combined ~69 at.%.

**Microstructural Changes**
- High concentration of Ti and V solute atoms in the BCC matrix phase.
- Severe lattice distortion, as evidenced by a high density of lattice misfit in IFFT analysis (Fig. 1h).
- High volume fraction of the BCC solid solution matrix.

**Property Improvements**
- High yield strength: 1418 MPa (298 K), 931 MPa (873 K), 760 MPa (1073 K).
- Maintained strength at elevated temperatures (760 MPa at 1073 K).

**Risks and Limitations**
- Severe lattice distortion may contribute to limited compressive plasticity at 298 K (ε_f = 17.9%).
- The alloy fractures at room temperature, consistent with typical BCC RHEA brittleness.
- Not explicitly quantified as a separate strengthening increment.


## Mechanism: Second-Phase Strengthening (C14-Laves Phase)

**Related Elements**
Al, Zr (Laves-phase-forming elements).

**Typical Alloys**
Al0.8Nb0.5Ti2V2Zr0.5 (present paper).

**Key Compositions**
Al molar ratio = 0.8, Zr molar ratio = 0.5.

**Microstructural Changes**
- C14-Laves phase (hexagonal structure) forms at BCC grain boundaries as a thick network layer.
- GB wetting phenomenon: Some GBs completely wetted, others partially wetted.
- Semi-coherent interface between BCC matrix and C14-Laves phase.
- Laves phase is enriched in Al and Zr.

**Property Improvements**
- Network Laves strengthens the alloy by constraining BCC matrix deformation.
- Incomplete GB wetting may improve high-temperature grain structure stability and prevent grain growth (based on refs. [11–13]).
- Contributes to high specific yield strength retention at high temperatures.

**Risks and Limitations**
- C14-Laves is described as "hard and brittle."
- Thick GB layers (μm-scale) may reduce grain-to-grain plastic flow, contributing to limited room-temperature plasticity (17.9% fracture strain).
- Full GB wetting by a brittle intermetallic may facilitate grain boundary embrittlement, though not explicitly discussed in the original paper.


## Mechanism: Chemical Bonding / Atomic Interaction Enhancement

**Related Elements**
Al (with Nb, Ti, V, Zr).

**Typical Alloys**
Al0.8Nb0.5Ti2V2Zr0.5 (present paper).

**Key Compositions**
Al molar ratio = 0.8.

**Microstructural Changes**
- Al forms strong chemical bonds with all four other elements (Nb, Ti, V, Zr).
- Negative binary mixing enthalpies of Al with each element (Fig. 2d).

**Property Improvements**
- Enhances the intrinsic strength of the alloy.

**Risks and Limitations**
- The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the strengthening increment from this mechanism.
- Strong chemical bonding may also reduce atomic mobility, affecting plastic deformation ability.


## Mechanism: Grain-Boundary Stabilization (via Incomplete GB Wetting)

**Related Elements**
Al, Zr (C14-Laves forming elements).

**Typical Alloys**
Al0.8Nb0.5Ti2V2Zr0.5 (present paper).

**Key Compositions**
Al molar ratio = 0.8, Zr molar ratio = 0.5.

**Microstructural Changes**
- C14-Laves phase wets grain boundaries, with some boundaries completely wetted and some partially wetted.
- The incomplete GB wetting layers stabilize grain structure at high temperatures.

**Property Improvements**
- Prevents grain growth at high temperatures (cited from refs. [11–13]).
- May contribute to retention of high-temperature strength (760 MPa at 1073 K).

**Risks and Limitations**
- The paper presents this as a potential benefit based on literature rather than direct experimental verification in this alloy.
- No quantitative grain growth measurements are provided.


# Key Conclusions

1. A novel lightweight refractory high-entropy alloy, Al0.8Nb0.5Ti2V2Zr0.5, was successfully prepared by vacuum arc melting.

2. **Density:** 5.35 g/cm³, among the lowest reported for RHEAs.

3. **Hardness:** 5.86 GPa.

4. **Microstructure:** Dual-phase structure consisting of:
   - BCC solid solution matrix (enriched in Ti and V)
   - Minor C14-Laves phase (enriched in Al and Zr) distributed as a network at BCC grain boundaries, exhibiting GB wetting behavior

5. **Compressive properties:**
   | Temperature | Yield Strength | Peak Stress | Fracture Strain |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 298 K | 1418 ± 35 MPa | 1617 MPa | 17.9% |
   | 873 K | 931 ± 43 MPa | 1311 MPa | 21.8% |
   | 1073 K | 760 ± 32 MPa | 824 MPa | >50% (no fracture) |

6. **Specific yield strength:**
   | Temperature | SYS |
   |---|---|
   | 298 K | 302 kPa·m³/kg |
   | 873 K | 245 kPa·m³/kg |
   | 1073 K | 154 kPa·m³/kg |
   The specific yield strengths at all tested temperatures are significantly higher than those of other RHEAs and Inconel 718. At 298 K, the SYS is nearly twice that of Inconel 718.

7. **Strengthening mechanisms identified by the authors:**
   - Solid-solution strengthening from Ti and V in the BCC matrix (severe lattice distortion)
   - Second-phase strengthening from the hard C14-Laves phase at grain boundaries
   - Strong chemical bonding due to negative mixing enthalpy of Al with all other elements

8. **Comparison with similar alloy compositions:**
   - AlNbTiVZr (density 5.79 g/cm³): σ_ys = 550 MPa at 1073 K
   - CrNbTiVZr (density 6.57 g/cm³): σ_ys = 615 MPa at 1073 K
   - Al0.8Nb0.5Ti2V2Zr0.5 (density 5.35 g/cm³): σ_ys = 760 MPa at 1073 K
   The present alloy achieves better high-temperature strength with lower density.


# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Only one alloy composition studied:** The paper reports a single composition (Al0.8Nb0.5Ti2V2Zr0.5) without systematic composition variation to establish optimal element ratios.

- **As-cast condition only:** No post-casting heat treatment (e.g., homogenization, annealing) was performed. The effects of heat treatment on microstructure and properties are not addressed.

- **Limited compositional characterization:** The paper does not provide exact at.% or wt.% values for the alloy; only molar ratio notation is given. Impurity levels (C, O, N, H) are not reported.

- **No tensile testing:** Only compression testing was performed. Tensile properties are not reported. RHEAs typically have very limited tensile ductility, but this is not addressed.

- **No oxidation resistance testing:** Neither oxidation weight gain nor oxide layer characterization was performed. Service temperature limits regarding oxidation are not discussed.

- **No creep testing:** No creep or stress-rupture data are provided, although creep resistance is critical for high-temperature applications.

- **No quantitative grain size measurement:** Grain size is not reported.

- **No fracture toughness measurement.**

- **Limited high-temperature range:** The maximum compression test temperature is 1073 K (800 °C). Behavior above this temperature (e.g., 1200 °C, 1400 °C, 1600 °C) is not evaluated.

- **Mechanisms partially supported by literature:** The benefit of incomplete GB wetting for high-temperature grain structure stabilization is cited from literature (refs. [11–13]) and applied by analogy; it is not directly verified experimentally in this alloy.

- **Trade-off between strength and ductility not quantitatively analyzed:** Although the alloy has high strength, the role of the brittle C14-Laves phase in limiting room-temperature plasticity is acknowledged qualitatively ("hard and brittle") but not quantified.

- **No microstructural evolution at high temperature:** The microstructure after high-temperature compression testing is not examined.

- **Applications suggested by the authors:** The paper broadly suggests potential use in cutting tools, abrasives, turbine blades, welding materials, and high-temperature furnace refractory materials, but these suggested applications are not experimentally validated.
