# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Strong and ductile niobium-based refractory alloy via deformable zirconia nanoparticles
- **Authors**: Jianan Chen, Chang Liu, Yifan Wang, Wangwang Ding, Qiying Tao, Gang Chen, Wei Cai, Mingli Qin, Xuanhui Qu
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2024 (Available online 25 October 2023)
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106451
- **Alloy system studied**: Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr (Nb521) niobium-based refractory alloy
- **Research objective**: To design and fabricate a Nb521 alloy with semi-coherent nanoscale ZrO₂ precipitates via laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) followed by heat treatment, achieving superior strength–ductility synergy through deformable oxide precipitates that exhibit transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) and twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP).
- **Material category**: Additively manufactured refractory alloy; Nb521 (Nb-W-Mo-Zr) niobium-based alloy; oxide dispersion-strengthened (ODS-like) refractory alloy with in situ ZrO₂ precipitates

- **Alloy designation**: Nb521
- **Material category**: Niobium-based refractory alloy fabricated by laser powder bed fusion (LPBF)

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Powder preparation
- Raw feedstock: hydride-dehydride (HDH) Nb521 powders modified by jet milling
- Powder morphology: quasi-spherical
- Powder flowability: 38.6 ± 0.6 s/50g
- Average particle size (D₅₀): 39.1 μm

## Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF)
- Equipment: SLM Solutions 125HL (Germany), equipped with a 400 W IPG fiber laser
- Substrate preheating: 200 °C in flowing argon atmosphere
- Laser power: 210 W
- Scanning speed: 800 mm/s
- Layer thickness: 30 μm
- Hatch distance: 0.10 mm
- Scanning strategy: stripe of zigzag pattern, stripe length 10 mm, 67° rotation after each layer
- Relative density achieved: 99.2 ± 0.2%

## Heat treatment
- Atmosphere: vacuum furnace, vacuum degree ~1 × 10⁻³ Pa
- Temperature: 1400 °C
- Duration: 1 h
- Purpose: relieve internal stress; promote ZrO₂ precipitate growth via Ostwald ripening and tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation upon cooling
- Specimen designation: heat-treated (HTed), also referred to as “as-fabricated” specimens

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Matrix
- Body-centered cubic (BCC) Nb matrix, confirmed by XRD for both LPBFed and HTed specimens.

## Precipitates
- **Type**: ZrO₂ (zirconia), in situ synthesized due to reaction between oxygen and zirconium
- **Distribution**: Homogeneously dispersed both intragranularly (within grains) and intergranularly (at grain boundaries)
- **Intergranular ZrO₂ precipitates**: 
  - Mean particle size: ~237 nm
  - Contain both monoclinic (m-ZrO₂) and tetragonal (t-ZrO₂) phases; ~36.7% t-ZrO₂ retained
  - Contain stacking faults (SFs) and nanotwins; twin types observed: (100) twinning plane, (002)[100] twin variant
- **Intragranular ZrO₂ precipitates**: 
  - Mean particle size: ~60 nm
  - Single monoclinic phase only
  - Orientation relationship with Nb matrix: (002)ₘ // (101)Nb; [100]ₘ within [111]Nb orientation
  - Semi-coherent interface with lattice mismatch estimated as 11.3% (based on d(101)Nb = 0.234 nm and d(002)m-ZrO₂ = 0.262 nm)
- **Crystal structures**:
  - m-ZrO₂: monoclinic, space group P121/c1; a = 0.515 nm, b = 0.521 nm, c = 0.531 nm, β = 99.3°
  - t-ZrO₂: tetragonal, space group P42/n; a = b = 0.364 nm, c = 0.527 nm

## Grain structure
- Average grain size: 17.8 ± 18.1 μm (large deviation)
- Fine equiaxed grains (<10 μm) at melt pool boundaries
- Large columnar grains in melt pool centers aligned with heat flux direction
- Fraction of low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs, 2–10°): ~40.6%
- Grain orientations: random, weak texture

## Dislocations
- LPBFed material exhibits high-density dislocations.
- After heat treatment, residual stress is relieved.

## Strengthening mechanisms discussed in the paper
- Solid-solution strengthening: not explicitly discussed as dominant; implied through base alloy chemistry
- Precipitation/dispersion strengthening: ZrO₂ precipitates pin dislocations
- Orowan bowing mechanism: dislocations pile up around ZrO₂ precipitates and interact via Orowan mechanism
- Grain-boundary strengthening / grain refinement: ultrafine grains from AM contribute to strength
- Transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP): t-ZrO₂ → m-ZrO₂ transformation during deformation
- Twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP): (100)[001] deformation twinning in ZrO₂ precipitates
- Dislocation-precipitate skeleton (DPS) architecture: dynamic Hall–Petch effect
- Oxide dispersion strengthening: high density of homogeneously dispersed ZrO₂ precipitates

# Mechanical Properties

## Room-temperature tensile properties (testing conditions: strain rate 6.7 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹, temperature ~20 °C)
- **Ultimate tensile strength (UTS)**: 628.7 ± 2.3 MPa
- **Fracture strain / total elongation**: 17.8 ± 0.6%
- **Work-hardening capacity**: ~230 MPa
- **σ_YS/σ_UTS**: 0.63
- **Uniform elongation**: from ~1% true strain to fracture (excellent uniform elongation, implying high uniform elongation but exact numerical value not directly given)
- **Yield strength**: Not explicitly stated as a numerical value in the extracted text; the ratio σ_YS/σ_UTS = 0.63 implies YS ≈ 0.63 × 628.7 MPa ≈ 396 MPa (derived, not explicitly stated)
- **Engineering stress drop at ~5.5% engineering strain**: attributed to activation of specific deformation mechanism (initiation of twinning in intragranular m-ZrO₂ precipitates)

## High-temperature mechanical properties
Not tested in this study. The paper focuses on room-temperature tensile behavior only.

## Hardness
Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

## Elastic modulus
Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

## Density
Relative density of LPBFed specimens: 99.2 ± 0.2%

## Fracture morphology
- Dominant ductile feature with numerous fine dimples with slight cleavage appearance
- High density of nanoscale particles embedded in fine dimples (likely secondary ZrO₂ precipitates detached from Nb matrix after tensile fracture)

## Comparison with literature
- The achieved UTS (628.7 MPa) and ductility (17.8%) represent a record of strength–ductility combination for AMed Nb521 alloys to date.
- AMed Nb521 alloys typically show yield strength higher than 100–150 MPa compared to cast-and-wrought Nb521 parts, but often suffer early plastic instability and premature failure.
- Previous LPBFed Nb521 alloy (Ref [11], without optimal ZrO₂ size/distribution) showed fracture elongation <6%.

## [Zr]

- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Nb521 (Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr) niobium-based refractory alloy
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 1.70 wt.% Zr (nominal 1 wt.%)
- **[Main Function]**: Reacts with oxygen to form in situ ZrO₂ precipitates; acts as an oxide-forming element that enables dispersion strengthening.
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Zirconium has high thermodynamic affinity for oxygen; in the oxygen-rich HDH powder feedstock (~1600 ppm O), Zr reacts with O during LPBF and heat treatment to form nanoscale single-crystalline ZrO₂ precipitates, suppressing carbide precipitation.
- **[Property Effect]**: Primary contributor to the dispersion strengthening and deformability of the alloy. The resulting ZrO₂ precipitates enable TRIP/TWIP mechanisms, contributing to UTS of 628.7 ± 2.3 MPa and elongation of 17.8 ± 0.6% at room temperature.

- **[Original Evidence]**: Table 1; Section 1 states: “the higher oxygen content (~1600 ppm) of the jet-milled HDH Nb521 powders... is thermodynamically favored to react with zirconium to form nanoscale ZrO₂ precipitates and suppress the carbide precipitation.”

## [O]

- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Nb521 niobium-based refractory alloy fabricated from HDH Nb521 powders (LPBF)
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 0.13 wt.% (~1600 ppm) in powder feedstock
- **[Main Function]**: Enables formation of in situ ZrO₂ oxide precipitates when combined with Zr.
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Oxygen reacts with Zr to form nanoscale ZrO₂ precipitates during LPBF and heat treatment. Conventional atomized powders have lower oxygen content and instead form carbide precipitates.
- **[Property Effect]**: Indirectly responsible for the strength–ductility synergy through ZrO₂ precipitates.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: Excess oxygen in Nb-based alloys can cause embrittlement if not effectively gettered by oxide-forming elements such as Zr.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Table 1 lists O at 0.13 wt.%; Section 1 discusses oxygen content of ~1600 ppm in jet-milled HDH powders.

## [C + Zr]

- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Nb521 niobium-based refractory alloy
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: C: 0.014 wt.%; Zr: 1.70 wt.%
- **[Main Function]**: Competitive precipitation: Zr preferentially reacts with O to form ZrO₂ rather than C to form ZrC; suppresses carbide precipitation.
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: The thermodynamic favorability of Zr–O reaction over carbide formation suppresses (Nb,Zr)C or Nb₂C carbides in favor of ZrO₂ precipitates. This is crucial because brittle carbides (with covalent bonding) reduce ductility, while deformable ZrO₂ enables TRIP/TWIP.
- **[Property Effect]**: Avoidance of brittle carbides and formation of deformable ZrO₂ is key to achieving high ductility (17.8 ± 0.6% elongation) without sacrificing strength (628.7 MPa UTS).
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: If carbon content were higher and oxygen lower, brittle carbides could form, compromising ductility.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Section 1 and Section 4.2.2: “Unlike the brittle ZrC precipitates with the covalent bonding [53] in the conventional Nb521 alloys, the plastically deformable ZrO₂ precipitates in this study... show a different deformation behavior under stress.”

## [Zr + O (ZrO₂)]

- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Nb521 niobium-based refractory alloy (LPBF + heat treatment)
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: ZrO₂ volume fraction ~3.1%; intragranular particle mean size ~60 nm; intergranular mean size ~237 nm
- **[Main Function]**: In situ oxide dispersion strengthening; enables TRIP and TWIP; accommodates strain and relieves stress concentration.
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: 
  - Semi-coherent interface with Nb matrix (lattice mismatch ~11.3%) transmits incoming dislocations and accommodates strain.
  - Intergranular ZrO₂ undergoes t→m martensitic transformation and (100)[001] deformation twinning.
  - Intragranular ZrO₂ undergoes deformation twinning and significant plastic deformation; piles up dislocations, forming high-density dislocation networks (dislocation-precipitate skeleton, DPS architecture) via Orowan mechanism.
- **[Property Effect]**: Room-temperature UTS = 628.7 ± 2.3 MPa; elongation = 17.8 ± 0.6%; work-hardening capacity ~230 MPa; record strength–ductility combination for AMed Nb521 alloys.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: If ZrO₂ precipitates are too small (<30 nm), they may not be deformable due to high activation energy for phase transformation and twinning.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Sections 3.1–3.3, 4.1–4.2; Conclusions 1–5.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## [Solid-Solution Strengthening]

- **Related Elements**: W, Mo in Nb matrix
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb521 (Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr)
- **Key Compositions**: W 5.33 wt.%, Mo 1.73 wt.%
- **Microstructural Changes**: Substitutional solutes in BCC Nb lattice
- **Property Improvements**: Contributes to baseline strength of Nb521 alloy. The original paper does not provide specific quantification of the solid-solution contribution relative to other mechanisms.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Not explicitly discussed in this paper.

## [Oxide Dispersion Strengthening (ZrO₂ Precipitates)]

- **Related Elements**: Zr, O; ZrO₂ precipitates
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb521 (LPBF + heat treatment)
- **Key Compositions**: ~3.1 vol.% ZrO₂; intragranular mean size ~60 nm; intergranular mean size ~237 nm
- **Microstructural Changes**: Copious semi-coherent nanoscale single-crystalline ZrO₂ precipitates homogeneously dispersed in BCC Nb matrix and at GBs. Precipitates pile up dislocations; high-density dislocation network/DP skeleton forms around precipitates.
- **Property Improvements**: UTS = 628.7 ± 2.3 MPa; elongation = 17.8 ± 0.6%; work-hardening capacity ~230 MPa; dynamic Hall–Petch effect from dislocation-precipitate skeleton; Orowan bowing interaction.
- **Risks and Limitations**: If ZrO₂ precipitates are too small (<30 nm), phase transformation and deformation twinning are suppressed (high activation energy), leading to low ductility (<6% elongation as in previous study). ZrO₂ must be single crystalline to exhibit deformability.

## [Transformation-Induced Plasticity (TRIP)]

- **Related Elements**: ZrO₂ (t→m martensitic transformation)
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb521 with ZrO₂ precipitates
- **Key Compositions**: Intergranular ZrO₂ particles with retained metastable t-ZrO₂ (~36.7%)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Stress-induced martensitic transformation of tetragonal t-ZrO₂ to monoclinic m-ZrO₂ during tension, accompanied by formation of twin-related variants. The phase transformation initiates at the phase boundary at low plastic strains (~3%), continues with increasing strain, and completes at final deformation stage.
- **Property Improvements**: TRIP effect enables plasticity and improves fracture toughness; contributes to the high uniform elongation and work-hardening capacity (~230 MPa).
- **Risks and Limitations**: Retained t-ZrO₂ is metastable and may transform prematurely under thermal fluctuation. Polycrystalline ZrO₂ can suffer transformation mismatch stresses leading to cracks; this risk is mitigated by using single-crystalline nanoscale precipitates.

## [Twinning-Induced Plasticity (TWIP)]

- **Related Elements**: ZrO₂ precipitates; (100)[001] deformation twinning
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb521 with ZrO₂ precipitates
- **Key Compositions**: Nanoscale ZrO₂ precipitates >60 nm mean size
- **Microstructural Changes**: Deformation twinning of (100)[001] type in ZrO₂ precipitates during tension; SFs provide necessary condition for subsequent twinning; detwinning at final deformation stage leaves SFs behind and enables significant plastic deformation.
- **Property Improvements**: TWIP effect improves ductility by accommodating strain and relieving stress concentration; contributes to work hardening. Together with TRIP, provides capacity to accommodate strain and relieve stress concentration.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Deformation twinning in ZrO₂ is size-dependent; ultrafine ZrO₂ particles (<30 nm) show suppressed twinning propensity due to high activation energy.

## [Grain-Boundary Strengthening / Grain Refinement]

- **Related Elements**: Not element-specific; intrinsic to AM process
- **Typical Alloys**: AMed Nb521
- **Key Compositions**: Not applicable
- **Microstructural Changes**: Ultrafine equiaxed grains (<10 μm) at melt pool boundaries; large columnar grains in melt pool centers; average grain size 17.8 ± 18.1 μm; ~40.6% LAGBs.
- **Property Improvements**: Grain refinement contributes to alloy strength, as noted in the paper.
- **Risks and Limitations**: High density of GBs, heterogeneous interfaces, and dislocations in AMed parts can lead to stress concentration, early plastic instability, and premature failure (brittle fracture in LPBF and LMD parts).

## [Dislocation–Precipitate Skeleton (DPS) Architecture / Dynamic Hall–Petch Effect]

- **Related Elements**: ZrO₂ precipitates; dislocations in Nb matrix
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb521 with ZrO₂ precipitates
- **Key Compositions**: ~3.1 vol.% ZrO₂
- **Microstructural Changes**: High-density dislocation networks form around ZrO₂ precipitates during tension; dislocation walls form around precipitates consisting of tangled dislocations; dislocations interact via Orowan bowing mechanism and plastically deform ZrO₂ precipitates.
- **Property Improvements**: Reduces mean free path of dislocations; causes dynamic Hall–Petch effect; enhances work-hardening response; stabilizes DPS architecture through semi-coherent interface bonding and stress accommodation via deformation twinning.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Requires semi-coherent interface for stability; brittle precipitates (such as carbides) would not provide the same deformability and would compromise ductility.

## [Additive Manufacturing-Oriented Composition Control]

- **Related Elements**: O, Zr, C
- **Typical Alloys**: Nb521 fabricated by LPBF using HDH powders with elevated oxygen content
- **Key Compositions**: 0.13 wt.% O; 1.70 wt.% Zr; 0.014 wt.% C
- **Microstructural Changes**: Elevated O content in HDH powders favors ZrO₂ precipitation instead of carbide precipitation, resulting in deformable oxide dispersions rather than brittle carbides.
- **Property Improvements**: Combined with optimal ZrO₂ particle size (>60 nm) and heat treatment at 1400 °C/1 h, achieves record strength–ductility combination for AMed Nb521 alloy (UTS 628.7 MPa, elongation 17.8%).
- **Risks and Limitations**: Requires precise control of powder oxygen content and heat treatment to achieve optimal precipitate size and distribution; excessive oxygen without proper gettering could embrittle the alloy.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Successful in situ incorporation of ZrO₂ precipitates**: Homogeneously and semi-coherently dispersed nanoscale single-crystalline ZrO₂ precipitates were formed in Nb521 alloy via LPBF using cost-effective jet-milled HDH powders followed by vacuum heat treatment (1400 °C/1 h). Precipitate mean sizes: ~60 nm (intragranular) and ~237 nm (intergranular).

2. **Dual-phase structure in intergranular precipitates**: Intergranular ZrO₂ precipitates contain both tetragonal and monoclinic phases with minor (002)[100] annealing twins; intragranular ZrO₂ precipitates are single monoclinic phase with good coherency with the Nb matrix (semi-coherent interface, lattice mismatch ~11.3%).

3. **Deformation mechanisms of ZrO₂ precipitates**: 
   - Intergranular ZrO₂: martensitic phase transformation (t→m) and deformation twinning are dominant mechanisms.
   - Intragranular ZrO₂: (100)[001] deformation twinning and significant plastic deformation, with shape change from quasi-spherical to lamellar.

4. **TRIP and TWIP effects**: The stress-induced transformation-induced plasticity and twinning-induced plasticity in nanoscale ZrO₂ precipitates effectively accommodate strain and relieve stress concentration during tension, contributing to improved ductility.

5. **Dislocation-precipitate skeleton**: Intragranular ZrO₂ precipitates effectively pile up incoming dislocations from the Nb matrix, forming high-density dislocation networks that interact via the Orowan mechanism and result in significant plastic deformation of the precipitates, contributing to work hardening.

6. **Overall property achievement**: The as-fabricated Nb521 alloy achieved room-temperature UTS of 628.7 ± 2.3 MPa and elongation of 17.8 ± 0.6% with work-hardening capacity of ~230 MPa, representing the record strength–ductility combination for AMed Nb521 alloys to date.

7. **Comparison with prior work**: The improvement over previous LPBFed Nb521 alloy (Ref [11]) is attributed to the larger ZrO₂ particle size (>60 nm vs. <30 nm) enabling phase transformation and deformation twinning, and the heat treatment relieving residual stress.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Room-temperature focus**: All tensile tests were conducted at room temperature (~20 °C). The high-temperature mechanical properties, creep performance, and oxidation resistance at service temperatures were not evaluated in this study.

2. **No quantitative high-temperature oxidation test**: Although the alloy is intended for high-temperature aerospace applications, the paper does not provide oxidation weight gain data, oxidation layer characterization, or testing at elevated temperatures. Comments on ZrO₂ oxidation resistance refer to the intrinsic property of ZrO₂ material, not the oxidation performance of the overall Nb521 alloy.

3. **Single alloy composition**: Only the Nb521 composition was studied. The applicability of this strategy to other Nb-based alloys (e.g., C103), Mo-based, W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloys is not experimentally validated in this paper.

4. **Single heat treatment condition**: Only one heat treatment (1400 °C/1 h) was employed. The effects of varied heat-treatment parameters on precipitate size distribution and mechanical properties were not systematically investigated.

5. **Limited mechanical property characterization**: No fracture toughness, fatigue, creep, or high-temperature tensile data are reported. Hardness and elastic modulus were not characterized.

6. **Qualitative understanding of solid-solution strengthening**: The individual contributions of W and Mo to solid-solution strengthening are not quantified.

7. **Density limitations**: Relative density of 99.2 ± 0.2% still indicates ~0.8% porosity; the influence of residual porosity on mechanical behavior is not discussed in detail.

8. **Absence of yield strength explicit value**: The yield strength is not explicitly stated as a single numerical value, though σ_YS/σ_UTS = 0.63 is given.

9. **Limited dislocation type analysis**: The dislocation characterization was performed on a single grain in one specimen; statistical robustness is limited.

10. **Retained t-ZrO₂ stability**: The paper does not discuss the long-term stability of retained metastable t-ZrO₂ under thermal cycling or prolonged high-temperature service, which could affect microstructural stability.

11. **Applicability to conventional manufacturing**: The paper focuses on LPBF. Whether the ZrO₂ dispersion strategy can be transferred to conventional powder metallurgy or cast-and-wrought routes is not discussed.

12. **Mechanisms validated only at room temperature**: TRIP and TWIP mechanisms in ZrO₂ were characterized only under room-temperature tensile loading; behavior at elevated temperatures or other loading modes is not addressed.
