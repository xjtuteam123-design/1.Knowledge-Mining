# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Achieving a balance between mechanical properties at room and elevated temperatures of lightweight NiAlFeCrMoV high-entropy alloy
- **Authors**: Yake Xiao, Xiangpeng Chang, Xianghe Peng
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2023 (Volume 114, Article 106243; received 6 January 2023, accepted 24 April 2023, available online 28 April 2023)
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106243
- **Alloy System Studied**: Lightweight high-entropy alloy with nominal composition (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45; a dual-phase BCC + B2 eutectic HEA based on Ni-Al B2 and Fe-Cr-Mo-V BCC constituents
- **Research Objective**: To design and prepare a novel lightweight HEA that simultaneously achieves low density, good room-temperature ductility, and high elevated-temperature strength, and to investigate its microstructure, compressive properties, and deformation mechanisms
- **Material Category**: Lightweight high-entropy alloy (HEA) with BCC + B2 dual-phase eutectic structure; not classified as a refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) in the strict sense, but designed for high-temperature structural applications and compared with RHEAs. The alloy incorporates refractory elements Mo and V.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system**: (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45 high-entropy alloy, a pseudo-binary eutectic HEA consisting of ordered B2 (NiAl-rich) and disordered BCC (Fe-Cr-Mo-V-rich) phases
- **Research objective**: To overcome the limitations of previous alloys by combining: (1) B2 NiAl phase for strength and oxidation resistance with low density; (2) BCC FeCr phase for coherent interface with B2; (3) high-melting-point elements Mo and V for high-temperature strength and to avoid TCP phase formation
- **Material category**: Eutectic high-entropy alloy (EHEA) with BCC + B2 structure; lightweight (density 6.72 g/cm³)

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phases**: Two-phase eutectic structure consisting of:
  - **B2 phase** (ordered BCC, matrix phase): rich in Ni and Al
  - **BCC phase** (disordered, lamellar phase): rich in Fe, Cr, Mo, V
- **Microstructure morphology**: Uniform eutectic structure with lamellar morphology; no obvious shrinkage defects; excellent castability
- **Phase identification methods**: XRD (BCC + B2 phases), SEM, TEM, SAED, HAADF-EDS
- **Crystal structure confirmation**:
  - SAED along [011] zone axis of matrix phase (Site A) shows superlattice diffraction, confirming B2 structure
  - SAED of lamellar phase (Site B) confirms BCC structure
- **Lattice mismatch at B2/BCC interface**: δ = (a_BCC − a_B2)/a_BCC ≈ 2.25%, indicating very small lattice mismatch
  - Interplanar spacing of B2 phase: 0.2091 nm
  - Interplanar spacing of BCC phase: 0.2139 nm
- **Interface structure**: Few dislocations at the interface before deformation; atomic planes continuous across the interface; high interfacial bonding strength
- **Elemental segregation**: Matrix (B2) phase enriched in Ni and Al; lamellar (BCC) phase enriched in Fe, Cr, Mo, V (confirmed by HAADF-EDS)
- **Grain size**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper
- **Precipitates / second phases**: No carbide, oxide, boride, or silicide precipitates reported
- **Formation enthalpy**: Elemental aggregation attributed to formation enthalpy reflecting binding force between elements
- **Melting point (calculated by rule of mixtures)**: Tm = 1752 K

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Card 1: Ni + Al (B2-forming combination)

- **[Element or Element Combination]**: Ni + Al (B2-ordered phase former)
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45 lightweight eutectic HEA; also relevant to FeCrNiAlx HEAs with BCC + B2 structures
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 55 at.% of the total alloy as NiAl (i.e., Ni 27.5 at.% + Al 27.5 at.% nominal); B2 matrix phase enriched in Ni and Al
- **[Main Function]**: Forms ordered B2 intermetallic phase that provides high strength, improves oxidation resistance, and significantly reduces density
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Ordered B2 phase with small lattice mismatch (≈2.25%) against BCC phase, creating coherent interfaces; superlattice structure confirmed by SAED along [011] zone axis. B2 is an ordered intermetallic compound with high melting temperature and excellent oxidation resistance (per cited literature). Compared to an FeCrNiAl0.77 HEA (also BCC+B2), increasing B2 phase fraction can enhance high-temperature strength.
- **[Property Effect]**: Contributes to room-temperature yield strength of 1739.5 MPa and 1073 K yield strength of 950.7 MPa. In cited FeCrNiAlx alloys, increasing B2 fraction from 28.72% to 50.92% increased 1073 K compressive yield strength from 329 MPa to 363 MPa. Specific numerical contribution of B2 phase to the present alloy is not isolated in the original paper.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: Ordered intermetallic B2 phases can be inherently brittle; however, in this alloy the eutectic structure with BCC phase and small lattice mismatch mitigates brittleness, resulting in 24.9% room-temperature fracture strain.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Section 1 Introduction: "the B2 phase composed of elements Ni and Al can not only improve the strength and oxidation resistance, but also significantly reduce the density"; Section 4.2: "the B2 phase composed of Ni and Al is an ordered intermetallic compound that has long been a candidate for high-temperature structural applications due to its high melting temperature and excellent oxidation resistance"; Figs. 1–3; References [15–17,48].

## Card 2: Fe + Cr (BCC-forming combination)

- **[Element or Element Combination]**: Fe + Cr (BCC phase formers)
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45 lightweight eutectic HEA; also relevant to other BCC + B2 eutectic HEAs
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: Combined 22.5 at.% nominal in the (FeCrMoV)45 portion (Fe 11.25 at.% + Cr 11.25 at.% nominal). The BCC lamellar phase is rich in Fe and Cr alongside Mo and V.
- **[Main Function]**: Forms disordered BCC phase that creates a coherent interface with the B2 phase, improving mechanical properties due to small lattice mismatch
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: BCC phase has a small lattice mismatch (≈2.25%) with the B2 phase; the coherent interface and continuous atomic planes across the phase boundary enable dislocations to slip across the interface and reduce long-range internal stress. Fe and Cr tend to partition into the BCC phase due to formation enthalpy considerations.
- **[Property Effect]**: Contributes to maintaining ductility (24.9% fracture strain at room temperature) while achieving high strength; the phase interface between BCC and B2 hinders dislocation motion, contributing to strain hardening. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values isolating the contribution of Fe + Cr.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: BCC phase has lower high-temperature strength and stability compared to B2 phase, softening more easily at high temperature; this contributes to the strength decrease at 1073 K and 1273 K.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Section 1 Introduction: "the BCC phase composed of elements Fe and Cr can form a coherent interface with B2 phase, which can improve the mechanical properties due to the small lattice mismatch"; Figs. 2(d),(e) showing Fe and Cr enrichment in lamellar phase; Section 4.1: "the BCC phase is more easily to deformation at high temperatures compared to B2 phase due to its lower high temperature strength and stability."

## Card 3: Mo + V (high-melting-point solid-solution strengtheners)

- **[Element or Element Combination]**: Mo + V
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45 lightweight eutectic HEA; also relevant to refractory HEAs containing Mo and V such as CrMoNbV, NbMoTaW, VNbMoTaW
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: Combined 22.5 at.% nominal in the (FeCrMoV)45 portion (Mo 11.25 at.% + V 11.25 at.% nominal)
- **[Main Function]**: Improve high-temperature strength; avoid formation of topological close-packed (TCP) phases (mainly σ and Laves phases); contribute to solid-solution strengthening
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Mo and V partition into the BCC lamellar phase (confirmed by HAADF-EDS). Their high melting points contribute to high-temperature strength and softening resistance of the BCC phase. The paper states that adding high-melting-point elements Mo and V "can not only effectively improve the high temperature strength of the alloy, but also avoid the formation of topological close-packed phase."
- **[Property Effect]**: Compared with FeCrNiAl0.77 HEA composed of BCC and B2 phases, the compressive yield strength at 1073 K is increased from 363 MPa to 950.7 MPa. The paper attributes the high strength of the HEA mainly to the addition of Mo and V. Room-temperature yield strength: 1739.5 MPa; 1073 K yield strength: 950.7 MPa; 1273 K yield strength: 421.8 MPa.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: Not explicitly discussed in the original paper. Mo and V are high-density elements; however, the overall alloy density remains low (6.72 g/cm³) due to the high Ni + Al content.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Abstract: "The high strength of the HEA can mainly be attributed to the addition of high melting point elements Mo and V."; Section 1 Introduction: "adding high melting point elements Mo and V can not only effectively improve the high temperature strength of the alloy, but also avoid the formation of topological close-packed phase (TCP, mainly including σ and Laves phases)"; Section 4.2: "the high temperature performance of the BCC phase is greatly improved due to the addition of high melting point elements Mo and V. Compared with FeCrNiAl0.77 HEA composed of BCC and B2 phases, the compressive yield strength at 1073 K is increased from 363 MPa to 950.7 MPa."

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Card 1: Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related elements**: Mo, V (primary high-temperature strengtheners); Fe, Cr (BCC matrix formers); Ni, Al (B2 formers)
- **Typical alloys**: (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45 HEA; also relevant to CrMoNbV, NbMoTaW, VNbMoTaW, and other refractory HEAs containing Mo and V
- **Key compositions**: Mo 11.25 at.% and V 11.25 at.% in the BCC lamellar phase
- **Microstructural changes**: Mo and V partition into the disordered BCC phase, increasing its high-temperature strength and resistance to softening. The B2 phase is intrinsically strong due to ordered intermetallic bonding.
- **Property improvements**: High yield strength at room temperature (1739.5 MPa) and elevated temperatures (950.7 MPa at 1073 K; 421.8 MPa at 1273 K). The 1073 K yield strength is increased by approximately 588 MPa compared with FeCrNiAl0.77 (from 363 MPa to 950.7 MPa), attributed mainly to Mo and V addition.
- **Risks and limitations**: Mo and V increase density relative to purely Al-based lightweight alloys; however, in this alloy the overall density remains 6.72 g/cm³. The original paper does not discuss other side effects specific to Mo and V.

## Card 2: Interface Strengthening / Phase-Boundary Strengthening

- **Related elements**: Ni, Al (B2 phase); Fe, Cr, Mo, V (BCC phase)
- **Typical alloys**: (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45 eutectic HEA; AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic HEA; other BCC+B2 eutectic HEAs
- **Key compositions**: 55 at.% NiAl / 45 at.% (FeCrMoV)
- **Microstructural changes**: Eutectic lamellar structure with numerous phase interfaces. The lattice mismatch between B2 and BCC is only ≈2.25%, producing a coherent interface with strong bonding and minimal long-range internal stress. Dislocations accumulate at phase interfaces during deformation and can also slip across the interface.
- **Property improvements**: The phase interfaces hinder dislocation motion, improving strain hardening and achieving high strength without sacrificing ductility. Room-temperature fracture strain reaches 24.9% while yield strength is 1739.5 MPa. The small lattice mismatch enables high interfacial bonding strength.
- **Risks and limitations**: At high temperature, the BCC phase softens more readily than the B2 phase, causing the interface to become a dislocation accumulation site; this is not necessarily detrimental but contributes to the overall high-temperature behavior. No specific fracture or delamination risk at interface is reported.

## Card 3: Dislocation Strengthening / Work Hardening

- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; operates through phases and interfaces
- **Typical alloys**: (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45 HEA
- **Key compositions**: BCC + B2 dual-phase eutectic structure
- **Microstructural changes**: Dislocations form and are stored in both BCC and B2 phases during deformation at room temperature and 1073 K. Dislocations also accumulate at phase interfaces. After room-temperature compression, few dislocations remain at the interface due to small mismatch; after compression at 1073 K, a large number of dislocations accumulate at the interface.
- **Property improvements**: Dislocation strengthening enhances strength without sacrificing ductility. This mechanism is one of four reasons cited for the excellent comprehensive properties (see Section 4.2 of the paper).
- **Risks and limitations**: The original paper does not discuss specific risks. However, dislocation accumulation at interfaces at high temperature may be associated with softening of the BCC phase, as the stress slightly descends after yield at 1073 K and 1273 K.

## Card 4: Lightweight Alloying / Density Reduction

- **Related elements**: Ni and Al (low-density elements, especially Al)
- **Typical alloys**: (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45 HEA; FeCrNiAlx HEAs; TiZrVNb and other lightweight RHEAs
- **Key compositions**: 55 at.% NiAl (27.5 at.% Ni + 27.5 at.% Al nominal)
- **Microstructural changes**: High Al and Ni content promotes B2 phase formation and reduces overall alloy density.
- **Property improvements**: Density reduced to 6.72 g/cm³ (theoretical) and 6.78 g/cm³ (measured). Specific yield strength: 258.9 MPa·cm³/g at room temperature; 141.2 MPa·cm³/g at 1073 K; 62.8 MPa·cm³/g at 1273 K.
- **Risks and limitations**: High Al content can lead to formation of brittle intermetallic phases if not balanced with ductile phases; in this alloy, the eutectic structure with coherent interfaces mitigates this risk.

## Card 5: Ductility Preservation via Eutectic Structure and Small Lattice Mismatch

- **Related elements**: All constituent elements
- **Typical alloys**: (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45 HEA
- **Key compositions**: BCC + B2 eutectic with δ ≈ 2.25%
- **Microstructural changes**: Coherent B2/BCC interface with continuous atomic planes; dislocations can slip across the interface; few misfit dislocations at the interface before deformation.
- **Property improvements**: Room-temperature fracture strain of 24.9%, significantly higher than many RHEAs (typically <6% for CrMoNbV; <3% for NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW) and better than AlMo0.5NbTa0.5TiZr and V37Nb31Ti12Cr5Si15 (≈12% fracture strain).
- **Risks and limitations**: The ductility benefit is specific to room-temperature conditions; at elevated temperatures, ductility generally increases for most alloys and yield strength becomes the more critical parameter.

# Key Conclusions

1. The novel lightweight (NiAl)55(FeCrMoV)45 HEA, prepared by vacuum arc melting, is composed of BCC and B2 phases with a uniform eutectic structure. The lattice mismatch at the B2/BCC interface is only ≈2.25%, indicating very small mismatch and strong interfacial cohesion.

2. The HEA has a low density of only 6.72 g/cm³ (theoretical) / 6.78 g/cm³ (measured).

3. The HEA exhibits excellent compressive mechanical properties:
   - Room temperature: yield strength 1739.5 MPa, specific yield strength 258.9 MPa·cm³/g, fracture strain 24.9%
   - 1073 K: yield strength 950.7 MPa, specific yield strength 141.2 MPa·cm³/g
   - 1273 K: yield strength 421.8 MPa, specific yield strength 62.8 MPa·cm³/g
   - These values are superior to most EHEAs, RHEAs, and conventional alloys (including Inconel 718 and Haynes 230) reported previously.

4. The plastic deformation mechanism at both room and elevated temperatures is governed by dislocation slip. Dislocations form in both BCC and B2 phases and accumulate at phase interfaces.

5. The excellent comprehensive mechanical properties are attributed to four factors:
   - Dislocation strengthening and interface hindrance
   - Small lattice mismatch providing high interfacial bonding strength
   - Ordered B2 NiAl phase with high strength and oxidation resistance
   - Addition of high-melting-point elements Mo and V improving high-temperature strength

6. The high strength is mainly attributed to Mo and V additions; compared with FeCrNiAl0.77, the 1073 K yield strength increases from 363 MPa to 950.7 MPa.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **The original paper does not report**:
  - Tensile properties (only compressive)
  - Creep, fracture toughness, fatigue, or rupture life
  - Hardness or elastic modulus values
  - Oxidation resistance experimental data
  - Grain size measurements
  - Impurity levels (C, O, N, H)
  - Weight percentages of elements
  - Effects of heat treatment (only as-cast condition studied)
  - Long-term phase stability or aging behavior

- **Alloy category**: The alloy is a lightweight HEA rather than a true refractory HEA. It contains refractory elements Mo and V but is based on Ni-Al B2 and Fe-Cr BCC phases. This paper is relevant to the domain of high-temperature structural alloys but should be distinguished from Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, W-based, or Nb-W-Mo-Zr alloy literature, as well as ODS alloys and additively manufactured refractory alloys.

- **Applicable scope**:
  - Lightweight high-temperature structural materials
  - Eutectic HEAs with BCC + B2 structures
  - Alloy design strategies combining low density with high-temperature strength
  - Mo and V as high-temperature strengtheners in multi-principal element alloys
  - Coherent interface engineering for strength-ductility balance

- **The paper's design rationale for TCP phase avoidance (Mo + V) is based on references [19–21] and is not experimentally verified in this paper for the specific alloy studied.**

- **The author's statement that "B2 phase improves oxidation resistance" is based on literature [15–17] and not experimentally confirmed in this study. No oxidation experiments were performed.**

- **The stability of the eutectic BCC + B2 structure beyond 1273 K or under long-term exposure is not addressed.**

- **The melting point (Tm = 1752 K) is calculated by rule of mixtures and not measured; test temperatures of 1073 K and 1273 K correspond to 0.61 Tm and 0.73 Tm, respectively.**
