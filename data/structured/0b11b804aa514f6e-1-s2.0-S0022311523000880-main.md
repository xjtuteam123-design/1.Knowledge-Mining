# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** DBTT and tensile properties of as-sintered tungsten alloys reinforced by yttrium-zirconium oxide
- **Authors:** Jiahao Ye, Liujie Xu, Yunchao Zhao, Zhou Li, Hua Yu, Shizhong Wei, Huahai Shen
- **Journal:** Journal of Nuclear Materials
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jnucmat.2023.154318
- **Alloy system studied:** W-Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) tungsten alloys
- **Research objective:** To investigate whether adding ZrO₂ to W-Y₂O₃ alloys can refine grain size, lower DBTT, and improve high-temperature tensile properties while reducing reliance on costly rare-earth Y₂O₃
- **Material category:** ODS tungsten alloy (W-based), prepared by powder metallurgy / spark plasma sintering

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Base alloy system:** Tungsten (W) with oxide dispersion strengthening
- **Primary alloying additives:** Y₂O₃ and ZrO₂
- **Motivation stated in paper:** Y₂O₃ is a costly rare-earth oxide; ZrO₂ offers high hardness, high melting point, good high-temperature stability, and lower cost. Combination of Y₂O₃ and ZrO₂ is intended to retain dispersion strengthening while reducing rare-earth usage.
- **Comparison samples:** Pure W (literature), W-0.5wt%Y₂O₃ (WY05, literature), W-1.0wt%Y₂O₃ (WY10, literature)

# Alloy Composition

Four samples were prepared in this study:

| Sample | W (wt.%) | Y₂O₃ (wt.%) | ZrO₂ (wt.%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| WY | Balance | 0.5 | 0 |
| WYZ05 | Balance | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| WYZ10 | Balance | 0.5 | 1.0 |
| WYZ20 | Balance | 0.5 | 2.0 |

- **Raw materials:**
  - Pure W powder: average particle size ~2 μm, purity 99.9%, trace metal elements
  - m-ZrO₂ powder: average particle size ~500 nm, purity 99.9%
  - Y₂O₃ powder: average particle size ~1 μm, purity 99.9%
- **Impurity levels:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper; the paper mentions O, N, C impurities at grain boundaries qualitatively as a known influence on grain-boundary strength, but no quantitative ppm values are given.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Powder mixing:** Roller ball milling for 48 h
- **Ball-to-material ratio:** 1:6
- **Rotating speed:** 240 rpm
- **Ball milling medium:** ZrO₂ balls (chosen to minimize contamination given the ZrO₂ additive)
- **Sintering method:** Spark plasma sintering (SPS)
- **SPS mold:** Graphite mold, 30 mm diameter
- **Uniaxial pressure:** 40 MPa
- **Heating:** Pulse current, 1000 A–5000 A, heated to 2000 °C
- **Heating rate:** 120 °C/min above 600 °C; 100 °C/min below 600 °C
- **Cooling:** Furnace cooling
- **Sintered sample thickness:** ~14 mm
- **Post-sintering deformation:** None; samples tested in as-sintered state
- **Literature comparison samples:** Swaged W-Y₂O₃ (DBTT reduced to ~100 °C after swaging); cold-rolled W foils (UTS ~2 GPa for pure W, ~3 GPa for K-doped W at room temperature) are cited but not fabricated in this work

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase:** BCC tungsten
- **Second-phase particles:** Y₂O₃ and ZrO₂ particles uniformly distributed at grain boundaries and inside grains
- **Important structural observation:** In WYZ05, EDS mapping and line scans show Y and Zr signals at the same locations where W is absent, with highly consistent Y-Zr distribution. Selected area electron diffraction (SAED) identifies the particle as c-ZrO₂ (cubic ZrO₂).
- **Mechanism of c-ZrO₂ formation stated:** Y₂O₃ reduces the crystal transition temperature of ZrO₂ and stabilizes the electronic structure of vacancies and impurities in the ZrO₂ crystal, changing m-ZrO₂ into c-ZrO₂.
- **Grain size:**
  - WY: 5.5 μm (EBSD average)
  - WYZ05: 2.0 μm (EBSD average); Abstract reports ~1.95 μm; Conclusions report ~2.7 μm for the same sample. The body text value of 2.0 μm is the most consistently reported EBSD measurement.
  - WYZ10: 2.0 μm
  - WYZ20: 1.9 μm
- **Grain size reduction relative to WY:** WYZ05 decreased by 63.6%
- **Grain boundary character:** Grain orientation is random and disordered; grain boundaries are dominated by high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs, θ > 10°), shown as black lines in EBSD. White lines indicate low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs, 2° < θ < 10°).
- **Second-phase particle size:**
  - WY: average 1.6 μm
  - WYZ05: average 0.5 μm
  - WYZ10: average 0.6 μm
  - WYZ20: average 0.8 μm
- **Second-phase volume fraction (area fraction from SEM):**
  - WY: 2.6%
  - WYZ05: 4.6%
  - WYZ10: 5.5%
  - WYZ20: 8.4%
- **Grain growth inhibition mechanism:** Oxide particles pin grain boundaries during SPS, inhibiting grain growth. The rapid heating/short holding time of SPS also limits grain growth. The paper states that W grain size is positively correlated with second-phase particle size.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed:** Fine-grain strengthening (Hall-Petch / GB strengthening) and Orowan strengthening. The paper explicitly states that the material is as-sintered (no plastic deformation), so strengthening contribution comes from the joint effect of GB strengthening and Orowan strengthening.

# Mechanical Properties

- **Relative density:**
  - WY: ~96%
  - WYZ05: >98%
  - WYZ10: >98%
  - WYZ20: >98%
  - Relative density increases with ZrO₂ content.
- **Microhardness (HVT-1000, 200 g load, 10 s dwell, 30 positions averaged):**
  - WY: 381.7 HV
  - WYZ05: 496.7 HV (29.9% higher than WY)
  - WYZ10: 486.0 HV (27.3% higher than WY)
  - WYZ20: 489.5 HV (28.2% higher than WY)
  - Hardness stabilizes near ~490 HV regardless of further ZrO₂ addition.
- **High-temperature hardness:** The abstract and text state that WYZ05 has higher high-temperature hardness compared with pure W and WY alloys, but no specific high-temperature hardness values are provided in the original paper.

## Tensile properties

- **Test conditions:** Instron-5582, constant extension rate 0.06 mm/min, specimen working length 5 mm, effective cross-section 1.5 mm × 1 mm, temperatures 300 °C, 400 °C, 500 °C
- **Gauge strain measurement:** No extensometer used; total elongation measured by vernier caliper
- **Brittle fracture at 300 °C:** All four alloys show typical brittle fracture.
- **DBTT:** Between 300 °C and 400 °C for SPS-sintered W-Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ alloys of this work. The paper states that DBTT of commercial pure W and as-sintered W alloys previously reported is mostly between 400 °C and 600 °C.
- **Nil ductility temperature (NDT):** ~300 °C for the SPS-sintered alloys in this work; WY05 and WY10 literature controls show NDT ~400 °C.
- **At 500 °C:**
  - WYZ05: UTS = 525.2 MPa, TE = 13.5%
  - WYZ10: UTS = 544.1 MPa, TE = 8.2%
  - WYZ20: UTS = 586.2 MPa, TE = 6.7%
- **Comparison at 500 °C:**
  - WY05 (literature): UTS = 404 MPa, TE = 10 ±0.4%
  - WY10 (literature): UTS = 538 MPa, TE = 3.6 ±0.4%
  - Pure W (literature): UTS at 600 °C = 211 MPa; at 700 °C UTS = 337 MPa, TE = 11.6 ±0.4%
- **At 500 °C, WYZ05 vs WY05:** UTS 30% higher, TE 35% higher
- **At 500 °C, WYZ05 vs WY10:** UTS 2.4% lower, TE 275% higher
- **At 500 °C, WYZ10 vs WY05:** UTS 34.7% higher, TE 18% lower than WY05
- **At 500 °C, WYZ20 vs WY05:** UTS 45.1% higher, TE 33% lower than WY05
- **Trend with ZrO₂ content:** UTS first increases, then decreases, then increases again (525.2 → 544.1 → 586.2 MPa at 500 °C). Total elongation decreases monotonically with increasing ZrO₂ content (13.5% → 8.2% → 6.7% at 500 °C).
- **At 400 °C:**
  - WY05 (literature): 483 MPa, brittle fracture
  - WY10 (literature): 484 MPa, brittle fracture
- **Fracture mode at 500 °C:** Ductile fracture with dimples observed by SEM. Second-phase particles identified as dimple nucleation sites. The paper cautions that larger second-phase particles can block dislocation passage across grain boundaries, leading to high dislocation accumulation rates and strong stress concentration near ZrO₂, causing premature fracture.

## Strengthening model calculations for WYZ05 (at 500 °C)

- **Orowan-Ashby formula input parameters:**
  - Taylor factor, m = 2.733 (BCC)
  - Shear modulus of W matrix, μ = 137.3 MPa
  - Burgers vector, b = 0.274 nm
  - Particle size used in calculation: d (not explicitly given in calculation section; based on measured second-phase particle size, average ~0.5 μm for WYZ05)
  - Volume fraction used: f = 3.48%
- **Calculated Orowan strengthening contribution (Δσ_or):** 20.2 MPa
- **Hall-Petch coefficient:** k = 1597 MPa·μm^½ (determined by linear fitting, close to reported value of 1568 for BCC metals; n₁ = n₂ = 1 for well-developed subgrain LAGB case)
- **Calculated grain-boundary strengthening contribution (Δσ_gb):** 503.8 MPa
- **Calculated total UTS increment (Δσ_UTS):** 525.2 MPa
- **Relative contributions:** Orowan strengthening 3.85%, GB strengthening 96.15%
- **Note:** The paper states that Orowan contribution uses f = 3.48%; this differs from the measured area fraction of 4.6% for WYZ05. The discrepancy is not explained in the original paper.

## Literature comparison values for W-based alloys cited in paper

- Pure W DBTT ≈ 800 °C, recrystallization temperature ≈ 1200 °C
- Pure W UTS at 700 °C: 337 MPa, TE 11.6%
- W-HfO₂ alloy: 97% densification, grain size ~200 nm, compressive strength 3 GPa
- W-TiC ultra-fine grain: fracture strength ~2 GPa at room temperature, superplasticity at 1400–1700 °C
- W-1.5ZrO₂: microhardness 538.8 HV; average grain size 4.65 μm (vs 8.42 μm for pure W)
- W-ZrO₂: compressive strength 1941.3 MPa, failure strain 23.1%
- Sintered W-Y₂O₃: grain size 3.6 μm, DBTT 400–500 °C, UTS 404 MPa at 500 °C, TE 10%; DBTT reduced to ~100 °C after swaging
- Cold-rolled 0.1 mm W foil: ductile at ambient temperature, UTS ~2 GPa
- K-doped cold-rolled W foil: UTS ~3 GPa at room temperature

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## ZrO₂ (Zirconia)

### [Applicable Alloy System]
W-Y₂O₃ ODS tungsten alloy (ternary W-Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ system)

### [Addition Amount or Composition Range]
0.5–2.0 wt.% ZrO₂ (samples WYZ05, WYZ10, WYZ20)

### [Main Function]
- Grain refinement via grain-boundary pinning during SPS
- Reduction of DBTT
- Increase of high-temperature tensile strength
- Improvement of relative density
- Orowan dispersion strengthening

### [Microstructural Mechanism]
- m-ZrO₂ transforms to c-ZrO₂ in the presence of Y₂O₃; Y₂O₃ lowers the crystal transition temperature of ZrO₂ and stabilizes the electronic structure of vacancies and impurities in the ZrO₂ crystal.
- Nanoparticles pin tungsten grain boundaries during SPS, inhibiting grain growth.
- Intragranular ZrO₂ particles impede dislocation motion, generating dislocation loops (Orowan mechanism).
- ZrO₂ addition increases the volume fraction of second-phase particles (from 4.6% at 0.5 wt.% to 8.4% at 2.0 wt.%), increasing grain-boundary area.

### [Property Effect]
- Grain size of WYZ05 (0.5 wt.% ZrO₂ added) reduced from 5.5 μm to 2.0 μm, a 63.6% decrease relative to WY.
- Relative density increased from ~96% (WY) to >98% (all ZrO₂-containing samples).
- Microhardness increased from 381.7 HV (WY) to 496.7 HV (WYZ05), an improvement of 29.9%.
- DBTT reduced from 400–600 °C typical for as-sintered W alloys to 300–400 °C.
- At 500 °C, UTS increased from 404 MPa (WY05 literature) to 525.2 MPa (WYZ05), 544.1 MPa (WYZ10), and 586.2 MPa (WYZ20).
- At 500 °C, TE decreased from 13.5% (WYZ05) to 8.2% (WYZ10) and 6.7% (WYZ20).

### [Potential Side Effects]
- Total elongation decreases monotonically with increasing ZrO₂ content.
- Larger second-phase particles can block dislocation passage, causing stress concentration and premature fracture at grain boundaries.
- The paper notes that oxide particles at grain boundaries may introduce stress concentrations and become crack-initiation sites, reducing ductility.

- Fig. 2: BSE images showing second-phase distribution; Fig. 3: EBSD grain size maps; Fig. 4: grain size statistics; Fig. 5: second-phase size distribution; Fig. 6: STEM-EDS showing Y-Zr colocalization; Fig. 7: SAED identifying c-ZrO₂; Fig. 8–9: density and hardness; Fig. 10–11: tensile curves and UTS/TE data; Fig. 12–13: fracture surfaces and EDS of dimple sources.

## Y₂O₃ (Yttria)

### [Applicable Alloy System]
W-Y₂O₃ ODS tungsten alloy as baseline; combined with ZrO₂ in this study

### [Addition Amount or Composition Range]
0.5 wt.% Y₂O₃ (fixed in all four samples of this work)

### [Main Function]
- Dispersed oxide phase for dispersion strengthening
- Co-stabilizer with ZrO₂, promoting c-ZrO₂ formation
- Grain-growth inhibition during sintering

### [Microstructural Mechanism]
- Y₂O₃ acts as a rare-earth oxide dispersion phase.
- In the presence of Y₂O₃, m-ZrO₂ transforms to c-ZrO₂; Y₂O₃ lowers the ZrO₂ transition temperature and stabilizes the electronic structure of vacancies and impurities in the ZrO₂ crystal.
- Y and Zr signals are colocalized in STEM-EDS line scans, confirming co-distribution of Y₂O₃ and ZrO₂ in the same particle populations.
- Y₂O₃ particles pin grain boundaries, though with larger average particle size (1.6 μm) and less grain-refinement effect compared with Y₂O₃+ZrO₂ combinations.

### [Property Effect]
- WY (0.5 wt.% Y₂O₃, no ZrO₂) shows relative density ~96%, microhardness 381.7 HV, grain size 5.5 μm, UTS 404 MPa (literature), TE 10% at 500 °C.
- The paper does not provide a direct comparison of pure W with Y₂O₃ addition in this work, relying on literature values.

### [Potential Side Effects]
- High cost as a rare-earth oxide limits industrial production.
- When used alone, Y₂O₃ gives larger second-phase particles (1.6 μm) and coarser W grain size (5.5 μm) compared with Y₂O₃+ZrO₂ mixtures.

### [Original Evidence]
- Fig. 6 shows Y distribution; Discussion section states Y₂O₃ can stabilize c-ZrO₂; Introduction cites W-Y₂O₃ literature values (Refs. [27], [28]).

## Combined Y₂O₃ + ZrO₂

### [Applicable Alloy System]
W-Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ ternary ODS tungsten alloy

### [Addition Amount or Composition Range]
0.5 wt.% Y₂O₃ + 0.5–2.0 wt.% ZrO₂

### [Main Function]
- Simultaneous reduction of DBTT and improvement of high-temperature strength
- Synergistic grain refinement stronger than Y₂O₃ alone
- Partial replacement of expensive Y₂O₃ with cheaper ZrO₂

### [Microstructural Mechanism]
- Y₂O₃ stabilizes c-ZrO₂; the combination gives smaller, more numerous oxide particles (average size 0.5–0.8 μm vs 1.6 μm for Y₂O₃ alone) that pin grain boundaries more effectively.
- Multiparticle pinning increases grain-boundary volume fraction and reduces relative impurity concentration at grain boundaries.
- Intragranular particles provide Orowan strengthening.

### [Property Effect]
- WYZ05 at 500 °C: UTS = 525.2 MPa, TE = 13.5%, combining high strength and ductility superior to both pure W and W-Y₂O₃ controls.
- DBTT reduced to 300–400 °C.
- Hardness increased to ~490 HV from 381.7 HV.

### [Potential Side Effects]
- None additional beyond those of ZrO₂.

### [Original Evidence]
- Fig. 6: STEM-EDS shows Y and Zr colocalization; Fig. 7: SAED c-ZrO₂; Fig. 11: UTS/TE comparison; Table 1: literature comparison values; Table 2: strengthening contribution calculations.

## Oxide Dispersion Strengthening (Orowan Strengthening)

- **Related elements:** Y, Zr, O
- **Typical alloys:** W-Y₂O₃, W-ZrO₂, W-Y₂O₃-ZrO₂
- **Key compositions:** 0.5 wt.% Y₂O₃ + 0.5–2.0 wt.% ZrO₂
- **Microstructural changes:** Uniformly distributed oxide particles at grain boundaries and within grains. Y₂O₃ promotes cubic ZrO₂ formation. Particles pin boundaries and impede dislocation motion inside grains.
- **Property improvements:** Orowan strengthening calculated at 20.2 MPa for WYZ05 using Orowan-Ashby formula; contribution is only 3.85% of total UTS increment.
- **Risks and limitations:** Larger oxide particles increase stress concentration, reducing ductility. Excessive oxide content (ZrO₂ > 1.0 wt.%) drastically decreases total elongation.

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening / Fine-Grain Strengthening (Hall-Petch)

- **Related elements:** Y₂O₃, ZrO₂ (as grain-growth inhibitors)
- **Typical alloys:** All four W-Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ samples of this work
- **Key compositions:** WYZ05, WYZ10, WYZ20
- **Microstructural changes:** Grain size refined from 5.5 μm (WY) to 1.9–2.0 μm (all ZrO₂-containing samples). HAGBs dominate; grain orientation is random.
- **Property improvements:** GB strengthening calculated at 503.8 MPa, representing 96.15% of total UTS increment. Hall-Petch coefficient k = 1597. Reduced DBTT to 300–400 °C; NDT ~300 °C.
- **Risks and limitations:** HAGB-dominant microstructure may still contain strain incompatibility and crack sensitivity, as noted in Introduction. The paper states that doped oxides at GBs may introduce stress concentrations and crack initiation sites.

## Grain Refinement

- **Related elements:** ZrO₂, Y₂O₃
- **Typical alloys:** W-Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ ODS W alloys
- **Key compositions:** WYZ05 (0.5 wt.% ZrO₂) achieves 63.6% grain size reduction relative to WY.
- **Microstructural changes:** Oxide particles pin W grain boundaries during SPS; rapid SPS heating limits grain growth. W grain size positively correlated with second-phase particle size.
- **Property improvements:** Higher hardness (~490 HV vs 381.7 HV), higher UTS at 500 °C (525.2–586.2 MPa vs 404 MPa for WY05), lower DBTT (300–400 °C).
- **Risks and limitations:** Further ZrO₂ addition beyond 0.5 wt.% yields little additional grain refinement (grain size stable at 1.9–2.0 μm) but continues to reduce elongation.

## Second-Phase Strengthening

- **Related elements/compounds:** Y₂O₃, ZrO₂, c-ZrO₂
- **Typical alloys:** W-Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ ODS W alloys
- **Key compositions:** 0.5 wt.% Y₂O₃ + 0.5–2.0 wt.% ZrO₂
- **Microstructural changes:** Second-phase particles at GBs and in grains; particle sizes 0.5–1.6 μm; volume fractions 2.6–8.4%.
- **Property improvements:** UTS at 500 °C of 525.2–586.2 MPa; TE of 6.7–13.5%; hardness 486.0–496.7 HV; DBTT 300–400 °C.
- **Risks and limitations:** High second-phase content reduces ductility; particle size growth can cause premature failure via dislocation pile-up and stress concentration near particles.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Relative density:** All W-Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ samples achieved ~98% relative density; ZrO₂ addition increases density from ~96% (WY) to >98%.
2. **Grain refinement:** ZrO₂ addition significantly refines W grain size. WY grain size is 5.5 μm; WYZ05, WYZ10, and WYZ20 have grain sizes of 2.0, 2.0, and 1.9 μm, respectively. ZrO₂ inhibits grain growth during SPS.
3. **DBTT:** DBTT of W-0.5wt%Y₂O₃-(0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0)wt%ZrO₂ alloys determined by tensile testing is between 300 °C and 400 °C, lower than typical as-sintered W alloys (400–600 °C). NDT is ~300 °C.
4. **High-temperature strength:** Adding small amounts of ZrO₂ improves high-temperature tensile strength. WYZ05 at 500 °C has UTS = 525.2 MPa and TE = 13.5%, representing a good strength-ductility combination.
5. **Excessive ZrO₂ effect:** Adding more than 1.0 wt.% ZrO₂ greatly reduces elongation while tensile strength continues to increase. WYZ20 at 500 °C has UTS = 586.2 MPa but TE only 6.7%.
6. **Strengthening mechanism:** The improvement in strength is mainly attributed to fine-grain strengthening caused by Y₂O₃/ZrO₂ pinning grain boundaries to prevent grain growth (Δσ_gb = 503.8 MPa, 96.15% of total), with a smaller contribution from Orowan strengthening by dispersed intragranular oxide particles (Δσ_or = 20.2 MPa, 3.85%).
7. **Phase evolution:** Y₂O₃ promotes the formation of cubic ZrO₂ (c-ZrO₂) from monoclinic m-ZrO₂, and the particles are uniformly distributed at grain boundaries and inside grains.
8. **Low-temperature ductility advantage:** The low NDT (~300 °C) indicates that subsequent thermo-mechanical processing has a wider temperature window, allowing possible warm processing without cracking.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **No plastic deformation processing:** All samples were tested in the as-sintered state; no rolling, swaging, forging, or extrusion was performed. Therefore, the results apply to as-sintered ODS W alloys; further deformation processing may change grain size, dislocation density, and DBTT.
- **No oxidation testing:** Oxidation resistance and service stability in oxidizing environments are not assessed.
- **No radiation testing:** Although the application target is plasma-facing materials for fusion, no irradiation or radiation damage data are provided.
- **No measurement of O, N, C impurities:** The paper qualitatively mentions that impurities at grain boundaries influence grain-boundary strength, but no quantitative impurity measurements are reported.
- **Discrepancy in grain size values for WYZ05:** The abstract reports ~1.95 μm, the body text reports 2.0 μm, and the conclusions report ~2.7 μm. The most consistent values from Figs. 3–4 are 2.0 μm (WYZ05), 2.0 μm (WYZ10), and 1.9 μm (WYZ20). Users should be aware of this inconsistency in the original paper.
- **Discrepancy in Orowan volume fraction:** The Orowan calculation uses f = 3.48% for WYZ05, while the measured area fraction for WYZ05 is reported as 4.6%. The original paper does not explain this discrepancy.
- **Tensile measurements without extensometer:** Total elongation was measured by vernier caliper rather than an extensometer, which may affect precision of strain values and stress-strain curve shapes.
- **Limited temperature range for tensile tests:** Tensile tests were only performed at 300 °C, 400 °C, and 500 °C. The paper does not report high-temperature tensile data at 600 °C, 700 °C, or above for the new alloys, making direct comparison with pure W literature data at 700 °C limited.
- **No creep or fatigue data:** The paper does not evaluate creep resistance, rupture life, or fatigue behavior, though the W-Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ alloy is intended for high-temperature nuclear applications.
- **Microhardness only at room temperature:** Although the abstract mentions high-temperature hardness, the original paper does not provide specific high-temperature hardness values; the microhardness measurements appear to be at room temperature.
