# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Microstructure and mechanical properties of additive manufactured TiC-reinforced Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10 high-entropy alloy composites
- **Authors:** Zijian Wang, Fei Xing, Guojian Xu, Weijun Liu, Hongyou Bian
- **Journal:** Optics & Laser Technology
- **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.optlastec.2025.112870
- **Alloy system studied:** Non-equiatomic Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10 high-entropy alloy and its TiC-reinforced high-entropy alloy composite
- **Research objective:** To investigate the effect of 5 wt% TiC ceramic particles on the microstructure, mechanical properties, and friction/wear properties of non-equiatomic Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10 high-entropy alloy fabricated by laser melting deposition (LMD)
- **Material category:** High-entropy alloy composite (HEAC), non-equiatomic Fe-Cr-Co-Ni system, additively manufactured refractory-related alloy — **However, this is NOT a refractory high-temperature alloy system.** It is a Fe-Cr-Co-Ni high-entropy alloy composite fabricated by laser melting deposition (LMD, a directed energy deposition method). No W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Hf, Zr, Re, or other refractory elements are present in the matrix.

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies:
- **T0 sample:** Non-equiatomic Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10 high-entropy alloy (HEA) fabricated by laser melting deposition (LMD)
- **T5 sample:** 5 wt% TiC-reinforced Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10 high-entropy alloy composite (HEAC) fabricated by LMD

The matrix is a single FCC solid solution. After adding 5 wt% TiC, the composite forms an FCC + TiC dual-phase structure. The research focuses on grain refinement, strengthening mechanism analysis (Hall-Petch, dislocation, Orowan), and wear resistance improvement.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Phase constitution (from XRD and EBSD):

**T0 (no TiC):**
- Single FCC solid solution structure
- No second phase detected

**T5 (5 wt% TiC):**
- Composite structure: FCC solid solution + TiC particles
- TiC retained its original crystal structure; TiC particles not completely dissolved in the matrix
- XRD confirmed TiC diffraction peaks

## Grain structure and morphology:

**T0 sample:**
- Coarse columnar crystal structure
- Obvious directionality (preferential orientation)
- Growth of columnar crystals along (001) direction, parallel to building direction
- Texture strength: 26.88
- Average grain size: 138 μm
- Fraction of high-angle grain boundaries: 24.6%
- Some tiny holes (porosity) present

**T5 sample:**
- Composite structure consisting of: columnar dendrites, cellular dendrites, and a small amount of unmelted TiC particles
- Cellular dendrites predominantly distributed around unmelted TiC ceramic particles
- Significant grain refinement
- Average grain size: 57.06 μm
- Fraction of high-angle grain boundaries: 66.2%
- Texture strength: 11.24 (significantly reduced)
- More dispersed grain growth orientation
- No holes observed; improved density

## Second-phase particles (TiC) effect:

- TiC particles diffusely distributed within the HEA matrix
- TiC provides additional nucleation sites for grains
- TiC has high thermal conductivity, increasing the temperature gradient around it, inhibiting dendrite growth
- TiC pinning effect on grain boundaries inhibits grain boundary movement
- TiC particles reduce the temperature gradient (G) and increase the growth rate (R), promoting transition from columnar to columnar dendrites + cellular dendrites
- Mean precipitate radius (r̄): 70 μm (used in Orowan strengthening calculation)

## Elemental segregation:
- SEM/EDS mapping confirmed uniformity of elemental distribution in the HEA composite
- No significant elemental segregation reported

# Mechanical Properties

## Room-temperature tensile properties (tested along building direction):

|----------|-------------|----------------|-------------|
| Ultimate tensile strength (UTS) | 542 MPa | 635 MPa | +17.2% |
| Yield strength (YS) | 203 MPa | 254 MPa | +25.1% |
| Elongation | 55% | 25% | −54.5% |
| Hardness (HV₀.₅) | 165 | 204 | +23.6% |

**Testing conditions:**
- Universal testing machine: MTS Criterion Model 45
- Tensile direction parallel to building direction
- Each sample tested three times
- Vickers hardness: load 500 g, dwell time 10 s
- Round bar samples: diameter 6.5 mm, length 50 mm

## Tensile fracture morphology:
- **T0:** Dense number of dimples — typical ductile fracture
- **T5:** Many dimples still present; some unmelted TiC particles found on fracture surface; TiC achieved perfect bonding with metal matrix (did not flake off)

## Strength calculation (for T5):

| Strengthening component | Contribution (MPa) |
|------------------------|-------------------:|
| Hall-Petch strengthening (σ_Hall) | 153 |
| Dislocation strengthening (σ_Dis) | 15.4 |
| Orowan strengthening (σ_Orowan) | 58 |
| **Calculated total** | **226.4** |
| Actual measured YS | 254 |
| Discrepancy | 27.6 MPa |

# Key Conclusions

1. **Fabrication:** 5 wt% TiC/Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10 high-entropy alloy composites were successfully fabricated by laser melting deposition (LMD).

2. **Phase transformation:** The addition of 5 wt% TiC transformed the single FCC solid solution structure into an FCC + TiC composite structure.

3. **Microstructure transformation:** The original single columnar crystal structure evolved into a composite structure of columnar dendrites, cellular dendrites, and unmelted TiC particles. The G/R ratio decrease is responsible for this transition.

4. **Grain refinement:** TiC particles significantly refined the grain structure — average grain size decreased from 138 μm to 57.06 μm (58.6% reduction). TiC increased high-angle grain boundary fraction from 24.6% to 66.2% and reduced texture strength from 26.88 to 11.24.

5. **Mechanical property improvement:** Yield strength increased from 203 MPa to 254 MPa (+25.1%), tensile strength from 542 MPa to 635 MPa (+17.2%), and hardness from 165 HV₀.₅ to 204 HV₀.₅ (+23.6%).

6. **Ductility reduction:** Elongation decreased from 55% to 25% (54.5% reduction).

7. **Strengthening mechanisms:** The yield strength enhancement is attributed to synergistic effects of:
   - Hall-Petch strengthening: 153 MPa
   - Dislocation strengthening: 15.4 MPa
   - Orowan dispersion strengthening: 58 MPa
   - Calculated total: 226.4 MPa vs. measured 254 MPa (discrepancy attributed to enhanced lattice friction of non-equiatomic composition)

8. **Wear resistance:** TiC addition significantly improved room-temperature friction and wear performance; coefficient of friction reduced from 0.52 to 0.42 (19.2% reduction).

9. **Fracture behavior:** T5 retained ductile fracture features (dimples) with TiC particles remaining bonded to the matrix.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

## Limitations of the Original Paper:

1. **No high-temperature mechanical testing:** All mechanical tests were performed at room temperature only. No data for 800 °C, 1000 °C, or above.

2. **No oxidation resistance testing:** The paper does not investigate oxidation behavior, oxide layer formation, or high-temperature service stability.

3. **No creep testing:** No creep or stress-rupture data provided.

4. **Limited TiC composition range:** Only 0 wt% and 5 wt% TiC were studied. The influence of higher TiC content was not explored (noted by authors as a future direction).

5. **No post-processing heat treatment:** All samples characterized in as-printed condition; no effect of subsequent annealing or heat treatment investigated.

6. **Large TiC particle size:** The mean precipitate radius is 70 μm (micrometer-scale), which limits the efficiency of Orowan strengthening compared to nanoscale dispersoids.

7. **High ductility loss:** Adding 5 wt% TiC caused a 54.5% reduction in elongation (55% → 25%), a significant trade-off.

8. **No fracture toughness data:** Not tested.

9. **No density measurement:** Porosity qualitatively observed but not quantitatively measured.

10. **No strain rate data:** Strain rate for tensile testing not explicitly stated.

11. **No impurity analysis:** No ppm-level C, O, N, H content reported.

12. **Limited elemental segregation analysis:** EDS mapping confirmed uniformity but no quantitative segregation data provided.

## Applicable Scope:

- This paper is applicable to **non-refractory** Fe-Cr-Co-Ni-based high-entropy alloys and their ceramic-reinforced composites fabricated by laser melting deposition (LMD) at room temperature.
- The strengthening mechanism analysis (Hall-Petch, dislocation, Orowan) is general, but the specific values apply only to the studied alloy system.
- **This paper does not concern refractory high-temperature alloys** (no W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Hf, Zr, Re, or other refractory elements are present). The alloy operates as an FCC solid solution at room temperature and is not designed for high-temperature structural applications.
- The findings may inform the design of additively manufactured high-entropy alloy composites for applications such as aero-engine combustion chambers (as suggested by the authors), but the paper does not provide data to validate high-temperature service performance.
