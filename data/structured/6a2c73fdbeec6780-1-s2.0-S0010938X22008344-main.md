# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** Tailoring coating composition for the associated microstructural stability of a single-crystal superalloy: An experimental and simulation study
- **Authors:** Lilun Geng, Wenyue Zhao, Yi Ru, Yanling Pei, Shusuo Li, Shengkai Gong
- **Journal:** Corrosion Science
- **Year:** 2023 (Volume 211, Article 110916; available online 13 December 2022)
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.corsci.2022.110916
- **Alloy system studied:** High-Mo Ni₃Al-based single-crystal superalloy (IC21) with NiCrAlYSi and NiAlHfZr coatings
- **Research objective:** To make a trade-off between oxidation resistance and microstructural stability at 1100 °C for a high-Mo single-crystal superalloy coated by NiAlHfZr or NiCrAlYSi, and to uncover the mechanisms of Al and Cr affecting protective oxide scale formation, interdiffusion behaviors, and deleterious Mo-rich phase precipitation. Additionally, to establish an efficient route utilizing thermodynamic calculation and kinetics simulation to evaluate coating-associated microstructural stability and tailor coating compositions.
- **Material category:** Ni₃Al-based single-crystal superalloy (IC21) with protective metallic coatings (NiCrAlYSi and NiAlHfZr)

**Note:** The input paper focuses on Ni-based single-crystal superalloys and MCrAlY/NiAl coatings. It is not a refractory high-temperature alloy paper (Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, refractory high-entropy alloy, or additively manufactured refractory alloy). The following extraction is performed according to the instructions, but the paper's scope is Ni-based superalloys and coatings.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## IC21 Substrate Processing

- **Preparation method:** Directional high-rate solidification (HRS) technique, producing a single-crystal rod with [001] orientation.
- **Rod dimensions:** 15 mm diameter, 170 mm length.
- **Solution treatment:** 1310 °C/5 h + 1320 °C/5 h + 1325 °C/6 h + 1330 °C/6 h
- **Aging treatment:** 1150 °C/4 h + 870 °C/24 h
- **Resulting microstructure:** γ/γ′ structure

## Coating Deposition

- **Deposition method:** Multi-arc ion plating
- **Diffusion treatment:** Not conducted in this work (deliberately, to eliminate the effect of diffusion treatment process parameters on the initial coating state and explore the intrinsic characteristics of the interface).

## Oxidation Exposure

- **Oxidation temperature:** 1100 °C
- **Oxidation duration:** Up to 100 h (isothermal)
- **Furnace:** GSL1600X muffle furnace
- **Crucible preparation:** Alumina crucibles were pre-conditioned at 1200 °C until no mass change was observed.
- **Mass measurement:** Discontinuous weighing with an electronic balance (Sartorius, SQP) with a sensitivity of 10⁻⁵ g; each sample weighed three times.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## As-Deposited Coatings

- **NiAlHfZr coating:** γ + γ′ phases (from XRD). No new phases formed by Y, Si, Hf, Zr.
- **NiCrAlYSi coating:** γ/γ′ + β-NiAl/α-Cr phases (from XRD). The wide diffraction peaks suggest γ/γ′ or β/α exist as amorphous or nano-crystalline phases. No new phases formed by Y, Si, Hf, Zr.
- **Coating thickness:** NiAlHfZr ~40 μm; NiCrAlYSi ~30 μm.

## Microstructures After Oxidation at 1100 °C

### NiAlHfZr/IC21 System
Three main zones:
- **Zone I:** γ/γ′ structure region of coating
- **Zone II (IDZ):** Thin layer of γ′ phase beneath the coating/substrate interface
- **Zone III:** γ/γ′ structure region of substrate

No SRZ observed. A small amount of noodle-like NiMo phase precipitated in the γ/γ′ substrate. The NiMo phase EDS composition: Ni-8.27Al-3.46Cr-1.83Re-0.78Ta-25.02Mo (at%).

### NiCrAlYSi/IC21 System
Six characteristic zones:
- **Zone I (OZ, Outer Zone):** γ single phase without β phase
- **Zone II:** γ/β microstructure. β phase contains more Al than Cr; γ phase contains 28.39 at% Cr and 8.68 at% Al.
- **Zone III:** β-depletion zone at coating/substrate interface
- **Zone IV (IDZ):** γ layer with a small amount of α-Cr
- **Zone V (SRZ):** Many spherical and rod-shaped Mo-rich particles precipitated in γ′ phase
- **Zone VI:** Unaffected substrate with γ/γ′ structure

After 70 h, γ/γ′ phases appeared in the coating. TCP fraction in SRZ increased from 0.542% (at 9 h) to 2.247% (at 100 h).

### Mo-Rich Phase Structures (from thermodynamic calculations)
Depending on Al and Cr contents near the interface, three Mo-rich phase structures can precipitate:
- **BCC (α-Mo):** On the high-Al, low-Cr side
- **σ phase:** On the high-Cr side with high Al content
- **P phase:** On the high-Cr side with low Al content

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

## Oxidation Kinetics at 1100 °C

- **Initial stage (first few hours):** Rapid mass increase
- **After 10 h:**
  - NiAlHfZr: 0.74 ± 0.02 mg/cm²
  - NiCrAlYSi: 0.44 ± 0.06 mg/cm²
- **Steady oxidation stage (10–100 h):**
  - NiAlHfZr average oxidation rate: approximately three times faster than NiCrAlYSi
- **Final mass gain after 100 h:**
  - NiAlHfZr: 0.96 ± 0.02 mg/cm²
  - NiCrAlYSi: 0.59 ± 0.01 mg/cm²

## Oxide Scale Formation

- **NiCrAlYSi coating:** Continuous and dense Al₂O₃ film formed after 9 h; maintained without obvious spallation during 100 h.
- **NiAlHfZr coating:** Continuous and dense Al₂O₃ film formed after 100 h. NiAl₂O₄ spinel gradually formed after 70 h; oxide spallation occurred after 100 h.

### Reactions Involved in Mixed Oxide Scale Formation
1. 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃
2. 2Ni + O₂ → 2NiO
3. NiO + Al₂O₃ → NiAl₂O₄

- **Internal oxidation:** Observed in the surface layer of NiAlHfZr coating during initial oxidation stage; not observed in NiCrAlYSi coating (continuous oxide scale prevented further inward O₂ diffusion).

## Effect of Cr on Oxidation Resistance

- Cr promotes selective oxidation of Al, slowing mass gain in the early oxidation stage.
- Cr helps form a stable and dense α-Al₂O₃ scale.
- Cr oxides are unstable above 871 °C.

## Mo (Molybdenum)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** IC21 Ni₃Al-based single-crystal superalloy (substrate).
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 8.0–12.6 wt% (nominal for IC21); 10 wt% used in thermodynamic calculations.
- **Main Function:** Solid-solution strengthening (implied from "high-Mo strengthened" terminology); contributes to low density compared with conventional Ni-based superalloys.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Mo diffusion from substrate to coating causes evaporable Mo oxide (MoO₃ or MoO₂) formation on coating surface; Mo-rich phases precipitate at coating/substrate interface. Mo increases Al activity in Ni-Al-Mo system.
- **Property Effect:** Higher Mo content decreases oxidation resistance of superalloys. In this study, Mo-rich phases precipitate in the SRZ/IDZ:
  - NiMo phase (noodle-like) at NiAlHfZr/IC21 interface: Ni-8.27Al-3.46Cr-1.83Re-0.78Ta-25.02Mo at%
  - Mo-rich phase (spherical/rod-shaped) at NiCrAlYSi/IC21 interface: Ni-49.98Al-6.68Cr-15.57Re-3.05Ta-1.27Mo-23.45 at%
  - After 100 h oxidation, Mo outward diffusion from substrate: ~3.3 wt% for NiAlHfZr case; ~1.5 wt% for NiCrAlYSi case.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Decreases oxidation resistance; diffuses outward causing Mo oxide formation; promotes TCP phase precipitation at interfaces; contributes to SRZ formation.
- **Original Evidence:** Abstract, Introduction, Sections 3.3, 4.1, 4.2; Figures 7, 8; Table 2.

## Al (Aluminum)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** IC21 superalloy substrate and both coatings (NiAlHfZr: 10–14 wt%; NiCrAlYSi: 10–12 wt%; IC21: 6.5–8.2 wt%).
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 
  - IC21 substrate: 6.5–8.2 wt%
  - NiAlHfZr coating: 10–14 wt% nominal; ~12 wt% as-deposited
  - NiCrAlYSi coating: 10–12 wt% nominal; ~11 wt% as-deposited
  - Suggested optimized coating Al content: 8–10 wt%
- **Main Function:** Oxidation resistance via formation of stable, dense α-Al₂O₃ oxide scale. Also promotes γ′ phase formation.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** 
  - Forms protective Al₂O₃ scale
  - High Al content increases the fraction of γ′ phase, leading to dissolution of refractory elements from γ phase
  - β-NiAl→γ-Ni + [Al]; 3γ-Ni + [Al]→γ′-Ni₃Al reactions at interface
  - Al diffusion from coating to substrate promotes IDZ and SRZ formation
  - Precipitation of TCP phases is more sensitive to Al content than Cr content (for the same 2 wt% increase)
- **Property Effect:**
  - After 100 h at 1100 °C, NiAlHfZr coating still contains ~10 wt% Al; NiCrAlYSi coating Al content decreased from ~11 wt% to ~7 wt% after 70 h
  - Al in NiCrAlYSi coating can be lower than substrate Al content, yet chemical potential of Al in coating remains higher, leading to uphill diffusion
  - Critical Al content to avoid Mo-rich phase precipitation: Al < 5 wt% (in the substrate near interface)
  - P phase precipitates in Cr-free coatings only when Al content is 12 wt%
- **Potential Side Effects:** Excessive Al in coating promotes TCP precipitation; excessive inward Al diffusion degrades substrate microstructure.
- **Original Evidence:** Abstract, Introduction, Sections 3.2, 3.3, 4.1, 4.2; Figures 10, 11, 12, 13, 15; Table 2.

## Cr (Chromium)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** NiCrAlYSi coating (20–22 wt% nominal; ~22 wt% as-deposited); IC21 substrate (1.3–3.2 wt%).
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:**
  - NiCrAlYSi coating: 20–22 wt% nominal; ~22 wt% as-deposited
  - Suggested optimized coating Cr content: ≤20 wt%
- **Main Function:** Promotes selective oxidation of Al; promotes formation of early external α-Al₂O₃ scale; contributes to oxidation/corrosion resistance.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** 
  - Increases Al activity in Ni-Cr-Al-Mo system (verified by thermodynamic calculations)
  - Increases β/α phase formation (γ/γ′ + β + α-Cr phase constitution in as-deposited coating)
  - Cr diffusion from coating to substrate results in α-Cr precipitation at interface
  - Cr is a constituent element of the precipitated Mo-rich phases (P and σ phases)
  - Higher Cr content leads to increased amount of TCP phase precipitation
- **Property Effect:**
  - NiCrAlYSi coating average oxidation rate is about one-third that of NiAlHfZr coating at 1100 °C
  - After 10 h at 1100 °C: NiCrAlYSi mass gain 0.44 ± 0.06 mg/cm² vs NiAlHfZr 0.74 ± 0.02 mg/cm²
  - After 100 h: NiCrAlYSi mass gain 0.59 ± 0.01 mg/cm² vs NiAlHfZr 0.96 ± 0.02 mg/cm²
  - Continuous dense Al₂O₃ formed after 9 h on NiCrAlYSi coating without spallation for 100 h
  - Higher Cr content in coating promotes SRZ and TCP formation in substrate
- **Potential Side Effects:**
  - Cr oxides are unstable above 871 °C
  - Cr diffusion into substrate promotes TCP phase precipitation, SRZ formation, and microstructure degradation
  - Higher Cr content in coating increases Al activity, promoting Al interdiffusion and substrate damage
- **Original Evidence:** Abstract, Introduction, Sections 3.2, 3.3, 4.1, 4.2; Figures 11, 12, 13, 14; Table 2.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Oxidation resistance comparison at 1100 °C:** The NiCrAlYSi coating exhibited superior oxidation resistance compared with the NiAlHfZr coating. Final mass gains after 100 h: 0.59 ± 0.01 mg/cm² (NiCrAlYSi) vs 0.96 ± 0.02 mg/cm² (NiAlHfZr). The average oxidation rate of NiAlHfZr was approximately three times faster than NiCrAlYSi.

2. **Oxide scale behavior:** NiCrAlYSi formed continuous and dense Al₂O₃ after 9 h and maintained it without spallation for 100 h. NiAlHfZr required 100 h to form continuous dense Al₂O₃, with NiAl₂O₄ spinel forming after 70 h and oxide spallation after 100 h.

3. **Microstructural degradation:** The NiCrAlYSi/IC21 system showed more severe damage to the alloy substrate, including β-depletion zone, IDZ, and SRZ with Mo-rich TCP phase precipitation. TCP area fraction in SRZ increased from 0.542% (9 h) to 2.247% (100 h).

4. **Interdiffusion behavior:** Al diffused from both coatings into the IC21 substrate. Cr diffused from NiCrAlYSi into the substrate, causing α-Cr and Mo-rich phase precipitation. Mo diffused from substrate to coating: ~3.3 wt% (NiAlHfZr case) and ~1.5 wt% (NiCrAlYSi case) after 100 h.

5. **Thermodynamic condition for Mo-rich phase suppression:** When Al < 5 wt% and Cr < 20 wt% (near the interface), no Mo-rich precipitate forms in IC21 alloy at 1100 °C.

6. **Effects of Al and Cr on TCP precipitation:**
   - P phase is the main TCP phase.
   - α phase forms only when Al + Cr > 30 wt% in the coating.
   - TCP precipitation is more sensitive to Al content than Cr content (for same 2 wt% increase).
   - Higher Al content increases γ′ fraction, dissolving refractory elements from γ.
   - Cr is a constituent element of the precipitated Mo-rich phases.

7. **Coating composition recommendation:** For high-Mo Ni₃Al-based single-crystal alloys like IC21, a NiCrAlYSi coating design with **low Cr (≤20 wt%)** and **moderate Al (8–10 wt%)** is recommended to balance oxidation resistance and microstructural stability. Al content should not exceed 12 wt%.

8. **Simulation validation:** The homogenization model with Thermo-Calc/DICTRA (TCNi10 and MobNi5 databases) successfully reproduced experimental phase types and compositions, with boundary movement within ~10 μm of experimental observations at 1100 °C after 100 h.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **No mechanical property testing:** This paper does not report any mechanical properties (yield strength, tensile strength, creep, hardness, fracture toughness, etc.) of the coated systems. The impact of microstructural degradation on mechanical performance is only inferred from prior literature.

2. **No diffusion treatment:** The study deliberately omitted post-deposition diffusion treatment to isolate intrinsic interface behavior. Industrial practice typically includes diffusion treatment; its effect is not evaluated here.

3. **Reactive element effects not analyzed:** Si, Y, Hf, and Zr were excluded from diffusion analyses due to limited EDS accuracy. Their roles in oxidation resistance enhancement (reactive element effect) are not quantitatively addressed.

4. **Isothermal oxidation only:** The study focuses on isothermal oxidation at 1100 °C. Cyclic oxidation behavior, which is more relevant for turbine blade applications, is not studied.

5. **Single oxidation temperature:** Only 1100 °C was tested. Validation at other temperatures is not provided.

6. **Simulation simplifications:** The diffusion of Si, Y, Hf, and Zr was not considered in simulations. The Wiener upper bound (rule of mixtures) was used, assuming diffusion only in γ, γ′, and β phases. Boundary condition choices (constant flux or constant surface activity) can affect results for specific cases.

7. **Composition recommendation needs verification:** The suggested coating composition (Al 8–10 wt%, Cr ≤20 wt%) is based on thermodynamic and kinetic calculations. The authors explicitly note: "further experimental verification should be carried out."

8. **The as-deposited NiCrAlYSi coating composition (Ni-22Cr-11Al wt%) is outside the suggested range**, which explains the observed TCP precipitation and motivates further composition tuning.

9. **Cr oxide instability above 871 °C** is acknowledged as a fundamental limitation for high-temperature applications.

10. **Applicability beyond IC21:** The specific thermodynamic thresholds (Al < 5 wt%, Cr < 20 wt%) apply to the IC21 alloy system with approximately 10 wt% Mo. Generalization to other high-Mo Ni₃Al-based or Ni-based superalloys requires separate thermodynamic evaluation.
