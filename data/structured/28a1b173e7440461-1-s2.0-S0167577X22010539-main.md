# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** A nickel-base superalloy with refined microstructures and excellent mechanical properties prepared by selective laser melting
- **Authors:** Kai Chang, Yi Tan, Liang Ma, Xiaogang You, Jun Lv, Bin Liu, Pengting Li
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.matlet.2022.132700
- **Alloy System Studied:** GH4099 nickel-base superalloy (γ′–Ni₃(Al, Ti) strengthened)
- **Research Objective:** To prepare GH4099 alloy by selective laser melting (SLM) with refined microstructures, to retard hot cracking induced by the brittle γ–γ′ eutectic phase, and to achieve excellent mechanical properties through suitable heat treatment.
- **Material Category:** Nickel-base superalloy (additively manufactured / SLM-processed).  
  **Note:** This paper is NOT a refractory high-temperature alloy (Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloy). It is a Ni-based superalloy. The following extraction reflects this accurately.

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

- Alloy: GH4099 (a γ′–Ni₃(Al, Ti) strengthened Ni-base superalloy)
- Working temperature capacity: up to 1000 °C for aerospace components
- Additive manufacturing route: Selective Laser Melting (SLM)
- Key strengthening elements in GH4099: W, Mo, Al, Ti, Co
- Research problem addressed: γ–γ′ eutectic phase formation in interdendritic regions and on grain boundaries during DED/WAAM/LSF processing causes high hot cracking susceptibility; SLM is used to suppress this.

# Alloy Composition

- The paper does **not** provide nominal composition of GH4099 powder in at.% or wt.%.
- It states:
  - “GH4099 is a typical γ′– Ni₃(Al, Ti) strengthened superalloy.”
  - “GH4099 alloy exhibits supreme thermal stability due to the strengthening effect of W, Mo, Al, Ti, and Co elements.”
- Therefore, the elements involved include: **Ni (matrix), Al, Ti (γ′ formers), W, Mo, Co (solid-solution/strengthening), Cr (from observed Cr₂₃C₆ / M₂₃C₆ carbides), C (from M₂₃C₆ carbides)**.
- Specific at.%, wt.%, and impurity levels such as C, O, N, H are **not explicitly stated in the original paper**.
- Multiple alloy samples are not separately named by composition variants; the same GH4099 composition is studied in three states: as-deposited, solution-treated, and solution + aging treated.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- Powder particle size: 15–45 μm
- SLM equipment: LiM-X400A (Tianjin Radium Laser Technology Co., Ltd.)
- SLM process parameters:
  - Layer thickness: 40 μm
  - Laser power: 295 ± 5 W
  - Spot diameter: 90 μm
  - Scanning speed: 960 mm/s
  - Laser scanning path between adjacent layers: deflected by 60°
  - Protective gas: circulating argon
- Post-processing heat treatment:
  - **Solution treatment:** 1115 ± 5 °C for 2 h, followed by water cooling
  - **Aging treatment:** after solution treatment, aged at 800 °C for 8 h, then air quenched
- Etching reagent: 1 ml HF + 10 ml HNO₃ + 50 ml HCl + 39 ml H₂O
- Characterization methods:
  - FESEM: ZEISS SUPRA55
  - TEM: JEOL JEM-2100F
  - EBSD IPF maps, pole figures
- Tensile testing:
  - Universal testing machine: WDW
  - Strain rate: 1 mm/min
  - Test temperatures: room temperature, 800 °C, 900 °C

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **As-deposited condition:**
  - Fine dendrites and cellular dendrites distributed in the molten pool
  - Dendrite/cellular dendrite spacing: about 500 nm (much smaller than DED GH4099 with about 4 μm dendrite spacing)
  - Second dendrite arms are underdeveloped
  - Epitaxial growth columnar grains observed
  - Rotating cube texture observed, strongest texture intensity max = 16.05, with (100) crystal planes parallel to deposition direction
  - Preferential growth direction: 〈100〉; heat flow during SLM leads to 45°–60° angle between substrate/primary dendrite and previously deposited layer
  - Large number of statically stored dislocations observed
  - Precipitation of γ′ phase inhibited due to instantaneous melting and rapid cooling
  - Spherical Cr₂₃C₆ carbides observed, with zone axis [237], size about 25 nm

- **After solution treatment (1115 ± 5 °C / 2 h / water cooling):**
  - Dendritic morphology disappears
  - Twin boundaries appear in grains
  - Carbides incompletely dissolved
  - Columnar grains replaced by equiaxed grains via static recrystallization
  - Crystallographic texture intensity decreases
  - Small-sized primary carbides (M₂₃C₆) dissolved into matrix
  - Larger M₂₃C₆ at grain boundaries transform from spherical to blocky shape due to decrease in surface energy and element aggregation
  - Cr₂₃C₆ carbide size about 50 nm

- **After solution + aging (800 °C / 8 h / air quench):**
  - Gray orientation becomes more random
  - Maximum texture intensity of aged sample: 3.58
  - Secondary Cr₂₃C₆ carbides observed
  - Carbide size about 50 nm, slight change compared with solid solution
  - Zone axis of Cr₂₃C₆: [241]
  - Large number of spherical/ellipsoidal γ′ precipitates observed
  - γ′ precipitate size: less than 10 nm
  - Zone axis of matrix: [110]
  - Coherent relationship between γ′ precipitates and matrix confirmed by FFT and IFFT

- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed:**
  - Dislocation strengthening (statically stored dislocations from non-equilibrium solidification)
  - Grain refinement via SLM rapid cooling and static recrystallization after solution treatment
  - Precipitation strengthening from fine γ′ nano-precipitates after aging
  - Carbide strengthening/effects from Cr₂₃C₆ / M₂₃C₆
  - Coherent distortion zone strengthening from γ′ precipitates

# Mechanical Properties

- **Room-temperature tensile properties:**
  - As-deposited SLM GH4099:
    - Tensile strength: 1217.8 MPa
    - Yield strength: 783.1 MPa
    - Elongation: 35.6%
  - The 1217.8 MPa value is reported as ultimate tensile strength (UTS) in the abstract and conclusion.  
    **Careful note:** In the Conclusion section, the paper states: “The tensile strength, yield strength of SLM GH4099 alloy at room temperature are 1217.8 and 955.0 MPa, respectively.” This is inconsistent with the Results section, which gives yield strength as 783.1 MPa and elongation 35.6% at room temperature. The 955.0 MPa value in the Conclusion appears to conflict with the main results; the original paper does not explicitly clarify this discrepancy. Based on the Results section, the room-temperature values are: UTS 1217.8 MPa, YS 783.1 MPa, elongation 35.6%.

- **High-temperature tensile properties:**
  - Tensile strength at 800 °C: Not explicitly stated as a standalone numerical value in the extracted text.
  - Tensile strength at 900 °C: Not explicitly stated as a standalone numerical value in the extracted text.
  - However, the paper shows high-temperature data in Fig. 3(b) and compares with other additively manufactured superalloys in Fig. 3(d). The exact 800 °C and 900 °C strength values are **not explicitly stated in the original paper text**.

- **Comparison with forged sample:**
  - Forged GH4099: UTS 980–1079 MPa, YS 686–726 MPa
  - SLM GH4099 values are better than forged sample.

- **Qualitative tensile/fracture observations:**
  - As-deposited: large number of statistically stored dislocations gives considerable strengthening effect
  - After solution treatment: decrease in dislocation density leads to decrease in strength and increase in ductility; dimples are deep and relatively large, consistent with higher plasticity
  - After aging: dispersed γ′ phase forms coherent distortion zone with matrix and increases hindrance of dislocations; strength rises, plasticity decreases; dimples appear but are relatively small
  - SLM GH4099 shows better comprehensive mechanical properties at room and high temperature compared with DED alloys because of finer microstructure (grain, dendrite structure, strengthening phase)

- **Hardness, elastic modulus, density, fracture toughness, creep, rupture life:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

## [Al + Ti]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** GH4099 Ni-base superalloy (SLM-processed)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Main Function:** γ′ phase formation; precipitation strengthening; high-temperature strength.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Al and Ti combine with Ni to form γ′ (Ni₃(Al, Ti)). After aging at 800 °C for 8 h, a large number of spherical or ellipsoidal γ′ precipitates form, with size less than 10 nm. The coherent relationship between γ′ precipitates and the matrix is confirmed. The γ′ precipitates form a coherent distortion zone with the matrix and increase hindrance of dislocations.
- **Property Effect:** After aging, dispersed γ′ phase increases strength and decreases plasticity. The exact numerical contribution of Al+Ti to strength is not specified. Room-temperature UTS after aging: 1217.8 MPa; YS reported as 783.1 MPa in Results but 955.0 MPa in Conclusion (inconsistent in original paper).
- **Potential Side Effects:** Enrichment of Al and Ti on grain boundary in LSF GH4099 weakens the grain boundary, which results in crack nucleation during solidification. In SLM, rapid cooling inhibits γ′ precipitation during solidification, reducing hot cracking susceptibility. Cracking in DED/WAAM is caused by γ–γ′ eutectic phase formation.
- **Original Evidence:** “The enrichment of Al and Ti elements on the grain boundary in the LSF GH4099 alloy weakens the grain boundary, which results in crack nucleation during the solidification process.” Also: “A large number of spherical or ellipsoidal γ′ precipitates are observed. The size of the γ′ precipitates is less than 10 nm.”

## [γ′ phase (Ni₃(Al,Ti))]

- **Applicable Alloy System:** GH4099 Ni-base superalloy (SLM-processed)

# Key Conclusions

1. GH4099 alloy with refined microstructures and excellent mechanical properties was prepared by SLM.
2. SLM effectively reduces element segregation and eliminates the low-melting phase (γ–γ′ eutectic), retarding cracking during solidification.
3. After aging at 800 °C for 8 h, a large number of γ′ nanoprecipitates (size <10 nm) form, contributing greatly to the excellent mechanical properties.
4. Room-temperature properties of SLM GH4099 (aging state):

   - Tensile strength: 1217.8 MPa
   - Yield strength: Inconsistent in original paper — Results section gives 783.1 MPa; Conclusion gives 955.0 MPa
   - Elongation: 35.6%

5. SLM GH4099 shows better mechanical properties than forged GH4099 (forged UTS: 980–1079 MPa, YS: 686–726 MPa).
6. SLM alloy has better tensile strength than DED alloy due to finer microstructure (grain, dendrite, strengthening phase).
7. This study provides theoretical guidance for high-performance GH4099 superalloy component fabrication by SLM.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy system mismatch:** This paper studies a Ni-base superalloy (GH4099), not a refractory high-temperature alloy (Nb-W, Mo-W, refractory HEA/MPEA, ODS refractory alloy, etc.). Therefore, any retrieval in the context of refractory metal alloys should note this difference.
- **Composition not provided:** The paper does not provide the nominal chemical composition of GH4099 powder. Element concentrations (wt.% or at.%) for Ni, Cr, Co, W, Mo, Al, Ti, C are not reported.
- **Numerical inconsistency:** The paper contains an inconsistency between the yield strength values reported in the Results (783.1 MPa) and the Conclusion (955.0 MPa) for room-temperature SLM GH4099 alloy. The original paper does not clarify this discrepancy.
- **High-temperature property values:** Although tensile tests were performed at 800 °C and 900 °C, the specific tensile strength values at these temperatures are not given in the extracted text.
- **Oxidation resistance:** Not studied or reported.
- **Creep, fracture toughness, hardness, modulus, density:** Not reported.
- **Microstructural quantification:** Grain size distribution is shown in Fig. 1(l), but specific grain size values are not given in the text.
- **Carbide volume fraction, number density, and spatial distribution:** Not quantitatively reported.
- **Element segregation quantification:** Not measured quantitatively; only qualitative comparison between SLM and DED is made.
- **Applicable scope:** The conclusions are specific to SLM-processed GH4099 Ni-base superalloy with the reported SLM parameters and the solution+aging heat treatment (1115 °C/2 h/water cooling + 800 °C/8 h/air quench).
