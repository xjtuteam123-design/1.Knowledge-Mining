# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Microstructure and properties of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic high entropy alloy manufactured by selective laser melting
- **Authors**: Tao Yu, Guangming Zhou, Yuanguang Cheng, Fuchao Hu, Tianfan Jiang, Tao Sun, Yifu Shen, Yiming Zhou, Junping Li
- **Journal**: Optics & Laser Technology
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.optlastec.2023.109396
- **Alloy system studied**: AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic high-entropy alloy (EHEA)
- **Research objective**: To investigate the effect of different laser volume energy density (VED) during selective laser melting on relative density, microstructure, mechanical properties, and high-temperature oxidation properties of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA.
- **Material category**: Eutectic high-entropy alloy; additively manufactured refractory/transition-metal high-entropy alloy (note: this alloy system contains Al, Co, Cr, Fe, Ni—not a refractory-metal HEA in the Nb/W/Mo/Ta sense, but is included here as an additively manufactured HEA with BCC + FCC eutectic structure).

# Alloy System and Research Object

The study focuses on the AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic high-entropy alloy, prepared by selective laser melting (SLM), a laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) additive manufacturing process. The nominal composition is a near-eutectic multi-principal-element alloy that solidifies with a coupled FCC + BCC lamellar eutectic microstructure.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Manufacturing method**: Selective laser melting (SLM), also referred to as laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF).
- **Machine/equipment**: Nd:YAG laser with a maximum power of 500 W and a laser spot diameter of 110 μm. Substrate: Q235 steel.
- **Preheating**: Powder preheated at 80 °C; substrate preheated at 150 °C.
- **Protective atmosphere**: Argon with oxygen content below 500 ppm.
- **Process parameter matrix**:
  - VED = 75 J/mm³: laser power 240 W, scanning speed 1000 mm/s, hatch spacing 0.08 mm, layer thickness 0.04 mm.
  - VED = 109.4 J/mm³: laser power 280 W, scanning speed 800 mm/s, hatch spacing 0.08 mm, layer thickness 0.04 mm.
  - VED = 125 J/mm³: laser power 320 W, scanning speed 800 mm/s, hatch spacing 0.08 mm, layer thickness 0.04 mm.
  - VED = 187.5 J/mm³: laser power 360 W, scanning speed 600 mm/s, hatch spacing 0.08 mm, layer thickness 0.04 mm.
- **Scanning strategy**: 67° rotation between consecutive layers (layer N and N+1).
- **Post-deposition heat treatment**: None reported. All testing was performed on as-printed samples.
- **Arc melting, vacuum melting, induction melting, powder metallurgy, mechanical alloying, hot pressing, spark plasma sintering, HIP, homogenization, rolling, forging, annealing, aging**: Not applied in this study, although the paper cites literature that used casting, arc melting, rapid solidification, spark plasma sintering, and rolling/annealing for the same alloy for comparison.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phases**: Dual-phase FCC + BCC. XRD indicates that the alloy consists of FCC and BCC structures. The literature cited describes the BCC phase as an ordered B2 phase in this alloy; however, in this paper the terms “BCC” and “FCC” are used without detailed ordering analysis.
- **Microstructure type**: Lamellar eutectic structure with nanosized interlamellar spacing.
- **Lamellar spacing (λ)**:
  - VED = 75 J/mm³: 320 nm
  - VED = 109.4 J/mm³: 298 nm
  - VED = 125 J/mm³: 226 nm
  - VED = 187.5 J/mm³: 186 nm
- **Grain size**: When VED = 187.5 J/mm³, the average lamellar spacing is 186 nm, which is described as much smaller than that obtained by casting and finer than the ~600 nm ultrafine grains obtained by arc melting + cold rolling + annealing at 800 °C (Wani et al.) or the λ = 215 nm obtained by L-PBF (Ren et al.).
- **Cellular structure**: Observed near melt pool boundaries at lower VED; the area fraction of cellular structure decreases with increasing VED and is almost invisible at 187.5 J/mm³. The cellular structure is interpreted as an evolution from the lamellar eutectic, analogous to transformation from lamellar pearlite to granular pearlite.
- **Elemental segregation**: EDS surface scans and line scans on the VED = 187.5 J/mm³ sample show uniform elemental distribution without obvious Al-Ni segregation, which is attributed to the very short melting/solidification times in SLM.
- **Texture and grain orientation**:
  - At lower VED, the FCC phase shows predominantly ⟨111⟩ orientation along the build direction.
  - At VED = 187.5 J/mm³, the orientation changes predominantly to ⟨001⟩ in the FCC phase.
  - The BCC phase shows mainly ⟨001⟩ and ⟨101⟩ textures, with the weakest texture in ⟨111⟩.
  - Both FCC and BCC phases show strong fiber texture on the {100} plane.
  - Texture strength is weakest at VED = 187.5 J/mm³, indicating weak anisotropy of mechanical properties.
- **Grain-boundary features and precipitate particles**: Not explicitly characterized. No carbide, boride, or oxide precipitates were reported in the as-built microstructure. No dispersion strengthening or ODS phase is discussed for this alloy.

# Mechanical Properties

- **Test temperature**: Room temperature for hardness and tensile tests.
- **Loading mode**: Tensile, uniaxial; microhardness indentation; tensile loading rate 1 mm/min.
- **Microhardness**:
  - VED = 75 J/mm³: not explicitly given as a single value; low due to porosity/defects.
  - VED = 109.4 J/mm³: not explicitly given as a single value.
  - VED = 125 J/mm³: not explicitly given as a single value.
  - VED = 187.5 J/mm³: average microhardness = 479.04 HV.
  - Microhardness increases with increasing VED and hardness fluctuation decreases.
- **Tensile properties**:
  - VED = 75 J/mm³: premature fracture; very low strength and ductility due to pores/unfused defects.
  - VED = 109.4 J/mm³: yield strength = 1199 MPa; ultimate tensile strength = 1421 MPa.
  - VED = 125 J/mm³: values not explicitly given in the extracted text.
  - VED = 187.5 J/mm³: yield strength = 1151 MPa; ultimate tensile strength = 1364 MPa; elongation at fracture = 16.37 %.
- **Compressive properties**: Not reported in the extracted text.
- **High-temperature strength (800–1600 °C)**: Not reported. The only high-temperature test is oxidation at 850 °C.
- **Fracture toughness**: Not reported.
- **Creep performance/rupture life**: Not reported.
- **Strain rate**: Not reported; only loading rate of 1 mm/min is given.
- **Hardness comparison**: SLM-AlCoCrFeNi2.1 microhardness is significantly higher than that of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 prepared by rapid solidification and spark plasma sintering (SPS).
- **Fracture morphology**:
  - VED = 75 J/mm³: fracture surface contains pores of ~20 μm, which reduce the effective stress-bearing area and act as crack initiation sites.
  - VED = 109.4 J/mm³: instantaneous brittle fracture zones observed, attributed to residual stress.
  - VED = 187.5 J/mm³: smaller and more numerous dimples, evenly distributed; ductile fracture.
- **Elastic modulus**: Not reported.
- **Density**: Relative density values only. VED = 75 J/mm³: 97.00 %; VED = 125 J/mm³: 99.28 %; VED = 187.5 J/mm³: 99.51 %.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Test condition**: Isothermal oxidation at 850 °C (1123 K) in air for 100 h in a GSL-1800X vacuum tube furnace. Ramp rate: 8 °C/min. Weight gain measured at 5, 10, 20, 35, 55, 75, and 100 h.
- **Oxidation kinetics**:
  - Early-stage oxidation rate (first 5 h) for VED = 75 J/mm³ sample: 98.68 μg/cm²·h.
  - For other samples, the oxidation rate levels off after ~35 h.
  - Oxidation kinetics follow a parabolic law.
  - Oxidation rate index, n:
    - VED = 75 J/mm³: n = 6 (sixth power parabolic law), R² = 0.99
    - VED = 109.4 J/mm³: n = 6, R² = 0.96
    - VED = 125 J/mm³: n = 8 (eighth power parabolic law), R² = 0.98
    - VED = 187.5 J/mm³: n = 8, R² = 0.97
  - All n values are greater than 2, indicating excellent oxidation resistance.
- **Oxide layer type**:
  - Predominantly Al2O3 inner layer and Cr2O3 outer layer.
  - At low VED (75 and 109.4 J/mm³), spinel phases NiCr2O4 and NiFe2O4 are also detected.
  - At VED = 187.5 J/mm³, no spinel phases are detected.
- **Oxide formation order** (based on Gibbs free energy considerations cited from literature): Al2O3 < Cr2O3 < FeO < Fe3O4 < NiO < Fe2O3, so the ability to form oxides is Al > Cr > Fe > Ni > Co.
- **Effect of VED on oxidation**:
  - Low VED samples have more porosity; O²⁻ ions penetrate into the matrix and form oxides within pores, aggravating oxidation.
  - Higher VED leads to a more uniform, continuous, dense oxide film with less spallation.
  - Grain refinement at high VED reduces the critical concentration for external oxidation of Cr and Al according to Wagner’s theory, promoting formation of protective Cr2O3 and Al2O3 films.
- **Spinel effect on Pilling-Bedworth ratio**: NiCr2O4 and NiFe2O4 spinels increase the P-B ratio, increasing internal stress and promoting oxide film spallation. This is more serious at low VED.
- **Potential issues**: At low VED, porosity-driven internal oxidation, spinel phase formation, and oxide spallation are identified. At high VED, the oxide film is dense and protective. No discussion of nitrogen embrittlement, oxygen embrittlement at grain boundaries, or DBTT in this paper.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Al + Cr (with Ni)

- **Element or Element Combination**: Al + Cr (with Ni)
- **Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic high-entropy alloy prepared by selective laser melting.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Nominal formula AlCoCrFeNi2.1; exact at.% or wt.% not stated in the original paper.
- **Main Function**: Formation of protective oxide layers during high-temperature oxidation; Al2O3 inner layer and Cr2O3 outer layer.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: At 850 °C, Al and Cr oxidize preferentially. Al2O3 forms on the matrix surface first; as oxidation progresses, Cr2O3 granular oxide forms on top of the Al2O3 layer. Grain refinement at high VED reduces the critical concentration for external oxidation of Al and Cr, promoting continuous protective oxide scale.
- **Property Effect**: Oxidation rate index n = 8 at VED = 187.5 J/mm³; dense and continuous oxide film; low spallation. At lower VED, spinel formation (NiCr2O4, NiFe2O4) is observed due to incomplete/dense oxide scale.
- **Potential Side Effects**: At low VED with high porosity, protective oxide scale is discontinuous and spinel phases form, increasing P-B ratio and promoting spallation. The original paper does not separately discuss Alloying-induced room-temperature ductility loss due to Al or Cr.
- **Original Evidence**: Section 3.5, Figs. 17–19, Eq. (6)–(7); statements “the composition of the oxide film is mainly Al2O3” and “dense Cr2O3 and Al2O3 oxide films are formed.”

## Fe + Ni

- **Element or Element Combination**: Fe + Ni
- **Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic high-entropy alloy prepared by SLM.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Nominal formula AlCoCrFeNi2.1; exact composition not restated.
- **Main Function**: Matrix elements participating in spinel oxide formation during high-temperature oxidation.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: At low VED, Ni and Fe diffuse upward through Al2O3 and Cr2O3 layers and react with oxygen to form spinel oxides. NiO reacts with Cr2O3 to form NiCr2O4 and with Fe2O3 to form NiFe2O4.
- **Property Effect**: Spinel phases increase the Pilling-Bedworth ratio and internal stress of the oxide film, leading to oxide spallation. This is observed at VED = 75 and 109.4 J/mm³.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Deterioration of oxidation resistance at low VED due to spinel-induced spallation.
- **Original Evidence**: Eq. (6)–(7), Figs. 17 and 19; text in Section 3.5.

## Al

- **Element or Element Combination**: Al
- **Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA (SLM).
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper beyond the nominal formula.
- **Main Function**: Formation of protective Al2O3 oxide layer; also cited as having the lowest boiling point (2327 °C) and lowest heat of evaporation (291.4 kJ/mol) among the elements, leading to possible evaporation at high VED and hence shift of XRD peaks.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: During rapid solidification/SLM, Al and Ni do not enrich because melting/solidification is very fast; this suppresses the Al-Ni segregation seen in conventional casting. During oxidation, Al2O3 forms first because Al has the strongest oxide-forming ability. Little Al2O3 is detected on the outside of the oxide film because the Al content is low and Al diffusion to the oxide interface is slow.
- **Property Effect**: Contributes to oxidation resistance by forming Al2O3; shifts XRD diffraction peaks at high VED; microstructural uniformity is improved compared to casting because Al–Ni segregation is suppressed.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Possible evaporation of Al at high energy density; low Al content limits the ability to form a continuous outer Al2O3 layer.
- **Original Evidence**: Section 3.2 (Bragg’s law discussion, Al evaporation); Section 3.3 (EDS); Section 3.5 (oxide formation order).

## Ni

- **Element or Element Combination**: Ni
- **Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA (SLM).
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not explicitly stated.
- **Main Function**: Matrix element; participates in spinel formation (NiCr2O4, NiFe2O4) during oxidation.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Ni has high concentration; NiO is continuously generated and reacts with Cr2O3 and Fe2O3 to form spinel oxides.
- **Property Effect**: At low VED, Ni diffusion through oxide layers leads to spinel phase formation and associated oxide spallation.
- **Potential Side Effects**: At high VED with a dense oxide film, Ni diffusion and spinel formation are suppressed.
- **Original Evidence**: Eq. (6)–(7), Section 3.5.

## Cr

- **Element or Element Combination**: Cr
- **Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA (SLM).
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not explicitly stated.
- **Main Function**: Formation of Cr2O3 outer oxide layer; spinel formation with NiO and Fe oxides.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Cr2O3 forms on top of Al2O3 as oxidation progresses. At high VED, refined grains reduce the critical Cr concentration for external oxidation.
- **Property Effect**: Dense Cr2O3 layer at high VED improves oxidation resistance; at low VED, Cr participates in NiCr2O4 spinel formation, increasing spallation tendency.
- **Potential Side Effects**: None explicitly discussed for room-temperature properties.
- **Original Evidence**: Sections 3.5, Figs. 17–18, Eq. (6).

## Co

- **Element or Element Combination**: Co
- **Applicable Alloy System**: AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA (SLM).
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not explicitly stated.
- **Main Function**: Matrix element; has the lowest oxide-forming ability among the principal elements because Co oxide formation requires higher temperature.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Gibbs free energy for oxide formation is more negative for Al2O3, Cr2O3, FeO, Fe3O4, NiO, Fe2O3 than for Co oxides; hence Co does not form a stable oxide at 850 °C under these conditions.
- **Property Effect**: Not directly reported.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not specified.
- **Original Evidence**: Section 3.5, statement “the oxidation temperature of Co element is higher” and oxide formation order.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Fine-Grain Strengthening / Grain Refinement (Hall-Petch)

- **Related elements**: Not specifically element-based; driven by SLM process conditions.
- **Typical alloys**: SLM-AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA.
- **Key compositions**: AlCoCrFeNi2.1.
- **Microstructural changes**: Lamellar eutectic spacing decreases with increasing VED from 320 nm to 186 nm; finer lamellae correspond to smaller grain size. Cellular regions at low VED disappear at high VED.
- **Property improvements**: Microhardness increases to 479.04 HV at VED = 187.5 J/mm³; tensile strength reaches 1364 MPa with 16.37 % elongation.
- **Risks and limitations**: At too high VED, Al evaporation may occur; at too low VED, porosity and unfused defects dominate and degrade properties.
- **Original Evidence**: Section 3.3 and 3.4; Table 1; Fig. 12, Fig. 13.

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- The paper does not provide a detailed quantitative discussion of solid-solution strengthening in this particular SLM-processed alloy, except for general reference to lattice distortion from rapid solidification and the high-entropy effect in the introduction. 
- Original evidence: Section 3.2 (lattice distortion from laser printing), and introductory references to lattice distortion effect.

## Precipitation / Dispersion Strengthening

- Not applicable to this alloy in the as-printed condition. No precipitates such as carbides, borides, or oxides are reported. The strengthening effect is attributed mainly to fine lamellar spacing and relative density.

## Second-Phase Strengthening

- The dual-phase FCC + BCC lamellar structure is key. The paper references the dual-phase heterogeneous lamellae (DPHL) concept from Shi et al. and the coupled FCC/B2 lamellar structure, but does not quantify the individual phase contributions to strength in the SLM samples. Fine lamellar spacing is the dominant microstructural factor.

# Key Conclusions

1. Increasing VED during SLM improves surface quality and relative density of AlCoCrFeNi2.1. Relative density reaches a maximum of 99.51 % at VED = 187.5 J/mm³.
2. The SLM-processed alloy exhibits a dual-phase FCC + BCC lamellar eutectic structure. With increasing VED, FCC peak intensities increase while BCC peak intensities decrease, and the lamellar spacing decreases from 320 nm to 186 nm, indicating grain refinement.
3. At VED = 187.5 J/mm³, the best combination of properties is obtained: average microhardness = 479.04 HV, yield strength = 1151 MPa, tensile strength = 1364 MPa, elongation = 16.37 %.
4. Increasing VED improves high-temperature oxidation resistance at 850 °C. The oxidation rate index increases to 8 at high VED, indicating excellent oxidation resistance. The oxide scale consists mainly of inner Al2O3 and outer Cr2O3, with spinel phases (NiCr2O4 and NiFe2O4) appearing only at low VED.
5. The improvement in oxidation resistance with increasing VED is attributed to improved relative density and grain refinement, which promote formation of continuous, dense protective oxide films.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- The alloy studied is AlCoCrFeNi2.1, a eutectic high-entropy alloy based on Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni. It is not a refractory-metal alloy such as Nb-W, Mo-W, or a refractory high-entropy alloy containing W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf, Ti, or V. Therefore, direct extrapolation to refractory high-temperature alloys should be done with caution.
- The paper does not provide the exact chemical composition of the powder (no independent elemental analysis), so actual deviation from the nominal formula cannot be assessed.
- Mechanical testing was performed only at room temperature. No high-temperature tensile or compressive strength, creep, or fracture toughness data are provided.
- The only high-temperature property examined is oxidation at 850 °C for 100 h. No oxidation testing at higher temperatures (e.g., 1000 °C or above) is reported.
- The paper does not report post-deposition heat treatment; all data are for as-printed samples.
- The oxide layer was characterized mostly by SEM-EDS and XRD; no cross-sectional TEM or detailed phase quantification was performed.
- The conclusion that high VED leads to grain refinement is based on XRD peak broadening and lamellar spacing measurements; it is not supported by direct grain-size measurements using EBSD, although EBSD orientation maps were used for texture analysis.
- The paper states that the sample with VED = 109.4 J/mm³ showed the highest yield strength (1199 MPa) and tensile strength (1421 MPa), but the best overall combination of strength and ductility (YS 1151 MPa, UTS 1364 MPa, elongation 16.37 %) was obtained at VED = 187.5 J/mm³. This is an important distinction when extracting property values for different VED levels.
