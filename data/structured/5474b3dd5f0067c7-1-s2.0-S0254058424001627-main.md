# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title**: Incorporating microstructural and mechanical heterogeneity to Ti–Zr–Nb alloy by partial high-energy ball milling
- **Authors**: Muhammad Akmal, Aamir Malik, Wonjong Jeong, Ho Jin Ryu
- **Journal**: Materials Chemistry and Physics
- **Year**: 2024
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matchemphys.2024.129037
- **Alloy system studied**: Ti–Zr–Nb (TZN) β-Ti alloy, specifically Ti–20Zr–23Nb (at.%), also described in the paper as a medium-entropy alloy
- **Research objective**: To improve the tensile yield strength of a Ti–Zr–Nb (TZN) β-Ti alloy without significantly increasing its elastic modulus, by incorporating microstructural and mechanical heterogeneity through partial high-energy ball milling followed by spark plasma sintering (SPS).
- **Material category**: Ti–Zr–Nb β-titanium alloy / medium-entropy alloy; not a refractory high-entropy alloy in the strict sense, but processed by powder metallurgy (not additive manufacturing).

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Primary alloy**: Ti–20Zr–23Nb (at.%), abbreviated as TZN.
- **Alloy class**: β-Ti alloy, described in the paper as a medium-entropy alloy.
- **Research purpose**: Biomedical implant material requiring low elastic modulus and high strength; the work is aimed at avoiding Al- and V-containing β-Ti alloys because Al and V have been reported to be neurotoxic and responsible for Alzheimer's disease and osteomalacia.
- **Deformation mode context**: β-Ti alloys may deform by slip, TRIP (transformation-induced plasticity), TWIP (twinning-induced plasticity), or both, depending on β-phase stability. The paper explicitly recognizes that higher alloying-element content can suppress TRIP/TWIP and reduce plasticity.
- **Alloy design principle**: The d-electron concept based on mean bond order (Bo) and mean d-orbital energy level (Md) is used to design low-elastic-modulus Ti–Zr–Nb alloys.

# Alloy Composition

- **Base alloy designation**: Ti–20Zr–23Nb (TZN), using atomic percent nomenclature.
- **Ti**: balance (not explicitly stated with a number, but constitutes the matrix of Ti–20Zr–23Nb).
- **Zr**: 20 at.% (nominal, from alloy designation).
- **Nb**: 23 at.% (nominal, from alloy designation), acting as a β stabilizer.
- **Oxygen**:
  - Pre-alloyed pristine TZN powder oxygen content: 0.083 ± 0.01 wt.% (measured by ONH determinator).
  - 100-RPM milled powder: 0.12 wt.% O.
  - 150-RPM milled powder: 0.27 wt.% O.
  - 200-RPM milled powder: 0.19 wt.% O.
- **Other elements (W, Mo, Ta, Hf, TiC, ZrC, Y2O3, etc.)**: Not present in the alloy composition. The paper does not study W, Mo, Ta, Hf, carbides, borides, silicides, or oxide dispersoids.
- **Impurities**: Only oxygen is reported; C, N, H contents are not reported.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Feedstock**: Pre-alloyed Ti–20Zr–23Nb powder from Guangzhou Sailong Additive Manufacturing Co., China, average particle size 50 μm.
- **Milling step 1 (high-energy planetary ball milling)**:
  - Pristine TZN powder was milled at 100, 150, 200, and 300 rpm for 3 h.
  - Equipment: planetary ball mill (Pulverisette 5, Fritsch).
  - Milling media: WC balls and vials.
  - Ball-to-powder ratio: 10:1.
  - Atmosphere: argon.
- **Milling step 2 (low-energy mixing)**:
  - Milled TZN powders were blended with pristine TZN powder in equal weight proportions (1:1) by a 3-h milling at 125 rpm using a low-energy horizontal ball mill.
  - Atmosphere: air.
  - Milling container: plastic bottles with WC balls.
  - Ball-to-powder ratio: 5:1.
- **Sintering**:
  - Method: spark plasma sintering (SPS).
  - Temperature: 1200 °C.
  - Holding time: 10 min.
  - Heating rate: 100 °C/min.
  - Die: 13 mm graphite die.
- **Excluded sample**: The powder milled at 300 rpm was excluded from the study owing to accumulated porosity during milling.
- **No post-sintering heat treatment**: Conventional solution treatment followed by aging was not employed; SPS was considered sufficient for the required α precipitation.
- **Suppression of ω phase**: A higher Nb content and the higher cooling rate of SPS were used to suppress excess ω phase formation and avoid brittleness.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Predominantly single β phase (BCC), confirmed by XRD.
- **Second phase/precipitates**: α phase (Zr-rich α precipitates) within the β matrix.
  - Precipitate size in 100-RPM-TZN: approximately 5 μm (coarse).
  - Precipitate size in 150-RPM-TZN and 200-RPM-TZN: approximately 300 nm (fine); TEM confirmed precipitates less than 300 nm in patterned trajectories.
  - Some larger α precipitates around other inhomogeneities were approximately 2 μm.
  - TEM confirmed the presence of ω phase within α precipitates.
- **Grain size**:
  - Pristine TZN sintered sample: average grain size approximately 297 μm.
  - 100-RPM-TZN: 122 μm.
  - 150-RPM-TZN: 111 μm.
  - 200-RPM-TZN: 96 μm.
- **Grain size distribution**: Marginal bimodal distribution; the proportion of finer grains introduced by milled powder increases slightly with milling energy.
- **Elemental segregation**: Zr enrichment in α precipitates; TEM analysis of precipitates confirms high Zr content. Oxygen segregation was not observed in the α-rich regions.
- **Microstructural heterogeneity**: Layered structure around parent powder particles formed by powder wrapping during milling. These layers are potential sites for α precipitation because of high defect density. The resulting sintered samples show soft and hard regions based on differences in α precipitation fraction.
- **Precipitate fraction (Fα) estimated from elastic modulus using rule of mixtures**:
  - Pristine TZN: assumed 0% α precipitates.
  - 100-RPM-TZN: ~6%.
  - 150-RPM-TZN: ~8%.
  - 200-RPM-TZN: ~13%.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed**:
  - Grain refinement.
  - Precipitation strengthening by Zr-rich α precipitates.
  - Solid-solution strengthening/hardening by oxygen inclusion.
  - Hetero-deformation-induced strengthening from soft/hard grain partitioning, with geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) accumulating at soft/hard interfaces.

# Mechanical Properties

- **Tensile properties (true stress–strain from tensile test)**:
  - Tensile yield strength:
    - Pristine TZN: 590 MPa.
    - 100-RPM-TZN: 670 MPa.
    - 150-RPM-TZN: 880 MPa.
    - 200-RPM-TZN: 1000 MPa.
  - Tensile ductility: limited to 10% for samples milled at speeds of more than 100 rpm; not explicitly stated for pristine TZN and 100-RPM-TZN, but fractography shows deeper stretched zones in pristine TZN than in 100-RPM-TZN, and cross-sectional area reduction decreased for 150-RPM-TZN and 200-RPM-TZN.
- **Compressive plasticity**:
  - All samples maintained more than 50% plasticity under compression.
  - Increase in compressive yield strength: approximately 450 MPa (from pristine to milled samples).
- **Elastic modulus (ultrasonic pulse-echo technique, ASTM E494)**:
  - Pristine TZN: 65.72 GPa.
  - 100-RPM-TZN: 68.61 GPa.
  - 150-RPM-TZN: 69.27 GPa.
  - 200-RPM-TZN: 71.48 GPa.
  - Change from pristine to 200-RPM-TZN: approximately 6 GPa, still lower than commercially pure Ti (~110 GPa).
  - The paper notes that tensile-test-derived modulus was lower than acoustic-method modulus, but no specific tensile modulus values are given.
- **Nanoindentation hardness**:
  - Matrix hardness: approximately 4 GPa (blue zones in hardness map).
  - Higher hardness zones (green and red) associated with α precipitation; precipitate hardness is size-dependent (smaller precipitates → higher hardness).
- **Hardness trend**: Nanoindentation hardness maps show localized hardening due to fine α precipitates, consistent with tensile strength increase.
- **Fracture mode**: Dimple-like ductile fracture for all samples after tensile failure.
- **Deformation mechanism**:
  - TRIP (stress-induced martensitic transformation) observed in pristine TZN and 100-RPM-TZN.
  - In 100-RPM-TZN, TRIP is mostly associated with finer grains and strain localization, with dislocation pile-up in smaller grains triggering TRIP.
  - TEM of 100-RPM-TZN strained at 10% confirmed a TRIP/martensite region sandwiched between β matrix; selected-area diffraction of β/martensite interface is shown.
  - TRIP effect becomes less pronounced in 200-RPM-TZN because of matrix compositional change (Zr depletion into Zr-rich α precipitates) and the presence of ω phase.
  - In 200-RPM-TZN, the maximum Kernel Average Misorientation is located in finer grains with high precipitation fraction.
- **Strengthening comparison**:
  - 100-RPM-TZN strengthening dominated by grain refinement.
  - 150-RPM-TZN and 200-RPM-TZN strengthening dominated by fine precipitation and oxygen inclusion.
- **Testing conditions**:
  - Tensile and compression sub-size samples per ASTM-E8 and ASTM-E9.
  - Universal testing machine: Instron 8848.
  - Test speed: 0.005 mm/s.
  - Strain measurement by digital image correlation.
  - Atmosphere for mechanical testing: not explicitly stated.
- **High-temperature mechanical properties**: Not studied in this paper.
- **Creep, fracture toughness, rupture life, strain-rate sensitivity**: Not studied.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxidation behavior**: Not studied in this paper.
- **Oxygen contamination during processing**:
  - Milling increased oxygen content:
    - Pristine: 0.08 wt.% O (0.083 ± 0.01 wt.%).
    - 100-RPM powder: 0.12 wt.% O.
    - 150-RPM powder: 0.27 wt.% O.
    - 200-RPM powder: 0.19 wt.% O.
  - Explanation for lower O at 200 rpm than 150 rpm: particle size significantly increases beyond 150-rpm milling, reducing total surface area and likely reducing oxygen inclusion.
- **Role of oxygen from milling**: Oxygen inclusion contributes to strengthening; oxygen stabilization of α precipitation is referenced from previous work (O-induced refinement of α precipitates).
- **Embrittlement considerations**: The authors intentionally used higher Nb content as β stabilizer and higher SPS cooling rate to suppress excess ω phase and avoid brittleness.
- **No discussion of high-temperature oxidation, Al2O3, Cr2O3, SiO2, scale formation, or oxidation resistance.**

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Zr

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ti–Zr–Nb β-Ti biomedical alloys; specifically Ti–20Zr–23Nb in this paper.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 20 at.% in Ti–20Zr–23Nb.
- **Main Function**: Zr-rich α precipitates form in the β matrix and provide precipitation strengthening; Zr is a key constituent of the low-modulus Ti–Zr–Nb system; Zr depletion in the β matrix after precipitation suppresses TRIP and can reduce elongation.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Zr-rich α phase precipitates nucleate preferentially at high-energy sites such as grain boundaries and cold-worked high-defect-density regions produced by high-energy ball milling. The incoherent nature of α precipitates requires high-energy nucleation sites. High Zr content is confirmed by TEM in α precipitates. Zr-rich precipitation changes the β matrix composition, making TRIP less favorable.
- **Property Effect**: Tensile yield strength increased from 590 MPa (pristine) to 1000 MPa (200-RPM-TZN) in part due to Zr-rich α precipitation; elastic modulus changed by less than 6 GPa (65.72 to 71.48 GPa); compressive plasticity remained above 50%; tensile ductility of samples milled above 100 rpm was limited to 10%. The paper does not give separate quantitative values for Zr-only strengthening because α precipitation and grain refinement effects are combined. Estimated α fraction from modulus: ~6% (100 rpm), ~8% (150 rpm), ~13% (200 rpm).
- **Potential Side Effects**: Zr-rich α precipitation depletes Zr from the β matrix, which suppresses the TRIP mechanism and can reduce tensile elongation; formation of ω phase associated with α precipitates alters deformation mode and may reduce ductility.
- **Original Evidence**: “The precipitates in the matrix... TEM analysis of the precipitates confirms their α phase and high Zr content.”; “the TRIP effect becomes less pronounced in the 200-RPM-TZN sample because of the compositional change of the matrix due to the Zr enrichment in the precipitates.”; Fig. 2(b) and 2(c) show Zr-rich precipitates.

## Nb

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ti–Zr–Nb β-Ti biomedical alloys; Ti–20Zr–23Nb in this paper.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 23 at.% in Ti–20Zr–23Nb.
- **Main Function**: Nb acts as a β-phase stabilizer; higher Nb content suppresses excess ω-phase formation and associated brittleness.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Nb stabilizes the β phase, making the alloy a predominantly single-β-phase system after SPS. The paper states that a higher Nb content and higher SPS cooling rate were used to suppress formation of excess ω phase.
- **Property Effect**: Not directly quantified in this paper. Contributes to achieving a single β phase matrix and avoiding brittleness associated with ω phase. Elastic modulus of the β matrix (pristine TZN) is 65.72 GPa.
- **Potential Side Effects**: The paper notes in the introduction that higher alloying-element content can decrease plasticity because TRIP or TWIP behaviors may vanish; however, for this specific Nb-bearing composition, TRIP is still observed in pristine and 100-RPM samples.
- **Original Evidence**: “A higher Nb content, which works as a β stabilizer in the selected composition, and a higher cooling rate of the SPS were used to suppress the formation of the excess ω phase, thereby avoiding brittleness.”

## O (Oxygen)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ti–Zr–Nb β-Ti alloys processed by ball milling and SPS.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Pristine powder: 0.083 ± 0.01 wt.% O; 100-RPM: 0.12 wt.%; 150-RPM: 0.27 wt.%; 200-RPM: 0.19 wt.%.
- **Main Function**: Oxygen inclusion contributes to strengthening; oxygen can increase α-precipitation stability and induce finer α precipitate morphology during heat treatment/sintering.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Oxygen increases defect density and α-phase precipitation stability; previous work cited in the paper reports oxygen-induced refinement of α precipitates in aged metastable β Ti alloys. Oxygen content is not segregated in the α-rich regions according to TEM analysis in this paper.
- **Property Effect**: The paper attributes part of the strength increase in 150-RPM-TZN relative to 100-RPM-TZN to greater oxygen inclusion, since the estimated α fraction does not significantly differ between these samples. The original paper does not report a separate quantitative oxygen strengthening value.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Unavoidable oxygen contamination during ball milling; the authors intentionally avoided excess ω phase and brittleness, but no direct quantitative ductility penalty from oxygen is reported here.
- **Original Evidence**: “In addition to the precipitation, oxygen inclusion also plays an important role in strengthening. As it can be observed that the volume fraction of the precipitates in the 150-RPM-TZN sample does not significantly differ from that of the 100-RPM-TZN sample, henceforth, the presence of a greater amount of oxygen inclusions can explain the increase in strength.”

## W (Tungsten)

- Not an alloying element in this paper. WC was used only as milling balls/vials, and no intentional W addition was made. The paper does not discuss W as an alloying element. 

## Mo, Ta, Hf, Ti, V, Cr, Al, Si, Re, C, B, Y, La

- Not present in the studied alloy. The paper mentions Ta, Nb, and Mo as suitable β-stabilizers for biomedical β-Ti alloys in the introduction, but Mo and Ta are not studied in the experimental alloy. Al and V are discussed as elements to avoid because of neurotoxicity.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Grain refinement

- **Related elements**: No additional alloying element is required; achieved by high-energy ball milling of part of the powder.
- **Typical alloys**: Ti–20Zr–23Nb (TZN) in this paper.
- **Key compositions**: Pristine TZN mixed 1:1 with TZN milled at 100, 150, or 200 rpm.
- **Microstructural changes**: Average grain size decreased from ~297 μm (pristine) to 122 μm (100-RPM), 111 μm (150-RPM), and 96 μm (200-RPM); marginal bimodal grain size distribution.
- **Property improvements**: Yield strength increased from 590 MPa (pristine) to 670 MPa (100-RPM-TZN); the strengthening in the 100-RPM sample is attributed primarily to grain refinement.
- **Risks and limitations**: Grain refinement alone (100-RPM) gives a smaller strength increment (~80 MPa) than the combination with fine precipitation at higher milling energies; further milling (300 rpm) caused porosity and the sample was excluded.

## Precipitation strengthening (α-phase, Zr-rich)

- **Related elements**: Zr, with contribution from oxygen and from milled-in defects; ω phase assists fine α formation.
- **Typical alloys**: Ti–20Zr–23Nb (TZN).
- **Key compositions**: Milled powder fractions: 100, 150, 200 rpm for 3 h, mixed 1:1 with pristine powder, SPS at 1200 °C/10 min.
- **Microstructural changes**: Zr-rich α precipitates; precipitate size ~5 μm at 100 rpm, ~300 nm or less at 150–200 rpm; α fraction estimated as ~6% (100 rpm), ~8% (150 rpm), ~13% (200 rpm) from elastic modulus rule-of-mixtures; ω phase present inside α precipitates.
- **Property improvements**: Tensile yield strength 670 MPa (100-RPM), 880 MPa (150-RPM), 1000 MPa (200-RPM); elastic modulus only increased from 65.72 to 71.48 GPa; compressive plasticity above 50%.
- **Risks and limitations**: Coarse precipitates (100-RPM) give less strengthening; high precipitation fraction suppresses TRIP and limits tensile ductility to 10% for >100 rpm samples; ω phase formation may alter deformation behavior.

## Solid-solution strengthening / interstitial hardening by oxygen

- **Related elements**: Oxygen.
- **Typical alloys**: Ti–Zr–Nb with ball-milling-induced oxygen contamination.
- **Key compositions**: 0.12 wt.% O (100 rpm), 0.27 wt.% O (150 rpm), 0.19 wt.% O (200 rpm).
- **Microstructural changes**: Oxygen is not segregated into α precipitates but is present in the powder; increases defect density and stabilizes finer α precipitates.
- **Property improvements**: Partly explains the strength increase of 150-RPM-TZN over 100-RPM-TZN; no separate quantitative value given.
- **Risks and limitations**: Uncontrolled contamination during milling; possible ductility loss, though not directly quantified in this paper.

## Hetero-deformation-induced strengthening (soft/hard grain heterogeneity)

- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; created by partial high-energy ball milling.
- **Typical alloys**: Ti–20Zr–23Nb with bimodal-like soft/hard grain structure.
- **Key compositions**: 1:1 mixture of pristine and milled TZN powders.
- **Microstructural changes**: Soft grains (pristine) and hard grains (milled, α-precipitated); strain partitioning analogous to dual-phase steel; geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) accumulate at soft/hard interfaces; Kernel Average Misorientation localized in finer grains with high precipitation.
- **Property improvements**: Maintains high compressive plasticity (>50%) while increasing yield strength up to 1000 MPa; elastic modulus remains low (<6 GPa change).
- **Risks and limitations**: Increasing precipitation suppresses TRIP and reduces tensile elongation; excessive milling (300 rpm) causes porosity and sample exclusion.

## Grain-boundary strengthening

- The paper does not explicitly separate grain-boundary strengthening as a quantitative mechanism; grain refinement and the associated boundary strengthening are acknowledged as the dominant strengthening mechanism for the 100-RPM-TZN sample. No Hall–Petch constants or specific grain-boundary strengthening values are provided.

## Oxidation-resistant alloying

- Not applicable. The paper does not study oxidation-resistant alloying or oxide scale formation.

# Key Conclusions

1. A novel method was successfully introduced: milling half of the total TZN powder at different rpm, mixing with the other half of pristine powder, and sintering by SPS, to create a heterogeneous material with improved strength and low elastic modulus.
2. Yield strength increased from 590 MPa (pristine TZN) to 1000 MPa (200-RPM-TZN), mainly through precipitation strengthening; the 100-RPM sample was strengthened mainly by grain refinement.
3. Compressive plasticity remained above 50% for all sintered samples.
4. Elastic modulus increased by less than 6 GPa, from 65.7 GPa (pristine) to 71.5 GPa (200-RPM-TZN), which is still below that of commercially pure Ti (~110 GPa).
5. Deformation occurred predominantly by TRIP in pristine and 100-RPM-TZN samples; TRIP was absent/reduced in 150-RPM and 200-RPM samples, resulting in reduced tensile elongation.
6. The heterogeneous structure, involving soft and hard grains with fine α precipitates, provides a combination of high strength, high compressive plasticity, and low elastic modulus, moving toward the high-strength/low-modulus domain of O-containing β-Ti gum metals.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy scope**: The findings are specific to Ti–20Zr–23Nb at.%, a β-Ti/medium-entropy biomedical alloy. The paper does not study refractory high-temperature alloys such as Nb-W, Mo-W, W-based alloys, or refractory high-entropy alloys.
- **Temperature scope**: Only room-temperature tensile and compression behavior is studied. No high-temperature strength, creep, or oxidation behavior is reported.
- **Oxidation**: Not studied.
- **Excluded sample**: The 300-rpm-milled powder sample was excluded because of porosity, so the conclusions are limited to 100–200 rpm milling under the described conditions.
- **Mechanism quantification**: The paper does not provide fully separated quantitative contributions of grain refinement, precipitation strengthening, and oxygen strengthening; the relative roles are qualitative or inferred from modulus-based α fraction estimates.
- **Oxygen contamination**: Oxygen content was measured only for powders before and after milling; sintered-sample oxygen values are not explicitly reported.
- **Elastic modulus measurement**: The ultrasonic pulse-echo method and tensile test gave different modulus values; the paper notes the tensile-test modulus is lower, but no tensile modulus values are tabulated.
- **Processing specificity**: Results apply to spark plasma sintering at 1200 °C for 10 min with a heating rate of 100 °C/min; the authors intentionally avoided conventional solution treatment plus aging. The approach relies on SPS to produce α precipitation.
- **Biomedical relevance**: The paper is motivated by biomedical implant applications and the avoidance of toxic Al/V; the constitution of the alloy and its deformation modes (TRIP/TWIP) are β-Ti-specific and should not be extrapolated directly to other alloy systems.
