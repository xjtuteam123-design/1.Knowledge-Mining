# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: The impact of Nb on the microstructure and mechanical properties of Stellite 21 alloy coatings
- **Authors**: Yongsheng Zhao, Chao Ge, Ying Wu, Yang Deng, Wen Zhang, Xianhao Liu, Yan Liu, Hui Chen
- **Journal**: Surface & Coatings Technology
- **Year**: 2024
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.surfcoat.2024.131113
- **Alloy system studied**: Co-based Stellite 21 alloy coatings with Nb additions, deposited by laser cladding
- **Material category**: Cobalt-based alloy coating, laser-clad Stellite 21 alloy with Nb alloying
- **Research objective**: To investigate the effect of Nb addition on the microstructure, microhardness, micro-shear properties, and wear resistance of Stellite 21 alloy coatings for high-speed train brake disc friction surfaces.

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies Co-based Stellite 21 alloy coatings prepared by laser cladding on 24CrNiMo steel substrates, with varying Nb contents (0, 1, 3, and 5 wt%). The primary goal is to improve the wear resistance and mechanical properties of brake disc friction surfaces for high-speed trains.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Processing method: Laser cladding (LC)**

**Laser cladding parameters:**
- Laser power: 4400 W
- Scanning speed: 22 mm/s
- Powder feeding rate: 25 g/min
- Overlap rate: 40%
- Carrier gas flow: 5 L/min
- Protective gas flow: 25 L/min
- Carrier and protective gas: Pure argon (99.999%)
- Laser system: IPG YLS-12000 (12.0 kW fiber laser)
- Coaxial laser cladding head with focal length 15 mm, spot size 4.5 mm
- Three layers deposited orthogonally to each other to minimize internal stress
- Coating thickness: approximately 4–5 mm

**Powder mixing:** All powders were mechanically mixed in a planetary ball mill (YXQM-2L) at 200 rpm for 3 h.

**Substrate:** 24CrNiMo steel

**No post-deposition heat treatment was reported.**

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Matrix phase:** γ-Co (fcc) is the predominant phase in all coatings.

**Phases identified (XRD, EBSD, Pandat simulations):**
- γ-Co (fcc) – primary matrix
- ε-Co (hcp) – formed via martensitic transformation (γ-Co → ε-Co) induced by Nb addition
- M23C6 carbide (primarily Cr23C6)
- M7C3 carbide (detected by XRD but not by EBSD)
- Laves_C14 phase (primarily Cr2Nb) – precipitated due to Nb addition

**Grain structures:**
- S21 and S21Nb1: Mainly columnar grains interlaced with cellular structures
- S21Nb3 and S21Nb5: Decrease in columnar grains; cellular structures become more predominant; more short and small columnar grains, dendritic grains, and cellular grains
- The addition of Nb prevents the continuous growth of columnar grains

**Phase content quantification (from EBSD, Fig. 18):**

| Sample | γ-Co (%) | ε-Co (%) | Laves (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| S21 | 98 | ~0 | Minimal/undetectable |
| S21Nb1 | 96 | 0.3 | Minimal/undetectable |
| S21Nb3 | 95.7 | 2.6 | 0.2 |
| S21Nb5 | 92.1 | 5.9 | 0.8 |

**Microstructural evolution:**
- The addition of Nb induces martensitic transformation (γ-Co → ε-Co) in the coating
- The precipitation of Laves_C14 (Cr2Nb) phase increases with increasing Nb content
- M23C6 precipitation decreases with increasing Nb content – attributed to the inhibition of M23C6 precipitation by higher Nb addition
- Nb-rich intermetallic phases: primarily granular morphology at 1% Nb; more continuous at 3% Nb; mesh-like structure at 5% Nb
- Nb-rich intermetallic phases (Laves) are distributed near grain boundaries
- Mo-rich intermetallic phases are present in all coatings

**Elemental segregation:**
- Nb-rich intermetallic phases form at/near grain boundaries
- Mo-rich intermetallic phases are present in all coatings

**Grain size data:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed:**
- Solid-solution strengthening (Nb dissolving into γ-Co)
- Precipitation strengthening (Laves Cr2Nb phase)
- Martensitic transformation strengthening (γ-Co → ε-Co)
- Grain-boundary effects (Nb-rich intermetallic phases near grain boundaries)

# Mechanical Properties

## Microhardness (load: 200 g, 15 s dwell)

| Sample | Average microhardness (HV0.2) |
|---|---|
| S21 | ~420 |
| S21Nb1 | ~440 |
| S21Nb3 | ~480 |
| S21Nb5 | ~510 |

- Hardness improvement from S21 to S21Nb5: approximately 21.4%
- Higher hardness in HAZ attributed to martensite formation in the substrate due to laser energy influence
- Hardness decreases from top to bottom of the coating due to thermal effects of subsequent deposition layers (tempering effect)

## Micro-shear Properties (room temperature, shear speed: 1.8 mm/min)

| Sample | Average shear strength (MPa) | Indentation rate (%) |
|---|---|---|
| S21 | 521 | Highest (value not explicitly stated) |
| S21Nb1 | Not explicitly stated | Lower than S21 |
| S21Nb3 | Not explicitly stated | Lower than S21Nb1 |
| S21Nb5 | 685 | Lowest |

- Shear strength improvement: approximately 31.5% (from S21 to S21Nb5)
- The shear process undergoes yielding, plastic deformation, and complete fracture
- Addition of Nb increases shear strength but reduces plasticity (indentation rate)
- All coatings showed higher shear strength than the 24CrNiMo substrate

## Fracture behavior (shear fracture morphology)

- S21: Mixed fracture mode with dimples and cleavage steps; uneven fracture surface
- S21Nb1: Fewer dimples, larger cleavage steps; uneven fracture surface
- S21Nb3 and S21Nb5: Smoother fracture surfaces; secondary cracks and cleavage steps; many small steps
- Mo-rich and Nb-rich intermetallic compounds present at fracture surfaces

## Wear Properties (dry sliding, room temperature, 600 r/min, 2 kg load, 45 min)

**Friction coefficient (COF):**

| Sample | Average COF |
|---|---|
| S21 | 0.349 |
| S21Nb1 | 0.352 |
| S21Nb3 | 0.325 |
| S21Nb5 | 0.315 |

**Wear volume:**

| Sample | Wear volume (mm³) |
|---|---|
| S21 | 0.91 |
| S21Nb1 | 0.81 |
| S21Nb3 | 0.74 |
| S21Nb5 | 0.61 |

- Wear resistance improvement: approximately 1.5 times (49%) with 5 wt% Nb addition compared to Nb-free coating
- All COF values below 0.4, indicating stable friction process

**Wear mechanisms:**
- S21: Predominant abrasive wear with adhesive wear; deep furrows, large and numerous abrasives; oxidative wear also present
- S21Nb1: Reduced abrasives and shallower furrows; still exhibits spalling; abrasive and adhesive wear
- S21Nb3: Finer abrasives, reduced spalling; abrasive and adhesive wear predominant
- S21Nb5: Smoother wear marks, shallower furrows, smaller abrasives and spalling; Laves phase effectively inhibits wear

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Nb

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Co-based Stellite 21 alloy coatings, laser-clad cobalt-based alloys

- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 1 wt%, 3 wt%, 5 wt% Nb powder mechanically mixed with Stellite 21

- **Main Function**: Induces martensitic transformation (γ-Co → ε-Co), promotes Laves phase (Cr₂Nb) precipitation, provides solid-solution strengthening, inhibits columnar grain growth, improves wear resistance and micro-shear strength

- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Nb dissolves into γ-Co causing lattice distortion and local stress concentration; Nb addition promotes γ-Co → ε-Co martensitic transformation; Nb precipitates as Laves_C14 (Cr₂Nb) phase at grain boundaries; Nb suppresses M₂₃C₆ carbide precipitation; Nb prevents continuous growth of columnar grains

- **Property Effect**:
  - Microhardness: 420 HV0.2 (0% Nb) → 510 HV0.2 (5% Nb); ~21.4% increase
  - Micro-shear strength: 521 MPa (0% Nb) → 685 MPa (5% Nb); ~31.5% increase
  - Wear volume: 0.91 mm³ (0% Nb) → 0.61 mm³ (5% Nb); ~49% improvement in wear resistance
  - COF: 0.349 (0% Nb) → 0.315 (5% Nb)
  - ε-Co content: 0% (0% Nb) → 5.9% (5% Nb)
  - Laves content: 0% (0% Nb) → 0.8% (5% Nb)

- **Potential Side Effects**:
  - Reduced plasticity/ductility (indentation rate decreases with increasing Nb)
  - Smooth fracture surfaces with secondary cracks and cleavage steps
  - Reduction in M23C6 carbide precipitation
  - Changes from mixed ductile-brittle fracture to more brittle fracture mode

- **Original Evidence**: Figs. 4–8, 10–12, 14–15, Tables 1–3; Discussion Section 4.1; Conclusions

## Mo

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Co-based Stellite 21 alloy, laser-clad cobalt-based coatings

- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 5.5 wt% Mo in base Stellite 21 composition; measured 5.04–5.38 wt% in coatings

- **Main Function**: Forms Mo-rich intermetallic phases; contributes to wear resistance enhancement in low-carbon Stellite alloys

- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Forms Mo-rich intermetallic phases (carbides) in the coating that enhance hardness and wear resistance

- **Property Effect**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Mo contribution, but cites that Mo-rich intermetallic compounds enhance wear resistance in low-carbon Stellite alloys (references [28, 29, 66])

- **Potential Side Effects**: Not discussed in the current study

- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 5, Table 3 (Mo-rich phases), Introduction references to refs [28, 29]

## Cr

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Co-based Stellite 21 alloy, laser-clad cobalt-based coatings

- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 27.0 wt% in S21 base powder; 25.65–27.22 wt% in measured coatings

- **Main Function**: Forms chromium carbide (M23C6/Cr23C6); contributes to solid-solution strengthening; constituent of Laves Cr2Nb phase

- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Cr participates in M23C6 carbide precipitation at grain boundaries and forms Cr2Nb Laves phase when Nb is added

- **Property Effect**: Cr-rich phases contribute to coating hardness and wear resistance; the original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Cr-only contribution

- **Potential Side Effects**: Not discussed

- **Original Evidence**: Figs. 6–8, Tables 1 and 3

## C (Carbon)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Co-based Stellite 21 alloy coatings

- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0.250 wt% nominal in S21; 0.237–0.250 wt% in powder mixtures; 0.17–0.87 wt% measured in different coating regions (EDS variation)

- **Main Function**: Forms carbides (M23C6, M7C3); combines with Cr to form Cr23C6 carbides at grain boundaries

- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Carbon combines with Cr and Mo to form carbides; M23C6 precipitates at grain boundaries as small particles

- **Property Effect**: Original paper does not provide specific numerical values for C-only contribution

- **Potential Side Effects**: Not discussed

- **Original Evidence**: Table 1, Table 3, Fig. 6–7

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: Nb
- **Typical Alloys**: Stellite 21 + Nb laser-clad coatings
- **Key Compositions**: 1–5 wt% Nb added to Stellite 21
- **Microstructural Changes**: Nb dissolves into γ-Co fcc solid solution; causes lattice distortion and local stress concentration; peak positions shift slightly in XRD
- **Property Improvements**: 
  - Hardness increases from 420 to 510 HV0.2 (21.4%)
  - Shear strength increases from 521 to 685 MPa (31.5%)
  - Wear resistance improves by ~49%
- **Risks and Limitations**: Reduced plasticity of the coating
- **Original Evidence**: Discussion Section 4.1, XRD analysis (Section 3.1), Conclusion 2

## Laves Phase Precipitation Strengthening (Cr₂Nb)

- **Related Elements**: Nb, Cr
- **Typical Alloys**: Stellite 21 + Nb coatings
- **Key Compositions**: Nb ≥ 1 wt% (3–5 wt% most effective); Laves content 0.2% (3% Nb) and 0.8% (5% Nb)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Cr₂Nb Laves_C14 phase precipitates at grain boundaries; morphology evolves from granular (1% Nb) to continuous (3% Nb) to mesh-like (5% Nb)
- **Property Improvements**: Hardness and wear resistance enhancement; shear strength increase
- **Risks and Limitations**: Laves phase distribution near grain boundaries may affect grain-boundary stability and fracture behavior; reduced ductility
- **Original Evidence**: Figs. 5–8, 12, 18; Section 3.2.1; Discussion 4.1, 4.2

## Martensitic Transformation Strengthening (γ-Co → ε-Co)

- **Related Elements**: Nb
- **Typical Alloys**: Stellite 21 + Nb coatings
- **Key Compositions**: Nb content 1–5 wt%; ε-Co content 0.3% (1% Nb), 2.6% (3% Nb), 5.9% (5% Nb)
- **Microstructural Changes**: γ-Co → ε-Co martensitic transformation induced by Nb; lattice distortion from Nb addition creates local stress concentrations that promote transformation
- **Property Improvements**: Increased hardness and shear strength; contributes to improved wear resistance
- **Risks and Limitations**: Martensite and Laves phase precipitation reduce plasticity
- **Original Evidence**: Figs. 6–8, 18; Discussion Section 4.1; Conclusion 1

## Second-Phase Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: Nb, Mo, Cr, C
- **Typical Alloys**: Stellite 21, Stellite 21 + Nb coatings
- **Key Compositions**: Mo-rich intermetallics (5.5 wt% Mo); Cr₂Nb Laves (from Nb addition); Cr₂₃C₆ carbides (from Cr and C)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Formation of Mo-rich intermetallic phases, Nb-rich Laves phases, and Cr₂₃C₆ carbides distributed in the γ-Co matrix
- **Property Improvements**: Wear resistance improvement; hardness increase
- **Risks and Limitations**: Intermetallic phases at grain boundaries may reduce ductility
- **Original Evidence**: Figs. 5, 8, 12, 17, 19; Discussion Section 4.2

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: Nb
- **Typical Alloys**: Stellite 21 + Nb coatings
- **Key Compositions**: 1–5 wt% Nb
- **Microstructural Changes**: Nb-rich intermetallic phases (Laves) form near grain boundaries; Nb prevents continuous growth of columnar grains, resulting in finer grains (short columnar, dendritic, and cellular grains)
- **Property Improvements**: Increased hardness and shear strength
- **Risks and Limitations**: Nb-rich phases at grain boundaries may affect fracture behavior
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 5, Discussion Section 4.1

## Grain Refinement

- **Related Elements**: Nb
- **Typical Alloys**: Stellite 21 + Nb coatings
- **Key Compositions**: 3–5 wt% Nb most effective
- **Microstructural Changes**: Transition from elongated columnar grains to shorter columnar, dendritic, and cellular grains at higher Nb contents
- **Property Improvements**: Indirectly contributes to hardness and wear resistance
- **Risks and Limitations**: Not discussed
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 4, Section 3.1, Conclusion 1

## Wear Resistance Alloying

- **Related Elements**: Nb, Mo
- **Typical Alloys**: Stellite 21 + Nb laser-clad coatings
- **Key Compositions**: 1–5 wt% Nb addition
- **Microstructural Changes**: Laves phase precipitation, martensitic transformation, Mo-rich intermetallic formation
- **Property Improvements**: Wear volume reduced from 0.91 to 0.61 mm³ (49% improvement); COF reduced from 0.349 to 0.315
- **Risks and Limitations**: Reduced plasticity may affect coating toughness under impact
- **Original Evidence**: Section 3.2.3, Discussion 4.2, Fig. 19

# Key Conclusions

1. The microstructure of S21, S21Nb1, S21Nb3, and S21Nb5 alloy coatings primarily consists of dendrites, columnar grains, and cellular grains. The S21 coating contains mainly γ-Co and a small amount of Cr₂₃C₆. Nb addition induces martensitic transformation (γ-Co → ε-Co), promotes Laves phase precipitation, and suppresses Cr₂₃C₆ precipitation. The ε-Co content in S21Nb1, S21Nb3, S21Nb5 coatings is approximately 0.3%, 2.6%, and 5.9%, respectively.

2. Nb addition increases microhardness and micro-shear strength of S21 cobalt-based coatings. Maximum microhardness reaches 510 HV0.2 and maximum micro-shear strength reaches 685 MPa with 5 wt% Nb, representing improvements of approximately 21.4% and 31.5%, respectively. This is attributed to solid-solution strengthening, Laves phase precipitation, and martensitic transformation.

3. Wear resistance improves with increasing Nb content. Wear volumes decrease from 0.91 mm³ (S21) to 0.61 mm³ (S21Nb5), representing approximately 1.5× improvement. The improvement is attributed to solid-solution strengthening, martensitic transformation, and Laves phase precipitation.

4. The friction coefficients of all coatings are below 0.4, indicating a stable friction process. The wear mechanism transitions from predominantly abrasive wear (with adhesive and oxidative wear) in S21 to a mixture with reduced severity in higher Nb content coatings.

5. Plasticity decreases with Nb addition as indicated by reduced indentation rate during micro-shear testing.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations of the study:**
1. No high-temperature mechanical property testing was performed — all tests were at room temperature
2. No high-temperature oxidation testing was conducted
3. No creep testing was performed
4. Grain size was not quantitatively measured
5. The exact composition of the ε-Co phase was not verified compositionally; it was inferred from XRD/EBSD phase identification
6. The direct contribution of each strengthening mechanism (solid-solution vs. precipitation vs. martensitic transformation) was not deconvoluted quantitatively
7. The mechanism of M23C6 suppression by Nb was not fully explored or experimentally verified
8. The role of Fe (1.5 wt% in base alloy, with dilution from substrate) on the microstructure was not discussed
9. No tensile testing was performed; only micro-shear tests were used to evaluate mechanical properties
10. Elemental distribution of Nb within the γ-Co matrix (dissolved vs. precipitated) was not directly quantified

**Applicable scope:**
- Laser-clad Co-based Stellite 21 alloy coatings for wear-resistant applications
- Surface modification of 24CrNiMo steel brake discs for high-speed trains
- Nb additions up to 5 wt% to cobalt-based alloys prepared by laser cladding
- Room-temperature dry sliding wear conditions
- Room-temperature micro-shear loading conditions
- The study focuses on coating applications; applicability to bulk alloys is not addressed

**Not addressed in the original paper:**
- Effects of Nb on high-temperature performance beyond room temperature
- Oxidation behavior of the coatings at service temperatures
- Long-term thermal stability of the martensitic ε-Co phase and Laves phase under brake disc operating conditions
- Residual stress evolution with Nb addition
- The effect of substrate dilution on the final coating composition across all tested Nb contents in a systematic manner
- Fatigue performance under cyclic braking conditions
