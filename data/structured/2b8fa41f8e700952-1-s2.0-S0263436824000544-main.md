# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: The effects of V or Sn addition on the microstructural characteristics and mechanical properties of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy
- **Authors**: Zhaobo Li, Liangshun Luo, Binbin Wang, Baoxian Su, Lei Luo, Liang Wang, Yanqing Su, Jingjie Guo, Hengzhi Fu
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2024
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2024.106606
- **Alloy system studied**: Nb-Si based alloys (Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr base alloy with V or Sn additions)
- **Research objective**: To systematically investigate the effects of individual additions of V (2–20 at.%) and Sn (1–7 at.%) on the microstructural characteristics and mechanical properties of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy, with the purpose of achieving a balance between toughness and compression strength.
- **Material category**: Nb-Si based in-situ composite / multi-phase refractory alloy (Nbss + Nb₅Si₃ silicide-based composite)

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Base alloy**: Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr (at.%)
- **Alloy family**: Nb-Si based in-situ composites consisting of ductile Nb-based solid solution (Nbss) and brittle silicide phases (Nb₃Si, Nb₅Si₃)
- **Studied variants**: 
  - V-added alloys: Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr-xV (x = 2, 5, 10, 15, 20 at.%)
  - Sn-added alloys: Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr-ySn (y = 1, 3, 5, 7 at.%)
- **Prior base alloy characteristics (from reference [22])**: The Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy consisted of Nbss, α-Nb₅Si₃, and a small amount of Nb₃Si. Its room-temperature fracture toughness was 13.5 MPa·m^1/2. It possessed petal-like eutectics (42.2 vol%) and reticulate-like eutectics (3.3 vol%).

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Melting method**: Non-consumable vacuum arc furnace under argon atmosphere in a water-cooled copper crucible
- **Ingot weight**: 100 g button ingots
- **Remelting**: Each button ingot was remelted at least five times to ensure uniform distribution of alloying elements
- **Raw material purity**: >99.4 wt% high-purity elements
- **Specimen preparation**: Cubic samples (6 × 6 × 3 mm³) cut from the center of button ingots by electro-discharge machining (EDM)
- **Heat treatment**: No homogenization, annealing, aging, or other heat treatment was reported. All characterization was performed on as-cast alloys.
- **Deformation**: High-temperature compression test at 1200 °C with 20% strain at a strain rate of 0.001 s⁻¹, followed by immediate water cooling
- **No powder metallurgy, MA, HP, SPS, HIP, AM, rolling, or forging** was involved in this study.

## Grain size and grain boundary features

- **Nbss average diameter**: 3.6–7.2 μm (varies with alloy)
- **Nb₅Si₃ average diameter**: 3.7–7.1 μm (varies with alloy)
- **Primary Nb₅Si₃ average diameter**: 16.1–58.6 μm
- **Grain boundaries (EBSD)**: LAGBs (2–15°) and HAGBs (>15°) were characterized. Before deformation, LAGBs and HAGBs mainly existed in ductile Nbss phase, but hardly appeared in brittle Nb₅Si₃. After hot compression, LAGBs became connected, indicating subgrain formation in Nbss; increase in HAGBs indicated recrystallized grains. LAGBs and HAGBs were derived from rearrangement of dislocations.
- **Subgrain boundaries in Nbss** were formed in as-cast 2V alloy due to dislocation accumulation (TEM observation).

## Elemental segregation and phase partitioning

- **V**: Preferentially dissolves in Nbss (2.5→25.6 at.% V in Nbss with increasing nominal V); also dissolves in Nb₅Si₃ (1.3→6.5 at.% V) and Tiss (3.7→17.2 at.% V)
- **Sn**: Preferentially dissolves in Nbss (1.6→7.8 at.% Sn); small amounts in Nb₅Si₃ (0.5→1.6 at.%) and Nb₃Sn (8.3–9.6 at.%)
- **Ti**: Mainly partitions to Nbss and Tiss; Ti content in Nbss increases when Sn is present (up to 28.5 at.% in 1Sn Nbss)
- **Cr**: Preferentially dissolves in Nbss (1.9–4.5 at.%)
- **Al**: Preferentially dissolves in Nbss (1.0–3.2 at.%)
- **Sn increases Ti solubility in Nbss**, which suppresses Tiss formation.

## Precipitates and second phases

- **Primary Nb₅Si₃** (α or β polymorph): blocky morphology; constitutes primary phase
- **Tiss phase**: Ti-rich solid solution phase at Nbss/Nb₅Si₃ interfaces in V-added alloys only
- **Nb₃Sn phase**: A15-type intermetallic; appears in 5Sn and 7Sn alloys; brighter contrast than Nbss in BSE
- **No carbide, oxide, boride, or silicide precipitates** (other than Nb₅Si₃) were reported.

## Strengthening mechanisms discussed

The paper discusses:
- **Solid solution strengthening/softening of Nbss** by V and Sn
- **Solid solution strengthening of Nb₅Si₃** by V
- **Composite strengthening** via volume fraction of Nb₅Si₃
- **Toughening via chaotic eutectic structures** (crack deflection and bridging)
- **Softening of Nbss by V** (solid solution softening phenomenon)
- No precipitation strengthening, ODS strengthening, or grain-boundary strengthening was explicitly discussed.

## Solidification paths

**2V and 1Sn alloys:**
L → primary β-Nb₅Si₃ + L₁ → primary α-Nb₅Si₃ + Nbss + Nb₃Si → primary α-Nb₅Si₃ + Nbss + α-Nb₅Si₃

**5V, 10V, 15V, 20V, and 3Sn alloys:**
L → primary β-Nb₅Si₃ + L₁ → primary β-Nb₅Si₃ + Nbss + β-Nb₅Si₃

**5Sn and 7Sn alloys:**
L → primary β-Nb₅Si₃ + L₁ → primary β-Nb₅Si₃ + Nb₃Sn + L₂ → primary β-Nb₅Si₃ + Nb₃Sn + Nbss + β-Nb₅Si₃

# Mechanical Properties

## Room-temperature fracture toughness (K_Q)

| Alloy | K_Q (MPa·m^1/2) |
|-------|------------------|
| Base Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr (from [22]) | 13.5 |
| 2V | Not explicitly stated as a single value; progressively increases with V content |
| 20V | 19.8 |
| 1Sn | 15.1 |
| 7Sn | 4.3 |

**Key observations:**
- V addition continuously elevates K_Q with increasing V content. 20V alloy reaches 19.8 MPa·m^1/2.
- Sn addition: 1Sn alloy (15.1 MPa·m^1/2) is 11.9% higher than the base alloy. Beyond 1 at.% Sn, K_Q decreases. 7Sn alloy has the lowest K_Q = 4.3 MPa·m^1/2.
- 1Sn alloy toughness is slightly higher than 2V alloy toughness.

## Fracture mode (room temperature)

- All alloys exhibit brittle fracture characteristics at room temperature (loading curves drop almost vertically after peak).
- Typical quasi-cleavage fracture mode.
- **2V alloy**: relatively flat cleavage planes + few tearing ridges; river patterns in Nbss dendrites (cleavage fracture of Nbss)
- **10V and 20V alloys**: increased tearing ridges; smaller cleavage planes; dimples appear in 20V alloy → Nbss fracture mode changes from cleavage to ductile fracture
- **1Sn alloy**: only tearing ridges, no dimples; river patterns on Nbss (cleavage fracture); smaller tearing ridges than 2V
- **7Sn alloy**: cleavage planes fill nearly entire fracture surface; brittle fracture of β-Nb₅Si₃ and Nb₃Sn

## High-temperature compressive strength at 1200 °C

Test conditions: 1200 °C, strain rate 0.001 s⁻¹, 20% strain, argon atmosphere

| Alloy | Compressive strength (MPa) |
|-------|---------------------------|
| 2V | Not explicitly stated as single value (lower than 10V) |
| 5V | Not explicitly stated as single value (lower than 10V) |
| 10V | Highest among V-added alloys (exact value not stated) |
| 15V | 95 |
| 20V | 85 |
| 1Sn | 278 |
| 3Sn | 239 |
| 5Sn | 446 |
| 7Sn | 442 |

**Key observations:**
- V-added alloys: compressive strength increases with V content up to 10 at.%, then decreases. 15V (95 MPa) and 20V (85 MPa) exhibit solid solution softening effect.
- Sn-added alloys: 1Sn (278 MPa) > 3Sn (239 MPa) < 5Sn (446 MPa) ≈ 7Sn (442 MPa). Except for 3Sn, all Sn-added alloys have higher strength than the base Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy.
- 5Sn strength is 86.6% higher than 3Sn.

## Elastic modulus and nanohardness (nanoindentation)

**V-added alloys:**
- Nbss elastic modulus: increases with increasing V content
- Nbss nanohardness: increases with increasing V content
- α-Nb₅Si₃ (in 2V) has higher elastic modulus than β-Nb₅Si₃ (in other V alloys)
- Reference values from Duan et al. [43] (first-principles calculations): α-Nb₅Si₃ = 345.6 GPa; β-Nb₅Si₃ = 301.6 GPa
- V has minimal effect on elastic modulus of β-Nb₅Si₃ but increases its nanohardness

**Sn-added alloys:**
- Sn increases elastic modulus and nanohardness of Nbss
- α-Nb₅Si₃ (in 1Sn) has higher elastic modulus and nanohardness than β-Nb₅Si₃
- β-Nb₅Si₃ elastic modulus and nanohardness increase with increasing Sn content
- Nb₃Sn elastic modulus: 224.7 GPa (5Sn) and 213.6 GPa (7Sn), higher than calculated value of 195.3 GPa [44], due to solid solution of Ti, Al, Cr in Nb₃Sn

## Deformation behavior at 1200 °C

- Work hardening at initial stage (<5% strain), then flow softening with increasing strain
- Work hardening caused by massive increase in dislocations in Nbss and Nb₅Si₃
- Flow softening caused by dynamic recovery and dynamic recrystallization via dislocation rearrangement
- Primary Nb₅Si₃ blocks fragment at initial stage of deformation, then dynamic recovery and recrystallization of Nbss and Nb₅Si₃ occur
- 15V and 20V alloys show minimal microstructural change after deformation due to elliptical primary β-Nb₅Si₃ (low aspect ratio 1.59, 1.57) and softened Nbss
- 10V alloy shows numerous cracks and holes extending through primary β-Nb₅Si₃

## KAM (Kernel Average Misorientation) analysis

- 2V alloy Nbss: KAM values 0–1 increase from 84.1% (as-cast) to 92.8% (deformed); KAM 1–2 decrease from 15.1% to 6.3%
- 10V alloy Nbss: KAM values 0–2 before deformation, up to 4 after deformation
- Nbss undergoes greater deformation than Nb₅Si₃ during hot compression

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

**[V (Vanadium)]**

**Applicable Alloy System**: Nb-Si based in-situ composites (Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr-xV, x = 2–20 at.%)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 2–20 at.% nominal; dissolves in Nbss (2.5–25.6 at.%), Nb₅Si₃ (1.3–6.5 at.%), and Tiss (3.7–17.2 at.%)

**Main Function**: 
- Toughening element for Nb-Si based alloys (decreases cleavage fracture tendency due to low ductile-brittle transition temperature, <298 K)
- Promotes formation of β-Nb₅Si₃ over α-Nb₅Si₃
- Promotes chaotic eutectic formation; suppresses reticulate and cellular eutectics
- Promotes eutectoid decomposition of metastable Nb₃Si
- Changes Nbss fracture mode from cleavage to ductile fracture
- Solid solution softening of Nbss at high V content

**Microstructural Mechanism**:
- V substitutes Nb sites in α-Nb₅Si₃ lattice; V₅Si₃ has I4/mcm space group (same as β-Nb₅Si₃), with lattice parameters a = b = 9.425 Å, c = 4.758 Å, similar to β-Nb₅Si₃ (a = b = 10.028 Å, c = 5.070 Å), thus promoting α→β transformation
- V atomic radius (1.35 Å) is smaller than Nb (1.47 Å), causing Nbss lattice shrinkage (XRD peak shift to the right)
- Shrinks Nbss lattice
- Enhances dislocation gliding/climbing by promoting thermally activated processes
- Increases elastic modulus and nanohardness of Nbss
- Increases nanohardness of β-Nb₅Si₃

**Property Effect**:
- Room-temperature fracture toughness: continuously increased from 13.5 MPa·m^1/2 (base, 0V) to 19.8 MPa·m^1/2 (20V)
- High-temperature compressive strength (1200 °C): increases with V up to 10 at.%, then decreases; 15V = 95 MPa; 20V = 85 MPa (solid solution softening dominates)
- Nbss elastic modulus: increases with increasing V content
- Nbss nanohardness: increases with increasing V content
- Volume fraction of primary Nb₅Si₃ increases from 3.8 vol% (2V) to 14.1 vol% (15V)

**Potential Side Effects**:
- High V content (>10 at.%) causes solid solution softening of Nbss, reducing high-temperature compressive strength
- Increases brittle phase (Nb₅Si₃) volume fraction
- Reduces volume fraction of ductile Nbss (58.6→42.8 vol% from 2V to 20V)
- Tiss phase persists at interfaces

**[Sn (Tin)]**

**Applicable Alloy System**: Nb-Si based in-situ composites (Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr-ySn, y = 1–7 at.%)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 1–7 at.% nominal; dissolves in Nbss (1.6–7.8 at.%), Nb₅Si₃ (0.5–2.4 at.%), and forms Nb₃Sn (8.3–9.6 at.% Sn in Nb₃Sn phase)

**Main Function**:
- Promotes formation of β-Nb₅Si₃ over α-Nb₅Si₃
- Promotes chaotic eutectic formation; suppresses reticulate and cellular eutectics
- Promotes eutectoid decomposition of metastable Nb₃Si
- Promotes Nb₃Sn phase formation (at ≥5 at.% Sn)
- Increases Ti solubility in Nbss, thereby suppressing Tiss phase
- Refines Nbss grain size
- Can eliminate "pest oxidation" in Nb-Si alloys at 750–950 °C (from literature)

**Microstructural Mechanism**:
- Sn atoms preferentially replace Si sites in α-Nb₅Si₃ lattice
- Nb₅SiSn₂ has I4/mcm space group (same as β-Nb₅Si₃), with lattice parameters a = b = 10.541 Å, c = 5.138 Å, similar to β-Nb₅Si₃, thus promoting α→β transformation
- Sn atomic radius (1.57 Å) is larger than Nb (1.47 Å), causing Nbss lattice expansion (XRD peak shift to the left)
- Expands Nbss lattice
- Increases elastic modulus and nanohardness of Nbss
- Increases elastic modulus and nanohardness of β-Nb₅Si₃

**Property Effect**:
- Room-temperature fracture toughness: 1Sn = 15.1 MPa·m^1/2 (11.9% higher than base 13.5 MPa·m^1/2); 7Sn = 4.3 MPa·m^1/2 (lowest)
- High-temperature compressive strength (1200 °C): 1Sn = 278 MPa; 3Sn = 239 MPa; 5Sn = 446 MPa; 7Sn = 442 MPa
- Nbss elastic modulus: increases with Sn addition
- Nbss nanohardness: increases with Sn addition
- Nb₃Sn elastic modulus: 224.7 GPa (5Sn), 213.6 GPa (7Sn)

**Potential Side Effects**:
- Higher Sn content (≥3 at.%) forms brittle β-Nb₅Si₃ instead of tougher α-Nb₅Si₃
- Sn ≥5 at.% forms brittle Nb₃Sn phase
- Severe decrease in fracture toughness
