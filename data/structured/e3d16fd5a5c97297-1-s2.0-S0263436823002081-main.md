# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Designing NbTiTa-Cr/Al refractory complex concentrated alloys with balanced strength-ductility-oxidation resistance properties: Insights into oxidation mechanisms
- **Authors:** C.Y. Liu, Z.L. Ma, H.Y. Li, Z.Q. Xu, X.W. Cheng
- **Journal:** International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106308
- **Alloy system studied:** NbTiTa-based refractory complex concentrated alloys with Cr or Al additions
- **Material category:** Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs), single-phase BCC alloys
- **Research objective:** To design RCCAs with balanced strength-ductility-oxidation resistance properties by adding low contents of Cr or Al to ductile NbTiTa RCCAs, and to elucidate the oxidation mechanisms

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper investigates two novel single-phase BCC refractory complex concentrated alloys based on the NbTiTa system, with low additions of Cr or Al to improve oxidation resistance while maintaining tensile ductility. The research addresses the key bottleneck of ductility-oxidation resistance trade-off in RCCAs.

# Alloy Composition

**Alloy 1: Cr10**
- Nominal composition: Nb₄₀Ti₄₀Ta₁₀Cr₁₀ (at.%)
- Elements present: Nb (40 at.%), Ti (40 at.%), Ta (10 at.%), Cr (10 at.%)
- Valence Electron Concentration (VEC): 4.7

**Alloy 2: Al10**
- Nominal composition: Nb₄₅Ti₃₀Ta₁₅Al₁₀ (at.%)
- Elements present: Nb (45 at.%), Ti (30 at.%), Ta (15 at.%), Al (10 at.%)
- Valence Electron Concentration (VEC): 4.5

Both alloys were prepared from high-purity metals (>99.95 wt%). The actual chemical compositions measured by ICP-OES were close to targeted compositions. No ppm-level impurity concentrations (C, O, N, H) are explicitly reported in the original paper.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Melting method:**
- Vacuum arc melting under argon atmosphere
- High-purity Ti used as getter to avoid contamination from residual oxygen and nitrogen

**Specimen preparation:**
- Tensile test specimens (14 mm × 4 mm × 1 mm) were wire-cut from ingots
- Oxidation test specimens (5 mm × 5 mm × 3 mm) were wire-cut

**Condition:** As-cast (no subsequent homogenization, rolling, forging, or annealing reported)

**Strain rate for tensile testing:** 5 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Matrix phase:**
- Both Cr10 and Al10 are single-phase BCC (body-centered cubic)
- No B2 phase detected in Al10 despite Al addition (confirmed by TEM SAED from different zone axes and XRD at low scanning rates of 0.5°/min)

**Grain size:**
- Cr10: ~116 μm
- Al10: ~154 μm
- Measured by EBSD

**Microstructure features:**
- Both alloys exhibit dendritic microstructures
- Bright dendrites enriched with heavy element Ta
- Dark interdendritic regions concentrated with Ti and Cr/Al
- Nb nearly homogeneously distributed

**Lattice parameters:**
- Cr10 as-cast: 0.326 nm
- Al10 as-cast: 0.327 nm
- Al10 has slightly higher lattice constant attributed to larger atomic radius of Al (r_Al = 0.143 nm) compared with Cr (r_Cr = 0.125 nm)

**Ductility criterion:**
- VEC < 5 predicted ductility; Cr10 (VEC = 4.7) and Al10 (VEC = 4.5) satisfy this criterion

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

**Oxidation test conditions:**
- Temperature: 800 °C
- Atmosphere: Air
- Duration: 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, and 20 hours
- Specimen size: 5 mm × 5 mm × 3 mm
- Three samples tested per condition

**Oxidation kinetics (mass gain at 800 °C for 20 h):**
- Cr10: 4.57 mg/cm²
- Al10: 7.18 mg/cm²
- Both alloys exhibit parabolic oxidation kinetics
- Time exponent n: 0.52 for Cr10, 0.47 for Al10

**Comparison with other RCCAs (800 °C - 20 h mass gain):**

| Alloy | Mass Gain (mg/cm²) | RT Ductility |
|-------|-------------------|--------------|
| Nb₄₀Ti₄₀Ta₁₀Cr₁₀ (Cr10) | 4.57 | 22.6% tensile |
| Nb₄₅Ti₃₀Ta₁₅Al₁₀ (Al10) | 7.18 | 20.1% tensile |
| AlNbTiZr | 3.13 | 17.9% compression |
| VNbTiTaSi₀.₁ (hot-rolled) | 58 | 8% tensile |
| Ti₃₈V₁₅Nb₂₃Hf₂₄ | 41.9 | 20.6% tensile |
| Hf₀.₅Nb₀.₅Ta₀.₅Ti₁.₅Zr | 1381.1 | 18.8% tensile |
| VNbTiTa | 68.4 | 14% tensile |

**Oxide scale constitution after 800 °C oxidation:**

After 1 h:
- Both alloys form rutile-type oxides and (Nb,Ta)₂O₅
- Cr10: ~53% rutile, ~47% (Nb,Ta)₂O₅
- Al10: ~28% rutile, ~72% (Nb,Ta)₂O₅

After 10-20 h:
- Ti₂(Nb,Ta)₁₀O₂₉ and/or Cr(Nb,Ta)₁₁O₂₉ form, mainly at expense of (Nb,Ta)₂O₅
- Al10 (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ fraction reduced from ~72% at 1 h to ~34% at 20 h

**Oxide types identified:**
- Rutile-type: Ti₁₋₂ₓ(Nb,Ta)ₓCrₓO₂ (Cr10) or Ti₁₋₂ₓ(Nb,Ta)ₓAlₓO₂ (Al10), x ≈ 0.03-0.16
- Orthorhombic (Nb,Ta)₂O₅
- Complex oxides: Ti₂(Nb,Ta)₁₀O₂₉, Cr(Nb,Ta)₁₁O₂₉

**Oxide scale structure:**
- Cr10: Alternative layer structure of dense rutile layers and porous mixed oxide layers
- Al10: Single mixed oxide scale with significant voids and cracks
- Cr10 oxide scale (1 h): ~2 μm thick
- Al10 oxide scale (1 h): ~3.4 μm thick

**Oxygen diffusion zone:**
- Not observed in Cr10 after 1 h and 10 h; present after 20 h
- Observed in all Al10 specimens
- Much thicker in Al10 compared to Cr10

**Lattice parameter changes after oxidation:**
- Cr10: 15.9% increase from 0.326 to 0.327 nm (+0.001 nm, ~0.2%)
- Al10: 0.327 to 0.329 nm (+0.002 nm, ~3.2%) — note: original text states ~3.2% but this appears to be a typographical error; actual calculation is ~0.6%

**Pilling-Bedworth Ratio (PBR) values:**

| Alloy | Oxide | PBR Range |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| Cr10 | (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ | 2.34-2.54 |
| Cr10 | Ti₁₋₂ₓ(Nb,Ta)ₓCrₓO₂ | 1.03-1.76 |
| Al10 | (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ | 2.05-2.51 |
| Al10 | Ti₁₋₂ₓ(Nb,Ta)ₓAlₓO₂ | 1.27-1.43 |

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Cr (in NbTiTa-based RCCAs)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
NbTiTa-Cr refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs), single-phase BCC

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
10 at.% Cr (Nb₄₀Ti₄₀Ta₁₀Cr₁₀, Cr10)

**[Main Function]**
Improves high-temperature oxidation resistance while maintaining tensile ductility when added at low concentrations

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Cr is incorporated as a solid solute in rutile-type Ti₁₋₂ₓ(Nb,Ta)ₓCrₓO₂ oxide (x ≈ 0.03-0.16)
- Cr doping significantly reduces the Pilling-Bedworth ratio (PBR) of rutile oxide compared with (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ (1.03-1.76 vs. 2.34-2.54)
- Lower PBR means lower volume mismatch between oxide and matrix, reducing internal stress
- Cr addition promotes higher volume fraction of rutile oxide (53% vs. 28% in Al10)
- Enables formation of alternative dense/porous layer structure that releases stress and prevents cracking

**[Property Effect]**
- Mass gain after 800 °C - 20 h: 4.57 mg/cm² (best among tensile-ductile RCCAs reported)
- Room-temperature yield strength: ~814 MPa
- Room-temperature fracture elongation: ~23%

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- High Cr concentrations can induce Laves or B2 phases that degrade ductility (noted for other RCCAs, but 10 at.% Cr in this study avoided this issue)

**[Original Evidence]**
"Cr10 exhibits much slower oxidation kinetics compared with Al10, attributed to i) the dominant rutile-type Ti₁₋₂ₓ(Nb,Ta)ₓCrₓO₂ oxide that has lower volume mismatch with the alloy matrix compared with (Nb,Ta)₂O₅, as indicated by low Pilling-Bedworth ratio (PBR) values of 1.03-1.76" (Abstract, Section 3.5.2, Table 5)

## Al (in NbTiTa-based RCCAs)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
NbTiTa-Al refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs), single-phase BCC

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
10 at.% Al (Nb₄₅Ti₃₀Ta₁₅Al₁₀, Al10)

**[Main Function]**
Improves high-temperature oxidation resistance at low concentrations without inducing brittle B2 phase

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Al is incorporated as solid solute in rutile-type Ti₁₋₂ₓ(Nb,Ta)ₓAlₓO₂ oxide (x ≈ 0.03-0.14)
- Al-doped rutile has PBR of 1.27-1.43, lower than (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ (2.05-2.51)
- Does not form Al₂O₃; Al acts as dopant in rutile structure
- At 10 at.% Al, single-phase BCC retained without B2 formation

**[Property Effect]**
- Mass gain after 800 °C - 20 h: 7.18 mg/cm² (second best among tensile-ductile RCCAs)
- Room-temperature yield strength: ~797 MPa
- Room-temperature fracture elongation: ~20%

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Higher Al concentrations can introduce B2 phase (as reported in other RCCAs like AlMo₀.₅NbTa₀.₅TiZr), which decreases ductility
- Higher volume fraction of (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ (72% at 1 h) compared with Cr10 (47%), leading to more cracking
- Thicker oxygen diffusion zone beneath oxide scale

**[Original Evidence]**
"As-cast Al10 shows a slightly higher lattice constant in comparison with Cr10, which could be the result of the higher atomic radius of Al (r_Al = 0.143 nm) compared with Cr (r_Cr = 0.125 nm)" (Section 3.1)
"Although Al and Cr neither contribute to new oxides (e.g. Al₂O₃ and Cr₂O₃) nor alter the oxide type (i.e. rutile oxides based on TiO₂), they significantly reduce the PBR values of rutile oxides" (Section 3.6)

## Nb (in NbTiTa-based RCCAs)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
NbTiTa-Cr/Al refractory complex concentrated alloys

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
40 at.% (Cr10) or 45 at.% (Al10)

**[Main Function]**
Base refractory element providing solid-solution strengthening; forms (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ during oxidation

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Nearly homogeneously distributed in both alloys (no significant dendritic segregation)
- Contributes to BCC solid solution
- During oxidation, Nb forms (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ solid solution with Ta (interchangeable Nb and Ta due to similar atomic/ionic radii)

**[Property Effect]**
The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Nb's individual contribution to mechanical properties.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ has high PBR (2.05-2.54) causing large volume mismatch and internal stress
- (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ formation results in pore formation and scale spallation (per cited literature)
- Higher Nb+Ta content in Al10 (~60 at.%) vs. Cr10 (~50 at.%) correlates with higher (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ fraction and more cracking

**[Original Evidence]**
"Nb is nearly homogeneously distributed in the alloys" (Section 3.1, Fig. 1)
"(Nb,Ta)₂O₅ are reported to result in pore formation and scale spallation" (Section 3.3)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
NbTiTa-Cr/Al refractory complex concentrated alloys

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
40 at.% (Cr10) or 30 at.% (Al10)

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

**Related elements:** Nb, Ti, Ta, Cr/Al in BCC solid solution

**Typical alloys:** Nb₄₀Ti₄₀Ta₁₀Cr₁₀ (Cr10), Nb₄₅Ti₃₀Ta₁₅Al₁₀ (Al10)

**Key compositions:** 40-45 at.% Nb, 30-40 at.% Ti, 10-15 at.% Ta, 10 at.% Cr or Al

**Microstructural changes:** Single-phase BCC solid solution with lattice constants of ~0.326-0.327 nm; dendritic segregation of Ta to dendrite cores and Ti/Cr/Al to interdendritic regions

**Property improvements:** 
- Yield strength ~814 MPa (Cr10) and ~797 MPa (Al10)
- Tensile elongation ~23% (Cr10) and ~20% (Al10)

**Risks and limitations:**
- Addition of Al or Cr at higher concentrations can lead to B2 or Laves phase formation, reducing ductility
- As-cast dendritic microstructure may limit mechanical property optimization

## Oxidation-Resistant Alloying (Cr and Al Addition)

**Related elements:** Cr, Al (as low-level additions to NbTiTa-based RCCAs)

**Typical alloys:** Cr10, Al10

**Key compositions:** 10 at.% Cr or 10 at.% Al

**Microstructural changes:**
- Cr or Al incorporated as solid solute in rutile-type Ti₁₋₂ₓ(Nb,Ta)ₓ(Cr/Al)ₓO₂ oxides
- Reduced PBR of rutile oxide compared with undoped TiO₂
- Cr10: alternative dense/porous layer structure
- Al10: mixed oxide structure with voids and cracks

**Property improvements:**
- Cr10: 4.57 mg/cm² mass gain after 800 °C - 20 h (best among tensile-ductile RCCAs)
- Al10: 7.18 mg/cm² mass gain after 800 °C - 20 h
- Both alloys maintain excellent tensile ductility (20-23%)

**Risks and limitations:**
- Neither Cr nor Al forms protective Cr₂O₃ or Al₂O₃ in these alloys
- Al10 develops more extensive cracking due to higher (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ fraction
- Higher Cr/Al concentrations induce brittle phases (Laves, B2) in other RCCA systems

# Key Conclusions

1. Two novel single-phase BCC RCCAs, Nb₄₀Ti₄₀Ta₁₀Cr₁₀ (Cr10) and Nb₄₅Ti₃₀Ta₁₅Al₁₀ (Al10), exhibit excellent balanced strength-ductility with yield strength/elongation of ~814 MPa/~23% and ~797 MPa/~20%, respectively.

2. Both alloys demonstrate the best oxidation resistance at 800 °C among all tensile-ductile RCCAs reported, with mass gains of 4.57 mg/cm² (Cr10) and 7.18 mg/cm² (Al10) after 20 h exposure.

3. Cr10 exhibits superior oxidation resistance compared with Al10, attributed to: (i) lower PBR of Cr-doped rutile (1.03-1.76) vs. Al-doped rutile (1.27-1.43); (ii) higher volume fraction of rutile oxide in Cr10 (53% vs. 28% after 1 h); and (iii) alternative dense/porous layer structure in Cr10 that releases stress and prevents cracking.

4. Neither Cr nor Al forms protective Cr₂O₃ or Al₂O₃ in these alloys; instead, both are incorporated as solid solutes in rutile-type Ti₁₋₂ₓ(Nb,Ta)ₓ(Cr/Al)ₓO₂ oxides.

5. A new thermodynamic model connecting Gibbs free energy of formation to dynamically varied alloy compositions was proposed to explain the sequential and alternative formation of different oxides during oxidation.

6. PBR analysis reveals that (Nb,Ta)₂O₅ has high PBR values (2.05-2.54), causing large volume mismatch, internal stress, void formation, and cracking, while rutile oxides have much lower PBR values (1.03-1.76), contributing to better oxide scale integrity.

7. The paper demonstrates that tailoring oxide types and structures through composition design (adding Cr or Al at low levels, tuning Nb+Ta content) is a feasible approach to achieve RCCAs with balanced oxidation resistance and mechanical properties.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Temperature limitation:** Oxidation behavior was studied only at 800 °C. The authors note that the dense oxide formed below 1000 °C in some RCCAs decomposes above 1000 °C; whether similar behavior occurs for Cr10 and Al10 is not investigated.

2. **As-cast condition:** All results are for as-cast materials. The authors note this leaves "large space for property improvement," but post-processing (rolling, forging, annealing) effects are not explored.

3. **Short oxidation duration:** Maximum oxidation time was 20 h, which is insufficient to assess long-term oxidation resistance or service lifetime.

4. **No high-temperature mechanical testing:** Mechanical properties were measured only at room temperature. The authors do not report high-temperature strength, creep, or fatigue data, which are critical for refractory alloy applications.

5. **No Cr₂O₃ or Al₂O₃ formation:** The alloys do not form the conventional protective oxides (Cr₂O₃, Al₂O₃) typical of Ni-based superalloys; their oxidation resistance relies on rutile-type oxides, which may not be as protective at higher temperatures (above 1000 °C).

6. **PBR calculations based on assumptions:** PBR values are calculated with assumptions about element diffusion; actual values may vary. The paper acknowledges the range approach for PBR.

7. **Thermodynamic model assumptions:** The model assumes constant P_O₂ (0.21 atm or equilibrium partial pressures) and uses TCHEA1 database for activity calculations; kinetic factors (diffusion coefficients, interface reactions) are not fully integrated.

8. **Generalizability:** Conclusions are specific to NbTiTa-based RCCAs with low Cr/Al additions. Whether similar strategies work for other refractory alloy systems (e.g., Mo-W-based, Hf-containing RCCAs) requires further investigation.

9. **The paper does not report specific numerical values for density, hardness, elastic modulus, fracture toughness, or high-temperature strength of Cr10 and Al10.**
