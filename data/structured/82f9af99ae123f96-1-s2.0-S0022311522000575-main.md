# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Effect of annealing temperature on the microstructure and mechanical properties of laves phase reinforced FeCrAl alloy thin-walled tubes
- **Authors:** Jing-yi Tuo, Rui-qian Zhang, Zhen-bing Cai, Pei-nan Du, Yan-qing Yu
- **Journal:** Journal of Nuclear Materials
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnucmat.2022.153561
- **Alloy System Studied:** FeCrAl-based ferritic alloy (Fe-13Cr-4Al-2Mo-0.65Nb-0.4Ta, wt.%)
- **Research Objective:** To investigate the effect of annealing temperature on microstructure evolution, texture development, and mechanical properties of Laves phase reinforced FeCrAl alloy thin-walled tubes for accident-tolerant fuel (ATF) cladding applications in light water nuclear reactors.
- **Material Category:** FeCrAl ferritic alloy (accident-tolerant fuel cladding material). This is not a refractory high-temperature alloy in the Nb-W/Mo-W/refractory HEA sense, but rather an iron-based ferritic alloy with refractory microalloying additions (Mo, Nb, Ta).

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies an FeCrAl ferritic alloy (N1) with nominal composition Fe-13Cr-4Al-2Mo-0.65Nb-0.4Ta (wt.%). The alloy is designed for accident-tolerant fuel cladding tube applications in light water reactors as a replacement for traditional Zr alloys. The alloy was prepared as a thin-walled tube with 9.5 mm diameter and 0.35 mm wall thickness by cold rolling after vacuum melting, forging, and multi-pass rolling.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

The processing route was as follows:

1. **Melting:** Vacuum melting of alloy powder
2. **Casting:** Cast into ingots
3. **Homogenization and solution treatment**
4. **Forging and rolling:** Five passes of forging and rolling. After each forging, the ingot was annealed at 800 °C for 30 min to reduce stress and defects. The forging temperature was maintained at no less than 600 °C.
5. **Drawing:** Final drawing process into a full-size thin-walled tube with length ~4000 mm, diameter 9.5 mm, wall thickness ~0.35 mm.
6. **Annealing treatments:**
   - S1: Untreated (as-rolled)
   - S2: Annealed at 700 °C for 1 h (stress relief annealing)
   - S3: Annealed at 800 °C for 1 h (recrystallization annealing)
   - Cooling: Placed in air and cooled to 25 °C
7. **Aging:** S2 and S3 specimens aged at 400 °C for 1 to 240 h to verify abnormal mechanical properties

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Matrix Phase:** α-Fe ferritic BCC matrix

**Second Phases/Precipitates:**
- **Fe₂Nb-type Laves phase:** Identified by XRD and TEM-EDS. The composition of precipitates was approximately Fe:(Nb+Mo+Ta) = 2.37 and 2.11, close to the atomic ratio of Fe₂Nb-type Laves phase. The Laves phase contains Fe:54.73, Cr:9.36, Al:10.80, Nb:14.07, Mo:4.46, Ta:4.48 (at.%) for point 1# and Fe:54.01, Cr:9.35, Al:10.60, Nb:13.98, Mo:4.97, Ta:6.69 (at.%) for point 2#.
- **Fe₂Mo₄C:** A very small amount identified in S3 specimen.
- **Fe₂AlCr:** New diffraction peaks detected at 63.68° and 80.95°, preliminarily identified as Fe₂AlCr.
- **α' phase and α″ phase:** Formed during aging at 400 °C due to amplitude-modulated decomposition.

**Grain Size:**
- S1 (as-rolled): 5.35 μm equivalent grain size
- S2 (700 °C/1h): 8.39 μm equivalent grain size
- S3 (800 °C/1h): 13.04 μm equivalent grain size

**Recrystallized Area Fraction:**
- S1: 0.398%
- S2: 18.1%
- S3: 83.3%

**Laves Phase Statistics:**
- Particle sizes: 0.425 μm (S1), 0.434 μm (S2), 0.468 μm (S3)
- Area fractions: 3.47% (S1), 4.10% (S2), 5.92% (S3)

**Texture:**
- Cold-rolled (S1): Strong α-fiber texture (<110>//RD) with 78.3% area fraction, γ-fiber (<111>//ND) with 4.18%, θ-fiber (<100>//ND)
- After 700 °C/1h (S2): α-fiber 61.6%, γ-fiber 9.4%
- After 800 °C/1h (S3): <110> fraction 65.8%, <111> fraction 6.4%

**Grain Boundary Features:**
- Low-angle grain boundaries (orientation difference <15°) decreased from 87.0% (S1) to 70.5% (S2) to 51.8% (S3) with increasing annealing temperature.
- Laves phase particles at grain boundaries pin grain boundary migration.

**Strengthening Mechanisms Discussed:**
- Solid-solution strengthening (Cr, Al, Mo in matrix)
- Precipitation strengthening (Laves phase, Fe₂Mo₄C)
- Dislocation forest hardening
- Grain boundary strengthening (Hall-Petch)
- Fine grain strengthening

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

The paper does not report specific oxidation testing results. However, it discusses related information:

- The paper states that FeCrAl alloys form Cr₂O₃ at temperatures below 1273 K and α-Al₂O₃ at temperatures above 1273 K (from references).
- The addition of Y and other reactive elements results in better adhesion of the α-Al₂O₃ oxidation protective layer.
- The paper does not provide specific oxidation weight gain, oxide layer characterization, or oxidation test data.

**Service Stability:**
- At 400 °C (light water reactor working temperature), the second-phase particles have good stability.
- After aging for 240 h at 400 °C, average particle diameter tends to be 0.38 μm and area fraction tends to be stable (4.7% for S2, 3.6% for S3).
- Strength provided by second-phase particles at 400 °C accounts for about 6.5% (S2) and 12.59% (S3) of total yield strength.
- At 800 °C, the proportion increases to 80% of the total yield strength.
- Annealing at 800 °C for 10 h: Laves-free phase region appeared in grains, more Laves phases gathered at grain boundaries, elongated rod-like Laves phase observed at grain boundaries.
- Annealing at 900 °C for 1 h: Large number of rod-like Laves phases at grain boundaries.
- At 1000 °C for 10 min: Part of second-phase particles redissolve; residual particles concentrated at grain boundaries.
- At 1000 °C for 30 min: Second-phase particles almost completely redissolve; time margin provided by particles is not more than 30 min.
- The remelting temperature of second-phase particles is about 1000 °C.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Card 1: Nb (Niobium)

**[Element or Element Combination]:** Nb (Niobium)

**[Applicable Alloy System]:** FeCrAl ferritic alloy (Fe-13Cr-4Al-2Mo-0.65Nb-0.4Ta wt.%)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]:** 0.65 wt.% Nb

**[Main Function]:** Forms Fe₂Nb-type Laves phase precipitates, which provide precipitation strengthening and pin grain boundaries during recrystallization.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]:** Nb forms Fe₂Nb-type Laves phase (Fe:(Nb+Mo+Ta) ratio ≈ 2.1-2.4). The Laves phase particles pin grain boundaries during recrystallization and annealing, hindering grain growth. Larger particles at grain boundaries impede boundary migration during recrystallization. The precipitates also provide dislocation pinning strengthening.

**[Property Effect]:** 
- Laves phase area fraction increases from 3.47% (as-rolled) to 5.92% (800°C/1h)
- Precipitation strengthening contribution (σpre): 35.0 MPa (S1), 34.2 MPa (S2), 42.4 MPa (S3)
- At 400 °C, second-phase particle strengthening accounts for 6.5-12.59% of total yield strength; at 800 °C, the proportion increases to 80%.
- Room temperature yield strength of as-rolled tube: 766.7 MPa

**[Potential Side Effects]:** 
- At 400 °C, the α″ phase precipitation occurs more readily with Nb, Ta, and Mo additions, leading to ductile-brittle fracture behavior.

**[Original Evidence]:** Table 1 (composition), Fig. 5 (SEM/TEM images), Fig. 6 (HAADF and EDS analysis), Table 6 (calculated strengthening contributions), Section 4.2 (tensile properties), Section 4.3 (high-temperature thermal stability)

## Card 2: Ta (Tantalum)

**[Element or Element Combination]:** Ta (Tantalum)

**[Applicable Alloy System]:** FeCrAl ferritic alloy (Fe-13Cr-4Al-2Mo-0.65Nb-0.4Ta wt.%)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]:** 0.4 wt.% Ta

**[Main Function]:** Promotes Laves phase precipitation at grain boundaries, similar to the effect of Zr, thereby refining grains and providing high-temperature microstructural stability.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]:** Ta makes the Laves phase precipitate more easily at grain boundaries, impeding grain growth. Ta has a similar effect to Zr, which can promote precipitation of the Laves phase at grain boundaries, so that the alloy has higher high-temperature and refractory stability. Ta replaces some Nb content but does not contribute to the formation of second-phase particles; its effect is to mark the second-phase particles existing at grain boundaries.

**[Property Effect]:** 
- With Ta addition, recrystallization area fraction at 800 °C/1h reaches 83.3% (higher than FeCrAl with 1%Nb studied in reference [12], which had <10% recrystallization at 700°C/1h)
- Ta confers Fe-bcc matrix with solid solution strengthening and microstructure optimization
- The alloy maintains yield strength above 20 MPa even at 900 °C

**[Potential Side Effects]:** 
- At 400 °C, the addition of Nb, Ta, and Mo decreases the α″ phase precipitation temperature, increasing the probability of α″ phase precipitation at 400 °C.
- May contribute to ductile-brittle fracture behavior at 400 °C.

**[Original Evidence]:** Table 1 (composition), Section 4.1 (Microstructure evolution), Section 4.3 (High-temperature thermal stability), Fig. 6 (HAADF image showing Ta enrichment in precipitates)

# Key Conclusions

1. The FeCrAl alloy thin-walled tube exhibits typical cold-rolled BCC texture with strong α-fiber (<110>//RD) orientation (78.3% area fraction), which is distinct from FeCrAl plates that typically show stronger γ-fiber orientation.

2. Annealing temperature directly controls recovery and recrystallization: 700 °C/1h results in 18.1% recrystallization and 79.6% recovery, while 800 °C/1h results in 83.3% recrystallization. Equivalent grain size increases from 5.35 μm (as-rolled) to 13.04 μm (800°C/1h).

3. The Fe₂Nb-type Laves phase (with Fe:(Nb+Mo+Ta) ratio ≈ 2.1-2.4) and Fe₂Mo₄C precipitates provide thermal stability and strengthening. Laves phase particles have high thermal stability below 800 °C and effectively pin grain boundaries and dislocations.

4. Ta addition promotes Laves phase precipitation at grain boundaries, similar to Zr, impeding grain growth and improving high-temperature microstructural stability.

5. Room temperature mechanical properties: As-rolled alloy has yield strength of 766.7 MPa with specific elongation >15%. Annealing at 800 °C/1h increases specific elongation to 27.2% but reduces yield strength to 477.2 MPa.

6. At 400 °C (light water reactor operating temperature), the alloy exhibits a ductile-brittle fracture due to α″ phase precipitation, with reduced elongation compared to room temperature.

7. The second-phase particles have good thermal stability at 400 °C (up to 240 h tested), contributing to high-temperature strength. At 800 °C, they contribute 80% of the total yield strength.

8. The remelting temperature of second-phase particles is about 1000 °C. Complete dissolution occurs after 30 min at 1000 °C, defining the upper temperature limit for particle-mediated strengthening.

9. Even at 900 °C, the yield strength remains above 20 MPa, indicating some high-temperature strength capability.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations:**
1. This paper studies an FeCrAl ferritic alloy, not a refractory high-temperature alloy in the Nb-W/Mo-W/refractory HEA sense. The maximum testing temperature is 1000 °C, which is below the typical operating range for refractory alloys.

2. The alloy composition is fixed at Fe-13Cr-4Al-2Mo-0.65Nb-0.4Ta (wt.%), and the paper does not explore composition variations beyond this single alloy.

3. The paper does not report specific oxidation test results (no weight gain, oxide layer characterization, or oxidation kinetics data).

4. The paper does not provide detailed information on the role of Y (0.05 wt.%) in the alloy.

5. The strengthening model calculation shows discrepancies between calculated and actual yield strength values, particularly for the annealed specimens (S2 and S3), suggesting the model may not fully capture all strengthening mechanisms.

6. The abnormal tensile behavior at 400 °C (decreased elongation) is not fully explained and requires further verification.

7. The paper does not provide detailed information on the crack formation and pass rate during tube fabrication.

**Applicable Scope:**
1. This work is specifically applicable to FeCrAl ferritic alloy thin-walled tubes for accident-tolerant fuel cladding applications in light water nuclear reactors.

2. The findings are relevant for understanding recrystallization behavior and texture evolution in cold-rolled BCC ferritic alloys.

3. The data on Laves phase stability and precipitation behavior at 400-1000 °C is relevant for FeCrAl alloys with Nb, Ta, and Mo additions.

4. The processing route (vacuum melting, forging, rolling, drawing, annealing) provides insight into manufacturing FeCrAl thin-walled tubes.
