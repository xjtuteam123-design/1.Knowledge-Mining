# Paper Metadata
- **Paper title:** Microstructures and properties of selective laser melted K447A/IN718 Ni-based superalloy composites
- **Authors:** Yu Zhang, Zhen Ding, Bing Tian, Fengchun Jiang, Mehrdad Zarinejad, Yunxiang Tong
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.matlet.2025.138598
- **Alloy system studied:** K447A/IN718 Ni-based superalloy composite (mixed powder system)
- **Research objective:** To explore the microstructure and mechanical properties of SLM-processed K447A/IN718 composites, focusing on crack suppression and strengthening mechanism; identify the optimal composition ratio between K447A and IN718 powders.
- **Material category:** Additively manufactured Ni-based superalloy composite (SLM-processed). Not a refractory high-temperature alloy, Nb-W-based alloy, Mo-W-based alloy, or refractory high-entropy alloy.

---

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Base alloy system:** Ni-based superalloy composites fabricated by mixing K447A and IN718 powders.
- **K447A:** A non-weldable Ni-based superalloy highly prone to hot cracking during AM processing; contains higher Al + Ti content (implied by comparison to IN718).
- **IN718:** A widely used AM-processable Ni-based superalloy with good formability and minimal cracking due to lower Al + Ti content.
- **Research samples:** Four composites with different weight fractions of K447A and IN718:
  - K447A (unmixed reference, 100% K447A)
  - KI46 (40 wt.% K447A + 60 wt.% IN718)
  - KI28 (20 wt.% K447A + 80 wt.% IN718)
  - KI19 (10 wt.% K447A + 90 wt.% IN718)
  - IN718 (unmixed reference, 100% IN718)
- **Research objective:** To reduce cracking susceptibility while retaining high-temperature performance in AM-processed K447A by blending with IN718.

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Powder preparation:** Two commercial powders, K447A and IN718 (purchased from Avic Mette Additive Technology Co., LTD), mixed using planetary ball milling for 8 hours.
- **Additive manufacturing method:** Selective Laser Melting (SLM) using an AFS-M260 machine.
- **SLM processing parameters:**
  - Laser power: 240 W
  - Laser spot diameter: 75 μm
  - Scanning speed: 900 mm/s
  - Powder layer thickness: 40 μm
  - Hatch spacing: 80 μm
  - Scanning strategy: Bidirectional with 67° rotation between layers
- **Heat treatment:** None reported. The paper states that "appropriate thermal treatment" is needed to mitigate adverse effects of Laves phase, but no post-processing heat treatment was performed in this study.
- **Arc melting, vacuum melting, induction melting, powder metallurgy, mechanical alloying, hot pressing, SPS, HIP, rolling, forging, homogenization, annealing, aging:** Not applicable / not stated in this paper.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
- **Matrix phase:** Face-centered cubic (FCC) γ phase in all powder samples, confirmed by XRD with prominent (111), (200), (220), and (311) diffraction peaks.
- **XRD peak shift:** The (111) peak shifts progressively to lower angles (43.64° → 43.47°) with increasing IN718 content, consistent with the larger lattice parameter of IN718 (~3.62 Å) compared to K447A (~3.58 Å). Mechanical mixing did not induce phase transformations.
- **Melt pool morphology:**
  - K447A and KI46: Keyhole-like shape with cracks observed at the center, indicating brittleness.
  - KI28: Fish-scale pattern (most stable morphology).
  - KI19 and IN718: More irregular morphology than KI28.
  - Average melt pool areas: K447A = 4842 μm²; KI46 = 4072.25 μm²; KI28 = 2961 μm²; KI19 = 4108.5 μm²; IN718 = 5393.5 μm².
  - KI28 has the smallest melt pool area.
  - Increasing IN718 proportion results in wider yet shallower molten pools, enhancing thermal stress accommodation and reducing crack formation.
- **Second phases:**
  - **Laves phase:** Enriched in Nb, primarily (Ni, Cr, Fe)₂(Nb, Mo, Ti), identified by EDS.
  - Laves phase content: K447A = 0.58%; KI46 = 0.71%; KI28 = 2.79%; KI19 = 2.68%; IN718 = 5.02%.
  - Morphology evolution with increasing IN718: punctate (KI46) → short-chain (KI28) → long-chain (KI19) → network-like (IN718).
- **Grain size, grain boundary features, elemental segregation, precipitate size/distribution:** Quantitative values not provided in the paper, except the Laves phase content and morphology descriptions.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed:**
  - Crack reduction as the primary strengthening mechanism in composites (not classical solid-solution, precipitation, dispersion, or grain-boundary strengthening).
  - The paper notes that Laves phase is brittle and generally reduces tensile strength and promotes crack growth; however, for KI28, the moderate short-chain Laves phase "contributed positively to strengthening" despite its inherent brittleness.
  - Strengthening contribution from K447A phase (referred to as "strengthening factors from the K447A phase").

- **Trend:** Both strength and ductility enhance as IN718 content increases. KI28 demonstrates the most significant enhancement.   - IN718: Shallow dimples suggesting inferior plasticity compared to KI28/KI19.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Nb]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
K447A/IN718 Ni-based superalloy composites (SLM-processed)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
Not explicitly stated in the original paper (Nb content in K447A and IN718 powders not provided). Nb is identified in Laves phases via EDS.

**[Main Function]**
Primary constituent of Laves phase formation in the as-built SLM microstructure.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
Nb segregates to interdendritic regions during SLM solidification, forming Laves phase (Ni, Cr, Fe)₂(Nb, Mo, Ti) enriched in Nb. The morphology evolves from punctate (low IN718) to short-chain, long-chain, and network-like structures (high IN718) as Laves phase content increases from 0.58% (K447A) to 5.02% (IN718).

**[Property Effect]**
The brittle nature of Laves phase in SLM-built superalloys reduces tensile strength and promotes crack growth (as stated for excessive Laves phase). However, in KI28, moderate short-chain Laves phase (2.79%) "contributed positively to strengthening." KI28 achieved YS = 784 ± 10 MPa, UTS = 1105 ± 13 MPa, elongation = 23.7 ± 0.2%.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
Excessive Laves phase (network-like morphology in IN718, 5.02%) compromises both strength and plasticity. The paper states the increased presence of Laves phase "may compromise both strength and plasticity, necessitating appropriate thermal treatment to mitigate its adverse effects."

**[Original Evidence]**
"EDS analysis was conducted. The results indicate that these Laves phases are enriched in Nb and primarily consist of (Ni, Cr, Fe)₂(Nb, Mo, Ti) (Supplementary table 1)." "The calculated Laves phase content is 0.58 %, 0.71 %, 2.79 %, 2.68 %, and 5.02 % for K447A, KI46, KI28, KI19 and IN78, respectively." "the brittle nature of Laves phase in SLM-built superalloys is well-documented as an undesirable factor, reducing tensile strength and promoting crack growth" (Section 3, Fig. 3 discussion).

## [Al + Ti]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Ni-based superalloys processed by additive manufacturing (K447A vs. IN718 comparison)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
Not explicitly stated in the original paper. The paper only states K447A has higher Al + Ti content than IN718.

**[Main Function]**
Determines weldability/cracking susceptibility during AM processing. Higher Al + Ti content (K447A) increases hot cracking susceptibility; lower Al + Ti content (IN718) enables good formability with minimal cracking.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
The paper does not provide a detailed microstructural mechanism for the Al + Ti effect on cracking in this specific study. The implication is that higher Al + Ti alters solidification behavior and promotes cracking (likely through precipitation of γ′ phase and solidification range effects, though this is not explicitly stated in the paper).

**[Property Effect]**
K447A (higher Al + Ti) exhibits extensive cracking during SLM; IN718 (lower Al + Ti) shows good formability with minimal cracking. Blending with IN718 reduces cracking in the composite.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
High Al + Ti content leads to non-weldability and extensive hot cracking, as demonstrated by K447A's significantly low strength and ductility due to extensive cracking during SLM.

**[Original Evidence]**
"IN718 has been extensively processed using various AM techniques and demonstrated good formability with minimal cracking due to its lower Al + Ti content [9]." "K447A is a well-known non-weldable alloy that is highly prone to hot cracking [4]." (Introduction)

## [IN718 (as an alloying/composite component)]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
K447A/IN718 Ni-based superalloy composite (SLM-processed)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
0–100 wt.% IN718. Optimal balance at 80 wt.% IN718 (KI28: 20% K447A + 80% IN718).

**[Main Function]**
Primary role in composite: reduces crack formation during SLM by widening and shallowing the melt pool, improving thermal stress accommodation, and reducing melt pool area. Also improves ductility at room temperature.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
Increasing IN718 proportion results in wider yet shallower molten pools, enhancing ability to accommodate thermal stress and reducing crack formation. KI28 (80% IN718) exhibits the smallest melt pool area (2961 μm²) and fish-scale pattern, indicating minimal heat-affected zone and reduced cracking tendency. IN718's lower Al + Ti content contributes to its lower cracking susceptibility.

**[Property Effect]**
KI28 (80% IN718): YS = 784 ± 10 MPa, UTS = 1105 ± 13 MPa, elongation = 23.7 ± 0.2% — the most favorable mechanical properties among all samples. Fracture surface shows fine, tough dimples (ductile fracture).

**[Potential Side Effects]**
Excessive IN718 (90%+) reduces strength, likely due to reduced contribution of strengthening factors from the K447A phase. IN718 alone (100%) exhibits shallow dimples suggesting inferior plasticity compared to KI28. High IN718 content also increases Laves phase formation (5.02% for IN718 vs. 2.79% for KI28).

**[Original Evidence]**
"the increased proportion of IN718 results in wider yet shallower molten pool, enhancing its ability to accommodate thermal stress and reducing crack formation." "The average melt pool areas are measured as... 2961 μm² for KI28". "Both strength and ductility enhance as the IN718 content increases. Notably, KI28 demonstrates the most significant enhancement" (Sections 3 and 4).

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
K447A/IN718 Ni-based superalloy composite (SLM-processed)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
0–100 wt.% K447A. Optimal balance at 20 wt.% K447A (KI28: 20% K447A + 80% IN718).

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Crack Suppression via Compositional Blending (AM-oriented composition control)

**Related elements:** Ni, Cr, Fe, Nb, Mo, Ti (constituent elements of K447A and IN718), Al + Ti (key differentiator between the two alloys)

**Typical alloys:** K447A/IN718 blended composites (KI46, KI28, KI19)

**Key compositions:** KI28 = 20 wt.% K447A + 80 wt.% IN718 (optimal); KI46 = 40% K447A + 60% IN718; KI19 = 10% K447A + 90% IN718

**Microstructural changes:**
- Melt pool morphology changes from keyhole-like (K447A) to fish-scale (KI28) with increasing IN718.
- Average melt pool area decreases from 4842 μm² (K447A) to a minimum of 2961 μm² (KI28).
- Wider yet shallower molten pools with increasing IN718.
- Laves phase morphology evolves: punctate (KI46) → short-chain (KI28) → long-chain (KI19) → network-like (IN718).

**Property improvements:**
- KI28 achieved the most favorable mechanical properties: YS = 784 ± 10 MPa, UTS = 1105 ± 13 MPa, elongation = 23.7 ± 0.2%.
- Ductile fracture mode (fine, tough dimples) in KI28 and KI19 vs. brittle fracture (cleavage planes, cracks, pores) in K447A and KI46.
- Reduced cracking susceptibility in KI28 and KI19.

**Risks and limitations:**
- Excessive IN718 (90%+) reduces strength due to reduced K447A strengthening contribution.
- Laves phase content increases with IN718 (up to 5.02% for IN718), which generally reduces tensile strength and promotes crack growth.
- The paper explicitly notes that "appropriate thermal treatment" is needed to mitigate adverse effects of Laves phase.
- No high-temperature mechanical testing was performed in this study, so high-temperature performance is not confirmed.

## Laves Phase Formation and Its Dual Role

# Key Conclusions
1. **SLM processing was successful:** Ni-based superalloys were successfully fabricated via SLM using mechanically mixed K447A and IN718 powders. Ball milling (8 h) preserved the crystal structure and ensured compositional uniformity.
2. **Optimal composition identified:** KI28 (20 wt.% K447A + 80 wt.% IN718) was identified as the optimal composition.
3. **Melt pool and cracking relationship:** Increased IN718 proportion led to reduced melt pool area (minimum 2961 μm² for KI28), wider yet shallower molten pools, improved thermal stress accommodation, and enhanced cracking resistance. KI28 and KI19 showed the most stable morphology (fish-scale pattern for KI28).
4. **Laves phase behavior:** KI28 exhibited moderate formation of short-chain Laves phases (2.79%), which, despite inherent brittleness, contributed positively to strengthening.
5. **Mechanical properties:** KI28 demonstrated the most favorable mechanical properties: YS = 784 ± 10 MPa, UTS = 1105 ± 13 MPa, elongation = 23.7 ± 0.2%.
6. **Strengthening mechanism:** The primary mechanism for property improvement is the reduction in crack formation due to IN718 addition (better thermal stress accommodation), combined with strengthening contribution from the K447A phase at moderate content. Excessive IN718 (90%+) reduces strength due to reduced K447A strengthening contribution.
7. **Fracture mode transition:** K447A and KI46 showed brittle fracture (cleavage planes, open cracks, pores); KI28 and KI19 showed ductile fracture (fine, tough dimples).

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Alloy system relevance:** This paper addresses Ni-based superalloys (K447A/IN718), not refractory high-temperature alloys (Nb-W, Mo-W, etc.), refractory high-entropy alloys, or ODS refractory alloys. The cracking suppression approach by blending a weldable alloy (IN718) with a non-weldable alloy (K447A) may have conceptual relevance to AM of refractory alloys facing similar cracking challenges, but no direct refractory alloy data is provided.
- **No high-temperature mechanical testing:** All mechanical tests were performed at room temperature. High-temperature performance — the primary application motivation for K447A — was not experimentally verified in this study.
- **No full chemical composition provided:** The paper does not provide the elemental compositions of K447A and IN718 powders. Only mixture weight ratios are given. This limits the ability to extract precise element-function relationships.
- **No heat treatment performed:** All samples were tested in the as-built (SLM) condition. The paper acknowledges that thermal treatment is needed to mitigate Laves phase adverse effects, but no heat treatment data is provided.
- **Limited microstructural quantification:** Grain size, dendrite spacing, and quantitative segregation data are not provided. Only Laves phase content (calculated via image analysis, presumably) and melt pool area are quantified.
- **Single set of SLM parameters:** Only one set of SLM parameters (240 W, 75 μm spot, 900 mm/s, 40 μm layer, 80 μm hatch) was used. The effect of varying process parameters on the composite was not explored.
- **No oxidation data:** Despite mentioning K447A's exceptional oxidation resistance in the introduction, no oxidation testing was performed.
- **Statistical limitations:** Tensile results averaged from three samples, but no statistical analysis beyond standard deviation is provided.
- **References to prior work:** The paper cites prior studies on K447A cracking mitigation (pulsed-wave laser duty cycle modulation, shot peening, laser surface remelting) from other publications; these data are not part of the current study's experimental results.
