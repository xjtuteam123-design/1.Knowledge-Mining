## {10-12}α Twins (Deformation Mechanism, not an alloying element)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ti–6Al–4V titanium alloy under impact loading at low temperatures
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not applicable (deformation mechanism, not a composition parameter)
- **Main Function**: Twinning acts as a coordinated deformation mode that enhances crack propagation resistance and contributes to higher impact toughness at 298 K and 223 K.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**:
  - {10-12}α twins formed near impact fracture at 298 K and 223 K with a misorientation of approximately 85° with the matrix.
  - No twins formed at 77 K due to weakened lattice thermal vibration and increased lattice resistance, preventing slip systems from reaching critical shear stress.
  - Twins can transfer grain boundaries (TT) when the misorientation angle (MA) is small; at 298 K, TT occurred for MA ranges 7°–35.6°, and twin blockage (TB) occurred for MA ranges 46.8°–86.3°.
  - At 223 K, TT occurred for MA ranges 4.3°–35.3°, and TB for MA ranges 45.6°–81.3°.
  - Temperature difference of 75 K has minimal effect on TT behavior.
- **Property Effect**:
  - 298 K: Impact toughness 53.3 J/cm² with twins present.
  - 223 K: Impact toughness 45.7 J/cm² with twins present.
  - 77 K: Impact toughness 21.2 J/cm² without twins.
  - Higher impact toughness at 298 K and 223 K is attributed to coordinated deformation by dislocations and twins, creating more tortuous crack propagation paths.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not applicable.
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 4 and associated discussion on Pages 4–5; Conclusion section on Page 5.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Twin-Induced Toughening

- **Related Elements**: Not element-specific; deformation mechanism in Ti–6Al–4V alloy.
- **Typical Alloys**: Ti–6Al–4V (α+β titanium alloy)
- **Key Compositions**: 5.9 wt.% Al, 4.1 wt.% V, balance Ti
- **Microstructural Changes**:
  - {10-12}α twins form under impact loading at 298 K and 223 K but not at 77 K.
  - Twin formation is enhanced when multiple slip systems are active.
  - Twin transfer (TT) occurs across low-angle grain boundaries (small MA); twin blockage (TB) occurs at high-angle grain boundaries (large MA).
- **Property Improvements**:
  - Impact toughness: 53.3 J/cm² at 298 K and 45.7 J/cm² at 223 K vs. 21.2 J/cm² at 77 K.
  - Higher toughness correlates with twin presence, tortuous crack paths, larger shear lip regions (~48–49% at 298 K and 223 K vs. 34% at 77 K), and more undulating fracture surfaces (5.9 mm and 5.5 mm height at 298 K and 223 K vs. 1.18 mm at 77 K).
- **Risks and Limitations**:
  - At 77 K, lack of twin activation and fewer slip systems lead to rapid crack propagation and low toughness.
  - Low temperature reduces the number of active deformation modes, causing a linear crack propagation path.

# Key Conclusions

1. Ti–6Al–4V alloy (hot-rolled, annealed at 700 °C for 4 h) has an equiaxed α+β duplex microstructure.
2. Impact toughness decreases with decreasing temperature:
   - 298 K: 53.3 J/cm²
   - 223 K: 45.7 J/cm²
   - 77 K: 21.2 J/cm²
3. {10-12}α twins formed near impact fracture surfaces at 298 K and 223 K but were absent at 77 K, indicating a temperature-dependent deformation mechanism.
4. The presence of twins and multiple slip systems at 298 K and 223 K leads to a tortuous crack propagation path, larger shear lip regions, and higher impact toughness.
5. At 77 K, the absence of twins and limited slip system activation result in a more linear crack path, lower shear lip area, and rapid crack propagation.
6. Twin transfer (TT) across grain boundaries occurs when the misorientation angle is small (≈4.3°–35.6°); twin blockage (TB) occurs at large misorientation angles (≈45.6°–86.3°). Temperature (298 K vs. 223 K) has minimal effect on TT behavior.
7. TT releases system energy during impact, contributing to higher impact toughness.
