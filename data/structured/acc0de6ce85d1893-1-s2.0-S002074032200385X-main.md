# Paper Metadata
- **Paper title:** A microstructural rafting state-based constitutive model for single crystal Ni-based superalloys at elevated temperature
- **Authors:** Y.S. Fan, L. Tan, X.G. Yang, D.Q. Shi, R.Z. Li, J. Huang, C. Wang
- **Journal:** International Journal of Mechanical Sciences
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmecsci.2022.107484
- **Alloy system studied:** Single crystal Ni-based superalloy (DD6)
- **Research objective:** To develop a thermodynamically microstructural state-based constitutive model for single crystal Ni-based superalloys that captures the effect of rafting on both long/short-term deformation behaviours at high temperature by introducing a rafting state variable into the crystal plasticity framework.
- **Material category:** Single crystal Ni-based superalloy (γ/γ′ two-phase microstructure)

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Material:** DD6, a second generation single crystal Ni-based superalloy.
- **Alloy system:** Ni-based superalloy with γ (FCC matrix) + γ′ (L1₂ ordered precipitate) two-phase microstructure.
- **Key microstructural features:** High volume fraction (70%) of cuboidal γ′ precipitates embedded in γ matrix channels. Initial γ′ precipitate width ≈ 550 nm, initial γ matrix channel width ≈ 50 nm.
- **Research focus:** Constitutive modelling of microstructural rafting (directional coarsening of γ′ precipitates under stress at high temperature) and its effect on inelastic deformation behaviour, including tensile, cyclic, and creep responses.
- **Relevance to refractory/high-temperature alloys:** This is a Ni-based superalloy system, not refractory alloys (Nb-W, Mo-W, W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloys). It is included for its relevance to high-temperature constitutive modelling and microstructural degradation mechanisms.

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Heat treatment (virgin DD6):**
  - Two-stage solution heat treatment:
    - Primary solution treatment: 1563 K/1 h, 1573 K/2 h, 1588 K/4 h
    - Secondary solution treatment: 1393 K/4 h
  - Aging treatment: 1143 K/32 h
  - Resulting γ′ volume fraction at virgin state: 70%
  - Statistical mean γ′ precipitate width: 550 nm
  - Statistical mean γ matrix channel width: 50 nm
- **Pre-rafting treatment:**
  - Condition 1: 1323 K / 100 MPa / 50 h → rafting state ξ = 0.48
  - Condition 2: 1323 K / 100 MPa / 100 h → rafting state ξ = 0.68
  - Condition 3 (centre-hole specimen): 1323 K / 100 MPa / 60 h
- **Processing methods relevant to this paper:** Not applicable (alloy is supplied as single crystal cast material; processing methods focus on heat treatment and pre-rafting treatments rather than alloy fabrication).
- **Rolling, forging, mechanical alloying, SPS, HIP, AM methods:** Not mentioned in this paper.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
- **Matrix phase:** γ phase (FCC Ni-based solid solution)
- **Strengthening phase:** γ′ precipitates (L1₂ ordered Ni₃(Al,Ti,Ta) type), initial volume fraction = 70%, initial cuboidal morphology with width ≈ 550 nm.
- **γ matrix channels:** Initial width ≈ 50 nm. Rafting causes γ channel widening.
- **Second phases/precipitates:** γ′ precipitates only. No carbide, oxide, boride, or silicide precipitates discussed in this paper.
- **Elemental segregation:** The paper discusses elemental diffusion (Al, Ti, Ta) between γ and γ′ phases during rafting (Section 2.1), driven by chemical potential gradients at the γ′/γ interfaces under applied stress.
- **Grain boundaries:** Not applicable (single crystal).
- **Microstructural degradation mechanisms discussed:**
  - Rafting (directional coarsening of γ′ precipitates under stress)
  - P-type rafting: needle-like morphology parallel to applied compressive stress (negative misfit)
  - N-type rafting: plate-like morphology perpendicular to applied tensile stress (negative misfit)
  - Isotropic coarsening (Lifshitz-Slyozov-Wagner (LSW) law)
  - γ′ volume fraction decrease during rafting (from 70% virgin to lower saturation values)
  - Topological inversion (mentioned as related to micro cracks and voids, but not modelled explicitly)
  - TCP phase precipitation (mentioned as important in creep damage, but not modelled explicitly)
- **Dislocation-related microstructures:**
  - Dislocation networks at γ′/γ interfaces
  - Equivalent dislocation wall model (thickness t_wall, volume fraction f_w)
  - Dislocation density evolution in γ matrix channels
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed:**
  - Orowan bowing mechanism (γ′ precipitates impede dislocation motion; Orowan stress depends on γ matrix channel width)
  - Dislocation network hardening (back stress)
  - Dislocation density-based isotropic hardening (Bailey-Hirsch relationship)
  - Solid-solution strengthening: Not explicitly discussed as separate mechanism, but elements like W, Mo, Re, Ta provide strength.

# Mechanical Properties
- **General note:** This paper is primarily a constitutive modelling paper; mechanical properties are presented as stress–strain data for model calibration and validation. Specific mechanical property values are extracted below.
- **Tensile data (DD6 along [001] orientation):**
  - Test conditions: 1253 K and 1373 K, strain rate 1.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹
  - Rafting states tested: virgin (ξ = 0), pre-rafted 50 h (ξ = 0.48), pre-rafted 100 h (ξ = 0.68)
  - Key observation: Yield stress decreases with increasing rafting extent (ξ) due to γ matrix channel widening and reduction of Orowan stress.
  - Variation in ξ and [001] γ channel width during tensile test at 1253 K and 1373 K under 4% total strain: increase in ξ is "very small during tensile process" and can be ignored for short-time deformation.
- **Cyclic data (DD6, strain-controlled, 1253 K):**
  - Strain rates: 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹, 5.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹, 1.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹
  - Strain ratio: -1
  - Rafting states tested: virgin (ξ = 0), ξ = 0.48, ξ = 0.68
  - Progressive loading scheme: Δε = 1.4% for 30 cycles; Δε = 1.6% for 20 cycles; Δε = 2.0% for 10 cycles; Δε = 2.4% for 10 cycles; Δε = 3.0% until fracture.
  - Key observations:
    - Hysteresis loop becomes "shorter and stouter" as ξ increases.
    - No noticeable cyclic softening/hardening overall; stabilizes within first few cycles at 1253 K.
    - 5th hysteresis loop used for model validation.
- **Creep data (DD6):**
  - Data from Zhang et al. [80] for model calibration and validation.
  - Conditions at 1253 K: 220 MPa (validation), 250 MPa (calibration), 300 MPa (validation)
  - Conditions at 1373 K: 125 MPa (validation), 140 MPa (calibration), 160 MPa (validation)
  - Key observation: Model can simulate tertiary creep stage via coupled microstructural rafting model, without explicit creep cavitation damage mechanisms. Underestimates creep strain near final failure due to excluded mechanisms (micro cracks, voids growth, TCP precipitation, topological inversion).
- **Back stress behaviour:**
  - Back stress in {110}<111> slip direction initially increases with ξ, then reduces quickly (Fig. 11). Competitive effect between γ′ precipitate size and volume fraction.
  - When ξ is small, f_γ′ is stable, so back stress hardens via dislocation wall thickening; as ξ increases, f_γ′ decreases, reducing dislocation wall volume fraction and back stress.
- **Yield strength related parameters (model-based):**
  - Orowan stress τ_Or = αμb/[ω₀(1-ξ)] – decreases as ξ increases (γ channel widens).
  - Initial threshold stress: τ₀_g = 350 MPa at 1253 K; τ₀_g = 230 MPa at 1373 K.
  - Reference slip rate: 0.12 s⁻¹ at 1253 K; 0.8 s⁻¹ at 1373 K.
  - Rate sensitivity exponent n = 10 (1253 K); n = 8 (1373 K).
  - Elastic constants: C₁₁ = 169.5 GPa, C₁₂ = 108.4 GPa, C₄₄ = 78 GPa at 1253 K; C₁₁ = 150 GPa, C₁₂ = 100 GPa, C₄₄ = 62 GPa at 1373 K.
- **Centre-hole specimen analysis:**
  - Applied stress: 100 MPa (tensile along [001]) during rafting treatment at 1323 K for 60 h.
  - Stress concentration near hole accelerates rafting. Equivalent inelastic strain accumulates near hole root.
  - Rafting type distribution: Most locations show N-type rafting (OF_k = {-1,-1,2}); upper corner shows needle-like P-type prediction (OF_k = {1,-2,1}) but experimentally cubic (due to low stress).
- **Hardness, fracture toughness, elastic modulus, density, elongation, compressive properties:** Not explicitly provided in original paper.

# Key Conclusions
1. A thermodynamically consistent constitutive model for SC Ni-based superalloys is proposed, coupling microstructural rafting with crystal plasticity framework through a rafting state variable ξ.
2. The rafting state variable ξ = 1 - ω₀/ω_max is shown to satisfy positive dissipation requirements (thermodynamic second law).
3. The coupling between rafting and constitutive behaviour is achieved through:
   - Reduction of Orowan stress due to γ matrix channel widening: τ_Or = αμb/[ω₀(1-ξ)]
   - Modification of dislocation density evolution via mean free path increase
   - Microstructure-based back stress model incorporating dislocation wall theory, γ′ morphology, and γ′ volume fraction variation.
4. γ′ volume fraction decreases with rafting according to power law: f_γ′ = f₀_γ′ - Δf · ξ^fa (Δf = 0.48, fa = 3).
5. The model successfully validates:
   - Tensile behaviour at 1253 K and 1373 K for virgin and rafted states (ξ = 0.48, 0.68).
   - Cyclic behaviour at 1253 K with hysteresis loop morphology changes (shorter and stouter with increasing ξ).
   - Creep deformation at 1253 K and 1373 K, including tertiary creep via microstructural rafting coupling without explicit damage mechanisms.
   - Rate-dependent behaviour at 1.0E-4 s⁻¹ and 5.0E-3 s⁻¹.
6. Back stress initially increases with ξ due to dislocation wall thickening, then decreases as f_γ′ decreases at higher ξ.
7. Centre-hole specimen analysis demonstrates capability for 3D engineering component analysis with inhomogeneous microstructural rafting. Model predicts N-type rafting in most locations, consistent with experiments.
8. Limitations: Only octahedral slip systems considered; cubic slip ignored. Rafting extent considered but not rafting orientation effect on deformation. Micro cracks, voids, TCP precipitation, and topological inversion not explicitly modelled (underestimates creep strain near final failure).

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Alloy system:** DD6 SC Ni-based superalloy only. Not directly applicable to refractory Nb-W, Mo-W, W-based alloys, or refractory high-entropy alloys. However, the constitutive modelling framework for microstructural degradation (rafting) may be conceptually adaptable to other two-phase high-temperature alloy systems.
- **Temperature range:** Validated at 1253 K and 1373 K. Applicability to lower or higher temperatures not demonstrated.
- **Slip systems:** Octahedral {111}<110> slip only. Cubic slip ignored. Inelastic deformation along directions deviated from principal crystal axis cannot be modelled well.
- **Rafting orientation:** Model only considers rafting extent ξ, not rafting orientation effect on deformation. Morphology (plate-like vs needle-like) orientation effects on mechanical properties are not captured.
- **Damage mechanisms:** Micro cracks, void growth, TCP phase precipitation, topological inversion, and creep cavitation are not explicitly modelled. Model underestimates creep strain near final failure.
- **Small strain framework:** Model formulated under small strain theory, though extensible to finite deformation.
- **Feasibility for other alloy systems:** The element-function knowledge cards are limited to the DD6 composition; W, Mo, Ta, Re, Al, Hf, C are present, but their individual property contributions are not quantified, and oxidation resistance is not addressed.
- **Processing consideration:** Applicable to as-cast and heat-treated SC superalloy microstructure; no consideration of AM processing, ODS, or other processing routes.
