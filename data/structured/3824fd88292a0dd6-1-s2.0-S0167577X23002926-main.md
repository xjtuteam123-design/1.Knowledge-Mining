# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy System Studied:** AlCrFeMnNi₂ high-entropy alloy (HEA)
- **Material Category:** BCC/B2 dual-phase high-entropy alloy with eutectic microstructure. Not a refractory high-entropy alloy in the strict sense; it is an Al-Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni transition-metal HEA. This paper falls outside the specific refractory Nb-W/Mo-W/refractory HEA domain, but is provided for literature parsing according to the general high-entropy alloy domain.
- **Research Objective:** To develop a new BCC-based high-entropy alloy with an excellent combination of high strength and plasticity by introducing a unique sunflower-like eutectic microstructure. The paper aims to address the low plasticity limitation of BCC-based HEAs.
- **Alloy Designation:** Near-eutectic BCC/B2 AlCrFeMnNi₂ HEA
- **Phase Constitution:** Dual-phase, consisting of disordered BCC matrix phase and ordered B2 precipitates.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix Phase:** Disordered BCC phase.
- **Ordered Second Phase:** Ordered B2 phase, present in two morphologies:
  - **Primary B2 (p-B2):** Rod-shaped precipitates in the petal region.
  - **Secondary B2 (s-B2):** Spherical or ellipsoidal precipitates in the flower disk region.
- **Lattice Parameters:**
  - BCC phase: 2.875 Å
  - B2 phase: 2.904 Å
- **Crystal Structure Confirmation:** XRD (Fig. 1a) and TEM selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns (Fig. 1e and 1f). The p-B2 phase was identified by unique B2 superlattice reflections (100 and 111̄). The s-B2 phase was similarly identified.
- **Coherency:** Both p-B2 and s-B2 phases maintain a coherent relationship with the BCC matrix phase.
- **Grain Size / Colony Size:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper. The microstructure consists of eutectic grains or colonies, each resembling a sunflower-like morphology with a flower disk region and a petal region.
- **Grain Boundary Features:** Thick layers (light-grey area) separate all elliptical grains. This is related to “grain-boundary wetting” phenomena, as referenced from Ref [11] (Straumal et al., 2021). The wetting layers are associated with grain boundaries, and the paper states this morphology can influence phase transition and mechanical properties, but no specific elemental composition or thickness of the wetting layer is reported.
- **Elemental Segregation:** EDS results (Table S1 in supplementary materials) indicate:
  - **p-B2 and s-B2 phases are enriched in Ni and Al.**
  - **Disordered BCC phase is enriched in Fe and Cr.**
- **B2 Precipitate Size and Distribution:**
  - **p-B2 (petal region):** width ~0.5–2 μm, length ~1–10 μm; spacing between p-B2 precipitates ~0.1 to ~0.5 μm. Rod-shaped precipitates are alternately arranged in the petal region.
  - **s-B2 (disk region):** size ~200 nm; uniformly distributed in the disk region.
- **Overall Microstructure Description:** Sunflower-like eutectic microstructure with a bimodal distribution of B2 precipitates. Each eutectic colony comprises a flower disk region (containing ellipsoidal s-B2 precipitates) and a petal region (containing rod-shaped p-B2 precipitates). This is distinct from spinodal decomposition microstructure typical of dendritic BCC/B2 HEAs.
- **Discussion of Strengthening Mechanisms Present in Paper:**
  - The paper explicitly attributes mechanical properties to coherent interfaces between BCC and B2 phases and bimodal distribution of B2 precipitates.
  - The paper mentions that coherent internal boundaries can absorb dislocations and hinder dislocation movement (citing Lu, Lu, Suresh, Science 324 (2009) 349–352), synergistically improving strength and plasticity.
  - The paper mentions that s-B2 phase can hinder dislocation movement and crack growth.
  - The paper mentions that the petal region lath eutectic structure can change crack direction and inhibit crack propagation, improving fracture strength.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Al, Cr, Fe, Mn, Ni (multi-principal element solid solutions)
- **Typical Alloys:** AlCrFeMnNi₂ BCC/B2 HEA
- **Key Compositions:** Nominal Al:Cr:Fe:Mn:Ni = 1:1:1:1:2

- **Property Improvements:** Yield strength 1206 MPa; fracture strength 3437 MPa.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Not explicitly discussed in this paper. The paper does not isolate solid-solution strengthening as a separate mechanism and does not report individual element contributions.

## Coherent Interface Strengthening (Coherent Internal Boundary Strengthening)

- **Related Elements:** Not element-specific; related to BCC/B2 coherent interfaces enabled by Al+Ni enrichment and Fe+Cr matrix partitioning.
- **Typical Alloys:** AlCrFeMnNi₂ BCC/B2 HEA.
- **Key Compositions:** Nominal Al:Cr:Fe:Mn:Ni = 1:1:1:1:2; BCC/B2 lattice mismatch not explicitly quantified but lattice parameters differ slightly (2.875 Å vs 2.904 Å).
- **Microstructural Changes:** Coherent interfaces between BCC matrix and B2 precipitates. TEM after 20% compression shows a large number of dislocations in BCC phase and dislocation accumulation around the coherent interface between p-B2 and BCC phase.
- **Property Improvements:** The coherent interface absorbs dislocations and hinders dislocation movement, synergistically improving strength and plasticity. Paper attributes 1206 MPa yield strength, 3437 MPa fracture strength, and 37.5% plastic strain partly to coherent internal boundaries.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Not explicitly stated. However, the paper notes that spinodal decomposition microstructures in dendritic BCC/B2 HEAs have low plasticity (e.g., AlCoCrFeNi with 10% plastic strain), and the benefit here is attributed specifically to the coherent interfaces present in the eutectic microstructure.

## Second-Phase Strengthening (B2 Precipitate Strengthening)

- **Related Elements:** Al and Ni (B2 phase), Fe and Cr (BCC matrix).
- **Typical Alloys:** AlCrFeMnNi₂ BCC/B2 HEA; referenced BCC/B2 HEAs: AlCoCrFeNi, AlCoCrNi.
- **Key Compositions:** Bimodal B2 precipitates: rod-shaped p-B2 (width 0.5–2 μm, length 1–10 μm, spacing ~0.1–0.5 μm) in petal region; ellipsoidal s-B2 (~200 nm) uniformly distributed in disk region.
- **Microstructural Changes:** B2 precipitates act as obstacles to dislocation motion. After deformation, fine fragments embedded with s-B2 precipitates form in disk region. In petal region, cracks initiate along phase boundaries between p-B2 and BCC phase rather than penetrating.
- **Property Improvements:** s-B2 hinders dislocation movement (strength) and crack growth (plasticity); lath B2 structure changes crack direction and inhibits single-direction crack propagation, improving fracture strength.
- **Risks and Limitations:** BCC/B2 spinodal dendritic HEAs are often brittle; coarse, ultrafine, or nano-grained single BCC/B2 HEAs are brittle due to inherent BCC brittleness. The paper attributes these limitations to lack of the sunflower-like coherent B2 distribution.

# Key Conclusions

1. A new near-eutectic BCC/B2 AlCrFeMnNi₂ high-entropy alloy was fabricated by arc melting with a unique sunflower-like eutectic microstructure.
2. The microstructure consists of a disordered BCC matrix, ordered ellipsoidal B2 precipitates (s-B2, ~200 nm) in the flower disk region, and rod-shaped B2 precipitates (p-B2, width 0.5–2 μm, length 1–10 μm) in the petal region. Both B2 morphologies are coherent with the BCC matrix.
3. The alloy exhibits a compressive yield strength of 1206 MPa, fracture strength of 3437 MPa, and large compressive plastic strain of 37.5% at a strain rate of 5 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹.
4. These mechanical properties are superior to many other BCC/B2 high-entropy alloys, including AlCoCrFeNi (yield 1380 MPa, plastic strain 10%) and HfMoTaTiZr (yield 1600 MPa, plastic strain 4%).
5. The excellent combination of strength and ductility is attributed to the coherent interfaces between BCC and B2 phases and the bimodal distribution of B2 precipitates.
6. Fracture analysis shows large plastic deformation in elongated eutectic colonies; s-B2 hinders dislocation movement and crack growth; the lath eutectic structure changes crack direction and inhibits crack propagation.
7. After 20% compressive strain, numerous dislocations accumulate around coherent p-B2/BCC interfaces, indicating that coherent internal boundaries play a key role in simultaneously improving strength and plasticity.
8. The paper concludes that this alloy design may provide a meaningful reference for improving the plasticity of BCC-based high-entropy alloys.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Alloy Domain:** This is an Al-Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni transition-metal high-entropy alloy, not a refractory Nb-W, Mo-W, Zr/Ta-containing, or additively manufactured refractory alloy. The paper is parsed for general HEA knowledge purposes; it is not within the specialized refractory-alloy scope.
- **Mechanical Testing:** Only room-temperature compressive tests were performed. No tensile, high-temperature compressive, creep, or fracture toughness tests are reported. The high-temperature capability of this alloy is not established.
- **No High-Temperature or Oxidation Data:** The paper does not provide any oxidation resistance measurements, high-temperature mechanical properties, or long-term service stability data.
- **Processing Details:** The arc-melting procedure is not fully described; readers are referred to the authors’ prior publication (Ref [9]) for details. No heat treatment, homogenization, or thermomechanical processing was applied.
- **Microstructure Quantification:** Grain/colony size is not quantified. The composition and thickness of the grain-boundary wetting layer are not reported. The exact lattice mismatch and coherency strain are not quantified.
- **Element Distribution:** Mn partitioning is not discussed. Individual contributions of alloying elements to strengthening are not quantified.
- **Comparison Data:** Detailed compressive data for comparison BCC/B2 HEAs are in supplementary Table S2; those data are not fully reproduced in the main text.
- **Single Alloy Studied:** Only one nominal composition (AlCrFeMnNi₂) is investigated, so the effect of varying Al, Ni, Cr, Fe, or Mn content is not directly assessed in this paper.
- **Fabrication Route:** The results are specific to the as-cast arc-melted condition. The behavior after hot working, additive manufacturing, or heat treatments is not covered.
- **Mechanism Attribution:** The authors attribute the property enhancement to coherent BCC/B2 interfaces and bimodal B2 precipitate distribution, but this is not validated by finite element/phase-field modeling or systematic variation of microstructure in the current study.
