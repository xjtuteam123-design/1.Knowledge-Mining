# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** Molecular dynamics simulation of thermomechanical fatigue properties of Ni-based single crystal superalloys
- **Authors:** Wen-Ping Wu, Zi-Jun Ding, Yun-Li Li, Chao Yu, Guozheng Kang
- **Journal:** International Journal of Fatigue
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2023.107667
- **Alloy system studied:** Ni-based single crystal superalloys (γ matrix phase: pure Ni with FCC structure; γʹ precipitate phase: Ni3Al with L12 structure)
- **Research objective:** To investigate the thermomechanical fatigue (TMF) properties and microstructure evolution of Ni-based single crystal superalloys using molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, and to compare with isothermal fatigue (IF) behavior
- **Material category:** Ni-based single crystal superalloy (modeled as γ/γʹ two-phase system, not a refractory alloy or refractory high-entropy alloy)

**Note:** This paper is a computational molecular dynamics simulation study of Ni-based single crystal superalloys. It does NOT involve refractory high-temperature alloys, Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. The paper contains no experimental alloy composition data, no processing/metallurgical routes, no oxide dispersion strengthening, no carbide/boride precipitation, and no oxidation resistance data.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase:** γ phase (pure Ni, FCC structure)
- **Precipitate phase:** γʹ phase (Ni3Al, L12 structure)
- **Interface:** Coincidence site lattice (CSL) with misfit dislocation network; 35-fold Ni3Al lattice corresponds to 36-fold Ni lattice (n ≈ 35)
- **Initial microstructure after relaxation:** Regular interfacial dislocation network (tetrakaidecahedron dislocation network at the γ/γʹ interface)
- **Dislocation evolution under TMF loading:**
  - High-temperature half-cycle: Higher dislocation density; higher temperature promotes activation of more slip systems; formation of dislocation loops and tangling in γ matrix channels and at γ/γʹ interface; Orowan bypassing mechanism
  - Low-temperature half-cycle: Lower dislocation density; more stacking faults (SFs) formed; perfect dislocations decompose into Shockley partial dislocations and SFs via reaction: 1/2[10̄1] → 1/6[2̄11] + 1/6[1̄1̄2] + SF (IP-TMF) or 1/2[101] → 1/6[1̄12] + 1/6[211] + SF (OP-TMF)
  - Under IP-TMF: SFs remain confined to γ matrix channels in low-temperature compression half-cycle
  - Under OP-TMF: SFs and extended dislocations severely cut into γʹ precipitate phase in low-temperature tension half-cycle
- **Second phases or precipitates:** Only γʹ (Ni3Al); no carbides, oxides, borides, silicides, or other strengthening precipitates
- **Grain size:** Not applicable (single crystal model; periodic boundary conditions)
- **Elemental segregation:** Not discussed (binary Ni-Al system model)
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed:**
  - Solid-solution strengthening: Not discussed (no solute atoms other than model components)
  - Precipitation strengthening: Implicitly relevant via γʹ precipitate providing dislocation pinning and strain hardening; but not framed as a design variable
  - Dispersion strengthening: Not discussed
  - Grain-boundary strengthening: Not discussed (single crystal)
  - ODS strengthening: Not discussed
  - Second-phase strengthening: γʹ phase acts as second phase impeding dislocation motion; dislocation loops and Orowan mechanism prevent dislocations from cutting γʹ at high temperature

- **Testing temperatures (simulated):**

- **Mean stress behavior:**
  - IP-TMF: σm ≈ −0.3 GPa (compressive mean stress)
  - OP-TMF: σm ≈ +1.5 GPa (tensile mean stress)
  - IF (both 300 K and 1200 K): σm ≈ 0 (tension–compression symmetric)
- **Cyclic stress range:**
  - OP-TMF: >9 GPa (highest)
  - IP-TMF: ~4 GPa (lowest)
  - IF-300 K and IF-1200 K: intermediate values

  - Highest under OP-TMF loading (stable stage)
  - Lowest under IP-TMF loading (stable stage)
- **Cyclic hardening/softening behavior:**
  - IF: Initial cyclic hardening in both tension and compression half-cycles, then cyclic stabilization

- **Fatigue life analysis:**
  - Coffin-Manson equation and Ostergren energy-based model used
  - OP-TMF has lower fatigue life than IP-TMF (based on higher Ostergren parameter and higher plastic strain energy density)

- **Element or Element Combination:** Al (in Ni3Al, γʹ phase)

## Combined Effect of Ni + Al (γ/γʹ Two-Phase System)

- **Element or Element Combination:** Ni + Al (γ/γʹ two-phase microstructure)
- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ni-based single crystal superalloy (idealized binary system)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** γ volume fraction = 27.1%, γʹ volume fraction = 72.9%; γ matrix = Ni, γʹ = Ni3Al
- **Main Function:** The γ/γʹ interface and the two-phase structure control the fatigue damage mechanism and determine the asymmetry in thermomechanical fatigue behavior
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** 
  - Interfacial dislocation network at γ/γʹ interface controls dislocation motion; at high temperature, dislocations form loops and concentrate at the interface (Orowan bypassing mechanism); at low temperature, dislocations and SFs cut into γʹ as extended dislocations

- **Property Effect:** 
  - Under TMF loading, significant tension–compression asymmetry is observed: IP-TMF has compressive mean stress (~−0.3 GPa) and OP-TMF has tensile mean stress (~+1.5 GPa)

# Key Conclusions

1. **Tension–compression asymmetry in TMF:** Ni-based single crystal superalloys exhibit a compressive mean stress under IP-TMF loading (~−0.3 GPa) and a tensile mean stress under OP-TMF loading (~+1.5 GPa). In contrast, IF loading produces near-zero mean stress.

2. **Cyclic stress range and energy dissipation:** OP-TMF loading produces the highest cyclic stress range (>9 GPa) and the highest plastic strain energy density (ΔWp), while IP-TMF loading produces the lowest cyclic stress range (~4 GPa) and the lowest ΔWp. This indicates that OP-TMF is the most damaging loading mode and leads to the shortest fatigue life.

3. **Temperature-dependent dislocation behavior:** High temperature promotes dislocation nucleation and emission, leading to higher dislocation density in the high-temperature half-cycle. Low temperature promotes the formation of stacking faults (SFs).

4. **Microstructure evolution differences:** In TMF loading, the high-temperature half-cycle is characterized by dislocations forming loops and concentrating in γ matrix channels and at the γ/γʹ interface (Orowan bypassing mechanism). In the low-temperature half-cycle, dislocations and SFs tend to cut into the γʹ precipitate phase as extended dislocations.

5. **Tensile strain promotes γʹ shearing:** At the same temperature, tensile strain is more favorable than compressive strain for dislocations and SFs to cut into the γʹ precipitate phase. This leads to more severe plastic deformation and is the primary reason why OP-TMF loading (with low-temperature tension half-cycle) causes the earliest failure.

6. **Fatigue life prediction:** Both Coffin-Manson and Ostergren energy-based analyses predict shorter fatigue life under OP-TMF loading than IP-TMF loading. The Ostergren parameter σ_max_t·Δεp is 24.65 GPa² for OP-TMF vs. 5.83 GPa² for IP-TMF (Table 1).

7. **Comparison with IF:** The TMF damage mechanism is fundamentally different from IF. In IF, dislocation motion forms are almost identical in tension and compression half-cycles, while in TMF, asymmetric temperature-dependent dislocation behavior dominates.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Computational idealization:** The model is a highly idealized binary Ni-Al system representing Ni-based single crystal superalloys. It does not include Cr, Co, Mo, W, Re, Ta, Ti, Hf, C, B, or other alloying elements present in real Ni-based superalloys.
- **No chemical degradation:** Oxidation, corrosion, and environmental effects are not modeled. The simulation operates effectively in vacuum.
- **No grain boundaries / defects:** The model is a single crystal with periodic boundary conditions; no grain boundaries, twins, voids, or inclusions are present.
- **High strain rate:** The strain rate used (1.0 × 10⁹/s) is many orders of magnitude higher than typical experimental fatigue tests (typically 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁵/s). This may affect the quantitative comparison with experiment.
- **Small model size:** The model size (14.08 × 14.08 × 14.08 nm³) is very small; it represents a representative volume element but cannot capture macroscopic crack initiation, propagation, or full fatigue crack growth.
- **No quantitative oxidation data:** The paper does not provide any oxidation weight gain, oxide layer composition, or oxidation resistance data.
- **No experimental alloy composition:** The paper does not provide any actual alloy composition (at.% or wt.% values) or processing route data.
- **Scope limitation:** This paper is not within the domain of refractory high-temperature alloys, Nb-W-based alloys, Mo-W-based alloys, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. The findings are specific to Ni-based single crystal superalloy thermomechanical fatigue behavior at the atomistic simulation scale.
- **Unverified extrapolation to real alloys:** The authors state that the model "can well reflect the TMF behaviors of Ni-based single crystal superalloys and prove the correctness of MD simulations" based on qualitative consistency with experimental mean stress trends; however, quantitative life prediction is not validated against experimental data.
- **Speculation vs. confirmed conclusion:** The claim that OP-TMF produces shorter fatigue life is supported by the simulation's Ostergren parameter and plastic strain energy density calculations, but direct cycle-to-failure simulations are not performed (Nf = 30 cycles only).
