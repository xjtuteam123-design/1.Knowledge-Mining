# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Cracking behavior and microstructural, mechanical and thermal characteristics of tungsten – rhenium binary alloys fabricated by laser powder bed fusion
- **Authors**: Takafumi Yamamoto, Masanori Hara, Yuji Hatano
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2021
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2021.105651
- **Alloy system studied**: W–Re binary alloys
- **Research objective**: To investigate the effect of Re alloying on crack suppression, microstructure, hardness, and thermal diffusivity of W–Re binary alloys fabricated by laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF)
- **Material category**: Additively manufactured refractory alloy (W-based alloy, laser powder bed fusion)

# Alloy System and Research Object

This study investigates W–Re binary alloys fabricated by laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF). Four bulk specimens were prepared: pure W, W–1%Re, W–3%Re, and W–10%Re (mass percent of Re). The research focuses on cracking behavior, microstructural evolution, mechanical properties (hardness), and thermal characteristics (thermal diffusivity) as functions of Re content. The alloys are intended for potential use as plasma-facing materials (PFMs) in fusion reactors, where W is a promising candidate due to its high melting point (3420 °C), high thermal conductivity (174 W/mK at 27 °C), good high-temperature strength, and neutron irradiation resistance.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Manufacturing method**: Laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF), also known as selective laser melting (SLM)

**Equipment**: EOSINT M280 (EOS, Krailling, Germany) equipped with a 400 W-class fibre laser (maximum input power: 370 W, wavelength: 1070 nm, spot diameter: ~0.1 mm)

**Powder preparation**: 
- Mixed powder of pure W particles and pure Re particles
- Powders blended using a rotary mixer to uniformly disperse Re particles
- Re particles adhered to W particles and were distorted during mixing

**L-PBF process parameters** (optimized previously for pure W in reference [21]):
- Laser power: 370 W
- Scan speed: 500 mm/s
- Scan distance (hatch spacing): 0.09 mm
- Layer thickness: 0.02 mm
- Atmosphere: Ar
- Oxygen concentration in process chamber: maintained below 0.1%
- Base plate: carbon steel, not preheated

**Post-processing**: 
- Specimens were NOT post-annealed
- No heat treatment was applied after L-PBF

**Specimen preparation for characterization**:
- Cut by electric discharge machining (EDM)
- EBSD specimens: mechanically polished then electropolished (1.5% NaOH, 10 V)
- Crack observation specimens: finished using Ar ion beam milling (IM4000PLUS; 6 kV, 3.5 h, cryo-function at 20 °C)
- Hardness specimens: cubes 5 mm × 10 mm × 5 mm, indentation surface mechanically polished
- Thermal diffusivity specimens: discs 5 mm diameter × 1 mm thick, mechanically polished

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Matrix phase**: Single-phase W-BCC solid solution
- XRD of as-fabricated W–Re alloys showed only single-phase W peaks; all Re diffraction peaks disappeared, indicating formation of W–Re solid solution
- No intermetallic compounds detected (Re content within solubility limit of ~26 mass%)

**Lattice constant**:
- Lattice constant decreased with increasing Re content (consistent with literature data from Fujitsuka et al. [36])
- L-PBF specimens showed slightly larger lattice constants than conventionally processed (stress-relieved) specimens

**Grain structure**:
- Columnar grains elongated along the building direction (BD) in all specimens
- Grain refinement occurred with increasing Re content:
  - Pure W: 57 ± 40 μm (horizontal), 92 ± 60 μm (vertical)
  - W–1%Re: 45 ± 26 μm (horizontal), 111 ± 70 μm (vertical)
  - W–3%Re: 48 ± 31 μm (horizontal), 75 ± 48 μm (vertical)
  - W–10%Re: 36 ± 19 μm (horizontal), 48 ± 31 μm (vertical)
- Grain size reduction rate: 16% (horizontal) and 18% (vertical) at 3% Re; 38% (horizontal) and 47% (vertical) at 10% Re
- Grain refinement effect of Re was weaker in L-PBF material than in conventionally rolled material
- No strong crystallographic texture observed; Re addition had almost no effect on grain orientation distribution

**Elemental segregation**:
- Local Re enrichment and depletion observed by EPMA
- Re contents in "Area 1" (normal solid solution regions) matched bulk compositions
- Re enrichment regions ("Area 2"): 4.3 mass% in W–1%Re, 15.1 mass% in W–3%Re, 14.2 mass% in W–10%Re
- Re enrichment remained within solubility limit (26 mass%)
- No clear correlation between Re content distribution and crack distribution

**Grain boundary character**:
- Pure W: 51.6% low-angle grain boundaries (LAGB, φ ≤ 15°), 48.4% high-angle grain boundaries (HAGB, φ > 15°)
- W–10%Re: dominated by HAGBs with φ ~20–60°
- Higher proportion of HAGB in W–10%Re may increase oxygen segregation sites

**Nanopores**:
- Nanopores observed inside grains and at grain boundaries in all materials
- Nanopore area ratios: 0.17% (pure W), 0.12% (W–1%Re), 0.24% (W–3%Re), 0.33% (W–10%Re)
- Nanopore density increased with Re content, but density of agglomerated nanopores at grain boundaries did not increase
- Nanopores attributed to W oxidation/gasification during L-PBF (WO3 boiling point: 1837 °C; WO2: 1730 °C) and Ar gas entrainment

**Cracking behavior**:
- Cracks propagated preferentially along BD at high density in all specimens
- Crack area ratio: 2.45% (pure W), 1.86% (W–1%Re), 1.52% (W–3%Re), 1.01% (W–10%Re)
- 10 mass% Re addition reduced crack area by 59% compared to pure W
- Crack width and length gradually decreased with increasing Re content
- Some cracks observed in both Re-enriched and depleted areas; no clear correlation with Re distribution

# Mechanical Properties

**Hardness testing conditions**:
- Method: High-temperature micro-Vickers hardness (tester: HTM-1200II)
- Indenter: Sapphire
- Load: 0.981 N (HV0.1)
- Holding time: 30 s
- Temperature range: Room temperature (RT) to 1200 °C
- Atmosphere: Vacuum
- Five measurements per temperature; average and standard deviation reported

**Room-temperature hardness**:
- Pure W: 432 ± 7 HV0.1/30
- W–1%Re: slightly lower than pure W (solid solution softening)
- W–3%Re: lower than pure W (solid solution softening)
- W–10%Re: comparable to pure W
- Pure W L-PBF hardness (432 ± 7 HV0.1/30) comparable to conventionally rolled W (410–475 HV at RT)

**High-temperature hardness**:

| Temperature | Pure W | W–1%Re | W–3%Re | W–10%Re |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 400 °C | 179 ± 4 HV0.1/30 | Not explicitly stated (slightly lower than pure W) | Higher than pure W | 281 ± 5 HV0.1/30 |

Additional details:
- From RT to 400 °C: hardness decreased by 59% (pure W), 60% (W–1%Re), 45% (W–3%Re), and 35% (W–10%Re)
- Above 400 °C: hardness decreased more slowly for all specimens
- At ≥600 °C: hardness increased with increasing Re content (solid solution hardening)
- W–3%Re showed solid solution hardening at ≥200 °C (ΔHV = +20–50 at ≥300 °C)
- W–10%Re showed remarkable solid solution hardening at ≥200 °C (ΔHV = +90–120)
- Cracks around indentations were sometimes observed in all specimens, attributed to weak grain boundaries

**Comparison with conventionally processed W–Re alloys** (from reference [39]):
- Forged W–Re alloys at RT: 357 HV1 (0% Re), 348 HV1 (1.0% Re), 331 HV1 (2.8% Re), 348 HV1 (9.1% Re)
- Forged W–Re alloys at 457 °C: 93 HV1 (0% Re), 115 HV1 (1.0% Re), 145 HV1 (2.8% Re), 230 HV1 (9.1% Re)
- The dependence of hardness on Re content and temperature in L-PBF material was similar to that of forged material

**Other mechanical properties** (tensile strength, elongation, fracture toughness, creep): 
Not measured or reported in this study.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

**Oxidation resistance**: Not directly measured in this study. However, the following relevant information is discussed:

- Oxygen concentration in the process chamber was maintained below 0.1% to minimize oxidation during L-PBF
- Oxygen content in the Re powder (2800 ppm) was significantly higher than in W powder (120 ppm)
- Oxygen, nitrogen, and carbon impurities should be kept at low levels in the final product because their presence in W increases the ductile-to-brittle transition temperature (DBTT) [43]
- W oxidation and subsequent gasification during L-PBF is one origin of nanopore formation: WO3 (boiling point 1837 °C) and WO2 (boiling point 1730 °C) remain gaseous during W solidification
- Re oxide (Re2O7, boiling point 362 °C) gasification during L-PBF may have contributed to increased nanopore density; Re2O7 boiling point is markedly lower than W–Re melting temperature (~3420 °C)

**Possible issues**:
- Oxygen segregation at high-angle grain boundaries mentioned as a concern (oxygen impurities segregate at HAGB [32])
- The higher proportion of HAGB in W–10%Re may lead to increased oxygen segregation sites
- Reducing the concentration of interstitial impurities (O, N, C) in raw powder is identified as one of the most promising methods to improve the strength of HAGBs in W–10%Re bulk specimens

**High-temperature stability**:
- Post-fabrication annealing at high temperature (above 1900 °C) was mentioned (based on previous work [21]) as effective for reducing nanopore number density via grain growth and gas release
- No high-temperature long-term service stability data (creep, oxidation, embrittlement) were measured in this study

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Re (Rhenium) in W-based alloys

**[Element or Element Combination]**  
Re (Rhenium)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**  
W–Re binary alloys fabricated by laser powder bed fusion; also applicable to conventionally processed W–Re alloys

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**  
0, 1, 3, and 10 mass% Re. Solubility limit of Re in W: ~26 mass%.

**[Main Function]**  
1. Grain refinement during L-PBF  
2. Solid solution softening at low temperatures (RT) for certain compositions  
3. Solid solution hardening at elevated temperatures (≥200 °C)  
4. Crack suppression (reduction of crack area ratio)  
5. Reduction of thermal anisotropy

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**  
- Suppression of grain boundary migration during grain growth by substitutional Re solute atoms in the W matrix, leading to grain refinement  
- Re atoms in W lattice cause lattice contraction (decreased lattice constant)  
- Interaction of Re solutes with double-kink nucleation process (for solid solution softening)  
- Re atoms scatter electrons and phonons, reducing thermal conductivity/diffusivity  
- Grain refinement increases grain boundary area, disperses thermal stress, and reduces crack propagation driving force  
- Increased time for wetting of solidified layer surface and penetration of molten metal into open cracks due to reduced thermal diffusivity  
- Reduced temperature gradient across molten pool boundary due to reduced cooling rate

**[Property Effect]**  
- **Grain size**: Reduced from 57 ± 40 μm (pure W, horizontal) to 36 ± 19 μm (W–10%Re, horizontal); vertical: 92 ± 60 μm (pure W) to 48 ± 31 μm (W–10%Re)  
- **Crack area ratio**: Reduced from 2.45% (pure W) to 1.01% (W–10%Re); 10% Re addition reduced crack area by 59%  
- **Hardness at 400 °C**: 179 ± 4 HV0.1/30 (pure W) vs. 281 ± 5 HV0.1/30 (W–10%Re)  
- **Solid solution hardening at ≥200 °C**: ΔHV = +90–120 for W–10%Re  
- **Solid solution softening at RT**: W–3%Re showed softening at RT  
- **Thermal diffusivity at RT**: Reduced by 68% in z direction and 26% in xy direction for W–10%Re compared to pure W  
- **Thermal anisotropy (Dz/Dxy)**: Decreased from 3–4 (pure W) to ~1.8 (W–10%Re)  
- **Nanopore area ratio**: Increased from 0.17% (pure W) to 0.33% (W–10%Re)  
- **Grain boundary character**: W–10%Re dominated by HAGB (φ~20–60°); pure W had 51.6% LAGB and 48.4% HAGB

**[Potential Side Effects]**  
- Increased nanopore area ratio (from 0.17% to 0.33% at 10% Re), possibly due to Re oxide (Re2O7, boiling point 362 °C) gasification  
- Increased O, N, C impurity content due to high oxygen in Re powder (2800 ppm O in Re powder)  
- Higher proportion of high-angle grain boundaries may increase oxygen segregation sites, which is harmful for crack reduction  
- Reduced thermal diffusivity (68% reduction at RT in z direction for 10% Re)  
- Cracks not completely suppressed even at 10 mass% Re  
- Local Re enrichment and depletion due to insufficient diffusion during rapid solidification (cooling rate ~5 × 10^6 K/s)

**[Original Evidence]**  
- Section 3.1 (Microstructure): "The grains were gradually refined as the Re content increased... The average grain diameters of pure W were 57 ± 40 μm and 92 ± 60 μm in the horizontal and vertical cross-sections, respectively... 36 ± 19 μm and 48 ± 31 μm in W–10%Re."  
- Section 3.2 (Cracking behavior): "It was revealed that the 10% Re addition reduced the crack area ratio by 59% in comparison with pure W."  
- Section 3.3 (Hardness): "The micro-Vickers hardnesses of pure W and W–10%Re at 400 °C were 179 ± 4 HV0.1/30 and 281 ± 5 HV0.1/30, respectively."  
- Section 4 (Discussion): "Suppression of grain boundary migration during grain growth by addition of substituted Re solute atoms into the W matrix has also been reported."  
- Section 4 (Discussion): "The decrease in thermal diffusivity induced by the Re addition leads to an increase in the time for solidification of the molten metal and subsequent cooling, which are expected to have two positive effects on the reduction in crack density."  
- Figure 4: Lattice constant decreased with increasing Re content.  
- Figure 15: Grain boundary misorientation angle distribution showing W–10%Re dominated by HAGB.

## O (Oxygen) in L-PBF W-based alloys

**[Element or Element Combination]**  
O (Oxygen), as impurity

**[Applicable Alloy System]**  
L-PBF W and W–Re alloys

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**  
- W powder: 120 mass ppm  
- Re powder: 2800 mass ppm  
- Bulk specimens: 180 ppm (pure W), 148 ppm (W–1%Re), 192 ppm (W–3%Re), 247 ppm (W–10%Re)

**[Main Function]**  
Detrimental impurity; contributes to nanopore formation and grain boundary weakening

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**  
- Forms volatile tungsten oxides (WO3, boiling point 1837 °C; WO2, boiling point 1730 °C) during L-PBF that remain gaseous during solidification and create nanopores  
- Forms Re2O7 (boiling point 362 °C), which vaporizes and may contribute to nanopore formation  
- Oxygen impurities segregate at high-angle grain boundaries, weakening grain boundary bonding  
- Presence of interstitial O in W increases DBTT

**[Property Effect]**  
- Nanopores at grain boundaries reduce grain boundary strength  
- Aggregation of nanopores forms small cracks  
- Increased oxygen content in W–10%Re (247 ppm) compared to pure W (180 ppm) is attributed to oxygen in Re powder  
- Higher proportion of HAGB in W–10%Re increases oxygen segregation sites

**[Potential Side Effects]**  
- Increased nanopore formation  
- Reduced grain boundary strength  
- Increased DBTT  
- Facilitated crack initiation at grain boundaries  
- Weakened high-temperature mechanical properties

**[Original Evidence]**  
- Section 1 (Introduction): "The boiling point of WOx (WO3: 1837 °C, and WO2: 1730 °C) is lower than the melting point of W, WOx remains in a gaseous state during W solidification after laser irradiation and form nanopores."  
- Section 2.1: "The oxygen content in the Re powder was significantly higher than that in W powder."  
- Section 3.1: "Oxygen, nitrogen and carbon impurities should be kept at low levels in the final product, because their presence in W increases the DBTT [43]."  
- Section 4 (Discussion): "The gasification of Re oxides formed by the oxidation of pure Re during L-PBF contributed to the increase in the number density of nanopores."  
- Section 4 (Discussion): "Oxygen impurities segregate at HAGB... Reducing the concentration of interstitial impurities in the raw powder is one of the most promising methods to improve the strength of HAGBs in the W–10%Re bulk specimens."

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening / Softening

**Related elements**: Re in W matrix

**Typical alloys**: W–Re binary alloys (W–1%Re, W–3%Re, W–10%Re)

**Key compositions**: 1–10 mass% Re

**Microstructural changes**:
- Re atoms substitute W atoms in BCC lattice
- Lattice constant decreases with increasing Re content (Figure 4)
- Local Re enrichment/depletion observed due to insufficient diffusion during rapid solidification

**Property improvements**:
- Solid solution hardening at elevated temperatures (≥200 °C): ΔHV = +90–120 for W–10%Re
- High-temperature hardness: 281 ± 5 HV0.1/30 at 400 °C for W–10%Re vs. 179 ± 4 HV0.1/30 for pure W
- Hardness at ≥600 °C increased with increasing Re content

**Risks and limitations**:
- Solid solution softening at RT for W–3%Re (and slightly for W–1%Re)
- Softening at low Re content (<3.3 mass%) below ~162 °C
- Hardening at high Re content (>7.7 mass%) reported in literature

**[Original Evidence]**  
- Section 3.3: "The hardness of W–1%Re was slightly lower than that of pure W in the temperature range from RT to 400 °C, indicating that solid solution softening had occurred. Its hardness at temperatures ≥600 °C was comparable to that of pure W. Significant solid solution softening was observed for W–3%Re at RT, but it showed hardening at temperatures ≥200 °C (ΔHV = +20–50 at ≥300 °C). In the case of W–10%Re... remarkable solid solution hardening was observed at ≥200 °C (ΔHV = +90–120)."

## Grain Refinement / Grain Boundary Strengthening

**Related elements**: Re promoting grain refinement during L-PBF

**Typical alloys**: W–Re binary alloys fabricated by L-PBF

**Key compositions**: 1–10 mass% Re (most effective at 10 mass% Re)

**Microstructural changes**:
- Columnar grains elongated along BD were gradually refined with increasing Re content
- Average grain diameter reduced from 57 ± 40 μm (pure W) to 36 ± 19 μm (W–10%Re) in horizontal cross-section
- From 92 ± 60 μm to 48 ± 31 μm in vertical cross-section
- Mechanism: Suppression of grain boundary migration by Re solute atoms
- Grain boundary character changed: W–10%Re dominated by HAGB

**Property improvements**:
- Increased grain boundary area disperses thermal stress
- Reduced stress driving crack propagation
- Reduced crack area ratio (from 2.45% to 1.01%, a 59% reduction)
- Shorter and narrower crack morphology
- Reduced density of agglomerated nanopores at grain boundaries

**Risks and limitations**:
- Higher proportion of HAGB increases potential oxygen segregation sites
- Grain refinement effect weaker in L-PBF than in conventional processing
- Cracks were not completely suppressed even at 10% Re
- Increased nanopore area ratio (0.17% → 0.33%) due to Re oxide gasification

**[Original Evidence]**  
- Section 3.1: "The grains were gradually refined as the Re content increased... the average grain diameters gradually decreased as the Re content increased."  
- Section 3.2: "It appears that an increase in grain boundary density induced by the grain refinement prevented agglomeration of nanopores at the grain boundaries and subsequent growth of agglomerated nanopores to larger cracks."  
- Section 4 (Discussion): "The grain refinement achieved by Re addition should increase the area of grain boundaries and consequently reduce the stress driving crack propagation by dispersing the force induced by the heat. This is considered to be the cause of the reduced crack area ratio observed for W–10%Re."

## Crack Suppression via Thermal Property Modification

**Related elements**: Re in W matrix

**Typical alloys**: W–Re binary alloys (L-PBF)

**Key compositions**: 10 mass% Re most effective

**Microstructural changes**:
- Reduced thermal diffusivity due to increased electron and phonon scattering by Re atoms
- RT thermal diffusivity reduction by 10% Re: 68% in z direction, 26% in xy direction
- Longer solidification and cooling time
- Reduced temperature gradient across molten pool boundary

**Property improvements**:
- Increased time for wetting of solidified layer surface, assisting penetration of molten metal into open cracks
- Reduced thermal stress from local heating during L-PBF
- Reduced crack area ratio from 2.45% to 1.01%
- Mitigation of thermal anisotropy (Dz/Dxy: 3–4 for pure W → ~1.8 for W–10%Re)
- Promotes gas release from solidified layer due to longer exposure to high temperatures

**Risks and limitations**:
- Reduced thermal conductivity may be detrimental for fusion applications requiring high heat removal
- Gas release incomplete (nanopores still observed in W–10%Re)
- W–10%Re shows unusual temperature dependence of thermal diffusivity (increases with temperature)

**[Original Evidence]**  
- Section 3.4: "The value of Dz decreased as the Re content increased at all temperatures examined."  
- Section 4 (Discussion): "First, increased time to wet the surface of solidified layer may assist the penetration of molten metal into open cracks. The other is the reduction in the temperature gradient across the boundary between the molten pool and the surrounding material caused by the reduced cooling rate of the solidified layer."

# Key Conclusions

1. High-density bulk W–Re binary alloy specimens (relative density >98.0%) were successfully fabricated by L-PBF using mixed W and Re powders under the same laser irradiation conditions optimized for pure W.

2. The Re powder dissolved completely into the W matrix during L-PBF; XRD confirmed single-phase W-BCC solid solution without intermetallic compounds.

3. Columnar grains elongated along the building direction were gradually refined with increasing Re content. The most remarkable grain refinement was observed in W–10%Re (36 ± 19 μm horizontal vs. 57 ± 40 μm for pure W).

4. Hardness at high temperatures (≥600 °C) increased with increasing Re content due to solid solution hardening. The micro-Vickers hardness at 400 °C was 179 ± 4 HV0.1/30 for pure W and 281 ± 5 HV0.1/30 for W–10%Re. Solid solution softening was observed at RT for W–3%Re.

5. The crack area ratio decreased progressively with increasing Re content: 2.45% (pure W), 1.86% (W–1%Re), 1.52% (W–3%Re), and 1.01% (W–10%Re). The 10 mass% Re addition reduced crack area by 59% compared to pure W.

6. The nanopore area ratio increased with increasing Re content (0.17% for pure W to 0.33% for W–10%Re), attributed to Re oxide (Re2O7) gasification. However, the density of agglomerated nanopores at grain boundaries did not increase due to grain refinement.

7. Thermal diffusivity decreased with increasing Re content. In the z direction (parallel to BD), the RT reduction was 68% for W–10%Re compared to pure W.

8. Thermal anisotropy (Dz/Dxy) decreased from 3–4 for pure W to ~1.8 for W–10%Re, indicating that at high Re content, thermal diffusivity is less affected by cracks than by the isotropic scattering effect of Re atoms on heat carrier transfer.

9. The unusual temperature dependence of thermal diffusivity for W–10%Re (increasing with temperature rather than decreasing) is consistent with theoretical predictions (Wang and Zhao [51]) and is related to the temperature dependence of electron carrier heat transfer contribution.

10. Cracks could not be completely suppressed even at 10 mass% Re. Local Re enrichment and depletion were observed due to insufficient diffusion during rapid melting/solidification (cooling rate ~5 × 10^6 K/s). Future work is needed using W–Re solid solution powders and reducing interstitial impurity contents.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations of this study:**

1. **No tensile or compressive testing**: Mechanical properties were evaluated only by Vickers hardness testing. Tensile strength, compressive strength, elongation, and fracture toughness were not measured.

2. **No post-annealing**: All specimens were characterized in the as-fabricated condition without post-annealing. The authors suggest that high-temperature post-annealing could further improve grain boundary structure.

3. **Mixed powder approach**: W–Re alloys were fabricated from mixed W and Re powders rather than pre-alloyed W–Re solid solution powders. This resulted in local Re enrichment and depletion.

4. **High oxygen in Re powder**: The Re powder had 2800 ppm oxygen, which contributed to increased O content in the W–10%Re specimen (247 ppm) and potentially to increased nanopore formation.

5. **Limited Re content range**: Only up to 10 mass% Re was investigated; the solubility limit is ~26 mass%.

6. **No oxidation resistance testing**: Oxidation behavior at elevated temperatures was not systematically studied.

7. **No creep or long-term stability data**: The paper does not report creep performance, rupture life, or long-term service stability.

8. **No crack-free specimens**: Even at 10% Re, cracks were not completely suppressed.

9. **No specimen preheating**: The base plate was not preheated during L-PBF, which may have contributed to cracking.

10. **Grain boundary strength issues**: Cracks around hardness indentations were observed in all specimens, attributed to weak grain boundaries.

**Applicable scope:**
- The findings are applicable to W–Re binary alloys fabricated by L-PBF (SLM) under the specific processing conditions described (370 W laser power, 500 mm/s scan speed, 0.09 mm hatch spacing, 0.02 mm layer thickness, Ar atmosphere with <0.1% O2).
- The results may be relevant for fusion reactor plasma-facing material applications where crack-free, high-density W components are required.
- The observed trends in solid solution softening/hardening with Re content and temperature are consistent with conventionally processed (forged, rolled) W–Re alloys, suggesting broader applicability of the mechanisms.
- The study demonstrates that Re alloying can partially suppress cracking in L-PBF W, but complete suppression requires additional measures such as preheating, high-purity powders, or post-annealing.
