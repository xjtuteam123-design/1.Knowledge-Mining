# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** A lightweight NbMoZrTi refractory high entropy alloy with high specific strength
- **Authors:** Wenjie Chen, Xinmei Li
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2024 (Available online 2 October 2023)
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matlet.2023.135330
- **Alloy system studied:** NbMoZrTi refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA), also referred to as lightweight RHEA (LRHEA)
- **Research objective:** To develop a new low-density Nb-Mo-Zr-Ti refractory high-entropy alloy with improved room-temperature ductility and high specific yield strength for potential aerospace and engineering applications. The goal is to address the limitations of conventional RHEAs (high density >10 g/cm³ and poor room-temperature ductility) that hinder their use.
- **Material category:** Quaternary equiatomic refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA), lightweight RHEA, single BCC-phase refractory alloy prepared by vacuum arc melting.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Material category:** Refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA); specifically, a lightweight refractory high-entropy alloy (LRHEA) with a single BCC solid-solution phase.
- **Alloy system:** Nb-Mo-Zr-Ti quaternary equiatomic system.
- **Research object:** The as-cast microstructure, phase constitution, density, hardness, room-temperature compressive properties, and specific yield strength of the NbMoZrTi LRHEA. The paper also seeks to validate thermodynamic predictions using empirical parameters (δ, VEC, Ω) and computational phase diagrams (Thermo-Calc with TCHEA3 database).

# Alloy Composition

- **Alloy name:** NbMoZrTi LRHEA
- **Nominal composition:** Quaternary equiatomic Nb-Mo-Zr-Ti, i.e., 25 at.% Nb – 25 at.% Mo – 25 at.% Zr – 25 at.% Ti (equiatomic).
- **Alloying elements:** Nb, Mo, Zr, Ti.
- **Elemental purity:** All raw metals > 99.9 wt.%.
- **Weight percentages:** Not explicitly calculated in the original paper. The paper only provides the equiatomic atomic composition.
- **ppm-level impurities:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Contains:** Nb, Mo, Zr, Ti. Does not contain W, Ta, Hf, V, Cr, Al, Si, Re, C, B, Y, La, O as intentional alloying additions per the paper. However, the paper mentions "NbMoZrTiAl LRHEA" once in the text (Fig. 2 caption section) which appears to be a typographical error, as the composition throughout the paper is consistently NbMoZrTi. The abstract and conclusions state the alloy is NbMoZrTi without Al.
- **Other elements:** Not applicable.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Preparation method:** Vacuum arc melting using a WK-II type non-self-consuming vacuum arc furnace.
- **Raw materials:** Pure metals of Nb, Mo, Zr, and Ti (> 99.9 wt.%).
- **Ingot weight:** 60 g.
- **Remelting:** At least 8 times to achieve chemical homogeneity.
- **Liquid state holding:** Kept in liquid state for 10 minutes during each melting.
- **Turning:** Ingots were turned before each remelting.
- **Homogenization heat treatment:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Rolling/forging:** None.
- **Annealing/aging:** None.
- **Cooling method:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper. The as-cast microstructure is characterized, and it is mentioned that the cooling during the melting process influences grain size distribution; however, the specific cooling rate is not stated.
- **Deformation amount:** No mechanical deformation reported; tested in as-cast condition.
- **Other processing details:** The repeated remelting and reheating/cooling cycles during melting resulted in alternating large and fine equiaxed grains.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase:** Single-phase body-centered cubic (BCC). Confirmed by XRD and consistent with Thermo-Calc phase diagram predictions.
- **Second phases/precipitates:** None reported. The alloy maintains a single BCC phase from solidification down to 646.85 °C according to the Scheil-Gulliver non-equilibrium solidification calculation.
- **Grain size:** Average grain size = 34.1 µm, measured by EBSD. The IPF shows alternating large and fine equiaxed grains.
- **Microstructure:** Typical as-cast dendritic organization. The dendritic region (DR) is enriched in Nb and Mo (high-melting-point elements); the inter-dendritic region (IR) is enriched in Zr (low-melting-point element); Ti is almost uniformly distributed in both DR and IR.
  - Grey areas = dendritic region (DR)
  - Black areas = inter-dendritic region (IR)
- **Grain boundary features:** Thick layers of dark-grey Zr-rich phase separate the grains of light-grey Nb- and Mo-rich phases. Some grain boundaries are not completely wetted by the melt (non-zero contact angle), which is attributed to the so-called incomplete grain boundary wetting phenomenon.
- **Elemental segregation:** Yes. Nb and Mo segregate to dendritic regions; Zr segregates to inter-dendritic regions; Ti is nearly homogeneously distributed. The segregation is attributed to differences in elemental melting points.
- **Solidification range:** From T1 = 2201.85 °C to T2 = 646.85 °C (non-equilibrium Scheil-Gulliver model). Equilibrium solidification starts below 2205.3 °C.
- **Thermodynamic parameters:** δ < 6.6%, Ω > 1.0, VEC < 6.87, which are the necessary conditions for a single BCC solid solution.
- **Dislocation-related features:** KAM (Kernel Average Misorientation) maps show local orientation deviations mainly within and near grain boundaries, indicating the presence of geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs). The KAM color code reflects Schmidt coefficient magnitude: yellow indicates lower Schmidt coefficients, red indicates higher Schmidt coefficients.
- **Strengthening mechanism discussed:** Solid-solution strengthening of the BCC phase due to severe lattice distortion from the large atomic size difference between Zr and the other elements (Nb, Mo, Ti). The paper does not discuss precipitation strengthening, ODS strengthening, or grain-boundary strengthening explicitly.

# Mechanical Properties

- **Room-temperature compressive yield strength:** 1462 MPa
- **Peak compressive strength:** 2158 MPa
- **Compressive strain at break / compressive plasticity:** 24.6 %
- **Hardness:** 522 HV (average of 15 measurements after excluding maximum and minimum; indenter load 100 g, holding time 15 s)
- **Density:** 7.4331 g/cm³ (Archimedes method, average of 5 measurements)
- **Specific yield strength:** 196.69 MPa·g⁻¹·cm³ (calculated as yield strength / density)
- **Loading mode:** Compression
- **Testing temperature:** Room temperature
- **Strain rate:** 1 × 10⁻³ /s
- **Sample size:** Φ 4 × 6 mm
- **Atmosphere:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Elastic modulus:** Not reported.
- **Fracture toughness:** Not reported.
- **Creep performance / rupture life:** Not reported.
- **High-temperature strength (800–1600 °C):** Not reported. The paper states that future work will study high-temperature microstructure and mechanical properties.
- **Fracture morphology:** Flat fracture surfaces with river patterns; typical cleavage fracture morphology indicating brittle fracture.
- **Comparison with other RHEAs:** The alloy’s specific yield strength (196.69 MPa·g⁻¹·cm³) is stated to be the highest among all reported RHEAs considered in the comparison when ductility is also accounted for. Considering only yield strength and density alone, the alloy is at an average level compared to reported RHEAs; the combined specific strength + plastic strain is superior to most similar alloys [references 5, 6, 12–19].

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Zr]
- **[Applicable Alloy System]:** NbMoZrTi lightweight refractory high-entropy alloy (this paper).
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]:** Equiatomic 25 at.% Zr in the quaternary NbMoZrTi alloy.
- **[Main Function]:** Solid-solution strengthening via severe lattice distortion due to large atomic size mismatch; contributes to lowering alloy density.
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]:** Zr has a large atomic radius compared to Nb, Mo, and Ti. Its incorporation into the BCC solid solution causes severe lattice distortion, leading to significant solid-solution strengthening. Zr segregates to inter-dendritic regions during solidification due to its lower melting point. Zr-rich thick layers form at grain boundaries; some boundaries show incomplete wetting by the melt (non-zero contact angle). The repeated melting leads to incomplete grain boundary wetting by Zr-rich phase.
- **[Property Effect]:** Reported properties for the equiatomic alloy: density 7.4331 g/cm³, hardness 522 HV, room-temperature compressive yield strength 1462 MPa, peak strength 2158 MPa, strain at break 24.6 %. The specific yield strength is 196.69 MPa·g⁻¹·cm³, which is the highest among all reported RHEAs in the comparison when ductility is considered. However, the paper does not isolate the individual contribution of Zr. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Zr alone.
- **[Potential Side Effects]:** Zr is a relatively low-melting-point element among refractory elements and segregates to inter-dendritic regions, leading to elemental inhomogeneity. The fracture mode at room temperature is brittle (cleavage). Zr is also relatively heavy compared to Ti, so its presence at equiatomic level still results in a density above 7 g/cm³ (though lower than conventional RHEAs > 10 g/cm³).
- **[Original Evidence]:** "The as-cast microstructure of NbMoZrTi LRHEA can be seen in Fig. 2 (a), which shows that the microstructure of the alloy is a typical dendritic organization, with grey and black areas corresponding to the dendritic region (DR) and inter-dendritic region (IR), respectively." "The high melting point Nb and Mo elements are enriched in DR, the low melting point Zr is enriched in IR." "Thick layers of dark-grey Zr-rich separates the grains of light-grey Nb- and Mo-rich phases from each other, and some GBs are not completely wetted by the melt (non-zero contact angle)." "The compressive strength of the alloy is about 1462 MPa, the peak strength is 2158 MPa, the strain at break is 24.6 %, and the average hardness is 522 HV." "This may be attributed to the large difference in the atomic size between the Zr element and the Nb, Mo, and Ti elements, and thus the formation of the BCC solid solution phase causes severe lattice distortion, which results in a significant solid solution strengthening effect." (Section 3, Figs. 2 and 3)

- **[Main Function]:** Form a low-density, single-BCC-phase refractory high-entropy alloy with high specific yield strength and improved room-temperature compressive ductility compared to first-generation RHEAs (NbMoTaW, NbMoTaWV).

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening
- **[Related Elements]:** Zr (primarily), Nb, Mo, Ti.
- **[Typical Alloys]:** NbMoZrTi lightweight RHEA (this paper); also applies to other BCC RHEAs discussed in the introduction (NbMoTaW, NbMoTaWV).
- **[Key Compositions]:** Equiatomic NbMoZrTi (25 at.% each).
- **[Microstructural Changes]:** Single BCC disordered solid solution with severe lattice distortion due to large atomic size differences (especially between Zr and Nb, Mo, Ti). KAM maps show local orientation deviations and geometrically necessary dislocations concentrated within and near grain boundaries. Dendritic segregation creates composition gradients: Nb/Mo-rich dendritic regions and Zr-rich inter-dendritic regions.
- **[Property Improvements]:** Room-temperature compressive yield strength = 1462 MPa; peak strength = 2158 MPa; hardness = 522 HV; strain at break = 24.6 %; specific yield strength = 196.69 MPa·g⁻¹·cm³.
- **[Risks and Limitations]:** Solid-solution strengthening via large atomic size mismatch (Zr) also causes atomic-level distortion that may contribute to brittleness; fracture mode remains brittle cleavage. Elemental segregation between dendritic and inter-dendritic regions creates local composition variation that may affect mechanical uniformity. The paper does not quantify the individual contribution of solid-solution strengthening versus other possible mechanisms.
- **[Original Evidence]:** "NbMoZrTi LRHEA with a single BCC phase has an excellent specific yield strength, which may be attributed to the large difference in the atomic size between the Zr element and the Nb, Mo, and Ti elements, and thus the formation of the BCC solid solution phase causes severe lattice distortion, which results in a significant solid solution strengthening effect." "The enhancement of hardness and strength of the alloy comes from the solid solution strengthening effect of BCC." (Section 3)

# Key Conclusions

1. A new low-density equiatomic NbMoZrTi lightweight refractory high-entropy alloy (LRHEA) was successfully prepared by vacuum arc melting.
2. The as-cast alloy exhibits a single BCC phase, as confirmed by XRD and consistent with Thermo-Calc phase diagram predictions (equilibrium and Scheil-Gulliver non-equilibrium). The solidification range extends from approximately 2201.85 °C to 646.85 °C.
3. The alloy has a density of 7.4331 g/cm³ and a hardness of 522 HV.
4. Room-temperature compressive properties: yield strength = 1462 MPa; peak strength = 2158 MPa; strain at break = 24.6 %.
5. The specific yield strength of NbMoZrTi is 196.69 MPa·g⁻¹·cm³, which is stated to be the highest among all reported RHEAs considered in the comparison when considering both specific strength and plastic strain.
6. The microstructure shows typical dendritic organization with Nb and Mo enriched in dendritic regions, Zr enriched in inter-dendritic regions, and Ti uniformly distributed. Zr-rich layers at grain boundaries exhibit incomplete wetting.
7. Fracture morphology shows river patterns and flat surfaces, indicating brittle cleavage fracture at room temperature.
8. The combined mechanical properties (specific strength + ductility) of the NbMoZrTi LRHEA are superior to most similar RHEAs reported in the literature.
9. The authors state that future work will investigate high-temperature microstructure evolution and mechanical properties.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Scope:** The study is limited to a single equiatomic NbMoZrTi composition prepared by vacuum arc melting and tested in the as-cast condition at room temperature.
- **Unreported properties:** High-temperature strength, creep, oxidation resistance, elastic modulus, fracture toughness, tension behavior, and fatigue are not reported.
- **Processing:** Only vacuum arc melting was used; the applicability to other processing routes (e.g., powder metallurgy, additive manufacturing) is not addressed.
- **No compositional screening:** Only one equiatomic composition is studied. The paper does not investigate non-equiatomic variations or the effect of varying individual element concentrations.
- **Segregation:** Elemental segregation between dendritic and inter-dendritic regions is present; the impact of this inhomogeneity on high-temperature stability and fracture behavior is not fully explored.
- **Oxidation resistance:** Not studied. Although RHEAs are generally credited with good high-temperature oxidation resistance, no data are provided for this specific alloy.
- **Brittleness:** Despite improved compressive ductility (24.6 %), the fracture morphology remains brittle cleavage at room temperature; true tensile ductility and toughness are not evaluated.
- **High-temperature applicability:** The authors acknowledge that high-temperature microstructure and mechanical properties will be addressed in future work.
- **Typographical concern:** The paper includes "NbMoZrTiAl LRHEA" once in the results text (Fig. 2 caption context), which appears inconsistent with the rest of the paper; the actual alloy composition is consistently stated as NbMoZrTi without Al. This should be noted when using this source for retrieval.
