# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Alloy system studied:** Al-Nb-Ti-V-Zr multi-principal element alloy (MPEA), specifically Al₈Nb₂₇Ti₂₉V₂₈Zr₈
- **Material category:** Refractory multi-principal element alloy (MPEA); the paper notes that in the context of this work these alloys are simply named MPEA, while acknowledging related terms such as refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCA), and refractory superalloys (RSA)
- **Research objective:** To develop a MPEA for structural engine airplane components with a working temperature of about 700 °C, with superior specific yield strength relative to conventional superalloys. Elements Al, Nb, Ti, V, and Zr were chosen aiming to produce a low-density single-phase alloy, based on the extensive mutual solubility among Nb, Ti, V, and Zr as well as the proved high solubility of Al in both Al-Nb-Ti and Al-Ti-V systems.
- **Associated but not primary system:** AlNbTiVZr₀.₅ alloy (from references [9,15]) is used for comparison; its composition is given as Al₁₁Nb₃₄Ti₁₉V₁₉Zr₁₈ (at.%) in Table 1.

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Melting method:** Arc melting in a water-cooled copper crucible under high-purity argon atmosphere (min. 99.995%), with titanium as a getter
- **Ingot details:** Multiple ingots produced; each ingot inverted and remelted at least five times to ensure chemical homogeneity; resulting ingots weighed approximately 50 g with dimensions near 10 mm × 16 mm × 63 mm
- **Homogenization:** 1200 °C / 24 h, aiming to eliminate segregation from the as-solidified microstructure and to obtain a BCC single-phase microstructure
- **Subsequent heat treatment:** 700 °C / 168 h to evaluate phase stability
- **Heat treatment atmosphere:** Samples vacuum sealed in quartz tubes filled with titanium chips to prevent oxidation
- **Cooling method:** After each heat treatment, tubes with samples were removed from furnace and allowed to air cool to room temperature
- **Specimen preparation:** Cylindrical compression specimens (Φ 4–5 mm × 8–10 mm) obtained by electric discharge machining (EDM) from homogenized + heat treated ingots
- **Post-processing (rolling, forging, etc.):** Not applicable — no rolling, forging, or additional deformation processing reported

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
- **Matrix phase:** A2 disordered BCC (body-centered cubic) structure (confirmed by TEM in homogenized + heat treated condition)
- **Second phases in homogenized condition:** BCC matrix containing small fraction of micrometric Zr-rich particles (86.8% Zr by EDS); the light gray Zr-rich particles in Fig. 2 could not be characterized by TEM because such regions could not be located in samples
- **Second phases in homogenized + heat treated (700 °C/168 h) condition:**
  - Micrometric Zr-rich particles (77.5% Zr by EDS)
  - High volume fraction of nanometer-sized particles finely precipitated inside grains; EDS chemical analysis could not evaluate them due to small size
  - TEM identified nanometer-sized particles as **Al₃Zr₅** (hexagonal lattice) and **Laves AlZrV** (hexagonal lattice, C14-type per context)
- **XRD results:** Both homogenized and homogenized + heat treated alloys present reflections essentially from BCC lattice; non-identified peaks may be associated with ZrAlV and Zr₅Al₃ phases observed by TEM
- **Grain size:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper
- **Grain boundary features:** Not explicitly discussed in the original paper
- **Elemental segregation:** Zr-rich particles observed in both conditions; Zr content in micrometric particles: 86.8% Zr (homogenized) and 77.5% Zr (homogenized + heat treated)
- **Precipitate size and distribution:** Nanometer-sized Al₃Zr₅ and Laves AlZrV particles finely distributed inside grains (high volume fraction); micrometric Zr-rich particles distributed in matrix
- **Comparison from cited literature:** In homogenized NbTiVZr, almost entirely single BCC solid solution; in Al-containing alloys, nanometer-sized C14 Laves and hexagonal Zr₂Al-type phases found in BCC matrix, with intermetallic volume fraction increasing with Al content [8]; later TEM showed these are ZrAlV and Zr₅Al₃ phases with ordered B2 matrix phase [9]. AlNbTiV homogenized alloy had ordered B2 single phase; alloying with Zr changed the degree of order of B2 and precipitated Zr₅Al₃ and C14 Laves ZrAlV phases with increasing Zr content [9].

# Mechanical Properties
- **Density:** 6.34 g/cm³ (measured by helium pycnometer)
- **Room-temperature compressive properties (homogenized + heat treated alloy):**
  - Yield strength (σ₀.₂): 1250 MPa
  - Compressive ductility: 38% until fracture
  - Specific yield strength: 197.16 kPa·m³/kg
- **Elevated-temperature compressive properties (from Table 1):**
  - At 700 °C: σ₀.₂ = 720 MPa; specific σ₀.₂ = 113.6 kPa·m³/kg
  - At 800 °C: σ₀.₂ = 690 MPa; specific σ₀.₂ = 108.8 kPa·m³/kg
  - Note: Plasticity at elevated temperatures was not reported because specimens were only deformed up to 20% without cracking
- **Comparison alloys at elevated temperature (from Table 1):**

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Al]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** Al-Nb-Ti-V-Zr MPEA; AlNbTiVZrₓ refractory high-entropy alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 8 at.% in primary alloy Al₈Nb₂₇Ti₂₉V₂₈Zr₈; referenced AlNbTiVZr₀.₅ has 11 at.% Al; AlNbTiVZr equimolar composition has approximately 20 at.% Al
- **Main Function:** Promotes formation of second phases (Al₃Zr₅ and Laves AlZrV intermetallic nanoparticles); contributes to low density; influences the degree of order of the B2 phase
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Al additions have a very pronounced effect on MPEA structure, resulting in second-phase formation. In homogenized NbTiVZr (without Al), the alloy has almost entirely single BCC solid solution; Al-containing alloys form nanometer-sized C14 Laves phase and hexagonal Zr₂Al-type phase in BCC matrix, with intermetallic volume fraction increasing with Al content [8]. Later TEM showed these phases as ZrAlV and Zr₅Al₃, with ordered B2 matrix phase [9]. In the current alloy, Al participates in forming Al₃Zr₅ and Laves AlZrV nanoparticles.
- **Property Effect:** The current Al₈Nb₂₇Ti₂₉V₂₈Zr₈ alloy shows 1250 MPa yield strength at RT with 38% compressive ductility, and 720 MPa at 700 °C. The authors attribute differences in mechanical properties between this alloy and AlNbTiVZr₀.₅ to phase balance effects of Al and the presence of fine second-phase particles. Specific numerical values for pure Al effect: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for Al-only contribution.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Increased Al content increases volume fraction of intermetallic phases, which may affect ductility; not explicitly quantified in this paper
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3, Results and discussion, paragraphs 3–4: "Al and Zr additions have very pronounced effect on the structure of MPEAs, resulting in second phase formation... the AlNbTiV homogenized alloy, in turn, had an ordered B2 single phase structure, but alloying with Zr resulted in change of the degree of order of the B2 phase and precipitation of the Zr₅Al₃ and C14 Laves ZrAlV phases, according increase of Zr-content [9]." Also: "The difference in mechanical properties of these alloys may be attributed to the phase balance effects of Al on such MPEAs and the presence of a fine second phase precipitated inside the grains."

## [Zr]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** Al-Nb-Ti-V-Zr MPEA; AlNbTiVZrₓ refractory high-entropy alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 8 at.% in primary alloy Al₈Nb₂₇Ti₂₉V₂₈Zr₈; referenced AlNbTiVZr₀.₅ has approximately 18 at.% Zr (from Table 1 wt.% conversion)
- **Main Function:** Promotes precipitation of Zr-rich intermetallic phases (Al₃Zr₅, Laves AlZrV, Zr₅Al₃); changes degree of order of B2 phase; forms micrometric Zr-rich particles

- **Property Effect:** Contributes to precipitation strengthening from nanoscale Zr-containing phases and to solid-solution strengthening. The alloy exhibits 1250 MPa RT yield strength and 720 MPa at 700 °C. Specific numerical values for Zr-only contribution: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

## [Al + Zr combination]
- **Applicable Alloy System:** Al-Nb-Ti-V-Zr MPEA; AlNbTiVZrₓ refractory high-entropy alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Al 8 at.% + Zr 8 at.% in primary alloy

## Precipitation Strengthening by Intermetallic Nanoparticles
- **Related Elements:** Al, Zr; phases: Al₃Zr₅ and Laves AlZrV (also referenced Zr₅Al₃ and C14 Laves ZrAlV)
- **Typical Alloys:** Al₈Nb₂₇Ti₂₉V₂₈Zr₈; AlNbTiVZrₓ [8,9,15]
- **Key Compositions:** Al 8 at.% + Zr 8 at.%; Al 11 at.% + Zr ~18 at.% in AlNbTiVZr₀.₅
- **Microstructural Changes:** Nanometer-sized Al₃Zr₅ and Laves AlZrV (hexagonal) particles finely precipitated inside BCC grains after 700 °C/168 h heat treatment. High volume fraction of nanoscale precipitates. Micrometric Zr-rich particles also present.
- **Property Improvements:** The homogenized + heat treated alloy exhibits yield strength of 1250 MPa at RT with 38% compressive ductility, and 720 MPa at 700 °C. The authors attribute differences in mechanical properties to "the phase balance effects of Al... and the presence of a fine second phase precipitated inside the grains." AlNbTiVZr₀.₅ with similar microstructure exhibits superior specific yield stress (262.4 kPa·m³/kg) and ductility (50%) at RT, and 680 MPa at 800 °C.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Coarse Zr-rich particles form in addition to fine precipitates; authors note that compressive ductility is not as meaningful as tensile ductility for damage tolerance and additional work is needed.

## Low-Density Alloy Design Strategy
- **Related Elements:** Al (2.70 g/cm³), Ti (4.51 g/cm³), V (6.11 g/cm³), Zr (6.51 g/cm³), Nb (8.57 g/cm³)
- **Typical Alloys:** Al₈Nb₂₇Ti₂₉V₂₈Zr₈
- **Key Compositions:** 8 at.% Al, 27 at.% Nb, 29 at.% Ti, 28 at.% V, 8 at.% Zr
- **Microstructural Changes:** Selection of relatively low-density elements while maintaining BCC solid-solution matrix and intermetallic precipitation
- **Property Improvements:** Measured density 6.34 g/cm³ — approximately 23% lower than Inconel 718 (8.19 g/cm³), 25% lower than Inconel 625 (8.44 g/cm³), and 19% lower than 15-5PH (7.8 g/cm³). Higher than Ti alloys Ti6Al4V (4.42 g/cm³) and Ti-β 21s (4.93 g/cm³). Specific yield strength at 700 °C is 113.6 kPa·m³/kg, nearly twice that of Ti6Al4V (67.7 kPa·m³/kg — note: paper text says 67.9 in Table 1; abstract says 67.7) and Inconel 625 (46.2 kPa·m³/kg), and more than three times higher than Ti-β 21S (35.4 kPa·m³/kg abstract; 35.5 in Table 1).
- **Risks and Limitations:** Density still higher than Ti alloys; high Nb content (40 wt.%) adds density

# Key Conclusions
1. The homogenized + heat treated Al₈Nb₂₇Ti₂₉V₂₈Zr₈ alloy is composed of an A2 disordered BCC matrix structure containing nanometer-sized particles of Al₃Zr₅ and Laves AlZrV phases. Micrometric Zr-rich particles are also present in both homogenized and homogenized + heat treated conditions.
2. The alloy shows high specific yield stress (197.16 kPa·m³/kg) and good compressive ductility (38%) at room temperature.
3. At elevated temperatures, the alloy shows 720 MPa yield strength at 700 °C and 690 MPa at 800 °C. Specific yield strength at 700 °C is 113.6 kPa·m³/kg, nearly twice that of Ti6Al4V and Inconel 625, and more than three times higher than Ti-β 21S.
4. The measured density is 6.34 g/cm³, approximately 23–25% lower than Inconel 625 and Inconel 718.
5. The alloy is potentially attractive for continued development for aerospace structural applications up to at least about 700 °C.
6. The authors note that the tests performed and results obtained were not sufficient to explain the simultaneous high yield stress and ductility at room temperature.
7. The authors also caution that compressive ductility is not as meaningful as tensile ductility, especially regarding damage tolerance properties, and additional work will be necessary.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Testing limitations:** Only compression testing performed; no tensile testing, fracture toughness, creep, or fatigue testing reported. Compressive ductility at elevated temperatures was not reported because specimens were deformed only up to 20%.
- **Characterization limitations:** The micrometric light gray Zr-rich particles could not be characterized by TEM. EDS chemical analysis of nanometer-sized particles was not possible due to their small size. XRD non-identified peaks could only be tentatively associated with TEM-observed phases.
- **Mechanistic understanding:** The authors explicitly state that the tests performed were insufficient to explain the simultaneous high yield stress and ductility at room temperature.
- **Oxidation behavior:** Not evaluated; no conclusions can be drawn about oxidation resistance from this paper.
- **Oxidation protection during processing:** Though not an oxidation study, the heat treatment atmosphere (vacuum-sealed quartz tubes with Ti chips) and Ar protection during compression testing are reported.
- **Composition scope:** Only one primary alloy composition plus comparative literature data for AlNbTiVZr₀.₅; no systematic composition variation in this paper.
- **Processing scope:** Only arc melting plus two heat-treatment conditions (homogenized 1200 °C/24 h; homogenized + 700 °C/168 h); no other processing routes, no wrought processing, no AM processing.
- **Applicability:** Aerospace structural components operating up to about 700 °C where high specific yield strength is required.
- **External validity:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper; the paper notes that additional work is necessary to establish tensile ductility and damage tolerance.
