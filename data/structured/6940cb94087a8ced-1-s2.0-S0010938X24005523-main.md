# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: γ matrix (FCC-structured Ni-based solid solution)
- **Second phase**: γ′ precipitates (L1₂-structured Ni₃Al)
- **Original powder surface**: An amorphous oxide layer approximately **10 nm** thick, containing oxide particles such as NiO, CoO, Cr₂O₃, and Al₂O₃ (Fig. 1)
- **After initial sintering**:
  - The oxide layer thickness increased from **30 nm to 50 nm** (Note: the paper says “increased from 30 nm to 50 nm after initial sintering compared to the original powder surface”; this appears to be a typo in the original text, as the original oxide layer thickness is stated as ~10 nm in Section 3.1. Not explicitly clarified in the original paper.)
  - Stable oxide particles at sintering necks/PPBs: **α-Al₂O₃** and **ZrO₂**
  - Unstable oxides that decompose: **NiO, CoO, Cr₂O₃, TiO₂**
- **γ′ precipitation**: γ′ precipitation was observed during sintering, indicating significant elemental diffusion even over a brief sintering period
- **O distribution**: O content is higher in γ matrix (0.3–0.8 at.% near powder surface) than in γ′ particles (below 0.3 at.% near powder surface); O content in γ matrix far from powder surface is ~0.25 at.%
- **Grain boundaries and phase boundaries**: No significant O segregation was found at grain boundaries or γ/γ′ interfaces

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxidation weight gain**: Not reported in the original paper.
- **Oxide layer type on original powder**: Amorphous oxide layer containing NiO, CoO, Cr₂O₃, Al₂O₃ (and TiO₂ mentioned later)
- **Oxide particles at PPBs after sintering**: α-Al₂O₃ and ZrO₂
- **Oxidation temperature and duration**: Sintering at 1000 °C for 10 min; heat treatment at 1000 °C for 10 min in Ar
- **Oxide layer evolution**:
  - Original oxide layer is amorphous, ~10 nm thick
  - Before sintering necks form: amorphous oxide layer undergoes nucleation and growth, forming stable (Al₂O₃, ZrO₂) and unstable (NiO, CoO, Cr₂O₃, TiO₂) crystalline oxide particles
  - During sintering: unstable oxides decompose due to decreasing O partial pressure; stable Al₂O₃ and ZrO₂ particles remain at PPBs
- **Effects of alloying elements**:
  - Zr and Nb segregate to outer part of oxide layer after sintering (outward diffusion)
  - Ti and Al enrich in the inner part of the oxide layer
  - Al and Ti diffuse into Cr₂O₃ oxide particles during formation
  - Co, Mo, W are trapped at the oxide particle/matrix interface due to their higher Gibbs free energy for oxide formation compared to Cr, Al, Ti
- **Internal oxidation**: Very weak; no oxide particles found inside powder interior; O exists as interstitial solid solution atoms in γ matrix
- **Issues**: PPB oxides cause stress concentration and can become crack sources; O dissolution in γ matrix

## [Oxygen (O)]

### [Applicable Alloy System]
FGH96 Ni-based PM superalloy

### [Addition Amount or Composition Range]
0.038 wt.% O in original FGH96 powder (Table 1)

### [Main Function]
O is present as an impurity from powder atomization. It forms an amorphous oxide layer on powder surfaces and exists as interstitial atoms in the γ matrix after sintering. O is involved in the formation and decomposition of oxides at prior particle boundaries (PPBs).

### [Microstructural Mechanism]
- O diffusion during sintering is sourced intrinsically from the powder surface (termed "intrinsic O")
- O solid solution into octahedral (Oc) interstitial sites of FCC Ni (γ matrix) has the lowest formation energy (-8.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ J) compared to Ni₃Al (γ′) Oc sites (-7.90 × 10⁻¹⁹ J and -7.00 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)
- O does not preferentially segregate at grain boundaries or γ/γ′ interfaces in the low-O-content sintering condition
- O diffuses through γ channels preferentially over γ′ phase

### [Property Effect]
- O content in γ matrix near powder surface: 0.3–0.8 at.%
- O content in γ′ particles: below 0.3 at.%
- O content in γ matrix far from powder surface: ~0.25 at.%
- No specific mechanical property values are provided in the original paper.

### [Potential Side Effects]
- PPB oxides cause stress concentration and can become crack sources
- O dissolution in γ matrix
- Original paper does not provide specific mechanical property values regarding O effects.

# Key Conclusions

1. The amorphous oxide layer on original FGH96 powder surface is ~10 nm thick and contains various oxides (NiO, CoO, Cr₂O₃, Al₂O₃).

2. Before sintering necks form, the amorphous oxide layer undergoes crystallization, forming stable (Al₂O₃, ZrO₂) and unstable (NiO, CoO, Cr₂O₃, TiO₂) crystalline oxide particles.

3. During sintering, unstable oxides decompose due to decreasing O partial pressure. Stable Al₂O₃ and ZrO₂ particles remain at PPBs.

4. O diffuses preferentially through γ matrix channels as interstitial atoms in octahedral (Oc) sites, not through grain boundaries or phase boundaries.

5. DFT calculations show:
   - O in Ni (γ matrix) Oc site: formation energy -8.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
   - O in Ni₃Al (γ′) Oc sites: formation energy -7.00 × 10⁻¹⁹ J and -7.90 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
   - O in Ni cell Oc site: -7.74 × 10⁻¹⁹ J (lowest among Oc, Te, and Su sites)
   - O in Ni₃Al (2×2×2 supercell): Ni-O = -8.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, Ni₃Al-O1 = -7.00 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, Ni₃Al-O2 = -7.90 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

6. The sequence of preferential reactions involving dissolved O in the matrix is: solid solution in Oc sites of γ > solid solution in Te sites of γ > oxidation to form oxides.

7. Internal oxidation is weak due to high energy barriers and low O partial pressure. O ultimately dissolves as solid solution atoms in the matrix.
