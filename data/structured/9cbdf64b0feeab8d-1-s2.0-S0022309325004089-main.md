# Alloy System and Research Object

This paper investigates the use of a binary Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ bulk metallic glass (BMG) as a superplastic interlayer for solid-state diffusion bonding of two high-temperature alloys: a TiAl-based alloy and GH4099 Ni-based superalloy. The focus is on the joining process, interfacial reaction, and joint mechanical properties rather than bulk alloy development. The material system is not a refractory W/Mo/Nb-based alloy system, but rather a joining technology for high-temperature structural alloys.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Interlayer Fabrication
- **Method**: Vacuum die-casting using a custom-designed copper mold
- **Vacuum**: High-purity vacuum environment (base pressure not explicitly specified for casting)
- **Form produced**: 0.5 mm-thick Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ amorphous interlayer
- **Characterization**: XRD showed broad diffuse peak confirming amorphous structure; TEM showed homogeneous grain-free structure; SAED showed characteristic diffuse halo

## Thermal Properties of Interlayer
- **DSC results (Table 2)**:

| Heating rate | Tg | Tx | Tm | Tl |
|--------------|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| 10 °C/min | 878 °C | 903 °C | 1446 °C | 1480 °C |
| 20 °C/min | 892 °C | 932 °C | 1450 °C | 1483 °C |
| 30 °C/min | 903 °C | 952 °C | 1452 °C | 1486 °C |

- **Solidus temperature (Ts)**: 1177 °C
- **Liquidus temperature (Tl)**: 1210 °C
- **Crystallization time from room temperature**: Approximately 27 min (calculated using Kissinger equation)

## Bonding Process
- **Configuration**: Sandwich structure — TiAl base alloy / Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ BMG interlayer / GH4099 base alloy
- **Equipment**: Vacuum furnace hot pressing
- **Base pressure**: 10⁻³ Pa
- **Applied pressure**: 20 MPa (P = 20 MPa)
- **Stage 1**: Heating at 20 °C/min to 530 °C, isothermal holding at 530 °C to allow amorphous interlayer to fill interfacial gaps
- **Stage 2**: Ramp at 10 °C/min to 1100 °C (to minimize crystallization), followed by 60-minute isothermal hold at 1100 °C for interfacial atomic diffusion
- **Surface preparation**: Ground with SiC abrasive papers to 600 grit finish, ultrasonic degreasing in acetone (10 min, 40 kHz)

## Orthogonal Experimental Conditions

|-----------|-------------|------|
| A | 1100 °C | 30 min |
| B | 1100 °C | 120 min |
| C | 1060 °C | 60 min |
| D | 1080 °C | 60 min |

- **Grain size vs. temperature**: Grain size increases exponentially with temperature (R² = 0.92): 1.2 ± 0.1 μm at 1060 °C vs. 1.8 ± 0.2 μm at 1100 °C
- **Note**: At 1060 °C, interfacial reaction was insufficient; a long strip defect was observed near the TiAl-based alloy interface. Defects disappeared as diffusion temperature increased from 1060 °C to 1080 °C.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Base Material Microstructures
- **TiAl-based alloy**: Well-defined lamellar microstructure, alternating γ-TiAl and α₂-Ti₃Al phases (confirmed by TEM, Fig. 2a)
- **GH4099**: Equiaxed grain structure with uniform grain size distribution (confirmed by TEM, Fig. 2b)

## Post-Superplastic Filling
- Interlayer retained amorphous structure (XRD showed no sharp crystalline peaks)
- Complete infiltration and elimination of interfacial defects (cracks, voids) at both TiAl and Ni-based alloy interfaces

## Post-Atomic Diffusion (1100 °C / 60 min)
Three distinct regions from TiAl-side to Ni-side:
- **Layer I**: TiAl-based diffusion reaction region
- **Layer II**: Intermediate crystallization region (crystallized amorphous filler)
- **Layer III**: GH4099-based diffusion reaction layer

## Phase Composition (TiAl side → GH4099 side)
Confirmed by EDS, XRD, TEM, and SAED:
**γ-TiAl / Ti₃Al / AlNi₂Ti / NiNb / Ni₃Nb / γ-Ni**

## Layer I (TiAl-based interface)
- **Phase A**: Ti₃Al — rectangular phase, formed by atomic diffusion of Ni and Nb from intermediate region into TiAl-based alloy
- **Phase B**: AlNi₂Ti (τ₂-Al₂NiTi) — fine black phase, formed by interaction of TiAl alloy with amorphous interlayer
- EDS results (at.%): A = Ti 50.39, Al 37.75, Ni 9.94, Nb 1.93; B = Ti 23.71, Al 40.48, Ni 23.93, Nb 11.89

## Layer II (Intermediate crystallization region)
- Composed of fine lumpy phases and basement phase
- **Phase C**: Ni₃Nb (EDS: Ni 60.29, Nb 39.26 at.%)
- **Phase D**: Ni₇Nb₆ (EDS: Ti 5.46, Al 0.85, Ni 52.49, Nb 41.20 at.%)
- **Average grain size after crystallization**: ~1.462 μm (at 1100 °C/60 min)
- Grain growth limited during crystallization

## Layer III (GH4099-based interface)
- No new phases formed in Ni-based diffusion reaction layer

## Phase Formation Thermodynamics
- Phases with large negative mixing enthalpy are easier to form
- Mixing enthalpy comparison used to infer Ti₃Al and AlNi₂Ti formation

# Mechanical Properties

## Nanoindentation
- **Test parameters**: Maximum load 150 mN, hold time 20 s (to minimize creep effects)
- **Base hardness at TiAl/interlayer interface**: 4.2 ± 0.3 GPa
- **Interfacial hardness after bonding**: 6.1 ± 0.4 GPa
- **Hardness increase**: 35–45%
- **Attributed to**: γ′ precipitation and Al-Ni-Ti intermetallic formation
- **Note**: Phases with high elastic modulus and microhardness exhibited low plastic deformation capability; joint prone to cracking in high-hardness regions

## Shear Strength
- **Loading rate**: 0.15 mm/min
- **Testing temperatures**: Room temperature (30 °C) and 750 °C
- **Maximum shear strength (1100 °C / 120 min or optimal condition)**:
  - Room temperature: **315 MPa**
  - 750 °C: **250 MPa**
- **Base material references**:
  - TiAl alloy at room temperature: ~480 MPa
  - TiAl alloy at 750 °C: ~350 MPa
- Both the room-temperature and 750 °C shear strength values are slightly lower than the TiAl base material
- Shear strength at both temperatures increases with elevating bonding temperature and extending holding time

## Fracture Behavior
### Room-temperature fracture
- Distinct cleavage morphology with step heights ranging 2–5 μm
- Brittle fracture mechanism confirmed
- EDS on fracture surface: rich in Al, Ni, Ti
- Fracture occurred at the TiAl-interface

### 750 °C fracture
- Presence of slip bands, indicating localized plastic deformation
- Mixed fracture mode dominated by brittle fracture
- No dimples (no characteristic plastic deformation features)
- Joint possesses good high-temperature resistance

- **Not explicitly studied in the original paper.** The paper focuses on the joining process, microstructure, and mechanical properties (shear strength at room temperature and 750 °C) but does not address oxidation resistance or long-term service stability.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Ni (in Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ BMG interlayer)

**Applicable Alloy System**: Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ bulk metallic glass interlayer for joining TiAl-based alloy and GH4099 Ni-based superalloy

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 62 at.% in the amorphous interlayer; also present in GH4099 as the base element

**Main Function**: Forms the high-melting-point amorphous interlayer; provides structural basis for superplastic deformation in supercooled liquid region; enables atomic diffusion bonding and metallurgical bond formation

**Microstructural Mechanism**: In the supercooled liquid region, Ni participates in viscous flow enabling gap filling; during 1100 °C diffusion, Ni from interlayer diffuses into TiAl interface and reacts to form intermetallic compounds (AlNi₂Ti, Ni₃Nb); Ni also diffuses from GH4099 into interlayer

**Property Effect**: Joints achieve shear strength of 315 MPa at room temperature and 250 MPa at 750 °C; high melting point (1483 K) enables service temperatures exceeding bonding temperature

**Potential Side Effects**: Formation of Al-Ni-Ti intermetallics at TiAl-side interface increases local hardness (35–45% increase), leading to brittle fracture tendency at the TiAl-interlayer interface

**Original Evidence**: "When the temperature reaches 1100 °C, the Ti, Al, and Ni elements in the base material will diffuse into the intermediate layer driven by thermodynamic forces and concentration gradients, reacting with the Ni and Nb within it."

## Nb (in Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ BMG interlayer)

**Applicable Alloy System**: Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ bulk metallic glass interlayer for TiAl/GH4099 joining

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 38 at.% in the amorphous interlayer; 2 wt.% in TiAl base alloy; 17–20 wt.% (appears in table) in GH4099

**Main Function**: Forms binary Ni-Nb amorphous alloy with high melting point; enables glass-forming ability; participates in crystallization reaction to form Ni₃Nb and Ni₇Nb₆ phases

**Microstructural Mechanism**: In supercooled liquid region, amorphous Ni-Nb exhibits dramatic viscosity reduction enabling superplastic filling; during high-temperature diffusion, Nb diffuses into TiAl side causing Ti₃Al formation; crystallized interlayer contains Ni₃Nb and Ni₇Nb₆ phases

**Property Effect**: Refined grain structure (average ~1.462 μm) after crystallization contributes to favorable mechanical properties via Hall-Petch strengthening

**Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**Original Evidence**: "Meanwhile, under thermodynamic driving forces, the atomic structure of the amorphous alloy intermediate layer — characterized by long-range disorder and short-range order — undergoes rearrangement into a three-dimensional periodic atomic arrangement corresponding to a crystalline structure, namely a crystallization reaction, which generates Ni₃Nb and Ni₇Nb₆ phases."

## Ti + Al (TiAl-based alloy side)

**Applicable Alloy System**: TiAl/GH4099 dissimilar joint with Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ BMG interlayer

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: TiAl base alloy: 48 wt.% Al, 48 wt.% Ti, 2 wt.% Cr, 2 wt.% Nb

**Main Function**: TiAl base provides lamellar γ-TiAl/α₂-Ti₃Al structure, low density, and high-temperature capability; Al and Ti from TiAl side diffuse into interlayer and react with Ni to form intermetallics

**Microstructural Mechanism**: At 1100 °C, Ti and Al diffuse from TiAl side into interlayer, reacting with Ni and Nb to form Ti₃Al (rectangular phase A) and AlNi₂Ti (fine black phase B); the large negative mixing enthalpy drives intermetallic formation

**Property Effect**: Joint achieves 315 MPa room-temperature shear strength and 250 MPa at 750 °C, slightly lower than TiAl base material (~480 MPa RT, ~350 MPa at 750 °C); interfacial hardness increases from 4.2 ± 0.3 GPa to 6.1 ± 0.4 GPa

**Potential Side Effects**: Formation of brittle intermetallics (Ti₃Al, AlNi₂Ti) at interface creates high-hardness zone where cracking preferentially occurs; room-temperature fracture shows cleavage morphology with 2–5 μm step heights, confirming brittle fracture

**Original Evidence**: "From the high magnification of layer I, it can be found that the atomic diffusion of Ni, Nb from the intermediate region into the TiAl-based alloys caused the formation of rectangular phase A. The interaction of TiAl-based alloy and amorphous interlayer caused the formation of fine black phase B."

**Applicable Alloy System**: TiAl/Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ BMG/GH4099 dissimilar joint

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening (Hall-Petch)

**Related Elements**: Not element-specific; relates to crystallized microstructure of Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ interlayer

**Typical Alloys**: Crystallized Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ interlayer in TiAl/GH4099 joint

**Key Compositions**: Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ (at.%), crystallized into Ni₃Nb and Ni₇Nb₆ phases

**Microstructural Changes**: Crystallized amorphous filler forms refined grains with average size of ~1.462 μm; grain size increases exponentially with bonding temperature (R² = 0.92), from 1.2 ± 0.1 μm at 1060 °C to 1.8 ± 0.2 μm at 1100 °C

**Property Improvements**: Refined microstructure enhances joint mechanical properties through grain boundary strengthening (Hall-Petch relationship); absence of liquid-solid phase transformation results in finer phase formation compared to conventional brazing

**Risks and Limitations**: Thermally-activated grain growth at higher bonding temperatures degrades joint performance

**Original Evidence**: "The absence of liquid-solid phase transformation during bonding results in significantly finer phase formation within the joint compared to conventional liquidus joining methods. This microstructural refinement, consistent with Hall-Petch strengthening mechanisms, enhances the joint's mechanical properties through grain boundary strengthening."

## Superplastic Filling Mechanism

**Related Elements**: Ni, Nb (in amorphous interlayer)

**Typical Alloys**: Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ bulk metallic glass

**Key Compositions**: 62 at.% Ni, 38 at.% Nb

**Microstructural Changes**: Amorphous interlayer enters supercooled liquid region where viscosity dramatically decreases; external pressure (20 MPa) drives viscous flow filling interfacial gaps

**Property Improvements**: Complete elimination of interfacial defects (cracks, voids) at both TiAl and Ni-based alloy interfaces; enables defect-free metallurgical bonding

**Risks and Limitations**: Crystallization of amorphous filler can significantly degrade ductility and impair superplastic flow; crystallization time from room temperature estimated at ~27 min, imposing time constraints

**Original Evidence**: "Within this temperature regime, the amorphous alloy exhibited a dramatic reduction in viscosity with increasing temperature, enabling the external pressure to effectively fill the interfacial gaps between the base metal and the amorphous filler."

## Diffusion Bonding / Reaction Diffusion

**Related Elements**: Ti, Al, Ni, Nb (interdiffusion across interfaces)

**Typical Alloys**: TiAl / Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ BMG / GH4099

**Key Compositions**: Not applicable; temperature-dependent process

**Microstructural Changes**: Interdiffusion of Ti/Al from TiAl side into interlayer; Ni from interlayer into TiAl; Ni and Nb from interlayer into base materials; formation of intermetallic phases

**Property Improvements**: Metallurgical bonding established; joint achieves 315 MPa shear strength at room temperature and 250 MPa at 750 °C; superior high-temperature performance compared to conventional brazed joints

**Risks and Limitations**: Reaction layer thickness on TiAl side much greater than on GH4099 side; insufficient interfacial reaction at 1060 °C (long strip defect observed near TiAl interface)

**Original Evidence**: "This interdiffusion behavior, observed following isothermal treatment at 1100 °C, confirms the establishment of effective metallurgical bonding between the interlayer and substrate alloys."

# Key Conclusions

1. **Innovative joining strategy**: Successfully demonstrated a novel method combining vacuum die-casting of Ni₆₂Nb₃₈ BMG interlayer with superplastic diffusion bonding for joining TiAl and GH4099 alloys.

2. **Defect-free bonding**: Complete elimination of interfacial voids and cracks achieved via superplastic filling in the supercooled liquid region under 20 MPa pressure.

3. **Phase constitution**: The joint phase sequence from TiAl side to GH4099 side is: γ-TiAl / Ti₃Al / AlNi₂Ti / NiNb / Ni₃Nb / γ-Ni.

4. **Mechanical performance**: Maximum shear strength of **315 MPa at room temperature** and **250 MPa at 750 °C**, slightly lower than TiAl base material (480 MPa RT, 350 MPa at 750 °C).

5. **Interfacial hardening**: Nanoindentation shows 35–45% hardness increase at TiAl/interlayer interface (4.2 ± 0.3 GPa → 6.1 ± 0.4 GPa) due to γ′ precipitation and Al-Ni-Ti intermetallic formation.

6. **Microstructural refinement**: Crystallized interlayer exhibits fine grain size (~1.462 μm at 1100 °C/60 min), benefiting from Hall-Petch strengthening; grain size increases exponentially with bonding temperature.

7. **Enhanced high-temperature capability**: Unlike brazed joints limited to service below brazing temperature, superplastic diffusion bonded joints can potentially serve above their bonding temperature.

8. **Three advantages over conventional diffusion bonding**: (1) reduced sensitivity to surface preparation; (2) refined joint microstructure with enhanced phase stability; (3) superior mechanical reliability of crystallized interlayer.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

## Limitations
- **Brittle fracture at room temperature**: The joint exhibits cleavage fracture morphology with 2–5 μm step heights, confirming brittle behavior; fracture preferentially occurs at the TiAl-interlayer interface in the high-hardness Al-Ni-Ti intermetallic region.
- **Insufficient interfacial reaction at lower temperatures**: At 1060 °C, a long strip defect was observed near the TiAl-based alloy interface, indicating incomplete bonding.
- **Grain growth with increasing temperature**: Crystallized interlayer grain size increases exponentially with temperature (1.2 μm at 1060 °C vs. 1.8 μm at 1100 °C), degrading performance.
- **Thicker reaction layer on TiAl side**: The reaction layer thickness on the TiAl side is much greater than on the GH4099 side, suggesting unequal diffusion kinetics.
- **Joint strength below base material**: Shear strength values (315 MPa RT, 250 MPa at 750 °C) are lower than TiAl base material (480 MPa RT, 350 MPa at 750 °C).
- **Time constraint from crystallization**: The amorphous interlayer has a limited processing window (~27 min from room temperature to crystallization), constraining bonding process design.

## Applicable Scope
- **Joining of dissimilar high-temperature alloys**: Method specifically demonstrated for TiAl-based alloy and GH4099 Ni-based superalloy.
- **Bonding temperature range**: 1060–1100 °C (below the TiAl phase transformation point and below the solidus of the amorphous interlayer at 1177 °C).
- **Potential for service above bonding temperature**: Unlike brazed joints, this method may enable high-temperature service exceeding the bonding temperature.
- **Reduced surface preparation requirements**: Compared to conventional diffusion bonding, this method is less sensitive to surface roughness.
- **The methodology may be transferable**: The concept of using superplastic BMG interlayers could potentially be applied to other dissimilar high-temperature alloy combinations, though this is not directly demonstrated in the paper.

**Note on oxidation resistance**: The original paper does not address oxidation resistance, long-term thermal stability under service conditions, creep performance, or fatigue behavior of the joint.
