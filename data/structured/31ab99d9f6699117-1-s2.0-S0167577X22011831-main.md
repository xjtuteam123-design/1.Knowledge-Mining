# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Preparation route**: Magnetron sputtering → alternating stacking → hot pressing → reaction → homogenization

1. **Magnetron sputtering of V**: V layers with different thicknesses were deposited on the single side of Ni foils using ZXT-500 equipment. Sputtering time: 1–4 hours (to obtain different V contents).

2. **Alternating stacking**: 14 pieces of Ni(V) foils and 13 pieces of Al foils were alternately stacked (100 × 100 mm²).

3. **Step 1 — Hot pressing**: 620 °C / 15 MPa / 1 h — to obtain a laminated Ni(V)/Al sheet with a diffused intermediate zone.

4. **Step 2 — Reaction (solid–liquid)**: 800 °C / 0.5 h / no pressure — to quickly consume the low-melting Al foils.

5. **Step 3 — Homogenization**: 1250 °C / 1 h / 40 MPa — to obtain a homogeneous NiAlV sheet.

**Key processing features**:
- The V layer on one side of the Ni foil hinders mutual diffusion between Ni and Al foils. A diffusion channel is left on the side of the Ni foil without V to ensure rapid reaction-diffusion of the Al layer.
- The reaction at 620 °C forms a diffusion zone (NiAl₃ and Ni₂Al₃) on the V-free side and a (Ni, Al, V) transition layer (mainly Al₈V₅ phase) on the V-containing side.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Matrix phase
- **NiAl**: B2 ordered body-centered cubic intermetallic

## Intermediate reaction phases (at 620 °C)
- **Diffusion zone** (side of Ni foil without V layer): NiAl₃ and Ni₂Al₃ intermetallic compounds (identified by SEM/EDX)
- **Transition layer** (side of Ni foil with V layer): primarily Al₈V₅ phase (identified by XRD, standard PDF cards)

## Final microstructure (after homogenization at 1250 °C)
- **Bimodal micro-laminated structure**: composed of alternative arrays of coarse grain layers (original Ni foil regions) and fine grain layers (original Al foil regions)
- **V precipitates at NiAl grain boundaries**: dark-contrast V phase precipitates observed at NiAl grain boundaries
- **Lamellar V precipitates within NiAl grains**
- SAED patterns confirmed:
  - NiAl matrix along [1̄ 1 1] zone axis
  - V phase along [1̄ 1 1] zone axis

## Grain size
- Not quantified with specific numerical values in the original paper. Described qualitatively as "coarse grain layers" (original Ni foil regions) and "fine grain layers" (original Al foil regions).

## Precipitate details
- V precipitates at grain boundaries
- Lamellar V precipitates within grains
- Solubility of V in NiAl documented as ~12% at high temperature, decreasing sharply with lowering temperature → V is excluded and precipitates during cooling.

## Strengthening mechanisms discussed in the paper
- **Solid-solution strengthening** by V (dominant at lower V content)
- **Precipitation strengthening** by V precipitates (dominant at higher V content)
- The paper does NOT discuss dispersion strengthening, ODS strengthening, carbide/boride strengthening, or grain-boundary strengthening in specific detail, though the bimodal grain structure may contribute.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

---

## Knowledge Card 1: V (Vanadium)

**[Element or Element Combination]**
V (Vanadium)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
NiAl-based intermetallic (B2 ordered), specifically NiAlV sheets prepared by magnetron sputtering + foil-reaction

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
0.27 to 1.01 (unit not explicitly stated in the original paper; likely at.%, but this is an assumption that should be verified). Prepared by varying magnetron sputtering time from 1 to 4 hours.

**[Main Function]**
- Improves high-temperature strength of NiAl through solid-solution strengthening (at low V content) and precipitation strengthening (at higher V content)
- Retains considerable fracture strain (>50%) at high temperature
- As stated in the paper: "V is the only element that can form both pseudo-binary eutectic and Heusler phase in NiAl alloy. And V has good mechanical property and low density, which is more suitable to be introduced into NiAl matrix as microalloying element."

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- V dissolves in NiAl matrix at high temperature (solubility ~12%), then precipitates during cooling as V phase
- Forms V precipitates at NiAl grain boundaries (darker contrast in HAADF) and lamellar V precipitates within grains
- During intermediate reaction at 620 °C, V forms Al₈V₅ transition layer phase
- V layer hinders Ni–Al interdiffusion on the coated side of the Ni foil, creating asymmetric diffusion zones

**[Property Effect]**
- UTS at 1000 °C increases from 80.1 MPa (NiAl-0.27V) to 166.4 MPa (NiAl-1.01V)
- YS at 1000 °C increases from 76.2 MPa (NiAl-0.27V) to 160.2 MPa (NiAl-1.01V)
- Fracture strain maintained at >50% (54.5%) at 1000 °C for NiAl-1.01V
- UTS of NiAl-1.01V is 128.9% higher than pure NiAl at 1000 °C
- No room-temperature mechanical property data reported

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- V layer on one side of Ni foil hinders Ni–Al interdiffusion, requiring a diffusion channel on the V-free side to ensure adequate Al reaction. This creates a bimodal grain structure (coarse grain layers from Ni foils, fine grain layers from Al foils), which may be advantageous or disadvantageous depending on application.

**[Original Evidence]**
- "The improved high-temperature strength of NiAl-0.27V sheet with relatively fewer V content is mainly attributed to the solution strengthening effect of V, in comparison with pure NiAl." (Section 3, Results and Discussion)
- "With the increasing of V content, the V phases are continuously precipitated from the supersaturated NiAl matrix, which significantly improves the high-temperature strength of NiAlV alloy with relatively more V content by precipitation strengthening." (Section 3)
- "It is documented that the solubility of V in NiAl is ~12% at high temperature and the solubility decreases sharply with lowering the temperature." (Fig. 3 discussion)
- Fig. 3: TEM cross-sectional images showing V precipitates at grain boundaries and lamellar V precipitates within grains
- Fig. 4: Tensile stress–strain curves at 1000 °C

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

---

## Knowledge Card 1: Solid-Solution Strengthening

**[Related Elements]**
V (vanadium)

**[Typical Alloys]**
NiAlV (low V content: NiAl-0.27V)

**[Key Compositions]**
V content ~0.27 (unit not specified)

**[Microstructural Changes]**
- V atoms replace a small amount of Al atoms in NiAl matrix, forming NiAlV solid solution
- At low V content and high temperature, V remains dissolved in the NiAl matrix

**[Property Improvements]**
- UTS at 1000 °C: 80.1 MPa for NiAl-0.27V (compared to pure NiAl, though pure NiAl value not directly stated; the conclusion states improvement is attributed to V solution strengthening)

**[Risks and Limitations]**
- At higher V content, V precipitates, transitioning the dominant mechanism from solid-solution to precipitation strengthening

**[Original Evidence]**
- "the improved high-temperature strength of NiAl-0.27V sheet with relatively fewer V content is mainly attributed to the solution strengthening effect of V, in comparison with pure NiAl"

## Knowledge Card 2: Precipitation Strengthening

**[Related Elements]**
V (vanadium)

**[Typical Alloys]**
NiAlV (higher V content: NiAl-0.75V, NiAl-1.01V)

**[Key Compositions]**
V content ~0.57 to 1.01 (unit not specified)

**[Microstructural Changes]**

**[Property Improvements]**

**[Related Elements]**

# Key Conclusions

1. A novel NiAlV sheet with improved high-temperature mechanical properties was successfully fabricated using magnetron sputtering (of V on Ni foils) and foil-reaction (with Al foils).

2. The three-step processing route is:
   - 620 °C / 15 MPa / 1 h (hot pressing to create laminated structure)
   - 800 °C / 0.5 h / no pressure (solid–liquid reaction to consume Al foils)
   - 1250 °C / 1 h / 40 MPa (homogenization)

3. The final microstructure consists of a NiAl matrix with V precipitates at grain boundaries and lamellar V precipitates within grains. A bimodal grain structure (coarse/fine layers) is formed due to asymmetric diffusion caused by the V layer.

4. With increasing V content, UTS and YS at 1000 °C increase from 80.1 MPa and 76.2 MPa (NiAl-0.27V) to 166.4 MPa and 160.2 MPa (NiAl-1.01V), respectively.

5. The strengthening mechanisms are solid-solution strengthening (at low V content) and precipitation strengthening (at higher V content).

6. The UTS of NiAl-1.01V (166.4 MPa at 1000 °C) is 128.9% higher than that of pure NiAl, while maintaining a fracture strain >50% at 1000 °C.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations of the paper**:
1. The unit of V content (at.% vs wt.%) is not explicitly stated in the main text.
2. The pure NiAl baseline tensile value at 1000 °C is not directly given in the main text; only the improvement percentage (128.9%) is reported.
3. Room-temperature mechanical properties are not reported.
4. Oxidation resistance data are not provided.
5. Creep performance and rupture life data are not provided.
6. Hardness, density, and elastic modulus are not reported.
7. The contribution of the bimodal grain structure to mechanical properties is not explicitly analyzed.
8. Grain size values are not quantified.
9. The atmosphere during high-temperature tensile testing is not specified.
10. The strain rate for tensile testing is not specified.

**Applicable scope**:
- This study is specifically relevant to NiAl-based intermetallics with V microalloying, prepared via foil-reaction techniques.
- The results are directly applicable to thin-walled NiAl components for aerospace and automotive high-temperature applications.
- The magnetron sputtering + foil-reaction approach may be adaptable to other brittle intermetallic systems (e.g., TiAl as referenced in the paper).
- The knowledge is NOT directly applicable to Nb-W, Mo-W, W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloys, though the principle of microalloying for high-temperature strengthening and the foil-reaction processing strategy may have conceptual relevance.

**Note on relevance to refractory alloy domain**: While this paper concerns NiAl-based intermetallics rather than Nb/W/Mo-based refractory alloys or refractory high-entropy alloys, the strategies of:
- Microalloying to induce precipitation strengthening
- Foil-reaction to fabricate brittle-material thin sheets
- Bimodal grain structure manipulation
may provide transferable insights for processing brittle refractory alloys into sheet form.
