# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** Discovery of a Ni-based superalloy with low thermal expansion via machine learning
- **Authors:** Yifeng Tang, Guoliang Zhu, Qingbiao Tan, Decheng Kong, Yifan Cao, Rui Wang
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2024
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.matlet.2024.137047
- **Alloy system studied:** Ni-based superalloys (Ni-Mo-W system primarily)
- **Research objective:** To discover Ni-based superalloys with low coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) at elevated temperatures using machine learning (SVR model combined with genetic algorithm)
- **Material category:** Ni-based superalloy (not a refractory alloy; this is a conventional Ni-based superalloy with high Mo and W content)

> **Note:** This paper does not concern Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, refractory high-entropy, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. It is a Ni-based superalloy study. The following extraction reflects what is actually present in the paper.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Primary alloy system:** Ni-Mo-W system (uncommon system identified by machine learning)
- **Target property:** Low coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) from room temperature to 900 °C
- **Baseline for comparison:** Lowest CTE in existing Ni-based superalloys database: 13.2 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹ (RT to 900 °C)
- **Traditional Fe-based low-expansion superalloys (for context):** Incoloy 903, Incoloy 907 (CTE exceeds 13.0 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹ at 900 °C due to loss of Invar effect above Curie temperature of 400–500 °C)
- **Traditional Ni-based superalloys (for context):** CTE typically exceeds 14.0 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹ at room temperature
- **Paper specifically notes:** “The finally selected alloy demonstrated both low thermal expansion and superior mechanical properties compared to traditional superalloys with low thermal expansion.”

**[W (Tungsten)]**

- **Applicable Alloy System:** Ni-based superalloys (Ni-Mo-W system), specifically the low-CTE alloys discovered in this study
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** 2.96–20.00 wt.% in the alloys tested; Alloy 3-4 and Alloy 3-1 contain 16.00 wt.% and 16.26 wt.% W, respectively; Alloy 3-5 contains 20.00 wt.% W
- **Main Function:** Reduction of coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) at elevated temperatures
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** The original paper does not provide a microstructural mechanism for W's effect on CTE. It states only that “the high content of Mo and W is the primary reason for these alloys to maintain low CTE at high temperatures.” The SVR model is noted to have “poor interpretability regarding the effect of each element on the CTE of Ni-based superalloys.”
- **Property Effect:** Alloy 3-4 (16 wt.% W, 30 wt.% Mo, bal. Ni) achieved CTE of 11.15 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹ (RT to 900 °C) and UTS of 544.3 MPa at 900 °C with 43.8% elongation. Increasing W/Mo ratio (comparing Alloy 3-4 and Alloy 3-5) increased CTE from 11.15 to 11.71 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹.

- **Original Evidence:** Table 2; Section 3, Results and discussion; Fig. 2(c)–(d). Yamamoto formula (Equation 5) shows CTE = {13.9 + 0.073C_Cr − 0.08C_W − 0.082C_Mo − 0.018C_Al − 0.16C_Ti} × 10⁻⁶, indicating W has a negative coefficient (reduces CTE).

**[Low Thermal Expansion Alloying]**

- **Related Elements:** Mo and W (primary); Cr (negative impact)
- **Typical Alloys:** Ni-Mo-W system alloys discovered in this study (Alloy 3, Alloy 3-1, Alloy 3-4, Alloy 3-5)
- **Key Compositions:** 
  - Alloy 3-4: Ni + 16 wt.% W + 30 wt.% Mo → CTE = 11.15 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹ (RT–900 °C)
  - Alloy 3-1: Ni + 16.26 wt.% W + 30.44 wt.% Mo + 6.88 wt.% Ta + 1.31 wt.% Cr + 0.46 wt.% C → CTE = 11.0 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹ (lowest achieved)
  - Alloy 3: Ni + 16.43 wt.% W + 30.65 wt.% Mo + 4.69 wt.% Ta + 1.60 wt.% Cr + 0.45 wt.% C → CTE = 11.26 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹
- **Microstructural Changes:** Not discussed in the original paper

- **Risks and Limitations:** 
  - Adding Cr (for oxidation resistance) increases CTE from 11.15 to 14.33 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹
  - Increasing W/Mo ratio increases CTE (11.15 to 11.71 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹ when changing from Ni-16W-30Mo to Ni-20W-20Mo)
  - No oxidation resistance data available for the low-CTE alloys
- **Original Evidence:** Table 2; Fig. 2(c)–(d); Section 3, Results and discussion; Section 4, Conclusion.

# Key Conclusions

1. **ML model development:** A Support Vector Regression (SVR) model was developed with R²_train = 0.822, MSE_train = 0.611, R²_test = 0.844, MSE_test = 0.401 for predicting CTE of Ni-based superalloys.

2. **Alloy discovery:** Using genetic algorithm (GA) combined with the SVR model, ten samples were selected and produced from a pool of candidates.

3. **Lowest CTE achieved:** Alloy 3-1 achieved CTE of 11.0 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹ (RT to 900 °C), representing a 16.7% reduction compared to the best performance (13.2 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹) in the existing database.

4. **Alloy system:** The low CTE was found in the “uncommon Ni-Mo-W system.”

5. **Key alloying effect:** “High content of Mo and W is the primary reason for these alloys to maintain low CTE at high temperatures.”

6. **Cr trade-off:** Adding Cr (antioxidant element) increases CTE. Specifically, 10 wt.% Cr increased CTE from 11.15 to 14.33 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹.

7. **W/Mo ratio effect:** Increasing the W/Mo ratio increases CTE. Changing from Ni-16W-30Mo to Ni-20W-20Mo increased CTE from 11.15 to 11.71 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹.

8. **Mechanical properties of best simple composition:** Alloy 3-4 (Ni-16W-30Mo) exhibited UTS of 544.3 MPa and fracture elongation of 43.8% at 900 °C.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Paper scope mismatch:** This paper is about Ni-based superalloys, not refractory alloys (Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloys) and does not involve additive manufacturing. It is relevant only as a case study in machine-learning-guided alloy discovery for thermal property optimization.

2. **Microstructural characterization absent:** The original paper provides no microstructural information (phases, precipitates, grain size, segregation). The underlying mechanisms for CTE reduction are not explained at the microstructural level.

3. **Model interpretability limitation:** “The SVR model has inherent drawbacks, such as poor interpretability regarding the effect of each element on the CTE of Ni-based superalloys.”

4. **Composition representation:** Compositions are given only in wt.%; at.% values are not provided.

5. **Processing details absent:** No heat treatment, homogenization, or post-casting processing details are provided.

6. **Mechanical property data limited:** Only one alloy (Alloy 3-4) was mechanically tested; only 900 °C tensile properties are reported. Room-temperature properties, creep, hardness, and other properties are not reported.

7. **Oxidation resistance not tested:** No oxidation tests were performed on the discovered alloys. The trade-off between Cr addition (for oxidation resistance) and CTE is identified but not experimentally characterized.

8. **Impurity content not reported:** O, N, H levels and ppm-level impurities (other than C) are not reported.

9. **CTE testing details:** CTE was measured using DIL 402 EXpedis (dilatometry), but specific heating rates, atmosphere, and exact measurement protocol details are not provided in the main text.

10. **Applicability to refractory alloy systems:** The Yamamoto formula (Equation 5) shows W and Mo reduce CTE in Ni-based alloys; whether similar effects would apply in refractory alloy systems is not addressed in this paper and would require separate investigation.
