# Paper Metadata
- **Title**: Molybdenum and tungsten manufactured by selective laser melting: Analysis of defect structure and solidification mechanisms
- **Authors**: J. Braun, L. Kaserer, J. Stajkovic, K.-H. Leitz, B. Tabernig, P. Singer, P. Leibenguth, C. Gspan, H. Kestler, G. Leichtfried
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals & Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2019
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2019.104999
- **Alloy System Studied**: Commercially pure molybdenum and tungsten processed by Selective Laser Melting (SLM)
- **Research Objective**: To investigate the impact of oxygen content in powders, process atmosphere, and substrate plate temperature on the structural and mechanical properties of SLM-processed molybdenum and tungsten, and to clarify the defect formation mechanisms (cracking and residual porosity)
- **Material Category**: Additively manufactured refractory metals (commercially pure Mo and W)

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Material System**: Commercially pure molybdenum and tungsten (not alloys in the strict sense; metallic purity >99.9%)
- **Research Focus**: Defect structure (cracks, porosity), solidification mechanisms, oxygen-induced embrittlement, and hot/cold cracking in SLM-processed pure refractory metals
- **Application Context**: To establish the baseline for understanding oxygen-induced defects in additively manufactured Mo and W; to determine whether substrate plate heating to 1000 °C can suppress cracking

# Processing and Heat Treatment
## Selective Laser Melting Parameters
- **Equipment**: Customized lab-scale SLM machine with 400 W continuous Nd:YAG fiber laser
- **Line energy input (E_L)**: 0.67 J/mm (where E_L = P/v; P = laser power, v = scanning speed)
- **Hatch distance**: 100 μm
- **Layer thickness**: 30 μm
- **Scanning rotation between adjacent layers**: 90° or 67°
- **Process atmosphere**: Argon with oxygen content varied; <20 ppmv (low-oxygen experiments) up to 800 ppmv (high-oxygen experiments)
- **Substrate plate temperature**: No heating (for 90° rotation experiments); 1000 °C (for 67° rotation experiments with low oxygen)
- **Specimen dimensions**: 10 × 10 × 10 mm (constant for all experiments)

## Heat Treatment
- Not explicitly stated in the original paper. The work focuses on the as-built state of SLM material.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
## Matrix Phase
- Columnar grain structure (BCC matrix for both Mo and W) — not explicitly stated as "BCC" but implied by the body-centered cubic crystal structure of pure molybdenum and tungsten.

## Grain Structure
- Columnar grains form along the building direction due to epitaxial growth along the thermal gradient.
- No preferred grain orientation observed in EBSD maps.
- Grain boundaries at melt track borders are predominantly high-angle grain boundaries.
- Only few small-angle boundaries (5–15°) are visible; boundaries between 2–5° not visible in [111] orientation; misorientations within grains below 2° cause color shifts in inverse pole figure maps.

## Second Phases / Precipitates
- **Oxygen segregation at grain boundaries**: 6–7 nm thick layer of segregated oxygen found at high-angle grain boundaries (TEM/EDX).
- **Molybdenum oxide (MoO₂)** and **tungsten oxide (WO₂)** precipitates at grain boundaries, up to several hundred nanometers in size; precipitates form in different morphologies depending on local concentration (eutectic reaction between Mo and MoO₂, W and WO₂).
- **Carbides**: Carbon-containing precipitates up to 50 nm in size detected within grains and at small-angle grain boundaries (STEM + EDX); these carbides pin dislocations.

## Defects
- **Crack network**: Dense, regularly spaced three-dimensional crack network along columnar grain boundaries; cracks are intergranular; reflect the scanning strategy; spacing corresponds to hatch distance transverse to build direction.
- **Two types of porosity**: (1) large pores, often filled with unmolten and/or partially molten powder particles, oriented parallel to substrate plate and extending over several layers, attributed to coating defects; (2) smaller, roundly shaped unfilled pores, attributed to melt pool convection and coagulation of pores formed by outgassing of low-melting oxygen compounds and argon entrapment.
- Crack network already present in the as-built state (confirmed by X-ray microscopy before metallographic preparation).

## Strengthening / Embrittlement Mechanisms Discussed
- The paper does not discuss strengthening mechanisms in the conventional sense. Instead, it focuses on embrittlement mechanisms:
  - **Grain-boundary oxygen segregation embrittlement**: oxygen segregates at high-angle grain boundaries, weakening grain-boundary strength and raising the Ductile-to-Brittle Transition Temperature (DBTT).
  - **Hot cracking**: oxygen enriches in residual liquid during solidification; eutectic liquid (Mo-MoO₂, W-WO₂) forms films between grains with a lower melting point than the matrix, causing hot tearing.
  - **Cold cracking**: thermal stresses during rapid cooling exceed the strength of oxygen-weakened grain boundaries below the DBTT.
  - **Dislocation pinning by fine carbides**: fine carbides (≤50 nm) effectively pin dislocations, restricting plastic deformation and thereby increasing DBTT.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability
## Oxidation Behavior
- Molybdenum and tungsten do not form a passivating layer and continuously pick up oxygen when stored under air or in contact with moisture.
- Oxygen uptake during powder storage: Mo powder oxygen increased from 535 to 1216 μg/g after 6 months in plastic containers.
- Oxygen contamination is retained in the built part (Mo built part contained 1150 μg/g O vs. 1216 μg/g in powder, a decrease of <5%).

## Oxidation Layer and Oxide Formation During Processing
- Oxygen segregates at grain boundaries as molybdenum oxide (MoO₂) and tungsten oxide (WO₂) through eutectic solidification between Mo/W and their respective dioxides.
- MoO₂ and WO₂ precipitates at grain boundaries range up to several hundred nanometers.
- Oxygen has negligible solid solubility in both Mo and W at ambient temperature; it continuously enriches in the residual liquid during primary α-Mo solidification and solidifies by eutectic transformation.

## Effects of Oxygen on Processing
- High oxygen in powder or process atmosphere increases spatter formation, alters Marangoni convection (from inward centrifugal to outward centripetal flow), and initiates pore nucleation, stabilization, and growth (cited from Leung et al. [14] for Invar 36, transferred to Mo/W due to similar oxide behavior).
- Oxygen impurities lead to severe balling during SLM of tungsten (cited from Zhou et al. [6]).
- Increased oxygen content in the process atmosphere (200 ppmv vs. <50 ppmv) reduced achievable density from 97.1% to 94.4% (cited from Iveković et al. [11]).

## High-Temperature Service Embrittlement Issues
- **Oxygen embrittlement**: Segregated oxygen at grain boundaries raises DBTT and causes intergranular brittle fracture.
- **DBTT elevation**: Oxygen segregation at grain boundaries increases DBTT above 400 °C (per Iveković et al. [11]); the present authors find that even 1000 °C substrate heating does not prevent cracking in W with 227 μg/g oxygen powder, indicating the DBTT of the oxygen-containing grain boundaries exceeds the building temperature or that hot cracking is the dominant mechanism.
- **Hot cracking**: Eutectic liquid films (Mo-MoO₂, W-WO₂) at grain boundaries cause hot tearing during solidification, independent of DBTT.
- **Carbon-related dislocation pinning**: Fine carbides (≤50 nm) pin dislocations, increase DBTT, and contribute to brittle behavior.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Oxygen (O)]
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Commercially pure molybdenum and tungsten processed by SLM; also relevant to AM of other refractory metals.
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: Powders: 227–1216 μg/g; process atmosphere: <20 ppmv to 800 ppmv; built material: up to 1150 μg/g retained oxygen.
- **[Main Function]**: Detrimental impurity. Causes grain-boundary embrittlement, elevates DBTT, induces hot cracking via eutectic liquid film formation, alters Marangoni convection and wetting, and promotes porosity.
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Oxygen has negligible solid solubility in Mo and W; it segregates at high-angle grain boundaries (6–7 nm thick layer). During solidification, oxygen enriches in residual liquid and forms MoO₂/WO₂ eutectics with lower melting points than the matrix, leading to hot cracking. At grain boundaries, oxygen segregation weakens intergranular cohesion and raises DBTT. Oxygen also affects melt pool behavior (spatter, balling, Marangoni flow alteration, pore nucleation).
- **[Property Effect]**: SLM Mo with 1216 μg/g powder oxygen and 800 ppmv atmosphere oxygen: transverse tensile strength 50 MPa, longitudinal tensile strength 350 MPa, no plasticity, intergranular fracture. Maximum relative densities of 96.0% (Mo) and 97.5% (W) with 800 ppmv oxygen. Increasing process atmosphere oxygen from <50 to 200 ppmv reduced W density from 97.1% to 94.4% (cited from Iveković et al.). Oxygen in the built part remains nearly identical to powder oxygen (decrease <5%).
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: Hot cracking, cold cracking, grain-boundary embrittlement, elevated DBTT, porosity, balling, spatter, reduced densification, loss of ductility, intergranular fracture.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Table 1; Figs. 2–10; Section 3 Experimental results; Section 4 Discussion of defect formation mechanisms; Section 5 Conclusions: "Oxygen impurities in the powder and the process atmosphere lead to an increased oxygen content in the material processed by SLM. The oxygen is segregated as molybdenum or tungsten oxide at the grain boundaries leading to hot or cold cracks."

## [Carbon (C)]
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Commercially pure molybdenum (SLM); small amounts present in powder as impurity (18–34 μg/g in Mo powder).
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: 18–34 μg/g in Mo powder; precipitates up to 50 nm in size detected in the built material.
- **[Main Function]**: Forms fine carbide precipitates within grains and at small-angle grain boundaries; pins dislocations; increases DBTT by restricting dislocation mobility.
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Under high SLM cooling rates, carbon-containing precipitates ≤50 nm form; they are smaller than carbides typically found in sintered refractory metals. These precipitates effectively pin dislocations, preventing plastic deformation around crack tips and raising DBTT [28].
- **[Property Effect]**: Not reported as isolated effect. The authors note that the fine carbides increase DBTT and contribute to crack sensitivity. No specific numerical values for the carbon effect alone are provided.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: Increased DBTT, reduced ductility, restricted plastic deformation at crack tips.
- **[Original Evidence]**: Fig. 9; Section 3 (last paragraph): "As established in literature [17–27] this segregation of oxygen embrittles the grain boundary and thus raises DBTT. Within the grains and at small angle grain boundaries carbon containing precipitates could be detected... They effectively pin the large number of dislocations [28]. By restricting the mobility of the dislocations DBTT is increased [28]."

## [Hydrogen (H) and Nitrogen (N)]
- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Mo and W SLM powders.
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: Mo as-spheroidized: H 6 μg/g, N <5 μg/g; Mo stored: H 18 μg/g, N 50 μg/g; W as-spheroidized: H 2 μg/g, N <5 μg/g.
- **[Main Function]**: Impurities that potentially contribute to embrittlement and defect formation (not elaborated in detail in this paper).
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: Not discussed in detail for H and N. Cited as impurities that increase DBTT in tungsten (reference [11]).
- **[Property Effect]**: The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the effects of H and N alone.
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: Potential contribution to grain-boundary embrittlement and DBTT increase (referenced but not directly demonstrated in this work).
- **[Original Evidence]**: Table 1; Introduction (reference to Iveković et al.): "presence of impurities, such as oxygen, nitrogen or carbon, segregated at the grain boundaries, increases the DBTT above 400 °C."

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

The present paper is not a strengthening-mechanism study. It focuses on defect formation and embrittlement mechanisms in SLM-processed pure Mo and W. The following cards are derived from the mechanisms discussed in the paper.

## [Grain-Boundary Oxygen Segregation Embrittlement]
- **[Related Elements]**: Oxygen (O)
- **[Typical Alloys]**: Commercially pure Mo and W processed by SLM; also conventional powder-metallurgical Mo and W.
- **[Key Compositions]**: Powder oxygen 227–1216 μg/g; process atmosphere oxygen <20–800 ppmv; built material oxygen up to 1150 μg/g.
- **[Microstructural Changes]**: 6–7 nm thick oxygen segregation layer at high-angle grain boundaries; MoO₂/WO₂ precipitates up to several hundred nanometers at grain boundaries; intergranular fracture surfaces revealing prominent grain-boundary precipitates.
- **[Property Improvements]**: None. This is a degradation mechanism.
- **[Property Degradations]**: Transverse tensile strength of SLM Mo drops to 50 MPa (vs. expected strengths of dense, crack-free Mo); longitudinal strength 350 MPa but still without plasticity; intergranular brittle fracture; DBTT raised above 400 °C (per literature cited); cannot achieve crack-free, fully dense parts even with 1000 °C substrate heating.
- **[Risks and Limitations]**: Oxygen segregation cannot be eliminated by process parameter optimization alone; must be controlled by powder handling under inert atmosphere or alloying (e.g., carbon addition per Kaserer et al. [30]).

## [Eutectic Oxide-Induced Hot Cracking]
- **[Related Elements]**: Oxygen (O), molybdenum (Mo), tungsten (W)
- **[Typical Alloys]**: Pure Mo and W in SLM; welding of refractory metals.
- **[Key Compositions]**: Mo with 535–1216 μg/g O; W with 227 μg/g O; atmosphere O 800 ppmv.
- **[Microstructural Changes]**: During solidification, oxygen enriches in residual liquid; Mo-MoO₂ / W-WO₂ eutectic liquid films form between primary α-Mo/W grains; films break up by Plateau-Rayleigh instability into droplets/nanopores along grain boundaries; MoO₂/WO₂ precipitates with varying morphologies according to local concentration ratio.
- **[Property Improvements]**: None.
- **[Property Degradations]**: Hot cracks along planar solidified grain boundaries, creating a three-dimensional network; porosity; reduced tensile strength (Mo transverse: 50 MPa, longitudinal: 350 MPa).
- **[Risks and Limitations]**: Hot cracking occurs during solidification and cannot be avoided by reducing thermal stress alone (e.g., by substrate heating); requires oxygen elimination or alloying.

## [Carbide Dislocation Pinning and DBTT Increase]
- **[Related Elements]**: Carbon (C)
- **[Typical Alloys]**: Pure Mo processed by SLM.
- **[Key Compositions]**: Mo powder with 18–34 μg/g C; carbide precipitates ≤50 nm.
- **[Microstructural Changes]**: Fine carbon-containing precipitates (≤50 nm) within grains and at small-angle grain boundaries; they pin dislocations and restrict mobility.
- **[Property Improvements]**: None in this context. The authors suggest that a high dislocation density and fine grain structure due to fast cooling could, in principle, improve DBTT, but the carbide pinning counteracts any benefit.
- **[Property Degradations]**: DBTT increased; triaxial stresses at crack tips cannot be relieved by plastic deformation.
- **[Risks and Limitations]**: Carbide precipitation is a secondary impurity effect that interacts with the primary oxygen-driven embrittlement, further reducing damage tolerance.

## [Substrate Plate Heating]
- **[Related Elements]**: None (processing parameter).
- **[Typical Alloys]**: W (pure) processed by SLM.
- **[Key Processing Parameters]**: Substrate temperature 1000 °C; atmosphere oxygen <20 ppmv; powder oxygen 227 μg/g; layer rotation 67°.
- **[Microstructural Changes]**: Specific crack length reduced; no continuous crack network; cracks still intergranular and extend up to 400 μm along columnar grains; no large powder-filled pores observed; remaining porosity mainly from widened cracks.
- **[Property Improvements]**: Relative density remains 97.5% (same as with 800 ppmv oxygen, unheated). Crack spacing wider; no continuous network.
- **[Property Degradations]**: Cracks still present; density not improved beyond 97.5%; still no crack-free, fully dense material.
- **[Risks and Limitations]**: Heating to 1000 °C does not prevent cracking caused by oxygen segregation; the detrimental effect of oxygen on solidification and grain-boundary strength must be eliminated by other means (e.g., alloying).

# Key Conclusions
1. **Oxygen is the primary cause of defects**: Impurities, mainly oxygen, in the powder and process atmosphere are identified as the cause of the predominant defect structure (cracks and residual porosity) in SLM-processed molybdenum and tungsten.
2. **Hot cracking mechanism**: During SLM, oxygen in the form of molybdenum/tungsten oxide segregates at grain boundaries; the lower melting point of the Mo-MoO₂/W-WO₂ eutectic relative to the matrix induces hot cracking. When the eutectic liquid volume is low, the liquid film breaks up by Plateau-Rayleigh instability and coagulates into droplets/nanopores along grain boundaries.
3. **Cold cracking mechanism**: Oxygen impurities weaken grain boundaries, increasing the risk for cold cracking and raising DBTT. During rapid cooling from melting point, thermal stresses exceed the strength of weakened grain boundaries, producing cold cracks.
4. **Crack network formation**: The combination of hot cracking at planar solidified grain boundaries and cold cracking along weakened grain boundaries during cooling creates the crack network generally found in SLM Mo and W.
5. **Substrate heating insufficient**: A substrate plate temperature of 1000 °C does not prevent the formation of cracks in tungsten caused by oxygen segregation, even with low process atmosphere oxygen (<20 ppmv) and a 67° layer rotation.
6. **Powder storage effects**: Storing plasma-spheroidized Mo powder for 6 months in air increased oxygen from 535 to 1216 μg/g and led to extremely low tensile strength (50 MPa transverse, 350 MPa longitudinal) with no plasticity.
7. **Oxygen transfer to built material**: Oxygen content in built parts is nearly identical to powder oxygen (decrease <5%), indicating that SLM does not remove oxygen impurities.
8. **Marangoni and balling effects**: Oxygen contamination alters Marangoni convection, increases spatter and balling, and initiates pore nucleation, which is transferable from studies on other alloy systems to Mo and W.
9. **Need for alloying**: To achieve fully dense, crack-free SLM Mo and W, oxygen must be eliminated or controlled by alloying (e.g., carbon addition as demonstrated in Kaserer et al. [30]). Process parameter optimization alone is insufficient.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Compositional limitations**: The study addresses only commercially pure molybdenum and tungsten (purity >99.9%); no alloys or intentional additions are investigated.
- **Mechanical property data limited**: Comprehensive tensile data are reported only for SLM Mo produced from stored powder (1216 μg/g O). No high-temperature mechanical properties, hardness, fracture toughness, or creep data are reported.
- **No quantitative DBTT measurements**: The paper relies on literature values for DBTT behavior; no direct DBTT measurements are performed on SLM specimens.
- **Density reproducibility**: Although densities up to 96–97.5% are reported, no higher densities were achieved; the findings indicate that oxygen is the limiting factor, but the paper does not demonstrate a direct route to higher density.
- **Microstructural characterization is primarily qualitative**: Crack networks, grain structures, and precipitates are characterized qualitatively; no quantitative precipitate size distributions or grain size measurements are given.
- **Atmosphere oxygen as a process variable**: Only two atmosphere conditions (800 ppmv and <20 ppmv) are systematically compared, and the 800 ppmv experiments were performed without substrate heating, while the <20 ppmv was performed at 1000 °C, making some comparisons confounded (noted explicitly by the authors when citing Iveković et al.).
- **No thermodynamic modeling**: The eutectic solidification and oxide formation are discussed based on microstructural evidence and literature but not supported by computational thermodynamics in this paper.
- **Scanning strategy interaction**: The paper discusses 90° and 67° rotation strategies, but the primary findings focus on the 90° strategy for defects and the 67° strategy for the heated-substrate experiment; the effect of rotation angle is not isolated from oxygen effects.
- **Element-function knowledge is inferred**: The roles of H and N are not thoroughly investigated; their possible contributions to embrittlement are mentioned but not demonstrated. The paper's reference to carbide effects is based on a single observation in Mo.
- **Applicability**: The findings are directly applicable to SLM (and to some extent laser powder-bed fusion in general) of pure W and Mo, and provide guidance for future alloy development (e.g., carbon addition) to mitigate oxygen-induced defects. They do not directly apply to non-laser AM processes such as EBM or DED, nor to alloyed refractory systems, which may respond differently to oxygen and thermal gradients.
