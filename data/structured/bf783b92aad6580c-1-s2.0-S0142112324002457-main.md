# Key Conclusions

(Adapted from Section 4 of the paper)

1. **Surface quality**: At 20 °C, surface quality improves with higher cutting speeds but declines with increased cutting depths. At -120 °C, the same trends persist, with surface quality being better than at 20 °C for cutting speeds of 2000–2800 mm/min and depths of 0.6–1.4 mm.

2. **Tensile properties**: Yield and tensile strengths are slightly higher when machined at -120 °C. At 2000 mm/min, these strengths increased by 6.2 % and 10.4 %, respectively, compared to 20 °C, with further increases at 2800 mm/min. Strengths are higher at lower cutting depths, with the best results at -120 °C and 0.6 mm depth showing 1.16× and 1.4× increases over room temperature.

3. **Fatigue life**: The number of fatigue cycles rises with cutting speed in both thermal conditions. At 2800 mm/min, fatigue life is 10.65× higher at 20 °C and 8.86× higher at -120 °C compared to 2000 mm/min. Fatigue life decreases with deeper cuts but is significantly better at -120 °C than at 20 °C, with a notable 51.6 % improvement at 0.6 mm depth.

4. **Optimal machining condition**: Higher cutting speeds and shallower cutting depths under low-temperature (-120 °C) conditions achieve optimal machining performance.
