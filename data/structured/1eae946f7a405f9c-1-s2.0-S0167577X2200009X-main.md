# Paper Metadata
- **Title:** Mutation in TCP phases and superior stress rupture life led by W/Mo ratio in Ni-based single crystal superalloys
- **Authors:** Jinbin Chen, Jingyang Chen, Qinjia Wang, Yidong Wu, Qing Li, Chengbo Xiao, Xidong Hui
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.matlet.2022.131656
- **Alloy system studied:** Ni-based single crystal superalloys
- **Research objective:** To investigate the effect of W/Mo ratio on TCP phase formation, microstructure stability, and stress rupture life in second-generation Ni-based single crystal superalloys
- **Material category:** Ni-based single crystal superalloy (Note: This paper is about Ni-based superalloys, not refractory high-entropy alloys, Nb-W-based alloys, or Mo-W-based alloys. However, it contains relevant information on refractory elements W, Mo, Re, and Ta in superalloy systems.)

# Alloy System and Research Object

The paper studies two second-generation Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys denoted as NS6 and NS8, which have similar nominal compositions and equal total content of W+Mo, but different W/Mo ratios. The research focuses on how the W/Mo ratio influences TCP (topologically close-packed) phase precipitation (μ phase vs. P phase), microstructure evolution during long-term isothermal exposure at 950 °C, and the resulting stress rupture life.

**Research objective (from the abstract):** "Balancing the strengthening effect and microstructure instability led by refractory elements is crucial for superalloys. In this work, comparative study was conducted on two second-generation Ni-based single crystal superalloys which have similar components and total content of W + Mo except for different W/Mo ratios."

# Alloy Composition

Two Ni-based single crystal superalloys, NS6 and NS8, were investigated. Both have similar nominal compositions (wt.%): balanced Ni, 8Co, 6Cr, 6Ta, 5.6Al, 3Re, minor Hf, B, and C, with different W and Mo contents.

| Ta | 6 | 6 |
| Al | 5.6 | 5.6 |
| Re | 3 | 3 |

**Key relationships:**
- NS6: W/Mo ratio = 6.5/1.5 ≈ 4.33 (high W/Mo ratio)
- NS8: W/Mo ratio = 5.5/2.5 = 2.2 (low W/Mo ratio)
- W/Mo ratio of NS8 is half of that in NS6
- Both alloys have equal total W+Mo content: NS6: 6.5 + 1.5 = 8 wt.%; NS8: 5.5 + 2.5 = 8 wt.%

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Single crystal preparation:**
- The SX bars were prepared by high-rate solidification (HRS) process.

**Heat treatment:**
- NS6: Solution treatment at 1290 °C/4 h
- NS8: Solution treatment at 1280 °C/4 h
- Both alloys: 1120 °C/4 h and 900 °C/16 h (aging treatment)

**Isothermal exposure:**
- Temperature: 950 °C
- Durations: 500 h, 1500 h, 5000 h, and 10,000 h

**Stress rupture testing:**
- Specimens machined from alloys subjected to 10,000 h of isothermal exposure
- Plate-type specimens
- Conditions: 950 °C and 250 MPa

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Initial heat-treated state:**
- Matrix phase: γ (FCC matrix) + γ′ precipitates (Ni3Al-based phase)
- γ′ volume fraction: NS6: 63.3 ± 1.1%; NS8: 63.6 ± 1.2% (similar initial state)
- γ′ precipitate size: NS6: 380 ± 54 nm; NS8: 391 ± 49 nm

**Microstructure evolution during isothermal exposure at 950 °C:**
- After 500 h: γ′ precipitates coarsened in both alloys
- After 1,500 h: A fraction of γ′ precipitates coalesced; needle-like phase appeared in NS6 (high W/Mo ratio alloy); γ′ precipitates deteriorated more seriously in NS6
- After 5,000 h: Needle-like phases in NS6 became longer and increased in number; tiny granular particles emerged (less than 100 nm) in NS8 (low W/Mo ratio alloy)
- After 10,000 h: γ′ precipitates in both alloys seriously coarsened and coalesced; large amount of needle-like phase in NS6; small granular particles slightly increased in amount and size in NS8

**TCP phase identification:**
- NS6 (high W/Mo ratio): Needle-like μ phase precipitated after isothermal exposure for 1,500 h at 950 °C
- NS8 (low W/Mo ratio): Only sub-micron P phase particles formed even after 10,000 h of isothermal exposure
- Both μ phase (NS6) and P phase (NS8) are enriched in W, Mo, Re, and Cr
- The needle-like μ phase in NS6 is Co-rich; the granular P phase in NS8 is not Co-rich
- SAED patterns confirmed: needle-like precipitate = μ phase; granular particle = P phase
- The P phase in NS8 remained sub-micro scale even after 10,000 h, indicating significant kinetic stability

**Element segregation (Table 1):**
- W segregation ratio (R): NS6: 1.30; NS8: 1.22 (more severe in NS6)
- Re segregation ratio (R): NS6: 1.86; NS8: 1.62 (more severe in NS6)
- Other elements showed similar R values in both alloys
- Despite higher solution treatment temperature for NS6 (1290 °C vs 1280 °C), segregation of W and Re was still more severe in NS6

**Partitioning behavior (γ/γ′ partitioning coefficients, K_γ/γ′):**
- Heat-treated state: K_γ/γ′ of Cr, Co, Mo, and W in NS6 slightly lower than in NS8; K_γ/γ′ of Al, Ta, and Re similar in both alloys
- After 10,000 h exposure: K_γ/γ′ of Al and Ta in NS6 lower than in NS8 (Al and Ta more enriched in γ′ phase of NS6); K_γ/γ′ of Re, Mo, Cr, and Co higher in NS6 than in NS8; especially Re and Cr are notably higher
- In NS8, partitioning coefficients are closer to unity, indicating more even element distribution in γ and γ′ phases
- γ′ phase of NS8 accommodates more Re, W, Mo, and Cr, resulting in the γ matrix being less oversaturated and less inclined to form TCP phases

# Mechanical Properties

**Stress rupture test conditions:**
- Temperature: 950 °C
- Stress: 250 MPa
- Specimen type: Plate-type
- Specimens machined from alloys subjected to 10,000 h of isothermal exposure at 950 °C

**Stress rupture life results:**
- NS6 (high W/Mo ratio, μ phase precipitated): 243 h
- NS8 (low W/Mo ratio, P phase formed): 446 h
- Improvement: NS8 stress rupture life increased by approximately 80% compared with NS6 (446/243 ≈ 1.84, or 84% increase)

**Other mechanical properties (room temperature yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, elongation, etc.):** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

The paper does not discuss oxidation weight gain, oxidation layer types, or the effects of alloying elements on oxidation resistance in detail. The primary service stability concern addressed is:
- Microstructure instability due to TCP phase formation (μ phase vs. P phase) during long-term isothermal exposure at 950 °C
- The high W/Mo ratio alloy (NS6) precipitated μ phase after only 1,500 h at 950 °C
- The low W/Mo ratio alloy (NS8) exhibited superior microstructure stability, with μ phase completely suppressed and only sub-micron P phase particles formed after 10,000 h

The paper does not discuss high-temperature embrittlement, oxygen embrittlement, nitrogen embrittlement, or grain-boundary oxidation explicitly.

## [W]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Ni-based single crystal superalloys (second-generation)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
NS6: 6.5 wt.%; NS8: 5.5 wt.%

**[Main Function]**
Solid-solution strengthening (SSS) in Ni-based superalloys.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- W segregates to the dendrite core during solidification and is difficult to homogenize due to low diffusivity.
- W is enriched in TCP phases (μ phase and P phase) along with Mo, Re, and Cr.
- W participates in μ phase formation in high W/Mo ratio alloys.

**[Property Effect]**
- In combination with Re, W segregation in the dendrite core contributes to thermodynamic instability, leading to μ phase precipitation.
- The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for W solid-solution strengthening contribution separate from other elements.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Enhances dendrite segregation, leading to inhomogeneous distribution and reduced thermodynamic stability of the dendrite core.
- Contributes to TCP phase (μ phase) formation when present in higher proportions relative to Mo, which can lead to depletion of solid solution strengthening elements in the γ matrix and destruction of coherent γ/γ′ interface, impairing service performance and causing premature failure.

**[Original Evidence]**
- "W and Re segregate to the dendrite core during solidification and are difficult to homogenize due to low diffusivity [5]."
- From Table 1: W segregation ratio R = 1.30 for NS6 (6.5 wt.% W) compared to 1.22 for NS8 (5.5 wt.% W), indicating more severe W segregation in high W/Mo ratio alloy despite higher solution treatment temperature.
- "Precipitates in both alloys are enriched in W, Mo, Re, and Cr" (from HAADF-EDS analysis, Fig. 2).

## [Re]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Ni-based single crystal superalloys (second-generation)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
Both alloys: 3 wt.% Re

**[Main Function]**
Solid-solution strengthening and high-temperature strength enhancement.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**

## [Mo]

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Ni-based single crystal superalloys (second-generation)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
NS6: 1.5 wt.%; NS8: 2.5 wt.%

**[Main Function]**
Solid-solution strengthening in Ni-based superalloys. According to the cited reference (Fleischmann et al.), Mo provides stronger SSS effect than W of the same mass fraction in SX alloys.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Mo is enriched in TCP phases (μ phase and P phase) along with W, Re, and Cr.
- Substituting W with Mo (i.e., lower W/Mo ratio) relieves the segregation of W and Re and results in more Re in γ phase (from cited reference [7,8]).
- Mo stabilizes P phase instead of μ phase (from cited reference [11]; confirmed in current results).

**[Property Effect]**
- The original paper reports that in the low W/Mo ratio alloy (NS8 with 2.5 wt.% Mo), only P phase formed after 10,000 h at 950 °C, while μ phase formed in high W/Mo ratio alloy (NS6 with 1.5 wt.% Mo) after 1,500 h.
- No specific numerical values for Mo solid-solution strengthening contribution are provided in this paper.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- According to cited reference [9] (Ma et al.), Mo may have the strongest potential for μ phase formation among refractory elements: "Mo had the strongest potential on the formation of μ phase, followed by Re, W and Cr."
- According to cited reference [10] (Pan et al.), increasing Mo/W ratio reduced the activation energy of γ′ precipitate coarsening and led to microstructure instability.

**[Original Evidence]**
- "Fleischmann et al. [6] found that Mo provided stronger SSS effect than W of the same mass fraction in SX alloys."
- "Increasing Mo/W ratio relieved the segregation of W and Re and resulted in more Re in γ phase in SX alloys [7,8]."
- "Rae et al. [11] found that the replacement of W by Mo stabilized P phase, which coincides with the current result."

# Key Conclusions

1. **TCP phase type depends on W/Mo ratio:** In the high W/Mo ratio alloy (NS6, W/Mo = 4.33), needle-like μ phase precipitated after isothermal exposure for 1,500 h at 950 °C. In the low W/Mo ratio alloy (NS8, W/Mo = 2.2), μ phase was completely suppressed and only sub-micron P phase particles formed even after 10,000 h of isothermal exposure.

2. **P phase shows extraordinary kinetic stability:** The P phase in NS8 remained sub-micro scale even after 10,000 h of isothermal exposure at 950 °C.

3. **W/Mo ratio affects dendrite segregation:** The segregation of W and Re in NS6 (high W/Mo) is more severe than in NS8 (low W/Mo), despite the higher solution treatment temperature used for NS6. The substitution of W with Mo is beneficial for the homogenization of Re and W.

4. **W/Mo ratio affects element partitioning between γ and γ′ phases:** In NS6, after long-term exposure, the partitioning coefficients of Re, Mo, Cr, and Co (especially Re and Cr) are higher, leading to a more oversaturated γ matrix prone to TCP phase formation. In NS8, the partitioning coefficients are closer to unity, meaning the element distribution is more even between γ and γ′ phases.

5. **Low W/Mo ratio alloy shows superior stress rupture life:** After 10,000 h of isothermal exposure, NS8 alloy exhibited a stress rupture life of 446 h versus 243 h for NS6 at 950 °C and 250 MPa. The stress rupture life of NS8 increased by about 80% compared with NS6.

6. **Mechanism of performance improvement:** The mutation in TCP phase from μ to P (by tuning W/Mo ratio) prevents the depletion of solid solution strengthening elements from γ matrix and preserves the coherent γ/γ′ interface, thereby improving service performance.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Alloy system limitation:** This study is specifically about second-generation Ni-based single crystal superalloys, not about Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory high-entropy alloys.

2. **Composition range limitation:** The findings are based on two alloys with 3 wt.% Re, 6 wt.% Ta, 5.6 wt.% Al, 6 wt.% Cr, 8 wt.% Co, and total W+Mo = 8 wt.%. The conclusions may not be directly applicable to alloys with significantly different compositions.

3. **Temperature limitation:** The isothermal exposure and stress rupture tests were only conducted at 950 °C. The behavior at other temperatures (e.g., 800 °C, 1000 °C, 1200 °C) is not addressed in the paper.

4. **Stress level limitation:** Only one stress level (250 MPa) was tested for stress rupture life.

5. **Mechanical property data limited:** The paper does not report room-temperature strength, high-temperature strength, elongation, ductility, or other mechanical properties beyond stress rupture life at one condition.

6. **Cited conflicts on Mo effect:** The paper notes uncertainty in the literature regarding Mo's effect on TCP phase formation. While the current results show that low W/Mo ratio suppresses μ phase formation (favoring P phase), some cited references suggest Mo may have strong potential for μ phase formation, and increasing Mo/W ratio may reduce γ′ precipitate coarsening activation energy.

7. **Long-term exposure condition:** The maximum exposure time studied was 10,000 h, and the P phase in NS8 remained stable throughout this period. Longer-term stability beyond 10,000 h is not verified.

8. **The paper does not address oxidation resistance or environmental degradation:** The study focuses on microstructure stability and creep-related stress rupture life only.
