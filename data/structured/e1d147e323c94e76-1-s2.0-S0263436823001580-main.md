# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Effect of different crystal forms of ZrO₂ on the microstructure and properties of tungsten alloys
- **Authors**: Yunchao Zhao, Liujie Xu, Zhou Li, Mingyi Guo, Changji Wang, Xiuqing Li, Shizhong Wei
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106258
- **Alloy System Studied**: Tungsten alloys strengthened with different crystal forms of zirconia (m-ZrO₂, t-ZrO₂, c-ZrO₂)
- **Research Objective**: To investigate the effect of different crystal forms of zirconia (monoclinic, tetragonal, cubic) on the microstructure, compressive properties, tensile properties, and hot processing behavior of tungsten alloys, compared with pure tungsten.
- **Material Category**: W-based alloy (oxide dispersion-strengthened tungsten alloy)

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Base material**: Pure tungsten (PW) with particle size of approximately 919 nm, purity ~99.95%
- **Reinforcement**: ZrO₂ in three crystal forms:
  - m-ZrO₂ (monoclinic), purity ~99.90%, particle size ~30 nm
  - t-ZrO₂ (tetragonal), purity ~94.70%, stabilized with 3 mol% Y₂O₃, particle size ~30 nm
  - c-ZrO₂ (cubic), purity ~86.50%, stabilized with 8 mol% Y₂O₃, particle size ~30 nm
- **Objective**: Compare the effect of crystal form (rather than content) of ZrO₂ on tungsten alloy properties

# Alloy Composition

Four materials were studied:

1. **PW**: Pure tungsten (99.95% purity, ~919 nm particle size)
2. **WM1**: W-1.0 wt% m-ZrO₂ (monoclinic zirconia, ~99.90% pure)
3. **WT1**: W-1.0 wt% t-ZrO₂ (tetragonal zirconia stabilized with 3 mol% Y₂O₃, ~94.70% pure)
4. **WC1**: W-1.0 wt% c-ZrO₂ (cubic zirconia stabilized with 8 mol% Y₂O₃, ~86.50% pure)

**Note**: The mass fraction of the three different crystal forms of zirconia is 1% (1.0 wt%) in all cases.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Powder mixing**: Pure tungsten powder and zirconia powders (different crystal forms) were fully mixed with a multidimensional mixer for 20 hours.
- **Cold isostatic pressing**: Mixed powder was loaded into rubber grinding tool, pressed at 180 MPa for 30 min, held at 180 MPa for 3 min.
- **Sintering**: Pressed billets sintered in a medium frequency induction sintering furnace at 2300 °C in hydrogen atmosphere for 5 hours.
- **Rolling**: Sintered samples were rolled into 2 mm plates by multiple passes, total deformation of 92%.
- **Sample preparation**: Sintered samples were wire cut into φ6 × 10 mm (room temperature compression) and φ8 × 12 mm (high temperature compression) cylindrical specimens, polished before testing.
- **Heat treatment after rolling**: Not explicitly described in the original paper (rolling was the final processing step before tensile testing).

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## As-Sintered Microstructure

- **Grain size of PW**: ~15 μm
- **Grain size of WT1 and WC1**: ~5 μm (reduced by 61.6% and 61.1% compared with PW, respectively)
- **Grain size of WM1**: Previously reported [8], similar to WT1 and WC1
- **ZrO₂ distribution**: Uniformly dispersed in the boundaries and inside of tungsten grains
- **Grain orientation**: Sintered tungsten alloy doped with t-ZrO₂ and c-ZrO₂ shows no preferential growth (isotropic)
- **Phase constitution**: W matrix with dispersed ZrO₂ second phase particles

## Rolled Plate Microstructure

- **Fibrous structure**: Both PW and WM1 plates show fibrous structure after 92% deformation
- **Fiber width of PW**: ~4 μm
- **Fiber width of WM1**: ~2 μm (zirconia pinned grain boundaries to refine grains during rolling)
- **Shear bands in PW**: Periodic shear bands observed, inclined at ~33° to RD direction; single shear band with shear angle ~26°
- **Shear bands in WM1**: Present but reduced compared to PW
- **Texture of WM1**: Mainly {111} ⟨110⟩ and {001} ⟨100⟩ textures, texture strength 11
- **Texture of PW**: More texture types including {111}⟨112⟩, {011}⟨111⟩, {011}⟨211⟩, texture strength ~8.33
- **Interface relationship (sintered WM1)**: Semicoherent interface between W (110) and m-ZrO₂ (110), mismatch rate 16.1%
- **Interface relationship (rolled WT1 and WC1)**: Several nanothick amorphous transition layers between W and ZrO₂ interfaces
- **Sub-grain structure**: Grains of same color composed of sub-grains with orientation difference < 15°

# Mechanical Properties

## As-Sintered Samples

### Relative Density
- **PW**: 95.6%
- **WT1 and WC1**: ~98%

### Vickers Hardness
- **PW**: ~331 HV (calculated from stated increase)  
- **WT1**: 391.08 HV (17.97% higher than PW)
- **WC1**: 385.38 HV (16.25% higher than PW)

### Room-Temperature Compression (strain rate 1.67 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹)
- **PW ultimate compressive strength**: Baseline value not explicitly stated in paper, but referenced from previous work [8]
- **WM1**: +51.94% compared with PW
- **WT1**: +46.06% compared with PW
- **WC1**: +47.39% compared with PW
- **Strain-to-failure**: All three zirconia-doped tungsten alloys ~33%, ~97% higher than PW
- **Complete fracture strain**: Reached an "amazing" 60% for zirconia-strengthened alloys
- **PW fracture mode**: Intergranular fracture, with unclosed pores in fracture surface
- **WM1, WT1, WC1 fracture mode**: Mixed fracture mode (intergranular + transgranular), transgranular proportion significantly increased

### High-Temperature Compression (1300–1600 °C, strain rates 0.005–1 s⁻¹)
- **Peak stress increase at high temperature**: 15–30% higher than PW under same compression conditions
- **Dynamic softening**: WT1 and WC1 reach peak stress earlier than PW, indicating improved dynamic processing performance at high temperatures

### Hot Deformation Activation Energy (Q)
- **PW**: 388.06 kJ/mol
- **WM1**: 349.29 kJ/mol
- **WT1**: 374.25 kJ/mol
- **WC1**: 365.42 kJ/mol

### Constitutive Equations
- **WT1**: ε̇ = 2.39 × 10¹¹ [sinh(0.003386σ)]¹¹·⁶⁶ exp(−374250/RT)
- **WC1**: ε̇ = 1.25 × 10¹¹ [sinh(0.003331σ)]¹¹·⁶² exp(−365418/RT)

### Strengthening Model Contribution (WT1 and WC1)
- **Orowan strengthening (σ\(_{or}\))**: Only ~5% of total strengthening
- **Grain refinement (σ\(_{gb}\))**: Main strengthening mechanism

## Rolled Plate Properties

### Relative Density
- **Tungsten alloy rolled plates**: > 99.5%

### Hardness
- **WM1 RD-ND surface**: 470 HV (~25% higher than sintered state)

### High-Temperature Tensile (1000–1600 °C, strain rate 0.01 s⁻¹)
- **Ultimate tensile strength at 1000 °C**: Tungsten alloys ~30% higher than PW
- **PW tensile fracture at 1400 °C**: Ductile fracture with dimples ~20 μm diameter
- **WM1 at 1000 °C**: Dense, small dimples with uniformly distributed zirconia particles
- **Trend**: With increasing temperature, number of dimples decreases and diameter increases, explaining strength decrease with temperature

### Optimal Hot Working Conditions
- **PW**: 1500–1600 °C, strain rate ~0.1 s⁻¹
- **WM1, WT1, WC1**: 1550–1600 °C, strain rate ≥1 s⁻¹

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- The original paper does not explicitly discuss oxidation resistance or oxidation layer types. The experiments were conducted in vacuum environment for high-temperature tests. No oxidation weight gain data, oxide layer analysis, or atmospheric exposure data are provided.

## ZrO₂ (Zirconia) - General Oxide Dispersion

- **Applicable Alloy System**: W-based alloys
- **Addition Amount**: 1.0 wt% (all crystal forms)
- **Main Function**: Grain refinement, dispersion strengthening, modification of fracture behavior
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: ZrO₂ nanoparticles uniformly dispersed at tungsten grain boundaries and within grains, pinning grain boundaries during sintering and rolling; creates semicoherent interface with W matrix (mismatch 16.1% for m-ZrO₂)
- **Property Effect**:
  - Room-temperature ultimate compressive strength: +46–52% vs. PW
  - High-temperature peak stress: +15–30% vs. PW
  - Strain-to-failure: ~33% vs. ~17% for PW (~97% improvement)
  - Ultimate tensile strength at 1000 °C: +30% vs. PW
  - Grain size reduction: ~61.6% vs. PW
  - Relative density: ~98% vs. 95.6% for PW (sintered)
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly identified in the paper; Orowan strengthening contribution only ~5% of total strengthening.
- **Original Evidence**: Figures 3–5, 8–10; Sections 3.2, 3.3, 3.8, 3.9.

## m-ZrO₂ (Monoclinic Zirconia)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: W-m-ZrO₂ alloy (WM1)
- **Addition Amount**: 1.0 wt%, purity ~99.90%, particle size ~30 nm
- **Main Function**: Grain refinement, fracture mode modification, high-temperature strength improvement
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Semicoherent interface with tungsten matrix (W (110) and m-ZrO₂ (110)), lattice mismatch 16.1%; effectively pins dislocations and grain boundaries; reduces shear band formation during rolling; promotes uniform {111}⟨110⟩ and {001}⟨100⟩ texture
- **Property Effect**:
  - Room-temperature ultimate compressive strength: +51.94% vs. PW
  - High-temperature peak stress: 15–30% higher than PW
  - Hot deformation activation energy Q: 349.29 kJ/mol (vs. 388.06 kJ/mol for PW)
  - Rolled plate hardness: 470 HV (RD-ND surface)
  - Hot working window: 1550–1600 °C, strain rate ≥1 s⁻¹
- **Potential Side Effects**: At temperatures above 1170 °C, monoclinic phase transforms to tetragonal phase (cannot be stabilized to high temperatures).
- **Original Evidence**: Figures 3–9, Table 1; Sections 3.2–3.9; comparison data from previous work [8].

## Y₂O₃ Stabilizer (in t-ZrO₂ and c-ZrO₂)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: W-t-ZrO₂ and W-c-ZrO₂ alloys
- **Addition Amount**: 3 mol% (in t-ZrO₂) or 8 mol% (in c-ZrO₂)
- **Main Function**: Phase stabilization of tetragonal/cubic ZrO₂ over the entire temperature range (prevents phase transformation during high-temperature testing)
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Y₂O₃ doping stabilizes tetragonal and cubic zirconia phases; t-ZrO₂ and c-ZrO₂ maintain crystal form stability over the whole temperature range confirmed by earlier calcination behavior study
- **Property Effect**: Ensures that t-ZrO₂ and c-ZrO₂ remain phase-stable during high-temperature testing (whereas m-ZrO₂ transforms to tetragonal above 1170 °C)
- **Potential Side Effects**: Higher Y₂O₃ content reduces zirconia purity (94.70% for t-ZrO₂, 86.50% for c-ZrO₂)
- **Original Evidence**: Section 3.9; composition details in Section 2.1; phase transformation discussion referencing [31].

## Grain Refinement

- **Related Elements**: ZrO₂ (all crystal forms)
- **Typical Alloys**: WM1, WT1, WC1 (W-1.0 wt% ZrO₂)
- **Key Compositions**: 1.0 wt% ZrO₂ (m-, t-, or c-)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Tungsten grain size reduced from ~15 μm (PW) to ~5 μm in all three zirconia-strengthened alloys (~61% reduction); zirconia particles uniformly dispersed at grain boundaries and within grains pin grain boundaries during sintering and rolling

## Second-Phase Strengthening (Dispersion Strengthening by ZrO₂)

- **Related Elements**: ZrO₂ (all crystal forms)
- **Typical Alloys**: WM1, WT1, WC1
- **Key Compositions**: 1.0 wt% nano-sized ZrO₂ particles (~30 nm)

## Deformation-Texture Strengthening

# Key Conclusions

1. **Crystal form effect is weaker than ZrO₂ content effect**: The microstructure and properties of tungsten alloys strengthened by different crystal forms of zirconia (m-ZrO₂, t-ZrO₂, c-ZrO₂) are remarkably similar. The type of crystal form has less impact on tungsten alloy properties than the zirconia content. This is attributed to the high similarity of zirconia morphology and distribution in tungsten alloys.

2. **Significant improvement over pure tungsten**: The tungsten alloys showed ~50% increase in room-temperature ultimate compressive strength, ~97% increase in strain-to-failure, and 15–30% increase in high-temperature peak stress compared with PW. At 1000 °C, the ultimate tensile strength of rolled tungsten alloy was ~30% higher than PW.

3. **Main strengthening mechanism**: Grain refinement caused by zirconia particles (accounting for most strengthening), not direct Orowan strengthening (~5% contribution).

4. **Improved hot workability**: Addition of zirconia reduces activation energy Q (from 388.06 kJ/mol for PW to 349–374 kJ/mol for tungsten alloys) and improves dynamic processing performance.

5. **Optimal hot working conditions**: For zirconia-strengthened tungsten alloys, temperature 1550–1600 °C, strain rate ≥1 s⁻¹.

6. **Improved texture**: Zirconia addition reduces shear band formation, increases texture strength from ~8.33 to 11, promotes uniform {111}⟨110⟩ and {001}⟨100⟩ texture.

7. **Phase stability issue**: Monoclinic zirconia transforms to tetragonal above 1170 °C; tetragonal and cubic zirconia (stabilized with Y₂O₃) maintain crystal form stability over the entire test temperature range.

8. **Interface analysis**: The semicoherent W/m-ZrO₂ interface (mismatch 16.1%) effectively pins dislocations. Under rolling, amorphous transition layers form at W/ZrO₂ interfaces in WT1 and WC1 plates.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **High-temperature phase stability**: Monoclinic zirconia transforms to tetragonal phase above 1170 °C, so high-temperature property comparisons involving m-ZrO₂ are not at the stated phase condition.

2. **No oxidation resistance data**: The original paper does not investigate oxidation resistance, oxidation weight gain, or oxide scale formation; high-temperature tests were conducted in vacuum.

3. **Compression testing focus**: Mechanical property testing was primarily compression-based; tensile testing was performed only at high temperature (1000–1600 °C) on rolled plates. No room-temperature tensile data are provided.

4. **Density limitation**: Sintered relative density of tungsten alloys only reached ~98% (vs. >99.5% after rolling), suggesting some residual porosity in the as-sintered state.

5. **Crystal form purity differences**: The three ZrO₂ powders have different purities (99.90%, 94.70%, 86.50%) due to Y₂O₃ stabilizer content, which may introduce confounding variables in addition to crystal form.

6. **Single ZrO₂ content studied**: Only 1.0 wt% ZrO₂ addition was compared across crystal forms; the effect at other ZrO₂ contents was not examined in this paper.

7. **Environmental exposure**: The paper does not address irradiation resistance (except briefly referencing earlier work [17]), or service stability in oxidizing or nitriding environments. No grain-boundary oxidation or embrittlement analysis is provided.
