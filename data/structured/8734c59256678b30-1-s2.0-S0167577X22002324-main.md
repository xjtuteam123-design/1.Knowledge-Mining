# Paper Metadata

- **Authors**: Jiaen Lin, Biliang Yuan, Chuanqiang Li, Yong Dong, Peng Zhang, Zhengrong Zhang
- **Journal**: Materials Letters
- **Year**: 2022
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.matlet.2022.131879
- **Material category**: High-entropy alloy (FCC-based)

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system studied**: Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu high-entropy alloy (FCC-based)
- **Research objective**: To achieve strength-ductility synergy in Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu HEA by combining recrystallized fine grains (grain-boundary strengthening) and discrete L1₂ ordered nano-precipitates (precipitation strengthening) through cold rolling (90% reduction) and subsequent annealing (1000°C / 5 min + 600°C / 10 h). The authors hypothesize that simultaneous improvement of strength and ductility can be achieved in FCC-based HEAs if cold mechanical processing and subsequent annealing are controlled rationally.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Melting method**: Induction melting with high-purity (>99.9% purity) monomers in high-purity argon gas
- **Initial condition**: As-cast, supplied as ingot
- **Cold rolling (CR)**: 90% thickness reduction
- **Recrystallization annealing (CRR)**: 1000 °C / 5 min
- **Subsequent aging (CRR-600)**: 600 °C / 10 h
- **Sample naming convention**: 
  - As-cast: as-solidified condition
  - CR: after cold rolling to 90% reduction
  - CRR: after cold rolling + recrystallization at 1000 °C / 5 min
  - CRR-600: after cold rolling + recrystallization at 1000 °C / 5 min + annealing at 600 °C / 10 h

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Face-centered cubic (FCC)
- **Second phases**: 
  - Cu-rich phase at interdendritic regions (as-cast); Cu-segregation into inter-dendritic regions; the matrix is depleted of Cu
  - L1₂ ordered nano-precipitates (discrete, uniformly distributed within the recrystallized matrix after CRR-600 treatment)
- **Grain size**: 
  - Recrystallized matrix grains: ~1 to 1.5 μm (after CRR and CRR-600)
  - Cu-rich region grains: ~2 to 8 μm (larger than matrix grains)
- **Grain boundary features**: Fully recrystallized grains with low dislocation density (after annealing)
- **Elemental segregation**: Cu segregates to interdendritic regions
- **Precipitate size and distribution**: L1₂ nano-precipitates uniformly distributed within the recrystallized matrix; Cu partitions to the L1₂ phase and facilitates stabilization of the ordered phase
- **Phase transformation**: The (2 2 0) peak of CRR and CRR-600 samples splits into two distinctive peaks, associated with stress relaxation and phase transformation [14]

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed**: Grain-boundary strengthening (Hall-Petch), precipitation strengthening (L1₂ order precipitates), dislocation interactions with precipitates (dislocation cutting to bowing).

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Cu] (Copper)

- **[Applicable Alloy System]**: Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu high-entropy alloy (FCC-based)
- **[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**: Cu is a principal element in equimolar proportion to Co, Cr, Fe, Ni; matrix region contains 13.7–16.8 at.% Cu while interdendritic Cu-rich regions contain 66.2–69.3 at.% Cu.
- **[Main Function]**: 
  - Elemental segregation into interdendritic regions, creating a Cu-rich second phase
  - Facilitates L1₂ precipitation via initial Cu-cluster formation within the FCC matrix
  - Stabilizes the ordered L1₂ phase through partitioning
- **[Microstructural Mechanism]**: 
  - Cu has a strong tendency to segregate into inter-dendritic regions during solidification due to weak combination ability between Cu and other elements, attributed to partially wetting effect in the final stages of solidification
  - The mixing enthalpy (ΔH_mix) of Al₀.₃CoCrFeNi HEA increases from -7.27 kJ/mol to 0.16 kJ/mol with the addition of Cu to form Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu HEA [5,17]
  - Initial Cu-clusters within the FCC matrix act as heterogeneous nucleation sites for L1₂ precipitates [15]
  - Cu partitioning to L1₂ phase stabilizes the ordered phase [15]
- **[Property Effect]**: 
  - Contributes to the formation of L1₂ precipitates, which provide ~126 MPa precipitation strengthening contribution to yield strength (based on reference [24])
  - Cu-rich phases with larger grain size (2–8 μm) contribute to the discrepancy between calculated (~966 MPa) and experimental (~880 MPa) yield strength
  - The CRR-600 sample achieves 880 MPa YS, 970 MPa UTS, and 29% elongation with Cu-assisted L1₂ precipitation
- **[Potential Side Effects]**: 
  - Cu segregation reduces Cu content in the matrix, which suppresses long-distance diffusion because diffusional kinetics is slow at low temperature
  - Cu-rich phases have larger grain size than matrix, potentially reducing strength
  - Ordered L1₂ precipitation possesses less slip systems and slightly deteriorates ductility [26–28]
- **[Original Evidence]**: Table 1 (composition of different regions); Sections 3 Results and discussion; References [5], [15], [17], [24]

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related elements**: Al, Co, Cr, Fe, Ni (principal elements); the alloy does not contain heavy refractory elements.
- **Typical alloys**: Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu HEA (FCC-based)
- **Key compositions**: Al₀.₃ with equimolar Co, Cr, Fe, Ni, Cu
- **Microstructural changes**: Severe lattice distortion in the FCC solid-solution matrix; lattice distortion retards grain boundary transfer and grain growth during recrystallization, leading to uneven recrystallized grains in duplex structure [18]
- **Property improvements**: Not quantified separately. The overall CRR-600 sample achieves 880 MPa YS, 970 MPa UTS, 29% EL, but solid-solution contribution is not separately quantified in the paper.
- **Risks and limitations**: Solid solution ductility, while significantly strengthened by cold rolling (CR UTS = 1200 MPa), is achieved at the cost of plasticity (brittle cleavage fracture); precipitation hardening and grain refinement are more effective and compatible with ductility in this alloy system.
- **Original evidence**: Section 3 (discussion of lattice distortion in FCC matrix); reference [18]

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening (Hall-Petch)

- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; achieved through processing (cold rolling + recrystallization annealing)
- **Typical alloys**: Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu HEA (FCC-based)
- **Key compositions**: Not composition-dependent; grain refinement to ~1–1.5 μm in matrix
- **Microstructural changes**: Fully recrystallized fine grains with low dislocation density; uneven recrystallized grains due to severe lattice distortion in FCC matrix retarding grain boundary transfer and growth
- **Property improvements**: Grain boundary (Hall-Petch) contribution to yield strength of ~840 MPa (based on average grain size of ~1 μm, using the Hall-Petch relationship for CoCrFeNi HEA [23])
- **Risks and limitations**: The existence of Cu-rich phases with larger grain size (2–8 μm) reduces the overall strengthening efficiency compared to the calculated value; the experimental YS (~880 MPa) is lower than the calculated combined contribution of grain boundary + precipitation (~966 MPa) due to Cu-rich phases.
- **Original evidence**: Section 3, equations and calculations; references [23]

## Grain Refinement

- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; achieved through cold rolling (90% reduction) followed by recrystallization annealing (1000 °C / 5 min)
- **Typical alloys**: Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu HEA (FCC-based)
- **Key compositions**: Not composition-dependent
- **Microstructural changes**: As-cast interdendritic structure with Cu segregation; after CR, elongated grains without recrystallization; after CRR, fully recrystallized fine grains (~1–1.5 μm); CRR-600 maintains recrystallized grains
- **Property improvements**: 
  - As-cast: YS = 360 MPa, UTS = 700 MPa, EL = 60%
  - CR: UTS = 1200 MPa, plasticity lost (brittle)
  - CRR: YS = 750 MPa, UTS = 850 MPa, EL = 31%
  - CRR-600: YS = 880 MPa, UTS = 970 MPa, EL = 29%
- **Risks and limitations**: Cold rolling alone leads to brittle fracture due to abundant dislocation accumulation; recrystallization anneal is necessary to recover ductility.
- **Original evidence**: Section 3, Figure 3; references [7], [8], [12]

## Second-Phase Strengthening (L1₂ Precipitation)

- **Related elements**: Cu (partitions to L1₂ phase and stabilizes the ordered phase), Al (contributes to phase stability), Ni (part of the L1₂ structure)
- **Typical alloys**: Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu HEA (FCC-based)
- **Key compositions**: Al₀.₃ with equimolar Co, Cr, Fe, Ni, Cu; L1₂ phase is Cu-partitioning and Al-containing
- **Microstructural changes**: Discrete L1₂ ordered nano-precipitates uniformly distributed within the recrystallized matrix; L1₂ present after CRR-600; initial Cu-clusters in FCC matrix act as heterogeneous nucleation sites for L1₂ formation; Cu partitioning to L1₂ stabilizes the ordered phase
- **Property improvements**: Precipitation strengthening contribution of ~126 MPa to yield strength for L1₂ phases (based on reference [24]); combined grain boundary + precipitation strengthening calculated to be ~966 MPa
- **Risks and limitations**: Ordered L1₂ precipitation possesses fewer slip systems and slightly deteriorates ductility [26–28]; long-distance diffusion is suppressed due to slow kinetics at low temperature [20], limiting precipitate growth and potentially contributing less than theoretical strengthening
- **Original evidence**: TEM/SADP analysis showing (100) super-lattice reflection; Section 3; references [10], [11], [15], [20], [22], [24], [26–28]

# Key Conclusions

1. A strength-ductility synergy is achieved in FCC-based Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu HEA by coupling grain-boundary strengthening (recrystallized fine grains) with precipitation strengthening (discrete L1₂ ordered nano-precipitates).
2. The optimal processing route is: cold rolling with 90% thickness reduction → recrystallization annealing at 1000 °C / 5 min → aging at 600 °C / 10 h.
3. The resulting CRR-600 sample exhibits yield strength of 880 MPa, ultimate tensile strength of 970 MPa, and tensile elongation of 29% at room temperature.
4. The calculated grain boundary (Hall-Petch) contribution is ~840 MPa, and the calculated precipitation strengthening contribution is ~126 MPa, yielding a combined estimated yield strength of ~966 MPa. The discrepancy with the experimental value (880 MPa) is attributed to Cu-rich phases with larger grain size.
5. The as-cast sample shows YS = 360 MPa, UTS = 700 MPa, EL = 60%; the CR sample shows UTS = 1200 MPa with lost plasticity; the CRR sample shows YS = 750 MPa, UTS = 850 MPa, EL = 31%.
6. Cold rolling alone introduces abundant dislocation accumulation and causes brittle fracture with cleavage planes.
7. The deformation mechanism changes from dislocation cutting to dislocation bowing during tensile deformation in the precipitation-hardened state, contributing to the higher strength of the CRR-600 sample.
8. Cu segregation at interdendritic regions is attributed to weak bonding between Cu and other elements and the positive change in mixing enthalpy upon Cu addition (from -7.27 kJ/mol for Al₀.₃CoCrFeNi to 0.16 kJ/mol for Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu).

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- This is not a refractory alloy study. The alloy system is an FCC transition-metal high-entropy alloy (Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-Cu), so it is outside the domain of Nb-W-based, Mo-W-based, or refractory multi-principal element alloys. The only overlaps with the user's focus area (refractory high-temperature alloys) are general processing strategies (cold rolling + annealing, precipitation strengthening, grain refinement) that could be conceptually transferred to other alloy systems.
- The paper does not report high-temperature mechanical properties, creep, oxidation resistance, density, or thermal stability of the L1₂ precipitates.
- The nominal composition is not explicitly stated in the Materials and Methods section, only referenced as Al₀.₃CoCrFeNiCu.
- Impurity levels (C, O, N, H) are not reported.
- The precise composition of the L1₂ precipitates is not chemically characterized in this paper (EDS inside precipitates not provided).
- The Hall-Petch calculation is based on literature values for CoCrFeNi HEA, not directly measured for this specific alloy; this may introduce uncertainty.
- The strengthening mechanism analysis is simplified and assumes additive contributions from Hall-Petch and precipitation strengthening; interactions between mechanisms are not considered.
- The paper does not discuss the thermal stability or coarsening behavior of L1₂ precipitates above 600 °C, which is critical for high-temperature refractory alloy applications.
- The authors do not discuss the effect of the Cu-rich phase on high-temperature stability or long-term service.
- The alloy contains no W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf, or other refractory elements; therefore, the conclusions cannot be directly extrapolated to refractory alloy systems without experimental validation.
