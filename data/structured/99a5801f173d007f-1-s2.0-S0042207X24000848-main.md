# Alloy System and Research Object

This paper studies a Co-free eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA) with composition Al0.9Cr0.8FeMn0.8Ni2.0, designed for nuclear power applications as a reduced-activation material (Co eliminated due to high radioactivity under neutron irradiation).

The alloy was designed using the VEC (Valence Electron Concentration) criterion. The alloy has a VEC value of 7.47, matching that of AlCrFeNiCo1.9 EHEA designed by Vikram et al. The design follows the approach of Wu et al., who showed that alloys designed under the same VEC conditions, within the VEC range of 6.87–8, will exhibit EHEA characteristics.

Key thermodynamic parameters (Table 1):
- δ = 5.58%
- Ω = 1.55
- ΔHmix = 12.95 kJ/mol (note: original paper lists this as positive 12.95, though this appears unusual for typical HEA formation criteria; the paper states all parameters satisfy the rules of Guo et al.)
- VEC = 7.47

# Processing and Heat Treatment

**Melting**:
- Arc melting in argon atmosphere
- Using commercially available high-purity metal blocks (~8 mm × 8 mm × 4 mm)
- Ingots flipped at least 7 times for homogeneity

**Heat treatment (annealing)**:
- After arc melting, ingots sealed in vacuum tubes
- Isochronous annealing at 300 °C, 400 °C, 500 °C, 600 °C, 700 °C, 800 °C
- Holding time: 2 h
- Cooling method: Air cooling to room temperature

**No additional processing** reported (no rolling, forging, HIP, SPS, powder metallurgy, mechanical alloying, or additive manufacturing).

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## As-cast state
- Dendritic structure: FCC phase (enriched in Fe and Cr)
- Sunflower petal-like eutectic structure: alternating FCC + BCC phases
- Sunflower disc-like spherical/ellipsoidal matrix structure: BCC phase (enriched in Ni and Al)

## Annealing at 300–500 °C
- Eutectic structure in petal region gradually disappears, replaced by coarser dendritic structure
- FCC dendrites gradually coarsen and dissolve the eutectic structure
- No significant static recrystallization (SRX)
- Phenomenon most pronounced at 300 °C

## Annealing at 600 °C
- Dual-phase microstructure similar to as-cast state retained
- BCC phase becomes dominant
- Static recrystallization (SRX) activated → significant grain refinement
- Eutectic structure in petal region and matrix in disc region exhibit significant growth
- Petal-like eutectic and disc-like BCC structures grow, occupying surrounding dendrites → reduced FCC dendrite proportion

## Annealing at 700–800 °C
- FCC phase continues to dissolve
- Dendritic and eutectic structures undergo gradual dissolution
- Needle-like ordered B2 phase precipitates (rich in Al and Ni) form within the residual FCC dendrites
- SRX grains coarsen; only BCC phase grains coarsen

## Phase identification (TEM/SAED, Fig. 1h)
- P1: Disc region matrix = BCC phase
- P2: Dendritic structure = FCC phase
- P3: Spherical disordered BCC structured phase within FCC structure
- P4: Needle-like B2 phase (ordered BCC structure) precipitate

## Elemental distribution (EPMA, Fig. 2)
- As-cast: FCC dendrites enriched in Fe and Cr; BCC matrix enriched in Ni and Al
- Eutectic petal region: alternating Ni-Al (BCC) and Fe-Cr (FCC) enrichments
- Mn: Initially homogeneous in as-cast state, gradually enriches in BCC phase with increasing annealing temperature (attributed to Mn destabilizing FCC solid solution at higher content)
- At 800 °C: B2 needle-like precipitates enriched in Al and Ni within FCC dendrites (due to higher diffusion rates of Al and Ni at this temperature)

## Grain size evolution (EBSD, Fig. 3)
- Average grain size first decreases then increases with annealing temperature; turning point at 600 °C
- 300 °C annealed sample: largest average grain size, highest FCC phase proportion
- FCC phase proportion → monotonic decrease with temperature
- BCC phase proportion → monotonic increase with temperature
- At 600 °C: SRX activated, significant grain refinement
- At 700–800 °C: recrystallized grains coarsen (only BCC grains)

# Mechanical Properties

**Testing conditions**:
- Uniaxial tensile tests
- Dog-bone-shaped specimens: gauge length 5 mm × 1.2 mm × 0.25 mm
- Instron 5564 testing system
- Strain rate: ~10⁻³ s⁻¹
- At least 3 samples tested per condition
- No traditional extensometer used due to small sample size; lateral comparison focused

## Tensile results

| Condition | Tensile Strength | Elongation |
|-----------|-----------------|------------|
| As-cast | Not explicitly stated numerically | Not explicitly stated numerically |
| 300 °C | Lowest (relative) | Unchanged vs 500 °C |
| 400 °C | Intermediate | Unchanged vs 500 °C |
| 500 °C | Intermediate | Unchanged |
| 600 °C | >1000 MPa (approximately 20% increase vs 500 °C) | 7.3% (approximately 22% increase vs 500 °C) |
| 700 °C | Decreased | Decreased |
| 800 °C | Decreased | Decreased |

**Key observations**:
- Tensile strength gradually increased from 300–500 °C (correlated with BCC phase increase)
- At 600 °C: ~20% strength increase vs 500 °C; ~22% elongation increase vs 500 °C
- At 700–800 °C: Both strength and elongation decline due to grain coarsening
- Elongation remained unchanged from 300–500 °C
- Strength directly correlated with BCC phase content
- Ductility inversely correlated with FCC phase content (i.e., as FCC increases, ductility decreases — this is the paper's stated observation)

**Comparison with literature (Fig. 5c)**: The 600 °C annealed EHEA exhibits a good balance of strength and ductility compared to cast conventional metals and alloys and HEAs from literature [20–31]. Tensile strength >1000 MPa with 7.3% elongation.

**Room-temperature yield strength, compressive strength, high-temperature strength, fracture toughness, creep performance, hardness, elastic modulus, density**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Al (Aluminum)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Al0.9Cr0.8FeMn0.8Ni2.0 Co-free EHEA
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0.9 subscript (~16.4 at.% calculated)
- **Main Function**: BCC phase stabilizer; contributes to B2 phase precipitate formation with Ni; contributes to strength enhancement
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Al enriches in BCC matrix phase. In as-cast state, Al is enriched in the BCC disc region. At higher annealing temperatures (800 °C), Al and Ni atoms have high diffusion rates and aggregate within FCC dendrites to form needle-like ordered B2 precipitates. Al and Ni exhibit higher miscibility and affinity, favoring BCC structure formation.
- **Property Effect**: Al-Ni-rich BCC structure exhibits high strength and hardness, contributing to improved mechanical properties. Specific numerical values not stated for Al alone; the 600 °C annealed alloy with BCC-dominant structure achieved >1000 MPa tensile strength and 7.3% elongation.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated. (In general, high Al content in HEAs can lead to B2 ordering, but this is not explicitly discussed as a side effect in this paper.)
- **Original Evidence**: EPMA maps (Fig. 2): "the matrix in the disc region shows an enrichment of Ni and Al in the BCC phase"; "Al and Ni atoms exhibited higher miscibility and affinity, favoring the formation of the BCC structure"; "needle-like precipitates of the B2 phase were observed to be enriched in aluminum and nickel within the dendritic FCC phase" (800 °C annealing).

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## 1. Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related elements**: Al, Ni (BCC solid solution), Fe, Cr (FCC solid solution), Mn
- **Typical alloys**: Al0.9Cr0.8FeMn0.8Ni2.0 EHEA
- **Key compositions**: BCC phase enriched in Ni and Al; FCC phase enriched in Fe and Cr
- **Microstructural changes**: Dual-phase BCC + FCC solid solutions with sunflower petal-like eutectic morphology; Mn redistribution from homogeneous to BCC-enriched with increasing annealing temperature
- **Property improvements**: BCC phase increase correlates directly with tensile strength increase (from 300–600 °C annealing). The BCC phase provides high strength and hardness.
- **Risks and limitations**: FCC phase reduction leads to ductility decrease; Mn enrichment in BCC at high annealing temperatures destabilizes FCC; at 700–800 °C grain coarsening negates strength gains.
- **Original evidence**: "the relationship between strength and the BCC phase is direct: as the BCC phase increases, strength also increases."

## 2. Grain Refinement (via Static Recrystallization, SRX)

- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; microstructural mechanism
- **Typical alloys**: Al0.9Cr0.8FeMn0.8Ni2.0 EHEA
- **Key compositions**: N/A (mechanism applies to annealing at 600 °C)
- **Microstructural changes**: At 600 °C, static recrystallization initiates, significantly refining as-cast grains. Eutectic microstructure promotes recrystallization by: (a) reducing grain boundary energy and enhancing mobility; (b) increasing total interface length between phases providing more migration paths; (c) facilitating boundary rearrangement.
- **Property improvements**: Elongation increases by ~22% at 600 °C vs 500 °C (from ~6.0% to 7.3% — calculated from stated percentages), despite simultaneous BCC phase strengthening. The increase in grain boundaries hinders dislocation and crack movement, limiting crack propagation and improving ductility.
- **Risks and limitations**: At 700–800 °C, SRX grains coarsen, leading to decline in both strength and elongation.
- **Original evidence**: "the pronounced recrystallization effect at 600 °C"; "the increase in the number of grain boundaries has a hindering effect on dislocation and crack movement, which limits crack propagation and further improves the ductility of the material"; EBSD recrystallization distribution map (Fig. 3o).

## 3. Second-Phase Strengthening / Precipitation (B2 Ordered Phase)

- **Related elements**: Al and Ni
- **Typical alloys**: Al0.9Cr0.8FeMn0.8Ni2.0 EHEA (at 700–800 °C annealing)
- **Key compositions**: Needle-like B2 ordered precipitate (Al, Ni-rich) within FCC dendrites
- **Microstructural changes**: At 700–800 °C, needle-like ordered B2 phase precipitates form within residual FCC dendrites due to high Al and Ni diffusion rates. At 600 °C, lower diffusion rate impedes precipitation. B2 precipitates coexist with dissolving FCC phase and growing BCC phase.
- **Property improvements**: Not observed in this study. Despite B2 phase having inherently high strength and hardness, mechanical properties (both strength and elongation) decline at 700–800 °C due to grain coarsening and FCC dissolution.
- **Risks and limitations**: B2 precipitate formation occurs simultaneously with microstructural degradation (grain coarsening, FCC loss); no net strengthening achieved at 700–800 °C.
- **Original evidence**: "a needle-like B2 phase with an ordered BCC structure precipitate (P4)"; "needle-like precipitates of the B2 phase were observed to be enriched in aluminum and nickel within the dendritic FCC phase."

## 4. Eutectic Microstructure Strengthening

- **Related elements**: N/A (phase morphology)
- **Typical alloys**: Al0.9Cr0.8FeMn0.8Ni2.0 EHEA
- **Key compositions**: Sunflower petal-like eutectic (FCC + BCC alternating arrangement)
- **Microstructural changes**: The sunflower-like eutectic morphology results from component segregation during solidification. The eutectic structure enhances mechanical properties, hardness, wear resistance, and corrosion resistance.
- **Property improvements**: Retained eutectic microstructure in petal region at 600 °C contributes to strengthening during deformation. Contributes to >1000 MPa tensile strength.
- **Risks and limitations**: Eutectic structure dissolves gradually with annealing temperature (300–500 °C), and structural integrity compromises at 700–800 °C.
- **Original evidence**: "the retained eutectic microstructure in the petal region can contribute to strengthening during the deformation process"; "this unique morphology enhances the mechanical properties, hardness, wear resistance, and corrosion resistance of the alloys."

## 5. Grain-Boundary Strengthening

- **Related elements**: N/A
- **Typical alloys**: Al0.9Cr0.8FeMn0.8Ni2.0 EHEA (600 °C annealed)
- **Key compositions**: N/A
- **Microstructural changes**: Recrystallization-induced grain refinement increases grain boundary density.
- **Property improvements**: Hinders dislocation and crack movement, limits crack propagation, improves ductility (7.3% elongation at 600 °C).
- **Risks and limitations**: Reversed by grain coarsening at 700–800 °C.
- **Original evidence**: "the increase in the number of grain boundaries has a hindering effect on dislocation and crack movement, which limits crack propagation and further improves the ductility of the material."

# Key Conclusions

1. **As-cast microstructure**: The Al0.9Cr0.8FeMn0.8Ni2.0 EHEA exhibits three microstructural morphologies: dendritic (FCC), sunflower petal-like eutectic (FCC + BCC), and sunflower disc-like matrix (BCC) structures.

2. **Annealing at 300–500 °C**: No significant static recrystallization; eutectic structure dissolves and FCC dendrites gradually coarsen; phenomenon most pronounced at 300 °C.

3. **Annealing at 600 °C**: BCC phase becomes dominant; static recrystallization initiates, significantly refining grain size. This yields the optimal balance of strength and ductility: tensile strength >1000 MPa (~20% increase vs 500 °C) and elongation 7.3% (~22% increase vs 500 °C).

4. **Annealing at 700–800 °C**: FCC phase continues to dissolve; needle-like B2 phase (rich in Al, Ni) precipitates within residual FCC dendrites; SRX grains coarsen; both strength and elongation decline.

5. **Phase-property correlation**: Tensile strength directly correlates with BCC phase content; ductility inversely correlates with FCC phase content. Mn enriches in BCC phase at higher annealing temperatures.

6. **Nuclear application potential**: The Co-free EHEA design is promising for nuclear power applications due to reduced activation; future work will address high-temperature stability and irradiation resistance.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Mechanical testing limitations**: Small sample size prevented use of a traditional extensometer; the study focused on lateral comparison rather than absolute precision of strain measurements.
- **No high-temperature mechanical testing**: All tensile tests performed at room temperature; no elevated-temperature strength data provided.
- **No oxidation or corrosion testing**: The paper mentions Cr's potential role in corrosion resistance but provides no experimental verification.
- **No irradiation testing**: Despite nuclear power application motivation, no irradiation data presented; stated as future work.
- **No long-term aging**: 2 h isochronal annealing only; long-term microstructural stability not addressed.
- **No specific numerical values** reported for as-cast, 300 °C, 400 °C, 500 °C, and 700 °C tensile strength/elongation values (only relative trends and 600 °C values are provided).
- **Alloy system scope**: This is an Al-Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni alloy system, not a refractory metal (Nb, W, Mo, Ta, Zr, Hf) based alloy. It is relevant to reduced-activation HEAs for nuclear applications but not to high-temperature refractory alloy systems (Nb-W, Mo-W, refractory HEA, ODS refractory alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys).
- **Mn redistribution mechanism**: The paper attributes Mn enrichment in BCC at high annealing temperature to thermodynamic solid solubility and crystal structure stability considerations, but this explanation is presented as the authors' interpretation rather than a rigorously verified conclusion.
