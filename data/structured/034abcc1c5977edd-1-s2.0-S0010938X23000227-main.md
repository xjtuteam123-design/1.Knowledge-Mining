# Paper Metadata
- **Title:** Isothermal oxidation behavior of high-Nb-containing TiAl alloys doped with W, B, Y, and C/Si
- **Authors:** Tielong Sun, Zhichao Guo, Jun Cao, Yongfeng Liang, Junpin Lin
- **Journal:** Corrosion Science
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.corsci.2023.110980
- **Alloy system studied:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys doped with W, B, Y, and C or Si
- **Research objective:** To investigate the synergistic effects of multiple alloying elements (W, B, Y, C/Si) on the microstructure and isothermal oxidation behavior of high-Nb-containing TiAl alloys at 900 °C in ambient air
- **Material category:** TiAl-based intermetallic alloy (high-Nb-containing TiAl alloy with multicomponent minor alloying additions). Not a Nb-W alloy, Mo-W alloy, refractory high-entropy alloy, or additively manufactured refractory alloy in the strict sense; however, it is a high-temperature structural intermetallic containing refractory elements Nb and W.

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Alloy system:** γ-TiAl-based intermetallic with high Nb content (8 at.% Nb), modified with trace amounts of W, B, Y, and C or Si.
- **Base alloy:** Ti45Al8Nb (nominal, at.%)
- **Two investigated alloys:**
  - **WBYC alloy:** Ti45Al8Nb0.2W0.2B0.1Y0.2C (at.%)
  - **WBYS alloy:** Ti45Al8Nb0.2W0.2B0.1Y0.2Si (at.%)
- **Material subclass:** Not a refractory high-entropy alloy; it is a γ-TiAl intermetallic-based alloy with refractory metal additions (Nb, W). The paper deals with high-temperature oxidation resistance rather than mechanical strengthening mechanisms.

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Melting route:** Induction skull melting (ISM) in an argon atmosphere.
- **Ingot size:** φ 50 × 140 mm.
- **Melting procedure:** Ingots were melted three times, each melting lasting ~3 min, under electromagnetic levitation to minimize composition segregation.
- **Heat treatment:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper. The alloys were tested in the as-cast condition; no homogenization, rolling, forging, annealing, or aging is described.
- **Powder metallurgy:** Not used.
- **Additive manufacturing:** Not used.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
- **Matrix phases (as-cast):** γ phase (dominant) and α₂ phase, as shown by XRD and BSE imaging. Both alloys were full-lamellar.
- **Other phases detected by XRD after oxidation (200 h):** TiO₂, Al₂O₃, γ phase, minor TiN, YAl, and NbO₂. In the as-cast state, B2 phase, borides, and Y- and Si-rich precipitates were not detected by XRD due to their low contents.
- **Precipitates/second phases identified by BSE-EDS:**
  - Y-rich precipitates: white spots and rod-like particles; identified by reference as YAl₂ and Y₂O₃ (per Zhao et al.).
  - B-rich phases: line-like borides.
  - Si-rich precipitates: light gray irregular-shaped silicide in the inter-dendritic area, enriched in Nb (likely niobium silicide); found only in WBYS alloy.
  - C-containing precipitates: None observed in WBYC alloy, attributed to low C content and high C solubility in high-Nb-containing TiAl alloy.
- **Grain size:** Not explicitly quantified. The WBYS alloy had finer α₂/γ lamellar size and smaller colony size than the WBYC alloy, attributed to more precipitates (Y-rich, B-rich, and Si-rich particles).
- **Segregation:** Severe S-segregation (liquid segregation from Al segregation to inter-dendritic liquid region) and β-segregation (incomplete β→α phase transformation) were observed in inter-dendritic and dendritic regions, respectively.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed:** Not the primary focus. However, grain refinement by precipitates (B-rich, Y-rich, Si-rich) is mentioned; the paper notes that fine lamellar structure is conducive to room- and high-temperature performance. The paper does not discuss solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, or dispersion strengthening in the traditional mechanical-property sense; it focuses on oxidation resistance and oxide peg formation.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability
- **Test conditions:** Isothermal oxidation at 900 °C in static laboratory air for 200 h; two duplicate specimens per alloy.
- **Mass gains after 200 h:**
  - WBYC: 0.903 mg/cm²
  - WBYS: 0.874 mg/cm²
- **Oxidation kinetics:** Both alloys followed parabolic growth (n ≈ 2).
  - n values: WBYC = 2.13; WBYS = 2.28
  - Oxidation rate constants k_p:
    - WBYC: 3.87 × 10⁻³ mgⁿ·cm⁻²ⁿ·h⁻¹
    - WBYS: 2.99 × 10⁻³ mgⁿ·cm⁻²ⁿ·h⁻¹
- **Comparison with literature:** Both WBYC and WBYS alloys exhibited the best oxidation resistance at 900 °C for 100 or 200 h among TiAl alloys reported previously (Fig. 4).
- **Oxide scale structure after 200 h:**
  - **WBYC:** From outside to inside: TiO₂-rich layer → Al₂O₃-rich layer (discontinuous) → mixed (Ti, Al, O, N, Nb) layer → Nb-rich layer. Oxide scale thickness ≈ 5.5 μm.
  - **WBYS:** TiO₂-rich layer → Al₂O₃-rich layer (intact) → mixed (Ti, Al, O, N, Nb, Si) layer → Nb-rich layer. Oxide scale thickness ≈ 5 μm.
- **Oxide phases detected by XRD after oxidation:** TiO₂, Al₂O₃, γ phase, TiN, YAl, NbO₂.
- **Role of Y:** Promotes oxide peg formation, enhancing adhesion between oxide scale and substrate; however, oxidized Y-rich phase near the surface caused internal oxidation of the surrounding substrate, which is detrimental.
- **Role of Si:** Promotes formation of an intact Al₂O₃-rich layer by enhancing Al ion activity, reducing oxidation rate.
- **Role of C:** C ions are more active than Si ions; C inhibits TiO₂ growth in early stages but is rapidly consumed, leading to oxide cluster formation (attributed to volatilization of carbon oxides), which interrupts the continuous oxide scale and increases oxygen transport and spalling tendency.
- **Role of W:** W⁶⁺ ions in TiO₂ annihilate oxygen vacancies, decreasing TiO₂ growth rate and promoting Al₂O₃ growth by reducing oxygen solubility. W was not enriched in any particular oxide layer; it was depleted in the Al₂O₃-rich layer.
- **Oxide pegs:** Protrusions of oxide into the substrate increased contact area and improved oxide scale adhesion and spalling resistance.
- **Spallation:** No spalling observed after 200 h for either alloy, in contrast to undoped Ti45Al8Nb which showed severe spallation in prior studies.
- **High-temperature embrittlement/oxygen/nitrogen embrittlement:** Not explicitly discussed.
- **Grain-boundary oxidation:** Implied—B-rich, Y-rich, and Si-rich phases at grain boundaries blocked oxygen inward diffusion and reduced dissolved oxygen, thereby improving oxidation resistance; however, oxidized Y-rich phases near the surface promoted internal oxidation.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## W (Tungsten)
- **Applicable Alloy System:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys (Ti45Al8Nb-based) with W, B, Y, C/Si additions.
- **Addition Amount:** 0.2 at.% W (nominal).
- **Main Function:** Reduces TiO₂ growth rate and promotes Al₂O₃ formation, improving oxidation resistance.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** W⁶⁺ ions incorporated in TiO₂ annihilate oxygen vacancies, slowing TiO₂ growth; W addition also reduces oxygen solubility in the alloy, indirectly promoting Al₂O₃ formation. The paper states that W distribution in the Al₂O₃-rich layer is barren, with no obvious enrichment in other layers.
- **Property Effect:** Contributes to the excellent oxidation resistance of WBYC and WBYS alloys: mass gains of 0.903 and 0.874 mg/cm² after 200 h at 900 °C. The original paper does not provide a direct comparison of the same alloy without W under identical conditions; the improvement is inferred from comparison with literature data for Ti45Al8Nb alloys.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 4, Discussion: “the addition of W can decrease the growth rate of TiO₂ because W⁶⁺ ions in TiO₂ annihilate oxygen vacancies and promote the Al₂O₃ growth by reducing oxygen solubility”; Fig. 9 and associated text on W distribution.

## B (Boron)
- **Applicable Alloy System:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys (Ti45Al8Nb-based).
- **Addition Amount:** 0.2 at.% B (nominal).
- **Main Function:** Grain refinement and formation of B-rich precipitates (borides) that block inward oxygen diffusion and improve oxide scale adhesion via oxide peg formation.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** B addition forms line-like borides at grain boundaries; these precipitates inhibit grain boundary migration, refine grains/lamellar colonies, and increase grain boundary density. B-rich phases at grain boundaries block inward oxygen diffusion and reduce dissolved oxygen.
- **Property Effect:** Contributes to the excellent oxidation resistance: mass gains < 1 mg/cm² after 200 h at 900 °C. WBYS with B (plus Y and Si) had finer lamellar size and better oxidation resistance than WBYC. The original paper does not provide specific mechanical property values for B addition alone.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence:** Fig. 1(c, d) showing line-like borides; Section 4, Discussion: “the B-rich, Y-rich and Si-rich phases at grain boundaries can block the inward diffusion of oxygen and reduce the dissolved oxygen in alloy”; “the precipitated phases can inhibit the migration of the grain boundaries and refine the grains”.

## Y (Yttrium)
- **Applicable Alloy System:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys (Ti45Al8Nb-based).
- **Addition Amount:** 0.1 at.% Y (nominal).
- **Main Function:** Promotes oxide peg formation, improving oxide scale adhesion; removes oxygen impurities during melting; but can cause internal oxidation near the oxide scale.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Y has a strong affinity for oxygen; Y-rich precipitates (YAl₂ and Y₂O₃) form. During oxidation, Y-rich phases react via 2AlYₓ + 3xO₂ → xAl₂O₃ + xY₂O₃, forming oxide pegs that mechanically anchor the oxide scale to the substrate. Y also alters local oxygen partial pressure, promoting selective oxidation of Al. However, Y-rich phases near the oxide scale can absorb oxygen and cause oxidation of surrounding substrate.
- **Property Effect:** Enhanced oxide scale adhesion and spalling resistance; both WBYC and WBYS alloys showed no spalling after 200 h at 900 °C. However, Y addition has a “counterbalancing effect”: oxidized Y-rich phase inside the substrate near the oxide layer caused severe internal oxidation (Fig. 10 c, d). The original paper does not provide a direct quantitative comparison of alloys with and without Y under identical conditions.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Internal oxidation of substrate near oxidized Y-rich phases; excessive Y addition is reported in prior literature to be detrimental due to coarsening of Y-rich precipitates and increased lattice defects (per Xiang et al.).
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.4 and Fig. 10: “the Y-rich phase was oxidized owing to the strong affinity between the Y atom and O atom through the oxidation reaction of 2AlYx + 3xO₂ → xAl₂O₃ + xY₂O₃”; “the surrounding substrate of the oxidized Y-rich phase underwent severe internal oxidation, which is adverse on the oxidation resistance of alloys”; Section 3.2: Lang et al. reported YAl phase formation reduced oxygen activity and promoted Al₂O₃ formation.

## C (Carbon)
- **Applicable Alloy System:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys (WBYC alloy).
- **Addition Amount:** 0.2 at.% C (nominal).
- **Main Function:** Inhibits TiO₂ growth during early oxidation; however, rapid consumption of C leads to oxide cluster formation and deterioration of long-term oxidation resistance.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** C ions are more active than Si ions; C ions incorporate into oxide scale and inhibit TiO₂ growth. C addition did not form visible carbides in the alloy, attributed to low C content and high C solubility in the high-Nb TiAl alloy. During oxidation, carbon oxides may volatilize, forming oxide clusters (2–4 μm) that interrupt the continuous oxide scale.
- **Property Effect:** WBYC oxidation mass gain after 200 h at 900 °C: 0.903 mg/cm², slightly higher than WBYS (0.874 mg/cm²), indicating slightly poorer oxidation resistance than Si-containing counterpart. Oxide clusters formed in WBYC are linked to carbon oxide volatilization; these clusters increase oxygen transport rate and spalling tendency. The original paper does not provide a direct comparison with a C-free alloy under identical conditions.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Formation of oxide clusters, increased oxygen transport, increased spalling tendency, harmful to long-term oxidation resistance.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 3.2: “the diffraction peak intensities of TiO₂ in the WBYC alloy were relatively weak”; Section 3.3: “the oxide cluster formed in WBYC alloy may be related to the volatilization of carbon oxides on the substrate surface, which may interrupt the growth of the continuous oxide scale”; Section 4: “the fast consumption of C is harmful to the long-term oxidation resistance”.

## Si (Silicon)
- **Applicable Alloy System:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys (WBYS alloy).
- **Addition Amount:** 0.2 at.% Si (nominal).
- **Main Function:** Promotes formation of an intact Al₂O₃-rich layer, reducing oxidation rate and improving long-term oxidation resistance.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Si reduces the activity of Ti⁴⁺ ions by improving outward diffusion of Al, thereby promoting Al₂O₃-rich layer formation. Si forms Si-rich precipitates in the inter-dendritic area, enriched in Nb (likely niobium silicide). Si-rich phases at grain boundaries block inward oxygen diffusion and contribute to grain refinement.
- **Property Effect:** WBYS oxidation mass gain after 200 h at 900 °C: 0.874 mg/cm², slightly lower than WBYC (0.903 mg/cm²). The WBYS alloy had an intact Al₂O₃-rich layer, whereas WBYC had a discontinuous Al₂O₃-rich layer. The original paper does not provide a direct comparison with a Si-free alloy under identical conditions; improvement is attributed to Si doping.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence:** Section 4: “the addition of Si can reduce the activity of Ti⁴⁺ ions by improving the outward of Al to form Al₂O₃-rich layer, thereby improving the oxidation resistance of the alloys”; Fig. 9(d): WBYS had an intact Al₂O₃-rich layer; Fig. 1(e): Si precipitates enriched in Nb.

## Nb (Niobium)
- **Applicable Alloy System:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys (base alloy Ti45Al8Nb).
- **Addition Amount:** 8 at.% Nb (nominal).
- **Main Function:** Improves oxidation resistance and enhances high-temperature strength and creep resistance of TiAl alloys.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Nb addition leads to S-segregation and β-segregation in as-cast microstructure. Nb is enriched in Si-rich precipitates (likely niobium silicide) in the WBYS alloy. During oxidation, an Nb-rich layer forms at the interface between the oxide scale and substrate. NbO₂ was detected in the oxide scale after oxidation.
- **Property Effect:** The base Ti45Al8Nb alloy without W, B, Y, C/Si additions exhibited severe spallation at 900 °C in prior studies, whereas the doped alloys showed no spalling. The original paper does not provide direct mechanical properties for Nb addition; property benefits are inferred from prior literature (e.g., Chen et al. reported Nb addition of 6–10 at.% improves oxidation resistance and high-temperature strength/creep resistance).
- **Potential Side Effects:** Severe S-segregation and β-segregation in as-cast state; not explicitly described as harmful in this paper.
- **Original Evidence:** Introduction: “Chen et al. reported that Nb addition (6–10 at%) to TiAl (high-Nb-containing TiAl) alloys can improve the oxidation resistance of the alloy and greatly enhance the strength and creep resistance at high temperatures”; Fig. 1(e): Nb enrichment in Si precipitates; Fig. 9: Nb-rich layer in oxide scale; XRD after oxidation shows NbO₂.

## Y-rich precipitates (YAl₂ + Y₂O₃)
- **Applicable Alloy System:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys doped with W, B, Y, C/Si.
- **Addition Amount:** Resulting from 0.1 at.% Y addition.
- **Main Function:** Promote oxide peg formation and enhance oxide scale adhesion; may also act as nucleation sites for selective Al oxidation.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Y-rich precipitates are white spots and rod-like particles of YAl₂ and Y₂O₃. During oxidation, Y-rich phases oxidize to form Y₂O₃ surrounded by Al₂O₃, which act as oxide pegs and increase contact area between oxide scale and substrate. Y-rich phases near the surface alter oxygen partial pressure, promoting selective oxidation of Al.
- **Property Effect:** No spalling after 200 h at 900 °C for both alloys. However, internal oxidation around oxidized Y-rich phases inside the substrate was observed. The original paper does not provide specific quantitative adhesion measurements.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Internal oxidation of surrounding substrate; excessive Y is reported in prior literature to be detrimental due to coarsening of Y-rich precipitations and increased lattice defects.
- **Original Evidence:** Fig. 1(c, d): white spots and rod-like Y-rich precipitates; Fig. 9(a, b), Fig. 10: oxide pegs and internal oxidation around Y-rich phase; Section 3.3: EDS point 2 in Table 2 shows Y-rich phase with high Y (10.26 at.%) on the oxide scale after 10 h.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Grain Refinement by Precipitates
- **Related Elements:** B, Y, Si (and their compounds: borides, Y-rich precipitates, silicides).
- **Typical Alloys:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys doped with W, B, Y, C/Si (WBYC and WBYS).
- **Key Compositions:** Ti45Al8Nb0.2W0.2B0.1Y0.2C and Ti45Al8Nb0.2W0.2B0.1Y0.2Si (at.%).
- **Microstructural Changes:** B-rich (line-like borides), Y-rich (white spots, rod-like), and Si-rich (irregular-shaped silicide) precipitates at grain boundaries inhibit grain boundary migration and refine the lamellar colony size. WBYS had finer α₂/γ lamellar size and smaller colony size than WBYC.
- **Property Improvements:** Finer grain structure and increased grain boundary density; higher density of grain boundaries on the surface facilitates formation of oxide pegs, improving oxide scale adhesion and spalling resistance. The paper states that fine lamellar structure is conducive to room- and high-temperature performance (citing Yan et al.). The original paper does not provide specific mechanical property values in this study.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Excessive precipitates may cause brittleness or other detrimental effects; not explicitly stated in this paper.

## Oxide Peg Formation (Oxide Scale Anchoring)
- **Related Elements:** Y (primary), also B and Si indirectly (via grain refinement).
- **Typical Alloys:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys doped with Y.
- **Key Compositions:** 0.1 at.% Y in Ti45Al8Nb-based alloys.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Y-rich precipitates oxidize and form Y₂O₃ surrounded by Al₂O₃, creating protrusions of oxide into the substrate (oxide pegs). These pegs increase the contact area between oxide scale and substrate, anchoring the scale and improving spalling resistance.
- **Property Improvements:** No spalling after 200 h at 900 °C; improved adhesion between oxide scale and substrate.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Internal oxidation around oxidized Y-rich phases inside the substrate; Y has a counterbalancing effect—while improving adhesion, it can promote internal oxidation of the substrate near the oxide scale. Excessive Y in prior studies is detrimental due to coarsening of Y-rich precipitates and increased lattice defects.

## Al₂O₃ Protective Layer Formation
- **Related Elements:** Si, W (promote Al₂O₃); C (inhibits Al₂O₃ layer integrity in long term).
- **Typical Alloys:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys.
- **Key Compositions:** Ti45Al8Nb0.2W0.2B0.1Y0.2Si (WBYS); Ti45Al8Nb0.2W0.2B0.1Y0.2C (WBYC).
- **Microstructural Changes:** Formation of an intact Al₂O₃-rich layer (WBYS) vs. discontinuous Al₂O₃-rich layer (WBYC). Si promotes Al₂O₃ formation by enhancing Al ion activity; W reduces oxygen solubility and TiO₂ growth, indirectly promoting Al₂O₃. C is initially beneficial via TiO₂ inhibition but rapidly consumed, leading to oxide clusters and a discontinuous Al₂O₃ layer after prolonged oxidation.
- **Property Improvements:** WBYS had slightly lower mass gain (0.874 mg/cm²) than WBYC (0.903 mg/cm²) after 200 h at 900 °C; intact Al₂O₃ layer reduces inward oxygen diffusion.
- **Risks and Limitations:** C addition can cause oxide cluster formation, increased oxygen transport, and spalling tendency in long-term oxidation; the fast consumption of C is harmful to long-term oxidation resistance.

## Grain Boundary Blocking of Oxygen Diffusion
- **Related Elements:** B, Y, Si (their precipitates at grain boundaries).
- **Typical Alloys:** High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys with B, Y, Si additions.
- **Key Compositions:** Same as above.
- **Microstructural Changes:** B-rich, Y-rich, and Si-rich precipitates located at grain boundaries block inward oxygen diffusion and reduce dissolved oxygen in the alloy.
- **Property Improvements:** Contribution to the excellent oxidation resistance of both alloys; prevented severe spallation observed in undoped Ti45Al8Nb alloys.
- **Risks and Limitations:** The paper does not provide quantitative measurements of oxygen diffusivity; the blocking effect is inferred from microstructural observations and literature.

# Key Conclusions
1. The addition of W, B, Y, and C/Si effectively improved the oxidation resistance of Ti45Al8Nb alloys at 900 °C. After 200 h isothermal oxidation, mass gains were 0.903 mg/cm² for WBYC and 0.874 mg/cm² for WBYS. Both alloys exhibited parabolic oxidation kinetics (n ≈ 2).
2. The oxide scales of both alloys exhibited a similar layered structure: TiO₂ layer + Al₂O₃ layer + mixed multilayer + Nb-rich layer (from outside to inside). The presence of Si promoted formation of an intact Al₂O₃-rich layer, whereas C was harmful to the integrity of the Al₂O₃-rich layer after prolonged oxidation (oxide cluster formation and discontinuous Al₂O₃ layer).
3. Y addition had a dual effect: it promoted oxide peg formation and improved scale adhesion, but Y-rich phases near the oxide scale increased oxygen partial pressure and caused internal oxidation of the surrounding substrate.
4. W addition decreased TiO₂ growth rate by W⁶⁺ annihilation of oxygen vacancies and promoted Al₂O₃ formation by reducing oxygen solubility.
5. Both WBYC and WBYS alloys exhibited outstanding oxidation resistance, better than other TiAl-based alloys from the literature at 900 °C for 100–200 h, attributed to the synergistic effects of W, B, Y, and C/Si.
6. The WBYS alloy had slightly better oxidation resistance than WBYC, mainly due to the beneficial effect of Si in promoting an intact Al₂O₃-rich layer and the absence of carbon oxide volatilization-induced oxide clusters.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Alloy system specificity:** The results are specific to high-Nb-containing TiAl alloys with trace additions of W, B, Y, and C/Si. They should not be directly extrapolated to refractory high-entropy alloys, Nb-W alloys, Mo-W alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys without further validation.
- **No mechanical property data:** The paper focuses solely on oxidation behavior; no mechanical properties (strength, ductility, creep, toughness) were measured in this study.
- **As-cast condition only:** The alloys were tested in the as-cast state. The effect of heat treatment, deformation processing, or additive manufacturing on oxidation resistance was not investigated.
- **Single oxidation temperature:** Isothermal oxidation was performed only at 900 °C; oxidation behavior at lower or higher temperatures was not tested in this study.
- **Static laboratory air only:** Oxidation tests were conducted in static laboratory air; the effects of water vapor, sulfur, or service atmospheres were not investigated.
- **Limited exposure time:** Maximum exposure time was 200 h; long-term oxidation behavior (e.g., > 1000 h) was not assessed in this study.
- **Microstructural quantification:** Grain size, colony size, and precipitate volume fractions were not quantitatively measured; only qualitative comparisons were made.
- **Elemental composition balancing:** The nominal compositions are given as Ti45Al8Nb0.2W0.2B0.1Y0.2C and Ti45Al8Nb0.2W0.2B0.1Y0.2Si (at.%); the remaining balance (presumably ~46.4 at.% Ti) is not explicitly stated in the paper. Actual compositions measured by ICP were nearly the same as nominal.
- **Reference-dependence for comparisons:** The paper relies heavily on comparisons with literature data for other TiAl alloys; direct side-by-side experimental control alloys (e.g., Ti45Al8Nb without W, B, Y, C/Si) were not tested in the same study.
