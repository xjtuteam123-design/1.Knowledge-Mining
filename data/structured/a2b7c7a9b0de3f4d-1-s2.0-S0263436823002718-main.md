# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: High-temperature mechanical behaviors of a WTaRe refractory alloy manufactured by selective electron beam melting
- **Authors**: Bang Xiao, Wenpeng Jia, Jian Wang, Lian Zhou
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106371
- **Alloy system studied**: W-Ta-Re refractory multi-principal element alloy (MPERA)
- **Material category**: Additively manufactured refractory multi-principal element alloy (SEBM-fabricated WTaRe alloy)
- **Research objective**: To investigate the high-temperature mechanical behaviors (1200 °C, 1400 °C, 1600 °C) of a selective electron beam melted WTaRe refractory alloy, and to understand the underlying strengthening mechanisms responsible for its high-temperature strength and ductility.

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy system**: W-Ta-Re ternary refractory multi-principal element alloy (MPERA)
- **Fabrication route**: Selective electron beam melting (SEBM), an additive manufacturing process
- **Comparison alloys**: As-cast Nb25Mo25Ta25W25 (WMoTaNb) and V20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20 (WMoTaNbV) refractory high-entropy alloys from Senkov et al. [2]
- **Research focus**: High-temperature compressive mechanical behavior, microstructural thermal stability, and strengthening mechanisms

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## As-Deposited Condition

- **Matrix phase**: Single body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution phase
- **Cellular structures**: Nanoscale cellular structures consisting of dislocations and solute segregation, similar to those found in additively manufactured stainless steels
- **Elemental homogeneity**: High compositional homogeneity of W, Ta, and Re (attributed to low atomic size difference δ = 1.596 and limited lattice distortion)
- **Grain size after deformation**: Mean grain size of 8.47 μm (after compression, measured from EBSD IPF)
- **Texture**: Strong 〈001〉 texture normal to the compressive loading axis after deformation

## After High-Temperature Compression (1200–1600 °C)

- **Phase stability**: Single BCC structure retained after deformation at all tested temperatures (confirmed by XRD)
- **Microstructural evolution with increasing temperature**:
  - Deformation defects become increasingly scarce with increasing temperature
  - Evidence of dynamic recovery and occasional dynamic recrystallization at higher temperatures (notably 1600 °C)
  - Deformed cellular structures and dislocation arrays persist
  - Some coarsened grains present alongside refined grains
- **Low angle grain boundaries (LAGB, <15°)**: Negligible variation after deformation
- **Sub-structure features**: Sub-grain boundaries, dislocation tangles, dislocation loops, dislocation densification

## Strengthening Mechanisms Discussed

- **Cellular structure strengthening**: Dislocation walls combined with solute segregation act as barriers to dislocation movement
- **Solid-solution related mechanisms**: High compositional homogeneity inhibits diffusion-driven weakening
- **Diffusion barriers**: High activation energies for W, Re, and Ta reduce dislocation climb and diffusional softening
- **Dislocation network strengthening**: Dislocation tangles and densification strengthen the BCC matrix
- **Dynamic recovery/recrystallization**: Partial softening at very high temperatures (1600 °C) due to annealing effects
- **Re solution-softening**: Rhenium reportedly promotes a "softening effect" (solution-softening mechanism) at room temperature, contributing to improved ductility

No carbide, oxide, boride, or silicide second phases were reported.

# Mechanical Properties

## High-Temperature Compressive Properties of As-Deposited WTaRe Alloy

| Temperature (°C) | Compressive Yield Strength (MPa) | Ultimate Compressive Strength (MPa) | Total Deformation Before Fracture (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1200 | 1183 ± 34 | 1242 ± 34 | 38.91 ± 3.23 |
| 1400 | 605 ± 24 | 822 ± 51 | 29.17 ± 4.52 |
| 1600 | 356 ± 15 | 731 ± 15 | ≥ 50 (reported as >40% in abstract) |

- **Strain rate**: 10⁻³/s
- **Atmosphere**: Ar
- **Loading mode**: Uniaxial compression
- **Hardness, elastic modulus, density, fracture toughness, creep performance, rupture life**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

## Comparison with Literature Alloys (Senkov et al. [2])

### At 1200 °C
- WTaRe yield strength: approximately two times that of as-cast WMoTaNb
- WTaRe yield strength exceeds as-cast WMoTaNbV by ~400 MPa

### At 1400 °C
- WTaRe yield strength: ~180 MPa higher than WMoTaNb
- WTaRe yield strength: ~50 MPa lower than WMoTaNbV

### At 1600 °C
- WTaRe yield strength: ~50 MPa lower than WMoTaNb; ~120 MPa lower than WMoTaNbV
- WTaRe ultimate compressive strength: exceeds WMoTaNb by ~131 MPa; exceeds WMoTaNbV by ~252 MPa

## Strain-Hardening Behavior

- At 1200 °C: Clear work-hardening process with considerable plastic deformation
- At 1400 °C: Steady-state strain hardening before fracture
- At 1600 °C: Long steady strain-hardening process; significant boosted strain hardening at ~50% strain (attributed to dislocation-cellular structure interaction)

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## W (Tungsten)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA; also a component in comparison WMoTaNb and WMoTaNbV alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not explicitly provided for WTaRe alloy (equiatomic or near-equiatomic assumed; exact at.% not stated). In comparison alloys: 25 at.% in WMoTaNb, 20 at.% in WMoTaNbV.
- **Main Function**: Primary base element; provides exceptional high-temperature strength and thermal resistance; establishes high melting point basis of the alloy
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: High activation energy for self-diffusion inhibits diffusion-controlled deformation; contributes to stabilization of BCC phase; high melting point (3410 °C) sets the temperature regime for diffusion-controlled deformation (~0.5–0.6 Tm)
- **Property Effect**: At 1600 °C, WTaRe alloy exhibits yield strength of 356 ± 15 MPa and ultimate compressive strength of 731 ± 15 MPa. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the individual contribution of W to these properties.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Intrinsic brittleness at room temperature (mentioned as an issue for refractory alloys generally); high density; severe impediment to conventional machining
- **Original Evidence**: "W, Ta, and Re have an atomic radius of 0.1430 nm, 0.1367 nm, and 0.1375 nm, respectively"; "Activation energies for W and Re outperform other elements in alloys"; melting point calculation references Section 4 Discussion.

## Ta (Tantalum)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA; also in WMoTaNb and WMoTaNbV comparison alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not explicitly provided for WTaRe alloy. In comparison alloys: 25 at.% in WMoTaNb, 20 at.% in WMoTaNbV.
- **Main Function**: Contributes to high-temperature strength and phase stability; enhances vacancy formation barrier for improved high-temperature performance
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: W–Ta system owns a higher activation barrier for forming monovacancy than W–Re system; Ta introduction positively increases probability of monovacancy generation in W (with Re); limits lattice distortion due to size similarity with W (0.1367 nm vs. 0.1430 nm)
- **Property Effect**: At 1600 °C, WTaRe alloy exhibits yield strength of 356 ± 15 MPa. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the individual contribution of Ta to these properties.
- **Potential Side Effects**: High activation energy for Ta diffusion may contribute to atom-exchange barriers; not explicitly discussed as causing negative effects
- **Original Evidence**: "Investigation on W-Ta/Re alloy systems suggests that Ta and Re in W can positively introduce a higher possibility for the generation of monovacancy. Moreover, the W–Ta system owns a higher activation barrier in forming monovacancy for accelerating diffusion than the W–Re system."

## Re (Rhenium)

- **Applicable Alloy System**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Not explicitly provided for WTaRe alloy
- **Main Function**: Promotes "softening effect" (solution-softening) at room temperature, enhancing room-temperature ductility; improves high-temperature strength via high diffusion barrier; stabilizes BCC phase
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: High activation energy for diffusion inhibits diffusion-controlled deformation; Re in W increases probability of monovacancy generation; solution-softening mechanism may involve changes in dislocation core structure or Peierls barrier; participates in Re ↔ W ↔ Ta ↔ Re site-exchange (limited Kirkendall effect)
- **Property Effect**: WTaRe alloy exhibits total deformation ≥50% at 1600 °C; high-temperature compressive properties exceed WMoTaNb in ultimate strength at all tested temperatures. From previous work (Ref [14]): exceptional synergy of ductility-strength at 1200 °C, 1400 °C, 1600 °C, and ambient temperature. The original paper does not provide specific numerical values for the individual contribution of Re to room-temperature ductility.
- **Potential Side Effects**: May contribute to Kirkendall effect during high-temperature deformation (though described as occurring in small quantity); solution-softening may reduce strength at room temperature (trade-off between room-temperature ductility and strength)
- **Original Evidence**: "Rhenium with a high homogeneity in this alloy substantially promoted the 'softening effect' of microstructure with its postulated solution-softening mechanism [19]". Reference [19] is Luo, Jacobson, Shin, "Solution softening mechanism of iridium and rhenium in tungsten at room temperature."

## W+Ta+Re Combination

- **Applicable Alloy System**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA fabricated by SEBM
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Exact composition not stated; three elements W, Ta, Re in a ternary MPERA
- **Main Function**: Combined high-temperature strength, microstructural stability, and room-temperature ductility; printable refractory alloy with excellent high-temperature mechanical behaviors
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Low atomic size difference (δ = 1.596) promises limited lattice distortion, enabling inherent mutual solubility and high compositional homogeneity; cellular structures (dislocation + solute walls) provide robust barriers to dislocation movement at high temperature; high activation energies for diffusion of all three elements inhibit diffusional softening; strong 〈001〉 texture after deformation
- **Property Effect**: Yield strength of 1183 ± 34 MPa at 1200 °C, 605 ± 24 MPa at 1400 °C, 356 ± 15 MPa at 1600 °C; ultimate compressive strength of 1242 ± 34 MPa at 1200 °C, 822 ± 51 MPa at 1400 °C, 731 ± 15 MPa at 1600 °C; total deformation 38.91 ± 3.23% at 1200 °C, 29.17 ± 4.52% at 1400 °C, ≥50% at 1600 °C; microstructural stability maintained after 1600 °C deformation (single BCC retained)
- **Potential Side Effects**: Drastic strength decrease from 1200 °C to 1400 °C (yield strength drops from 1183 MPa to 605 MPa, ~49% decrease); possible oxidation susceptibility at high temperatures (hypothesized but not confirmed); partial dynamic recrystallization at 1600 °C
- **Original Evidence**: Table 1 provides all high-temperature compressive properties; Fig. 3 shows homogeneous distribution of W, Ta, Re after deformation at all temperatures; Fig. 4 confirms single BCC solid solution; calculated δ = 1.596 in Section 4.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Cellular Structure Strengthening (Dislocation + Solute Networks)

- **Related Elements**: W, Ta, Re (solute segregation at dislocation cell walls)
- **Typical Alloys**: SEBMed WTaRe alloy; concept analogous to cellular structures in additively manufactured stainless steels
- **Key Compositions**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA (exact composition not stated)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Nanoscale cellular structures consisting of organized dislocation boundaries and solute segregation; deformed cellular structures persist after compression at up to 1600 °C; severe shape alteration of cellular structures under high-temperature uniaxial loads; dislocation-cellular structure dissociation at high strains (~50%) leads to enhanced strain hardening
- **Property Improvements**: Provides robust barriers to dislocation climbing and movement at high temperatures; contributes to sustained strain hardening at 1600 °C; supports superior durability at 1600 °C (≥50% total deformation); enhances high-temperature strength stability
- **Risks and Limitations**: Dynamic recovery at high temperatures can annihilate newly generated dislocations, leaving defect-free regions where substructures cannot reach; cellular structures may gradually degrade at very high temperatures due to diffusion; partial dissociation at very high strains
- **Original Evidence**: "Cellular structures consisting of dislocations and solutes can play an important role in deformation at high temperatures"; "The unified hindering walls established by the previously formed and well-organized dislocation boundaries and solutes, pose overwhelmingly robust barriers to the intrinsically hard diffusion as well as climbing and movement of newly generated or already existing dislocations."

## Solid-Solution Strengthening via High Compositional Homogeneity

- **Related Elements**: W, Ta, Re
- **Typical Alloys**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA
- **Key Compositions**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA (exact composition not stated)
- **Microstructural Changes**: High homogeneity of W, Ta, Re distribution retained after compression at 1200 °C, 1400 °C, and 1600 °C; single BCC solid solution phase confirmed by XRD after all test temperatures; low atomic size difference (δ = 1.596) limits lattice distortion
- **Property Improvements**: High yield strength at 1200 °C (1183 ± 34 MPa, approximately two times that of WMoTaNb); ultimate compressive strength greater than both WMoTaNb and WMoTaNbV at all tested temperatures
- **Risks and Limitations**: Low lattice distortion may limit intrinsic solid-solution strengthening compared to high-δ alloys; diffusional behaviors can still play a deleterious role in softening at high temperatures; may not fully explain the mechanical behavior without considering dislocation-based mechanisms
- **Original Evidence**: "Such high-level compositional homogeneity and stability directly correspond to the inhibited diffusion behaviors during high-temperature deformation."

## Diffusion-Controlled Strengthening (High Diffusion Barriers)

- **Related Elements**: W, Ta, Re (all three with intrinsically high activation energies for diffusion)
- **Typical Alloys**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA
- **Key Compositions**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA (exact composition not stated)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Suppressed diffusion at high temperatures; limited vacancy formation and diffusion; minimal Kirkendall effect; high stability of the BCC solid solution; defect-free regions formed only by dynamic recovery/recrystallization at 1600 °C
- **Property Improvements**: Maintains significant strength even at 1600 °C (yield strength 356 ± 15 MPa, ultimate compressive strength 731 ± 15 MPa); microstructural stability maintained (single BCC retained, mean grain size 8.47 μm after deformation)
- **Risks and Limitations**: 0.5–0.6 Tm range (~1517–1875 °C) indicates that diffusion may still aggravate softening at 1600 °C; Kirkendall effect still occurs in small quantities; partial dynamic recrystallization observed at 1600 °C; may limit oxidation resistance at high temperatures (not confirmed in this study)
- **Original Evidence**: "Activation energies for W and Re outperform other elements in alloys, following a quite high activation energy of Ta"; "The as-calculated ~0.5–0.6 Tm is ~1517–1875 °C..., which indicates that diffusion may aggravate the softening process during deformation at 1600 °C."

## Dislocation Network/Tangling Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: W, Ta, Re (dislocations in BCC matrix)
- **Typical Alloys**: SEBMed WTaRe refractory MPERA
- **Key Compositions**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA (exact composition not stated)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Dislocation arrays, dislocation tangles, dislocation loops observed in as-deformed microstructure; dislocations emitted from cellular structures glide and climb under high temperature loading; dislocation densification in BCC matrix; sub-grain boundary movement at 1600 °C suggests dynamic recrystallization
- **Property Improvements**: Enhanced strain hardening at ~50% strain at 1600 °C (boosted strain hardening event); sustained plastic deformation (≥50%) at 1600 °C; strong 〈001〉 texture development during compression
- **Risks and Limitations**: Dynamic recovery at high temperatures can annihilate dislocations; defect-free regions observed where dislocation substructures cannot reach; potential loss of work-hardening capacity at higher temperatures
- **Original Evidence**: "The dislocations repelled from the cellular structures glide and climb under driving force from the remained high-temperature regime and loads"; "The presence of dislocation tangles and densification also strengthened the BCC matrix."

## Re-Induced Solution Softening (Room-Temperature Ductility Enhancement)

- **Related Elements**: Re
- **Typical Alloys**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA; also reported in W-Re binary systems (Luo et al. [19])
- **Key Compositions**: WTaRe ternary refractory MPERA (exact composition not stated)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Re homogenously distributed in BCC matrix; solution-softening mechanism (from literature: changes in dislocation core structure, reduced Peierls barrier, or altered stacking fault energy)
- **Property Improvements**: Exceptional room-temperature ductility (from Ref [14], not detailed in this paper); contributes to overcoming strength-ductility trade-off in MPERAs at ambient and elevated temperatures; printable refractory alloy with both excellent high-temperature and room-temperature mechanical behaviors
- **Risks and Limitations**: Postulated mechanism, not directly verified in this study; solution-softening may reduce room-temperature strength (trade-off); mechanism from W-Re binary system may not fully translate to ternary WTaRe
- **Original Evidence**: "Rhenium with a high homogeneity in this alloy substantially promoted the 'softening effect' of microstructure with its postulated solution-softening mechanism [19]."

## Additively-Manufacturing-Oriented Composition Control (Printable Refractory Alloy Design)

- **Related Elements**: W, Ta, Re combination
- **Typical Alloys**: SEBMed WTaRe refractory MPERA
- **Key Compositions**: Ternary WTaRe MPERA (exact composition not stated); comparison with WMoTaNb and WMoTaNbV systems demonstrates the benefit of Ta and Re substitutions for Mo and Nb/V
- **Microstructural Changes**: Nanoscale cellular structures typical of additive manufacturing; high preheating temperature (1100 °C) and post-deposition thermal compensation (850–900 °C) enable formation of dislocation-based cellular structures without cracking; homogeneous elemental distribution
- **Property Improvements**: Printable alloy without room-temperature brittleness issue (common in WMoTaNb systems); superior high-temperature ultimate compressive strength compared to WMoTaNb and WMoTaNbV; excellent strength-ductility combination at 1200–1600 °C
- **Risks and Limitations**: Higher cost of Re; lower yield strength at 1600 °C compared to WMoTaNbV; oxidation susceptibility at high temperature (hypothesized); process parameter sensitivity
- **Original Evidence**: "Here we employ a selective electron beam melting (SEBM) process to fabricate a WTaRe MPERAs, which sequentially displayed an exceptional synergy of ductility-strength at 1200 °C, 1400 °C, 1600 °C, and ambient temperature."

# Key Conclusions

1. **Successful fabrication**: A WTaRe refractory multi-principal element alloy was successfully fabricated by selective electron beam melting (SEBM) with high-temperature preheating (1100 °C) and post-deposition thermal compensation (850–900 °C).

2. **Superior high-temperature mechanical properties**: The as-deposited WTaRe alloy exhibits exceptional high-temperature compressive properties:
   - At 1200 °C: yield strength 1183 ± 34 MPa, ultimate compressive strength 1242 ± 34 MPa, total deformation 38.91 ± 3.23%
   - At 1400 °C: yield strength 605 ± 24 MPa, ultimate compressive strength 822 ± 51 MPa, total deformation 29.17 ± 4.52%
   - At 1600 °C: yield strength 356 ± 15 MPa, ultimate compressive strength 731 ± 15 MPa, total deformation ≥50%

3. **Comparison with conventional refractory HEAs**: The WTaRe alloy demonstrates superior ultimate compressive strength compared to as-cast WMoTaNb and WMoTaNbV at all tested temperatures, with particularly notable advantages at 1200 °C (yield strength is approximately two times WMoTaNb) and 1600 °C (ultimate compressive strength exceeds WMoTaNb by ~131 MPa and WMoTaNbV by ~252 MPa).

4. **Microstructural thermal stability**: The WTaRe alloy maintains a single BCC solid solution phase with homogeneous elemental distribution after deformation at temperatures up to 1600 °C; no new phases are formed.

5. **Primary strengthening factors**: The strong high-temperature strength originates from (a) large diffusion barriers and high melting points of all three elements, and (b) cellular structures with complex dislocation arrangements that enhance high-temperature strength.

6. **Unexpected strain hardening at 1600 °C**: A boosted strain-hardening event at ~50% strain is attributed to the interaction and eventual dissociation of dislocations from cellular structures, followed by dislocation glide, climb, and tangling.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Exact composition not provided**: The paper does not explicitly state the atomic percentages or weight percentages of W, Ta, and Re in the WTaRe alloy. Refer to Ref [14] (Xiao et al., Additive Manufacturing 54 (2022) 102738) for detailed composition information.
- **Room-temperature data only from previous work**: Room-temperature mechanical properties are referenced from Ref [14] but not detailed in this paper.
- **Compression testing only**: Mechanical testing is limited to uniaxial compression; no tensile, creep, or fracture toughness testing was performed.
- **No systematic composition variation**: Only a single WTaRe composition was studied; the effects of varying W, Ta, or Re contents are not explored.
- **Oxidation behavior not characterized**: The paper hypothesizes that oxidation may contribute to the strength decrease at 1400 °C, but no oxidation testing or characterization was performed.
- **Room-temperature ductility not fully explained**: The solution-softening mechanism of Re is described as "postulated" and is based on literature from W-Re binary systems.
- **Applicable scope**: The findings are applicable to additively manufactured W-Ta-Re refractory alloys for high-temperature structural applications, particularly those fabricated by selective electron beam melting with high-temperature preheating regimes.
