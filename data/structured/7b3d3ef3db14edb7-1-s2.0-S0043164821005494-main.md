# Paper Metadata

- **Title:** Microstructure and tribological properties of TiZrV₀.₅Nb₀.₅Alₓ refractory high entropy alloys at elevated temperature
- **Authors:** Xuhui Pei, Yin Du, Xiaoxiao Hao, Hanming Wang, Qing Zhou, Hongxing Wu, Haifeng Wang
- **Journal:** Wear
- **Year:** 2022 (Volume 488–489, Article 204166; available online 28 October 2021)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.wear.2021.204166
- **Alloy system:** TiZrV₀.₅Nb₀.₅Alₓ refractory high entropy alloy (RHEA)
- **Research objective:** To solve the problems of high density, poor oxidation resistance, and poor wear resistance of refractory high entropy alloys at elevated temperatures by adding Al, and to investigate the effect of Al addition on microstructure, mechanical properties, and tribological behavior at temperatures from RT to 800 °C.
- **Material category:** Refractory high entropy alloy (RHEA)

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Base alloy system:** TiZrV₀.₅Nb₀.₅ refractory high entropy alloy with varying Al additions.
- **Alloy designations:** Al0 (x = 0), Al0.25 (x = 0.25), Al0.5 (x = 0.5), Al0.75 (x = 0.75)
- **Material class:** Low-density refractory high entropy alloy with BCC matrix and Laves phase precipitation upon Al addition.

**Elemental roles in the alloy:**
- **Ti:** Matrix constituent; forms TiO₂ on worn surfaces.
- **Zr:** Matrix constituent; enriches in Laves phase; forms ZrO₂ on worn surfaces.
- **V:** Matrix constituent; enriches in Laves phase; forms VOₓ on worn surfaces.
- **Nb:** Matrix constituent; enriches in BCC phase; forms Nb₂O₅ on worn surfaces.
- **Al:** Added up to x = 0.75; promotes Laves phase formation; forms Al₂O₃ on worn surfaces; reduces density.

- Al0 alloy: ~6.11 g/cm³
- Al0.25 alloy: ~5.85 g/cm³
- Al0.5 alloy: ~5.63 g/cm³
- Al0.75 alloy: ~5.44 g/cm³

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Preparation method:** Arc melting of high-purity elements (99.95 wt%) in a water-cooled copper crucible under a Ti-gettered Ar atmosphere.
- **Remelting:** All ingots were flipped and remelted repeatedly to ensure homogeneity.
- **Casting:** Melted alloys were poured into a water-cooled copper mold to produce plate-like cast alloys of 6 × 20 × 50 mm³.
- **Homogenization annealing:** 1100 °C for 24 h in a vacuum environment.
- **No further thermomechanical processing** such as rolling, forging, or aging is reported.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

**Phase constitution as a function of Al addition:**
- **Al0 alloy:** Single BCC solid solution phase. Average grain size of millimeter level with uniform elemental distribution.
- **Al0.25 alloy:** BCC matrix + chain-like Laves phase precipitating at grain boundaries and inside grains. Laves phase is enriched in Zr, V, and Al. Laves phase volume fraction = 17.9% ± 0.33%.
- **Al0.5 alloy:** BCC matrix + increased volume fraction of Laves phase = 36.4% ± 1.1%.
- **Al0.75 alloy:** BCC matrix + highest Laves phase volume fraction = 40.2% ± 1.2%.

**Phase identification:**
- BCC matrix is enriched with Ti and Nb.
- Laves phase has a crystal structure verified by SAED; enriched in V, Zr, and Al.

**Microstructural features:**
- Al0.25 alloy exhibits a unique microstructure of acicular Laves phase distributed uniformly within the BCC matrix at submicron scale.
- With higher Al content (Al0.5 and Al0.75), the Laves phase grows to a scale of ten microns, causing reduced plasticity and poor crack resistance.
- The submicron-scale Laves phase in Al0.25 confines crack propagation via a high density of hetero-interfaces.

**Grain size:**
- Al0 alloy: millimeter-level average grain size.
- Other alloys: equiaxed grains with Laves phase at boundaries and inside grains; specific grain sizes for Al-containing alloys are not explicitly quantified.

**Elemental segregation:**
- Zr, V, and Al segregate to the Laves phase.
- Ti and Nb segregate to the BCC matrix.

# Mechanical Properties

**Hardness (Vickers, 2 N load, 5 s dwell time):**
- Al0 alloy: 329.3 HV
- Al0.25 alloy: 413.4 HV
- Al0.5 alloy: 473.5 HV
- Al0.75 alloy: 462.6 HV
- Explanation: Hardness increases from Al0 to Al0.5 due to the increasing volume fraction of hard, brittle Laves phase; Al0.75 maintains hardness within error because the Laves volume fraction is comparable to Al0.5.

**Theoretical melting points:**
- Al0: 1900 °C
- Al0.25: 1804 °C
- Al0.5: 1723 °C
- Al0.75: 1652 °C
- Implication: All alloys have excellent softening resistance below 1000 °C (0.6 × T_m rule).

**Tensile, compressive, yield strength, elongation, fracture toughness, creep:** Not explicitly stated in the original paper.

**Density:**
- Al0: ~6.11 g/cm³
- Al0.25: ~5.85 g/cm³
- Al0.5: ~5.63 g/cm³
- Al0.75: ~5.44 g/cm³
- Density decreases linearly with increasing Al content.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

**General trend:**
- Below 400 °C: mass gain of all four alloys almost does not change with temperature; wear behavior is dominated by mechanical wear, not oxidation wear.
- Above 600 °C: oxidation rates increase rapidly. Oxidation resistance shows a positive correlation with Al addition.

**Al0 alloy (no Al):**
- Exhibits catastrophic oxidation above 600 °C.
- Oxide tribo-layers are loose, complex metal oxides composed mainly of Zr and Nb oxides (with some Ti and V oxides). The layer is not chemically dense and contains pores. It provides limited protection.
- EDX shows the tribo-layer is thicker than 5 μm and rough at 800 °C.

**Al-containing alloys (Al0.25–Al0.75):**
- At temperatures above 600 °C, a stable, dense oxide glazed-layer forms on the surface before wear.
- The oxide layer is enriched in Al and O (primarily Al₂O₃), and has a BCC structure (as shown by SAED in Al0.25 at 600 °C).
- The layer is composed of equally distributed matrix element oxides with no oxide segregation or voids.
- This dense oxide layer has high hardness and protects the alloy matrix by completely isolating contact between the Al₂O₃ ball and the alloy matrix.
- The oxide glazed-layer undergoes strain hardening (dislocation cells observed) during sliding.

**XPS analysis of worn surfaces (all alloys):**
- Ti, Zr, V, and Nb form ZrO₂, TiO₂, Nb₂O₅, and VOₓ. The types of tribo-oxides are independent of testing temperature.
- Al₂O₃ peak intensity is weak between RT and 400 °C, indicating a weak correlation between wear behavior and aluminum oxide formation at low temperature.

**High-temperature embrittlement:** Not explicitly discussed in relation to the base matrix. However, the Laves phase is characterized as hard and brittle, and higher Al content increases Laves phase volume fraction, leading to reduced crack resistance.

**Oxygen/nitrogen embrittlement or grain-boundary oxidation:** Not explicitly addressed in the original paper.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Al (Aluminum)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** TiZrV₀.₅Nb₀.₅Alₓ refractory high entropy alloy (RHEA)
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 (nominal). Measured Al in BCC phase: 4.75 at.% (Al0.25) to 15.54 at.% (Al0.75); measured Al in Laves phase: 8.05 at.% (Al0.25) to 20.41 at.% (Al0.75).
- **Main Function:**
  - Reduces alloy density.
  - Promotes Laves phase precipitation in the BCC matrix.
  - Enhances oxidation resistance and high-temperature wear resistance via formation of dense Al₂O₃-containing oxide glazed-layer.
  - Increases hardness (up to Al0.5).
- **Microstructural Mechanism:**
  - Al addition promotes the formation of a Laves phase enriched in Zr, V, and Al at grain boundaries and inside grains.
  - The Laves phase volume fraction increases with Al addition: 17.9% ± 0.33% (Al0.25), 36.4% ± 1.1% (Al0.5), 40.2% ± 1.2% (Al0.75).
  - At high Al content, Laves phase grows from submicron acicular morphology to ten-micron scale, causing brittleness.
  - Above 600 °C, Al promotes formation of a dense, stable Al₂O₃-containing oxide layer with BCC structure that isolates the alloy matrix from the counterface.
- **Property Effect:**
  - Density decreases from ~6.11 g/cm³ (Al0) to ~5.44 g/cm³ (Al0.75), linearly.
  - Hardness: 329.3 HV (Al0) → 413.4 HV (Al0.25) → 473.5 HV (Al0.5) → 462.6 HV (Al0.75).
  - Wear rate at 200 °C: Al0.25 wear rate is one order of magnitude lower than Al0; Al0.75 exhibits abnormally high wear rate at RT–400 °C.
  - At 600–800 °C, Al-containing alloys show wear rates as low as ~10⁻⁶ mm³ N⁻¹ m⁻¹.
- **Potential Side Effects:**
  - Excessive Al addition (x ≥ 0.5) increases Laves phase volume fraction and coarsens Laves phase to ten-micron scale, causing poor crack resistance, brittle fracture, and higher wear rate at RT–400 °C.
  - Hard alumina particles in the tribo-layer can hinder plastic flow and cause stress concentration, leading to cracking and spallation of the tribo-layer below 200 °C.
  - Theoretical melting point decreases from 1900 °C (Al0) to 1652 °C (Al0.75).
- **Original Evidence:**
  - “The increase in Al addition could promote precipitation of Laves phase in the BCC matrix, thus regulation the mechanical properties.” (Abstract)
  - “The stable dense oxide layer has a high hardness, and the layer could protect the alloy matrix by completely isolating the contact between the alumina ball and the alloy matrix” (Section 3.5)
  - “the addition of excessive Al element would have a negative impact on the wear resistance of the alloy below 200 °C” (Section 3.4)
  - Table 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 5, Fig. 15

## Laves Phase (Zr, V, Al-rich intermetallic)

- **Applicable Alloy System:** TiZrV₀.₅Nb₀.₅Alₓ RHEA with x ≥ 0.25.
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range:** Volume fraction 17.9% ± 0.33% (Al0.25), 36.4% ± 1.1% (Al0.5), 40.2% ± 1.2% (Al0.75). Composition: Zr-rich (31.62–32.92 at.%), V-rich (23.22–30.10 at.%), Al-rich (8.05–20.41 at.%).
- **Main Function:** Precipitation strengthening agent; increases hardness.
- **Microstructural Mechanism:** Precipitates at grain boundaries and inside equiaxed grains. At submicron acicular scale (Al0.25), gives high density of hetero-interfaces that confine crack propagation and enable homogeneous plastic deformation. At ten-micron scale (Al0.5, Al0.75), causes stress concentration and brittle fracture.
- **Property Effect:** Hardness increases from 329.3 HV (Al0, 0% Laves) to 473.5 HV (Al0.5, 36.4% Laves). At RT–400 °C, Al0.25 with submicron Laves has the lowest wear rate; Al0.5 and Al0.75 show high wear rates due to poor crack resistance.
- **Potential Side Effects:** Hard and brittle; reduces crack resistance and plasticity when volume fraction is high and phase is coarse.
- **Original Evidence:** “the Laves phase has the characteristics of hard and brittle comparing with the BCC phase” (Section 3.1); Fig. 2, Fig. 3; Table 1.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Ti, Zr, V, Nb in the BCC matrix.
- **Typical Alloys:** TiZrV₀.₅Nb₀.₅ (Al0) single-phase BCC RHEA.
- **Key Compositions:** Ti 31.99 at.%, Zr 31.31 at.%, V 17.55 at.%, Nb 19.15 at.%.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Single BCC phase with uniform elemental distribution and millimeter-level grain size.
- **Property Improvements:** Hardness 329.3 HV; theoretical melting point 1900 °C.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Low hardness compared to alloys with Laves phase; catastrophic oxidation above 600 °C and poor high-temperature wear resistance.
- **Original Evidence:** Fig. 1 XRD; Fig. 2a; Fig. 3b hardness data.

## Laves-Phase Precipitation Strengthening

- **Related Elements:** Al, Zr, V (Laves-forming elements) with Ti and Nb partitioning to BCC matrix.
- **Typical Alloys:** TiZrV₀.₅Nb₀.₅Alₓ (x = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75).
- **Key Compositions:** Laves phase volume fractions: 17.9% ± 0.33% (Al0.25), 36.4% ± 1.1% (Al0.5), 40.2% ± 1.2% (Al0.75).
- **Microstructural Changes:** Chain-like Laves phase precipitates at grain boundaries and inside grains. Al0.25 exhibits submicron acicular Laves phase; Al0.5 and Al0.75 feature coarsened Laves phase.
- **Property Improvements:** Hardness increases from 329.3 HV (Al0) to 473.5 HV (Al0.5). At RT–400 °C, submicron acicular Laves phase in Al0.25 improves crack resistance and wear resistance (lowest wear rate among all alloys).
- **Risks and Limitations:** Coarse Laves phase (Al0.5, Al0.75) reduces plasticity and crack resistance, leading to brittle fracture and fatigue cracking of tribo-layers at RT–400 °C. Excessive Al addition negatively impacts wear resistance below 200 °C.
- **Original Evidence:** Fig. 2, Fig. 3; Table 1; Fig. 5 wear rate data.

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening (via Laves Phase Precipitation)

- **Related Elements:** Al, Zr, V.
- **Typical Alloys:** Al-containing TiZrV₀.₅Nb₀.₅Alₓ RHEAs.
- **Key Compositions:** Not explicitly quantified.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Laves phase precipitates at grain boundaries, creating a high density of hetero-interfaces in Al0.25.
- **Property Improvements:** In Al0.25, submicron-scale hetero-interfaces confine crack propagation and improve crack resistance during wear at RT–400 °C.
- **Risks and Limitations:** With excessive Al, boundary Laves phase coarsens, reducing crack resistance.
- **Original Evidence:** “the submicron scale confinement on the Laves phase due to the high density of hetero-interface in the microstructure can significantly improve crack resistance of the Al0.25 alloy” (Section 3.5).

## Oxidation-Resistant Alloying (Al Addition)

- **Related Elements:** Al, O.
- **Typical Alloys:** TiZrV₀.₅Nb₀.₅Alₓ RHEAs (x = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75).
- **Key Compositions:** Al up to x = 0.75 nominal; measured Al in Laves phase up to 20.41 at.%.
- **Microstructural Changes:** Above 600 °C, a dense, BCC-structured oxide glazed-layer forms on the surface before wear. The layer contains Al₂O₃ and other matrix element oxides, evenly distributed without segregation or voids. The layer undergoes strain hardening with dislocation cell formation.
- **Property Improvements:** Wear rates decrease to ~10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵ mm³ N⁻¹ m⁻¹ at 600–800 °C. The Al-containing alloys show nearly no wear at 600 °C and 800 °C (Al0.25 and Al0.5). The dense oxide layer isolates the alloy matrix from the Al₂O₃ ball.
- **Risks and Limitations:** Without Al (Al0 alloy), catastrophic oxidation occurs above 600 °C, resulting in porous, non-protective tribo-layers and high wear rates.
- **Original Evidence:** Fig. 15 mass gain curves; Fig. 12 TEM cross-section; Fig. 13–14 XPS spectra.

# Key Conclusions

1. **Phase evolution:** TiZrV₀.₅Nb₀.₅ (Al0) is a single BCC solid solution. Al addition (x ≥ 0.25) promotes precipitation of a Zr-, V-, and Al-rich Laves phase in the BCC matrix. The Laves phase volume fraction increases from 17.9% ± 0.33% (Al0.25) to 40.2% ± 1.2% (Al0.75).

2. **Density reduction:** Density decreases linearly from ~6.11 g/cm³ (Al0) to ~5.44 g/cm³ (Al0.75) with increasing Al addition.

3. **Hardness improvement:** Hardness increases from 329.3 HV (Al0) to 473.5 HV (Al0.5), then slightly decreases to 462.6 HV (Al0.75). The increase correlates with Laves phase volume fraction.

4. **Wear behavior below 400 °C:** Wear is dominated by mechanical wear, not oxidation wear. Al0.25 exhibits the lowest wear rate due to its unique microstructure of submicron acicular Laves phase uniformly distributed in the BCC matrix, which provides both high strength and good crack resistance. Al0 has the highest wear rate due to lowest hardness. Al0.5 and Al0.75 exhibit high wear rates due to poor crack resistance from excessive coarse Laves phase.

5. **Wear behavior above 600 °C:** The dominant wear mechanism changes from mechanical wear to oxidation wear. Al-containing alloys form a dense, stable, BCC-structured oxide glazed-layer (enriched in Al₂O₃) that protects the alloy matrix, resulting in very low wear rates (~10⁻⁶ mm³ N⁻¹ m⁻¹). Al0 undergoes catastrophic oxidation with loose, porous tribo-layers and poor wear resistance.

6. **Optimal alloy:** Al0.25 provides the best combination of low density, high hardness, good crack resistance, and excellent wear resistance over a wide temperature range (RT–800 °C).

7. **Excessive Al addition:** Al0.75 exhibits abnormally high wear rate at RT–400 °C due to coarse Laves phase causing brittle fracture and stress concentration, and hard Al₂O₃ particles in the tribo-layer causing additional wear. However, at 800 °C, Al0.75 reaches a wear rate comparable to Al0.25 and Al0.5.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

**Limitations:**
- The paper focuses only on wear and limited mechanical properties (hardness, density); no tensile, compressive, yield strength, elongation, fracture toughness, or creep data are reported.
- No detailed grain size quantification for Al-containing alloys beyond qualitative descriptions.
- The specific oxide composition of the dense glaze layer is inferred from EDX and XPS but not fully crystallographically quantified (only the BCC structure is identified by SAED).
- Oxidation tests were performed only up to 800 °C under dynamic heating (3 K/min) in air, not under long-term isothermal conditions.
- The effect of Al on specific mechanical properties such as strength and ductility at room and elevated temperatures is not measured; only hardness is reported.
- The paper does not investigate possible oxygen/nitrogen embrittlement or grain-boundary oxidation in the alloy matrix.

**Applicable scope:**
- Refractory high entropy alloys with BCC matrix and Laves phase precipitation for high-temperature wear applications up to 800 °C.
- Alloy design strategies using Al addition to reduce density and improve high-temperature oxidation and wear resistance.
- The principle of submicron-scale Laves phase confinement to improve crack resistance in dual-phase RHEAs.
- The results may be applicable to other low-density refractory multi-principal element alloys, especially those with Ti, Zr, V, Nb, and Al.

**Not explicitly stated in the original paper:**
- Any additive manufacturing processing.
- Any mechanical property data other than hardness.
- Any specific numerical values for oxidation mass gain (only qualitative curves shown in Fig. 15).
- Any data on impurity contents (C, O, N, H) in the bulk alloys before wear or oxidation tests.
