# Paper Metadata
- **Paper title**: Achieving excellent strength-ductility synergy via cold rolling-annealing in Al-containing refractory high-entropy alloys
- **Authors**: Yaxi Ma, Lixin Sun, Yang Zhang, Zhongwu Zhang
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106263
- **Alloy system studied**: Al-containing refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA), Zr-Ti-Nb-Al-Ta system
- **Research objective**: To investigate the effect of cold rolling-annealing technology on the microstructure and mechanical properties of Al-containing RHEAs, specifically to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off and prevent brittle Laves phase formation.
- **Material category**: Refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) with B2 nanoprecipitates in a BCC matrix

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Alloy system**: Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅ (at.%) refractory high-entropy alloy
- **Material category**: Refractory high-entropy alloy with Al addition, exhibiting BCC matrix + B2 nanoprecipitates structure
- **Sub-category**: Al-containing RHEA with ordered B2 precipitates
- **Research object**: To optimize strength-ductility synergy through cold rolling followed by short-time annealing, avoiding brittle Laves phase formation.

# Alloy Composition
- **Nominal composition**: Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅ (at.%)
- **Element list**: Zr (35 at.%), Ti (30 at.%), Nb (20 at.%), Al (10 at.%), Ta (5 at.%)
- **Raw material purity**: > 99.95 wt%
- **Impurity levels (C, O, N, H)**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Two alloy samples (heat treatment variants)**:
  - R850: cold-rolled, annealed at 850 °C for 5 min, water-quenched
  - R1050: cold-rolled, annealed at 1050 °C for 5 min, water-quenched
- **Dendritic and interdendritic compositions** (Table 1):
  - R850 IR (interdendritic region): Zr 39.1, Ti 28.7, Nb 17.5, Al 11.3, Ta 3.4 (at.%)
  - R850 DR (dendritic region): Zr 33.9, Ti 29.8, Nb 22.5, Al 8.5, Ta 5.3 (at.%)
  - R1050 IR: Zr 39.0, Ti 30.2, Nb 17.5, Al 10.1, Ta 3.1 (at.%)
  - R1050 DR: Zr 34.0, Ti 29.8, Nb 23.0, Al 8.0, Ta 5.3 (at.%)
- **Elemental segregation**: Nb and Ta (high melting point) are enriched in DR; Zr and Al (low melting point) are enriched in IR; Ti is almost uniformly distributed between DR and IR.

# Processing and Heat Treatment
- **Preparation method**: Vacuum arc melting in high-purity argon atmosphere, melted six times for chemical homogeneity
- **Casting**: Suction casting to a rod of φ 20 mm × 300 mm
- **Deformation**: Multipass cold rolling at room temperature to a final thickness of 2 mm; reduction per pass = 0.1–0.2 mm
- **Annealing**:
  - R850: sealed in argon-filled vacuum (10⁻² Torr) quartz tubes, annealed at 850 °C for 5 min, water-quenched
  - R1050: same as above but annealed at 1050 °C for 5 min, water-quenched
- **Heat treatment temperatures**: 850 °C and 1050 °C
- **Heat treatment time**: 5 min
- **Cooling method**: Water quenching
- **Deformation amount**: Cold rolling from φ 20 mm rod to 2 mm thick sheet; exact total reduction not explicitly stated in the original paper.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
- **Matrix phase**: Single-phase BCC, confirmed by XRD. Lattice constants: R850 = 0.3385 nm; R1050 = 0.3376 nm.
- **Second phase / precipitates**: Ordered B2 nanoprecipitates dispersed in the BCC matrix, confirmed by SAED, HR-TEM, and DF imaging. No other phases detected.
- **Precipitate–matrix relationship**: Coherent, cocontinuous mixture on the nanometer scale; no formation of brittle Laves phase after short-time annealing.
- **Precipitate size**:
  - R850: ~1.67 ± 0.05 nm
  - R1050: ~2.35 ± 0.05 nm
- **Precipitate volume fraction**:
  - R850: ~0.39 ± 0.01
  - R1050: ~0.42 ± 0.01
- **Grain size**:
  - R850: average ~4.03 ± 0.05 μm
  - R1050: average ~22.54 ± 0.05 μm
- **Grain boundary features**: Not explicitly described in terms of grain boundary character; grain refinement is noted as a strengthening contributor.
- **Dislocation structure**:
  - R850: uniform high-density dislocation entanglements and dislocation accumulation bands
  - R1050: only dislocation accumulation bands; dense dislocation cells disappear
- **Dislocation density** (calculated by Williamson–Hall method):
  - R850: ρ = 1.351 × 10¹⁴ m⁻²
  - R1050: ρ = 0.743 × 10¹⁴ m⁻²
- **Elemental segregation**: Nb and Ta enriched in dendritic regions (DR); Zr and Al enriched in interdendritic regions (IR); Ti nearly uniform.
- **Precipitate composition**: Could not be determined directly by TEM EDS due to small precipitate size. Paper references prior work: in AlNbTiVZrₓ alloys, B2 phases are enriched in Zr and Al; after 873 K aging, B2 nanoprecipitates in Al₀.₄Nb₀.₅Ta₀.₅TiZr₀.₈ RHEA were identified as a Zr–Al phase.
- **Deformation microstructures after tensile fracture**: Deformation microbands (MBs), high-density dislocation walls (HDDWs), dislocation pinning, and dislocation cross-slip observed in both R850 and R1050. No TWIP or TRIP effect observed.

# Mechanical Properties
- **Testing conditions**: Room-temperature tensile tests, sample size 2.0 mm × 10.0 mm × 58.5 mm, initial strain rate 1.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹, Instron 5566 tensile tester.
- **R850 alloy**:
  - Yield strength: ~997 MPa
  - Ultimate tensile strength: ~1043 MPa
  - Elongation: ~20%
- **R1050 alloy**:
  - Yield strength: ~841 MPa
  - Ultimate tensile strength: ~857 MPa
  - Elongation: ~15%
- **As-cast reference alloy** (from prior work): Yield strength ~850 MPa, elongation 25%.
- **Yield strength increase for R850 vs. as-cast**: +143 MPa
- **Ductility retention**: ~20% (no strength-ductility trade-off)
- **Strength difference R850 vs. R1050**: Δσ₀.₂ ≈ 156 MPa; Δε ≈ 5%
- **Strengthening mechanism contributions to strength difference (R850 vs. R1050)**:
  - Grain boundary strengthening: Δσ_gb ≈ 64 MPa
  - Dislocation strengthening: Δσ_pi ≈ 84 MPa
  - Nanoprecipitate strengthening: Δσ_ppt ≈ −7 MPa
  - Sum: 64 + 84 − 7 = 141 MPa (vs. experimental 156 MPa)
- **Work hardening behavior**: Both alloys show multistage work hardening. R850 maintains higher work-hardening rate; R1050 work-hardening rate decreases after ~6% plastic deformation in stage II.
- **Fracture morphologies**:
  - R850: densely distributed tear ridges, a small number of dimples, many micropores in dimple fractures
  - R1050: tear ridges almost disappear, large dimples appear, many micropores remain
- **Hardness**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Elastic modulus / shear modulus**:
  - Shear elastic modulus G = 38.5 GPa (estimated by Rule of Mixture: G_Zr = 33 GPa, G_Ti = 44 GPa, G_Nb = 38 GPa, G_Al = 27 GPa, G_Ta = 69 GPa)
  - Shear modulus difference between matrix and nanoprecipitates: ΔG ≈ 4.6 GPa
- **Density**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **High-temperature strength / creep / fracture toughness**: Not investigated in this paper.
- **Strain rate**: 1.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹
- **Atmosphere**: Room-temperature air; not explicitly stated.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability
- **Oxidation resistance**: Not directly studied in this paper.
- **High-temperature structural stability**: The paper discusses that long-term high-temperature heat treatment of Al-containing RHEAs leads to gradual coarsening of B2 nanoprecipitates and inevitable formation of brittle Laves phases, which destroys ductility and toughness. Short-term annealing (5 min) prevents Laves phase formation.
- **Oxygen/nitrogen embrittlement**: Not explicitly investigated in this paper.
- **Oxidation weight gain / oxide layer**: Not studied in this paper.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## [Al]
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Al-containing refractory high-entropy alloys, Zr-Ti-Nb-Al-Ta RHEA, AlNbTiVZrₓ, Al₀.₄Nb₀.₅Ta₀.₅TiZr₀.₈
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 10 at.% in Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅
- **Main Function**: Introduces ordered B2 nanoprecipitates in the BCC matrix; regulates the synergistic effect of B2 nanoprecipitates and BCC matrix phase; improves room-temperature compressive ductility by forming a dispersed B2-in-BCC microstructure.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Strong mixing enthalpy between Al and Zr (−44 kJ/mol, the minimum in this system) promotes B2 phase formation enriched in Zr and Al. B2 nanoprecipitates are coherent with the BCC matrix and dispersed as nanometer-scale precipitates. Short-time annealing keeps them stable and prevents coarsening into brittle Laves phases.
- **Property Effect**: R850 alloy (with B2 nanoprecipitates ~1.67 nm, volume fraction 0.39) reaches yield strength ~997 MPa and elongation ~20%; R1050 (~2.35 nm B2 nanoprecipitates, volume fraction 0.42) reaches ~841 MPa and ~15% elongation. The original paper indicates the B2 nanoprecipitates contribute strengthening, but the calculated difference in precipitation strengthening between R850 and R1050 is only about −7 MPa, which is negligible compared to grain boundary (~64 MPa) and dislocation (~84 MPa) contributions.
- **Potential Side Effects**: After long-term high-temperature heat treatment, B2 nanoprecipitates gradually coarsen and are replaced by brittle Laves phases, destroying ductility and toughness. The B2/BCC coherent microstructure is difficult to stabilize in alloys composed of Al and early transition metals.
- **Original Evidence**: “Al was alloyed to RHEAs to introduce B2 nanoprecipitates”; “the mixing enthalpy between Zr and Al is −44 kJ/mol, which is the minimum value in this alloy system, indicating that Zr and Al are more easily combined than other element pairs” (Section 3.1); “After long-term high-temperature heat treatment, the B2 nanoprecipitates in Al-containing RHEAs will gradually coarsen, and brittle LAVES phases will inevitably form” (Section 4.2).

## [Zr]
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Al-containing refractory high-entropy alloys, Zr-rich RHEAs, AlNbTiVZrₓ
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 35 at.% in Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅
- **Main Function**: Combines with Al to form the B2 ordered phase; lowers melting point relative to Nb/Ta, causing segregation to interdendritic regions; contributes to solid-solution strengthening.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Zr and Al have the lowest mixing enthalpy (−44 kJ/mol), so Zr–Al low-melting-point solution is repelled from dendrite regions during solidification, enriching in the interdendritic region. In the B2 nanoprecipitates, Zr and Al are the main constituents.
- **Property Effect**: Specific numerical values for Zr-only contribution are not provided. The B2 Zr–Al phase contributes to precipitation strengthening, but the paper calculates that the difference in nanoprecipitate strengthening between R850 and R1050 is only about −7 MPa, which is negligible compared to grain boundary and dislocation contributions.
- **Potential Side Effects**: High Zr content (35 at.%) may promote B2 phase formation, which can evolve into brittle Laves phases during long-term high-temperature exposure.
- **Original Evidence**: “The mixing enthalpy between Zr and Al is −44 kJ/mol, which is the minimum value in this alloy system… During the solidification process, Ti, Nb and Ta elements first solidify to form dendrites, while Ti/Nb/Ta elements with Zr are less compatible, which makes the Zr–Al low-melting-point solution repulsive to the dendrite regions” (Section 3.1); “In AlNbTiVZrx (x = 0–1.5) alloys, the mixing enthalpy between Al and Zr is the lowest (−44 kJ/mol), indicating that they easily form B2 phases enriched in Zr and Al” (Section 3.1).

## [Zr + Al]
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Al-containing refractory high-entropy alloys; B2-containing RHEAs
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Zr:Al = 35:10 (at.%) in the nominal composition of Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅
- **Main Function**: Forms ordered B2 nanoprecipitates dispersed in BCC matrix; improves room-temperature ductility and strength synergy by preventing brittle phase formation during short-term annealing.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Negative mixing enthalpy between Zr and Al promotes formation of B2 phase enriched in Zr and Al. B2 nanoprecipitates are coherent with BCC matrix. Short-time annealing (5 min) after cold rolling keeps the nanoprecipitates fine (~1.67–2.35 nm) and prevents their coarsening or transformation into Laves phases.
- **Property Effect**: R850: yield strength ~997 MPa, elongation ~20%; R1050: yield strength ~841 MPa, elongation ~15%. The original paper notes that the difference in nanoprecipitate strengthening between R850 and R1050 is only about −7 MPa (calculated), suggesting B2 nanoprecipitates are not the dominant factor differentiating the two heat-treatment conditions.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Long-term or excessive high-temperature exposure causes B2 nanoprecipitates to coarsen and transform into brittle Laves phases, causing ductility degradation.
- **Original Evidence**: “The B2 nanoprecipitates and BCC matrix phase form a coherent cocontinuous mixture (on the nanometer scale) without the formation of other phases” (Section 3.1); “After long-term high-temperature heat treatment, the B2 nanoprecipitates in Al-containing RHEAs will gradually coarsen, and brittle LAVES phases will inevitably form” (Section 4.2).

## [Ti]
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Zr-Ti-Nb-Al-Ta RHEA, Al-containing RHEAs, refractory high-entropy alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 30 at.% in Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅
- **Main Function**: Contributes to solid-solution strengthening; participates in BCC matrix solid solution; nearly uniform distribution between dendritic and interdendritic regions.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Ti has intermediate melting point and mixing enthalpy characteristics, leading to nearly uniform distribution between DR and IR. Contributes to lattice distortion and solid-solution strengthening in the BCC matrix.
- **Property Effect**: Specific numerical values for Ti-only contribution are not provided. The overall yield strength of the R850 alloy is ~997 MPa, of which the solid-solution contribution is considered the same between R850 and R1050 because composition is the same.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence**: “The Ti element is almost uniformly distributed in the DR and IR” (Section 3.1).

## [Nb]
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Zr-Ti-Nb-Al-Ta RHEA, refractory high-entropy alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 20 at.% in Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅
- **Main Function**: Solid-solution strengthening in BCC matrix; refractory element contributing to high strength.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: High melting point, enriched in dendritic regions (DR: 22.5–23.0 at.% vs. IR: 17.5 at.%). Contributes to solid-solution strengthening and lattice distortion.
- **Property Effect**: Specific numerical values for Nb-only contribution are not provided in this paper.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence**: “The high melting point Nb and Ta elements are enriched in the DR, while the low melting point Zr and Al elements are enriched in the IR” (Section 3.1).

## [Ta]
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Zr-Ti-Nb-Al-Ta RHEA, refractory high-entropy alloys
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 5 at.% in Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅
- **Main Function**: Solid-solution strengthening; refractory element contributing to high strength.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: High melting point, enriched in dendritic regions (DR: 5.3 at.% vs. IR: 3.1–3.4 at.%). Contributes to lattice distortion and solid-solution strengthening.
- **Property Effect**: Specific numerical values for Ta-only contribution are not provided in this paper.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Original Evidence**: “The high melting point Nb and Ta elements are enriched in the DR” (Section 3.1).

## [Cold Rolling + Annealing (Process Card)]
- **Applicable Alloy System**: Al-containing RHEAs, especially those prone to brittle Laves phase formation after long-term heat treatment
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Cold rolling from φ 20 mm to 2 mm thickness at room temperature; annealing at 850 °C or 1050 °C for 5 min, water quenched.
- **Main Function**: Introduces high-density dislocations and fine grains via cold rolling; short-time annealing retains dislocations, optimizes grain size, and prevents brittle Laves phase formation.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Cold rolling generates high-density dislocation entanglements and accumulation bands. Short-time annealing (5 min) allows partial recovery without full recrystallization, retaining high dislocation density (R850: 1.351 × 10¹⁴ m⁻²; R1050: 0.743 × 10¹⁴ m⁻²). Annealing also optimizes grain size (R850: ~4.03 μm; R1050: ~22.54 μm). The short annealing time is insufficient for precipitation/growth of brittle Laves phases.
- **Property Effect**: R850: yield strength ~997 MPa, tensile strength ~1043 MPa, elongation ~20%; R1050: yield strength ~841 MPa, tensile strength ~857 MPa, elongation ~15%. Compared to as-cast (yield ~850 MPa, elongation 25%), R850 gains +143 MPa yield strength while retaining ~20% ductility, demonstrating no strength-ductility trade-off.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly stated. However, the paper indicates that longer or higher-temperature annealing could promote Laves phase formation and reduce ductility.
- **Original Evidence**: “Here, we demonstrate a new strategy to optimize strength-ductility in RHEAs through cold rolling-annealing treatments”; “Short-term annealing can prevent the generation of the brittle LAVES phase in Al-containing RHEAs”; “The fine grain size induced by cold rolling and short annealing times contributes to the strength” (Abstract).

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Solid-Solution Strengthening
- **Related elements**: Zr, Ti, Nb, Ta, Al in the BCC matrix
- **Typical alloys**: Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅ RHEA
- **Key compositions**: Multi-principal element BCC solid solution with 5 elements at 5–35 at.%
- **Microstructural changes**: Complex multi-component BCC solid solution with lattice distortion; dendritic/interdendritic segregation (DR enriched in Nb, Ta; IR enriched in Zr, Al)
- **Property improvements**: The solid-solution strengthening contribution is considered the same between R850 and R1050 because the composition is identical.
- **Risks and limitations**: Not explicitly discussed separately; overall alloy design avoids brittle phases by keeping B2 nanoprecipitates dispersed.

## Grain-Boundary Strengthening (Hall–Petch)
- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; controlled by processing route
- **Typical alloys**: Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅ RHEA (R850 and R1050)
- **Key compositions**: Same composition; different grain sizes from different annealing temperatures
- **Microstructural changes**: Grain size reduction from ~22.54 μm (R1050) to ~4.03 μm (R850) via lower annealing temperature (850 °C vs. 1050 °C)
- **Property improvements**: Δσ_gb ≈ 64 MPa difference between R850 and R1050 (calculated using Hall–Petch with k = 240 MPa·μm¹ᐟ²)
- **Risks and limitations**: Finer grains also help distribute plastic deformation more uniformly and improve ductility (R850 elongation ~20% vs. ~15% for R1050).

## Dislocation Strengthening
- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; induced by cold rolling
- **Typical alloys**: Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅ RHEA (R850 and R1050)
- **Key compositions**: Cold-rolled + short-time annealed
- **Microstructural changes**: High-density dislocation entanglements and accumulation bands introduced by cold rolling; retained by short annealing. Dislocation density: 1.351 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² (R850) vs. 0.743 × 10¹⁴ m⁻² (R1050).
- **Property improvements**: Δσ_pi ≈ 84 MPa difference between R850 and R1050 using Bailey–Hirsch formula (M = 3.6, α = 0.6, G = 38.5 GPa, b = 0.29 nm). High-density dislocation networks also improve uniform strain hardening and ductility.
- **Risks and limitations**: Excessive dislocation density may reduce ductility; however, the R850 alloy with higher dislocation density actually shows higher ductility (~20%) than R1050 (~15%), attributed to uniform dislocation distribution and finer grain size.

## B2 Nanoprecipitate Strengthening
- **Related elements**: Zr, Al (forming B2 ordered phase)
- **Typical alloys**: Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅ RHEA; Al₀.₄Nb₀.₅Ta₀.₅TiZr₀.₈; AlNbTiVZrₓ
- **Key compositions**: B2 nanoprecipitates coherent with BCC matrix
- **Microstructural changes**: Nanometer-scale coherent B2 precipitates (1.67 nm R850, 2.35 nm R1050; volume fractions 0.39 and 0.42) uniformly dispersed in BCC matrix. No brittle Laves phases detected.
- **Property improvements**: Calculated strengthening difference Δσ_ppt ≈ −7 MPa between R850 and R1050, using dislocation shear mechanism (m = 0.85, ΔG = 4.6 GPa). Precipitation strengthening contributes to overall yield strength but is not the dominant differentiator between the two heat-treatment conditions in this paper.
- **Risks and limitations**: Long-term high-temperature exposure causes coarsening and transformation to brittle Laves phases; short annealing must be maintained. Very fine precipitate size makes composition analysis difficult by TEM EDS.

## Grain Refinement (Ductility Enhancement)
- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; controlled by annealing temperature
- **Typical alloys**: Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅ RHEA
- **Key compositions**: Cold rolling + annealing at 850 °C (finer grains ~4.03 μm) vs. 1050 °C (coarser grains ~22.54 μm)
- **Microstructural changes**: Fine grains distribute plastic deformation more evenly among more grains; internal stress distribution more uniform; increased grain boundary fraction resists plastic deformation.
- **Property improvements**: R850 with finer grains has both higher yield strength (~997 MPa vs. ~841 MPa) and higher ductility (~20% vs. ~15%) compared to R1050.
- **Risks and limitations**: If annealing temperature is too high, grain growth occurs and beneficial effects diminish.

## Work Hardening / Microband-Induced Plasticity
- **Related elements**: Not element-specific; deformation mechanism
- **Typical alloys**: Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅ RHEA (R850 and R1050)
- **Key compositions**: Cold-rolled + short-annealed material with retained dislocations
- **Microstructural changes**: Deformation microbands (MBs), high-density dislocation walls (HDDWs), dislocation pinning, and dislocation cross-slip form during tensile deformation. Wider microbands in R850; no TWIP or TRIP observed.
- **Property improvements**: Microbands and dislocation cross-slip provide additional work hardening, maintain stable plastic deformation, and delay necking; R850 maintains a higher and more stable work-hardening rate than R1050.
- **Risks and limitations**: Narrow microbands in R1050 lead to decreased work-hardening rate after ~6% plastic deformation, causing early necking. Ductility is enhanced only if the dislocation network is uniformly distributed (as in R850).

# Key Conclusions
1. Cold rolling-annealing is an effective strategy to achieve excellent strength-ductility synergy in Al-containing RHEAs by avoiding brittle Laves phase formation and retaining a favorable BCC + B2 coherent microstructure.
2. R850 (annealed at 850 °C for 5 min) achieves a tensile yield strength of ~997 MPa, tensile strength of ~1043 MPa, and elongation of ~20%; R1050 (annealed at 1050 °C for 5 min) achieves ~841 MPa yield strength, ~857 MPa tensile strength, and ~15% elongation.
3. The strength difference between R850 and R1050 (Δσ ≈ 156 MPa) is primarily attributed to grain boundary strengthening (~64 MPa) and dislocation strengthening (~84 MPa), with precipitation strengthening only contributing about −7 MPa.
4. High-density dislocation entanglements induced by cold rolling and retained by short-time annealing contribute to uniform strain hardening and improved ductility.
5. Short-term annealing prevents the generation of brittle Laves phases, which are a common cause of ductility degradation in Al-containing RHEAs after long-term heat treatment.
6. B2 nanoprecipitates remain finely dispersed in the BCC matrix after short annealing (1.67–2.35 nm), coherent with the matrix, and do not coarsen significantly between 850 °C and 1050 °C anneals.
7. Fine grain size (R850: ~4.03 μm) contributes to both higher strength and higher elongation compared to coarse grain size (R1050: ~22.54 μm).
8. Deformation microbands, dislocation pinning, cross-slip, and HDDWs are active deformation mechanisms; no TWIP or TRIP was observed. The R850 alloy maintains a superior work-hardening rate and more stable plastic deformation than R1050.
9. Compared to the as-cast alloy (yield strength ~850 MPa, elongation 25%), the R850 alloy gains +143 MPa yield strength while retaining ~20% elongation, demonstrating elimination of the typical strength-ductility trade-off.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Testing temperature**: Only room-temperature mechanical properties were tested. High-temperature strength, creep, and oxidation resistance were not investigated in this paper.
- **Alloy system specificity**: The conclusions are drawn for the specific Zr₃₅Ti₃₀Nb₂₀Al₁₀Ta₅ RHEA. Generalization to other Al-containing RHEAs or refractory alloys requires further verification.
- **Annealing time**: Only a very short annealing time (5 min) was studied. The effects of longer annealing times or different annealing temperatures on Laves phase formation, precipitate coarsening, and mechanical properties were not systematically investigated in this work.
- **Precipitate composition**: The exact chemical composition of the B2 nanoprecipitates could not be directly determined by TEM EDS due to their extremely small size (~1.7–2.4 nm). The paper infers Zr–Al enrichment based on mixing enthalpy arguments and prior literature.
- **Deformation mechanisms**: No TWIP or TRIP effects were observed; the conclusions on deformation mechanisms are based on post-mortem TEM observations after tensile fracture.
- **Oxidation resistance**: Not studied; the paper does not address oxidation behavior, oxygen embrittlement, or nitrogen embrittlement.
- **Impurity levels**: C, O, N, H contents are not reported; thus the potential effect of interstitial impurities on mechanical properties cannot be assessed.
- **Workability**: The paper demonstrates that multipass cold rolling of the suction-cast rod to 2 mm is feasible for this alloy, but the workability of other RHEAs with higher brittle-phase susceptibility may differ.
- **Comparison to as-cast alloy**: The as-cast reference data for yield strength (~850 MPa) and elongation (25%) are taken from the authors' prior publication (Ref. [27]); they are not reproduced in this paper.
- **Generalizability to additive manufacturing**: The paper does not discuss additive manufacturing; all conclusions are based on suction-cast and cold-rolled sheet material.
