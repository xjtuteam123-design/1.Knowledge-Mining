- **Authors**: Wenzhuang Wang, Chongchong Wu, Hailiang Huang, Jing Zhou, Mengdi Zhang, Yang Chen, Jianwei Teng, Jinglong Qu, Yanhong Yang

- **Research objective**: To determine the critical Al content required to form a continuous Al₂O₃ film on Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys at 870 °C using a novel "3D printing–high temperature diffusion" high-throughput method, and to study the effect of continuous gradient Al on high-temperature oxidation behavior.

  4. Verify high-throughput results using discrete samples with different Al contents.
  5. Investigate "oxidation weight loss" and "Kp-antioxidation positive correlation" phenomena in humid air.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Powder preparation
- Gas atomization method for all powders:
  - Ni-Cr-Co based superalloy powder (IMR, China)
  - Al powder, 99.9% purity
  - Ni powder, 99.9% purity

## High-throughput sample preparation

### Step 1: 3D printing (laser cladding)
- Equipment: Four-channel coaxial powder feeding laser cladding system (Nanjing Huirui Photoelectric Technology Co., iLAM511C).
- Powders individually fed into three channels (Ni-Cr-Co, Al, Ni).
- Substrate: 304 stainless steel.
- Sample size: approximately 15 × 15 × 20 mm.
- Printing parameters:
  - Laser scanning speed: 10 mm/s
  - Powder feed rate: 5 g/min
  - Single pass width: 1.3 mm
  - Overlap rate: 23%
  - Single layer lift of laser head: 0.5 mm
  - Laser power: **550 W** for lower layer (0% Al); **450 W** for upper layers (2.5% Al) to reduce Al burning loss.
- Powder dried at 80 °C for 12 h in vacuum drying oven before printing.

### Step 2: High-temperature diffusion
- Diffusion temperature: **1150 °C** (above γ′ solvus temperature)
- Diffusion time: **24 h**
- Atmosphere: sealed in vacuum tube, treated in GSL-1400× tubular furnace
- Cooling: air cooled to room temperature

### Step 3: Aging heat treatment
- Temperature: **750 °C**
- Time: **24 h**
- Atmosphere: sealed in vacuum tube, tubular furnace
- Cooling: air cooled to room temperature
- Purpose: eliminate residual stresses and maintain microstructure similar to engineering Ni-Cr-Co superalloy.

## Discrete sample preparation
- Same 3D printing process with laser power **450 W**.
- Sample size: approximately 20 × 20 × 8 mm.
- Post-printing: wire cut from substrate.
- Diffusion treatment: **1150 °C / 24 h**.
- Heat treatment: **750 °C / 24 h**.

## Oxidation test conditions
- High-throughput sample:
  - Cut into 6 × 6 × 15 mm blocks
  - Polished (200 grit, 800 grit, 2000 grit)
  - Ultrasonic cleaned in anhydrous ethanol for 10 min
  - Dried at 80 °C for 10 min in vacuum drying oven
  - Oxidized at **870 °C for 100 h** in air in GSL-1400× tubular furnace
  - Air cooled to room temperature
- Discrete samples:
  - Same pre-oxidation preparation
  - Oxidized at **870 °C / 100 h** in air in box furnace
  - Weighed at intervals: 1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100 h
  - Experiment repeated twice
  - Air humidity: approximately **40%** (laboratory near the sea)

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## As-printed state
- Relatively dense with only a small number of micro-pores (not significantly affecting oxidation).
- Grains: coarse columnar morphology, length 100–500 μm, oriented along heat transfer direction.
- "Fish-scale" morphology due to intermittent crystallization from rapid cooling-heating cycles and significant internal stress.
- Fish-scale morphology more evident at interface where Al content changes due to pausing to adjust laser power.

## Diffusion-treated state
- Grains changed to **equiaxed shape** due to recrystallization during diffusion process.
- Grain size refined to approximately **100 μm**.
- Fish-scale structure alleviated due to release of internal stress.

## Oxidized state (after 870 °C/100 h)
- Overall structure consistent with diffusion-treated state, no significant macroscopic changes.

## Al gradient characteristics
- As-printed: Al diffusion limited to ~20 μm (from 100 μm to 120 μm).
- After diffusion (1150 °C/24 h): Al composition gradient continuously distributed from 400 μm to 1300 μm, distance ~900 μm.
- Actual diffusion distance much larger than Thermo-Calc calculation (~440 μm) due to fast diffusion pathways at grain boundaries and pores.
- After oxidation: Al composition gradient observed in range 300–1200 μm, diffusion distance almost same as diffusion-treated state, indicating no obvious Al diffusion during oxidation.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

## Oxidation conditions
- Temperature: **870 °C**
- Duration: **100 h**
- Atmosphere: laboratory air, humidity ~40%
- Oxidation mode: static oxidation

## Surface oxide phases (XRD, Fig. 6a)
- Main phases: **Cr₂O₃**, **TiO₂**, **NiO**, **NiCr₂O₄** (spinel)
- Spalled areas reveal: **Al₂O₃** and γ-austenite matrix phases

## Three-layer oxide structure (with sufficient Al, i.e., 2.5% Al region)
1. **Outer oxide film**: Cr₂O₃ + NiCr₂O₄ spinel + TiO₂ solid solution
2. **Sub-outer oxide film**: Cr₂O₃ + TiO₂ (bright white granular TiO₂, dark gray Cr₂O₃)
3. **Internal oxide film**: continuous dense Al₂O₃

## Two-layer oxide structure (without Al, 0% Al region)
1. **Outer oxide film**: thick Cr₂O₃ + NiCr₂O₄ spinel
2. **Sub-outer oxide film**: Cr₂O₃ + TiO₂
- No internal Al₂O₃ layer; nodular-like TiO₂ oxides form within matrix due to O diffusion through non-protective Cr₂O₃ film.

## Critical Al content for continuous Al₂O₃ film
- **0.8 wt% Al** (from high-throughput gradient sample analysis)
- Confirmed by discrete samples: critical Al content lies between **0.65% and 0.97%**

## Oxidation behavior with Al content

### Al < 0.8 wt%
- Oxide film: thick, interface between film and matrix is rough
- External oxide film: porous, mainly Cr₂O₃ and NiCr₂O₄
- Internal oxides: granular Al₂O₃ and nodular TiO₂ in matrix
- No continuous Al₂O₃ protective layer
- Severe internal oxidation

### Al > 0.8 wt%
- Oxide film: thin, interface between film and matrix is smooth
- External oxide film: mainly Cr₂O₃ with some NiCr₂O₄
- Internal oxide film: continuous dense Al₂O₃
- Good oxidation resistance

## Oxidation mechanisms

### Transient oxidation stage
- Rate-controlling step: oxidation reaction (chemical reaction)
- Duration: relatively short
- Cr preferentially oxidized to Cr₂O₃; Ni partially oxidized to NiO; NiO further reacts with Cr₂O₃ to form NiCr₂O₄ spinel
- Al hardly involved
- Similar oxidation behavior in high-Al and Al-free regions

### Steady-state oxidation stage
- Rate-controlling step: diffusion process
- Duration: longer
- In Al-containing region: Al oxidized under low oxygen partial pressure to form dense internal Al₂O₃ film; Ti⁴⁺ diffuses to surface and oxidizes to TiO₂
- In Al-free region: oxidation continues through porous Cr₂O₃ film; no protective Al₂O₃ layer

## "Oxidation weight loss" phenomenon
- Initial oxidation data points above fitted parabolic curve; later points below curve
- Caused by **volatilization of Cr and Mo oxides** in humid air at 870 °C
- Cr₂O₃ transforms to volatile CrO₃ and CrO₂(OH)₂ in water vapor environment
- Mo oxides significantly volatilize at 475 °C; at >725 °C, volatilization rate so rapid that gas-phase diffusion is rate-controlling
- In this study, volatilization weight loss dominated by Cr oxides due to high Cr content

## "Kp-antioxidation positive correlation" phenomenon
- Kp (parabolic rate constant) increases with Al content for samples 2#–5# (0.97–2.02 wt% Al)
- Contrary to conventional expectation (higher oxidation resistance → lower Kp)
- Explanation: In humid air, weight change = oxidation weight gain − volatilization weight loss
  - Higher Al → denser Al₂O₃ film → harder for Cr to diffuse outward → less Cr oxide volatilization weight loss
  - Net measured weight change is therefore positively correlated with Al content
- Sample 1# (0.65% Al) shows abnormal Kp behavior due to lack of continuous Al₂O₃ film

## Cr loss depth in matrix (from EDS mapping, Fig. 14)
- Sample 1# (0.65% Al): 7.5 μm
- Sample 2# (0.97% Al): 6.3 μm
- Sample 3# (1.17% Al): 6.0 μm
- Sample 4# (1.77% Al): 5.8 μm
- Sample 5# (2.02% Al): 5.3 μm
- Cr loss depth decreases with increasing Al content, indicating reduced outward Cr diffusion and better oxidation resistance.

## Pilling-Bedworth ratio (PBR) of oxides
- Al₂O₃: 1.28
- TiO₂: 1.70
- Cr₂O₃: 2.07
- NiCr₂O₄: slightly greater than Cr₂O₃
- Higher PBR → higher growth stress → outer oxide film (Cr₂O₃ + NiCr₂O₄) has high growth stress, prone to cracking/peeling.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Al (Aluminum)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys (Waspaloy/GH4738-type)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
- High-throughput sample: 0–2.5 wt% continuous gradient
- Discrete samples: 0.65, 0.97, 1.17, 1.77, 2.02 wt%
- **Critical content for continuous Al₂O₃ film: 0.8 wt%**

**[Main Function]**
Primary antioxidation element; forms continuous dense Al₂O₃ internal oxide film that prevents further oxygen infiltration into the matrix during steady-state oxidation.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- During transient oxidation, Al is hardly involved.
- During steady-state oxidation, Al has the lowest reacting oxygen partial pressure among alloying elements (order of −ΔG: Al > Ti > Cr > Ni from Ellingham diagram), so it preferentially oxidizes under low oxygen partial pressure within the matrix to form internal Al₂O₃.
- Diffusion coefficients of Al in superalloy and Al₂O₃ are 2 and 8 orders of magnitude smaller than other elements, making Al diffusion difficult.
- When Al content ≥0.8 wt%, Al₂O₃ forms a continuous, dense internal film; below 0.8 wt%, Al₂O₃ is discontinuous and granular.
- Continuous Al₂O₃ internal film blocks both inward O diffusion and outward Cr/Ti diffusion.

**[Property Effect]**
- At 870 °C/100 h in air:
  - Al ≥ 0.8 wt%: thin oxide film, smooth film/matrix interface, continuous dense internal Al₂O₃ film
  - Al < 0.8 wt%: thick oxide film, rough film/matrix interface, porous external oxide film, granular Al₂O₃ and nodular TiO₂ in matrix
- Cr loss depth decreases from 7.5 μm (0.65% Al) to 5.3 μm (2.02% Al), indicating reduced alloy consumption.
- Al₂O₃ film hinders Cr outward diffusion, reducing Cr oxide volatilization weight loss in humid conditions.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- With increasing Al content, outward diffusion of Cr becomes more difficult, resulting in thinner external Cr₂O₃ film. However, in humid air, this also reduces Cr oxide volatilization weight loss, complicating interpretation of oxidation kinetics (Kp-antioxidation positive correlation).
- No other negative effects explicitly discussed in the paper.

**[Original Evidence]**
- "The critical Al content to form a continuous and dense Al₂O₃ film in the Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys of this article is 0.8%." (Section 3.4, Conclusions)
- "When Al content is 2.5%, a continuous Al₂O₃ oxide film forms inside of the oxide layer. When Al content is 0.8%–1.2%, Al₂O₃ film changes from continuous to discontinuous." (Section 3.3)
- Fig. 10: EDS mapping showing continuous → discontinuous Al₂O₃ transition at 0.4–1.2% Al region.

## Cr (Chromium)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys (Waspaloy/GH4738-type)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
- Base powder: 18.26 wt%
- Discrete samples: 19.44–20.94 wt%

**[Main Function]**
Major solid-solution strengthening element and antioxidation element; forms external Cr₂O₃ oxide film during transient oxidation.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Cr is preferentially oxidized by O₂ during transient oxidation due to higher −ΔG than Ni but lower than Al and Ti.
- At >800 °C and Cr >25 wt%, dense Cr₂O₃ can rapidly form; in this study, Cr content (~20%) is insufficient to prevent Ni oxidation, so Ni and Cr are oxidized simultaneously.
- Cr₂O₃ reacts with NiO to form NiCr₂O₄ spinel.
- Cr within matrix has high diffusion coefficient, allowing easy diffusion to surface and oxidation, creating a Cr-poor zone in the matrix.
- External Cr₂O₃ film alone is porous and does not effectively prevent O inward diffusion.

**[Property Effect]**
- Provides baseline oxidation resistance at 870 °C through Cr₂O₃ formation.
- Insufficient Cr content prevents rapid formation of protective Cr₂O₃, making Al₂O₃ internal film critical for long-term oxidation resistance.
- Cr loss depth in matrix (7.5–5.3 μm after 100 h at 870 °C) serves as indicator of alloy consumption during oxidation.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- **Cr oxide volatilization** in humid air: Cr₂O₃ forms volatile CrO₃ and CrO₂(OH)₂ in water vapor at 600–900 °C, causing oxidation weight loss and deviation from conventional parabolic kinetics.
- Cr₂O₃ has high PBR (2.07), generating high growth stress in outer oxide film, contributing to wrinkling, cracking, and peeling.
- NiCr₂O₄ spinel formation (PBR slightly >2.07) further increases growth stress.

**[Original Evidence]**
- "When oxidation temperature exceeds 800 °C and the Cr content is higher than 25%, a dense Cr₂O₃ layer can rapidly form. In our sample, Cr content is not sufficient (around 20%) to inhibit the oxidation of Ni." (Section 3.2)
- "In a water vapor environment, solid Cr₂O₃ would transform into various volatile substances mainly composed of CrO₃ and CrO₂(OH)₂." (Section 3.4, citing references)
- Table 1, Fig. 9 (Cr-poor zones)

## Ti (Titanium)

**[Applicable Alloy System]**
Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys (Waspaloy/GH4738-type)

**[Addition Amount or Composition Range]**
- Base powder: 5 wt%
- Discrete samples: 2.15–6.79 wt%

**[Main Function]**
Precipitation-strengthening element (γ′ phase); antioxidation element forming TiO₂.

**[Microstructural Mechanism]**
- Ti⁴⁺ has the largest diffusion coefficient among alloying elements in the alloy.
- During steady-state oxidation with continuous Al₂O₃ film present: Ti diffuses to surface and oxidizes to TiO₂, then undergoes solid solution with Cr₂O₃ in outer oxide film.
- Without continuous Al₂O₃ film: Ti is oxidized within the matrix, forming nodular-like TiO₂ oxides.
- Diffusion coefficient and reaction free energy of Ti are both intermediate between Cr and Al.

**[Property Effect]**
- In high-Al samples: Ti contributes to outer oxide film (TiO₂ in Cr₂O₃/TiO₂ layer).
- In Al-free/low-Al samples: Ti forms nodular TiO₂ internal oxides that intrude into matrix, indicating oxygen penetration through non-protective oxide film.
- TiO₂ has PBR of 1.70, contributing to growth stress.

**[Potential Side Effects]**
- Nodular TiO₂ formation within matrix indicates severe internal oxidation when no continuous Al₂O₃ film exists.
- No other specific negative effects explicitly discussed.

**[Original Evidence]**
- "Ti⁴⁺ which has the largest diffusion coefficient in the alloy diffuses to the surface and is oxidized to TiO₂." (Section 3.2)
- "In LIII region, where the external Cr₂O₃ layer fails to hinder the O inward diffusion, Ti is oxidized within the matrix, leading to the formation of nodular oxides." (Section 3.3, Fig. 9b)

# Key Conclusions

1. A novel "3D printing–high temperature diffusion" high-throughput method successfully produced Ni-Cr-Co superalloy samples with continuous gradient Al (0–2.5 wt%), enabling rapid and efficient study of Al content effects on oxidation resistance.

2. The oxidation of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys at 870 °C occurs in two stages:
   - **Transient oxidation**: rate-controlled by oxidation reaction; Cr and Ni oxidize to Cr₂O₃, NiO, and NiCr₂O₄; short duration.
   - **Steady-state oxidation**: rate-controlled by diffusion; Al oxidizes to form internal Al₂O₃ film in Al-sufficient regions.

3. The ability to form a continuous Al₂O₃ film is crucial for assessing the oxidation resistance of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys.

4. **The critical Al content for forming continuous dense Al₂O₃ film is 0.8 wt%** for the studied Ni-Cr-Co superalloy at 870 °C.
   - Al < 0.8 wt%: thick porous oxide film, rough interface, granular Al₂O₃ and nodular TiO₂ in matrix, severe internal oxidation
   - Al > 0.8 wt%: thin protective oxide film, smooth interface, continuous dense internal Al₂O₃ film

5. The critical Al content was confirmed by discrete samples (critical content between 0.65% and 0.97% Al).

6. In humid air (~40% humidity) at 870 °C, Ni-Cr-Co superalloys exhibit:
   - **"Oxidation weight loss" phenomenon**: weight change deviates from conventional parabolic kinetics due to Cr and Mo oxide volatilization
   - **"Kp-antioxidation positive correlation" phenomenon**: Kp increases with Al content because higher Al content leads to denser Al₂O₃ film, reducing Cr outward diffusion and Cr oxide volatilization loss, resulting in higher net measured weight gain despite better actual oxidation resistance

7. The high-throughput method is suitable for characterizing single-element or multi-element coupling effects on superalloy oxidation resistance.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

1. **Alloy system limitation**: This paper exclusively studies Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys (Waspaloy/GH4738-type). Results may not be directly applicable to other Ni-based superalloys with significantly different Cr, Ti, or Co contents.

2. **Oxidation conditions**: Oxidation was studied at a single temperature (870 °C) and duration (100 h) in laboratory air with ~40% humidity. Results may differ at other temperatures, oxygen partial pressures, or humidity levels.

3. **Humidity effect**: The oxidation experiments were conducted in humid air, which affects Cr and Mo oxide volatilization. Results from dry air oxidation may show different kinetics behavior.

4. **Synergistic effects not studied**: The paper explicitly states that the synergistic effect of Al and Cr, and the third-element effect of Ti, have not been discussed. "More study should be done in the future."

5. **Microstructure evolution**: The paper does not discuss γ′ phase precipitation, grain boundary oxidation, or other microstructural changes during oxidation in detail. The focus is primarily on oxide layer formation and elemental diffusion.

6. **Mechanical properties**: No mechanical property data (tensile, creep, fatigue, etc.) are provided. The effect of Al content on mechanical properties is not discussed.

7. **Long-term oxidation behavior**: Oxidation was limited to 100 h. Longer-term oxidation (e.g., >1000 h typical of service life) may produce different oxide evolution, Cr depletion profiles, or Al subsurface depletion effects.

8. **Oxide volatilization quantification**: The paper qualitatively discusses Cr and Mo oxide volatilization but does not provide quantitative measurements of volatilization rates or volatilized species amounts.

9. **High-throughput method limitations**: The method involves laser cladding (3D printing) followed by diffusion annealing; resulting microstructure may differ from conventionally cast or forged superalloys despite attempts to maintain similar structure.

10. **Critical Al content applicability**: The critical Al content of 0.8 wt% is specific to the studied composition (Cr ~18–21%, Ti ~2–7%, Mo ~4%, Co ~13–16%) and oxidation conditions (870 °C, 100 h, humid air). Different compositions or conditions may shift this critical value.

11. **Not refractory alloy relevant**: This paper does not contain data on Nb-W alloys, Mo-W alloys, refractory high-entropy alloys, or additively manufactured refractory alloys. Its findings are applicable only to Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys for oxidation resistance.

12. **Diffusion calculations reliance**: Thermo-Calc calculations were used for diffusion predictions, but actual diffusion distances were larger due to defects (grain boundaries, pores) not accounted for in idealized TC calculations.
