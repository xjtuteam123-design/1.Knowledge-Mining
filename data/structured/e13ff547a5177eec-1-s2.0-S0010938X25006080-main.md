# Paper Metadata
- **Title**: Enhancing high-temperature oxidation resistance in directed energy deposition-processed Ni-based superalloys via heat treatment
- **Authors**: Hao Yu, Quan Zhao, David san Martín, Jinguo Li, Wei Xu
- **Journal**: Corrosion Science
- **Year**: 2025
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.corsci.2025.113281
- **Material Category**: Additively manufactured Ni-based superalloy (not a refractory alloy system; processed via directed energy deposition, DED)

# Alloy System and Research Object
- **Alloy System**: AMS-OR Ni-based superalloy
- **Base System**: Ni-based superalloy
- **Research Objective**: To investigate the effect of post-build heat treatment (solution treatment + aging) on high-temperature oxidation resistance of a DED-processed Ni-based superalloy, focusing on elemental segregation homogenization, oxide layer evolution, and protective Al₂O₃ formation.
- **Note**: Although the paper focuses on a Ni-based superalloy rather than a refractory alloy system, the elemental roles of W, Nb, Ta, and carbides, as well as oxidation mechanisms, may inform refractory alloy design.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution
**As-Printed Condition:**
- **Matrix phase**: γ (BCC not applicable; Ni-based superalloy with FCC γ matrix)
- **Precipitates**: γ′ phase (round shape), average particle size approximately 78 nm
- **Second phases**: (Nb,Ta)C carbides in interdendritic regions, continuous short-rod morphology
- **Dendritic microstructure**: Dendrites aligned with printing direction, penetrate melt pool boundaries
- **Grain size**: Average grain size approximately 547.7 ± 163.4 μm
- **Grain orientation**: Strong <001> crystallographic orientation
- **Grain boundary types**: LAGBs (2–15°) approximately 32.3%, HAGBs (>15°) approximately 67.8%
- **Grain boundary density**: 0.0121 μm⁻¹
- **Grain boundary spread**: <2° fraction approximately 23.8%; >2° fraction approximately 76.2%
- **Elemental segregation**: W and Co segregate to dendritic cores; Al and Cr slightly enriched in interdendritic regions; W dendritic core concentration 3.5 wt.% vs. 2.0 wt.% in heat-treated condition

**Heat-Treated Condition:**
- **Matrix phase**: γ
- **Precipitates**: γ′ phase, cuboid shape, average particle size approximately 172 nm
- **Second phases**: (Nb,Ta)C carbides transformed to high-curvature granular particles (partial dissolution)
- **Grain size**: Average grain size approximately 869.7 ± 127.7 μm
- **Grain orientation**: Strong <001> orientation retained
- **Grain boundary types**: LAGBs approximately 29.0%, HAGBs approximately 71.0%
- **Grain boundary density**: 0.0135 μm⁻¹
- **Grain boundary spread**: <2° fraction approximately 70.9%; >2° fraction approximately 29.1%
- **Elemental segregation**: All segregation coefficients approach unity after heat treatment

**Note**: The paper discusses second-phase strengthening via (Nb,Ta)C carbide precipitation, but does not explicitly address oxide dispersion strengthening, solid-solution strengthening, or grain-boundary strengthening in the context of strengthening mechanisms. The discussion focuses on oxidation resistance.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability
**Oxidation Test Conditions:**
- **Temperature**: 1000 °C
- **Duration**: 24 h (isothermal oxidation)
- **Atmosphere**: Synthetic air (50 mL/min)
- **Specimen size**: 10 × 10 × 1 mm³, sectioned perpendicular to building direction
- **Surface preparation**: Ground sequentially with SiC sand paper up to 5000 grit

**Oxidation Results:**
- **Final mass gain (as-printed)**: 0.82 mg/cm²
- **Final mass gain (heat-treated)**: 0.53 mg/cm² (35% reduction)
- **Parabolic rate constant Kp (as-printed)**: 6.86 × 10⁻⁶ mg²·cm⁻⁴·s⁻¹
- **Parabolic rate constant Kp (heat-treated)**: 3.92 × 10⁻⁶ mg²·cm⁻⁴·s⁻¹ (43% reduction)
- **Transient mass loss**: ~0.01 mg/cm² at 5.1 h in as-printed sample, attributed to volatile WO₃ formation
- **Oxide layer thickness (as-printed)**: approximately 3.7 μm
- **Oxide layer thickness (heat-treated)**: approximately 2.3 μm (38% reduction)
- **Internal nitridation zone depth (as-printed)**: approximately 6 μm
- **Internal nitridation zone depth (heat-treated)**: approximately 3.5 μm
- **γ′ depletion zone depth (as-printed)**: approximately 13.9 μm
- **γ′ depletion zone depth (heat-treated)**: approximately 4.8 μm
- **Oxide products**: Al₂O₃, Cr₂O₃, NiO, NiCr₂O₄, NiAl₂O₄, CoCr₂O₄, Nb₂O₅
- **Oxide layer structure**: Outer Ni(Al,Cr)₂O₄ spinel layer + inner Al₂O₃/Cr₂O₃ mixed layer (as-printed); continuous Al₂O₃ layer in heat-treated condition
- **Surface roughness Sa (as-printed)**: 1.119
- **Surface roughness Sa (heat-treated)**: 1.024

**Oxide Features:**
- **As-printed**: porous spinel structure due to volatile WO₃ evolution from W-segregated dendritic cores; no continuous Al₂O₃ layer
- **Heat-treated**: dense, pore-free spinel layer; continuous protective Al₂O₃ layer formed
- **(Nb,Ta)C carbide oxidation**: Oxidation of NbC to Nb₂O₅ (volume expansion coefficient 2.38) does not induce spallation in either condition
- **Internal AlN formation**: Beneath oxide layer, with compositions of 43 at% N and 33 at% Al

## W (Tungsten)

**Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-based superalloy (AMS-OR)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 2.25 wt.% nominal; dendritic core segregation concentration 3.5 wt.% in as-printed condition; uniform 2.0 wt.% after heat treatment

**Main Function**: In the context of this paper, W is shown to negatively affect oxidation resistance when segregated. In Ni-based superalloys generally, W serves as a solid-solution strengthener for high-temperature mechanical properties.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: W segregates preferentially to dendritic cores during rapid DED solidification. Higher W content (3.5 wt.%) drives Al partitioning from the γ matrix into the γ′ phase, reducing γ-phase Al content from 5.12 wt.% (at 2.0 wt.% W) to 4.38 wt.% (at 3.5 wt.% W). Thermodynamic calculations (Thermo-Calc, TTNi8 database, 1000 °C, 1 atm) confirmed this partitioning shift. Localized W enrichment also promotes volatile WO₃ formation during oxidation, causing porous oxide morphology and transient mass loss.

**Property Effect**:
- As-printed with W segregation: Mass gain 0.82 mg/cm², Kp = 6.86 × 10⁻⁶ mg²·cm⁻⁴·s⁻¹
- Heat-treated with uniform W: Mass gain 0.53 mg/cm², Kp = 3.92 × 10⁻⁶ mg²·cm⁻⁴·s⁻¹
- γ′ depletion zone: 13.9 μm (segregated W) vs. 4.8 μm (uniform W)
- Oxide layer thickness: 3.7 μm (segregated) vs. 2.3 μm (uniform)

**Potential Side Effects**: In as-printed condition with W segregation — porous oxide morphology due to WO₃ volatilization, prevention of continuous Al₂O₃ layer formation, deeper γ′ depletion, reduced oxidation resistance. After homogenization — no adverse effects noted.

**Original Evidence**: Fig. 10 (segregation coefficients), Fig. 11 (Al partitioning as function of W content), Fig. 13 (oxidation mechanism schematic), Section 4.1 (element configuration), Section 4.2 (oxidation kinetics), Section 4.3 (oxide scale differences)

## Al (Aluminum) + Cr (Chromium)

**Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-based superalloy (AMS-OR)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Al 5.83 wt.%, Cr 7.28 wt.% (nominal)

**Main Function**: Form protective Al₂O₃ and Cr₂O₃ scales at elevated temperatures for oxidation resistance.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Al and Cr diffuse outward during oxidation to form oxides. Continuous Al₂O₃ layer formation requires sufficient Al availability in the γ matrix. In as-printed condition, W segregation drives Al partitioning into γ′ phase, reducing diffusible γ-phase Al. Heat treatment redistributes Al into the γ matrix, enabling sustained continuous Al₂O₃ formation. Selective oxidation theory (Wagner's theory) was applied; critical volume fraction of internal oxides for transition to external oxidation g* ≈ 0.3.

**Property Effect**:
- As-printed (insufficient γ-phase Al): mixed Al₂O₃/Cr₂O₃ layer, no continuous Al₂O₃, deeper γ′ depletion (13.9 μm)
- Heat-treated (sufficient γ-phase Al): continuous Al₂O₃ layer, shallower γ′ depletion (4.8 μm), reduced internal nitridation depth (3.5 μm vs. 6 μm)
- γ-phase Al content: 5.12 wt.% at 2.0 wt.% W vs. 4.38 wt.% at 3.5 wt.% W (1000 °C, Thermo-Calc)

**Potential Side Effects**: Al depletion during oxidation leads to γ′ dissolution and γ′ depletion zone formation.

**Original Evidence**: Fig. 9 (EPMA elemental distribution), Fig. 11 (Al partitioning), Fig. 12 (TEM EDS maps), Section 4.3, Eq. 3 (selective oxidation criterion)

## (Nb,Ta)C Carbides

**Applicable Alloy System**: Ni-based superalloy (AMS-OR)

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: Nb 2.1 wt.%, Ta 0.66 wt.%, C 0.21 wt.% (nominal); carbides form in interdendritic regions

**Main Function**: In this paper, the carbide role is primarily analyzed in the context of oxidation behavior and its evolution during heat treatment.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: (Nb,Ta)C carbides form as continuous short-rod structures in interdendritic regions in as-printed condition. During solution treatment at 1200 °C for 4 h, partial dissolution occurs, transforming them to high-curvature granular particles. During oxidation, (Nb,Ta)C carbides oxidize to Nb₂O₅ (volume expansion coefficient 2.38), with Nb₂O₅ also containing Ta incorporation. Carbide oxidation does not induce spallation of the surrounding oxide layer.

**Property Effect**: 
- Oxidation of NbC to Nb₂O₅ generates significant growth stress (volume expansion)
- No spallation observed in either condition
- Carbide network fragmentation after heat treatment contributes to more uniform oxidation

**Potential Side Effects**: In general Ni-based superalloys, carbides may undergo accelerated oxidation kinetics during thermomechanical fatigue due to heterogeneous interfaces, promoting oxidation-assisted microcracking (noted from references). In this specific alloy, no detrimental spallation was observed.

**Original Evidence**: Fig. 1 (carbide morphology evolution), Fig. 5 (oxide surface with Nb/Ta-rich phases at ~10 μm intervals), Fig. 6 (carbide oxidation morphology), Section 3.3

## Compositional Homogenization-Driven Oxidation Resistance Enhancement

**Related Elements**: W (primary), Al, Cr

**Typical Alloys**: DED-processed AMS-OR Ni-based superalloy

**Key Compositions**: W 2.25 wt.%, Al 5.83 wt.%, Cr 7.28 wt.%

**Microstructural Changes**: 
- Elimination of W dendritic core→interdendritic segregation (K_i approaches unity)
- Al redistribution from γ′ to γ matrix
- γ′ coarsening from 78 nm (round) to 172 nm (cuboid)
- (Nb,Ta)C carbide partial dissolution, transformation from continuous short-rod to granular
- Reduction in spinel grain size refinement

**Property Improvements**: 
- 35% reduction in mass gain (0.82 → 0.53 mg/cm²)
- 43% reduction in parabolic rate constant Kp (6.86 × 10⁻⁶ → 3.92 × 10⁻⁶ mg²·cm⁻⁴·s⁻¹)
- 38% reduction in oxide layer thickness (3.7 → 2.3 μm)
- Continuous Al₂O₃ layer formation
- Reduced internal nitridation depth (6 → 3.5 μm)
- Reduced γ′ depletion zone depth (13.9 → 4.8 μm)

**Risks and Limitations**: 
- Not applicable to alloys where compositional homogenization reduces beneficial segregation effects (contradictory findings exist in literature, e.g., IN625 and some single-crystal superalloys show as-cast/as-printed conditions with better oxidation resistance)

**Original Evidence**: Sections 4.1–4.4, Fig. 13 (schematic illustration)

## Protective Al₂O₃ Layer Formation

**Related Elements**: Al, Cr, W (indirectly through Al partitioning)

**Typical Alloys**: Ni-based superalloys

**Key Compositions**: Al 5.83 wt.%, Cr 7.28 wt.%

**Microstructural Changes**: Continuous, pore-free Al₂O₃ inner layer formation after heat treatment; dense spinel outer layer

**Property Improvements**: Lower oxygen and nitrogen partial pressure beneath oxide layer; reduced internal oxidation/nitridation

**Risks and Limitations**: Requires sufficient Al availability in the γ matrix; W segregation can inhibit this by partitioning Al into γ′ phase

**Original Evidence**: Fig. 12 (TEM EDS maps showing continuous Al-rich layer), Eq. 3 (selective oxidation criterion), Section 4.3

# Key Conclusions
1. Heat treatment (1200 °C/4 h + 900 °C/24 h) of DED-processed AMS-OR Ni-based superalloy reduces oxidation mass gain by 35% (from 0.82 to 0.53 mg/cm²) and parabolic rate constant by 43% (from 6.86 × 10⁻⁶ to 3.92 × 10⁻⁶ mg²·cm⁻⁴·s⁻¹) at 1000 °C for 24 h in synthetic air.
2. Dendritic W segregation in the as-printed condition (3.5 wt.% in dendritic cores vs. 2.0 wt.% after heat treatment) drives Al partitioning into the γ′ phase, reducing γ-phase Al availability and preventing continuous Al₂O₃ formation.
3. Heat treatment homogenizes W distribution, enabling Al redistribution into the γ matrix, forming a continuous protective Al₂O₃ layer.
4. W-rich dendritic cores in the as-printed condition promote volatile WO₃ formation, creating porous oxide morphologies and transient mass loss (~0.01 mg/cm² at 5.1 h).
5. The heat-treated sample forms coarser spinel grains than the as-printed condition, reducing oxygen ingress and establishing lower oxygen partial pressure, further promoting continuous Al₂O₃ formation.
6. γ′ depletion zone depth reduces from 13.9 μm (as-printed) to 4.8 μm (heat-treated); internal nitridation zone depth reduces from 6 μm to 3.5 μm.
7. Oxide layer structure in both conditions: outer Ni(Al,Cr)₂O₄ spinel layer + inner Al₂O₃/Cr₂O₃ mixed layer; the heat-treated condition additionally forms a continuous Al₂O₃ layer.
8. (Nb,Ta)C carbide oxidation to Nb₂O₅ (volume expansion coefficient 2.38) does not cause spallation in either condition.
9. The improvement in oxidation resistance is attributed to microstructural homogenization, not compositional changes, providing a scalable post-build strategy for enhancing high-temperature durability in AM superalloys.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope
- **Alloy System Limitation**: This study focuses on a Ni-based superalloy (AMS-OR), not a refractory alloy system. The findings are most directly applicable to Ni-based superalloys with similar W, Al, Nb, and Ta contents processed via DED. Extrapolation to Nb-W, Mo-W, refractory high-entropy alloys, or other refractory alloy systems requires caution.
- **Heat Treatment Specificity**: Results apply to the specific heat treatment schedule (1200 °C/4 h solution + 900 °C/24 h aging, air cooling, 10 °C/min heating rate). Other heat treatment protocols may yield different results, as evidenced by conflicting literature findings (e.g., IN625 as-printed showing better oxidation resistance than solution-treated).
- **Testing Condition Specificity**: Oxidation tests were performed at 1000 °C for 24 h in synthetic air. Behavior at other temperatures, durations, or atmospheres (e.g., different oxygen partial pressures, presence of contaminants) is not addressed.
- **Mechanical Properties**: The paper does not address the effect of heat treatment on mechanical properties of the alloy; the trade-off between oxidation resistance improvement and mechanical performance changes is not evaluated.
- **Sample Orientation**: Oxidation specimens were sectioned perpendicular to the building direction. The effect of specimen orientation (e.g., parallel vs. perpendicular to build direction) on oxidation behavior is not investigated, though anisotropic microstructures in AM alloys are noted as relevant in the literature.
- **Long-Term Stability**: The 24-hour isothermal test does not capture long-term cyclic oxidation behavior, thermal cycling spallation resistance, or sustained service performance.
- **Microstructural Coarsening**: Grain growth after heat treatment (547.7 → 869.7 μm) is not discussed in terms of mechanical property implications (e.g., Hall-Petch strengthening, creep resistance).
- **Elemental Analysis Detail**: Atomic percentages for composition are not provided; only weight percentages are given.
