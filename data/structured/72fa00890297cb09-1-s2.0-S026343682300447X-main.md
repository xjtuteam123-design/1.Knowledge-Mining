# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Preparation method**: Vacuum arc melting
- **Atmosphere control**: Vacuum chamber evacuated to 3 × 10^(-4) Pa, then flushed with Ar gas to 0.05 MPa. Evacuation and flushing repeated three times to prevent oxidation.
- **Homogenization**: Ingots remelted and flipped five times during arc melting to ensure homogeneous elemental distribution.
- **Specimen preparation**: Cylindrical specimens with dimensions Φ 4 mm × 6 mm machined from as-cast ingots using electric discharge machining (EDM).
- **Heat treatment**: None reported. All characterization and testing performed on as-cast material.
- **Additive manufacturing**: Not used in this study.
- **Rolling, forging, annealing, aging**: Not applied in this study.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phases**: BCC (body-centered cubic) and HCP (hexagonal close-packed). ND analysis confirms BCC and HCP phases in all three alloys. EBSD phase maps show a dominant BCC phase with minor HCP phase. TEM reveals the presence of both HCP phase and two distinct BCC phases (BCC1 and BCC2).

- **HCP phase characteristics**:
  - HCP phase content decreases with increasing Ti content (confirmed by both ND and EBSD).
  - HCP phase is enriched in Zr and depleted in Ti and Nb (based on TEM-EDS).
  - The HCP phase in TiZrNbMoV: Zr 33.95 at.%, Ti only 7.17 at.%, Nb 8.73 at.%.
  - The HCP phase in Ti_2ZrNbMoV: Zr 59.39 at.%, Ti 21.99 at.%, Nb 5.96 at.%.
  - HCP-phase nanocrystals are observed at BCC phase grain boundaries.

- **BCC1 and BCC2 phases**:
  - BCC2 phase is depleted in Zr (TiZrNbMoV: Zr 6.72 at.%; Ti_2ZrNbMoV: Zr 9.54 at.%).
  - BCC1 phase is enriched in Zr in TiZrNbMoV (Zr 35.07 at.%) and Ti_2ZrNbMoV (Zr 24.67 at.%).
  - In Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV, BCC1 composition approaches the nominal value.

- **Grain size**:
  - TiZrNbMoV: average grain size ≈ 81 μm
  - Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV: average grain size ≈ 33 μm
  - Ti_2ZrNbMoV: average grain size ≈ 16 μm
  - Grain size decreases with increasing Ti content.
  - TiZrNbMoV shows coarse and fine grains with large differences in grain size; higher Ti contents produce more uniform grain-size distribution.

- **Grain morphology**:
  - TiZrNbMoV and Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV: typical dendritic microstructure.
  - Ti_2ZrNbMoV: equiaxed dendritic microstructure.
  - Increase in Ti content causes transition from dendritic to equiaxed dendritic.

- **Grain boundary features**:
  - Many high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs, >15°).
  - Low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs, 5°–15° and 2°–5°) present around HAGBs and inside dendritic structures.
  - LAGBs are attributed to second-phase nanocrystals and severe lattice distortion.

- **Elemental segregation**:
  - HCP phase is enriched with Zr, depleted with Ti and Nb.
  - BCC2 phase is depleted with Zr.
  - BCC1 phase is enriched with Zr in TiZrNbMoV and Ti_2ZrNbMoV; approaches nominal composition in Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV.

- **Dislocation structure**:
  - KAM maps show high-strain state (high dislocation density) in regions containing HCP-phase nanocrystals.
  - Dislocations may be caused by high cooling rate during vacuum arc melting of small samples.

- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed**: Solid-solution strengthening (lattice distortion from atomic radius differences), second-phase strengthening (nanocrystals at grain boundaries causing dislocation accumulation), grain refinement (finer grains promote uniform plastic deformation), and the role of VEC (valence electron concentration) in ductility.

- **Hardness**: Not measured in this study (nanoindentation used for creep testing, but hardness values not reported).

- **Elastic modulus**: Not reported.

- **Compressive plasticity** (total compressive strain): Implicit in fracture strain, but no separate value given.

- **Fracture toughness**: Not reported.

- **Creep performance (nanoindentation creep)**:
  - Creep testing conducted at room temperature using Berkovich indenter, loading rate 2 mN/s, maximum load 400 mN, holding time 60 s.

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

## Element: Ti

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ti_xZrNbMoV refractory high-entropy alloy
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: x = 1 (20 at.%), 1.5 (27.27 at.%), 2 (33.33 at.%)
- **Main Function**: Grain refinement; promotion of equiaxed dendritic microstructure; improvement of room-temperature ductility; reduction of density; suppression of HCP second phase; reduction of lattice distortion (δ).
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Ti enrichment ahead of the solid–liquid boundary increases undercooling in the diffusion layer at the advancing solid/liquid interface, limiting crystal growth and refining grains (average grain size decreases from 81 μm at 20 at.% Ti to 16 μm at 33.33 at.% Ti). Higher Ti content inhibits formation of HCP phase and promotes transition from dendritic to equiaxed dendritic morphology. Ti reduces VEC (from 4.80 to 4.67), facilitating shear instability and dislocation nucleation during plastic deformation.
- **Property Effect**: 
  - Yield strength decreases with increasing Ti content (specific values not directly tabulated; SYS decreases from 312 to 237 kPa·m^3/kg).
  - Fracture strain increases with increasing Ti content (specific values not directly extracted; qualitatively stated).
  - Density decreases from 7.13 to 6.69 g/cm^3.
  - Creep stress exponent n decreases from 105 to 34; strain rate sensitivity increases from 0.00952 to 0.0294.
- **Potential Side Effects**: Reduction in solid-solution strengthening due to lower lattice distortion (δ decreases from 5.78% to 5.28%); reduction in second-phase strengthening due to lower HCP fraction; increased creep rate and lower creep resistance (lower n, higher m).
- **Original Evidence**: "The average grain sizes of the TiZrNbMoV, Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV, and Ti_2ZrNbMoV alloys were approximately 81, 33, and 16 μm, respectively, indicating that the increase in Ti content led to a reduction grain size." "The proportion of the second phase decreased with increasing Ti content." "The density of the Ti_xZrNbMoV alloys decreased with increasing Ti content." Table 2; Section 3.2; References [14–16,21,22].

## Element: Zr

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ti_xZrNbMoV refractory high-entropy alloy
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 20 at.% (TiZrNbMoV), 18.18 at.% (Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV), 16.67 at.% (Ti_2ZrNbMoV)
- **Main Function**: Formation of HCP phase; contributes to second-phase strengthening; contributes to solid-solution strengthening through atomic radius mismatch.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: Zr strongly partitions to the HCP phase (HCP phase contains 33.95–59.39 at.% Zr depending on alloy). Zr is depleted in BCC2 phase (6.72–10.45 at.% Zr) and enriched in BCC1 phase in TiZrNbMoV and Ti_2ZrNbMoV (35.07 and 24.67 at.% Zr, respectively). The large atomic radius of Zr contributes to lattice distortion and δ.
- **Property Effect**: Not directly quantified in this paper for Zr alone. The HCP phase formed by Zr enrichment acts as a second phase that contributes to strengthening through dislocation accumulation; reduction of HCP fraction with increasing Ti reduces strength.
- **Potential Side Effects**: HCP phase has been associated with embrittlement in some RHEAs; however, in this study the HCP phase is a minor second phase and is not characterized as detrimental.
- **Original Evidence**: "According to TEM-EDS analysis, the HCP phase was enriched with Zr and depleted with Ti and Nb." Table 1.

## Element: V

- **Applicable Alloy System**: Ti_xZrNbMoV refractory high-entropy alloy
- **Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 20 at.% (TiZrNbMoV), 18.18 at.% (Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV), 16.67 at.% (Ti_2ZrNbMoV)
- **Main Function**: Constituent of solid-solution BCC matrix; the paper cites literature indicating V can inhibit impurities and brittle compounds at grain boundaries and improve plasticity; formation of detrimental V_2Zr phase can be suppressed by increasing Ti content.
- **Microstructural Mechanism**: V is enriched in the BCC2 phase (22.78–38.83 at.%) relative to the nominal composition, suggesting V partitions preferentially to BCC2. V contributes to lattice distortion and solid-solution strengthening.

- **Potential Side Effects**: With Zr, V can form detrimental V_2Zr phase if Ti content is insufficient (based on cited literature [13]).
- **Original Evidence**: "Wang [10] increased the content of V in the RHEAs that inhibit impurities and brittle compounds at grain boundaries, making the alloy prone to transgranular fracture, and thus improving their plasticity." # Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Mechanism: Solid-Solution Strengthening

- **Related Elements**: Ti, Zr, Nb, Mo, V (all constituent elements have large atomic radius differences).
- **Typical Alloys**: Ti_xZrNbMoV (x = 1, 1.5, 2).
- **Key Compositions**: TiZrNbMoV with δ = 5.78%; Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV with δ = 5.51%; Ti_2ZrNbMoV with δ = 5.28%.
- **Microstructural Changes**: Random occupation of elements with differing atomic radii causes severe lattice distortion, leading to high shear stress for dislocation slip.
- **Property Improvements**: Contributes to high yield strength. The alloy with highest δ (TiZrNbMoV) shows highest SYS (312 kPa·m^3/kg).
- **Risks and Limitations**: As Ti content increases, δ decreases, reducing lattice distortion and solid-solution strengthening; strength decreases.

## Mechanism: Second-Phase Strengthening (HCP phase nanocrystals)

- **Related Elements**: Zr (primary HCP-forming element); Ti content controls HCP fraction.
- **Typical Alloys**: Ti_xZrNbMoV.
- **Key Compositions**: HCP phase contains up to 59.39 at.% Zr (Ti_2ZrNbMoV), 35.13 at.% Zr (Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV), 33.95 at.% Zr (TiZrNbMoV). HCP fraction decreases with increasing Ti.
- **Microstructural Changes**: Nanocrystalline HCP-phase particles precipitate at BCC grain boundaries; these nanocrystals cause dislocation accumulation and obstruct dislocation slip.
- **Property Improvements**: Second-phase strengthening contributes to yield strength. TiZrNbMoV, with the highest HCP fraction, has the highest SYS (312 kPa·m^3/kg).
- **Risks and Limitations**: Too much second phase can seriously deteriorate plasticity (cited from reference [20]). Reduction of HCP phase with Ti addition weakens second-phase strengthening.

## Mechanism: Grain Refinement (Grain-Boundary Strengthening)

- **Related Elements**: Ti (primary grain refiner in this alloy system).
- **Typical Alloys**: Ti_xZrNbMoV.
- **Key Compositions**: Ti at 20, 27.27, and 33.33 at.%; grain sizes of 81, 33, and 16 μm respectively.
- **Microstructural Changes**: Ti enrichment ahead of solid/liquid interface increases undercooling, limits crystal growth, and refines grains. Transition from dendritic to equiaxed dendritic structure with higher Ti.
- **Property Improvements**: Finer grains promote uniform plastic deformation, prevent excess local stress concentration, and suppress crack initiation/propagation. Ductility improves with increasing Ti.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Finer grains reduce creep resistance (lower n, higher strain-rate sensitivity) due to increased grain boundary area; coarser grains are favorable for creep resistance (references [45,46]).

## Mechanism: Valence Electron Concentration (VEC) Control for Ductility

- **Related Elements**: Ti (reduces VEC); all constituents contribute to VEC.
- **Typical Alloys**: Ti_xZrNbMoV.
- **Key Compositions**: VEC = 4.80 (TiZrNbMoV), 4.73 (Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV), 4.67 (Ti_2ZrNbMoV).
- **Microstructural Changes**: Lower VEC lowers Fermi level, promoting shear instability and activation of dislocation nucleation energy during plastic deformation.
- **Property Improvements**: Ductility increases as VEC decreases (higher Ti → lower VEC → higher fracture strain).
- **Risks and Limitations**: Not stated in paper; reduction in VEC may also influence other properties not investigated.

## Mechanism: Creep Resistance via Lattice Distortion and Second-Phase Pinning

- **Related Elements**: All constituent elements (severe lattice distortion); Ti (reduces distortion); HCP nanocrystals (second-phase pinning).
- **Typical Alloys**: Ti_xZrNbMoV.
- **Key Compositions**: n = 105 (TiZrNbMoV), 72 (Ti_1.5ZrNbMoV), 34 (Ti_2ZrNbMoV); m = 0.00952, 0.01389, 0.0294 respectively.
- **Microstructural Changes**: Lattice distortion impedes component diffusion; HCP nanocrystals at grain boundaries provide obstacles to dislocation motion and creep.
- **Property Improvements**: High creep stress exponent (n > 3) indicates dislocation-climb-controlled creep; low strain-rate sensitivity indicates resistance to creep under load.
- **Risks and Limitations**: As Ti content increases, lattice distortion decreases, and creep resistance decreases (lower n, higher m, larger creep displacement). Grain refinement also decreases creep resistance.

## Mechanism: Density Reduction (Lightweight Alloying)

- **Related Elements**: Ti (primary lightweight element; density 4.51 g/cm^3).
- **Typical Alloys**: Ti_xZrNbMoV.
- **Key Compositions**: Density decreases from 7.13 g/cm^3 (x=1) to 6.69 g/cm^3 (x=2).
- **Microstructural Changes**: Not directly a microstructural mechanism, but substitution of heavier elements (Zr, Nb, Mo) with lighter Ti reduces calculated density.
- **Property Improvements**: Higher specific yield strength potential; TiZrNbMoV achieved 312 kPa·m^3/kg, described as excellent compared to previously reported RHEAs.
- **Risks and Limitations**: Density reduction via Ti addition comes at the cost of reduced yield strength. Optimal balance must be selected for target applications.

# Key Conclusions

1. Ti_xZrNbMoV (x = 1, 1.5, 2) RHEAs mainly comprise a BCC matrix phase with minor HCP second-phase nanocrystals located primarily at BCC grain boundaries. The HCP phase fraction decreases with increasing Ti content, and the microstructure transitions from dendritic to equiaxed dendritic.

2. Average grain size decreases significantly with Ti content: 81 μm (x=1), 33 μm (x=1.5), 16 μm (x=2).

3. Increasing Ti content improves room-temperature compressive ductility but reduces strength. The reduction in strength is attributed to both decreased lattice distortion (δ decreases from 5.78% to 5.28%) and reduced HCP second-phase strengthening. Improved ductility results from grain refinement and reduced VEC (4.80 → 4.67).

4. Ti_xZrNbMoV RHEAs possess low densities (7.13–6.69 g/cm^3) and high specific yield strengths (312–237 kPa·m^3/kg). TiZrNbMoV has the highest SYS of 312 kPa·m^3/kg, which is superior to many reported RHEAs.

5. Nanoindentation creep tests show that all three alloys exhibit dislocation-climb-controlled creep (n > 3). Creep stress exponent n decreases from 105 to 72 to 34 with increasing Ti content; strain rate sensitivity m increases from 0.00952 to 0.0294. Low strain-rate sensitivity is attributed to severe lattice distortion and second-phase nanocrystals.

6. The alloy system offers a good balance of strength, ductility, low density, and creep resistance, making it a promising candidate for lightweight, high-temperature structural applications.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Testing temperature**: Mechanical properties and creep behavior are evaluated only at room temperature. No high-temperature compressive or tensile tests, oxidation tests, or long-term thermal stability studies are reported. High-temperature mechanical behavior of these alloys is not addressed in this study.

- **Loading mode**: Only compression testing was performed. No tensile testing, fracture toughness, or fatigue data are provided.

- **Processing state**: All results are for as-cast vacuum arc melted material. No heat treatment, thermomechanical processing, or post-processing effects are studied.

- **Microstructure characterization**: Phase identification uses ND, EBSD, and TEM. However, quantitative phase fractions are not provided as exact percentages. The presence of two BCC phases is based on TEM; bulk phase quantification from ND Rietveld refinement is implied but not detailed.

- **Mechanical property quantification**: While yield strength and fracture strain are shown graphically, the extracted text does not provide explicit numerical values for these parameters; only SYS values are tabulated. Users should refer to original figures for exact yield strength and fracture strain values.

- **Oxidation resistance**: Not investigated. The paper makes no claims regarding high-temperature oxidation behavior or environmental stability.

- **Applicability**: Results are specific to Ti_xZrNbMoV (x = 1, 1.5, 2) alloys produced by vacuum arc melting. Generalization to other RHEA systems with different constituent elements or to additively manufactured or powder-metallurgy variants is not supported by this work.

- **Creep data limitations**: Nanoindentation creep results are limited to room temperature and high strain rates. Conventional high-temperature creep tests (tensile/compressive creep) are not performed, and the applicability of nanoindentation-derived creep parameters to bulk creep behavior at elevated temperatures is not established in this paper.
