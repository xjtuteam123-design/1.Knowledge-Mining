# Paper Metadata

- **Paper title:** Double-wire arc additive manufacturing of AlCoFeNi eutectic high entropy alloy with balanced strength and plasticity
- **Authors:** Yifei Huang, Xizhang Chen, Shengguo Ma, Ming Wen, Yu Wang, Yanhu Wang
- **Journal:** Materials Letters
- **Year:** 2024
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matlet.2024.136312
- **Alloy system studied:** AlCoFeNi eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA)
- **Material category:** Eutectic high entropy alloy / refractory element-free high entropy alloy; prepared by double-wire arc additive manufacturing (D-WAAM)
- **Research objective:** To prepare a novel Al20.33Co21.60Fe30.45Ni27.62 eutectic high entropy alloy using double-wire arc additive manufacturing and investigate its phase constitution, microstructure, and mechanical properties.

---

# Alloy System and Research Object

- **Alloy family:** Eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA), specifically AlCoFeNi
- **Classification:** Biphasic lamellar eutectic high entropy alloy composed of ordered BCC (B2) phase + ordered FCC (L12) phase
- **Reference alloy family:** AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA (introduced by Lu et al., cited as Ref [4])
- **Not a refractory high-entropy alloy:** This paper does not involve W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf, Ti, V, Cr, Re, or other refractory elements. It is an Al-Co-Fe-Ni system.
- **Research objective as stated:** To solve the strength-ductility trade-off in single-phase HEAs by using a eutectic lamellar microstructure; to demonstrate that D-WAAM technology can produce EHEAs with balanced strength and plasticity at lower cost than casting; to locate the eutectic point by adjusting bypass Al wire feeding speed.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

## Additive manufacturing method

- **Method:** Double-wire arc additive manufacturing (D-WAAM)
- **Equipment:** Cold metal transfer smart arc additive manufacturing (CMT-based WAAM)
- **Primary wire:** Combined cable wire (CCW), self-designed; composed of 2 Fe wires, 2 Co wires, and 3 Ni wires
- **Wire diameters:** CCW constituent wires all 0.6 mm
- **Main wire (CCW) feeding speed:** 7 m/min
- **Secondary wire:** Pure Al wire, diameter 0.8 mm, fed by a side-axis wire feeder
- **Al wire feeding speed:** 3.4 m/min
- **Process rationale stated:** The double-wire feeding system uses CCW as the main wire and locates the eutectic point by adjusting bypass Al wire feeding speed, improving alloy design flexibility.

## Heat treatment

- **Post-deposition heat treatment:** Not reported. No homogenization, annealing, aging, HIP, or other heat treatment is explicitly stated.
- **Cooling condition after deposition:** Not explicitly stated, but the paper notes that D-WAAM uses an arc heat source with a faster cooling rate than casting, leading to a finer lamellar structure.

## Other processing routes

- Arc melting, vacuum melting, induction melting, powder metallurgy, mechanical alloying, hot pressing, SPS, HIP, rolling, forging: Not used in this study.
- Reference to other processing routes: The paper compares D-WAAM results with EHEAs prepared by casting and arc melting (Fig. 3b).

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

## Matrix and second phases

- **Phase constitution:** Two phases:
  1. Ordered FCC phase with L12 structure (soft phase)
  2. Ordered BCC phase with B2 structure (hard phase)
- **XRD evidence:** FCC + BCC diffraction peaks; presence of the (100) superlattice peak suggests BCC phase is at least partially ordered BCC (B2).
- **TEM evidence:** Selected area electron diffraction patterns show weak superlattice spots confirming both FCC and BCC phases are ordered L12 and B2, respectively.
- **STEM/EDS evidence:** Table 1 lists the compositions of L12 and B2 phases (see Alloy Composition section).

## Microstructural morphology

- **Morphology:** Complete and regular lamellar eutectic microstructure (biphasic lamellar eutectic).
- **Grain size and lamellar spacing:** Not given quantitatively in the original paper. The paper qualitatively states that D-WAAM produces a finer lamellar structure than casting/arc melting because of faster cooling rate.
- **Precipitates:** No carbide, oxide, boride, or silicide precipitates are discussed. No ODS particles or dispersoids.
- **Grain boundary features:** Not explicitly discussed. The paper focuses on lamellar eutectic phase boundaries rather than grain boundaries.
- **Elemental segregation:** EDS results show partitioning of Al and Ni to the B2 phase and Co and Fe to the L12 phase.

## Strengthening mechanisms identified

- **Two-phase laminate strengthening / dual-phase composite strengthening:** Soft L12 phase provides plasticity; hard B2 phase provides strength.
- **Interface strengthening:** Finer lamellar structure from D-WAAM increases the density of interfaces between L12 and B2; interface accumulation of dislocations suppresses dislocation movement.
- **Work-hardening / strain-hardening behavior:** Stage II nearly constant hardening attributed to slip systems of soft and hard phases plus dislocation accumulation at phase boundaries.
- **Solid-solution strengthening:** Not explicitly discussed in detail.
- **Precipitation strengthening:** Not applicable (no precipitates).
- **Dispersion strengthening:** Not applicable.
- **Grain-boundary strengthening / grain refinement:** Not explicitly quantified; refinement is discussed at the level of lamellar spacing, not grain size.

## Strain hardening behavior

- The alloy shows typical three-stage strain hardening behavior:
  - Stage I: sharp decrease in strain hardening rate (attributed to dynamic recovery of dislocations)
  - Stage II: nearly constant, slow decrease (attributed to slip systems of soft/hard phases and dislocation accumulation at phase boundaries)
  - Stage III: sharp decrease until fracture (attributed to exhaustion of deformation capacity)
- The excellent work-hardening ability is identified as the main reason for the alloy's high plasticity.

## Fracture surface observation

- Fracture surface microstructure is mainly groove-shaped.
- Mechanism: During tensile deformation, the soft L12 phase is stretched and thinned; edges protrude; the hard B2 phase separates from the soft L12 phase at the bottom of the groove, creating elongated groove structures.
- This unique microstructure is credited with promoting the strength-plasticity balance.

## Comparison to other EHEAs

- The D-WAAM Al20.33Co21.60Fe30.45Ni27.62 EHEA shows better strength-ductility combination than EHEAs prepared by casting and arc melting (Fig. 3b).
- Comparison is qualitative in the text; no tabular numerical comparison is given in the extracted sections.

# Key Conclusions

1. D-WAAM technology was successfully used for the first time to prepare an Al20.33Co21.60Fe30.45Ni27.62 eutectic high entropy alloy.
2. The alloy exhibits a biphasic lamellar eutectic microstructure composed of an ordered BCC (B2) phase and an ordered FCC (L12) phase.
3. EDS shows the L12 phase is enriched in Co and Fe, while the B2 phase is enriched in Ni and Al.
4. Room-temperature tensile properties: yield strength 632 MPa, ultimate tensile strength 1022 MPa, elongation 13.2% (engineering). True stress–strain: YS 667 MPa, UTS 1152 MPa, true strain 12.3%.
5. The alloy shows a three-stage strain-hardening behavior with excellent work-hardening ability, which is cited as the main reason for high plasticity.
6. The mechanical properties are superior to those of most EHEAs produced by casting and arc melting.
7. The combination of the soft L12 phase and the hard B2 phase, plus the nano-lamellar morphology and high interface density from fast cooling in D-WAAM, is responsible for the excellent strength–plasticity balance.
8. Fracture surface exhibits a groove-shaped morphology caused by stretching of the L12 phase and separation of the hard B2 phase from the soft L12 phase.
9. D-WAAM offers high deposition efficiency, high material utilization, and low equipment cost, with potential for engineering applications of EHEAs.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

## Limitations

- **No high-temperature property data:** The paper only reports room-temperature tensile tests. No high-temperature strength, creep, rupture life, or oxidation data are provided.
- **No post-deposition heat treatment studied:** The effect of annealing, aging, HIP, or homogenization on phase stability and mechanical properties is not investigated.
- **No quantitative microstructural parameters:** Lamellar spacing, grain size, and phase volume fractions are not numerically reported.
- **No impurity analysis:** C, O, N, H levels are not reported.
- **Single alloy composition:** Only one nominal composition Al20.33Co21.60Fe30.45Ni27.62 is studied; no composition optimization or parametric sweep around the eutectic point is performed.
- **No long-term service stability:** No creep, fatigue, or oxidation performance.
- **No discussion of anisotropy or build-direction dependence:** Not addressed.
- **No comparison with other additive manufacturing methods beyond qualitative Fig. 3b:** The claim of superiority to casting/arc melting is based on a single figure comparison; no statistical analysis or detailed numerical comparison table is provided.

## Applicable Scope

- The results are applicable to Al-Co-Fe-Ni eutectic high entropy alloys prepared by double-wire arc additive manufacturing.
- The knowledge may be relevant to design of dual-phase lamellar eutectic HEAs with balanced strength and ductility.
- The processing concept (double-wire feeding with CCW plus side-axis Al wire to locate eutectic point) may be transferable to other eutectic HEA systems, but validation would be required.
- For refractory high-temperature alloy or refractory high-entropy alloy knowledge bases, this paper provides little direct transferable information because it contains no W, Mo, Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf, Ti, V, Cr, Re, Si, B, C, O, Y, La, or oxide-dispersion elements; and it contains no high-temperature or oxidation data.
