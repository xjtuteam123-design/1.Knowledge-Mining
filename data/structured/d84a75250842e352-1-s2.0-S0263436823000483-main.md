- **Paper title**: Microstructure and high temperature properties of tungsten processed via electron beam melting additive manufacturing
- **Authors**: Christopher Ledford, Patxi Fernandez-Zelaia, Tim Graening, Quinn Campbell, Julio Ortega Rojas, Andrés Márquez Rossy, Yutai Kato, Michael M. Kirka
- **Journal**: International Journal of Refractory Metals and Hard Materials
- **Year**: 2023
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.ijrmhm.2023.106148
- **Alloy system studied**: Pure tungsten (unalloyed W) processed via electron beam melting (EBM) additive manufacturing
- **Research objective**: To develop a suitable EBM processing window for achieving high-density, crack-free pure tungsten; to characterize the microstructure (crystallographic texture, subgrains, dislocation density); and to evaluate high-temperature tensile properties and anisotropy.
- **Material category**: Additively manufactured refractory metal (pure W); W-based material; electron beam melting powder bed fusion (EB-PBF)

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Additive manufacturing method**: Electron beam melting (EBM) powder bed fusion, Arcam Q10+ machine
- **Accelerating voltage**: 60 kV (fixed)
- **Powder**: Highly spherical pure tungsten powder from Tekna; particle size distribution 45–90 μm; Hall flow 5 s/50 g; tap density 11.6 g/cm³
- **Build plate**: Pure tungsten, 100 mm diameter, 10 mm thick, from Rembar
- **Preheat temperature**: Surface temperature estimated at 1800 °C using two-color pyrometer (achieved via reduced volume custom build chamber)
- **Build atmosphere**: Vacuum
- **Cooling during build**: Radiative cooling measured up to 9 °C/s after preheat between sequential part printing
- **Process parameters** (Table 1): Speeds from 100 to 1000 mm/s; currents from 5 to 25 mA; hatch spacing from 10 to 100 μm; surface energy density from ~40 to ~114 J/mm²; linear energy density from ~0.05 to ~0.12 J/mm
- **Optimal settings**: Multiple settings achieved high density >99.9%. Example optimal parameters include:
  - V = 358.6 mm/s, I = 12.0 mA, h = 30.0 μm, E_s = 66.9 J/mm² (sample 33, 100.00% density)
  - V = 358.6 mm/s, I = 12.0 mA, h = 35.0 μm (sample 34, 100.00% density)
- **Post-processing heat treatment**: None. All mechanical testing and characterization were performed on as-fabricated material.
- **Not explicitly stated in the original paper**: No rolling, forging, HIP, or annealing treatments were applied.

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: Body-centered cubic (BCC) tungsten
- **Grain morphology**: Columnar grains oriented predominantly along the build direction; grains “bending” toward edges due to local heat flux variation
- **Crystallographic texture**: Three distinct build direction (BD) fiber textures observed:
  - ⟨001⟩ BD preference
  - Mixed ⟨001⟩/⟨111⟩ BD preference
  - Fully ⟨111⟩ BD preference
  - Texture switching correlated with linear energy density; lower speeds/currents favor ⟨111⟩
- **Subgrain structure**: Equiaxed low-angle grain boundary cells approximately 10–20 μm in scale. Subgrain boundaries exhibit high dislocation density.
- **Dislocation density**: 
  - Subgrain boundaries: ~10¹⁵/m²
  - Subgrain interiors: ~10¹⁴/m²
  - Median defect density measured by HREBSD: 3 × 10¹³/m²
  - 99th percentile: 1 × 10¹⁶/m²
- **Second phases**: None reported (pure tungsten). No carbides, oxides, or other precipitates are mentioned.
- **Grain size**: Columnar grains; subgrain size 10–20 μm (low-angle boundaries)
- **Elemental segregation**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Strengthening mechanisms discussed**:
  - Dislocation substructure strengthening (process-induced plastic deformation leading to dislocation cells/subgrains)
  - Crystallographic texture-induced anisotropy
  - No solid-solution strengthening (pure metal), no precipitation strengthening, no ODS strengthening, no grain-boundary strengthening in the classical Hall-Petch sense beyond subgrain boundary effects

- **Testing method**: 
  - Subscale SSJ3 miniature tensile samples at 800 °C (gage section 1.2 mm × 0.75 mm); strain estimated from crosshead displacement
  - Larger tensile bars at ~650–720 °C with DIC strain measurement; gage section 9.65 × 5.33 × 2.03 mm³; heated by induction in argon atmosphere
- **Strain rate**: approximately 10⁻² s⁻¹ during plastic deformation (constant crosshead displacement)
- **Atmosphere**: Argon (for larger bars); subscale tests presumably under protective atmosphere or vacuum (not explicitly specified)
- **Key observations**:
  - Significant tensile anisotropy between longitudinal (parallel to BD) and transverse (perpendicular to BD) orientations at 800 °C
  - Longitudinal yield strength (150–204 MPa) exceeds transverse yield strength (85–101 MPa) at 800 °C
  - Discontinuous yielding observed at ~700 °C but not at 800 °C
  - Schmid factor analysis: For BCC {110}⟨111⟩ slip, longitudinal loading on ⟨111⟩-BD material yields Schmid factor ~0.27; transverse loading (⟨011⟩) yields Schmid factor ~0.4, explaining lower flow stress in transverse orientation
- **Comparison with literature**:
  - PM tungsten at 800 °C: yield ~120 MPa, UTS 280 MPa, ductility 30–40%
  - Extruded tungsten at 800 °C: yield ~450 MPa, immediate softening to rupture at 20%
  - Drawn K-doped W wire: 600 °C yield >1 GPa (work-hardened); ~100 MPa after 2300 °C annealing
  - Fine-grain pure W via SPS: yield >300 MPa; work-hardened to 450–600 MPa
  - Current EBM AM tungsten (800 °C longitudinal): yield 150–204 MPa, UTS 193–263 MPa, elongation 22–38% — closer to annealed/recrystallized state

- **Density**: Bulk density achieved up to 100.00% relative density (samples 33, 34)
- **Fracture toughness**: Not reported.
- **Creep**: Not tested.

# Oxidation Resistance and Service Stability

- **Oxidation testing**: Not performed in this paper. No oxidation weight gain data, oxidation layer characterization, or oxidation temperature/duration are reported.
- **Protective atmosphere**: Tensile testing performed in argon or under vacuum to prevent oxidation during high-temperature testing.
- **Effect of impurities on grain-boundary cracking**: Prior literature (cited in Introduction) attributes EBM cracking to the presence of oxides in small fractions along grain boundaries, which promotes intergranular cracking. The current work achieved crack-free material, likely assisted by high preheat temperature (1800 °C) and vacuum processing that reduced oxygen content (from 37 to <5 ppm wt).
- **High-temperature embrittlement concerns**: Tungsten exhibits DBTT well above room temperature; the paper discusses that strength and ductility depend strongly on thermomechanical processing history. Annealing at 2000 °C results in DBTT of 675 °C; cold rolling reduces DBTT to 125 °C. Neutron irradiation increases DBTT. DBTT decreases with decreasing strain rate.
- **Not explicitly stated in the original paper**: No direct oxidation resistance testing or oxidation layer characterization was performed on the EBM AM tungsten in this work.

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

## Mechanism: Dislocation Substructure Strengthening (Process-Induced)

- **Related Elements**: None (pure W); mechanism driven by processing conditions
- **Typical Alloys**: EBM AM pure tungsten; also observed in EBM AM pure Mo and LPBF Ta
- **Key Compositions**: Pure W, no alloying additions
- **Microstructural Changes**: Formation of subgrains (10–20 μm scale) bounded by low-angle grain boundaries; dislocation density ~10¹⁵/m² at subgrain boundaries and ~10¹⁴/m² in interiors; subgrain formation driven by process-induced plastic deformation at elevated temperature
- **Property Improvements**: Contributes to strength in as-fabricated condition; material strength closer to annealed/recrystallized state rather than work-hardened state, suggesting moderate contribution
- **Risks and Limitations**: Elevated processing temperature (1800 °C preheat) promotes recovery and reduces overall defect density relative to room-temperature LPBF processing; subgrain structure may be non-uniform due to part cooling sequence during layer printing
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 8 (KAM maps); Fig. 9 (dislocation density maps); Fig. 10 (STEM comparison); Section 3 Results; Section 4 Discussion

## Mechanism: Crystallographic Texture-Controlled Mechanical Anisotropy

- **Related Elements**: None (intrinsic to pure W); processing-controlled
- **Typical Alloys**: EBM AM pure tungsten with ⟨111⟩-BD fiber texture
- **Key Compositions**: Pure W, as-fabricated condition
- **Microstructural Changes**: Sharp ⟨111⟩ build direction fiber; columnar grain structure; texture switching from ⟨001⟩ to mixed to ⟨111⟩ with decreasing speed/current (linear energy density)
- **Property Improvements**: 
  - Longitudinal loading yields Schmid factor ~0.27 for {110}⟨111⟩ slip → higher flow stress
  - Transverse loading yields Schmid factor ~0.4 → lower flow stress
  - 800 °C longitudinal yield: 150–204 MPa vs. transverse yield: 85–101 MPa
  - Simultaneously enables mesoscale "microtextured composite" design (referenced from prior Mo work)
- **Risks and Limitations**: Significant anisotropy may limit design flexibility for isotropic loading conditions; scatter in longitudinal test results may arise from stray ⟨001⟩ grains in small-scale specimens
- **Original Evidence**: Fig. 6 (pole figures/IPF); Fig. 7 (fiber fraction); Fig. 13 (IPF with Schmid factor analysis); Table 4; Section 4 Discussion

## Mechanism: Oxide-Induced Intergranular Cracking Mitigation (Process Control)

- **Related Elements**: Oxygen (impurity)
- **Typical Alloys**: EBM AM pure tungsten (current work) and prior EBM W attempts
- **Key Compositions**: Oxygen reduced from 37 ppm wt (powder) to <5 ppm wt (solid)
- **Microstructural Changes**: Vacuum EBM processing removes oxygen; reduced oxide presence at grain boundaries suppresses intergranular cracking tendency
- **Property Improvements**: Achievement of crack-free, high-density (up to 100.00%) material; elimination of microcracking previously observed in LPBF W
- **Risks and Limitations**: Small undetected defect fractions may still exist; prior EBM W studies showed reduced flexural strength despite high density
- **Original Evidence**: Table 2; Introduction (citing [15,17,13]); Fig. 1 (crack-free micrograph)

# Key Conclusions

1. **Process feasibility**: EBM AM is a suitable manufacturing route for processing pure tungsten, achieving high-density (up to 100.00% relative density), crack-free material using a preheat temperature of 1800 °C under vacuum.

2. **Process parameter correlations**: Linear energy density (P/V) was more predictive of porosity and crystallographic texture than surface energy density or volumetric energy density. Higher linear energy density produces wider melt pools and lower porosity.

3. **Chimney porosity**: Chimney porosity exhibits transient behavior, with nucleation and growth from part sidewalls and persistence across layers. It can be mitigated by process settings that produce wider melt pools, and it may heal during the build as part thermal history evolves (parts become surrounded by insulative powder, reducing heat loss).

4. **Crystallographic texture**: Three distinct BD fiber textures were observed — ⟨001⟩, mixed ⟨001⟩/⟨111⟩, and fully ⟨111⟩ — with texture switching correlated with linear energy density. Lower speeds/currents favor ⟨111⟩ texture.

5. **Subgrain structure**: As-fabricated material exhibits 10–20 μm scale subgrains with low-angle boundaries showing dislocation densities of ~10¹⁵/m² (boundaries) vs. ~10¹⁴/m² (interiors). This confirms process-induced plastic deformation at elevated temperature drives subgrain formation.

6. **Mechanical properties**: High-temperature tensile testing (650–800 °C) reveals that EBM AM tungsten exhibits properties closer to annealed/recrystallized tungsten (i.e., lower strength than work-hardened W) with reasonable ductility. Significant mechanical anisotropy exists due to strong crystallographic texture; longitudinal 800 °C yield strength (150–204 MPa) exceeds transverse (85–101 MPa).

7. **Schmid factor explanation**: Anisotropy can be explained by Schmid factor differences: longitudinal loading on ⟨111⟩-BD material yields Schmid factor ~0.27 for {110}⟨111⟩ slip, while transverse loading yields ~0.4, predicting lower transverse flow stress.

8. **Grain rotation during deformation**: Longitudinal samples exhibit significant grain rotation near fracture surface — ⟨111⟩ → ⟨011⟩ and ⟨111⟩ → ⟨112⟩ — consistent with larger plastic strain. Transverse samples show less rotation, attributed to earlier fracture along grain boundaries.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- **Material scope**: Results apply specifically to pure (unalloyed) tungsten processed via EBM AM with a very high preheat temperature (1800 °C). The findings may not directly transfer to alloyed tungsten systems, lower preheat temperatures, or LPBF processes.
- **Mechanical testing scope**: Only elevated-temperature tensile tests (650–800 °C) were performed. No room-temperature tensile, compressive, fracture toughness, creep, or fatigue testing was conducted. Room-temperature mechanical behavior of the as-fabricated EBM AM tungsten is not reported.
- **Data scatter**: Significant variability exists among longitudinal 800 °C tensile results (e.g., 0.2% yield of 149.9 vs. 203.8 MPa; elongation 21.86% vs. 38.3%). This may be caused by small specimen size (1.2 mm × 0.75 mm gage) and presence of stray ⟨001⟩ grains. Larger-scale or multiple-directional testing would improve statistical confidence.
- **Texture mechanism**: The fiber texture switching mechanism is only partially understood and is attributed to melt pool shape evolution and crystallographic solidification behavior. The paper explicitly states that "Additional work is needed to fully address the mechanism behind the fiber switching phenomena."
- **Process map dependency**: The process parameter correlations (e.g., linear energy density vs. porosity/texture) were established for the specific geometry, powder type, and machine configuration used in this work (Arcam Q10+, 1800 °C preheat, custom reduced-volume chamber). Generalization to other geometries, heat transfer conditions, or machines requires caution.
- **Subgrain formation mechanism**: The driving factors behind subgrain formation, particularly the influence of transient cooling and effective preheat temperature variations across the build, are not fully resolved. The paper states: "Additional work is needed to understand the influencing factors driving the formation of these subgrains and their potential impact on material properties."
- **Oxidation behavior**: No direct oxidation testing was performed. Scavenging of oxygen by vacuum processing was inferred from impurity analysis but not directly linked to cracking behavior in this specific study.
- **Chimney porosity mechanism**: The role of print sequence and part-scale thermal history in chimney porosity evolution is hypothesized based on in-situ imaging but not conclusively verified across all process conditions.
- **Limited comparison**: The authors compare their material to literature data for various tungsten conditions (wrought, recrystallized, K-doped drawn wire, PM W, extruded W), but these are different microstructural states, processing routes, and test conditions; the comparison is qualitative rather than rigorously controlled.
