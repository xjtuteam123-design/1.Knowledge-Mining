# Paper Metadata

- **Title**: Alloy Hardening and Softening in Binary Molybdenum Alloys as Related to Electron Concentration
- **Authors**: Joseph R. Stephens and Walter R. Witzke
- **Journal**: NASA Technical Note (NASA TN D-6810)
- **Year**: 1972
- **DOI**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper (NASA TN D-6810)
- **Alloy system studied**: Binary molybdenum (Mo) alloys with sixth-period transition elements: Hf, Ta, W, Re, Os, Ir, Pt
- **Research objective**: To determine the effects of alloy additions of Hf, Ta, W, Re, Os, Ir, and Pt on the hardness of Mo, with special emphasis on alloy softening and the role of electron concentration
- **Material category**: Binary Mo-based alloys (BCC refractory metal alloys)

# Alloy System and Research Object

This is a fundamental study of binary molybdenum alloys, not a multi-component engineering alloy system. The paper investigates solid-solution hardening and softening behavior in binary Mo alloys across the sixth-period transition elements (Hf through Pt), with particular focus on the correlation between electron concentration and hardness.

# Processing and Heat Treatment

- **Melting**: Nonconsumable triple-arc-melting of 70-gram charges in a water-cooled copper mold, followed by drop casting into a square-cross-section mold.
- **Homogenization/annealing**: Slices (approximately 3 mm × 15 mm) were cut from cast ingots. Specimens were annealed in vacuum for 1 hour at 0.7 Tm or at a higher temperature within the solid solution range to reduce segregation and produce single-phase, equiaxed, strain-free specimens.
- **Surface preparation**: Metallographic polish on the face to be hardness tested.
- **Cooling method**: Not explicitly stated in the original paper.
- **Deformation amount**: Not applicable (cast and annealed condition).

# Microstructure and Phase Constitution

- **Matrix phase**: BCC (body-centered cubic) Mo solid solution. All alloys were annealed to produce single-phase, equiaxed, strain-free specimens within the solid solution range.
- **Second phases**: For alloys within solid solubility limits, single-phase solid solutions were targeted. The Mo-Re system has a σ phase at higher Re contents, producing extremely high hardness; the study notes the formation of the σ phase in the Mo-Re system near the Re-rich end. Mo-Pt alloys reach maximum hardness near the maximum solubility of Pt in Mo.
- **Solubility limits**: Ta and W are completely soluble in Mo. Hf solubility is limited (maximum ~16 at.% Hf studied). Re has a solubility limit near 40 at.%. Os, Ir, and Pt have intermediate to limited solubility. Pt solubility in Mo is only a few atomic percent.
- **Grain size**: Not explicitly quantified. Hardness readings were taken only of impressions within grains.
- **Lattice parameter changes** (from data in Fig. 7): Hf increases the Mo lattice parameter; Re increases it modestly; W decreases it slightly; Ta increases it; Pt shows no change (based on author's measurements and Ref. 9, Ocken and Van Vucht).
- **Elemental segregation**: Not discussed in detail; annealing was intended to reduce segregation.

**Strengthening mechanisms discussed**: The paper focuses on solid-solution strengthening/softening. It explicitly discusses:
- Atomic size misfit (Fleischer, Mitchell and Raffo references)
- Electron concentration effects
- Peierls stress modification
- Interstitial scavenging (and its rejection as the dominant mechanism)

# Element-Function Knowledge Cards

---

**[Element: Rhenium (Re)]**

**Applicable Alloy System**: Binary Mo-Re alloys

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0 to ~40 at.% Re (solubility limit); studied at 2.0, 5.3, 8.2, 10.2, 20.3, and 32.6 at.%

**Main Function**: Produces alloy softening in Mo at low concentrations and low temperatures; hardening at higher concentrations.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Excess s+d electrons compared to Mo (Re has 7 s+d electrons vs. Mo's 6). Reduces the Peierls stress by reducing the directionality of electron bonding. Alloy softening is attributed to an intrinsic effect, not interstitial scavenging. The σ phase forms at high Re contents, producing extremely high hardness.

**Property Effect**:
- Hardness minimum at 77 K occurs at approximately 8 at.% Re (VHN drops from 391 for unalloyed Mo to 315 at 8.2 at.% Re).
- At 411 K, the hardness minimum is at approximately 2 at.% Re or lower.
- Softening diminishes rapidly with increasing temperature.
- At 300 K, initial softening occurs followed by parabolic hardening up to the solubility limit.
- Hardening rate: at 8 at.% Re, VHN decreases by approximately 75 at 77 K.

**Potential Side Effects**: σ phase formation near the Re-rich end produces extremely high hardness and likely embrittlement (not explicitly stated as embrittlement but implied by high hardness).

**Original Evidence**: Figures 4(d), 5(d), 9(b), 10, 11; Table II; Conclusions 2 and 3.

**[Element: Osmium (Os)]**

**Applicable Alloy System**: Binary Mo-Os alloys

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0 to approximately 16 at.% Os (solubility limit); studied at 1.11, 1.72, 2.61, 3.64, 5.23, 9.85, and 15.76 at.%

**Main Function**: Produces alloy softening in Mo at low concentrations and low temperatures; hardening at higher concentrations.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Excess s+d electrons compared to Mo. Reduces the Peierls stress. Softening is intrinsic, not due to scavenging, despite Os having a negative free energy of formation for its oxide.

**Property Effect**:
- Hardness minimum at 77 K occurs at approximately 2.5 at.% Os.
- At 8 at.% Os content (after softening), VHN increases by approximately 160 at 77 K.
- Softening occurs over the homologous temperature range 0.027–0.143 Tm.

**Potential Side Effects**: Not explicitly discussed beyond hardening at higher concentrations.

**Original Evidence**: Figures 4(e), 5(e), 9(b), 10, 11; Table II; Table III.

---

**[Element: Iridium (Ir)]**

**Applicable Alloy System**: Binary Mo-Ir alloys

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0 to approximately 10 at.% Ir (solubility limit); studied at 0.63, 1.12, 1.71, 2.49, 3.31, 6.76, and 10.46 at.%

**Main Function**: Produces alloy softening in Mo at very low concentrations; rapid hardening at higher concentrations.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Excess s+d electrons compared to Mo. Reduces the Peierls stress.

**Property Effect**:
- Hardness minimum at 77 K occurs at approximately 1 at.% Ir.
- At 8 at.% Ir, VHN increases by approximately 380 at 77 K.
- Extremely rapid hardening rate after the softening minimum.

**Potential Side Effects**: Rapid hardening after softening minimum limits the useful softening range.

**Original Evidence**: Figures 4(f), 5(f), 9(b), 10, 11; Table II.

---

**[Element: Platinum (Pt)]**

**Applicable Alloy System**: Binary Mo-Pt alloys

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0 to approximately 8 at.% Pt (solubility limit is only a few atomic percent); studied at 0.20, 0.71, 1.2, 2.0, 2.5, 4.8, and 7.6 at.%

**Main Function**: Produces alloy softening in Mo at extremely low concentrations; fastest hardening rate after the minimum.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Excess s+d electrons compared to Mo. Reduces the Peierls stress. The Mo-Pt system shows no change in lattice parameter with additions up to 12 at.% Pt, ruling out atomic size misfit as a controlling mechanism.

**Property Effect**:
- Hardness minimum at 77 K occurs at approximately 0.6 at.% Pt.
- At 8 at.% Pt, VHN increases by approximately 620 at 77 K (largest hardening increase observed).
- Softening range is very narrow due to low Pt solubility.

**Potential Side Effects**: Rapid hardening after the very narrow softening range.

**Original Evidence**: Figures 4(g), 5(g), 9(b), 10, 11; Table II; Figure 6.

**[Element: Hafnium (Hf)]**

**Applicable Alloy System**: Binary Mo-Hf alloys

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0 to approximately 16 at.% Hf (solubility limit); studied at 0.92, 2.7, 3.9, 4.8, 9.9, and 15.9 at.%

**Main Function**: Produces alloy hardening in Mo at all temperatures studied. Does not produce alloy softening.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Fewer s+d electrons than Mo (Hf has 4 s+d electrons vs. Mo's 6). Hardening can be correlated with atomic size misfit (Hf has the largest atomic radius ratio of solutes studied). Hf is a strong potential scavenger of C, O, and N based on thermodynamics, but despite this, softening is not observed.

**Property Effect**:
- Parabolic hardening of Mo at 300 K.
- At 8 at.% Hf, VHN increase is approximately 160 at 77 K.
- Largest hardening among elements producing only hardening (compared to Ta and W).

**Potential Side Effects**: Significant lattice parameter increase, which may affect workability.

**Original Evidence**: Figures 4(a), 5(a), 9(a), 10, 11; Table II; Table III.

**[Element: Tantalum (Ta)]**

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0 to 100 at.% Ta (complete solubility); studied at 0.23, 2.2, 4.9, 7.9, 17.1, 25.3, 38.2, 59.3, and 81.5 at.%

**Main Function**: Produces alloy hardening in Mo at all temperatures studied. Does not produce alloy softening.

**Microstructural Mechanism**: Fewer s+d electrons than Mo (Ta has 5 s+d electrons vs. Mo's 6). Hardening correlates with atomic size misfit. Ta is a potential scavenger of C, O, and N, but softening is not observed.

- Apparent parabolic hardening of Mo at 300 K; maximum hardness near 50 at.% Ta.

**[Element: Tungsten (W)]**

**Addition Amount or Composition Range**: 0 to 100 at.% W (complete solubility); studied at 1.1, 3.4, 5.5, 12.1, 18.8, 37.2, 58.3, and 82.4 at.%

# Strengthening Mechanism Knowledge Cards

---

**[Mechanism: Solid-Solution Softening (Alloy Softening)]**

**Related Elements**: Re, Os, Ir, Pt (elements with excess s+d electrons compared to Mo); also observed previously in Cr-Re, Mo-Re, W-Re systems.

**Typical Alloys**: Mo-Re, Mo-Os, Mo-Ir, Mo-Pt binary alloys.

**Key Compositions**: 
- Mo: 8–10 at.% Re at 77 K; 2–5 at.% Re at 411 K
- Mo: 2.5 at.% Os at 77 K
- Mo: 1 at.% Ir at 77 K
- Mo: 0.6 at.% Pt at 77 K

**Microstructural Changes**: No microstructural change; softening occurs within the BCC single-phase solid solution region. Attributed to reduction of the Peierls stress via modification of electron directional bonding.

**Property Improvements**: 
- Mo-8.2 at.% Re at 77 K: VHN 315 vs. 391 for unalloyed Mo (40% reduction in hardness).
- Softening reduces the temperature sensitivity of hardness compared to unalloyed Mo.
- The magnitude of softening diminishes rapidly with increasing temperature.

**Risks and Limitations**: 
- Softening only occurs at low homologous temperatures (<0.16 Tm, ~461–487 K for Mo alloys).
- Softening range shifts to lower solute contents as temperature increases.
- At higher solute contents, rapid hardening dominates, especially for Os, Ir, and Pt.
- Not a high-temperature strengthening mechanism.

---

**[Mechanism: Solid-Solution Hardening Explained by Electron Concentration]** 

**Related Elements**: All sixth-period elements from Hf to Pt in Mo.

**Typical Alloys**: Mo-Hf, Mo-Ta, Mo-W, Mo-Re, Mo-Os, Mo-Ir, Mo-Pt.

**Key Compositions**: Hardening at 8 at.% at 77 K:
- Mo-W: ΔVHN ≈ 30
- Mo-Ta: ΔVHN ≈ 80
- Mo-Hf: ΔVHN ≈ 160
- Mo-Os (after softening): ΔVHN ≈ 160
- Mo-Ir (after softening): ΔVHN ≈ 380
- Mo-Pt (after softening): ΔVHN ≈ 620

**Microstructural Changes**: Solid solution formation. Hardening correlates with the square root of solute content and the difference in s+d electron count (ΔV) between solute and Mo.

**Property Improvements**: Alloy hardening increases with both solute content and |ΔV|.

**Risks and Limitations**: The electron concentration-based correlation was derived for low-temperature hardness data (77–411 K). Applicability to high-temperature strength is not demonstrated in this paper.

---

**[Mechanism: Atomic Size Misfit Hardening]**

**Related Elements**: Hf, Ta, W (in Mo).

**Typical Alloys**: Mo-Hf, Mo-Ta, Mo-W.

**Key Compositions**: Hf (largest atomic radius ratio, ~1.14), Ta (~intermediate, ~1.06), W (~1.02).

**Microstructural Changes**: Lattice parameter changes proportional to atomic radius ratio. Hf increases lattice parameter most; W slightly decreases it.

**Property Improvements**: Initial hardening rates dH/dCS at 411 K for W, Ta, and Hf correlate with da/dCS (rate of lattice parameter change).

**Risks and Limitations**: Does not explain hardening/softening in Mo-Re, Mo-Os, Mo-Ir, Mo-Pt alloys. Atomic size factor is considered a minor contributor compared to electron concentration effects.

---

**[Mechanism: Peierls Stress Reduction]**

**Related Elements**: Re, Os, Ir, Pt (in Mo).

**Typical Alloys**: Mo-Re, Mo-Os, Mo-Ir, Mo-Pt.

**Key Compositions**: Any dilute concentration where softening is observed.

**Microstructural Changes**: Alloying Mo with elements having more s+d electrons reduces the directionality of electron bonding, thereby reducing the Peierls stress.

**Property Improvements**: Lower hardness at cryogenic temperatures; reduced temperature sensitivity of hardness/yield stress.

**Risks and Limitations**: Proposed mechanism, not directly measured; based on correlation with electron concentration.

# Key Conclusions

1. Hardness of binary Mo alloys with Hf, Ta, W, Re, Os, Ir, and Pt can be correlated with electron concentration.

2. Alloy softening is produced in Mo by elements having an excess of s+d electrons compared to Mo (Re, Os, Ir, Pt). Elements with fewer s+d electrons (Hf, Ta) or an equal number (W) do not produce alloy softening.

3. Alloy hardening of Mo-Hf and Mo-Ta alloys and alloy softening of Mo-Re, Mo-Os, Mo-Ir, and Mo-Pt alloys can be correlated with the square root of solute content and the difference in electron concentration (ΔV). Alloy hardening in Mo-Re, Mo-Os, Mo-Ir, and Mo-Pt alloys obeys a similar correlation.

4. Scavenging of interstitials by solute elements does not appear responsible for alloy softening in Mo–sixth-period transition metal alloys.

5. Atomic size factor may play a minor role in hardness behavior of Mo-Hf, Mo-Ta, and Mo-W alloys but cannot be correlated with hardness behavior of Mo-Re, Mo-Os, Mo-Ir, and Mo-Pt alloys.

6. Alloy softening is not expected above approximately 487 K (0.169 Tm) in binary Mo–sixth-period element alloys.

7. The hardness minimum occurs at lower values of the electron concentration parameter (Ω = Cs^1/2 × ΔV) with increasing temperature; extrapolation to 0 K gives Ωmin = 3.17 (at.%)^1/2, corresponding to hardness minima at 10 at.% Re, 2.5 at.% Os, 1.1 at.% Ir, and 0.6 at.% Pt.

# Limitations and Applicable Scope

- This paper is a **hardness-only study**; no tensile, compressive, creep, or fracture toughness data are reported.
- Testing is limited to **low homologous temperatures** (77–411 K, i.e., 0.027–0.143 Tm for Mo). Conclusions about alloy softening are not directly applicable to high-temperature service conditions.
- The study is limited to **binary Mo alloys with sixth-period transition elements** (Hf, Ta, W, Re, Os, Ir, Pt). The findings may not extend to multi-component alloys or to other transition metal solvents without verification.
- The authors' conclusions about the controlling mechanism (Peierls stress reduction via electron concentration) are **inferred from correlations**, not from direct microstructural or electronic structure measurements. The paper notes that magnetic susceptibility measurements might provide further insight but does not include such data.
- The paper was published in 1972 and does not consider modern refractory high-entropy alloys, multi-principal element alloys, or additively manufactured alloys.
- No oxidation resistance data or high-temperature mechanical properties are reported.
- The applicability to modern engineering alloys is limited to understanding fundamental solid-solution effects in BCC refractory metal binary systems.
