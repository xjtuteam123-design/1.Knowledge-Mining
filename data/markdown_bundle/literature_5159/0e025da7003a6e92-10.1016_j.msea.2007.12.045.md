Abstract
The addition of 3 wt.% ruthenium (Ru) was found to improve both the intermediate temperature/intermediate stress and the high temperature/low
stress creep behaviours of two high refractory content single crystal Ni-base superalloys. Creep rupture tests, creep activation energy measurements
and microstructural analysis indicate that Ru serves a dual role; it enhances microstructural stability by suppressing the precipitation of deleterious
topologically close-packed (TCP) phases and it increases the level of solid solution strengthening of the /H9253phase. Its effectiveness as a TCP
suppressant increases with temperature whereas its potency as a strengthening element decreases with temperature. Ru reduces the stacking fault
energy of the /H9253phase and this is thought to have a signiﬁcant contribution towards its strengthening beneﬁt at intermediate temperatures. The
reduction in /H9253
′volume fraction upon the addition of Ru, which is central to its effectiveness as a stabilising element, appears to be the principal
cause of its diminishing strengthening contribution at elevated temperatures.

1. Introduction
Single crystal Ni-base superalloys have been through three
generations of development since their inception in the early
1980s. Advances have been achieved by alloying with increas-
ing levels of dense refractory elements, most notably rhenium
(Re), to speciﬁcally enhance the high temperature creep resis-
tance to better withstand the high centripetal forces at elevated
temperature imposed upon turbine blades in service[1–3].H o w -
ever, this trend in alloy design has been accompanied by a
progressive destabilisation of the/H9253//H9253
′microstructure due to the
precipitation of topologically close-packed (TCP) phases fol-
lowing prolonged exposures to elevated temperatures [2,4,5].
These phases disrupt the microstructure and deplete the impor-
tant strengthening elements from the desired phases resulting
in premature creep rupture at elevated temperatures [4,6–8].

2. Experimental procedure
Creep rupture tests were performed over a range of temper-
atures and stresses to assess the effect of a 3 wt.% Ru addition
on the creep lives of two third generation single crystal super-
alloys, SRR300D and CMSX-10N. The compositions of each
alloy are listed in Table 1. Note that the compositions of the
alloys are slightly diluted by the addition of Ru, however, these
changes are well within the elemental ranges of typical melt
chemistry speciﬁcations. SRR300D is an unstable experimen-
tal alloy prone to TCP precipitation whereas CMSX-10N is a
stable, commercially available alloy. Thus, the creep behaviour
of SRR300D+3 Ru was investigated to observe the effect of Ru
as a stabilising element, while CMSX-10N+3 Ru was tested to
observe the effect of Ru as a solid solution strengthening ele-
ment predominantly at temperatures where TCP precipitation is
not prevalent.
⟨001⟩oriented single crystal test bars of each alloy were
supplied by Rolls-Royce plc in the fully heat treated condition
and tested to rupture using constant load creep rigs under the
conditions listed in Table 2.
To rationalise the observed differences in creep rupture lives
upon the addition of Ru, the four alloys were subjected to con-
stant stress creep tests with systematic jumps in temperature to
discern any change in the creep activation energy.

3. Results
The creep rupture lives for all four alloys are plotted in the
form of a Larson–Miller diagram in Fig. 1. The addition of 3
wt.% Ru improved the creep life of both alloys over all the
conditions investigated with SRR300D beneﬁting most from
the addition. As illustrated, the improvement in creep life of
SRR300D upon the addition of Ru increases with temperature
as the base alloy becomes increasingly prone to TCP precipi-
tation. Indeed, at 1100
◦C the addition of 3 wt.% Ru yields an
almost nine-fold improvement in creep rupture life. This trend is
consistent with a previous study investigating the high temper-
ature creep performance of an experimental fourth generation
single crystal superalloy where Ni had been directly substi-
tuted by Ru [12]. This therefore indicates that any potential
additive effects from the dilution of the principal strengthening
elements in SRR300D+3 Ru are minor relative to the effect of Ru
itself.
The trend for CMSX-10N is opposite to that of SRR300D
with the beneﬁts in creep rupture upon the addition of Ru
diminishing with increasing temperature. For example, the
improvement in creep life associated with a 3 wt.% Ru addition
decreases from a factor of ∼1.9 at 850
◦C/490 MPa to∼1.04 at
1050 ◦C/140 MPa.

addition of 3 wt.% Ru improved the creep lives of both SRR300D
and CMSX-10N at all the conditions investigated, not simply
at those temperatures where TCP precipitation is life limit-
ing. This indicates that Ru serves a dual role as it enhances
both microstructural stability and the level of solid solution
strengthening. The changes in the creep activation energies
associated with a 3 wt.% Ru addition mirror the creep rupture
results. The associated beneﬁts increase with temperature for

As thermal activation rises with increasing temperature, both
the thermal lattice vibrations and the equilibrium number of
vacancies increase and the creep resistance becomes increas-
ingly controlled by diffusion and microstructural stability with
respect to TCP precipitation. Solute atoms tend to segregate
around dislocations to reduce the strain in the surrounding lat-
tice and thus minimize the overall strain energy. Work is required
to break the dislocation away from the solute cluster due to
the increase in the strain energy of the system. However, if the
relaxation time for the solute atom to diffuse along with the dislo-
cation is less than that for the dislocation to break away, then the
motion of the dislocation will involve the movement of the solute
pinning point along with the dislocation [24]. Furthermore, the
dislocation networks formed during primary creep at the /H9253//H9253
′
interfaces limit the mean free path of gliding dislocations. This
contribution to strain hardening is balanced during steady state
creep by dislocation recovery processes involving the rearrange-
ment and annihilation of dislocations to allow for continued
slip. The dislocation recovery process becomes increasingly
diffusion dependent with rising temperature as vacancies dif-
fuse towards or away from pinned dislocations allowing the
dislocation line to climb out of its original slip plane and into
an unimpeded conﬁguration. Therefore, at temperatures where
diffusion becomes appreciable, it is desirable to have a high con-
centration of slow diffusing elements in the /H9253channels which
continue to act as obstacles to dislocation propagation and inhibit
the rate of vacancy diffusion assisted climb of dislocations along
the /H9253//H9253
′interface. Expressions for the interdiffusion coefﬁcients
of Ru and the /H9253-strengthening elements in Ni have been derived
by Karunaratne et al. [25–27]. The diffusion coefﬁcients sug-
gest that at temperatures where diffusion becomes appreciable,
the faster diffusing Mo atoms would contribute less to the creep
resistance of the /H9253matrix. In fact, their presence may degrade
the alloy’s creep performance by promoting the precipitation
of TCP phases. In contrast, Ru appears to be an effective addi-
tion with an interdiffusion coefﬁcient between those of W and

enhances the creep response throughout the intermediate tem-

creep regimes. The suppression of TCP precipitation in the
presence of Ru translates to marked improvements in the creep
response at temperatures where TCP formation is prevalent. This
ensures the potent strengthening elements stay in solution and
strengthen the primary phases. This is complemented by the

5. Conclusions
• An addition of 3 wt.% Ru extends the creep rupture lives and
increases the steady state creep activation energies of both
SRR300D and CMSX-10N throughout the intermediate tem-
perature/intermediate stress and high temperature/low stress
creep regimes.
• The improvements increase with temperature for the alloy
prone to TCP precipitation, SRR300D, and decrease with
temperature for the inherently stable CMSX-10N alloy.
• Ru reduces the stacking fault energy of the /H9253matrix but its
effectiveness as a solid solution strengthener diminishes with
increasing temperature due to the corresponding destabilisa-
tion of the /H9253
′phase.
• Despite the reduced strengthening contributions from Ru at
elevated temperatures, the creep resistance of the base alloy
is enhanced by the suppression of TCP formation which
ensures the potent solid solution strengthening elements stay
in solution and strengthen the desired alloy phases and that
the /H9253//H9253
′microstructure remains uninterrupted by undesirable
secondary phases.
