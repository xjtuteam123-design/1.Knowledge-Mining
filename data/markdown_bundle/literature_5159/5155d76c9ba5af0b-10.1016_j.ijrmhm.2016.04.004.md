Microstructure, mechanical properties and fracture behavior of Nb with
minor Si addition

Controlling the content of elements in niobium solid solution (NbSS) is signiﬁcant for better comprehensive prop-
erties of Nb-Si based alloys. In this paper, the inﬂuences of minor Si (0, 0.5, 1, 2 and 3.5 at.%) on microstructures
and mechanical properties of arc melted Nb at room temperature have been investigated. The increasing addition
of Si would promote grain reﬁnement and increase Si concentration in NbSS.N b3Si and (NbSS +N b3Si) eutectic
phases are found when Si content is higher than 1 at.%. With the increase of Si concentration in NbSS, the Vickers
hardness, ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of Nb SS increases while elongation decreases. However, the fracture
toughness and UTS decrease from 1 at.% to 3.5 at.% because of the grain boundary precipitation of silicides. In ad-
dition, with Si addition from 0.5 at.% to 1 at.%, the fracture feature transforms from dimple to cleavage and re-
duces the elongation rapidly (23.4% to ~0%) because of the embrittlement of Nb
SS. Controlling Si concentration
to 0.5 at.% is beneﬁcial for strengthening the NbSS and retaining its good ductility and fracture toughness.

1. Introduction
Nb and Nb alloys possess attractive properties such as high melting
point, decent high temperature strength, room temperature ductility
and relatively low density compared to other refractory materials[1,2].
In recent decades, Nb-silicide based in-situ composites have attracted
considerable attention owing to their potential to replace nickel based
superalloys in high temperature structural applications in future gas tur-
bine engines [3–10]. Nb-Si binary alloy consists of Nb
SS and Nb-silicide
phase (Nb5Si3 and/or Nb3Si). Silicide phases exhibit good creep resis-
tance, high temperature strength and oxidation resistance but low
fracture toughness, while Nb
SS phase possesses decent ductility and
toughness but relatively low strength[11–14]. However, the conﬂict be-
tween strengthening and toughening in Nb-Si based alloys limits its ap-
plication[15]. To achieve excellent comprehensive properties, a strategy
is to limit the amount of solid solution and improve its room tempera-
ture properties [16]. The higher the ductility and toughness of the Nb
SS
in Nb-Si alloys, the less Nb SS is needed to achieve a particular value of
the fracture toughness. Besides, a smaller Nb SS volume fraction trans-
lates into improved oxidation and creep resistance. Therefore, it is of
great importance to improve the properties of NbSS.
Alloying is a common way to strengthen and toughen NbSS. Previous
studies have revealed that appropriate contents of alloying elements
(V, W, Mo, Ta, Ti, Hf, and O etc.) have a positive effect on improving
the strength of Nb in different levels [17–20], among which Ti and Hf
are the optimum solid solute elements without increasing the brittle-
to-ductile transition temperature (BDTT) and are bene ﬁcial for im-
proving the fracture toughness of Nb
SS [21,22].
As the indispensable element to form silicides, Si is also one of the
most important micro-solute elements in NbSS. According to the Nb-Si
binary phase diagram, the maximum solid solubility of Si in Nb is merely
3.5 at.% at 1915 °C [23], and drops rapidly to 0.5 at.% at 1675 °C [14].
Besides, the content of Si in NbSS changes with different processes. It is
1–2.7% of Si in Nb SS for arc-melted Nb-Si alloys, while it reduces to 0 –
1.2% after heat treatment [24]. The micro-amount of Si in Nb SS has
great inﬂuences on its properties, e.g., it is found that minor Si addition
(0–2a t . % )r eﬁnes NbSS grains but promotes the precipitation of brittle
Cr2Nb and silicide phases, thereby causing the fracture toughness of
NbSS alloys to decrease at ﬁrst but then increase [24]; Murayama et al.
have reported that the fracture toughness of NbSS single/multi-phase al-
loys would increase when decreasing the content of (Si + Al) in Nb SS
[25]. Due to the complex interaction of other alloying elements with Si
in NbSS,i nﬂuences of Si on the properties of Nb SS are still ambiguous.
Therefore, in this study, the microstructure and room temperature me-
chanical properties of (Nb, Si)
SS within the solubility of Si have been
systematically evaluated. It is found that micro-Si addition have signiﬁ-
cant effect on ductility, strength, toughness and fracture behavior of
Nb, which provides important guidance for improving the properties
of NbSS.

2. Experimental procedures
Nb-Si alloys with nominal compositions of Nb- xSi (x = 0, 0.5, 1, 2,
3.5 at.%) were produced using pure Nb and high-purity Si
(99.999 wt%) as the raw materials. The impurities present in the as re-
ceived Nb are given in Table 1, and it shows a high purity of Nb. Homo-
geneous master alloy button ingots were prepared by vacuum non-
consumable arc melting for six times in high purity protecting argon,
each ingot weighs 500 g. The arc melted samples were marked as
AM0, AM0.5, AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 respectively based on the Si content.
Samples for microstructure observation and property testing were
cut from the same position in each alloy ingot by electro-discharge
machining. X-ray diffraction (XRD; D/max 2200PC) with Cu K
α radia-
tion was carried out for phase identi ﬁcation. Optical microscopy (OM;
Leica Optical Microscope) was used for observing the morphology and
dimension of crystal grains, which were mechanically polished and
etched by an etchant consisting HF, HCl, HNO 3 and H2O with ratio of
6:6:3:2 for 30 s [19]. Grain sizes were determined as the average linear
intercept length on polished and etched specimens. Microstructures
were investigated by electron probe micro-analyzer (EPMA; Model
JXA-8100) and transmission electron microscope (TEM; FEI Tecnai
F30). Energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS; Oxford INCAPentaFET-x3)
was carried out to analyze the chemical composition. The result was
the average of at least 6 individual measurements. Phase volume frac-
tions were quantitatively analyzed by Image-Pro Plus software. A FM-
800 type micro-sclerometer with Vickers diamond pyramidal indenter
was used for micro-hardness testing with indentation loads of 100 g
for 15 s. Fracture toughness testing using single-edge U-notched
three-point bending specimens with dimensions of 30 × 6 × 3 mm

3. Results
3.1. Microstructures of arc melted alloys
The optical microstructures of Nb containing different levels of Si are
shown in Fig. 1(a–e). All the alloys exhibit mainly single-phase micro-
structure. As can be seen from Fig. 1(f), Nb grains are re ﬁned dramati-
cally with the addition of Si.
The X-ray diffraction patterns of the arc melted samples indicate
that all the alloys are mainly composed of Nb, and Nb
3Si is detected in
AM3.5, as shown in Fig. 2.
According to the results of EDS point analysis, the average Si con-
tents in Nb SS of AM alloys are given in Fig. 3 . With the addition of
Si, its concentration in Nb SS increases. In AM0.5, the average concen-
tration of Si in Nb is 0.48 at.%. The measurement values are very
close to their nominal composition values of master alloys. However,
the average Si contents in Nb SS of AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 alloys are
0.91 at.%, 1.58 at.% and 1.87 at.% respectively, which are less than the
nominal compositions values. It suggests that when Si content is not
less than 1 at.%, part of Si would precipitate from liquid to form silicide
phases.
The back scattered electron (BSE) images of arc melted samples
are displayed in Fig. 4 . The primary Nb
SS phases with white contrast
predominate in the microstructure, and the precipitates with black con-
trast are found in AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 samples. According to the XRD
and EDS results, these precipitates are Nb
3Si. Some Nb3Si particles inde-
pendently locate at the interior of NbSS phase, and others mainly exist in
(NbSS +N b3Si) eutectic cells. Eutectic phases tend to be located at grain
boundaries. In the eutectics, the secondary Nb SS particles with white
contrast display dot-like and rod-liked shapes due to their different
dimensional orientation. According to the binary Nb-Si phase diagram
[23], the Nb-rich side contains an eutectic reaction L → Nb
3Si + Nb
(18.2 at.% Si) at 1915 °C. Nb 3Si is often retained at room temperature
because the kinetics of the eutectoid decomposition of Nb 3Si to
Nb and α-Nb5Si3 are very slow [26]. A small number of Nb 3Si and
(NbSS +N b3Si) eutectic precipitates are distributed in AM1 and AM2
alloys but are not detected by XRD ( Fig. 2 ). Much more eutectic
precipitates occur in AM3.5 alloys. The volume fractions of Nb 3Si and
(NbSS +N b 3Si) eutectic phases in AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 are ~ 0.15%,
~ 1.2% and ~ 6.0%, respectively. In addition, no silicide is observed in
AM0 and AM0.5 alloys.
TEM bright ﬁeld images demonstrate the ﬁne structures and
morphologies of those precipitates in AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 alloys, as
illustrated in Fig. 5 .N b
3Si phases with relatively smooth surface
are several micrometers in diameter. The selected-area diffraction pat-
terns (SADPs) taken from different phases identify that Nb3Si phase
possesses a tetragonal form of PTi
3 structure, and the primary Nb SS
phase is a body centered cubic (bcc) structure. In addition, it can be
seen that in (Nb
SS +N b3Si) eutectic phase, Nb3Si is the edge of eutectic
cell.
3.2. Mechanical properties of arc melted alloys
The Vickers hardnesses of primary NbSS versus Si content (at.%) are
shown in Fig. 6(a). Each micro-hardness test data point reported here
represents an average of 10 individual measurements with identical
load. With the increase of Si content, Vickers hardness of the primary
NbSS increases signi ﬁcantly. AM3.5 alloy shows the highest hardness
(369.5 HV), which is ~4.5 times of AM0 alloy (81.8 HV).
The average room-temperature plane strain fracture toughness (KQ)
values of AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 alloys using three-point bending speci-
mens are presented inFig. 6(b). Each fracture toughness value reported
here represents an average of at least 5 tests. The fracture toughness
decreases from 24.4 MPa·m
1/2 to 12.6 MPa·m 1/2 with the addition of
Si. AM0 and AM0.5 alloys are bent but not ruptured under the same
pressure load because of their excellent plastic deformation.
The average ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and percent elongation
of arc melted alloys are illustrated in Fig. 6(c). Each tensile data point
reported here represents an average of at least 5 tests. The typical
engineering stress-strain curves of AM tensile samples are shown in
Fig. 6(d). The UTS of AM samples increases dramatically with the in-
crease of Si content in the range from 0 at.% to 1 at.%, but then decreases
from 1 at.% to 3.5 at.%. AM1 shows the highest UTS 424.7 MPa. Besides,
the elongation decreases with the increase of Si content, and plastic de-
formation stage is not found in AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 samples during
their tensile tests. AM0 displays the best ductility among the arc melted
alloys with 26.4% of elongation. It is noteworthy that the average UTS of
AM0.5 samples (383.7 MPa) is ~ 2.5 times of AM0 (152.1 MPa), and the
average elongation of AM0.5 samples (23.4%) can be comparable to
AM0. It indicates that 0.5 at.% Si improves tensile strength of Nb a lot
without signi ﬁcant deterioration of ductility. Moreover, a sharp de-
crease in elongation is observed from AM0.5 (23.4%) to AM1 (~ 0%). It
demonstrates that although 1 at.% Si improves the UTS dramatically, it
reduces ductility severely.

4. Discussions
4.1. Vickers hardness
A gradual increase in Vickers hardness with increasing Si is observed
and the graphic trend of Vickers hardness versus Si content is similar to
Fig. 3. Solid solution strengthening of Si on Nb is considered to be the
main reason due to the obvious difference of the atom radius between
Nb and Si. Since lattice distortion of the matrix increases with the
increase of the concentration of Si in Nb
SS, it impedes the dislocation
mobility and increase the deformation resistance of the matrix [22],
therefore improving the micro-hardness of Nb.
Another possible reason for improving micro-hardness is precipita-
tion hardening. Hard and brittle Nb 3Si precipitates disperse in Nb ma-
trix, which would contribute to enhance the micro-hardness of NbSS.
4.2. Room-temperature fracture toughness
Fracture toughness is reduced gradually with the Si addition from
1 at.% to 3.5 at.%. This may be due to the grain boundary embrittlement.
Fig. 7 shows the crack tips in three-point bend samples of AM1 and
AM3.5 alloys. The bridging mechanism of the main-cracks is found
both in AM1 alloy and AM3.5 alloys. Cracks propagate in the interior
of Nb
SS grains of AM1 alloy, however, cracks mainly propagate along

4.3. Room-temperature tensile properties
Macroscopic observation reveals that in AM0 and AM0.5 tensile
test samples, primary Nb SS phase shows a ridge-type fracture, which
strongly suggests a necking of the Nb SS prior to crack propagation.
However, the fracture sections of AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 tensile test
samples are extremely bright, which is a brittle fracture feature. It indi-
cates that the addition of Si transforms the fracture behavior of Nb.
Scanning electron micrographs of the tensile fractured surfaces of the
arc melted samples displayed in Fig. 8 reveal the fracture behavior.
The fracture surfaces of AM0 and AM0.5 samples are mainly featured
by dimples, which is a typical character of ductile fracture mode.
Dimples are generally deformed under the axial tensile force and
shear force due to the necking effect of samples. Therefore, AM0 and
AM0.5 display good ductility at room temperature. It indicates that
0.5 at.% Si dissolved into Nb will retain the intrinsic ductility of Nb ma-
trix. However, AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 samples are transgranular cleav-
age fractures characterized by river patterns and cleavage steps, and
the intergranular fracture characters are also observed in AM3.5 sample,
which shows a brittle fracture behavior. It seems that the dramatic de-
crease of ductility from AM0.5 to AM1 is caused by the transformation
of fracture behavior.
As mentioned above, although ~ 0.15% precipitates including Nb
3Si
phases and (Nb SS +N b 3Si) eutectic phases are found in AM1 alloy,
the embrittlement of grain boundaries may be one of reasons for the re-
duction of ductility, and the deformation of Nb
SS is restrained by grain
boundary precipitates. In addition, a similar phenomenon has been
reported that an addition of 0.5 wt% Si reduces the room temperature
ductility of Mo from 25% to 3% because of the embrittlement of grain
boundaries, which results in intergranular fracture of Mo[16,27].H o w -
ever, the Nb SS grains are ruptured by cleavage ( Fig. 8(c)) without no-
ticeable plastic deformation, and no intergranular fracture feature is
observed. It indicates that the NbSS is completely brittle in AM1 alloy be-
cause the Si concentration in NbSS increases and enhances the deforma-
tion resistance of NbSS and reduces its ductility signiﬁcantly. Chan et. al.
have reported that Al and Cr increase the number of (d + s) electrons in
the alloy, increase the Peierls-Nabarro (P-N) barrier energy, and reduce
the dislocation mobility, tensile ductility and fracture toughness. The
values for the number of (d + s) electrons per atom are 5 and 6 for
Nb and Cr, and (d + s) values of 6 and 7 are used for Al and Si respec-
tively because of the contribution of p electrons to bonding in the

The UTS of alloys improve gradually with the increase concentration
of Si in the range from 0 at.% to 1 at.% (Fig. 6 (c)). The solid solution
strengthening of NbSS by Si seems to be the major reason for improving
the room-temperature tensile strength. Because no silicides are found in
AM0 and AM0.5 and merely a few Nb 3Si precipitates are observed in
AM1, the Nb 3Si precipitation strengthening would play a limited role
in improving the UTS. Besides, Sankar reported that the tensile strength
is not affected by grain size [19], therefore the tensile strength may be
insensitive to grain reﬁnement. However, the UTS of alloys decrease un-
expectedly with the increase of Si addition from 1 at.% to 3.5 at.% in spite
that more Si dissolves in Nb and more silicides precipitate as strength-
ening phases. Some other factors are responsible for the strength degra-
dation, which are discussed in detail as follows.
In the longitudinal section of tensile samples for AM3.5 alloy, holes
and cracks with black contrast are close to the fracture section, as
shown in Fig. 9(a). Besides, there are relatively less and smaller holes
at the corresponding position of tensile samples in AM2 alloy. These
holes are mainly located in the (NbSS +N b3Si) eutectic areas, especially
in the intersections of grain boundaries. Some of Nb 3Si grains near the
fracture section might be internally broken after tensile testing, and
these Nb 3Si grains may drop out of the matrix during grinding or
polishing to form holes. In addition, cracks propagate along grain
boundaries where precipitates tend to exist. Therefore, the brittlement
of grain boundaries is one of the factors for reducing the strength.
Further studies show that in the longitudinal section of tensile sam-
ples of AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 alloys, high-density dislocation networks
are observed around the interfaces between (Nb
3Si + Nb SS) eutectic
phases and primary NbSS phases. The typical morphologies are shown
in Fig. 9(b). Actually, the (Nb 3Si + Nb SS) eutectic/primary-NbSS inter-
face is also the interface of Nb 3Si/primary-NbSS because Nb3Si phase is
the edge of (Nb 3Si + Nb SS) eutectic cell, as shown in Fig. 4 (f). Since
these alloys are composed of relatively ductile Nb SS and brittle Nb 3Si
phases, the difference in the elastic modulus and hardness between
the two phases may result in a mismatch at the interfaces, especially
in continuity of deformation [15]. When the primary NbSS suffers from
plastic deformation, dislocation glide would be impeded by brittle
Nb
3Si particles. Dislocations gather at Nb3Si/primary-NbSS interfaces to
form dislocation networks, which easily cause the stress concentration
at the phase interfaces during the tensile test. It may lead to the fracture
of brittle Nb
3Si phases, or cause the debonding of Nb 3Si/primary-NbSS
interfaces, resulting in the premature failure of alloys. Some sub-
microcracks mainly distributing in (Nb
SS +N b3Si) eutectic phases or
along the interfaces of Nb 3Si/primary-NbSS in AM3.5 alloy, as marked
in Fig. 10 . Relatively fewer sub-microcracks are also found in AM2
alloys. In earlier modeling studies, the Nb SS/silicide phase interface is
often considered to be theoretically strong [28]. Sha et al. have found
that with two-phase NbSS/Nb5Si3 microstructures, the compressive dam-
age at high temperature was dominated by debonding of the interfaces
between the primary Nb
SS and the Nb 5Si3, while the fracture mode at
room temperature is transgranular, controlled by the primary NbSS cleav-
age [15]. In addition, Shang et al have calculated that Nb-Si bond is weak-
er than Nb-NbSi and Nb-Nb bonds in the Nb (001)/ α-Nb5Si3 (001)
interface, which means that the Nb-Si bond in the interface is the most
possible site for the microcracks generation [29]. The debonding of

In conclusion, controlling the Si concentration to 0.5 at.% is beneﬁ t
for strengthening (Nb, Si) SS and retaining good ductility and fracture
toughness at room temperature. Optimizing heat treatment of Nb-
silicide based in-situ composites is considered to be an effective way
in controlling the Si concentration in NbSS phase improving the compre-
hensive performance of Nb-Si based alloys, which is necessary in further
investigations.
5. Conclusions
The inﬂuences of minor Si on the microstructures and mechanical
properties of arc melted Nb at room temperature were investigated.
Several conclusions could be derived from this study:
(1) With the addition of Si, the concentration of Si in Nb
SS increases
gradually. Nb3Si and (Nb SS +N b3Si) eutectic phases are found
in AM1, AM2 and AM3.5 alloys. The increasing addition of Si
promotes grain re ﬁnement and increases the concentration of
Si in NbSS.
(2) With the gaining content of Si, the Vickers hardness of the prima-
ry Nb SS increases while the elongation decreases. Particularly,
the elongation of Nb drops rapidly from 23.4% to ~0% with Si ad-
dition from 0.5 at.% to 1 at.% at room temperature. It is mainly
caused by the embrittlement of Nb SS because of solution of Si,
and transforming the fracture behavior from ductile dimple frac-
ture to brittle cleavage fracture.
(3) The UTS increases dramatically with the increase of Si content in
the range from 0 at.% to 1 at.% because of the solid solution
strengthening of Si. However, the UTS and fracture toughness de-
crease from 1 at.% to 3.5 at.% as results of the grain boundary pre-
cipitation of brittle Nb
3Si and (NbSS +N b3Si) eutectic phases.
(4) Controlling the Si concentration to 0.5 at.% is bene ﬁcial for
strengthening the NbSS and retaining its good ductility and frac-
ture toughness at room temperature.
