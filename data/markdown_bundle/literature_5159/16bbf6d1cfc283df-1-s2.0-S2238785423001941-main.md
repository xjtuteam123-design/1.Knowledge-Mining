abstract
High-strength and low-density materials are always needed in the aerospace, trans-
portation, and other ﬁelds where lightweight is strictly required. The concept of the high-
entropy alloy provides a new idea for designing the alloy with lightweight and high-
strength. Mechanical properties and microstructure of the (TiZrV)
100-xAlx (x ¼ 10, 16, 25,
at. %) medium-entropy alloys with different element Al content were investigated. With
the increase of the element Al content in the (TiZrV)
100-xAlx (x ¼ 10, 16, 25, at. %) medium-
entropy alloys, the densities of the specimens with the x ¼ 10, 16, and 25 are 5.49, 5.37, and
5.15 g cm /C0 3, respectively. The (TiZrV) 90Al10 alloy and the (TiZrV) 84Al16 alloy consist of the
Ti(Zr,V) matrix phase and the ZrAl 2 and Zr eV second phases, while the second phases in
the (TiZrV) 75Al25 alloy include the ZrAl 2 and Zr eV and VAl 2 phases. Also, more element Al
is added, the coarser second phase grains tend to form in the alloys, resulting in decreasing
of the room-temperature ductility and increasing of the high-temperature mechanical
strength. Compressive ratio of the (TiZrV)
100-xAlx (x ¼ 10, 16, 25, at. %) medium-entropy
alloys are larger than 30% at 1000 /C14 C, and the (TiZrV) 75Al25 alloy has a yield strength
184 MPa. The second phase grains are dispersed in the matrix, which can improve the
deformation resistance of the alloy.

1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) and Medium-entropy alloys
(MEAs) have attracted extensive attention due to their supe-
rior properties, such as excellent ductility, high strength, and
toughness [1,2]. High-entropy alloys are those containing ﬁve
or more principal elements in equal or near equal atomic
ratios, and medium-entropy alloys contain three or four
principal elements [3]. Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs),
containing refractory elements including Ti, Zr, V, Cr, Nb, Mo,
Ta, W, Hf, etc, are potential to be applied at a high-
temperatures on account of their excellent performance at
elevated-temperature [ 4,5], and there are many RHEAs have
been studied thoroughly, such as TiZrHfMoNb [ 6] and TiZrV-
MoNb alloys [ 7]. However, these RHEAs normally have a

for their application [ 7]. In general, the TiZrVAl system MEAs
have some advantages of the TiAl alloy and provides a new

It was noted that the addition of the element Al could sta-

However, the element Al tends to form the intermetallic

However, the ductility of this alloy is low at room-temperature,

the room-temperature ductility can be improved by altering
the microstructure characteristics, Zheng et al. [16] carried out
heat treatment of the Ti-43.5Al e4Nbe1Mo-0.1 B alloy, gener-

In the current work, a series of TiZrVAl MEAs with different
element Al contents were developed. Effects of the element Al
addition on the microstructure, phase composition, and
density of the (TiZrV)

studied. The compressive mechanical properties and micro-

and high-temperature (1000

strengthening mechanism for the (TiZrV) 75Al25 alloy at the
high-temperature has been discussed.
2. Materials and methods
The (TiZrV) 100-xAlx (x ¼ 10, 16, 25, at. %) MEAs ingots were
prepared using the vacuum-arc-melting method in a high-
purity argon atmosphere inside a water-cooled copper cruci-
ble. Purities of the original materials were better than 99.9%
and the ingots were ﬂipped over and re-melted at least 2 times
to assure the chemical homogeneity. Actual chemical
composition of the prepared ingot was determined by the
OPTIMA8000 inductively coupled plasma-atomic emission
spectrometer (ICP-AES) (PerkinElmer, The United States).
Cylindrical specimens, with sizes of 12 mm in length and
8 mm in diameter, for the compression testing were electric-
discharged machined from the ingots and carefully polished
before testing. The quasi-static compression tests at room-
temperature were conducted on the Instron 8802 testing sys-
tem machine (Instron Co., The United States) with a strain rate
of 1 /C2 10
/C0 3 s/C0 1. High-temperature compression tests were car-
ried out on the Gleeble 3180 thermal simulator (Data Sciences
International, The United States) at 1000 /C14 C, using a strain rate
of 1 /C2 10/C0 1 s/C0 1 until a total reduction of 35% was achieved. With
a ﬁxed heating rate of 10 /C14 Cs /C0 1, after heating the sample to the
sited temperature, then held for 240 s to ensure temperature
uniformity. After compression, the specimens were water
quenched immediately to preserve the deformed microstruc-
tures. All experiments at the high-temperature were carried
out in a chamber ﬁlled with pressurized argon to reduce
oxidation of the samples. For microstructural observations, the
hot-deformed samples were cutting along the compression
direction, the surfaces were carefully ground with sandpapers
and electropolished to a mirror ﬁnish.
The phase composition of the as-cast MEAs were identiﬁed
via the X-ray diffractometry (XRD) using the D/MAX-2550
diffractometer (Rigaku Corporation, Japan) with a Cu-K a ra-
diation operating at 40 kV/250 mA and a scanning step 0.02
/C14
from 2q ¼ 20/C14 e100/C14 . Ingot samples were sectioned, ground and
ﬁnally polished sequentially with a diamond paste (1.5 mm)
suspension. Then, the polished faces were chemically etched
in an aqueous solution containing 5% hydroﬂuoric acid, 15%
nitric acid, and the optical microstructures were acquired
using the POLYVER-MET optical microscope (OM) (Leica, Ger-
many). The elements distributions of the as-cast ingots were
analyzed by the JXA-8230 electron probe microanalyzer
(EPMA) (JEOL, Japan). The morphologies of the MEAs were
investigated by the MIRA3 LMH ﬁeld emission scanning elec-
tron microscope (SEM) (TESCAN, The Czech Republic) oper-
ated at 7.5 kV equipped with the XMAX20 energy dispersive X-
ray spectrometry (Oxford Instruments, Britain). Densities of
the alloys were measured using a pycnometer.

similar atomic sizes and can form inﬁnite solid solutions, so
the addition of the element V can reduce the lattice distortion
and release residual stress.
In the TiZrVAl system alloys, content values of the ele-
ments Ti, Zr and V are similar. Element Al may form com-
pounds with the elements Ti, Zr, and V, among which the
ZreAl compound has the smallest formation enthalpy. In
addition, ﬁrst-principles calculations show that the ZrAl
2

stable compound in the Zr eAl system [ 25]. So, in the TiZrVAl
system alloys, the ZrAl

the Zr eAl compounds (ZrAl 3、ZrAl2、Zr2Al3、ZrAl、Zr5Al4、

physical properties, the largest elastic modulus, the strongest
atomic average bonding strength [ 26], and the highest hard-
ness [ 27]. In the TiZrVAl system alloys, the Laves phase
dominated by the elements V and Zr may be formed, that
means in a Zr eAl system and without enough available ele-
ments Al for Zr, the Zr eV phases may appear in the alloy.
4.2. Deformation mechanism at high-temperature
The second phases and matrix phase of the (TiZrV) 100-xAlx
(x ¼ 10, 16, 25, at. %) alloys play a coordinating role under
compression deformation at the high-temperature, resulting in
high macroscopic plasticity of the alloys. Different from the
(TiZrV)
90Al10 and (TiZrV)84Al16 alloys, second phases appear in
the (TiZrV) 75Al25 alloy in the process of high-temperature
deformation. Shape of the second phase grains are mainly
rod-like and spherical, and the size ranging from 0.5mmt o2 mm.
The second phases in the (TiZrV)
75Al25 alloy might be ZrAl2 and
VAl2 phases, due to the decomposition of supersaturated AleZr
alloy during aging or homogenization, many Al 2(Zr,V) disper-
sions are formed, and these dispersions have thermal stability
at high-temperatures [28,29]. At the high-temperatures, these
intermetallic have good deformation ability, which can
improve the strength of the (TiZrV)
75Al25 alloy while ensuring
the plasticity. In general, the second phase grains with sizes
below 0.1 mm play the role of phase transformation strength-
ening. However, the second phase particles larger than 1 mm
can promote the crack growth because of their inherent brit-
tleness and low bonding strength with the matrix, which leads
to the possibility of the crack nucleation in the second phase
and tends to expand towards the phase interface to release the
stress concentration at the tip.
A schematic illustration of the deformation process of the
(TiZrV)
75Al25 alloy in compressive test at 1000 /C14 C is shown in
Fig. 11. As shown in Fig. 11 (I) , at the initial stage of deforma-
tion, the second phase grains formed dynamically. The sec-
ond phase grains are dispersed on the matrix, which can
hinder the movement of grain boundaries, and accumulate
and proliferate grain boundaries nearby, which improves the
work hardening effect of the alloy. At the same time, when the
diameter of the second phase grains is smaller and the
spacing is smaller, it is more inclined to hinder the dendritic
deformation, which makes the strengthening and softening
effect form a dynamic balance. As deformation continues, the
second phase grains grew up, the grain boundaries are difﬁ-
cult to cut through or bypass the second phase grains with
larger hardness and size, so the dislocation will gather near
the particle. The interaction between grains leads to the for-
mation of cellular substructures and eventually to the for-
mation of new grains with large angles, as shown in Fig. 11 (II).
The second phase grains with a certain size in the alloy can
promote the dendritic deformation, so that the softening ef-
fect is greater than the hardening effect, resulting in a second
peak on the curve, and the ﬂow stress begins to decline.

As shown in Fig. 11 (III) , with the increase of deformation
amount, a deformation zone with a certain gradient of
orientation difference appears around the second-phase
grains, causing stress concentration. Moreover, there is a
difference in the slip performance between the second phases
and the Ti matrix phase, so the pores are generated at the
interface of the two phases. Due to the failure of dislocation
slip and diffusion creep to release stress rapidly, and the faster
nucleation rate of the pores, independent distribution of
microscopic pores appeared in the alloy. When the amount of
deformation continues to increase, some microscopic pores
grow up or coalesce to form pores of different sizes. When the
expansion and aggregation of the pores reach a certain degree,
they will connect with the nearby pores, and then generate
larger pores and even microscopic cracks, as shown in Fig. 11
(IV). The formation and expansion of the cavity can relax the
stress concentration, strengthen the softening effect and in-
crease the sawtooth amplitude of the rheological curve.
5. Conclusions
(1) With the increase of the element Al content in the
(TiZrV)100-xAlx (x ¼ 10, 16, 25, at. %) MEAs, its beneﬁcial
effect is to reduce the density, which are 5.49 g cm /C0 3,
5.37 g cm /C0 3, and 5.15 g cm /C0 3, respectively. At room
temperature, the (TiZrV) 90Al10 alloy has better me-
chanical properties than the other two alloys, the frac-
ture strength and fracture strain reaching 1216 MPa and
6.72%, respectively. More intermetallic compounds
exist in alloys with higher element Al content, which
reduces their strength and fracture strain.
(2) At 1000
/C14 C, the (TiZrV) 100-xAlx (x ¼ 10, 16, 25, at. %)
MEAs showed superior compressive plastic strain
compared with conventional TiAl superalloy, reaching
from 35 at. % to 45 at. %, which result from the coor-
dination between soft matrix phase and hard second
phase grains.
(3) At 1000
/C14 C, second phases are dynamically generated in
the (TiZrV) 75Al25 alloy. The second phase grains are
dispersed on the matrix, which can improve the defor-
mation resistance of the alloy by hindering the grains
motion and play a role in the phase transformation
strengthening. When sizes of the second phase grains
are larger, it is easier to promote the growth and
expansion of the crack, release the stress, strengthen
the softening effect, and improve the plasticity.
