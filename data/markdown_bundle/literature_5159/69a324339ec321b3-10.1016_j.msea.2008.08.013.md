abstract
A new approach to the design of Ni-based single crystal superalloys is proposed. It is based on a concept that
under given structural conditions, the creep-rupture characteristics of superalloys are mainly determined
by interatomic bonding given by the cohesive energy. In order to characterize the individual contribution
of each alloying element to the strength properties at high temperature, we introduce a parameter, /US,
which is the partial molar cohesive energy of an alloy component. This parameter is then obtained in the
total energy ﬁrst-principles calculations for a usual set of alloying elements. We demonstrate that creep-
rupture characteristics of alloys indeed correlate with the total gain partial molar cohesive energy due
to alloying and ﬁnd that W, Ta, and Re have the highest values of /US, and should therefore play the major
role in providing high-temperature strength of superalloys. Based on this ﬁnding, we design three new
superalloys with a high content of W and show that they have superior creep-rupture properties compared
not only with their counterparts with the lower content of W, but also with the best Ru-bearing Ni-based
superalloys.

1. Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys are key materials for gas turbine
engines where high strength at high temperatures is required.
During the last three decades, the working characteristics of super-
alloys have been remarkably improved mostly due to the use of
single crystal technology [1] and alloying elements such as Ta, Re
[2–4] and Ru [5,6]. However, the use of a Pt-group element Ru,
which improves essentially the creep strength, increases the cost
of superalloys dramatically. Therefore a design of Ni-based superal-
loys without Pt-group elements having the same level of the creep
strength is of a great practical importance. At the same time, this is
a very difﬁcult problem, since mechanical properties of an alloy is
a very complex function of not only alloy composition but also its
structure and microstructure.
However, taking into consideration the fact that modern Ni-
based superalloys are single crystals without grain boundaries, one
can try to solve this task qualitatively, neglecting the structural

case, the creep strength of the material is directly related to the

identify the relevant physical parameter, which can be determined
either theoretically or experimentally.
In this paper, we suggest to use as such a parameter the par-
tial molar cohesive energy of an alloy component in a Ni-fcc solid
solution. We calculate this quantity using state-of-the-art ﬁrst-
principles technique and employ the obtained parameters as a
guide for a design of a new generation of Ni-based superalloys.
We test our theoretical predictions on three alloy compositions
and show that they have in fact superior mechanical properties in
comparison with the latest generation of Ni-based superalloys.

according to our results, Os should produce much better results

of Os as an alloying addition in Ni-based superalloys.

The results presented above clearly show the importance of W
for providing high strength of Ni-based superalloys. It is interesting
to note in this respect, that 50 years ago such well-known Ni-
based superalloys as MAR-M200 and GS-6U did not have Ta and Re
as alloying additions but instead they contained a relatively large
amount of W: 12.5 wt.% in MAR-M200 and 10.5 wt.% in GS-6U. Later,
when ﬁrst Ta and then Re were introduced into the alloying system,
W was partially substituted for Ta and Re. As a result, the amount
of W in the Ni-based superalloys was gradually decreasing with
increasing the concentration of Ta and Re. This tendency can be
easily seen, for instance, in the evolution of the chemical compo-
sitions of a number of CMSX superalloys (CMSX is a trademark of
the Cannon-Muskegon Corporation). The concentration of W in the
CMSX-2 (the ﬁrst generation Ni-based superalloys for single crystal
blades) was 8 wt.% and the concentration of Ta 6 wt.%. In the CMSX-
4 (the second generation) the concentration of W was 6 wt.%, but it
also consisted of 6.5 wt.% of Ta and 3 wt.% of Re. Finally, in the CMSX-
10M (the third generation) the concentration of Ta and Re was
increased up to 8 and 6 wt.%, respectively, and the concentration
of W was reduced to 5 wt.%.
Although Ta and Re exhibit almost equally large values of
/US, therefore producing an approximately equal effect on creep-
rupture properties as W, it is clear that a much better approach
would be to add them at the expense of Ni while maintaining a
high concentration of W. If we take into account a large solubility
of W in Ni such an approach seems to be quite reasonable.

6. Conclusions
A new concept facilitating the design of nickel-based superal-
loys for single crystal turbine blades is developed. It is shown that
the best alloying elements for achieving high-temperature strength
properties are W, Ta, and Re.
Three experimental Ni-based superalloys for single crystal tur-
bine blades are designed: (1) KS-1 based on (Ni–Al)–16W–5Ta
without Re has an improved long-term strength in comparison with
CMSX-2; (2) KS-2 based on (Ni–Al)–12W–8Ta–2Re has the same

a better performance than CMSX-10 and the Ru-bearing superalloy
