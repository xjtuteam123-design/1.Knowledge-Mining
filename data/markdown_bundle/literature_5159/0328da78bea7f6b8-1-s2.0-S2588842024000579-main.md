ABSTRACT
Long-term thermal exposure-induced γ ′ coarsening strongly influences the mechanical properties of Ni-base
superalloys and high/medium entropy alloys (H/MEAs), which has long been of scientific and industrial
concern. In revealing the coarsening behavior, a great deal of theorical research has been made over several
decades. One major advance is the development of Ostwald ripening kinetics theories, which allows for a
quantitative description of γ ′ coarsening kinetics. Nowadays, there have been two types of theorical models in
wide acceptance, which advocate the matrix-diffusion controlled (LSW-family models) and trans-interface-
diffusion controlled (TIDC model) kinetics mechanisms, respectively. Both of them have been validated in ex-
periments and computational simulations. Besides, another major advance is the theorical revelation of the
particle morphology evolution as γ ′ coarsening, by means of energetic calculations and phase-field simulations. It
has been shown that the morphological evolution depends on the combined effects of interfacial energy, elastic
strain energy, and elastic interaction energy. The latter two generally play a dominate role in particle shape
changes and regular spatial rearrangements, respectively. Based on these theories, the γ ′ coarsening kinetics and
morphological evolution patterns in Ni-base superalloys and H/MEAs has been clearly revealed and compared in
many studies. Herein, we present a review on the development of these γ ′ coarsening theories and their appli-
cations. This is not only instructive for alloy design and failure prevention, but also informative for further
theorical extensions.
1. Introduction
High-temperature alloys are mainly faced with the needs of aero-
space, power, and nuclear industries for use in major heat-bearing
components. The γ ′ -strengthened Ni-base superalloys, developed from
the earliest Nimonic75 to the most advanced fifth-generation single-
crystal systems, are still the workhorses in service to date [ 1 ]. In recent
years, a new concept alloy system, i.e. high/medium entropy alloys
(H/MEAs) [ 2 ], has been gradually emerging, which are expected to
break through the performance limits of conventional alloys and bring a
revolution in the applications of metallic materials. Amongst, a class of
3d transition metal H/MEAs [ 3 , 4 ], like NiCoCr-systems, are regarded as
the extensions of Ni-base superalloys in terms of compositional designs.
As inheriting the γ ′ -strengthening strategy, they have exhibited better
thermo-mechanical properties for being a promising candidate in
elevated-temperature fields.
The typical microstructural component, γ ′ precipitation, with an L1
2
order crystal structure, is generally a strengthener against high tem-
perature softening. It has gained importance as early as the 1929s in
high-temperature austenitic steels [ 5 ], then culminated in Ni-base su-
peralloys [ 6 , 7 ], and enjoys a renewed appreciation nowadays in
H/MEAs [ 8 , 9 ]. Unfortunately, despite the decent thermal stability, the
inevitable coarsening hazard of γ ′ precipitates under prolonged thermal
exposure has long been of concern, whether scientifically or industrially.
As a large amount of material failure analyses for heat-bearing compo-
nents has pointed the finger at γ ′ coarsening [ 10 , 11 ], where the temporal
variations of particle sizes, shapes, arrangements, etc., are responsible
for the deterioration of service performance. Over the past decades, this
issue has received extensive attention in Ni-base superalloys, particu-
larly with the binary Ni – Al system as a typical prototype [ 12 – 14 ]. With
the recent development of H/MEAs into high-temperature applications,
the same concern of γ ′ coarsening has permeated into them as well [ 3 , 4 ].

How the coarsening behavior of multicomponent γ ′ precipitates in
H/MEAs is similar or different from that of single-principal-component
ones in Ni-base superalloys; and whether the γ ′ coarsening problem
can be effectively mitigated in H/MEAs compared with Ni-base super-
alloys, have received more and more attention [ 15 ].
The coarsening of γ ′ precipitates typically initiates at the endpoint of
phase separation where the residual-supersaturation is minimal. This is
driven by the intrinsically thermodynamic instability, i.e., the systemic
state does not meet the minimum energy configuration because of the
excess surface energy represented by particulate ensembles [ 16 ]. In this
case, the large γ ′ particles always grow at the expense of smaller ones in
order to reduce the interface area and thus the total interfacial energy.
This is known as Ostwald ripening [ 17 ]. Such the interfacial
energy-driven spontaneous behavior controlled by atomic diffusion,
which is closely related to the chemical concentration gradients. Spe-
cifically, the equilibrium solute concentration at the surfaces of larger
particles is lower than that of smaller ones, thus the solute atoms will
flow along the concentration gradients to larger particles. In other
words, according to the curvature dependence of chemical potential, the
mass transfer always proceeds from the regions with higher interfacial
curvature to those with lower interfacial curvature [ 18 ]. Although the
Ostwald ripening mechanism having been uncovered early on, surpris-
ingly, there has been little reasonable theoretical description for it about
60 years following its initial discovery. Until the first theoretical model
was published by Lifshitz and Slyozov [ 19 ] and Wagner (LSW theory)
[ 20 ], which immediately stimulated a surge of interest in γ ′ coarsening
kinetics. Various modifications and extensions have been successively
made to the LSW model to account for more reality factors, such as
precipitate volume fractions and multicomponent conditions [ 21 ]. The
LSW-family theories all support a matrix-diffusion-controlled kinetics
mechanism. Recently, another novel Ostwald ripening theory was pro-
posed by Ardell et al. [ 22 ], which advocates a
tran-interface-diffusion-controlled (TIDC) kinetics mechanism. All of
these theorical models have widely been applied to quantitatively
evaluate the γ ′ coarsening kinetics. It has been shown that most of the
H/MEAs generally present better γ ′ thermal stability compared to the
conventional Ni-base superalloys [ 3 , 4 , 6 , 7 , 15 ].
Apart from the coarsening kinetics, the particle morphology evolu-
tion as γ ′ coarsening has also been of interest, which mainly involves of
the particle shapes and spatial arrangements [ 23 , 24 ]. A series of
morphological evolution theories based on energetic calculations and
phase-field simulations have been developed [ 25 , 26 ]. It is revealed that
the γ ′ morphology evolution depends on the combined effects of inter-
facial energy, elastic strain energy, and elastic interaction energy.
Generally speaking, the interfacial energy favors the particles to remain
spherical [ 27 ], while the elastic strain energy contributes to the shape
changes from spherical into fourfold and then to twofold symmetric
[ 28 ], and the elastic interaction energy promotes the regular spatial
arrangements of γ ′ particles along the < 100 > crystal directions [ 29 ].
The actual morphological evolution closely relates to the lattice misfit,
particle size, and thermal exposure temperature, etc., due to their in-
terferences on the competition of interfacial energy and elastic energy.
Based on these, there have been some empirical criteria developed and
validated in Ni-base superalloys and H/MEAs [ 30 , 31 ]. Besides, in actual
γ ′ coarsening, such as particle coalescence [ 32 ], particle splitting [ 33 ],
and particle arrangement defects [ 34 ], are also noteworthy, which can
interfere with the particle morphology deviating from the ideal equi-
librium state. With the support of these known theories, the particle
morphology evolvement patterns as γ ′ coarsening have been uncovered
in many Ni-base superalloys and H/MEAs [ 6 , 15 ]. In summary, all of the
morphological evolutions are in response to minimizing the total system
energy.
This paper revisits the development of Ostwald ripening kinetics
theories and particle morphology evolution theories of γ ′ coarsening.
The major theoretical achievements as well as the logical lines of
theoretical progress are summarized. Also, the research status of γ ′
coarsening in Ni-base superalloys and H/MEAs is reviewed. This work
will not only provide guidance for alloy design and failure prevention,
but also provide a systematic reference for further theorical extensions.
2. Progress in Ostwald ripening kinetics theories
2.1. LSW theory

3. Dominant roles of elastic energy on particle morphology
evolvement in γ′ coarsening
Above, we mainly review the development of Ostwald ripening ki-
netics theories. A substantially acknowledged understanding of γ ′
coarsening pattens has been established with respect to the particle size
growth, PSDs, and so on. However, the coarsening kinetic theories focus
only on the interfacial energy effect, but not the elastic energy effect. In
actual γ ′ coarsening, the elastic energy unavoidably ensues from the
lattice mismatches, modulus mismatches (elastic strain energy), and
elastic strain field interactions (elastic interaction energy). It can be of

3.1. Theorical equilibrium shapes of isolated particles in γ ′ coarsening

equilibrium shapes must be convex and tend to end up being spherical
with γ ′ coarsening, i.e., the Wulff configuration [ 27 , 91 ]. However, this is
not the case in fact. A series of experiments have witnessed the shape
changes with γ ′ coarsening, like from spheres to cubes, or to plates [ 6 , 92 ,
93 ]. It was clarified that the elastic strain energy plays an important role.
All the morphological evolutions are catered for minimizing the sum of
elastic and interfacial energies, rather than the later only.
Initially, a great deal of research has focused only on the elastic
energy-based calculations to reveal its effect on the shape evolution of γ ′
particles [ 89 , 90 ]. For a more realistic description, Johnson et al. [ 94 , 95 ]

time. In these studies, one noteworthy fact is that most of the
energy-based calculations have followed a restriction where the deter-
mined particle shapes get limited within a fixed class of geometric

convenient for determining the elastic energy, it is questionable whether
the particle shape given in this way is in thermodynamic equilibrium
[ 90 ].

3.2. Spatial correlation arrangements and related equilibrium shape
transitions
The interest with respect to the spatial correlation arrangements of γ ′
particles can be traced back to the 1950s, with some studies on the
“ modulation ” structures in Ni-base superalloys [ 23 ]. Corresponding
underlying mechanisms have been elucidated based on energy-based
calculations [ 104 – 106 ]. As proven that the periodic arrays of γ ′ pre-
cipitates along the < 100 > directions are mainly attributed to the elastic
interaction energy. The < 110 > and < 111 > alignments present a
repulsive force (positive values), while the < 100 > alignment exhibits an
attractive force (negative values), as shown in Fig. 5 a. Detailed calcu-
lations can be referred in the several excellent papers [ 25 , 29 , 107 , 108 ].
In fact, the shape evolutions of γ ′ particles generally proceed together
with the spatially regular particle rearrangements [ 23,111 – 114 ]. Su
et al. [ 109 ] researched the effects of elastic stress on the particle shape
changes and spatial arrangements with γ ′ coarsening. As shown in
Fig. 5 b, the two equal-sized circular particles are initially aligned at an
angle to the [ 100 ] direction, and the attractive interactions cause the
particles to translation through the morphological adjustments until the
centers-of-mass are aligned along the [ 100 ] direction. Such the
morphological change is a result of the strong local mass transfer be-
tween the two particles. It was concluded that the elastic interactions
could cause a deviation from the isolated-particle equilibrium shapes.
Specifically, the aspect ratio (the largest length divided by the smallest
one) of particles is increased when the L < 5 . 6, while the opposite
happens when the L > 5 . 6. In other words, the smaller particles tend to
be the twofold symmetry, while the larger ones could remain the four-
fold symmetry. These phenomena have often been observed in experi-
ments [ 115 ]. Thornton et al. [ 88 ] studied the effects of elastic
interaction energy on particle shapes and spatial correlations by means
of 2D simulation. As shown in Fig. 6 a, the spatial alignments along the
< 100 > directions and the shape evolution can be observed as the L
value increases. Notably, for the snapshot taken at L = 7 . 1, there exist
some large particles with square-like shapes and some small ones with
twofold symmetric shapes. This does conform to Su ’ conclusion [ 109 ].
The authors suggested that the elastic interaction effects can be
considered as a flaw for the supercritical bifurcation theory. For L < 3,
the elastic interaction is not strong enough to influence the particle
shapes significantly; for L ≈ 4, the particles become elongated than the
isolated-particle equilibrium shape; for L > 5 . 6, the fourfold shapes can
be stabilized in some cases. Furthermore, Lund et al. [ 116 ] also arrived
at some similar conclusions. They found that the larger particles tend to
be more equiaxed than the smaller ones. It was rationalized by consid-
ering the interparticle configurational force, as shown in Fig. 6 b. For the
two particles aligned along the elastically soft < 100 > directions, the
interparticle configurational forces are repulsive at the face centers and
attractive at the particle corners. A simplified schematic diagram is
shown in Fig. 6 c, where the particle on the left is growing while the right
one is dissolving. In this process, the particle corners tend to remain
aligned due to the attractive force, which results in the particles with
relatively constant edge lengths along the X- and Z-axis directions, so
that they mainly grow or dissolve along the Y-axis direction. This is the
reason why the large particles are more likely to be isometric than the
small ones. Furthermore, it is not difficult to understand that such the
configurational force pattern is responsible for the formation of concave
cubic shapes, as shown in Fig. 6 d.
In summary, the shapes of equilibrium-separated-aligned γ ′ particles
depend strongly on the elastic interaction instead of being a function of
the parameter L alone. Recently, more and more computational simu-
lation studies have focused on the role of elastic interaction energy on
the particle shape evolution [ 119 – 123 ].

3.4. Controversy on γ ′ splitting
In the early 1980s, Miyazaki et al. [ 140 , 141 ] carried out a remark-

significant deal of theoretical and experimental research on particle
splitting. Two types of prevalent splitting patterns have extensively been
reported in Ni-base superalloys, i.e., doublets of plates and octets of

5. Morphological evolvement of γ′ particles in coarsening
As stated above, there is not only sized growth but also morpho-
logical evolvement during γ ′ coarsening, which is controlled by the
combined effects of interfacial energy and elastic energy. The shapes and
spatial arrangements of γ ′ particles have long been of interest as they
play a critical role in mechanical properties [ 203 , 204 ]. So far, with the
support of these known theoretical conditions, it is possible to explain
the principles of particle morphology evolvement in actual γ ′ coarsening.
All the factors that could affect the competition between interfacial
energy and elastic energy are the ones to be focused on, such as the
lattice mismatch, thermal exposure temperature, particle size, and
precipitate volume fraction.
5.1. Empirical criteria
Many studies have been carried out on the morphological evolution
of γ ′ precipitates in Ni-base superalloys under various thermal exposure
conditions [ 205 , 206 ]. Furrer et al. [ 205 ] summed up that the particles
could progress from spheres to cubes, to octet cubes, to plates, to
solid-state dendrites, etc. Of these, the spherical-to-cubic transition ap-
pears to be the most common form [205 – 208]. One empirical criterion
shown that the development of cubic γ ′ particles is favored when the
lattice mismatches within a certain range of 0 . 1% < | ε | < 0 . 5% [ 30 ]. For
example, three typical Ni-base superalloys, USTB F1, F5, and F6, with
the lattice mismatches of 0.04 %, 0.54 %, and 1.01 %, respectively,
where the γ ′ particles shown spherical, cubic, and raft-like after pro-
longed aging [ 207 ]. This can be simply interpreted as the fact that when
the lattice mismatch is sufficiently small, the elastic strain energy is
insufficient to cause the shape changes; while the lattice mismatch is
excessively large, the lattice distortion energy isotropy among the three
main directions is easily perturbed, thus breaking the balanced growth
of cubic shapes. Yang et al. [ 206 ] prepared two superalloys with the
lattice mismatches of 0.3 % and 0.11 %, respectively, in which the aged
γ ′ particles were cubic and spherical. Sluytman et al. [ 208 ] designed a set
of platinum group metal-containing Ni-base superalloys for quantitative
analyzing the γ ′ shapes. They found that the shape parameter ratios of γ ′
particles as a function of high-temperature lattice misfit magnitude. This
confirms to the prediction of the empirical criterion, as shown in
Fig. 14 a. Similar phenomena were reported by Zhang et al. [ 209 ], as
shown in Fig. 14 b, in which some experimental and commercial Ni-base
superalloys are also showcased as exemplars [ 210 – 212 ]. Indeed, this
empirical criterion has widely been valid in many Ni-base superalloys
[ 213 – 215 ] and H/MEAs [ 163 , 169 , 167 ].

6. Concluding remarks
The present review shows the development of γ ′ coarsening theories
and their applications in Ni-base superalloys and H/MEAs, from the
perspective of coarsening kinetics and particle morphology evolution.
These theories have generally been generalized on the basis of taking
more and more reality factors into account to release the ideal restrictive
assumptions. For instance, the Ostwald ripening kinetics theories have
been subjected to substantial modifications and extensions to account
for the effects of precipitate volume fractions, multicomponent condi-
tions, and so forth. The morphological evolution theories have also
undergone many extensions, such as from isolated particle to multi-
particle systems, from considering only elastic energy effect to the
combination effects of elastic and interfacial energies, from restrictive to
arbitrary particle shapes, and from elastic homogeneity to elastic in-
homogeneity, etc.
Through the quantitative evaluations, it has been shown that most of
the H/MEAs generally present better γ ′ coarsening-resistance relative to
the traditional Ni-base superalloys. Herein, one point should be noted is
that either the matrix-diffusion controlled kinetics (LSW-family theory),
or the trans-interface-diffusion controlled kinetics (TIDC theory), or a
competitive transformation of both, have been validated in Ni-base su-
peralloys, whereas it appears that only the first one is always applicable
to H/MEAs. However, it is uncertain as to whether this is just a
phenomenological rule in term of the current research or a definitive
conclusion, because of the limited studies to date. With respect to the
evolution of particle shapes, the spherical-to-cubic transition is gener-
ally delayed in H/MEAs due to the slower γ ′ coarsening. Furthermore,
either in Ni-base superalloys or H/MEAs, when the spherical-to-cubic
transition occurs, there is usually a simultaneous spatial rearrange-
ment along the < 100 > crystal directions.
Despite the significant theorical advances in describing the γ ′
coarsening, there are still some limitations, as summarized: (1) Incor-
porating the elastic energy into the Ostwald ripening kinetics theories
remains unachievable because it is difficult to establish the relationship
between elastic field and chemical components. (2) The lattice misfit is
time-varying and its effect on interfacial and elastic energies is very

In order to reveal the essential reasons for the superior coarsening
resistance of multicomponent γ ′ precipitates in H/MEAs, rather than just
generalizing to the slow diffusion effect, there may be some key points
needing to be focused on in the future: (1) Whether the real rate-limiting
factor in γ ′ coarsening is the matrix element or the solute element, or the
coupling between them, and what the mechanism causing the slowest
diffusion rate of it. (2) Whether there is a competition for as the domi-
nant factor determining the γ ′ coarsening rate, between the dynamically
varying thermodynamic factors (lattice misfit, interfacial energy, elastic
energy) and kinetic factors (diffusion rates), in the prolonged thermal
exposure. (3) Whether the γ ′ / γ interface with multicomponent elements
in H/EAs is wider and less atomically sharp (or the opposite), and what
effect this has on the trans-interface-diffusion rate. (4) Whether there is a
correlation between the temporal variations of mixing entropy, mixing
enthalpy, and electronegativity of the multicomponent γ ′ precipitates
with the coarsening rates.
In response to these issues, it is believed that more in-depth studies
required to be conducted in the future to reveal the γ ′ coarsening thor-
oughly. For instance, the elemental diffusivity under thermal exposure is
necessary to be quantitatively evaluated, by means of tracer diffusion
methods, calculation methods of phase diagrams, or inverse simulation
methods, etc. The dynamics variations of particle morphology, size, and
some intrinsic parameters are also desired to be evaluated via in-situ
characterization technologies. In terms of the theoretical extension to
the multicomponent systems with dense precipitates, it is logically
reasonable to modify the numerical models by means of the diffusion-
screening theory or the thermodynamic extrema principle, on the
premise of the Gibbs-Thomson effect extension to multicomponent
systems. In contrast, the phase field simulation methods can not only
eliminate many restrictive assumptions as in theoretical models, but also
reflect the dynamic evolution of precipitates, which has been believed to
be a more effective way to study the γ ′ coarsening, should be vigorously
developed in the future.
