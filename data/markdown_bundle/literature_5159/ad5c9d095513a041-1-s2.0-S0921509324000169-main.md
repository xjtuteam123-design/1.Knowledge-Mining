ABSTRACT 
A series of Ti-46Al- x Zr ( x = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 at.%) alloys were prepared to systematically investigate the effect of 
variation in Zr content on the microstructure and high-temperature tensile properties. The results show that the 
addition of Zr intensifies the solidification segregation and promotes the formation of blocky γ phase in TiAl 
alloys. With the increase in Zr content from 1 to 5 at.%, the volume fraction of the blocky γ phase increases from 
0.2 to 12.8 %, and the solidification microstructure is transformed from a fully lamellar to a nearly lamellar 
microstructure composed of the blocky γ phase and lamellar colonies. Because Zr stabilises the γ phase, 
continuous and discontinuous coarsening occur simultaneously; the former is observed inside the colony, 
whereas the latter occurs between two adjacent lamellar colonies. The high-temperature tensile results show that 
the ultimate tensile strength at 800 
◦
C of the Ti-46Al-4Zr alloy (692 MPa) is significantly greater than that of the 
Ti-46Al alloy (351 MPa), which is attributed to solid solution strengthening and the coordinated deformation of 
blocky γ phase. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl is one of the most promising intermetallic compounds for high- 
temperature applications and has been widely studied owing to its low 
density, high specific strength, and good oxidation and creep resistance 
[ 1 – 3 ]. However, properties such as ductility, fracture toughness, and 
creep resistance must be further improved at higher temperatures for 
practical applications. Alloying is an effective method for adjusting the 
microstructure and properties of γ -TiAl alloys. Solid solution elements 
such as Nb [ 3 ], Cr [ 4 , 5 ], Mo [ 6 , 7 ], W [ 1 , 8 ] and interstitial elements such 
as B, C have been added to improve the mechanical properties and 
service temperature of γ -TiAl alloys, demonstrating the potential for 
new fields of application. 
In recent years, Zr has attracted the attention of researchers because 
of its ability to enhance mechanical properties [ 9 – 11 ] and its effect on 
microstructural stability [ 12 ]. Although Zr tends to occupy the Ti site 
[ 13 ], similar to β -stabilising alloying elements such as Nb, the effect of 
Zr addition on the microstructure and phase content of TiAl alloys is 
significantly different from that of Nb. Musi et al. [ 14 ] clearly defined Zr 
as a γ -stabilising alloying element for the first time, which is consistent 
with the findings of previous studies. First, they observed that the con -
tent of γ phase in the alloy increases with the addition of Zr [ 14 , 15 ]. 
Second, according to the study of element partitioning behaviour, Zr 
tends to segregate into the γ phase [ 16 , 17 ]. When the alloy is in the L + β 
phase region, Zr tends to segregate to the liquid phase. At temperatures 
ranging from 1000 to 1300 
◦
C, Zr tends to segregate into the γ phase, 
followed by the β phase [ 18 ]. The B2 phase is not observed at room 
temperature, even when the Al content of the TiAl alloy is as low as 44 
at.% and the Zr content is 7.5 at.%. Finally, T
γ 
(the temperature at which 
the γ phase begins to appear during cooling) was increased by the 
addition of Zr. Cheng et al. [ 19 ] observed that, when Zr was used to 
replace Nb, T

is also stabilised at much lower temperatures, which was also confirmed 
by Musi et al. [ 14 ]. Moreover, studies also determined that Zr is bene -
ficial for improving the stability of the lamellar microstructure [ 12 , 20 ], 
which is of great significance for the practical application of the alloy.

The performance improvement achieved by adding Zr is often 
attributed to its significant solid solution strengthening effect. As both Zr 
and Ti are Group 4 elements, Zr has similar chemical properties as Ti and 
can form an infinite solid solution. Furthermore, Zr has a significantly 
larger atomic radius (0.160 nm) than Ti (0.145 nm), which results in an 
obvious lattice distortion that hinders the dislocation movement; this 
plays a significant role in solid solution strengthening. Imayev et al. [ 20 ]

strength and improved creep resistance, while maintaining nearly the 
same ductility below the higher brittle-to-ductile transition tempera -

the microstructure and mechanical properties of certain TiAl alloys with

has not been considered as the main strengthening element in these 
alloys. Thus, there is a lack of systematic research and analysis exists on 
the effect of Zr alone on the solidification microstructure and mechan -
ical properties of TiAl alloy. 
In this study, the microstructural formation mechanism and high- 
temperature tensile properties of ternary TiAl alloys with different Zr 
contents were studied. First, ternary alloys with Zr content ranging from 
1 to 5 at.% were prepared via arc melting, and the microstructure and 
phases were investigated using optical microscopy (OM), scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM). The elements distribution was paid special 
attention to in this stage. The segregation behaviour and formation 
mechanism of solidification microstructure, especially the blocky γ 
phases, were elaborately analysed. Second, high-temperature tensile 
tests of the alloys with different Zr contents were conducted, and frac -
ture morphologies were conducted to analyse the change of fracture 
mode. Finally, the relationship between element segregation, micro -
structure, and the strengthening mechanism under high-temperature 
tensile deformation is discussed. 
2. Experimental methods 
2.1. Designed alloy preparation 
The designed alloys with nominal compositions of Ti-46Al- x Zr (x =
0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 at.%, hereinafter except the special illustration) were 
prepared using a non-consumable vacuum arc melting furnace with 
magnetic agitation under argon atmosphere using Ti sponge (99.9 wt%), 
high-purity Al (99.9 wt%), and Zr sponge (99.4 wt%). Each alloy ingot 
weighing 60 g was melted and flipped five times to promote complete 
mixing and melting. The actual chemical compositions of the investi -
gated alloys were examined using an inductively coupled plasma spec -
trometer (ICP), and the results are listed in Table 1 . Samples for 
subsequent analysis and testing were obtained from the centre of the 
button ingots to prevent the effect of chilling, which typically occurs at 
the bottom, on the microstructure of the alloys. 
2.2. High-temperature tensile property test 
High-temperature tensile tests were conducted using an Electronic 
Universal Material Testing Machine (AG-X plus 250 kN, Shimadzu 
Corporation, Japan) in air with a strain rate of 1.25 × 10

Tensile specimens with a gauge cross-section of 2 mm × 2 mm and a 
gauge length of 15 mm were cut from middle height of the ingot. At least 
three tests were performed for each alloy. Fracture morphology was 
obtained using the secondary electron (SE) mode. 
2.3. Microstructure analysis 
XRD, OM, SEM, and TEM analyses were performed for microstruc -

stituents of the ingots and samples were examined via XRD with Cu K α 
radiation using a Philips X-ray diffractometer. Moreover, the samples of

of the microstructures and the deformation characteristics, which was 
undertaken using a Talos F200X (FEI, USA) microscope. Thin foils for 
TEM were prepared by first mechanically grinding the samples to 60 μ m 
thickness, following by ion thinning using GATAN 695 (Gatan, USA) ion

3.2. High-temperature tensile properties of Ti-46Al-xZr alloys 
Fig. 7 (a) shows the high-temperature tensile properties of Ti-46Al- 
xZr alloys at 800 
◦
C. When compared to the binary alloy, even if only 
1 % Zr is added, the high-temperature tensile strength of the alloy is 
significantly improved. This is because Zr has a significantly larger 
atomic radius (160 p.m.) than Ti (145 p.m.) and significantly strong 
solid solution strengthening effect. However, with the increase in Zr 
content, the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and yield strength (YS) of 
the alloy increase at first and then decreases as shown in Table 3 , but the 
change in elongation is not obvious, which is consistent with the results 
of the study by Kawabata on the mechanical properties of Ti-50Al-xZr (x 
= 0, 2, 4, 6 %) alloys at approximately 600~800 
◦
C [ 25 ]. TiAl alloys are 
relatively brittle materials with little plasticity because of their bonding 
characteristics. It is considered that the alloy will have obvious yielding 
and good plasticity when it is above the BDTT [ 26 ]. In previous studies, 
Zr addition was thought to significantly increase the BDTT, and there is 
no obvious yielding in the stress-strain curves of TiAl alloys containing 
Zr until 850 
◦
C [ 20 ]. In this work, the tensile property test was carried 
out at 800 
◦
C, at which temperature the ternary alloy is understandably 
difficult to yield. Meanwhile, the addition of Zr element has obvious 
solid solution strengthening effect by hindering the movement of dis -
locations, which is usually at the expense of plasticity. The trend of UTS 
indicates that the effect of Zr on the properties of the alloy is not related 
to only solid solution strengthening, because the solid solution 
strengthening effect should be enhanced monotonously with the incre -
ment in the concentration of solid solution elements. 
The high-temperature tensile property of Ti-46Al-4Zr alloy was 
compared with that of other alloys specified in the references [ 27 – 36 ], 
as shown in Fig. 7 (b). When compared to other ternary TiAl alloys, the 
Zr-containing TiAl alloy has superior high-temperature strength. Its 
properties are even better than most multicomponent TiAl alloys. This is 
due to the strong solution strengthening effect of Zr addition, of which 
the atomic radius is significantly larger than that of Ti and Al. At the 
same time, it has been confirmed in previous studies that the addition of 
Zr elements coupled with other elements can effectively improve the 
stability of lamellar microstructure [ 12 , 20 ], which is of great signifi -
cance to work stably in high temperature environment. Therefore, the 
TiAl alloy containing Zr has great application potential for components 
working in high temperature for a long time, such as turbine blades. 
However, the elongation of the alloys must be improved, which will be 
one of the development trends of TiAl containing Zr as the primary 
strengthening element.

Therefore, a significant variation in the effect on the solidification

diffusion behaviour of Al atoms to the solid-liquid interface is inhibited. 
The mixing enthalpy of elements represents an affinity with each other, 
which contributes to elemental separation or compatibility [ 37 – 39 ]. A 
lower mixing enthalpy between elements indicates a stronger binding 
force with each other. According to the model proposed by Miedema 
[ 40 ], the mixing enthalpy between Al and Zr ( 44 kJ/mol) is signifi -
cantly smaller than that between Al and Ti ( 30 kJ/mol). Therefore, the 
higher Zr content in the inter-dendritic region, in contrast to the interior 
of the dendrites, promotes the segregation of Al elements into the 
inter-dendrite region. 
Zr atoms are eliminated to the residual liquid phase during the for -
mation of β dendrites, which continues in the subsequent peritectic re -
action, because the equilibrium concentration of Zr in the α phase is 
smaller than that in the β phase at high temperature [ 18 , 19 ]. These two 
stages cause the concentration of Zr in the residual liquid phase to be 
significantly higher than that in the nominal composition, which also 
hinders the diffusion of Al in the residual liquid phase, resulting in the 
enrichment of Zr and Al in the liquid phase. This solidification segre -
gation is intensified with the increase in Zr content; consequently, the 
composition of the residual liquid phase shifts to the high-Al phase field 
and solidifies through the following path: L → L + α → α + γ → γ . The α

shown by the arrow in Fig. 9 (d). Lamellar degradation is generally 
observed after annealing of TiAl alloys, and Zr is widely considered to 
have a better lamellar structural stability effect than Nb because of the 
larger atomic radius hindering the diffusion process; however, the 
coarsening of γ

ternary alloy. This primarily occurs because the ordering process of 
α → α

[ 43 ], which leads to the precipitation of γ
L 
after partial ordering of the α 
phase, and the cooling rate is relatively fast during solidification after 
arc melting. Consequently, the content of α
2 
phase at room temperature 
is higher than that in the equilibrium state, which provides a greater 
driving force for lamellar coarsening. Moreover, considering the sta -
bilising effect of Al and Zr on the γ phase, the element segregation inside 
the colony may also be one of the factors promoting γ phase growth. Qin 
et al. [ 44 ] determined that, after the formation of the primary lamellar 
structure, the composition of the γ phase is close to its equilibrium state, 
but the α
2

chemical driving force for the decomposition of the α

resulting in the coarsening of the γ

formation mechanisms in the microstructure, which are γ

that is formed

discontinuous coarsening processes, according to their content ranging 
from high to low, respectively. On the one hand, Zr can hinder the 
diffusion process of lamellar coarsening and prevent the decomposition 
of α
2

stabilise the γ phase. The region that is rich in Zr in the lamellar colony 
promotes continuous coarsening. At the same time, Zr can also prevent

supersaturation of the α

4.4. Deformation behaviors of Ti-46Al-xZr alloys during high- 
temperature tensile tests 
Solid solution strengthening is considered to be the primary 
strengthening mechanism resulting from Zr addition. In this work, 
owing to the intensification of solidification segregation by the addition 
of Zr element, the microstructure changed from fully lamellar micro -
structure to nearly lamellar microstructure. This type of element 
segregation behaviour and the microstructure characteristics also 
exhibit a significant impact on the deformation behaviour. The addition 
of Zr led to a higher content of Al in the blocky phase; therefore, the 
content of Al in the lamellae was indirectly lower than the nominal 
composition, which may also be one of the strengthening modes of Zr 
addition. Therefore, to explain the deformation behaviour of alloys with 
Zr as the main strengthening element, the deformation characteristics in 
the lamellar colonies, blocky γ phases, and at the interface between them 
was elaborately analysed via TEM. 
Fig. 11 (a) – (c) show the lamellar microstructure of the alloy with Zr

The formation of nanometer-scaled lamellae can significantly improve

impact of nanometer-scaled lamellae on mechanical properties. In pre -

after heat treatment [ 49 , 50 ]. 
The reason of the formation of nano-lamellar structure is that the 
high cooling rate suppresses the element diffusion needed for γ precip -

low temperature region and makes the microstructure of the alloy in an 
unstable high energy state. In this work, on the one hand, the atomic 
diffusion was significantly suppressed due to the Zr addition, and the α
2

ubility. On the other hand, the co-segregation behavior of Al and Zr

5. Conclusions 
The formation mechanism of the solidification microstructure and 
high-temperature mechanical properties of Zr-alloyed γ -TiAl-based al -
loys were studied. The achieved results are summarised as follows. 
(1) When Zr was added to γ -TiAl alloy, the solidification segregation 
of Al and Zr in the alloy increased, which promoted the formation 
of dendrites and blocky γ -TiAl phase in the microstructure. With 
the increase of Zr content, the volume fraction of blocky γ -TiAl 
phase increased from 0.2 to 12.8 %, and the microstructure of the 
γ -TiAl alloy changed from a fully lamellar to nearly lamellar 
microstructure. 
(2) Continuous coarsening was observed in the nearly lamellar 
microstructure of Ti-46Al-4Zr, which is due to the significant 
stabilising effect of Zr on the γ phase. Different types of discon -
tinuous coarsening also occurred at the interface of adjacent 
lamellar colonies. 
(3) With the increase in Zr content, the fracture mode of the alloy 
changed from inter-to trans -lamellar fracture. When the content 
of Zr was 4 %, the alloy exhibited the best tensile properties at 
800 
◦
C, and the UTS and elongation reached 692 MPa and 4.1 %, 
respectively. 
(4) The improvement in tensile properties is attributed to solid so -
lution strengthening and the coordinated deformation of blocky γ 
phase. The lower Al content of lamellar colonies caused by the co- 
segregation of Zr and Al and the formation of nanometer-scaled 
lamellae are also in favour of tensile properties.
