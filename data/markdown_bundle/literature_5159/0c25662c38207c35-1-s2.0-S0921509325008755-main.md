ABSTRACT
The effect of Re on the microstructure evolution and creep behavior at 1100 
◦
C/100 MPa of low-density (8.0 g/ 
cm
3
) Ni
3
Al-based superalloys has been comprehensively investigated. Compared with 0Re alloy, 1.5Re alloy 
exhibits a larger negative misfit value and higher cubic degree of γ ′ phase after completed heat treatment, with 
Re predominantly partitioning into the γ phase. Additionally, the addition of 1.5 wt% Re increases the creep life 
by three times, significantly better than its effect on Ni-based superalloys. The primary mechanisms by which Re 
affects creep performance are elucidated by examining solid solution strengthening. For the latter mechanism, a 
comprehensive evaluation was conducted to assess the contributions of γ ′ phase composition, size, and distri -
bution to strengthening. The results demonstrate that the addition of Re not only induces solid solution 
strengthening but also significantly enhances precipitation strengthening efficacy through modulation of γ ′ phase 
size and distribution. The latter ’ s enhancement in Ni
3
Al-based superalloys is significantly higher than its 
strengthening effect on Ni-based superalloys and is the dominant and unique role in improving the creep life. 
Furthermore, Re optimized the structure and composition of the dislocation network, forming a denser and more 
regular interfacial dislocation network with Re segregation, which can effectively hinder dislocations cutting the 
γ ′ phase and further improve the creep performance. Consequently, the 1.5Re alloy not only maintains a lower 
density but also exhibits better creep performance compared with commercial superalloys, and this investigation 
offers insights and guiding principles for optimizing the composition design of Ni
3
Al-based superalloys.
1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal superalloys, widely utilized as high- 
temperature structural materials, are predominantly employed in tur -
bine blade applications for industrial gas turbines and aircraft engines 
[ 1 , 2 ]. However, the high density of Ni-based superalloys, often 
exceeding 9.5 g/cm
3 
due to the significant presence of refractory ele -
ments, particularly in 3rd and 4th generation superalloys [ 3 , 4 ], poses a 
critical challenge by severely limiting the thrust-to-weight ratio of en -
gines. Moreover, the excessive incorporation of refractory elements 
markedly increases susceptibility to topologically close-packed (TCP) 
phase formation during service. This microstructural instability not only 
accelerates performance degradation but also raises substantial safety 
concerns through potential catastrophic brittle fracture mechanisms. 
Consequently, there is an imperative need to develop alternative ma -
terials with reduced density and superior high-temperature properties to 
meet the increasing demand for lightweight designs. Ni
3
Al-based su -
peralloys, characterized by a high γ ′ phase volume fraction, exhibit 
exceptional microstructural stability, creep resistance, and oxidation 
resistance at elevated temperatures, making them promising candidates 
[ 5 – 7 ]. To date, IC-type and VKNA-series superalloys, including IC218, 
IC6SX, and VKNA-25, have been successfully employed in the 
high-temperature components of gas turbines [ 8 , 9 ]. Therefore, compo -
sitional optimization remains a crucial strategy in the development of 
high-performance superalloys and warrants continued focus.
It is widely recognized that Re plays a critical role in enhancing the 
high-temperature performance of Ni-based superalloys, and its incor -
poration is often considered as a hallmark in the evolution of these

numerous studies have demonstrated that Re significantly refines the

Furthermore, the addition of Re has been reported to alter the parti -

example, Zhang et al. [ 17 ] found that in high-Ta alloys, Re promotes the

single-crystal superalloys.
Creep performance is a critical metric for evaluating the service life 
of these alloys, and Re is widely acknowledged as a pivotal element in 
improving high-temperature and low-stress creep resistance in Ni-based 
single-crystal superalloys when they are free of topologically close- 
packed (TCP) phase precipitates [ 19 , 20 ]. Up to now, three primary 
strengthening mechanisms have been proposed to explain the role of Re 
in the improvement of creep performance: solid solution strengthening, 
precipitation strengthening and diffusion-based strengthening mecha -
nisms [ 11 ]. Generally, Re, W, and Mo contribute significantly to solid 
solution strengthening, while Ti and Ta enhance the γ ′ phase by 
substituting Al atoms in the Ni
3
Al lattice. Meanwhile, the composition of 
the γ phase is a main factor in diffusion-based strengthening mecha -
nisms. Moreover, the morphology and density of dislocation networks 
are closely correlated with creep properties, and the higher the density 
of dislocation network, the longer the creep life [ 21 ]. Notably, Re 
inevitably impacts elemental partitioning along with the size and dis -
tribution of γ ′ phase. These microstructural characteristics collectively 
induce precipitation strengthening effects, particularly in Ni
3
Al-based 
single-crystal superalloys strengthened by high volume fraction of γ ′ 
phase. However, the strengthening behavior induced by this effect is 
frequently overlooked in Ni-based single-crystal superalloys. Conse -
quently, investigating the influence of Re on creep properties and 
strengthening mechanisms in Ni
3
Al-based single-crystal superalloys 
holds substantial scientific significance.
Considering the aforementioned factors, optimizing alloy composi -
tion through the addition of Re has become a focal point of research 
works, both for Ni-based and Co-based superalloys [ 22,23 ]. However, 
very few studies have thoroughly analyzed the effect of Re on the creep 
behavior of Ni
3
Al based single crystal superalloys with high γ ′ volume 
fraction. In this study, a Ni
3
Al-based single-crystal superalloy with lower 
density ( ≤ 8.0 g/cm
3

The systematic investigation focused on the impact of Re addition on 
microstructures after complete heat treatment, mainly creep behavior at 
1100 
◦

provide valuable insights for guiding the composition design of Ni
3

4.3. The dislocation configuration and fracture behavior
The steady-state creep stage is accompanied by the proliferation of 
dislocations, dislocation networks formation at the γ and γ ′ interface and 
dislocation shearing into the γ ′ phases in the later period, occupying a 
relatively long part of the whole creep life, as displayed in Fig. 5 (a).

affecting creep life, especially the dislocations spacing, the distribution

4.4. Overall evaluation of the strengthening mechanisms and comparison 
of alloy performance
Based on the analysis of strengthening mechanisms and the calcu -
lated results, it is evident that the strengthening effect of Re in Ni
3

based alloys exhibits both similarities to and distinctions from that in 
conventional Ni-based superalloys. Specifically, Re imparts a pro -
nounced positive effect on solid solution strengthening, enhances ther -
mally activated controlled dislocation climb, and promotes the 
formation of a dense interfacial dislocation network ( Fig. 13 (a)). 
Notably, in Ni
3
Al-based alloys, Re contributes a distinct and excep -
tionally significant precipiatation-strengthening effect, underscoring its 
unique role within these alloys, as shown in Fig. 13 (b). Besides, Fig. 14

including multiple commercially available superalloys. The majority of 
the alloys in Fig. 14 are compared under identical testing conditions. 
While a small number of alloys differ slightly in testing parameters, their 
testing conditions are still less demanding than the service conditions 
outlined in this work. The results shows that 1.5Re alloys has lower 
density and superior creep performance, ranking at higher level among 
the listed alloys.

(3) The impact of Re on multiple strengthening mechanisms were

enhanced the solid solution strengthening and precipitation
