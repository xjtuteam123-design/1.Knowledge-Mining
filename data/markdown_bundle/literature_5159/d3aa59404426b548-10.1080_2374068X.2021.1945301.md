ABSTRACT
The structural materials are essential for the present important 
advancements in economics, for reliability, safety, sustainability, 
etc., over presently operating reactor technologies of Generation 
IV reactors, advanced fast reactor core and fusion reactors. In order 
to avoid the release of radioactive fission products in the environ -
ment, the consistency of the cladding tube is necessary. Thus, 
increasing the operating temperature of these steels by oxide dis -
persion strengthening (ODS) makes them promising candidate 
materials. For the present study, the samples were prepared from 
the 430 L pre-alloyed powder using mechanical alloying through 
vacuum hot pressing. The hot-pressed alloys A, B, and C structural 
changes were examined through transmission electron micro -
scope-energy dispersive spectroscopy (TEM-EDS) analysis and the 
mechanical properties, such as sintered density(g/cc), Vickers hard -
ness (HV), compressive strength (650°C), and elongation (%) were 
measured. The corrosion resistance was measured through an elec -
trochemical corrosion test of the alloys A, B, and C. From the TEM- 
EDS analysis, it was observed that the nano oxide particles and 
complex oxides particles were uniformly distributed. Alloy A has a 
higher compressive strength of 1307 MPa when compared to alloy 
Band alloy C. Moreover, alloy Chas higher corrosion resistance was 
observed than alloys A& B.

1. Introduction
Oxide dispersion strengthened ferritic steel (ODS ferritic steel) is a promising structural 
material. In general, ferritic ODS steel is used in nuclear-cladding tubes and in corrosion 
environments due to its good-swelling resistance, high-temperature strength, thermal 
ageing friction, high corrosion resistance and resistance to neutron irradiation of 
advanced nuclear reactors while compared with austenitic stainless steel [ 1 ]. Also, the 
addition of oxide particles as dispersed materials such as yttria, zirconium, titanium in 
ferritic steel leads to an increase in operating temperature of more than 600°C in strength 
and thermally induced creep resistance temperature (nuclear cladding) [ 1 , 2 ]. However, 
there are several factors affecting the mechanical properties of ferritic ODS steel, such as

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials
With the addition of yttria, zirconium, and aluminium, the alloys were developed from 
pre-alloyed powders of 430 L. The powder of 430 L pre-alloyed steel with an average 
particle size of 22 µm with a purity level of 99.9999%, zirconium oxide and yttria (III) 
powder with a particle size of 44 µm and 10 µm with a purity level of 99.9999% were used 
respectively. The tungsten and aluminium powders with purity levels of 99.9999% and 
having a particle size of 30 µm and 67 µm respectively were taken. The size of the 
titanium metal powder was 50 µm. The particle size of all powders was measured using 
the master size 3000 (UK). The powders flow rate was measured as per the ASTM B213 
standard using the Hall Flowmeter Funnel [ 27 ].

through mechanical alloying followed by vacuum hot pressing. The vacuum hot pressed 
ferritic ODS steel results were summarised as follows.
● Despite the adoption of the vacuum hot-pressing method, the high-temperature 
compression test at 650°C was conducted for alloys A, B & C. The observation 
showed that the alloy A, has a higher compressive strength of 1307 MPa while 
compared to alloys B (1284 MPa) and C (1112 MPa), respectively.
● This improvement is due to the presence of ultra-fine grains of alloy A, where as in 
alloys B and C the presence of aluminium content shows a coarsened grains 
structure as observed. This also leads to the reduced compressive strength in alloys 
B and C.
● However, the percentage of elongation was reduced for alloy A (17%) than in alloy B 
(26%) and alloy C (34%).
● Aluminium free alloy A has a lower corrosion resistance than the aluminium 
contained alloys B and C, due to the higher current density of alloy A. The corrosion 
resistance being higher in 17Cr −2Al ODS alloy B and 17Cr – 4Al ODS alloy C is due 
to the protection of oxide film formation ((Cr, Fe)
3
O
4 
and Al
2
O
3
) on the surface of 
alloys B and C which is evident from the SEM-EDS analysis.
● Based on the observation in the present study, the best suited alloy for nuclear 
cladding tube is alloy C because of better corrosion resistance, high temperature 
compressive strength and good ductility (percentage of elongation). Also has a 
better oxidation resistance with 4% aluminium content in the alloy C.
