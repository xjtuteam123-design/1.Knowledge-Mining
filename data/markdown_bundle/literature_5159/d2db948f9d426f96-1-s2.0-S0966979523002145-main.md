ABSTRACT 
NiCoCr, as a model medium entropy alloy has been investigated extensively in the past decades. To further 
improve the mechanical properties of NiCoCr, there are mainly two methods. One is thermal-mechanical 
treatment. The other is to add some proper alloying elements to introduce second phase to fabricate the pre -
cipitate strengthening medium entropy alloys. Inspired by this, a superior medium entropy alloy (NiCoCr)
95
V
5 
was successfully fabricated by cold rolling and subsequent two-step annealing in this study. The strength and 
plasticity product exceeds 86 GPa ⋅ % that is comparable to the currently reported high-performance medium and 
high entropy alloys. The microstructure characterizations indicate that the dislocation slip and interacting with 
high density nanoscale precipitates, the initiation of primary and secondary deformation twinning which further 
segmenting the grains are the main reasons for such high properties. This work enriches the design and devel -
opment of medium and high entropy alloys with high-strength and high plasticity. 
1. Introduction 
Multicomponent solid solution alloys, namely high and medium 
entropy alloys, have been investigated extensively due to their superior 
properties [ 1 – 3 ], such as excellent ductility [ 4 ], good corrosion resis -
tance [ 5 ] and high radiation tolerance [ 6 ]. Among these alloys, NiCoCr 
appears to be a model alloy which attracts great interests because of 
relatively good combination of yield strength and ductility compared 
with other high and medium entropy alloys (MEAs) [ 7 – 14 ]. However, 
the yield strength of NiCoCr is still relatively lower than that of other 
structural materials, such as high strength steels [ 15 ], which restricts its 
applications. To improve the mechanical properties of NiCoCr, there are 
mainly two methods. One is thermal-mechanical treatment. The other is 
to add some proper alloying elements, such as Al or Ti, to introduce 
second phase to fabricate the precipitate strengthening MEAs [ 16 , 17 ]. A 
representative work of the former method is completed by Muxin Yang 
et al. [ 8 ]. The NiCoCr alloy was cold rolled 95% in thickness and 
annealed at different temperatures. These thermal-mechanical treat -
ment methods can generate the heterogeneous grain structure which 
impart the alloy a high yield strength about 1.2 GPa at room tempera -
ture and 1.6 GPa at cryogenic temperature. A representative work of the 
latter method is finished by Y.L. Zhao et al. [ 10 ]. A specific ratio of Al 
and Ti was added to the NiCoCr-based alloy which introduces the 
nanoscale L1
2 
type particles. Recently, Sohn et al. fabricated a NiCoV 
MEA by using an equal ratio vanadium (V) to substitute Cr to develop 
superior mechanical properties of MEAs [ 18 ]. The element V can in -
crease the level of lattice distortion which can significantly increase the 
lattice friction stress for dislocations. Inspired by the above works, in 
this study, a proper ratio of V is added to the base alloy NiCoCr com -
bined with thermal-mechanical treatment method to explore effective 
methods for improving the strength and plasticity of this type of MEAs at 
different temperatures and their corresponding strengthening 
mechanisms.

the microstructural foundation for its excellent mechanical properties. 
4.2 Origin of excellent mechanical properties of 5V – 700 sample at 
low temperature. 
From Fig. 4 (a), it can be seen that the yield and tensile strength of the 
5V-700-RT sample are comparable to those of the 5V sample, but the 
elongation has increased by nearly 16%. The microstructure charac -
terization results of the two samples can well explain the above phe -
nomenon. In the 5V sample, due to the insufficient annealing, there are a 
high density of residue dislocations, which is beneficial for yield 
strength. However, only a small amount of second phases precipitate 
along grain boundaries and the sides of dislocation slip bands, which 
brings the weak precipitation strengthening effect. In the 5V-700-RT 
sample, although an obvious decrease in dislocation density is not 
conducive to the yield strength, the high density precipitates inside the 
grains effectively compensate for the yield strength loss caused by the 
decrease in dislocation density, so the yield strength of the two samples 
is equivalent. However, the initial density of dislocations in the grains of 
the 5V-700-RT sample is low, which allows it to accommodate more 
dislocation proliferation during deformation, resulting in a significant 
increase in its elongation.

5. Conclusions 
In this paper, the effect of V addition on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of NiCoCr MEAs were investigated by cold rolling 
and annealing technique. The following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) The addition of 5 at.%V into the NiCoCr MEA has obtained the 
best comprehensive mechanical properties, especially at low 
temperatures, with excellent strength and plasticity product over 
86 GPa ⋅ %. 
(2) Through cold rolling and two-step annealing, the microstructure 
of the 5V sample exhibits high-density nanoscale precipitates 
uniformly distributed on a clean FCC matrix, providing the 
microstructure assurance for its excellent properties. 
(3) The dislocation slip, initiation of primary and secondary twinning 
which further segmenting the grains, and the dislocation pinning 
effect by the precipitates making the clean austenite matrix to 
accommodate high-density dislocations and deformation twins 
are the origins of excellent mechanical properties for 5V-700 
sample at low temperatures.
