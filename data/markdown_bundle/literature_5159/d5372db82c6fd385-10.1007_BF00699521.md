FORMATION OF PORES IN NICKEL HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH 
ALLOYS IN ANNEALING 
A. Ya. Shinyaev and V. P. Zryumov UDC 669.24:620.192.47:621.785.3 
The production of porosity-free metallic materials is one of the most important problems 
for metallurgists and engineers, since the presence of pores in a metal leads to a decrease 
in the resistance to an applied load. The simplest and most reliable method of measurement 
of porosity is precision measurement of density, the accuracy of which at present is 0.001 
g/cm 3 (hydrostatic weighing method). 
For a comparative analysis of the results of determination of density the theoretical 
density calculated from the lattice parameter and the number of atoms in it for all of the 
phase constitutents of the alloy is used. It is characteristic that the density of pure 
metals obtained by various methods is always less then their theoretical value. For single 
crystal specimens the difference between the theoretical and experimental densitites is a 
minimum but with the change to polycrystalline and deformed specimens it gradually changes, 
which is caused by the increase in them of the quantity of point (vacancies) and linear (dis- 
location) defects. 
It is known that with an increase in temperature the number of point defects in metallic 
materials increases as the result of formation of thermal vacancies. At temperatures close to 
the melting point the concentration of thermal vacancies in metals and alloys may reach 0.5- 
0.8% [i]. With high concentrations of vacancies coagulation of them with subsequent forma- 
tion of pores occurs. This process has its own particular features for each material, which 
is related to the difference in the energy for movement of vacancies and the energy for for- 
mation of vacancy complexes. 
This article presents the results of an investigation of the influence of heating tem- 
perature on the residual porosity in nickel high-temperature strength alloys. 
Wrought 15x60 mm blanks of EI698-VD (0.05% C, 13.95% Cr, 2.92% Mo, 2.65% Ti, 1.68% AI, 
2.04% Nb) and KhN62BMKTYu (EP742) (0.04% C, 14.0% Cr, 5.03% Mo, 2.61% Ti, 2.49% AI, 10.10% 
Co, 2.6% Nb) alloys [2], which were heated in an electric resistance furnace in air and held 
for 1 h at temperatures of I150-1250°C, were used as the original material for the investi- 
gation. During heat deformation processing EP742 and EP698-VD melts were heated to 1200 
and 1250°C, respectively.

In microatructural investigation it was established that in the specimens with a density 
close to the original there are no pores (Fig. la). In the specimens with a reduced density 
there are observed pores which are formed primarily at the joints of the grains and close to 
carbide phase precipitates (Fig. ib). 
The presence of pores at the grain boundaries of alloys of such a type in heating to 
1200°C was also noted in [3]. An increase in heating temperature to 1250°C leads to deve- 
lopment of the fusion process in the alloys preferentially at the grain boundaries (Fig. ic). 
The mechanical properties of EI698-VD and EP742 alloys at different temperatures are 
given in Table I. It may be seen that at 1200°C for EP742 alloy and at 1250°C for EI698-VD 
alloy the tensile strength, elongation, and impact strength drop sharply (condition of almost 
complete loss of plasticity), which is related to the action of pores as stress raisers. An

perties of the alloys showed that there is a definite relationship between the heating tem-

The area of brittle fracture of EP742 alloy corresponds to the I180-1200°C temperature

Therefore to provide a high level of mechanical properties of the investigated alloys 
annealing must be done at temperatures at which the concentration of thermal vacancies is 
insufficient for coagulation of them into pores. For EI698-VD and EP742 nickel alloys the 
temperature of the start of formation of pores is 1150 and 1200°C, respectively. 
Conclusions. I. In heating of EI698-VD and EP742 nickel high-temperature strength alloys 
above a certain temperature micropores are formed at the grain boundaries. 
2. The critical concentration of pores leading to almost complete loss of technological 
and service properties of the nickel alloys corresponds to a 0.3-0.5% drop in their density. 
3~ The limiting heating temperature of EP742 and EI698-VD nickel alloys above which 
pores are formed is 1150 and 1200°C, respectively.

INFLEUENCE OF MOLYBDENUM, TUNGSTEN, AND COBALT ON THE CORROSION 
OF HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH NICKEL ALLOYS IN MOLTEN SALTS 
I. V. Oryshich and O. S. Kostyrko UDC 669.14.018.44 
During service high-temperatute strength alloys are subjected to high-temperature cor- 
rosion caused by compounds of sulfur, vanadium, and chlorine precipitated in gas turbines 
in combustion of fuel. While the corrosion of the outer surfaces of the blades is caused by 
sodium sulfate and vanadium pentoxide, for the cooled channels of the blades the real danger 
under marine conditions and in regions with a saline soil is sodium chloride entering from 
the compressor together with compressed air. Sulfide corrosion caused by sodium sulfate has 
been investigated in sufficient detail since for high-temperature strength nickel alloys it 
is the most dangerous. However, there is not a common opinion relative to the influence of 
the individual alloy elements, including molybdenum, tungsten, and cobalt, on the corrosion 
resistance of alloys in molten surfaces, For example, in [i, 2] it was shown that refractory 
metals reduce it. Other investigators [3-5] assume them to be additions weakening sulfide 
corrosion. The author of [6] states that with the addition of cobalt to high-temperature 
strength nickel alloys there is an increase in their resistance to sulfide corrosion and ex- 
plains it by the higher melting point (by approximately 230°C) of the Co--CoS sulfide eutectic 
than of the Ni--Ni3S2 eutectic. In other works there is information on its neutral [3] or even 
negative [7] influence on corrosion resistance of alloys. There is no information in the 
literature on the influence of these metals on the resistance in molten chlorides (chloride 
corrosion). 
The purpose of this work is an investigation of the influence of additions of molybdenum, 
tungsten, and cobalt on the resistance to sulfide and chloride corrosion of type ZhS6K, EP539, 
and ~P958 high-temperature strength nickel alloys. 
The alloys were produced by vacuum induction melting of the charge materials with subse- 
quent pouring of the molten metal into ceramic miniature molds previously heated to 950°C.
