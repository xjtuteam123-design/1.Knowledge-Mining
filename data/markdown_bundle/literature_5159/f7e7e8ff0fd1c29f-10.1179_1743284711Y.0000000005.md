Mechanical properties of Nb based
multiphase alloy studied by nanoindentation
and atomic force microscopy
W. Q. Wei *, H. W. Wang, C. M. Zou, Z. J. Zhu and Z. J. Wei
The mechanical properties and deformation behaviours of as cast and heat treated Nb–21Ti–4C–
xAl ( x: 0, 5, 10 and 15 at-%) alloys are comprehensively investigated using nanoindentation and
atomic force microscopy. For the Nb–21Ti–4C alloy, nanoindentation tests are performed for the
Nb solid solution (Nbss) matrix, carbide and the interface between them. The results show that the
hard carbide, which has a strong bonding with the Nbss matrix, can enhance the alloy before and
after heat treatment, and the eutectoid transformation of the large sized carbide after heat
treatment leads to less possibility for the forming of cracks on the carbide surface, which in turn
improves the toughness. For the Nb–21Ti–4C–(0, 5, 10, 15)Al alloys, the hardness of the Nbss
matrix increases significantly with increasing Al fraction for both as cast and heat treated alloys.
However, deviations of the elastic modulus are inconspicuous with the Al fraction for the as cast
and heat treated alloys.
Keywords: Nanoindentation, Nb–Ti–Al alloy, Carbide, Arc melting
Introduction
The modern development of airframe and missile
technologies has made necessary a new appraisal of
possible high temperature alloys.
1 Compared with the
conventional Ni or Co based superalloys, Nb based
superalloys and their composites are considered to be
novel high temperature structural materials.
2,3 As the
nature of refractory metal, pure Nb has relatively low
density and high melting temperature (2468 uC), as well
as excellent ductility at room temperature. However, the
strength of Nb decreases substantially when the
temperature rises above 900 uC. To resolve this problem,
it is documented that the antiplastic deformation
behaviour of Nb solid solution (Nbss) alloys at high
temperature can be enhanced through the solid solution
method, i.e. doping metal elements with higher valence
than Nb. These kinds of Nb based superalloys with
additions of Ta, Mo, Hf and W elements have been
previously reported, among which the Nb–Mo–W alloy
system exhibits relatively high strength.
4–6 For example,
the strength of the Nb–10Mo–(10–15)W–10Ti–18Si
superalloy can be 800 MPa even at 1500 uC.2 Although
the strength of Nb based superalloys is enhanced, the
density, which is higher than that of pure Nb, has been
the bottleneck for further application in aerospace
structure materials.
In recent years, studies on the microstructures and
mechanical properties of Nb based alloys, with Ti, Zr,
Al and Si (light elements) as primary doping elements,
draw much attention. It is reported that the Ti doped
Nbss alloys have excellent mechanical properties at both
low and high temperatures.
7 In addition, Nbss with
enhanced phases [such as carbides, 8–11 Nb3Al (Refs. 12
and 13) or Nb 5Si3 (Ref. 14)] are also explored. For
example, superalloys with homogeneous distribution of
reinforced carbide particles can offer outstanding
mechanical properties at high temperature.
8 For these
alloys, the size and mechanical properties of the
enhanced precipitations play a key role for the proper-
ties of the alloys. Furthermore, the mechanical proper-
ties of the superalloys are also strongly dependent on the
microstructure and bonding of the interfaces between
the Nbss matrix and the enhanced phase. A compre-
hensive understanding for both of the above aspects will
enable better design and fabrication of Nb based
superalloys. The previous study has investigated the
hardness and elastic modulus of the matrix (c phase) and
the precipitations ( c9 phase) in Ni based superalloys
using nanoindentation and atomic force microscopy
(AFM).
15 Although the nanoindentation measurement
combined with AFM technique 16 can be used to study
the mechanical properties of such particle reinforced
superalloys, few studies have been dedicated on this
ﬁeld.
Especially, the Nb–Ti–(Al)–C alloy, with enhanced
phases of Nb
3Al and carbide, has great potential for
application at temperature higher than 1100 uC.17
Consequently, in the present paper, nanoindentation
and AFM were performed for the mechanical properties

Experimental
Pure Nb and Al with high purity, Ti sponge and TiC
powder were used as raw materials. The starting
materials were cleaned by acid washing and water
scrubbing. The alloys were prepared using non-consum-
able arc melting furnace under a positive pressure of
high purity Ar and cast into buttons. The buttons were
melted ﬁve times to ensure compositional homogeneity.
The nominal compositions of the alloys are Nb–21Ti–
4C, Nb–21Ti–4C–5Al, Nb–21Ti–4C–10Al and Nb–
21Ti–4C–15Al (at-%), and the precise compositions are
listed in Table 1.
The buttons were cut into samples with dimensions of
1061063 mm using wire electrical discharge machines.
The samples for heat treatment were encapsulated in
quartz tubes, which were evacuated to y5610
22 Pa in
order to avoid oxidation, and then post-heated to
1100uC and held for 24 h. After heat treatment, the
samples were quenched with cold water. To ensure the
comparability, all the as cast and heat treated samples
for microstructure observations were cut from the same
positions of the buttons. The microstructures and
compositions of the samples were characterised using
X-ray diffraction analyser (XRD, Rigaku D/max-RB)
and scanning electron microscope (SEM, S-4700,
equipped with energy dispersive spectroscopy).
The nanoindentation and AFM experiments were
carried out using a Nano Indenter XP (MTS Systems
Corp.) equipped with continuous stiffness measurement,
which offers a direct measure of hardness and elastic
modulus during the loading portion of an indentation
test and allows an accurate observation of small volume
deformation. A three-sided pyramid diamond Berkovich
indenter with radius of 20 nm and normal angle of 65 ?3u
between the tip axis and the faces of triangular pyramid
was used. The vertical resolution of the instrument is
better than 1 nm, and the force resolution is y50 nN.

Microstructural analysis

cast and heat treated samples. For the as cast samples

peaks for Nb–21Ti–4C–(0, 5, 10, 15)Al alloys. These
peaks can be determined to be a body centred cubic
structured Nbss phase (PDF no. 35-0789) and a face
centred cubic structured (Nb,Ti)C phase (PDF no. 47-
1418). With increasing atomic fraction of Al, some weak
peaks belonging to a cubic structured Nb
3Al phase
(PDF no. 12-0085) can also be observed in the Nb–

The previous study pointed out that the solid
solubility limit of the Al element in the Nbss phase can
be improved when the Ti element is added into the Nb–
Ti–Al system. It is also well known that the composition
of Nb–21Ti–5Al is located at the b solid solution region,
while the composition of Nb–21Ti–(10, 15)Al is located
at the eutectic region of the b solid solution (Nbss) phase

quently, a supersaturated Al element dissolving into
the Nbss matrix will result in less possibility for the

(10, 15)Al alloys.

pattern of the Nb–21Ti–4C alloy is the same as that of
the as cast sample, indicating the existence of identical

Conclusions
Based on the microstructure analysis and nanomecha-
nical tests of the Nb–21Ti–4C–(0, 5, 10, 15) alloys, the
following conclusions can be drawn:
1. For the as cast samples, the alloys consist of the
Nbss matrix and the carbide, except that a small fraction
of the Nb
3Al phase precipitates in the Nb–21Ti–4C–15Al
alloy. After heat treatment, the large sized carbides evolve
into the eutectoid structure of Nbss and carbide, and the
secondary carbides form in the Nbss matrix. Moreover,
the fraction of Nb
3Al increases significantly in the Nb–
21Ti–4C–(10, 15)Al alloys.
2. The mechanical properties and deformation beha-
viours of the Nbss matrix, the interface (between Nbss
and carbide) and the carbide are characterised for the
Nb–21Ti–4C alloy. For the as cast sample, the hardness
and elastic modulus of the interface and the carbide
decrease with increasing indent depths due to the
substrate effect and the formation of cracks in the
brittle carbide. Comparatively, it has less possibility for
the formation of cracks after heat treatment, which in
turn leads to the slight decrease in hardness and
modulus of the interface with increasing indent depths.
Furthermore, a strong bonding between the carbide and
the matrix can be obtained, suggesting that these
multiphase alloys can be enhanced by the carbide.
3. The hardness of the Nbss matrix increases
significantly with increasing Al fraction for both as cast
and heat treated alloys. However, the hardness of the
Nbss matrix in each alloy obviously decreases after heat
treatment compared with that of the as cast samples. In
addition, a higher Al addition will cause a smaller
deviation in hardness between the as cast and heat
treated samples. On the other hand, heat treatment and
the composition of the alloys (i.e. the fraction of Ti, Al
and C elements) influence the elastic modulus slightly.
