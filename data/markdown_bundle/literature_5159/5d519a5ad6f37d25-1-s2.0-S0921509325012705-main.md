1. Introduction
To fulfill the rigorous service demands in extreme environments, the 
development of cost-effective alloys with excellent corrosion resistance, 
high strength and larger ductility across a wide temperature range is 
particularly crucial for cutting-edge industries like outer space explo -
ration, aviation and so on [ 1 , 2 ]. However, the conventional alloys with 
certain exceptional properties are often constrained within narrow ser -
vice environment, rendering them inadequate to fulfill these re -
quirements. For example, maraging steels generally exhibit excellent 
room temperature tensile properties, however, they become brittle at 
 196 
◦
C and demonstrate poor corrosion resistance [ 2 – 6 ]. This is due to 
the higher strength of the matrix compared to the grain boundaries and 
insufficient Cr content as well as galvanic corrosion [ 2 – 6 ]. On the con -
trary, corrosion-resistant austenitic stainless steels perform very well at 
cryogenic temperatures, but has low yield strength at high temperatures 
[ 4 , 7 ], which is caused by the lack of second-phase strengthening. In fact, 
such trade-off between strength and corrosion resistance is generally 
observed in various alloy systems, as most strengthening mechanisms 
tend to introduce negative factors, such as compositional segregation 
and local high-energy regions that weaken passive films. To address this 
challenge, it requires careful tuning of the chemical properties between 
different phases and increasing the protectivity of the whole surface 
passive film while strengthening. Therefore, despite persistent en -
deavors in alloy development, attaining alloys with exceptional 
corrosion-resistant and strength-ductility synergy still presents consid -
erable challenges.
In recent years, the discovery of high/medium entropy alloys (H/ 
MEAs), also referred to as multi-principal element alloys, have been 
recognized as a versatile paradigm for developing novel alloys with 
excellent comprehensive properties [ 1 , 2 , 8 ]. So far, various H/MEAs 
with excellent properties have been developed by composition and

μ m at 10 % strains. Fig. 5 a2 shows the formation of thick slip bands 
characterized by a high dislocation density, along with regions featuring 
stacking faults (SFs) and low dislocation density between these slip 
bands. A large number of SFs and SFs networks can be also observed

2

precipitates in the matrix (as shown by the green arrow), as shown in

through L1
2

4. Conclusions
In this study, we designed a novel nanoprecipitation-strengthened 
Fe-based MEA, exhibiting excellent corrosion resistance and remark -
able mechanical properties. The fundamental mechanisms of corrosion 
and deformation were thoroughly investigated. The key findings can be 
summarized as follows. 
(1) The MEA contains unique microstructures with coherent L1
2 
nanoprecipitates and minimal grain boundary precipitate (B2- 
NiAl, Cr-rich σ phase and TiC). The mean size and volume frac -
tion of the L1
2 
precipitates are estimated to be ~15 nm and 28 %, 
respectively. The L1
2 
phase is rich in Ni, Ti, and Al but depleted in 
Cr and Fe, while the FCC matrix shows the opposite element 
distribution.
(2) Excellent tensile properties from 196 to 700 
◦
C were obtained in 
the present MEA. The MEA had a YS of 820 MPa, UTS of 1170 
MPa, and elongation of 31 % at 25 
◦
C. At 196 
◦
C, its strength 
and ductility improved, with YS reaching 970 MPa, UTS of 1500 
MPa, and elongation increasing to 40 %. These excellent me -
chanical properties result from the synergistic effects of L1
2 
nano- 
precipitates, slip bands, stacking faults, and Lomer-Cottrell locks. 
Furthermore, the MEA maintains a high yield strength of 720 
MPa and elongations of 15 % at 700 
◦
C, due to the shearable L1
2 
precipitate and dislocation slips.
(3) The surface passive film of the MEA features an amorphous 
structure about 3 nm thick. L1
2 
precipitates are preferentially 
dissolved during the corrosion process, facilitating the hetero -
geneous nucleation of Cr
2
O
3
. Key components, Cr
2
O
3 
and TiO
2
, 
contribute to its excellent localized corrosion resistance.
