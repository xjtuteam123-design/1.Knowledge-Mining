ABSTRACT 
A single-phase FeCrNi medium-entropy alloy (MEA) with a face-centered cubic structure was successfully 
fabricated by selected laser melting (SLM). The SLMed FeCrNi MEA exhibits hierarchical microstructures, 
including molten pools, coarse columnar grains, submicron cellular structures and high-density dislocations, and 
shows an excellent strength-ductility combination. Notably, the SLMed FeCrNi MEA has ultra-high yield strength 
and ultimate tensile strength of 1.1 GPa and 1.5 GPa, respectively, at 77 K, which are almost 1.5 times higher 
than those at room temperature, while still maintaining a high fracture elongation of 49%. It was also found that 
the SLMed FeCrNi MEA deformed at 77 K could produce more nanotwins compared with that deformed at room 
temperature. The twinning-prone matrix can produce more significant twinning-induced plasticity effects, which 
contributes to a high plasticity. Additionally, the high-density cellular substructure can effectively hinder 
dislocation motion, which is the main reason for the high strength. 
1. Introduction 
Structural materials with admirable mechanical performance under 
cryotemperature service conditions are exceedingly demanding in 
modern engineering applications, such as aerospace, polar develop -
ment, and liquid nitrogen transport. At cryotemperature, the movement 
of dislocations in most traditional alloys becomes difficult, resulting in a 
significant decrease in plasticity [ 1 , 2 ]. Such ductile-brittle transition 
phenomenon may pose a challenge for traditional alloys to meet future 
performance requirements. The proposal of medium/high entropy al -
loys (MEAs/HEAs) breaks the traditional alloy design strategy and 
provides pathways for the development of high-performance structural 
materials. In general, MEAs/HEAs are defined as alloys consisting of 
three or more major elements in equimolar or near-equimolar ratios, 
which are prone to form simple body-centered-cubic (BCC) or 
face-centered-cubic (FCC) structures with severe lattice distortion [ 3 , 4 ]. 
Due to the unique compositional and structural characteristics, 
MEAs/HEAs exhibit excellent cryotemperature toughness and corrosion 
resistance, which makes them strong candidates for replacing traditional 
alloys at cryotemperature [ 5 – 8 ]. For example, FeCoCrNiMn HEA has 
good tensile strength and elongation of 460 MPa and 57%, respectively, 
at 77 K [ 3 ], and CoCrNi MEA also achieves high tensile strength and 
elongation of 560 MPa and 45%, respectively, at 4.2 K [ 9 ]. However, 
one of the challenges that limits the application of MEAs/HEAs is their 
relatively low strength, which usually needs to be enhanced through 
complex thermomechanical processing (homogenization, rolling, 
annealing, etc.) [ 10 , 11 ]. Therefore, developing a simple method for the 
preparation of high-performance MEAs/HEAs remains a tremendous 
challenge. 
Additive manufacturing (AM) is an emerging alloy preparation 
method that can realize the integrated forming of complex components 
serving in extreme environments. Among various additive 
manufacturing techniques, selected laser melting (SLM) has received 
extensive attention owing to its high dimensional accuracy and high

Fig. 4 presents the EBSD data of the XY plane of SLMed FeCrNi MEA.

boundaries (HAGBs), the mean size of coarse grains is determined to be 
~54 μ m. Fig. 4 c shows the kernel average misorientation (KAM) map. It 
is found that both LGABs and HGABs have higher local orientation dif -

calculated local misorientation with an average value of 0.92
◦

Fig. 5 a shows the SEM image of the coarse grain and the corre -

average size of ~645 nm are tightly arranged inside the whole coarse 
grain. The interfaces between adjacent cellular substructures can be 
observed. It can be found that the width of interfaces is less than 10 nm. 
The cellular substructures in the AMed materials are considered to be a 
typical non-equilibrium structure, and their formation is usually related 
to the temperature gradient and undercooling rate during the SLM 
process [ 21 , 22 ]. When the ratio of a temperature gradient to the grain 
growth rate (G/R) is low, a typical equiaxed dendritic is easily formed 
along the temperature gradient direction. In contrast, with increasing 
G/R ratios, the solidification morphology will change from equiaxed 
dendritic to columnar dendritic, cellular structure, and planar structure 
[ 23 ]. During the SLM process, the temperature gradient is up to 10

tends to promote the formation of cellular structures [ 24 ]. Compared to 
traditional alloys, a higher G/R was enabled by high-entropy effect of 
MEAs/HEAs, which is also more conducive to the formation of cellular 
substructures [ 25 ]. Fig. 5 b presents the TEM bright-field (BF) image of 
the cellular substructures. The effective mass of dislocations twines 
around the boundaries of the cellular substructures, thus forming a thick 
dislocation concentration area. However, inside the cellular sub -

sults have been reported in previous works [ 26 – 28 ].

4. Conclusions 
A FeCrNi MEA with a single FCC phase was manufactured by SLM. 
Due to the ultra-high cooling rate and temperature gradient during the 
SLM process, the SLMed FeCrNi MEA exhibits a hierarchical micro -
structure, including molten pools, coarse columnar grains, submicron 
cellular structures and high-density dislocations. The SLMed FeCrNi 
MEA has an exceptional strength-ductility synergy, especially with an 
ultra-high tensile strength of 1.5 GPa and an adequate elongation of 49% 
at 77 K. The critical twining strain of the FeCrNi MEA at 77 K is obvi -
ously lower than that at 298 K, and the twinning ability is stronger at 
low temperatures, which makes the deformation mechanism at cry -
otemperature to be dominated by twining. The activated twinning can 
relieve stress concentration and promote homogeneous plastic defor -
mation, resulting in adequate ductility. At the same time, high-density 
cellular structures provide numerous extra boundaries to hinder dislo -
cation movement, which is the main reason for the strength 
enhancement.
