ABSTRACT 
Precipitation-hardening is an essential and promising method to enhance the strength of alloys. However, this 
strengthening mechanism will destroy the plasticity of the alloy and lead to premature failure. Here, we report a 
strategy to improve the strength of the alloy by producing highly-coherent nanoprecipitates (HNPs) through 
spinodal decomposition. The HNPs can be sheared by dislocations to eliminate the stress concentrations and 
premature failure. We designed and fabricated a bulk CoCrNiAl
0 ⋅ 1
Mo
0.1 
medium entropy alloys (MEAs) to consist 
of face-centered cubic phase and HNPs structure involving high temperature recrystallized and low temperature 
aging. Compared with the samples without precipitation, HNPs structure contribute to obtain a satisfactory trade 
off with the yield strength of 900 MPa, the tensile strength of 1215 MPa and 40% tensile ductility. 
1. Introduction 
The high-entropy alloys (HEAs) proposed by Yeh et al. are single 
phase solid solution consist of multiple principal elements in an equi -
molar or near-equimolar ratio [ 1 ]. Meantime, HEAs possess attractive 
mechanical properties [ 2 , 3 ], excellent corrosion resistance [ 4 , 5 ] and 
excellent radiation tolerance [ 6 ]. However, in the past few years, some 
researcher has explored the medium entropy alloys (MEAs), which are 
defined as the alloys composed of two to four principal elements. Me -
dium entropy alloys have drawn much attention due to their similar or 
even better properties to the HEA [ 7 ]. The stacking fault energy of 
CoCrNi MEAs is 22 ± 4 mJm
 2
, which is ~25% lower than that of 
CoCrFeMnNi [ 8 ]. Low stacking fault energy makes CoCrNi need a lower 
critical stress than CoCrFeMnNi to form twin in deformation, which why 
the tensile strength and ductility of the CoCrNi are higher than CoCr -
FeMnNi [ 9 , 10 ]. However, the combination strength and ductility of the 
as-cast CoCrNi does not meet the requirements in engineering applica -
tion. Hence, many attempts have been made to enhance the strength of 
CoCrNi MEA [ 11 , 12 ]. For instance, Liu et al. reported a superior room 
temperature strength (1.6 GPa and 407.6 HV) in the single FCC phase 
CoCrNi MEA by carefully tuning the formation of multiple lattice defects 
[ 13 ]. In addition, doping large radius elemental in the alloy can refine 
the grain and produce nanoprecipitates, which is an effective way to 
enhance the strength. Kai et al. [ 13 ] reported a high strength 
CoCrNi-based MEA by nanoscale L1
2
-(Ni, Co, Cr)
3
(Ti, Al)-type particles 
with heterogeneous and homogeneous precipitate, and the strength 
increased from ~750 MPa to ~1.3 GPa. In previous studies, we designed 
precipitate hardening to enhance strength by simple thermal treatment 
in CoCrNi-based MEAs [ 14 ]. However, the incoherent μ -nano -
precipitates are non-shearable by dislocation resulting in stress con -
centrations and premature failure of the alloy. In order to eliminate this 
negative effect, we changed the heat treatment route of the alloy. In this 
study, high temperature transient heat treatment was selected to obtain 
fine grains and avoid the formation of μ phase. Finally, the 
highly-coherent nanoprecipitate （ HNPs ） phase was obtained by the 
spinodal decomposition during low temperature ageing. Due to the high 
density of dislocations and more nucleation sites in the rolled alloy, the 
precipitates are easier to form at grain boundaries during aging [ 15 ]. On 
the contrary, the nanoprecipitates produced by the spinodal decompo -
sition have more uniform distribution and less lattice misfit with the 
matrix [ 16 ]. The HNPs impede dislocation movement by providing 
strong diffuse attractive obstacles to trap moving dislocations and create 
antiphase boundaries on the slip planes of these nanoprecipitates when 
they are sheared by dislocations [ 17 , 18 ]. 
In this work, select the same composition proportion CoCr -
NiAl
0 ⋅ 1
Mo
0.1 
as the previous study. Among the five elements in 
Co – Cr – Ni – Al – Mo system, Al and Mo have the largest metallic radius (Co 
(0.125 nm), Cr (0.124 nm), Ni (0.124 nm), Al (0.143 nm), Mo (0.139 
nm)), were added into the CoCrNi matrix in order to produce pre -
cipitates and achieve solid solution strengthening.

4. Discussion 
The typical deformation near the fracture of the Age24 alloy after 
tensile test is shown in Fig. 6 (A-D). A large number of dislocations 
accumulated in the grain boundaries and the grain are slightly deformed 
by rotation ( Fig. 6 (A)). In addition, Fig. 6 (B) shows that the dislocation 
is separated and accumulated by a series of superimposed twin bound -
aries. However, according to morphology and relatively rough spacing, 
they are probably annealing twins existing in the grains before defor -
mation, and it is beneficial to the improvement of work hardening 
ability and plasticity [ 22 , 23 ]. Moreover, the deformation occurs by 
dislocation planar slip along primary {111} planes and significant 
amount of SFs in some grain, accompanying with high density disloca -
tion, consistent with other studies on bulk HEAs with fine grain structure 
( Fig. 6 (C-D)) [ 23 ].

5. Conclusion 
In summary, we have developed a new production route to enhance 
the performance materials by spinodal decomposition, which lead to a 
HNPs structure with the yield strength of 900 MPa, a high tensile 
strength of 1215 MPa and good ductility of 40% at ambient temperature. 
This is enabled by a novel anneal process that aging treatment based on 
the full-recrystallized structure of fine grains. The high-coherent L1
2 
nanoprecipitates produced by spinodal decomposition can achieve an 
improved strength – ductility synergy. Our new strategy by controllable 
precipitating nano-structures provides a promising pathway to 
strengthen single phase alloys.
