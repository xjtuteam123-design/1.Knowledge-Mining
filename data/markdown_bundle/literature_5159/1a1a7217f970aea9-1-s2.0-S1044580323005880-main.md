ABSTRACT 
The effect of Ta on microstructural evolution and mechanical properties of a novel Ni-Co-based superalloy was 
investigated by thermodynamic calculations and selective laser melting (SLM). The results show that the addition 
of Ta promotes the precipitation of γ ' phase and carbide, and slightly increases the volume fraction of γ ' phase, 
consistent with thermodynamic calculations. The formation of stacking faults with enrichment of Ta and Ti in the 
alloys contribute to the preferential nucleation sites of the η phase, causing a transition from the γ ' phase to the η 
phase. The η phases precipitate from the 3%Ta and 4%Ta alloy and prefer lateral growth at the expense of the γ ' 
phase, resulting in large aspect ratio morphology. Moreover, the addition of Ta content of less than 2.0% in this 
SLM Ni-Co-based superalloys can significantly increase high-temperature yield and tensile strength. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys are widely used in high-temperature compo -
nents of aero-engines and gas turbines due to their good mechanical 
properties [ 1 ]. The excellent creep resistance was derived from the high 
volume fraction of γ ' phase by the addition of γ ' particles forming ele -
ments, such as Al, Ti, Nb, Ta, etc. [ 2 – 4 ]. The shape, size, and distribution 
of γ ' phase have an important effect on temperature-bearing capacity 
and long-term exposure [ 5 , 6 ]. The stability of γ ' phase was affected by 
adding a slow diffusing element such as Ta that partitioned to the γ ' 
phase [ 7 ]. 
Many research have been carried out on the effect of Ta on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of Ni-based superalloys 
[ 8 – 12 ]. The addition of Ta to the superalloy can increase the Al/Ti ratio 
in the γ’ phase, promote the precipitation of γ’ phase and also inhibit the 
aggregation and growth of γ ' precipitates, thus improving the high- 
temperature phase stability. Zhu et al. [ 13 ] revealed that Ta additions 
promote the γ ' volume fraction, the precipitation of MC carbide and η 
phase, but inhibit the precipitation of M

and creep properties was due to the increase in the grain boundary

the increase of Ta content in cast IN617 alloy increased the size and area 
fraction of MC carbides, rather than influencing as-cast microstructure 
features. Smith et al. [ 16 ] revealed that ME3 and ME501 with higher 
concentrations of the elements titanium, tantalum, and niobium 
encourage a shear-induced solid-state transformation from the γ ' to η 
phase along stacking faults in the γ ' precipitates, leading to significant 
improvement in creep properties. Antonov et al. [ 17 ] explored spanning 
a broad range of compositions in ternary eutectic γ - γ '- δ ( η ) Ni-base su -
peralloys and the precipitation of δ or η phase can be determined by 
evaluating the compositional ratio for (Nb + Ta)/(Al + Ti). Zheng et al. 
[ 18 ] reported that the addition of Ta improved the stress fracture 
properties of nickel-based superalloys. 
For the single-crystal superalloys with the addition of Ta, the struc -
tural stability of the γ ' precipitates was improved and the formation of 
the topologically close-packed phase during long-term thermal exposure 
was suppressed [ 19 – 21 ]. In the powder metallurgy Ni-base superalloy 
with the increase of Ta additions, the previous particle boundaries were

spheres to cuboids shape, begins to split and preferential growth occurs 
along the 〈 111 〉 direction [ 22 ]. The room-temperature properties were 
enhanced due to the antiphase boundary strengthening of the bimodal γ '

3. Results and discussion 
The alloys with different Ta contents characterized by EPMA are 
shown in Fig. 3 . The unmelted Ta powders identified by the red region 
were observed and increased with the increase of Ta content. However, 
by scanning the region of 1 mm × 1 mm without unmelted Ta powder 
(the red box shown in Fig. 3 ), it is found that the Ta content is within the 
design range. Subsequent analysis was focused on these regions. 
Fig. 4 shows the macrostructures of as-built samples by optical mi -
croscopy. Combined with the EPMA results of Fig. 3 , in the 1%Ta alloy 
only a few pores were observed due to the sputtering in the process. 
There are some unfused Ta powders except for the pores in the 2%Ta, 
3%Ta, and 4%Ta alloys. 
Fig. 5 shows the transversal microstructure of as-built alloys with 
different Ta contents. All the alloys formed the equiaxed cellular mi -
crostructures with a size of 0.5 – 1.0 μ m. The white particles are carbides 
precipitation. It is evident that Ta promotes the precipitation of carbides 
due to the strong binding force of Ta with C. In the SLM process, layer- 
by-layer repeated heating and cooling cycles caused repeated liquid- 
solid phase transformations. The cooling rate is ultra-fast and in the

To explore the mechanism of η phase formation, the composition, 
and distribution of precipitates in different Ta-content alloys were 
further studied by TEM. Fig. 8 shows the TEM, HRTEM morphologies, 
and diffraction patterns of the different Ta content alloys. In the 1%Ta 
alloy, the fine γ ' phases are homogeneously distributed in the matrix and 
the dislocations bowed out the γ ' precipitates. In the 2% Ta alloy, the 
dislocations are tangled and no η phase precipitation was found. How -
ever, stacking faults are formed in the γ ' phase by HRTEM observation. 
In the 3%Ta alloy, the needle-like phase formed on the γ ' phase is 
identified by SADE, which is the η phase, consistent with the X-ray 
diffraction results. 
For alloys in which δ (Ni
3
(Nb, Ta)) and/or η phase precipitates were 
formed, the prevalent phase could be determined by evaluating the 
compositional ratio for (Nb + Ta)/(Al + Ti) [ 17 ]. The ratio less than 1 
resulted in the precipitation of η phase and greater than 1 resulted in the 
precipitation of δ phase. In the present study, the ratios in the 1%Ta, 2% 
Ta, 3%Ta, and 4%Ta alloys are 0.089, 0.119, 0.149, and 0.180, 
respectively. These values are far below 1, and thus the predominance of 
the η phase precipitates would occur. The stability of η phase in the 
superalloy system proposed by Antonov et al. [ 17 ] can be predicted by 
the ratio of Al/(Nb + Ta + Ti). A ratio less than 0.85 resulted in the 
preferential stability of η phase. The ratios in the 1%Ta, 2%Ta, and 3% 
Ta alloys are 0.88, 0.86, and 0.79, respectively. Thus the γ ' phases in the 
3%Ta and 4%Ta alloys will become metastable and transform into the η 
phase, in agreement with the experimental results. 
Fig. 9 shows the η phase morphology observed in STEM mode using 
an Annular Bright Field (ABF) mode. The η phase with 600 nm length 
was selected to analyze the distribution of the elements by EDS line 
scanning mode. The Ta and Ti are significantly segregated in the η 
platelets. Ta and Ti are mainly distributed in the η and γ ' phases and 
decrease sharply at the interface between the η and γ phases. The η phase 
contained high Ta content by EDS analysis, indicating that Ta might play 
an important role in the formation of η phase.

4. Conclusions 
In this work, the effect of Ta addition on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of a novel Ni-Co-base superalloy was investi -
gated. The main conclusions can be drawn: 
1) The different Ta content alloys in the range of 1.0% to 4.0% were 
prepared by SLM according to Thermo-calc calculations. The as-built 
alloys exhibit the appearance of equiaxed cellular microstructures. 
The cellular grain size increases with the increase of Ta content due 
to the decrease in melting temperature. 
2) The addition of Ta promotes the precipitation of the γ ' phase as well 
as the precipitation of carbide distributed at grain boundaries and 
slightly increases the volume fraction of γ ' phase. The large aspect
