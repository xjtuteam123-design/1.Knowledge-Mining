Abstract 
 
Evolution of lattice characteristics in ordered 
γ′ and γ phases affected by Ta/W ratio was investigated in 
experimental Ni-base superalloys. Experimental analysis using Energy Dispersive Spectroscopy (EDS) 
and X-Ray Diffraction (XRD) and theoretical calculations illustrated that the ordering degree can be 
enhanced by the substitution of Ta for Al sites in the γ'-Ni 3(Al,Ta) lattice. A gradual increase in Ta/W 
ratio led to a noticeable increase in atomic ordering degree of γ'-Ni 3(Al,Ta) precipitates and the intensity 
values of the γ′ superlattice peaks normalized to that of the γ+γ' fundamental peaks in XRD patterns. EDS 
elemental analyses indicated that this phenomenon was associated with Ta atoms occupying Al sites in 
Ni 3(Al x,Ta 1-x) superlattice and raise its ordering tendency. Deconvolution of overlapped γ+γ' XRD peaks 
indicated that ′ γ lattice constant varies with the concentrations of Ta and Al. Furthermore, increasing 
Ta/W content up to 0.5 leads to substitution of Al and Ta for W atoms in the ordered ′ γ lattice. Also, by 
increasing Ta/W ratio far from 0.5, Ta atoms substitute for Al and W atoms. Meanwhile, γ/γ' lattice misfit 
and lattice parameters of ordered γ′ varied systematically with Ta, W and Al contents of γ′ superlattice. 
 
Keywords : Ni-base superalloy; ordering transformation; ordered 
γ′; lattice misfit; superlattice

1. Introduction 
Nickel-based superalloys with γ′ -Ni 3Al precipitate strengthening have been widely used for turbine blades 
application in high-temperature aero engines and land-based gas turbines [1–3]. For energy efficiency in 
power generation, when operating temperatures are beyond about 800 °C, there is a need for increasing 
strength and volume fraction of ′ γ via a refractory ′ γ forming element such as Ta [4,5]. 
In the new generation of Ni-based superalloys, an increase in Ta content up to 8 wt% has led to an 
obvious increase in ′ γ [6,7] and γ+γ' eutectic volume fraction [5,8]. In multicomponent superalloys having 
high Ta/W ratio, incoherent γ/γ' interface, Ta atoms tend to preferably partition into ′ γ, while W atoms 
inversely tend to partition into γ phase [9,10]. The tendency of Ta atoms to substitute for Al and W sites 
in the ′ γ lattice is due to its lower substitutional free energy in Ni 3Al lattice [11]. 
Ordered γ′ precipitate acts as an obstacle against the dislocation glide on a slip plane during creep 
deformation. The ordered γ′ -Ni 3Al phase is of the FCC type and contains four atoms (three nickel and 
one aluminum) per unit cell. The passage of a dislocation through half of a γ′ precipitate generates an 
unfavorable rearrangement of the aluminum and nickel atoms so that the Ni and Al atoms are opposite 
one another along that part of the slip plane that was traversed by the dislocation. This arrangement of 
nonpreferred atom pairs on the slip plane creates an antiphase domain boundary (APB) [12]. Since there 
is an additional energy associated with the APB, which depends on the degree of order in the lattice, 
dislocation motion is restricted [13]. Also, it has been reported that the principal source of strengthening 
in Ni-based alloys containing superlattices is due to the APB hardening arising from the ordered ′ γ 
particles [14,15]. 
Ta substitution for Al sites in ordered 
′ γ structure causes an increase in long-range order (LRO) degree 
[16] so that order parameter for Ta in the ′ γ lattice is near to one which shows that Ta has severe tendency 
to occupy Al sites [17]. 
Recent studies have indicated that 
′ γ nucleation in γ matrix is started by chemical ordering mechanism 
(atomic jump in short-range order) followed by spinodal decomposition (atomic diffusion in long-range 
order) [18,19]. Also, ordering usually takes place in γ matrix areas where more accumulation of Ta atoms

based on CM247 LC composition. Also, the effect of Ta/W content on the intensity ratios of superlattice 
peaks compared to the fundamental ones has been studied in more details considering the ′ γ composition 
changes. 
 
2. Materials and methods 
Ni-base superalloys were designed based on CM247 LC alloy. For increasing 
γ' phase stability at the 
high-temperature condition, the content of Ta as a strong γ' former element was increased from 2 to 8 
wt%. The total Ta+W content (in wt%) was kept constant around 12 wt%. in order to prevent TCP 
(Topologically Close-Packed) phase formation. Chemical compositions of the studied alloys are given in 
Table 1. The alloys were melted and cast in a vacuum induction melting (VIM) furnace under a 10 -5 bar 
vacuum into a preheated alumina mold. Alloys chemical compositions were determined using OXFORD 
Optical Emission Spectrometer (OES). γ' precipitates and γ+γ′ eutectics were detected after 
electrochemical etching in a solution composed of 5 cc Oxalic Acid + 95 cc Water. Volume fraction of 
γ+γ′ eutectic was measured by image analysis of SEM micrographs with low magnification using ImageJ

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Analysis of x-ray diffraction patterns of ordered and disordered γ' 
XRD patterns of designed alloys in direct aged condition are presented in Fig. 1. The position of the peak 
with maximum intensity differs within the alloys, due to the large grain size of around 10-20 mm and 
directional solidification condition for each grain [26]. In CM247 LC, CM0606 and CM0804 alloys the 
peak with maximum intensity is related to (110) plane of γ' phase, while in CM0210 and CM0408 the 
peak with maximum intensity is related to (111) plane of γ+γ' phases. Also, in CM0309 the peak with 
maximum intensity is reflected from (200) plane of γ+γ' phases. In Fig. 1, characteristic peaks of γ' phase 
appear in a wide range of intensities related to a distinct γ' phase having lattice characteristics different 
from common γ' phase (γ+γ' reflections). 
Evidently, increasing the Ta/W content led to a noticeable increase in intensity values of distinct ′ γ peaks 
normalized to that of the common γ+γ' peaks and also, the number of distinct ′ γ peaks has been increased. 
For instance, in the CM0804 pattern, there are more characteristic ′ γ peaks such as (100) and (211) which 
cannot be seen in CM247 LC pattern. It is known that in fcc cell, reflections will occur when (hkl) indices 
are unmixed (all h, k and l values are odd or even) [26]. Thus, in γ' phase with fcc structure, reflections 
will occur for (111) plane and not for the planes (100) and (110). This specific reflection is associated 
with a superlattice structure formed during an ordering transformation. 
If the atoms in a substitutional solid solution of γ' are completely randomly arranged each atom position is 
equivalent and γ' phase is named “disordered γ'” and contain short-range order (SRO). While In γ'

3.2. Characterization of ordered and disordered γ' lattices 
Lattice parameters of γ and ′ γ were calculated using Bragg’s law. Peak deconvolution was performed with 
high accuracy using pseudo-Voigt mathematical model which is a very applicable method to fit XRD raw 
data [24,31]. Fig. 3 depicts the γ, ordered ′ γ and disordered ′ γ peaks deconvoluted using pseudo-Voigt 
model by Origin software. Since the ordered and disordered ′ γ peaks have different diffraction angle 
(dissimilar lattice size), thus they are associated with γ'ID and γ'DC respectively. As stated in literature [24], 
γ lattice parameter is larger than ′ γ in CM247 LC superalloy. Thus the γ peak has the smallest 2θ value 
(larger lattice constant) among deconvoluted peaks (see Fig. 3). Also, according to the Bragg’s law, the 
ordered ′ γ represented by distinct ′ γ peaks, has the smallest lattice constant (the largest 2θ value). The 
calculated 2 θ values, lattice parameters of γ, ordered ′ γ and disordered ′ γ and γ/γ' lattice misfits are
