ABSTRACT
Refractory multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs) are promising for high-temperature applications because of 
their excellent mechanical properties. Developing RMPEAs with both high strength and ductility is a key priority 
in materials science. Previous studies have emphasized hafnium ’ s (Hf) crucial role in enhancing the strength and 
maintaining ductility of RMPEAs at room temperatures. However, the strengthening mechanisms induced by Hf 
are not fully understood and require further investigation. To address this, a series of Hf
x
NbTaTiV alloys with 
varying Hf concentration were designed and investigated by combining experiments with theoretical calcula -
tions. The alloys prepared by vacuum arc melting present body-centered cubic solid solutions, high tensile 
strength, and good ductility at room temperature. The addition of Hf substantially increases the alloys ’ tensile 
strength and hardness. Theoretical analyses indicate that solid solution strengthening is the primary mechanism 
responsible for strengthening these alloys. The increase of lattice distortion induced by Hf addition plays a key 
role in strengthening RMPEAs. This study not only clarifies the effects of Hf addition on the phase formation, 
lattice distortion, and strengthening mechanisms of Hf
x
NbTaTiV alloys, but also offers valuable insights for 
designing RMPEAs with high strength and ambient ductility.
1. Introduction
In metallurgical research, multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs), 
also referred to as high-entropy alloys or complex concentration alloys, 
have attracted considerable attention as a key area of study [ 1 ]. Based 
on their crystal structures, these alloys can be categorized into three 
main groups: body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution alloys, 
face-centered cubic (FCC) solid solution alloys, and intermetallic com -
pounds [ 1 ]. BCC MPEAs are primarily composed of pre-transition metals 
from groups IVB, VB, and VIB in the periodic table, e.g., titanium (Ti), 
zirconium (Zr), hafnium (Hf), vanadium (V), niobium (Nb), tantalum 
(Ta), chromium (Cr), molybdenum (Mo), and tungsten (W) [ 2 – 4 ]. These 
elements are known for their high melting points, which is why BCC 
MPEAs are often referred to as refractory multi-principal element alloys 
(RMEPAs). In contrast, FCC MPEAs are primarily composed of 
post-transition metals, e.g., cobalt (Co), chromium (Cr), iron (Fe), 
manganese (Mn), and nickel (Ni) [ 1 , 5 ]. FCC MPEAs, such as CoCr -
FeMnNi [ 6 , 7 ], CoCrFeNi [ 8 , 9 ], and CoCrNi [ 10 , 11 ], are recognized for 
their superior strength and ductility.
RMPEAs differ significantly from FCC MPEAs in melting points and 
high-temperature performance [ 2 ]. Their high melting points provide 
them with excellent strength and stability at elevated temperature over

high-temperature strength of RMPEAs, but reduce their ductility, while 
elements like Zr, Ti, and Hf can enhance both strength and ductility [ 4 ].

In RMPEA design, computational materials science, especially first- 
principles calculations, has become an essential tool [ 29 , 30 ]. 
First-principles calculations provide quantum mechanics-based pre -
dictions of physicochemical properties without the need for empirical 
data, thus complementing experiments. They are particularly useful for 
exploring the vast compositional space of RMPEAs, where experiments 
are often constrained by resources and time limitations [ 31 ]. 
First-principles calculations can reveal how chemical compositions 
affect material properties like electronic structure, phase stability, 
thermodynamics, and mechanics, enabling predictions of key properties 
like elastic modulus, strength, and plasticity [ 30 , 32 ]. For example, 
first-principles calculations accurately predicted the elastic constants of 
NbV, NbTiV, and MoNbV alloys [ 32 ]. Adding Ti to NbV raises the Zener 
anisotropy ratio to 0.99, while Mo lowers it to 0.52 [ 32 ]. In the 
TiZrHfNbTa series, increasing VB group elements boosts the elastic 
modulus, hardness, and strength, with TiHfNbTa reaching a yield 
strength of 1.28 GPa [ 33 ]. The addition of Hf improves Poisson ’ s ratio 
and dislocation nucleation, enhancing toughness through metallic 
bonding [ 33 ]. Further calculations reveal that Mo, W, and Re elements 
increase strength and hardness in NbTaTiZr-based alloys [ 34 ]. V and Hf 
alloying improves alloys ’ plasticity, with NbTaTiZrHf showing good 
ductility [ 34 ]. Furthermore, first-principles calculations are essential for 
understanding how local lattice distortions and chemical environments 
impact performance, which is critical for solid solution strengthening 
and phase transformation in RMPEAs. Such as, the first-principles cal -
culations revealed that V addition tends to increase local lattice distor -
tion and electronic delocalization in the HfNbTaTiV
x 
alloys, which plays 
a key role in maintaining the high strength and ductility of RMPEAs 
[ 23 ]. Overall, first-principles calculations aid in understanding 
atomic-scale behavior, guiding experiments, improving material prop -
erties, and accelerating the development of new materials.

2.3. Thermodynamic and first-principles calculations
The CALPHAD method was utilized to analyze the phase formation 
and solidification behaviors of Hf
x
NbTaTiV alloys using the TCHEA5 
database of Thermo-Calc software [ 35 ]. Furthermore, the effects of Hf 
content on the structural stability, elastic properties, anisotropy, and 
lattice distortion of the alloys were explored using the DFT calculations. 
Supercells for DFT calculations were constructed using the special 
quasi-random structure (SQS) method to simulate chemical randomness 
[ 36 ]. The SQS method has been widely applied to represent disordered 
chemical distributions in high-entropy materials [ 37 , 38 ]. A larger 
supercell usually exhibits greater disorder, yet the computational cost 
also escalates [ 39 ]. To strike a balance between the accuracy of supercell 
and computational expense, each SQS supercell comprises 160 atoms. 
The Hf atom count in SQS supercells was set to 0, 8, 16, 24, and 32, 
corresponding to molar fractions of 0.00, 0.21, 0.44, 0.71, and 1.00, 
ensuring the Hf concentration closely mirrored the experimental com -
positions. Fig. 1 displays typical SQS supercells for each alloy. For each 
alloy composition, geometric optimization simulations were conducted 
on the five SQS supercells exhibiting favorable disorder, as reflected by 
the lowest objective function values [ 40 , 41 ]. The supercell with the 
lowest energy was chosen for subsequent calculations. The elastic 
properties were calculated using a finite strain-stress method based on 
the generalized Hook ’ s law σ = C ε , where C , σ , and ε mark elastic con -
stants, stress, and stain matrix, respectively. Seven strains ( ε = 0.000, 
± 0.005, ± 0.010, ± 0.015) were applied in each deformation degree of 
freedom. Several crucial mechanical parameters, such as Young ’ s 
modulus ( E ), shear modulus ( G ), bulk modulus ( B ), and Poisson ’ s ratio 
( ν ), were predicated based on the calculated elastic constants according 
to the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximation [ 42 ]. All DFT calculations were 
performed using the VASP package [ 43 ], with the GGA-PBE exchan -
ge-correlation functional [ 44 ] and PAW pseudopotentials [ 45 ]. A 
plane-wave energy cutoff of 550 eV was implemented. Convergence was 
reached when the energy and force criteria were satisfied at 10

4. Conclusion
In this work, Hf
x
NbTaTiV RMPEAs were synthesized via vacuum arc 
melting, which display a single-phase BCC structure with high tensile 
yield strength and ductility at room temperature. The addition of Hf 
markedly improves yield strength and hardness, primarily through solid 
solution strengthening due to atomic radius and shear modulus dispar -
ities. DFT calculations confirm that Hf-induced lattice distortion is a key 
factor in strength enhancement, with increased distortion and inhomo -
geneous charge distribution accompanying higher Hf concentrations. 
Further research is needed to understand the reduction in ductility with 
Hf addition and its effects on dislocation motion in BCC RMPEAs, as well 
as to explore elastic anisotropy mechanisms.
