ABSTRACT
Polycrystalline high/medium entropy alloys (HEA/MEAs) strengthened by dense L1
2
-structured nanoparticles 
have demonstrated significant promise for high-temperature structural applications. However, their widespread 
adoption is hindered by severe intergranular embrittlement starting from intermediate temperatures. In our 
present work, we introduce coherent intergranular D0
19
-structured precipitates into a polycrystalline L1
2
- 
strengthened MEA to address such thorny problem. Unlike conventional polycrystalline counterparts that suffer 
from pronounced intergranular embrittlement, the newly developed structure exhibits exceptional resistance to 
intergranular fracture at an intermediate temperature of 800 
◦
C. At this temperature, our designed alloy exhibits 
a superior yield strength of ~ 713 MPa and excellent fracture elongation of ~ 24.3 %. Microstructural analysis 
reveals that the enhanced ductility arises from the suppressed cracking by coherent intergranular precipitates, in 
combination with the high-density heat-assisted twinning facilitated by Nb segregation. These findings provide a 
novel strategy for the design of high-temperature structural materials.

MEAs) strengthened by high-density L1

L1

The excellent mechanical properties of our 1020A alloy tested at 800

D0
19

embrittlement. Subsequently, we focused on the crack nucleation and 
propagation behaviors to reveal the structure origins of the suppressed 
intergranular brittleness. The fractured ends of the two investigated 
alloys were carefully analyzed by SEM and EBSD techniques ( Fig. 3 b- 
d and f-h). Large cracks are observed in the fractured end of the 1200A 
alloy ( Fig. 3 b). The EBSD maps ( Fig. 3 c and d) further reveal that local 
stress is primarily concentrated in the vicinity of HAGBs, simultaneously 
the propagation of large crack along HAGBs is found in the tested 1200A 
alloy. As indicated by literature, HAGBs have relatively higher energies 
and activities [ 22 , 48 ], as well as high local stress (arising from the 
accumulation of dislocations during slip-assisted grain boundary 
sliding) [ 19 ], which make them as the preferred sites or paths for crack 
initiation and propagation. Moreover, intergranular oxidation takes 
place preferentially at HAGBs, which in turn leads to dynamical envi -
ronmental intergranular embrittlement [ 15 , 18 , 19 , 25 , 49 ]. Once inter -
granular microcracks form in oxidizing environments at elevated 
temperatures, continuous penetration of oxygen at the crack tips ac -
celerates the rapid propagation of them when exposed to increased 
tensile stress. Surprisingly, intergranular embrittlement is significantly 
suppressed in the 1020A alloy. As evident in SEM image ( Fig. 3 f), 
massive separated microcracks are heterogeneously distributed in the 
fracture end of 1020A alloy. Further investigations ( Fig. 3 g and h) 
reveal that those microcracks initiate at HAGBs, and terminate at 
intergranular D0

Furthermore, we examined the deformation characters through TEM 
technique to reveal the underlying mechanisms of the impressive 
ductility of our designed alloys tested at 800 
◦
C. In the 1200A alloy, TEM 
BF image ( Fig. 4 a) clearly demonstrates a typical planar slip behavior of 
dislocations substructure. HRTEM image ( Fig. 4 b) reveals that SFs are 
activated in the two {111} planes. In addition, the immobile Lomer- 
Cottrell (L-C) lock is observed as the extra carrier of plasticity 
( Fig. 4 b), resulting from the reaction of dissociated partial dislocations 
[ 51 ]. Different with the SFs-mediated model in the 1200A alloy, 
deformation twinning (DT) was activated as the dominate deformation 
mechanism in the 1020A alloy. As evident by TEM BF and DF images 
( Fig. 4 c and d), high density parallel DTs (confirmed by inset SAED in 
Fig. 4 c) are observed. Intersecting DTs along two {111} planes (Fig. S8) 
are also found in the tested 1020A alloy. The HAADF-STEM image 
( Fig. 4 e) clearly demonstrates a nanotwin and a 
superlattice-extrinsic-stacking fault (SESF, acting as a twin embryo) 
within the FCC matrix. Interestingly, the interfacial regions of nanotwin 
and SESF are brighter in comparison with the bulk of surrounding FCC 
matrix, which suggests the segregation of heavy elements. The 
atomic-resolution EDS mapping ( Fig. 4 f) confirms that these features 
shear through the ordered L1
2
-structured nanoparticle, leaving signifi -
cant Nb element segregation around the nanotwin and SESF. Similar 
heavy elements segregation (such as Nb, Ta, Mo, W) has been reported 
to facilitate the formation of twinning in Ni-based superalloys [ 52 – 54 ]. 
The twinning process during creep or high-temperature tensile is asso -
ciated with the shear of the a/6 < 112 > Shockley partials into 
L1
2
-structured domains and FCC matrix [ 29 , 55 , 56 ], resulting in a 
complex stacking-fault (CSF) characterized by a high-energy two-layer 
structure. This CSF is notably marked by wrong nearest neighbour B-B 
bonds, i.e., combination or mixture of Al-Al and Al-Nb wrong bonds in 
the A
3
B unit cell. To transit these high-energy CSFs to SESFs, a series of 
heat-assisted two-step reordering is required, including the substitution 
of high-energy wrong Al-Nb bonds with lower-energy Al-Al bonds, fol -
lowed by the replacement of Al-Al bonds with Ni-Al bonds [ 57 ]. 
Through these reordering steps, the original fault sequence of the SESFs 
will shift outwards, i.e., perpendicular to the shearing planes, thereby 
creating the twin boundaries [ 54 ]. Subsequently, the solute Nb posi -
tioned at the previous SESFs diffuses towards the twin boundaries via 
cross-plane diffusion processes [ 58 , 59 ]. Therefore, the observed 
heat-assisted Nb segregation ( Fig. 4 f) in the tested 1020A alloy at 800

intergranular degradation, an enhanced creep resistance at intermediate
