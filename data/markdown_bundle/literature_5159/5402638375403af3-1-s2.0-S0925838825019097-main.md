ABSTRACT
High-entropy alloys with a single-phase FCC structure are widely employed as structural materials due to their 
excellent ductility and plasticity. Nevertheless, the strength and oxidation resistance of single-phase FCC alloys 
remain inadequate. Conventional methods for enhancing strength often compromise plasticity, rendering them 
unsuitable for engineering applications. This study presents a strategy for the preparation of CoCrNi-based 
AlxCoCr
0.6
NiV
0.6 
alloys, characterized by unequal atomic ratios and high entropy, incorporating aluminum 
and vanadium. Short-range order was observed in each alloy composition, varying in size and crystal plane 
families. At an aluminum content of x = 0.85, both FCC and BCC dual phases were present in the alloy. The 
heterogeneous structure and dual-phase presence significantly enhanced the strength and high-temperature 
oxidation resistance of the high-entropy alloy. The yield strength of the high-entropy alloy increased from 
235 MPa to 974 MPa, while the oxide layer thickness decreased from 37.5 μ m to 8.3 μ m, representing a reduction 
of 77 %. This study provides a method for adjusting the cross-scale heterostructures in high-entropy alloys 
(HEAs) and guides the design of CoCrNi-based HEAs with high strength, high ductility, and excellent antioxidant 
properties.
1. Introduction
With the rapid development of the aerospace industry, the main key 
components are subjected to high temperatures and high loads in 
extreme working conditions. The structural materials not only have a 
certain degree of strong plasticity but also should have good high- 
temperature oxidation resistance [1,2] . As a new type of material 
whose composition, structure and composition can be regulated, high 
entropy alloy (HEA) has attracted great attention since its discovery [3] . 
Compared to conventional alloys, HEAs have flexible design freedom in 
composition [4,5] . HEAs based on CoCrNi have become the most 
important research objects due to their good plasticity and ductility 
[6 – 8] . However, the low yield strength (less than 400 MPa) of the as-cast 
CoCrNi alloy restricts its engineering applications under extreme 
loading conditions [9,10] . Consequently, further optimization of the 
mechanical properties of CoCrNi-based alloys. Considering the appli -
cation conditions, one should also pay attention to the high-temperature 
oxidation resistance of CoCrNi-based HEAs.
To enhance the overall performance of HEAs, extensive works have 
been conducted on several aspects: developing novel sintering tech -
nology [11,12] , elemental alloying [13 – 17] , introducing a second phase 
of reinforcement particles [18 – 20] , and post-treatment post-treatment 
[21 – 24] . Current research indicates that introducing a heterogeneous 
structure is one of the more effective strategies, as it maintains a 
coherent relationship with the matrix [25 – 36] . The cross-scale hetero -
structure affects the strengthening and work hardening rates of high 
entropy alloys to varying degrees. Atomic-scale heterogeneous structure 
has only been reported and observed in recent years [37] . The presence 
of short range order (SRO) increased the yield strength of the 
high-entropy alloy by about 100 MPa and doubled the work hardening 
rate. Cao et al. gave the atomic models of disordered structures and 
different kinds of heterostructures [26] . They also revealed that the 
heterostructure of the VCoNi system was related to the higher energy of 
V-V than V-Co and V-Ni. The addition of V facilitates the formation of 
heterogeneous structures. Su et al. [38] reported that SRO can be 
induced by irradiation in NiCoFeCrMn. Bo et al. [39] reported that in
