FRACTOGRAPHIC STUDIES OF ALLOYS
OF THE Ti – Al – Nb SYSTEM WITH HIGH CONTENT OF NIOBIUM
G . A. Bochvar,1 V . S. Salenkov,1 and A. S. Fainbron 1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 3, pp. 38 – 40, March, 2008.
Results of a study of fracture surfaces of two alloys of the Ti – Al – Nb system tested for tensile strength and
impact bending strength at room temperature after heat treatment performed in different modes are presented.
INTRODUCTION
High-temperature alloys based on intermetallic com -
pounds including titanium aluminide have attracted much in-
terest among researchers in the recent years [1]. The greatest
interest is drawn to titanium aluminide “ortho”-alloys that
possess better adaptability to manufacture and elevated duc-
tility at room temperature as compared to other alloys based
on these intermetallics [2, 3].
One of the directions of research in Russia and other
countries consists in determining the regular features of for-
mation of the phase composition, structure, and mechanical
properties of semiproducts depending on the conditions of
deformation and modes of heat treatment [4 – 8].
Most intermetallic alloys have low parameters of ductil -
ity at room temperature due to their brittleness. In this con -
nection it is interesting to study the fracture behavior of such
alloys as a function of the mode of heat treatment, phase
composition, and structure.
The aim of the present work was to study the fracture be-
havior of Ti – 10% Al – 43.5% Nb and Ti – 13.5% Al –
42.5% Nb alloys depending on the kind of heat treatment.
METHODS OF STUDY
We studied fracture surfaces on specimens of two alloys,
i.e., Ti – 10 wt.% Al – 43.5 wt.% Nb (Ti – 21.67 at.% Al –
27.39 at.% Nb) and Ti – 13.5 wt.% Al – 42.5 wt.% Nb (Ti –
26.65 at.% Al – 24.39 at.% Nb).
The specimens fabricated from semiproducts by pressing
and rolling were subjected to tensile and impact bending
tests at room temperature after heat treatment performed in
different modes.
The specimens were studied after water quenching and
air hardening from the temperatures of existence of /c98- and
/c972-phases (1000 – 1050°C) close to the transition to a sin -
gle-phase /c98-state and after subsequent annealing at the tem-
peratures of formation of an O-phase (below 900°C).
Fractographic studies were performed with the help of a
“Tsitoval” stereomicroscope in a set with JXA-733 x-ray
microanalyzer.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Our studies showed that the fracture behavior of rupture
and impact specimens of one alloy under the same kind of
deformation (pressing or rolling) and the same mode of heat
treatment of the semiproducts was virtually the same.
A typical appearance of fracture surfaces on specimens
of alloy Ti – 10% Al – 43.5% Nb is presented in Fig. 1.
The pressed alloy (which has a greater size of equiaxed
grains) after quenching from 1050°C for 1 h with air cooling
is characterized by transcrystalline fracture with equiaxed
cleavage facets and river lines (Fig. 1a ). The phase composi-
tion of the specimens is represented by a /c98-phase and a low
amount of an /c972-phase.2
After heat treatment consisting of quenching from
1050°C, 1 h, air cooling + 820°C, 5 h, air cooling fracture
develops by the intercrystalline mechanism with prevalence
of smooth grain-boundary and virtually equiaxial facets
(Fig. 1b ). The phase composition of the specimens is repre -
sented by /c98+ /c972 +O .
In the rolled alloy, which is characterized by the presence
of grains elongated over the rolling direction and different
degrees of decomposition of the /c98-phase, the fracture deve -

Typical appearances of fractures in specimens of alloy
Ti – 13.5% Al – 42.5% Nb is presented in Fig. 2.
Pressed alloy quenched from 1050°C for 1 h with air
cooling is characterized by fracture with plane and curved
inter- and transcrystalline facets (Fig. 2 a ). After heat treat -
ment consisting of quenching from 1050°C, 1 h, air cooling
+ 820°C, 5 h, air cooling the fracture surface is much
rougher but the mechanism of the fracture is also predomi -
nantly brittle (Fig. 2 b ). After both modes of heat treatment
the alloy contains /c972- and O-phases and, in contrast to alloy
Ti – 10% Al – 43.5% Nb, it does not bear a /c98-phase.
Rolled alloy quenched from 1050°C for 1 h with water
cooling (the phase composition is represented by /c98+ traces
of /c972 ) undergoes transcrystalline fracture with formation of
cleavage facets with river lines (Fig. 2 c ). After heat treat -
ment including quenching from 1050°C, 1 h, air cooling +
820°C, 5 h, air cooling, the alloy undergoes stepped trans -
crystalline fracture and bears fine facets (Fig. 2 d ). Just as in
the case of the pressed alloy, the fracture surface after such
heat treatment is rougher.
All kinds of fracture observed in the studied specimens
of alloy Ti – 13.5% Al – 42.5% Nb (even after water quench-
ing) can be classified as brittle ones.
Analysis of the results of the fractographic study shows
that alloys Ti – 10% Al – 43.5% Nb and Ti – 13.5% Al –
42.5% Nb primarily undergo different types of transcrystal-
line and intercrystalline brittle fracture. Fracture with consid-
erable regions of ductile component is observed only in
rolled specimens of alloy and Ti – 10% Al – 43.5% Nb
cooled in water or in air from the temperatures of the sin -
gle-phase /c98- or double-phase ( /c98+ /c972 )-states; these speci -
mens contain a /c98-phase.
The presence of /c98-phase in the structure of the specimens
promotes the development of ductile fracture and ensures
higher ductility parameters. The ductility is also affected by
the size and morphology of the structural components.
The development of intercrystalline fracture in the alloys
may be a result of segregation of particles of /c972-phase of dif-
ferent discreteness over boundaries of /c98-grains [8].
The increase in the roughness of fracture surfaces of
specimens of the alloys after heat treatment consisting of
quenching from 1000 – 1050°C for 1 h with air cooling fol -
lowed by quenching from 820°C for 5 h with air cooling is
connected with the hardening of grain bodies due to segrega-
tion of an O-phase in the second stage of such treatment.
