Abstract
Al was replaced partially by Nb, Ta and Ti in precipitation-hardened Pt-base alloys, resulting in elevated compression strengths at high tempera-
tures. The polycrystalline Pt-base alloys containing 5 at.% Nb and Ta, respectively, are stronger than the single-crystal Ni-base superalloy CMSX-4
in [0 0 1] orientation above 1200◦C. By the addition of Nb, Ta and Ti the /H9253′solvus temperature is increased.

1. Introduction
Within the last 10 years, new precipitation-hardened alloys
based on platinum group metals (PGM) with high melting points
have gained large interest. Research in this ﬁeld was initiated
by Yamabe et al.[1], who presented Ir-base alloys with remark-
able strength at temperatures well above 1150
◦C, the maximum
operation temperature of sophisticated Ni-base superalloys like
CMSX-4 [2]. As in Ni-base superalloys, coherent L1
2-ordered
intermetallic cuboidal particles (called /H9253′) are precipitated from
the PGM-rich fcc matrix (called/H9253) for effective hardening up to
high temperatures and maintaining a good ductility.
/H9253′-hardened Pt-base alloys are developed for service up to
1300 ◦C and especially in oxidative media. In previous stud-
ies, the authors investigated alloys in the systems Pt–Al–Cr
and Pt–Al–Cr–Ni [3–7]. Although these alloys show the desired
/H9253–/H9253
′microstructure with high /H9253′volume fraction, their strength
at high temperatures requires further improvement. Hill et al.
[8] reported Pt-base alloys with Nb-, Ta- and Ti-rich precipi-
tates to have high room-temperature hardness. These elements
were then added to the ternary alloy Pt
82Al12Cr6 [7] as sub-
stitute for 5 at.% Al, and the strength of the resulting alloys

3. Results
The crystallographic structures of the /H9253matrix and the /H9253′
phase in Pt 82Al7Cr6Nb5,P t 82Al7Cr6Ta5 and Pt 82Al7Cr6Ti5
were identiﬁed by XRD analysis as fcc and L1 2. The room-
temperature lattice misﬁts after ageing for 120 h at 1200◦C are
about −3 × 10−3 in all alloys. After homogenization heat treat-
ment the alloys show bimodal size distributions of /H9253′particles
(Fig. 1a–c). Beside coarse and irregularly shaped particles with
a volume fraction in-between 10 and 20%, small cuboids of up
to 300 nm in size are observed in all alloys. After subsequent
ageing for 264 h at 1200
◦C with water quenching, measure-
ment of the chemical compositions revealed Nb and Ta to be
almost evenly distributed to /H9253matrix and /H9253

is enriched in the /H9253′phase (Table 1). The volume fractions of
all /H9253′phases after ageing are estimated by image analysis [4]
to 34% in Pt 82Al7Cr6Nb5, 33% in Pt 82Al7Cr6Ta5 and 35% in

4. Discussion and conclusion
A successful solution heat treatment for 6 h at 1500
◦C and 6 h
at 1510◦C showed that in Pt82Al12Cr6 the /H9253′phase is fully solu-
ble in the/H9253matrix [7]. However, Pt82Al7Cr6Nb5,P t82Al7Cr6Ta5
and Pt 82Al7Cr6Ti5 remain two-phased at 1510 ◦C. Hence,
the /H9253′ solvus temperature is increased by the addition
of the elements Nb, Ta and Ti with their high melting
points.
Especially, Nb and Ta additions lead to a rise in mechanical
strength at high temperatures, which is – in case of Ta – in
accordance with the observations of Rudnik[9].P t
82Al7Cr6Nb5
shows the second largest strength amongst the precipitation-
hardened Pt-base alloys known up-to-date after Pt74.5Hf17Rh8.5
[10].
Almost equal /H9253′volume fractions of the alloys Pt 82Al7Cr6
Nb5 and Pt 82Al7Cr6Ta5 having high strengths compared to
Pt83Al12Cr5 and Pt 82Al7Cr6Ti5 with lower strengths indicate
good solid solution strengthening of the /H9253matrix by Nb and Ta.
At all temperatures, inter-granular rupture occurs in the poly-
crystalline Pt-base alloys. In the same way as in the Pt–Al–Cr–Ni
alloys tested previously [6,7], weak grain boundaries appear to
be the limiting factors for the high-temperature strengths of the
alloys tested in this study. Investigations with additions of B as
grain boundary strengthener are underway and will be published
soon.
