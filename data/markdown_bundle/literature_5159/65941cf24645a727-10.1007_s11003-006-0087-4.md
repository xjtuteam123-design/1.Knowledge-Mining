We study new alloys of the Ti – Si – Al – Zr system. It is shown that the high-temperature and cy-
clic corrosion crack-growth resistances of cast materials remain practically constant up to tempe-
ratures of 700–800°C and in a 3% NaCl solution (pH = 7), respectively. Annealing and
quenching from the ( α + β )- and β-phase regions of Ti – 4Si – 5Al – 5Zr cast alloy lead mainly to
changes in the structure of the matrix and do not improve its strength and cyclic crack-growth re-
sistance in the investigated working media, except the increase in the threshold of corrosion fa-
tigue after treatment from the 
 β-region and formation of a lamellar α-matrix. The increase in the
strength, plasticity, and cyclic crack-growth resistance of these alloys at room and high tempera-
tures is attained as a result of the formation of a globular structure of the matrix and silicide
phase after thermomechanical treatment with deformation of at least 
 90%. 
The development of contemporary machine building is connected with the necessity of creation new struc-
tural materials with high physicochemical and mechanical characteristics stable under the action of elevated tem-
peratures and corrosive media. The indicated requirements are especially well satisfied by alloys based on titani-
um and extensively used, due to their high specific strength and corrosion resistance, in the aircraft, ship-build-
ing, chemical, and power-generating industries, in medicine (as biocompatible materials), etc. [1–3]. However,
their working temperature is 
 400–600°C, which is insufficient in the contemporary stage of development of the
equipment [4]. The restricted sphere of applicability of titanium alloys is also explained by their susceptibility to
hydrogen embrittlement and corrosion cracking [5–7]. 
At present, extensive research works are carried out in the field of creation of new materials with improved
characteristics of high-temperature strength. In this connection, we can especially mention cast alloys of the 
 Ti –
Si – Al – Zr system created at the Institute for Problems in Materials Science of the Ukrainian National Academy
of Sciences and the Ukrainian National Metallurgical Academy [8–11]. Similar materials are also proposed by
scientists from the other countries [12–15]. Due to the presence of frame-type eutectics ( α-Ti + Ti 5 Si3 ), they
are regarded as in-situ composites. These materials are characterized by high strength in temperature ranges
broader than conventional titanium alloys, since the melting temperature of the reinforcing silicide phase
(Ti5Si3 ) is equal to 2130°C. However, in view of their low plasticity, these materials are sensitive to the influ-
ence of stress concentrators and brittle fracture. Their m echanical characteristics can be im proved by thermo-
mechanical treatment [16]. However, in this case, the heterogeneity of the structure increases, which can negati-
vely affect the corrosion properties of these metallocomposites. Therefore, it is important to optimize their chem-
ical composition, structure, and phase state by applying the methods of structural and corrosion fracture mech-
anics of materials. Note that the methods aimed at the evaluation of the characteristics of fatigue fracture resis-
tance of materials are of especial interest because the threshold cyclic crack-growth resistance (and, hence, the
level of admissible stresses in structural elements) can be much lower than the corresponding static crack resis-

In order to determine the cyclic crack-growth resistance according to the diagram of growth rates of fatigue
macrocracks ( da / dN, Δ K ), beam specimens 4 × 8 × 40 mm in size with lateral notches were tested by three-
point bending [20]. 
The lateral sides of the specimens were polished in order to directly observe the process of crack growth
with an MPB-2 optical microscope with a measurement error of 0.02 mm. A notch 0.12 mm in width was made
by the method of electroerosion. The specimens were cyclically loaded with a given amplitude of displacements,
a frequency of 10 Hz, and the stress ratio R = Pmin / Pmax = 0.1 and 0.6. The same specimens without notches
were tested for strength under the conditions of three-point bending and monotonic loading. The tests were car-
ried out in laboratory air at temperatures of 20, 700, and 800°C and in a 3% aqueous solution of NaCl at 20°C.

Results of Investigations and Discussion
The microfractographic specific features of fracture and the characteristics of cyclic crack-growth resis-
tance of cast metallocomposites subjected to thermal treatment and thermal deformation in laboratory air are de-
termined by their microstructure. Thus, the M0 and T0 cast modifications include a globular-lamellar α-matrix
and colonies of silicides (whose structure is, for the most part, dendrite) arranged in the form of a reinforcing
frame (Table 2, Figs.
 1a, b). The deformation of composites of this sort is impossible without the fracture of sili-
cides and the formation of a branched network of microcracks, which leads, as follows from the data of fracto-
graphic analyses (Fig.
 2a), to the brittle fracture of these materials despite the fact that the titanium matrix is rela-
tively plastic. As a result, the cyclic fracture toughness of the composites becomes quite low ( Δ Kfc = 14 –
16 MPa m ). At the same time, their threshold cyclic crack-growth resistance ( Δ Kth = 4 – 5 MPa m ) is suffi-
ciently high and corresponds to the levels of fatigue thresholds of high-strength steels and titanium alloys. 
The procedures of annealing and quenching from the ( α + β )- and β-regions affect mainly the structures of
the matrices of the analyzed composites (Table 2, Figs. 1c, d).

As the structure becomes lamellar (see Fig. 1d) after thermal treatment from the β-region (M016 and M022
modifications), this positively affects the threshold cyclic crack-growth resistance and increases the value of
Δ Kth by 50% (curves 3 and 4 in Fig. 3a). The role of the lamellar structure as a barrier for the growth of fatigue
cracks is connected with the stimulation of a micromechanism of fatigue fracture of partial shear (Fig. 2b), in-
crease in the roughness of the fracture surface, and strengthening of the effect of fatigue-crack closure. After an-
nealing in the ( α + β )-region (M05 alloy), the globular matrix (see Fig. 1c) does not change the value of Δ Kth
as compared with the as-cast state (curve 2 in Fig. 3a). In all cases, the critical crack-growth resistance Δ Kfc in-
significantly (by 10–15%) increases as a result of thermal treatment of cast materials. 
The thermomechanical treatment of cast composites leads to the fracture of the eutectic colonies of silicides
and a significant grinding of structural components (Table 2, Figs. 1e, f). As a result, after preliminary defor-
mation [to a level of strains of 90% (T1 modification)], the level of plasticity in tension increases to 1.5–2%
[16]. This affects the cyclic crack-growth resistance of the material in two ways (Fig. 3b): the threshold value
Δ Kth somewhat decreases (as can be expected due to the formation of the globular structure) but the critical val-
ue Δ Kfc significantly increases (by 40–50%). This is reflected by the microfractographic features of the frac-
ture surfaces observed in the form of microregions of ductile fracture of quasipitting nature (Fig. 2c). The ther-
momechanical treatment with a level of strains of 41% (M4 modification) weakly affects the cyclic crack-
growth resistance of the material in this state as compared with cast materials (Fig. 3b). 
The comparison of the fatigue-crack growth resistances of the best thermally treated and thermally de-
formed alloys (versions 4 and 7) with the well-known titanium alloys shows (Fig. 3c) that the former have cer-
tain advantages (the fatigue threshold Δ Kth is 1.5 – 2 times higher, respectively) for products operating under
the conditions of low cyclic loads (in the region of high-cycle fatigue). For high loading amplitudes, the proper-
ties of metallocomposites are somewhat worse than the corresponding properties of the conventional alloys
(
 Δ Kfc ≤ 22 and 30–40 MPa m, respectively), although the values of their cyclic fracture toughness Δ Kfc for
the same α-matrix are comparable.

The behavior of the tested metallocomposites in corrosive media is similar to the behavior of conventional
titanium alloys [25] characterized by high corrosion resistance for smooth surfaces but sensitive to the action of
media in the presence of notches and cracks [26]. Thus, the middle section of the kinetic diagrams is especially
sensitive to the action of corrosive media for low stress ratios (
 R = 0.1 ) (Fig. 7). For R = 0.6, the crack re-
mains open for the entire loading cycle and noticeable tensile stresses permanently act near the crack tip. As a
result, the character of influence of corrosive media changes. The difference between the values of Δ Kth ob-
tained in corrosive media and in air is insignificant. However, the corrosion-fatigue crack-growth rate rapidly
increases with the level of loading, which strongly decreases the critical values of Δ Kfc (Fig. 7).

CONCLUSIONS
The experimental data obtained in laboratory air at high temperatures and in corrosive media show that the
problem of relatively low cyclic fracture toughness (Δ Kfc ≤ 22 MPa m ) of subeutectic alloys of the Ti – Si –
Al – Zr system (“titanium cast irons” [8]) remains open. This is explained by the brittleness of the reinforcing
silicide phase whose content can be as high as 30%. The solution of this problem is obviously connected with
changes in the morphology and amount of the silicide phase and, hence, opens broad prospects for hypereutectic
alloys 
 (“titanium steels” [8]).
