Refractory metals and alloys based on these metals are used on an increasing scale as 
structural materials. These materials are most promising for applications at very high temper 
atures, thus opening new possibilities in a number of branches of industry. 
During service, the components made of the refractory metals and alloys, and used in 
equipment of space technology, thermoelectronic converters, etc., are subjected to the effect 
of cyclically varying loads and temperatures. It is therefore important to examine the rela- 
tionships governing the deformation and failure of these materials in the conditions of low- 
cycle mechanical and thermal loading. It should be mentioned that while the relationships 
of the deformation and failure of structural and creep-resisting alloys in these conditions 
have been studied in detail [1-8], the amount of experimental data available for the refrac- 
tory metals and alloys is very small and this complicates the determination of general rela- 
tionships governing the deformation and fracture of these materials. 
In this report, the authors describe the results of investigations into the relation- 
ships of deformation and fracture of a polycrystalline molybdenum alloy at constant and 
cyclically varying (according to a specific program) temperatures and loads, the development 
of a method of evaluating the load-carrying capacity of materials based on molybdenum, the 
determination of the controlling mechanism of localizatio~ of deformation and fracture, and 
the explanation of local structural changes and their effect on the mechanical properties. 
The investigations were conducted on low-alloy molybdenum alloy 4604 of the Mo--V-C sys- 
tem [9, i0], which is a typical alloy with carbide hardening and is used as a structural 
material at high temperatures. The alloy was produced by conventional production technology, 
which included a number of operations, such as melting of the ingot in a vacuum-arc furnace, 
pressing, high-temperature annealing, forging, etc. Prior to testing, the specimens were 
vacuum-annealed for 1 h at 1773~ 
The mechanical properties of the given material in a wide temperature range, determined 
in the conditions of active static tensile loading at a relative strain rate of approximately 
1.5"10 -3 sec -I, are given in Table i. 
The investigations were carried out using proportional cylindrical specimens with a 
gauge diameter of 3.0 mm.

According to the data published in a number of investigations [12-18], the controlling 
factor in the evaluation of endurance in the creep testing of refractory alloys under the 
effect of cyclically varying loads and temperatures is (.like in static loading) the duration 
of loading the alloy at high temperatures and not the number of load cycles. In these investi- 
gations, the criterion used for evaluating the endurance of the refractory alloys in these 
conditions is represented by the total holding time at the maximum temperatures or stress 
cycles [12, 16], the holding time at certain equivalent temperatures and stress cycles [13-15], 
or by the total time during which the material is stressed at temperatures higher than the 
recrystallization temperature [17, 18]. 
Some of the authors assume that the deformation and fracture of the alloys are determined 
mainly by creep which takes place during loading of the specimen at the maximum temperature 
and cycle stress. However, these authors do not take into account the creep processes occur- 
ring at the minimum and intermediate cycle stresses and temperatures, which can often exert 
(depending on the temperature and force loading parameters) a strong effect on the creep and 
long-term strength characteristics of the tested material. On the other hand, other authors 
overestimate the effect of holding at the minimum and intermediate cycle stresses and tempera- 
tures. 
We believe that in developing and selecting the criteria for evaluating the endurance 
of the refractory metals and alloys in the conditions of isothermal and nonisothermal creep 
it is essential to take into account the parameters of temperature--force loading and, in 
particular, the level and amplitude of the cNclic stresses and the extent and duration of 
loading above a specific stress level at which creep starts to take place (e.g., yield 
stress). 
Taking this into account, it is assumed that the process of deformation and fracture of 
the material is determined, regardless of the conditions of nonstationary force and thermal 
loading, mainly by creep which takes place when the specimen is subjected to the load equal 
to or higher than the yield stress. The time during which the material of the specimen is

CONCLUSIONS 
i. The relationships governing the deformation and rupture of the 4604 molybdenum-based 
polycrystalline alloy in the conditions of isothermal and nonisothermal cycle creep were 
examined and the corresponding characteristics of the long-term strength and creep of the 
material in these conditions were determined. 
2. The constant sign cyclic loading of the polycrystalline Mo alloy in isothermal and 
nonisothermal conditions in the given stress and endurance ranges reduces the ductility and 
creep rate and increases the endurance in comparison with these characteristics in the condi- 
tions of static isothermal creep. 
3. Like in static loading, the factor which controls the fracture process in testing 
the 4604 molybdenum alloy in the conditions with cyclically varying stresses is (regardless 
of the form of the cycle) the duration of stressing the material at high temperatures and not 
the number of load cycles. 
4. The load-carrying capacity of the specimens of the refractory materials in the cyclic 
creep conditions with complex force and thermal loading was evaluated using the criterion of 
the duration of stressing the specimen with stresses higher than yield stress.

It was shown in [i] that in the conditions of isothermal high-temperature creep in the 
uniaxial tensile loading of molybdenum single crystals oriented in the direction of single 
slip, the transition to the steady-state stage is accompanied by locali~ation of the strain. 
Localized strain regions have the form of relief bands distributed in the direction normal 
to that of the slip relief. The metallographic and x-ray diffraction examination of the 
structure of tested crystals indicated the presence of regions characterized by different 
etchability, deformation, and substructure. The investigations have shown that the localiza- 
tion of the strain is caused by plastic rotations leading to fragmentation and a gradual in- 
crease in the angle of misorientation of the fragments. This results in cracking and frac- 
ture of the crystal along the fragment boundaries. 
Fragmentation in the neck of the specimen in the low-temperature active tensile loading 
of polycrystalline nickel and molybdenum was reported in [2, 3]. Data have been published 
according to which the nature of the structure in microvolumes in plastic deformation in a 
nonuniform external field, e.g., in rolling [4, 5], differs. The authors of these reports 
also detected the presence of regions with elongated structural constituents (fragments) as 
the characteristic structure of the strain bands. 
In the work described here, the authors examine changes in the structure taking place 
during the high-temperature loading of the 4604 molybdenum alloy in the conditions described 
and analyzed in Report i.
