Effects of discontinuour coarsening of lamellae on 
creep strength of fully lamellar TiAl alloys 
R. Yamamoto,” K. Mizoguchi, G. Wegmann & K. Maruyamat 
Department of Materials Science, Graduate School of Engineering, Tohoku University, Aoba-yama 02, Sendai 980-8579, Japan 
(Received 13 April 1998; revised version received 6 May 1998; accepted 7 May 1998) 
High temperature creep of a binary Ti42mol%Al alloy with fully lamellar 
structure was studied to examine effects of lamellar spacing on creep strength. 
Strain hardening is more significant in a finer lamellar material, resulting in 
higher creep strength at high stresses. Discontinuous coarsening of lamellae takes 
place during creep, and is more substantial in the finer lamellar material at low 
stresses. Because of the microstructural degradation, the strengthening by fine 
lamellae diminishes at low stresses. Some specimens were annealed at high tem- 
peratures to finish the discontinuous coarsening prior to creep testing. In these 
specimens, the strengthening by fine lamellae becomes effective even at low 
stresses. 0 1998 Elsevier Science Limited. All rights reserved 
Key words: A. titanium aluminide, B. high temperature creep, C. lamellar struc- 
ture, D. lamellar spacing, E. discontinuous coarsening.

2 EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE 
The material used was a binary Ti--42mol%Al 
alloy having equal volume of y and a2 phases. The 
alloy was arc-melted in a vacuum, and then hot 
extruded at 1473 K to 80% reduction of area. 
Compression creep specimens, 2x2 mm in cross 
section and 3 mm in height, were machined, so that 
their stress axis was parallel to the extruded direc- 
tion. All the specimens were annealed in a vacuum 
at 1500 K (a single phase regime) for 600 s, fol- 
lowed by controlled cooling. All of the specimens 
have a fully lamellar structure with the same grain 
size of 180pm. The feathery structure reported by 
Herrouin et ~1.~ was not formed in the present 
alloy. Specimens having three different lamellar 
spacings are referred to as Sp.A, B and C, here- 
after. Sp.A, with 0.1 pm lamellar spacing and flat 
grain boundaries, was furnace cooled at an initial

3.2 Cause of lamellar spacing dependence of creep 
rate 
Figure 2 shows representative creep curves of the 
three different lamellar structures. Change in creep 
rate during each creep test is drawn as a function 
of test duration. The decrease of creep rate in the 
primary creep stage is due to strain hardening, and 
becomes more significant with the decreasing 
lamellar spacing. Several types of microstructural 
degradation take place during creep, resulting in 
creep acceleration in the tertiary creep stage. The 
degradation is characterized by the slope dlns/dt in 
the accelerating stage. The degradation occurs 
throughout the whole creep stages together with 
the strain hardening, and minimum creep rate is 
attained by dynamic balance between the strain 
hardening and the microstructural degradation.8 
The slope of acceleration is insensitive to lamellar 
spacing at high stresses (see Fig. 2(a)). Therefore, 
minimum creep rate is primarily determined by the 
strain hardening, and decreases with decreasing 
lamellar spacing. 
The strain hardening is always significant in the 
fine lamellar structure. At low stresses, however, 
the creep acceleration becomes more substantial as 
lamellar spacing decreases (see Fig. 2(b)). This 
adverse effect of fine lamellae on creep acceleration

3.3 Stability of lamellar structure 
Two types of microstructural degradation were 
evident during creep of the Ti-42mol%Al alloy. 
One is void formation at grain boundaries. At 
stresses below lOOMPa, however, voids were not 
formed in any specimen. Therefore, the void for- 
mation cannot explain the differences in slopes of 
tertiary creep acceleration among the three speci- 
mens in Fig. 2(b). The other microstructural 
degradation is changes of lamellar structure. At 
high stresses spheroidization of lamellae occurred 
during creep, while discontinuous coarsening9 of 
lamellae took place at low stresses. The spher- 
oidization (nucleation type dynamic recrystalliza- 
tion (DRX)) has been reported by other 
researchers,10,’ 1 and is known to increase creep rate 
of fully lamellar TiAl alloys.1° The discontinuous 
coarsening (bulging type DRX) is accompanied by 
migration of interphase and/or grain boundaries. 
The migrating boundaries sweep away dislocations 
which caused the strain hardening in the primary 
creep, resulting in the softening during the tertiary 
creep. Area fraction of DRX region (nucle- 
ation + bulging types of DRX) is given in Fig. 3 as 
a function of creep stress. The fraction is small in 
all the specimens at high stresses, but increases 
with decreasing lamellar spacing at low stresses.

vented in order to recover the strengthening by fine 
lamellar structure at low stresses. Some specimens 
of Sp.A (lamellar spacing 2 = 0.1 pm) were sub- 
jected to stabilization at higher temperatures to 
finish DC prior to creep testing. The area fractions 
of DRX region formed during creep of Sp.Al and 
A2 are included in Fig. 3, indicating that the sta-

curves and creep rate-time curves of the stabilized 
specimens are compared in Fig. 4 with those of 
non-stabilized Sp.A. Creep acceleration in the ter- 
tiary creep stage, namely dlns/dt decreases with 
increasing area fraction of prior DC region,
