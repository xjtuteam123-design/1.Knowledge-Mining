[12]
In the case of Ni-based alloys, bulk metallic glasses were
prepared in the complex alloy systems, such as Ni-Nb-Cr-
Mo-P-B,
[13] Ni-Ti-Zr-(Si,Sn),[14] Ni-Nb-Ti-Zr-Co-Cu,[15] Ni-Nb-
Ti-Zr-Si-Sn,[16] Ni-Nb-Sn,[17] Ni-Cu-Ti-Zr-Al,[18] etc. Recently,
up to 5 mm thick samples have been fabricated in the Ni-Cu-
Ti-Zr-Al and Ni-Nb-Ti-Zr-Si-Sn systems, respectively, which
is the critical casting dimension for Ni-based BMGs at pres-

amorphous in a very wide composition range of 40 ∼ 60 at.%
Nb by rapid solidification process a few decades ago.
[19] Also,
Ni-Nb binary alloys were known as one of the formers with
the highest glass-forming ability during the period of the tra-
ditional amorphous alloys. Up to now, no bulk metallic
glasses prepared in that system directly by mould casting
method were reported except that bulk amorphous samples
up to 2 mm thick were prepared by quenching of highly un-
dercooled Ni
59.5Nb40.5 melts.[20] In this letter, we report the
formation and properties of the binary Ni-Nb bulk metallic
glasses. 1.5 mm diameter fully amorphous rods are fabricated
in Ni
61.5Nb38.5 alloy by copper mould injection casting meth-
od. The samples show no evidence for the long-range atomic
ordering in either X-ray diffraction or transmission electron
microscopy (TEM), and retain a distinct glass transition and a
wide supercooled liquid region. 1.5 mm-diameter fully me-
tallic glassy samples possess much high fracture strength of
3.4 GPa and still a plastic strain of about 2 % in the uniaxial
compression tests at room temperature.

In rapid solidification, amorphous alloys are formed by
bypassing the nucleation and growth of the crystalline phases
while cooling through the temperature interval from the li-
quidus temperature T
l (below which crystallization is ther-
modynamically possible) to the glass transition temperature
Tg (below which the melt is frozen into the solid). Thus, the
identification of the crystalline phases in the amorphous +
crystalline samples is helpful to understand why the binary
Ni-Nb system alloy is capable to be cast into bulk metallic
glasses. Based on a match the data in the powder diffraction
files,
[21] the crystalline phases are analyzed as a mixture of an
orthorhombic phase Ni 3Nb with the lattice constant of
a=5.114 Å, b=4.244 Å, c=4.536 Å, and a rhombohedral phase
NiNb with the lattice constant of a=4.900 Å, c=26.64 Å, and
some unknown phases, shown in Figure 2. Those three crys-
talline phases are the competitors against glass formation
during cooling. Configurationally, the orthorhombic Ni
3Nb
and rhombohedral NiNb belong to the class of topologically
close-packed polytetrahedral structures.

In a summary, refractory Ni-based bulk metallic glasses
are prepared in the binary Ni-Nb system. Ni
61.5Nb38.5 alloy
exhibits the highest glass-forming ability, corresponding to
the biggest supercooled liquid region, amongst the
Ni
59.5+0.5xNb40.5–0.5x (x=1,2,3,4,5) at.% alloys, which can be
cast into the 1.5 mm diameter fully amorphous rods by using
copper mold injection casting method. The bulk metallic
glassy sample possesses high glass transition temperature
894 K, high on-set crystallization temperature 932 K and
Vickers hardness (about 7.8 GPa). Room temperature uniaxial
compression tests yield about 3.4 GPa for the high fracture
strength and about 2 % for compressive plastic strain. As
mentioned at the beginning, the successful preparation of the
binary Ni-Nb bulk metallic glass with excellent mechanical
properties is of great interest to prepare large dimension Ni-
based bulk metallic glasses as structural materials.

Experimental
Alloy ingots of nominal composition Ni 59.5+0.5xNb40.5-0.5x (x=1,2,3,4,5) at.%
near the eutectic consisting of Ni 3Nb and Ni 6Nb7 in the equilibrium phase dia-
gram were prepared by arc melting a mixture of ultrasonically cleansed Ni and
Nb with a purity of above 99.9 % on a water-cooled copper hearth under Ti-get-
tered high purity argon atmosphere. The chemical homogeneity was obtained
by repeated melting at least four times. The ingots were then remelted under
high vacuum in a quartz tube by using induction heating coil and injected
through a nozzle with 0.5 ∼ 1 mm in diameter into the copper mould with a
cavity of 1.5 mm and 2 mm diameter. Ribbon samples with about 60 lmi n
thickness were prepared by single-roller melt spinning method. The structure
was characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Philips PW1050, Cu-K a radia-
tion), transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEOL2010). Thin foil specimens
for transmission electron microscopy were prepared by twin-jet polishing
method using a HClO 4–C2H5OH solution (volume ratio: 1:10) at about -30 °C.
The thermal properties with glass transition, crystallization and melting behav-
iors were studied by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC, Netzsch DSC
404C). Mechanical properties were measured with the samples of 1.5 mm di-
ameter and 3 mm length on a servo-hydraulic materials testing system (MTS
810). To perform compression tests under a constant strain rate of 2 × 10 –4 s–1,a
MTS strain gauge was used. Vickers hardness of the amorphous samples was
obtained using a MVK-H3 microhardness tester under a loading of 1000 g.

between 300 and 30 nm), and novel nanolithography tech-

lar surface pattern formation is achieved by self-organization
of random nanoextrusions caused by the external periodic
mechanical influence (constrained cyclic mechanical tension).
