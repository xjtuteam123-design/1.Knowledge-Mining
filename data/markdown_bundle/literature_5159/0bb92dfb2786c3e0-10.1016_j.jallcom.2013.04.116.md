Abstract 
An investigation of the effect of the Si/B ratio on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of high temperature Mo -Si-B a lloys, doped with 0.9wt% 
lanthanum oxide(La 2O3), is presented. Mechanical properties are measured at room 
temperature for three α-Mo-Mo3Si-Mo5SiB2 containing alloys produced by powder 
metallurgy processing. It is found that the alloys with Si/B ratios of 2 .4 and 1.4 
possess a continuous α-Mo matrix with homogeneously distributed intermetallic 
particles while the alloy with the Si/B ratio of 0.7 is a homogeneous mix of α-Mo, 
Mo3Si and Mo5SiB2 phases without continuous ones obsevered. Measurements on 
mechanical properties show that the flexural and compression strength , and fracture 
toughness of Mo-Si-B alloys were increased with the Si/B ratio raised. The 
continuous α-Mo phase with high volume fraction is beneficial for ameliorating the 
room temperature strength and fracture toughness. 
Key words: Mo-Si-B alloys; lanthanum oxide; microstructure; mechanical property 
1.Introduction 
Mo-Si-B alloys consisting of a Mo solid solution (Mo ss) phase and intermetallic

## Page 3

phases Mo 3Si and Mo 5SiB2 (T2) are a leading candidate for the next generation of a 
jet turbine engine blades and have the potential to raise the operating temperature by 
300-400℃, which would dramatically increase power and efficiency[1-3]. 
Schneibel and Kruzic[4,5] have pointed out that optimal room temperature damage 
tolerance is afforded by coarse, continuous α-Mo grains. In contrast, the three -phase 
alloys with very small discontinuous grains are best for oxidation resistance[6], leading 
to competing desirable microstructural morphologies. The key to solve this difficulty 
is improving the ductility of α-Mo phase that less α-Mo would be required to obtain 
satisfied fracture toughness. It is well known that the ductility of α-Mo phase can be 
improved by reducing its grain size . Thus, one of the approaches to ductilize Mo ss in 
our early researches[7-9] is to add La2O3 particles, a thermodynamically stable oxide. It 
is found that the molybdenum alloys doped with lanthanum oxide possess higher 
strength and fracture toughness than pure molybdenum [10]. The strengthening and 
toughening mechanisms related to the La 2O3 particles involve refining grains, 
dispersion strengthening and particle toughening. 
The presence of the T2 phase in Mo-Si-B alloys would improve the oxidation 
resistance by promoting the formation of a protective borosilicate glass layer [11,12]. 
However, the oxidation resisitance is apparently influenced by a strong alloy 
composition sensitivity since variations in alloy compositions will change 
solidification pathways that can result in a difference in the microstructure as well as 
the relative amou nts of co -existing phases[11,13]. Supatarawanich et al. [14] studied the 
effect of Si/B ra tio on the o xidation behavior of multiphase Mo -Si-B alloys . The

## Page 4

oxidation resistance was improved with the volume fraction of the T2 phase increased 
at 800℃. Conversely, the near eutectic alloy with a Si/B=1 was found to have the best 
oxidation resistance at 1300 ℃ which indicated the Mo3Si phase provides a source of 
silicon for controlling the composition of the glass scale at 1300 ℃. However, few 
effects of Si/B ratio on the mechanical propertise of these alloys are known up to now. 
In this paper, 0.9 wt% La 2O3 doped Mo-Si-B alloys with different Si to B ratio were 
prepared by powder metallurgy method, and the effects of the Si/B ratio on the 
microstructures and mechanical properties were investigated. Additionally, the 
strengthening and toughening mechanisms were also discussed on the basis of present 
results. 
2.Experimental 
In order to assess the effect of the Si to B ratio, Mo-Si-B alloys with Si/B ratios 
of 2.4, 1.4 and 0.7 were prepared by powder metallurgical processing corresponding 
to the nomial compositions of Mo-12Si-5B, Mo-12Si-8.5B and Mo -12Si-17B alloys, 
respectively, shown in Fig. 1. Powders of elemental Mo (2-3.5m), Si(<5m) and 
B(≤1.0m) of 99.95wt%, 99.99wt% and 99.95wt% purity, respectively, were mixed. 
About 0.9wt% La 2O3(80-100nm) were added. MA was carried out for 22h under a 
protective(argon) atmosphere in a planetary ball mill(Retsch PM 400) with a speed of 
300rpm and a powder to ball weigh t ratio of 1:10. After milling, the powders were 
hot-pressed at 50MPa and sintered in vacuum at 1600℃ for 3h. 
Microstructures of the samples w ere characterized by optical metallography 
(GX71 of OLYMPUS) and scanning electron microscopy (JSM-6700). Samples were

## Page 5

polished and etched with Murakami ’s etch(an aqueous solution of potassium 
ferricyanide and sodiu m hydroxide). Phases were identified by X -ray 
diffraction(XRD-7000S) with Cu Kα radiation. 
Flexure specimens with a cross section of 3×4mm2 and a span of 20mm were 
electro-discharge machined, and subsequently tested in a universal material testing 
machine (Instron 1185) in a three -point bend fixture with the cross-head speed of 
0.5mm/min at ambient temperature in the air. Uniaxial compression test were carried 
out in the air using a computer-controlled machine (HT-2402) that yielded a nominal 
strain rate o f 2.5 ×10-4s-1 at room temperature. The ends of the specimens 
(5mm×5mm×10mm) were parallel to each other. Fracture toughness values were 
determined by three-point bend tests using specimens that were 3mm wide and 4mm 
high with a notch tip depth of 2mm at a crosshead speed of 0.05mm/min. Pior to 
testing, the planes of crack growth of the samples were ground and polished . A crack 
initiated at the tip of the notch and propagated gradually throug h the specimen until 
fracture occurred during testing. Fracture toughness values were determined on basis 
of an energy criterion by integrating the load-displacement curves. 
3.Results and disscusion 
3.1 Phase composition and microstructure 
 Fig. 2 depicts the XRD patterns of the alloys with Si/B ratios of 2.4, 1.4 and 0.7. 
It shows that all specimens are consisted of α-Mo, Mo3Si and Mo5SiB2 phases, which 
are in accord with the isothermal three -phase section of the Mo -rich portion of the 
Mo-Si-B phase diagram [16]. As a common effect of decreasing Si/B ratio the

## Page 6

diffraction peaks of the T2 phase are strengthened whereas those of α-Mo and Mo3Si 
phases are weakened , indicating that the volume ratio of the T2 phase increases. 
Moreover, there is no peaks of other phases detected. 
The microstructures of the Mo-Si-B alloys are shown in Fig. 3 which illustrate 
that the powder metallurgy method creates fine and homogeneous microstructures 
over the range of compositions . Moreover, the presence of La2O3 particles located at 
grain boundaries can impede grain boundary movement, and t herefore stabilize a 
small grain size during powder metallurgical (PM) process. It also can be seen from 
the microstructures that a lloys with Si/B ratios of 2.4 and 1.4 possess a continuous 
α-Mo matrix with homogeneously distributed interm etallic particles, in contrast, a 
microstructure of homogeneous mixed distribution of fine α-Mo, Mo3Si and Mo5SiB2 
particles is gained for the alloy with the Si/B ratio of 0.7 without continuous phases 
observed. For the α-Mo-Mo3Si-Mo5SiB2 alloys, the variations in the Si/B ratio of the 
alloy composition will change the volume fractions of the α-Mo, Mo3Si and Mo5SiB2 
phases. Quantitative analyses of the α-Mo phase volume fraction showed that the 
α-Mo phase vol ume fraction decreased from 44.1 % to 20.8% with the Si/B ratio 
changed from 2.4 to 0.7, s o that the alloy with the Si/B ratio of 0.7 can not form the 
continuous α-Mo matrix as the volume fraction of α-Mo phase was very low. 
3.2 Mechanical properties 
3.2.1 Strength of Mo-Si-B alloys at room temperature 
The room temperature strength of the powder metallurgical Mo-Si-B alloys is 
listed in Table 1 which is deteriorated with the Si/B ratio declined . Fig. 4 shows

compression stress-strain curves for Mo-Si-B alloys at a strain rate of 2.5×10-4s-1 at 
room temperature. The compression behavior of the alloy with Si/B ratio of 2.4 and 
1.4 are similar. There is n o obvious yield point observed and the stress-strain curve 
character is deformation strengthening, as a resu lt the flow stress keep rising until the 
materials failed. Noticed that the behavior of the alloy with Si/B ratio of 0.7 is in 
accordance with the typical room-temperature compression curve for brittle materials, 
i.e. the sample failed without plastic defo rmation. Consequently, the fracture strength 
can be determined rather than yield strength for Mo-12Si-17B+La2O3 alloy(Si/B 
=0.7). 
 An examination of the fracture surface s of the room temperature three-point 
bending specimens of Mo-Si-B alloys that were failed in a completely brittle manner 
reveals that fracture mode is a mix of intergranular and transgranular (Fig. 5). The 
intergranular fracture is caused by the concentrations of impurities on the grain 
boundaries and the elastic incompatibility of adjacent particles which induce the crack 
initiation on the two-phase interfaces. In the case of the alloys with Si/B ratio of 2.4 
and 1.4, transgranular cleavage is noted in the α-Mo phase, as Fig. 5(b) and (d) shown, 
while the Mo3Si and Mo5SiB2 intermetallic phases exhibit a large percentage of 
intergranular fracture . The micrograph in Fig. 5(e) reveals the relatively flat 
featureless regions on the fracture surface of the alloys with Si/B ratio of 0.7 
correspond mostly to fracture of the Mo3Si and Mo5SiB2 intermetallic particles which 
prematurely failed caused by internal defects. 
At room temperature, the flexural strength of Mo-12Si-8.5B +La2O3 alloy in this

work is 1160.1MPa, which is higher than the 412MPa found for powder-metallurgical 
Mo-12Si-8.5B(at%) alloys [15]. The main reason for increment is that mechanical

boundaries and the dispersed La 2O3 particles. The room temperature strength of 
Mo-Si-B alloys is related to the matrix which deteriorated with the Si/B ratio 
decreased. In the case of the alloys with Si/B ratio of 0.7, the presence of numerous 
flaws in the intermetallic phases dictated the strength which caused the stress 
concentration and premature factured is often due to internal defects in the material. 
3.2.2 Fracture toughness 
 During fracture toughness testing at ambient temperature, all of samples failed 
immediately at the ons et of crack initiation . In this work, the fracture toughness 
values were nominated as K q owing to the overestimation of the fracture toughness 
KIC by chevron-notch in Choe and Ritchie ’s study[15]. The fracture toughness values 
were determined and shown in Fig. 6. It can be seen that alloys with Si/B ratios of 2.4 
and 1.4 have nearly identical fracture toughnesses which are higher than the alloy 
with the Si/B ratio of 0.7 . It is generally believed that a continuous α-Mo matrix is 
favourable for room temperature damage tolerance by crack trapping which will be 
discussed in the following text. Moreover, ductile phase toughening increases with the 
volume fraction and size of the toughening phase increase[15,16]. 
The SE M micrographs of room temperature fracture surfaces of the Mo-Si-B 
alloys (Fig. 7) shows the mixed-mode fracture to be the failure mechanism, and some 
residual cavities have either not been completely eliminated by the hot -pressing, or

## Page 9

been caused by intermetallic particles pulled-out. The differences in the elastic 
modulus and yield strength of the α-Mo, Mo3Si and Mo 5SiB2 phase are responsible 
for the mixed -mode failure. Consequently, the crack extending from one phase to 
another would tend to follow the easiest path owing to sharp changes in the stress 
intensity factor [17]. The particlelike features are combined with the smaller α-Mo 
zones because the crack tends to circumvent smaller spherical particles and ultimately 
bursts through the larger ones. 
A large volume fraction o f the continuous α-Mo phase is benefit for achieving 
high fracture toughnes s values . It is concluded that crack trapping and bridging by 
α-Mo phase were identified as the toughening mechanisms responsible for this 
behavior. When the main crack propagates, the crack tip reaches the regions of higher 
toughness and consequently is a rrested. The crack continues to extend with the load 
increased, and bows out between the pinning particles which results in the increase of 
stress intensity at the pinning points. Once the stress intensit y surpasses the fracture 
toughness, the crack will b urst through. It is an important mechanism to improve the 
toughness of Mo -Si-B alloys intrinsically [18]. According to Kruzic’s reseach [5], the 
ductility of the α-Mo phase is adequately high to remain intact in the crack wake, it 
can act extrinsically as crack bridges. In addition, the high degree of plastic constraint 
imposed on the α-Mo particles by the surrounding intermetallic Mo3Si/Mo5SiB2 
matrix in the alloy with Si/B ratio of 0.7 limits the ductility of the α-Mo phase and 
hence lowers the degree of ductile -phase bridging. Thus, t he fracture toughness is 
improved for the alloy with Si/B ratio of 2.4 because higher effectiveness of the crack

## Page 10

trapping and bridging mechanisms when there is a continuous α-Mo matrix, since the 
crack can not avoid the relatively ductile α-Mo phase. 
4.Conclusions 
The microstructure and mechanical properties of three -phase (α-Mo+Mo3Si+T2) 
Mo-Si-B alloys doped with 0.9wt% lanthanum ox ide (La2O3) are examined as a 
function of the Si/B ratio (Si/B=2.4, 1.4 and 0.7). The alloys were prepared by powder 
metallurgical processing. No continuous matrix was clearly defined in the alloy with 
Si/B ratios of 0.7 but fine and homogeneously mixed α-Mo, Mo3Si and Mo5SiB2 
phases. The alloys with Si/B ratios of 2.4 and 1.4, however, possess a continuous 
α-Mo matrix. The strength and fracture toughnes of Mo-Si-B alloys degraded with the 
Si/B ratio decreased . The room temperature strength and high toughness deteriorate 
when a continuous α-Mo matrix is established instead of a microstructure of 
homogeneously mixed α-Mo, Mo3Si and Mo5SiB2 phases with a high volume fraction 
of intermetallics.
