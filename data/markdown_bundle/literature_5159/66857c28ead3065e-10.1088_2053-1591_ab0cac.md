Abstract
Recrystallization phenomena in Mg-10Gd-3Y-2Ag-0.4Zr(GWQ1032K) alloy cold-rolled at 40%
cumulative depression were observed in specimens during annealing at different temperatures using
OM, TEM and Vickers hardness tester. Based on the cold-rolled magnesium alloy, we studied the
effect of annealing temperature and time on the microstructure and properties of the ultra-high
strength magnesium alloy. The results showed that a large number of defects occurred in the grain of
the alloy after rolling at room temperature, and the grain reﬁnement effect was not obvious and no
obvious dynamic precipitation. From 300°C to 400 °C, because the rare earth elements in cold rolled
GWQ1032K alloy would precipitate at low temperature in a short time, reducing the mechanical
properties of the alloy. When the sample was annealed at 450°C, the continuous annealing
recrystallization of GWQ1032K sheet is moved up and the grain growth was delayed. The grain
recrystallization occurred and the precipitation of rare earth elements decreased in the interior of
grains after annealed at 530°C for 20s. Meanwhile, the softening phenomenon of the alloy by
annealing was very obvious, and it further proved that the effect ofﬁne crystal reinforcing and
precipitation reinforcing of cold rolled magnesium alloys could be maximized by high temperature
rapid annealing treatment.
1. Introduction
Magnesium alloy is the lightest metallic structural material with highly speciﬁc strength, speciﬁc stiffness and
thermal conductivity[1]. It is known for its good performance, good damping and shock absorption, excellent
processing performance and easily to reuse and recycle[2, 3]. It has great potential in theﬁelds of automobiles,
aerospace, electronic component and otherﬁelds [4– 6]. Due to the close-packed hexagonal structure, magnesium
alloy can start with less slip at room temperature. As a result, the performance of plastic forming at room
temperature is very poor. Plastic processing at high temperature not only wastes energy and pollutes the
environment, but also leads to a large number of high temperature processing defects and signiﬁcantly reduces the
surface quality of the material. The surface of hot-rolled and warm-rolled sheet is easy to be oxidized, especially
when the thickness of the sheet is thin. The wave will rise, and the sheet with good shape and surface quality cannot
be obtained. Therefore, the cold rolling process of magnesium alloy is the main direction of research and
development of magnesium alloy rolling technology in the future. The cold rolling ability of magnesium alloy
plates mainly depends on alloy composition, rolling process and microstructure of rolled sheet. Many scientists
tried to increase the cold rolling ability of magnesium alloy by adding rare earth elements[7, 8].
Mg-Gd-Y alloys exhibited high strength, high heat-resistant and high hardness properties, and extensive
researches have been carried on these series of Mg alloys. Some progresses regarding reﬁning this series of alloy
have been made by adding a small amount of Zr, Mn, Zn, Ag and other elements[9– 12]. How to improve the

2. Experimental
Raw materials for Mg-10Gd-3Y-2Ag(GWQ1032K) alloy smelting: pure Mg, pure Ag, Mg-25Y, Mg-30Gd,
Mg-25Zr, an alloy block with a planned size of 30 mm×20 mm×2 mm is cut form the ingot. According to
the phase diagram of Mg-Gd binary and Mg-Y-Gd alloy, the solid solution temperature is selected at 500°C for
12 h for Mg-Gd-Y alloy. The solution treatment process is conducted in an air resistance furnace andﬂame-
retarded by argon gas, and all samples directly move from furnace into cold water which quenches them,
followed by rolling. The amount of deformation of the alloy during rolling is 5%. After 8 passes rolling, the
thickness of the alloy is 1.6 mm, that is, the cumulative deformation is 40%. At this time, the boundary position
of the alloy is not signiﬁcantly cracked. In order to study the effect of annealing treatment on the microstructure
and mechanical properties of the alloy, the alloy after rolling was placed in an electric resistance furnace at
300 °C– 530 °C for annealing treatment, and the annealing treatment time was between 0 min and 30 min.
The microstructures were observed by an optical microscope(GX71, OLYMPUS, Tokyo, Japan). The
transmission electron microscopy(JEM-2100, JEOL, Tokyo, Japan) was used to analyze the microstructure of
GWQ1032K alloy with as-cast alloy, conventional heat treatment alloy and electric pulse aging after solid
solution alloy.

3.2. Effect of annealing process on microstructure of GWQ1032K magnesium alloy
During rolling, severe deformation occurs in the shear deformation band of magnesium alloy, resulting in high
deformation energy storage in the alloy. At the same time, the formation of shear band divides the original grains
into many ﬁne sub-grains, which provides energy and microstructure preparation for the recrystallization of
experimental alloy. The recrystallized grain is easy to nucleate at the original grain boundary, near the shear
deformation zone, in the twin layer or at the twin boundary.
As shown inﬁgure 2, the rolled alloy was annealed at 300°C, 350 °C, 400 °C and 450 °C for 1800s, and then
the microstructure was observed. Figure2(a) is a metallographic picture of cool rolled sheet annealed at 300°C.
Annealing at this temperature does not cause recrystallization of the rolled sheet, and the twin crystal remains in
the form ofﬂakes. The rolled sheet is mainly composed of deformed structure. Besides, there are obvious
deformation twins inside the large grains. In addition, the rare earth phase in the alloy is conspicuously
precipitated. A small amount of new grains in the annealed sample appeared only at the intersection of some
twin boundaries. The small portion of the rolled sheet which has been annealed at 350°C has recrystallized, and
the recrystallized grains are gathered into a strip shape, as shown inﬁgure 2(b). Most of the twins remainﬂaky
and no obvious recrystallization was observed, the recrystallized grains mainly appear in the concentrated areas
of granular and in the vicinity of the grain boundaries. These areas have signiﬁcant stress concentration and
sufﬁcient strain storage energy, which provides a driving force for static recrystallization[15]. Recrystallization

twins. Part of the unidirectional rolled sheet annealed at 400°C has undergone recrystallization, as shown in

4. Conclusions
1. The room temperature deformation can inhibit the dynamic precipitation. When the annealing time is
short and the temperature is low, the annealing and recrystallization ability is weak, and the precipitation of
rare earth elements exists in the crystal grains. Annealing at solution temperature, the a recrystallization
occurred and precipitation was inhibited.
2. The optimum annealing process was 530 °C for 20 s. Under such condition, the dynamic precipitation can
be effectively suppressed, and the effects ofﬁne grain strengthening and precipitation strengthening are
maximized.
3. When the alloy is subjected to a short-time annealing treatment at a high temperature, there were a few
precipitations in the matrix, which can be ascribed to the short time for the elements to migrate to the grain
boundary.
