ABSTRACT 14 
Quaternary AlCoCrNi alloy was designed by removing the heavy constituent of Fe from the dual-phase 15 
AlCoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy to achieve low densit y with good mechanical properties. The AlCoCrNi all oy 16 
exhibited a nano-scale dual-phase structure consist ed of Cr-rich A2 and Ni(Co)-Al-rich B2 phases with a high 17 
degree of coherence in both dendritic and interdend ritic regions. In particular, the Ni(Co)-Al-rich B2 phase 18 
revealed the non-stoichiometric composition between the Ni and the Al, which deviated with the Ni-Al-r ich B2 19 
phase with a stoichiometric composition in the prev ious AlCoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy. The chemical 20 
evolution in the constituent phases strongly affect ed the mechanical properties of the dual-phase high -entropy 21 
alloy. Based on these microstructural features of t he AlCoCrNi alloy, the mechanical properties were 22 
systematically investigated at wide temperature ranges. 23

1. Introduction 28 
The high-entropy concept has been recognized as a n ew strategy for the alloy design 29 
method to break the limits regarding the physical/m echanical properties of the conventional 30 
alloy systems with one or two principal elements [1 ,2]. High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are 31 
usually defined as the alloys consisting of multi-p rincipal elements, which should have a 32 
concentration between 5 and 35 at.% in order to ach ieve significantly high mixing entropies 33 
in liquid or random solid solution states [3,4]. Fr om multi-principal elements, the HEAs have 34 
possessed interesting and unique properties, inclu ding high strength, large macroscopic 35 
plasticity, good wear resistance, remarkable mechan ical properties at a high and low 36 
temperature as well as a valuable oxidation resista nce [5-12]. Among the HEAs, some alloys 37 
are solidified into a single phase alloy from the distinct alloy design concept. A CoCrFeMnNi 38 
alloy first reported by Cantor et al. is one of the most studied simple phase HEAs with a face-39 
centered cubic (fcc) solid solution, which is stabi lized by the significant high entropy of 40 
mixing in spite of containing multiple elements wit h different crystal structures [13]. The 41 
simple solid solution fcc phase in the CoCrFeMnNi H EA can be introduced under a slow 42 
solidification process by obstructing formation of the equilibrium σ phase by kinetic [14], 43 
whereas the σ phase also can precipitate under rapid solidificat ion process on additive 44 
manufacturing such as selected laser melting (SLM), which induces the favoring phase 45 
precipitation by providing a large number of diffus ion paths, such as grain boundaries, by 46 
formation of ultrafine-grains and a number of dislo cations [14]. Nevertheless, the single-47 
phase HEA with large grain sizes exhibits much impr oved cryogenic and high-temperature 48 
mechanical properties than that of conventional all oys due to the generation of beneficial 49 
deformation twining by reducing the twinning stress [9,15]. Moreover, the refractory 50 
NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV HEAs with a body-centered cubi c (bcc) single solid solution 51

2. Experimental procedures 112 
The master ingots of quaternary equi-atomic Al 25 Co 25 Cr 25 Ni 25 HEA were fabricated 113 
using an arc melting with high purity Al, Co, Co an d Ni elements of 99.95 at.% under a high 114 
purity Ar gas atmosphere (99.99%). The ingots were remelted at least five times to ensure 115 
chemical homogeneity. As-cast rod samples were prep ared using suction casting into a 116 
cylindrical rod-shaped copper mold with diameters o f 3 mm and 50 mm in length. The phase 117 
identification of the as-cast sample was performed using X-ray diffraction (XRD, D2 phaser, 118 
Bruker) with Co K α radiation ( λ = 1.79026 Å) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, 119 
Tecnai-F20) with energy-dispersive spectrometry (ED S). Thin film specimens for TEM 120 
analysis were prepared by ion milling with liquid n itrogen cooling. The cross-sectional 121 
microstructure of the as-cast sample was investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, 122 
JEOL JSM-6390; JEOL) with energy-dispersive spectro metry. The volume fraction of 123 
constituent phases in the as-cast sample was estima ted using a pixel analysis of the SEM 124 
micrographs obtained from the cross-sectional area and the TEM high-angle annular dark 125 
field (HAADF) images of the as-cast sample. The mechanical properties of the as-cast sample 126

4. Conclusions 277 
In the present work, the microstructural features a nd the related mechanical 278 
properties of the AlCoCrNi HEA designed by modulati ng the alloy component from the 279 
AlCoCrFeNi HEA were systematically investigated. Th e detailed investigation on the 280 
microstructural features of the alloy revealed that the nano-scale Cr-rich A2 and Ni(Co)-Al-281 
rich B2 dual-phase structure with a high degree of coherence were formed in both the micro-282 
scaled DR and ID regions. The Ni(Co)-Al-rich B2 pha se in both regions revealed the non-283 
stoichiometric atomic ratio between the Ni and Al e lements, which deviated with that of the 284 
Ni-Al-rich B2 phase in the previous AlCoCrFeNi HEA. From the modest change in the 285 
chemical composition of the Ni(Co)-Al-rich B2 phase and the high degree of coherence 286 
between the constituent phases, the AlCoCrNi HEA wi th a lower density compared with the 287 
AlCoCrFeNi HEA exhibited a highly improved yield st rength and the remarkable SYS 288 
without a trade-off between the strength and the ductility at room temperature. Moreover, The 289 
AlCoCrNi HEA also represented superior strength at high temperatures up to 873 K, which 290 
was much higher than that of the commercial Ni-base d superalloys. Based on these results, it 291 
is believed that tailoring the chemical composition or the atomic ratio on the constituent 292 
phases through the alloy designing can act as an ef fective way to improve the mechanical 293 
properties of the dual-phase HEAs. Furthermore, the development of the AlCoCrNi HEA 294 
with remarkable mechanical properties at wide tempe rature ranges can contribute to 295 
extending the potential of dual-phase HEAs for structural applications. 296
