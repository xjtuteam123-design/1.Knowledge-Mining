abstract
Effects of temperature and strain rate on the tensile properties of Cu-Cr-Zr-Ti alloy in annealed condition
were investigated by varying the initial strain rates from 10 /C0 4 s/C0 1 to 10 /C0 1 s/C0 1 at RT, 300 °C, 450 °C,
600 °C and 700 °C. The yield strength (YS) and ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of the alloy increased with
an increase in strain rate at RT and 300 °C, whereas at 450 °C, the material exhibited the highest YS and
UTS at the lowest strain rate of 10 /C0 4 s/C0 1.A t6 0 0 °C, the YS and UTS of the alloy increased with an
increase in strain rate and showed the highest YS compared to all the temperatures tested. The alloy
showed serrated yielding at 300 °C and at an initial strain rate of 10 /C0 4 s/C0 1 whereas at 450 °C, serrated
yielding was observed at 10 /C0 3 s/C0 1 and 10 /C0 4 s/C0 1 strain rates. Microstructural observations showed the
presence of creep cavities at 450 °C and strain rate of 10 /C0 4 s/C0 1. The creep cavities were present at higher
strain rates as the test temperature is increased. Fractography indicates that the fracture is pre-
dominantly dimple fracture at RT and 300 °C and intergranular cracks were observed at 600 °C and
700 °C.

1. Introduction
Regeneratively cooled rocket engine thrust chamber consists of
high conductivity copper alloy inner shell, which is exposed to
high temperatures and an outer shell made of superalloy or steel,
which acts as reinforcement. The inner and outer shells are joined
by brazing or electroforming [1]. The copper inner shell is actively
cooled by passing liquid hydrogen or propellants through the
cooling channels so that the average temperature is maintained in
the range of 450 °Ct o6 0 0 °C during service [2]. Even though pure
copper possesses high thermal conductivity, it cannot retain
strength at elevated temperatures. Different copper based alloys
were developed such as Cu-Cr-Nb [3], NARloy – Z [4] Cu-Cr-Zr [5]
to retain high strength at elevated temperature without con-
siderably reducing the thermal conductivity. Among these alloys,
owing to high ductility, Cu-Cr-Zr alloy [3] can be easily fabricated
into nozzle shape using ﬂow forming. Several researchers studied
the mechanical properties of Cu-Cr-Zr alloy subjected to different
heat treatments [6–10]. A small quantity of titanium is added to
Cu-Cr-Zr alloys to reduce sulphur embrittlement [1 1–13]. A new
Cu-Cr-Zr-Ti alloy was proposed and the effect of heat treatment
and simulated brazing cycle was studied earlier [14,15].
During the engine operation, owing to high stresses at elevated
temperature, the cooling channels are subjected to deformation
beyond yield point [16]. They exhibit progressive thinning and
bulging during each ﬁring cycle. Apart from excessive deformation,
the rate of deformation also changes in the cooling channel wall
[17]. In order to predict the exact deformation during service, it is
important to evaluate the tensile properties at different tempera-
tures and strain rates. Apart from structural analysis, such prop-
erties are essential for processing the alloy at elevated tempera-
ture. So the present study was undertaken to understand the
tensile behaviour of Cu-Cr-Zr-Ti at different temperatures and
strain rates.

3. Results and discussion

ening is observed due to dislocation-dislocation interaction and
hence the UTS increases with an increase in strain rate. The same
is also evident from the value of strain hardening exponent, which
is systematically increasing with an increase in strain rate and is
shown in Fig. 5 .

4. Conclusions
Based on the results obtained in the present study on the
tensile properties of Cu-Cr-Zr-Ti alloy in annealed condition at
different temperatures (25 °C (RT), 300 °C, 450 °C, 600 °C and
700 °C) and at different initial strain rates (10
/C0 4 s/C0 1,
10/C0 3 s/C0 1,10/C0 2 s/C0 1and 10 /C0 1 s/C0 1), the following conclusions are
drawn.
At RT (25 °C) and 300 °C, YS and UTS of the material increase
with an increase in strain rate.
At 450 °C, the material shows the highest strength at 10 /C0 4 s/C0 1
strain rate. Precipitation of Cr rich particles during soaking and
testing is the main reason for the highest strength.
At 600 °C, material exhibits higher YS. As the strain rate is in-
creased, both YS and UTS increase. A probable reason for higher YS
is the formation of Cr rich precipitates during heating and soaking
of the specimen.
At 700 °C, the material exhibits the lowest UTS. Uniform elon-
gation is absent below 10
/C0 2 s/C0 1 strain rate.
Cavities are observed in the specimens tested at 450 °C and an
initial strain rate of 10 /C0 4 s/C0 1, indicating the activation of creep
phenomena. At higher temperatures, cavities were observed at a
higher strain rate of 10 /C0 3 s/C0 1 as well.
