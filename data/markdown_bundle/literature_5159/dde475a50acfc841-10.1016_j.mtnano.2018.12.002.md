ABSTRACT 
Similar to the conventional alloys, attaining excel lent combination of high strength 
and ductility is still challenging in the high-entr opy alloys (HEAs). In this work, a 
fully recrystallized ultrafine-grained (UFG) micros tructure was introduced into the 
single-phase CoCrFeMnNi HEA via cold rolling and an nealing process. The UFG 
HEA displays ultrahigh yield and ultimate tensile strengths of 1.24 GPa and 1.46 GPa, 
respectively, and excellent ductility with a unifor m elongation of 41% at 77 K. The 
exceptional strength-ductility combination in the U FG CoCrFeMnNi HEA surpasses 
those of all existing HEAs and other advanced engin eering alloys. Such unparalleled 
mechanical properties stem from the fully recrystal lized ultrafine grains that 
significantly strengthen the material and simultaneously provide high strain-hardening 
capability, and the activations of multiple deforma tion mechanisms consisting of 
prominent dislocation activities, profuse stacking faults and deformation twins. The 
fully recrystallized UFG microstructures do not onl y shed light on the design and 
development of HEAs with superior mechanical proper ties, but also enrich the 
potential applications of HEAs at the cryogenic environment.

1. Introduction 
Unlike the conventional alloys with a major element , such as Fe for steels and Al 
for Al alloys, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) usually contain five or more principal metal 
elements with equal or near equiatomic ratios [1-4] . Due to the feature of high 
configurational entropy, the simple solid solution structures, such as face-centered 
cubic (FCC), body-centered cubic (BCC), or hexagona l close-packed (HCP) 
structures, are generally formed in HEAs [5-7]. The sluggish diffusion and severe 
lattice-distortion that are induced by the mixture of multiple elements endow HEAs 
with exceptional structure stability and outstandin g mechanical properties [8-11]. 
Therefore, the discovery of these compositionally c omplex alloys provides excellent 
opportunities for the materials community to explore and harvest novel properties that 
are difficult to be achieved in conventional alloys [12-15]. 
Among these newly designed HEAs, an equiatomic CoCr FeMnNi HEA with a 
single-phase FCC structure developed by Cantor et a l. [1] manifests excellent 
combination of high strength and large ductility at ambient temperature due to the 
easy motions and interactions of Shockley partials [16]. More spectacularly, the 
twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) effect that play s dominant roles in the 
deformation at cryogenic temperatures enables the e nhancement of strength and 
ductility [17], while maintaining a high fracture t oughness up to 219 MPa·m 1/2 (at 77 
K) [13]. These remarkable mechanical properties make i t a highly potential candidate 
for the wide cryogenic applications. 
Although the CoCrFeMnNi HEA exhibited promising mec hanical performance, 
materials with ultrahigh yield strength and outstan ding ductility were required for the 
cryogenic applications in the fields such as liquef ied gas storage, energy conversion, 
high-energy physics, superconductivity, and so on [ 18-20]. Due to their exclusive 
relationship, the attainment of both ultrahigh stre ngth and excellent ductility is still 
challenging for the HEAs although several ingenious HEA design strategies to 
introduce TWIP [21, 22] and transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) [6, 23] effects 
into HEAs via tailoring the chemical compositions and concentrations were developed

2. Materials and Methods 
2.1. Material preparation 
Bulk CoCrFeMnNi HEAs with a diameter of 110 mm and height of 90 mm were 
casted using magnetic levitation melting technique, and more details of the casting 
process can be found elsewhere [27]. The as-cast in got was firstly homogenized at 
1100 °C for 2 h, and then hot forged at 1000 °C. A 30-mm diameter rod was obtained, 
and the rod was subsequently cold rolled into sheet s having a thickness of about 2.5 
mm at room temperature. To achieve the fully recrys tallized UFG structures, the 
cold-rolled sheets were heat treated at 675 °C for 30 min in argon atmosphere. 
Furthermore, the coarse-grained (CG) specimens are also prepared in this work for 
comparison, which were cut from the forged rod and annealed at 1200 °C for 60 min 
in argon atmosphere. 
2.2. Mechanical tests

Microstructural analyses were carried out by variou s advanced microscopy

An illustration of the transition of deformation me chanisms of the UFG HEAs 
deformed at 293 K and 77 K is shown in Fig. 5. The UFG specimen exhibits a 
significant balance of tensile strength and elongat ion at 293 K, which results from 
efficient storage of dislocations [27]. At the init ial stages with low strains, planar 
dislocation slip dominate the deformation mode, similar to those in CG reference [17]. 
The planar character of dislocations is gradually t ransformed to the dislocation cell 
structures with the increase of the strain, as show n in Fig. S6c. The transformation 
from planar slip to dislocation cell structures wit h the increase of strain is supposed to 
be induced by the dislocation multipoles formed at low strains. Though dislocation

cell structures form at high strains, the planarity of dislocation glide and low stacking 
fault energy (SFE) make the recovery processes larg ely constrained, resulting in 
substantial storage of dislocations and considerabl e strain-hardening capability. When 
the deformation temperature decrease from 293 K to 77 K, a transition from 
dislocation-dominated plasticity to multiple deform ation mechanisms consisting of 
dislocations, stacking faults and deformation twins occurs. 
With decreasing the temperature from 293 K to 77 K, the yield strength is 
dramatically enhanced, and the underlying mechanism s will be meticulously 
discussed in the following. The temperature-depende nt phenomenon for the yield 
strength is generally observed in FCC alloys [46, 4 7], because there are two parts, 
thermal and athermal components, that contribute to the yield strength, and the 
thermal contribution is sensitive to the deformatio n temperature [46-48]. The increase 
of the deformation temperature results in the glide of thermally activated dislocations, 
which could overcome the lattice friction barrier and the pinning effect of solid atoms, 
resulting to the decrease of yield strength [49]. F urthermore, when the deformation 
temperature decreases from 293 K to 77 K, a signifi cant increment with 442 MPa in 
the yield strength was obtained in UFG HEA, which is higher than that of 275 MPa in 
the CG HEA. This implies that small grain size can induce large yield strength 
increment with decreasing the temperature, which ca n also be deduced from the 
Hall-Petch relationship at 293 K and 77 K. During t he plastic stage, the flow stress 
should be resulted from the contribution of disloca tions and twins. The strengthening 
effect of dislocations and twins were calculated in the Supplementary Materials. 
Furthermore, the individual contributions of friction stress, gr ain boundaries, 
dislocations and twins to the flow stress of the UF G HEAs were summarized in Fig. 
S9. It is obvious that dislocation plays significan t roles in the evolution of flow stress, 
while the hardening effect directly contributed by twins apparently increases with 
plastic strain, especially at the later stage of deformation. 
With respect to the ductility, the fully recrystallized UFG microstructures enable the 
extensive dislocation activities and the remarkable storage of dislocations at 293 K,

both of which are beneficial to maintain the high s train hardening ability and enhance 
the ductility. As shown in Fig. 2, the exceptional ductility was achieved at 77 K, and 
this can be attributed to the transformation from d islocation-dominated mode to 
multiple deformation mechanisms consisting of dislo cations, stacking faults and 
deformation twins, as shown in Fig. 4. In general, deformation twinning is related to

present CoCrFeMnNi HEA. It is clear that the flow s tress is insufficient to activate

may contribute collectively to the notable ductilit y. Firstly, dislocation slip in planar 
mode can induce apparent strain hardening via effic ient dislocation storage [51], 
while lowering the temperature facilitates the supp ression of dynamic recovery, 
promoting the dislocation storage. Secondly, stacking faults can make beneficial 
contributions to the deformation of FCC alloys by p reventing the glide of dislocations 
[52]. Thirdly, as exhibited in Fig. 4f, the TBs cou ld provide ample room for 
dislocation accumulation via extensive dislocation- TBs interaction that is propitious 
to the enhancement of the strain-hardening capacity , which could delay the onset of 
necking instability and improve the ductility [16, 53].

deformation twins at 77 K. Compared to other struct ures, the recrystallized UFG 
structure possesses outstanding combination of stre ngth and ductility and present 
exceptional fatigue strengths [54, 55]. Another adv antage of the recrystallized UFG 
microstructure is the convenient fabrication by emp loying simple cold rolling and 
annealing treatment, which can be employed readily to other materials system and 
also promisingly applied with a large scale in indu stries. The current findings provide 
us not only with an excellent opportunity to achiev e unprecedented mechanical 
properties, extend the application of UFG HEAs in t he cryogenic environment, and 
deepen our understanding on the relationship betwee n the microstructures and 
properties of UFG materials, but also present an at tractive guideline to manufacture 
UFG alloys with excellent mechanical properties for broad applications.
