Abstract 
The reactive element effects (REEs) have been prove n to improve the thermal 
oxidation resistance of 
β-NiAl. However, their effects on mechanical propert ies, 
which are significant implications for the Ni-based superalloys substrate and β-NiAl 
bond coat of thermal barrier coatings (TBCs), have not been well understood so far. In 
this work, the basic elastic properties, ideal stre ngths, and stacking fault energies of 
reactive elements (REs: Hf, Zr, Dy, Y , La) doping γ-Ni, γ’-Ni 3Al, β-NiAl systems 
have been calculated using the first-principles met hod. The elastic properties results 
suggest that all five REs can enhance the ductiliti es of γ-Ni, β-NiAl, and γ’-Ni 3Al, of 
which La performs best. More interesting, the compl icated stacking fault (CSF) and 
anti-phase boundary (APB) energies of γ-Ni and β-NiAl decrease with the addition of 
REs in order of La>Y(Dy)>Zr>Hf, showing a totally o pposite trend to that of 
commonly accepted the REEs on oxidation. The signif icant charge density 
redistributions could explain the physical origin o f the improvement of 
plastic deformation ability for β-NiAl. This work gives insight into the mechanism o f 
RE effects on the ductility and strength of γ-Ni, γ’-Ni 3Al, β-NiAl materials, providing 
theoretical guidance for Ni-based superalloys desig n via a combinational analysis of 
bond topology and electronic structure.

1. Introduction 
As valuable elevated-temperature structural materials, Ni-based superalloys have 
been widely used in the hot sections of gas turbine s. It is generally believed that the 
excellent high-temperature strength and creep resis tance of Ni-based superalloys are 
primarily attributed to the microstructural strengt hening by the temperature-activated 
barrier of 
γ’-Ni 3Al (L1 2) ordered phase, which is coherently embedded in a solid 
solution matrix of the γ-Ni matrix [1, 2]. Not only that, due to its high m elting points 
and excellent isothermal oxidation resistance, β-NiAl (B2) is considered to be a 
potential metallic coating material for protecting the underlying Ni-based superalloys 
substrate. Under high temperature, a slow-growing α-Al 2O3 scale is formed on the 
β-NiAl surface of Ni-based superalloys, contributing to the oxidation resistance. It 
should also be noted that under thermal activations , the interdiffusion of Ni, Al 
elements occurs between the β-NiAl coating and Ni-based superalloys, causing the 
relevant phase transformation: β-NiAl + γ-Ni → γ'-Ni 3Al [3, 4]. Therefore, the above 
three phases of γ’-Ni 3Al, γ-Ni, and β-NiAl coexist and generate thermodynamic 
interactions in engine components. 
In the past decades, various elements such as Pt, W , Re, Co, Cr, Ti, Ce, Mo have 
been introduced to strengthen the γ’-Ni 3Al precipitates in the γ-Ni matrix and improve 
the ductility and oxidation resistance of β-NiAl. Consistence conclusions with the 
experimental and calculational results are obtained that these elements have different 
effects on the oxidation behavior and the mechanica l properties of β-NiAl [5-22], 
γ’-Ni 3Al [22-42] and γ-Ni [43-48]. However, special attention should be p aid that the 
doping behaviors of these alloying elements mostly focus on only one aspect of either 
mechanical strength or oxide film growth of γ’-Ni 3Al/γ-Ni and β-NiAl. As mentioned 
above, γ’-Ni 3Al, γ-Ni, and β-NiAl coexist under thermal activations. Moreover, it will 
inevitably lead to mechanical and oxidative failure of the component if the stress 
conduction between β-NiAl and underlying Ni-based superalloys during 
high-temperature thermal cycling. Therefore, it is necessary to simultaneously study 
the γ’-Ni 3Al, γ-Ni, and β-NiAl systems, whether in oxidation or mechanical property.

It has been reported extensively that trace additio n of reactive elements (REs) 
such as Hf, Zr, Y , Dy, and La, can segregate to the interface and significantly improve 
the oxide/alloy interfacial adhesion. However, the previous studies mainly focus on 
the reactive element effects (REEs) on thermodynami c oxidation properties of β-NiAl 
or γ’-Ni 3Al [49-51]. However, limited detailed knowledge exi sts on the 
afore-mentioned structural instability caused by te nsion or shear during the oxidation 
and phase transformation. Permanently, the poor ductility and embrittlement, limit the 
broad applications of β-NiAl and Ni-based superalloys [3, 5]. As known to us, the 
ideal strength and elastic modulus are fundamental to understand the mechanical 
behaviors of engineering materials and to develop p ractical applications. Advances in 
computational methods offer us an insight into the charge origin of material strength 
and structural deformation, such as general stackin g fault (GSF) energy and 
anti-phase boundary (APB) energy [9, 19, 31, 32, 34 -38, 42, 44-46], whereas it is 
unfeasible to be determined directly in experiments . Therefore, if the structural 
deformation and ductility of the REs-doped β-NiAl and Ni-based superalloys can be 
systematically studied by using first-principles calculations, it is expected to design an 
optimal structural and component configuration for the engines with both oxidation 
resistance and high mechanical properties. 
In this work, we investigate the mechanism of the t ypical REs, including Hf, Zr, 
Y , Dy, and La doping on the mechanical properties o f Ni-Al materials. The ideal 
strength, elastic properties as well as stacking fa ult energies of the RE doped γ-Ni, 
γ’-Ni 3Al, β-NiAl systems are calculated systematically using t he density functional 
theory (DFT) based the first-principles method in this manuscript. 
2. Computational methods 
First-principles calculations [52, 53] were perform ed using the Vienna ab initio 
simulation package (VASP) code with the Perdew-Burk e-Ernzerhof (PBE) [54] 
version of Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA) for exchange-correlation 
functional. The projector augmented wave (PAW) pseu dopotential was taken into 
account here.

The lattice structures of γ-Ni, β-NiAl, and γ’-Ni 3Al unit cells were relaxed firstly. 
The integration in the Brillouin zone was done on s pecial k points of 35 × 35 × 35 
grids for the phases under consideration. Moreover, an energy cutoff of 600 eV was

Atomic positions were relaxed using the conjugate gradient method until the forces on 
all the atoms converged to less than 10 -3 eV/Å. 
The ideal strength calculations of stress-strain de pendences conducted here were 
technically similar to our previous studies [49, 55, 56] to which we referred for further 
details. The atomic basis vectors of a given unit c ell were firstly projected onto the 
Cartesian coordinate system with one cell axis vect or being parallel to the strain 
direction for the tension loading. In the case of shear deformation, one cell axis vector 
was perpendicular to the slip plane, and another on e was parallel to the slip direction 
in that plane. Afterwards, the crystal was incremen tally deformed by transforming the 
unstrained cell basis vector matrices to the strain ed ones using the deformation 
matrices. In order to keep the crystal under a stre ss state of uniaxial tension or shear, 
the strained cell was relaxed for both the cell bas is vectors and for the atom position 
inside the unit cell by keeping the applied strain component fixed and relaxing the 
other five strain components until their conjugate stress components, i.e., 
Hellmann-Feynman stresses reached negligible values . Based on the slip planes and 
slip directions of cubic structure, four unit cells of specific orientation were selected, 
including (1) x [100], y [010], z [001], (2) x , y , z [111], (3) x , y 
, z [111], (4) x , y [110], z [001]. These four cel ls could be used to simulate 
three tensile and four shear deformations. The para meters setting of atomic 
relaxations convergence were the same as above. A 25 × 25 × 25 k-mesh was used for 
the k points sampling.

4. Conclusions 
As a systematic first-principle effort to study the fundamental mechanical 
properties of Ni-based superalloys, the effects of different reactive elements (Hf, Zr,

## Page 23

Dy, Y, La) on the variety of structures, ideal stre ngths, elastic properties, alias shear 
deformations and electronic origins for typical γ-Ni, β-NiAl and γ’-Ni 3Al metals, are 
researched in this paper. The following conclusions have been summarized as: 
(1) The ideal strength results indicate that the lowest tensile strength is on the (110) 
plane of γ’-Ni 3Al, which is the most easily stretched plane in the alloy. The elastic 
property results show that ductility of γ-Ni, β-NiAl, and γ’-Ni 3Al all increase with 
five kinds of REs doping, among which La contribute s the best influence while Hf 
shows the weakest effect. 
(2) The REs can also decrease the stacking fault energy of γ-Ni in order of 
La>Y>Dy>Zr>Hf. It could significantly improve APB, SISF, and CSF energies 
when RE is doped in γ’-Ni 3Al, which is beneficial to improving the mechanical 
properties of Ni-based superalloys. The decrease of the APB energies and γus of 
two slips for RE-doped β-NiAl is in the order of La>Y(Dy)>Zr>Hf. It is 
demonstrated that the enhanced plastic deformation ability of β-NiAl is due to the 
glide of [111] lattice direction activating the dislocation. 
(3) The origin of the considerable reduction of stackin g fault energy on (110) plane is 
the significant charge density redistributions betw een RE and host atoms, which 
hinders the shear deformation of β-NiAl. It provides theoretical support for 
understanding the REEs on mechanical properties and guides the optimal design 
of the Ni-based superalloy composition.

Highlights 
 La doping significantly enhances the ductility of t he γ-Ni, β-NiAl and γ’-Ni 3Al, 
especially for β-NiAl. 
 The five reactive elements decrease the stacking fa ult energy of γ-Ni and β-NiAl in 
order of La>Y>Dy>Zr>Hf. 
 Reactive elements doping strengthens the anti-phase boundary and stacking fault of 
γ’-Ni 3Al, thus improving the strength of superalloys effectively.

the shear deformation ability of β-NiAl.
