A Preliminary Study of the Development of 9Cr Fe-base ODS Alloys: 
The Effects of Minor Elements and Cooling Rate 
Jinsung Jang 1, a , Young Moon Jang 2,b , and Yin Zhong Shen 1,c 
1 KAERI 150 Deokjin-dong, Yuseong-gu, Daejeon, KOREA (south) 
2HHI 1 Jeonha-dong, Dong-gu, Ulsan, KOREA (south) 
ajjang@kaeri.re.kr, bex-jym1101@kaeri.re.kr, cshenliu8@snu.ac.kr 
Keywords: Fe-base ODS alloy, MA (mechanical alloying), high temperature tensile property, 
cooling rate effects 
Abstract. Three 9Cr Fe-base ODS alloys were prepared by MA (mechanical alloying) and HIP (hot 
isostatic processing) processes. The addition of Ti was confirmed to have a significant effect on the 
tensile properties at high temperature as well as at room temperature, while the V addition did not 
clearly show its effect on the microstructure or on the mechanical porperties at both temperatures. 
Fine and evenly distributed Y and Ti containing complex oxide was detected in the Ti added ODS 
alloy. They were attributed to the good high temperature strengths. Different cooling rates from the 
normalizing temperature did not result in a significant change in the grain boundary characters, but 
rather significant differences were observed in the mechanical properties. This is mainly attributed 
to the difference of the matrix phase rather than to the grain size distribution or the grain boundary 
characters. 
Introduction 
ODS (oxide dispersion strengthened) alloys are well known to have good strength at high 
temperatures due to the uniformly dispersed fine oxide particles that are known to be stable at the 
temperatures. Besides the good high temperature strengths, it is also widely known to possess an 
excellent resistance to the neutron irradiation swelling [1,2]. Accordingly, Fe-base ODS alloys are 
among the potential candidate alloys for the in-reactor components of the next generation nuclear 
power plants [3]. Commercially available Fe-base ODS alloys in general contain about 19% Cr. 
They usually have a good resistance to the aggressive environments at high temperatures, but are 
susceptible to the thermal embrittlement at around 500 
oC due to the spinodal decomposition [4,5]. 
In this study, three 9Cr Fe-base ODS alloys with different constituents were prepared by MA 
(mechanical alloying) process. Tensile tests were carried out at R.T and at 600 oC to evaluate the 
effects of the minor constitutents of the alloys, and investigations were performed on the 
microstructural evolutions of the ODS alloys, especially on the finely dispersed oxide particles, 
using TEM (transmission electron microscope) with EDS (energy dispersive spectroscopy). The 
effects of the cooling rate during the normalizing stage were also studied with respect to the 
mechanical behaviors and to the microstructural evolution using EBSD (electron backscattered 
diffraction) method. 
Experimental Procedure 
Three kinds of 9Cr Fe-base ODS alloys, sample A: Fe-9Cr-0.2Y 
2O3, sample B: Fe-9Cr-0.2V-
0.2Y 2O3, and sample C: Fe-9Cr-0.2V-0.2Ti-0.2Y 2O3, were prepared by MA (mechanical alloying) 
and HIP (hot isostatic pressing) processes. Raw metal powders of commercial purity Fe, Cr, V and 
Ti were alloyed with 20-30 nm yttria powders. Metal powders were of 10 to 20 micron size, except 
for V of less than 45 micron. Proportionally mixed powders were alloyed using a planetary ball mill 
in Ar gas atmosphere. Milling media were steel balls of 5 mm in diameter. Various ball-to-powder 
ratios (B/P) were combined with different milling times from 30 min to 20 hrs. Powders after MA 
were analyzed with XRD (x-ray diffractometer) and SEM (scanning electron microscope). The

MA powders were hipped at 1150 oC under about 100 MPa (15,000 psi) for 4 hrs following the 
canning and degassing processes. Hipped samples were hot rolled at 1150 oC, and then normalized 
at 1150 oC for 1 hr and tempered at 750 oC for 1 hr through air cooling respectively. At room 
temperature and at 600 oC tensile tests were carried out according to ASTM E8. The gauge section 
of the round specimen was 3 mm in diameter and 12.5 mm long. In addition to the regular 
normalizing treatment through air cooling with the cooling rate of 5 K/sec, sample B (Fe-9Cr-0.2V-
0.2Y2O3) specimens were also cooled with different cooling rates (0.5 and 800 K/sec) from the 
normalizing temperature. The change in the grain boundary character distribution was investigated 
using EBSD (electron backscattered diffraction) and the tensile behavior was also estimated. 
Results and Discussion 
After MA process, the powders were observed using SEM and the crystallite size distribution was 
analyzed by XRD. Fig. 1 shows the evolution of the crystallite size of the MA powders with MA 
time at various B/P ratios obtained. We can easily see that the crystallite size becomes stabilized 
after about 12 hrs of MA at B/P ratio of 10:1 or 15:1. In case of the B/P ratio of 20:1 even 8 hrs of 
MA seems enough to ensure a stable size distribution of the powders.

Considering the powder yield and the process time, the milling was carried out at the optimal 
milling condition as follows: Milling was done for 20 hrs at the B/P ratio of 15:1. SEM and EDS 
analyses of the MA particles revealed that the particle size distribution and the uniformity of the 
chemistry of the MA particles were satisfactory at the above milling condition. 
Fig. 2 shows the stress-strain curves of the ODS samples at R.T and at 600 oC. Samples A and B 
show a similar behavior compared with sample C. The addition of V to 9Cr Fe-base ODS alloy 
appears hardly affect the tensile behavior at R.T or at 600 oC. The yield and the tensile strengths of 
one sample are very similar to those of the others, although the elongation decreased by about 8% at 
R.T on the sample B. Ti addition (sample C), however, significantly changed the tensile behavior of 
9Cr Fe-base ODS alloy. Tensile strength increased from 700 to 1800 MPa at R.T, and from 390 to 
600 MPa at 600 oC. The elongation decreased from 25 to 8% at R.T, and from 30 to 20% at 600oC. 
Fig. 3 illustrates a TEM micrograph of the sample C replica. Fine Y and Ti complex oxide particles 
(5 to 20 nm in diameter) were distinguishable in sample C in contrast to the sample B, and it is 
considered to be attributable to the higher strengths at R.T or at high temperatures [7,8].

Sample B (Fe-9Cr-0.2V-0.2Y 
2O3) was cooled with the different cooling rates of 800, 5, and 0.5 
K/sec from the normalizing temperature of 1150 oC. Grain size distributions and grain boundary 
character distributions were estimated using EBSD. The average grain size of the sample with the 
highest cooling rate (800 K/sec) was about 2.1 micron in diameter, and those of the others (5 and 
0.5 K/sec) were about 4.5 and 6.4 micron in diameter, respectively. The change in the grain 
boundary character distribution with the different cooling rates was not noticeable, i.e. the fractions 
of low angle boundaries or CSL (coincidence site lattice) boundaries were not significantly changed, 
but only the fraction of the twin boundary reduced as the cooling rate decreased. Fig 4 illustrates the 
different tensile behavior of the sample B with the different cooling rates. The sample of the higher 
cooling rate (800 K/sec) showed a much higher strength but at the same time a significantly lower 
elongation (less than 5% in total). This is attributed to the finer grain size and the matrix of full 
martensite phase. The samples with the slower cooling rates of 5 and 0.5 K/sec showed 
progressively the lower strength and that might be due to the less fraction of the martensite phase in 
the matrix with the slower cooling rate. However, the longer elongations could be considered as a 
good compromise of the lower strengths for the real application.

Summary 
The effects of V and Ti addition on 9Cr Fe-base ODS alloys were investigated. Ti addition, contrast 
to V showed a significantly positive effect on the tensile strength to some extent, but showed to 
have a trade off in terms of the elongations. Y and T containing oxide complex was observed in 
sample C and identified as an orthorhombic structure Y 
2TiO 5, and these fine and evenly distributed 
oxide particles are attributed to the better high temperature strengths. Cooling rate from the 
normalizing temperature was revealed to influence mechanical strengths.
