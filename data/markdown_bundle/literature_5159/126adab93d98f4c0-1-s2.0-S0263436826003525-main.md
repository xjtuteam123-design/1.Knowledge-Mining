ABSTRACT
Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) with a bcc + B2 structure are promising for high-temperature 
applications, but the compositional stability of the B2 phase remains poorly understood. Here, we systematically 
determine the compositional boundaries for the as-cast bcc + B2 structure within the Nb-Mo-Co-Ti alloy system 
by varying Co + Ti concentration, Nb:Mo ratio, and Co:Ti ratio. We establish that a predominant bcc + B2 
structure exists only within a narrow compositional window: total Co + Ti must exceed 20 at.% and the Nb:Mo 
ratio must remain near equiatomic. Outside this compositional window, the B2 phase becomes increasingly off- 
stoichiometric due to the incorporation of Nb and Mo, which triggers the nucleation of concurrent μ phase within 
compositionally modified B2 regions with a reproducible orientation relationship, {011}

bcc + B2 structure in RCCAs where the B2 phase competes with topologically close-packed intermetallics.
1. Introduction
For the past 16 years, refractory complex concentrated alloys 
(RCCAs) have attracted considerable attention as promising materials 
for advanced aerospace applications [1,2] . They offer a higher strength 
[3,4] , superior oxidation resistance [5,6] , toughness [7,8] , or better 
processability [9,10] than traditional refractory alloys. However, a sig -
nificant challenge remains: most RCCA exhibit unbalanced properties. 
Alloys with exceptional high-temperature strength are often brittle at 
lower temperatures [11] , while those with good processability tend to 
lose strength rapidly at elevated temperatures [12] .
Inspired by the historic success of nickel-based superalloys, which 
balance strength and ductility through a coherent two-phase micro -
structure, researchers seek a similar strategy for RCCAs. Since RCCAs are 
typically based on a bcc (body-centred cubic) matrix, the analogous 
reinforcing phase is the crystallographically compatible B2 compound 
[13,14] . However, the key challenge lies in stabilising the B2 phase at 
the temperatures required for service.
The thermal stability of the B2 phase is highly composition- 
dependent. In some Al-containing systems, it forms either as a 
metastable compound [15 – 17] or as a compound which dissolves at T <

work is to establish the as-cast stability window, which is directly 
relevant for arc-melted and additively manufactured components, 
where controlling phase formation during solidification is essential for 
achieving reproducible microstructures.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Thermodynamic predictions of phase stability
CALPHAD modelling ( Fig. 1 ) was performed using both equilibrium 
calculations and Scheil-Gulliver non-equilibrium solidification simula -
tions. According to equilibrium phase diagrams, reducing the Co + Ti 
content from 40 to 10 at.% would preserve the dual-phase bcc + B2 
structure, with only a monotonic decrease in the B2 phase volume 
fraction. In contrast, the Scheil-Gulliver simulations predicted a more 
complex solidification pathway, involving the formation of additional 
C14/ С 15 Laves and μ phases within the same Co + Ti compositional 
range.
Compared with Co + Ti variation, changes in the Nb:Mo ratio were 
predicted to have a stronger influence on phase selection. Both 
equilibrium and non-equilibrium calculations indicated that increasing 
Nb content enhances the tendency for μ -phase formation, whereas Mo- 
rich compositions were predicted to favour bcc + B2 structures or 
Laves phase formation. Specifically, equilibrium calculations indicated 
the possible formation of C36 Laves phase, while Scheil-Gulliver simu -
lations predicted C15 Laves phase during solidification.

EDS analysis revealed that, as Co + Ti decreased, the B2 phase became 
highly Ti-deficient (the Ti content dropped by ~15 at.%), while the Co 
content decreased only slightly (~3.7 at.%). This deficit was compen -
sated by enhanced solubility of Nb and Mo. The associated composi -
tional changes were reflected in the calculated B2 lattice parameters 
(Table S2). The excess refractory atoms on the B2 sublattices inducing 
off-stoichiometry that directly reduced its thermodynamic stability. The 
destabilised B2 phase then provided a favourable nucleation site for the 
μ phase, which grew with a well-defined orientation to the parent B2 
phase. The fact that the μ phase invariably appeared first inside the B2 
phase layers and obeyed this reproducible OR was compelling evidence 
that the μ phase formed at the expense of the B2 phase, not as an in -
dependent solidification product.
This sublattice-driven destabilisation explains why a minimum 
content of B2-forming elements is required: the B2 phase tolerates only a 
limited degree of off-stoichiometry before a more stable intermetallic 
compound nucleates. The empirically determined threshold of ~20 at.% 
Co + Ti thus provides a quantitative stability limit for alloy design. 
Moreover, the displacement of Ti from the B2 phase to a far greater 
extent than Co indicates that Ti occupancy is more critical for B2 phase 
stability, a point exploited in Section 3.4 .

Together with the Co + Ti results, these data define the as-cast 
compositional stability window: a predominant bcc + B2 phase struc -
ture is obtained only when the Co + Ti content exceeds 20 at.% and the 
Nb:Mo ratio is near equiatomic. Decreasing the Co + Ti content below 
this threshold or deviating significantly from the equiatomic Nb:Mo 
ratio promotes μ -phase formation and reduces the B2 fraction. This 
experimentally determined window is narrower than predicted by 
equilibrium CALPHAD calculations, which do not anticipate the μ phase 
at 20 at.% Co + Ti. Scheil-Gulliver simulations do predict the μ phase in 
this region, but they overestimate its occurrence compared with 
experiment.
Regarding the thermal stability of the μ phase, equilibrium CAL -
PHAD calculations suggest that it may persist up to the B2 solvus in some 
compositional ranges ( Fig. 1 ). Therefore, although heat treatment may 
modify phase fractions and chemical partitioning, experimental verifi -
cation is required to determine whether μ phase dissolution and an in -
crease in B2 phase fraction can be achieved.
A comparison of measured с hemical compositions with equilibrium 
CALPHAD calculations, which were performed at the temperatures 
selected to match the experimental B2 phase volume fractions 
(Table S1), revealed good agreement for the bcc phase (within a few at. 
%) but a systematic discrepancy for the B2 phase. Experimentally, the 
Nb content in the B2 phase ranged from 14.2 to 28.2 at.% and the Mo 
content from 3.0 to 31.0 at.%, whereas CALPHAD predicted only 
0.3 – 1.2 at.% Nb and 0.3 – 5.9 at.% Mo. This indicates that the TCHEA5 
database treats the B2 phase as an overly stoichiometric compound, 
missing its considerable off-stoichiometric solubility. The data reported 
here thus provide the necessary phase-composition dataset to remodel 
B2 as a proper solution phase.

4. Conclusions
The stability of the bcc + B2 phase structure within the Nb-Mo-Co-Ti 
alloy system was systematically investigated as a function of Co + Ti 
concentration, Nb:Mo ratio, and Co:Ti ratio. The following conclusions 
were drawn: 
1) A predominantly dual-phase bcc + B2 structure is obtained in the as- 
cast state only when the Co + Ti content exceeds 20 at.% and the Nb: 
Mo ratio is 1:1. Decreasing the Co + Ti content and/or deviating from 
the near equiatomic Nb:Mo ratio results in a decrease in the fraction 
of the B2 phase and the concurrent appearance of the μ phase, with 
the OR of {011}
B2 
|| { 211 – 3}
μ
, 〈 0 11 〉
B2 
|| < 110 – 1 >
μ
.
2) The formation of the μ phase is triggered by destabilisation of the 
parent B2 phase due to progressive incorporation of Nb and Mo onto 
its sublattices.
3) Shifting the Co:Ti ratio from 1:1 to 1:2 suppresses μ -phase formation 
and restores the bcc + B2 phase structure even at only 10 at.% (Co +
Ti).
Collectively, these findings establish a compositionally guided 
framework for understanding B2 stability in the Nb-Mo-Co-Ti alloy 
system. Further studies involving annealing treatments and mechanical 
property evaluations are needed to assess the thermal stability and 
performance implications of the identified as-cast phase configurations.
