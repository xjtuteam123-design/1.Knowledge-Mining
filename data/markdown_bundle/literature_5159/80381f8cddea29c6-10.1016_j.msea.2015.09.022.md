abstract
Enhancement of the physical and mechanical properties of polycrystalline Ni-base superalloys may be
achieved through control of the grain boundary structure and is dependent on the optimization of the
thermal– mechanical processing parameters. Superalloys containing grain boundary networks that are
comprised with a suf ﬁciently high fraction of Σ3/twin boundaries have been reported to exhibit en-
hanced creep and fatigue properties. In this report, the density and length fraction of twin boundaries in
annealed samples of powder processed Ni-base superalloy RR1000 were quanti ﬁed and expressed as a
function of the average grain diameter. The results were found to be consistent with classical models
relating density and length fraction to grain size. The effects of varying hot deformation parameters on
twin density and length fraction were also quanti ﬁed and modi ﬁed models were derived to describe the
relationships.

1. Introduction
Ni-base superalloys are widely used in advanced gas turbine
engines due to their superior fatigue, creep, and corrosion re-
sistance in high temperature environments. Among the critical
components that constitute turbine engines, materials used as
structures for turbine blade and disk applications require the
highest temperature capability as they operate directly within the
pathways of the combustion gases. Single crystal superalloys are
state-of-the-art for use as blades in advanced turbine engines
where creep deformation tends to be the principal life-limiting
mechanism [1,2]. Conversely, polycrystalline Ni-base superalloys
are used as disk rotors that operate at relatively lower tempera-
tures and higher stresses where fatigue and fracture properties are
of much greater concern [3– 6]. However, as the operating tem-
perature of advanced gas turbine engines increase to improve
performance and reduce emissions, this requires turbine disk
materials to possess a temperature capability of 750 °C or above.
At these elevated temperatures, creep and resistance to hot cor-
rosion become important mechanisms that will interact with fa-
tigue mechanisms. For this reason, improvements in the physical
and mechanical properties of polycrystalline Ni-base superalloys
are required for the development of next generation gas turbine
engines.
Grain boundary engineering (GBE) has been used to improve
the properties of various polycrystalline materials by modifying
the character of the grain boundaries in the microstructure of the
alloy [7]. Introducing special low-
Σ boundaries, such as Σ3 twin
boundaries, is key to GBE and the improvement of properties such
as resistance to corrosion [8], creep deformation [9], and fatigue
crack propagation/initiation [10,11 ]. In polycrystalline materials,
each grain boundary can be characterized by its orientation de-
ﬁned by the two adjacent grains. The coherency between the
lattices of the two neighbor grains is de ﬁned using the coincidence
site lattice (CSL) model which classi ﬁes the degree of coherency
using a Σ value attributed to the grain boundary [12]. Low CSL
boundaries, or Σ429, possess large concentrations of crystalline
defects and vacancies which promote diffusive mechanisms at
elevated temperatures thereby leading to environmental de-
gradation and poor resistance to creep deformation. Conversely,
coherent interfaces i.e. high CSL boundaries, or
Σo29, contain few
crystalline defects and therefore, the mechanisms by which dif-
fusion and mass transport occur along these interfaces are mini-
mized. Employing GBE techniques to introduce a high proportion
of Σ3 boundaries in the material and improve the properties of the
alloy may potentially extend the temperature capability of existing
Ni-base superalloys and enable them to accommodate operation at
higher temperatures.

To date, GBE has been used in a limited number of commer-
cialized applications [13] and consists of iterations of cold rolling
to 5 – 20% strain at room temperature followed by short time an-
nealing of a few minutes [14]. Due to the high stresses associated
with cold work combined with the iterative deformation and an-
neal cycles, conventional processing routes for GBE are not ideally
suited for production of physically large and geometrically com-
plex Ni-base superalloys components. In addition, the thermal
inertia involved during heat treatment of physically large and
complex shaped components is not compatible with short an-
nealing times. Consequently, GBE approaches that utilize thermo-
mechanical processes capable of producing near net shape com-
ponents at stresses applicable to the forging capabilities of current
turbine engine manufacturers need to be considered. In order to
understand the fundamental mechanisms governing the forma-
tion of the desirable
Σ3 boundaries and facilitate the development
of a robust GBE processing route for Ni-base superalloys, models
need to be developed to effectively relate the effects of thermo-
mechanical process parameters and the grain boundary character
of the material.
2. Experimental procedure
In this investigation, 10 mm diameter discs of Ni-base super-
alloy RR1000, of composition listed in Table 1 , were cut from an
as-consolidated billet for a series of heat treatments. The proces-

previously [15]. The billet was ﬁrst heat treated at 1 1 10 °C for 4 h

The formation of annealing twins has been described and
several models have been developed over the years. The growth
accident model is based on the formation of a coherent twin
boundary due to a stacking error at the migrating grain boundary
during grain growth [18] and has been supported by a number of
recent investigations [17,19]. Based on the suggested formation
mechanisms, two expressions of the twin density in FCC metals
during grain growth regime have been commonly used: Gleiter
[20] expressed the probability that a {1 1 1} plane is a coherent twin
boundary leading to the expression of the twin density as a
function of the grain size and annealing temperature, and Pande
et al. [21] showed that the twin density is proportional to the grain
size only. Pande et al.'s model has been validated in a number of
studies and the same grain size dependence can be seen in the
RR1000 samples, Fig. 1 . Regardless of the annealing temperature
and time, the twin density is shown to be proportional to the grain
size. Therefore, Pande et al.'s expression was chosen for compar-

4. Conclusion
In conclusion, considerable changes in twin density and length
fraction were observed as a function of the grain size in powder
processed Ni-base superalloy RR1000 when hot deformation
parameters were varied. Deformation conditions promoting su-
perplastic ﬂow resulted in a minimal twin density and length
fraction upon annealing, whereas parameters resulting in the
storage of strain energy in the form of intragranular misorienta-
tions were found to increase both the density and length fraction
of twin boundaries for a given grain size. Within the range of
deformation parameters studied in this investigation, Pande and
Gleiter’s model for predicting twin density and length fraction as a
function of grain size were effectively modi ﬁed to account for the
effects of hot deformation.
