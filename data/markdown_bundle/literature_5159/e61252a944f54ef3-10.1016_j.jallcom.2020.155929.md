Abstract
A non-equiatomic FeCr 1.2NixAl alloys were introduced and characterized employing various characterization techniques. The
great application prospect is expected because of their high mechanical properties and superior speciﬁc strength. In this study, the
eﬀect of Ni addition on the microstructure, mechanical properties and work-hardening behavior at room and high temperatures
have been systematically investigated. Dual phases of ordered (Ni, Al) structure and disordered [Fe, Cr] structures are involved
in alloys. The EDS analysis indicates the formation of Fe /Cr-rich regions in interdendrite and Ni /Al-rich regions in grains. The
alloys show remarkable advantages in density and microhardness. The experimental results show that the Ni addition signiﬁcantly
increases the compressive strength of the as-cast alloys attributed to the solution strengthening and ﬁne grain strengthening, and
more importantly, simultaneous enhancements in speciﬁc strength. However, the additive Ni is found to cause a little decrease at
high temperature owing to the crystal boundary weakening, accompanied with a decline in speciﬁc strength. The alloys possess
higher work-hardening ability rather than many other alloys and metallic materials. The value of work-hardening exponent and
capacity slightly increase with the additive Ni and the FeCr 1.2Ni1.3Al alloy performs the most desirable. Due to these signiﬁcant
advancements, the proposed alloys are attractive for use in aerospace and automotive technology ﬁelds.
Keywords: multi-component alloys, microstructure, microhardness, density, mechanical property, work hardening behavior.
1. Introduction
As a breakthrough of conventional alloy design, high-entropy
alloys(HEAs) or multi-component alloys, have become a fa-
vorite topic for analysis, which was ﬁrst proposed by Yeh et
al. [1] and Cantor et al [2]. This kind of alloys commonly
consist of more than ﬁve principal elements with equal or near-
equal atomic ratios [3–5], which facilitates the highly stable
and simpler solid solution structures in alloys. Four remark-
able features, unique high entropy, slow diﬀusion, heavy lattice
distortion and cocktail eﬀect [6–9], have brought many unique
structures and excellent performance characteristics, such as
low density [10, 11], exceptional mechanical properties [12–
16], and excellent temperature resistance [17, 18]. Thus then,
considerable investigations have attempted to design new alloys
with promising mechanical properties.
Recently, many researches have found that the HEAs with
(near-) equiatomic is di ﬃcult to obtain the great combination
of strength and ductility [19, 20]. Li et al. [21] proposed that
strong and ductile HEAs can be obtained by multiphase non-
equiatomic compositional conﬁgurations. With the further de-
velopment of HEA, quaternary and ternary alloys in an equal
molar ratio are also belong to the concept of HEA with great
properties [22, 23]. The presence of this novel concept has

2. Experiment procedure:
The present FeCr 1.2NixAl (x=1.1,1.3,1.5 denoted as Ni1.1,
Ni1.3, Ni1.5, respectively) alloy ingots were synthesized by
vacuum arc melting techniques in protective Ar atmosphere.
The raw elemental metals have a purity higher than 99.9 weight
percent. To achieve a homogeneous distribution of elements in
the alloys, each arc-melted ingots were ﬂipped and re-melted at
least ﬁve times and was in a liquid state for about 5 min during
each melting event. Thus then, the prepared ingots were given
in Fig.1.
Fig. 1. Prepared alloy ingots.
Phase composition and microstructure of the alloy were iden-
tiﬁed using X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) and scanning electron mi-
croscopy (SEM). The XRD analysis was carried out on an X-
ray diﬀractometer (XRD, XPERT diﬀractometer with Cu radi-
ation target) with the 2 θ ranging from 20◦ to 100◦ at a speed
of 5◦/min. SEM investigations were performed utilizing Zeiss
Merlin Compact microscopy equipped with energy-dispersive
(EDS) detector for the chemical-composition analysis. Prior to
examination, the specimens were carefully mechanical ground
and polished thus then cleaned with ethanol. Density of the al-
loy was measured via Archimedes drainage method. Vickers
microhardness was measured on polished surfaces using HVS-
1000 digital microhardness meter under a 0.5kg load applied
for 10s. For each specimen, 15 points were tested and average
the results to ensure the measurement precision.
Samples with 4 mm diameter and 6 mm height were ex-
tracted from the ingots for the compressive tests. The AG-X
Plus 250kN/50kN Electronic Universal Testing Machine was
employed with an initial strain rate of 0.5 mm /min. For high
temperature compressive tests, the samples were placed in pre-
heated furnace and held for 10 min before test. The testing was
performed with strain rate of 2 mm/min at 900◦C until the frac-
ture of the specimen or reaching height reduction of 45%.

According to the mechanical behavior analyzed above, it is
amazing to note that the curves undergo a transition to a linear
hardening stage once the stress overpass the yield stress. The
large plasticity and work hardening capacity suggest the im-
portance of investigations on the work-hardening behavior of
the proposed alloys. Fig.10(a) and (b) present the true stress-
strain curves of the prepared alloys, where the parameters for
the work-hardening behavior analysis were determined from
the curves. Generally, the work-hardening characteristics in
the process of compressive deformation was analyzed using the
work-hardening rateθ [44], which is expressed as equation (4).
θ = dσ/dε (4)
whereσ andε can be determined from the true stress-true strain
curvesand the strain hardening rate was plotted in Fig.10(c)
and (d). As can be seen, the work-hardening was divided into
three representative stages. The work hardening revealed by
this analysis is in great agreement with several earlier obser-
vations for Al 0.1CoCrFeNi HEAs [45]. At early period, the
work hardening rate is characterized by the rapid decrease with
the increasing true strain. This may relate to the annihilation
and recombination of dislocations at early deformation stage
according to the previously reported researches [46]. After
the elastic-plastic transition, the stage II exhibits an increasing
work-hardening rate with the further increasing of true strain,
which can be explained by the slip and climb motion of disloca-
tions at grain boundaries. As appearant evidence by the incline
of stage II, it is inferred that the Ni addition in solid solution
can particularly decrease the dislocation cross-slip. Eventually,
the stage-III witnessed the rapid decline of work-hardening rate
due to the strain softening. The research [47] proposed by C.
Q. Huang ascribed the strain softening to the dislocation rear-
rangements and annihilations, meanwhile, accompanied with
the dynamic recovery processes. When the hardening and soft-
ening mechanisms progressively achieve dynamic equilibrium,
it suggests a very slight decrease of work-hardening rate. Be-

4. Conclusion
In the present investigation, FeCr1.2NixAl alloys was synthe-
sized by the arc-melting method in as-cast state. Systemati-
cally, the crystal structure, microstructure were characterized,
accompanied with the evaluation of phase stability. The den-
sity, microhardness, compressive properties at room and high
temperature of the alloys were investigated. Besides, attempts
have also been made to understand the work hardening charac-
teristics. Alloying eﬀect of Ni on the phase formation and me-
chanical properties were discussed in detail. Based on above
results and analyses, the major conclusions can be drawn from
the current work as follows.
(1) The interesting metallographic features are the observa-
tion of a double-phase, which is identiﬁed to be the combina-
tion of the ordered phase (Ni, Al) and disordered phase [Fe, Cr].
The EDS analysis indicates the formation of Fe/Cr-rich regions
in interdendrite and Ni/Al-rich regions in grains.
(2) The alloys possess high-hardness( > 450kgf/mm)
and low-density(< 7g/cm3),which is attractive for use in
lightweight technology ﬁelds. Besides, the addition of Ni
brings about a slight increase of the density and microhardness
to the FeCr1.2NixAl alloys.
(3) The alloys possess an excellent combination of fracture
strength and ductility and even the superior speciﬁc strength

due to the combination of ﬁne grain strengthening and solid so-

The strength exhibits a slight decrease due to the addition of
elemental Ni, simultaneously accompanied with a decrease to
the speciﬁc strength. Despite this, it o ﬀers superior strength

superalloys particularly attributed to the dual-phase structures.

other alloys and metallic materials. The Ni addition leads to a
