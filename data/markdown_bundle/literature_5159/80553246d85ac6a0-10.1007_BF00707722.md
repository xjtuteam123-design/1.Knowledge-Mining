Short-term strength tests were carried out in tension at a rate of 0.05 mm/sec in an 
atmosphere of industrial purity argon. The prescribed temperature was held with an accuracy 
of ± 2.5°C. Stress-rupture tests were carried out at 900°C in a vacuum (~ 0.1MPa) with test 
duration up to 1300 h. 
Maximum strength in the test temperature range is exhibited by the binary alloy of vana- 
dium with niobium. There is a significant increase of strength of alloys in the V--Zr--C 
system on adding niobium or molybdenum. The greater the content of niobium or molybdenum, 
then the greater is the strength of complex alloys of the system V--Zr--C (Fig. l). 
Niobium increases the temperature for the start of marked loss of strength for alloys 
in the system V--Zr--C. Loss of strength for alloys in the system V--Nb--Zr-C begins above 
temperatures of 400-800°C. At 900°C loss of strength slows down, and with a further in- 
crease in temperature the strength changes little. 
Alloying with niobium and molybdenum for V--Zr--C alloys leads to a marked increase in 
stress-rupture strength (Fig. 2). With an increase in content of niobium or molybdenum 
stress-rupture strength also increases (see Table i). 
It was established by x-ray structural analysis of specimens that alloying vanadium 
with niobium leads to an increase in lattice parameter for the matrix. The lattice parameter 
of unalloyed vanadium VnM-2 is a = 0.3032 nm [i], and for spectrographically pure vanadium 
a = 0.03024 nm [2]. The lattice parameter of the matrix of alloy V--20% Nb is greater and 
it is a = 0.3070 nm. As a result of marked lattice distortion, the matrix of this alloy ex- 
hibits high short-term strength. However, in view of the comparatively high temperature of 
recrystallization (900°C) the effect of test duration on stress-rupture strength is signifi- 
cant, i.e. ~ioo = 120 MPa; Olooo = 30 MPa. 
The stress-rupture strength of alloy VTsU depends little on test duration, and this is 
connected with alloy structure. On the basis of x-ray structural and electron microscope 
studies it may be concluded that the alloy is strengthened mainly as a result of secondary 
phase particles. The lattice parameter of the alloy matrix is less than the parameter for 
unalloyed vanadium (VnM-2) and it is 0.3031 nm. The low value of matrix lattice parameter 
for alloy VTsU is explained by the refining effect of zirconium introduced into the alloy 
which binds practically all of the carbon. Electron microscope study of thin films made it 
possible to detect in the alloy structure coarse equilibrium particles of globular shape. 
Scanning of electron diffraction patterns indicated that coarse equilibrium particles of 
globular shape are precipitates of carbide ZrC with an fcc lattice. Molybdenum and niobium 
promote carbide phase refinement.
