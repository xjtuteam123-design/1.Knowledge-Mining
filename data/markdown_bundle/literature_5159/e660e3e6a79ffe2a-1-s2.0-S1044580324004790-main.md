ABSTRACT 
In this study, a novel heat-resistant Al-Si-Fe-Mn-Ni alloy suitable for selective laser melting technique was 
developed. By optimizing the process parameters, as-built alloy with few defects and superior mechanical 
properties was manufactured at a laser power of 350 W and a scanning speed of 1300 mm/s, with horizontal and 
vertical ultimate tensile strengths of 589 and 529 MPa, respectively. Conventional annealing, solid solution and 
T6 treatments were conducted to investigate the effect of post heat treatments on the microstructure and me -
chanical properties. Owing to the low diffusion capacity of Fe, Mn and Ni elements, the alloys show excellent 
high-temperature stability. The initial eutectic structure is retained after annealing, with few changes in the 
morphology of the precipitated phases. During solution treatment, the Al
–
Si eutectic breaks up to form diffusely 
distributed Si particles, and Fe, Mn-rich intermetallic compounds as well as AlFeNi phases gradually precipitate 
out, which further occurs in T6 state. Due to its high-temperature resistance, the alloy developed in this study 
exhibits better high-temperature mechanical properties than conventional commercial AlSi10Mg alloys, showing 
higher strength in high-temperature tensile experiments from 150 
◦
C to 300 
◦
C. 
1. Introduction 
Selective laser melting (SLM), as a high-precision additive 
manufacturing technology for near-net forming, has been widely used in 
the fabrication of various metal materials. Owing to the ability to rapidly 
manufacture complex components and the flexibility to adapt to struc -
tural modification, the SLM technology has gained a lot of attention in 
aerospace, energy storage facilities, equipment production and other 
fields, and is expected to demonstrate its advantages in the era of model 
replacement and industrial upgrading [ 1 , 2 ]. Currently, the alloys 
commonly used in SLM manufacturing include iron [ 3 , 4 ], nickel [ 5 – 7 ], 
titanium [ 8 , 9 ], and aluminum [ 10 , 11 ] alloys. Among them, aluminum 
alloy has great development potential in novel energy vehicles and space 
shuttles due to its low expense and density with excellent specific 
strength and stiffness. However, the types of aluminum alloys suitable 
for SLM technology are limited. On one hand, Al
–
Cu and Al
–
Zn alloys 
have extremely high cracking sensitivity and are prone to dehiscence 
during deposition [ 12 , 13 ]. On the other hand, Al-Mg-RE (RE is rare 
earth element) alloys lose the cost advantage of Al alloys due to the 
presence of precious rare earth elements, which is not conducive to the 
commercial use and application promotion. In addition, in terms of 
aviation applications, rockets will experience severe friction in the at -
mosphere during takeoff, resulting in a large amount of heat generation. 
This heat accumulation means that aerospace equipment, especially 
structural components, has a requirement for serving at high tempera -
ture, and therefore exists a high demand for their thermal stability as 
well as high-temperature mechanical properties. While current research 
on SLM-Al alloys has little consideration for their properties at high- 
temperature.

Al

kinds of alloys. Jiang et al. [ 14 ] investigated the effect of molten pool 
boundaries on the anisotropy of mechanical properties of AlSi10Mg and 
found that the deformation incompatibility between fine equiaxed

3.1. Parameters optimization of SLM process 
The process parameters optimization was carried out in the first 
place. Fig. 3 shows the relative density of the as-built alloys under 
different printing parameters. It is not difficult to find that with the in -

3.3. Mechanical properties 
Fig. 12 shows the room temperature engineering stress-strain curves

3.4. Fracture morphology 
The room temperature tensile fracture morphology of the alloys 
under different heat treatment conditions are shown in both horizontal 
( Figs. 13 (a, d, g, j)) and vertical ( Figs. 13 (b, e, h, k)) directions, with 
Figs. 13 (c, f, i, l) for the magnified images of localized regions of the 
vertical fracture. As can be seen in Figs. 13 (a-c), numerous tear ridges 
are visible in the horizontal fracture of the as-built alloy, which signals 
the poor ductility of the alloy. The vertical fracture clearly shows a long 
strip-like melt channel morphology, which indicates that the edge of the 
molten pool is a weak zone where the fractures tend to occur. High 
magnification images show that the interior of the channel consists of 
numerous small dimples whose size smaller than 1 μ m, which corre -
spond to the eutectic morphology. Annealed alloy makes little discrep -
ancy compared with the as-built state in macroscopic terms, with tear 
ridges and cleavage planes as the main features in horizontal direction, 
and continuous melt channel is still visible in the vertical direction. 
Some of the circular porosity at the fracture originates from the unes -
caped gas during the deposition process, and this kind of porosity defects 
is an inevitable fabrication problem in SLM [ 31 , 32 ]. As can be seen from 
Fig. 13 (f), some nano-precipitated particles appeared inside the tiny 
dimples at this time, which coincided with the formation of Fe-rich and 
Mn-rich phases in the annealed state analyzed above. 
After solution treatment, the fracture surface changed significantly, 
the differences between the horizontal and vertical direction are no 
longer remarkable, both organized by a large number of dimples. At this 
time, the size of the dimples has increased significantly to about 5 μ m 
and inside which coarse precipitation particles are clearly visible. The 
enlargement and deepening of dimples imply that the plasticity of the 
alloy has been greatly improved. The morphology of the T6 state is 
basically the same as that of the solution treated state, with numerous 
precipitated particles inside the dimples as well as microcracks around 
it.

will ultimately affect the fracture mode. As it can be seen from Fig. 13

pool is a weak location for the fracture to occur. According to Fig. 9 , the 
SLM alloy exhibits a typical grain structure with interleaved distribution

process characteristics of SLM. During the process of layer-by-layer 
stacking, a large number of equiaxial crystals are formed at the bot -
tom of the molten pool due to the chilling effect from the alloy under -
neath, while columnar crystals grow along the direction of transmitted 
heat flow inside the molten pool. In tensile deformation, equiaxial grains 
at the boundary of the molten pool become the location where dislo -
cation accumulates and stacks, thus forming a place of stress concen -
tration. What ’ s more, the molten pool boundary is also the place where 
Fe-rich and Mn-rich phases precipitate, and these intermetallic com -
pounds also hinder the movement of dislocations and further form 
dislocation locks around them, which ultimately lead to crack initiation 
and expansion. However, the horizontal fracture ( Fig. 13 (a)) does not 
exhibit obvious melt channel characteristics since the molten pool 
boundary does not form a continuous plane in the transverse direction.

change due to the initial state of the precipitated phase basically 
retained, so the melt channel characteristics in the longitudinal fracture

dimples in high magnification image ( Fig. 13 (f)). After solid solution 
treatment, the dislocation accumulation and stress concentration inside 
the molten pool no longer show significant differences from the 
boundary region due to the coarsening of the equiaxial grains and the

higher temperature will lead to the fracture of the eutectic organization, 
thus account for the significant reduction in strength.
