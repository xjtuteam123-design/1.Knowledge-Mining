ABSTRACT 
Improving high-temperature strength and resistance to high-temperature softening is an important method to 
promote the application of high-performance Cu-Cr-Zr alloy in fields such as resistance welding electrodes and 
high-speed railway contact wires. A Cu-1.0Cr-0.4Zn-0.1Zr-0.05Si alloy was designed and the combining effects of 
Zn and Si elements on the microstructure and high-temperature mechanical properties of the alloy were studied. 
The tensile strength of the alloy at room temperature was 556 MPa, and it was 349 MPa at 500 ℃ with a softening 
temperature of 620 ℃ . The main strengthening phases of the alloy were submicron Cr
3
Si and nano-scaled Cr-rich 
precipitates. The Zn elements were uniformly solid-solved in the Cu matrix, and the addition of Zn and Si ele -
ments significantly retarded the phase transformation of the Cr-rich precipitates. Thermodynamics and kinetics 
analysis showed that Zn and Si elements promoted the dispersive precipitation of the nano-scaled FCC coherent 
Cr-rich precipitates by reducing the nucleation energy barrier, while the Si and Zr elements inhibited the 
coarsening of the Cr-rich precipitates by enriching at the phase boundaries, effectively impeding dislocation 
motion and grain boundary migration, which mainly contributed to good high-temperature strength and resis -
tance to softening of the Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy. 
1. Introduction 
Cu-Cr-Zr alloys have been widely used in various fields, such as in -
tegrated circuit lead frames, resistance welding electrodes, overhead 
conductors for electric locomotives, and thrust chambers of rocket en -
gine, due to their high strength, good electrical and thermal conductivity 
[1,2] . In different complex service environments, Cu-Cr-Zr alloys are 
subjected to large dynamic loads and resistance to frictional wear while 
playing a role in controlling high current transmission, generating a 
large amount of Joule heat, arc burn and material transfer, which seri -
ously affects the service performance [3 – 5] . 
Take resistance welding electrodes as an example, which must 
withstand pressure load and dissipate heat during the welding. While 
the conductivity of traditional Cu-Cr-Zr electrode reaches more than 
75% IACS, the hardness reaches 140 – 170 HV, and the softening tem -
perature is 550 ℃ [6] , they have a short lifespan when welding coated 
steel sheets and aluminum alloys due to high-temperature softening, 
deformation, and adhesion [7] . Therefore, it is important to further 
improve the high-temperature softening resistance and high- 
temperature strength of Cu-Cr-Zr alloy while maintaining its suffi -
ciently high electrical conductivity. 
High strength of Cu-Cr-Zr alloy primarily results from the dispersed 
nano-scaled Cr-rich precipitates during the aging, which is a charac -
teristic of typical precipitation strengthening alloys. The rapid migration 
of grain boundaries of the matrix grains and the coarsening of pre -
cipitates are the main reasons for the decrease in softening resistance 
and high-temperature strength [8 – 10] . However, Zr atoms tend to 
segregate on the surface of the precipitates, which hinders the coars -
ening and enhances the anti-aging properties [11] . In addition, Zr-rich 
phase at grain boundaries improves the strength of grain boundaries 
[12] . 
Sun et al. [13] revealed that Ag element improved the recrystalli -
zation activation energy and delayed the recovery and recrystallization 
of the Cu-0.2Cr-0.12Ag (wt.%) alloy, leading to the increase of the 
softening temperature from 500 ~ 525 ℃ to 550 ℃ . Guo et al. [8] re -
ported that the Cu-0.47Cr-0.16Nb (wt.%) alloy had a high-temperature

through solid solution strengthening [17] , while the addition of Si 
element can improve the mechanical properties of Cu-Cr-Zr alloys at

unclear. Additionally, both Zn and Si elements can serve as deoxidizers 
in Cu alloys melting to remove oxygen from the melt and improve the 
quality of Cu alloy ingots [19 – 21] . In this work, Zn and Si were chosen as 
the micro-alloying elements of the Cu-1.0Cr-0.1Zr (wt.%) alloy. The 
effects of Zn and Si elements on the mechanical properties and micro -
structure at room temperature and high temperature were studied. 
Additionally, the mechanism of the combining effect of Zn and Si ele -
ments on improving the high-temperature mechanical properties of the 
Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy was revealed. 
2. Experimental method 
Cu-1.0Cr, Cu-1.0Cr-0.1Zr and Cu-1.0Cr-0.4Zn-0.1Zr-0.05Si (wt.%) 
ingots were prepared using an intermediate frequency induction melting 
furnace. The raw materials included electrolytic copper (99.95 wt%), 
Cu-10Cr (wt.%) intermediate alloy, Cu-40Zr (wt.%) intermediate alloy, 
pure silicon (99.95 wt%), and pure zinc (99.95 wt%). The melting 
temperature was 1350 ℃ , and the melting holding time was about 5 min. 
The composition of the three ingots was determined by inductively 
coupled plasma atomic emission spectroscopy (ICP-AES) and the results 
are shown in Table 1 . 
The ingots were subjected to homogenization annealing at 960 ℃ for 
4 h. The homogenized ingots were then hot-rolled with a cumulative 
deformation of 80% at 900 ℃ . The hot-rolled plates were solution 
treated at 960 ℃ for 2 h and quenched by water-cooling. The solution- 
treated plates were then cold-rolled with a deformation of 70% and 
then aged at 400 ℃ , 450 ℃ , and 500 ℃ . 
The HV-1000 Vickers hardness tester was used to test the hardness of 
the samples under different aging conditions, with a test load of 500 g 
and a dwell time of 15 s. For each sample, five points of raw data were 
tested and the average value was taken as the final result. The QJ36s-DC 
direct current resistance tester with temperature correction function was 
used to measure the resistance values of the alloy under different aging 
conditions. The sample size was 50 mm × 3 mm. Each sample was tested 
three times, and the average value was taken to calculate the conduc -
tivity. The peak aging samples of the three components were placed at 
500 ℃ ~700 ℃ for 1 h for heating and then cooled by air to measure the 
softening temperature. The RWS50 tensile testing machine with heating 
function was used for room-temperature tensile testing and high- 
temperature tensile testing (400 ℃ , 450 ℃ , 500 ℃ , 550 ℃ , 600 ℃ ). The 
tensile specimens were cut along the rolling direction of the plate, and 
the tensile rate was 0.5 mm/min. Three repeated experiments were 
conducted under each testing condition. Before the high-temperature 
tensile test, the furnace temperature was first raised to the target tem -
perature, and then the sample was assembled and kept for 15 min before 
the tensile test was conducted. 
The SEM images were obtained using a TESCAN MIRA3 field emis -
sion scanning electron microscope, with an operating voltage of 20 kV, 
and the samples were mechanically polished prior to observation. TEM 
samples were prepared by cutting disks with a diameter of 3 mm and a 
thickness of 120 μ m, which were then polished to a thickness of 50 – 70 
μ m. The TEM samples were prepared using an RL-4 electro-polishing 
device at 35 ℃ and 10 V. The microstructure of the samples was 
observed using a TITAN G260-300 transmission electron microscope. 
The 0.5 mm × 0.5 mm × 20 mm needle-shaped 3D-APT samples were 
prepared using a two-step electro-polishing. The 3D-APT measurements 
were conducted using a LEAP 5000 XR electron probe microanalyzer 
(CAMECA), with a laser pulse repetition rate of 200 kHz, pulse fraction 
of 15%, sampling rate of 0.5%, and a testing temperature of 243 ℃ . 
The data analysis software used was IVAS 3.8.0.

3.2. Tensile properties 
Tensile tests were carried out on the alloys aged at 450 ℃ for 30 min 
under different temperature conditions (20 ℃ , 400 ℃ , 450 ℃ , 500 ℃ , 
550 ℃ , 600 ℃ ). As shown in Fig. 3 , the tensile strength of the Cu-Cr-Zn- 
Zr-Si alloy reached 556 MPa at room temperature, which was higher 
than that (529 MPa) of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy and much higher than that 
(428 MPa) of the Cu-Cr alloy. In addition, the elongation to failure of the 
Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy was 11.2%, slightly higher than that (10.1%) of the 
Cu-Cr-Zr alloy, but lower than that (17.8%) of the Cu-Cr alloy. Fig. 3 (a-1 
~ a-3) show the fracture surfaces of the three alloys at room tempera -
ture. All three samples show obvious tear-type toughness dimples, with 
clearly visible tearing marks. The size and depth of the dimples on the 
fracture surface of the Cu-Cr alloy were larger than those of the other 
two samples, indicating that the Cu-Cr alloy had better ductility. The 
nucleation sites of dimple on the fracture surface of the Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si 
alloy were more and deeper than those of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy, indi -
cating that the grain size of the Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy was smaller and its 
ductility was greater. 
High-temperature tensile tests were carried out at 400 ℃ -600 ℃ , and 
the results are shown in Fig. 3 (b, c). At 450 ℃ and 500 ℃ , the tensile 
strength of the Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy was 381 MPa and 349 MPa, 
respectively, with the elongation of 17.5% and 18.7%, both higher than 
those of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy. When the temperature exceeded 500 ℃ , the 
tensile strength of the Cu-Cr and Cu-Cr-Zr alloys decreased significantly 
compared to that of Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy, as shown in Fig. 4 . At 600 ℃ , 
the elongation of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy was increased to ～ 30%, indicating 
a notable enhancement in the high-temperature softening rate. How -
ever, the elongation of the Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy remained ～ 20% at the 
same temperature, as showed in Fig. 3 (d).

3.5. 3D-APT analysis 
During the aging, the nucleation and growth of precipitates are 
driven by thermodynamics and the diffusion of elements. Different 
precipitates have different thermodynamic stable states [23,24] . To 
investigate the relationship between the distribution of elements and the 
size of precipitates in the matrix, 3D atom probe tomography (3D-APT) 
was performed on the Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy aged at 500 ℃ for 20 h, and 
the results are shown in Fig. 10 . 
As shown in Fig. 10 (a-e), the Zr and Si atoms were both segregated to 
the Cr-rich precipitates, while the Zn atoms were uniformly distributed 
in the copper matrix without obvious segregation. As shown in Fig. 10 (f, 
g, h), the ellipsoidal precipitates with an approximate size of 4 nm, the 
shuttle-shaped precipitates with a size of 9 nm, and the rod-shaped 
precipitates with a size of 15 nm were observed in the scanned region. 
These precipitates corresponded to FCC precipitates with the Cube-on- 
Cube OR in the early stage of precipitation and BCC precipitates with 
the N-W and K-S OR generated in the later stage of precipitation, 
respectively. The composition statistics and analysis were performed on 
these precipitates in the blue regions marked. As shown in Fig. 10 (i, j, k), 
the Cr content in the ellipsoidal precipitates with a size of about 4 nm 
was only 58 at.%, while the Cr content increased to 68 at.% and 85 at.% 
in the shuttle-shaped and rod-shaped precipitates with the sizes of 9 nm 
and 15 nm, respectively. It is worth noting that the statistical results 
show that there were fluctuations in the composition of Zr and Si ele -
ments near the interface between the Cr-rich precipitates and the copper 
matrix. With the increase in the size of precipitates, the enrichment

4. Discussion 
4.1. Effect of Si element on primary phases of Cu-Cr-Zr alloy 
In high-strength and high-conductivity (HSHC) Cu-Cr-Zr alloys, the 
Cr content ranges from 0.3 to 1.2 wt%, with the maximum solid solu -
bility of Cr in Cu being only 0.8 wt% [25] . Even if the amount of Cr 
added exceeds the maximum solubility, it is difficult to significantly 
enhance the precipitation strengthening effect of the material. The 
excess Cr will exist in the form of large-sized ( ≥ 0.5 μ m) Cr-rich primary 
phases in the matrix [22] , which are prone to produce stress concen -
tration in deformation, becoming the source of failure behaviors [26] . 
However, these primary phases can also play a role in pinning grain 
boundaries and refining grain size in recrystallization. Moreover, 
increasing the Cr content in the Cu-Cr-Zr alloys is beneficial for 
improving the arc extinguishing performance and extending their ser -
vice life when they serve as electrical contact materials [27] . In this 
study, the alloy composition had a Cr content of 1.0 wt%. Therefore, 
controlling the morphology and size of the Cr-rich primary phases is 
particularly important. 
Compared with Cr primary phases, Cr

4.3. Precipitation behavior

the Cr-rich precipitates in Cu-Cr-Zr alloys suggests that during the aging, 
with the extension of aging time, the precipitation sequence of the Cr- 
rich precipitates is as follows: supersaturated solid solution → GP 
zone → FCC Cr-rich precipitates with a Cube-on-Cube OR → BCC Cr-rich 
precipitates with an N-W OR → BCC Cr-rich precipitates with a K-S OR 
[37 – 39] . Furthermore, the shape and size of the Cr-rich precipitates 
correspond to its precipitation sequence. In this paper, the analysis of 
the precipitates in the Cu-Cr-Zr alloys is consistent with this view. The 
addition of Zn and Si elements to Cu-Cr-Zr alloys in this study has slowed 
down the transformation of the Cr-rich precipitates, as detailed in

Based on the microstructure in section 4.2 and the thermodynamic 
and kinetic calculations, the evolution of precipitates during the aging of 
the Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy was depicted to analyze the precipitation 
behavior of the Cr-rich precipitate, as shown in Fig. 13 . In the early stage 
of aging, the Cr atoms aggregated at the nucleation site, replacing the Cu 
atoms and maintaining the original FCC lattice structure. Meanwhile, Zr 
and Si atoms aggregated inside the Cr-rich precipitate, increasing the 
lattice parameter of the precipitate, which could rise the lattice 
mismatch between the precipitate and the matrix, lowering the nucle -
ation barrier of the FCC Cr-rich precipitate and promoting precipitation. 
With the prolongation of aging time, Cr atoms in the copper matrix 
continuously diffused into the Cr-rich precipitate, increasing the pro -
portion of Cr in the precipitate and promoting the transformation from 
the FCC structure to the BCC structure. Zr and Si atoms in the Cr-rich 
precipitate were replaced by newly diffused Cr atoms and were 
expelled to the interface between the precipitate and the matrix. Due to 
the difference in interdiffusion coefficients of different elements at high 
temperatures (T greater than 400 ℃ ) in a short time [48 – 50] , Zr and Si 
atoms could diffuse from the Cr-enriched region to the Cu-enriched re -
gion. However, when Zr and Si atoms reached the interface between the 
matrix and the precipitate, they were easily combined with vacancies at 
the phase boundary, resulting in Zr and Si atom segregation at the phase 
boundary. On the one hand, it effectively reduced the lattice mismatch 
between the matrix and the precipitate, alleviating the interface elastic 
distortion and reducing the driving force of the phase transformation. 
On the other hand, it impeded the diffusion of Cr atoms from the copper 
matrix into the precipitate, which delayed the phase transformation at 
high temperature. Consequently, it inhibited the coarsening of the Cr- 
rich precipitate and promoted the formation of thermally stable Cr- 
rich precipitates in the Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy. 
4.4. Combining effect of Zn and Si elements on high temperature 
mechanical properties of Cu-Cr-Zr alloy 
From the comparison of the properties of the three alloys in Section 
3.1 , it can be seen that compared to the Cu-Cr-Zr and Cu-Cr alloys, the 
Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy has higher hardness, room-temperature strength, 
high-temperature strength and resistance to high temperature softening 
(with a softening temperature of 620 ℃ ) while maintaining good con -
ductivity. This indicates that the composite addition of Zn and Si ele -
ments can significantly improve the high-temperature mechanical 
properties of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy, which is closely related to the formation 
of heat-resistant and stable precipitates with good dispersion in the Cu- 
Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy. 
Precipitation strengthening is the main strengthening mechanism of 
the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy. During aging, a large amount of Cr elements

precipitates, which effectively impeded dislocation movement. The

to the recrystallization behavior of the copper matrix. Cold rolling in -

provide driving force for the precipitation, recovery, and recrystalliza -

which lowered the alloy ’ s stacking fault energies, making it more 
difficult for dislocations to undergo rearrangement through cross-slip. 
Additionally, the added Zn element increased the rate of work hard -
ening of the alloy, forming more crystal defects such as vacancies and 
dislocations. It promoted the precipitation of the smaller and dispersed 
Cr-rich precipitates [53] , which also effectively inhibited the recovery 
and recrystallization [37] . Moreover, there were submicron-sized Cr

high-speed railway contact lines, promising a bright future. 
5. Conclusions 
(1) A Cu-1.0Cr-0.4Zn-0.1Zr-0.05Si alloy was designed, which was 
subjected to a solid solution treatment at 960 ℃ for 2 h, followed 
by 70% cold rolling and aging at 450 ℃ for 30 min. The alloy 
exhibited a hardness of 189 HV, a tensile strength of 556 MPa, an 
elongation of 11.2%, an electrical conductivity of 71.2% IACS, a 
softening temperature of 620 ℃ , and a tensile strength of 349 
MPa at 500 ℃ . The mechanical properties and heat resistance of 
the alloy were higher than those of Cu-1.0Cr and Cu-1.0Cr-0.1Zr 
alloys. 
(2) The precipitation sequence of Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy was G.P. zone 
→ coherent FCC Cr-rich phase with the Cube-on-Cube OR → 
coherent BCC Cr-rich phase with the N-W OR → semi-coherent 
BCC Cr-rich phase with the K-S OR. The addition of Zn and Si 
elements significantly delayed the phase transformation of the 
Cu-Cr-Zr alloy. 
(3) At the early stage of aging, some Si and Zr atoms in the Cu-Cr-Zn- 
Zr-Si alloy were distributed inside the Cr-rich precipitates, 
reducing nucleation energy barrier and promoting dispersive 
precipitation of the nano-scaled coherent FCC Cr-rich pre -
cipitates. With the extension of aging time, Si and Zr atoms in the 
Cr-rich precipitates diffused to the phase boundaries, reducing 
the interfacial energy and inhibiting the coarsening. 
(4) The addition of Zn element reduced the stacking fault energy of 
the Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy and delayed the recrystallization of the 
alloy. Meanwhile, the combination of trace Si addition and 
thermomechanical treatment induced formation of submicron 
Cr
3
Si particles and inhibited the coarsening of small Cr-rich 
precipitates under high-temperature condition. The combining 
effects of Zn and Si addition effectively impeded dislocation 
movement and grain boundary migration, which mainly 
contributed to good high-temperature strength and resistance to 
softening of the Cu-Cr-Zn-Zr-Si alloy.
