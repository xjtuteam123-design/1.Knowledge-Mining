1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) have been getting attention for the vast 
compositional space and unpredicted properties [ 1 – 3 ]. By inheriting the 
characters of refractory metals and HEAs, refractory high entropy alloys 
(RHEAs) have been substantiated as promising candidates for 
high-temperature applications due to the desirable thermal stability and 
mechanical properties [ 4 , 5 ]. Senkov et al. firstly reported NbMoTaW 
and NbMoTaWV RHEAs with the yield strength as high as 405 MPa and 
477 MPa at 1600 
◦
C which is far beyond the melting temperature of 
nickel-based superalloys [ 6 ]. At the same time, a recent study suggested 
that CrMoNbV RHEA retained the competitive yield strength (over 1000 
MPa) at the temperature of 1000 
◦
C [ 7 ]. 
However, poor room-temperature ductility faced by the most RHEAs 
seriously limits the engineering applications. As a result, some strategies 
were proposed to optimize the strength-ductility synergy for RHEAs. 
Transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) was employed to achieve 
excellent combination between strength and plasticity in Ta

TiZrHfNb RHEA, which enhanced strength and ductility simultaneously 
[ 10 ]. An et al. developed a high-strength and high-ductility HfNbTaTiV 
RHEA by combining the maximum lattice distortion, intrinsic ductility 
of elements and valence electron concentration (VEC) criterion [ 11 ]. 
Nevertheless, the number of reported ductile RHEAs is still limited at 
present. 
Herein, we aim to develop a ductile RHEA by selecting intrinsically 
ductile elements Hf, Nb, Ta and Ti. Beyond these, a small amount of Mo 
is also added to improve the strength by solid solution strengthening. As
