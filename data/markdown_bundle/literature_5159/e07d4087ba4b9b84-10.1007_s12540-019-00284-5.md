Abstract
The correlation among the strength, ductility and precipitates in a dual-phase Al0.5CoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy has been 
investigated. The property of plastic was improved in the recrystallized Al0.5CoCrFeNi HEA prepared as the master alloy. 
Formation of the stable nanosized L12 phases after aging at 650 °C was revealed, along with the lath-like BCC phases 
precipitated in the grain, as the primary contribution of the strength enhancement. Excellent balanced tensile properties at 
room temperature were achieved through aging treatment, as the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength show 2.68, 1.63 
times higher in 650 °C/140 h aged condition than that in initial states, respectively, superior to many high-entropy alloys 
and conventional alloys.

1 Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are defined as solid solutions 
consisting of multiple principle elements in an equimolar or 
near-equimolar ratio [1, 2]. Due to the high mixing entropy, 
HEAs often tend to form simple crystal structures such as 
FCC [1], BCC [2], or HCP [3, 4], instead of complex inter-
metallic phases and show unique properties [5 –9]. Up to 
date, all kinds of ways have been developed to enhanced the 
mechanical properties, like alloying, solid solution, or cold 
deformation, etc. [10– 12]. Previous studies have indicated 
that dislocations strengthening mainly leads to the high 
strength at the expense of plasticity, and fine grain strength-
ening may not enhance the strength substantially. Recently, 
researches adopted precipitation hardening combined with 
other strengthening methods to gain high entropy alloys with 
adjustable properties that were prospective for application.
Through the formation of the proper secondary pre-
cipitates in a matrix phase, the comprehensive mechanics 
performance has been greatly improved in HEAs [12– 18]. 
Liang et al. [18] has recently proposed an approach to design 
the precipitation-hardening high-entropy alloys (PH-HEAs) 
with ultrahigh tensile strength by phase separation. Zhao 
et al. [17] pointed out that the L1
2 precipitates possessed 
remarkable thermal stability, and showed tardiness during 
the coarsening in the (NiCoFeCr)
94Ti2Al4 alloy than that in 
many traditional Ni-based alloys. Gwalani et al. [13] dem-
onstrated that different experimental conditions like the 
initial state of the HEAs (as-cast or deformed), and aging 
time periods, may also cause the discrepancy in the precipi-
tate behavior and phase transformation, for the strengthen-
ing effect strongly depends on the thermal stability of the 
coherent precipitates, especially at elevated temperatures. He 
et al. [12] found that nanosized coherent rein-forcing phase 
can be obtained and extraordinary balanced tensile prop-
erties at room temperature were achieved mainly through 
precipitation strengthening. It is, thus, has attracted much 
attention and extensive efforts were made in the last decade.

In general, the casting process for HEAs usually produces 
a coarse microstructure and furthermore introduces cast 
defects. Hence, cold-rolling and subsequent annealing were 
adopted in present work, in order to eliminate cast defects 
and obtain the fine equiaxed grains as well. In the present 
paper, we demonstrate that the ordered L1

its tensile ductility.
2 Experimental Procedures
The Al0.5CoCrFeNi alloy ingots are prepared by induction-
melting with the mixture of Al, Co, Cr, Fe and Ni (purity 
larger than 99.9%) in a vacuum of 0.05 MPa backfilled 
with pure argon. The melting process is held at 1550 °C for 
15 min, and the liquid metal is then casted into a steel mold 
with upper inner diameter of 140 mm, bottom inner diameter 
of 130 mm, and a length of 180 mm.
The plates with dimensions of 70 × 20 × 8 mm
3 obtained 
from the master alloy are cold rolled to a thickness reduc-
tion of 70%. Samples annealed at 1200 °C for 1 h are aged 
at 650 °C for 4 h and 140 h, hereinafter named 650-4 and 
650-140, respectively. Microstructure of the cold-rolled 
and annealed alloys is studied from the longitudinal plane 
(RD × ND plane) by JSM-6390 scanning electron micro -
scope (SEM) equipped with energy dispersive X-ray spec-
trometry (EDS, JEOL, JSM-6390, Tokyo, Japan). The crys-
talline structure is characterized by a D8 ADVANCE X-ray 
diffraction instrument (XRD, Bruker, Germany). Standard 
bright and dark field images are obtained with a FEI Tec-
nai G2 F30 Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM). Dog 
bone-shaped tensile specimens are prepared along the RD 
direction for room temperature tensile test with a gauge 
length of 14 mm, a width of 2 mm and a thickness around 
1.5 mm. Uniaxial tensile tests are carried out with a MTS 
SANS CMT5105 testing machine at room temperature with 
strain rate of 10
−3 s−1.
3 Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructures
A fully recrystallized sample with small grain size was 
chosen to investigate the potential influence of precipita-
tion strengthening, as shown in Fig.  2a. The XRD results 
obtained from both initial state and experimental state are 
shown in Fig. 1. All samples consist of FCC and BCC struc-
ture, which is consistent with previous research [19, 20]. 
Figure  2b–f show the BSE images of the Al
0.5CoCrFeNi 
alloy after aging at different temperatures. The alloy is 
composed of dendritic microstructures, where the dendritic 
region is the FCC phases while the interdendritic region is 
the BCC phases. At 650 °C, abundant lath-like particles are 
formed in the matrix as shown in Fig. 2f. 
A summary of TEM results from both the 650-4 and 650-
140 sample are shown in Fig.  3. The dark field image in 
Fig. 3a exhibits precipitation of L1
2 phases forming within 
the matrix, as can be inferred from the presence of extra 
superlattice reflections at {001} and {011} positions in the 
 B
fcc = [011] SAED pattern. The lath-like phase precipitating 
sparsely in the matrix is identified to be the BCC phase in 
Fig. 3c, d, and the corresponding SAED pattern indicates a 
definite Kurdjumov–Sachs (K–S) type orientation relation 
between these BCC precipitates and the parent FCC matrix. 
Figure 3e shows the nano-precipitates in the dendritic region 
of the 650-140 sample. After longer aging time, the precipi-
tates distribute more homogeneously in the grain. The L1
2 
phases grow into a lamellar structure, which can be clearly 
seen in Fig. 3i as the white arrow refers to, certified by the 
superlattice spot shown in Fig. 3f, inducing substantial low-
misfit nanoscale interfaces between the FCC matrix and 
ordered L1
2 phases. A representative high resolution TEM 
(HRTEM) image and corresponding Fast Fourier Transform 
(FFT) of the disordered FCC matrix and ordered L1
2 phases 
is given in Fig. 4, showing the coherent relationship between 
precipitates and matrix. Further, the dark field image and 
corresponding SAED pattern are shown in Fig.  3g, h, indi-
cating that long time period aging treatment is propitious 
for the precipitation and uniform distribution of the BCC 
phases in the matrix. A HRTEM image showing the inter -
face between a particle and the FCC matrix is presented in 
Fig. 3j.

3.2 Mechanical Properties
The tensile mechanical behavior of the aged Al0.5CoCrFeNi 
high entropy alloy samples is shown in Fig. 5. The increase 
in both yield strength (YS) and ultimate tensile strength 
(UTS) is evident from the stress–strain plots. The YS 
increases from ~ 365 to ~ 725 MPa and ~ 980 MPa, and the 
corresponding UTS increases from ~ 783 to ~ 1061 MPa 
and ~ 1283  MPa in 650-4, 650-140 samples, respec-
tively. Compared with the as-cast [19] and recrystallized 
 Al
0.5CoCrFeNi alloy, the UTS increases by 37% as well as 
the plasticity increases from 33.7 to 49.9%, with a little loss 
of the YS. A superior combination of strength and plasticity 
is obtained after aging at 650 °C for 140 h, with a tensile 
elongation of 25.6%. The data used in Fig.  6 refers to ten-
sile tests and thus refined grain size together with precipita-
tion enable stronger coupling effects in the enhancement of 
the tensile properties [12, 20–26]. The excellent integrated 
tensile properties of aging hardened Al
0.5CoCrFeNi HEA 
can be attributed to the strain field causing by the abundant 
low-misfit coherent interfaces and hard and brittle BCC 
particles for strength. Such an interesting phenomenon is 
also reported in other HEAs like Al
0.3CoCrFeNi [27], and 
 Al0.5Cr0.9FeNi2.5V0.2 [18]. Gwalani et al. [27] reported that 
strained single-phase FCC structure when aged at 620 °C 
for 50 h resulted in a tri-phase mixture of FCC + B2 + sigma 
phase. Abundant fine-grain boundaries in recrystallized 
regions provide heterogeneous nucleation sites for both 
the B2 and sigma phases, and this tri-phase microstructure 
spreads across the entire grain with increasing aging period. 
Especially, based on an innovating alloying strategy of high 
entropy alloy, Liang et al. [18] had successfully developed 
ultrastrong and ductile HEAs by using different strength-
ening mechanism. A coherent nanostructure combining a 
disordered FCC matrix with high-content ductile Ni
3Al-type 
ordered nanoprecipitates was obtained, which contributes to 
a strength increasing and achieving one of the highest tensile 
strength (1.9 GPa) among all bulk HEAs reported previ-
ously. Precipitation behavior study of Al
0.5CoCrFeNi HEAs 
in this study suggests the strength increased dramatically 
in both 650-4 and 650-140 samples can owe to the BCC 
phase, and the L1
2 phase dispersed in the grains, inducing 
substantial low-misfit nanoscale interfaces that resist dislo-
cation motion effectively. Note that the plasticity remained 
after long time period aging treatment, mainly due to the 
ordered nano-sized L1
2 precipitates, which is ductile and 
low-misfit with the matrix.

strengthening with the precipitation strengthening, was car-

shows a great potential for precipitation in subsequent aging 
treatment. Plasticity increased from 33.7 to 49.9%, with a
