Abstract 
A novel CoNiFeNb 
0.45 eutectic multi-principal element alloy (EMPEA) wit h a 
dual-phase structure is designed and synthesized by arc melting and suction casting. The 
compression strength of the CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA is 2.5 GPa at room temperature and high 
compression strength (1.2 GPa), furthermore, the ex cellent ductility with 40% fracture strain 
are maintained at 700 °C. The dislocation walls in the soft fcc phase provide the 
CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA with high ductility and work hardening, whil e the dislocations pined 
in the fcc/laves phase boundaries provide the alloy with high strength. The CoNiFeNb 0.45

1. Introduction 
Multi-component alloys have aroused interests due t o their unique properties [1-7]. 
According to configurational entropy, multi-principal element alloy (MPEA) can be classified 
as high entropy alloys (HEAs) and medium entropy al loys (MEAs) [8]. MPEAs have 
extraordinary mechanical and functional properties such as strength and fracture toughness at 
cryogenic temperature [3, 9], temperature resistance [10], oxidation resistance [11], creep and 
fatigue resistance [12], corrosion resistance [13], as well as magnetic properties [14]. It has 
also been shown that the cryogenic properties of MEAs are better than those of HEAs [15-17]. 
However, most MPEAs have weak liquidity and casting ability due to the high entropy 
effects of multiple elements. From the practical s tandpoint, alloys possessing excellent 
mechanical properties especially at high temperatur e together with casting ability are highly 
desirable. Eutectic alloys have few segregation an d shrinkage cavities because there is no 
solidification temperature region and some eutectic high entropy alloys (EHEAs) with 
excellent casting ability and mechanical properties have been proposed [18-20]. So far, 
most EHEAs have been produced by trial-and-error ex periments or serendipitously [18, 21]. 
Although some new strategies have been proposed to prepare EMPEAs, for instance, by

mixing the enthalpy of equimolar binary alloys [20] and CALPHAD (calculation of phase 
diagrams) [19], in-depth investigations are lacking and especially, there have been few

work, based on the design of EHEAs proposed by Jian g [19], a novel CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA 
with a dual phase structure is designed by taking a dvantage of the binary eutectic phase using

2. Materials and Methods 
The eutectic CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA composed of Co, Ni, Fe, and Nb (99.96 wt%) wa s 
prepared by arc melting under high-purity argon gas . The sample was melted under 
electromagnetic stirring at least five times to ens ure homogeneity and cylindrical samples 6 
mm in diameter were obtained by suction casting usi ng a copper mold followed by 
homogenization at 1000 °C for 24 h. 
The phase composition of the samples was determined on the D8 Discover X-ray 
diffractometer (XRD) at 40 kV / 30 mA from 20° to 9 0° at a rate of 8°/min. The 
microstructure and alloying element distribution we re determined by scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM), energy-dispersive X-ray spectrome try (EDS), and transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM). The compression and high-tempera ture tensile tests were conducted on 
the CMT5105 at a strain rate of 10 -3 s-1. The compression specimens with a diameter of 6 
mm and thickness of 12 mm and uniaxial tensile spec imens with a gauge length of 10 mm 
were cut from the as-cast alloy bar by electrical d ischarge machining using the universal

electronic tensile testing machine. The isothermal compression tests were carried out on the 
Gleeble 3000 thermo-simulator at 600 °C, 700 °C, and 800 °C with a strain rate of 10 -3 s-1 and 
the maximum logarithmic strain( ε) was 0.5. The specimens were heated to the deform ation 
temperature at a constant heating rate of 5°C /s an d maintained for 300 s to stabilize the 
temperature. Afterwards, the specimens were quench ed in cold water to retain the 
recrystalline microstructure. 
The electrochemical measurements were conducted on the vesaSTAT electrochemical 
workstation. The conventional three-electrode cell comprised the alloy, platinum electrode, 
and Ag/AgCl (SSE) as the working electrode, auxiliary electrode, and reference electrode, 
respectively. The samples with a cross section of 1 cm 2 were ground with SiC paper (P2000 
grit). Potentiodynamic polarization was carried ou t at the potential range between -1 mV 
and 2 mV at a scanning rate of 1 mV/s and the test was performed 5 times to improve the 
statistics. 
 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1 Design of EMPEAs 
According to the binary enthalpy tables , the negative mixing enthalpies of Nb with Co, 
Fe, and Ni are -16, -25, and -30 KJ/ mol, respectiv ely, making it possible to form eutectic 
alloys. In this respect, CoNiFeNb x EHEAs are designed by taking advantage of the binary 
eutectic phase constituted by Co, Fe, Ni, and Nb wh ere x is shown as follows: 
(1/3)Ni 84.5 Nb 15.5 + (1/3)Co 86.1 Nb 13.9 + (1/3)Fe 89.4 Nb 10.6 = Co 28.7 Ni 28.5 Fe 29.8 Nb 13 ≈ 
CoNiFeNb 0.45 . To evaluate the effectiveness of this method, th ermodynamic calculation is

performed using the JMatPro software to determine t he eutectic phase and temperature of the 
alloy as shown in Fig. 1a. The phase diagram of th e CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA shows that the 
eutectic phases are fcc (face-centered cubic) and L aves and the eutectic temperature is 
1230 °C. The theoretical results are consistent wi th the XRD (Fig. 1b) and DSC data (Fig. 
1c). According to the phase diagram, the primary f cc phase starts to nucleate from the 
liquid metal at the solidification temperature of 1 250 °C and as the temperature drops to 
1230 °C, a solid composed of the fcc and Laves phas es is formed. A classical columnar 
eutectic microstructure is formed in the CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA and Fig. 1d illustrates the 
eutectic transformation.

the compression strength and ductility of the untre ated sample are weaker. The distribution 
of the constituent elements is relatively random in the untreated one thereby resulting in 
fluctuations in the concentration waves in the regi ons. The dislocations are pinned in these 
areas leading to forming dislocation walls (Fig.S3( a)). The reason why the homogenized 
CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA possesses superb mechanical properties such as strength and ductility 
are shown in Figs. S3(b) to (d). Numerous dislocat ions emerge from in the soft fcc phase 
and the interface of the fcc phase, but few disloca tions can be found from the hard / brittle 
laves phase. Besides, with increasing dislocation densities, overlapping and propagating of 
dislocations lead to the formation of dislocation w alls in the fcc phase. The dislocation 
walls in the soft fcc phase provides the CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA with high ductility and work 
hardening, whereas the dislocations pined in the fc c/laves phase boundaries produce high 
strength. 
 The specific energy of the interface is usually hi gh and increases with dissimilarity in 
the phase. There is a large gap in the interface s pecific energy of soft fcc phase and brittle 
laves. So this high energy between dual phase is f ormation a barrier to 
dislocations(Fig.S3d). Although the laves phase is not a dislocation “carrier”, it can serve as 
obstacles for dislocation migration. The stress fi elds surrounding the laves phase can be 
antagonistic and block propagation of the stress fi eld of migrating dislocations. Hence, the 
laves phase acts as the “pinning point”. It has be en shown that the laves phase is a brittle 
one but possesses high strength at high temperature [23-25]. The high compressive strength 
of 810 MPa observed from the CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA is preserved at even 800 °C due to the 
strength and stability of the laves phase at elevat ed temperature. The Nano-laves phases

4. Conclusion 
A novel CoNiFeNb 0.45 eutectic medium entropy alloy with a dual phase st ructure was 
designed by taking advantage of binary eutectic pha se using the JMat-Pro software. The 
eutectic phases are fcc and Laves. The eutectic tem perature was1230 °C ,which are

## Page 18

16 
 
corroborated by XRD and DSC. The CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA possesses superior mechanical 
properties including high compression strength and ductility. The maximum compression 
strength of the homogenized sample was 2.5 GPa and fracture strain was close to 50%. The 
high compression strength (1.2GPa) and excellent du ctility with 40% fracture strain of 
CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA sample are maintained at 700 °C. The high c ompressive strength of 
810 MPa of the CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA was preserved at even 800 °C due to the stre ngth and 
stability of the laves phase at high temperature. Electrochemical tests disclosed that the 
corrosion current density ( icoor ) increases with the solution temperature and icoor was sensitive 
to the temperature. The alloy has excellent corros ion resistance in 3.5 wt% NaCl at room 
temperature and the corrosion rate is 0.003 mm/y. All in all, the CoNiFeNb 0.45 EMPEA 
possesses excellent casting ability and corrosion r esistance boding well for industrial 
application.
