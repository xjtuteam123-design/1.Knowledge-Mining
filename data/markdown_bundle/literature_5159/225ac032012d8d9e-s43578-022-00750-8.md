Ni‑based single crystal (SX) superalloys with low specific weight are essential for developing aero engine 
turbine blades with great strength to weight ratios. To develop such alloys, numerical analysis methods 
for specific weight, the volume fraction of γ′ phase and elemental partitioning coefficients between 
γ and γ′ phases are collected and analyzed based on literature data of Ni‑based superalloys. Specific 
weight can be well predicted based on the weight percentage of each element, and tungsten has the 
most significant effect. Rhenium has the greatest elemental partitioning coefficient which might provide 
a strengthening effect for the γ matrix. Thus, we design four model alloys with rhenium but without 
tungsten for experimental validation with the aid of thermodynamic calculation. It is found that after 
solid solution and aging treatments, four alloys all have required typical γ/γ′ two phase microstructure, 
and specific weight is all lower than 8.4 g/cm3.
Introduction
Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys are widely used for 
aero engine turbine blades due to their excellent mechanical 
properties and oxidation resistance at high temperatures [1–3]. 
Superior properties originate from their special γ/γ ′ two phase 
structure and a large amount of alloying elements, in which Cr, 
Co, Re, Mo, etc., are γ matrix strengtheners, and Ta, Ti, Nb, etc., 
are γ′ precipitation strengtheners [4 ]. In recent decades, with 
increasing requirements for higher performance aero engines 
and thus, Ni-based SX superalloys with higher temperature 
capability, six generation alloys have been developed (tempera-
ture capability between each generation is about 30 °C). It is 
found that adding refractory elements (Re + Ta + W + Mo + Ru) 
is beneficial for temperature capability; Thus, higher generation 
superalloys generally contain more refractory elements [5 –7]. 
For example, the first generation superalloys contain an aver -
age of 11 wt% refractory elements, while the refractory con -
centration of fifth generation TMS-162 is as high as 26.3 wt%, 
leading to about 110 °C higher temperature capability [1 , 8]. 
Generally, refractory elements are also heavy elements, resulting 
in a higher specific weight of high generation superalloys. For 
example, the specific weight of the first generation superalloy 
is normally lower than 8.7 g/cm 3 [5], while the specific weight 
of the fifth generation TMS-162 reaches up to 9.12 g/cm3 [7].
It should be noted that the mass of turbine blades exerts 
a significant effect on the overall weight of aircraft [9 ]. For a 
given aircraft, a reduction in specific weight of turbine blades 
enables increasing the strength to weight of aero engines, also 
enables reducing the weight of other parts throughout the tur -
bine rotor, including the disk, hub, and shaft, as well as sup-
porting structures [1 , 10]. Researchers have proposed several 
methods for designing SX alloys with low specific weight. By 
using a Design of Experiments approach, Mackay et al. [10, 11] 
select a base alloy 6.1Al–12Mo–6.2Ta–Ni (Bal.) (wt%), modify 
concentrations of Cr, Co, Mo, Re, and obtain seven low spe -
cific weight Ni-based SX superalloys. After comparing creep 
properties, microstructural stability, and the resistance of cyclic 
oxidation and hot salt corrosion of the seven alloys, they have 
proposed that low specific weight can be achieved by lowering 
Re concentration, increasing Mo concentration, and removing

refractory elements, especially heavy elements (Re, W , Mo, Ta, 
etc.). However, refractory elements are essential to enhance the 
temperature capability. Therefore, balancing refractory elements 
concentration and high temperature mechanical properties is 
of great importance for designing alloys with high performance 
and low specific weight.
No matter how the compositions and specific weight of six 
generation superalloys vary, they possess similar typical γ/γ′ 
two phase microstructure after heat treatments [ 1], which is 
of the most crucial microstructure features for all Ni-based 
SX superalloys. Thus, designing low specific weight Ni-based 
SX superalloys still needs to pay attention to the partitioning 
coefficient of elements, γ ′ volume fraction, size and shape of γ′ 
precipitations, and other microstructural parameters. In addi-
tion, the partitioning behavior of alloying elements between 
two phases is very different, which would determine the proper-
ties of the two phases under service conditions [16]. Therefore, 
elements that can effectively participate in and strengthen the 
weaker phase at high temperature can still be carefully added 
to the alloy because the total amounts of heavy elements can 
be reduced.
Ni-based SX superalloys have been developed for more 
than 40 years, and there are large amounts of data on compo-
sitions, microstructural parameters, and mechanical proper -
ties [17– 22]. And various numerical analysis models are also 
adapted to explain those data, which can effectively provide 
some guidelines for developing new alloys; however, the quali -
ties of those numerical models are varied. Here, we first analyze 
numerical models for specific weight, the volume fraction of γ ′ 
phase, and elemental partitioning coefficients between γ and γ′ 
phases based on literature data of Ni-based superalloys. Based 
on the valued model prediction, we design four model alloys 
and experimentally fabricate and heat treat them to validate the 
important microstructural features as well as the specific weight, 
which might provide microstructural and compositional guide-
lines for the development of Ni-based SX superalloys with low 
specific weight.
Numerical models of specific weight 
and microstructure
The microstructure of Ni-based SX superalloys is relatively simple, 
which is a typical γ/γ′ two phase structure, including two impor-
tant characteristic parameters: γ′ volume fraction and elemental 
partitioning coefficient. Besides the parameters related to micro-

for applications. Many numerical models in the literature describe 
these parameters [17–22], but their accuracy varies. To evaluate 
these models, we collect data from nearly one hundred Ni-based

Alloy design principle
When designing a Ni-based SX superalloy with low specific 
weight, how to maintain or guarantee the mechanical properties 
of the alloy at high temperatures also needs to be considered. Ding 
and coauthors [25] recently studied the mechanical properties of a

second generation SX superalloy, concluding that the strength of 
the γ phase is much weaker than γ′ phase at temperatures higher 
than 850 °C. Hence, strengthening the γ phase, which is the FCC 
solid solution phase, is necessary to improve the mechanical prop-
erties of Ni-based SX superalloys.
According to the solid solution strengthening theory [26], 
the γ phase can be strengthened by two mechanisms, namely, 
modulus mismatch and atomic size mismatch. Considering the 
modulus mismatch between refractory strengthening elements 
and Ni, the order of strengthening effect of refractory elements 
is Re > Ru > W > Mo > Cr > Ta [27]. Considering atomic size mis-
match, the order of strengthening effect of refractory elements is 
Ta > W > Mo > Re > Ru > Cr [28]. In combination, Re, W , and Mo 
are the most important refractory elements used to strengthen the 
solid solution phase.
Because of the two phase structure of Ni-based SX superalloy, 
solid solution strengthening elements with large partitioning coef-
ficients can effectively distribute into the γ matrix to strengthen 
it. Among strengthening elements with kγ /γ′
> 1 (Fig. 3), Re has 
the largest partitioning coefficient. Recent research indicates that 
the solid solution strengthening effect of 1 wt% Re is equivalent to 
2.2 wt% Mo or 4.3 wt% W [16]. Although Re has a larger effect on 
increasing the specific weight of alloy, Re can still be added to the 
alloy because its quantity can be reduced to reach the same strength-
ening effects. In addition, Cr and Co can further benefit mechanical 
properties of the γ phase by reducing the stacking fault energy of the 
γ matrix [29]. Therefore, we design several alloys with ~ 3 wt% Re, 
without W to conduct experimental validation. Concentrations of 
Cr, Co, and Mo are maintained at 5 wt%, 15 wt%, and 6 wt%, respec-
tively. Concentrations of Al, Ta, and Ti is adjusted to guarantee the 
volume fraction of γ′ phase between 60 and 80%.

Determination of heat treatment processes
To eliminate the compositional and microstructural segregation, 
as cast alloys need to be solid solution treated. The γ′ solvus 
and initial melting temperatures of LD alloys are determined 
by analyzing differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) heating 
curves to determine the solid solution windows, which are listed 
in Table 2. Generally, higher temperature and longer treatment 
time in solid solution treatment are beneficial to eliminate the 
composition segregation, because solid solution treatment is 
a diffusion controlled process. However, melting of the local 
eutectic region limits the highest temperature for solid solution 
treatment. Based on the DSC results, a process of 1290 °C/2 
h + 1305 °C/2 h + 1320 °C/4 h/AC is selected as solid solution 
treatment for the four alloys. And two step aging treatment 
(1080 °C/4 h/AC + 870 °C/20 h/AC) is used to generate typical 
γ/γ′ two phase microstructure [30].
Microstructures
Figure 5 shows scanning electron microscope (SEM) micrographs 
of the four LD alloys. To reflect the microstructures of SX superal-
loys, all SEM images are captured in grains with [001] orientation, 
which is confirmed by electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) 
inverse pole figures (insets of Fig. 5). In addition, the γ′ particle 
sizes are measured, and average edge sizes of γ′ particle are listed 
in Table 3. The γ′ particle sizes of LD361, LD062, LD371, and 
LD072 alloys are 274 nm, 291 nm, 242 nm, and 271 nm, respec-
tively. It can be seen that the reduction of Al concentration about 
1 wt% decreases γ′ particle sizes by ~ 30 nm for alloys LD361 and 
LD371, and ~ 20 nm for alloys LD062 and LD072.

Shape of γ′ particles
The cubic degree of γ′ particle is represented by shape param-
eter η, which is defined schematically in Supplementary Fig. 
S1. Parameter A is the linear length of a γ′ particle edge, and 
parameter B is the distance between the two parallel edges. Thus, 
if the γ′ particle is perfectly cubic, η is 1; If the γ′ particle is 
perfectly spherical, η is 0 [31]. The shape parameters η of LD 
alloys are measured by using Image J software, and the results are 
also listed in Table 3. The average shape parameters η of LD361 
(0.64) and LD062 (0.60) are lower than that of LD371 (0.76) and 
LD072 (0.80), which indicate that the γ′ morphology of LD371 
and LD072 alloy is more cubic. Hence, increasing 1 wt% Al can 
effectively increase the cubic degree of the γ′ particle.

Summary and conclusion
To develop Ni-based  SX superalloys with low specific weight, 
numerical analysis models for specific weight, the volume frac-
tion of γ′ phase and elemental partitioning coefficients between γ 
and γ′ phases are collected and analyzed based on literature data. 
Based on the selected model prediction and thermodynamic cal-
culation, four compositions are designed and manufactured as the 
model alloys to validate the important microstructural features. 
The following conclusions can be drawn:
(1) Specific weight for Ni-based superalloys is relatively 
easy to be predicted according to the concentration of 
each alloying element based on four numerical models. 
All four models have an R2 larger than 0.82. Among 
them, Zhang model has the highest R2. Those numeri-
cal models show that the amounts of Re, W , and Ta 
are the elements that most significantly influence the 
specific weight of alloys.
(2) Logunov model can well reflect the influence of dif-
ferent elements on the γ′ volume fraction. Among all 
elements, Al and Ti have the largest effects on the γ′ 
volume fraction.
(3) For elements partitioning into γ phase, Re has the 
highest partitioning coefficient (12.2 in average) and W 
has the lowest value (1.4 in average), indicating that Re 
strongly segregates into γ phase.
(4) The specific weight of model alloys is below 8.4 g/cm3, 
which all exhibit typical γ/γ′ two phase structure of 
Ni-based SX superalloy, with γ′ size about 240-290 nm, 
after solid solution and aging treatment.
(5) The volume fraction of γ′ phase ranges from 70 to 80% 
in four alloys. Meanwhile, the shapes of γ′ particles are 
all cubic. As Al concentration increases from 6 to 7 
wt%, the volume fraction of γ′ is increased, the γ′ sizes 
are reduced while the γ′ particles become more cubic.
(6) The elements with the highest partitioning coefficient 
in all four LD model alloys are Re, although partition-
ing behavior of Re is strongly affected by other alloying 
elements, such as Ti and Ta. After replacing 1 wt% 
Ti with 3 wt% Ta, the partitioning coefficient of Re is 
increased.
Methods and experiments
Alloy fabrication and processing
Ingots were prepared by arc melting pure Ni, Co, Mo, Re, Ti, 
Ta, Al, and Cr (purity > 99.9 wt%) in an argon atmosphere. To 
ensure thorough mixing of all elements, a two step melting 
method was adopted. The refractory elements (including Re, 
Mo, Ta) and a certain proportion of Ni were first melted to form 
an intermediate alloy and then remelted with remaining ele-
ments. At each step, the alloy buttons were flipped and remelted 
more than five times. The arc melted alloys were then drop cast 
into a water chilled copper mold to produce round rods with 
12.5 mm in diameter and 120 mm in length.
The DSC technique was used to experimentally measure 
the γ′ solvus and melting temperatures. Based on the measured 
phase transition temperature, the as cast rods were solid solu-
tion treated in a stepwise process [1290 °C/2 h + 1305 °C/2 h + 
1320 °C/4 h/air cooling (AC)] to eliminate the compositional/
microstructural inhomogeneities, and then aged by two step 
process (1080 °C/4 h/AC + 870 °C/20 h/AC) to obtain the typical 
well defined γ/γ′ two phase microstructures. All heat treatments 
were carried out in argon.
The actual specific weight for the alloy after heat treatment 
was measured by the Archimedean method, experimental 
details can be found elsewhere [34].
Metallography and microstructures
Samples with a thickness of 2 mm were cut from the heat treated 
rods and metallographically prepared for SEM observation. The 
samples were ground down to1200 grit SiC paper, then mechan-
ical polished. To reveal detailed information of the microstruc-
tures, the polished samples were electrochemically etched in a 
90% H2O + 10% H3PO4 solution at room temperature with a 
voltage of 9 V .
SEM images and EBSD patterns were captured in a FEI 
quanta 650 microscope, using an accelerating voltage of 15 kV 
and a 12 mm working distance. Subsequently, the average size and 
cubic degree of γ′ particles were measured by Image J software.
STEM samples were prepared using a twin-jet electropo-
lisher in a solution containing 5 vol% perchloric acid and 95 
vol% ethanol at −30 °C. The composition of two phases in the 
four alloys was analyzed by EDS equipped on a FEI Tecnai G2 
F20 TEM operated at 200 kV .
