Effect of tungsten addition on microstructure and mechanical properties 
of TiAl-based alloy 
Hongliang SUN, Zewen HUANG, Degui ZHU 
Key Laboratory of Advanced Technologies of Materials, Ministry of Education, Southwest Jiaotong 
University, Chengdu 610031, PR China 
Keywords: TiAl-based alloy; tungsten; microstructure; mechanical properties 
Abstract. The effect of different tungsten addition on micros tructure and mechanical property of 
TiAl-based alloy was investigated. The results indicate that the size and amount of β (B 2) phase and 
equiaxed γ phase gradually increase with increasing content of tungsten. The grain size and lamellar 
spacing obey a parabolic law. The tungsten addition can increase the room temperature strength, 
high temperature strength but have a little effect on the ductility. The creep rupture life has invers e 
correlation relationship with the content of tungsten. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl-based intermetallic alloys have been considere d as one of the new type of high 
temperature structure materials. These materials ar e considered to have extensive application 
prospect and development potential because of their low density, high strength at high temperatures, 
high hardness and good oxidation resistance [1-3] . However, the application of these materials is 
tremendously restricted by high brittleness and low workability at room temperature. Besides, the 
performance when they service at high temperature d oes not match when they service at room 
temperature. The properties of TiAl-based alloy are very sensitive to the microstructure, the 
researches have shown that [4-5] fine fully lamellar or duplex microstructure has g ood mechanical 
properties. The adjustment of the microstructure of the alloy is an important method to gain good 
combination property. The alloying elements addition is one of the important methods to change the 
microstructure. The tungsten which is a kind of str engthening β (B2) phase stabilizing elements has 
an important influence on the microstructure and properties of the alloys [6-8] . The aim of the present 
study is to determine the relation between the micr ostructure and mechanical properties of the 
TiAl-based intermetallic alloys with different tung sten content under the condition of hot isostatic 
pressing.

water cooled crucible with a protective atmosphere .An ingot with a diameter of 20mm was cast and

Samples for tensile tests and creep tests were cyli ndrical with gauge size of

rate of 0.7 ×10 -4 s-1 at room temperature and 700 ℃ respectively to obtain the stress-strain curve. The 
ultimate tensile strength and the plastic elongatio n to failure were determined by the stress-strain

at an accelerating voltage 20kV . The microhardness was measured with a HXD-1000TM digital 
microhardness tester for dwell time 15s under 300gf.

4. Discussion 
4.1 Effect of tungsten on the microstructure 
Fig.1 shows that the size and amount of β (B
2) phase and equiaxed γ phase gradually increase 
with increasing content of tungsten. This is mainly attributed to the two reasons: (1) the nucleation 
of equiaxed γ phase takes place in α or α 2 matrix and (2) the segregation of W added to the 
TiAl-based alloy occurs on the grain boundary. Thus , tungsten has nearly no effect on the position 
of the nucleation. It is found that there is high t ungsten content in the α 2 phase but relatively low 
tungsten content in the γ phase. Tungsten helps the β (B 2) phase precipitate from the α2 phase. The β 
(B 2) phase could precipitate from either the lamellar colonies or the position connected with 
equiaxed γ grains. Thus, the number of γ phase nucl ei increases, the size and amount of γ phase 
gradually increase with increasing content of tungsten. 
Fig.2 shows that the grain size and lamellar thickness of the alloy with 1% tungsten content are 
the minimum. The grain size and lamellar spacing ob ey a parabolic law with increasing content of 
tungsten. The precipitation process of γ phase from (α 2+γ) lamellar colonies existed in the 
TiAl-based alloy and the growth of the lamellar are dominated by terrace-ledge-kink (TLK) model. 
The TLK model, which depends on atomic migration an d terrace movement, accelerates the 
formation of the coupled interface between α 2 phase and γ phase. The TiAl-based alloy with a 
certain tungsten content exists tungsten segregatio n on the γ/γ interface and α 2/γ interface [9] . 
Tungsten segregation has negative effects on latera l epitaxial growth of α 2 phase and γ phase, the 
rate of growth on the direction of lamellar thickne ss decreases. Moreover, the obstacles play a 
growing role and the lamellar thickness of the allo y decreases with increasing content of tungsten. 
Tungsten segregation has certain pinning effects on lamellar colonies, but the pinning effects are 
limited. The refinement effect on lamellar thicknes s is much less pronounced when the tungsten

4.2 Effect of tungsten on the tensile properties 
The coarse grains in TiAl alloy inhibit the disloca tion motion, it is easy for dislocation pile-up 
and tangling to form during the deformation process , which leads to failure due to cleavage fracture 
prematurely. The yield strength and grain size of t he TiAl-based alloy are in line with Hall-Petch 
formula, i.e.σ
0.2 =σ 0+Kd -1/2 .Thus, the ductility and strength can be improved b y refining grain. It is 
noted by Z.C.Liu [10] and Larson [11] that a proper addition of tungsten can improve the room 
temperature strength and high temperature strength of Ti-Al-Nb-W alloy. But the addition of 
tungsten induces microsegregation, which is detrime ntal to the ductility. Moreover, the existence of 
arborescent structure can induce cracks, which make s the strength decreasing with the dropping of 
temperature. 
As is shown in Fig.3, a proper addition of tungsten has a certain promotion to the refinement 
effect on the grain size and lamellar thickness, th e strength exhibits an increasing tendency, the 
maximum strength is measured when the addition of t ungsten reaches 1.4%. That is because W 
functioned as a solution strengthening element, wil l increase the resistance of dislocation motion in 
grains interior as well as in the lamellars interio r and the friction in dislocation motion interior. 
Solution strengthening has positive correlation rel ationship with the difference between solute 
atoms and solvent atoms radius. Tungsten atoms radi us has a difference of about 5.7% with Ti and 
Al atoms radius. Therefore, tungsten atoms have int ensively interaction with the twinning 
dislocation of 1/6<112] as well as the conventional dislocation of 1/2<110], which reduces the 
movability significantly and improves the strength of alloy. The elongation in Fig.3 indicates that 
the elongation of the alloy first increases and the n decreases with the tungsten addition. The 
maximum elongation is measured when the addition of tungsten reaches 1%. The elongation 
decreases when the tungsten content exceeds 1%. The changing trend of elongation is closely 
related to microstructure variation .That the addit ion of tungsten reaches 1% has the most efficient 
refinement effect on the grain size and lamellar thickness, which maximizes the elongation. 
Tungsten addition has contradictory effects on the creep rupture life (as shown in Fig.4), the 
highest creep rupture life is observed when the add ition of tungsten reaches 0.75%. The size and 
amount of (B 2+ω) phase and equiaxed γ phase gradually increase w ith increasing content of 
tungsten, which causes the creep rupture life decre asing. Researches have shown that TiAl-based 
alloy with fully lamellar microstructure exhibits t he best creep-rupture properties, which is because 
the high density lamellar interface within fully la mellar microstructure has an efficient inhibition t o 
the dislocation motion. But if there are minor equiaxed γ grains within fully lamellar microstructure, 
creep resistance of TiAl-based alloy will be reduce d significantly. The γ grains will exhibit priority 
deformation in the creep process and induce dynamic recrystallization. The fine equiaxed γ grains 
formed in the creep process promote grain boundary sliding, which will reduce microstructure 
stability and make the alloy get into the tertiary creep stage prematurely. Finally, the creep rupture 
life decreases.

5. Conclusions 
1. Effects of tungsten on the microstructure were i nvestigated. The results showed that the size 
and amount of β (B 2) phase obeyed a parabolic law with increasing cont ent of tungsten. That the β 
(B 2) phase existed mainly either on colony boundaries or triple junctions accelerated the pinning 
effect on lamellar colonies with the composition of α2 phase and γ phase and the refinement effect 
on the grain size and lamellar spacing. The alloy w ith 1% tungsten content had the most efficient 
refinement effect. 
2. The tungsten addition could increase the room te mperature strength and high temperature 
strength, and the strength was the maximum when the tungsten content was 1.4%. However, the 
elongation first increased and then decreased with the tungsten addition. When the tungsten content 
was the same, the high temperature strength was 50 ～70MPa less than the room temperature 
strength, but the elongation measured at high tempe rature didn't appear to be much different from 
the elongation measured at room temperature. 
3. The size and amount of equiaxed γ phase graduall y increased but the creep rupture life of 
the TiAl-based alloy decreased with the addition of tungsten.
