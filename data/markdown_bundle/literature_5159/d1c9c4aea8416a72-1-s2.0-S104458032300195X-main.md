ABSTRACT 
In this paper, an innovative design of CrFeCoNiCu high entropy alloy (HEA) interlayer was selected to produce 
high-strength diffusion bonding joints of 93W-4.9Ni-2.1Fe tungsten alloy (93W, wt%) and 30CrMnSiNi2A steel 
(steel, wt%). The microstructure evolution and mechanical properties of the joints bonded at different temper -
atures were investigated. At the 93W/HEA interface, a curved and continuous μ -Fe

W
6

density stacking faults was found in the diffusion zone between W particles and HEA, whereas the diffusion 
zone between γ -(Ni, Fe) phase and HEA was dominated by solid solution, companied by some nano- μ phase 
precipitation. The sluggish diffusion effect of HEA effectively inhibited the formation and growth of μ layer, 
despite the fact that the thickness of μ layer increased with the increasing of bonding temperature. Meanwhile, 
rod-like Cr

23
C

strength of the bonded specimens is as high as 592 MPa at the bonding temperature of 950 
◦
C. This study 
provides a new design strategy of interlayer for producing high-performance tungsten alloy/steel composites. 
1. Introduction 
Due to the high density and high melting point of tungsten alloy and 
the high mechanical properties of structural steel, tungsten alloy/steel 
composites are used in kinetic-energy penetrators [ 1 ]. However, the 
existence of brittle Fe
–
W intermetallic compounds (IMCs) [ 2 , 3 ] and the 
high thermal residual stresses, caused by the difference in coefficients of 
thermal expansion between tungsten alloy and steel [ 4 ], tend to result in 
low mechanical properties of tungsten alloy/steel diffusion bonding 
joints. Aiming at these problems, many researchers have proposed the 
method of adding interlayers to adjust the interfacial structure and 
mechanical properties of the joints. At present, single metal interlayers 
(such as Ni [ 5 ], Ti [ 6 ], Cu [ 7 ]) and composite interlayers (such as Ti/Ni 
[ 8 ], Ti/Cu [ 9 ], V/Cu [ 10 ]) have been used for tungsten alloy/steel 
diffusion bonding to reduce residual stresses or suppress the generation 
of brittle IMCs. However, the interfacial structure and joint strength of 
tungsten alloy/steel joints prepared by inserting these interlayers were 
always unsatisfactory. Existing studies show that the single pure metal 
interlayers were difficult to avoid the formation of brittle IMCs, while 
the composite interlayers were relatively complex to assemble without 
significant improvement in the mechanical properties of the joint. 
Therefore, the innovative design of interlayer is critical to achieving the 
high-quality bonding of tungsten alloy/steel joints. 
High entropy alloys (HEAs), composed of five or more elements with 
nearly equal atomic ratios, have excellent machinability, radiation 
resistance, mechanical properties, high-temperature stability, and dy -
namic shear properties [ 11 – 13 ]. As a result, HEAs have been recognized 
as highly promising candidates for extreme environments, especially in 
nuclear, aerospace, and military applications, which fit well with the 
application fields of tungsten alloy and steel joints. Furthermore, it has 
been experimentally proven that the sluggish diffusion effect of HEAs 
can effectively inhibit the formation and growth of the interfacial IMC 
layer [ 14 , 15 ]. For example, Li et al. [ 14 ] used an AlCoCrCuNi
2 
HEA 
interlayer to diffusion bonding titanium alloy and stainless steel, and the 
thickness of the interfacial IMC layer was only 1/3 of that of pure Ni 
interlayer which had the same IMC layer. Thus, the sluggish diffusion 
effect will be very beneficial in improving the problem of brittle Fe
–
W 
IMCs in tungsten alloy/steel composites. In summary, it is of potential 
significance to study the weldability of the tungsten alloy/steel joints 
using HEA interlayer.

The composition design of the HEA interlayer used in this paper is 
based on the following aspects. Firstly, the high melting point of tung -
sten alloy and the sluggish diffusion effect of HEA make it difficult for 
the element diffusion, which means that this is a significant challenge for 
diffusion bonding. Thus, the selected HEA interlayer should have good 
compatibility with tungsten alloy to promote interfacial metallurgical 
bonding. Panigrahi et al. [ 16 ] reported that the solid solution of W in 
CrFeCoNi-based HEAs can reach 12 at.%, which indicates that W has 
good solubility with CrFeCoNi-based HEAs. Secondly, it has been proven 
that a soft interlayer is more beneficial to releasing interfacial residual 
stresses and achieving high-quality bonding [ 17 , 18 ]. Thus, the HEA 
interlayer should have a certain degree of plastic deformation capability 
to release the residual stresses. A large number of studies have shown 
that the addition of Cu to CrFeCoNi-based HEAs leads to the precipita -
tion of Cu-rich phases, and these Cu-rich phases can improve the 
strength and ductility of the alloy [ 19 , 20 ]. Furthermore, considering the 
extreme environments in which tungsten alloy/steel composites are 
used, the selected interlayer should have good high-temperature sta -
bility and dynamic mechanical properties. 
Based on the aforementioned considerations, the CrFeCoNiCu 
interlayer was employed in this work to explore the feasibility of using 
HEAs as an interlayer for 93W-4.9Ni-2.1Fe tungsten alloy and 
30CrMnSiNi2A steel diffusion bonding. The interfacial microstructure 
and mechanical properties of tungsten/steel joints with CrFeCoNiCu 
HEA interlayer were focused on, and the effect of bonding temperature 
on the microstructure and mechanical properties of tungsten alloy/steel 
joints with CrFeCoNiCu HEA interlayer was also systematically studied.

the 93W/steel joint with the HEA interlayer at different bonding tem -
peratures, respectively. As seen in Fig. 3 (a), as the temperature rose from 
900 to 1000

cially, the joint obtained at 950 
◦

4. Conclusions 
In conclusion, diffusion bonding of 93W/30CrMnSiNi2A steel was 
successfully achieved by designing a CrFeCoNiCu HEA interlayer, and a 
joint with the maximum tensile strength of 592 MPa was obtained, 
which was much higher than the current level. The effects of different 
bonding temperatures on the microstructure evolution and mechanical 
properties were analyzed and discussed. The following conclusions 
could be obtained: 
(1) At the 93W/HEA interface, a curved and continuous μ -Fe
