abstract
Fatigue tests of the Inconel 600, a type of nickel–chromium based heat resistant alloy used for steam
generator tubes in nuclear power plants, were carried out. Temperature increase to 320 ◦ C did not change
the fatigue strength much, but the fretting condition caused a signiﬁcant reduction in the fatigue strength.
The reduction at 10
7 cycles was about 70% for both of room and the high temperatures. An apparatus to
realize the fretting condition has been developed and instrumented to measure the normal and friction
forces. The bridge type of contact pad was fabricated of SUS 409 stainless steel. Fracture surfaces and wear
scars were observed by electron microscope and the proﬁles of wear scar were measured by non-contact
3D-proﬁler.

1. Introduction
A slight relative motion between mating surfaces, which is due
to either structural vibration or cyclic deformation under repeated
load, induces fretting wear damage on the contact surface. The
wear process is accompanied by material loss and formation of
notch, stress concentration and even surface crack formation. Fret-
ting wear damage on steam generator tubes in nuclear power
plants has been a concern since early 1970s. An example of severe
material loss of the tube can be seen from the paper by Attia
(2006). Many researchers have made efforts to estimate the loads
and relative motions between tube bundles and their support-
ing structures by solving the ﬂow-induced vibration problems in
both of analytical and experimental approaches ( Khushnood et al.,
2004). Fretting wear resistance of the steam generator tubes and
supporting structure materials has also been characterized under
various environments and by using many different types of test rigs.
Recently, a theoretical model to predict fretting wear damage for
impact and sliding motion has been proposed ( Attia, 2009). How-
ever, the fretting wear alone is such a complicated phenomenon
that any uniﬁed theory is still difﬁcult to predict the damage in
general cases.
In case materials are subject to fatigue loading, being exposed
to fretting wear condition simultaneously, premature fatigue fail-
ure occurs in consequence. The inﬂuence of fretting on the fatigue
characteristics depends on many factors including materials and
loading conditions, and it is known that the fatigue strength may
be reduced by as much as 50–70% under fretting condition com-

pared with the plain fatigue ( Shaffer and Glaeser, 1996 ). Damage
from fretting fatigue on steam generator tubes in nuclear power
plants has a substantial impact from both of safety and economy

Although it has been pursued since early 90s ( Attia and
Waterhouse, 1992 ), test method for fretting fatigue has not been
standardized yet. One of the popular methods is to use bridge type
of contacting pads clamped to a fatigue specimen. Even with some
inherent drawbacks ( Nowell et al., 2006 ), it is still being used in
many studies. Also, cylindrical pads have been used by a large
number of researchers ( Hills et al., 1988; Szolwinski and Farris,
1998). More practical approaches employ the specimen geometries
resembling actual components with load conditions experienced
in use ( Attia and Waterhouse, 1992; Kwon et al., 2004 ). As for the
tubes in steam generators, the environment of high pressure and
temperature requires the experimental investigations to be more
elaborate.
The Inconel 600 is one of the austenitic nickel–chromium based
super-alloys. Its high strength is retained over wide range of tem-
perature, and easy formation of stable passivating oxide layer leads
to excellent corrosion resistance. Such superior properties are suit-
able for the application in extreme environments including the
steam generators in nuclear power plants. There are some other
Inconel alloys with different chemical composition and modiﬁed
properties than Inconel 600, but many steam generators are still
in use of it. Fretting fatigue characteristics of this alloy, especially
in elevated temperature, has been studied by few researchers, but
Attia reported that the strength reduction factor of Inconel 600 due
to fretting fatigue ranges over 2.2–3.5 in 265
◦C steam environment
(Attia, 2000).

2. Materials and experimental procedures
The fatigue specimens were fabricated from hot-rolled Inconel
600 plates. Testing tube specimens applied in the steam genera-
tors would have been more desirable from the practical point of
view, but material availability hindered it. The mechanical prop-
erties depend on the geometry and heat treatment in general.
However, the difference between the plates and tubes are known
to be moderate, as explained later. The bridge type of pad was fabri-
cated of SUS 409 stainless steel, which also has excellent corrosion
resistance and is used for the tube supporting structures in steam
generators.

cence) of the fatigue specimen and the pad are enlisted in Table 1.
The tensile properties of the materials measured in room temper-
ature according to the special metals data sheet, hot-rolled Inconel
600 plate has typical tensile strength of 580–760 MPa while anneal-
ing reduces it to 550–725 MPa. Yield strength is reduced from
240–450 MPa to 205–345 MPa by annealing, and the elongation is
slightly increased from 50–30% to 55–35%. The mechanical proper-
ties in Table 2 we measured fall inside the overlapped interval of the
annealed and as-rolled hot-rolled plates. In case of tube, the afore-
mentioned data sheet tells that hot-ﬁnished tubes have the tensile
strength of 520–690 MPa and the yield strength of 170–345 MPa.
Tensile and yield strength of cold drawn tubes are 550–690 MPa and
170–345 MPa, respectively. Elongation is within 55–35% for both of
hot-ﬁnished and cold drawn tubes.

The friction force of 240 MPa specimen is slightly higher than
that of 350 MPa specimen, differently from the room temperature
cases. The magnitudes of friction forces are so close, and it may need
to be explained. The contact pad is screw-loaded before test runs,
and thereafter the normal force is not adjusted during the test. Thus,
if the width of the fatigue specimen is reduced by fretting wear or
even plastic deformation, the friction force would be limited. This
is a weakness of the present test apparatus, if constant normal load
condition is desired.
Figs. 6 and 7 show the SEM micrographs of the fracture sur-
faces for maximum stress of 205 MPa at room temperature and
220 MPa at high temperature specimens, respectively. The longitu-
dinal direction of the fatigue specimens, which coincides with the
fatigue loading direction, is perpendicular to the photographs. The
thickness of the specimen corresponds to the height of photo (a)’s in
the ﬁgures. As observed in Figs. 6(a) and 7(a) , cracks were initiated
at the contact regions on each side. The cracks have grown ini-
tially in oblique directions to the fracture surface as usual, and then
ﬁnal fracture surface has turned into the vertical direction to the
loading direction ( Endo and Goto, 1976 ). Beach marks are obvious
especially in the Mode II + I region of the high temperature spec-
imen. The reason that it is clearer in high temperature specimen
is thought to be due to the larger strain induced from the reduced
stiffness of the material. Figs. 6(b) and 7(b) magnify the oblique
crack regions, and material loss in forms of grooves is observed
similarly to Mutoh’s work (1995). The regions in Figs. 6(c) and 7(c)
correspond to the stable growth of fatigue crack, and multiple
striations are clearly observed. Figs. 6(d) and 7(d) of the ﬁnal
fracture regions show dimples where slip lines have been well
developed, and it is believed to be the evidence of ductile frac-
ture.
Theoretically, the appearance of the surfaces should be symmet-
rical with respect to both the horizontal and vertical centerlines in
photo (a)’s. However, the test results were not. There are possi-
bilities of slight misalignment of the pads contacting the sides of
the specimen. Also, the contact loadings applied by the separate
screws on the sides are not guaranteed to be exactly same over the
test period, especially if the wear scars develop in non-symmetrical
manner.
Wear scars formed by fretting wear under the two legs of pad
are shown in Figs. 8 and 9 . The surface of the room temperature
specimen looks more complicated. Ploughed scratches formed by
abrasive wear exist all over tribologically transformed thin-layered
structures in scar region, similarly to typical fretting wear scars

A non-contact 3D-proﬁler was employed to measure the pro-
ﬁle of the wear scars. Fig. 10 , which has superposed multiple
proﬁles measured in longitudinal direction of specimens, com-
pares the extent of worn area and wear depth. The width
of scar was close to the width of the pad foot in both
cases, while the depth was much deeper in high temper-
ature specimen. The curved proﬁle outside of the pad foot
area indicates that plastic deformation by the combined load
of the normal compressive force and the tensile force has
occurred.
Friction force was higher in room temperature as shown in
Fig. 5 , while the wear groove was much deeper in high temper-
ature as shown in Fig. 10. Certainly, the fatigue strength is reduced
by fretting wear, but the degree of reduction does not seem to
be directly related with the amount of worn-out material. Fret-
ting fatigue is such a complicated phenomenon, especially when
the temperature effect is incorporated. The test parameters such
as applied stress and contact stress are combined to determine the
fretting fatigue strength with the material behaviors under wear
and fatigue conditions. Temperature also has a major inﬂuence
on the material characteristics such as elasto-plastic properties,
wear resistance, crack formation and propagation resistance and
so on. It was too difﬁcult to isolate a single parameter or mate-
rial property and to investigate its inﬂuence on the fretting fatigue.
Accumulated test results may rather ﬁnd their practical use more
easily.
4. Summary
Fretting fatigue tests of Inconel 600 alloy have been performed
in room temperature and 320
◦C, and the results were compared
with plain fatigue characteristics. An apparatus composed of a prov-
ing ring, arms, and loading screws has been developed for both
of the test temperatures in order to realize the fretting condition.
Bridge type of contact pads made of SUS 304 was compressed
against the fatigue specimens, and the fatigue life was measured.
The elevation of temperature reduced fatigue strength, but the
difference was not signiﬁcant. On the other hand, fretting condi-
tion was found to reduce the fatigue strength signiﬁcantly. In both
of room temperature and 320
◦C, about 70% reduction of fatigue
strength was measured at 10 7 cycles.
At the contact area with pad, deep groove formed by the loss of
material due to fretting wear was observed. Starting from the root
of the groove, fatigue crack has grown to reach the ﬁnal fracture
failure. Multiple dimples and striations were observed in the region
of mode I ﬁnal fracture surface.
The wear-induced groove was deeper in high temperature spec-
imen, but the surface of the wear scar was much smoother than the
room temperature specimen, which had a typical fretting wear scar
surface of ductile materials with tribologically transformed thin
layer structures. Multiple scratches on the scar surface were the
evidence of the abrasive wear.
