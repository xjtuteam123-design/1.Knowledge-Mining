Mechanical properties and neutron-irradiation effects in the welds 
of molybdenum and its alloys 
Fumio Morito’ and Kensuke Shiraishi ’ 
’ National Research Institute for Metals I -2-l Sengen, Tsukuba 305, Japan

We tried to evaluate the possibilities of electron-beam welds of MO and its alloys for nuclear applications. Mechanical 
properties of MO and its alloys were generally degraded by electron-beam welding and by neutron irradiation. Addition of 
carbon was effective to suppress the intergranular embrittlement, which was caused by electron-beam welding and neutron 
irradiation. Segregation of carbon and precipitation of carbides seemed to enhance the intergranular cohesion and consider- 
able strength and ductility was maintained. TZM showed higher strength at high temperatures but lower bend ductility in the 
case of neutron irradiation. 
1. Introduction 
Molybdenum and its alloys are one of attractive 
materials for the first wall and protection components 
of fusion reactors, because of their excellent strength at 
high temperatures. However one of the problems re- 
stricting the extensive application of MO and its alloys 
is the intergranular embrittlement in recrystallized or 
welded materials. A further problem for nuclear materi- 
als generally comes from a reduction of mechanical and 
physical properties by neutron irradiation. Irradiation 
studies have been conducted from fundamental and 
microstructural aspects in unwelded polycrystals and 
single crystals of MO and its alloys [l-4]. However little 
work has been done on the irradiation behavior of the 
welds. From such a standpoint, we tried to evaluate the 
possibilities of electron-beam welded joints of MO and 
its alloys for nuclear applications. 
2. Experiment 
The materials of 1 mm thickness used in this study 
were powder metallurgy MO (PM-MO), vacuum arc 
melted MO alloy (TZM) and electron-beam melted Mo- 
0.56%Nb alloy. The chemical compositions are listed in 
table 1. Tensile test for cold specimens were performed 
with specimens of 4 mm width and 14 mm length at a 
strain rate of 2.4 X 10m4 s-‘. Tensile test for neutron- 
irradiated specimens were done with specimens of 4 mm 
width and 30 mm length at a strain rate of 1.1 X 10e4 
S -‘. The bend test specimens were 3-5 mm in width 
and 25-30 mm in length. Two types of punch radius, 
i.e. 2.5 mm and 1.58 mm, were used for cold test and 
the latter one for irradiated materials. The support 
distance was 16 mm. The cross head speed was constant 
at 1.0 mm/mm. Heat treatments were conducted for 3.6 
ks or 7.2 ks between 1273 K and 1873 K in a vacuum 
less than 6.5 X lop4 Pa. Carbon was doped for 1.8 ks at 
1773 K after deposition in the vacuum chamber. The 
parameters of electron-beam welding were described 
elsewhere [5]. Neutron irradiation was carried out with 
JRR-2 and JRR4 in JAERI to fluences of lo*’ n/m* 
to 1O24 n/m* (E, > 1 MeV) at temperatures ranging 
from 348 to 1073 K.

4. Summary 
We examined mechanical properties of electron-beam 
welds of MO and its alloys for nuclear applications. The 
main results are as follows. 
(1) It was shown that mechanical properties of MO 
and its alloys were generally degraded by electron-beam 
welding and further by neutron irradiation. 
(2) Addition of carbon was effective to suppress 
intergranular embrittlement which was caused by elec- 
tron-beam welding and neutron irradiation. It is consid- 
ered that segregation of carbon and precipitation of 
carbides enhanced the intergranular cohesion so that 
considerable strength and ductility was maintained. 
(3) TZM showed higher strength at high tempera- 
tures, but it seemed unavoidable to improve lower bend 
ductility especially in the case of neutron irradiation to 
1.2 X 1O24 n/m2 at 1073 K. 
(4) The embrittlement of MO-0.56% Nb was not so 
significant at room temperature under the irradiation to 
6.0 X lO*s n/m2 at 1073 K. It is considered that good 
mechanical properties of the unirradiated weld of Mo- 
0.56% Nb were maintained in this case.
