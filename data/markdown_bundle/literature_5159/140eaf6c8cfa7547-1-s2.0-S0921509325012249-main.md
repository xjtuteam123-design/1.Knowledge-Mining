ABSTRACT
A comparative study of the low-temperature fracture characteristics of V – Cr – W – Zr and V – Cr – Ta – Zr vanadium 
alloys under impact and tension conditions was performed. The impact toughness characteristics, yield strength 
and ductility values were determined in the temperature range from 83 to 293 K. Scanning electron microscopy 
methods were used to reveal the main fracture types depending on the deformation method and temperature. It 
was established that the temperature range of the brittle-ductile transition of V – Cr – W – Zr alloy under impact 
conditions is in the range from 173 to 223 K, while for V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy this range shifts to the cryogenic 
temperature region from 83 to 173 K. It was shown that the strength of the studied alloys at cryogenic tem -
peratures is not determined by their structural-phase state, at the same time, a structural dependence of ductility 
is observed. The ductility of V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy is almost twice as high as that of V – Cr – W – Zr alloy, as a conse -
quence of the greater extent of grain boundaries. It has been established that the Tabor relation is not satisfied for 
V – Cr – Ta – Zr and V – Cr – W – Zr heterophase alloys.
1. Introduction
Interest in vanadium alloys is largely determined by the possibility of 
their high-temperature application in power engineering [ 1 – 13 ]. The 
physical basis for the high-temperature strength of vanadium alloys, 
which are representatives of refractory heterophase systems, is the 
complex implementation of solid-solution, dispersion and substructural 
(deformation) strengthening [ 14 – 25 ]. At the same time, if a fairly large 
number of works are devoted to the study of deformation and fracture of 
vanadium alloys in the high-temperature region [ 2 – 4 , 14 , 16 – 25 ], then in 
the low-temperature region these issues still remain practically un -
studied. But it is important to note that one of the key requirements 
determining the manufacturability of such alloys during processing is 
the preservation of a sufficient reserve of ductility at low and room 
temperatures. In this regard, the issue of the influence of the elemental 
composition, as well as the structural-phase state of heterophase vana -
dium alloys on both the features of their low-temperature deformation 
and fracture, and the temperature of the brittle-ductile transition re -
mains relevant.
To date, research has been primarily focused on vanadium alloys of 
the V – Cr – Ti system [ 3 , 4 , 6 , 9 , 14 , 17 , 21 ], however, some studies [ 15 ,
23 – 25 ] have shown that zirconium-containing vanadium alloys can 
become an alternative, demonstrating a good combination of heat 
resistance and processability.
The currently established concepts of the temperature dependence of 
metallic materials strength characteristics are mainly based on the 
consideration of individual and collective behavior of dislocations 
[ 26 – 29 ]. Such approaches describe well the behavior of materials in the 
range of moderate and high temperatures. However, in the 
low-temperature region, the suppression of thermal activation mecha -
nisms prevents the implementation of diffusion and dislocation plas -
ticity. In this regard, the study of the features and regularities of 
low-temperature deformation and fracture of materials of different 
classes seems to be an urgent task, including for engineering 
applications.
In this paper, a comparative study of the fracture characteristics of 
zirconium-containing vanadium alloys of different compositions under 
impact and tension conditions at low temperatures was carried out.

2. Materials and methods
Table 1 shows the elemental compositions of the investigated va -
nadium alloys, produced by JSC Bochvar High-Technology Research 
Institute for Inorganic Materials (Moscow). Further the alloys will be 
designated V – Cr – W – Zr and V – Cr – Ta – Zr. Alloys samples were subjected 
to thermomechanical treatment (TMT): several (at least 3) cycles “ 50 % 
rolling at room temperature + vacuum annealing at Т = 1273 K ” with 1- 
h stabilizing annealing in vacuum at 1323 K.
Impact strength tests were carried out on samples with geometric 
dimensions of 3 mm × 4 mm × 27 mm (with a V-shaped stress 
concentrator) at temperatures of 83 K, 173 K, 223 K and 293 K on the 
impact testing machine VEB Werkstoffprufmaschinen Leipzig. Liquid 
nitrogen and its mixtures with ethanol were used as a coolant. The 
sample temperature was monitored using a Testo 735-2 thermometer 
with a type-K thermocouple. Before determining the impact work, the 
samples were kept at the specified temperatures for 15 min.

Studies of the structural and phase state of alloys using transmission

The study of fractographs after impact toughness and tensile tests

3. Results
Fig. 1 shows SEM and TEM images illustrating the microstructure of 
V – Cr – W – Zr ( Fig. 1 a – c) and V – Cr – Ta – Zr ( Fig. 1 d – f) alloys after TMT and 
stabilizing annealing at 1323 K. V – Cr – W – Zr alloy is characterized by 
strong inhomogeneity of the grain structure, with the grain sizes varying 
from 10 to 40 μ m ( Fig. 1 a). There is no trace of rolling on the grain 
orientation map, and the grains of complex shape with curved bound -
aries indicate incomplete recrystallization under the conditions of sta -
bilizing annealing. Using the transmission electron microscopy method, 
it was found that in the bright-field images the curved boundaries of the 
grains of complex shape consist of almost rectilinear sections several μ m 
long ( Fig. 1 b). The presence of a large number of thin extinction con -
tours indicates the absence of structural states with high crystal lattice 
curvature.
In V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy after the same treatment, the grain structure 
retains the rolling legacy: the grains have an elongated shape in the 
rolling direction (RD) ( Fig. 1 d). At the same time, the grain structure is 
more homogenous. The grain sizes in the direction perpendicular to RD 
vary from 5 to 10 μ m, while in the direction parallel to RD they vary 
from 10 to a rare 25 μ m. The non-equiaxiality ratio of most grains is 1:3. 
Broadened extinction contours are observed in the bright-field images, 
the complex diffraction contrast in which indicates a high density of fine 
particles and dislocations ( Fig. 1 e).
Using dark-field analysis methods, a detailed study of the defect and 
heterophase structure was carried out in both alloys. In the dark-field 
electron microscopic images of V – Cr – W – Zr ( Fig. 1 c) and V – Cr – Ta – Zr 
( Fig. 1 f) alloys microstructure, nanosized particles of second phases that 
pin dislocations are observed. In Refs. [ 23 , 25 ], it was shown that the 
heterophase structure of the alloys of these systems is represented by 
precipitates in the form of particles of carbides and oxycarbonitrides 
based on Zr and their compounds. Estimates of the volume fractions of 
the second phase were made on the basis of the elemental composition 
data ( Table 1 ) taking into account the type of the crystal lattice of the

Fig. 2 a shows the temperature dependence of the impact toughness 
of V – Cr – W – Zr and V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloys in the range from 83 to 293 K. For 
both alloys, a decrease in the test temperature is accompanied by a 17- 
fold decrease in the values of impact work (KV) and impact toughness 
(KCV) ( Table 2 ).
Scanning electron microscopy showed that impact toughness testing 
at 293 K results in only partial fracture of both alloys ( Fig. 3 a – e). The 
concentrator in V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy is significantly less opened ( Fig. 3 e) 
than in V – Cr – W – Zr alloy ( Fig. 3 a). This trend continues with decreasing 
temperature ( Fig. 3 b – f). At 173 K, V – Cr – W – Zr alloy samples demon -
strate complete fracture ( Fig. 3 c), while V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy samples do 
not yet break completely ( Fig. 3 g). Moreover, a narrow unfractured 
region around the perimeter of the sample is sometimes retained on the 
samples of this alloy even after testing at 83 K ( Fig. 3 h).
A detailed study of the fractographs ( Fig. 4 ) made it possible to 
identify the main types of fracture of the alloys at different tempera -
tures. After testing at 293 K, the cellular relief indicates the ductile na -
ture of fracture of both alloys ( Fig. 4 a – e). This type of fracture also 
prevails in the case of tests at 223 K ( Fig. 4 b – f). A decrease in the test 
temperature to 173 K is accompanied by the appearance of trans -
crystalline fracture elements with a river pattern in both alloys, which 
indicates the manifestation of brittle fracture ( Fig. 4 c – g). In this case, the 
proportion occupied by brittle fracture fragments in V – Cr – W – Zr alloy is 
significantly higher compared to V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy. After impact testing 
at 83 K, almost the entire fracture in both alloys, with the exception of a 
thin edge near the surface and the breakage area, is characterized by the 
brittle fracture type ( Fig. 4 d – h). The transcrystalline fracture elements 
in V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy are 1.5 – 2 times larger than those in V – Cr – W – Zr 
alloy. In the classical definition, the temperature range of the brittle- 
ductile transition corresponds to the transition range from predomi -
nantly ductile to predominantly brittle. Based on the impact test data, in 
the case of V – Cr – W – Zr alloy this range is from 173 to 223 K, while for 
V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy this range is from 83 to 173 K. The KCV value deter -
mined in the experiment characterizes the work expended on crack 
formation in the zone of plastic deformation and fracture. Thus, the 
work expended on fracture of the more ductile V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy is 
significantly higher ( Table 2 ).

4. Discussion
Comparison of the results obtained shows that the character of the 
brittle-ductile transition of zirconium-containing vanadium alloys is 
determined by the deformation method and the structural-phase state. 
The high-speed nature of impact deformation in combination with low 
temperatures allows the conditions under which the absence of the 
possibility of structural accommodation is accompanied by material 
embrittlement. The presented temperature dependence of the yield 
strength ( Fig. 2 c) shows that as the tensile temperature decreases, the 
yield strength values of V – Cr – Ta – Zr and V – Cr – W – Zr alloys begin to 
equalize, and in the range from 173 K to 83 K they practically coincide. 
Under these conditions, despite the significant values of true logarithmic 
deformation ( Table 4 ), the temperature-sensitive processes of disloca -
tion and diffusion activity are completely suppressed. Moreover, in the 
specified range of negative temperatures, the effects of dispersion, solid- 
solution and deformation strengthening of alloys are completely leveled 
out, regardless of their compositions, volume fractions and dispersion of 
the second phase. Thus, at cryogenic temperatures, the structural-phase 
state of V – Cr – Ta – Zr and V – Cr – W – Zr alloys is not a determining factor of 
their strength.
It should be noted that at room temperature the effects of dispersion 
strengthening can be realized, and therefore the strength characteristics 
( Fig. 2 c) of the V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy with a higher volume fraction of nano- 
sized particles are somewhat higher than those of the V – Cr – W – Zr alloy.
At the same time, despite the very high strength, the ductility of the 
studied alloys in the entire analyzed temperature interval is at a high 
level ( Table 3 ). In the cryogenic temperature range, the ductility of 
V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy is twice as high as that of V – Cr – W – Zr alloy, and rea -
ches 23 % despite the higher volume fraction of the second phase. In our 
opinion, unlike strength, this feature in the ductility of alloys is deter -
mined precisely by the specificity of their structural state. Despite the 
fact that both alloys are characterized by a fine-crystalline state, 
V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy grain size is significantly smaller ( Fig. 1 d) compared 
to V – Cr – W – Zr alloy ( Fig. 1 a). In accordance with [ 33 ], using the for -

the formation of ultrafine-grained and nanocrystalline structural states 
in metals and alloys of different classes, in which it is the high extent of 
grain boundaries that is one of the reasons for maintaining ductility even 
against the background of a multiple increase in strength [ 34 , 35 ].
One of the determining factors in stabilizing the heterophase struc -
ture elements and grain boundaries is the elemental composition of the 
alloy. While tantalum (at a content of less than 9 at.%) forms a solid 
solution with vanadium [ 36 ], the specifics of obtaining vanadium alloys 
with tungsten are extremely complex, which is due to the vicinity of 
tungsten melting point to vanadium boiling point. In Ref. [ 37 ], it was 
shown that the distribution of tungsten in a vanadium alloy is extremely 
non-uniform. In turn, it is well known [ 38 ] that alloying with W 
significantly reduces the density of second-phase particles along the 
grain boundaries of BCC alloys, which is due to the need for 
diffusion-controlled release of tungsten, which has higher migration 
activation energy, from the region of their formation.
In the case of the V – Cr – W – Zr alloy studied in this work, alloying with 
tungsten leads to inhomogeneity of the grain ( Fig. 1 a), subgrain 
(polygonal) ( Fig. 1 b) and disperse heterophase ( Fig. 1 c) structure, 
which negatively affects the ductility, especially at cryogenic tempera -
ture, and similarly affects the impact toughness. The V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy, 
even after thermal stabilization at 1323 K, due to the uniform distri -
bution of nano-sized particles ( Fig. 1 f), retains a finely crystalline state 
( Fig. 1 d) with elements of a polygonal structure ( Fig. 1 e).
However, the identification of the mechanisms of plastic deforma -
tion and fracture at cryogenic temperatures, including those explaining 
the high plasticity of the V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy discovered in this work, re -
quires additional research.
5. Conclusions
The temperature of the brittle-ductile transition of zirconium- 
containing vanadium alloys, in addition to the elemental composition, 
depends significantly on the deformation method. The temperature 
range of the brittle-ductile transition of V – Cr – W – Zr alloy under impact 
testing is in the range from 173 to 223 K, while for V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy this 
range is from 83 to 173 K. In this case, the work spent on the formation 
of a crack in the zone of plastic deformation and fracture of V – Cr – Ta – Zr 
alloy is more than twice as high as that of V – Cr – W – Zr alloy.
In the case of tension at cryogenic temperatures, under conditions of 
suppressed dislocation and diffusion activity, the structural-phase state 
of the V – Cr – Ta – Zr and V – Cr – W – Zr alloys is not a determining factor in 
their strength. At the same time, the higher ductility of V – Cr – Ta – Zr alloy 
is a consequence of an almost twofold increase in the length of grain 
boundaries compared to V – Cr – W – Zr alloy.
Using the example of V – Cr – Ta – Zr and V – Cr – W – Zr alloys, it is shown 
that in the case of heterophase systems with complex strengthening, 
combining solid-solution, grain-boundary, dispersion and substructural, 
the Tabor relationship is not fulfilled.
