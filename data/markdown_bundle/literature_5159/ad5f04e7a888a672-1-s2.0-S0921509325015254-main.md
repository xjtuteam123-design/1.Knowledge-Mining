1. Introduction
With the continuous development of materials science, high-entropy 
alloys (HEAs) have garnered significant attention as an emerging 
metallic material. Unlike conventional alloys, HEAs are typically 
composed of a variety of equimolar or near-equimolar elements. This 
unique compositional design provides greater opportunities for tuning 
and flexibility in developing alloys with superior property combinations 
[ 1 , 2 ]. It has been noted that HEAs containing only an FCC phase tend to 
show outstanding ductility but relatively low strength, whereas those 
consisting solely of a BCC phase exhibit high strength but limited plastic 
deformation [ 3 ]. To address the limitations of HEAs — poor casting flow 
characteristics and compositional inconsistencies — Lu and colleagues 
pioneered the development of a novel dual-phase AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
eutectic 
high-entropy alloy (EHEA). This innovative material features FCC and 
BCC microstructure, offering a promising solution to these longstanding 
challenges. This adoption of the eutectic alloy design concept to develop 
a HEAs aims to obtain a new alloy that combines the advantages of both 
high-entropy and eutectic alloys [ 4 ]. It has been found that EHEAs 
exhibit excellent material properties, including high hardness and 
strength, good thermal stability, and excellent wear and corrosion 
resistance under a variety of environmental conditions, allowing them to 
show great potential in marine engineering applications such as offshore 
platforms, ship structures and propellers [ 5 – 8 ].
However, in the marine environment characterized by high humid -
ity, high chloride ion concentration, and chemical corrosion coupling, 
the failure of metallic materials is primarily driven by localized corro -
sion, which is triggered by the destruction of the surface passivation film 
[ 9 , 10 ]. EHEAs are considered ideal candidates for overcoming the lim -
itations in strength, plasticity, and corrosion resistance of metallic ma -
terials, thanks to their multiphase synergistic effects. Among these, the 
AlCoCrFeNi-based EHEAs developed by Professor Yiping Lu have ach -
ieved a synergistic optimization of fracture strength, plasticity, and 
corrosion resistance. This is accomplished through the synergistic con -
struction of FCC/B2 biphasic laminates, which include nanoscale 
L12/BCC precipitated phases, combined with the formation of dense

Based on this, the present study proposes an integrated design 
concept of “ composition-structure-function ” and employs a pseudo- 
ternary method to construct a triple-phase eutectic high-entropy alloy 
(TEHEAs) comprised of FCC, B2, and Laves phases. The findings indicate 
that this triple-phase eutectic system exhibits a more complex micro -
structure compared to traditional duplex alloys. This unique micro -

This study examines the role of Nb content (x = 3 – 16) in tailoring the 
microstructure, mechanical performance, and corrosion behavior of 
marine-grade (CoCrFeNi)
82-x
(NiAl)
18
Nb
x 
TEHEAs, with key conclusions 
outlined below: 
(1) In Nb-modified (CoCrFeNi)
82-x
(NiAl)
18
Nb
x 
TEHEAs, increasing 
Nb content elevates the Laves phase volume fraction, promoting a 
dendritic phase transition from FCC to Laves. Concurrently, the 
eutectic morphology shifts from lamellar (FCC + Laves) to (FCC 
+ B2) configurations.
(2) In (CoCrFeNi)
82-x
(NiAl)
18
Nb
x 
TEHEAs, incipient phase selection 
and phase volume fraction critically govern plasticity and 
strength. Among the alloys, the Nb6 alloy demonstrates superior 
mechanical performance, with a yield strength of 956.8 MPa, 
fracture strength of 2176.4 MPa, and plastic strain of 37.8 %.
(3) Electrochemical testing in 3.5 wt% NaCl demonstrated superior 
corrosion resistance in Nb6 and Nb9 alloys, arising from the 
synergistic effect of Nb
2
O
5 
and Cr
2
O
3 
enrichment within the 
passivation film. Notably, the charge transfer resistance ( R
ct
) of 
the Nb9 alloy reached a peak value of 1.85 × 10
6 
Ω cm
2
, 
demonstrating capacitor behavior close to ideal conditions. XPS 
analysis further confirmed that the enrichment of Nb
2
O
5 
and 
Cr
2
O
3 
effectively reduced the density of oxygen vacancies, 
significantly enhancing the stability of the passivation film. 
However, when the Nb content is excessively high, it leads to the 
depletion of Cr and uneven distribution of oxides, resulting in 
decreased corrosion resistance of the alloy.
