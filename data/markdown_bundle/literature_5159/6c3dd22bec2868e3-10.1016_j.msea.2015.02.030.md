Abstract 
Ti-3.5Al-5Mo-6V-3Cr-2Sn-0.5Fe is a high-strength β titanium alloy that shows 
excellent tensile properties. This paper presents t he effect of hot rolling and heat 
treatment on the microstructure and tensile propert ies of this alloy. Microstructure 
observation shows that the α+β rolled alloy leads to smaller β grain size than the β 
rolled alloy in all heat treatment conditions. Solu tion treatment of the alloy in the α+β 
field can also leads to smaller β grain than solution in the β field. Tensile results 
indicated that the strength of the alloy was improv ed greatly by aging heat treatment, 
which was attributed to the secondary α phase. The 790ºC rolled alloy can obtain an 
ultimate strength of 1744MPa by solution at 800ºC p lus aging at 440ºC. The strength 
decreases and ductility increases with the increase of aging temperature. Moreover, 
the ductility of the alloy is significantly determi ned by the rolling and solution

procedure. Rolling and solution treated at the α+β field results in better ductility than 
rolling and solution at the β field. Therefore, to obtain the high ductility, th e α+β 
rolling and α+β solution should be chosen as long as possible befo re aging heat 
treatment.

1. Introduction 
β titanium alloys exhibit the highest strength-to-we ight ratio of all titanium 
alloys [1]. The high strength can be obtained by pr ecipitation of fine secondary α 
phase during aging at a lower temperature. Many hig h-strength β titanium alloys have 
been developed over the decades since the first β titanium alloy Ti-13V-11Cr-3A1 was 
developed and used extensively on the SR-71 "Blackb ird" reconnaissance airplane [2]. 
Ti-10V-2Fe-3Al alloy used to be the most widely use d high-strength β titanium 
alloy developed by Timet in 1971 [3]. In 1989, Timet developed an oxidation-resistant 
β titanium alloy, Ti-15Mo-2.7Nb-3Al-0.2Si, for plug and nozzle assembly for the trent 
800 engine [2,3]. In the 1990s, a high-strength β titanium alloy Ti-5Al-5Mo-5V-3Cr 
was developed based on the Russian alloy VT22 to re place the Ti-10V-2Fe-3Al alloy 
on the main landing gear of a Boeing plane [4,5]. 
Recently, the U.S. Army has used titanium alloys on current combat vehicles [6]., 
which has caused concern among some researchers reg arding the use of the alloys in 
military field due to the good mechanical propertie s of high-strength β titanium alloys. 
Bartus [7] compared the ballistic performance betwe en Ti-5553 with different heat 
treatment conditions and Ti-6Al-4V plates, founding that the Ti-5553 plate after 
solution at 827ºC and aging at 593ºC showed a highe r limit velocity than the

Ti-6Al-4V plate against ballistic impact and fragme nt simulation projectile tests. 
Sukumar et al. studied the ballistic impact behavio r of β-CEZ in different 
microstructural conditions against 7.62 mm armor pi ercing projectiles and results 
show that the β-CEZ titanium alloy in as-rolled and at β solution treated and aged 
conditions has no significant improvement in ballis tic penetration resistance 
compared to Ti-6Al-4V alloy [8]. In addition, β-21S mortar barrel was designed, 
manufactured, and tested by the U.S. Army to reduce the weight of the barrel on the 
81 mm M253 mortar [9]. 
In general, β titanium alloys could achieve good mechanical prop erties by 
different solution and aging heat treatment after f orging or rolling, but the 
microstructure and mechanical properties are very s ensitive to deformation and heat 
treatment [10-14]. Li et al. has studied the effect of solution temperature on 
microstructures and tensile properties of high-stre ngth Ti-6Cr-5Mo-5V-4Al alloy, 
founding that the alloy in α/β solution and aging condition showed a better 
combination of strength and ductility than the allo y in β solution and aging condition 
[12]. Ivasishin et al. have performed a comprehensive comparison of the 
mechanical properties of some high strength β titanium alloys (TIMETAL-LCB, 
Ti-15-3, β21S, and VT22) with different conditions [13]. Santhosh et al. studied 
single and duplex aging of Ti-15V-3Cr-3Sn-3Al alloy , showing that the duplex aging 
resulted in higher hardness and strength compared t o single aging treatments [14]. 
Although a few projects have researched the relatio nship between the 
processing-microstructure-property of high-strength β alloy, further research is 
needed.

developed in China [15]. The moly equivalent (Mo Eq ) of the alloy is 11.4, which

2. Materials and methods 
The nominal composition of the alloy used in the pr esent work is 
Ti-3.5Al-5Mo-6V-3Cr-2Sn-0.5Fe, which has a β transus temperature of approximately 
815 º C. The alloy was double melted by vacuum arc re-mel ting in a 40 kg ingot. The 
bottom of the ingot was cut and the surface was als o machined to remove the defect 
parts. The ingot with a diameter of 150 mm was firs tly forged down to a round bar, 
80 mm in diameter. 20-mm thick rectangular material s were cut from the bar and hot 
rolled at 790 º C ( α+β field) and 870 º C( β field) to sheets with 2-mm in thick sheets. 
Samples from the as-rolled sheet alloy were solutio n treated at 800 º C for 1 h 
and 830 º C for 0.5 h respectively. The aging heat treatment s were performed at a 
lower temperature ranging from 440 º C to 560 º C for 8h followed by solution 
treatment. All of the samples were air cooled after solution and aging heat treatment. 
The program of solution and aging heat treatments is shown in Fig. 1.

3. Results 
3.1 Microstructure observation 
Fig. 1 shows the microstructure of the alloy sheet after rolling at 790°C ( α+β 
field) and 870°C (β field) respectively. The microstructure of the all oy consists of an 
equiaxed and elongated (parallel to the rolling dir ection) shape of the primary α phase 
after rolling at 790°C, whereas the 870°C rolling y ields an elongated or pancake prior 
β structure. 
In order to obtain a uniform distribution of second ary α precipitates, the solution 
treatment should be performed at high temperature b efore aging heat treatment [16]. 
Fig. 2 shows the microstructure of the alloy rolled at 790°C followed by solution 
treatment at 800°C (α+β field) and 830°C (β field). The primary α phase exhibits only 
equiaxed shape and the elongated α disappeared after solution treatment at 800°C. 
Solution treatment at 830°C of the 790°C rolled all oy leads to coarse β grains. Fig. 3 
shows the microstructure of the 870°C rolled alloy followed by solution treated at 
800°C and 830°C. Fig. 3 shows that the alloy still retains an elongated shape of β

size than solution treatment at 800°C, which is dif ferent from the 790°C rolled alloy

The microstructures of the alloy after aging heat t reatment at different 
temperatures are shown in Figs. 4-7. Fig. 4 shows t hat the primary α phase distributes 
in the β grain boundary. Duerig et al. have reported that t he α phase could limit the 
recrystallization and growth of β phase grains [17]. The growth limitation of β grains 
is also reflected in Fig. 4. Overall, the fine seco ndary α phase precipitates in the β 
matrix during aging heat treatment, and the seconda ry α phase shows an acicular 
shape and an increased size with the increase of ag ing temperature. The lower aging 
temperature provides a high undercooling degree and the secondary α phase is 
feasible to nucleate, which results in a large numb er but a small size of secondary α 
phase. The higher aging temperature provides a larg e driving force for growth of the 
secondary α phase but reduces the nucleation driving force due to the low 
undercooling [18].

4. Discussion 
As seen from Table 1, the strength and ductility ar e significantly affected by 
aging temperature such that the strength decreases and the ductility increases with 
the increase of aging temperature. The strength and ductility are mainly controlled by 
β grain size and secondary α phase [16,19]. Figs. 4-7 show that the size of the 
secondary α increases with the increase of the aging temperatu re. In other words, the 
larger size of the secondary α phase will result in lower strength of β titanium alloys. 
Previous research has also obtained the same conclu sion [12,15]. The secondary α 
phase is the most important factor influencing the properties of β titanium alloys. For 
β titanium alloys, distribution of fine secondary α phase in the β matrix causes a 
microstructure condition similar to a dispersion st rengthened system [20]. The high 
volume fraction of the secondary α phase in the β matrix will provide more α/β 
interfaces, which could act as effective dislocatio n barriers [21]. It has been reported 
that the strength of β titanium alloy is inversely proportional to the sp acing of 
secondary α phases, such that it can be written as YS =850 +70 d-1 [22], where YS is 
the yield strength of the alloy, and d is the spacing of secondary α phases. Our 
previous work [15] has shown that the volume fracti on of secondary α decreases and

## Page 9

8 
 
the size increases with the increase of aging tempe rature. In other words, the lower 
aging temperature will lead to a higher number of s econdary α phases in the matrix. 
The spacing of secondary α phases d is proportional to V/N , where N is the number 
of secondary α phase and V is the volume fraction of secondary α phase. Thus, 
according to the literature [22], the high volume f raction of secondary α phase will 
lead to a small value of d, thus resulting in a higher strength of β titanium alloy. 
Therefore, the α/β solution treatment should be chosen before aging heat treatment to 
obtain the best match of strength and ductility. 
The secondary α phase can also affect the tensile ductility of β titanium alloys. 
The secondary α phase has a smaller size at lower aging temperatur es than at higher 
aging temperatures (Figs. 4-7). Therefore, there is reason to believe that the alloy with 
larger size of secondary α phase has good ductility. Hamajima et al. presente d that the 
α phase is softer than β phase and that lead to the earlier deformation of α phase than 
the β matrix during tensile deformation; the small α phase is harder to deform 
plastically when the β matrix reaches to the yield stress due to the fast strain 
hardening behavior [23]. Terlinde et al. [20] consi dered that both α and β are plastic 
phases and deformed at the same time in the primary stage of deformation. Thus the 
stress is distributed uniformly in the matrix at the beginning of deformation. However, 
with the continuous deformation, the independent st rain hardening of the two phases 
becomes more difficult due to the incompatibility of the different strengths for the two 
phases. A high volume fraction of fine secondary α phase in the matrix will promote 
the strain hardening behavior, and the strain local ization can be so extreme that it 
leads to macroscopic brittleness, according to the literature [20]. From another 
perspective, a good ductility can be obtained from a system with a larger size of 
secondary α phase, and the strength should be sacrificed.

Fig. 8 shows the comparison of strength and elongation between the α+β solution 
plus aging and the β solution plus aging conditions. For α+β rolled alloy, the solution 
treatment at α+β field plus aging condition shows higher strength a nd ductility than 
the β solution plus aging condition which can be attribu ted to the small β grain size. 
Solution treatment at the α+β field plus aging heat treatment leads to a bi-moda l 
structure with small β grain size. As mentioned above, the primary α phase can be 
obtained during α+β thermal processing. The growth of β grains can be restrained by 
the primary α phase and the high volume fraction of primary α phase leads to small β 
grain size [24,25]. As is well known, the small gra in size is beneficial to the strength 
according to the Hall-Petch relationship [26]. Furt hermore, the small β grain size can 
reduce the slip length and increase crack nucleatio n resistance, thus improving the 
tensile ductility [27]. Moreover, the previous rese arch has shown that the size of the 
secondary α phase can be significantly refined by small β grains [28], and thus can 
also results in high strength for α+β solution plus aging heat treatment. Solution 
treatment at β field of β rolled alloy leads to growth of the β grains, therefore, solution 
treatment at the α+β field plus aging condition shows higher strength a nd ductility 
than β solution plus aging condition of β rolled alloy. Fig. 9 shows the comparison of 
strength and elongation between α+β rolled and β rolled alloy followed by different 
heat treatment conditions. The elongation of α+β rolled alloy is much higher than that 
of β rolled alloy, because of that the α+β rolled alloy shows smaller β grain size than β 
rolled alloy in all heat treatment conditions. 
In summary, the aging heat treatment at low tempera ture will result in high 
strength but poor ductility of the β titanium alloy. However, the ductility in the same 
aging temperature can be improved by an α+β or α+β solution. Therefore, to obtain a 
good ductility, before aging heat treatment, the β titanium alloy should be rolling at

the α+β field, and the solution heat treatment should also be performed at α+β field. 
5. Conclusions 
In summary, the effect of hot rolling and heat trea tment on microstructure and 
tensile properties of the Ti-3.5Al-5Mo-6V-3Cr-2Sn-0.5Fe alloy sheet was investigated 
systematically. 
(1) The α+β rolled Ti-3.5Al-5Mo-6V-3Cr-2Sn-0.5Fe alloy shows s maller β 
grain size than β rolled alloy in all heat treatment conditions. Sol ution 
treatment of the alloy in α+β field leads to smaller β grain than solution at β 
field in following aging conditions. 
(2) The Ti-3.5Al-5Mo-6V-3Cr-2Sn-0.5Fe alloy sheet exhib its excellent strength 
ductility after solution and aging heat treatment. The high ultimate strength 
of 1744MPa was obtained for the 790ºC rolled alloy followed by 800ºC 
solution and 440ºC aging heat treatment. 
(3) The strength of the alloy can be improved by aging heat treatment, and the 
strength decreases with the increase of aging temperature. 
(4) The ductility of β titanium alloy is significantly determined by the rolling 
and solution procedure. Rolling and solution treatm ent at α+β field results in 
better strength and ductility than at β field rolling and solution. Therefore, in 
order to obtain the high strength and ductility, th e α+β rolling and α+β 
solution should be chosen as long as possible before aging heat treatment.
