Reducing the exploration of multi-principal element alloy space is a key challenge to design high- 
performance U-based high-entropy alloy (UHEA). Here, the best combination of multi-principal element 
can be eﬃciently acquired because proposed alloying strategy and screening criteria can substantially 
reduce the space of alloy and thus accelerate alloy design, rather than enormous random combinations 
through a trial-and-error approach. To choose the best seed alloy and suitable dopants, the screening 
criteria include small anisotropy, high speciﬁc modulus, high dynamical stability, and high ductility. We 
therefore ﬁnd a shortcut to design UHEA from typical binary (UTi and UNb) to ternary (UTiNb), qua- 
ternary (UTiNbTa), and quinary (UTiNbTaFe). Finally, we ﬁnd a best bcc senary UHEA (UTiNbTaFeMo), 
which has highest hardness and yield strength, while maintains good ductility among all the candidates. 
Compared to overestimation from empirical strength-hardness relationship, improved strength prediction 
can be achieved using a parameter-free theory considering volume mismatch and temperature effect on 
yield strength. This ﬁnding indicates that larger volume mismatch corresponds to higher yield strength, 
agreeing with the available measurements. Moreover, the dynamical stability and mechanical properties 
of candidates are greatly enhanced with increasing the number of multi-principal element, indicating the 
feasibility and effectiveness of adopted alloying strategy. The increasing of multi-principal element cor- 
responds to the increasing valence electron concentration (VEC). Alternatively, the mechanical properties 
signiﬁcantly improve as increasing VEC, agreeing with measurements for other various bcc HEAs. This 
work can speed up research and development of advanced UHEA by greatly reducing the space of alloy 
composition.

1. Introduction 
Pure uranium (U) metal has three polymorphic forms: α, β, and 
γ phases [1] . The α (face-centered orthorhombic) phase is stable 
up to 662 °C. The β (tetragonal) phase is stable between 662 °C 
and 772 °C. The γ (body-centered cubic, bcc) phase is stable from 
772 °C to the melting temperature of 1132 °C [2] . Among the three 
allotypes, bcc phase ( γ-U) shows the best symmetry and isotropy. 
U and its alloys are most widely applied as an essential component 
of nuclear weapons [3–9] and nuclear fuels [10–12] owning to the 
excellent comprehensive properties. Particularly, experiments man- 
ifest that U-Ti and U-Nb conventional alloys (CAs) [3–9] have at- 
tracted considerable interest as high-density and high-performance

Current ﬁrst-principles calculations mainly focused on the elas- 
tic properties of pure U and U-based dilute solid solutions [26–35] . 
Till now, there is still lack of investigations on multi-principal el- 
ement U-based alloys. Since the intrinsic nature of HEAs, both the 
trial-and-error methods and traditional quantum mechanical cal- 
culations are infeasible to deal well with the huge space of alloy 
composition in multi-principal alloys. The former has the disad- 
vantages of long experimental cycle and high cost, particularly for 
heavy U metal possessing radioactive toxicity and chemical toxic- 
ity. The latter cannot deal well with multi-principal element alloys 
and the 5 f electrons in U. Therefore, an alternative computational 
tool is required to solve the above multi-principal issues. To date, 
the exact muﬃn-tin orbitals alloy theory has been an immensely 
successful tool [36–38] , that reasonably predicts the ground-state 
properties of both CAs [ 39 , 40 ] and HEAs [41–45] systems. 
To rapidly reduce the huge composition space of U-based high- 
entropy alloys (UHEAs) from a traditional trial-and-error approach, 
here we adopt a multi-principal alloy design strategy and screen- 
ing criteria to develop equiatomic bcc UHEAs. To rapidly select 
the best seed alloy and suitable alloying element, we propose four 
screening criteria including small anisotropy, high speciﬁc modu- 
lus, high dynamical stability, and high ductility. To this end, we 
calculate the single-crystal and polycrystalline elastic properties, 
Zener anisotropy, single-crystal Young’s and shear modulus of all 
the candidates using the ﬁrst-principles method. We ﬁnd a short- 
cut to design UHEA through gradually ﬁxing the best combina- 
tion of multi-principal elements rather than randomly combining 
elements from a trial-and-error approach. First, alloying typical 
UTi and UNb binaries generates a UTiNb host alloy. Next, a best 
UTiNbTa is optimized upon alloying UTiNb. Then, doping UTiNbTa 
obtains a best UTiNbTaFe. Finally, a best bcc UTiNbTaFeMo UHEA 
has lower weight, highest hardness, and highest strength, while 
maintains good ductility, which surpasses all the candidates. Fur- 
thermore, mechanical properties of the candidates are greatly en- 
hanced with increasing the number of multi-principal element, in- 
dicating the feasibility and effectiveness of adopted alloying strat- 
egy. Besides, the increasing of the number of multi-principal el- 
ement relates to the increasing of valence electron concentration 
(VEC). The predicted mechanical properties signiﬁcantly improve 
as increasing VEC. This work can substantially reduce the explo- 
ration of alloy space and thus accelerate alloy design. Details of 
the computational methods are described in Section 2 . Compari- 
son between present theoretical and available experimental elas- 
tic properties and yield strength for four selected UHEAs are de- 
scribed in Section 3 , implying that accurate strength prediction can 
be reached by a parameter-free theory involving temperature effect 
and volume mismatch. Details of theoretical calculations of single- 
crystal Young’s and shear modulus, Vickers hardness, comparison 
between different yield strength prediction models, as well as cor- 
relation associated mechanical properties with the VEC are pre- 
sented in Section 4 . We provide conclusions in Section 5 . 
2. Methodology 
The present exact muﬃn-tin orbitals (EMTO) calculations 
were performed in the framework of the ﬁrst-principles density- 
functional theory (DFT). The EMTO method was performed us- 
ing the scalar-relativistic (SR) Green’s function technique that was 
based on the improved screened Korringa–Kohn–Rostoker (KKR) 
method [ 46 , 47 ]. The one-electron potential was represented by op- 
timized overlapping muﬃn-tin potential spheres. The single-site 
coherent-potential approximation (CPA) [48–50] were adopted to 
treat the substitutional disorders. The short-range ordering and 
local lattice relaxation effects of atoms were ignored with the 
CPA scheme. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [51] exchange- 
correlation functional was employed to perform the self-consistent 
calculations and compute the total energies. Apart from the static 
calculations, we further investigated temperature dependence of 
elastic properties for several selected UHEAs by calculating the co- 
eﬃcient of thermal expansion (CTE) based on Debye model. 
The scalar-relativistic approximation and soft-core scheme were 
used to solve the one-electron Kohn-Sham equations. The electro- 
static correction to the CPA was described using the screened im- 
purity model [52] with screening parameter 0.6. The Green’s func- 
tion was calculated for 32 complex energy points used to integrate 
the valence states below the Fermi level. We included the s, p, d , 
and f orbitals in the basis set. Due to the complex valence electrons 
of U element, we integrated the Green’s function over a complex 
energy contour (with a 3.5 Ry diameter) using a Gaussian integra- 
tion technique with 40 points on a semi-circle enclosing the oc- 
cupied states. When optimizing the alloy volume, we used 17 ×
17 × 17 uniformly distributed k -points in the irreducible Brillouin 
zones. When calculating the elastic modulus, we used 20,0 0 0–
27,0 0 0 uniformly distributed k -points in the irreducible Brillouin 
zones, which ensures high accuracy for elastic constants. Similar to 
dominant d electron bonding in the transition metals, the 5 f elec- 
trons in the light actinides participated in bonding [53] . According 
to our chosen energy contour, we carefully chose and tested the 
valence states for all alloying elements considered here to achieve 
high convergence.

3. Results 
3.1. Scheme of a multi-principal element alloy design strategy 
According to traditional trial-and-error concept, there is a huge 
composition space of UHEA, e.g ., 1001 and 2002 kinds of 5- and 
6-principal element UHEAs if alloying 14 elements into U. It is im- 
possible to search the complete alloy space using a trial-and-error 
approach and even high-throughput DFT calculations. To solve this 
critical challenge, we propose a multi-principal element alloy de- 
sign strategy and screening criteria to greatly reduce alloy space 
and thus accelerate alloy design in Fig. 1 (top panel). It is achieved 
by ﬁxing majority of multi-principal element in advance. Therefore, 
we postulate four screening criteria including small anisotropy, 
high speciﬁc modulus, high dynamical stability, and high ductility, 
aiming to choose the best seed alloy and suitable dopants. We also 
display the details of screening criteria, the predicted mechanical 
properties for the best seed alloys, as well as the evolution route 
of the best seed alloys (low panel). 
Following our alloying strategy, ﬁrst, a best UTiNb host al- 
loy is selected by doping 14 representative alloying elements 
to typical UTi and UNb binaries [3–9] , respectively. The best 
UTiNb is selected from our 26 ternaries, instead of 91 ternar- 
ies induced by a trial-and-error approach. Next, a best UTiNbTa 
is optimized upon alloying UTiNb. Then, doping UTiNbTa ob- 
tains a best UTiNbTaFe. Finally, we successfully approach a tar- 
get UTiNbTaFeMo from 4 senaries instead of 2002 senaries. The 
designed UTiNbTaFeMo UHEA has lower weight, highest hard- 
ness, and highest strength, while maintains good ductility among 
all the candidates. UTiNbTaFeMo has excellent strength-ductility 
combination and displays signiﬁcant potential to overcome tradi- 
tional strength-ductility trade-off. The mechanical properties are 
greatly enhanced with increasing the number of multi-principal el- 
ement, indicating the feasibility and effectiveness of adopted alloy- 
ing strategy. The increasing of multi-principal element associates 
with an increasing VEC. Alternatively, the mechanical properties 
also show a strong correlation with the VEC and are signiﬁcantly 
improved as increasing VEC, agreeing with measurements for other 
various bcc HEAs. This work develops a general and versatile ap- 
proach for rapidly designing advanced UHEA by greatly reducing 
the space of alloy composition.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Screening procedure of the best seed alloys 
Alloying is the most-widely effective way to design high- 
performance materials. To design low weight, high hardness, and 
high strength UHEAs, the chosen representative lightweight alloy- 
ing elements have different crystalline structures and various mag- 
netic states, including non-magnetic simple metals (Be, Al), non- 
magnetic transition metals (Ti, V, Zr, Nb, Mo, Hf, Ta), ferromag- 
netic transition metals (Fe, Co, Ni), and rare earth metals (Y, Ce). To 
choose the best suitable dopants and seed alloy, we propose four 
screening criteria including small anisotropy, high speciﬁc modu- 
lus, high dynamical stability, and high ductility. To reach the re- 
quirements of screening criteria, we calculate the single-crystal 
elastic constants and polycrystalline elastic properties based on 
conventional procedures [ 41 , 42 ]. 
Typically, elastic anisotropy is expressed by Zener anisotropy 
( A = C44 / C ’), where A = 1 denotes elastic isotropy and any depar- 
ture from unity evaluates the degree of elastic anisotropy. For a 
bcc structure, dynamical (mechanical) stability [54] demands that 
B > 0, C ’ > 0, and C44 > 0. It most generally takes tetragonal elas- 
tic modulus C ’ as an index to estimate the dynamical (mechanical) 
stability of alloys [ 54 , 70 ], where C ’ > 0 implying dynamical sta- 
bility. Based on the calculated screening criteria for all the candi- 
dates in Fig. S1, most of ternaries, all the quaternaries, quinaries, 
and senaries are dynamically stable due to their C ’ > 0. Particu- 
larly, our selected best seed alloys possess the best combination of 
high E / ρ and small A , compared to their counterparts. Addition- 
ally, Pugh’s ratio ( B / G ) [71] , Poisson’s ratio ( v ) [72] , and Cauchy 
pressure ( C12 − C44 ) [73] are usually used to judge the ductility 
and brittleness of materials. Material behaves a ductile manner if 
B / G > 1.75, v > 0.26, and C12 − C44 > 0; otherwise, it is brittle. 
Alternatively, Cauchy pressure can evaluate the toughness of ma- 
terials [73] , where C12 − C44 > 0 corresponds to high toughness. 
Note that toughness is a compromise between strength and duc- 
tility [74] . According to the empirical brittleness/ductility criteria 
shown in Fig. S2, most of ternaries, all the quaternaries, quinar- 
ies, and senaries are ductile due to their B / G > 1.75, v > 0.26, and 
C12 − C44 > 0. 
According to our proposed multi-principal element alloy de- 
sign strategy, ﬁrst, 3-principal element equiatomic bcc U-based 
medium-entropy alloys (UMEAs) are originated by doping 14 rep- 
resentative alloying elements to typical UTi and UNb binaries [3–

4.3. Single-crystal shear moduli of the candidates 
The dislocation motion of the material is closely connected 
with shear modulus of the material [76–78] . According to dislo- 
cation theory, the average stress and strain energy in the stress 
ﬁeld of a single dislocation are proportional to shear modulus. The 
higher shear modulus, the higher the energy required for disloca- 
tion movement, and the larger the dislocation strengthening co- 
eﬃcient [79–81] . For screw dislocations, elastic anisotropy affects 
their propagation [82] . Namely, dislocation motion not only re- 
lates to the magnitude of shear modulus, but also relates to the 
directivity of shear modulus. According to dislocation strengthen- 
ing theory and solution strengthening theory, the increase of ma- 
terial strength is directly proportional to the isotropic shear mod- 
ulus [ 79 , 82 , 83 ]. To improve isotropic shear modulus, we calculate 
the anisotropy of single-crystal shear modulus G( n , m ) of all can- 
didates. To achieve high strength, the UHEAs must simultaneously 
possess a nearly isotropic and high shear modulus. For the sake of 
convenience and simplicity, upon Eq. (5) we calculate and plot the 
three-dimensional surface diagrams of maximum ( Gmax ) and min- 
imum ( Gmin ) of single-crystal shear moduli G( n , m ) in Fig. 4 and 
Figs. S4 and S5. The Gmax and Gmin are used to demonstrate alloy- 
ing effect on the directionality of shear modulus.

is closer to sphere if A is closer to 1. However, the shapes of both 
Gmax and Gmin are much more complex than that of E[ hkl ] . On one

departure from 1, the larger the change of shape between Gmax and 
Gmin . For example, there is a larger shape change between Gmax 
and Gmin of UNbMo ( A = 0.22), corresponding to a smaller shape 
change for UTiNb ( A = 1.18).

4.6. Correlations between the mechanical properties and the VEC 
The regular change of the C ’ originates from the electronic 
structure of transition metals, CAs, and HEAs [96–99] . Accordingly, 
the variation of the C ’ is governed by the D D-band ﬁlling [96–99] . 
Particularly, the D-band ﬁlling is conveniently accessible from ﬁrst- 
principles calculations and can be embodied by the VEC. It is well- 
known that the increase of D-band ﬁlling is parallel with the in- 
crease of VEC. Therefore, the VEC can be used as an accessible and 
reasonable parameter to tailor elastic properties and mechanical 
properties [99] . To clarify this point, we show the calculated C ’ of 
equiatomic bcc UHEAs in Fig. S6, indicating that the C ’ generally 
increases with increasing the VEC. Most importantly, increasing G 
is directly related to the increase of the C ’ [79] . It is argued that 
the G is one of the most important factors affecting alloy strength 
[ 94 , 100 , 101 ]. Consequently, we can directly associate the VEC with 
strength. 
It is generally accepted that VEC parameter qualitatively pre- 
dicts the bcc/fcc phase stability [ 41 , 102 ], hardness [103] , and 
strength [104–113] in HEAs. The VEC of the current bcc UHEAs 
ranges from 4.0 to 5.4, which is consistent with the empirical VEC 
criterion, namely, the bcc is stable when VEC < 6.87 [102] . The 
theoretical calculated G , E , and E / ρ for all the candidates are plot- 
ted as a function of VEC in Fig. S7. Except for Ti-free UHEAs like 
UNbMoTaHfFe, the G , E , and E / ρ are greatly enhanced with increas- 
ing the number of multi-principal element. In particular, the G , E , 
and E / ρ show a strong correlation with VEC and generally increase 
with increasing VEC, which is consistent with the measured E / ρ
of bcc TiZrNbHfTaMox [108] . The target UTiNbTaFeMo has the best 
combination of properties among all the candidates. 
Regardless of empirical hardness models [ 85 , 86 ] and empirical 
strength-hardness correlations [87–90] , the target UTiNbTaFeMo 
UHEA has the highest Vickers hardness and yield strength in Fig. 6 . 
Besides, with a few exceptions of Ti-free UHEAs, the predicted 
hardness and strength strongly enhance as increasing the number 
of multi-principal element in Fig. 6 . This reﬂects the feasibility and 
applicability of the adopted alloy design strategy. The enhanced 
hardness and strength in Fig. 6 corresponds to the reduced duc- 
tility in Fig. S2, indicating the traditional strength-ductility trade- 
off. However, UTiNbTaFeMo has excellent strength-ductility com- 
bination and displays signiﬁcant potential to overcome strength- 
ductility trade-off. Note that the increasing of the number of multi- 
principal element corresponds to an increasing VEC. Namely, me- 
chanical properties exhibit a strong correlation with the VEC and 
greatly increase with increasing the VEC. However, UTiNbMoHf, 
UTiNbFeHf, UTiNbTaFeMo, and UTiNbMoHfFe are exceptions of cor-

phenomenon in the following text. 
Three factors affecting the athermal yield strength are shear 
modulus, atomic size mismatch parameter ( δa ), and elastic mod- 
ulus mismatch parameter ( δG ) [94] . The relationship between VEC 
and σy shown in Fig. 6 is essentially the correlation associating 
G with σy . When considering the δa and δG of all the candidates 
shown in Fig. S8, there is signiﬁcant discrepancy in the δa for all 
the candidates, whereas the negligible difference established in the 
δG . Consequently, the magnitude of δa undoubtedly dominates the 
change of the σy . In other words, the higher the value of the δa , 
the higher the σy , and vice versa. This ﬁnding indicates that the 
σy of a speciﬁc alloy would be signiﬁcantly higher (lower) than 
the overall trend if its δa is much larger (smaller) than the others. 
Following this scenario, it is found that UTiNbTaFeMo, UTiNbMo-

Fig. 6 . Note that the UTiNbMoFe also has a much larger δa but 
does not possess higher σy , which is caused by its large anisotropy 
( A = 0.64). This phenomenon suggests that elastic anisotropy can 
be used as one of the screening criteria as shown in Fig. 1 . The δa 
is easy to tailor when containing more numbers of multi-principal 
element. Namely, higher strength of UHEA is assessable to design 
in terms of its larger δa and δG .

5. Conclusions 
As compared to a traditional trial-and-error concept, our pro- 
posed multi-principal element alloy design strategy and screening 
criteria can substantially reduce the exploration of alloy space and 
thus accelerate alloy design. Namely, we ﬁnd a shortcut to design 
advanced UHEA through gradually determining the best combina- 
tion of multi-principal elements rather than randomly combining 
elements from a trial-and-error approach. To choose the best alloy 
and suitable dopants, the assumed screening criteria include small 
anisotropy, high speciﬁc modulus, high dynamical stability, and 
high ductility. To this end, we calculate single-crystal and polycrys- 
talline elastic properties, Zener anisotropy, single-crystal Young’s 
and shear moduli of equiatomic bcc UMEAs and UHEAs using the 
ﬁrst-principles EMTO–CPA calculations. 
Following our alloying strategy, we ﬁnd a shortcut to design 
UHEA from typical binary (UTi and UNb) to ternary (UTiNb), qua- 
ternary (UTiNbTa), and quinary (UTiNbTaFe). Finally, we ﬁnd a best 
bcc senary UHEA (UTiNbTaFeMo), which has lower weight, high- 
est hardness, and highest strength, while maintains good duc- 
tility among all the candidates. As compared to overestimated 
yield strength predicted from the empirical strength-hardness re- 
lationship, signiﬁcant improvements in strength prediction can be 
achieved using a parameter-free theory containing volume mis- 
match and temperature effect on yield strength. This ﬁnding in- 
dicates that larger volume mismatch corresponds to higher yield 
strength, agreeing with the available measurements. Furthermore,
