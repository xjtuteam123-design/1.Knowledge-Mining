In recent years, it was found that highly purification remarkably improves mechanical properties of Fe��Cr alloys. In this study, a high-purity 60 mass%Cr��Fe alloy was prepared and its mechmical properties such as defonnability and tensile properties were investigated. The deformability was investigated in the temperature range between 1273 and 1573 K, and tensile properties were investigated in the tempera- ture range between 293 and 1073 K. It was found from the present investigation that: (1) A high-purity 60 alloy has the excellent deformability at high temperature. (2) The high-purity 60 alloy with fine grains possesses higher strength than 50 mass%Cr��Fe alloy without decreasing ductility by increasing in chromium content. Introduction Chromium is a very important alloy addition to improve properties such as strength, corrosion resistance and oxida- tion resistance at elevated temperature for high-temperature applications. Expecting these advantageous properties, de- veloping Cr-base alloys has been attempted, but it has been not practically used because of their brittleness. Cr��Fe alloy is thought to be one of the most attractive Cr-base alloys for high-temperature service because that the melting point is higher and the density is smaller than those of Ni-base, Co-base and Fe-base superalloys: for example, the melting point and the density of 50 mass%Cr��Fe alloy are about 1820 KD and 7.5 x 103 kg/m3,2) respectively, and those of Ni-base superalloys are about 1470 to 1670K and about 7.9 to 8.8 x 103 kg/m3,3) respectively. In this study, the mechanical properties of a high-purity 60 mass%Cr��Fe alloy were investigated as the first step of investigations on high- purity Cr-base alloys. Generally, mechanical properties of Fe��Cr alloys such as ductility and defonnability decline with the increment of chromium content.4) In an Fe��Cr alloy containing high chromium, 4750C embrittlement and a-phase embrittlement is also appeared.5'6) The limit of chromium content in a com- mercial Fe��Cr alloy is therefore about 30%. The authors have investigated various propel-ties of a high- purity 50 alloy (referred to as 50Cr). It was found that by purification, 50Cr alloy becomes deformable while maintaining the high strength, and a-phase formation can be suppressed.2'7'8) The mechanical properties of 50Cr and a 50 mass%Cr��Fe��(5��8) mass%W alloy were also inves- tigated. Isozaki et al. showed the effect of W on the strength and the ductility.9) Kako et al. showed the four types of twin- ning deformation and the deformation mechanism. 10) These investigations confirmed that the increase of chrornium is not inherently harmful to an Fe��Cr alloy containing up to *Present address: Nik&o Materials Co., Kitaibaraki 319-1535, Japan. 50 mass% chrornium. From these investigations on 50Cr, a highly-purified Cr�� Fe alloy containing more than 50 mass% chromium may be expected not to become brittle while maintaining the higher strength than an Fe��Cr alloy containing up to 50 mass% chromium. However, there are no reports available con- cerning the mechanical properties of high-purity Cr��Fe al- loys containing more than 50 mass% chromium. The aim of this investigation is to study the mechanical propel-ties of a high-purity 60 mass%Cr��Fe alloy and the effect of increas- ing chromium content up to 60 mass%. The authors have prepared a high-purity 60 mass%Cr��Fe alloy and investigated its mechanical properties such as deformability for study on high-temperature strength and hot ductility, and as tensile properties for study on deformation mechanism. 2. Experimental Procedure A high-purity 60 mass%Cr��Fe alloy (referred to as 60Cr) was prepared from 99.98 mass% chromium and 99.995 mass% electrolytic iron by melting under an argon at- mosphere of 4 x 104 Pa using a newly designed copper-cold- crucible furnace with high frequency induction heating.ll) The chemical composition of 60Cr is shown in Table 1. The purity of 60Cr was higher than 99.98 mass% after the analy- sis of 21 elements. It is reported that ductility and toughness of Fe��Cr alloys are affected by gaseous impurities when the chromium content is higher than 20% 12' 13) The total content of gaseous impurities such as carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and sulfur is about 59 mass ppm. This amount is very Iow for a high chromium alloy. The ingot was hot-forged and hot-rolled in air to round rods of 8 and 7 mm in diameter after heating at 1473 K in a high- purity argon atmosphere. The cast macrostructure was de- stroyed and changed to a fine unifonn grain by forging. When a newly designed heat-resistant alloy is developed, it is important to know its deformability by studying high- temperature strength and hot ductility.
