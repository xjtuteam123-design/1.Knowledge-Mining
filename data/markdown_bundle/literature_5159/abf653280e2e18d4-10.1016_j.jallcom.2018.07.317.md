abstract
The g0/g evolution in nickel superalloys under different stress states and stress values at 1223 K was
simulated through the phase- ﬁeld method to study the 45 /C14 -type rafting mechanism and the in ﬂuence of
orientation deviation on the g0/g evolution. The simulation results demonstrate that for the shear stress
case, 45 /C14 -type rafting appears under the external shear stress of 137 MPa. In 45 /C14 -type rafting, the
equivalent stress inhomogeneity in the g channel induces a directional diffusion of g0-forming elements
into the low-equivalent-stress areas which are distributed along the diagonal directions. Based on the
simulations, a model for the 45
/C14 -type rafting mechanism has been proposed. In addition, the in ﬂuence of
orientation deviation on g0/g evolution originates from the introduction of shear stress components.
With increasing external stress, the shear stress component is increased, which causes the rafting di-
rection to approach the 45
/C14 direction. Furthermore, the existence of orientation deviation accelerates the
process of g0 rafting.

1. Introduction
Nickel-based single-crystal superalloys are among the most
important high-temperature structural materials for
manufacturing turbine blades used in both aeronautical and sta-
tionary gas turbines; single-crystal blades with the [001] crystal-
lographic orientation ([001] single-crystal blades) are of particular
importance [ 1]. During the service of the turbine engine, [001]
single-crystal blades are subjected to combinations of high tem-
peratures and stresses, mainly uniaxial tension produced by cen-
trifugal force. Under such conditions, the initial cubic g0 precipitates
tend to coarsen directionally in the phenomenon known as g0
rafting, which profoundly affects the mechanical performance of
the superalloys [ 2e4]. Therefore, extensive efforts have been
devoted to studying
g0 rafting under tension or compression, by
means of experiments [ 5e9], theoretical studies [ 10,11] and nu-
merical simulations [ 12e19].
However, during service, the [001] single-crystal blade endures
not only uniaxial tension but also biaxial stress produced by thermal
gradients in cooled regions, ﬂowing gas on the blade and local stress
concentration [20]. Therefore, multiple states of stress, such as ten-
sion, compression, and shear, are applied to the single-crystal blades.
In engineering applications, the [001] crystal orientation of the [001]
single-crystal blade is not strictly aligned to the axis of the casting,
but usually deviates within an angle of 15
/C14 (i.e. q < 15/C14 )[ 1]. This angle
is generally called the orientation deviation angle and is shown in
Fig. 1a. When the uniaxial tension sZ is applied along the axis of a
single-crystal blade, it can be decomposed into two components with
the 〈001〉 directions regarded as Cartesian axes (i.e. a YʹOZʹ coordinate
system): a normal stress component along the [001] orientation (i.e.
sZʹ) and a shear stress component within the (001) plane (i.e.sZʹYʹ), as
shown in Fig. 1b. Therefore, during service, both tensile and shear
stresses are applied on the [001] single-crystal blades because of
orientation deviation. Thus, the effect of shear stress on single-crystal
blades during service should also be investigated.

g0 rafting during tensile or compressive creep tests, while the in-

quently reported. Based on a double shear creep test technique [21],
Kamaraj et al. [ 20] observed that g0 rafting occurred at an angle of
45/C14 relative to the direction of shear stress in the CMSX /C0 6 super-
alloy. In the present study, this rafting is called 45 /C14 -type rafting.
Furthermore, Touratier et al. [ 22] found that 45 /C14 -type rafting
appeared at very high cumulated strain values. In addition, it has
been reported that after high-temperature creep deformation, the
g0 precipitates in the necked zone generally raft along the direction
reoriented 45/C14 (or less) away from the tensile axis [ 22,23]. It is well
known that both tensile and shear stresses appear in the necked
zone. The mechanism controlling such 45 /C14 -type rafting has not yet
been clearly elucidated, thus it requires further studies.
With the development of computational simulations, the phase-
ﬁeld method (PFM) has emerged as a powerful tool to simulate
phase transformations and to explore the mechanism of micro-
structural evolutions [ 13e17,19,24,25]. By coupling the plastic
strain ﬁeld with the phase- ﬁeld model [ 13,14,17,19,25], the g-
channel plasticity during high-temperature creep can be consid-
ered in simulation. The 45/C14 -type g0 rafting is accompanied by a high
plastic strain accumulation. Thus, the coupled phase- ﬁeld model
enables the simulation of g0/g evolution under shear conditions.
In this study, we use a coupled phase- ﬁeld model [25] proposed
earlier to simulate the g0/g evolution during creep. Three kinds of
stress states, i.e., shear, monoclinic loading, and tension, are applied
to the [001] single-crystal sample. Here, the [001] crystal orienta-
tion is strictly aligned to the axis of the [001] single-crystal sample.
The schematic of the loading on the sample is shown in Fig. 2. The
shear stress is applied within the plane normal to the [001] direc-
tion, the monoclinic loading is applied along the direction 15 /C14 away
from the [001] direction, and the tensile stress is applied along the
[001] direction. The monoclinic loading of the strict [001] single-
crystal sample is equivalent to the tension applied to the [001]
single-crystal sample with an orientation deviation angle of 15 /C14 .
The simulation results for the tension case are used for comparison
with the monoclinic loading case. The aim of the present study is to
investigate 45 /C14 -type rafting mechanism and the in ﬂuence of
orientation deviation on g0/g evolution.

3.3. Local stress and plastic strain ﬁelds
Fig. 7 shows the equivalent stress ﬁeld in the g/gʹ phases at the
starting and ﬁnal stages of microstructural evolution for three
stress states under the external stress of 137 MPa. It is clear that the
equivalent stress for the applied shear condition is higher than
those for the other two conditions. This is related to the plastic stain
accumulation and spatial arrangement of the gʹ precipitates. In
addition, it should be noted that at the early stage of applied
external stress, the equivalent stress distribution in the g channels
is anisotropic. For the shear stress case, the equivalent stresses in
the g channel near the corners of gʹ precipitates are signi ﬁcantly
different. As shown in Fig. 7 a1, the equivalent stress in the [01 1]
direction is higher than that in the [0 11] direction. This anisotropic
distribution of equivalent stresses in the g channels is explained in
Section 4.1. For the tensile stress case, the equivalent stress in the
horizontal g channels is higher than that in the vertical g channels,
as shown in Fig. 7c1. During the monoclinic loading, the equivalent
stress is higher not only in the horizontal g channels but also in the
cross-sections of the g channels, as shown in Fig. 7 b1. This is the
combined outcome of mixed tension and shear. Later in stress
loading, the distribution of the equivalent stress ﬁeld becomes
uniform for the tensile case, as shown in Fig. 7 c2. However, for
shear and monoclinic loading, the equivalent stress ﬁelds are
changed remarkably, and higher equivalent stresses are concen-
trated at the gʹ precipitates, especially near the gʹ/g interface, as
shown in Fig. 7 a2 and b2. These changes in the stress ﬁelds arise
from the plastic strain accumulation in g channels.
Fig. 8 shows the corresponding equivalent plastic strainﬁelds for
the three stress states with s ¼ 137 MPa. The equivalent plastic
strain in the initial gʹ precipitates is zero under the assumption that
the plastic deformation is only generated in the g channels. In the g
channels, the equivalent plastic strain is very small at the early stage
of microstructural evolution, as shown in Fig. 8a1-c1. As the loading
time increases, the plastic strain accumulates within the
g channels,
and the equivalent plastic strain is the highest for the shear case,
intermediate for the monoclinic loading, and the lowest for the
tension case as shown in Fig. 8 a2-c2. This indicates that the 45 /C14
coarsening of gʹ precipitates is along with high strain accumulations,

4. Discussion
4.1. 45 /C14 -type rafting mechanism
As mentioned above, the 45 /C14 -type rafting is formed under shear
condition (s ¼ 137 MPa) and the distribution of equivalent stress in
the g channels is signi ﬁcantly inhomogeneous. The stress distrib-
uted in the gʹ and g phases is comprised of the coherent internal
stress originating from the gʹ/g lattice misﬁt and the internal stress
derived from the external stress. Without the external stress, the
biaxial internal compressive stress prevails in the g channels and is
balanced by biaxial internal tensile stress in the gʹ precipitates, due
to the negative gʹ/g lattice mis ﬁt, as shown in Fig. 10a. Under an
external stress, an additional internal stress is produced inside the
gʹ and g phases. Under the shear stress conditions ( Fig. 10bec), the
internal stresses induced by the external shear stress are obtained
according to the theorem of conjugate shearing stress and are
shown in Fig. 10b. Note that the elastic equilibrium is always ful-
ﬁlled in the kinetic model; the theorem of conjugate shearing stress
can thus be established. The overlay of the two kinds of internal
stress (that generated by
gʹ/g lattice mis ﬁt and that by external
shear stress) in gʹ/g phase is named as the overlay stress and is
shown in Fig. 10c. Obviously, the distribution of overlay stress in the
g channel near the gʹ/g interface is asymmetric: one corner accu-
mulating stress and the adjacent ones offsetting stress. Thus, the
high- and low-overlay-stress areas in the g channels become
diagonally distributed, as shown in Fig. 10 c. Therefore, the
equivalent stress in the [01 1] direction is higher than that in the
[011] direction under the applied shear stress conditions ( Fig. 7a1).
It is known that the driving force for diffusion is the differences in
the chemical potential of atoms, and the chemical potential of
atoms in the high-equivalent-stress area is higher than that in the
low-equivalent-stress area [ 36]. Furthermore, under this non-
uniform overlay stress, the
g lattice spacing in the horizontal g
channels is increased along the [001] axis but decreased along the
[010] axis, while the
g lattice spacing in the vertical g channels is
increased along the [010] axis but decreased along the [001] axis, as
shown in Fig. 1 1b, resulting in the formation of preferential diffu-
sion channels. Under the interaction of inhomogeneous equivalent
stress ﬁeld and preferential diffusion channels, the Al atoms (i.e., gʹ-
forming elements) diffuse along the preferential diffusion channels
to the area where the overlay stress is lower (i.e., the area where the
equivalent stress is lower), as marked by the dotted arrow in
Fig. 10c. Thus, 45 /C14 -type rafting is formed under shear condition.
A sketch of the N-type rafting mechanism under tension is
shown in Fig. 10d. It is observed that high-overlay-stress areas are
formed in horizontal g channels while the low-overlay-stress areas
are generated in vertical g channels, yielding high and low equiva-
lent stresses in the horizontal and vertical g channels, respectively,
under external tension ( Fig. 7 c1). Under the combined action of
equivalent stress ﬁeld and lattice distortion (Fig. 1 1c), the gʹ-forming
elements diffuse preferentially along the [010] direction to the ver-
tical g channels where overlay stress (or equivalent stress) is lower,
as marked by the dotted arrow in Fig. 10d, thus resulting in the N-
type rafting. The P-type gʹ rafting mechanism under the compres-
sion condition is analogous with the N-type rafting mechanism.
According to the analysis of the gʹ rafting mechanisms for different
stress states, it can be concluded that the formation of high- and

4.2. In ﬂuence of orientation deviation on g0/g evolution
In the present study, the in ﬂuence of orientation deviation on
the g0/g evolution is investigated through the equivalent simulation
of the microstructural evolution under monoclinic loading in a
sample with a strict [001] crystallographic orientation. The orien-
tation deviation introduces a shear stress component under tensile
stress application along the sample axis. The shear stress compo-
nent causes the equivalent stress in g0 and g phases to increase, and
thus the equivalent plastic strain in the g channels is accumulated
more rapidly. These changes in the equivalent stress and plastic
strain can further affect the process of g0 and g phase evolutions.
When the tensile stress value is lower ( <100 MPa), the inﬂuence of
orientation deviation on the g0/g evolution is negligible, because
the value of the shear stress component is lower. It is noted that
when the tensile stress value is higher ( >137 MPa), the inﬂuence of
orientation deviation is more obvious. The higher shear stress
component drives the g0 precipitates to raft along directions devi-
ated from the 〈001〉 orientations and approach the 45 /C14 direction.
5. Conclusions
In this study, a phase- ﬁeld model coupled with a viscoplastic
model was used to simulate the g0/g evolution under different
stress states at 1223 K. The corresponding evolutions of equivalent
stress and equivalent plastic strain ﬁelds were analyzed. The pur-
pose was to study the 45 /C14 -type rafting mechanism and the in ﬂu-
ence of orientation deviation on the g0/g evolution. The following
conclusions were obtained:
1. The 45 /C14 -type rafting appears under the shear condition with the
shear stress value of 137 MPa. Under shear conditions, the high-
and low-equivalent-stress areas in the g channels are distrib-
uted along diagonal directions, and preferential diffusion
channels are generated along the [001] (/[010]) direction in
horizontal (/vertical) g channels, causing the gʹ-forming ele-
ments to diffuse directionally from the high-to low-equivalent-
stress areas along the preferential diffusion channels, thus
forming 45/C14 -type rafting.
2. Under applied stress conditions, the existence of orientation
deviation in a non-strict [001] single-crystal sample accelerates
the process of g0 rafting because of the introduction of a shear
stress component; higher external stress values give to larger
shear stress components and thus cause the rafting direction to
approach 45/C14 direction.
3. When the external stress values are equal, the equivalent stress
under shear conditions is higher than that under monoclinic
loading conditions, and the equivalent stress under tension
conditions is the lowest; the equivalent plastic strain under these
three conditions has the same order as the equivalent stress.
