Introduction 
The tensile creep anisotropy at 760°C of y' strengthened single crystal superalloys such as 
Mar-M200, Mar-M247 and Mar-M002 has previously been investigated by some workers (1,2,3). In 
particular it has been shown that although the <001> oriented crystals have excellent creep 
resistance, the highest stress rupture lives are attained with crystals whose orientations are 
close to <iii>. It was further shown that when the orientations move away from these two direc- 
tions, rupture life decreases and it becomes drastically short for orientations close to <011>. 
The object of this note is to extend the investigation regarding the effect of mechanical 
anisotropy on creep to two other recently developed single crystal superalloys, CMSX-2 and alloy 
454 destined for turbine blade applications (4,5). 
Materials and Experimental Procedures 
The chemical compositions of CMSX-2 and alloy 454 are given in Table i. Dendritic single 
crystal bars were cast by the withdrawal process using either seed crystals or a selector. The 
cristallographic orientations were determined by the Laue back reflection technique. The CMSX-2 
crystals were solution treated at 1315°C for 30 minutes and then air cooled. The ageing treatment 
was carried out at i050°C for 16 hours followed by 850°C for 24 hours. Single crystals of alloy 
454 were solution treated at 1285°C for 4 hours, air cooled and then aged at 870°C for 32 hours. 
The 7'precipitates, about 0.5~m in size, were found to have a cuboidal morphology in both alloys 
after the precipitation heat treatments. Constant load creep tests were performed in air both 
at ONERA and IHI at 760°C. Most tests were run to rupture, but some others were interrupted 
well before failure. Thin foils were cut from the interrupted creep specimens normal to the 
tensile stress axis in order to identify the operative deformation mechanisms by transmission 
electron microscopy. After rupture, the final orientations of selected specimens were determined 
by X-ray or electron diffraction.

These results are in disagreement with those reported for Mar-M200 single crystals which show a 
drastic decrease in creep strength for relatively small misorientations around [001]. In the case 
of CMSX-2 alloy the rupture lives are extremely short for crystal orientations close to the 
[011] - IT11] boundary. Large primary creep and short rupture lives are associated with high 
total elongations. The orientation dependence in creep at 760°C of alloy 454 is similar to that 
of CMSX-2, and unlike Mar-M200 (and Mar-M247) these two recent alloys exhibit extremely poor 
creep resistance in the [~II] orientation. 
Based on T.E.M. work and mechanical studies, Leverant and coworkers have previously shown 
(1) that the primary creep of <001> oriented Mar-M200 single crystals at 760°C is controlled by 
~iII~ <i12> slip. They explained the orientation dependence of creep strength in terms of Schmid 
factors and multiplicity of slip for [1111 <112> systems. Their argument was based on the fact 
that <001> and <Iii> oriented crystals would favour multiple slip leading to a strong work 
hardening and consequently long rupture lives. On the other hand, crystals oriented for single 
slip cannot be work hardened and therefore the rupture lives would be shorter. Analysis of the 
lattice rotations during creep at 774°C of Mar-M247 single crystals tends to corroborate these 
overall observations (2). Indeed, crystals oriented for single slip require large rotations 
before the additional intersecting slip systems become operative and hence one observes large 
primary creep, high ductility and short life for such orientations. On the contrary, specimens 
oriented for multiple slip require little or no rotation prior to the onset of the so-called 
secondary creep ; the creep rate is therefore low and the life is long. The observation that the 
<iii> orientations inMar-M247 or Mar-200 single crystals are the most creep resistant was 
justified on the basis of low Schmid factor for {iII~<112> slip. Although the creep behaviour 
of near <001> and near <011> oriented crystals of CMSX-2 and alloy 454 can be rationalized in 
terms of Schmid factor values and observed rotations, these considerations obviously cannot 
explain the extremely poor creep resistance of <111> oriented specimens. An attempt is made here 
to correlate the operative deformation mechanisms and the macroscopic creep behaviour of CMSX-2 
single crystals whose orientations are relatively close to [011] and [511]. 
Specimen G has an orientation of 13 ° off [011] ; its creep life and elongation are 30 hours 
and 8.6% respectively. After failure, the tensile axis of the specimen has rotated approximat- 
~%y towards [~Ii] which is the slip direction for the most highly stressed system (III) [~Ii]. 
Another specimen machined from the same single crystal bar was crept to a strain of 1.8% and 
the deformation structure is shown in Figure 4. The high density of slip induced stacking 
faults indicates intense slip activity on the (111) [~11] system ; this is in agreement with 
the measured rotation of specimen G. After failure, this specimen has an elliptical cross sec- 
tion and it shows a smooth planar surface with intense coarse slip traces parallel to the 
fracture surface throughout the gauge length. These observations confirm that slip is confined 
to a single plane. The fracture surface in this ~se contrasts with the rough, fibrous fracture 
surface and circular cross section of specimens whose orientations are within 20 ° of [001], 
and which shows multiple slip characteristics. 
Specimen D has an orientation of 8 °. off [TII] near the [TlI]-[011] boundary. It has a very 
short creep rupture life (32 hours), and a very high elongation at failure (26%). The cross 
section after failure is elliptical as in the case of specimen G, but the fracture surface is 
highly irregular and not planar. The crystal axis has rotated towards [511], but the failure 
occurred before the [~ll] -[011] boundary was reached. The deformation structure after i% creep 
in a specimen whose orientation is identical to specimen D is shown in Figure 5. In this case 
the dislocations are primarily confined to the matrix and there is no evidence of ~' shearing 
by {111~<112> slip. Two families of matrix dislocations with a/2[~01] and a/2[i10] Burgers 
vectors were identified. Considering the initial orientation of specimen D, the rotation towards 
[~11] is consistent with the fact that there is an almost equal activity on the (ill) [i01] and 
(Ill) [TI0] slip systems which are the most highly stressed systems. The resulting 
[211] glide direction is the bisector of the two elementary glide directions [~01] and [TIO]. 
This coplanar slip would not result in any strain hardening of the material and this can explain 
the high creep strain and poor resistance of specimen D. 
In order to gain a better insight into the creep behaviour of <Iii> oriented crystals of 
various superalloys, the following aspects have to be considered. Clearly, from Schmid factor 
considerations, orientations near [~II] would favour cube slip. Indeed, cube slip has been 
observed by Giamei (6) in creep at 899°C in the matrix of PWA 1444 which is a cobalt stripped 
version of Mar-M200, this latter alloy being highly creep resistant in the [~II] orientation.

orientation becomes very strong. This indicates that the creep response in the [TII] orientation 
is extremely sensitive to the alloy composition. It has been argued (6) that the matrix of PWA 
1444 is presumably susceptible to the occurence of cube slip and that addition of about 6% rhenium 
counteracts this effect by lowering the stacking fault energy of the matrix~ thereby preventing 
cross slip to'{0011 planes.. Comparing the creep behaviour of PWA 1444 and Mar-M200, one can argue 
that the effect of'cobalt is similar to that of rhenium in lowering the stacking fault energy 
of the matrix, thus preventing the occurence of cube slip in<ill>oriented Mar-M200. Based on 
T.E.M. observations, which did not show any clear evidence of cube slip in CMSX-2 at 760°C, it 
appears that the poor creep resistance of near<ill>oriented single crystals of CMSX-2 and alloy 
454 may well be related to the confinement of dislocation activity in an octahedral plane of the 
matrix between the aligned cuboidal ?' precipitates. This alternative explanation is further 
supported by the microstructural studies on Mar-M200 and PWA 1444 single crystals which show that 
the elimination of cobalt results in a change of morphology and size of the?'precipitates. Indeed, 
in PWA 1444 (cobalt-free version of Mar-M200) the precipitates are large and cuboidal, as opposed 
to the smaller odd-shaped particles in Mar-M200, providing rather long matrix "corridors". It 
is precisely the PWA 1444 single crystals which are shown to possess poor creep resistance in 
the ~ll]orientation. These overall observations suggest that the relative geometrical 
arrangement of ? and ~' phases and them'particle size, which can be modified by compositional 
changes and/or heat treatments, presumably play a much more important role in determining the 
poor (or good) creep resistance of <111> oriented crystals than the matrix stacking fault energy 
effect proposed by Giamei (6).
