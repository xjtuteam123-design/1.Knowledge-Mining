abstract
Al0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high entropy alloy was synthesized by mechanical alloying (MA) and spark plasma
sintering (SPS). Only a FCC structure phase during MA was observed, while an ordered BCC phase and a
FCC phase after SPS. The ordered BCC phase was much ﬁner than the FCC phase after SPS. Moreover,
twinning occurred only in the FCC phase during the SPS process. Al
0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high entropy alloy
with a density of 98.2% and nanoscale twins exhibits excellent mechanical properties at room
temperature. The yield stress, compressive strength and Vickers hardness of the alloy are 2732 MPa,
3172 MPa, 10.1% and 712 7 12 H
V, respectively. The fracture mechanism of this alloy is observed as
intergranular fracture.

1. Introduction
A novel class of alloys deﬁned as high entropy alloys (HEAs)
without one or two principal elements deﬁnitely have expanded
the scope of conventional notions of designing alloys since
2004 [1]. Unlike traditional alloys [2,3], HEAs with multi-principal
elements contain at least ﬁve principal elements, the atomic
fractions of each element is between 5 at. % and 35 at. %. The
reported works reveal that HEAs predominantly consist of a
mixture of simple FCC and/or BCC solid solutions owing to high
entropy of mixing, instead of intermetallic phases or other
complex phases [4–6]. A wide range of HEAs exhibit high hard-
ness, high strength, distinctive electrical and magnetic properties,
high-temperature softening resistance, as well as favorable com-
bination of compression strength and ductility [7–9]. This combi-
nation of properties and the particular structures of HEAs are
attractive for a number of potential engineering applications.
Though the synthesis of HEAs have been achieved by many
different approaches, almost all r eported mechanical properties of
HEAs were obtained by compression testing or measuring hardness in
cast conditions (vacuum arc melting). Recently, only several litera-
tures are yet available where a few high entropy alloy systems
processed by mechanical alloying (MA) have been exploited[10–15].
However, the reported HEAs synthesized by MA were concentrated
on microstructures, the mechanical properties have not yet been
completely investigated. Thus in this paper, mechanical properties of
HEAs fabricated by MA were systemic ally investigated. Considering
spark plasma sintering (SPS) could rapidly consolidate powders to
high density by applying pressure and passing electric pulse current
within short soaking time[16], the combination of MA and SPS was
selected in this paper. To achieve desirable mechanical properties and
unique microstructures, Al and Ti with larger atomic radius than
other constituent elements are usually added into some HEAs
[4,17–19], such as AlxCrFeNiCoCu, AlCrFeNiCoTix,e t c .H o w e v e r ,H E A s
with high Al or/and Ti content possess high compressive strengths
while inferior fracture strains unfortunately. Increasing concentration
of Al or/and Ti could lead to higher strength and hardness, but
plasticity of HEAs dr opped signiﬁcantly [17,20]. Thus, to achieve
optimum properties, the concentrations of Al and Ti elements must
be in an appropriate range. In addition, according to previous report
[15], the alloying sequence during the MA process for the studied
HEAs was concluded as Al-Cu-Co-Ni-Fe-Ti-Cr. Comprehen-
sively considering all the factors discussed above, Al
0.6CoNiFeTi0.4
high entropy alloy was designed and fabricated by MA and SPS in this
paper. The structural and phase evolution during milling and sub-
sequent consolidation processes were carefully investigated, as well
as microstructure and mechanical properties after SPS.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. XRD analysis
Fig. 1 shows the XRD patterns of Al 0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high entropy
alloy. It is clear that the initial blending powder (0 h) includes
diffraction patterns of all the pure elements from Fig. 1 (a). As
milling time increases, drastic decrement of diffraction intensity
is observed. The peaks of both Al and Co elements dissolve most
rapidly after 6 h of milling. Most of the diffraction peaks can
hardly be seen after being ball milled for 15 h. The complete
disappearance of all the elemental peaks and the formation of a
simple FCC solid solution are evident within 30 h of milling. To
reﬁne grain size and facilitate the formation of ﬁnal homogeneous
solid solution, the milling time was extended to 45 h. Subse-
quently, the powder was subjected to wet ball milling (in ethanol)
for 4 h with the aim to achieve ﬁne metallic powder for being
conducive to sintering. As the milling time prolongs, the diffrac-
tion peaks exhibit no change except for a minor broadening after
ball milling for 45 h, but a signiﬁcant increment of diffraction
intensity as the milling time reaches up to 49 h. Based on
Scherrer’s formula, the crystallite size and lattice strain for the
FCC phase (listed in Table 1 ) in different milling time are
estimated to be 12 nm and 1.02% (30 h), 9 nm and 1.16% (45 h),
5 nm and 1.85% (49 h), respectively. The calculated results are in
agreement with the crystallite size of the FCC phase decreasing
predominantly and the lattice strain of the simple FCC solid
solution increasing gradually with extended milling time.
Furthermore, it’s obvious that the diffraction peaks exhibit no
change except for a minor broadening and a signiﬁcant increment
of diffraction intensity when milled hour reaches up to 49 h can
be attributed to the signiﬁcantly reﬁned nanocrystalline and high
lattice strain. One thing need to be noted here is that the
increment of lattice strain could be due to increasing dislocation
density and increasing grain boundary fraction during MA, as well
as size mismatch effect between the constituent elements [14].
According to previous reports [10,12,14], the circulation of crush-
ment and agglomeration caused by intensive plastic deformation
can lead to reﬁnement of crystallite and formation of the FCC
solid solution. In addition, the energy stored in the nanoscale
grain boundaries also can better account for the formation of the
FCC solid solution. Yeh proposed that high entropy of mixing

The calculated results of L and d for Al 0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high
entropy alloy are 1.17 and 6.46%, which conform to Yang’s
proposition, demonstrating that the formation of FCC solid solu-
tion is reasonable in this alloy system.
The XRD pattern of Al 0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high entropy alloy after
SPS at 1000 1C is shown in Fig. 1 (b). It suggests that the alloy
consolidated after SPS contains not only a FCC phase, but also a
newly formed BCC phase. According to the relative intensity,
it could be inferred that the main phase of Al
0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high
entropy alloy is FCC phase, demonstrating SPS causes the partial
transformation from FCC to BCC phase. Based on the X-ray
diffraction results, the lattice parameters for the FCC and BCC
phases are calculated to be 3.69 ˚A and 3.08 ˚A, respectively. After
SPS, crystallite sizes of some phases might not be retained in
nanoscale. Hence, the crystallite sizes do not satisfy Scherrer’s
formula which can estimate crystallite in nanoscale. Lattice strain
of the FCC phase reduces drastically from 1.85% to less than 0.16%,
indicating defects introduced during the MA process are nearly
annihilated after consolidation by SPS, which indicates that
formation of the BCC phase is caused by reordering due to the
annihilation of defects introduced during the MA process [13].
Moreover, complex pulsed electric current during the SPS process
also can promote phase evolution and lead to the formation of the
BCC phase [16].
3.2. Microstructure and phase analysis
Fig. 2 illustrates the SEM analysis results of bulk Al 0.6CoNi-
FeTi0.4 high entropy alloy consolidated by SPS. Porosity is
observed in Fig. 2 (a) and two distinctive regions (regions A and
B) are shown in high magniﬁed image ( Fig. 2 (b)). Actually, the
alloy achieves a high relative density up to 98.2% which is
calculated with respect to actual density and theoretical density.
It is illustrated in Fig. 2 (b) that regions A and B are irregularly
polygonal areas. Region B might be the phase removed by aqua
regia solution. Based on Fig. 2 , region A accounts for a higher
volume fraction which reveals that region A is most likely to be
the main phase of the alloy. It can be conﬁrmed from Fig. 1(b) that
region A corresponds to the FCC phase while
region B is relative to the BCC phase. It is of interest that the
sizes of most phases are smaller than 1
mm. Consequently
elemental compositions of these two distinctive regions are not
able to be accurately detected by EDS/SEM with micron-sized
accuracy. These phases are ultra-scale in size which is mainly
attributed to short-time centering during the SPS procedure, thus
there is not enough time for grains to grow.
The TEM images and SAED patterns of Al 0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high
entropy alloy after SPS are shown in Fig. 3. It is clear that there are
three phases, gray contrast phase, dark contrast phase and granular
nanoscale phase with a white contrast from Fig. 3 (a). The corre-
sponding [011] zone axis diffraction pattern ( Fig. 3 (b)) of region
1 conﬁrms a FCC structure, the corresponding [001] zone axis
diffraction pattern (Fig. 3(c)) of region 2 reveals the presence of an
ordered BCC phase (B2) after SPS. In other words, the gray contrast
phase is FCC phase and the dark contrast phase is BCC (B2) phase.
Based on the SAED analysis results, the lattice parameters for the
FCC and BCC (B2) phases are calculated to be 3.70 ˚A and 3.11 ˚A,
which could be postulated that the results are approximately in
agreement with 3.69 ˚A (FCC) and 3.08 ˚A (BCC) based on XRD
results respectively. However, according to the chemical analysis
results of EDS/TEM, some black contrast areas and white areas are
FCC phase in Fig. 3(a). Chemical analysis results of phases by EDS/
TEM are listed in Table 2 and most of the phases are labeled in
Fig. 3 (a). Based on Table 2 , as compared to nominal composition
of Al
0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 alloy, the FCC phase is Fe-rich, Al-depleted and
Ti-depleted. But the granular nanoscale phase is Al-rich and Ni-
depleted, as well as Ti-depleted. The composition of the BCC (B2)
phase deviates slightly from nominal composition of Al 0.6CoNi-
FeTi0.4 high entropy alloy. The grain size of the FCC phase shows a
wide distribution ranging from several hundred nanometers to
1 mm, in contrast, the average grain size of the BCC (B2) phase is
approximately 300 nm and the granular nanoscale phase is not
more than 100 nm. It is a reasonable explanation that the BCC
(B2) phase is formed from phase evolution and reordering of the
initial FCC phase during SPS. The granular nanoscale phase which
is not characterized noticeably in the XRD pattern of the alloy
after SPS can better be accounted for the characteristic peak of the
phase which is abnormally weak caused by nanocrystalline
nature and small volume fraction of the phase.
Fig. 3(d) and (e) illustrate FCC twins which have been veriﬁed by
EDS/TEM and the corresponding diffraction patterns of region 4 (the
inset in Fig. 3 (d)) and region 5 ( Fig. 3(f)) with typical twin electron
diffraction patterns. The lamella thickness of the FCC twin (Fig. 3 (d))
is less than 50 nm, and the lamella thickness of the FCC twin
(Fig. 3(e)) is between 40 nm and 70 nm. To the best of our knowledge,
this is the ﬁrst time nanoscale twins have been obtained in HEAs
processed by MA and SPS. It is worth pointing out that the similar
nanoscale twins in Al 0.6CoNiFeCrTi0.4 high entropy alloy which has
not been reported have also been ob served by our research group.

3.3. Mechanical properties
Mechanical properties of part of the investigated HEAs at
ambient temperature are listed in Table 3 . The listed HEAs
possess the most excellent mechanical properties in their alloy
systems. According to all reported works, the compressive yield
strength and fracture strength of mostly investigated HEAs are
not more than 1800 MPa and 2200 MPa except some HEAs
illustrated in Table 3 . Besides, a majority of HEAs’ compression
ratios are below 15%. Apparently, bulk Al 0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high
entropy alloy displays excellent mechanical properties. The yield
strength (sy), compressive strength ( smax), compression ratio ( ep)
and Vickers hardness (H V) of the alloy are 2732 MPa, 3172 MPa,
10.1% and 712 7 12 HV, respectively. Fig. 4 shows stress–strain
curve of Al 0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high entropy alloy after SPS under
compression at ambient temperature. Zhang et al. has reported
two HEAs prepared by copper-mold vacuum suction casting (with
a diameter of 5 mm) which possess the best compressive proper-
ties [18,24], viz., AlCoNiFeCrTi 0.5 and AlCoNiFeCrNb 0.5. Based on
Table 3 , the compressive properties of Al 0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high
entropy alloy also can compare favorably with these two alloys,
especially Al0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high entropy alloy exhibits the highest
yield strength compared with all reported HEAs.
The high strength and high hardness of Al 0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high
entropy alloy is associated with the ordered BCC (B2) phase and
nanoscale phases, as well as solid solution strengthening mechan-
ism of Ti and Al atoms. In addition, although the volume fraction
of nanoscale twins is less than 10%, these nanoscale twins in the
FCC phase might have a strong effect on strengthening the alloy
[31]. Generally the strengths of traditional alloys could be con-
trolled by dislocation interactions with grain boundaries [32,33],
in contrast, dislocation interactions with nanoscale twins might
be more complicated [34]. During the compression process,

Intergranular fracture feature is characterized clearly in Fig. 5 .
Some white particles are evidently observed on the fracture

be postulated that the particles correspond to the granular
nanoscale phase observed in TEM images. Moreover, it can be
inferred that the particles are not combined ﬁrmly with the
matrix from Fig. 5 . In conclusion, the fracture mechanism of
Al0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high entropy alloy is performed at intergranular
fracture mode.
4. Conclusions
Al0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high entropy alloy with high performance has
been successfully designed and synthesized by MA and SPS. Only
a FCC phase is observed during MA. After SPS, partial FCC phase
transforms into a BCC phase (B2), together with the formation of
nanoscale twins in the FCC phase. Granular nanoscale phase with
a white contrast has been clearly observed both in TEM and
fractographic images. Al 0.6CoNiFeTi0.4 high entropy alloy with
nanoscale twins performs with high strength and high hardness
at room-temperature, particularly displaying the highest yield
strength compared with all reported HEAs. Yield strength, com-
pressive strength and Vickers hardness (H V) are as high as
2732 MPa, 3172 MPa and 712 712 HV, respectively. Compression
ratio of the alloy is 10.1%. Intergranular fracture feature is
observed from the fracture surface of the alloy reveals that the
fracture mechanism of the alloy is mainly intergranular fracture.
