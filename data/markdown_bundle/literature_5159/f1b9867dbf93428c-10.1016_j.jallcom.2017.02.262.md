abstract
In this study, the temperature-dependent antiphase boundary energy is established from a view of
energy conservation. Subsequently, a theoretical model for yield strength anomaly of Ni-base superalloys
at elevated temperature is developed on the basis of pre-existing yield strength model and traditional
precipitation strengthening theories. The model is veri ﬁed at different temperatures by comparison with
experimental data of several Ni-base superalloys. The excellent agreement between predictions and
experiments shows a good prediction ability of this model. This study provides a quantitatively theo-
retical characterization method for yield strength anomaly of Ni-base superalloys at elevated
temperature.

1. Introduction
Ni-base superalloys mainly strengthened by g0 precipitates are
widely applied in turbine engines for power generation, gas turbine
jet engines for aircraft, and nuclear power reactors due to their
excellent strength, good corrosion resistance [1e3]. The service
process is inevitable to experience different high temperature
environment. Therefore, the yield strength of g0-strengthened Ni-
base superalloy at elevated temperature is always attracting more
and more attention.
In the past, much effort has been done to study the yield
strength of g0-strengthened Ni-base superalloy at elevated tem-
perature [1e6]. Large of experiment results show that g0-
strengthened Ni-base superalloys exhibit the obvious yield
strength anomaly (YSA) with the increment of temperature [4e6]:
the yield strength initially changes little with temperature and
reaches a peak at about 800 /C14 C, and beyond the peak temperature, a
sharp decrease appears [4e6]. This YSA occurs at the temperatures
where these materials are commonly used [7], which makes it
become an important study.
The possible reasons causing YSA has been surveyed. Five
alternative dislocation mechanisms were enumerated by Eckhard
[7], which might cause the peak of the strength of g0-strengthened
Ni-base superalloys. However, the recognized explanation is Kear-
Wilsdorf mechanism [8,9], which believes the screw super-
dislocations locally cross slip onto the {100} plane from {1 1 1}
plane as a result of thermal activation. The cross-slipped segments
form the K-W locks which prevent the subsequent motion of the
remaining parts of the dislocations on the (1 1 1) [ 101] slip system
[10]. With the temperature increasing, more cross slips are pro-
moted and therefore a higher yield strength is achieved [10]. Be-
sides, heat treatment [1], microstructural parameters such as g0
volume fraction and size [1 1,12], antiphase boundary energy [13],
grain boundary carbide [14] etc., affect YSA as well.
Through the factors affecting the YSA already have been iden-
tiﬁed, every time a new derivative of Ni-base superalloy appears,
the study of YSA is still con ﬁned to experiment which may be
difﬁcult to accomplish generally. Although some scholars give the
preliminary ideas by means of strengthening contributions super-
position [1 1,12], they couldn't provide an explicit expression of the
model. Probably they haven't ﬁnd a suitable way of considering
temperature. Therefore, the YSA of Ni-base superalloys at elevated

2. Theoretical model
The yield strength of g0-strengthened Ni-base superalloys ex-
hibits YSA with the increment of temperature. The dominant
mechanisms are shearing of the g0 precipitates by dislocations and
cross slips of dislocations. There are many factors causing the peak
of the strength of g0-strengthened Ni-base superalloys, such as g0
volume fraction and size, antiphase boundary energy, grain
boundary carbide, in which the antiphase boundary energy plays a
vital role. However, antiphase boundary energy under different
temperatures are dif ﬁcult to determine, which make it hard to
describe and predict the yield strength theoretically. In this study,
the temperature-dependent antiphase boundary energy is estab-
lished ﬁrstly. As a result, an explicit theoretical model for YSA of Ni-
base superalloys is established. Speci ﬁc modeling process is as
follows:

Some scholars [8,22] established a yielding model to describe
the temperature-dependent yield strength only for speci ﬁc Ni-base
superalloys. However, the in ﬂuence of temperature on some
physical quantities, such as antiphase boundary energy and shear
modulus, is ignored. Our model ﬁrst considers the temperature
dependence of every physical quantity and gives the explicit
expression of yield strength for g0-strengthened Ni-base superal-
loys, and it could be applied to many g0-strengthened Ni-base su-
peralloys instead of a speci ﬁc Ni-base superalloys. The physical
parameters needed in the model are easy to obtain as well. Besides,
the modeling thought is corresponding to the description that any
decrease in yield strength of the g matrix due to rise in temperature
is compensated for by an increase in yield strength of the g0
strengthened phase [1e3,5], and the effect of thermally activated
cross slip of dislocations is revealed through introducing a proba-
bility function [1e3,5].
4. Conclusions
In summary, a theoretical model for YSA of Ni-base superalloys
at elevated temperature is established on the basis of a
temperature-dependent yield strength model and the traditional
precipitation strengthening theories. By comparing predicted
values of model and experimental results, it could be found that the
model possesses an amazing predicted ability. This study provides a
quantitatively theoretical characterization method for the YSA of
Ni-base superalloys at elevated temperature.
