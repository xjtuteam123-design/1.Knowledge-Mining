ABSTRACT 
To optimize the microstructure and fracture toughness of Nb
–
Si based alloys, the effect of Zr addition on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties was investigated in Nb
–
16Si alloy. The results show that Zr element 
promotes the sluggish eutectoid reaction of (Nb, Zr)
3
Si → Nbss + α -(Nb, Zr)
5
Si
3
, and Nb-16Si-8Zr alloy only 
contains Nbss and α -(Nb, Zr)
5
Si
3 
phases. From the energy point of view, as the Zr content reaches 4 at.%, (Nb, 
X)
5
Si
3 
phase is more stable than (Nb, X)
3
Si phase. With the increase of Zr content, the fracture toughness in -
creases since high Zr content refines eutectic structure and increases the content of primary Nbss. The thickness 
of primary Nbss has a strong influence on crack propagation. Most of the cracks can destroy primary Nbss phase 
with the thickness of (20 ± 2) μ m and continue to propagate in the original direction. However, it will deflect 
when it intersects primary Nbss phase with a thickness of about (30 ± 2) μ m. The improvement of compressive 
properties was caused by solid solution of Zr. 
1. Introduction 
Nb
–
Si based alloys are thought to be one of the alternative high- 
temperature structure materials used by aeronautic and aerospace ap -
plications due to their relatively low density ( < 7.2 g/cm
3
) and high 
melting point ( > 1750 
◦
C) [ 1 – 5 ]. Nb
–
Si based alloys are composed of 
intermetallic silicide phases and niobium solid solution (Nbss) phases. 
The improvement of room-temperature fracture toughness is mainly 
caused by Nbss phases, while the high-temperature mechanical prop -
erties are mainly ameliorated by silicide phases. However, their poor 
room-temperature fracture toughness becomes a crucial issue in 
restricting the application of Nb
–
Si based alloys [ 6 – 8 ]. The room- 
temperature fracture toughness of Nb
–
Si based alloys is far below the 
threshold needed for material processing and assembly. 
Different Si element contents form different silicide phases, for 
example, the silicides in Nb
–
40Si (at.% is used throughout the text for 
the compositions) and Nb
–
16Si alloys are NbSi
2 
and Nb
3
Si, respectively 
[ 9 ]. In order to obtain excellent mechanical properties, the contents of Si 
element typically range from 10 at.% to 30 at.% [ 2 , 10 , 11 ]. It should be 
noted that the high silicon contents lead to primary phase 
transformation from Nbss to brittle phases (Nb
5
Si
3
, Nb
3
Si), which has a 
significant impact on the room-temperature fracture toughness [ 12 , 13 ]. 
In binary Nb
–
Si alloys, the eutectoid reaction occurs at 1770 
◦
C (Nb
3
Si 
→ Nbss + α -Nb
5
Si
3
), but the decomposition reaction is sluggish [ 14 , 15 ]. 
By heat-treating at 1300 
◦
C – 1600 
◦
C or alloying, the decomposition 
reaction of eutectoid can be accelerated [ 16 – 18 ]. 
Compared with Nb
3
Si, Nb
5
Si
3 
is more beneficial to room- 
temperature fracture toughness [ 19 – 21 ]. Earlier studies have shown 
that the addition of Ti and Zr has a positive effect on accelerating 
eutectoid decomposition [ 22 – 24 ]. The influence of Zr on multi-element 
Nb
–
Si based alloys has been studied. However, multi-element Nb
–
Si 
based alloys contain more Ti (usually above 20 at.%) [ 25 , 26 ]. Therefore, 
the single Zr is rarely studied in binary Nb
–
16Si alloys. In this paper, 
the effect of Zr addition on the microstructure and mechanical proper -
ties of Nb
–
16Si alloys has been systematically investigated, and there is 
a concern on the phase composition and solidification path, which is 
expected to provide a method and reference for improving the room- 
temperature fracture toughness of Nb
–
Si based alloys.

2. Experimental procedures 
The nominal compositions of Nb
–
Si based alloys employed in this 
paper are Nb
–
16Si, Nb-16Si-1Zr, Nb-16Si-2Zr, Nb-16Si-4Zr and Nb- 
16Si-8Zr, respectively. Buttons of those alloys were prepared inside a 
water-cooled copper crucible by arc melting with a non-consumable 
tungsten electrode in argon atmosphere. The purity of raw materials 
used in this experiment was 99.95 wt% of Nb, 99.99 wt% of Si, 99.94 wt 
% of Zr. In order to reduce the segregation of elements and obtain ho -
mogeneous specimens, every button ingot was remelted six times. The 
mass of each button ingot is about 120 g. The weight loss of the buttons 
obtained by melting is less than 0.5%, so the composition of alloys is 
nearly the same as the nominal composition in this paper. Table 1 shows 
the composition of all ingots with wavelength dispersive X-ray spec -
troscopy (JEOL JXA-8230 scanning electron probe micro analyzer). The 
size of the measured region is 3 mm × 3 mm. Besides, the chemical 
concentration of alloys was determined by inductively coupled plasma 
optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES) apparatus (Thermo, iCAP 
7400), as shown in Table 2 . The results indicate that all alloy ingots have 
essentially the same composition as the nominal composition. 
X-ray diffraction (Panalytical, XRD, Empyrean) was used to identify 
the phase constituents of Nb-Si-Zr alloys using Cu K α radiation at 40 kV 
with scanning speed of 8
◦
/min. The microstructures and the chemical 
compositions of Nb-16Si- x Zr ( x = 0, 1, 2, 4, 8) alloys were analyzed by 
JEOL JXA-8230 scanning electron probe micro analyzer (EPMA) in 
backscattered electron (BSE) mode and wavelength dispersive X-ray 
spectroscopy (WDS). Three-dimensional morphologies of the fracture 
surface for alloys were studied by 3D measuring laser microscopy (LEXT 
OLS4100). Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software was used to measure the area 
fraction of primary Nbss phase, and three BSE images at the same 
magnification (250 × ) were used for each specimen condition. 
The single-edge notched three-point bending specimens (20 mm × 4 
mm × 2 mm) were used for room-temperature fracture toughness test on 
an electronic universal mechanical testing machine (Instron 5569), as 
shown in Fig. 1 . The specimens were prepared by electro-discharge 
machining from the buttons. The notch of the specimen was prepared 
by a Mo wire with 0.1 mm in diameter. In the test, the loading rate was 
0.05 mm/min, and the testing spanning was 16 mm. The size of speci -
mens for the room-temperature compression test was Ø 4 mm × 6 mm, 
with a loading rate is 0.5 mm/min. In order to keep the accuracy, the 
room-temperature fracture toughness and compression test of each alloy 
were tested three times.

4.5. Effect of Zr on the compressive properties 
It is reported that the solid dissolution of Zr element in silicide is the 
reason for improving the room-temperature compressive strength [ 27 ]. 
Therefore, the compressive strength of the Nb-16Si-4Zr alloy (almost 
reaches the solid solution limit of Nb
3
Si) reach 1957 MPa. Compared 
with the based alloy, the maximum compressive strength in Nb-16Si-1Zr 
alloy is only increased by 23 MPa, which suggests that the addition of 1 
at.% Zr only plays a minor role in solid solution strengthening. The 
fracture strain of alloy is mainly affected by the following factors: (1) the 
volume fraction of primary Nbss phase increases with the increase of Zr 
element; (2) the fine lamellar Nbss/silicide eutectic structure is 
favourable for plastic deformation [ 40 ]. The fine layered eutectic 
structure has more phase boundaries. Therefore, the boundary is more 
prone to ductile deformation and interface debonding, thereby 
increasing the fracture strain, which is in agreement with viewpoints of 
Kim [ 12 ], Sekido [ 15 ] and Tian [ 22 ]. The solid solution effect of Zr 
increases the c/a ratio and reduces the plastic deformation capacity of 
α -(Nb, Zr)
5
Si
3 
phase. Therefore, the fracture strain of Nb-16Si-8Zr alloy 
is slightly reduced to 9.3%.
