ABSTRACT 
The CoCrNi medium-entropy alloy (MEA) has attracted much attention owing to its promising comprehensive 
properties. However, the low yield strength of the CoCrNi alloy at room temperature is a major hurdle in its 
engineering application. In this pursuit, the present study proposed a strategy for strengthening the CoCrNi MEA 
by tailoring the heterogeneous grains resulting from heterogeneous precipitates regulated by thermo-mechanical 
processing. Specifically, a Ta-doped CoCrNi MEA ((CoCrNi)

1. Introduction 
Alloys with high strength and sufficient ductility are highly desirable 
in modern engineering applications. High-entropy alloys (HEAs) and 
medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) based on the multi-principal compo -
nents provide a broad space in the composition design and performance 
research [ 1 – 4 ]. Among them, the CoCrNi MEA with a face-centered 
cubic (FCC) single-phase structure shows an excellent combination of 
strength-ductility and damage tolerance at cryogenic temperatures due 
to its low stacking fault energy (SFE) [ 5 ]. However, the yield stress of the 
CoCrNi MEA in recrystallized state at room temperature is insufficient 
for its use in structural applications [ 6 ]. Therefore, there is a need to 
optimize the mechanical properties of the CoCrNi MEA at 
room-temperature. 
It is well known that the mechanical properties of crystalline mate -
rials can be improved by modifying the microstructures (such as grain 
sizes, dislocations, and twins) and/or chemical structures (e.g., varying 
composition and/or introducing precipitates, etc.) [ 7 ]. In the former 
modifications, grain refinement is a normal route for optimizing the 
microstructure, but this method is likely to be accompanied by the 
sacrifice of ductility [ 8 ]. Recent studies indicate that tailoring the het -
erogeneity of the grain size in HEAs/MEAs leads to an effective 
hetero-deformation inducing (HDI) hardening, which results in an 
excellent strength-ductility synergy [ 9 – 17 ]. For instance, Yang et al. 
[ 14 ] deployed a three-level heterogeneous grain structure with grain 
sizes spanning the nanometer to micrometer range in CoCrNi MEA that 
imparted a high yield strength of 1 GPa. Lu et al. [ 15 ] introduced a 
gradient nanograined (GNG) structure in the CoCrNi MEA by means of 
high energy shot peening, and the resulting GNG alloy exhibited a yield 
strength of ~1215 MPa. In the modification of chemical structures, 
micro-alloying is an effective way to change the chemical structure. 
Similar to some Ni-based superalloys, precipitation hardening is also 
very common in HEAs/MEAs [ 18 ]. Wu et al. [ 19 ] added 3 at. % W into 
the ternary CoCrNi matrix, which resulted in an increase in the intrinsic

Inspired by the above concerns, we proposed a method in the present

work can provide a feasible way to strengthen the CoCrNi MEA, and

Fig. 3 shows the micrographs of the samples under different pro -

conducive to the generation of HDI stress, so as to obtain excellent 
mechanical properties [ 40 , 41 ]. 
The formation of heterostructure in this work can be mainly attrib -
uted to the particle-stimulated nucleation (PSN) mechanism. In 
aluminum alloys and magnesium alloys, the effect of large particles in 
promoting recrystallization via the PSN mechanism is well known and 
widely observed [ 42 , 43 ]. The mechanism involves a rapid sub-boundary 
migration in the deformation zone that forms around large ( > 1 μ m) hard 
particles, leading to the creation of new high-angle grain boundaries 
(GBs) [ 43 ]. This is conducive to the formation of new grain nucleus, 
which may then either stagnate, or grow to produce a recrystallized 
grain. Therefore, fine recrystallized grains tend to be generated around 
the coarse particles during recrystallization. In addition, the particles of 
all types tend to reduce or inhibit GB migration, wherein the effects 
depend critically on the type of particle features [ 22 ]. Simulation results 
indicated that particles with a large length/diameter ratio have an 
efficient grain growth restraining effect [ 44 , 45 ]. In the present study, 
the hindrance of coarse and irregular precipitated particles on GBs 
migration was more significant. This demonstrates that the heteroge -
neous distribution of precipitation led to the heterogeneity of grain size 
distribution.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Annealing-induced hardening 
The T2-700 sample showed a higher yield stress than the cold-rolled 
sample. As stated earlier, it can be attributed to the annealing-induced 
hardening. In general, the annealing-induced hardening is mostly 
related to the precipitation hardening. The backscatter micrographs in 
Fig. 3 a and b shows that the density of precipitation of T2-700 sample 
was higher than that of the cold-rolled sample, which is in good 
agreement with the previous reports [ 47 , 48 ]. Moreover, it can be 
observed that the GBs were not resolved clearly in Fig. 3 b. The 
ill-defined GBs indicate that the recrystallization did not occur. Praveen 
et al. [ 23 ] studied the annealing-induced hardening of the CoCrNi MEA 
and observed that the decrease of dislocation density without significant 
change in grain size and GBs relaxation after low-temperature annealing 
led to the annealing-induced hardening. The dislocation sources are not 
abundant in the nanograins and the generation of dislocations from the 
relaxed GBs is more difficult. Therefore, we believe that in addition to 
the precipitation hardening, the reduced dislocation density and the GB 
relaxation can also play an important role in the annealing-induced 
hardening process. 
4.2. Strengthening mechanisms of the T2-800 sample 
As widely acknowledged, to maintain the continuity of the domain 
interfaces during deformation, geometrically necessary dislocations 
(GNDs) pile up against the interfaces. The piling-up is known to produce 
an internal stress, i.e., back stress in the soft domains, and a forward 
stress in the adjacent hard domain. The HDI stress is a combined effect of 
the back stress and forward stress. LUR tensile tests were conducted to 
reveal the evolution of the HDI stress of the T2-800 and T2-900 samples 
( Fig. 7 a). The width of hysteresis loop of the T2-800 sample was larger 
than that of the T2-900 sample ( Fig. 7 b). Qualitatively, the loop width 
(characterized by residual plastic strain following elastic unloading) is 
directly proportional to the strength of the HDI hardening [ 49 ]. For 
quantitative analysis, we accurately calculated the HDI stress. The HDI

incoherent high angle GBs, which facilitates grain refinement [ 15 , 62 ]. 
In other words, in the presence of higher strain, the corner twins evolve 
into nanograins.

alloy. The main conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(1) The MEA showed superior strength and ductility synergy prop -

ultimate tensile stress of ~1200 MPa and ~1500 MPa, respec -

(2) The HDI stress resulting from the heterogeneous deformation 
contributed to the extraordinary tensile mechanical property. 
The HDI hardening was significant at low strain. With an increase

origin of the enhanced HDI hardening was the dynamically 
reinforced heterogeneous grain structure through numerous DTs.

ductility synergy in the CoCrNi-based MEAs.
