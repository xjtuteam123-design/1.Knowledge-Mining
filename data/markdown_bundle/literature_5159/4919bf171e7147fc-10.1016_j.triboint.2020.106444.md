Abstract 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) that have revolutionized the alloy design of 
strength-ductility trade-off, provide a pathway for the development of high strength 
solid-lubricating composites. Herein, a high perfor mance AlCoCrFeNi HEA based 
composite reinforced with different contents of sol id lubricant Ag was prepared by 
Spark Plasma Sintering technique. The composites re tain the structure of 
nano-coupling network matrix and uniformly disperse Ag particles located at the grain 
boundaries, thereby exhibiting high strength of abo ve 1917 MPa. Meanwhile, Ag 
particles render the composites excellent lubricity and significantly reduce the 
adhesion and wear of the frictional interface at te mperature from room temperature to 
800 °C in vacuum.

1. Introduction 
There are numerous motion pairs or assembles on the satellites and spacecraft, 
which are confronted with complicated friction and wear problems in a complex and 
extreme environment including vacuum, high temperature, overload and irradiation [1, 
2]. In vacuum, severe adhesion, abrasion wear or tr ibo-chemistry are the most critical 
issues for the mechanical systems and cause system failure [3, 4]. The metal atoms at 
the clear frictional interfaces in vacuum are readi ly cohesive and welding, thus 
fragments are pulled off one surface and adhere to the other during sliding, which is 
termed as adhesive wear [5]. On the other hand, the hard asperities on one surface or 
wear debris will plough a series of grooves on the other soft sliding surface, thus 
abrasion wear occurs [6]. Third, tribo-chemistry li ke chemistry adsorption and 
oxidation is inevitable due to the limited vacuum d egree, in some cases the oxide 
debris on the sliding surfaces will be produced and peeled off to increase the wear [7, 
8]. Development of high strength, good lubricity, a nti-adhesive solid-lubricating and 
wear-resistance composites in vacuum is effective t o solve these issue and crucial to 
the service safety of the spacecraft. In the past d ecades, researchers have paid 
extensive attention to design solid-lubricating and wear-resistance composites in 
vacuum. Zheng et al. [9] have added nanoparticles L a 
2O3 into copper alloy and 
improved the hardness and wear resistance of the co mposites in vacuum, but the 
friction coefficient (COF) slightly increased and i s high. Cheng et al. [10] have

investigated the tribological behaviors of NiCrMoAl -Ag-CaF 2/BaF 2 composites at 
temperature from room temperature (RT) to 800 °C in vacuum. The results showed 
that this solid-lubricating composite has the excel lent lubricity in vacuum 
characterized by the low COF of below 0.2 and wear rate of 10 -7-10 -5 mm 3/Nm. 
Huang et al. [11] have reported that the electrical sliding COF and wear rate of 
Cu-MoS 2-graphite-WS 2 nanotubes composites after about one hour test in vacuum are 
stable at about 0.2 and 4.5 × 10 -8 mm 3/Nm, respectively. Niu et al. [12] have 
observed that solid lubricant Ba 0.25 Sr 0.75 SO 4 reduces the COF and wear rate of Fe 3Al 
alloy in vacuum by about 2 times and 3-7 times, res pectively. Zhen et al. [13] have 
depicted that the addition of Al and Mo elements ca n promote the precipitation of 
intermetallic in nickel alloy matrix composites, th ereby improving strength and wear 
resistance in vacuum. The above literatures demonstrated that the introduction of solid 
lubricants and hard nanoparticles into the metal ma trix is feasible to improve their 
tribological performance in vacuum. However, there is a problem that the mechanical 
properties and lubricity are mutually exclusive due to the discrepancies in the 
physicochemical properties of the metal matrix and solid lubricants [14]. For 
instances, the tensile and compressive strength of a Ni-based solid-lubricating 
composite decreased by 19 % and 11 %, respectively, when the fluoride eutectic 
content increased from 5 wt. % to 10 wt. % [10]. On e of the general concepts 
employed in solving this imbalance of solid-lubrica ting composite is to seek an alloy 
matrix with high strength and good ductility simultaneously [15]. 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are a class of novel met allic materials with

## Page 6

4 
 
revolutionized alloy design concept [16]. They are high-concentration solid solutions 
that composed of at least four principal elements a chieving chemical disorder and 
topological order, and thereby overcome the strengt h-ductility trade-off 
unconventionally. In the HEA, adjacent elements in a severely distorted lattice cause 
an atomic-level pressures fluctuation field through charge transfer, thereby obtaining a 
strong solid solution strengthening effect while ma intaining excellent plasticity [17, 
18]. For example, Liang et al. [19] stated that Al 0.5 Cr 0.9 FeNi 2.5 V0.2 HEA achieves 
excellent ultimate tensile strength (1.9 GPa) while retains good ductility (>9 %) due 
to the order-disorder coherent nanostructure created by spinodal decomposition. Lei et 
al. [20] found that the strength and ductility of T iZrHfNb HEA are simultaneously 
improved by 48.5 % and 95.2 % through the formation of the ordered oxygen 
complexes in the interstices of the lattice. As sum marized in the previous reports 
[21-25], AlCoCrFeNi HEAs exhibit an outstanding com bination of compressive yield 
strength (1138 - 1702 MPa), plastic strain (10 % - no fracture) and Vickers hardness 
(>520 HV). Moreover, the high-temperature tribologi cal properties of Al xCoCrFeNi 
HEAs in air are superior to traditional iron-based and nickel-based alloys [26, 27]. 
Therefore, AlCoCrFeNi HEAs provide a pathway for th e development of high 
strength, good lubricity solid-lubricating composites. 
Previous studies have found the exceptional combina tion of high-temperature 
tribological and mechanical properties of the HEA b ased solid-lubricating composite, 
which are superior to most of the traditional alloy based composites [28, 29]. 
According to Zhang et al. [14, 30] and Verma et al. [31], solid lubricants such as

graphite and MoS 2, Ag-BaF 2/CaF 2 and Cu were added into CoCrFeNi HEA and 
enhance the tribological properties over a wide tem perature range without sacrificing 
mechanical properties. Besides, Zhang et al. [32] a lso found that the addition of FeS 
solid lubricant improves the yield strength of CoCr FeNi HEA matrix from 387 MPa 
to 556 MPa while reducing the COF in the range from RT to 800 °C. Yadav et al. [33] 
reported that the wear rate of (AlCrFeMnV) 90 Bi 10 -TiB 2 HEA composites is in the 
magnitude of 10 -5 mm 3/Nm at RT due to the synergistic effects of refined grains and 
significantly high hardness. 
Therefore, in this work, solid lubricant Ag, becaus e of its superior lubricity in 
vacuum [2, 34], was selected and introduced into th e AlCoCrFeNi HEA for the 
purpose of preparing high strength, good lubricity and anti-adhesive solid-lubricating 
composites in vacuum. Effects of Ag content on the microstructure, mechanical and 
vacuum high temperature tribological properties of AlCoCrFeNi HEA were 
systematically studied. The wear mechanism and trib o-chemistry of the AlCoCrFeNi 
HEA based composites were analyzed in vacuum at temperature from RT to 800 °C. 
2. Experimental procedures 
The argon-atomized AlCoCrFeNi HEA powder with a par ticle size of less than 
50 μm (Jiangsu Wei La Li New Material Technology Co., China) and Ag powder (300 
meshes) were used as the starting materials. Two we ight fractions of Ag particles, 5 
and 10 wt. %, were chosen to study the effects of A g content on the microstructure, 
mechanical and tribological properties of the AlCoCrFeNi HEA. At first, AlCoCrFeNi 
HEA powder and Ag powder with the designed weight r atio were mixed uniformly in

## Page 8

6 
 
a planetary ball milling (QM-3SP4, Nanjing Nan Da Y i Qi Co., China) under the 
protective Ar atmosphere with a duration of 4 h, a speed of 300 r/min and a stainless 
steel ball to powder ratio of 1.5 : 1. Then, both t he pure AlCoCrFeNi HEA powder 
and as-milled mixture powders were put into a graph ite die with a diameter of 50 mm 
and consolidated by Spark Plasma Sintering techniqu e at a sintering temperature of 
1100 °C and a pressure of 35 MPa holding for 8 min (SPS, LABOX-3010KF, Japan). 
It is noted that the average heating rate and press ure loading rate were 75 oC/min and 
3 MPa/min, respectively. Finally, the bulk AlCoCrFe Ni HEA, AlCoCrFeNi-5 wt. % 
Ag and AlCoCrFeNi-10 wt. % Ag composites (designate d as AlCoCrFeNi HEA, 
composite 5A and 10A, respectively) were taken out when the furnace was cooled, 
and machined into the required specimens for the following characterization and test. 
The phase constitutions of the AlCoCrFeNi HEA powde r, bulk and its 
composites were identified by X-ray diffraction (D/ max-2400 Rigaku). The scanning 
electron microscope (SEM, JSM-5600 LV and JSM-6700 F) equipped with energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) and electron backscat ter diffraction (EBSD) detector 
was used to analyze the microstructure and composit ion of the AlCoCrFeNi HEA and 
composites. The hardness tests of the rectangle spe cimens with a size of 10 mm × 10 
mm × 3 mm were performed on the Archimedes HTV-PHS3 0 (Archimedes Industry 
Technology Co., UK) tester with a diamond indenter at temperature from RT to 
800 °C. The tests were conducted under an applied load of 3 N holding for 10 seconds, 
and repeated at least three times at each testing t emperature. Compression tests were 
carried out using WDW-200 material testing machine (Sans Material Test Instrument

Co., China) with a stain rate of 1 × 10 -2 mm/min, and each test repeated at least four 
times. The compressive specimens are the cylinders with a dimension of Ø 3 mm × 6

GHT-1000E high-temperature vacuum pin-on-disc rotat ion tribometer (Zhong 
Ke Kai Hua Co. Ltd, China) was selected for evaluat ing the friction and wear 
behaviors of the AlCoCrFeNi HEA, composite 5A and 1 0A. During the tests, the 
AlCoCrFeNi HEA, composite 5A and 10A disc (18.5 mm × 18.5 mm × 3.0 mm, Ra = 
0.4 μm) slides against stationary Inconel 718 alloy pins (Ø 5 mm). The vacuum 
degree of all the friction tests was controlled wit hin 3 Pa, and selected test 
temperatures were RT, 200, 400, 600, and 800 °C. In addition, the vacuum degree 
maintains below 3 Pa achieved by the synergism betw een mechanical and molecular 
pumps. Inconel 718 alloy is widely used in high tem perature applications like engine 
turbines, blades, bearing, compressor machines, etc ., and thus selected as the counter 
material [26, 35]. The applied load, sliding speed, rotating diameter, and testing time 
were 5 N, 0.19 m/s, 10 mm and 30 min respectively. According to the Hertz contact 
stress theory [36], the maximum contact stress under the applied load of 5 N is as high 
as about 1.06 GPa in this work, which is in the ran ge of high friction stress. The 
selection of moderate sliding speed (0.19 m/s) is b ased on the measurement range of 
the apparatus in our laboratory, and meanwhile try to simulate the practical sliding 
conditions [37]. The COF was recorded by the tribom eter, the wear volumes and 
cross-section profiles of the worn surfaces were me asured by the non-contact 
three-dimensional profiler (MicroXAM-800, KLA-Tencor Co., USA). Referencing to

The high strength and good ductility of the AlCoCrF eNi HEA-Ag 
solid-lubricating composites are attributed to two aspects. During the sintering 
process, soft Ag not only maintains its own phase stability, but also uniformly diffuses 
into the grain boundaries of the AlCoCrFeNi HEA matrix, which is due to the positive 
mixing enthalpy of Ag and the principal elements of the AlCoCrFeNi HEA. On the 
other hand, the high-entropy effect reserves the na no-coupled network structure of 
BCC precipitated phase and B2 matrix phase that is key to enhance strength. It is 
expected that the hardness of the composites slight ly decreases with the increase in 
the soft Ag content. Microstructure observation sho ws that the particle size of Ag 
phase is lager in composite 10A than composite 5A, thus slightly decreasing the 
compressive yield strength.

lubricity, significantly reducing the friction, adh esion and wear of the AlCoCrFeNi 
HEA and the counterpart. Moreover, within a certain range, the higher the Ag content, 
the more prominent the above-mentioned effects. Str ikingly, the wear resistance of 
composite 5A and 10A are in the magnitude of 10 -7-10 -5 mm 3/Nm in vacuum over a 
wide temperature range, which is superior to the tr aditional nickel-alloy (10 -6-10 -5 
mm 3/Nm) [10, 13], iron-alloy (10 -3 mm 3/Nm) [12] and copper-alloy (10 -4-10 -3

3.3.2. Composite 5A/10A-Inconel 718 alloy contact pairs in vacuum 
Figs. 9 and 10 show the worn surface morphologies o f composite 5A and 10A 
discs and corresponding Inconel 718 alloy pins (ins erted in the right corner) from RT 
to 800 °C in vacuum, respectively. It is apparent t hat the worn surface morphologies 
of composite 5A is very similar to composite 10A. T able 4 lists the compositions of 
the different regions on the worn surface of compos ite 5A at all the test temperatures. 
Compared to the AlCoCrFeNi HEA-Inconel 718 alloy sliding pairs, the worn surfaces 
of composite 5A and 10A are very smooth and accordi ngly the wear scar areas of the

## Page 24

22 
 
Inconel 718 alloy pins decrease dramatically at all the test temperatures. This 
indicates that abrasion wear and adhesive wear betw een the AlCoCrFeNi HEA and 
Inconel 718 alloy pins in vacuum are effectively su ppressed by the addition of the 
lubricity Ag element, leading to the significantly lower COF and wear. EDS results 
(Table 4) and XPS observation (Fig. 11) on the worn surface of composite 5A in the 
range from RT to 600 °C suggest the formation of th e local Ag tribo-film. EDS 
mappings (Figs. 9e and 10e) show that the transfer layer on the worn surface of 
Inconel 718 pins at 800 °C contains not only Al and Co elements, but also Ag element. 
These two points clearly proofs the existence and lubricity of solid-lubricant Ag at the 
frictional interfaces. It is inferred that during s liding, soft Ag particles diffuse and 
smear onto the frictional interfaces under the syne rgism between the frictional heat, 
high external temperature and shear stress, making the composite self-lubricity and 
excellent wear resistance [10, 34]. In addition, Ag particles has low interatomic 
cohesion force and metallurgical mutual solubility with the principal elements of the 
AlCoCrFeNi HEA matrix [46], also contributing to th e low COF and alleviation of 
abrasion wear at RT and 800 °C, and adhesive wear w ithin temperature range 200 - 
600 °C. 
Within temperature range RT - 600 °C, the worn surf aces of composite 5A and 
10A are characterized by slight grooves, except som e scattered debris containing high 
oxygen content appear at 600 °C, indicating that th e dominant wear mechanism is 
slight abrasive wear. EDS analysis and XPS results (Table 4 and Fig. 11) suggests that 
solid lubricant Ag in the composites diffuses onto the worn surfaces and forms an

## Page 25

23 
 
intermittent lubricating Ag film. This lubricity Ag significantly reduces the adhesive 
transfer from the counterpart Inconel 718 alloy ont o the composites within 
temperature range 200 - 600 °C, thus various molybd enum, niobium and nickel 
oxides are disappeared or suppressed (Fig. 11). Ins tead, more cobalt and iron arising 
from the AlCoCrFeNi matrix are prone to oxidized in this temperature range (Fig. 11). 
Therefore, the COFs of the composites sliding again st Inconel 718 alloy pins are 
maintained within 0.5 at temperature from RT to 600 °C, and their wear rates are low. 
In addition, the softening of the Ag tribo-film at 600 °C also decreases the COF of 
composite 5A. 
At 800 °C, fine scratches and micro-pits can be observed on the worn surfaces of 
AlCoCrFeNi HEA-Ag composites from Figs. 9e and 10e. Moreover, a transfer layer 
from the composites appears on the worn surface of the Inconel 718 pins. These 
phenomena suggest that the main wear mechanisms are abrasive and mild adhesion 
wear. Moreover, double-check of XPS and EDS results at 800 °C suggests that a very 
small amount of Ag was detected on the frictional p airs, and the worn surface 
composition of the composite is the same as that of AlCoCrFeNi HEA at this 
temperature (Al 2O3, CoCrO 4, Cr 2O3, MoO 3 and MoO 2). This may because that Ag

melting point (960 °C) [15]. Thus the COF and wear rate of the composites and 
Inconel 718 alloy pin increase at 800 °C.

4. Conclusions 
In this work, a high strength, good lubricity and anti-adhesive AlCoCrFeNi HEA 
based composite containing Ag was successfully prep ared, and effects of Ag content 
on the microstructures, mechanical and vacuum high- temperature tribological 
properties were systematically studied. The conclus ions could be summarized as 
follows: 
(1) The AlCoCrFeNi HEA-Ag solid-lubricating composi tes display the high 
strength of above 1917 MPa, and good plastic strain of above 19.4 %, due to their 
nano-coupled interpenetrating network structure and uniformly dispersed Ag particles 
at the grain boundaries. 
(2) The AlCoCrFeNi HEA in vacuum at RT displays abr asive wear, contributing 
to relatively high COF of about 0.57 and wear rare of 1.9 × 10 -5 mm 3/Nm. The high 
temperature range of 200 °C to 600 °C make the elem ent diffuse and cohesive of the

## Page 30

28 
 
sliding pairs, thus the wear is mainly from the mat erial transfer of the Inconel 718 
alloy whose wear scar diameter increases by about t wo times compared to that at RT. 
However, at 800 °C, surface oxidation inhibits the adhesion but the oxide tribo-layer 
is easily broken during the sliding, contributing t he highest COF of about 0.86 and 
wear rare of 11.8 × 10 -5 mm 3/Nm. 
(3) Solid lubricant Ag renders the AlCoCrFeNi HEA excellent lubricity and wear 
resistance in vacuum. Within temperature range RT - 600 °C, the COF is of the 
AlCoCrFeNi HEA-Ag composites sliding against Incone l 718 alloys is about one 
third lower than that of the AlCoCrFeNi HEA, and th e severe abrasion or adhesion 
transfer of the sliding pairs is completely avoided due to the formation of lubricity Ag 
film. At 800 °C, Ag element on the worn surface is very low and the composites have 
similar tribo-oxidation behaviors compared to the AlCoCeFeNi HEA, leading the high 
wear rate of 14.0 × 10 -5 mm 3/Nm.
