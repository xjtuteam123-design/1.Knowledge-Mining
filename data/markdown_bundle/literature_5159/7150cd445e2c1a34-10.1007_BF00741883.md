MECHANISM OF FORMATION AND PROPERTIES OF ALUMINUM COATINGS 
ON HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH NICKEL ALLOYS 
V. P. Lesnikov, V. P. Kuznetsov, M. V. Kukhtin, 
Yu. A. Belykh, and Yu. O. Goroshenko 
UDC 621.793.8 
In modern power-machinery building, particularly for the production of gas-turbine equip- 
ment, wide use is made of nickel-base high-temperature strength alloys. Under the operating 
conditions of gas-turbine equipment with increased temperatures and service life these alloys 
do not possess sufficient resistance to high-temperature oxidation, and therefore heat- 
resistant coatings (primarily aluminum) are used for protection of them, which makes it pos- 
sible to increase the reliability and life of expensive parts [I]. The morphology of these 
coatings depends upon the production method, and the composition is determined by the compo- 
sition of the alloy to which they are applied and the impregnating medium. 
This article presents the results of composite investigations of aluminum coatings pro- 
duced by the circulation method [2] with forced circulation of the carrier gas in the work- 
ing chamber of the furnace and in an FeAI powder mixture (50 wt.% AI) in the same furnace on 
samples of ZhS6K, ZhS6U, and EP539LM alloys. NH~CI and AICIs were used as the activator. 
After application of the coating the samples were annealed at I050°C for 2 h with air cool- 
ing. 
The coatings obtained were investigated by metallographic, hardness-testing, and gravi- 
metric methods. The x-ray diffraction analysis was made on a DRON-I diffractometer. The 
distribution of the chemical elements in the coatings was investigated on a Cameca MS-46 mi- 
croanalyzer. The heat-resistance tests were made in air at IIO0°C for I00 h. 
The defectiveness of the oxide films during their high-temperature growth on samples 
with a coating was investigated by the method of thermally stimulated electron emission with 
synchronous recording of the thermal luminescence. The measurements of the thermally stimu- 
lated electron emission and the thermal luminescence were made in heating of samples previ- 
ously excited by x-ray radiation on the instrument described in [3]. 
The comparative evaluation of the effectiveness of the coatings was made under condi- 
tions close to service conditions on an experimental gasdynamic stand [4] in nonisothermal 
creep with a constant stress of 150 MPa. The temperature cycle was 150 ~---I050~C, the heat- 
ing time 40 sec, the hold i0 sec, and the cooling 20 sec. The tests were made in still air 
(M* = 0) and in a rapid air flow (M = 1.3).

In application of the coatings by the circulation method the temperature, the time of 
impregnation, the ratio of the reaction surfaces, and the composition of the gaseous phase 
were changed, which made it possible to reveal certain features of the mechanism of coating 
formation and the parameters determining their structure and properties. Aluminum coatings 
may be formed by two basic mechanisms: active and relatively inactive [5]. In the active 
process the coating is formed by diffusion of aluminum through the layer of B-phase NiAI 
forming, and the outer zone of the coating contains all of the alloy elements of the alloy. 
The relatively inactive process is characterized by diffusion of nickel through the B-phase. 
Since the solubility of the refractory elements of the alloy in the B-phase is low, in the 
relatively inactive prQcess there is practically no entry of them into the outer layer. Con- 
sequently, the distribution of the refractory elements across the thickness of the coating 
may be an indicator of the mechanism of coating formation. 
For coatings obtained in ferroaluminum powder the active mechanism of their formation 
is characteristic. This is indicated by the presence of all of the alloy elements of the al- 
loy in the outer zone of the coating (Figs. 1 and 2). In these coatings a strongly developed 
outer zone with an increased aluminum content (30-33 wt.% AI) is observed. The refractory 
elements present in the outer zone form phases in the form of carbides, intermetallides, and 
solid solutions of them. In comparison with the outer zone the diffusion zone, which is de- 
pleted of nickel, is enriched with refractory elements forming dispersed phases and is char- 
acterized by increased hardness. The thickness of this zone is 20-30% of the total coating 
thickness. Increasing the temperature of the aluminizing process from 950 to I050°C under 
otherwise similar conditions leads to an increase in coating thickness, but there is prac- 
tically no change in the structure and formation mechanism. 
The thickness and formation mechanism of the coating in aluminizing by the circulation 
method depend upon the activity of the subchlorides in the working chamber, which is con- 
trolled by the ratio of the reaction surface of exchange by aluminum between the gaseous 
phase and the powder mixture or by selection of the activator. In the relatively inactive 
process the thickness of the outer and diffusion zones is practically the same. In the outer 
zone there are no refractory element carbides and excess phases (Figs. 1 And 2), but such ele- 
ments of the alloy as cobalt and chromium, which stabilize the B-phase, are present. With an 
increased refractory element content in the diffusion zone a barrier layer is created and the 
thermal stability of the coating is increased. The distribution of the elements and the 
structure obtained by the circulation method in the active process are similar to the corre- 
sponding characteristics of the coating obtained in ferroaluminum powder (Figs. 1 and 2). 
Variation in the temperature of impregnation by the circulation method leads to a change in 
the coating thickness, but does not have a significant influence on the mechanism of its for- 
mation.

specified structure and composition. The high-temperature test results showed that the alu-

thermal spectra are recorded from the oxidized samples with coatings. With an increase in 
oxidation time the temperature position of the maxima, the structure, and the number of

is significantly greater than from the coatings obtained in the highly active process. 
The difference in the emission parameters from the aluminum coatings is an indication 
of the fact that in high-temperature holds on the surface of a coating obtained in the rela- 
tively inactive process there is formed a layer of a-A1203 oxides with an increased defect 
concentration and a greater thickness than on the surface of the coating formed in the high- 
ly active process. The increased defect concentration in the oxide leads to an increase in 
the diffusion rate through the oxide and, consequently, to a decrease in the heat resistance 
of such a coating. This may explain the shape of the (Am/S) = f(T) relationship curves of 
samples in high-temperature tests (Fig. 3). The unsteady relationship of the intensity of 
the thermally stimulated electron emission and thermal luminescence to the oxidation time is 
related both to a change in oxide layer thickness and to a change in defect concentration in 
the growing oxide film [7]. The results of determination of the quantity of ~-A1203 oxides 
formed in oxidation on the surface of coatings formed according to different mechanisms are 
shown in Fig. 3. The quantity of ~-A1203 oxides was determined by the method of measurement 
of the ratios of the intensities of the analytical lines [8]. 
The results of the investigation indicate the positive influence of aluminum coatings 
formed by the different mechanisms on the service characteristics of an alloy in tests both 
in still air and in a high-velocity air flow. This is related, above all else, to protec- 
tion of the alloy from oxidation, corrosion crackling, and depletion by the alloy elements 
of the grain boundaries coming to the surface. It has been shown [9] that failure at the 
grain boundaries is the basic process under these conditions. An alloy with a coating ob- 
tained in the relatively inactive process with 24-28 wt.% A1 in the NiAI B-phase possesses 
the best properties, which may be explained by the high plasticity of such a coating and the 
increased adhesion capacity of the oxide film to its surface. 
In comparison with the Tf of unaluminized the number of cycles until failure Tf of alu- 
minized ZhS6U increased by two to four times in tests in a high-velocity air flow and by two 
to three times in testing in still air. Coatings formed by the relatively inactive process 
have a longer life by 20-30% than coatings obtained in the active process. 
CONCLUSIONS 
I. The aluminum content, the thickness, the structure, and the composition of aluminum 
coatings obtained bylthe circulation method are determined to a significant degree by the ac- 
tivity of the impregnation process. 
2. As the result of regulation of the activity of the process, coatings with different 
properties may be obtained on the same material with the same temperaturesand pressure. Con- 
sequently, the activity of the aluminizing process is an important technical parameter which 
must be selected based on the composition of the alloy and the requirements for its service 
properties. 
3. Despite the somewhat lower heat resistance under isothermal conditions, for opera- 
tion in nonisothermal creep under nonsteady conditions coatings with 24-28 wt.% A1 in the 
NiAI B-phase formed according to the relatively inactive mechanism are preferable.

V. G. Sorokin, and N. B. Vandysheva

The high protective properties of thermal diffusion protective coatings are obtained 
with a certain content of the diffusants in the layer. It has been shown that, for examD!e, 
the first zone of an aluminosilicide coating must contain 26-28% A1 and not less than 6-8% Si 
[i, 2]. Such a coating reliably protects high-temperature strength nickel alloys from high- 
temperature oxidation and corrosion in long high-temperature holds, especially in agressive 
gaseous media [3]. This is the result, first, of the fact that at the operating temperatures 
there is spontaneously created a stable silicide diffusion barrier between the coating and 
the alloy being protected, providing a significant increase in coating life, and, second, of 
the fact that as the result of microalloying the ~-AI=O3 oxide with silicon the concentration 
of point defects in the oxide film is decreased,accompanied by a decrease in its diffusion 
permeability. In addition, as the result of the reaction of silicon with the refractory ele- 
ments contained in the alloy entering into the coating and the formation of silicides there 
is a decrease in the harmful influence of these elements on the heat resistance and corrosion 
resistance of the coatings [4]. 
However, with a repeated change in temperature the protective properties of a!uminosi!i- 
cide coatings become partially poorer. Under conditions of thermal cycling as the result of 
the occurrence of additional thermal stresses the quite dense oxide layer spalls and falls 
from the surface, as the result of which the process of redistribution and burning off of the 
aluminum is accelerated.

alloys containing nickel, aluminum, and chromium provides a significant improvement in their 
heat resistance [5]. Several different mechanisms of the positive influence of rare-earth
