1. Introduction 
Due to the much higher oxidation resistance over a wide tem- 
perature range and great mechanical properties, the ferritic FeCrAl 
alloys are considered to replace the commercial zircaloy and to be 
used as the accident tolerant fuel (ATF) cladding in the light-water 
reactors [ 1 , 2 ]. In FeCrAl alloys, Cr and Al contents are needed to 
form protective alumina scales on the surface, which play an im- 
portant role on the superior high temperature oxidation resistance 
of FeCrAl alloys [ 3 , 4 ], however extensive high-temperature oxida- 
tion experiments indicate that too high Cr content can form Cr- 
rich α’ precipitates, which can result in radiation-induced embrit- 
tlement [5–7] , while too low Cr content (below 12%) could not of- 
fer suﬃcient oxidation resistance. In the same way, too high Al 
content (higher than 8%) would cause diﬃculties in welding and

enough to satisfy the demands of ATF cladding. 
In fact, smaller amounts of other elements such as Mo and/or

Mo addition mainly acts as solid solution strengthening element, 
while a minor addition of Nb tends to act as precipitation strength-

In addition, Internal friction (IF) is a very sensitive technique

In FeCrAl alloys, Fe 2 Nb-type Laves phase particles pin the disloca-

dependent IF (TDIF) technique was introduced to investigate the 
mechanical properties of Nb containing FeCrAl alloys, especially for 
the main strengthening mechanism of the second particles precip- 
itation. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Materials preparation 
A series of Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-xNb alloys ( x = 0, 0.5, 1.0 
and 1.5 wt%, respectively) were prepared from raw materials of 
99.99 wt% purity by vacuum induction melting. The chemical com- 
position of the alloys after melting was determined by using high 
resolution inductive coupled plasma emission spectrometer (ICP). 
The as-cast ingots were homogenized at 1200 °C for 2 h, followed 
by a successive rolling process at 1200 °C with a total thickness 
reduction of 90%. After that, the hot-rolled FeCrAlMo based alloys 
plates were warm-rolled at 700 °C with a total thickness reduction 
of 60%. Subsequently, the warm-rolled sheets were further cold- 
rolled at room temperature with a ﬁnal thickness of around 2 mm. 
For studying the evolution of dispersed Fe 2 Nb precipitates, above 
cold-rolled FeCrAlMo based alloys were subjected to different heat 
treatments (80 0 ∼110 0 °C) under Ar atmosphere followed by water 
quenching. Schematic diagram of the thermomechanical route for 
the as-cast FeCrAl alloy was shown in Fig. 1 . The investigated alloy 
number with different Nb contents and the corresponding prepa- 
ration processes are shown in Table 1 . It is worthwhile to point out 
that in addition to a large deformation of 60%, amounts of Fe 2 Nb 
laves phases particles could be precipitated at a warm-rolling tem- 
perature of 700 °C, because it is reported that a wide tempera- 
ture range of 400 °C to 10 0 0 °C could result in the precipitation of 
Fe 2 Nb laves phases in Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-1Nb alloy [8] . 
2.2. Sample characterization 
The surface morphology of the investigated FeCrAlMo based al- 
loys, including the grains and Laves precipitate particles in the fer- 
ritic matrix were characterized by ﬁeld emission scanning electron 
microscope (FESEM, Sirion 200, FEI) and optical microscope (ZEISS- 
AX10) after polishing and etching (1 g FeCl 3 , 15 ml HCl and 50 ml 
distilled water). The particle size and the density of laves pre- 
cipitate particles have been statistically analyzed using an image 
analysis software (Image J) based on the SEM results of FeCrAlMo 
based samples. The grain size of the FeCrAlMo based alloys with 
different annealing tem perature is statistically analyzed based on 
the optical microscope. 
For tensile testing, the investigated FeCrAlMo based alloys were 
cut with a cross-section of 1.5 × 1.0 mm 2 and a working length of 
5 mm along rolling direction. Three samples were tested for each 
preparation condition for average using an Instron-5967 machine 
with a strain rate of 2 × 10 −4 s −1 . Tensile test was carried out 
at RT, 300 °C, 50 0 °C and 80 0 °C, respectively. During the high 
temperature tensile tests, samples were held at test temperature 
for 15 min to ensure a homogeneous temperature over the entire 
working length.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Tensile properties of Nb containing FeCrAl alloys 
Figure 2 (a–d) shows the typical room temperature (RT) engi- 
neering tensile stress-strain curves of the manufactured FeCrAlMo 
based alloys annealed at 80 0 °C, 90 0 °C, 10 0 0 °C and 110 0 °C, 
respectively. For each heat treatment condition, the ultimate ten- 
sile strengths (UTS) increase with the increasing of Nb contents 
and achieve a maximum value at 1.5 wt% Nb. For example, the 
UTS of FeCrAlMo alloy with 1.5 wt% Nb corresponding to differ- 
ent annealing temperature of 800 °C, 900 °C, 1000 °C and 1100 °C 
is 628 MPa, 562 MPa, 692 MPa and 466 MPa, which is 44.0%, 
27.7%, 48.2% and 20.0% higher than that of Nb-free FeCrAlMo al- 
loy with the same annealing temperature, respectively. All above 
results indicate that the mechanical properties of the FeCrAlMo al- 
loys have been effectively improved by Nb addition in the range 
studied. 
The comparison of UTS of each FeCrAlMo alloys with different 
heat treatment conditions have also been studied. Taking N10 and 
N15 alloys as examples, the typical RT engineering stress-strain 
curves are shown as Fig. 3(a) and ( b ). It is worth to note that the 
UTS of N10 and N15 alloys increase with the annealing tempera- 
ture at ﬁrst and then decrease after passing a maximum value at 
10 0 0 °C. A higher annealing temperature of 1100 °C for FeCrAlMo 
alloys is unfavorable to the improvement of their mechanical prop- 
erties.

for Nb-addition FeCrAlMo alloys in the range studied are deter-

3.3. Internal friction assessment 
Except for microstructural analysis, in fact, the mechanism of 
such a strengthening effect can also be illustrated through the in- 
ternal friction measurement. Fig. 8 shows the variation of IF ( Q −1 ) 
versus temperature at 1 Hz for N0 and N0 800 samples with the 
1st and 2nd heating cycles, respectively. The ever-reached highest 
temperature is 800 °C. For N0 sample (cold-rolled sample), there 
are three IF peaks labeled as P 1 , P 2 and P 3 in the 1st heating cy- 
cle, while for N0 sample in the 2nd heating cycle and N0 800 sam- 
ples (annealed sample) in the 1st and 2nd heating cycles, there 
are only two IF peaks (P 1 at 560 °C and P 3 at 720 °C) are detected, 
which means that P 2 peak appears around 680 °C disappear after 
the ﬁrst measurement (heating) process or the typical heat treat- 
ment process of 800 °C for cold-rolled N0 sample. In fact, this phe- 
nomenon has been reported in other materials, such as the ECAP- 
processed Al-Mg alloys [23] and T91 steel [24] , what these mate- 
rials have in common is that there are abundant dislocations in- 
troduced by a large plastic deformation. Hence it can be concluded 
that P 2 peak is related to the recovery of dislocations produced by 
the cold rolling process and recrystallization during the heat pro- 
cess. And the height of P 2 peak depends on the degree of defor- 
mation [ 24 , 25 ]. 
Based on the microstructure analysis as shown in Fig. 5 , there 
is no precipitation particles in Nb-free FeCrAlMo alloy, whatever in 
matrix or at grain boundary. Hence the dislocations introduced by 
cold rolling process and grain boundaries can move freely, there- 
fore P 2 peak of N0 sample corresponding to the recovery and re- 
crystallization process are detected at 680 °C in the ﬁrst heating 
cycle of IF measurement. While after a ﬁrst heating cycle, which 
mean the cold-rolled N0 sample undergo a total heat treatment 
process of 800 °C, so in the second heating cycle, there are only 
two IF peaks P 1 (Zener peak) and P 3 (grain boundary peak) ap- 
pear. Here it is worth to note that the appearance of P 3 peak indi-

According to early similar researches [ 22 , 27 ], P 1 peak, a typi- 
cal Zener peak, is mainly originated from a stress-induced reori- 
entation process of the solute atom pairs such as Cr-Cr. And P 3 
peak, a typical grain boundary peak, is originated from a stress- 
induced sliding process of the grain boundaries. Hence comparing 
with the IF curve of N0 800 samples in the ﬁrst heating cycle, P 1 
peak in the second heating cycle increases, which is attributed to 
the fewer dislocations induced by cold rolling process after a ﬁrst 
IF measurement (an annealing process of 800 °C), its pinning effect 
on the reorientation process of the Cr-Cr solute atom pairs could 
be signiﬁcantly weakened. In addition, the IF value (magnetome- 
chanical damping) during 300 °C ∼430 °C increases too, which is 
attributed to the stress-induced irreversible movement of magnetic 
domain walls. Comparing with the IF curve of N0 800 samples in the 
ﬁrst heating cycle, the magnetic domain walls are also pinned by 
the residual dislocations induced by cold rolling process. 
Figure 9 shows the temperature dependence of IF ( Q −1 ) curves 
for N0 800 , N05 800 , N10 800 and N15 800 samples in the ﬁrst heating 
cycle at 1 Hz. It can be seen that comparing with the IF curve of 
N0 800 sample, P 1 peaks are still located at about 560 °C while P 3 
peaks disappear in Nb containing FeCrAlMo based alloys. In addi- 
tion, there is no signiﬁcant IF value variations with Nb contents 
for the Nb containing FeCrAlMo based alloy samples in the inves- 
tigated temperature range. 
According to the results of recrystallization process as shown 
in Fig. 7 , the temperature to begin recrystallization increases with 
the increasing Nb contents because of the pinning effect of Fe 2 Nb 
nanoscale particles on dislocations. hence comparing with the IF 
peaks of Nb-free FeCrAlMo alloy, P 2 peak corresponding to the re- 
covery and recrystallization process disappear in each Nb contain- 
ing FeCrAlMo based alloys during the ﬁrst heating cycle of IF mea- 
surement. 
In addition, comparing with the IF measurement results of N0 
and N0 800 samples, P 3 (grain boundary peak) also disappear in the 
Nb containing FeCrAlMo alloys (N05 800 , N10 800 , N15 800 samples). 
The reason is that nanoscale Fe 2 Nb particles dispersed on grain 
boundaries could block the sliding process of the grain boundaries. 
At the same time, this pinning effects on the grain boundary effec- 
tively improve the tensile strength of the Nb containing FeCrAlMo 
alloys. 
3.4. Discussion 
In a word, Nb contents and heat treatment temperature are 
both important factors to affect the precipitate morphology. For Nb 
contents, it can be seen that more and more Fe 2 Nb Laves phase 
particles have been precipitated with increasing Nb contents (less 
than 1.5 wt% in our studies). However, for heat treatment temper- 
ature, it is too complex. For example, the dispersed Fe 2 Nb particles 
may dissolve back (for low Nb addition) or agglomerate (for high 
Nb addition) when the annealing temperature is too high (such as 
1100 °C), while the density of Fe 2 Nb particles isn’t high enough for 
achieving a best strengthening effect when the annealing temper- 
ature is too low (lower than 10 0 0 °C). The particles with a higher 
density and smaller size exhibits the highest strength improve- 
ment. Hence N15 10 0 0 samples with a high density of 6 × 10 21 m −2

4. Conclusions 
In this paper, the dispersed morphology of Fe 2 Nb precipitate 
particles varied with Nb contents and annealing conditions were 
studied in Nb containing FeCrAlMo alloys, and an internal friction 
measurement combined with microstructure analysis were em- 
ployed to research its strengthening mechanism. The main results 
could be summarized as follows: 
1) High density nanoscale Fe 2 Nb particles with a maximum 
density of 6 × 10 21 m −2 and a small particle size of 200 nm were 
precipitated under the appropriate prepared conditions: Nb con- 
tents is about 1.5 wt% and annealing temperature is 10 0 0 °C. 
2) N3 (Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-1.5Nb, wt%) alloys annealed at 
10 0 0 °C exhibited the highest tensile strength and an acceptable 
ductility, even at high test temperature of 300 °C, 500 °C and 
800 °C. 
3) P 2 peak, a predominant IF peak disappeared in Nb contain- 
ing FeCrAlMo alloy was related to the recovery of the dislocations 
and recrystallization process, the pinning effect by the high-density
