Abstract
High entropy alloys (HEAs) are multi-component metallic materials renowned for their exceptional thermal stability, 
superior corrosion resistance, and other outstanding properties, which make them highly promising for various applica -
tions. This study investigates the tensile mechanical characteristics of the Al 0.3CoCrxFeNi high entropy alloys with vary -
ing chromium (Cr) contents (at%) through Molecular Dynamics simulations (MD). The results indicate that the tensile 
strength and elongation exhibit similar fluctuations. The mechanism of plastic deformation in the alloy transitions from 
a combined process involving both dislocation slip and twin deformation to one that is primarily characterized by single 
twin deformation. This change can be attributed to the lattice distortion induced by the incorporation of Cr into the 
alloy. Furthermore, a thorough investigation was conducted to assess the influence of temperature and strain rates on the 
mechanical properties of these alloys. Temperature fluctuations significantly affect the mechanical characteristics of the 
Al0.3CoCrxFeNi high-entropy alloys. As the temperature increases, the Young’s modulus, tensile strength, and toughness 
decrease. Under the conditions of low strain rates and gradually rising temperature, the tensile strength, Young’s modulus, 
and elongation of the Al 0.3CoCr1.0FeNi HEAs decreased by 4.55 GPa, 12.91 GPa, and 2.9%, respectively. The plastic 
deformation mechanism gradually shifts from predominantly twin deformation to the coexistence of twin and dislocation 
slip. The tensile strength and elongation rise with an increase in strain rate. This phenomenon is attributed to the high 
stress levels at elevated strain rates, which activate multiple dislocation sources simultaneously, thereby increasing the 
number of dislocations and their interactions, leading to enhanced strength.

Traditional experimental and material property charac -
terization techniques are the predominant research method-
ologies employed by the majority of materials researchers 
today. However, this approach presents several limitations, 
including long periods and high costs; challenges in con -
ducting in-depth investigations of complex systems; and 
the potential for experimental results be greatly affected by 
human factors. Over the past decade, computer simulation 
technology has received considerable attention, and numer-
ous researchers have utilized this method to overcome the 
limitations of traditional experimental conditions [ 16–18]. 
This present a novel persective for research in materials 
science.
Molecular dynamics [19] is a type of computer simulation 
technology that investigates the microstructure and macro -
scopic properties of materials by simulating the motion of 
atoms and molecules. Its advantages include the ability to 
elucidate the intricate processes of chemical reactions, pre -
dict material properties, optimize drug design, and provide a 
comprehensive understanding of complex systems [20–22].
MD simulations serve as a valuable resource that goes 
beyond conventional theoretical and experimental methods. 
This is primarily due to their ability to accurately predict 
and uncover the physical and chemical properties of materi-
als, as well as the mechanisms of atomic level elastic-plastic 
deformation.
Hoang et al. [ 23] conducted a study on the deforma -
tion mechanisms of the Al x(CoCuFeNi)1−x alloys during 
nanoindentation using molecular dynamics simulations. 
The research aimed to examine the effects of temperature, 
grain size, and alloy composition on mechanical properties. 
The results suggest that as the temperature and Al content 
increase, both the indentation force and hardness exhibit 
a decreasing trend. The enhancement of ductility in the 
alloys is associated with the growth of shear bands within 
the matrix, which results from higher Al content and larger 
grain size. The movement of prismatic dislocations plays 
a critical role in the deformation mechanisms occurring in 
these materials. Zhang et al. [24] employed MD simulations 
to explore the relationship between elemental concentration 
and the compression deformation mechanisms in single-
crystal Co x(CrNi)100−x high entropy alloys. The findings 
indicated that increased Co content leads to improvements in 
both yield stress and the elastic modulus. As the concentra-
tion of Co rises, the mechanism of deformation shifts from 
dislocation slip to twinning. Zhang et al. [ 25] investigated 
how varying strain rates influence the deformation mecha -
nisms of alloys using molecular dynamics simulations. The 
findings clearly indicate that the CoCrFeMnNi HEAs dem -
onstrate distinct deformation mechanisms at varying strain 
rates. At low strain rates, the nucleation and propagation 
of stacking faults and twins are intricately governed by the 
dynamics of Shockley dislocations, which play a pivotal 
role in the material’s deformation behavior. A continuous 
phase transition mechanism from the FCC to the hexagonal 
close-packed (HCP) crystal structure has been observed at 
high strain rates.
These researchers conducted their study using molecular 
dynamics simulations. Molecular dynamics is recognized 
for its high accuracy in calculations and its capability to 
simulate the microscopic movements of complex systems, 
thereby providing detailed atomic-level information, which 
make it well-suited for addressing challenges in fields such 
as biological macromolecules and materials science.
However, previous studies did not reveal the atomic-
scale mechanical properties or the mechanisms of elastic 
and plastic deformation in the Al0.3CrxFeCoNi high-entropy 
alloys. Molecular dynamics simulation is a powerful tool 
for investigating these properties. This study employs MD 
simulations to investigate the effect of Cr on the mechanical 
properties of high-entropy alloys. Additionally, the influence 
of temperature and strain rate was systematically examined.

2 MD model and method
In this paper, the molecular dynamics simulation software 
Lammps [26] is utilized to perform tensile simulations of the 
Al0.3CoCrxFeNi (x = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, at %) high entropy 
alloys, aiming to investigate their mechanical properties 
under external forces. The models of the Al 0.3CoCrxFeNi 
HEAs with varying Cr contents are depicted in Fig. 1.
Before performing the stretching, first create a simulation 
box and add one kind of atom into the box. Subsequently, 
use the “type/ratio” command to convert the first type of 
atom into atoms of the other four elements. Meanwhile, the 
conversion ratio can be adjusted to create a system with the 
target ratio. Then, use the “pair_coeff” command to add the 
potential function file to the model, which describes the 
interactions among all elements in the alloy system.

simulations, with the EAM potential function employed to 
describe interatomic interactions. All studies were carried 
out by stretching the model along the Z-axis.
