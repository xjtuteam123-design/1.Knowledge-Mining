Abstract
Microstructure and tensile properties in the nickel intermetallics, Ni–20Al–27.5Fe and Ni–20Al–27.5Fe–1Ti (at.%), were
investigated in order to understand the effect of zirconium addition. It was found that the addition of zirconium causes formation
of a thin layer along the phase boundary of NiAl and Ni
3Al. The thin layer was identiﬁed as an ordered fcc phase with 6.9 A /p44
in lattice parameter. The compressive deformation caused by hot extrusion results in ﬁne precipitates and a mixture of subgrains
within the matrix phase in zirconium-doped alloy. Additional zirconium gives signiﬁcant improvement in the room and high
temperature fracture strength and strain. The extruded zirconium-doped alloy exhibited a high tensile elongation of above 10%,
and also a relatively high tensile strength of 1350 Mpa at room temperature, keeping it as high as 600 Mpa at 700°C. © 2002

1. Introduction
In order to develop nickel aluminides, such as NiAl
and Ni 3Al for high-temperature structural applica-
tions, extensive work has been focused on improve-
ment of the mechanical properties. Much work has
been carried out with single-phase Ni
3Al alloys with
L12 structure. As a result, remarkable improvements
in these areas have been achieved [1]. Single-phase
NiAl with B2 structure has also been studied to some
extent [2], but a remedy for room temperature brittle-
ness has not yet been proposed. Recent attempts to
improve the ductility through alloying and mi-
crostructural modiﬁcation have shown encouraging
results in (NiAl +Ni
3Al) two-phase alloys [3,4]. A few
studies on the two-phase Ni–Al intermetallics have
indicated that an attractive approach for improving
the ambient ductility is to select Ni–Al–Fe ternary
alloys [5–7]. It was reported by Law and Blackburn
that the two-phase Ni–20Al–27.5Fe alloy processed
by casting exhibited as much as 12% tensile strain to
fracture at ambient temperature [5]. The ductile be-
havior was attributed to the combination of ﬁne grain
size by dual phase structure and a suppression of the
crack development. Despite these attractive ambient
tensile properties, the two-phase Ni–Al–Fe alloy has
been restricted in its use as a high-temperature struc-
tural material since it has poor high-temperature
strength [5,8]. The present investigation was under-
taken to characterize the low- and high-temperature
tensile properties about Ni–20Al–27.5Fe–1Ti alloys
with and without zirconium for which no detailed
characterization is currently available although some
preliminary tensile results were reported recently by
Choe [8].

2. Experimental details
Two heats of different chemical compositions, Ni –
20Al–27.5Fe–1Ti and Ni –20Al–27.5Fe–1Ti–1Zr in
atomic percent, were prepared by a vacuum induction
melting process. Sections (100 mm) from both ingots
(50 mm diameter, 130 mm long) were placed in 70 mm
OD cans of mild steel and extruded to rod at 1100 °Ca t
9:1 reduction in area and air cooled. Sections, 100 mm
long, of extruded material were re-canned in mild steel
cans and re-extruded at 1100 °C at 4:1 reduction in area
and air cooled. Tensile specimens of 6 mm diameter
and 25 mm gauge length were prepared from the ingots
and the double-extruded rods, and tensile tested at
room and 800 °C temperature range. Phase identi ﬁca-
tion was performed in a JEOL scanning electron micro-
scope with an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS)
system using a standard calibration method. Thin foils,
prepared by electro-thinning in a 10% perchloric and
90% methanol solution at −30°C and 40 V, were
examined in a JEOL 200CX transmission electron mi-
croscope operated at 200 kV.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
It was found that both alloys of Ni –20Al–27.5Fe–
1Ti and Ni –20Al–27.5Fe–1Ti–1Zr are basically com-

treatment. The zirconium-doped alloy exhibits a thin
layer along the phase boundary as shown in Fig. 1b,
whereas the zirconium-free alloy does not show this
feature.

3.2. Tensile test
It is believed that the ﬁne structure of subgrains and
precipitates improve tensile properties. The tensile
strength and elongation in the temperature range be-
tween room temperature and 800 °C are shown in Fig.
7. The extruded specimens of zirconium-doped alloy
exhibited a high tensile elongation of above 10% at all
of test temperatures. The two-step hot extrusion has
inﬂuence on increasing tensile strength up to 1350 Mpa
at room temperature and keeping it high up to 600
Mpa at 700 °C.
The room and high temperature strength level of
zirconium-doped alloy (NAF –Zr) exceeds the strength
level of IC 218 alloy which is one of the most successful

developed alloys in nickel intermetallics. Moreover, the
extruded NAF –Zr alloy has a similar strength level as
the forged Alloy 718, the most popular superalloys for
high temperature applications. It shows that the addition
of zirconium in extruded alloys causes a signi ﬁcant
improvement on tensile properties over the whole test
temperature range.
It seems that the zirconium segregated thin layer at the
phase boundary brought about a microstructure re ﬁne-
ment by recrystallization and precipitation within matrix
grains during hot extrusion (Figs. 5 and 6).
From the previous microstructural analyses about
boron-doped alloy such as zirconium-doped aluminide
[3], it was reported that boron addition induced the thin
layer along grain boundaries, and the thin layers have a
lower level of aluminum content in the matrix. Accord-
ingly, the aluminum content in the matrix of the boron-
doped alloy is higher than that of the boron-free alloy.
The different behavior of dynamic recrystallization in the
two alloys is likely to be caused by the different alu-
minum contents in the matrix of the alloys. The high
plastic strain behavior at the maximum stress in the
boron-doped alloys seems to be a result of the promoted
dynamic recrystallization [3].
Like the effect of boron addition in Ni aluminide, the
microstructure reﬁnement of the zirconium-doped alloy
can be affected by the higher level aluminum content in
matrix than that of the zirconium-free alloy, and play a
role of improving the tensile properties since it can
provide the sites of boundary obstacles for dislocation
behavior or crack propagation the promoted dynamic
recrystallization of zirconium-doped alloy.
In addition the relatively soft phase of the thin layer
in phase boundary (as shown in Fig. 8) may give an effect
of the restriction on crack propagation in zirconium-
doped alloy. Tensile fracture strength and strain at room
and high temperatures increased with zirconium addition
owing to the enhanced grain boundary cracking resis-
tance of the soft thin layers, in which much zirconium
is segregated.
4. Conclusions
(1) Addition of zirconium causes formation of a thin
layer along phase boundary for the Ni –20Al–27.5Fe–
1Ti–1Zr. The thin layer is identi ﬁed as an ordered fcc
with 6.9 A/p44in lattice parameter, of which the chemical
composition is 59Ni –10Al–15Fe–16Zr in atomic
percent.
(2) Compressive deformation caused by hot extrusion
results in ﬁne precipitates and a mixture of subgrains
within matrix phase in zirconium-doped alloy.
(3) The double extruded specimens of zirconium-
doped alloy exhibited a high tensile elongation of above
10% at all of test temperatures, and also a relatively high
tensile strength of 1350 Mpa at room temperature, as
keeping it high up to 600 Mpa at 700 °C.
