Abstract 
The m icrostructure and tensile properties of dual structure superalloys were 
investigated. Dual structure supera lloys are directionally solidified into fine grain s 
and columnar grains with aid of seeds technique . In the transition zone coarse grains 
and pseudo columnar grains are observed. MC carbides evolve from blocky to 
script-like and volume fraction of micro-pores decreases during transition from fine 
grains to columnar grain s. The ultimate tensile strength of dual structure superalloys 
is slightly lower than that of the base superalloys with only fine grains or columnar 
grains in the present experiment , but reduction of ductility of the dual structure 
superalloys is not observed. The deformation and fracture behavior s of dual structure 
superalloys are an alyzed based on t he grain rotation and surface deformation 
morphologies. 
Keywords: 
Superalloys; tensile properties; dual structure; fine grain; columnar grain

Blisk, an inte gral turbine of blades and disk, has been widely used in small gas 
turbines [1-2]. To meet the demand of the creep properties of blades as well as the low 
cycle fatigue properties of disk, the dual structure bl isk with directionally solidified 
(DS) blades and fine-grain disk is designed [3, 4]. Several innovative manufacturing 
processes have been developed for the dual structure blisk. For example, directional 
solidification and grainex (DS/GX) casting [3], diffusion bonding by hot isostatic 
pressing (HIP) [5-7], and linear friction welding [8, 9]. The mechanical properties of 
the dual structure superalloys with columnar grains and fine grains have critical 
effects on the performance of the blisk. 
It has been reported that dual structure superalloys exhibit excellent tensile 
strength but low ductility at intermediate temperatures [5-7]. In general, poor ductility 
at i ntermediate temperatures is prevalent in conventionally cast equiaxed and DS 
Ni-based superalloys [10-12]. The tensile elongation of dual structure joints bonded 
by HIP diffusion bonding is very poor [5-7]. For example, the tensile elongation of 
CMSX-4 and U720 bonding joints at 750 oC is only about 3 %, which is significantly 
lower than that of base materials (10 %) [7]. Tensile ductility of dual structure alloy 
with single crystalline DD6 and powder metallurgy FGH96 superalloys at 750 oC is 
also less than 4 % [6]. 
The reasons for the low ductility are not clear. Results from Larker [7] revealed 
that the reduction of ductility may be related to the large difference of elastic modulus 
at the joint . However, low elongation has also been observed in the linear friction 
welding of powder metallurgy alloy LSHR and cast Mar-M247 with similar elastic

modulus [13]. In addition, the dual structure superalloys solidified by DS/GX casting 
process exhibit the same level of ductility as the base material [3]. Therefore, the 
tensile behaviors of dual structure superalloys at intermed iate temperatures remain 
controversial. 
In the present study, dual structure superalloy bars with fine grains and columnar 
grains are solidified. To eliminate the influence of ′ and micro-pores on the tensile 
properties of superalloys, HIP and heat treatments are employed. Microstructures and 
tensile properties at intermediate temperatures are investigated. 
2. Experimental 
The actual chemical composition of the master superall oy K417G used in this 
work is as follows (in wt.%): 0.18C, 8.94Cr, 10.01Co, 3.05Mo, 5.26Al, 4.33Ti, 0.79V , 
0.07Zr and 0.018B. Firstly, the master alloys were cast into 16 120 mm bars by 
fine-grain casting process. Then, fine-grain seeds with diameter of 15 mm and length 
of 100 mm were machined from these bars . During the DS process part of the 
fine-grain seeds was remelted and directionally solidified , which result s in dual 
structure bars with fine grains on one side and columnar grains on the other side . For 
the reference tests, fine-grain bars and columnar -grain bars with the same chemical 
compositions were also used. 
All the bars were HIPed and heat treated. The HIP process was performed at 
1200 °C for 4 h under 17 0 MPa, f ollowed by furnace cooling. Th e heat treatment s 
consist of 1120 °C/4 h and 845 °C /24 h, both followed by air cooling. After full heat 
treatments, tensile tests of superalloys with dual structure, fine g rains and columnar

4. Discussion 
4.1 Microstructure 
During solidification of dual structure bars, the top of fine-grain seed is remelted 
into liquid and solidified into columnar grain s by high directional temp erature 
gradient (Fig. 1). However, the unfavorably oriented grains are not able to overgrow 
the favorably oriente d grains with <001> direction during DS casting [14, 15 ]. 
Therefore, pseudo columnar grains deviated from <001> direction form . In addition, 
the coarse /′ eutectics in the fine -grain region dissolve partly (Fig. 2a) , the volume 
fraction of /′ eutectics decreases and coarse grains form in the TAZ, as shown in Fig. 
2b. In addition, script-like carbides are prone to form during transition from fine 
grains to columnar grains [16, 17]. The high temperature gradient during DS casting 
is obviously an important reason for the reduction of micro-pores in the 
columnar-grain region (Fig. 5b) [18, 19]. 
The primary coarse ′ forms during HIP process followed by furnace cooling 
(Fig. 4e-h). Then the primary coarse ′ dissolves partly during the heat treatment at 
1120 oC and fine secondary ′forms at matrix channels during the following air

cooling [20]. Therefore, bimodal distribution of ′ in dual structure superalloys is 
observed. 
4.2 Deformation behaviors 
Octahedral slip systems {111} <110> are usually operated in Ni-based 
superalloys during tensile deformation process. In columnar-grain superalloys with 
<001> grain orientation, multiple slip bands (SB) appear near the fracture surface, 
and the angle between SB and the tensile axis is about 37° , as illustrated in Fig. 7c. In 
addition, grain rotation occurs d uring tensile deformation [21]. The columnar grains 
with similar orientation rotate around the axis of <001> direction (Fig. 9), while the 
rotation direction of fine grains with different crystal orientations is random. 
Therefore, the deformation compatibility of columnar grains is better than that of fine 
grains (Fig.7a), and larger tensile elongation of columnar -grain superalloys is 
observed (Fig. 6). 
Rotation direction of fine -grain region and c olumnar-grain region of dual 
structure superalloys is different during tensile deformation. Larger tensile strain may 
be accumulated near the transition zone between fine-grain region and columnar-grain 
region, which is confirmed by the severe surface d eformation in dual structure 
specimens (Fig. 8b). The deformation incompatibility between fine-grain region and 
columnar-grain region also result in the change of stress field near the interface, as 
illustrated in the previous study [8]. The low elongation of dual structure joints [5-7] 
may be resulted from stress concentration in the bonding interface. However, the 
formation of mixed grain microstr uctures in transition zone of the cast dual structure

superalloys (Fig. 1b) , such as coarse grains and pseudo columnar grains, provides 
excellent deformation compatibility. In addition, the tensile strain of columnar grains 
induced by deformation is larger than that of fine grains because of the lower elastic 
modulus of columnar grains (Fig.6a). Therefore, the elongation o f dual structure 
superalloys is larger than that of fine -grain superalloys (Fig.6b). However, grain 
boundary is one of the main factors that affect the tensile property at 760 oC. The dual 
structure specimens fail in the fine-grain region due to the format ion of intergranular 
cracks, and the elongation of dual structure superalloys is the same as that of 
fine-grain superalloys at 760 oC (Fig. 6d). 
4.3 Fracture behavior 
It was believed that the tensile strength of superalloys depend s on the grain 
boundary and ′ characters [22, 23 ]. Considering the same heat treatment s of 
fine-grain superalloys and columnar -grain superalloys, the size and morphology of ′ 
are similar (Fig. 4d and Fig. 4f). At low temperature (600 oC), the strength of the grain 
boundary is higher than that of the grain interior [24]. The ultimate tensile strength of 
fine-grain superalloys is higher than that of columnar -grain superalloys. The dual 
structure superalloys fail near the columnar-grain region (Fig. 8a). In columnar-grain 
specimen with <001> orientation, the different slip bands result in the stress 
concentration near the MC carbides or micro-pores (Fig. 7c), and transgranular cracks 
are initiated (Fig. 8c), and the final broken occurred with the connection of internal 
and surface cracks. However, more triple junctions in pseudo columnar grains of dual 
structure superalloys are observed, which may result in lower strength of dual

## Page 12

11 
 
structure superalloys. 
The strength of grain boundary decreases faster than that of grain interior with 
increasing temperature [25]. The ultimate tensile strength of columnar -grain 
superalloys is higher than that of fine-grain superalloys at 760 oC. Therefore, the dual 
structure superalloys fail near the fine-grain region (Fig. 8d). In addition, intergranular 
cracks were initiated with the intersection between grain boundaries and SBs (Fig. 7b). 
Moreover, grain boundary cracks form preferentially due to the low strength of grain 
boundaries with increasing temperature (Fig. 8e) and the elongation of fine -grain 
superalloys is also decreased for the rapid prop agation of cracks along the grain 
boundaries. 
 5. Conclusions 
The microstructure and tensile properties of dual structure superalloys with fine 
grains and columnar grains are investigated. It can be concluded as follows: 
(1) Dual structure superalloys solidified by directional solidification process 
consist of fine grain s, coarse grains , pseudo columnar grains and columnar grains. 
The pseudo columnar grains are deviating from <001> direction. During transition 
from fine grains to columnar grains, morphology of MC carbides evolves from blocky 
to script-like, and the size and volume fraction of micro-pores decrease. 
(2) At 600 oC the tensile strength of dual structure superalloys is slightly lower 
than that of the base superalloys. While the tensile strength of fine-grain superalloys is 
higher than that of columnar -grain superalloys, and fracture of dual structure 
specimens occurs near the columnar-grain region.

## Page 13

12 
 
(3) At 760 oC the tensile strength of dual structur e superalloys is slightly lower 
than that of the base superalloys. While the tensile strength of columnar -grain 
superalloys is higher than that of fine -grain superalloys, and the fracture of dual 
structure specimens occurs near the fine-grain region. 
(4) The deformation incompatibility of fine -grain region and columnar -grain 
region results in the stress concentration near the transition zone . The formation of 
mixed grain microstructures near the transition zone provides the excellent 
deformation compatibility and reduction of tensile ductility of dual structure 
superalloys is not observed.
