Design, strength prediction of Ti35Nb xSnyZrzMo alloys with
low elastic moduli and experimental veriﬁcation on
their mechanical properties
Yu Wang, Shi-Juan Dai, Feng Chen*,
Xin-Quan Yu, You-Fa Zhang
Received: 31 October 2012 / Revised: 22 March 2013 / Accepted: 10 April 2013
/C211The Nonferrous Metals Society of China and Springer-Verlag Berlin Heidelberg 2013
Abstract Based on the d-electron alloy design method
and the average valence electron concentration theory,
three kinds of metastable b-type Ti–35Nb-based alloys
containing different quantities of Sn, Zr, and Mo alloying
elements were designed, whose Md = 2.45 eV and
e/a = 4.24, and Bo values = 2.869, 2.866, and 2.860,
respectively. Meanwhile, a formula for predicting the solid
solution strength of Ti–Nb-based alloys was developed
using JMatPro software. The experiments show that the
designed alloys are all composed of single b phase after
solid solution treatment, the tensile strength of the alloys
coincides well with that predicted by the formula (the
relative error \4 %), and the elastic moduli decrease with
Bo values increasing. The Ti35Nb3.7Zr1.3Mo alloy has the
biggest Bo value (2.869) and the lowest elastic modulus
(54 GPa) among the three designed alloys.
Keywords Ti–Nb alloys; Elastic modulus; d-Electron
alloy design
1 Introduction
Pure titanium and Ti6Al4V alloys are now the most widely
used surgical implant materials, but their elastic moduli
(*110 GPa) are much higher than those of human bone
(3–35 GPa) [1, 2]. Moreover, V and Al elements in Ti6Al4V
alloy are cytotoxic and harmful to the human body. There-
fore, the development of the b-type titanium alloys com-
posed of non-toxic elements such as Nb, Ta, Zr, Sn, and Mo
[3] with lower elastic moduli has become one of the hot
topics in the ﬁeld of bio-implant materials in recent years.
The d-electron alloy design method is often used in the
design of titanium alloys with low elastic moduli, which
can predict the range of elastic moduli of titanium alloys by
employing two electronic parameters [ 4]: one is the bond
order (Bo) which is calculated from the overlap populations
of the d-electrons between atoms in the cluster and the
other is the metal d-orbital energy level ( Md) which cor-
relates with the electronegativity and metallic radius of
elements. References [ 5, 6] calculated the average values
of Bo ([2.84) and Md of different titanium alloys and
plotted them on the Bo /C0 Md map. It is found that the
elastic moduli of these alloys in the b region decrease with
Bo and Md values increasing. The authors of the present
paper prepared 51 kinds of b Ti–Nb-based alloys in a
previous study and found a similar tendency that the elastic
moduli of b Ti–Nb-based alloys decreased with increasing
Bo þ Md values, as shown in Fig. 1. Moreover, Satio and
Furuta [ 7] pointed out that the alloys consisting of the
elements in Group IVB and VB and having the value of
Bo * 2.87, Md * 2.45 eV, and e/a * 4.24 may exhibit
ultralow elastic modulus and ultrahigh strength after cold
working. The above results can offer reference for
designing titanium alloys with low elastic moduli.
Moreover, the strength of the designed alloys should
also be predicted by some means so as to obtain an alloy
having a high strength and a low elastic modulus simul-
taneously. JMatPro is a kind of thermodynamic simula-
tion software based on the CALPHAD method. The
strength simulation of titanium alloys using JMatPro soft-
ware now mainly focuses on Ti–Al system and the cal-
culated strength is consistent with the experimental one
[8–10].

In this study, three new b-type Ti35Nb xSnyZrzMo

the tensile strength of the alloys after solid solution treat-

alloys were prepared and their mechanical properties were
tested so as to verify the validity of the theoretical
predictions.

3 Experimental
Titanium alloys with the nominal compositions listed in
Table 1 were prepared by the arc-melting method under an
Ar atmosphere. Then, they were hot forged to the plates
with a thickness of 3 mm. The hot-forged plates were
ﬁnally solution treated at 800 /C176C for 30 min, followed by
water quenching.
The samples were burnished, mechanically polished and
eroded, and their microstructures were observed using an
optical microscope (OM) and a transmission electron
microscope (TEM, FEI Tecnai G2 T20). The phase struc-
tures of the alloys were analyzed by an X-ray diffractom-
eter (XRD, XR-3A).
The mechanical properties of the alloys such as tensile
strength r
b, yield strength r0.2, elongation d, and elastic
modulus E were obtained by an electronic universal test
machine (CMT 5105).
For evaluating the b stability of the designed alloys, the
solution-treated samples were heated at 450 /C176C for 12 h
followed by air cooling, and then were observed using a
scanning electron microscope (SEM, FEI Sirion).

Figures 3 and 4 show the microstructures and XRD proﬁles

As shown in Table 2, the relative errors between the

The results show that with the increase of Sn content, the
Bo values of the alloys decrease, leading to a higher elastic
modulus (see Fig. 7b; Table 2). Bo value represents the
covalent bond energy determined by the overlapping
d-electron cloud. The density of state of Ti–Sn alloy reveals
that the bonding mainly occurs among the valence electrons
of Ti 3p, 3d, and Sn 5p orbits, which leads to the interaction
between d-orbital electrons in Ti–Sn bond, which is weaker
than that in Ti–Ti bond [ 25], making
Bo values of the
designed alloys decrease with Sn content increasing.
The effect of Sn content on the elastic modulus can be
explained according to the values of mixing enthalpy
between Ti–M (M = Sn, Zr and Mo). The mixing enthalpy
of Ti–Sn, Ti–Zr, and Ti–Mo binary alloys is -21, 0, and
-4k J/C1 mol
-1, respectively, which means the atomic
bonding force between Ti–Sn is stronger than that between
Ti–Mo or Ti–Zr [26]; thus, decreased Sn content leads to a
lower elastic modulus.
It is shown that if the position of a b-type titanium alloy
in the Bo /C0 Md diagram is near to the b/b ? x phase
boundary (e.g., an alloy with weaker b-stability), it has a
lower elastic modulus [ 5]. From Fig. 6, it is seen that the
Ti35Nb3.7Zr1.3Mo alloy has the weakest b stability,
which may be another reason for the alloy to have the
lowest elastic modulus among the three alloys.
