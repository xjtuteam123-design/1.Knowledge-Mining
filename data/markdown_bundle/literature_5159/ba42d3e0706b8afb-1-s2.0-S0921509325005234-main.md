ABSTRACT
Refractory multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs) have garnered attention due to their high strength at 
elevated temperatures. However, their insufficient uniform elongation and thermal stability hinder their prac -
tical application. In this work, a single-phase BCC-structured (VNb)
80
(TaTi)
20 
RMPEA is designed based on the 
considerations of mixing enthalpy and Labusch solid solution strengthening model, which exhibits a tensile 
strength over 900 MPa and uniform elongation of ~19 %. Unfortunately, the alloys containing high- 
concentration interstitial impurities (e.g. 300 wppm O, HO-alloy) suffer from severe embrittlement during 
long-term thermal aging, mainly due to the formation of Ti-(C/N/O) precipitates. By applying the Zr-foil get -

greatly enhances thermal stability. The LO-alloy can maintain the microstructural stability and mechanical

mation, twinning is observed at room temperature, which is believed to enhance both strength and ductility.
1. Introduction
The refractory multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs), composed 
of multiple elements with high melting points with near equal atomic 
concentration, have received significant research interest due to their 
strong resistance to high-temperature softening [ 1 , 2 ]. For example, 
RMPEAs such as VNbTaTi, MoNbTaW, and MoNbTaVW have exhibited 
significantly higher yield strength than traditional refractory metals at 
temperatures above 800 
◦
C [ 3 ], which has been attributed to the slug -
gish diffusion effect [ 4 ]. Nonetheless, the majority of RMPEAs suffer 
from poor ductility at room temperature [ 5 ]. Even though several

strong deformation instability and limit their engineering application 
[ 7 ].
Moreover, the single-phase solid solution structure of many RMPEAs 
tended to be unstable in high-temperature environments, causing 
embrittlement during thermal aging. For example, the single-phase BCC- 
structured HfNbTaZrTi alloy has good ductility with fracture elongation 
over 15 %, but precipitation of the ω phase occurs after heat-treated at 
400

higher temperature of 500 – 700

These issues severely limit the further application of RMPEAs as engi -
neering structural materials in high-temperature environments.
The VNbTaTi RMPEA with a single-phase body-centered-cubic (BCC) 
structure has exhibited excellent mechanical properties and thermal 
stability. For example, the as-cast VNbTaTi alloy, through Charpy 
impact testing and fracture analysis, has been demonstrated with high 
toughness in the temperature range of 150

the homogenized VNbTaTi alloy has exhibited high yield strength and 
excellent ductility at room temperature, along with exceptional phase

temperature-dependent elastic anisotropic deformation behavior was 
less pronounced compared to conventional metallic materials [ 3 , 12 ].

The thermal stability of VNbTaTi has been demonstrated to come 
from the relatively low chemical, lattice, and structural enthalpies from 
the mixing of these principal elements [ 10 ]. Nonetheless, like most re -
fractory metals or alloys, its thermal stability and consequently me -
chanical properties face strong challenges from interstitial impurities 
such as O, N, and C [ 13 , 14 ]. For example, as has been demonstrated in 
conventional V-4Cr-4Ti alloy, after long-term thermal aging, the inter -
stitial atoms tend to combine with the alloying elements with a strong 
affinity to them to form brittle precipitates such as Ti-(C/N/O), which 
will be highly detrimental to the ductility and toughness of the alloys 
[ 14 – 16 ]. These problems severely affect its performance stability as a 
structural material, especially during long-term service, and therefore 
should get enough attention when designing RMPEAs.
It needs to be further noted that numerous studies have demon -
strated that RMPEAs do not necessarily need to stand right at the center 
in the phase diagram to reach the desired properties [ 17 , 18 ]; that is, the 
mechanical properties of the equiatomic VNbTaTi alloy are highly likely 
further optimized with proper modification. For example, by carefully 
tuning the lattice distortion and modulus mismatch, the yield strength of 
alloys could be further enhanced without increasing the density so that 
the specific strength could be improved which is critical to the appli -
cation in space exploration. In this work, we designed a non-equiatomic

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure and mechanical properties of the 8020-HO alloy
The crystal structure of the as-cast 8020 alloy is examined using XRD, 
as presented in Fig. 3 (a). All the diffraction peaks correspond to the BCC 
phase, with no evidence of secondary phases; the lattice constant of the 
as-cast alloy is 3.219 ± 0.003 Å. Fig. 3 (b – d) shows the SEM-BSE images 
of the as-cast 8020 alloy along with the corresponding EDS results. As 
shown in Fig. 3 (b – d), the as-cast alloy displays a typical dendritic 
structure, resulting from the varying melting points of its constituent 
elements. Due to its highest melting point, Ta solidified first and pref -
erentially segregates within the dendrites. Meanwhile, the lower melting 
point elements, Ti and V, remain in the liquid phase longer, leading to 
their segregation in the interdendritic regions. In contrast, Nb, with a 
melting point between these extremes, exhibits a more uniform distri -
bution throughout the alloy.
As shown in Fig. 3 (e), the homogenized alloy also exhibits a single- 
phase solid solution BCC structure, with a similar lattice constant as 
the as-cast alloy. As shown in Fig. 3 (f) and (g), the homogenized alloy 
exhibits a typical equiaxial microstructure with an average grain size of 
201 ± 19 μ m and a uniform elemental distribution.

3.3. Microstructure and mechanical properties of the 8020-LO alloy 
before and after thermal aging
The XRD patterns, SEM-BSE maps, and corresponding SEM-EDS re -
sults of the 8020-LO alloy are shown in Fig. 8 (a – c). The 8020-LO alloy 
exhibits a single-phase BCC structure, with an XRD-measured lattice 
constant of 3.210 ± 0.003 Å, closely resembling the 8020-HO alloy ’ s 
value, indicating that minor O element content does not significantly 
change the lattice constant. Neither a second phase is observed at the 
grain boundaries or within the grains, nor is any elemental segregation 
detected.
As shown in Fig. 8 (d) and (g), no diffraction peaks of secondary 
phases appear after annealing based on XRD characterization. In 
contrast to the alloys with high O concentration, precipitates are not 
found in both grain boundaries and grain interiors. as shown in Fig. 8 (e) 
and (h); also, the elemental distribution maintains uniform, further 
demonstrating excellent structural stability. Clearly, the reduction of the 
O content suppresses the formation of the Ti-(C/N/O) precipitates.

4. Discussion
The uniform elongation and specific yield strength of the 8020 alloys 
investigated in this paper are compared with those of previously re -
ported RMPEAs with tensile plasticity, as shown in Fig. 11 (a). While 
many RMPEAs exhibit high fracture elongation, only a few of them have 
considerable uniform elongation. For example, the Ti
38
V
15
Nb
23
Hf
24 
alloy has a fracture elongation of up to 22.5 %, but its uniform elon -
gation is only 1.4 % [ 6 ], which limits the material ’ s resistance to acci -
dental overload. Additionally, some alloys, e.g., VNbTa, offer both high

Comparing the room temperature tensile curves between the 8020 
alloys with high and low impurity concentrations, as shown in Figs. 4(a) 
and 9(a) , it can be seen that the stress reduction phenomenon on the 
tensile curves occurs only in the elastic-plastic transition zone when the 
O content is high, while it continues to occur throughout the homoge -
neous deformation stage when the O content is low. Such a result can be 
attributed to the continuous generation of deformation twinning during 
the deformation process, and it can be assumed that reducing the O 
content in alloy 8020 promoted the formation of deformation twinning. 
Similar results have been reported for pure metals such as Ti and Nb [ 58 ,
59 ]. It is noteworthy that stress drops can also be attributed to the for -
mation of the Cottrell atmosphere. However, an increase in oxygen 
impurity concentration is expected to facilitate the formation of the 
Cottrell atmosphere, leading to a more pronounced reduction in stress. 
During the uniform deformation stage, serrations are observed in the 
8020-LO alloy, which are not present in the 8020-HO alloy. In this stage, 
deformation twinning plays a dominant role.
The reduction in O content, on the one hand, weakens the degree of 
lattice distortion, reduces the frictional resistance of the lattice, and 
diminishes the effect of interstitial solid solution strengthening; On the 
other hand, it facilitates the formation of deformation twinning, im -
pedes the movement of dislocations, and strengthens the alloy. 
Compared with the 8020-HO alloy, the tensile strength of the 8020-LO 
alloy is reduced by about 60 MPa, indicating that the reduction of the 
O content in the alloy weakens the effect of interstitial strengthening to a 
greater extent than that caused by deformation twinning, thereby 
reducing the alloy ’ s overall strength.
In the 8020 alloys, the electronegativity differences between Ti 
element and C, N, and O elements are 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0, respectively 
[ 60 ], so Ti element will first combine with O element, and then N and C 
elements. It can be speculated that when the conditions for the nucle -
ation of clusters or the second phase are met, Ti first combines with O to 
form Ti-O clusters or the second phase. Subsequently, C and N may enter 
these Ti-O clusters or the second phase, replacing some of the O atoms, 
which could result in the formation of the Ti-(C/N/O) phase.
