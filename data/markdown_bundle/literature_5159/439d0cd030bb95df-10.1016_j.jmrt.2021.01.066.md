abstract
Grain boundary segregation engineering (GBSE) is a promising approach for accurately
manipulating chemical composition, structure and properties of grain boundaries (GBs).
With the aim of attenuating or even removing disadvantages of GBs in nickel-based su-
peralloys, the GB segregation behaviors of 3 d (TieCo), 4d (ZreRh) and 5 d (HfeIr) transition-
metal (TM) solutes X and their effects on the thermodynamic stability and fracture
strength of Ni S3 [110](111) symmetrical tilt grain boundary were systematically investi-
gated, using ﬁrst-principles total-energy calculations based on density functional theory.
We found that all TM solutes considered herein can segregate towards the Ni S3 [110](111)
GB. For TM elements from same row of the periodic table, their segregation energies show a
concave-up parabolic-like dependency on the atomic number of TM elements. The GB
segregations of all considered solutes can lead to a reduction in GB energy and render the
Ni GB more stable, and the GB energies of X-segregated GBs vary linearly with the GB
segregation energies of solutes X. Except elements Zr, Hf and Rh, the GB segregations of the
other 15 TM alloying elements increase the GB fracture strength. The Mn and Re segre-
gations improve the GB fracture strength and thermodynamic stability to a maximum
extent, respectively. The underlying mechanisms for segregation-induced GB stabilizing
and strengthening effects were further revealed at the electronic level by analyzing the
crystal orbital Hamiltonian populations and deformation charge densities. Our work could
provide new insights into designing Ni-based superalloys with desired performances based
on the concept of GBSE.

1. Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys have been developed for application
as the key ‘hot section’ materials of advanced aero-engines
and gas turbines such as turbine disks and blades due to
their excellent high-temperature strengths and creep re-
sistances [ 1e3]. Grain boundaries (GBs) play a signiﬁcant role
in microstructures and mechanical properties of such super-
alloys. They can hinder the dislocation motion and thus help
to improve some mechanical properties of superalloys such as
strength, hardness and so on [ 4e10]. For instance, the
coherent nano-twin boundaries generated by the micro-
twinning deformation under medium-temperature and
high-stress conditions are well known for strengthening both
the single-crystal and the polycrystalline superalloys [ 11e15].
However, GBs are thermodynamically unstable lattice defects
since the energetic cost of intergranular boundary formations
cannot be recompensed by their relatively low entropic
contribution to free energy [ 16]. The thermodynamic insta-
bility of GBs may cause a migration or even a disappearance of
GBs under certain temperature and stress conditions and thus
increase the microstructural and mechanical instabilities of
superalloys, although GBs can be commonly helpful to the
yield strength of superalloys, following the classical
HallePetch relationship [17]. Besides, the strengthening effect
of GBs also can be transformed into a softening effect at some
nano-scale grain sizes for some alloys due to the intrinsic
instability of GBs (i.e., inverse Hall ePetch effect) [ 18].
As compared with grain interiors (hereafter termed the
bulk phase), GBs are usually the mechanically weak regions in
materials [ 19]. The local stress concentration induced by the
limited motion of dislocations at GBs can result in a formation
of cracks or voids near GBs or even an intergranular fracture,
which almost demolishes the desirable properties of super-
alloys and severely limits their industrial applications [20e23].
Although one can grow single crystals to avoid these problems
caused by GBs, the extremely complicated processes with long
production cycles and high fabrication costs for this technique
are hard to bear. In order to attenuate or even remove the
aforementioned disadvantages of GBs and design beneﬁcial
superalloy behaviors, it is essential to explore other methods
that can improve the thermodynamic stability and mechani-
cal properties of GBs in Ni-based superalloys.
Using the common segregation behavior of solutes at GBs
as a manipulation tool, which allows the chemical composi-
tion, structure and properties of GBs to be adjusted, grain
boundary segregation engineering (GBSE) proposed by Raabe
et al. has been proved to be a promising method for enhancing
the thermodynamic stability and mechanical strength of GBs
[24e29]. For example, Liu et al. have found that with the
presence of B atoms in Ni
43.9Co22.4Fe8.8Al10.7Ti11.7B2.5 alloy, Fe
and Co atoms show a strong tendency to segregate to GB re-
gions. The multielement segregation induced disordered
interfacial nanolayer can be used to design ultrahigh-strength
and ductile nano-grained alloys with enhanced GB stability
and associated coarsening resistance [ 29]. Due to the strong
interface segregation of austenite stabilizing element (Mn)
and the release of elastic stresses from host martensite, it has
been found that nanosized austenitic reversion layers form at
decorated martensite GBs. The austenite phase gradually be-
comes stabilized at the martensite GB with the segregation of
sufﬁcient Mn atoms and thus yields the ductile and tough
martensite [ 24]. Khalajhedayati et al. have found that the
element Zr spontaneously segregates to Cu GBs and effec-
tively prevents the grain coarsening during annealing treat-
ments, resulting in a signiﬁcant increase (27%) in the yield
strength of the alloy [ 27].
Based on the concept of GBSE, the GB segregation ten-
dencies of alloying elements in Ni-based superalloys and their
effects on structural and mechanical properties of GBs have
been studied. For example, Razumovskiy et al. have found
that elements Hf, Nb, Ta and B can segregate to the Ni S5
[001](210) GB and increase the fracture strength of the GB.
Using these alloying elements, a new powder metallurgy Ni-
based superalloy with higher tensile strength and creep
resistance had been designed [ 30]. However, one solute can
have distinctly different segregation behaviors at GBs with
different structures and not all elements with the GB segre-
gation tendency can deﬁnitely strengthen the GB. Stefano
et al. have found that the Ni S3 [110](111) GB does not trap H
atoms while the Ni S5 [001](210) GB acts as an effective two-
dimensional sink for H atoms [ 31]. It was also found that the
GB segregations of H and P elements at the Ni S5 [001](210) GB
are harmful to the GB fracture strength [ 32]. Experimentally,
Sahu et al. have found that the S3, S9, and S11 GBs are widely
existing in Ni-based superalloys [ 33], although solute segre-
gation behaviors in these GBs and their effects on the ther-
modynamic stability and mechanical properties of GBs are far
from clear. Hence, it should be indispensable to clarify these
issues, aiming at stabilizing and strengthening Ni GBs via the
promising GBSE method.
As the ﬁrst-principles total-energy calculation method
based on density functional theory (DFT) has been suc-
cessfully employed to investigate the solute-segregation-
related structural, thermodynamics and mechanical prop-
erties of GBs and phase interfaces [ 34e36], so it was used to
study the solute-segregation-induced stabilizing and
strengthening effects on the speciﬁc GB in this work.
Considering that the Ni S3 [110](111) GB appears frequently
in Ni-based superalloys due to its relatively lower GB excess
energy, the segregation tendencies of 3 d (TieCo), 4 d (ZreRh)
and 5 d (HfeIr) transition-metal (TM) solutes X in the Ni S3
[110](111) symmetrical tilt GB and their dependences on the
atomic number of solutes X were ﬁrstly studied in present
work. Then, the effects of GB segregation of alloying ele-
ments X on the thermodynamic stability (GB energy) and
fracture strength (work of separation) of Ni S3 [110](111)
symmetrical tilt GB were systematically investigated.
Finally, through the analysis of crystal orbital Hamiltonian
populations (COHP) [ 37e41] and deformation charge den-
sities of GBs, the underlying mechanisms for solute-
segregation-induced stabilizing and strengthening effects
on the Ni S3 [110](111) GB were discussed at the electronic
level. Our work might be helpful for manipulating the
composition, structure, properties of GBs based on the
concept of GBSE and designing Ni-based superalloys with
desired performances.

relaxing lattice parameters and atomic positions of the initial
GB supercell, the stable Ni S3 [110](111) GB supercell model
with the lowest total energy and optimal interfacial distance
between two grains was obtained.
It should be mentioned that grains in the GB model (see
Fig. 1 (a)) should be thick enough to simulate the bulk envi-

layers with 4 atoms per layer for both grains as contained in

c ¼ 24.40 /C23A) were considered to be sufﬁcient in this work. As
shown in Fig. 1(b), the bulk model of Ni crystal with the same

surface (FS) models, which are grains contained in the Ni S3
[110](111) GB supercell and thus possessing a half number of
Ni atoms of the GB supercell, were built to calculate the work
of separation of GBs.
2.2. First-principles total-energy calculations
All calculations were performed within the framework of
density functional theory (DFT) by using the Vienna ab initio
simulation package (VASP) [ 44e46]. The projector augmented
wave (PAW) method was employed to describe the
electroneion interactions [ 44,47]. The exchange-correlation
functional was treated by using the Per-
deweBurkeeErnzerhof (PBE) form within the generalized
gradient approximation (GGA) [ 47]. A reasonable value of
plane-wave cut-off energy (350 eV) and G-centered k-points
mesh (6 /C2 3 /C2 1) was selected after convergence tests [ 48,49].
During the relaxation of models, convergence criterions of
energy and force were selected as 1 /C2 10

respectively. The spin polarization was set for all calculations
[42].

3.1. GB segregation behaviors of TM alloying elements in
Ni S3 [110](111) GB
As shown in Fig. 2, segregation energies (Eseg) of 18 TM alloying
elements are all negative, indicating that all TM alloying ele-
ments considered in present work can segregate to the Ni S3
[110](111) GB. By ranking GB segregation energies of these TM
solutes from the highest value of Fe ( ¡0.16 eV) to the lowest
value of Re ( ¡0.53 eV), we found that the E
seg decreases in the
order of 0 > Fe ¼ Mn > Co > Cr > Rh ¼ Hf ¼ Ta > Ir ¼ Ti ¼ Zr ¼ Ru
¼ Nb > Os ¼ V ¼ W > Mo > Tc > Re. The elements Re and Tc,
which are loacted in the same family and possess similar
valence electrons, have the strongest GB segregation ten-
dencies due to their lowset segregation energies of ¡0.53 eV
and ¡0.48 eV. Their lowest GB segregation energies should be
associated with their same valence conﬁgurations of half-
ﬁlled d bands and full-ﬁlled s bands.
Moreover, the strong GB segregation tendency of Re has
also been observed in the low angle GB of Ni-based single
crystal superalloy by using electron-backscattered diffraction
and atom probe tomography [ 12]. By contrast, GB segregation
energies of the other TM alloying elements range from ¡0.16
eV to ¡0.22 eV, signalling the relatively weak GB segregation
tendencies of these alloying elements. Comparing with the
average segregation energy of the other 16 solutes, GB segre-
gation energies of Re and Tc decrease by 165% and 140%,
respectively. In addition, for TM alloying elements in same
row of the periodic table, their GB segregation energies always
show a concave-up parabolic-like dependency on their atomic
numbers. Similar phenomenon for the segregation energies of
TM alloying elements in the Al S5 [001](210) GB has also been
found by Mahjoub et al. recently [ 43].

3.3. Electronic properties of segregated and
unsegregated Ni S3 [110](111) GBs
3.3.1. Crystal orbital Hamiltonian populations (COHPs) of
segregated and unsegregated Ni S3 [110](111) GBs
Using the crystal orbital Hamiltonian population (COHP)
method, we can analyze the bonding (positive value of
¡COHP) and anti-bonding (negative value of ¡COHP) states of
a chemical bond easily. As anti-bonding states at high energy
levels reﬂect an electronic instability, so one can try to deplete
anti-bonding areas or adjust the Fermi energy of materials
such as to remove electrons from these unfavorable areas and
stabilize the system. Therefore, COHPs of Ni- X and Ni eNi
bonds contained in the Ni S3 [110](111) GBs segregated by
different TM alloying elements X were calculated to clarify the
segregation-induced GB stabilization mechanism on the
electronic level, using the lobster software [ 37e41]. The
average COHPs for all NieX bonds formed in X-segregated GBs
as well as the corresponding Ni eNi bonds contained in the
unsegregated GB were obtained to analyze the impacts of GB
segregations of TM alloying elements on the GB thermody-
namic stability. Since COHP curves for all segregated GBs are
similar in shape and the GB segregation of Re maximizes the
thermodynamic stability of Ni S3 [110](111) GB, the COHP
curves for some typical GBs including the unsegregated GB
and Re-segregated GBs as well as the Ni bulk structure were

the atomic layer containing Ni1, Ni2, Ni3, Ni4 atoms is
enhanced, in comparison to the unsegregated GB. That is,
more work is required to break down the XeNi and Ni eNi
bonds at the GB and separate the Mn-segregated GB into two
free surfaces. This should be the strengthening mechanism of
the GB segregation of Mn at the electronic level. The GB seg-
regations of other alloying elements including Fe, Co, Cr, Ta,
Ir, Ti, Ru, Nb, Os, V, W, Mo, Tc and Re have a similar
strengthening mechanism due to the similar views of defor-
mation charge densities for GBs segregated by these solutes.
On the contrary, the segregation of solute Zr reduces the
charge densities in the middle regions between Zr and Ni2,
Ni3, Ni4 atoms as well as Ni5 and Ni3, Ni4 (dark blue parts), as
shown in Fig. 6 (f) and (g). Hence, the strengths of Zr eNi2,
ZreNi3, Zr eNi4, Ni5 eNi3 and Ni5 eNi4 bonds are weakened.
As compared with the unsegregated GB, less work is required
to break down these bonds forming at the Zr-segregated GB
and thus the fracture strength of Zr-segregated GB gets lower.

segregation-induced depletion of charge densities across the

4. Conclusion
The segregation behaviors of 3 d (TieCo), 4 d (ZreRh) and 5 d
(HfeIr) transition-metal (TM) solutes X in the Ni S3 [110](111)
grain boundary (GB) and their effects on the thermodynamic
stability, fracture strength and electronic properties of Ni S3
[110](111) GB were systematically investigated from ﬁrst-
principles calculations. In order to clarify the segregation
tendencies of TM elements in the speciﬁc GB of nickel-based
superalloys as well as the segregation-induced GB stabilizing
and strengthening mechanisms. Our results can be summa-
rized as follows:
(I) All 18 TM alloying elements considered in this work
prefer segregating to the Ni S3 [110](111) GB. Their GB
segregation tendencies increase in the order of Fe ¼
Mn < Co < Cr < Rh ¼ Hf ¼ Ta < Ir ¼ Ti ¼ Zr ¼ Ru ¼ Nb < Os
¼ V ¼ W < Mo < Tc < Re. For TM solutes from same row
of the periodic table, their segregation energies decrease
ﬁrst and then increase with the increase of atomic
number of TM elements. The strongest GB segregation
tendencies of elements Re and Tc should be associated
with their same valance conﬁgurations of half-ﬁlled
d bands and full-ﬁlled s bands.
(II) The variation of GB energies ( g
GB) with the GB segrega-
tion energies (Eseg) of alloying elements X can be linearly
expressed as gGB ¼ 0.74Eseg þ 0.17, indicating that the
stronger the segregation tendency of TM element X, the
higher the thermodynamic stability of X-segregated GB.
(III) The segregations of all considered TM alloying elements
mainly focus on improving the GB thermodynamic
stability rather than the GB fracture strength. Except
elements Zr, Hf and Rh, the GB segregations of the other
15 TM elements (Fe, Mn, Co, Cr, Ta, Ir, Ti, Ru, Nb, Os, V,
W, Mo, Tc, Re) can increase the GB fracture strength.
The GB segregation of Mn can improve the fracture
strength of the Ni S3 [110](111) GB to a maximum de-
gree, while the GB segregations of Re and Tc have the
greatest contributions to the thermodynamic stability
of the GB.
(IV) The segregation-induced stabilizing effect on the Ni S3
[110](111) GB is due to the reduction of anti-bonding
states occupying at high energy levels and the in-
crease of bonding states occupying at low energy levels
below the Fermi level caused by the GB segregations of
TM elements. For the GB fracture strength, the GB
strengthening or weakening mechanism of solute seg-
regations can be mainly attributed to the accumulation
or depletion of charge densities across the Ni S3
[110](111) GB.
It should be noted that the works of separation of GBs
calculated herein have not considered any lattice defects and
thus are ideal values. They are mainly used to predict the
potential effects of alloying elements on improving the
fracture strength of GBs. Since the realistic fracture strength
may never reach the ideal value due to the effect of lattice
defects in practical materials or GBs, the ideal GB fracture
strengths also could help to identify the reasonability of
experimental or theoretical results.
