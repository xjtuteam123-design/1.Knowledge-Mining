ABSTRACT
Control of the oxygen content is the key to the strengthening and toughening of the TZM (Mo-Ti-Zr-C) alloy. To 
reduce the oxygen content, Mo-C@ZrC-C@TiC alloy was synthesized by the liquid phase dispersion method, and 
then TiC/ZrC was carbon-coated in situ based on the TZM alloy components. Compared with Mo-ZrC-TiC-C and 
Mo-ZrH
2
-TiH
2
-C alloys, the oxygen content of the synthesized Mo-C@ZrC-C@TiC alloy was significantly reduced 
to 160 ppm, and the uniform dispersion of the secondary phase was ensured, thus improving the density and 
hardness of the alloy. The tensile strength at room temperature was 915.4 MPa and the elongation was 14.8 %. 
Compared with Mo-ZrC-TiC-C and Mo-ZrH
2
-TiH
2
-C, the tensile strength and elongation increased by 10.4 % and 
5.1 %, respectively, while the elongation increased by 17.8 % and 24.1 %, respectively. The recrystallization 
temperature of the alloy increased, and its strength was maintained after annealing. This study illustrates that the 
carbon-coated second-phase exhibits solid solution strengthening at high temperatures and dispersion 
strengthening at low temperatures.
1. Introduction
Molybdenum has the characteristics of a low coefficient of thermal 
expansion, high thermal conductivity, and significant corrosion resis -
tance, and plays a crucial role in advancing technology, modernizing 
infrastructure, and enhancing national defense capabilities [ 1 – 3 ]. TZM 
alloys (the addition of titanium, zirconium, and carbon) are widely used 
high-temperature molybdenum alloys. They have a high elastic 
modulus, low thermal expansion coefficient, low saturated vapor pres -
sure, exceptional corrosion resistance, high melting point, and superior 
temperature durability [ 4 ]. Additionally, they are applicable in X-ray 
rotating anode components, die casting molds, high-temperature 
furnace heat energy generators, and thermal insulation panels [ 5 ]. 
Furthermore, they have been extensively employed in high-temperature 
support components of nuclear energy systems [ 6 , 7 ]. Consequently, 
they have extensive applications in high-precision equipment 
manufacturing, the energy sector, aerospace, and nuclear power. How -
ever, at the same time, there is also a high oxygen content, the 
second-phase particles are easily oxidized, and other problems need to 
be solved. Currently, TZM alloys are predominantly processed via 
powder metallurgy. The powder metallurgy process is intricate, and 
impurity elements, such as oxygen, can be easily introduced at various 
stages. The elevated oxygen content in TZM alloys significantly impairs 
their tensile strength and elongation at elevated temperatures [ 8 – 10 ].

In the TZM alloy prepared by laser powder bed cladding, zirconium

A low-oxygen TZM alloy was prepared by vacuum sintering and

ygen content was too low [ 17 ]. Organic carbon was used as a reducing 
agent for the first time to reduce the oxygen content of the TZM alloy,

[ 18 ]. The highly dense TZM alloy was prepared by high-energy ball 
grinding, and the oxygen adhesion area was reduced by reducing the 
pores, but the particle size of the raw powder was strictly required and 
controlled [ 19 ].
Elevated oxygen levels in TZM alloys convert carbides into oxides, 
shifting the strengthening mechanism from solution to dispersion 
strengthening. This transition impacts both the tensile strength and 
elongation properties of the alloy. The results indicate that the ductility- 
brittleness transition temperature, recrystallization temperature, and 
toughness of molybdenum alloys are correlated with the alloy ’ s oxygen 
content [ 20 ].
The oxidation of molybdenum alloys can be effectively controlled by 
adding Hf, mullite and other composite coatings on the surface of mo -
lybdenum alloy, but the oxygen introduced in the alloy is difficult to be 
removed during the preparation process [ 21 – 23 ]. The hardness and 
oxidation temperature of the MHC alloy obtained by introducing Hf and 
C elements in molybdenum alloy were improved, but the oxidation was 
aggravated by the oxide stripping defects formed under long-term 
oxidation conditions [ 24 ]. The addition of nano-structured Y-Zr-O 
composite oxides can significantly strengthen molybdenum alloys, but 
the refined grains provide more oxygen diffusion boundaries [ 25 ]. 
Therefore, on the basis of strengthening molybdenum alloy, it is 
necessary to reduce its oxygen content.
The control of the carbon content and form, uniform dispersion of 
the second-phase, and oxidation resistance in TZM alloys still lack 
effective research [ 26 ]. The polymer coating prepared for SiC/TiC/ZrC 
carbides significantly improves the anti-oxidation ability of the material 
system, and shows a good application prospect in the field of 
high-temperature antioxidation coating [ 27 – 29 ]. At the same time, the 
dispersion of the second-phase in TZM alloys prepared by different 
doping methods also affects the sintering process and mechanical 
properties of the alloys.
Therefore, this paper proposes a novel approach involving carbon- 
coated second-phase liquid dispersion to address the issues of carbide 
oxidation in a molybdenum matrix and high oxygen content in TZM 
alloys. A Mo-C@ZrC-C@TiC alloy with a coating thickness of less than 3 
nm was successfully synthesized, and the effect of the concentration of 
organic matter on the thickness of the coating layer was investigated. 
The oxygen content of the TZM alloy was significantly reduced, the 
deoxidation mechanism was elucidated, and improvements in the me -
chanical properties of the TZM alloy were demonstrated, along with an 
explanation of the underlying mechanisms.

The phase composition of the Mo alloy powder was analyzed using 
an X-ray diffractometer (D8 ADVANCE A25). Energy dispersive spec -
troscopy (EDS), field emission scanning electron microscopy (Gemini 
SEM 300), electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD), and transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM) were employed to investigate the micro -
structure and morphology of the powders and alloys. The density of the 
sample was determined using the Archimedes principle, the average 
hardness was assessed with a hardness tester (Walbert 401 MVD), and 
the tensile properties at room temperature were evaluated using a uni -
versal testing machine (LD26.105). The gas content was measured using 
an oxygen and nitrogen analyzer (RO-316).

3.3. Effect and control mechanism of the carbon-coated second-phase on 
the mechanical properties of the alloy
3.3.1. Hardness and density
Fig.5 shows that the second-phase particle size for sample a# ranged 
from 400 to 500 nm, which is the smallest among the samples. The 
homogeneous dispersion of second-phase particles achieved through 
liquid dispersion evaporation prevents hard agglomeration. However, 
the largest Mo grain size is 40 – 80 μ m, indicating severe coarsening.
Fig.5 (a#) and (b#) show that while the composite powder particle 
size is small, the Mo grain size reaches a maximum of 40 – 80 μ m. This is 
because, during the sintering process, fine powder particles smaller than 
1 μ m behave differently compared to the 3 – 4 μ m powder particles. An 
excessively high sintering temperature and prolonged holding time, 
typically used for 3 – 4 μ m powder, is detrimental to the sintering process 
of fine, compacted powders.
The particle sizes of the second-phase in samples b# and c# were 
significantly larger than that in sample a#. This increase in size is 
attributed to the aggregation of nanoparticles during the direct doping 
process and the fusion of grain boundaries during sintering, which leads 
to coarsening of the second-phase particles. A comparison of samples b# 
and c# reveals that, despite having similar particle sizes as shown in 
Fig. 6 (c) and (d), the grain sizes differ after sintering. The second-phase 
particles in sample c# were more uniformly distributed and inhibited 
the migration and diffusion of grain boundaries, resulting in a smaller 
grain size for sample c# than for sample b#.

3.3.2. Sintered billet fracture morphology and oxygen content

the grain boundaries and intercrystalline, thereby strengthening the 
matrix through both second-phase strengthening and solid solution 
effects.
As the oxygen content of the alloy increased, titanium carbide and 
zirconium carbide were partially converted into oxides during the sin -
tering process, leading to a further increase in oxide size, as illustrated in 
Fig.7 . This results in a markedly non-uniform distribution of the second- 
phase. The observed agglomeration and coarsening of the second-phase 
led to stress concentration, reduced effectiveness of solid solution 
strengthening, and diminished resistance of the second-phase to dislo -
cation slip. Consequently, these changes adversely affected the me -
chanical properties of the alloys.

resulting in a significant increase in the strength of the molybdenum 
alloy [ 32 ]. Despite the low oxygen content in the sample, the material 
brittleness primarily results from the large grain size, loose grain

4. Conclusion
(1) The thickness of the carbon-coating layer of the Mo-C@ZrC- 
C@TiC alloy was less than 3 nm and the distribution was uniform.
(2) The hardness and density of the Mo-C@ZrC-C@TiC alloy have 
significant advantages over those of the other two alloys, and the 
second-phase particles do not undergo significant coarsening.
(3) The tensile strength and elongation of Mo-C@ZrC-C@TiC alloy 
before and after annealing are superior to those of other alloys, 
which is the result of the combined action of solid solution 
strengthening and dispersion strengthening. Moreover, the 
recrystallization temperature of Mo-C@ZrC-C@TiC alloy 
increased due to the interaction of the second-phase.
(4) The oxygen content of the Mo-C@ZrC-C@TiC alloy was less than 
160 ppm, and the second-phase particles were uniformly 
distributed and were not oxidized.
