ABSTRACT 
In order to improve the mechanical strength of Cantor alloy and obtain the uniform microstructure, about 9.1 at. 
% of Al element was introduced and a new CoCrNiFeMnAl
0.5 
HEAs was prepared by gas atomization combined 
with oscillatory pressure sintering. The effects of powder sizes on the microstructure and mechanical properties 
of the as-sintered alloys were investigated and the corresponding strengthening mechanism was discussed. The 
results show that the CoCrNiFeMnAl
0.5 
HEAs were composed of FCC phase with good plasticity and BCC phase 
with high strength. The as-sintered alloys have higher relative density and uniform microstructure without the 
defects of composition segregation and coarse structure like the as-cast alloy. The yield strength and micro -
hardness of the as-sintered alloys increased to 775 MPa and 369.3 HV, which were 173% and 104% higher than 
that of the as-cast alloy, respectively. It is indicated that the strength and microhardness of the as-sintered alloys 
depend on the powder sizes. The strengthening mechanisms of the as-sintered alloys were mainly attributed to 
the second phase strengthening and the grain boundary strengthening. 
1. Introduction 
Since the concept of high entropy alloy (HEA) was proposed, it has 
attracted much attention due to its excellent properties, such as high 
hardness, high wear resistance, and high corrosion resistance, etc. [ 1 – 5 ]. 
HEA has high hybrid entropy so that the compatibility between the 
principals is increased. It is more inclined to form a single solid solution 
phase rather than an inter-terminal solid solution or intermetallic 
compound [ 6 ]. Single-phase Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) exhibits excel -
lent ductility [ 7 , 8 ]. CoCrNiFeMn HEAs (Cantor alloy), as single FCC 
phase solid solution representative, have been widely studied by re -
searchers [ 7 – 9 ]. However, the low strength of the Cantor alloy severely 
limits its engineering applications, and thus improving its strength is a 
key to solve the problem. 
Alloying is an effective method to improve the strength of Cantor 
alloy. Melting and casting methods are often used to fabricate these 
HEAs. However, the melting and casting methods tend to produce some 
defects such as composition segregation, coarse structure et al., and thus 
deteriorates its mechanical properties. Therefore, how to further regu -
late the preparation method to achieve a homogeneous structure of the 
alloys is the key to further improve the mechanical properties of alloys. 
As a simple and promising alloy preparation method, powder metallurgy 
can effectively inhibit these defects and fabricate HEAs with uniform 
microstructure and excellent mechanical properties [ 10 , 11 ]. Further -
more, oscillation pressure sintering, a new forming process in powder 
metallurgy, can accelerate the reaction diffusion of alloy elements by 
continuously applying oscillation pressure during the sintering process. 
And it can effectively eliminate the residual pores inside the material, 
achieving higher relative density compared with the conventional sin -
tering method [ 12 – 15 ]. However, the method of oscillatory pressure 
sintering (OPS) to prepare HEAs is rarely reported. In addition, some 
previous work has found that the addition of Al about 9.1 at.% form 
Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) phase in Cantor alloy, effectively enhancing 
the mechanical properties of the HEAs [ 16 – 18 ]. 
In this work, in order to refine the microstructure of Cantor alloy and 
improve its mechanical properties, we introduced Al element to produce

2. Experiments and methods 
2.1. Sample preparation 
Gas-atomized CoCrNiFeMnAl
0.5 
HEA powders with an atomic ratio 
of 1:1:1:1:1:0.5 were prepared and their particle size distribution were 
less than 25 μ m, 15 – 45 μ m, 45 – 150 μ m, and greater than 150 μ m, 
respectively. The microstructure and composition of the powders are 
shown in Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 , respectively. Gas-atomized powders exhibit 
high sphericity and uniform particle size at different particle size. The 
powders were placed in a graphite mold with an inner diameter of 30 
mm. Graphite paper was placed between the mold and the powders to 
facilitate demolding. After 10 MPa pre-pressing, OPS was carried out in 
a vacuum environment (vacuum degree < 5 Pa). Then the temperature 
was increased to 1000

applied and an additional 5 MPa oscillation pressure was added in 
1000

with the size of Ф 30 mm × 6 mm was obtained. The specimens were 
marked by particle size as 25, 15 – 45, 45 – 150, 150 + , respectively. For 
comparison, the as-cast alloy was prepared by arc melting method with 
Co, Cr, Ni, Fe, Mn, Al metal blocks (purity of 99.9%) with the same 
atomic ratio as the as-sintered alloys. The ingot was repeatedly melted at 
least four times to ensure its chemical homogeneity. 
2.2. Microstructure and property characterization 
In order to observe the microstructure, the specimens were cut by 
DK77 EDM wire cutting machine. These specimens were cold inlaid with 
epoxy resin and then grounded with SiC sandpaper of different particles 
sizes. The scratches were removed by mechanical polishing machine to 
get a flat and smooth surface. The phase compositions of the specimens 
were analyzed by an X-ray diffractometer with a scan angle of 20 – 80
◦
and a scan rate of 5
◦

characterized using Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and the dis -
tributions of elements were observed and analyzed using the energy- 
dispersive spectrometry (EDS). The densities of sintered specimens 
were measured by Archimedean drainage method. The room tempera -
ture compression mechanical properties in two different directions of Ф 
5 mm × 8 mm cylindrical specimens were tested by a CMT5105GL type 
electronic universal tensile tester at 0.5 mm/min. The microhardness of 
the specimens was tested by using HVST-100Z type semi-automatic 
microhardness tester and each specimen was measured in five areas to 
obtain the average value with loading of 100 g.

4. Conclusions 
New CoCrNiFeMnAl
0.5 
HEA was designed and prepared by GA 
combined with OPS. The effects of different powder particle sizes on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of the as-sintered alloys were 
investigated. The following conclusions were obtained: 
(1) The as-sintered alloys prepared by powder metallurgy have finer 
grain size and more uniform microstructure than the as-cast alloy 
prepared by arc melting. 
(2) The mechanical properties of the as-sintered alloys were signifi -
cantly improved compared with the as-cast alloy. The yield 
strength and microhardness of the as-sintered alloys were 173% 
and 104% higher than that of the as-cast alloy, respectively. 
(3) The excellent mechanical properties of the as-sintered alloys 
originate from the second phase strengthening and the grain 
boundary strengthening.
