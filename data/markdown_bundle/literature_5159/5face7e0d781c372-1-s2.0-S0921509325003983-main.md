ABSTRACT
Ni-Co-based superalloys are recognized as promising materials for the turbine discs of next-generation aero- 
engines, but the internal strength-ductility trade-off limits their applications. Here, we present a dual hetero -
structured Ni-Co-based superalloy characterized by harmonic grain heterostructure comprising fine grains and 
ultrafine grains, which is accompanied by bimodal-sized γ ′ precipitates. The superalloy exhibits an outstanding 
strength-ductility synergy, with high yield strength (~1.5 GPa) and ultimate tensile strength (~1.8 GPa), con -
current with high uniform elongation (~21 %), which is much higher than its solution and aging counterparts 
and superior to most superalloys. The excellent tensile properties primarily originate from its distinctive het -
erostructure and work hardening mechanism. The inhomogeneous plastic deformation leads to a high density of 
geometrically necessary dislocations pile-up near the hetero-zone boundaries, yielding so-called hetero-defor -
mation-induced hardening. Besides, the bimodal-sized γ ′ precipitates effectively impede dislocations slip to 
improve work hardening capacity. Additionally, stacking faults, Lomer-Cottrell locks and twins also contributed 
to the strain hardening. These findings suggest that the dual heterostructure design strategy is promising to 
improve the strength-ductility synergy in Ni-Co-based alloys.
1. Introduction
Ni-Co-based superalloys exhibit attractive mechanical properties, e. 
g. high temperature strength, fatigue and creep resistance, therefore, 
they are regarded as promising materials for next-generation aero-en -
gine turbine discs [ 1 – 4 ]. However, the strength-ductility trade-off has 
been identified in most superalloys, which constrain their potential 
applications [ 5 – 8 ]. Refining grains can enhance strength based on the 
classical Hall-Petch relationship, and reduce stress concentration by 
allocating dislocations to other grains, thus improving the material ’ s 
toughness [ 9 , 10 ]. Therefore, numerous works have focused on prepar -
ing fine-grained materials with high strength and ductility by severe 
plastic deformation (SPD) in the past two decades [ 11 , 12 ]. For example, 
following friction stir processing to obtain a refined microstructure 
comprising fine grains and nano-twins, the GH4068 superalloy 
exhibited excellent tensile properties with a yield strength (YS) of 1290 
MPa and a uniform elongation (UE) of 18 % [ 13 ]. It has been proved that 
there exists an optimum grain size in the homogeneous microstructure 
that enables the best combination of strength and ductility [ 14 , 15 ]. If 
the grain size is too small (e.g. < 1 μ m), an excessive number of grain 
boundaries (GBs) will result in a notable reduction of space for dislo -
cation accumulation and thus a loss of ductility. The YS of nano-grained 
Ni-based superalloys produced by cold rolling can reach 2 GPa, but the 
UE is less than 3 % [ 16 ]. This implies that the simple grain refinement 
method may be inherently limited to achieve good strength-ductility 
synergy.
As a potential strategy to achieve high mechanical properties, het -
erogeneous microstructure design is proved to be efficient in over -
coming the trade-off between strength and ductility of Ni and Ni-Co- 
based alloys [ 17 ]. For instance, Ma et al. [ 18 ] prepared a gradient

Because of the co-existence of γ ′ particles and γ matrix, we employed 
TEM to further explore the interaction between dislocations and γ ′ 
precipitates in the HS sample, as detailed in Fig. 7 . The interaction be -
tween dislocations and γ ′ particles can be divided into two models: 
Orowan bypassing and the anti-phase boundary (APB) mechanism of 
dislocation pairs cutting, depending on the size of γ ′ precipitates [ 38 ]. 
On the one hand, it is difficult for dislocations to cut through large-size 
primary γ ′ phases, which will constrain dislocations in the vicinity of 
primary γ’ . Therefore, dislocations bypass the precipitates via the Oro -
wan mechanism, causing apparent dislocation accumulation near the 
primary γ ′ , as shown in Fig. 7 a. On the other hand, for the nanoscale 
secondary γ ′ particles, the APB mechanism of dislocation pair cutting is 
widely identified as the preferred mechanism of interaction between 
precipitates and dislocations [ 13 , 39 ]. Here, we analyze how nanoscale γ ′ 
precipitates interact with dislocations by high-resolution TEM 
(HRTEM), as shown in Fig. 7 c – f. Fig. 7 c presents a γ ′ -rich area, as 
demonstrated by the fast Fourier transform (FFT) pattern in Fig. 7 d. 
High density of dislocations was found in the corresponding inverse fast 
Fourier transform (IFFT) map ( Fig. 7 e). It is seen that lots of dislocations 
accumulate at the boundary and some dislocations have slipped into the 
γ ′ nanoparticle. Geometric phase analysis (GPA) was conducted to 
obtain the horizontal normal strain map ( Fig. 7 f), which is overlapped 
with IFFT images using Bragg filter to observe precipitate-dislocation 
interactions. There are many high strain fields not only in the matrix 
but also in γ ′ nanoparticles, which should be produced by dislocations 
cutting through the secondary γ ′ nanoparticles.
Furthermore, during the plastic deformation, high density of 
stacking fault (SF) networks ( Fig. 7 g) accompanied by a number of 
Lomer-Cottrell (L-C) locks ( Fig. 7 h) are formed due to the reduction of 
SFE by a high content of Co. It is known that SFs and L-C locks are strong 
obstacles for impeding and accumulating dislocations [ 40 ]. Contrary to 
expectations, just a small number of deformation twins (DTs) are 
observed ( Fig. 7 i). This phenomenon is also found in a recent study on a 
novel Ni-based superalloy [ 28 ]. A possible explanation for the lack of 
DTs is that the large amount of nanoscale secondary γ ′ particles mark -
edly augments the SFE in the γ ′ -rich area, thereby producing intersecting 
SFs instead of DTs during deformation. In brief, the secondary γ ′ nano -
particles constrain the formation of DTs [ 31 ]. SFs may be less effective 
than DTs in blocking dislocations. However, a substantial quantity of SFs 
is still able to impede dislocations and thus enhance work hardening.

4. Discussion
4.1. Superior strength-ductility synergy of HS sample
The product of UTS*TE of the studied superalloys are compared with 
other Ni and Ni-Co-based alloys, as shown in Fig. 9 . In order to ensure 
data consistency, the gauge lengths of samples surveyed in the literature 
are all larger than 1 mm. This is because miniature samples are more 
susceptible to size effect and the measurement error will be larger [ 41 ]. 
As illustrated in Fig. 9 , the strength-ductility synergy of the studied 
superalloy together with heterostructures and precipitates far surpasses 
other microstructural design. This strategy is also applicable to other Ni 
and Ni-Co-based alloys, extending the boundaries of the Ashby map into 
previously unchartered field.
4.2. Strain partitioning and HDI hardening
Following the intuitive understanding of the exceptional strength- 
ductility synergy of the HS sample offered by Fig. 9 , the underlying 
mechanisms for the simultaneous improvement of the strength and 
ductility in the HS sample can be discussed. Because of the mechanical 
incompatibility of soft/hard zones, strain partitioning is widely 
observed in HS materials during plastic deformation [ 23 , 27 , 56 ]. Here, 
we investigated the strain partitioning in the HS sample by nano -
indentation mapping tests at 0 and 10 % tensile strain, as visualized in 
Fig. 10 . Upon the figure, we find that the hardness of both soft and hard 
zones increased after tensile deformation. It is safe to determine that the 
soft FG domains were hardened as well as hard UFG domains. This 
observation is identical to the experimental results of a 
three-dimensional (3D) core – shell nanostructured Cu [ 57 ].

5. Conclusion
In this study, conventional rolling and heat treatments are employed 
to produce a dual HS Ni-Co-based superalloy. The microstructure, me -
chanical properties, and deformation mechanisms are systematically 
investigated. The main conclusions are summarized as follows. 
(1) A unique dual heterostructure consisting of harmonic grain 
structure (with fine grains and ultrafine grains) and bimodal- 
sized γ ′ precipitates is produced. The ultrafine grains are 
diffusely distributed around fine grains. Both fine and ultrafine 
grains are fully recrystallized, exhibiting a low dislocation den -
sity and a considerable number of annealed twins. The distribu -
tion of L1
2
-Ni
3
(Al, Ti) γ ′ precipitates is not uniform. The primary 
γ ′ phases are concentrated inside the ultrafine grains, while the 
fine nanoscale secondary γ ′ precipitates are dispersed within the 
material.
(2) The HS sample shows both high strength and high ductility. The 
YS and UTS are 1540 MPa and 1870 MPa, respectively, accom -
panied with UE of 21 %, all of which are superior than the 
samples in ST and ST-aging states. Such strength-ductility syn -
ergy far surpasses other conventional superalloys.
(3) The superior tensile properties of the HS sample should be 
attributed to multiple deformation mechanisms. Firstly, the har -
monic grain structure provides strong HDI strengthening and 
hardening by producing high density of GNDs near zone in -
terfaces. Secondly, the presence of bimodal-sized γ ′ precipitates 
intensify the interactions between dislocations and precipitates. 
Thirdly, SFs and L-C locks hinder dislocations movements, 
enhancing dislocations density furthermore. All these mecha -
nisms contribute to both high strength and high strain hardening 
capability.
