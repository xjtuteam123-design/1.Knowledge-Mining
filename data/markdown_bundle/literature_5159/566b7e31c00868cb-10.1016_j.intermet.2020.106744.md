ABSTRACT 
High-entropy alloys with a superalloy-like microstructure show excellent mechanical properties at elevated 
temperatures. In this paper, a multi-phase high-entropy alloy CrMnFeCoNiAl
0.75 
was developed. Based on the 
microstructure characterization by XRD, SEM, TEM and 3D-APT, it was found that the alloy was composed of 
FCC phase, BCC phase, and B2 phase. The mechanical properties evaluation showed that the alloy had high 
compression yield strength of 1194 MPa at room temperature and could preserve high yield strength of 1070 
MPa at 500 
�
C. Further increase in the temperature beyond 600 
�
C resulted in alloy softening. The alloy showed 
limited strain of 2.72% at room temperature, but it exhibited adequate deformability at elevated temperatures up 
to 500 
�
C (strain > 20%). The strengthening mechanisms of the alloy at different temperatures were discussed. 
1. Introduction 
In the last decade, with the development of material-design concept, 
high-entropy alloys (HEAs) [ 1 – 5 ] have attracted considerable attention. 
HEAs with high mixing entropies contained at least five principal ele -
ments and showed promising properties for a wide range of applications. 
For example, the combination of excellent thermal stability, 
high-temperature strength, and ductility of HEAs makes the alloys good 
candidates for high-temperature applications [ 6 , 7 ]. According to the 
previous studies [ 8 – 12 ], phase-structure types are the dominant factor 
in determining the mechanical properties. It is known that the 
face-centered-cubic (FCC) structure has slip planes with the closest 
packing, while the body-centered-cubic (BCC) structure has no 
close-packed slip planes. The critical stress for slip in the FCC structure is 
basically smaller than that in the BCC structure. Thus, HEAs with a BCC 
structure usually exhibit high yield strength and limited ductility [ 13 ], 
while the FCC-structured HEAs have relatively low yield strength and 
high ductility [ 14 ]. Hence, an alloy consisting of BCC and FCC phases is 
expected to possess high strength and good ductility [ 15 , 16 ]. As re -
ported by Tang et al. [ 17 ], the compression yield strength of a FCC phase 
structured alloy Al
0.5
CoCrCuFeNi was low, but the ductility of the alloy 
was good. On the other hand, a dual-phase FCC þ BCC structured 
AlCoCrCuFeNi alloy showed high compression yield strength at 600 
�
C 
( > 600 MPa), which was even higher than the yield strength of refractory 
high-entropy alloy NbMoTaW at the same temperature [ 18 ]. Hence, the 
FCC þ BCC dual-phase structured HEAs may possess high strength and 
good ductility, if the volume fraction ratio of FCC to BCC was proper. 
Some efforts have been made to achieve the dual-phase structured 
HEAs. Recently, the Al
0.7
CoCrFe
2
Ni alloy with a superalloy-like micro -
structure had been developed [ 19 ]. It was found that the cuboidal B2 
(ordered BCC) precipitate coherently embedded in the BCC matrix, 
which was very similar to the microstructure of conventional Ni-base 
superalloys. During the deformation process, the dislocations mainly 
moved in the BCC channel, and the B2 precipitate effectively impede the 
motion of dislocation. Hence, the alloy showed both high strength and 
good ductility at elevated temperatures. By changing the alloy compo -
sition, it is also possible to obtain eutectic HEAs with a dual-phase 
structure [ 20 – 22 ]. It was found that AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
alloy possessed a 
fine lamellar FCC/B2 microstructure. Eutectic HEAs showed an un -
precedented combination of high tensile ductility and fracture strength 
at room temperature, due to the formation of a mixed microstructure of 
alternating soft FCC and hard BCC phases [ 21 , 22 ].

3.2. Chemical compositions of the phases and element distribution 
Chemical compositions of the FCC, BCC and B2 phases analyzed by 
TEM-EDS were listed in Table 1 . It could be seen that FCC phase was 
composed of Cr, Fe, Co, Mn, and Ni. BCC phase had a composition (in 
atomic percent, at.%) of 37.42 Cr, 28.37 Fe, 16.94 Co, 13.86 Mn, 2.20 
Ni, and 1.18 Al. The content of Cr (3.2 at.%) and Fe (7.92 at.%) was low 
while the content of Ni (36.17 at.%) and Al (20.35 at.%) was high in B2 
phase. Therefore, the BCC phase was a Cr – Fe rich phase while the B2 
phase was a Ni – Al rich phase. The addition of Al into the single phase 
CrMnFeCoNi was expected to introduce a large lattice distortion, and to 
promote the formation of FCC þ BCC/B2 multi-phase structure [ 13 ]. As 
the mixing enthalpy between Al and Ni is the most negative, as 
compared to those between other constituent elements ( Table 2 [ 32 ]), it 
is favorable to the combination of Al and Ni during the alloy solidifi -
cation. The formation of NiAl-rich phase in the HEAs containing Ni and 
Al were often observed in the literature [ 19 , 21 , 23 , 29 , 30 , 33 ]. 
3D-APT was used to investigate the element distribution and the 
chemical composition in details for the eutectic-like region shown in 
Fig. 2 (b). The elemental maps with different colors of the overall and 
individual atoms were shown in Fig. 3 (a), indicated clearly that the 
distribution of constituent elements was non-uniform. Fe and Cr, Ni and 
Al elements could be divided into two regions based on the enrichment 
of constituent elements, whereas the distribution of Co and Mn was 
relatively homogeneous in two regions. A concentration-depth profile 
was plotted using a cylinder of a 10 � 10 nm
2

central line ( Fig. 3 (b)) through the analyzed volume. It is obvious from 
the concentration-depth profile ( Fig. 3 (c)) that the region on the top left 
corner (B2) was rich in Ni (~40 at.%) and Al (~20 at.%), suggesting that 
the B2 phase was a NiAl-rich phase, and the BCC phase was mainly 
enriched in Fe (~30 at.%) and Cr (~35 at.%). The 3D-APT result was 
consistent with the TEM-EDS result.
