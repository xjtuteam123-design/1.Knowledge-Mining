ABSTRACT
The hot deformation behavior of Ti-B25 high-strength titanium alloy, primarily used in naval antenna tubes, was 
systematically investigated through stress-strain analysis and microstructure characterization using Electron 
Backscatter Diffraction (EBSD). The results demonstrate the occurrence of dynamic phase transition (DPT) and 
dynamic recrystallization (DRX) under different deformation conditions. In the α + β phase region, the DPT 
mechanisms, including α -phase interfacial penetration and volume contraction, lead to microstructural refine -
ment, while DRX in the β phase region primarily governs flow softening. The deformation temperature and strain 
rate significantly influence the microstructural evolution, with higher temperatures promoting continuous dy -
namic recrystallization (CDRX). Optimal hot rolling parameters, determined through thermal simulations, 
confirmed superior mechanical performance, achieving 1435 MPa tensile strength and 10.7 % elongation at 900 
◦
C. These findings provide valuable insights for the processing of Ti-B25 alloy in marine applications.
1. Introduction
Titanium alloy is gradually becoming the main structural material 
for marine engineering equipment due to its excellent mechanical 
properties, unique physical properties (including magnetic properties), 
high specific strength, and excellent corrosion resistance [1 – 4] . Tradi -
tional naval tubing mostly uses two-phase titanium alloys, with factors 
such as long processing cycles and high processing costs limiting its 
large-scale application. High-strength β titanium alloys (HS-TAs), which 
have high strength and good formability, are now receiving wider 
attention. The dynamic phase transition (DPT), dynamic recovery 
(DRV), and dynamic recrystallization (DRX) of HS-TAs during thermo -
mechanical processing (TMP) directly affect the microstructural evolu -
tion and hence the properties [5 – 8] . Therefore, improving the properties 
by clarifying the hot deformation mechanism of HS-TAs has become a 
hot research topic nowadays.
DRX is an important process in hot deformation, which can improve 
the plasticity and workability of the alloy [9 – 11] . Recent studies have 
shown multiple mechanisms of DRX occur in many metastable β tita -
nium alloys during hot deformation. Zhang et al. [12] investigated the 
influence of strain rate on the high-temperature deformation behavior 
and mechanism of Ti-6554 alloy. They found that the mechanism of 
continuous dynamic recrystallization (CDRX) gradually migrated from 
the grain interior to the grain boundaries with the increase of strain rate, 
and the DRX mechanism at high strain rate is closely related to the 
dislocation density distribution. Lu et al. [13] found a sudden increase in 
the orientation difference from the grain interior to the boundary when 
Ti-35421 alloy was deformed at different strain rates in the β phase 
region, suggesting that CDRX following progressive lattice rotation is 
the main structural evolution. Zhang et al. [14] found the deformation 
behavior of Ti-6Al-2Sn-4Zr-6Mo alloy is mainly controlled by the 
deformation rate. The strain-induced grain boundary migration leads to 
grain boundary expansion at low deformation rates, the sub-grain 
rotation transforms from low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) to 
high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs) at high deformation rates. Lu et al. 
[15] studied the hot deformation and DRX of Ti-3Al-5Mo-4Cr-2Zr-1Fe 
alloy in the α + β phase region and showed that both CDRX and 
discontinuous dynamic recrystallization (DDRX) occurred at high true 
strains and the number of DRX grains increased with increasing true 
strain. Significant flow softening occurs at low temperatures and strain 
rates due to the bending and fragmentation of the α phase, the inverse 
change in the weave strength between the α and β phases, and DRX.
In recent years, many scholars have found that when the titanium 
alloy is deformed in the α + β phase region, there is also a dynamic phase

deformation conditions in detail by hot compression tests, hot rolling, 
and EBSD. The research results can provide useful guidance for accu -

4. Discussion
4.1. DPT phenomenon
4.1.1. Driving force of DPT
The DPT phenomenon in titanium alloys was first noted during the 
study of their superplastic deformation, which only occurs in titanium 
alloys with mixed α and β initial microstructure when deformed in the 
α + β phase region [47 – 49] . In Ti-B25 alloy, Al is the α -phase stabilized 
element, and the increase of its content helps to form and stabilize α 
phase. Correspondingly, the elevated content of Mo and Fe as the 
β -phase stabilized elements can promote the formation of β phase.
By observing the changes in the distribution of elements after 
compression with different deformations, it was found that the DPT is 
mainly related to the diffusion of elements. Fig. 9 is the distribution of 
the elements of Al, Mo, and Fe in samples with different reductions 
obtained by using the EPMA surface scanning at 800 
◦
C. the mass 
fraction of Al in α phase of the compressed samples is low compared with 
that of the uncompressed samples, on the contrary, the mass fractions of 
Mo and Fe in α phase have been increased, and the area of β phase has 
been increased as well. The variation of solute elements with increasing 
deformation suggests that compressive deformation promotes DPT by 
regulating the diffusion of elements between the α and β phases. The 
diffusion of Mo element in the titanium alloy is much more difficult 
compared to Fe element [50] , but Fig. 9 (e)-(f) shows that the diffusion of 
Mo element after 40 % compression changes more obviously, and its 
content in α phase increases, while in β phase decreases. Combined with 
the above changes in the content of elements, it shows that many defects 
occur at the phase interface between the α and β phases during defor -
mation, providing many diffusion channels for elemental diffusion.
The decrease in the volume fraction of the α phase with increasing 
deformation temperature in Fig. 4 (b) is because the higher temperature 
allows more elemental diffusion, which induces more α phase to trans -
form into β phase. The deformation amount and strain rate can also have 
an important effect on the DPT process except the deformation tem -
perature. The transition from α to β belongs to the diffusion phase 
transition which is mainly determined by the diffusion difficulty and the 
length of deformation time. As shown in Fig. 10 (a), the content of the α 
phase decreases as the deformation amount increases. On the one hand, 
the increase in the time that the specimen is in the deformation state is 
conducive to the long-range diffusion of solute elements. On the other 
hand, the increasing defects of the structure can also promote diffusion 
[51,52] .
The strain rate mainly affects the diffusion of elements by influencing 
the deformation time. Guo et al. [24] studied TC4 alloys and found that 
the promoting effect of compression gradually decreases with the 
decrease of strain rate. However, the α phase distribution increases 
significantly with increasing strain rate as shown in Fig. 10 (b). That 
means high strain rates are unfavorable for DPT in the Ti-B25 alloy. 
Although a high strain rate produces more deformation energy and 
defect density, the short deformation time results in insufficient element 
diffusion due to the high content of solute elements and the difficulty of 
diffusion.

deformation of neighboring grains, which will continuously absorb 
dislocations to make LAGBs develop into HAGBs and then further form 
the DDRX grains. In general, DDRX is more likely to be completed 
compared to CDRX, which requires sufficient deformation storage en -
