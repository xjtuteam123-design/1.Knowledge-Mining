Intrinsic Stage I Crack Growth of Directionally 
Solidified Ni-Base Superalloys during 
Low-Cycle Fatigue at Elevated Temperature 
MASAKAZU OKAZAKI, TETSUO TABATA, and SHINICHIROH NOHMI 
Strain-controlled low-cycle fatigue tests of cylindrical smooth specimens of two kinds of 
directionally solidified Ni-base superalloys, RENE 80+Hf and CM 247LC, were carried out at 
a temperature of 873 K, and the successive process from the crack initiation to small crack 
propagation was investigated by employing a replication technique. Both materials exhibited 
typical features of stage I fatigue fracture; that is, the fracture occurred on the crystallographic 
{111} planes, the most important slip planes in face-centered cubic (fcc) materials. It was found 
that the rate of stage I crack growth, when not influenced by a nearby grain boundary, pro- 
portionally increased with the crack length. However, as the crack tip neared a grain boundary, 
the rate rapidly decreased. It was also shown that the crack growth rate fell when the crack 
deflection occurred due to secondary slip. Comparison was also made between the stage I crack 
growth rate and the long crack growth rate in polycrystalline Ni-base superalloys.

B. Small Fatigue Crack Growth 
A typical example of the fatigue crack propagation on 
the specimen surface of CM247LC-DS with strain cy- 
cling, N, is precisely illustrated in Figure 7(a), where the

of crack tip does not change in spite of the increase of 
N corresponds to such a phenomenon). The detail of crack 
initiation and the growth behavior near grain boundary 
will be discussed later. 
As already described, because the crack propagated on 
a crystallographic plane which was inclined to the load- 
ing axis, the crack length was represented by the length 
measured along the crack growth path. Some crack growth 
curves are plotted in Figure 8. The relationships between 
the crack growth rate and the half crack length are given 
in Figures 9 through 11, where the mark of ~ on the 
abscissa indicates that the crack growth rate was greatly 
reduced. On the lower sides of these figures, the cor- 
responding crack morphologies on the specimen surface 
were also illustrated precisely. The notations of "GB" 
in these illustrations represent the grain boundaries. 
Figure 9 corresponds to the crack in Figure 7. From these 
figures, it is found that the crack growth rate increased 
proportionally with the half crack length when the crack 
tip was far apart from, or was not influenced by, nearby 
grain boundaries. The above proportionality will be clearer 
later (Figure 12). However, the rate drastically de- 
creased as the crack approached a grain boundary. Note 
that such a decrease in crack growth rate can be found 
at the point before, and not exactly when, the crack tip 
reached the grain boundary. The phenomenon that the 
slip deformation ahead of crack is blocked by grain 
boundary, which is caused by orientation mismatch be- 
tween neighboring grains, seems to be responsible for 
the above crack growth behavior, f11,12,131 Based on the 
model that the slip deformation ahead of small crack is 
blocked by grain boundary, Tanaka and Akiniwa t~2,13) 
theoretically discussed the small fatigue crack growth near 
grain boundary, assuming that the crack growth rate is 
proportional to crack tip sliding displacement. Accord- 
ing to their theoretical calculation, the small crack growth 
rate proportionally increases with crack length when the 
crack tip is far from a grain boundary, and it drops rap- 
idly when the plastic zone reaches a grain boundary. Al- 
though quantitative data on stage I fatigue crack growth 
are scarce, Okazaki and Yamahira reported that the stage I 
fatigue crack growth rate in the directionally solidified 
aluminum alloy increased proportionally with the crack 
length, in addition to specific behavior near the grain 
boundaryJ TM The crack growth behavior shown in 
Figures 9 through 1 1 is quite analogous to the charac- 
teristics in the above reports. [12'13'151 
Not only near the grain boundary, the crack growth

the change of crack propagation plane due to secondary

IV. CONCLUSIONS 
Strain-controlled low-cycle fatigue tests of cylindrical 
smooth specimens of two kinds of directionally solidi- 
fied Ni-base superalloys, RENI~ 80+Hf and CM 247LC 
(denoted by RENE80H-DS and CM247LC-DS, respec- 
tively), were carried out at a temperature of 873 K, and 
the intrinsic small fatigue crack growth behavior was 
investigated by employing an elaborate replication 
technique. 
The main conclusions introduced are as follows: 
1. Both materials exhibited typical features of stage I 
fatigue fracture; that is, the fracture occurred on 
the crystallographic {111} planes, the most important 
slip planes in fcc materials. In addition, the fatigue 
strength of CM247LC-DS was higher than that of 
RENE80H-DS. 
2. The rate of stage I crack growth, when not influenced 
by a nearby grain boundary, proportionally increased 
with the crack length. However, as the crack tip neared 
a grain boundary, the rate rapidly decreased. In some 
cases when the crack plane deflected or the crack 
switched to propagate from one {111} plane to an- 
other, a decrease of the crack growth rate was also 
found. 
3. The stage I fatigue crack growth rates in both ma- 
terials were apt to grow even at a lower stress inten- 
sity factor range than the long crack threshold, Agth , 
in the polycrystalline alloys. The stage I crack growth 
rate below AKth is located where the stage II or Paris 
law portion of the long crack growth curve for poly- 
crystalline alloys was extrapolated. 
4. Three types of crack initiation processes were ob- 
served: the first is due to cracks emanating from 
micropores; the second is due to cracks initiating from 
the slip plane itself; and the last is due to cracks 
from the carbide precipitation at grain boundary. 
5. The crack growth rate of RENE80H-DS was higher 
than that of CM247LC-DS. In addition, the crack ini- 
tiation life of CM247LC-DS was longer than that of 
RENI~80H-DS. Hence, the differences in crack ini- 
tiation and propagation lives between both materials 
resulted in that of the fatigue life shown in (1).
