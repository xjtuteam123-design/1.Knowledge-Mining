ABSTRACT 
The influence of boron addition on oxidation behaviors of high-Nb-containing TiAl alloys was investigated using 
isothermal oxidation tests at 900 
◦
C in air. The results indicated that boron addition refined the lamellar colony 
structure and significantly improved the high-temperature oxidation resistance. Fine lamellar colonies were 
conducive to reducing thermal stress during oxidation, which delayed the cracking of the oxide scale. Further -
more, the generated oxide implants intensified the adhesive strength between the oxide scale and substrate, 
further enhancing the oxide-scale spallation resistance. In summary, the superior adhesion of the oxide scale 
resulted in a better oxidation resistance of the boron-containing alloy. 
1. Introduction 
High-Nb-containing TiAl alloys have excellent high-temperature 
mechanical properties with low densities, and they are regarded as the 
best candidate materials for the turbine blades used in gas turbines and 
aircraft engines [ 1 – 5 ]. However, the poor oxidation resistances at high 
temperatures (up to 900 
◦
C) impede the further application of these al -
loys [ 6 , 7 ]. Many efforts have been made to overcome this challenge, 
such as alloying and surface modification [ 8 , 9 ]. From the perspective of 
cost control, alloying is the most extensively used and basic method. 
The effect of alloying on the oxidation resistance of TiAl-based alloys 
has been widely studied, and the functions of elements can be divided 
into two categories [ 10 , 11 ]. On one hand, some elements, such as Nb, 
Mo, W, can improve the oxidation resistance by suppressing the growth 
of the undesirable TiO
2 
layer [ 12 ]. On the other hand, other elements, 
including V, Cr, and Mn, can accelerate the oxygen diffusion and hinder 
the formation of a protective oxide layer, which are both harmful to 
oxidation resistance [ 13 , 14 ]. Boron has been regarded as an indis -
pensable alloying element for high-Nb-containing TiAl alloys [ 15 ]. 
However, its effect on the oxidation behavior is still unclear. 
It has been reported that adding some amount of boron (B) in high- 
Nb-containing TiAl alloys can refine the grain size [ 16 – 18 ] and improve 
the mechanical properties [ 19 – 25 ]. Several studies [ 26 – 32 ] have 
explored the effects of the grain size on the high-temperature oxidation 
behaviors of alloys, for example, Fe – Mn – Al – C steels [ 28 ], nanocrystal -
line SS304 [ 29 ], and Ni-based alloys [ 30 ], contributing to the 
improvement of the oxidation resistance by enhancing the oxide scale 
spallation resistance and the diffusivity of Cr. In contrast, the Cr – Mo 
steels with finer grain sizes exhibited higher oxidation rates and less 
spallation resistance [ 31 , 32 ]. Accordingly, the oxide-scale spallation 
resistance is related to the grain size of the alloy, but the mechanism is 
not fully understood. This is still an open question, especially for 
high-Nb-containing TiAl alloys. 
Therefore, the aim of this study was to tailor the oxidation resistance 
of a high-Nb-containing TiAl alloy by grain refinement through boron 
addition. With this objective, the grain size, micro-segregation and high- 
temperature (900 
◦
C) oxidation behaviors of Ti45Al8Nb (at.%) alloys 
with and without boron were investigated. In particular, the role of the 
grain size in the high-temperature oxidation behaviors of the high-Nb- 
containing TiAl alloys is discussed.

3.1. Microstructures of alloys 
Fig. 2 a and b shows the back-scattered electron (BSE) SEM micro -
graphs of the as-cast alloys before oxidation. Both the alloys exhibited 
typical as-cast dendrite structures, but their microstructures differed 
significantly. For the 0B alloy, the microstructures were composed of a 
dendritic matrix (grey) and two types of micro-segregation regions. The 
EPMA analysis results of the compositions in different regions are listed 
in Table 1 . Based on the EPMA results, the black regions in the inter-

the β -segregation almost disappeared. Additionally, borides with needle-

the colony size was reduced from ~400 μ m to ~70 μ m, suggesting that 
the 0.5 at.% addition of boron could refine the grains remarkably.

oxidation. Based on the XRD results, the main constituent phases of both

4. Discussion 
4.1. Effect of boron on the microstructure 
Based on the microstructure analysis in Section 3.1 , boron addition 
not only greatly refined the lamellar colony size but also reduced the 
micro-segregation significantly. According to the quasi-phase diagram 
of Ti – Al containing Nb (8 at.%) [ 34 ], the solidification path of the alloys 
in this study was β -solidification. The refinement of the primary β by 
boron addition can be explained by constitutional undercooling. A 
previous study [ 35 ] found that constitutional undercooling criterion is 
as follows:

solute distribution coefficient. As an interstitial solute, the solubility of 
boron in all kinds of TiAl phases was restricted significantly due to its 
relatively large atomic size compared with those of other interstitial 
solutes. Thus, a large quantity of boron solutes were rejected into the 
liquid because the solubility of boron in the solid and liquid phase 
differed remarkably. Under the condition of non-equilibrium solidifi -
cation, the solute does not diffuse adequately in the liquid, resulting in a 
constant accumulation of the built-up layer [ 36 ]. Furthermore, the 
diffusion coefficient of the solute Al decreased in the regions richer in 
boron [ 18 ]. Thus, with a 0.5 at.% boron addition, a constitution 
undercooled zone was present at the solidification front, which provided 
an additional nuclear driving force for β dendrites, resulting in finer 
dendrites. With further solidification, eutectic borides precipitated in 
the solidification front and finally stacked in the inter-dendritic regions, 
suppressing the dendrite growth. The boride-containing β phases pro -
vided additional nucleation sites for the α phase, producing a refined 
microstructure. As is well-known, micro-segregation occurs due to the 
element redistribution process during solidification and solid-state 
phase transformations. Grain refinement provides more diffusion 
channels and a shorter diffusion distance, reducing the extent of element 
redistribution. Therefore, the micro-segregation extent of the 0.5B alloy 
was reduced compared with that of the 0B alloy. 
4.2. Correlation between microstructure and oxidation behavior 
As mentioned above in Section 3.2 and 3.3 , the alloy containing 
boron exhibited superior oxidation resistance, which was attributed to 
the better adhesion of the oxide scale. In the initial stage of oxidation, 
the rate of mass increase of the 0.5B alloy with finer lamellar colonies 
was faster than that of the 0B alloy. Corresponding to this phenomenon, 
two significant contributions to the oxidation reactions on the surfaces 
of the alloys were made by the grain refinement. On one hand, the 
refinement of grains provided abundant grain boundaries as the nucle -
ation sites for oxides, promoting the occurrence of oxidation reactions.

On the other hand, the diffusivity of the solute atoms was enhanced due 
to the short-circuit diffusion along the grain boundaries [ 26 ]. Thus, the 
much finer and denser oxide scale formed on the surface of 0.5B alloy 
after oxidation for 8 h. 
As the oxidation time increased, the rate of mass increase of the 0.5B 
alloy with finer lamellar colonies became lower than that of the 0B alloy 
due to the stronger adhesion of the oxide scale. The formation and relief 
of the stress in the oxide scale are the key determinants affecting the 
oxide scale adhesion. On the surface of the alloy, the level of thermal 
stress in the oxide scale was proportional to the difference in the thermal 
expansion coefficients between the oxide scale and the metallic sub -
strate, and the thermal expansion coefficient of oxide scale formed on 
the finer grain alloy was larger than that on the coarser grain alloy [ 26 ]. 
Therefore, the difference in the thermal expansion coefficients between 
the oxide scale and substrate of the 0.5B alloy was less than that of the 
0B alloy, resulting in a low level of thermal stress formed on the 0.5B 
alloy. 
In addition, the stress relief is contributed to maintaining the oxide 
scale integrity without cracking and spallation. Stress relief is a process 
of plastic deformation that occurred in the oxide scale and the substrate. 
Thus, the superior plastic deformation of ability of the oxide scale and 
substrate was beneficial for relieving the stress in the oxide scale. Ac -
cording to a previous study [ 26 ], a smaller grain size result in better 
plastic deformation ability. Thus, the stress that formed in the oxide 
scale of the 0.5B alloy was much easier to relieve during oxidation than 
that of the 0B alloy, which maintained a low level of thermal stress and 
reduced the risk of oxide scale cracking. The result presented above 
showed there was no cracking in the oxide scale formed on the 0.5B 
alloy, while cracking was evident on the 0B alloy ( Fig. 7 ). 
Moreover, the resistance of spallation had a critical influence on the 
oxidation behavior, which was related to the adhesive strength of the 
oxide scale. Oxide pegs generated near the interface are regarded as 
anchors to enhance the adhesive strength between the oxide scale and 
substrate. Given the fast diffusion of solute atoms along the grain 
boundaries, upon decreasing the grain size, the density of the grain 
boundary on the alloy surface increased, which was beneficial for the 
formation of pegs in these areas. Thus, the adhesive strength between 
the oxide scale and substrate was inversely proportional to the grain 
size. As shown in Fig. 9 , many peg-like oxides formed near the boundary 
of the oxide scale and substrate in the 0.5B alloy, while no spallation 
occurred on the surface of the 0.5B alloy during oxidation. 
Based on the discussion presented above, the effect of the grain size 
on the oxidation behaviors of the alloys is shown schematically in 
Fig. 10 . For the 0B alloy with larger lamellar colonies, cracking and 
spallation occurred due to the higher level of thermal stress, which gave 
rise to further oxidation. In contrast, the 0.5B alloy with finer lamellar 
colonies exhibited outstanding oxidation resistance and oxide scale 
adhesion due to the low level of thermal stress and the formation of 
oxide pegs nearby the interface of the oxide scale and substrate. 
5. Conclusions 
In this study, the influence of boron addition on the microstructure 
and oxidation behaviors of Ti45Al8Nb alloys at 900 
◦
C was investigated. 
The conclusions were as follows: 
(1) Boron addition reduced the micro-segregation and refined the 
full-lamellar microstructure of the Ti45Al8Nb alloy. With 0.5 at. 
% boron addition, the lamellar colony size was reduced from 
~400 μ m to ~70 μ m, and the β -segregation almost disappeared. 
(2) Boron addition significantly improved the high-temperature 
oxidation resistance of the Ti45Al8Nb alloy by enhancing the 
spallation resistance of the oxide scale. 
(3) Grain refinement enhanced the spallation resistance of the oxide 
scale in two aspects: (1) grain refinement reduced the thermal 
stress in the oxide scale, delaying the cracking of the oxide scale
