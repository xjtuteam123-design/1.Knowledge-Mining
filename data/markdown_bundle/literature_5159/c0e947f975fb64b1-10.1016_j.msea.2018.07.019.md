Abstract 
The effect of boron addition on the microstructure evolution and high-temperature properties of 
Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr alloys has been studied in this paper. With the boron content increasing from 
0at.% to 1.0at.%, the ultimate tensile strength(UTS) at high temperature increase dramatically while 
the alloys with 1.0at%B exhibit UTS as 656.12±20.54MPa at 800°C, 642.43±14.44MPa at 850°C 
and 508.44 ±16.12MPa at 900 ºC, respectively. Meanwhile, boron addition could stabilize the 
high-temperature UTS of the alloys in 500-700MPa with the temperature incresasing. Three types of 
strengthening mechanism in high-temperature strength caused by boron addition are also discussed 
and concluded. 
 
Keywords: TiAl alloys; High-temperature properties; Microstructure evolution; Boron addition; 
Dislocations; Strengthening mechanism 
 
1. Introduction 
TiAl alloys have gained great interest for research on aerospace applications due to low density 
and high specific strength in recent years [1,2]. However, the insufficient high-temperature[3] strength 
decided the application and development of TiAl alloys. High-Nb TiAl alloys have been developed 
based on the traditional γ -TiAl alloys for this problem [4]. Boron addition was also an effective way 
for refinement and TiAl alloys strengthening [5,6]. The crystal structure and shape of the borides 
exhibited a large impact on the properties of TiAl alloys [7,8]. The reduction of boron concentration 
was attempted to avoid the long boride s precipitates which induced premature cracking[9]. When 
nucleation takes place in borides from the β at high temperature, the α nucleation on borides surfaces 
or the possible α result in the refinement[10]. 
Tian[11] et al found that the increased quantity and volume fraction of the grain boundaries could 
provide the high strength for forged Ti-44Al-8Nb-0.2W-0.2B-0.1Y alloys with fine block-like γ/α 2 
phases. Niu[12] et al forged the Ti-43Al-6Nb-1B alloys and found the broken borides could strengthen 
the alloys. The recent researches almost focus on the boron refinement for strengthening the TiAl

## Page 3

alloys at room temperature. Less researches reported the effect of boron addition on the 
high-temperature properties of novel Nb containing TiAl alloys . Therefore, the effect of boron 
addition on tensile properties and the microstructure evolution of Ti -43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr alloys at 
high temperatures are studied in this paper. 
Cheng[13]et al reported that more than 0.3 at% boron was needed to refine the grain size in 
Ti-44Al-8Nb while the derivatives and the compositional undercooling w ere operative. Hu[14] et al 
reported 1.0at%B made beta dendrites much finer in Ti-45Al-2Mn-2Nb-1B alloys. Kartavykh[15,16] et 
al thought that the boron content over 1 at.% could not cause further refinement. Therefore, the boron 
content of addition in this paper were designed as 0, 0,3, 0.6 and 1.0at.%. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Materials 
The β-solidify alloys were produced by means of induction skul l melting (ISM) and melted under 
an Ar atmosphere (pressure of ~ 800Pa) while the chemical composition were listed in Table.1 . The 
present materials used in this study contained Ti bar (99.99wt%), pure Al (99.99wt%), pure Cr 
(99.99wt%), Al-Nb alloys (Nb:52.4at%), Al-Mo (Mo:50.5at%) alloys and TiB2 powders (99.99wt%). 
The as-cast alloys were then was hot isostatic pressed (HIPed) at 1200°C for 4 h under a pressure of 
140MPa and aged at 900°C for 48 h. 
2.2 High-temperature tensile tests 
The tensile tests were applied in Instron -5500 testing machine at 800, 850, and 900 °C while the 
strain rate is 1×10 -3s-1. Tensile specimens with 2mm in thickness and 60mm in length were cut by 
electric discharge method (EDM) from the ingots. 
2.3 Microstructure observation 
The microstructures were observed on the polished and etched surface s by scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscope (TEM). Thin foils for TEM observation 
were prepared from 0.5mm thick slices and ground to about 100μm in thickness (3mm in diameter 
discs). Then the discs were punched from the slices and further thinned using a lon -thinning unit. 
Thin foils were examined in Talos F200X.

3.2 High temperature tensile properties 
The tensile properties curves and the properties comparison of Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr-xB(x=0, 0.3, 
0.6 and 1.0at.%) alloys at 800°C, 850°C and 900°C are exhibited in Fig.4 and Table.1. In Fig.4(a-c), it 
can be found that the ultimate tensile strength(UTS) of alloys increase with boron content increasing. 
In Fig.4(d), the comparison of high -temperature tensile properties are listed. The high-temperature 
elongation are reduced after 0.3at.% boron addition while the elongation increase with boron content 
increasing from 0.3at.% to 1.0at.%. The brittle-ductile transition temperature (BDTT) of T1 stay in 
the range of 800 -850°C which could be ensured in Fig4(a -c). Boron addition would enhance the 
BDTT of the alloys in the range of 850-900°C and cause the brittle fracture and decreased elongation 
of T2, T3 and T4 alloys below 850°C. However, a much larger elongation of T4 appear at 900 °C 
which would be analyzed in section 3.5. 
About the increased high -temperature UTS after boron addition , two phenomenon could be 
observed as follow: (i) From above experimental results, boron addition would refine the colonies 
and increase the length and fraction of colonies boundaries. The colonies boundaries are the weak 
position at high temperatures. Longer and more boundaries means more weak positi ons for cracks 
formation. Based on this claim , boron addition would worsen the high -temperature properties of 
alloys. However, the alloys were strengthened by boron addition at high temperature s. (ii) It is all 
known that the increased temperatures reduce the strength and enhances the elongation of alloys in 
the high-temperature tensile tests. However, the UTS of T1 alloys without boron addition decreased 
dramatically, while the UTS of T2, T3 and T4 alloys with boron addition decreased slowly with 
temperature increasing. The distances of tensile curves between alloys with boron addition and the 
alloys without boron addition increase with temperature increasing in Fig.4 (a-c). This phenomenon 
indicates that boron addition could stabilize the high-temperature UTS of alloys. The reason for these 
two phenomenon would be discussed by microstructure evolution in next section.

3.3.1 The weak boundaries 
In Fig.8(a), the crack propagation along the α2/γ lamellae indicates the first type of weak position. 
In Fig.8(c), the holes on the colonies boundaries could be observed clearly. The boundaries of (α2+γ) 
lamellar colonies with many dislocations represent high stacking fault energy. The colonies 
boundaries are the second type of weak positions at high temperatures while most of cracks sources 
form on the colonies boundaries. The cracks propagation would break the alloys. In Table.1, the 
(α2+γ) lamellar colonies refinement from boron addition would cause the increased fraction of 
boundaries in alloys. The longer and more tortuous boundaries would increase the weak positions to 
worsen the high-temperature properties. 
3.3.2The boron solid solution 
As the α stabilizer[26], boron could promote α formation and distribute in the interstitial lattice. The 
boron addition leads to boron atoms solid solute into the matrix alloys. The boron solid solution 
causes solid solution strengthen to strengthen α and γ phase. The α2/γ lamellae strengthened by boron 
addition enhance the high-temperatures strength. 
3.3.3 The (α2+γ) lamellae obstacles 
 In Fig.9(a), many dislocations are hindered by the α2/γ lamellae and pile up in the (α2+γ) lamellar 
colonies. The α2/γ lamellae act as the obstacles for dislocations movement to strengthen the alloys at 
high temperatures. Long stripe shaped TiB distributing among the α2/γ lamellae and the dislocations 
around them can be also seen in Fig.9(b). The dislocations movement are prevented by the special 
structure consisting of TiB and the α2/γ lamellae. In this case, TiB could also hinder the cracks 
propagation along the lamellae interfaces. In addition, the hard bulk α2 besides TiB could also hinder 
dislocation slipping up due to less slip systems in hexagonal structure in Fig.9(c). 
Detailed analysis showed that no slip systems could transfer freely into neighbouring lamellae 
without leaving fragments of dislocations on the interfaces [27]. Most of the dislocations left on the

lamellar interfaces should have Burgers vectors out of the interfaci al plane. Those dislocations can 
apply a back stress to their successors [28]. These types of dislocations block cause the increased 
strength at high temperatures. 
3.3.4The borides strengthening 
In section 3.1, much stripe or needle shaped TiB distribute among the as-cast microstructure after 
boron addition. When the boron content is below 0.3at.%, only little TiB form along the colonies 
boundaries. With boron content increasing, TiB content would be increased , the TiB would be much 
longer and distribute not only along the colonies boundaries but also in the colonies. In addition, 
from section 3.1, r educed boron concentration below a certain level would change the boride s 
morphology from straight rod shape or ribbon shape to a curvy ribbon shape. Different shape and 
morphology borides would influences the microstructure evolution during tensile tests. 
TiB acts as the obstacle for dislocations slipping -up while dislocations piling -up on the TiB 
surfaces, as exhibited in Fig.10. The details of regi on A and B can be observed in Fig.10(b,c) . The 
dislocations pile up when they slip to the TiB surfaces. The anomalous strengthening mechanism is 
related to the cross -slip of <011] superdislocations and 1/2<110] ordinary dislocations in TiAl 
alloys[29]. Li et al [30] reported that the cross-slip of superdislocations occur ring more easily and the 
pinning effect of the dislocation locks being more remarkable led to strengthening at 700°C, with the 
further temperature increasing above 700°C, dislocation sliding and climbing could be activated. The 
dislocations block could strengthen the alloys effectively at high temperatures[31-33]. 
In addition, much deformation could be seen besides TiB in Fig.1 1(a,b). In the TEM bright -field 
images, TiB maintain the shape and many deformation regions distribute in the α2/γ lamellae along 
TiB. It means that α2/γ lamellae would deform but the hard TiB would not deform during the tensile 
test at high temperatures. TiB formation would increase the resistance for plastic deformation. In 
Fig.11(c), the dislocation piling -up on the TiB su rfaces indicates that TiB obstacles could hinder 
dislocations movement. Dislocations block could strengthen the alloys. In this case, a conclusion can 
be ensured which TiB would strengthen the alloys. The TiB or TiB2 would still maintain the strength 
below 1000 °C[34]. The borides act as the ligament bridges during the crack propagating and these 
phase were broke off or pulled out of the matrix when the alloy break [35]. In the high -temperature 
tensile tests, therefore, various types of TiB could hinder the deformation of matrix alloys and 
dislocation movement, which would cause stress concentration around the TiB. 
3.4 The strengthening mechanism 
 Based on the above microstructure observation, three types of strengthening mechanism caused by 
boron addition could be concluded in Fig.12. The first type of s trengthening mechanism results from 
the α2/γ lamellae with boron solid solution. Boron solid solution strength increased strength of α2/γ 
lamellae. In Fig.12(a), dislocations movement hindered by the strengthened α2/γ lamellae and 
piling-up on the interface s of α2/γ lamellae. The increased dislocation block in strengthened α2/γ 
lamellae would increase the matrix strength. 
 The second type of strengthening mechanism is caused by TiB formation besides α2 or on the 
colonies boundaries, such as the TiB and bulk α2 and γ in Fig.3. In Fig.12(b), TiB obstacles hinder 
dislocations movement to increase the strength of alloys directly. In addition, boron addition refine 
the grains and cause the fraction of colonies boundaries increasing. The colonies boundaries with 
high stacking energy are the weak positions and are always the cracks sources at high temperatures. 
The refinement worsen the high-temperature properties of alloys. Nevertheless, TiB formation 
through the colonies boundaries could pin the boundaries and hinder the crack propagation, which

could strengthen the weak boundaries at high temperatures. 
 The third type of strengthening mechanism results from the special structure between TiB and α2/γ 
lamellae. This special structure appear commonly in Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr-(0.3,0.6,1.0at.%)B 
alloys. In Fig.12(c), two types of interfaces could hinder dislocations movement to strengthen alloys: 
(i)The interfaces of α2/γ lamellae; (ii)The interfaces between TiB and α2/γ lamellae. Additionally, the 
stable triquetrous structure consisting of TiB and α2/γ lamellae could also hinder dislocations 
movement and crack formation along the interface of α2/γ lamellae. This triquetrous structure 
provides increased and stable strength at high temperatures for the alloys. . 
3.5 The variety of elongation 
The TiB pin the lamellar interfaces and the colonies boundaries to prevent the cracks propagation. 
However, the TiB obstacles hinder the dislocations movement and cause dislocati on piling-up. The 
increased content of dislocation block causes more stress concentration and cracks nucleation. These 
two factors influence the elongation together . In Fig.4, the T1 alloys exhibited ductile fracture and 
plastic deformation in the tensile tests above 850 °C. However, boron addition strengthening and 
hardening the alloys were discussed in section 3.4 . Therefore, when the T2, T3 and T4 alloys were 
tested below 850°C(included 850 °C), the strengthen ing effe ct would reduce the trend of plastic 
deformation in these alloys . The stress concentration from TiB could not be released quickly in a 
moment. In this case, the effect of stress concentration is much larger than the effect of TiB pinning 
on the alloys. It would cause the brittle fracture of T2, T3 and T4 alloys below 850 C(included 850°C) 
and the BDTT of T2, T3 and T4 increasing. The elongation are decreased dramatically after 0.3at.% 
boron addition in Fig.4(d). The temperature increasing would further activate the slipping systems in 
matrix. The more slipping systems provide positions for dislocations movement to release the stress 
concentration. With TiB content increasing, meanwhile, the dislocation block with low energy would 
be divided by more TiB. The stress concentration could not reach critical values of cracking in a 
moment, which provide time for the dislocation movement. The increased temperature also promote 
the dislocations movement and reduce the stress concentration ar ound the TiB. Dislocations 
movement promote plastic deformation and cause the ductile fracture in T2, T3 and T4 at 900 °C. In 
addition, the TiB would pin the weak boundaries and interface s to impede cracking . Therefore, the 
elongation are increased less(to be stable) below 850 C with boron increasing due to less slipping 
systems at low temperature. T he elongation are enhanced more with boron increasing at 900°C due 
to more slipping systems and less stress concentration. 
In addition , the elongation of T4 alloys is much larger than the T2 and T3 alloys at 900 °C. 
Because the TiB obstacles content in T4 is much larger than th at in T2 and T3, dislocations block 
would be divided into different parts by TiB obstacles. The separated dislocation block indicate the 
separated stress concentration and the low energy. The low and divided stress concentration could 
not cause cracking but increase the strength. The separated dislocations block need much more time 
to absorb the energy from dislocations movement. Li [30] et al also reported that the plastic 
deformation of TiAl alloy takes place mainly by dislocation movements or mechanical twinning. If 
the energy of dislocati on block can not reach the critical values of TiB breaking, the dislocations 
movement would be sustained until TiB br oken by stress con centration. The sustained dislocation 
movement provide the i ncreased plastic deformation for T4 alloys . In addition, much more TiB 
pining the colonies boundaries and lamellar interfaces would prevent the cracks formation and 
propagation. This factor guarantees the alloys not being broken during plastic deformation. Therefore, 
the T4 alloys with 1.0at.%B display much larger e longation than T2 and T3 alloys at 900 °C due to

much more TiB . The less TiB obstacles in T2 and T3 alloys cause the ductile fracture during 
yielding. 
 
Conclusion 
The (α2+γ) lamella e with β o and γ o phase occur in the as -cast microstructure of 
Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr-xB(x=0, 0.3, 0.6 and 1.0at.%) alloys. From high-temperature tensile cur ves, 
the effect of boron addition on the high -temperature properties and strengthening mechanism were 
concluded as follow. 
(1)Boron addition could increase the high -temperature UTS of Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr-xB(x=0, 0.3, 
0.6 and 1.0at.%) alloys. With the boron content increasing from 0at.% to 1.0at.%, the 
high-temperature UTS increase dramatically and the alloys with 1.0at%B exhibit UTS as 656.12±
20.54MPa at 800°C, 642.43±14.44MPa at 850°C and 508.44±16.12MPa at 900ºC, respectively. 
(2)Boron addition could stabilize the high-temperature UTS of Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr-xB(x=0, 0.3, 
0.6 and 1.0at.%) alloys. With temperatures increasing, the UTS of the alloys without boron addition 
decrease dramatically, but the UTS of the alloys with boron addition maintains stability in the range 
of 500-700MPa due to TiB obstacles for dislocations block. 
(3)Boron addition would enhance the brittle-ductile transition temperature and reduce the elongation 
of Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr alloys below the BDTT. 0.3at.%B decrease the high -temperature 
elongation dramatically while the elongation increase with boron content increasi ng from 0.3at.% to 
1.0at.%. The 1.0at.% boron addition could increase the elongation of alloys dramatically at 900°C. 
(4)Three types of s trengthening mechanism in high -temperature strength are caused by boron 
addition.(i)Boron solid solution strengthening in α2/γ lamellae; (ii)TiB obstacles for dislocation block 
and pinning the weak colonies boundaries; (iii) The stable triquetrous structure consisting of TiB and 
α2/γ lamellae. These three types of strengthening mechanism improve the high-temperature strength 
by strengthening the weak colonies boundaries and the lamellar interfaces.
