ABSTRACT
In this study, the Fe
50
Mn
30
Co
10
Cr
10 
high-entropy alloy with a deformation of 84 % was successfully prepared by 
two-stage cold rolling at intermediate annealing temperatures from 700 
◦
C to 1000 
◦
C. Microstructure evolution 
during the whole processing process and mechanical properties were investigated. The results indicate that an 
increase in intermediate annealing temperature promoted recrystallization and grain growth in the micro -
structure of the primary cold-rolled sheet. The transverse cracking in the second cold-rolled sheet was signifi -
cantly improved under elevated intermediate annealing temperature, although an HCP structure ranging from 
93.3 % to 95.5 % was formed in all second cold-rolled microstructure. In addition, the dependence of strain 
induced martensitic transformation process on deformation during second rolling was different in different in -
termediate annealed sheets. This is related to the dislocation slip ability and the stability of FCC phase affected by

perature. As a result, the strength of the alloy was decreased while its elongation was improved. At an inter -
mediate annealing temperature of 1000 
◦

with a ultimate tensile strength of 985 MPa, yield strength of 610 MPa and an elongation of 35.4 %.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure of the annealed hot band and first cold-rolled sheet
The XRD pattern and microstructure of the annealed hot band (as 
shown in Fig. 2 ) reveal a dual-phase microstructure comprising FCC and 
HCP phases. The HCP phase accounts for 26.9 % of the total volume. The 
FCC phase exhibit equiaxed grains with an average grain size of 
approximately 45.7 μ m. Within these FCC grains, lath-shaped HCP 
phase structures of thickness ranging from 4 to 6 μ m are observed 
( Fig. 2 b). This indicates that the phase transition from FCC to HCP oc -
curs during the annealing process of hot band, which is similar to the 
results reported by Li et al. [7] . In addition, the EDS analysis ( Fig. 2 d) 
confirms a homogeneous distribution of Fe, Mn, Co, and Cr elements 
throughout the microstructure, with no evidence of elemental 
segregation.
The macro-morphology and microstructure of the first cold-rolled 
sheet are shown in Fig. 3 . After cold rolling with reduction of 60 %, a 
small number of transverse cracks appear at both ends of the sheet 
( Fig. 3 a), which is consistent with our previous research results [27] . 
After severe plastic deformation, the microstructure of the annealed hot 
band is severely flattened and elongated. The grains are fragmented into 
numerous fine substructures and deformation bands ( Fig. 3 c). These 
deformed microstructures exhibit a preferred orientation along [1000] 
// ND ( Fig. 3 b). The fraction of HCP phase increases to ~84.5 %, indi -
cating a further transformation from FCC to HCP phase during cold 
rolling. According to the phase content in the annealed hot band, about 
50 % of the FCC phase has undergone a phase transition during first cold 
rolling process. As described in the literature [14,18] , dislocation slip, 
twinning and phase transformation will successively maintain plastic 
deformation with the increase of rolling reduction rate in FeMnCoCr 
alloy. However, these deformation mechanisms are only applicable to 
the deformation not exceeding ~60 %.

4. Discussion
The results of this work show that the intermediate annealed 
microstructure exhibits temperature-dependent characteristics, as 
illustrated in Fig. 4 . At a lower intermediate annealing temperature (700 
◦
C), partial recrystallization occurs in the first cold-rolled sheet, forming 
fine new grains. This grain refinement enhances the FCC phase stability 
in the intermediate annealed microstructure [32] , therefore suppressing 
the development of heat-induced HCP martensite during subsequent 
cooling [18] . With increasing intermediate annealing temperature, the 
recrystallization and grain growth process are promoted. Here, the grain 
coarsening reduces the FCC phase stability in the intermediate annealed 
sheet, thus promoting the formation of HCP phase during cooling, as 
demonstrated in Fig. 4 c.

sheets significantly influence deformation behavior and geometric sta -

For the final annealing process, the elevated intermediate annealing 
temperature reduces the recrystallization rate in the second cold rolled 
sheet. This behavior originates from the change of deformation stored 
energy - a critical determinant of recrystallization dynamics [41,42] . 
Microstructural analysis reveals that material A (lower intermediate 
annealing temperature) exhibit refined deformed substructure and ac -
cumulates higher deformation energy during second cold rolling 
( Fig. 7 ). This improves the driving force of recrystallization and pro -
duces smaller grains during final annealing ( Fig. 10 ). Meanwhile, ma -
terial A demonstrates elevated HCP phase content and enhanced 
annealing twin density. In comparison, lower dislocation density and 
deformation energy in second rolled material C retards recrystallization 
kinetics and leads to larger grains. This microstructural evolution ac -
companies decreased twin density and obvious residual deformation 
subgrains ( Fig. 10 ). Therefore, the temperature-dependent recrystalli -
zation behavior establishes a fundamental correlation: intermediate 
annealing parameters regulate final microstructure through dislocation 
density and stored energy in deformed structure, directly controlling 
recrystallization completeness, grain size distribution.

5. Conclusions
In this study, the Fe
50
Mn
30
Co
10
Cr
10 
high entropy alloy was prepared 
by two-stage cold rolling process. The effect of intermediate annealing 
temperature on microstructure evolution and mechanical properties was 
elucidated.The conclusions are summarized as follows: 
(1) The first cold-rolled sheet contained a large number of fine sub -
structures and deformation bands, with a content of 84.5 % HCP 
phase. The intermediate annealed sheet at 700 
◦
C exhibited a 
partially recrystallized FCC microstructure. As the intermediate 
annealing temperature increased to 900 – 1000 
◦
C, complete 
recrystallization occurred, accompanied by a gradual increase in 
average grain size and the formation of some lath HCP phase.
(2) As the intermediate annealing temperature increased, the trans -
verse cracking in the second cold-rolled sheet was significantly 
improved. All second cold-rolled sheets contained 93.3 – 95.5 % 
HCP phase, but the phase transformation processes in the mate -
rials were not consistent. When the rolling reduction was less 
than 30 %, the martensitic transformation rate in the higher 
temperature intermediate annealed sample was faster. However, 
the lower temperature intermediate annealed sample achieved 
nearly complete martensitic microstructure more rapidly when 
the deformation was further increased to 60 %.
(3) As the intermediate annealing temperature increased, the defor -
mation stored energy of the second cold-rolled microstructure 
decreased, resulting in a lower degree of recrystallization and an 
increased average grain size in the final annealed sheet.
(4) With increasing intermediate annealing temperature, the yield 
strength and tensile strength of the final annealed alloy decreased 
progressively from 726 MPa and 1027 MPa to 610 MPa and 
985 MPa, respectively.
