Phenomenological aspects of fatigue life and fatigue crack initiation in 
high strength alloys at cryogenic temperature 
Osamu Umezawa* and Keisuke Ishikawa** 
National Research Institute for Metals, 1-2-1 Sengen, Tsukuba, lbaraki 305 (Japan)

Abstract 
Based on the S-N (stress cycling) data of Ti-6A1-4V alloys and high Cr-Ni steels (JN1 and YUS170) at 4, 77 and 293 K, 
the fatigue strength and crack initiation behavior in the long-life range were investigated in this study. Ti-6A1-4V rolled 
alloys and YUS 170 steel exhibited a good fatigue strength in the long-life range at cryogenic temperatures. In Ti-6A1-4V 
forged alloys and JN1 steel, however, there was a rather sharp drop in the fatigue strength at 4 and 77 K between 106 and 
10 7 cycles. Subsurface crack initiation occurred in the long-life range for each material, and the location of its site was 
determined. The size of the subsurface crack initiation site obviously increased for Ti-6AI-4V forged alloys and JNI 
steel as the number of cycles increased. We then proposed that the fatigue strength in the long-life range for the present 
alloys is related to the microcrack growth behavior in their microstructure. 
1. Introduction 
Our research group has investigated the cryogenic, 
high-cycle fatigue properties of some alloys, such as 
titanium alloys, and austenitic steels which are the can- 
didate materials for cryogenic structural use [1-9]. For 
the materials tested, the fatigue strength after 10 6 
cycles at 4 K did not always depend on their tensile 
strength, and the reason for this has not been made 
clear. Indeed, in some of the materials tested, two kinds 
of fatigue crack initiation site were seen. One was con- 
ventionally at the specimen surface and the other was 
in the specimen interior. Table 1 shows the sum- 
marized results of previous studies of the fatigue crack 
initiation site and subsurface crack origin. Subsurface 
crack initiation apparently occurs without the existence 
of any defects, such as inclusions or pores, at lower 
temperature in some high strength alloys. 
For Ti-6A1-4V alloy, subsurface crack initiation has 
been reported, regardless of the testing and metal- 
lurgical conditions (below room temperature, fre- 
quency, stress ratio, environment, manufacturing 
process, impurities concentration and microstructure) 
[4, 12]. Ti-6A1-4V alloy and high Cr-Ni steel, such as

2.2. Fatigue testing condition and examinations 
Hourglass-type, unnotched specimens with waist 
diameters of 4.5 mm (Ti-6A1-4V alloys) and 6 mm 
(high Cr-Ni steels) were fatigued in liquid helium (4 K), 
liquid nitrogen (77 K) and in air (293 K) [4, 5]. Using a 
cryogenic servohydraulic fatigue testing machine [13], 
load-control tests were carried out with a sinusoidal 
waveform and a minimum-to-maximum stress ratio R 
of 0.01. The longitudinal direction of the specimens 
was the L-direction for the Ti-6AI-4V alloys and the 
T-direction for the high Cr-Ni steels respectively. 
The subsurface crack initiation sites were examined 
by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). At low magni- 
fication, the subsurface crack initiation sites appeared 
flat and were inclined to the principal stress axis. In this 
study, the subsurface crack initiation site was defined 
as the whole of the inclined area which was not on the 
fatigue crack propagating plane. 
The distance d from the subsurface crack initiation 
site to the nearest specimen surface was evaluated from 
SEM photographs. The size of the subsurface crack 
initiation size was also quantified by a crack length 
parameter. The subsurface crack size fs was defined as 
the minor axis of an orthogonal projection of the sub- 
surface crack initiation site on the main crack pro-

3.2. ('rack-initiation location 
In general, fatigue crack initiation is understood to 
occur on the specimen surface as a result of the irrever- 
sible process of extrusion and intrusion through slip 
deformation. In the Ti-6A1-4V alloys and high Cr-Ni 
steels, however, subsurface crack initiation was 
detected at a lower peak stress and/or at a longer life 
over 105 cycles, as shown in Fig. 1. 
Figure 2 shows the distance d of the subsurface crack 
initiation site, from the surface at 4 and 77 K. In 
Ti-6AI-4V alloys, the internal sites are mostly around 
120 pm from the surface, which agrees with the results 
from previous work [14]. However, in the JN1 and 
YUS170 steels, the site is somewhere between the 
vicinity of the specimen surface and the center of the 
specimen for each sample. There is no stress depen- 
dence on the location for both the Ti-6AI-4V alloys 
and the high Cr-Ni steels in this figure. Since most axial 
fatigue test equipment is misaligned to a certain degree, 
bending moments will develop during the loading 
cycle. As a result, some locations at the specimen 
surface will develop higher tensile stresses [14]. For the 
Ti-6A1-4V alloys, subcracks (microcracks) were 
initiated just below the stage i and/or stage II fracture

3.3. Subsurface crack initiation site 
No defect, such as an inclusion or pore, was detected 
at the subsurface crack initiation sites, and cleavage 
fracture did not appear either. The subsurface crack 
initiation sites consisted of one or more facets for each 
alloy. 
The morphology of the subsurface crack initiation 
site in the Ti-6A1-4V alloys was associated with their 
microstructure [7, 8]. Figure 3 shows SEM photo- 
graphs of subsurface crack initiation sites for the 
Ti-6AI-4V alloys. In the forged materials, the sub- 
surface crack initiation site consists of elongated facets 
which are aligned (Fig. 3(b)). In the rolled materials, 
however, the aspect ratio of the facets is much higher 
than that in the forged materials, and each facet is 
inclined at random (Fig. 3(e)). For forged and rolled 
materials, the facets were identified as a cracked a 
phase, by a characterizing their chemical composition 
and morphology [7, 8]. 
Figure 4 shows SEM photographs of the subsurface 
crack initiation sites for the JN1 and YUS170 steels. 
The subsurface crack initiation site in the JN1 steel was 
identified as an intergranular facet. The presence of a 
triple point along the facets confirmed the integranular 
cracking, and protrusions at the sites matched in an 
interlocking manner on opposite fracture surfaces. 
These features were commonly observed in the inter- 
granular facet of 32Mn-7Cr-0.1N steel [9]. For the 
YUS170 steel, the subsurface crack initiation site is a 
facet, as shown in Fig. 4(b). The facet showed the 
feature of microcracking but its origin has not been 
clarified yet. 
3.4. Size of subsurface crack initiation site 
As the number of cycles to failure (N f) increases, the 
area of the subsurface crack initiation site in the 
Ti-6AI-4V alloys becomes larger with the morpho- 
logical changes, as indicated by arrows in Fig. 3. The 
area of the initiation site in the JN1 steel also became 
large in the longer-life range. 
Figure 5 shows the dependence of the subsurface 
crack size f, on Nf for the Ti-6A1-4V Normal alloy 
and the high Cr-Ni steels. As the Nf increases, f~ con- 
tinuously increases from several micrometers to 
several tens of micrometers at 4 and 77 K in the 
Ti-6AI-4V forged material, as shown in Fig. 5(a). In 
the Ti-6A1-4V rolled material, f~ gradually increases 
from several micrometers to about 20 /~m as Nf 
increases from 105 to 5 × 10 6 cycles. In the forged 
material, therefore, the subsurface crack initiation site 
was larger than that in the rolled material of the same 
alloy after a similar number of cycles above 106. 
The variation of fs with the increase in Nf at 4 and 
77 K is obviously different for the JN1 and YUS170 
steels, as shown in Fig. 5(b). For the JN1 steel, fs 
sharply increases from about 10 to 80 pm in this figure 
as Nf increases from 105 to 10 7 cycles. In contrast, for 
YUS170 steel, fs is equal to about 10 pm between 105 
and 107 cycles. Thus, fs of the JN1 steel is much 
greater than that of the YUS 170 steel in the longer-life 
range.

4. Conclusions 
The high-cycle fatigue strength and subsurface crack 
initiation behavior of the high strength alloys, i.e. 
Ti-6A1-4V, JN1 and YUS 170, in the long-life range at 
cryogenic temperatures were investigated. The results 
are as follows. 
(1) The fatigue strength of the Ti-6AI-4V forged 
material was much lower than that of the rolled mate- 
rial. The fatigue strength of the JN1 steel was also 
lower than that of the YUS170 steel, although their 
tensile strengths were almost the same at each tempera- 
ture. 
(2) In the Ti-6A1-4V alloys, the subsurface crack 
initiation sites were mostly around 120 ym from the 
surface. However, in the JN1 and YUS170 steels, the 
site was somewhere between the vicinity of the speci- 
men surface and the center of the specimen for each 
sample. From these phenomena, the microcracking 
behavior is supposed to be different between the 
Ti-6A1-4V alloys and the high Cr-Ni steels. 
(3) The size of the subsurface crack initiation site 
obviously increased in the long-life range for the 
Ti-6A1-4V forged material and the JN1 steel. This 
result agrees with a rather sharp drop in the fatigue 
strength at 4 and 77 K above 106 cycles. 
(4) According to the discussion on the subsurface 
crack initiation, we propose that the fatigue strength in 
the long-life range for the present alloys is related to 
the microcrack growth behavior in their microstruc- 
tures.
