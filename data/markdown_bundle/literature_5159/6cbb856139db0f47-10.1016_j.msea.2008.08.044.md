abstract
Nanocrystalline alloy Ti49.4 Ni50.6 (numbers indicate at.%) in the shape of a disc 20 mm in diameter has been
successfully produced by high-pressure torsion. High-pressure torsion and annealings at temperatures
from 400 to 550
◦C resulted in formation of nanocrystalline structures with the mean grain size ( D)i n
the range 20–300 nm. The alloys with D = 20 nm possess very high ultimate tensile strength (more than
2000 MPa) but limited ductility. The alloys with D = 300 nm possess high ductility and enhanced ultimate
tensile strength. There are areas of strain-induced transformation B2–B19 ′ on the tensile curves, but
their extent depends on the grain size. The relationship between mechanical behavior and martensitic
transformation in nanocrystalline TiNi alloys is considered and discussed.

1. Introduction
TiNi alloys are well-known materials displaying the shape-
memory effect. They are very promising for many structural and
functional applications in engineering and medicine [1,2]. Recent
investigations testify that an effective way to enhance properties
of various metals and alloys is the formation of the nanocrystalline
(NC) structure using severe plastic deformation (SPD) [3,4]. Previ-
ous studies have already shown that deformation via high-pressure
torsion (HPT) of TiNi alloys results in the formation of an amor-
phous structure, and further annealings may lead to a NC state
[5–7]. However, the small size of HPT samples (about 10 mm in
diameter and 0.2 mm thick) used in Ref. [5–7] prevented a com-
prehensive investigation of the mechanical properties. In this work,
new special HPT equipment was used to produce samples with a
diameter of 20 mm and a thickness up to 1 mm. The TiNi samples of
these dimensions open new possibilities to investigate microstruc-
ture and mechanical properties. The strain-induced martensitic
transformation in the NC TiNi was given close consideration
here.

2. Experimental details
AT i 49.4 Ni50.6 alloy (numbers indicate at.%) was used as initial
material for the investigations. The temperature Ms of the start of
the martensitic transformation В2 (austenite) → В19′(martensite)
of the quenched alloy was about 15 ◦C. The TiNi alloy was exposed
to HPT on the new device with enhanced capacity at a pressure of
6 GPa, and the number of rotations was n = 5. The microstructure of
the samples after processing was studied by transmission electron
microscopy (TEM). Room temperature tensile tests were carried
out on ﬂat small samples with a gage of 1 mm × 0.25 mm× 3m m
and a strain rate of 3 × 10
−4 s−1 using a specially designed tensile
machine.
3. Results and discussion
Using HPT, contiguous samples were obtained with a diam-
eter of 20 mm and a thickness of 0.7 mm. The TEM studies of
the microstructure showed that the obtained HPT samples had
an amorphous-nanocrystalline structure ( Fig. 1 a). Annealing at
temperatures up to 300
◦C (for 1 h) did not result in complete
crystallization, and the amorphous phase was retained. After
annealing at 400
◦C, 20 min complete nanocrystallization took
place, and an average grain (crystallite) size was about 20 nm
(Fig. 1 b). Annealing at 450
◦C for 20 min resulted in the occur-
rence of a nanostructure with an average grain size of about 70 nm.

with a grain size of 20 nm can be explained as following. The high
dislocation yield stress of NC TiNi samples makes it possible to apply
sufﬁciently high loads for the strain-induced martensitic transfor-
mation to occur. Even cooling down to low temperatures does not
provide the required driving force for the martensitic transforma-
tion B2→ B19
′in the TiNi alloy with a grain size less than 40 nm [8].

Another interesting result is a very high ductility of the alloy
with a grain size of 300 nm ( ı > 70%). In the initial coarse-grained
state, the Ti 49.4 Ni50.6 alloy had a ductility of about 80%. Thus,
formation of the UFG structure in TiNi 50.6 did not result in a
signiﬁcant reduction in ductility. The formation of the UFG struc-
ture in other metals and alloys decreases the ductility two times
and more [3]. Retention of high ductility of UFG TiNi is proba-
bly caused by the strain-induced martensitic transformation and
the low modulus of elasticity of TiNi [1 1]. These factors possibly
increase the strain hardening and ﬂow stability during tension of
TiNi. When the grain size of TiNi decreases down to 50 nm, the duc-
tility of the material reduces considerably, but remains higher than
the ductility of other nanocrystalline materials with similar grain
size.
4. Conclusions
(i) The HPT technique was successfully applied to fabricate
Ti
49.4 Ni50.6 specimens with an amorphous-nanocrystalline
structure of relatively large dimensions (20 mm in diameter
and 0.7 mm in thickness). Annealing at 400–500
◦C resulted
in the formation of a nanocrystalline state with various grain
sizes.
(ii) The processed specimens have a record strength level (up to
2300 MPa). Moreover, annealing at 400
◦C does not deteriorate
the strength, increases the ductility and leads to the appearance
of the ability for martensitic transformation under load.
(iii) The TiNi alloy with a grain size of about 20 nm is able of
the strain-induced martensitic transformation B2–B19
′phase.
Here, the stress of martensitic transformation is /ESCm ≈ 450 MPa,
which is almost three times higher than/ESCm in the initial coarse-
grained state ( /ESCm ≈ 160 MPa). X-ray diffraction conﬁrms that
the B19′ phase remained after the failure of the samples and
load removal.
(iv) Correlation between the length of the plateau region on the
curve /ESC–ε, connected with the strain-induced martensitic
transformation, and the ductility during tension of the sample
has been observed, which is indicative of the important role of
martensitic transformations in the mechanical behavior of NC
alloys.
