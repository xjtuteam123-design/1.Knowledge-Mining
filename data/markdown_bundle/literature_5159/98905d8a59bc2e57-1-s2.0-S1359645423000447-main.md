ABSTRACT 
The temperature-dependent yield strengths of [ 123]-oriented single crystals of four quinary non-equiatomic Cr- 
Mn-Fe-Co-Ni high-entropy alloys (HEAs) were investigated at temperatures of 10-1173 K. They exhibit similar 
trends with a rapid decrease of the critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) from 10 K to 300 K, followed by a much 
slower rate of decrease with increasing temperature and a plateau above 873 K. The HEAs with the highest and 
lowest Cr contents exhibit the highest and lowest yield strengths respectively at 10 K, but almost identical yield 
strengths at 300 K. Thus, at cryogenic temperatures, the magnitude of the temperature dependence of yield 
strength is positively correlated with Cr content. All the non-equiatomic HEAs also exhibit similar dislocation 
structures with planar arrays of smoothly-curved dislocations without any preferred orientations. Their stacking 
fault energy (SFE) decreases with increasing Cr content. An empirical expression is proposed to describe the 
composition dependence of SFE in the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni quinary system. A new route for optimizing the alloy 
design of non-equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni alloys in terms of strength and ductility through the combination of 
mean-square atomic displacement (MSAD) and SFE is discussed based on the results obtained. 
1. Introduction 
High- and medium-entropy alloys (HEAs/MEAs) are single-phase 
solid-solution alloys typically composed of multiple principal elements 
( ≥ 3) with all elements at non-dilute concentrations. The quinary 
equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni alloy with the face-centered cubic (FCC) 
structure [1] , is a model high-entropy alloy (HEA) that has attracted 
considerable interest and is one of the most studied HEAs in the last 
decade [2 – 18] . This stems from its interesting combination of me -
chanical properties such as outstanding high yield strength, excellent 
tensile ductility [ 3 , 4 , 6 , 10 ] and fracture toughness [ 5 , 7 ] at cryogenic 
temperatures. However, these excellent mechanical properties may not 
be unique to the equiatomic composition and there is a high chance that 
even better mechanical properties are obtained at alloy compositions 
that deviate from the equiatomic one. To develop HEAs of the 
Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni system with enhanced mechanical properties, it is 
indispensable to determine material parameters that play crucial roles in 
describing these excellent mechanical properties such as strength and 
ductility. The mechanistic origins of these excellent and unusual me -
chanical properties of HEAs have been gradually unraveled [ 6-11 , 
19-26 ]. The high yield strength is believed to be closely related to the 
severely distorted crystal lattice and models have been proposed to 
correlate the strength with certain materials parameters that describe 
the severely distorted lattice [ 19-23 , 26 ]. These models adopt atomic size 
[ 19 , 21 , 22 , 23 ] and shear modulus [ 19 , 22 ] misfit as the materials pa -
rameters to describe the strength of HEAs extending the treatment of 
existing models of solid-solution hardening (SSH) applicable to dilute 
binary alloys [27 – 31] . We also have proposed mean-square atomic 
displacement (MSAD) as a materials parameter to scale the strength of 
HEAs [20] . However, the accuracy of these models in predicting the 
strength of HEAs has not been fully verified due mainly to the fact that 
only a limited number of experimental results of single crystals is 
available. The excellent low-temperature tensile ductility, on the other 
hand, is believed to be due to the occurrence of deformation twinning, as 
verified by many experiments with polycrystals of various HEAs [ 4 , 10 , 
11 , 32 – 35 ]. The stacking fault energy is then the materials parameter to

In the present study, we investigate the temperature-dependent yield 
stress of single crystals of four non-equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEAs in 
the temperature range of 10-1173 K. These results are used to check the 
accuracy of some models proposed to predict the strength of HEAs at low

composition in the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni system. We then discuss a new route

terms of strength and ductility through the combination of MSAD and

2. Experimental procedures 
Rods, 10 mm in diameter and 80 mm long, were prepared by arc- 
melting high-purity ( > 99.9%) Cr, Mn, Fe, Co and Ni in an argon at -
mosphere. Four different alloys were made in which the concentration 
of one of the five constituent elements (Cr or Co) was varied while those 
of the other four elements were kept constant and identical to each 
other, see Table 1 , which also lists the equiatomic alloy as a reference.

40Co alloys. From these polycrystalline castings, single crystals were

Pugh [45] postulated that the tendency for materials to become 
brittle increases as the ratio of the shear modulus to bulk modulus ( G / B ) 
increases. Gilman [46] suggested a value of G / B ~ 0.6 for this ‘ducti -
le-brittle ’ transition. Pettifor [47] pointed out that the Cauchy pressure, 
which is a descriptor of the bonding in a material, tends to be positive in 
most metallically bonded materials and negative in covalently bonded 
materials that are highly resistant to bond bending. Based on these two 
concepts, Niu et al. [48] recently suggested that the cross-over point in a 
plot of the Young ’ s modulus normalized Cauchy pressure versus G / B 
indicates a transition between ductile and brittle behavior such that 
materials whose G / B is greater than ~0.57 are brittle and vice versa. 
When judged on the basis of Pugh ’ s criterion, the equiatomic and all our 
non-equiatomic HEAs are predicted to be borderline brittle with the 
exception of the 40Co alloy that is predicted to be brittle. Negative 
Cauchy pressure and small values of Poisson ’ s ratio are generally 
believed to be an indicator of brittleness. From this perspective, the 
40Co alloy would be expected to be brittle ( Fig. 1 (c)). However, we have 
shown previously that such predictions do not agree with experimental 
results [40] , as simple criteria based on elastic constants cannot describe 
the deformation behavior in the plastic regime when tensile elongations 
exceed 50%.

3.2. Temperature dependence of CRSS 
The temperature dependence of CRSS for slip on (111)[ 101], which 
was calculated from the yield stress defined as the 0.2% offset flow stress 
and corresponding Schmid factor (0.467), for the four quinary non- 
equiatomic HEAs is shown in Fig. 2 , together with that for the quinary 
equiatomic HEA determined in our previous study [40] . Optical mi -
croscopy, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and TEM observations 
confirmed the operation of the (111)[ 101] slip system and the absence 
of deformation twinning in the strain range of easy glide at all temper -
atures investigated. The non-equiatomic HEAs behave similarly to the 
equiatomic HEA. Firstly, below room temperature (300 K), the CRSS 
increases rapidly with decreasing temperature without a significant 
dulling of the temperature dependence of CRSS at cryogenic tempera -
tures (well-known as inertial effect, which is clearly observed in some 
HEAs [49] and MEAs such as Cr-Co-Ni [50] ). Secondly, an inflection in 
the CRSS curves is observed at around 473 K for all the non-equiatomic 
HEAs except the 25Cr alloy. Such an inflection of the temperature 
dependence of CRSS is also observed in the Cr-Co-Ni equiatomic MEA 
[50] , although its origin has yet to be identified. Finally, there is a 
plateau or slight increase of the CRSS above 873 K, as has also been 
observed in the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni equiatomic HEA [40] and Cr-Co-Ni 
equiatomic MEA [50] . From the serrated flow seen in the stress-strain 
curves in the corresponding temperature range, this has been inter -
preted to be due to the Portevin-Le Chatelier (PLC) effect, in which the 
interaction between solute atoms and dislocations plays an essential role 
[ 40 , 50 ]. Of interest to note in Fig. 2 is that the 25Cr alloy exhibits the 
highest CRSS while the 5Cr alloy exhibits the lowest CRSS at 10 K but the 
CRSSs of the two alloys are identical at room temperature (300 K). At 
still higher temperatures, the trend is reversed with the CRSS of the 5Cr 
alloy being higher than that of the 25Cr alloy. This indicates that the 5Cr 
alloy exhibits the weakest temperature dependence of CRSS among the 
four quinary non-equiatomic HEAs investigated. 
3.3. Deformation structures 
3.3.1. Slip lines 
Fig. 3 depicts deformation markings observed on two orthogonal 
surfaces of the [ 123]-oriented single crystals of the 5Cr ( Figs. 3 (a),(b)), 
25Cr ( Figs. 3 (c),(d)), 10Co ( Figs. 3 (e),(f)), and 40Co ( Figs. 3 (g),(h)) al -
loys deformed at room temperature to a plastic strain of about 3%. These 
markings are slip lines parallel to the traces of (111), the most stressed of 
the four different {111} planes. SEM observation with electron back -
scattered diffraction (EBSD) mapping as well as TEM observations 
confirmed the absence of deformation twinning in the small strain range 
of easy glide at all temperatures investigated. Slip lines in Fig. 3 are all 
quite straight, passing through the specimen without interruption and 
showing no sign of cross-slip. This indicates that slip on (111) is quite 
planar for all four quinary non-equiatomic HEAs. 
3.3.2. Dislocation structures 
Figs. 4 (a)-(d) show dislocation structures observed in [ 123]-oriented 
single crystals of the quinary non-equiatomic HEAs, 5Cr, 25Cr, 10Co and 
40Co alloys, respectively, after compression at room temperature to 
2~3% plastic strain. The thin foils of Fig. 4 were all cut parallel to the 
(111) slip plane. Planar arrays of dislocations, together with multipoles 
(higher order dipoles) are observed without any preferred orientations 
for their alignment and without any evidence of dislocation cell for -
mation, indicating the absence of anisotropy in mobilities of edge and 
screw dislocations and the low stacking fault energy for all these HEAs. 
Consistent with the expected low stacking fault energy, widely separated 
coupled Shockley partial dislocations are clearly visible when imaged by 
weak-beam microscopy ( Figs. 4 (e)-(h)). The stacking fault energies of 
these quinary non-equiatomic HEAs are estimated by measuring the 
separation distance as a function of angle ( θ ) between the Burgers vector 
and line vector and the results are shown in Fig. 5 . Although some 
scatter in dissociation widths at a given θ is observed in Fig. 5 , the extent 
of the scatter is similar to those in elemental metals such as pure Cu and 
Ag [ 51 , 52 ] and in the equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA [ 9 , 40 , 44 ]. No 
significant local variation in dissociation widths was observed in the 
present study, unlike in some previous reports [53 – 55] . The stacking 
fault energies estimated from the dissociation widths and elastic con -
stants measured in the present study are 52 ± 10, 23 ± 6, 38 ± 10 and 
19 ± 4 mJ/m

Comparison of these values with that (30 ± 5 mJ/m

equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA indicates that additions of Cr and Co

4.4. Stacking fault energy 
The stacking fault energy of FCC solid-solution alloys such as Cu- and 
Ag-alloys has long been known to be correlated with the electron to 
atom ratio ( e / a ratio); specifically, it decreases with increasing e / a ratio 
[ 68 , 69 ]. However, the e / a ratio of the quinary HEAs is ambiguous. 
Therefore, it is of interest to check whether valence electron concen -
tration (VEC) can be a predictor of stacking fault energy in FCC HEAs as 
the e / a ratio is for Cu- and Ag-alloys. Valence electron concentration has 
indeed been used for evaluating the FCC phase stability of HEAs [70] . 
The stacking fault energies of the four non-equiatomic and one equia -
tomic quinary HEAs are plotted in Fig. 12 (a) as a function of the aver -
aged VEC, together with those of the quinary HEAs reported previously 
[36 – 38] . The stacking fault energy seems not to be correlated with VEC 
in a simple manner. One of the reasons could be that VEC can only 
represent the average number of total electrons in the valence band in 
each alloy but the local electronic structure of alloys with complex 
chemical composition may not be well described by VEC alone. 
Although stacking fault energy cannot be predicted by a simple 
scaling factor (such as VEC), it may still be useful to deduce an empirical 
expression for the dependence of stacking fault energy on alloy

4.5. Alloy design using MSAD and SFE for high strength and ductility 
According to the previous discussion in the present paper, Cr is 
effective in increasing the MSAD and decreasing the stacking fault en -
ergy in HEAs of the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni system. This strongly suggests that 
the quinary non-equiatomic 25Cr alloy must be better in terms of 
strength and ductility than the quinary equiatomic (20Cr) alloy. To 
verify this, we tensile tested polycrystalline specimens of these two al -
loys. They were prepared by annealing at 1273 K for 1 hour after cold- 
rolling of arc-melted ingots to 90% reduction in thickness. For com -
parison, we similarly prepared polycrystals of the ternary equiatomic Cr- 
Co-Ni MEA, which is reported to exhibit the highest strength and 
ductility among the equiatomic subsystems of the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni sys -
tem [6] . Fig. 13 shows engineering stress-strain curves obtained in 
tension at room temperature and 77 K for polycrystals of the quinary 
equiatomic, 25Cr non-equiatomic alloys and the ternary equiatomic 
Cr-Co-Ni MEA. As tabulated in Table 6 , the yield strength as well as the 
tensile ductility (strain at failure) is higher at 77 K than at room tem -
perature for all three alloys. The quinary 25Cr non-equiatomic alloy 
exhibits a higher yield strength and much higher tensile ductility than 
the quinary equiatomic alloy, as expected based on their MSADs and 
stacking fault energies. Therefore, a higher MSAD and a lower stacking 
fault energy are preferred for high strength and high ductility, respec -
tively. However, it should be noted that, although a lower stacking fault 
energy is in principle desirable, there is a limit below which low stacking 
fault energies ( < ~10 mJ/m
2
) will lead to deformation-induced fcc-hcp 
transformation and premature failure with low ductility in poly -
crystalline materials, as mentioned in [ 73 , 74 ]. In other words, alloy 
design should aim to make the SFE low enough to activate twinning and 
limited fcc-hcp phase transformation, but not so low that it triggers 
massive fcc-hcp transformation. Besides, when the solid solubility limit 
of Cr (~26 at. %) in the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni system is exceeded, the σ phase 
precipitates in alloys with Cr concentration higher than 26 at. % [38] . 
Therefore, the quinary 25Cr non-equiatomic alloy is the alloy with the 
highest Cr concentration that remains as a single-phase solid solution in 
the present system. 
The strength and ductility of the quinary 25Cr non-equiatomic alloy 
are comparable to (or a bit larger than) those of the ternary equiatomic 
Cr-Co-Ni MEA, known as the alloy of the highest strength and ductility. 
This is also consistent with their similar values of MSAD and stacking 
fault energy. MASD and stacking fault energy are thus proved to be nice 
predictors for the strength and ductility and can be effectively used for 
alloy design of HEAs not only of the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni system but also 
other systems. 
5. Conclusions 
The temperature-dependent yield strengths as well as dislocation 
structures of [ 123]-oriented single crystals of four quinary non- 
equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have been 
investigated in the range 10-1173 K. The results obtained may be 
summarized as follows. 
(1) All the quinary non-equiatomic HEAs exhibit a similar trend of 
the temperature dependence of CRSS as the quinary equiatomic 
HEA; a rapid decrease in yield strength from 10 K to 300 K 
without a significant dulling of the temperature dependence of 
CRSS at cryogenic temperatures (well-known as the inertial ef -
fect), followed by a much slower rate of decrease with increasing 
temperature and a plateau above 873 K. 
(2) The non-equiatomic HEAs with the highest and lowest Cr con -
tents exhibit the highest and lowest yield strengths, respectively, 
at 10 K, while exhibiting almost identical yield strengths at 300 K. 
This clearly indicates that the temperature dependence of yield 
strength is positively correlated with Cr content. 
(3) The temperature-dependent yield strengths are compared with 
those predicted by three different models to check their accuracy. 
Varvenne et al ’ s model tends to underestimate the strength and 
its temperature dependence, whereas the model of Toda- 
Caraballo et al. and that based on MSAD seem to fit well with 
the experimental results though the latter two need fitting 
constants. 
(4) All the non-equiatomic HEAs exhibit similar dislocation struc -
tures with planar arrays of smoothly-curved dislocations without 
any preferred orientations. The stacking fault energy estimated 
from the separation distances of partial dislocations in these 
HEAs decreases with increasing Cr content. Based on these re -
sults, an empirical expression is proposed to describe the 
composition dependence of stacking fault energy in the Cr-Mn- 
Fe-Co-Ni quinary system. 
(5) A new route for optimizing the alloy design of non-equiatomic Cr- 
Mn-Fe-Co-Ni alloys in terms of strength and ductility through the 
combination of MSAD and SFE is discussed based on the results 
obtained and is verified by mechanical tests performed on poly -
crystals. The strength and ductility of the 25Cr HEA are much 
better than those of the quinary equiatomic HEA and comparable 
to those of the Cr-Co-Ni equiatomic MEA.
