ABSTRACT 
To further develop Ni-based powder metallurgy (PM) superalloys for aero-engines, it is critical to understand 
their deformation mechanisms at various service temperatures. For turbine disks, the tensile strength and 
absorbed impact energy are critical parameters. The varying stress during tensile deformation makes it essen -
tially different from creep. In this study, a high-performance PM superalloy with various Ta contents was 
evaluated by tensile tests at different temperatures from room temperature to 815 
◦
C. The relationship between 
the temperature and plastic deformation mechanisms was explored at multiple scales using scanning electron 
microscopy and transmission electron microscopy, and the temperature dependence of the deformation mech -
anism map was established. The results demonstrated that the main deformation mechanism gradually changed 
from antiphase boundary shearing to superlattice stacking fault shearing as the temperature increases. The 
extended stacking faults (ESFs) were the source of the microtwins, which extended within the individual grains 
and thickened with increasing temperature. In addition, a novel mechanism of ESF formation was observed, i.e., 
Shockley shearing separated by one layer of atoms. Furthermore, the effects of Ta on the tensile strength and 
plasticity in PM superalloys were discussed. 
1. Introduction 
Since the service environment of an aero-engine is complex and 
harsh, the performance requirements for Ni-based alloys used in turbine 
disks are strict [ 1 – 3 ]. The advantage of the powder metallurgy (PM) 
process is that it can yield alloys with high alloying degrees and ho -
mogeneous microstructures PM superalloys have received reasonable 
attention from engine designers over the past half-century. The disks 
require materials with the highest possible tensile strengths, fatigue 
properties and creep resistances. However, there are essential differ -
ences in the mechanical deformation mechanisms. The creep deforma -
tion tends to be the low-rate plastic deformation below the yield stress. 
In contrast, a tensile test achieves faster plastic deformation at a constant 
strain rate. The tensile deformation behaviors of some commercial single 
crystal, cast and wrought (C & W) superalloys have been reported [ 4 – 9 ]. 
The temperature greatly affects the tensile strength and dislocation 
motion. For example, Sengupta [ 8 ] reported the relationship between 
the strength and temperature of CMSX-4 from room temperature to 
1100 
◦
C. This alloy reached the strength extreme at 800 
◦
C, then the 
strength decreased with increasing temperature. The author attributed 
this result to the dislocation climbing and stacking fault increase due to 
the thermal activation at high temperatures, but the deformed sub -
structure was not observed and analyzed. In contrast, Zhang et al. [ 4 , 5 , 
10 – 13 ] performed detailed work on the tensile plastic deformation of 
single crystal and directional solidification alloys at various tempera -
tures. It was generally observed that the individual a/2 < 110 > disloca -
tions began to dissociate at the γ / γ
′
interface, resulting in isolated 
superlattice stacking fault (SSF) shearing [ 5 , 10 , 11 , 13 ] at room tem -
perature, but not at 650 
◦
C [ 4 ]. Furthermore, the extended stacking 
faults (ESFs) were observed from room temperature to 750 
◦
C. Hence, 
temperature assistance is not necessary for the formation of ESFs [ 12 ]. 
Ding et al. [ 14 ] found the SSF + APB + SSF configuration in the γ
′
precipitates at room temperature, and Kear-Wilsdorf locks appeared at 
medium temperature (T ≤ 750 
◦
C). In polycrystalline superalloys, grain 
boundaries are one of the factors affecting the tensile strength [ 15 , 16 ]. 
For example, Han et al. [ 17 ] suggested that M

at the grain boundaries to change the tensile strength in the 
solution-strengthened GH3535 alloy (without γ

4. Discussion 
4.1. Relationship between temperature and deformation mechanism 
For PM superalloys, the tensile strength depends on many factors, 
including grain boundaries, PPBs, γ -matrix and γ
′
phases. As the tensile 
temperature increased, the grain boundary strengthening could not be a 
reliable strengthening mode because it was limited by the equicohesive 
temperature [ 36 ]. Furthermore, the PPB structure was essentially 
eliminated in Ta-containing alloys [ 31 ]. Therefore, discussions on the 
deformation mechanism of the experimental alloys should focus on the 
synergistic deformation of the γ and γ
′
phases. There was a cubic-cubic 
coherent orientation relationship between the γ -matrix with the FCC 
crystal structure and the γ
′
phase with the L1
2 
crystal structure. This 
coherent relationship made it possible for plastic deformation to occur 
simultaneously between the two phases. The various complex crystal 
defects became the main reason for the strengthening and weakening of 
the alloys due to the γ
′
phase with a strong degree of chemical order. The 
tertiary γ
′
precipitates undoubtedly contributed greatly to the yield 
strength, since matrix dislocations needed to cut them in pairs first. Also, 
more importantly, for PM superalloys, the complicated morphological 
evolution and size distribution of secondary γ
′
precipitates appeared to 
dynamiclly change during the deformation process, which is an addi -
tional concern. It should be emphasized that although the grain 
boundary strengthening was weakened at high temperatures, its 
contribution still indirectly affected the deformation behavior within the 
grains [ 37 ]. Hence, the deformation mechanism of the PM superalloy 
was slightly different from that of the SX superalloy. This section mainly 
discusses the commonalities of the mechanisms in the Ta-containing 
alloys. Large-area EBSD scans were performed near the longitudinal 
section of the fracture, and it was found that the {111} < 1 1 0 > and 
{111} < 11 2 > slip systems formed with a high fraction of high Schmid 
factors (0.45 – 0.5) in the fractured specimens from RT to 815 
◦
C (refer to 
Supplementary file Fig. S2 ). During the initial plastic deformation at RT, 
the coupled movement of paired a/2 < 110 > dislocations occurred 
within the γ -channel. This suggested that a pervasive APB shearing event 
occurred for the tertiary γ
′
precipitates within the channel [ 38 , 39 ]. The 
dislocation pairs need to first cut through the fine tertiary γ
′
precipitates 
before continuing to move in the channel. Hence, the APB shearing on 
the {111} plane of the tertiary γ
′
precipitates was the main contribution 
to the critical resolved shear stress. Subsequently, the coupled 
a/2 < 110 > dislocations were concentrated at the secondary γ
′
/ γ 
interface, and their sliding and interactions were strongly hindered by 
the γ / γ
′
mismatch strain field. While these two processes worked 
together, the alloy continued to undergo work hardening. As the tensile 
deformation continued, numerous a/2 < 110 > dislocation pairs in the 
matrix cut through the secondary γ
′
precipitates. This indicated that 
most of the secondary γ
′
precipitates participated in the plastic defor -
mation, which manifested as the microscopic morphology being elon -
gated in the direction of the {111} < 1 1 0 > slip band (refer to Fig. 7 ). In 
addition, it is worth discussing the ESFs, because they had already 
formed in the initial plastic deformation and could even thicken into 
microtwins at RT. ESFs, whose formation mechanism is still controver -
sial, are widely found in polycrystalline superalloys after creep defor -
mation [ 40 – 46 ]. It seems unlikely that a/6 < 112 > partial shearing 
occurred in terms of the energy and crystallography, as it would lead to 
the fault of the L1
2 
structure [ 45 ], and the CSF had the highest energy of 
the planar defects in the γ
′
precipitates [ 47 ]. However, numerous reports 
[ 41 , 48 , 49 ] confirmed that CSF shearing is the essence of microtwin 
formation. The recent theory [ 50 ] states that the identical a/6 < 112 >
dislocations propagate through the γ
′
precipitates on consecutive {111} 
planes to form extended superlattice extrinsic stacking faults (SESFs), 
and then they continue this process to form microtwins. The most 
important claim for this shear process is that the diffusion-controlled 
reordering is performed at these erroneous Al – Al nearest neighbors, 
allowing L1
2 
to recover to eliminate pseudotwins. Unocic [ 51 ] suggested 
that the reordering is related to thermal activation during creep. How -
ever, this twin formation mechanism has been observed in experimental 
alloys at the initial plastic deformation of the RT tensile process, which 
has rarely reported in PM superalloys.

temperatures 
In the above discussion, the changes of the tensile plastic deforma -
tion mechanism for PM superalloys with Ta in the possible service 
temperature range have been summarized in detail. The temperatures 
significantly affected the deformation behaviors of the PM superalloy 
disks. Further understanding of these mechanisms and the alloying 
element effects is critical for the development of next-generation PM 
superalloys. Fig. 14 summarizes the main mechanisms in the current 
alloys. It is worth noting that ESF shearing occurred during the tensile

dislocations. If the number and widths of the ESFs could be controlled by 
the chemical composition and the pre-deformation, it is also expected to 
achieve the tensile strengthening. 
5. Conclusions 
In this paper, the effect of temperature on the tensile properties of 
superalloys with Ta addition is evaluated and discussed in detail. The 
following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) In the studied temperature range (room temperature to 815 
◦
C), 
the main active dislocations in the γ matrix were a/2 < 110 >
dislocations. When the tensile temperatures rose above 750 
◦
C, it 
was also found that isolated stacking fault shear formed by the 
dissociated a/6 < 112 > partial dislocations in the γ -matrix chan -
nel, which means that the open {111} < 112 > slip system in the 
γ -matrix was temperature dependent. 
(2) The deformation mechanisms of γ
′
precipitates were closely 
related to the temperature. With the increase in the temperature, 
the patterns of dislocation shearing through the γ
′
precipitates 
gradually transformed from APB-coupled dislocations shearing to 
isolated SSF shearing, and finally, dislocation climb and cross-slip 
occurred at higher temperatures. ESFs across the whole grain 
appeared frequently over the entire temperature range, which 
was the source of the microtwins and gradually thickened with 
increasing temperature. The thermal activation caused the 
thickening of microtwins rather than being a necessary condition 
for their formation. 
(3) Regarding the formation mechanism of ESFs, in addition to the 
traditional belief that the same a/6 < 112 > partials sheared on 
adjacent {111} planes, we also found a new formation mecha -
nism, namely, two a/6 < 112 > dislocations sheared on the (111) 
plane separated by one atomic layer. 
(4) Ta addition to the alloy did not significantly alter the main tensile 
mechanism. However, Ta additive significantly improved the 
tensile strength over the entire test temperature range but 
reduced the morphological stability of the secondary γ
′
phase. For 
the experimental PM superalloy system, the appropriate Ta con -
tent could ensure the enhancement of the high temperature 
strength and ductility.
