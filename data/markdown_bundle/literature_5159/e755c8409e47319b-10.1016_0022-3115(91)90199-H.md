Modified V-Ti-Cr-Si type alloys containing Al and Y were prepared in order to improve the oxidation resistance for 
fusion reactor applications. The fabrication procedure and the condition of heat treatment were determined for two alloys 
referred as V-STi-5Cr- and V-25Ti-15Cr-. Mechanical properties were measured using miniature-type tensile specimens at 
temperatures from 300 to 1273 K. The alloy V-STi-SCr- showed the same level of strength and ductility as a reference alloy 
of V-15Cr-5Ti and the alloy V-25Ti-15Cr- was twice as strong as the reference alloy at temperatures from 300 to 873 K. 
Dynamic strain aging and work softening phenomena with large elongation were observed at intermediate and high 
temperature regions, respectively. 
1. Introduction 
Selected vanadium alloys are candidate structural 
materials for fusion reactors because of their good 
mechanical properties and low induced radioactivity. 
Several alloys including V-lSCr-5Ti and V-3Ti-1Si 
have been developed and their mechanical properties at 
elevated temperatures were better than those of 
austenitic and ferritic steels [l]. Problems such as less 
oxidation resistance and high hydrogen permeability are 
yet to be solved. Although additions of Ti and Cr 
improved oxidation resistance of vanadium alloys at 
low oxygen pressure [2-41, their oxidation resistance at 
higher oxygen pressure at high temperatures must be 
improved. Additions of Al and Y are expected to con- 
tribute to the formation of a protective oxide layer, 
since these metals have a larger affinity for oxygen than 
Ti [5,6]. In order to solve the problem of high hydrogen 
permeability, surface. treatments using bonding or coat- 
ing techniques as well as alloying must become effec- 
tive. 
The induced activity of alloying elements must be 
considered to maintain low activation. According to 
calculations, alloying with unlimited amounts of the 
elements, Ti, Cr, Si and Y satisfy Class C limits of 
lOCFR61 (Part 61 of Title 10 of Code of Federal 
Regulations). In the case of Al, if the neutron fluence is 
less than 1 MW y m -’ it is classified as a low activation 
materials. But at an exposure higher than 10 MW y 
mm2 the formation of 26A1 can not be ignored. The 
acceptable concentration of Al is less than 10% [7]. 
We prepared two vanadium alloys as low activation 
structural materials, containing Ti, Cr and Si for high 
temperature strength and oxidation resistance and Y 
and Al for further oxidation resistance. In this paper, 
the fabrication procedure, heat treatment condition and 
tensile behavior at high temperatures of miniature-type 
specimens are presented. Experiments on oxidation, 
bonding and irradiation behavior of these alloys are 
now in progress. Results of bonding between vanadium 
alloys and alumina were presented elsewhere [8].

3. Results and discussion 
The temperatures, at which full recrystallization ap- 
peared in the optical microstructures, were found to be 
1373, 1273, and 1173 K for V-25Ti-15Cr-, V-STi- 
5Cr- and pure V, respectively. The alloy specimens for 
tensile tests were annealed at 1373 K for 3.6 ks to 
obtain a grain size of 20 pm. Specimens of pure V were 
annealed at 1173 K for 1.8 ks to obtain a grain size of 
30 pm. In fig. 2, the hardness of pure V decreases above 
an annealing temperature of 1273 K while those of 
alloys increases. The reason may be that in pure V 
interstitial impurities such as oxygen and carbon are 
reduced by the chemical gettering effects of zirconium 
foil [9], but in the alloys internal oxidation with Ti, Al 
and Y occurred at high temperatures above 1273 K [lo].

V-25Ti-15Cr- shows higher strength levels in compari-

K, work hardening was larger than at other tempera-

was observed. At 1273 K, a kind of work softening was 
observed and the total elongation became very large. 
Test temperature dependence of tensile properties is 
shown in fig. 5, where data of pure V and V-15Cr-5Ti 
are also shown in comparison. The 0.2% proof stress of 
pure V has a peak around 673 K where dynamic strain 
aging occurred. In the case of alloys, these peaks move 
to higher temperature, that is 873 K. This might be 
caused by interaction between the substitutional solute 
atoms and interstitial atoms such as oxygen and carbon.

Fig. Sb shows that the strength does not vary appre- 
ciably between 300 K and 873 K. Alloy V-25Ti-15Cr- 
is twice as strong and V-STi-SCr- is as strong as 
V-15Cr-5Ti. The total elongations below 573 K of 
V-25Ti-15Cr- specimens were small compared with 
those of V-STi-SCr-. The elongation changed gradu- 
ally from 300 to 973 K, but above 1073 K it increased 
rapidly with test temperatures. At high temperatures all 
of specimens indicated ductile fracture surfaces. But 
alloy V-25Ti-15Cr- indicated a rather brittle fracture 
mode at 300 K, where transgranular cleavage with river 
patterns and intergranular separation were observed. 
Alloy V-STi-SCr- showed a uniform dimple rupture 
even at room temperature as shown in fig. 6. 
Flow stress in the two alloys V-25Ti-15Cr- and 
V-STi-SCr- were compared with the data of binary 
vanadium alloys [ll]. The values of proof stress of 
V-25Ti-15Cr- and V-STi-SCr- were estimated to be 
670 and 360 MPa respectively, assuming the additive 
contribution of alloying elements. Actually V-25Ti- 
15Cr- and V-STi-SCr- show the proof stress of 950 
and 530 MPa, respectively. Therefore, the strengthening 
can not be interpreted as a simple solid-solution mecha- 
nism. Dynamic strain aging at an intermediate tempera- 
ture region might be attributed to the interaction be- 
tween dislocations and interstitial solute atoms [12]. 
Work softening phenomena and recovery of ductility at 
high temperatures might be attributed to the interaction 
between dislocations and substitutional solute atoms 
[13]. Deformation mechanisms based on the dislocation 
microstructures will be studied elsewhere. Alloy V- 
25Ti-15Cr- is hard compared with typical vanadium 
alloys, such as V-15Cr-5Ti and V-3Ti-1Si [ll]. Con- 
sidering radiation embrittlement in fusion conditions, 
however, the low temperature elongation may not be 
high enough. Alloy V-STi-SCr- shows the same level 
of strength and ductility as V-15Cr-5Ti. It was re- 
ported that V-15Cr-5Ti was subjected to unacceptable 
losses in ductility due to neutron irradiation and hydro- 
gen pick-up and this embrittlement might be related to 
the high amount of Cr [14]. From this point of view, 
V-STi-SCr- is expected to improve embrittlement re- 
sistance. Further optimization of alloying based on V- 
5Ti-5Cr- must be carried out.

4. Conclusions 
(1) The recrystallized behavior of the alloys is differ- 
ent from that of pure V, that is, hardening occurs above 
recrystallization temperature in the alloys, while soften- 
ing occurs in pure V. 
(2) The stress-strain curves of the two alloys are 
characterized with dynamic strain aging at intermediate 
temperatures and work softening phenomena and large 
elongation at high temperatures. 
(3) The tensile strength of V-STi-SCr- is the same 
as that of V-15Cr-5Ti and that of V-25Ti-15Cr- is 
twice that of reference alloy. Alloy V-STi-SCr- shows 
ductile fracture in the temperature range 300 to 1273 K, 
but V-25Ti-15Cr- shows cleavage and intergranular 
fracture at lower temperature.
