The mechanical properties ofγ′-strengthened Co–Ni–Al–W–Cr model superalloys extending from pure Ni-base to
pure Co-base superalloys have been assessed. Differential scanning calorimetry measurements and thermody-
namic calculations match well and show that the γ′ solvus temperature decreases with increasing Co-content.
The γ/γ′ lattice misﬁt is negative on the Ni- and positive on the Co-rich side. High Ni-contents decelerate the
oxidation kinetics up to a factor of 15. The creep strength of the Ni-base alloy increases by an order of magnitude
with additions of Co before it deteriorates strongly upon higher additions despite an increasingγ′ volume fraction.

The results of calculations and experiments qualitatively match
quite well: The γ′ solvus temperature decreases with increasing Co
concentration, whereas solidus and liquidus temperatures are hardly af-
fected. TCNI6 predicts a minimum of solidus and liquidus temperatures
in the intermediate composition range, which was not observed in the
experiments. The difference of experimental and calculated values for
the γ′ solvus temperature (ΔT

which was created for the description of Ni-base superalloys, ﬁts bet-
ter for the Co-rich side of the system. Maybe this is because the high
W-content of 8 at.% (≈ 22.5 wt. %) is outside of the composition range
of the established Ni-base alloys.

However this is only one aspect that changes in the present alloys.
With increasing Co-content, also the partitioning behaviour of all the
elements changes. As mentioned previously, in Ni-base alloys W is
typically slightly enriched in the γ matrix, where it can act as an effec-
tive solid solution hardening element. When the Co-content in the
present alloy system is continuously increased, W will tend more and
more to distribute to the γ′ precipitates and thus weaken solid solution
strengthening in the matrix channels. This correlates with the observed
decrease of creep performance with increasing Co. Another aspect
inﬂuencing the high temperature strength is the γ/γ′ lattice mis ﬁt.
The sign of the lattice mis ﬁt changes from negative to positive values
in the present alloys, which reverses the internal stress states at the
channel/precipitate interfaces and should therefore have an in ﬂuence
on the mechanical properties. However the question, which sign and
magnitude of the lattice misﬁtw o u l db eb e n eﬁcial for creep properties
remains unclariﬁed. In a recent publication, Mughrabi concludes that
a positive mis ﬁt might be bene ﬁcial for the high temperature creep
performance [15]. The dramatic change in the creep rate could also be
caused by a change in the deformation mechanisms. It has already
been observed, that Co-base and intermediate Co/Ni-base alloys deform
differently, because the planar fault energies, e.g. superlattice intrinsic
stacking fault (SISF) energy or antiphase boundary (APB) energy, of
the γ′ phase change[11]. If the planar fault energies were lower, precip-
itates could be sheared more easily by dislocations. However Titus et al.
come to the conclusion that both SISF and APB energies increase from
the intermediate CoNi alloys to the pure Co-base alloys, which would
lead to an increase of creep performance in that direction instead of
the decrease, which was observed here. The stacking fault energy
(SFE) of the matrix phase is also of great importance. If the deformation
concentrates in theγ matrix channels, where the dislocation movement
happens by a combined glide and climb process, a low SFE would be
beneﬁcial, as recombination of partial dislocations would be impeded.
More detailed studies are necessary to fully understand the dramatic
change of creep properties.

Summarising, it can be stated that several properties of the model
superalloys under investigation vary over a large range when the
Ni/Co-ratio changes. Increasing the Co-content leaves the solidus and
liquidus temperatures of the system unaffected, but decreases the γ′
solvus temperature. The γ′ volume fraction is higher on the Co-rich
side, but further intermetallic phases form, when no Ni is present in
the alloy. The γ/γ′ lattice mis ﬁt is negative in the Ni-rich alloys and
shifts to positive values when the Co-content increases. The creep
strength shows a maximum at an intermediate composition around
18.8 at.% Co before it deteriorates strongly with a further increase in
Co. The oxidation resistance diminishes, when the Co-content increases.
