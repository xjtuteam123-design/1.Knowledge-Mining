ABSTRACT
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) show good mechanical properties like high strength, hardness and
wear resistance, and this makes them attractive for application in diamond tools. However,
there are severe obstacles to improving the mechanical properties of normal diamond tools
with metal-matrix alloys. Here, we report the formation of FeCoCrNiMo HEAs by powder
metallurgy processing, starting from a gas-atomised powder followed by mechanical milling
and consolidation by spark plasma sintering. Our results show that FeCoCrNiMo HEAs with
pure FCC phase can be obtained by this method. Specimens with higher hardness can be
obtained at higher temperatures and low pressures, while specimens with higher bending
strength are formed at lower temperatures and pressures. FeCoCrNiMo HEAs have
remarkably higher hardness and better wear behaviour than metal-matrices commonly in
used diamond tools, which indicates that a new type of diamond tool with superior
properties can be realised from HEAs.

1. Introduction
In recent times, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have
received great attention due to their unique micro-
structure and properties, which can be tailored to
specific applications [1– 3]. HEAs are composed usually
of five or more principal elements in equal or near-
equal atomic ratios and their configuration entropy
of mixing could reach a maximum in the solid-solution
phase [ 4]. HEA solid-solutions that crystallize in a
single phase with body-centred-cubic or face-centred-
cubic (FCC) crystal structure have novel properties;
however, these properties cannot be obtained when
using a conventional micro alloying approach based
on a single major element for the preparation of
HEAs [4].
Different techniques such as arc melting and pow-
der metallurgy (PM) have been adopted for the pro-
duction of HEAs; arc melting is popularly used due
to its easy availability and low cost. However, due to
the complex chemical composition of HEAs, it is
often difficult to avoid chemical segregation and evap-
oration of alloying elements with lower melting points
[5]. In PM processing, metals are first mechanically
alloyed, after which, the alloy is consolidated by hot-
pressing [ 6], hot-isostatic pressing [ 7,8] or spark
plasma sintering (SPS) [ 9,10]. In an HEA such as
CuNiCoZnAlTi, the compressive strength can be as
high as 2.76 GPa and the obtained hardness value of
8.79 GPa is higher than that of most commercial
hard-facing alloys [ 7]. Recently, in our study on the
CrMnFeCoNi HEA system [ 11], we used gas-atomised
powder as the starting material due to its higher purity,
as well as its homogeneous composition and mor-
phology as compared to a mechanically alloyed pow-
der. After further milling and consolidation of the
gas-atomised powder using SPS, nanocrystalline
CrMnFeCoNi HEAs with a high tensile strength of
around 1000 MPa and high ductility can be obtained.
Owing to their extraordinary properties, HEAs have
promising applications as components in structural
and functional materials. For example, CrMnFeCoNi
HEA shows excellent mechanical properties even at
very low temperatures and, therefore, this alloy is
highly suitable for cryogenic applications [ 2]. CuCoNi-
CrAl0.5FeB1.0 HEA has excellent wear behaviour,
which is superior even to SUJ2 wear-resistant steel,
making it suitable to be applied in ambient- and
high-temperature moulds, tools, and structural
materials [ 12]. A particularly promising application
of HEAs is in diamond tools. The metal-matrix (bond-
ing phase) in traditional diamond tools is usually
produced by consolidating a pure metal powder or a
pre-alloyed powder containing Cu, Sn, Co, Fe and
Mn, among others [ 13]. However, the wear resistance,
hardness and strength of these alloys are much lower
than those of diamond grains, and this reduces the
performance of the diamond tool and limits its appli-
cation [ 14]. It is expected that by replacing the soft

In this study, Fe 24.1Co24.1Cr24.1Ni24.1Mo3.6 HEAs
were prepared using the PM route. The pre-alloyed
powder produced by gas atomisation was mechanically
milled and consolidated by SPS under different temp-
eratures and pressures. The influence of sintering par-
ameters on the microstructure and mechanical
properties of the resulting alloy was studied. It was
found that both hardness and wear resistance behav-
iour were superior to those obtained for metal-matrices
in commonly used diamond tools. These findings are
expected to provide a strong technical base, from
which, potential applications of these diamond tools
can be developed.
2. Experimental
The powder with a nominal composition Fe 24.1Co24.1-
Cr24.1Ni24.1Mo3.6 (all compositions are in atomic per
cent (at.-%) throughout the article) was prepared by
gas atomisation. More details on powder production
can be found in previous works [ 11,15]. Next, the pow-
der with a particle size under 200 mesh was mechani-
cally milled using conventional planetary milling
equipment. The weight ratio between the powder and
the stainless-steel balls was 1:10 and ethanol was
added as the milling medium. The milling time was
20 h and the milling speed was 300 rev min
– 1.
SPS was used to consolidate the milled powder; SPS
was carried out in an HPD 25/3 SPS equipment under
reduced pressure (10
−3 Pa). The mechanically alloyed
powder was added into a graphite die of 40 mm diam-
eter and sintered at different temperatures (1000, 1050
or 1100°C) and pressures (30, 35 or 40 MPa) for 480 s.
Finally, consolidated billets were quenched in water to
room temperature.
The microstructure and chemical compositions of
the gas-atomised powder and the sintered HEAs alloys
were investigated by FEI Quanta FEG 250 scanning
electron microscope (SEM) equipped with an Energy
Dispersive X-ray analyzer. X-ray diffractometer
(XRD, Rigaku D/MAX-2550 VB+ 18 Kw) with Cu Ka
radiation was used to characterise the crystallographic
phase contents of the powders and sintered samples.
The real density of the sintered samples was measured
by Archimedes method; reported density values are
averages of three measurements. The hardness values
of the alloys were determined by Buehler 5104 hard-
ness tester under a load of 200 g for 15 s and each
value was averaged from six measurements. The bend-
ing strength of the samples (size: 30 × 30 × 30 mm
3)
was determined by Instron 3369 mechanical testing
facility using the three-point method, and wear tests
were performed using ball-on-disc HSR-2M equip-
ment. The testing parameters for the wear tests are as
follows: load 50 N, f 15 Hz (900 rev min
– 1), friction
distance 5 mm, and friction time 5 min. The weight
of the test samples before and after wearing was
measured and the wear mass loss was calculated. To
compare the wear behaviour of our samples with that
of the metal-matrix of commercial diamond tools, a
reference specimen with code HFXP-58 (a metal-
matrix of the diamond tool that has the best perform-
ance available in the Chinese market currently) was
tested under the same conditions.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure

was measured to be Fe 25.7Co22.74 Cr23.99Ni23.23Mo4.34,
which is close to the nominal composition. The gas-
atomised powder has a spherical or near-spherical
morphology with some satellite structure, as shown
in Figure 1(a). The formation of this satellite structure
has been discussed in previous reports [ 11] and has
been attributed to the adhesion of smaller melt droplets
with a faster cooling rate onto larger droplets during
solidification. The higher magnification image in

As discussed in earlier reports [ 11], mechanical
milling can be used to further refine the microstructure
of the gas-atomised powder and activate the sintering

shows that both gas-atomised and milled powders

the milled powders are larger due to grain refinement
and an increase in the lattice strain caused by milling.
Additionally, it is found that the peak positions are
shifted to lower diffraction angles (by around 2°) for
the milled powder, indicating an increase in lattice
parameter.
Figure 3 shows the particle size distribution of the
gas-atomised and milled FeCoCrNiMo HEA powders.
It is found that after milling, the fraction of small-sized
particles (<10 μm) increases but this increase is more
pronounced for the powder with larger particle sizes.
Thus, after mechanical milling, the particle size distri-
bution becomes more uniform in both the powders.
The density of the FeCoCrNiMo HEA after sinter-
ing at different temperatures and pressures is plotted
as a function of the sintering temperature in Figure 4.
For all sintering pressures, the density increases with
temperature. For the same sintering temperature, a
higher density can be achieved under a higher pressure.
Additionally, it is found that when increasing pressure
from 30 to 40 MPa, the increase in density ( Δg) at 1000
and 1050°C (around 0.1 g/cm
3) is higher than that at
1100°C (around 0.06 g/cm 3). Similarly, when increas-
ing temperature from 1000 to 1100°C, Δg is around
0.7, 0.5 and 0.3 g/cm 3 at pressures of 30, 35 and
40 MPa, respectively. Thus, the density increase is
less pronounced when increasing the pressure at a
higher sintering temperature or when increasing the
temperature at a higher sintering pressure. The highest
density of about 8.19 g/cm
3, which is very close to the
theoretical density of 8.286 g/cm 3, can be obtained by
sintering at 1100°C under 40 MPa. The increased den-
sity can be explained from the observation that at
higher temperatures and pressures, atom diffusion is
more intense, which promotes the sintering process
resulting in higher density. Thus, to get fully densified
specimens and remove porosity, a higher temperature
and/or higher pressure and/or longer holding time
may be necessary.
The phase constitution of the alloys after sintering at
all pressures and temperatures was investigated by
XRD, as shown in Figure 5 (a). All the sintered speci-
mens show the pure FCC structure similar to the gas-
atomised and milled powders, and no reflections
from other phases, such as intermetallic brittle phases,
are detected. A Cr-rich σ intermetallic phase has been
reported in HEA systems with a high content of Cr
[16,17], which has a deleterious effect on mechanical
properties, especially ductility [ 15]. In this study, by
changing sintering parameters, i.e. the temperature
and pressure, the alloy retains the pure FCC phase

3.2. Mechanical properties
The Vickers hardness values of sintered FeCoCrNiMo
HEAs as a function of temperature and pressure are
shown in Figure 6 . It is clearly seen that under the
same sintering pressure, the hardness increases with
sintering temperature. The hardness is around 350 –
375 at 1000°C, but increases to around 460 – 520 at
1100°C. At sintering temperatures of 1000 and 1050°
C, the hardness increases with pressure, whereas, at
1100°C, it shows an opposite behaviour and decreases
with pressure. The hardness can be influenced by den-
sity, phase constitution and grain size, among other
factors. It is seen that the single FCC phase is retained
after sintering (Figure 5). By increasing the density, the
hardness can be improved; however, at a higher temp-
erature, a lower hardness could result from a relatively
coarser grain size [18]. The grain size of the sample sin-
tered at 30 MPa and 1000°C is around 300 nm [ 11] and
increases to around 500 nm in the sample sintered at
1100°C with the same pressure. For a sintering temp-
erature of 1100°C, although the density increases
with pressure, the grain size of the HEAs decreases
with pressure. It increases to about 700 nm when sin-
tering at 40 MPa.
In Table 2, the hardness values of metal-matrices of
commonly used diamond tool materials [19] are shown
and compared with that of FeCoCrNiMo HEA. It is
clearly seen that FeCoCrNiMo HEA is around 2 – 4
times harder than the metal-matrices used as diamond
tools.

4. Conclusions
In this study, the solid-solution phase of FeCoCrNiMo
HEAs with high hardness and good wear resistance was
successfully produced by PM technique. With increas-
ing sintering temperature and pressure, a greater
specimen densification is observed. However, the indi-
vidual influences of the temperature and pressure on
the hardness, bending strength and wear resistance
are different. Specimens with a higher hardness can
be obtained at a higher temperature but lower pressure,
while a higher bending strength can be obtained at a
lower temperature and pressure. Variation of the temp-
erature and pressure has no obvious influence on the
wear behaviour of the FeCoCrNiMo HEAs. Thus,
when choosing sintering parameters, a balance must
be found to achieve optimised properties of the alloys.
The outstanding mechanical properties including high
hardness and wear resistance of the FeCoCrNiMo
HEAs as compared to those of the metal-matrix of a
commercial diamond tool suggest that new high-per-
formance diamond tools with superior properties can
be expected in the future.
