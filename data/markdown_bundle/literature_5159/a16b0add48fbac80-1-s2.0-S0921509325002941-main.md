ABSTRACT
High-temperature titanium alloys are ideal for extreme environments due to their lightweight and high strength 
at elevated temperatures. Laser direct energy deposition (L-DED) enables the production of high-performance, 
complex high-temperature titanium alloy components, offering significant potential in the aerospace sector. 
However, the significant anisotropy in the deposit, caused by the presence of epitaxially grown columnar β 
grains, severely restricts its further application. Although isotropy has been achieved in L-DED Ti or Ti6Al4V 
using eutectoid elements, the high-temperature service conditions limit their applicability. This study used a 
synergistic alloying modification of Si and B to promote the columnar to equiaxed transition (CET) of β grains in 
L-DED near- α titanium alloy Ti6242S. The effects of Si and B alloying on microstructure evolution and both 
room- and high-temperature mechanical properties were analyzed. Si and B exhibit a notable mutual replace -
ment effect in promoting CET. The synergistic modification of Si and B effectively prevents the formation of 
silicide and TiB by reducing their individual contents. Fully equiaxed β grains were achieved in L-DED Ti6242S- 
0.16Si-0.05B alloy deposit, which exhibited a high yield strength of approximately 900 MPa and 13 % elongation 
at room temperature. The high-temperature yield strength at 480 
◦
C reached 590 MPa, and the step-creep 
rupture life increased significantly for the Ti6242S-0.16Si-0.05B alloy deposit at 525 
◦
C under 480 MPa – 680 
MPa.
1. Introduction
Near- α high-temperature titanium alloys exhibit excellent mechani -
cal properties and oxidation resistance at elevated temperatures [ 1 ], 
making them ideal for critical aerospace components such as bulkheads, 
nacelle center-beam frames, pressurized engine disks, and rotor blades 
[ 2 – 4 ]. The rapid advancement of additive manufacturing has estab -
lished laser direct energy deposition (L-DED) as a key technology for 
producing high-performance, complex titanium alloy parts, with me -
chanical properties comparable to those of forged components [ 5 ]. 
Additionally, L-DED can reduce the weight of high-temperature aero -
space components, enhancing the buy-to-fly ratio, lowering fuel con -
sumption, and reducing harmful gas emissions. Boeing and GE have 
successfully developed and integrated L-DED titanium alloy parts into 
both military and commercial aircraft [ 2 ]. Moreover, the L-DED 
technique enables in-situ alloying during the deposition process, facili -
tating the rapid alloy design and development of new titanium alloys 
[ 6 ].
Although traditional near- α titanium alloys, such as Ti6242S, 
IMI834, and Ti1100, are known for their superior high-temperature 
mechanical properties and good oxidation resistance [ 7 ], the mechani -
cal properties of L-DED near- α titanium alloys are influenced by 
epitaxially grown columnar β grains and the associated long straight 
grain boundary α phases in the deposit. These features lead to reduced 
and anisotropic mechanical properties, including room-temperature 
tensile properties [ 8 , 9 ], creep resistance [ 10 ], high-temperature fa -
tigue properties [ 11 ], and high-temperature oxidation resistance [ 12 ]. 
This limits the broader applications of L-DED traditional near- α titanium 
alloy. Therefore, achieving fully equiaxed β grains in the deposit is 
essential for the design and development of L-DED near- α titanium alloy.

To achieve the superior and isotropic mechanical properties in L- 
DED titanium alloys, suitable alloying modifications to traditional tita -
nium alloys have garnered increasing attention [ 13 ]. To date, a series of 
eutectoid β -stabilizing elements, such as Cu, Fe, Ni etc., had been 
introduced to modify L-DED Ti and Ti6Al4V titanium alloys [ 14 – 17 ]. 
This modification successfully results in fully equiaxed β grains in the 
deposits, elimination anisotropies in mechanical properties. However, 
for near- α high-temperature titanium alloys, the content of eutectoid 
β -stabilizing elements (e.g., Ni, Fe, Cu) is strictly limited due to their 
detrimental effects on high-temperature creep resistance [ 18 , 19 ]. Apart 
from these eutectoid β -stabilizing elements, silicon and boron are among 
the few selectable elements that exhibit a constitutional undercooling 
effect [ 20 ], and can be used to modify near- α titanium alloys [ 7 , 21 ].
Most previous research on the alloying modification of Si and B el -
ements has focused on the L-DED CP Ti and Ti6Al4V alloys, with limited 
studies on L-DED near- α high-temperature titanium alloys. Xue et al. 
[ 22 ] introduced trace amounts of B (up to 0.25 wt%) into L-DED 
Ti6Al4V and observed that B was distributed along the grain boundaries 
as needle-like TiB. As the B content increased, the size of the columnar β 
grains was significantly refined, and the grain morphology gradually 
transitioned to a partially equiaxed structure, resulting in a mixture of 
equiaxed and columnar β grains. However, the elongation progressively 
deteriorated. They found that 0.08 wt% B alloying resulted in an optimal 
combination of strength and elongation in L-DED-processed Ti6Al4V. 
Zhang et al. [ 23 ] added 0.05 – 1 wt% B to L-DED Ti6Al4V. They observed 
that the columnar β grains were significantly refined with increase of B 
content, and nearly fully equiaxed β grains were obtained. However, 
excessive precipitation of TiB at the grain boundaries led to a significant 
deterioration of the plasticity. Mereddy et al. [ 24 ] added 0.75 wt% Si to 
WAAM CP Ti, resulting in a mixture of equiaxed β grains and refined 
columnar β grains in the deposit. Chen et al. [ 25 ] added 0.1 wt% Si to 
WAAM Ti6Al4V alloy, refining columnar β grain refinement but without 
promoting the formation of equiaxed β grains.
Previous studies consistently show that both Si and B elements pro -
mote the columnar-to-equiaxed transition (CET) in titanium alloys. 
While higher concentrations of Si or B accelerate this transition, exces -
sive amounts may negatively affect the mechanical properties. Given the 
synergistic effect of Ni and B in promoting a fully equiaxed micro -
structure in laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) CP Ti by generating suitable 
constitutional undercooling in the molten pool [ 26 ], it is expected that 
the simultaneous alloying of Si and B elements will facilitate CET in 
near- α high-temperature titanium alloy deposits, while reducing the 
content of each element, thus improving the balance of strength and 
plasticity.
This study uses the near- α high-temperature titanium alloy Ti6242S 
(Ti-6Al-2Sn-4Zr-2Mo-0.1Si) as the matrix and introduces a novel strat -
egy involving in-situ synergistic alloying with Si and B elements to 
control grain morphology evolution and second phase precipitation 
behavior. Based on phase diagram calculation and mechanical property 
analysis, this work contributes to the development of a novel L-DED 
near- α high-temperature titanium alloy with both isotropic and 
advanced room- and high-temperature mechanical properties.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructures
Fig. 3 illustrates the morphology and size of β grains in the L-DED 
Ti6242S- x Si- y B specimens with varying Si and B contents. The L-DED 
Ti6242S specimen exhibits millimeter-sized columnar β grains, growing 
along the building direction ( Fig. 3 a1). As the Si content increases, the 
grain morphology in L-DED Ti6242S- x Si specimens initially changes to a 
distribution of equiaxed grains in the central region and columnar grains 
at the edges of the deposit ( Fig. 3 a2-a3 ). The volume fractions of 
equiaxed grains in the 0.16Si and 0.33Si alloys are 25.7 % and 45.7 %, 
respectively. When the Si content reaches 0.54 wt% (0.54Si), the grain 
morphology becomes fully equiaxed ( Fig. 3 a4). With the addition of 
0.05 wt% B (0.05B), the Ti6242S- x B deposit exhibits a mixture of 
equiaxed and columnar β grains ( Fig. 3 b1), with the volume fractions of 
equiaxed grains being 37.5 %. As B content increases to 0.1 wt%, the as- 
deposited microstructure consists of fully equiaxed β grains ( Fig. 3 b2). 
The synergistic addition of 0.16 wt% Si and 0.05 wt% B (0.16Si0.05B) 
also results in fully equiaxed β grains in the deposit ( Fig. 3 c), demon -
strating that the synergistic addition of low level of Si and B can achieve 
a transition from columnar to fully equiaxed grains in the L-DED 
Ti6242S deposit.
The above results show that the alloying of both Si and B elements 
can refine the grain size to the micrometer scale in the L-DED Ti6242S 
deposit. Alloying with 0.54 wt% Si refines the β grains to about 500 μ m, 
while 0.1 wt% B alloying refines the β grains to less than 100 μ m. The 
synergistic alloying of 0.16 wt% Si and 0.05 wt% B refines β grains to 
about 250 μ m. These results indicate that the B element has a much 
greater grain-refining effect than the Si element. The grain refinement 
mechanism of trace B element, including both solidification grain 
refinement and the inhibition of solid-state grain coarsening, has been 
extensively studied and validated [ 27 – 29 ].

3.3. High-temperature mechanical properties
Longitudinal high-temperature tensile tests at 480 
◦
C were further 
performed on the Ti6242S and 0.16Si0.05B deposits, as shown in Fig. 9 . 
The Ti6242S alloy deposit shows the lowest high-temperature yield and 
ultimate tensile strengths, 498.6 MPa and 660.7 MPa, respectively, but 
the highest elongation of 19.7 %. Compared to the Ti6242S alloy de -
posit, the high-temperature yield and ultimate tensile strength of the 
0.16Si0.05B alloy deposit are significantly improved to 589.9 MPa and 
778 MPa, respectively, while the elongation remains at 17.4 %. This 
demonstrates that the 0.16Si0.05B alloy deposit, with synergistic 
alloying modification, achieves a superior combination of high- 
temperature strength and plasticity.
In comparison to the room-temperature tensile curves, the high- 
temperature tensile curves of the Ti6242S and 0.16Si0.05B alloys 
display distinct serrated features. It is well known that, under specific 
strain rates and temperatures, the stress-strain curves of metallic mate -
rials can exhibit serrated fluctuations, known as the Portevin-Le Cha -
telier (PLC) effect [ 33 ]. Studies have shown that the PLC effect observed 
in high-temperature tensile tests of titanium alloys is caused by inter -
stitial atoms, such as C and N [ 34 ]. Kartik Prasad et al. [ 35 ] proposed 
that Si atoms in the IMI834 alloy trigger the PLC effect during 
high-temperature tensile deformation. In this study, the interstitial Si 
atoms in the as-deposited Ti6242S and 0.16Si0.05B alloys are present in 
a solid solution, which induces the PLC effect and results in the serrated 
fluctuations in the high-temperature tensile curves. The 
high-temperature tensile results, along with the SEM and TEM analyses, 
confirm that Si atoms exist in a solid solution state in the Ti6242S and 
0.16Si0.05B deposits.

In summary, compared to the Ti6242S alloy deposit, the novel L-DED 
high-temperature titanium alloy (0.16Si0.05B) deposit demonstrates 
superior high-temperature microstructure stability, tensile properties 
and durability.

4.3. Influences of Si and B on anisotropies
In the L-DED Ti6242S alloy, coarse columnar grains epitaxially 
grown along the building direction lead to significant anisotropies in 
strength and elongation, with strength anisotropy degree around 9 % 
and elongation anisotropy degree reaching approximately 114 %. In 
contrast, L-DED 0.1B and 0.16Si0.05B alloys, which exhibit ductile 
fractures, form fully equiaxed grains that substantially reduces anisot -
ropies, with strength anisotropy degree not exceeding 5 % and 
elongation anisotropy degree not exceeding 7 %. The generation and 
elimination of strength anisotropy can be explained by the soft/hard 
grain theory [ 57 , 58 ]. In L-DED Ti6242S alloy, the columnar grains 
exhibit a strong crystallographic texture. During loading, the grain 
orientation determines the activation of slip systems. When the crystal 
orientation aligns with the loading direction, the grains adopt a “ soft 
orientation ” , facilitating easier slip and resulting in lower yield strength. 
Conversely, when the crystal orientation is perpendicular to the loading 
direction, the activation of slip systems becomes more difficult, and the 
grains behave as “ hard grains ” , leading to higher yield strength. In the 
0.1B and 0.16Si0.05B alloys with full equiaxed grains, the random dis -
tribution of crystal orientations eliminates the fixed correlation between 
the loading direction and slip system activation. As a result, the pro -
portions of soft and hard grains are consistent in both the longitudinal 
and transverse loading directions. Therefore, the addition of Si and B 
promotes the formation of fully equiaxed grains, eliminating directional 
differences in slip system activation and significantly reducing strength 
anisotropy.
Elongation anisotropy is primarily influenced by differences in grain 
boundary distribution under longitudinal and transverse loading. Grain 
boundary α serves as a relatively weak region, acting as both a site for 
crack initiation and a pathway for crack propagation [ 59 ]. In Ti6242S 
alloys with epitaxially grown columnar grains, a larger proportion of 
grain boundaries are nearly parallel to the transverse loading direction 
and nearly perpendicular to the longitudinal loading direction. Under 
transverse loading, these grain boundaries experience higher stress, 
making cracks more likely to initiate and propagate along them, which 
ultimately reducing elongation. In contrast, for the 0.1B and 
0.16Si0.05B alloys with full equiaxed grains, there is no significant

fracture. For the 0.16Si0.05B alloy deposit, due to the high cooling rate 
during the L-DED and relatively low Si content, the Si element primarily 
exists as a solid solution, which not only provides solid solution 
strengthening but also prevents the negative effects of silicide on plas -
ticity [ 25 ]. The trace B element significantly refines the prior β grain 
size, enhancing strain partitioning between grains. Meanwhile, limiting 
B content reduces the tendency for cleavage fracture caused by excessive 
B and prevents plasticity deterioration.

basketweave structure with grain boundary α colonies in the

leading to crack nucleation at their junctions, which then propagate 
along the colony boundaries [ 71 ]. In the basketweave structure, crack 
nucleation predominantly occurs at the α lath boundaries. As strain in -
creases, additional cracks nucleate at the boundaries of adjacent α laths,

propagation [ 72 ]. Moreover, grain boundary α clusters protect the 
relatively weaker continuous grain boundary α phase, significantly

globular silicide at the α / β interface within the grains. A high B

solidification. The synergistic alloying modification of Si and B 
effectively inhibits the formation of silicide and TiB by reducing their 
individual contents.
3. Sufficient Si and B content promotes the formation of fully equiaxed 
β grains and eliminates differences in slip system activation and grain 
boundary alignment across loading directions, significantly reducing 
both strength and plastic anisotropy. Residual anisotropy may arise 
from microstructure evolution with height in the as-built state.
4. A high content of Si or B elements promotes the formation of brittle 
silicide or TiB at prior β grain boundaries, leading to a tendency for 
cleavage fracture and a reduction in plasticity. The synergistic 
addition of Si and B prevents the formation of these phases, thus 
preserving the matrix ’ s robust deformation capacity. The L-DED
