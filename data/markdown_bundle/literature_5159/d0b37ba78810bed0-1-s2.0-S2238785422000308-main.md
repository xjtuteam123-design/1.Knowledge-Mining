abstract
Niobium (Nb)-based alloys have not yet been considered to replace nickel (Ni)-based alloys
as engineering component in high temperature applications. In spite of its elevate melting
temperature, Nb is known for very limited oxidation resistance as well as lower fracture
toughness and ductility. These disadvantages combined with relatively high cost, have not
favor the high temperature engineering applications of Nb-based alloys. Therefore, for the
ﬁrst time, the present work developed spark plasma sintered novel Nb-based alloys with
additions of Ni, Cu and WC, that allowed them to compete with conventional, Ni-based
alloys. Alloys with composition Nb-xNi-2Cu e2WC (x ¼ 5, 10 and 15 wt.%) were fabricated
by sintering at 1000, 1100 and 1200
/C14 C. The novel alloys microstructure, porosity, hardness
and oxidation resistance were characterized and evaluated. X-ray diffraction analysis
revealed the formation of Nb
2C, d Ni3Nb intermetallic. Nb alloy with 10 wt.% Ni spark
plasma sintering at 1200 /C14 C displayed superior properties of 97% densiﬁcation, 1205 Vickers
hardness, coherent granular Nb 2O5 oxides, and 1.84 /C2 10/C0 7 g2cm4.s/C0 1 oxidation parabolic
rate, as compared to other common Nb alloys and some conventional Ni-based alloys for
high temperature applications.

1. Introduction
Niobium (Nb)-based alloys have been applied in the aerospace
industry under conditions of high pressure and temperature
levels for vehicles with advanced turbine engines [ 1]. To reach
aerospace technological demands, however, it is required
materials with increasingly higher mechanical strength
associated with toughness for withstanding extreme high
temperature environmental conditions [ 1]. As stated, nickel
(Ni)-based superalloys are the most frequent metal alloys used
for engineering components at high temperatures, but the
maximum operating temperature is 1070 e1100
/C14 C[ 2,3]. The
lower density, good thermal conductivity, higher melting and
boiling points, as well as high temperature strength, room
temperature (RT) ductility and low ductile to brittle transition
temperature make Nb-based alloys promising candidates for
ultra-high temperature structural applications [ 4e10]. On the
other hand, factors such as poor oxidation resistance, severe
dimensional degradation above 900
/C14 C, high cost, low fracture
toughness and low ductility restrict their use at high
demanding operations [ 4e10].
As Nb-based alloys begin to oxidize at temperatures
around 250 /C14 C and the oxidation rate increases rapidly above
500 /C14 C[ 11], their thermal performance must be improved. The
addition of elements to form passivating layers has been
implemented in the development of superalloys [ 12]. In Ni-
based superalloys, chromium (Cr) is added not only for solid
solution strengthening but also to form a dense Cr
2O3 layer
[13]. In this way, one might suggest that adding Cr to Nb-base
alloys can be an auspicious choice to reduce the oxidation
resistance rate of these alloys. The addition of Ni to Nb-based
alloys can also promote better oxidation resistance due to the
formation of passivating layers [ 14]. However, considering
that these elements, like any other, might yield limited suc-
cess and led to the deterioration of the mechanical properties,
there exists a need to compare Nb eCr and Nb eNi systems
[14,15]. It was reported by Ye et al. [ 16] that the addition of 2wt
% of Nb to a Ni superalloy resulted in the formation of more
continuous and compact oxide scales, inhibiting oxygen
diffusion and consequently increasing oxidation resistance.
The addition of Ni to a Nb-based alloy is expected to strength
the material through dispersed precipitations of in-
termetallics like Ni
3Nb, which has the same effect that Nb
causes in Ni super-alloys [ 17]. Ni eNb alloys have been
extensively investigated relating to thermodynamics, phase
diagram and heat treatment, but there is still a lack of infor-
mation related to the microstructure of alloys with Nb content
that use Ni as an alloying element [ 18]. As for Cr alloying,
brittle Laves phases, NbCr
2, can be formed in the Nb-rich
composition range [ 19], so this element was not considered
in the development of the Nb-based alloys in this work.
Tungsten (W) has been appointed by Li et al. [ 20]a sa n
element able to improve the oxidation resistance and the
mechanical strength. The authors investigated the addition of
W to Nb alloy, varying between 10 and 30wt%, as expected, W
was totally solubilized in Nb and the formation of solid
solution between the two elements increase toughness and
ductility due to the elastic interaction resulting from the dif-
ference in atomic size in the crystal lattice. Furthermore, the
authors highlighted the improvement in the resistance to
oxidation by the passivating layer between the oxide Nb
5O2
and WO 3, corroborating other authors [ 21]. Indeed, W is a re-
fractory metal with a very high melting point (3410 /C14 C) and go
into solid solution with Nb, so is efﬁcient in imparting the
alloy high temperature strength [ 21,22].
The manufacture of high temperature alloys consisting
mainly of refractory metals, such as Nb and W, can be done
efﬁciently by powder metallurgy (PM) processes [ 22]. These
processes have advantages for the development of alloys,
such as increasing the solubility limit of the solute elements
and reﬁning the particle size [ 23]. Previously, composite
structures with a good combination of mechanical properties
that can withstand oxidation were produced by mechanical
alloying aluminide compounds with Nb-based alloys [ 24]. In
spite of advantages, the inherent pore generation of the sin-
tering process is a compromising factor to the mechanical
properties. Thus, transition metals, such as copper (Cu), can
be added to the alloy to improve densiﬁcation through
achieving liquid phase sintering [ 25]. As W eCu and Nb eCu
systems have mutual insolubility, liquid phase sintering is
hampered. In fact, Costa et al. [ 26] reported that when using
Cu in Nb and W alloys, it promotes the growth of necks be-
tween the Cu-based phases in sintering, forming a kind of
skeleton for the structure, enabling the reorganization of solid
particles and causing high densiﬁcations. Furthermore Cu is
also known to increase the corrosion resistance of alloys in
various environments [ 27,28].
Among the known PM consolidation processes, spark
plasma sintering (SPS) has received growing attention [ 29].
This technique uses uniaxial pressure and high-intensity,
low-voltage, pulsed current. As a result, an effective consoli-
dation is obtained, which has the advantages of a high heating
rate with shorter sintering time as well as the condition
temperature and formation of a ﬁner microstructure
compared to that resulting from conventional sintering [30]. In
a recent work Liao et al. [ 31] compared the use of the SPS
technique and arc fusion for the production of Nb-based alloy.
The authors reported that with the use of SPS superior alloys
were obtained, with greater fracture toughness (25.7 MPa m
0.5)
and a more reﬁned microstructure, with is a beneﬁcial char-
acteristic for the material. This superiority of alloys produced
by SPS compared to those produced by more traditional
techniques has also been reported by other authors [ 7,32e34].
In this work, novel Nb-based alloys with a novel compo-
sitions Nb-xNi-2Cu e2WC (x ¼ 5, 10 and 15 wt.%) were fabri-
cated via SPS. Three different sintering temperatures were
employed in the process to evaluate their effect on densiﬁ-
cation, microstructure and hardness of these novel alloys.
Hardness at room temperature (RT) and oxidation perfor-
mance at 1000
/C14 C in an oxidizing atmosphere were compara-
tively investigated. A relationship among alloy composition
and sintering temperature to acquire better oxidation
behavior, densiﬁcation and hardness was established.

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Sintering
The materials used in this research were Nb powders, sup-
plied by the Metallurgy and Mining Company (CBMM, Brazil)
with 99.8% purity and particle size of less than 40 mm; Ni
(Merck, Brazil), with 98.7% of purity and average particle size
of 17.1 mm; and Cu and WC powders (Multicel, Brazil) with
particle size smaller than 45 mm, and purity of 99.6% and 99.2%
respectively. These powders were used to make alloys with
the compositions shown in Table 1.
The experimental procedure followed the steps presented
in the ﬂowchart in Fig. 1.
The mixtures were homogenized in a high energy mill
(Spex CertiPrep, model 8000 Mixer/Mill ®, Metuchen, USA)
with stainless-steel balls in a 10:1 ball/powder ratio for 2 h,
under an air atmosphere. After that, the mixtures were sub-
jected to pulsed spark plasma sintering (SPS) (FUJI, model SPS-
211-LX, Tsurugashima, Japan) at 1000, 1100 and 1200
/C14 C, under
a constant pressure of 35 MPa in a graphite matrix 10 mm in
diameter by 35 mm in height, using a heating rate of 100
/C14 C/
min. The samples were processed at the aforementioned
sinter temperatures for 10 min under vacuum and then slowly
cooled to RT. Sintering was performed in triplicate for each
condition studied.
2.1.1. X-ray diffraction (XRD)
All samples were analyzed by an X-ray diffraction (XDR,

ation with scanning in the 2q range from 20e100
/C14 e6/C14 /min with

composition and crystal structure of the specimens.
2.1.2. Scanning electron microscopy/energy dispersive

Initially, the sintered samples were prepared metallographi-
cally and then sent for characterization by scanning electron
microscopy (SEM). The SEM microscope, model JSM6610LV,
JEOL, Peabody, USA) equipped with energy dispersive spec-
troscopy (EDS) (Bruker, XFlash ® Detector 6|10, Billerica, USA)
was used to observe the microstructures and homogeneity of
the sintered samples.

3.4. Vickers hardness
The Vickers hardness of each manufactured specimen are
shown in Fig. 8.
When performing the statistical test on the hardness
values shown in Fig. 8, it was concluded that, both at 1000 and
1100 /C14 C, the hardness of M2 and M3 compositions did not
show any statistical difference. At 1200 /C14 C, however it is
observed that the hardness of the M2 sample is the only one
that can be considered having a superior value.
The hardness found in the samples sintered at 1100 and
1200
/C14 C are considerably higher than those reported in many
works found in the literature with compositions similar to
those in the present work, as can be seen in Table 2.
This high hardness found in the novel alloys studied in the
present work can be attributed to the application of the high-
energy milling process with SPS [ 32], together with the com-
bined effect of the following mechanisms: precipitation
strengthening of the intermetallic NbNi
3 [18,21], dispersion
reinforcement caused by the formation of carbides [ 32] and,
ﬁnally, strengthening by interstitial solid solution attributed
to the presence of dissolved oxygen in the matrix [ 54]. These
mechanisms act as barriers for the movement of dislocations,
generating this increase in hardness of the novel alloys. It is
important to emphasize that these mechanisms were also
discussed in other works [ 18,21,55], a fact that corroborates
the results found here.

analysis and SEM observation were performed. It was noticed
that all the conditions studied presented very similar micro-
structures and developed phases. Figs. 10 and 11 respectively
show the XRD diffractogram and the morphology aspect of the
oxide layer formed during the oxidation test of the M2 sample
sintered at 1200
/C14 C.
From Fig. 10 it is possible to identify peaks related to the
oxides NbO 2,N b 2O5,W O 3 and the complex NiNb 2O5. Among
these, Nb2O5 is considered the most stable oxide, being found
in other Nb alloys after oxidation [ 59e61]. Initially, the oxide
NbO2 was formed, fragile and difﬁcult to adhere to the surface
of the sample. With the advancement of the test time, there
was an increase in the concentration of oxygen diffused in the
alloy. It is proposed that NbO
2 turns into Nb2O5, which is more
stable and has a reasonable adhesion to the alloy surface,
increasing protection against oxidation and thus justifying the
observed parabolic behavior.
In short, the oxidation mechanism in the present work is
characterized by a fast process at the beginning of the test
and, after 20 h of exposure to high temperature, a decrease in
k
p occurred. This is due to the formation of Nb 2O5 that gives
rise to a passive layer on the alloy surface, which reduces the
oxygen diffusivity. This behavior is similar to other oxidation
investigations on Nb alloys [ 60e63].
When analyzing Fig. 11a it is noted that the surface formed
in the alloy is rough and porous, which is characteristic of the
fragmentation of the formed oxides. Fig. 11 b shows a typical
morphology of granular oxides, which is considered to be a
characteristic aspect of Nb
2O5 [63].
4. Summary and conclusion
With the obtained development and characterization of
sintered novel Nb-based alloys containing Ni (5, 10 and
15 wt.%); Cu (2wt.%); and WC (2 wt.%) results, it is possible
to concluded that the proposed spark plasma sintering
methodology, was efﬁcient and reaching values of the
analyzed parameters according to works reported in the
literature.
/C15 The sintering temperature had a great inﬂuence on the
specimens, as its increase improved all the properties
studied. This was justiﬁed by the fact that the increase in
temperature generated better homogenization of the ele-
ments in the alloy, as observed by EDS, thus presenting a
greater stability as compared to alloys sintered at lower
temperatures. Thus, relating to the sintering temperature,
it is concluded that for these compositions, temperatures
greater than 1100
/C14 C are necessary to obtain a good ho-
mogenization of the alloy elements.
/C15 Regarding the variation in composition, in densiﬁcation,
the composition with 10 wt.% Ni had the highest values,
reaching almost 97%. Compositions with 5 and 15 wt.% Ni
were statistically equal. For hardness, again the composi-
tion with 10 wt.% had the highest hardness of 1205 HV.
/C15 In the oxidation test, the mass gains varied between 156
and 231 mg/cm
2, showing a parabolic behavior, charac-
terized by the formation of a passivating layer composed of
Nb
2O55 oxide. The best resistance to oxidation was ach-
ieved in the 5 wt.% Ni alloy sintered at 1200 /C14 C, presenting a
kp of 1.84 /C2 10-7 g2/cm4.s, close to that of studies using Ti
and Si, elements widely used to prevent oxidation,
demonstrating the potential of application at high tem-
peratures of the present alloys.
/C15 Finally, it can be concluded that the temperature of 1200 /C14 C
and the composition with 10 wt.% Ni were the conditions
that, together, produced the best novel alloy. Since with
this combination of composition and sintering tempera-
ture the highest densiﬁcation (96.97%), as well as the
highest hardness (1205,1 HV) was achieved, together with
an appreciable oxidation resistance.
