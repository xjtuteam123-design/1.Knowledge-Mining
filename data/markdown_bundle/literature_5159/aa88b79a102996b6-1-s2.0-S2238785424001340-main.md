ABSTRACT 
Oxidation is one of the main failure modes of superalloys, and the initial oxidation of a superalloy provides clues 
for its failure analysis. NiCo-based superalloys exhibit superior mechanical properties, but their oxidation 
behaviour is still debatable, especially in terms of the ratio of Ni/Co. Here, we show the effect of the Ni/Co ratio 
on the initial oxidation behaviour at the nanoscale by in situ environmental transmission electron microscopy 
(ETEM), which provides the initial microstructural changes in the γ / γ ′ structure-based alloy. The results indicate 
that Ni-rich alloys exhibit better structural stability at high temperatures than Ni-deficient alloys. A high content 
of Ni promotes the formation of a dense oxide layer, inhibits the oxidation rate, and improves the oxidation 
resistance. This result will help to elucidate the basic process of oxide formation in NiCo-based single-crystal 
superalloys and provide ideas for improving their oxidation resistance for alloy design.

4. Discussions 
4.1. Effect of Co/Co + Ni ratio on microstructure 
The ratio of Co/(Co + Ni) affects the microstructure, elemental dis -
tribution/partition coefficient and initial corrosive behaviours. Co, Ni 
and Fe (base elements) have similar physical properties, while they have 
distribution preferences for the γ and γ ′ phases. NiCo-based superalloys, 
however, display different partitioning coefficients than pure Ni- or Co- 
based superalloys. Pure Ni- or Co-based superalloys usually have a high 
lattice misfit between interfaces, while CoNi-based superalloys have a 
low lattice misfit if their content is equivalent. The NC25 alloy has a Co/ 
(Co + Ni) ratio of approximately 1/3 and displays a rectangular γ ′ phase 
shape, while the ratio of NC60 is approximately 1 and displays a round γ ′ 
phase shape. The measured data on (Fe + Co + Ni) in the NC25 alloy 
have contents of 69.8 % and 74.4 % in the γ and γ ′ phases, respectively, 
while they are 74.3 % and 75.5 % in the γ and γ ′ phases, respectively, in 
the NC60 alloy. The reverse distribution of Co, compared with Ni, makes 
the alloy have equivalent base elements in the γ and γ ′ phases in NC60 
alloy and thus results in less lattice misfit and corresponding interface 
stress than NC25 alloy.

5. Conclusion 
The in situ oxidation behaviour of two NiCo-based single-crystal su -
peralloys with γ / γ ′ structures at 0.3 mbar pressure under a high-purity 
O
2 
atmosphere was systematically studied by environmental trans -
mission electron microscopy (ETEM). The change in the γ / γ ′ phase 
element content during the oxidation process was analyzed, and the 
effect of the Ni content on the oxidation resistance of the NiCo-based 
single-crystal alloy was discussed. The following conclusions can be 
drawn: 
1. The increase in Ni content reduces the lattice parameters of the γ 
phase and γ ′ phase and increases the lattice mismatch between the 
two phases, and the shape of the γ ′ phase changes from spherical to 
cubic. 
2. The increase in Co content leads to the widening of the γ channel at 
high temperature and the formation of loose and porous oxides, 
which leads to a large amount of oxygen entering the original alloy 
and accelerates the oxidation of the alloy. 
3. A continuous dense oxide film containing small particles formed on 
the NC25 alloy surface hinders violent expansion. The γ phase in the 
NC60 alloy exhibits obvious preferential oxidation, resulting in a 
large expansion coefficient. 
4. The increase in Ni content in the alloy promotes the diffusion of Al
3 +
and Cr
3 +
, accelerates the formation of a continuous protective layer, 
inhibits the oxidation rate, and improves the oxidation resistance.
