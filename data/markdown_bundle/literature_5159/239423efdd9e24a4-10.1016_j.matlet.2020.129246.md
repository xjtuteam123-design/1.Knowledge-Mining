abstract
A novel as-cast Al 0.7CoCrFeNiTi0.3 high entropy alloy was prepared by vacuum arc melting and its
microstructure and compression properties were investigated. The alloy has an excellent combination
of high strength and large plasticity. The yield strength, fracture strength and compressive plasticity of
the alloy at room temperature were as high as 1438 MPa, 2990 MPa, and 37.7%, respectively. The alloy
was composed of alternating nano-scale face center cubic (FCC), body center cubic (BCC) and ordered
L2
1 phase. The excellent mechanical properties of the alloy are attributed to the combination of the soft
FCC phase and the hard BCC/L2 1 phase. In addition, the large number of phase interfaces provided by the
nano-scale lamellar microstructure and precipitated ordered phase also produce a strengthening effect.
The fracture mechanism of the alloy is mainly performed at compound fracture mode.

1. Introduction
The high-entropy alloy (HEA) is a new type of alloy obtained by
mixing multiple components based on chemical disorder [1,2].
Despite their complex composition, HEAs tend to form simple solid
solutions (FCC, BCC or mixed) due to their high mixing entropy.
Previous studies have indicated that HEAs with single FCC struc-
ture have high plasticity but poor strength, while HEAs with single
BCC structure have high strength but low ductility [3]. Indeed, sin-
gle phase HEAs are difﬁcult to meet the balance of strength and
plasticity. In order to achieve this goal, Lu et al. [4] proposed the
idea of a eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA). Based on this concept,
the researchers have reasonably designed a series of EHEAs with
excellent combination of high strength and good ductility [5–7].
CoCrFeNi HEA was found to have typical FCC structure and good
plasticity [8]. It was found that the phase structure of Al xCoCrFeNi
HEA changed from FCC to BCC with the increase of Al content.
When x is between 0.3 and 0.9, the alloy has a mixed structure
of FCC and BCC phases [9]. Ti with a larger atomic radius is added
to HEAs to increase the lattice distortion effect, and the strength of
the alloy is improved by solid solution strengthening [10]. In addi-
tion, due to the strong interactions between Al/Ti and CoCrFeNi-
based HEAs, the HEAs can produce phase transformations and
some intermetallic phases can be precipitated from the matrix,
resulting in an enhancement of strength [11]. In this letter, we pre-
pared a Al 0.7CoCrFeNiTi0.3 EHEA composed of FCC and BCC/L2 1
phases by adding appropriate Al and Ti to CoCrFeNi HEA, and the
alloy was ﬁnally proved to have excellent compressive properties
at room temperature.

3. Results and discussion
The XRD pattern ( Fig. 1 (a)) shows that the alloy has a three-
phase crystal structure of L2 1, FCC and BCC, with lattice constants
of 5.832, 3.596 and 2.873 Å, respectively. The SEM back scattered
electron (BSE) image of the as-cast Al 0.7CoCrFeNiTi0.3 HEA is shown
in Fig. 1 (b). The macro-structure of the alloy shows a petal-like
dendritic structure, including dendrite (DR) region and inter-
dendrite (ID) region.
Fig. 1(c) and (d) show the BSE images and bright-ﬁeld (BF) TEM
micrographs with corresponding selected area electron diffraction
(SAED) patterns of the DR region and the ID region, respectively.
The entire DR region is composed of a regular nano-scale lamellar
near-eutectic microstructure consisting of alternating bright BCC/
L21 phase and dark FCC phase. A typical eutectic microstructure
with some nano-scale particles was also found in part of the ID
region where the BCC phase and L2 1 phase were separated clearly.
According to our investigations, the DR region is dominated by FCC
phase and the ID region is dominated by BCC/L2 1 phase.
In order to further determine the morphology and structure of
phases in the HEA, dark-ﬁeld (DF) TEM observations were carried
out in different regions. In the DR region, the FCC phase and the
L21 phase are alternating lath-like structure with a width in the
range of 100–200 nm ( Fig. 2 (a, b)). In the near ID region, the L2 1
phase exhibit the near-ellipse shape. Some elongated BCC phase
with a width of about 30 nm were found in the ellipse ( Fig. 2 (c)).
From the TEM-EDS results ( Table 1), the BCC phase has a chem-
ical composition similar to the alloy, the L2 1 phase is enriched with
Al, Ti and Ni, and the FCC phase is enriched with Fe and Cr. This
result is similar to that of Stepanov et al. [12]. The large negative
mixing enthalpy of Al-Ni (–22 kJ/mol) and Ti-Ni (–35 kJ/mol) are
conducive to the formation of the Al-Ni-Ti rich phase during solid-
iﬁcation [13]. Moreover, excessive amount of Al/Ti can promote the
formation of L2 1-Ni2AlTi phase rather than the B2-NiAl phase [11].
We can also ﬁnd that the total amount of Al + Ti (33.9 at.%) is close
to that of Ni (29.2 at.%) in L2 1 phase, further indicating that the L2 1
phase has the same crystal structure as Ni 2AlTi.
The compressive engineering stress–strain curve of the alloy is
shown in Fig. 3 (a). The yield strength, fracture strength and com-
pressive plasticity of the alloy were 1438 MPa, 2990 MPa, and
37.7%, respectively. Furthermore, we obtain the true stress–strain
curve and the corresponding work hardening rate (d r/de) curve
of the alloy. We can infer that the plastic deformation of the alloy
is dominated by dislocation slip due to the characteristic of the
curves: the true stress–strain curve has a higher yield point, and
the work-hardening rate of the alloy continues to decrease with
plastic deformation [14]. It is worth noting that the alloy has a
good work hardening response, and the uniform plastic deforma-
tion of the alloy exceed 30%. As shown in Fig. 3(b), the comprehen-
sive compressive properties of the alloy are better than some
reported HEAs [15–20]. The excellent mechanical properties of
the alloy can be attributed to the composite effect of the soft FCC
phase and the hard BCC/L2
1 phase and the precipitation strength-
ening by ordered phase of solid solutions. In addition, the nano-
scale lamellar eutectic microstructure provides a large number of
phase interfaces, which inhibit the movement of dislocations and
produce a strengthening effect [7].

4. Conclusions
In summary, we studied a novel as-cast Al 0.7CoCrFeNiTi0.3 HEA
with an excellent integrated strength-ductility combination. The
alloy consists of alternating nano-scale lamellar FCC phase and
BCC/L21 phase. This as-cast Al 0.7CoCrFeNiTi0.3 HEA presents a yield
strength of 1438 MPa, a fracture strength of 2990 MPa, and a com-
pressive plasticity of 37.7% in engineering stress–strain condition
at room temperature. The mechanical properties of the alloy are
superior to most reported HEAs.
