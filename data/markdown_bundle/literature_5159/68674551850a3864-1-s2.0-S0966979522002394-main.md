ABSTRACT 
β -solidified γ -TiAl alloys are popular lightweight structural materials well suited for high temperature applica -
tions, however, whose service temperature limit is greatly restricted due to the insufficient high temperature 
strength and creep resistance. In present work, a hybrid reinforced β -solidified γ -TiAl alloy was successfully 
developed with the hybrid addition of C and Y
2
O
3 
by induction skull melting (ISM) technique, accordingly, the 
microstructure, high temperature tensile and creep properties of the alloy are detailly discussed. The results 
indicate that the hybrid addition of C and Y
2
O
3 
can improve the high temperature tensile strength of this nearly 
lamellar TiAl alloy with the increase of about 13.7%. More importantly, creep resistance of this alloy is signif -
icantly enhanced, which is attributed to multiple strengthening mechanisms: ( ⅰ ) the second phase strengthening 
from multi-scale Y
2
O
3 
particles and dynamically precipitated H – Ti
2
AlC carbides; ( ⅱ ) mechanical twins and twin 
intersections can act as special obstacles to dislocation movement, which is of great significance in decreasing the 
steady-state creep rate. Particularly, this alloy exhibits excellent microstructure stability during creep. On the one 
hand, nano-scale Y
2
O
3 
and dynamically precipitated H – Ti
2
AlC particles could pin the B2/ γ interface to 
strengthen lamellar colony boundary. On the other hand, dispersed B2 phase particles could precipitate along α
2 
lamellae and grow up with the consumption of the α
2 
phase, which will inhibit the degradation of α
2
/ γ lamellae. 
This study offers a new composition design approach to optimize high temperature performance of β -solidified 
γ -TiAl alloys. 
1. Introduction 
With the rapid development of aerospace engine industry towards 
high performance, high fuel efficiency and low cost, seeking a kind of 
lightweight structural material with excellent properties is extremely 
urgent [ 1 , 2 ]. Intermetallic TiAl alloys, as the potential candidate ma -
terial to replace conventional Ni-based superalloys in the temperature 
range of 650 – 850 
◦
C, possess advantages of sufficient specific strength 
and specific stiffness, remarkable high temperature oxidation resistance 
and low density of 3.9 – 4.2 g/cm
3 
(about half of 8 – 8.5 g/cm
3 
of Ni-based 
superalloys) [ 1 , 3 – 5 ]. Since 2006, Ti – 48Al – 2Cr – 2Nb (GE4822) alloy 
developed by GE in the 1980s has been used for low pressure turbine 
(LPT) blades installed on GEnX-1B engines [ 6 ]. Subsequently, the 
maiden flight of the Airbus A320 neo carrying the geared turbofan 
(GTF ™ ) engines with high speed LPT blades made of 
Ti-43.5Al – 4Nb – 1Mo-0.1B alloy was successfully carried out in 2014 [ 7 ], 
which is an important milestone for the engineering application of 
β -solidified γ -TiAl alloys. In binary Ti – Al alloys, the ordered 
face-centered tetragonal γ -TiAl (L1

α

γ -TiAl alloys, adding β -stabilizing elements of Nb, Mo and Cr into Al-lean 
alloys (42 – 44 at.%) will lead to the formation of disordered 
body-centered cubic β phase (A2 structure) at high temperature [ 8 , 9 ], 
which can provide sufficient independent slip systems [ 10 ], thus 
obtaining wider hot working window for TiAl alloys and attracting 
extensive attention in recent years [ 11 ]. 
At lower temperatures, β phase will transform into ordered body-

2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Material preparation 
Based on β -solidified γ -TiAl alloying concept, two alloys with the 
nominal compositions (in at.%) of Ti – 43Al – 6Nb – 1Mo – 1Cr and 
Ti – 43Al – 6Nb – 1Mo – 1Cr-0.5C-0.05Y
2
O

(hereinafter referred to as 
TNMC and TNMC
+

duction skull melting (ISM) under argon atmosphere. The raw materials 
used in this study contain pure titanium bars (99.9 wt%), pure 
aluminum (99.9 wt%), pure chromium (99.9 wt%), Al – Mo alloys (Mo: 
50.5 wt%), Al – Nb alloys (Nb: 52.4 wt%), graphite powders (99.9 wt%) 
and nano-Y

uniform distribution, graphite powders and nano-Y
2

smelted. The obtained ingots are cylinders with a diameter of 50 mm 
and a height of 100 mm. To reduce element segregation, the as-cast 
alloys were subjected to homogenization heat-treatment at 950 
◦
C for 
36 h.

4.3.3. Effect of microstructure evolution on creep resistance of TiAl alloys 
Fig. 18 depicts the effect of microstructure evolution on creep 
resistance of the investigated alloys. For TNMC alloy with the high 
content of strong β -stabilizing elements Nb, Mo and Cr, β phase can be 
retained as ordered B2 phase at room temperature and is alternately 
arranged with blocky γ phase, which exists in the lamellar colony and at 
the colony boundary, called α -segregation and β -segregation, respec -
tively, as shown in Fig. 18 (a). During creep two kinds of cracks (i.e., 
crack I and crack II) are formed in TNMC alloy, which are depicted in 
Fig. 18 (b) and (c). On the one hand, α
2
/ γ interfaces strongly restrain the 
dislocation propagation, which promotes the nucleation of voids at 
ledges. Subsequently, crack propagates along the lamellar interface and 
deflects when it encounters α -segregation, thus, crack I is formed and the 
characteristics of interlamellar and translamellar cracks appear 
accordingly. On the other hand, in order to coordinate the incompatible 
deformation among the lamellar colonies with different orientations, the 
slip systems in the γ and B2 phases existed at the colony boundary are 
activated successively. However, due to the different deformation ca -
pacities of γ (face-centered tetragonal, FCT) and B2 (body-centered 
cubic, BCC) phases, severe dislocation pile-ups occur at the B2/ γ inter -
face, thus inducing nucleation of voids at the B2/ γ interface. As the voids 
grow and gather, crack II is formed and the characteristic of dimple is 
present.
