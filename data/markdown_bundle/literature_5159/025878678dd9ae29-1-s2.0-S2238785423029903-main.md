ABSTRACT 
In this study, the effects of V on the microstructure, lattice misfit, solvus temperatures, and oxidation behaviors 
of the Co – Ni – Al – V – Ta-based superalloys are investigated. The results show that all these alloys exhibit the γ’ / γ 
two-phase structures. More importantly, the γ’ solvus temperature increases, and the oxidation resistance is 
significantly improved with the reduction of V concentration. Subsequently, the Ti (2 at.%) was added into 
Co – 40Ni – 10Al – 4Ta – 1V – 12Cr alloy to further raise the properties of the superalloy. It is found that the 
Co – 40Ni – 10Al – 4Ta – 1V – 12Cr – 2Ti alloy exhibits a higher solvus temperature reaching 1200 
◦
C of the γ ′ phase, a 
low density of 8.48 g/cm
3
, a low lattice misfit of only + 0.37 %, as well as exceptional oxidation resistance and 
mechanical properties. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys exhibit excellent mechanical properties at 
elevated temperatures and are widely used in the aircraft industry [ 1 ]. 
However, the working temperatures of the Ni-based superalloys are 
reaching the melting point of Ni, which prevents their further 
improvement [ 2 ]. Although Co has a higher melting point than Ni, the 
conventional Co-based superalloys have inferior high-temperature 
properties due to the lack of precipitation-strengthening. The discov -
ery of γ ′ -Co
3
(Al, W) in the Co – Al – W system by Sato et al. [ 3 ] provides a 
new path for developing novel Co-based superalloys. However, the 
development and application of Co – Al – W-based superalloys are con -
strained by their high density attributable to the presence of W. In recent 
years, W-free Co-based superalloys have attracted extensive attention 
[ 4 – 7 ]. Considerable effort has been devoted to the development of new 
Co-based superalloys in the Co-Al-Mo-Ta [ 7 ], Co-Al-Mo-Nb [ 8 – 10 ], 
Co – Nb – V, Co – Ta – V [ 11 , 12 ], Co-Ti-Mo [ 6 ], and Co – Ti – Cr [ 13 ] alloy 
systems. However, it has been found that their melting temperatures and 
γ ′ phase stability are lower than those of Co – Al – W-based alloys. Xu et al. 
employed DFT techniques to investigate the impact of alloying on the 
metastable L1
2
-Co
3
Al phase and suggested that W, Ta, V, Ti, Nb, Re, Mo, 
Pt, and Ir could be served as stabilizing agents for the metastable 
γ ′ -Co
3
Al phase [ 33 ]. These results lend support to the notion that an 
L1
2
-ordered γ ′ -Co
3
(Al, X) phase could potentially be realized in the 
Co – Al-X ternary system. 
In 2019, our team developed a new class of W-free Co – Al – V-based 
superalloys [ 14 ]. The Co – Al – V ternary alloy displays a wide γ - γ ′ 
two-phase region, with the Al and V contents respectively ranging from 
1 to 6 at.% and 10 to 20 at.%. Chen et al. [ 14 ] investigated the impact of 
various elements on the γ / γ ′ microstructure and the solvus temperature 
of γ ′ phase in the Co – V – Al system. Their findings revealed that Ni pos -
sesses the capability to expand the γ - γ ′ two-phase region. Moreover, they 
reported that Ta can elevate the γ ′ solvus temperature and increase the 
lattice misfit between the γ and γ ′ phases. Furthermore, they explored 
the effects of the Al/V ratio on the alloy properties, in which a higher 
Al/V ratio is beneficial to the γ ′ solvus temperatures. On this basis, the 
developed Co – 30Ni – 10Al – 5V – 4Ta – 2Ti alloy exhibits a very high γ’ 
solvus temperature (1243 
◦
C) with a low mass density (8.46 g/cm
3
).

However, it has been reported that the inclusion of V in Co-based alloys 
weakens their oxidation resistance [ 15 ]. Therefore, it is important to 
investigate the effect of V content on the oxidation resistance of 
Co – Al – V-based superalloys. Additionally, Cr has been reported to 
greatly enhance the oxidation resistance in Co-based superalloys [ 16 ]. 
In this study, the Co – 40Ni – 10Al – 4Ta- x V-12Cr ( x = 0, 1, 3, 5) alloys

microstructure, lattice misfit, γ’ solvus temperature, and oxidation

Co – Ni – Al – Ta – V – Cr superalloy, a 2 at.% of Ti was incorporated into the 
studied Co – 40Ni – 10Al – 4Ta – 1V – 12Cr alloy. 
2. Methods 
All alloy compositions are given in at.%. Co, Ni, Al, Ta, V, Cr, and Ti 
with high purity (purity > 99.9 %) were used to prepare samples. The 
raw materials were ultrasonically cleaned with anhydrous ethanol. The 
prepared metal material was melted at least 8 times in the arc-melting 
furnace to obtain the alloy samples with homogeneous composition. 
The samples to be heat-treated were vacuum-encapsulated in a quartz 
tube filled with a small amount of argon gas as a protective gas and then 
quenched in cold water to obtain microstructure at the expected tem -
perature. All alloy samples were solid solution treated at 1200 
◦
C or 
1250 
◦
C to obtain γ single phase and then aged at 900 
◦
C to obtain γ / γ ′ 
two-phase microstructure. 
In order to obtain the oxidation rate of the sample, the bulk samples 
were cut into small regular geometries and placed in a tube furnace at 
1000 
◦
C under an air atmosphere for 25 h, 50 h, and 100 h. After defined 
periods, the mass gain was obtained by calculating the weight difference 
between the before and after samples and the surface area samples 
divided by surface area. 
The samples were etched with a corrosion solution with a 3:1:1 ratio 
of HNO
3 
and HCI and water for microstructure observation. The γ / γ ′ 
microstructure was observed using a field emission scanning electron 
microscope (FE-SEM: SU70, Hitachi, Japan). Components were deter -
mined by electron probe microanalysis (EPMA: JEOL JXA-8100R) with a 
beam current of 10

ZAF calibration method uses pure elements as standards. A minimum of 
10 component data points were measured for each phase, and after 
removing the two-component data points with the largest overall stan -
dard deviation, the remaining component data points were averaged to 
obtain the desired phase composition. 
The size and volume fraction of the γ ′ phase was determined using 
ImageJ software. The phase transformation temperatures of the as-aged 
alloys were tested by a NETZSCH 404 F3 differential scanning calo -
rimetry (DSC) at the heating rate of 20

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. The effects of V content on the Co – 40Ni – 10Al – 4Ta-xV-12Cr alloy 
3.1.1. Effects of V on the microstructure and γ ’ solvus temperature 
For the sake of brevity in the following, the Co – 40Ni – 10Al – 4Ta- x V- 
12Cr ( x = 0, 1, 3, 5) alloys will be named as 1V12Cr, 3V12Cr, and 
5V12Cr, respectively. Fig. 1 shows the microstructures of 0V12Cr, 
1V12Cr, 3V12Cr, and 5V12Cr alloys aged at 900 
◦
C for 48 h. All the 
studied alloys maintain good γ / γ ′ two-phase organization, without any 
detrimental phase. The microstructure of the alloys was shown to be 
affected by the V content. In the 0V12Cr and 1V12Cr alloys, the γ ′ phase 
shows a cubic morphology. In 3V12Cr and 5V12Cr alloys, the γ ′ phase 
tends to be spherical. The change of shape is ascribed to the lattice 
misfit, as seen in Fig. 2 . The γ / γ ′ two-phase lattice misfits of the 0V12Cr, 
1V12Cr, 3V12Cr, and 5V12Cr series alloys were + 0.60 %, + 0.42 %, 
+ 0.38 %, and + 0.19 %, respectively. With the decrease of V content, the 
γ / γ ′ two-phase lattice misfit of the alloys showed a decreasing trend. In 
general, the morphology of γ ′ phase in Ni-based superalloys is deter -
mined by the lattice misfit. Spherical, cuboidal, and plate-like shapes of 
γ ′ phase are observed for lattice misfits of 0.02 %, 0.5 – 1.0 %, and above 
1.25 %, respectively [ 21 ]. This indicates that the addition of V is 
beneficial in reducing lattice misfit and thus improving the micro -
structural stability of the γ ′ phase [ 22 ]. However, the γ ′ phase fractions of 
the 0V12Cr, 1V12Cr, 3V12Cr, and 5V12Cr alloys are 67.59 %, 65.19 %, 
60.16 %, and 54.14 %, respectively, indicating that higher content of V 
decreases the γ’ phase fraction. Therefore, the content of V needs to be 
strictly controlled in the alloy design. The addition of a low content of V 
to the alloys can obtain higher microstructure stability and higher γ ′ 
volume fraction simultaneously.

3.2. Microstructure, oxidation resistance, and yield strength of the 
Co – 40Ni – 10Al – 4Ta – 1V – 12Cr – 2Ti (1V12Cr2Ti) alloy 
3.2.1. Microstructure and γ ’ solvus temperature of the 1V12Cr2Ti alloy 
Ti is usually used as a stronger γ ′ phase forming element [ 14 , 29 ] in 
Co-based superalloys, which can not only increase the solid solution 
temperature [ 29 , 30 ] and high-temperature mechanical properties [ 14 , 
30 ] of the γ ′ phase but also effectively reduce the density [ 14 , 18 , 31 ]. 
Here, 2 at.% Ti was added into the Co – 40Ni – 10Al – 4Ta – 1V – 12Cr – 2Ti 
alloy (named as 1V12Cr2Ti alloy in the following text), aiming at 
achieving higher γ ′ solvus temperature and better mechanical proper -
ties. The 1V12Cr2Ti alloy was prepared and solid solution treated at

It can be seen that the 1V12Cr2Ti alloy still maintains good γ / γ ′ 
two-phase microstructure after aging at 900

900

beneficial to improve the long-lasting creep life. Note that the volume 
fraction of γ ′ phase of 1V12Cr2Ti is comparable to that of 1V12Cr (67.3 
%), indicating that 2 at.% Ti has no significant effects on the γ / γ ′ 
two-phase organization. Fig. 5 (b) shows the X-ray diffraction pattern of 
the (111) crystal plane of the 1V12Cr2Ti. Fig. 5 (c) shows the γ / γ ′ 
two-phase lattice misfit of 1V12Cr2Ti alloy ( + 0.37 %) compared with 
Co – Ni – Al – Ta – Cr-based [ 30 ], Co – Ti – W-based [ 6 , 13 , 33 ], Co-(Ni) –

( + 0.20 % to + 0.53 %) and Co – Ni – Al – Mo-based [ 23 ] ( + 0.19 % to +
