High-Strength Titanium-Based Alloy
for Low-Temperature Superplastic Forming
A.V. MIKHAYLOVSKAYA, A.O. MOSLEH, P. MESTRE-RINN, A.D. KOTOV,
M.N. SITKINA, A.I. BAZLOV, and D.V. LOUZGUINE-LUZGIN
Reducing the deformation temperature is an important research task for superplastic forming of
Ti-based alloys. This study demonstrates that the additions of Fe and B signiﬁcantly improve
microstructural homogeneity and superplastic performance, increase the post-forming mechan-
ical strength, and reduce the superplastic deformation temperature of a Ti-Al-Mo-V alloy. The
designed alloy exhibits an excellent superplastic deformation behavior with elongation of 590 to
1050 pct at 675 /C176 C to 775 /C176 C with a constant strain rate in a range of 5 9 10/C04 to 5 9 10/C03 s/C01,
and a high room temperature yield strength of 1020 MPa, a UTS of 1080 MPa, and
elongation-to-failure of about 6 pct both after annealing and after superplastic deformation
with a strain of 0.69 at 775 /C176 C. The microstructure and the strain-induced changes in the size
and shape of grains are discussed. The modiﬁcation of the b-phase morphology leads to an
increase in the curvature of interphase boundaries in the modiﬁed alloy. Advanced superplas-
ticity and improved mechanical properties make the studied alloy a very attractive material for
complex parts in numerous advanced applications.

I. INTRODUCTION
DUE to their high strength and corrosion resistance,
titanium alloys are widely used in aeronautic, trans-
portation engineering, medicine, and chemical engineer-
ing, with the Ti-6Al-4V alloy being the most common
one.[1] Many studies have been conducted to optimize
the production of Ti alloys parts by reducing the
temperature and stress needed to achieve superplastic
forming (SPF), known as a good technique to produce
complex-shaped parts with a unique geometry. [2–8] The
important forming parameters are strain rate and
temperature. The desirable strain rate is above 10 /C04 to
10/C03 s/C01. The SPF temperature for conventional

The formability is improved in two ways. The ﬁrst
approach is the formation of ultraﬁne grains obtained
by thermomechanical treatment, including severe plastic
deformation (SPD). [2,7,17–20] After SPD, Ti alloys
demonstrate a uniform ultraﬁne-grained structure, a
low-temperature superplasticity at 500 /C176 Ct o
700 /C176 C,[15,21–23] a steady ﬂow, and, as a result, excellent
formability, though the SPD methods seriously increase
the product cost, in addition to size limitations. The
second approach is based on a decrease of the b-transus
temperature due to alloying with b-stabilizing elements
and decrease the content of a-stabilizing Al. [24,25]
Addressed alloying by b-stabilizing elements with high
tracer diﬀusivities, [24,26–28] such as Fe, Ni, and Cr,
facilitate the accommodation of grain boundary sliding
(GBS) at low temperatures. Several recently developed
Ti-0.75Al-0.5V-2.75Fe-0.25Ni-1Mo-0.05B, Ti-1.5Al-
1.5V-2.75Fe-0.25Ni-3Mo-0.1B, and Ti-2.25Al-1.5V-0.25Fe-
2.75Ni-2Mo-0.15Si alloys [24] exhibit superplasticity at
650 /C176 C to 750 /C176 C. However, due to low Al content,
room temperature strength of these alloys is rather low.
A decrease of the SPF temperature helps to increase the
SPF productivity, but ﬁnal mechanical properties are
also important for engineering applications. Therefore,
Ti alloys with low-temperature superplasticity and
increased mechanical properties are required.
Iron is one of the most perspective, cost-eﬀective
alloying elements for designing Ti-based alloys. [29,30] A
highly diﬀusive b-stabilizing element Fe signiﬁcantly
decreases the SPF temperature [27,29] and increases the
mechanical strength of Ti alloys. [31,32] However, the
concentration of eutectoid-forming b-stabilizer ele-
ments, such as Fe, should not exceed 2 to 3 wt pct to
prevent the formation of the x-phase that decreases
ductility and fracture toughness. [24]
An improvement of the processing and mechanical
properties of Ti-based alloys is also possible due to
alloying with a small addition of 0.05 to 0.15 wt pct
B.[33–39] Boron is a small-solute interstitial element that
forms ﬁne TiB particles. [35,37] Borides act as nucleation
sites for the a- and b-Ti phases. [35,39] Several authors
have observed an increase in a recrystallization and
spheroidization ability due to minor additions of
boron.[39,40] As a result, borides reﬁne the grain size
during solidiﬁcation, hot deformation, and heat treat-
ment that ensure excellent microstructural homogeneity
of ingots and thermomechanically treated
sheets.[32,34,40–44] A trace addition of B improves the
superplastic formability of the Ti-6Al-4V alloy. [34,40]
The conventional Ti-4Al-3Mo-1V alloy is widely used
in the industry due to a good combination of service and
processing properties. This alloy exhibits superplasticity
in a range of 825 /C176 C to 875 /C176 C (similar to that of for the
Ti-6Al-4V alloy) with the maximum elongation-to-fail-
ure at 875 /C176 C.[45–47] Due to alloying with a slowly
diﬀusing element Mo, the Ti-4Al-3Mo-1V alloy
demonstrates increased recrystallization resistance. A
partially recrystallized initial grain structure leads to
dynamic recrystallization and softening during the
superplastic ﬂow of the Ti-4Al-3Mo-1V alloy. [45] The
non-uniform deformation and high SPF temperature are
the main disadvantages of the Ti-4Al-3Mo-1V alloy for
the SPF process. [48]
This work studies the inﬂuence of Fe and B additions
on the microstructure and superplasticity of the
Ti-4Al-3Mo-1V alloy [45] to provide a homogeneous
ultraﬁne grain structure, a decrease of the superplastic
forming temperature, and an increase of the room
temperature strength. 2 wt pct Fe was added to improve
superplastic forming and room temperature mechanical
strength but to avoid embrittlement taking place at
higher Fe concentrations. 0.1 wt pct B is a typical
concentration that provides grain reﬁnement and
microstructural homogeneity in the Ti-based alloys.
II. MATERIALS AND METHODS
The desired Ti-4Al-3Mo-1V-2Fe-0.1B (wt pct) alloy
was processed using pure titanium, aluminum, vana-
dium, molybdenum (all 99.99 wt pct purity), iron (99.95
wt pct), and boron. The ingots were re-melted ﬁve times
by an argon-arc method to ensure the alloy’s homoge-
nous composition and cast in a copper mold. The alloy
sheets were processed using a two-step treatment. The
ﬁrst step was vacuum annealing in the b-phase ﬁeld at
1000 /C176 C with subsequent water quenching to form a
ﬁne-martensitic structure. The second step was hot
rolling in the a + b ﬁeld at 740 ± 20 /C176 C for 90 pct total
reduction in six passes. The thickness reduction per pass
was 1 to 2 mm (20 to 50 pct). The Ti-4Al-3Mo-1V alloy
sheets were studied as a reference material, with the
processing parameters being similar for both materials.
The chemical composition of the alloys and the
microstructure were studied by scanning electron micro-
scopy (SEM) with a Tescan Vega 3-LMH microscope
equipped by Oxford Instruments EDS X-MAX 80. The
composition of twelve microscopy ﬁelds at a magniﬁca-
tion of 9300 to 500 was averaged out, and the resulted
compositions were found to be Ti-4.1Al-3.1-
Mo-0.9V-2.0Fe (excepting undeﬁned 0.1 wt pct B) and
Ti-4.1Al-3.0Mo-1.1V with a conﬁdence interval of alloy-
ing elements < 0.1 wt pct. The samples for SEM were
mechanically polished and etched in a Kroll agent. To
determine the appropriate heat treatment and
hot-working temperatures, the b-transus of the pro-
duced alloy was theoretically calculated by the
Thermo-Calc software (TTTi3 database) and experi-
mentally determined by the diﬀerential thermal analysis
(DTA) via a Setaram Labsys instrument. The X-ray
diﬀraction was performed on a Bruker D8 Advance
diﬀractometer using monochromatic Cu-K a radiation.
The phases volume fraction and the grain size were
studied after annealing in a temperature range of
625 /C176 C to 900 /C176 C for 30 minute with subsequent water
quenching. The actual volume fraction and the grain
size of each phase were calculated by a linear intercept
method, using SEM images.

IV. DISCUSSION
The studied Ti-Al-Mo-V-Fe-B alloy demonstrated a
relatively low-temperature superplasticity with a
stable ﬂow and a strain rate sensitivity m-index of near
0.5 in a strain rate range of 10 /C04 to 10 /C02 s/C01 in a
temperature range of 675 /C176 C to 775 /C176 C. These temper-
atures were 100 /C176 C to 200 /C176 C lower than those for the
reference Ti-Al-Mo-V alloy. [45] The optimum superplas-
tic behavior with elongation higher than 1000 pct was
observed at 775 /C176 C, and about 600 pct of elongation was
achieved at 675 /C176 C in the alloy of the modiﬁed compo-
sition. The reference Ti-Al-Mo-V alloy exhibits the
maximum elongation-to-failure of 750 pct at 875 /C176 C,
and less than 300 pct at 775 /C176 C. Low-temperature
superplasticity with high elongation was realized in the
Ti-Al-Mo-V-Fe-B alloy due to several factors. First, the
presence of Fe reduces the b-transus and moves the
temperature of the a/b volume fraction of 50/50 pct,
which is typically the best for grain stability in
duplex-structured alloys, from 865 /C176 C to 875 /C176 C typical
of the reference Ti-Al-Mo-V alloy to 775-800 /C176 C of the
studied one. Second, the as-processed grain structure of
the modiﬁed alloy is signiﬁcantly more equiaxed and
homogeneous than that of in the reference Fe- and
B-free alloy. Fe and B additions promote
spheroidization (or globularization) [8] of the structure
as it was observed in References 27, 28, 35, 40 and 49.
The authors in Reference 40 suggested that a small
addition of boron promotes dynamic globularization
due to enhanced a/b interfacial boundary sliding.
According to Reference 39, B addition inhibits a
recovery, promotes a recrystallization process, and helps
to form ﬁne recrystallized grains. As a result, homoge-
neous and equiaxed microstructure forms at the begin-
ning of the tensile test at 775 /C176 C, which provides a
stable superplastic ﬂow with a large elongation. Neck-
ing-free superplastic deformation at lower temperatures
of 675 /C176 C and 725 /C176 C accompanied by strain softening
suggests an occurrence of dynamic recrystallization, at
least at low strains. In the Fe- and B-free alloy, the
similar deformation behavior with strain softening was
observed during superplastic deformation at 875 /C176 C.
Due to increased recrystallization resistance, the refer-
ence alloy exhibits an inhomogeneous microstructure at
the beginning of the deformation at 775 /C176 C and the
non-superplastic behavior accompanied by necking. Fe
and B decrease recrystallization resistance and increase
microstructural homogeneity that leads to an increased
amount of the a/b boundaries playing a signiﬁcant role
in the GBS process. As a result, the studied Ti-Al-
Mo-V-Fe-B alloy exhibits 3.5 times lower stress than
that obtained for the Ti-Al-Mo-V alloy at 775 /C176 C and 1
9 10/C03s/C01 (Figure 6(c)) as well as stable superplastic
ﬂow and signiﬁcantly higher elongations. Third, as far
as the diﬀusion rate controls the superplastic deforma-
tion mechanisms, fast diﬀusing Fe facilitates the GBS
accommodation at decreased temperatures. [24] One
should note that due to alloying by additional b-stabi-
lizer Fe, b-transus temperature decreases only by about
35 /C176 C, while the superplastic deformation temperature
decreases by 100 /C176 C to 200 /C176 C.
The evolution of the b-phase morphology underlines
the importance of the rapid diﬀusion in the b-phase at
superplastic deformation. The b-phase rearrangement
signiﬁcantly inﬂuences the superplastic behavior and
provides low cavitation in superplastic Ti alloys. The
BCC b-phase has more dislocation slip systems and
higher diﬀusivity than the HCP a-phase. Grains move-
ment during GBS inevitably creates cavities, [3,50–53]
which are immediately ﬁlled with the b-phase, and
creates new a/b boundaries required for further GBS.

The accommodation of stress concentration at grain
boundaries by b-precipitation during superplastic defor-
mation of the Ti-6Al-4V alloy was suggested in Refer-
ence 54. Thus, the b-phase rearrangement process
provides eﬀective accommodation of grain boundary
sliding, and, as a consequence, high elongation with a
stable ﬂow and extremely low residual cavitation typical
of Ti alloys. [5,55,56] There are two accommodation
mechanisms known in superplastic materials: (1) dislo-
cation slip/creep and (2) diﬀusional creep. [57,58] The
dislocation slip/creep accommodation theory is wide-
spread,[5,6,29,52,59] and the diﬀusional creep accommoda-
tion theory is also experimentally conﬁrmed in
References 3, 57, 60–64. Both mechanisms can act
simultaneously and lead to the b-phase rearrangement
with an increase in the curvature of the interphase
boundaries at superplastic deformation in the studied
alloy. The dislocation mechanism of the BCC b-phase
deformation with the formation of low-angle grain
boundaries at triple junctions of grain boundaries is
shown in several studies. [29,33,59,65] Since the diﬀusion is
rapid on low-angle grain boundaries, their formation
leads to an increase in diﬀusivity in the b-phase and
facilitate the GBS accommodation. The signiﬁcant
b-phase rearrangement observed at large strains indi-
rectly conﬁrms an increased role of the diﬀusional creep
that accommodated GBS on the a/b boundaries during
superplastic deformation of the studied Ti-Al-Mo-V-
Fe-B alloy. An experimental study of the surface
structure evolution during superplastic deformation is
required to identify the role of accommodation
mechanisms.
V. CONCLUSIONS
The microstructural evolution, room temperature
tensile mechanical properties, and superplasticity of
the Ti-4Al-3Mo-1V-2Fe-0.1B alloy were studied in
comparison with the similarly thermomechanically pro-
cessed reference Fe- and B-free Ti-4Al-3Mo-1V refer-
ence alloy. The main conclusions are summarized as
follows:
1. The Ti-Al-Mo-V-Fe-B alloy exhibits 35 /C176 C lower
b-transus temperature, 100 /C176 C lower temperature of
an equal volume fraction a/b = 50/50 pct, and a
more homogeneous and equiaxed grain structure
after annealing than the Fe- and B-free alloy does.
2. The studied alloy exhibits an excellent superplastic
deformation behavior at 675 /C176 C to 775 /C176 C with
elongation-to-failure up to 1050 pct, while the
Ti-Al-Mo-V alloy demonstrates a non-superplastic
behavior at these temperatures. A structural rear-
rangement of the b-Ti phase in the studied alloy
taking place during superplastic deformation pro-
vides an increased elongation without residual
cavitation.
3. The designed Ti-Al-Mo-V-Fe-B alloy exhibits a
high room temperature yield strength of 1020 MPa,
an ultimate tensile strength of 1080 MPa, and
elongation-to-failure of about 6 pct both after
annealing and after 100 pct strain at an optimum
superplastic deformation temperature of 775 /C176 C.
The strength values of Fe and B modified alloy are
/C25100 to 200 MPa higher as compared to the
unmodified conventional alloy Ti-Al-Mo-V alloy.
Enhanced superplasticity and high mechanical
properties make the studied alloy of a modified
composition a very attractive material for complex
parts in numerous advanced applications.
