ABSTRACT
Ternary FeCrAl alloys are key structural materials for high-temperature applications, recognized for their 
exceptional oxidation resistance and mechanical strength. This study uses the particle swarm optimization al -
gorithm with first-principles calculations to predict and analyze the crystal structure of FeCrAl alloys. Four new 
ternary FeCrAl alloy structures were identified: two orthorhombic structures, one trigonal structure, and one 
tetragonal structure. Their structural stability, mechanical properties, and electronic structure were systemati -
cally investigated. Elastic constant and elastic modulus calculations suggest that all structures are brittle. Among 
them, the orthorhombic ( Cmcm -1) FeCrAl phase is the most energetically favorable and demonstrates the highest 
elastic modulus and Debye temperature.
1. Introduction
High-temperature alloys are a class of materials known for their 
outstanding performance under extreme heat conditions [ 1 – 4 ]. In in -
dustrial settings characterized by high temperatures and pressures, the 
stability and reliability of these materials are crucial. Owing to their 
exceptional oxidation resistance, corrosion resistances and mechanical 
properties, these alloys have become indispensable in automotive, 
chemical, aerospace, and nuclear industries [ 5 – 7 ].
Among high-temperature materials, Al-based and Cr-based alloys 
have garnered considerable attention for their outstanding oxidation 
resistance and mechanical properties at high temperatures [ 8 – 11 ]. 
Ternary FeCrAl alloys are characterized by their excellent oxidation 
resistance, corrosion resistance, and exceptional mechanical strength 
under high-temperature conditions [ 12 – 14 ]. In their study of FeCrAl

2. Computational methods
For the ternary FeCrAl alloy structure predictions, we employed the 
CALYPSO crystal structure prediction software [ 29 ] along with 
first-principles calculations [ 30 , 31 ] based on density functional theory 
(DFT). During the structure prediction process using CALYPSO, a pop -
ulation size of 50 was set to ensure thorough search space exploration. 
The maximum number of evolutionary iterations was set to 30. More -
over, a local PSO algorithm was employed, performing three rounds of 
local optimization for each structure to refine its energy and geometric 
configuration. The newly obtained structures were optimized and 
analyzed using DFT in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) 
[ 32 ], evaluating physical properties such as total energy, elastic 
modulus, and electronic structure. The generalized gradient approxi -
mation and Perdew – Burke – Ernzerhof functional [ 33 ] were used for the 
electronic exchange-correlation calculations of the ternary FeCrAl alloy. 
The phonon spectra were calculated using the phonopy software pack -
age [ 34 ]. To ensure accuracy, the plane wave cutoff energy for all 
ternary FeCrAl alloys was set to 400 eV, achieving good convergence of 
the total energy, with an energy convergence criterion of 1 × 10
 2 
eV/atom. During structural optimization, both the lattice parameters 
and atomic positions of the crystal structure were fully relaxed. More -
over, specific k -point meshes were defined for the different ternary 
FeCrAl alloy structures: the k -point meshes for the orthorhombic 
( Cmcm -1 and Cmcm -2) FeCrAl phases were set to 12 × 3 × 8 and 8 × 4 ×
8, respectively; the k -point mesh for the trigonal ( P 3m1) FeCrAl phase 
was set to 12 × 12 × 4; and the k -point mesh for the tetragonal ( I 4 mm ) 
FeCrAl phase was also set to 12 × 12 × 4.
3. Results and discussion

new ternary FeCrAl alloy structures: a tetragonal FeCrAl phase (space

156), and two orthorhombic FeCrAl phases (space group: Cmcm , No. 
63). Fig. 1 presents the four crystal structure models of FeCrAl.

Bulk modulus B and shear modulus G are essential parameters in 
analyzing a material ’ s mechanical properties. B primarily reflects a 
material ’ s deformation resistance during plastic deformation, while G 
indicates its ability to resist fracture. As a critical parameter for 
evaluating the mechanical properties of materials, the B/G ratio is 
commonly employed to assess a material ’ s brittleness or ductility. Ac -
cording to Pugh ’ s criterion, a B / G ratio of 1.75 is the critical threshold 
for determining whether a material exhibits brittle or ductile behavior 
[ 39 ]. In particular, when the B/G ratio exceeds 1.75, the material 
demonstrates good ductility; conversely, when the ratio falls below this 
threshold, the material exhibits brittleness. Thus, through precise 
calculation and in-depth analysis of the B/G ratio, we can effectively 
predict and assess the mechanical properties of materials, providing a 
scientific basis for material selection and application. Using the data in 
Table 3 , the B/G ratio for the ternary FeCrAl alloys was calculated. The 
B/G ratio for all four ternary FeCrAl alloys is < 1.75, indicating that all 
structures exhibit a certain degree of brittleness. This observed brittle -
ness may be attributed to the atomic arrangement and bonding in the 
FeCrAl structure. In addition, Poisson ’ s ratio is another important in -
dicator for assessing a material ’ s brittleness or ductility. It reflects the 
ratio of transverse strain to longitudinal strain in a material subjected to 
axial stress. For crystalline structures, Poisson ’ s ratio describes the 
relation between deformation in different directions in the crystal when 
subjected to applied forces. In crystalline structures, Poisson ’ s ratio is 
closely linked to the covalent properties of the crystal. The strength and 
directionality of covalent bonds considerably influence the material ’ s 
deformation behavior under applied forces. Poisson ’ s ratio can also be 
employed to evaluate a material ’ s brittleness or ductility, with a critical 
value of 1/3. When the Poisson ’ s ratio falls below this threshold, it in -
dicates that the material may to exhibit brittle characteristics [ 40 ]. 
According to the data in Table 3 , the Poisson ’ s ratio of all four ternary 
FeCrAl alloys is < 1/3, suggesting that all four alloys demonstrate 
considerable brittleness. Notably, the alignment between the B/G ratio 
and Poisson ’ s ratio in evaluating brittleness highlights the reliability and 
accuracy of the computational methods employed herein.

The dynamical stability of a crystal structure is a key indicator of its 
overall stability. Consequently, the dynamical stability of the FeCrAl 
structure was further examined to evaluate its structural stability. 
Phonon dispersion reveals the vibrational patterns and energy distri -
bution of atoms in a crystal and is commonly employed to assess the 
dynamical stability of structures.
By analyzing the phonon dispersion relations and atomic vibration 
frequencies, we can perform a comprehensive assessment of the 
dynamical stability of the FeCrAl structures [ 44 ]. As illustrated in Fig. 3 , 
we calculated and plotted the phonon spectra for the FeCrAl structures. 
The presence of imaginary frequencies in the phonon spectrum typically 
indicates dynamical instability. Notably, the phonon spectra of all 
FeCrAl structures show no imaginary frequencies in the Brillouin zone, 
clearly indicating that all FeCrAl structures are dynamically stable. In 
summary, the phonon spectrum calculations and analyses provide 
strong confirmation of the dynamical stability of the FeCrAl structures.
The structural stability and mechanical properties of the high- 
temperature material FeCrAl are closely related to its chemical 
bonding and electronic structure [ 45 ]. Based on the previous discus -
sions, we performed a more comprehensive analysis of the electronic 
structure of FeCrAl alloys, with a primary focus on their band structure. 
The evaluated band structure of FeCrAl alloy is presented in Fig. 4 . Fig. 4
clearly depicts the band distribution of the ternary FeCrAl alloy along 
high-symmetry directions. The band structure diagram shows that the 
valence and conduction bands of all four ternary FeCrAl alloys intersect 
the Fermi level with no apparent band gap. This phenomenon indicates 
that electrons can move freely in the bands, demonstrating that all four 
alloys exhibit typical metallic properties. The nonzero electronic states 
near the Fermi level further support this conclusion. Combined with the 
band structure distribution, the Cr-3 d and Fe-3 d orbitals can be inferred 
to be dominating near the Fermi level. The contribution of these tran -
sition metal d orbitals directly influences the strength of chemical bonds 
in the material. These findings provide a foundation for further explo -
ration of the practical application potential of this alloy.
Expanding on the discussion of the band structure of ternary FeCrAl 
alloys, this study further investigates the distribution of electronic DOS 
in these materials. The electronic DOS characterizes the distribution of 
electron energy levels, indicating the number of available states in a 
specific energy range. The behavior of the DOS at the Fermi level has a 
direct impact on the electronic properties of the material. Fig. 5 presents 
the total DOS (TDOS) and partial DOS (PDOS) distributions for the four 
ternary FeCrAl alloys. As shown, the electronic DOS at the Fermi level is 
nonzero for all four alloys, further confirming the metallic nature of all 
four alloys. As illustrated in the figure, the TDOS curves for the four 
ternary FeCrAl alloys predominantly comprise Cr-3 d and Fe-3 d states 
across the entire energy range. A deeper analysis of the PDOS curves 
reveals that the electronic DOS at and near the Fermi level is primarily 
derived from Cr-3 d and Fe-3 d states. The localized hybridization be -
tween the Al-3 p states and the Fe-3 d and Cr-3 d states strengthens the 
formation of Fe – Al and Cr – Al bonds, respectively, highlighting the 
crucial role of Al in enhancing the structural stability and bonding 
characteristics. Moreover, the hybridization between Fe-3 d and Cr-3 d 
states results in the formation of Fe – Cr bond formation. These Cr – Al, 
Fe – Al, and Fe – Cr bonds formed through hybridization strengthen 
interatomic bonding, thereby enhancing the mechanical properties and 
stability of the ternary FeCrAl alloys. The combination of localized d- 
electrons and hybridized bonds forms strong bonding, a characteristic 
that allows the involved alloys to maintain high structural stability at 
elevated temperatures. The findings from the band structure and elec -
tronic DOS indicate that all four ternary FeCrAl alloys exhibit metallic 
behavior. The chemical bond formed by interatomic hybridization im -
proves the mechanical properties of the structure.

principles calculations. This approach enabled identification of four 
new crystal structures of FeCrAl alloys: one tetragonal FeCrAl (space 
group: I4mm), one trigonal FeCrAl (space group: P3m1), and two 
orthorhombic FeCrAl structures (space group: Cmcm). Using first- 
principles calculations, we examined the structural stability, mechani -
cal properties, Debye temperature, and electronic structure of the four 
ternary FeCrAl alloys. The structural stability of the FeCrAl alloys was 
evaluated through formation energy and phonon dispersion analyses. 
The results demonstrate that all structures exhibit thermodynamic and 
dynamic stabilities. Compared to the other FeCrAl structures, the 
orthorhombic (Cmcm-1) FeCrAl exhibits superior thermodynamic sta -
bility. We calculated the elastic constants and elastic modulus of the 
FeCrAl structures; based on the Born stability criteria, all structures 
exhibit excellent mechanical stability. The orthorhombic (Cmcm-1) 
FeCrAl structure has the highest elastic moduli, indicating its superior 
mechanical performance. According to the Pugh criterion and Poisson’s 
ratio, all FeCrAl structures display brittleness. The calculated Debye 
temperatures for the FeCrAl alloys are 673.1 (Cmcm-1), 637.3 (P3m1), 
659.4 (Cmcm-2) and 659.9 (I4mm) K. An analysis of the electronic band 
structures and DOS indicate that all four ternary FeCrAl alloys exhibit 
metallic properties. Overall, this study aims to establish a solid theo -
retical foundation for the optimized design and practical application of 
ternary FeCrAl alloys, thereby promoting their widespread use and 
development for high-temperature materials.
