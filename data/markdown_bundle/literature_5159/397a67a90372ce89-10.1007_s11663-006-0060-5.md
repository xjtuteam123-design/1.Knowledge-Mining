The Effect of g9 Content on the Densities of
Ni-Based Superalloys
ZUSHU LI and KENNETH C. MILLS
The g9 phase increases the mechanical strength of Ni-based superalloys. Equations have been derived
to calculate the atomic percent of g9 phase from the Al content of the alloy. It is shown that increased
g9 phase content results in an increase in density (of the solid) and a decrease in the thermal
expansion coefﬁcient. Equations based on the Al content of the alloy have been derived to calculate
the magnitude of these effects. The coarsening of the g9 phase from 800 °Ct o1 0 0 0°C was found to
be accompanied by an increase in the temperature dependence of the thermal expansion coefﬁcient.
Densities for the entire temperature range of the solid alloys calculated with the equations are within
2 pct of measured values. The strong bonding between the Ni and Al was maintained in the liquid and
resulted in increased densities. Equations were derived to quantify the effect on density (of the liquid)
and the calculated density values for Ni-based superalloys were within 2 pct of the measured values.
These uncertainties are commensurate with the experimental uncertainty in the measurements.
I. INTRODUCTION
MA THEMA TICAL modeling has become an estab-
lished tool for improving product quality and process
control of casting and solidiﬁcation processes. Physical
property values of the speciﬁc alloy are needed as input
data to these models, and it has been shown that the model
predictions improve with accurate property values.
[1,2]
Ni-based superalloys are used in gas turbine engines for
both aircraft and power generation. These alloys contain a
large number of elements, which can be divided into three
groups: (1) Ni, Cr, Co, and Fe; (2) heavy metals, W, Re, Ta,
Mo, Nb, etc.; and (3) Al. The heavy metals provide high-
temperature solution strength and Al imparts further
strength by the formation of the g9 phase (an ordered pre-
cipitate possessing L1
2 crystal structure), which prevents
the movement of dislocations. In these alloys, the g9 phase
can be considered as Ni 3Al or (Ni, Co) 3(Al, Ti). The ele-
ments Ti, Ta, and Nb are considered to promote the for-
mation of g9 phase. However, the g9 phase is also formed in
Al-free Ni-based superalloys where Ni
3Fe or Ni 3Cr can be
formed. Thermodynamic calculations show that these phases
decompose at 506 °C and 540 °C, respectively.[3] Rosen
et al. [4] have shown that Ta substitution of Al in the Ni 3Al
promotes the nucleation rate more efﬁciently than Ti.

It should be pointed out that densities (like enthalpies)
are not particularly sensitive to structural changes, but the
temperature coefﬁcients, dr/dT (or the thermal expansion
coefﬁcient (dV/VdT)) and Cp, are very sensitive to structural
changes. For this reason, it is possible to calculate reason-
able estimates (within 5 pct) of the density for many alloys
using ideal mixing rules. In the case of Ni-based super-
alloys, these calculated values of density tend to be lower
than the measured densities and are of insufﬁcient accuracy
for use in mathematical models.
Consequently, the two objectives of this investigation are
as follows: (1) to determine the effect of the g9 phase on the
densities of Ni-based superalloys; and (2) to develop a
method of calculating reliable values of density and (d r/dT)
for Ni-based superalloys from their chemical compositions.

V . CONCLUSIONS
1. The atomic percent of g9 phase formed in Ni-based
superalloys can be represented by equations based on
the Al content of the alloy.
2. It is shown here that increasing g9 phase content
increases the density and decreases the thermal expan-
sion coefﬁcient of solid Ni-based superalloys; the mag-
nitude of effects can be calculated using equations based
on the Al content of the alloy.
3. The coarsening of the g9 phase above 800 °C is accom-
panied by a marked increase in the slope of expansion
coefﬁcient as a function of temperature.
4. There is evidence to support the view that the density
is particularly sensitive to the amount of Ni
3Al in the
g9 phase.
5. The density of solid over its entire range can be calcu-
lated reliably (within 2 pct) for Ni-based superalloys
from the equations given.
6. The strong bonding between Ni and Al in superalloys is
still present in the liquid and results in higher densities
than those calculated from ideal mixing rules ( r
ideal); the
difference between the measured value ( rmeas) and rideal
can be reliably calculated from equations based on the
Al content of the alloy.
7. The density values for liquid Ni-based superalloys cal-
culated using these equations are within 62 pct of the
measured values; this uncertainty is commensurate with
the experimental uncertainty of the measurements.
