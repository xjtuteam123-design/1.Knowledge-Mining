ABSTRACT 
In order to improve the room-temperature strength and hardness of Ta-W alloys, Zr was chosen for alloying and 
strengthened Ta-W-Zr alloys were developed. The strengthening effect of Zr alloying was investigated based on 
first-principles calculation and experimental verification. Calculations revealed that the doped of Zr atoms 
increased the shear modulus G and Young ’ s modulus E of the alloys and promoted the formation of metallic and 
ionic bonds, which improved the hardness of the alloys. To verify the calculated results, hardness and 
compression tests were carried out for Ta-W-Zr alloys. The results showed that the compressive strength and 
hardness of the alloys increased with increasing Zr content, which was consistent with the trend predicted by the 
calculations. Among them, the maximum compressive strength of Ta-11.3 W-11.2Zr alloy was as high as 1850.53 
± 6.8 MPa and the maximum hardness was up to 478.40 ± 8.3 HV, which was almost twice that of the Ta-10 W 
alloy. It was indicated that Zr alloying can effectively strengthen Ta-W alloys, which provided a guidance for the 
development of high-performance Ta-W alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based high-temperature alloys are widely used in many in -
dustrial fields due to their excellent high-temperature mechanical 
properties [1,2] . However, new challenges have been posed to the 
limiting service temperature of nickel-based high-temperature alloys 
with the rapid development of various industrial fields [3] . The service 
temperature of nickel-based high-temperature alloys is generally in the 
range of 650 – 1200 ℃ [4,5] , improving their high-temperature perfor -
mance by alloying other elements is still unable to meet the performance 
requirements of some ultra-high-temperature environments [6,7] . It is 
well-known that the melting point of pure Ta is ~3020 
◦
C, and its 
plasticity is better [8] . It is also considered to be a bioinert material due 
to its ability to generate an oxide film on its surface and its high 
corrosion resistance and is used in the field of biomaterials [9] . B ˘alțatu 
et al. found that Ta alloying in Ti-based alloys to form Ti-Mo-Zr-Ta alloy 
was a good potential biomaterial and induced the formation of a denser 
oxide layer when the Ta content was increased [10,11] . However, the 
yield strength of Ta is poor and only about 240 MPa. The melting point 
of pure W is ~3620 
◦
C and it has excellent high-temperature strength. 
Unfortunately, it is brittle at room temperature and has unsatisfactory 
ductility [8,12] . Luckily, alloying is an effective method to improve the 
strength of alloys. Adding a small amount of W elements into pure Ta 
forming a solid solution of Ta-W alloys can strengthen the alloy prop -
erties [13,14] . Meanwhile, Ta-W alloys have a high melting point, 
corrosion resistance, wear resistance, low vapor pressure, and low 
evaporation rate at high temperatures [15] . Especially, their room 
temperature processing properties and resistance to ultra-high temper -
atures are more outstanding than those of ceramic composites, which 
have been widely used in the aerospace and weapon industries [16,17] . 
Ta-W alloys solve the problems of the brittle of pure W and the poor 
strength of pure Ta. However, their room temperature strength and 
hardness still need further improvement to broaden their engineering 
application. Refractory elements have high melting points, and alloying 
refractory elements will not cause the melting point of Ta-W alloys to 
decrease too much, ensuring that the alloys still have good 
high-temperature strength. It has been confirmed that alloying re -
fractory elements can effectively improve the room-temperature me -
chanical properties of Ta-W alloys. In comparison to other elements, Ti 
elements have smaller mixing heat and lower VEC with Ta and W

Among many refractory elements, the large lattice size of Zr can 
enhance the lattice distortion effect to improve the mechanical proper -

is easy to generate oxides on the surface of the alloys at high tempera -

investigate whether it can improve the mechanical properties of Ta-W 
alloys. We firstly calculated the elastic constants, hardness, charge 
density and differential charge density of Zr elements replacement 
doped Ta-W alloys through first-principles calculation to predict the 
effect of Zr elements alloying on the mechanical properties of Ta-W al -

characterized based on the calculated data, their compressive mechan -

observed and analyzed, in the hope of investigating their influencing 
mechanisms from a microscopic point of view. 
2. Methods 
2.1. Computational 
The Ta-W-Zr alloys were modelled and calculated via the CASTEP 
module in the Materials Studio (MS) based on the first-principles 
calculation in density flooding theory (DFT). The special quasi- 
disordered supercell (SQS) method was adopted to build crystal 
model. In the calculation, the electron-electron exchange-correlation 
interaction was described by the Perdew Burke Ernzerhof (PBE) gener -
alized function under the Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA) 
[23] . Norm conserving pseudopotential for all the elements in reciprocal 
space was applied. Schr ¨odinger ’ s equation was solved via 
Broyden-Flecher-Goldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) algorithm [24] . During 
structural optimization, the cells and atoms were fully relaxed until the 
SCF iterative convergence conditions were met: energy < 1 × 10
 5 
eV/atom, maximum force < 0.03 eV/Å, maximum pressure < 0.05 GPa, 
and maximum displacement < 0.001 Å.

2.2. Experimental 
Ta-W-Zr alloy ingots were prepared by non-consumptive arc melting 
under a high-purity Ar atmosphere. All ingots were re-melted seven 
times and turned over before each melt to ensure the homogeneity of 
their structure and composition. The microstructure of the alloy speci -
mens was observed using a Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) and the 
elemental distribution was analyzed via equipped with Energy Disper -
sive Spectrometer (EDS) in SEM. The compression mechanical proper -
ties of the alloy specimens were tested with Φ 4 × 6 mm employing an 
electronic universal tensile tester with a rate of 0.5 mm/min. No 
extensometer was used due to the small size of the prepared samples. To 
ensure the accuracy and consistency of the compression tests, the ends 
and cylindrical surfaces of all test specimens were polished with sand -
paper prior, and three specimens of each composition were prepared for 
the compression and hardness tests. 
3. Results and discussion

From the previous microstructures, it can be seen when a small 
amount of Zr was doped, Zr solid solution with a small portion of Ta 
produced solid solution strengthening mechanism, which improved the 
hardness of the alloys. With the increase of Zr content, Zr started to solid 
solution in the whole Ta-W alloy, forming stronger solid solution 
strengthening, and the hardness and strength of the alloys were further 
improved. As can be seen, the experimental results show the same trend 
as the calculated results. 
4. Conclusions 
(a) Calculations revealed that the doped of Zr atoms increased the 
shear modulus G and Young ’ s modulus E of the alloys and pro -
moted the formation of metallic and ionic bonds, which enhanced 
the hardness of the alloys. Meanwhile, the hardness of the alloys 
further increased with increasing Zr content. 
(b) The experimental results showed that alloying Zr elements 
significantly increased the compressive strength and hardness of 
Ta-W alloys. The compressive strength and hardness increased 
with increasing Zr content. The compressive strength and hard -
ness of the Ta-11.3 W-11.2Zr alloy reached about 1850.53 ±
6.8 MPa and 478.40 ± 8.3 HV respectively, which was close to 
twice that of the Ta-10 W alloy. 
(c) The Ta-W-Zr alloys prepared were single-phase BCC structures, 
which proved that the model developed for the calculations was 
correct. The solid solution strengthening mechanism was the 
main strengthening mechanism for improving the mechanical 
properties of alloys. 
The results obtained from the experiments coincide with the trend 
predicted by the calculations, demonstrating that alloying Zr can 
significantly improve the mechanical properties of Ta-W alloys. It 
pointed the way to the preparation of high-strength Ta-W alloys.
