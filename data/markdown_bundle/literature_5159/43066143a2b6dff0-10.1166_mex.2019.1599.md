Microstructure evolution of a Ti–Al–Sn–Zr based
alloy during the hot compression deformation

ABSTRACT
The Ti–Al–Sn–Zr based alloy was fabricated and its deformation behavior was investigated with increasing of
temperature and strain rate. Microstructure and phase of the as-fabricated, heat treated and deformed alloy
was characterized by OM, XRD and TEM. The results exhibited that the addition of Mo, Nb and T a promoted
the precipitate of /SLbetaphase at relative low heat treatment temperature. And the /SLbetaphase has relative good phase
interface with the /SLalphaphase. During the compression test, the compressive strength of the alloy dropped with the
strain rate decreasing and the testing temperature rising. When the compression deformation was preformed
above /SLalphaphase transformation temperature, the high strain rate would promote the phase transformation. When
the alloy was compressed with strain rate of 1 s
−1, it almost had the similar compressive strengths at 960 /compBC
and 940 /compBC. Moreover, the same phenomenon appeared when the compression was carried out at 960 /compBC and
980 /compBC with stain rate of 0.01 s −1. Such a phenomenon should be ascribed to the existence of high amount of
/SLbetaphase which could contribute to the strength.
Keywords: Ti–Al–Sn–Zr Based Alloy, Microstructure, Compressive Strength, Hot Compression.
1. INTRODUCTION
Titanium and its alloys have been paid so much attention
and applied in many ﬁelds, due to their good biocompat-
ibility, high strength to weight ratio, excellent corrosion
resistance, reasonable creep and fatigue properties at rel-
ative high temperature [1–3]. Among the titanium alloys,
the high-temperature titanium alloy are widely applied
in aircraft engine and the typical alloy includes IMI834,
Ti-1100, BT36, Ti600, etc. [4–7]. These high-temperature
titanium alloys could be used as compressor components
and are designed based on composition of Ti–Al–Sn–Zr
which mainly contains /SLalphaphase microstructure. However,
the strength of the high-temperature titanium alloy with
near-/SLalphamicrostructure would decrease greatly when the
working temperature is above 600
/compBC [5, 6]. In order
to conquer such a problem, many researches have been

2. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
The alloy ingot with chemical composition of Ti–5.7Al–
3.6Sn–3Zr–0.5Mo–0.4Ta–0.4Si–0.4Nb (wt.%, Ti–Al–Sn–
Zr based alloy for short) was fabricated from Al, Ti, Zr,
Mo, Sn, Si, Ta and Nb with purity above 99.5% using vac-
uum arc furnace. To ensure the composition homogeneity,
the ingot was remelted for four times. After then, the heat
treatment at 980 /compBC for 1 h was carried out on the alloy
ingot to homogenize composition further. The heat treated
alloy ingot was hot forged into billet subsequent followed
solution treatment of 1200 /compBC for 2 h and air cooling.
The specimens for compression test and heat treatment
were cut from the as-fabricated billet by electric discharge
machining with the dimension of /UPPhi6m m × 8 mm. The
heat treatments were performed at 900
/compBC, 960 /compBCa n d
1050 /compBC with water quenching to study the microstructure
evolution.
The Gleeble 3800 was employed to perform the com-
pression tests at 900 /compBC, 920 /compBC, 940 /compBC, 960 /compBC, 980 /compBC
and 1050 /compBC, respectively. The initial stain rate was
set as 0.001, 0.01 and 1 s −1. During the hot compres-
sion, the specimen was induction heated rapidly to the
deforming temperature and kept for 5 mins. Then the
compression was performed at constant temperature and
the deformation fraction of the tests was about 50%.
After compression, the specimen was water quenched
to keep the deforming microstructure. The specimen for
microstructure observation w as prepared by mechanical
polishing and chemical etching with the Kroll solution
(HF:HNO
3:H2O = 1:2:6, volume fraction). The Axiovert
200 MA T optical microscope (OM) was used to per-
form the microstructural analysis. The phase constituent
was characterized by the X-ray diffraction (XRD) analy-
sis using the Rigaku D/mac-2400 diffractometer with Cu
K/SLalpharadiation. The JEM-2100 transmission electron micro-
scope (TEM) was used to perform the crystal structure
characterization. The sp ecimen for TEM was cut from the
as-fabricated billet with the thickness of 0.4 mm. Then the
specimen was polished to 25 /SLmum and shaped into /SLvarphi3m m
in diameter followed by ion milling to perforation

4. CONCLUSIONS
The Ti–Al–Sn–Zr based alloy was fabricated and its defor-
mation behavior with increasing of temperature and strain
rate was investigated. With the microstructure characteri-
zation, some conclusions can be drawn.
(1) The addition of Nb, Mo and Ta promote the formation
of /SLbetaphase at relative low heat treatment temperature. And
the /SLbetaphase has relative good phase interface with the /SLalpha
phase.
(2) During the compression test, the compressive strength
of the alloy drops with the strain rate decreasing and the
testing temperature rising. When the deformation tempera-
ture is higher than the /SLalphaphase transformation temperature,
the high strain rate would promote the phase transforma-
tion.
