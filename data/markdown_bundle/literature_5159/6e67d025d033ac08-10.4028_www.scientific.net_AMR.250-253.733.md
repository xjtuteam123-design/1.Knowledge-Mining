Key words: Ferro superalloy, Nickel, High temperature tensile strength, Oxidation resistance 
Abstract. The affecting law of nickel on structure and properties of ferro superalloy, which was cast 
by intermediate frequency induction furnace, were studied by orthogonal experimental method. The 
tensile strength and oxidation weight gain rate at 1000 ℃ were tested. The matrix microstructure 
and scale morphologies and composition were studied using scanning electron microscope (SEM) 
and X-ray diffraction (XRD) respectively. It was fo und that the high temperature tensile strength 
went up with the increase of nickel by the forming of high temperature strengthening phase of γ’. 
After 100 hours aging at 1000 ℃, γ’ phase separated in blocks. Certain content of nickel improved 
the high temperature oxidation resistance of test alloys, but excess of it caused the higher oxidation 
weight gain rate and loose structure oxide scales w ith holes and exfoliating. In considering of high 
temperature tensile strength and oxidation resistance, the ideal content of nickel in ferro superalloy 
should be 9wt.%, with which at 1000 ℃ the tensile strength of test alloys mostly exceede d 70MPa, 
and the average oxidation weight gain rate was only 0.55g.m -2.h -1, reaching the strong oxidation 
resistance. Such scale was compounded by Cr 2O3 and Fe(Ni)Cr 2O4 with compact structure, fine and 
even oxide grains. 
Introduction 
Ferro superalloy, a special type of alloy based on iron matrix, could be improved greatly in 
high temperature mechanical properties and erosion resistance by multi elements alloying. 
With lower price and predominant performance compar ing to cobalt or nickel superalloys, 
ferro superalloy is applied broadly in energy sourc es, aviation and petrochemical industry 
high temperature structural materials[1]. 
Adjusting of elements proportion is the straight vi rtual technique to ameliorate the 
capabilities of ferro superalloy. So this paper wil l research deeply into the effects of nickel on the 
high temperature tensile strength and oxidation res istance. This study is expected to provide 
necessary experimental basis for developing new kinds of superalloys with lower nickel content and 
higher qualities.

Results and discussion 
The relation curve (see Fig.1(a)) of nickel content versus high temperature tensile strength was 
made by calculating the sum strength of the same content, which showed that the tensile strength of 
test alloys went up gradually with content increase of nickel. When a few nickel presents in alloys, 
most of them dissolve in austenite, enlarging auste nite phase area, raising recrystallization 
temperature and thus enhancing the high temperature creep resistance of matrix. With the add of 
nickel, some of them begins to form γ’ (Ni
3Al), a sort of high temperature strengthening phase 
intensifying the matrix, besides those in austenite . With features of dispersed nucleating, fine 
particle with coherent lattice, narrow grain space, low interface energy, high stability, strength and 
plasticity, γ’ phase is easy to separate during agi ng at high temperature and strengthen the matrix 
 
mightily with temperature going up, which endowed i t the strongest strengthening phase in matrix 
of superalloys. When less than 6% in weight, nickel was found to form γ’ hardly but dissolve in

Therefore, the quantity of γ’ phase in matrix could be controlled by proper adjustment of 
composition to optimize the high temperature mechan ical properties .The majority of test alloys

The effects of nickel content on the oxidation resi stance was shown in Fig.1(b). The sum of the 
oxidation weight gain rate of the same five content level decreased with nickel in the zone of 
0~9wt.%, but ran up in 9wt.%~12wt.%, and the oxidation resistance went up steadily with nickel at 
first, then fell over 9wt.%. Nickel which had serve d an important role to raise high temperature 
tensile strength bore considerable corrosion resist ance. Being chemical inactivity, uneasy to be 
oxidated, hard to combine with sulphur or chlorine, nickel endued the structure of alloys with 
comparatively high stabilization, so that nickel superalloys were employed widely. On the one hand, 
nickel raised the electrode potential of matrix, lightening electrochemistry corrosion and enhancing 
oxidation resistance of alloys. On the other hand, nickel formed rich nickel oxide scales stable in 
chemistry on the surface of grains, it usually rein forced the corrosion resistance of alloys with the 
help of chromium by compounding and compensating th e scale of Cr 2O3 with spinal oxide of 
Fe(Ni)Cr 2O4 created from ferro, nickel and chromium together. Fig.3 showed the scale morphology

Conclusions 
(1) Sufficient nickel formed γ’ phase, strengthening the matrix effectively at high temperature. 
(2) γ’ 
 phase separated in blocks at 1000 ℃ aged for 100 hours and the percentage in matrix co uld be 
controlled by adjusting the composition of test alloys. 
(3) Proper content of nickel increased the oxidatio n resistance of ferro superalloy, but overfeeding 
acted adversely. 
(4) Taking the two capabilities of tensile strength and oxidation resistance into account synthetically, 
the ideal content of nickel should be 9% in weight. 
(5) At 1000 ℃, with 9wt.% nickel, ferro superalloy presented ove r 70MPa tensile strength and 
compounded composition oxide scale of Cr 
2O3 and Fe(Ni)Cr 2O4 with compact structure and fine 
grains.
