Effect of niobium and carbon on
microstructure and compressive yield strength
of as cast ESR Fe–8 .5Al alloy
R. G. Baligidad *1, Abhijit Dutta 1 and A. Sambasiv Rao 1
The effect of niobium (1 .5 and 3 .5 wt-%) addition on the microstructure and mechanical
properties of as cast ESR Fe–8 .5Al–0.1C alloy has been studied. Alloys were prepared by a
combination of air induction melting with flux cover (AIMFC) and electroslag remelting (ESR). As
cast ESR alloys were characterised using an optical microscope, a scanning electron microscope
(SEM) and an electron probe microanalyser (EPMA). Compression tests were carried out on as
cast ESR alloy samples at temperatures up to 800 uC. Addition of 1 .5 wt-% Nb to Fe–8 .5Al–0.1C
alloy resulted in a marginal improvement in the compressive yield strength at test temperatures up
to 1073 K, whereas addition of 3 .5 wt-% Nb significantly improved the strength. This significant
improvement in the room and high temperature compressive strength may be attributed to solid
solution strengthening as well as precipitation hardening by the presence of fine and higher
volume fractions of niobium and niobium carbide precipitates, in contrast to the relatively soft
Fe3AlC0.5 precipitates present in the Fe–8 .5Al–0.1C alloy.
Keywords: Iron aluminides, Fe 3AlC0.5, Electroslag remelting, Nb addition, Microstructure, Compressive strength
Introduction
Iron aluminides based on Fe3Al are being considered for
high temperature applications. 1,2 They could be poten-
tial replacements for the more expensive high tempera-
ture structural alloys containing strategic elements such
as nickel and chromium. However iron aluminides
generally exhibit poor room temperature ductility, low
fracture toughness and poor creep properties. They
also exhibit poor machinability, and susceptibility
to hydrogen embrittlement in the presence of water
or atmospheric moisture.
2,3 Fe3Al based alloys contain
13–21 wt-%Al. Recently, it has been reported that a
reduction of aluminium content to 8.5 wt-% results in an
enhanced ductility without signiﬁcant loss in strength. 2,4
The improvement in ductility has been attributed to a
reduced susceptibility to environmental embrittlement
because of the reduction in aluminium content. More
recently it has been shown that addition of carbon to
Fe–8.5Al alloy resulted in improved strength.

been attributed to the formation of perovskite based

Experimental procedure
Melts of approximately 40 kg of three alloys were
prepared in an Inductotherm medium frequency air
induction melting furnace of 50 kg capacity (all compo-
sitions are in wt-% unless otherwise speciﬁed). The
nominal composition of the base alloy was Fe–8 .5Al–
0.1C and 1 .5% and 3 .5% of niobium was added to the
base line composition, respectively. The base line, 1 .5%
and 3.5% Nb alloys will be referred as ‘0 Nb’, ‘1 .5 Nb’
and 3 .5 Nb’ alloys in the remaining text. Commercial
purity aluminium, niobium and steel scrap were used as
raw materials. The total amount of impurities (Mn, Si,
Cu, etc.) present in the melt stock was about 0 .7 wt-%.
Alloys were prepared by air induction melting (AIM).
The melt was protected from the atmosphere with a ﬂux
cover during AIM. The AIM ingots were reﬁned by
electroslag remelting (ESR). All further evaluation and
processing were carried out using the ESR ingots.
The melting practice has been described in detail
elsewhere.7–9 The soundness of ESR ingots was eval-
uated by a radiography unit using a 18 .561010 Bq 60Co
c-ray source. Longitudinal sections of the ingots were
cut using a bimetallic band saw blade. The cut-off
sections were mechanically polished to 0 .5 mm grade
diamond powder ﬁnish, and etched by 3 .3%
HNO3z3.3% CH 3COOHz3.3% H 2Oz0.1% HF (by
volume). Scanning electron microscope (SEM) and
electron probe microanalysis (EPMA) studies were
carried out to identify the phases present in the alloys.

Results and discussion
Radiography did not reveal shrinkage or gas porosity in
the ingots. Microcracks were observed (Fig. 1) in all the
ingots. These cracks were attributed to the high
hydrogen concentrations (6 ppm) 10 and were probably
responsible for the failure of a few compression test
samples during fabrication. This is in contrast to the
crack free nature of ingots produced by other routes that
use vacuum or inert gas for atmospheric protection,
leading to low hydrogen pick up.
2,11

contrast precipitates were observed in the Nb containing
alloys (Fig. 2). The compositions of the alloy phases

The compression test results are summarised in Fig. 6.
Addition of 1.5% niobium to the base alloy resulted in a
marginal improvement of bulk hardness (241–254 HV),
room temperature yield strength (480–520 MPa) as
well as high temperature yield strength up to 1073 K.
However, a further increase in the niobium addition to
3.5 wt-% resulted in a signiﬁcant increase of bulk
hardness (254–336 HV), and also the room temperature
yield strength (from 520 to 980 MPa). The yield strength
at temperatures up to 400 uC was found to increase
signiﬁcantly with niobium content. Above 673 K all the
three alloys rapidly lost strength, indicating no signiﬁ-
cant beneﬁt of Nb addition (Fig. 6). Niobium addition
to the binary Fe
3Al alloy was previously found to
increase yield strength at temperature up to 923 K. 13

Conclusions
1. As cast ESR ingots of base alloy Fe–8 .5Al–0.1C
(alloy 1) exhibited a two-phase microstructure having a
small volume fraction of Fe
3AlC0.5 precipitates in the
Fe–Al ( a) matrix. Addition of 1 .5 wt-%Nb to the base
alloy resulted in the precipitation of niobium carbide in
the Fe–Al (a) matrix. The addition of 3.5 wt-%Nb to the
base alloy resulted in the precipitation of niobium in
addition to niobium carbide.
2. Alloys containing niobium exhibited much smaller
grain size as compared to the base alloy. This may be
attributed to the grain reﬁnement during solidiﬁcation
by formation of high melting point niobium and
niobium carbide precipitates.
3. Addition of 1 .5 wt-%Nb to Fe–8 .5Al–0.1C alloy
resulted in a marginal improvement in the compressive
yield strength at test temperatures up to 1073 K whereas
a3 .5% addition of Nb signiﬁcantly improved the
strength. This improvement may be attributed to the
solid solution strengthening and precipitation hardening
by the presence of ﬁne and more volume fractions of
niobium and niobium carbide precipitates.
