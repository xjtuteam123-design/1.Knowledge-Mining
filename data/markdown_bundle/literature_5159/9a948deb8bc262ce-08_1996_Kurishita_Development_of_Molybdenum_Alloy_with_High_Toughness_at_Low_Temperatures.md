Materials Transactions, JIM, Vol. 37, No. 1 (1996), pp. 89 to 97 Development of Molybdenum Alloy with High Toughness at Low Temperatures Hiroaki Kurishita, Yuji Kitsunait, Yutaka Hiraokatt, Tamaki Shibayama and Hideo Kayano The Oarai Branch, Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Oarai, Ibaraki 311-13, Japan The irradiation embrittlement and recrystallization embrittlement of molybdenum and its alloys are major prob- lems for their application to plasma facing components in the future fusion reactors. In order to overcome the problems, the basic idea of alloy design and microstructure control was proposed, and a TiC dispersed molybdenum alloy was de- veloped by applying mechanical alloying and hot isostatic pressing treatments. The alloy, designated as MTC-02, con- tained an additive of 0.2 mass% titanium carbide. TEM observations revealed that the characteristic of microstructures of the alloy was extremely small grain size, a high density of dislocations and a fairly large number of very fine particles of Ti-oxy-carbide which exist along grain boundaries and in grain interiors. The low-temperature toughness of MTC-02 in four different states, i.e., the as-rolled, stress-relieved, recrystallized and neutron irradiated states, was examined by impact three-point bending tests using miniaturized flat specimens (1 mm x 1 mm x 20 mm). For comparison, the same examination was conducted for TZM alloy which is known to have the highest Iow-temperature toughness among the commercially available molybdenum alloys. It is shown that in each of the four states MTC-02 exhibits a much lower duc- tile-brittle transition temperature and a considerably higher strength than TZM, and the fracture of MTC-02 is complete- ly transgranular, indicating that the grain boundaries are strengthened. It is argued that MTC-02 is unsusceptible to the detrimental effect of oxygen impurity. Introduction Plasma facing components of future fusion reactors will be subjected to very severe environments of high heat load and high energetic particle irradiations. At present, however, there are no materials that can be ensured for the use under such severe conditions. Very recently, molybdenum and its alloys, together with tungsten and its alloys, have again received much attention as the most promising materials for the plasma facing components be- cause of the high melting points, good thermal conductiv- ity, high resistance to sputtering and erosion, and high level of These materials, however, are very susceptible to intergranular failure and thus have high ductile-to-brittle transition temperature In particular, the intergranular failure occurs in the recrystallized state (recrystallization embrittlement) and in the neutron irradiated state (irradiation embrittle- ment), which are major problems for their applications. In molybdenum alloys, with which this paper is con- cerned, the highest temperature for the occurrence of full recrystallization is reported to be approximately 2073 K for 1.8 ks-anneal(10) and approximately 1873 K for 3.6 ks- anneal(ll). These temperatures, however, may be exceed- t Graduate Student, Tohoku University. tt Department of Applied Physics, Okayama University of Science, I-I Ridai-cho, Okayama 700, Japan. ed by the high heat load to which plasma facing compo- nents should be exposed, resulting in recrystallization embrittlement. TZM alloy (Mo-O.5Ti-0.1Zr) is known to exhibit the highest low-temperature toughness among the commercially available molybdenum alloys. However, once it is fully recrystallized, it shows a drastic loss in duc- tility and its DBTT increases to above room temperature, as measured by impact three-point bending In addition, TZM alloy suffers from significant irradiation It is therefore necessary to develop the molybdenum alloys that are unsusceptible to both the recrystallization embrittlement and the irradiation embrittlement. In this paper, the basic idea of alloy design and micro- structure control for overcoming recrystallization embrit- tlement and irradiation embrittlement is first described and is applied to molybdenum. The low-temperature toughness of a developed alloy in the as-rolled, stress- relieved, recrystallized and neutron irradiated states are examined by impact three-point bending tests and com- pared with that of TZM alloy. II. Alloy Design and Microstructure Control The target for alloy development is to suppress both recrystallization embrittlement and irradiation embrittle- ment significantly. In addition, in view of the fact that both the recrystallization embrittlement and the irradia- tion embrittlement inevitably occur to some extent, it

First, in order to lower the DBTT in the unirradiated state, it is necessary to strengthen grain boundaries, be- cause the poor low-temperature toughness of molybde- num and tungsten is known to be caused by the weakness of grain which is the intrinsic properties of those Secondly, in order to reduce the recrystallization embrittlement, it is necessary either to in- crease the recrystallization temperature above the oper- ating temperatures by hindering the migration of grain boundaries at high temperatures or to reduce the degree of recrystallization embrittlement by maintaining the strengthening of grain boundaries after recrystallization. Thirdly, in order to reduce the irradiation embrittlement, it is necessary to suppress the change in microstructure due to irradiation (radiation damage) by providing a large number of sinks for vacancies and interstitials in- duced during irradiation. One of the authors showed that the addition of TiC to molybdenum has the beneficial effect of improving the low-temperature toughness by strengthening the grain TiC addition is also expected to have the effects of increasing the recrystallization temperature and relieving the irradiation damage significantly because TiC may hinder the migration of grain boundaries and the interface between TiC and molybdenum matrix will act as sinks for irradiation-induced defects. Besides grain boundaries and dislocations are also effective sinks. It is thus deduced that the key to the alloy development is to achieve the homogeneous distribution of very fine TiC particles at grain boundaries and the introduction of many grain boundaries and dislocations in molybdenum. In order to achieve such microstructures, mechanical alloying (MA) treatment is expected to be most suitable. In this study, therefore, MA treatment is applied to pre- pare a TiC-dispersed molybdenum alloy. The MA treated powders are subjected to hot isostatic pressing (HIP) treatment for densification because the HIP permits to sinter the powders at fairly low temperatures where no appreciable grain growth may occur. III. Experimental Procedure 1. Materials and specimens The starting materials were the powders of pure molyb- denum (an average particle size 5 gm and purity 99.9%) and TiC (0.57 ,um, 98%). These powders were mixed to provide the target composition of Mo��O.2 mass%TiC, which is designated as MTC-02. The mixed powders were put into a vessel made of WC and then subjected to MA treatment by ball milling with a planetary ball mill for 180 ks in an argon atmosphere (purity 99.9999%). All of these procedures were conducted in a specially designed glove box that enables to prevent the powders from being contaminated with oxygen and nitrogen in air before and after MA treatment. The X-ray analysis was made to de- termine the grain size of the MA-treated specimen and examine whether or not new compounds are formed by MA treatment. The result showed that the average grain diameter was approximately 30 nm and new substances were not detected. Hot isostatic pressing (HIP) treatment was conducted at about 1580K at 200 MPa for 18 ks in an argon at- mosphere using a Dr. HIP of KOBELCO. The measured density of the HIP treated body was 10.16 g/ cm3, approx- imately 99.8% of theoretical one, and no pores were ob- served by optical and transmission electron microscopy. The densified body was hot forged at around 1500 K and then hot and warm rolled to approximately 1 mm thick. Table 1 shows the chemical compositions of the as-rolled sheet of MTC-02 together with those of TZM. It should be noted that carbon added as TiC is all retained, but MTC-02 contains a considerable amount of oxygen com- pared with TZM. The as-rolled sheets of MTC-02 and TZM were machined to the dimensions of 1 mm by I mm by 20 mm with the longitudinal direction parallel to the rolling direction. The miniaturized specimens were mechanically polished with emery paper of #300 to 1500 and finally buff polished with A1203 particles of 1.0 to 0.3 gm. Some of the specimens were annealed in a vacuum of 10-5 Pa at 1199 K for 0.9 ks for stress relief and at 2073 K for 3.6 ks for full recrystallization: the full recrystallization tem- peratures for 3.6 ks-anneal of MTC-02 and TZM are found to be approximately 2073 and 1673 K, respec- tively(28). Chemical analysis of the annealed MTC-02 showed that for the stress-relieved specimens there are no appreciable changes in titanium, carbon, nitrogen and oxygen, but for the fully recrystallized specimens carbon, nitrogen and oxygen were decreased, the amounts of loss being 150, 110 and 58 mass ppm, respectively. Neutron irradiation was performed for some of the as- rolled specimens to a fast-neutron fluence of 1023 n/m2 (E > 0.1 MeV) at about 573 K in the Japan Materials Test- ing Reactor (JMTR). This fluence corresponds to 0.01 dpa (displacement per atom) and this irradiation tempera- ture was reported to cause a drastic embrittlement of refractory metals(S). V. Discussion The application of the alloy design and microstructure control proposed in this paper to overcome the recrystalli- zation embrittlement and irradiation embrittlement in the group Vla refractory metals led to the development of molybdenum alloy with much more improved low tem- perature toughness than T ZM alloy regardless of heat treatment and neutron irradiation. This production proc- ess is expected to be applicable also to tungsten, which is our another target, having the highest melting point (3683 K) of metals, thus possibly showing the highest resistance to high heat load. In this study MA and HIP treatments, followed by forging and rolling, were applied to obtain the micro- structure that may meet our idea of alloy design. Further, the as- rolled specimen of MTC-02 showed the microstructures, the characteristic of which is extremely small grain size, very fine particles existing along grain boundaries though some of particles are present in grain interior, as men- tioned in Section IV-I, and a high density of disloca- tions. These results are essentially the same as our expec- tation stated in Section II. However, chemical analysis of the as-rolled MTC-02 showed that a considerable amount of oxygen, approximately 1000 mass ppm, is con- tained as impurity. Oxygen impurity is known to give a detrimental effect of decreasing drastically the grain thus losing the boundary strength of molybdenum low temperature toughness significantly. In commercial molybdenum alloys, e.g., in P/M fabricated TZM the level of oxygen impurity is reduced to 500 mass ppm or less (Table 1). In the developed alloy, however, a con- taminant level of oxygen impurity attained approximate- ly 1000 mass ppm in the as-rolled and stress-relieved specimens and 850 mass ppm in the full recrystallized specimens. The reduction of carbon and oxygen in the recrystallized MTC-02 may be caused by reaction of C+O*CO, since the reduced amount of carbon and oxygen was almost the same, as shown in Section III-I. It was found that such high oxygen levels in the as-rolled sheets are es- sentially independent of the amount of TiC additions as far as the present process is applied(28). In spite of this high oxygen level, however, it is surprising that MTC-02 exhibited much improved toughness at low temperatures in the as-rolled, stress relieved, fully recrystallized and neutron irradiated states. Therefore, we can say that the microstructure of the developed alloy is unsusceptible to the detrimental effect of oxygen impurity. As shown previously, the grain boundary appeared to be covered with a very thin layer of titanium oxide and there existed very fine particles along the grain bound- aries. Such particles were identified to be Ti-oxy-carbide, not pure titanium carbide and were found to have good coherency with either of matrices adjacent to the parti- cles. The presence of titanium oxide and Ti-oxy-carbide indicates that TiC has decomposed into titanium and car- bon atoms during the production process and the carbon atoms may be changed into molybdenum carbides and/ or free carbon atoms segregating at grain boundaries because the solubility of carbon in molybdenum is ex- tremely low at and below the processing temperature. A study on grain boundary chemistry of MTC-02 using FIM is now in progress. Although the effects of thin titanium oxide layer and Ti-oxy-carbide particles on the grain boundary strength are not yet known, the particles may contribute to in- crease the grain boundary strength since the particles showed good coherency with either of matrices adjacent to the particles. Conclusions The proposed alloy design and microstructure control to overcome the recrystallization embrittlement and irradiation embrittlement in the group Vla refractory metals proved to make a great improvement of low tem- perature toughness of molybdenum in the as-rolled, stress-relieved, fully recrystallized and neutron irradiated states. The molybdenum alloy with 0.2 mass% TiC addi- tion, developed in this study and designated as MTC-02, exhibited the microstructures, the characteristic of which is extremely fine grains with a high density of dislocations

Such microstruc- tures appeared to be unsusceptible to the detrimental effect of oxygen impurity. The comparison of impact three-point bending test results for MTC-02 and TZM showed that in each of the above four states MTC-02 takes much lower DBTT (mostly by about 90 K) and 1.2- 1.5 times higher maximum strength than TZM. This great improvement of Iow-temperature impact toughness is attributable to the effects of grain boundary strengthen- ing and grain refinement, which may be maintained after recrystallization and neutron irradiation. Further ex- perimental study on the effect of higher level of neutron irradiation on the low temperature toughness of MTC-02 is required.
