Effects of Nb on the High Temperature Mechanical Properties of 
TiNiAl Alloys 
G.S.Qiu a, X.Q.Zhao b, L.J.Meng and H.B.Xu 
School of Materials Science and Engineering, Beijing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, 
Beijing 100083, China 
aqiugaosong@.sohu.com, bxinqing@buaa.edu.cn 
Key words: TiNiAl, Nb, Intermetallics, high temperature 
Abstract. A series of NiTiAl based alloys with different amount of Nb addition were prepared and the 
effects of Nb on both the mechanical properties and microstructure evolution were investigated. The 
addition of Nb can remarkably enhance the high temperature yield strength of these alloys. The 
highest yield strength of the alloys with 10 at% Nb reaches 1237MPa at 873K and 875MPa at 973K, 
respectively. The Ti 2Ni(Al,Nb) precipitates and the solution strengthening effect might be responsible 
for the high yield stress achieved at high temperature. 
Introduction 
As potential high temperature structural materials, NiTiAl based intermetallics recently attracted 
attention, in attempt to obtain high temperature intermetallics combined room temperature ductility 
and high temperature strength. This idea originates from that TiNi binary alloys, known as shape 
memory alloys, not only performs high melting point and good tensile strength, but presents 
exceptional good ductility even at room temperature 
[1-4] . It has been recognized that any composition 
deviation from the stoichiometry give rise to the precipitation of second phases, which could be 
helpful for the strengthening or hardening of the TiNi based alloys [5-7] . Y.koimuzi found that with the 
Al addition substituting for Ti in Equiatomic TiNi, the compression strength was dramatically 
improved, especially when the Al amount was high enough to precipitate Ni 2TiAl phase coherented to 
NiTi(B2) matrix. Recent investigation indicated that the substitution of Al for Ni could remarkably 
reinforce the TiNi based intermetallics by precipitation of (Ti,Al) 2Ni phase and by solid solution 
strengthening effects [6] . As a widely used alloying element, Nb could play key role in strengthening 
and oxygen resistance improving superalloys and intermetallics [6-9]. In the present paper, we report 
the influence of additive Nb on the microstructure evolution and high temperature mechanical 
properties of TiNiAl alloys. 
Experimental 
High purity Ti(99.9%), Ni (99.99%), Nb (99.95%), and Al (99.99%) were used as raw materials to 
prepare 
 alloys. Ingots (about 40g) were made by arc melting on a water-cooled copper hearth. Five 
types of alloys Ti 50 Ni 44-xAl 6Nb x (x=2, 4, 6, 8, 10) were designed with Nb addition substituting the Ni 
of Ti 50 Ni 44 Al 6. All the ingots were homogenized at 900 °C for 24h in vacuum and cooled down to 
room temperature in the furnace. Specimens with dimensions of Φ6 mm ×9 mm for compressive 
mechanical tests and other experiments were prepared by the spark cutter, and polished by the grinder. 
Compression testing were done by a 880 MTS materials test system, and the test conditions were 
respectively 873K, 923K, 973K and 1073K in the open air. The microstructures of the specimen were 
determined by D/max 2200pc X-ray diffraction. And the backscattered electron images and chemical 
constitution were prepared and examined by a JSM5600 scanning electron microscope (SEM) 
equipped with an X-ray energy-dispersive spectrometry (Link ISIS-300, Oxford Instruments).

Results and discussions 
Effects of Nb on microstructures. X-ray diffraction measurements as shown in Fig.1 indicates
 that 
TiNiAl intermetallic alloy exhibits dual-phase microstructure, i.e. TiNi (Al) based matrix and 
(Ti,Al) 2Ni precipitates. Fig.2. shows the SEM images of alloys with different amount of Nb. One can 
see from the figure that only TiNi matrix and precipitated Ti 2Ni (Al,Nb) phase can be observed in the 
alloy with 2at%Nb. In the samples with Nb content greater than 4% Nb, a Nb-rich Ti 2Ni phase in 
white color can be observed. The amount of Ti 2Ni(Al,Nb) and Nb-rich Ti 2Ni phase increases with the 
increasing Nb addition, and the amount of TiNi matrix decreases.

Characteristics of high temperature mechanical properties. Fig.3 is the Stress-strain curves from 
compression tests individually at 873K and 973K, we found that TiNiAl(Nb) alloys indicated 
outstanding high temperature strength at these temperature. The yield strength increased with the 
increasing Nb addition At 873K, and the highest yield strength of 10at% Nb alloys could reach 
1237Mpa, even the 2at% Nb alloys could get 980Mpa yield strength. At 973K, the yield strength of 
these alloys decreased obviously than that of 873k, but 875Mpa can still be obtained at 10at%Nb 
alloys . 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Fig.3.Stress-strain curves for thecompressiontestsat873k and 973K 
Fig.4 shows the Stress-strain curves for the compression tests at 1073 K. It is found that the yield 
strength of all the alloys decreased sharply corresponding to the yield strength at 873 K and 973K, 
approximate 350MPa of yield strength was achieved. All these alloys with different amount of Nb 
exhibited almost the same yield strength. The effects of Nb on strengthening alloys seems to be 
obscure at 1073 K.

Conclusions 
The addition of Nb can remarkably enhance the high temperature yield strength of these alloys, 
especially at 873 K and 973 K. The Ti 
2Ni(Al,Nb) precipitates and the solution strengthening effect 
might be responsible for the high yield stress achieved at high temperature.
