Abstract
Starting from the base composition with 12 at.% Al, 6 at.% Cr, 5 at.% Ni and Pt balance, precipitation hardened Pt eAleCreNi alloys with
additions of 2 at.% Mo, Re, Ru and W, respectively, were investigated. After homogenization heat treatment and air cooling, all ﬁve-component
alloys show a bimodal distribution of g0 precipitates. Along with a fraction of ﬁne g0 precipitates embedded in a Pt-rich matrix, some coarse
primary g0 particles are observed. Mo, Re and W additions increase strength above 1000 /C14 C, whereas Ru has no beneﬁcial effect on strength.
W increases the g0 volume fraction most effectively, however, the oxidation resistance is worsened dramatically by W additions. The Re-con-
taining alloy shows the best resistance against g0 growth and coarsening during long-term ageing.

1. Introduction
In the ﬁeld of materials for high-temperature application
and high mechanical loads, Ni-base superalloys are being
used and enhanced for a couple of decades. Main reason for
the success of these alloys is their optimized precipitation
hardened microstructure promoting high creep strength. In
general, best mechanical behavior is achieved in alloys with
initially about 500 nm sized cuboidal and L1
2-ordered
Ni3(Al, Ti) particles (called g0 phase) coherently embedded
in a Ni-rich matrix or g phase. The pure intermetallic g0 phase
Ni3Al shows a ﬂow stress anomaly, i.e. maximum ﬂow stress
at temperatures between 500 /C14 C and 800 /C14 C, depending on
several factors like the respective composition and orientation
of the load axis [1e3]. A subject to extensive research is to
raise strength, temperature capability and phase stability by
adding more high melting point elements such as Re, Ru, Ir
or Mo [4,5]. However, the potential operation temperatures
of recent Ni-base superalloys gradually come closer to the
melting point of the base metal Ni [4,5].
Numerous studies during the last decade have demonstrated
that in alloys based on platinum group metal microstructures
similar to Ni-base superalloys can be generated by speciﬁc
heat treatment and ageing [6e20]. So-called ‘refractory super-
alloys’ [6] based on Ir and Rh with comparable strength at
much higher temperatures were developed; for example an al-
loy with 4.25 at.% Nb, 9 at.% Zr and Ir balance shows a 0.2%
ﬂow stress beyond 400 MPa at 1800
/C14 C [7].
Precipitation hardened Pt-base alloys are being developed
by groups in the UK [8,9], and partially in joint research by
groups in South Africa, Japan and Germany [9e20]. Due to
the high price and density of the base metal Pt, these alloys
are not supposed to become a substitute for commercial Ni-
base superalloys in turbine blades. In fact, precipitation hard-
ened Pt-base alloys have a potential to be used in aerospace
[21] or as structural materials in glass industry [8], where

6. Discussion
The segregation of individual elements in as-cast Pt-base
alloys was investigated. Ni is displaced from the dendrite to
the interdendritic region, when the refractory elements Mo,
Re, Ru or W are added. In contrast to the equal distribution
of Cr and Mo to both dendrite core and interdendritic region
in the Ni-base superalloy CMSX-4 [24], these elements are
preferentially found in the dendrite core of the investigated
Pt-base alloys. Re and W segregate much stronger to the den-
drite core in Pt-base alloys compared to CMSX-4.
After homogenization heat treatment, bimodal g
0 particle
distributions in Pt-rich matrices were found in all alloys except
Pt
77Al12Cr6Ni5. Large areas of g0 occur in addition to small
cuboidal or shamrock like g0 particles ( Fig. 3 bee). V o¨lkl
et al. [16] concluded from investigations of the PteAleSc sys-
tem that the ﬁne particles are secondary precipitates from the
supersaturated g matrix during slow cooling, whereas former
eutectic regions have coarsened to large connected areas of
g
0 during homogenization heat treatment. The quaternary alloy
Pt77Al12Cr6Ni5 has a monomodal distribution of ﬁne g0 parti-
cles after homogenization heat treatment and is completely
homogenized ( Fig. 3 a). Hence the ﬁve-component alloys
could not be completely homogenized at 1510
/C14 C in contrast
to Pt77Al12Cr6Ni5. The elements Mo, Re, Ru and W obviously
alter the course of the g0 solvus line so that the nominal com-
position at homogenization temperature is still situated in the
g þ g
0 two-phase ﬁeld, not in the single-phase g region
(Fig. 11).

In agreement with Hill et al. [14], Mo, Re, Ru and W are
enriched in the g matrix. Similar partitioning behavior of
Mo, Re and W was reported by Wanderka and Glatzel [23]
for the Ni-base superalloy CMSX-4 after standard heat treat-
ment. Adding such g-forming elements to the base alloy
Pt
77Al12Cr6Ni5 was expected to lead to a smaller g0 volume
fraction, fg0, depending on the magnitude of the respective el-
ement distribution coefﬁcient. However, precisely the opposite
was observed in this study. Analysis of SEM micrographs re-
vealed that Mo, Re and W additions lead to increased g
0 vol-
ume fractions compared to Pt 77Al12Cr6Ni5. In conclusion,
these refractory elements may act in two different ways. Either
they increase the solubility of g-forming elements such as Pt
and Ni in the g
0 phase (mechanism ‘‘A’’ inFig. 11) or they de-
crease the solubility of g0 formers like Al in the g matrix
(mechanism ‘‘B’’ inFig. 11).
Pt77Al12Cr6Ni5W2 shows the highest concentration of Ni
and W in the g0 phase amongst the ﬁve-component alloys.
In addition, the concentration of the major g0 former Al in
the g matrix as well as in the g0 phase is lower than in all other
alloys. Thus, W acts according to mechanisms ‘‘A’’ and ‘‘B’’
in Fig. 11. Mo increases the g0 volume fraction by mechanisms
‘‘A’’ and ‘‘B’’, too, but with weaker intensity than W. Re ad-
dition also lowers the Al solubility in the g phase (mechanism
‘‘A’’ inFig. 11 ), whereas the g
0 composition is almost the
same as in Pt 77Al12Cr6Ni5. Ru addition leads to ‘‘inverse A’’
behavior coupled with ‘‘B’’ behavior. Both behaviors in com-
bination have no inﬂuence on the g
0 volume fraction.

7. Conclusions
Mo, Re and W additions lead to a measurable increase of
the g0 fraction in g0 precipitation hardened Pt-base alloys.
Amongst the refractory elements added, W is the strongest
promoter for g0 precipitation, whereas Ru is the weakest.
It was not possible to achieve a single-phase g matrix in the
ﬁve-component alloys by a homogenization heat treatment at
1510
/C14 C.
Re as alloying element slows down g0 growth and coarsen-
ing during ageing much more effectively than Mo, Ru and W.
An increase of the high-temperature strength by Mo, Re
and W additions was measured.
In the temperature range from 800 /C14 C to 1200 /C14 C there is
still a large gap between the strength of the presented
polycrystalline Pt-base alloys compared to the single-crystal
Ni-base superalloy CMSX-4.
Alloy Pt
77Al12Cr6Ni5 shows a dense corundum scale after
ageing in air, also with additions of 2 at.% Mo, Re and Ru,
pointing at a good oxidation resistance. W addition leads to
internal oxidation.
