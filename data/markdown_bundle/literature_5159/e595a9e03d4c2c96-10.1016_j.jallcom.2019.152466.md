abstract
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) are promising high temperature structural materials because of
their excellent high temperature strength and high melting point. With introducing secondary particles,
the high temperature mechanical properties can be further improved. However, its poor ductility limits
the industrial application. In the present work, a new ductile NbTaTiV RHEA-based composite reinforced
with Ti-C-O particles were in-situ synthesized through spark plasma sintering (SPS). The composite
exhibits excellent room temperature compressive properties with yield strength, ultimate strength and
fracture strain of 1760 MPa, 2270 MPa and 1 1%, respectively. A model is presented to study the
strengthening mechanism, and the theoretical calculation shows that the dramatically strength
enhancement is mainly due to the in-situ formation of Ti-C-O particles.

1. Introduction
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs), which are developed
mainly based on the refractory metallic elements (W, Mo, Ta, Nb, Hf,
Zr, Ti and V, etc.) have attracted substantial research interest due to
their excellent properties, such as ultra-high melting points,
desirable high temperature strength and good oxidation resistance
[1e3]. For example, Senkov et al. [ 4,5] reported that the WMoTaNb
RHEA exhibits a desirable high yield strength of 405 MPa at 1600 /C14 C,
which is the urgently needed properties for advanced high-
temperature structural materials. However, most of the RHEAs
containing W, Ta and Mo elements have high densities (>12 g/cm3).
What’s more, these alloys are usually extremely brittle at room
temperature, which limits their industrial application.
To overcome the heavy and brittle bottlenecks of the RHEAs
containing W, Ta and Mo elements, a series of intrinsic plastic
RHEAs based on group IV and V elements (Nb, Ta, Ti, V, etc.) have
been developed. For example, NbTaTiV RHEA, NbTaVW RHEA and
NbTaTiVW RHEA [ 6] exhibit excellent high compressive fracture
strain (>40%) at room temperature with considerable compressive
yield strength of 965 MPa, 1530 MPa and 1420 MPa, respectively.
However, the high temperature strength of these alloys is obviously
lower than that of the RHEAs based on W, Mo and Ta. The yield
strength of NbTiVZr RHEA and HfNbTaTiZr RHEA [ 7,8] at 1200 /C14 C
are only 58 MPa and 92 MPa, respectively. Therefore, how to
improve the strength of these RHEAs while maintain their high
ductility is an important scienti ﬁc problem encountered in the
development of RHEAs.
It is well known that addition of secondary ceramic particles,
such as carbides, nitrides, or silicides, is an effective method to
improve the room/high temperature strength of metallic materials
[9,10]. Based on this design strategy, some RHEA-based composites
were development. For example, a HfMo 0.5NbTiV0.5 RHEA-based
composite [ 11] reinforced with silicides shows a high strength at
high temperature (1000 /C14 C), which is almost twice of the
HfMo0.5NbTiV0.5 matrix RHEA. Most of the reported RHEA-based
composites are synthesized by casting method. The exist of mul-
tiple components in these casting composites could lead to many
problems, such as shrinkage porosity, coarse dendritic structure,
the compositional segregation, and so on. In addition, coarse
ceramic reinforcement phase is always distributed along the
boundaries of dendrites, which seriously affect the ductility of the

Powder metallurgy method (P/M) is considered as an effective
way to eliminate the intrinsic casting defects. At the same time, the
P/M technology can promote a uniform distribution of secondary
phases (or ceramic particles) [ 12e14], which make the P/M tech-
nology an ideal method to fabricate RHEAs or RHEA-based com-
posites. To some extent, the P/M technology has been applied to
study the RHEAs, such as NbTaTiV RHEA [14], TiNbTa0.5ZrAl0.2 RHEA
[15], Al 0.1CrNbVMo RHEA [ 16], and the RHEA-based composites,
such as NbMoCrTiAl RHEA-based composite [ 17] and W xTaTiVCr
RHEA-based composite [ 18]. In particular, some P/M RHEAs were
reported to have excellent strength and ductility combination. For
example, Guo et al. [ 14] reported a P/M NbTaTiV RHEA with a high
yield strength of 1370 MPa and excellent fracture strain of 23% at
room temperature, which has obvious advantages compared with
the traditional RHEAs.
In the present study, a new ductile NbTaTiV RHEA-based com-
posite reinforced with Ti-C-O particles was synthesized by spark
plasma sintering (SPS) method. Dense Ti-C-O particles were in-situ
formed through reaction between the NbTaTiV RHEA matrix and
the C source (stearic acid). Effects of the sintering temperature on
the microstructure and the mechanical properties of the RHEA-
based composite were investigated. The corresponding strength-
ening mechanisms were also discussed.
2. Experimental procedure
High purity ( >99.5 wt.%) Nb, Ta, Ti and V powders with particle
size less than 45
mm were used as raw materials. The powders were
mixed in equiatomic composition and milled in planetary ball-
millers for 12 h at 250 rpm in an argon atmosphere. Tungsten car-
bide vials and balls were utilized as the milling media with a ball-
to-powder mass ratio of 10:1. Stearic acid (4 wt.%) was added into
the mixed powders as C source to in-situ form Ti-C-O particles.
SPS method was used to densify the mixed powders. A graphite
die of 40 mm in diameter ﬁlled with the as-milled powders was
placed inside the SPS chamber between two graphite electrodes.
The as-milled powders were consolidated in the SPS machine (FCT
HP D 25/3, German) at 1500 /C14 C, 1600 /C14 C and 1700 /C14 C, respectively,
with a heating rate of 100 /C14 C/min. Each of sintering process was
held for 30 min with a constant pressure of 30 MPa under pure
argon atmosphere. After sintering, the sample was furnace cooled
to room temperature.
Cylindrical samples (f 6m m /C2 9 mm) for compressive test were
cut from the sintered bulk using electrical discharge machining.
Before testing, the surface of test samples was polished with a
2000-grit SiC paper to eliminate scratches. Room temperature
compressive test was carried out on a universal testing machine
(Instron-3369, USA) at a strain rate of 1 /C2 10/C0 3 s/C0 1. All the samples
were compressed until fracture. High temperature compressive test
was carried out on a thermo-mechanical simulator (Gleeble-3180,
USA) at a temperature range of 700 e10 0 0/C14 C and a strain rate of
1 /C2 10/C0 3 s/C0 1. Samples were heated to the test temperature with a
heating rate of 10 /C14 C/s, held for 5 min, and then compressed to 50%
deformation. Thermocouples were welded on the samples to
measure the actual temperature.
The particle size distribution of the as-milled powders was
investigated by a laser particle size analyzer (MICRO-PLUS, UK). The
microstructure of the samples was characterized by a ﬁeld emission
scanning electron microscope (FESEM, JSM-6360LV, Japan), equip-
ped with the energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS, MICROMET
5104, USA). The element distribution was investigated by the
electron probe microanalysis (EPMA, EPMA-1600, JEOL, Japan).
Phase composition was identi ﬁed by X-ray diffraction (XRD, D/
MAX-2250, Rigaku, Japan) with a Cu K a radiation at 40 kV and
200 mA. Vickers hardness (HV) was measured by a Vickers micro-
hardness tester (MicroMet-5104, Buehler, USA) under an indenter
load of 0.2 kg applied for 20 s. Details of microstructures were
studied by using a transmission electron microscope (TEM, FEI
Tecnai 20) with operating voltage of 200 KV.

3.2. Microstructure of (NbTaTiV) BCC/Ti-C-O composites
Fig. 2 shows the XRD patterns of the composites sintered at
150 0/C14 C, 1600 /C14 C and 1700 /C14 C, respectively, which are named as
sample T 1,T 2 and T 3. As reported by Guo et al. [ 14], the NbTaTiV
RHEA prepared by P/M method is mainly composed of BCC phase.
In the present study, some other diffraction peaks can be observed
in Fig. 2, indicating the formation of a secondary phase. According
to the JCPDS cards and the work reported by Jiang et al. [ 19], the
secondary phase can be identi ﬁed as Ti-C-O phase.
Fig. 3 illustrates the BSE images of the T 1,T 2 and T3 composites
sintered at 1500 /C14 C, 1600 /C14 C and 1700 /C14 C, respectively. To further
study the element distribution, EPMA analysis was conducted and
the results are shown in Fig. 4. As shown in Figs. 3 and 4 , there are
some residual pores exist in the composites, and the microstructure
of the composites exhibits two distinguishable regions: dark region
and grey region. The dark region with distinct boundaries is
enriched with Ti, C, and O. The grey region is enriched with Nb, Ta,
Ti and V. From the SEM and EDS results, the dark areas can be
identiﬁed as Ti-C-O phase, while the grey areas correspond to the
matrix. It should be noted that there are some white regions in the
sintered composites. The EDS analysis (as shown in Fig. 4 ) shows
that the white region is enriched with element Ta, which may result
from the low diffusion rate of the element Ta. With the increase of
sintering temperature, the microstructure become homogeneous,
the white region disappears gradually, and the amount of the re-
sidual pores is apparently decrease, as shown in Fig. 3 b, d and f.
Fig. 5 exhibits the EBSD results of the T 1,T 2 and T 3 composites.
The inverse pole ﬁgure (IPF) maps show that all the composites
have an equiaxed microstructure, the average grain sizes for the T 1,
T2 and T3 composites are 20 mm, 27 mm and 31 mm, respectively. The
phase maps also con ﬁrm that the composites are composed of BCC
phase and Ti-C-O phase. The morphology of the Ti-C-O particles are
basically ellipsoidal. With the increase of sintering temperature, the
grain growth is not obvious, which may be attributed to the
blocking effect of the strengthening particles on grain boundary
migration. To further understand the microstructure of the com-
posites, TEM analysis was conducted, as shown in Fig. 6. The bright
ﬁeld TEM images show that the Ti-C-O particles are dispersed in the
NbTaTiV RHEA matrix. The particles are in ellipsoidal shape with
average particle size of about 0.43
mm. The composition of the
particle circled with white dots is veri ﬁed by EDS, and the detailed
data is listed in Table 2 . The results show that the particle is
enriched with Ti, C, and O. The SAED patterns along [001] zone axes

4. Discussion
4.1. Phase prediction
The mixing enthalpy between C and Ti is about /C0 109 kJ/mol,
which is apparently lower than those of Nb ( /C0 102 kJ/mol), Ta
(/C0 101 kJ/mol) and V (/C0 82 kJ/mol). At the same time, the element O
also has a high af ﬁnity with the element Ti [ 23]. Therefore, stable
Ti-C-O particles can be formed during the high temperature sin-
tering. Similar result has also been reported by Xiong et al. [ 24],
where in TiC alloy, stable (Ti, W)C xOy particles are formed during
vacuum sintering. For the HEA matrix, several parameters
[6,25e27] have been used to verify the phase formation, including
mixing enthalpy ( DHmix), mixing entropy ( DSmix), mixing param-
eter ( U)d e ﬁned as T DSmix=DHmix, atomic size difference ( d) and
valence electron concentration (VEC). The calculated results are list
in Table 3 . According to Zhang et al. [ 28], random solid solution
alloys tend to be formed when the DHmix are in the range
of /C0 15e5 kJ/mol and d is below 6.6%. For the present composites,
the DHmix and d are /C0 29.0 kJ/mol and 1 1.8%, respectively, which
suggests that single phase solid solution cannot be formed. This is
in accordance with the previous EBSD and TEM results, where two
kinds of phases, i.e. HEA matrix phase and Ti-C-O phase, are formed
during the synthesis process. In addition, a FCC solid solution is
usually stable when VEC /C21 8, while a BCC solid solution is stable
when VEC /C20 6.78 [ 11]. For the present composites, the VEC is 4.6,
which suggest that a BCC matrix can be obtained during the sin-
tering process.

5. Conclusions
In the present work, a (NbTaTiV)
BCC/Ti-C-O composite was
synthesized through powder metallurgy method. A ﬁne and ho-
mogeneous microstructure with BCC RHEA matrix and in-situ
formed Ti-C-O particles was obtained in the sintered (NbTa-
TiV)
BCC/Ti-C-O composites. The composites exhibit desirable
compressive yield strength of 1.76 GPa and ultimate strength of
2.27 GPa with moderate fracture strain of about 1 1% at room tem-
perature. At the same time, the composites possess an excellent
high strength (about 685 MPa) at high temperature (1000 /C14 C). The
outstanding strength and ductile combination of the sintered
composites is mainly attributed to the in-situ formation of Ti-C-O
particles and ductile NbTaTiV RHEA matrix. This successful explo-
ration provides us an effectively way to develop ductile and high
strength RHEA by using a controllable powder metallurgy method.
The excellent high temperature mechanical properties of the
composites make them to be potential materials for high temper-
ature structural applications.
