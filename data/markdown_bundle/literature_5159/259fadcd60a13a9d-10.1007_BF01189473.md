DEGRADATION AND RECOVERY OF THE 7'-PHASE 
IN HEAT-RESISTANT NICKEL ALLOYS

High-alloy nickel alloys are the principal structural material for parts of hot channels of gas turbine engines 
(GTE), which withstands high temperatures and loads and has a long service life. The high high-temperature 
strength is a result of complex alloying and phase composition that ensures the stability of the alloy structure 
in a field of high-temperatures and stresses. The )e-phase (Ni3Al-base intermetallic), whose content is 
determined mainly by the content of AI and amounts to 60 - 65% in modem alloys, plays a special role in the 
strengthening process. The article presents results on the phase composition of some high-temperature nickel 
alloys after service. The degree of degradation of the ),'-phase in a field of high temperatures and stresses and 
the degree of its recovery under the effect of regenerative heat treatment are investigated. 
It is known [1] that disperse particles of),'-phase usually 
have a cubic shape and can impede the motion of dislocations 
and the development of creep. They are very sensitive to 
varying factors of the ambient and they react by coarsening, 
sticking together, elongating in the direction normal to tensile 
stresses, i.e., they undergo degradation. These processes have 
been studied well by the method of electron microscopy and 
their intensity in many cases determines the service proper- 
ties of the material [2]. There is a mathematical dependence 
between the rate of coagulation of the particles of the )"-phase 
and the factors controlling this process [3]. 
Purely structural changes are accompanied by the evolu- 
tion of excess phases of unfavorable morphology (M6C , 
TPU-phases, etc,), which hastens the processes in alloys. The 
optimum structural condition of an alloy corresponds to a 
uniform distribution of particles of)"-phase 200 - 400 nm in 
size in the matrix and the absence of excess phases.

occur at high temperatures, which produce parked noticeable

The method of physicochemical phase analysis not only 
gives a quantitative characteristic of the change in the phase 
composition of an alloy under the effect of temperatures and 
stresses but also allows us to estimate the capacity of the de- 
graded phase for regeneration during regenerative vacuum 
heat treatment (RVHT) [6]. The reversibility of the phase re- 
actions determines the possibility in principle of restoring the 
mechanical characteristics to their initial values using re- 
peated high-temperature heating of the alloy, and it depends 
on the degree of unfavorable changes.

at c~ = 140 N/mm 2 and t = 1025°C the ZhS6U alloy exhib- 
ited marked changes: the degree of degradation of the 
y'-phase C a = 43%, the content of M6C was 2%. RVHT at 
1210°C for l h partially regenerates the "/'-phase but does not 
dissolve the binary carbide. In a milder testing regime 
close to the operating conditions of an engine (975°C for 40 h, 
cr = 200 N/mm 2) the degree of degradation C a = 18% and the

In VZhL12U alloy, which is not liable to carbide reac- 
tions, the regeneration of the structure is limited by irre- 
versible changes, in particular, an increase in the dislocation 
density above the critical value, which results in microcracks 
that are not eliminated by RVHT. 
Physicochemical phase analysis is an integral method 
that characterizes phase reactions in the bulk of the material 
as distinct from the electron microscopy method, which 
mostly reflects local changes in the morphology of the 
y'-phase and the fine structure of alloys. 
By combining the two methods we can make the control 
of the degree of damage in the structure, the quality of its re- 
generation, and the reversibility of the phase composition of 
alloys more reliable. 
CONCLUSIONS 
1. It is recommended that the service life and the state of 
a material during operation be evaluated by the degree of 
degradation of the 7'-phase and the presence of undesirable 
carbides M6C , which can be determined by the method of 
physicochemical phase analysis of an isolated precipitate. 
2. Repeated high-temperature heating in vacuum de- 
creases the degree of unfavorable changes arising in heat-re- 
sistant nickel alloys in service.
