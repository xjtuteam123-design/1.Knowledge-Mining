jet milled and annealed tungsten powder

The correlation between tungsten powder properties and porosity characteristics of porous tungsten matrix have
been investigated in this paper. With the deagglomeration and classiﬁcation, monodisperse tungsten powders of
different particle size with narrow particle size distribution have been obtained. Multi-step annealing operation
with continuously increasing temperature was taken to reduce the particle activity, which are conductive to par-
ticle size homogenization, shape modiﬁcation and puriﬁcation simultaneously. The press-sintered porous tung-
sten matrix prepared by jet milled and annealed tungsten powder presents homogeneous inter-connected pores
with uniform pore size and distribution, indicating that low-activity monodisperse powder with narrow particle
size distribution is required for manufacturing homogeneous porous tungsten matrix.

1. Introduction
Owing to its high melting point and boiling point, the lowest vapor
pressure and low thermal expansivity, tungsten (W) plays an important
role in many technology ﬁelds such as atomic energy, military, electro
vacuum and other ultra-high temperature applications [1–6]. Typically,
porous tungsten matrix is one of the most commonly used materials for
dispenser cathode substrate, tungsten-based metal-metal and ceramic-
metal composites manufactured by melt-inﬁltration [7–11]. The poros-
ity characteristics, including open porosity, pore connectivity, pore
shape, pore size and distribution, are critical to determine the material
properties [10–13].
Due to the mechanical properties of tungsten, powder metallurgy
(PM) has been generally used to fabricate tungsten products [ 14]. The
properties of tungsten products formed by PM method are closely re-
lated to the raw powders [ 15]. Melnikova reported that powder parti-
cles with low activity are required for manufacturing long lifetime
cathodes [16, 17]. Bao employed a reduction of oxide in hydrogen atmo-
sphere above 700 °C which made the pore distribution more uniform
[18]. Li fabricated homogeneous porous tungsten matrix with open
pore channel by using spherical W powder with average size of 13
μm, and he pointed out that spherical particles could keep initial
shape and favor the preservation of inter-connected pores with narrow
pore size distribution [19]. The results of these studies indicate that the
improvement of the properties of W powder i.e., powder activity, parti-
cle size distribution, purity and surface morphology, all lead to the im-
proved performance of porous tungsten matrix. Thus, it is necessary to
study the correlation of various properties of tungsten powder and po-
rosity characteristics of porous tungsten matrix in detail.
In this study, jet milling treatment which is successfully applied in
dealing with brittle materials in other industries has been taken for our
experiments as an innovative method to modulate the particle size distri-
bution [ 20–24]. First W power was treated with jet milling for
deagglomeration and classi ﬁcation. This was then followed by multi-
step annealing to reduce the activity of W powder. Three kinds of W pow-
ders were consolidated by pressing and sintering. The microstructural
analysis and pressure mercury method are used to provide insight into
the microstructure and porosity characteristics of the sintered samples.

The particle size and size distribution were measured by using a
LMS-30 laser particle size analyzer. The morphology of W powders
and microstructure of sintered porous tungsten matrix were carried

fraction meter. The oxygen content of W powders was measured by
the inert gas pulses-infrared thermal conductivity method, and the car-
bon content was measured by high-frequency combustion-infrared
measurement. The porosity characteristics of sintered porous tungsten
matrix were characterized using QUANTACHROME mercury
porosimetry. The Vickers microhardness test was completed on the
polished samples using microhardness tester under a 100 g f loading

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Jet milling treatment
SEM photograph and particle size distribution of as-received W
powder is exhibited in Fig. 1 a. The particles show polygonal shape
with irregular edges and many small particles agglomerate to big aggre-
gates, presenting a broad size distribution in the range of 3.02 μmt o
27.64 μm. Fig. 1b–e show the SEM photographs and particle size distri-
bution of W powders at different rotating speed continuously reducing
from 4200 rpm to 2800 rpm. As shown in Fig. 1 b–e, monodisperse
W powders with similar particle size and smooth surface have been
produced by jet mill treatment. The morphology of the powder col-
lected at high rotating speed of 4200 and 3500 rpm is shown in Fig. 1b
and Fig. 1c, the powder is mainly composed of coarse nearly spherical
particles and some much ﬁner debris. With the decrease of rotating
speed, the ﬁner debris signiﬁcantly decrease, and the powder collected
at 3150 and 2800 rpm is basically composed of nearly spherical particles
of similar size, as shown in Fig. 1d and Fig. 1e. For grinding powder in
the jet mill, the raw particles are accelerated by means of a jet stream
of high purity nitrogen, and mechanical impact collision drastically oc-
curs among inter-particles. Thus, jet milling treatment lead to improved
powder dispersity and favorable shape modi ﬁcation of W powder. By
comparing particle size distribution before and after treatment, it is
clear that the medium particle size increases with the decline of the ro-
tating speed. The W powders with medium diameter particle size vary-
ing from 3.18 μmt o5 . 5 9μm with a narrow particle size distribution
have been obtained. According to the calculation of span value, all
grinded samples fall in category of medium distribution (S = 1.9–2.3).
The span value reduces from 3.45 to 1.99, indicating the particle size dis-
tribution is narrowed. As the rotating speed decreases, the value of S de-
creases, indicating that the particle size distribution of the powder
becomes more uniform. The powder ( Fig. 1d) with medium diameter
particle size of 4.83 μm was selected for the following experiments.
3.2. The annealing and grinding
Fig. 2 demonstrates the morphology of W powders annealed at
600 °C, 800 °C and 1000 °C for 120 min. No aggregates can be found

tungsten particles remain dispersed. The morphology of W powder
sample annealed at 1000 °C is shown in Fig. 2c, while part of the parti-
cles remain dispersed, apparent agglomeration with agglomerates of
different sizes can be observed.

ing process can be optimized by multi-step operation during which the
temperature is increased in multiple steps. The jet milled W powder is
ﬁrstly annealed at 800 °C (Fig.3a), tungsten particles remain dispersed.

3.3. Phase constitution and chemical composition analysis
The phase constitution analysis results are shown in Fig. 4. For the
three kinds of powders, all the peaks in the XRD patterns of the jet
milled and heat treated powder can be well indexed to the α phase of
W. For the jet mill grinding, large quantities of W powder were treated
in a short time at high purity nitrogen atmosphere. The particles are ac-
celerated in an inert gas jet stream and reduced in size mainly during
the particle-particle collision, which make the process free of contami-
nation. The subsequent annealing was carried out in a high purity hy-
drogen atmosphere, and act as a powder reduction. Further analysis of
the chemical composition is shown in Table 1. The oxygen and carbon
contents are about 0.0400 wt% and 0.0046 wt% for the as-received pow-
der, respectively. In contrast, for the jet milled powder, the oxygen and
carbon contents are 0.0600 wt% and 0.0110 wt%, respectively. While for
the heat treated powder, the oxygen and carbon contents of 0.0018 wt%
and 0.0024 wt% reveal that the annealing process is conducive to pow-
der puriﬁcation.

In general, the presence of aggregates in the as-received powder
makes the uneven microstructure of the green compact and causes un-
controllable shrinkage and uneven deformation of the subsequent
sintering process, resulting in uneven pore size and distribution. During
sintering, initial pores with relatively large size enlarge due to coarsen-
ing mechanism, and small pores shrank or even disappeared at the
same time [27, 28]. The ultraﬁne particles with high activity are apt to
diffuse to path between the large particles and easy to grow up,
resulting in channels blocking and closed pores formation. With the
deagglomeration and classiﬁcation of as-received W powder, the uni-
formity of pore size and distribution is improved by using jet milled
powder. The existence of oversintering areas is mainly due to the high
sintering activity of jet milled powder. Jet milling process results in cre-
ation of new surfaces and thereby a higher surface energy of the parti-
cles. At the same time, high-intensity energy delivered by jet milling
onto the particles also increases the activity of W powder. Furthermore,
the high activity of the jet milled powder leads to obtain a low open po-
rosity and small pore size concurrently during the same sintering condi-
tions. Due to the activity reduction and particle size homogenization of
the annealed powder, the formation of closed pores is prevented and
the inter-pore connectivity is enhanced. For the preparation process,
the mechanical properties of the powder are important as any small de-
fects formed during the initial stages of PM enlarge during subsequent
processing steps. The near spherical morphology and similar particle
size of W powder allow regular packing at pressing stage and evenly
shrinkage in sintering procedure, which ensure the uniformity of pore
size and distribution. Porous tungsten matrix prepared by jet milled
and annealed tungsten powder shows fully interconnected nearly
spherical pores with uniform pore size and distribution, indicating
that it is required low activity monodisperse powder with narrow par-
ticle size distribution for manufacturing homogeneous porous tungsten
matrix.
Since monodisperse W powders of different particle size with nar-
row particle size distribution can be prepared by jet milling process, po-
rous tungsten matrix with various porosity characteristics can be
obtained by selecting monodisperse powder with different particle
size and adjusting the sintering parameters. It provides a way to fabri-
cate homogeneous porous tungsten matrix with various porosity
characteristics.

4. Conclusion
(1) Jet milling treatment lead to improved powder dispersity, effective
classiﬁcation, and shape modiﬁcation. In this study, monodisperse
W powders with an average particle size varying from 3.18 μmt o
5.59 μm were obtained by jet milling treatment to the W powder
with particle size of 5 μm.
(2) Multi-step annealing operation in the range of 800 –1200 °C was
successfully employed in this study to reduce particle activity, im-
prove size homogeneity, particle sphericity and facilitate particle
puriﬁcation.
(3) Porous tungsten matrix prepared by jet milled and annealed tung-
sten powder shows fully interconnected nearly spherical pores
with uniform pore size and distribution, indicating that it is required
low activity monodisperse powder with narrow particle size distri-
bution for manufacturing homogeneous porous tungsten matrix.
