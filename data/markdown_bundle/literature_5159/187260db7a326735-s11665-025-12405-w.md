ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
Competition of Mechanical Properties Between Mg-Al
and Mg-Sn Alloys at Different Temperatures
and Corresponding Microstructure Characterization
Li Wandong, Yang Hongmei, Luo Xinyu, and Lou Zongyong
Submitted: 24 May 2025 / Revised: 22 August 2025 / Accepted: 29 August 2025
The differences in mechanical properties at different temperatures and microstructure between extruded-
aged Mg-11Al-1Ag (abbreviated as A11-Q alloy) and extruded-aged Mg-8Sn-2Al-1.5Bi-1Ag alloys (ab-
breviated as TA82-BQ alloy) were systematically investigated. The extruded-aged A11-Q alloy obtains high
UTS (385.2 MPa) and EL (9.4%), which are higher than the UTS (358.9 MPa) and EL (7.1%) of extruded-
aged TA82-BQ alloy at room temperature. However, the UTS (160.2 MPa) of extruded-aged TA82-BQ alloy
is higher than that (140.1 MPa) of extruded-aged A11-Q alloy at 160 /C176C, which is closely related to their
microstructure. The microstructure of extruded-aged A11-Q alloy is composed of a-Mg and Mg
17Al12
precipitates. And a-Mg, Mg 2Sn precipitates and few Mg 17Al12 precipitates are observed in extruded-aged
TA82-BQ alloy. The extruded-aged A11-Q alloy obtains a smaller average grain size with a weaker grain
texture and fewer deformed grains. Furthermore, a number of rodlike and ellipsoidal precipitates form in
both extruded-aged A11-Q and TA82-BQ alloys, which plays a role on precipitation strengthening. The
Mg
2Sn precipitates and corresponding elements distributions result in a higher tensile strength at elevated
temperature and Mg 17Al12 precipitates mainly contribute to a higher tensile strength at room temperature.
Keywords Mg-Sn alloy, Mg-Al alloy, mechanical property,
microstructure, precipitation behavior
1. Introduction
Recent years, Mg-Sn alloys have become an important heat-
resistant magnesium alloy without rare earth (RE) elements.
The microstructure and strength of heat-resistant Mg alloys is
of great signiﬁcance for their applications, such as aerospace,
automotive and motor bodies, in wide ranged temperatures (Ref
1). The noticeable heat resistance of Mg-Sn alloys is mainly
attributed to the Mg
2Sn phase with a relatively high melting
point (770 /C176 C) (Ref 2, 3). In addition, Mg-Sn alloy obtains an
excellent age hardening response because of the different solid
solubility contents of Sn element in a-Mg matrix at different
temperatures (Ref 4, 5). Huang et al. reported the microstruc-
ture and age hardening response of Mg-5Sn and Mg-7Sn alloys
(Ref 6). The micro-hardness value of Mg-7Sn-1Ag-1Zn alloy
aged at 200 /C176 C increased to 77.5 HV from 46 HV depending
after extrusion and aging treatment (Ref 7). Mendis et al. also
indicated that extruded-aged Mg-10Sn alloys can achieve a
high strength via Ca, Ag, Na, Al and Mn elements addition
(Ref 8). In addition, some Mg
2Sn precipitates dynamically
formed during hot deformation (Ref 9). Comparing with
commercial and mature alloys containing Al and Zn, the
mechanical property of Mg-Sn alloys still is relatively poor and
worth further studying. It is well known that the well tensile
strength and elongation were ascribed to the reﬁned grain size,
recrystallization behavior, precipitates size and densities and
other factors of extruded alloys by elements addition and heat
treatments (Ref 10).
Pan and Chen et al. systematically studied the inﬂuences of
Ce, Ca, Sb, Di and Y addition on the microstructure and
mechanical property of Mg-Sn alloys. These elements are easy
to form XMgSn ternary phases, which can reduce ductility and
inhibit the aging effect due to Sn consumption (Ref 11-15).
However, Al and Ag elements are hard to form second phase in
Mg-Sn alloys when their contents are under 2 wt.% (Ref 16,
17). And Al addition can improve the strength of Mg alloys due
to solid solution strengthening and grain reﬁnement effect. It is
well known that Ag is recognized as one of the most effective
elements to improve the age hardening response of Mg-Sn, Mg-
Zn and Mg-Gd alloys (Ref 18-20). Ag atoms can segregate the
interface between precipitates and a-Mg matrix to reﬁne
precipitates. Meanwhile, some Ag clusters can act the roles
of heterogeneous nucleation sites for grains and precipitates
formation (Ref 21, 22). Recently, Bi is known as a newly and
potential element to enhance the mechanical behavior of Mg
alloys under a high temperature. Because Mg
3Bi2 phase plays a
similar role in Mg alloys comparing with Mg 2Sn phase. At
present, it conﬁrmed that Mg 3Bi2 and Mg2Sn co-existed in Mg-
Sn-Bi alloys (Ref 23, 24). However, the Bi element was
enriched in or nearing the areas of Mg 2Sn precipitates in
unextruded Mg-Sn-Bi alloys based on current reports (Ref 25).
Therefore, the extrusion process and heat resistance of Mg-Sn-
Bi alloys with Al and Ag addition is necessary to be further
studied. Meanwhile, the Mg-Al alloys are frequently studied
due to their noticeable strength and decay resistance (Ref 26,

2. Materials and Experimental
The designed chemical compositions of different experi-
mental alloys are shown in Table 1. All alloy ingots were
prepared by high pure Mg, Sn, Al, Ag and Bi metals. Raw
materials were melting with the mixture protective gas of 99%
CO
2 +1 %S F 6 in carbon steel and electric resistance furnace.
The melt was held at 710 /C176 C for 30 min and 750 /C176 C for
20 min, and then it was poured into a steel mold when the melt
temperature slowed down to 700 /C176 C. The melt was mixed and
stirred at 700 /C176 C and the casting mold was preheated to 250 /C176 C
before pouring. The temperatures of heat treatment of both
alloys are different according to their phase diagrams (ASM
Handbook). The homogenized temperature and time of as-cast
TA82-BQ alloy were 400 /C176 C and 16 h, respectively. And the
homogenized TA82-BQ alloys were solid solutioned at 480 /C176 C
for 4 h followed by water quenching. The homogenized
temperature and time of as-cast A11-Q alloys were 350 /C176 C
and 2 h, reepectively. And the homogenized A11-Q alloys were
solid solutioned at 430 /C176 C for 20 h followed by water
quenching. Both solid solutioned alloys were extruded with
an extrusion ration of 25 at 320 /C176 C by hydraulic machine. The
solid solutioned alloys were cut into bars with a diameter of
25 mm and the bars were extruded into bars with a diameter of
5 mm. The rate of exported extrusion was 20 mm/min. The
further aging temperatures and times of extruded A11-Q and
TA82-BQ alloys were 200 /C176 C for 10 h and 200 /C176 C for 130 h,
respectively. The aging temperature can not change the grain
size since it is lower than the recrystallization temperature of
Mg alloys.
All the tensile samples were machined from the extruded-
aged bars along the extrusion direction (ED). The mechanical
properties of different alloys were tested with a tensile rate of
1910
/C0 3/s at room temperature and 160 /C176 C. The tensile samples
were preheated at 160 /C176 C for 20 min before tensile tests. The
microstructure were characterized by electron backscattered
diffraction (S-3400N), transmission electron microscope
(JE0L-2100F) and differential scanning calorimeter
(STA449). XRD was used to conﬁrm the phase compositions
with a scanning rate of 0.1 /C176 /s from 20 to 80 /C176 . EBSD was used
to characterize the microstructure with texture and measure
grain size with a step size of 0.18 lm and analyzed by Channel
5. The tensile fracture morphology was observed by scanning
electron microscopy. The precipitates morphology and ele-
ments distributions were characterized by TEM test. The foils
for TEM observation were prepared by mechanical polishing
and then punched into disks with a diameter of 3 mm.
Subsequently, the treated foils were further thinned to 30 nm
by ion beam thinning.
3. Results
3.1 Mechanical Properties
Figure 1 shows the representative tensile engineering stress-
strain curves of extruded-aged A11-Q and TA82-BQ alloys at
room temperature and 160 /C176 C. And corresponding mechanical
property parameters are listed in Table 2. As shown in Fig. 1(a),
the extruded-aged TA82-BQ alloy exhibits a lower ultimate
tensile strength (UTS) of 358.9 MPa and a lower elongation to
failure (EL) of 7.1% compared with extruded-aged A11-Q alloy
at room temperature. The UTS and EL of extruded-aged A11-Q
alloy reach to 385.2 MPa and 9.4%, respectively. However, the
contrary strength results are obtained at 160 /C176 C. The extruded-
aged TA82-BQ alloy exhibits a UTS of 163.2 MPa and the
UTS of extruded-aged A11-Q alloy is only 140.1 MPa at
160 /C176 C. The EL (68.4%) of extruded-aged TA82-BQ alloy is
still lower than that (77.9%) of extruded-aged A11-Q alloy. The
differences in mechanical properties between extruded-aged
A11-Q and TA82-BQ alloys are probably attributed to grain
size and precipitates differences caused by different elements
addition.

4. Discussions
In this work, the tensile properties and microstructure are
different between extruded-aged A11-Q and TA82-BQ alloys.
The microstructure characteristics are responsible for the
mechanical properties, which will be discussed in this section.
The results emphasize that the tensile strength and elonga-
tion to failure of extruded-aged A11-Q alloy are higher than
those of extruded-aged TA82-BQ alloy. This can be explained
by followings. Firstly, the growth restriction factor (GRF) is
used to measure the effect of elements on grain reﬁnement.
Some added elements can act as heterogeneous nucleation sites
for grains during solidiﬁcation and extrusion process. In
addition, solute atoms can restrict the grain growth since the
solute diffusion occurs slowly, resulted in limiting the rate of
crystal growth (Ref 39, 40). The GRF values of Al and Sn are
4.32 and 1.47, respectively. Therefore, A11-Q alloy has a
smaller average grain size due to the high GRF of Al. The
strength increment is attributed to grain boundaries in part,
which can be explained by Hall-Petch relationship (Ref 41, 42).

5. Conclusions
In this study, the mechanical properties at room and elevated
temperatures and microstructure of extruded-aged A11-Q and
TA82-BQ alloys were investigated systematically. The main
conclusions are drawn as follows:
The extruded-aged A11-Q alloy obtains high UTS
(385.2 MPa) and EL (9.4%), which are higher than the
UTS (358.9 MPa) and EL (7.1%) of extruded-aged TA82-
BQ alloy at room temperature. However, the UTS
(160.2 MPa) of extruded-aged TA82-BQ alloy is higher
than that (140.1 MPa) of extruded-aged A11-Q alloy at
160 /C176 C, indicating that extruded-aged TA82-BQ alloy ex-
hibits a noticeable heat resistance.
The microstructure of extruded-aged A11-Q alloy is mainly
composed of a-Mg and Mg
17Al12 precipitates. And a-Mg,
Mg2Sn and Mg 17Al12 precipitates are observed in ex-
truded-aged TA82-BQ alloy. The extruded-aged A11-Q al-
loy obtains a smaller average grain size with a weaker
grain texture and fewer deformed grains.
A number of rodlike and ellipsoidal precipitates form in ex-
truded-aged A11-Q and TA82-BQ alloys, which play the
role of precipitation strengthening. And Mg 2Sn precipitates
and corresponding elements distributions contribute to a
high tensile strength for extruded-aged TA82-BQ alloy at
elevated temperature.
