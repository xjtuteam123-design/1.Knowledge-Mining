ABSTRACT
By synergizing dual-laser powder bed fusion with post-heat treatment, this study resolved unmelted particles, 
fusion defects, and cracks in additively manufactured tungsten alloys, achieving record-breaking compressive 
strength ( > 3.4 GPa) and toughness (strain > 69 %). Dual-laser in-situ remelting and annealing enabled 99.84 % 
density, stress relief, and γ -phase precipitation, forming fully equiaxed grains — providing a novel strategy for 
processing high-melting-point brittle tungsten alloys.
1. Introduction
Tungsten heavy alloys (WHAs) are extensively utilized in aerospace, 
defense and electronic industries due to their superior comprehensive 
performance, including high hardness, high strength and radiation 
resistance [ 1 ]. Recently, laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) technology has 
presented substantial opportunities for the production of intricate 
tungsten components with excellent performance [ 2 ]. Nevertheless, 
LPBF WHAs faces significant challenges owing to tungsten ’ s elevated 
melting point (3420 
◦
C), ductile-to-brittle transition temperature 
(BDTT: 200 – 400 
◦
C or higher [ 3 ]), and liquid viscosity during melting 
[ 4 ]. Un-melted W particles, lack-of-fusion holes, and cracks are common 
defects in LPBF WHAs. Although numerous studies have mitigated these 
defects by optimizing LPBF process parameters, cracking continues to be 
a particularly challenging defect that is difficult to fully eliminate [ 5 – 7 ]. 
Additionally, optimizing process parameters necessitates multiple 
rounds of trial experiments, inevitably leading to increased 
manufacturing costs.
Based on this, this work proposes a combination of dual laser- 
induced in-situ remelting and cracks self-healing via high-temperature 
annealing to fabricate high-performance LPBF WHAs. The in-situ 
remelting strategy is simple and economical, which can well solve the 
defects such as un-melted W particles and lack-of-fusion holes. More -
over, the liquid phase produced by high-temperature annealing can 
induce crack healing and alleviate the residual stresses inside LPBF 
WHAs. These methods are expected to provide effective guidance for 
additively manufactured refractory alloys with high crack sensitivity.
2. Experiment and methods
In this study, 98W-1.4Ni-0.6Fe (wt.%, 98W) mixed powder was 
prepared by a self-developed double laser-powder bed fusion (DL-PBF) 
system. The schematic diagram of DL-PBF process is illustrated in Fig. 1
(a), with a dual laser interval distance of 0.2 mm. The entire preparation 
process was carried out in a forming chamber filled with argon (oxygen 
content: < 100 ppm). The laser powder, scanning speed, layer thickness 
and hatch spacing were set to 350W, 600 mm/s, 20 μ m, and 0.1 mm, 
respectively. Both lasers operated under identical parameters. The 
physical photos of the 98W samples after laser processing are detailed in

Typically, in a single-laser PBF process, the molten pool exhibits 
multiple modes of heat transfer with the surrounding environment, 
substrate or pre-cured part, resulting in extremely fast cooling rates and 
unstable non-equilibrium thermodynamic evolution within a few mi -
croseconds [ 17 ]. In this processing mode, the pronounced temperature 
gradient promotes the development of coarse columnar grains along the 
Z-deposition direction, as grain growth preferentially occurs in the di -
rection opposite to the heat flow [ 12 ]. However, dual laser-induced 
in-situ remelting could increase heat transfer, promote flow within the 
molten pool, and provide a more uniform temperature distribution [ 18 ].

4. Conclusions
In summary, this study demonstrates the successful fabrication of a 
high-density 98W-1.4Ni-0.6Fe alloy with minimal residual stress 
through an innovative dual-laser powder bed fusion strategy coupled 
with optimized post-heat treatment. The final microstructure features 
equiaxed tungsten grains uniformly distributed within a γ -phase matrix. 
Remarkable densification was achieved through dual-laser-induced in- 
situ remelting combined with post-heat-treatment-enabled crack self- 
healing mechanisms. The resulting material exhibits exceptional me -
chanical properties, reaching compressive strength of 3420 ± 132 MPa 
and compressive strain of 69.77 ± 0.26 % — values that surpass other 
tungsten heavy alloys produced by both additive and traditional 
manufacturing methods. This breakthrough introduces a novel pro -
cessing route for manufacturing tungsten-based alloys with unprece -
dented strength-toughness combinations, significantly advancing the 
field of high-performance refractory metal additive manufacturing.
