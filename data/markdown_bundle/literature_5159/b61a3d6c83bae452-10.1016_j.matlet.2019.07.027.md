Abstract 
The mechanical properties and microstructure evolution of a p recipitation-strengthened refractory high -
entropy alloy, HfNbTaTiZr, with equiaxed grains are reported. The as-cast alloy shows a high yield strength 
of 356 MPa at 1200°C, and 1597 MPa with good ductility at room temperature . The enhancement in 
strength is attributed to precipitation strengthening. Afterwards, microstructure evolution was investigated 
under several annealing treatment conditions : 1000°C for 24 h, 1200 °C for 24 h, 1400 °C for 24 h, and 
1450°C for 168 h. The hcp-1 phase appears at 1000 °C and gradually dis solves up to 1200 °C. When the 
temperature rises to 1400°C, this hcp-1 phase disappears, and fine nanoprecipitates begin to form. After 
annealing at 1450°C for one week , however, this alloy exhibits phase decomposition . With the formation 
of two kinds of new fcc phase, fine nanoprecipitate only remains in bcc matrix , and another new hcp -2 
phase forms in the grain. 
Keywords: Refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA), Precipitation strengthening, High temperature, Phase 
transformation, HfNbTaTiZr 
1. Introduction 
Refractory high -entropy alloy s (RHEAs) have bee n investigated because of their outstanding 
mechanical properties and phase stabilities at high temperature since 2010 [1–4]. However, their 
applications at lower temperatures are limited due to poor ductility and processability . Senkov et al. 
reported that HfNbTaTiZr alloy showed an excellent ductility at room temperature (RT) but with an 
insufficient yield strength at 1200°C [5,6]. After that, research on HfNbTaTiZr focused on the improvement 
of its mechanical propert ies at RT by solid solution strengthening , grain boundary strengthening , and 
dislocation strengthening [7–11]. On the other hand, phase stability, as a vital performance for applications

at high temperatures , was revealed recently. The as -rolled single bcc HfNbTaTiZr alloy showed a 
decomposition with two bcc phases after annealing at 800°C [8,10,11]. Moreover, a further decomposition 
was observed after annealing at 500°C and 700°C [9,12,13]. However, there are few reports on the phase 
stability at higher temperatures (e.g., 1200°C or more than 1400°C). Besides, mechanical property at high 
temperature also need to be improved. Herein, the mechanical properties of as-cast HfNbTaTiZr alloy were 
investigated at RT and 1200°C. Moreover, microstructure evolution was studied to reveal its phase stability 
during annealing at high temperatures (1000–1450°C). 
2. Experimental Procedures 
Equimolar HfNbTaTiZr alloy was prepared by arc melting under an atmosphere shielded with argon 
gas. The ingot was remelted 12 times for homogenization. Rectangle-shaped ingots (180 g) were obtained 
with a lustrous surface. Phase analyses were performed using X-ray diffraction (XRD, X’Pert MPD) with 
Cu Kα radiation. The 2θ was set from 20º to 140º with a step size of 0.00872 º/s. Microstructure observations 
were conducted by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JXA-8430F, JEOL). Chemical composition was 
analyzed via field-emission electron probe micro -analysis (FE-EPMA, JXA -8430F, JEOL). Cylindrical 
specimen was used for compression experiment with the size of Ф6 × 9 mm and Ф8 × 12 mm for testing at 
RT and 1200°C separately. Compression tests were performed using a hot deformation simulator (Thermec 
Mastor-Z, Fuji Electronic Industrial Co. Ltd.) under a strain rate of 10-2 s-1. To investigate the microstructure 
evolution, heat treatment was conducted at 1000 °C, 1200°C, 1400°C, and 1450 °C in a heat treatment 
furnace (NEMS Co., Ltd) under an atmosphere filled with argon gas following with furnace cooling. 
3. Results and discussion 
XRD profile of as -cast alloy indicates a single bcc matrix as shown in Fig . 1. The microstructure and 
elemental distribution of the as-cast alloy observed by EPMA are shown in Figs. 2a and 3a, respectively. 
Table 1 summarises the results of point composition analysis and segregation coefficient of each element. 
In as-cast condition, HfNbTaTiZr alloy shows equiaxed grain s with almost no segregation , where the 
maximum value K𝑇𝑎 is just 1.14 . Moreover, the higher magnification image (inset of Fig. 2a) reveals 
nanoparticles disperse densely in the bcc matrix, which is first observed in HfNbTaTiZr alloy. The phase 
constitution of as-cast alloy obtained herein is different from that of the as-rolled alloy reported with single 
bcc phase previously.

Engineering stress-strain curve and mechanical properties of compression tests are shown in Fig. 4a. 
This as-cast alloy shows a yield strength of 1597 MPa (161.2 MPa∙cm3∙g-1 in specific value calculated by 
dividing density) at RT with no evidence of fracture even up to a strain of 50%. Moreover, this alloy shows 
a high yield strength of 356 MPa (35.9 MPa∙cm3∙g-1 in specific value) at 1200°C. The yield strength of the 
as-cast HfNbTaTiZr alloy is approximately 1.5 times at RT and four times at 1200°C of that obtained by 
Senkov (Fig. 4b) [5,6]. This significant improvement can be attributed to precipitation strengthening by the 
nanoparticles [14].

Moreover, XRD profiles and the microstructure of the HfNbTaTiZr alloy annealed under several 
conditions are shown in Figs. 1 and 2, respectively. After annealing at 1000°C for 24 h, as same as that in 
deformed alloys [13], the hcp phase (denoted by hcp-1 in this work) forms both on grain boundaries and in 
grains (Fig. 2b), which shows the same phenomenon of complete wetting as in Zr–Nb alloys [15]. Elemental 
distribution and point concentration are shown in Fig. 3b and Table 1. In the hcp-1 phase, Hf and Zr were 
enriched, with a small amount of Ti as well. When annealed at 1200°C for 24 h, hcp-1 phase disappeared 
in grains and partly dissolved on grain boundaries (Fig. 2c), which suggested the bcc phase is more stable 
at 1200°C. After annealing at 1400°C for 24 h, the hcp-1 phase was entirely dissolved (Fig. 2d), yet densely 
dispersed nanoprecipitates, the same morphology as in as-cast condition, were observed in the bcc matrix 
(inset of Fig. 2d). After annealing at 1450°C for 168 h, a new phase (denoted by fcc-1), composed of Hf, 
Zr, and Ti ( Fig. 3c and Table 1 ), formed on grain boundar ies (Fig. 2e ). Meanwhile, another fcc phase 
(denoted by fcc-2) also formed in grains, with a similar composition (lower Ti and higher Hf and Zr). These 
two fcc phases (Fig. 1) has not been previously reported in the HfNbTaTiZr system. Moreover, another 
small hcp phase enriched in Hf and Zr (denoted by hcp-2) also formed in grains — the lowest-level areas 
in the Ti map of Fig. 3c — and its XRD peaks in Fig. 1 were close to the intermetallic compound ZrHf0.302 
(ICDD PDF No. 18-0599). Fine precipitates just remained in the bcc matrix (Fig. 2f). 
4. Conclusions 
Herein, HfNbTaTiZr with equiaxed grain s was fabricated by arc melting. Mechanical properties and 
microstructure evolution at high temperatures were investigated. In as-cast condition, the microstructure 
comprises bcc matrix and nanoprecipitate, providing a high yield strength (1597 MPa) and good ductility

6 
 
at RT. Moreover, a high strength (356 MPa) at 1200°C was obtained, which is almost four times that in 
reported data. The significant enhancement can be attributed to precipitation strengthening. After annealing 
at 1000°C, the hcp-1 phase forms and gradually dissolves as the temperature increases up to 1200°C. When 
at 1400°C, hcp-1 phase entirely disappears and fine nano precipitate forms. The microstructure after 
annealing at 1450°C for 168 h indicates phase decomposition. With the generation of two fcc phases and 
an hcp-2 phase, nanoprecipitate only remains in the bcc matrix.
