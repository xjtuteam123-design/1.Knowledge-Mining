1. Introduction 
Currently, the operating temperatures of advanced jet turbine engine 
have reached the limits imposed by melting temperature ( T
m
) of the 
nickel-based superalloys [ 1 , 2 ]. New structural materials with higher 
temperature bearing capacity are the urgent issue for development of 
the next generation jet engine with higher thrust-weight ratio and effi -
ciency. With the stable microstructure and temperature bearing capacity 
up to 1670 
◦
C and lower density, Nb – Si based alloys show great po -
tential to be applied as ultra-high temperature materials for the next 
generation engine. To balance strength and toughness, generally, the 
Nb – Si based materials usually contain toughening/stiffening two-phase 
structure, in which the toughening phase, Nb solid solution (Nb
SS
), 
supplies room-temperature ductility and toughness, while the stiffening 
Nb
5
Si
3 
or Nb
3
Si silicide offers high-temperature strength and creep 
resistance [ 3 – 5 ]. However, the mechanical and environmental proper -
ties of the Nb – Si based alloys still need to be further improvement 
through alloying and innovation in processing and production technol -
ogies [ 6 ], where the alloying [ 7 – 10 ] and the processing technologies 
[ 11 – 14 ] may either optimize microstructure (size, fraction and distri -
bution of the constitution phases and oriented microstructure) or reduce 
defects, and subsequently, enhance the mechanical properties at room 
and high temperature. In terms of the alloying, Ti [ 7 ] and Hf [ 8 ] may 
toughen Nb, while Mo and W are solid solution strengthening elements 
of Nb [ 9 ]. Al and Cr may restrain the internal oxidation of Nb and in -
crease the oxidation resistance of Nb [ 10 ]. 
Over the past few years, a variety of processing approaches, 
including vacuum arc melting [ 11 ], directional solidification [ 12 ], 
powder metallurgy [ 13 ] and physical vapor deposition [ 14 ], etc., have 
been employed to fabricate the multi-component Nb – Si based alloys and 
a great progress in improvement of the mechanical properties has been 
made, while the sort and the extent of the increased mechanical prop -
erties depend on the processing technology and alloying composition. 
For example, a typical Nb – Si based composite with a composition of 
Nb – 16Si – 22Ti – 3Cr – 3Al-2Hf fabricated via electromagnetic cold cruci -
ble directional solidification has achieved a balance in mechanical 
properties, i.e., the tensile strength of up to 180 MPa at 1250 
◦
C and a 
fracture toughness ( K
Q
) of 12.4 MPa m
1/2 
at room temperature [ 12 ].

However, there are still many critical unsolved problems, such as the 
macro-segregation of alloying element and coarse microstructure 
resulting from slow cooling rate in those technologies that are relative to 
solidification process [ 15 ], which greatly restricted engineering appli -
cations of the Nb – Si based alloy for the inadequate toughness at room 
temperature due to the cleavage fracture of the coarsening Nb phase and 
poor oxidation resistance at high temperature [ 2 ]. Furthermore, me -
chanical parts made of the Nb – Si based alloys with complex shape are 
difficult to be formed and machined because the intrinsic brittleness and 
low work hardening rate of the stiffening Nb
5
Si
3 
or Nb
3
Si silicide in the 
microstructure [ 16 ]. Additive manufacturing technologies, such as laser 
powder bed fusion (L-PBF), laser directed energy deposition (L-DED), 
electron powder bed fusion (E-PBF), etc., however, provide an integra -
tive solution to directly produce components with advantages of com -
plex geometry, cost saving and high efficiency of net forming, as 
compared with traditional manufacturing methods, such as melting and 
solidification [ 17 – 19 ]. Most importantly, the additive manufacturing 
methods can modify the microstructure of the alloy to achieve superior 
properties. GE Company fabricated fuel nozzles of Co – Cr alloy for the GE 
LEAP aero engine via laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) and 25% lighter 
and stronger than the previous conventional design was achieved [ 20 ]. 
The 7075 aluminum alloy prepared by the L-PBF process using 
pre-alloyed gas-atomized spherical alloy powder as raw material had 
fine grain and crack-free microstructure, subsequently, the strength was 
double that of the most common aluminum alloy [ 21 ]. Similarly, the 
ultra-fine columnar grains were also obtained in Rene ’ 41 superalloy by 
using the laser directed energy deposition (L-DED), and the yield and 
tensile strengths along the longitudinal section considerably exceed that 
of a minimum allowable specification, 637 MPa and 735 MPa, respec -
tively [ 22 ]. 
To date, only a few works trailed on behaviours of the Nb – Si based 
alloys fabricated via the additive manufacture in terms of the processing, 
microstructures and properties [ 23 – 28 ]. For instance, an 
Nb – 22Si – 26Ti – 6Cr-3Hf – Al alloy was deposited by laser directly forming 
using pre-alloyed powders, and it was found that the higher powder 
feeding rate and lower scanning rate were necessary for stable forming 
process [ 23 ]. The precipitation behaviors of as-built Nb – Si-based alloy 
during the laser directed energy deposition (L-DED), including the types 
of precipitates, ORs and interfacial structure between the precipitates 
and the Nb
SS 
phase were investigated [ 24 ]. The dense Nb – 17Si – 23Ti 
alloy with refined and directionally solidified microstructure success -
fully fabricated by in-situ reaction L-DED technique using Nb, Si and Ti 
elements powders and different laser power, which lead to excellent 
indentation fracture toughness (20.3 MPa m
1/2
) [ 25 ]. The L-PBFed 
Nb – 18Si – 24Ti – 2Cr – 2Al-2Hf alloy showed a significant refined micro -
structure containing the spherical Nb
SS 
phase with the maximum 
diameter of 300 nm [ 26 ]. This SLMed part exhibited better high tem -
perature oxidation resistance at 1300 
◦
C for 5 h than that of the 
arc-melted alloy. Unfortunately, the scientific reports with regard to 
effect of the additive manufacture technology on evolutions of the 
microstructure and the mechanical properties of the Nb – Si based alloys 
are still absent and far from system to form a principle and guide the 
engineering applications. 
In this work, dense Nb – 16Si binary ingot with ultra-fine and rapid 
directionally solidified microstructure was obtained by laser directed 
energy deposition (L-DED) using premixed pure Nb powder and Nb
5
Si
3 
alloy powder. A detailed investigation on microstructure, room tem -
perature hardness and fracture toughness ( K
Q
), crack propagation and 
failure mode of the L-DEDed alloy was performed. Finally, a comparison 
of microstructure and mechanical properties between the L-DEDed and 
the arc-melting Nb – 16Si alloy were characterized to better under -
standing the role of the above processing technologies on fracture 
behaviours.

2.2. Preparation of the Nb – 16Si ingot using L-DED 
The laser directed energy deposition technology (L-DED) was 
employed to prepare the Nb – 16Si ingot. To avoid compositional in- 
homogeneities and deviations in the Nb – 16Si ingot during the L-DED 
process, the Nb and Nb
5
Si
3 
powders were mixed at room temperature in 
a planetary ball mill machine without stainless steel vials under an argon 
atmosphere, at a rotation speed of 120 rpm for 4 h. 
L-DED process was conducted using a 6 kW optical fibre lasers pro -
cessing system with six-axis numerical control working table and a 
synchronous powder feeder with two independent feeding units. In the 
L-DED process, only one powder feeder was applied because the Nb and 
Nb
5
Si
3 
powders had been pre-mixed uniformly. A pre-grounded Ti6Al4V 
coupon with the dimension of about 50 × 30 × 20 mm was used as 
substrate for laser deposition. Fig. 1 d schematically illustrates the 
powder feeding way and the scanning path of laser beam during L-DED 
process. The laser beam moved in a zigzag way and deposited an area of 
approximately 22 mm × 10 mm in size in the first layer, while in the 
subsequent layers the scanning path of laser was rotated by 180
◦
layer 
by layer until the total height of the as-deposited Nb – 16Si ingot reached 
35 mm. Based on a series of preliminary experiments, the optimized L- 
DED parameters were obtained as follows: laser power = 900 W, laser 
spot diameter = 0.8 mm, scanning speed = 600 mm/s, hatch distance =
0.5 mm, powder feed rate = 20 g/min. To reduce oxidation, argon was 
used as the shielding gas and the powder carrier gas for protection of the 
melted pool. 
2.3. Characterization of microstructure and mechanical properties of the 
L-DEDed Nb – 16Si ingot

μ m. Secondary electron microscope (SEM) and back-scattered electron 
(BSE) images of the raw powders, the crack paths and the fracture sur -
faces of the L-DEDed alloy after three-point-bent test were obtained 
using an ZEISS SUPRA55 scanning electron microscope equipped with 
energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS, BRUKER XFlash Flat 
QUAD). Prior to SEM and EBSD examination, the samples were mounted 
by epoxy resin and mechanically polished by abrasive paper and

3.1. Phase constitution and microstructure characteristics of the L-DEDed 
Nb – 16Si ingot 
The L-DEDed Nb – 16Si binary ingot prepared under the optimized L- 
DED parameters is shown in Fig. 2 a. The ingot shows a perfectly 
cuboidal shape without macro-defect, such as cracks, delamination and 
warping. As revealed by the X-ray diffraction patterns of the L-DEDed 
ingot at different height positions, such as 10 mm, 20 mm and 30 mm 
from the substrate (position a, b and c in Fig. 2 a), which is shown in 
Fig. 2 b, respectively, the characteristic diffraction peaks of two phases of 
the Nb

Fig. 5 presents EBSD analysis of the L-DEDed Nb – 16Si alloy in terms 
of crystal orientation maps, grain size distribution patterns and grain

Note that a grain may contain some primary Nb
SS 
arms and the eutectic

sample had equiaxed grains, and the average grain size was approxi -

the arc-melting Nb – Si based alloy in the previous study (about 375 μ m)

a larger size distributed inside the melted pool, i.e., the scaly-like zone,

difference of the grain size distribution may be related to the thermal 
profile that associated with the cooling rate difference along the longi -
tudinal and transverse sections in the melted pool during L-DED [ 40 , 41 ].

resulted from two-directional dissipating heat of a scanning track, while 
the longitudinal section just had single dissipating heat towards the 
melted pool bottom. The grain boundary with different character dis -

suggests that the L-DED process facilitates higher fraction of the LAGBs 
in the Nb – 16Si alloy along the building direction, which is favorable for 
the dislocation transferring the GBs and the plastic deformation in this 
direction.
