abstract
The inﬂuence of temperature on the deformation mechanisms of a new Ni-based super-
alloy named K4800 containing 35 vol% g
0 were investigated. We studied the temperature
dependence of deformation mechanisms and used the alloying method to optimize the
high-temperature properties of the alloy. By analyzing slightly deformed microstructures,
the dominant deformation mechanisms of alloy K4800 at the yielding stage were identiﬁed
as: the anti-phase boundary (APB) shearing from RT to 600
/C14 C, stacking faults (SFs) shearing
and Orowan looping at 600 e800 /C14 C, and Orowan looping/cross-slip/climbing above 800 /C14 C.
We found that it was the activation of thermally assisted processes declined the
strengthening effect of g
0 phase and impaired the high-temperature strength of the alloy
above 800 /C14 C. First-principles calculations were employed to investigate the effects of
alloying elements on the formation and stability of stacking faults in K4800 alloy by using a
48-atom Ni
3(Al, Ti) orthorhombic supercell. Considering the effectiveness of elements in
lowering stacking faults energy, Co and W elements were chosen to alleviate the strength
degradation of K4800 alloy above 800
/C14 C. The deformation mechanism and properties of
these three alloys at elevated temperature were also revealed and discussed.

1. Introduction
With the increase in engine thrust-weight ratio, the
temperature-bearing capacity requirements of aero-engine
components have been continuously improved. Load-
bearing frame is an important hot end component in aero-
engine components. Its complex structure limits the applica-
tion of advanced casting technologies like single crystal
casting or directional solidiﬁcation casting. As a result, con-
ventional polycrystalline precision investment casting is
commonly employed. Polycrystalline nickel-based superal-
loys, such as Inconel 718 [ 1], U720Li [ 2], TMW [ 3], are exten-
sively utilized in casting applications. However, their
temperature-bearing capacity typically ranges from 650 to
725
/C14 C, which is insufﬁcient to meet the higher temperature
requirements. As to the polycrystalline nickel-based superal-
loys with higher temperature-bearing capacities (800 e850
/C14 C),
alloys like IN939 [ 4] and Waspaloy [ 5] are widely used in in-
dustrial gas turbine blades. However, these alloys exhibit
limited high-temperature plasticity and weldability due to
their relatively high volume fraction of g0 phase ( >40%).
Currently, there is a scarcity of materials suitable for elevated
temperature load-bearing frames (800
/C14 C), creating an urgent
need for the development of new materials. In this condition,
a new cast nickel-based superalloy named K4800 is developed,
and it shows excellent mechanical properties at temperatures
up to 800
/C14 C.
As temperature rises, the yield strengths of nickel-based
superalloys typically experience a rapid decrease at a spe-
ciﬁc temperature [ 6e8]. Reasons for that include but are not
limited to the dissolution of g
0 precipitates and operations of
thermally activated dislocation processes like cross-slip and
climbing. The rapid declines in the yield strengths of alloys
limit their maximum service temperatures. Given the rapid
decrease in the strength of some nickel-based superalloys at
medium temperature due to thermal activation, some
scholars [ 8,9] proposed improving the high-temperature
strength of alloys by reducing the alloy 's stacking fault en-
ergy (SFE). Reducing SFE of alloy can promote dislocation
dissociation, increasing the number and width of SFs in the
material. The movement of these extended dislocations is
highly resistant to deformation and must be condensed into a
complete dislocation for effective plastic deformation. As a
result, lowering SFE makes cross-slip migration more difﬁcult
to occur and becomes a practical method of improving the
high-temperature mechanical performance of alloys [ 9].
The contents of alloying elements signiﬁcantly inﬂuence
the SFE of a material, and adjusting the contents of key ele-
ments is one of the primary methods for altering the SFE of an
alloy. Theoretical calculations have demonstrated their
increasing importance in alloy design and optimization,
providing an important complementary perspective to guide
experimental studies [ 10,11]. Effects of alloying elements on
the SFEs of the Ni phase and Ni
3Al phase have been exten-
sively studied. According to Xia et al. [ 10], they found the
alloying elements Re, Ru, Ta, Mo, and W reduce the SFE of Ni
but improve the SFE of Ni
3Al phase through density functional
theory (DFT). However, the strengthening phase g0 of Ni-based
casting alloys is Ni 3(Al, Ti), in which Ti and Al randomly
occupy the position of Ni. There is currently a lack of theo-
retical calculations for Ni 3(Al, Ti) phases. Hence, more theo-
retical calculations, especially the ones associated with
experimental investigations, are needed for Ni-based casting
alloys to establish the relationship among element contents,
SFE and alloy mechanical properties.
The present study investigated the temperature depen-
dence of tensile properties and deformation mechanisms of a
newly developed Ni-based casting alloy, and their correlations
were revealed. Moreover, by conducting ﬁrst-principles cal-
culations and alloying method, we successfully employed
cobalt (Co) and tungsten (W) alloying to effectively delay the
degradation of strength at high temperatures.

3.2. Deformation microstructures
The deformation microstructures of specimens at various
temperatures are shown in Fig. 3 . Consistent with many lit-
eratures [ 6,20,21], the deformation mechanism of cast super-
alloys at low temperatures is mainly APB shearing. Within the
slip bands, dislocations are in a paired manner, as arrowed in
Fig. 3 (a). Moreover, considering the small distance between
the pairs, the deformation mechanism can be categorized as
the strongly coupled dislocations shearing. During deforma-
tion, a/2 <110> dislocations are generally produced in the g
matrix at ﬁrst driven by the applied shear stress. Fig. 3 (e)
shows the deformation microstructure of the sample under-
going 0.19% plastic strain at 850
/C14 C. No slip band can be wit-
nessed in the matrix, and the deformation is homogeneous.
Some dislocation loops exhibit prismatic morphology, as
arrowed in Fig. 3 (e). Formation of such prismatic dislocation
loops generally signiﬁes that dislocations start to leave their
primary slip planes and bypass g
0 phase through prismatic
cross-slip [ 8]. Apart from that, some jogged dislocations are
also observed, which do not ﬁt the interface of g/g'. These
ﬁndings are in agreement with those reported by Vennemann
et al. [ 22], who observed that a homogeneous distribution of
dislocations is created when cross-slip and climb dislocations
dominate plastic deformation. At 900
/C14 C, there is a signiﬁcant
decrease in the number of dislocations. Additionally, heavily
jogged dislocations are frequently observed climbing over the
g
0 phase. This phenomenon implies that the climbing process
of dislocations becomes increasingly active at elevated
temperatures.
Based on the above experimental observations and ana-
lyses, the dominant deformation mechanism of alloy K4800
changes from APB shearing (RT-600
/C14 C) to Orowan looping
(600e800 /C14 C), and to cross slip and local climbing ( >800 /C14 C).
When the temperature rises from 800 to 900 /C14 C, the yield
strength of K4800 alloy shows a rapid decrease for 283 MPa.
Similar to those previously studied [ 6e8,23,24], it is because
the thermal activation results in widespread dislocation
cross-slip and climbing ( Fig. 6). In this condition, dislocations
can easily overcome g
0 phase and the resistance of g0 phase
and the whole g/g0 structure against deformation is severely
impaired, which restricts the service temperature of the alloy
to no higher than 800 /C14 C. Accordingly, for the sake of the high-
temperature performance of K4800 alloy, it is critical to pay
more attention to improving the alloy 's resistance against the
thermally activated dislocation migration.

reducing the SFE of g compared to Co [ 26], which generates
more SFs in the matrix and agrees with the microstructures

alloy. Thirdly, the W element makes the recovery process of

wider continuous SFs form in K4800Co. Therefore, given the
current conditions of elemental addition, the addition of the
W element is better than the Co element to improve the high-
temperature strength of the K4800 alloy.
From this research, reducing SFE can optimize the high-
temperature performance of K4800 alloy to a certain extent.

4. Conclusions
1. The dominant deformation mechanism of alloy K4800
changes from APB shearing (RT-600 /C14 C) to Orowan looping
(600e800 /C14 C), and to cross slip and local climbing
(800/C14 Ce900 /C14 C). Cross-slip and local climbing assisted by
thermal activation induce the rapid decline in alloy
strength above 800
/C14 C.
2. The addition of solutes, particularly Cr and Co, can pro-
mote the formation of stack-ing faults in the Ni 3(Al, Ti)
phase. At the same time, W makes the stacking fault re-
covery process more difﬁcult and generates a steadier
stacking fault conﬁguration in the Ni 3(Al, Ti) phase.
3. The addition of W and Co elements delays the reduction in
the tensile strength of the alloy at temperatures between
800
/C14 C and 850 /C14 C, suppressing the cross-slip through a
large amount of stacking faults.
