Abstract High-strength β titanium alloys represented by
near β titanium alloy and metastable β titanium alloy are
preferred materials for large-scale load-carrying structures.
In order to achieve the precise regulation of microstructure in
the deformation process, massive efforts have been made to
study the ﬂow behavior and microstructure evolution of β
titanium alloy in the hot deformation process. This paper
reviews the ﬂow behavior of high-strength titanium alloy,
including the effects of initial microstructure, deformation
process parameters, work hardening, and dynamic softening
on ﬂow stress. Furthermore, the effects of deformation
process parameters on the apparent activation energy for
deformation and strain rate sensitivity coefﬁcient are ana-
lyzed. The discontinuous yield phenomenon is discussed,
and the constitutive models of ﬂow stress are summarized.
Furthermore, some microstructural evolution models are
reviewed. Finally, the development direction and difﬁculties
of the ﬂow behavior and constitutive model are pointed out.
Keywords High-strength β titanium alloy; Hot
deformation; Flow behavior; Constitutive model
1 Introduction
Titanium alloys have been widely applied to aviation,
chemical processing and pharmaceutical due to high
speciﬁc strength, speciﬁc stiffness and excellent corrosion
resistance [ 1, 2]. Controlled alloying and thermomechani-
cal processing can produce different titanium alloys
according to the difference in microstructure and compo-
sition so that the mechanical and physical properties of the
alloy can be changed according to speciﬁc applications. β
titanium alloy contains a great amount of β stabilizers,
including isomorphism β stabilizers such as V, Mo, Nb, Ta,
etc., and eutectoid β stabilizers such as Cr, Mn, Fe, Si. β
titanium alloys can be further divided into near β titanium
alloys, metastable β titanium alloys, and stable β titanium
alloys [ 3]. High-strength titanium alloys are generally near
β titanium alloys and metastable β titanium alloys. The
high-strength β alloys have good process plasticity in
annealed or solid solution. It also has better room tem-
perature strength, fracture toughness and hardenability than
α?β alloy. Based on the above advantages and charac-
teristics, they are widely used in large-scale load-carrying
structures such as landing gears and connecting compo-
nents to meet the design requirements of lightweight, long
life and high reliability of aircraft [ 4, 5].
In industrial production, the forming method of the
above large-scale load-carrying structures is still mainly
die forging. The practice has proved that the forging parts
have higher density and reasonable interior streamline
distributing, which can signiﬁcantly improve the mechan-
ical properties and service life. However, titanium alloy is
susceptible to forging process parameters (deformation
temperature, deformation amount, deformation speed and
cooling rate after forging) [ 6] and initial state (equiaxed
microstructure, lamellar microstructure, mixed

3 Flow behavior of β titanium alloy during high-
temperature compression
The stress–strain curve at high-temperature compression is
a comprehensive reaction of the microstructure evolution
and properties during plastic deformation. The ﬂow stress
is signiﬁcantly affected by the deformation parameter and
the initial state of the sample. Different deformation
mechanisms in the deformation process will lead to dif-
ferent types of curves. Therefore, studying the ﬂow
behavior of β titanium alloy has important theoretical
signiﬁcance and engineering value for optimizing titanium
alloy forging deformation process, die design and equip-
ment selection.
3.1 Flow stress –strain curve of typical β titanium
alloy
Figure 2 summarizes the variation characteristics of ﬂow
stress and the microstructure after the deformation of high-
strength β titanium alloys [ 6, 34–38]. The ﬂow stress curve
during hot compression deformation can be roughly divi-
ded into the following types.
1. Continuous softening curve. At the beginning of
deformation, the ﬂow stress increases rapidly with
the strain raising. After reaching the peak stress, the
ﬂow stress decreases with the increase in strain,
showing a dynamic ﬂow softening. It usually occurs
in low deformation temperature and high strain rate.
The dynamic recrystallization (DRX) (single-phase
region), dynamic globularization (two-phase region)
and ﬂow localization are the primary softening
mechanism.
2. Near-steady-state curve. In the initial stage, it is
similar to the continuous softening curve. When a
speciﬁc strain value is reached, the curve changes from
continuous decline to a steady ﬂow. At this time,
dynamic softening and work hardening reach a
dynamic balance. It is usually observed at low strain
rates. There are multiple softening mechanisms inside
the sample.

3.2 Affecting factors of ﬂow stress of β titanium
alloy
It is well known that the initial states, such as initial
microstructure, chemical composition and physical prop-
erties, have an important inﬂuence on the ﬂow behavior. In
addition, the deformation parameters also affect the ﬂow
behavior. For this reason, many researchers have analyzed
in detail the effects of the initial state and deformation
parameters on the ﬂow behavior.

At the same time, the lamellar thickness is also one of

has proved to be an effective method to improve forming
accuracy of parts. To this end, Zhao et al. [ 37, 48, 49]
studied the hot deformation behavior of PM and ingot
metallurgy (IM) Ti–5553 alloys. The PM alloy has lower
ﬂow stress than IM alloy (in the single-phase region and
two-phase region). This is mainly because PM alloy has
smaller grains and higher grain boundary density, which
will promote the viscous ﬂow of grain boundary and dis-
locations slip, climb in hot deformation. Moreover, it also
conﬁrmed that the PM alloy has better hot workability [ 49].
In short, the initial microstructure, especially the shape
and size of the α phase, will have an important inﬂuence on
the hot deformation behavior of the β titanium alloy. The
lamellar α phase is more prone to fragmentation and
globularization than the equiaxed α phase, but it will have a
higher deformation resistance in the initial period. The
greater the thickness of the lamellar α is, the better the
deformation resistance is. The PM alloy has lower defor-
mation resistance due to the ﬁne grains.

3.2.2.3 Strain Generally speaking, the effect of strain
on the ﬂow stress of β titanium alloy is related to the strain
rate and deformation temperature. The strain is the critical
factor that affects the work hardening and dynamic soft-
ening of the alloy, and the internal mechanism of stress
change is the interaction of work hardening and dynamic
softening [ 28, 36, 40, 59]. Lin et al. [ 52] found many
substructures in the β phase at a true strain of 0.36, indi-
cating that many dislocations are generated in the work
hardening process and increased deformation resistance.
As the strain increases, the number of substructures
decreases, and DRX grains are gradually produced, as
indicated in Fig. 6a–c [ 60]. The softening caused by DRX
further reduces the ﬂow stress. For the titanium alloy with
the initial microstructure of lamellar α phase, the burgers
orientation relationship (BOR) of the α/β interface is bro-
ken with the increased true strain, which can accelerate the
globularization of lamellar α phases [ 53, 61], as shown in
Fig. 6d–f [ 43]. Moreover, the globularization process also
leads to the signiﬁcant decrease in ﬂow stress. For β tita-
nium alloys with equiaxed α phase, the size and volume
fraction of α phase will gradually decrease with the strain
raising at the same deformation temperature and strain rate.
The researchers believed that the deformation heating
increases with strain increasing [ 54]. For β titanium alloys
with a single β phase microstructure, Yang et al. [ 62] found
that the volume fraction of DRX increases with the
increase in deformation, and the deformation texture
decreases gradually.

3.5 Discontinuous yielding phenomenon
At the beginning of deformation, the stress–strain curve
rapidly reaches the peak value and then rapidly decreases
to the yield point. This phenomenon is called the discon-
tinuous yielding phenomenon. Based on current researches,
most of the discontinuous yielding behavior of β titanium
alloy has the following characteristics [ 28, 35, 60, 69, 73,
77, 78, 97]: (1) most of the discontinuous yielding phe-
nomena occur at the high deformation temperature or high
strain rate; (2) strain rate and deformation temperature have
signiﬁcant effects on the ﬂow stress difference between the
upper and lower yield points; (3) strain rate has a signiﬁ-
cant effect on the strain at the end of ﬂow stress oscillation.
The factors that affect the discontinuous yielding phe-
nomenon of β titanium alloy during high-temperature
plastic deformation are very complicated. Wang and Li
[98] conﬁrmed that discontinuous yield was attributed to
the moving dislocation produced by grain boundaries. The
latest research found that the Ti–7333 alloy also exhibited a
discontinuous yielding phenomenon at a deformation
temperature of 700 °C and low strain rate (0.001 s
−1). This
is due to the fragmentation of grain boundary α and the
rapid increase in dislocation density in the β grain bound-
ary region (Fig. 11)[ 42, 99]. In Fig. 11b, β grain boundary
is covered by grain boundary α. In addition, in Fig. 11d,
almost all β grain boundaries are deformed, and the ori-
entations of β regions near the grain boundary are greatly
deviated from those of their gravity center, indicating the
formation of high density dislocations. Transmission
electron microscope (TEM) observation further conﬁrms
the occurrence of dislocations (Fig. 11e, f), and the slip
systems of most dislocations are {110} \111 [ , which are
movable dislocations. Abbasi et al. [ 89] also found that the
yield point elongation and ﬂow stress at the upper and
lower yield points are related to the Zener–Hollomon
parameters.

high, the ANN model does not have physical meaning,
limiting its application to a certain extent.

5 Summary and outlook
In recent years, the ﬂow behavior and constitutive model of
high-strength β titanium alloy during hot deformation are
reviewed in this paper. Generally speaking, there still exist
some problems in the hot deformation behavior of high-
strength β titanium alloys based on hot compression
experiments:
(1) How to precisely control the initial microstructure of
the sample and explore the relationship between the
microstructure and mechanical properties after hot
deformation? The microstructure is the decisive
factor inﬂuencing titanium alloy properties. The
difference in initial microstructure will affect the
mechanical properties of forgings after hot deforma-
tion. However, although some studies have reported
the effects of different initial states on the hot
deformation behavior, they are only limited to a
single morphology and size of α phase. Furthermore,
it is difﬁcult to quantitatively analyze the relationship
between mechanical properties and microstructure
after hot deformation. Therefore, it is necessary to
study the inﬂuence of α phase morphology, size
change, proportion and β grain size on the microstruc-
ture evolution and the quantitative relationship
between microstructure and properties.
(2) Lacking of quantitative description and model for
ﬂow stress and microstructure of high-strength β
titanium alloy. Some classical models with simple
forms and convenient applications have drawn wide
attention in terms of ﬂow stress models, but they do
not involve microscopic plastic deformation mecha-
nisms. Therefore, considering the diversity and com-
plexity of the initial microstructure and softening
mechanism of the high-strength β titanium alloy, its
application in the deformation process of the high-
strength β titanium alloy reduces the prediction
accuracy of the ﬂow behavior. Besides, the traditional
microstructure model is only a function related to
deformation parameters. It does not consider a series
of internal mechanisms such as the morphology, size
and volume fraction of α phase, subgrain size, and
dislocation evolution energy of high-strength β tita-
nium alloy during deformation.
Based on the problems mentioned above, this article
proposes the following important development directions:
(1) Establishment of a quantitative relationship between
microstructure and ﬂow softening. The complex
initial microstructure and deformation process param-
eters during the hot deformation of titanium alloys
will signiﬁcantly affect work hardening and ﬂow
softening. In addition, deformation heating is also a
key factor affecting ﬂow behavior. Therefore, it is
necessary to study the inﬂuence mechanism of initial
microstructure, such as different morphology and
volume fraction of α on work hardening and ﬂow
softening, and establish the quantitative relationship
between microstructure and ﬂow softening after
deformation. Finally, the precise control of the
microstructure of titanium alloy before and after
deformation is realized.
(2) Developing a macro–micro coupling model that
accurately describes the hot deformation behavior of
high-strength β titanium alloys. The classic Arrhenius
model is only related to deformation parameters and
has a simple form. However, high-strength β titanium
alloys have multiple softening mechanisms in the
single-phase region and the two-phase region, signif-
icantly increasing the complexity of deformation
behavior and microstructure evolution. Therefore, it
is necessary to deeply study the mutual coordination
and quantitative relationship between various soften-
ing mechanisms and establish a macro–micro cou-
pling model including substructure, dislocation
density, grain size, recrystallization and other inﬂu-
encing factors from the perspective of internal
variables.
