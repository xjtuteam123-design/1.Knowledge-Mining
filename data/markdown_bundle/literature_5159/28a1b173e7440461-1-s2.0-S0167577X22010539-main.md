ABSTRACT 
The brittle γ–γ’ eutectic phase in the inter-dendritic region is the major cause of cracking of GH4099 alloys 
prepared by laser additive manufacturing technology. In this study, selective laser melting (SLM) was employed 
to prepare GH4099 alloy with refined microstructures. The alloy showed excellent mechanical properties, while 
the alloy cracking was retarded effectively. Fine γ
′
precipitates were formed after aging treatment, which 
contributed greatly to the high strength of GH4099 alloy. The tensile strength of the alloy at room temperature 
and high temperature were 1217.8 and 955.0 MPa, respectively. 
1. Introduction 
GH4099 is a typical γ’– Ni
3
(Al, Ti) strengthened superalloy. GH4099 
alloy exhibits supreme thermal stability due to the strengthening effect 
of W, Mo, Al, Ti, and Co elements, and this alloy can work steadily at 
temperatures as high as 1000 
◦
C in aerospace components. The additive 
manufacturing of GH4099 alloy has attracted an increasing attention in 
recent years. 
Additive manufacturing technology mainly includes laser solid 
forming (LSF), wire and arc additive manufacturing technology 
(WAAM), and directional energy deposition technology (DED) [1] . 
However, γ - γ
′
eutectic phase easily forms in the inter-dendritic region 
and on the grain boundaries due to the element segregation in the DED 
and WAAM technologies [2,3] , which causes high hot cracking suscep -
tibility. The enrichment of Al and Ti elements on the grain boundary in 
the LSF GH4099 alloy [4] weakens the grain boundary, which results in 
crack nucleation during the solidification process. Restraining the pre -
cipitation of γ–γ
′
phase during solidification is considered to be an 
important way to reduce the cracking of GH4099 alloy during additive 
manufacturing. Compared with these additive manufacturing technol -
ogies, the selective laser melting (SLM) technology has higher heating 
and cooling rates (10
6
– 10
8 
K/s), which significantly reduce element 
segregation [5] . SLM technology has of great potential in fabrication of 
GH4099 alloy. 
In this study, GH4099 alloy is prepared by an optimized SLM process, 
which eliminates the low-melting phase and avoids the alloy cracking 
during the solidification process. A suitable heat treatment process is 
also adopted to regulate the microstructure. As a result, the SLM 
GH4099 alloy shows a homogeneous microstructure and excellent me -
chanical properties. The work extends the application of GH4099 alloy 
in the aerospace engineering. 
2. Materials and methods 
The particle size of GH4099 powder is 15 ~ 45 μ m. The SLM 
equipment used in this experiment is the LiM-X400A produced by 
Tianjin Radium Laser Technology Co., Ltd. The layer thickness is 
controlled at 40 μ m, and the laser power is set to be 295 ± 5 W. The spot 
diameter is 90 μ m and the scanning speed is 960 mm/s. The direction of 
the laser scanning path between layers is deflected by 60
◦
. The circu -
lating protective gas is argon. The as-deposited sample was first sub -
jected to a high-temperature solution treatment at 1115 ± 5 
◦
C for 2 h, 
followed by water cooling. The solution treated samples were then aged 
at 800 
◦
C for 8 h and then air quenched. The surface topography was 
etched after polishing with a reagent of 1 ml HF + 10 ml HNO
3 
+ 50 ml 
HCl + 39 ml H
2
O. The microstructures were detected using a field 
emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM) with ZEISS SUPRA55 
and transmission electron microscope (TEM) with JEOL JEM-2100F.

3. Result and discussion 
The microstructures on XZ and XY directions ( Fig. 1 (a, b)) show that 
the fine dendrites and cellular dendrites are distributed in the molten 
pool inside with the spacing of about 500 nm (much smaller than the 
dendrite spacing in DED GH4099 alloy (about 4 μ m)) [3] . Moreover, the 
second dendrite arms are underdeveloped ( Fig. 1 (e, f)). This reason is 
that SLM shows a faster cooling rate than DED, which greatly reduces 
element segregation and refines the dendritic structure [5] . The cellular 
dendrites are believed to be altered primary dendrites caused by the 
inherently high heating and cooling rates of the solidification process 
[6] . The dendritic morphology disappears and twin boundaries appear 
in the grains after solution and aging treatment ( Fig. 1 (c, d)). The car -
bides are incompletely dissolved after heat treatment ( Fig. 1 (g, h)). The 
EBSD IPF maps show that the microstructure is mainly composed of 
epitaxial growth columnar grains ( Fig. 1 (i)). After heat treatment, the 
columnar grains are replaced by equiaxed grains ( Fig. 1 (j, k)). The as- 
deposited sample shows a rotating cube texture in Fig. 1 (m). Preferen -
tial growth direction 〈 1 0 0 〉 and direction of heat flow during SLM 
process lead to 45
◦
– 60
◦
angle between substrate or primary dendrite and 
previously deposited layer, which results in rotated cube texture [7] . 
The as-deposited sample has the strongest texture intensity (max =
16.05) with (1 0 0) of crystal planes parallel to the deposition direction. 
After solution treatment, the epitaxial growth columnar grains are 
replaced by fine equiaxed grains through static recrystallization; thus, 
the crystallographic texture ( 〈 1 0 0 〉 -oriented) gradually decreases 
( Fig. 1 (n)). The 〈 1 0 0 〉 orientation spreads to other orientations in the 
aged sample, and the grain orientation becomes more random; as a 
result, the maximum texture intensity of the aged sample is 3.58 ( Fig. 1 
(o)).

deposited samples ( Fig. 2 (a)). The SLM forming process is a non- 
equilibrium solidification process [8] , and the precipitation of γ

1217.8 MPa, 783.1 MPa, and 35.6%, respectively. A large number of 
statistically stored dislocations are generated due to the non-equilibrium 
solidification process, which results in considerable strengthening ef -
fect. After solution treatment, the static recrystallization occurs in the 
SLM GH4099 alloy, and the decrease in dislocation density leads to the 
decrease in strength and the increase in ductility. Many dimples appear 
in the solid solution fracture, which are deep and relatively large in size; 
the phenomenon is consistent with the higher plasticity of the alloy. 
After aging treatment, the dispersed γ
′
phase in the SLM GH4099 alloy 
forms a coherent distortion zone with the matrix and increases the 
hindrance of dislocations, which leads to the rise in strength and the 
decrease in plasticity. A large number of dimples appear in the aging 
fracture, which are relatively small in size. The SLM GH4099 alloy 
shows relatively good strength at high temperature ( Fig. 3 (b)). The 
mechanical properties of superalloys prepared by additive 
manufacturing at room temperature and high temperature in recent 
years are summarized in Fig. 3 (c, d). By contrast, SLM GH4099 alloy 
shows better comprehensive mechanical properties at room temperature 
and high temperatures. SLM alloy has better tensile strength than DED 
alloy because it shows finer microstructure (e.g., grain, dendrite struc -
ture, and strengthening phase) than DED GH4099 alloy. 
4. Conclusion 
GH4099 alloy with refined microstructures and excellent mechanical 
properties was prepared by the SLM. This method reduces element 
segregation and eliminates the low-melting phase. γ
′
nanoprecipitates 
can be increased greatly through aging treatment, which contributes to 
the excellent mechanical properties of the alloy. The tensile strength, 
yield strength of SLM GH4099 alloy at room temperature are 1217.8 and 
955.0 MPa, respectively. These values are better than those of forged 
sample (UTS: 980 – 1079 MPa, YS: 686 – 726 MPa). This study will
