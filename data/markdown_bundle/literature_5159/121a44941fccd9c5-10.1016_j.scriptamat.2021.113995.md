Severe plastic deformation (SPD) leads to substantial strengthening in materials via grain reﬁnement, 
however, the main drawback of the materials processed using known SPD techniques is the lack of uni- 
form plastic deformation due to low strain hardening capability. In this study, equal channel angular 
pressing (ECAP) at high temperatures was utilized to activate simultaneous twinning-induced (TWIP) and 
transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) in the CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy (HEA), which are anoma- 
lous at high temperatures and reported for the ﬁrst time. Transmission electron microscopy and electron 
backscattered diffraction analysis were performed to reveal microstructural features. The presence of the 
transformed and twinned regions as well as grain reﬁnement in this alloy yielded a high strength level 
(around 1 GPa) with uniform plastic deformation ability in room temperature tension experiments. The 
observation of TWIP/TRIP was attributed to high strength levels applied via ECAP and relatively low stack- 
ing fault energy of the present HEA.

High entropy alloys (HEAs) form solid-solution phases and ex- 
hibit unique properties such as high strength-ductility combina- 
tion with remarkable work hardening rate that is not only due 
to dislocation plasticity but also via twinning induced-plasticity 
(TWIP) [ 1 , 2 ] and transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) [3] . The 
equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi alloy is the most well-known HEA with 
a stable fcc crystal structure, which exhibits such outstand- 
ing strength-ductility combination at low temperatures associated 
with the transition from dislocation glide to deformation twinning 
[4] . 
A major drawback of CoCrFeMnNi HEA is its low yield strength 
( σy ) [4] . Severe plastic deformation (SPD) has recently been shown 
as a feasible method to enhance σy of HEAs [5–10] . However, most 
SPD processed HEAs exhibit low ductility levels because of the sig- 
niﬁcant reduction in work hardening and the saturation of defect 
densities after processing [5–10] . To counteract the restricted plas- 
tic deformation ability, alloys are usually subjected to heat treat- 
ments after SPD to increase ductility at the expense of strength 
[6–8] . Annealing can improve strain hardening rate, delaying the 
onset of necking, via either decreasing the defect density or achiev- 
ing a bimodal grain size distribution [ 11 , 12 ]. However, annealing at

relatively high temperatures can also form undesirable precipitates 
[13] , or result in phase decomposition [14] . 
Equal channel angular pressing (ECAP) is advantageous in com- 
parison to other SPD techniques due to its capability to achieve a 
homogeneous deformation throughout the bulk material via sim- 
ple shear, while maintaining a constant cross-sectional area that 
allows for repeated processing until desired deformation levels are 
reached [15] . ECAP is applicable to relatively large samples [16] , 
and therefore can be used to generate potentially strong and duc- 
tile alloys for a variety of applications. Although ECAP has been 
utilized in reﬁning the microstructure of many fcc alloys, there 
are only a few studies reporting ECAP for grain reﬁnement in 
HEAs [ 9 , 10 ]. Shahmir et al. [9] reported a high yield strength 
(~1GPa) due to grain reﬁnement in CoCrFeMnNi ( d ≈10 0 nm) with- 
out strain hardening under tension after four pass ECAP at 400 °C 
in a ECAP die with a channel angle of 110 °. Similarly, very high 
yield strength (~ 1.2 GPa) has been obtained at the expense of 
ductility (80% vs. 8%) in CoCrNi medium entropy alloy (MEA) af- 
ter 3 pass ECAP in a die with 120 ° channel angle [10] . With lim- 
ited studies on ECAP of M/HEAs, the exact microstructural evolu- 
tion in these alloys during SPD is not well understood, and the fol- 
lowing questions remain outstanding: (1) Is it possible to achieve 
concurrent strengthening and toughening without a need for 
post-processing? (2) Are other strengthening mechanisms such as 
TWIP/TRIP occur in CoCrFeMnNi HEA during SPD as well as grain 
reﬁnement?

Deformation twinning is one of the effective grain reﬁnement 
methods since twin boundaries act as barriers towards dislocation 
motion, leading to Hall-Petch hardening [1] . While deformation 
twinning has been rarely reported in CoCrFeMnNi at room temper- 
ature (RT), it often takes place at cryogenic temperatures [4] . TRIP 
is another deformation mechanism that leads to toughening in fcc 
crystals, especially at ambient and low temperatures [3] . Therefore, 
both TWIP and TRIP are desired for better mechanical properties in 
fcc crystals. 
In the present study, we demonstrate simultaneous activation of 
TWIP and TRIP mechanisms during medium and high temperature 
ECAP in a HEA. Combined with the bimodal, composite grain struc- 
ture, the existence of twin and ε-martensite boundaries lead to a 
high strength-ductility combination. Ultimately, this study is aimed 
to improve the understanding of the complex interactions among 
grain reﬁnement, dislocation structure, TWIP/TRIP phenomena and 
their effects on the mechanical properties.

The higher resolution EBSD map of the B2 ECAP sample in 
Fig. 2 d reveals two different regions, forming a composite mi- 
crostructure. The ﬁrst region consists of a big elongated grain, 
while the second includes recrystallized ultra-ﬁne grains (UFG). 
This heterogeneous microstructure leads to a composite effect dur- 
ing plastic deformation, i.e. big grains providing extended plastic 
deformation while small grains contributes to strengthening. Many 
fcc materials exposed to SPD show very high strength levels but 
lack both ductility and strain hardening because of the satura- 
tion of the defect densities [5–10] . To improve plastic deforma- 
tion ability, a heterogeneous grain distribution is desired which 
can be achieved after annealing [1 1] . The present ECAP HEA sam- 
ples, on the other hand, demonstrate such desired heterogeneous 
microstructure after two passes at high and medium temperatures 
without the need for post-processing heat treatments.

The activation of twinning and ε-martensite at high tempera- 
tures can be linked to the effect of applied stress on partial dislo- 
cation separation [19–22] . The equilibrium separation distance be- 
tween the Shockley partials can increase or decrease with applied 
stress in HEAs and other low and medium SFE fcc metals, depend- 
ing on grain orientation and applied stress direction [1] . This leads 
to an effective SFE, which dictates the deformation mechanism in 
that particular grain [ 19 , 23 ]. Shockley partials are known to play a 
signiﬁcant role in nucleation of both twins and martensitic trans- 
formation [ 24 , 25 ]. In fact, it was discovered in cobalt, cobalt al- 
loys, and low SFE stainless steels that fcc-to-hcp martensitic trans- 
formation takes place in the presence of extensive wide stacking 
faults and stair-rod dislocations, as shown in the works of Fujita 
and Ueda [24] and Christian et al. [26] . All these planar defects oc- 
cur via increased partial dislocation separation in low to medium 
SFE materials. Therefore, the effective SFE phenomenon due to the 
effect of applied stress on partial dislocation separation should be 
responsible for simultaneous twinning and ε-martensite formation 
during ECAP in the present HEA. 
Indeed, ECAP leads to high stress levels on partial dislocations 
due to the high strain level and strain rate applied. This makes par- 
tial dislocations separation easier in grains with favorable orien- 
tation and reduces effective SFE [ 1 , 12 ]. The reduced effective SFE 
causes the formation of both twinning and ε-martensite during 
ECAP, which provides more grain reﬁnement. The dynamic Hall- 
Patch effect decreases the dislocation mean free path and should 
increase the material’s strength [1] .

and strengthening in CoCrFeMnNi HEAs at RT [4] , ECAP clearly 
shows a great potential for strengthening these materials via grain 
reﬁnement, high defect density and the activation of simultane-

formed via ECAP can improve plastic deformation ability and pro-

In Fig. 4 b, the compression test results show that the B1 and B2 
ECAP samples exhibit tension/compression (T/C) asymmetry, while 
the HE does not. Strong texture ( Fig. 2 d) and oriented internal 
stresses ( Fig. 3 g) in the ECAP microstructure are the main rea- 
sons for the observed T/C asymmetry [ 12 , 29 , 30 ]. The strong T/C 
asymmetry in UFG copper was linked to dislocation polarity and 
back stress accumulation at GBs dictated by ECAP [30] , which gov- 
erns the ﬂow stress during the reversed straining. Furthermore, ex- 
perimental observations pointed out that low angle GBs produce 
back stress leading to T/C asymmetry in UFG copper [30] . EBSD

The above microstructural studies demonstrate that the ECAP 
processed CoCrFeMnNi HEA possesses a composite microstructure 
with large elongated grains, mostly having high defect density, re- 
ﬁned recrystallized grains, and deformation induced twinning and 
ɛ -martensite lamella. It is well-known that twinning and marten- 
sitic transformation are both orientation dependent. For example, 
the tensile deformation along the [ 111 ] oriented grain is domi- 
nated by twinning, while the tensile deformation along the [001] 
oriented grain is dominated by multiple slip systems resulting in 
cross-slip that causes formation of dislocation tangles and dislo- 
cation wall-structure in low SFE crystals [ 1 , 19 ]. A microstructure 
with different orientations, therefore, can easily experience these 
different kinds of deformation modes. As a result of this hetero- 
geneous microstructure and their effects on dislocation mean free 
path, high tensile and compressive strength levels (1 GPa) ( Fig.4a- 
b ) have been obtained with the relatively high uniform elongation 
levels in the ECAP processed materials. This unique microstructure 
is schematically presented in Fig.4c. 
In summary, ECAP of CoCrFeMnNi HEA at medium and high 
temperatures resulted in an unexpected formation of deformation 
twinning and ε-martensite, in addition to grain reﬁnement, in- 
crease in dislocation density, and heterogeneous grain size distri- 
bution. This composite microstructure was responsible for the high 
strength levels ductility combination at room temperature. The si- 
multaneous TWIP/TRIP effect at high temperatures was attributed 
to the effect of low SFE and high stress levels on partial disloca- 
tion separation. The composite microstructure achieved with ECAP 
enables defect storage, provides strain hardening capability, and 
postpones neck instability, which ultimately provides uniform plas- 
tic deformation. The observations and mechanisms discovered here 
are also applicable to other M/HEAs and will help tailor their mi- 
crostructure to improve the properties further.
