Effect of Solid Solution Treatment on Microstructure of 
Fe-Ni Based High Strength Low Thermal Expansion Alloy 
ZHANG Jian-fu' , TU Yi-fan' , XU Jin' , ZHANG Jian-sheng' , ZHANG Jing-lin' 
(1. Central Iron and Steel Research Institute, Beijing 100081, China; 2. University of Science and 
Technology Beijing, Beijing 100083, China) 
Abstract: The influence of solid solution treatments on the dissolution of carbides precipitates, the grain size, and the 
hardness of high strength low expansion alloy were investigated through XRD analysis, microstructure observations, 
and theoretical computation. It was seen that most primary Mo2C type carbide band dissolved in a temperature range 
of 1 100-1 150 "c. When the temperature was over 1 200 "c , the grain size increased remarkably, which led to the 
reduction of hardness. 
Key words: high strength low expansion alloy; solid solution temperature; microstructure; carbide dissolution 
Low expansion alloy (Invar alloy) has been widely 
used in shadow mask, measuring instruments, integrat- 
ed circuits, and several other precise devices since it was 
first invented in the 1930~"-~'. However, low expan- 
sion alloy has relatively low strength and hardness, 
and this feature has limited its use since it can not be 
used as structural materialc3'. 
The strengthening methods of low expansion al- 
loys have been the subject of several studies at all 
 time^[^-^'. These investigations have shown that the 
addition of alloy elements (such as Ti, Al, Be) and 
the subsequent ageing treatments can increase the 
strength of low expansion alloys For 
example, as little as Be of 0. 5% increases the ulti- 
mate tensile strength of Fe-36%Ni Invar alloys from 
5 00 MPa up to 1 100 MPa in the peak aged condi- 
tion. Generally, precipitation of the secondary phase 
not only strengthens the low expansion alloy but al- 
so decreases the disadvantageous effect on the ther- 
mal expansion coefficient, which is the important 
property of low expansion alloys. 
Recently, more and more studies are focused on 
the strengthening mechanism of these alloyscg-'". 
However, the solution treatment of the Fe-Ni based 
high strength low expansion alloy has received less 
direct attention, although appropriate solid solution

1 Experimental 
The composition of the investigated FeNi based 
high strength low expansion alloy with Mo is (mass 
percent, %) : Ni 38.0, Mo 1.0, C 0. 12, Mn 0.27, 
Si 0. 012, and Fe balance. The alloy was melted in a 
vacuum induction furnace, forged into rods, from which 
specimens were prepared for experiments. These speci- 
mens were subjected to the following heat treatments: 
solution treatment at 1 050 'C, 1 100 "C, 1 150 "C, 
and 1 200 'C for 2 h, respectively, followed by wa- 
ter quenching. 
Specimens were observed using a Neophot type 
optical microscope and X-ray diffractometers, re- 
spectively. The hardness was measured on a Vickers 
tester at a load of 9.8 N/kg. In addition, the amount 
variation of precipitates with the solution tempera- 
ture was analyzed using the Thermo-calc Software.

2 Results and Discussion 
2. 1 Effect of different solution treatments on micro- 
structures 
Fig. 1 shows the initial microstructure of the 
specimen before solution treatment, which is a mix- 
ture of unevenly forged structure and MozC type 
carbides [Fig. 4 (b)]. It is seen that most carbides 
aggregate to form the dense "band" shape, which is 
a forged metallographic characteristic of primary 
carbides produced during the smelting process and 
the rest carbides are distributed in the grain and 
boundary. The appearance of pre-existing primary 
carbides band is harmful to the process of aging 
treatment of low expansion high strength alloy'"'. 
Thus, the solution treatment of the specimen brings 
about two results, one is to eliminate uneven de- 
formation structure and to homogenize the struc- 
ture; the other, more importantly, is to dissolve 
primary carbides into austenite. 
Fig. 2 shows the corresponding microstructure 
of different solid solution treatments. It can be seen 
that dissolving the primary carbides back into the 
austenite matrix is the main course of the solid solu- 
tion treatment. Heat treatment at 1 050 "C leads to 
the reduction of the thickness of the carbides band. 
Generally, the ability of diffusion of elements is im- 
proved at higher heating temperature, which accel- 
erates the dissolution of carbides. With a heating 
temperature of 1 100 "C , the amount of residual car- 
bides decreases and the microstructure of the speci- 
men is homogenized markedly compared with the ini- 
tial hot deformation structure [Fig. 2 (b)]. There are

2.2 Effect of different solution treatments on hardness 
Fig. 6 shows the relationship between the hardness 
and the solution temperature. The hardness of the speci- 
men increases first when heated at 1 050-1 150 'C , and 
then decreases with temperature raising to 1 200 "C. 
With increasing heating temperature, the carbides 
begin to dissolve into austenite gradually. Since the 
dissolution of Mo and C atoms leads to the distortion 
of austenite lattice, this process causes the solution 
to strengthen to a certain extent. Thus, the hard- 
ness increases. As for the substantial reduction of 
hardness at 1 200 "C, the abnormal austenite grain 
growth counteracts the solution strengthening effect.

known that homogenous structure and less residual

3 Conclusions 
(1) Solution treatment of the FeNi based high 
strength low thermal expansion alloy led to the dis- 
solution of Mo2C carbides. The higher the solution 
temperature, the lesser were the carbides found. 
(2) The average grain size of the investigated 
alloys increased from 14 pm to 28 pm in the temper- 
ature range of about 1 050 "C to 1 150 "C. When the 
temperature reached 1 200 'C, the grains grew ab- 
normally to 46 pm. 
(3) The hardness increased with increasing the 
treatment temperature, which is largely due to the 
increase in solute content associated with the disso- 
lution of MozC carbides. The hardness began to de- 
crease at 1 200 "C for the sake of abnormal growth. 
(4) The dissolution of carbides and the homo- 
geneity of the microstructure indicate that the opti- 
mal solution treatment temperature must be 1 100- 
1 150 "C.
