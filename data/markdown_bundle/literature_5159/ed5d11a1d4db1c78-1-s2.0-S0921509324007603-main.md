ABSTRACT 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) with face-centered cubic crystal structure (FCC) usually have good ductility but 
relatively low strength. In this work, we propose a new strategy to address the insufficient strength of FCC 
structured HEAs. The strategy is carried out in two ways: (1) By reducing the stacking fault energy of alloy system 
to improve its work hardening ability; (2) By adjusting the content of alloying element to control the quantity of 
L1
2
, σ and κ phases, thereby strengthening its matrix. Based on the above ideas, the non-equimolar Fe
2
CoNiV
x 
HEAs were designed by adjusting the element content of the classic FeCoNiV HEA. The results show that 
Fe
2
CoNiV
1.3 
alloy has excellent work-hardening ability and strength, of which work hardening index is improved 
by 36 %, the yield and fracture strength are increased by 12.6 % and 40.8 % compared to FeCoNiV HEA, 
respectively. Additionally, the Fe
2
CoNiV
1.3 
alloy also has the best comprehensive mechanical properties. Its yield 
strength, ultimate tensile strength and elongation are 315.5 MPa, 724.2 MPa and 33.9 %, which is superior to the 
vast majority as-cast FCC structured HEAs. This study not only reduces the cost, improves the alloy ’ s strength and 
toughness, but also provides ideas for the composition design of new advanced HEAs. 
1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs), also known as multi-principal element 
alloys, were first proposed by Yeh in 2004 [ 1 ]. Different from traditional 
alloys, the design strategy of HEAs shifts the emphasis to the central area 
of multi-component phase diagram, and opens up more possibilities for 
alloy designing. Due to high entropy effect, slow diffusion effect, lattice 
distortion effect and cocktail effect [ 2 ], HEAs usually shows excellent 
comprehensive properties such as high strength, high hardness, wear 
resistance, corrosion resistance and high temperature resistance [ 3 – 7 ]. 
Early scholars mainly focused on HEAs with equal atomic ratio, which is 
great beneficial for maximizing the entropy of alloys to overwhelm the 
enthalpy of forming intermetallic compounds, thus tending to form 
simple solid solutions, such as CoCrFeNiMn, CoCrFeNiCu, TaNbHfZrTi, 
NbMoTaW, VNbMoTaW HEAs [ 8 – 12 ]. However, these kinds of alloys 
usually show great plastic elongation, but relatively low strength. 
Recent studies have found that the localized ordered structures and the 
uniformly distributed intermetallic compounds in HEAs can signifi -
cantly improve the mechanical properties [ 13 – 20 ]. 
It is known that L1
2

FCC solid solution [ 21 – 24 ]. In addition, the L1

cantly reduce the plasticity. Therefore, many researchers strengthen the 
alloy matrix by controlling the generation of L1

improving mechanical properties of the metallic alloys. Improving the 
work hardening ability of alloys through second phase precipitation is

motion of dislocations, and then improving the strength of the alloys.

FeCoNiV alloy exhibits good plasticity and fracture toughness at room

materials. Therefore, improving its strength becomes the key to solve 
this problem.

4. Discussion 
In this work, we put forward a new strategy to solve the insufficient 
strength of FCC structured HEAs at room temperature. The strategy is 
carried out in two ways: (1) By increasing the content of Fe element to 
reduce the stacking fault energy of alloy matrix, thereby improving its 
work hardening ability; (2) By adjusting the content of V element to 
control the quantity of L1
2
, σ and κ phases, thereby strengthening its 
matrix. The results show that the as-cast Fe
2
CoNiV
1.3

work-hardening ability, of which work hardening index is improved by 
36 % from 0.25 of contrasting FeCoNiV alloy to 0.34. Additionally, the

properties. Its yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and elongation 
are 315.5 MPa, 724.2 MPa and 33.9 %, which is superior to the vast 
majority as-cast FCC structured HEAs. Especially compared with FeCo -
NiV alloy, its tensile yield strength and fracture strength have increased

alloy solidifies, atoms in the melt preferentially stack at low-energy 
crystal plane such as the {111} plane of FCC. Therefore, the main di -

peak generally appears at {111} plane such as the XRD results of 
FeCoNiV. This indicates that during solidification process of FeCoNiV 
alloy, the crystal plane at the front of the solid-liquid interface is mainly 
{111} plane, and the < 111 > direction is preferred for growth. In the 
EBSD images of multiple grains, the grains with {111} plane should 
account for the majority as shown in Fig. 2 (c). However, when Fe con -

2
CoNiV alloy ap -

front of the solid-liquid interface changes from {111} to {200}, and 
< 200 > becomes the preferred growth direction. This indicates that as 
the Fe content increases, the crystal plane energy changes, and the 
{200} plane transitions from high-energy plane to low-energy plane. 
The variation of crystal plane energy indirectly reflects the change in 
stacking fault energy. Generally speaking, stacking faults in FCC alloys 
usually occur in {111} planes, as the energy of {111} plane is the lowest 
and stacking faults are more likely to occur compared to other planes. 
However, the increase of Fe content reduces the energy of the {200} 
plane, making it possible for stacking faults to occur in {200} planes as 
well, as shown in Fig. 10 (c) and (d). Thus, the alloy ’ s work hardening 
ability has been improved and enhanced.

sponding to V1.4 alloy, the main peak of X-ray diffraction returns to 
{111} plane again. This indicates that the influence of Fe and V elements 
on crystal plane energy is opposite, which may be related to the for -
mation of ordered L1
2
, κ and σ phases with increasing V content. It is 
known that the ordering process of alloy system is a process of energy 
reduction. 
5. Conclusion 
In this work, we put forward a new strategy to solve the problem of 
insufficient strength of FCC structured HEAs at room temperature. The 
non-equimolar Fe
2
CoNiV
x 
HEAs were designed. The design concept is by 
reducing the stacking fault energy of the matrix to improve its work 
hardening ability, and by controlling the precipitation of L1
2
, κ and σ 
phases to strengthen the matrix. The microstructure, crystal structure 
and mechanical properties of Fe
2
CoNiV
x 
HEAs were characterized, and 
the mechanism was discussed in detail. The main conclusions are as 
follows. 
1. The as-cast Fe
2
CoNiV
1.3 
alloy has excellent work-hardening ability, 
of which work hardening index is improved by 36 % from 0.25 of 
contrasting FeCoNiV alloy to 0.34. This alloy also shows best 
comprehensive mechanical properties and great potential for 
application. Its yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and elonga -
tion are 315.5 MPa, 724.2 MPa and 33.9 %, which is superior to the 
vast majority as-cast FCC structured HEAs. Especially compared with 
FeCoNiV alloy, its tensile yield strength and fracture strength have 
increased by 12.6 % and 40.8 %, respectively. 
2. V element is conducive to form local ordered L1
2
, precipitate κ and σ 
phases with the surrounding Fe, Co as well as Ni atoms in the alloy 
matrix. The κ and σ phases mainly precipitates at grain boundaries, 
which can significantly improve the strength, but will lead to a sig -
nificant decrease in the plasticity. However, the ordered L1
2 
struc -
ture mainly forms locally in the grains, and has a coherent 
relationship with the matrix. This can further improve the strength of 
the alloy without significantly reducing its plasticity. 
3. The increase of Fe content can effectively reduce the stacking fault 
energy and improve stacking fault density of the alloy system, 
thereby improve the work hardening ability of Fe
2
CoNiV
x 
HEAs. 
Double Fe content will cause the preferred growth direction of the 
alloy changing from {111} plane to {200} plane, while the influence 
of V element is opposite.
