ABSTRACT 
Conventional rare-earth magnesium alloys have high strength but are not accepted by some fields because of 
their relatively high price. Magnesium alloys with the addition of small amounts of rare earth elements can 
improve the mechanical properties by severe plastic deformation while saving cost. In this paper, the effects of 
multidirectional forging (MDF) process on the microstructure and mechanical properties of low-cost rare earth 
magnesium alloy Mg-7Gd-2Y – 1Zn-0.5Zr were investigated, and the plastic deformation mechanism, dynamic 
recrystallization mechanism and property strengthening mechanism during the MDF process were analyzed. The 
results showed that the yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and elongation of Mg-7Gd-2Y – 1Zn-0.5Zr alloy 
were 395.2 MPa, 435.3 MPa and 17.6% after extrusion, MDF at reduced temperature conditions and T5 treat -
ment. The increase in alloy strength is related to solid solution strengthening and is more attributed to the 
combined effect of grain refinement and aging precipitation. The dynamic recrystallization mechanism leading to 
grain refinement converts from discontinuous dynamic recrystallization to continuous dynamic recrystallization 
during MDF at reduced temperatures. And the decrease in deformation temperature leads to difficulties in 
prismatic and pyramidal slipping, where grain boundary sliding from fine grains and twinning in a few relatively 
coarse grains play a role in coordinating deformation. 
1. Introduction 
Because of the excellent specific strength and specific rigidity, 
magnesium alloys are expected to replace aluminum alloys and steels to 
achieve weight lighting [ 1 ]. However, certain load-bearing structural 
parts in aerospace, automotive and other fields have higher re -
quirements for the strength of magnesium alloys [ 2 ]. Adding rare earth 
(RE) elements to magnesium alloys can obtain relatively higher me -
chanical properties than conventional Mg – Al or Mg – Zn series alloys, 
which is one of the important ways to strengthen magnesium alloys [ 3 ]. 
Gd and Y have strong age hardening ability in magnesium alloys, 
making Mg – Gd and Mg – Y the most widely studied and used RE mag -
nesium alloys [ 4 ]. When the Gd content is about 10 wt%, a good 
enhancing effect can usually be obtained after aging [ 5 ]. On this basis, 
the addition of Y element can further strengthen the alloy [ 6 ]. However, 
the rare-earth element content of the currently reported high-strength 
Mg-Gd-Y alloys mostly exceeds 10 wt% [ 1 ], which causes the cost to 
be greatly increased [ 7 ]. In order to enable the price of high-strength 
magnesium alloys to be accepted by more fields, some scholars have 
focused on magnesium alloys with only a small amount of RE added, but 
the decrease in the content of RE will affect the mechanical properties of 
magnesium alloys [ 6 ]. 
Severe plastic deformation is an effective method to improve the 
properties of magnesium alloys. However, there are still few studies on 
the severe plastic deformation of magnesium alloys with a small amount 
of RE added. Chen et al. reported that the yield strength (YS) of Mg-8Gd- 
3Y-0.4Zr alloy increased to 249 MPa and the ultimate tensile strength 
(UTS) increased to 326 MPa after five passes of rolling [ 8 ]. Zhou et al. 
obtained a high-performance Mg-4.9Gd-3.2Y-1.1Zn-0.5Zr alloy through 
multi-directional forging, of which the YS is 331 MPa and the UTS is 390 
MPa [ 9 ]. In the current research, the strength of low-cost RE magnesium 
alloys still has the possibility of further improvement. 
Among the severe plastic deformation methods that can be used in 
magnesium alloys, the multi-directional forging process can produce 
large billets. In addition to refining grains and improving the strength 
and toughness of magnesium alloys, it also has a wide range of industrial 
applications [ 10 , 11 ].

After extrusion, the microstructure of Mg-7Gd-2Y – 1Zn-0.5Zr alloy

3.3. Changes in microstructure and properties during multi-directional 
forging 
The extruded Mg-7Gd-2Y – 1Zn-0.5Zr alloy shows a significant 
refinement of grain size during MDF: from 10 μ m in the extruded alloy to 
1.14 μ m. Fig. 5 shows the OM during MDF. The average grain size after 
the first forging pass is 7.56 μ m, the originally flat grain boundaries are 
bent and the LPSO phase is kinked. The region of the kinked LPSO phase 
provides nucleation sites for DRX, which can stimulate DRX and exert 
the pinning effect to inhibit the grain growth [ 18 ], as shown in Fig. 5 (a). 
Fig. 5 (b) shows that new grains formed by DRX can already be observed 
inside the twisted LPSO after the second pass (marked with triangle). 
The LPSO phase inside the matrix is gradually diffused, which reduces 
the anisotropy of the material and improves the second phase 
strengthening effect. The average grain size is 5.83 μ m. In the third to 
fifth pass, the average grain size is 2.37 μ m, 2.00 μ m and 1.46 μ m, 
respectively, as shown in Fig. 5 (c – e). The LPSO phase is severely broken 
and partially dissolved into the magnesium matrix ( Fig. 14 ), and no 
continuous LPSO phase can be observed in the original microstructure.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Plastic deformation mechanisms of the MDF process 
The deformation mechanisms involved in the MDF process under 
reduced temperature conditions are dislocation slip, twinning and grain 
boundary sliding. During the first three passes, a large number of 
microstructure pictures were observed and no twinning was found in -
side the grains of the magnesium matrix ( Fig. 9 (a)). At relatively high 
temperatures, there are sufficient active slip systems that do not require 
twinning for coordinated deformation. After forging at 345 
◦
C, tensile 
twins can be observed within the microstructure ( Fig. 9 (b)). A large 
number of twins form within the coarse grains ( Fig. 9 (c)) and even 
transferred ( Fig. 9 (d)) between grains with increasing passes. The acti -
vation of tensile twins causes a significant decrease in the Schmid factor 
of the prismatic slip system and a significant increase in the Schmid 
factor of the pyramidal slip system of magnesium alloys [ 21 ]. Although 
there is also a decrease in the prismatic slip Schmid factor at the 
beginning of the third pass in the MDF process, the change in prismatic 
slip is not significant ( Fig. 10 ). Because grain refinement to the micron 
level, the contribution of grain boundary sliding increases [ 22 , 23 ] and 
may even dominate the deformation [ 24 ], which inhibits twinning for -
mation. The activation of twinning within only a small number of coarse

4.2. DRX mechanism of the MDF process 
The refinement of grains during MDF of Mg-7Gd-2Y – 1Zn-0.5Zr alloy 
under reduced temperature conditions is achieved mainly by DRX. 
Under reduced temperature conditions, the growth rate of the grains 
decreases with the decline in the thermally-activated motion of atoms 
[ 26 ], which leads to a gradual decrease in the size of the DRXed grains. 
Because the deformation temperature is higher in the first few passes, 
the dislocations are activated easily. A large number of dislocations 
move to the edge of the grain boundary. Some of them are swallowed by 
the grain boundary, while some dislocations still pile up at the grain 
boundary. According to the theory of Bailey [ 27 ], dislocation accumu -
lation causes a dislocation density difference on both sides of the grain, 
resulting in grain boundary bulges (marked in red in Fig. 11 ). During the 
forging process at 420

tallization (DDRX) occurs mainly within the alloy, and the schematic

changes from DDRX to continuous dynamic recrystallization (CDRX)

4.3. Strengthening mechanisms of the MDF process 
The strengthening of the properties of the Mg-7Gd-2Y – 1Zn-0.5Zr 
alloy during MDF under reduced temperature conditions is attributed to 
the combined effect of several factors. In the first three passes of forging, 
the strength of the alloy not only did not increase but even weakened. 
This is because the temperature in the first few passes of the MDF was set 
higher than the extrusion temperature in order to obtain a larger cu -
mulative deformation and smaller grain size. The precipitated phases 
during extrusion impede the movement of dislocations and thus increase 
the strength, as shown in Fig. 13 (a). In the early stages of MDF, the effect 
of the second phase strengthening is greatly reduced due to the re- 
dissolution of the precipitated and LPSO phases at increasing tempera -
tures. However, due to the grain boundary strengthening resulting from 
the decreasing grain size, the strength decreases but is still higher than 
that of the original cast alloy. Fig. 14 shows the LPSO phase re- 
dissolution observed in the third pass. In passes four to seven, forging 
at lower temperatures caused the re-precipitation of a diffuse second 
phase within the matrix ( Fig. 13 (c)), which reached a peak after aging.

5. Conclusions 
The low-cost rare-earth magnesium alloy Mg-7Gd-2Y–1Zn-0.5Zr 
exhibits enhanced mechanical properties after the MDF process and 
subsequent ageing treatment. The YS, UTS and elongation of the final 
alloy were 395.2 MPa, 435.3 MPa and 17.6%, respectively. The grain 
size is significantly refined, reaching 1.14 μ m. 
(1) The strength enhancement of the low-cost rare-earth magnesium 
alloys after MDF is related to solid solution strengthening and 
more attributed to the combined effect of the grain refinement 
and aging precipitates. 
(2) During MDF under reduced temperature conditions, the DRX 
mechanism transforms from DDRX to CDRX. The reduced tem -
perature leads to the difficulty of prismatic and pyramidal slip -
ping. The grain boundary sliding coordinates the deformation of 
the fine grains, and twinning plays this role in a few relatively 
coarse grains.
