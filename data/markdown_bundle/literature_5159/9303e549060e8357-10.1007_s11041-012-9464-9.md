STRUCTURALLY INHOMOGENEOUS FCT-MARTENSITE IN ALLOYS
BASED ON THE Mn – Cu SYSTEM: STRUCTURE, STRAIN MECHANISM
AND STRENGTH CHARACTERISTICS
G . I. Nosova1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 3, pp. 11 – 15, March, 2012.
The conditions of occurrence of high-temperature fcc /c174fct martensitic transformation in alloys of the
Mn – Cu system containing two isomorphic nanocrystalline /c1031 and /c1032 phases with high and low manganese
content, respectively, are considered. The structure formed as a result is studied. The mechanisms of straining
of the inhomogeneous fct-martensite and the factors determining its strength properties are determined.
Key words: Mn – Cu alloys, martensitic transformation, strength properties.
INTRODUCTION
Alloys of the Mn – Cu system containing over 70 at.% Mn
undergo a transformation due to antiferromagnetic ordering
of the magnetic moments of the manganese atoms when
cooled from the high-temperature range. The transformation
is accompanied by lowering of the symmetry of the crystal
lattice; the initial fcc-cell becomes tetragonal at T < TN (TN is
the Néel temperature) and has the axes ratio c/c47a < 1 [1, 2].
Being antiferromagnetic in nature, the fcc /c174fct transforma-
tion in these alloys has features typical for a thermoelastic
martensitic transformation. In particular, the points of the
start and end of the transformation are well determinable; the
fcc /c174fct rearrangement of the crystal lattice develops by a
directed shift of specific crystallographic planes, which leads
to the appearance of texture on the surface of the lap; the
formed fct structure is internally twinned. This allows us to
treat the fcc /c174fct transition in alloys of the Mn – Cu system
and the structure formed as martensitic ones in the metallo -
graphic aspect.
In the present paper we describe the conditions of imple-
mentation of the fcc /c174fct transformation in alloys of the
Mn – Cu system containing two isomorphic fcc phases of
different compositions, the special features of the structure of
the martensite formed as a result, the mechanisms of its
straining, and the nature of the hardening. These data widen
the knowledge of special features of martensitic transforma-
tions in alloys with different structures and are interesting
from the standpoint of manifestation of a reversible shape
memory effect in Mn – Cu-based alloys; the characteristics
of this effect are determined both by the special features of
the martensitic transformation and the structure of the
fct-phase and by the mechanisms of plastic straining of the
latter and its strength characteristics. It should be noted that
we will deal not only with binary alloys but also with alloys
bearing low additives of Cr, Ni and Ge. This alloying does
not violate the general laws of phase and structural transfor -
mations in the alloys but may change somewhat their tem -
perature and kinetic parameters.

The rearrangement of an fcc lattice into an fct one in the
transformation develops by collective shift of crystallo -
graphic planes {110} in directions /c225110/c241. In this case planes
{110} are invariant. The strain in the transformation is 1 – 2%.
As a result of the transformation the alloy acquires a
twinned structure consisting of thin and coarse plate fct-do -
mains-twins, the thickness of which is about 1 and 10 /c109m,
respectively. The boundaries of the thin twins are parallel to
crystallographic planes {110}, and the tetragonal axes in
neighbor crystals are mutually perpendicular. The colonies of
thin twins with a common twinning plane form larger
(coarser) twins limited by planes {110} arranged at an angle
of 60° to the boundaries of the thin twins [5 – 7]. A scheme
of a fragment of such internally twinned fct structure with
two variants of coarse twins is given in Fig. 2.
The described concentration dependence of the tempera -
ture of martensitic transformation corresponds to the cases of
quenching of alloys from the range of homogeneous /c103-solid
solution. However, in accordance with the data of [8, 9] a do-
main of layering of the fcc solid solution into two metastable
isomorphic nanocrystalline (7 – 30 nm in size) phases ( /c1031
and /c1032 enriched with and depleted of manganese, respec -
tively) exists in a wide range of manganese concentrations
(about 35 – 95 at.%) at a temperature exceeding that of the
martensitic transformation (see also Fig. 1). The layering oc-
curs the most intensely at 400 – 500°C. In this temperature
range metastable equilibrium in alloys of the Mn – Cu sys-
tem and in alloys with low additives of Cr, Ni, Ge is attained
aftera2–5 - h hold.
As a result of metastable layering, the manganese con-
tent in the manganese-depleted /c1032-phase may become sub-
stantially lower than the concentrations at which martensitic
transformation is possible. For example, the structure of al-
loys containing from 40 to 70% Mn after a hold at 400°C re-
sulting in metastable equilibrium is represented by a fine dis-
persed mixture of two phases bearing 95 and 37% manga-
nese, respectively [4].3 In accordance with the phase diagram
(Fig. 1), a single-phase alloy with 37% Mn cannot undergo
martensitic fcc /c174fct transformation due to cooling to room
temperature. The possibility of the development of the trans-
formation in the volume of a heterogeneous ( /c1031 + /c1032 ) alloy
(i.e., in quenching after a hold at a temperature matching the
range of metastable layering) is determined by several factors.

Indeed, the studies of the magnetic and crystal structure
of two-phase Mn – Cu alloys of various compositions by
methods of x-ray and neutron diffraction analyses performed
in [11, 12] have shown that the tetragonal shift that starts in
cooling of a ( /c1031 + /c1032 ) alloy to T < Ms in manganese-rich re -
gions can propagate to regions /c1032 with a low manganese con-
centration at which the transformation does not occur in a
single-phase alloy. Such transition develops under the condi-
tions of high fineness of the phases and coherent bonding be-
tween the phases. Such a situation has been observed, for ex-
ample, in quenching of Mn – Cu alloys from the two-phase
(/c1031 + /c1032 ) range and in quenching of alloys with Cr, Ni and Ge
additives if the size of the particles of the /c1032-phase in them
did not exceed 10 – 15 nm [11, 12]. In this case the x-ray
diffractograms of the alloys bear a single system of reflec-
tions from the tetragonal lattice and exhibit no reflection
from the cubic phase. Analysis of the scattering of the x-rays
has shown that the formed fct structure is modulated with re-
spect to parameter c and the period of the modulation corre -
sponds to the size of the dispersed phases. This means that
the regions reach with and depleted of manganese possess
close parameters a and periodically changing [from (1 + /c101)t o
(1 – /c101)] parameters c (/c101is the deviation from the value of c
for an average lattice). Such martensite with explicitly mani-
fested modulation of parameter c will be called structurally
inhomogeneous one.
It should be noted that in the cases of implementation of
fcc /c174fct transformation in the whole of the volume of a he -
terogeneous (/c1031 + /c1032 ) alloy the behavior of the concentration
dependence of the temperature of this transformation
changes with respect to that described for homogeneous al -
loys (see Fig. 1).
Upon coarsening of the structure, which disturbs the co -
herence between the isomorphic /c1031 and /c1032 phases, the tetra -
gonal shift in cooling of the alloy to a temperature below Ms
occurs only in manganese-rich microvolumes and does not
propagate to manganese-depleted zones. For example, in a
Cu – Mn alloy with an additive of Ge, which contains /c1032-par-
ticles 11.5 nm in size after a hold at 450°C, quenching yields
an fct-inhomogeneous martensitic structure in the whole of
the volume, whereas in the same alloy quenched after a lon-
ger treatment in the two-phase range, which increases the
size of the /c1032-phase to 20 nm, the fcc /c174fct transformation
during develops during quenching only in the manganese-en-
riched zones, and the manganese-depleted zones preserve an
fcc structure. Then the x-ray diffractograms of the alloy ex-
hibit well manifested reflections from two phases with fcc
and fct crystal lattices [12].
Plastic straining of inhomogeneous tetragonal martensite
with internally twinned structure may develop by two me -
chanisms, namely, by twinning in systems {110}/c225110/c241and by
octahedral slip in systems {111} /c225110/c241[13 – 15].4 The possi-
bility of implementation of the first mechanism is a result of
the high mobility of twin boundaries typical for an internally
twinned structure; their motion, which causes reorientation
of martensite crystals, may occur upon application of quite
low external stresses [13]. The value of the strain due to
twinning depends on the degree of tetragonality of the lattice
of the fct martensite and on the direction of the application of
the load. The highest possible strain due to the action of this
mechanism amounts to (1 – c/c47a).

Figure 4 shows the variation of the yield strength for an
alloy with 82% Mn and a structure of inhomogeneous

Let us consider the possible factors of strengthening of
an alloy in different structural states. It is shown in [4], where
the authors determine the lattice parameters of a number of
alloys of the Mn – Cu system in the state of metastable
(/c1031 + /c1032 ) equilibrium, that the lattice parameters of phases /c1031
and /c1032 are virtually equal above the points of martensitic
transformation, i.e., in the two-phase ( /c1031 + /c1032 ) state the
stresses in the fcc lattice determined by the coherence be -
tween these two phases should be virtually absent. Then, in
accordance with the data of [16], the work performed due to
cutting of /c1032-particles isomorphic to the matrix on an area of
1c m2 is determined in the case of conservative motion of
dislocations in the shear plane as /c116s b = /c115s, where /c116s is the
tangential shear stress, b is the Burgers vector of the disloca-
tion, s is the total area of the steps formed due to cutting of
particles by the dislocation, and /c115is the surface energy of
these steps.
The values of /c116s computed by the formula presented
above with the use of the thermodynamic and structural cha -
racteristics known for alloys of the Mn – Cu system [17]
have turned out to be equal to several kg /c47mm2, i.e., match
the corresponding experimental data. Thus, we may state that
the resistance to the motion of dislocations when they cut
segregations of the second phase is the main factor determin-
ing the strengthening of an alloy in state (/c1031 + /c1032 ).
The picture is different for alloys with inhomogeneous
martensite. According to the data of [4], in this case the /c1031
and /c1032 phases that are in equilibrium at room temperature
have substantially different (by almost 1%) lattice parame -
ters. In the case of coherent bonding between the phases this
difference in the lattice parameters should lead to the appear-
ance of elastic stresses that lower the degree of tetragonality
of phase /c1031 (it is extended over axis c and compressed over
axes a) and increase the tetragonal strain of phase /c1032. The
arising fields of elastic stresses in plastic deformation of the
alloy should affect the moving dislocations.
In principle, it is possible to evaluate the forces acting of
a dislocation on the side of fields of elastic stresses and, ac-
cordingly, the shear stresses in the slip plane, using the
method of Mott and Nabarro [18]. To evaluate approximately
the value of the stresses applied from the outside and com-
pensating the internal stresses, which means, in accordance
with [18], the attainment of the yield point, we use the data
on Young’s modulus; for alloys of the Mn – Cu system it
amounts to about 60 GPa. Then, at a difference in the degree
of tetragonality in the stressed and unstressed phases
amounting about 1%, the computed yield strength of an alloy
should be 600 MPa, which is quite close to the experimen -
tally determined value at room temperature. We may thus
state that the stresses arising upon formation of inhomo -
geneous (with modulation of the period) fcc martensite make
a considerable contribution into strengthening of the alloys,
and growth in the modulation of the period is accompanied
by increase in the degree of the strengthening.
CONCLUSIONS
Occurrence of martensitic fcc /c174fct transformation in
the bulk of alloys of the Mn – Cu system containing two iso-
morphic nanocrystalline phases with higher and lower con -
tents of manganese (at the lower content the transformation
cannot develop in a single-phase alloy) yields a single
tetragonal structure modulated with respect to the lattice pa -
rameter c. The possibility of propagation of the transforma -
tion that starts in regions with high content of manganese to
regions with its low content is determined by the favorable
