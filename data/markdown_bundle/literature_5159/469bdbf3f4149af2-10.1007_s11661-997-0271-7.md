Creep and Low-Cycle Fatigue Behavior of Ferritic Fe-24Cr-
4Al Alloy in the Dynamic Strain Aging Regime: Effect of
Aluminum Addition
S.C. TJONG and S.M. ZHU
Creep and low-cycle fatigue behavior of ferritic Fe-24Cr-4Al alloy was studied in the temperature
range of 673 to 873 K, where dynamic strain aging (DSA) occurrence was found. The DSA of the
alloy manifested in the form of serrated ¯ow, negative strain rate sensitivity, and the peak or plateau
in the variations of yield strength (YS) and ultimate tensile strength (UTS) with temperature. The
characteristic creep behavior of the alloy was experimentally veri®ed as that for a class I solid
solution. However, this ferritic alloy showed an anomalous high stress exponent (n 5 5.7) and high
activation energy ( Q
c 5 285 kJ/mol) of the secondary creep, which were commonly exhibited by
class II solid solutions. During cyclic deformation, the alloy displayed serration in the stress-strain
hysteresis loops, increased cyclic hardening, and enhanced planarity of dislocations. On the basis of
the observed experimental results and proper analysis, it was proposed that there was strong elastic
interaction between solute aluminum atoms and dislocations in the DSA temperature domain. The
anomalous creep and fatigue features were interpreted in terms of the interaction of aluminum with
the dislocations.
I. INTRODUCTION
FERRITIC stainless steels, containing between 11.5 and
26 pct, are increasingly being used in elevated temperature
applications because of their low raw materials cost, su-
perior oxidation resistance, and low thermal expansion co-
ef®cient.[1] It is generally known that the addition of
aluminum to Fe-Cr alloys has bene®cial effects for resis-
tance to oxidizing and sul®dizing gases. On the other hand,
aluminum addition ( .8 wt pct) to iron-based alloys may
also lead to the occurrence of high-temperature embrittle-
ment. The aluminum content required in Fe-Cr alloys for
satisfactory corrosion resistance and mechanical properties
has been found to be around 2 to 6 wt pct.
[2,3,4] The Al-
bearing ferritic stainless steels have been used as materials
for automotive exhaust systems, nuclear reactors, power
plants, and petroleum re®neries.
[5] The use of the Fe-Cr-Al
alloys at high temperatures requires a comprehensive un-
derstanding of their deformation behavior under monotonic
and cyclic loading conditions.
The plastic behavior of a bcc Fe single crystal and Fe-
base alloys deformed monotonically or cyclically is char-
acterized by the low-temperature deformation mode, i.e.,
strong temperature dependence of the yield strength (YS)
stress asymmetry, and shape changes.
[6,7,8] These effects are
a result of slip asymmetry in the bcc lattice, i.e., dislocation
glide occurs on different systems in tension and compres-
sion, which is further related to relative lower mobility of
screw dislocations.
[7,8] The glide properties of dislocations
in bcc Fe single crystals are sensitive to low strain rates,
high temperatures, and the presence of interstitial ele-
ments.
[9] Lower strain rate or higher temperatures tend to

IV. DISCUSSION
A. Anomalous Creep Behavior
The creep test results show that the alloy exhibits anom-
alous creep behavior in the DSA regime. The primary
creep, transient creep, and dislocation structure exhibit typ-
ical class I characteristics, while the stress and temperature
dependence of the secondary creep rate are almost similar
to those of class II alloys.
It is well known that class I creep is governed by the
viscous glide motion of dislocations dragging the solute
atmosphere. The class I creep behavior is usually observed
in solid solution alloys having a large size difference be-
tween the solute and solvent atoms, which leads to a strong
interaction between the solute atoms and the dislocations.
[24]
The present alloy can be considered as a class I alloy, be-
cause the difference in the atomic size between Al and Fe
is comparable to that of typical class I alloys such as Al-
Mg and Fe-Mo. Thus, the discussion will focus on the in-

C. Mechanism of DSA: Effect of Aluminum Addition
In a certain range of temperatures and strain rates, both
interstitial and substitutional solid solutions may exhibit
serration in the stress-strain curve, and this phenomenon is
generally known as serrated yielding or the Portevin±Le
Chatelier effect.
[34±39] In Fe-Cr-Ni austenitic stainless steels
and Ni-base superalloys, the most widely accepted mech-
anism for the serrated yielding is DSA due to diffusion of
solute atoms to dislocations during the test. At temperatures
below 673 K, it has been proposed that the carbon-vacancy
pairs and atmosphere of carbon atoms are interacting with
glide dislocations. With increasing temperature, chromium
atoms become more mobile and contribute to the disloca-
tion-solute interaction in the temperature range of 673 to
873 K. At temperatures above 923 K, DSA has been as-
cribed to the pinning effect exerted by the deformation-
assisted precipitation of ®ne carbides on glide dislocations.
In the present study, the unlocking type C serration,
which is considered to be due to the interaction between
the substitutional alloying element atmosphere and dislo-
cations, has been observed in both the tensile and low-cycle
fatigue tests. A rough estimation of the applied stress,

V. CONCLUSIONS
Creep and low-cycle fatigue behavior of ferritic Fe-24Cr-
4Al alloy were studied in the DSA domain regime, with
emphasis on the effect of aluminum addition and its inter-
action with the dislocations. The following conclusions
were drawn.
1. The DSA was observed in the tensile tests in the tem-
perature range of 573 to 873 K. The DSA manifested in
the form of serrated ¯ow, negative strain-rate sensitivity,
and the plateaus in the variations of YS and UTS with
temperature.
2. The characteristic creep behavior of the alloy was ex-
perimentally veri®ed as that for a class I solid solution.
However, this particular alloy showed anomalous high
values of stress exponent ( n 5 5.7) and activation en-
ergy (Q
c 5 285 kJ/mol) of the secondary creep rate.
3. The DSA played an important role in cyclic deformation
behavior of the alloy. During cyclic deformation, the
alloy displayed serration in the cyclic stress-strain hys-
teresis loops, increased cyclic hardening, and enhanced
planarity of dislocations.
4. There was strong elastic interaction between the solute
aluminum atoms and dislocations for the alloy in the
DSA temperature domain. The interaction of the alu-
minum atoms with dislocations accounted for the ob-
served anomalous creep and fatigue features.
