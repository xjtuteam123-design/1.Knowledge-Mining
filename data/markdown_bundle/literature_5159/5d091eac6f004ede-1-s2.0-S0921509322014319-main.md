ABSTRACT 
In this study, microstructure evolution, tensile and fracture behaviors of Ti – 22Al – 24Nb-0.5Mo alloy processed 
under different heat treatment processing routes were investigated systematically. The results revealed the ex -
istence of bimodal-size microstructures of the primary O lamellae and the secondary acicular O phase. The 
strength and ductility of alloy specimens were optimized by accommodating the volume fractions and mor -
phologies of the precipitates. With the increase of the solution temperature, more acicular O phase precipitated 
out, which led to the increase of the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength. It was observed that the acicular 
O phase led to the increase of the strength at elevated temperature without sacrificing plasticity. Owing to the 
improvement of microscopic strain homogeneity under coupled actions of stress and high temperature, the 
tensile fracture mode changed from quasi-cleavage to ductile transgranular creep fracture. The fracture tough -
ness of this alloy was determined by the B2 phase and the acicular O phase. It was evaluated that the fracture 
toughness reduced with a higher volume fraction of the acicular O phase, which in turn affected the contiguity of 
the B2 matrix. Considering this, balanced strength and toughness were achieved by tailoring the relative contents 
of the B2 and acicular O phase. 
1. Introduction 
Intermetallic compounds have received significant research atten -
tion during the last few years because they offer excellent material 
characteristics with low density and high-temperature strength. Pres -
ently, the applications of intermetallic compounds are common in 
aircraft and aerospace industries [ 1 – 3 ]. Notably, Ti
3
Al intermetallics are 
one of the promising candidate materials for such applications; none -
theless, they lack ductility and fracture toughness at room temperature 
[ 4 – 7 ]. However, the published studies have shown that by introducing a 
certain amount of Nb into the Ti
3
Al-based alloys, the ordered ortho -
rhombic phase (O phase) of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys can be developed in 
the alloy, which offers excellent ductility and fracture toughness [ 8 – 12 ]. 
Thus, Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys are promising candidate materials to replace 
the conventional Ti
3
Al-based and Ni-based high-temperature alloys in 
some application fields at elevated temperatures. 
As well known that Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys generally contain of three 
phases, they are body centered B2/ β phase, close-packed hexagonal α
2 
phase, and orthorhombic O phase [ 11 , 12 ]. Among these phases, B2/ β 
phase is the base phase and the main contributor to plastic deformations, 
and O phase is regarded as the main strengthening phase, providing 
higher strength and creep properties of the Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys [ 13 ]. 
As for α
2 
phase, which is also the strengthening phase but with the poor 
deformation compatibilities with B2/ β phase, however, it plays the 
important role of suppressing the growth of B2 phase, thus refining the 
grain of B2 phase [ 14 ]. Notably, the tensile properties of Ti
2
AlNb-based 
alloys are sensitive to their microstructures at a given temperature [ 15 ]. 
Recently, Zhao et al. investigated the tensile and fracture behavior of 
Ti – 22Al – 24Nb-0.5Mo alloy from 25 to 650
◦

developed through heat treatment processes [ 16 ]. The study revealed 
that the largest elongation was produced by the α
2
+ B2 microstructure, 
and an excellent balance between strength and plasticity was produced 
by the O/ α
2
+ B2 microstructure. Moreover, some studies have also 
explained that the grain size of the B2 phase, morphologies, and volume

IC

The microstructure and crack propagation paths were examined by

fact, solution temperature not only affects the sizes, distribution and 
volume fractions of the precipitates, but also dominates the supersatu -

strong strengthener element of Al in the B2 matrix got enriched but the 
weak strengthener element of Nb in the B2 matrix got diluted for 
Ti

increase of the solution temperature, the higher Al composition results 
in a stronger solution strengthening effect in the B2 matrix. It was 
assumed that the lattice distortion caused by the solution strengthening 
increased the Peierls – Nabarro stress and increased the motion activation 
energy of dislocations, which result in the enhancement of the YS. As 
well known that yielding of a multiphase microstructure starts from the 
weak phase which has the lowest YS, thus the supersaturated B2 solid 
solution with the lowest YS in Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys control the initial 
yielding behavior during the tensile process. Therefore, the higher Al 
enriched in B2 matrix results in the higher degree of supersaturation, 
which leading to the higher YS with the increase of solution 
temperature. 
On the other hand, once the acicular O phases were precipitated in 
the supersaturated B2 solid solution, a great number of phase bound -
aries would be formed. These phase boundaries would impede the 
movement of activated dislocations, which play the significant role of 
precipitation strengthening. Different with the solution strengthening 
effect which dominates the YS, the precipitation strengthening de -
termines the subsequent UTS. Thus, with the increase of the volume 
fraction of acicular O phases, the UTS of alloys would be enhanced 
significantly but sacrificing certain plasticity. The highly positive cor -
relations between UTS and volume fraction of acicular O phases was 
depicted in Fig. 6 , which further suggested that the UTS was dominated 
by secondary acicular O phases. The variations of elongation and 
reduction of the area, showing the linear decreasing trends with the 
increase of solution temperature, as shown in Fig. 5 (b). In comparison 
with the HT-2 treated sample, the YS and UTS of the HT-4 treated sample 
decreased by 10% and 6%, but the elongation and area reduction 
increased by 25% and 36%. This phenomenon can be attributed to the 
weaken of solution strengthening effect of B2 phase and certain coarsen 
of acicular O phases.

In general, with tensile temperature increasing, the thermal acti -
vated processes which can effectively promote the movements of dis -
locations and the coordination deformation ability between the grain 
deformation was also enhanced, which result in the reduce of strength 
but remarkably improvement of ductility. Specifically, the temperature 
sensitivity of the yield strength is related to the Peierls-Nabarro stress. 
Thus, with the increasing of testing temperature, the yield strengths of 
alloys decreased by approximately 30% in comparison with at room 
temperature. Instead, the elongations and area reductions increased 
significantly at high temperature because of more slip systems were 
activated of O phase. It was reported that slip on the pyramidal system 
[114] or [102], (221) is also evident, and an extensive amount of cross 
slip has also occurred for “ c ” component dislocations at high tempera -
ture, thus, a greater number of independent slip systems are activated 
for O phase [ 32 ].

Abovementioned phenomena can actually be attributed to the fact 
that the toughness of metals depends on the amount of plastic defor -
mation accompanied with extension of cracks. A general rule is that the 
toughness is proportional to the plasticity ability of metals and alloys. 
Ritchie et al. [ 18 ] have proposed the classical theory in which the 
fracture toughness of the metals is actually determined by two factors, 
one is the intrinsic contribution which is related to the inherent property 
of the materials, and the other is the extrinsic contribution which is 
associated with the trajectory of crack propagations. The intrinsic 
damage mechanisms involve the processes which create microcracks 
and voids at the crack tip. Thus, one of the effective intrinsic toughening 
mechanisms involves the elevation of the plasticity of alloys, which can 
blunt the crack tip and lessen the local high-stress concentrations. On the 
other hand, the extrinsic crack shielding mechanism is related to the 
crack path tortuosity, such as crack deflection and meandering, which 
mainly operated primarily in the wake of the crack tip to inhibit the 
growth of a crack by shielding it from the applied crack-driving force 
from both crack deflection and asperity contact between the mating 
crack surfaces. Thus, alloys with the higher tortuosity of the crack path, 
higher extrinsic fracture resistance can be achieved. Hereinafter, the 
fractographies of T
2

fracture mechanisms and the corresponding strategies were proposed to 
elevate the resistance to fracture for Ti

AlNb-based alloys.

to the transgranular fracture. Moreover, some river-like patterns and 
stair-like morphologies were observed on the cleavage facets. Further 
carefully inspections at this macroscopic scale revealed that greater 
degrees of crack deflections, leading to more undulated fracture surfaces 
have occurred for Ti

such as HT1-and HT4-treated alloys. Thus, it can be inferred that crack 
deflection or reorientation as the crack propagations need consume 
more fracture energy due to the increasing of fracture areas that must be 
traversed, which contributes to the enhancement of fracture toughness.

are often associated with the second-phase particles that fracture at the 
elastic – plastic zone boundary [ 35 ]. For Ti

AlNb-based alloys, the stress

stress and from that coming from geometrical dislocations which 
generated by the requirement of material contiguity across the O – B2 
interface. This is because both stresses are expected to be most condu -
cive to particle fracture at the elastic-plastic zone boundary [ 35 ]. Thus, 
for HT3-treated sample with higher density of second-phase particle 
(such as acicular O phase) and small plastic-zone size, O/B2 boundaries

resistance of the crack propagation, which promote crack growth by 
merging with the principal crack. But for the HT4-treated alloys with

zone and the linkup stage is in a manner of a stepwise progression. As a 
result, the linkup across the zone is occupied by the fibrous fracture of 
the B2 matrix in the microscopic scale, and a higher level of fracture 
toughness can be achieved.

crack more effectively, which impedes the merging of the principal 
crack and the microcracks. The tearing ridges formed on the fracture 
surface, as shown in Fig. 9 (b) are the results when the microcracks are 
blunted. However, microcracks are easily developed at O/B2 boundaries 
due to the slip incompatibility, which promote crack growth by merging 
with the principal crack. 
4. Conclusions 
In this study, the effects of microstructure evolution in Mo-modified 
Ti
2
AlNb alloy were investigated systematically. The study focused on 
the effect of solution and aging treatments on tensile properties at room 
and elevated temperatures, and fracture toughness of alloys. The main 
conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(1) With the increase of solution temperature, the O lamellae got 
transformed to the rim O + α
2 
structure, and a greater number of 
acicular O phases precipitated during aging treatment. Thus, the 
desired mechanical properties were achieved in the HT2 scheme 
for Ti – 22Al – 24Nb-0.5Mo alloy with a bimodal microstructure. 
(2) Tensile properties either at room or elevated temperature are 
sensitive to the microstructures of Ti – 22Al – 24Nb-0.5Mo alloy. It 
was shown that the YS and UTS at room temperature exhibited a 
positive linear correlation, while the elongation and area reduc -
tion showed a negatively linear correlation with the solution 
temperature. The acicular O phase is desirable to elevate the high 
temperature tensile strength but without significant sacrifice the 
plasticity. The roles of typical phases on tensile properties for 
Ti – 22Al – 24Nb-0.5Mo alloy were clarified. 
(3) In contrast to the room temperature fracture surface that 
exhibited the quasi-cleavage features, ductile fracture features of 
transgranular creep fracture characterized with larger area 
reduction emerged at high temperature for Ti – 22Al – 24Nb-0.5Mo 
alloy, which can be attributed to the enhancement of the homo -
geneity of microscopic strains under coupled actions of stress and 
high temperature.
