ABSTRACT 
In this study, a high – performance Ti(C,N)-based cermet was prepared by vacuum hot-press sintering technology 
using Ti(C,N) as the hard phase material, WC, TaC and Mo
2
C as the second-phase carbides, Al
0.3
CoCrFeNi high- 
entropy alloy (HEA) as the metal binding phase and ZrO
2 
whiskers as the reinforcement phase. The effects of 
whiskers and the HEA on the room-temperature and high-temperature properties of cermets were studied, 
including oxidation resistance, hardness, bending strength, fracture toughness and frictional wear properties. The 
research showed that the high-temperature properties of cermets were improved by ZrO
2 
whiskers and HEAs 
compared to those of conventional cermets with Ni/Co as the binding phase. The mechanisms for the weakening 
of cermets at high temperatures were grain boundary softening and material oxidation. Moreover, the high- 
temperature strengthening mechanism was attributed to the strength of the grain boundaries in the cermet, 
high – temperature hardness and oxidation resistance of the HEA binding phase, as well as the strengthening of 
the ZrO
2 
whiskers at high temperatures. The results proved that the wear mechanism of cermet materials was 
adhesive wear at high temperatures. 
1. Introduction 
High-speed cutting technology has the advantages of high efficiency, 
high quality, low consumption and environmental protection. It is 
widely used in industries such as the national defence, military, aero -
space, automobile and shipping industries [ 1 , 2 ]. As an efficient cutting 
tool, Ti(C,N)-based cermets have excellent red hardness, thermal sta -
bility and wear resistance. Moreover, compared with those of cemented 
carbide, the cutting efficiency and service life are nearly 3 and 4 times 
better, respectively. This provides significant advantages in high-speed 
finishing and semi-finishing applications for hardened steel and stain -
less steel. However, it is difficult to simultaneously realize good heat 
resistance and toughness of Ti(C,N)-based cermets. When cutting 
stainless steel and other difficult-to-machine materials, the high tem -
perature easily leads to deformation and creep of the binding phase of 
the cermet. This not only reduces the production efficiency but also 
affects the stability of the tool and the surface quality of the workpiece 
[ 3 , 4 ]. 
To improve the heat resistance, researchers have conducted many 
studies on Ti(C,N)-based cermets, mainly including the following as -
pects: (1) Reducing the content of the conventional Ni/Co binding phase 
decreases the bending strength and fracture toughness of cermets [ 5 , 6 ]. 
(2) Adding rare earth elements, such as Y, Ce and La, improves the 
interface binding strength of the Ni/Co binding phase and Ti(C,N) par -
ticles, but the effect of heat resistance enhancement is not obvious [ 7 , 8 ]. 
(3) An intermetallic compound with good high-temperature properties 
is selected as the binding phase. However, the strength and toughness of 
cermets are low due to the brittleness of intermetallic compounds 
[ 9 , 10 ]. and (4) High-entropy alloys with high creep resistance, good 
toughness, high plasticity, good wetting behaviour on Ti(C,N) sub -
strates, and excellent physical compatibility are ideal potential materials 
for the binding phase [ 11 – 13 ]. Wang [ 11 ] prepared a Ti(C,N)-based 
cermet with a high-entropy alloy as the binding phase. The wear rate 
of the Ti(C,N)-CoCrFeNiCu cermet at 800 
◦

2. Experimental materials and methods 
2.1. Sample preparation 
In this study, Ti(C,N), WC, TaC, Mo
2
C, Ni, and Co were purchased 
(Shanghai Shuitian Technology Co., Ltd., 99% pure, 1 μ m), ZrO
2 
whis -
kers were prepared by the research group (approximately 600 – 1000 nm 
in length by 50 – 100 nm in width and a length to diameter ratio greater 
than 5) and Al
0.3
CoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy was obtained (Kairui New 
Material (Beijing) Technology Co., Ltd., 99% pure, 100 μ m). A com -
parison of the specific experimental compositions is shown in Table 1 . 
The process to prepare cermet was as follows. First, the weighed and 
marked materials were poured into a wide-mouth plastic bottle filled 
with absolute ethanol. The ratio of raw materials to grinding balls 
(spherical cemented carbide, YG-8) was 1:10, the rotating speed was 
300 r/min, and the time was 24 h. The TA and Z7.5A specimens were 
mixed with argon as the shielding gas to prevent the oxidation of the 
high-entropy alloy powder. Next, the above dispersed and finely mixed 
slurry was put into a drying oven (DHG-9070A, Ningbo Jiangnan In -
strument Factory, China) at 90 
◦
C for 4 h. The dried and cooled powder 
was passed through two sets of 60- and 100-mesh sieves in a mechanical 
vibrating device. Finally, the sieved powder was loaded into graphite 
moulds and moved into a vacuum sintering furnace with sintering 
pressure 8 MPa, temperature 1400 
◦
C and a duration of 1 h. This sin -
tering process was selected for the optimal room temperature perfor -
mance of the four groups of samples [ 15 , 16 ]. After the temperature 
decreased to room temperature, a sintered cylindrical sample with a 
diameter of 30 mm and a height of 5 mm was obtained. Additionally, the 
completed sintered specimen blocks were later processed by wire cut -
ting (BM400C-CT, Suzhou Baoma CNC Equipment Co., Ltd., China) and 
ground with a diamond disk into specimens according to the test 
requirements. 
2.2. Performance testing 
The actual density was measured by the Archimedes drainage 
method at room temperature, and the relative density was obtained by 
dividing the actual density by the theoretical density. In addition, the 
porosity of the polished surface was analysed using an optical light 
microscope (Lab. A1, ZEISS, Germany) and calculated by quantitative 
metallographic analysis (ProImaging, ZEISS, Germany). The bending 
strength and fracture toughness of the cermet at different temperatures 
were evaluated by a universal testing machine (INSTRON 8801, Instron, 
USA). Because temperatures in the range of 200 – 400 
◦
C were too low, 
tests were not performed. The bending strength was evaluated by the 
three-point bending method, and the specimen size was3*4*35 mm. The 
fracture toughness was measured by the single edge-notch beam method 
[ 17 ], and the specimen size was 2*3*35 mm. The hardness at different 
high temperatures was evaluated using a hardness tester (HVT-1000, 
Lanzhou Zhongke Kaihua Technology Co., Ltd., China) with a load of 
196 N and a holding time of 15 s. The mass gain curve was obtained by 
an isothermal oxidation experiment. First, the specimen was put into a 
chamber electric furnace (YFX5/16Q-YC, Shanghai Yifeng Electric 
Furnace Co., Ltd., China) for 4 h at a moderate temperature of 1000 
◦
C, 
and three samples were tested in each set. Then, the weight gain of the 
oxidized specimen after cooling was determined. The oxidation resis -
tance was determined by the oxidation mass gain per unit. High- 
temperature vacuum friction and wear tests were conducted with a 
friction and wear tester (GHT-1000E, Lanzhou Zhongke Kaihua Tech -
nology Co., Ltd., China), with a test temperature of 900 
◦
C, a rotating 
speed of 200 rpm, a load of 4.9 N, a sliding time of 10 min, and a YG-8 
friction pair. 
A scanning electron microscopy (SEM) system (S-4800, Hitachi, 
Japan) was used to observe the microstructure, including the surface, 
fracture and propagation of surface indentation cracks. Energy disper -
sive spectroscopy (EDS, Link ISIS, Oxford, U.K.) determined the 
elemental composition, atomic percentage, and distribution of different 
elements in each microstructure region. The phase structure of the 
powder and cermet was investigated with X-ray diffraction (XRD, D2, λ 
= 0.15406 nm, Bruker, Germany). The oxidation weight gain curve of 
the sample was obtained by a comprehensive thermal analyser (TG-DSC, 
STA 449f3, Netzsch, Germany).

the dislocation slip system within the material [ 16 ]. When the temper -

strength decreases, facilitating dislocation slip. Related studies [ 30 – 32 ] 
have shown that the dislocation density increases in the early stage of 
deformation, especially in the region of the substrate/reinforcement 
interface. However, for multielement alloys (including HEA metal), the 
stacking fault energy is low, which is beneficial to the formation of twin 
crystals. Additionally, the lower stacking fault energy can effectively 
prevent dislocation slip and form dislocation pileups. Dislocation 
pileups are usually located at the grain boundary or particle region, 
which is conducive to crystal twinning. The interaction between dislo -
cations and twin crystals further enhances the hardness of the material. 
Moreover, the dislocation pileup leads to dislocations with higher 
stacking fault energy, which hinders their movement in the dislocation- 
prone slip plane and strengthens the hardness of materials. In this study, 
compared with that of the Ni/Co binding phase, the stacking fault en -
ergy of HEA was relatively low, which facilitated the formation of twin 
crystals. The interactions between crystal twins and dislocations

4. Conclusions 
This paper describes the study of the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of a Ti(C,N)-based cermet with Ni/Co and HEA as the binding 
phase and ZrO
2 
whiskers as the reinforcement phase, and the following 
conclusions are drawn. 
(1) There was no chemical reaction between the two groups of 
cermet specimens during sintering. The t-ZrO
2 
diffraction peak of Z7.5A 
was weaker than that of Z7.5. The microstructure showed that the core- 
rim structures of TA and Z7.5A with HEA as the binding phase were 
incomplete, and the adjacent core-rim structures overlapped with un -
clear boundaries. In addition, the Cr in Z7.5A specimens was present not 
only in the high-entropy alloy but also in the annular phase in the form 
of carbide through the processes of dissolution – precipitation and solid 
solution diffusion. 
(2) In the high-temperature test environment at 1000 
◦
C, Z7.5A had 
the best overall properties; its hardness, bending strength, fracture 
toughness and elastic modulus reached 10.78 GPa, 913 MPa, 6.57 MPa

binding phase and the high-temperature property of ZrO

synergistically improved the high-temperature oxidation resistance of 
cermets. The factors responsible for decreasing the high-temperature 
properties of the two groups of cermets included grain boundary soft -
ening and material oxidation. The high grain boundary strength of 
cermets, the high-temperature hardness and oxidation resistance of the 
HEA binding phase, and the high-temperature synergistic strengthening 
of ZrO
