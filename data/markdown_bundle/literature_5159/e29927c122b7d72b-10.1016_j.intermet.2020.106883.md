ABSTRACT 
In order to improve high-temperature mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys, Ho element 
was added into Ti46Al4Nb1Mo alloy. The microstructure, high-temperature tensile property and oxidation 
behavior of Ti46Al4Nb1Mo alloy with different Ho content (0, 0.15%, 0.3%, atomic percent, hereafter in at.%) 
were investigated in this study. The results showed that Ho
2
O
3 
particles with square and rod-like shape formed 
by internal oxidation and the α
2
/ γ lamellar spacing was refined with 0.15% Ho addition. When Ho content was 
0.3%, irregular and large Al
2
Ho phases appeared at grain boundaries. Compared with Ti46Al4Nb1Mo alloy, the 
high-temperature tensile strength was improved with 0.15% Ho and decreased with 0.3% Ho. The improvement 
of the strength resulted from the Ho
2
O
3 
second phase strengthening, the decreasing of the strength was caused by 
the formation of large irregular Al
2
Ho phase. Oxidation testing results indicated that 0.15% Ho improved 
oxidation resistance of Ti46Al4Nb1Mo alloy, which was due to suppression of inward diffusion of oxygen and 
better adhesion between oxide film and matrix. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl alloys have many excellent mechanical properties such as low 
density and high creep property, and there are many advanced methods 
to prepare TiAl alloys [ 1 – 3 ]. The applications of TiAl alloys are in 
automobile and aerospace field such as aircraft engine, turbines and car 
valves, and they are regarded as promising candidates instead of nickel 
based alloys [ 4 – 7 ]. However, insufficient high-temperature strength and 
oxidation resistance restrict the wide applications of TiAl alloys in 
high-temperature field [ 8 , 9 ]. 
Rare earth elements have great effects on improving the mechanical 
properties and oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys. Gu et al. [ 10 ] reported 
that fine granular YAl
2 
particles in TiAl alloys impeded the movement of 
dislocations, which contributed to the improvement of strength. Sun 
et al. [ 11 ] found that 0.25 at.% Nd addition improved tensile strength of 
TiAl alloys at 900 
◦
C, which was due to the presence of fine Nd
2
O
3 
particles. The addition of Ce and La improved the tensile yield strength 
of Ti45Al3Mo2Nb alloys at 800 
◦
C [ 12 ]. Columnar to equiaxed 
transition occurred in Ti47Al2Nb2Cr alloy with 0.8% Er addition, and 
the compressive properties were improved with 0.2% Er content [ 13 ]. 
The peak flow stress of Ti43.5Al4Nb1Mo0.1B alloy was improved with 
0.2% La addition, and oxide particles retarded the dynamic recrystalli -
zation [ 14 ]. Wu et al. [ 15 ] found that Y addition suppressed the growth 
of TiO
2 
and improved the oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys. 
Ho as lanthanide rare earth element has important applications in 
NiAl intermetallic alloys. For example, Sheng et al. [ 16 ] have reported 
that Ho atoms in NiAl alloys are discharged into the solid-liquid inter -
face, and the formation of Ni
2
Al
3
Ho phase led to the improvement of 
strength in NiAl alloy with 0.1 at.% Ho content. Guo et al. [ 17 ] found 
that appropriate Ho content improved the oxidation resistance of NiAl 
alloys. Similarly, on account of the low solubility of Ho in TiAl inter -
metallic alloys, it is expected that Ho could react with TiAl melt during 
the solidification process, and the formation of Ho-rich phase has pos -
sibility to improve the mechanical properties of TiAl alloys. In addition, 
the presence of oxygen was inevitable during the smelting process [ 18 ], 
Ho as rare earth element has affinity with oxygen, the formation of oxide

2. Experimental methods 
Ti46Al4Nb1Mo alloys with Ho content (0, 0.15%, 0.3%), termed C1, 
C2 and C3 alloys, were fabricated by vacuum arc-melting furnace under 
argon protection. Sponge titanium (99.7%, purity), Al-Nb master alloy 
(72.65 wt% niobium), aluminum (99.99%, purity), molybdenum parti -
cles (99.99%, purity) and holmium (99.9%, purity) were used as raw 
materials. The ingots were melted four times to ensure uniform 
composition with same smelting process. 
The X-ray diffraction analysis (XRD, Empyrean, Holland) was used to 
examine the new phases of Ti46Al4Nb1Mo alloys with different Ho 
content. Microstructure and chemical composition were characterized 
by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with energy dispersive spec -
trometry (EDS). The new phases were further confirmed by transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Talos F200x). 
High temperature tensile properties of C1, C2 and C3 alloys were 
tested by electronic universal material testing machine (AG-X Plus 
250kN/50 kN) at 800 
◦
C with a crosshead speed of 0.3 mm/min. The 
gauge length and cross section of tensile specimens were 15 mm × 5 mm 
× 2 mm, and three tests of each sample were carried out. The tensile 
specimens were ground to eliminate the defect and stress caused by wire 
cutting. C1 and C2 alloys were cut into cubes with size of 15 mm × 10 
mm × 5 mm for oxidation testing. The oxidation specimens were grin -
ded with SiC papers and ultrasonically cleaned with ethanol. Oxidation 
resistance of C1 and C2 alloy was carried out in furnace under air at -
mosphere for 100 h, and the oxidation temperature was 900 
◦
C. When 
the weight gain of specimens was measured for a stage of oxidation, they 
were put in air furnace for oxidation again. Two parallel specimens of C1 
and C2 alloys were tested to ensure repeatability of oxidation testing.

4. Discussions 
4.1. The strengthening mechanism of high-temperature tensile properties 
Wu et al. [ 24 ] found that Y refined the α
2
/ γ lamellar spacing due to 
the formation of Y
2
O
3 
by internal oxidation. Wang et al. [ 13 ] reported 
that the lamellar thickness was reduced by Er addition, which resulted 
from the decrease of stacking fault energy. Ho as an active element has 
strong affinity with oxygen. In this study, as shown in Fig. 9 (a) and (b), 
0.15% Ho addition refines the α
2
/ γ lamellar thickness from 493 ± 65 to 
366 ± 37 nm, which results from the Ho
2
O
3 
formation by internal 
oxidation. Due to refined α
2
/ γ lamellar spacing by Ho addition, more 
α
2
/ γ lamellar interfaces form, which benefits for the room temperature 
tensile strength. However, lamellae coarsening occurs when the samples 
were kept at 800 
◦
C for 5min before testing. Moreover, fine interface 
space had large interface energy, which generated more driving force 
and resulted in lamellae coarsening [ 25 ]. As C2 alloy has more α
2
/ γ 
lamellar interfaces compared with C1 alloy, which is prone to lamellae 
coarsening. As a result, refined lamellar space is not effective to increase 
the strength at high temperature. According to the Ti-Al-Nb phase dia -
gram [ 26 ], no phase transformation to the β phase occurs at 800 
◦
C, 
which indicates that phase transformation has no obvious effect on the 
mechanical properties in this work.

3 
precipitate with a 
hexagonal structure enhanced tensile strength by pinning the disloca -
tion slip at elevated temperature. Fig. 3 (a) and Fig. 4 shows that there 
are two sizes of Ho

200~500 nm, and the length of large particles is between 1 and 3 μ m. As

◦

through and then bend around the particles. As a result, Ho
2
O
3 
particles 
have pinning effect on dislocations and then increase the slip resistance, 
which results in the improvement of strength. Ho
2

particles at the 
nanometer-level have Orowan strengthening effect, while the effect of

4.2. The antioxidant mechanism of Ti46Al4Nb1Mo alloy with 0.15% Ho 
addition 
There is segregation of Al elements at grain boundaries due to the 
discharge of Al atoms into the dendrite gap during solidification. As 
shown in Fig. 2 (a) and (b), C2 alloy has larger area of Al segregation 
compared with C1 alloy. Peng et al. [ 32 ] reported that Al enrichment at 
grain boundary and grain refinement accelerated Al diffusion and pro -
moted the formation of compact Al
2
O
3 
layer, which indicates C2 alloy is 
prone to obtain protective Al
2
O
3 
layer. Due to low solubility of Ho atoms 
in TiAl alloys, Ho atoms are repelled into solid-liquid interface, and 
Ho
2
O
3 
particles form along grain boundaries. Previous study reported 
that RE oxides at the grain boundaries promoted the phase trans -
formation from θ to α -Al
2
O
3 
by acting as nucleation sites [ 33 ], therefore, 
Ho
2
O
3 
particles can accelerate the formation of α -Al
2
O
3
, and C2 alloy 
tends to form the stable Al
2
O
3 
layer. As most Ho
2
O
3 
particles in C2 alloy 
distribute along the colony boundaries, and grain boundary is the inner 
diffusion channel of oxygen. Gong et al. [ 34 ] have reported that the 
inward diffusion of oxygen is prevented by Y-rich and B-rich phases 
distributed along grain boundaries. In this work, the segregation of 
Ho-rich phase at the lamellar colony boundaries of C2 alloy suppresses 
the inward diffusion of oxygen and impedes the internal oxidation, 
which leads to the reduction of oxidation rate. On the contrary, the in -
ternal diffusion of oxygen in C1 alloy is not suppressed effectively, and 
the larger growth stress by rapid internal oxidation accelerates the 
spalling of oxide scale. In addition, previous studies [ 35 , 36 ] reported 
that rare earth oxide near the interface improved the resistance of 
spalling by preventing the formation of voids and pinning oxide film at 
the interface. Therefore, as shown in Fig. 6 , it can be seen that fine 
Ho
2
O
3 
particles distributed along the grain boundaries of C2 alloy have 
great resistance of spalling compared with the oxidation morphology of 
C1 alloy. 
5. Conclusions 
In this paper, microalloying effects of Ho on microstructure and high 
temperature properties of Ti46Al4Nb1Mo alloy were investigated, and 
the conclusions were drawn as follows: 
(1) When the Ho content was 0.15%, Ho
2
O
3 
particles with square 
and rod-like shapes formed by internal oxidation in C2 alloy, and 
many Al
2
Ho phases were observed in C3 alloy. 
(2) Compared with C1 alloy, the high-temperature tensile strength 
was improved with 0.15% Ho addition. Second phase strength -
ening resulted in the improvement of high-temperature tensile 
strength. 
(3) Compared with C1 alloy, the oxidation resistance was improved 
with 0.15% Ho addition. The suppression of inward diffusion of 
oxygen and better adhesion between oxide film and matrix 
contributed to the improvement of oxidation resistance.
