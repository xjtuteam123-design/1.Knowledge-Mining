ABSTRACT 
This study examines the impact of low-temperature cooling on the surface integrity and fatigue properties of the 
FeCoCrNiAl
0.6 
high entropy alloy during machining. Utilizing Abaqus2023/Fe-safe simulations, liquid nitrogen 
cooling, and fatigue tensile tests, it was observed that low-temperature machining improves surface quality and 
increases tensile strength and fatigue life compared to room temperature machining. At a cutting speed of 2800 
mm/min while the temperature drops to 120 
◦
C, the surface roughness can be minimized to the maximum 
extent possible. Yield strength and tensile strength increased by 7.6 % and 12.5 %, respectively, at this condition, 
compared to room temperature. Moreover, material strength improves significantly under low-temperature 
conditions, with yield and tensile strengths at 120 ℃ being 1.16 and 1.4 times higher than at room tempera -
ture. The fatigue life of specimens also improved under low-temperature conditions, with lives reaching 2.82 ×
10
6 
cycles at 120 ℃ , an 86.9 % increase over room temperature. The study concludes that optimal machining 
performance is achieved at higher cutting speeds and shallower cutting depths under low-temperature 
conditions. 
1. Introduction 
FeCoCrNiAl0.6 high-entropy alloy materials exhibit excellent prop -
erties, such as high strength, high ductility, high wear resistance, and 
high corrosion resistance [1 – 6] , which provide them with broad appli -
cation prospects in the manufacturing of key components in industrial 
molds, aerospace, nuclear power equipment, and other fields [7 – 11] . 
However, the difficult-to-machine characteristics such as high hardness, 
high strength, and high elongation rate of FeCoCrNiAl0.6 high-entropy 
alloys have hindered their further production and application in China 
[12 – 15] . As a novel cutting method, cryogenic cooling with liquid ni -
trogen effectively suppresses wear, improves the surface quality of the 
machining process, and enhances the fatigue life and performance of 
difficult-to-machine materials. The selection and optimization of 
machining process parameters for difficult-to-machine materials in a 
cryogenic cooling environment has become a hot topic in current 
research. 
Sartori et al. [16] analyzed the surface integrity of Ti6Al4V after 
semi-finishing under different cryogenic cooling conditions. The results 
suggest that samples machined with N2 cooled to 150 
◦
C had the best 
surface smoothness, no surface defects were present, and compared to 
room temperature dry cutting, there were fewer irregular wrinkles or 
feed indentations, significantly improving surface quality. The 
machined samples under liquid nitrogen (LN2) cooling developed 
higher residual compressive stress on the processed surface. Addition -
ally, due to the extreme cooling capacity of LN2, it can completely 
inhibit thermal softening of the material, thereby enhancing its fatigue 
resistance and extending its service life. The paper reveals that, 
compared to room temperature machining, surface integrity of Ti6Al4V 
was vastly improved under cryogenic cooling conditions, increasing the 
material ’ s brittleness, suppressing the formation of surface pits and 
microcracks, hence strengthening material properties and extending 
service life. An analysis was performed on the surface quality after 
cutting with three different cooling agents, establishing the best process 
parameters and cryogenic environment temperature, providing a spe -
cific experimental plan for Ti6Al4V and other difficult-to-machine ma -
terials. Cryogenic cooling during cutting not only plays a positive role in 
improving material surface quality but also significantly reduces tool 
wear. Yuan et al. [17] pointed out in their study of the effects of different 
cooling air temperatures on cryogenic machining of Ti6Al4V alloy that a 
combination of minimal lubrication with different cooling temperatures 
can significantly reduce cutting force, tool wear, and surface roughness 
during the cutting process. Based on experimental results, compared to 
other cooling air temperatures (0

air temperature of 15

2.4. Model construction 
A model of the high-entropy alloy sheet fatigue specimen was 
established, with dimensions as shown in Fig. 2 .Using Abaqus, a finite 
element model of the tensile fatigue specimen was created for cutting 
and tensile simulation, as shown in Figs. 3 and 4 . The sample is meshed 
by a four-node quadrilateral element with thermal coupling plane strain 
(CPE4RT), and the tool is meshed by a three-node triangular element 
with thermal coupling plane strain (CPE3T), in which the workpiece is 
divided into 1,367,200 mesh elements and the tool is divided into 
99,438 mesh elements. In Fig. 4 , the right end of the tensile specimen is
