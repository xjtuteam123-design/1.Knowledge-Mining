ABSTRACT
The structure and mechanical properties of non-equiatomic
Al8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb4,A l 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb2Ti2 and
Al8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 high entropy alloys (HEAs) were
investigated using X-ray di ﬀraction (XRD), scanning
electron microscope (SEM), transmission electron
microscope (TEM), hardness and tensile testing. TEM and
XRD analyses revealed the formation of γ’’-Ni
3Nb/γ (FCC),
double FCC and γ’-Ni3Al/γ (FCC) structure for as-cast
Al8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb4,A l 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb2Ti2 and
Al8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 HEAs, respectively. The hardness and
yield strength values were found to increase with the
addition of Nb while the ductility of alloys increased with
the increase in Ti concentration, signi ﬁcantly. The as-cast
Al
8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 alloy exhibited an excellent
combination of ultimate tensile strength of 844 MPa and
an elongation of 29.8% due to the coherent phase relation
between γ’ precipitates and γ matrix. The fractured surfaces
of as-cast Al
8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb4,A l 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb2Ti2
and Al 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 HEAs showed typical dimple type
ductile fracture under tension test.

1. Introduction
Regarding their remarkable and unique properties such as high strength and
hardness [ 1–3 ], excellent corrosion and oxidation resistance [ 4,5], superior
high temperature mechanical properties [ 4–6 ], magnetic properties [ 7] and
impressive thermal stability [ 8], high entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted
much attention. Since their discovery by Cantor et al. [ 9] and Yeh et al. [ 10],
much eﬀort has been given to understand the concept of HEAs and numerous
types of HEA systems were discovered with impressive properties. HEAs may
have intermetallic or even amorphous phases, together with solid solution
phases due to di ﬀerences in the atomic sizes and electronegativities between
constituent elements, valence electron conﬁ guration (VEC) and elemental
compositions. Among the HEA systems, Al–Co–Cr–Fe –Ni system has attracted

a compressive plasticity of 23.3% at room temperature with the addition of

the yield and maximum strengths due to its e ﬀect on the grain structure and
solid solution phases. HEAs with single FCC structure exhibit high ductility
with low strength while the alloys with single BCC structure show high hard-
ness and yield strength but their ductility is low. The formation of intermetallic
phases such as Laves, L1
2 and B2 improves the strength and ductility of the
HEA alloys, signiﬁcantly. It was reported that the addition of Nb into the CoCr-

embedded in the FCC solid solution matrix [ 20]. However, laves phase is
known as a brittle phase thus the formation of L1
2 and B2 phases in the
HEAs serves the excellent combination of strength and ductility. The precipi-
tation of coherent Ni
3(Al, Ti) L1 2 structured phase in FCC (γ) matrix oﬀ ers
eﬀective strengthening in superalloys [ 21]. Due to the similarity of L1 2 type
Ni-, Al-, Ti- rich γ’ precipitates embedded in γ matrix to the precipitation
strengthened Ni-based super alloys, these HEAs are named as high entropy
superalloys (HESAs) [22,23]. The volume fraction of the L1
2 phase precipitated
in single-crystal Ni-based superalloys was reported as 65–70% [24 ]. The L1 2-
strenghthened HEAs and complex HEAs were also reported previously [ 25–
29]. Yang et Al. [ 29] stated that the precipitation of the L1 2 phase in Al 7(Fe,
Co, Ni) 86Ti7 HEA improves the tensile yield strength and elongation up to
1028 MPa and 47.8% at room temperature, respectively. The formation of
the L12 phase in the interdendritic regions of the as-cast Co 37.6Ni35.4Al9.9Mo4.9-
Cr5.9Ta2.8Ti3.5 HEA was also reported previously [30]. Zhao et Al. [31] reported
that the co-addition of Al and Nb enables the desired formation of the coherent
L12 precipitates in the Al –Co–Cr–Fe –Nb – Ni system. Besides the L12 phase,
D022-ordered γ’’-Ni3Nb phase is the other well-known precipitate in the γ
matrix used to enhance the strength of superalloys, such as Inconel 718 [ 32].

FeNi and CoCrFeNiTi
0.5Al0.5 HEA [33]. We reported that the crystal structure
of CoCrFeNi HEA has changed from FCC to a mixture of FCC and double BCC
structures by the addition of Ti and Al in CoCrFeNiTi
0.5Al0.5 HEA. The com-
pression test revealed that CoCrFeNiTi 0.5Al0.5 HEA yields at 1997MPa and
fractures at 2344 MPa with a cleavage mode [ 33]. It has been reported that

2. Material and methods
The alloy ingots with nominal compositions of Al 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb4 (RA01)
Al8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb2Ti2 (RA02) and Al 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 (RA03) alloys
were prepared using high purity elements (0.999 Al, Co, Cr, Fe, Ni, Nb, and
Ti, by weight) by Edmund Bühler MAM-1 vacuum arc melter with Turbomole-
cular pumping system HVT52/G under an Ar atmosphere. The alloy ingots with
am a s so f3– 5 g were cast three times to ensure chemical homogeneity. The alloys
were then cast into 4 mm diameter copper moulds via suction casting for tension
tests. The structural analysis and phase stability of the as-cast alloys were inves-
tigated by XRD (D8 Advance Bruker) using Cu K α radiation. The microstruc-
tures of the as-cast alloys and their fractured surfaces after tension tests were
examined using SEM (FEI 430 NanoSEM-30 kV). The elemental distribution
analysis was carried out using Energy-dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy detector
(EDS) inserted in FEI Nova NanoSEM 430 SEM. The microstructure of as-cast
alloys was examined using transmission electron microscopy (TEM) using
JEOL JEM-2100F microscope at 200 kV. TEM samples were prepared using a
focused ion beam (FEI-Nova 600i FIB). The Vickers microhardness tests of the
as-cast alloys were performed using a load of 10 kgf due to the ASTM E92 stan-
dard using a microhardness tester (InnovaTest Nexus 7501) at room tempera-
ture. At least 10 indentation measurements were taken for each specimen, and
then the average Vickers Hardness (HV) values with standard deviations were
calculated. The HV values were also converted into MPa by multiplying by
9.807 for convenience. The tensile test specimens of as-cast alloys were prepared
due to ASTM E8/E8M standard using Instron 5582 mechanical testing machine.
Tension tests were operated with a crosshead speed of 1 mm/min.

4. Conclusion
The structure and mechanical properties of Al
8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb4,A l 8Co30-
Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb2Ti2 and Al 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 HEAs were investigated using
XRD, SEM, TEM, hardness and tensile testing. The following conclusions
can be derived as follows:
- The TEM and XRD analyses indicate double phase formation for Al8Co30Cr18-
Fe9Ni31Nb4,A l 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb2Ti2 and Al 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 HEAs.
TEM and XRD analysis revealed the formation of γ’’-Ni3Nb precipitates in
γ-matrix, double FCC phase and γ’-Ni3Al precipitates in γ-matrix of as-cast
Al8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb4,A l8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb2Ti2 and Al8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31-
Ti4 HEAs, respectively.
- The hardness and the yield strength values were increased by increasing the
Nb content.
- The highest σy were achieved in Al 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Nb4 due to precipitation
of γ’’-Ni3Nb in γ-matrix.
- Ti addition facilitates the formation of γ’crystals and thus improves the duct-
ility of the alloy signi ﬁcantly.
- The tensile test revealed an excellent combination of high strength and duct-
ility in Al8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 HEA. Al8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 HEA alloy exhib-
ited an excellent combination of ultimate tensile strength of 844 MPa and an
elongation of 29.8% due to the coherent phase relation between γ’precipitates
and γ matrix.
- SEM analysis of fracture surfaces of alloys also showed high-density dimple-
like structure that reveals ductile fracture mode. The larger sizes of the
dimples con ﬁrm the higher ductility of Al
8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 HEA.
-A l 8Co30Cr18Fe9Ni31Ti4 alloy has high potential to serve as a high entropy
superalloy (HESA).
