OPTIMIZATION OF HEAT TREATMENT REGIMES
FOR BLADE PREFORMS FROM EXPERIMENTAL TITANIUM ALLOY
N. A. Nochovnaya, 1 A. A. Shiryaev, 1 E. B. Alekseev, 1 and V . G . Antashev1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 12, pp. 22 – 26, December, 2014.
The effect different modes of heat treatment on the phase composition and structure of shaped preforms from a
novel refractory titanium alloy is studied with the aim to raise the high-temperature characteristics (the
long-term strength in particular) without deterioration of the high short-term strength and ductility. The me -
chanical properties of the shaped preforms are studied after heat treatment by the chosen regimes. The me -
chanical characteristics of the alloy and of its domestic and foreign counterparts are compared.
Key words: refractory titanium alloys, blade (shaped) preforms, heat treatment, mechanical pro-
perties.
INTRODUCTION
Advancement of the science of materials and creation of
novel structural materials with effective production pro-
cesses is an important task [1 – 3] that allows designers to
implement their conceptions in the form of promising arti -
cles with enhanced engineering and operating characteristics.
An important role in the solution of these tasks belongs to
developers of refractory alloys that serve the principal struc -
tural materials in the aerospace industry (engine and rocket
making) as well as in power engineering (steam and gas tur -
bines), transport engine making and other industries, where
the volume of consumption of various titanium alloys grows
progressively [4, 5].
In the world, refractory titanium alloys are commonly
produced on the basis of /c97-phase. The advantages of the
/c97-alloys are thermal stability of their structure, good resis -
tance to oxidation at a temperature of up to 600°C, good
weldability, high heat resistance, and high specific strength
[6 – 9].
Recent refractory titanium alloys based on /c97-phase can
be used at a temperature of up to 600°C and, for a short time,
at up to 650°C [10]. However, in both cases the service life is
commonly quite limited, which is disappointing from the
standpoint of the turnaround time of modern gas turbine en-
gines. It seems expedient to search for more effective pro-
duction processes and to optimize heat treatment modes for
prolonging the service life of engines at 600°C and to work
at raising their operating temperatures.
The aim of the present work was to study the effect of
various modes of heat treatment on the mechanical proper -
ties, phase composition, and structure of blade preforms from
a novel refractory pseudo-/c97-titanium alloy of type VT18U.
METHODS OF STUDY
We studied shaped preforms from an experimental re -
fractory alloy of the Ti – (6 – 8)% Al – Mo – Sn – Zr – Ta –
Nb – W – Si system the closest analog of which is refractory
pseudo-/c97-titanium alloy VT18U.
To make the preforms we fabricated a batch of small-size
ingots with a mass of 23 – 25 kg each by the method of triple
vacuum arc remelting.
The phase composition and the structure are known to
depend on the temperature and time parameters of the pro -
cess used to fabricate the preforms and on the modes of their
subsequent heat treatment [11]. We chose a process with
the following generalized scheme to make the blade pre -
forms [6]:
– uniform forging of the ingot aimed at crushing the cast
structure;

The laps for the metallographic analysis were prepared

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
With allowance for the special features of titanium alloys
and the /c98/c155/c97polymorphic transformation typical for them
we can obtain a wide range of mechanical properties in the
semiproducts by varying the heat treatment parameters (the
time and the temperature in the first turn). The required level
of properties is chosen depending on the specific task and is
provided by controlling the morphology, the size and quanti-
tative proportions of the phase and structural states.
In modern aircraft engines the blades are subjected to
high alternating loads, which makes the fatigue limit one of
the main characteristics of their material. The experience in
the field of operation of titanium alloys shows that the high -
est level of fatigue strength is provided in the case of forma -
tion of a globular structure with globules no more than
40 /c109m in size. However, such a structure does not provide
enough characteristics of heat resistance, i.e., the creep
strength and the long-term strength. An alternative variant of
structure for blade (shaped) preforms from refractory tita -
nium alloys may be a bimodal structure and a “basket braid -
ing” structure formed by HT [12]. A bimodal structure con -
sists of a combination of globular and lamellar /c97-phase mor-
phologies. This combination of phases provides an optimum
compromise of different properties required for operation.
Structure of Shaped Preforms
The studied titanium alloy created on the base of the Ti –
Al – Mo – Sn – Zr – Ta – Nb – W – Si alloying system has a
combination of molybdenum ([Mo] eq = 1.6) and aluminum
([Al]eq = 14.2) equivalents that provides a good adaptability
to manufacture, a high coefficient of thermal stability
(Kts = 9.6), and enhanced characteristics of high-temperature
strength [6, 13].

and mechanical treatment. The macrostructure of the pre -

seen that it is represented by fine uniformly distributed
grains, the size of which ranges within 100 /c109m (this corre -
sponds to scale division 1 of the scale of macrostructures).
The macrostructure bears no flaw or undesirable inclusion.
The microstructure of the blade preform after the defor -
mation (Fig. 2 b ) consists of fine dispersed precipitates of a
lamellar /c97-phase. Boundaries of primary /c98-grains have not
been detected. The microstructure also bears traces of dy -

Modes of Heat Treatment of Shaped Preforms
As we have mentioned, control of the morphology and of
the parameters of the microstructure components (for exam -
ple, the relative content of dispersed and globular compo -
nents in a bimodal structure) can provide the desirable com -
promise of the strength, heat resistance, and ductility charac-
teristics.
To test different modes of HT and obtain a wide spec -
trum of properties we cut specimens from the shaped pre -
forms from the experimental refractory titanium alloy. The
chosen HT modes were as follows:
(1 ) annealing for 6 h at t = Tpt – 370°C (Tpt is the tem -
perature of the polymorphic transformation);
(2 ) quenching in air after a 1-h hold at t = Tpt – 70°C +
aging at t = Tpt – 380°C for 8 h;
(3 ) quenching in air after a 1-h hold at t = Tpt – 30°C +
aging at t = Tpt – 380°C for 6 h;
(4 ) quenching in air after a 5-min hold att = Tpt + 20°C +
aging at t = Tpt – 400°C for 24 h;
(5 ) quenching in air after a 30-min hold at t = Tpt –
10°C + aging at t = Tpt – 360°C for 2 h.
The microstructure of specimens of the experimental re-
fractory titanium alloy after the HT is presented in Fig. 3.
The values of the short-term strength at 20 and 600°C and
the time before failure in the test for long-term strength at
600°C at a load of 310 MPa are given in Table 1.
Heat treatment by variant 1 (annealing at 640°C) is used
for stabilization of the structure and phase composition, lev-
eling of the internal stresses after the deformation treatment
and raising the degree of homogeneity of the mechanical
properties [14]. After the annealing the structure of the alloy
(Fig. 3a and b ) is virtually identical to its structure after the
deformation. The alloy with such structure has high enough
strength and ductility properties. The ductility of the alloy in-
creases markedly at 600°C, which indicates a loss in the re -
fractory characteristics. The decrease in the high-temperature
strength is explainable by decomposition of the /c97/c162-phase,
which agrees with the results of the tests for long-term
strength (see Table 1).

The subsequent modes of heat treatment are hardening
ones and involve quenching with aging. The efficiency of the
action of this kind of treatment on the properties of titanium
pseudo-/c97-alloys is not very high due to the low content of
/c98-phase in them as compared to the ( /c97+ /c98)- and pseudo- /c97-
alloys. Despite this fact, it is more advantageous than anneal-
ing. The possibility to choose an optimum mode of heat
treatment eliminating substantial superheating of the metal
makes it possible to obtain a set of mechanical properties
most suitable for specific operating conditions.
Heat treatment by variants 2 and 3 with quenching tem-
perature below T
pt promotes formation of globular compo -
nents in the microstructure, which is typical for titanium
pseudo-/c97-alloys (Fig. 3c – f ). The alloys with such structure
have a high ductility, but their strength and heat resistance
parameters are substantially lower than after annealing,
which is confirmed by the results of the mechanical tests (see
Table 1).
It should be noted that the HT by variant 2 yields a fully
globular recrystallized /c97-phase, whereas the HT by variant 3
produces a bimodal structure with a high fraction of globular
recrystallized /c97-phase resembling a “bamboo” pattern. This
difference in the structures is responsible for the higher duc-
tility characteristics of the specimens of blade preforms after
HT by variant 2. Tests have shown that the types of structure,
and hence the heat treatment modes, mentioned do not pro-
vide a high long-term strength at the operating temperature.
In order to raise the characteristics of strength and
long-term strength at elevated temperatures, we performed
HT of specimens of blade preforms by variant 4. Such a
treatment was expected to promote formation of a fine dis -
perse lamellar structure due to the hold at T
pt + 20°C (in the
/c98-range) and subsequent cooling in air. Then the preforms
were subjected to a long-term aging at Tpt – 400°C for 24 h.
We expected that the HT4 variant would preserve the la -
mellar morphology of the particles of the /c97-phase and pro -
vide a virtually total decomposition of the supersaturated
/c97/c162-phase transforming it into equilibrium /c97- and /c98-phases.
The x-ray diffraction analysis showed (Fig. 3 g and h ) that
the HT by variant 4 forms a fine lamellar microstructure with
traces of boundaries of a high-temperature /c98-phase. As a
rule, the alloy with such structure “resists” well the loads at
an elevated temperature. However, in our case the values of
the short-term and long-term strengths turned out to be not
high enough, and this treatment mode had to be corrected.
To elevate the strength and heat resistance of the blade
preforms from the experimental titanium alloy we tested
variant 5. Its special feature was heating for quenching at a
temperature T
pt – 10°C. This provided a fine lamellar mor -
phology of the /c97-phase. Subsequent short-term aging (for
2 h) at a relatively high temperature (650°C) caused decom -
position of the /c97-phase. As a result, we obtained a typical
“basket braiding” structure represented by primary and se -
condary lamellar /c97-phases and a secondary /c98-phase (Fig. 3i
and j ). Analysis of the mechanical characteristics presented
in the Table shows that this very structure gives an optimum
combination of mechanical properties with elevated heat re -
sistance, which meets the level required from modern refrac-
tory titanium alloys [15 – 17].
CONCLUSIONS
According to the results of our tests, the new experimen-
tal refractory titanium alloy has good perspectives. After the
heat treatment developed, the alloy is not inferior, and in
some aspects is even superior, to the active domestic grades
(VT18U, VT41) and their foreign counterparts (IMI834, etc.).
The tested variants of heat treatment of the alloy have
provided a wide spectrum of mechanical characteristics in
the experimental alloy. Depending on the range of applica -
tion, which determines the production and use of the
semiproducts and articles from them, the alloy may have a
high enough ductility and a high strength, i.e., /c100=8–1 4 %
and /c115
r = 1020 – 1120 MPa.
