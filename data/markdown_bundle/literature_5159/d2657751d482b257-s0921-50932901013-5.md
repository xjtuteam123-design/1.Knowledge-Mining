Abstract
This paper presents the microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb/Nb5Si3 in situ composites prepared from Nb–Mo–Si and Nb–W–Si
systems by casting and heat-treatment. The solidiﬁcation behavior and the phase relationships at 1700 ◦C of these alloys are described. We
observe that a new eutectic of L → Nbss (Nb solid solution) + /H9252Nb5Si3 (W5Si3-type, D8m) exists over a wide range of Mo or W content in
the Nb–Mo–Si and Nb–W–Si ternary systems, which is absent in the Nb–Ti–Si and Nb–Hf–Si systems. A very ﬁne two-phase structure of
Nb and Nb-silicide can be obtained through this eutectic reaction. Mo and W produce strong solid-solution-hardening effect on the Nb phase,
and this effect persists to very high temperature. In contrast, the solid-solution-strengthening of Si operates effectively only at relatively low
temperatures.

1. Introduction
The niobium silicide, Nb 5Si3 is an attractive material
for use at high temperatures because of its high melting
temperature (2520
◦C) [1], low density (ρ = 7.16 g/cm3) [2],
high strength, excellent creep resistance [3] and superior
oxidation resistance. The monolithic silicide, however, is
completely brittle at room temperature [4] and therefore
difﬁcult to use in practical applications. To resolve this, the
in situ composites consisting of a ductile phase of Nb-based
solid solution (Nb
ss) and a silicide phase have been pro-
posed [5,6]. In these composites, the ductile metal-phase
of Nb ss can accommodate some plastic deformation, and
thus improve the fracture toughness at room temperature
(RT), and the Nb
5Si3 intermetallic phase serves to sustain
mechanical load at high temperatures. The term “in situ”
was hence adopted to distinguish these composites from the

The basis for the Nb/Nb5Si3 in situ composite is the Nb-

phase [8,9]. In order to achieve a good balance of high-
temperature strength, room-temperature fracture toughness,
and oxidation resistance, it is important to optimize the mi-
crostructure and phase chemistry of the in situ composites
[10].

2. Experimental procedure
Pure metals of Nb (99.9 mol%), Mo (99.9 mass%), W
(99.9 mass%), and Si (99.999 mass%) were used as starting
materials. Alloy ingots were prepared by conventional
arc melting under a puriﬁed Ar atmosphere. Each ingot
was melted at least three times to ensure compositional
homogeneity. The weight loss of ingots was measured to
be less than 0.1 mass%. It is, therefore, reasonably believed
that the melted ingots were consistent with the designed
compositions. Heat treatment was conducted at 1700
◦C
for 48 h, followed by furnace cooling to room temperature.
Microstructures of the cast and heat-treated samples were
examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) using
secondary or backscattered electron imaging and optical mi-
croscopy (OM). Electron probe X-ray microanalysis (EPMA)
was also employed to determine the constituent phases and
phase composition. High-purity Nb, Si, Mo and W metals
were used as standard materials for quantitative analysis.
The matrix corrections to calculate the mol% compositions
from the measured X-ray intensities were performed with
conventional ZAF technique. X-ray diffraction (XRD) using
Ni-ﬁltered Cu K /H9251radiation was also performed in order to
determine the crystal structure of the individual phases.
The compositions of the samples for mechanical property
measurement were determined on the basis of the isothermal
section diagrams of the Nb–Mo–Si [11] and Nb–W–Si [12]
ternary systems at 1700
◦C, which are given in the following
text. The compression properties of studied alloys at 1500◦C

3.1. Solidiﬁcation behavior
3.1.1. Nb–Mo–Si system
Fig. 1 shows a schematic diagram of the liquidus projec-

Extensive investigations in recent years of the Nb–Ti–Si and
Nb–Hf–Si systems have concluded that such eutectics are

4. Discussion
4.1. Effect of Mo or W addition on the microstructure
The ductility or the fracture toughness at RT always takes
on great importance in developing intermetallic-based com-
posites. Two ways are useful to improve the ductility of in-
termetallic materials. The ﬁrst is the change of the lattice
structure of intermetallic compound from ‘brittle’ to ‘ductile’
type through appropriate alloying. For example, the Nb
5Si3
with tI32 structure can be changed to hP16 with appropri-
ate incorporation of Mo, Ti, and Hf [18]. The second is
the modiﬁcation of the microstructure from ‘brittle’ to ‘duc-
tile’ type, also through alloying. The present study clearly
shows that the two-phase structure of Nb and Nb
5Si3 is mod-
iﬁed from ‘brittle–ductile’ type in Nb–Si binary alloys to
‘ductile–brittle’ type for compressive loading in Nb–Mo–Si
and Nb–W–Si ternary alloys.
The present results also indicate that the eutectic reac-
tion of L → Nb
ss + /H9252silicide exists in the Nb-rich side of
the Nb–Mo–Si and Nb–W–Si ternary systems. Various eu-
tectic structures, regular or complex regular lamellar eutec-
tic, may be obtained in this range. The microstructure, espe-
cially the distributions of silicide, can be directly controlled
in liquid–solid processing.
Moreover, the microstructures can further be modiﬁed
through annealing after solidiﬁcation. ‘Ductile–brittle’ type
microstructures of silicide particles distributed in a con-
tinuous Nb
ss matrix can be produced in the Nb–Mo–Si
and Nb–W–Si systems with a low W content after heat

lends to the enhancement of deformability of the in situ com-

We therefore envisage that Mo and/or W incorporation has
not been given its deserved attention in the development of
Nb/Nb silicide in situ composites. Presumably, this situation
has mainly been due to the fact that the solid-solution hard-
ening of Mo and W will usually diminish the deformability
of Nb
ss at RT. This study suggests that the microstructural
modiﬁcation of the silicide phase in the composites by Mo
or W additions may alleviate this negative effect.
4.2. Strengthening mechanism in Nb–Mo–Si and
Nb–W–Si system
The present results clearly indicate that the high-
temperature strength increases with increasing Mo or W con-
tent, as established in Figs. 10 and 13 . As for the Nb
5Si3
phase, it is to be noted that the solubility limit of Mo and W
in /H9251Nb5Si3 is very low (<2 mol%). We believe that a detailed
investigation of the effect of Mo or W addition on the strength
of the Nb
5Si3 phase is necessary.
Figs. 12 and 15 show remarkable increase in compressive
yield strength at RT in alloys with 1–3 mol% Si. Here, little
silicide phase formed through the eutectic reaction. The in-
crease in strength is mainly caused by the solid solution of
silicon in Nb
ss and perhaps by very ﬁne particles of silicide
precipitated from Nbss, though we did not found such parti-
cles by SEM. At 1500 ◦C, no signiﬁcant increase in strength
was observed in these alloys. These results suggest that the
Si solid-solution hardening and the ﬁne silicide-precipitate
hardening are two important strengthening mechanisms at
RT. At high temperatures, the hardening mechanism of Si
solid solution becomes ineffective.
Finally, silicide volume fraction in the composites greatly
affects the high-temperature strength. The strength in-
creases with increasing silicide volume fraction, as shown
in Figs. 12 and 15.
5. Summary
The microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb/Nb
silicide in situ composites prepared from the Nb–Mo–Si and
Nb–W–Si systems are described. The solidiﬁcation behavior
and the phase relations at 1700
◦C of these alloys are pre-
sented. It is observed that a new eutectic reaction of L →
Nbss + /H9252silicide exists over wide Mo or W content ranges in
the Nb–Mo–Si and Nb–W–Si ternary systems, which have
not been established in the Nb–Ti–Si and Nb–Hf–Si sys-
tems. Very ﬁne two-phase structures of Nb
ss and Nb-silicide
were obtained though this eutectic reaction. Mo and W ex-
hibit a strong solid-solution-hardening effect on the Nb
ss
phase, and this effect can be retained to very high temper-
ature. The strengthening mechanisms of Si solid-solution
hardening and ﬁne silicide-precipitate hardening operate only
at relatively low temperatures. The strength increases with
increasing silicide volume fraction both at room and high
temperatures.
