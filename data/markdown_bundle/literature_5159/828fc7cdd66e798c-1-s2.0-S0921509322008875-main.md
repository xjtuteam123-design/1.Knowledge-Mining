ABSTRACT 
The demand for high-performance magnesium alloys, including high strength, high thermal conductivity, and 
good heat resistance, is growing rapidly in practical applications. Here, we report a high performance 
Mg – 7Al – 3Ca (AX73, wt.%) alloy with high strength, good thermal conductivity, and acceptable heat resistance 
via the high-pressure die casting (HPDC) method. Compared to the commonly used Mg – 4Al – 4RE (AE44) and 
Mg – 9Al – 1Zn – 0.25Mn (AZ91D), the HPDC AX73 exhibited a much higher yield strength (YS) at room temper -
ature (RT) and 175 
◦
C. The high YS was attributed to the combinations of fine-grain strengthening, second phase 
strengthening from numerous Al
2
Ca eutectic phases, and solid solution strengthening from Al solutes. After the 
aging treatment, the room-temperature YS of AX73 alloy was further improved from 184 MPa to 196 MPa due to 
fine Al
2
Ca precipitation. Since the existence of Ca obviously reduced the Al solutes in the α -Mg matrix, the AX73 
and AX73 aged alloys possessed reasonable thermal conductivity of 67 and 68.5 W(m ⋅ K)
 1
, respectively. 
1. Introduction 
With the growing attention to reduce carbon emissions, the demand 
for lightweight and high-performance structural materials increases 
rapidly. Magnesium (Mg) alloys show great advantage and application 
potential in the modern industry because of their low density, high 
specific strength, high damping property, and environment friendly [ 1 , 
2 ]. In the automobile industry, the application of Mg alloys as power -
train components must possess high strength at room temperature and 
good heat resistance at elevated temperatures (150
◦
C-200 
◦
C) to avoid 
failure [ 3 , 4 ]. In the communication industry, Mg alloys used as heat 
dissipation structural components should have high strength and 
reasonable thermal conductivity [ 5 , 6 ]. Therefore, multi-functional Mg 
alloys with high performance, including high strength, reasonable 
thermal conductivity, electrical conductivity, and good heat resistance, 
are demanded in practical applications. 
At present, most Mg components are usually processed by the high- 
pressure die casting (HPDC) method due to its low cost and high effi -
ciency [ 7 , 8 ]. The HPDC Mg – Al based alloys, such as AZ91 (Mg – 9Al – 1Zn, 
wt.%) and AM60 (Mg – 6Al – 0.3Mn, wt.%), are commonly used because 
of their good mechanical properties at room temperature and excellent 
die – castability. However, these alloys are known to be unsuitable for use 
above 150 
◦
C due to their poor mechanical properties at elevated tem -
peratures [ 9 , 10 ]. Furthermore, these alloys generally show low thermal 
conductivity as a large amount of Al element is dissolved in the Mg 
matrix [ 6 , 11 ]. To solve the above-mentioned problems, HPDC 
Mg – Al – RE system alloys, such as AE42 (Mg – 4Al – 2RE) and AE44 
(Mg – 4Al – 4RE), are developed and studied in the past few years, which 
exhibit good mechanical properties at elevated temperature and rela -
tively high thermal conductivity [ 12 – 14 ]. On the one hand, the addition 
of RE (Ce, La, Gd, Pr, Sm) elements to the Mg – Al system can form the 
thermal stable Al-RE intermetallic compounds and thus suppress the 
formation of low – melting – point Mg
17
Al
12
, which effectively improves 
the heat resistance [ 15 – 17 ]. On the other hand, the formation of Al – RE 
intermetallic compounds significantly reduces the Al solute atoms in the 
Mg matrix and thus enhances the thermal conductivity [ 14 , 18 ]. 
Compared with REs, Ca is a promising alloying element for Mg 
because of its low density and low cost. It was reported that Ca also

3.1. Microstructures 
To evaluate the casting defects and grain size of these HPDC alloys, 
the macrostructures and microstructures are shown in Fig. 2 . One can 
see from Fig. 2 (a) – (c) that small porosities (marked by red arrows) with

porosity in all these alloys is relatively low, indicating good compact -

alloys, the average grain size of AX73 alloy is smaller. Furthermore, the 
difference in average grain size between the core and edge region of 
AX73 alloy is also smaller than that of both AE44 and AZ91D alloys, 
indicating a more uniform microstructure.

3.4. Thermal conductivity 
Thermal conductivity is one of the most important parameters for Mg 
alloys used as heat dissipation structural components, such as the outer 
shell of 5G base stations and smartphones. High thermal conductivity of 
structural components can avoid overheating of the electronic device 
and thus ensure the normal operation of the equipment [ 18 ]. In this 
study, the electrical conductivity and thermal conductivity of the HPDC 
AX73 and AX73-T5 alloys are investigated and compared with the HPDC 
AZ91D and AE44 alloys. 
The electrical conductivity of the HPDC alloys shown in Fig. 10 (a) 
indicates that the AE44 alloy exhibits the highest electrical conductivity, 
the AX73 and AX73-T5 alloys have moderate electrical conductivity,

with the AZ91D alloys, the Al solutes in the AX73 and AE44 alloys are

the lattice volume of Mg solid solution is in the following sequence: 
AE44 > AX73 > AZ91D, which is opposite to the sequence Al solute con -

that of the Mg atom, the lattice volume of Mg solid solution decreases 
with the increase of Al solute concentrations. As the lattice volume de -
creases, the opportunity for electrons to be scattered becomes larger, 
and the mean free path of electrons is shortened, leading to a decrease in 
thermal conductivity [ 14 ]. Therefore, the negative influence of solute 
atoms on the thermal conductivity of the studied HPDC alloys are in the 
following sequence: AZ91D > AX73 > AE44. 
The microstructural observations confirm that the intermetallic 
compounds in these HPDC alloys are mainly concentrated at grain 
boundaries and become networked ( Fig. 5 ). Compared with the AZ91D 
and AE44 alloys, the AX73 alloy has finer grain sizes ( Fig. 3 ), which 
brings more interfaces between intermetallic compounds and the α -Mg 
matrix. That introduces additional scattering sources for the free 
movement of electrons and phonons [ 45 ]. As a result, the negative in -
fluence of intermetallic compounds and grain boundaries on the thermal 
conductivity of the AX73 alloy is higher than that of the AZ91D and 
AE44 alloys. Furthermore, the negative influence of the casting defects

similar level of the casting defects in these alloys ( Fig. 2 ), which can be 
improved by optimizing the HPDC process. It should be noted the

4. Conclusions 
A high-performance Mg – 7Al – 3Ca was fabricated by the high- 
pressure die casting (HPDC) method, and the microstructure, tensile 
properties and thermal conductivity were investigated in comparison 
with commercial AZ91D and AE44 alloy. The following conclusions can 
be drawn: 
(1) The HPDC AX73 alloy exhibited a much higher yield strength 
than that of AE44 and AZ91D alloys under tensile tests at room 
temperature (RT), and its strength was further improved by the 
aging treatment due to the precipitation of fine Al
2
Ca particles. 
The AX73 aged alloy exhibited high mechanical properties at RT 
with YS, UTS, and elongation of ~196 MPa, ~238 MPa, and 
~3.9%, respectively. 
(2) The high YS of AX73 and AX73-T5 alloys was attributed to the 
combinations of the fine-grain strengthening, the second phase 
strengthening from numerous Al
2
Ca eutectic phases and solid 
solution strengthening from the solute Al. 
(3) The AX73 alloy showed better heat resistance than AE44 and 
AZ91D alloy at 175 
◦
C, as the YS of AX73 and AX73-T5 alloy still 
kept a high level of ~128 MPa and ~147 MPa, respectively. The 
large amounts of high-melting-point Al
2
Ca eutectic phases 
distributed at grain boundaries contributed to the good heat 
resistance of AX73 and AX73-T5 alloys. 
(4) The formation of Al
2
Ca eutectic phases decreased Al solute con -
centration in Mg matrix, therefore the AX73 alloy showed me -
dium room-temperature thermal conductivity, which was higher 
than the HPDC AZ91D alloy.
