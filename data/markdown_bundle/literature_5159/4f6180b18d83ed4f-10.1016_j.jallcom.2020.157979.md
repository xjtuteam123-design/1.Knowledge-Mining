abstract
To balance the relationship between weldability and properties, a series of Ni-based superalloys with
varying amount of Cr-like elements were designed using a “cluster-plus-glue-atom” model, and fabri-
cated by laser additive manufacturing (LAM). The microstructure of the as-deposited superalloys consists
of few topological close packet (TCP) phases distributed between
g-Ni dendrites with epitaxial growth
characteristic. With the increase of Cr-like elements amount, TCP phases change from granular Laves
phase to long-striped Laves phase plus acicular s phase, and exhibit a trend of ﬁrst decreasing and then
increasing in amount. The mechanism for such microstructure evolution was analyzed. Performance tests
reveal that the NiCr
like-2 alloy (Ni 54.8Fe20.3Cr17.3Mo1.50Al1.56 Nb3.47Ti1.22 at.%), which has the fewest
amount of TCP phases, presents a novel combination of strength, ductility, high temperature oxidation-
resistance, and good weldability.

1. Introduction
The Ni-based superalloys are important candidate materials for
high temperature applications, due to their excellent oxidation
resistance and high mechanical stability at elevated temperature
[1e3]. However, poor ductility and low toughness at ambient
temperature make them dif ﬁcult to process through conventional
processing routes [ 4]. Laser additive manufacturing (LAM) is a
process of laser cladding to fabricate a metal component with the
complex structural shape directly from a 3-D CAD model, without
the need of part-speciﬁc tooling or preproduction costs [5,6], which
provides a new approach for fabricating dif ﬁcult-to-machine alloy
components. Thus intensive researches have been performed to
investigate LAM Ni-based superalloys [ 7e14]. Until now, most of
these related studies have focused on the conventional Ni-based
superalloys，such as IN718 and IN625 etc. However, these alloys
were designed without considering the speci ﬁcity of LAM, metal-
lurgical defects of pore, inadequate fusion and cracks were easily
produced. These defects could not be conquered completely though
they may decrease with the development of LAM technology, so
that the controls of shape and properties were much limited
[15e17].
The main feature of LAM is that the alloy undergoes a rapid
process from melting to solidi ﬁcation [18e20]. Except for the LAM
parameters, it is signi ﬁcant to choose proper LAM alloys in order to
effectively control the process. In general, weldability of alloys
should be taken into consideration, since good weldability is
associated with low metallurgical defect susceptibility. As is well
known, the weldability strongly depends on alloy composition. In
term of the action of alloying elements in Ni-based superalloys,
they can be classi ﬁed into three series: Ni-like elements (including
Ni, Fe, and Co), Al-like elements (including Al, Ti, and Nb), and Cr-
like elements (including Cr, Mo, and W). Ni-like elements are
basic constituents which can be regarded as solvents; Al-like ele-
ments are used to form
g0 or g00 precipitated phases; Cr-like ele-
ments play a role of solid solution strengthening [ 21,22]. Statistical
analysis reveals that the weldability of Ni-based superalloys is very
sensitive to Al-like elements amount [ 23,24]. When the amount of
Al-like elements reached over 8.0 at.%, the alloys became non-
weldable due to the formation of high fraction of g0 and the TCP
phases such as the laves or h phase [25,26], so that the amount in
the weldable Ni-based superalloys was con ﬁned to a narrow range
of 2.0 e7.0 at.%, although high fraction Al-like elements could pro-
mote the precipitation of g0 or g00 phase [ 27,28]. Unlike Al-like el-
ements, Cr-like elements still maintain the superalloys good
weldability within a wide range of 16.0 e30.0 at.%, but excessive Cr-
like elements easily cause the formation of harmful s and P phases,
weakening the mechanical properties [29e33]. Thus it is important
to control the amount of Cr-like elements in order to balance the
relationship between weldability and property.

In the present work, new Ni-based superalloys alloys with
varying amount of Cr-like elements were designed using a “cluster-
plus-glue-atom” model, and then were prepared by laser additive
manufacturing on pure nickel substrate. The in ﬂuence of Cr-like
elements on microstructure and the properties of the alloys were
investigated.
2. Composition design
It is widely accepted that liquid of solid solution is characterized
by chemical short-range order cluster that maintains certain simi-
larities with solid solution structure [ 34,35]. Therefore, a cluster-
plus-glue-atom model is proposed for designing the Ni-based su-
peralloys [36]. In this model, any structure can be dissociated into a
cluster part and a glue atom part, so that the phase composition is
always described by the cluster formula [cluster][glue atom] x
[37,38].
In the weldable Ni eCr-(Al, Ti) superalloy system, for instance,
there is a CN12 [AB 12] type cluster derived from FCC structure.
Accordingly, a universal cluster formula for the system can be
expressed as [(center atom) 1-(shell atom)12](glue atom)x. Here the
subscript number denotes the number of atoms, and x is the range
of 2 e6[ 39,40]. Based on the cluster close-packing principle, the
cluster is the nearest-shell polyhedron centered by any non-
equivalent atomic site in the unit cell of the alloy phase, which
generally consists of elements having strong negative mixing en-
thalpies, while glue atoms, that are located between the clusters,
are composed of elements having weak mixing enthalpies with
solvent. Therefore, Ni-like elements including the Fe and Co, which
have near zero mixing enthalpies ( DH) with Ni, partly substitute Ni
at the ﬁrst shell of the cluster. Al-like elements, which exhibit
strong negative mixing enthalpies with Ni, occupy the central site
of the cluster. Cr-like elements, which have weak negative mixing
enthalpies with Ni, occupy the central site of the cluster or glue site.
The above universal cluster formula can be expressed as [(Al
like,
Crlike)1- (Ni like)12](Crlike)x. Such a typical example is IN718 alloy,
whose cluster formula is [(Al 0.25Ti0.20Nb0.55)1
-(Ni0.73Fe0.27)12](Cr0.92Mo0.08)3.8. Note that the number of Al-like
atoms is approximately one for most Ni-based superalloys with
good weldability and high strength [ 23], such as IN718 and IN625
alloys. In this case, the cluster formula can be further simpli ﬁed as
[(Allike)1 -(Nilike)12](Crlike)x, in which the central site of the cluster
will be completely occupied by Al-like elements. Based on the
simpliﬁed cluster formula, a series of Ni-based superalloy with
varying amount of Cr-like elements was designed. The aim is to
balance the relationship between weldability and property through
quantitative adjustment of Cr-like elements. The detailed compo-
sition of the designed alloys is listed in Table 1. Here it is note-
worthy that Al and Ti content in the designed alloys is limited
within 4 at.% in order to inhibit precipitation of g0 phase with high
hot cracking sensitivity. As an alternative, g00 srengthening phase is
desirable because its sluggish precipitation contributes to hinder-
ing hot cracking. However, the longer precipitation time makes it
impossible for designed alloys to precipitate g00 phase during LAM.
Like other superalloys (such as Inconel 718 and Inconel 625), post
heat treatment is required to precipitate enough g00 srengthening
phases. As a result, the strength of the alloys can be remarkably
enhanced.
3. Experimental procedure
Pure nickel plate with the dimension of
40 mm /C2 40 mm /C2 10 mm was chosen as the substrate. According to
the composition listed inTable 1, elemental powders of Ni, Cr, Mo, Fe,
Al, Nb, and Ti with mass purity of 99.90 e99.99% and granularity of
53e150 mm were blended by ball grinder for 10 h in a vacuum, which
were chosen as LAM feedstock.
There are four primary components of the LAM assembly: the
laser system, the powder delivery system, the controlled environ-
ment, and the CAD driven motion control system. A 5 kW CO 2 laser
unit was used in the present experiment. Based on the preliminary
experiments, the optimized laser processing parameters were
adopted as follows: laser power 2 KW, scanning velocity 5 mm/s,
laser beam diameter 2 mm, overlapping rate 50%, powder feeding
rate 6.8 g/min, argon ﬂow rate 7.0 L/min. During LAM processing,
laser beam was focused on the substrate to create a melt pool into
which the powder feedstock is delivered through an inert argon gas
ﬂowing through a special nozzle, where the powder streams
converge at the same point on the focused laser beam. An inert
argon gas shroud was used as a protective atmosphere for pre-
venting oxidation during deposition. Specimens with size of
27 mm /C2 27 mm /C2 1 7 mm were prepared using a strategy of
bidirectional (zigzag) scanning.
The LAM samples with an image plane perpendicular to the
scanning direction were sectioned by a wire electro discharge
machining device, and then prepared through standard metallo-
graphic polishing and etching procedures for microstructure
observation, and the etching solution is 5 ml H
2Oþ10 ml HClþ5m l
H2O2. Phase identiﬁcation of these specimens was carried out using
an XRD-6000 X-ray diffraction, equipped with a Ni ﬁltered, Cu K a
radiation operating at 40 kV and 40 mA. The microstructural
characteristics and composition were analyzed using a Zeiss Supra
55 (VP) scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with an EPMA-1 720
electron probe micro-analyzer (EPMA). Percipitated phase was
investigated by a Tecnai G220 S-Twin transmission electron mi-
croscopy (TEM). In order to study the thermodynamic behaviors of
these alloys, differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) experiment
was carried out using a Mettler TGA/SDTA851 at a heating rate of
10 /C14 C/min. Vickers hardness was measured with a DMH-2LS micro-
hardness tester under a load of 0.981 N and a dwell time of 30s.
Three tensile specimens for each alloy were extracted in scanning
direction, and then machined to a standard tensile specimen with
22 mm gauge length and 4 mm gauge diameter. Tensile tests were
out using a CMT5105 material testing machine at a strain rate of
1 /C2 10/C0 3 1/s. Continuous variable - temperature oxidation experi-
ment was performed on TGA/SDTA85 thermal analyzer at a heating
rate of 10 /C14 C/min and a temperature range of 200 e110 0/C14 C. The
specimens for oxidation testing were 3 mm /C2 2m m /C2 0.5 mm. To
evaluate the weldability of alloy system, three cross-sections of
each alloy were sectioned along the vertical scanning direction, and
prepared through standard metallographic polishing and etching
procedures. These sections were observed using the LEICA DMi8
optical microscope.

tion in interdendritic area, whereas Al, Cr, and Fe elements with
large redistribution coef ﬁcient (k > 1) mostly distribute within
dendrite [ 43,44]. Such a representative example is shown in
Table 4 . To further evaluate segregation degree of each alloy,
segregation coefﬁcient, deﬁned as the ratio of the average content
of an element in interdendritic area to one in dendritic area, is
calculated. The results show that segregation coef ﬁcients of Ni, Fe,
Al elements remain relatively unchanged with the amount of Cr-
like elements. Segregation coef ﬁcient of Mo and Ti elements

2 alloy ( Fig. 4 ). Note that the segregation coef ﬁcient of Cr is

in the alloys NiCr like-4 and NiCr like-5 alloys, the higher content of
Cr-like elements will increase the Cr concentration in the inter-
dendritic region. This well explains why the amount of TCP phases
has a compositional dependence: the larger the segregation co-
efﬁcients of Mo and Nb, the more the amount of TCP phases as
shown in Fig. 5 , and the higher content of Cr in the interdendritic
area will lead to the formation of s phase and extend the solidi ﬁ-
cation temperature range. It is worth mentioning that the amount
of TCP phases in the alloy system is 1.0 e2.2 vol%, much lower than
that of LAM conventional Ni-based superalloys (typical values are
in the range of 3.5 e5.5 vol%) [ 45,46,50].
In order to study the thermodynamic behaviors including the
liquidus temperature (TL) and the solidus temperature (T s) of these
alloys, the differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) analysis was
carried out. As shown in Fig. 6, the solidi ﬁcation range DT( DT ¼ TL
eTs) ﬁrst decrease and then increase with the increase of Cr-like
elements amount, with the narrowest solidi ﬁcation range DT ob-
tained in NiCr like-2 alloy (see inset in Fig. 6 ). The result shows a
good matching relationship with segregation degree of alloying
elements: the narrower the solidi ﬁcation range, the lower the
segregation degree of alloying elements. This well explains why
NiCrlike-2 alloy has the lowest micro-segregation.
By above analyses, it can be inferred that the microstructure
evolution of the as-deposited alloys is as follows: the super-cooled
liquid is solidi ﬁed by dendritic growth of the primary g-Ni phase,
which is followed by eutectic transformation of the remaining
interdendritic liquid to form g-Ni plus Laves phases, due to severe
Nb segregation in the interdendritic area. The amount of Laves
eutectic phase strongly depends on the segregation degree of Nb:
the larger the segregation degree of Nb, the higher the amount of
Laves eutectic phase. With the increase of Cr-like elements amount,
increased Cr segregation will induce formation of an additional s
phase near ( g-Ni þ Laves) eutectic.

From the deformation behavior information, it can be concluded
that the fracture mechanism of as-deposited alloys is mainly ductile
rupture. Owing to the absence of strengthening phases, the
strengthening of as-deposited alloys mainly results from solution
hardening and higher hardness of TCP phases. The solution hard-
ening is essentially a result of the interaction between dislocation
and solute atoms. Because of the difference in atomic radius of
solute and solvent atoms, lattice strain ﬁeld will be formed around
solute atoms, which can be regarded as the obstacles of dislocations
movement, leading to strengthening of alloy. Thus the lattice mis ﬁt
between solute and solvent atoms plays an important role in so-
lution hardening: larger lattice mis ﬁt is associated with higher
solution hardening [ 30,56]. This can be used to explain why hard-
ness and yield strength of as-deposited alloys increase with the
increase of Cr-like elements with larger atomic radius. Nb is also
deemed as the element of solution hardening. However, it is prone
to segregation due to small redistribution coef ﬁcient, which in-
duces formation of Laves phases in the interdendritic region. It is
generally accepted that the hard and brittle Laves phases are
harmful for mechanical properties of superalloys, which signi ﬁ-
cantly depends on the morphology and size of Laves phase. When
irregular and long-striped Laves phases exist in the microstructure,
they are not easy to move with the matrix during the tensile pro-
cess. Thus, these Laves phases often fail by internal fracture. This
fracture behavior, coupled with the foramtion of brittle
s phases,

4.3. High temperature oxidation-resistance
Fig. 10 shows continous variable-temperature oxidation curves
of the alloy system. It is clear that all alloys undergo two oxidation
stages: the initial stage obeys the asymptotic level linear law, which
is featured by a slight increase of mass gain with temperature,
indicating that oxidation scale yielded during this stage is discon-
tinuous or thiner. The second stage obeys a parabolic law, which is
demonstrated by a signi ﬁcant increase of mass gain with temper-
ature, being attributable to both oxidative product evolution and
local oxidation scale spallation. Although these alloys exhibit
similar oxidation curves, their kinetic parameters change with alloy
composition. As shown in Table 6 , the initial temperature of the
vigorous oxidation ﬁrst increases and then decreases with the in-
crease of Cr-like elements amount, with the maximum value ob-
tained for NiCr like-2 alloy, whereas the total mass gain and mass
gain rate change in the opposite trend. Based on the datum, one can
draw a conclusion that NiCr like-2 alloy has the best high tempera-
ture oxidation-resistance.

Pore and inadequate fusion are frequently found in LAM Ni-
based superalloys.The former is due to trapped gases, while the
latter is produced by inadequate bonding between layers. These
defects not only decrease compactness of deposited layers, but also
cause stress concentration, inducing initiation and propagation of
microcracks. Therefore, control of these defects is critical to
ensuring the forming quality. The experiment research has shown
that among the various parameter of the LAM process, the powder
feed rate was found to be a signi ﬁcant factor affecting the porosity.
Excessive powder feed rate caused higher possibility of pore for-
mation. It was also found that increased laser power decreased the
porosity level regardless of powder mass ﬂow rate. Unlike pore,
inadequate fusion strongly depended on scanning velocity. With
higher scanning velocity, the cladding at the sample edges some-
times cannot catch as much powder as the internal sample cladding
does, therefore the former cladding cannot deposit fast enough to
reach the desired height, leaving gaps in the cladding pass. In
contrast, higher laser power reduced inadequate fusion, but an
excessively high laser power caused melt pool instabilities to
deteriorate surface ﬁnish. These results are similar to those re-
ported in other materials [ 60]. Thus the optimization of process
window was required. Hot cracking is the main defect in LAM of Ni-
based superalloys, which are known as solidi ﬁcation cracking and
liquation cracking. Regarding the cracking mechanisms, some ex-
planations have been proposed [ 61,62]. It has been generally
accepted that solidi ﬁcation cracking was caused by the separation
of residual liquid under tensile stress at last stage of solidi ﬁcation,
and characterized by extended openings that formed during so-
lidiﬁcation in the mushy zone, where the liquid feeding was dif ﬁ-
cult and the transverse shear strength of the material was very low.
Thus liquid feeding is one of the most important factors affecting
initiation and propagation of solidi ﬁcation cracks [ 63]. As is well
known, liquid feeding is closely related to solidi ﬁcation range:
narrower solidiﬁcation range is associated with higher liquid-phase
ﬂuidity and lower microsegregation, which will cause more plen-
tiful liquid feeding. In the present study, the solidi ﬁcation range of
the alloys is 76.4 e161.1
/C14 C as shown in Fig. 6, and is narrower than
that of conventional Ni-based superalloys [ 64,65]. This implies that
the studied alloys have full liquid feeding during solidi ﬁcation,
which will contribute to inhibiting solidi ﬁcation cracking. It has
been recognized that liquation cracking results from liquation of
low melting point eutectic at grain boundary and the tensile stress
[14,63]. LAM is a track-by-track deposition process. Repeated
thermal cycle easily causes the liquation of low melting point
eutectic, forming a thin liquid ﬁlm. The occurrence of liquid ﬁlm is
detrimental to the ductility of the material by weakening the
capability of accommodating the stress, so that liquation crack will
be formed when the accumulated strain overcomes the low
ductility of the material during cooling. Thus liquid ﬁlm may act as
the initiation cracking sites under the tensile stress. For the studied
alloy system, despite absence of
g-g0 eutectic, there are visible g-
TCP eutectics at the grain boundary, which are also likely to be
liquated and form the liquid ﬁlm during the subsequent deposition,
depending on their thermostability. However, no evidence of

5. Conclusions
A series of Ni-based superalloys with varying amount of Cr-like
elements were designed using a “cluster-plus-glue-atom” model,
and fabricated by laser additive manufacturing. The in ﬂuence of Cr-
like elements on microstructure, mechanical properties, high
temperature oxidation-resistance, and weldability of as-deposited
superalloys was investigated. The main results are summarized as
follows:
1) The microstructure of the as-deposited superalloys consists of
few TCP phases distributed between g-Ni dendrites with
epitaxial growth characteristic. With the increase of Cr-like el-
ements amount, TCP phases change from granular Laves to long-
striped Laves plus acicular s, and exhibit a trend of ﬁrst
decreasing and then increasing in amount, with the lowest value
obtained in NiCrlike-2 alloy.
2) Microhardness and yield strength of the as-deposited alloys
monotonically increase with the increase of Cr-like elements
amount, while ultimate tensile strength and elongation ﬁrst
increase and then decrease, with the highest ultimate tensile
strength and the best elongation obtained for NiCrlike-3 and
NiCrlike-2 alloys, which suggests that few and granular Laves
phases are beneﬁcial for a good match between the strength and
the ductility.
3) High temperature oxidation-resistance of the as-deposited al-
loys increases with the increase of Cr-like elements amount,
with the maximun value obtained for NiCr like-2 alloy, and then
decreases afterwards. Owing to a combination of composition
control and process parameter optimization, all as-deposited
alloys exhibit good weldability.
4) By comprehensive comparison, NiCr like-2 alloy with the fewest
Laves phase has a novel combination of strength, ductility, high
temperature oxidation-resistance, and weldability.
