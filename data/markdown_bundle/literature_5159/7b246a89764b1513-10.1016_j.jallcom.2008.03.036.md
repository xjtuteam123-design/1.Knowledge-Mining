abstract
The directionally solidiﬁed (DS) Nb–22Ti–16Si–7Cr–3Al–3Ta–2Hf–0.1Ho (at.%) alloy was fabricated by
using optical ﬂoating zone (OFZ) melting technique. Microstructural characteristics and high tempera-
ture compressive properties at 1623 K of the alloy were investigated by scanning electron microscopy
(SEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD), high-resolution electron microscopy (HREM) and compressive tests. The
DS microstructure exhibits an obvious cell structure and strong orientations of (Nb, Ti)ss (1 1 0) and (Nb,
Ti)
5Si3 (3 1 0) along the growth direction. No deﬁnite crystallographic orientation relationship either
between (Nb, Ti) 5Si3 and (Nb, Ti)ss or (Ti, Nb) 5Si3 and (Nb, Ti)ss was found. The interfaces between sili-
cides and (Nb, Ti)ss are clean, but not straight and sharp. The high temperature compressive behavior of
the DS alloy is superior to that of the conventional cast (CC) alloy at 1623 K.

1. Introduction
Intermetallic Nb 5Si3 has attracted considerable attention
for possible high temperature structural applications due to
its high melting point, low density, excellent high tempera-
ture strength and creep resistance [1,2]. However, low room
temperature fracture toughness restricts its commercial use.
Nowadays, there have been many efforts to improve ductility
and toughness of advanced intermetallics. Incorporating a duc-
tile phase to form a two-phase or multi-phase alloy is one of
the most effective methods to improve the toughness [3–8].
But, to some extent, this method will degenerate high tem-
perature strength. Recently, we have studied the mechanical
properties of Ho-doped Nb–22Ti–16Si–7Cr–3Al–3Ta–2Hf alloys.
The results showed that the proper Ho addition improved
the room temperature compressive ductility and high tem-
perature strength at the same time. However, the mechanical
properties were still insufﬁcient for the requirements of prac-
tical applications [9]. Previous research [10–13] showed that
directional solidiﬁcation was one of the useful fabrication pro-
cesses to control the microstructures and to improve mechanical
properties of Nb-silicide based composites. Considering these

2. Experimental procedures
The master alloy ingot with the nominal composition Nb–22Ti–16Si–
7Cr–3Al–3Ta–2Hf–0.1Ho (at.%) was prepared by arc-melted using a non-consumable
tungsten electrode under an argon atmosphere in a water-cooled copper cru-
cible. The starting materials are high pure Nb, Ti, Si, Cr, Al, Ta, Hf and
Ho. The alloy ingot was turned over and re-melted at least ﬁve times to
ensure chemical homogeneity. The master rods used for directional solid-
iﬁcation were cut from the button by electro-discharge machining (EDM).
The dimensions of the rods are 8 mm in diameter and 70 mm in length.
An optical ﬂoating zone furnace was used to produce the directionally
solidiﬁed bar at the growth rates of 9 mm/h under an Ar gas ﬂowing atmo-
sphere.
Microstructural analysis was performed by SEM, XRD and HREM. Samples for
HREM were thinned mechanically to a thickness of 50 /H9262m and then by ion milling
to the ﬁnal thickness. High-resolution electron microscopy observation was carried
out on JEM-2010 with a point-to-point resolution of 0.194 nm. The compression
specimens with the size of 4 × 4 × 6m m
3 were cut from the DS bar by EDM and the
loading direction was parallel to the solidiﬁcation direction. Each surface of samples
was mechanically ground with 600-grit Al
2 O3 abrasive prior to compression test. The
compression tests were conducted using a Gleeble 1500 testing machine at 1623 K
in vacuum with an initial strain rate of 2 × 10
−3 s−1 .

3.1. Microstructural characteristics

7Cr–3Al–3Ta–2Hf–0.1Ho alloys are shown in Fig. 1 . As reported
previously [14], the microstructures of the CC and DS alloys were
composed of gray-white (Nb, Ti)ss matrix, gray (Nb, Ti) 5Si3, black
(Ti, Nb) 5Si3 and a small amount of white Ho 2Hf2O7.F r o m Fig. 1 ,
there are two apparent differences in the microstructures between
the DS and CC alloys. One is that the (Ti, Nb)
5Si3 phase in the CC
alloy presents uneven distribution in the (Nb, Ti)ss matrix, while
the (Ti, Nb)
5Si3 phase in the DS alloy exhibits almost continuous
network distribution at the intercellular regions and its amount

longitudinal section, the microstructure is characterized by lamel-
lar silicide phases aligned parallel to the growth direction and
embedded within the (Nb, Ti)ss matrix. Some growth faults, such
as discontinuous silicides and transversal dendrites, are commonly
observed.

will become more difﬁcult during elevated temperature defor-
mation. On the contrary, grain boundary sliding in the equiaxed
structure may contribute to the plastic yield, to some extent.

4. Conclusions
The microstructure of the cross-section in the DS alloy exhibits
an obvious cell structure. The microstructure of the longitudinal
section shows that the lamellar silicide phases are aligned paral-
lel to the growth direction. The DS microstructure shows strong
orientations of (Nb, Ti)ss (1 1 0) and (Nb, Ti)
5Si3 (3 1 0) along the
growth direction. No deﬁnite crystallographic orientation relation-
ship either between (Nb, Ti)
5Si3 and (Nb, Ti)ss or (Ti, Nb) 5Si3 and
(Nb, Ti)ss was found. The interfaces are clean, but not straight and
sharp. The (Nb, Ti)
5Si3 and (Ti, Nb) 5Si3 phases bond well with
the (Nb, Ti)ss matrix, respectively. The DS alloy shows a strong
strain hardening behavior. The high temperature compressive yield
strength is higher than that of the CC alloy.
