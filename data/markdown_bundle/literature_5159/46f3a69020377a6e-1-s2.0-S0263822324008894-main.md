ABSTRACT
This study proposes a novel multi-scale numerical approach to explore the mechanical behavior and damage 
evolution in nickel (Ni)-based superalloys containing various carbide particles. At the nanoscale, the elastic 
properties of two distinct carbides are determined using first-principles calculations. At the microscale, finite 
element simulations (FEM) in ABAQUS are used to analyze the stress – strain relationship and local stress dis -
tribution within a three-dimensional representative volume element, as well as damage and fracture behavior. 
The model integrates the elastic – plastic response of the Ni matrix, the elastic-brittle fracture of micro-scale 
carbides, and the interface behavior between carbide and matrix. FEM findings are consistent with tensile test 
data, indicating that skeletal carbide promotes plasticity while blocky carbide elevates strength. The interface 
between blocky carbide and matrix is susceptible to cracking. When the carbide is oriented at 45
◦
to the load 
direction, offering a balance of strength and plasticity. Stress concentration is reduced when carbides are uni -
formly distributed and present in high-volume fractions. The damage evolution mechanism of nickel-based su -
peralloy influenced by carbide is elucidated. The numerical method is well-suited for a thorough analysis of the 
comprehensive behavior of reinforced phase/metal-matrix composites.
1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys are extensively used in aero engines and gas 
turbines owing to their excellent thermal stability, creep resistance, 
thermal corrosion resistance, and other properties [1 – 3] . Metal carbide 
[5] is a prominent precipitated phase besides the most prevalent matrix 
phases, γ and γ’ [4] . Superalloys include elements such as Ta, Ti, Mo, Nb, 
and Hf, which facilitate the formation of primary MC-type carbides. The 
effects of carbide composition, volume fraction, size, and morphology 
on the properties of superalloys are complex and cannot be ignored 
[6,7] . On the positive side, MC carbide has the ability to enhance the 
strength of superalloys [8] . However, it might also be a potential cause 
of cracking due to its high hardness and brittleness [9] . As a result, 
understanding the morphology and distribution of carbides in Ni-based 
superalloys, as well as controlling the type of carbide precipitation, is 
critical for improving mechanical properties.
A lot of research has been carried out on carbides in Ni-based su -
peralloys [10 – 12] . However, the influence of carbides with various 
morphologies on superalloys is still controversial. Li et al. [13] showed 
that small rod and flake carbides are more likely to cause cracking in Ni- 
based single-crystal superalloys than skeleton carbides. Feng et al. [14]
proved through quasi-in-situ tensile testing and the crystal plastic finite 
element method that small blocky carbides are more susceptible to stress 
concentration and breaking than skeletal carbides. Nevertheless, Xing 
et al. [15] found that the presence of large-particle skeleton-like car -
bides tends to reduce material plasticity and increase the cracking 
probability. Ultimately, the impact of carbide morphology on the evo -
lution of material damage remains uncertain, and further investigation 
is required.
However, traditional experimental testing is time-consuming, labor- 
intensive, and costly, and the test results are restricted; thus, there are 
some limitations in predicting the mechanical properties and damage

In this paper, a multi-scale numerical calculation method is proposed 
to investigate the mechanical behavior and damage mechanism of 
carbide/Ni-based superalloys with various carbide types, sizes, and 
volume fractions. The geometric morphology and atomic proportion of 
the reinforced phase were determined based on the superalloy ’ s exper -
imental microstructure. The supercell models of two types of carbides 
(skeleton and block) are established using first principles calculation, 
and their corresponding density and elastic properties are determined 
and utilized as parameters for FEM. By establishing a three-dimensional 
representative volume element model, considering the elastic – plastic 
constitutive relationship of the matrix, damage response, elastic-brittle 
fracture behavior of micro-scale carbides, and the tug-separation 
behavior of the carbide/matrix interface, the strengthening and dam -
age mechanisms of different types of carbides on Ni-based superalloys 
are thoroughly analyzed, and the experimental results are compared and 
verified. This numerical method is predicted to provide substantial 
support for research into the integrated behavior of reinforced phase/ 
metal-matrix composites, as well as a foundation for micro-mechanism 
analysis and the design of regulated reinforced phases to enhance Ni- 
based superalloys.

2. Multi-scale simulation methods
The multi-scale coupling analysis method is used to get effective 
information on macro, micro, and nanoscales. The multi-scale model 
correlation scheme is described below: (1) At the nanoscale, the elastic 
properties of carbide supercells are calculated using first principles; (2) 
At the microscopic scale, the first-principles calculation parameters are 
taken as the input parameters of the FEM.
The RVE of Ni-based superalloys considering carbide is established 
by experimental observation and first-principle calculation, and the 
overall mechanical response is determined. Additionally, the zero 
thickness interface is added to take into consideration the damage or 
fracture initiation that is brought about by stress concentration, as well 
as its influence on the effective properties of the composite. (3) At the 
macroscale, first-principle modeling is based on the carbide atomic ratio 
recorded by SEM, while the FEM relies on the carbide morphology 
observed by SEM. The experimental tensile fracture morphology at room 
temperature is compared with the FEM results. The specific scheme is 
shown in Fig. 1 .

3.3. Model verification
During the experiment, we conducted tensile tests on Sample A and 
Sample B at room temperature, resulting in the corresponding stress – -
strain curves. Through uniaxial tensile finite element modeling, we 
obtained the stress – strain elastic – plastic relationship of the two mate -
rials, as shown in Fig. 7 . The findings indicate a strong correlation be -
tween the simulation and experimental results. Fig. 8 shows the FEM 
fracture morphology of Sample A and Sample B. The fracture mode of 
both samples is characterized by ductile fracture. There are obvious 
blocky carbide particles at the fracture site, and cracks tend to propagate 
along the interior of the carbide or at the interface between the carbide 
and the matrix, which is in line with the findings of our experiment 
( Fig. 4 ). This proves that the nano-micro – macro multi-scale numerical 
analysis method used in this work for predicting the mechanical prop -
erties of Ni-based superalloys reinforced with various kinds of carbides. 
Furthermore, it demonstrates that the simulation ’ s parameter settings 
are reasonable and reliable.
4. Numerical exploration
4.1. Strengthening effect of carbides
Fig. 9 illustrates the stress comparison between the composite RVE 
model and the matrix model under the tensile strain load of 0.054. Both 
Sample A and Sample B demonstrate that the maximum value of 
equivalent stress in RVE of composite material is obviously higher than 
that in the matrix, and the former is roughly three times larger than the 
latter. This suggests that during the loading process, the internal stress is 
transferred from the matrix to the carbide, and the carbide becomes the 
main stress carrier, serving to transfer the load and improve the overall 
strength of the matrix [15] .
4.2. Effects of carbides on fracture
Fig. 10 describes the evolution of damage in Sample A and Sample B 
caused by carbides when subjected to tensile strain loads of about 0.75, 
0.105, 0.138, and 0.167, respectively. The results indicate that during 
plastic deformation, the composite ’ s damage begins with the internal 
blocky carbide, which is easily transformed into the stress concentration 
point, as shown in Fig. 10 (a) and (b). The damage of Sample B occurs at 
0.736, earlier than that of Sample A, but there is little disparity in the 
equivalent stress levels between the two samples. As the strain increases, 
the stress eventually transfers to the interface between the blocky car -
bide and the matrix, and destructive elements form in the matrix.

5. Conclusion
In this paper, a multi-scale numerical calculation method was pro -
posed to study the impact of carbide characteristics on the mechanical 
properties of Ni-based superalloys. The strengthening and damaging 
mechanisms of two types, sizes, and volume fractions of carbides in 
elastoplastic Ni-matrix are investigated by combining first-principles 
calculation and finite element analysis. The stress – strain response of 
two kinds of Ni-matrix under tensile load was simulated, and the find -
ings are in good agreement with the experimental data. The results 
reveal that the skeletal carbide improves the material ’ s plasticity, 
whereas blocky carbide enhances its strength. The cracking is more 
likely to occur at the interface of blocky carbide and matrix, then extend 
to the matrix, and then crack at the interface between the skeletal car -
bides and matrix, before ultimately expanding into the matrix. When the 
carbide and the load direction are 45
◦
, the material may exhibit a 
balanced combination of strength and plasticity. Carbides with a high 
volume fraction and uniform distribution are advantageous for reducing 
stress concentration. This numerical method is expected to give sub -
stantial support for studying the comprehensive behavior of reinforced 
phase/Ni-based superalloys and serve as a foundation for analyzing 
microscopic mechanisms and designing advanced Ni-based superalloys.
