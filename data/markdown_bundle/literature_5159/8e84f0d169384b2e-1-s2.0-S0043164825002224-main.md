1. Introduction
Aluminum alloys and titanium alloys are the two most widely used 
types of lightweight alloys. Aluminum alloys, with a low density of less 
than 3 g/cm
3
, high specific strength, good formability, and low cost, 
generally exhibit excellent performance below the temperature range of 
200~300 
◦
C [ 1 , 2 ]. For higher service temperatures, titanium alloys, 
which offer better high-temperature performance, are preferred. Tita -
nium alloys have their density ( > 4 g/cm
3
) larger than those of Al alloys 
but possess superior high-temperature performance over aluminum al -
loys and even comparable to that of steels. However, due to the issues of 
formability and high manufacturing cost, the use of titanium alloys is 
limited within specific fields such as aerospace and biomedical 
applications [ 3 , 4 ]. Therefore, it is of importance to develop new light -
weight alloys with a density of 3~4 g/cm
3 
for applications within a 
medium temperature range of 300~500 
◦
C, thereby bridging the gap 
between aluminum alloys and titanium alloys.
Al-Ti alloys and compounds are among the most attractive candidate 
materials for lightweight components. The intermetallic Al
3
Ti, among 
various Al-Ti alloys and compounds, exhibits several superior proper -
ties, such as a low density around 3.36 g/cm
3

around 216 GPa, a high melting point around 1350 
◦
C, and outstanding 
oxidation resistance [ 5 – 8 ]. However, Al
3
Ti has a stable low-symmetry 
tetragonal D0
22 
crystal structure, which leads to brittleness at room 
temperature and thus largely limits its applications [ 9 , 10 ]. Fortunately, 
it has been found that the crystal structure of Al

2. Experimental procedures
The Al
3
Ti-based lightweight alloys were prepared using the arc 
melting method. High-purity Al, Ti, Cr, Mn, Fe and Cu powders were 
mixed manually and pressed into disc-shaped bulk samples using a 
powder presser, followed by melting in an arc melting furnace in pure 
argon atmosphere. Each alloy sample was re-melted four times to reduce 
compositional inhomogeneity.
The phases in the samples were identified using an X-ray diffrac -
tometer (Rigaku XRD Ultima IV, Japan) with Cu K
α 
radiation at a 
scanning speed of 4
◦
/min. The microstructure was characterized using 
scanning electron microscopes (SEM, Zeiss Sigma VP300-FESEM, and 
Zeiss EVO) equipped with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). 
Thin-foil TEM specimens were prepared by electro-polishing after me -
chanical thinning and analyzed using a transmission electron micro -
scope (JEOL-JEM 2100 equipped with a Gatan Imaging Filter, operated 
at 200 kV). Selected area electron diffraction (SAED) was employed to 
identify crystal structures of the phases.
The macro-hardness of the alloys was evaluated using a ZHR Rock -
well hardness tester (ZwickRoell, Canada) under a load of 60 kgf at room 
temperature. The micro-hardness at elevated temperatures was 
measured using a HTV-PHS30 high-temperature Vickers hardness tester 
(ARCHIMEDES, China) with a load of 2 kgf. For both macro- and micro- 
hardness measurements, seven points were tested on each sample, and 
the average values were calculated. Compressive tests at room temper -
ature were carried out on an electromechanical universal testing ma -
chine (TestResources, USA) at a strain rate of ~1 × 10

5. Conclusions
Lightweight Al
3
Ti-based MEAs were designed and fabricated, which 
showed improved mechanical properties and wear resistance, particu -
larly at elevated temperatures. The conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(a) The addition of alloying elements, Cr, Mn, Fe, and Cu, helped 
transform the brittle tetragonal D0
22 
structure of Al
3
Ti into a 
ductile cubic L1
2 
structure, along with formed B2 and D8a second 
phases.
(b) Increasing contents of the alloying elements increased fractions 
of B2 and D8a phases with correspondingly decreased L1
2 
phase 
fraction, leading to increased hardness and compressive strength.
(c) Al
3
Ti-based MEAs exhibited superior wear resistance compared 
to the traditional Al-matrix composite reinforced by 30 wt% SiC 
particles (Al/SiC
p
) at both room and elevated temperatures.
(d) The representative Al
60
Ti
20
Cr
5
Mn
5
Fe
5
Cu
5 
alloy showed a high 
yield strength of 442 MPa and high hardness of 270HV2 at even 
500 
◦
C, superior to those of Ti6Al4V, as the long-range ordering 
in L1
2
, B2 and D8a phases results in high strength with high 
thermal stability.
The obtained results suggest that Al
3
Ti-based MEAs could serve as an 
effective bridge between aluminum and titanium alloys, offering 
adjustable combinations of low density, high strength, and desirable 
ductility. The developed alloys are promising candidates for medium- 
temperature mechanical and tribological applications requiring light -
weight materials with robust mechanical properties and wear resistance.
