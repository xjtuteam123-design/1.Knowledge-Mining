The nature of stability of dendritic microsegregation of rhenium in single crystals of intermetallic ( /c103/c162+ /c103)a l-
loys based on /c103/c162-Ni3Al is determined under a high-temperature heat treatment and under creep and endurance
tests. It is assumed that steady dendritic microsegregation of rhenium is a factor determining the high
high-temperature strength of rhenium-containing ( /c103/c162+ /c103) alloys based on /c103/c162-Ni
3Al.
Key words: intermetallic, single crystal, heat treatment, segregation, high-temperature strength.
INTRODUCTION
Alloys based on /c103/c162-Ni3Al intermetallic (IC6, IC50,
IC218, IC221, IC396M, IM927 etc.) have been known from
the 1990s [1 – 3]. Today specialists work on optimization of
the compositions of intermetallic alloys of type VKNA /c47VIN
without boron (the main alloying elements are Cr, Mo, W,
Co, Re, REM) [4 – 5] and of the processes of their produc -
tion and treatment.
Ni
3Al-base alloys are lighter than the refractory nickel
alloys of the second and third generations [6, 7] due to the
higher content of aluminum and lower (by a factor of
1.5 – 2.3) content of heavy refractory metals. Alloys of type
VKNA/c47VIN in single-crystal condition are used for opera -
tion at 1100 – 1150°C for a long-term and at 1200 – 1250°C
for a short term, which exceeds the operating temperatures of
nickel superalloys. The main factor responsible for the high
high-temperature strength of heterophase ( /c103/c162-Ni
3Al + /c103-Ni)
alloys is preservation of the heterophase structure until melt -
ing. In these alloys /c103-Ni is a ductile structural component
[2, 3].
Intermetallic alloys used for making the hot duct of gas
turbine engines, especially for rotor or jet blades, are fabri-
cated by directed crystallization [8, 9]. The structure, and
hence the mechanical properties, of castable intermetallic al-
loys produced by directed crystallization depend substan -
tially on the temperature gradient and on the crystallization
rate [4, 5, 10, 11]. An important factor determining the struc -
ture and properties of single crystals of castable nickel and
intermetallic alloys not subjected to deformation is dendritic
microsegregation under crystallization, i.e., nonuniform dis -
tribution of alloying elements over the section of the dendrite
cell at a uniform distribution over the height of the single
crystal and its cross section. Intermetallic alloys of type
VKNA based on /c103/c162-Ni
3Al have the same phase composition
(/c103/c162+ /c103) as refractory nickel superalloys ( /c103/c162+ /c103). Refractory
nickel superalloys are characterized by presence of a single-
phase region of nickel-base /c103-solid solutions between the
solvus [(/c103/c162+ /c162/c103sec)/c47/c103] and solidus [(/c103/c47(L + /c103)] lines. Long-term
homogenizing annealing of nickel superalloys in the /c103-region
removes dendritic segregation, yields a highly alloyed
nickel-base solid solution, and provides formation of parti -
cles of a hardening /c103/c162-phase with homogeneous composition,
size and morphology in the whole of the volume of the mate -
rial at a temperature below T
solvus . In contrast to nickel
superalloys, single-crystals of intermetallic ( /c103/c162+ /c103)-alloys

Dendritic Segregation in Alloys of Type VKNA
It has been shown earlier in [5, 11] that the distribution
of alloying elements in dendrite arms and interdendritic re -
gions in single-crystal alloys of type BKNA based on Ni 3Al
under crystallization may differ and is characterized by the
distribution coefficient kd = cda /c47c0 (here c0 is the composi -
tion of the alloy in the liquid phase (melt) and cda is that in
the core of the first-order dendrite arms in the solid phase).
Intermetallic alloys obey the rule derived for nickel-base
two- and three-components alloys, which also holds for re -
cent complexly alloyed nickel superalloys. If the alloying
element lowers the melting temperature of the matrix metal,
k
d < 1 (Al, Ti, Cr, Mo), and the element enriches the inter -
dendritic space. If the alloying element raises the melting
temperature of Ni, kd > 1 (Co, W, Re), and the element en -
riches the dendrite arms.

DISCUSSION
We established that growth in the temperature and /c47or
prolongation of the heating of single crystals of intermetallic
(/c103/c162+ /c103)-alloys of type VKNA with cellular-dendritic struc -
ture was accompanied by lowering of the degree of dendritic
segregation, and elevation of the temperature was more ef -
fective than increase of the heating time. The coefficient of
segregation of rhenium was preserved below ks = 1.3,
whereas for the other elements (Al, Ti, Cr, Mo, Co) including
the most refractory tungsten ks = 1. Let us try to explain the
“stability” of dendritic segregation of rhenium in interme -
tallic (/c103/c162+ /c103)-alloys from a standpoint allowing for the differ -
ences in the distribution of the alloying elements (AE) in
dendrite arms and interdendritic space, in the solubility of the
AE in the phases and structural components, in the diffusion
mobility of the AE, and in the formation of fine precipitates
of additional rhenium-alloyed phases in the rhenium-en -
riched arms. Our analysis has sown the following.
(1) As it has been shown earlier in [5, 11], rhenium raises
the melting temperature of nickel in addition to tungsten and
cobalt; its distribution coefficient kd > 1. Under crystalliza -
tion, these elements enrich the dendrite arms, while alumi -
num, chromium and molybdenum, which lower the melting
temperature of nickel, enrich the interdendritic space (den-
dritic microsegregation).
(2) It is interesting that Re is virtually insoluble in the
coarse primary precipitates of /c103/c162-Ni3Al concentrated in the
interdendritic space. By the data of various reports on the
structure of the Ni – Al – Re ternary diagram gathered in [7],
the solubility of Re in /c103/c162-Ni3Al is 1 – 1.5 orders of magnitude
lower than in the /c103-Ni solid solution. Thus, there exist a kind
of correlation between the difference in the solubility of Re
in the (/c103/c162+ /c103)-dendrites (in fact, in the /c103-phase) and in the pri-
mary precipitates of /c103/c162-Ni3Al and the “stability” of the den-

of the intermetallic alloy, i.e., at a distance of less than
100 /c109m, as it occurs for the more refractory tungsten (Tables
2 and 3).
(4) Fixation of rhenium in dendrite arms and stabilization
of nonuniformity of its distribution within a dendrite cell are
promoted by formation of fine precipitates of rhenium-al -
loyed additional phases based on the Ni – Mo – Re system in
the rhenium-enriched dendrite arms already during crystalli -
zation; these precipitates are steady under high-temperature
annealing (1100 – 1320°C) and creep tests at 1100°C.
We may assume that such steady nonuniformity of the
distribution of rhenium in a dendrite cell (“stable” dendritic
segregation) is one of the main factors determining the high
characteristics of high-temperature strength of rhenium-con -

CONCLUSIONS
1. Short-term annealing of single crystals of intermetallic
alloys based on /c103/c162-Ni3Al (of type VKNA) at tan < Tsolvus af-
fects weakly the parameters of dendritic segregation but re -
lieves the casting stresses, which raises their endurance.
2. Steady dendritic microsegregation of rhenium (ks = 1.3)
has been detected under high-temperature annealing of sin -
gle crystals in the temperature range of Tsolvus – Tsolidus and in
endurance tests, while for the other alloying elements
k
s = 1.0. This is connected with the kind of the distribution of
rhenium in a dendrite cell under crystallization, absence of
solubility in primary precipitates of /c103/c162-Ni3Al in the
interdendritic space, low diffusion mobility of rhenium, and
formation of particles of P- and /c103/c162-phases based on the
Ni – Mo – Re system in the dendrite arms under crystalliza -
tion; these particles bind rhenium and are stable at high tem -
peratures.
3. We assume that the steady dendritic microsegregation
of rhenium is a factor determining the high characteristics of
high-temperature strength of rhenium-containing ( /c103/c162+ /c103)-al-
loys based on /c103/c162-Ni3Al.
