High-Resolution Diffraction Imaging
of Misorientation in Ni-Based Single Crystal
Superalloys
Robert Albrecht, Maciej Zubko, Kamil Gancarczyk, and Dariusz Szeliga
Abstract
In the present work, the novel high-resolution X-ray
diffraction technique for imaging misorientation and
mosaicity in single crystal superalloys is introduced.
The technique is based on classical X-ray diffraction
topography geometries combined with high-resolution
diffraction and post-processing of obtained data including
color coding and 3D projections of each diffraction
images. For the investigations, the single crystal rods
were produced from CMSX-4 superalloy at a withdrawal
rate of 1, 3, 5, and 7 mm ⋅min
−1. The high spatial and
angular resolution of the method allows to visualize the
complex nature of mosaicity present in single crystal
superalloys. It was observed that mosaics blocks differ in
size and misorientation on the multi-scale level from the
small present in dendrite arms, through single dendrites,
group of dendrites, up to subgrains. Measurements of
misorientation were done on cross and longitudinal
sections of the castings. It was proved that increasing
withdrawal rate in ﬂuences the mosaicity structure and
mechanisms of its evolution. Solidi ﬁcation at withdrawal
rates from 1 to 5 mm ⋅min
−1 possesses higher misorien-
tation between dendrites. With higher misorientation
between dendrites, when the widening of solidi ﬁcation
front occurs, the mosaicity spreads across the casting in
the form of misoriented dendrite lines as the solidi ﬁcation
progress by growing long secondary dendrite arms. Also,
it was stated that 3 –5m m ⋅min
−1 rates possess a higher
possibility of creation of subgrains or selector grains
defects in the casting.

Introduction
The materials design for high-pressure turbine blades is a
vast challenge for the scientist since the ﬁrst commercially
built gas turbine [ 1]. Such materials must withstand opera-
tion under severe conditions such as heavy mechanical
loads, high temperature, and oxidizing gas environments.
Since the early days of development, it became clear that
nickel-based alloys are the most promising group of mate-
rials possessing excellent high-temperature properties [ 2].
Nowadays, the commercially used superalloys contain
approximately ten chemical elements with rare earth addi-
tions and well-controlled level of impurities. The exact
chemical composition of superalloys evolves with its man-
ufacturing technology [ 3]. The manufacturing process of the
turbine blades critical components became sophisticated to
the level of single crystals casting from multi-component
alloys. The nomenclature was taken directly from a typical
single crystal growth process, however, the technology and
microstructure behind superalloys are much different [ 4, 5].
The obtained single crystalline turbine blades possess
well-oriented columnar dendritic structure and consist of two
phases: c and c′. However, almost the same lattice parame-
ters and similar crystal structure of both phases make pos-
sible, with some limitations, to treat the turbine blades as a
single crystalline material. Since the early days of single
crystal turbine blades production, the primary crystallo-
graphic orientation was considered as directly in ﬂuencing
the creep resistance properties [ 6]. Each blade during the
production process is controlled in terms of deviation of
main crystallographic direction from the load axis. Mostly,
single crystal turbine blades are manufactured with [001]

However, it is surprising that the Laue technique is still in

able analytical techniques, the researchers now focused also
on secondary (perpendicular) crystallographic orientation as
well as on mosaicity present in single crystal superalloys,
increasing the knowledge of misorientation defects [ 12, 13].
Real crystals are a conglomerate of crystalline blocks,
tilted to each other (differ in size and misorientation). Each
block is separated from the surrounding blocks by faults,
dislocations, and cracks. The mosaicity is a measure of
crystal lattice planes that are misoriented with each other and
belongs to well-known defects of good quality single crys-
tals with high perfection. Many available investigation
methods for its measurements and visualization were
developed [ 14–16]. However, in the case of directionally
solidiﬁed castings, like superalloys, we are still in a matter of
conjecture on how the mosaic structure looks like. This is
caused by the high level of defects that are present at dif-
ferent scales from macro to nano. Numerous attempts have
been made to analyze the mosaicity including optical
investigations of dendritic structure [ 17] or diverse diffrac-
tion methods such as multi-point Laue mapping [ 18], X-scan
mapping [ 11], various types of X-ray topography with the
use of laboratory X-ray sources [ 19, 20], synchrotron sour-
ces [ 21–23], neutron diffraction [ 24], or electron diffraction
[25–27]. The last developments in the electron backscattered
diffraction (EBSD) techniques gave especially interesting
results concerning the mosaicity at the dendritic scale. The
latest EBSD measurements revealed high dendrite to den-
drite misorientation present in single crystal superalloys
[28]. The published measurements of mosaicity were mostly
focused on small areas containing a few dendrites. However,
the local mosaicity can be misleading, and it is challenge to
draw any conclusions of its origin.
In the present work, we introduce the novel, high-
resolution X-ray diffraction method that makes possible the
investigation of the internal misorientation in large areas of
single crystals (especially Ni-based superalloy materials)
with high spatial and angular resolution. Recently, X-ray
imaging techniques have progressed with the improvements
in sources (rotating anodes), detectors, and computer image
processing. These new features like two and three-
dimensional imaging with arc-sec angular resolution and
spatial resolution in the micrometer range open new
possibilities in determining crystal quality. The presented
method was developed as an extension of classical X-ray
topography methods combined with high-resolution X-ray
diffraction and post-processing of obtained diffraction ima-
ges. The active measurement area of centimeter size together
with high angular and spatial resolutions allows the visual-
ization of defects from primary dendrite arms to the whole
surface of the samples. For the presentation of the method
capabilities, we prepared castings from CMSX-4 Ni-based
superalloys at different withdrawal rates, between 1 and
7m m ⋅min
−1. We have analyzed cross and longitudinal
sections before and after heat treatment. Due to the fact that
the proposed method is based on the direct X-ray diffraction
images, the presented results show the direct evidence of the
complex mosaic structure of single crystal superalloys and
open discussions about the understanding of low angle
boundaries as well as explain the in ﬂuence of solidi ﬁcation
rate on the mosaic structure.
Experimental
For the investigations, single crystalline rods were produced
from CMSX-4 superalloy at withdrawal rates of 1, 3, 5, and
7m m ⋅min
−1 in the Research and Development Laboratory
for Aerospace Materials at Rzeszow University of Tech-
nology. The nominal chemical composition in wt% was as
follows: 5.6 Al, 1.0 Ti, 6.5 Ta, 6.5 Cr, 0.6 Mo, 6.0 W, 9.0
Co, 3.0 Re, 0.1 Hf, Ni bal. The standard directional solidi-
ﬁcation Bridgeman process was carried out in a vacuum
furnace ALD VIM-IC 2 E/DS/SC. The mold design consists
of a starter block, selector, turbine blade, and 12 mm
diameter rod. The perpendicular and longitudinal samples
were cut from the rod 100 mm above the selector and
30 mm above turbine blade. The surfaces were then
mechanically polished. The longitudinal samples were cut
parallel to the growth direction for better visualization of
dendrites mosaicity. The microstructures were observed on a
JEOL JSM-6480 scanning electron microscope (SEM) using
backscattered electrons (BSE).
X-ray diffraction experiments were performed on
custom-built X-ray diffractometer designed for measure-
ments of large single crystals in transmission and back
reﬂection geometries with the use of high-resolution
diffraction (Fig. 1). The X-ray beam is produced by
Rigaku tabletop 9 kW rotating anode RA-MultiMax9 with a
copper target. The direct polychromatic beam can be used
for obtaining Laue diffraction patterns for determining the
initial orientation of the sample. For further measurements,
the beam can be monochromatized by two or four germa-
nium (220) crystals. The diffraction beam is recorded by
Rigaku FC 5400 X-ray CCD camera with an active window
of 18.0 /C213.5 mm

Method
The presented method of the mosaicity measurements is
based on collecting the series of diffraction images (X-ray
topographs) from the whole sample surface containing the
full misorientation distribution (across the rocking curve
line). For this purpose, the sample is initially orientated on
certain lattice planes chosen for the measurements. There are
a limited number of accessible diffraction spots that can be
chosen in nickel-based superalloys for the measurements of
the cross or longitudinal cut samples. Depending on the
crystal orientation, this can be 002, 113, or 022 diffraction
spot. The higher angle re ﬂections (like 420) are not recom-
mended due to the decrease of the diffraction peaks intensity
and blurriness of the diffraction images caused by the slight
difference in lattice parameters of c and c′ phases. After
alignment of the sample on certain lattice planes, the mea-
surement of the crystallographic misorientation on the whole
sample surface is performed. Data collection is conducted in
the selected angular range in a stepwise manner. The single
step of data acquisition consists of positioning the sample on
the desired x angle and recording the diffraction image of
the whole sample surface by the CCD camera. In order to
achieve the high precision of crystallographic misorientation
determination, the X-ray parallel beam has to be used. By
the use of four monochromators, it is possible to select only a
single K
a1 line. The use of single K a1 line (not as usually K a1
and Ka2 doublet) is necessary in order to prevent observation
of double re ﬂection of mosaic blocks. The four-crystal
monochromator is shaping the beam as a narrow line, which
covers only a few millimeters of the sample surface. In order
to overcome this limitation and measure the whole sample
surface, the movable transverse table is used to scan the
sample surface by the beam. The transverse movement must
combine both sample and detector translation in order to
exclude overlapping of diffraction images. Depending on the
size of the sample and intensity of the diffracted beam, the
single step can take up to 10 min. The measurements con-
tinue stepwise by chosen Dx angle resolution from x
start to
xﬁnish covering all mosaic blocks present in the sample. For
the analysis of the mosaic structure of the Ni-based super-
alloys, the angular resolution of Dx = 0.025° is suf ﬁcient.
The ﬁnal data set obtained after measurements consists
typically of approximately 140 diffraction images [ 29].
The received images are afterward post-processed by
commonly available ImageJ [ 30, 31] and Chimera [ 32, 33]
software packages. The post-processing involves combining
of all diffraction images into 2D or 3D visualization of the
mosaic structure. Diffraction images recorded at the same x
angle are colored by the same color from the rainbow pal-
ette. By integration of all recorded diffraction images, one
can obtain a 2D image stack representing the whole sample
surface with misorientation angle indicated by the color
table. For better visualization of the mosaic blocks, misori-
entation diffraction image can be spread in the 3D space
according to its x angle (misorientation angle). Such a
procedure emphasizes the misorientation and allows for a
straightforward interpretation of the recorded diffraction
images. The performed procedure allows for intuitive visu-
alization of the high and low angle boundaries in reference
to the sample dendritic structure. Nevertheless, further
numerical analysis of the recorded data can be performed.
For instance, rocking curves can be easily traced containing
full surface or selected area data (even a single pixel rocking

Discussion
In the present work, a novel method of high-resolution
diffraction imaging of misorientation in single crystals is
described. The method combines the high spatial and
angular resolution with large surface analysis, which makes
the method especially recommended for complex
microstructural materials like Ni-based superalloys. The
method was developed using laboratory, custom-built X-ray
diffractometer as an extension of typical X-ray topography
measurements. The biggest disadvantage of the known
X-ray topography and other X-ray diffraction single crystal
methods is overlapping of diffraction contrast, small mea-
surement area, and low resolution (angular and/or spatial).
The presented method solves some of the problems by step
measurement, scanning of the entire sample surface, appli-
cation of modern X-ray detectors and sources for high spatial
and angular resolution, and post-processing of the data with
color coding. It has been shown that the received results can
be useful in the case of highly textured materials with a high
level of defects and complex microstructure. The shown
misorientation is limited to certain lattice planes, however,
from the experience of previous research [ 34], the use of
reﬂex from lattice planes inclined to the growth direction or
combined vertical and longitudinal section measurement can
receive the complete information of misorientation level and
defect location.
It was shown that mosaicity present in Ni-based single
crystalline superalloys is very complex and different from
what is known from a typical single crystal material and
should be taken into consideration. The solidi ﬁcation
parameters inﬂuence the mosaicity and the mechanism of its
creation. We can distinguish single dendrites misorientation,
area or line of dendrites, and large subgrains defects. The
overall angle of misorientation present in the casting was in
all cases around 3° and increases with a higher withdrawal

Summary
In the present study, we introduce a novel method of
high-resolution diffraction imaging of misorientation as a
promising technique for precise analysis of mosaicity in
single crystal superalloys. The mosaicity is a rather inevi-
table property of every single crystalline superalloy casting
and thus a greater attention should be given for its charac-
teristics, especially when introducing novel alloy composi-
tion or solidi ﬁcation process parameters. The solidi ﬁcation
