Mechanical properties and high-temperature deformation behaviour of 
particle-strengthened NiAl alloys 
B. Zeumer, W. Wunnike-Sanders, G. Sauthoff 
Max-Planck Institutfir Eisenforschung GmbH, 40074 Diisseldoti Germany 
Abstract 
Alloying of NiAl with Ta or Nb leads to an alloy with high high-temperature strength. Additional alloying with Cr leads to additional 
precipitation hardening. Highest strength is obtained by directional solidification, while alloys with improved ductility have been 
prepared by powder metallurgy. High-temperature deformation results in dynamic and meta-dynamic recrystallization of the Laves 
phase. Prototype components for engine application have been produced. 
Keywords: Deformation; Nickel; Aluminium; Alloys; Particle strengthening 
1. Introduction 2. Experimental procedures 
Intermetallic phases, in particular NiAl, are promis- 
ing materials for high-temperature applications. NiAl 
offers the opportunity to develop a new class of alloys 
with comparatively low density, high strength up to 
1200 “C and excellent oxidation resistance [l-3]. The 
use of single-phase NiAl as structural material is 
limited because of its lack of ductility at room tempera- 
ture and the low creep resistance, in particular at tem- 
peratures above 1000 “C [4]. 
Small ductility improvements have been achieved by 
micro-alloying, e.g. with molybdenum, gallium or iron 
[3,.5]. A second possibility is the combination of NiAl 
with a second, more ductile phase, e.g. N&Al [6]. 
Strengthening mechanisms have been studied exten- 
sively, including solid-solution hardening, particle 
hardening by dispersoids, e.g. TiB, [7,8] or HfC [9], or 
precipitation hardening in two-phase alloys, e.g. 
NiAl+ a-Cr or NiAl+ Laves phases [ 10-131. The 
present study is directed at NiAl alloys which are 
strengthened by a Laves phase. It is to be clarified 
whether and at what temperature there is plasticity in 
the composite alloy, which mechanisms control the 
creep behaviour, in which way dislocations interact 
with particles and whether there is a possibility to 
develop a commercial alloy. 
In this study more then 120 alloys have been 
checked. Five representative alloys are discussed in 
this paper. The nominal chemical compositions are 
given in Table 1. The average concentration of impuri- 
ties in these alloys is listed in Table 2. The cast alloys 
were produced by directional solidification (DS). 
Powder metallurgy (PM) was applied to gas-atomised 
powders. The powders were produced from pre- 
alloyed ingots by H.C. Starck, Laufenburg. Consolida- 
tion was done by hot isostatic pressing (HIP) at 
1250 “C. Forging of the alloys was done by thermo- 
mechanical treatments (TMT) with constant strain rate 
at 1100°C. Large forged specimens with diameters 
above 25 mm were provided by Martin Marietta, MA.

3. Results and discussion 
Particle-strengthened NiAl alloys can be obtained 
by casting in several NiAl-X systems, where X is, for 
example, tantalum, niobium, hafnium or zirconium 
[ 141. Most of these systems form quasi-eutectic systems 
where NiAl forms a two-phase equilibrium with a 
Laves phase. For applications, alloys with small volume 
fractions of Laves phase are of particular interest 
because their deformation behaviour is closer to that of 
the ‘ductile’ NiAl than to that of the brittle Laves phase. 
The embrittling effects of Laves phases on mechanical 
properties require careful consideration of microstruc- 
ture and particle distribution, however.

After solidification, alloys with volume contents of 8 to 
40% Laves phase form a cellular structure of Laves 
phase separating the NiAl grains. This microstructure 
is stable with respect to technical heat treatments, e.g.

3.2. Deformation at high temperature 
The differences in the deformation behaviour of 
alloys which have been processed by casting, PM or 
TMT can be explained by comparing the stress-strain 
curves of these alloys. Fig. 2 shows the stress-strain 
curves of alloy 3 at 1100 “C, processed by casting, PM 
and TMT. The cast alloy possesses the highest strength 
and shows a yield point. PM and TMT alloys exhibit a 
lower strength compared to the cast alloy and show no 
strain-hardening. The deformation behaviour of the 
PM and TMT alloys is similar to that of fine-grained 
NiAl (see Ref. [ 1.51, for example). Therefore it can be 
assumed that the particles have only small effects on 
the yield strength of these alloys. In comparison to 
analogously produced cast single-phase NiAl, particles 
lead to an increase of strength of about 34 MPa at 
1100 “C (NiAl, 44 MPa; alloy 3 (TMT ), 78 MPa).

TEM studies of alloys with different amounts of 
deformation clarify the deformation behaviour and the 
plasticity of the two-phase cast alloy (alloy 1). The as- 
cast material shows only low dislocation densities in 
NiAl and the Laves phase. Deformation of this alloy at 
1100 “C results in an increase of dislocation density, 
both in NiAl and the Laves phase. The distribution of 
dislocations after 6% compression is shown in Fig. 3. 
The Laves phase shows regions with high dislocation 
density and other, already recrystallized regions. This 
recrystallization may be due to meta-dynamic 
recrystallization, as there are no dislocations visible in 
the new grains. The occurrence of dynamic recrystalli- 
zation, however, cannot be excluded. Further deforma- 
tion leads to an increase of recrystallized grains and to 
a cracking of the cellular Laves phase structure. This 
process is similar to the spheroidization of cementite in 
pearlite during hot deformation [ 16,171. 
The microstructure after 50% deformation is similar 
to that shown in Fig. l(d). Based on these observations, 
the mechanical behaviour at high temperatures (Fig. 2) 
can be explained by the change of microstructure 
during deformation. In cast alloys the first step during 
deformation is the cracking of the Laves phase matrix. 
This needs an additional force, from which the yield 
point of the cast alloys results. High amounts of 
deformation crack the cellular Laves phase, so that the 
cast microstructure then becomes similar to that after 
PM or TMT processing. Therefore it is supposed that 
after total destruction of the cellular structure - with 
about 25% strain - the deformation behaviour of all 
Laves-phase-strengthened NiAl alloys is similar and 
the strength is determined by the volume fraction of the 
second phase, irrespective of the prior processing.

3.5. Interactions between particles and dislocations 
The interaction between particles in the NiAl matrix 
and dislocations was investigated by TEM studies of 
deformed PM-processed NiAl alloys. PM specimens 
were chosen in view of the small particle sizes. Particle 
size and distribution in the NiAl matrix are a function 
of the content of alloying elements and the processing 
parameters during atomization. Within the specimen it 
was possible to observe regions with small (Fig. 6(a)) 
and with large interparticle spacings (Fig. 6(b)). The 
figures show that dislocations are either stopped by 
particles with small spacing, or they pass the particles 
with larger spacings by an Orowan mechanism. These 
observations of dislocation pinning or a Orowan 
mechanism are in agreement with the mechanical data, 
which show a particle-hardening-induced increase of 
flow stress of about 34 MPa (see above) in TMT- and 
PM-processed NiAl alloys with a Laves phase.

4. Alloy development 
Based on the experimental results of this work, 
Laves phase-strengthened NiAl alloys are proposed 
for structural applications. Components can either be 
produced by directional solidification, by (isothermal) 
forging or by powder metallurgical methods. The 
brittleness at room temperature restricts shape- 
forming processes to high temperatures. The only pos- 
sibilities of machining are electrostatic discharge 
machining or grinding. Below the BDTT, deformation 
of these alloys is only elastic. As the alloys show no 
significant plasticity below 900 “C, the stress at room 
temperature should not exceed a design stress level to 
prevent catastrophic failure. For ceramic composites 
this design level is proposed to be 30% below the UTS 
[27]. Load limits for this type of alloy may be rated in 
the same way. 
Possible applications include components in Diesel 
engines and gas turbines, in particular combustors and 
heat-protective components. A first prototype com- 
ponent is shown in Fig. 7, which was produced by 
casting with directional solidification and EDM. The 
component was produced from an alloy with a high 
volume fraction of Laves phase (more than 70%), 
which results in very high brittleness. The higher ductil- 
ity of the alloys in the current research and the success- 
ful production of prototype components are promising 
factors for further alloy development. 
5. Conclusions 
Laves-phase-strengthened NiAl alloys show high 
strength in the temperature regime above 1000 “C. The 
addition of this Laves phase increases both the creep

resistance and the brittleness. The alloys show 
plasticity at temperatures of 1100 “C and higher, and 
can be deformed plastically by more then 50%. Micro- 
structural control by thermomechanical treatment or 
powder metallurgy lowers the brittle-to-ductile transi- 
tion temperature, compared to cast material. Further 
significant improvements in ductility and strength can 
be achieved by additional alloying. Despite the 
inherent brittleness of Laves-phase-strengthened 
alloys, prototype components can be machined from 
cast material. The alloys studied are proposed for 
applications in Diesel engines and gas turbines as com- 
ponents which are less complex than turbine blades. 
This necessitates much further work for alloy 
optimization and determination of application-relevant 
properties, in particular corrosion resistance and 
dynamic strength.
