ABSTRACT
Traditional approaches for improving the mechanical performance of alloys entail modifying interfaces, 
particularly grain boundaries, with elemental segregation or secondary phases. However, these methods face 
challenges in concurrently improving the strength, plasticity, and high-temperature softening resistance of al -
loys. Here, we uncovered that stable isostructural coherent interfaces effectively address these challenges. In the 
model body-centered cubic (BCC) MoTaVW refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) fabricated by mechanical 
alloying and spark plasma sintering, controlling the sintering temperature enhances the preferential segregation 
of W at interfaces. This results in a distinct BCC W-enriched nanolayer between micrometer-scale grains. This 
nanolayer facilitates dislocation slip and prevents grain growth, thereby improving both plasticity and resistance 
to high-temperature softening. Consequently, the MoTaVW RHEA featuring stable isostructural coherent in -
terfaces achieves an ultrahigh yield strength of 1410 MPa and a plasticity of 22 % at ambient temperature. Even 
at 1200 
◦
C, it maintains a yield strength of 575 MPa under hot compression.
Interfaces, crucial components within the structure of alloys, exert 
significant influence on their mechanical performance [ 1 ]. Whether 
arising from grain boundaries and sub-grain boundaries resulting from 
different orientations or phase boundaries stemming from differing 
structures and compositions, these interfaces typically exhibit cleanli -
ness (i.e., absence of elemental segregation) and atomic disorder [ 2-4 ]. 
They impede dislocation motion, induce stress concentration, and 
diminish plasticity. Simultaneously, the disordered interfaces facilitate 
rapid atomic diffusion, leading to reduced high-temperature stability 
and pronounced softening behaviors at elevated temperatures [ 5-8 ].
Interface modification stands as a widely adopted strategy for 
addressing the aforementioned challenges. In most scenarios, elemental 
segregation lowers the cohesive energy of interfaces, thereby facilitating 
crack initiation and propagation, culminating in catastrophic brittle 
fracture along the interfaces. However, in rare instances, elemental 
segregation can enhance interface cohesion, thus improving plasticity 
[ 9-11 ]. Conversely, owing to the interfacial instability resulting from 
rapid solute atom diffusion under thermal loads and irregular atomic 
arrangements, alloys often exhibit poor high-temperature stability and 
strength [ 11 , 12 ]. Decorating interfaces with a secondary phase induces 
the Zener pinning effect, effectively impeding interface migration and 
thereby enhancing the high-temperature stability and strength of the 
alloy [ 13-15 ]. Nevertheless, the secondary phase typically demonstrates 
significant structural disparities from the matrix, rendering the in -
terfaces non-coherent [ 16 ]. During plastic deformation, the secondary 
phase markedly hinders dislocation movement, leading to significant 
stress concentration at the interface and ultimately resulting in cata -
strophic brittle fracture [ 17-20 ]. Therefore, traditional methods relying 
on elemental segregation and non-coherent decoration of interfaces with 
secondary phases encounter difficulties in simultaneously enhancing the 
strength, plasticity, and high-temperature softening resistance of alloys.
Here, we addressed these issues by designing stable isostructural 
coherent interfaces in the model body-centered cubic (BCC) MoTaVW 
refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA). While all constituent elements in 
the alloy exhibit a BCC structure, their diffusion coefficients differ 
substantially at high temperatures, which facilitates the formation of 
these distinctive interfaces. We fabricated the iso-atomic quaternary 
MoTaVW alloy using segmented refrigerated ball milling and spark

remarkable strengthening effects at both room temperature and high

We observed stable isostructural coherent interfaces within the 
1700

W at interfaces promoting their formation. Notably, W, Mo, Ta, and V 
are all stable elements in the BCC structure, and they can indefinitely 
dissolve into each other pairwise [ 44 ]. Consequently, despite differences 
in W and Mo content between the matrix and the W-segregated nano -
layers, both exhibit a BCC crystal structure. Moreover, the compositional 
disparity between the two BCC phases is solely in W and Mo content. W 
and Mo, belonging to the same group, possess very similar chemical 
properties and atomic sizes. Thus, the difference in lattice constants 
between the two BCC phases is minimal, resulting in a high degree of 
coherency. Specifically, for the BCC W-segregated nanolayers, the con -
centrations of W, Mo, Ta, and V were measured to be 33.0 ± 1.0 at.%, 
17.0 ± 1.0 at.%, 25.0 ± 1.0 at.%, and 25.0 ± 1.0 at.%, respectively. This 
BCC phase demonstrates an ideal configurational mixing entropy of 
approximately 11.52 J ⋅ mol

for high-entropy alloys by Yeh et al [ 45 , 46 ]. Therefore, the BCC 
W-segregated nanolayers also possess high thermal stability.
The W-segregated nanolayers enhance the binding force of grain 
boundaries through robust electronic interactions and strong bonds at 
the coherent interfaces, thereby hindering dislocation motion. 
Leveraging the coherent relationship between the W-segregated nano -
layers and the matrix, dislocations can traverse the nanolayers and 
persist in motion [ 47 , 48 ]. As a result, the shearing mechanism assumes a 
dominant role, with its strengthening effects primarily attributed to 
coherent and modulus strengthening [ 49 ]. The elastic interaction effect 
between coherent stress-strain field and dislocation increases the critical 
shear stress [ 50 ]. Additionally, the distinct elastic modulus between the 
matrix and W-segregated nanolayers alters the energy dynamics during 
dislocation cutting, consequently contributing to the strengthening. 
Notably, the stable isostructural coherent interfaces do not significantly 
compromise the alloy ’ s plasticity, thereby breaking the well-known 
strength and plasticity trade-off. During deformation, dislocations 
easily penetrate through the coherent interfaces, effectively avoiding 
stress concentration and thus enhancing plasticity [ 51 ]. Moreover, the 
minimal lattice mismatch between the W-segregated nanolayers and 
matrix lowers the elastic interaction between dislocations and in -
terfaces, effectively avoiding crack initiation along grain boundaries 
(Supplementary Fig. S8). These favorable factors collectively contribute 
to the high strength and large plasticity of the 1700
◦
C
◦
C-sintered 
MoTaVW RHEA.
Furthermore, the stable isostructural coherent interfaces effectively 
retard grain growth, thereby enhancing resistance to high-temperature 
softening. In the W-segregated nanolayers, multi-principal element 
mixing results in high configurational entropy and consequently low 
Gibbs energy of the grain boundaries, reducing the thermodynamic 
driving force for grain growth. Additionally, the Zener pinning effect 
induced by the W-segregated nanolayers significantly suppresses grain 
coarsening and growth [ 52 ]. These nanolayers prevent grain boundary 
movement by exerting a pinning pressure, thereby counteracting the 
driving force that pushes the boundaries and enhancing the 
high-temperature softening resistance of the alloy.
In conclusion, these distinctive interfaces confer the MoTaVW RHEA 
with high strength, large plasticity, and outstanding high-temperature 
softening resistance. They not only impede dislocation motion but also 
facilitate dislocation nucleation and accommodation, thereby synergis -
tically improving strength and plasticity. Moreover, they effectively 
suppress grain growth, further enhancing resistance to high-temperature 
softening. Importantly, this concept of interface design provides an 
efficient approach to meet the evolving demands for future high- 
performance structural materials.
