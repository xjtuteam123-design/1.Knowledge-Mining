abstract
Due to the limitations of utilizing pure metals, several elements were combined to make an alloy in order
to provide and improve certain features as needed for the application. The concept of alloy making has
diversiﬁed over a thousand years. In addition, A new concept of alloy design has been discovered early
decades and named high entropy alloy (HEA). Multiple principal elements are being adopted with a high
concentration to develop new material. The multi-dimension composition space has several unrevealed
properties that the researchers are exposing. However, an ample number of properties have been uncov-
ered. Despite this, alloys based on intermetallic compounds have developed as high-temperature mate-
rials with the primary goal of replacing nickel-based superalloys due to their low melting temperatures.
For applications in sensitive environments, there is still a lot of interest, thus these alloys are continu-
ously being developed. Mechanical strength, microstructural stability, and corrosion/oxidation resistance
are all requirements for alloys used in high-temperature applications. High entropy alloys may be the pri-
mary materials used in aviation turbine engine hot section components and high-temperature environ-
ments.
Nevertheless, there must be many other concealed properties to date, and it is likely to be discovered
soon, seeing the ample research going on it. In this urge, this review explores the up-to-the-minute mile-
stones in the high-temperature application achieved by the HEAs. The conversant assessment also
includes different processing routes of HEAs, properties at high temperatures, and advanced HEAs that
could be exploited for high-temperature applications. In a nutshell, this artifact emphasizes the potential
application of HEAs that could be advanced high-temperature materials in the future. Thus, the review
outlines HEAs and covers various advantageous information for working on materials exposed to high-
temperature applications.

2. The concept behind high entropy alloy
The uniqueness of the HEAs is another identity in metallic
material. The alloy has several properties, mainly due to the signif-
icant core effects that make its properties exceptional. It is worthy
to note that the core effects are also responsible for phases’
strength and ductility, mainly due to solution hardening [16].A
brief understanding of the four-core effect has been discussed in
this section.
2.1. The high entropy effects
The entropy mainly refers to conﬁgurational entropy related to
Gibb’s free energy. As per the concept of high entropy, the entropy
will be high, and hence free energy becomes low, leading to the
stability of the phase. The enhancement in mutual solid solubility
between the alloying elements and the number of phases pre-
sented in the HEAs could manifestly diminish. It could be appreci-
ated by the Gibbs free energy equation, which is G = H-TS; here, G
is the Gibbs free energy, and H, S, and T belong to the enthalpy,
entropy, and temperature of the system, respectively. If the tem-
perature is sufﬁciently high, the entropy term will dominate, and
high entropy will lead to the stability of a phase. The entropy of
metal would be high at the melting state compared to the solid-
state, and it could be said that the difference between the two
states is roughly equal to gas constant R by Richard’s law [17,18].
An example of CuNiAlCoCrFeSi HEA can explain the effect of
alloying composition on phase formation. The alloy exhibited the
BCC and FCC as a major phase from binary to septenary. However,
it includes a small amount of intermetallic, which is not revealed in
the XRD analysis ( Fig. 3 ). The formation of a simple phase also
denies the conventional prospects of forming several phases. Thus,
the author also clears that the formation of simple solid solutions
as a high entropy effect at high temperatures is not valid [17].
Besides, the increase in conﬁgurational entropy could stabilize
the single-phase solid solution, which has also rarely been seen.

2.2. Sluggish diffusion effect
In the high entropy alloy, it has been seen that the diffusion in
the HEAs is slow as compared to the conventional alloys. Diffusion
becomes a crucial parameter while considering the high-
temperature strength or high-temperature structure stability.
Slowness in diffusion indicates a slower phase transformation or
slower kinetics, which is a core effect in high entropy alloy. The
sluggish diffusion in the HEAs case is very different from another
alloy system because of the different arrangement of neighbor
atoms. The atom’s jumping into a vacancy brings a different neigh-
bor to the lattice site. Due to the local atomic arrangement differ-
ence, the atoms have different bonds and local energy for each site.
Thus, when an atom jumps into the lower energy sites, it becomes
trapped, and there are lesser chances to jump out. On the other
hand, if the jumping site is a higher energy site, there is a high
probability of jumping back to its original site. In the other aspect,
each constituent element has its diffusion rate. Some elements are
highly active, and some have low activity depending upon their
properties. So, the element is less active and has lower possibilities
to achieve the vacancy by jumping successfully. Unlike the conven-
tional alloy, the circumstances are entirely different in the HEAs;
thus, the kinetics of diffusion is sluggish. Moreover, researchers
applied high entropy ﬁlms to resist the diffusion due to their out-
standing thermal and diffusion resistance [19].
Sluggish diffusion kinetics is an important contributor to the
outstanding properties of high-entropy alloys. However, the diffu-
sion kinetics in high-entropy alloys has never been probed directly.
The initial investigation suggested that diffusion may be slow at an
inverse homologous temperature but not at absolute tempera-
tures. Other experimental investigations also suggested that slug-
gish diffusion kinetics may not be true in some HEAs. Few
interdiffusion investigations supported the sluggish diffusion
effects, while others suggest that the sluggish diffusion effect can-
not be generalized to all HEAs. Some studies show that the diffu-
sion coefﬁcients in the HEAs are lower than those in the
constituent metals while the activation energies in the high-
entropy alloys are higher than those in the constituent metals.
Moreover, the trend of the normalized activation energy is posi-
tively related to the number of composing elements in the matrix.
A quasi-chemical model is proposed to analyze the ﬂuctuation of
lattice potential energy in different matrices and to explain the
observed trend in activation energies. Greater ﬂuctuation of lattice
potential energy produces more signiﬁcant atomic traps and
blocks, leading to higher activation energies, and thus accounts
for the sluggish diffusion in high-entropy alloys [20]. Sluggish dif-
fusion kinetics was not observed in alloys with higher conﬁgura-
tion entropy in comparison to alloys with lower conﬁguration
entropy. Correspondingly, potential energy ﬂuctuations in alloys
with higher conﬁguration entropy may not always result in
anomalously slow diffusion kinetics [21].
2.3. Lattice distortion effect
In multi-component alloys solid solution matrix, every atom is
surrounded by another differently sized atom. Due to this atomic
mismatch, the lattice suffers high strain and stress. The difference
in atomic size is the leading cause of such lattice distortion. The
existence of the different neighboring atoms, the different bonding
energy, and the different crystal energy can also cause lattice dis-
tortion. The unsymmetrical bonding and electronic structure can
be seen unevenly in the lattice site [20]. Thus, it has been con-
ﬁrmed that the lattice distortion in HEAs is more severe than in
the conventional alloy. It is also noted that the strain energy is
related to the lattice distortion, which promotes the overall free
energy of the HEA. Due to the phenomenon, the properties of
HEA have been affected severely. The occurrence of solid solution
strengthening is reasoned for the lattice distortion, which is mainly
due to the hindering of dislocation movements. In addition, it also
affects the electrical and thermal conductivity [17,22]. The atomic
size difference is the major cause of the lattice distortion. Thus, an
understanding of this parameter is essential.
The study of lattice distortion is not limited to experimental
work; it has also attained several computation analysis milestones.
The consequence of lattice distortion on HEAs’ elasticity has been
studied widely; mainly, molecular dynamics and molecular statics
have been used for this purpose. Recently, Yen et al. have
employed another model named Lennard-Jones to regulate the
strata of lattice distortion. In this study, the effect on young’s mod-
ulus and Poisson ratio has been calculated in various directions.
Finally, the work suggested that the Lennard-Jones model is insuf-
ﬁcient to predict the elastic properties until the electron density
consistency is not considered [23]. Wang et al. have experimented
with evaluating the outcome of lattice distortion on the diffusion
behavior of HEA via oxidation behavior. The work ﬁnding could
be exploited to adapt to HEAs’ diffusion and oxidation behavior
by governing the lattice distortion [24].

2.4. Cocktail effect
In 2003, Ranganathan proposed for the ﬁrst time for publication
the ‘‘alloyed pleasures: multimetallic cocktails.” The article sug-
gested that the properties of the alloy are not limited to the prop-
erties of the individual elements. In contrast, the alloy shows
composite properties due to mutual interactions between all the
constituent elements. The composite properties also differ from
the unpredictable mixture rules. The properties can be seen as
comprehensive, from an atomic scale to a micro-scale multiphase
composite effect [25].
Further, yeh et al. presented the work in 2006, which demon-
strated the cocktail effect. The experiment shows that the Alu-
minium addition helps phase transformation from FCC to BCC in
AlXCoCrCuFeNi HEAs [26]. Moreover, the addition of Al also
increases the hardness of the HEA while Al is soft and has a low
melting point material. Afterward, Senkov et al. presented another
example of a cocktail effect. The authors evaluated NbMoTaWV
and VNbMoTaW refractory high entropy alloy with a melting tem-
perature above 2600 ℃ as per the rule of mixture. However, the
alloy has more softening resistance than superalloys and shows
high yield strength above 400 MPa at 1600 ℃. The refractory alloy
has excellent potential for high-temperature applications [27,28].
Besides these core effects, recently, yang et al. proposed a Local
Atomic Conﬁguration (LAC) as a core effect mainly due to the
essential difference in local chemical conﬁguration at the nanos-
cale [29]. Thus, these effects of the HEAs could be understood
brieﬂy by presenting different core effects in Fig. 4 .
3. Processing routes for a high entropy alloy
The processing of the material to develop a high entropy alloy is
not much different from conventional alloy. There are several ways
to manufacture the HEAs in which the widely used methods are
casting, sputtering, splat quenching, and powder metallurgy [13].
Apart from these, High-pressure Torsion (HPT) has also been used
to develop nanocrystalline materials with high strength and struc-
ture stability [31]. Commonly, the development of the HEAs has
been classiﬁed into three categories, i.e., melting and solidiﬁcation
(Arc melting, die casting and induction melting), powder metal-
lurgy (mechanical alloying and sintering), and deposition (plasma
spraying, sputtering) [32]. The mentioned categories can be
described in a further section. In addition, Table 2S (Appendix A)
represents the phase formation of different alloys due to different
processing routes and proposed applications. The coarse as-cast
grains and/or second phases are frequently broken and reﬁned
via thermo-mechanical processing. However, because the
improved structure’s dislocation storage capacity was diminished,
increased strength was nearly invariably accompanied by lower
work hardenability. To optimize both strength and ductility, a rea-
sonable design of the thermo-mechanical processing is required.
Because heat treatment had a limited inﬂuence on mechanical
qualities, a variety of procedures were used to strengthen the
materials by inducing the plasticity of the HEAs, including rolling,
forging, tension, compression, and torsion. Deformation and subse-
quent annealing might optimize thermal–mechanical processes,
resulting in grain reﬁnement and the formation of the strengthen-
ing phase.
3.1. Melting and solidiﬁcation route
Classically, melting and solidiﬁcation are extensively used for
synthesizing the alloy. The melting and casting route has been
used to produce the HEAs since starting. The Vacuum Arc Melting
(VAM) technique is the most favorable method [33-36], especially
to achieve a high temperature. Since there is variation in the melt-
ing point of individual elements in the HEAs, the method worked
for most metals. However, the most signiﬁcant disadvantage of
the method is the evaporation of the low boiling point metals;
thus, it is difﬁcult to maintain the compositional control in the
HEA [37]. The melting and casting route has several advantages:
less time consumption, cost-effectiveness, and energy-saving.
Segregation at high temperatures has certain limitations, like den-
drites and interdendritic formation [11]. The cooling rate during
the solidiﬁcation process has played a vital role in altering the
microstructure and properties. The dendrites and volume fraction’s
growth in the interdendritic region and dispersion of the alloying
elements could be seen in several works [38].
3.2. Powder metallurgy route
Powder metallurgy is the net shaping or near-net shaping man-
ufacturing process, which combines the shape-making technology
and design properties during the consolidating [39]. It is a solid-
state powder processing technique involving repeated welding,
fracturing, and rewelding of powder particles by the high-energy
ball mill [40]. Mechanical alloying (MA) also involves the diffusion
of species into one another to form a homogenous alloy. As a result,
several advanced materials could be formed, such as nanomaterial,
intermetallic, quasicrystal, and amorphous [37]. Thus, the MA has
several advantages and is known for its versatility. The method is
also helpful for several hard, brittle materials and ductile metal
alloys. A ﬁne-milled powder must be consolidated to form a bulk
or dense pellet for further characterization. Spark Plasma Sintering
(SPS) is the widely used method for consolidating pulverized pow-
der [41]. There are several other methods used for the purpose, like
Vacuum Hot Pressing Sintering (VHPS) [35], Microwave sintering
[42,43], and Hot Isostatic Pressing (HIP) [44].
The Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS) is one of the novels, high-tech,
and proﬁtable techniques to consolidate the powder and convert it
into bulk material, which involves simultaneous heating and press-
ing the material. The SPS’s unique features are the lower holding
time and lower operating temperatures than conventional casting
and laser diffusion techniques. Short holding time and low temper-
ature lead to lesser grain growth. Eventually, the SPSed product
obtained a better microstructure with an improved mechanical
property [45]. It is frequently seen that the alloy made by MA fol-
lowed by SPS has excellent mechanical properties as compared to
VAM products. Kumar et al. showed differentiation in the material
processing route. The investigation involved CoCrCuFeNiSi x HEA
synthesis by arc melting and MA, followed by the SPS technique.
The FCC + r phase forms via SPS while only the FCC phase forms
through VAM, which has increased the Si content to some extent.
The study suggested that the SPSed sample exhibits better
mechanical properties than the VAM sample, mainly due to the
r phase formation during the SPS process [46]. An outline of the
application area of mechanical alloying has been given in Fig. 5 .

4. High entropy alloy for high-temperature applications
Presently, most efforts have been expended to develop the
HEAs, staying in critical environments such as high temperatures
and hot corrosion atmospheres. The alloy should not only with-
stand these conditions but should also exhibit excellent mechani-
cal behavior. Various engineering components are used at a high
temperature. Due to the application at high temperatures, studying
the material behavior at working temperature has been crucial. We
also need to modify the material used for these applications for
better performance as these components’ performance depends
on temperature. For example, for any heat engine, working capac-
ity (i.e., the efﬁciency) increases with a rise in working tempera-
ture, and similarly for jet engines [55]. Another positive aspect of
high temperature is reducing fuel use in various industries like
nuclear, oil and gas, etc. There are many more such high-
temperature applications, and hence the high-temperature mate-
rial is the need of the hour. Various Ni-based superalloys are being
used for high-temperature applications. However, due to the melt-
ing point being close to 1300 ℃, the applications are limited to
working temperatures in the range of 1160 and 1277 /C176C [1,56].
Thus, various properties of HEAs at high temperatures will be dis-
cussed in this section.
4.1. The HEAs resistance to oxidation
It is essential to inspect the oxidation behavior of various mate-
rials at higher working temperatures; materials tend to react and
get oxidized and degrade their properties, which can be detrimen-
tal to the applications. Various studies have been performed on the
oxidation behavior of HEAs. The resistance of the alloy
AlSiTiCrFeCoNiMo
0.5 and AlSiTiCrFeNiMo 0.5 against oxidation was
a crucial element in Huang et al. [57], which mainly concentrated
on the processing and microstructure of the alloys. The conclusion
that was drawn out from the study was that the reason for the
excellent oxidation resistance of these materials lies in the pres-
ence of the chromium-based layer beneath the outermost titanium
oxide scale.
Butler et al. examined Al-Co-Cr-Ni-(Fe or Si) multi-component
High-Entropy Alloys [58]. Arc Melting technique (raw materials
with purity /C21 99.5%) was utilized to produce alloys. The alloy but-
tons with the composition of Al 10Cr22.5Co22.5Ni22.5Fe22.5 (AL10F),
Al20Cr25Co25Ni25Si5 (AL20S), and Al 15Cr10Co35Ni35Si5 (AL15S) were
manufactured on a water-cooled copper hearth under an inert
argon atmosphere. Each button was remelted with several ties to
enhance uniformity. Oxidation tests (at constant temperature)
were conducted in a normal room atmosphere at 1050 /C176C on as-
cast specimens by varying the duration of tests from 1 h to

can replace superalloys in various high-temperature applications.
HEA possesses excellent high-temperature properties, making
them a better choice for various applications like jet engines,
nuclear industry, oil, and gas industries. The HEAs shows good oxi-

attributed to passive oxide-forming elements in alloys like Cr, Al,
and Ti. Their passive oxide layer protects the material from further
oxidation at high temperatures. Thus, it was conﬁrmed by many
studies done by researchers on various alloys, as discussed above.
Strength has been a signiﬁcant drawback for many materials at
high temperatures as they become unstable and lose strength.
HEAs show excellent high-temperature strength. The crystal

like Ti, Ta, Nb, Mo, etc. They exhibit exceptional high-High-

7. Future direction
Since the high-temperature application is the prime require-
ment of industries for better performance of the components, a
RHEA could play a signiﬁcant role in the purpose. Thus, RHEAs
have been extensively investigated by the HEA communities. How-
ever, RHEA production is much costlier compared to conventional
HEAs.
Instead of employing all refractory elements in a HEA, a few
refractory elements, W, Ti, Mo, and Nb, would be used along with
the conventional elements. The idea could reduce the production
cost of RHEA drastically. The resultant properties can show signif-
icant remarks. By tuning the refractory element’s content, we could
achieve the desired properties up to some extent at a lower pro-
duction cost. An extensive amount of research work is going under
the umbrella of HEAs. That can make the RHEA a suitable and
proper choice in the near future as it can sustain high working tem-
peratures, which provides us with better efﬁciency. Concluding the
various studies, the refractory high entropy alloy (RHEA) exhibits
excellent high-temperature properties, be it stability, corrosion
resistance, or strength. Hence these materials can be beneﬁcial in
the industrial sector as they would enhance efﬁciency and provide
a path for further research in this area. Various RHEAs have been
analyzed to ﬁnd an excellent mechanical property in previous
investigations.
The refractory high-entropy alloys, which are mostly made up
of refractory elements, have the best potential for high-
temperature strength. In addition, single-phase systems were the
ﬁrst representative RHEAs, which showed some enhanced perfor-
mance but had quite high densities. A lightweight refractory high
entropy alloy (LWRHEA) with low density and high speciﬁc yield
strength was recently studied. In compression, however, it had
poor room-temperature plasticity, with work softening beginning
practically soon after yielding, indicating breaking from the com-
mencement of plastic deformation. These lightweight composi-
tions should be researched further in order to ﬁnd strategies to
increase room-temperature plasticity. As a result, these alloys
become less expensive and have higher strength, making them
ideal for future industrial applications.
