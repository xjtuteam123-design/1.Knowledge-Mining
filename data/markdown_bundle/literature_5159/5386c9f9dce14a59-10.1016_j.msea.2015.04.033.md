Abstract 
The  precipitate plays a critical role in improving the mechanical properties of Ni -based 
superalloys. An undesirable phenomenon referred to as the Portevin -Le Chatelier (PLC) effect 
always appears in Ni -based superalloys deformed within specific ranges of str ain rate and 
temperature. In order to systematically investigate the influence of the  precipitates on the PLC 
effect, four Ni -based superalloys with various  contents were designed and fabricated. 
Microscopic observations from transmission electron mi croscopy (TEM) indicated that the 
volume fraction of the  phase was consistent with the designed value. Furthermore, analysis of 
energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) results revealed that the  matrix of all the alloys consisted 
of the same components. U niaxial tensile tests were performed at strain rates and temperatures 
ranging from 1×10 -4-3×10-3 s-1 and 300-500C, respectively. We found that the ultimate strength 
increased while the elongation decreased with increasing  content. In addition, the serr ation 
changed from type A to type B and to type C with increasing temperature, decreasing strain rate or 
increasing  content; the amplitude of type B serrations was described by unimodal or bimodal 
distributions. Increasing volume fraction of  precipitates shifted the region in which the PLC 
effect occurred, to the range of low temperatures and high strain rates. Moreover, the serration 
amplitude increased with increasing  content at a given temperature, which indicated that the  
precipitate increases the dynamic strain ageing (DSA) effect. 
Key words:  precipitate;  content; Ni-based superalloy; Dynamic strain ageing; Portevin –Le 
Chatelier effect

2] and are therefore, extensively used in the aerospace and aviation industry. The  precipitates, 
which are dispersed in the  matrix, play a significant role in enhancing the strength and 
influencing the creep behavior of metal alloys. However, previous studies have shown that 
repeated serrations in the plastic regime are a predominant featu re in the deformation of 
superalloys at intermediate or elevated temperatures, over a broad range of strain rates [3-5]. 
Jerky flow or plastic instabilit y, referred to as the Portevin-Le Chatelier (PLC) effect, results 
in serration of the loading curve and simultaneous localization of deformation in PLC bands. The 
PLC effect is manifested as the formation of staircases in the strain -time curves of a locali zed 
region on the specimen [6, 7]. On the micro -scale, the PLC effect is generally attributed to 
dynamic strain ageing (DSA) [8-10], which stems from dynamic pinning and unpinning 
interactions between mobile dislocations and diffusing solute atoms during plastic flow. This 
theory stipulates that the mobile dislocations are blocked and released repeate dly by the solutes, 
resulting in repeated serrations in the stress -strain curves. Penning proposed a phenomenological 
model, which is mathematically based on negative strain rate sensibility (nSRS), to explain the 
occurrence of inhomogeneous plastic flow w ith the PLC effect [11]. According to this model, the 
strain rate at any point in the specimen varies with the stress, i.e., the strain rate must jump 
whenever the stress leaps. The increase in the strain rate and the corresponding severe deformation, 
leading to bands, are associated with the unpinning effects in DSA; these unpinning effects result 
from dislocation motion. 
The PLC effect has been observed repeatedly [6, 12-15] since Portevin and Le Chatelier 
made the first observation during tensile testing of Al -Cu alloys [16]. This plastic instability was 
also recently observed in superalloys subjected to specific conditions [3-5, 17]. As a source of 
potential force barriers, precipitates contribute significantly to enhancing the strength of materials, 
especially in superalloys where they hinder dislocation motion [18]. In fact, from the viewpoint of 
dislocation arrest, the precipitates may exert a similar influence to that of s olute atoms in the PLC 
effect. 
Brechet and Estrin discovered a new phenomenon, commonly referred as the pseudo -PLC 
effect, where serrations result from deformation -induced dissolution and re -precipitation of 
strengthening precipitates [19, 20]. The pseudo -PLC effect differs entirely from the DSA effect. 
However, Chmelik demonstrated that shearing precipitates, which are considered to be responsible

## Page 4

for serrated flow in Al alloys by other re searchers, might not be directly supported by acoustic 
emission (AE) [21]. Furthermore, Pink used various annealing and ageing treatments to 
investigate the effect of precipitat es in Al -Li alloys, and proposed that the  precipitates are 
possible sites for dislocation pile-ups during particle shearing; this new mechanism concurred with 
the experimental results of Chmelik [22]; i.e., the serration induced by precipitates was attributed 
to significant dislocation motion through a series of passages resulti ng from sheared precipitates. 
In addition, Sun explored the influence of solute clouds and precipitates on the spatiotemporal 
characteristics of the PLC effect in A2024 Al alloy, which underwent various solution treatments. 
The mechanism proposed in that w ork outlined the competitive roles of precipitates and solutes, 
and indicated that the former has a significant influence on the latter as well as on DSA effects 
[23]. 
However, the mechanism governing the influence of precipitates on DSA effects is still not 
completely understood. The precipitates in the aforementioned studies were controlled by 
modifying the heat-treatment regime. More importantly, there is a significan t difference between 
the chemical components and precipitates of Ni -based superalloys and Al alloys. For example, 
maximum precipitate volume fractions of 80% in the former, may be unattainable in the latter. 
The  precipitate content of Ni -based superall oys increases, in general, with increasing solute 
concentration of the matrix. Consequently, altering the solution treatment may not be suitable for 
evaluating, solely, the effect of  precipitates on the PLC effect in Ni-based superalloys. Although 
the smelting of Ni -base superalloys with different volume fractions of precipitates has been 
studied, the effect of  precipitates in the DSA regime has not been systematically investigated. 
Based on the literature available on smelting alloys [1], we fabricated four Ni -based 
superalloys with different  contents in order to systematically investigate the influence of  
precipitates on the PLC effect. This work focuses on macroscopic analyses of the strengthening 
features and serration characteristics. The macroscopic analyses and calculation of the activation 
energy allowed us to determine the possible effect of precipitates on the mechanical properties, 
serration morphology, and diffusion characteristics in the DSA effect.

3.2. Tensile Properties 
3.2.1. Strengthening features with  content 
For the sake of convenience, the 15%  alloy was used to show the dependence of the 
strength on the strain rate and temperature. Nominal stress-strain curves were obtained at different 
temperatures, under a constant imposed strain rate (Fig. 4a); curves were also measured at a 
constant temperature and various imposed strain rates (Fig. 4b). As Fig. 4a shows, thermal 
softening is man ifested as decreasing ultimate strength with increasing temperature. In contrast, 
Fig.4b reveals small changes in the elongation and the ultimate strength at various strain rates; 
these small changes are characteristic of strain rate insensitivity. Further more, the serrations in 
Fig.4a and Fig.4b are indicative of nSRS. The characteristic negative strain rate hardening is, 
however, not observed in the curves measured at various strain rates. 
The three -containing alloys exhibit similar characteristics. F ig. 5a and Fig. 5b summarize 
the variations in the ultimate strength (Fig.5a) and the elongation (Fig.5b), respectively, with the  
content, temperature, and strain rate. Second -phase strengthening in the four alloys is manifested 
as morphologically upwar d serrations (Fig.5a) and downward staircases ( Fig.5b), as denoted by

## Page 7

the red dashed lines. The trends revealed in Fig. 5a and Fig. 5b also confirm the occurrence of 
thermal softening and strain rate insensitivity. 
In a certain range of temperatures, the s trength of  precipitates increases, in general, only 
slightly with increasing temperature [27]. The strength of the grain boundary and the  matrix of 
the alloys are, however, sensitive to the temperature. The increased strength of the  precipitates 
with increasing tempe rature might not compensate for the decreased strength of the grain 
boundary and the  matrix. Moreover, the solution temperature is significantly higher than the 
temperatures used in the experiments. The solution strengthening stemming from the 
concentration of solid solution atoms should, therefore, have only a small effect on the hardening 
behavior exhibited in the stress-strain curves. On the other hand, with increasing temperature, slip 
systems are easily activated owing to cross -slip, which results in softening of the material and a 
subsequent decrease in the ultimate strength. This is confirmed by examining the dislocation 
morphology of the 5%  alloy tensile-tested at different temperatures, under a constant strain rate 
of 3×10-4 s-1. Comparing Fig. 6a and Fig. 6b reveals the presence of a higher number of 
slip-systems for testing performed at 500 C than at 300 C; this indicates that elevated 
temperatures promote cross-slip and thereby increase the number of activated slip bands. As such, 
the thermal softening observed in Fig. 4 is attributed primarily to matrix softening. 
3.2.2. Second-phase strengthening 
The strengthening ability of second -phase particles is examined by evaluating the 
stress-strain curves (Fig. 7a) obtained at a constant imposed strain rate and temperature of 1× 10-4 
s-1 and 500 C, respectively, and varying  volume fractions; the variation in the ultimate stress 
and elongation with different experimental conditions (  content, temperature, and strain rate) is 
summarized in Fig.7 b and Fig.7c. As previously shown in section 3.2.1, the ultimate strength 
increases and the elongation decreases with increasing volume fraction of  precipitates; this 
simultaneous increase and decrease exhibit approximately linear dependence on the temp eratures 
and strain rates. 
From the particular viewpoint of the arrested dislocations, it is not difficult to understand the 
approximately linear dependence shown in Fig. 7 .  precipitates, as a kind of dispersed, 
small-scale (with diameter of 10-50 nm) particles in  matrix, have a significant effect on the 
ultimate strength; simultaneously, these precipitates hinder dislocation motion, thereby resulting in

3.3. Serration Characteristics: morphology and distribution 
3.3.1. Morphology 
Fig. 8 shows the stress-strain curves obtained at different  contents (Fig. 8a), temperatures 
(Fig. 8c), and strain rates ( Fig. 8e); magnified views of the data in section A, section B, and 
section C are shown as Fig. 8b, Fig. 8d, and Fig. 8f, respectively. A s the figure shows, the 
serration type and amplitude in the loading curves vary with the  content, experiment 
temperature, and strain rate. Furthermore, from the viewpoint of morphology, the serration 
changes continuously from type A to type B and then to type C. The serration amplitude changes 
in accordance with the aforementioned trend. 
We obtained a schematic of the serra tion type distribution (Fig. 9) by t aking all of the 
experimental conditions and their corresponding serration types into account. Fig. 9 shows the 
variation of the serration type as a function of the strain rate, temperature, and  content; the  
content has a significant influence on the serration type. In fact, the region in which the PLC effect 
occurs shifts to low temperatures and high strain rates with increasing  content. The schematic 
also shows that the DSA regime shifts to the bottom ri ght with increasing  content. On the 
micro-scale,  precipitates hinder dislocation motion and thereby contribute significantly to 
enhancing the material strength. Therefore, the resistance to mobile dislocations increases with 
increasing precipitate co ntent of the alloys, and hence significant energy is required for 
dislocations to break through the induced Cottrell atmospheres. As a result, the arrested mobile 
dislocations in alloys with high  contents participate in only two processes. After attaini ng 
sufficient energy via thermal activation, dislocations that are insufficiently arrested by the  
precipitates might break through obstacles and hence, generate unpinning effects and downward 
serrations in the loading curves. However, even with adequate thermal energy, sufficiently arrested 
dislocations are unable to break away from the barriers of solutes and  precipitates; this inability 
to break away results in pinning effects and smooth loading curves. Therefore, under appropriate 
conditions, type C serrations are easily generated in specimens with high  contents. The effect of 
the  precipitates results in a shift of the DSA regime to the low -temperature high -strain-rate 
region, as shown in Fig. 9. 
3.3.2. Distribution of serration amplitude

## Page 9

High temperature, low strain rate, and high  content can each lead to type C serrations and a 
high concentration of clustered solutes at the dislocations during the effective waiting time. Type 
C serrations are associated with high average strength of both pinning and unpinning in alloys, and, 
as such, have larger amplitudes than those of their type A or B counterparts [28, 29]. Fig. 10 
confirms that the different serration types have significantly different average amplitudes and 
that the stress drop of the type C serration is substantially larger than those of type A and B. 
A large serration amplitude is indicative of a strong DSA effect, which stems from dislocation 
arrest by solutes. According to Jiang [13], the serration ampli tude (or the stress drop) may be 
expressed as: Δσ = γCs, where γ is the pinning strength of the solutes and Cs , which is 
determined by the effective ageing time, denotes the solute concentration surrounding the mobile 
dislocation line. Previous studies have shown that the blocking effect induced by diffusing solutes 
increases with increasing temperatures, decreasing strain rates or increasing  contents. In other 
words, Cs increases as the serration type changes from type A to type B and then to type C. If the 
coefficient γ is assumed to be constant, then a higher amplitude is obtained for the type C 
serration, compared to those of type A and B, as indicated by the results shown in Fig. 10. 
The distribution of serration amplitude with different experim ental conditions was also 
examined, as illustrated by the histograms in Fig. 11. The amplitudes of type A serrations are 
described by a power -law distribution whereas their type B or C counterparts are described by 
both unimodal and bimodal peak -shaped dis tributions. These observations are somewhat 
consistent with previous studies on Al alloys [30, 31]. The type B serrations constitute the main 
difference between the results obtained in this work and those of previous studies; i.e., in previous 
studies, the type B serrations were described by an intermediate distribution, which corresponds to 
a shifting of the power -law distribution to the small stress -drop range. However, the type B 
serrations in this work were described by a peak-shaped distribution.

However, the serration characteristics were heavily influenced by the fraction of  precipitates

Our findings concur with those of Chmelik that precipitates contribute to DSA effects. For 
example, the temperature and strain rate lead to accelerated diffusion of solutes and multiplication 
of mobile dislocations, respectively. However,  precipitates enhance the pinning effect by acting 
as additional obstacles to mobile dislocations, and thereby result in increased ageing time. 
Therefore, the mobile dislocations may become sufficiently aged with the aid of  precipitates. 
The  precipitates also promote solute atom aggregation at the mobile dislocations. As such, the 
serration amplitude increases with increasing  phase content, as stipulated in the model proposed 
by Jiang. 
4. Conclusions 
Ni-based superalloys with various  contents were designed and fabricated in order to 
systematically study the role of  precipitates in the PLC effect. These alloys were subjected to 
uniaxial tensile tests at various temperatures ( 300C-500C) and strain rates ( 1×10-4-3×10-3 s-1). 
The tensile mechanical properties were determined from stress -strain curves, which provided a 
statistical analysis of thermal softening, strain -rate insensitivity, and precipitated -phase 
strengthening. Investigation of jerky flow at the selected temperatures and strain rates revealed the 
following: 
1)  precipitates have a pronounced effect on the serration characteristics and shift the 
region in which the PLC effect occurs, to low temperatures and high strain rates. 
2) The amplitude of type B serrations is described by a peak-shaped distribution. 
3) With lengthening the effective ageing time,  precipitates promote the DSA effect to be 
aggravated.
