Abstract 
A grain refined FeCrNiAlTi 
0.2 high entropy alloy (HEA) was firstly produced by 
powder metallurgy, and the alloy combines well with high strength, good ductility and 
low cost. The sintered HEA exhibits a major body-centered cubic (BCC) matrix and a 
minor ordered BCC (B2) phase. The alloy possesses a n excellent properties, reaching 
the yield strength of 1877 MPa and plastic strain o f 26.6% at room temperature.

Compared with the current FeCrNi-based HEAs, the co mpressive strength is 
improved distinctly without sacrificing plastic str ain. The analysis of strengthening 
mechanism suggests that the main strengthening cont ribution is the grain boundary 
strengthening. 
Keywords: high entropy alloys; grain boundary strengthening; powder metallurgy; 
mechanical properties; scanning electron microscopy. 
1. Introduction 
For traditional alloys, to prevent the production of intermetallic compounds, they 
are primarily based on one principal element, and j ust a slight amount of other 
alloying elements are added to control microstructu res for better properties[1][2]. In 
2004, a new type of multi-component alloys, termed as high entropy alloys (HEAs), 
was proposed by Yeh and Cantor et al.[3][4]. Import antly, the majority of HEA 
systems form a stable body-centered cubic (BCC) and face-centered cubic (FCC) 
solid solutions for their high entropy of mixing [5 ][6]. In terms of properties, HEAs 
possess excellent mechanical properties [7][8–10], outstanding resistance of oxidation, 
wear and corrosion [11–13], hence it has becoming a studying focus for great 
potential applications. 
To date, numerous methods have been applied to prep are HEAs, such as 
arc-melting [4], electro-deposition [14], laser cladding [15], magnetron sputtering [16] 
and so on. Among these approaches, the arc-melting is suitable to prepare the bulk 
metallic alloy [17][18]. However, for arc-melting, the composition segregation and 
dendritic structure formed during solidification ca n influence the mechanical

## Page 5

properties tremendously, and even following treatme nts are required [19][20]. So 
mechanical alloying combining with hot-press sinter ing was proposed to prepare bulk 
HEAs [21][22]. Grain refinement and homogeneous com position can be realized by 
mechanical alloying, and hot-press sintering can co nsolidate powders to form stable 
solid solution with high density. 
Among plenty of HEAs systems reported so far, FeCrN i-based has aroused wide 
interests for an excellent combination of good prop erties and low-cost[9][23–25]. 
Additionally Al and Ti were to improve the strength because the two elements can 
induce large lattice strain and stress for their larger atomic radius among the elements, 
and high Al content was to avoid brittle intermetal lic of FeAl while a slight addition 
of Ti is functioned as strengthening for forming fine precipitates[22][26,27]. 
Therefore, a novel FeCrNiAlTi 0.2 HEA was prepared by powder metallurgy. The 
microstructure and mechanical properties were explo red systematically. The alloy 
reached quite high strength and plastic strain comp aring with other reported 
FeCrNi-based HEAs. The strength mechanism was also analyzed. This new type of 
HEA get well along with the concept of cost-effecti ve design, and the study would 
further broaden the potential application for HEAs in high strength alloys. 
2. Experimental details 
High-purity Fe, Cr, Ni, Al and Ti ( >99.5wt %) powders were used to synthesize 
FeCrNiAlTi 0.2 HEA via mechanically alloying (MA) combining with vacuum 
hot-press sintering (VHPS). The initial particle size was about 45 µm (-325 mesh). The 
MA was carried out in a high energy planetary ball milling at rotation speed of 350

## Page 6

rpm under Ar atmosphere, the ball-to-powder weight ratio of 10:1. The milling was 
break 5 min every 30 min. After milling in Ar for 3 5 h, 3wt% ethanol was injected 
into the vial as a process controlling agency (PCA) to prevent cold welding during 
milling, and the powder was subsequently milled for 5 h. When milling was finished, 
the milled powder was dried in a vacuum oven, and t hen the powder was allow to 
pass through a 75 µm sieve. Subsequently, the milled powder was consol idated using 
VHPS at 1150 °C for 2 h with heating rate of 10 °C/min, and a constant uniaxial 
pressure of 50 MPa was used during the sintering. T he structure of the samples were 
characterized by X-ray diffractometry (XRD) with a Cu K α radiation. Scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM) with an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS) was to 
observe morphology of bulk alloy. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was also 
used for further structural characterization. Vicke rs micro-hardness was tested, the 
applied load and dwell time were 0.5kgf and 10s res pectively. The compressive 
strength was tested using an Instron5569 testing system at a strain rate of 1 × 10 -3 s-1.

4. Conclusions 
The novel FeCrNiAlTi 0.2 HEA was firstly fabricated via powder metallurgy w ith 
relative density of 97.42%. The grain size of bulk HEA is about 174.1 nm, the alloy is 
composed of a major BCC enriched in Fe-Cr and a min or B2 enriched in Ni-Al solid 
solution phases with Ti distributed. The FeCrNiAlTi 0.2 HEA has an excellent 
mechanical properties, the hardness is 643 HV 0.5 , the yield strength, the compressive 
strength and plastic strain are 1877 MPa, 2745 MPa and 26.6% respectively. The 
analysis of strengthening suggests that grain refin ed strengthening and dislocation 
strengthening occupy a dominant position in the all oy, and the oxides due to surface 
contamination triggered the dispersion strengthenin g is beneficial to high strength 
HEAs. Compared with other FeCrNi-based HEAs, the Fe CrNiAlTi 0.2 reaches a 
balance between strength and plastic strain. The re lationship between composition 
and properties of the HEAs via powder metallurgy is required to consider in the future 
research.
