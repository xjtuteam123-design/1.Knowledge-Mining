The microstructure and mechanical properties of Mg–9RY–4Zn (wt.%) alloy were investigated. In the
as-extruded
 condition, the typical microstructure is plenty of ﬁne DRX grains and 14H long period stack-
ing
 ordered (LPSO) structure with two forms: the secondary phase with bending long strip morphology
distributed

ing
 LPSO structure. The as-extruded Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy exhibits high strength and ductility whether at
room

353

gains
 containing LPSO lamellae and the bending LPSO strips along the direction of extrusion with good
characteristics
 such as high microhardness, plasticity and thermal stability as well as good combination
with
 the Mg matrix are mainly responsible for the excellent mechanical properties of this alloy.

1. Introduction
Magnesium alloys, as the lightest metallic structural materi-
als, have great potential applications in automotive, aerospace and
electronic industries because of their low density, high speciﬁc
strength, good damping capacity and easy recycling [1–5]. How-
ever, the inherently weak properties of Mg alloys such as low
strength, inferior ductility and poor thermal stability seriously hin-
der their wide applications [6,7].
Therefore, increasing efforts have been devoted to further
improving the comprehensive mechanical properties of magne-
sium alloy. Magnesium alloys containing high content of rare earth
(RE) elements have received tremendous attention due to their high
strength at both room and elevated temperatures. For example,
the Mg–RE based alloys, Mg–10Gd–2Nd [8], Mg–10Gd–3Y–Zr [9],
Mg–10Gd–6Y–0.6Mn [10] and Mg–10Gd–3Y–0.4Zr [11], exhibit
signiﬁcant superior strength compared with the conventional
magnesium alloys. However, these precipitation hardening alloys
increase their strength at the expense of ductility in most cases
and their elongation is usually less than 5% at room temperature
[12,13]. Therefore, the applications of those Mg–RE alloys are still
limited because of their inferior ductility [14,15].

Zn is one of the most important alloying elements which can
further improve strengthening response of Mg–RE alloys [16]. Var-
ious novel structures of LPSO phases, including 6H, 10H, 14H, 18R
and 24R, have been observed in the Mg–RE–Zn (RE = Y, Gd, Tb, Dy,
Ho, Er, Tm) system [17–19]. Recently, LPSO phases, as the important
strengthening sources, have been generally acknowledged [20–26].
The optimal microstructural constituents are ultra-ﬁne grains com-
bined with reﬁned LPSO phase which could result in super high
mechanical properties [23,27]. The typical example is Mg97Zn1Y2
(at.%) alloy prepared by rapidly solidiﬁed powder metallurgy (RS
P/M) process which exhibits a high yield strength of ∼600 MPa and
an elongation of about 5% at room temperature [28]. Recently, it
is very appealing to prepare alloys with the LPSO structure com-
bined with ﬁne grains by a conventional preparation method. As
we all known, hot extrusion is one of the industrial production pro-
cesses and is considered to be an effective way of microstructure
reﬁnement.
Yttrium-rich misch metal (RY) extracted from ore is abundant
in China, and its price is much lower than that of pure yttrium
(Y) metal and gadolinium (Gd) metal which are usually used in
Mg–RE based alloys. Moreover, considering that some other rare
earth metals coexist in RY and the synergetic strengthening effects
of these elements may be more beneﬁcial to the performance
improvement [29]. On the other hand, RY has been seldom used
in magnesium alloys except in WE [30] (WE43, WE54) series up
to now. Therefore, it is signiﬁcant to try to develop the application
of RY to obtain the low cost and high mechanical properties Mg
alloys.

2.
 Experimental procedures
The investigated alloy with actual composition of
Mg–8.3RY–3.8Zn (wt.%) was prepared from commercial pure
Mg (99.95 wt.%), Zn (99.95 wt.%) and Mg–20 wt.% RY master alloy
in an electric resistant furnace under the protection of a mixed
atmosphere of CO2 and SF6. The alloy melt was homogenized at
about 750 ◦C for 0.5 h. Then it was poured into a water-cooled
mould at approximately 720 ◦C. The ingot was homogenized at
500 ◦C for 12 h. Then it was extruded at 460 ◦C with an extrusion
ratio of about 17. The commercial AZ31, as a reference alloy, was
also prepared and processed at the same extrusion ratio. The
main chemical compositions of the investigated alloy and the RY
were determined by inductively coupled plasma atomic emission
spectroscope (ICP-AES). Table 1 lists the main compositions of RY.
Only Y content was analyzed in the investigated alloy and the RY
content was calculated according to its composition.
The microstructure, phase structure and composition of the
alloy were characterized by optical microscope (OM), scanning
electron microscope (SEM) equipped with an X-ray energy-
dispersive spectrometer (EDS), X-ray diffraction (XRD) and
transmission electron microscope (TEM). The thin foil samples
for the TEM observation were prepared using the argon ion thin-
ning technique. The grain size was determined by liner intercept
method. Microhardness was measured by Vickers hardness (Hv)
testing machine with the load of 50 g and the loading time of 10 s.
The specimens for tensile test are dumbbell test pieces with the
gauge size of 35 mm × 5 mm × 3 mm and their length direction is
parallel to the extrusion direction. The tensile tests were carried
out using a standard tensile testing machine at room and elevated
temperatures under an initial strain rate of 1 × 10−3 s−1. The value
of the tensile tests in the study was the average of at least four
measurements.
3.
 Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
Fig. 1(a) shows the optical microstructure of the as-cast
Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy. It reveals that the as-cast alloy is mainly com-
posed of primary Mg dendrites and intermetallic phase at grain
boundaries or in the interdendritic regions, which is the typical
microstructure of as-cast Mg alloys. However, besides the den-
drites, obviously additional gray regions occupying most of /H9251-Mg
matrix were observed in the as-cast Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy. Fig. 1(b)
is the enlarged SEM image. It demonstrates that the as-cast alloy
mainly consists of ﬁve phases, which are Mg matrix, gray phase
with block-like morphology at the grain boundaries or in the inter-
dendritic regions, ﬁne-lamellar phase (corresponding to the gray
region in Fig. 1(a)) in the grain interior of Mg matrix, very few
amounts of eutectic compound phase and small particulate phase
mostly presenting cuboid shape. Both of the ﬁne-lamellar phase
and the gray block-like phase have a special structure which has
been reported as the LPSO structure. The conﬁrmation of the LPSO
structure is discussed in the following sections. The ﬁne lamellae
grow uniformly and are distributed in the whole grain. The average
dendritic arm spacing (DAS) of the as-cast alloy is 45 ± 5 /H9262m. The
microhardness of the Mg matrix with lamellar LPSO phase (denoted
as Mg′-matrix) is 85.2 ± 4.6 Hv while the value for the block-like
LPSO phase is 97.0 ± 5.8 Hv, which indicates that the block-like
LPSO phase is harder than the Mg′-matrix.
Fig. 2 shows the optical microstructure of the as-extruded
Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy. After hot extrusion, as shown in Fig. 2(a) and
(b) observed in the transverse direction, the gray LPSO phase is
changed to disperse uniformly in the Mg matrix in the shape of
bent block. On the longitudinal section, the LPSO phase presents
long strip shape and is rearranged along the extrusion direction,
as seen in Fig. 2(c) and (d). In addition, it can be found that
many ﬁne dynamic recrystallization (DRX) gains are oriented ran-
domly around the strip-like LPSO phase. Hagihara et al. [31] have
comparatively investigated the Mg/LPSO two-phase Mg97Zn1Y2
(at.%) extruded alloy and Mg-solid-solution Mg99.2Zn0.2Y0.6 (at.%)
extruded alloy whose alloy composition is almost the same as

Fig. 3 shows the magniﬁed SEM images further revealing the
LPSO phase in the as-extruded alloy. Most of the block/strip-like
LPSO phase is bended to some extent. As shown in Fig. 3(b), the
bending angle of the LPSO phase is more than 90◦. The observa-
tion forms a powerful testament that the LPSO phase possesses
high plasticity-toughness as well as good combination with the Mg
matrix. As for the ﬁne LPSO lamellae in the interior of the DRX gains,
all of them are straight and orientated in a same direction in one
grain in the alloy after extrusion, as shown in Fig. 3(c) and (d). This
indicates that this ﬁne-lamellar phase is generated during extru-
sion process instead of casting solidiﬁcation process. Accordingly,
it can be known that the external stress can effectively enhance
the nucleation and growth of the LPSO structure during the ther-
mal deformation process, which was ﬁrst observed and proposed
by Abe et al. [33].
To know what the secondary phases in the Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy
are, the alloys in as-cast and as-extruded conditions were mea-
sured by EDS in SEM mode. Fig. 3(a) shows the SEM image of the
secondary phases in the as-extruded alloy and Fig. 4(a)–(d) present
the corresponding EDS analyses of the points A–D, respectively.
The small irregular particles marked as A are determined to be
the Mg3RY2Zn3 phase because their Zn/RY (in at.%) ratio of 1.48 is
close to 1.5. The Mg3RY2Zn3 particles are the brittle eutectic com-
pounds in the as-cast sample which were crushed during extrusion
[34]. Although the fcc structure Mg3Y2Zn3-type phase is quite com-
mon in Mg–Y–Zn alloys, it is generally not considered as effective
strengthening phase [35]. According to the EDS results, the cuboid-
shaped phase marked as B is Mg24(RY, Zn)5, and both the bent
block-like phase marked as C and ﬁne-lamellar phase marked as
D can be inferred as the LPSO Mg12RYZn. XRD results of the alloys
are shown in Fig. 5. Only the Mg12YZn-type compound can be deter-
mined due to the too small amount of the Mg3RY2Zn3 and Mg24(RY,
Zn)5 phases, and in view of their trace, it is believed that the two
phases have little effect on the performance of this alloy.

LPSO phase and dislocations, and suggested that the formation of
LPSO phase increases the critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) of the
basal plane so as to hinder the basal slip which is beneﬁcial to the
strengthening of the alloys. In our alloy, the high volume fraction of
the LPSO phase including the bending block and the ﬁne lamellae
has been obtained, which is believed to effectively hinder the basal
slip and signiﬁcant for the strengthening of alloy. Furthermore, lots
of c + a dislocations piling up at interfaces between the LPSO phase
and the /H9251-Mg matrix were observed as shown in Fig. 6(b), which
is also considered to respond to the increase of strength. It has
been reported that the (0 0 0 1) basal slip is the dominant defor-
mation mode in the LPSO phases [39,40]. As shown in Fig. 2(c) and
Fig. 3(c), it is easy to infer that the LPSO strips are well aligned and
the (0 0 0 1) basal plane is roughly parallel to the extrusion direc-
tion of the alloy [31]. For this reason, the Schmid factor for basal slip
is very small in aligned LPSO strips and the operation of basal slip
is strongly limited when loading direction is parallel to the extru-
sion direction. Therefore, the aligned microstructure is considered
to effectively strengthen the extruded alloy. Furthermore, as for the
LPSO phase itself, many excellent characteristics have been found
in the previous [40,41] and present studies. The LPSO phase exhibits
high thermal stability (the reason for obtaining excellent mechani-
cal properties as the temperature up to 300 ◦C in the present work)
[40], elastic modulus (66.7 GPa much higher than 40 GPa for pure
Mg [41,42]), microhardness (especially for the bent LPSO phase as
shown in the above section) and ductility as well as good combina-
tion with the Mg matrix. Among these characteristics, the latter two
ones are considered as the reasons for the high ductility of the as-
extruded Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy. Besides, the visible c + a dislocations
as shown in Fig. 6(b) suggest that the LPSO phase is favorable to
activation of non-basal slip in the Mg matrix and it can contribute
to the improvement of the ductility in the alloy containing LPSO
phase, as was ﬁrst conﬁrmed and proposed by Matsuda et al. [38].
Conclusively, the LPSO phase plays a unique role to overcome the
conﬂicting properties of strength and ductility, which is beneﬁcial
for the comprehensive mechanical properties of the present alloy.
On the other hand, the grain reﬁnement induced by hot-extrusion
and LPSO phase is effective in increasing strength as well as duc-
tility. What is more, a special microstructure of ﬁne DRX grains
containing LPSO lamellae is obtained in the present extruded alloy,
which is considered to be close to the optimal microstructure for
the super high performance of the alloys with LPSO phase although
the grain size of our alloys seems still relatively large [1,28]. It
can be predicted that by controlling and reducing the grain size,
the potential mechanical properties of this extruded alloy can be
further improved.
In addition, the as-extruded Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy exhibits large
elongation of ∼80% at the temperature of 350 ◦C with a strain rate
of 1 × 10−3 s−1. This indicates that the as-extruded Mg–9RY–4Zn
alloy may have a superplastic behavior if the tensile conditions are
controlled properly. More details about superplastic behavior of
this alloy are under investigation.
4. Conclusions
In this study, the microstructure and mechanical properties of
Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy prepared by hot extrusion have been investi-
gated. The main results are summarized as follows.
(1) The as-extruded Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy exhibits typical
microstructure of 14H LPSO structure with two different
forms: the bent long strip-like ones along extrusion direction
and the ﬁne lamellar ones in the interior of ﬁne DRX gains.
(2)
 The ﬁne DRX grains are induced by hot-extrusion and LPSO
strips, and the lamellar 14H LPSO structure in the interior of
DRX grains are formed during the hot extrusion.
(3) The as-extruded Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy exhibits high strength and
ductility both at room temperature and elevated temperatures.
The yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and elongation
are 265 MPa, 353 MPa and 9.7% at room temperature, 204 MPa,
247 MPa and 29.2% at 300 ◦C, respectively.
(4) Two effective strengthening sources are considered for the as-
extruded Mg–9RY–4Zn alloy. One is the aligned LPSO phase
with excellent characteristics, such as high thermal stability,
microhardness and ductility as well as good combination with
the Mg matrix, the other is the ﬁne DRX grains containing LPSO
lamellae.
