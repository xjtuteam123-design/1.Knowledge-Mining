ABSTRACT 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) utilize multiple deformation mechanisms to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off 
by deformation treatment and low temperature conditions. To develop original engineering materials with 
strength-ductility synergy properties, we induced multiple deformation strengthening mechanisms into the as- 
cast face-centered cubic (FCC) HEAs at room temperature. A metastable as-cast Co

1. Introduction 
HEAs with multiple principal elements have been widely studied in 
the past decade [ 1 , 2 ]. HEAs with an FCC single phase structure exhibit 
an exceptional combination of mechanical properties, such as high 
strain hardening ability and strength-ductility, especially at cryogenic 
temperatures [ 3 – 6 ]. The research breaks the strength-ductility trade-off 
in traditional alloys. The strength-ductility synergy of HEAs is attributed 
to the transformation of strengthening mechanisms from dislocation slip 
to twinning induced plasticity (TWIP) and transformation induced 
plasticity (TRIP) at cryogenic temperatures, which can be candidates for 
the next generation of structural materials [ 7 – 9 ]. At room temperature, 
the driving force of dislocation slip increases and the structural stability 
is improved in HEAs, which causes the low stain hardening ability of 
HEAs. The deformation twins of as-cast HEAs are extremely difficult to 
activate at room temperature due to high critical twinning stress 
[ 10 , 11 ]. Therefore, it is necessary to reduce the structure metastability 
of HEAs and introduce multiple deformation mechanisms to improve the 
properties of HEAs at room temperature. 
SFE can effectively adjust the deformation mechanism of FCC HEAs 
[ 12 , 13 ]. The low SFE increases the splitting distance of two Shockley 
partial dislocations, hindering full dislocation slip to activate multiple 
slip systems and generate a large number of twins. CoCrFeMnNi HEA 
with low SFE (20 – 25 mJ/m
2
) has high strength and ductility at cryo -
genic temperatures by TWIP effect [ 5 , 14 ]. In addition to the effect of 
temperature, the SFE can be tailored by adjusting the composition of the 
alloy. The Co and Cr elements reduce the SFE and structural stability of 
HEAs [ 15 ]. The Co element based on the first principle calculation has a 
significant impact on lattice parameters [ 16 ]. The Cr element promotes 
the formation of hard phases and improves strength in HEAs [ 17 ]. At 
present, the structure metastability of HEAs with high properties is 
improved by cold deformation and recrystallization, which also increase 
the material preparation cost. However, the multiple deformation 
mechanisms of as-cast HEAs have rarely been studied at room temper -
ature. Therefore, the transformation of strengthening mechanisms at 
room temperature can be realized by regulating the chemical composi -
tion of as-cast HEAs.

4. Conclusions 
In summary, a novel metastable FCC phase Co
30

30
Fe
20
Ni
20 
HEA 
with exceptional as-cast strength-ductility synergy has been developed 
at room temperature by utilizing multiple deformation mechanisms. The 
increasing Co and Cr elements in the Co-Cr-Fe-Ni alloy further induce 
large lattice distortion ( δ ), strong atomic binding force ( Δ H
mix
), and low 
SFE. The solid solution strengthening and strong atomic binding force 
induce the improvement of yield strength, which is 92.2% higher than 
that of Co
25
Cr
25
Fe
25
Ni
25 
HEA. The large grain size and low SFE reduce 
the critical stress of twins (601 MPa) of alloy at room temperature. The 
crossing slip, blocked slip and nano twins multiple strengthening 
mechanisms are activated during tensile deformation. The multiple slip 
systems and nano twins boundaries hinder the movement of dislocation 
to induce high strain hardening ability by the dynamic Hall-Petch effect, 
which realizes strength-ductility synergy with a tensile strength of 613 
MPa and a fracture strain of 96.2%. The design concept of multiple 
deformation mechanisms at room temperature opens a new strategy to 
develop as-cast strength-ductility synergy by adjusting the chemical 
components of HEAs.
