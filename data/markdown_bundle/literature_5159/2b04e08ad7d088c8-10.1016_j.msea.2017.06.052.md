ABSTRACT
The microstructural characterization, fracture toughness and oxidation resistance of Nb-15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf
(at%, V-free) alloys and Nb-15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf-1V (at%, 1V) alloys were investigated. Results showed that
the V-free alloys and 1V alloys both consisted of Nbss, αNb5Si3 and γNb5Si3 phases, and V was primarily par-
titioned in Nbss phases. Compared with the V-free alloys, the 1V alloys were featured by a better continuity of
Nbss, ﬁner average diameter of silicides and lower volume fraction of γNb5Si3. The 1 at% addition of V enhanced
the fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys from 9.87 to 12.98 MPa m 1/2 at room temperature. The fracture
surfaces of 1V alloys were more undulated, compared with those of the V-free alloys. The 1 at% addition of V
signiﬁcantly improved the oxidation resistance of Nb-Si based alloys, by reducing the weight gain from 264.7 to
148.5 mg/cm
2 after oxidation at 1300 °C for 100 h. The transition from a paralinear oxidation kinetic to a
parabolic oxidation kinetic was triggered by the addition of V.
1. Introduction
Nb-Si based ultrahigh temperature alloys have captured consider-
able attention for structural applications in aircraft engines and land-
based gas turbines, because of their better high temperature capability
than current Ni-based superalloys [1,2]. A superior balanced perfor-
mance of Nb-Si based alloys has been demonstrated to rely on the
combination of the intermetallic Nb
5Si3 phases and the metallic Nb
solid solution (Nbss) phases. The industrial applications of Nb-Si based
alloys are now challenged by their poor room temperature mechanical
properties and insu ﬃcient high temperature oxidation resistance [3–5].
The Nb-Si binary system has been extended during the last three
decades to obtain an expected overall performance, while the Si content
typically ranges from 12 to 25 at% [6]. Alloying elements, mainly Cr,
Ti, Al, Hf, Mo, B, Ge, Ta, W, Ga, Fe, Sn, have been investigated to date,
aiming at improving the mechanical property and the oxidation re-
sistance of Nb-Si based alloys [6–19]. The additions of Ti, Hf, Cr, Al, Sn,
B, Ge, W have been found to enhance the high temperature oxidation
resistance [8–11]. The additions of Hf, Cr and Al could restrict the
oxygen di ﬀusion and reduce the solubility of oxygen in Nb-Si based
alloys [6]. The oxidation-resisting Cr
2Nb phases are typically in-
troduced with higher content of Cr additions [10]. The element of W
has been found to bene ﬁt the oxidation resistance, as the WO 3 oxides
improve the scale adherence and suppress the inward di ﬀusion of
oxygen [11].
The additions of Ti, Hf, B and Ga have been found to enhance the
fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys [6,13–17]. The fracture
toughness of Nb-Si based alloys is typically increased by promoting slip
by partial dislocations, decreasing the Peierls-Nabarro barrier energy
and reducing the stacking fault energy [12]. Accordingly, the positive
eﬀect of Ti addition was elaborated by Chan [12]. Addition of Ga to the
Nb-Si binary alloy increased the fracture toughness form 9.2 ± 0.05 to
24.1 ± 0.5 MPa m
1/2, bene ﬁting from the stabilization of β-Nb5Si3 and
the suppression of Nb 3Si [17]. Higher fracture toughness was achieved
with Hf additions, resulting from a re ﬁned microstructure, accom-
panying with a fracture manner transition of Nbss phases from brittle
cleavage to plastic stretching [19].
Some other elements tend to exert an adverse in ﬂuence on the
fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys. With the Fe content increasing
from 1 at% to 6 at%, the fracture toughness exhibits an gradual drop
from 13.6 to 7.9 MPa m
1/2 [18]. With increasing Mo content, either the
fracture toughness or the high temperature oxidation resistance of Nb-
Si based alloys su ﬀers from an obvious reduction [11,20]. The rare
earth elements have also been employed in some embodiments, in-
cluding Y and Ho, to improve the ductility and the oxidation resistance
of Nb-Si based alloys [21–23]. Besides, the alterations in the micro-
structural length scale and the fraction of each phase caused by alloying
typically exert an important in ﬂuence on their fracture toughness
[17,24–27]. Kashyap et al. [24] reported that the decrease in the eu-
tectic spacing in Nb-Si based alloys resulted in an increase in the

Unfortunately, the alloying elements mentioned above could im-
prove a single property with a certain addition amount. Few alloying
elements have been demonstrated to simultaneously improving both
the mechanical properties and the high temperature oxidation re-
sistance of Nb-Si based alloys. Cr and Al are able to improve their
oxidation resistance, but decrease their mechanical properties [21,28].
Ti can improve both the fracture toughness and the oxidation resistance
of Nb-Si based alloy, but lower the melting point of Nb-Si based alloy,
as well as their high temperature mechanical property.
In this work, Nb-Si based alloys were alloyed with vanadium, be-
cause of its low ductile-brittle transition temperature (DBTT, < 25 °C),
and the positive e ﬀects of V on both the mechanical properties and
oxidation resistance of directional solidiﬁ ed Nb-15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf
(at%) alloys were presented. The results were believed to be of great
importance for engineering application.
2. Materials and methods
The nominal compositions of Nb-Si based alloys employed in this
work were Nb-15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf alloy (at%, V-free alloy) and Nb-
15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf-1V (at%, 1V alloy), respectively. Button ingots
of the two alloys were ﬁrstly prepared by non-consumable arc-melting
in argon atmosphere. The buttons were melted ﬁve times for the sake of
chemical homogeneity. Master alloy rods for directional solidi ﬁcation
(DS) with an inner diameter of approximately 13 mm were prepared by
electro-discharge machining (EDM), and then assembled into Y
2O3
crucibles. DS experiments were performed in a liquid-metal-cooled
(LMC) furnace at 1980 °C. The furnace chamber was heated when the
vacuum pressure reduced to 5.0 × 10
−3 Pa. High purity Ar (99.99 wt
%) was back ﬁlled into the furnace chamber, when the temperature
exceeded 1000 °C. The DS experiments were conducted at 30 mm/min.
After DS experiments, heat treatments were carried out at 1450 °C for
12 h in a high vacuum furnace, followed by furnace cooling.
Fracture toughness measurements were performed at room tem-
perature on a SANS hydraulic servo machine, using the single-edge
notched three-point bending specimens (30 mm × 6 mm × 3 mm). The
notch in each specimen, normal to the DS direction, was prepared by
EDM using a Mo wire with 0.20 mm in diameter. The size of the notch
was up to a/w = ~0.5, where a was the notch length and w represented
the width of the specimen. The loading rate was 0.1 mm/min, and the
testing span was 24 mm. The fracture toughness K
Q was determined
according to ASTM E399 [29]. The specimens were cut and grounded
using metallographic abrasive papers up to 800# and ultrasonically
cleaned. Oxidation experiments were conducted at 1300 °C in a tube
furnace in static air. The exposure time was 100 h. The weight gain per
unit area was measured using an analytical balance with an accuracy of
10−5g.
The phases of Nb-Si based alloys were identi ﬁed by X-ray di ﬀraction
(XRD, D/max-2500). Microstructural observation was performed using
a ﬁeld-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Quanta 200F) and
electron microprobe analysis (EPMA, JXA-8230) with wave dispersive
spectroscopy (WDS). All the microstructures were taken in the back-
scattered electron (BSE) imaging mode. The average diameter of sili-
cides and the volume fraction of Ti-rich Nb
5Si3 particles in heat treated
V-free and 1V alloys were measured using the Image Pro Plus 6.0
software. As shown in Fig. 1, the measurement of the average diameter
of silicides was performed by passing through their centroids at 2° in-
tervals. More than four hundred silicides were selected on the trans-
versal microstructures of Nb-Si based alloys for measurement. The
three-dimensional topographies of fracture surfaces were obtained
using a laser confocal scanning microscopy (Olympus LEXT OLS4000).
The Ellingham diagram illustrating the thermodynamic stability of se-
lected oxides of interest was plotted with the HSC Chemistry 6.0 soft-
ware.

3.3. Oxidation behavior
Fig. 10 shows the weight gain per unit area as function of exposure
time at 1300 °C for V-free and 1V alloys via DS. After oxidation for
100 h, the total weight gains of V-free and 1V alloys are 264.7 mg/cm 2
and 148.5 mg/cm 2, relatively. Namely, in comparison with the V-free
alloys, the 1V alloys exhibit lower oxidation rates at 1300 °C. According
to the thermogravimetric curves in Fig. 10, the V-free alloy exhibits a
paralinear oxidation kinetic, i.e., a parabolic oxidation kinetic at the
early stage and then linear oxidation kinetics, while the 1V alloy shows
a parabolic oxidation kinetic with the exposure time up to 100 h. In the
ﬁrst 20 h during oxidation, no obvious di ﬀerence on the weight gain is
observed between V-free and 1V alloys. But the weight gain of the V-
free alloy increases sharply with the longer exposure time, while that of
the 1V alloy rises slowly. It is more accurate to state that the V-free
alloy alloys exhibit a higher linear oxidation rate at 1300 °C.
Based on the XRD patterns displayed in Fig. 11, the oxides grown on
V-free and 1V alloys after oxidation at 1300 °C for 100 h are primarily
composed of TiNb
2O7,T i2Nb10O25,N b 2O5, CrNbO 4 and TiO 2. The XRD
patterns of CrNbO4 and TiO2 are basically the same, resulting from their
identical crystal structures and similar lattice parameters [33]. The
sectional microstructure of 1V alloy after oxidation at 1300 °C for 100 h
is shown in Fig. 12. In the outer oxide scale, the black contrast SiO
2
phases (67.46O-32.54Si, at%) are observed among the oxides of
TiNb
2O7,T i2Nb10O25,N b2O5, CrNbO4 and TiO2. The SiO 2 phase grown
on Nb-Si based alloys during high-temperature oxidation is believed to
be amorous [9,34]. The glassy SiO
2 typically improves the oxidation
resistance of Nb-Si based alloys by healing cracks and ﬁlling the gaps
among oxides during oxidation. In the inner oxide scale, Nbss and si-
licides have been severely been oxidized, but not completely been
oxidized into oxides, as their phase features are still evident
(Fig. 12 (c)). The 1V alloy part under its oxide scale is slightly oxidized
by the oxygen di ﬀused through the oxide scale, especially at the phase
interfaces of Nbss and silicides as well as inside Nbss phases
(Fig. 12(d)). The white contrast particles in Fig. 12(d) are HfO 2
(65.78O-34.22Hf, at%), and the black oxides in contrast are TiO 2
(56.92O-6.17Nb-32.30Ti-4.61V, at%). The elements of Hf and Ti are
preferentially oxidized because of their higher a ﬃnity of oxygen. Fur-
thermore, the sizes of Nbss phases in the inner oxide scale ( Fig. 12(c))
are qualitatively larger than that in the alloy ( Fig. 12(d)), suggesting the
oxidation of Nbss induces a volume expansion.
4. Discussion
4.1. E ﬀect of V on the fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys
The fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys is remarkably a ﬀected
by their microstructure and the processing route [6,26]. The details of
the previous reports [5,13,16,17,24,31] with respect to the alloying
eﬀect on the fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys is shown in
Table 4 . Well-aligned microstructures of V-free and 1V alloys are ob-
tained by DS, which has been demonstrated to produce a positive e ﬀect
on the fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys [35]. It has been widely
accepted that Nbss phases are capable of providing signi ﬁcant tough-
ening of intermetallic Nb 5Si3 by plastic-stretching, interface-debonding
and crack-bridging mechanisms [36–38]. The addition of 1 at% V
produces no obvious e ﬀect on the microstructure of directionally soli-
diﬁed Nb-15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf alloy (at%). But the di ﬀerences on the
microstructures between V-free and 1V alloys after heat treatment at
1450 °C for 12 h are signi ﬁcant, which alter the fracture toughness at
room temperature.
In spite of the similar volume fraction of Nbss and silicides (in-
cluding αNb5Si3 and γNb5Si3) in V-free and 1V alloys, the improvement
in the fracture toughness of the 1V alloy is primarily due to its better
continuity of Nbss phases and the re ﬁnement of Nb5Si3 particles, as well
as the reduction of the volume fraction of Ti-rich γNb5Si3. It is known
that V has a lower melting point than Nb, resulting in an enhanced
diﬀusivity during heat treatment, which is controlled by a thermally
activated process. Ostwald ripening is thus promoted in 1V alloy during
heat treatment, accompanying with the disappearance or the coar-
sening of small original Nb
5Si3 particles, which are still observed in
base alloy (Fig. 5). Resultantly, Nbss phases in the 1V alloy interconnect
to form a more continuous matrix and the average diameter of Nb 5Si3
particles is smaller, in comparison with the V-free alloy. The Nbss
phases in 1V alloy with an improved continuity are able to promote the
bridging and the arresting of cracks during the fracture process, and is
more conductive to interface decohesion, as opposed to the irregularly
distributed Nbss phases in the V-free alloy [13,39]. In addition, the
Nbss/Nb
5Si3 interface chemistry may also be altered with V addition,
beneﬁting the interface decohesion in the fracture process. Those

The secondary cracks found in 1V alloys ( Figs. 7 and 8) indicate the
occurrence of secondary cracking toughening mechanism. Secondary
cracking normally alters the crack front and redistribute the stresses,
thereby e ﬀectively reducing the local crack tip driving force and im-
proving the fracture toughness of 1V alloys [43,44]. Another important
positive e ﬀect of V addition on the fracture toughness of Nb-Si based
alloys is the reduction of the volume fraction of silicides, especially
reducing Ti-rich γNb
5Si3 phases from 5.3% to 3.8% ( Fig. 5). Namely,
αNb5Si3 phases are more stable with the addition of V, which probably
results from the selective substitution of V for Nb sites and the resultant
increase in the c/a ratio of Nb 5Si3 lattice [32]. Shi et al. [45] in-
vestigated the ideal cleavage energy and cleavage strength of Nb 5Si3 by
ﬁrst-principles calculations, and con ﬁrmed a concentration-sensitive
eﬀect of Ti on the toughness of Nb 5Si3. It was reported that a low
amount of Ti increased the toughness of Nb 5Si3, but Ti-rich γNb5Si3
phases exhibited a signi ﬁcantly reduced toughness [45]. Meanwhile, a
number of experimental results [12,46] have shown that more micro-
cracks were found in the Ti-rich γNb5Si3 phases than regular αNb5Si3
phases, thereby damaging the fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys.
Overall, the 1V alloy has a stronger ability to bridge or de ﬂect a crack,
resulting in larger amount of energy absorption during crack propa-
gation. This explains why the fracture surface of 1V alloy with a higher
fracture toughness is created with severer unevenness and larger frac-
ture steps ( Figs. 8 and 9 ).

based alloys is due to their inability to form compact and protective
oxide scale. The continuous absorption, dissolution and inward di ﬀu-
sion of oxygen are typically through Nbss phases, in which the solubi-
lity of oxygen is higher and the di ﬀusion rate of oxygen is faster [47].I n

signiﬁcant volume expansion up to 169%, and the generation of large
residual stress in the oxide scales resultantly [48,49]. The oxides scales
formed on Nb-Si based alloys are typically porous and less protective,
and have a high tendency of spallation during oxidation. Thus it is the
key to enhance the oxidation resistance of Nb-Si based alloys by im-

The V-free alloy exhibits a parabolic oxidation kinetic at the early
stage and then linear oxidation kinetic at high temperature, while 1V
alloy solely shows a parabolic oxidation kinetic ( Fig. 10). It indicates
the addition of V change the oxidation kinetic of Nb-Si based alloys,
especially suppress the linear oxidation during longer exposure time.
This change signi ﬁcantly reduces the weight gain after oxidation at
1300 °C for 100 h. The parabolic law indicates the oxidation rate is
determined by the di ﬀusion through the oxide scale, and the linear law
suggests the oxidation rate is controlled by surface-reaction step [51].
Resultantly, it can be inferred that the oxide scale grown on the 1V
alloy is relatively more protective than that on the base alloy, though
the oxide scales on the base and the 1V alloys both exhibit severe
spallation during oxidation. Gang et al. [50] reported that the addition
of V to the Nb-Si systems increased their oxidation resistance, due to the
formation of the protective V
2O50.9Nb2O5 oxides. The increase in the
protectiveness of the oxide scale grown on the 1V alloy provides more
restriction on the inward di ﬀusion of oxygen, and the di ﬀusion through
the oxide scale remains to be the controlling step during oxidation.
Conversely, the less protective oxide scale formed on the base alloy
grows thicker with the exposure time, leading to the occurrence of
cracking and spallation. The oxidation rate of the V-free alloy is thus
increased, and the oxidation kinetic changes from a parabolic law to a
linear law ( Fig. 10).
5. Conclusions
With 1 at% addition of V, both the fracture toughness and high-
temperature oxidation resistance of directionally solidiﬁ ed Nb-15Si-
24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf (at%) alloys are improved. The microstructures of V-
free alloy and 1V alloy are composed of Nbss, αNb
5Si3 and small vo-
lume fractions of γNb5Si3 phases, and V is primarily partitioned in Nbss
phases. Compared with the V-free alloys, the 1V alloys are featured by a
better continuity of Nbss, a ﬁner average diameters of silicide and a
lower volume fraction of γNb5Si3, contributing to the improvement in
the fracture toughness of the 1V alloys. The fracture surfaces of 1V
alloys are more undulated with higher crack-path tortuosity, in com-
parison with the V-free alloys. The elements of Hf, Ti and V have a
higher oxygen a ﬃnity than Nb, and they are preferentially oxidized at
the Nbss/silicides boundaries as well as inside Nbss phases. SiO 2 phases
are observed in the outer oxide scale. After oxidation at 1300 °C for
100 h, the 1 at% addition of V reduces the weight gain of Nb-Si based
alloy from 264.7 mg/cm 2 to 148.5 mg/cm 2, and results in the transition
from a paralinear oxidation kinetic to a parabolic oxidation kinetic.
