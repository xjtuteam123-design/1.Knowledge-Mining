ABSTRACT
Different weight ratios of cerium (0.5, 1 and 2 wt.%) were produced in a controlled atmosphere 
by High Pressure Die Cast method to Mg-6Al-0.3Mn-1Sn alloy. The influence of cerium on 
microstructure and high-temperature mechanical properties was investigated. The microstruc -
ture study revealed that the main alloy contains intermetallic phases such as α-Mg, β- Mg
17
Al
12
, 
Mg
2
Sn and Al
11
Ce
3
. The hardness values of the alloys were increased with the addition of 0.5%, 
1 and 2 cerium. With the addition of 0.5 wt.% Ce, the yield strength increased to 145 MPa. With 
the addition of 2 wt.% of Ce, it was found to be 160 MPa with an increase of 14%. Tensile 
strength was obtained as 190 MPa. Elevated temperature tensile test was carried out at 120°C, 
180°C and 240°C, to all the alloys. It was found that increased Ce ratio increased the high- 
temperature mechanical strength. At 120°C, the highest tensile and yield strength values were 
obtained with 159 MPa and 147 MPa, respectively, in the alloy with 2% wt. cerium added. In the 
tensile test performed at 180°C, the highest tensile and yield strength were measured as 91 
MPa and 77 MPa, respectively. The operating temperature of Mg alloys produced industrially 
by HPDC has a significant effect on mechanical properties. High operating temperatures 
reduce mechanical properties. However, the mechanical properties at high test temperatures 
were increased by the addition of Ce metal to the AM60-1Sn-XCe alloy produced with HPDC.

1. Introduction
In recent years, especially in aerospace, automotive 
industry, the most basic criteria for selecting engineer -
ing materials are defined as strength-to-weight ratio 
and durability. Mg alloys with a density (1.74 g/cm
3
) 
are more advantageous than other light alloys. 
Therefore, the use of Mg alloys can be more efficient 
in the automotive and aerospace industries where 
carbon emissions and low fuel consumption are at 
the forefront [ 1 ] [ 2 , 3 ]. Good castability, high- 
dimensional stability and easy recyclability, which are 
also found in Mg alloys, stand out as additional engi -
neering properties [ 4 ]. However, the strength and duc -
tility of Mg is often lower than the aluminium alloy 
because of its HCP structure [ 5 ]. In order to increase the 
industrial application, it is essential to enhance the 
high-temperature mechanical properties of magne -
sium alloys [ 6 ] [ 7 ]. Engineering alloy selection requires 
not only increasing mechanical properties but also 
considering economically manufacturability. For this, 
it is necessary to correctly select alloying elements that 
will provide both commercially suitable alloying ele -
ments and critical properties (e.g. High Temperature 
Strength). The effects of aluminium and tin added to 
Mg are available in the literature. Al increases the 
fluidity of magnesium, and it provides both solid solu -
tion hardening at room temperature and increases the 
strength due to the phase formation of the secondary 
intermetallic (β-Mg
17
Al
12
). However, due to this inter -
metallic low melting temperature, it has a negative 
effect on the mechanical properties at temperatures 
above 125°C. In order to eliminate this negative effect, 
the researchers need to sort out two important sub -
jects. The first is the addition of the third alloy element 
to control and reduce the beta phase (for example, to 
form intermetallic with aluminium) or to form addi -
tional intermetallic. The second is the modification of 
a new binary alloy system and better mechanical prop -
erties by adding a third or fourth alloying element. 
When the Mg-Sn binary equilibrium diagram is exam -
ined, the tin is dissolved in the magnesium crystal 
lattice at 561°C at about 14.85% by weight. At room 
temperature, this resolution is extremely low. 
Therefore, Mg
2
Sn intermetallic phase with high melt -
ing temperature in magnesium-tin binary alloys is 
a solution for high-temperature applications. Mg
2
Sn 
intermetallic has thermal stability and improves the 
mechanical properties of magnesium at high tempera -
ture [ 8 ] [ 9 ], .
Among commercial cast Mg alloys, Mg-RE-based 
alloys have higher yield strength and the best tensile 
properties at elevated temperatures (150–250°C) [ 10 ]. 
In order to increase the high-temperature resistance, 
Ce element was chosen as a common, economical and 
effective alloying element. The addition of Cerium 
improves the mechanical properties by controlling 
the structure of the B phase. The improvement of 
mechanical properties is achieved not only at room 
temperature but also by the preventive effect of inter -
metallics, such as Al
11 
Ce
3 
and CeMg
2 
[ 11 ] with a high melting temperature at 
the grain boundaries. Another effect is that, even at 
low Ce ratios, Al
4
Ce particles of both nano and micron 
sizes are formed and dispersed very well in the primary 
α-Mg matrix. This provides many advantages in form -
ing processes by increasing ductility [ 12 ]. In the litera -
ture, it is noteworthy that the amount of RE elements, 
such as Ce increases the engineering properties by 
approximately 1 wt.%. When Ce addition is more 
than 1.0 wt%, grain size increases inversely [ 5 ] [ 12 ].
In order to determine the optimum engineering and 
performance values, the addition of Ce was selected as 
0.5, 1, 2% by weight. Thus, by keeping the amount of 
Ce at a minimum level, the ideal high-temperature 
strength values can be determined.
Cold chamber die casting technique, which is the 
ideal casting method for producing a final industrial 
product in casting, to obtain a finer-grained structure 
and to make a continuous production, has been 
selected.
2. Experimental procedures
The nominal composition of Mg-6Al-0.3Mn-1Sn-xCe 
(x = 0, 0.5 wt.%, 1 wt.%, 2 wt.%) alloys were prepared 
from commercially pure magnesium, aluminium and 
tin as cast material. Cerium and manganese were 
added in Mg-%30 Ce and Al-%10Mn form as a master 
alloy. The specimens were melted in a medium fre -
quency induction furnace using a silicon carbide cru -
cible under a protection gas mixture of carbon dioxide 
(CO
2
) and sulphur hexafluoride (2% SF
6
). Each alloy was 
held at 750°C casting temperature for 5 min, poured 
into a mould at 200°C (with an oil heating/cooling 
system) and immediately casted into the die, shown 
in Figure 1 , using a 100-tonnes clamping and 76 kN 
injection force a regular type cold chamber high- 
pressure die cast machine.
Specimens were prepared for (FE-SEM) investiga -
tion using the standard metallographic route and che -
mically etched in acetic glycol (20 ml acetic acid, 1 ml 
nitric acid, 60 ml ethylene glycol, 19 ml distilled water).
Brinell hardness tests were carried out with a 2.5 mm 
ball and an applied load of 31.25 kg, making at least 20 
indentations with 15 s of loading duration ( Figure 1 
Microstructure test specimen). Uniaxial tensile tests, 
using rectangular test specimens ( Figure 1 Tensile test 
specimen) in accordance with ASTM standard E8/E8M, 
were performed at room temperature, 25°C, 120°C, 180°C 
and 240°C with a universal testing machine with a ram 
velocity of 0.2 mm/min. Before each high temperature

3.2. Mechanical testing
The room and elevated temperature tensile properties 
of AM60-1Sn with different Ce concentrations are 
listed Figure 6 .
As seen from Figure 6 , the macro hardness of the 
Am60-1Sn alloy was measured as 60 Brinell. It was 
found that the hardness values of the alloys modified 
with the addition of 0.5%, 1 and 2 cerium alloy ele -
ments increased to 64, 68 and 70 Brinell, respectively. 
One reason for the increase in hardness is the dissolu -
tion of an element of the alloy in the α-Mg phase, and 
consequently the hardness of this α-Mg phase is 
increased. Another reason is to obtain smaller sized 
grains and to create additional new intermetallic 
phases. Fan et al. confirmed the formation of the 
finer β-Mg
17
Al
12 
and Al
4
Ce strengthening phase by 
adding 2% Ce by weight to the cast AZ91D alloy [ 14 ].
The yield and tensile strength of the main alloy 
(AM60-1Sn) were determined as 140 MPa and 161 
MPa, respectively. With the addition of 0.5% wt. Ce, 
the yield strength of the main alloy increased by 3.5% 
to 145 MPa and the tensile strength increased by 8.5% 
to 175 MPa with the addition of 1% wt Ce, the yield 
strength increased by 7% to 150 MPa and the tensile 
strength increased by 19% to 190 MPa. The highest 
yield strength was obtained with the addition of 2% wt 
Ce and was found to be 160 MPa with an increase of 
14%. In the tensile strength, no change was observed 
and 190 MPa was obtained. Ce modified alloys did not 
make a significant change in the% elongation value of 
the main alloy.

4. Conclusions
AM60-1Sn alloy, the 0.5, 1 and 2 wt.% Ce/AM60-1Sn 
alloys were successfully produced by high pressure 
die casting. The effect of Ce addition on the micro -
structure and mechanical behaviour of AM60-1Sn 
alloy was investigated and the following findings 
obtained;
(1) The microstructure study revealed that the 
AM60-1Sn alloy consisted of intermetallic 
phases, such as α-Mg, β- Mg
17
Al
12 
and Mg
2
Sn. 
With the addition of Cerium, an Al
11
Ce
3 
inter -
metallic phase was detected.
(2) The macro-hardness of the AM60-1Sn alloy was 
improved with the addition of Ce.
(3) The tensile properties of the AM60-1Sn alloy 
were enhanced both at room and high tempera -
ture. The best properties were attained in the 
AM60-1Sn-2Ce alloy with 70 HB of hardness, 160 
MPa of yield strength and 190 MPa of tensile 
strength at room temperature.
