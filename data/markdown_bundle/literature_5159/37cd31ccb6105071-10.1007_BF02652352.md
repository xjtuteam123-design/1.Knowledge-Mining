Microstructure and Mechanical Behavior of 
In Situ Intermetallic Composites 
Cr-Cr2Hf 
K.S. RAVICHANDRAN, D.B. MIRACLE, and M.G. MENDIRATTA 
A detailed investigation of the effects of microstructural changes on the mechanical behavior of two 
in situ intermetallic composites with Cr and Cr2Hf phases in the Cr-Hf system was performed. The 
nominal compositions (at. pct) of the alloys were Cr-5.6Hf (hypoeutectic) and Cr-13Hf (eutectic). 
The study included evaluations of strength, ductility, and fracture toughness as a function of tem- 
perature and creep behavior. Two microstructures in each alloy were obtained by heat treatments at 
1250 ~ (fine microstructure) and 1500 ~ (coarse microstructure). A decrease in elastic strength 
(stress at the onset of inelastic response in the load-deflection curve) with the coarsening of the 
microstructures was noted for both alloys below 1000 ~ The Cr-13Hf alloy retained strength to a 
higher test temperature, relative to Cr-5.6Hf alloy, under both microstructural conditions. The alloys 
showed no evidence of ductility at room temperature. However, in the coarse microstructure of the 
Cr-5.6Hf alloy, the primary Cr exhibited ductility at and above 200 ~ ductility in primary Cr could 
be seen only at and above 1000 ~ for the fine microstructure. In other words, the temperature at 
which ductility was first observed decreased from about 1000 ~ to about 200 ~ due to high- 
temperature heat treatment in this alloy. Both microstructures of Cr-5.6Hf alloy showed a significant 
increase in fracture toughness with increasing test temperature. However, the increases in fracture 
toughness with temperature for the Cr-13Hf alloy microstructures were relatively small. Both alloys 
showed about four orders of magnitude reduction in steady-state creep rates relative to pure Cr at 
1200 ~ The results are analyzed in the light of deformation characteristics and fracture rnicrome- 
chanisms. The effects of microstructural factors, such as the size and continuity of phases, solubility 
levels of Hf as well as interstitial elements in Cr, on the observed mechanical behavior are discussed. 
I, INTRODUCTION

als, such as MoSi2, NbsSi 3, and Cr3Si , are of significant 
research interest in attempts to develop materials for gas 
turbine applications. A major problem has been poor room- 
temperature ductility and fracture toughness of intermetallic 
materials. An approach involving the incorporation of duc- 
tile phases, such as Nb, Cr, and Ta, to improve the damage 
tolerance of intermetallics is being pursued.[l-*] In addition

reactions at high temperatures leading to degradation of me-

II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE 
Alloys with nominal compositions of (at. pct) Cr-5.6Hf 
and Cr-13Hf were arc melted and cast in copper molds. 
The ingots were enclosed in Mo cans and extruded at 1500 
~ with an approximate reduction ratio of 4:1. The speci- 
men blanks were heat treated at 1250 ~ and 1500 ~ for 
100 hours under the flow of high-purity (>99.5 pct) get- 
tered argon in a tube furnace and furnace cooled to room 
temperature. Before heating to the desired temperature, the 
furnace was flushed with argon three times. Specimens 
were wrapped in Ta foil during heat treatment. The sizes 
of Cr and Cr2Hf phases were measured from microstruc- 
tures on the plane transverse to the extrusion direction. Bars 
of rectangular cross section, with dimensions of 32 x 6.35 
x 3.2 mm, were machined by electrodischarge machining 
(EDM) for flexure strength evaluation by three-point bend 
testing. Bars of size 32 x 6.35 X 6.35 mm were also ma- 
chined for fracture-toughness tests. All specimen surfaces 
were then polished to a 600-grit finish to remove the surface 
layers affected by heat treatment. Notches, 2.5 mm in depth 
and about 50/~m in root radius, were machined by EDM 
in fracture-toughness specimens after heat treatment. 
Flexure and fracture-toughness tests were conducted at a 
displacement rate of 8.5 X 10 -3 mm/min in three-point 
bending at room temperature and at 200 ~ 400 ~ 600 
~ 800 ~ 1000 ~ and 1200 ~ in an argon atmosphere. 
The length of span was 25 mm for both types of tests. The 
displacement of the loading_ ram was taken as the bending 
deflection. Stresses at the specimen surface were calculated 
on the basis of the elastic bending of bars (o- = Mt/2L in 
which M is the bending moment, t is the specimen thick- 
ness, and I is the moment inertia of the cross-sectional 
area). The stress levels at which the stress-deflection curve 
deviated from linearity were taken as the elastic strength 
for all the tests. Compression creep tests were performed 
at 1200 ~ in argon atmosphere, on cylindrical specimens 
(5 mm in diameter and 10 mm in length) cut from the 
extrusions after heat treatment. The specimens were incre- 
mentally loaded and at each load level and allowed to creep 
for 50 to 200 hours to attain steady state, depending on the 
load level and the alloy. SiC platens were used on both 
sides of the specimen in the loading fixture. Steady-state 
creep rates were determined from creep strain vs time data.
