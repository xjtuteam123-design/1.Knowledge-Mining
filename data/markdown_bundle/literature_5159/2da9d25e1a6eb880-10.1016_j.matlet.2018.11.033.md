abstract
The novel Al-Si-Ni-Fe alloy with high compression strength at elevated temperatures has been success-
fully produced by selective laser melting at an average volumetric energy density of about 100 J/mm 3 and
has a relative density of 99,86%. The hardness of as fabricated Al-Si-Ni-Fe alloy is 186HV and it decreases
during heat treatment to 124HV due to stress relief and silicon particle growth. The yield compression
stress is 355 MPa at 200 /C176C. This high strength is provided by the ﬁne structure formed by the Si, Al
5Fe
(Ni,Cu) and Al 3(Ni,Cu) phases. The good strength properties of the alloy at elevated temperatures make
it a quite promising lightweight material for high temperature applications.

1. Introduction
Additive manufacturing is a technology which promises to
replace conventional casting methods in the near future [1]. The
versatility delivered by this process is of great interest. One of
the most common additive manufacturing methods is selective
laser melting (SLM) [2]. The Al-Si alloys produced by SLM technol-
ogy have been widely studied previously and possess high strength
at room temperature [3–6]. However, these alloys did not demon-
strate high levels of strength at temperatures higher than 300 /C176C
[7]. Different alloying elements were added to Al-Si based alloys
in order to improve the mechanical properties [8,9]. The
microstructure and mechanical properties of the Al-Si-Fe alloys
obtained using SLM were studied earlier [10–12]. It is well known
that Ni can improve the mechanical properties of the Al-Fe-Si
alloys due to the control of the size of the needle-shaped precipi-
tates [13]. Porosity and defects, such as balling and unmelted pow-
der, are typical problems accompanying fabrication of parts by
selective laser melting [14–16]. The aim of this work is to study
the microstructure and mechanical properties of the novel heat-
resistant Al-Si-Ni-Fe alloy manufactured by SLM technology.
2. Materials and methods
The Al-Si-Fe-Ni alloy powder with the nominal composition
(wt.%) Al-12Si-1.4Fe-1.4Ni with minor additions of Cu was pro-
duced by gas atomization at the UC RUSAL. A scanning electron
microscope (SEM) image and the size distribution of the powder
are presented in Fig. 1 . The average powder size is 39 ± 5 mm and
the average shape factor is 1,13 ± 0,03.
The samples for microstructure investigation and mechanical
tests were fabricated on an SLM Solutions 280HL machine
equipped with a 1064 nm wavelength Yb laser. The samples with
the lowest porosity of 0,14 ± 0,02% were obtained at the following
parameters: laser power 200 W, scanning rate 400 mm/s, overlap
0,15 mm and layer thickness 0,03 mm. These parameters provide
an average volumetric energy density of about 100 J/mm 3.
Tescan Vega 3LMH SEM, JEOL 2100 transmission electron
microscope (TEM) and Brucker Advance D8 X-ray diffractometer
(XRD) analysis were used for the qualitative and quantitative anal-
ysis of the microstructure.

3. Results and discussions
The microstructure of the tested alloy is presented in Fig. 2. The
track core and hatch overlap can be seen in the microstructure as
two distinctive zones. The size of the particles in the hatch overlap
zones is signiﬁcantly larger than in the other parts due to remelting
and consequently higher energy density. In the track core the pre-
cipitates are small and oriented along the heat transfer direction.
The (Al), Si, Al 5Fe(Ni,Cu) and Al 3(Ni,Cu) phases were identiﬁed in
the microstructure of as produced samples using XRD analysis
(Fig. 2f). The phase composition did not change signiﬁcantly during
the heat treatment. As one can see in Fig. 2f), only the width of the
silicon phase peaks after annealing at 495 /C176C for 3 h decreased due
to the growth of the silicon particles.
Fig. 3 demonstrates TEM images and the elemental distribution
in the microstructure of the Al-Si-Ni-Fe alloy in as fabricated state
and after heat treatment. It can be seen that the size of the Al 5Fe
(Ni, Cu) and Al 3(Ni, Cu) phases did not change signiﬁcantly. At
the same time the size of the silicon particles increased from
300 nm to 800 nm after annealing at 495 /C23C for 3 h. Water

Compression tests were performed at different temperatures in
the 200–400 /C176C range to determine the heat resistance of Al-Si-Fe-
Ni in as built state. The stress–strain compression curves are
presented in Fig. 4 . The yield stress decreased from 355 MPa at
200 /C176C to 125 MPa at 400 /C176C. As can be seen from Fig. 4 , deforma-
tion proceeded without fracture up to a true strain of 0,6 due to the
thermal activation of structure recovery in the aluminum solid
solution. The yield stress of the investigated alloy at 250 /C176Ci s
315 MPa and it is signiﬁcantly larger than for A336
(AlSi12CuMgNi) alloy (132 MPa) and for Al-5%Cu-7%B4C compos-
ite material (143 MPa) [18]. Higher heat resistance of the studied
alloy is achieved due to ﬁne thermally stable Al
5Fe(Ni, Cu) and
Al3(Ni, Cu) particles. The good strength of the alloy at elevated
temperatures makes it a quite promising lightweight material for
the high temperature applications.
4. Conclusions
1. The novel Al-Si-Fe-Ni alloy with a low porosity of 0,14 ± 0,02%
was successfully fabricated using SLM technology at an average
volumetric energy density of about 100 J/mm 3.
2. The aluminium solid solution and Si, Al 5Fe(Ni,Cu) and Al 3(Ni,
Cu) phases ﬁne particles are identiﬁed in the microstructure
of as fabricated samples and after heat treatment using SEM,
TEM and XRD analysis.
3. The hardness of the Al-Si-Ni-Fe alloy in as built state is 186HV.
Quenching and aging of the alloys decrease the hardness due to
stress relief and the growth of silicon particles.
4. The Al-Si-Ni-Fe alloy demonstrates high compression strength
at elevated temperatures: its maximum true stress increases
from 125 MPa at 400 /C176C to 355 MPa at 200 /C176C.
