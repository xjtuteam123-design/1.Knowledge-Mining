Abstract
Ternary Ni60Nb40−xSnx (3 ≤ x < 9 at.%) and quaternary Ni60(Nb100−xTax)34Sn6 (x = 20, 40, 60 and 80 at.%) Ni-base alloys were found to form
bulk amorphous structures using conventional metallic-mold casting. These bulk amorphous alloys have very high glass transition temperatures
(881 K <T
g < 895 K). They also exhibit high Young’s modulus (180–200 GPa), shear modulus (54–59 GPa), estimated strength (3–3.8 GPa) and
measured compressive yield strength (2–2.8 GPa). The measured strength of the ternary alloys depends strongly on the Sn content and is generally
lower than the strength estimated from modulus and hardness. Small-angle neutron scattering studies show that phase separation occurs in these
alloys. The phase separation may be responsible for the strong composition dependence of the measured strength and the obvious discrepancy
between the measured and the theoretical strengths (i.e. the premature fracture behavior).

1. Introduction
There has been an ongoing effort to develop processable
amorphous alloys with greater strength, elastic modulus and
hardness. These refractory alloys include Co-, Fe- and Ni-base
alloys. A variety of Ni-base amorphous alloy systems have been
developed [1–5]. Complex Ni
Nb Cr Mo P B alloys have
been prepared in 1 mm-diameter amorphous rods by metal-mold
casting [1], amorphous rods of up to 2 mm in diameter have
been fabricated in the Ni
Ti Zr (Si, Sn) system [2] and in
the complex, multi-component Ni Nb Ti Zr Co Cu system
amorphous rods up to 3 mm in diameter have been found[3].I t
has been reported that these Ni-base alloys possess high thermal
stability and strength up to 2.7 GPa. More recently, amorphous
rectangular strips up to 3 and 5 mm in thickness have also been
fabricated in the ternary Ni
Nb Sn system and the quinary
Ni Cu Ti Zr Al system, respectively [4,5].
Earlier [4] we reported that the simple ternary Ni Nb Sn
alloys show exceptionally high Vickers hardness (1000–1280

2. Experimental procedure
The ternary (and quaternary) alloy ingots were prepared by
arc melting a mixture of ultrasonically-cleaned elements with a
purity of 99.7 at.% or better in a Ti-gettered high-purity argon
atmosphere. The alloy ingots were then re-melted under high
vacuum in a quartz tube using an induction-heating coil and
then injected through a ∼1–1.5 mm (i.d.) nozzle into a copper

Zr

4. Conclusions
We have performed detailed mechanical tests on the recently
developed Ni Nb Sn bulk metallic glasses. The measured
strength of these refractory amorphous alloys is generally lower
than their theoretical strength calculated from their Vickers
hardness. The measured strength also exhibits much stronger
composition dependence than the elastic moduli or the Vickers
hardness. Small angle neutron scattering experiments revealed
that phase separation (spinodal decomposition) occurs in these
alloys which might be responsible for the embrittlement of these
alloys. In addition, the effects of replacing Nb with Ta on the
structural, thermal and mechanical properties of a representative
ternary glass Ni
60Nb34Sn6 have been reported.
