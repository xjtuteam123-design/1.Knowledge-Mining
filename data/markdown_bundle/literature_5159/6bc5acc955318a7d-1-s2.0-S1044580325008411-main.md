ABSTRACT
Oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) Cu alloy is a kind of structural material with high thermal conductivity and 
high stability. It is promising to maintain mechanical integrity under thermal conductivity and high temperature 
conditions. These excellent properties are directly or indirectly attributed to the ultra-high number density of 
nano oxide particles, which act as a strong barrier for grain growth, dislocation slip and grain boundaries (GBs) 
slip. In this study, Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si and ODS Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloys were prepared by combining mechanical 
alloying with spark plasma sintering (SPS) technology. The influence of Cu
2
O on the microstructure, mechanical 
properties, and thermal stability of the Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloy were investigated. Both alloys exhibited a het -
erogeneous mixture of coarse and fine grain structures. However, the addition of Cu
2
O led to grain refinement 
and the precipitation of a high density of uniformly distributed, nano-sized oxide particles throughout the copper 
matrix. Compared to the Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloy, the ultimate tensile strength of the ODS Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloy 
escalated from 409 MPa to 482 MPa, while the elongation slightly decreased from 17.2 % to 15.2 %. The ODS Cu- 
Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloy also demonstrates a room temperature thermal conductivity equivalent to 62.8 % of pure 
copper, and exhibits ultra-high microstructural stability after annealing at 600 
◦
C for 100 h. The outstanding 
strength, ductility and high-temperature stability are primarily owing to the coherent nano-sized Y-X-O (X = Al, 
Ti, Si) particles dispersed in the ultra-fine grain copper matrix. These findings indicate that by integrating 
component design and process optimization, it is possible to effectively and efficiently produce ODS Cu-Cr-Y-Al- 
Ti-Si alloy, which holds promising application prospects in structural materials for nuclear fusion reactors.
1. Introduction
Copper (Cu) alloys are widely employed in electronic equipment, 
marine systems, and nuclear fusion devices owing to their high thermal 
conductivity and favorable mechanical properties [ 1 – 3 ]. Conventional 
precipitation-strengthened (PS) Cu alloys, such as Cu-Ti [ 4 ], Cu-Cr [ 5 ], 
Cu-Zr [ 6 ], and Cu-Fe [ 7 ], are unsuitable for high-temperature applica -
tions due to precipitate coarsening at elevated temperatures. Developing 
heat-resistant Cu-based materials has thus become crucial for advanced 
engineering applications. Although the addition of alloying elements 
can extend precipitate stability, their effectiveness in enhancing high- 
temperature performance remains limited [ 8 , 9 ]. Typical PS Cu alloys 
exhibit inadequate resistance to thermal softening, often failing to meet 
the mechanical performance requirements for demanding applications 
such as defense systems [ 10 ]. This performance gap underscores the 
need for innovative material design strategies to improve the high- 
temperature endurance of copper alloys.
Recent advances have highlighted dispersion strengthening (DS) as a 
promising approach for developing high-temperature Cu alloys. Ceramic 
particles such as SiC, TiC, WC, Al

Cu alloys can achieve superior comprehensive properties when a fine

thereby improving thermal stability via the Zener pinning effect [ 25 ]. 
Strategic integration of uniformly distributed nanoscale oxides into 
optimized copper matrices presents a viable pathway for performance

tensile properties, and high-temperature stability, utilizing mechanical 
alloying combined with SPS consolidation as the primary fabrication 
route.

The introduction of Cu

Al-Ti-Si alloy system ( Fig. 4 and Fig. 6 ). Firstly, it significantly induces 
grain refinement through the grain boundary pinning effect, suppressing 
grain boundary migration during thermal processing, with the average 
grain size reduced by approximately 35 % (compared to the matrix 
alloy). Secondly, during the sintering process, Cu
2
O undergoes in-situ 
interfacial reactions with active alloy elements (Y, Si, Al, Ti), gener -
ating a high density of Y-X-O (X = Si, Al and Ti) nano-oxide dispersion 
phases. TEM analysis ( Fig. 7 ) reveals that these nanoparticles (5 – 15 nm) 
exhibit coherent/semi-coherent interfaces with the copper matrix, and 
their thermodynamic stability is significantly higher than that of con -
ventional precipitates. The uniaxial tensile stress-strain curves of the Cu- 
Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si and ODS Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloys under different aging 
times tested at room temperature were obtained, as shown in Fig. 8 b-c. 
The sintered ODS Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloy exhibited an UTS value of 484 
MPa at room temperature ( Fig. 8 c), which was 18 % higher than that for 
Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloy. As the aging time increases, the strength of Cu- 
Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si and ODS Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloys show an increasing 
trend, and the plasticity is well maintained. Therefore, Ti contributes to 
the formation of coherent nanoscale Y-Ti-O dispersoids, enhancing 
thermal stability by impeding dislocation motion and grain growth. Si 
participates in forming coherent nanoscale Y-Si-O dispersoids, further 
strengthening the matrix and suppressing grain boundary sliding. Both 
elements (Ti and Si) synergize with Y and Al to stabilize the ultra-fine 
grain structure, improving the alloy ’ s strength, ductility, and high- 
temperature performance in nuclear fusion environments.
Through the analysis of the fracture morphologies of two alloys after 
tensile testing, the results are shown in Fig. 9 . Both alloys (those without 
Cu
2

2

characteristics at the tensile fracture surfaces. The alloy with ODS 
addition displayed finer (nano-scale) and more uniformly distributed 
dimples on the fracture surface. This is due to the introduction of Cu
2
O 
during the mechanical alloying process, which reacts with the metal 
powder during ball milling to form fine oxide particles. These particles 
promote the refinement of the metal powder, leading to the formation of 
a fine-grained structure. The fine-grained structure provides more sites 
for dislocation pile-up and twin nucleation, which in turn aids in stress 
dispersion and the formation of smaller dimples.
As shown in the comparative analysis in Fig. 10 , the ODS Cu-Cr-Y-Al- 
Ti-Si alloy developed in this study exhibits superior mechanical prop -
erties, including strength and ductility, compared to traditional copper 
alloys enhanced by nano W, Ta, Mo particles or ceramic phases. Spe -
cifically, the ultimate tensile strength of the ODS Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloy 
is increased by 15 – 20 %, while its fracture elongation remains at 8 – 12 % 
under room temperature conditions, demonstrating a balanced syner -
gistic effect of strengthening and toughening. This performance 
enhancement primarily stems from the thermally stable Y-X-O com -
posite oxide particles, which effectively inhibit dislocation movement 
and grain boundary migration.

4. Conclusion
This study fabricated Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si matrix alloys and ODS Cu-Cr- 
Y-Al-Ti-Si alloys with 0.25 wt% Cu
2
O addition through a synergistic 
process of MA and SPS, systematically investigating the influence 
mechanisms of oxide doping on the microstructural evolution, me -
chanical properties, and high-temperature stability of the alloys. 
(1) The incorporation of Cu
2
O into the Cu-Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si alloy during 
mechanical alloying and SPS processing resulted in significant 
grain refinement and the precipitation of a high density of uni -
formly distributed, nano-sized oxide particles (primarily coherent 
Y-Al-Ti-Si-O phases) within the copper matrix.
(2) The addition of Cu
2
O and the resulting nano-oxide dispersoids led 
to a significant increase in ultimate tensile strength, rising from 
409 MPa in the base alloy to 482 MPa in the ODS alloy, repre -
senting an 18 % improvement. Moreover, the material exhibited 
exceptional microstructural stability when subjected to pro -
longed annealing at an extreme temperature of 600 
◦
C for 100 h.
(3) The superior strength-ductility-thermal stability synergy stems 
from ultra-fine grains reinforced by dense coherent Y-X-O dis -
persoids, effectively suppressing dislocations and grain boundary 
sliding. This multi-component design (Cr-Y-Al-Ti-Si + Cu
2
O) 
combined with MA + SPS processing enables high-performance 
ODS Cu alloys for nuclear fusion structural applications.
