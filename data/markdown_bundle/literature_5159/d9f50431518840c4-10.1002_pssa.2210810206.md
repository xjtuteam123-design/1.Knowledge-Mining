1. Introduction 
The structural and mechanical properties of P-NiAl phase has been studied by many 
workers [l to 181. The P-phase, based on NiA1, is not nornially used to provide strength- 
ening, although it is an important constituent of coatings for nickel-based superalloys 
[l, 16 to 181. 
It was first observed by Guard and Turkalo (1960) [6] that the P-phase undergoes 
a niartensitic transformation on quenching to an acicular structure. Various crystal 
structures of the P-phase have been reported [8 to 101. The most detailed investigation 
of the structure of the martensite has been made by Moskovic (1974) [19] in a Ni- 
30.3 at76 Al-6.6 atyo Cr alloy on water quenching froni the solution treatment tem- 
perature. 
The purpose of the present investigation has been to study the structural and 
mechanical properties of nickel-based alloys treated in the (y’ + P)-phase field and to 
investigate the effect of the ternary addition, tantalum, on structure and mechanical 
properties.

4. Discussion 
4.1 Substructure of @-martensite 
From the combined examination of the striations in the image and diffraction pat- 
terns several fine, planar fault systems appear to exist in the p-martensite. If the 
faults are taken into consideration as necessary for martensite transformation, it 
appears that multishear mechanisms must be taken into account to predict the 
crystallographic features of p-martensite. Besides the classical phenomenological 
theory, more general approaches to the martensite crystallography have been made in 
terms of double or multiple shears [23 to 251. On the basis of the calculation of the total 
energy, which consists of surface, strain, and chemical driving force, for the transfor- 
mation from a matrix to martensite, it was shown by Easterling and Tholen (1976) 
[26] that twinned nuclei of martensite have a lower energy than untwinned nuclei 
and also further strain energy reduction can be obtained if twins form on two or more 
systems icstead of only one; therefore, the existence of the different variants of 
twinning and other types of faults in the p-martensite occurs in order to reduce the 
energy of the martensite formation. 
As shown in Fig. 6 within one martensite plate striations in two directions exist. 
These striations have been interpreted as twinning faults lying on the two variants of 
the (101) plane. Different faults or the different variants of tlhe same fault in one 
martansite plate have also been reported in the past by a number of workers [20 to 22. 
27, 281. Warlimont (1965) [27] has suggested that this effect is due to interactions of 
different martensite plates during growth; in other words, impingement of martensite 
plates. Evidence of such an impingement in the present case is shown in Fig. 6 (at 
C-C). However, as mentioned above two variants of this fault in a martensite plate 
exist in order to reduce the strain energy [26], which seems more likely.

4.2 Nucleation and morphology of p-martemsite 
The concept of lattice instability has been introduced to explain martensitic transfor- 
mation [34]. A nickel-rich p-phase with composition A1-63.2 atyo Ni which is similar 
to the compositions of the @-phase in the present study shows the temperature depend- 
ence of the shear constant C' = 1/2(Cll - Clz), i.e. for the shear along the [OlI] 
direction in the (011) plane [35]. A formula for the shear stress on the (011) plane along 
the [Oli] direction has been given by Mercier and Melton (1976) [36]. They have used 
the influence of an anisotropic elastic medium on the nucleation of the martensite 
phase. The decrease of the shear constant C' (the shear stress on the (011) [Oil] 
decreases according to the formulae given by these workers) towards Ms temperature 
(the beginning of martensite formation) suggests that elastic softening, i.e. lattice 
instability along [Oil] on (Oll), exists in the p-phase. Therefore, the martensitic 
transformation may be nucleated by (011) [Or11 shears of the phase lattice [36, 371. 
The hypothesis that the lattice instability is a trigger of the martensitic transforma- 
tions has been used in the past [27, 381. 
The growth of a self-accommodating system in the p-martensite is favoured over 
that of individual martensite plates because it is associated with a lower rate of in- 
crease in strain energy during growth [39 to 411. 
There is a tendency for the morphology to change from mainly plate-like to acicular- 
type martensite morphology with increasing nickel content in the p-martensite. This 
may be seen by comparing the general features of alloy 2 in the homogenized and the 
as-cast states of alloy 1 (Fig. 2, Fig. 8, Table 2). The morphology changes from coni- 
pletely plate-like in p-phase with 60 at yo Ni in the homogenized state to the acicular- 
type in p-martensite with 66.7 atyo Ni in the as-cast state. This suggests that the 
tendency for the transition from plate-like to acicular-type morphology increases with 
increasing transformation temperature (M,). 
Solution treatment of the alloys 1 and 2 at 1260 "C followed by water quenching has 
produced the martensitic phase with body-centred tetragonal structure. In the as-cast 
state the P-phase has also undergone a martensitic ttype transformation; it is suggested 
that rapid cooling is not a necessary condition for the @-phase to undergo martensitic 
transformations in these alloys. 
Fine, disc-shaped precipitate particles on { 110 1 planes have been observed to exist 
in the untransformed and transformed @-phase. 
The nucleation of the p-martensite has been considered in terms of elastic moduli. 
It was suggested that the fact that the shear elastic coefficient C' = (ell - Cl,)/2 
decreases with decreasing temperature, favours the nucleation of martensitic trans- 
formation by (011) [OlI] shear in the ordered b.c.c. (B2) P-phase.
