ABSTRACT
Body-centered cubic (BCC) refractory compositionally complex alloys (RCCAs) have emerged as promising 
candidates for aerospace, nuclear energy, and automotive applications due to their exceptional high-temperature 
strengths. It is well-known that dislocations play a critical role in the mechanical properties of refractory alloys. 
In this study, we examine the fundamental properties of edge and screw dislocations, including core energies and 
dislocation shear stresses (DSSs) in MoNbTi, NbMoTaW, and CrTaVW, at various temperatures using atomistic 
simulations with the state-of-the-art machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs). Our findings reveal that at 
high temperatures, the DSS of edge dislocations exceed those of screw dislocations in MoNbTi and NbMoTaW 
alloys. This behavior is attributed to cross-kink diffusion and annihilation in screw dislocations, which leads to a 
more significant decrease in DSS as temperature increases. Furthermore, the DSS values of screw dislocations at 
low temperatures and those of edge dislocations at high temperatures closely align with experimental yield 
strengths. These results show that edge dislocations are primarily responsible for the high-temperature strengths 
of some of the RCCAs and are crucial for tuning their mechanical properties. Additionally, we observe that screw 
dislocations exhibit lower core energies than edge dislocations across all temperatures in the investigated alloys, 
indicating their greater thermodynamic stability. These findings underscore the importance of considering 
different types of dislocations at various temperature regimes in BCC RCCAs, which is essential for guiding alloy 
design within the vast compositional space.
1. Introduction
Compositionally complex alloys (CCAs), which are also called multi- 
principal element alloys (MPEAs) or high entropy alloys (HEAs), have 
recently ignited significant interest due to their promising mechanical 
properties [1 – 6] . Especially as a subclass of MPEAs, the body-centered 
cubic (BCC) refractory compositionally complex alloys (RCCAs) 
composed of refractory elements (Ta, Cr, W, Mo, Ti, etc.) exhibit supe -
rior high-temperature strength compared to conventional nickel-based 
superalloys [7] and refractory metals [8] . Therefore, these RCCAs are 
considered for aerospace, nuclear, and automobile applications [9,10] . 
For instance, the MoNbTi [11] exhibits a combination of decent yield 
strength (324 MPa) at 1200

comparison, the NbMoTaW [7] maintains a yield strength of 405 MPa up 
to 1600

of 600 – 1000

1200

high-temperature yield strength of RCCAs remain an active area of 
research.
In conventional BCC metals and alloys, plastic deformation and yield 
strength are mainly controlled by screw dislocations, especially at low 
temperatures [14,15] . The dominance of screw dislocations is also found 
in certain RCCAs [4,16,17] , such as TiZrHfNbTa [18] and TiZrNbHf 
[19] . However, recent studies suggest that edge dislocations also play a 
crucial role in some RCCAs [11,20,21] . For instance, Wang et al. [11]
observed a large fraction of non-screw segments of dislocations in 
MoNbTi at room temperature after deformation and suggested that its 
yield stress is controlled by edge dislocation slip resistance. Similarly, 
Pozuelo et al. [22] noted a great number of edge dislocations in 
NbMoTaW after compression. Additionally, Lee et al. observed a higher 
edge dislocation population than screw dislocations in NbTaTiV and 
CrMoNbV [20] . Furthermore, Tseng et al. inferred that the high strength 
of HfNbMoTaW and Hf0.5NbMoTaW originates from the dominance of 
edge dislocations at high temperatures [23] . In addition to experimental 
results, theoretical models by Maresca and Curtin et al. show that edge

2. Methodologies
All simulations were performed using the Large-scale Atomic/Mo -
lecular Massively Parallel Simulator (LAMMPS) [39] , and all structures 
were visualized using OVITO [40] . Specifically, MTPs were utilized for 
NbMoTaW [32] , CrTaVW [35] , and MoNbTi systems [31] . For the 
NbMoTaW system, we also conducted comparative simulations using 
the SNAP [34] and GAP [33] . These MLIPs were selected based on their 
availability and demonstrated accuracy, as they closely align with 
density functional theory (DFT) results. For the MoNbTi-MTP system, 
mean absolute errors (MAEs) of 4.1 meV/atom and 0.067 eV/Å for en -
ergies and forces were achieved, along with 6 meV/atom of dislocation 
energy MAEs [31] . The NbMoTaW-MTP model achieved MAEs of 4.6 
and 4.3 meV/atom for training and test data, respectively, with force 
MAEs reduced by half compared to the prior NbMoTaW-SNAP model 
[34] and tenfold cross-validation yielding average MAEs of 4.4 meV/ 
atom for energies and 0.056 eV/Å for forces [32] . The NbMoTaW-GAP 
system also showed an accuracy of 2 meV/atom for the alloy [33] ; 
however, due to its lower computational efficiency, it was only used for 
dislocation energy calculations. For NbMoTaW-SNAP, MAEs were 
within 6 meV/atom for energies and 0.15 eV/Å for forces, with a unity 
slope in predicted energies and forces relative to DFT [34] . The CrTaVW- 
MTP reported root-mean-square errors (RMSEs) of 2.304 and 6.481 
meV/atom for training and testing data energies and 0.088 and 0.206 
eV/Å for atomic forces, with high consistency in elastic constants 
compared to DFT [35] .
We employed the Periodic Array of Dislocations (PAD) method [41]
to model edge and screw dislocations. This method has been used in 
several prior studies [42 – 44] . The simulation cell orientation for edge 
dislocations was x [ 11 2 ] , y [ 111 ] , and z [ 1 10 ] , while for screw dislocations, 
it was x [ 111 ] , y [ 11 2 ] , and z [ 1 10 ] . Both types of dislocations shared a 
Burgers vector of a / 2 [ 111 ] and a ( 110) slip plane, with their dislocation 
lines along the x-direction. For both dislocation systems, the periodic 
boundary conditions (PBC) were applied in the x-direction, while free

3.2. Peierls stress and dislocation shear stress
Peierls stress is the applied resolved shear stress needed to make a 
dislocation glide in an otherwise perfect crystal at 0 K [47] . In con -
ventional BCC metals, edge dislocations exhibit much lower Peierls 
stress compared to screw dislocations. For example, in the Mo system, 
the Peierls stress of an edge dislocation is approximately 10 MPa, 
whereas that of a screw dislocation exceeds 1000 MPa, as shown in Fig. 6
(a). Therefore, the low Peierls stress allows the edge dislocations to glide 
easily to microstructural features, such as grain boundaries, where they 
annihilate, thereby limiting their population. Due to the easy motion 
and limited population of edge dislocations, screw dislocations are 
considered dominant dislocations and control the overall strength of 
simple BCC metals.
However, this conventional picture no longer holds in RCCAs the 
Peierls stress of edge dislocations is more comparable to that of screw 
dislocation in RCCAs, as shown in Fig. 6 (b). The higher Peierls stress of 
edge dislocations in RCCAs increases their resistance to glide, thereby 
reducing the likelihood of gliding out and annihilating compared to pure 
BCC metals. This aligns with experimental observations, where a higher 
fraction of non-screw dislocation segments or an increased population of 
edge dislocations is found after deformation [11,22] . Prior studies 
[34,59 – 61] have reported an elevated edge/screw ratio in RCCAs at 0 K. 
The RCCAs in this study also exhibit a markedly larger edge/screw ratio 
compared to their elemental components at 0 K. Furthermore, we found 
that this ratio increases further at high temperatures, exceeding 100 % at 
1500 K in MoNbTi and NbMoTaW. This higher edge/screw DSS ratio 
indicates that edge dislocations play an important role in RCCA me -
chanical properties, particularly at high temperatures, where their DSS 
is comparable to that of screw dislocations.
Furthermore, we studied the temperature effects on DSS, as shown in 
Fig. 7 . We find that the DSS of screw dislocations is higher than that of 
edge dislocations at low temperatures, e.g., room temperature, in all 
three RCCAs. As temperature increases, we observe that the DSS of edge 
dislocations exceeds that of screw dislocations in MoNbTi and NbMo -
TaW, as shown by a crossover in Fig. 7 a and 7b. For MoNbTi and 
NbMoTaW (using MTP potential), the crossover is around 1200 K. A 
similar phenomenon is observed in NbMoTaW with the SNAP potential, 
though the crossover occurs at a higher temperature of ~ 1500 K. In 
CrTaVW, the differences in DSS between screw and edge dislocations 
reduce significantly, although no crossover occurs up to 1800 K.

differences in the mechanisms governing edge and screw dislocation 
motion. The motion of screw dislocations in RCCA is controlled by cross- 
kinks, which are non-glissile structures commonly observed in disloca -
tions in these alloys. These cross-kinks possess high energy barriers and 
exhibit strong temperature dependence as shown in previous studies 
[17,62] . At low temperatures, screw dislocations require high DSS 
values to overcome the self-pinning from cross-kinks [17,63] ; in 
contrast, at high temperatures, the thermally activated diffusion of 
cross-kinks causes a sharp decrease in screw DSS [17,62,64,65] . 
Therefore, the screw DSS decreases rapidly with increasing temperature. 
In contrast, the motion of edge dislocations in RCCAs is primarily gov -
erned by atomic size misfit [24,66] . Unlike in pure metals, RCCAs 
exhibit a large atomic size misfit which induces strong lattice distortions 
that impede the motion of edge dislocations. For example, in our sim -
ulations, the edge Peierls stresses of pure metals Mo and W — both 
lacking significant atomic misfit — are only 27 MPa and 60 MPa, 
respectively, whereas that of NbMoTaW is 675 MPa, primarily due to 
large lattice distortions. Since the atomic size misfit dominates edge 
dislocation motion, the temperature dependence of the edge DSS is 
directly affected by the temperature dependence of the misfit. Yeh et al. 
[67] reported, based on their analysis of the decrement of XRD in -
tensities in MPEAs, that the atomic misfit caused by the thermal vibra -
tion of atoms is smaller than the atomic misfit from the intrinsic lattice 
distortion. Similarly, Hu et al. [68] conducted a quantitative study on 
various RCCAs and found that in NbTaVW — a composition similar to 
those in our systems — the thermal contribution constitutes only about 
40 % of the overall lattice distortion. Thus, the edge DSS exhibits a weak 
temperature dependence and consequently decreases with increasing 
temperature at a slower rate compared with that of screw DSS, leading 
to the observed crossover.
For the NbMoTaW-MTP system, Yin et al. also observed a higher 
decay rate of DSS in screw dislocations than edge dislocations with 
increasing temperature [32] . However, their results for the DSS of screw 
dislocations were consistently higher than those of edge dislocations 
across all temperature ranges without exhibiting the crossover phe -
nomenon observed in the NbMoTaW-MTP, NbMoTaW-SNAP, and 
MoNbTi-MTP systems in our study. This difference may stem from the 
details of the simulation setups: our simulations use a longer simulation 
cell along the dislocation line direction and apply lower strain rates, 
both of which affect the DSS results based on our convergence tests.

4. Summary
We conducted atomistic simulations to investigate the dislocation 
energies, Peierls stress, and DSS properties of MoNbTi, NbMoTaW, and 
CrTaVW systems at various temperatures using advanced MLIPs. The 
results of dislocation energies indicate that screw dislocations remain 
thermodynamically more stable than edge dislocations across all the 
RCCAs we studied. Nevertheless, the core free energy calculations 
indicate a potential increase in the population of edge dislocations at 
high temperatures, although the populations of edge dislocations should 
be lower than that of screw dislocations. For dislocation motion, the 
screw dislocation Peierls stress is higher than edge dislocation Peierls 
stress in all studied systems at 0 K. In comparison at high temperatures, 
the results of DSS emphasize the importance of edge dislocations in 
RCCAs. The edge/screw DSS ratios of RCCAs are significantly higher 
than those of simple BCC metals across various temperatures. Moreover, 
the DSSs of edge dislocations in NbMoTaW and MoNbTi were observed 
to exceed those of screw dislocations at elevated temperatures, indi -
cating a potential shift from screw dislocation dominance to edge 
dislocation dominance in yield behavior and explaining their high yield 
strength at elevated temperatures. 
(1) In the MoNbTi, NbMoTaW, and CrTaVW systems, screw dislo -
cations are energetically more favorable than edge dislocations at 
all considered temperatures. Therefore, there will be more screw 
dislocations than edge dislocations at all temperatures under 
thermodynamic equilibrium.
(2) As temperature increases, the DSS of edge dislocations has a 
much lower decay rate than that of screw dislocations in RCCAs. 
This results in a crossover point in NbMoTaW and MoNbTi alloys 
at high temperatures, where the DSS of edge dislocations exceeds 
that of screw dislocations, explaining their high yield strength at 
elevated temperatures.
(3) The edge/screw DSS ratio is significantly higher in RCCAs than in 
their single-element components. This characteristic makes the 
motion of edge dislocations in microstructural features relatively 
more difficult in RCCAs, thereby maintaining a higher population 
of edge dislocations within the crystal structure compared to 
simple BCC metals.
Our research highlights the role of edge dislocations in enhancing the 
strength of RCCA at high temperatures. While at lower temperatures, 
screw dislocations predominantly determine the strength, aligning with 
conventional theories in BCC metals, and at higher temperatures, edge 
dislocations significantly contribute to the high strength in certain 
RCCAs. Accordingly, both screw and edge dislocations need to be 
carefully considered across varying temperatures for the design and 
development of high-performance RCCAs. This study also demonstrates 
that MLIPs have the potential to aid alloy design by exploring the vast 
space of compositions and configurations. This highlights the need for 
developing universal potentials tailored for alloys, with a focus on 
dislocation behavior and mechanical properties.
