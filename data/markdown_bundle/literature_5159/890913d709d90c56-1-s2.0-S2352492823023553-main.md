ABSTRACT 
An embedded atomic method based on Chen ’ s lattice inversion (CLI-EAM) was developed for the Zr
3
Al binary 
alloy. The reliability of the potential function was established through calculations of fundamental properties of 
the Zr
3
Al binary alloy, including vacancy formation energy, elastic constants, and mechanical modulus. Utilizing 
the CLI-EAM model, the high-temperature mechanical behavior of the Zr
3
Al alloy was investigated, and com -
parisons were made with other existing EAM models and experimental data. The outcomes manifested the high 
temperatures augment disorder within the alloy lattice structure, culminating in the abatement of the maximum 
tensile strength of Zr
3
Al, but could still maintain a strength of 8 GPa at 700 K. Additionally, the mechanical 
performance of Zr
3
Al subjected to elevated temperature irradiation conditions was distinctly influenced by 
radiation-induced structural damage. The mechanical attributes of Zr
3
Al endured constancy under room tem -
perature and 5 keV irradiation, and a substantial regression in both ultimate tensile strength and elongation was 
evident at 700 K. Finally, molecular dynamics simulations coupled with visual analysis elucidated the reasons for 
the divergence in the mechanical performances of irradiated systems at different temperatures. These findings 
could underscore the enhanced reliability of CLI-EAM potential model in dynamic simulations and microstruc -
tural evolution studies of Zr
3
Al alloys. 
1. Introduction 
In the development of aerospace and automotive industries, Zr-based 
alloys have gradually become a research hotspot because of their 
excellent high-temperature properties [1 – 3] . Among many incorporated 
elements, Al is an effective element to improve the mechanical proper -
ties of Zr alloy. The mechanical properties of Zr at room temperature and 
high temperature can be significantly improved by adding a small 
amount of Al element [4] . The typical Zr
2
Al phase in Zr-Al alloy has the 
characteristics of high melting point and high hardness, but its plasticity 
at room temperature is almost zero, while the Zr
3
Al phase formed by 
Zr
2
Al through clathrate reaction shows good ductility at high tempera -
ture, has more potential as a matrix phase for high-temperature mate -
rials. Neutron irradiation is an important factor affecting the mechanical 
properties of Zr
3
Al-based alloys. Rosinger tested the mechanical prop -
erties of Zr
3
Al-based alloys under different irradiation intensities. The 
yield strength and tensile strength of the alloys under the irradiation 
intensity of 10
25

the plasticity decrease slightly, when this critical value is exceeded, the 
plasticity of the Zr

will decrease obviously [5] . Birtcher et al. observed the microstructural 
changes of Zr
3
Al alloy during Kr ion irradiation at 1.5 MeV. They found 
that disordered irradiation of large crystalline grains resulted in the 
formation of new grains on a scale of 10 nm, and the fine-grained ma -
terials are highly resistant to amorphization of the material at lower 
irradiation temperatures [6] . Therefore, exploring the microscopic 
mechanism that affects the mechanical properties of Zr
3

for better developing of Zr
3

For the study of the mechanical properties of superalloys, molecular 
dynamics (MD) methods have unique advantages. MD can quickly 
process large-scale atomic systems, and accurately reflect their micro -
scopic mechanism, and at the same time can consider many influencing 
factors such as temperature and pressure in simulation calculations [7] . 
It is worth noting that potential functions are used to describe inter -
atomic interactions and play a decisive role in the simulation results of 
MD. In general, the potential of embedded atomic method (EAM) [8] is 
often used to describe interatomic interactions in alloy system. Daw and 
Baskes first used EAM potential to fit six FCC transition metal elements,

Johnson extended the embedded energy in EAM to an analytical form. 
This analytical embedded atomic method (AEAM) proposed to make the 
EAM model more physical [9] . Subsequently, Baskes et al. considered 
the non-spherical uniform distribution of the electron cloud, proposed a 
modified embedded atom method (MEAM or 1NN-MEAM), and 
extended the EAM potential to materials with covalent bonds and 
transition metals [10] . Since 1NN-MEAM only considers the interaction 
pair potential between the nearest neighbor atoms, it cannot accurately 
predict the properties of some bcc metals. On this basis, Baskes and 
collaborators continued to develop the pair potential and electron 
density of the two nearest neighbor atoms and proposed second 
nearest-neighbor modified embedded atom method (2NN-MEAM), 
which obtains good effect [11] . In recent years, EAM has gradually 
developed towards the large-scale multi component alloys (such as 
high-entropy alloys) [12] and the combination of machine learning 
[13] . Therefore, EAM has become an indispensable theoretical frame -
work and physical model for metal material performance simulation. 
However, there are few EAM potentials that can accurately describe 
Zr-Al binary alloys. For example, Zhou et al. proposed an EAM potential 
in the form of a function when simulating the behavior of transition 
metals and transition metal oxides [14] . This potential has a good effect 
in describing the elemental elements of Al and Zr, but the calculated 
mechanical properties of Zr-Al alloy have a large deviation from the 
reference values. The reason may be that their crossover potential does 
not consider the structure or mechanical properties of the specific binary 
alloy phase, resulting in a large discrepancy between this potential and 
the experimental results in the process of describing the alloy dynamics. 
In order to construct a set of EAM potentials that effectively describe 
Zr-Al binary alloys, Chen ’ s lattice inversion (CLI) method [15] needs to 
be considered. The CLI method obtains the semigroup series related to 
the lattice by calculation, and obtains the relevant parameters of the pair 
potential by inversion from the cohesive energy curve. In the early stage, 
the author used CLI modified EAM to construct several sets of binary 
alloy potentials, including fcc [16] , bcc [17] and hcp [18] metals, these 
EAM potentials can accurately describe the mechanical properties of 
multiple binary compounds. Then Duan et al. constructed the potential 
functions of eight FCC transition metals by lattice inversion method, the 
basic physical properties were consistent with the experimental data, 
and some predictions were made for the structural and thermal prop -
erties [19] . These works prove that CLI method can match the types of 
different crystal structures to strictly obtain the pair potential of 
neighbors at any distance. 
Recently, a MEAM potential containing Al-Zr binary alloy was pro -
posed, which tends to describe the static properties of multiple phase 
structures [20] . However, it is uncertain whether the potential is reliable 
for defect structure evolution and dynamic simulations. Therefore, it is 
necessary to construct an EAM potential that can more accurately 
describe the high temperature and irradiation properties of Zr
3
Al alloy. 
Meanwhile, experimental studies have shown that irradiation has a 
greater impact on the high-temperature mechanical properties of Zr
3
Al, 
and the microscopic mechanism of this phenomenon still not widely 
recognized. Therefore, the purpose of this study is to develop the 
interatomic potential of Zr-Al binary systems by lattice inversion 
method. The based physical properties of Zr-Al alloy systems at high 
temperatures were calculated. The tensile curves of Zr
3
Al at different 
temperatures were simulated and the microscopic mechanism of the 
effect of irradiation on the Zr
3
Al compound was explored.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Basic properties 
The Zr-Al CLI-EAM potential was constructed as a force field file that 
can be used in LAMMPS simulations. The basic properties of the material 
were then calculated using the potential, and the results were compared 
with experimental and other data to verify the reliability of the potential 
function. All simulations were performed at 0 K with a cut-off distance 
of 6.875 Å. 
Table 2 shows the physical properties of elemental Al and Zr, 
respectively. The lattice constants of Al and Zr are 4.06 Å and 3.23 Å, 
and the cohesive energies of Al and Zr are 3.36 eV and 6.25 eV, 
respectively, which are as same as the experimental values. This is 
because the total energy we referred contains lattice and energy infor -
mation, so the CLI-EAM constructed by lattice inversion is accurate. The 
elastic constants were determined using the calculation script provided 
by LAMMPS, where Al is a highly symmetrical face-centered cubic (FCC) 
structure with only three independent elastic tensors, and Zr is a hex -
agonal close-packed (HCP) structure containing six independent tensors, 
the results are found to be in good agreement with the reference data.

3.3. Irradiation performances 
Irradiation-induced damage is known to significantly impact the 
mechanical properties of materials [44] , including Zr
3
Al alloy. There -
fore, studying the evolution of the microstructure of the Zr
3
Al system 
during the irradiation process is crucial, particularly with regards to 
understanding the primary damage system caused by the cascade 
collision process. The defect evolution process of Zr
3
Al under irradiation 
by varying the irradiation intensities and temperature conditions were 
explored. The tensile simulations were conducted on the 
irradiation-damaged system to explore the changes in its mechanical 
properties. The displacement cascade process can be visualized as a 
sequence of atomic collisions caused by incident particles. 
To investigated the effects of irradiation damage on Zr
3
Al, a simu -
lation model with a size of 40 Å × 40 Å × 40 Å and a time step of 
0.001 ps was utilized to simulate the irradiation process. After relaxa -
tion for 20 ps under the NVT ensemble, the central atom was selected as 
the primary knock-on atom (PKA) for the irradiation simulation. we set 
PKA as 0.5 keV, 5 keV and 10 keV for three kinds of irradiation in -
tensities. The irradiation was carried out for 50 ps under the NVE 
ensemble. 
We set two temperatures to observe the performance of Zr
3
Al after 
irradiation at room temperature (300 K) and high temperature (700 K). 
Using Ovito for structural analysis, we counted the atomic percentage of 
the damaged structure to characterize the size of the damaged area of 
the system after irradiation. As shown in Fig. 9 , it is found that as the 
irradiation intensity increases, the irradiation damage area significantly 
increases. Moreover, the damaged area under high temperature became 
more severe with increasing irradiation intensity. The result is consistent 
with the observation of transmission electron microscopy in the exper -
iment, as opposed to the initial highly ordered structure, an escalation in 
irradiation ion dose yields a progressively randomized and oriented 
arrangement of the crystal [12] , this indicated that our potential can 
well describe the irradiation performance of Zr
3
Al alloy. Under the 
irradiation condition of 0.5 keV, the Zr
3
Al is almost undamaged, and 
maintains its original structure. This suggests that Zr
3
Al can resist 
low-intensity irradiation. However, under high-intensity irradiation 
conditions at 10 keV, the system suffers extensive damage, which is 
irreversible even after a long period of time. This indicates that the Zr
3
Al 
system has produced irreversible damage at 10 keV.

4. Conclusions 
We employed the lattice inversion method to construct the 
embedded atom method (CLI-EAM) potential for the Zr
3
Al binary alloy 
and conducted investigations on the high-temperature mechanical 
properties and irradiation damage behavior. By converting the lattice 
information of the relevant crystal into a semigroup sequence and uti -
lizing Chen ’ s lattice inversion method, we successfully obtained the 
parameters of the corresponding potential functions. The CLI-EAM po -
tential is found to accurately reproduce the fundamental material 
properties of the Zr
3
Al system, showing excellent agreement with 
experimental and first-principles calculations. 
Through the comparison of experimental data and visualization 
analysis, we observed a significant decrease in the mechanical proper -
ties of Zr
3
Al with increasing temperature and system disorder. At 
elevated temperatures, the atoms in Zr
3
Al possess higher energies, 
making them more susceptible to deviating from their lattice sites, 
resulting in the formation of numerous disordered structures within the 
crystal. Consequently, the mechanical properties of Zr
3
Al experience a 
rapid decline under the combined influence of high temperature and 
disordered structures. 
Irradiation induces cascade collisions between primary knock-on 
atoms (PKA) and surrounding atoms in the system, leading to the for -
mation of larger vacancy clusters and interstitial atoms in Zr
3
Al. This 
phenomenon causes localized disorder in the material and reduces the 
strength and ductility of the alloy. In addition, the temperature has a 
notable impact on the mechanical properties of the Zr
3
Al alloy after 
irradiation. While the alloy demonstrates resistance to 5 keV irradiation 
at room temperature, such irradiation leads to a reduction in the ulti -
mate tensile strength and ductility at 700 K.
