Microstructure and mechanical properties of bulk MgToCa10A120 alloys 
produced by extrusion of atomized amorphous powders 
A. Kato, H. Horikiri, A. Inoue and T. Masumoto 
Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980 (Japan) 
Abstract 
The application of high-pressure Ar atomization to MgT~Ca~Alz~ ~ alloy causes the formation of amorphous and Mg 
phases, and subsequent extrusion of the atomized powder at 673 K results in the formation of a bulky alloy with a finely 
mixed structure consisting of AI;Ca embedded in an Mg matrix. The mixed phase alloy exhibits a high yield strength of 
600 MPa, a high specific yield strength of 3.3 MNm kg- l, a high Young's modulus of 49 GPa at room temperature, a high 
elevated-temperature strength above 300 MPa in the range up to 550 K, and good corrosion resistance; these properties 
are superior to those of commercial Mg-based alloys. The simultaneous achievement of high mechanical strength and 
good corrosion resistance is attractive for subsequent development as a new type of high-strength materials. 
1. Introduction 2. Experimentalprocedure 
Recently, the formation of Mg-based amorphous 
alloys has been discovered in a number of Mg-based 
alloy systems [1, 2] and bulk amorphous or crystalline 
alloys have been produced in the Mg-Ln-Cu and 
Mg-Ln-Ni systems [3]. These alloy systems were 
chosen because they have large glass-forming ability 
and high mechanical strengths in the amorphous state 
[2, 3]. The tensile fracture strength of the resulting bulk 
Mgs7.sYT.sCu 5 crystalline alloys is as high as 740 MPa 
[3], but good corrosion resistance has not been 
obtained because of dissolution of the transition metal 
(Ni or Cu). In a subsequent study on the development 
of Mg-based bulk alloys with high specific tensile 
strength and good corrosion resistance, we looked at 
the Mg-Ca-A1 alloy system, because of our previous 
experience of the formation of an amorphous single 
phase in this alloy system by melt spinning at a rather 
low wheel rotation speed [4]. The first aim of this study 
was to examine the composition range in which non- 
equilibrium phases, including the amorphous phase, 
form in the Mg-Ca-AI system under high-pressure gas 
atomization, and to examine the crystallization- 
induced phases. The second aim was to present the 
microstructure, mechanical properties and corrosion 
resistance of bulk alloys produced by extrusion of the 
atomized powder and to compare the present bulk 
alloys as a possible structural material with commercial 
Mg-based crystalline alloys. 
Ternary Mg-Ca-A1 alloys were used in the present 
study because these alloys consisted of metallic 
elements with low densities. The ingots were prepared 
by induction melting a mixture of pure metals in a puri- 
fied Ar atmosphere. The compositions are nominally 
expressed in atomic per cent hereafter. From the 
master alloy ingots, rapidly solidified ribbons were 
produced by melt spinning. Furthermore, non- 
equilibrium phase powder was produced of a limited 
alloy composition (Mgv0Cal0Al20) by the high-pressure 
Ar atomization technique. About 330 g of the pre- 
alloyed ingot was charged into the atomization crucible 
and remelted under an Ar atmosphere. Figure 1 is a 
schematic illustration of the equipment specially 
designed for the production of atomized Mg-based 
alloy powder and the subsequent compaction pro- 
cesses which consist of collecting, sieving and pre- 
compacting the powder and then sealing it in a Cu can. 
The prealloyed ingot was atomized from 1048 K with 
Ar gas at an applied pressure of 9.8 MPa in a carefully 
controlled Ar gas atmosphere with oxygen and 
moisture contents of less than 1 ppm. Subsequently, the 
powder was collected and screened in the controlled 
atmosphere. Fine powder particles below 25 ktm in 
size were placed in a cylindrical Cu can with an inner 
diameter of 20 mm and precompacted to about 78% 
density at 300 K by uniaxial pressing. The powder in 
the can with the cap was outgassed for 900 s at 300 K

3. Results and discussion 
Based on previous results [4] on the compositional 
range in which amorphous Mg-Ca-A1 alloys form by 
melt spinning at significantly different wheel rotation 
speeds, as well as the dependence on composition of 
the mechanical properties of crystalline alloys obtained 
from the amorphous phase, we chose MgToCal0A120 as 
an appropriate atomization alloy because this alloy has 
rather high glass-forming ability and the largest hard- 
ness of the crystallized alloys with good bending ductil- 
ity. Figure 2 shows the surface morphology of 
as-atomized MgToCal0A120 powder. The powder par- 
ticles have a spherical morphology and the average 
particle size is evaluated to be about 20 k~m by laser 
diffraction. The as-atomized powder with particle size 
of less than 25 ~m consists of amorphous and Mg 
phases without any trace of peaks corresponding to 
A12Ca, as shown in Fig. 3. Considering the structure of 
the melt-spun ribbon consists of an amorphous single 
phase [4], the difference in structure of the as- 
quenched as-atomized powder and the melt-spun 
ribbon obtained at a rotation speed of 11 m s-1 must 
be due to the difference in cooling rate. The as- 
atomized powder without AleCa is judged to be useful 
as a raw material for the production of a high-strength 
bulk alloy with a finely mixed structure consisting of 
'fine AI2Ca particles embedded in an Mg matrix.
