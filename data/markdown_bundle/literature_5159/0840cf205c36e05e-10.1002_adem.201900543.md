Abstract 
 
Creep tests of additively manufactured (AM) Inconel 625, both hot isostatically pressed (HIPed) 
and non-HIPed were performed at various stresses between 65 and 658 MPa at 650 to 800 oC. 
The creep behaviour of AM samples were compared with that of (conventional) wrought alloys. 
Tests were performed over a 24 hour period for initial heat-treated samples and also after a heat 
exposure at the service temperature of 650 oC for six months. The six -month tests consisted of a 
time-temperature profile similar to Inconel 625 service conditions in jet engines. We found that 
the creep strength of AM 625 has equal or superior creep strength at 650 to 800 oC compared to 
the wrought alloy , even after 6 months exposure to the test temperature . The HIPed AM alloys 
had significantly superior ductiliy over the non -HIPed AM Inconel 625, as expected. However, 
the ductility of the HIP ed AM Inconel 625 is still substantially lower than the wrought alloy,

Introduction 
Additive manufacturing (AM), is widely known as 3D printing, has been attracting attention 
from academic and industrial areas. The main goal of AM is to achieve cost and time saving s in 
manufacturing processes. AM technology has had several successes in metal manufacturing 
processes because the design of products can be easily adapted or changed, and it produces 
minimal waste during its entire process [1,2]. One major application of metal AM technology for 
industry is components for aircraft engines. Han has suggested that over 75% of the conventional 
aircraft engine parts can be replaced by AM products . Since the most widely used alloys in an 
aircraft turbine are nickel alloys [ 3,4,5,6], these superalloys need to be considered first as the 
target material for AM research on the aircraft engine components. 
 
Among various Ni-based superalloys, Inconel 625 has wide commercial applications in aviation 
industries [7]. Directed metal laser sintering (DMLS) has generally been considered for the 
fabrication of AM 625 [8,9,10,11]. Kreitcberg et al. [10] conducted high temperature tensile tests 
for DMLS Inconel 625 alloys. They reported that, at elevated temperature, the tensile strength of 
DMLS Inconel 625 alloy does not change significantly. The tensile strength of AM Inconel 625 
samples was comparable with that of wrought Inconel 625 while the ductility of AM fabricated 
samples w as inferior to the wrought alloy at elevated temperature of 760°C [ 11]. However, 
despite of its importance in long -term service assessment, high temperature creep tests for AM 
Inconel 625 have rarely been conducted, if at all, over the short or long-term.
