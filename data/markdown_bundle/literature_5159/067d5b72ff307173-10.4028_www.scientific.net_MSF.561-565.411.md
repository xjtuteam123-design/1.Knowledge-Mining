The Effects of Nb and Cr Addition on Mechanical and Chemical 
Properites of Cold-Rolled Ni 3(Si,Ti) Intermetallic Foils 
Yasuyuki Kaneno 1, a and Takayuki Takasugi 1,2, b 
1 Department of Materials Science, Graduate School of Engineering, 
Osaka Prefecture University, 1-1 Gakuen-cho, Naka-ku, Sakai, Osaka 599-8531, Japan 
2 Osaka Center for Industrial Materials Research, Institute for Materials Research, 
Tohoku University, 1-1 Gakuen-cho, Naka-ku, Sakai, Osaka 599-8531, Japan 
akaneno@mtr.osakafu-u.ac.jp, btakasugi@mtr.osakafu-u.ac.jp 
Keywords: Nickel Silicide; L1 2 crystal structure; Cold rolling, Recrystallization; Tensile property 
Abstract . Nb and/or Cr added Ni 3(Si,Ti) as well as unalloyed Ni 3(Si,Ti) intermetallic thin foils (i.e., 
Ni 3(Si,Ti), Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Nb, Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Cr and Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Nb,Cr) were fabricated from arc-melted 
polycrystalline ingots by thermomechanical process and subsequent heavy cold-rolling. Tensile 
property at room temperature as well as at high temperature and oxidization behavior of the 
cold-rolled foils with a thickness of ~200 µm were investigated. The Ni 3(Si,Ti) and Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Nb 
alloys showed a single-phase microstructure consisting of L1 2 phase, while the Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Cr and 
Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Nb,Cr alloys exhibited a two-phase microstructure with A1 (fcc) Ni solid solution phase 
within the L1 2 grains. All the cold-rolled foils showed high tensile strength (over 2GPa) at room 
temperature although no plastic elongation was observed. The addition of Nb and/or Cr slightly 
enhanced the room-temperature tensile strength of the Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy. On the other hand, the 
addition of Nb and/or Cr prominently enhanced high-temperature tensile strength as well as 
oxidization resistance, while the addition of Cr improved high-temperature elongation. 
Introduction 
L1 2 -type Ni 3(Si,Ti) intermetallic alloy which has been developed by Ti addition to Ni 3Si alloy [1] 
possesses many attractive properties as high temperature structural materials: Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy shows a 
positive temperature dependence of yield strength [1] as well as Ni 3Si [2], and additionally exhibits 
increased strength and peak temperature in the strength vs. temperature curve compared with Ni 3Si 
[1]. In addition, the Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy exhibited high tensile ductility over a wide range of temperatures 
according to a prevention of the propensity for its intergranular fracture [1]. Recently, we have 
succeeded in fabricating the cold-rolled thin foils of the Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy as well as the Co 3Ti and the 
Ni 3Al alloys with L1 2 structure [3]. Conventionally arc-melted polycrystalline ingots are 
thermomechanically treated and then cold-rolled to thin foil with a thickness below 200 µm without 
intermediate annealing. The obtained cold-rolled intermetallic foils show high tensile strength at 
room temperature as well as at high temperature due to heavy cold rolling. To understand not only 
mechanical property but also chemical property at high temperature is of importance for practical use 
of high-temperature structural materials. In this study, cold-rolled Nb and/or Cr added Ni 3(Si,Ti) foils 
as well as the Ni 3(Si,Ti) foil were fabricated by the method mentioned above and their tensile property 
at high temperature as well as at room temperature and oxidization behavior were investigated. 
Experimental procedures 
The nominal compositions of the alloys used in this study were Ni 79.5 Si 11 Ti 9.5 (Ni 3(Si,Ti)), 
Ni 79.5 Si 11 Ti 7.5 Nb 2 (Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Nb), Ni79.5 Si 11 Ti 7.5 Cr 2 (Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Cr) and Ni 79.5 Si 11 Ti 5.5 Nb 2 Cr 2 
(Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Nb,Cr). All the alloys were doped with 50 wt.ppm boron. These alloys were prepared by 
arc melting and subsequent heat treatment for homogenization. The homogenized ingots with a 
thickness of ~10mm were rolled at 573K and then annealed at 1273K for 5 h. This procedure was

Results and discussion 
Microstructure. The Nb and/or Cr added Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloys as well as the Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy were 
successfully cold rolled to more than 90% reduction in thickness without edge cracks, indicating that 
Nb and Cr addition did not spoil cold-rolling workability of the Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy. All the cold-rolled 
Ni 3(Si,Ti) foils show deformation markings within heavily distorted grains which run along the 
transverse direction of rolling. Such deformation markings are assumed to correspond to shear bands.

microstructure. In contrast, the Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Nb,Cr (Fig.1b) foil as well as the Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Cr foil exhibit

Oxidization property. Figure 6 shows mass gain in air as a function of exposure time at 1173K for 
the cold-rolled foils. Data for the conventional nickel alloy (Inconel X-750) and stainless steel (Type 
310) are also included in this figure. All the alloys show similar mass gain curves: a decelerated mass 
gain with increasing exposure time occurred at an initial stage, and a steady state-like mass gain 
occurred at a later exposure time. Oxidization resistance was worst in the Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy among the 
alloys investigated. However, by addition of Nb and/or Cr, oxidization resistance of Ni 3(Si,Ti) is 
remarkably improved: the Nb and/or Cr added Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloys show higher oxidization resistance 
than Inconel X-750 and Type 310 stainless steel. The EPMA analysis revealed that for the Cr added 
Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloys, nearly continuous Cr 2 O3 thin layer together with discontinuous SiO 2 layer was 
formed at interface between matrix and oxidized scale. Therefore, the improved oxidization 
resistance observed in the Cr added Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloys is attributed to the formation of protective Cr 2 O3 
layer. On the other hand, for the Nb added Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy, protective SiO 2 layer may be enhanced by 
addition of Nb or diffusion process during oxidization may be suppressed by Nb. 
Conclusions 
The effects of Nb and Cr addition on mechanical and chemical properties of the Ni 3(Si,Ti) foil were 
investigated. The following results were obtained in the present study. 
(1) The Ni 3(Si,Ti) and Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Nb alloys showed a single-phase microstructure consisting of 
L1 2 phase, while the Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Cr and Ni 3(Si,Ti)+Nb,Cr alloys exhibited a two-phase 
microstructure, A1 (fcc) Ni solid solution phase within the L1 2 grains. 
(2) The Nb and/or Cr added Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloys as well as the Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy prepared through the 
thermomechanical process from arc-melted ingots were successfully cold-rolled to thin foils 
with a thickness of 200 µm. For all the alloys, cold-rolling with over 90% reduction in thickness 
was possible without providing intermediate annealing. 
(3) All the cold-rolled foils showed high tensile strength (over 2GPa) at room temperature although 
no plastic elongation was observed. Also, high temperature annealing of the cold-rolled foils 
resulted in a considerably high tensile elongation of ~30% irrespective of alloys. The addition of 
Nb and/or Cr slightly enhanced the room-temperature tensile strength and yield strength of the 
Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy. 
(4) The addition of Nb and/or Cr enhanced high-temperature tensile strength while the addition of 
Cr improved high-temperature tensile elongation. By addition of Nb and Cr to Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy, 
brittle intergranular fracture mode at high temperature deformation changed to ductile 
transgranular fracture mode. 
(5) Addition of both Nb and Cr to Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy was useful for improving the oxidization 
resistance of the Ni 3(Si,Ti) alloy. 
(6) The Nb and/or Cr added Ni 3(Si,Ti) foil showed enhanced tensile properties at high temperature 
as well as at room temperature and also good oxidization resistance, compared with 
conventional nickel alloys and stainless steels.
