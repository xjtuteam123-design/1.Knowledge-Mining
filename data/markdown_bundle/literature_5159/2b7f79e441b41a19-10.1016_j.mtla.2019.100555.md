Abstract
Generalised stacking fault energy surfaces (Γ-surfaces) are calculated for Co-
Al-W-based and Ni-Al-based superalloys from ﬁrst-principles calculations. A
Special Quasi-random Structure is employed in the calculation of the ternary
compound, Co3(Al,W). Phase ﬁeld simulations are used to compare dislocation
cores present in Co-based and Ni-based superalloys. The higher planar fault
energies of the Co-based system lead to a more constricted dislocation which
can have implications on both the bowing of dislocations as well as cross-slip.
Additionally, planar fault energies of various L1 2 compounds are compared to
explain observed segregation pathways in both types of superalloy. Both the
planar fault energies and the segregation pathways are discussed within the
context of strengthening mechanisms in superalloys.
Keywords: superalloys, density functional theory, stacking fault energy, phase
ﬁeld simulation, dislocations

2. Methodology53
The Γ-surface is obtained by cutting a crystal along one of its crystallo-54
graphic planes and displacing the upper half with respect to the lower half by55
a displacement vector, u, in the plane. The energy change due to this dis-56
placement is then plotted as a two-dimensional surface, Γ( u). The Co-based57
and Ni-based superalloys used in gas turbine engines feature L1 2 ordered γ′58
precipitates embedded in a disordered γ phase matrix; both of which are fcc59
crystals. Therefore, the crystallographic plane of interest for this work is the60
close-packed{111}-type plane of the primary slip system and the fault vector,61
u, is a superposition of the orthogonal coplanar vectors [ ¯110] and [¯1¯12].62
The Γ-surface was ﬁrst conceptualised by Vitek [4] to investigate stacking63
fault stability in bcc crystals by using a central force interaction in a hard64
sphere model. Since 1970, Γ-surfaces have been calculated using either den-65

There exist two other options when calculating Γ-surfaces using the supercell142
approach in DFT codes that implement periodic boundary conditions. Both143
options oﬀer simpler relaxation schemes, but require larger supercells. Two144
bulk slabs can be displaced with respect to each other to create a fault on145
the displacement plane. However, suﬃcient vacuum is required to ensure only146
one type of fault is created. The vacuum also creates free surfaces that must147
be treated carefully [40, 15]. Alternatively, three bulk slabs can be slid with148
respect to one another to create three identical faults which requires a very149
large supercell [29]. Hence, the tilted cell approach was chosen, as it allows for150
the smallest supercell to be used.151
Rather than mapping out the entire surface, energy calculations were per-152

Γ-surface. These points are shown in Figure 1d and their energies are numeri-154

the energy of the compound. Therefore, the VCA is not appropriate in distin-170

of the periodic table, but not for elements that are far apart, such as Al and173
W. Converging the self consistent ﬁeld loop during a DFT calculation is very174
diﬃcult in such cases.175

4. Conclusions671
Generalised stacking fault energy surfaces have been calculated for a range672
of Ni-based and Co-based compounds, using a method that includes the explicit673
relaxation of both atomic positions as well as cell volume. A special quasiran-674
dom structure was employed for the Γ-surface calculation of L12-Co3(Al,W) and675
the subsequent planar fault energies were found to be signiﬁcantly dependent676
on both local composition and conﬁguration of the Al/W atoms. The planar677
fault energies were generally found to be in agreement with values from litera-678
ture. However, the more robust relaxation method did tend to agree more with679
studies that implemented larger cells. This implies that the calculation method680
presented here, provides a robust platform from which PFMD can beneﬁt in681
simulating deformation mechanisms in superalloys.682
As such, the PFMD was used compare the dislocation cores in bothγ andγ′683
phase of Co-Al-W-based and Ni-Al-based superalloys. The higher planar fault684
energies in both phases for Co-Al-W-based superalloys caused a more constricted685
dislocation with a higher line energy.686
The Γ-surfaces for various Ni-based and Co-based L1 2 compounds were also687
calculated. The planar fault energies were compared to elucidate common solute688
segregation that has been observed in the planar faults of both Ni-based and689
Co-based superalloys. The relative values of the planar fault energies were690
generally successful in predicting the direction of solute segregation found via691
experiments.692
The range of planar fault energies found for the L1 2-Co3(Al,W) presents an693
opportunity to calculate the Γ surface of oﬀ-stoichiometric L12-Co3(Alx,W1−x).694
Additionally, the novel Γ surfaces of Co-Al-W-based superalloys can now be used695
in the PFMD to investigate the shear of the γ′ precipitates in a range of mi-696
crostructures by diﬀerent types of dislocations. This will aid in the development697
of the next generation of Co-Al-W-based superalloys.698
