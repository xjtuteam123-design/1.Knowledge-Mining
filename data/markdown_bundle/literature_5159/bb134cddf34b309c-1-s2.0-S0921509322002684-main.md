ABSTRACT 
Multiple lattice defects play a crucial role in improving the mechanical properties of FeCoCrNiMn high entropy 
alloys (HEAs). In the present work, the strength and hardness of the La
0.008
Al
0.08
FeCoCrNiMn HEA were first 
increased and then decreased during the annealing process due to the competitive mechanism between the 
recrystallization softening and precipitated phase hardening. Especially, a superior room temperature ultimate 
tensile strength (1403 MPa) and a fracture strain (9.1%) have been obtained, which was treated by quenching in 
the liquid metal, cold rolling at room temperature, and annealing at 600 
◦
C for 32 min. The characterizations and 
analysis indicate that the fundamental cause of the strengthening is attributed to the synergistic effects of nano- 
sized precipitated particles (mean size: 20.2 nm), dislocation cells, and nano-twins (~10 nm in thickness) 
together with Lomer-Cottrell locks. Meanwhile, the effects of different lattice defects on strength contribution 
have been quantified. 
1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) with equimolar or approximate equi -
molar multi-component have drawn increasing attention due to their 
potentially superior mechanical properties, exceptionally, including an 
uncommon balance between strength and ductility since the concept 
was put forward by prof. Yeh. in 2004 [ 1 ]. As a widely known class of 
HEAs, the FeCoCrNiMn HEA with a single face-centered cubic (FCC) 
lattice structure has excellent ductility; however, the strength is unde -
sirable for engineering application [ 2 – 5 ]. Therefore, acquiring 
ultra-high-strength with satisfactory ductility is pursued by many re -
searchers. Recently, many strengthening mechanisms have been used 
for improving the strength of the FeCoCrNiMn HEAs [ 6 – 9 ]. Compared 
among the above strengthening mechanisms, the precipitation 
strengthening of the secondary phases is the highly effective and 
economical approach to acquiring the high strength in the alloying 
materials via doping elements, rolling, and annealing. As Li et al. re -
ported, the strengthening FeCoCrNiMn HEA by the σ and Ni-Al rich 
phases precipitated with Al adding [ 10 ]. Carbide precipitation 
strengthening for the FeCoCrNiMn HEA, was also reported by Peng and 
his co-workers [ 11 ]. Ti and C simultaneous adding into the FeCoCrNiMn 
HEA which formed TiC second-phase particles to improve the 
room-temperature compressive yield strength, was reported by Cheng 
and his co-workers [ 12 ]. 
The above strategies depend on the single or multiple effects of the 
lattice defects. The representative defects of the FeCoCrNiMn HEA can 
be mainly categorized into four groups: (1) point defects via interstitial 
atoms; (2) linear defects such as dislocations and Lomer-Cottrell locks; 
(3) interface defects such as twins, stacking faults, and grain boundaries; 
and (4) volume defects such as precipitates and phase transformation. 
However, all the fracture strains of the HEAs were less than 7% when the 
yield strength values were higher than 1100 MPa during the tensile 
testing at room temperature. In addition, most of the above FCC-based 
HEAs have very low stacking fault energies and form twins during 
deformation [ 7 , 13 – 15 ]. The nano-twins strengthening is also a practical 
strengthening approach due to serve as effective barriers against the 
dislocation transmission [ 16 , 17 ]. 
In a word, the suitable combination of dislocations, twins, stacking 
faults, and phase transformation in the FeCoCrNiMn alloy imparted a 
good strength-ductility synergy. It is pointed out that rare earth (RE) and

4.2. Fracture mechanism 
The primary plastic deformation mechanism of metal materials was 
dislocation glide, and when the dislocations were tangled up, the 
dislocation glide became more and more complex. As a results, the 
dislocation glide was seriously inhibited, and the fracture occurred [ 36 ]. 
Fig. 7 exhibits the local phase and grain boundaries EBSD maps of 
sub-near and near the tensile fracture surfaces areas of the CR + A 32 
min sample. It is well known that, the GBs include the high-angle 
(misorientation > 15
◦

◦

Meanwhile, the low-angle boundaries were mainly consisted of dislo -
cations. Therefore, the distribution of dislocations can be reflected via 
the distribution of low-angle boundaries and the states of the disloca -
tions can be observed via the EBSD technology. There were a lot of 
crisscrossed deformation bands that consisted of dislocations in the 
sub-near area (SNA) of the tensile fracture surfaces, as shown in Fig. 7 
(a). Fig. 7 (b) demonstrates the near area (NA) of the tensile fracture 
surfaces; plenty of dislocations were getting together and entangled as a 
radial pattern. Therefore, the fracture of the tensile samples was mainly 
attributed to the dislocation tangles during the dislocation glide process. 
In addition, due to the size of the precipitated phase was growing with 
the increase of the annealing time. Therefore, the interface bonding 
between the precipitated phase and matrix became weaker and weaker, 
which led to decreases of the Young ’ s modulus and UTS [ 37 ]. As the 
result, besides the recrystallization softening, the UTS of the sample was 
decreasing after annealing at 600 
◦
C for 32 min which can be mainly 
attributed to the σ phase growing.
