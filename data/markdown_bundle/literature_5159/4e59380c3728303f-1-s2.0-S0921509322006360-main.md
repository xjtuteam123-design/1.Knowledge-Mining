ABSTRACT 
In order to investigate the effect of temperature on the tensile strength of perforated Ti – 6Al – 4V (TC4) alloy, the 
tensile mechanical properties of TC4 alloy with various micro-hole array distributions were obtained at a tem -
perature in a range from 20 
◦
C to 500 
◦
C by using a novel mechanical-thermal coupling tensile test instrument 
developed by ourselves. The experimental results showed that the tensile strength of all types of TC4 alloy 
specimens decreased with the increase in temperature, and the elastic modulus of most types of TC4 alloy 
specimens decreased with the increase in temperature. The tensile strength of the perforated specimen iii was 
14.8% lower than that of the solid specimen and the elastic modulus of perforated specimen ii was 5.5% lower 
than that of the solid specimen at room temperature (RT), and all types of the perforated specimens continued to 
exhibit relatively lower tensile strengths and elastic modulus at 180 
◦
C and 340 
◦
C. Abnormally, the tensile 
strength of the perforated specimen iii was 2.1% higher than that of the solid specimen and the elastic modulus of 
perforated specimen ii was 23% higher than that of the solid specimen at 500 
◦
C. With the increase of tem -
perature from 20 
◦
C to 500 
◦
C, the fracture mode of the solid specimen gradually changed from equiaxed dimple 
to shear dimple. Regarding the perforated specimens, the cracks and dimples adjacent to the holes gradually 
decreased as a function of temperature. The existence of prefabricated holes in the specimens dissipated part of 
the thermal stress, maintained the integrity of the micro-hole structure, and made the nearby section relatively 
flat, which induced a relatively higher tensile strength and structural stiffness at an elevated temperature. The 
relatively lower stress in the gauge section of the perforated specimens obtained through finite element analysis 
also indicated the temperature-induced enhancement of tensile strength. 
1. Introduction 
Titanium alloys exhibit high specific strength [ 1 , 2 ], low density [ 3 ], 
excellent biocompatibility [ 4 ], wear resistance [ 3 ], and favorable 
toughness [ 5 ], which could meet the needs of modern aviation materials 
and become a potential substitute for steels and aluminum alloys [ 6 ]. 
TC4 alloy was the first generation of high-temperature titanium alloy 
developed by the United States in 1954. At present, the use of TC4 alloy 
has reached more than 50% of the use of titanium alloy in the world [ 7 ]. 
Although titanium alloys have various excellent properties, their 
mechanical properties will be weakened at elevated temperatures 
[ 8 – 17 ]. In particular, the micro failure mechanism and deformation 
damage behavior of titanium alloy under mechanical-thermal coupling 
need to be taken into further account. Meanwhile, Lu et al. [ 18 ] pre -
pared a new high strength titanium alloy Ti-1300 with superior strength 
by controlling the precipitation behavior of the alpha phase. Through 
the hot tensile deformation of TA32 titanium alloy sheets at 800 
◦
C, Fan 
et al. [ 19 ] found that increasing the heat treatment temperature can 
increase the ultimate tensile strength of titanium alloy. In addition to the 
optimization of alloy composition and heat treatment to improve the

Novel testing instruments are needed to reveal the micro failure 
mechanism and deformation damage of titanium alloys. In particular, 
in-situ testing can obtain the correlation between mechanical properties 
and microstructure evolution at the same time [ 20 ]. The existing test 
instruments are seldom mentioned to realize the in-situ quasi-static test 
of titanium alloy specimens with characteristic size at millimeter under 
the condition of mechanical-thermal coupling, which limits the in-depth 
understanding of its micro failure mechanism and deformation damage 
[ 21 – 27 ]. The micro-hole array structure can play an active role in the 
mechanical properties of the matrix. For example, many studies have 
shown that the residual stress of the structure can be released through 
the design of holes, Lee et al. [ 28 ] designed a micro-hole array structure 
on flexible electrodes and thin film transistors to release bending stress. 
Many biological hard tissues also achieve high strength and impact 
toughness through their hole structure. The plate-like spongy bones with 
a hole structure in the woodpecker ’ s head may have served to bear high 
stress or absorb shock stress resulting from pecking or probing [ 29 ]. 
Therefore, the preparation of micro-hole array structure on the solid 
specimen may be an effective way to improve the deformation capacity 
and strength characteristics of the body. Especially considering that the 
micro-hole array structure and solid structure show different thermal 
expansion and thermal stress effects under elevated temperature, the 
micro-hole array structure may absorb part of the thermal stress and 
improve the high-temperature strength. 
In this paper, the mechanical-thermal coupling quasi-static tensile 
tests of TC4 alloy at various temperatures were carried out combined 
with the design of micro-hole array, the change law of strength was 
obtained, and the mechanism of micro-hole array structure on strength 
improvement was described in detail. 
2. Experimental procedures 
The material used in the test was annealed TC4 alloy sheet with a 
thickness of 1 mm. The holes in the specimen were processed by laser 
drilling. The shape of the specimen was processed by the wire cut 
electrical discharge machine. The geometry of the specimen is shown in 
Fig. 1 , in which the gauge segment size was 3 mm × 16 mm. The number 
of holes in the specimen from left to right increased in turn. Specimen i 
was solid without holes, and the number of holes in specimens ii, iii, and 
iv was 3, 7, and 11 in turn. The diameter of the micro-hole array 
distributed on the symmetry axis in the vertical direction was 300 μ m. 
The center distance between them was 3.5 mm. The diameter of holes 
symmetrically distributed in the center was 200 μ m. The spacing of the 
center of the circle in the horizontal direction was 1.5 mm and the 
spacing in the vertical direction was 3.5 mm. The symmetrical semi -
circle of the clamping part at both ends was designed to locate the 
specimen and weaken the axial deviation. 
The mechanical-thermal coupling tensile test was completed on an 
independently developed testing instrument. As shown in Fig. 2 , the 
instrument was mainly composed of charge-coupled devices, zoom 
lenses, force sensors, lead screws, and heating blocks. TC4 alloy speci -
mens were positioned by four screws and gaskets and provided with 
sufficient clamping force by tightening. The motors drove the lead 
screws to drive the square area clamped at both ends of the specimen to 
move at the same time to realize the quasi-static tensile process. The 
heating block provided high-temperature loading to simulate the service 
of the specimen in the complex environment of mechanical-thermal 
coupling. The force sensors recorded the tensile load on the specimen.

4. Conclusions 
Through uniaxial static tensile tests and finite element analysis of 
solid and perforated Ti – 6Al – 4V specimens under mechanical-thermal 
coupling conditions, the temperature-induced enhancement of tensile 
strength and structural stiffness of perforated specimens was experi -
mentally verified. Although the tensile strengths and structural stiffness 
of both solid and perforated specimens decreased as a function of tem -
perature, the abnormal strength and structural stiffness enhancement of 
the perforated specimen at 500 
◦
C compared with the solid specimen 
was confirmed. The prefabricated holes dissipated part of the thermal 
stress and maintained the integrity of the micro-hole structure, then 
improved the structural stiffness of the specimen effectively. The frac -
ture morphologies indicated disparate fracture modes of solid and 
perforated specimens, as the cracks nucleated at the edges of the pre -
fabricated holes. Although the prefabricated hole induced a stress con -
centration effect and the finite element analysis also revealed the local 
high-stress distribution adjacent to the hole, the average stress level in 
the gauge length section of the perforated specimens was lower than the 
average stress of solid specimen. Attributed to the existence of
