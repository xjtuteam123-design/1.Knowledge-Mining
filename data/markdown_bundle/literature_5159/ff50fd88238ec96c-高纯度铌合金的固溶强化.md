Solid Solution Strengthening of High Purity Niobium 
Alloys 
J. R. SLINING AND D. A. KOSS 
Athermal solid solution strengthening was examined in substitutional Nb(Cb)-Hf, Nb-W, and 
Nb-Ta alloys deformed at 900 K. Ultrahigh vacuum degassed single crystals were tested in 
compression. The concentration dependence of the yield stress is nearly linear in Nb-Hf 
and Nb-W alloys for the range of compositions tested (< 6 at. pct). The rate of hardening 
with solute addition was the greatest for the Nb-Hf alloys, considerably less for the Nb-W 
alloys, and the least for the Nb-Ta alloys which exhibited little strengthening. The results 
indicate that athermal strengthening in these alloys appears to be primarily dependent on 
the atomic size misfit between solvent and solute atoms and not on the elastic modulus mis- 
fit. Results on the slip behavior are also presented. 
IN the past ten years, substitutional solid solution hard- 
ening in bcc alloys has been the subject of several stud- 
ies. Interpretation of the resulting data is complicated 
by two factors. First, the low temperature flow stress 
of these alloys consists of both thermally activated and 
athermal components. I Quite often little attempt has 
been made to separate the two components and to ana- 
lyze them individually. Secondly, it has been recently 
shown that small amounts of interstitial impurities can 
have significant effects on the deformation properties 
of bcc substitutional alloys, at least at low tempera- 
tures. ~'4 Thus, examination of the mechanism of sub- 
stitutional hardening requires the near absence of in- 
terstitial impurities, lest they mask the substitutional 
solute effects. Although thermally activated solution 
hardening has been examined in "purified" substitu- 
tional systems, a'4 only the very recent study by Jax s 
examined the athermal flow stress in bcc alloys of 
high interstitial purity. Jax observed the athermal 
yield stress of selected Nb-Mo, Nb-Pd, and Nb-W al- 
loys employing ultrahigh vacuum degassed crystals. 
The resultant data indicated that the yield stress was 
influenced by interstitial content and increased in ap- 
proximately a linear manner with substitutional con- 
centration. However, interpretation of the rate of hard- 
ening in terms of atomic size or modulus misfit was 
complicated by the relatively large magnitude of both 
the atomic size and modulus misfit between solute and 
solvent atoms; as a result, no completely satisfactory 
correlation was found. The present study examines the 
athermal yield stress of three high purity bcc substi- 
tutional alloy systems which consist of: a) Nb-Ta 
(small modulus misfit and nearly no difference in 
atomic sizes), b) Nb-Hf (small modulus misfit plus 
large atomic size misfit), and c) Nb-W (large modulus 
misfit plus large atomic size misfit).

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
A) Stress-Strain Behavior and Slip Line 
Analysis 
The effect of solute content on the deformation be- 
havior in compression of Nb-Hf single crystals tested 
at 900 K is shown in Fig. 2. Because of the end con- 
straints acting on the specimen during the tests, only 
the behavior at relatively small strains is shown. Op- 
tical microscopy confirms that only one macroscopic 
slip system is active. The primary effect of increas- 
ing substitutional solute content is to raise the yield 
stress. With the exception of the Nb-4.7 Hf alloy, the 
rate of work hardening is only slightly affected, usu- 
ally increasing a very small amount with increasing 
alloy content. This is true not only for the dilute Nb- 
Hf alloys but also for the Nb-Ta and Nb-W alloys, 
which strain harden at approximately the same rate 
as pure niobium but with the yield stress displaced to 
slightly higher values. The most concentrated Nb-4.7 
I-If alloy (which has the highest yield stress of all the 
alloys tested) shows a tendency for parabolic harden- 
ing with a higher work hardening rate than the dilute 
alloys. 
The active macroscopic slip plane was determined 
from deformed crystals. For all alloys, the observed 
slip plane was within 4 deg of the maximum resolved 
shear stress (MRSS) plane. These results are consis- 
tent with the behavior of UHV degassed niobium crys- 
tals 9 in which slip tending to the MRSS plane was ob- 
served as the deformation temperature approached 
the athermal, plateau region.

C) The Rate of Hardening 
Athermal solid solution hardening is due to the long 
range interaction of dislocations with solute atoms. 
The size of the interaction determines the effective- 
ness in immobilizing dislocations and therefore the 
rate of hardening per solute addition. The magnitude 
of the dislocation-solute interaction can be expressed 
in terms of misfit parameters based on a difference 
in atomic size and/or modulus. 14 
The atomic size misfit parameter, 1/a (da/dc), rep- 
resents the difference in atomic size between solvent 
and solute atoms and has been determined from mea- 
surements of the lattice parameter (a) as a function of 
solute composition for high vacuum degassed Nb-W and 
Nb-Hf alloys. 1~'~8 Lattice parameter data are also 
available for the Nb-Ta system ~7'~8 and indicates that 
tantalum has a very small size misfit. The values of 
the atomic size misfit parameters are listed in

The data in Table II shows that the rate of harden- 
ing appears to be primarily dependent on the atomic 
size misfit and not on the elastic modulus misfit. Haf- 
nium, which strengthens niobium, at a rate which is 
nearly twice that for tungsten, appears to have a mod- 
erately small modulus misfit for both edge and screw 
dislocations (eK11 and eKaa, respectively). Our estimate 
of this modulus misfit would have to be grossly in er- 
ror (by a factor of more than three ) if one is to ac- 
count for the observed strengthening primarily on the 
basis of elastic modulus misfit. Differences in inter- 
stitial puritY also cannot account for the large differ- 
ence in hardening rates between the Nb-Hf and Nb-W 
alloys. It is likely that the substitutional solute addi- 
tions cause changes in the activity of the interstitals, 
thereby affecting the residual gas content. However, 
chemical analysis shows no gross differences in inter- 
stitial purity between the Nb-Hf and Nb-W alloys. In 
addition, Jax s has shown that the rate of athermal 
hardening of substitutional niobium alloys is a weak 
function of interstitial solute content. Jax's data 5 sug- 
gests that only about 25 pct of the observed difference 
in hardening rates between Nb-Hf and Nb-W could be 
explained if the bib-I-If were as impure as typical zone 
refined material (chemical analyses indicates the Nb- 
Hf to be of better purity). On the other hand, the haf- 
nium atom differs considerably in atomic size from 
the solvent niobium (6.0 pet). The introduction of a 
relatively large elastic strain field around the larger 
hafnium atoms appears to be the primary cause for the 
rapid hardening in these alloys. 
The rate of hardening cannot be interpreted solely 
on the basis of atomic size misfit. Although the rate 
of hardening in Nb-Hf alloys is nearly twice that for 
Nb-W, the atomic size misfit is only about 20 pet 
greater. Thus the rate of hardening would have to be 
an unreasonably sensitive function of size misfit (tom-

C ONC LUSIONS 
The concentration dependence of the athermal yield 
stress for high purity Nb-Hf and Nb-W alloys is nearly 
linear, although no unique concentration dependence 
was observed. Among the substitutional alloy additions 
examined, hafnium was found to be about twice as ef- 
fective in strengthening niobium as was tungsten; tan- 
talum had little strengthening effe.ct. The results indi- 
cate that the principal, but not sole, cause of solid 
solution hardening in these alloys is the difference in 
atomic size between solvent and solute atoms. Maxi- 
mum resolved shear stress slip is observed. The ini- 
tial work hardening rate for the dilute alloys shows a 
very small increase with solute content.
