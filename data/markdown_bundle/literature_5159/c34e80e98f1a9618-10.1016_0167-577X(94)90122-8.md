Diffusion bonding of the high temperature Ti alloy IMI 829 
to Fe-Ni alloy IN 907 using interlayers 
A. Wisbey, C.M. Ward-Close 
Structural Materials Centre, Defence Research Agency, Farnborough, Hants, GU14 6TD, UK 
Received 17 May 1994; accepted 15 June 1994 
Abstract 
There is considerable interest in the use of titanium alloys in place of steels and nickel alloys, however, the poor tribological 
properties of titanium have mitigated against its widespread use. Wear resistant surfaces of an iron-nickel alloy IN 907 have 
been diffusion bonded to the high temperature titanium alloy IMI 829, using interlayers to aid bonding. High shear strength 
joints have been produced. A combination of Ta and Ni interlayers has been found to be particularly advantageous, increasing 
both the shear strength and the plastic deformation to fracture. 
1. Introduction 
The use of titanium alloys is limited by their poor 
wear resistance [ 11, fretting fatigue and galling. 
However, significant weight savings in aerospace ap- 
plications may be possible if titanium alloys were used 
in components currently manufactured in steel or 
nickel alloys. Many of these components are associ- 
ated with gas turbines and aerodynamic control sur- 
face actuation systems. In the gas turbine, elevated 
temperature performance is required and in this work 
the Ti alloy IMI 829, capable of operation at a tem- 
perature of 540°C has been used as the substrate for 
the diffusion bonding of a more wear resistant sur- 
face. The wear resistant surface was the Fe-Ni alloy 
IN 907, which has a similar coefficient of thermal ex- 
pansion to the IMI 829 and should thus minimise any 
thermal stresses at the joint interface. 
Diffusion bonding (DB) can produce high per- 
formance joints and also permits close control of the 
joint interface. This control makes the technique par- 
ticularly advantageous for producing dissimilar metal

4. Discussion 
Diffusion bonds have been made successfully be- 
tween the high temperature Ti alloy IMI 829 and the 
Fe-Ni alloy IN 907. Various interlayers and combi- 
nations of interlayers were tested and a significant 
improvement in the shear strength was measured 
when using a Ta and Ni interlayer combination. The 
Ta and Ni interlayers increased the plastic deforma- 
tion prior to fracture in the joints. For joints without 
interlayers there was little or no plastic deformation 
at the joint interface before fracture through brittle 
intermetallic compounds. The fracture of the joints 
with Ta and Ni interlayers depended on the local shear 
strain and strain hardening in the interlayers. Intense 
deformation in the Ta and Ni interlayers was ob- 
served and this has also been reported for Ti-6 Al-4 
V and Co base Stellite HS6 DB joints [ 71; increases 
in the interlayer microhardness were found after shear 
testing, consistent with the high shear strengths mea- 
sured. These high shear strengths, in some cases higher 
than would be predicted for the bulk interlayer ma- 
terial, were probably a result of the constraint im- 
posed on the thin interlayers by the stronger IMI 829 
and IN 907. 
The majority of the interlayers gave little advan- 
tage in shear strength compared to no interlayers. 
With the V and Cu interlayer combination no reac- 
tion products were detected, excepting the p stabilis- 
ation, and fracture occurred through the Cu inter- 
layer suggesting that the shear strength was 
determined by the mechanical properties of the soft 
Cu interlayer. However, the other interlayers formed 
intermetallic compounds either with the IN 907 (e.g. 
Zr or Ta) or between the interlayers themselves (e.g. 
Nb and Ni interlayers) and the shear strength of the 
joint was dependent on the mechanical properties of

Elsewhere it has been shown that once sufficient re- 
action had occurred to form a joint then the strength 
of a pure Ti to steel joint decreased as the reaction 
layer thickness increases [ 8 ] ; this trend may be seen 
with the Ta interlayer joints bonded for 1 and 4 h. 
After the DB thermal cycle the IN 907 parent metal 
shear strength had reduced from = 770 to z 630 MPa 
and this suggests that the joint may require post DB 
heat treatment to recover the full IN 907 strength. 
The mechanical properties of the joint may be fur- 
ther enhanced by the use of a contoured interface [ 91. 
FezTi and NiTi* intermetallic compounds were 
formed during the DB of IMI 829 and IN 907 with- 
out interlayers; the FezTi compound has also been 
noted in DB joints of Ti-6242 and stainless steel 3 16L 
[ lo], whilst NiTi, has been found in DB joints of 
pure Ti and Ni [ 111. The studies of the Ti/stainless 
steel and Ti/Ni joints showed very complex interfa- 
cial regions with multiple reaction layers and al- 
though not fully detailed in this work, since only X- 
ray diffraction of shear fracture surfaces was used, 
similar behaviour may be expected here. 
In other published work, shear testing has been used 
extensively to evaluate the strength of DB joints [ 12 1, 
but much of this has used simple tensile overlap type 
test specimens. Work on DB of aluminium alloys has 
demonstrated that significant peel stresses occur in 
tensile overlap specimens [ 13,141. In an attempt to 
reduce these peel stresses and more nearly achieve 
pure shear the compressive shear arrangement used 
in this work was devised. Despite the provision of 
constraining blocks adjacent to the test blocks a small 
degree (5-10” from the vertical) of tilting of the test 
blocks during shearing was still observed but was sig- 
nificantly less than for the same overlap length using 
tensile shear testing. 
5. Conclusions 
High shear strength diffusion bonds have been 
produced between the Ti alloy IMI 829 and the more 
wear resistant Fe-Ni alloy IN 907. Several interlay- 
ers were investigated as an aid to bonding and a com- 
bination of Ta and Ni interlayers was found to signif- 
icantly enhance the joint shear strength and plastic 
deformation prior to fracture. Complex interfacial 
microstructures were produced in the DB joints.
