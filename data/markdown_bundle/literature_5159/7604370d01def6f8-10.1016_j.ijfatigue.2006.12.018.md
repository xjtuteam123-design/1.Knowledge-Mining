Fatigue life evaluation of a single crystal Ni-base superalloy,
accompanying with change of microstructural morphology
Motoki Sakaguchi *, Masakazu Okazaki *
Nagaoka University of Technology, Tomioka, Nagaoka 940-2188, Japan
Received 31 August 2006; received in revised form 15 December 2006; accepted 15 December 2006
Available online 7 February 2007
Abstract
A new micromechanics model was proposed to estimate the eﬀects of microstructural geometry on fatigue life of single crystal Ni-base
superalloys. The Eshelby’s theory, the averaging ﬁeld approximation and the hybrid composite theory were combined and extended so
that it makes possible to discuss many types of microstructural factors: volume fraction of c
0 precipitates, c/c0 lattice misﬁt, and inho-
mogeneities between c/c0 phases against elastic or plastic deformation. The presented method can be applied to such a material system in
which the c/c0 morphology is changed during the cyclic loading at high temperature. The numerical calculations demonstrated that the
plate-type rafted structure perpendicular to uni-axial stress direction may have detrimental eﬀect on the isothermal fatigue strength for
negative misﬁt superalloys.

1. Introduction
Single crystal Ni-base superalloys are used nowadays
more and more commonly as blade materials for industrial
gas turbines or aircraft engines, since they have superior
creep strength at elevated temperatures [1]. Generally, the
superalloys have naturally developed composite micro-
structure consisting of a solid solution matrix, c, with
Ni
3(Al, Ti) intermetallic precipitates, c0 [1,2]. The micro-
structure in high- c0-volume fraction alloys such as
CMSX-4 and SRR-99 consists of regularly arrayed cuboi-
dal c
0 precipitates surrounded by narrow channels of c
matrix [1,2]. Many researchers have pointed out that this
type of unique geometry of the c/c microstructure substan-
tially inﬂuences the mechanical behaviors of single crystal
superalloys [2–5]. During the gas turbine’s operation per-
iod, on the other hand, severe directional coarsening, the
so-called rafting, of the initially cuboidal c0 precipitates
to the plate-like or needle-like structure can occur locally
in hot sections [1,2]. The plate-like rafts, which are devel-
oped perpendicular to the uni-axial applied stress, are usu-
ally associated with negative misﬁt superalloys stressed in
tension, or positive misﬁt ones stressed in compression
[2,6,7]. On the contrary, the needle-like rafts, which are
developed parallel to the stress direction, are associated
with positive misﬁt superalloys stressed in tension, and neg-
ative misﬁt ones stressed in compression [2,6,7]. Several
investigations have dealt with the inﬂuence of rafted micro-
structures on high temperature creep strength [8], while less
attention has been focused on the eﬀects on the high tem-
perature fatigue properties [9,10]. As an example, Ott and
Mughrabi [9] performed the LCF tests on CMSX-4 speci-
mens with three diﬀerent initial c/c
0 morphologies; cuboi-
dal c0 particles, plate-like rafts (rafts perpendicular to
stress axis) and needle-like rafts (rafts parallel to stress
axis), respectively. Their experiments showed that the fati-
gue lives were reduced for specimens with the plate-like
rafts, and extended for specimens with the needle-type

ﬁcial for high temperature creep resistance [8], since the
continuous c/c
0 interfaces perpendicular to stress direction
prevent the dislocations climb motion. However, the pres-
ent work suggests that the plate-type rafts may rather
reduce the fatigue strength.
As documented in Section 2, a homogeneous damage
evolution process in the narrow c channel in which all
the slip systems are activated to achieve a homogeneous
plastic deformation is assumed in this work. This process
should not be always the case. Thus, the present model
should be extended for a case in which a limited slip sys-
tem(s) is (are) activated, where an internal stress ﬁeld diﬀer-
ent from that in this work may be built up. This aspect will
be discussed in the near future.
4. Conclusions
An extended micromechanics model was proposed,
based on the Eshelby’s theory, Mori–Tanaka’s averaging
ﬁeld approximation and the Hybrid composite theory.
The presented method enables us to quantitatively discuss
the eﬀects of the c/c
0 microstructural geometry on the high
temperature fatigue strength as a function of the c/c0 lattice
misﬁt. It was found by the numerical calculations that the
plate-type rafted structure perpendicular to uni-axial stress
direction may have detrimental eﬀect on the isothermal low
cycle fatigue strength for negative misﬁt superalloys.
