ABSTRACT 
In order to increase the ductility of alumina-forming Co-Cr-Al oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) superalloys, 
Ni alloying was introduced into the superalloys. Four grades of Co-20Cr-15Al-xNi (x = 0, 10, 20 and 30) at.% 
ODS superalloys were fabricated by mechanical alloying (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS). The superalloys 
were composed of a Co solid solution matrix with face-centered cubic (fcc) structure and an Al-rich (Co, Ni)Al 
phase with body-centered cubic (B2) structure. Ni addition did not change the phase components, but inhibited 
the formation of the brittle B2 phase by expanding the solid solution limit of Al in both the fcc matrix and the B2 
phase. Compared to the completely brittle fracture of Ni-free superalloy, Ni-added superalloys exhibited ductile 
fracture and a linear increase in ductility with increasing Ni content, facilitated by the reduction of B2 phase. 
Preferential fracture starting from B2 phase was confirmed during the tensile test at room temperature. 
Deformation-induced nanotwins in the fcc phase was observed, which is expected to improve the strength and 
ductility of the Ni-added Co-Cr-Al ODS superalloys. 
1. Introduction 
Alumina-forming Co-Cr-Al ODS superalloys possess promising ap -
plications as high temperature alloys due to their superior oxidation 
resistance and outstanding strength stability at high temperatures [ 1 – 3 ]. 
To ensure the formation of an external alumina scale, a relatively high Al 
addition is needed. In contrast to the detrimental effect of high Al 
addition on the strength of conventional carbide or γ’ precipitation 
strengthened Co-based alloys [ 4 – 6 ], the Al addition in Co-Cr-Al ODS 
superalloys does not weaken the strength because its negative effect on 
the reinforcing precipitates, namely, dispersed oxide nanoparticles, can 
be overcome by the doping of Hf to form fine Y

typical highly brittle phase, and its formation will significantly reduce 
the ductility of the Co-Cr-Al ODS superalloys [ 8 ]. Previous work on the

needs to be found. However, it seems to be extremely limited to main -

concentration, as even the alloy with 10 Al (at.%) addition showed 
limited plastic deformation with only 1.2% elongation in tensile tests at 
room temperature (RT) [ 8 ]. 
Microstructural modulation and the addition of alloying elements 
are two of the most commonly used methods to improve the ductility of 
alloys. Considering that ODS alloys are characterized by a very stable 
microstructure, it is more feasible to add alloying elements than to 
change the microstructure to improve the ductility of the alumina- 
forming Co-Cr-Al ODS superalloys. The introduction of Ni alloying 
into the Co-Cr-Al ODS system was first considered because Ni is also a 
high-temperature alloying element and is a well-known stabilizer of the 
plastic fcc phase. It has been shown that the addition of Ni to CoCrFeAl 
high-entropy alloys (HEA) induces the formation of the plastic fcc phase 
and leads to a significant increase in ductility [ 11 ]. Mukherji et al. 
investigated the effect of Ni addition on the mechanical properties of Co- 
based alloys and found that the addition of 15 at.% Ni to Co-17Re-23Cr

3.2. Microstructure evolution 
The BSE images of the microstructure of the fabricated Co-Cr-Al ODS 
superalloys are presented in Figs. 3 (a-d). Based on the apparent contrast 
differences in the BSE images, it is certain that the four superalloys 
consist of two main phases, namely, a light grey matrix and a dark region 
of 0.3 – 2.5 μ m scale. As shown in the XRD results above, since the Co 
solid solution was found to dominate the crystalline structure of the 
samples, it is reasonable to assert that the light grey matrix in the BSE 
images is the one with this Co solid solution with fcc structure, and the 
light grey matrix should be the (Co, Ni)Al phase with B2 structure. In 
order to know the overall elemental distribution of each component 
phase, EPMA elemental mapping was conducted for the samples. As 
shown in the Figs. 4 (a-d), the grey matrix is enriched in Co and Cr, while 
the dark region is significantly enriched in Al. In addition, with the 
addition of Ni in the Co-Cr-Al ODS superalloys, Ni enrichment was found 
in the dark region. Previous work using the computational thermody -
namic software Pandat calculated that the Al concentration of the CoAl 
phase formed in the Co-Cr-Al alloy is approximately three times greater 
than that of Al in the Co matrix [ 7 ]. Therefore, it is certain that the Al- 
rich dark region corresponds to the CoAl phase, and the addition of Ni

In addition, it is also noteworthy that the Al content in the (Co, Ni)Al-

decreased with the addition of Ni because the total Al content in each

WDS data, respectively. It is clear that the content of the B2 phase 
decreased with increasing Ni content, which is in good agreement with 
the XRD results in Fig. 2 .

The increment of Al concentration in the (Co, Ni)Al-B2 phase is 
thought to be caused by the increased affinity for Al by the Ni substi -
tution for Co. Liu et al. [ 17 ] calculated the Gibbs energy of formation for 
CoAl-B2 and NiAl-B2 using CALPHAD, which confirmed that NiAl-B2

high temperatures above 1500 
◦

Gibbs energy of the NiAl-B2 and CoAl-B2 at 1100

finity for Al of Ni than Co could be expected in the B2 intermetallic 
compounds, which induced the substitution of Ni for Co in the original 
CoAl phase and led to the formation of the (Co, Ni)Al phase in the Ni- 
added samples. Even though the addition of Ni may accelerate the for -
mation of B2 by forming the (Co,Ni)Al-B2 with a lower Gibbs energy of 
formation, the process should still be governed by the Al content 
remaining in the alloy after solid solution in the Co-fcc matrix.

adding Ni is beneficial to improve the tensile ductility of the Co-Cr-Al 
ODS superalloys at RT. 
In order to understand the fracture mechanism during the tensile 
loading, TEM observation was performed for the 20Ni sample after the 
RT tensile test, where the TEM sample was picked up by the FIB machine 
near the fracture edge. Figs. 10 (a-d) show the bright field (BF) and 
corresponding high-angle annular dark field (HAADF) STEM images and 
selected area diffraction (SAD) patterns. Based on the bright contrast in 
the BF image in the Fig. 10 (a), it is obvious to find that cracks appeared 
after the RT tensile test. Besides, the cracks were totally located in the 
dark region of the HAADF image in the Fig. 10 (b). According to the SAD 
patterns of Figs. 10 (c) and (d), it is evident that the dark and the grey 
regions are identified as B2 and fcc structures, respectively. Due to the 
inherent brittleness of the B2 structure, it is reasonable to accept that the 
cracks initially appeared into the B2 phase during the tensile testing.

In the case of the deformation happened in the fcc matrix, according 
to the HAADF STEM image in Fig. 12 (a), it demonstrated that the 
deformation zones (DZs) were located at the phase boundaries of the B2 
and fcc matrix, as shown by the green arrow. Fig. 12 (b) shows corre -
sponding BF image and an interesting finding is that deformation 
nanotwins can be observed in the fcc phase close to the DZs, as shown by 
the red arrows. A local enlargement of the bits where the nanotwins 
appear is shown in Figs. 12 (c) and (d), i.e., the BF and low-angle annular 
dark field (LAADF) STEM images, where the DZs induced by tensile 
loading can be clearly identified by showing a white contrast in the 
LAADF image due to their lower thickness than the surroundings. Since 
the fcc-matrix possesses a metastable fcc structure and twinning defor -
mation would occur during the tensile deformation at RT, which are able 
to increase both the UTS strength and ductility [ 21 , 22 ]. Ni addition is 
not favorable for the formation of the deformation twins because it 
would increase the stacking fault energy of the Co-based alloys [ 23 , 24 ]. 
Thus, a lower stacking fault energy should be present in 0Ni, making it 
easier to form the deformation nanotwins in the RT tensile test. How -
ever, the extremely low elongation of 0Ni means that the effect of 
deformation nanotwins on improving ductility is still limited by its 
intrinsic brittleness.

deserving of consideration. Even though the dense Y

addition. A detailed investigation on the SFE and slip patterns of the fcc 
matrix in the Co-Cr-Al ODS superalloys with different Ni contents should 
be carried out next.
