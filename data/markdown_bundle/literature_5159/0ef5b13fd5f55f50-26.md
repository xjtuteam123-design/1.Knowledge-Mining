ABSTRACT
The grain size of as-cast light weight high entropy alloy (LHEA) is required to be refined and its mechanical 
property should be improved further to satisfy its engineering application. In this paper, we investigated the 
effect of TiN on the microstructure and mechanical property of as-cast TiZrVNbAl LHEA with body-centered 
cubic (BCC) structure. TiN can serve as the potent nucleating agent, and reduce the grain size of as-cast ingot 
from 275 μ m to 97 μ m with 5 wt% addition. Part of TiN would dissolve during melting and reprecipitate again in 
the plate-like shape (~0.3 μ m thickness) at the grain interiors and grain boundaries during solidification, which 
could increase the strength but decrease the ductility. 3 wt% TiN addition can increase the yield strength of 
TiZrVNbAl LHEA near two times, from 804 MPa to 1603 MPa and maintain ~25 % compression ductility. The 
significant strength improvement is ascribed to the synergy strengthening effect of TiN, interstitial N atom and 
refined grains. Our work is a valuable reference for the improvement of mechanical properties of as-cast BCC 
LHEAs.
1. Introduction
Lightweight high-entropy alloys (LHEAs) with body-centered cubic 
(BCC) structures have received extensive attention due to their high 
specific strength [1 – 3] , high elevated temperature strength [4] , good 
corrosion [5] and wear resistance [6] . TiZrNbVAl LHEA system usually 
exhibits considerable strength and ductility owing to the solid solution 
strengthening effect and low valence electron concentration [7,8] , 
which are promising candidates for the applications in the field of 
aerospace, automobile, and marine.
Nevertheless, the exploration of BCC-LHEAs with superior mechan -
ical properties is still a hot research topic. To date, several strengthening 
strategies have been applied to enhance the strength of BCC-HEAs. For 
example, the solid solution strengthening resulting from atomic radius 
mismatch [9,10] , the precipitation strengthening caused by the intro -
duction of the second phase [11,12] , and the grain boundary strength -
ening following deformation and recrystallization treatments [13,14] . 
However, these strengthening methods have a limited strength 
improvement on BCC-LHEAs, generally less than 300 MPa, which limits 
their application in fields demanding high strength.
Recent studies have shown that the addition of ceramic particles, 
such as carbide [15] , nitride [16] and oxide [17] can significantly 
improve the strength of the HEAs. Wang et al. [18] increased the yield 
strength from 1250 MPa to 2500 MPa by adding Al

is still maintained well ( > 10 %). Therefore, adding the ceramic phase 
having low lattice mismatch with the matrix is an effective approach to 
improve the strength of BCC-LHEAs and not to scarify its ductility 
significantly.

In this work, non-equimolar TiZrVNbAl BCC-LHEA system was 
selected as the model alloy because of its high specific yield strength, 
excellent ductility, and excellent phase stability, which had been indi -
cated in our previous reports [3] . The as-cast structure of this LHEA has a 
wide application prospect in many engineering components, such as 
aircraft rudder transmission brackets, shielding plates and wings. Then, 
TiN was selected as the low lattice mismatch ceramic phase in it to refine 
the grain size and strengthen the alloy (Supplementary Note 1 and 
Fig. S1 of Supplementary materials .). The effect of TiN on the micro -

2. Experimental procedures
Non-equiatomic TiZrNbVAl LHEA with different TiN addition (0, 3, 
and 5 wt%) were prepared by the levitation melting furnace in argon 
atmosphere. Hereafter, they were named as TiN-0, TiN-3 and TiN-5 
alloy, respectively. Because TiN would decompose to generate N atom 
in TiZrNb-based HEAs [19,25,26] , and the solid solubility limit of N in 
Zr is the smallest among the binary phase diagram of N and metal ele -
ments of the investigated composition in this study, which is 4.4 at% 
( Fig. S2 of the Supplementary materials ), we designed alloys with TiN 
concentrations greater than 4.4 at% (5 wt% in the experiment) to keep 
more TiN particles in the alloy. For comparison, we also designed the 
content of TiN less than 4.4 at% (3 wt% in the experiment). The raw 
material purity of Ti, Zr, Nb, V, and Al was more than 99.95 wt%, and 
that of TiN was and the size of TiN particle (The purity was more than 
99 %) was ~300 nm. In order to ensure the elemental uniformity of the 
ingot, each ingot was melted three times, and the alloy ingot was flipped 
before each melting. Then, TiN was uniformly put to the surrounding of 
the ingot and the ingot was melt again and hold for 30 s followed by 
furnace cooling. The ingot had a diameter of 50 mm and a height of 
50 mm. The oxygen concentration in the alloy was tested by G8 Galileo 
ON/H analyzer, and the actual oxygen concentration of TiN-0, TiN-3 
and TiN-5 LHEAs is shown in Table S1 of Supplemental materials . The 
results show that only a small amount of oxygen impurities is introduced 
in the melting process, which has a slight effect on the microstructure 
and mechanical properties of the alloy.
The distribution and morphology of TiN in the TiZrNbVAl LHEA 
were analyzed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, HITACHIS4800), 
and the grain morphology and crystal orientation were analyzed by 
electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD). The element distribution was 
analyzed by energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) detector. The con -
stituent phases were detected by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker AXS D8 
Advance diffractometer) using Cu-K ray in a diffraction angle (2 θ ) range 
of 20 – 100

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructural characterization
Fig. 1 shows the XRD pattern of as-cast TiZrNbVAl LHEA with 
different TiN contents. As shown in Fig. 1 (a), all the alloys exhibited the 
strong diffraction peak of BCC structure, which stemmed from the ma -
trix. In TiN-3 and TiN-5 alloys, only the weak diffraction peaks ( Fig. 1
(b)) from TiN with FCC structure could be detected beside those from 
BCC matrix. In order to accurately analyze the crystal structure of TiN-0, 
TiN-3 and TiN-5 alloys, Fig. S3 of Supplementary materials shows the 
TEM bright-field images and selected area electron diffraction of the 
three alloys, and the results show that the lattice constants of TiN-0, TiN- 
3 and TiN-5 were found to be a = 3.301 Å, a = 3.324 Å and a = 3.335 Å, 
respectively, indicating that the lattice constants increase after the 
addition of TiN. This was caused by the fact that after TiN decomposi -
tion, a part of Nitrogen occupies the gap position of the alloy and thus 
increases the lattice parameters of the LHEAs. It should be noted that 
TiN could decompose during the melting process, because its decom -
position temperature (1050 ℃ [25] ) is lower than the melting temper -
ature (~1650 ℃ ) of the alloy. This means that the decomposed N may 
combine with other reactive elements in alloy, such as Zr and Ti. 
However, no new phase was detected by XRD, which suggests that the 
decomposition of TiN does not generate new phases. Table S2 of Sup -
plemental materials shows the mixing enthalpy of the atomic pairs in 
TiZrNbVAl-TiN LHEAs, and the results show that the mixing enthalpies 
between N and four metallic elements are all negative, and the mixing 
enthalpy between N and Ti/Zr is the most negative. Moreover, Fig. S2
(a-b) of Supplementary materials shown that at high temperature 
( > 2000 ℃ ), compared with ZrN, the concentration of N required for TiN 
is smaller, and the concentration of Ti (50 at%) in TiZrNbVAl LHEA is 
much higher than that of Zr (18 at%), which leads to the decomposed N 
may dissolve in the matrix or combine with Ti again during solidifica -
tion process.
Fig. 2 shows the microstructure in TiZrNbVAl LHEAs with different 
TiN contents. As shown in Fig. 2 (a-c), all the specimens exhibited the 
equiaxed grains, and the grain size decreased with increasing the TiN 
content. The precipitates with two different sizes could be found in TiN- 
3 and TiN-5 alloys. The larger precipitates (indicated by the light blue 
arrows in the insert images of Fig. 2 (b-e)) were near spherical shape, 
whose diameter was ~5 μ m, and mainly existed at the grain interiors. 
The elemental line scan (yellow line in Fig. 2 (d)) across them ( Fig. 2 (f)) 
shows they were rich in Ti and N, which suggests they were artificially 
added TiN particles. It should be noted that their sizes were much larger 
than those of originally added TiN particles ( Fig. S4 of Supplementary 
materials ), suggesting the reprecipitated TiN may preferentially grow on 
the original TiN particles and make them grow bigger. The EDS point 
analysis shows that the N content of the TiN-3 and TiN-5 alloys was 
2.0 at% and 3.4 at%, respectively. This suggests that part of N still exist 
in the matrix, which is consistent with the results of Fig. S3 of Supple -
mentary materials . The fine precipitates (indicated by the red arrows in 
Fig. 2 (d, e)) were plate-like shape, whose thickness was ~0.3 μ m, and 
they could be found at the grain interiors and grain boundaries ( Fig. S5
of Supplementary materials shows the statistical results of the size of the 
fine precipitates). These precipitates were also rich in Ti and N, thus they 
should be the reprecipitated TiN during solidification.
The TEM analysis for the nano sized precipitates is shown in Fig. 2 (g- 
h). As shown in Fig. 2 (g), the precipitates with the length of 100 – 300 nm

3.2. Mechanical properties
Fig. 4 shows the compressive stress strain curves of TiZrNbVAl LHEA 
with different TiN contents at room temperature and its mechanical 
property comparison with other reported LHEAs. Firstly, as shown in 
Fig. 4 (a), with the increase of TiN content, the yield strength of the LHEA 
gradually increases, but the plasticity of the LHEA decreases. The 
indentation results of hardness test for LHEA with different TiN contents 
( Fig. S7 of the Supplementary materials ) also indicate that plastic 
deformation becomes difficult with the increase of hardness after adding 
TiN. ” was added. The yield strength of TiN-0 is 803 MPa and the 
compression strength is 2274 MPa. When the TiN content is increased to 
3 wt%, the yield strength is increased to 1603 MPa, an increase of 2 
times. However, compared with TiN-0, the compression ductility 
decreased from more than 50 % to 24.6 ± 3.2 %, which was reduced by 
more than half. Furthermore, the yield strength of TiN-5 is 2124 MPa,

had the best strength and ductility matching, which superior to most of 
the other reported LHEAs. This suggests that adding low lattice mis -
matching particle is a promising strategy to improve the mechanical 
property of the LHEAs.

Fig. 6 shows the TEM bright field images of TiN-3 alloy after defor -
mation. In Fig. 6 (a), there were abundant dislocation entanglement and 
plugging around the grain boundaries, which suggests that the grain 
boundaries impede the dislocation motion. In regions far from the grain 
boundaries, the dislocations slip in the plane and form the dislocation 
networks. The grain size in TiN-3 alloy is decreased by TiN addition, 
thus its grain boundary density is higher than that in TiN-0 alloy. This 
could result in more dislocation entanglement and plugging at the grain 
boundaries of TiN-3 alloy, leading to its yield strength increase. In 
addition, it is worth noting that interactions between dislocations at the 
interface play a predominant role in the mechanical properties. As 
shown in Fig. 6 (b-c), when the dislocation arrays meet the precipitates of 
TiN, the propagation of the dislocation in the arrays is hindered, which 
increases the resistance of the dislocation movement. Moreover, these 
randomly distributed dislocations become entangled around the 
precipitated phase further enhancing resistance against their movement 
and increasing the yield strength. This is another reason for the yield 
strength increase in TiN-3 alloy.

mechanism to the yield stress of TiZrNbVAl LHEAs with different TiN

indicating the credibility. It can be seen that it is the second phase

reprecipitate on the grain boundaries during solidification. Therefore, 
although increasing the addition content of TiN could increase the alloy 
strength, it will increase the amount of reprecipitated TiN on the grain 
boundaries at the same time and decrease the ductility of alloy greatly. 
In order to utilize the strengthening effect of TiN, it is necessary to 
decrease the decomposition of TiN and inhibit its precipitation on the 
grain boundaries by optimizing the addition way of TiN and the pro -
cessing parameter in the future study. It is noteworthy that the inter -
stitial N atoms increase the strength of TiZrVNbAl prominently, and it 
could remain the good ductility based on the interstitial solid solution 
strengthening mechanism. Thus, N addition reprents a promising strat -
egy for improving the mechanical properties of TiZrVNbAl LHEA.
4. Conclusions
In this study, the effects of TiN contents on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of TiZrNbVAl LHEAs were systematically studied. 
The results show a gradual reduction in grain size of the as-cast 
TiZrNbVAI LHEA with increasing TiN contents. Specifcally, when the 
TiN content is 5 wt%, the grain size decreases from 275 μ m to 97 μ m. 
According to the E2EM model, TiN acts as a heterogeneous nucleation 
agent for as-cast TiZrNbVAl LHEA, promoting nucleation rate and 
refining grain size during solidification. The incorporation of TiN 
simultaneously induces interstitial strengthening, grain boundary 
strengthening and dispersion strengthening mechanisms that enhance 
the strength of TiZrVNbAl LHEA from 803 MPa to 1603 MPa after 
adding 3 wt% TiN, while maintaining ~25 % compressive ductility. The 
analysis of the strengthening mechanism reveals that interstitial solid 
solution of N atoms increases the lattice distortion and exhibits excellent 
interstitial strengthening effect. Moreover, TiN can not only refine and 
improves grain boundary strengthening effect, but also hinders the 
movement of dislocations, which significantly improves the strength. 
Our findings provide valuable data and unique insights into refining 
grain refinement techniques and enhancing mechanical properties of as- 
cast BCC-LHEAs.
