1. Introduction
Recently, Ti–Al Nb alloys have attracted much
attention as potential high temperature (HT) structural
materials because of their outstanding HT strength.
Great progress has been gained in phase diagram,
oxidation resistant studies [1–4]. From second and
third generation Ti–Al alloys [5–7], it is found that Nb
is the essential and effective element enhancing their
mechanical properties, especially, HT strength [8,9].
The main reason is that the critical resolved shear stress
for ordinary slip is improved due to high Nb addition.
The ordinary slip near the phase interface and at the
grain boundaries was prevented. High Nb addition
lessens the stacking fault energy, decreases the climbing
rate of dislocations at HT and pins the dislocations. It
can be explained that Nb addition with high melting
point makes the alloy’s melting point, simultaneously,
and order–disorder transus rising. So, it results in the
applied temperature amounting to 900°C [10]. Those
alloys coincide with the conventional Ti–Al alloys
characterized as low density, simple lattice, moreover,
easy to have microstructure evolution in balancing me-
chanical properties. It was reported that high Nb addi-
tion maybe generate the third phase [11,12], such as B2
damaging their mechanical properties, which keep a

2. Experimental procedures
A Ti–Al based alloy containing high Nb with nomi-
nal composition of Ti–45Al–8.5Nb–0.3W–0.05Y at.%
was obtained in the form of as-cast after twice melting
by VAR. In order to reduce the composition segrega-
tion, annealing at 1200°C for 24 h was conducted. The
as-annealed ingots were made into f812 mm samples
with near fully lamellae (NFL) microstructure. The hot
compressing test was carried out using a hot simulator
modeling Gleeble-1500. The results show that these
alloys can be cladding-forged at the slow rate. Then the
ingot with the size of f100120 mm was made into
pack-ingot welded, with heat insulation materials tak-
ing the function of lubrication and heat preservation.
The pack-ingot was forged by type Y3314 hydropress
with the pressure 2000 tons. As same as the former, the
samples with the size of f812 mm gained have
Duplex (DP) microstructure after 1280°C:2 h annealing
and investigation was taken through hot compressing
test. Furthermore, NFL and DP microstructures were
studied via LM and TEM.

4. Discussion
4.1. Nb solid solution strengthening
The dislocations in the alloy can slide along certain
slip plane, generally. Sometimes, at the considerable
high temperature, the hot simulation can cause the
dislocation to slide, and the edge dislocation to climb
along another parallel plane through absorbing or re-
leasing voids and interstitial atoms, resulting in lessen-
ing their HT strength, too. The alloy used in this study
with high Nb solution is not amounting to the limita-

4.2. Yttrium oxide precipitation strengthening
Chieﬂy, Y intrinsic oxidation reduces existence rate
of O atom in the matrix. And the other function is
when the relative yttrium oxide (Y
2O3) is incoherent
with the matrix, for which they would localize at the
grain boundary and near the dislocation as the matrix
and lattice is different, yttrium oxide particles were
precipitated. Since Y addition is considerably few, yt-
trium oxides (black spots in Fig. 6) particles are few
and small, but contrastedly homogeneous.
4.3. Reﬁning grain
RE element Y addition can reﬁne the as-cast mi-
crostructure, especially dendritic microstructure. W ad-
dition can promote g laths nucleating and depositing,
and reﬁne lamellae interlamellae spacing to make a base
for reﬁning the grains. The grain size of NFL is 200–
250 mm, less than that in fully lamellae (FL) microstruc-
ture with the size of 300–400 mm under the same
condition [5]. The interlamellae spacing is 1–4 mm less
than that 10 mm of FL. More grains undertake the
deformation homogeneously releasing the stressing con-
centration. The alloy exhibits considerably better
plasticity without failure. Besides, due to the
different orientation of neighbor grain, various grains
inclination is different. As a grain bears a plastic defor-
mation at the mercy of restraining by near grain, the
elastic deformation will make a balance with the hin-
drance.
For the slip system in the alloy is absent, severe stress
concentration can appear resulting in forming the twins
during the hot mechanical deformation, especially after
taking a force along the unfavorable sliding direction.
The twins in Ti–Al alloys are relative to 1 :3 [ 111 ]
Frank partial dislocation. As the twins grow, they
appear like a plate. Since the twins form under high
stress, the growth stress is greater than that for propa-
gation. If stacking faults have existed in the growing
direction, the jog of twinning and stacking faults will be
formed there.
5. Conclusions
1. NFL microstructure of the new alloy developed,
whose nominal chemical composition is Ti–45Al–
8.5Nb–0.3W–0.05Y at.%, has higher strength and
lower ductility compared with DP microstructure at
HT.
2. The combination of Nb solid solution strengthening
and yttrium oxide (Y
2O3) precipitation strengthen-
ing contribute to their high temperature strength.
Reﬁning grains bring the better ductility.
3. In DP microstructure, lots of stacking faults, twins,
and jogs of stacking faults and twins result from the
coordination of deformation.
