Developing alloys with exceptional strength-ductility combinations across a broad temperature range is 
crucial for advanced structural applications. The emerging face-centered cubic medium-entropy alloys 
(MEAs) demonstrate outstanding mechanical properties at both ambient and cryogenic temperatures. 
They are anticipated to extend their applicability to elevated temperatures, owing to their inherent ad- 
vantages in leveraging multiple strengthening and deformation mechanisms. Here, a dual heterostructure, 
comprising of heterogeneous grain structure with heterogeneous distribution of the micro-scale Nb-rich 
Laves phases, is introduced in a CrCoNi-based MEA through thermo-mechanical processing. Additionally, 
a high-density nano-coherent γ’ phase is introduced within the grains through isothermal aging treat- 
ments. The superior thermal stability of the heterogeneously distributed precipitates enables the dual 
heterostructure to persist at temperatures up to 1073 K, allowing the MEA to maintain excellent mechan- 
ical properties across a wide temperature range. The yield strength of the dual-heterogeneous-structured 
MEA reaches up to 1.2 GPa, 1.1 GPa, 0.8 GPa, and 0.6 GPa, coupled with total elongation values of 28.6 %, 
28.4 %, 12.6 %, and 6.1 % at 93 K, 298 K, 873 K, and 1073 K, respectively. The high yield strength primar- 
ily stems from precipitation strengthening and hetero-deformation-induced strengthening. The high ﬂow 
stress and low stacking fault energy of the dual-heterogeneous-structured MEA promote the formation 
of high-density stacking faults and nanotwins during deformation from 93 K to 1073 K, and their den- 
sity increase with decreasing deformation temperature. This greatly contributes to the enhanced strain- 
hardening capability and ductility across a wide temperature range. This study offers a practical solu- 
tion for designing dual-heterogeneous-structured MEAs with both high yield strength and large ductility 
across a wide temperature range.

1. Introduction 
Face-centered cubic (fcc) structured high/medium-entropy al- 
loys (H/MEAs) with multiple principal elements have garnered 
widespread attention in the materials science community [1–5] . 
Notably, the CrCoNi-based MEAs are emerging as promising ma- 
terials for applications in extreme service conditions, thanks to 
their exceptional combination of tensile strength, ductility, strain- 
hardening capability, and fracture toughness at both ambient and 
cryogenic temperatures [6–8] . This is primarily attributed to the 
low stacking fault energy (SFE) of CrCoNi-based MEAs, enabling

formation of stacking faults (SFs), deformation-induced twinning, 
martensitic transformation, and even crystalline-to-amorphous

of CrCoNi-based MEAs remains relatively low, as the mentioned 
deformation mechanisms predominantly occur in the plastic de- 
formation stage, offering no contribution to yield strength im- 
provement [3] . Recently, heterogeneous grain structures have been 
introduced into CrCoNi-based MEAs, effectively enhancing yield 
strength without signiﬁcantly compromising ductility because of 
hetero-deformation-induced (HDI) hardening [14–16] . However, it 
is noteworthy that heterogeneous grain structures generally exhibit 
poor thermal stability at elevated temperatures [ 17 , 18 ]. As a result, 
heterogeneous-structured CrCoNi-based MEAs tend to experience 
rapid softening under elevated temperatures. Therefore, the design

The introduction of intermetallic phases with high thermal 
stability at grain boundaries represents a desirable strategy for 
achieving a heterogeneous structure with good thermal stability 
[19] . These intergranular precipitates play a signiﬁcant role in in- 
hibiting high-angle grain boundary migration through the Zener 
pinning effect [ 20 , 21 ], thereby restricting grain growth at elevated 
temperatures. For example, Zhang et al. utilized the incoherent 
B2 particles to enable the FeNiCoAlTaB MEA to maintain a stable 
heterogeneous grain structure at temperatures as high as 1400 K 
[22] . In addition, a single source of HDI hardening is often insuﬃ- 
cient to realize the desired yield strength in CrCoNi-based MEAs. 
Therefore, synergistic strengthening mechanisms are required. In 
Ni-base superalloys, a high density of coherent L12 γ’ phase is 
commonly introduced into the fcc γ matrix to maintain suﬃcient 
strength at elevated temperatures [23] . This design concept has 
been successfully applied to fcc H/MEAs, resulting in substantial 
strength at ambient and cryogenic temperatures [24–27] . Simulta- 
neously, the γ’ phase, known for its high thermal stability, con- 
tributes to enhancing the high-temperature mechanical properties 
of fcc H/MEAs [ 28 , 29 ]. The challenge arises when introducing both 
intragranular and intergranular precipitates into fcc MEAs, aiming 
to endow them with high yield strength across a wide tempera- 
ture range while carefully considering the associated ductility loss 
[30] . To address this, it becomes crucial to introduce sustainable 
strain-hardening sources to suppress premature plastic instability, 
striving for an optimal strength-ductility balance [ 31 , 32 ]. The plas- 
tic deformation mode of fcc MEAs is directly related to the SFE, 
typically favoring dislocation slip, the formation of SFs, deforma- 
tion twinning, and martensitic transformation with decreasing SFE 
[33–36] . Recent studies have demonstrated that certain coherent 
nanoprecipitates can effectively reduce SFE by depleting alloying 
elements that enhance the SFE of the fcc matrix [ 37 , 38 ]. Leverag- 
ing the combined effect of reduced SFE and increased ﬂow stress 
from precipitation strengthening and HDI hardening, the formation 
of SFs and deformation twinning can be activated to introduce ex- 
tra strain-hardening during plastic deformation [39] . Therefore, the 
primary challenge lies in incorporating both intragranular and in- 
tergranular precipitates into heterogeneous-grain-structured MEAs. 
In response to the aforementioned challenges, we design and 
fabricate a Ni46 Co23 Cr23 Al2 Ti2 Nb4 (at.%) MEA to showcase the 
integration of HDI hardening, precipitation strengthening, and 
SFs/twinning-induced strengthening for realizing high strength and 
large ductility across a broad temperature range. Firstly, we add 2 
at.% of Al and Ti elements to induce the precipitation of the γ’ 
phase [ 40 , 41 ]. The addition of Nb plays a catalytic role in form- 
ing the Nb-rich Laves phase with high thermal stability [42] . Sec- 
ondly, we produce a heterogeneous grain structure through a se- 
quence of hot-rolling and cold-rolling, followed by solid solution 
annealing. The inherent inhomogeneity in microstructural defor- 
mation during hot-rolling leads to varied defect densities in the 
fcc matrix, ultimately fostering the heterogeneous nucleation and 
growth of the Nb-rich Laves [ 43 , 44 ]. The ﬁnal heterogeneous grain 
structure is obtained through solid solution annealing under the 
pinning effect of pre-existing heterogeneous precipitates [45] . Sub- 
sequently, isothermal annealing for different durations is employed 
to obtain the γ’ phase with varying sizes and volume fractions. By 
combining this dual heterogeneous structure with nanoprecipitates 
in this MEA, exceptional mechanical properties spanning tempera- 
tures from 93 K to 1073 K are achieved. This work provides a viable 
methodology for tailoring dual heterogeneous structures in MEAs 
for applications at a wide temperature range by combining HDI 
hardening, precipitation strengthening, and SFs/twinning-induced 
strengthening. 
2. Material and methods 
The MEA ingot with a nominal composition of 
Ni46 Co23 Cr23 Al2 Ti2 Nb4 (at.%) was fabricated by arc melting in 
a high-purity argon atmosphere. The ingot was inverted and 
remelted ﬁve times before being cast into the copper mold 
with dimensions of 150 mm × 50 mm × 12 mm. The plate- 
shaped ingot was ﬁrst homogenized at 1473 K for 5 h and 
then hot rolled at 1373 K to reduce its thickness to 7 mm. 
Subsequently, the hot-rolled sheet was annealed at 1373 K for 
10 min and then cold rolled (CR) at room temperature to achieve 
a further 50 % thickness reduction. The cold-rolled sheet was 
annealed at 1423 K for 10 min for recrystallization, followed 
by annealing at 923 K for 24, 96, and 192 h to introduce the 
nanoprecipitates and modulate their precipitation behaviors (size, 
distribution, and volume fraction). The samples obtained from 
the solid solution treatment and the aging treatments at 923 K 
for different durations are designated as CRS, CRSA-24, CRSA-96, 
and CRSA-192 samples, respectively. The schematic detailing 
the preparation process for the current MEA is illustrated in 
Fig. 1 . 
Microstructural characterization was carried out using a scan- 
ning electron microscope (SEM, Tescan GAIA3 Ga) equipped with 
electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) and a transmission elec- 
tron microscope operating at 200 kV (TEM, JEOL 2100F). The el- 
ement distribution was studied using scanning TEM-EDS (STEM- 
EDS). Specimens for EBSD observation were prepared using careful 
mechanical polishing, and then electropolished by using a solution 
of 10 % perchloric acid and 90 % ethanol at 253 K with a direct 
voltage of 30 V. TEM foils were prepared by mechanical grind- 
ing to a thickness of about 30 μm using 40 0–30 0 0 grit sandpa- 
per, and then dimpled by Gatan 656, ﬁnally ground using an ion 
thinning instrument by Gatan PIPS 695. The grain size of the sam- 
ples was measured by using the linear intercept method in BSE 
images. The volume fraction of particles was assessed based on 
their area fraction in the SEM-BSE images. Since the particles ex- 
hibit different contrast with the matrix under the BSE images, Im- 
ageJ software was employed to quantify the area fraction of the 
particles. 
Flat dog-bone-shaped tensile samples were machined using 
electrical discharge machining with the longitudinal axes paral- 
lel to the rolling direction, with dimensions of 18 mm in gage 
length, 3 mm in width, and 1.6 mm in thickness. The tensile 
tests were performed at an engineering strain rate of 10–3 s-1 
in an electronic universal testing machine (SUNS UTM5105). The 
elongation at room temperature is measured using the YYJ-5/15- 
L electronic extensometer. The elongation at low and high tem- 
peratures was determined using the formula ( l- l0 )/ l0 , where l0

3.2. Room-temperature mechanical properties 
The room-temperature engineering stress-strain curves of MEA 
samples at various states are shown in Fig. 6(a) , and the values of 
yield strength (YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS), and total elon- 
gation are summarized in Table 2 . The CR sample exhibits an ultra- 
high YS of 1745 MPa but a very low total elongation of 3 %. For the 
CRS sample, its YS and UTS decrease to 527 MPa and 1068 MPa, 
respectively, while the total elongation increases to ∼50.7 %. After 
the precipitation of the nanoscale γ’ phase at 923 K-isothermal ag- 
ing, the YS of the CRSA-24 sample doubles compared to that of the 
CRS sample (1123 MPa), while still maintaining a large ductility of 
∼28.4 %. The CRSA-24 sample has a similar grain size and Laves 
phase volume fraction to the CRS sample, so the doubling of its YS 
can be attributed to the precipitation of high-density γ’ phases. 
Aging at 923 K for a longer time further increases the strength 
but reduces ductility. For example, the YS and UTS of the CRSA- 
96 sample are 1206 MPa and 1586 MPa, respectively, while the to- 
tal elongation is 21.7 %. This can be attributed to the nucleation 
and growth of the intergranular η phase during long-term aging. 
As compared to the CRSA-96 and CRSA-192 samples, the CRSA-24 
sample displays a good comprehensive mechanical property. More-

The strain-hardening rate (SHR) curves of MEAs are shown in 
Fig. 6(b) . It is observed that all aged samples exhibit a signiﬁ- 
cantly higher SHR compared to that of the CRS sample in the early 
stages of deformation, likely attributed to the strong hindrance of 
high-density nanoprecipitates to dislocation motion. The CRSA-192 
sample exhibits a monotonically reduced SHR. In contrast, the SHR 
curves of the CRS, CRSA-24, and CRSA-96 samples exhibit three 
stages. In Stage I, there is a steep decrease in SHR correspond- 
ing to the elastoplastic transition. Interestingly, all the mentioned 
samples exhibit a signiﬁcant improvement in SHR during stage II 
(strain 2 %−4 %). This can be attributed to the incompatible defor- 
mation among different regions in the dual heterogeneous struc- 
ture, leading to the generation of high-density geometrically neces- 
sary dislocations (GNDs) in the early stages of deformation. These 
GNDs accumulate at heterogeneous interfaces, contributing to the 
increase in the strain-hardening ability of the MEA. In stage III, 
both CRSA-96 and CRSA-192 samples show a continuous decrease 
in SHR with increasing strain, while the SHR of the CRSA-24 sam- 
ple ﬁrst maintains at a high level and then decreases with increas- 
ing strain. The exceptional strain-hardening ability of the CRSA-24 
sample contributes to its high UTS and large ductility, while the YS 
remains at 1103 MPa.

4. Conclusions 
In this study, we produced a dual heterogeneous structure in 
the Ni46 Co23 Cr23 Al2 Ti2 Nb4 MEA through thermo-mechanical pro- 
cessing. This structure comprises a heterogeneous distribution of 
Nb-rich Laves phases and heterogeneous grain structure with high- 
density nanoscale γ’ precipitates. Our investigation delved into the 
tensile properties of this MEA across a wide temperature range, 
shedding light on the associated deformation and strengthening 
mechanisms. The primary conclusions are outlined below: 
(1) The microscale plastic deformation inhomogeneity occurring 
during hot rolling induces varying defect densities in the fcc 
matrix, thereby initiating the heterogeneous nucleation and 
growth of the Laves phase. The resultant heterogeneously dis- 
tributed Laves phase generates differing grain boundary migra- 
tion resistance, facilitating the development of heterogeneous 
grain structures during solid solution treatment. Subsequently, 
isothermal aging at 923 K for 24 h introduces a high-density 
coherent γ’ phase into the fcc matrix. With increasing aging 
time to 96 h and 192 h, a brittle η phase is formed at grain 
boundaries. Among the various aged samples, the CRSA-24 
sample demonstrates optimal mechanical properties at 298 K, 
with a YS of 1103 MPa, UTS of 1485 MPa, and a ductility of 
28.4 %. 
(2) As the temperature decreases from 298 K to 93 K, the CRSA-24 
sample demonstrates an increase in both YS and UTS, reach- 
ing 1260 MPa and 1803 MPa, respectively, without any com-

promise in ductility. Moreover, the remarkable thermal stability 
of the intergranular Laves phases and intragranular γ’ phase 
allows the dual heterostructure to persist at temperatures up 
to 1073 K. This prolonged stability enables the MEA to exhibit 
strain-hardening instead of experiencing thermal-induced soft- 
ening at elevated temperatures. This is evident in its excep- 
tional UTS of 1139 MPa and 755 MPa at 873 K and 1073 K, re- 
spectively. These ﬁndings underscore the viability of dual het- 
erostructures with outstanding thermal stability in the design 
of MEAs with superior mechanical properties across a wide 
temperature range. 
(3) The high YS of the current MEA across a broad temperature 
range can be attributed to the synergistic effect of precipita- 
tion strengthening and HDI strengthening. In addition, the en- 
hanced ﬂow stress and low SFE enable the generation of high- 
density SFs and nanotwins during tensile deformation, and the 
density of these defects increases with decreasing deformation 
temperature. These newly formed SFs and nanotwins can shear 
through the coherent γ’ phase, contributing to a reduction in 
stress concentration within the fcc matrix and delaying the on- 
set of plastic instability. Moreover, SFs and nanotwins dynam- 
ically generate high-density interfaces, effectively diminishing 
the mean free path of dislocations. This intricate interplay re- 
sults in superior SHR, consequently yielding high UTS and sub- 
stantial ductility.
