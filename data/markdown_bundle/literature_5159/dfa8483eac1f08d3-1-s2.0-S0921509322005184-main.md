ABSTRACT 
WMoNbTaV refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) is facing the contradiction between strength and plasticity at 
room temperature, which greatly restricts its processing, forming and subsequent engineering applications. In the 
present work, WMoNbTaV and WMoNbTaV with 1 mass% additions of m-ZrO
2 
(WMoNbTaV-1ZrO
2
) RHEAs were 
successfully prepared by ball milling and spark plasma sintering technologies. The grain refinement of the one 
phase body-centered cubic alloy by presence of m-ZrO
2 
is determined by microstructural analyses to be 31.5%. 
The yield strength, ultimate strength and fracture strain of WMoNbTaV-1ZrO
2 
are 2171.1 MPa, 2461.4 MPa and 
12.7% respectively, which are surprisingly increased by 69.0%, 62.5% and 32.3%, in comparison with that of 
WMoNbTaV without m-ZrO
2
, respectively. More importantly, they are remarkably superior to the same alloy 
prepared by the vacuum arc-melting method. A model is presented to study the strengthening mechanism of 
WMoNbTaV-1ZrO
2
, and the theoretical calculation shows that the extraordinary high strength is mainly due to 
the inherent strength in the multi-principal element WMoNbTaV alloy and grain boundary strengthening caused 
by the addition of m-ZrO
2 
particles. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys with the highest high-temperature strength are 
one of the most widely used superalloys at present. Its appearance has 
solved many engineering problems under high-temperature conditions 
[ 1 – 3 ]. With the advancement in technology, more stringent re -
quirements are put forward for the high-temperature resistance and 
high-temperature structure stability of superalloys. Due to the limitation 
of melting point and other factors, Ni-based superalloys are difficult to 
meet the harsh high-temperature working conditions only by adjusting 
the composition of the alloy [ 4 , 5 ]. How to improve the properties of 
Ni-based superalloys has been a difficult problem for researchers in the 
last 60 years. Recently, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) introduced by Yeh 
has attracted much attention in the research field of advanced metal 
materials, which combines multiple elements to form simple 
solid-solution structure (such as face-centered cubic (FCC), 
body-centered cubic (BCC) and hexagonal close-packed (HCP)) [ 6 , 7 ]. 
According to the Gibbs phase rule, the addition of elements will result in 
the formation of some brittle intermetallic compounds in the alloy 
[ 8 – 10 ]. However, Yeh et al. [ 6 ] found that high mixing entropy 
enhanced the phase stability of the solid-solution structure, inhibited the 
formation of brittle intermetallic compounds, finally promoted the for -
mation of simple solid-solution structure. Different from conventional 
alloys, HEAs are composed of various metal elements in equal or nearly 
equal molar ratios [ 6 , 11 ]. HEAs have many unique mechanical prop -
erties, such as extraordinary high-temperature strength [ 12 , 13 ], excel -
lent fracture toughness [ 14 , 15 ] and superior resistances to fatigue [ 16 , 
17 ] and radiation [ 18 – 21 ]. 
Among currently reported HEAs, there are two types of HEAs that 
can be applied at high-temperatures: (1) HEAs based on the 3d-transi -
tion metals (TM-HEAs) Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni and Cu; and (2) 
RHEAs based on refractory elements Zr, Nb, Mo, Hf, Ta and W. The first 
TM-HEAs were prepared independently by each of Yeh et al. [ 6 ] and 
Cantor et al. [ 22 ] in 2004. Many TM-HEAs have excellent strain

2.2. Sample preparation 
In this work, the samples were successfully prepared by powder 
metallurgy method. Fig. 1 shows the preparation process of the samples. 
The metal powders of W, Mo, Nb, Ta and V in equal molar ratio were 
used as raw materials. The raw powders were milled in a planetary ball 
milling (QM-3SP2, China) with tungsten carbide (WC) balls of 5 mm in 
diameter. Ball milling process was conducted under high purity argon 
atmosphere to prevent oxidation without a process control agent. The 
ball to powder weight ratio was kept to be 4:1 and the rotating speed was 
350 rpm for 6 h. WMoNbTaV-1ZrO
2

2 
(1

temperature for the SPS process was 1700 
◦

carbon felt-covered graphite mold to prevent heat loss. The samples 
prepared in this experiment were cylinders with the diameter of 20 mm 
and height of 10 mm. All samples were machined to the shape appro -

3.3. Mechanical properties 
3.3.1. Hardness and nanoindentation 
Twelve locations are randomly selected to measure the Vickers 
microhardness of both two alloys and the results are shown in Fig. 9 after 
removing a maximum and a minimum [ 39 , 45 – 54 ]. The average Vickers 
hardness of WMoNbTaV and WMoNbTaV-1ZrO
2 
alloys are 864.05 HV 
and 997.77 HV, respectively. The higher microhardness of the alloy may 
be due to the small grain size and the dispersion strengthening of ZrO
2 
[ 55 ]. ZrO
2 
can refine the grain and increase the grain boundary area per 
unit volume, so the dislocation motion will be hindered, and the hard -
ness of the WMoNbTaV alloy will be improved. It is particularly note -
worthy that the two WMoNbTaV alloys sintered by SPS in this work are 
significantly higher than most reported RHEAs prepared by casting or 
powder metallurgy ( Fig. 9 ). Fig. 10 shows the nanoindentation image of

the SEM image. There are 900 indentations evenly distributed in Fig. 10

areas, namely a low-hardness blue area, a medium-hardness green area,
