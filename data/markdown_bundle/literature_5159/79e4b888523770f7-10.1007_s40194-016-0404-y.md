RESEARCH PAPER
High temperature tensile properties of laser-welded high-strength
Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloy in as-welded and heat-treated conditions
Lyuyuan Wang1,2 & Jian Huang1,2 & Jie Dong 3 & Zhuguo Li 1,2 & Yixiong Wu1,2
Received: 9 August 2016 / Accepted: 23 November 2016 / Published online: 20 December 2016
# International Institute of Welding 2016
Abstract The high temperature tensile properties of fiber
laser-welded Mg-Gd-Y -Zr alloy in the as-welded and heat-
treated state were systematically studied. The elevated temper-
ature tensile properties of as-welded and heat-treated joints
declined slightly from room temperature to 200 °C. With the
temperature reaching 300 °C, the strengths of the two types of
joints plummeted. High temperature tensile properties can be
enhanced significantly by theβ′ precipitates in the fusion zone
and heat-affected zone after heat treatment. The ultrafine
grains and unstable divorced eutectic in the fusion zone of
as-welded joints deteriorated the tensile properties at 300 °C.
The deformation mechanism of the heat-treated samples at
300 °C is the dislocation slip with void growth at the grain
boundaries due to the dissolution of precipitates.
Keywords (IIW Thesaurus) Magnesium alloys . Post-weld
heat treatment . Mechanical properties . Microstructure .

1 Introduction
Magnesium alloys are especially attractive in the aerospace
and automotive industry applications due to the good
strength/weight ratio and high damping capacity [ 1]. Among
various magnesium alloys, Mg –Gd system alloys are one of
the most promising candidates due to the remarkable age-
hardening response and good creep resistance at room and
elevated temperature [2–4]. In the aerospace and automotive
industry, magnesium alloys are usually used for welded struc-
tures in different parts like seat frames, dashboard parts, gear-
box casings, oil sumps, helicopter turbines, and aircraft
wheels [5]. Hence, it should be necessary to make some re-
searches on the weldability of magnesium alloys. Although
weldability of the magnesium alloys under different fusion
welding methods such as arc welding, laser welding, laser-
arc hybrid welding, electron beam welding, and resistance
spot welding has been widely investigated in recent years,
most of these works mainly focused on the AZ series alloys,
especially on the effects of welding processing parameters on
the microstructure and mechanical properties of welded joints
at room temperature [6–13]. There has been little research into
laser welding of Mg –Gd system alloys, and this field is still
under development to further broaden the application fields.
Because the precipitation strengthening is the largest
contributor to the strength of the Mg-Gd-Y -Zr alloy in
T6 condition, the precipitate evolution during welding pro-
cess plays a critical role in determining the mechanical
properties [ 14, 15]. The evolution of the precip-
itates in the heat-affected zone (HAZ) and partially melted
zone (PMZ) for the welded joint in T6 condition weak-
ened the mechanical properties as a result of the welding
process. Weisheit et al. investigated the microstructure and
mechanical properties of CO

The high-strength wrought Mg-Gd-Y -Zr alloy sheets

macroscopic defects using a fiber laser system.
However, the low elevated temperature mechanical
properties of laser-welded joints due to the ultrafine
grains in the FZ and dissolution of precipitates in the
HAZ limit their application areas. As one of the impor-

chanical properties, the effects of post-weld heat treat-
ment on the high temperature tensile properties of laser-
welded Mg-Gd-Y -Zr alloy joints were systematically ex-
amined in this paper. Moreover, this research also pro-
vided a reference for further studies on performance of
welded joint at the high temperature.

2.1 Materials
The used material was 4-mm thick hot-forged Mg-Gd-Y -Zr
alloy sheets with a size of 100 mm × 50 mm. The chemical
composition of the alloys is listed in Table 1. Before welding,
the top surface of each specimen was cleaned with acetone to
remove grease and residue, and brushed with abrasive paper to
remove oxides. The experimental samples were fixed tightly
into a fixture to ensure a zero gap.
2.2 Experimental procedure
Experiments were carried out using a fiber laser system (IPG
YLS-10000, IPG) with a maximum output power of 10 kW .
The diameter of the laser beam focus is 0.72 mm. In the laser
welding, the argon (99.9%) was used to protect the molten
pool of the top and bottom welds with the constant flow rate
of 12 and 10 L/min, respectively. The position of the focal
point was adjusted to 0 mm from the surface of workpiece.
The welding speed was 3.0 m/min and laser power was
2.2 kW. After welding, post-weld heat treatment was per-
formed. The parameters of the heat treatment are given in
Table 2.
The welded joints were etched by a solution of 4 vol% nital
for microstructure observation by an optical (AxioCam
MRc5, Carl Zeiss) and transmission electron microscope
(TEM) (JEOL-2100, JEOL). The microhardness tests of the
welded joints were carried out by digital Vickers hardness
tester (Zwick Zhμ, Zwick) with 200-g load and dwelling time
of 15 s. The high temperature tensile properties were tested at
a high temperature tensile test machine (Zwick Z50, Zwick)
with the condition of 1 mm/min in cross-head speed. Before
the joints were processed into samples for tensile tests, the top
and backsides of the welds were minimally ground to achieve
a smooth surface. The tensile fracture surface was also inves-

3.2.2 High temperature tensile properties
Figure 5 shows the elevated temperature tensile properties of
the as-welded and heat-treated specimens of the welded joints.
The strengths of two types of joints decline slightly from room
temperature to 200 °C. Further increasing temperature to
higher than 250 °C, the strengths of the two types of joints
decrease significantly. Compared with as-welded samples, the
heat-treated samples show higher UTS at different tempera-
ture. The fracture locations of most samples for different types
of joints after tensile tests at different temperature are listed in
Table 3.

4C o n c l u s i o n s
The effects of post-weld heat treatment on microstructure and
mechanical properties of laser-welded joints of Mg-Gd-Y -Zr
alloy were investigated. Based on the above investigation, the
conclusions from this paper can be summarized as follows:
1. Post-weld heat treatment can significantly enhance the
high temperature tensile properties of laser-welded Mg-
Gd-Y -Zr alloy.
2. The elevated temperature tensile properties of as-welded
and heat-treated joints dropped slightly from room tem-
perature to 200 °C. Further increasing temperature to
higher than 250 °C, the strengths of the two types of joints
decreased significantly.
3. The ultrafine grains and unstable divorced eutectic in the
fusion zone of as-welded joints deteriorated the tensile
properties at 300 °C. The failure mechanism of heat-
treated joints at 300 °C was the decrease of blocking effect
of the β′ precipitates due to the dissolution of precipitates
and the void growth at the grain boundaries.
