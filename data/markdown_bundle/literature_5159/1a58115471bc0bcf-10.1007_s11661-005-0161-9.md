Processing, Microstructure, and Mechanical Properties 
of (Nb)/Nb 5Si3 Two-Phase Alloys
YOSHISATO KIMURA, HIROAKI YAMAOKA, NOBUAKI SEKIDO, 
and YOSHINAO MISHIMA
It has been shown that a fine lamellar structure composed of Nb solid solution, (Nb), and Nb5Si3 is formed
through eutectoid decomposition in the Nb-Si binary system and its ternary derivatives. Such alloys would
exhibit a high strength at over 1400 K, yet showing room-temperature toughness of over 10 to 20 MPa m1/2
if a proper lamellar spacing is chosen. In the present work, effects of processing on the microstructure
evolution and mechanical properties are investigated on the Nb-18 at. pct Si alloys prepared by hot press-
ing (HP) and spark-plasma sintering (SPS). The powders used in the present work are of pure Nb and
Nb5Si3 in order for the fabrication to become possible at temperatures higher than the melting point of
Si and to reduce the formation of SiO 2. The results show that the SPS yields more uniform two-phase
microstructure but the alloy fabricated through HP tends to provide higher elevated temperature strength.
I. INTRODUCTION
IN order to drastically improve the fuel efficiency and
at the same time to effectively reduce CO 2 emission in gas
turbine engines, development of ultra-high-temperature struc-
tural materials has been attempted by many investigators to
provide elevated temperature mechanical properties that are
“beyond the nickel-base superalloys.” For such alloy design,
it is natural to select a refractory metal element as a base,
and previous research can be classified into the following:
(1) heavily solid solution-hardened Nb, Mo, and W-base
alloys;
[1,2] (2) Nb or Mo alloys in which bcc solid solution
matrix is strengthened by silicides or aluminides of a base
metal;[3–11] and (3) precious metal alloys with L1 2 precipi-
tates as strengtheners of fcc solid solution matrix, being anal-
ogous to the nickel-base superalloys.
[12,13]
The present authors have investigated the binary Nb-Si and
the ternary Nb-Si-Ti systems within the scheme of the sec-
ond type of the preceding classification. The binary Nb-Si
phase diagram is shown in Figure 1.
[14] The microstructure
consisting of Nb solid solution, hereafter (Nb), and an inter-
metallic compound Nb 5Si3 in a wide range of composition
through various heat-treatment paths can be seen. The Ti addi-
tions to the binary system have been shown to stabilize the
Nb
3Si phase toward lower temperatures, because the Nb 3Si
phase forms a continuous solid solution with Ti3Si, the melt-
ing point of which is much lower than that of Nb 3Si. The
main feature of the as-cast microstructure of this class of alloys
is the lamellar consisting of (Nb) and Nb 5Si3 resulting from
the eutectoid decomposition of a high-temperature phase,
Nb
3Si, and the additional feature is the primary solidification
phase of either (Nb) or Nb 5Si3. As the alloy composition is
chosen toward the Si-lean side, a higher volume fraction of
ductile primary (Nb) phase is introduced, whereas if it is
chosen toward the Si-rich side, a higher volume fraction of
brittle primary Nb
5Si3 phase with excellent elevated tempera-
ture strength is introduced. The elevated temperature strength
and room-temperature toughness could also be controlled by
the lamellar spacing in the eutectoid decomposition products.
Sekido et al.
[15,16] have shown that in a Nb-20 at. pct Si-10
at. pct Ti alloy, both the room-temperature toughness and
the elevated temperature 0.2 pct compressive flow stress at
above 1473 K are higher with larger lamellar spacing.
In these previous works, it has often been pointed out that
the alloys based on the refractory metal element containing
silicides and aluminides by a large volume were difficult to
prepare through ingot metallurgy. Typically, it has been shown
that even by very careful arc melting, cracks and pores often
remain in an ingot. In most cases, it is not possible to fabri-
cate tensile test specimens and even special care has to be
taken in order to obtain a sound compression test specimen.
The reason for the defect introduction, for example, in the
process of arc melting, is most likely to be the thermal stress
arising between the top and bottom of the ingot during cool-
ing on the water-cooled copper hearth after melting. Since
such a solidification defect might hinder the intrinsic mechan-
ical properties, particularly the ductility and toughness of the
alloy, the design of a fabrication and processing method to
avoid the disadvantage in the ingot metallurgy is anticipated.
The purpose of the present work is to examine the microstruc-
tural evolutions in a Nb-18 at. pct Si alloy prepared by two
different types of powder metallurgy: hot pressing (HP) and
spark-plasma sintering (SPS), with emphasis on the development
of eutectoid decomposition of the Nb
3Si phase into (Nb) and
Nb5Si3 resulting in a lamellar microstructure. Comparisons of
elevated temperature strength and room-temperature toughness
are made between alloys prepared by different processing.

II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
A. Preparation of Powders
Composition of the alloy used in the present work is Nb-18
at. pct Si and the alloy was prepared by powder metallurgy.
Powders used were of pure Nb, 99.9 pct in purity and under
45 /H9262m in diameter, and of Nb 5Si3, prepared as follows: (1)
an ingot of Nb 5Si3 was first made out of pure Nb, 99.9 pct
and Si, 99.999 pct, in purities, by arc melting; (2) the pow-
ders were pulverized in a mortal and separated into powders
of under 45 /H9262m in diameter; and (3) the powders were crushed
in a planetary ball mill under an argon atmosphere using
alumina balls. Then, both Nb and Nb5Si3 powders were mixed
by a roller mixer at 32 rpm for 24 hours to achieve the over-
all composition of the powders as Nb-18 at. pct Si.
B. HP and SPS
The powders were loaded in a carbon die with a cross-
sectional area of 67 /H1100367 mm
2, and a compact of 300 g was
fabricated by HP under 50 MPa at 1873 K for 4 hours. Before
reaching the temperature for HP, de-gassing was conducted
at 873 K for 1 hour followed by filling argon to 0.15 MPa.
For the fabrication of the alloy by SPS, the powders were
loaded in a carbon die 10 or 15 mm in diameter. Spark-plasma
sintering was conducted by using SPS510A equipment made
by Sumitomo Coal Mining Co. Ltd. The conditions for the
sintering were at 1673 K under 30 to 50 MPa for 0.6 ks.
Note that the actual temperature of the alloy during sintering
may be higher by 100 K, for which accurate measurement
cannot be made.
Relative density was measured by the Archimedes method
under as-hot-pressed and spark-plasma sintered conditions.
C. Heat-Treatment Schedule
Heat treatment on the alloy was conducted by a furnace
with a carbon heater in an argon atmosphere. The schedule
of the heat treatment is basically twofold. The first heat treat-
ment is conducted at 2103 K for 72 to 200 hours, bringing
the alloy into the (Nb)/Nb
3Si two-phase region in order to
eliminate the Nb5Si3 phase formed during casting. Although
the heat treatment is done in the two-phase region, the heat
treatment is hereafter denoted as the solution treatment (ST),
because the aim is to maximize the amount of Nb
3Si that is
expected to decompose into (Nb) and Nb 5Si3 through the
eutectoid reaction. The second heat treatment is therefore for
the eutectoid decomposition to take place and is at 1673 K
for 100 to 200 hours, which is designated as HT hereafter.
D. Microstructure Observation and Phase Identification
Microstructural observations were made on the mechani-
cally polished samples to 1-/H9262m diamond paste with backscat-
tered electron image (BEI) or secondary electron image (SEI)
using a JEOL* JSM5300 scanning electron microscope (SEM).
*JEOL is a trademark of Japan Electron Optics Ltd., Tokyo.
Phase identification was mainly based on the difference in
contrast of the phase observed by BEI due to the difference
in compositions.
Electron probe microanalysis (EPMA) using a JEOL JXA-
8900 was carried out in order to examine the Si concentration
in the (Nb) phase in the alloys fabricated in different processes.
E. Mechanical Property Evaluation
Compression tests to evaluate 0.2 pct flow stress at vari-
ous temperatures were conducted using an Instron-type test-
ing machine under a strain rate of 1.0 to 1.6 /H1100310/H110024 s/H110021. Tests
were conducted at temperatures between room temperature
to 1673 K using a pillar shaped specimen with a dimension
of 2 /H110032 /H110035 mm3. Evaluation of room-temperature fracture
toughness was made either by three-point bending, using a
specimen of 1 /H110032 /H1100310 mm3 in dimension having a straight
notch for the spark-plasma sintered alloy, or by four-point
bending, using a Chevron notched specimen of 5 /H110035 /H11003
30 mm
3 in dimension with inner and outer spans of 7 and
21 mm for HP alloy. Both types of the notch were introduced
by an electrodischarge machine.

B. Microstructure Evolution by Heat Treatment
The alloys were then heat treated first at 2103 K in the
(Nb)/Nb
3Si two-phase regions according to the binary phase
diagram shown in Figure 1. Figure 4 shows the microstructure
of the alloys fabricated by HP after this heat treatment (ST) for
different times. After heating for 72 hours, formation of Nb3Si,
with a gray contrast, can be found in Figure 4(a) but not to a
large extent, and a substantial amount of Nb5Si3 phase remains.
The volume fraction of Nb3Si phase increases after heating for
120 hours, as shown in Figure 4(b), but the microstructure has
not reached the equilibrium to become the (Nb)/Nb3Si two phase.
Note that it has been often pointed out by previous investiga-
tors that the decomposition of Nb 3Si into (Nb) and Nb 5Si3
through the eutectoid decomposition is sluggish[4,5,11,15]Also, it
is interesting to note that the formation of the Nb3Si phase takes
place at the boundary between the (Nb) and Nb5Si3 phase in
Figure 4(a), but it takes place within the Nb5Si3 phase in Fig-
ure 4(b), the reason for which is hard to be found. The
microstructure evolution and kinetics of peritectoid reaction to
form Zr
3Al from Zr and Zr2Al have been reported by Shulson
and Graham,[17] and it was shown that the nucleation of Zr3Al
occurs heterogeneously and that the growth takes place by dif-
fusion-controlled migration in opposite directions of the Zr3Al/Zr
and Zr3Al/Zr2Al. A more detailed investigation is necessary to
understand the formation process of Nb3Si in the present case.
Figure 5 shows the microstructure of the alloy fabricated
by SPS after heat treatment for 72 hours. By this heat-treat-
ment condition, the formation of Nb3Si has been completed
and a fine mixture of (Nb) and Nb 3Si established. The dif-
ference in kinetics of the formation of Nb3Si from (Nb) and
Nb5Si3 at this temperature should come from the difference
in microstructures before the heat treatment, as shown in
Figure 3. The finer the mixture, the more interfaces for the
diffusional reaction to proceed, resulting in faster kinetics.

As shown in the figure, crack bridging and crack deflection
are often found both in as-hot-pressed alloys and in ST and
HT heat-treated alloy. The major reason these alloys to show
a better fracture toughness should be that the ductile (Nb)
phase exists as a relatively continuous phase within the
microstructure containing brittle phases. The ductile phase
toughening mechanism more effectively operates in the alloy

mainly of a lamellar mixture of (Nb) and Nb

form than that of the hot-pressed alloy. It has been shown that
ductile phase toughening could work in the material consist-
ing of a ductile and a brittle phase in a lamellar form, but a
certain thickness of the ductile phase is required for crack
bridging and deflection to occur. The average lamellar spac-
ing found in the spark-plasma sintered alloy must therefore
be larger for improved room-temperature toughness.

IV. CONCLUSIONS
Microstructural evolution and the mechanical properties
of a Nb-18 at. pct Si alloy are investigated. Powder metal-
lurgy of two different techniques is employed to fabricate
the alloy in which using Nb and Nb
5Si3 powders, instead
of Nb and Si elemental powders, is the feature of the pre-
sent work. Alloys prepared by HP and SPS are investigated
for their microstructural development involving Nb solid
solution, Nb 3Si, and Nb 5Si3 through heat treatment. Then,
the room-temperature toughness of the alloys and 0.2 pct
compressive strength of the alloy with different microstruc-
tures are examined. The following are the conclusions
drawn.
1. A Nb-18 at. pct Si alloy with relative density of around
0.95 is fabricated by HP and SPS using elemental Nb
and prefabricated Nb
5Si3 powders.
2. In as-fabricated condition, the alloy fabricated by SPS
has a more uniform and finer mixture of (Nb) and Nb5Si3
phases.
3. Accordingly, after heat treatment at 2103 K in the (Nb)
and Nb3Si two-phase region, a uniform mixture consist-
ing of the two phases is obtained in the alloy fabricated
by SPS. The microstructure of the hot-pressed alloy con-
tains residual Nb 5Si3 after the heat treatment.
4. The following heat treatment for eutectoid decomposi-
tion of Nb3Si phase into (Nb) and Nb5Si3 results in a fine
lamellar microstructure with discrete (Nb) phase in the
spark-plasma-sintered alloy, while the alloy fabricated by
HP exhibits an inhomogeneous microstructure consisting
of large and relatively continuous (Nb) phase, discrete
islands of residual Nb 5Si3 and Nb 3Si phase, and the
decomposition product of a lamellar mixture of (Nb) and
Nb5Si3 phase.
5. Room-temperature toughness of about 12 MPa m 1/2 is
exhibited in the alloy fabricated by HP followed by heat
treatment because of the occurrence of crack bridging and
deflections by the presence of the continuous ductile (Nb)
phase, which provided ductile phase toughening.
6. Elevated temperature strength at 1673 K is also higher
in the alloy fabricated by HP as compared with that fab-
ricated by SPS under the conditions examined in the pre-
sent work.
