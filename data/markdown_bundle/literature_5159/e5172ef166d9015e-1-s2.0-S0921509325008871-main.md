ABSTRACT
In recent years, significant progress has been made in the development of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) that possess 
both high strength and high ductility. The application of the metastable engineering strategy to enhance the 
strength and ductility of materials has also gradually been employed, and cryogenic treatment is one of the 
important techniques in this regard. Through cryogenic liquid nitrogen treatment, this study significantly 
enhanced the strength and ductility of (CrMnFeCoNi)
45
Fe
55 
and (CrMnFeCoNi)
40
Fe
60 
HEAs prepared by laser 
melting deposition (LMD). Compared to the initial samples, the fracture strength of (CrMnFeCoNi)
45
Fe
55 
increased from 480 MPa (at room temperature) to 980 MPa (after cryogenic treatment), and the fracture 
elongation alloy increased from 60 % (at room temperature) to 62 % (after cryogenic treatment). The fracture 
strength of (CrMnFeCoNi)
40
Fe
60 
alloy increased from 550 MPa (at room temperature) to 1150 MPa (after 
cryogenic treatment), and the fracture elongation decreased from 55 % (at room temperature) to 45 % (after 
cryogenic treatment). The reduction in stacking fault energy (SFE) through cryogenic liquid nitrogen treatment 
enables the formation of stacking faults in the alloy system. These stacking faults act as nucleation sites for phase 
transformations, encouraging the shift of the matrix from a face-centered cubic (FCC) phase to a body-centered 
cubic (BCC) phase or other phases. This triggers phase transformation-induced plasticity effects (TRIP). The 
occurrence of phase transformation was confirmed using in-situ cryo-TEM. Simultaneously, the coexistence of 
two phases mechanism achieves an advantageous balance between strength and ductility. Our study offers novel 
insights and methodologies for acquiring high-entropy alloys with exceptional comprehensive mechanical 
properties.
1. Introduction
Unlike traditional alloys, which consist of one or two primary ele -
ments, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) are multi-component alloys 
composed of five or more main elements at concentrations ranging from 
5 % to 35 at.%, which was initially proposed by Cantor et al. and Yeh 
et al., in 2004 [ 1 , 2 ]. Since their emergence, high-entropy alloys have 
been widely used in aerospace, mechanical engineering, medical de -
vices, oil drilling, electronic devices, and other fields due to their 
excellent properties such as outstanding oxidation resistance and wear 
resistance, high strength, fracture toughness and corrosion resistance 
[ 3 – 7 ]. In particular, CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy, as one of the most 
extensively studied alloys since 2004 [ 8 ], has received significant 
attention. Recently, it has been astonishingly discovered that its

2. Material and methods
2.1. Material preparation
The CrMnFeCoNi spherical powder and iron powder were prepared 
using a thermal plasma spheroidization method in this study. The par -
ticle sizes of the powders were determined using a laser particle size 
analyzer (Microtrac S3500), with average particle sizes of 130 μ m and 
65 μ m, respectively. The oxygen contents of the two powders are 282 
ppm and 295 ppm, respectively. As shown in Fig. 1 , the CrMnFeCoNi 
and Fe powders are generally spherical, with smooth surfaces and no 
obvious defects. The two types of powders were weighed and mixed 
according to the predetermined composition ratio. Subsequently, they 
were dried in a vacuum environment at 120 
◦
C for 5 h. After the powders 
had cooled to room temperature, they were poured into the powder 
feeding container of a printing device. Argon gas was introduced into the 
container to remove any remaining air, and the container was tightly 
sealed with a cover. As shown in Fig. 2 (a), during the laser melting 
deposition process, the mixed powders were conveyed to the laser melt 
pool on a 316L substrate through a coaxial nozzle. The argon gas flow 
rate was set between 12 and 20 L/min, and the powder feeding rate 
ranged from 7 to 9 g/min. Argon gas was introduced into the printing 
chamber to provide protection, and the water and oxygen content inside 
the chamber was kept below 15 ppm. Wavelength 1070 nm, laser beam 
distributes like a top hat [ 47 ], spot diameter 1.8 mm, laser power 800 W, 
and scanning speed 400 mm/min were set as the processing parameters 
for the Ytterbium-doped fiber laser unit. The distance between the laser 
head and the melting pool is 8 mm, and the laser head is raised by 0.2 
mm after each scan. During the printing process, a high-density energy 
from the laser was used to generate a localized melt pool, where the

primarily arises from the reduction in SFE of the alloys at lower tem -

alloy ’ s stacking fault energy. This reduction prompts the emergence of a 
significant presence of dislocations and twins within the material. When 
external forces impact a material, the movement and sliding of dislo -

deformation and consequently boosting its strength. Twins, as unique 
structures within a crystal, provide supplementary pathways for dislo -
cation motion, facilitating easier plastic deformation of the material 
under stress. Furthermore, the creation of twins contributes to stress 
dispersion and absorption, curbing the spread of cracks within the 
crystal and thereby augmenting the material ’ s toughness. The
