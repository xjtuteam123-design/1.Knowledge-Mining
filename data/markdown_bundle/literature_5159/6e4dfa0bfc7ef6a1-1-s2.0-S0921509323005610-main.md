ABSTRACT 
Stress shielding at the implant-bone interface is the main causes of failure in orthopedic implant surgery, which is 
induced by the modulus mismatch between implants and bones. Reducing the Young ’ s modulus of biomedical 
alloys is beneficial to alleviate the stress shielding effect. Recently, biomedical multiprinciple elemental (Bio- 
MPEAs) have aroused widespread concerns due to their low modulus, high strength, and superior biocompati -
bility. In this work, a series of (HfNbTa)
1-x
Ti
x 
alloys are designed to investigate the effects of Ti content on the 
structural and mechanical properties of Bio-MPEAs. The obtained results reveal that the designed alloys have a 
single-phase body-centered cubic (BCC) structure, and exhibit ambient ductility with a tensile elongation > 12% 
and cold-rolling thickness reduction > 86%. Ti addition helps to reduce the Young ’ s modulus of Bio-MPEAs. Their 
super formability stems from the movability of < 1 1 1 > /2-type dislocations. Solid solution strengthening is the 
major strengthening mechanism of the alloys. Local lattice distortion plays a key role in solid solution 
strengthening, which is induced by atomic size mismatch and electronegativity difference of multiple elements. 
This work not only highlights the positive role of Ti addition for designing low-modulus Bio-MEAs, but also 
clarifies the physical mechanisms of solid solution strengthening and lattice distortion. 
1. Introduction 
Recently, a sub-group of multiprinciple elemental alloys (MPEAs), 
composed of non-toxic and non-allergenic elements (Ti, Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta, 
and V), have attracted a great deal of attention due to their superior 
mechanical, biocompatible, and antibacterial properties [ 1 ]. Among 
them, a series of low-modulus MPEAs have been investigated as 
biomedical multiprinciple elemental alloys (Bio-MPEAs) for orthopedic 
implants [ 2 ]. Such as Ti – Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta alloys exhibit low Young ’ s 
modulus (56 – 68 GPa), high compressive yield stress (834 – 890 MPa), 
large compressive strain ( > 45%), good bio-corrosion resistance, and 
high cell viability, compared to Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy [ 3 ]. The elastic 
modulus of Ti-containing Bio-MPEAs can be modified in the range of 
57 – 138 GPa by changing chemical composition [ 4 ]. For example, 
increasing Ti content of Ti – Zr – Nb – Mo alloys can considerably increase 
their resilience and reduce their elastic moduli, density, and composi -
tion segregation [ 5 ]. The Ti

4. Conclusion 
The main findings and novelties are summered as follows. Several 
new low-modulus Bio-MPEAs are designed successfully by combining 
experimental and computational methods. The designed alloys exhibit 
high yield strength, excellent tensile ductility, and super rolling form -
ability at room temperature with a tensile elongation > 12% and cold- 
rolling thickness reduction > 86%. The excellent ductility of the alloys 
are originated from the movability of < 1 1 1 > /2-type dislocations. It is 
found that increasing Ti content helps to reduce the elastic moduli ( B , G , 
and E ), and to increase the ductility of the alloys. Solid solution 
strengthening is the major strengthening mechanism for single-phase 
Bio-MPEAs, which is mainly caused by atomic size misfit and shear 
modulus misfit. Interestingly, the atomic size misfit and electronega -
tivity difference of multiple elements result in serious local lattice 
distortion and electron localization within the alloys. The lattice 
distortion is enhanced with the increase of Ti content. This work not 
only reveals the effects of Ti addition on the lattice distortion, sold- 
solution strengthening, and mechanical properties of (HfNbTa)
1- x
Ti
x 
Bio-MPEAs, but also provides valuable research data and ideas for 
designing low-modulus metallic biomaterials. However, the study is still 
need to deepen. For example, the dislocation mechanisms of the alloys 
need to be further confirmed by high resolution transmission electron 
microscopy. The basic physical mechanisms for enhancing the ductility 
of the alloys are still ambiguous. In addition, the biocompatibility, 
corrosion resistance, antibacterial property, and wear resistance of the 
alloys need to be tested and improved before applications.
