abstract
g΄ precipitates strengthened Co eNieAleW-base ODS alloys, produced by mechanical alloying, are
promising high-temperature structural materials. The alloys were found to be composed of g/g΄ two-
phase region and discontinuously formed area. The element Ni was effective in enhancing the solu-
bility limit of Al and W within
g matrix, thus promoting the precipitation of g΄ phase and restricting the
formation of secondary phases, such as Co 7W6,C o3W, and AlxCo phases. The ﬂow stress of the alloys was
effectively enhanced by the addition of Ni. The peak stress has decreased with increasing deformation
temperature. The improved low-temperature strength was mainly attributed to the pining effect of oxide
dispersion,
g0 precipitates and ﬁne-grained structure. The sharp fall in the yield strength in the
temperature range of 600 e700 /C14 C was ascribed to grain boundary sliding, diffusion-controlled void
formation, and the formation of shear band.

1. Introduction
Co-base oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) alloy, strength-
ened by coherent g0 precipitates and thermodynamically stable
complex oxides, is a novel type of heat-resistance material exhib-
iting enhanced high-temperature mechanical properties and
excellent corrosion resistance for the hot-section components used
in the aerospace and power-generation industries [1e3].I ti s
already documented that g0 precipitation hardening mechanism is
more effective in intermediate-temperature range (700 e900 /C14 C),
while oxide dispersion strengthening further ameliorates the high
temperature (>1000 /C14 C) mechanical properties [4,5]. g0-Co3(Al,W)
phase was discovered in Co eAleW system by Sato et al. [6]. The
Co3(Al,W) phase has displayed a high stability and anomalous
temperature dependence of ﬂow stress, rendering it an interesting
strengthening phase for enhancing the intermediate-temperature
strength of Co-base alloys [7e9]. As far as the dispersoid particles
are concerned, it has been found by the authors that the addition of
Hf to the Co-base ODS alloys yields nanosized Y eHfeO complex
oxides with high thermal stability and partially coherent interface
[10,1 1]. This method provides an effective route to re ﬁne the
particle size of the oxides and to control the interfacial structure.
Hence, the optimization of aforementioned two strengthening
phases made it possible to further increase the mechanical prop-
erties of Co-base ODS alloys.
Mechanical alloying has been widely employed to produce Ni-
base ODS alloys strengthened by both the g΄ and g phase and the
dispersions of yttria [12,13]. Nonetheless, the fabrication of Co-base
ODS alloys by mechanical alloying still presents an uphill task. It has
previously reported that Co eAleW-base ODS alloys exhibit
complex microstructural evolution, completely differing from the
g/g΄ two-phase microstructure in as-cast Co-base alloys [14]. This
phenomenon has been attributed to the extremely low solubility of
Al and W in the cobalt matrix at room temperature [15,16].

Moreover, the mechanical alloying has also induced the formation
of supersaturated solid solution but not the fully alloyed powder,
resulting in the generation of low volume fraction of g΄ precipitates
and a large amount of secondary phases such as Co 7W6,C o3W and
CoxAl phases [1 7e19]. The existence of such secondary phases
signiﬁcantly hampers the amelioration of high-temperature
strength of Co-base ODS alloys [20].
Enhancing the solubility of Al and W in cobalt matrix is of
fundamental importance in synthesizing the Co-base ODS alloys by
mechanical alloying. The efﬁcacy of Ni to extend the solubility limit
of Al and W in cobalt matrix as well as to enhance the solvus
temperature of g΄ precipitates has already been demonstrated [21].
In order to promote the formation of g΄ precipitates, Ni has been
introduced into mechanically alloyed Co eAleW-bases ODS alloys.
It is found that the Ni greatly in ﬂuences the microstructural
evolution of Co-base ODS alloys by modifying the partition
behavior of alloying elements in g and g΄ phases [22]. The objective
of the present work is to investigate the effects of Ni on the
microstructure and mechanical properties of the Co eNieAleW-
base ODS alloys. The results of present work are of great value for
the composition design and manufacturing process optimization of
the Co-base ODS alloys.
2. Experimental
Five alloys with Ni concentration variation were designed,
and the nominal compositions of these alloys are listed in

mechanically alloyed powder was consolidated by spark plasma

After etching with the solution of 30%HNO 3 þ 70% CH 3OH,
grain structure of the specimens was observed using a LEICA-
2100 optical microscope. The microstructure of the aged speci-
mens was observed on A LEO1450 secondary electron microscopy
(SEM), and the chemical compositions of the phases were
analyzed by an electron probe microanalyzer. Phase constituents
were studied by Siemens D 5000 X-ray diffraction meter using Cu

ning. Subsequently, the specimens were observed using JEM 200

conducted on Gleeble-1500 simulator in air at the strain rate of
10
/C0 4 s/C0 1 between room temperature to 1000 /C14 C using cylindrical
samples with 10 mm height and 5 mm diameter. The specimens
were deformed to the height reduction of 70%. As soon as the
deformation was completed, the deformed specimens were
immediately quenched and put in water. Finally, the deformed
specimens were cut parallel to the deformation axis and the
microstructure was observed by SEM.

3.1. Initial microstructure
Al and W are prerequisite g΄ forming elements in Co eAleW
system. However, it is revealed in Co eAl binary phase diagram
that Al and Co do not dissolve with each other at room
temperature [15]. At the same time, Co eW system also indicates

enhances the solubility of Al and W in the cobalt matrix, which
exert great in ﬂuence on the microstructure. Fig. 1 presents the
microstructure of Co eNieAleW-base ODS alloys with various
contents of Ni after annealing at 900 /C14 C for 24 h. It is obvious in
Fig. 1 (a) that a large amount of white phase is present in the
microstructure of 10Ni alloy with the exhibition of a few irreg-
ular grey contrasting precipitates in the matrix, as indicated by
the arrow ( Fig. 1 (b)). Referring from the results of XRD and EDS,
these white precipitates were identi ﬁed as Co 7W6 or Co 3W
phases, while the grey phase is g΄ precipitate. For the 20 at.% Ni
additions, the population of white precipitates as well as the
particle size have been greatly reduced, as shown in Fig. 1 (c).
Moreover, it is indicated in Fig. 1(d) that extremely ﬁne spherical
g΄ precipitates are formed in the matrix. Even with the Ni
contents of 30 at.%, the population of white precipitates are not
further reduced, albeit the particle size of the white phase is
found to be much larger than that in Fig. 1 (c), as shown in
Fig. 1 (e). Fig. 1 (f) demonstrates the uniform distribution of
cuboidal g0 precipitates with an average size of approximately
24 nm. As the Ni contents are further increased up to 50 at.%,
a few large white precipitates are still visible in the micro-
structure, as shown in Fig. 1(g). Further, the ordered arrangement
of g0 precipitates is achieved ( Fig. 1 (h)). It is noted that the
morphological evolution of g0 precipitates exhibits the trans-
formation from spherical to cuboidal with increasing Ni content.
Since the morphology of g0 precipitates is closely associated with
the lattice mismatch between g0 and g, thereby the above
transformation suggests the increase of lattice mismatch with
increasing Ni content [23]. It is noted that the fraction of g΄ phase
increases with increasing Ni contents, indicating that Ni indeed
promotes the formation of g0 precipitates.
Fig. 2 shows the XRD patterns of the Co eNieAleW-base ODS
alloys with various content of Ni. It is obvious that the intensities of
the peaks of Al xCo, Co 3W and Co 7W6 phases are relatively intense
and the peaks of g΄ precipitates are the lowest for the 10Ni alloy.
With regard to the 30Ni alloy, the peaks of Al xCo, Co3W and Co7W6
phase are evidently decreased and the intensity for g΄ phase is
enhanced. In the case of the 50Ni alloy, the intensity of Co 3W and
Co7W6 phases is not further weakened, and relatively intense peaks
of g΄ phase have been detected. The XRD results are consistent with
the microstructural observations displayed in Fig. 1.

The existence of discontinuously formed area may be related to the
grain structure of the alloys. The discontinuously formed area is
a kind of high-energy area and the solute atoms preferentially
segregate in these areas, resulting in the deviation of alloying
compositions from the g/g΄ two-phase area and the formation of
disordered microstructure. g΄ precipitates would be preferentially
formed in the special regions that the alloying compositions locate
within the g/g΄ two-phase ﬁeld, leading to the sporadic distribution
of the g΄-forming regions with ordered arrangement of g΄ precipi-
tates. Moreover, each array is found to be composed of small cube-
type g΄ precipitate, as shown by the arrows in Fig. 3(e). The contents
of W in the white precipitates reach 46.01 at.%, which is close to
that of the Co 7W6 phase. Additionally, the g΄ precipitates appear to

specimens are examined as a function of hot compression param-

4. Conclusions
The microstructure and mechanical properties of mechanically
alloyed Co eNieAleW-base ODS alloys are investigated, and the
following conclusions can be drawn.
(1) The microstructure of the alloys consists of g/g΄ two-phase
region and discontinuously formed area, and the g/g΄ two-
phase regions were surrounded by the discontinuously
formed area.
(2) Co eNieAleW-base ODS alloys exhibit an extended solubility
limit of Al and W within g matrix, leading to the enhanced
volume fraction of g΄ phase and the reduction secondary pha-
ses, such as Co 7W6,C o3W and Al xCo phases.
(3) The pronounced strain hardening observed at low temperature
is attributed to the pining effect of oxide dispersion, g0
precipitates, and ﬁne-grained structure. The high-temperature
softening of Co-base ODS alloy arises from the grain boundary
deformation, diffusion-controlled void formation, and defor-
mation along discontinuously formed area.
