OPTIMIZATION OF THE HEAT TREATMENT CYCLES OF 
WROUGHT SEMIFINISHED PRODUCTS OF MEDIUM-ALLOY 
HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH NICKEL ALLOYS 
O. N. Vlasova, N. N. Korneeva, 
V. I. Eremenko, B. S. Lombert, 
A. M. Subbotin, and V. P. Gorokhov 
UDC 669.14.018.44:621.785 
The method of production of gas-turbine engine disk and shaft forgings of 
KhN73MBTYu (EI698VD) and KhN77TYuR (EI437BUVD) medium-alloy high-temperature 
strength nickel alloys includes a standard heat treatment including double solu- 
tion treatment (the first solution treatment from temperatures 50-70°C higher 
than the temperature of complete solution of ~'-phase and the second from tem- 
peratures 50-70°C below it) and aging. In a number of cases after the standard 
heat treatment, semifinished products have reduced strength or plasticity. In 
this work two variations of heat treatment for increasing and stabilizing the 
mechanical properties of KhN73MBTYu and KhN77TYuR forgings are proposed. 
Features of the production operation of forging of shaft forgings of high-temperature 
strength nickel alloys are the absence of deformation in the zone of their base in the last 
operation and nonuniform deformation of the flange zone. This results in the probability of 
formation of a coarse-grained structure in these zones during heating for the first solution 
treatment in the standard heat treatment. With a content of horophilic elements (B, C, Pb) 
and undesirable impurities (P, S) corresponding to the upper limit of the specified composi- 
tion with an increase in grain size the mechanical properties of the alloys, particularly 
the impact strength, drop and an intergranular fracture is observed. A reduction in the 
first solution treatment temperature promotes a fine-grained structure and an increase in 
strength and plasticity of the alloys. 
It also should be noted that in specifying the temperature of the second solution 
treatment of KhN73MBTYu, the temperature of full solution of 7'-phase (Tf.s¥,) , the tempera- 
ture range of existence of secondary carbides of different heats, and, consequently, the 
change in the quantity of coarse particles of 7'-phase and secondary carbides in the struc- 
ture were not taken into consideration. 
In connection with this, correction of the standard heat treatment, primarily of the 
second solution treatment, is necessary. 
The purpose of this work* was an investigation of the structure and mechanical proper- 
ties of hollow shaft forgings of standard KhN73MBTYu and KhN77TYuR alloys (Table i) after 
low-temperature heat treatment including annealing, solution treatment from temperatures 
approximately equal to Tf.sy,, and aging at 750°C for 16 h. t In addition, the standard 
heat treatment of KhN73MBTYu alloy disks was corrected, particularly the second solution 
treatment. 
During preliminary annealing in low-temperature heat treatment relief of the residual 
stresses occurs. This promotes creation of favorable conditions for occurrence of recrystal 
lization in heating for solution treatment and for obtaining a uniform grain size. Solution 
treatment was done at temperatures 5-I0°C below Tf.sy, but not lower than 80°C below the 
temperature of maximum precipitation of secondary carbides.

Investigations of the structure of KhN73MBTYu shaft forgings showed that in low-temper- 
ature heat treatment using this cycle a uniform fine-grained recrystallization structure is 
formed. In all zones the grain size is Nos. 4-5 and in the zone of the base it is not 
greater than No. 3.

capacity of the alloys and the absence of notch sensitivity. 
Tables 2 and 3 give the mechanical properties of KhN73MBTYu shaft forgings after heat 
treatment by the different methods and Table 4 the results of low-cycle fatigue tests. It 
may be seen that the strength, plasticity, and low-cycle fatigue resistance of low-tempera- 
ture heat treated specimens are significantly higher than after the standard heat treatment. 
Stress-rupture strength tests at 650 and 750°C showed that the life of specimens after low- 
temperature heat treatment satisfies the specification requirements. The results of control 
mechanical tests of an experimental lot of shaft forgings (Table 2) indicate that low-tem- 
perature heat treatment provides a high and stable level of strength and plasticity of 
KhN73MBTYu alloy and also a 10% increase in low-cycle fatigue resistance at 650°C.

Conclusions. i. Use of the proposed low-temperature treatment in place of the stand- 
ard one provides an increase in and stabilization of the mechanical properties and low-cycle 
fatigue resistance (by 10%) of KhN73MBTYu and KhN77TYuR alloy forgings as the result of for- 
mation of a uniform fine-grained recrystallized structure, more intense precipitation of 
carbides at the grain boundaries, and formation of uniform 7'-phase particles. 
2. With use of the standard heat treatment of KhN73MBTYu alloy correction of the sec- 
ond solution treatment temperature is recommended taking into consideration the temperature 
of full solution of 7'-phase of the specific heat. With this a stable level of mechanical 
properties of the alloy is obtained.
