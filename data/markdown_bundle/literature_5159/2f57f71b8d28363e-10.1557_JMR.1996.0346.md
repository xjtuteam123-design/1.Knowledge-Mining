Superplastic deformation characteristics and constitution
equation in rapidly solidiﬁed Mg–Al–Ga alloy
A. Uoya and T. Shibata
Sendai Institute of Materials Science and Technology, YKK Corporation, Tomiya Miyagi 981-33, Japan
K. Higashi
Department of Mechanical System Engineering, College of Engineering, University of Osaka Prefecture,
Sakai, Osaka 593, Japan

Institute of Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai Miyagi 980, Japan
(Received 19 January 1995; accepted 1 July 1996)
The hot deformation characteristics of a newly typed, high strength magnesium-based
alloy, Mg–8.3 wt. % Al–8.1 wt. % Ga alloy, produced by rapidly solidiﬁed and powder
metallurgy method have been investigated. Tensile tests were carried out at a temperature
range from 523 to 623 K and a strain rate range from 10
–4 to 1 s–1. Superplastic
characteristics were found and, especially, a maximum elongation-to-failure of 1080%
was obtained at 573 K and at a relatively high strain rate of 10–2 s–1. Because of the
presence of ﬁne microstructures at high temperatures, the optimum superplastic strain
rate of the Mg–Al–Ga alloy was higher than that of the reported conventional
superplastic aluminum and magnesium alloys.
I. INTRODUCTION
The strain rates for superplastic behavior in
conventional commercial aluminum alloys are relatively
low compared to the rates of many manufacturing
processes.1,2 The most straightforward microstructural
feature that can be modiﬁed to achieve high-strain-rate
superplasticity is to decrease the grain size. Therefore,
many methods have been developed for attainment of
ﬁne grain structures in metal-based materials.
Additional methods for grain size reﬁnement, rapid
solidiﬁcation (RS), and powder metallurgy (PM) have
been used in conventional commercial aluminum alloys,
such as Al–Zn–Mg system (7XXX) alloys,
3–5 Al–Li
alloys,6,7 and Al–Mg alloys 8 with a high content of
zirconium, chromium, or manganese, which have ﬁne
microstructure about 2–5mm grain diameters, which are
stable at high temperatures, and for which superplastic
deformation has been observed. The strain rates for
superplastic behavior in RS alloys have been higher than
those in ingot metallurgically (IM) processed alloys.
Recent research
9–11 on rapidly solidiﬁed aluminum-
based alloys has led to a new type of high strength
aluminum-based alloys. Advanced RS techniques were
used to obtain an amorphous phase and new metastable
crystalline phases with extended solid solubility. Using
amorphous or metastable crystalline powders pro-
duced by a high pressure gas atomization technique,
Al–Ni–Mm system (Mm ­ misch metal) bulks were
obtained by a powder extrusion method.
12,13 The
structure of the Al–Ni–Mm alloys was a mixed structure
consisting of homogeneously distributed intermetallic
phases in a fcc-aluminum matrix. The mean grain size
of the aluminum matrix was 100–200 nm.
The present authors have reported that high strain
rate superplasticity occurs in RS Al–14Ni–14Mm
14 and
Al–14.8Ni–6.6Mm–2.3(wt. %)Zr alloys. 15 The super-
plastic strain rate range of 0.1 to 10 s–1 for Al–Ni–Mm
and Al–Ni–Mm–Zr alloys is many orders of magni-
tude higher than that attainable for typical commercial
superplastic alloys.1
Recently, a high speciﬁc strength magnesium-based
alloy, Mg–Al–Ga, was fabricated from rapidly solidiﬁed
ribbon. The high strength results from both grain size
reﬁnement of the Mg matrix and dispersion strength-
ening by intermetallic compounds.
16 The grain size of
the magnesium matrix was about 600 nm, so we ex-
pect that high-strain-rate superplasticity occurs in these
Mg–Al–Ga alloys.
In this paper, we demonstrate the superplastic be-
havior of the Mg–Al–Ga alloy in the deformation
range of strain rates from 10
–4 to 1 s–1 and tempera-
tures between 523 and 623 K. Based on the mechanical
data, superplastic deformation mechanisms are consid-
ered and a constitution equation for superplastic ﬂow of
the Mg–Al–Ga alloy is developed.
