abstract
Arc-melting (AC) and directional solidi ﬁcation (DS) techniques were used to prepare Nb-15Si-22Ti-2Al-
2Hf-2V-(2, 14) Cr alloys (hereafter referred as to 2Cr and 14Cr alloys, respectively), and the micro-
structural evolution and mechanical properties, including Vickers hardness, room temperature fracture
toughness and high temperature strength, of the two AC and DS alloys were compared. The results
showed that with heat-treatment at 1350
/C14 C for 50 h, the AC-2Cr alloy composed of Nb solid solution
(NbSS) and a-Nb5Si3 silicide, while Laves C15-Cr 2Nb phase arose in the 14Cr alloy. With two-phase Nb SS/
a-Nb5Si3 microstructure, the AC-2Cr alloy showed excellent room-temperature fracture toughness ( KQ:
14.2 MPa m1/2) and 0.2% yield strength at 1250 /C14 C( s0.2: 315 MPa) and 1350 /C14 C( s0.2: 294 MPa), better than
the AC-14Cr alloy with tri-phase Nb SS/a-Nb5Si3/C15-Cr2Nb microstructure ( KQ: 9.4 MPa m 1/2, s0.2:
189 MPa at 1250 /C14 C and 87 MPa at 1350 /C14 C). The DS technique was found not to change the phase
constituent of each alloy, but it made the microstructure slightly orient to the growth direction, resulting
in a signi ﬁcant improvement in room-temperature fracture toughness (by ~43%) and high-temperature
yield strength s0.2 (by ~55%), as compared with the AC samples.

1. Introduction
With the increasing thrust to weight ratio of advanced aero-
nautic and astronautic propulsion systems, the structural materials
used to prepare the hottest components in the propulsion systems
should possess a higher operating temperature (temperature
capability), they also need a balanced combination of properties,
including ductility and toughness at low temperatures, and
strength and creep resistance at elevated temperatures, as well as
environmental stability. Recent research has shown that Nb eSi
binary alloys, whose melting point is as high as 1880 /C14 C, have great
potential for overcoming the operating temperature barrier of Ni
superalloys and of improving the ef ﬁciency of jet engines [1e5].
The microstructures of these Nb eSi based alloys basically comprise
ductile Nb solid solution (Nb SS) and stiffening Nb 5Si3 silicide, in
which the Nb SS offers room-temperature toughness, while the
stiffening Nb 5Si3 silicide supplies the high-temperature strength
and creep resistance.
To further improve and balance the mechanical properties of the
NbeSi based alloys, alloying [6e9] and directional solidi ﬁcation
(DS) techniques [1e4,8,10e12] are often used. Ti and Hf are the only
alloying elements that do not increase the ductile-to-brittle tran-
sition temperature (DBTT) of Nb [9]; on the other hand, they are the
toughening and ductilizing elements for Nb. For example, the
fracture toughness of Nb-16Si alloy is less than 6 MPa m 1/2, how-
ever, a considerable improvement in the fracture toughness up to a
range of 13 e16 MPa m 1/2 has been obtained when a 40 at.% Ti is
added [13]. The DS technique is found to be one of the most
effective approaches in enhancing high-temperature strength
[8,12]. It has been reported that in Nb-10Ti-18Si-10Mo-15W alloy,
the compressive strength at 1500
/C14 C is increased from 350 MPa for
the as-cast sample, to 650 MPa for the directionally solidi ﬁed
sample [8].
For the two-phase Nb SS/Nb5Si3 microstructure, the balance be-
tween toughness and strength can be achieved by adjusting the
fraction and morphology of the constituent phases. But this two-
phase microstructure cannot provide oxidation resistance, which
might result in disastrous consequences for the Nb eSi based alloys
when they serve in oxygen atmosphere at high temperature. It has
been con ﬁrmed that the Laves Cr 2Nb intermetallic compound is

The aforementioned review strongly suggests that break-

individually achieved for various Nb eSi composition alloys. How-

this work, we have performed a detailed phase constitution and
microstructural evaluation of arc-molten and directly solidiﬁed Nb-
15Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-2V-(2, 14)Cr alloys. It is expected that signiﬁcant
changes in the microstructures from two-phase Nb SS/Nb5Si3 to tri-
phase Nb SS/Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb will be obtained through sharply
increasing the Cr content from 2 at.% to 14 at.%, and consequently,
the comprehensive effect of the Cr 2Nb phase and the DS technique
on the room-temperature fracture toughness and high-
temperature strength of the current Nb eSi based alloys was
investigated.
2. Experimental procedures
The purity of all raw elements used was 99.50% (in mass) or
higher. Two button ingots of Nb-15Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-2V-2Cr (2Cr
alloy) and Nb-15Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-2V-14Cr (14Cr alloy), each with a
weight of 1500 g, were prepared by vacuum non-consumable arc
melting (AC) in a copper water-cooled crucible under high purity
argon atmosphere with a tungsten electrode. The button ingots
were melted six times to ensure chemical homogeneity. The master
alloy rods with a diameter of 14 mm and a length of 70 mm were
cut from the ingots by electro-discharge machining (EDM) and then
two of them were assembled within an Al
2O3 crucible
(F15 mm /C2 200 mm) with high purity Y 2O3, laid on the interior
wall surface. The assemblage was set into the induction coil of a
vertical Bridgman furnace with argon atmosphere, below the in-
duction coil there was a liquid-metal pool for cooling. Heated at
190 0 /C14 C for 20 min, the assemblage was withdrawn from the in-
duction coil and subsequently quenched in the pool with a liquid
GaeIneSn bath when the withdrawal distance reached 150 mm,
while withdrawing rates of 18 mm/min and 36 mm/min were
employed. DS cylinders with smooth surfaces were then obtained
after breaking the Al 2O3 crucible. It should be pointed out that to
ensure the testing specimens of an alloy having the same original
composition, the materials for preparation of the AC and DS sam-
ples were taken from the centre of the button ingot. Zhang [19]
found a reaction layer of HfO 2 and Y 2O3 formed only at metal-
emould interface (3 e8 mm in thickness) in the Nb eSi based alloy
and the crucible system. In addition, the majority of the HfO 2eY2O3
inclusions concentrated around the solid eliquid interface rather
than in steady state growth region, however, some HfO 2eY2O3 in-
clusions were retained in the metal matrix. Therefore, the
contamination of the DS Nb eSi based alloys by the HfO 2eY2O3
inclusions should affect the behaviour of the bulk alloys during
testing.
The AC ingots and DS cylinders were heat treated at 1350 /C14 C for
50 h in a vacuum, followed by furnace cooling. The phase consti-
tutions of the as-cast, directly solidi ﬁed and heat-treated samples
were identi ﬁed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis using Cu Ka
(l ¼ 0.15405 nm) radiation at 40 kV and 40 mA (Rigaku D/Max
2500PC). The microstructures were observed using scanning elec-
tron microscopy (SEM) (JEOL JXA-8100) in back-scattered mode
(BSE), equipped with an energy-dispersion X-ray spectroscopy
(EDS, Oxford Instruments, UK) to analyse phase compositions. The
phase fraction was statistically determined by quantitative metal-
lographic analysis of microstructure images taken in the BSE mode.
Bulk Vickers hardness ( Hv) was measured under an applied load of
9.8 N on a digital HXZ-1000 micro-sclerometer; 1 1 Hv measure-
ments were taken for each sample to obtain an average value.
Single-notched three-point-bending specimens 30 mm in length,
6 mm in width and 3 mm in thickness were used for fracture
toughness (KQ) measurements, while the longest side (30 mm) was
parallel to the growth direction and taken from the centre of the bar
to avoid contamination of the atmosphere in the furnace on the
testing specimens. A notch of 3 mm perpendicular to the growth
direction was introduced on the middle of the bend sample by
electrical discharge machining with a 0.2-mm diameter Cu wire.
The three-point-bending tests were conducted on an Instron 8801
testing machine with a loading span of 25 mm and a crosshead
displacement rate of 0.1 mm/min. The compressive tests at 1250 /C14 C
and 1350 /C14 C were conducted in argon atmosphere at a strain rate of
3 /C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1 using a Gleeble 1500 testing machine. The tempera-
ture increase rate was 200 /C14 C/min and the specimens were held for
ﬁve minutes at the testing temperature, prior to testing, in order to
homogenize the temperature throughout the heating area. The
compressive cylinders also taken from centre of the bar were 8 mm
in diameter and 12 mm in length (the growth direction), to which
the loading direction is parallel. At least three samples for each
alloy were made for measurements of toughness and strength to
obtain the average value.

Recently, P. Tsakiropoulos et al., con ﬁrmed the C14 NbCr 2 Laves
in their microstructures [20,21,25], through combination of The

the Nb eCreAl system at concentrations S5 at.%. The discrepancy
between our work and Tsakiropoulos' work [20,21] is the Al con-
tent. The Al content in our work is 2 at.%, while is 5 at.% in Tsakir-
opoulos' work, which may result in different Cr 2Nb structure. The V
addition might be another factor that results in the formation of the
C15-Cr2Nb phase, but not the C14-Cr 2Nb phase. In addition, due to
the high coincidence in diffraction peaks of the C15 and C14
structure (Thoma and Perepezko's work [22]), the detected peaks
may be from the either phase or both, so the crystal structure of the
Cr2Nb phase directly conﬁrmed by the XRD analysis may have some
uncertainty. Recently, we investigated an Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-
17Cr alloy (this composition is similar to the 14Cr alloy in this
work, which is to be published in MMTA [30]) with the Cr2Nb phase
by TEM. Analysis revealed that the Cr 2Nb phase has a typical
diffraction pattern of the cubic crystal structure. Referred to the
result [30] and the fact that the Al content in this work is lesser than
the criteria that Al stabilizes the C14 Laves phase in the Nb eCreAl
system at concentrations S5 at.%, we primarily con ﬁrm that the
Cr2Nb in this work adopts a C15 structure. In fact that the C15-
Cr2(Nb, Ti) phases exists over a wide range of Ti and Nb composi-
tions [23], and also exists with the Mo and W additions [9].
Comparing the Refs. [20,21,23,25,27,30,9] with this work, it is
suggested that the stability of the C14-Cr 2Nb phase and the crystal
structure of the multi-component Cr 2Nb phase at room tempera-
ture are sensitive to the alloying elements. To accurately determine
the Cr 2Nb phase structure in the Nb eSi Cr based alloys; the
diffraction pattern analysis is required for further consideration.

3.3. Mechanical properties at room and high temperatures
Table 3 summarizes the average values and standard deviations
of the bulk Hv and the fracture toughness ( K
Q) of the heat-treated
samples at room temperature. As the Cr content increases from
2 at.% to 14 at.%, the bulk Hv increases, whereas the KQ decreases.
For example, the Hv and KQ of the AC-2Cr alloy are respectively 765
and 14.2 MPa m1/2, and 897 and 9.4 MPa m 1/2 for the AC-14Cr alloy.
For each alloy, the DS samples show a little higher Hv than the AC
samples, but the growth rate does not seem to affect the KQ of the
DS samples. It should be emphasized that the fracture toughness of
the 2Cr and 14Cr alloys is signi ﬁcantly improved by the DS tech-
nique. As can be seen from Table 3, the KQ of the 2Cr alloy increases
from 14.2 MPa m 1/2 of the AC sample to 17.5 MPa m 1/2 of the DS
sample (average value of the KQ at growth rates of 18 mm/min and
36 mm/min), by an amplitude of about 23%. As for the 14Cr alloy,
the improvement amplitude of the KQ by the DS technique is about
43%. It should be noticed that some hafnia-yttria inclusions were
still retained in the microstructure ( Fig. 4(b0) and (c0)). These oxide-
inclusions might affect the mechanical properties of the solidi ﬁed
microstructure at some extent, especial ductility and toughness, as
compared with that of the AC sample, which depends on the
fraction and distribution of the inclusions, as well as the solidi ﬁ-
cation conditions. The mechanism is that the oxide-inclusions are
usually the position of the crack initiation in structural materials
through cracking of themselves or debonding of the interfaces
between the inclusions and the matrix, resulted from the stress
concentration on the inclusions. Further work is needed to under-
stand the quantitative relationship between the properties and the
inclusion in the DS Nb eSi based alloys.
Fractographic observations on the fracture surface of the bended
AC-2Cr and AC-14Cr samples, shown in Fig. 6 , reveal that the
fracture modes of the three phases are different. On the fractured
Nb
SS phase, river patterns are observed ( Fig. 6(a)), which illustrates
the cleavage failure mode of the Nb SS phase under bending. The a-
Nb5Si3 and Cr 2Nb phases show very smooth fracture surfaces
without any plastic deformation traces, as seen in Fig. 6(b) , which
suggests that they fail in a brittle manner. As for the DS samples, the
morphologies of the fractured phases are quite similar to those in
the AC samples shown in Fig. 6. Therefore, the cleavage and brittle
mechanisms remain in the directional Nb SS and a-Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb
phases, respectively.

4. Discussion
In this investigation, the phase constitution and the micro-
structure of the AC Nb-15Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-2V alloy are changed
dramatically ( Figs. 1 and 2 ) as the nominal Cr content added is
increased from 2 at.% to 14 at.%. These metallurgical changes
include: 1) the phase constitution from the Nb SS/a-Nb5Si3 to the
NbSS/a-Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb and 2) the reduction of the Nb SS fraction due
to 12 at.% Nb being replaced by Cr in the 14Cr alloy as compared to
the 2Cr alloy. Careful observation on Fig. 3 ﬁnds that there are small
amount of the Nb SS phase within the black Laves C15-Cr 2Nb phase,
so the Laves C15-Cr 2Nb on interstices of the Nb SS þ a-Nb5Si3
eutectic colonies and the dendritic Nb SS arms is formed by eutectic
reaction of L / NbSS þ C15. Quantitative metallographic analysis of
the microstructure images of the AC samples gives an Nb SS volume
fraction, of 60 ± 4% for the 2Cr alloy and 37 ± 3% for the 14Cr alloy
(63 ± 5% for silicides and Cr2Nb). A modiﬁed rule of mixtures (ROM)
was used to evaluate the bulk toughness of the Nb eSi-based alloys
[26,28]. The ROM toughness is given by:

alloy. Because Ti alloyed Nb 5Si3 may have a higher toughness than
the unalloyed Nb 5Si3 phase [9], the bulk estimated KQ of the 14Cr
alloy should be higher than 1 1.3 MPa m 1/2, if the toughness of the
alloyed Nb5Si3 phase is available. As there is no the KQ data of the
Nb solid-solution alloy with a composition close to Nb-23.5Ti-2.2Cr
in the AC-2Cr alloy (Table 2), the estimated bulk KQ can not be given
by Eq. (1). It should be pointed out that Cr is an element that in-
creases the DBTT of Nb or embrittles Nb [9], therefore, the Nb SS
phase in the AC-2Cr alloy containing lower Cr concentration
(2.13e2.41 at.%) than that of the Nb SS phase in the AC-14Cr alloy
(9.8e1 1.8 at.%), must have a higher toughness than 18.5 MPa m 1/2,
and a higher estimated bulk KQ for the AC-2Cr alloy is expected by
Eq. (1) if the Kd value of the Nb SS solid-solution alloy with a
composition close to Nb-23.5Ti-2.2Cr is available. The above phe-
nomena suggest that an increasing nominal Cr content is harmful to
the toughness of the bulk Nb eSi based alloys.
Interestingly, the multi-component Cr2Nb phase is found to play
a negative role in the strength of the current alloys at 1250 /C14 C and
1350 /C14 C, as can be seen in Fig. 7 and Table 4. At 1400 /C14 C the strength
of the Cr2Nb binary was measured to be about 300 MPa [29], which
is almost at the same level as that of the Nb SS/a-Nb5Si3 micro-
structure at 1350 /C14 C( Table 4). Therefore, the Cr 2Nb binary should
act as a strengthening phase for the Nb eSi-based alloys; subse-
quently, the 14Cr alloy, with a higher fraction of the stiffening a-
Nb5Si3 and Cr2Nb phases, should exhibit greater strength at 1250 /C14 C
and 1350 /C14 C, than the 2Cr alloy with a lower fraction of the stiff-
ening a-Nb5Si3 and Cr2Nb phases. However, the results are contrary
(Table 4 ). In this work, Ti, Hf, Al, Si and V solute into the Cr 2Nb
phase, resulting in a decrease in the melting point of this multi-
component Cr 2Nb phase, that is, from 1770 /C14 C of the binary
Cr2Nb phase [24] to a temperature lower than 1500 /C14 C [30]. This
suggests that as the testing temperature is 1250 /C14 C and above
(closer to the melting point of the multi-component Cr 2Nb), the
multi-component Cr 2Nb phase might become a soft phase
compared with the Cr 2Nb binary and thus, decrease the bulk
strength. On the other hand, the Cr addition weakens the Nb eSi
based alloys at high temperatures. The fact that the strength of the
14Cr alloy decreases at temperatures above 1250 /C14 C, faster than
that of the 2Cr alloy, also supports the above deduction. Further
work concerning the effect of the alloying elements on the
strengthening or weakening mechanisms of the Laves Cr 2Nb phase
is still needed, which would be the theoretical base for composition
design.
Comparing mechanical properties in Tables 3 and 4 it is found
that the DS technique dramatically increases the fracture toughness
and high-temperature yield strength of the 2Cr and 14Cr alloys at
the same time, by 23% e29% and 35% e55%, respectively, as
compared with the corresponding alloys prepared by the AC
technique. In our previous studies [8,12], improvement in high-
temperature strength by the DS technique was achieved in wide
range of Nb eSi based alloys. For example, increment of the
compressive strength at 1200 /C14 C and 1400 /C14 Cb y5 0 e100% was
achieved in Nb-(10, 20)Mo-22Ti-18Si-(1 e10)C and Nb-(10,20)Mo-
10Ti-18Si-(0,5)W alloys. Similarly, the well-aligned microstructure
of the DS Nb-22Ti-12Si-14Cr-2Al-2Hf alloy showed that the fracture
toughness was 50% higher than that of alloy fabricated by non-
consumable AC [10]. These results strongly con ﬁrm that the
microstructural orientation and the phase morphology play key
roles in the mechanical properties of an Nb eSi based alloy with a
ﬁxed phase constituent. It has been revealed that the sliding of the
interface between the Nb SS and a-Nb5Si3 phases or Nb SS and Cr2Nb
phases, as well as the collapse of the a-Nb5Si3 and Cr 2Nb phases,
results in damage and the failure of the Nb eSi based alloys under
the high-temperature compression condition [8,12,31]. With the
more uniform microstructure, the AC samples have more interfaces
that incline to the loading direction, which might provide more
sites that slide and easily cause damage. Whereas in the DS samples
with oriented and continuous microstructures ( Figs. 4 and 5 ), the
interfaces among the phases that incline to the loading direction
have lower frequencies than those in the AC samples, in other
words, most of the interfaces are slightly parallel to the growth or
the loading direction. With such an oriented and continuous
microstructure, the DS samples usually have few interfaces for
sliding damage, and thus display excellent deformation resistant
capabilities and strength, better than the AC samples ( Table 4).
The oriented and continuous ductile Nb
SS phase is still beneﬁcial
to the fracture toughness KQ. The KQ values of the Nb eSi based
samples are relative to the propagation path of the crack or the
interaction between the crack and the Nb SS phase [10,1 1]. In the AC
NbeSi based alloys [9,31] without the oriented and continuous
NbSS phase, the crack was found to propagate mostly along the
interface of the Nb SS/a-Nb5Si3 or bypass the Nb SS phase rather than
go through the Nb SS phase when the crack met the Nb SS phase.
Therefore, the NbSS phase could not fully contribute the toughening
characteristic itself to the bulk KQ of the NbeSi based alloys through
breaking. In the DS samples, the crack initiated from the notch is
perpendicular to the oriented and continuous NbSS phase and it has
to go through the NbSS [1 1]. It is believed that, in this case, the strain
energy at the crack tip might be fully consumed by the broken Nb SS
phase, then the Nb SS phase exerts its toughening role on the Nb SS/
a-Nb5Si3 and NbSS/a-Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructures, resulting in an
outstanding improvement in the bulk fracture toughness of the DS
NbeSi based alloys.
5. Conclusions
A comparison of the microstructure, room-temperature fracture
toughness and high-temperature compressive strength of the AC
and DS Nb-15Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-2V-(2, 14) Cr alloys was investigated,
and the following major conclusions were drawn:
1) The AC Nb-15Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-2V-2Cr alloy comprises the Nb SS
and a-Nb5Si3 silicide, while the AC Nb-15Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-2V-
14Cr alloy contains the Nb SS, a-Nb5Si3 and Laves C15-Cr 2Nb
phases. The DS samples have the same phase constituents as the
corresponding AC samples, but they show slightly oriented and
continuous microstructures along the growth direction;
2) With the two-phase Nb SS/a-Nb5Si3 microstructure, the 2Cr alloy
shows greater fracture toughness KQ and 0.2% yield strength s0.2
at 1250 /C14 C and 1350 /C14 C than those of the 14Cr alloy with the
three-phase Nb SS/a-Nb5Si3/C15-Cr2Nb microstructure. The
Cr2Nb phase is found to be harmful to both properties of the
multi-component Nb eSi based alloys; for example, the KQ and
s0.2 at 1350 /C14 C of the AC-2Cr alloy are 14.2 MPa m 1/2 and
