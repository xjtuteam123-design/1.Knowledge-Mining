Elevated temperature Mg 97Y2Cu1 alloy with
long period ordered structure
Yoshihito Kawamura, * Takayuki Kasahara, Shogo Izumi and Michiaki Yamasaki
Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Kumamoto University, Kumamoto 860-8555, Japan
Received 6 February 2006; revised 9 May 2006; accepted 10 May 2006
Available online 9 June 2006
Mg97Y2Cu1 (at.%) cast alloy has an 18R-type long period ordered (LPO) structure that forms coherently with the a-Mg matrix
during casting. Hot working improved the mechanical properties of the LPO Mg 97Y2Cu1 cast alloy. Hot-extruded LPO Mg 97Y2-
Cu1 alloy exhibited high tensile mechanical properties. The yield strength, tensile strength and elongation were 297 MPa,
377 MPa and 8.1%, respectively, at ambient temperature, and 273 MPa, 344 MPa and 16.3%, respectively, at 473 K.

Keywords: Magnesium; Mg–Cu–Y alloy; Long period ordered (LPO) structure; Mechanical properties; Extrusion
In the last decade, magnesium alloys have been used
as structural components in automotive, aerospace
and electronic industries where weight reduction is re-
quired [1,2]. Recently, new magnesium alloys with high
mechanical strength and heat-resistance have been
developed in the Mg–RE–Zn system (where RE repre-
sents rare earth elements), and have been widely noticed
within industrial circles. Several research groups have
attempted to strengthen the alloys by quasicrystalline
dispersion [3–9]. These quasicrystalline-containing al-
loys are composed of a smaller amount of Y than Zn,
typically with a Y/Zn ratio below about 0.3. Our previ-
ous studies developed Mg–RE–Zn alloys that are
characterized by a long period ordered (LPO) structure
[8–19]. These alloys have a higher RE concentration
than Zn, typically with an RE/Zn ratio of around 0.5
or higher. The Mg–RE–Zn alloys containing the LPO
phase exhibit greatly superior mechanical properties
than the quasicrystalline-containing Mg–RE–Zn alloys
at both ambient and elevated temperatures. The LPO
Mg–RE–Zn alloys are, therefore, promising candidates
for lightweight structural materials.
In 1994, Luo et al. reported that the structure of the
X-phase in Mg–Y–Zn cast alloy is characterized by an
18R LPO structure [20,21]. The research of Kawamura
et al. revealed that the LPO Mg–RE–Zn alloys are high
strength magnesium alloys. In 2001, LPO Mg
97Y2Zn1
(at.%) alloy with a yield strength of 610 MPa and elon-
gation of 5% at room temperature was developed by
rapidly solidiﬁed powder metallurgy processing [8].I n
2004, it was reported that plastically deformed LPO
Mg
97Y2Zn1 cast alloy exhibited a high yield strength
of 375 MPa and a reasonable elongation of 4% at room
temperature [9–14]. Moreover, the mechanical proper-
ties and LPO-phase formation have been systematically
investigated for Mg
97RE2Zn1 alloys, where RE is Y,
La, Ce, Pr, Sm, Nd, Dy, Ho, Er, Gd, Tb, Tm or Yb
[10,13,15]. As a result, it was found that the LPO Mg–
RE–Zn alloys can be classiﬁed into two types. Type I
includes Mg–Y–Zn, Mg–Dy–Zn, Mg–Ho–Zn, and
Mg–Er–Zn alloys where the LPO phase forms during
solidiﬁcation [10,13,19]. The LPO structure is 18R and
changes mainly to 14H with high temperature annealing
[16,19]. Type II includes Mg–Gd–Zn, Mg–Tb–Zn, and
Mg–Tm–Zn alloys in which there is no LPO phase in
the as-cast ingots and the 14H LPO phase precipitates
from a
0-Mg supersaturated solid solution by annealing
at high temperature [15]. The mechanical properties of
these LPO Mg–RE–Zn alloys are drastically improved
by plastic deformation. Extruded LPO Mg–RE–Zn
alloys exhibit high yield strengths, greater than
350 MPa, and elongations of 4% or higher at ambient
temperature, and high elevated temperature yield
strength greater than 300 MPa at 473 K [12,13]. Matsu-
ura et al. reported in 2005 that a 14H LPO phase adjoin-
ing amorphous phase was observed in a melt-spun
Mg
98Y1Cu1 ribbon [22]. However, there are still no re-
ports on the formation of LPO phase and mechanical

LPO phase will be formed in cast Mg–Y–Cu alloys with
high Mg concentrations and that their wrought alloys,

Mg97Y2Cu1 (at.%) cast ingots were produced by
induction melting of pure Mg and Cu metals and pre-
alloyed Mg 89.5Y10.5 ingot in a carbon crucible under
an argon atmosphere, and then cast into a steel die
(30 mm D · 100 mm L). Some cast ingots were annealed
at 773 K for 10 h in air. The ingots were machined into
round bars (29 mm D · 70 mm L). These bars were ex-
truded at an extrusion ratio of 10:1, a temperature of
598 K, and a ram speed of 2.5 mm s
/C01. Several commer-
cial alloys (AZ31B, AZ61A, AZ91D, AM60) were also
extruded under the same conditions for comparison.
The structure of the alloy was investigated by scan-
ning electron microscopy (SEM), and transmission elec-
tron microscopy (TEM). Tensile tests were carried out
using an Instron-type tensile testing machine at room
and elevated temperatures with an initial strain rate of
5 · 10
/C04 s/C01. The diameter and gauge length of tensile
samples were 3.0 mm and 25 mm, respectively, for ambi-
ent temperature tests, and 2.0 mm and 10 mm, respec-
tively, for elevated temperature tests. The strain at
ambient temperature was measured with an electrical
strain gauge. The tensile axis was along the direction
of extrusion.
Figure 1 shows the SEM micrograph of the as-cast
Mg
97Y2Cu1 alloy. Lamellar contrast was observed
around the cell boundaries. A TEM micrograph taken
from the area of the lamellar contrast in the SEM micro-
graph is shown in Figure 2(a). Microscopically, the area
of the lamellar contrast consisted of lamellar structures
composed of LPO and 2H–Mg phases. The selected
area electron diﬀraction (SAED) pattern shown in
Figure 2 (b) indicates an 18R-type LPO structure
[19,21]. Figure 3 shows the TEM micrograph and SAED
pattern of the Mg
97Y2Cu1 alloy annealed at 773 K for
10 h. The TEM micrographs of the LPO structure ob-
served in the as-cast and heat-treated alloys show the
same stacking contrast of (0 0 3) 18R with a period of
1.56 nm. Although diﬀerent spacing was occasionally
observed in places, the SAED patterns indicate that its
structure is practically 18R structure ( Fig. 3 (b) and
(c)). This 18R LPO structure is considered to be the
same structure as the 18R-type LPO structure observed
in Mg–Y–Zn cast alloy [19]. This result suggests that the
18R-LPO structure is stable on annealing at 773 K.

erties of the as-cast and wrought Mg 97Y2Cu1 alloys.

of the wrought Mg

Zn alloys, can be strengthened by hot working. Figure 5

LPO Mg–Y–Cu alloys with further improved mechani-

composition or process conditions.
The LPO phase forms in Mg–RE–Zn alloys where
RE is Y, Dy, Ho, Er, Gd, Tb or Tm [13]. These LPO
Mg–RE–Zn alloys are classiﬁed into two types. In
type-I alloys where RE is Y, Dy, Ho or Er, the LPO
phase forms during solidiﬁcation [10,13,16]. In contrast,
in type-II alloys where RE is Gd, Tb or Tm, the LPO
phase forms by annealing [15]. Since the Mg
97Y2Cu1
alloy had the 18R-LPO phase in the as-cast state, the
LPO Mg 97Y2Cu1 alloy may be classiﬁed as type-I along
with Mg–(Y, Dy, Ho, and Er)–Zn alloys. Although the
LPO structure in the type-I Mg–RE–Zn alloys changes
mostly from 18R to 14H by annealing at 773 K for
10 h [11,14], the 18R-LPO structure of the as-cast
Mg
97Y2Cu1 alloy was unchanged by annealing. The
18R-LPO structure of the Mg–Y–Cu alloy was ther-
mally stable compared to the LPO Mg–RE–Zn alloys.
Moreover, like the LPO Mg–RE–Zn alloys, Mg–RE–
Cu alloys are expected to form the LPO phase where
RE is Dy, Ho, Er, Gd, Tb or Tm.
It has been pointed out that the characteristics of the
constituents of the LPO Mg–RE–Zn alloys are negative
mixing enthalpy of each pair, large diﬀerence in atomic
size, hexagonal closed packed (hcp) structure at room
temperature, and large solid solubility limit above
several at.% in magnesium [13,23]. Elemental Cu has a
face-center cubic structure and its solid solubility limit
of only 0.013 at.% in magnesium is much lower than
the 2.4 at.% for Zn [24]. The atomic size of Cu
(0.128 nm) is close to that of Zn (0.133 nm) [25]. The
mixing enthalpy of the Mg–Cu and Y–Cu pairs is simi-
lar to Mg–Zn and Y–Zn pairs, respectively [26]. There-
fore, the characteristics of the constituents of LPO
Mg–RE–X alloys may be modiﬁed as follows: Mg, RE
and X have a negative mixing enthalpy for each pair,
in particular the mixing enthalpy of the RE–X pair is
large and negative; RE elements have hcp structure at
room temperature and large solid solubility limits above
about 3.75 at.% in magnesium; and the atomic size of X
and the RE element is considerably diﬀerent from that
of Mg.

sults are summarized as follows: (1) The 18R-LPO
phase was observed in the Mg 97Y2Cu1 alloy. The 18R
structure was stable on annealing at 773 K for 10 h.
The Mg 97Y2Cu1 alloy was classiﬁed into type-I LPO
alloys that include Mg–Y–Zn, Mg–Dy–Zn, Mg–Ho–
Zn and Mg–Er–Zn. (2) Although the as-cast LPO
Mg
97Y2Cu1 alloy exhibited poor mechanical properties,
hot extrusion drastically improved its mechanical prop-
erties. The wrought LPO Mg 97Y2Cu1 alloy had tensile
yield strength, ultimate strength, and elongation of
