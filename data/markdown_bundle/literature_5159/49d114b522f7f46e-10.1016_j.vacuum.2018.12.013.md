Abstract 
The effect of withdrawal rates on the dendrite growth and defects formation in the rejoined platforms of 
a Ni-based single crystal superalloy during directi onal solidification was studied by experimental 
investigations and ProCAST simulations. The results showed that the dendrite pattern in the rejoined 
platforms mainly comprised two regions: original pr imary dendrite region and lateral growth region. 
With increasing withdrawal rate, the lateral growth was more severe and the tendency of low angle 
grain boundaries (LABs) was greater because of dend rite deformation. Once the withdrawal rate 
exceeded 100 µm/s, local fragmentation appeared in the middle of the platforms and some 
ternary dendrites were detached from their parent d endrites. When the withdrawal rate was increased to 
150 µm/s, lots of dendrite fragments formed and the inte grity of single crystal was completely 
destroyed, which might be caused by the solute enri chment and was further promoted by the unstable 
growth conditions. 
Keywords : Ni-based superalloys; dendrite growth; LABs; fragmentation. 
1. Introduction 
Owing to the excellent properties at elevated tempe rature, Ni-based single crystal superalloys are 
widely used in the turbine blades and guide vanes o f the advanced aero-engines and industrial gas

turbines [1-4]. However, stray grains (SGs) are eas ily formed on the edge of turbine blades [5-7], and 
introduce high-angle boundaries to damage the mecha nical properties of the single crystal blades [8, 9 ]. 
Therefore, the grain continuator technique was prop osed to inhibit the formation of SGs at the platfor m 
ends of blades. According to the theory of seeding method, an assistant bar was added to introduce the 
primary grain to fill up the platform ends before n ucleation of SGs [10, 11]. However, this technique 
was easy to introduce LABs in the platform ends, because the dendrites went through branching growth, 
orientation deviation and subsequent impingement [1 2]. Similar phenomena appeared in the nozzle 
guide vanes due to their similar geometrical featur es: rejoined structure [13]. Owing to the 
re-converging structure resulting complex dendritic growth path, the rejoined platforms are considered 
to be the most sensitive sites prone to casting defects. 
Over the past few decades, many studies have been c arried out to investigate the formation 
mechanisms of casting defects in directional solidi fication. The majority of these studies were relate d 
to the SGs and the LABs within the shrouds [9, 11, 13, 14]. Some investigations reported that SGs were 
derived from the undercooling nucleation at the shroud’s ends which were isolated thermal undercooled 
regions [7, 11, 15, 16]. The generally accepted vie ws on the formation of LABs were: dendritic 
deformed during branching growth, resulting in the orientation deviation, and LABs formed when the 
dendrites re-converged [13, 17, 18]. Some studies t ried to reveal the effect of dendrite branching on the 
formation of LABs within the shrouds at a certain w ithdrawal rate [13, 17, 19], while no consistent 
conclusion was reached. Furthermore, the influence of processing parameter on the microstructure 
formation of single crystal castings mainly aimed a t the cylindrical bars [20-22]. Besides that, Wang et 
al. [14] studied the effect of withdrawal rates on the three-dimensional dendrite growth within the 
shrouds of single crystal blades, and found that th e defects were increased with increasing withdrawal

2. Experimental and simulation methods 
2.1 Experimental methods 
A Ni-based superalloy with composition of 5.6 Al, 7 .5 Ta, 2.0 Mo, 8.0 W, 2.0 Re, 4.3 Cr, 9.0 Co, 0.5 
Nb, 0.1 Hf, 0.006 C, and the balance Ni(mass fracti on) was used in current experiments. The solidus 
and liquidus of the alloy were determined to be 134 2 °C and 1399 °C by differential scanning 
calorimetry (DSC, SDT Q600) analysis. A model with three rejoined platforms was used to prepare the 
single crystal castings, and the platforms were labeled as 1, 2 and 3 from bottom to top, as shown in Fig. 
1a. For ease of description later, the platforms were divided into three regions: left (L), middle (M) , and 
right (R). 
The single crystals were casted using traditional h igh rate solidification method and the furnace main ly 
consisted of three units: a vacuum-induction meltin g unit, a graphite heater unit and a cooling zone, as

shown in Fig. 1b. Firstly, the furnace chamber was vacuumed and the mold shell was preheated to 1540 
ºC. Secondly, the superheated molten alloy (1580 °C) was poured into the mould and held for 10 min. 
Finally, the ceramic mold was pulled out from the f urnace at predetermined withdrawal rates: 60 µm/s,

The castings were cleared and the platforms were se ctioned from the sample transversely (parallel to

with a solution of HNO 3, HF, and C 3H8O3 (volume ratio 1 : 2 : 4). The microstructure was o bserved 
with an optical microscopy (OM, Leica DM-4000 M). T he electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) 
analysis technology was employed for determining th e dendrite orientations, and it was performed in 
Tescan Mira 3 XMU scanning electron microscope (SEM ) equipped with Channel 5 analysis software. 
In order to determine the composition changes in di fferent regions of the rejoined platforms, energy 
dispersion spectrum (EDS) tests were carried out in the regions of interest. 
2.2 Simulation methods 
In order to exhibit the thermal profile during the directional solidification process, the castings we re 
simulated by ProCAST software[23]. The initial stat e of simulation was after the holding process, the 
melt temperature was set to 1550 ºC and the chill-p late temperature was 400 ºC. During directional 
solidification, the heat transfer was assumed to be heat conduction and heat radiation, and heat 
convection was ignored. Radiation heat transfer was calculated by a radiation algorithm, thus the 
reﬂections, the obstructions and the shadowing effe cts were taken into account. The thermophysical 
parameters were calculated by ProCAST and the therm odynamic software JMatPro. The thermal 
conductivity and thermal emissivity coefficients we re gained from the references [7, 24-28]. The

3. Results and discussions 
3.1 Dendrite morphology in the different regions of the platforms 
The dendritic structures of the platform 3 in XY plane at different withdrawal rates are shown in Figs 2, 
3, 4, and 5, with a series of magnified local micro -graphs and corresponding EBSD results. A similar 
dendrite pattern was observed regardless of the wit hdrawal rate, and the pattern mainly consisted of 
two regions marked by region I (I-L and I-R) and re gion II (II-L and II-R), as shown in Figs. 2a, 3a, 4a 
and 5a. Region I, the original primary dendrite arm s grew regularly and were cross-shaped with four 
orthogonal secondary dendrite arms, as shown the ma gnified local micro-graphs in Figs. 2b, 3b, 4b and 
5b. These regions were formed from the branching of original dendrites from the spiral grain selector. 
Region II, it was mainly composed of long secondary dendrite arms which were formed through lateral 
growth. 
At the withdrawal rate of 60 µm/s, a slight lateral growth of secondary branches was observed and no 
obvious grain boundaries (GBs) was found in the reg ion M, as shown the local magnified micro-graph 
in Fig. 2c. Its corresponding polar diagram in Fig. 2d indicated that no orientation deviation or GBs 
appeared in the region M. With increasing the withd rawal rate to 75 µm/s, the lateral growth was more 
severe at the same position. Meanwhile, a GB was pr oduced when the dendrites converged in the 
middle region of the platforms, as shown in Fig. 3c . The corresponding polar diagram in Fig. 3d 
indicated a sense of slight rotation of <001> directions, while the deviation angle was less than 2°.

3.2 Lateral growth of the secondary dendrites and the LABs formation in the region II 
As mentioned above, the lateral growth of secondary dendrites appeared in the middle regions of all th e 
platforms in current experiments regardless of the withdrawal rate, as shown the regions II in Figs. 2 a, 
3a, 4a and 5a. The lateral growth was more severe w ith increasing withdrawal rates, as shown the 
magnified local graphs in Figs. 2c, 3c, 4e and 5c. This phenomenon was related to the evolution of 
thermal field during the solidification process in the platforms. The curved solidification interface 
could lead to the substantial lateral heat extracti on and result in the lateral growth of the branches [30]. 
Fig. 6 is the thermal field evolution and the isoth erm shapes at different times in the rejoined platf orms 
predicted by the thermal simulations. It showed tha t the solidification interface became inclined 
quickly after it entered the platforms. The inclina tion occurred earlier and had a greater slope at a 
higher withdrawal rate, as shown in Figs. 6a-6d. As the solidification continued, the shape of the 
solidification interface was from inclined to conc ave, as shown in Figs. 6e-6h. However, in the later 
solidification stage of the platform, the shape of the interface became an ellipsoidal surface at the 
withdrawal rate of 100 µm/s and the curvature of the surface was greater at 150 µm/s, as shown in Figs. 
6i-6l. Therefore, it indicated that the lateral gro wth of secondary branches in the platforms was 
inevitable and was more severe at higher withdrawal rates. 
Fig. 7 was the simulation results of temperature gr adient at different withdrawal rates,it showed that the 
total temperature gradient in the region M increased with increasing withdrawal rates (Figs. 7a-7d) ，the 
lateral temperature gradients (along X in Figs. 7e- 7h and along Y in Figs. 7i-7l) were larger than the 
axial temperature gradient (along Z in Figs. 7m-7p) , and the temperature gradient along X direction

3.3 A new type of defect: dendrite fragmentation in the middle regions of the platforms 
Generally, the microstructure evolution and the for mations of all potential defects during directional 
solidification were dependent on the local thermal condition, specifically the thermal gradient and 
solidification rate [8, 30]. Hansen et al. [31] dem onstrated that it might not involve significant 
detachment of dendrites under steady state conditio ns of growth rate and temperature gradient, but 
detachment might be induced with fluctuations of th e solidification rate, particularly with decelerati on

[32, 33], possibly preceded by acceleration [35]. A ctually, dendrite growth was a three-dimensional 
process, and the changes of growth conditions in ev ery direction (X, Y and Z) were not synchronous 
because of the complex geometry. Therefore, the flu ctuations of growth conditions in any direction 
might lead to the different evolution of dendrite structures. 
In current experiments, when the withdrawal rate ex ceeded 100 µm/s, local dendrite fragments 
occurred in the region M of platforms after a perio d of lateral growth, and the broken grains had a 
comparable size to the ternary dendrite arms, as sh own in Figs. 4e and 5c. In these local fragmentatio n 
zones, the secondary arms were well developed, and the ternary arms grew inadequately near the 
primary arms and were becoming stronger away from t he primary arms. The complicated and confused 
dendrite structure was not conducive to solute diff usion. Meanwhile, due to the transverse growth, 
some positive segregation elements were rejected into the interdendritic regions and accumulated easil y 
at the roots of dendrite arms, and the accumulated elements refined the dendrite arms. Thus their root s 
or nodes became necked because of excess radial sol ute rejections from primary arms, secondary arms, 
and even ternary branches [36, 37]. Consequently, s ome of fragile ternary branches around the primary 
stems were subjected to enrichment of solute in poc kets and remelted from their parent stems. 
Consequently, a remelting process at the roots was initiated driven by solute enrichment. However, 
owing to the limited space for movement, these brok en arms could only drift and deflect near their 
original positions and were with different orientat ions from their parent stems, as shown the polar 
diagrams in Figs. 4g and 5e. A similar phenomenon a lso occurred in the specimens at the withdrawal 
rate of 150 µm/s, as shown in Figs. 5a and 5c.

to a lag in the thermal response which could cause local solute pileup and eventually lead to crystal 
fragments. Besides that, as well known, the dendrit e arm spacing will decrease with increasing 
withdrawal rate, which means the solute diffusion d istance decreases and it is beneficial for solute 
diffusion. However, at the same time, the solute di ffusion time also decreases and that is bad for 
diffusion. Therefore, the influence of withdrawal r ate exists two sides. In current experiment, the 
second dendrites of lateral growth formed the main framework in the middle regions of the platforms, 
and they were the main force for rejecting solutes to surroundings. From the dendrite morphology in 
Fig. 5f, it was obviously that, the fragile ternary arms had already been eroded before they grew up 
because of insufficient growth time. After all, the solute diffusion rate was much lower than that of 
dendrite growth, and the solute released during sol idification could not be fully diffused into the li quid

In order to verify the existence of solute enrichme nt, the distribution of alloy composition in differ ent

orientation deviations of these fragments were more serious than those in the region II. There was no 
doubt that the integrity of the single crystal was destroyed completely. In order to avoid the occurre nce 
of dendrite fragmentation, the withdrawal rate should be controlled within a certain range. 
4. Conclusions 
In the current study, the dendrite growth in the re joined platforms of Ni-based superalloy during 
directional solidification at different withdrawal rates was investigated using both experiments and 
simulation methods. According to the microstructure evolution and the EBSD results in different

regions of the platforms, together with the simulat ion results from ProCAST, the main conclusions are 
obtained as follows: 
(1) Independent of the withdrawal rate, the dendrite pa tterns in the rejoined platforms mainly 
comprised of two regions: the region I was a origin al dendrite zone and the region II was a lateral 
growth zone. With increasing withdrawal rate, the l ateral growth became more severe in the middle 
regions of the platforms. 
(2) In the region II of lateral growth, the long second ary branches were prone to orientation deviation 
caused by the unstable thermal field, final to lead to the LABs in the middle regions of the platforms. 
(3) Once the withdrawal rate exceeded 100 µm/s, the local ternary dendrite fragments appeared in the 
middle regions of the platforms, which were caused by the solute enrichment at the fragile ternary 
dendrite roots in the pockets around the primary stems. 
(4) With the withdrawal rate was increased to 150 µm/s, a new region with a large area of fragments 
formed in the middle regions of the platforms, whic h was caused by the solute enrichment leading to 
the reduction of solidus. Since this defect could d estroy the integrity of single crystal castings, th e 
solidification condition should be controlled to limit its occurrence.
