Alloying effect on microstructure and mechanical
properties of thermomechanically processed
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys
Four different alloys of L1 2-type Ni 3(Si, Ti,X), where X =
Al, Cr, Co and Mo were prepared in thin sheet form by ther-
momechanical processing. The effect of alloying elements
on microstructure and tensile properties was investigated
using recrystallized Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys. The Al-added Ni3(Si,
Ti) alloy showed an L1 2 single-phase microstructure, while
the Cr-, Co- and Mo-added Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys exhibited a
two-phase microstructure consisting of L1 2 and A1(fcc Ni
solid solution). Room-temperature strength of the Ni3(Si, Ti)
alloy was enhanced by the addition of all quaternary ele-
ments, whereas high-temperature strength was enhanced
especially by the addition of Mo and Co. High-temperature
tensile elongation was remarkably improved by the addition
of all quaternary elements via suppression of intergarnular
fracture. Also, the oxidation resistance of the Ni
3(Si, Ti) al-
loy was improved particularly by the addition of Al and Cr.
The observed mechanical properties are discussed from the
microstructural as well as alloying points of view.
Keywords: Ni-base silicides; Mechanical properties at
high temperatures; Heat treatment; Microstructure
1. Introduction
Among a number of transition metals, Ti has the largest sol-
id solubility ( *11 at.%) in Ni 3Si with L1 2 structure and
substitutes for Si sites [1, 2], as shown in Fig. 1. Ni 3(Si, Ti)
alloy developed by one of the present authors has unique
tensile strength and ductility properties [3, 4]. It was re-
ported that the Ni
3(Si, Ti) alloy exhibited an increase in
flow strength with increasing temperature and also dis-
played high tensile ductility over a wide range of test tem-
peratures [3, 4]. In particular, its strength level was ex-
tremely high compared to other L1 2 ordered alloys such as
Ni3Al [5]. The Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys are also expected to have
an excellent corrosion resistance in H2SO4 solution and oxi-
dation resistance in air, at ambient and elevated tempera-
tures, respectively [5]. Thus, these alloys are considered as
candidate materials which could be used as high-tempera-
ture structural parts or structural parts in chemical plants.
To further improve the mechanical properties of the
Ni
3(Si, Ti) alloy, a small amount of interstitial atoms or
small diameter atoms have been added [3, 4, 6]. The addi-
tion of B [3, 4] and C [6] improved the tensile ductility,
while the addition of Be [6] moderately enhanced the
strength. An improvement in the mechanical properties of
the Ni3(Si, Ti) alloy was also attempted by macro-alloying,
i. e., addition of transition metals such as 1 at.% of Hf, Nb,
Cr and Fe, and 2 at.% of Mn that were mostly soluble in
L1
2 matrix [7]. However, dramatic improvement by the ad-
dition of any transition metals chosen in the previous study
was not found, either for tensile strength or for tensile elon-
gation [7]. In this study, transition metals of Al, Cr, Co and
Mo that have not been studied previously were added at
2 at.% level into the Ni
3(Si, Ti) alloy. The quaternary
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys were arc melted, thermo-mechanically
processed, cold-rolled to a thin sheet, and finally annealed

2. Experimental procedure
The composition of the base alloy (i. e., the unalloyed
Ni3(Si, Ti)) was 79.5 at.% Ni, 11 at.% Si, and 9.5 at.% Ti.
Taking into account the substitution behavior of the alloy-
ing elements in Ni 3Si [8], four kinds of quaternary
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys, i. e., Ni79.5Si11Ti7.5Al2,N i78.5Si11Ti8.5Cr2,
Ni77.5Si11Ti9.5Co2, and Ni79.5Si11Ti7.5Mo2 each doped with
50 wt.ppm boron (B) were arc-melted in an argon atmo-
sphere and cast into a water-cooled copper hearth. Alloys
used in this study were prepared from starting raw materials
of 99.9 wt.% Ni, 99.999 wt.% Si, 99.9 wt.% Ti, 99.99 wt.%
Al, 99.9 wt.% Cr, 99.9 wt.% Co, 99.9 wt.% Mo and
99.9 wt.% B. 50 wt.ppm B was doped to suppress intergra-
nular fracture of the Ni
3(Si, Ti) alloy in polycrystalline form
[3, 4]. The quaternary transition metals were added at
2 at.% level into the Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloy. Al and Mo were
added, assuming the substitution for Si(Ti) sites. Co was
added, assuming the substitution for Ni sites. Cr was added,
assuming for the substitution for both sites. The button in-
gots with a diameter of 50 mm were homogenized at
1 323 K for 48 h in a vacuum. The homogenized buttons
were cut into plates with a thickness of about 10 mm using
an electro-discharge machine (EDM), and were then warm
rolled to sheet with a thickness of 2 mm by thermomechani-
cal processing. Here, warm rolling at 573 K and subsequent
annealing at 1 273 K for 5 h in a vacuum were repeatedly
conducted. These sheets were finally annealed at 1 273 K
for 1 h to prepare starting materials for cold rolling. Cold
rolling was conducted to 90 % reduction in thickness with-
out intermediate annealing. The thickness of the obtained
cold-rolled thin sheets was approximately 0.2 mm.
Microstructural observation was carried out using scan-
ning electron microscopy (SEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD)
and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The speci-
mens in each condition were mechanically abraded on SiC
paper and then electrolytically polished in a mixed solution
of 15 ml H
2SO4 +8 5m lC H3OH at 243 K. TEM foils cut
from the recrystallized specimens were mechanically
thinned to about 0.1 mm and finally jet polished in a mixed
solution of 15 ml H 2SO4 +8 5m lC H3OH at 253 K. TEM
observation was carried out using a JEM-2000FX operating
at 200 kV. Also, XRD was performed to determine consti-
tuent phases and second-phase dispersions, using Ni-fil-
tered Cu-K
a radiation at an accelerated voltage of 30 kV.
Room-temperature tensile testing was conducted in air
using the cold-rolled and fully recrystallized materials. Ten-
sile specimens with gauge dimension of 2 · 1 · 10 mm3
were cut from the thin sheets in each condition using an
EDM. High-temperature tensile testing was also conducted
from room temperature to 973 K in a vacuum using fully re-
crystallized materials. A strain rate of 8.4 · 10 –5 s–1 was
used in both room-temperature and high-temperature tensile
tests. Tensile elongation was calculated from load – elonga-
tion curves drawn in the recorded chart, and defined as plas-
tic strain to fracture. The fracture surfaces of the tensile de-
formed specimens were examined using SEM.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructures
Figure 2 shows SEM microstructures of the quaternary
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys homogenized at 1 325 K for 48 h. The
Al-added Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloy exhibited an L1 2 single-phase
microstructure with a large grain size of several tens of
lm. Therefore, the addition of 2 at.% Al is apparently be-
low the solubility limit in L1 2 phase. On the other hand,
the other quaternary Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys exhibited a two-
phase microstructure consisting of L12 and Al (fcc Ni solid
solution) phases. Consequently, the addition of 2 at.% Cr,
Co and Mo is apparently beyond the solubility limit in L1 2
phase. Bimodal microstructures were observed in the alloys

3.2. Tensile properties at room temperature
Figure 7 shows the nominal stress versus nominal strain
curves for cold-rolled and fully recrystallized Ni3(Si, Ti) al-
loys which were tensile deformed in air at room tempera-
ture. For the cold-rolled state, the quaternary Ni 3(Si, Ti)
alloys including the unalloyed Ni 3(Si, Ti) showed a high
fracture strength (over 2 GPa), although plastic elongation
was little observed. The fracture strength of the quaternary
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys was slightly higher than that of the unal-
loyed Ni3(Si, Ti). For the recrystallized state, the quaternary
elements mostly enhanced not only the tensile strength but
also the yield strength of the Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys. In particular,
the yield strength of the Mo-added Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloy was
much higher than that of the other quaternary Ni3(Si, Ti) al-
loys including the unalloyed Ni3(Si, Ti). The enhanced flow
strength in the quaternary Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys may be attribut-
ed to the fine-grained two-phase microstructure in addition
to the solid solution hardening in Al and/or L1
2 phase.
Furthermore, specifically high flow strength in the Mo-
added Ni3(Si, Ti) alloy may be due to high density of tiny
L12 particles dispersed in Al (fcc Ni solid solution) phase
region. Consequently, it is noted that the presence of A1
phase (the Ni solid solution) is not harmful to the flow
strength of the Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys at room temperature.
Figure 8 shows SEM fractography of cold-rolled and
fully recrystallized Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys which were tensile
deformed in air at room temperature. The Al-added
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloy with an L1 2 single-phase microstructure
and the Cr-added Ni3(Si, Ti) alloy with a two-phase micro-
structure are representatively shown in this figure. The frac-
ture mode at room temperature was not so much different
among the observed quaternary Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys, regard-
less of cold-rolled state or recrystallized state. Ductile
transgranular fracture with dimple patterns prevailed on
their fracture surfaces, regardless of a single-phase or a
two-phase microstructure, and also regardless of cold-
rolled or recrystallized states. However, it is noted that the
depth of the dimples was shallower in the cold-rolled state
than in the recrystallized state.

3.3. Tensile properties at high temperatures
Figure 9 shows changes in yield stress (0.2 % proof stress),
tensile strength, and tensile elongation with temperature
for the quaternary and unalloyed Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys that were
annealed at 1 173 K for 1 h and then deformed at various
temperatures. For all Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys tested, the tensile
strength decreased monotonically with increasing tempera-
ture. On the other hand, the yield stress increased with in-
creasing temperature, reached a maximum around at
773 K and then decreased with further increasing tempera-
ture. However, it should be noted that no tests were done
between 300 K and 773 K, and therefore the peak tempera-
ture is not well defined here. It has been shown that the
Ni
3(Si, Ti) alloys display the positive temperature depen-
dence of strength caused by Kear–Wilsdorf (K–W) locking
[9, 10]. The alloying elements used in this study more or
less enhanced both tensile strength and yield stress in a
wide range of temperatures. Particularly, Co and Mo were
effective in the enhancement of the high-temperature
strength of the Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloy. The yield stress of these
two alloys was beyond 1 GPa even at 873 K. For the Al-
added Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloy with an L1 2 single-phase micro-
structure, the enhanced strength is attributed to the solid so-
lution hardening in the L12 matrix. For the other quaternary
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys with a two-phase microstructure, the en-
hanced strength may be attributed to the two-phase micro-
structure of L1 2/fcc Ni solid solution. However, the situa-
tion in these alloys is quite complicated. Ni/(Si + Ti) ratio
is changed and can be one of the reasons for the strengthen-
ing or weakening. Also some amount of quaternary ele-
ments may exist in L12 phase and also in fcc Ni solid solu-
tion, which is expected to increase the strength of these
phases. After the data on the composition of each phase
were obtained, reliable discussion on this point would be
possible. In contrast, the tensile elongation of the quaterna-
ry Ni
3(Si, Ti) alloys showed a quite different temperature
dependence from that of the unalloyed Ni 3(Si, Ti). While
the tensile elongation of the unalloyed Ni 3(Si, Ti) steadily
decreased from room temperature with increasing tempera-
ture and dropped to 0 % at 973 K, that of the quaternary
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys interestingly remained at high values be-
yond 30 % up to 773 K and then showed a minimum, fol-
lowed by rapid increase at the highest temperature tested
(973 K). The increase in the tensile elongation at the high-
est temperature 973 K has been attributed to dynamic re-
crystallization during high-temperature deformation [7].

3.4. Oxidation properties
Figure 11 shows the mass gain in air as a function of expo-
sure time at 1 173 K for the cold-rolled Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys.
The mass gain of the quaternary Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys was gener-
ally reduced less than a factor 2 from that of the unalloyed
Ni3(Si, Ti). Particularly, the addition of Al and Cr was more
effective in improving the oxidation resistance than that of
Co and Mo. It is presumed that protective surface layers such
as Al2O3 and Cr2O3 may be formed in the Al- and Cr-alloyed
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys, leading to the improved oxidation resis-
tance. Also, there is a possibility that the oxidation resistance
is influenced through the reduction of the Ti content. It has
been reported that the oxidation resistance of Ni 3Si was re-
duced by Ti addition [11]. The Ti content is larger in the un-
alloyed and Co-alloyed Ni 3(Si, Ti) (9.5 at.% Ti) than in the
other quaternary Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys (7.5 or 8.5 at.% Ti), con-
sequently resulting in poor oxidation resistance in the former
alloys. The chracterization of the oxide layer formed on the
surface of the Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys is currently conducted and
will be reported in a separated paper.
4. Conclusions
The effects of alloying elements (Al, Cr, Co and Mo) on
mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of the ther-
momechanically processed Ni
3(Si, Ti) alloy were investi-
gated. The following results were obtained:
1. All quaternary Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys used in this study were
readily cold-rolled to thin sheets with a thickness of
0.2 mm, revealing that these alloying elements do not
spoil the cold-rolling workability of the Ni3(Si, Ti) alloy.
2. The Al-added Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloy consisted of an ordered
L12 single-phase similar to the unalloyed Ni 3(Si, Ti),
while the other quaternary Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys (i. e. the
Cr-, Co- and Mo-added Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloys) contained
fcc Ni solid solution within the L12 grains.
3. The room-temperature strength of the recrystallized
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloy was generally enhanced by the addition
of the quaternary elements while the room-temperature
tensile elongation of the recrystallized Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloy
was slightly reduced by the addition of the quaternary
elements.
4. High-temperature tensile strength was enhanced especially
by the addition of Co and Mo, and attributed to the two-
phase microstructure and/or the solid solution strengthen-
ing in the L1
2 matrix and/or Ni solid solution phase.
5. High-temperature tensile elongation was remarkably in-
creased by the addition of all quaternary elements via
the suppression of the intergranular fracture, and attrib-
uted to the enhanced intergarnular cohesive strength in
the case of Al addition, and/or to the presence of the Ni
solid solution phase in the case of the alloys with a
two-phase microstructure.
6. The oxidation resistance of the Ni 3(Si, Ti) alloy was im-
proved particularly by the addition of Al and Cr.
