Abstract 
W-rich nanoclusters were directly observed using the high angle angular dark 
field-scanning transmission electron microscopy and mass -thickness contrast in a 
W-rich Ni superalloy. These nanoclusters, contributed to the strength with , the solid 
solution and grain boundary strengthening together, were found to uniformly 
distribute within γ, γ′ and phase boundaries.

Introduction 
The mechanical properties of nickel-base directionally solidified superalloys depend 
strongly upon the  precipitates, the solid solution and grain boundary strengthening, 
which, in turn, are controlled by the chemical composition and the processing 
conditions.1-3 The intrinsic strength of pure  precipitate is low at low temperatures 
and increase with temperature up to a maximum at about 700∼750°C. 4 / interfaces 
play an important role due to the considerable expenditure of energy for matrix

## Page 3

dislocations to penetrate  precipitates. The shearing resistance of  can be enhanced 
by alloying optimization. 4 Solid solution strengthening by high melting point 
elements, e.g. Re, W and Mo, is an effective way to provide strong lattice cohesion 
and reduce diffusion particularly at high temperatures. The addition of these atoms to 
the  or  lattice causes the stress fields, which interact with those surrounding 
dislocations. The magnitude of the strengthening effect is a function of the binding 
energy between the solute atom and the dislocation, and of the solute concentration. 
The grain boundaries are reinforced by carbides and borides to increase boundary 
cohesion. The creep rupture strength can be improved by increasing the discontinuous 
carbides. However, properties can be severely degraded, if carbides precipitate as a 
continuous film which provides an easy fracture path. 
Researchers always pursue new strengthening mechanisms to improve the 
mechanical prop erties. The thermodynamically very stable nanosize yttria particles 
and a high number density of nanometer scale titanium -, yttrium - and 
oxygen-enriched nanoclusters derived from yttria can extensively improve the high 
temperature strength of oxide dispers ion strengthened (ODS) alloys.5-8 However, the 
oxide dispersion strengthening cannot be applied in nickel-base directionally 
solidified or single crystal superalloys due to the process ing limit. Furthermore, Re–
rich clusters distributed in the γ matrix have been proposed to increase the resistance 
of dislocations movement and enhance the creep properties at high temperature. 9-10 
However, Mottura et al. 11-12 argued about this, and pointed out that no evidence was 
found to support the hypothesis that Re at oms, as well as the other 5d solutes Ta, W, 
Pt and Ir, were prone to clustering in the Ni–X binary system. 
The element W is a  stabilizer and increases the melting temperature, therefore 
some nickel-base superalloys containing 12~16 wt.% W have been developed, such as 
K441 and Haynes 230. However, these alloys are very sensitive to the composition 
limits and cast process, as the large blocky primary M6C carbides and primary µ 
phase are easily formed to seriously deteriorate the mechanical properties of the alloys. 
13 Hence, the W content is restricted to avoid the formation of these abnormal phases 
when the conventional casting with the limited cooling capability is applied. A higher

## Page 4

cooling efficiency process - Bridgman process using water as the cooling medium is 
applied to result in a general refinement of microstructure. 
In the present paper, the high W-bearing nickel-base directionally solidified 
superalloy has been developed. It is controversial about the existence of clusters in 
superalloys, and this manuscript focused on the W-rich nanoclusters formed in the 
present superalloy, which was directly observed using the high angle angular dark 
field-scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) and 
mass-thickness contrast. These nanoclusters were believed to contribute to the 
strength with  precipitates, the solid solution and grain boundary strengthening 
together. These results may be also helpful to understand other problems concerning 
second phases undergoing phase transformations. 
Experimental procedure 
The nominal compositions are mainly Ni-16Cr-8W-5Al-2Mo-1Nb (wt. % ). The 
alloy w as directionally solidified by high rate solidification (HRS) process in an 
industrial vacuum induction furnace. The withdrawal rate was 5 mm /min. Mold was 
heated by two heat sources, so that the upper temperature was 1500 °C and the lower 
one 1550°C. The alloy with [001] orientation was obtained, and t he size of cylindrical 
specimens was φ15×180 mm. Creep tests and tensile properties tested at temperatures 
ranging from room temperature to 980 °C were carried out according to the GB/T 
2039-2012, GB/T 228.1 -2010 and GB/T 4338 -2006, respectively. Specimens were 
mechanically polished and then etched electrolytically (2V d.c. and 0.2 A) by 210 ml 
H3PO4 + 170 ml H 2SO4 + 120 ml H 2O. Cross -sectional specimens and the tensile 
sample tested at room temperature were then observed using a JSM -6301 scanning 
electron microscope (SEM). Thin slices of material were prepared by mechanical 
polishing and a final twin jet electropolishing using a solution of 5 pct perchloric acid 
in alcohol. Foils were observed by a Tecnai F30 Field Emission Gun transmission 
electron microscope (TEM) with attached energy -dispersive analytical x -ray systems 
(EDS). Foils of the crept specimen were observed by a Tecnai G2 20 TEM.

Results and discussion 
The primary solidification path in nickel -base directionally solidified superalloys 
corresponds to freezing of the  matrix, which constitute s the dendrites. As 
solidification proceeds, the solidified  matrix contains a large amount of  forming 
elements. This process result s in a highly inhomogeneous distribution of chemical 
compositions and microstructures.  subsequently precipitates from the solidified  
matrix.  is a geometric closed packed (GCP) phase and has an ordered FCC structure 
based on Ni3Al. Its growth morphology has been proposed to progress from spheres to 
cubes, to doublet of plates, to octet of cubes and then to thin plates. 14 Fig. 1 shows the 
typical as-cast microstructures of the present alloy. The shape of the γ′ precipitates in 
the as-cast specimens was basically cubic with the average size of about 300 nm. The 
precipitation of  phase from the  matrix belongs to the ordering reaction, which 
proceeds without any composition change or long -range diffusion if solid solution 
elements are not considered . The reaction can be simply expressed as: 
(disordered)→(ordered). The disordered  matrix has a higher internal energy than 
the ordered  precipitates, on account of the greater number of the high -energy 
like-like atom bonds. Hence, the mixed energy parameter is the driving force for this 
ordering reaction. Configuration entropy is the resistance for the ordering reaction. 
The free energy change associated with the nucleation process will have the following 
three contributions: a new interface must be created during the process, which gives a 
interfacial energy (G) increase; the crystal lattice of  phase will not fit perfectly with 
that of  matrix, and this gives rise to a coherent strain energy (
sG ); the precipitation 
leads to the decrease of the chemical free enthalpy (
vG ). Amouyal et al. 15 
investigated the γ/γ′ heterophase interfaces using atom -probe tomography and 
first-principles calculations. Flat {100} interfaces exhibit a confined (nonmonotonic) 
Gibbsian interfacial excess of W, Γ W=1.2±0.2 nm -2, corresponding to a 5 mJ   m-2 
decrease in interfacial free energy. Also, fir st-principles calculations for a Ni –Al–W 
system having a {100} interface indicate a decrease in the interfacial energy of 5 
mJ  m-2 due to W segregation. 
HAADF-STEM has emerged as a key tool for atomic resolution characterization of

## Page 6

materials, allowing the use of Z-contrast imaging mode. The STEM Z-contrast 
technique can provide an incoherent image of crystals at atomic resolution, and the 
intensity of atom columns directly reflects their mean square atomic number. The 
mean square atomic number of γ′ should be less than that of γ without considering 
their solid solution elements. Consequently, the brightness of γ′ should be less than 
that of γ. However, it is not always true as shown in Fig. 1b. The atomic numbers of 
elements of the present alloy follow: Al<Cr<Ni<Mo<W. The partition ratios of W, Mo 
and Cr between γ and γ′ are reported to be 1:0.88, 1:0.33 and 1:0.14 respectively.16 
Furthermore, W addition was found to lead to stronger partitioning of Al to γ′ and Cr 
to γ.17 These are identified by the STEM-EDS element mapping (See Fig. 2). Thus, 
these solid solution elements may have an influence on the brightness. Fortunately, W 
and Mo are excluded when  precipitates from the solidified  matrix. They are 
concentrated in the phase boundary, and this makes  precipitates easily be 
distinguished. This may influence the growth of γ′ precipitates, which is believed to 
be helpful for the refining of them. 
Also, there are many W-rich nanoclusters located at the interface of γ/γ′, as shown in 
Fig. 1b. This indicates that G decreases because of the W segregation to the interfaces, 
and this may be helpful for the precipitation of γ′. This also indicates that the 
precipitation of  precipitates from the  matrix may involve composition changes 
and long-range diffusion. Furthermore, many nanoclusters uniformly distribute within 
the γ matrix and γ′ precipitates. Apparently, these nanoclusters are isolable particles of 
sizes less than 50 nm. These brightest points were identified to be W-rich nanoclusters 
according to STEM-EDS spot analysis results, in which 1 nm probe was used (See 
Fig. 2). The distribution of solute atoms in a solvent lattice is not usually completely 
random. Solute atoms group preferentially at dislocations, stacking faults, low-angle 
boundaries, phase and grain boundaries. However, even in a perfect lattice the atoms 
would not be completely random. For a solid solution of W and Mo atoms within the 
γ matrix and γ′ precipitates, these atoms tend to group themselves preferentially. 
Apparently, the tendency for clustering increases with increasing solute additions. 
Decomposition of the γ matrix and γ′ precipitates supersaturated with W and Mo

## Page 7

atoms may result in creation of an ensemble of nanoclusters. Compared with the 
W-rich nanoclusters in the γ matrix, those in the γ′ precipitates are smaller. This may 
be due to the less W and Mo atoms within the γ′ precipitates. 
Fig. 3 shows tensile properties tested at temperatures ranging from room 
temperature to 980°C and an elongation to fracture in the range from 22 to 45% 
depending on the test temperature. A ductility minimum was observed at about 750°C. 
The fresh fracture surfaces of the tensile sample tested at room temperature were 
studied using the SEM (See Fig. 4). Notable ductile deformation has been observed 
before fracture occurred. The fracture surface was shown in dark gray. The cracks 
propagated along the dendrites. The dimple extended along a certain direction to form 
a shear toughened dimple and a toughened dimple as a result of the shear stress and 
bending stress. The size and depth of the dimple depended on the number distribution 
of the γ′ precipitates and the plastic deformability of the γ matrix. The ductility was 
not deteriorated by the nanoclusters. 
Fig. 5 shows the creep curve of the present alloy and the microstructure of the 
crept specimen. The interaction of electrons with W heavy atoms is stronger than with 
Ni light atoms. The thickness of the TEM specimen is homogeneous, and the W -rich 
areas are darker than the surrounding areas with Ni light atoms. As shown in Fig. 6, 
there exist many darker spots in the γ matrix, which are the mass -thickness contrast 
images of nanoclusters. Apparently, the STEM Z -contrast is better than the 
mass-thickness contrast in this case. Dislocatio n network in the specimen crept at 
980°C/100MPa is complex. Indeed, the interactions of dislocations and nanoclusters 
are rarely observed. Fortunately, one dislocation was found to pass through the 
nanoclusters (See black cycle in Fig. 6). It is clearly shown that dislocations will 
preferably cut across the nanoclusters. This is different from the oxide strengthening 
mechanism in ODS alloys, in which bypass mechanism is dominated. Although the 
hardness of nanoclusters cannot compete with oxides, these nanoclusters can block 
the dislocation movement, and enhance the creep strength. 
 
Conclusions

## Page 8

W-rich nanoclusters were directly observed using HAADF-STEM and 
mass-thickness contrast in the high W -bearing directionally solidified nickel -base 
superalloy. Decomposition of the γ and γ′ supersaturated with W, Mo and Cr atoms 
results in creation of an ensemble of nanoclusters. There are many W -rich 
nanoclusters located at the phase boundary, and this may be helpful for the 
precipitation of γ′. The p recipitation of  phase from the  matrix may involve 
composition changes or long -range diffusion. Dislocations will preferably cut across 
the nanoclusters, and these nanoclusters can block the dislocation movement, and 
enhance the creep strength.
