Introduction Rapidly solidified Al-based alloys with nonequilibrium phases have attracted great interest because of the expec- tation of achieving high mechanical strength with low density. An amorphous single phase with high tensile strength has been formed in rapidly solidified Al-rich al- loys containing less than about 88 at%Al in Al��TM��Ln (TM=IV to VIII transition metals, Ln=lanthanide metal) It has subsequently been that a further increase in Al content to 88 to 90 at% causes the formation of finely mixed fcc-Al and amor- phous phases which exhibit higher tensile strength by about 1.4 times as compared with the corresponding amorphous single phase. More recently, the use of Al�� A1-Cr-Lfi(10), A1-Cr-Ln- and A1-V-TM-Ln(lS) (TM=Fe, Co or Ni) alloys containing the icosahedral-forming ele- ments Mn, Cr and V was found to cause the formation of nanoscale icosahedral particles surrounded by fcc-Al phase with an interparticle spacing of about 10 nm in a rapidly solidified state. The volume fraction of the icosa- hedral phase is as high as 60 to 70% and the tensile strength reaches as high as 1390 MPa even in the high Al concentration range of 92 to 94 at%. Since the nanoscale icosahedral particles have random orientations and are al- ways surrounded by fcc-Al phase, the icosahedral parti- cles have been to be formed as a primary phase from the liquid, followed by the solidification of the Al phase from the remaining liquid. Furthermore, the replacement of Mn and Cr by V was found to cause the formation of a new nanogranular amorphous phase in the presence of the fcc-Al The nanogranu- lar amorphous phase has also been presumed to result from the suppression of the phase transition from the supercooled liquid to nanoscale icosahedral particles. This presumption has also been supported from some that the decomposition of the nanogranular amorphous phase takes place through two stages of amor- phases and the decrease in the rotation speed of a melt spinning wheel causes the transition from amorphous to icosahedral phases. The suppression of the development of the icosahedral phase is presumably because V has a lower diffusivity in more multistage peritectic reactions(20) and higher icosa- hedral-forming tendency(21) as compared with those for Mn and Cr elements in Al. It is known that Ti in Al has a much lower diffusivity than that of and does not have an icosahedral-forming ability(21). Consequently, the examination of the microstructure and mechanical properties of rapidly solidified Al��Ti��Fe alloys and the subsequent comparison with those for the AI-V-Fe al- loys give us a unique opportunity to investigate the roles of the diffusivity and icosahedral-forming ability in the formation of the nanogranular icosahedral base struc- ture. This investigation is expected to cause the clarifica- tion of the mechanism for the formation of the nanogranular amorphous phase which was found for the

Experimental Procedure Ternary Al��Ti��Fe alloys containing more than 85 at%Al were used in the present study. The ingots were prepared by melting a mixture of pure Al (99.99 mass%), Ti (99.8 mass%) and Fe (above 99.9 mass%) metals in an arc-furnace with a purified argon atmosphere. The com- positions are nominally expressed in atomic percent. From the master alloy ingots, ribbons with a width of about 1 mm and a thickness of about 20 um were pre- pared by a single roller melt-spinning technique in an ar- gon atmosphere. The as-quenched and annealed struc- tures of the melt-spun ribbons were examined by X-ray diffractometry and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The TEM samples were prepared by electrolyti- cally thinning the melt-spun ribbons in a solution of 25% nitric acid and 75% methanol at 243 K. The volume frac- tions of fcc-Al and amorphous phases were measured from the TEM images by an image analyzer (PIAS-III). The decomposition behavior of the as-quenched phase was also examined by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a heating rate of 0.67 K/ s. Tensile strength of the ribbon specimens was measured by an Instron-type testing machine operating at a strain rate of 8.3 x 10 4 s-l. Five tensile test data were used to determine the ten- sile fracture strength. Strips having a gauge dimension of 10 mm were cut from the melt-spun ribbon and used for the measurement of tensile fracture strength. Subsequent to tensile testing, the cross-sectional area at the fracture site of each specimen was measured by optical micros- copy and scanning electron microscopy in order to minimize error in the estimation of the tensile strength. On the other hand, the AI-V-Fe alloys consist of nanogranular amorphous phase in co- existence with the network of the Al It has been that the nanogranular amorphous phase is formed as a primary phase from the liquid, fol- lowed by the precipitation of Al phase from the remain- ing liquid. The difference in the solidification process is presumably due to the difference in the local atomic configurations of their liquid phase. That is, the liquid of the AI-V-Fe alloys containing the icosahedral-forming element (V) includes a high density of icosahedral clusters in which the atomic rearrangement on a long range scale during cooling is diffcult. It is therefore thought that the icosahedral cluster region is solidified as the primary amorphous phase. On the other hand, it is thought that the Al��Ti��Fe alloy liquid without icosa- hedral-forming element does not include icosahedral clusters and hence the Al��Al pairs, which exist as a major component in the liquid, precipitate as a primary Al phase. As a result, the solute concentration in the remain- ing liquid increases significantly and the enrichment of the solute elements causes the increase in the amorphous- forming ability, leading to the formation of the network of the amorphous phase. The feature of the solidification process for the Al��Ti��Fe alloys is in agreement with that for the conventional metallic alloys. The fff value is about 1400 MPa for the A194V4Fe2 alloy and about 1200 MPa for the A193Ti5Fe2 alloy. The sig- nificant difference in Of is presumably due to the differ- ence in the grain sizes of the constituent phases. That is, the grain size of the former alloys is about 10 nm for the nanogranular amorphous phase and about 7 nm for the coexistent fcc-Al phase, while the size of the latter alloy is about 20 to 30 nm for the fcc-Al phase. Besides, the volume fraction of the amorphous phase is measured to be 60 to 70% for the nanogranular type and about 30% for the network type. It is therefore said that the lower fff value is due to the larger grain size of the fcc-Al phase and the lower volume fraction of the amorphous phase with high strength and good ductility. The finding that the mixed structure consisting of equiaxed fcc-Al grains embedded in the network of the amorphous phase is found in the rapidly solidified Al-rich Al��Ti��Fe alloys and exhibits high tensile strength combined with good ductility is important for the development of high- strength Al-based alloys. IV. Summary The microstructure and mechanical properties of rap- idly solidified A198-xTixFe2 (x=3 to 10 at%) alloys were examined, with the aim of obtaining a high specific strength combined with good ductility through the forma- tion of a nonequilibrium phase. The results obtained are summarized as follows: (1) The as-quenched structure consists of amor- phous+fcc-Al phases in the concentration range of 3 to 8 at%Ti and an amorphous phase at 10 at%Ti. The mix- ed structure is composed of equiaxed Al grains embed- ded in the network of an amorphous phase. The fcc-Al phase has an average grain size of about 25 nm and the interparticle spacing is about 7 nm. (2) The finely mixed structure appears to be formed through the process of primary fcc-Al phase, followed by the solidification of the amorphous phase with network morphology from the remaining liquid. (3) The crystallization temperature increases from 641 to 701 K with increasing Ti content from 3 to 10 at%. The Tx is higher by about 70 to 130 K than those for Al�� TM-Ln amorphous alloys, presumably because of the much lower diffusivity of Ti. (4) The finely mixed nanogranular Al and amor- phous phase alloys exhibit Of of about 1200 MPa for the A193Ti5Fe2 alloy and the further increase in Ti content causes a significant decrease in Of to 600 MPa because of the lack of good ductility for the amorphous phase con- taining more than 7 at%Ti. The formation of the finely mixed amorphous and Al phases with high tensile strength is important as a fundamental information for the development of a new type of high specific strength material.
