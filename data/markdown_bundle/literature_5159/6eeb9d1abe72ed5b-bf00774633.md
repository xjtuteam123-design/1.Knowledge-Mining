CREEP AND STRESS-RUPTURE STRENGTH OF ROLLED SHEET 
AND WELDED JOINTS OF NIOBIUM ALLOY OF THE SYSTEM Nb- 
W- Mo-Zr AT HIGH TEMPERATURES 
V. A. Borisenko, V. V. Bukhanovskii, and V. K. Kharchenko UDC 539.4.016 
High-temperature strength characteristics of rolled sheet, welded joints, and weld metal of niobium alloy 
5VMTs of the system Nb - W-Mo-Zr at 1520-2020 K and stresses comprising 0.4-1.0 of the material 
ultimate strength are given. It is shown that welded joints and east weld metal under high-temperature 
prolonged loading conditions are the same as the basic metal, and in a number of cases they surpass it 
in high-temperature strength characteristics, Empirical dependences are obtained connecting the 
short-term material strength with its creep resistance characteristics and stress-rupture strength at 
temperatures above the start of dynamic recrystallization. 
Metal structural materials based on niobium are distinguished by high elastic and strength properties which are 
very promising tbr preparing welded articles for critical purposes intended for high-temperature operation. Alloy 5VMTs 
of the Nb-W-Mo-Zr system is one of the most extensively used niobium alloys and it relates to low-alloy niobium with 
solid solution strengthening. Due to the optimum content of alloying elements it conveniently combines high strength 
indices with low-temperature ductility and technological efficiency during welding and machining [I, 2]. In spite of the fact 
that the mechanical properties, creep, and stress-rupture strength of alloy 5VMTs have been studied in detail [3, 4] there 
is almost no published information about the operating capacity of its welded joints under prolonged static loading condi- 
tions at temperatures above 0.5T m. 
Results are presented in this communication for experimental studies of the short-term creep and stress-rupture 
strength of rolled sheet, welded joints, and cast weld metal of alloy 5VMTs grade 5V2MTs-2 at 1523, 1773, and 2023 K. 
Tests were performed in a vacuum not worse than 1.3.10 .2 Pa on flat fivefold proportional specimens cut from rolled 
sheet 1.0 mm thick in the as-supplied condition (annealed at 1673 K for 2 h) and welded joints prepared by tungsten 
electrode arc-welding in a controlled atmosphere of helium at a rate of 2.8 mm/sec [2]. Specimens for determining weld 
metal and welded joint characteristics were cut respectively along and across the welded joints axis. 
In creep and stress-rupture strength tests the level of applied stresses was 0.4-1.0 of the material ultimate strength 
at the corresponding temperature. From the experimental results the creep rate e, the time to specimen failure rf, and the 
plastic strain accumulated at the instant of failure ef were found. In view of a marked increase in the rate of irreversible 
damage accumulation accompanied by unavoidable subsequent specimen failure with loss of stability for material plastic 
flow on creep curves the coordinates of points (e") and (r") were determined specifying the changeover from the second- 
ary creep stage to accelerated creep. The experimental device, the procedure for performing the studies, and statistical 
treatment of the results obtained have been described previously [5-7]. 
In the as-supplied condition rolled sheet of alloy 5VMTs is recrystallized and the material structure is fine-grained 
with equiaxed polyhedral grains. Welded joints have a clearly defined cellular structure typical for niobium alloys with 
solid solution strengthening [2]. The mechanical properties of the basic metal, welded joints, and weld metal obtained with 
active static tension with a strain rate of 2.2.10 .3 sec- ! in the temperature range 290-2270 K are given in Table 1.

The results obtained may be explained from the point of view of differences in physical processes which occur in 
the material with mechanical loading at temperature before and after the start of dynamic recrystallization which is directly 
indicated by the difference by a factor of 1.4-2.0 of numerical values of coefficients c~ and {3 for 1523, and also for 1773 
and 2023 K respectively (Table 2). As noted above, all of the test temperature range is in the region of high homologous 
temperatures (T > 0.5T m) It is assumed that in the case of pure metals strength properties in the high-temperature region 
are controlled by a single leading plastic deformation mechanism. Strain hardening of the material is insignificant, there are 
creep processes, and deformation is accomplished by mutual shifting of grains or sliding over certain crystallographic 
planes and it is accompanied by grain boundary migration, structure formation, and dynamic material recrystallization [i0]. 
However, for alloy 5VMTs containing tungsten, molybdenum, and zirconium, in different areas of the high temperature 
region these physical processes may develop to a greater or lesser extent. A study of structural transformations and plasdc 
deformation mechanisms for the alloy in the range from room temperature to 2270 K carried out by optical and transmis- 
sion electron microscope methods showed that at 1523 K, which corresponds to 0,55T m and is at ,+he start of the high 
temperature region, there are no structural transformations in the material* and plastic deformation occurs primariiy by 
grain-boundary sliding alongside single slips within individual grains. 
At above 1670 K alloy strength is controlled to a considerable extent by polygonization and dynamic 
recrystallization of the material. After mechanical tests in the temperature range t770-2270 K the material has a subgrain 
structure and grain fragmentation into individual blocks is observed. Alloy' plastic deformation at these temperatures is 
realized by uniform single slips. 
Thus, experimental data for the high-temperature strength and mechanical properties of alloy 5VMTs obtained 
respectively for 1573 K and also for 1773 and 2023 K, although the)' pertain to a common high-temperature region but are 
found within different areas of it within whose limits the significance of physical processes and mechanisms are controlled 
by plastic deformation and strength, may differ markedly. In fact, all these data may belong a single general group. 
Therefore statistical treatment of experimental results obtained before and after the temperature for the start of dynamic 
material recrystallization should be performed separately.

Fractographic studies of failed specimens showed that due to the exceptional ductility of alloy' 5VMTs in almost all 
cases for both basic metal and for welded joint metal ductile intragranular failure is observed with formation of a "knife" 
fracture with 100 % transverse reduction of area. Welded joints under prolonged static loading conditions at 1523 and 1773 
K only failed through the basic metal. However, in a number of cases cracks were recorded along the boundaries of 
welded joint crystals. With a temperature of 2023 K welded joints failed primarily through the joint and the fusion zone, 
and more rarely through the basic metal.
