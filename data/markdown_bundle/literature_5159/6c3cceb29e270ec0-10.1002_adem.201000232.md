A Novel Strategy for the Design of Advanced Engineering
Alloys- - -Strengthening Turbine Disk Superalloys via
Twinning Structures**
By Yong Yuan*, Yuefeng Gu, Chuanyong Cui, Toshio Osada, Tadaharu Yokokawa and
Hiroshi Harada
Superalloys provide the backbone for many applications
within key industries, such as chemical and metallurgical
processing, oil and gas extraction and reﬁning, energy
generation, and aerospace propulsion.
[1] By elevating the
service temperature of superalloy, CO 2 emission and fuel
consumption can be reduced due to higher thermal efﬁciency.
Therefore, the advancement of superalloys can contribute
markedly to resolve the critical problems of energy and
environment in the modern society. Main challenges for
advanced superalloy are to improve and to stabilize its
mechanical properties at high temperature.
Disk superalloys used for land-base gas turbine and aero
engines are the typical representative of engineering alloys
and has the highest requirements on mechanical properties
including strength and deformation resistances against
fatigue, fatigue crack growth, and creep. Therefore, main
traditional strengthening methods, including (i) solid solution
hardening; (ii) work hardening; (iii) precipitation strengthen-
ing; (iv) grain reﬁnement, have been considered in the design.
However, concerning the process of wrought superalloys,
the limitations of all these strengthening methods are clear.
First, large addition of alloying refractory elements for solid
solution strengthening may cause segregation during cast and
wrought (C&W) process, and then it will be very difﬁcult to
process and to homogenize the alloy. Secondly, work
hardening is often adopted to strengthen the alloy not used
at high temperature and its strengthening effect at elevated
temperature is limited because the dynamic recovery process
of dislocations is relatively fast. The volume fraction of g
0
phase has great inﬂuence on the mechanical properties.
Normally, the higher the volume fraction alloy has, the better
the properties the alloy may possess. However, an advanced
C&W disk alloy cannot be practically processed if the volume
fraction of g
0 phase is over 50%. Basically, for engineering
structural materials, the strength ( s) and life of low cycle
fatigue (LCF) increase as decreasing the grain size (d), whereas
creep and crack growth resistance increase with increasing
the grain size. In order to optimize the overall mechanical
properties of disk superalloys, the grain size is usually
controlled in the range of /C25 10–50 mm. Therefore, the
tunability of grain size and precipitation strengthening for
the design of new advanced disk superalloys is limited. In a
word, conventional methods have little space for further
improving the mechanical properties of polycrystalline disk
superalloys.
At National Institute for Materials Science (Japan), we
are trying to design a new kind of advanced turbine disk
superalloys, which can be processed by normal C&W,
and have high strength at practical temperature region
(650–750 8C). The designed disk superalloys, named TMW
alloys, contain high content of Co and increased Ti
addition.
[2–8] According to our original designing ideas,
increasing Co content can improve phase stability and lower
g
0 solvus temperature, which widen its processing window
and reduce the stresses induced by controlled cooling and
quenching. Ti is a strong g
0 former and readily substitutes
for Al in g0 phase for anti-phase boundary strengthening.

A novel strategy for designing advanced engineering superalloys using twin structure is presented. By
inducing numerous annealing and deformation twins, a new advanced polycrystalline Ni–Co-base
superalloy (TMW-4M3 alloy) has been developed, which has low stacking fault energy, enhanced
tensile and creep strength without degrading other mechanical properties such as low cycle fatigue and
crack growth resistance. Based on TEM analysis, the twin strengthening mechanism is proposed.

Accurately quantifying the contribution of various
strengthening mechanisms is quite difﬁcult in superalloy
because of its complex microstructure. In TMS-138 alloy
(Ni-based single crystal developed at NIMS), ﬁne interfacial
dislocation networks contribute mainly to creep strength.
[9–11]
In Ni-based single crystal CMSX-3, the relative contributions
of Orowan, solid solution, dislocation, and frictional resis-
tances to creep strength has been roughly estimated to be 72, 8,
5, 15%, respectively.
[12,13] So far, there is no such kind of report
in engineering disk alloy. It is therefore signiﬁcant to identify
various strengthening mechanism in TMW-4M3 and evaluate
their contributions. So we investigated systematically the
microstructures of superalloys TMW-4M3 and U720Li before
and after deformation.
Based on the observations and analysis, we suggest that an
additional strengthening factor, the high density of annealing
and deformation twins, contributes greatly to improved
mechanical properties of TMW-4M3. Our ﬁnding implies a
novel strategy for designing new advanced engineering
polycrystalline superalloys. Here, we illustrate our results
on TMW-4M3 alloy and U720Li.
The nominal compositions of TMW-4M3 and U720Li are
listed in Table 1. The wrought alloys were heat treated at 1
100 8C for 4 h and then aging at 650 8C for 24 h and 760 8C for
16 h.
The overall mechanical behaviors of heat treated U720Li
and TMW-4M3 have been reported.
[2] The tensile tests were
conducted from room temperature (RT) up to 750 8C. The
results show the UTS strength ( sb) of TMW-4M3 is about 10%
higher than that of U720Li in the whole temperature range.
The yield strengths ( s

700–750 8C. Meanwhile, TMW-4M3 also demonstrated great
improvement in creep resistance as mentioned before. It is
worth noting that the LCF life and cyclic fatigue crack growth
rate of TMW-4M3 remain comparative with U720Li. It is clear
that we can get stronger alloy TMW-4M3 without deteriorat-
ing other mechanical properties.

In principle, strengthening the material is to increase the
resistance of dislocation motion because dislocation motion is
the main carrier of plastic deformation. To explain why the
high density annealing twins cause the higher strength, the
interaction between dislocation and TB was investigated.
Figure 2 shows the interaction of dislocation with coherent TB.
In the low magniﬁcation image, dislocations could not
penetrate the annealing twin and the dislocation density
inside the annealing twin was rather low, which means the
coherent TBs impede the dislocation motion. In Figure 2(b), a
lot of stacking faults (SFs) existed in the matrix while SFs in the
annealing twin were sparse. Interaction of dislocations with
TB caused the TB was not ﬂat at atomic scale. Many steps were

How to induce high density of twin including annealing
and deformation twins into alloys? Usually, annealing twin is
produced during materials processing, whereas deformation
twin is formed via deformation during materials service. It
is well known that stacking fault energy (SFE) is a control
parameter to the forming of twin in materials, and the
tendency of twinning is higher in metals and alloys with lower
SFE, and vice versa.
[22–25] Annealing of deformed fcc metals
with low SFE usually leads to the formation of annealing twin
with coherent TB. Deformation twinning is often observed
after plastic strain in the materials with low SFE, e.g., Cu,
Cu–Zn alloys, Cu–Al alloys,
[25,26] where its TB is incoherent.
In order to clarify the relationship between the SFE and the
annealing twin, we investigated the SFEs in U720Li and
TMW-4M3 through HRTEM. Details of the HRTEM analysis
will be presented in another paper under preparation. SFE of
the matrix was determined to be 35.9 /C6 3.7 mJ m
/C0 2 in U720Li
and 19.9 /C6 2.5 mJ m/C0 2 in TMW-4M3. Therefore, the SFE
decreases 44% by changing the compositions. We could not
ﬁnd the simple relationship between the compositions and
SFE in TMW alloys, and believe that the increase of Co content
in TMW-4M3 may mainly correspond to the decrease of the
SFE.
[27–29] More work is needed to ﬁnd a useful method to
decrease SFE and to induce high density of twin into alloys.
Also another beneﬁt factor of strengthening by inducing
twin should be considered. As reported in the references
[15,30] dislocation motion is highly temperature dependent
while the twinning stress is temperature insensitive. The
twinning stress in fcc crystal structure has very low
temperature dependence, either it is positive or negative.
Dislocation slipping and dynamic recovering become easier at
high temperature, which may decrease the contribution of
strengthening techniques associated with the density of
dislocation. However, twinning process may be useful to
increase and to maintain the strengthening effect at high
temperature, owing to the insensitive temperature depen-
dence of twinning stress. This point is very important for
superalloy because it performs at high temperature. More-
over, recent progress in materials science has demonstrated
that nano-scale coherent TB can serve as an important
strengthening mechanism.
[14,31,32] However, the materials
studied in their researches are metals or alloys with grain
size at nano- to submicron scale. The characteristic scale of
material is known to have important inﬂuence on its
performance.
[33] Our results indicate that coarse-grained
engineering alloy with high density of annealing and
deformation twins can also exhibit higher strength without
deteriorating other mechanical properties. It means twin
strengthening may be effective in both nano-crystalline and
conventional coarse-grained materials.
In summary, we present a new strengthening mechanism
in turbine disk superalloys, i.e., twin strengthening. By

Experimental
The detailed process of preparing U720Li and TMW-4M3 alloy
ingots can be found in our previous paper [2]. The rolled plate was
heat treated following the standard procedure (1100 8C/4 h/air
cooling, then aging at 650 8C/24 h/air cooling þ760 8C/16 h/air
cooling), then samples for mechanical tests were machined from
the heat-treated plates. The microstructure after heat treatment was
characterized with SEM. Tensile tests with strain rate of 1 /C2 10
/C0 4 s/C0 1
were conducted from RT to 750 8C. Creep experiments were
performed at 725 8C/630 MPa. After tensile and crept to rupture,
the TEM disks with thickness of around 300 mm were cut from the
tested samples perpendicular to the stress axis. Then the disks were
manually ground down to 50 mm and perforated by twin-jet
electro-polisher at 40 V and 0 8C. The electrolyte consists of 50 mL
perchloric acid, 100 mL ethanol, and 300 mL 2-butoxyethanol. The
conventional TEM microstructures of specimens were investigated
using a JEM 4010 TEM operated at 400 kV, and high resolution
imaging was conducted with a JEM 2100F ﬁeld emission gun TEM
operated at 200 kV.
