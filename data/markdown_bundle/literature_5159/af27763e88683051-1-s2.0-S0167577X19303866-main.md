1. Introduction
Nb–Si-based alloys have shown high potential to replace Ni-
based superalloys due to their high melting point (1750 /C176C) and
low density (6.6–7.2 g/cm 3) as well as attractive speciﬁc mechani-
cal properties at elevated temperatures [1–4]. Due to excellent
strength retention at elevated temperature [5]. Nb-Si based alloys
have been fabricated by a series of processes, e.g. arc melting,
investment casting, directional solidiﬁcation, vapor deposition,
and powder metallurgy methods [6–8].
However, Nb-Si based alloys are still limited by their poor
room-temperature fracture toughness and insufﬁcient high-
temperature oxidation resistance. Improvement in mechanical
and environmental properties will be achieved by alloying with
several elements and the development of advanced processing
and production technologies. One of the advanced powder-based
production processes are selective laser melting and direct energy
deposition additive manufacturing technologies [9].
In previous works [10–13] the different alloys for additive man-
ufacturing by powder metallurgy were obtained, the pre-alloyed
Nb-Si based alloy powders with high sphericity and chemical
homogeneity is still a challenge to achieve. In present work, a syn-
thesis of Nb-Si based alloy powder by mechanical alloying and
plasma spheroidization processes is investigated.
2. Materials and methods
The blended elemental powders of Nb, Ti, Cr, Al and Si (all of
them 99,9% purity) were mechanically alloyed using attritor in
the argon atmosphere for 100, 250, 300 and 350 min. The steel
balls of 10 mm diameter were used as milling media, ball-to-
powder ratio 20:1. After mechanical alloying for 300 min, the
powder was classiﬁed to 45–71
lm and spherodized in the Tekna
TEK-15 plant with ICP plasma torch power 15 kW, feeding rate
30 g/min. The phases were analyzed by XRD Bruker D8 ADVANCE.
The microstructure of samples was characterized using SEM Mira3
Tescan. The chemical elements distribution was measured by EDS
line scan analysis. All of the chemical composition below are given
in atomic%.

When the number of subgrains increases to a certain extent,
the subgrains will transform into grains so that the grains will
be reﬁned. Grain reﬁnement and lattice distortion lead to broad-
ening and reducing the intensity of the diffraction peaks of Nb
[15,16]. Meanwhile, Ti, Cr, Al, and Si enters the lattice interstices
of Nb and form a solid solution, causing the lattice constant of
Nb will increase and the diffraction peak will shift toward the
lower angle.
The microstructures of Nb-25Ti-2Cr-2Al-16Si milled for dif-
ferent times are shown in Fig. 2 . The samples after 100 and
250 min of mechanical alloying shows great inhomogeneity,
layered and even particles of raw powders ( Fig. 2 a, b). How-
ever, after 300 min there is no presence of separated elemental
powder and particles consist of a layered structure of relatively
uniformly distributed contrasting areas. With increasing the
time of mechanical alloying to 350 min, the microstructure
became more homogeneous, and particles morphology is
near-equiaxed.
The result of the EDS line scan analysis ( Fig. 3) conﬁrms the lack
of homogeneity after 100 and 250 min of mechanical alloying, and
more uniform distribution of chemical elements after 300 and
350 min. However, after 300 min, there’s a greatly increased the
amount of Fe, that appears in particles through the contamination
by grinding media. This effect increases with increasing the time of
mechanical alloying. According to the above, the sample after
300 min, with relatively uniform distribution and lesser contami-
nation was selected to further investigation.
The Microstructure of the Nb-25Ti-2Cr-2Al-16Si composite
powder after thermal plasma spheroidization consist of three con-
trasting phases of black, gray, and white ( Fig. 4). Phases containing
heavier elements exhibit brighter contrast in BSE images. Accord-
ing to XRD patterns ( Fig. 5 ) black, gray and white phases are Nb
5-
Si3,N b 3Si, and Nb ss respectively. All of the phases are uniformly
distributed across the powder particle and size less than 1 lm.
However, during the solidiﬁcation process, phases are growing
up to 5 lm at the center of the particle.
4. Conclusions
In the present study, the powder of Nb-based composite with
the chemical composition of Nb-25Ti-2Cr-2Al-16Si was obtained
by mechanical alloying and plasma spheroidization. With the frac-
tion of 45–71 lm, high sphericity and homogeneous phase distri-
bution, this powder can be used for additive manufacturing
technologies such as selective laser melting and direct energy
deposition.
Mechanical alloying leads to the formation of the Nb solid solu-
tion, reducing the intensity and broadening Nb diffraction peaks.
At the 300 min of milling, a uniform distribution of chemical ele-
ments can be obtained. Further increasing of milling time leads
to drastically increase of the Fe contamination from grinding
media.
After plasma spheroidization, the microstructure of the powder
consists of uniformly distributed Nb
5Si3,N b3Si, and Nb ss. The melt-
ing and rapid quenching process during plasma spheroidization
tend to reﬁne the phases and stabilize Nb silicides.
