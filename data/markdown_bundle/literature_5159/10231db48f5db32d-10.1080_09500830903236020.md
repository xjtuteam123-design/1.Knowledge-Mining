Microstructure evolution and its influence on the mechanical properties of
(Ti65.5Fe34.5)100/C0 xNbx (x ¼ 0, 3, 5 and 7) ultrafine composites has been
investigated. The Nb-containing alloys exhibit simultaneously improved
compressive mechanical properties, i.e. high yield (1.9–2.2 GPa) and
fracture (2.2–2.6 GPa) strength together with large plasticity (6–13%),
due to a favorable modulation of their ultrafine eutectic microstructure.
The enhanced mechanical properties are strongly related to the length-
scale, volume fraction and distribution of proeutectic phase as well as to the
refinement and interphase coherency of the alternating ultrafine eutectic
layers.
Keywords: Ti alloys; shear bands; plasticity; deformation; strength;
hypereutectic; pseudo-binary eutectic
1. Introduction
Bimodal bulk nanocrystalline composites with heterogeneities of different length-
scale have received a lot of attention as an effective approach for novel
microstructural design to enhance the plasticity of high-strength nanostructured
materials [1–6]. The enhanced mechanical properties originate from the combination
of multiphases with different size and strain accommodation capability [7–11]. In
particular, nanostructure–dendrite composites and nano–ultrafine eutectic compo-
sites have been highlighted for practical applications due to their easy and simple
processing (casting) [12–17]. Recently, a variety of ferrous–non-ferrous metal-based
bulk nano–ultrafine composites with outstanding mechanical properties have been
developed in multicomponent and simple eutectic systems [18–22]. Among
them, Ti-based alloys are more attractive than other metal-based alloys due to their
low density, high specific strength, good corrosion resistance and excellent

hypereutectic Ti–Fe alloys exhibit relatively better mechanical properties than eutectic

causes embrittlement and decreases ductility. Later, the influence of various
constituent elements has also been studied [32–35]. Addition of Cu and Nb improves
plasticity, whereas small additions of B (boron) enhance the strength. This reveals that
microstructural modification and optimization by additional elements in ultrafine
eutectic alloys are key factors to control the mechanical properties.
Following this approach, we selected the hypereutectic Ti
65.5Fe34.5 alloy as a
starting material and investigated the effect of Nb addition on the microstructure
and the mechanical properties. Nb was selected because it is well known as body
centered cubic (bcc) /C12-Ti stabilizer and has a larger solubility in Ti than Fe [36]. In
this study, we report on a systematic investigation of the microstructure evolution of
the hypereutectic Ti
65.5Fe34.5 alloy by controlled variation of the Nb content ( x ¼ 0,
3, 5 and 7), and its influence on the mechanical properties. With increasing Nb
content, non-localized homogeneous plastic deformation is induced throughout the
whole sample, yielding significantly enhanced macroscopic plasticity.
2. Experimental
A series of (Ti 65.5Fe34.5)100-xNbx alloys with x ¼ 0, 3, 5 and 7 were prepared by arc
melting of the pure elements under an argon atmosphere followed by direct casting
into cylindrical rods with 3 mm diameter and 50 mm length using a suction casting
facility. The microstructure of the cast samples was analyzed by scanning electron
microscopy (SEM, Jeol, JSM-6390, Tokyo, Japan). Phase analysis was done using an
energy dispersive X-ray spectrometer (EDX, Kevex superdry, Forster City, CA, US)
attached to the SEM. X-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku RINT2000, monochromatic
Cu-K/C11radiation, Tokyo, Japan) was performed for structural characterization. The
elastic properties were determined with an Olympus Panametrics-NDT 5900PR
ultrasonic testing device (20 MHz shear wave and 100 MHz longitudinal wave). In
order to evaluate the mechanical properties under compression, cylindrical
specimens with 2:1 aspect ratio were prepared and tested under quasistatic loading
using an initial strain rate of 1 /C2 10
/C0 3 s/C0 1 at room temperature. The lateral and
fracture surface morphologies of deformed samples were investigated by SEM.

4. Discussion
Recent investigations reveal that nano–ultrafine eutectic composites with structural
and spatial heterogeneities exhibit enhanced room temperature strength as well as
plasticity [37,38]. In the case of Ti-based nanostructure–dendrite composites with
high strength and enhanced deformability, the deformation mechanism is strongly
linked to the ultrafine eutectic matrix by nucleation, pinning and multiplication of
shear bands during deformation [39]. In addition, the lattice strain, local rotation
processes and structural short-range ordering also play a significant role in
enhancing the macroscopic plasticity [40–44]. Hence, it is very important to control
the microstructural features of ultrafine eutectic composites.
In this study, detailed microstructural investigations reveal that the addition of
Nb to the hypereutectic Ti
65.5Fe34.5 alloy distinctly changes the size and the volume
fraction of the primary TiFe phase together with the morphology and the lamellar
spacing of the eutectic matrix. Surprisingly, despite the decrease of the volume
fraction of the hard primary TiFe intermetallic compound, the yield stress, the
fracture stress and the plastic deformability considerably increase, as illustrated in
Figure 3 and Table 1. These results can be linked to the increase of the amount of
interphase boundaries in the microstructure. Therefore, the enhanced mechanical
properties are mainly attributed to the synergistic effect of supersaturation of the
/C12-(Ti, Fe, Nb) solid solution phase (solid-solution hardening) and refinement of the
lamellar spacing (Hall–Petch-like effect). Randomly distributed ultrafine laminated
structure (extremely fine eutectic structure) can minimize deformation instabilities
by improving the strain compatibility between the alternating phases. In addition, a
recent report reveals that propensity for strain localization has a strong relationship
with the length-scale of lamellar structure [45]. Based on this understanding, one can
consider that the microstructural characteristics of the constituent phases, such as
their relative amount, as well as their distribution and size, are responsible for the
overall deformation behavior.

On the other hand, the change of the lattice parameter of the eutectic phase
[/C12-(Ti, Fe, Nb)] and their intrinsic elastic properties are also associated with the
improvement of plasticity. Detailed investigations on the deformation mechanism of
Ti–Fe–Sn ultrafine eutectic alloys have suggested that the addition of Sn is effective
to introduce a spatial heterogeneity at the interfacial areas, thus minimizing the
interfacial strain between the /C12-Ti solid solution and the TiFe compound that
constitute the alloy [29,37,44]. In the present study, it is also noted that the addition
of Nb causes a quite large lattice mismatch between the /C12-Ti and TiFe phases, as
revealed by the increase of the /C12-Ti lattice parameter, and simultaneously introduces
a coherency strain as well as minimizes the coherency stress at the interface, which
may be favorable to absorb dislocations from the /C12-(Ti, Fe, Nb) phase. In addition,
strengthening of the soft /C12-Ti layers by solid solution hardening also possibly reduces
excessive stress concentrations at the interface between alternating layers [46,47]. In
other words, it is assumed that well-designed interphase boundary conditions may be
useful to inhibit the propagation and growth of major shear bands, which otherwise
could lead to premature failure along a single shear band [48]. Thus, Nb-bearing
alloys present the improved deformation behavior in terms of branching and
multiplication of principle shear bands, together with a homogeneous distribution of
numerous fine shear bands. At the same time, tuning the elastic constants of Ti–Fe
ultrafine composites by the addition of Nb can also improve the intrinsic plasticity. It
is well known that the more ductile materials tend to have a higher Poisson’s ratio ( /C23)
or lower shear modulus ( G) to bulk modulus ( K) ratio G/K [49,50]. Normally, in case
of Ti alloys, /C12-stabilizers have been widely used to decrease G and Young’s modulus
(E), followed by the electronic softening phenomenon [23,51–54]. In this work,
average elastic constants of ultrafine eutectic composites follow a volume-weighted
average value of the two phases. By increasing the Nb contents from 0 to 7, average
G/K value of alloys continuously decreased from 0.46 to 0.44 and /C23increased from
0.30 to 0.31, respectively. This reveals that the increase in the intrinsic toughness of
Nb-bearing alloys resulted from the decrease of G for /C12-(Ti, Nb, Fe) solid solution
phase. As mentioned above (EDX analysis), the change of G value for FeTi
intermetallic phase is almost negligible due to the limited solubility of Nb. Therefore,
fairly uniform plastic deformation (intense shear banding) occurs rather than shear
localization by the preferred propagation of single shear bands. As a result, proper
selection of additional elements based on the structural point of view is important for
tailoring enhanced microstructural compatibility, including both structure and
length-scale, in ultrafine eutectic composites [55,56]. Accordingly, enhancing the
plastic deformability of high-strength ultrafine eutectic composites can be success-
fully achieved by controlling the length-scale and the intrinsic properties of the
constituent phases, leading to the delocalization of the shear deformation, which
favorably releases the shear stress and accommodates the shear strain.
5. Conclusion
In summary, the addition of Nb to the hypereutectic Ti
65.5Fe34.5 alloy yields
enhanced mechanical properties by the modulation of eutectic microstructure.
