ABSTRACT
Servicing as the blade of aircraft engine, Ni-based superalloys suffer the axis
stress in working. Driven by the creep strain energy, the c0-Ni3Al precipitates
will be rafted via the element diffusion in Ni-based superalloys, and the rafting
of c
0 phase has great effects on the creep properties of the superalloys. By
coupling the sublattice free energy and crystal plasticity model, the phase-ﬁeld
modeling is performed to investigate the evolution kinetics of c
0-Ni3Al phase in
the Ni–15Al–2Mo (at.%) superalloy under creep stress, and the elements
directional diffusions between the c/c
0 phases are revealed. The increase of Al
concentration and the decrease of Mo concentration in the c channel both pro-
mote the directional connection of c0 phase. An initial stage increase in partition
coefﬁcient of Kc0=c
Mo indicates that the fast rafting rate of c0 phase repulses the Mo
to the connection area of c0 phases. The effects of diffusion potential including
elastic potential and chemical potential on the diffusion and rafting of c0 phase
are clariﬁed. The elements directional diffusion between c/c0 phases is driven by
the difference of chemical potential and elastic potential between c phase and c0
phase, and the elastic contribution to the diffusion of Mo is larger than that of Al
and plays a leading role in the rafting of c0 phase.

refractory alloying elements, such as Re, W, Mo and
Ru, can elevate the creep performance of the 4th Ni-
based single crystal superalloys [ 4]. Ai et al. [ 5] found
the Mo in Ni-based single crystal superalloys can
signiﬁcantly increase the atomic partition of the alloy

The stress-induced elements migration in the bi-

the creep resistance [ 14]. Zhang et al. [ 15] indicated
that Mo can increase the density of c/c0 interfacial
dislocation networks in Mo-rich Ni-based single
crystal superalloys, and inﬂuences the elemental
partitioning behavior between c and c

obtaining the superior creep resistances at 1373 K.
Therefore, it is essential to study the effect of the
element migration and partition on the rafting and
precipitation kinetics of the c

superalloys, in order to understand the strengthening
mechanism.

the c/c

study the rafting of c

Results and discussion
Rafting is the main characteristic of morphology
evolution of Ni-based superalloys in the creep pro-
cess. The elements redistribution during the creep
can change the morphology of c
0 phase and inﬂuence
the properties of the alloy. However, the effects of
compositions and stresses on the microscopic mor-
phology of c
0 phase and elemental migration between
c/c0 phases are not clearly studied in Ni–Al–Mo
alloys [ 6].

Conclusion
Phase-ﬁeld modeling coupling with the ordered sub-
lattice free energy and crystal plasticity theory is
developed to study the stress-induced diffusion
behaviors of Mo and Al in c and c
0 phases in Ni –
15Al–2Mo (at.%) superalloy at 1073 K under ten-
sile and compressive stresses, and the inﬂuences of
stress on the elements diffusion during the coarsen-
ing and rafting of c
0 phase are revealed. Conclusions
are achieved as following.
In process of rafting of c0 phase, Al diffuses from c
phase to c0 phase, Mo diffuses from c0 phase to c
phase, the diffusion of Al is the main factor causing
the c0 phase directional connection and rafting, while
the Mo slows down the rafting process because of its
lower atomic diffusion rate. Tensile stress along the x-
axis activates the dislocation slipping in the hori-
zontal c channel to cause the plastic deformation, and
compressive stress produces the high-strain area in
the vertical c channel of Ni–15Al–2Mo (at.%) alloy.
Under the tensile stress, the c
0 phase in Ni–15Al–2Mo
(at.%) alloy coarsens and rafts along the direction of
tensile stress, and perpendicular to the direction of
compressive stress. The effects of diffusion potential
on the stress-induced diffusion and rafting formation
of c
0 phase under stress are clariﬁed. The directional
coarsening of c0 phase is dominated by the elastic
potential, while the diffusion rate of Al and Mo ele-
ments affected by the chemical potential slows down
the process of rafting.
