Abstract: Microstructure evolution and properties of hot-extruded Inconel 625 alloy were investigated at 
different creep temperatures, aging time and strain rates. The experimental results indicate that the Inconel 625 
alloy exhibits an excellent creep resistance at 700 ℃ and below. When the creep temperature rises to 750 ℃, the 
creep resistance falls drastically due to the failure of phase transformation strengthening and the precipitation of 
a large amount of δ phase and σ phase at the grain boundary. The special temperature-sensitive characteristics 
of Inconel 625 alloy play a very important role in its fracture. When the strain rate is 8.33×10
-3
 s -1
, the 
strength of the specimen is higher than that of other parameters attributed to the effect of phase transformation 
strengthening. With the increase of Ni3 (Al, Ti), the phase transformation strengthening inhibits thickening of the 
stacking faults into twins and improves the overall mechanical properties of the alloy. With the increase of the 
aging time, the granular Cr-rich M
23C6 carbides continue to precipitate at the grain boundary, which hinders the 
movement of the dislocations and obviously increases the strength of the samples. Especially, the yield strength 
increases several times.

1 Introduction
Inconel 625 has been used extensively in gas tur -
bine engine and piping systems, nuclear power plant 
and nuclear reactor components, boilers and turbines 
for ultra-supercritical power plants where the envi-
ronments involved a wide variety of creep resistance, 
oxidation, corrosion, and abrasion, typically at elevated 
temperatures
[1,2]
. A superior combination of mechanical 
properties, including creep resistance, oxidation resis-
tance yield strength and fatigue crack growth rate, are 
required at service temperatures
[3]
. At the same time, 
Inconel 625 alloy exists some problems, including nar-
row service temperature range and unstable high tem-
perature microstructure, with it applied under high-tem-
perature environment. When the temperature of service 
environment exceeds a certain range, the mechanical 
properties of materials decrease obviously. Hence, it is 
necessary to enhance the high temperature capability of 
Inconel 625 alloy remains of critical importance. There 
are some researches indicated that the properties of 
Inconel 625 alloy can be enhanced through the heating 
process and hot extrusion
[2,4]
. The average grain size 
increased with the increase of extrusion ratio. Better 
microstructure would be achieved when the extrusion 
speed was controlled between 100 mm/s and 160 mm/
s. With the increase of the real extrusion temperature, 
the M
23C6 carbide and segregation Nb dissolved into 
the matrix to make a stronger solid solution strengthen-
ing and improving the strength, as well as boosting the 
corrosion rate due to the special technique. Therefore, 
compared with Inconel 625, the mechanical and envi-
ronmental properties of hot-extruded Inconel 625 offer 
sufficient benefits for their adoption in critical compo-

In the process of thermoplastic forming, hot-ex-
truded Inconel 625 alloy main features are large de-
formation resistance, narrow deformation temperature 
range and unstable high temperature microstructure. 
The formation of these special features is the result of 
the interaction of the constituent elements of the al-
loy, the internal structure and the thermal processing 
parameters. However, hot-extruded Inconel 625 alloy 
also exhibits a particular sensitivity to temperature 
changes during hot deformation and high temperature 
service. Therefore, by clarifying the mechanism of 
temperature-sensitive characteristics (TSC) of alloys, 
it is possible to broaden the stable plasticity window 
and improve the quality of the thermoplastic forming 
of the alloy, as well as to improve service life and ser -
vice performance of the alloy under high-temperature 
load environment. In the hot working process, the 
alloy strengthening mechanism manifests itself as a 
hindrance to the heat deformation process of the alloy. 
Due to the variety of strengthening mechanisms, the 
alloy structure often interacts with each other, and the 
main strengthening mechanism of the alloy will change 
under different service conditions
[5,6]
. The alloy exhibits 
great mechanical instability in the limited temperature 
range, which relates to strengthening mechanisms
[7]
. 
Therefore, the study of the main strengthening mecha -
nism in the process of thermal deformation has signif-
icance in analyzing the thermal deformation behavior 
and optimizing the thermal processing technology of 
the alloy. 
High temperature performance and thermal 
efficiency of alloy can be used to develop by tradi-
tional strengthening mechanisms such as solid solu-
tion strengthening, grain refinement and dispersion 
strengthening
[8]
. However, as a new strengthening 
mechanism, the “phase transformation strengthening” 
(PTS) can enhance the creep resistance of nickel-based 
superalloys through the phase transition under high 
temperature service conditions
[9,10]
. Similarly, without 
changing the composition, it is also possible to greatly 
improve the overall performance of the material by ad-
justing and controlling different scales of defects on the 
material
[11,12]
. By further studying the precipitation and 
evolution of the phase during hot deformation of the 
extruded Inconel 625 alloy, the nature of the thermal 
deformation characteristics of the alloy and its mecha -
nism of action can be revealed
[13]
. 
In this work, the creep and tensile tests at high 
temperatures were carried out to study the effect of 
phase transition and TSC on the mechanical properties 
of hot-extruded Inconel 625 alloy. The causes of fatigue 
cracks, the strengthening mechanisms and the changes 
of properties of the specimens were also investigated. 
By studying the influence of process parameters on 
the microstructure and properties in the process of hot 
deformation, the optimal processing window was ob-
tained to optimize the thermoplastic forming process of 
the alloy. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS), transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM), and X-ray diffraction (XRD) were 
used to study the microstructures.

3 Results and discussion
The creep curves of Inconel 625 alloy at 650, 700, 
750, and 800 ℃ is shown Fig.2(a). The creep curves 
are full life curves with obvious creep characteristics 
at 750 and 800 ℃. The creep life of the sample de-
creases sharply when the temperature rises from 750 to 
800 ℃. When the creep test was carried out until 312 h 
at 750 ℃, the specimen fractured. However, when 
the temperature rose to 800 ℃, it broke at 228 h. The 
reason for the breakage is that the increasing plastic de-
formation produces micropores and cracks in the grain 
boundaries, and the specimen begins to shrink, result-
ing in decreasing of the actual stress area and increas-
ing of the real stress. Therefore, the plastic deformation 
rate is accelerated, eventually resulting in fracture of 
the specimen
[14,15]
. The creep curves were terminated 
after 400 h at 650 and 700 ℃. Fig.2(c) shows the en-
larged view of the creep curve at 650 and 700 ℃ in 
Fig.2(a). As shown in Fig.2(c), the above two curves 
show the deceleration creep stage and the stable creep 
stage, and do not show the accelerated creep stage. The 
strain does not change substantially with the increase 
of time. The results show that under the creep load of 
235 MPa, the alloy has excellent resistance to creep 
properties at 700 ℃ and below. When entering the sta-
ble creep stage, the deformation of the work hardening, 
recovery and recrystallization occurs simultaneous-
ly, and the sample is not further hardened further, so 
the deformation rate remains essentially constant
[16]
. 
When the temperature rises from 700 to 750 ℃, the 
accelerated creep stage appears. In addition, once the 
creep enters the accelerated creep stage, the strain rate 
of the alloy increases gradually up to fracture
[17]
. It is 
noteworthy that with temperature rising, the strain rate 
increases, eventually leading to a sharp decline in creep 
resistance, which is related to the TSC of the alloy. 
In the creep curve of 800 ℃, there is no deceleration 
creep stage. After a short stable creep stage, it enters 
the accelerated creep stage. The TSC of Inconel 625 
alloy makes great contribution to change the strain rate 
from stable creep stage to the acceleration stage, which 
may be related to the phase transformation of the alloy 
at different temperatures, promoting the further study 
on the mechanism of PTS.

results show that under the creep load of 250 MPa, the 
alloy still has excellent resistance to creep properties at 
720 ℃ and below. By further studying the microstruc -

4 Conclusions
a) The hot-extruded Inconel 625 alloy exhibits an 
excellent creep resistance at 700 ℃ and below. When 
the creep temperature rises to 750 ℃, the creep resis-
tance falls drastically due to the failure of PTS and a 
large amount of δ phase and σ phase precipitating at the 
grain boundaries, eventually leading to the sample frac-
ture.
b) The theory of PTS is suitable for Inconel 625 
alloy. The mechanism of PTS is related to Ni
3 (Al, Ti). 
With the increase of Ni3 (Al, Ti), the η phase continues 
to precipitate along stacking faults in γ’ precipitates, 
which inhibits thickening of the stacking faults into 
twins and significantly improves the creep properties of 
the alloy.
c) The TSC of the hot-extruded Inconel 625 alloy 
are related to the precipitation of the δ phase. As the δ 
phase precipitates, it exhibits great mechanical instabil-
ity in the limited temperature range. When the tempera-
ture rises to 800 ℃, the creep life is greatly shortened, 
and the alloy rapidly enters the accelerated creep stage 
from the stable creep stage.
