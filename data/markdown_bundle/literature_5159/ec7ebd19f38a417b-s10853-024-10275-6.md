ABSTRACT
The evolution kinetics and creep characteristics of the γʹ phase are closely related 
to the mechanical properties of multicomponent Ni-based superalloys. Thus, an 
investigation of the continuous precipitation-to-creep behavior of the γʹ phase is 
needed. By using crystal viscoplastic multicomponent phase-field simulation, the 
effects of Co on the evolution kinetics of the γʹ-(Ni, Co)3(Al, Ta) phase and creep 
rafting are revealed in Ni-12.2Al-xCo-2.5Ta (x = 6, 8, and 10 at.%) multicomponent 
superalloys. Co promotes the precipitation of the γʹ phase with increasing volume 
fraction and particle number, while the particle radius and coarsening rate of the 
γʹ phase are reduced. The high Co content results in a low creep strain and long 
creep life, and the degeneration of the γʹ phase becomes slow in the accelerated 
creep stage. A higher volume fraction of the γʹ phase is maintained, and the stabil-
ity of the γʹ rafts is elevated after creep. Moreover, the contributions of the acti-
vated slip systems to the creep properties are analyzed, and the octahedral slip

Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys are key structural materials 
used in the turbine disks and blades of aircraft engines 
because of their excellent mechanical properties at 
high temperatures, which benefit from the ordered 
γ′ phase embedded in the disordered γ matrix [1 , 2]. 
Studies have shown that the morphology, size, volume 
fraction, and distribution of the γ′ phase significantly 
affect the strength and creep performance of nickel-
based superalloys [3 , 4]. During long-term service at 
high temperatures, the coarsening and creep rafting of 
the γ′ phase significantly reduce the creep properties 
and creep life of nickel-based superalloys. However, 
for multicomponent nickel-based superalloys, the 
continuous precipitation-to-high-temperature creep 
behaviors of the γ′ phase have still not been clearly 
analyzed, but these behaviors are strongly correlated 
with the kinetic evolution and creep properties of 
superalloys.
The morphology of the γ′ phase is reportedly 
related to the lattice misfit between the γ and γ′ phases 
[5, 6] and element partition [7, 8], and the lattice misfit 
can be adjusted by the elemental partition between the 
γ phase and γ′ phase [9 , 10]. Al is the main element 
in nickel-based superalloys, and other elements, such 
as Co, Mo, W, and Ta, can improve the mechanical 
properties of nickel-based superalloys [11, 12]. How-
ever, owing to the limited solubility of the refractory 
elements Mo, W, and Re in the γ matrix, they tend 
to induce harmful topologically close-packed (TCP)

Phase-field simulations are powerful tools for study-
ing the microstructure and creep properties of super-
alloys [27–29]. Ju et al. [30] investigated the evolution 
kinetics of the γ′ phase during creep by coupling the 
sublattice free energy and a crystal plasticity model, 
and revealed the effects of the diffusion potential on the 
diffusion and rafting of the γ′ phase. Mushongera et al. 
[31] investigated the contributions from solute segrega-
tion to rafting in multicomponent Ni-based superalloys, 
they reported that Re accumulation zones can hinder 
the motion of γ/γ′ interfaces and slow rafting. There-
fore, the effects of alloying elements on the morphology 
and creep properties of superalloys can be revealed via 
phase-field simulations, especially for long durations 
and high temperatures with complex stress states.
In this work, we combine the crystal viscoplasticity 
theory with the multicomponent phase-field model to 
study the effects of Co on the γ′ evolution kinetics and 
creep fracture properties. First, the morphology evolu-
tion and precipitation kinetics of the γ′ phase are exam-
ined with increasing Co content, and the volume fraction, 
particle radius, and growth and coarsening rates of the γ′ 
phase are quantitatively clarified. Second, the creep raft 
morphology evolution, creep strain, and contribution of 
slip systems are investigated. In this study, the morphol-
ogy and creep characteristics of multicomponent nickel-
based superalloys are understood in a continuous frame.

Creep morphology and composition variation of the γ′ 
phase
During the creep process, the γ′ phases undergo direc-
tional coarsening and rafting under the combined 
influence of applied stress and lattice misfit stress, 
and these stresses drive the directional diffusion of 
elements to form the rafting morphology. The creep 
deformation of superalloys can be divided into three 
stages [71]. In the primary creep stage, deformation is 
concentrated in the γ phase, and rafting is not obvi -
ous due to the short duration of this stage, while the 
creep strain rate is very high. The second stage is the 
steady-state creep stage, in which the γ and γ′ phases 
form a raft morphology, and the creep strain has a low 
variation rate. In the accelerated creep stage, the intact 
γ′ phase rafts are gradually disrupted, leading to creep 
fracture of the alloy [72, 73].
We use the γ′ morphology in Fig. 1d, i, and n as the 
initial state of creep, and the creep morphology evolu-
tion is shown in Figs. 5 and 6.
In Fig. 5, under a tensile stress of /u1D70Eyy = 150 MPa, the 
γ′ phases form a raft-like morphology perpendicular 
to the stress direction, i.e., N-type rafting. At differ-
ent creep stages, the evolution of creep morpholo-
gies varies greatly for different Co contents. The first 
stage is not observed in the creep strain curves (Fig. 8). 
Liang and Gu et al. [74–76] investigated the creep of 
nickel-based, single-crystal superalloys and reported 
that only the steady-state and accelerated creep stages 
occurred in the creep strain curves.
In steady-state creep (t* < 20), the γ′ phase coalesces 
and rafts, as shown in Fig. 5b, g, and l. In the acceler-
ated creep stages (t* > 20), the raft structure gradually 
breaks down and dissolves obviously in the 6Co and 
8Co alloys, whereas in the 10Co alloy, only slightly γ′ 
rafts dissolve.
Generally, during the accelerated creep stage, 
mobile dislocations accumulate at the γ/γ′ phase inter-
face, forming dislocation entanglements and leading 
to stress concentrations. When the concentrated stress 
is greater than the strength of the γ phase, the inter-
facial dislocation network is destroyed. The disloca-
tion network lost its coordinating role in maintaining 
dynamic equilibrium, and the dislocations cut into the 
γ′ phase along the collapse site of the dislocation net-
work. After being cut by dislocations, the resistance to 
deformation of the γ′ phase is weakened, and the plas-
tic strain of the alloy increases, leading to fracture [26, 
77]. As shown in Fig. 5d and i, raft fracture eventually 
causes alloy failure. Therefore, superalloys with high 
Co contents help retain a complete raft morphology 
for long-term creep, as shown in Fig. 5k–n.

Compared with the tensile stress, the γ′ phase dis-
solves faster under compressive stress, and less of the 
γ′ phase remains after creep, which induces a large 
creep strain /u1D700pl (Fig. 8). This is because of the tension/
compression asymmetry in the yield strength and 
creep strength of Ni-based superalloys [ 82, 83], and 
the compressive creep strength of single-crystal super-
alloys is lower than the tensile creep strength. Owing 
to the high tensile residual stress produced by the 
greater stress relaxation during compressive loading, 
the high temperature and thermomechanical fatigue 
(TMF) strength of the single-crystal superalloy can be 
reduced [84, 85].
Average shear strain and effective plastic strain
On the basis of the characteristic shear stress /u1D70Fs of 
the activated slip systems, the average shear strain /u1D6FEs 
on each slip system is classified into S1–S12 [45]. For 
/u1D70Eyy =± 150 MPa, /u1D6FEs can be divided into three groups 
represented by slip systems S1, S2, and S8, as shown 
in Fig. 10a and b.
Slip system S8 under tensile stress and slip system 
S1 under compressive stress provide the largest contri-
butions to the creep strain, whereas S2 has the small-
est contribution to both the tensile and compressive 
stresses. The activated slip systems are different under 
the applied stress, and the shear deformation on the 
same slip system also varies, which results in com -
plicated deformation during the creep of superalloys. 
Therefore, this section explores the evolution of alloy 
rafting and creep performance from the perspective of 
microscale plastic deformation mechanisms.
Figure 11 shows the evolution of the effective plas-
tic strain /u1D700pl in the Ni-12.2Al-10Co-2.5Ta at.% alloy in 
the creep state, where the yellow regions represent the 
plastic strain generated in the γ channels and the blue 
regions indicate the small plastic strain in the γ′ rafts.
/u1D700pl accumulates significantly in the γ channels, 
and the plastic strain in the γ′ phase remains at a low 
level. We observed that the high strain concentration 
area is mainly in the matrix channel, and a high strain 
concentration area also exists at the γ ′/γ phase inter-
face but is not as severe as the matrix channel [49, 78], 
whereas the maximum value is greater in the compres-
sive stress state.
