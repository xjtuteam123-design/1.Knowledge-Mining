ABSTRACT 
Ti alloy clad plate is of great interest for marine and petrochemical industry applications. However, high 
interfacial bonding is hardly obtained in the Ti alloy clad plate due to the brittle intermetallic compounds (IMCs) 
forming at the interface. In this study, a Ti-6Al-4V alloy/EH690 steel clad plate was fabricated by vacuum rolling 
and subsequently tempered to control the mechanical property of the steel substrate. The tempered sorbite 
appeared in the EH690 steel after quenching and tempering (QT). A decarburized zone, Fe
2
Ti + TiC mixed 
phases layer, and β -Ti layer were formed at the interface between steel and Ti. The brittle IMCs with high lattice 
mismatch resulted in a low interfacial strength of 108 MPa. To improve bonding strength, Nb was inserted 
between Ti and steel as an interlayer, which effectively inhibited the inter-diffusion of Ti, Fe, and C without 
brittle TiC or Fe-Ti IMCs. A (Ti, Nb) solid solution layer with high plasticity was present at the Nb/Ti interface. 
Fe
2
Nb and Fe
7
Nb
6 
phases were detected at the steel/Nb interface, exhibiting less brittleness and hardness 
compared to Fe-Ti IMCs. The interfacial shear strength was up to 265 MPa after adding the Nb interlayer, 
showing a mixed fracture mode of brittleness and ductility. 
1. Introduction 
EH690 steel is a leading high-strength QT steel and has been widely 
used in ocean engineering because of its excellent properties and 
weldability. However, high-strength steels are easily subjected to 
corrosion fatigue fracture caused in marine environments due to the 
coupling effect of severe corrosion environment and stress [ 1 ]. Ti/steel 
clad plates exhibit excellent corrosion resistance, weldability, form -
ability, and cost-effectiveness, making them widely used in the petro -
chemical industry, nuclear power equipment, aerospace, and marine 
industry [ 2 ]. 
Currently, explosive welding and rolling cladding have been used to 
manufacture Ti/steel clad plates [ 3 – 6 ]. However, the shock wave and 
noise generated during explosive welding will adversely affect the sur -
rounding environment. In comparison, the rolling cladding method of -
fers advantages such as high production efficiency, environmental 
friendliness, and low energy consumption, making it the mainstream 
production method for Ti/steel clad plates. In particular, vacuum rolling 
can achieve more desirable interfacial bonding performance due to its 
higher level of vacuum environment at the interface [ 7 ]. Additionally, 
brazing, as a commonly used metal bonding method, has been 
extensively studied [ 8 – 10 ]. Nonetheless, compared to rolling cladding, 
brazing involves higher temperatures and may lead to the formation of 
thick IMCs layers at the interface, thereby resulting in reduced bonding 
strength. 
Some brittle IMCs of TiC, FeTi, and Fe
2
Ti usually produce at the Ti/ 
steel interface, which can dramatically reduce bonding strength. Various 
efforts have been made to control the formation of interfacial products. 
The appropriate heating and heat treatment processes are beneficial to 
obtain a high-strength interface during rolling cladding [ 11 – 13 ]. In 
addition, the carbon content in steel and the orientation of Ti can also 
influence the interfacial products [ 14 , 15 ]. However, the complete 
elimination of these IMCs remains challenging. Adding an interlayer has 
been recognized as an effective method to inhibit the formation of TiC 
and Fe-Ti IMCs at the Ti/steel interface. Some scholars have discovered 
that adding V, Ni, Nb, pure iron, and Mo at the interface of the clad plate 
can effectively inhibit the generation of excessive brittle phases [ 16 – 19 ]. 
Xie et al. [ 20 ] used the Nb interlayer to obtain 279 MPa shear strength 
for Ti/steel clad plates at a heating temperature of 800 
o
C. Zhao et al. 
[ 21 ] found that the Fe interlayer effectively reduced the amount of TiC 
at the interlayer, achieving an average shear strength of 256.3 MPa. 
Above studies mainly focused on pure Ti/steel clad plates, while limited

2. Experimental procedure 
The hot rolled and annealed Ti-6Al-4V alloy with the size of 100 ×
80 × 10 mm, the hot rolled EH690 QT steel with the size of 140 ×
100 × 25 mm, and a 0.1 mm thick Nb foil (99.99 at. %) were adopted. 
Table 1 shows the chemical compositions of EH690 marine steel and Ti- 
6Al-4V alloy. 
The vacuum rolling method is shown in Fig. 1 . Firstly, the Ti alloy, 
steel, and Nb interlayer were cleaned by mechanical polishing. Second, a 
pure Nb foil was placed between the Ti alloy and steel, and then the 
built-up blank was packed by an electron beam at a vacuum level of 5 ×
10

Pa. Subsequently, the welded blank was heated at 865 
o

and rolled in 8 passes. The built-up blank was packed at a rolling speed 
of 1 m/s. After rolling, the sample was water-cooled to ambient tem -
perature and subsequently tempered at 660 
◦
C for 1 h. Finally, the 
sample was air-cooled to ambient temperature. 
The microstructure samples were cut from the center of the clad plate 
along the rolling direction. The microstructure of the clad interface was 
characterized by an optical microscope (OM, OLYMPUS-DSX 500). The 
interface morphology and element diffusion were observed by a field 
emission electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA, JEOLJXA-8530F) 
equipped with a wavelength dispersive spectroscope (WDS). The scan -
ning electron microscope (SEM, ZEISS, Crossbeam 550) equipped with 
electron-backscatter diffraction (EBSD) was used for SEM and EBSD 
characterization. The microstructure of the clad interface was analyzed 
using a transmission electron microscope (TEM) and scanning TEM 
(STEM), performed with a Talos F200S G2 microscope. The TEM spec -
imen at the Ti alloy/steel interface was prepared by a focused ion beam 
(FIB, TESCAN, LYRA3 GMH). The morphology and phase composition of 
the fractured surface was analyzed using a scanning electron microscope 
(SEM, Ultra-55) and X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Smart Lab). The di -
mensions of the tensile shear test specimens and a schematic diagram of 
the tensile shear test are shown in Fig. 2 a and b, respectively. The shear 
test was carried out with a GOTECH AI7000 testing machine at a shear 
rate of 0.5 mm/min. The interfacial bonding strength ( σ ) is determined 
using the equation σ = F/S, where F is the maximum force required to 
separate the interface, and S is the bonding area at the notch location of 
the shear specimen. Three specimens per condition were tested, and the 
results were averaged for analysis. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructures and mechanical properties of Ti alloy/steel clad 
interface 
Fig. 3 shows the microstructure of the Ti alloy/steel clad interface. 
The process of hot rolling promotes the thermal diffusion of atoms, 
breaks the surface oxides, and establishes a continuous and straight clad 
interface, exhibiting excellent metallurgical bonding without any visible 
defects. After online quenching and high-temperature tempering, a 
decarburized layer of about 8 μ m was formed in the steel close to the 
interface, while the microstructure at the far interface was tempered 
sorbite. In addition, most of the grains in the steel were fine equiaxed 
recrystallized grains, and rolling strips were observed. In the rolling 
process of microalloyed steel, the appearance of a strip structure is 
almost inevitable, and it is difficult to eliminate it by heat treatment 
[ 23 ]. The Ti alloy demonstrated a bimodal structure, with elongated Ti 
alloy grains along the rolling direction and β -Ti distributed along the 
α -Ti grain boundaries. Luo et al. [ 24 ] have noted that the dynamic 
recrystallization and phase transformation do not occur in Ti-6Al-4V 
alloy until the hot rolling is conducted at a temperature higher than 
900 
◦
C. Additionally, in this study, the finish rolling temperature of 830 
o
C and tempering temperature of 660 
o
C did not reach the static 
recrystallization temperature of the Ti-6Al-4V alloy (850 ~ 950 
◦

Therefore, the grains in Ti alloy did not exhibit obvious recrystallization 
characteristics after undergoing multi-pass rolling deformation and heat 
treatment. 
Fig. 4 shows the SEM of the Ti alloy/steel interface and the corre -
sponding WDS line scan curves. Due to the concentration gradient of Fe, 
C, and Ti between the Ti-6Al-4V alloy and EH690 steel, the driving force 
for interdiffusion was provided, resulting in severe diffusion at the clad 
interface during rolling. The width of the diffusion layer between Fe and 
Ti was 2.7 μ m, with a diffusion distance of 1.8 μ m for Fe in the Ti alloy 
and 0.9 μ m for Ti in the steel. The difference is own to the higher 
diffusion activation energy of Ti in Fe (293.2 kJ/mol) than that of Fe in 
Ti (144.2 kJ/mol) [ 25 ]. In addition, the C element was enriched at the 
interface, which was closely related to the decarburization layer. 
Fig. 5 illustrates the EPMA maps of the Fe, Ti, C, Al, and V elements in 
the clad plate. On the Ti alloy side, Al and V are stable elements in α -Ti 
and β -Ti phases, respectively, resulting in their segregation within the 
α -Ti and β -Ti phases observed in the EPMA analysis. Moreover, an 
obvious diffusion layer of Fe, Ti, and C was found at the interface. The 
diffusion coefficients play a significant role in determining the extent of

3.2. Microstructures and mechanical strength properties of Ti alloy/steel 
clad interface with an Nb interlayer 
Fig. 11 shows the Ti alloy/steel clad interface after adding an Nb 
interlayer, where both the steel/Nb and Nb/Ti interfaces exhibit no 
notable defects. In comparison to the clad plate without an Nb inter -
layer, the microstructure of steel showed no obvious decarburization. 
Furthermore, an uneven interface on the Nb/Ti side was observed, while 
the steel/Nb interface appeared relatively flat. This is attributed to the 
significant difference in high-temperature deformation resistance be -
tween Nb and Ti-6Al-4V during the rolling process, which led to a 
disparity in the metal flow between Nb and Ti-6Al-4V. The easily 
deformable metal layer (Nb) caused the hard deformable metal layer 
(Ti-6Al-4V) to flow and deform together. As the deformation increased, 
the difference in flow speeds between the two metals enlarged, leading 
to the unevenness of the interface [ 48 ]. 
Fig. 12 shows the SEM of Ti alloy/steel clad interface with an Nb 
interlayer and the corresponding WDS line scan curves. It can be 
observed that the thickness of Nb interlayer was about 15 μ m, the 
diffusion distance between Ti and Nb was 2.7 μ m, and the distance be -
tween Nb and Fe was 1.9 μ m. The Nb element separated the contact 
between Fe and Ti elements, and no noticeable elemental aggregation 
was found in the diffusion layer on both sides of the interface, which 
indicated that the formation of IMCs was effectively inhibited. 
Fig. 13 shows the EPMA maps of the Fe, Ti, C, Al, V and Nb elements 
at the Ti alloy/steel clad interface with an Nb interlayer. After adding 
the Nb interlayer, the C element was blocked on the steel side. Although 
the interdiffusion of Ti, Fe, and C between the matrix and the cladding 
was inhibited by adding the Nb interlayer, Fe-Nb IMCs and (Ti, Nb) solid 
solution may form at the steel/Nb and Nb/Ti interfaces, respectively.

According to XRD analysis and the morphology of the fracture, it can be 
concluded that brittle fracture occurred in the Fe-Nb IMCs, and cracks 
mainly nucleated at the interface of the Fe-Nb IMCs and the steel matrix 
and expanded along the inner side of the Nb interlayer. 
It is generally believed that the plastic deformation of crystals de -
pends mainly on the movement of dislocations, and the easier the 
movement of dislocations, the better the ductility of crystals [ 59 ]. For 
the Ti alloy/steel clad plate with an Nb interlayer, the (Ti, Nb) solid 
solution formed at the Nb/Ti interface possesses high ductility. This can 
be attributed to the non-directional bonding between atoms in the solid 
solution, allowing dislocations to slide freely on closely packed crystal 
planes. On the other hand, at the steel/Nb interface, the Fe-Nb IMCs 
formed exhibit strong directional bonding, leading to a significant 
reduction in the rate of dislocation migration and hindering dislocation 
movement, thereby decreasing ductility [ 60 ]. Hence, the Nb/Ti side 
exhibited better plastic deformation capabilities compared to the 
steel/Nb side, similar to the findings drawn by Kundu S et al. [ 61 ] in 
their work on the fabrication of P-Ti/304 clad plates using an Nb 
interlayer. In addition, Li et al. [ 39 ] compared the hardness of Ti/SS and 
Ti/Nb/SS weld joints at different exposure temperatures and found that 
the Ti/SS weld joint hardened due to the appearance of Fe-Ti IMCs. 
However, the hardness did not significantly change when Fe-Nb IMCs 
appeared in the Nb/SS weld joint. This suggests that the impact of Fe-Nb 
IMCs on the interface performance is lower than that of Fe-Ti IMCs. 
Therefore, the addition of an Nb interlayer effectively prevented the 
formation of highly brittle TiC and Fe-Ti IMCs, resulting in a Ti 
alloy/steel clad plate with high shear strength.
