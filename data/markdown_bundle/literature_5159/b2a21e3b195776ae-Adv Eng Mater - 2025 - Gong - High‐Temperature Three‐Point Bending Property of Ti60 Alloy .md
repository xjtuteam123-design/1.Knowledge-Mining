1. Introduction
In recent years, with the rapid development of the aviation ﬁeld,
people have higher and higher requirements for the property of
aircraft.[1] As the core part of aviation equipment, aeroengine
determines the ﬂight quality and safety of aircraft. The service
environment of components serving in aeroengines is very
harsh.[2] Components are usually affected by many factors such
as temperature and load. The bending property seriously affects
the ﬂight safety of the aircraft. [3]
Ti alloy serves as the primary material
for aeroengines owing to its excellent
high temperature resistance and speci ﬁc
strength. At present, some researchers have
studied its bending property. Kikuchi et al.[4]
carried out four-point bending fatigue tests
on Ti-6Al-4 V alloy with bimodal harmonic
structure. It is found that the ﬁne grain
structure will lead to the decrease of bend-
ing fatigue life under low cycle state.
The research emphasized the relationship
between microstructure and fatigue life.
Srivatsan et al.[5] studied the fracture behav-
ior of Ti-6242 alloy under bending fatigue
deformation. It is believed that the main
mechanism for the rapid growth of macro-
cracks under large loads is the tensile, while
the ﬁne microcracks are carried out through
the synergy of shear mode and tensile
mode. This research contributes to our
understanding of crack propagation mecha-
nisms in Ti alloys under fatigue loading. Hu et al. [6] studied the
relationship between microstructure and impact toughness of
Ti80 alloy. Through the study of crack de ﬂection behavior, it is
considered that equiaxed αp and disordered lamellar structure
have excellent crack deﬂection ability, which makes the crack path
tortuous and the energy dissipation high. Its research reveals the
relationship between microstructure and impact toughness.

The components used in aeroengines are usually affected by both temperature and
load. The high-temperature bending fracture caused by the combined action of high
temperature and load is a huge hidden danger. In this article, the high-temperature
three-point bending test of Ti60 alloy rolled sheet is carried out. In order to improve
the bending property of Ti60 alloy, the a uthors carry out vacuum solution aging
treatment. The microstructure and crack propagation behavior are observed, and the
bending property is studied. The results show that the bending strength σbb of the
rolled sheet treated by solution aging treatment are increased. This is because when
the crack propagates in lamellar structure, it needs to constantly change its direction
to bypass the lamellar structure. This will consume more energy. The original rolled
sheet has a strong {0001}<10-10> texture with a texture strength of 9.08, and the
rolled sheet treated by vacuum solution aging treatment has a strong {11-20}
<0001> texture with a texture strength of 45.59. Although they are both the hard
orientations, the texture intensity of {11-20}<0001> is much higher, which con-
tributed to its high bending strength σbb.

The research provided valuable insights into the microscopic
damage mechanism of bending fatigue and helped to develop
strategies to improve the fatigue resistance of titanium alloys.
However, the current research mainly focuses on bending
fatigue and damage failure. High-temperature bending fracture,
induced by the coupled effects of high temperature and load
mechanical loading, poses a signi ﬁcant safety risk. The above
studies have not taken into account the high-temperature and
load coupling effects unique to aeroengine components.
Zhao et al. [8] studied the tensile-bending composite test of
Ti-6Al-4 V alloy under different microstructures. It is found that
the pr-stretching load can improve the energy dissipation
capacity of the bimodal structure so that the maximum bending
load increases with the increase of the prestretching ratio. Zhao
et al. [3] also studied the elastic – plastic deformation behavior of
Ti-6Al-4 V alloy under the combined action of high temperature
and tension-bending coupling. It is found that the maximum
bending load and dissipated energy at high temperature are
signiﬁcantly higher than the maximum bending load and dissi-
pated energy at room temperature, because the deformation mode
changes from bending to stretching. These studies are among the
few that consider the high temperature factor, but they are still
limited to loading conditions. Zhao et al. [9] studied the bending
deformation behavior of Ti65 alloy sheet. It is considered that
the α-Ti phase grains will rotate into transverse texture grains
under the in ﬂuence of bending shear stress, which leads to the
formation of transverse texture α-Ti phase macrozones of
unidirectionally rolled Ti65 alloy sheet. The Ti65 alloy sheet
prepared by multidirectional rolling will exhibit a transverse
basal α phase texture, which can improve its bending property.
This research provides insights into the relationship between
processing methods, microstructure evolution, and bending
property, but it does not directly address the high-temperature
bending behavior required for aeroengine applications.
In summary, previous studies have laid a solid foundation for
understanding the bending property of Ti alloys. However, the
research on high temperature bending property is still far from
perfect, especially for newly developed alloys such as Ti60. As a
new near-α-type alloy developed for the aerospace ﬁeld, Ti60 alloy
is expected to be widely used in the future, [10] but the bending
property of Ti60 alloy is still unclear. In view of the above
research, high-temperature bending tests were conducted on
the Ti60 alloy rolled sheets. Vacuum solution aging treatment
was carried out on the original rolled materials to enhance their
high-temperature bending property. [11] The parameters of the
solution aging treatment were selected based on the phase
transformation point of the Ti60 alloy and relevant literature. [12]
It was found that compared with the original rolled sheet, the
bending property of Ti60 alloy rolled sheet treated by vacuum
solution aging treatment was obviously improved.
2. Experimental Section
In the test, 2-mm-thick rolled Ti60 alloy sheets were utilized.
The material underwent rolling at 1000 °C via the multidirectional
rolling approach for eight passes. After each pass, it was replaced
into the furnace for heat retention. Eventually, annealing at 600 °C
for 4 h yielded the as-rolled Ti60 alloy. The phase transformation
point of the rolled Ti60 alloy was 1050 °C. Subsequently, the
as-rolled sheets were placed in a glass tube under vacuum.
Solution treatment was carried out at 1090 °C for 2 h, followed
by aging treatment at 700 °C for 2 h. Their heating rate is
10 °C min/C0 1. Both treatments were air-cooled to room tempera-
ture (20 °C) at cooling rates of 4.3 and 2.7 °C s /C0 1, respectively.
Ti60 alloy rolling sheet before and after vacuum solution aging
treatment were processed into the samples shown in Figure 1 by
wire cutting method. The notch part was an equilateral triangle
with a height of 2 mm. The GTS-Instron test machine was used
to carry out the three-point bending test of the unnotched samples
and the notched samples. The test temperature was 600 °C, the
loading speed was set to 0.5 mm min/C0 1, and the loading direction
was perpendicular to rolling direction (RD) as shown in Figure 1.
The microstructure of the rolled sheet before and after
vacuum solution aging treatment were observed by scanning
electron microscope (SEM). The crack propagation behavior
was observed by SEM. The curve was integrated by Origin
software to calculate the energy dissipated by the fracture of
the sample. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) was used
to observe the microstructure of the rolled sheet before and after
vacuum solution aging treatment. The AZtecCrystal software
was used to calculate the area of the grains and analyze the
texture orientation. During the EBSD scanning process, the step
size was set to 0.8 μm, and X0 was parallel to RD and perpendic-
ular to the loading direction. The SEM samples were prepared by
standard metallographic preparation method. EBSD samples
need to be electropolished treated after standard metallographic
treatment. The polishing solution was 60 vol% methanol,
35 vol% n-butanol, and 5 vol% perchloric acid. The polishing
temperature was /C0 18 °C, and the polishing time was 45 s.

3.2. Unnotched Sample Bending Test
Bending property is an important factor to measure the safety
and reliability of components. Especially for materials serving
in aeroengines, they may be affected by high temperature in
addition to loads during service, resulting in a variety of failure
modes.[3] Therefore, the authors carried out the three-point
bending test at 600 °C. The schematic diagram of the three-point
bending test is shown in Figure 3. The results of maximum force
Fmax, fracture de ﬂection Fbb and compressive strength σbc are
shown in Table 1 . The maximum force intuitively re ﬂects the
maximum load that the material can withstand in the case of
three-point bending. In practical engineering, it can be judged
whether the material can meet the bearing requirements of
speciﬁc structures or components. The maximum force Fmax
increased from 2.097 and 2.110 kN to 2.434 and 2.459 kN treated
by vacuum solution aging treatment. The compressive strength
reﬂects the ability of the material to resist damage under
pressure. When designing structures that withstand the combined
effects of pressure and bending, the compressive strength is often
used to determine the size and shape of the structure to ensure
that the structure works within a safe range. The compressive
strength σbc increased from 0.167 and 0.168 kN mm /C0 2 to 0.194
and 0.196 kN mm/C0 2 treated by vacuum solution aging treatment.
The above results show that the bending property of the rolled
sheet treated by vacuum solution aging treatment is generally
better than that of the original rolled sheet.

Observing the microstructure of the crack propagation path

strength of the material. [14,15] Figure 5 a– d shows the overall
morphology of the cracks and the locally enlarged SEM images

this test, it can be seen from the measurement results of
Figure 5a that the fracture path and the normal stress direction
are 35.31°, which is due to the shear fracture mode caused by the

This result is the same as that of Zhao. [3,8] It can be seen from
the microstructure of Figure 5b,c that the grains of the original
rolled sheet are extremely ﬁne, which is a typical equiaxed ﬁne
grain structure. Only a small number of tearing ridges appear in
Figure 5b. Figure 5b,c is the ﬁrst half of the crack, the crack is
straight. Only in Figure 5d, that is, the tip of the crack, there is a
tiny deﬂection. There are many holes near the tip of the crack in
Figure 5d; it shows that the tip of the crack propagation belongs
to the microvoid aggregation fracture. During the bending
deformation process, the movement of dislocations causes the
material around the holes to continuously undergo plastic
deformation, providing a material ﬂow for the growth of holes.
Meanwhile, as the deformation continues, the material between
adjacent holes is continuously stretched. When it reaches a
certain extent, the material between the holes fractures, enabling
the holes to connect and aggregate with each other. Eventually, a
macroscopic crack is formed, leading to the fracture of the
material.

The lamellar structure of Ti60 alloy is composed of lamellar
colony with different orientations, and there is an interface
between the lamellar colony. When the crack propagation
encounters the lamellar interface, the crack propagation
direction will be hindered and changed due to the different
crystal orientations of different lamellae. In order to continue
to expand, the crack needs to consume additional energy to
bypass or pass through the lamellae, which makes the crack
propagation path become tortuous. [12] For example, the crack
may de ﬂect along the lamellar interface, or temporarily stop
expanding when encountering the lamellae whose orientation
is not conducive to expansion, and then break through the
lamellae to continue to move forward after accumulating suf ﬁ-
cient energy. The change of this path is one of the important
reasons for the formation of tearing ridges. Because the crack
propagation in different directions is blocked, the high and
low undulating tearing ridges will be formed on the crack
propagation surface, as shown in the Figure 6f,g. The greater
the resistance of crack propagation, the higher the toughness
of the material, because the material can consume more energy
during crack propagation and delay the occurrence of fracture.
The size and shape of the tearing ridges re ﬂect the complex paths
experienced in the process of crack propagation.

According to the statistics of grain number and size, the average
grain area in Figure 7a is 16.69 μm2. The average grain area in

three-point bending test, the crack is easy to propagate along
the grain boundary due to the large number of grain boundaries.
However, the grain boundary is a weak area in the crystal. [20]
When it is subjected to stress, once the crack initiates at the grain
boundary, it will expand rapidly along the grain boundary, result-
ing in a decrease in the bending property of the material. [21]
The lamellar structure makes the crack propagation path more
tortuous.[22] When the crack encounters a lamellar crystal, it
cannot propagate as smoothly as at an equiaxed grain bound-
ary.[23] It needs to constantly change direction, bypassing these
lamellar structure, which will consume more energy. [24]

3.3. Notched Sample Bending Test
A large number of research and experimental results show that the
surface defects or cracks of Ti alloy will greatly reduce its prop-
erty.[24] The surface of the Ti alloy sheet is usually fragile.
When serving at high temperature, a slight impact or bump
may cause local damage to the surface, [25] and in severe cases,
it is more likely to produce a notch. The notch usually changes
the stress distribution of the components under load.
When the material is in service, the stress concentration is easy
to occur at the notch, resulting in premature crack initiation,
which brings great potential safety hazard to the service of the
equipment.[24] The three-point bending test of the sample with
notch is usually used to evaluate the sensitivity of the material
to notch or crack. [26]

4. Conclusions
In this study, the high-temperature three-point bending test was
carried out on the Ti60 alloy rolled sheet before and after vacuum
solution aging treatment. The microstructure and crack propaga-
tion behavior were observed, and the bending property was
studied. The conclusions are as follows: 1) Treated by vacuum
aging-solution treatment of 1090 °C/2 h/AC þ 700 °C/2 h/AC,
the microstructure of Ti60 alloy rolled sheet changes from ﬁne
equiaxed microstructure to Widmanstatten microstructure
(Σ11 twin grain boundary) dominated by lamellar structure.
2) In the three-point bending unnotch test, the bending property
treated by vacuum solution aging treatment was better.
The bending strength σbb increased from 1.572 and
1.582 MPa to 1.845 and 1.926 MPa, and the fracture work U
increased from 2.023 and 2.322 kJ to 2.962 and 3.127 kJ.
3) The original rolled sheet has a strong {0001} <10-10> texture
with a texture strength of 9.08, and the rolled sheet treated by
vacuum solution aging treatment has a strong {11-20} <0001>
texture with a texture strength of 45.59. Although they are both
the hard orientations, the texture intensity of {11-20} <0001> is
much higher, which contributed to its high bending strength σbb.
4) In the three-point bending notch test, the value of plastic defor-
mation work and fracture work of the rolled sheet treated by
vacuum solution aging treatment is generally better than that
of the original rolled sheet. The total work increased from
176.287 and 176.679 J to 313.305 and 444.411 J. This shows that
the crack sensitivity of the rolled sheet treated by vacuum
solution aging treatment is lower than that of the original rolled
sheet and the total energy required for fracture is more. The
higher fracture work, indicating better toughness, enhances
component reliability. Parts such as engine blades and landing
gears can withstand unexpected impacts and overloads during
long-term service, reducing damage and failure frequencies, thus
cutting down maintenance and replacement costs. 5) In the
three-point bending test, due to the large number of grain bound-
aries of ﬁne equiaxed grains, when it is subjected to tensile stress,
cracks begin at the grain boundary and expand rapidly along the
grain boundary, resulting in a decrease in the bending property
of the material. However, when the crack propagates in the
lamellar structure (Widmanstatten microstructure), it needs to
constantly change the direction to bypass the lamellar structure,
making the crack propagation path more tortuous. This will
consume more energy. Even if a crack initiates due to fatigue,
high-altitude stress, or other factors, the energy-consuming crack
propagation behavior within the lamellar structure slows down
the crack ’s growth rate. This extended crack growth time
provides maintenance crews with more opportunities to detect
and repair the crack before it reaches a critical size that could
lead to catastrophic failure.
