ABSTRACT
The well-controlled high-temperature performance of magnesium alloys is crucial for broadening their appli -
cation in aerospace components but remains a long-standing challenge due to the inherent Ostwald ripening of 
precipitates. In this work, micron/nano-Al
2
Y and nano-TiB
2 
particles were screened to engineer the mechanical 
performance of Mg-5Y-0.6La alloys. The dual-scale particles not only significantly promoted dynamic recrys -
tallization but also substantially refined the grains and dispersed the second phases (Mg
24
Y
5
, Mg
12
La, and Y-rich 
phases). Interestingly, the strength was greatly enhanced along with appreciable ductility over a wide range of 
temperatures, i.e., 298 – 623 K. In particular, the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) increased from 193 MPa to 
230 MPa at 573 K, outperforming many Mg alloys with higher rare earth (RE) additions. This prominent 
strengthening effect was derived from the grain-boundary pinning of the dual-scale Al
2
Y and TiB
2 
particles; the 
co-segregation of Y, La, and Al along the grain boundaries; and the bimodal microstructure with fine grains (3.5 
μ m). It is reasonable to expect that this work will provide a theoretical and experimental basis for dual-scale Al
2
Y 
and TiB
2 
particle-reinforced Mg matrix composites with high performance at temperatures above 573 K.
1. Introduction
Magnesium (Mg) alloys offer benefits such as being lightweight and 
having high specific strength, indicating that they have great potential 
for application in various fields, including automobile, aerospace, and 
defense equipment [1 – 3] . At present, Mg-RE alloys, such as 
Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn(-Zr), Mg-Y-Sn, and Mg-Y-Nd-Zn-Zr series alloys, exhibit 
excellent mechanical properties at 298 – 523 K [4 – 12] . However, the 
performance of these alloys significantly degrades above 523 K due to 
the inherent Ostwald ripening of precipitates. Increasing the RE content 
is a traditional method for improving the high-temperature performance 
of Mg-RE alloys. Su et al. [13] reported that the YS and UTS of a 
Mg-14Gd-2Y-1Zn-0.5Mn alloy at 573 K were 196 MPa and 225 MPa, 
respectively, with increases of 72 MPa and 49 MPa over those of a 
Mg-7Gd-4Y-1Zn-0.5Mn (YS: 124 MPa, UTS: 176 MPa) alloy, respec -
tively. Zhou et al. [14] investigated the effect of the Gd/Y ratio of RE 
elements on the microstructure of as-extruded Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Zr series 
alloys. They reported that the Mg-7Y-4Gd-1Zn-0.3Zr alloy exhibited an 
excellent UTS of 230 MPa at 573 K. Yu et al. [15] discussed the effect of 
the LPSO phase on the thermal stability of the microstructure and its 
strengthening mechanism. The results show that the 
Mg-11Gd-4.5Y-1.5Zn-1Nd-0.5Zr alloy containing thermally stable LPSO 
phases exhibited a UTS of 193 MPa at 573 K. Zhou et al. [16] used 
multi-pass cold rolling on the as-extruded alloy to create a preset twin 
network. Twin-induced recrystallization produced recrystallized grain 
regions, promoting the preferential formation of the thermally stable

Fig. 5 presents the EBSD images of the as-extruded composites, 
where fine dynamically recrystallized (DRX) grains were dispersed 
around the non-recrystallized (unDRX) grain boundaries, exhibiting a 
classical bimodal microstructure. As reported, the bimodal grain struc -
ture exhibited excellent thermal stability [45,46] . The fine DRX grains 
(1 – 2 μ m in size) and the strongly textured un-DRX grains enhanced the 
elevated-temperature mechanical properties of the Mg alloy at 573 K 
[47] . The bimodal structure composed of fine grains effectively 
improved the high-temperature strength of the 4WE51 composite. In 
this work, grains with a grain orientation spread (GOS) less than 2 were 
defined as DRX grains [48,49] . As shown in Fig. 5 (e – h), the fractions of 
DRXed grains in WE51, 2WE51, 4WE51, and 6WE51 were 59.3 %, 
77.1 %, 71.5 %, and 81.0 %, respectively. A high proportion of 
randomly oriented DRX grains weakens the basal texture and reduces 
anisotropy [26,48] . Moreover, it can effectively refine the grains, 
thereby comprehensively enhancing the strength and ductility of the 
composite material. With increasing Al
2
Y-TiB
2 
content, the size of the 
DRX grains first decreased but then increased within the composites. 
The average sizes of the DRX grains in the WE51, 2WE51, 4WE51, and 
6WE51 alloys were 10.1 μ m, 9.3 μ m, 3.5 μ m, and 8.9 μ m, respectively. 
First, micron-sized particles ( Fig. 4 (e – h)) can promote DRX via a 
particle-stimulated nucleation mechanism [50,51] . On the other hand, 
during the extrusion process, the high thermal stability of the Al
2
Y and 
TiB
2 
particles inhibited GB migration and hindered the growth of DRX 
grains, thereby refining the microstructure of the 4WE51 composite.

Mg-RE alloys at 298 – 623 K [66 – 68] . In this study, the co-segregation of 
Y, La, and Al along the GBs improved the strength of the 4WE51 com -
