ABSTRACT
High-strength, high-conductivity (HSHC) materials find broad application in environments demanding both high 
stress tolerance and efficient power transmission, where a precise balance between strength and conductivity is 
critical. In this study, refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) with excellent strength and thermal stability- 
equiatomic TiZrNbTa is developed as novel reinforcement materials in Cu matrix. The nanocrystalline 
TiZrNbTa phase, synthesized through mechanical alloying, provides superior reinforcing effect in the Cu matrix 
compared to coarse-crystalline TiZrNbTa phase. Controlled elemental diffusion between the two phases, ach -
ieved by adjusting the sintering temperature, further optimizes the interfacial bonding, resulting in Cu-20 wt% 
TiZrNbTa composites with ultra-high strength (1081 ± 33 MPa) and excellent conductivity (41.5 ± 2.5% IACS). 
The finding broadens the application potential of nanocrystalline RHEA as stiffening phases, promoting their 
integration into the design of HSHC alloys.
1. Introduction
Cu-based alloys were renowned for their exceptional electrical and 
thermal conductivity, making them integral to a wide range of appli -
cations across electronics, aerospace, transportation, and other indus -
trial sectors [ 1 , 2 ]. Achieving high strength was critical for their practical 
use, typically accomplished through the addition of alloying elements or 
the introduction of reinforcing phases [ 3 – 5 ]. Considerable modifications 
of the material strength can be implemented by manipulating various 
defects in the Cu alloy, such as vacancies, dislocations, and grain or 
phase boundaries [ 6 ]. Nevertheless, the pursuit of high strength and 
high conductivity (HSHC) was inherently challenging, as increased 
strength is typically accompanied by a reduction in electrical conduc -
tivity. The electrical conductivity in Cu-based alloys was limited to 8% – 
25% International Annealed Copper Standard (IACS) [ 7 ] when the 
strength exceeded 1000 MPa, as observed in systems like Cu-Be [ 8 , 9 ], 
Cu-Ni [ 10 , 11 ], Cu-Ti [ 12 , 13 ] etc. The added alloying elements intensi -
fied the scattering of electrons and thereby greatly elevated the 
electrical resistance. Additionally, the inherent instability of defects al -
lows for potential annihilation and conversion during manufacturing or 
utilization, further limiting the broad applicability of these alloys.
Exploiting less-alloyed Cu alloys or Cu-based composites is one of the 
effective means to maintain high electrical conductivity while accom -
plishing high strength [ 14 ]. The trade-off between strength and con -
ductivity can be addressed via microstructure modulation, phase 
structure regulation, morphology dimensioning, interface optimization, 
etc. Ceramic particles with high melting points and excellent mechanical 
properties make good candidates for HSHC reinforcements such as TiB
2 
[ 15 ], TiC [ 16 ], SiC [ 17 , 18 ], etc. However, they suffered from some 
drawbacks including poor reinforcement/matrix interfacial bonding and 
particulates agglomeration. The adhesion or bond strength at the 
interface directly bears on the mechanical behavior of the composite 
material [ 18 , 19 ]. The metals as reinforcement enable a closer interfacial 
bond with the Cu matrix. Recently, refractory metal strengthened Cu 
matrix composites (e.g., Cu-Mo [ 20 , 21 ], Cu-Ta [ 22 ], Cu – W [ 23 , 24 ], 
Cu-Nb [ 25 ], Cu-Ti [ 26 ], etc) have received much attention in the

High thermal stability of the strengthening particles and the inter -

critical to create homogeneous dispersed components and high densified 
composites. Nevertheless, due to the melting points differences of the 
two different phases, the refined microstructure and remarkable per -
formances were hard to attain by conventional fabrication techniques. 
Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS) technology provided the benefits of quick, 
low temperature and high efficiency. Pulsed current technology was 
implemented for powder sintering, where inter-particle discharges 
generate plasma [ 33 ]. The large amount of discharge heat and joule heat 
generated between the powder particles under the action of 
high-frequency pulsed current would promote the diffusion of atoms 
between the powder particles, with a larger diffusion coefficient than 
that of the conventional hot-pressing conditions. Luo et al. [ 34 ] suc -
cessfully realized the activated sintering of Cu – W alloys, promoted 
sufficient diffusion of interfacial elements through appropriate SPS 
sintering temperature adjustment. Simultaneously improved mechani -
cal and electrical properties of the non-porous Cu – TiC composites were 
obtained by tuning the SPS sintering temperature as presented by Wang 
et al. [ 35 ]. It was well established that the strength, ductility and elec -
trical conductivity of metal matrix composites (MMCs) are strongly 
associated with the interface quality [ 36 – 38 ]. The establishment of 
metallic bonds between the reinforcing phase and the Cu matrix by 
diffusion contributed to the favorable properties of the interface. Thus, it 
is essential to perform sintering temperature adjustment to create 
favorable interfaces between the two phases and to maintain adequate 
electrical conductivity of the Cu matrix.

sintering temperature (653 K – 853 K) was optimized to promote the

3. Results
3.1. Powder preparation and characterization
Fig. 1 a- c) display the SEM images of original Cu powders, and me -
chanically alloyed TiZrNbTa HEA powders and Cu-20 wt% TiZrNbTa 
composite powders after BM, respectively. The original Cu powders 
appear as spherical shape with smooth surface and a particle size of less 
than 5 μ m. TiZrNbTa powders are obtained by mechanical alloying 
method, which are finely dispersed and exhibits irregular shapes and 
rough surfaces. After BM, the obtained TiZrNbTa reinforcement powders 
are encapsulated and aggregated by soft Cu powders to form large 
particle composite powders. The cross-section of the composite powder 
is observed and displayed in Figs.1 e, e-1), indicating the development of 
mechanical interlocking between Cu and TiZrNbTa particles. The light 
phase is TiZrNbTa HEA phase, where the atomic percentages of Ti, Zr, 
Nb, and Ta elements are approximately equiatomic. The sharp diffrac -
tion peaks corresponding to Cu and broad diffraction peaks of TiZrNbTa 
phase are identified from the XRD spectra in Fig. 1 d). TiZrNbTa pow -
ders are obtained after 40 h mechanical alloying process, and only the 
characteristic peaks of solid solution phase of body-centered cubic (BCC) 
structure are determined [ 39 ]. Under the high-speed impact of the 
stainless-steel balls, the alloy powders form a complete solid solution 
phase, the grains are refined and the internal dislocation density rapidly 
accumulates.

3.3. Mechanical and electrical properties
As illustrated by the density measurements of the Cu-20 wt% 
TiZrNbTa MMCs obtained at different sintering temperatures ( Fig. 3 a), 
the densification sintering of the material is promoted with the 
increasing sintering temperature. Elevated sintering temperature in -
creases the relative density of the fabricated composites from 97.2% 
(653 K) to 99.4% (853 K). The relative density of the composites is 
higher than 99% when the sintering temperature is not lower than 813 
K. Under the action of the pulsed current, a thermal gradient is formed 
from the center of the particle to the particle contact zone, and the very 
high temperature in the particle contact zone promotes neck formation 
[ 45 ]. The number and size of the ‘necks ’ increase with the sintering 
temperature, thus promoting the densification of the powder material 
[ 46 , 47 ]. The electrical conductivity and the compressive stress-strain 
curves of the Cu-20 wt% TiZrNbTa MMCs sintered at different sinter -
ing temperatures are depicted in Fig. 3 b and c). The strength of the 
composite specimens increases with the increasing sintering tempera -
ture, which is accompanied by a loss of conductivity. The variations of 
the strength and conductivity correlate with the evolution of the 
microstructure. The enhanced strength may result from the reduction of 
internal defects due to improved densification during sintering, as well 
as the intensified interfacial bonding facilitated by interfacial diffusion. 
When the sintering temperature is lower than 693 K, the optimized 
interfacial bonding caused by the increased sintering temperature could 
contribute to the simultaneously enhanced strength and electrical con -
ductivity. In contrast, at higher temperatures (T > 693 K), the 
augmented interfacial elemental diffusion allows impurity atoms into 
the Cu matrix, leading to excessive electron scattering of the Cu matrix, 
which poses a greater negative impact on electrical conductivity than 
the positive impact of optimized interfacial bonding. Compared to 
specimens obtained at a sintering temperature of 653 K, the yield 
strength of the specimens sintered at 813 K is increased by more than 
200 MPa, while only 8% IACS reduction in electrical conductivity is 
achieved. The ultra-high strength of ~1080 MPa and electrical con -
ductivity of ~41% IACS can be achieved in the Cu-20 wt% TiZrNbTa 
MMCs sintered at 813 K, the compressive yield strength-electrical con -
ductivity trade-off is depicted in Fig. 3 d). Compared with the reported 
literatures [ 32 , 40 , 41 , 48 – 59 ], the Cu-20 wt% TiZrNbTa MMCs fabri -
cated at 813 K achieve superior compressive yield strength while 
maintaining higher electrical conductivity. It offers potential electrical 
applications as lead frame material, which requiring high strength over 
600 MPa and sufficient electrical conductivity of ~40% IACS.

4.1. Analysis of element diffusion between the two phases
To further investigate the elemental exchange and distribution be -
tween TiZrNbTa phase and the Cu phase, EPMA measurements are 
conducted on the Cu-20 wt% TiZrNbTa MMCs sintered at 693 K and 813 
K. Fig. 4 illustrates the presence of the elements Cu, Ti, Zr, Nb and Ta in 
the specimens prepared at 693 K and 813 K. TiZrNbTa hard particles are 
diffusely and finely distributed in the Cu matrix, with some particles 
smaller than 1 μ m. Absence of clear boundary exists between these 
smaller particles and the Cu matrix, indicating the dissolution and 
diffusion of TiZrNbTa particles. The Cu element concentrates into a thin 
layer surrounding the larger particles with lower level inside, as shown 
in Fig. 4 a-1 and b-1). The presence of distinct elemental transition layers 
of Ti, Zr, Nb and Ta can be observed at the interfaces between the two 
phases in Fig. 4 a-2~a-5) and Fig. 4 b-2~b-5). Fig. 4 b-2 and b-3) display

compared to Fig. 4 a-2 and a-3). The diffusion of Nb and Ta elements in 
the Cu matrix is not significantly affected as the sintering temperature is 
increased, which may be due to the diffusivity difference in the Cu 
phase, as depicted in Fig. 4 a-4, a-5) and Fig. 4 b-4, b-5).

the enhanced consolidation quality at higher temperatures. Addition -

4.3. Analysis of the two-phase interfacial bonding
The two-phase interfaces of the composite material play a crucial 
role in determining its mechanical and physical properties. A well- 
bonded interface positively affects composite strengthening by 
enhancing load transfer efficiency between the two phases and pro -
moting effective dislocation-particle interactions [ 64 ]. Compressive 
loading is exerted on the composite compression specimens of 693 K and 
813 K respectively, with testing halted at various strain levels (~7%,

pathways, the results of which are shown in Fig. 9 a-e, f- h). When the

accompanied by significant material deformation. At higher strain

different behavior under the same compressive strain. At ~7% strain, 
stress concentration is localized at the interfaces, but no cracks initiation 
is observed, indicating a well-bonded interface between the two phases. 
By increasing the strain to ~14%, the stress concentration becomes 
more pronounced and a small number of cracks are initiated at the in -
terfaces. By ~21% strain, crack propagation occurs, though the number 
of cracks is significantly reduced compared to the sample of 693 K, 
suggesting improved deformation resistance in the 813 K specimens. 
Fracture morphology analysis in Fig. 9 d- e) reveals cracks and exposed 
particles at the interfaces in the Cu-20 wt% TiZrNbTa specimens sin -
tered at 693 K. Fig. 9 i- j) illustrate a smooth fracture surface in the 813 K 
specimens with minimal interfacial debonding between the phases, 
which also evidence the enhanced interfacial adhesion between the two 
phases.

between the two phases, as is shown in Fig. 10 a- c). The corresponding

phase and TiZrNbTa phase, respectively. The grains are randomly ori -

with different orientations of the TiZrNbTa phase are characterized in

determined.
4.4. Contribution of nanocrystalline structure TiZrNbTa in strengthening 
and toughening synergy
As observed in Fig. 10 c), the TiZrNbTa phase exhibits a nano -
crystalline structure. To further investigate the influence of nano -
crystallinity on the mechanical properties of the composites, a control 
group is established. This group consists of Cu-20 wt% TiZrNbTa com -
posites with a grain size of approximately 5 μ m for the TiZrNbTa rein -
forcement phase ( Fig. 11 a- c)), which are prepared under the same 
conditions. In contrast, we fabricate TiZrNbTa with nanocrystalline 
structure (~10 nm) in this work. Compared to Cu-coarse-crystalline 
TiZrNbTa composites, the fabricated 20 wt% nanocrystalline 
TiZrNbTa composites demonstrate remarkably superior mechanical 
properties with enhanced strength and plasticity as shown in Fig. 11 d).
On the one hand, the grain boundaries serve as interfaces that 
separate individual grains in nanocrystalline materials, effectively 
impeding dislocation motion and thereby enhancing the mechanical 
properties of the material [ 65 , 66 ]. The nanocrystalline structure 
significantly promotes the intrinsic strength of TiZrNbTa phase, which

5. Conclusions
The fine and dispersed TiZrNbTa powders with high thermal stability 
were successfully synthesized via mechanical alloying and innovatively 
applied to fabricate HSHC Cu – TiZrNbTa MMCs. TiZrNbTa powders 
featured ultrafine crystallites (~10 nm), offering exceptional strength 
and promising reinforcement potential. Exceptional interfacial cohesion 
and enhanced performance of Cu-20 wt% TiZrNbTa MMCs were realized 
through SPS sintering temperature regulation. Controlled elemental 
diffusion between the two phases of the composite improved sintering 
quality, strengthening both the interface and TiZrNbTa phase. Despite 
grain growth at elevated sintering temperatures, the Cu – TiZrNbTa 
MMCs exhibited significant strengthening. However, the accompanying 
elemental diffusion, while beneficial for mechanical strength, led to a 
trade-off with reduced electrical conductivity. Cu – TiZrNbTa MMCs 
fabricated at 813 K sintering temperature exhibited outstanding prop -
erties with an ultimate compressive strength of 1081 ± 33 MPa and 
electrical conductivity of 41.5 ± 2.5% IACS.
