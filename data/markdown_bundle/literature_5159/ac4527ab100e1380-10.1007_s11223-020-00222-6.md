OF CAST SOLID-SOLUBLE HIGH-ENTROPY ALLOYS

The strength properties of cast solid-solution high-entropy alloys based on bcc and fcc lattices in
the temperature range from /c4570 to 900 /c176C have been investigated. Melting ingots weighing up to
200 g were carried out in a vacuum-arc furnace MIFI-9 by melting the mixture in an atmosphere of
purified argon with a non-consumable tungsten electrode on a water-cooled copper hearth. The
X-ray phase analysis and scanning electron microscopy in combination with INCA X-ray microanalyzer
were used. Hardness ( HIT ) and reduced elastic modulus ( Er ) were determined using automatic
microindentation on a Micron-gamma device using the Berkovich pyramid with a 2 N load in
accordance with ISO14577-1:2015. High-temperature indentation of the Vickers pyramid was
carried out at a 9.8 N load in vacuum. Thehigh-entropy composition of Nb1.5Cr1.25MoV0.75Ta0.5,
which is a solid solution based on the bcc lattice, is characterized by high values of hardness up to
900/c176C. The yield stress and plasticity of solid-solution alloys are determined depending on the
temperatures of the compression tests and the type of crystal lattice. For the high-entropy
composition TiZrHfVNbTa based on the bcc lattice, the yield points are almost twice as high as
those of the CrMnFeCoNi composition based on the fcc lattice. To create high-entropy alloys with
improved characteristics of hot hardness, it is necessary to take into account such factors as the
melting point, enthalpy of mixing, and dimensional mismatch in the lattice. It was found that alloys
based on bcc lattice at positive values of the enthalpy of mixing are characterized by high
characteristics of plasticity and deformability, as well as normalized hardness HEIT r and
compressive yield strength at room temperature. This can be attributed to the cluster structure and
distortion of the crystal lattice.
Keywords: high-entropy alloys, strength, plasticity, mixing enthalpy, dynamic strain aging, lattice dimensional
mismatch.
Introduction. The appearance of a new class of high-entropy alloys (HEA) containing more than five
elements, each in the range of 5–35 at.% by content, attracts the attention of researchers because of the high-strength
characteristics [1–5]. Improved characteristics of hardness and strength are mainly associated with the presence of a
cluster structure and crystal lattice distortion, which complicates the sliding of dislocations [6–8]. The degree of
crystal lattice distortion is mainly caused by the difference in the atomic radii of the elements contained in the alloys.
It should be taken into account that the more elements are in the crystal lattice of alloys, the more difficult is to
maintain the chemical composition homogeneity in the volume. Systems of sufficient complexity, including HEA,
which are characterized by a number of interacting elements and such bonding parameters as enthalpy of mixing,
electronegativity, and relatively high values of fluctuation probabilities, are capable of forming solid solutions.

In [7], it was shown that clusters formed during crystallization differ in chemical composition but have
insignificant differences in the lattice parameters. Sometimes, this leads to formation of two lattice parameters in the
solid-soluble HEA: bcc [9] and fcc [10] lattice-based ones.
One of the most important characteristics that determine the possibility of the formation of multicomponent
single-phase HEA, which are solid substitution solutions, is the integral enthalpy of mixing of elements. These values
are mainly in the range from 5 to /c4515 kJ/mol [11].
On the other hand, it is known [12] that diffusion in alloys occurs due to the vacancy mechanism, i.e., atoms
interact with each other through vacancies. One of the factors determining the facilitation or complication of the
diffusion of atoms of heterogeneous elements in the alloy is the change in its heat composition, which occurs when
mixing the elements, namely if the heat input is increased, the interaction (bonding) energy of heterogeneous atoms
with each other decreases (the positive enthalpy of mixing). If the heat input is decreased, the energy of interaction of
heterogeneous atoms with each other increases, accordingly (the negative enthalpy of mixing).
In the case of the positive enthalpy of mixing, the alloy atoms leave their places in the crystal lattice easier
and move faster (compared to the independent element), i.e., the diffusion rate by the vacancy mechanism increases;
in the case of the negative enthalpy of mixing they leave their places less easily and move slower, i.e., diffusion rate
by the vacancy mechanism decreases.
The role of alloy deformation with regard to the vacancy diffusion mechanism of atoms of elements is in that
it leads to the additional increase in the number of vacancies [12].
Thus, we can identify the main factors, which influence the properties of the cast solid-soluble HEA: the
system entropy, phase composition of the solid solution, the enthalpy of mixing, and the distortion degree of the
crystal lattice.
This paper aims to study the effect of various factors on strength properties of the cast solid-soluble HEA.
Experimental Methodology. Alloys, mainly equiatomic ones, were selected based on the dependence of the
phase composition of the HEA on the electron concentration [13]. Melting of ingots was performed in the
vacuum-arc furnace MEPI-9 by melting the mixture of 100 g in the atmosphere of purified argon with a
non-consumable tungsten electrode on the water-cooled copper hearth. Ingots were melted six or seven times to
homogenize the composition; the melt was cooled at a rate of 80–100 K/s.
The following research and testing methods were used: the X-ray phase analysis (the DRON-UM1
diffractometer in monochromatic CuK /c97radiation); the scanning electron microscopy in combination with the INCA
X-ray microanalyzer (Superprobe-733; electron beam diameter is 0.7 nm).
Hardness (HIT ) and the reduced elastic modulus ( Er ) were defined using automatic microindentation on the
Micron-gamma device by the Berkovich pyramid with a 2N load, in accordance with ISO14577-1:2015.
High-temperature ( T /c6120–900/c176Ñ) indentation of polished plates with a size of 8.5 /c1808.5 /c1804 mm by the

bench developed for the hot hardness evaluation in the Design Bureau of the Frantsevich Institute of Materials

Results and Discussion. The study of the cast HEA structure showed that it does not differ from that of
solid-soluble alloys. Figure 1 shows a typical structure on the example of the TiZrHfVNbTa alloy.
At the same time, it was previously established [7] that electron microscopy of direct resolution find features
for a similar alloy. Nanograins (clusters) are observed inside the grains; their size is quite small (2 to 5 nm). The
chemical composition analysis with a probe spot of 0.5 nm shows that each nanograin differs significantly in
chemical composition.
The presence of a cluster structure and the crystal lattice distortion due to differences in the atomic radii of
the elements contained in the HEA cause high values of hardness and the HEIT r ratio (Table 1), which are
specific to pure metals only in the nanostructured state [18].
The characteristics of strength and plasticity of solid-soluble alloys with different phase compositions
depending on the compression test temperatures and the type of crystal lattice in the temperature range from /c4570 to

All studied alloys are characterized by plasticity at room temperature. The TiZrHfVNbTa and CrMnFeCoNi
alloys show plasticity also at a temperature of 4.2 K [22].
The strength values at room temperature of all alloys with the bcc lattice are quite high ( /c621200 MPa).
Analyzing individual components of the alloy, the strength characteristics of a 1000 MPa level are observed only in

CONCLUSIONS
1. The characteristics of strength and plasticity of the solid-soluble HEA with bcc and fcc lattice in the
temperature range from /c4570 to 900 /c176C are investigated.
2. It is shown that the alloys with bcc lattices have high values of the HEIT r ratio ( /c620.04) and
compressive yield strength at room temperature ( /c621200 MPa). The reason is the presence of the cluster structure and
the distortion of the crystal lattice.
3. It is shown that to make alloys with high characteristics of hot hardness it is necessary to take into account
two factors: the melting point and the enthalpy of mixing.
4. It was found that the solid-soluble HEA based on the bcc lattice at positive values of the enthalpy of
mixing are characterized by plasticity combined with high strength.
