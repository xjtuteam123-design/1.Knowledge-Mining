Superplastic-Like Behavior and Enhanced Strength of
a Two-Phase Titanium Alloy with Ultra ﬁne Grains
Grigory S. Dyakonov, Georgy I. Raab, Mikhail V. Pesin, Alexander V. Polyakov,*
Irina P. Semenova, and Ruslan Z. Valiev
1. Introduction
Two-phase titanium alloys are advanced structural materials due
to high speci ﬁc strength and strong corrosion resistance, which
makes them attractive for a wide range of applications in engi-
neering, primarily in the aerospace industry.[1,2] Their wide appli-
cation in the aviation industry began in the 1960s, but at that time
manufacturing complex-shaped products from them was prob-
lematic. In this connection, great interest was aroused by the
study of the origin of superplasticity in Ti alloys and its practical
use.[1,3] This effect in Ti alloys, where tensile elongation reaches
about 1000%, normally occurs in a temperature range of
800– 1000 /C14 C and strain-rate range of 10 /C0 4– 10/C0 3 s/C0 1 when a
structure with a grain size of 5 – 10 μm is formed. This effect
is actively utilized in the superplastic forming of complex-shaped
products from Ti alloys.[3,4] At the same time, the development of
recrystallization processes during the superplastic forming at ele-
vated temperatures often creates problems in the preservation of
the strength properties of the produced items.
In recent decades, keen interest has
been aroused by the studies of the so-called
low-temperature and high-strain-rate
superplasticity observed in two-phase Ti
alloys where an ultra ﬁne-grained (UFG)
structure with a grain size below
0.5– 1.0 μm is formed. [5,6] This effect was
ﬁrst discovered in UFG Al alloys processed
by severe plastic deformation (SPD) and
gave a large boost to the development
of the nano-SPD research. [7] In the
Ti– 6Al– 4V titanium alloy with a UFG struc-
ture, superplasticity at lower temperatures
and higher strain rates was demonstrated
by different researchers. [8– 16] This alloy in
the UFG state exhibits high elongation
even at temperatures 625 and 650 /C14 C and very high strain rates
of 10 /C0 2– 10/C0 1 s/C0 1 due to a UFG structure with an average grain
size of 0.1– 0.2 μm.[11] As it was also demonstrated, the alloy with
a mean grain size of 0.6 μm exhibits high tensile elongations in a
temperature range of 700– 800 /C14 C with a high strain-rate sensitiv-
ity during compression tests. [16] Decreasing the temperature of
metal deformation in isothermal forging not only has processing
advantages, but also creates conditions for increasing the service
properties of produced articles due to the preservation of the
UFG structure in the alloy. [17]
The object of the present study is the commercial alloy
Ti-5.7Al-3.8Mo-1.2Zr-1.3Sn (Russian name VT8M-1) that was
designed for the compressor blades of aircraft engines and
has a higher level of thermal stability at temperatures up to
450 /C14 C and due to the composition of alloying elements a
reduced sensitivity to stress raisers as compared to the popular
alloy Ti-6Al-4V. [18] Our recent articles show that an UFG struc-
ture can be successfully produced in this alloy by equal-channel
angular pressing (ECAP), and this enables enhancing consider-
ably the alloy’s mechanical properties at room and operating tem-
peratures.[18– 21]
The aim of this work is to study the mechanical behavior of the
VT8M-1 alloy processed by ECAP during compression tests in a
temperature range of 650 – 800 /C14 C, as well as to evaluate the
degradation of the UFG structure and its response to the
mechanical properties of the billet during a physical experiment
imitating isothermal forging at 700 /C14 C.

Here, the two-phase titanium alloy Ti-5.7Al-3.8Mo-1.2Zr-1.3Sn (Russian name
VT8M-1) with an ultra ﬁne-grained (UFG) structure is successfully produced
by equal-channel angular pressing (ECAP). This UFG alloy demonstrates a
signiﬁcant increase in strength at room temperature (RT) and superplastic-like
behavior during compression at elevated temperatures in a range of 650 –800 /C14 C.
This unusual behavior is used in this work to study the forging of the alloy at
700 /C14 C, which simulates the process of making an aircraft engine blade. It is
shown that after this forging the alloy has signi ﬁcantly higher strength properties
than after standard processing. The origin of such superior behavior of this UFG
alloy is discussed.

3.2. The Mechanical Behavior of the UFG VT8M-1 Alloy During
Compression Tests
Figure 6 shows the dependence curves of true stress on strain
during compression of the coarse grained (CG) and UFG
VT8M-1 alloy in the temperature range of 650 – 800 /C14 C.
Comparing the true compression curves in Figure 6a – c, it can
be seen that the UFG structure provided a signi ﬁcant reduction
in upsetting stress compared to the coarse-grained state.
Deformation of samples with a CG structure led to an increase
in forces/stresses, while UFG samples were deformed without
hardening (Figure 6a). On the stress – strain curves of the UFG
alloy, at the initial stage of deformation, a ﬂow stress peak can
be distinguished, after which softening or a steady- ﬂow stage is
observed.
Figure 7 shows that at a compression temperature of 650 /C14 Ci n
the strain rate range of 0.07 – 0.0007 s/C0 1, softening is observed at
the peak stress.
Such softening in Ti alloys is provided by the development of
dynamic recovery and dynamic recrystallization. [23] Increasing
the compression temperature to 700 /C14 C leads to a somewhat
different curve view at all the investigated strain rates. At the ini-
tial stage of plastic deformation, a peak stress can be distin-
guished, then softening conditioned by recrystallization
processes occurs, after which there is a stabilization of the ﬂow
stress value with enhanced strain rate due to the involvement of
the grain-boundary sliding (GBS) mechanism, which is a sign of
the material’s superplasticity.[1,2] A certain increase in ﬂow stress
at a strain close to 50% can be attributed to an increase in dislo-
cation density in the recrystallized grains of the α- and β-phases.
At a strain rate of 0.0014 s /C0 1 and temperatures of 750 and
800 /C14 C, the UFG samples exhibit a decrease in the ﬂow stress
values below 100 MPa (Figure 6b). At temperatures of 650 and
700 /C14 C after peak stresses of 480 and 230 MPa, respectively, were
achieved, a slight softening was observed at the material ’s steady-
ﬂow stage (Figure 6b).

3.3. Microstructure and Mechanical Properties of the Billets
After Isothermal Forging
Traditionally, the temperature of the isothermal forging of parts
from Ti alloys reaches 910– 950 /C14 C.[25] The aim of this experiment
was to evaluate the possibility of decreasing the isothermal forg-
ing temperature when using a billet from a UFG alloy exhibiting
superplastic behavior at temperatures below 800 /C14 C. In this
experiment, we selected the temperature of billet heating and fur-
ther forging /C0 700 /C14 C. This was done due to several reasons: ﬁrst,
to prevent the occurrence of static recrystallization and grain
coarsening during the pre-deformation heating of the billet that
is possible at temperatures above 750 /C14 C; second, a considerable
increase in the speci ﬁc force applied on the tool at 650 /C14 C may
lead to its failure. [25]
The microstructure of the experimental samples with a UFG
structure was analyzed after the deformation process at a temper-
ature of 700 /C14 C (Figure 1). It can be seen in Figure 9 a that the
grains of the primary α-phase have a shape elongated along the
direction of material ﬂow, and its fraction in the alloy ’s structure
practically has not changed. According to the TEM data, the aver-
age size of the α- and β-phase grains in the two-phase regions of
the microstructure has slightly grown and equals about 0.40 μm
(Figure 9b). Meanwhile, some changes can be observed in the
microstructure after the deformation processing of the UFG bil-
lets. In particular, a high dislocation density is visible in the grain
interiors, the grain boundaries are clear and straight-lined, which
indicates the development of the dynamic recrystallization
processes during the deformation processing of the alloy. The

4. Discussion
In this study, a UFG structure was successfully produced in the
two-phase VT8M-1 alloy billets by ECAP. The microstructure of
the ECAP-processed alloy was characterized by a mean size of the
secondary α-phase of 0.32 μm, and in the primary grains of the
α-phase predominantly low-angle subgrains were formed (see
Figure 4). In this case, the UFG structure formed in the
VT8M-1 alloy was similar to the UFG structure in the two-phase
alloy Ti-6Al-4V processed by ECAP, as reported in. [7]
Grain size reduction led to an increase in the UTS at room
temperature to 1216 MPa, elongation being 10%. During the
deformation by compression of this alloy, it was found that soft-
ening and an extensive stage of the material ’s steady ﬂow was
observed already at 650 – 700 /C14 C practically over the whole
range of the investigated strain rates, from 10 /C0 2 to 10 /C0 4 s/C0 1
(Figure 6, 7). Such softening in titanium alloys is accompanied
by a considerable increase in the strain-rate sensitivity of ﬂow
stress (Figure 8a), when the value of the m parameter grows
above 0.3 at a temperature of 700 /C14 C and higher. Such an
increased value of the m parameter directly indicates the active
involvement of grain-boundary sliding in the deformation pro-
cess of the UFG alloy and also points to its superplastic-like
behavior.[1,2,6] Unlike the UFG alloy, in the case of the CG alloy
the increase in ﬂow stress was observed during compression, and
the m parameter value was below 0.1, which is typical for
conventional hot deformation of Ti alloys.
The obtained data for the mechanical behavior of the UFG
VT8M-1 alloy under the investigated conditions of deformation
by compression are in good agreement with the data reported
recently for the less alloyed VT8 alloy (Ti-6.0Al-3.2Mo), where
the formation of a submicrocrystalline structure (grain size
0.06 μm) decreased the temperature of superplasticity down to
550 /C14 C.[25] In the ECAP-processed Ti-6Al-4 V alloy, a decrease
in the temperature of superplasticity to 650 and 700 /C14 C was also
observed in a range of strain rates from 10 /C0 4 to 10 /C0 3 s/C0 1.[16]

It is known that compressor blades from conventional
two-phase Ti alloys are produced by isothermal forging, includ-
ing that with the use of superplastic deformation, normally in a
range of temperatures from 850 to 950 /C14 C.[17,25] The use of UFG
billets in the forming of blades makes it possible to implement
isothermal forging under the conditions of low-temperature
superplasticity. First, this enables, in addition to saving energy
resources, using cheaper materials for the forging die-set.
Second, in this temperature range of deformation the thickness
of the alpha phase of the billet decreases, which considerably
reduces the scope of subsequent machining. In this work, we
have produced experimental forgings from the UFG VT8M-1
alloy at a temperature of 700 /C14 C using a standard press where
strain rate reaches 10 /C0 2 s/C0 1. The results from the study of the
microstructure and mechanical properties of the experimental
samples demonstrate the possibility to preserve the UFG micro-
structure and enhanced mechanical properties in the billets after
forging. In particular, we have demonstrated that the preserva-
tion of the average grain size of the secondary α-phase up to
0.4 μm provided a UTS of 1220 MPa, i.e., practically at a level
of the ECAP-processed material (Table 3). Worth noting is the
large uniform elongation of the samples that characterize a mate-
rial’s capacity for strain hardening. In this case, the UFG struc-
ture in the forged billet is characterized by more equilibrium
grain boundaries with a high-angle misorientation, as well as
an equiaxed shape. Such regularities in the transformation of
the UFG structure of the Ti– 6Al– 4V alloy in the process of super-
plastic deformation were also observed in L. Saitova. [16]
Based on the performed experimental studies into the
mechanical behavior of the VT8M-1 alloy with a UFG structure
at relatively low temperatures of 650 – 800 /C14 C, we have shown that
forging at a temperature of 700 /C14 C does not lead to degradation of
the UFG structure and, consequently, to a considerable decline
in the alloy’s mechanical properties at room temperature. Recent
studies of the long-term strength of the UFG Ti – 6Al– 4V alloy at
operating temperatures (not higher than 350 /C14 C) show its obvi-
ous advantage over its coarse-grained counterpart. [27] As for the
VT8M-1 alloy, in a recent work, it was observed that the UFG
samples at the operating temperature (400 /C14 C) withstand 100 h
without destruction at a signi ﬁcant ultrahigh voltage, after the
CG samples. [28] This behavior was explained by the presence
of silicides, which ﬁx the grain boundaries and prevent the
motion of dislocations during creep tests. [29]
Thus, this work has demonstrated a potential possibility to
increase the service properties in complex-shaped items during
the forming of a billet previously subjected to ECAP due to the
effect of superplastic-like behavior. However, the real application
of UFG Ti alloys in an aircraft-engine building requires further
studying of a whole range of mechanical properties, including
impact toughness, fracture toughness, long-term strength at
operating temperatures, etc.
5. Conclusions
1) In the VT8M-1 alloy, ECAP processing produced a UFG struc-
ture with an average grain size of the secondary phases
of 0.32 μm and enhanced mechanical properties at room temper-
ature: UTS ¼ 1216 MPa, 0.2 YS ¼ 1140 MPa and δ ¼ 9.5%; 2) In
the process of the deformation by compression of the VT8M-1
alloy with a UFG structure in a temperature range of 600 – 850 /C14 C
and in a range of strain rates from 10 /C0 2 to 10/C0 4 s/C0 1, at the initial
stage of deformation a ﬂow stress peak was distinguished, fol-
lowed by softening and/or the steady- ﬂow stage with an
enhanced strain rate sensitivity m > 0.3, which characterizes
the superplastic-like behavior of the UFG alloy; and 3) Using
forging on a hydraulic press with a model die-set, experimental
billets of the UFG VT8M-1 alloy were produced at a deformation
temperature of 700 /C14 C. It was shown that forging did not lead to
the degradation of the UFG structure and preserved a high
strength at a level of the UTS after ECAP processing (about
1220 MPa) and ductility (10% elongation).
