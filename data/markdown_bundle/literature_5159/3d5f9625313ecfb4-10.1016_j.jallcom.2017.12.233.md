Abstract: 
In order to explore the mechanical and tribological properties of 
high-temperature alloy materials, the present study aims to investigate the synthesis, 
microstructure, composition, mechanical and tribolo gical properties of 
high-temperature monolithic MCrAlY (M=Ni or NiCo) a lloys. The alloys were 
prepared by spark plasma sintering technique. The m icrostructure and composition 
were studied by Scanning Electron Microscopy and X- ray diffractometry. The 
tribological properties of Ni-based alloys were tes ted by friction tester against Si 3N4 
balls at elevated temperatures. The results showed that the NiCrAlY alloy was 
composed of γ-Ni(Cr) solid solution, β-NiAl, and γ'-Ni3Al with little CrAl. The 
NiCoCrAlY alloy was consisted of γ-Ni(Cr/Co) solid solution, β-NiAl and γ'-Ni 3Al.

There are many tribological components working in a dvanced industrial gas 
turbines, the power generation industries and aeron autical as well as chemical 
industries under high temperature and aggressive se rvice conditions [1-2]. In this 
regard, researchers have never stopped developing a nd researching materials with 
novel corrosion and high-temperature oxidation resi stance, outstanding antiwear and 
self-lubrication performance at the high temperatur e conditions. Among the various 
materials, high-temperature alloys are especially a ttractive. MCrAlY (M=Ni, Co or

2. Materials and methods 
2.1 Samples preparation 
Gas atomized prealloyed Ni-based alloy powders: NiC rAlY (commodity code is 
962) and NiCoCrAlY (commodity code is 386-3) supplied by Sulzer Metco were used 
in this study. The powder was weighed 200 g and sin tered at 1150 °C in vacuum (<15 
Pa) under a pressure of 30 MPa at a cylindrical gra phite mold with a 50 mm diameter 
by spark plasma sintering technique, SPS (SPS-20T-1 0, Shanghai Chen Hua 
Technology Co., Ltd). The heating rate was 50 °C/mi n and the holding time 10 min. 
The sample was cooled down by circulating water in the intenders. The as-prepared 
NiCrAlY and NiCoCrAlY alloys were polished down to 80, 400, 800, 1200, and 1500 
mesh silicon carbide waterproof abrasive papers. 
2.2 Mechanical testing methods

The Brinell hardness of alloys was measured by a di gital small load Brinell

applied load of 62.5 kgf with the ball diameter of 2.5 mm and a dwell time of 30 s. The

speed was 0.5 mm/s and the span was 16 mm. Five sam ples were randomly selected 
for each mechanical property test. The final testin g result of mechanical performance 
is that of the average for the five samples. 
2.3 Friction and wear tests 
The high-temperature tribometer (HT-1000, made by Z hong Ke Kai Hua 
Corporation) with a ball-on-disk configuration was applied to test the friction and 
wear in the atmosphere. The disks were MCrAlY alloys which rotated with a constant 
speed of 18.8 cm/s over a period of 45 min. The cou nterparts were commercial 
available Si 
3N4 balls with a diameter of 6.43 mm. The normal load was 10 N and the 
rotation radius was 5 mm for all tests. The test te mperature was RT, 200, 400, 600, 
and 800 °C. The friction coefficient µ was recorded automatically by the computer 
connecting friction tester during the test. The fri ction and wear test for each specimen 
was repeated three times. The worn surface of speci men was examined by Scanning

2.4 Characterization 
Phase compositions of NiCrAlY and NiCoCrAlY alloy w ere analyzed by an

According to the particle reinforcing principle of composite, particle reinforced 
composites are composed of larger size (> 1 µm) hard particles and matrix. Under the 
external load, the particles bear the load together with the matrix, obstruct the 
dislocation movement of the matrix and restrict the deformation of the substrate. 
Additionally, the smaller reinforced particle size and the higher volume fraction of 
particles, the better effect of the particle reinfo rcement on the composite material.

4. Conclusions 
The NiCrAlY and NiCoCrAlY alloys were successfully fabricated by the 
technology of SPS. Their phase composition and micr ostructure were analyzed. The 
mechanical and tribological properties were investi gated, and the wear mechanisms 
were discussed in details as well. The conclusions are as follows: 
a. The main phase of NiCrAlY alloy is γ-Ni(Cr) solid solution, β-NiAl and 
γ′ -Ni 3Al with little CrAl. The main composition of NiCoCr AlY alloy is γ-Ni(Cr/Co) 
solid solution, β-NiAl phase and γ′ -Ni3Al. The microstructure of NiCoCrAlY alloy is 
more uniform with relatively fine and uniformly distributed reinforced phases and less 
structural defects. 
b. The hardness of NiCrAlY alloy is higher than tha t of NiCoCrAlY alloy which 
attributed to the higher content of reinforced phas e γ'-Ni 3Al. The more uniform 
microstructure of NiCoCrAlY alloy along with the ev enly dispersed fine reinforced 
phases and the less pores defects contributes to the higher flexural strength. 
c. As the temperature rises, the µ and WR of NiCrAlY and NiCoCrAlY alloys 
increase firstly to reach the highest value at 200 and 400 °C and then decline. The WR 
of NiCrAlY are lower than that of NiCoCrAlY for all the temperatures. Adhesive 
wear dominates the wear mechanism of NiCrAlY/Si 3N4 tribo-pair, while abrasive 
wear is the main wear mechanism for NiCrAlY/Si 3N4 tribo-pair. At high temperature,
