Temperature effects on microstructure and mechanical properties
of sintered high-entropy equiatomic Ti20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 alloy
for aero-gear application
Ayodeji Ebenezer Afolabi1 & Olawale Popoola2 & Abimbola P. I. Popoola1 & Fatai O. Aramide1,3 & Daniel T. Oloruntoba3
Received: 17 February 2020 / Accepted: 18 May 2020
# Springer-Verlag London Ltd., part of Springer Nature 2020
Abstract
This work presented an upgraded high-entropy alloy by the addition of chromium to the conventional Ti-10V-2Fe-3Al alloy to
fabricate equiatomic Ti
20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 high-entropy alloy via spark plasma sintering powder processing at different temper-
ature of 700 °C, 800 °C, 900 °C, 1000 °C, and 1100 °C respectively under a constant heating rate of 100 °C /min, the pressure of
40 MPa, and holding time of 5 min. The microstructure and phase transformation of the sintered alloyed were studied with a
scanning electron microscope equipped with energy dispersive spectroscopy. The constituent phases present in the sintered high-
entropy alloy were analyzed by X-ray diffraction and were found to show increased development of body-centered cubic solid
solution alloys across the temperature gradients. The mechanical properties over a temperature range of 700 ≤ T° C ≤ 1100
generally show an increase in hardness from 3363 to 8480 MPa, tensile strength from 1097.17 to 2766.58 MPa, and yield
strength from793.61 to 2001.13 MPa respectively. The SEM-EDS of Ti
20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 equiatomic high-entropy alloys show
the existence of a nano-net-like spinodal structure at the optimum temperature of 1100 °C, which are rich in body centered cubic
structure. At this elevated temperature, the presence of strong body-centered cubic–forming elements such as Cr, Fe, and Al was
established from the corresponding EDS. Ti
20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 high-entropy equiatomic alloy has been successfully fabricated
by spark plasma sintering. The effects of temperature on microstructural and mechanical properties of the sintered
Ti
20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 alloy demonstrated a general improvement of the alloys at the elevated region.
Keywords High-entropy alloys . Aero-gear . Sintering . Equiatomic . Temperature . Microstructure
1 Introduction
The structure and mechanical characteristics of the material
are the key determinants of suitability for engineering appli-
cations, particularly when the material is stressed by high tem-
peratures, excessive mechanical vibrations, or chemical spe-
cies. Under these limitations, materials usually fail as phase-
out mechanisms, which lead to failure over time. High-
property profile content is of great importance in aeronautical
space applications, such as landing gear, to support aircraft
landing and ground operations. The material thus needs to
have good high strength, tiredness, wear, and corrosion and
must also be capable of withstanding high-impact strengths
due to airframe loads [ 1].
The designs of conventional alloys, including Al, Fe, Ti,
Co and Ni-based alloys, have for a long time been primarily
based on the use of one element as the main component and
the addition of small elements to boost the properties. Since
traditional theories of metallurgy indicate that the use of multi-
structural alloys can create some complex structures and com-
pounds, which make it difficult to study the microstructure
and cause mechanical characteristics to degrade. Such prob-
lems make it difficult to align conventional alloys with mod-
ern applications [2].
Ti-10V-2Fe-3Al (Ti-10-2-3), which is still susceptible to a
tribology problem during application due to certain other

Ti-10-2-3 is not a high-entropy equiatomic alloy with an
atomic ratio of over 40% that reduces alloy reliability during
service. According to Aristeidakis and Tzini [3], the quest for
the alloy with ratios of below 35% began in 1996. It also
opened how several key metallic elements could be combined
to create more usable, highly entropic alloys in similar atomic
proportions. Such high-entropy alloy species (HEAs) are now
better replaced by conventional ones, finding their way into
aero-gears. Yeh et al. [ 4] followed up by other research
groups, such as Tung et al. [ 5], Zhang et al. [ 6], and Ji et al.
[7], provided the first outstanding HEAs studies.
HEA is primarily a metal-based element combined with
five or more elements in a range between 5 and 35% [ 8].
The high-entropy effect of HEAs removed the opportunity
to form intermetallic phases, in a most basic face-centered
cubic (FCC) or body-centered cubic (BCC) alloys according
to some research results [ 9]. The results of this investigation
indicate that the profound properties of HEAs resulted.
Rather, HEA has solid phases of resolution with deeper prop-
erties such as high hardness [10], better physical and mechan-
ical efficiency at cryogenic temperatures [ 11], high fracture
strength, yield strain, and plastic strain [ 12]. As a result of
these characteristics, HEAs are outstanding materials for the
aerospace industry, especially in critical components such as
engines and landing gears.
In the course of the years, HEAs were systematically
examined through vacuum arc fusion, which is restricted
in size, form, and properties. Many powder-based
manufacturing routes for HEA such as spark plasma
sintering (SPS), laser additive manufacture (LAM), me-
chanical alloy, and sputtering have been recently devel-
oped [ 13]. Specifically, the high heating rate over a short
time of SPS and the ability to prevent grain growth
forming the intermetallic alloy phases at high-
temperature gradient were stated to be extremely success-
ful in the novel fabrication of HEAs. Sintered HEAs are
therefore highly specifically designed for the aerospace
and automotive industries, their strength and thermal sta-
bility, strong densificati on, and fatigue resistance [ 2, 14].
The temperature plays an important role in achieving good
density and improved microhardness during SPS. Mohanty
et al. [ 14] have recorded optimal hardness at highest temper-
atures of 1273 K in the manufacture of the multi-component
equiatomic AlCoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy. The findings al-
so showed that the Al
0.5CrFeNiCo0.3C0.2 high-entropy alloy
with strength and Vickers microhardness were 2131 MPa and
617 HV respectively and resulted in good mechanical behav-
iors [12]. However, studies on the effect of Cr content on the
Ti-10-2-3 alloy have been less reported. In the present study, a
novel Ti 20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 equiatomic HEAs is developed
using plasma spark sintering methods, which are accompanied
by thorough characterization of synthesized alloys for proper-
ty profiles and performance evaluation.
2 Experimental procedure
2.1 Materials
In order that a homogeneous composition of the micron was
achieved, pure titanium, vanadium, aluminum, iron, and chro-
mium were measured equally at a 20% weight percentage
(equiatomic). The properties of the powders obtained are
shown in Table 1. The powder was pulled in the 40 mm die
ready for calibration at various temperature profiles after the
metal powder was combined. The samples were checked fol-
lowing sintering. For studying the phase structure of the HEA,
X-ray diffraction (XRD) has been used to assess the surface
toughness of the specimen with the microhardness test. Each
specimen was measured and measured at 5 points at a distance
of 3 mm from the center of the specimen. Each indentation
distance was 2 mm and the load was 0.98 N. It was 10 s. The
average hardness value of the 5 samples was taken when the
calculation was done. Finally, SEM has been examined for the
microstructure of the high-entropy alloy.
2.2 Characterization processes
Samples were prepared for microstructural and mechanical
testing after the sintering of the Ti 20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 alloys.
The sintered HEAs were divided into test samples that were
prepared to provide a smooth surface profile by grinding and
polishing. With a Future-Tech 700 microhardness test at 100
gf for 15 s, the hardness of the prepared HEA samples was
evaluated. The length of each sample was determined by an
average of 10 different indentations. Scanning electron micro-
scope (SEM) fitted with energy dispersive spectroscopy
(EDS) was used in the microstructure and phase transforma-
tion of the sintered alloy. Analyzing the component phases in
the sintered HEAs samples.

3 Results and discussion
3.1 Microstructural evolution
In Fig. 1, the evolution of the microstructure and EDS shows
the nature of the phases in the sintered HEAs with different
temperatures of 700 °C, 800 °C, 900 °C, 1000 °C, and 1100
°C, respectively, at a constant heating rate of 100 °C/min,
40 MPa pressures, and 5-min hold time as presented in Figs .
1(a) and (b), 2(a) and (b), 3(a) and (b), 4(a) and (b),a n d 5(a)
and (b). EDS microstructures showed a variable dissolution of
the individual elements within a solid alloy solution in an
equimolar proportion across the temperature gradient. It can
be established that there has been a different behavior of acti-
vation in the temperature growth of the thermodynamic re-
sponse of the component material of the HEAs alloy that
dictates the type of phases formed and the contents.
A minimum sintering Ti
20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 alloy at 700 °C
is shown in Fig. 1 (a) and the microstructure is a good solid
solution with well-defined grains, boundaries, and phases.
The lightweight phases of the FCC-rich solid solution are
predominant. The progression of sintering at 800 °C is illus-
trated in Fig. 2(a). As the temperature increases, high entropy
becomes significantly enhanced. The tendency of forming in-
termetallic phases or ordered solid solutions is limited, which
makes it easy to form random solid solution during solidifica-
tion. The strong body-centered cubic elements (BCCs), such
as Cr, Fe, and Al, are established by the corresponding EDS at
this high temperature with stable BCC structures, as stated by
Gou et al. [ 17]. Chromium, on the other hand, strongly in-
creases the thermodynamic driving force for the gamma phase
formation, which makes its mobility to be much higher in the
BCC phase stabilized by the aluminum and is reported by
Senkov et al. [ 18] and Alcalá et al. [ 19]. Also, as the temper-
atures rise, the darker phases become rich in BCC structure,
showing significant grain coarsening in Figs. 3(a) and 4(a).
The dendrites network structures show that the coefficient of
atomic diffusion is pronounced when more of the grain
boundaries migrate from FCC- into BCC-like structures. The
hardness of HEAs increases as a result of the substitution and
incremental transition of the FCC phase into the hard BCC
phase over temperature gradients. This is further confirmed
via their high hardness values. The developed FCC and
BCC phases in the interdendrite are transformed completely
at the optimum temperature of 1100 °C shown by Fig. 5(a)
into spinodal-like phases that are verified as having the highest
temperature strength, stress and yield strengths among the
SPSed HEAs specimens.

3.3 Mechanical properties
For the determination of alloy e fficiency, the mechanical
properties of the as-sintered Ti 20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 HEAs
ranged from hardness, tensile strength, and production power.
Ti20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 hardness is shown in Table 2 for five
temperature gradients. The key mechanical characteristics
from the lowest to the highest temperature have markedly
improved. The hardness, tensile strength, and performance
strength of 700 °C respectively range up to HV 342.91 H,
HV 1097.17, and HV 793.61, and their maximum temperature
to HV 8480 HV, 2766.58 HV, and 2001.13 HV. The trend
showed how high-temperature resistance is with the devel-
oped Ti
20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 HEA. Since temperature is a mea-
sure of material strength, the developed HEAs are therefore
shown to be good for applications requiring extreme temper-
atures, such as a turbine and aero-engine compressor at the
top. The lower mechanical efficiency at the lowest frying tem-
perature could be due to poor dissemination, while the rate of
nuclear dissemination in the solid HEAs solution is faster at
higher temperatures, which promotes better mixture synerget-
ic and better performance.
4 Conclusion
The effects of temperature on microstructure and mechanical
properties of sintered high-entropy equiatomic
Ti
20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 alloy for the aero-gear application have
been successfully carried out with the following findings:
1. Over the temperature region 700 ≤ T° C ≤ 1100, the me-
chanical properties of the alloy typically improve with
temperature gradients. Hardness increases from 3363 to
8480 MPa, tensile strength decreases from 1097.17 to
2766.58 MPa, and yield strength increases from 793.61
to 2001.13 MPa. The mechanical proficiency shows that
the developed alloys are suitability for aero-gear, and oth-
er high-temperature, stress-induced, and fatigue-oriented
applications.
2. Dominant cubic structure step BCC
1 or BCC2 based from
XRD spectrographs. The principal phase in the alloy
change from BCC
1 to BCC2 as the sintering temperature
increases and the presence of the AlTi3 intermetallic com-
pound is at 1000 °C. With the holding time, the alloy
transforms at 1100 °C from BCC
1 to BCC2, and grades
are slowly refined and a stabilizing step is formed with the
rise in the sintering temperature. As the holding time
maintained, the high-entropy alloy displays a good net-
work structure.
3. The SEM-EDS from the equiatomic HEA
Ti
20V20Al20Fe20Cr20 shows that nano-net-like spinodal
structures rich in BCC structure and at optimum temper-
atures of 1100 °C. The presence of strong BCC forming
elements such as Cr, Fe, and Al from the corresponding
EDS was determined at this high temperature. In con-
trast, chromium increases the thermodynamic driving
force for the μ phase formation significantly. This makes
its mobility significantly higher in the aluminum-
stabilized BCC phase.
