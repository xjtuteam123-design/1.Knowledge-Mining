ABSTRACT
Developing advanced structural materials with high strength and excellent ductility is crucial for meeting
aerospace and military requirements. High/medium entropy alloys (H/MEAs) have garnered significant atten-
tion due to their superior mechanical properties. In this study, through severe deformation followed by heat
treatment at temperatures in the range of 700 – 900
◦
C, we successfully obtained a fully recrystallized structure
with double precipitates in non-equiatomic CoCrNiTi
0.1
. Tensile tests at room temperature revealed that the alloy
annealed at 700
◦
C for 1 h exhibited exceptional mechanical properties, with a yield strength of 1575 MPa,
tensile strength of 1735 MPa, and ductility of 15 %. The ultrahigh yield strength is primarily attributed to the
synergistic strengthening mechanisms including precipitation strengthening, grain-boundary strengthening, and
HDI strengthening. The FCC/L12 interface facilitated dislocation transfer during deformation, reducing stress
concentration and promoting uniform deformation. Additionally, deformation mechanisms such as stacking
faults and trace twinning contributed to the alloy ’ s excellent ductility, ensuring substantial work-hardening
capability and achieving a remarkable strength-ductility combination. These findings not only offer an effec-
tive strategy for enhancing the mechanical performance of non-equiatomic MEAs but also deepen our under-
standing of synergistic strengthening and deformation mechanisms in alloys.
1. Introduction
The rapid advancement in aerospace, marine industry, national de-
fense, and other fields requires the development of advanced structural
materials. The pursuit of developing and fabricating high-strength and
tough materials remains a perpetual endeavor for researchers. High/
medium entropy alloys (H/MEAs) have attracted significant attention
from researchers due to their broader composition design principles and
excellent mechanical properties [ 1 – 9 ]. Among these, due to the
remarkable combination of extremely high fracture toughness and
ductility with outstanding corrosion resistance, single-phase face--
centered cubic (FCC) CoCrNi MEAs have attracted considerable atten-
tion. However, the insufficient yield strength limits the application and
development of CoCrNi MEAs in engineering applications. Therefore,
enhancing the strength of CoCrNi MEAs without compromising ductility
significantly is a primary research focus in the materials field [ 10 – 14 ].
Various strengthening mechanisms, including solid-solution
strengthening [ 15 , 16 ], precipitation strengthening [ 17 , 18 ],
grain-boundary strengthening [ 19 ], dislocation strengthening [ 20 ],
twinning-induced plasticity [ 21 , 22 ], and transformation-induced plas-
ticity [ 23 , 24 ], are effective strategies for enhancing the strength of
H/MEAs. Among these, precipitation strengthening has been widely
recognized as particularly effective [ 25 – 27 ]. Precipitates impede dislo-
cation movement, thereby enhancing the material ’ s work-hardening
capacity. The type, size, and distribution of precipitation significantly
influence the efficacy of precipitation strengthening [ 28 ]. Introducing
brittle phases such as the Laves phase [ 29 ], μ phase [ 30 ], and σ phase
[ 31 ] as secondary phases in FCC matrix alloy can notably enhance the
strength of the alloys, albeit often at the expense of ductility. Adding
trace amounts of Al, Ti, and Ta elements to induce the L1
2
phase in
CoCrNi/CoCrFeNi matrix alloys is considered the most promising
approach to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off. Zhang et al. [ 32 ]
systematically investigated the impact of Ni and Al ratios on HEAs. The
findings reveal that a Ni/Al ratio exceeding 5 promotes the generation of
L1
2
to the maximum extent. Furthermore, the maximum volume fraction
reaches 33 %, resulting in superior comprehensive mechanical

strategies for the development of high-performance alloys.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Alloy design
Recent studies have indicated that CoCrNi MEAs, compared to qua-
ternary CoCrFeNi HEAs or quinary CoCrFeMnNi HEAs, demonstrate
superior mechanical properties at low and room temperatures due to
their lower stacking fault energy [ 35 , 47 ]. Therefore, CoCrNi MEAs have
been selected as the matrix alloy. Despite exhibiting excellent tensile
strength and ductility, their application as engineering materials is
limited by their low yield strength [ 48 , 49 ]. The addition of Ti, with its
larger atomic radius compared to Co, Cr, and Ni atoms, induces signif-
icant lattice distortion, thereby enhancing the solid solution strength-
ening of the alloy. Furthermore, Ti element acts as a nucleating agent for
L1
2
precipitates; even trace amounts of Ti element promote the pre-
cipitation of these precipitates. Chang et al. [ 50 ] observed that when the
Ti content exceeds 4 at.%, hexagonal close-packed (HCP) phases pre-
cipitate, adversely affecting the alloy ’ s ductility. Consequently, the alloy
composition CoCrNiTi
0.1
has been determined based on these
considerations.
2.2. Alloy fabrication
The CoCrNiTi
0.1
MEA with a nominal composition was fabricated
through vacuum melting using a mixture of constituent metal elements
(purity > 99.95 wt%) under an argon atmosphere with Zr-gettered
material. Electromagnetic stirring was employed, and the melting pro-
cess was repeated 6 times to ensure a homogenous composition. Sub-
sequently, the alloy underwent multiple passes of rolling in a two-high
mill, reducing its thickness to 8 mm. A solid solution treatment was then
carried out at 1200
◦
C for 2 h. Following this, the alloy was subjected to
further rolling passes until achieving a thickness of 1.6 mm, representing
a final deformation of 80 %. After cold rolling, samples were annealed at
temperatures of 700
◦
C, 800
◦
C, and 900
◦
C for 1 h each. Throughout
the text, the annealed samples are denoted as CR-700, CR-800, and CR-
900, respectively.

4. Discussion
In this study, a non-equiatomic CoCrNiTi
0.1
MEA was designed to
systematically investigate the microstructure evolution and related
mechanical properties at different heat treatment temperatures
following cold rolling. Microstructure diagrams in different states were
plotted to better understand these evolutions, as presented in Fig. 6 .
After cold rolling, as depicted in Fig. 6 (a), the microstructure exhibits
elongated grains along the rolling direction, accompanied by the accu-
mulation of high-density dislocations. These dislocations act as nuclei
for subsequent recrystallization and precipitation phases. Additionally,
significant shear bands are observed, resulting from uneven micro-
deformation during cold rolling. The high dislocation density within
these shear zones promotes localized recrystallization. During annealing
at 700
◦
C, grain growth occurs primarily in the recrystallized regions,
forming FG areas and establishing a HGS consisting of UFGs and FGs, as
illustrated in Fig. 6 (b) . Within the grains, a high density of spherical L1
2
precipitates and strip η precipitates is evident. Subsequent annealing at
700
◦
C facilitates the grain boundary migration. The abundant grain
boundaries provide a large number of nucleation sites, accelerating the
short-range diffusion of solute atoms, and promoting precipitation at the
grain boundaries. The precipitation of the L1
2
phase and η phase im-
mobilizes the grain boundary and hinders grain refinement. Upon
annealing at 800
◦
C, grain growth and coarsening of precipitates are
observed, as evidenced by Fig. 6 (c) and (d). The results in reduced
strength of CR-800 and CR-900 alloys, albeit with a partial improvement
in ductility. By optimizing the annealing temperature, the CR-700 alloy
demonstrates outstanding comprehensive mechanical properties at
room temperature, achieving a σ
y
of 1575 MPa and a ε
TE
of 15.3 %. To
further elucidate the exceptional performance of the CR-700 alloy, an
analysis of its strengthening mechanism and deformation mechanism
was conducted.
4.1. The formation of precipitation
The nucleation of HEAs precipitates occurs due to a supersaturated
solid solution formed by solute atoms dissolving in the matrix, primarily
influenced by alloy composition and heat treatment temperature. The
phase composition of the CoCrNiTi
x
(0 < x < 5 at.%) alloy was predicted
using ThermoCalc software and the CALPHAD method based on solution
thermodynamics, as shown in Fig. 7 . Between 700 and 900
◦
C, the alloy
reaches a triple phase equilibrium state of FCC + L1
2
+ η , consistent with
our experimental findings. During cold rolling, direct annealing leads to
simultaneous precipitation of precipitates and recrystallization [ 53 ].
The high-density shear zones introduced during cold rolling provide
numerous nucleation sites for precipitation and recrystallization of
precipitates [ 33 ]. At 700
◦
C, grain boundary migration sweeps the su-
persaturated FCC solid solution forward after recovery. The grain
boundary serves as a short-circuit diffusion path for Ni and Ti solute
atoms, resulting in the precipitation of strip η precipitates at the grain
boundaries [ 54 ]. These precipitates stabilize the grain boundaries,
facilitating further thermal activation and migration of grain bound-
aries. Elastic coherent stress at the interface with the solute-rich matrix
induces solute migration and growth of η precipitates. Previous studies
indicate that the thermal activation of grain boundary migration and the
presence of a supersaturated solid solution are critical for discontinuous
precipitation [ 55 ]. Additionally, the FCC matrix is supersaturated,
precipitating a uniform L1
2
precipitate before the recrystallization
process, thereby preventing discontinuous precipitation during recrys-
tallization and promoting the formation of spherical L1
2
precipitate
[ 54 ]. Annealing at higher temperatures leads to grain growth and
coarsening of the precipitated phases.

4.3. Deformation mechanism
To further understand the deformation mechanisms of ultra-high
strength alloys, detailed TEM characterization was conducted on CR-
700 alloy after fracture, as depicted in Fig. 9 . Fig. 9 (a) illustrates the
presence of high-density dislocations in the fractured alloy. These dis-
locations tend to entangle, forming dislocation clusters, as illustrated in
Fig. 9 (b). Moreover, abundant stacking faults (SFs) were observed and
confirmed through high-resolution TEM (HRTEM), Fourier transform,
and inverse Fourier transform analyses, depicted in Fig. 9 (c – e).
Furthermore, Fig. 9 (f) shows the presence of deformation twins (DTs),
which act as fast pathways for dislocation glide, thereby facilitating
plastic deformation by impeding dislocation motion and enhancing their
pile-up capacity [ 72 ]. The excellent ductility of CR-700 alloy can be
attributed to several factors. Firstly, during plastic deformation, the
generation of high-density SFs and additional DTs enhance the
work-hardening ability of the alloy, delaying necking and fracture.
These defects contribute positively to the alloy ’ s work-hardening and
ductility. Secondly, the L1
2
phase minimizes the accumulation of elastic
strain due to dislocation shearing, thereby preventing crack initiation at
phase boundaries and promoting ductility. Therefore, the
non-equiatomic CoCrNiTi
0.1
alloy maintains excellent high strength and
ductility even after annealing at 700
◦
C for 1 h, achieving a combination
of high strength and outstanding ductility.

In the present work, we fabricated a CoCrNiTi
0.1
MEA and processed
it through cold rolling followed by annealing at temperatures ranging
from 700 to 900
◦
C for 1 h. Through comprehensive analysis of micro-
structure evolution, mechanical properties, and strengthening and
deformation mechanisms, the following conclusions can be drawn:
(1) After severe plastic deformation and annealing at temperatures
between 700
◦
C and 900
◦
C for 1 h, the heterogeneous structures
comprising ultra-fine grain, fine grain, nanoscale spherical L1
2
precipitate, and strip η precipitate were achieved. As the
annealing temperature increases, the grain size gradually in-
creases, and the L1
2
and η precipitates exhibit growth and
coarsening.
(2) Mechanical testing at room temperature revealed that as the
annealing temperature increased, the strength and hardness of
the CR-700 alloy gradually decreased, while ductility corre-
spondingly increased. The CR-700 alloy exhibited an excellent
combination of strength and ductility.
(3) The yield strength of CR-700 MEA is primarily attributed to back
stress strengthening (495 MPa), grain boundary strengthening
(480 MPa), and precipitation strengthening (385 MPa). The main
deformation mechanism observed in the CR-700 MEA involved
dislocation slip, high-density stacking faults, and partial defor-
mation twins.
