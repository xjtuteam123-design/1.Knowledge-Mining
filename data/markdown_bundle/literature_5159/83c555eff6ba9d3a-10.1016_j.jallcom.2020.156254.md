abstract
Four multi-elemental Nb eSi based ultrahigh temperature alloys with nominal compositions of Nb e22Ti
e15Sie5Cre5Moe4Zre3Al-2Hf-xV( x ¼ 0, 3, 5 and 10, respectively) (at.%) were prepared by vacuum
non-consumable arc-melting. The effects of V addition on the microstructure, room temperature fracture
toughness and microhardness, and compressive performance and isothermal oxidation behavior at
1250
/C14 C of the alloys have been investigated. The results reveal that the microstructures of four alloys are
all comprised of primary g(Nb,X)5Si3, Nbss/g(Nb,X)5Si3 eutectic and Nbss/ g(Nb,X)5Si3/Cr2Nb three-phase
eutectic. However, the size, morphology and the amounts of constituent phases obviously vary with
increase in V content in the alloys. The microhardness of
g(Nb,X)5Si3 slightly increases with increase in V
content in the alloys, while that of Nbss shows an opposite trend due to the solid solution softening effect
of V addition. The room temperature fracture toughness is the highest at 3 at.% V addition, and then
obviously reduces at higher content of V addition. Both compression strength and oxidation resistance at
1250
/C14 C of the alloys have been degraded by V addition. However, alloying with V obviously improves the
adhesion of oxide scale of Nb eSi based ultrahigh temperature alloys.

1. Introduction
Currently, aimed at meeting the demands of higher thrust to
weight ratio of aircraft engines, the materials of their hot sections
have to withstand the temperature above 1200 /C14 C, which is close to
the melting point of the nickel based superalloys currently in use
[1]. Among various high temperature structure materials, the
NbeSi based in situ composites are one of the most promising al-
ternatives due to their lower densities (6.6 e7.2 g/cm3) and excel-
lent high temperature strength [ 1,2]. The microstructures of these
alloys mainly consist of hard-brittle intermetallic Nb 5Si3 or Nb 3Si
which can provide high temperature creep strength and the ductile
phase Nb solid solution (Nbss) which offers the room temperature
fracture toughness [ 1e3]. Nevertheless, the insuf ﬁcient room
temperature fracture toughness and poor high temperature
oxidation resistance of Nb eSi based alloys are the bottleneck of its
application and development [ 1e3]. Among various methods to
improve the comprehensive properties of Nb eSi based alloys,
alloying is the most common and effective way considering the
relationship of component, synthesis, microstructure and property
[1e3].
During the last three decades, the effects of B, C, Al, Ti, V, Cr, Fe,
Co, Ge, Zr, Mo, Sn, Hf, Ta and W additions on microstructure, phase
selection, mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of Nb eSi
based alloys have been investigated [ 4e13]. The alloy ’s develop-
ment has been extended from Nb eSi binary system to multi-
elemental systems [ 1e3]. The additions of Ti and Cr can amelio-
rate the oxidation resistance of alloys due to formation of TiNb 2O7
and CrNbO 4 which possess relatively suitable value of Pilling-
Bedworth ratio (PBR), while excessive addition of Ti will decrease
the melting point of alloys and thus deteriorate the high temper-
ature creep-rupture strength of the composites [ 4,5,14]. Adding
excessive Cr in the alloys will promote the formation of brittle
Cr2Nb phase that often locates at grain boundaries and results in
poor room temperature fracture toughness [ 4,15,16]. Alloying with
Zr and Hf in Nb eSi based alloys will stabilize the g(Nb,X)5Si3 phase
and improve both room temperature and high temperature
strength by solid solution strengthening [ 7,17].
Recently, alloying with V in NbeSi based alloys has drawn much
attention due to its lower ductile brittle transition temperature
(DBTT, < 298 K) which can reduce the cleavage fracture tendency of
the alloys [ 11,18e22]. However, up to now, the investigation of V* Corresponding author.

ternary or quaternary systems. Although a few studies of V addition
in multi-elemental Nb eSi based alloys have been reported, these
literatures primarily focus on a narrow range of V addition content
[18e20,23]. For instance, Guo et al. [ 20] reported that adding 1 at.%

Thus, the motivation for this work is to systematically investigate
the effects of V content in a wide range on the microstructure and
properties of multi-elemental Nb eSi based ultrahigh temperature
alloys. In the present work, the microstructure, room temperature
fracture toughness and microhardness, and compressive behavior

2. Experimental procedures
Four multi-elemental NbeSi based ultrahigh temperature alloys
with nominal compositions of
Nbe22Tie15Sie5Cre5Moe4Zre3Al-2Hf-xV( x ¼ 0, 3, 5 and 10,
respectively) (at.%) were prepared by vacuum non-consumable arc-
melting under an argon atmosphere in a water cooled copper
crucible. For convenience, the four alloys are referred to 0 V, 3 V, 5 V
and 10 V alloys here after in this paper, respectively. The raw ma-
terials used in this investigation were the commercial Nb, Ti, Si, Cr,
Al, Hf, Mo, V, Zr blocks or sponges which had been picked and the
possible oxide ﬁlms on their surfaces were removed by washing
them in acid or alkali solution. In order to ensure the same external
conditions during solidi ﬁcation, the water pressure in the internal
wall of the copper crucible and argon pressure in the chamber of
vacuum non-consumable arc-melting furnace were maintained
constant.
The cubic specimens with the size of 7 /C2 7 /C2 7m m3 were taken
from the center of button ingots by electro discharge machining
(EDM) for the microstructure analysis and oxidation test.
The compositions of each ingot were measured by energy
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS, Inca X-sight) and were presented in
Table 1. The measured region was a 2 /C2 2m m 2 rectangle in the
specimen for microstructure analysis. The results suggested that
the compositions of each ingot were essentially in agreement with
their nominal compositions.
The oxidation tests were performed at 1250 /C14 C in an open-
ended tube electric furnace. The furnace was ﬁrstly heated to
450 /C14 C with a rate of 10 /C14 C/min, then heated up to 1250 /C14 C with a
rate of 27 /C14 C/min and kept at this temperature for 1 or 20 h. Finally,
the specimens were cooled down to room temperature with a rate
of 5 /C14 C/min. The dimensions of cubic specimens were measured
using a vernier caliper with an accuracy of 0.02 mm. The mass
changes of the specimens were weighed in an electronic balance
with an accuracy of 0.001 g.
The room temperature toughness (K Q) of the alloys was
measured by single-edge notched bending test using an electric
universal mechanical testing machine (Instron-3382). The single-
edge notched bending specimens with dimension of
2.5 /C2 5 /C2 30 mm3 were prepared by EDM, and the notch was with a
depth of 2.5 ± 0.1 mm. Three specimens from each alloy ingot were
measured and their average value was explored. The microhard-
ness of phases was measured by an HXP-1000TM hardness ma-
chine under a load of 0.98 N or 0.25 N for 15s. The microhardness
values were the average of at least 5 indentations.
The compressive strength of alloys at 1250 /C14 C was tested in a
Gleeble 3500 testing machine at a strain rate of 0.005 s /C0 1, the
height reduction was 30%. The temperature of machine rose with a
rate of 3 /C14 C/s and then was held at 1250 /C14 C for 3 min before
compression. The compressive specimens were a cylinder with
8 mm in diameter and 12 mm in length.
X-ray diffraction analysis (XRD, Panalytical X ’Pert Pro, Cu K a)
and micro-area X-ray diffraction analysis (XRD, Bruker D8 Discover,
Co K a) were used to identify the constituent phases. Microstruc-
tures of the specimens were observed by scanning electron mi-
croscopy (SEM, Tescan Vega 3 LMU). The chemical compositions of
the constituent phases were analyzed by energy dispersive spec-
troscopy (EDS, Inca X-sight). Quantitative metallographic analysis
of the amount and size of the constituent phases and the thickness
of scales of oxidation samples were performed using Image-Pro
Plus version 6.0 high resolution imaging analysis software.

that external morphology of a crystal depends on its intrinsic
crystal structure, external growth conditions and solute distribu-
tion (i.e. solute concentration gradient) in multi-elemental alloy
systems [ 24]. Since the cooling conditions of four alloys in this
paper are similar, the external growth conditions are not the main
factors affecting the morphological changes. Therefore, the mor-
phologies of primary g(Nb,X)5Si3 blocks are mainly affected by
solute distribution which can destroy the stability of solid-liquid
interface by causing constitutional supercooling and ﬁnally affect
the morphologies of crystals. According to the solidi ﬁcation theory,
the classical constitutional supercooling criterion can be written as
G < m$Gc, where G is the temperature gradient in melt, m is the
slope of liquidus in the phase diagram of the alloys, and G c is the
concentration gradient of alloying elements [ 25]. Under the same

3.2. Room temperature fracture toughness
The average K Q values of room temperature fracture toughness
of the alloys with different V contents are given in Fig. 4 . The K Q
value of alloys ﬁrstly increases and then decreases with increase in
their V content. The highest K Q value is presented in 3 V alloy
(10.25 MPa m1/2 in average). Fig. 5 shows the fractographies of the
alloys with different V contents. It is observed that these fractog-
raphies are mainly composed of cleavage facets and tearing ridges,
suggesting that all the four alloys rupture in a quasi-cleavage failure
mode. According to the EDS analysis ( Table 4) and the features of
phases in original microstructure of arc-melted condition, it can be
conﬁrmed that the large smooth cleavage facets are from the
rupture of primary g(Nb,X)5Si3 blocks. Small cleavage facets
showing a river pattern are from the rupture of Nbss dendrites. It is
noteworthy that partial tearing ridges show a similar composition
with Nbss (site 1, Table 4 ), while another part exhibits a close
composition to that of silicide (site 2, Table 4). Considering the facts
that tearing ridges are formed by the plastic split between small
cleavage facets, it is thus reasonable to speculate that these tearing
ridges in multi-elemental Nb eSi based alloys are from the rupture
of ﬁne eutectics. In the overview of four fractographies in Fig. 5,i ti s

The volume fraction, size as well as geometry of phases in the

the size of primary silicide blocks, which is propitious to room
temperature fracture toughness of alloys, However, adding V also
increases the amounts of primary silicides and Nbss dendrites, as
shown in Table 3, that show a cleavage facets in the fractographies

will inﬂuence the room temperature fracture toughness of alloys in

adding V results in the increase in the amount of primary silicides
and especially Nbss dendrites, which dominates the deterioration
of room temperature fracture toughness of the alloys with higher V
content. Additionally, the three-phase eutectic containing brittle
Cr2Nb phase, which is mainly located in the connected areas of
eutectic cells. Its amount also shows an increase with increase in V
content in the alloys. This will dominate the cracks to grow along
the boundaries of eutectic cells, and bring about a negative effect on
the room temperature fracture toughness in higher V content alloys
[12,15,28,31].
3.3. Microhardness
Table 5 gives the microhardness values of phases presented in
these alloys. It is observed that the microhardness of primary
g(Nb,X)5Si3 shows an increase trend with increase in V content in
the alloys. According to EDS analysis results ( Table 2), the increase
in microhardness of silicide is maybe attributed to the increased
concentrations of V and Ti in silicide, having an effect of solid so-
lution strengthening [ 32]. However, the microhardness of Nbss
exhibits decrease with increase in V content in the alloys. It has
been reported that solid solution softening is commonly observed
in the group-V (Nb, Ta) and group-VI (Mo, W) b. c. c. metals at room
temperature [33e35]. Some literatures [ 36,37] also revealed that V
addition in NbSi 2 and MoSi2 alloys would reduce the hardness and
compressive yield strength at room temperature. It is therefore
reasonably to infer that the decreased microhardness of Nbss is also
attributed to the solid solution softening from V addition. The
explanation of solid solution softening in b. b. c. metals is still highly
controversial [33e37]. Clustering of the solutes is thought to be the
predominant reason for solid solution softening in VA group (Nb,
Ta) [35,38e40].

morphology of arc-melted condition, suggesting a strong defor-
mation resistance of Nbss at 1250 /C14 C. Therefore, it can be concluded
that the reduction of compressive strength is primarily due to the
softening of Nbss by V addition, which is in agreement with the
decrease in microhardness of Nbss, as mentioned in section 3.3.
Additionally, the primary silicide blocks in four alloys distribute
along the direction vertical to the compression axis and some
cracks have indicated within them, as shown by the arrows, illus-
trating that the primary silicide blocks have been squeezed by Nbss
during compression at 1250 /C14 C. This con ﬁrms that the primary
silicide blocks have the obstacle role for the deformation of Nbss.
However, the reﬁnement in primary silicide blocks by V addition, as
mentioned in section 3.1, possibly reduces this ability to obstruct
deformation of Nbss. As discussed above, the softening of Nbss and
reﬁnement of primary silicide blocks are mainly responsible for the
decrease in compressive strength at 1250 /C14 C of the alloys by V
addition.
3.5. Oxidation behavior of the alloys
Fig. 8 shows the macrographs and mass gains per unit area of
the alloys with different V contents after oxidation at 1250 /C14 C for 1
and 20 h, respectively. The scales of 0 V alloy partially spalled
during cooling after holding at 1250 /C14 C for 1 and 20 h, while the
scales of V-containing alloys well attached to substrate, indicating
an improvement of the adhesion of scales by V addition, as shown
in Fig. 8. However, the V-containing alloys with well adhered scales
exhibit a deteriorated oxidation resistance, since both the mass
gains per unit area of alloys after oxidation for 1 and 20 h,
respectively, obviously increase from 1 1.2 and 30.3 mg/cm2 to 140.1
and 302.8 mg/cm 2 with increase in V content from 0 to 10 at.% in
the alloys. Interestingly, it is obvious that the crucibles used to place
V-containing alloys exhibit an orange morphology after oxidation,
which is different from the crucible employed for 0 V alloy that
shows white morphology. EDS analysis of crucibles shows that
these orange crucibles have been contaminated by an amount of V
(~10.2 at.% for the crucible used for 5 V alloy, Fig. 9). This indicates
that V 2O5 possibly has formed in the oxide scales of V-containing
alloys. The oxide V 2O5 would melt and ﬂow into crucibles at
1250 /C14 C for its low melting point (~675 /C14 C) [42e44].
The scales of the alloys after oxidation at 1250 /C14 C for 1 h were
striped and grounded into powders for phases identi ﬁcation by
XRD test, as shown in Fig. 10. The constituent oxides of scales of 0 V
alloy are mainly TiNb 2O7 (JCPDS, 70 e2009), Nb 2O5 (JCPDS,
70e2679) and TiO 2 (JCPDS, 77 e0446). For V-containing alloys, in
addition to those oxides, the SiO 2 (JCPDS, 81 e1232), CrVNbO 6
(JCPDS, 36e0788) and Nb 9VO25 (JCPDS, 49e2289) oxides also form
due to V addition in the alloys.

According to above discussions, the oxidation behavior of V-
containing alloys can be summarized as follows. At the initial stage
of oxidation, the alloying elements were rapidly oxidized to form
simple oxides. These simple oxides then combined together and
formed the complex oxides (TiNb 2O7, CrNbO 4,N b 9VO25 and
CrVNbO6). Once the oxides covered the surface of alloys, the pro-
cess of O 2- transporting through the outer layer of scales became
the key step of oxidation. Thereafter, O 2- diffused into substrate via
the interface of Nbss/silicide and drove the elements Hf, Zr and Ti to
diffuse outward. This ﬁnally resulted in the formation of (Hf, Zr)O 2
and (Ti,X)O2, as shown in the internal oxidation zones ( Fig. 12). As
the oxidation process extending, the oxides Nb 2O5 would be
formed and took participate in forming TiNb 2O7 and CrNbO 4.
Additionally, based on the observation and EDS analysis of crucibles
used to place alloys, it can be con ﬁrmed that V 2O5 would form in
the scales of V-containing alloys and rapidly melted due to its lower
melting point. Then, partial liquid V 2O5 took participate in the
formation of CrVNbO 6 and Nb 9VO25 in the scales, but the residual
V2O5 ﬂowed into crucibles and caused some holes in the scales, as
shown in Fig. 1 1 bed. Consequentially, O 2- easily penetrated
through the scales and resulted in serious oxidation of V-containing
alloys. Meanwhile, these holes simultaneously released the stress
generated during oxidation and cooling in the scales, and improved
the adhesion of scales.
4. Conclusions
In this paper, the effects of V addition on the microstructure,
mechanical and oxidation behavior of multi-elemental Nb eSi
based ultrahigh temperature alloys have been revealed. The main
conclusions can be drawn as follows:
(1) Four multi-elemental alloys with V contents from 0 to 10 at.%
are mainly composed of primary g(Nb,X)5Si3, Nbss/
g(Nb,X)5Si3 eutectic and Nbss/ g(Nb,X)5Si3/Cr2Nb three-
phase eutectic. More Nbss dendrites and H-shaped primary
g(Nb,X)5Si3 with small size form in the alloys with higher V
content. The addition of V in the alloys promote the forma-
tion of three-phase eutectic. V primarily dissolves in Nbss.
Additionally, alloying with V slightly increases the concen-
trations of Ti and Cr in Nbss, and the concentration of Ti in
silicide, while decreases the concentrations of Ti and Cr in
three-phase eutectic.
(2) All the alloys rupture in a brittle quasi-cleavage mode, and
the lower V addition (3 at.%) improves the fracture toughness
while the higher V addition (5 and 10 at.%) degrades it. The
microhardness of
g(Nb,X)5Si3 increases with increase in V
content, but the addition of V obviously decreases the
microhardness of Nbss due to the solid solution softening.
The compressive strength of the alloy at 1250 /C14 C shows a
decrease trend with increase in V content.
(3) Both the mass gains per unit area and the thickness of the
scales obviously increase with increase in V content after
oxidation at 1250 /C14 C for 1 and 20 h. The scales are well
adhered to the alloy substrate for V-containing alloys. The
oxidation products in the scales of V-containing alloys are
mainly TiNb 2O7, CrNbO 4, (Ti,X)O 2,N b 9VO25, CrVNbO 6, and
SiO2. The formation of liquid V 2O5 causes lots of holes in the
scales, which is responsible for the deteriorated oxidation
resistance and the well adhesion of scales of the V-contain-
ing alloys.
