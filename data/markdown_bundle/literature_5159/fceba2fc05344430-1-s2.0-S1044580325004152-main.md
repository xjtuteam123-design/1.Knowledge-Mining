◦

strength was closely related to the ZrB
2

size of Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy resulted from ZrB
2 
was beneficial to the improvement of the strength at 1100 
◦
C but

◦

◦

1200 
◦

promoted the increase of compressive strength at 1000 – 1200

2. Materials and methods
Mo-12Si-8.5B alloys with 0 wt%, 1.5 wt% and 2.5 wt% ZrB
2 
were 
produced by powder metallurgy from 99.9 % Mo, 99.9 % Si, 99.6 % B 
and 99.3 % ZrB
2 
in purity. The PM processing started with elemental 
powder mixing and after that mechanical alloying was performed in a 
planetary ball mill (Retsch machine, PM 400). The mechanical alloying 
was carried out for a duration of 15 h with a speed of 250 rpm under 
protective argon atmosphere, and WC-ball to powder weight ratio was 
fixed at 10:1. The mechanical alloyed powders were then consolidated 
using hot pressing in vacuum ( < 10
 3 
Pa) in a 60-mm diameter graphite 
die at 1600 
◦
C and 49 MPa for 2 h and then heat treated at 1600 for 2 h. 
The sintered Mo-12Si-8.5B alloys with 0 wt%, 1.5 wt% and 2.5 wt% ZrB
2 
were named as MSB, MSB-1.5 and MSB-2.5 alloys. Additionally, the 
sintered billet of Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy with 1.5 wt% ZrB
2 
was further heat 
treated at 1700 
◦
C for 2 h in vacuum to obtain similar grain size and 
scale microstructures with MSB alloy, named as MSB-1.5-H alloy.
High temperature compressive tests were carried out on thermo 
mechanical simulator (Gleeble-1500D, DSI, America) under three 
different deformation temperatures (1100 
◦
C, 1200 
◦
C and 1300 
◦
C) in 
vacuum and strain rates of 1 × 10
 3 
s
 1 
was used. The tested samples 
were compressed till a true strain of 0.35. For compressive testing, 
cylinder-shaped specimens with ø 11 × 5.5 mm were prepared using 
electrical discharge machining. The specimens were grinded using 1200- 
grit size SiC papers and were subsequently ultrasonically cleaned in 
ethanol.
The microstructure of various Mo-12Si-8.5B-ZrB
2 
alloys was inves -
tigated using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-6700F) and 
selected samples were characterized by JEM-ARM200CF NEOARM 
aberration-corrected TEM (ACTEM) and transmission electron micro -
scopy (TEM, JEM-3010) combined with selected area electron diffrac -
tion (SAED). Samples for SEM investigations were ground, polished and 
etched in modified reagent (7 g potassium ferricyanide, 2 g NaOH in 
100 mL water) to reveal the phase structure. The TEM samples were 
prepared by JIB 4000 focused ion beam (FIB). After the compressive 
tests, the microstructure and dislocation patterns were investigated by 
ACTEM and TEM. The detailed microstructures and dislocation config -
urations after compression tests were further characterized by electron 
back scatter diffraction (EBSD) using a ZEISS MERLIN Compact SEM. 
The operating voltage was 20 kV and the scan step was 0.4 μ m.

4. Discussion
The addition of ZrB
2 
significantly facilitates the improvement of 
high-temperature strength of Mo-Si-B alloys. Notably, its strengthening 
effect is more obvious at higher temperature in comparison to lower 
temperature, which is mainly related to the deformation behaviors of 
alloys at different temperature.
The deformation behavior of Mo-Si-B alloy consists of the dislocation 
movement and sliding of grain boundary [ 20 ]. At 1100 
◦
C, the defor -
mation of the alloy seems mainly relies on dislocation slip. Therefore, 
compared with the coarse-grained MSB-1.5-H alloy, the fine-grained 
MSB-1.5 alloy exhibits higher yield strength and compressive strength 
since fine-grained alloy containing more grain boundaries which will 
impede dislocation movement effectively, thereby strengthening the 
alloy. The TEM result of the deformed MSB-1.5 sample after compressed 
at 1100 
◦
C shows that dislocation pile-up observed at the gain boundary 
( Fig. 5 (d)), which result in the obvious grain boundary strengthening 
effect. Due to this strengthening effect, MSB-2.5 alloy with finest grains 
even exhibits highest yield strength. However, as the temperature in -
creases to 1200 
◦
C and 1300 
◦
C, the atomic diffusion increases and the 
glutinousness at grain boundaries decreases due to thermal activation, 
thereby promoting grain boundary sliding and leading to the dimin -
ishment of fine-grain strengthening [ 21 ]. In this case, too many grain 
boundaries are not conducive to the alloy strength at higher tempera -
ture. Thus fine-grained MSB-1.5 alloy exhibits lower strength due to its 
more grain boundary areas compared with the coarse-grained MSB-1.5- 
H alloy at 1200 
◦
C and 1300 
◦
C ( Fig. 3 (b) and (c)). However, at these 
higher temperatures, the particle strengthening effect induced by Zr-rich 
particles is prominent, and the substantial increase in the strength of 
MSB-1.5-H alloy with ZrB
2 
addition at 1200 
◦
C and 1300 
◦
C clearly 
supports such evidence.

MSB-1.5-H alloy observed after compressed at 1200

as well as the corresponding EDS results. At this stage along with work- 
hardening, significant changes occur in the microstructure, as the 
equiaxed α -Mo phase is elongated, while the intermetallic phases remain 
unchanged and retain their equiaxed shape ( Fig. 4 (a)). α -Mo experi -

while Mo
3

5

2

without obvious dislocations. It can be seen that dislocations pinning by 
the intragranular Zr-rich particles can be observed commonly in the 
TEM images, and moreover the dislocations near the intragranular 
particles usually exhibit very curved shapes showing strong interactions 
between them and the nanosized Zr-rich particles ( Fig. 4 (b) and (c)), 
which is a reliable evidence of particle strengthening. High-resolution 
TEM (HRTEM) image of the deformed α -Mo gain is shown in Fig. 4 (d). 
Lattice distortion induced by dislocation defects are visible within the 
image, where noticeable stress concentrations along with lattice 
distortion arising from α -Mo deformation are also observed ( Fig. 4 (e)). 
Additionally, the pinning of Zr-rich particles at triple junctions from the 
grains and at the grain interfaces can be clearly observed in the TEM 
images ( Fig. 4 (f) and (g)), indicating that intergranular particles are 
effective in retarding the grain boundary sliding during hot deformation. 
Therefore, the Zr-rich particles strengthening is thought mainly results 
from two concurrently acting processes, on the one hand, the intra -
granular particles serve as obstacles to movement of dislocations in the 
Mo gain interior, and on the other hand, the intergranular particles 
could hinder the grain boundaries (GBs) sliding, similar with the 
strengthening effects of La
