ABSTRACT
Niobium alloys have excellent high-temperature, high strength, low density, and 
excellent processability, and is widely used in key components, such as combus-
tion chambers, engine blades, rocket and missile nozzles. However, these niobium 
alloys have poor oxidation resistance and are easily affected by "pest oxidation" in 
high-temperature oxidation environments, which greatly limits their application 
in the aerospace field. This work provides a comprehensive overview of the devel-
opment and application of niobium alloys, and the microstructure, mechanical 
properties and oxidation characteristics of three high-temperature niobium alloys 
(PWC-11, C-103, and Nb521) were discussed. In addition, the microstructure and 
phase composition evolution, oxidation behavior and failure mechanism behav-
ior of the surface anti-oxidation coatings on high temperature niobium alloys 
were also investigated. Finally, some suggestions and prospects for improving 
the mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of high temperature niobium 
alloys in aerospace applications were proposed.
Introduction
With the development of hypersonic vehicles and 
aero-engines, extreme environments during flight 
(high-temperature oxidation, thermal vibration, 
and high-temperature ablation) have become a chal-
lenge [1 , 2]. Therefore, developing new materials 
that integrate high strength and toughness, high-
temperature resistance, long service life, and oxida-
tion resistance for application in various key parts, 
such as engines, is an urgent task. Unfortunately, 
conventional Fe–Ni alloys are insufficient to meet the 
demands for prolonged use in high-temperature envi-
ronments [3 , 4]. In contrast, refractory metals with 
excellent high-temperature strength and oxidation 
resistance (W, Mo, Ta, Nb, etc.) can withstand tempera-
tures above 2000 °C [5–7]. Among these, the combina-
tion of excellent high-temperature strength and low 
density makes niobium alloys ( PWC-11, C-103, and 
Nb521 et. al) promising candidates for applications in 
the aerospace industry [8–11].

sity (8.57 g/cm3), high melting point (2477 °C), high-
temperature strength, and excellent machinability, and

Microstructure, mechanical properties 
and strengthening mechanisms
Additive manufacturing can increase the procurement 
ratio of niobium alloys raw materials and reduce mate-
rial waste. A number of niobium alloys additive manu-
facturing methods have been developed, using differ-
ent energy sources (laser, electron beam and electric 
arc) and different types of raw materials (wire, sheet 
and powder) [37, 38]. Electron beam melting and laser 
powder bed melting have become the most effective 
methods for the preparation of niobium alloys [39–42].

the mechanical and anti-oxidation properties of nio -

an effective approach for engineering applications of 
niobium alloys [21–27].

from long-term service in high temperature aerobic

substrate [28– 31]. For instance, Zhang et al. [32, 33]

Mechanical properties
Compared with other preparation methods, 
although the ductility of niobium alloys pre-
pared by LPBF is lower, the yield strength (YS) 
and ultimate tensile strength (UTS) are signifi-
cantly higher [39–42]. The impact of LPBF tech-
nique parameters on the mechanical properties of 
Nb alloys has additionally been stated in preced-
ing researches. For example, Griemsmann et al. [51] 
investigated the impact of diverse parameters on the 
microstructural changes and mechanical properties of 
Nb specimens. From Fig. 4a, e, it can be observed that 
the tensile fracture surface of the PC13 (120 W, 300 J/
mm3, 556 ppm) sample was primarily characterized 
by ductile dimples and locally layered structures. The 
fracture morphology confirmed the existence of duc-
tile and transgranular fractures. Furthermore, the YS 
and UTS of all PC specimens (Fig. 4i) ranged from 309 
to 324 MPa and from 444 to 525 MPa, respectively, and 
the elongation was within the range of 8% to 16%. Pure 
Nb prepared by LPBF has higher strength and lower 
toughness. Some researchers have pointed out that the 
oxygen content in LPBF materials is the reason for this 
phenomenon [52]. Liu et al. [46] investigated the influ-
ence of oxygen content and varying energy densities 
on the microstructural and mechanical properties of 
pure Nb. It was observed that when the energy den-
sity exceeded 146 J/mm3, the porosity increased with 
rising energy density, whereas plasticity gradually 
diminished. The Nb-1 (350 W, 146 J/mm3, 510 ppm) 
sample (Fig. 4b, f) exhibited a significant presence 
of small pores, with the predominant fracture mode 
being ductile fracture. Upon reaching an energy den-
sity of 243 J/mm3, a notable enlargement of the pores 
and the emergence of distinct cleavage steps were 
observed in the Nb-2(350W, 243 J/mm3, 510 ppm) sam-
ple (Fig. 4c, g). Regrettably, with increasing oxygen 
content, the fracture surface of the Nb–O (350 W, 146 J/
mm3, 2300 ppm) sample (Fig. 4d, h) displayed distinct 
cleavage steps, suggesting that the sample experienced 
brittle fracture. As the oxygen content increased, both 
the YS and UTS of the LPBF-built Nb alloys improved 
significantly, while their ductility decreased mark -
edly. Specifically, both YS and UTS increased by a big 
margin. Concurrently, the fracture elongation rate 
diminished from 26% to 1.7% as the oxygen density 
increased from 0.05% to 0.23%, as shown in Fig. 4j.
The pure niobium manufactured by AM methods 
has better mechanical properties and has been widely 
used, compared with the traditional process. However, 
the strength of pure niobium is drastically reduced, 
when the temperature exceeds 1000 °C. Accordingly, 
it is unable for long-term use in high-temperature 
environments [15– 17]. The addition of alloying ele-
ments is one of the effective ways to increase its ser-
vice temperature. Davidson et al. [53] demonstrated 
that the creep strength of the Nb-1Zr alloy at 1027 
℃ was superior to that of pure niobium. Sarkar et al. 
[54] investigated the hot deformation characteristics 
of Nb-1Zr-0.1C from 800 to 1700 ℃. It was found that 
the alloy did not undergo dynamic recrystalliza-
tion until above 1400 °C. Behera et al. [55, 56] inves-
tigated the effects of temperature and strain rate on 
the dynamic recrystallization behavior of Nb-1Zr and 
Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloys. During the range of 1500–1600 °C, 
the Nb-1Zr alloy exhibited dynamic recrystallization, 
as shown in Fig. 5a, b. In particular, when the strain 
rate increased from 0.01s −1 to 0.1s −1, the number of 
recrystallized grains with more uniform and fine 
grains increased significantly. Under the deforma -
tion conditions of 1500–1600 °C and the deformation 
rate of 0.1 s−1, the thermal deformation experiments 
of Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloys with deformation of 0.9 and 0.6 
were carried out, respectively. Compared to Nb-1Zr, 
the grain refinement in Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy was more 
pronounced, as presented in Fig. 5c, d.

Oxidation of Nb‑base alloys
The oxidation kinetics of pure niobium in air is lin-
ear oxidation, and an obvious "Pesting oxidation" 
phenomenon can be observed when the temperature 
exceeds 350℃ [63]. The oxide film does not adequately 
protect the substrate and tends to crack, exposing 
more of the substrate surface and worsening the oxi-
dation process [64]. A practical method to improve 
oxidation resistance is to incorporate suitable alloy-
ing elements [65, 66].
Microstructure
Borowski et al. [66] examined the oxidation process 
of pure Nb in the air at 425 °C, 450 °C and 500 °C for 
4 h. The results indicated that subsequent to oxidation 
at 425 °C, the irregular surface morphology (Fig. 12a) 
possesses pores and forms a thin oxide layer. How-
ever, no irregular structures like those on the surface 
were observed in the cross-section (Fig. 12d), indicat-
ing that the oxidation at this temperature did not cause 
serious damage to the inner structure of the base mate-
rial. In the subsequent oxidation processes at 450 °C 
and 500 °C, the sample surface (Fig. 12b, c) changed 
from a uniform spherical structure at 450 °C to an une-
ven block-like structure at 500 °C. Cracks appeared 
in the cross-section (Fig. 12 (e, f)) and expanded with 
increasing oxidation temperature. The average oxide 
thickness decreased by more than two times from 
22.56 ± 0.04 to 9.95 ± 1.22 μm. The authors pointed out 
that this is attributed to the pure niobium degradation 
and the generation of unprotected Nb2O5 oxide.
Niobium alloys exhibit poor oxidation resistance

Surface coating and oxidation behavior
Surface coating technology enhances the high-temper-
ature oxidation resistance of substrate metals while 
maintaining their mechanical properties [84]. In par-
ticular, siliconized coatings exhibit excellent thermal 
stability and operate at temperatures up to 1600 °C. 
This is because the SiO2 thin film on the surface of the 
coating acts as a diffusion barrier, with effect inhibit-
ing oxygen from penetrating the substrate. The low 
viscosity of SiO2 at elevated temperatures provides 
self-healing and anti-deformation capabilities [85]. 
However, traditional methods for preparing single 
coatings are not able to serve long-term in harsh envi-
ronments such as high temperature, thermal erosion, 
and ablation. Consequently, it is necessary to develop 
new processes for the preparation of modified and 
composite coatings through multi-step approaches.

Conclusions
This work offers an overview of pure niobium, PWC-
11, C-103, and Nb521 alloys in aerospace applications, 
highlighting the effects of various additive elements 
on their microstructure and mechanical properties, as 
well as the enhancement mechanisms. The effects of 
high temperatures on bare alloys have also been stud-
ied; however, reactions and oxides produced during 
high-temperature oxidation make these alloys unsuit-
able for long-term use. Finally, this paper reviews the 
protective efficacy of different oxidation-resistant coat-
ings on niobium-based alloys.
1. Additive manufacturing of niobium alloys demon-
strates superior strength and toughness, compared 
to traditional manufacturing methods. Meanwhile, 
PWC-11, C-103, and Nb521 alloys prepared by 
adding different elements also exhibit more out-
standing high-temperature mechanical properties. 
PWC-11 contains (Nb, Zr)C and Zr2C carbides, 
which produce precipitation strengthening. The 
addition of Ti and Hf significantly enhances the

2. Nb-based alloys undergo "pesting oxidation" at

the (Nb2O5 +  WO3) solid solution to inhibit the 
formation of Nb2O5. Zr, Ti and Hf act as preferred

and HfO2. However, the serious internal oxidation 
will appear when the continued increase in Hf con-

phase boundary. Therefore, the alloying approach 
is unable to strike a balance between oxidation 
resistance and mechanical properties.
3. Silicide coatings are the most commonly used 
on niobium alloys. The coating’s structure and 
composition significantly influence its oxidation 
resistance. Single coatings with simple structures 
are prone to defects at high temperatures, while 
modified and composite coatings effectively resist 
thermal corrosion. Additionally, silicide compos-
ite coatings prepared by a two-step method fea-
ture uniform composition and compact structure, 
allowing them to withstand erosion from oxy-acet-
ylene flames at 1800 ℃.
4. Adopting advanced preparation methods and syn-
ergistic alloy elements is crucial for enhancing the 
high-temperature mechanical properties of nio-
bium alloys. Additionally, integrating multi-step 
coating processes to develop modified and com-
posite coatings will address challenges related to 
single coatings, representing a key direction for 
future advancements in this field.
