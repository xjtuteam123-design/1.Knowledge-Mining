ABSTRACT 
Grain refinement of non -equiatomic Cr -rich CoCrFeMnNi high -entropy alloys with an 
fcc structure was studied focusing on the precipitation of a Cr -rich  phase along grain 
boundaries and shear bands. We found for the first time that the  phase can be 
precipitated at the grain boundaries and the shear bands in the alloys at a significantly fast 
precipitation rate. The  phase effectively suppressed the grain growth of the fcc matrix 
during recrystallization of the alloy after conventional cold rolling, resulting in a 
fine-grained microstructure with a mean diameter of approximately 1 m. The alloys 
with this fine microstructure containing the  phase exhibit high strength over a wide

temperature range due to grain boundary strengthening and precipitation strengthening 
caused by the  phase. Moreover, the alloys exhibit large ductility not only at room and 
high temperatures, but also at low temperature s. Deformation-induced twinning plays an 
important role in this ductility.

1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) are multicomponent alloys based on the novel alloy 
design concept of mixing a large number (5 or more) of principal elements in equiatomic 
or near equiatomic proportions. The term “high entropy alloys” was introduced by Yeh et 
al. [1], based on the hypothesis that high configurational entropy would stabilize the solid 
solution phase over the competing intermetallic phase. Several HEAs such as 
AlCoCrCuFeNi [1,2], AlCoCrFeNiTi [ 3], CoCrFeMnNi [ 4], VNbMoTaW [5] and 
TiNbTaZrMo [6] have been developed and well -studied, focusing on their microstructure 
and mechanical properties. These reports predict that HEAs are prime candidates for next 
generation st ructural materials in various fields such as chemical plants [ 7], nuclear 
power plants [ 8], aerospace applications [9 ] and biomedical implants [ 6], due to their 
excellent balance between strength and ductility [ 10,11], significant high -temperature 
strength [5], high wear resistance [12] and excellent fatigue properties [ 13]. In recent 
years, the deformation behavior of HEAs, including strengthening mechanism has also 
been examined. For instance, Yasuda et al. [14] and Ma et al. [15] succeeded in 
producing single crystals of Al 0.3CoCrFeNi alloys by the floating zone and the Bridgman 
methods, respectively, and reported on the deformation behavior of the alloys. Okamoto 
et al. [16] examined the deformation behavior of CoCrFeMnNi alloys by micro -pillar 
compression. Hong et al. [17] analyzed dislocation motion in CoCrFeMnNi alloys by 
thermally activated deformation analyses at low temperature s. Okamoto et al. [18] also 
investigated the mechanism of solid solution hardening in CoCrFeMnNi alloys by 
synchrotron X -ray diffraction study and first-principles calculation. These studies 
demonstrated that HEAs show exclusive behavior and properties compared to 
conventional alloys.

On the other hand, the mechanical properties of HEAs need to be improved to ensure 
the comple te safety of their application in the above -mentioned fields. In order to 
improve the mechanical properties of HEAs through microstructure control, some unique 
processes have been applied to them. Schuh et al. [19] and Shahmir et al. [20] evaluated 
the microstructure and mechanical properties of CoCrFeMnNi alloys subjected to severe 
plastic deformation (SPD) including high-pressure torsion (HPT). They found that it is 
possible to refine the grain s of the matrix to nanometer size using the HPT [19,20]. The 
alloys after HPT exhibit an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of approximately 1700 MPa 
and a tensile elongation (EL) of approximately 5% [ 20]. However, high process cost and 
limitation of the specimen size of the process are of significant concerns. Therefore, new 
techniques need to be established for microstructure control using conventional 
processes. 
In the previous study, we found that ultrafine -grained microstructure can be obtained 
in non-equiatomic Al0.3CoCrFeNi alloys by the combination of convent ional cold rolling 
and annealing due to rapid precipitation of the B2-type NiAl precipitates along the fcc 
grain boundaries [ 21]. Grain growth of the fcc matrix during recrystallization was 
inhibited by grain boundary pinning of the precipitates. As a result, the alloys prepared by 
the thermomechanical treatment exhibited a matrix grain size of 0.7 m, a high UTS 
above 1000 MPa and a large EL above 40% [ 21]. This report predicts that the grain 
boundary precipitation in non-equiatomic HEAs is effective in the refinement of the 
matrix grains and in the improvement of the strength -ductility balance. One of the 
candidate HEAs which can have a fine -grained microstructure by the combination of 
conventional cold rolling and annealing is CoCrFeMnNi alloy, becau se a tetragonal  
phase can be precipitated [ 22]. In addition, deviation from the equiatomic composition is

likely to accelerate the formation of the  phase, which is favorable for the grain 
refinement. 
In the present study , the microstructure of equiatom ic and non -equiatomic 
CoCrFeMnNi alloys subjected to conventional cold rolling followed by annealing were 
examined, focusing on the Cr content and the annealing condition. As a result, we found 
for the first time that fine -grained structure was formed in n on-equiatomic Cr -rich 
CoCrFeMnNi alloy due to the fast precipitation of the  phase during the recrystallization 
process after conventional cold rolling. The precipitation behavior of the  phase and the 
grain refinement mechanism of the alloys were also studied based on the quantitative 
analyses of the microstructure. The effect of the fine -grained microstructure on the 
mechanical properties was also discussed. 
 
2. Experimental 
Master ingots of Co 20-Crx-Fe20-Mn20-Ni40-x (X=10, 20, 25) (at.%) alloys were 
prepared by plasma arc melting using high -purity Co, Cr, Fe, Mn and Ni in an Ar 
atmosphere. The ingots were then cold-rolled to 30%, 90% or 95% reduction in thickness 
at room temperature (RT) and then annealed at 1073 –1373 K for 60 s to 100 h in an Ar 
atmosphere for recrystallization, followed by water quenching. Hereafter, these alloys are 
referred to as 10Cr, 20Cr and 25Cr depen ding on Cr content. In addition , to investigate 
the time-temperature-transformation (TTT) di agram for the precipitation of the  phase 
in the 25Cr, the cold -rolled plates of the 25Cr were solutionized at 1373 K for 3.6 ks in 
an Ar atmosphere, followed by water quenching. The solutioniz ed plates were then aged 
at 973–1373 K for 10 s to 1 h in an Ar atmosphere, followed by water quenching. 
The microstructure of the alloys was analyzed using a scanning electron microscope

detector (DigiView 5, EDAX/TSL) controlled by OIM Data Collections software 
(OIMDC ver. 5.31, OIM/TSL) or a transmission electron microscope (TEM , JEM-3010, 
JEOL) operated at an accelerating voltage of 300 kV . Note that the mean grain diameter

volume fraction (fp) of the precipita tes were evaluated from the SEM -backscattered 
electron (BSE) images. The chemical composition of the precipitates was analyzed using 
an SEM-energy dispersive X -ray spectrometry (EDX , EX -23000BU, JEOL ). The 
specimens for SEM, EBSD, TEM and EDX analyses were mechanically polished using 
waterproof emery papers of up to #2000 grit and subsequently electrically polished in a 
perchloric acid: acetic acid (10: 90 vol%) solution. 
The mechanical properties of the alloys annealed at various temperature s after 90%

the annealed plates of the alloys by an electro -discharge machining . The specimens for 
the tensile tests were also polish ed mechanically and electrolytically, similar to that for 
microstructural observation. The fracture surfaces and deformation structure of the 
tensile-deformed specimens were observed using an SEM and an EBSD, respectively.

3.2 Precipitation behavior of the  phase 
Fig. 4 (a) shows the TTT diagram for the  phase precipitated from the solutionized 
25Cr. The precipitation of the  phase in the 25Cr takes place in the temperature range 
between 873 K and 1 273 K, while no precipitate can be observed at 1373 K . Moreover, 
the TTT diagram shows a typical C -shaped curve of which nose temperature is 1173 K. 
The precipitation of the  phase at 1173 K is so fast that precipitates can be seen even 
after 10 s, as shown in Fig. 4 (b) . It is also noted that the  phase appears after aging at 
973 K for 30 0 s. In contrast, in equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi alloy, it takes 1000 h to obtain 
the  phase at 973 K [25]. This means that the precipit ation of the  phase in the 25Cr is 
much faster than that in the equiatomic alloy . It should also be noted that the  phase is 
formed preferentially along the fcc grain boundaries, though there is no precipitate along 
the twin boundaries (Fig. 4 (b)). This means that the high entropy state is broken along 
the grain boundaries excluding annealing twins, and therefore, the  phase is thermally 
stable at the boundaries. In a previous study, we reported that the NiAl precipitates are 
also precipitated along the fcc grain boundaries in non-equiatomic Al0.3CoCrFeNi alloy at 
a significantly fast precipitation rate [21 ]. Thus, it is supposed that the fast precipitation

In order to understand the initial stage of the  phase precipitation from the 
cold-rolled alloys, the 25Cr cold-rolled to 90% reduction was annealed at 1073 K for 60 s. 
The microstructures of the alloys before and after the annealing are shown in Fig. 5 (a) 
and (b) , respectively. Note that the observed areas before and after the annealing are 
exactly the same. As shown in Fig. 5 (a), shear bands can be seen before the annealing. 
On the other hand, after the annealing, there are numerous fine  precipitates in the shear 
bands (Fig. 5 (b)). This means that the shear bands act as a preferential nucleation site for 
the  phase, similar to the fcc grain boundaries. In addition , ultrafine-recrystallized fcc 
grains with numerous  precipitates can be observed along the shear bands. This strongly 
suggests that the  phase inhibits the growth of the recrystallized fcc grains. 
Fig. 6 (a)–(c) shows EBSD phase maps of the 25Cr cold-rolled to 30%, 90% or 95% 
reduction and then annealed at 1073 K for 1 h . Variation in the dm and fp of the alloy as 
a function of reduction is also shown in Fig. 6 (d). The area fraction of the  phase 
(green), which corresponds to the fp, increases with increasing red uction. It is well 
known that high reduction of cold rolling is favorable for the formation of shear bands 
in metals and alloys [26]. Thus , an increase in number of the shear bands at higher 
reductions leads to an increase in the fp of the  phase. Moreover, the dm decreases with 
increasing reduction. The  phase precipitated in the shear bands effectively suppresses 
the grain growth of the fcc matrix, as shown in Fig. 5 (b). Thus, higher fp at higher 
reductions results in smaller dm.

4. Discussion 
The microstructure and tensile properties of 10Cr, 20Cr and 25Cr subjected to cold 
rolling followed by annealing at different temperatures were investigate d. As a result, it 
was found that the fine  phase plays an important role in the grain refinement of the 
25Cr. Thus, the mechanism of the grain refinement in the 25Cr is discussed focusing on 
the precipitation behavior of the  phase. In addition, t he fine-grained 25Cr containing 
the fine  phase exhibits high strength and large ductility in the temperature range of 93–
873K. The deformation behavior of the 25Cr with the fine -grained structure is also 
discussed. 
 
4.1 Grain refinement mechanism of cold-rolled 25Cr 
Based on the microstructure observation of the cold-rolled 25Cr before and after the 
annealing (Fig. 5), i t is clarified that the  phase is precipitated preferentially along the 
shear bands at the initial stage of the annealing. The precipitation rate of the  phase is so 
fast that the phase effectively suppresses the grain growth of the fcc matrix . Here, let us 
consider the mechanism of the grain refinement by the  precipitates. First, i n order to

understand the growth behavior of the  phase, the dp and fp of the  phase in the 25Cr 
are plotted against annealing time ( t) at 1273 K after 90% cold rolling (Fig 11 (a)). It 
should be noted that the dp is proportional to t1/3, suggesting that the Ostwald ripening of 
the  phase occurs during the annealing. On the other hand, the fp remains almost 
constant up to 18 h and decreases drastically thereafter. The  phase is preferentially 
formed at the fcc grain boundaries. However, the number of grain boundaries decreases 
due to grain growth. In addition, the precipitates along the grain boundaries are stable, 
whereas the precipitates left in the interior of the grains tend to disappear due to high 
stability of the solid solution phase in the interior of the grains (Fig. 1 (c)). As a result, the 
fp decreases with increasing annealing time greater than 18 h. Second, the dm of the 25Cr 
is also plotted against t1/3 in Fig. 11 (b). The dm is also proportional to t1/3, similar to the 
dp. This means that the  phase strongly affects the grain growth of the fcc matrix.

Fig. 12 shows a schematic drawing for the mechanism of grain refinement in the 25Cr 
during recrystallization. First, initial grains are deformed by cold rolling and the shear 
bands are introduced. Not e that higher reduction is favorable for the formation of the 
shear bands (stage I). Thereafter, the  phase is precipitated at the shear bands at a fast 
precipitation rate during annealing at 973–1273K. At the same time, the fcc matrix starts 
to be recrystallized. The precipitation of the  phase is so fast that the growth of the 
ultrafine-recrystallized fcc grains are effectively inhibited by grain boundary pinning at 
the precipitates, resulting in the formation of fine-grained microstructure of the fcc matrix 
at the initial stage of the annealing (stage II). The fcc grains grow slowly with increasing 
annealing time, associated with the continuous precipitation and growth of the  phase 
along the grain boundaries . Moreover, the grain growth of the fcc matrix is consist ent 
with the modified Z -S model. On the other hand , the  phase left in the interior of the 
grains tends to disappear during grain growth. This is because the  phase in the interior 
of the grains is not thermally stable , which is one of the features of the non-equiatomic 
HEAs (stage III).

In general, alloys containing hard and brittle precipitates exhibit high strength but 
significantly low ductility. However, it is interesting to note that the 25Cr with the fine  
phase demonstrates large ductility at RT; the EL of the alloy annealed at 1273 K is 56% 
(Fig.7 (b)). The activation of deformation twin s is one of the reasons for the large 
elongation of the 25Cr, similar to the 20Cr (Fig. 8 (b), (d)) [28]. One of the important 
factors that influence the formation of deformation twins is a stacking fault energy (SFE). 
In general, the defor mation-induced twining is easy to take place at low SFE [ 33]. 
Zaddach et al. calculated the SFE of equiatomic and non-equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi alloys 
[34]. They found that the SFE of the alloys decreases with increasing Cr content ( 19.6

It should also be noted that the 25Cr with the fine  precipitates exhibits large 
ductility even at 93 K (Fig. 9 (c)). In general, a critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) for 
slip deformation increases with decreasing temperature [3 5,36]. On the other hand, the 
temperature dependence of the CRSS for twinning is generally insignificant, compared 
with slip deformation [37]. As a result, the deformation-induced twinning takes place 
easily at low temperatures. Therefore, the large ductility of the 25Cr at low temperatures 
is closely related to the TWIP effect, which was also r eported for equiatomic 
CoCrFeNiMn alloys [28]. 
 
Conclusions 
In the present study, the microstructure and mechanical properties of equiatomic and 
non-equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi HEAs subjected to cold rolling followed by annealing 
were examined, focusing on the Cr content and the annealing conditions. Furthermore , 
the precipitation behavior of the  phase and the grain refinement mechanism for the 
alloys containing 25 at.% Cr were investigated. The conclusions drawn in this study are 
given below: 
1. The 25Cr annealed in the temperature r ange of 1073 –1273 K exhibits the 
fully-recrystallized fine fcc matrix and the fine  precipitates. In particular, the dm 
was 1.4 m after annealing at 1073 K.

2. The precipitation temperature of the  phase in the 25Cr is in the range of 873–1273 
K. The  phase was precipitated preferentially along the fcc grain boundaries at a 
significantly fast rate. 
3. The shear bands introduced by cold rolling also act as a preferential nucleation site 
for the  phase. Moreover, the fast precipitation of the  phase results in the 
suppression of the grain growth of the fcc matrix by the grain boundar y pinning , 
which is consistent with the modified Zener-Smith model. 
4. The fine-grained 25Cr containing the fine  phase demonstrates higher strength than 
the 10Cr and 20Cr in the temperature range of 93 –873K. The high strength of t he 
25Cr results from the Hall-Petch effect and precipitation strengthening caused by the 
 phase. The 25Cr also exhibit a large elongation, even at low and ambient 
temperatures due to t he deformation-induced twinning, though the alloy contains the 
hard and brittle  phase. 
5. The excellent mechanical properties of the 25Cr indicate that the alloys have a great 
potential as next-generation structural materials.
