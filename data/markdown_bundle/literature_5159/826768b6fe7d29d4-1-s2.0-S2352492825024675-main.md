ABSTRACT
In this study, a novel multi-objective optimization design framework is proposed for developing high- 
temperature alloy steels with superior creep rupture time and tensile strength. The framework integrates ma -
chine learning techniques, such as data extrapolation via transfer learning, machine learning models enhanced 
by intelligent optimization algorithms, along with an improved multi-objective optimization methodology. A 
database containing 845 alloy steel samples was established. To mitigate data imbalance, conditional generative 
adversarial networks were employed for data augmentation. Feature selection was performed using Pearson 
correlation analysis and recursive feature elimination, yielding eight optimal descriptors. The eXtreme Gradient 
Boosting (XGBoost) model, optimized via particle swarm optimization, achieved high predictive accuracy for 
creep rupture time and tensile strength, with coefficients of determination (R²) of 0.98 and 0.99, respectively. To 
address data scarcity, transfer learning was introduced by leveraging features from related high-temperature 
alloys, enabling extrapolation to generate 80 additional high-temperature data points. To develop high- 
temperature alloys with excellent properties, multi-objective optimization was then applied to balance the 
trade-offs between key performance metrics effectively. Using the augmented dataset, multi-objective optimi -
zation was conducted within a vast search space of approximately 1.22 × 10² ⁸ . An enhanced version of the non- 
dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II), improved by incorporating simulated annealing, was used for 
the optimization. As a result, 40 sets of recommended compositions and testing temperatures for high- 
performance high-temperature alloy steels are proposed. The newly designed materials surpass both conven -
tional superalloy and specialized high-temperature steel. This work provides a new design strategy combining 
transfer learning and multi-objective optimization, offering valuable insights for developing advanced high- 
temperature alloys.
1. Introduction
High-temperature alloy steel is a specialized metal material known 
for its ability to maintain structural stability and superior mechanical 
properties in high-temperature environments [1,2] . Common types 
include nickel-based [3] , cobalt-based [4] , and iron-based alloys [5] . 
Owing to their exceptional strength, creep resistance, and oxidation 
resistance at elevated temperatures, these alloys are extensively used in 
aerospace, energy, and chemical industries [6 – 9] .
Designing high-temperature alloys with excellent comprehensive 
performance remains a major challenge in materials science. A funda -
mental difficulty lies in the enormous parameter space involved, which 
includes composition, processing parameters, grain size, and external 
stress, among others [10 – 12] . Traditional trial-and-error approaches are 
incapable of effectively traversing such a vast variable space. Although 
computational methods such as thermodynamic modeling [13,14] , 
density functional theory (DFT) [15,16] , and molecular dynamics (MD) 
[17] have been introduced to guide alloy design, they often suffer from 
high computational cost and limited ability to handle complex 
multi-component systems, particularly under extreme service 
conditions.
In recent years, data-driven machine learning (ML) methods have 
become increasingly prevalent in materials science. Liu et al. demon -
strated the application of ML for the multi-performance optimization of 
γ ′ -strengthened Co-based superalloys [18] . Xue et al. introduced an 
adaptive design strategy for the accelerated search of materials with 
targeted properties [19] . Similarly, Wang et al. [20] and When et al. 
[21] implemented property-oriented ML frameworks for designing

Several studies have focused specifically on design of high-entropy

Furthermore, the integration of physical principles with ML has gained 
traction, Ren and Xiao developed a physics-guided neural network for 
fatigue life prediction in FCC-based multi-principal element alloys [28] .
These approaches typically employ supervised learning techniques 
to establish mappings between material features (e.g., composition, 
processing parameters) and target properties. For instance, studies have 
utilized ensemble methods like Random Forest and Gradient Boosting to 
predict mechanical properties, while others have applied neural net -
works for more complex nonlinear regression tasks. Specifically in high- 
temperature alloys, research has focused on predicting individual 
properties such as creep life [29,30] , hardness [31] and fatigue resis -
tance [32,33] . The studies commonly employ feature engineering to 
handle material descriptors and advanced algorithms like transfer 
learning [34,35] to address data scarcity challenges. Although these 
studies demonstrate the capability of ML in predicting single perfor -
mance metrics, real-world applications require alloys that simulta -
neously excel across multiple properties — such as creep life and tensile 
strength, which often exhibit trade-offs. Relying solely on 
single-objective prediction is therefore insufficient for guiding the 
design of high-performance alloys suited for extreme environments.
This gap highlights the necessity of multi-objective optimization 
(MOO), which enables the balanced co-optimization of competing ma -
terial properties. For the MOO and interpretable ML, Ma et al. [36]
employed ML combined with MOO to accelerate the discovery of novel 
HEAs with tailored properties. In a significant advancement for 
aluminum alloys, Jiang et al. [37] utilized interpretable ML to syn -
chronously enhance strength, toughness, and stress corrosion resistance, 
providing both predictive models and physical insights. L. Lian et al. 
proposed a strategy integrating thermodynamic simulations, ML, and 
MOO to optimize multiple targets in Ni-based superalloys [38] . For 
high-temperature alloy steel applied in extreme environments, creep 
properties and tensile strength are two crucial properties [39] . However, 
research combining machine learning with multi-objective optimization 
for the design of high-temperature alloy steels remains scarce. Designing 
high-temperature alloy steels that simultaneously offer superior creep 
resistance and high tensile strength under extreme conditions presents 
unique challenges that have not been fully addressed.
The main difficulties include: (1) scarcity of high-quality high-tem -
perature data, complicating reliable model training; and (2) the efficient 
navigation of an ultra-high-dimensional composition – process – property 
space to identify Pareto-optimal solutions. In response, this study pro -
poses a novel multi-objective optimization framework that incorporates 
transfer learning-assisted data extrapolation, machine learning models 
enhanced by intelligent optimization algorithms, and an improved 
multi-objective evolutionary algorithm. This framework aims to address 
the challenges of data scarcity and large-parameter-space exploration, 
providing a new pathway for the design of high-temperature alloy steels 
with balanced performance.
In this paper, we develop an innovative framework that integrates 
machine learning with multi-objective optimization for the design of 
high-temperature alloys exhibiting superior creep rupture time and 
tensile strength. A key methodological advancement is the introduction 
of a hybrid optimization algorithm that effectively couples the Non- 
dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) with simulated 
annealing (SA), significantly enhancing the efficiency and convergence 
quality in exploring complex compositional and process parameter 
spaces. To address the challenge of limited high-temperature data, 
transfer learning (TL) is employed for the first time to perform data 
extrapolation into unseen regimes, enabling the generation of physically 
meaningful virtual samples with superior tensile strength property at 
high temperatures. Furthermore, the XGBoost model, hyperparameter- 
optimized via particle swarm optimization (PSO), achieves exceptional 
accuracy in predicting creep rupture time and tensile strength, with R² 
values of 0.98 and 0.99, respectively. The proposed NSGA-II + SA algo -
rithm efficiently navigates an immense search space (~1.22 × 10² ⁸ ), 
identifying 40 high-performance Pareto-optimal solutions that offer 
optimal trade-offs between target properties. This work provides a new 
and efficient pathway for the design of high-temperature alloys with 
balanced excellence in multiple mechanical properties.

2.4. NSGA-II multi-objective optimization algorithm
Multi-objective optimization problems are more complex compared 
to single-objective optimization problem. The Non-dominated Sorting 
Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) represents an enhancement of the clas -
sical non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm (NSGA) [41] . NSGA-II 
generates a set of non-dominated solutions efficiently and maintains 
population diversity by identifying a Pareto front, which represents an 
approximation of optimal solutions. The Pareto front can be obtained by 
performing genetic operations, including selection, crossover, and mu -
tation. To ensure the quality of the solutions, NSGA-II utilizes a 
non-dominated sorting algorithm to classify the solutions. Ultimately, 
the NSGA-II results in the creation of Pareto fronts, which provides an

To enhance performance of NSGA-II in solving complex multi- 
objective optimization problems, we introduce an optimization strat -
egy based on the Simulated Annealing (SA) algorithm [42] . Simulated 
Annealing is a probabilistic algorithm inspired by the process of 
annealing in metallurgy. It searches for a global optimum by sometimes 
accepting worse solutions to escape local optima. NSGA-II faces issues of 
slow convergence and getting stuck in local optimal in large-scale or 
high-dimensional space. By introducing the simulated annealing algo -
rithm, the above issues can be addressed. The combination of NSGA II 
and SA can lead to a more robust and efficient multi-objective optimi -
zation approach.
In this study, we propose a multi-objective optimization method that 
combines NSGA-II and SA. First, an initial population is generated and 
the objective function value of each individual is evaluated. Subse -
quently, the population is classified using the non-dominated sorting 
algorithm of NSGA-II to identify the non-dominated solution set. To 
enhance the global search capability of the algorithm and avoid local 
optima, we apply the simulated annealing (SA) algorithm to the non- 
dominated solution set for local search: during the SA iteration pro -
cess, the temperature is gradually reduced according to a predefined 
cooling schedule, new solutions are generated within the neighborhood 
of the current solution, and inferior solutions are accepted with a certain 
probability to strengthen the algorithm ’ s exploration ability. Next, ge -
netic operations including selection, crossover, and mutation are per -
formed on the population to produce the next generation. Finally, the 
above steps are repeated until the termination criteria are met (e.g., the 
maximum number of iterations is reached or the objective function 
converges). Through this combined approach, NSGA-II can more effi -
ciently generate a non-dominated solution set, while SA helps improve 
the convergence speed and the quality of the solution set.
3. Data preparation and feature selection
3.1. Dataset of alloy steel
The data of alloy steel utilized in this work are collected from the 
publicly available Creep Data Sheet provided by National Institute of 
Materials Science (NIMS) https://mits.nims.go.jp . The original dataset 
of alloy steel contains 845 samples for 29 different steels, encompassing 
high chromium steel, austenitic steel, low alloy steel and stainless steel. 
The original dataset consists of 42 descriptors, such as chemical 
composition, process condition, Rockwell hardness, austenite grain size 
number, test stress, test temperature; some descriptors are shown in 
Table 1 . Notably, the target properties are the creep rupture time and 
tensile strength. Performing data preprocessing is a crucial step before 
model training. The following techniques are applied in this paper: (1) 
Data Cleaning. Missing values and outliers are removed to ensure the 
integrity and reliability of the dataset. (2) Standardization. All feature 
variables are standardized to eliminate differences in scale across vari -
ables, preventing features with larger magnitudes from disproportion -
ately influencing the model.

method that leverages the knowledge gained from a model trained on

able to utilize the rich information from the source task to improve the 
performance of the target task.

data of alloy steel includes different types of steel, but lacks of data with

The architectures of the generator and discriminator, along with the 
loss functions used in our CGAN training, are structured as follows. The 
generator is designed to map a random noise vector z and conditional 
variables y into synthetic data. Its architecture begins with an input 
layer that accepts z and y with a total dimension of latent_dim. This is 
followed by three hidden layers: the first dense layer outputs 128 units 
using LeakyReLU (alpha = 0.2) and batch normalization; the second 
layer outputs 256 units with the same activation and normalization; the 
third layer outputs 512 units, again with LeakyReLU and batch 
normalization. The output layer is a dense layer with output_dim units 
and a sigmoid activation, ensuring generated values fall within [0, 1].
The discriminator, tasked with distinguishing real from generated 
data, receives inputs of either real or synthetic data along with their 
conditional variables, with dimension input_dim. Its hidden structure 
consists of three dense layers: the first has 512 units with LeakyReLU 
(alpha = 0.1) and dropout (rate = 0.4); the second has 256 units, also with 
LeakyReLU and dropout; the third uses 1024 units with the same acti -
vation and dropout. The output layer is a single unit with sigmoid 
activation, producing a probability indicating data authenticity.

5. Conclusion
A novel machine learning framework for multi-objective optimiza -
tion design of high-temperature alloy steel is proposed, which involves 
NSGA-II, data augmentation and transfer learning. Here are the key 
conclusions from our work: 
(1) To address the issue of data imbalance, a data augmentation
