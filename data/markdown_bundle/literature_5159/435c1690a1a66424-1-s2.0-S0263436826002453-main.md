ABSTRACT
NbMoTaW-based refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have potential as candidates for extreme-temperature 
applications due to their stable body-centered cubic (BCC) structures and high-temperature mechanical per -
formance. However, literature is still scattered and there is no systematic review that clearly correlates the 
fabrication routes and alloying additions (Ti, V, Zr, Hf, Re, B, C, Si) as well as Cu-containing NbMoTaW – Cu 
immiscible composites, with the resulting phase structure, microstructure evolution and mechanical behavior. 
This review correlates the processing methodology and phase structure outcomes to hardness, room-temperature 
plasticity, high-temperature strength and thermal stability. Overall, powder-metallurgy routes most consistently 
achieve the best strength – ductility balance through ultrafine grains and controlled second phases, while cast 
routes often show coarse dendritic segregation and higher sensitivity to brittle networks and oxygen-embrittled 
grain boundaries. Adding Ti/V/Zr/Hf/Re generally strengthens NbMoTaW via solid-solution effects, grain 
refinement and dual-phase/heterostructure formation, whereas excessive Si/B/C additions can form continuous 
brittle silicide/boride/carbide networks. This review integrates the reported results into an explicit proc -
essing – alloying – microstructure – property framework, in which casting/Vacuum arc melting, powder metallurgy 
routes, and Additive manufacturing are linked to alloying-controlled phase stability, microstructure evolution, 
lattice-parameter changes, strengthening mechanisms, and degradation behavior. Overall, the article provides a 
clearer basis for selecting processing routes and alloying strategies to control the microstructure and mechanical 
behavior of NbMoTaW-based RHEAs, supporting future development for high-temperature structural materials 
and protective coatings in aerospace, gas turbines, nuclear energy, and advanced manufacturing.
1. Introduction
The principle of high entropy alloys (HEAs) or multicomponent al -
loys was introduced by Cantor et al. and Yeh et al. back in 2004 [1,2] , 
consist of five or more principal elements with equal or almost equal 
mole fractions of 5% to 35% [3 – 6] . The HEAs are characterized by their 
unique and superior features such as high hardness [7 – 10] and strength 
[11 – 16] , hydrogen storage [17] , high wear corrosion resistance 
[18 – 22] , exceptional electrical and magnetic properties [23 – 28] and 
excellent thermal stability [29,30] [31] . These features have captured 
the extensive tension of material engineers or material scientists to 
explore the possibility of replacing the traditional alloys with state-of- 
the-art HEAs.

1.1. Bibliometric analysis
Since the first report of equiatomic NbMoTaW in 2010 by Senkov 
et al. [48] the number of publications on this alloy has increased sharply, 
especially in the last two years as shown in Fig. 1 . A Web of Science 
search (performed on 27th January 2026) using the query TS = (TITLE- 
ABS-KEY (NbMoTaW) OR TITLE-ABS-KEY (Nb25Mo25Ta25W25)) 
returned 145 journal articles and 3 conference papers. Most studies 
focus on alloying strategies (Ti, Hf, V, Zr, Re, B, C, Si, Cu) and on 
fabrication methods, with mechanical alloying (MA) + Spark plasma 
sintering (SPS)/ Hot Pressing (HP), arc/levitation melting and additive 
manufacturing emerging as the dominant routes. This trend highlights 
the need for a dedicated review that systematizes how processing and 
alloying affect NbMoTaW microstructure and properties. The biblio -
metric counts are reported to indicate publication growth rather than to 
serve as a complete census, because database coverage and indexing can 
vary by year and document type.
1.2. Review methodology and data extraction
The literature was collected using database searches (Web of Science, 
Scopus, and Google Scholar) with keyword combinations including 
“ NbMoTaW ” , “ VNbMoTaW ” , “ refractory high-entropy alloy ” , “ RHEA ” , 
“ mechanical alloying ” , “ spark plasma sintering ” , “ hot pressing ” , “ LPBF ” , 
“ SLM ” , and “ thin film ” . Publications were screened to retain studies that 
reported (i) a clearly defined Nb – Mo – Ta – W – based composition, (ii) a 
stated fabrication route and key processing parameters, and (iii) at least 
one microstructure metric (phase constitution, grain size, segregation 
features, or second-phase description) together with mechanical data 
(hardness, yield strength, and/or plastic strain) at a stated temperature. 
Data were extracted into Tables 3 – 5 and grouped by fabrication route 
(LSP, SSP, AM, and TFD). When comparing mechanical results across 
studies, the manuscript distinguishes compression from tension and 
avoids direct equivalence where specimen geometry, strain rate, or ox -
ygen content were not comparable. Where multiple values were re -
ported, the values corresponding to the authors ’ stated “ optimized ” or 
“ representative ” condition were used and the condition is explicitly 
listed in the tables.

2. Alloy design of NbMoTaW: compositions and empirical 
criteria
Designing alloys with desired properties such as better mechanical 
strength, enhancing corrosion resistance and improving high- 
temperature stability is mainly dependent on the rearrangement and 
interaction of the elements. Formation of the BCC crystal structure in 
NbMoTaW RHEA was expected because all four elements (Nb, Mo, Ta 
and W) have BCC crystal lattices and close atomic radii as shown in 
Table 1 . Moreover, the valence numbers are almost similar (6 for Nb and

formation of solid-solution phases and the thermal stability of RHEAs, 
which will be explained in the next paragraph. The lattice parameters of 
pure elements of NbMoTaW which were calculated theoretically by the

balancing between the strength and ductility [67] . Notwithstanding 
certain achievements, VEC correlation with strength and ductility is not 
applicable in some RHEAs or near equiatomic fraction of RHEAs.
3. Methods and fabrication routes
Manufacturing processes of NbMoTaW RHEA directly impact the 
mechanical properties. Among the main processes techniques named 
liquid-state processing (LSP), solid-state processing (SSP), thin-film 
deposition (TFD) and additive manufacturing (AM), as shown in 
Fig. 2 , the LSP (e.g. Vacuum Arc Melting (VAM) and Electromagnetic 
Stirring (EMS)) is the most common, simple, fast technique in synthe -
sizing RHEAs [46,48,52,67] . Inhomogeneous and coarse dendritic 
microstructure, porosity, segregation and vaporization of low-melting 
elements are the main challenges in LSP which make it challenging to 
produce NbMoTaW with excellent properties [67,68] . On the other 
hand, the SSP is commonly used for synthesizing nanostructures or 
ultra-fine homogenous structures and free intermetallic phases but the 
SSPs are lab-scale production. Moreover, contaminations are caused by 
the complicated setups and series of processes such as high-energy ball 
milling (HEBM), hot/cold/hydrostatic pressing, sintering and spark 
plasma sintering (SPS). Mainly, the contamination occurs during the 
mechanical alloying (MA) when the balls and the jars wear with the 
powders [12,69] . To mitigate these issues, Kang B. et al. suggested 
reducing the milling time in their research on the NbMoTaWV RHEA 
powders to 6 h [12] .
In terms of mechanical properties, NbMoTaW fabricated via MA 
followed by SPS achieves a higher yield strength of 3016 MPa and strain 
of 16.8% [70] than the NbMoTaW synthesized via arc melting which 
exhibits a yield strength of 1211 MPa and strain of 2.6% [48] . Sintering 
temperature directly affects the mechanical properties; the highest yield 
strength of VNbMoTaW is 2612 MPa which was obtained when the 
temperature of SPS was at its lowest 1500 
◦
C [12] .
While the powder metallurgy and bulk of NbMoTaW have been 
extensively investigated, the NbMoTaW thin film ’ s microstructural 
characterization, mechanical properties, oxidation and wear still not 
been thoroughly explored. Nonetheless, the thin film provides a prom -
ising protective coating via TFD techniques. Controlling composition 
and microstructure is feasible using magnetron sputtering for depositing 
nanometers up to micrometers of NbMoTaW [71,72] . Moreover, Addi -
tive manufacturing technologies can be employed to fabricate RHEAs 
and films. Among the seven categories of AM [73] only the powder bed 
fusion techniques (e.g. SLS, SLM, DMLS and EBM) and direct energy 
deposition (DED) techniques (e.g. LENS, EBAM, LMD-w, and WAAM) 
have been successfully used to fabricate NbMoTaW. Gu P. et al. [74]
successfully built a NbMoTaWV film on W substrate via selective laser 
melting (SLM). The paper focuses on the microstructure, morphology, 
defects and mechanical properties as the laser scanning speed varies. 
The primary defects observed in the SLMed NbMoTaWV were pores and 
cracks which are very common in AM technologies. Although the pores 
disappear at low laser scanning speed, the cracks were unavoidable due 
to thermal stress and solidification constraints. The literature indicates 
that most studies have conducted their experimental investigations 
either through Vacuum Arc-Melt (VAM) or MA with SPS, as shown in 
Fig. 3 .

4.1. Microstructure of NbMoTaW powders
The SEM of as-received Nb, Mo, Ta and W powders and mechanically 
milled NbMoTaW RHEAs are shown in Fig. 4 where the Mo powder is 
spherical and the other element powders are granular [74] . Fig. 4 is 
provided for illustrative purposes to show the typical phase evolution of 
NbMoTaW powders during mechanical alloying. Further discussion fo -
cuses on the effect of milling time on phase formation and particle/grain 
refinement. The grain size of NbMoTaW powders shows an inverse 
relationship with milling time. The grain size refines from ~50 μ m to 
1.32 μ m when milling time used 6 h at a speed of 470 rpm and ball- 
powder-ratio (BPR) 15:1 [83] . Most of the literature used MA to 
produce their powder before the solidification process. Overall, the 
literature reported that the phase dominant in the NbMoTaW RHEA 
system alloy is the BCC phase, either when the NbMoTaW powders were 
equiatomic or non- equiatomic (see Table 3 ). The mixture of Nb-Mo-Ta- 
W element powder shows high intensity diffraction peaks for each 
element during the early stage of the ball milling process. As the milling 
time increases, the elemental Nb, Mo, Ta and W peaks gradually 
decrease, broaden and emerge into a single solid solution BCC phase in 
the milled powders [70] . Previous study demonstrates the relationship 
between the dissolution rate and melting temperature, thereby elements 
with low melting temperature have a higher dissolution rate [84] . 
Correspondingly, the low melting temperature element peaks (e.g. Mo 
and Nb) start to dissolve first in the BCC solid solution. As the milling 
time increases, the W and Ta dissolve indicating completion of me -

5.3. Strengthening and toughening strategies
In previous literature, the findings demonstrate that NbMoTaW 
RHEAs exhibited excellent strength at elevated temperatures but 
exhibited low elasticity at low and room temperature, which highly 
impedes their applicability in such a sector that uses superalloys. Thus, 
many approaches have been conducted to increase the ductility of 
NbMoTaW RHEAs through introducing heterostructures (dendrite/ 
interdendrite contrast) and alloying with ductile elements. Moreover, 
alloying with large-atomic-radius elements (e.g., Ti, Hf, Re) increases 
lattice distortion and solid-solution strengthening simultaneously with 
improvement in toughening/ductility through oxygen scavenging, grain 
refinement, and microstructural heterogenization. Moreover, this sec -
tion provides an in-depth discussion of how the processing methodology 
and alloying strategy influence the microstructure and, consequently, 
the mechanical behavior of Mo – Nb – Ta – W – based RHEAs.
Table 6 summarizes the yield strength, ultimate compressive 
strength, fracture strain, and hardness of these alloys alongside their 
respective processing routes. Moreover, Fig. 26 summarizes the data 
from Table 6 by illustrating the relationship between ultimate 
compressive strength and plasticity at room temperature. Overall, 
Mo – Nb – Ta – W – based RHEAs produced via mechanical alloying exhibit 
higher ultimate compressive strength than those fabricated by other 
routes, but they exhibit lower plasticity than alloys made by the VAM

for NbMoTaW RHEAs, one is oxygen-induced GB films such as oxides or 
oxygen-rich carbides that affect tensile strength and toughness directly. 
Secondly, the overabundant formation of brittle silicide, carbide and 
boride networks weakens ductility, although increasing hardness and 
strength. The literature suggests enhancing strategies to overcome those 
degradations such as GB engineering with B/C, strict oxygen control in 
processing, and careful optimization of intermetallic/ceramic phase 
fractions. 
5. Conclusions and outlook
a) NbMoTaW-based RHEAs show very high melting points, stable BCC 
structures and excellent strength at elevated temperatures, often 
exceeding Ni-based superalloys such as Inconel 718 and Haynes 230 
above ~900 – 1000 
◦
C.
b) The main limitation is low room-temperature ductility and strong 
sensitivity to processing conditions, grain boundaries and second 
phases.
c) NbMoTaW lies in the BCC region of empirical parameters ( Δ S_mix, 
Δ H_mix, Ω , δ , VEC), and most alloying additions keep a BCC or dual- 
BCC matrix. Ti, V, Zr, Hf and Re mainly promote solid-solution 
strengthening and grain refinement, while C/B and Zr/Hf/Si can 
form carbides/borides/silicides that become brittle networks at high 
fractions.
d) Fabrication route has a similar impoance to alloy composition. Arc/ 
levitation melting gives coarse dendritic segregation and HIP can 
introduce Nb/Ta-rich grain-boundary oxides when oxygen is not 
well controlled, leading to very low tensile strength and almost no 
tensile ductility.
e) Mechanical alloying plus SPS/HP produces nanocrystalline or 
ultrafine-grained BCC solid solutions with cleaner grain boundaries 
or controlled carbide/oxide dispersions, enabling yield strengths 
above 2 GPa with 0.9 – 26.3% room-temperature compressive plastic 
strain. LPBF/SLM creates fine cellular/columnar BCC 
microstructures with high dislocation densities further improved by 
Ti or V through nanoscale precipitates and heterostructures.
f) Hardness and strength can be widely changed by combining pro -
cessing and alloying (Ti, V, Zr, Hf, Re, C, B, Si, ZrO ₂ ), especially 
through ultrafine grains and controlled second phases.
g) Degradation is mainly linked to oxygen segregation/GB oxides, 
excessive silicide networks, volatilisation of B
2
O
3
, complex oxides 
and dynamic recrystallisation in Ti-rich interdendritic regions. 
However, oxygen control and grain-boundary engineering with B 
and C improve grain-boundary cohesion.
h) For future work, more systematic studies are needed on tensile 
properties, creep and fatigue of NbMoTaW-based RHEAs, since many 
current results are limited to compression. The long-term stability of 
ultrafine microstructures, heterostructures and high-entropy ceramic 
phases at service temperatures also needs further investigation. In 
additive manufactured and thin-film NbMoTaW, the effects of pro -
cess parameters on defects, residual stress, phase formation and 
anisotropy should be studied in more detail to transform NbMoTaW- 
based RHEAs from model alloys into reliable high-temperature 
structural materials and coatings.
