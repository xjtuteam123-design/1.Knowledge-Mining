ABSTRACT
Pt-Ir-10Rh ternary alloys were designed and prepared, and the microstructure, mechanical 
properties and oxidative behaviour were investigated in the present study. The results revealed 
that the microstructure of Pt-Ir-10Rh alloy is composed of a single-phase solid solution of 
platinum with a face centred cubic structure. Adding 10 wt% rhodium to Pt-Ir alloys can 
significantly improve the room temperature ultimate tensile strength and oxidation resistance. 
As the iridium content increases from 15 to 25 wt%, the ultimate tensile strength of Pt-Ir-10Rh 
alloys shows a slight improvement at room temperature and 1600 ℃ , while the elongation 
decreases sharply. Pt-25Ir-10Rh exhibits the longest stress-rupture time and elongation among 
the three alloys at 1600 ℃ and 10 MPa. The higher the iridium content, the more severe the 
oxidation of Pt-Ir-10Rh alloys. The results of this article will provide beneficial references for the 
development of platinum-based alloys used in extreme environments.

Introduction
Pure platinum has a high melting point and strong 
antioxidant properties, but its strength is low and 
needs to be enhanced in many applications. The main 
strengthening mechanism of platinum is solid solution 
strengthening [ 1 ]. Iridium has high melting point, high 
hardness, and extremely strong corrosion resistance, 
which is one of the main strengthening elements of 
platinum matrix [ 2 ]. As a result, a series of solid solu -
tion strengthening Pt-Ir alloys have been developed [ 3 ]. 
Pt-Ir alloys are of outstanding chemical stability, and its 
mechanical properties have been significantly opti -
mised compared to pure platinum and pure iridium, 
making them widely used in electrical contact materials, 
spark plug electrodes, biomedical, catalysts and other 
fields [ 4–6 ]. Research has shown that as the iridium 
content increases, the tensile strength and Vickers 
hardness of Pt-Ir alloys rapidly improve at room tem -
perature. However, the ductility of Pt-Ir alloy has dete -
riorated with the increase of iridium, resulting in poor 
machinability. Pt-Ir alloys containing about 30 wt% or 
more iridium become extremely springy and practically 
unworkable. Therefore, the iridium content of widely 
used Pt-Ir alloy is almost always below 30 wt% due to its 
machinability [ 7 ]. The mechanical properties and 
strengthening mechanism of Pt-Ir binary alloys were 
evaluated using experimental-assisted first principles 
calculations [ 8 , 9 ]. The interatomic interaction is greatly 
enhanced with the increase of iridium content, which 
leads to the decrease of ductility, and modulus misfit 
plays a dominant role in solid solution strengthening of 
Pt-Ir alloys.
Adding a third component is an effective method to 
improve the mechanical, electrical, and processing 
properties of Pt-Ir binary alloys [ 10–15 ]. The addition 
of Y to Pt – Ir alloys can improve part of the electrical 
contact properties under certain conditions [ 10 , 11 ]. Li 
[ 16 ] studied the effect of Zr, Mo and Y doping on the 
properties of Pt-Ir alloys. The results indicate that 
these elements can effectively increase the mechanical 
properties and electrical resistivity, however, the elon -
gation of the alloys is decreased. In recent years, 
researches by Ge et al. [ 14 , 15 , 17 ] have shown that 
the mechanical properties of Pt-15Ir alloy can be 
effectively improved by adding different contents of 
hafnium and 0.5 wt% yttrium. This is achieved 
through the combined effect of introducing (Pt,Ir)

thermocouples, ignition contacts, aircraft engines, 
rocket propulsion nozzles, and catalyst gauzes, etc 
[ 19–24 ]. Most high-temperature components are sub -
jected to both chemical attack and mechanical loads in 
the operating environment, such as the high axial stress 
and strong corrosion of propellants required for aero -
space engines. There have been many studies on the 
mechanical properties of Pt-Ir alloys at high tempera -
tures, including elasticity, stress-rupture strength and 
creep behaviour of platinum alloys as a function of the 
alloying content at various temperatures [ 25 , 26 ]. The 
results indicate that the solid solution strengthening of 
platinum leads to an enormous increase in high- 
temperature mechanical properties. As would be 
expected, the effect increases with increasing amounts 
of the alloying addition. The relative decrease in stress- 
rupture strength caused by increasing the temperature 
from 1400°C to 1700°C is very similar for all the alloys, 
and Pt-30Ir alloy has the highest strength at all tem -
peratures. The strengthening of platinum alloys for 
high-temperature applications also includes dispersion 
strengthening and second phase precipitation strength -
ening [ 27–31 ]. It is worth noting that dispersed oxides 
or precipitates have a significant strengthening effect on 
platinum alloys, but they considerably reduce the alloy’s 
plasticity simultaneously [ 2 ]. Therefore, these two 
strengthening methods are commonly used for plati -
num and Pt-Rh alloys with good plasticity.
Another important issue with materials used in 
high-temperature oxidation environments is their 
antioxidant properties. The experimental results dis -
play that the volatilisation weight loss of Pt-Ir alloys in 
air at 1200 ℃ increases with the increase of iridium 
addition. When the mass percentage of iridium 
exceeds 20%, the volatilisation of the alloys increases 
sharply [ 3 ]. As a high-temperature structural material 
or coating material, Pt-Ir alloy has strong application 
potential [ 32 ]. However, its mechanical properties and 
oxidation resistance still need to be further improved 
to meet the usage needs of high-temperature compo -
nents in aggressive environments [ 33 , 34 ]. The antiox -
idant performance of rhodium is comparable to 
platinum and much stronger than iridium [ 35 , 36 ]. 
Therefore, rhodium is often used to improve the high- 
temperature creep strength and high-temperature sta -
bility of platinum alloys [ 25 ]. Choi [ 37 ] investigated 
the volatilisation behaviour of Pt-11Ir binary alloy and 
Pt–10Rh–11Ir ternary alloy at high temperatures ran -
ging from 1000 ℃ to 1400 ℃ . The results revealed that 
the high-temperature volatilisation was accelerated 
when iridium was added to platinum but suppressed 
when rhodium was added to the Pt – Ir alloys. 
Unfortunately, Choi’s research did not involve the 
mechanical properties of the alloys and its oxidation 
behaviour at higher temperatures.
Since the 1980s, platinum-based alloys have been 
applied in the aerospace field as the propulsion nozzles 
for aerospace engines, which can operate at tempera -
tures up to 1500 ℃ or even higher [ 38 , 39 ]. Combining 
the performance advantages of iridium and rhodium, 
Pt-Ir-10Rh ternary alloys with different iridium con -
tents were designed and prepared in this work. The 
effects of iridium content on the microstructure, high- 
temperature mechanical and oxidation behaviour at 
1600 ℃ of the Pt-Ir based alloys were investigated. 
This study aims to improve the mechanical perfor -
mance and oxidation resistance of Pt-Ir alloys, and 
broadens their applications in high-temperature oxi -
dising environments, such as aerospace engine nozzles 
and anti-oxidation coatings, etc.
Experiment methods
Material preparation
The Pt-xIr-10Rh (x = 15, 20, 25 wt%) alloys were 
designed and prepared by arc melting in an argon 
atmosphere using Pt, Ir, Rh powders with the purity 
of 99.95%. Each ingot was re-melted three to four 
times to obtain a homogeneous composition. The 
ingot mass and diameter are 80 g and 25 mm, respec -
tively. Then the ingots were placed in a vacuum fur -
nace and subjected to homogenisation heat treatment 
at 1300 ℃ for 2 h. Subsequently, the homogenised 
ingots were hot rolled to a sheet thickness of 2.0 mm 
at 1200 ℃ . During the alloy hot working process, the 
deformation of each pass should be controlled within 
10%, and the total processing amount between two 
annealing steps should be strictly controlled. When 
the cumulative deformation reaches 30%, perform 
stress relief annealing at a temperature of 1200 ℃ for 
1 h. Finally, surface treatment was carried out using 
methods such as nitric acid pickling and grinding to 
obtain alloy samples for testing.
Mechanical and oxidation tests
An electric discharge wire cutting machine was 
applied to cut the alloy sheets into the required sam -
ples for the mechanical properties tests, as shown in 
Figure 1 . The alloy samples were subjected to room 
temperature tensile testing using the Shimadzu 
X100KN electronic universal testing machine, with 
the cross head displacement rate of 3 mm/min. High- 
temperature tensile and stress-rupture tests were con -
ducted in the atmosphere on a high-temperature test -
ing machine (TSC304B, Shenzhen Wance Equipment 
Co., Ltd., China) at 1600 ℃ , with a loading stress 10 
MPa for stress-rupture tests. The maximum operating 
temperature of the testing machine is 1700 ℃ , 
equipped with a sample fixture made of alumina

Characterisation of structure and morphology
The metallographic specimens were prepared by grind -
ing and polishing and then underwent electrolytic etch -
ing at room temperature using the self-made apparatus 
equipped with graphite and platinum electrodes. The 
electrolyte is a mixed solution composed of 60% satu -
rated sodium chloride and 40% hydrochloric acid. The 
corrosion voltage during the electrolysis process is 12 to 
15 V, and the corrosion time is 2 to 4 min. The metallo -
graphic structure of the alloys was examined by 
OLYPUS-DSX510 metallographic microscope. The 
Nano Measurer1.2 grain size statistical software was 
applied to analyse statistically the grain size of alloy 
samples. X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was conducted 
within the range of 2θ = 20° to 90° at a scan rate of 1°/min 
using a Bruker D8 Advance diffractometer equipped 
with a Cu target to determine the phase structure of the 
alloys. The operational parameters were set with an 
operating current of 200 mA and an operating voltage 
of 40 kV. The fracture and oxidation surface morpholo -
gies were observed by scanning electron microscope 
(Hitachi SPM-S3400N) equipped with an energy disper -
sive spectroscopy (EDS). Transmission electron 
microscopy (Thermo Fisher Scientific Talos F200X, reso -
lution 0.16 nm) was employed to characterise the micro -
structural features of Pt-Ir-Rh alloys. The elemental 
distribution maps in the alloys were obtained using 
STEM-EDS analysis. The TEM samples were prepared 
by double beam focused ion beam (FIB) technique.
Results and discussion
Microstructure and phase structure
X-ray diffraction (XRD) testing was conducted, and 
the results are depicted in Figure 2 . It is shown that the 
XRD spectrum of Pt-Ir-10Rh alloys are basically con -
sistent with that of pure Pt, indicating that Pt-Ir-10Rh 
alloy is composed of a solid solution (Pt, Ir, Rh) phase 
with a face centred cubic structure. However, com -
pared with pure platinum, the diffraction peaks of Pt- 
Ir-10Rh alloys shifted to the right, and the peak shifts 
at high angles were more pronounced, indicating that 
rhodium and iridium with smaller atomic radii caused 
a certain degree of shrinkage in the unit cell of 
platinum.

see that the alloys with different iridium contents have 
basically equiaxed crystal structures. In addition, it 
was found that with the increase of iridium content, 
the grain size of the alloys gradually grew. The grain

The tensile mechanical properties of Pt-Ir-10Rh alloys 
were tested at 1600 ℃ , and the results are listed in Table 2 . 
With the increase of iridium content, the ultimate tensile

three alloys, Pt-15Ir-10Rh has the highest high-

strength (24 MPa), indicating that this alloy has the 
excellent high-temperature tensile mechanical properties.
The tensile fracture morphologies of Pt-Ir-10Rh

The most important performance of high- 
temperature alloys in application is their high- 
temperature stress-rupture characteristics. Table 3 
lists the rupture time and elongation of Pt-Ir-10Rh 
alloys under experimental conditions of 1600 ℃ and 
10 MPa loading stress. The results indicate that with 
the rise of iridium content, the rupture time of Pt-Ir

Among them, Pt-25Ir-10Rh has the highest rupture

(4.0 h) of Pt-20Ir-10Rh alloy obtained in this work is 
much higher than the literature reported value (1.0 h) 
of Pt-20Ir [ 39 ], further confirming the strong 
strengthening effect of rhodium on Pt-Ir alloys.

The SEM images of the surface of Pt-Ir-10Rh 
alloys oxidised at 1600 ℃ for 10 h are demonstrated 
in Figure 9 . The corrosion pits at the grain bound -
aries are clearly displayed after oxidation, suggest -
ing that the oxidation at the grain boundaries is 
more severe than within the grains. The surface 
composition of alloys was analysed by energy dis -
persive spectroscopy (EDS), and the results are 
shown in Table 4 . It can be seen that the iridium 
content at the grain boundaries is remarkably lower 
than that inside the crystals, and a higher oxygen 
content has been detected. Therefore, it is reason -
able to infer that the weight loss of the alloys is 
mainly caused by the volatilisation of iridium oxi -
des at the grain boundaries. The grain boundaries 
of alloy are relatively loose, and oxygen atoms are 
prone to diffuse into the interior of the grain 
boundaries, and then reacting with the more easily 
oxidisable iridium element to generate volatile oxi -
des, leading to the formation of corrosion pits at 
the grain boundaries.
Conclusions
In this paper, the microstructure, mechanical proper -
ties, and oxidation behaviour of Pt-Ir-10Rh alloys 
were studied and compared with the properties of Pt- 
Ir alloy. The primary findings obtained are sum -
marised below:
(1) The microstructure of Pt-Ir-10Rh alloy is 
a solid solution (Pt, Ir, Rh) phase with a face 
centred cubic structure, and no precipitate 
phase was detected. Iridium and rhodium ele -
ments are homogeneously distributed in the 
platinum matrix.
(2) Compared with Pt-Ir alloys with the same iri -
dium content, the room temperature ultimate 
tensile strength of Pt-Ir-10Rh alloys is signifi -
cantly improved. With the increase of iridium 
concentration, the ultimate tensile strength of 
Pt-Ir-10Rh alloys enhances, while their elonga -
tion decreases. Pt-20Ir-10Rh shows a good 
combination of strength and plasticity.
(3) The high-temperature ultimate tensile strength 
(1600 ℃ ) and the stress-rupture time (1600 ℃ , 
10 MPa) of the alloys improves with the iridium 
content goes up. Pt-25Ir-10Rh has the highest 
ultimate tensile strength (30 MPa) and rupture 
time (6.8 h) among the three alloys.
