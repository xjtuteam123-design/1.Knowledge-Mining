STRUCTURE AND PROPERTIES OF BORON- AND ZIRCONIUM-INOCULATED
REFRACTORY NICKEL ALLOYS SUBJECTED TO HTTM
N. A. Lysenko, 1 A. A. Pedash, 1 A. G . Kolomoitsev,1 and E. I. Tsivirko 2
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 4, pp. 7 – 12, April, 2014.
The effect of high-temperature treatment of melt (HTTM) and born and zirconium inoculation on the structure
and properties of castings from nickel refractory alloys ZhS3LS-VI and ZMI3U-VI is studied. Chemical and
gas analyses of the alloys are performed, the macro- and microstructures are investigated, the thermal conduc-
tivity, microhardness, mechanical properties at room temperature and the long-term strength are determined.
Variants of processes improving the structure and mechanical properties of castings are suggested.
Key words: refractory nickel alloy, inoculation, structure, high-temperature treatment of melt, zir-
conium, boron.
INTRODUCTION
The production of parts from refractory alloys in the air-
craft industry yields a considerable amount of waste, which
is then returned to deliver plants and requires colossal recy-
cling expenses.
This makes it necessary to develop recycling processes
for the waste of the production of parts of gas turbine engines
with the aim of lowering the cost of casting. The most fre-
quently used methods for improving the structure and pro -
perties of critical cast articles from refractory alloys are
high-temperature treatment of the melt (HTTM) [1 – 3] and
its inoculation [4, 5]. The inoculants used in casting of re -
fractory alloys are often boron and zirconium [6, 7].
The aim of the present work was to study the effect of
inoculation with boron and zirconium on the structure and
properties of castings from homogeneous (after HTTM)
nickel refractory alloys ZhS3LS-VI and ZMI3U-VI.
METHODS OF STUDY
Waste of cast alloys ZhS3LS-VI and ZMI3U-VI (runners
and primary casting waste) was subjected after preliminary
cleaning to HTTM in a VIP-10 facility in the following
mode. After heating the melt to 1600 /c17720°C at a residual
pressure of 0.66 – 1.33 Pa the working space of the furnace
was filled with argon of grade A to a pressure of 1/c18010
–2 Pa;
then the melt was heated to 1800 /c17720°C and held at this
temperature for 10 min. Now the temperature of the melt was
lowered to 1600 /c17720°C and it was poured into a metallic
mold to obtain a preform with /c19860 mm and a mass of about
10 kg. From the top part of the preform we cut the metal with
shrinkage void with a mass of 2.0 – 2.2 kg. The obtained bil-
lets of alloys ZhS3LS-VI and ZMI3U-VI with a mass of
about 8 kg were melted in the crucible of an UPPF-3M de-
vice. Then the melt was heated to 1580 /c17710°C; 1.5–2m i n
before pouring it into the charging unit of the furnace
inoculants wrapped with am aluminum foil were placed onto
the heel at a residual furnace pressure of 0.665 Pa. The
inoculants were zirconium of grade KTTS-100 (TU 95.2185)
and boron in the form of an NB-1 (Ni – B) addition (TU
14-5-106–78). At 1550°C the alloys were cast into invest -
ment-cast electrocorundum ceramic molds heated to
950 /c17710°C. For a comparative study the same process of bo-
ron and zirconium inoculation was applied to a commercial
raw stock (a “fresh” alloy melted in a casting shop).
Samples of the tested metal were used to perform chemi-
cal and gas analyses, to study the macro- and microstructure,
and to determine the thermal conductivity, the microhard -
ness, the mechanical properties at room temperature, and the
long-term strength.
The chemical composition of the alloys was determined
by spectrum and chemical analyses. The content of oxygen
and nitrogen was determined with the help of an “ON-900”
ELTRA gas analyzer by the method of vacuum melting.
Cast specimens were subjected to a heat treatment that
involved heating to 1190 /c17730°C, holding for 4 h, and air

The dendritic structure was studied on cast specimens by

The shape and the sizes of macrograins were determined
using an MBS-9 binocular microscope at a magnification
of /c1804.
The microstructure of the alloys was studied prior to and
after the heat treatment by the methods of qualitative and
quantitative metallography (methods P and L in the GOST
1778–70 Standard) on unetched laps and after electrolytic
etching in reagent R18 (10 g citric acid, 10 g ammonium sul-
fate and 1200 ml water). Method P was used to determine the
volume percent of the phase and method L was used to deter-
mine the carbide index Ic (a criterion for estimating the con -
tamination of the lap with nonmetallic inclusions).
The chemical composition of the structural components
was determined by the method local x-ray spectrum
microanalysis (XRSMA) using a MAR-3 microprobe. The
sizes and the kind of distribution of the /c103/c162-phase were deter-
mined using a JSM T-300 scanning electron microscope at a
magnification of /c18020,000 (the topographic contrast was ob -
served in reflected and secondary electrons).
The microhardness of the matrix and of the eutectic in
the alloys was measured prior to and after the heat treatment
using a “BUEHLER” microhardness meter at a load of 1 N
on the indenter and an “Epiquant” structure analyzer with an
mnp attachment (HA VEMAN). The ultimate rupture strength
was determined at room temperature with the help of an R-5
device. The long-term strength of specimens with functional
zone 5 mm in diameter was evaluated using a DST-500 de -
vice at a temperature of 950°C and a stress of 120 MPa (for
alloy ZhS3LS-VI) and 850°C and 350 MPa (for alloy
ZMI3U-VI).
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
According to the results of the chemical analysis alloy
ZhS3LS-VI (0.05 – 0.12% C, 15.0 – 18.0% Cr, 4.0 – 5.0% Co,
2.4 – 3.0% Ti, 2.4 – 3.0% Al, 3.0 – 4.5% W, 3.0 – 4.5% Mo)
and alloy ZMI3U-VI (0.07 – 0.15% C, 12.5 – 14.0% Cr,
4.0 – 6.0% Co, 4.0 – 5.5% Ti, 2.8 – 4.0% Al, 6.5 – 8.0% W,
0.50 – 1.25% Mo) meet the requirements of the performance
specification with respect to the content of the principal ele -
ments. The inoculation provided 0.020 – 0.028% B and
0.13 – 0.14% Zr in alloy ZhS3LS-VI and 0.086 – 0.094% B
and 0.14 – 0.18% Zr in alloy ZMI3U-VI (Table 1). The intro-
duction of the Ni-B alloying additive increased the nitrogen
content to 69 – 80 ppm. The content of oxygen in the alloys
ranged within 19 – 32 ppm and that in the metal with boron
increased to about 44 ppm. Noticeable cleaning of
ZhS3-LS-VI from nitrogen occurred after the HTTM fol-
lowed by inoculation of the melt with zirconium (Table 1). In
alloy ZMI3U-VI the content of nitrogen decreased after in-
oculation with zirconium and after inoculation of the homo-
geneous (after HTTM) melt with boron.
The study of fracture surfaces of the impact specimens
has shown that the HTTM raises the content of the ductile
component (as compared to the conventional process) in the
fractures. The same effect is typical for inoculation of the
melts with zirconium. The addition of boron even to the al -
loys subjected to HTTM increases the proportion of the brit -
tle component in the fractures causing cleavage and roughen-
ing of the fracture surface.
Growth in the thickness of the wall of the casting lowers
the efficiency of refinement macrograins for all the produc -
tion processes studied. In both alloys the mean size of macro-
grains in thin cross sections (as compared to the structure of
standard commercial alloys) decreases after the inoculation
with boron, HTTM, and inoculation with zirconium (Fig. 1 a
and b ).

ZMI2U-VI with boron and zirconium (as a result of higher
supercooling of the homogeneous melt and increase in the
rate of nucleation of crystallization centers).

The MeC carbides form from the melt at the liquidus
temperature and have the form of round inclusions. Cubic
Me23C6 carbides with a widely variable composition segre -
gate upon cooling of the /c103-matrix primarily over grain
boundaries in the form of a net of discrete particles. Carbides
with a minimum size (about 3.5 /c109m) are observed after inoc-
ulation of alloy ZhS3LS-VI with boron and after inoculation
of alloy ZMI3U-VI with zirconium. The size of the carbides
increases substantially after the HTTM of ZMI3U-VI (from
4.3 to 7.2 /c109m) and after inoculation of a homogeneous
ZhS3LS-VI with zirconium (from 4.0 to 7.0 /c109m). X-ray
spectrum microanalysis (in a mapping mode) shows that
both carbides contain W, Ti, Zr, Mo and Cr (Fig. 3). The car-
bide index Ic in alloy ZMI3U-VI is substantially lower after
inoculation of the homogeneous melt with zirconium. Inocu-
lation with boron promotes formation of a high content of
fine carboborides, which raises the index of these inclusions
in both alloys.
The (/c103– /c103/c162) eutectic and the carboboride eutectic (Fig. 2)
in the alloys with boron is arranged primarily in the dendrite
arm spacing and on grain boundaries. As a result of heat
treatment the sizes of the carboboride eutectic decrease, and
the ( /c103– /c103/c162) eutectic in the structure of alloy ZhS3LS dis-
solves considerably in the /c103-solid solution. The ( /c103– /c103/c162)
eutectic in alloy ZMI3U-VI has a size of 20 – 40 /c109m. A mi-
croscopic x-ray spectrum analysis of alloy ZMI3U-VI has
shown that the (( /c103– /c103/c162) eutectic contains Ni, Cr, Co, Ti, Al,
and W. It is enriched with elements with segregation coeffi -
cient K1 > 1 (Ti, Al) and is depleted of elements with K1 <1
(Cr, W, Co, Mo).
The intermetallic /c103/c162-phase has a spherical morphology in
alloy ZhS3LS-VI and a cubic morphology with four-particle
blocks in alloy ZMI3U-VI (Fig. 4). HTTM without inocula -
tion and with inoculation with zirconium decreases the mean
size of the particles of the /c103/c162-phase in alloy ZhS3LS-VI by
10% and lowers the carbide index Ic as compared to the con-
ventional production process. Inoculation of the homoge -
neous alloy ZMI3U-VI with zirconium and boron increases
the mean size of the /c103/c162-phase from 28.1 /c109m to 36.3 and
32.0 /c109m, respectively.

The microhardness of the eutectic, especially of the
boride one, in alloy ZhS3LS-VI in all the cases studied is
higher than that of the matrix (Fig. 5). The transfer of the
eutectic into the /c103-solid solution during heat treatment of the
alloy improves the homogeneity of the metal still more. The
long-term heat treatment typical for alloy ZMI3U-VI also
promotes noticeable growth in the microhardness of its
eutectic in all the treatment variants used, which indicates
occurrence of considerable diffusion processes in the zone of
the location of the eutectic.
The carboboride eutectic preserved after the heat treat -
ment on grain boundaries does not improve the structural
state of the alloys. For the cast alloy ZhS3LS-VI the HTTM
or the HTTM + zirconium inoculation make the structure
more homogeneous both before and after the heat treatment.

Mechanical tests have shown (Table 2) that the strength
characteristics and the elongation of alloy ZMI3U-VI meet
the performance specification at all the treatment variants.
HTTM raises the strength characteristics of both alloys. In
alloy ZhS3LS-VI the level of the ductility parameters is no -
ticeably better after HTTM and after HTTM + zirconium in -
oculation. Inoculation with boron has not provided the re -
quired level of impact toughness and even reduced the elon -
gation and contraction to the lowest values as compared to
the other treatment variants.
The long-term strength of both alloys after all the treat -
ment variants met the requirements of the performance spe -
cification (Table 2). The specimens of alloy ZhS3LS-VI after
HTTM + Zr inoculation exhibited a long time before failure
(> 200 h). The additives of zirconium and boron in the ho -

of the intermetallic /c103/c162-phase

Inoculation with boron and zirconium lowered the cha -
racteristics of long-term strength of the alloys noticeably
against the values of the standard alloys. This seems to be
connected with the presence of clusters of slag and carbo-
nitride inclusions in the alloys containing boron and zirco-
nium and the presence of a boride eutectic over grain boun-
daries in the alloys with boron.
CONCLUSIONS
1. In the case of the use of casting waste of refractory
nickel alloys (runners and primary rejects) in the process of
production of critical castings (blades for gas turbine units) it
is necessary to perform high-temperature treatment of the
melt (HTTM).

2. Inoculation of the studied alloys with boron (a Ni – B
additive) does not clean the metal from nitrogen, produces a
carboboride eutectic in the structure, and increases the con -
tent of fine not heat-resistant carbonitrides, which worsens
noticeably the homogeneity of the metal and its mechanical
