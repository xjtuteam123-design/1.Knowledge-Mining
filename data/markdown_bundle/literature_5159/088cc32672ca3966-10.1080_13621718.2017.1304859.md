ABSTRACT
Intermetallic alloys represent a unique class of materials with atomic arrangements that are dif-
ferent from those of conventional disordered alloys. Among them are alloys based on Ni 3Al,
Fe3Al, and TiAl. Intermetallic alloys have unique properties, such as high melting point, low
density, high-temperature strength, and high-temperature corrosion and oxidation resistance.
Their only disadvantage is the lack of ductility at room temperature and at elevated tempera-
tures. However, they can be ductilised by micro- and macroalloying. Application of intermetallic
alloys for structural use at elevated temperature depends on their ability to be welded using
conventional welding procedures. This paper focuses on the development of these alloys, their
behaviour when subjected to weld thermal cycles, and their weldability. Most intermetallic alloys
are susceptible to cracking during or after welding, but some can be modified to have good weld-
ability. The paper discusses welding and weldability of Ni
3Al-, Fe3Al-, and TiAl-based intermetallic
alloys. In addition, the weldability of other long-range ordered alloys, of the type (Fe, Ni) 3V and
(Fe, Co)3V, are briefly discussed.

Other long-range ordered alloys
Long-range ordered (LRO) intermetallic alloys are
based on a quasi-ternary system (Co
3V, Ni3V, and
Fe3V) containing LRO crystal structure below their
critical ordering temperature (Tc)[ 146]. They have
excellent properties compared to conventional disor-
dered alloys. Their yield strengths increase with an
increase in temperature. The LRO structure promotes
closer packing and strong bonding between atoms.
Lower atomic mobility in the ordered crystal lattice
results in slow diffusion processes and better creep
properties [146]. As is the case with other intermetal-
lic alloys, the major problem with LRO alloys is their
lack of ductility. The brittleness is due to (1) low sym-
metry in the ordered crystal structure with a limited
number of slip systems and (2) grain boundary weak-
ness. This problem has been overcome by macroalloy-
ing. By macroalloying, the stacking characteristics of
the ordered crystal structure were altered by control-
ling alloy electron density (i.e. the number of valence
electrons per atom). By altering the alloy electron
density,thestackingcharacteristicswerechangedfrom
purely hexagonal to purely cubic L1
2-type structure.
Liu [146] has been able to change the crystal struc-
ture of the LRO alloy by systematically and gradually
replacing Co with Fe, which results in alloys having
thefollowingcompositions:(Fe,Co)
3V, (Fe, Ni)3V, and
(Fe,Co,Ni) 3V.
