abstract
HVOF sprayed Ni–5Al coatings on Ni- and Fe-based superalloy substrates were character-
ized to assess the microstructural features and strength in the as deposition condition for
their applications in high-temperature corrosive environment of gas turbine. X-ray diffrac-
tion (XRD), optical microscopy, scanning electron microscopy (SEM), energy-dispersive X-ray
analysis (EDAX), and X-ray mapping analysis are used to characterize the Ni–5Al coatings.
The dense coatings with less porosity and inclusions were produced using HVOF process.
The deposited Ni–5Al coatings exhibited splat like layered morphologies due to deposition
and resolidiﬁcation of successive molten and semi molten powder particles. The hardness
of coatings on three different superalloy substrates was measured and it was in the range
of 210–272 Hv. The average bond strength and surface roughness of the as-sprayed coatings
were 42.62 MPa and 9.22–9.45 /H9262m, respectively. Diffusion of alloying elements from the sub-
strate into the coating has occurred in all the three superalloy substrates as observed from
the X-ray mapping analysis.

1. Introduction
Superalloys are traditional structural materials in gas turbine
industry but it is unable to provide both high-temperature
strength and high-temperature corrosion resistance simulta-
neously. T o obviate these difﬁculties, the protective coatings
on superalloys are used to provide high-temperature oxidation
resistance and hot corrosion resistance in the actual environ-
ment foreseen in the gas turbine. It may be mentioned that the
demand on protective coatings to achieve high-temperature
corrosion resistance and mechanical properties is ever grow-
ing due to the increasing operating temperature of gas turbine
as compared to the current operating temperature. T o accom-
plish this goal, the microstructural features and strength of
the as deposited coatings need to be assessed, initially, for its

Thermal sprayed NiAl coating is widely used as bond coat

The high velocity oxy-fuel (HVOF) process is widely used
to deposit various types of coatings, which have low porosity
and high hardness and are relatively well bonded to the sub-
strate being protected ( Sidhu et al., 2006a ). It belongs to the
family of thermal spraying technologies and is capable of pro-
ducing denser coatings with superior mechanical properties
when compared to other thermal spraying methods ( Herman
et al., 2000 ). The high kinetic energy of the powder particles
with shorter residence time result in the deposition of dense
coatings during HVOF process. The effect of thermal spray-
ing process on microstructure and mechanical properties of
Ni–5Al coating deposited on steel substrate has been studied
by ( Sampath et al., 2000 ). It is shown in their study that the
superior elastic and plastic properties of HVOF sprayed Ni–5Al
coating as compared to air plasma sprayed (APS) and twin
wire arc sprayed (TWA) are due to formation of denser coating
with less porosity and less oxide content in the former pro-
cess. Deshpande et al. (2006) have studied the mechanisms
of oxidation and its inﬂuence on microstructural evolution
of Ni–5Al coatings deposited using HVOF , APS, and Wire Arc
spraying. They observed that in-ﬂight oxidation is common in
APS, Wire Arc and Wire fed HVOF spraying but oxidation of
splat surface occurs for the powder fed HVOF . It is reported in
their study that the high quality of the HVOF sprayed Ni–5Al
coatings is due to its less porosity and oxide contents when
compared to that of APS and Wire Arc sprayed coatings. It is
reasoned that the slightly lower temperature, higher viscosity,
and higher particle velocities during HVOF spraying of Ni–5Al
coatings are responsible for obtaining denser coatings with
less undesirable features of inclusions and oxides. Although
the HVOF sprayed Ni–5Al coatings on steel substrates have
been reported in the literature, the microstructural features
of Ni–5Al coatings on different superalloy substrates were not
studied so far. The three superalloys viz. Superni 76 (Hastel-
loy X), Superni 750 (Inconel X-750) and Superfer 800 (Incoloy
800) are used in gas turbine parts, aviation and industrial
springs, bolts, bellows, furnace equipment, steam boiler, heat
exchanger and piping in chemical industry. The oxidation
and hot corrosion behaviour of HVOF sprayed Ni–5Al coat-
ing on superalloys under cyclic conditions at 900
◦C have been
studied previously by the authors and published elsewhere
(Mahesh et al., 2008a,b). The present work has been focused to
investigate the microstructural characteristics, bond strength,
and hardness of the HVOF sprayed Ni–5Al deposited on three
different superalloy substrates namely Superni 76 (Hastelloy
X), Superni 750 (Inconel X-750) and Superfer 800 (Incoloy 800).
The formation of different phases and the chemical composi-
tion of Ni–5Al coatings are analyzed by XRD and distribution
of different elements by X-ray mapping analysis, respectively.
The microstructural features such as grain morphology, poros-
ity and thickness of the Ni–5Al coatings are measured from
their respective optical and SEM micrographs. Microhardness,
surface roughness and bond strength of the coatings were also
measured.
2. Experimental procedure
2.1. Development of coatings
2.1.1. Substrate material
Ni- and Fe-based superalloy substrate materials (Superni
76, Superni 750 and Superfer 800) are used in the present
investigation. They are obtained from Mishra Dhatu Nigam
Limited, Hyderabad (India) in a rolled sheet form. The
chemical composition of the substrate materials is reported
in Table 1 . Specimens with dimensions of approximately
20 mm× 15 mm× 5 mm were cut from alloy sheets and pol-
ished with SiC papers down to 180 grit and subsequently grit
blasted with alumina powders (grit 20) prior to the deposition
of the Ni–5Al coatings by HVOF process.
2.1.2. Coating formulation
The powder was provided by Metallizing Equipments Com-
pany Pvt. Limited (MECPL) Jodhpur (India). The powder was
mixed in a ball mill rotated at 60 rpm for 1 h. The uniformly
mixed powder was immediately fed into the powder feeder
of the HVOF system prior to the deposition. The ﬁxtures were
used to hold the specimen and moved it in all directions for
the uniform deposition of the coatings in all the surfaces of
the samples. Ni–5Al coatings are developed using commercial
Hipojet-2100 HVOF system at MECPL. Liqueﬁed Petroleum Gas
(LPG) was chosen as a fuel as it is cost effective to achieve
the similar properties of coatings using other fuels such as
hydrogen and propane, which are expensive. A commercially
available Ni–Al powder (Fig. 1) of nominal composition Ni–5Al
(wt%) is used as a feed stock material and the coating is
deposited on all six sides of the specimen. The standard spray
parameters employed are; oxygen ﬂow rate 250 l/m, fuel (LPG)
ﬂow rate 60 l/m, air ﬂow rate 600 l/m, spray distance 20 cm,
fuel pressure 6 kg/cm
2, oxygen pressure 8 kg/cm2, and air pres-
sure 6 kg/cm2 as used in the manual of the Hipojet-2100 HVOF
system. The specimens are cooled with compressed air jets
during and after spraying. All the process parameters are kept
constant during the deposition of coatings.

2.2. Characterization of the coatings
The Ni–5Al coated samples are successively polished by using
different grades of SiC papers (grit size 1/0, 2/0, 3/0 and 4/0)
and then they are subjected to cloth polishing with 3 /H9262m
diamond suspensions followed by a ﬁnal step of 0.05 /H9262m alu-
mina suspensions. The polished samples are characterized
by optical microscope and SEM/EDAX to study the surface
and cross sectional microstructural features, and composi-
tion of the coatings. A Zeiss Axiovert 200MAT inverted optical
microscope, ﬁtted with imaging software Zeiss AxioVision
release 4.1 is used for microstructural characterization. The
porosity of the coating is measured by processing its optical
micrographs with an image analyzer in AxioVision multiphase
release 4.1 software, which is developed based on ASTM B276.
The sprayed sample is cut across its cross section using
Buehler ISOMET 1000 precision saw and then it is mounted
in transoptic powder for mirror polishing prior to studying
its cross sectional features using SEM. The coating thick-
ness is measured from the back-scattered electron images
(BSEI) collected with a scanning electron microscope (Leo 435
VP , England) attached to Robinson Back-Scattered detector
(RBSD).
The microhardness of the Ni–5Al coating is measured by
using microhardness tester Miniload 2 (Leitz, Germany). A 15 g
(147.1 mN) load is provided to the indenter for penetration of
as-sprayed coatings. Hardness value is calculated from the
relation Hv = 1854.4 × F/d
2 where ‘ F’ is the load in g and ‘ d’i s
the diameter of the indenter in micrometer. Surface rough-
ness tester (Mitutoyo SJ-201, Japan) was used to measure the
surface roughness of the as-sprayed coatings at ﬁve different
locations.
The bond strength of the coatings was tested using the
ASTM standard C633-01 for determining the adhesion or cohe-
sion strength of thermal spray coatings. The coated surface of
the cylindrical sample was glued to another identical cylin-
der using epoxy adhesive ﬁlm (HTK Ultrabond 100, Germany).
The glued cylinders were mounted in a tensile test machine
equipped with a self-aligning ﬁxture and pulled apart until
the coating failed. A data acquisition system continuously
recorded the tensile load exerted by the machine. A total of

3. Results
3.1. Measurement of coating thickness
The thickness of Ni–5Al coating is measured from its back-
scattered electron images obtained at the cross section of the
mounted samples. Fig. 2 shows the secondary electron image
(SEI) and back-scattered electron image of the coating and its
thickness values are reported in Table 2.
3.2. Measurement of porosity of the coatings
Porosity is one of the essential parameters of the coatings need
to be quantiﬁed as it affects the corrosion resistance of the
thermal spray coatings by providing preferential paths for cor-
rosive species to penetrate through the coatings and reach the
substrate. It is well established that the dense coatings usu-
ally provide better corrosion resistance than porous coatings
(Sidhu et al., 2006b ). Zeiss Axiovert 200MAT inverted opti-
cal microscope with stereographic image analysis software
based on ASTM B276 is used to measure the surface porosity
of the coating. The polished specimen is unetched and free
of grinding and polishing marks before scanning the speci-
men surface under the optical microscope at the appropriate
magniﬁcation. The optical micrographs obtained at different
areas on the specimen surface are processed by image analysis
software to calculate the porosity contents. Porosity of HVOF
sprayed Ni–5Al coatings is found to be around 2% and reported
in Table 2.
3.3. Surface roughness (Ra) values of the as-sprayed
coatings
The surface roughness (Ra) values of the as-sprayed coatings
were found to be in the range of 9.22–9.45 /H9262m. The centre line
average (CLA) method was used to obtain the Ra values.

3.4. Bond strength of Ni–5Al coatings
The bond strength of the Ni–5Al coatings was measured on
three specimens as per ASTM standard C633-01. The coating
failed at the substrate-coating interface while the remain-
ing part attached to the adhesive. Average bond strength of
42.62 MPa was observed for the coatings.
3.5. Microhardness of the coatings
Fig. 3 shows the microhardness proﬁles along the cross-
section of the coatings as a function of distance from the
coating substrate interface. The microhardness of the coating
is found to vary with the distance from the coating–substrate
interface. A maximum value of 250 Hv and a minimum value of
210 Hv are obtained in case of Ni–5Al coating on Superfer 800.
A maximum hardness value of 262 Hv and a minimum value
of 245 Hv are obtained for Superni 76. Superni 750 showed
hardness values with maximum of 272 Hv and a minimum of
232 Hv.

stationary substrate and the required thickness of the coating
is achieved by varying the number of passes. Lamellar struc-
ture of splats is observed in the coatings (SEI and BSEI) as
shown in Fig. 2. Few unmelted, partially melted particles and
inclusions are observed in the microstructure. It is evident that
the coating is dense with porosity around 2%. Tiny sized inclu-

showed that the nickel splats are unoxidised and aluminum

4. Discussion
HVOF process with LPG fuel is used to deposit successfully
Ni–5Al coating on Ni- and Fe-based superalloy substrates in
the present work. LPG fuel is chosen as it is very cost effective
when compared to hydrogen fuel. Due to the higher veloc-
ity, relatively lower temperatures, and higher impact of the
powder particles on the substrate material, the coatings with
dense microstructure and less porosity are formed in HVOF
process. Fig. 2 shows the cross-section morphology of the coat-
ings on different superalloy substrates. The coating exhibit
characteristic splat like, layered morphologies due to the depo-
sition and re-solidiﬁcation of molten or semi molten droplets.
Since the HVOF splats are less fragmented during deposition,
it ﬂattens well and improves the contact between splats and
substrate. Flattening of the HVOF splats is governed by its high
Re number as reported inSampath et al. (2000)work. The phys-
ical features of splats dictate the structure of dense coating
and thereby its mechanical and corrosion resistant properties.
During HVOF spraying of powders, long axis of the impacted
splats is oriented parallel to the substrate surface resulting in
lamellar microstructures. It is seen that the coating is uniform
and dense on all the superalloy substrates.
The measured surface porosity of the Ni–5Al coatings in
the present work is around 2% and it is in accordance with
the porosity values of HVOF sprayed NiAl coatings reported
in the literature ( Sampath et al., 2000; Deshpande et al., 2006;
Culha et al., 2008 ). The coating microstructure is comprised of
unmelted particles and inclusions but its content is very less
as compared to that of the coatings deposited by other process
techniques such as APS and Wire Arc ( Deshpande et al., 2006 ).
The high degree of ﬂattening and very less splat fragmentation
during HVOF spraying of Ni–5Al powders contribute for the
less porosity value in the as deposited coating. An increase in
kinetic energy of the powder particles would also reduce the
porosity in the deposit. The thickness of the Ni–5Al coating is
measured along the cross sectional images of the randomly
selected samples obtained using RBSD in SEM and it is found
to be in the range of 174–200 /H9262m. Uniform thickness of the
coating ensures good adherence over the substrates but very
high thickness variation across the coatings would have lead
to its disintegration.
Hardness is the most frequently quoted mechanical prop-
erty of the coatings ( T ucker, 1994). Fig. 3 shows the proﬁles
of microhardness of the coatings along the cross-section
on all the three different superalloy substrates used in the
present investigation. It is evident from this ﬁgure that
the microhardness of the coating varies with the distance
from coating–substrate interface. The non-uniformity of the
hardness values of the coatings, along the distance on all
the substrates, prob ably is attributed to the microstruc-
tural changes along the cross-section of the coatings. These
microstructural changes might be due to the presence of
porosity; oxidised, melted, unmelted and semi-melted parti-
cles in the coating structure as observed in SEM and optical
micrographs. The hardness of coatings on three different
superalloy substrates was measured and they lie in the range
of 210–272 Hv. Buta et al. (2005) , Hazoor et al. (2006) , and
Mahesh et al. (in press) have also reported the variation in

5. Conclusions
The HVOF technique has been used to deposit Ni–5Al coatings
on three different superalloy substrates and the coatings were
characterized for their microstructural features, hardness, and
bond strength in the present work. The following observations
were made based on the present study.
1. The coatings on three different superalloy substrates
(Superni 76, Superni 750 and Superfer 800) exhibited a
dense and uniform lamellar structure with the porosity
value around 2.0%.
2. The formation of solid solution of Ni and Al in the
as-sprayed coatings has been observed from the XRD
results.
3. The hardness and strength of the coatings were affected by
the microstructural characteristics such as porosity, oxide
inclusions and unmelted particles.
4. The diffusion of alloying elements (Cr, Fe, W , P , and S) from
superalloy substrates into the coatings has been conﬁrmed
by X-ray mapping analysis.
