ABSTRACT 
The oxidation resistance and high temperature strength of superalloys cannot coexist when they are used above 
1000 ℃ . The lowest critical value of Cr-Al for the formation of continuous Al
2
O
3 
layer to improve the oxidation 
resistance of superalloy at 1100 ℃ was determined on the basis of ensuring the excellent high temperature 
strength on Ni-18Co-18 W matrix. The alloy exhibits improved oxidation resistance at an optimal value of 
18Cr3.5Al. In addition, the experiment confirmed that the strength improvement can be achieved by reducing Cr 
and increasing the solid solubility of W. The tensile strength of these alloys is similar to that of Haynes 230 and 
Haynes 188. However, the rupture life of 18Cr3.5Al alloy at 1000 ℃ /40 MPa reaches 115 h, better than Haynes 
230 and Haynes 188. The results provide a definite reference value of Cr-Al for improving the properties of 
alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Superalloys are widely used in high temperature components of 
aeroengines due to their excellent high temperature strength, oxidation 
resistance and good fabricability [1] . With the rapid development of 
aerospace engines, combustor, as an indispensable high-temperature 
component, has an endless demand for increasing the service tempera -
ture of materials [2] . The combustor condition requires that material has 
excellent plasticity, high temperature oxidation resistance, strength and 
rupture life. The commercial superalloys currently in service with the 
highest temperature capacities for combustion chambers, such as Hay -
nes 230 and Haynes 188, have long term maximum service temperatures 
not exceeding 1000 ℃ [3,4] . Inevitably, two problems exist in further 
increasing the service temperature of such alloys. On the one hand, the 
Cr
2
O
3 
layer, which currently provides oxidation resistance, will be 
converted into volatile CrO
3 
above 1000 ℃ and lose the oxidation 
resistance of the alloy [5] . Studies have shown that the addition of Al can 
promote the formation of Al
2
O
3 
and show good oxidation resistance 
above 1000 ℃ . However, the addition of Al is detrimental to the plas -
ticity of the alloy, resulting in the difficulty of fabricability [2,6] . On the 
other hand, the high temperature strength of the alloy decreases rapidly 
due to the redissolution of the enhanced phase above 1000 ℃ . The high 
temperature strength of these alloys is mainly attributed to carbide 
strengthening and solution strengthening of Cr and W. However, the 
solution capacity of the alloy matrix has reached its limit. Therefore, the 
development of this superalloy is difficult to break through the limit of 
1000 ℃ . 
Recently, we developed a new theory to simultaneously improve the 
oxidation resistance and high temperature strength of the nickel-based 
superalloy at 1100 ℃ by adding the minimum amount of Al required 
to form Al
2
O
3 
layer, while reducing Cr content as much as possible to 
release the solid solution capacity of the matrix to W. However, a certain 
amount of Cr is also required to provide oxidation resistance and high 
temperature strength. In this study, the lowest Cr-Al value required for 
the formation of Al
2
O
3 
layer on the basis of ensuring the high temper -
ature strength of the alloy was determined. The oxidation resistance and 
mechanical properties of the modified alloys were compared with 
commercial Haynes 230 and Haynes 188 alloys.

However, in addition to improving the oxidation resistance, me -
chanical properties of alloys also need to be considered, especially 
plasticity at room temperature, high temperature strength and rupture 
life. Fig. 3 shows the mechanical properties of different alloys. It can be 
seen that the increase of Cr content can improve the tensile strength and 
rupture life at high temperature and plasticity at room temperature of 
alloys. This is mainly attributed to the solution strengthening of Cr. In 
addition, W
6
C precipitates can be observed below the oxide layer of 
18Cr3.5Al in Fig. 2 (f). However, due to the poor oxidation resistance, Cr 
is consumed below the oxide layer and depleted, resulting in W
6
C solid 
solution into the matrix in 12Cr and 15Cr alloys ( Fig. 2 (a-e)). This 
demonstrates that the sufficient solubility of Cr in the matrix leads to no 
excess solution space for W and precipitates W
6
C strengthened phase. 
However, with the increase of Al content, the rupture life of the alloy can 
be improved, but the plasticity at room temperature of the alloy are 
decreased. In order to ensure that the tensile strength and rupture life 
are better than those of commercial alloys Haynes 230 and Haynes 188 
above 1000 ℃ , only 18Cr3.2Al and 18Cr3.5Al can be selected for the Cr- 
Al value. It is worth noting that rupture life of 18Cr3.5Al alloy at 
1000 ℃ /40 MPa reaches 115 h, largely exceeding that of Haynes 230 
and Haynes 188. In the Ni-18Co-18 W-18Cr-3.5Al system, the alloy 
exhibits improved oxidation resistance and excellent high temperature 
strength. 
4. Conclusion 
The lowest critical value of Cr-Al for the formation of continuous 
Al
2
O
3 
layer of wrought nickel-based superalloy at 1100 ℃ was deter -
mined 18Cr3.5Al as on the basis of ensuring the excellent high tem -
perature strength. The alloy exhibits improved oxidation resistance at an 
optimal value of 18Cr3.5Al. The tensile strength of these alloys is similar 
to that of Haynes 230 and Haynes 188. However, the rupture life of 
18Cr3.5Al alloy at 1000 ℃ /40 MPa reaches 115 h, largely exceeding that 
of Haynes 230 and Haynes 188.
