Abstract With the development of nucleation theory, the
calculation of phase diagrams(CALPHAD) method and
microscopic elasto-plasticity mechanics, it is possible to
apply the phase-ﬁeld method to simulate the c
0 rafting
behavior of nickel-base single-crystal superalloys under the
conditions of heat treatment, fatigue and creep testing.
Based on the experimental progress in c
0 rafting behavior
and in combination with the microstructural simulation of
the precipitation and rafting of c0 phase, this paper reviews
the phase-ﬁeld investigation of c0 rafting in nickel–base
single-crystal superalloys.

1 Introduction
With the wide application of mathematics, physics, and
computer sciences in the ﬁeld of material sciences during
the past 30 years, it has become possible for material
research by means of numerical simulation at scales from
electron to atom, and then to continuous medium. Also a
series of methods and models used for numerical simula-
tion in materials research have been developed gradually.
The innovation of theoretical modeling, the improvement
of arithmetic, and the enrichment of experimental methods
have meant that materials simulations approach even more
closely to real experimental processes. This offers a pow-
erful tool to interpreting some experimental phenomena,
such as the rafting and coarsening of c
0 phase in Ni-base
single-crystal superalloys, the optimization of experimental
parameters, the prediction of materials performance, and
the estimation of mechanical properties of materials.
Ni-base superalloys have been widely used as the hot
section components of turbine engines because of their
superior high-temperature mechanical properties and oxi-
dation resistance. They have also become one of the key
components in the design and manufacture of advanced
propulsion systems. The microstructure of Ni-base super-
alloys is mainly composed of two phases, namely c
0 phases
as a primary strengthening phase, embedded coherently inc
matrix. In service, c0 phases coarsen along a speciﬁc
direction and form a rafted microstructure as a result of the
synergistic effect of temperature and stress, causing a
marked deterioration in the high-temperature mechanical
properties. c0 rafting has been considered as one of the main
reasons for degradation in superalloy performance. So it is
of great importance to clarify the failure mechanism of su-
peralloys by theoretical and experimental research of c
0
rafting. The main methods that can be used to simulate the
microstructure of Ni-base single-crystal superalloys during
the rafting process include the analytic method [ 1], phase-
ﬁeld method [ 2], Monte Carlo method [ 3], ﬁnite element
method [4], and so on, among which the phase-ﬁeld method
has become one of the most important methods in the area of
c0 rafting because of its superiority over physical and
mechanical methods. With the deeper investigation of
crystal defects using the phase-ﬁeld method, a more detailed
mechanism of c0 rafting has been revealed recently [ 5–7].

Investigation of the precipitation and rafting ofc0 phases via
experimental and simulation methods has revealed that they
have complex morphology: in terms of morphology, it had
circular, square, dendritic, and plate shape [8, 9]; in terms of
size, there were primaryc0 with comparable size precipitated
under supercooling, also there were secondary and tertiaryc0
with an uneven size [10, 11]; besides, adjacentc0 coarsened in
external ﬁelds orc0 divided into 3, 4, or 8 pieces during growth
[12, 13]; in terms of volume fraction, the total volume fraction
of c0 increased or decreased under heat treatment and external
ﬁelds [ 14]. The complexity of c0 morphology cannot be
interpreted through a speciﬁc si mple factor. The effects of
temperature ﬁeld, diffusiona l ﬂow ﬁeld, mechanical ﬁeld,
ordering, crystal defects, and so on may be involved.
Therefore, it is necessary to save experimental costs,
optimize the processing parameters and shorten the
research cycle by developing simulation methods to ana-
lyze the experimental results theoretically and simulate the
microstructure to predict the c
0 morphology under different
service conditions, subsequently to predict mechanical
properties. Among the simulation methods for c0 mor-
phology, the Monte Carlo method [ 2, 15] and phase-ﬁeld
method [ 3, 16] have been well developed, especially the
latter for interpreting precipitation and rafting of c0 phase.
In this paper, experimental and simulation investigations of
c0 rafting are introduced systemically, and the research
using the phase-ﬁeld method of precipitation and rafting of
c
0 phases is reviewed, offering some consideration for a
further research of c0 rafting by the phase-ﬁeld method.
2 Phase-ﬁeld method
The basic concept of phase-ﬁeld method is to set up a free
energy function based on the physical model and to solve the
dynamic equation (such as the Cahn–Hilliard equation and
Ginzburg–Landau equation) through the variational method,
and then to express the distribution of ordering parameters in
the ﬁeld by the matrix in order to reveal the dynamics of the
subjects under investigation. The advantages of the phase-
ﬁeld method are that it can separately consider the factors
that affect the dynamics of the subject under investigation by
setting up the free energy function; it can dynamically dis-
play the evolutions of the microstructure and it can analyze
the inner mechanism of the microstructural evolution. The
phase-ﬁeld method can be used to investigate the effect of
mechanical ﬁeld [14], ﬂow ﬁeld [17] and intrinsic defects on
the microstructure [18], and some simulation results such as
dendritic growth orientation [ 19], dislocation distribution
[20], crack propagation [ 21], and solid–solid phase trans-
formations [22] have been conﬁrmed by a few experiments.
The phase-ﬁeld method is mainly used to deal with den-
dritic growth [ 19], solid–solid phase transformations [ 22],
dislocations [20], martensite transformations [23], and twin
crystals [24]. The investigation of precipitation and rafting
of c
0 phases encompasses the process from binary to mul-
ticomponent systems, from elastic to elasto-plastic models
and from simple isothermal to complex undercooling pro-
cesses. Especially in recent years, the application of elasto-
plastic mechanical model in the phase-ﬁeld method has
extended the investigation area of the method. The simula-
tion method mainly included the discrete dislocation method
[25], effective plastic strain method [ 26], visco-plastic
mechanical method [ 27, 28] etc. The currently developed
phase-ﬁeld model is focused not only on c
0 rafting but also
on the interaction between defects and c0, such as the
shearing action of dislocations [5] and microtwins [7]o n c0,
offering a deeper understanding of thec0 rafting mechanism.
3 Phase-ﬁeld investigation of c0 precipitations
Investigations of the evolution of c0 morphology using the
phase-ﬁeld approach can be divided into two aspects: one
is focused on c
0 precipitation during heat treatment; the
other is on the deformation of c0 phase in service. Inves-
tigation of c0 precipitation during heat treatment with the
phase-ﬁeld method mainly includes c0 rafting, the precip-
itation of c0 in multicomponent nickel-based single-crystal
superalloys and the multimodal microstructure of c0 pre-
cipitation. This was accomplished by coupling the phase-
ﬁeld method with other theoretical models. For instance,
the phase-ﬁeld investigation of c0 rafting was developed
together with microscopic elasto-plastic mechanics and
dislocation theory; the phase-ﬁeld investigation of c0 pre-
cipitation in multicomponent Ni-base single-crystal super-
alloys were based on coupling the calculation of phase
diagrams (CALPHAD) method with the phase-ﬁeld
method; and the multimode states of c
0 precipitation were
investigated based on the nucleation theory.
The phase-ﬁeld investigation of c0 rafting generally
involved two stages. The ﬁrst stage is based on the elastic
model of c
0 rafting; the second stage is based on the elasto-
plastic model of c0 rafting. The elasto-plastic model can
also be divided into two classes: one is an elasto-plastic
model based on dislocations; the other is a crystal plasticity
model. The elastic model is adapted to simulate the inﬂu-
ences of the external stress ﬁeld, c/c
0 lattice mismatch and
elastic modulus differences between c0 and c phase on c0
rafting behaviors. For the crystal plasticity model, c0 rafting
evolutions are simulated to explain the mechanical
behavior of materials. Great progress has been achieved,
however, this work was limited to the Ni–Al binary alloy
system. One possible reason is that there are not enough
parameters, such as c/c
0 lattice mismatch and atom diffu-
sion coefﬁcients for multicomponent alloy systems.

3.1 c0 precipitation behavior in multicomponent Ni-
base superalloys
Simulation methods for multicomponent superalloys include
the ﬁrst-principle method [ 29], Monte Carlo method [ 15],
phase-ﬁeld method [16, 30] and so on. In spite of the fact that
the ﬁrst-principle and Monte Carlo methods can help in
obtaining the lattice constant of both c phase and c0 phase,
and the ﬁrst–principle method can allow one to acquire the
elastic constants of the two phases in ground state, the ﬁrst-
principle method can only deal with smaller systems con-
taining just a few atoms, and the Monte Carlo approach
depends very much on atomic interaction potential, which
limit their engineering applications. Thermal dynamics
modeling greatly simpliﬁes the simulation of precipitation
phase with complex atomic conﬁguration considering the
complexity of superalloys. In recent decades, the possibility
of the dynamic simulation of c
0 precipitation behavior in
superalloys has beneﬁted from developments of phase dia-
gram calculation technology and thermaldynamic data
accumulation of nickel-based superalloys [31–33].
In c0 precipitation in Ni–Al based superalloys, the pre-
cipitated c0 from c has a face-centered cubic (fcc) structure.
c is a kind of disordered solid solution based on Ni, while
c0 is an ordered intermetallic compound with L1 2 structure
in which Ni atoms occupy fcc face center positions and Al
atoms occupy fcc corner positions. In the phase-ﬁeld
model, the two-phase crystal structure is described by four
ﬁeld parameters, one composition ﬁeld parameter and three
order structure ﬁeld parameters [ 34]. The free energy
function described with four parameters acts as the input
for thermodynamic calculation with the phase-ﬁeld
method. Through solving the Cahn–Hilliard equation
associated with composition ﬁeld evolution and Ginzburg–
Landau equations associated with order parameter ﬁeld
evolution, the evolution of the ﬁeld parameters is obtained.
Generally, the free energy function mainly includes
chemical free energy (body free energy), interfacial energy,
elastic energy, etc. Chemical free energy density curves
have the form of a double potential well; therefore, chemical
free energy is always expressed with polynomials whose
coefﬁcients can be obtained by means of numerical ﬁtting
[22, 35]. Although polynomials succeed in expressing
chemical free energy, they are merely a mathematical
expression and convey nothing relating to the physical
nature of the material thermodynamics. With the develop-
ment of the CALPHAD method [ 30–33], especially the
framework of the sublattice model in the CALPHAD
method, it becomes convenient to denote the occupation of
solute atoms in lattices by way of a thermodynamic model,
which makes it possible to simulate the precipitation
behaviors of multicomponent alloys with the help of ther-
modynamic data. Zhu et al. [ 36] ﬁrstly applied the phase-
ﬁeld method based on the sublattice model to the simulation
of c
0 precipitation in Ni–Al alloy and initiated the simulation
of c0 precipitation by coupling the CALPHAD approach
with the phase-ﬁeld method. Based on that work, Wang et al.
[37] introduced interfacial energy and c/c0 lattice mismatch
obtained by the cluster variation method (CVM) to the
phase-ﬁeld method and simulated c
0 rafting. These works
make possible the simulation of c0 precipitation in multi-
component alloy systems. Based on coupling the CALP-
HAD method with the phase-ﬁeld method, Kitashima [ 38]
proposed the simulation of c
0 precipitation in multicompo-
nent superalloys. Although his model was based on the work
of Zhu et al. [ 36] and Wang et al. [ 37], he successfully
expanded the thermodynamic model to multicomponent
systems. Then for c
0 precipitation, the composition distri-
bution of alloy elements and the lattice occupation were
calculated in the Ni–Al–Re–Ta quaternary system [ 39] and
the Ni–Al–Co–Cr–Mo–Re–Ta–W multicomponent systems
[30]. The obtained results are given in Fig. 1.
However, the space scale calculated by such a model is
rather small, and another disadvantage is that the model
does not consider elastic effect into the model. Besides, c
0
precipitation is just restricted to the one-dimensional situ-
ation until now, which has not been realized in high-
dimensional systems.
3.2 c
0 Precipitations under supercooling conditions
c0 Precipitations previously studied by the phase-ﬁeld
method occur under isothermal conditions in which c0 only
precipitates once and sizes of c0 differ little. In the simu-
lation process, Langivin disturbance with Gauss distribu-
tion is introduced to the phase-ﬁeld equations to drive c0
precipitating. In this case, the Langivin disturbance term
meets ﬂuctuation–dissipation law [40] and vanishes once c0
nucleates. Although the simulation results match well the
actual isothermal nucleation process, this method cannot be
used to simulate multiple c0 precipitations under different

4 Progress of c0 rafting studies
4.1 Experimental study of c0 rafting
The c0 phase in nickel single-crystal superalloys is the most
important strengthening phase whose rafting behaviors
have a great effect on the comprehensive mechanical
properties of these alloys. So c
0 rafting has been attracted
much attentions. From experiments to simulations, the
studies of c0 rafting are mainly focused on two aspects, the
orientation and the driving force of the rafting, which can
be attributed to the inﬂuences of the applied stress, the
crystal lattice misﬁt and the c/c0 elastic modulus difference.
Besides, the relaxation of the strain energy, especially the
impact of dislocations, is very important to c0 rafting.
The experimental studies of c0 rafting started some time
ago. Tien and Copley [ 45] initially researched the occur-
rence of c0 rafting in nickel-based single-crystal superalloys
with [001] orientation and found that c0 rafting not only
changed thec0morphology but also inﬂuenced the formation

at the c/c0 interface. So it had a great effect on the
mechanical properties of these alloys [ 46]. By means of
Eshelby’s theory, Pineau [ 47] predicted c0 rafting under
uniaxial stress and proposed a rafting prediction map which
indicated the inﬂuences of the applied stress, the crystal
lattice misﬁt and the c/c0 elastic modulus difference on the
rafting direction and emphasized that thec0 rafting direction
depended on the ratio of the elastic constant difference
(c11 - c12) between c matrix and c0 precipitation. Based on
the analysis of the creep performance of single-crystal alloy,
Carry and Strudel [ 48] speculated that dislocations might
move along the boundary ofc0 particles by climbing and the
rafting direction be tightly related to the distribution of
dislocations on the boundary, which means that the disap-
peared c/c
0 interface was the interface where the plastic ﬂow
areas were covered by the formed dislocations. The plastic
deformation was the key factor in c0 directional rafting.
Miyazaki et al. [ 49] analyzed the anisotropy of c0 cubic
block and studied the c0 rafting direction by calculating the
energy and comparing the c0 size. According to the c0
morphology, Fredholm and Strudel [ 50] divided the rafts
into N-type and the P-type rafts, which were perpendicular
and parallel to the applied stress, respectively. Furthermore,
the N-type raft could weaken the fatigue performance of
alloys and, conversely, the P-type raft would improve it
[51]. In research of the creep properties of CMSX-4 alloy,
Nathal et al. [52] and Schneider et al. [53] pointed out thatc
0
rafting changed the connecting pattern between c phase and
c0 phase, in such a way that c matrix enclosing c0 phase
transited into the form ofc0 embedding c phase. This causes
alloys to lose the capability to deform and be susceptible to
cracking. So c0 rafting would make the creep intensity
decrease to some degree. c0 rafting from the directional
diffusion of alloy elements is induced by the stress gradient.
In other words, thec0 forming elements (Al, Ti, Ta, etc.) and
the c forming elements (Cr, Mo, etc.) diffuse in the opposite
direction, which leads to the growth and connection of c0
phase in a speciﬁc direction, and ﬁnally causes rafting.
Moreover, because the process of c0 rafting was mainly
controlled by solid-phase diffusion, the kinetics ofc0 rafting
took on a non-linear characteristic [ 8, 54]. Pollock and
Argon [55] studied the inﬂuence of plastic deformation and
dislocations on c0 rafting. They pointed out that the occur-
rence of c0 rafting was realized by local c0 solution and the
directional diffusion of alloy elements. The effect of solute
atoms embodied in the c/c0 lattice misﬁt, the rate of atom
diffusion during creep, and the driving force of c0 rafting
was based on the coherent stress of the c/c0 interface.
Meanwhile, the climbing and slipping of dislocations during
creep would promote c0 creep and accelerate the formation
of c0 rafts [ 56]. Although the rafted structure closed the
horizontal channel of dislocation motion and increased
creep resistance, after all the rafting was the result of c0
coarsening which has a negative effect on the properties of
alloy in most situations. Ignat et al. [ 57] studied the rela-
tionship between c0 rafting and the stress state. They pointed
out that atom diffusion driven by the unrelaxed crystal misﬁt
energy would weaken the interaction among the disloca-
tions. For N-type rafting, the atom diffusion was activated
on the four faces parallel to the stress direction. But for
P-type rafting, it was activated on the two faces perpendic-
ular to the stress direction. Matan et al. [58] proposed that for
a critical strain larger than 0.1 %, even a specimen without
loading still continued rafting. This can be explained by the
fact that when the critical strain was reached, more dislo-
cations twisted together in the c channel, which made the
dislocations hard to move. After the removal of the applied
stress, the dislocations accumulated at the c/c
0 interface,
which led to the occurrence of c0 rafting by dislocations
releasing lattice misﬁt energy. Fahrmann et al. [ 59] studied
the relationship between the c0 rafting direction and the c/c0
elastic modulus difference, and found that, for the elastic
model, the sign of the modulus difference would determine
the direction of c
0 rafting. Furthermore, the rafting direction
depended on the value [ r(Ec0 – Ec)/d(Ec)2], where r is the
applied stress, Ec0 and Ec are, respectively, the elastic
modulus of c0 phase and c phase, and d is the elastic
anisotropy constant; however, for elasto-plasticity model,
the direction ofc0 rafting just depended on the value [r/dEc].
Paris et al. [ 8] observed that the beginning of c0 rafting
coincided with the formation of dislocation networks, based
on which they derived that the mechanism of c0 rafting was
driven by the plasticity and the slip dislocations preferen-
tially formed in the horizontalc channels. c0 rafting behavior
involved three stages. The ﬁrst stage was the homogeneous
increasing of rafting. In the second stage, c
0 rafting speeded
up due to the redistribution of c0 forming elements (Al, Ti,
Ta etc.) on the length scale of individual precipitates by
diffusion ﬂow. The diffusion length scale of the precipitates
was about 100 nm. In the third stage, since the driving force
diminished, the speed of c
0 rafting slowed down. As the
dislocations at the c/c0 interface formed, the gradient of the
chemical potential changed. Compared with successive c/c0
interfaces, the dislocations at the interface were critical forc0
rafting. From the experiments, the integrated, successive c/
c0 interface was difﬁcult to preserve under long-time
annealing even for an applied stress as low as 40 MPa. It was
possible that the motion of dislocations beneﬁtted from the
inhomogeneous composition distribution and heating state.

0 rafting
In order to study c0 rafting by simulation, there are two
important questions to be considered. One is the selection
of methods, such as ﬁnite element [ 60], Monte Carlo [ 61]

and phase-ﬁeld methods [37]. The other is how to build the
model, such as thermodynamic model [ 62], mechanical
model [37], and plastic ﬂow model [ 25].
Based on the analysis of elastic energy, Socrate and
Parks [ 4] found that the driving force of c0 rafting was
proportional to the applied stress, the c/c0 misﬁt degree and
the c/c0 elastic modulus difference according to the results
obtained by the ﬁnite element method. c0 rafting could
obviously decrease the system energy. Matan et al. [ 58]
considered the inﬂuence of plastic deformation on c0 raf-
ting and found that c0 rafting must be followed by plastic
deformation, but the obvious c0 rafting needs to exceed the
critical strain by means of the plastic deformation model.
On this basis, Bufﬁere and Ignat [ 63] proposed the dislo-
cation criterion of c0 rafting, namely, dislocations ﬁrst
appeared in the c channels that relaxed the c/c0misﬁt stress.
And at the initial stage of creep, the interface propagation
would decrease the c/c0 misﬁt energy and promote c0 raf-
ting so as to decrease the energy of the system. Therefore,
the decrease in the system energy was the ultimate driving
force of c0 rafting. Osawa et al. [ 60] concluded from the
results of the ﬁnite element method that the distribution of
dislocations would favor the direction of c channels.
Ve´ron et al. [46], based on three-dimensional simulation
of c0 morphology, described the kinetics of c0 rafting, and
thought that the relaxation of the anisotropic lattice misﬁt
produced by dislocations was the driving force of c0 raf-
ting. In this model, the diffusional ﬂow of atoms caused by
composition gradient and by plastic ﬂow was calculated.
The local stress was obtained from the applied stress and
the misﬁt stress which depended on the distribution of
dislocations. The results showed that with the relaxation of
the lattice misﬁt, most of the loaded c channels would
ﬁrstly experience plasticity and dislocations preferentially
ﬁlling the channel interface vertical to the stress axis. But
the experimental results were in accord with the simula-
tions only at negative c/c
0 misﬁt under tensile conditions
and positive c/c0 misﬁt under compression conditions. It
was demonstrated that the channel preferentially ﬁlled by
dislocations relied not only on the loading direction but
also the sign of the lattice misﬁt. The results also revealed
that the heterogeneity of the elastic energy density drove
the diffusional ﬂow of atoms in the different type of
channels. The simulation showed that for N-type c
0 rafting
under tensile conditions and the P-type rafting under
compression conditions, c
0 rafting would speed up under
higher stress, and moreover, the speed of c0 rafting under
compression conditions was faster than that under tensile
conditions. Ve´ron et al. [ 46] presented a graph which
identiﬁed the relationship among the rafting direction, the
lattice misﬁt and the applied stress. However, the positive
misﬁt zone in the graph cannot be proved by experiments.
The reason might lie in too much attentions being paid to
the inﬂuence of plastic ﬂow on c
0 rafting. More analysis
showed that the effect of plastic ﬂow was opposite to the
effect of diffusional ﬂow of atoms in the positive misﬁt
zone. This was also proved in many creep experiments.
Only when the creep strain reached 0.2 % would c
0 rafting
ﬁnish. So the inﬂuence of plastic ﬂow was not the most
important if compared with the inﬂuence of the diffusional
ﬂow of atoms on c
0 rafting.
Nabarro [62] put forward a physical model regarding the
calculation of the thermodynamic pressure of c0 rafting.
The calculated results showed that the difference in the c/c0
elastic constant had little inﬂuence on c0 rafting when
plastic ﬂow occurs and the applied stress only increased the
free energy of c phase. According to the calculation of the
enthalpy and the c/c0 interface displacement, it was found
that the chemical thermodynamic pressure was initially
equivalent to 20 times the pure elastic thermodynamic
pressure. It can be predicted that interface migration under
the stress caused by free energy can be ignored. This means
that the higher thermal pressure caused by the body free
energy does not resist the thermal pressure of the elastic
effect. So c
0 rafting in this situation was impossible and
hard to be proved by experiments. In Nabarro’s model, the
equilibrium between only a single c channel and c0 phase
was considered. In fact, the difference in free energy
among the different kinds of c channel would lead to the
continuity of the diffusional ﬂow of atoms and the forward
migration of the c/c
0 interface. Besides, just from the
viewpoint of plasticity, the change in direction in the body
free energy of c phase under a particular stress is different
in the perpendicular or parallel c channel. If it is assumed
that a linear relationship would exist between local lattice
mismatch and a solute component, the reliability of the
Nabarro model is questionable.
Based on the calculation of the free energy density of
every c
0 cubic face, Arrell and Valle´s[ 64, 65] predicted the
morphology of c0 rafting by comparing the energy density,
but the model did not consider the inﬂuence of creep stress
and load caused by c0 rafting. The obtained interface
energy only came from the contribution of the dislocation

4.3 Phase-ﬁeld elastic model in c0 rafting studies
Applying the phase-ﬁeld method to study c0 rafting can
derive from the framework of microscopic elastic theory,
which had been summarized by Khachaturyan [ 68]. The
theory explained the relationship between the elastic ﬁeld
and c
0 morphology [ 69]. On this basis, considering the
difference in the elastic modulus tensor between matrix and
precipitate, microscopic elastic theory was extended to the
inhomogeneous elastic situation [ 70]. In the case, the der-
ivation of the inhomogeneous elastic formula provided the
theoretical basis for simulating the rafting behavior of c
0
phase by the phase-ﬁeld method. Chen and co-workers [ 71,
72] developed the perturb-iterative method and conjugate
gradient method to calculate the inhomogeneous elastic
ﬁeld, which prompted the improvement in the algorithm for
the calculation of the high-order elastic ﬁeld. Meanwhile,
semi-implicit Fourier spectral algorithm [ 73, 74] was pro-
posed, which obviously saved time and improved the cal-
culation accuracy in solving phase-ﬁeld equations. Wang
et al. [ 34, 75], and Rubin and Khachaturyan [ 22] consid-
ered the atom ordering through bringing the order param-
eter into the phase-ﬁeld model. This made the evolution of
c
0 morphology approximate to the results observed from
experiments. It successfully explained the splitting phe-
nomena in the process of c0 precipitation [ 13], and indi-
cated the inﬂuence of the ordering on c0 morphology. It
was pointed out that splitting occurred when the antiphase
interface energy was 0.5 times bigger than the boundary
energy. Based on the inhomogeneous elastic theory, Li and
Chen [ 76] analyzed c
0 rafting under the applied loading.
The results showed that the direction of the stress ﬁeld was
critical for the c0 rafting orientation, corresponding to the
experimental results. Yang et al. [ 77] and Wang et al. [ 37]
studied the inﬂuence of applied stress on the orientation of
c
0 rafting by means of the phase-ﬁeld method.
Using the elastic model to study the morphology of
N-type and P-type rafting, not only was the simulation
result conﬁrmed but also the inﬂuence of the applied stress,
the lattice misﬁt and the difference in the c/c
0 modulus was
considered in the phase-ﬁeld model. So this model has
advantages in the research of c
0 rafting. However, the
phase-ﬁeld elastic model regards c0 rafting as a diffusion
process induced by stress. It does not consider the inﬂuence
of crystalline defects, especially dislocations. According to
the experimental results of Ve´ron and Bastie [78], it is also
important to consider the inﬂuence of plastic ﬂow in the
process of c
0 rafting. It is necessary to develop the phase-
ﬁeld method with the elasto-plastic model to study c0
rafting.
4.4 Phase-ﬁeld elasto-plastic model in c0 rafting studies
In some experiments, a larger deformation of the sample
could result in spontaneous rafting even though stress was
not applied [ 46, 78], which illustrates that c0 rafting is
closely related to material plasticity. Recently, with the
development of the phase-ﬁeld method, the elasto-plastic
model was introduced into studies [ 10, 25, 79]. Hence the
elasto-plastic model of c0 rafting was developed. At pres-
ent, the elasto-plastic phase-ﬁeld model for the study of c0
rafting consists of three methods: Discrete Dislocation
Method (DDM) [ 25, 79], the effective plasticity strain
method [10], and the visco-plasticity method [ 11, 14]. The
ﬁrst two methods belong to the dislocation model of c0
rafting, which explains the rafting behavior in term of the
microscopic mechanism. The third method belongs to the
visco-plasticity model of c0 rafting, which puts the data of
macroscopic mechanics test into the phase-ﬁeld model to
investigate c0 rafting.
Many experiments have proved that dislocations are
related to c0 rafting and had a great effect on c0 rafting. Yeh
et al. [ 80] found that dislocations would gather on the
{001} c/c0 boundary of the \110[direction after applying
a load along the [001] direction in a period. Pollock and
Argon [ 55] found that for the negative misﬁt of single-

formed by the over-aging heat treatment was different from
the dislocation network brought about by loading. In their
research of CMXS-3 alloy crept at 850 /C176C/552 MPa, Pol-

stages. In the incubation period, the primary dislocations
increased. After that, the dislocation network began to
form. Finally, dislocations sheared the c

the dislocations perpendicular to the creep stress, the vis-

c

strated that the lattice misﬁt would absorb the dislocation
rings and make it difﬁcult for the dislocation rings to get
into the other type of c channel. Through the simulation of
phase ﬁeld [ 88], the preference of the dislocation channel
caused by the lattice misﬁt and the applied stress was
explained.
Use of the phase-ﬁeld method to simulate dislocations
was ﬁrst proposed by Le ´onard and Desai [ 89]. However,
applying to research of c
0 rafting has beneﬁtted from the
foundation of the DDM [ 25, 79, 90]. The initial thought
was to couple the dislocation method with solute diffusion
in the phase-ﬁeld model, which led to the generation of the
DDM phase-ﬁeld method. Zhou et al. [ 25] ﬁrst utilized the
DDM to simulate c
0 rafting. In this model, the dislocation
conﬁguration and the solute redistribution were decided by
the chemical gradient potential which was controlled by
three factors: c and c
0 microstructure, channel dislocations,
and applied stress. The c/c0 microstructure and the applied
stress could control the evolution of dislocations. The
fundamental thought was that the composition ﬁeld and the
dislocation ﬁeld could be brought into the phase-ﬁeld
model. The body free energy and interface energy in the
composition ﬁeld are the same as those in the general phase
ﬁeld, but the dislocation ﬁeld is expressed by several order
parameters, and it considers the contribution of every slip
system for system energy. Besides, the total elastic strain
stain consists of the stress-free stain, the strain brought
about by the channel dislocations and the applied stress.
Through the simulation of this model, the dislocation
conﬁguration and density in the c channel were ﬁnally
obtained, as well as the solute redistribution. The advan-
tage of the model was to take into consideration the con-
tribution of the dislocations to the rafting and ﬁgure out the
dislocation conﬁguration and density in c channels. How-
ever, nowadays the model does not consider the inﬂuence
of the ordering and all slip systems for c
0 rafting, so the
calculated dislocation conﬁguration is relatively simple.
Figure 3 shows the simulated results and Fig. 3b shows
that c
0 phase coalesces into a plate shape with the char-
acteristic of N-type rafting. This model was mainly focused
on the contributions of every dislocation, so it was named
the dispersive dislocation method. From simulation results,
it was proved that c
0 rafting is related to the dislocation
type and distribution. Meanwhile, the sign of the lattice
misﬁt and the direction of the load would determine the
dislocation conﬁguration in c channels.
Zhou et al. [ 26] introduced another elasto-plastic model
to simulate c0 rafting, called the effective plastic strain
model. The model does not consider the contribution of all
dislocations for c channel strain, but takes the dislocation
density into the phase-ﬁeld model and uses the equivalent
strain as the plastic strain of c channels. The difference
between the effective plastic strain model and the disper-
sive dislocation method is that the former does not consider
the effect of single dislocation, so it can not determine the
conﬁguration and arrangement of dislocations. It just takes
the plastic strain of c channels into the free energy

The phase-ﬁeld visco-plasticity method belongs to the
phenomenological model and was ﬁrst proposed in the
visco-plasticity study of solder alloy by Ubachs et al. [ 91]
Uehara et al. [ 92] and Guo et al. [ 93] applied the visco-
plasticity model to study phase precipitation in other
alloys. By using this model, Sarosi et al. [ 11] and Gaubert
et al. [ 14] investigated the behavior of c

obtained c0 morphology similar to that of experiment. They
also explained the coarsening of the anti-phase domains
and demonstrated the regularity of the strain orientation in
creep. The basic idea of the model is that it does not change
the body energy and interface energy in the elastic phase-
ﬁeld model, but the energy induced by stress consists of
two parts, the elastic energy and the visco-plastic energy.
The elastic energy can be obtained by subtracting the sum
of the stress-free strain and the plastic strain from the total
strain.
As it known, the slip system in Ni-base superalloys
includes twelve octahedrons, twelve dodecahedrons, and
six hexahedrons. But in the present study [ 14, 25, 26], just
the octahedral slip system is considered in the model. The
shear strain is deﬁned by the Norton ﬂow rule. The iso-
tropic hardening and kinematic hardening are considered in
the research. The visco-plastic energy only comes from the
inﬂuence of kinematic hardening. The parameters in the
phenomenological model rely on the ﬁnite element calcu-
lation or creep and fatigue experiments. The advantage of
the model lies in a good ﬁt with experimental results, and
furthermore it can connect macroscopic loading with
microscopic c
0 rafting. However, the model is based on the
coherent model of the phase ﬁeld, so it can only simulate
the ﬁrst two stages of creep. Besides, it can not explain the
rafting mechanism, as well as the dislocation model of the
phase ﬁeld can.
5 Prospects of the phase-ﬁeld method in c0 rafting
Though signiﬁcant progress has been made in the use of the
phase-ﬁeld method in investigating the c0 rafting behaviors
of Ni-base single-crystal superalloys, the level of simula-
tion of c0 rafting based on the phase-ﬁeld method is cur-
rently still in the initial period of setting up the
thermodynamics and mechanics model. As authors note,
the engineering application of the phase-ﬁeld method lies
in the accumulation and reliability of thermodynamic data,
which is a unique path to solve multicomponent problems.
The embodying of non-equilibrium thermodynamic con-
ditions in models and kinetic equations is quite important.
For a particular alloy system, it is necessary to obtain
information about crystal structure and micro-mechanics
for studying c
0 rafting, including the lattice constant and
elastic constant of c and c0phase, the motion and reaction
mechanisms of dislocations, etc. So realizing completely
the reappearance of c
0 rafting behaviors in true alloys is
still difﬁcult. It needs long-term developments to solve lots
of difﬁculties, including the lack of data, as well as the
improvement of models. In order to reach such aims, some
current challenges need to be overcome:
(1) To perfect the equilibrium thermodynamic database
of Ni-base superalloys and further improve the non-
equilibrium thermodynamic model. At the moment,
the non-equilibrium thermodynamic model for phase-
ﬁeld simulation is still oversimpliﬁed and can not be
used to deal with the complexity under non-equilib-
rium conditions.
(2) To set up the elasto-plastic model of multicomponent
alloys. The effects of individual alloying elements on
the lattice misﬁt, interfacial energy relaxtion, dislo-
cation reaction, and plastic ﬂow of matrix during
creep are different. It is necessary to take compre-
hensive consideration of all the factors of the elasto-
plastic model to reﬁne the phase-ﬁeld model.
(3) To simulate c
0 growth in the diffusional ﬂow ﬁeld.
The complexity of c0 morphology with dendrites,
spheres, and cubes indicated that c0 growth is closely
related to the diffusional ﬂow ﬁeld. The ﬂow ﬁeld
model has been used to simulate dendritic growth
during solidiﬁcation. However, it is open to discus-
sion to introduce it to solid-phase transformations.

previous works:

6 Summary
The phase-ﬁeld method is promising for use in investiga-
tion of c
0 rafting of Ni-base single-crystal superalloys with
the progress in the reﬁnement of the thermodynamic
model, extensive application of the CALPHAD method
and the improvement of the elasto-plastic model in the
phase-ﬁeld method. By coupling the phase-ﬁeld method
with the CALPHAD method, it is possible to simulate
multicomponent systems. The perfection of thermody-
namic models and their application in the phase-ﬁeld
method make the method suitable to simulate the c
0 rafting
of Ni-base single-crystal superalloys under near-engineer-
ing situation. Furthermore, the application of nucleation
theory in the phase-ﬁeld method allows it to simulate the c0
precipitation under different heat treatment process. A
synthetic application of the elasto-plastic model, CALP-
HAD method, and nucleation theory offers powerful
technical support for research into simulating the mor-
phology evolution during c
0 rafting of Ni-base superalloys
under the conditions of heat treatment, creep, and fatigue.
