ABSTRACT
Niobium alloys possess several advantages, including a high melting point, low density, high specific strength at 
elevated temperatures, and good workability, making them promising candidate materials for aerospace struc -
tural components. Investigation into their high-temperature properties and fracture behavior is therefore 
essential. This study examines the tensile properties of 0.5 mm-thick Nb – 5W – 2Mo – 1Zr alloy sheets over a 
temperature range of 400 – 1400 
◦
C. Based on the experimental results, stress-strain curves and predictive 
equations for the temperature dependence of the alloy ’ s mechanical properties are established. Furthermore, the 
deformation and fracture mechanisms of the alloy at various temperatures are elucidated. The results show that 
although the Nb – 5W – 2Mo – 1Zr alloy exhibits excellent high-temperature mechanical properties — with tensile 
strength, yield strength, and elongation at 800 
◦
C reaching 348.53 MPa, 183.5 MPa, and 36.73 %, respective -
ly — its tensile strength at 1400 
◦
C remains as high as 190.7 MPa. Nevertheless, the mechanical properties of this 
alloy are relatively sensitive to temperature, with strength decreasing as temperature increases. A marked 
reduction in tensile strength is observed when the testing temperature exceeds 400 
◦
C, with a more pronounced 
decline above 1300 
◦
C. Between 400 
◦
C and 1200 
◦
C, the primary tensile deformation mechanism is dislocation 
multiplication, and the dominant fracture mode is ductile transgranular fracture. At 1400 
◦
C, grain rotation and 
grain boundary sliding become the main deformation mechanisms, and the fracture mode transitions from a 
mixed transgranular and intergranular type at 1300 
◦
C to a typical intergranular fracture.
1. Introduction
Niobium alloys offer several advantages, including a high melting 
point, low density, excellent corrosion resistance, high specific strength 
at elevated temperatures, and good workability. These properties enable 
their use in manufacturing critical components for rocket engines, 
space-to-Earth shuttle vehicles, hypersonic aircraft, satellites, missiles, 
and nuclear reactors, positioning them as key candidate materials for 
aerospace structural applications [ 1 – 5 ]. Commonly used niobium alloys 
include PWC-11 (Nb – 1Zr-0.1C), C-103 (Nb-10Hf-1Ti), and Nb521 
(Nb – 5W – 2Mo – 1Zr) alloys [ 6 ]. In the aerospace sector, the most widely 
used alloy in the United States has been C-103, which operates within a 
service temperature range of 1200 – 1400 
◦
C, followed by alloys such as 
PWC-11 [ 7 , 8 ]. The Nb521 alloy, with a service temperature range of 
1200 – 1650 
◦
C, exhibits significantly enhanced high-temperature me -
chanical properties compared to the C-103 alloy. At 1600 
◦
C, its strength 
is 3 – 4 times greater than that of the C-103 alloy, and it has been suc -
cessfully employed in various orbital control and attitude control engine 
models [ 6 ].

et al. [ 9 ] investigated the high-temperature thermal deformation 
behavior of Nb – 1Zr-0.1C alloy using vacuum uniaxial compression tests 
in the temperature range of 700 – 1700

2. Material and method
Nb – 5W – 2Mo – 1Zr ingots were fabricated from high-purity raw ma -
terials ( > 99.9 %) via vacuum electron beam melting, provided by 
Ningxia Orient Tantalum Industry Co., Ltd. The chemical composition of 
the alloy was determined using inductively coupled plasma optical 
emission spectrometry (ICP-OES) and infrared absorption analysis, with 
detailed values presented in Table 1 . Following the application of an 
anti-oxidation coating, the ingots were heated to 1350 – 1400 
◦
C for high- 
temperature forging and subsequently forged into 30 mm-thick slabs. 
These slabs were subjected to multi-pass warm rolling at 400 – 500 
◦
C to 
produce 4 mm-thick sheets. After pickling to remove surface oxide 
layers, the sheets underwent three passes of cold rolling (each with a 
total reduction ratio of 50 %) and intermediate heat treatment to obtain 
0.5 mm-thick sheets. Final annealing was conducted in a vacuum heat 
treatment furnace at 1400 
◦
C for 1 h.
Tensile specimens for both room temperature (RT) and high- 
temperature (HT) tests were prepared along the rolling direction (RD).

length of 60 mm, and their geometry is illustrated in Fig. 1 (a). The HT 
specimens measured 110 mm in total length, with a 15 mm gauge 
length, as shown in Fig. 1 (b). The thickness for all specimens was 
maintained at 0.5 mm. Two tensile tests were performed for each con -
dition. RT tensile tests were conducted using an INSTRON 5582 elec -
tronic universal testing machine at a constant strain rate of 10

as illustrated in Fig. 6 (c).

5. Conclusion
In this study, 0.5 mm Nb – 5W – 2Mo – 1Zr niobium alloy sheets were 
fabricated through multi-pass cold rolling. The tensile deformation 
behavior and microstructural evolution of these alloy sheets were 
investigated over a temperature range of 400 – 1400 
◦
C. Predictive 
equations were established for the alloy ’ s stress – strain curves and 
temperature-dependent mechanical properties, and its deformation and 
fracture mechanisms were systematically analyzed. The main findings 
are summarized as follows. 
1. The microstructure of the 0.5 mm cold-rolled Nb – 5W – 2Mo – 1Zr alloy 
sheet after annealing at 1400 
◦
C for 1 h exhibited partial recrystal -
lization, with an average grain size of 11.2 μ m, indicating relatively 
fine grains. At RT, the alloy displayed an UTS of 443.8 MPa and an EL 
of 26.8 %, demonstrating a favorable combination of strength and 
ductility. Fracture morphology was primarily characterized by 
ductile tearing, with localized quasi-cleavage planes, suggesting a 
mixed fracture mode.
