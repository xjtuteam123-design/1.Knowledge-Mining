ABSTRACT 
Additive manufacturing is capable of achieving sophisticated geometries not attainable through conventional 
methods and it is especially attractive to fabricate conventionally hard-to-process Nb alloys via additive 
manufacturing for aerospace applications. In this study, a Nb521 (Nb-5W-2Zr-1Zr) alloy with relative density of 
99.0% was successfully processed by Selective Laser Melting (SLM). The SLM processed microstructure was 
characterized, showing high dislocation density, dislocation cell structure and a large population of low-angle 
grain boundaries. Nano-dispersoids of ZrO
2 
were found in the as-built microstructure whereas carbides were 
absent. Tensile testing was carried out at room temperature and 1200 
◦
C. The strengths of the SLM processed 
Nb521 were comparable or higher than those of the conventionally processed counterparts reported in literature 
at both room temperature and 1200 
◦
C, while the ductility was significantly lower due to high dislocation 
density, porosity and oxide dispersoids. The results of the present study are evidence to the feasibility of 
fabricating Nb521 by SLM and provide insights into the relationship between the SLM processed microstructures 
and the mechanical properties of Nb alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Among the refractory metals, Nb has been recognized for high 
temperature applications in the area of aerospace due to its relatively 
low density (8.58 g/cm
3
), high melting point (2472 
◦
C), relatively good 
high-temperature oxidation resistance and excellent mechanical prop -
erties [ 1 ]. Nb alloys can serve in sustained high temperature operating 
environments up to 1400 
◦
C [ 2 ] and are used in hot sections of aircraft 
and rocket engines [ 3 ]. However, the processability of Nb alloys has 
been a key drawback. Hot working is difficult because of the high 
strength at elevated temperatures [ 4 , 5 ], and subtractive machining 
methods limits the product to simple geometries and results in high 
buy-to-fly ratio. Recently, the emergence of additive manufacturing 
technology largely expands the design space of parts. Novel structures 
such as lattice structure and biomimetic flow channel, which are 
important for applications of heat exposure protection, can be fabricated 
conveniently [ 6 , 7 ]. In addition, additive manufacturing facilitates the 
integration of multiple parts/systems into one-piece, enhancing the 
performance and reducing the weight/volume, with a representative 
example being the rocket thruster with built-in cooling system made by 
Nb alloy [ 4 ]. Therefore, it is highly attractive to manufacture Nb alloys 
using additive methods. 
The existing published works on the fabrication of Nb alloys by ad -
ditive manufacturing are rather scarce and only a few reports are 
available, studying the most frequently used Nb521 [ 8 , 9 ] and C103 [ 4 , 
10 ] alloys. The results have shown that the additively manufactured Nb 
alloys can achieve similar or higher strengths compared with the 
wrought counterparts. Meanwhile, the microstructures of additively 
manufactured Nb alloys can be rather different from the conventional 
counterparts due to the incremental layer-by-layer fabricating process. 
Terrazas et al. [ 11 ] processed pure Nb with high relative density ( > 99%) 
using the Electron Beam Melting (EBM) method and achieved a higher 
strength but lower ductility compared with the wrought Nb. Yang et al. 
[ 9 ] fabricated a Nb521 alloy by EBM and manipulated the precipitation 
of carbides by taking advantage of the inherent thermal cycles of the 
EBM process. Chen et al. [ 8 ] investigated the microstructure and tensile

highly limited. Especially, to the authors ’ knowledge, no data is 
currently available regarding the high temperature mechanical behavior

their intended applications in aerospace propulsion systems. The present

between the SLM process, the as-built microstructures and the me -

2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Sample fabrication 
Powders of Nb521 alloy was prepared by Plasma Rotating Electrode 
Process (PREP) using alloy rods as the raw material. During the PREP 
process, the metal rod rotated about its longitudinal direction with one 
end of the rod melted, and the liquid metal was atomized centrifugally. 
The contents of Mo, Ta, W and Zr in the powders were analyzed by 
inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry and the contents of C and 
O were analyzed by the inert gas fusion method. Powders with the sizes 
within 15 – 53 μ m were sieved and used for the subsequent SLM process. 
The measured compositions of the alloy are shown in Table 1 . 
The SLM process was conducted in high-purity argon protective at -
mosphere ( ≥ 99.999%). An oxygen sensor was placed in the building 
chamber to monitor the oxygen level during processing and the sensor 
was integrated in a close-loop system for gas ventilation and oxygen 
level control. During SLM processing, the oxygen level of the building 
chamber was kept between 300 and 500 ppm. Still, Nb is highly sus -
ceptible to oxidation [ 23 ] especially in the form of powders, and it can 
be noted from Table 1 that the oxygen level increased from 0.005% 
before SLM to 0.080% after SLM. The laser parameters used in the SLM 
process are listed in Table 2 . The volumetric energy density (VED) is

The primary difference between the fabrication methods in Chen et al. ’ s 
and the present study is the powder feedstock. In that study, the powders 
were fabricated by a hydride-dehydride (HDH) process followed by jet 
milling. Whereas the powders in the present study were made by the 
PREP process. The HDH method is more cost-effective but the powders 
made are more angular and less spherical even after jet milling [ 4 ]; 
whereas the PREP process yields more spherical powders that are better 
suited for additive manufacturing [ 25 , 26 ]. Spherical powders could lead 
to better flowability during the SLM re-coating process and result in 
higher relative density. This difference is likely the reason behind the 
higher densification level achieved by SLM without HIP in this study.

carbide dispersoids are found in the microstructures [ 3 , 9 ]. 
In Nb521, Mo and W are infinitely miscible with Nb. The potential 
phases that could precipitate from the Nb solid solution include the 
meta-stable phase Nb
2

2 
when

processed Nb521 alloy; whereas in Chen et al. ’ s [ 8 ] study on an SLM 
processed Nb521, only ZrO
2

tions. The results of the present study agree with the latter. From the 
composition analysis of the Nb521 samples ( Table 1 ), although the ox -
ygen level of the fresh powder is very low (0.005 wt%), the SLM pro -
cessed bulk sample has a much higher content of oxygen (0.080 wt%). 
Oxygen likely enters the alloy during the handling of the powders and 
the SLM process (despite the argon protective atmosphere, there is still a 
300 – 500 ppm level of oxygen in the building chamber during 
processing). From Sankar et al. ’ s [ 30 ] investigation, an oxygen con -
centration of 500 ppm was sufficient to cause a ~16% increase in 
hardness near the surface region of a bulk sample of wrought C103 Nb 
alloy, indicating the penetration of oxygen. In the present study, the 
alloy powders used for SLM are expected to be even more susceptible to 
oxidation due to the significantly larger fraction of surface area 
compared with bulk material. The oxygen that entered the Nb solid 
solution interacts with Zr, forming ZrO
2 
in the as-built samples. In the 
Nb-Zr-O system, Nb can be in binary eutectic equilibria with ZrO
2 
[ 16 ] 
and the solubility of O in an Nb-Zr alloy is extremely low ( < 0.003 wt% at 
1450 
◦

thermodynamic calculations [ 8 ], Zr preferentially combine with O 
rather than forming the (Nb, Zr)C, which explains the presence of ZrO
2 
and the absence of (Nb, Zr)C. On the other hand, even without Zr, Nb 
alone can form Nb
2
C, which, however, is not observed in the present 
study either. This is likely due to the rapid cooling process of SLM and 
thus the precipitation of Nb
2
C is suppressed. 
3.2. Mechanical properties 
The Vickers microhardness of the as-built Nb521 samples is listed in 
Table 4 . The hardness values measured on different sample planes are 
very close to each other, and the hardness of the SLM processed Nb521 is 
closed to a Nb521 rolled with 50% deformation from Ivanchenko et al. ’ s 
[ 32 ] study. 
The engineering stress-strain curves of room temperature and 
1200 
◦
C tensile testing are plotted in Fig. 9 and the numeric strength and 
elongation data are listed in Table 5 . For room temperature tensile 
properties, the ultimate strengths of the present SLM processed Nb521 
(623 MPa) level with or exceed most of the reported values of the 
wrought (320 – 620 MPa [ 8 , 32 , 33 ]) and EBM processed (500 – 550 MPa 
[ 9 ]) counterparts. The high strength under room temperature is mainly 
attributed to the high dislocation density and the ZrO
2 
dispersoids as 
evidenced by the TEM micrographs in Fig. 7 . Compared with the 
wrought Nb521 alloys whose fracture elongation has been reported to 
be 12 – 20% [ 32 ], the measured ductility of the SLM processed Nb521 
(8.2%) is considerably lower. On one hand, despite the high relative 
density in general, there is still a number of micro-pores in the SLM 
processed Nb 521 samples ( Figs. 2 and 3 ). Stress concentration and crack 
generation at pores could cause early fracture and lead to large scat -
tering of the measured fracture elongation, as shown by the fractographs 
in Fig. 10 . Meanwhile, the high dislocation density in the microstructure 
also reduces the work hardening capability and deteriorate the ductility. 
Moreover, in the SLM processed samples the only type of dispersoid 
observed is ZrO
2 
( Figs. 7 and 8 ) and no carbides of Nb
2
C or (Nb, Zr)C 
were found. With same volume percentage of dispersoids, the 
strengthening effect of oxides in Nb is stronger than that of carbides due 
to smaller size and better dispersion [ 19 ], but the formation of oxides 
instead of carbides in Nb alloys is unfavorable for ductility [ 30 ]. The 
above factors all contribute to the low ductility of the SLM processed

4. Conclusions 
In this study, dense samples of a Nb521 alloy are processed by SLM. 
The as-built microstructure is characterized and the tensile properties at 
room temperature and 1200 
◦
C are tested. The main conclusions are as 
follows: 
(1) The average relative density of the as-built samples measured by 
Archimedes method is 99.0 ± 0.1% and the porosity measured on 
the micrographs is below 1.25 ± 0.37%. The primary type of pore 
is lack-of-fusion. 
(2) The average grain size is 14.2 ± 17.5 μ m on the X-Y plane and 
12.2 ± 15.7 μ m on the X-Z plane. A medium-strength texture of 
< 111 > ‖ Z direction is found. The as-built microstructure shows a 
high fraction of low-angle grain boundaries (5
◦
– 15
◦
), a high 
dislocation density and formation of dislocation cell structures 
due to the large thermal stress during SLM processing. The SLM 
processed Nb521 is highly sensitive to the processing atmo -
sphere. The only type of dispersoid observed the in the as-built 
samples is ZrO
2
, which forms because of oxygen diffusion into 
the Nb solid solution during powder handling and SLM process. 
(3) At room temperature, the SLM processed Nb521 sample shows a 
yield strength and ultimate strength of 539.9 ± 7.4 MPa and 
623.6 ± 0.9 MPa respectively. At 1200 
◦
C the average yield and 
ultimate strengths are 198.0 MPa and 222.0 MPa respectively. 
These strengths are generally similar with or higher than the 
conventionally processed counterparts due to high dislocation 
density and the presence of oxide dispersoids. However, corre -
spondingly, the ductility of the SLM processed Nb521 is much 
lower than that of the conventionally processed counterparts.
