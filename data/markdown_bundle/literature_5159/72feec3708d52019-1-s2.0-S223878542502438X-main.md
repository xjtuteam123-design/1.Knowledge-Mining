ABSTRACT
We developed a novel Al
0.3
CoCrNiTi
0.1 
high entropy alloy (HEA) comprising of face-centered-cubic (FCC) matrix 
and the L2
1
-Ni
2
TiAl precipitates. This alloy exhibits exceptional strength-ductility combinations at both room 
and cryogenic temperatures. The mechanical properties of this alloy increased when the temperature decreased. 
For instance, the yield strength, tensile strength and ductility at room temperature were estimated to 556, 1134 
MPa and 38 %, while those at cryogenic temperature were measured to 682, 1422 MPa and 36 %, respectively. 
Theoretical calculation of strengthening mechanisms indicated that the strengthening was mainly dominated by 
L2
1 
precipitates. Detailed investigation of deformation mechanisms through electron back-scattered diffraction 
(EBSD) and transmission electron microscope (TEM) revealed that the enhanced strain-hardening rate at cryo -
genic temperatures was associated with the L2
1 
precipitates, the reduced SF spacing and SF networks. These 
findings suggest that the Al
0.3
CoCrNiTi
0.1 
HEA could provide a promising candidate for advanced cryogenic 
structural applications.
1. Introduction
The pursuit of advanced structural materials with exceptional me -
chanical properties under extreme conditions has spurred significant 
interest in high entropy alloys (HEAs) [ 1 , 2 ]. These alloys, often char -
acterized by multi-principal elements and sluggish diffusion effects, 
exhibit remarkable properties such as high strength, exceptional 
ductility and enhanced fracture toughness across a broad temperature 
spectrum [ 3 , 4 ].
In general, HEAs form the face-centered cubic (FCC) or body- 
centered cubic (BCC) solid solutions, owing to the high configura -
tional entropy [ 5 , 6 ]. Among these, the FCC-based HEAs have demon -
strated outstanding mechanical performance at cryogenic temperatures 
[ 4 , 7 ]. For instance, CoCrNi HEA with an FCC structure exhibited 
excellent tensile strength exceeding 1 GPa, along with a notable strain 
hardening rate under cryogenic temperature [ 7 ]. These properties were 
ascribed with pronounced dislocation activity and deformation-induced 
nano-twinning due to the low stacking fault energy (SFE) of the CoCrNi 
HEA [ 7 , 8 ]. In fact, the SFE of CoCrNi HEAs is 22 mJ/m
2
, which is even 
lower than the well-known CrMnFeCoNi HEA (30 mJ/m
2
), facilitating 
twining-induced deformation at relatively early strains [ 8 ]. However, 
despite its remarkable cryogenic ductility, the insufficient yield strength 
(~300 MPa at room temperature) of CoCrNi HEA with single FCC phase 
restricts their potential in structural applications, which require high 
strength [ 9 ].
To overcome this limitation, several studies have explored conven -
tional strengthening mechanisms, such as solid solution strengthening, 
grain refinement, dislocation strengthening, and notably, precipitation 
strengthening [ 10 – 12 ]. Among various strengthening mechanisms, 
precipitation hardening is widely regarded as one of the most effective 
approaches for improving strength-ductility balance in FCC-based HEAs 
[ 11 , 13 ]. For example, in the CoCrFeNi alloy system, it was demonstrated 
that the development of L2
1
-Ni
2
TiAl type precipitates could lead to 
higher mechanical properties than that of the B2 – NiAl type precipitate

3.2. Tensile mechanical properties at room temperature
Fig. 4 (a) shows the mechanical properties of Ti 0, Ti 1 and Ti 2 alloys 
evaluated by tensile tests at room temperature. Note that the tensile tests 
were carried out twice, and the mechanical properties are presented as 
average values. The Ti 0 alloy shows the lowest yield and tensile 
strength of 359 and 808 MPa. With increasing Ti content, a progressive 
enhancement in strength is observed. For instance, the yield and tensile 
strength are 556 and 1134 MPa for Ti 1 and 668 and 1096 MPa for Ti 2 
alloy, respectively. Contrastingly, the Ti 0 alloy achieves the highest 
total strain of 46 %, but a gradual reduction is revealed from 38 % for Ti 
1 alloy and 5 % for Ti 2 alloy. These results suggest that mechanical 
performance is strongly influenced by the structural evolution of pre -
cipitates, such as size and volume fraction, indicating the importance of 
precipitation hardening effect.

assess this balance, the static toughness of the developed alloys was 
evaluated based on room-temperature tensile test results, as illustrated 
in Fig. 4 (b). Static toughness was determined by integrating the area

role in improving mechanical properties, which are frequently observed 
in materials with low SFE [ 37 ]. The Al
0.3
CoCrNi HEA showed the for -
mation of deformation twins [ 10 ]. In contrast to this, no deformation 
twins were observed with the addition of the Ti element. Although the 
addition of the alloying element results in a variation in SFE, the Ti 
element does not significantly increase the SFE from the first-principles 
calculations [ 42 ]. Furthermore, since the Ti element preferentially 
partitions into the precipitates rather than the matrix, a small amount of 
Ti element remains in the matrix ( Table 2 ). Thus, although the Ti 
element was added, the effect of the Ti element on the variation in SFE 
would be little.
Besides, to form the deformation twins, stress within the material is 
required to exceed the critical stress for twin formation, known as crit -
ical twinning stress σ
T 
[ 40 , 43 , 44 ]. This suggests that the critical twin -

0.3
CoCrNi HEA [ 10 ,
19 ]. Several studies on the precipitate-hardened alloys have reported 
that the precipitates play a role in increasing critical twinning stress [ 16 ,
40 ]. For instance, Zhao et al., reported that the presence of evenly 
distributed precipitates, such as L1
2 
precipitates, can lead to an increase 
in critical twinning stress by decreasing the width of FCC phase channel 
[ 40 ]. Hence, in contrast to the Al
0.3
CoCrNi HEA, which contains the 
B2 – NiAl type precipitates with a volume fraction below ~7 %, the 
absence of deformation twins in Ti 1 alloy could be related to the in -
crease in critical twinning stress due to the uniformly distributed L2
1 
precipitates with a volume fraction of ~18 % within the FCC matrix, 
thereby increasing the energy barrier for twin nucleation [ 16 , 45 ].
In addition, the temperature is another important factor influencing 
the deformation behavior [ 46 ]. Usually, the critical twinning stress is 
independent of variation in temperature, whereas the stress for dislo -
cation slip increases since the thermal activation of dislocations is 
difficult at lower temperatures [ 47 ]. That is, the deformation twin tends 
to form more quickly when the increased flow stress at lower tempera -
tures exceeds the critical twinning stress [ 47 ]. The deformation twin -
ning in Al
0.3
CoCrNi HEA is observed at lower temperatures ( 196 
◦
C) 
where thermal activation barriers exceed the critical twinning threshold 
[ 10 ]. On the other hand, the deformation temperature in this study is 
higher (i.e., 160 
◦
C), which could restrict the stresses required to 
initiate deformation twinning.

4. Conclusions
In summary, we successfully developed and characterized a novel 
Al
0.3
CoCrNiTi
0.1 
HEA strengthened by homogenously dispersed L2
1
- 
Ni
2
TiAl precipitates within an FCC matrix. The alloy demonstrated an 
outstanding synergy of high strength and ductility at both room and 
cryogenic temperatures, achieving a notable improvement over other 
single and dual-phase HEAs and cryogenic steels. At room temperature, 
the alloy achieved yield and tensile strength of 556 and 1134 MPa, 
respectively, and a total strain of 38 %. Upon testing at cryogenic 
( 160 
◦
C) temperature, both yield and tensile strength increased 
remarkably to 682 and 1422 MPa, respectively, with a negligible drop in 
total strain (about 36 %). Through theoretical calculation, we confirmed 
that the dominant strengthening mechanism at room temperature stems 
from precipitation strengthening through L2
1 
precipitates. An in-depth 
analysis and comparison of the deformation at room and cryogenic 
temperatures revealed that the superior strain-hardening capability, 
particularly at cryogenic temperatures, is associated with precipitation 
hardening through L2
1 
precipitates and reduced SF spacing and SF 
networks. In contrast to other low SFE FCC-based alloys, the absence of 
deformation twins in the Ti 1 alloy is associated with the fine distribu -
tion of L2
1 
precipitates and high deformation temperature. The current 
work offers valuable insights into achieving a refined balance between 
strength and ductility for cryogenic applications, positioning the 
designed Al
0.3
CoCrNiTi
0.1 
HEA as a promising candidate for structural 
materials operating under room and cryogenic environments.
