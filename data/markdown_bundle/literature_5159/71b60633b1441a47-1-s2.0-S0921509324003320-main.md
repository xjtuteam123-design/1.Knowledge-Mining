ABSTRACT 
Refractory multi-principle element alloys (RMPEAs) represent a promising class of high-temperature structural 
materials due to their excellent high-temperature mechanical properties. An important challenge in the appli -
cation of RMPEAs is achieving a suitable balance between high strength and ambient ductility. Previous in -
vestigations have suggested that vanadium (V) element plays a key role in maintaining the high strength and 
ductility of RMPEAs. Nevertheless, the fundamental mechanisms by which V influences the strength and ductility 
of RMPEAs remain unclear. To address this issue, a series of HfNbTaTiV
x 
( x = 0.00 – 1.00) alloys were designed to 
investigate the effects of V content on the microstructural and mechanical properties of RMPEAs. The results 
demonstrate that the prepared HfNbTaTiV
x 
alloys are single-phase body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solutions. V 
addition helps to improve the tensile yield strength of the alloys while concurrently preserving their ductility. 
The tensile yield strength increases from 729 MPa ( x = 0.00) to 997 MPa ( x = 1.00) with the increase of V. 
Impressively, the HfNbTaTiV
0.25 
alloy exhibits a tensile fracture plastic strain of 21.9%, which surpasses most 
existing RMPEAs. The increase in yield strength mainly comes from the intensification of local lattice distortion 
and solid solution strengthening caused by the introduction of V. Furthermore, the addition of V helps to 
decrease the elastic anisotropy of the alloys, which is advantageous for their processing quality. Overall, this 
study not only elucidates the role of V in conferring strength and ductility to RMPEAs but also provides valuable 
insights for the development and industrial application of RMPEAs. 
1. Introduction 
With the rapid development of aerospace, petrochemical and nuclear 
industries, more stringent requirements on high-temperature structural 
materials have been put forward [ 1 , 2 ]. Developing new materials with 
superior mechanical properties at high-temperature has always been 
one of forefront concerns in field of metallic materials [ 3 ]. Recently 
developed refractory multi-principle element alloys (RMPEAs) have 
aroused wide concerns as potential options for high-temperature 
applications due to their superior high-temperature strength, softening 
resistance, oxidation resistance, outstanding phase stability, and 
anti-irradiation properties [ 4 ]. Most existing RMPEAs consist of four or 
more refractory elements (IVB ~ VIB in the periodic table) in equiatomic 
or near-equiatomic ratio [ 5 ]. For example, the yield stress of NbMoTaW 
and VNbMoTaW alloys retains 405 MPa and 477 MPa at 1600

However, most of RMPEAs lack ductility at room temperature, similar to 
conventional refractory metals, which greatly hinders their practical 
engineering applications [ 1 ]. Such as, the compressive failure strain of 
NbMoTaW, VNbMoTaW, CoMoNbV and VNbMoTa alloys is just about

Therefore, developing new RMPEAs with high yield strength and 
ambient ductility is crucial for utilizing RMPEAs as structural materials. 
In recent years, several ductile RMPEAs have been designed by 
adjusting compositions. For example, adding Zr into the brittle NbMo -
TaW alloy can increase its compressive yield strength from 1070 ± 16.8 
MPa to 1618 ± 14.2 MPa [ 9 ]. The compressive failure strain improved 
from 1.5 ± 0.06 % (NbMoTaW) to 20 ± 0.60 % (NbMoTaWZr) by 
adjusting Zr content [ 9 ]. HfNbTaTiZr alloy exhibits high yield strength 
(875 MPa) and larger fracture elongation (22.8%) at 277 K [ 10 ]. The 
HfNbTaTiZr alloy withstands a compressive strain of > 50% without 
failure [ 11 ]. The HfNbTaTiZr alloy also presents a high ductility at 
cryogenic temperatures, i.e., the fracture elongation retains 20.8% at 77 
K, without obvious ductile to brittle transition in the temperature range 
of 277 K – 77 K [ 10 ]. An equiatomic VNbTa alloy presents high tensile 
yield strength (1033 MPa), outstanding fracture elongation ( > 19%) and 
super rolling formability ( > 80% thickness reduction) at room temper -
ature [ 12 ]. More recently, an ambient ductile HfNbTaTiV alloy was 
designed based on intrinsic element ductility principles, valence elec -
tron concentration criterion and calculation phase diagram (CALPHAD) 
[ 13 ]. The HfNbTaTiV alloy has high tensile yield strength (997 MPa), 
work hardening ability, and super cold rolling formability ( > 84% 
thickness reduction) [ 13 ]. The high strength of HfNbTaTiV alloy roots in 
severe local lattice distortion induced by the misfits of different elements 
in atomic sizes and mechanical moduli. Previous studies reveal that V 
element plays a key role in the solid solution strengthening and lattice 
distortion of HfNbTaTiV alloy [ 13 ]. 
Existing researches demonstrate that vanadium (V) is an optimal 
element for providing high strengthening in both body-centered cubic 
(BCC) and face-centered cubic (FCC) multi-principle element alloys 
[ 14 ]. The addition of V creates a significant solute misfit, leading to 
enhanced solid-solution strengthening in multi-principal element alloys.

These results reveal that V is a key element for designing RMPEAs with 
high yield strength and ductility. However, research on the effects of V 
addition on the material properties of RMPEAs is still in its early stages 
and has not been extensively explored. Especially, the physical mecha -
nisms responsible for the strengthening and ductilization effects of V in 
RMPEAs still remain unclear. The current effort is primarily focused on 
uncovering the effects of altering V content in RMPEAs from an alloy 
design perspective in order to develop new RMPEAs with improved 
ductility and customize their composition for specific applications. 
In this work, a series of HfNbTaTiV
x

designed to examine the effects of V content on the microstructural and 
mechanical properties of RMPEAs. To understand the impacts of V on 
the microstructure, phase formation, and composition segregation of the 
alloys, various analytical techniques such as X-ray diffraction (XRD), 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and CALPHAD were employed. In 
addition to the microstructural analysis, uniaxial tension tests and 
microhardness tests were conducted to evaluate the influence of V on the 
mechanical properties of the alloys. The effects of V on their strength -
ening mechanisms, lattice distortion, electronic and elastic properties 
were also discussed based on the results obtained from density func -
tional theory (DFT) calculations. These calculations provide insights 
into the fundamental mechanisms responsible for the observed changes 
in mechanical behavior due to the addition of V.

2.3. Computational methods 
CALPHAD calculations were performed to predict both equilibrium 
and non-equilibrium phase diagrams of the alloys. The TCHEA5 ther -
modynamic database of ThermoCalc was utilized for these calculations 
[ 20 ]. Furthermore, DFT calculations were conducted to investigate the 
impact of V addition on various properties of the alloys, including lattice 
distortion, mechanical anisotropy, electronic characteristics, and ther -
mal properties. To accomplish this, a set of supercells containing various 
V concentrations were constructed by using the special quasi-random 
structure (SQS) method [ 21 ]. Indeed, the SQS method has been widely 
employed in simulating the chemical disorder and random distribution 
of multiple elements within high-entropy materials [ 22 , 23 ]. The SQS

3.4. Elastic properties 
Elastic deformation is commonly recognized to be the first stage of 
material deformation. The elastic properties of materials play a crucial

4.3. Electronic structure 
The structural and mechanical properties of materials are closely 
related to their electronic structure. In order to understand the effect of 
V content on the electronic structure of HfNbTaTiV
x 
alloys, the elec -
tronic density of states (DOS) were calculated. The DOS provides in -
formation about the distribution of electronic states in the material, 
including the energy levels and their occupancy [ 43 ]. By examining the 
DOS, one can insight into the electronic band structure, such as the 
density of states near the Fermi level. Fig. 12 a plots the total density of 
states (TDOS) and projected density of states (PDOS) curves of 
HfNbTaTiV alloy. The vertical dash lines at 0 eV mark the Fermi energy 
level. The Fermi level passes through the shoulder of TDOS peak, indi -
cating the good electrical conductivity of the alloy. Comparing the TDOS 
and PDOS curves in Fig. 12 a, it is seen that the TDOS peak around the 
Fermi level is mainly superposed by d -states elections. This result in -
dicates that the metallic conducting feature of the alloy is mainly orig -
inated from the d -states elections of transition metal elements. Fig. 12 b 
displays the influence of V on the TDOS of HfNbTaTiV
x 
alloys. The 
distribution range and shape of the TDOS curves are not significantly 
affected by adding V. This phenomenon implies that these alloys exhibit 
similar electronic properties. It also implies that no solid-state phase 
transformation is caused by the addition of V. 
Fig. 13 compares the electron localization function ( ELF ) distribution 
on a close-packed (1 1 0) plane of V
0.00 
and V
1.00 
alloys. The magnitude 
of ELF ranges from 0 to 1, which is often used as an important indicator 
for assessing chemical bonding feature. ELF = 0 indicates the delocal -
ization of electrons. ELF = 0.5 implies homogeneous electron gas and 
metallic binding feature. ELF = 1 denotes the localization of electrons, 
corresponding to covalent bonding feature [ 36 ]. Fig. 13 a shows that the 
ELF distribution is inhomogeneous. Most of areas within four neighbor 
atoms are painted as green-to-yellow ( ELF ~0.5), demonstrating homo -
geneous electron gas in these regions. Inhomogeneous ELF distribution 
results in severe lattice distortion and variation in bond length and bond 
angles. The ELF values around V and Nb atoms are larger than those of 
Ta, Hf, and Ti atoms. It means that V and Nb addition tends to increase 
electronic localization. Ti atoms are surrounded by light blue (low ELF ) 
regions, demonstrating electronic delocalization around Ti atoms. 
5. Conclusions 
In this study, a series of ductile HfNbTaTiV
x 
single-phase BCC alloys 
were successfully fabricated using vacuum arc melting to investigate the 
influence of V content on their material properties. XRD and CALPHAD 
results demonstrate that the addition of V does not affect the phase 
formation of the alloys, but results in a decrease in the lattice constants 
and an increase in chemical microsegregations. The addition of V helps 
to improve the tensile yield strength and ductility of RMPEAs. Notably, 
the V
0.25 
alloy exhibited a fracture plastic strain of 21.9%, surpassing 
many existing RMPEAs. The enhanced strength is primarily attributed to 
solid solution strengthening facilitated by V addition. The V addition 
tends to increase local lattice distortion and electronic delocalization in 
the alloys, which in turn enhance their yield strength and hardness. The 
V addition has a limited effect on their elastic moduli, but tends to 
reduce their elastic anisotropy. The decrease of anisotropy is advanta -
geous in mitigating deformation texture and crack formation in plastic 
manufacturing process. This study not only elucidates the effects of V 
addition on the material properties of HfNbTaTiV
x 
alloys, but also 
provides valuable insights for designing novel RMPEAs with high 
strength and ambient ductility. However, the underlying physical 
mechanism behind the improvement in ductility of HfNbTaTiV
x 
alloys 
due to V addition remains unknown. Therefore, further investigation is 
warranted to explore the influence of V on the plastic deformation 
mechanisms of RMPEAs by utilizing transmission electron microscopy 
and atomistic simulations.
