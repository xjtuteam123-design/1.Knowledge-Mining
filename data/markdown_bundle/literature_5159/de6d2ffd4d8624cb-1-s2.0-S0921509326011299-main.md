ABSTRACT
Tungsten (W) and its alloys are widely used in micro-nano electronic fields, making the understanding of size and 
alloying effects on mechanical properties and deformation behaviors at these scales increasingly significant. In 
this study, the mechanical properties and deformation mechanisms of pure W, tungsten-rhenium (W-3Re), and 
tungsten-tantalum (W-3Ta) alloys were systematically investigated via micropillar compression. The results 
reveal that Re and Ta alloying exert distinct effects on the yield strength of micropillars with diameters ranging 
from 1 μ m to 5 μ m (Re decreases, Ta increases this property), with the strength value is positively correlated with 
the stress required for dislocation nucleation. Deformation behavior exhibits a marked dependence on both 
micropillar size and alloying. At the 1 μ m scale, the size effect plays a dominant role. The pronounced surface 
image force enhances the mobility of screw dislocations, inducing localized slip deformation dominated by mixed 
dislocations in all specimens. At larger diameters of 3 μ m and 5 μ m, the plastic deformation of pure W is gov -
erned by the cross-slip of screw dislocations, resulting in homogeneous slip deformation. In constant, the plastic 
deformations of W-3Re and W-3Ta alloys are dominated by localized slip, which is attributed to the increased 
mobility of screw dislocations. Further analyses show that Re alloying reduces the Peierls barrier for screw 
dislocations motion and thereby enhances their mobility. Differently, Ta alloying increases this barrier but re -
duces the activation volume required for kink pair nucleation, ultimately improving the mobility of screw 
dislocations.
1. Introduction
With the rapid development of micro-nano technology, the me -
chanical properties of materials under small-scales have attracted 
extensive attention [ 1 – 3 ]. The micropillar compression test has the 
unique advantages including precise sample size control, sensitive 
micro-mechanical response, and effective elimination of interference 
from macroscopic defects in bulk materials [ 4 – 6 ]. It has emerged as the 
standard experimental approach for quantitatively characterizing the 
strength size dependence and deformation mechanism of materials at 
micro-nano scale, such as typical face-centered cubic (FCC) [ 7 – 9 ] and 
body-centered cubic (BCC) [ 10 – 13 ] metal systems.
A significant size effect was observed in micropillar compression 
experiments, commonly summarized as “ smaller is stronger ” , where the 
flow stress increases monotonically with the reduction in micropillar 
diameter [ 14 – 16 ]. The size effect is usually described by a power-law 
relationship σ

low, and the plastic deformation is predominantly determined by screw 
dislocations [ 21 , 22 ]. These intrinsic characteristics determines the 
unique mechanical behavior and research value of BCC structure metals 
at small-scale.
Tungsten (W) as a typical BCC refractory metal, has been subjected to 
preliminary investigations regarding its yield strength evolution and 
deformation behavior under varying micropillar diameters and tem -
peratures [ 10 , 11 , 17 ], and the corresponding findings can provide a 
fundamental theoretical foundation for the micro-nano scale applica -
tions of pure W. Nevertheless, the inherent low-temperature brittleness 
and high processing difficulty of pure W severely restrict its practical 
application [ 23 – 25 ]. Alloying with rhenium (Re) and tantalum (Ta) has 
been demonstrated to be an efficient strategy for regulating the me -
chanical properties of W-based materials [ 25 – 31 ]. Re exhibits a 
solid-solution softening effect by reducing the Peierls barrier and 
enhancing screw dislocation mobility, thereby improving the ductility of 
W materials [ 25 – 28 ]. In contrast, Ta induces solid-solution strength -
ening through lattice distortion and grain refinement, resulting in 
enhanced strength [ 29 – 31 ]. However, these studies mainly focused on 
bulk W-based materials, and conventional macroscopic mechanical 
testing methods failed to elucidate the underlying mechanism of the 
alloying on mechanical properties and deformation behavior at 
small-scale. Moreover, the possible coupling interaction between size 
effect and Re/Ta alloying on W micropillar remains unclear.
In this study, pure W, W-3Re, and W-3Ta alloys were selected as 
research objects. Micropillar specimens with different diameters were 
prepared via focused ion beam (FIB) technique, and their stress-strain 
responses, yield strengths and deformation behaviors were analyzed 
using micropillar compression test. Based on the variation in yield 
strength and slip mode of three materials with different micropillar di -
ameters, we systematically analyzed the dislocation mechanism and 
revealed the distinct effects of Re/Ta alloying on mechanical behaviors 
of W single crystal micropillars. The findings of this work are dedicated 
to provide an experimental basis and theoretical reference for the ma -
terials design and practical application of W-based alloys at micro-scale.
2. Experimental and calculation methods
2.1. Sample preparation
Single crystals of pure W, W-3Re, and W-3Ta (wt. %) alloys were 
grown via electron beam floating zone melting technique using a < 001 >
orientated seed crystal. The Re content of 3 wt % was selected based on 
previous studies on W-Re alloys [ 25 , 32 – 34 ], where this concentration 
has been widely used as a representative alloying level. The same con -
centration was adopted for Ta to enable a direct comparison between the 
effects of both alloying elements. The as-grown single crystals were 
sliced into 3 mm thick disks using electric discharge wire cutting. Sub -
sequently, the disks were successively ground with diamond pastes and 
mechanically polished. To eliminate the surface stress layer induced by 
mechanical processing, electrochemical polishing was subjected in a 
1 wt % NaOH aqueous solution at a voltage of 20 V for 60 s. The elec -
tropolished single crystal samples were then utilized for crystallographic 
orientation verification, surface morphology analysis, and micropillar 
fabrication.
Micropillars were fabricated using a Helios G4 CX dual-beam FIB/ 
SEM equipped with a Ga ion source. Micropillars with target diameters 
of 1 μ m, 3 μ m, and 5 μ m were fabricated with a height-to-diameter ratio 
of approximately 2:1. Representative SEM images of the prepared 
micropillars are shown in Fig. S1 . The micropillars were prepared using 
a multi-step milling procedure. Coarse and intermediate milling were 
performed at an accelerating voltage of 30 kV using progressively 
decreasing beam currents of 21 nA, 9.3 nA, 2.5 nA, and 0.43 nA to 
gradually refine the micropillar geometry. A final low-energy cleaning 
step was carried out at an accelerating voltage of 5 kV with a beam 
current of 41 pA to reduce Ga ion implantation and FIB-induced surface 
damage. Previous studies have shown that the damaged surface layer 
induced by 30 kV Ga ion milling can be reduced to approximately 20 nm 
after 5 kV polishing [ 35 ], and Ga ion implantation is also significantly 
reduced during the low-energy cleaning process [ 36 ]. For the smallest 
1 μ m micropillars used in this work, a damaged-layer thickness of 20 nm 
corresponds to an affected cross-sectional area fraction of approximately 
7.8 %. The damaged region remains confined to a thin near-surface layer 
and is not expected to be the dominant factor responsible for the 
observed size-dependent strengthening.

W-3Ta alloys, no relevant studies reporting power-law exponents from 
micropillar compression experiments are currently available. Thus, the 
present results can serve as reference data for future experimental and 
theoretical studies on the size-dependent deformation behaviors of 
W-3Re and W-3Ta alloys.

3.3. Post-compression micropillar deformation mode analysis
Fig. 5 shows the SEM images of representative post-compression 
micropillars with different diameters. The W-3Re and W-3Ta alloy 
micropillars exhibit localized slip deformation characteristic (marked by 
red dotted line) across all diameter scales. In contrast, the deformation 
mode of pure W micropillars transforms from localized slip to homo -
geneous slip (indicated by blue arrows, characterized by the absence of 
discrete slip planes) as the micropillar diameter increases from 1 μ m to 
3 μ m and 5 μ m. The deformation behavior of BCC metal micropillars is 
governed by the mobility of screw dislocations, as schematically illus -
trated in Fig. 6 . The homogeneous slip morphology is dictated by the 
frequent cross-slip of screw dislocations. Owing to the unique non- 
planar core structure of screw dislocation in BCC metals, these screw 
dislocations can overcome the high Peierls barrier through thermally 
activated cross-slip between crystallographic planes intersecting along 
the < 111 > direction [ 49 ]. The localized slip indicates that a limited 
number of slip systems were activated, a process dominated by mixed 
dislocations. This deformation is confined to specific slip plane and the 
mobility of the screw dislocations increases to be comparable to that of 
edge dislocations [ 10 , 17 ].
It should be noted that the proposed deformation mechanism is 
inferred from room-temperature micropillar compression. Temperature- 
dependent micropillar studies on pure W have shown that its plastic 
deformation is closely related to thermally activated screw dislocation 
motion [ 10 , 11 ]. Re and Ta alloying can modify screw dislocation 
mobility of W micropillars, leading to deformation characteristics 
consistent with those observed in the temperature-dependent deforma -
tion conditions. In addition, all compression experiments in the present 
work were performed on single-crystal micropillars with the same 
< 001 > crystallographic orientation in order to isolate the intrinsic ef -
fects of Re and Ta alloying while eliminating the influence of crystal -
lographic orientation. Previous studies have shown that tungsten single 
crystals with different crystallographic orientations, including < 001 > , 
< 110 > , and < 111 > , exhibit different mechanical responses [ 50 , 51 ]. 
Therefore, the conclusions drawn in the present work should be inter -
preted within the context of < 001 > oriented tungsten micropillars. 
Whether similar alloying effects can be extended to other crystallo -
graphic orientations requires further experimental investigation.

3.4. Peierls barrier and activation volume analysis
The above analysis indicates that the deformation mode during the 
micropillar compression process is determined by the mobility of screw 
dislocations. The motion of screw dislocation is usually affected by 
Peierls barrier, a lower Peierls barrier indicates reduced resistance 
during dislocation slip and higher dislocation mobility. As shown in 
Fig. 8 (a), compared to pure W, the variation in the barrier after alloying 
can be represented by the value of Peierls misfit [ 21 ]. The negative 
misfit value of the W-3Re alloy indicates a reduced barrier, whereas the 
positive misfit value of the W-3Ta alloy indicates an increased barrier. 
Apart from the decisive factor of the Peierls barrier, the mobility of 
screw dislocations is also regulated by the nucleation and motion of kink 
pair. Screw dislocations overcome the barrier by kink pair nucleation 
and propagation, and ultimately drives the entire screw dislocation slip,

4. Conclusions
This study reports the systematic investigation on the micropillar 
compression tests of the W-3Re and W-3Ta alloys, with a focus on the 
distinct effects of Re and Ta alloying on the mechanical properties and 
deformation behaviors of W-based single crystal micropillars. A signif -
icant size effect on yield strength is observed in all micropillar samples, 
with strength increasing as the micropillar diameter decreases. The 
strength value correlates with the critical shear stress for dislocation 
nucleation, and Re and Ta alloying induce the solid solution softening 
and strengthening effects, respectively. For the deformation behavior of 
micropillars with a diameter of 1 μ m, the size effect plays a dominant 
role compared with alloying. The large surface image force increases the 
mobility of screw dislocations, thus inducing localized slip deformation 
in all three materials. For micropillars with diameters of 3 μ m and 5 μ m, 
pure W exhibits homogeneous slip deformation mediated by the cross- 
slip of screw dislocations. Compared with pure W, the increase in the 
mobility of screw dislocations in W-3Re and W-3Ta alloys leads to 
localized slip deformation governed by mixed dislocations. Re alloying 
decreases the Peierls barrier and further enhances the mobility of screw 
dislocations. Although Ta alloying raises the barrier, it reduces the 
activation volume required for kink pair nucleation, which also con -
tributes to the enhanced mobility of screw dislocations.
