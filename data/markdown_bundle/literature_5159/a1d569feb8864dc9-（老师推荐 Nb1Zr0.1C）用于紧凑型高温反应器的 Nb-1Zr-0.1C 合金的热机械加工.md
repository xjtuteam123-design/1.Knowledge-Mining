Abstract : Nb-1Zr-0.1C is a potential material for use in high temperature nuclear reactors. Use of this
alloy in components requires appropriate thermomechanical processing to break the cast microstructure
and to obtain uniformly distributed fine stable precipitates so as to produce the desired mechanical
properties at the high operating temperatures. This report reviews the thermomechanical processing of
Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy carried out over the years by other researchers and the high temperature creep behavior
of the alloy. The hot deformation of Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy carried out at Mechanical Metallurgy Division is
also presented here. From this review it is evident that most primary hot working studies were carried out
between 1500 to 1700 °C. The subsequent annealing treatments, which require holding at lower
temperatures of about 1100 to 1300 °C for very long times help further transform the precipitates from
coarse orthorhombic to very fine cubic. Our studies on Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy also confirm that optimum hot
working lies at temperatures beyond 1500 °C where dynamic recrystallization initiates, and optimally
around 1700 °C where dynamic recrystallization transforms the microstructure. Working at temperatures
lower than 1000 °C may lead to the undesirable effect of both micro as well as macro strain localization,
and should be avoided.

1 Introduction 
The development of Nb alloys for use as structural materials in  nuclear reactors 
was  started  in  the  early  1960’s  by  NASA  for  the  specific  applic ation  of  nuclear 
powered reactors in space vehicles referred to as Space Nuclear  Power Systems (for 
example see Ref. [1‐3]).  These are low powered systems of 10’s  to 100’s kW e with 
an operating life of around 5 to 10 years (see Ref. [4] for ove rview of these systems).  
The research in refractory alloys for nuclear applications did  halt for a decade after 
which  the  1980’s  saw  a  renewed  interest  in  the  use  of  refractory  alloys  in  nuclear 
f i s s i o n  r e a c t o r s  i n  s p a c e .   T h e  s p e c i f i c  r e q u i r e m e n t s  f o r  a l l o ys  to  be  used  in  Space 
Nuclear  Powered  Systems  were  their  ability  to  retain  strength  at  relatively  high 
temperatures  (1000  to  1200 °C),  long  term  thermal  creep  strength  (less  than  1%

liquid  alkali  metals,  and  low  parasitic  neutron  cross‐section  [5 ] .   N b  a l l o y s  s a t i s f y

6 Recommendations for hot working of Nb­1Zr­0.1C 
The  use  of  Nb‐1Zr  alloys  in  nuclear  reactors  would  require  processing  of  the 
cast alloy in appropriate steps to finally achieve the desired  form.  As seen from the 
discussion  in  this  report,  cast  Nb‐Zr‐C  systems  contain  micron  sized  Nb 2C 
precipitates  in  a  matrix  having  an  inhomogeneous  chemical  compo sition  (as 
characteristic  of  cast  alloys  in  general).    The  purpose  of  the primary  hot  working 
process is to break the cast structure, i.e. break dentritic formations, homogenize the 
alloying elements and refine the precipitates.  In the present  case of Nb‐1Zr‐0.1C the 
additional criteria would be to  transform the precipitates from Nb2C to (Nb,Zr)C.  As 
seen in section 3, most primary hot working studies were carrie d out between 1500

Hot deformation behavior of Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy in the temperature range 700-1700 °C 
A. Sarkar, R. Kapoor, A. Verma, J.K. Chakravartty†, A.K. Suri 
Materials Group, Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, Mumbai 400085, India. 
Abstract 
Nb-1wt%Zr-0.1wt%C was hot deformed in vacuum in uniaxial compression to a true strain of 
about 0.6 in the temperature range 700 to 1700 °C and strain rate range 10 -3 to 10 s -1. From the 
stress, temperature and strain rate data iso-strain rate sensitivity contour maps were generated. 
Two distinct regimes were identified in the processing map: one with zero to negative strain rate 
sensitivity ranging from 700 to 900 ºC at all th e strain rates, and second a high strain rate 
sensitivity domain around 1700 ºC and 10 -3 s-1. In the first regime shear bands were seen within 
grains indicating strain localization. On the basis of detailed metallographic investigation and 
kinetic analysis, it was established that the domain corresponding to strain rate sensitivity 0.2 is of 
dynamic recrystallization. At deformation cond itions away from this domain the microstructure 
was found to be heterogeneous with some gr ain boundaries showing wavy nature, some small 
grains located at these prior grain boundaries and n ear absence of new grains within larger grains, 
suggesting partial/incomplete dynamic recrystallizat ion. A stress exponent of 5 and activation 
volume of 200-500 b3 within this domain suggests a dislocation based mechanism during dynamic 
recrystallization. 
 
Keywords: Nb-1Zr-0.1C, hot deformation, dynamic recrystallization, strain localization. 
1 Introduction 
Nb alloys have been studied for their possibl e use in high temperature nuclear reactors, as 
they retain strength at relatively high temperatur es, have long term thermal creep strength, good 
compatibility with liquid alkali metals, and relativ ely low neutron absorption cross-section [1-4]. 
Of the various Nb alloys, Nb-1wt%Zr is one of th e earlier alloys to be studied and still remains an 
alloy of interest as a potential material for adva nced nuclear reactors [5-8]. Zr provides solid 
solution strengthening to the Nb matrix. A variant of this alloy contains 0.1 wt% C to provide 
dispersion strengthening due to Nb and Zr carbides [9]. Investigations on both Nb-1Zr and Nb-
1Zr-0.1C carried out in 1980’s and 90’s dealt pr imarily with precipitation characteristics in the 
carbon containing alloy [10-15] and the creep behavior for both alloys [16-24]. The creep studies 
on these alloys can be classified as creep to lo w strains by Titran and co-workers [16-19] and 
creep at higher stresses to steady state by Mukherjee and co-workers [20-23]. Some studies on 
the general mechanical behavior of Nb-Zr alloys at elevated temperature have also been carried 
out, but these cover a narrow strain rate and temperature regime [7, 25]. Apart from these studies 
there does not appear to be any comprehensiv e study available in open literature on the hot 
deformation behavior of Nb-1wt%Zr or Nb-1Zr-0.1 C alloy. The aim of the present investigation

3 Results and Discussion 
3.1 Flow behavior 
Fig. 1 shows representative stress-strain curves of Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy deformed at different strain 
rates and test temperatures. The nature of the flow curves exhibited by the material can be 
broadly classified in to three types. 
i) At temperatures lower than 1000 ºC and at all st rain rates the flow curves are clustered together 
indicating that the flow stress is virtually independent of temperature and strain rate. 
ii) At temperatures of 1000 °C and up to 1400 °C and strain rates higher than 0.1 s -1, the flow 
curves show a high strain hardening behavior. 
iii) At strain rates lower than 0.1 s -1 and at temperatures in the range 1000 to 1700 °C, the flow 
curves exhibit an initial rapid work-hardening at lower strains followed by a tendency to reach 
steady-state stress levels at large strains. 
From the σ vs. ε plots, the variation of σ with T at constant strain rate ε& and the variation 
of σ with ε& at constant T were plotted as shown in Fig. 2. It is seen that the flow stress is 
independent of temperature and strain rate up to 900 °C. At higher strain rates, the flow stress is 
independent of temperature up to 1000 °C. At te mperatures higher than 1000 °C and at all strain 
rates, the flow stress (at any strain or at steady state) is found to change significantly with strain 
rate and temperature, indicating that the flow beha vior is thermally activated. Further, it is seen

3.2 Processing maps 
In recent years, processing maps have been used to model the hot deformation behavior and 
to understand the microstructural e volution [26, 29-32]. The principle and basis of this approach 
have been described elsewhere [33]. In this study the processing maps were constructed from the 
σ, T, ε& data which are converted to a cont our map of strain rate sensitivity m in a frame of T and 
ε& as shown in Fig. 3 for a strain of 0.5. The contour numbers in Fig. 3 represent m and the 
shaded portion corresponds to negative m. The processing map exhibits a single domain starting 
at about 1300 °C and peaking at 1700 ºC and 10 -3 s-1. In this domain m increases from slightly 
positive to about 0.24 with increasing temper ature and decreasing strain rate. Since m is 
significantly greater than zero in this domain, th e hot worked product is not expected to exhibit 
flow instability. In addition, at temperatures lower than 1000 °C and at all strain rates, m is found 
to be negative (the shaded portion) indicating the possible occurrence of flow instability or strain 
localization. These will be confirmed by detaile d metallographic investigations dealt in next 
section.

3.5 Grain size – Z relation 
A characteristic feature of DRX is the inverse relation of the steady state grain size with Z as has 
been reported in several alloy systems undergoing DRX [26, 30, 34, 40]. A similar plot for the 
present data is shown in Fig. 10. It is seen that grain size is inversely related to Z and appears 
linear on a log-log scale. This confirms the occurrence of DRX in this domain. It has also been 
shown that steady state grain size is independent of the initial grain size and depends only on Z 
[34]. Thus grain refinement can be carried out at high Z values and grain coarsening will take 
place at low Z values. 
4 Summary and Conclusions 
The processing map obtained on Nb-1Zr alloy de formed in the temperature range of 700-
1700 °C and the strain rate range 10 -3 – 10 s-1 showed two distinctly different domains: (1) a zero 
to negative strain rate sensitivity domain from 700 to 900 ºC at all the strain rates and (2) a high 
strain rate sensitivity domain from 1300 to 1700 ºC and centered around 10 -3 s-1. The first regime 
of negative strain rate sensitivity showed strain localization both at the macro and microscopic 
levels as seen at 800 ºC and 10 -2 s-1. The strain-temperature conditions corresponding to the first 
regime should be avoided during hot working. At conditions outside the domain such as 1400 ºC 
and 0.01 s-1 wavy boundaries were observed indicating precursors to DRX. At 1500 ºC – 0.01 s -1, 
1600 ºC – 0.01 s -1 and 1700 ºC – 0.1 s -1 wavy boundaries and small grains within large grains 
were seen indicating incomplete DRX. At cond itions within the domain equiaxed grains were 
observed whose grain size varied inversely with temperature compensated strain rate Z, thereby 
confirming the occurrence of DRX. Thus is it s uggested that any hot working carried out on Nb-
1Zr alloy should be carried out at 1600 ºC or be yond and preferably at lower strain rates. 
However higher strain rates too may result in co mplete DRX if hot working is done to larger 
strains. In industrial practice Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy has been hot worked successfully at 
temperatures in the range 1500 to 1650 ºC [9, 10, 14]. 
On the basis of the above observations the following conclusions can be made. 
1. Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy exhibits dynamic recrystalli zation over a wide range of strain rates (10 -3 
to 10-1 s-1) and temperatures greater than 1400 ºC. The dynamically recrystallized grain size 
can be varied by deforming the material at the appropriate temperature and strain rate 
within the domain.

2. Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy exhibits a domain of stra in localization at temperatures below 1000 ºC 
and at all strain rates employed in this study. These deformation conditions should be 
avoided during industrial hot working.
