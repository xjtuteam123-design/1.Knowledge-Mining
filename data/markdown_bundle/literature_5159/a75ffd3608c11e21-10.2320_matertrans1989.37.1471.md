It was found that the crystallization of the three alloys proceeded through two stages. The AI particles smaller than 15 nm and compounds larger than 50 nm in diameter were formed during uniaxial tension tests and found to be uni- formly distributed in the amorphous matrix. In contrast, a significant decrease in strength was observed at a temperature above 580 K. This is presumably because the concentration of the solute atoms in the amorphous matrix was greatly decreased below the nominal concentration by the formation of the compounds. The temperature at which a largest elongation in the three alloys was ob- tained corresponded to the exothermic peak temperature in the DSC curve. This fact indicated that such a large elonga- tion originated from the precipitation of the crystalline phase. On the other hand, in a temperature range in which the AI phase is formed, deformation occurs through a shear deformation mode, whereas in a higher temperature range in which the compounds are formed, it occurs viscoelastically. Introduction The needs for light alloys having higher specific strength than conventional high strength alloys such as Al, Mg, Ti alloys are extremely high in order to meet new requirements in various industry fields. i.e, transporta- tion vehicles and energy consumption. Strengthening methods such as solid solution strengthening, precipita- tion hardening and grain refinement, etc. have been gener- ally used to improve the mechanical properties of the alloys. Recently it has been reported(l) that by using new strengthening processes for Al alloys such as rapid solidification and mechanical alloying, heat resistant properties as well as mechanical strength and wear resistance were greatly improved. It is noticeable that the form?tion of the amorphous Al-TM(transition metal)- RE(rare earth metal) alloy system was possible by the rapid solidification process, resulting in an excellent cor- rosion resistance, high strength over 1000 MPa and im- proved ductility(2) compared with the crystalline state. In addition, it is very attractive that dispersion of fine Al particles of about IO nm in diameter in the amorphous matrix further significantly increased the mechanical strength by more than 500/0(3) at room temperature and also exhibited a large elongation and high tensile strength at elevated temperatures. There have been many reports about the mechanical properties of amorphous Al alloys at the ambient but few at high tempera- tures(4). In this study, the microstructure, thermal stabil- ity and tensile properties of the amorphous Al-Ni-Fe�� Nd alloys were investigated in the temperature range from ambient temperature to 690 K in order to elucidate the high temperature mechanical characteristics. Change in tensile strength The amorphous A188Ni10-xFexNd2 alloys were found to have excellent mechanical properties. The very high tensile strength over 1200 MPa at room temperature could also be maintained at an 800 MPa level even at 580 K. These properties are now discussed in terms of the microstructure and the bonding nature among the con- stituent atoms in the amorphous matrix. As shown in Fig. 4, of the three alloys greatly in- creased in a temperature range from (TXI +20) K to TPI. In order to investigate such an increase in detail, the frac- ture area of the amorphous A188Ni10Nd2 alloy tensile-test- ed at 441 K was examined by high resolution electron microscopy (HREM) and its micrograph is shown in Fig. 10. Several Al particles are seen and a regular fringe con- trast within an Al particle is noticeable. These regular fringe contrast gives a piece of evidence that each Al parti- cle is defect-free and could be regarded as a perfect crys- 5nm Fig. IO High resolution electron micrograph of the amorphous A188Ni10Nd2 alloy tensile-tested at 441 K. tal. Moreover the fact that the regular fringe contrast remains unchanged even under a severe stress condition indicates that the Al particles are thermally very stable even at high temperatures. Hono et al.(5) have found that the primary Al crystals formed during aging of an amorphous A187Ni10Ce3 alloy have a much lower concentration of solute atoms than that for solute concentration of the master alloy. Similar- ly, when the formation of the Al phase in the present amorphous alloys starts, the solute atoms (Ni, Fe, Nd) are released from the Al particles and accumulated in the amorphous matrix. Therefore, the concentration of the solute atoms in the amorphous matrix becomes higher than the nominal concentration of the alloy. Such a con- centration gradient becomes larger as the precipitation continues. In this way, the amorphous matrix becomes thermally stablized so that the alloy can possess a high temperature resistance. In contrast, an abrupt decrease in near TX2 is due to the decrease in the solute concentra- tion in the amorphous matrix by the formation of inter- metallic compounds, which results in a weak bonding among the constituent atoms. It can be concluded that the strength of the alloy is greatly enhanced firstly by the dispersion strengthening effect of the defect-free Al par- ticles, and secondly by the thermal stablization of the amorphous phase due to the higher solute concentration. When the intermetallic compounds begin to form, the strength of the alloy decreases significantly because of an enhanced instability by the depletion of the solute atoms in the amorphous matrix. This demonstrates the absence of work harden- ing observed in the crystalline alloys. The high tempera- ture deformation mechanism of the present amorphous alloys becomes more complex in this case because of the formation of the crystalline phases. At TXI (413 K), the specimen exihibits a slight elongation. When the tempera- ture reaches TPI (433 K), the flow stress remains constant after the yield and the elongation increased significantly. An increase in the flow stress after the yield was also ob- served at 523 K where the formation of the Al phase was completed. A conclusion that the Al particles contribute to a dispersion strengthening effect can be drawn from this. It is seen that the flow stress keeps decreasing rap- idly from 588 to 633 K but the elongation increases significantly. A significant increase in the elongation at TPI can be ex- plained by the formation process of the Al phase. Defor- mation of an amorphous alloy is generally inititiated on the shear plane which is declined 450 to the tensile direc- tion having the maximum shear stress. As mentioned above, the adiabatic effect resulting from the inhomo- geneous deformation on the maximum shear plane will raise the temperature of certain shear slip planes locally. Then some of the Al particles will be formed from the amorphous matrix, because they reached instantaneously the precipitation reaction temperature. Once this hap- pens, the deformation is suppressed by the local harden- ing effect of the Al particles and subsequently further deformation occurs at the weaker regions where Al par- ticles are not formed. In this way, the deformed zone propagates continuously throughout the specimen. The deformation mechanism was further investigated using an optical microscope as shown in Fig. 12. Many shear deformation bands oriented at 450 to the tensile axis can be seen on the surface of the specimen. They show the evidence of the deformed zone propagated through the weaker region. It is concluded that an abrupt increase of the elonga- tion near TPI in the Al��Ni-Fe��Nd system can be ex- plained by the crystallization-induced plasticity(8). In order to have a more ductile nature, fracture has to be dalayed. In the present alloys, this could be achieved by the presence of the stable Al particles at elevated tempera- tures. Now let us discuss the second rapid increase of the elon- gation which was observed at the precipitation tempera- ture of the intermetallic compounds. At the temperatures above the glass transition temperature (TO, the deforma- tion of the amorphous alloy occurs through the viscous flow which is a well-known deformation mode of hot glassy materials(9). Although the present alloys did not exhibit Tg, the stress-strain curves at temperatures above 588 K in Fig. 11 show the same viscoelastic deformation as in other amorphous alloys exhibiting Tg(10). Further- more, at this temperature range, the amorphous matrix becomes more Al-rich by the depletion of the solute atoms and then softened. Consequently, this segregation seems to cause an ease of the viscous flow resulting in a significantly large elongation. The reason for the shift of the elongation peak to the higher temperatures with increaing Fe content can be ex- plained in the same manner as that of 0B in terms of the interatomic forces between the constitutional atoms. A larger number of Al��Fe atom pairs than the Al��Ni pairs are formed by adding Fe atoms. Because of the stronger bonding nature of the Al-Fe pairs than the Al-Ni pairs, the amorphous phase would obtain an enhanced thermal stability, increasing its formation temperature. Accord- ingly, the elongation peaks induced from the crystallized phase shift to the higher temperatures. If the elongation value at any temperature depended on the amount of the crystalline phase formed during the deformation, then the compositional dependence on ef could be explained by the change of the specific heat on the formation of the Al phase and the intermetallic com- pounds. Figure 13 shows ef vs changes of the specific heat at the precipitation peak temperatures of the Al and com- pound phases (Cplmax and CP2max) in the amorphous Al- Ni��Fe-Nd alloy system. It is seen that Ef decreases nearly linearly with decreasing CPImax and-cp-zmax. Therefore, it can be concluded that generally the elongation at a cer- tain temperature is propotional to the amount of the crys- talline phase precipitated at that temperature, i.e., crystal- lization-induced. Ductile properties (Cf=22�� 42%) of the Al-Ni��Fe��Nd alloy system obtained at a relatively low temperature range in which Al precipitation reaction is very active imply their useful applications in the production of the bulk form of the high specific strength amorphous alloy containing nanocrystalline Al particles.
