Abstract: The service temperature of high temperature titaniu m alloy has been raised 
from 400 
 to 650  in the last years. In this study, a new near α high temperature 
titanium alloy with high Si content (Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5 Mo-0.6Si, wt. %) which can serve 
at 700  was developed by powder metallurgy (P/M) method. The microstructure and 
mechanical properties were investigated. The result s show that the P/M titanium alloy 
exhibits an α + β two-phase microstructure with dispersed fine S 2 type silicide 
((TiZr) 6Si 3). The yield strength and ultimate tensile strength are 1023 MPa and 1190 
MPa at room temperature, with an elongation of 10 % . At 700 , the alloy still 
maintains a yield strength, an ultimate strength, a nd an elongation of 510 MPa, 651 
MPa and 13%, respectively. The high strength is mai nly attributed to the precipitation 
strengthening of the silicide and the formation of fine-grained α′ phase. 
Keywords: Titanium alloy; High temperature; Powder metallurgy; Tensile properties; 
Silicide

1 Introduction 
Titanium alloys have been widely applied in aerospa ce, and marine fields because 
of their high specific strength, good high temperature performance and good corrosion 
resistance [1-6]. With the development of supersoni c aircraft, the requirements of key 
components materials with high temperature resistan ce are increasing [7, 8]. High 
temperature titanium alloy become one of the vital development directions of titanium 
alloy in last decade. 
Typical high temperature titanium alloys, such as T i1100 [9], IMI834 [10], BT36 
[11] and Ti600 [12], have been successfully applied. In these alloys, Si is an important 
element since it can apparently increase tensile st rength and creep resistance at 
ambient/elevated temperatures. The existence of Si in titanium alloy can be solid 
solution atom or silicide. When the content of Si is higher than 0.4%, accompanied by 
coarse dendritic structures, coarse silicide can pr ecipitate during casting and 
subsequent annealing, which are of great harm to th e ductility and the industrial 
application. So most of commercial high temperature titanium alloys contain a small 
amount of Si (lower than 0.35 wt. %) [13-15]. 
Powder metallurgy (P/M) technology, i.e. mixing + p ressing + sintering, is a 
well-known method to prepare metallic materials with uniform microstructure without 
alloying elements segregation, which may be a poten tial method to prepare high Si 
content titanium alloy without coarse silicide prec ipitation. However, P/M titanium 
alloys usually have residual pores, which may worse n the mechanical properties. Hot 
working is considered a reliable and efficient way to improve the mechanical 
properties of titanium alloys by removing residual pores [16, 17]. At the same time, it 
is also possible to tailor microstructure and obtai n fine dispersed silicide on the 
titanium matrix [18-21]. Therefore, it is essential to study the high Si content titanium 
alloys through P/M + thermomechanical processing process. 
In the present study, a new near- α titanium alloy with high Si content 
(Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si) was prepared through eleme ntal P/M process. Hot rolling 
in the α + β phase field and subsequent annealing were used to further optimize the 
microstructure and improve the mechanical propertie s. The microstructure and 
ambient/elevated mechanical properties were investi gated, and the strengthening 
mechanisms were also discussed.

2 Experimental 
2.1 Preparation process 
Ti, Al, Mo, Zr and Si elemental powders were used a s raw materials in the present 
study. The characteristics of the raw powders are s hown in Fig. 1 and Table 1. The 
oxygen content of the Ti powder was less than 0.3 w t. %. The elemental powders 
were mixed in a V-type blender for 5h in argon atmo sphere at room temperature. The 
fully blended powders were compacted by cold isosta tic pressing (CIP) method with 
pressure of 200 MPa for 5 min. The size of the cyli ndrical compacts are d300×30 
mm 
3. The green compacts were then sintered at 1300  for 4h in a high vacuum 
furnace, and followed by furnace-cooling. After tha t, the as-sintered billets were hot 
rolled (HR) on a hot-rolling mill. The rolling temp erature was 950  and the total 
deformation was 80 %. Finally, the hot-rolled alloy s were subjected to annealing at 
500  for 2 h in a muffle furnace. The β phase transformation temperature was 
determined to be around 970  with the help of metallographic observation 
technique. 
2.2 Characterization and test methods 
The chemical compositions of the as-sintered sample s were characterized by 
chemical methods and the interstitial elements (C, O and N) were characterized by the 
fusion method on a Leco O/N analyzer (LECO TCH 600) . Microstructures were 
analyzed using a scanning electron microscope (SEM, Helios Nanolab 600i, FEI, 
USA) and a transmission electron microscope (TEM, J EM-2100F, JEOL, Japan). The 
samples for SEM observation were grounded using 500 -2000 grid SiC papers and 
polished using an OPS polishing solution. Thin foil s for TEM were firstly ground to 
60 
µm, then prepared by a twin-jet electropolishing tec hnique in a electrolyte of 
perchloric acid (5 %) + butanol (35 %) + methanol ( 60 %) at -35  and 30 V . Phase 
analysis was conducted in a X-ray diffractometer (X RD, Dmax-2550, Rigaku, Japan) 
with a Cu/K α resource at room temperature. The elemental distri butions of hot-rolled 
samples were analyzed by electron probe microanalys is (EPMA, JXA−8530F). 
Tensile tests were conducted on a MTS testing machi ne with an applied strain rate of 
1×10 -3 s -1. The gauge size of the samples for room temperatur e and elevated 
temperature tensile tests were 2×3×20 mm 3 and 2×3×10 mm 3, respectively.

3 Results 
3.1 Microstructures of as-sintered Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy 
The chemical composition (in wt. %) of the as-sinte red Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si 
alloy is shown in Table 2. The composition is consi stent with the nominal 
composition within the tolerance of the error. The aluminum equivalent ([Al]eq) and 
molybdenum equivalent ([Mo]eq) are calculated to be 10.1 and 0.5, respectively, 
which indicates that the alloy is a near 
α titanium alloy. Fig. 2 shows the XRD 
patterns and SEM image of the as-sintered Ti-6Al-4Z r-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy. The XRD 
pattern only shows diffraction peaks of α phase, which confirms that the present alloy 
is a near α titanium alloy. In Fig. 2b, the as-sintered alloy consists of a grey contrast α 
phase, a light contrast β phase and some residual pores with dark contrast. The 
rod-like primary α phases with a length of 20-50 µm and a width of 5-10 µm are 
surrounded by a small amount of β phases. According to the statistics of software 
image-pro-plus-6, the volume fractions of α phase and β phase are 97 % and 3 %, 
respectively. The relative density of as-sintered alloy is about 94 %. 
3.2 Microstructures of hot-rolled Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy 
Figure 3 shows the XRD patterns and SEM image of th e hot-rolled 
Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy. Diffraction peaks of α phase and β phase can be 
observed from Fig. 3a. In Fig. 3b, the residual por es are eliminated and a duplex 
microstructure was observed, which consists of the α phase (dark area) and the 
transformed-β phase (light grey area). Each of the transformed β grains is composed 
of several colonies and each colony consists of spa ced α/β lamellas. In addition, some 
fine precipitates (light dots) distribute homogeneo usly on the titanium matrix. Fig. 4 
shows the electron probing micro-analysis (EPMA) re sults of the hot-rolled 
Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy. It can be found that the Zr and Si elements concentrate 
in the bright precipitates (Fig. 4c and d). The Al element is enriched in the α phase 
and the Mo and Si elements mostly concentrate in th e β phase (Fig. 4d, e and f). 
Therefore, it can be inferred that the precipitate is a silicide rather than an oxide. 
Figure 5 shows the TEM images of the hot-rolled Ti- 6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy. 
Some plate-like α phases and precipitates can be found in the Fig. 5a . The particle size 
of the precipitate is approximately 1.5 µm. The composition of the precipitate is 
estimated by EDS method which is exhibited in Fig. 5b and the result is consistent 
with the EPMA result (Fig. 4). The corresponding se lected area electron diffraction

3.3 Mechanical properties of the Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy 
Tensile engineering stress-strain curve of the hot- rolled Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si 
alloy at room temperature is shown in Fig. 6a. The yield and ultimate tensile strength 
of hot-rolled Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy are 1023 MPa and 1190 MPa, respectively, 
with an elongation of about 10%. Fig. 6b exhibits a comparison of the ultimate tensile 
strength and elongation of the hot-rolled Ti-6Al-4Z r-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy with various 
near-
α titanium alloys (Ti1100 [9, 15, 22-24], IMI834 [15 , 25], Ti600 [26]) and TiAl 
alloys [27, 28] at room temperature. Obviously, the hot-rolled Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo 
-0.6Si alloy achieves superior comprehensive mechan ical properties at room 
temperature. Fig. 7 presents the tensile engineerin g stress-strain curves of the 
hot-rolled Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy at 500  and 700 . It demonstrates that the 
alloy has a yield strength, an ultimate strength an d an elongation of 778 MPa, 968 
MPa, and 12% at 500 . When the tensile temperature rises to 700 , the yield 
strength and the ultimate tensile strength maintain at 510 MPa and 651 MPa, with the 
elongation of 13 %. Table 3 compares the high tempe rature mechanical properties of 
the Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy with some typical high temperature titanium alloys. 
It is worth noting that the P/M Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0. 6Si alloy shows good mechanical 
properties at elevated temperature. At 700 , the present alloy even exhibits better 
comprehensive mechanical properties than gamma-TiAl alloys. Considering the 
excellent tensile properties of the present Ti-6Al- 4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy at ambient 
and elevated temperatures, it is promising for the industrial applications where high 
temperature mechanical properties are strongly demanded. 
4 Discussions 
 As shown in Fig. 2b, there is no silicide precipit ated in the as-sintered alloy. 
During thermomechanical deformation, a number of di spersed S 2 type silicide were 
precipitated from the titanium matrix (Fig. 3b). Si is a typical β phase stabilized 
element which has larger solubility in β phase than that in α phase. In the as-sintered 
condition, the Si element mainly dissolved in the β phase. During subsequent heating, 
silicide can precipitate at the α/β boundary due to the difference in solid solubility of

6 
 
Si in β phase and α phase [29-31]. When subjected to deformation, many defects, such 
as dislocations, were introduced into the microstru cture, which could provide 
nucleation sites, and the high interface energy aro und the defects would help the 
silicide to precipitate from the titanium matrix. T herefore, the method of powder 
metallurgy and hot rolling can promote the precipitation of fine and dispersed silicide, 
as shown in Fig. 4 and 5. It may improve the mechan ical properties of the titanium 
alloy effectively. 
Based on the results of Fig. 6 and 7, the present h ot-rolled Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si 
alloys exhibit excellent tensile properties at room and elevated temperatures. Fig. 8 
shows the bright field TEM images of Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5 Mo-0.6Si alloy after tensile test 
at 700 . A number of dislocations accumulate around the si licide (Fig. 8a). It 
indicates that the fine and dispersed silicides may hinder the movement of 
dislocations in the matrix and play an important ro le to resist plastic deformation and 
improve the strength. Similar results were also rep orted in titanium alloys and TiAl 
alloys [32, 33]. On the other hand, as shown in Fig . 8b and c, the average width and 
length of acicular α′ grain are about 100 nm and 800 nm, respectively, i ndicating that 
ultra-fine α′ phase was obtained after hot rolling of P/M Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy, 
which would be another positive effect on the mechanical properties. 
Silicide in Si-containing near- α type titanium alloys are generally presented in th e 
following forms: binary Ti 5Si3, ternary (TiZr) 5Si 3 (S 1 type) and (TiZr) 6Si 3 (S 2 type) 
[15, 34]. Flower et al. [35] pointed out that there were nume rous mismatched planes 
between Ti 5Si 3 and the matrix, which made the strain energy of Ti 5Si 3 precipitate 
nucleation in the matrix to be very high and the nu cleation of Ti 5Si 3 precipitates to be 
difficult even with high solute supersaturation. Ma dsen et al. [36] indicated that the 
addition of Zr would result in the formation of ter nary (TiZr) 5Si 3 and (TiZr) 6Si 3, 
where Ti atoms can be replaced by Zr atoms. The main reason is that these two atomic 
species have the similar size and valence state. Th e crystallographic matching 
between the matrix and precipitate is improved in t he Zr-containing alloy and it 
causes lower strain energy of nucleation of S 1 type and S 2 type precipitate [36]. The 
EDS and corresponding quantitative analysis results of the precipitates in Fig. 5a and 
5b indicate that the precipitates are the silicide with the stoichiometry of (TiZr) xSi (x 
is between 2 and 3). The corresponding selected are a electron diffraction (SAED) 
pattern of the silicide in Fig. 5d indicates that t he silicide have hexagonal closed 
packed (HCP) structure and the lattice parameters a re: a=0.724 nm, c=0.373 nm,

which is similar with the results of S 2 type structure features in the literature [37]. 
Therefore, based on the EDS and SAED results, the p resent silicide in 
Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy can be considered as the S 2 type silicide (TiZr) 6Si 3. 
5 Conclusions 
In summary, a new titanium alloy with composition o f Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si for 
high temperature application was prepared. The micr ostructure and mechanical 
properties were studied in detail. The following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) The as-sintered alloy exhibits a rod-like prima ry α phase with a length of 20-50 
µm and a width of 5-10 µm, surrounded by a small amount of β phases. After hot 
rolling, fine S 2 type silicide (TiZr) 6Si 3 with a length of about 1.5 µm are precipitated 
on the matrix. 
(2) The yield strength and ultimate tensile strengt h of the hot-rolled 
Ti-6Al-4Zr-0.5Mo-0.6Si alloy are 1023 MPa and 1190 MPa at room temperature, 
with an elongation of about 10 %. At 700 , the alloy still maintains an yield strength, 
an ultimate strength, and an elongation of 510 MPa, 651 MPa and 13 %, respectively. 
(3) The high strength is mainly attributed to the p recipitation strengthening of the 
silicide and the formation of needle-like fine-grained α′ phase.
