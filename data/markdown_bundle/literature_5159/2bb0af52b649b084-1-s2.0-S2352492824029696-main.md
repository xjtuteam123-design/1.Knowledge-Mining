ABSTRACT
Ti alloys are usually hot worked in the two-phase zone to avoid microstructure coarsening that reduces the 
ductility. In this work, the powder-sintered Ti64 billets were directly rolled at a temperature of 100 ℃ above the 
β transus after 1 – 2 passes with 60~90 % reduction. In the as-rolled microstructure, the original β grain is 
relatively small, and there is no complete grain boundary α phase when the deformation exceeds 80 %. After 
annealing, the material reaches the optimum ductility and the elongation reaches 17.8 %. After solution and 
aging, the alloy exhibits the optimal combination of strength and ductility (the ultimate tensile strength of 
~1165 MPa, the yield strength of ~1129 MPa, and the elongation of ~16.5 %). This study may offer a novel 
method for the high-temperature thermomechanical processing of Ti alloys.
1. Introduction
Plate and sheet are the primary finished and semi-finished titanium 
alloy products, and they have extensive use in aerospace, petrochemical, 
and military industries [1,2] . Hot rolling is the most viable option for 
producing titanium alloy plates of significant dimensions. Furthermore, 
the microstructure may be precisely adjusted by changing the rolling 
parameters such as temperature, strain rate [3] , and thickness reduction 
[4,5] . This allows for the optimization of the mechanical properties of 
titanium alloys.
Typically, temperature is the primary determinant in the rolling 
process. Due to the β -phase transition in titanium alloys, the conven -
tional way for hot rolling or other thermal mechanical processing is to 
maintain a temperature range that is 40 – 85 
◦
C lower than the beta 
transus [6] . Within the β phase region, the titanium atom exhibits a 
greater diffusion coefficient, resulting in the accelerated expansion of 
the initial β grain. This, in turn, leads to the formation of a large grain 
size that exceeds several millimeters, exacerbating the room tempera -
ture ductility seriously, calling “β brittleness ” phenomena. Conversely, 
increasing the thermomechanical processing (TMP) temperature can 
significantly reduce deformation resistance and improve the alloy ’ s 
formability. Therefore, given the superior deformability, rolling within 
the high-temperature β single-phase region seems to be a preferable 
choice, provided that a finer microstructure and enhanced ductility can 
be attained.
Some works had reported a comparative study of hot rolling in the 
α + β two-phase zone and the β single-phase zone. A near- α Ti60 alloy ( β 
transus: 1045 ℃ ) plate were β -rolled at 1070 ℃ and ( α + β )-rolled at 980 
℃ , leading to a lamellar Widmannstatten structure with original β grain 
boundary and fine basket-weave microstructure [7] . Another near- α 
DsTi700 alloy ( β transus: 992 ℃ ) were rolled at 1000~ 1100 ℃ with 
total reduction about 80 %. Aside from the clearly visible β grain 
boundary and grain boundary α phase in the matrix, the tensile strength 
at room temperature increases from 1316 MPa to 1390 MPa after hot 
rolling at 1100 ℃ compared to 1025 ℃ . However, the elongation re -
duces from 13.8 % to 2.3 %. However, plates that were rolled in the β 
phase showed weak textures, which were caused by the scattered ori -
entations of secondary α during the α to β phase transformation [8] . 
Grain growth and ductility reduction were also noted in the same way 
when the β -titanium alloy (Ti-3.5AL-5Mo-6V-3Cr-2Sn-0.5Fe) was rolled 
in the β field [9] .
The above illustrates that the “β brittleness ” phenomena, resulting 
from the rapid growth of β grains, is the primary issue limiting the 
application implementation of β phase hot working. Incorporating a 
small amount of grain growth inhibitors appears to be a feasible way.

This study utilized powder sintered billet for hot rolling within 
β -phase zone without cogging process. It was observed that the powder 
as-sintered billet exhibited superior microstructure stability at high 
temperatures compared to the as-forged billet. No large β grains were 
observed after rolling, resulting in a better ductility at room tempera -
ture. This study may offer a novel approach for the high-temperature 
deformation of titanium alloys.

3. Results and discussion
After sintering the HDH Ti64 powder at 1200 ℃ for 2 h, the density is 
about 4.32 ± 0.03 %. The calculated relative density is 96.8~98.2 %, and 
the residual pores is about 2 – 3 vol%. The O/N/H content of as-sintered 
sample is 0.22, 0.035 and 0.009 wt%. In addition to having a slightly 
greater oxygen content than the forged Ti-64 standard (ASTM B381 – 21), 
the nitrogen and hydrogen concentration are within acceptable limits. 
Considering that oxygen, as an α -type stable element, the oxygen will 
increase the β transus temperature. Take this into account, the β 
transition point temperature of the PM billet determined by differential 
scanning calorimetry (DSC) method was 1020 ℃ , which was slightly 
higher than that of the traditional forged Ti64 (998 ℃ ). The micro -
structure of as-sintered Ti64 alloy consists of large colonies of lamellar α 
plates and massive grain boundary α (GB- α ) network, shown in Fig. 1 (a). 
The dimensions of the α plates are around 55 μ m in length and 11 μ m in 
width, while the size of the α colonies is approximately 48 μ m, as 
determined by Image-Pro. At the same time, the residual pores in the 
matrix can be seen. The grain boundary diagram reveals that the large 
angle grain boundary constitutes the majority of the sintered sample, 
whereas the small angle grain boundary accounts for just 16.2 %. At the 
same time, the sintered samples ’ grain boundary angles are grouped 
between 60
◦

◦

that almost β phases reside between the elongated α plates, with 96.4 % 
α phase and 3.5 % β phase.
The hot rolling temperature is 1150 ℃ , higher than β transition 
temperature 130 ℃ , the material is located in the β single-phase region 
at this temperature. By different deformation, the as-rolled microstruc -
tures are shown in Fig. 2 (a-d). From RD-TD plane in Fig. 2 (a1-d1), the 
intact original β grain can be observed in the sample of 60 % deforma -
tion. With increase of deformation, the original β grain virtually van -
ished, leaving only a little quantity of broken discontinuous grain 
boundary α . The average size of the original β grain after hot rolling is 
200 – 400 μ m. Although the growth of β grain also occurs, the β grain size 
is significantly smaller than that of wrought Ti64 at this temperature. 
Ref [17] shows the grain size of as-wrought Ti64 has increased to around 
1.8 mm after being subjected to a heat treatment duration of 30 min at 
1150 ℃ . This relatively weak grain growth is due to the fact that the 
sintered lamellar structure, which has already been coarsened and 
equilibrated during the vacuum sintering process, lacks sufficient 
driving force to coarsen the microstructure or recrystallize. The same 
phenomenon is also reported in the heat treatment of powder metallurgy 
sintered samples [18] . In Fig. 2 (a3, c3), it can be clearly seen that re -
sidual pores still exist in the 60 % deformation sample, and almost 
completely disappear in 80 %. So, the residual pores may also have the 
pinning effect to inhibit grain growth in β phase area. After 60 % 
deformation, a Widmanst ¨adter structure with long and straight α lath 
appears in the matrix. After 80 % deformation, the length-width ratio of 
α lath in the lamellar structure decreases significantly, showing a typical 
basket-weave microstructure.
The inverted pole figures (IPFs) in Fig. 2 (a4, c4) exhibit distinct 
colors representing different grains orientations, in which different 
colors represented different grain misorientations including the red for 
{0001}, the green for {01 10} and the blue for { 12 10}. With the increase 
of rolling deformation, the grains of { 12 10} (blue area) and {0001} (red 
area) orientation increased, and the grains of {01 10} orientation (green 
area) decreased. The hot rolling process involves compressive tension in 
the ND direction and shear stress in the RD direction. The slip tilts to -
wards the RD-TD plane and is parallel to the RD direction, activating 
above-mentioned slip systems and causing slide during deformation 
[19] . As shown in the IPFs in Fig. 2 (a4) and (c4), the maximum value of 
texture strength increases from 2.44 to 5.20 with the increasing rolling 
reduction. At the same time, the texture type changes from { 12 10} 
non-basal texture to {0001} basal texture, in which revealed that larger 
anisotropy existed in the Ti64 sheets under higher strain rates and 
deformation and the dislocation slip behavior was changed by the strain 
state of the sheets, especially the type of the activated slip system. After 
hot rolling, the average Kernel average misorientation (KAM) of the 
Ti64 sheets with 60 % and 80 % rolling reduction was about 0.15 and 
0.13, indicating that the dislocation density and residual stress decrease 
with the increase of rolling deformation. Fig. 2 (a6, c6) show the grains 
type distribution with two different rolling reduction. The color blue 
denotes recrystallization grains, yellow shows recovery grains, and red 
indicates deformation grains. With the increase of deformation from

4. Conclusions
This work presents a high efficiency method for preparing Ti64 alloy 
plates by directly high temperature β single phase rolling using powder 
sintering billets. Due to the relatively good microstructure stability and 
the pinning effect of little residual pores, the β grain growth is inhibited 
during hot rolling. The as-rolled microstructure exhibits no complete β 
grain boundary α phase. As-annealed sample shows an optimum 
ductility, with the elongation of 17.8 %. The microstructure is composed 
with platelike α and a little intergranular β strips. After solution and 
aging, the sample exhibits a typical bimodal structure with fine primary 
α and transformed β , ultimately affording an excellent combination of 
strength and ductility (the ultimate tensile strength of ~1165 MPa, the 
yield strength of ~1129 MPa, and the elongation of ~16.5 %).
