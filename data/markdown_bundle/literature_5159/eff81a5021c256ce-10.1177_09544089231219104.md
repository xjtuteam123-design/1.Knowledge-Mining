Application of Ni-based superalloy
in aero turbine blade: A review
Asit Behera 1 , Ashok K Sahoo 1 and S S Mahapatra 2
Abstract
A superalloy is a high-performance alloy used for achieving stability and strength at elevated temperatures. Generally, a
temperature of about 800°C is developed at the ﬁrst stage of the aero turbine blade. So it is a challenge to choose a
material that can withstand such high temperatures. Nowadays advanced generation single crystal Ni-based superalloys
are being used for the ful ﬁllment of high-temperature requirements in aero turbine components. But the continuous
effort is being carried out to manufacture new materials that can withstand more and more temperatures generated
at aero turbine because the high-temperature operation of the turbine is directly proportional to the ef ﬁciency of a tur-
bine engine. The requirement of high-temperature application upgraded the Ni-based superalloy and different generations
of alloys were prepared from time to time those were discussed in the paper. Recent developments of superalloys in the
paper mainly include ﬁfth and sixth generations of superalloys those incorporate Hf, Re, and Ru-like elements in signi ﬁcant
amounts. A brief extension discussion has been placed on the types, processing, and hot deformation characteristics of
Ni-based superalloys. VIM melting, single crystal growth, directional solidi ﬁcation, and powder metallurgy techniques for
manufacturing Ni-based superalloys are discussed in detail. The strengthening mechanism and machinability behavior
depict the characteristics of superalloys in a mechanical direction. Heat treatment and hot deformation behavior of
Ni-based superalloy was precisely discussed in the context so as to correlate the strength of the material with the tem-
perature change. Overall, the paper describes the origin, processing and characteristics of Nickel based superalloy in
detail.
Keywords
Ni-based superalloy, γ phase, γ’precipitate, solid solution, turbine blade, hot deformation

Introduction
A superalloy indicates an alloy that is superior in property
to other alloys. When the word “superalloy” is added to
the word “alloy,” it shows the picture of additional
surface stability and strength at elevated temperatures. It
is based on group VIII elements that are added in a
certain proportion to enhance the property of the end
product. Superalloy is considered to be the most
complex metallic alloy designed and developed for com-
mercial purposes. This complexity allows the superalloy
to be produced with a wide range of different grades of
alloys. A variety of superalloys are meant to be developed
for high-temperature applications and lead to become very
sophisticated with the passage of time. But as it was
evident that sophistication demands money. Hence super-
alloy is generally very expensive compared to general fre-
quently used alloys. The sophistication and complexity of
superalloy lead the price to be in the range of 30 to 200
times that of plain stainless steel. However, these costlier
materials are able to decrease the maintenance cost and
increase the ef ﬁciency of the related system. Primarily
superalloy was developed to be used in aero blades of
gas turbine engines but later it was applied in various
parts of industry like heat exchangers, automotive turbo-
chargers, rocket components, petroleum production
equipment, nuclear reactors and biomedical devices.

Historical development and recent
trends of superalloy in turbine blade
The term superalloy has not come into the picture until
1940 until the ﬁctional character superman has been sur-
faced on television. From that time the word “super”
gained popularity in describing a dominating character.
Previously chrome cast irons were being used as
furnace grate and hearth materials. Hence those were con-
sidered to be superalloys because these are high-
temperature applications in a strenuous corrosive environ-
ment. The term “superalloy” was ﬁrst used shortly after
World War II to describe a group of alloys developed
for use in turbo superchargers and aircraft turbine
engines that required high performance at elevated tem-
peratures.
9 Various cast irons were also used in diesel
turbocharger casings and exhaust manifolds. The tem-
perature in these parts exceeded 760°C. So cast iron
was also considered to be superalloy. But now the de ﬁn-
ition of superalloy has been changed. These are high-
temperature operating materials made of Group VIII B
elements of the periodic table and usually consist of
various combinations of Fe, Ni, Co, and Cr, as well as
minor amounts of W, Mo, Ta, Nb, Ti, and Al that
exhibit enhanced properties like high mechanical property
at elevated temperature.
10 These superalloys are used
in high-temperature chemical process industries, gas
turbines, coal plants, etc. Among these, nickel-based
superalloy development has witnessed a large number
of comprehensive programs internationally. Around the
year 1905 vacuum melting was invented for material pro-
cessing especially to improve the reliability of steel.
Vacuum melting allowed elemental chemical control of
steel. This melting process is also able to refrain oxygen
and nitrogen from the melting pool of the steel. It can
also remove the trapping of gases from metal. Again
vacuum melting allows many refractory materials like
tungsten and Niobium to be added to the metal pool. So
with the help of vacuum melting alloys of molybdenum,
nickel, chromium, cobalt, etc., can be combined to form
superalloys that are free from nonmetallic impurities.
With the passage of time vacuum induction and vacuum
arc remelting again facilitate the formation of different
superalloys. The Wright brothers ﬂew their ﬁrst aircraft
on 17 December 1903.
11 Then the trend began to create
an aircraft engine that can ﬂy higher and faster.
Commercial and defence demand of aircraft led to the
development of jet engines. Gradually jet engines
demanded such kind of materials that can withstand
high temperatures at high stress. This gave way to the for-
mation of nickel- and cobalt-based high-temperature
alloys which with the assistance of vacuum melting
gave birth to superalloys.
At ﬁrst Elwood Haynes et al.
12 in 1907 planted the
seed for the growth of superalloy. Haynes was involved
in the development of cutting tool alloys that can retain
their high strength and hardness at elevated temperatures.
Because in those days as cutting speed increased the tool
wear increased very fast. After a lot of experiments,
Haynes found that cobalt-chrome alloys meet his expecta-
tions as a tool material. Through extensive research, he
developed an alloy named Vitallium which is considered
to be the ﬁrst superalloy. Simultaneously A. L. Marsh was
also investigating nickel –chrome alloys for electrical
resistance applications.
13 Nickel–chrome alloys also
entered the race to be considered as a superalloy. The
development of automotive engines and steam turbines
created the driving force for the development of various
superalloys. Dr Wilhelm Rohn and W. C. Heraeus ﬁrst
performed vacuum melting in an induction furnace in
Germany in 1916. They melted steel and produced clean
steel that was used in turbine engines of military aircraft
in World War II. Bedford and Pilling along with Merica
added some amount of aluminum and titanium to the
very popular nickel –chromium alloy. It was evident that
signiﬁcant creep resistance was achieved. Bradley and
Taylor in 1940 discovered the existence of tiny particle
γ’in nickel-based superalloy. Edisonian experimenters in
1930 were unaware of the tiny particle γ’. But they suc-
ceeded to form nickel and iron-based alloys containing
carbide-strengthened cobalt alloys alongwith chromium
and γ’.

Elemental practices for turbine blade
In early age alloys consisted only of iron or nickel with a
sufﬁcient amount of chromium for oxidation resistance.
Gradually small amounts of titanium, aluminum, colum-
bium, etc., were added to produce coherent γ’phase for
enhancing creep resistance. Reduction of chromium
leads to hot corrosion problems. So in superalloy it was
a must that chromium was mixed in alloying elements.
Again addition of aluminum, titanium, columbium, etc.,
promotes the formation of γ’ which is desirable for
high-temperature applications but the excess use of the
elements causes embrittlement in the superalloy.

Strengthening mechanism of Ni-based
superalloys
As we know that Ni-based superalloys contain matrix γ in
which intermetallic γ′ is precipitated. In the discussion for
strengthening Ni-based superalloys priority is given to
increasing the amount of γ
′ phase in γ matrix. An
attempt is made to add elements in the primary matrix
to form a substitutional solid solution and increase the
percentage of γ
′ in it. The higher the amount of γ′ pro-
duced in Ni-based superalloy larger will be the strength
of the superalloy at high temperatures. The grain bound-
ary is strengthened by the addition boron and zirconium
and also by carbide precipitation. The electronic con ﬁgur-
ation of Ni permits larger solubility for various elements
which leads to solid solution strengthening.
101 Solid solu-
tion strengthening is mainly dependent upon lattice distor-
tion. The more the lattice distortion the more will be the
tendency to form a solution and more the tendency to
have strength. Solid solution strengthening is a strength-
ening mechanism that occurs in metallic materials when
foreign atoms are introduced into the crystal lattice of
the base metal. This mechanism is dependent on lattice
distortion, which plays a critical role in increasing the
material’s strength. The lattice distortion can be either
by substitutional solid solution or by interstitial solid solu-
tion. In substitutional solid solution strengthening, foreign
atoms replace some of the host atoms in the crystal lattice
and in interstitial solid solution foreign atoms takes the
place of interatomic space in between two atoms. In
both cases, a size mismatch occurs between foreign
atoms and native atoms. Due to this, the movement of
crystal gets restricted that contributes to the strengthening
of the material. Lattice distortion is favorable if the atomic
size difference between the two elements increases. Again
reduction in diffusion and lattice cohesion were provided
by high melting point elements like W and Mo at high
temperatures. The matrix can also be strengthened by
atomic clustering or short-range ordering as observed
in Mo, W, Cr, and Al. But temperature above 0.6 T
m
strengthening due to short-range ordering decreases to a
large extent due to diffusion. 102 Various strengthening
mechanism for Ni-based superalloy is briefed below.
Solid-solution strengthening
In solid solution strengthening foreign materials are added
to the matrix. The difference in particle size between
matrix and foreign particle is such that substitutional or
interstitional solution takes place which in turn distorts
lattice structure. But there are some requirements for
solid-solution strengthening those are discussed below.
1. The matrix should have a wide range of solid
solubility.
2. The atomic size difference between both elements
must be more than 10%.
3. The foreign material should have a high melting point.
In addition, dislocation cross-slip and diffusion are
important at elevated temperatures to determine strength.
W and Mo diffuse slowly at high temperatures so better
solid solubility happens with their addition. Again ele-
ments like Co which have lower stacking fault energy
between partial dislocations also considered as better
additions.

the cooling process forms gamma-prime precipitation
but side by side detrimental effect of residual stress is
also developed. Solution heat treatment dissolves gamma-
prime precipitation and carbides present. While cooling it
forms new precipitates which are more organized and
have optimum mechanical strength. Cooling rate greatly
impacts the behavior of superalloy. A higher cooling
rate may increase mechanical strength but ultimately
induces residual strength in it. The higher the variation
in cooling rate larger the thermal stress developed.
Liquid quenching like oil quenching extract heat uni-
formly and very rapidly. But oil quenching is very effect-
ive to increase the mechanical property of the alloy.
In conventional heat treatment generally, solution-heat-
treatment is done which is followed by a rapid cooling
and aging process in order to gain the highest strength.

Summary
It is evident from the above discussion that Ni-based
superalloy has superior property in enhancing strength
at elevated temperatures. So it is more convenient to be
used in aero turbine parts. But the processing of these
alloys are bit dif ﬁcult. First of all, for melting of the
alloy VIM technique is used that needs careful handling
of the material. Again it ’s a challenge to decrease the
grain boundary for the alloy. Directional solidi ﬁcation
and single-crystal growth have provided partial solutions
for the problem. But still, research is going on to develop
material that can withstand more temperatures either by
decreasing grain boundary or by incorporating new ele-
ments in the alloy. Now it ’s a task for researchers to
develop a seventh generation of Ni-based superalloys
that can withstand even more temperatures. Again
machining and machinability of the alloy have stood as
a greater barrier for shaping the alloy. Being hard it ’s
very dif ﬁcult to machine but the emergence of new
machining techniques such as multiaxis drilling, CNC
contour machining, etc., have somehow managing the
process. Again, once there is wear or tear in the alloy
used in aero sector it ’s very dif ﬁcult to machine it and
reuse it. It ’s a general practice till now to replace the
total work material with a newer one. Therefore, lots of
effort and research are required to explore new machining
techniques for wear-out superalloys.
The presence of γ’ in the superalloy matrix increases
creep strength, fatigue strength, and stress rupture proper-
ties. In addition to γ’ phase the superalloy may consist of
y’’ phases, carbide phases, boride phases, nitride phases,
and TCP phases. As TCP phases are hazardous to the
health of superalloys so research is going on in reducing
the amount of formation of TCP phases in superalloys.
Generally, Ni-based superalloys get strengthened by pre-
cipitation hardening in which the main strengthening
element is γ’. So there is always continuous effort is estab-
lished to enhance the amount of formation of γ’ or γ’’
phases in superalloys. Discussing the properties of the
Ni-based superalloys, these superalloys have higher
creep strength as well as fatigue strength compared to
any other alloy. Due to this property, the alloy can
sustain its mechanical strength at elevated temperature.
So the Ni-based superalloys are used in various aero
turbine parts such as turbine blades, turbine discs, turbo-
chargers, turbine nozzles, combustion cans, etc., and
it is the only option so far for the manufacturing of
turbine blades. Table 3 illustrates the key ﬁndings of aero-
