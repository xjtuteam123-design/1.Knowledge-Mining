This article presents an extruded Mg–Gd–Zn–Zr alloy produced by conventional ingot
metallurgy, exhibiting high-strength and excellent ductility at room and elevated
temperatures. The superplastic behavior was observed in the Mg–Gd–Zn (–Zr) alloy at
elevated temperatures above 573 K. In the alloy, both the X phase in grain boundaries and
the lamellae within matrix have the 14H-type long period stacking ordered structure.
It indicates that the X phase and the lamellae within matrix play important roles in the
excellent mechanical properties.
I. INTRODUCTION
In recent studies, some magnesium (Mg) alloys with
high strength and heat resistance have been developed in
the Mg-RE system (where RE represents rare earth ele-
ments), where more attention has gradually been paid on
high-strength Mg–RE–Zn (–Zr) alloys, due to a combined
strengthening by solid solution, precipitation,
1,2 novel
long period stacking ordered (LPSO) structures, 3–24 as
well as grain refinement 8,12 in these alloys. Based on our
study3–5 and the previous reports, 6–23 various types of
LPSO structures such as 6H, 10H, 14H, 18R, 24R have
been observed in high-strength Mg–RE(RE = Y, Dy, Ho,
Er, Gd, Tb, Tm)–Zn (–Zr) alloys. For example, a high-
strength Mg
97Zn1Y2 (at.%) alloy with LPSO structure
was produced by rapidly solidified powder metallurgy
(RS/PM) in 2001 and exhibited remarkable mechanical
properties with a tensile yield strength of 610 MPa and an
elongation of 5% at room temperature.
8 The nanocrystal-
line Mg solid solutions with LPSO structure in RS/PM
Mg
97Zn1Y2 (at.%) alloy have been recognized as a deci-
sive strengthening factor.8
It has been found that conventional ingot metallurgy
(I/M) process can also refine the microstructure with
LPSO structures and improve mechanical properties of
Mg–RE–Zn (–Zr) alloys. 12,18,19,24 Extrusion is applied
as an effective process to achieve Mg alloys with excel-
lent mechanical properties by refining grains with
LPSO structure and dispersing the phases with LPSO
structure.
18,19
At present, the mechanical properties at elevated tem-
peratures of Mg–Gd–Zn (–Zr) alloys with LPSO struc-
ture have not been reported.
3–5,16–19 In this study, an
extruded Mg 96.32Gd2.5Zn1Zr0.18 alloy was produced by
conventional I/M process, in which both the X phase in
grain boundaries and the lamellae within Mg matrix
have the 14H-type LPSO structure. The extruded Mg–
Gd–Zn–Zr alloy has good tensile mechanical properties
with high strength and excellent ductility at room and
elevated temperatures. At 423 K, it exhibits the highest
strength with s
b = 390 MPa and better ductility with d =
20%. It is interesting to note that a superplasticity ( d =
233.7–495.4%) during tensile tests at temperatures
above 573 K was observed in the Mg–Gd–Zn (–Zr)
alloys with the 14H-type LPSO structure, which has
never been reported in previous work.
This article reports the tensile mechanical properties
and fracture behavior of extruded Mg
96.32Gd2.5Zn1Zr0.18
alloy at room and elevated temperatures (423–723 K).
Furthermore, it focuses on the evolution of microstruc-
ture, especially the plastic behavior of the X phase in
grain boundaries and the lamellae within a
0-Mg matrix
during deformation.

At present, there are many superplastic deformation
mechanisms in different alloy systems. Among them,
grain boundaries slip (GBS) is well known as the main
superplastic deformation mechanism in Mg alloys, and it
is sometimes accompanied by other mechanisms such as
DRX and cavity diffusion and aggregation modes for
different Mg alloy systems.
24–26
In the study, the superplastic deformation mechanism
is much more complex in Mg–Gd–Zn–Zr alloys. As
described above, the investigated alloy shows four fea-
tures: (i) the fine grains with sizes of approximately 1 to
4 mm; (ii) tension at stress rate of 1.67 /C210
/C03 s/C01;
(iii) good plasticity of the lamellar X phase in grain
boundaries and the lamellae within a
0-Mg matrix at
above 573 K; and (iv) thermal stability of microstructure
resulting from the high-thermal stability of the lamellar
X phase in grain boundaries and the lamellae within
a
0-Mg matrix. Moreover, the grains of the Mg–Gd–Zn–
Zr still show equiaxial in the specimens deformed above
300
/C14C, which may be attributed to these features. Because
keeping equiaxial grain structure even at high tensile elon-
gation is an important criterion for superplastic materials
deforming by GBS,
26 GBS may be the main superplastic
mechanism used to investigate Mg–Gd–Zn–Zr alloy.
It is well known that grain boundaries are potential
sources of weakness at elevated temperatures for Mg alloys
because they provide nucleation sites for cavities and paths
for the interlinkage of cavities. When cavities begin to
grow to the triple junctions of grain boundaries, GBS can
improve ductility by relaxing these microcavities. There-
fore, the dispersed microcavities are useful to GBS and can
improve superplasticity of the alloy. In this study, super-
plasticity of the Mg–Gd–Zn–Zr alloy is probably attributed
to the GBS accompanied by the DRX and cavity diffusion
and aggregation modes, which require additional studies.
IV. CONCLUSIONS
The extruded Mg–Gd–Zn–Zr alloy with high strength
and excellent elongation and superplasticity ( d = 233.7–
495.4%) at elevated temperatures (623–573 K) has been
prepared by conventional I/M process. Both the X phase
with the 14H-type LPSO structure in grain boundaries
and the lamellae with the 14H-type LPSO structure with-
in a
0-Mg matrix play important roles in the excellent
mechanical properties because of their good plasticity
and high-thermal stability at elevated temperatures.
Therefore, the extruded Mg–Gd–Zn–Zr alloy with the
14H-type LPSO structure is a promising candidate of
lightweight structural materials for applications at room
and elevated temperatures.
