The precipitation of an ordered L12 phase in the Co-Al-W ternary
system [1,2] has enabled the development of Co-base alloys with
increased high temperature strength [3-5]. Advantages of Co-Al-W
alloys include a large processing window between thec′solvus and
thesolidustemperature[6],makingthemsuitableaswroughtalloys,
as well as mechanical properties comparable to ﬁrst generation Ni-
base superalloys [7]. The high W content required to stabilise thec′
phase, however, unfavourably increases their density.
Makineni et al. [8-10] stabilised the c′ phase in the Co-Al-Mo
ternary with small additions of either Ta or Nb, thus substituting W
and achieving a welcome reduction in density from 9.3–9.9g/cm3 to
8.36–8.65g/cm3. Nb containing Co-Al-Mo alloys have superior spe-
ciﬁc yield strengths compared to Co-Al-W alloys [8,9]. Replacing Nb
with Ta improves the room temperature and high temperature yield
stressaswellasthe c
′solvustemperature[ 10].Theﬂowstressand c′
solvus temperatures can be further increased via Ni additions to the
Co-Al-Mo-Ta/Nb alloys [8,10] or Ti additions to Ta containing alloys

Theelementalpartitioningcoeﬃcient, ki,isdeﬁnedastheratioofthe
concentration of an element,i,i nt h ec′phase to that in thec phase,
ki = Cc′/Cc.
Co, Mo and Cr were all observed to partition to thec matrix, ki <
1. Co-Al-W superalloys with substantial Ni additions generally show
Co partitioning to thec phase [18-21], but do so more strongly as
the Ni content is increased, as observed in Ni superalloys [22]. The
partitioning coeﬃcients for Co observed here (0.65–0.67) are similar
to those reported by Makineni in alloys near 53Co-30Ni-10Al-5Mo-
2Ta, 0.53–0.74 [10,11].
Crfavoursthe cphasewithsimilarpartitioningcoeﬃcients, kCr ∼
0.25, for alloys 2Ta1Nb and 3Ta0Nb. Cr is generally found to parti-
tion to thec in c′forming Ni and Co superalloys. In Ni superalloys,
kCr ranges between 0.08 and 0.31, dependent on Co:Ni ratio [22].
Adding Ni in Co-Ni-Al-W superalloys tended to decreasekCr, to 0.35
for Co:Ni=3:2 [19] and further to 0.19 for Co:Ni=1:1 [18]. In gen-
eral, the more Ni rich thec′precipitates the stronger Cr partitions to
the c matrix.
In contrast, Cr additions do not affectkCr. For example, in the Co-
9Al-9W (at.%) ternary alloykCr remains ∼0.57 when doubling the Cr
content from 4 to 8at.% [20]. This may aid in explaining the similar-
ities in kCr ∼ 0.24 between this work and that of Nithin et al. [11]
despite the differences in Cr content, 13 vs 10at.% respectively.
Mo partitions to the c matrix with kMo =0 .48 and 0.55 in
2Ta1Nb and 3Ta0Nb, respectively. Interestingly, Mo partitions to the
c′phase, kMo =1 .26, in Co-9Al-10W [23]. The preference for thec
phase is an effect of Cr, e.g.kMo =0 .53 in Cr-containing Co-Ni-Al-
W-Mo-Ta[19]and0.78–0.87inCo-Ni-Al-Mo-Ta(W-free)alloys[ 11].
