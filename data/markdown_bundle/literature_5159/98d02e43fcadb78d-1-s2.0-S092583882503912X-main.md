ABSTRACT
Achieving excellent strength-ductility synergy in near- α TA18 Ti alloy by rolling deformation remains a huge 
challenge. In this paper, we have fabricated a TA18 Ti alloy that integrates heterogeneous lamella structure and 
high-density deformation twins by using a novel technique of hot rolling at the temperature of ( α + β ) two-phase 
region and subsequent cryogenic-temperature rolling treatment. Notably, an excellent strength-ductility synergy 
with a yield strength of ~1063 MPa and an elongation of ~20 % is achieved, which realized the goal of a 
simultaneous enhancement of both yield strength and elongation compared to that with homogeneous structure, 
pushing the boundary of the strength – ductility synergy map for near- α Ti to a site which is not achieved before 
and even be superior to that of TC4 and some other typical α + β Ti alloys. The high yield strength is mainly 
caused by the pronounce fine grain strengthening from dislocation movement obstruction at the interface be -
tween α -Ti and β -Ti lamellae in the FG regions. The GNDs formed by the hetero-deformation between FG and CG, 
the activation of stacking faults and pyramidal dislocations via DTs in the CG region, along with the nucleation of 
dislocations in β -Ti lamellae endows the TA18 Ti alloy with superior tensile ductility.
1. Introduction
Titanium (Ti) alloys have aroused great interest in the field of 
aircraft, engine and marine due to their high specific strength, corrosion 
resistance and excellent weld ability [1 – 2] . Based on the two basic 
phases of HCP- α and BCC- β , the Ti alloys could possess the microstruc -
ture of α , near- α , α + β , near- β and β , each of these microstructures for Ti 
alloys has merits and drawbacks [3 – 6] . As a near- α Ti alloy, TA18 alloy 
(Ti-3Al-2.5 V) possesses the combination of high strength, good plas -
ticity and welding performance, which is of great interests in 
manufacturing the hydraulic tubing systems of aeroengine application 
[7 – 8] . The new generation aeroengine with higher thrust-to-weight 
demands the hydraulic tubing systems to withstand greater pressure, 
which required to further increase the strength of TA18 Ti alloy without 
loss of plasticity. The quantity of β phases in near- α TA18 Ti alloy is very 
small at room temperature, so heat treatment cannot effectively 
strengthen it, and the main strengthening method is cold rolling defor -
mation. However, the obvious strength improvement of TA18 Ti alloy 
via cold rolling deformation is generally at the expense of plasticity, 
which is known as the strength-ductility trade-off dilemma and unfa -
vorable for fulfilling the stringent performance demands of new gener -
ation aeroengine. Therefore, how to obtain an excellent combination of 
strength and ductility is still an urgent problem for the TA18 Ti alloy 
applicated in the new generation aeroengine.
In recent years, the fabrication of heterogeneous lamella-structure 
(HL) by incorporating both hard and soft zones have become an effec -
tive strategy to possess unprecedented strength-ductility synergy for 
metal materials [9 – 17] . The superior strength-ductility synergy of HL 
structured materials stems from the hetero-deformation induced (HDI) 
strengthening and strain hardening, which is produced by the steep 
strain gradients between hard and soft zones during the plastic defor -
mation process. Such as, Wu et al. [9 – 10] have utilized the method of

alloy with a heterogeneous lamellar mixed grain structure via rolling

the intensified back stress effect induced by strain partitioning within

unprecedented strength-ductility synergy, i.e., the yield strength is close

that the introduction of HL structure into TA18 Ti alloy could effectively 
enhance the strength-ductility synergy. However, how to simply intro -
duce the HL structure into TA18 Ti alloy and realize the effective control 
of heterogeneity remains a challenge. In addition, the strengthening and 
ductility effect in HL structure still have room for further improvement 
due to the fact that the HDI-hardening effect depends mainly on the 
interface density, while the microstructure design within the grain of HL 
structured materials has been paid little attention.
The design of high-density twin boundaries has been proven to be 
another feasible strategy in the pursuit of enhancing the strength- 
ductility synergy for metallic materials [18 – 22] . Such twin 
boundaries-induced strength-ductility synergy is mainly because that 
the twin boundaries could act as either the barrier or source for dislo -
cation. When the dislocations cross twin boundaries, glissile or sessile 
dislocations and other defect types will be generated on the twin 
boundaries, and thereby increasing the strength and ductility. Such as, 
Lu et al. [18] have fabricated a copper with high-density twins, which 
resulted in a 400 % increase in tensile strength, while maintaining a 
comparable ductility. Zhao et al. [19] investigated the influence of grain 
size and deformation twins density on the mechanical properties of 
magnesium alloys, and found that with the increase in deformation twin 
density, both coarse grains and fine grains exhibit significant ductility 
enhancement; meanwhile, the increasing obstruction effect of grain 
boundaries and twin boundaries on dislocations improves the strength. 
Although twin boundaries could effectively enhance the 
strength-ductility synergy of metals, the introduction of high-density 
twin boundaries in α -Ti alloys remains a challenge, because the stack -
ing fault energy of α -Ti is high and the twin activity is low. Fortunately, 
some recent studies [17,23 – 25] have indicated that the deformation 
twins (DTs) could be introduced in the metal materials via plastic 
deformation at low temperature based on the principle of promoting 
twin boundaries formation by suppressing dislocation movement, which 
offered a new method for the introduction of twin boundaries in α -Ti 
alloys.
In this work, we have fabricated a TA18 Ti alloy that integrates HL 
structure and high-density DTs by using a novel technique. Specifically, 
a HL structure with coarse grain (CG) and fine grain (FG) layer was 
introduced in the TA18 Ti plate via hot rolling at the temperature of 
( α + β ) two-phase region. Then the cryogenic-temperature rolling (CTR) 
treatment was employed to induce high-density DTs in TA18 Ti plate. 
Surprisingly, an excellent strength-ductility synergy with a yield 
strength (YS) of ~1064 MPa and an elongation of ~20 % is achieved, 
which realized the goal of a simultaneous enhancement of both yield 
strength and elongation compared to that with homogeneous structure, 
pushing the boundary of the strength – ductility synergy map for near- α 
Ti to a site which is not achieved before and even be superior to that of 
TC4 and some other typical α + β Ti alloys. The deformation mechanisms 
for the strength – ductility synergy of HL structure coupling with DTs in 
TA18 Ti alloy were analyzed by the combination of local strain 
distribution analysis and elaborate microstructure characterization.
2. Materials and methods
Fig. 1 shows the preparation flow chart of TA18 Ti alloy with HL 
structure and high-density DTs. The initial TA18 Ti plate, with the 
dimension of 120 × 200 × 300 mm, was fabricated by melting in a 
plasma arc cold hearth furnace at temperatures above 1933 K (a tem -
perature above the β phase transition point), and it exhibits the typical 
microstructure characteristics of as-casted near- α Ti alloys, i.e., various 
acicular α phases with different directions are distributed into the coarse 
β grains. In addition, an infrared thermometer monitors real-time tem -
perature changes on the melt pool surface during smelting, and plasma 
gun stopping time is controlled to keep the melt temperature consistent. 
Then the TA18 Ti plate was hot-rolled at the ( α + β ) two-phase region 
temperature of 1253 K to obtain a HL structure with CG and FG layer. 
Hot rolling is carried out in two multi-pass rolling processes, the first of 
which rolls the sheet from 120 mm to 20 mm in 12 passes, the second of 
which reduces the thickness to 5.8 mm, and each of which is heated and 
held for 90 – 120 min before rolling. The reason for the formation of HL 
structure in TA18 Ti plate is that the acicular α phase with soft orien -
tation deforms first during the hot-rolling at the temperature of ( α + β ) 
two-phase region, numerous dislocations are accumulated inside the 
grain and the dynamic recrystallization (DRX) was occurred, forming CG 
layer. While the dislocation in the acicular α phase with hard orientation 
is less, the dynamic recovery occurs to produce various subgrains, and 
the β phase penetrates along the subgrain boundary to form FG layer. 
Then the CTR was performed at 77 K, reducing the thickness from 
5.8 mm to 2.9 mm with a total thickness reduction of 50 %, resulting in 
TA18 with an HL structure and DT (denoted as HL + DT-TA18). To ensure 
that the rolling process takes place at 77 K, the titanium plates are 
immersed in liquid nitrogen for 30 min prior to each roll. For compar -
ison, the hot-rolling at β phase region and room-temperature rolling 
with a 50 % total thickness reduction was also performed to obtain the 
TA18 Ti plate with homogeneous structure (denoted as HS-TA18).

3.2. Mechanical properties
Fig. 3 (a) plots the engineering tensile stress-strain curves of as- 
fabricated (HL + DT)-TA18 and HS-TA18 Ti alloy. Surprisingly, both 
the YS and fracture elongation of (HL + DT)-TA18 Ti alloy were simul -
taneously increased when compared to that of HS-TA18 Ti alloy: the YS 
increases from ~842 MPa for HS-TA18 Ti alloy to ~1063 MPa for the 
(HL + DT)-TA18 Ti alloy, the fracture elongation increases from ~15.7 % 
for HS-TA18 Ti alloy to ~20.5 % for the (HL + DT)-TA18 Ti alloy. In 
addition, a distinct “ discontinuous yielding ” characteristic was observed 
in the tensile stress – strain curve of (HL + DT)-TA18 Ti alloy, where 
transient stress reduction appears after reaching the yield strength, 
followed by a subsequent plastic deformation stage. which contrasts 
with the continuous yielding of HS-TA18 Ti alloy. The similar phe -
nomenon has also been observed in some other metals with HL structure 
or other intricate microstructural features [26 – 31] , which suggests that 
the “ discontinuous yielding ” of the (HL + DT)-TA18 Ti alloy mainly stems 
from the HL structure and high-density DTs.
Fig. 3 (b) shows the tensile true stress-strain curves and correspond -
ing strain hardening rate-strain curves of as-fabricated (HL + DT)-TA18 
Ti alloy and HS-TA18 Ti alloy, respectively. It could be clearly seen that 
the work hardening rate for HS-TA18 Ti alloy gradually decreases, 
whereas that for (HL + DT)-TA18 Ti alloy drops rapidly, followed by a 
steep upturn and subsequent decline, which is a typical feature of 
discontinuous yielding, which corresponds to the three typical defor -
mation stages observed in the metal with HL structure: elastic defor -
mation, yield drop, and fracture stage. Fig. 3 (d) compares the tensile 
properties of (HL + DT)-TA18 with those of some other α Ti, near- α Ti, 
α + β Ti alloys. It can be seen that α Ti alloys have high plasticity but 
relatively low yield strength. On the contrary, near- α Ti alloys and α + β 
Ti alloys have high yield strength but relatively low plasticity [32 – 47] . 
Through comparison, it is found that the (HL + DT)-TA18 Ti alloy in this 
work could push the boundary of strength – ductility synergy map for 
near- α Ti to a site which is not achieved before and even be superior to 
that of TC4 and some other typical α + β Ti alloys. This suggests that the 
incorporation of HL structure and high-density DTs imparts superior 
strength-ductility synergy to the near- α TA18 Ti alloys.

deformation between FG and CG, the activation of stacking faults and 
pyramidal dislocations via DTs in the CG region, along with the nucle -
ation of dislocations in β -Ti lamellae endows the TA18 Ti alloy with 
superior tensile ductility. We expect that our work could provide new 
insights into the design of Ti alloys with high strength and excellent 
ductility.
