IMPROVEMENT IN THE HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH OF 
ALLOYS BASED ON COBALT BY INTERNAL NITRIDING 
L. G. Petrova UDC 621.785.53:669.255 
Internal nitriding, as for internal oxidation, may be considered as a particular case of material dispersion 
strengthening. Internal nitriding is accompanied by preparation of a heterophase structure of a diffusion layer: 
The matrix is a solid solution with fine particles of alloying element nitride particles distributed within it. 
Internal nitriding may be of special importance as a version of dispersion hardening for high-temperature 
alloys. Possibilities are considered in this article for internal nitriding of cobalt alloys. 
Classical methods for dispersion hardening of high-temperature alloys based on iron, nickel, and cobalt are founded 
on precipitation in the metal phase of carbides or intermetallic compounds, or obtaining oxide precipitates within the matrix 
of powder materials [1-4]. The high level of fineness of phases precipitated at high temperatures has a decisive effect on 
material high-temperature strength. In turn the tendency toward particle coalescence will be less, the higher the thermodynamic 
stability of the dispersed phases and the lower the solubility of them in the individual components of the alloy matrix. Thus, 
obtaining fine particles of stable nitrides in alloys may provide good high-temperature properties. 
In order to carry out nitriding* successfully it is necessary to fulfill the following conditions: 
1. An alloying element nitride should exhibit a much higher free energy of formation compared with the matrix metal 
nitride, i.e., the matrix metal, which is inert with respect to nitrogen, should be alloyed with a nitride-forming element. 
2. The solubility and diffusion mobility of nitrogen in the alloy should be sufficient in order for growth of a diffusion 
layer. In order to fulfill this condition the impregnation process is performed at high temperature. 
3. In order that a continuous nitride layer does not form at the surface the minimum content of alloying element and 
the correct choice of nitriding schedule is necessary. 
As studies have shown [5], these conditions are achieved with nitriding of nickel alloys containing nitride-forming 
elements (Ti, Zr, or Hf). A very small amount of nitrogen dissolves in nickel (<0.07% N with t = 1200°C [1]) but this is 
overcome by adding chromium (20-30%) to the alloy. With nitriding of this alloy in the temperature range 1000-1200°C in 
an atmosphere of pure nitrogen or ammonia a zone if internal nitriding forms without a surface zone of continuous nitride. 
Alloy with this structure exhibits high-temperature strength characteristics (up to 1100°C) [5] compared with alloys with 
intermetallic strengthening. 
In its physical and many mechanical properties cobalt is analogous to nickel and therefore it is of interest to study the 
possibility of strengthening cobalt alloys by internal nitriding. It is traditional for high-temperature cobalt alloys to use carbide 
strengthening with carbide particles of titanium, tantalum, niobium, tungsten, etc., which precipitate as a result of the 
decomposition of supersaturated solid solution with cooling below the temperature of limiting solubility. For alloy I~I416 this 
is accomplished by quenching from 1225°C followed by aging at 800°C for 20 h [2]. 
Alloys with carbide strengthening have less high-temperature strength than with intermetallic strengthening since 
carbides are inclined toward coalescence with t > 700°C. In view of this the limiting working temperature for cobalt alloys

After nitriding at high-temperature (1200 ° C) apart from disordered precipitates, periodic precipitates of nitride particles 
with areas in the form of continuous bands perpendicular to the diffusion front appear in cobalt alloys (Fig. 2a). These bands 
are observed after oxidizing of alloys containing two components with different thermodynamic activity inclined toward forming 
a second phase [1]. The reason for appearance of these bands is the fact that in order to form nuclei a strictly determined 
concentration is reached at some distance from the surface where there is intense nucleus formation. The neighboring region 
is impoverished in elements which form the second phase. Particles of this phase may only nucleate in individual areas as a 
result of the fluctuation of the concentration of elements throughout the volume. As distance from the bands formed increases 
the concentration of elements increases and at a certain distance a new chain of nitrides is precipitated.

In photomicrographs of both cobalt and nickel nitrided alloys there is a dark band in the section near the surface of 
the internal nitriding zone. This makes it possible to talk about a nonuniform and complex phase composition of the nitrided 
layer connected with presence in the alloys of several alloying elements which are active with respect to nitrogen. 
The phase composition of the internal nitriding zone was studied by means of x-ray structural analysis in a DRON-3 
unit in chromium K,~-radiation. Absence of a zone of continuous nitrides is confirmed by analyzing x-ray pictures from the 
surface of specimens where apart from nitride phases in all cases peaks of ?-solid solution based on cobalt were identified. As 
far as different nitrides are concerned then, development of them depends on nitriding parameters and the phase composition 
changes through the layer thickness. In order to study nitride phases at a different distance from the surface specimens from 
which layers of different thickness were ground were subjected to x-ray analysis. The results of the studies are provided in 
Table 3. 
It can be seen that nitride phases form in relation to the nitriding temperature at a different distance from the surface 
in accordance with their thermodynamic stability. At the surface where the concentration of active nitrogen is high there is 
preferred formation of chromium nitrides CrN and Cr2N. Nitrides of titanium are not detected at the surface although they are 
more thermodynamically stable, which is connected with the low concentration of titanium compared with chromium. As 
distance from the surface increases the nitrogen concentration decreases, formation of chromium nitrides becomes impossible, 
and at a distance from the surface of more than 20-50 ~m (depending on temperature) only precipitates of stable titanium nitride 
are observed. 
The change in phase composition at the surface in relation to temperature corresponds to the change in thermodynamic 
stability of nitrides [7, 8]. At 1000°C chromium nitride CrN is more stable than Cr2N, but with t > 1030°C Cr2N is more 
stable. After nitriding at 1100°C apart from chromium nitrides at the surface there is tungsten nitride W2N (its free energy 
of formation depends little on temperature), although at 1200°C this nitride disappears.

In studying the kinetics of nitriding a parabolic rule is established for the rate of diffusion layer growth on time (Fig. 
5). For the maximum increase in high-temperature strength it is necessary that the zone of internal nitriding with nitride

on sheet specimens may be obtained only after impregnation at high temperature (1200°C). For a specimen 1.5 mm thick the 
time for through nitriding is about 25 h.

CONCLUSIONS 
1. In alloys based on cobalt containing nitride-forming elements it is possible to obtain an internal nitriding zone which 
is a prerequisite for an increase in its high-temperature strength. 
2. The internal nitriding zone in multicomponent cobalt alloys has a complex structure and the phase composition of 
nitrides depends on the thermodynamic activity of the alloying elements and on nitriding temperature. In the majority of the 
internal nitriding zones stable titanium nitride TiN precipitates. 
3. Internal nitriding of alloys based on cobalt makes it possible to increase their high-temperature strength and operating 
temperature as a result of strengthening by stable nitrides compared with alloys with traditional carbide strengthening. 
4. For the most effective strengthening it is necessary to obtain an extended or through internal nitriding zone through 
the section of an article which governs the choice of nitriding parameters.
