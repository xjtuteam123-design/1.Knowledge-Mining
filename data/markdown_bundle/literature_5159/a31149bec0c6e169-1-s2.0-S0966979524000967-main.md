ABSTRACT 
Improving the efficiency of turbines for power plants and aircraft engines is an increasingly important research subject. Ternary Mo-Si-B alloys, consisting of a 
molybdenum solid solution (Mo
ss
) phase and two intermetallic phases Mo
5
SiB
2 
(T2) and Mo
3
Si, are able to combine balanced room temperature fracture toughness, 
high temperature creep strength and good oxidation performance. However, the high density (typically > 9 g/cm
3
) of this class of alloys is a drawback when used as 
structural materials for rotating components. In this study, high potential Mo-V-Si-B materials which provide a reduced density by about 17 % as compared with the 
reference alloy Mo-9Si-8B were investigated. A comparative characterization was carried out on sintered (FAST) and arc-melted Mo-40V-9Si-8B alloys. Compressive 
creep tests reveal competitive creep resistance of this new type of alloys in the application relevant temperature regime. 
1. Introduction 
Mo-Si-B alloys are high-performance materials combining important 
high temperature properties. The most widely studied alloy Mo-9Si-8B 
composed of body-centered cubic (bcc) Mo solid solution (Mo
ss
), 
Mo
3
Si, and Mo
5

2

temperature fracture toughness, high-temperature creep strength and 
oxidation performance [ 1 ]. However, with ~9.6 g/cm
3 
[ 2 ], the density 
of this material, is comparably high and represents a drawback when 
used for flying or rotating applications. Promising alloying elements for 
density reduction can be found in the group of refractory metals such 
Nb, Cr, Zr, Ti and V. But, in case of Nb the density difference as 
compared with Mo is too small in order to cause an effective weight 
reduction in spite of a high solubility in Mo [ 3 ]. Even if Cr would cause a 
comparably higher density reduction, the miscibility gap in the binary 
Mo-Cr system represents an unfavorable feature [ 3 ]. More promising 
alloying elements are Zr, V and Ti. Especially for minor additions of Zr 
and Ti, many positive aspects on the mechanical properties of Mo-Si-B 
alloys have been reported in the last years, e.g. in Refs. [ 4 – 6 ]. Some 
initial investigations also stated positive effects of V on the Mo-Si-B 
system [ 4 , 7 ]. In contrast to Zr, the binary systems of Mo-Ti and Mo-V 
show a larger miscibility range [ 3 ], while V forms solid solutions with 
Mo in the entire concentration regime [ 8 ].

2. Experimental 
Initially, investigations were carried out on a Mo-40V-9Si-8B alloy 
processed via powder metallurgy. For this purpose, V (purity > 99.9%), 
Mo (purity 99.95%), Si and B powders (both purity 99.9%) were 
weighed and mixed under protective argon atmosphere and filled in 
grinding vials including stainless-steel balls (d = 10 mm) at powder-to- 
ball ratio of 1:14. The mixed powders were milled in a planetary ball 
mill (Retsch PM 400, Haan, Germany) at rotational speed of 200 rpm for 
30 h. In order to create compact specimen for characterization, the field 
assisted sintering (FAST) was performed in a hot press HP D (FCT Sys -
teme GmbH) at the Karlsruher Institute of Technology (KIT). Therefore, 
the loose, mechanically alloyed powder was filled into a graphite die and 
-punch unit, then directly heated using a direct current (DC) via the 
Joule effect. A heating rate of 100 K/min and holding times of 15 min at 
1100 
◦
C and 1500 
◦
C were used for processing. The whole process was 
performed under vacuum (P < 10 Pa) and uniaxial pressure of 50 MPa. 
Samples for examination by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and 
thermomechanical investigations were prepared from the sintered but -
tons (diameter 30 mm, height 9 mm) by electrical discharge machining 
(EDM). Some of these samples were heat treated (HT) using a HTRH 70- 
600/18 furnace (Carbolite Gero GmbH & Co. KG) at 1600 
◦
C for 10 h 
under argon atmosphere (at KIT) in order to create a coarser micro -
structure which is supposed to be beneficial in terms of creep resistance. 
In order to prove and visualize the continuity of the individual 
phases of the FAST alloy, a 3D reconstruction was done via ion-based 
slicing of a Mo-40V-9Si-8B (FAST) sample using a DualBeam FEI Scios 
SEM and merging of the individual slices into a 3D microstructure using 
the software Avizo for FEI System – Material Science. For this purpose, 
the sample was first conductively heat-embedded. The sample was then 
ground manually on a water-cooled disk grinder (speed: 300 rpm) in 
several stages: 320, 600, 800, 1200 and 2500 grit SiC paper with a 
pressure of around 10 N for 30-45 s per step. The polishing was then 
carried out on a semi-automatic machine using diamond suspensions 
with 9 μ m (on hard synthetic disk) and 3 μ m (on soft woolen disk). The 
parameters for polishing were 20 N, 150 rpm and 5 min for 9 μ m and 3 
min for 3 μ m step. The final step was a finishing with 0.06 μ m alumina 
suspension on short flocked, soft synthetic disk with a pressure of 15 N 
and 150 rpm for 3 min ( + 30 s distilled water). The prepared sample was 
then analyzed in the SEM-FIB dual-beam electron microscope. The area 
for 3D tomography was determined using SE and BSE images. A 2 μ m 
thick and (10x10) μ m large platinum protective layer was deposited on 
the surface using FIB to protect the surface and for more even slicing of 
the specimen. In the next step, a trench was created in front of and to the 
side of the platinum rectangle using FIB at a high beam current (30 nA). 
A space of 10 μ m was created around the area of interest on the right and 
left and approx. 20 μ m at the front. Accordingly, there is sufficient space 
for repositioning during slicing and (in SE mode) a guaranteed 
measuring area of at least (10 x 10) μ m on the side to be sliced. The 
exposed sides were then leveled with a cleaning cross-section at a lower 
beam current (3 nA). The sample volume prepared this way ( Fig. 2 a) was 
then removed slice by slice using FIB. Slicing was carried out at a beam 
current of 3 nA with a slice thickness of 50 nm. An SEM SE image of the 
exposed surface was taken between each slice. A total of 196 slices were 
made in approx. 7 h until the entire sample volume had been removed. 
The images were then transferred to the Avizo for FEI System - Material 
Science software and the grey values were optimized and distinguished 
for better phase classification. The resulting 3D structure was generated 
from 156 images (1-4 not useable due to side effects, image 160-161 
“ jump ” -sudden change in microstructure). 
In contrast to the phase formation in mechanically alloyed and sin -
tered materials, ingot metallurgy forms phases during solidification out 
of the melt. So, another Mo-40V-9Si-8B alloy was processed via arc 
melting (Arc). Usually 2.5 g samples were processed to investigate the 
microstructures and chemical compositions but also to prepare spec -
imen with dimensions of (2x2x3.5) mm for creep tests by EDM. The Mo- 
V-Si-B alloy was fabricated via conventional arc-melting of metal sheets 
in argon atmosphere employing a Buehler AM0.5 arc-melter. The pure 
metal sheets with purities of 99.95 % for Mo, 99.9 % for V as well as Si 
and 99.5 % for B were cut into flakes and mixed to obtain the target alloy 
composition. After flipping and remelting five times the samples solidify 
typically drop-shaped. Afterwards, the ingots were homogenized in an 
argon atmosphere (tube furnace HTM Reetz GmbH Typ Losic) for 24 h at 
1400 
◦
C.

4. Summary and conclusions 
The creep performance for PM (Field Assisted Sintering and heat 
treated) and arc melted Mo-40V-9Si-8B alloys was studied at 1093 
◦
C. 
The creep response of the PM alloys was mainly governed by the Mo
ss 
phase, as derived from the resulting activation energies (430-460 kJ/ 
mol) and shown by KAM images of the crept microstructure. It could be 
shown that the arc-melted alloy of Mo-40V-9Si-8B provided a superior 
creep resistance due to its inverse microstructure having an intermetallic 
matrix instead of a solid solution matrix phase. The FAST alloy therefore 
suffered from predominant dislocation creep within the Mo
ss 
phase. 
However, in case of FAST + HT Mo-40V-9Si-8B, it was shown that a 
grain coarsening from 0.5 μ m to 3 μ m resulted in a decrease of creep 
rates by more than one order of magnitude. There might be further 
potential to improve the creep resistance by additional grain growth 
treatments yielding to grain size of around 7 μ m for Mo
ss 
phase ac -
cording to the findings in other PM Mo-Si-B alloys [ 18 ]. It could be 
shown that the creep performance of PM Mo-Si-B alloys, even those 
modified by the lightweight element vanadium, and therefore, reducing 
the density of the alloy down to values < 8 g/cm
3
, can be competitive 
against Nickel-based superalloy CMSX-4. It was concluded that the 
highly reduced density (~7.8 g/cm
3
), which is even smaller compared 
with CMSX-4 (~8.7 g/cm
3
) [ 27 ], represents a huge advantage of this 
alloy class. In terms of the focused applications e.g. in turbine engines, 
the reduction in weight would lead to a decrease in centrifugal forces, 
which finally extends the creep lifetime of the material.
