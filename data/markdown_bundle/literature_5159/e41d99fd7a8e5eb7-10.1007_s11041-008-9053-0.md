MECHANICAL PROPERTIES OF HIGH-TEMPERATURE NICKEL ALLOY
ZhS36VI FOR SINGLE-CRYSTAL HPT BLADES
V . P. Kuznetsov,1 V . P. Lesnikov,1 E. V . Moroz,2 and A. S. Koryakovtsev 3
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 5, pp. 23 – 26, May, 2008.
The mechanical properties of carbon-free single-crystal alloy ZhS36VI [001] are studied. The effect of test
temperature on alloy short-term mechanical properties after standard heat treatment, and also the effect of
/c103/c162-phase particle size after on stress-rupture strength at 975°C and mechanical properties at 20°C are deter -
mined. A Larsen – Miller parametric curve is plotted for alloy stress-rupture strength.
INTRODUCTION
An increase in the life and reliability of aviation gas tur-
bine engines (AGTE) is complicated in view of the continu-
ous increase in temperature, dynamic and vibration loads.
This gives rise to an increase in the requirement for working
turbine blades operating under conditions of multicomponent
loading in contact with a corrosive high-velocity gas stream.
The efficiency and the life of high-pressure turbine
(HPT) blades of contemporary and future AGTE may only
be provided as a result of using single-crystal nickel alloys.
The higher operating properties of single-crystal heat-resis -
tant nickel alloys is due not only to avoiding intergranular
boundaries in castings, including carbides and borides, but
also preparation of uniform distribution of /c103/c162-phase particles
in a matrix of /c103-solid solution. Thus single-crystal HPT
blades govern the appearance and parameters of contempo -
rary AGTE, and they are also a unique example of using sin-
gle-crystal metals having anisotropic mechanical properties
in contemporary engineering [1, 2]. Significant progress in
improving heat-resistant nickel alloys has made it possible to
realize in the first domestic carbon-free single-crystal alloy
ZhS36VI of a second generation of development of the
All-Russia Institute for Aircraft Materials (FGUP VIAM).
However, the volume of existing experimental data for
the mechanical properties and stress-rupture strength of alloy
ZhS36VI [001] is clearly insufficient in order to calculate
strength and endurance of planned HPT blades with a sin -
gle-crystal structure.
The aim of this work is to study the mechanical proper -
ties of single-crystal specimens of alloy ZhS36VI [001] in
tension in the temperature range 20 – 1100°C and the
stress-rupture strength at 975°C.
METHODS OF STUDY
Cast heat-resistant nickel alloy ZhS36VI, alloyed with
rhenium, is related to the class of carbon-free single-crystal
alloys. The alloy is intended for casting cooled turbine blades
with a crystallographic orientation [001] operating at 1100 –
1150°C.
Preparation of single-crystal specimens with crystallo -
graphic orientation [001] was accomplished under industrial
conditions in devices of the type UVNK-8P without a liquid
metal coolant at solidification rate of 3 mm /c47min [3]. The
crystallographic orientation was prescribed by seeds of
Ni – W-alloy. The permissible deviation of the growing
structure from crystallographic orientation [001] did not ex -
ceed 8°.
The chemical composition of alloy ZhS36VI and its heat
treatment regime have been provided in [1]. Heat treatment
of the alloy is: homogenizing soaking at 1310 /c17710°C, 4 h,
followed by quenching at a cooling rate not below 100 K/c47min,
two-stage ageing at 1030 /c17710°C and 870 /c17710°C. After per-
forming standard heat treatment for the alloy it is typically a
uniform dispersed (/c103+ /c103/c162)-structure with a high volume frac-
tion of strengthening /c103/c162-phase particles (75%) with a size of
0.3 – 0.4 /c109m. In interaxial areas the size of the /c103/c162-particles
reaches about 1 /c109m. With directional solidification under
conditions of a low gradient in the growth front in casting of
specimens there is formation of a structure with an inter -
dendritic distance /c108/c32/c61220 – 230 /c109m.

Tensile and stress-rupture strength tests were performed
on standard ten-fold cylindrical specimens with a gage
length diameter of 5 mm prepared by machining of cast and
heat-treated billets with a diameter if 14 mm. tensile tests
were carried out in an R-5 rupture machine, and stress-rup-
ture tests were carried out with uniaxial extension in
VPK-11 machine.
According the specifications in [4] short-term strength
properties in tension, i.e. average values of/c115r , /c1150.2 , /c100, and /c121,
were determined from the results of testing nine specimens at
each temperature, and endurance (average time to failure)
was determined for no fewer than fifteen specimens in each
test regime.
A study of the structure, phase and chemical composition
of the alloy was carried out using a Neophot-22 light micro -
scope with a computer attachment, a Superprobe YCXA-733
x-ray microanalyzer, a Philips SEM 535 scanning electron
microscope with a stand for an EDAX microanalyzer.
Electron microscope studies were performed by diffrac -
tion electron microscopy of thin foils in transmitted light us -
ing a ÉMV 100L electron microscope. The objects studied
were castings 13 mm in diameter and the vane of a turbine
blade prepared with a solidification rate of 3 mm/c47min.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The experimental results for evaluating the effect of test
temperature on mechanical properties of single-crystal alloy
after standard heat treatment are provided in Fig. 1. With an
increase in test temperature to 750°C there is reduction in
strength properties and an increase in ductility. In the range
750 – 900°C there are anomalous mechanical properties, in -
cluding the fact that the yield point and ultimate breaking
strength are not reduced, as normal, with an increase in tem -
perature they increase up to 900°C. This anomalous depen -
dence is governed by the dislocation mechanism of plastic
deformation of intermetallic Ni 3Al ( /c103/c162-phase), differing at
high temperature from the deformation mechanism of disor -
dered solid solutions based on nickel ( /c103-phase) [5]. The rea -
son of an anomalous change in /c115r (T ) and /c1150.2 (T ) is speci -
fied by presence in the structure of blocking super-disloca -
tions and a change in octahedral slip to cubic with transition
through the peak temperature. Further alloy loss of strength
with an increase in temperature is connected with dissolution
of /c103/c162-phase [6].
The structure of the alloy in a deformed zone of a speci -
men is shown in Fig. 2. Flat billets for thin foils were cut per-
pendicular to the longitudinal axis of specimens with a crys -
tallographic orientation of [001]. In alloy deformed at 20 and
700°C no fundamental differences were revealed for the
main (/c103+ /c103/c162)-structural component, for which the following
are typical:
– retention in a considerable part of the alloy volume
of a sufficiently regular cuboid form of /c103/c162-phase particles
(Fig. 2a );
– localization of deformation processes predominantly
in thin interlayers of /c103-phase, whereas the density of defects
in /c103/c162-particles is relatively low (Fig. 2b ).

The effect of /c103/c162-phase size on the properties of sin -

heat treatment, led to a marked increase in such properties as
ultimate breaking strength ( /c115r ) and nominal yield point
(/c1150.2 ), and ductility (/c100and /c121) decreased.
An increase in the amount of /c103/c162-phase and improvement
of its morphology promoted a marked increase in stress-rup -
ture strength for alloy ZhS36VI (Fig. 4). As the strengthen -
ing /c103/c162-phase particle size increases there is change in the
mechanism of reaction of dislocations with /c103/c162-phase, and
creep rate increases. The mechanism of strain accumulation
and long-term failure under creep conditions of single crys -
tals of heat-resistant nickel alloys has been studied in detail
in [7, 8]. According to [2, 5] under the action of high temper-
ature and stresses in single-crystal alloy there is consolida -
tion of /c103/c162-phase particles and formation of a raft-structure
(Fig. 5). With an increase in stress this structure is coarsened

During standard heat treatment in the structure of alloy
ZhS36VI there is dissolution of nonequilibrium eutectic
(/c103+ /c103/c162eut ) and formation of spherical micropores [5]. In the
interaxial areas of the alloy during stress-rupture testing
these micropores acquire the shape of an ellipse drawn out
along the stress axis. In these micropores there microcrack
generation (Fig. 6a and b ) that during creep grow perpendic-
ular to the applied stress. A reduction in stress-rupture
strength of specimens made of alloy ZhS36VI promotes oxi -
dation of their surface and formation in the surface changed
layer of microcracks that develop into the volume of the al -
loy (Fig. 6c).
A dependence has been obtained of stress-rupture strength
of Larsen – Miller parameter (Fig. 7) for alloy ZhS36VI
[001]. On the basis of this dependence an estimate is made of
the average values of stress-rupture strength for the alloy at
900, 1000, 1100, and 1200°C and life to 1000 h (see Ta -
ble 1). For comparison stress-rupture strength data are pro -
vided for heat-resistant rhenium-containing alloys ZhS32VI,
ZhS47VI, ZhS49VI CMSX-4 and CSMX-10, whose calcu -
lated data were taken [9]. In the level of stress-rupture
strength alloy ZhS36VI [001] markedly surpasses known
overseas alloy CSMX-4 of the second generation.
CONCLUSIONS
1. Carbon-free single-crystal alloy ZhS36VI of the sec -
ond generation, prepared by special technology under indus -
trial conditions, has high stress-rupture strength and short-
term properties in the temperature range 20 – 1100°C.
2. A reduction in strengthening /c103/c162-phase size promotes a
marked increase in stress-rupture strength and short-term
properties of alloy ZhS36VI.
3. Alloy ZhS36VI after heat treatment exhibits high
structural and phase stability and it is not inclined towards
forming TCP-phase during testing at high temperature and
stresses.
