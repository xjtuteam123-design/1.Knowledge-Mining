Effect of Rolling Temperature on the Microstructure
and Tensile Properties of 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V Alloy
Y.B. Tan, C. Tian, W.C. Liu, S. Xiang, F. Zhao, and Y.L. Liang
(Submitted June 6, 2017; in revised form September 24, 2017; published online March 7, 2018)
The 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V (wt.%) alloy was solution-treated at 850 and 780 /C176C for 30 min and then rolled at
different temperatures. The effect of rolling temperature on microstructure and tensile properties was
investigated. The results showed that the microstructure contained a large amount of a phase at a rolling
temperature of 600 /C176C. As the rolling temperature increased, the amount of a phase decreased, whereas the
amount of b phase increased. When the rolling temperature was higher than 700 /C176C, the alloy was com-
posed of a single b phase. The tensile properties of the alloy depended strongly on rolling temperature. The
yield strength and tensile strength decreased with increasing rolling temperature, whereas the elongation
increased. At a given rolling temperature, low solution treatment temperature resulted in higher strength
and lower elongation than high solution treatment temperature. The fracture mode changed from cleavage
fracture and quasi-cleavage fracture to dimple fracture with increasing rolling temperature.
Keywords 47-45Ti-5Al-3V alloy, deformation, mechanical prop-
erties, microstructure, rolling, tensile test
1. Introduction
Zirconium and its alloys have been gaining increased
attention in nuclear and chemical engineering applications for
many years because of low thermal neutron absorption cross
section, adequate strength and ductility, good corrosion resis-
tance, long-term dimensional stability in an irradiation envi-
ronment, excellent compatibility with the fuel and coolant,
good thermal conductivity and adequate resistance to fracture
(Ref 1-3). A series of Zr alloys, including the Zr-Sn (Ref 4), Zr-
Ti (Ref 5), Zr-2.5Nb (Ref 6), Zr-Nb (Ref 7), Zr-2.5Nb-0.5Cu
(Ref 8), Zr-Nb-Sn-Fe (Ref 9, 10) and Zr-Mo (Ref 11) alloys,
have been developed over the past 40 years. However, the
tensile strength of these alloys was lower than 1000 MPa at
room temperature, which was insufﬁcient to meet the increas-
ing requirements in aeronautical industries. To broaden the
application of Zr alloys in aeronautical industries, a series of
new ZrTiAlV alloys have been recently developed as advanced
structural materials (Ref 12-14). The microstructure of ZrTiAlV
alloys is composed of b phase above the b transus temperature,
while consists of a phase when furnace-cooling to room
temperature. Similar to Mg alloys, ZrTiAlV alloys with
hexagonal close packed crystal structure also reveals a poor
plasticity at room temperature owing to the limited number of
operative slip systems resulting from the hexagonal close
packed crystal structure (Ref 15-17). However, the pre-existing
a phase can increase the tensile strength and yield strength of
ZrTiAlV alloys.
The microstructure and mechanical properties of ZrTiAlV
alloys are strongly affected by compositional variations, heat
treatments and thermomechanical processing. Liang et al. (Ref
14) studied the effect of Al addition on the structure and
mechanical properties of ZrTiAlV alloys. They found that the
amount of a phase increased with increasing Al content from
2.2 to 6.9 wt.%. When the Al content was 5.6 wt.%, the
ZrTiAlV alloy had high strength and good ductility. Hsu et al.
(Ref 18) investigated the structure and mechanical properties of
as-cast Zr-Ti alloys. The phase structure changed gradually
from the a phase to the b phase with increasing the Ti content
from 10 to 40 wt.%. The highest hardness and bending strength
were obtained at a Ti content of 10 wt.%. Liang et al. (Ref 19)
reported the effect of annealing treatments on the microstruc-
ture and mechanical properties of the Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V alloy.
They found that the tensile strength and yield strength
decreased with increasing annealing temperature from 500 to
700 /C176 C. However, there are little studies on the effect of
thermomechanical processing parameters on the microstructure
and mechanical properties of ZrTiAlV alloys.
Both Zr and Ti elements belong to the same main group
(IVB) in the periodic table of elements. Zr and Ti have the same
crystal structure, namely, b phase above the phase transition
temperature and a phase below the phase transition tempera-
ture. The b phase is a solid solution with body-centered cubic
structure and the a phase is a solid solution with hexagonal
close packed crystal structure. Ti and Zr can form inﬁnite solid
solution for the 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V alloy. Thus, the mechanical
properties of 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V alloys may be similar to those
of Ti alloys. The effect of rolling parameters on the mechanical
properties of Ti alloys has been extensively investigated (Ref
20-23). Song et al. (Ref 20) studied the effect of hot rolling
condition on the anisotropy of mechanical properties in Ti-6Al-
4V alloy. They found that the anisotropy in tensile properties

2. Experimental
Sponge Zr (Zr + Hf ‡ 99.5 wt.%), sponge Ti (99.7 wt.%),
industrially pure Al (99.5 wt.%), and V (99.9 wt.%) were used
to prepare the 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V (wt.%) alloy. The alloy was
melted three times using a vacuum consumable electro-arc
furnace to ensure a uniform chemical composition. The cast
ingot was multiply forged to completely break the cast structure
after homogenized at 1050 /C176 C for 1.5 h. The alloy billet was
heated to 930 /C176 C and hot-forged into bars of 43 mm in
diameter.
The hot-forged bar was cut into sheets with a thickness of
2.2 mm along the axial direction. To generate different initial
grain sizes, the sheets were solution-treated at 850 and 780 /C176 C
for 30 min in a tubular vacuum heat-treatment furnace with a
protective argon atmosphere, respectively, followed by water
quenching. The solution-treated sheets were respectively pre-
heated at 600, 650, 700, 750 and 800 /C176 C for 30 min in an air
furnace, and then immediately rolled in one pass on a
laboratory rolling mill with rolls of 230 mm in diameter at a
rate of 0.24 m/s. The rolling reductions were in the range of
10.3-20.9%. Immediately after hot rolling, the samples were
placed in the asbestos and then cooled to room temperature.
In order to study the effect of rolling temperature on the
microstructure and tensile properties, dog-bone tensile speci-
mens with a gauge length of 20 mm and a width of 4 mm were
machined along the rolling direction. The thickness of tensile
samples was equal to that of the hot-rolled sheets. Tensile tests
were performed on an Instron mechanical test system with a
crosshead speed of 0.3 mm/min. The measurements were
repeated three times for each condition. Ultimate tensile
strength (UTS), yield strength at 0.2% offset strain (YS), and
elongation (EL) were obtained from the engineering stress–
strain curves, and the average values were presented. Fracto-
graphic analysis of tensile failures was carried out using
scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The specimens for
optical microscopy observation were mechanically polished
and chemically etched using a solution of 10% hydroﬂuoric
acid (HF), 45% nitric acid (HNO
3), and 45% distilled water
(H2O). All micrographs were taken from longitudinal cross
sections as deﬁned by the rolling direction (RD) and the normal
direction (ND). X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was carried
out to investigate the phase constitution of the hot-rolled
sheets. XRD analysis was conducted using a CuK
a radiation
source with an accelerating voltage of 40 kV and a current of
200 mA.
3. Results
3.1 Phase Composition and Microstructure of Initial
Specimens
The XRD patterns of the 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V alloy solution-
treated at 780 and 850 /C176 C for 30 min are shown in Fig. 1. The
XRD patterns show clear peaks at 2 h = 37.7 /C176 , 54.4/C176 and 68.2/C176 ,
respectively. The peaks at 37.7 /C176 , 54.4/C176 and 68.2/C176 correspond to
the (110), (200) and (211) peaks of the b phase, respectively. It
was suggested that the alloy was composed completely of a
single b phase after solution treatment. For Zr alloys or Ti
alloys, generally, a¢ phase, a¢¢ phase, x phase may be formed in
order as the b phase stability increases after quenching from a
two-phase or single b phase region. The addition of V and Zr
elements into Ti alloy signiﬁcantly increased the b phase
stability of the Ti alloy (Ref 24). For instance, only the a¢ phase
was detected in the Ti-6Al-4V alloy, while the a¢¢ phase was
observed in the 30Zr-62Ti-5Al-3V alloy after quenching from
the a + b two-phase or b phase region (Ref 13). In the present
work, only a single b phase microstructure was obtained after
solution treatment. This indicated that the b phase of the 47Zr-
45Ti-5Al-3V alloy was more stable than that of Ti-6Al-4V and
30Zr-62Ti-5Al-3V alloys. Figure 2 shows the microstructure of
the 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V alloy solution-treated at 780 and 850 /C176 C.
An equiaxed recrystallized grain structure was observed after
heat treatment. The grain size for the alloy solution-treated at
780 and 850 /C176 C were measured using the Image-Pro Plus 6.0
software to be about 101 and 224 lm, respectively.
3.2 Phase Composition and Microstructure After Hot Rolling
Figure 3 shows the XRD patterns of the 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V
alloy solution-treated at 780 and 850 /C176 C and then hot-rolled at
different temperatures. At a rolling temperature of 600 /C176 C, the
diffraction peaks of a and b phase were observed, indicating
that the alloy consisted of the b and a duplex phase. The
amount of a phase was calculated from the XRD patterns to be
about 53.7 and 47.5% for 780 and 850 /C176 C solution-treated
samples, respectively. With increasing rolling temperature, the
amount of a phase gradually decreased. However, it was noted
that the amount of a phase for 780 /C176 C solution-treated samples
was more than that for 850 /C176 C solution-treated samples at a
given rolling temperature. When the rolling temperature was
higher than 700 /C176 C, the alloy consisted of a single b phase for
780 and 850 /C176 C solution-treated samples.
Figure 4 shows the optical micrographs of the 47Zr-45Ti-
5Al-3V alloy solution-treated at 780 and 850 /C176 C and then hot-
rolled at various temperatures. The grains were slightly
elongated along the rolling direction after rolling. At a rolling
temperature of 600 /C176 C, a large amount of a phase were
observed in the interior of the b grains for the specimens
solution-treated at 780 and 850 /C176 C, as shown in Fig. 4(a) and
(b). However, the size of a phase was different for the
specimens solution-treated at 780 and 850 /C176 C. The size of a
phase for 850 /C176 C solution-treated samples was larger than that
for 780 /C176 C solution-treated samples. With increasing rolling
temperature, the amount of a phase gradually decreased
(Fig. 4c and d). This may be attributed to a decrease in the
drive force of b ﬁ a transformation with increasing rolling
temperature, which restrained the transformation of b ﬁ a

3.3 Tensile Properties
Figure 5 shows the engineering stress-engineering strain
curves of the 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V alloy with different solution-
treated temperature under different conditions. It is seen that the
stress–strain curves of 780 and 850 /C176 C solution-treated samples
exhibited a double yielding phenomenon, and low elastic
modulus, low yield strength, high ultimate tensile strength and
good ductility were obtained. A similar phenomenon also was
observed in metastable b titanium alloys (Ref 25-28). This may
be associated with the formation of the stress induced
martensite a¢¢ phase during tensile test. Duerig et al. (Ref 26)
thought that the triggering of stress induced martensite was
generally associated with a typical double yielding or strain
plateau region in the engineering stress–strain curve. The
existent of the a¢¢ phase resulted in a low elastic modulus (Ref
29), and a low yield strength and higher elongation (Ref 30).
For hot-rolled samples, the engineering stress was linear
increase with engineering strain at a lower strain, exhibiting a
linear elastic behavior. With further increase in engineering
strain, the engineering stress gradually inclined to a constant
value. The double yielding phenomenon was not observed,
indicating that the stress induced martensite transformation did
not occur during tensile test.
Table 1 lists the tensile properties of the 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V
alloy with different solution-treated temperature, and the
relationship between tensile properties and rolling temperature
is shown in Fig. 6. At a rolling temperature of 600 /C176 C, the yield
strength, tensile strength and elongation were 1100, 1203 MPa
and 3.1% for the samples solution-treated at 850 /C176 C, respec-
tively. With increasing rolling temperature from 600 to 700 /C176 C,
the yield strength and tensile strength sharply decreased,
whereas the elongation evidently increased. When the rolling
temperature was higher than 700 /C176 C, the drop trend of the yield
strength and tensile strength gradually ﬂattened out, while the
elongation still increased signiﬁcantly (Fig. 6a). At a rolling
temperature of 800 /C176 C, the yield strength and tensile strength
slightly decreased to 818 MPa and 903 MPa, respectively,
whereas the elongation signiﬁcantly increased to a high value
of 16.3%. For the samples solution-treated at 780 /C176 C, the yield
strength, tensile strength and elongation were 1134, 1206 MPa
and 2.6% at a rolling temperature of 600 /C176 C, respectively. With
increasing rolling temperature, the yield strength and tensile
strength sharply decreased, whereas the elongation signiﬁcantly
increased (Fig. 6b). When the rolling temperature was 750 /C176 C,
the yield strength and tensile strength decreased to 866 and
940 MPa, respectively, and the elongation increased to 10.1%.
At a given rolling temperature, the yield strength and tensile
strength for the samples solution-treated at 780 /C176 C were higher
than those for the samples solution-treated at 850 /C176 C, while the
elongation for the samples solution-treated at 780 /C176 C was lower
than that for the samples solution-treated at 850 /C176 C.

4. Discussion
The results indicated that the strength and ductility of the
alloy were signiﬁcantly affected by rolling temperature. The
high strength and low elongation were obtained at a rolling
temperature of 600 /C176 C for the samples solution-treated at 780
and 850 /C176 C. The high yield strength and tensile strength were
associated with the strengthening effect of a phase (Ref 31-33).
Lu¨ tjering (Ref 34) and Sen et al. (Ref 35) pointed out that the
strength and ductility were mainly controlled by the size of b
phase and a phase as well as the volume fraction of a phase in
the titanium alloys. The a phase was the most important factor
inﬂuencing the properties of b titanium alloys. The distribution
of ﬁne a phase in the b matrix can cause a microstructure
condition similar to a dispersion strengthened system in aged
Ti-10V-2Fe-3Al alloy (Ref 36). It is seen from Fig. 3(a) and (b)
that a large amount of a phase precipitated when the alloy was
hot-rolled at 600 /C176 C. The amount of a phase was estimated
from the XRD pattern to be about 53.7% for samples solution-
treated at 780 /C176 C and 47.5% for samples solution-treated at
850 /C176 C, respectively. The high-volume fraction of the sec-
ondary a phase in the b matrix will provide more a/b
interfaces, which could act as effective dislocation barriers (Ref
37). Froes and Bomberger (Ref 38) reported that the strength of
titanium alloy was inversely proportional to the spacing of a
phase in the b matrix, which could be expressed as
YS ¼ 850 þ 70d/C0 1, where YS is the yield strength of the
alloy, and d is the spacing of a phase. According to Ref. 37, the
spacing of a phase ( d) decreased with increasing the volume
fraction of a phase. Thus, the high strength and low elongation
were obtained at a rolling temperature of 600 /C176 C. With

5. Conclusions
The effect of rolling temperature on the microstructure and
tensile properties of 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V alloy with different
solution-treated temperature was investigated. The results
obtained can be summarized as follows:
(1) The microstructure contained a large amount of a phase
at a rolling temperature of 600 /C176 C. As the rolling tem-
perature increased, the amount of a phase decreased,
whereas the amount of b phase increased. When the
rolling temperature was higher than 700 /C176 C, the alloy
was mainly composed of a single b phase.
(2) With increasing rolling temperature from 600 to 700 /C176 C,
the yield strength and tensile strength sharply decreased,
whereas the elongation evidently increased. When the
rolling temperature increased to 750 /C176 C, the drop trend of
the yield strength and tensile strength gradually ﬂattened
out, while the elongation still increased signiﬁcantly.
(3) At a given rolling temperature, low-temperature solution
treatment resulted in higher strength and lower elonga-
tion than high-temperature solution treatment.
(4) The fracture mode changed from cleavage fracture and
quasi-cleavage fracture to dimple fracture with increas-
ing rolling temperature.
