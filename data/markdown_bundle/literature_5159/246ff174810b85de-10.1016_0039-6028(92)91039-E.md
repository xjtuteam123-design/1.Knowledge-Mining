The influence of initial microstructure and Mo composition on the high-temperature creep behaviour of two crept model Ni-x 
at.%Mo-2.0 at.%Ta-13.7 at.%Al(x = 8.7 and 9.2 at.%) superalloys has been investigated by the techniques of transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM) and atom probe field ion microscopy (APFIM). Acicular ~ phase precipitates of approximate 
stoichiometry NiMo have been observed by TEM and APFIM in both alloys. However, a marked difference in y' morphology is 
observed between the alloys due to different initial microstructures. Small variations in the initial morphologies of the y' 
microstructure rather than Mo composition play a significant role in affecting creep strength in this alloy system. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based superalloys have been intensively 
studied in the past because of their attractive 
mechanical properties in high-temperature hos- 
tile environments. The applications of these al- 
loys include components such as turbine blades of 
advanced aircraft engines where a high creep 
strength at elevated temperatures is an importa.: 
requirement. Of lhe many superalloys investi- 
gated, nickel-based single crystal alloys contain- 
ing refractory elements such as Mo, Ta and Re 
have shown considerable promise [1,2]. The im- 
portant microstructural feature of these alloy sys- 
tems is the presence of ordered phases such as 
the L12-ordered 7' (Ni3AI) phase and the DO22- 
ordered 7" (Ni3Mo) phase that strengthen the 
material [1]. In the uncrept state, the microstruc- 
ture generally consists of 7' cuboids dispersed in 
a 7 matrix. In certain alloys, precipitation of a 7" 
(DO22) phase also occurs. This phase precipitates 
in threc crystallographic variants, corresponding 
to the closest lattice match on the faces of the 7' 
cuboids. This results in a morphology in which 
the 7' particles are surrounded by thin platelets 
of the DO22-ordered phase [1,3,4]. During high- 
temperature creep, with the stress applied along 
the (100) direction, the Y' cuboids directionally 
coarsen to form continuous lamellae or rafts 
which are perpendicular to the applied-stress axis 
[1,2]. This change in the Y' morphology has im- 
portant implications for creep behaviour. 
In polycrystalline alloys, coarsening of the y' 
precipitates leads to a reduction in creep life 
since deformation occurs by mobile dislocations 
bowing around the Y' precipitates [5]. I-iowever, 
in directionally coarsened single crystal alloys, the 
y' lamellae present an obstacle to disk)c:'; .... 
climb and creep deformation can occur only by 
the dislocations shearing the y' lamellae. In the 
Ni-Mo-Ta-AI system of alloys, the shearing ac- 
tion of the disl3cations is impeded by the dense 
network of misfit dislocations formed at the Y-7' 
interface due to the large negative lattice mis- 
match. This mechanism has been suggested to 
explain the dramatic improvement in creLp life at 
elevated temperatures in these alloys [1]. A num- 
ber of different factors which have been sug- 
gested for their possible influence on creep be- 
haviour are: (1) the volume fraction of 7', (2) the 
initial microstructure in terms of the 7' cuboid 
size and orientation, (3) the coherency strains due 
to lattice mismatch of the y-y' interface, (4) the 
composition of the 7 and Y' phases, (5) segrega- 
tion of molybdenum (Mo) at the 7-7' interface,

There is a considerable difference between thc

reveals evidence of coarscning at a rapid rate in 
addition to the formation of imperfect or incom-

behaviour on the degree of perfection of the y'

degree of perfection of the y Pameilae is thought 
to be a strong function of thc initial cuboid size 
and the alignment of the cuboids with respect to 
one another. Perfect lamellae in this alloy system 
have been reported to form from a fine disper- 
sion of cuboids having a mean size of about 0.15 
~na [10]. A higher density of cuboids results in a 
lower interparticle distance which facilitates dif- 
fusion during directional coarsening under creep. 
Aging of the material prior to creep has been 
shown to result in reduced creep strength be- 
cause of coarsening of the I" [9,11]. The reduc- 
tion occurs because the coarsened y' precipitates 
have a large interparticle distance, are misaligned 
with respect to one another and link up to form 
irregularly shaped lamellae. 
An explanation for the decrease in creep 
strength observed in the 9.2% Mo alloy may be 
based on the arguments presented i,, the previous 
paragraph. The irregularly shaped y' lamellae in 
this alloy make it easier for mobile dislocations to 
bow around them, whereas the long, thin y' 
lamellae in the 8.7% Mo alloy allow dislocation 
motion only by the more difficult shearing mech- 
anism. Long, thin lamellae also have a larger 
Y/3" interface area per unit volume of the mate- 
rial whi,-h, in turn, gives rise to a higher density 
t,f misfit dislocations. Mobile dislocations would, 
therefore, be more frequently pinned at the inter- 
face leading to a longer creep life in the 8.7% Mo 
~,qley. Although thc difference in creep behaviour 
between the two alloys can be explained on the 
basis of the experimentally observed y' morphol- 
ogy, the underlying mechanism tor the differ- 
ences in coarsening behaviour are less straight- 
forward. 
The atom probe composition determinations 
show a higher Mo content in the 3' matrix in the 
9.2% Mo alloy, whereas the y' composition re- 
mains practically unchanged in the two alloys. In 
addition, some of the DO22 phase dissolves dur- 
ing creep test; at 982 °C and produces an even 
higher Mo concentration in the y matrix. A high 
Mo concentratioh in the matrix would produce a 
larger lattice parameter for the 3/ matrix, which 
would lead to a larger lattice mismatch at the 
y/y' interface. Large negative lattice mismatch 
values (-0.8% in the 9.2% Mo alloy) arc be- 
lieved to provice a strong driving force for direc- 
tional coarsening of the 3" cuboids in this system

5. Conclusions 
This combined atom probe and TEM charac- 
terization revealed that the y' merphology in the 
9.2% alloy plays a more significant role in the 
creep behaviour than Mo compositional varia- 
tions. An acicular tS (NiMo) phase was detected

strength is believed to be of second order. The 
differences between the y' morphologies arose
