ARTICLE
Effect of Processing Parameters on Microstructure 
and Mechanical Properties in High Temperature 
Deformation of Ti-6Al-4V Alloy 
Yu Weixin, Li Miaoquan, Luo Jiao 
Northwestern Polytechnical University, Xi’an 710072, China 
 
 
Abstract: The effect of the deformation temperature, deformation degree, strain rate and their interaction on the microstructure 
and mechanical properties in the high temperature deformation of Ti-6Al-4V alloy was investigated. The experimental results sho w 
that the deformation degree and strain rate have significant influence on the microstructure and mechanical properties, in which finer 
microstructure and higher tensile strength can be obtained at a middle deformation degree and a higher strain rate. The interac tions 
between the deformation temperature and the deformation degree or the strain rate in the high temperature deformation of Ti-6Al-4V 
alloy are significant, especially being deformed at the temperature just under the ȕ transus, the ductility and tensile strength are 
sensitive to all of processing parameters including their interactions. So the interactions should be considered to optimize th e 
processing parameters in order to meet the requirements of the mechanical properties. 
 
Key Words: Ti-6Al-4V alloy; processing parameters; microstructure; mechanical properties 
 
 
 
 
Microstructures of titanium alloys are very sensitive to 
processing parameters, and the desired microstructure can be 
obtained at the proper processing parameters to obtain good 
mechanical properties such as strength, ductility, toughness, 
fatigue resistance and so on
[1í8]. A large number of studies on 
deformation mechanism of titanium alloys are available in the 
literature. Mythili et al [9] studied the deformation mechanism 
of Ti-4.4Ta-1.9Nb alloy by means of TEM and found that the 
Į phase deformed predominantly by slip although twinning 
was also observed occasionally and the ȕ particles do not act 
as effective barriers for the dislocation movement. Glavicic et 
al
[10] reported that prism slip is considerably easier to activate 
in the Į phase than basal slip during hot working of Ti-6Al-4V 
alloy. Ma et al [11] studied microstructure evolution of Ti-1100 
alloy during thermomechanical processing and found that the 
ȕ phase prefers to recrystallize at the junction of the boundary 
of the ȕ grains during deformation in the ȕ phase field. Li et 
al
[12] studied processing parameters of the microstructure of 
Ti-15-3 alloy and found that the grain size of the primary Į 
phase increases with raising of deformation temperature but 
decrease with increasing of deformation degree and strain rate. 
Sha et al [13] studied the effect of deformation temperature on 
mechanical properties of TC18 alloy and found that ultimate 
strength remains at about 1200 MPa with increasing of defor-
mation temperature, and impact and fracture toughness are 
sensitive to the deformation temperature. 
However, there are few studies on the interaction between 
the processing parameters by now. The hot deformation of ti-
tanium alloys is a mutual coupling process of deformation 
behaviour, heat transfer and microstructure evolution
[14í17]. 
Hence, deformation temperature, deformation degree, strain 
rate and their interactions should be treated as a function of 
processing parameter.

1 Experimental 
The as received Ti-6Al-4V alloy was a hot-rolled bar stock 
with 90 mm in diameter. The heat treatment procedure prior to 
isothermal compression was as follows: heated to 750 °C and 
held for 1.5 h, cooled in air to room temperature. The micro-
structure of Ti-6Al-4V alloy after the heat treatment is shown 
in Fig.1. Its chemical composition (in weight percent) was 6.5 
Al, 4.25 V, 0.04 Fe, 0.02 C, 0.16 O, 0.015 N, 0.0018 H and 
balance Ti. The ȕ transus for this material was approximately 
995 °C. 
The test parameters consisted of deformation temperatures 
of 850, 900, 950 and 980 °C, deformation degree of 35%, 
50% and 65%, and strain rates of 1.73×10
-3 and 1.73×10 -1 s-1. 
The dimensions of the specimens with 68 mm×12 mm×18 
mm, 68 mm×12 mm×24 mm and 68 mm×12 mm×32 mm 
corresponded to the deformation degree of 35%, 50% and 
65%, respectively. The compression tests were carried out at a 
2500 t isothermal hydrostatic machine. The specimens were 
coated with a borosilicate glass paste for lubrication and envi-
ronmental protection. 
After isothermal compression, the specimens were 
air-cooled rapidly to room temperature, sectioned parallel to 
the compression axis from one side of the deformed speci-
mens and the cut surface of the smaller part was prepared for 
metallographic examination using standard procedures. 
The bigger part of the deformed specimens was annealed at 
720 °C for 60 min and air-cooled. Tensile samples had 5 mm 
in diameter and 25 mm in gauge length. Tensile tests were 
carried out using a ZWICK/Z150 test machine at room tem-
perature and at a constant cross-head speed of 2 mm/min. Ul-

2.1.2 Deformation degree 
At a deformation temperature of 980 °C and strain rate of 
1.73×10-1 s-1, microstructure variation with deformation degree 
is shown in Fig.3. 
It can be seen from Fig.3 that the primary Į grains of the 
Ti-6Al-4V alloy deformed at the deformation degree of 35% 
exhibit flat morphology vertically to compression axis. At the 
deformation degrees of 50% and 65%, the primary Į grains 
exist in two morphologies of equiaxed and platelet, which 
means the partly dynamic recrystallization occurred in pri-
mary Į grains. The high temperature compression deformation 
mechanism of titanium alloy consists of dislocation sliding, 
dislocation climbing, and some twining in Į phase, so disloca-
tion density and distortion energy increase sharply with in-
creasing of deformation degree [20í22]. In the deformation proc-
ess, the primary Į grains were flattened, distorted and bro-
ken-up with increasing of deformation degree and the part 
primary Į phase recrystallized dynamically at the same time. 
At the deformation degree of 65%, some primary Į grains are 
very large resulting from the difference of distortion energy 
between the primary Į grains. The distortion energy of pri-
mary Į grains is accumulated being induced by the plastic de-
formation and decreases subsequently to as low as being re-
cystallized. Two primary Į grains with higher and lower dis-
tortion energy are easy to merge when they are adjacent and 
the kinetics is primarily the difference of their distortion en-
ergy and partly be attributed to the decreasing of total interfa-
cial energy. 
The morphology and distribution of secondary Į phase ex-
hibit significant changes with increasing of deformation de-
gree from 35% to 50%. At the deformation of 35%, the sec-
ondary Į phase exhibits a thin lamellar colony structure, but at 
the deformation degrees of 50% and 65%, the secondary Į 
phase shows as little grains or short platelets and distributes 
disorderly, which is also caused by the difference of the dislo-
cation density in the ȕ phase just after deformation. 
2.1.3 Strain rate 
At a deformation temperature of 980 °C and deformation 
degree of 65%, microstructure variation with strain rate is 
shown in Fig.4. It can be seen from Fig.4 that the strain rate 
has no evident influence on the volume fraction but affects the 
grain size and morphology of the primary Į phase significantly. 
At higher strain rates, one part of primary Į grains shows as 
equiaxed resulted from dynamic recrystallization, and the oth-
ers show as the plates having a typical recovery structure. Dy-
namic recrystallization and dynamic recovery are two contra-
dictory processes but exist simultaneously at most conditions. 
In general, it is difficult to occur for dynamic recovery and dy-
namic recrystallization simultaneously in the plastic deforma-
tion processes because the distortion energy releases through 
dynamic recovery and is accumulated difficultly to the level for 
dynamic recrystallization. Dislocation density, distortion en-
ergy and recrystallization locations increase with increasing of 
strain rate and so dynamic recrystallization is easy to occur at 
higher strain rates
[23,24]. On the other hand, deformation time 
decreases sharply with increasing of strain rate, and a great 
amount of primary Į grains does not conduct dynamic recrys-
tallization because of no enough time at higher strain rates. At 
lower strain rates, most of primary Į grains show as equiaxed

sponds to a smaller desired deformation degree at the defor-

The effect of the interaction of processing parameters on 
mechanical properties of the Ti-6Al-4V alloy can be explained 
from microstructure evolution. At the deformation temperature 
just under the ȕ transus, thermally activated process is greatly 
enhanced and adiabatic temperature rise has a great effect on 
the primary Į volume fraction. The deformation at a higher 
deformation degree and strain rate would cause significantly 
adiabatic temperature rise, which in turns cause the volume 
fraction and grain size of the primary Į phase to decrease 
sharply, so the ductility and tensile strength are at higher lev-
els. The deformation at a smaller deformation degree and a 
lower strain rate can prolong the deformation time, and part of 
the primary Į grains has enough time to refine through dy-
namic recrystallization, so a lower strain rate is needed when 
the desired deformation degree is smaller at the deformation 
temperatures just under the ȕ transus. 
3 Conclusions 
1) In the Į+ȕ two-phase area, the primary Į volume fraction 
decreases with increasing of deformation temperature. At the 
deformation temperature just under the ȕ transus, the defor-
mation temperature has a significant effect on the morphology 
of primary Į phase and secondary Į phase. The morphology 
and distribution of primary Į and secondary Į phase exhibit 
significant changes with deformation degree, and the defor-
mation degree of 50% can lead to a finer microstructure. The 
strain rate affects the morphology and grain size of primary Į 
phase significantly, and higher strain rates would result in a 
finer microstructure. 
2) The strain rate and deformation degree have significant 
influence on tensile strength in the deformation temperature 
range from 850 to 950 °C. Tensile strength increases with in-
creasing of strain rate, and increases first but then decreases 
with a peak value at 50% with increasing of deformation de-
gree. 
3) At the deformation temperature just under the ȕ transus, 
the interaction among the processing parameters is very sig-
nificant, and a higher strain rate is needed when the desired 
deformation degree is larger and lower strain rate is needed 
when the desired deformation degree is smaller.
