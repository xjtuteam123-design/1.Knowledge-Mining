ABSTRACT 
In this study, pure niobium (Nb) was additive manufactured by laser powder bed fusion (LPBF). The effect of 
energy density and oxygen content on the densification behavior, microstructure and mechanical properties of 
the LPBF-built Nb were investigated. It was demonstrated that the density steadily increased and the pores were 
gradually eliminated with the increase of energy density. A maximum density of 99.7% was achieved at the 
energy density of 146 J/mm
3
. Further increase of energy density led to an intense Marangoni convection and a 
slightly decrease of density. The average grain size of the LPBF-built Nb was 70 μ m approximately. A large 
fraction of low-angle grain boundaries and a high density of dislocations were observed in the LPBF-built Nb. The 
ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of the LPBF-built Nb increased steadily with the increase of energy density due to 
the increment of density. The fine grains and high-density dislocations resulted in high strength of the LPBF-built 
Nb. The maximum yield strength and UTS were as high as 550 MPa and 630 MPa respectively with a consid -
erable elongation of 26%. Oxygen deteriorated the mechanical properties of the LPBF-built Nb significantly. The 
increase of oxygen enhanced the tensile strength of Nb and reduced its plasticity significantly. The LPBF-built Nb 
became brittle when the oxygen content increased from 0.05 wt % to 0.23 wt %. The elongation to fracture of the 
LPBF-built Nb decreased to less than 2% regardless of the input energy density. It was proposed that the 
interstitial oxygen atoms exert a repulsive force on the dislocation and block its movement, leading to a sharp 
decrease in the plasticity of LPBF-built Nb. 
1. Introduction 
Niobium (Nb) is a promising material and has gained increasing 
attention in high temperature and biomedicine field owning to the high 
melting point (2468 
◦
C), high corrosion resistance, and outstanding 
biocompatibility [ 1 – 4 ]. The surface of Nb is generally oxidized to 
Nb
2
O
5
, which exists stably towards corrosive solution such as sodium 
hydroxide, dilute sulfuric acid, hydrochloric acid, or nitric acid under 
the oxygenated environment even at elevated temperature, giving the 
material extraordinary corrosion resistance [ 5 – 8 ]. Although the re -
searches of Nb start late, relatively backward compared with conven -
tional medical metals such as tantalum, titanium and stainless steel 
[ 9 – 11 ], considerable clinical studies in the past three decades have 
proven that Nb not only possesses good biocompatibility, low cytotox -
icity, but also outstanding osteogenic properties by increasing the ac -
tivity of alkaline phosphatase to promote calcification [ 12 ]. 
Nevertheless, as a body-centred-cubic (BCC) refractory metal, Nb is 
extremely sensitive to oxygen and its mechanical properties are affected 
greatly with a small amount of oxygen [ 13 – 16 ]. The expensive raw 
materials, high melting point and affinity to oxygen are posing a severe 
limitation on the application of pure Nb. And orthopedic implants 
require extremely high structural freedom and need to be customized for 
the individual, which is a great challenge for conventional methods. 
Fortunately, with the rapid development of additive manufacturing 
(AM) technology, which enabled huge breakthrough in the fabrication 
of clinical implants with complex structure matching human body, Nb 
appeals to scholars again. In particular, based on the three-dimensional 
mathematical model of parts, it is easy to tune the geometry, pore size 
and architectural features by AM process, realizing personalized treat -
ment and overcoming some potential defects of traditional processing, 
such as inhomogeneous pore size and mold limitation [ 17 – 20 ]. 
Using high-power laser beam under the control of a computer, laser 
powder bed fusion (LPBF), a type of AM technology, has developed 
rapidly in recent years, offers an almost unlimited flexibility and satisfies

The laser beam melted Nb powder and formed a deep hole in the 
center of the molten pool during the process of scanning. The high 
temperature within the molten pool increased the pressure around the 
molten pool, and the residual gas around the deep hole drove the molten 
liquid to fly out and form the splash droplets ( “ balling effect ” ). When the 
splashed droplets fell on the cold powder, a large cluster would be 
formed, and the accumulation of the cold powder to the splashed 
droplets leaded to a local lack of powder resulting in pores. Additionally, 
the droplets flying out of the keyhole also caused the liquid phase unable 
to fill the keyhole in time, leaving larger pores. 
The relation between the relative density and energy density of LPBF

The adhesion of the molten particles to the cold powder was more 
powerful than the friction between the cold powder particles, so the cold 
powder particles would be absorbed into the edge of the molten pool by 
the molten liquid, resulting in the absence of powder at the edge of the 
molten pool to form pores.

blocked by oxygen during the migration process, resulting in the 
enrichment of dislocations around oxygen atoms. According to first- 
principles calculation, the interaction between vacancy and interstitial 
oxygen was strongest among vacancy, interstitial oxygen and screw 
dislocation core [ 15 , 30 ] which mean that the combination of oxygen 
and vacancy was more likely to occur in the Nb matrix. There was an 
attraction between the vacancy and the screw dislocation core, which 
could make the dislocation migration easier, but decreased rapidly with 
the increase of distance. In this case the combination of oxygen and 
vacancy increased the distance between vacancy and screw dislocation 
nucleus, which weakened the attraction between vacancy and screw 
dislocation core. In addition, oxygen also had a repulsive interaction 
with the screw dislocation core. In consequence, the energy barrier 
required for migration of dislocations in Nb – O (0.23 wt %) was much 
larger than that in pure Nb, making migration of dislocations in Nb – O 
difficult. And this result endow Nb – O with significant brittleness during 
tensile testing. 
As shown in Table 2 , the optimum yield strength and UTS of Nb in 
this study were much higher than that of other methods such as straight 
extrusion, casting and cold drawing though the plasticity of LPBF-built 
Nb was reduced. The yield strength and ultimate strength of ordinary 
cast Nb with oxygen content of 50 ppm were 150 MPa and 200 MPa 
respectively, with an average grain size of 100 μ m. After double equal 
channel angular extrusion (D-ECAE), the average grain size of Nb with 
an oxygen content of 100 ppm declined to 5 μ m, and the yield strength 
and UTS only reached 157 MPa and 346 MPa separately, which were 
much lower than the yield strength of 553 MPa and the ultimate strength 
of 639 MPa that LPBF-built Nb had. The high heating/cooling rate of 
LPBF was characterized by the refinement of grains and the high-density 
dislocations. Therefore, it could be speculated that the high strength of 
LPBF-built Nb was due to fine-grain strengthening and dislocation 
strengthening.
