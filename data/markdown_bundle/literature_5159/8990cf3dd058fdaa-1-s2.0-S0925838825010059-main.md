ABSTRACT
FeCoNiCr high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have excellent plasticity and good processing performance, making them a 
potential candidate material for manufacturing high-performance parts. However, these HEAs are often prepared 
by casting, resulting in a coarse-grained (CG) microstructure and low yield strength. The synthesis of FeCoNiCr 
HEA through mechanical alloying (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS) results in finer grain size, leading to 
high yield strength but low fracture strain. Therefore, the FeCoNiCr HEAs have a strength-strain trade-off 
dilemma. This work utilizes a consolidation technique that uses high-pressure & high-temperature (HPHT) to 
produce an ultrafine-grained (UFG) FeCrCoNi HEA. The yield strength of UFG FeCoNiCr HEA is 1526 MPa, and 
its fracture strain is 35 %, which is greater than the yield strength of CG FeCoNiCr HEA (162 MPa), achieving an 
ideal conjunction of yield strength and fracture strain. The observed high yield strength is primarily credited to 
the Hall-Petch reinforcement of the UFG matrix caused by the implementation of high consolidation pressure 
(4 GPa). The high consolidation pressure has two effects: 1) reducing interparticle distances due to the enforced 
plastic deformation of individual granules, making for high bulk density, and 2) reducing the diffusivity, thereby 
reducing the growth speed of grains. The high strain is primarily the result of a reduction in the diameter and 
content of Cr-Mn-O-rich oxides - which reduce the interface area between the soft matrix and hard oxides, 
thereby suppressing the sources of crack generation - due to the utilization of high pressure.

comprising a powder are subjected to modifications, which may include 
the sliding of grains and turning, or the processes of decimation and

4.3. Origin of high strain
The microstructure of the UFG FeCoNiCr HEA after plastic defor -
mation to fracture is examined. Many stacking faults are seen in the 
deformed UFG FeCoNiCr HEA, as displayed in Fig. 6 (a) and (c). In 
comparison, no stacking faults are observed in the as-sintered UFG 
FeCoNiCr HEA, as displayed in Fig. 4 (a). The presence of many stacking 
faults recommends that the stacking fault energy of UFG FeCoNiCr HEA 
is moderately lower, making it susceptible to deformation twins ( Fig. 6
(b) and (d)) during the deformation process. The development of 
deformation twins can expand the strain [39] . It is notable that 
MA-SPSed-FeCoNiCr HEAs exhibiting a grain size of > 0.8 μ m display a 
larger strain, in excess of 50 % [14] , as a consequence of the nucleation 
stress of twin increases with the decrease of grain size [40] . However, 
the large grain size also results in low yield strength of only 657 MPa.

(particle size of 0.5 – 15 μ m) have been seen in high-carbon/high- 
chromium steels and HEAs [41 – 43] . Interfaces between hard oxides 
and soft matrix phases are known sources of crack initiation in materials 
[32] . The oxide particles with a larger diameter and a higher content

5. Conclusion
UFG FeCoNiCr HEA was prepared by MA and HPHT techniques. 
Applying a substantial consolidation pressure (4 GPa) leads to the UFG 
FeCoNiCr HEA achieving a relative density near 99 %. The consolidated 
UFG FeCoNiCr HEA exhibits an average grain diameter of approx. 
181 nm and displays exceptionally high yield strength of approx. 
1526 MPa - significantly higher than the yield strength (162 MPa) of CG 
FeCoNiCr HEA - and a high fracture of 35 %.
The high yield strength is predominantly brought about by the UFG 
matrix. Our calculations suggest that the fine-grained strengthening 
contributes 54 % to the total yield strength, recognizing them from 
regular CG FeCoNiCr HEA. The achievement of the UFG matrix can be 
attributed primarily to the application of a high consolidation pressure, 
which has two effects: 1) reduces interparticle distances because of the 
enforced plastic deformation of the individual powder particles and 2) 
reducing the diffusivity, thereby reducing the rate of grain growth.
The high strain is predominately induced by fine diameter and low 
content of coarse Cr-Mn-O-rich oxide. High sintering pressure sup -
presses the oxides to grow, reducing the interface area between soft 
matrix phases and hard oxides, and thus decreasing the sources of crack 
generation.
