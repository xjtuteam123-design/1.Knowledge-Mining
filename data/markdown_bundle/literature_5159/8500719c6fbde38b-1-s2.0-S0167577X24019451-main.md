ABSTRACT
The paper deals with severe plastic deformation (SPD) which leads to the formation of ultrafine-grained ( β + α +
ω ) and ( β + ω ) structures, respectively, in Ti-Nb-Zr and Ti-Nb-Zr-Ta-Sn alloys thus having a strong impact on their 
mechanical properties. The presence of the α -phase additionally contributes to the Ti-Nb-Zr alloy hardening and 
considerably improves its strength properties, reducing its ductility and Young ’ s modulus after SPD. Multi -
component alloying of the Ti-Nb-Zr-Ta-Sn alloy with Nb, Zr, Ta, Sn elements stabilizes the β -phase and does not 
reduce the alloy ductility and Young ’ s modulus. At the same time, the increase in the strength properties in this 
case turns out to be less significant.
1. Introduction
Titanium and titanium alloys are widely used in medical implant 
manufacturing due to their biocompatibility and high strength [1] . The 
biocompatibility improvement of titanium alloys is determined by the 
reduction of their Young ’ s modulus due to the use of the appropriate 
β -stabilizing alloying elements, precluding such metals as V, Al, Ni, Co, 
which have a toxic effect on the human body [2] . Low modulus β -Ti 
alloys of the latest generation, are compositionally complex alloys 
mainly based on the Ti – Nb system with addition of the Zr, Ta, and Sn 
elements [3] . This is due to their biocompatibility and the fact that these 
elements, when used in the proper concentration, stabilize the high- 
temperature Ti modification (body-centered cubic (BCC) β -Ti). This 
leads to a significant reduction in the Young ’ s modulus of titanium al -
loys from 35 to 60 GPa [4] which thus approaches the Young ’ s modulus 
of bones (10 – 30 GPa). Neutral hardeners Zr and Sn slightly affect the 
β -phase stability in the titanium alloy but improve its strength and 
corrosion resistance [5] .
The formation of an ultrafine-grained (UFG) structure in titanium 
alloys by different severe plastic deformation (SPD) methods enhances 
their mechanical properties without alloying with toxic elements [6,7] . 
At the same time, the issues related to obtaining the required mechanical 
properties and low Young ’ s modulus in the Ti-based multicomponent 
systems with the β -phase stabilized by SPD need further investigation. 
All this makes the development of the β -Ti alloys based on the multi -
component UFG systems an important task in medicine.
The aim of the present work is to study the influence of SPD on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of the Ti-Nb-Zr and Ti-Nb-Zr- 
Ta-Sn alloys.
2. Materials and methods
In the experiments, we used the TNZ and TNZTS alloys with the 
following compositions, wt.%: Ti-39.5Nb-5Zr (Ti ≤ 55.20, Nb ≤ 39.53, 
Zr ≤ 5.32, Fe ≤ 0.03) and Ti-39.5Nb-5Zr-2Ta-2Sn (Ti ≤ 55.20, Nb ≤
39.53, Zr ≤ 5.35, Ta ≤ 2, Sn ≤ 2). Hot-rolled alloy rods were smelted in 
“ VSMPO-AVISMA Corporation ” (Verkhnyaya Salda, Russia). The UFG 
structure was obtained by SPD, including uniaxial pressing and multi -
pass rolling in grooved rolls followed by pre-recrystallization annealing 
[8] . In order to obtain the coarse-crystalline (CG) state in the TNZ and 
TNZTS alloys, recrystallization annealing of samples in the UFG state

3. Results and discussion
Figs. 1, 2 present the TNZ and TNZTS alloys in the CG and UFG 
microstructures and phase compositions. In Fig. 1 a, the initial TNZ 
microstructure consists of equiaxed grains of the matrix BCC β -phase 
with an average grain size 15 μ m. In Fig. 1 b, one can see areas with 
spherical particles of the hexagonal simple (HS) α -phase along the ma -
trix grain boundaries. Their size varies from 30 to 50 nm. The selected- 
area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns show the presence of low- 
intensity extra reflections typical for the hexagonal close packed 
(HCP) ω -phase. The bright-field TEM image demonstrates ellipsoidal 
ω -phase particles ranging in size between 6 and 10 nm in matrix grains. 
The CG TNZTS alloy has a completely crystallized equilibrium structure 
consisting of polyhedral β -phase grains with an average size 45 μ m. The 
SAED patterns in Fig. 1 e, f show β - and ω -phase reflections.
SPD results in the grain refinement and grain-subgrain structure 
formation in the TNZ and TNZTS alloys. Development of the cellular- 
network dislocation structure is observed in the subgrains. The TEM 
images in Fig. 1 e- g demonstrate a large number of extinction contours 
localizing mostly along the subgrain boundaries and fragments, while 
the SAED patterns show a great deal of point reflections arranged 
circumferentially with typical azimuthal blur, indicating the presence of 
large- and small-angle misorientations of the sub- and grain boundaries.
The SAED patterns in Fig. 1 e- g show groups of reflections from three 
phases in the UFG TNZ alloy, namely the high-intensity reflections from 
the solid solution of titanium and niobium ( β -phase), the low-intensity 
reflections from the non-equilibrium α -phase, and the reflections from 
the ω -phase. The average size of structural elements of the β -, α - and 
ω -phases in the UFG TNZ alloy, is 0.2, 0.03 and 0.007 μ m, respectively. 
The SAED patterns in Fig. l i , k reveal the presence of major β - and 
ω -phases for the UFG TNZTS alloy, whereas the dark-field images in 
Fig. 1 j, l obtained in the β + ω reflection, show ellipsoidal ω -phase 
particles. For the UFG TNZTS alloy, the average subgrain size of β - and 
ω -phases is respectively 0.3 and 0.008 μ m.
The XRD patterns obtained for the TNZ and TNZTS alloys in the CG 
and UFG states are presented in Fig. 2 a. In both cases, the phase 
composition is the single β -phase. Other phases are not identified, thus 
indicating their low volume fraction. According to Table 1 , the lattice

alloy acquires the UFG state, its parameters increase as follows: micro -
hardness – by 28 %, yield strength – by 10 %, ultimate tensile strength – 
by 95 % (Tabl 1). As for ductility and Young ’ s modulus, they decrease 
respectively by 50 and 6.5 % as compared to the CG alloy. Young ’ s 
modulus of the CG and UFG TNZTS alloy remains unchanged. The 
strength properties of this alloy are however less improved. This dif -
ference between the alloys is explained by their different structure and 
phase composition. Mechanical properties of the TNZ alloy after SPD are 
improved by the ( β + α + ω ) UFG structure, which is due to the sub -
structure strengthening and dispersion hardening of the α - and ω -phase 
nanoparticles. Alloying with the Nb, Zr, Ta, Sn elements of the TNZTS 
alloy provides the β -phase stabilization. Therefore, the main contribu -
tion to the alloy hardening due to SPD is made by substructural 
strengthening of the matrix β -phase. The dispersion hardening ( ω -phase) 
is of lesser significance.
The CG structure of the TNZ and TNZTS alloys is characterized by a 
dimple rupture typical for the fracture toughness, as presented in Fig. 3 a, 
b. The dimple size in the CG structure of the TNZ alloy is 26 μ m on 
average, while that of the TNZTS alloy, is 31 μ m. This points to the 
fracture primarily along the grain boundaries. The average dimple size 
of the UFG TNZ and TNZTS alloys is 15 and 18 μ m, respectively ( Fig. 3 c, 
d). One can see the 0.2 – 0.8 μ m dimples at the separation boundaries, 
indicating the fracture of both UFG alloys along the subgrain boundaries 
formed during SPD as well as the boundaries of matrix grain.
We can thus conclude that SPD leads to formation of the ( β + α + ω ) 
and ( β + ω ) UFG structures in the TNZ and TNZTS alloys, respectively, 
which have a significant impact on their mechanical properties, defor -
mation and fracture.
