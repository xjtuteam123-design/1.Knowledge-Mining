l. Introduction
The aim of this work was to provide a predictive,
quantitative design tool which could be used to reduce
the cost and time associated with the development of
newNi-base superalloys. The tensile properties of poly-
crystalline alloys were modelled using an artificial neu-
ral network within a Bayesian statistical framework.
Neural networks of this type are capable of realising a
great variety of non-linear relationships of considerable
complexity. They are 'trained' using existing experi-
mental data which is presented to the network in the
form of input-output pairs, thus the optimumrelation-
ship is found between the tensile property and those
parameters which are considered to be of importance.
The yield strength was considered as this is a situa-
tlon where the metallurgical understanding of the con-
trolling factors Is good and so the neural network
modelling technique can be rigorously tested.
2. The Neural Network
Neural networks are powerful tools designed to imi-
tate a brain's ability to recognise patterns and draw
conclusions when presented with complex, noisy and
often irrelevant information.1) Neural network models
are composedof manysimple processing units, referred
to as nodes, arranged in a pattern inspired by biological
networks.2) The nodes are linked to each other by
weighted connections which typically are adapted during
the learning process to improve performance. In fact, the
strength of a neural network lies in the vast numberof
adaptable interconnections. In simple terms a node
performs a weighted sumof inputs, passes this through

values or those which slightly reduce the training error
by modelling the noise in the data and hencelead to bad

5. Conclusions
The yieid strength of polycrystalline superalloys was
modelled using a artificial neural network within a
Bayesian framework. A series of networks were trained
on a simple data base containing the yield strengths of
a range of polycrystalline wrought alloys as a function
of temperature. Input variables of composition and
temperature were used. The optimum networks con-
tained 6or 7 hidden nodes this resulted in a realistic
inferred noise level of 57MPa E =44MPaand' t**i~
E = 60 MPa.t**t
Thetrained neural networks were subjected to several
metallurgical tests to assess the metallurgical significance
of the models. It was found that the agreement of the
neural network predictions with published experimental
results wasa function of the range and depth of data in
the tralning and test data base. The inferred dependence
of yield strength on alloy chemistry was in qualitative
and often quantitative agreement with published ex-
perimental results whenthe alloying elements included
were adequately covered over the compositional range
in the data base, these inciude Al. T1, Co and Mo.
Conversely, whenInput variables were sparsely covered
in the data base, such as W,Nband Ta, Iess well defined
relationships were inferred. Howeverthe dependenceof
yield strength on these inputs was still in qualitative
agreement with those observed experimentally. The
uncertainty bars produced acted as a valuable warning
signal Indicating when the network output was badly
defined.
The most influential parameters were found to be
temper'ature followed by the y' and y" formers Al, Ti,
Tl/AI and Nband the refractory elements MoWand
Ta. Theother elements included werefound to have little
infiuence on yie]d strength. This was found to be in
