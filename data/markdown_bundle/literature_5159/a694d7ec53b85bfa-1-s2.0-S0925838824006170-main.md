1. Introduction 
For most alloys, strength and ductility always go in opposite direc -
tion. It is difficult to obtain an alloy with both high strength and 
ductility. The conventional methods of alloy strengthening and tough -
ening fail to address the inherent trade-off between strength and 
ductility [1 – 3] . The synergistic effect of high strength and ductility can 
improve energy efficiency, factor of safety and fatigue limit [4,5] , so it is 
particularly important to overcome the strength ductility trade-off effect 
and make the alloy strength ductility synergistic improvement [6] . The 
proposal of high entropy alloy (HEA) brings a new exploration direction 
for synergistic enhancement of strength and ductility of alloys. Since the 
concept of HEAs was proposed in 2004, they have attracted widespread 
attention from researchers due to their unique microstructure and 
outstanding performance characteristics [7 – 9] . Face-centered cubic 
(FCC) HEAs exhibit excellent ductility due to their low stacking fault 
energy (SFE) [10,11] , which leads to various deformation mechanisms 
such as dislocation [12] , twinning [13,14] , or phase transformation [15, 
16] during plastic deformation. However, FCC-HEAs with excellent 
ductility usually have low yield strength. Enhancing the strength of al -
loys through methods such as fine-grained strengthening, precipitation 
strengthening, and dislocation strengthening can effectively improve 
their strength. However, these approaches often compromise ductility. 
Some researchers endeavor to modify the SFE of the alloy in order to 
enhance its ductility through the effects of Transformation Induced 
Plasticity (TRIP) and Twinning Induced Plasticity (TWIP), without 
compromising the alloy ’ s strength. [17] . However, the TRIP effect im -
proves the plasticity of the alloy more [18,19] and has little effect on the 
strength, compared to other alloy strengthening methods [20,21] . 
Introducing heterostructures into alloys is also one of the methods to 
achieve both high strength and ductility [22 – 24] . The purpose of 
introducing heterostructure is to make heterogeneous structure with 
large grain size difference appear in the alloy. In high entropy alloys, 
common heterostructures include gradient dislocations and grain 
boundaries [25] , single-phase heterogeneous grain structure [26] , 
multi-phase heterogeneous grain structure [27] , et al. Among these 
structures, obtaining alloys with multi-scale grains through rolling and 
partial recrystallization annealing is the most used technical solution to 
actual industrial production [28 – 30] . Generally, with increasing rolling 
reduction, the recrystallization texture does not change qualitatively 
whereas the grain size tends to decrease due to a high density of 
nucleation sites [31] .

Moreover, large unrecrystallized grains are adjacent to many small 
recrystallized grains, greatly increasing the pathway for dislocation 
movement and avoiding stress concentration during deformation. As is 
well known, the hardness of HCP phase is higher than that of FCC phase, 
and the phase transformation during deformation can lead to the early 
occurrence of fracture. During the deformation process, the distribution 
of strain firstly focuses on FCC and then on HCP, which effectively delays 
the strain concentration caused by deformation. In addition to the 
original recrystallized-unrecrystallized areas interface of the alloy, the 
FCC matrix, deformed HCP, and mechanical twins are alternately 
distributed, greatly increasing the interface area of the heterostructure, 
hindering dislocation movement, and thus improving the strength of the 
alloy. TRIP and TWIP effects, such as the martensite transformation of 
FCC → HCP and the mechanical twinning of FCC and HCP ( Figs. 7 and 9 ), 
delay the occurrence of necking in the process of plastic deformation of 
the alloy, thus retaining the original plastic advantage of the alloy [40] .
