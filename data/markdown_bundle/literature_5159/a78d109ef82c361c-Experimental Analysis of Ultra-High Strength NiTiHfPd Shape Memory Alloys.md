Submitted: 18 March 2023 / Accepted: 22 April 2023 / Published online: 11 October 2023
Ultra-high strength of NiTiHfPd alloys have been promising for speciﬁc application areas of SMAs. Thus,
the main objective of this study is to further understand the high strength behavior of the alloys through
experimental and theoretical studies. Shape memory response of an ultra-high strength Ni
45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5
alloy was systematically investigated after aging at 550 /C176C for 5 h via constant-stress temperature cycling
and constant-temperature stress cycling experiments. Shape memory behavior under a wide range of
compressive stress levels from 300 to 1200 MPa was reported before and after stress cycling of 5000 times.
The alloys showed a reversible strain of 1.3% against an ultra-high stress of 2 GPa. It is concluded that the
combination of high strength and temperature capability mainly stems from high chemical complexity in
addition to aging and will be quite advantageous in practical applications.
Keywords advanced high strength, martensite, NiTiHfPd, shape
memory alloy
1. Introduction
Shape memory alloys (SMAs) are smart metallic materials
that have unconventional properties, such as shape memory
effect (SME) and superelasticity (SE). These two unique
properties are governed by thermo-elastic martensitic transfor-
mations (Ref 1). Out of SMAs, NiTi is well known for its
widely accepted superority in practical applications owing to
excellent shape memory properties in addition to corrosion
resistance. However, transformation temperatures (TTs) of NiTi
are limited to 100 /C176C, which restricts possible applications.
Matrix strength of NiTi alloys is usually below 700 MPa
without post-processing cold work (Ref 1).
The harsh nature of operation environment of some
applications such as aerospace, oil–gas, automobile etc.
requires SMAs that can simultaneously withstand high stresses
and temperatures (Ref 2, 3). Trying to improve both TTs and
matrix strength of SMAs is a challenging process. One of the
most powerful methods to improve TTs and strength has been
alloying. Thus, main focus has been placed on alloying of NiTi
alloys to tailor the abovementioned properties (Ref 2). Hf has
been selected as one of the most successful alloying addition to
NiTi compared to Zr, Pd, Pt and Au due to effectiveness and
lower cost of Hf (Ref 2). First Hf was replaced with Ti in a
study conducted by AbuJudom et al. and it was shown that TTs
were remarkably increased (Ref 4). Following this study, many
studies have been conducted on NiTiHf shape memory alloys
(Ref 5). Following studies focused on effects of alloying and
aging on NiTiHf alloys. Meng et al. showed that Ni-rich H-
phase formed by aging treatments can substantially increase
TTs, which could be above 200 /C176C (Ref 6, 7). Besides high
TTs, Ni-rich NiTiHf alloys showed high strength (Ref 5, 8). In a
study, polycrystalline Ni
52Ti28Hf20 (at.%) alloys showed
recoverable strain of higher than 1.5% under compressive
stress of 2000 MPa (Ref 9).
On the other hand, Ni-rich NiTiHf alloys suffer from high
brittleness limiting their potential real-life applications. Thus,
researchers have continued to search for better materials that
can work at high temperature and high stress levels. NiTiHfPd
alloys have been one of the most promising materials /C213family as
a quaternary alloy system. Studies on the NiTiHfPd alloys are
limited compared to NiTiHf ternary alloys. NiTiHfPd alloys
showed excellent shape memory behaviors at high strength and
at comparatively high temperatures (Ref 10-15). Perfect
superelastic response was observed under a huge compressive
stress of 2 GPa in addition to nearly 3% recoverable strain at a
constant compressive stress of 1 GPa in as-received condition
(Ref 15). Furthermore, simple aging treatments are effective to
tailor their shape memory properties and TTs (Ref 16, 17).
In this study, shape memory behavior of Ni
45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5
alloy was methodically investigated after aging at 550/C176Cf o r5 h .
The main intent is to further understand possible effects of high
chemical complexity and aging on the strength and shape memory
behavior of the material. In addition to the effects of stress cycling,
their behaviors under ultra-high stress levels are revealed.

3. Results and Discussion
It is known that the shape memory properties of NiTiHf-
based alloys are highly aging dependent (Ref 5). It was
previously shown that transformation temperatures above
100 /C176C are possible after proper aging treatments (Ref 15).
The change of TTs is closely related to presence of precipitates
in NiTiHf-based SMAs and was previously discussed in details
(Ref 15). The combination of high TTs and phase transforma-
tion stability is a strong sign of high strength in SMAs. Thus,
based on the previous results (Ref 15), aging at 550 /C176C for 5 h
is selected to conduct further experiments on as its austenite
ﬁnish ( A
f) temperature is above 150 /C176C.
The stress cycling tests (Step 2) of the aged Ni 45.3-
Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 alloys are conducted after the ﬁrst set of thermal
cycling experiments (Step 1) and they are presented in Fig. 1.
The aged Ni 45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 alloy was mechanically cycled
between 5 and 1600 MPa for 5000 times at 160 /C176C. The aim of
the stress cycling process was to introduce/re-arrange defects in
the microstructure without extensive deformation and to
observe the possible effects on the post-cyclic shape memory
properties. It is inevitable that fabricated SMA parts are
exposed to vibrations and undergo cyclic deformations in real-
life applications. Stress cycling experiments were conducted by
keeping the maximum applied stress level constant instead of
strain to simulate the cases where loading is the constrained
factor instead of displacement.
It was clear that the superelastic behavior (such as critical
stress for stress-induced phase transformation, superelastic
strain, stress hysteresis) changed considerably with stress
cycling. The critical stress levels for the forward phase
transformation were measured graphically by using an inter-
section method. The critical stress was 1390 MPa at the ﬁrst
cycle and decreased to 1080 MPa at the 100th cycle. The
critical stress for the 5000th cycle was not possible to be
detected since stress–strain curve became linear. Similar to
critical stress levels, the stress hysteresis, which was measured
at the midpoint of the plateau-like strain in stress–strain
responses, also decreased by stress cycling process. The stress
hysteresis was 330 MPa at the ﬁrst loading cycle while it was
only 55 MPa at the 100th cycle.
There was a residual strain of 0.2% after stress cycling
which can be attributed to plastic deformation and/or retained
martensite in the microstructure. The end strain level at
1600 MPa was decreased by 0.5% after cycling which can be
attributed to the ‘‘hardening effect’’ due to cycling. As the
cycling introduces defects, the critical stress decreases since the
defects act as nucleation sites for transformation. However, the
defects also impede the motion of phase front and thus higher
stress levels are required to complete the transformation. Thus,
the total strain reached at the end of loading for the pre-
determined constant stress decreases.
Figure 2 shows the constant-stress shape memory responses
of the aged Ni
45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 alloy before (step1, red solid
curves) and after (step3, blue-dotted curves) the stress cycling
experiments. Selected compressive stresses were applied above
Af temperatures and specimens were cooled down below
martensite ﬁnish ( Mf) temperatures followed by heating back
above Af temperatures under the constant load. Before the stress
cycling tests, martensite start ( Ms) temperature was 98 and
154 /C176C and recoverable shape memory strains were 0.95 and
1.9% under 300 and 1200 MPa, respectively. The irrecoverable
strain was 0.25% under 1200 MPa before the stress cycling
tests.
The thermal cycling under constant stress experiments
conducted after the stress cycling tests were shown as blue-
dotted lines in Fig. 2. It is clear that the shape memory curves
shifted to lower temperatures after the stress cycling experi-
ments where M
s decreased to 58 /C176C under 300 MPa and
125 /C176C under 1200 MPa. The decrease of Ms can be attributed
to hardening effect of generated dislocations upon the load
cycling. The generated and/or re-arranged defects may hinder
the martensitic transformation, thus further under-cooling, DT,
was required. Thereby, Ms decreased further below the
thermodynamic equilibrium temperature To which can be
shown as Ms ¼ To /C0 DT (Ref 18). The decrease in Ms has
also been reported for NiTi-based and Fe-based shape memory
alloys by many researchers after stress-free thermal cycling
experiments due to pinning effect of dislocations (Ref 19, 20).
It is clear that the decrease of Ms is less at 1200 MPa compared
to 300 MPa. As the applied stress increased, the amount of
newly introduced defects increased followed by a tendency of
saturation in the defect density. This saturation diminished the
dislocation effect on lowering the transformation temperature.
Thus, less temperature difference was recorded when the
applied stress was 1200 MPa. This phenomenon is in good
agreement with the reports where the transformation temper-
ature decrease was less as the number of cycles increased in
NiTi alloys (Ref 14).
Recoverable strains decreased slightly after stress cycling as
there were 0.7% under 300 MPa and 1.6% under 1200 MPa.
No irrecoverable strain was observed under 1200 MPa after the
stress cycling, which can be attributed to the work hardening
effect where further generation of defects can be impeded due
to the existing defects that leads to diminished plastic strain. It
was truly remarkable that aged Ni
45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 alloy can
fully recover about 2% under a very high compressive stress
level of 1200 MPa with no irrecoverable strain above 100 /C176C.
High strength at elevated temperatures has been always a
desired feature in metallic materials since the critical stress for
plastic deformation tends to decrease with temperature. Thus,
Ni
45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 alloy system has potentials to be used in
actuator applications that requires high strength at elevated
temperatures.

of 1.7 and 1.3% were achieved for 1500 and 2000 MPa,
respectively. On the other hand, the irrecoverable strains were
0.3 and 1.1% for the applied stresses of 1500 and 2000 MPa,
respectively. To the best of the authors /C213knowledge, with the
exception of two studies (Ref 9, 21), shape recovery under
these ultra-high stress levels was not reported for polycrys-
talline shape memory alloys. In one of the referred study,
polycrystalline Ni
52Ti28Hf20 (at.%) alloys showed recoverable
strain of higher than 1.5% under compressive stress of
2000 MPa (Ref 9). In the other study, Ni 54Ti46 (at.%) alloys
showed recovery strain of 1% at 1800 MPa after aging at
450 /C176C for 3 h (Ref 21). It should be noted that high strength of
Ni45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 alloy depend both on precipitation harden-
ing and naturally high complexity of Ni 45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5. The
additional hardening due to stress cycling might also contribute
to the improvement of the shape memory behavior under high
stresses.

The decrease in recoverable strains after the stress cycling

4. Conclusions
Based on the results presented in this study, following
conclusions can be formed for the aged multi-component
Ni45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 shape memory alloys. The stress cycling
tests caused a decrease in transformation temperatures (by 30-
40 /C176C) and recoverable strains (by 0.25%) in the constant stress
thermal cycling experiments. Transformation strain of reaching
2% was achieved under compressive stress of 1200 MPa. On
the other hand, reversible strain of 1.3% was achieved against
ultra-high stress of 2GPa owing to naturally high chemical
complexity besides aging and stress cycling.
