ABSTRACT
An equiatomic low-activated FeCrV ternary alloy was prepared by mechanical alloying and spark plasma sin-
tering. The BCC solid solution alloy exhibited an excellent strength-ductility synergy properties, which was 1.85
GPa compressive yield strength, 3.04 GPa fracture strength and more than 28% plasticity at room temperature.
Even at an elevated temperature of 600
/C14 C, its yield strength was still more than 1 GPa, revealing a pronounced
high temperature resistance. The FeCrV alloy had a homogeneous microstructure with high-density dislocations.
A nanocrystalline characteristic was demonstrated and the average grain size was about 322 nm, leading to the
improved mechanical properties. The strengthening mechanism was discussed in detail and the contributions of
strengthening factors were calculated. The FeCrV alloy was irradiated up to 160 dpa with 2 MeV Au
2þ ions at 573
K. The nanoindentation test was carried out to simply evaluate the hardening degree under such a high dose
irradiation with heavy ions. The semi-quantitative analysis results show that FeCrV alloy has an excellent irra-
diation tolerance compared with pure Fe.
1. Introduction
The concept of high entropy alloy (HEA) was ﬁrst proposed in 2004
[1], which is composed of ﬁve or more alloy elements with chemical
disorder. This strategy leads to the maximum mixing entropy to form
solid solution with simple microstructure instead of intermetallic phases
which might embrittle structural alloys. Soon after that, a worldwide
drive for alloy design has been created. Development on non-equiatomic
HEAs and medium-entropy alloys have led to the broader family of
multi-component alloys (MCAs) [ 2,3]. Due to a series of peculiar effects
such as high entropy effect, lattice distortion effect, sluggish diffusion
effect and cocktail effect, MCAs have exhibited great performance such as
the excellent strength-ductility synergy properties in Al
0.25Ti0.25CoFeNi
[4], the increased overall magnetization in CoFeNi 0.5Cr0.5Alx [5] and the
outstanding corrosion resistance in AlCoCrFeNi –SiC (10 wt%) [ 6].
Nanocrystalline metals typically show very high strength and good fa-
tigue resistance. During the deformation of polycrystalline materials,
grain boundaries may effectively impede dislocation movement to
improve the yield strength of the alloy. Grain reﬁnement can signiﬁcantly
increase the density of grain boundaries and allow materials to achieve
excellent strengthening effects, even improve the plasticity at the same
time. Therefore, nanocryatslline structure is often desired to improve the
mechanical properties of high entropy alloys [ 7–12]. For example,
nanostructured CoCrFeNi HEA [ 7] exhibits a high strain-hardening ca-
pacity, a continuous increase in hardness even at the equivalent strain of
εeq > 12 and an improved strain rate sensitivity of 0.058. Both
Cr20Mn24Fe30Co20Ni6 and Cr20Mn15Fe34Co20Ni11 HEAs [8] fabricated by
vacuum arc melting exhibit a simultaneous increase in strength and
ductility after grain re ﬁnement by cold rolling and annealing. Especially,
the Cr20Mn15Fe34Co20Ni11 HEA achieved a better combination of 795 /C6
10Mpa strength and 68.5 /C6 7.3% elongation. As the research develops
extensively, some attention has been arisen to the alloys with con ﬁgu-
ration entropy lower than 1.5R and higher than R, which are de ﬁned as
medium-entropy alloys(MEAs). Sohn et al. [ 13] found severe lattice
distortions in VCoNi multiprinciple element face-centered cubic alloys.
The Hall-Petch relationship suggests that these distortions can increase
the lattice friction stresses, thereby increasing the yield strength of VCoNi
MEA and ultimately achieving a good combination of 1Gpa yield strength
and 38% elongation. Equiatomic FCC FeCrNi high Cr content stainless
steels was obtained, showing improved tensile properties with an

Interestingly, some ternary alloys have better mechanical properties than

prove the potential applications of BCC MCAs as nuclear structural ma-

to enhance corrosion resistance. Considering the economics of the
application, Fe is also one of the best choices. Fe, Cr and V are inherently
easier to form a single solid solution due to their similar atomic radii and
physical properties. FeCrV multi-component single-phase alloy [ 28] and

as-cast material from the compression test at RT is 494Mpa, 50% and

et al. produced Cu xCrFeTiV high entropy alloy by ball milling and the
results showed that the alloy has excellent radiation stability [ 30]and
thermal properties [ 31].
To demonstrate and improve the mechanical performance further not
only at RT but also elevated temperatures, the strategy of nanocrystalline
is considered. By using mechanical alloying (MA) method, equiatomic
FeCrV MCA is fabricated in the present study due to its bene ﬁts of no
segregation, no anisotropy and nanocrystal formation. Simultaneously,
spark plasma sintering (SPS) owing to its shorter processing time relative
to conventional sintering and thus ensuring better retention of nano-
crystalline, has been used for the consolidation of alloying powders. The
microstructure, mechanical properties, thermal stability and response of
hardening to heavy ion irradiation with high dose have been investigated
in detail.
2. Materials and experimental details
High-purity (>99 wt%) elemental powders of Fe, Cr, V with particle
size /C20 50 μm were used as the starting materials. These powders were
mixed in the equiatomic proportion at room temperature and then milled
in a high-energy planetary ball mill (FOCUCY F –P400) using 316-steel
vials and balls with different diameters (10 mm:5 mm ¼ 5:3, wt ratio)
at the rotation speed of 225 rpm. The milling was carried out by dry
milling for 70 h in the Ar atmosphere with a ball to powder weight ratio
of 20:1. Milled powders were extracted at a regular interval of 10 h and
then characterized to understand the milling behavior. Subsequently, the
milled powders were sintered at 1050 /C14 C for 5 min with the pressure of
50 Mpa by spark plasma sintering (SPS, SUMITOMO SPS 1050). The
cylinder samples with a diameter of 20 mm and a height of 6 mm were
produced. Fig. 1 is the cross-section back-scattered electron(BSE) image
of FeCrV MCA and the corresponding X-ray energy dispersive spec-
trum(EDS) mapping. The matrix exhibited a uniform gray contrast, in
which small black particles with about 174 nm were embed. The
composition distribution results show that the FeCrV matrix was rela-
tively homogeneous with locally distributed V particles, as indicated by A
and B respectively in the BSE image. The chemical composition of the
two regions is listed in Table 1.
Microstructure characterizations were carried out by scanning elec-
tron microscopy (SEM, JSM-6510A operated at 30 KV with an energy
dispersive spectrometer), X-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku SmartLab, Cu-
Kα radiation with λ ¼ 0.154 nm) and FEI Tecnai F20 microscopy
equipped with an EDS. To obtain a suitable sample for Transmission
Electron Microscopy(TEM) analysis, as-sintered alloys were mechani-
cally thinned to the thickness of ~40 μm and subsequently thinned with a
Gatan 691 ion thinning instrument.
The mechanical properties were studied in terms of micro-hardness,
compression and thermal stability. The bulk hardness of the sectioned
and polished specimen was measured by an automatic micro-hardness
tester (EM-1500L) with a load of 50 g and dwell time of 10 s. At least
20 measurements were performed to obtain the average value for vickers
hardness. Small cylinders with a size of Φ3 /C2 6 mm for compression test
were cut from the middle of the ingots. The compression properties were
tested on a computer-controlled electronic universal testing machine
(WDW-200D) at a strain rate of 1 /C2 10/C0 3 s/C0 1.

ion accelerator with the acceleration voltage of 2 MeV to a ﬂuence of 3 /C2

university. As a comparison, pure Fe samples were also irradiated under
the same condition. All of the samples had been mechanically and

Materials deformation is inherently due to dislocation movement.
With the increase of temperature, dislocations can move easily through
sliding and climbing by combination of applied stress and thermal acti-
vation. In addition, the increase in temperature leads to a weakening of
the interatomic bonding forces which is the external force that prevents
dislocations from sliding. Furthermore, the nucleation rate of dynamic
recrystallization increases with the increase of temperature, which may
promote the generation of recrystallized grains thus increase the coor-
dination of deformation and the plasticity of the alloy [ 51]. Moreover,
grain boundary sliding under thermal deformation at high temperature
may also result in the plastic deformation of crystals though the defor-
mation degree is often much less than the one caused by dislocation slip.

The studies have shown that the yield strength( σy ) is a linear addition
of various strengthening mechanisms [ 52,53]. It can be expressed as
follows:

friction stress σ0 for element Fe, Cr and V is 130 Mpa, 320 Mpa and 150

3.4.1. Solid-solution strengthening
In traditional alloys, solid-solution strengthening has acknowledgedly
been ascribe to a relatively dilute solution alloys with a solvent lattice
through which the dislocations move and interact with discretely
distributed solute atoms. But for MCA, there are essentially no absolute
solvents or solutes because all the constituent atoms are assumed to be
randomly arranged on the lattice. Song et al. [ 58]. have simply treated
equiatomic CrMnFeVTi high-entropy alloy as a Fe solvent matrix con-
taining Mn, Cr, V, Ti solutes to obtain a rough estimation of the contri-
butions from solid-solution mechanism. The result was turned out to be
52.2 Mpa, which is very limited compared to grain boundary strength-
ening(1124 Mpa) and dislocation strengthening(578.3 Mpa). In such
case, it can be deduced that there is little contribution of solid solution
strengthening in equiatomic FeCrV alloys.

3.5. Irradiation effects on hardening and microstructure
Irradiation hardening, one of the important indexes for the selection
and development of candidate structural materials, is essential to eval-
uate the irradiation response in advanced nuclear energy systems.
Fig. 11 a gives the indentation-depth dependence of the averaged nano-
indentation hardness of FeCrV alloy and pure Fe before and after irra-
diation. The decrease of hardness with the increase of indentation depth
as h > 20 nm was observed in both unirradiated and irradiated samples,
which is known as the indentation size effect (ISE) [ 66] that can be
explained by the concept of geometrically necessary dislocation (GND)
[67]. The smooth and non-deformed surface caused by electrolytic pol-
ishing makes the reverse ISE only existed in the depth of h < 20 nm. The
average nanoindentation hardness of Fe after irradiation was largely
enhanced. While the increase for FeCrV alloy was signi ﬁcantly smaller.
The indentation-depth vs. the ratio of H irr/HUnirr is demonstrated in
Fig. 11 b. The ratio changed with the increase of the indentation depth
and there was an inﬂection point at a depth of about 50 nm in both FeCrV
and Fe which corresponds to the critical indentation depth (hcrit). Beyond
the hcrit, the nanoindentation hardness would be affected by unirradiated
matrix. Considering the shallow ion irradiation layer in the present study,
a semi-quantitative method [ 68] was used to evaluate the degree of ra-
diation hardening by comparing the hardening rate at the critical
indentation depth. The hardening rate of the FeCrV alloy at the h crit was
40%, while it was 212% for pure Fe at the same depth. The degree of
radiation hardening of FeCrV alloy signi ﬁcantly decreased compared to
Fe, indicating its excellent radiation resistance.

4. Conclusions
(1) A multi-component nanocrystalline FeCrV alloy has been fabri-
cated by MA and SPS, the ﬁnal obtained alloy is mainly a single-
phase BCC structure, accompanied a minor amount of unsolved
V element.
(2) The present FeCrV alloy possesses superior strength-ductility
synergy properties at room temperature. Even up to 600 /C14 C, it
remains the remarkable strength of more than 1 Gpa, indicating a
high thermal resistance.
(3) Theoretical calculation results indicate that the outstanding me-
chanical performance is mainly ascribed to the dislocation
strengthening and grain boundary strengthening, summarizing
that it is the intrinsic nature rather than the number of constituent
elements that determines the properties of MCAs.
(4) Through the semi-quantitative analysis of irradiation hardening
rate, FeCrV alloy has a good irradiation tolerance compared with
Fe.
