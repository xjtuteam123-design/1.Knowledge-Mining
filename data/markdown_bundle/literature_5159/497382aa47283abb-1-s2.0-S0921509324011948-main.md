ABSTRACT
Ti(CON) formation in high-purity V-10Cr-xTi alloys evidently depends on the Ti concentration. Precipitates are 
not observed in V-10Cr-0.1Ti alloy, whereas precipitation occurs in 0.5 wt% and higher Ti levels, providing 
similar precipitation strengthening. 2 wt% Ti addition retards the grain growth. Furthermore, both yield stress 
and ultimate tensile stress at room temperature and 973 K are steadily increased with rising Ti concentration, and 
elongation does not reveal clear dependence on Ti concentration. In comparison, lower Ti addition is more 
beneficial to high-temperature strength than that of room temperature. Additionally, V-10Cr-0.5Ti, V-10Cr-1Ti, 
and V-10Cr-2Ti alloys would be alternatives to the high-purity V-4Cr-4Ti alloy from the viewpoint of comparable 
strength and improved low-activation characteristics.
1. Introduction
V-4Cr-4Ti was recommended as reference composition for structural 
components in fusion reactors [ 1 – 4 ]. Interstitial impurities (i.e., C, N, O) 
in the vanadium alloys significantly affect mechanical properties, 
formability, weldability, and irradiation damage resistance. Alloying 
element Ti in the vanadium alloys not only scavenges those interstitial 
impurities by forming Ti(CON) precipitates to maintain a lower level of 
the interstitial impurities in the matrix, but also can be dissolved in the 
matrix to enhance the strength. However, Ti generates long-lived 
radioactive isotopes under the fusion irradiation conditions [ 5 ], which 
are disadvantageous to waste management and environments. Ti con -
centration should be lower than 1.4 wt% to meet the requirements of 
100-year cooling time as a hands-on level [ 6 ]. Therefore, it is possible to 
reduce the recycling time of V-Cr-Ti systems after use in the fusion re -
actors by decreasing the Ti concentration. In order to further improve 
the low-activation characteristics of V-Cr-Ti alloys, high-purity vana -
dium alloys with a much lower concentration of interstitial impurities 
and high-activation impurities were developed [ 7 , 8 ]. Ti(CON) pre -
cipitates were characterized in 1 wt% and higher Ti added high-purity 
V-4Cr-xTi alloys [ 7 ], and 1 wt% Ti addition can completely suppress 
void formation after neutron irradiation with a damage dose of 21 dpa 
(displacements per atom) at 673 – 773 K for V-4Cr-xTi alloys [ 9 ]. Some 
research reported that heat treatments and/or aging had obvious effects 
on the formation of Ti-rich precipitates in V-4Cr-4Ti alloy, such as shape, 
distribution, coherency, etc. [ 10 – 12 ]. On the other hand, there are few 
systematic research about the influences of lower Ti addition on the 
precipitation of Ti(CON) and mechanical properties of the developed 
high-purity vanadium alloys. In the previous study, it revealed that 
hardness and tensile strength of V-4Cr-xTi alloys were increased with 
rising Ti concentration from 1 wt% to 4 wt% [ 7 ], namely, Ti reduction 
gave rise to the degradation of hardness and tensile strength. By 
contrast, adding alloying elements was applied to improve the strength 
of vanadium alloys, such as Cr [ 13 ], Ta [ 14 , 15 ], and even forming 
vanadium-based high-entropy alloys [ 16 ]. Therefore, increasing Cr 
concentration was considered to enhance the strength of the V-Cr-Ti 
ternary systems with a lower Ti concentration. In the present study, it is 
to clarify lower Ti levels on the microstructure and tensile properties of 
high-purity vanadium alloys with a high-Cr level.
2. Material and methods
High-purity V-10Cr-xTi alloys (x = 0.1 wt%, 0.5 wt%, 1.0 wt%, and 
2.0 wt%) were prepared by arc-melting with high-purity raw materials. 
Ingots were separately sealed in the stainless steel containers and hot 
forged between 1273 K and 1473 K. Then, the hot forged specimens 
were machined to peel off the outside steel. Warm and cold rolling was 
carried out to obtain the sheets with about 0.25 mm in thickness. Discs 
with 3-mm diameter and miniature tensile test specimens with a gauge 
size of 5 mm (length) × 1.2 mm (width) were punched out. Prior to

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 shows SEM micrographs of high-purity V-10Cr-xTi alloys after 
annealing at 1273 K for 1 h, and the associated average diameter of 
grains versus Ti concentration. Almost equiaxial grains are observed in 
all the specimens, suggesting that recrystallization is complete. The 
average grain diameter is not significantly altered up to 1 wt% Ti, 
whereas it decreases to 13.4 ± 2.2 μ m in V-10Cr-2Ti, which is similar to 
the high-purity V-4Cr-2Ti alloy [ 7 ], indicating that 2 wt% Ti addition 
evidently retards the grain growth during annealing and results in the 
grain refinement.
Fig. 2 shows the TEM micrographs of the annealed high-purity V- 
10Cr-xTi alloys. No precipitates can be seen in V-10Cr-0.1Ti, while 
precipitates are formed with 0.5 wt% and higher Ti addition, suggesting 
that precipitates formation is dependent on Ti concentration. 0.5 wt% 
Ti, lower than 1 wt%, is necessary to scavenge the interstitial impurities 
in these high-purity vanadium alloys, which is consistent with the pre -
vious study that Ti(CON) precipitates would be formed with Ti con -
centration between 0.3 wt% and 1 wt% in V-4Cr-xTi alloys [ 9 ].

At room temperature, the ultimate tensile stress gradually increases 
from 366 ± 8 MPa to 419 ± 8 MPa as Ti concentration rises to 2 wt%,

total elongation and uniform elongation do not depict evident depen -

which are 38.5 ± 4.2 % and 16.0 ± 2.1 %, indicating more significant

elongation and uniform elongation slightly decreases.

strength, and would be alternative candidates containing higher Cr and 
lower Ti concentrations with enhanced low-activation characteristics. 
However, tensile strength is not sufficient, and other properties are also 
necessary, such as creep properties, irradiation resistance, corrosion 
resistance and so on.
4. Conclusions
Effects of lower Ti addition with a level of 0.1 – 2 wt% on micro -
structure and tensile properties of high-purity V-10Cr-xTi alloys are 
studied.
(1) Precipitation significantly depends on the Ti concentration. 0.5 
wt% Ti seems to be needed to form Ti(CON) precipitates in the 
high-purity vanadium alloys and maintain the precipitation 
strengthening. 2 wt% Ti addition retards grain growth during 
annealing.
(2) Tensile strength is enhanced with increasing Ti concentration at 
both room temperature and 973 K, and elongation is not obvi -
ously altered in the Ti concentration range of 0.5 – 2 wt%. 
Compared with the room temperature, Ti addition is more 
beneficial to improve the strength at 973 K.
(3) V-10Cr-0.5Ti, V-10Cr-1Ti, and V-10Cr-2Ti alloys could be alter -
natives to the high-purity V-4Cr-4Ti alloy from the viewpoint of 
comparable strength and improved low-activation 
characteristics.
