abstract
The effect of V addition on the microstructural evolution, martensitic transformation be-
haviors and mechanical performances of Ti eNieNb shape memory alloys was systemat-
ically investigated. The results reveal that the phase remains almost equal in Ti eNieNbeV
shape memory alloys, irrespective of the V content. With increasing V content, the amount
of b-Nb phase is increased, which further leads to a reduction in the interspace and width
of the b-Nb phase. When the V content changes, Ti eNieNbeV shape memory alloys
possess a single stage B2 #B19/C19martensitic transformation. In addition, the martensitic
transformation temperatures ﬁrst increase and then decrease owing to the variation in the
chemical composition of the TiNi matrix and microstructural features as the V content
increases. The higher yield strength of Ti eNieNb shape memory alloys can be obtained by
adding a moderate V content. In conclusion, the lower martensitic transformation tem-
perature and the higher yield strength can be tailored by optimizing the V content in Ti eNi
eNb shape memory alloys, which would further widen their application ﬁelds.

1. Introduction
Shape memory alloys (SMAs) are advanced intelligent mate-
rials that integrate temperature sensing and intelligent driving
based on the shape memory effect and superelasticity [1,2]. As a
smart material, shape memory alloys have been widely adop-
ted in automobile applications, aerospace ﬁelds and so on
[3e5]. To date, TieNieNb shape memory alloys have been used
as the original materials of coupling and sealing parts owing to
their wide hysteresis [3,6,7]. In addition to the inherent nature

yield strength is also necessary to guarantee the contact reli-
ability of Ti eNieNb shape memory alloy tube couplings sys-
tems [ 8]. In addition, the lower martensitic transformation
temperature ( Ms) is an important parameter for Ti eNieNb
shape memory alloys when they act as connectors or fasteners
[9]. Hence, both the lower martensitic transformation temper-
ature ( Ms) and higher yield strength for Ti eNieNb shape
memory alloys need to be considered.
To achieve high yield strength TieNieNb shape memory al-
loys, several measures, such alloying and thermo-mechanical
treatment, have been adopted [ 8,10e14]. The quaternary ele-
ments including Mo, Co, Zr, etc. have been added into TieNieNb
shape memory alloys. The results reveal that the improvement
of yield strength and maximum completely recoverable strain of
TieNieNb shape memory alloys can be simultaneously attained
by adding moderate Co [8,12]. The highest yield stress of about
650 MPa and the largest completely recoverable strain of 7.8%
can be tailored by optimizing 1.0 at.%Co addition in Ti eNieNb
based shape memory alloys. In addition, Co addition can
decrease the martensitic transformation temperature [8]. As Co
content is increased from 0 at.% to 2.0 at.%, the martensitic

not only results in a decrease in the martensitic transformation
temperature but can also achieve a higher yield strength and a
maximum complete recovery strain of 8% [ 11]. The micro-
structure of TieNieNb shape memory alloys undergoing equal
channel angular pressing (ECAP) features ultraﬁne grains.
Compared with the original Ti eNieNb shape memory alloys,
ECAPed Ti eNieNb shape memory alloys show a lower
martensitic transformation temperature and improved yield
strength, which is ascribed to the reﬁnement of grains and the
introduction of a higher density of dislocations [14].
Recently, it has been revealed that V addition can improve
the mechanical and functional properties of shape memory
alloys to some extent. For instance, substitution of Ti with V
enhanced the functional properties and increased the
martensitic transformation temperature of Ti ePt high-
temperature shape memory alloys [ 15]. V doping could
signiﬁcantly improve the mechanical properties of brittle
NieMn-Ga shape memory alloys as a result of forming a
ductile g phase [ 16]. The mechanical properties and shape
memory effect of CueAleNi high-temperature shape memory
alloys can be advanced by adding V element [ 17].
Hence, V addition into Ti eNieNb shape memory alloys is
attempted to improve its yield strength and hold a lower
martensitic transformation temperature. In addition, the effect
of V content on the microstructural evolution, martensitic
transformation temperature and kinetics and mechanical per-
formance of TieNieNb shape memory alloys was investigated.
2. Experiments
In the present study, a series of (Ti 44Ni47Nb9)100-x-Vx (x ¼ 0, 0.5,
1.0, 2.0, 3.0 at.%) shape memory alloys were fabricated in a
non-consumable arc-melting furnace under the protection of
higher-purity Ar. The raw materials of 99.95 wt% sponge Ti,
99.9 wt% electrolytic Ni, 99.9 wt% Nb and 99.95 wt% dendritic
crystal V were adopted. To obtain a uniform chemical
composition, TieNieNbeV alloys were remelted at least seven
times. These specimens were homogenized at 850
/C14 C for
holding for 6 h and then quenched in ice water. The thermally
treated Ti eNieNbeV alloys were hot-rolled into a 1.5 mm
sheet, which approached 90% deformation. The samples for
the following testing were prepared from hot-rolled sheets via
electro-spark wire electrode cutting. Then, the samples were
solution treated at 900
/C14 C for 3 h. The microstructural features
and chemical composition as well as the fracture images of
TieNieNbeV alloys were characterized by scanning electron
microscopy (SEM) equipped with energy-dispersive spectros-
copy (EDS). The phase constituents of Ti eNieNbeV alloys
were analyzed by X-ray diffraction (XRD). The martensitic
transformation behaviors were conducted by using differen-
tial scanning calorimetry (DSC) with a heating and cooling rate
of 10
/C14 C/min. The mechanical properties were characterized
by tensile testing at room temperature.

3.3. Mechanical properties
Fig. 7 (a) shows the room temperature tensile curves of
TieNieNbeV shape memory alloys with different V contents.
The results reveal that stress estrain curves exhibit four
different stages, which is frequently observed in common
TieNi-based shape memory alloys. Moreover, the above XRD
patterns and DSC results prove that the Ti eNieNbeV shape
memory alloys are in an austenite state at room temperature,
irrespective of V content. Hence, the deformation stages in the
present Ti eNieNbeV shape memory alloys can be attributed
to the elastic deformation of the austenite phase (stage I), the
plateau corresponding to the stress-induced B2 /B19/C19
martensitic transformation and plastic deformation of the b-
Nb phase (stage II), the reorientation of stress-induced
martensite and continuous stress-induced martensitic trans-
formation (stage III) and the last stage of plastic deformation
of B19/C19martensite (stage IV). The difference is that the V con-
tent has an inﬂuence on the mechanical properties. The
dependence of tensile yield stress and elongation on the V
content for quaternary Ti eNieNbeV shape memory alloys is
shown in Fig. 7 (b). It should be noted that the elongation of
TieNieNbeV shape memory alloys continuously decreases
with increasing V content. When the V content increases from
0 at.% to 3 at.%, the elongation is reduced from 28.6% to 10.9%.

TieNieNbeMo shape memory alloys [ 11]. In Ti eNieNbeMo
shape memory alloys, with increasing of Mo content, the

4. Conclusions
(1) V addition can promote the precipitation of theb-Nb phase
in Ti eNieNbeV shape memory alloys, which further
causes a reduction in the interface between the adjacentb-
Nb phase and the width of the continuousb-Nb phase.
(2) As the V content changes, a single-step B2 #B19/C19
martensitic transformation is observed during heating
and cooling of the Ti eNieNbeV shape memory alloy. In
addition, martensitic transformation temperatures ﬁrst
increase and then decrease with increasing V content.
(3) The higher yield strength in Ti eNieNbeV shape mem-
ory alloys can be optimized by changing the V content.
However, V addition results in a successive decrease in
the elongation of Ti eNieNb shape memory alloys.
