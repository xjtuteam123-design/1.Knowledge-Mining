ABSTRACT 
The face-centered cubic single-phase CoCrFeMnNi equi-atomic HEA has excellent mechanical properties in a 
wide temperature range, from cryogenic to room temperature. However, the low yield strength at room tem -
perature limits its application as a structural material. Herein, we propose a novel heterostructuring method 
named laser surface relaxation (LSR) as a strategy to overcome the strength-ductility dilemma. Unlike conven -
tional heterostructuring methods, the LSR method can freely tune the microstructure with spatial heterogeneity. 
The LSR-treated CoCrFeMnNi HEA exhibited exceptional mechanical properties with a synergetic strengthening 
effect. 
1. Introduction 
Over the past few decades, high entropy alloys (HEAs) based on the 
mixing of multi-principal elements with equi-atomic or near equi-atomic 
compositions have drawn considerable attention as a new class of 
metallic materials [ 1 – 4 ]. With its unique characteristics, such as single 
crystal structure, sluggish diffusion, and heavy lattice distortion, HEAs 
exhibit excellent mechanical properties and have become highly 
promising candidates for next-generation structural materials [ 5 , 6 ]. 
Among the numerous HEA systems, the equi-atomic composition of Co, 
Cr, Fe, Mn, and Ni, commonly referred to as cantor alloys, is the most 
extensively studied HEA [ 7 – 9 ]. The CoCrFeMnNi equi-atomic HEA with 
a single face-centered cubic (FCC) structure has been demonstrated to 
exhibit excellent mechanical properties over a wide temperature range 
from cryogenic (77 K) to room temperature (298 K) owing to the 
deformation mechanism by deformation twinning [ 10 , 11 ]. However, 
the equi-atomic CoCrFeMnNi HEA has a drawback of relatively low yield 
strength (YS) at room temperature mainly attributed to the FCC struc -
tural characteristics of the alloy [ 12 , 13 ]. Therefore, its application as a 
structural material is limited despite its reasonable mechanical 
properties. 
Meanwhile, tailoring microstructures and mechanical properties of 
developed materials in the desired direction is an endless aspiration in 
the materials science community [ 14 , 15 ]. The heterostructuring method 
is one of the most efficient strategies for overcoming the 
strength-ductility trade-off dilemma by controlling the microstructure 
[ 16 , 17 ]. Heterostructured materials accumulate geometrically neces -
sary dislocations (GNDs) to maintain strain continuity between the 
heterogeneous domains during yielding and plastic deformation, 
resulting in a synergetic strengthening effect due to the partitioning of 
plastic strain and the development of back stress [ 18 , 19 ]. In particular, 
the gradient distributions of stress and strain caused by the elastic/ -
plastic interaction between incompatible regions during yielding 
contribute considerably to the improvement in YS [ 20 , 21 ]. 
Therefore, in this study, we proposed a novel heterostructuring 
method to customize microstructures with spatial heterogeneity using 
laser surface relaxation (LSR) on cold-rolled plates. Unlike conventional 
heterostructuring methods (e.g., severe plastic deformation, surface 
modification, and lamination), the LSR method can easily control the 
microstructure and mechanical properties according to the number of 
layers irradiated with the laser [ 22 , 23 ]. Heterostructured CoCrFeMnNi 
HEAs with various strength-ductility combinations were manufactured 
under different process conditions of the LSR method and they exhibited 
superior mechanical properties compared to conventional homogeneous 
CoCrFeMnNi HEAs.

3. Results and discussions 
As shown in Table 1 and Fig. 1 (a – c), LSR-treated CoCrFeMnNi HEA 
was successfully fabricated with a homogeneous composition in all re -
gions without segregation. The inverse pole figure (IPF) maps show that 
all the LSR-treated specimens have the characteristics of hetero -
structured grain size distributions, with coarse grains near the surface 
and finer grains near the center ( Fig. 1 (d – f)). Grain boundaries and

the coarse-grained surface and fine-grained center of the LSR-treated 
samples is primarily related to the heat absorbed from the surface 
area; this is in contrast with the conventional heat treatment methods 
wherein energy is imparted from all directions. The reduced heat input 
from the surface to the center decreases the driving force of micro -
structural evolutions leading to recovery, recrystallization, and grain 
growth, resulting in gradient heterostructured microstructures [ 24 ]. 
The 1L specimen has four microstructural characteristics due to the 
difference in energy absorbed in the surface and center areas: coarse- 
grained (CG) region near the surface with sufficient driving force for 
recrystallization and grain growth, fine-grained (FG) and ultra-fine- 
grained (UFG) regions between the surface and the center with suffi -
cient recrystallization driving force but lacking grain growth driving 
force, and partially recrystallized (PR) region in the center with negli -

exhibits a unique reverse-gradient microstructure with a CG surface and 
a FG center. Such a reverse-gradient microstructure can be advanta -

gradient direction.

in an early plastic deformation of the soft CG region by activation of the 
dislocation source, leading to strain gradients [ 36 ]. Furthermore, the 
uniaxial stress applied to the heterostructured material is converted to a 
multiaxial state near the interface due to the strength heterogeneity, 
resulting in the activation of more slip systems in the soft domains [ 18 , 
21 , 37 ]. As the tensile deformation progressed, the overall trend of strain 
distribution increased while strain partitioning and shear bands per -
sisted [ 38 ]. 
The strain partitioning observed during plastic deformation is mainly 
due to the mechanical incompatibility between domains with different 
mechanical properties [ 39 ]. To maintain clear gaps in the strain distri -
butions occurring at the contact interfaces of the soft/hard domains, 
GNDs are generated near the interfaces of the heterogeneous domains 
during deformation. Fig. 5 shows the IPF maps ( Fig. 5 (a, c, e)) and GND 
maps ( Fig. 5 (b, d, f)) of the deformed microstructures at 10% applied 
strain. It was observed that the lower the number of irradiated layers and 
the smaller grain size, the higher the GND densities. This is because the 
grain boundaries interfere with dislocation motion, resulting in an 
accumulation of dislocations [ 40 ]. Nevertheless, the GND maps in the 
deformed state evidently indicate that more GNDs accumulate near the 
domain interfaces with heterogeneous grain sizes. These results suggest 
that in LSR-treated samples with heterostructured microstructures, the 
plastic incompatibility near the contact interfaces due to the clear gaps 
of strain partitioning was compensated by the accumulation of GNDs. 
4. Conclusions 
In summary, we developed a novel heterostructuring method to 
fabricate materials with spatially heterogeneous grain size distributions 
by subjecting the cold-rolled plate to laser irradiation. Unlike general 
heat treatment methods, the LSR method absorbs energy from the sur -
face area of the cold-rolled sheet; therefore, the driving force for 
microstructural evolutions decreases towards the center. As the total 
amount of absorbed energy varies with the number of layers irradiated, 
the LSR method can produce various combinations of microstructures 
with spatial heterogeneity. The heterostructured CoCrFeMnNi HEAs 
with different microstructures prepared using LSR method exhibited 
superior strength-ductility combinations owing to the synergetic 
strengthening effect.
