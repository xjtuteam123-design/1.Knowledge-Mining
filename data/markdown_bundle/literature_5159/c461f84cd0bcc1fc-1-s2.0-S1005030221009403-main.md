In this study, single-phase NbTiZr and NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) were investigated 
for their use in biomedical implants. The alloys were prepared by arc melting, and were then cold-rolled, 
annealed, and characterized in terms of phase analysis, mechanical properties, fractography, and wear 
resistance. Both alloys showed a single body-centered cubic phase with superior mechanical, and tri- 
bological properties compared to commercially available biomedical alloys. Mo and Ta-containing MEAs 
showed higher tensile yield strength (1060 ± 18 MPa)) and higher tensile ductility ( ∼20%), thus over- 
coming the strength–ductility trade-off with no signs of transformation-induced plasticity, twinning, or 
precipitation. The generalized stacking fault energy (GSFE) calculations on the {112} < 111 > slip system by 
the ﬁrst-principles calculations based on density functional theory showed that the addition of less than 
0.2 molar fraction of Mo and Ta lowers the GSFE curves. This behavior posits the increase in ductility of 
the alloy by facilitating slips although strength is also increased by solid solution strengthening. The wear 
resistance of both alloys against hardened steel surfaces was superior to that of commercial biomedical 
alloys. Thus, we concluded that NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 MEA with good tensile ductility is a potential candidate 
for biomedical implants.

1. Introduction 
Alloys with multi-principal components, commonly known as 
high-entropy alloys (HEAs) or complex concentrated alloys (CCAs), 
offer a vast compositional space for their application-speciﬁc de- 
sign [ 1 , 2 ]. Among these HEAs, body-centered cubic (BCC) HEAs 
consisting of Ti, Zr, Nb, Mo, Ta, Hf, W, and V have shown ex- 
ceptional properties, such as high strength at room temperature 
and high temperatures [3–12] . Most of the elements in BCC HEAs 
are refractory elements that also show adequate biocompatibility. 
Currently, Ti alloys serve as biomedical implants because of their 
superior mechanical and electrochemical properties and suﬃcient 
biocompatibility [13–15] . However, Ti alloys have poor wear re- 
sistance, which makes them unfavorable for long-term usage in- 
side the host body [16] . Researchers have focused on a member of 
the BCC HEA family for biomedical applications owing to its su- 
perior biocompatibility over commercially available biomedical al- 
loys: HEAs based on a MoNbTaTiZr composition [17–22] . The alloy

bearing structural applications, such as biomedical implants. Only 
one equiatomic BCC HEA so far, i.e. HfNbTaTiZr, has been exam-

them less competitive than existing commercial alloys [25–27] .

2. Materials and method 
NbTiZr and NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 alloys were arc-melted followed by 
multiple processing steps of re-melting, cold-rolled at a reduc- 
tion ratio of 40%, and homogenized at 10 0 0 °C for 4 h and fur- 
nace cooled. Phase analysis was performed using X-ray diffraction 
(XRD), electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD), and transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM). Preliminary mechanical testing was 
performed using nanoindentation. Deformed and strained samples 
were examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), EBSD, and 
TEM. Samples with sub-sized standards (ASTM E8) were cut us- 
ing electric discharge machining. Tensile samples were sprayed 
ﬁrst with white paint and then with a low-density black sprin- 
kler to generate a stochastic pattern on the surface. The strain was 
obtained using the digital image correlation method. Customized 
jigs were used to hold the sample using Instron 8848. Electron 
backscattered diffraction (EBSD) analysis was performed, using TSL 
Hikari Super, for unstrained and strained samples. After 5% and 10% 
strain, the tensile tests were stopped and the samples were taken 
for EBSD analysis. 
2.1. Wear test 
A pin-on-disk sliding wear test was used to evaluate the tribo- 
logical response of the MEAs and commercial alloys. The wear test 
samples were used in the form of a pin (4 × 6 mm). 15 kgf load 
was applied on the pin while sliding its one ﬂat end at the surface 
of a hardened steel (SKD61) disk. Surface roughness of pins and 
disk was measured by optical surface proﬁler (m-surf by Nano Fo- 
cus). The pin would cover an 1800 m distance in 3600 s. The mass 
loss was measured by a ﬁve decimal precision weigh balance. In 
order to make a precise comparison, the same set of conditions 
were used to evaluate commercial biomedical alloys like 316 L and 
CP-Ti. 
2.2. Calculation of stacking fault energies 
The lattice constants and the generalized stacking fault en- 
ergy (GSFE) curves on the {112} < 111 > slip system of the BCC 
NbTiZr(MoTa) x alloys were calculated by the ﬁrst-principles cal-

cial quasirandom structures (SQS) [36] . {112} < 111 > slip system is 
speciﬁcally considered here as it is the most frequent slip system 
observed in BCC multiple component alloys [37] .

alloys. The SQS-60vac supercells were made with three orthogo-

3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 3 (a) shows the XRD patterns for the two types of MEAs. 
Both samples showed a single BCC phase whose peaks were 
slightly shifted, similar to previous studies of NbTiZr [ 17 , 20 , 28 ]. 
The lattice parameter of the equiatomic MEA measured from XRD 
decreased from 341 pm (NbTiZr) to 339 pm (NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 ) with 
the addition of Mo and Ta. The EBSD and SEM images provided 
a rough idea of the large grain size of the sample as shown in 
Fig. 3 (b). Mo has the smallest atomic radius among all the con- 
stituents, and its combination with each of the other elements 
showed the most negative enthalpy among all other pairs as re- 
ported in previous work [21] . This caused a reduction in lattice 
parameters of NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 . 
The mechanical properties of both MEAs were demonstrated by 
nanoindentation, wear tests, and tensile tests. Alloying in ternary 
MEAs by Mo and Ta showed a signiﬁcant solid-solution strength- 
ening effect, as seen in the P-h curves of the MEAs ( Fig. 4 (a)). 
The addition of Mo and Ta, as reported earlier, provided signif- 
icant strengthening; however, there was no alloy in the previ- 
ous study reporting any tensile behavior [ 19 , 21 , 23 ]. The elastic 
moduli of the MEAs, calculated from nanoindentation using Oliver 
and Pharr’s method [56] , are ∼90 and ∼105 GPa for NbTiZr and 
NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 respectively. Although the Mo and Ta addition in- 
creased the elastic modulus of the ternary MEAs, the value of 
NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 is a little less than that of CP-Ti (110 GPa). 
A wear test was performed for the MEAs under investigation 
and two commercially available biomedical alloys, 316 L stainless 
steel and commercially pure Ti (grade-4), usually known as CP-Ti. 
It is important to have a smooth and same surface roughness to 
measure and compare the wear resistance of alloys precisely. The 
surface roughness of all the pin samples and hardened steel disk 
was less than 4 μm, which is a good value to rely on the wear 
test results, as shown in Fig. 4 (c). The graph in Fig. 4 (b) shows the 
weight loss in the samples after the wear test in terms of wear rate 
(cm 3 /m). The MEAs have noticeably higher wear resistance than 
those of the commercial alloys. Immense solid solution strength- 
ening imparted from the multiple principal components improved 
the tribological response of the alloys. The weight loss can be jus- 
tiﬁed from shape and height of the samples as shown in Fig. 4 (b). 
MEAs did not form a mushroom-like shape after the wear test, un- 
like 316 L and CP-Ti. Not many studies have reported on the wear 
characteristics of HEAs or MEAs; the study on Al x Co 1.5 CrFeNi 1.5 Ti y 
[57] is one of the few. Another study discussed the addition of Mo 
in an AlCoCrFe x Mo 0.5 Ni HEA and the effect of Fe in the matrix on 
the wear behavior [58] . Such HEAs have shown superior wear re- 
sistance against the surface of hardened steels. Nonetheless, wear 
behavior related to biomedical implants requires further investiga- 
tion. The coatings of TiTaHfNbZr HEAs over Ti-6Al-4 V alloys have 
shown promising tribological properties owing to lower wear loss 
[16] . 
Several BCC HEAs and MEAs have already shown enough plastic 
strain under compression. However, the formability and ductility of 
the alloy can be accurately depicted by the tensile stress-strain be- 
havior. The ternary MEA showed approximately 13.3 ± 4.5% plas- 
tic strain and approximately 783 ± 13 MPa yield strength under 
the tensile test, as shown in Fig. 5 (a). However, the MEA with 0.1 
Mo and Ta showed not only a signiﬁcant increase in yield strength 
(1060 ± 18 MPa) but also enhanced ductility i.e., 15.5 ± 5.4%, 
thus overcoming the strength–ductility trade-off. Arc melted alloys 
have a bigger grain size ( > 200 μm) as can be seen from SEM im- 
ages, henceforth the effect from the Hall-Petch relationship can be 
ignored. The NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 alloy is thus superior to previously 
reported MEAs and HEAs concerning a combination of high me- 
chanical properties such as strength, ductility, and wear resistance. 
Moreover, the elastic moduli measured from the tensile stress- 
strain curve were found to be 74.5 ± 0.4 and 91.2 ± 0.3 for NbTiZr 
and NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 , respectively, values that were less than those

Fig. 5 (b) shows the fractography of the samples, showing a typ- 
ical dimple-like morphology. A greater number of slip bands with 
wavy patterns was seen in the NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 alloy than in the 
NbTiZr alloy. Numerous micropores and typical ductile deforma- 
tion features are also evident in both samples, which absorbed 
stress during plastic deformation and increased the yield strength 
and ductility. It can be noticed that BCC MEAs do not show any 
distinct deformation mechanisms other than those shown by con- 
ventional BCC alloys. The ternary MEA exhibited strain softening 
at the beginning, as shown by the arrow in the magniﬁed region 
( Fig. 3 (c)) similar to a recently published study [34] . The reason 
for strain-softening is may not be due to smaller grain size as 
the result are consistent with the previous study where the sam- 
ples have smaller grain sizes due to excessive cold rolling (50%) 
and low-temperature homogenization (800 °C for 1 h). Henceforth, 
the strain-softening observed in NbTiZr [34] is might be its in- 
nate characteristics. The slip is localized in this alloy and there is 
no multiplication of dislocations. However, the elongation is ho- 
mogeneous for Mo- and Ta-containing alloy up to 15% strain and 
there is no more strain-softening. Although the value of strain 
hardening rate is not considerably high, however, Mo and Ta ad- 
dition did eliminate the strain-softening effect and resulted in an 
increase of both strength and ductility by delocalizing the slips 
and due to multiplications of dislocations. A signiﬁcant stretched 
zone before the initiation of the crack was seen. These results 
were supported by the EBSD images of unstrained, 5, and 10% 
strained MEA samples ( Fig. 5 (d)). There was no sign of twinning 
or transformation-induced plasticity from the EBSD images. Grain 
fragmentation and low-angle misorientation were observed in the 
strained samples. Furthermore, a greater number of slip bands was 
observed in the 10% strained sample of the NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 alloy 
than in the NbTiZr MEA. The observed planar and banded structure 
was similar to that of a TiZrHfNb alloy studied previously [59] . A 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM) image of the 10% strained 
(via tension) NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 samples is shown in Fig. 6 (a). A com- 
pletely homogenized matrix along with some wavy dislocations 
was observed in the elemental map ( Fig. 6 (b)). Wavy dislocations 
in BCC alloys were the result of recurring cross-slips along with 
the active slip system. The chemical complexity of the matrix also 
tends to make the moving dislocation wavy in shape [60] . Ow- 
ing to the three-dimensional intricacy of the core structure, dis- 
locations showed lower mobility and higher innate Peierls barri- 
ers that govern the ﬂow response of BCC metals [61] . No precip- 
itates or any other kind of deformation were observed. These re- 
sults showed that additions of 0.1 M each of Mo and Ta in NbTiZr 
facilitated slip at higher stresses. The increase in both stress and 
elongation was observed in NbTiZr(MoTa) 0.1 , overcoming the con- 
ventional dilemma of strength–ductility without any additional de- 
formation mechanism. 
GSFE is an important aspect to understand the movement of 
dislocations and investigate deformation mechanisms. The brittle- 
ness of bcc elements like W and its alloys is associated with their 
high GSFE of the most frequent slip systems like {110} ⟨ 111 ⟩ and 
{112} ⟨ 111 ⟩ [62] . When Ru and Mo-like elements are added into W, 
the ductilization is achieved by decreasing the GSFE of the afore- 
mentioned slip systems. The main slip systems of conventional 
bcc metals and alloys are {110} ⟨ 111 ⟩ and {112} ⟨ 111 ⟩ , nevertheless, 
the latter ({112} ⟨ 111 ⟩ ) is found to be a more frequent slip system 
for dislocation glides in multiprincipal component alloys or HEAs 
[ 63 , 37 ]. Henceforth, in current work {112} < 111 > slip system was 
considered for GSFE calculations of BCC HEA.

the measured experimental lattice constants for the NbTiZr(MoTa) x

curves and γusf of NbTiZr(MoTa) x system are shown in Fig. 7 . The 
trends in all the cases except NR show that GSFE is decreasing ﬁrst 
when Mo and Ta are added ( x = 0.167) and then start increasing 
again. The decrease of SFE in the W-X (Mo, Re, Mo, Os) system was 
explained based on the increased atom pairs of positive mixing en- 
thalpies [62] . In the NbTiZr(MoTa) x system, major binary combina- 
tions of constituent elements except with Mo (i.e., Zr-Mo, Ti-Mo, 
and Nb-Mo) have positive mixing enthalpies [21] . The mixing en- 
thalpies of important pairs are presented in Fig. 8 (a). The combina- 
tions of Ta with other elements (Nb, Ti, Zr) also show positive en- 
thalpies as given in Fig. 8 (a) thus help decrease the SFE similar to 
mentioned in W-X (Mo, Re, Mo, Os) studies [62] . It is probable that 
the decrease in SFE originates from a local variation of Ta-Ti, Zr- 
Ta, Zr-Nb, Ti-Nb pairs that have repulsive interactions unlike Mo-X 
pairs, which have attractive interactions. However, as the Mo and 
Ta concentrations increase, the amounts of Nb, Ti and Zr reduce as 
well, and it will contribute to the increase of SFE. The reason why 
the slight addition of Mo and Ta resulted in a minimized SFE is a 
question that needs to be investigated further. 
A recent investigation on electronic structure and multiatomic 
bonding in equiatomic HEAs gives us an insight that there is an 
unusual statistical distribution of partial bond order density from 
each pair that contributes to the total bond order [64] . It also 
showed Mo-Ta and Ti-Ta have a relatively larger partial bond order 
density in most of the alloys, which certainly does not follow the 
rule of mixture law but their bond order density. Also, the atomic 
bonding, attractive and repulsive nature of the bond that can be 
estimated from their mixing enthalpy sign, plays an important role 
in decreasing or increasing the SFE of the system as reported [37] . 
Positive mixing enthalpy will cause repulsive interaction hence- 
forth decreasing the SFE of the system and vice versa. Mo-Ta has 
negative mixing enthalpy and Ti-Ta has a positive mixing enthalpy 
[21] . The speculation is that Ti-Ta pairs in TiZrNb(MoTa) 0.1 play an 
important role in decreasing USFE. However, a detailed study is 
needed to analyze the case in depth.
