Yang Wang, Bo Zhu, Shanjin Lv, Lijun Yang
(Dept. of Mechanical Manufacture and its Automation, Harbin Institute of Technology)
ABSTRACT
High temperature alloy is a complex alloy with excellent high temperature strength, thermal stability and fatigue
property, which can work in 6OO—lOOOC oxidizing condition. However, it has poor machinability. This article
present experimental research of laser assisted cutting of GH4169 high temperature alloy. Comparative experimental
research between conventional cutting and laser assisted cutting is done to detect the cutting force variation curve
with cutting depth and cutting speed. The experiment results show that cutting forces in laser assisted cutting are
evidently reduced by approximately 50%, and tool wear is reduced leading to longer tool life.
Keywords GH4169 high temperature alloy, laser, heat assisted cutting
1. INTRODUCTION
High temperature alloy, a complex alloy with excellent high temperature strength, thermal stability and fatigue
property which can work in 600— lOOOC oxidizing condition, is now widely applied in aeronautics, astronautics,
shipbuilding and other industries. However, its poor machinability results in severe deformation, 1.5 times as great as
that of 45steel and severe work hardening tendency leading to a level at 2OO5OO%. Moreover, the cutting tool is
prone to wear in the cutting of high temperature alloy because of the severe work hardening of the alloy and the hard
particles in it. Therefore, this causes great difficulty in practical manufacture.
This article presents an experimental research of the cutting of GH4169 high temperature alloy with laser assisted
cutting method. GH4169 is a nickel base alloy which keeps high strength at 800t. Therefore, laser assisted cutting
of GH4169 demands a working temperature above 8OOC to obtain the heat softening effect. The determination of
laser parameters and cutting parameters in laser assisted cutting process is presented in this article on the base of
which comparative experimental research has been done between conventional cutting and laser assisted cutting with
different parameters. Fairly good experimental results have been achieved.
2. STUDY OF CUTTING TECHNOLOGY
Both cutting parameters and laser parameters have to be properly selected to achieve desirable cutting effects, or the
results will be bad with increased cutting forces, shorter cutting tool life and worse machined surface quality.

With laser beam spot position determined, laser scanning speed can be determined with finite element analysis
method from the delay time.
2.2 Determination of cutting parameters
Cutting parameters includes cutting speed, cutting depth and amount of feed.
1) Determination of cutting feed
Cutting speed or laser beam scanning speed can be determined described as above.
2) Determination of amount of feed
Amount of feed can be determined according to laser beam spot size. When laser beam spot diameter and cutting tool
plan angle are fixed, maximal amount of feed can be computed according to formula (2).
3) Determination of cutting depth
Maximal cutting depth can be determined according to the maximal softening depth attained by the laser beam
directed on the part. As analyzed above with finite element analysis method, when laser parameters are fixed,
maximal temperature at different material depths can be computed, and maximal cutting depth can be selected
according to thermomechanical properties of the material.

4. CONCLUSION
From the above discussion, conclusion can be drawn as follows:
1) It is important to determine proper laser parameters and cutting parameters in laser assisted cutting. Laser scanning
speed, laser heat point position and cutting depth can be determined according to heat transfer delay time with finite
element analysis method. The heat transfer delay time is the time for layers of different depth to attain the maximal
temperature.
2) The cutting forces of GH4169 high temperature alloy are evidently reduced in laser assisted cutting. Main cutting
force is reduced by approximately 50%atproper cutting depth and cutting speed.
3) The tool wear is evidently reduced and tool life lengthened in laser assisted cutting. In the conditions above, wear
of the tool flank can be reduced by approximately 50%.
