ABSTRACT 
The nickel-based superalloys prepared by additive manufacturing (AM) commonly exhibit inferior mechanical 
properties at high temperatures, particularly in terms of ductility and creep resistance, compared to the corre -
sponding conventional manufactured (CM)alloys, and this significantly hinders the widespread application of 
AM nickel-based superalloys in the aerospace industry. To address this problem, a novel nickel-based superalloy 
for additive manufacturing, Hastelloy X-AM (HX-AM), using the yttrium (Y) alloying strategy has been developed 
in this work. The results show that the Y element is present in the matrix as Ni
5
Y phase and yttrium oxide 
nanoparticles after laser additive manufacturing, exerting a significant inhibitory effect on the precipitation and 
coarsening of detrimental second phases at grain boundaries (GBs) during subsequent heat treatment or service 
processes. The HX-AM alloy exhibits an exceptional matching relationship between strength (ultimate tensile 
strength = 315 ± 3 MPa) and ductility (total elongation = 56 ± 1.7 %) at 900 
◦
C. What ’ s more, the creep 
performance of HX-AM alloy is significantly improved simultaneously compared with CM Ni-based alloys at 
900 
◦
C, overcoming the longstanding challenge of inferior creep performance in AM nickel-based superalloys at 
high temperature. The fatigue lifetime of HX-AM alloy reaches 295.6 h at 900 
◦
C/40 MPa, showing over fif -
teenfold enhancement compared to that of CM Hastelloy X alloy. 
1. Introduction 
Due to their excellent oxidation resistance and mechanical properties 
at elevated temperatures, nickel-based superalloys have been widely 
used in the aerospace industry, such as turbine discs, rocket engines, and 
nuclear reactors [ 1 – 3 ]. These nickel components are commonly manu -
factured by CM methods such as casting and forging. In recent years, 
additive manufacturing (AM) has gained increasing attention due to its 
design freedom and dimensional accuracy [ 4 ]. Therefore, this technique 
has been widely explored for nickel-based superalloy fabrication as it 
can manufacture fully dense complex metal parts by selectively melting 
fine metal powders layer by layer using high-energy lasers [ 5 ]. Unlike 
CM methods, the repeated compression-tension cycles resulting from 
local heating/cooling inhomogeneity during the AM process will form 
unique cell structures composed of high-density dislocations within the 
alloy [ 6 – 8 ]. In addition to interfering with dislocations during defor -
mation, cellular structures can enhance the hardening of materials [ 9 ]. 
As a result, AM nickel components have better tensile strength at room 
temperature than those processed in the conventional method [ 10 ]. 
However, the focused laser beam delivers highly localized heat input, 
leading to rapid solidification and significant temperature gradients 
within components during the AM process, resulting in severe residual 
stress levels [ 11 ], internal defects such as cracks and pores [ 12 ], and 
microscopic segregation (compositional inhomogeneity) [ 13 ], which 
can have a devastating impact on the service performance of AM nickel 
components such as creep and fatigue. Moreover, large-sized columnar 
grains with flat grain boundaries (GBs) also deteriorate the ductility of 
AM nickel-based components [ 14 ]. In recent years, various 
post-treatments for AM alloys, such as solution heat treatment and HIP, 
have been developed in order to achieve enhanced comprehensive me -
chanical properties through microstructure homogenization, residual 
stress elimination, and closure of defects like microcracks and poles. The 
ductility of AM alloys can be enhanced to a certain extent [ 15 – 17 ]. 
Unfortunately, the strength of AM nickel components will

enhances grain boundary strength [ 29 , 30 ]. Moreover, it promotes the 
formation of yttrium oxide second phase nanoparticles with excellent 
thermal stability, which hinders GB migration and sliding, ultimately 
leading to improved creep resistance [ 31 , 32 ].

Y-alloying strategy, a novel nickel-based alloy powder suitable for laser 
powder bed fusion (LPBF) has been developed, namely HX-AM. This 
strategy not only ensures that the addition of Y will not affect the final 
powder quality, in terms of both fluidity and sphericality, but more 
importantly, the in situ synthesized Y

2.1. Materials 
Two types of gas-atomized Hastelloy X powder material with

HX-AM powders was measured by Optical Emission Spectrometer

2.3. LPBF process and heat treatment 
The prepared powders were subjected to LPBF using a Concept Laser 
M2 printer equipped with a 400 W fiber laser source. The optimized 
LPBF printing parameters were a laser power of 300 W, a scanning speed 
of 1100 mm/s, a hatch spacing of 75 μ m, and a layer thickness of 50 μ m. 
The strip scanning strategy was implemented in conjunction with a 67
◦

◦

2.4. Microstructural characterization 
Microstructural investigations were conducted utilizing an optical 
microscope (OM) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) combined 
with energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) and electron backscatter

500 μ m, with a step size of 1.3 μ m. The SEM samples underwent elec -
trolytic etching in a solution comprising 20 % perchloric acid and 80 % 
ethanol at a voltage of 20 V for 15 s. To identify phases and characterize 
microstructures, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analyses were 
carried out using a Tecnai G2 F30 S-TWIN microscope operating at 300 
kV. Standard ion-milling with a Gatan PIPS II 695 instrument was used

4. Conclusions 
This work presents the design, characterization and properties of a 
novel nickel-based superalloy tailored for laser additive manufacturing. 
The fine Y
2
O
3 
nanoparticles and Ni
5
Y phase formed during the LPBF 
process maintain a coherent interface with the matrix, effectively 
pinning dislocations and enhancing strength. Moreover, Y
2
O
3

HX-AM matrix. During heat treatment or high-temperature service, the 
precipitation and coarsening of detrimental second-phase at the GBs are 
suppressed and the purification of GBs is improved in the HX-AM alloy, 
thus effectively suppressing its GB embrittlement. The results show that 
the HX-AM alloy simultaneously exhibits exceptional strength-ductility 
matching and excellent creep resistance at a high temperature of 900
