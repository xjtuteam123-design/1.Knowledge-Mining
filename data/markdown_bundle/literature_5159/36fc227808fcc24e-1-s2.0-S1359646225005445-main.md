ABSTRACT
Understanding recrystallization in Mo-Re alloys is important for their engineering performance. In this study, 
quasi-in situ EBSD was employed to investigate recrystallization nucleation in rotary-swaged Mo-5 wt.% Re 
alloys at 1200 
◦
C. Subgrain coarsening is found to be a key mechanism of recrystallization nucleation, either 
acting alone or in combination with strain-induced boundary migration. Statistical analysis indicates that the 
boundary migration rate depends on both misorientation and curvature. As a result, small/narrow domains 
bounded by high-angle boundaries shrink first while their neighbors coarsen. The role of dislocation walls and 
low-angle boundaries on driving recrystallization, as well as on pinning boundary migrating, is discussed. This 
work provides new insights into the recrystallization mechanisms of refractory alloys.
Molybdenum (Mo) alloys are pivotal in aerospace, nuclear energy, 
semiconductor technologies, and high-temperature structural applica -
tions owing to their outstanding thermal stability, creep resistance, and 
oxidation tolerance [ 1 – 3 ]. Rhenium (Re) additions tend to raise the 
recrystallization onset temperature in Mo-Re systems, thereby altering 
recrystallization behavior and high-temperature mechanical properties 
[ 4 – 6 ]. Given the extreme service environments of Mo-Re alloys [ 7 – 9 ], 
microstructural uniformity is critical for their engineering performance 
under ultra-high temperature conditions. Therefore, it is necessary to 
investigate the recrystallization mechanisms in Mo-Re alloys.
It is well known that recrystallization plays a crucial role in metallic 
processing [ 10 ]. Modern recrystallization theories have conventionally 
focused on both nucleation and recrystallizing grain growth [ 10 – 13 ]. 
However, the nucleation stage is particularly decisive in setting the final 
microstructure of refractory alloys. While prior studies have reported 
various nucleation mechanisms, such as subgrain coalescence [ 14 – 16 ] 
and strain-induced boundary migration (SIBM) [ 17 , 18 ], their relative 
contributions in Mo-Re alloys remain ambiguous. In refractory material 
systems, the exceptionally high recrystallization temperatures and the 
consequent challenges on relevant in situ characterization techniques, e. 
g., electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD), have long constrained in situ 
studies of recrystallization in refractory alloys.
In this study, we employed a quasi-in situ approach: Mo-5 wt. % Re 
alloy bars, produced by 90 % rotary swaging at 900 1000 
◦
C to a 
diameter of 12 mm, were polished to remove preparation stresses and 
marked with Vickers indentations. Following initial EBSD mapping of 
the deformation microstructure, each specimen was vacuum-sealed in 
quartz, annealed at 1200 
◦
C for cumulative intervals of 20, 40, 60, and 
80 min, and re-characterized at room temperature by EBSD (JEOL 
7800F/Oxford AztecCrystal) after each annealing step before re-sealing 
for annealing. For EBSD measurement, the same region was relocated 
via the indents. All annealing and cooling cycles were conducted under 
high vacuum ( < 10
 4

cooled from 1200 
◦

temperature recovery. Cross-section specimens were then prepared by 
mechanical grinding and twin-jet electrolytic polishing, and examined 
by transmission electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Tecnai F20).

Subgrain coarsening arises from boundary migration, which is 
influenced by multiple factors. While the stored deformation energy 
provides the overall driving force, two major local factors are: (i) the 
boundary curvature κ [ 20 ]; and (ii) the boundary misorientation, which 
influences the boundary energy γ (through the Read – Shockley relation 
[ 21 ]) and boundary mobility M. The resulting local driving force for 
subgrain boundary migration is commonly assumed as P = γκ and the 
boundary migration rate v = M ⋅ P.

The above observations indicate the important role of subgrain 
coarsening on the recrystallization nucleation in Mo-Re alloys. The 
coarsening is dominated by the elimination of small/narrow subgrains 
that are at least partially bounded by HABs, driven by the large 
boundary curvature and facilitated by the high mobility of HABs [ 23 , 27 ,
28 ]. Subgrain coarsening may directly lead to nucleation in regions with 
a strong orientation gradient, as well as in regions with a weak texture 
and a high fraction of HABs. Moreover, subgrain coarsening may induce 
SIBM nucleation. This combined process is illustrated in Fig. 5 . Some 
subgrains are first consumed, leading to a slight growth of neighboring 
subgrains. The coarsening process is dominated by HAB migration while 
supplemented by LAB migration. Regions swept by migrating bound -
aries typically exhibit reduced dislocation density. At a certain stage, an 
enlarged subgrain of reduced internal stored energy may grow into a 
high stored energy neighborhood by HAB migration, i.e., SIBM, trig -
gering recrystallization nucleation.
To conclude, this quasi-in situ EBSD study demonstrates that sub -
grain coarsening plays a key role in the recrystallization nucleation in 
Mo-5 wt. % Re alloys at 1200 
◦
C. The nucleation may occur directly 
through subgrain coarsening, or through subgrain coarsening followed 
by SIBM, depending on the local microstructure and stored energy. 
Statistical analysis indicates that the boundary migration rate depends 
on both the boundary misorientation and curvature. Dislocation walls 
and LABs store deformation energy, which serves as the overall driving 
force for subgrain growth and recrystallization, but they may also retard 
migrating boundaries during subgrain growth through dislocation 
wall boundary interaction. This study provides a new perspective for 
investigating recrystallization in refractory alloys.
