ABSTRACT
The equiatomic Cr-Co-Ni medium-entropy alloy (MEA) is known to exhibit the highest strength and ductility 
among high- and medium-entropy alloys in the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni and its subset systems and many attempts have 
been made to further improve its strength and ductility. A new strategy for alloy design is proposed for Cr-Co-Ni- 
based MEA by incorporating Mn as the alloying element with the utilization of Mean-Square Atomic Displace -
ment (MSAD) and Stacking Fault Energy (SFE) as predictors for strength and ductility. Our investigation reveals 
that the addition of 5 at.% Mn results in a modest reduction in MSAD and hence in 0 K yield stress, but the room 
temperature strength reaches the level achieved by the strongest Cr-rich Cr-Co-Ni MEA (45Cr-20Co-35Ni (in at. 
%) in our previous study), as the dulling of the temperature dependence of yield strength occurs below room 
temperature by the addition of Mn. Furthermore, at 77 K, a critical SFE value ( 35 mJ/m
2
) is identified below 
which the tensile ductility drops significantly due to the change in the additional deformation mode from 
deformation twinning to ε -martensite transformation, while at room temperature the tensile ductility increases 
monotonously with decreasing SFE. The designed alloy with a composition of 40Cr-5Mn-27.5Co-27.5Ni thus 
achieves an optimal balance of strength and ductility at room temperature, improving the tensile ductility of the 
45Cr-20Co-35Ni alloy by as much as 24 % without scarifying the yield strength.
1. Introduction
High- and medium-entropy alloys (HEA/MEAs) in the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co- 
Ni and its subset systems with the face-centered cubic (FCC) structure 
are of tremendous interest because of their capabilities of overcoming 
the strength-ductility tradeoff often encountered by many conventional 
structural materials [ 1 – 7 ]. It is now well recognized that neither the 
number of the constituent elements nor the equiatomic chemical com -
positions does not play an essential role as the origin for excellent me -
chanical properties of these HEA/MEAs [ 1 , 8 , 9 ]. Many efforts are thus 
made in recent years to design novel HEA/MEAs through tailoring the 
lattice distortion and stacking fault energies (SFEs), as they have been 
widely accepted as the indicators to predict the strength and ductility, 
respectively [ 10 – 15 ]. In particular, the Cr-Co-Ni ternary system has 
been the subject of intensive studies, as the Cr-Co-Ni MEA is known to 
exhibit the best combination of strength and ductility among the 
equiatomic quinary, quaternary and ternary HEA/MEAs derived from 
the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni system [ 16 – 19 ]. Theoretical calculation-guided 
alloy design strategies for higher strength and higher ductility are of 
considerable research interest presumably due to the broad single-phase 
composition range based on the equiatomic Cr-Co-Ni MEA. Some alloy 
design strategies, focusing on manipulating either lattice distortion or 
SFE, have proved the effectiveness in tunning strength and ductility, 
respectively [ 11 , 12 , 20 , 21 ]. Unsurprisingly, these alloy design strategies 
can rarely reach the alloy compositions that exhibit better strength and 
ductility simultaneously than the equiatomic one.
In our previous study [ 19 ], we have developed an alloy design 
strategy utilizing the Mean-Square Atomic Displacement (MSAD) and 
SFE to predict the yield stress and tensile ductility respectively for MEAs 
in the Cr-Co-Ni system. We found that the MSAD increases with 
increasing Cr and Ni contents while the SFE decreases with increasing Cr 
and Co contents in the Cr-Co-Ni system. We designed a series of Cr-rich 
non-equiatomic Cr-Co-Ni alloys and successfully proved by experiment 
that the 45Cr-20Co-35Ni alloy (compositions are given in atomic % 
throughout this paper) exhibits the best combination of strength and 
ductility, much better than that for the equiatomic Cr-Co-Ni MEA, as 26 
% and 14 % increase in yield strength and tensile ductility are observed 
77 K. In the course of our previous study, we found that while the yield

In the present study, we aim to develop strong and ductile Mn- 
alloyed Cr-Co-Ni medium entropy alloys through predictions by theo -

late MSADs and SFEs of a series of Cr-Mn-Co-Ni MEAs by cluster 
expansion (CE)-assisted density functional theory (DFT)-based compu -
tations [ 31 ]. We choose alloys with a high square root of MSAD ( ≳ 7.6 
pm) and low SFE for verification of their mechanical properties by ex -
periments. We investigate the effects of Mn alloying on the yield 
strength, its temperature dependence, SFE and tensile ductility to verify 
our alloy design strategy to simultaneously achieve high strength and 
high tensile ductility.
2. Methods and materials
2.1. Computational methods
Computations of MSAD and SFE values were performed with first- 
principles total energy calculations using the Vienna ab initio simula -
tion package (VASP) [ 32 , 33 ] based on DFT. Details on the calculation of 
MSAD are given in our previous study for the Cr-Co-Ni MEAs [ 19 ]. In the 
present study, the MSAD computations are further assisted by the cluster 
expansion (CE) [ 31 ] method to achieve better efficiency. Datasets 
sampled from the composition space of the FCC single phase by per -
forming traditional time-consuming DFT are collected, and CE methods 
are implemented to fit the MSAD (as an intrinsic property of lattice 
distortion in the scalar form). The final MSAD value for each composi -
tion is calculated from the optimized set of cluster correlation functions 
in CE theory based on the special quasirandom structures (SQS) [ 34 ]. 
The model performance is evaluated by Leave-Many-Out Cross- -
Validation (LMOCV) [ 35 ], details are in supplementary files. A CE model 
with a low LMOCV score ≈ 0.0916 pm per configuration is developed, 
which indicates an accurate evaluation of MSAD and can be applied to 
the Cr-Mn-Co-Ni system. A mixed space CE model [ 36 ] containing the 
real (r)-space term, based on the conventional CE, as well as the recip -
rocal (k)-space term based on the Fourier transform of the basis function, 
considering the finite size effect that MSAD decays as lattice size de -
creases [ 37 , 38 ], is utilized for the parameterization.
For SFE calculation, considering the magnetic contributions to the 
lattice energies by introducing Mn into the Cr-Co-Ni system [ 29 , 39 ], we 
employed non-magnetic (NM) as well as spin-polarized ferromagnetic 
(FM) scheme for Mn-contained alloys studied in the current work and 
the Cr-Co-Ni ternary alloys studied in previous work [ 19 ], in which the 
Cr and Mn are initialized with antiparallel magnetic moments to Co and 
Ni [ 29 , 40 , 41 ]. Meanwhile, the axial Ising model (AIM) [ 42 ] in 
first-order scheme is implemented into lattice modelling to ensure a 
three-dimensional periodic boundary condition (PBC). SFE is formu -
