Abstract 
Reliable brazing of TZM alloy and ZrC particle reinforced (ZrCp) W composite was achieved in this study 
by using Ti -28Ni eutectic brazing alloy. The typical interfacial microstructure of TZM/Ti -28Ni/ZrCp-W brazed 
joint consisted of a Ti solid solution (Ti(s, s)) layer, a continuous Ti2Ni layer and a diffusion layer mainly 
composed of W particles and (Ti, Zr)C particles. With an increase of brazing temperature, more ZrC particles and 
W particles entered the molten b razing alloy, which broadened the brazing seam and diminished the Ti 2Ni layer, 
resulting in the disappearance of the Ti2Ni layer eventually. Meanwhile, more Ti(s, s) stripes were observed on the 
TZM side. The presence of continuous Ti2Ni intermetallic phase and Ti(s, s) stripes structure in joints deteriorated 
the joining properties, which resulted in the formation of brittle fracture under shear test. In addition, the fracture 
path was related to the brazing temperature, and cracks ini tiate and propagate in the continuous Ti 2Ni layer at 
lower temperatures. However, the fracture path tended to be located at the TZM substrate close to the interface 
between TZM and the brazing seam when the brazing temperature exceeded 10 40°C. The optimal room 
temperature shear strength reached 120.5 MPa when brazed at 1040 ºC for 10 min and the fracture surface 
exhibited cleavage fracture characteristics, and the shear strength at high temperature of 800 ºC for the specimens 
with highest shear strength at room temperature reached 77.5 MPa. 
Keywords: TZM alloy, ZrCp-W composite, Vacuum brazing, Interfacial microstructure.

1. Introduction 
As one of the most important molybdenum alloys, TZM alloy (Mo -0.5Ti-0.08Zr-0.02C,wt. %) possesses 
unique properties including excellent creep strength, high-temperature stiffness, high erosion resistance, and good 
conductivity of heat and electricity [1,2] and is widely applied in nuclear energy and aerospace systems [3-5]. ZrC 
particle reinforced tungsten matrix composite (Z rCp-W composite) has excellent high temperature mechanical 
properties, such as excellent high temperature strength, thermal stability, high elastic modulus, and moderate 
thermal expansion [6, 7], and is used for many aerospace systems such as the throats of nozzles, rudders and noses 
of rockets [8-10], but its application is restricted in fabricating large scale or complex shapes components due to 
its poor sinterability and machinability. Accordingly, in order to broaden the applicatio n of the above materials in 
high-temperature applications, reliable joining technology will be proposed. 
Brazing is one of the most conventional methods to join dissimilar materials due to its simple operation, good 
repeatability, and relatively small ther mal effect on substrates [11]. Recently, many works have discussed the 
brazing of molybdenum and molybdenum alloys [12-19], tungsten and tungsten alloys [20-23], ZrC ceramic [24, 
25] and ZrC composites [26, 27]. Various brazing fillers such as Ag -based, Au -based, Ti -based and Ni -based 
fillers have been used to braze TZM alloy and ZrC ceramic. Chan et al. used pure Ag, 72Ag-28Cu and 95Ag–5Al 
to join Ti -6Al-4V and TZM alloy. They found that these silver -based fillers cannot wet the TZM substrate 
effectively [12-14]. To overcome this problem, active elements such as titanium (Ti) were added in the brazing 
fillers [15]. With the addition of a small amount of titanium, the wettability of the molten braze on the Mo 
substrates can be improved significantly. However , because of its relative low service temperature, silver -based 
braze fillers are not suitable for high temperature applications [17]. Au-based alloys also have been applied as 
brazing fillers to join Mo alloy to itself or to Si3N4 ceramics [18, 19]. The s tudies found that Au in the molten 
braze does not react with the Mo substrate, but element Ni added in the braze fillers preferentially reacts with the

Mo [18]. In addition, due to its high price, the use of gold based alloys as brazing fillers is not the first choice. As 
an active high temperature element, titanium is completely miscible with W or Mo, forming a solid solution [28], 
and can also react with ZrC to form (Zr, Ti)C solid solution [29]. Furthermore, nickel can withstand neutron 
irradiation and i s suitable for brazing nuclear energy equipment [29, 30]. The joints brazed using Ti -based or 
Ni-based brazing alloys commonly achieve high joining strength and good corrosion resistance due to the good 
compatibility between brazing alloys and TZM, ZrC or ZrC-SiC substrates [17, 26, 27]. In addition, Ti -based or 
Ni-based brazing alloys, as high temperature brazing alloys, show their advantages for high temperature 
applications. 
In this study, Ti-28Ni (wt. %) eutectic alloy was synthesized and used as a brazing filler to braze TZM alloy to 
ZrCp-W composite , and the highest service temperature of the joint may reach 700 °C. The interfacial 
microstructure of brazed joints was examined. Moreover, the effects of brazing temperature on the interfacial 
microstructure and joining properties of brazed joints were investigated. 
2. Experimental procedure 
The substrate materials used in the experiments are TZM alloy and ZrC p-W composite, with dimensions of 10 
× 20 × 3 mm3 and 4 × 4 × 5 mm3, respectively for metallographic observation and shear strength test. 
The hot rolled TZM plate was acquired from Shanghai Kaiye Metal Product Co., LTD, China . The ZrC p-W 
composite, supplied by Institute for Advanced Ceramics, Harbin Institute of Technol ogy, was hot -pressed with 
ZrC (30 vol. %) particles and W particles as the sintering additives to a relative density of 98% [31]. The 
microstructure of ZrC p-W composite is shown in Fig. 1. The white phase in the backscattered electron (BSE) 
image is tungsten-matrix, and the light gray phase is ZrC particles. 
Ti-28Ni (wt.%) alloy, selected as the brazing filler alloy, was first vacuum smelt 10 times using Ti wire (TA1) 
and Ni wire (N6)，and then processed to foil with the thickness of about 100 μm. Fig. 2(a) shows the typical

Fig. 4(a) illustrates the interfacial microstructure of TZM/Ti-28Ni/ZrCp-W joint brazed at 1020 ºC for 10 min. 
As shown in Fig. 4(a), the brazing seam consists of three characteristic zones which are classified by morphology, 
which are marked as: zone I (the gray layer with thick ness of about 15 μm), zone II (the continuous dark gray 
phase layer with thickness of ~16 μm), and zone III (the four-phases mixed region with thickness of ~ 55 μm). Fig. 
4(b) is the magnified image of zone III. Meanwhile, the EDS results of the phases in Fig. 4 are included in Table 
1. 
Based on the difference in contrast, the braze seam mainly consists of five phases: gray phase A, dark gray 
phase B, black phase C, white phase D and light gray phase D. It is observed that phase A contains a continuous 
reaction layer adjacent to TZM substrate and some island -like structures in zone II and zone III. The continuous 
phase B is mainly distributed in zone II and zone III. There are many black particles (phase C) and white particles 
(phase D) dispersed randoml y in phase B in Zone III. The main compositions of phase A are 73.85Ti, 14.29Mo 
and 7.01Ni (at. %). According to the Mo -Ni-Ti ternary phase diagram [32] and previous studies [17], phase A is 
Ti(s, s). The stoichiometry of Ti/Ni ratio in atomic percent of p hase B is close to 2:1, so it can be considered as 
Ti2Ni. Phase C is a new generated phase with a main composition of 77.90 Ti, 14.04 C and 6.51 Zr (at. %), since 
both TiC and ZrC have a NaCl -type crystal structure, and they can form a complete solid solut ion [33, 34], it is 
deduced that phase C is TiC particle dissolving a certain quantity of ZrC, e.g. (Ti, Zr)C . Phase D is similar to that 
of W particles in ZrC p-W substrate both in chemical composition and contrast. Phase E is the ZrC particle which 
did not react. 
XRD analysis was carried out in order to determine the phase constitution of the brazed joint and the results 
are shown in Fig. 4(c). The phases including W, Ti 2Ni, Ti(s, s), ZrC and TiC are detected. Therefore, it is 
concluded that the typical interfacial microstructure of TZM /ZrCp-W joint brazed at 1020 °C for 10 min is TZM/ 
Ti(s, s)/ Ti2Ni/ W(s, s)+(Ti, Zr)C+ Ti2Ni/ ZrCp-W.

Based on the above analyses, the evolution of the TZM/Ti -28Ni/ZrCp-W joint can be described as follows . 
The Ti-28Ni brazing filler began to melt as the temperature exceeded its melting point. On the TZM side, firstly, 
molten filler alloy elements wetted the substrate , then elements Ti and Ni diffused into TZM substrate and 
element Mo of TZM substrate dissolved into the molten brazing alloy subsequently. As observed in a previous 
study [17], the dissolved Mo is more likely to react with Ti to form a solid solution. Acco rding to the Ti –Mo 
binary phase diagram [28] , the lowest solidification temperature is about 600 ºC higher than the brazing 
temperature, this will result in the formation of a Ti(s, s) layer rapidly adjacent to the interface between the TZM

substrate and liquid brazing alloy during the brazing process. This Ti(s, s) layer separated the TZM and the molten 
brazing alloy and expelled the element Ni into the residual liquid phase [17]. Simultaneously, on the ZrCp-W side, 
molten Ti-Ni alloy entered into the comp osite and then reacted with ZrC particles and W particles. As ZrC is a 
kind of interstitial compound, the ratio of Zr to C in ZrC is not a fixed value [24, 25], and C atoms are capable of 
diffusing from ZrC into liquid Ti -Ni alloy during the interaction an d reacting with Ti. The reaction equation can 
be illustrated below: 
ZrCX → ZrCX-1 + C1 (1) 
C+Ti → TiC (2) 
The new generated TiC can react with residual ZrC, and form a complete solid solution. The reaction process can 
be shown as following: 
TiC+ZrC→ (Ti, Zr)C (3) 
It is believed that this reaction resulted in the destruction of the tight bond betwe en the ZrC particles and the W 
particles, and eventually caused them entering into the molten brazing alloy. However, it should be noted that 
there was no visible evidence to prove that W particles were involved in the reaction with the brazing alloy during 
the brazing process. As the temperature decreased, the residual liquid brazing alloy gradually cooled to form a 
continuous Ti2Ni phase. Meanwhile, the W and newly formed (Ti, Zr)C particles were fixed in the continuous 
Ti2Ni phase during the solidification process, and eventually formed the zone III. At the same time, a continuous 
Ti2Ni layer was formed in the area where these particles had not yet reached. 
3.2 Effect of brazing temperature on the interfacial microstructure of TZM/ZrCp-W brazed joints 
The microstructure evolution of the brazed joint at 1000 ºC, 1040 ºC, 1060 ºC and 1080 ºC is illustrated in Fig. 
6 (a-d), respectively. It can be noticed that brazing temperature affects the interfacial morphologies of the brazed 
joint dramatically. Firstly, as the brazing temperature increased, there was no significant variation in the thickness

of Ti(s, s) layer, but its morphology became coarse because of some crack-like stripes and island-like Ti2Ni phase; 
secondly, the thickness of the Ti 2Ni layer decreased obviously or even disappeared at/after 1060 °C; thirdly, the 
thickness of zone III became wider considerably. 
As described previously, the Ti(s, s) layer is supposed to be formed quickly at the beginning of the brazing 
process. The new solidified layer may delay the atom exchange between the TZM substrate and the molten alloy, 
and then restrained the growth of the Ti(s, s) layer, that is why the thickness of Ti(s, s) layer varied negligibly at 
different brazing temperature. The contents of elem ent Mo marked as A ′, B′, C′ and D ′ in Ti(s, s) layer were 
analyzed by EDS and the results are listed in Table 2 . With increasing brazing temperature, the percentage of Mo 
increased from 10.27 to 40.69 (at. %). As a result, the phase contrast of the Ti(s, s ) layer changed lighter in color. 
This is due to increasing brazing temperature accelerating the atom exchange, causing more Ti atoms to diffuse 
into the TZM substrate and more Mo atoms to dissolve into the brazing alloy at the beginning of the brazing 
process. The crack -like stripes marked as spot E ′ with the composition of 45.63 Mo and 47.68 Ti (at. %) were 
observed paralleled to the interface of TZM substrate. Since the hot -rolled TZM has an elongated grain structure 
parallel to the surface [16], when molten alloy diffused into TZM along the elongated grain boundary and reacted 
with Mo, some crack-like Ti(s, s) strips were formed during the brazing process. 
Furthermore, with increasing brazing temperature, both the flowability and activity of the brazing alloy were 
substantially improved. Hence, much more Ti -28Ni liquid alloy overflowed out of the joint and diffused into the 
ZrCp-W composite. Therefore, the thickness of zone III was widened and the Ti2Ni layer became thinner and even 
disappeared considerably.

4. Conclusions 
Reliable brazing of the TZM alloy and ZrCp-W composite is achieved by using Ti-28Ni eutectic brazing alloy. 
The effects of the brazing temperature on the interfacial microstructure and joining properties are investigated. 
Primary conclusions are summarized as follows. 
1) The typical interfacial microstructure of TZM/ZrCp-W brazed joints was TZM/ Ti(s, s) layer/continuous 
Ti2Ni layer/ Ti2Ni, (Ti, Zr)C and W particles/ZrCp-W composite. The Ti(s, s) layer solidified adjacent to 
TZM substrate due to the dissolution of Mo into molten brazing alloy. The interaction between the 
molten alloy and ZrC p-W substrate generated a mixture layer containing both Ti 2Ni, (Ti,Zr)C and W 
particles. 
2) With the increasing of brazing temperature, more ZrC and W particles appeared in the brazing seam, 
which broadened the brazed joint and result ed in the disappearance of continuous Ti 2Ni layer . The 
thickness of the Ti(s, s) layer did not change significantly. 
3) The brazed joints have demonstrated excellent joining properties. The specimen brazed at 1040 ºC for 
10 min with the optimal shear strength (120.5 MPa) fractures on the TZM substrate, and the fracture 
surface exhibited cleavage fracture characteristics. Both the continuous Ti 2Ni layer and Ti(s, s) stripes 
had adverse effects on the joints properties. The shear strength at high temperature of 800 ºC for the

Research Highlights: 
TZM alloy and ZrCp-W composite were successfully brazed using Ti-28Ni braze alloy. 
Brazing temperature played an important role on the interfacial microstructure. 
Ti2Ni layer and Ti(s, s) strips structures at TZM dominated the joints properties. 
The maximum average shear strength of TZM joints reached 120.5 MPa. 
Fracture locations varied greatly with increasing the brazing temperature.
