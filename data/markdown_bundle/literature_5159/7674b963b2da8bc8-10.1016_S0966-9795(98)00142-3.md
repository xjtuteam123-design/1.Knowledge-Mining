Abstract

lowed by isothermal hot-forging (IHF). The alloy, composed mainly of B2 and/C13phases with equiaxial grains of severalm in average

1. Introduction
Intermetallic compounds of the Ti–Al system are
expected to be innovative engineering materials for high-
temperature applications such as aerospace components
and also for application to advanced energy generation
systems. They are also attractive for nuclear applica-
tions [1], because of their low neutron-induced radio-
activity and high strength compared to those of
conventional reactor materials, such as stainless steels.
Recently, a radiation induced ductilization (RID) phe-
nomenon was observed in a Ti–47at%Al alloy [2]. This
phenomenon gives a possibility to develop innovative
materials with superior irradiation resistance. However,
more ductility and toughness at lower temperatures,
especially around room temperature, are required from
the viewpoint of safety and economic eciency in the
energy system applications.
Recent studies to improve ductility and fracture
toughness of Ti–Al alloys have been focused on the
