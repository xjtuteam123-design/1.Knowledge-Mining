ABSTRACT
A new elevated-temperature high strength Mg-0.6Er-0.6Y-1.2Zn-0.2Mn (at.%) alloy with decent thermal con-
ductivity was developed by semi-continuous casting, solid-solution treatment and hot-extrusion. Two secondary
phases, i.e. 18R-long period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase and W phase (Mg 3(Er,Y)2Zn3), would be formed in
the as-cast alloy mainly due to the alloy component of (Er + Y): Zn = 1: 1 and Er: Y = 1: 1 (at.%). After solid-
solution treatment and hot extrusion, the most signi ﬁcant microstructure characteristic formed in as-extruded
alloy is numerous nano-spaced basal plane stacking faults (SFs) in all the dynamically recrystallized (DRXed) and
un-DRXed grains, which is very rare in the previous reports. The as-extruded alloy exhibits excellent perfor-
mances from room temperature to 300 °C. The tensile yield strength can be maintained above 300 MPa at 200 °C
and above 250 MPa at 300 °C, and the thermal conductivity is 86.0 W/m·K at 300 °C. The superior elevated-
temperature strength is mainly related to the formation of the nano-spaced basal plane SFs throughout the whole
Mg matrix, the ﬁne DRXed grains with size of ~2 μm, the strongly textured un-DRXed grains with numerous sub-
structures, and ﬁne W phase particles as well as LPSO phases.
1. Introduction
The magnesium (Mg) alloy is the lightest metal structural material
in the practical engineering applications and has great application po-
tential. With the trend of light weight in the automotive and aerospace
industries and portability in the electronics, as well as their demand for
heat dissipation properties of materials, the higher requirements are
needed for the performances of Mg alloys [ 1–5]. However, the widely
used commercial Mg alloys such as AZ, AM, ZK series are of room
temperature (RT) Mg alloys whose strength is moderate at RT and de-
teriorates rapidly with increasing usage temperatures, and their
thermal conductivity is also unsatisfactory. Therefore, it is a great sig-
niﬁcance to develop new Mg alloys with both excellent mechanical
properties and thermal conductivity [ 6–10].
It has been known that the addition of rare earth (RE) has favorable
eﬀect on the mechanical properties of Mg alloys, and among all the RE-
containing Mg alloys, the Mg-RE-Zn system has great development
potential [11–15]. In the Mg-RE-Zn system, Mg-Y-Zn (or written as Mg-
Zn-Y) series alloys have been studied widely due to formation of some
phases with special structures. It has been reported that the dominating
secondary phases would change from I-phase to W-phase and to Z-
phase based on the increasing Y/Zn ratio (I-phase: Mg
3Zn6Y1, quasi-
crystal structure; W-phase: Mg 3Zn3Y2, cubic structure; Z-phase:
Mg12Y1Zn1/Mg10Y1Zn1, long period stacking order (LPSO) structure)
[11]. Singh et al. [ 16,17] developed the I-phase containing Mg-8Zn-2Y
and Mg-14Zn-3Y (wt%) extruded alloys and they exhibited both high
strength and high ductility at RT. Zheng et al. [ 18] designed a W-phase
containing Mg-10.3Zn-6.4Y-0.4Zr-0.5Ca (wt%) extruded alloy and this
alloy presented the ultra-high ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and yield
strength (YS) at RT. A more typical ultra-high strength alloy is the
LPSO-phase containing Mg-6.8Y-2.5Zn (wt%) with YS even above
600 MPa at RT via rapid solidi ﬁcation powder metallurgy (RS P/M)
processing [ 19]. It can be found that compared with commercial Mg
alloys, these alloys have signi ﬁcantly higher RT strength, indicating

2. Experimental procedures

high pure Mg (99.98 wt%), Zn (99.95 wt%), and Mg-20Y (wt%), Mg-

3.2. Formation and microstructure of solid-solution alloy
In order to determine the solid-solution temperature of as-cast alloy,
the sample was tested with DSC, and the result is shown in Fig. 4. The
heating curve of as-cast alloy presents one distinct endothermic peak,
with the initial temperature of 535 °C and the peak temperature of
543 °C, which also suggests that the typical eutectic W phase and the
lamellar LPSO phase has similar melting temperature in this alloy.
According to the DSC result, the solid-solution treatment conditions
with di ﬀerent holding time at 525 °C, 530 °C and 535 °C were designed
as preliminary tests. From the viewpoints of changes in grain size and
eutectic phase volume fraction, 530 °C for 10 h is selected as the op-
timum solid-solution treatment condition. The XRD patterns ( Fig. 2)
shows that the phase composition of the alloy does not change after
heat treatment at 530 °C for 10 h except for the change in peak in-
tensity.
Fig. 5 shows the microstructure of solid-solution treated alloy. It can
be seen that the majority of secondary phases are dissolved into the Mg
matrix, and only a small portion of the undissolved phases with total
volume fraction of about 3.9% remains at grain boundaries. Among
them the volume fraction of LPSO phase is about 1.4% and that of W
phase is about 2.5%. The total content of eutectic phases is reduced by
about 71% as compared to the as-cast alloy ( Fig. 1 av s Fig. 5a). It also
suggests that the LPSO phase and W phase are high temperature stable
phases and very di ﬃcult to be completely dissolved in the Mg matrix. In
addition, the grain size of solid-solution treated alloy seems to have
little change compared with that of the corresponding as-cast alloy.
3.3. Microstructure of as-extruded alloy
Fig. 6 shows the SEM images of as-extruded alloy. The as-extruded
sample exhibits the typical bimodal microstructure composed of ﬁne
equiaxed dynamic recrystallized (DRXed) grains and coarse deformed
un-DRXed grains elongated along the ED mainly due to the insu ﬃcient
deformation. There is no obvious change in phase composition between
the solid-solution treated alloy and the as-extruded alloy according to

un-DRXed grains, which is rarely reported in the extruded Mg alloys.

DRXed grain and have di ﬀerent orientations in di ﬀerent DRXed grains

As a whole, the microstructure of the new extruded alloy is rela-
tively complex. In addition to the formation of LPSO and W phase, the
most important characteristic of the new extruded alloy is the forma-
tion of nano-spaced basal plane SFs within both the DRXed and un-
DRXed grains. Although basal plane SFs and their solute segregation
have been reported in Mg-RE-Zn alloys [ 20,21,29], such high number
density SFs formed in both DRXed and un-DRXed grains has been rarely
reported in deformed Mg alloys. In this study, the formation of this
novel microstructure is related to the combination e ﬀect of alloy
composition design and preparation processing parameters (i.e. solid
solution treatment and extrusion process). Selecting RE (Er and Y) and
Zn can lower stacking fault energy and stabilize SFs or LPSO structure
[21,29,33]. Controlling the appropriate processing parameters ensure
that the ﬁnal formation is the nano-spaced ﬁne SFs rather than nu-
merous coarse LPSO plates.
The EBSD technology was used to examine the information related
to grains of the as-extruded alloy in detail. Fig. 11 shows the inverse
pole ﬁgure (IPF) maps, IPFs referring to ND and (0002) pole ﬁgures
(PFs) of whole region, un-DRXed region and DRXed region of the as-
extruded sample. The IPF maps analysis indicates that the volume
fraction of DRXed grains and un-DRXed grains is about 45% and 55%,
respectively, and the coarse deformed un-DRXed grains are certain
orientation roughly (most of them are red grains with 〈0001〉||ND)
while the ﬁne DRXed grains are relatively random orientations. In ad-
dition, the color gradient in one deformed un-DRXed grain suggests that
there still exists low angle misorientation in the un-DRXed grain. Both
the IPF and the (0002) PF of whole region reveal that the as-extruded
alloy has a strong basal texture, and the basal pole in PF is inclined at
22–31° toward the TD. It is considered that the texture distribution of
the basal plane mainly inclined toward the TD is related to the RE
elements added to the alloy [ 34,35]. The IPF and especially the (0002)

with the whole region except the higher texture intensity, while the IPF
and (0002) PF of DRXed grains shows very weak basal texture and the
large spread of (0002) orientation also exists in PF. The above analysis
indicates that the strong basal texture of this as-extruded alloy is mainly
contributed by the deformed un-DRXed grains. In other words, it also
conﬁrms that the DRXed Mg-RE-based extruded alloys usually have
weak basal texture.

3.4. Mechanical properties of as-extruded alloy
Fig. 13(a) shows the typical tensile stress-strain curves of as-ex-
truded alloy from RT to 400 °C. The average tensile properties including
UTS, YS and elongation to failure ( ε) are shown in Fig. 13(b). The as-
extruded alloy exhibits excellent tensile strength between RT and
300 °C, and the strength just decreases slightly especially within 250 °C.
Both the UTS (351 MPa) and YS (320 MPa) at RT are above 300 MPa.
The UTS (332 MPa) and YS (304 MPa) at 200 °C still can keep above
300 MPa. When the temperature reaches 250 °C, the UTS (311 MPa) can
still remain above 300 MPa and YS is 289 MPa. Even at 300 °C, the UTS
(264) and YS (251) remain above 250 MPa. However, when the tem-
perature reaches up to 350 °C, whether UTS (157 MPa) or YS (142 MPa)
decreases sharply though they are still higher than the most of reported
Mg alloys [ 38]. By comparing the elevated temperature strength of this
new alloy with those of previously reported Mg alloys, we can ﬁnd that
the newly designed extruded alloy exhibits superior high temperature
strength than most heat resistant Mg alloys containing comparative or
even higher RE content [ 38,39].
When tensile tests are performed at high temperature, the de-
formation for Mg alloy should be related to the basal dislocation slip,
the non-basal dislocation slips such as prismatic slip and pyramidal slip
due to the decreasing critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) with in-
creasing temperature, as well as the grain boundary sliding and lattice
rotation [ 40–43]. Based on the detailed microstructure observations,
we consider that the excellent high temperature strength within 300 °C
is mainly related to the following factors. First, the solute-segregated
SFs have the coherent interface with α-Mg matrix and high thermal
stability [ 20,21,29], and therefore, the basal plane SFs provide the ef-
fective obstacles to the non-basal dislocation slip at elevated tempera-
tures. It is considered that the nano-spaced ﬁne basal plane SFs con-
tribute much more than the basal plane LPSO plates to the strength due
to the more e ﬀective pinning [ 20,44]. Second, according to the Hall-
Petch relationship, the ﬁne DRXed grains with size of about 2 μm
contribute to the high YS through grain boundary reinforcement. Third,
the deformed un-DRXed grains possess very strong basal texture and
thus they su ﬃciently improve the YS by providing a hard orientation of
the basal dislocation slip [ 40,45]. In addition, the sub-structures

the increasing temperature.

thermal conductivity of elevate-temperature high strength Mg alloys.
Wang et al. [ 41,48] investigated the tensile properties and thermal

thermal conductivity of the new alloy is also superior than those of

4. Conclusions
In summary, we prepared a new Mg-0.6Er-0.6Y-1.2Zn-0.2Mn (at.%)
alloy via semi-continuous casting, solid-solution treatment and hot-
extrusion. A notably high strength and decent thermal conductivity at
elevated temperature have been obtained from the as-extruded alloy,
and some main conclusions can be drawn as follows:
(1) The mixed phases of 18R-LPSO phase and W phase are formed in
the as-cast alloy mainly based on the alloy component of (Er + Y):
Zn = 1: 1 and Er: Y = 1: 1 (at.%). Most of these compounds can be
dissolved in Mg matrix after solid-solution treated at 530 °C for
10 h.
(2) Nano-spaced basal plane SFs are formed throughout all the DRXed
and un-DRXed grains of as-extruded alloy due to the combined ef-
fect of suitable alloy composition and appropriate preparation
process parameters (mainly solid-solution treatment and extrusion
temperatures). In addition, ﬁne W phase rods would be formed by
dynamic precipitation during hot extrusion.
(3) The as-extruded alloy shows excellent tensile properties from RT to
300 °C. The UTS and YS can be maintained above 300 MPa at RT to
200 °C and above 250 MPa even at 300 °C. The superior elevated-
temperature strength is mainly ascribed to the formation of (a)
nano-spaced basal plane SFs with a large aspect ratio formed
throughout the Mg matrix, (b) ﬁne DRXed grains with size of
~2 μm, (c) strongly textured un-DRXed with numerous LAGBs and
(d) ﬁne W phase particles as well as LPSO phases.
(4) The as-extruded alloy also exhibits decent thermal conductivity as a
high strength Mg alloy at elevated temperature. Its thermal
conductivity is 62.1 W/m·K at RT and it increases to 86.0 W/m·K at
300 °C.
