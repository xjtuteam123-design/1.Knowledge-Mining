Abstract 
Solid solution strengthening of the  matrix is one key factor for improving the creep strength 
of single crystal nickel -base superalloys at high temperatures. Therefore a strong partitioning 
of solid solution hardening elements to the matrix is beneficial for high temperature creep 
strength. Different Rhenium -free alloys which are derived from CMSX -4 are investigated . 
The alloys have been characterized regarding microstructure, phase compositions as well as 
creep strength. It is found that increasing the Titanium (Ti) as well as the Tungsten (W) 
content causes a stronger partitioning of the solid solution strengtheners, in particular W, to 
the  phase. As a result the creep resistance is significantly improved. Based on these ideas, a 
Rhenium-free alloy with an optimized chemistry regarding the partitioning behavior of W is 
developed and validated in the present study. It shows comparable creep strength to the 
Rhenium containing second generation alloy CMSX -4 in the high temperature / low stress 
creep regime and is less prone to the formation of de leterious topologically close packed 
(TCP) phases. This more effective usage of solid solution strengtheners can enhance the creep 
properties of nickel -base superalloys while reducing the content of strategic elements like 
Rhenium. 
1. Introduction 
Rhenium (Re) has become an essential alloying element for single crystal nickel-base 
superalloys used for turbine blade applications in the hottest section of aero and industrial gas

## Page 3

2 
 
turbine engines. The introduction of Re [1,2] was one of the main development steps for 
reaching higher thermal capability in the last forty years. Since then single crystal superalloys 
have been classified into generations according to their content of Re, reaching a maximum of 
6 wt % for the 3 rd and 4th generation. However, Re is one of the rarest elements in the world 
and the increased demand has given rise to concerns on its availability. Consequently there is 
a high interest in reducing the content of this critical strategic element and recently Re -
reduced or -free alloys have been developed with almost similar creep strength compared to 
Re bearing alloys [3–5]. In order to design new alloys with less or no Re, strategies have to be 
found to compensate the positive effect of Re on the creep properties. Initially the formation 
of small Re clusters was attributed to cause the so called “rhenium effect” [6]. Newer studies 
could not find evidence for this theory [7–9], favoring two other characteristics as being 
responsible for the effect of Re. First ly, Re is the slowest -diffusing element in Nickel (Ni) 
amongst all alloying elements [10–12]. Secondly, Re has a strong tendency to be enriched in 
the  matrix [13–16]. Slowly diffusing elements seem to be particularly beneficial for 
retarding dislocation climb at the / interface and thereby enhancing the creep resistance 
[17]. They may influence creep rates directly or ind irectly by affecting the effective diffusion 
coefficient or by introducing a back stress, i.e. a solid solution strengthening contribution to 
internal stresses, see for example [17–19]. Especially in the regime where rafting occurs (high 
temperatures and low stresses), solid solution hardening of the  phase plays a major role. The 
applied stresses are not sufficiently high for cutting of the γ’ phase and thus the dislocations 
are confined to  matrix channels [20–23]. Besides Re, Tungsten (W) has the lowest diffusion 
coefficient of all 4d and 5d transition metals [24] if the extremely rare element Iridium and 
Osmium, which can form a hazardous oxide, are neglected. Thus by increasing the content of 
slow diffusing refractory elements, in particular W, Caron and Khan [5,25] already developed 
an Re free alloy with comparative creep resistance to Re bearing alloys. However, W is

the fact that W does not show the strong preference to partition to the  phase as Re.

2. Materials and Experimental Procedure 
1.1. Alloy compositions 
The investigated ERBO alloys originate from a study by Heckl et al. [32] and are based on the 
Re-free alloy called ASTRA1-00 which has been adapted from the c ommercially used 2 nd 
generation Ni-base superalloy CMSX-4. For the task of the present study, the concentration of 
the alloying elements Al, Ti and W was systematically altered ( Table 1) within the five 
derivative alloys. To maintain a similar  volume fraction, the sum of the concentration of -
forming elements Al, Ti and Ta was kept constant. To study the influence of Ti on the 
partitioning behavior, Ti was added at the expense of Al. In order to further improve the creep 
resistance, alloys with increased W content were investigated as well. In these alloys 
(ERBO/37-W, ERBO/38-WTi, ERBO/39-Opt) the W concentration is similar to the sum of 
the concentrations of the most effective solid solution hardeners W and Re in CMSX -4 (in 
at %). Moreover , the alloy ERBO/39-Opt was designed numerically by opt imizing the 
chemistry in such a way that the partitioning ratio of W is minimized, i.e. the concentration of 
W in the  matrix is maximized. For this purpose the multi -criteria optimization software 
MultOpt was used to optimize the alloy composition based on data of T hermoCalc Version 
3.1 and the database TTNi8 . This software has been developed in the past years for the 
computer-aided design of metallic alloys . For more details see Rettig et al. [33]. The 
optimization of the che mistry considered the following boundary conditions: The  volume 
fraction at 1100 °C should be similar compared to alloy ASTRA1-00 or CMSX -4, 
respectively. The Cr content has to be larger than 5 wt % for sufficient corrosion/oxidation 
resistance and the W content is set below 9 wt % for acceptable density. Additionally an 
(equilibrium) solid us temperature of at least 1320 °C is required for sufficiently high heat 
treatment temperatures. The lattice misfit , determined from the lattice parameters of the 

1.2. Material preparation 
Cigar shaped ingots with a length of about 70 mm were vacuum arc melted several times in a 
water cooled copper mold to solve all respective alloying elements homogenously. 
Afterwards the ingots were molten in a high-rate-solidification investment casting unit in 
vacuum and subsequently directionally solidified cylindrical bars (length: 180 mm, diameter: 
12 mm) of each alloy were produced . Further details regarding the casting process are 
published by Heckl et al. [34]. The melt was heated for 5 min at 1560 °C and cast into the 
1500 °C preheated mold, which was withdrawn with a speed of 3 mm/min. The heat treatment 
for the ERBO alloys was adapted from the standard heat treatment procedure of CMSX -4. 
ASTRA1-00 was solution heat treated at 1340 °C for 16 h and the ERBO alloys at 1310 °C

for 24 h , because the addition of Ti leads to a lower solidus te mperature. ERBO/38-WTi for 
example has a solidus temperature of 1321 °C and a  solvus temperature of 1234 °C, as 
determined by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) . The subsequent  age hardening was 
achieved by heat treating for 2 h at 1140 °C and 24 h at 870 °C according to [32]. 
All heat treatments were performed in the same furnace (Tube furnace R140/800/14, 
Nabertherm, Linienthal, Germany) and samples were air cooled between each steps. In order 
to produce a coarsened microstructure with sufficiently large  precipitates and  channels for 
EDS analysis by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), a special long term heat treatment at 
1100 °C for 1000 h in vacuum was performed. Afterwards the samples were water quenched. 
For microstructural investigations the samples were ground down to 4 000 grit and 
mechanically polished up to a final step using 1 m diamond suspension. Subsequently the 
samples were etched us ing a solution of 30 ml HCl (37 %), 20 ml HNO3 (65 %) and 20 ml 
CH3COOH (100 %) which preferentially removes the  precipitates. For transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM) investigations, thin foils with a thickness below 100 m were 
prepared and afterwards thinned by twin jet polishing using an agent of ethanol and perchlorid 
acid (Electrolyte A2, Struers GmbH, Germany) at a temperature of -30 °C.

3.2 Partitioning behavior of alloying elements 
The chemical composition of the  and  phase ha s been measured to determine the 
partitioning behavior of the alloying elements. The partitioning behavior of W, Cr, Mo, Ti and 
Al after the initial heat treatment and after long term aging for 1000 h at 1100 °C is shown in 
Figure 4 for comparison. Interestingly, W partitions preferentially to the  phase in the initial 
state of ASTRA1-00, which is typical for Ni -base superalloys containing a low number of 
alloying elements [15,27,30]and new Co -base superalloys [45]. By increasing the W content 
the partitioning behavior of W changes significantly and W is more enriched in the  phase in 
ERBO/37-W. A reasonable explanation for this could be a limited solubility of W in the  
phase, i.e. this is thermodynamically favorable. The more important finding is, however, that 
the addition of Ti reinforces this tendency and reduces the partitioning ratio of W further. For 
alloy ERBO/39-Opt the partitioning behavior of W is even further improved, reaching the 
lowest partitioning ratio and thus the highest W concentration in  of all alloys investigated. 
According to Sugui et al. [46] the strong preferential partitioning of Re to  in CMSX-4 leads 
to a reduced solubility of bigger atoms like W in the  phase and thus caus es the rejection of 
W atoms to the  precipitates. Hence, the combination of an increased Ti and W content and 
the fact that no Re is present seems to be responsible for the low partitioning ratios for W. 
According to Figure 4 similar effects as for W can be observed for other solid solution 
strengthening elements like Cr and Mo, but to a less significant extent.

3.3 Creep behavior 
ASTRA1-00 and ERBO/36-Ti (see Figure 6 ) e xhibit very poor creep strength due to t he 
unfavorable spherical  morphology combined with the lowest content of solid solution 
hardening and slowly diffusing elements. By increasing the W content, the creep rates of 
ERBO/37-W and ERBO/38-WTi decrease significantly. Optimization of the partitioning 
behavior of W helps in this respect as it leads to even higher W matrix concentration at a 
given nominal composition. An example is a further strong decrease of the minimum creep 
rate of ERBO/39-Opt, reaching a nearly equivalent creep resistance compared to CMSX -4 at 
1100 °C and 137 MPa. All creep experiments are summarized in a Larson -Miller plot (see 
Figure 6 d). The rafted microstructures of ERBO/39-Opt and CMSX-4 after interrupted creep 
experiments at 1 % plastic strain can be seen in Figure 7 . Both alloys show directionally 
coarsened  channels parallel to the stress axes with nearly equivalent channel widths. This 
confirms that they exhibit a negative lattice misfit at test temperature. Contrary to ERBO/39-
Opt, the Re containing alloy CMSX -4 shows the formation of TCP phases at this stage of 
creep.

4. Discussion 
The Re-free alloy ERBO/39-Opt provides an excellent creep strength similar to that of the 
2nd generation Ni -base superalloy CMSX -4, particularly in the high temperature and low 
stress creep regime. This has been achieved by improving the partitioning behavior of W 
which leads to an increased content of the solid solution hardening element W in the  phase. 
As a consequence diffusion controlled dislocation motion is affected and the creep strength is 
improved as discussed in the following. 
Generally the initial microstructure of CMSX -4 seems to be the most suitable one of all 
investigated alloys for achieving the highest creep strength. It has been shown in literature 
that an initially cuboidal  morphology with a  size of around 0.45 m [49] and strongly 
aligned thin  channels combined with high Orowan stresses [38,50,51] are beneficial. The 
most finely and most strongly rafted / microstructure after long term annealing of CMSX-4 
indicates the highest lattice misfit and the lowest coarsening rate. The high lattice misfit leads 
to densest / interfacial dislocation networks [38,52,53] that should also contribute to a 
superior creep strength. Additionally, CMSX -4 contains 1 at % of the slowest diffusing 
element Re that retards diffusion controlled processes . In CMSX-4 TCP phases form during 
creep at 1100 °C , as shown in Figure 7b, however the amount of TCP phases is very low . 
Similarly, a different hardening effect through the presence of short range ordering of the  
matrix cannot explain the rather similar creep properties between CMSX -4 and Re-free alloy 
ERBO/39-Opt, especially at high temperatures of 1050 °C and 1100 °C. According to Jouiad 
et al. [54,55], Glas et al. [56] and Pettinari et al. [57] the presence of a short range order in the 
 matrix could also lead to an improvement of the mechanical properties, at least at low 
temperatures. However a potential short range ordering vanishes almost completely at 
temperatures above 1000 °C [57] and the hardening eff ect is considered to become rather 
negligible.

Furthermore, to the current state of research , it is not clear whether the models given in 
equations 4-9 and the used activation energies and pre -exponential factors , that were 
determined for binary single -phase Ni solid solutions (see Table 2) , are fully appropriate to 
calculate the effective diffusivity in a compositionally complex  phase in order to describe 
the diffusion controlled climb of dislocations along the / interface. As stated by 
Wollgramm et al . [19] energetic and kinetic aspects regarding the diffusion at the / 
interface may be affected, due to the influence of / lattice misfit and dislocation stresses 
and interfacial dislocation networks. 
In general the results show a relationship between the increased content of solid solution 
hardening elements in the  channels and enhanced creep strength. However more detailed 
physical models are needed to deepen the mechanistic understanding of high temperature 
creep in Ni -base superalloys , although the simple model s for calculating the effective 
diffusion coefficients can be used as a first approach to predict the solid solution 
strengthening effect and the creep resistance of the alloys in the high temperature creep 
regime. For the design of new alloys it has to be considered that the addition of Ti leads to an 
improved partitioning behavior of the solid solution hardening elements, but may also lead to 
a lower oxidation resistance [71,72] and a reduction of the solvus temperature [25].

5. Conclusion 
Five Re-free derivatives of C MSX-4 with varying contents of Ti and W were investigated in 
order to understand the influence on the partitioning behavior of the solid solution hardening 
elements and the creep strength of nickel-base superalloys . The most important findings of 
this study are as follows: 
 Ti has a strong influence on the partitioning behavior of the solid solution 
strengtheners, especially of the low diffusing element W. 
 W has a strong influence on its own partitioning behavior and acts in a similar way as 
the alloying element Ti. 
 The partitioning ratio of W could be significantly decreased in alloy ERBO/39-Opt 
(

atoms in the  phase is increased. 
 The optimized partitioning behavior of solid solution strengtheners leads to a 
decreased effective diffusion coefficient and to an improved creep strength in 
particular at high temperatures and low stresses, where the creep deformation is based 
on dislocation movement in the  channels and along the / interface. 
 The higher efficiency of solid solution hardening elements in the investigated Re -free 
alloy ERBO/39-Opt results in cr eep properties comparable to Re containing 2 nd 
generation Ni-base superalloy CMSX-4 at least at high temperature s and low stres s 
creep conditions . Additional ly the Re -free alloys investigated in this study are not 
prone to the formation of deleterious TCP phases.
