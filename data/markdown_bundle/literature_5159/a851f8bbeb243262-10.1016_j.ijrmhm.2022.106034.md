ABSTRACT 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are important candidate materials for the design of advanced high- 
temperature structural materials. In this work, WMoVTiCr RHEAs were synthesized by mechanical alloying. 
The effect of Cr variation, second phase formation, and sintering temperature on microstructural evolution and 
mechanical properties of the model alloys were investigated. The results demonstrate that the alloy mainly 
consists of BCC solid solution phase, Ti-rich oxides, and a trace of Cr-rich phases. The hardness and compressive 
strength increase significantly with increasing Cr content, which could be ascribed to the dispersion strength -
ening of second-phase particles and the effect of Cr on the solid solution strengthening. However, the presence of 
a high amount of Cr content can lead to the reduction of compressive strain due to an increase in the brittle Cr- 
rich phases. Moreover, a lower sintering temperature caused an incomplete solid solution, thereby reducing 
material performance. 
1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) are usually composed of five or more 
principal elements in equimolar or near-equimolar ratios with an 
elemental concentration from 5 to 35 at.% [ 1 , 2 ]. Their high mixing 
entropy tends to form a simple solid solution, such as BCC, FCC, and HCP 
without intermetallic compound formation [ 3 – 5 ]. Refractory high- 
entropy alloys (RHEAs) consisting of Mo, W, V, Nb, Ta, Ti, Zr, and Hf 
refractory elements are important to improve high-temperature prop -
erties and radiation resistance, which enable increased operating tem -
peratures and are more resistant to neutron damage [ 6 – 9 ]. Therefore, 
RHEAs have attracted extensive attention in energy, nuclear power 
generation, aerospace, and automotive applications [ 10 – 12 ]. 
The most widely used method for the fabrication of RHEAs is in the 
liquid state using arc melting; however, it can cause elemental segre -
gation and dendrite structure [ 13 ]. Thereby, solid-state powder pro -
cessing by mechanical alloying (MA) is one of the most promising 
techniques for producing RHEAs. The ultra-fine grain and supersatu -
rated solid solutions in a nanocrystalline state can be synthesized by MA. 
A number of studies have shown excellent mechanical and physical 
properties of RHEAs fabricated by using mechanical alloying [ 14 – 16 ]. 
Among refractory elements, Cr plays a curial role in determining the 
properties of refractory high entropy alloys. The addition of Cr in RHEAs 
can not only improve corrosion resistance but also enhances the ductility 
of materials. However, the presence of Cr can lead to the formation of 
intermetallic phases such as Laves phase or sigma phase and thus 
significantly deteriorate the mechanical properties [ 17 – 19 ]. It has been 
proposed that the formation of disordered BCC solid solution phase and 
Cr-rich Laves phase was found in refractory WMoNbTiCr HEAs and as 
the content of Cr increased, the relative content of Laves phase increased 
[ 20 ]. However, the effect of Cr on the characteristics and synthesis of 
refractory high entropy alloys is still not clear. Therefore, in this work, 
the WMoVTiCr alloys with varied Cr contents prepared by MA were 
investigated. The aim of this work is to clarify the role of Cr on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of RHEAs. It is also important 
to understand how the Cr contents influence the second phase formation 
and the development of material performance. Besides, the WMoVTiCr 
alloys with different sintering temperatures were also studied in this 
work. The choice of sintering temperatures is crucial for the develop -
ment of second phases and oxide particles, which corresponds to the 
effectiveness of dispersion strengthening at elevated temperatures.

clearly observed and are the predominant failure mode by shear defor -

4. Conclusion 
The WMoVTiCr refractory high entropy alloys with the variation of 
Cr content were fabricated by mechanical alloying. Effects of Cr content, 
second phase, and sintering temperature on material properties were 
studied. The results indicate that the BCC solid solution phase was 
formed in the matrix and dispersed with Ti-rich oxide particles. A small 
amount of a Laves phase was only observed in the high Cr-containing 
RHEAs. In the present study, Ti-oxide is a dominant second phase, 
whereas the Laves phase formation is restricted to the model RHEAs. 
This could be ascribed to that Cr-rich oxides tend to form during high 
temperature sintering and might lead to the coexistence of mixed Laves 
phase and Cr-rich oxides. As the content of Cr increased, the hardness 
and compressive strength of the WMoNbTiCr HEAs were substantially 
increased. The result can be attributed to the microstructure refinement, 
solid solution, and oxide dispersion strengthening of the alloys. How -
ever, with an addition of 20 at.% Cr, the WMoVTiCr HEA exhibited a low 
compressive strain of ~6%. It implied that the high level of Cr encour -
ages the formation of brittle Cr-rich phases and thereby leads to 
embrittlement and reduction of ductility in RHEAs. Furthermore, the 
results show that a lower sintering temperature caused an incomplete 
solid solution, thereby reducing mechanical properties. On the other 
hand, considerable microstructural refinement and uniform chemical 
composition were achieved in the model alloys after a longer milling 
duration. In this stage, the dark particles associated with the Ti-rich 
oxide were dramatically refined and homogeneously dispersed in the 
BCC phase matrix. It resulted in a considerable increase in the hardness, 
compressive strength, and compressive strain with increasing milling 
times.
