abstract
Static tensile and fatigue fracture behavior of a high Nb-containing TiAl alloy with the composition of Ti-
45Al-8.5 Nb-(W, B, Y) fabricated by the plasma arc melting (PAM) have been investigated and correlated
with microstructure in this work. The results show that the as-obtained alloy preserves near lamellar
microstructure with homogeneous and relatively ﬁne lamellar colonies. Meanwhile, minor
β phase is
observed on colony boundaries or within lamellar colonies. The existence of the random distributed
borides is one of the main reasons for the re ﬁnement of the experimental alloy. The ultimate tensile
strength of the experimental alloy is 710 MPa and the fatigue limit with the 50% survival rate is
309.6 MPa. The fracture mechanism has been proposed and correlated with the microstructure of the
experimental TiAl alloy. It is found that ﬁne lamellar microstructure accounts for the high fracture
strength and moderate fatigue performance of the experimental high Nb-containing TiAl alloy. Fracto-
graphy analysis indicates the alloy exhibits a predominant transgranular and occasional interlamellar
fracture mode. The deformation incompatibilities occur at the boride/matrix and
β phase/matrix inter-
faces lead to the crack initiation and propagation in these positions, which are detrimental to the duc-
tility of the alloy.

1. Introduction
The progress in aerospace and automotive industries requires a
higher level of comprehensive properties and lower weight ma-
terials compared to the currently used ones. Due to the low den-
sity, high speci ﬁc strength and oxidation resistance at elevated
temperature, TiAl-based alloys have been attracted considerable
attention as strong candidates [1–5]. Recently, high Nb-containing
TiAl alloys have been designed and extensively investigated [6].
Comparing with the conventional TiAl alloys, high Nb-containing
TiAl alloys have been proven to preserve improved oxidation re-
sistance and creep strength at the elevated temperature [7–11 ].
Meanwhile, studies have shown that Ti-45Al-(5-10)Nb (in at%)
alloys with a small amount of alloying elements such as Mo, Cr, Ta,
C, B exhibit several desired properties and have been regarded as
the most promising high Nb-containing TiAl alloy for high tem-
perature structural applications [12–14].
However, as an intermetallic compound, low ductility and poor
fracture toughness of γ-TiAl alloys are still obstacles for their
practical applications [15,16]. During past decades, numerous
fruitful studies have been devoted on the composition design and
microstructure control to improve mechanical properties of γ-TiAl
alloys [1 7]. It has been reported that the fully lamellar and nearly
lamellar microstructures consisting γ and α2 have much higher
fracture toughness and creep resistance than those of other kinds
of microstructures [1]. At present, the thermo-mechanical pro-
cessing and heat treatment have been received considerable at-
tention in TiAl alloys [18,19]. However, based on the micro-
structure control, understanding the relationship between micro-
structure and properties is conducive to improve the performance
of TiAl alloys. Moreover, compared with titanium alloys and Ni-
base superalloys, TiAl alloys with low tolerance to long cracks al-
ways result in an uncertainty fatigue resistance [20,21]. Hence, it is
necessary to understand and handle these fatigue issues to assure
long-term reliability applications of these alloys. Based on the
relationship between the microstructure control and performance,
it is also necessary to illustrate the fatigue properties and the
corresponding fracture mechanisms of TiAl alloys to provide
guidelines for the optimization of microstructures.
The alloy with the composition of Ti-45Al-8.5 Nb-(W, B, Y) (in
at%) has been selected to illustrate microstructure related high
cycle fatigue and tensile mechanical properties in this work. The
fatigue properties of the experimental alloy are also evaluated.
And the relationship between microstructure and mechanical
properties, as well as the fracture mechanism, has been paid
special attention in this work. In addition, microstructure

2. Experimental procedures
The alloy with nominal composition Ti-45Al-8.5 Nb-(W, B, Y)
(at%) was prepared using the plasma arc melting (PAM) process by
double melting. The second melting was carried out from the
bottom of the ingot (330 mm /C2 750 mm /C2 1000 mm) to its top in
order to reduce the compositional heterogeneity. The chemical
analysis results showed that the composition of the ingot at the
different parts matched the nominal composition within the ex-
perimental error. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (VEGA-3
TESCAN) equipped with energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) was
employed for microstructural examination. All the samples for
SEM were prepared via conventional metallographic techniques.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Phase constitute and microstructural characterization
XRD pattern of the Ti-45Al-8.5 Nb-(W, B, Y) is shown in Fig. 2
(a). It is found that the alloy is mainly composed of γ and α2 phases
as shown in Fig. 2(a). Bragg peaks with low intensity from β phase
give evidences that the content of the β phase is relatively low
comparing with the dominant γ and α2 phases. According to the
phases identify via XRD pattern, it is observed that Nb atoms are
soluted in the matrix and occupy the site of Ti [22] as no diffrac-
tion peaks from niobium compounds can be observed in Fig. 2 (a).
In addition to the dominant γ and α2 phases, the existence of a
small amount of β phase can be attributed to the solidi ﬁcation
process of experimental high Nb-containing TiAl alloy. Based on
the pseudo-binary diagram of high Nb containing TiAl alloy pro-
posed by Chen et al. [7], Ti-45Al-8.5 Nb-(W,B,Y) solidi ﬁes from the
β primary phase during solidi ﬁcation. It has been reported pre-
viously that the Nb, W, Cr and Mo are β stabilizers in TiAl-based
alloys [23]. As the partition coef ﬁcients of Nb and W are both
higher than 1.0 in β solidiﬁcation stage, β phase is rich in Nb and
W [24]. Meanwhile, Nb addition decreases the transus tempera-
ture from β to α, and leads to the expansion of β phase ﬁeld to
higher Al concentration with the contraction of α phase ﬁeld [7].
Meanwhile, the β to α transformation is always incomplete owing
to the low diffusivity of these elements and fast cooling rate in the
experimental conditions. As a result, diffraction peaks from re-
sidual β phase are detected in the experimental high Nb-con-
taining TiAl alloy.
Fig. 2 (b) shows the microstructure of the experimental alloy in
a SEM-BSE, which is characterized by uniformly distributed re-
ﬁnement lamellar colonies. Using the intersection linear method,
the average lamellar colony size is about 90 μm. According com-
position analysis results listed in Table 1 , the phase with white
contrast to the matrix in the colony boundaries or inside colonies
is indenti ﬁed as the β phase. The reasons about the existence of β
phase have been mentioned above. It should be noted that the β
phase mainly exists either in colony boundaries and triple junc-
tions or in the lamellar colonies with different morphologies
(paralleling to the lamellar or undermining the continuity of the
lamella). According to Table 1, borides precipitation is detected in
the experimental alloy. The borides, in the forms of particle or
strip shape, which are random distributed and have no orientation
relationship with the matrix. Moreover, most of the acicular bor-
ides are parallel to the lamellar orient or penetrate into the la-
mellar colonies. In addition, the yttrium oxides have also been
observed in the alloy. Because of their low content, both borides
and yttrium oxides cannot be detected in the XRD pattern of the
alloy as shown in Fig. 2 (a).
It can be seen from Fig. 2 (b) that ﬁne lamellar colonies with
γ
and β phases along the colony boundaries are observed in the
experimental high Nb-containing TiAl alloy. A small amount of β
phases are parallel to the lamellar inside colonies, while borides
and yttrium oxides are random distributed within the matrix. The
uniform re ﬁned microstructure is attributed to the solidi ﬁcation
process and alloy composition. During the subsequent phase
transformation process from
β to α, the microstructure is char-
acterized by colonies with parallel α lamellas inside surrounded by
the residual β phase due to the formation of different orientation
variants of α within β grains. During the further cooling process, γ
phase precipitates from α phase according the orientation re-
lationship [25–27]. Finally, the re ﬁned microstructure is formed
with γþ(α2 þγ) lamellar structure. In addition to the re ﬁning ef-
fect of the β solidiﬁcation [26], it has been proven that boron
addition is an effective way to re ﬁne microstructure of TiAl alloys
[28,29]. This is one of the main reasons for the re ﬁnement mi-
crostructure of the experimental alloy. Due to the low solubility of
boron, borides have been found in the experimental high Nb-
containing TiAl alloy. It has been reported that the crystal struc-
ture and composition of the borides in TiAl alloys depend on the
aluminum content. When the aluminum content is higher than
44 at%, only TiB 2 is formed during solidi ﬁcation. On the contrary,
the type of boride depends on the boron content as the aluminum

3.2. Static tensile properties and characters of fractographs
The room temperature stress-strain curve of the experimental
alloy is shown in Fig. 3 with a strain rate of 7 /C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1. The ul-
timate tensile strength is 710 MPa with low plastic elongation
(0.43%). Compared with other high Nb containing TiAl alloys [31],
the experimental alloy has a higher tensile strength. Low ductility
denoted by the tensile elongation in this work can be ascribed to
the inherent nature of TiAl intermetallic including low mobility of
dislocation, low cohesive strength along different phases, etc. [32].
Fig. 4 presents the morphological features of tensile fracture
surfaces. It is apparent from Fig. 4 (a) that the fracture mode is a
typical cleavage fracture. Speci ﬁcally, the cracks predominantly
exhibit transgranular fracture manner in the region ‘1’. Meanwhile,
interlamellar fracture as manifested by small facets [33] and visi-
ble river patterns are also found occasionally in the region ‘2’ and
‘3’ respectively. Besides, as a distinct feature on fracture surface,
the long boride ribbons have been roughly aligned with the frac-
ture surface and approximately perpendicular to the tensile axis in
Fig. 4 (b) region ‘4’. Indentations are observed on the fracture
surface because of the boride ribbons have been pulled out of the
matrix during the process of fracture. Meanwhile, cracks in borides
can spread through their whole lengths instantaneously due to the
brittle nature of borides. Although the content of borides is less, it
can be inferred that the brittleness of the alloy is partially asso-
ciated with long boride ribbons.
Fig. 5 displays microstructures on the side of the failure sample,
enabling the determination of crack nucleation position and frac-
ture mode of the experimental alloy. It is frequently observed that
boride ribbons and strip-shaped
β phase present on the fracture
surfaces and internal of the fracture pro ﬁle. Fig. 5 (a) shows that
cracks nucleate at ‘1’ interior of β phase or ‘2’ interfaces of β phase/
matrix as well at ‘3’ and ‘4’ interfaces of boride/matrix in Fig. 5(b).
Meanwhile, partial boride ribbons have been pulled out of the
matrix during the tensile test as shown by arrow ‘3’ in Fig. 5 (b).
Combining with the smooth morphologies of the boride indenta-
tions, it indicates that a de-bonding between boride ribbons and
the matrix has occurred during the fracture process. Fig. 5 (c) and
(d) shows the modes of the local crack propagations in the ex-
perimental alloy. One case is that cracks nucleate inside the la-
mellar then propagate through the adjacent lamellar as shown in
region ‘5’ or along the lamellar colony boundaries as region ‘6’ in
Fig. 5(c). Another one is that cracks nucleate in the lamellar colony
boundaries or interfaces then propagate along the lamellar colony
boundaries as region ‘7’ or through the lamellar as region ‘8’ in
Fig. 5 (d). The fact can be inferred from comprehensive analysis of
Figs. 4 and 5 is that the deformation incompatibilities occurred at
boride/matrix and the
β phase/matrix lead to the interfacial stress
concentration, which in return induce the crack nucleations at

Compared with conventional TiAl based alloy [34,35], the ex-
perimental high Nb-containing TiAl alloy preserves a higher ten-
sile strength. In addition to the solid solution strengthening from
Nb atoms, it can be attributed to the re ﬁned lamellar colony size
resulted from the addition of boron. Nevertheless, as the interfaces
of boride/matrix are the preferred sites for the crack initiations
and propagations as discussed above, borides are believed to play
a negative effect on the ductility of high Nb-containing TiAl alloy.
Previous research [36] has shown that the nucleation sites of the
cracks in near lamellar TiAl alloys are expected to be formed at
α2/
γ interfaces or within γ grains during tensile testing. However, the
deformation incompatibilities occur at interfaces of boride/matrix
and the β phase/matrix due to the existence of borides and β
phase in the experimental alloy. The interfacial stress concentra-
tion induces cracks between their interfaces preferentially. It has
been reported that the fracture modes of the TiAl based alloys are
mainly related to the microstructures [36,37]. The fractographs in
Fig. 4 show a mixture of cleavage fracture in lamellar colonies and
partially transgranular failure in the equiaxed
γ grains, giving
evidences that the cleavage strain of the matrix is relatively higher
than cohesive strength of the lamellar colony boundaries.

The fatigue fracture morphologies of the experimental alloy are
shown in Fig. 7 . As discussed above about tensile properties, bor-
ide/matrix and the β phase/matrix interfaces also act as crack in-
itiation sites during fatigue tests. The fracture mode is a typical
cleavage-type transgranular fracture as indicated by region ‘1’ in
Fig. 7(a), which is predominant near the crack initiation sites with
visible river patterns in region ‘3’ [33]. The intergranular fracture
in region ‘2’ as manifested by small facet is occasionally observed
as shown in Fig. 7 (a). Similar to the tensile test fracture surfaces,
long boride ribbons have been also observed on the fracture sur-
faces as indicated by region ‘4’ in Fig. 7 (b). During the process of
fatigue fracture, boride ribbons are believed to be pulled out of the
matrix with the indentations left on the fracture surfaces. Fig. 7
(c) and (d) shows the formation of microstructure deformation,
hole and crack during the process of fatigue fracture.
In order to illustrate the fracture mode and crack initiation of
the alloy clearly, the pro ﬁle of the fatigue specimens are observed
with SEM images as shown in Fig. 8 . Two different kinds of sam-
ples, tested in the conditions of 315 MPa/1 /C2 10
7 cycles and
440 MPa/1.969 /C2 105 cycles ( si/Nf), are chosen to illustrate the
crack initiation of the experimental alloy. And unbroken sample is
better to re ﬂect the process of crack initiation. It is observed that
crack nucleation at ‘1’ interior of β phase or interface of β phase/
matrix in Fig. 8 (a) as well at ‘2’ interior of boride or interface of
boride /matrix in Fig. 8 (b). These results also con ﬁrm that cracks
always nucleate on the boride/matrix and the β phase/matrix in-
terfaces as discussed about tensile properties. Meanwhile, the
crack initiation sites also appear on yttrium oxides/matrix inter-
face as indicated by ‘3’ in Fig. 8(b), which has not been observed in
the tensile fracture. It indicates that the interfacial stress con-
centration leads to crack initiations on boride/matrix, the β phase/
matrix and yttrium oxide/matrix interfaces during fatigue tests.
The propagation of fatigue crack is the main reason for the failure
of the experimental alloy. However, under different stress levels,
the specimens show different fatigue fracture crack propagation
modes. At low applied stress 315 MPa, the fatigue cracks propa-
gation along the lamellar colony boundaries, boride/matrix and
the β phase/matrix interfaces as can be seen in Fig. 8(a) and (b). At
high applied stress 440 MPa, however, the fatigue cracks propa-
gate in a distinctly translamellar mode as can be seen in Fig. 8(c). It
indicates that the fracture mode is the preferentially intergranular
fracture and then transforms into a predominant transgranular
fracture during the process of approaching the fatigue limit of the
experimental high Nb-containing TiAl alloy. As shown in Fig. 8 (d),
slip bands are parallel to the lamellar interfaces in the broken
sample.
Due to the existence of β phase and borides in the experimental
high Nb-containing TiAl alloy, crack initiations at the interfaces of
boride/matrix and the β phase/matrix can be clearly seen in the
Fig. 8 . It has been reported that failure by surface or subsurface
crack nucleations can operate probabilistically at the same stress
level [40]. And subsurface cracks in unbroken sample in Fig. 8
(a) and (b) clearly re ﬂect the process of crack initiations. It means
that the subsurface crack nucleation mechanism plays a dominant
role on the fatigue fracture of the experimental alloy. Finally, the
fatigue cracks nucleate away from the specimen surface, which
contributes to improve the fatigue life of the experimental alloy.
During further crack propagation process, the expansion of cracks
has a close relationship with microstructure type [39], lamellar
spacing [41] and grain size [35], etc. The re ﬁnement of near la-
mellar microstructure makes the experimental alloy exhibit a good
resistance to crack propagation under cyclic loading conditions.
4. Conclusion
The experimental Ti-45Al-8.5 Nb-(W, B, Y) alloy is character-
ized by uniformly distributed re ﬁnement lamellar colonies. And
residual β phases mainly present on colony boundaries, triple
junctions and inside of the lamellar colonies. It is observed that the
tensile strength is higher than that of the conventional TiAl alloy
due to the Nb addition and ﬁne lamellar colonies. The deformation
incompatibilities occur at boride/matrix and the β phase/matrix
lead to the interfacial stress concentration. As a result, crack in-
itiations or propagations always take place on there, which are
both believed to account for the brittle failure of the experimental
alloy. Ti-45Al-8.5 Nb-(W, B, Y) exhibits the typical cleavage frac-
ture during tensile and fatigue measurements.
According to the equation of fatigue limit with the 50% survival
rate, fatigue limit s0 of the alloy is 309.6 MPa. In the process of
approaching fatigue limit, cracks nucleated in the subsurface ac-
count for the improvement of the fatigue life. And the
