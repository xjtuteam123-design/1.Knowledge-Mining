ABSTRACT 
Nb-1wt.%Zr was subjected to severe plastic deformation at room temperature using multi-axial forging (MAF) in 
a closed channel die to an effective strain of 4.6. Separate such MAF samples of Nb – 1Zr were subsequently 
annealed for 1 h at 450, 550, 600 and 700 
�
C to study the thermal stability of the microstructure after severe 
deformation. Electron backscatter diffraction measurements were used to characterize the microstructure in 
terms of grain size, local misorientations within grains and the fraction of recrystallization after annealing using 
the criteria of grain orientation spread. MAF increased the strength of Nb – 1Zr to 540 MPa as compared to 120 
MPa in the initial coarse grained condition. Subsequent annealing resulted in regions of recrystallized grains and 
an overall reduction in local misorientations. Annealing at 700 
�
C showed a bimodal grain size distribution with 
large recrystallized grains surrounded by deformed regions. The tensile strength reduced with increasing 
annealing temperature. The as-MAF and the 450, 550 and 600 
�
C annealed conditions showed low ductility 
(16%), whereas the 700 
�
C annealing condition showed a moderate ductility of 28% as compared to the coarse 
grained condition which showed a 44% ductility. It was estimated that the contribution of strength increase after 
MAF was more due to increase in dislocation density as compared to the reduction in grain size. 
1. Introduction 
The use of severe plastic deformation (SPD) to produce ultra-fine 
grained (UFG) materials is well established (see eg. Refs. [ 1 – 6 ]). In 
most materials, with increasing severity of deformation the grain size 
reduces and saturates to a value in the range of 100 – 500 nm [ 5 , 7 – 9 ] 
with the fraction of high angle grain boundaries saturating to between 
0.7 to 0.75 [ 5 , 10 , 11 ]. In addition, the dislocation density increases to 
high values of the order of 10
15 
m
 2 
[ 12 , 13 ]. This type of microstructure 
– ultrafine grained with a high dislocation density – results in an increase 
of the strength of material to about 3 times or more as compared to that 
with an annealed course grained microstructure. However the increase 
in strength comes at the cost of a significant reduction in ductility. 
Furthermore the microstructure is in a state of non-equilibrium, as the 
grain boundaries contain extrinsic dislocations [ 14 – 16 ]. This makes the 
microstructure of severely deformed materials unstable at elevated 
temperatures and amenable to grain coarsening [ 17 , 18 ]. Attempts have 
been made to stabilize the microstructure through annealing at inter -
mediate temperatures, such that sufficient energy is provided for the 
recovery of excess dislocations, but not enough for grain growth [ 17 , 
19 – 23 ]. Such a thermal treatment would also transform the low angle 
grain boundaries to high angle ones, thereby achieving a “ cleaner ” UFG 
microstructure with a possible improvement in ductility. 
Recently the technique of SPD has been applied to refractory metals 
and alloys such as V [ 24 ], Ta [ 25 – 29 ], Mo [ 23 ], Nb and Nb – Zr alloys 
[ 22 , 28 – 33 ]. Of these there are quite a few studies on SPD of Nb and its 
alloys as these are materials for bio-medical applications as well as 
conventional high temperature applications. The high temperature use 
of UFG Nb – 1Zr processed through SPD would require that their grain 
coarsening behavior be known. In one such study of Nb – 1Zr [ 22 ] it was 
shown that grain growth occurred only beyond an annealing tempera -
ture of 700 
�
C, below which the microstructure resembled that of the 
SPD condition. Most of the SPD carried out on Nb and its alloys was 
primarily using either the equal channel angular pressing (ECAP) or the 
high pressure torsion (HPT) technique. Multi-axial forging (MAF) is 
another SPD technique where the material is deformed sequentially 
along all three orthogonal axes [ 13 , 34 – 38 ]. So far there has been no 
reported work on subjecting Nb alloys using MAF. Whereas the imposed 
state of strain in ECAP and HPT is simple shear, that in MAF is plane 
strain, and hence it is possible that the evolution of microstructure

3.6. Identification of recrystallized grains 
Deformation results in an increase of dislocation density, of which 
the geometrically necessary dislocations give rise to the misorientations 
as measured through EBSD. Recrystallized grains would have lower 
levels of GNDs and hence lower misorientations. Whereas KAM is the 
measure of local misorientation at each pixel level, a measure of in-grain 
misorientation for a grain as a whole is the grain orientation spread 
(GOS); this is the average of the deviations of the orientation of each 
point in the grain from the average orientation of the grain. Thus each 
grain is assigned a GOS value. As the GND density is expected to be high 
for deformed grains, so would be the spread of orientation across the 
grain resulting in a large value of GOS. Conversely for a recrystallized 
grain the spread of orientations across the grain will be low resulting in a 
low value of GOS. In this study grains having GOS < 2

4. Discussion 
Nb-1wt.% Zr is a solid solution alloy which is relatively easy to 
deform at room temperature as compared to other refractory metals 
such as Mo and Ta based alloys. This work showed that Nb – 1Zr can be 
subjected to severe plastic deformation at room temperature via multi- 
axial forging in a channel die without causing material failure. The 
initial annealed condition of Nb – 1Zr had equiaxed coarse grains ranging 
from 10 to 400 μ m with a median of 100 μ m and with a large fraction of 
high angle boundaries. On undergoing SPD via MAF to six passes, i.e. to 
ε
eff 
¼ 4.6, a high fraction of low angle boundaries (about 88%) were 
observed and the grains with high angle boundaries were few. The 
spacing of these boundaries ranged from sub-micrometer values of about 
0.25 μ m to a few micrometers of around 3.5 μ m with a median value of 
0.9 μ m. Previous studies on SPD of Nb and its alloys using either ECAP or 
HPT technique showed that the resulting grain size was in the submicron 
regime. For example it ranged from 0.4 to 0.8 μ m for ECAP of Nb to ε
eff 
¼ 4.5 [ 32 ], an average grain size of 1.7 μ m after ECAP of Nb to ε
eff 
¼
5.75 [ 45 ], and an average grain size of 0.42 μ m after ECAP of Nb to ε
eff 
¼ 8.5 [ 29 ]. Previous study on Nb – 1Zr alloys ECAPed to an effective 
strain of 8 showed that the resulting grain size ranged from 250 to 500 
nm [ 30 ]. The grain size (including subgrain size) of Nb subjected to SPD 
depends on the extent of deformation (quantified by the effective strain 
of eq. (1) ) as well as the technique used. Most previous studies on Nb 
subjected to SPD used either ECAP or HPT, both being simple shear 
processes as compared to MAF which is a plane strain process. HPT is 
distinct from both ECAP and MAF in that a higher total strain can be 
imparted through HPT resulting in finer grains, eg. 100 – 350 nm after 
HPT of Nb [ 28 ]. When deformed to the same effective strain using either 
MAF or ECAP, the microstructural refinement in terms of subgrain sizes 
is similar. Another important microstructural feature is the fraction of 
high angle boundaries f
hab

showed that after ECAP to an effective strain of 16, f
hab 
was about 0.6 
[ 30 ]. Pure Nb after ECAP resulted in an even higher f

[ 32 , 45 ]. At present, comparing the fraction of high angle boundaries 
formed after ECAP and MAF is difficult as the effective strains of the 
present work and that in literature are different. Pure Nb however does 
seem to show a higher f

5. Summary and conclusions 
Nb-1wt.% Zr was subjected to severe plastic deformation at room 
temperature via multi-axial forging in a closed channel die to an effec -
tive strain of 4.6. This resulted in an ultra-fine grained microstructure 
with a large fraction of low angle boundaries. The ultimate tensile 
strength increased from 120 MPa for the initial coarse grained condition, 
to 540 MPa after SPD to an effective strain of 4.6. It was estimated that 
the strengthening caused by dislocation density increase was higher 
than that caused by reduction in grain size. Subsequent annealing for 1 h 
at 450 – 600 
�
C recovered the dislocation substructure and initiated 
recrystallization. Strength decreased with increasing annealing tem -
perature. Annealing at 700 
�
C for 1 h after MAF resulted in a bimodal 
grain size distribution, producing a reduced strength and increased 
ductility as compared to that of the as-MAF condition.
