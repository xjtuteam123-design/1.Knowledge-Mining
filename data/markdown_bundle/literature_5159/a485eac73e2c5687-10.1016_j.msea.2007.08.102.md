abstract
In this paper experimental data about the creep behavior of high-strength low-alloy (HSLA, ASTM A618)
steel are presented. For different elevated temperatures tensile tests are carried out and also for some
of the mentioned elevated temperatures uniaxial creep tests for different constant loads are made. The
results are useful for determining, e.g., the load-bearing capacity of a structure under ﬁre conditions as
well as for its lifetime prediction.

1. Introduction
Steam and gas turbines, automobile engines and other machines
and equipment use metallic materials at elevated temperatures
where time-dependent deformation and fracture process must be
considered in their design[1]. The phenomenon of time-dependent
deformation was ﬁrst referred to as stretch by Philips and as
viscous ﬂow by Andrade [2,3]. Later these became known as
creep.
Not so far all creep and all creep fracture studies are on metal-
lic materials, but creep of polymers is for example of considerable
importance in plastic and composite components while the creep of
ceramics is of interest in aerospace applications. However, the creep
of a structure may be deﬁned as a time-dependent behavior during
which its deformation continuously increase while the stress (load)
is kept constant [4]. Some materials, such as polymers or concrete
exhibit the creep behavior in the range of ordinary temperature, let
us say from 0 to 50
◦C, while metals exhibit the creep behavior at
about one-half of their absolute melting temperatureTm, for exam-
ple, at 205 ◦C for aluminum alloy, 370 ◦C for low-alloy steels, etc.
Creep may occur over a wide range of temperature, stress and time
combinations (T,/ESC, t) and it depends on material and environment.

5. Final remarks
Knowledge of material parameters enables ﬁnite element
modeling of structure response subjected to certain loads. In addi-
tion, although creep tests are short-time tests, the applicability
of structures made of ASTM A618 steel at elevated temperatures
is visible from creep curves. According to the experimentally
obtained results, based on creep tests, it may be concluded that
this material can be used in structure applications operating in
such environmental conditions where elevated temperatures
appear till certain levels. This implies the creep resistance at
temperature T =4 00
◦C and the load level less than 50% of F0.2 and
at T =5 00◦C and load level less than 40% ofF0.2.
