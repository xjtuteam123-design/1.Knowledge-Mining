ABSTRACT 
Metal carbide is a crucial reinforcing phase in Ni-base superalloys and plays a significant role in determining 
their mechanical properties. However, the interface between metal carbide and matrix is often considered as the 
starting point for crack formation in Ni-base superalloys. In this study, we have introduced nano-TiC into Ni-Cr- 
W superalloys through a casting method with varying nano-TiC content. The introduction of nano-TiC promoted 
the formation of MC carbides and restrained their growth by providing more nucleation sites for their formation. 
Additionally, it reduced the interfacial area between MC carbides and the matrix alloy. The impact of different 
nano-TiC contents on the microstructure and mechanical properties are also discussed for the Ni-Cr-W super -
alloys. The equiaxed crystal structure of the Ni-Cr-W superalloys has transformed from columnar to equiaxed due 
to the introduction of nano-TiC. The refinement of grains, dendrites, and MC carbides is more apparent, and the 
high-temperature tensile strength and yield strength significantly increased. The in-situ formation of MC carbides 
with nano-TiC as a nucleation matrix and the suppression of crystal growth contributed to the refinement of the 
crystal structure and improved the grain boundary strength. This work provides novel insights into the devel -
opment of high-strength and high-toughness nickel matrix composites. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based superalloys are known for their excellent thermal 
strength, which is mainly attributed to the eutectic strain strengthening 
caused by the precipitation of γ ′ phase in the alloy structure [1 – 4] . The 
strength of the γ ′ phase diminishes above 0.6 Tm (alloy melting point) 
due to its coarsening or even dissolution [5 – 7] . Metal carbide is one of 
the principal precipitation phases of Ni-based superalloys, and its impact 
on the strength and plasticity of these alloys should not be overlooked 
[8 – 10] . However, the interface between metal carbides and matrix is 
usually regarded as the nucleation site of creep and fatigue cracks in 
high-temperature alloys [11 – 13] . Long-term thermal exposure can lead 
to severe carbide decomposition, causing M
23
C
6 
precipitates to continue 
to coarsen and resulting in crack sprouting and extension [11,14,15] . 
Understanding the distribution and shape of carbides in the matrix of 
Ni-base superalloys, improving the interfacial relationship between 
carbides and matrix, and suppressing carbide decomposition are crucial 
for enhancing the mechanical properties of Ni-base superalloys. 
Prior research has demonstrated that the casting temperature of the 
alloy influences the morphology of MC carbides [16] . Due to the long 
solidification time of castings with high casting temperature, the MC 
carbides in the tissue grow continuously, resulting in mostly long 
chain-like or skeleton-like morphologies. Lowering the casting temper -
ature of the alloy can transform MC carbides from skeletal rod-like 
phases to granular ones [17 – 19] . MC carbides are typically distributed 
at grain boundaries and intra-grain, impeding dislocation movement, 
pinning grain boundaries, and providing some strengthening effect [20, 
21] . However, at high temperatures, MC carbides may decompose 
slowly during long aging or service and transform into M

3.1. Effect of the addition of nano-TiC on microstructure 
Fig. 3 shows the cross-section structure of the four samples. Fig. 3 (a) 
exhibits the low-magnification structure of Ni-Cr-W, which is composed 
of coarse columnar crystals growing inward from the shell, and the 
average macroscopic grain size is 8.9 ± 3.2 mm. Severe segregation 
during the solidification of the melt leads to the interlacing of dendrites 
inside the long columnar crystals of the matrix. The melt has solidified 
before feeding in time, resulting in clearly visible interdendritic voids. It 
can be observed in Fig. 3 (b) that the cross-section of the 0.5 wt% nano- 
TiC/Ni-Cr-W composite is equiaxed, and the average macroscopic grain 
size is 3.3 ± 0.9 mm. The dendrites and voids distributed inside the 
grains have been unable to be observed from the cross section, indi -
cating that grain refinement improves segregation. Fig. 3 (c) shows that 
1.0 wt% of nano-TiC particles further refine the grain size of Ni-Cr-W Ni- 
based high temperature alloy with smaller grain size relative to 0.5 wt% 
nano-TiC/Ni-Cr-W, with a macroscopic isometric fine grain size of 2.7 
± 0.6 mm on average and an aspect ratio closer to 1. Further increasing 
the mass fraction of nano-TiC ( Fig. 3 (d)), the average macroscopic grain 
size of 2.0 wt% nano-TiC/Ni-Cr-W is 2.5 ± 0.4 mm, and the grain size is 
almost unchanged.

3.4. Effect of the nano-TiC addition on mechanical properties 
Fig. 9 presents the stress-strain curves of the mechanical properties of 
the matrix alloy Ni-Cr-W and the Ni-Cr-W composites with 0.5 wt%, 
1.0 wt% and 2.0 wt% nano-TiC at room temperature. The tensile 
strength of Ni-Cr-W matrix alloy at room temperature is 735.9 
± 19.6 MPa, the yield strength is 532.9 ± 23.3 MPa, and the elongation 
is 8.9 ± 0.6%. When the addition amount of nano-TiC is 0.5 wt%, the 
room temperature tensile strength reaches 835.7 ± 26.1 MPa, which is 
13.45% higher than that of the original Ni-Cr-W sample, the yield 
strength reaches 601.6 ± 25.4 MPa, which is increased by 12.97%, and 
the elongation is 9.7 ± 0.6%, which is increased by 0.8%. When the 
addition amount of nano-TiC is 1.0 wt%, the room temperature tensile 
strength is 909.8 ± 22.3 MPa, an increase of 23.51%, the yield strength 
is 611.2 ± 22.5 MPa, an increase of 14.85%, and the elongation is 13.8 
± 1.0%, an increase of 4.9%. It can be seen that when the mass fraction 
of nano-TiC continues to increase on the original basis, the strength of 
the Ni-Cr-W composite material also increases. However, when the mass 
fraction of nano-TiC particles is 2.0 wt%, the room temperature tensile 
strength of the composite material is reduced to 801.9 ± 21.5 MPa, and 
the yield strength is 707.7 ± 21.8 MPa, and the material breaks imme -
diately after yielding, with almost no plastic deformation. It can be seen 
that the structure-activity relationship between the addition amount of 
nano-TiC particles and the room temperature tensile strength mainly 
depends on the effect of nano-TiC particles on the precipitation and 
morphology of MC carbides in the melt. When the mass fraction of nano- 
TiC particles increases, the morphology of MC-type carbides in the Ni- 
Cr-W structure changes from a long-chain skeleton to a fine particle, 
and is dispersed in the grains and on the grain boundaries. Under the 
combined action of smaller grains, spherical grain structure and 
dispersed small-grained MC-type carbides, the strength and plasticity of 
the material are simultaneously improved. When the mass fraction of 
nano-TiC particles is 2.0%, the morphology of MC-type carbides changes 
into long chains composed of fine particles, and the adjacent small- 
particle MC-type carbides in the spherical intergranular part contact 
each other to form large particles. The defects existing in or on the 
surface of the large-grained MC-type carbides formed by contact growth, 
under the action of load, the composite material is deformed, the grain 
boundary and grain deformation cannot be coordinated, and the large 
particle MC carbide is prone to instability and cracking, which greatly 
reduces the plasticity. 
When the material is in service at high temperature, the grain 
boundary will slide or even melt before the inside of the grain, which is 
usually the weak part of the material. Most of the hot end parts made of 
Ni-Cr-W superalloy serve in a high temperature environment of 
500 – 650
◦

morphology and distribution of MC-type carbides on the high- 
temperature mechanical properties of Ni-Cr-W Ni-based superalloys, 
all samples were subjected to a tensile test at 700

C higher than the 
service environment temperature.
