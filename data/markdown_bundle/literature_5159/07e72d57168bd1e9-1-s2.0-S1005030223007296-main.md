a b s t r a c t 
The prevention of hot cracking formation is of utmost importance in the production of the new Ni-Co 
based superalloys through the utilization of the electron beam smelting layered solidiﬁcation technique 
(EBSL), as it ensures exceptional homogeneity and dependable consistency of the specimens. In contrast 
to previous studies that focused on minimizing the liquid ﬁlm and solidiﬁcation range, our methodol- 
ogy adopts a distinct approach. In this research, a novel methodology was employed to mitigate internal 
stresses through the implementation of equiaxed grain layers via an alternately reduced cooling method. 
This ultimately resulted in the elimination of hot cracking. To be more speciﬁc, the transition from a 
columnar to an equiaxed structure was observed during the layer-by-layer construction process in the 
fabrication of the new Ni-Co based superalloy in EBSL. The EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy, when subjected to 
the alternating reduction cooling method, exhibited an internal stress of 49 MPa. This value represents 
a signiﬁcant reduction of 83.8% compared to the internal stress observed when employing the linear re- 
duction cooling method. Additionally, the solvus temperature of the γ- γ’ eutectic phases in EBSL-Ni-Co 
superalloys produced by the alternating reduction cooling method is signiﬁcantly higher. Intriguingly, the 
Nth layer of the EBSL-Ni-Co based superalloys produced by EBSL simultaneously heats treated with the 
preceding layers. And the low melting point phase gradually dissolved back into the matrix. The imple- 
mentation of an alternating reduced cooling method successfully mitigated the formation of the liquid 
ﬁlm in the γ- γ’ eutectic phase and the buildup of internal stresses in the EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy dur- 
ing its manufacturing process. These discoveries open up a novel preparation procedure pathway for the 
manufacture of crack-free superalloys with superior mechanical characteristics using EBSL.

1. Introduction 
The capacity of an aviation engine to function well at high tem- 
peratures is directly proportional to the engine’s overall eﬃciency 
[1] . The new Ni-Co based superalloy is the material of choice for 
the next generation of aero engines. This alloy has the ability to 
greatly enhance the service temperature of turbine discs by intro- 
ducing Co3 Ti, which improves the alloy’s service strength in region 
that service temperature [ 2 , 3 ]. The electron beam smelting layered 
solidiﬁcation technology (EBSL) is a recently developed technology 
that utilizes the high energy density, high vacuum, and high cool-

scopic segregation and the number of impurity elements within 
the sample [4–6] . Furthermore, when integrated with the layer-

it to fail to achieve its theoretical strength. Therefore, it is neces- 
sary to conduct research into the effect of the EBSL manufacturing 
process on the hot cracking susceptivity of the new Ni-Co based 
superalloy and take measures to control or eliminate the cracks.

The researchers employed experimental research methodologies 
to examine the underlying factors contributing to the development 
of hot cracks in superalloys characterized by their unweldable na- 
ture and susceptibility to hot cracking [10–13] . Recent research has 
shown that the CM247 alloy, which is produced through a pro- 
cess known as laser powder bed fusion, contains cracks that are 
simple to produce. Around the crack, there are columnar grains 
that are made up of grain boundaries that have low-angle grain 
boundaries. Additionally, the dislocation density is high around the 
grain boundaries, and there is a phenomenon known as Hf/W/Ti/Ta 
enrichment. Cracking was brought on by the buildup of internal 
stress in a large component made of CMSX-4 alloy that was man- 
ufactured using electron beam powder bed fusion [14] . This type 
of cracking is mainly represented by hot cracks, which usually oc- 
cur at the end of the solidiﬁcation process or during the remelt- 
ing process. Hot cracks are typically thought to be caused by el- 
emental segregation, which creates an unstable liquid ﬁlm. Addi- 
tionally, the synergistic effect between the liquid ﬁlm and the in- 
ternal stresses generated by cooling shrinkage leads to the devel- 
opment of hot cracks in the material [15] . Therefore, one of the 
most pressing concerns in this area of research involves how to 
prevent hot cracks through better management of the stability of 
the liquid ﬁlm and the distribution of internal stress in the sample. 
Research shows that the stability of the liquid ﬁlm is related to 
the chemical composition of the alloy. The researchers discovered 
that the microstructure size and type can be optimized in the alloy 
by adjusting the ratio of elements (Zr, Hf, Si, C, and B) to reduce 
the hot cracking tendency of the alloy [ 16 , 17 ]. Until now, there 
have been very few superalloys that have prevented hot cracking 
by optimizing the ratios of their elements [ 12 , 13 , 16 ]. In addition, 
it has been demonstrated that the mechanical properties of such 
alloys prepared by additive manufacturing with adjusted minor al- 
loying element content may be adversely affected by strength and 
creep life degradation [ 18 , 19 ]. Taking into account the fact that 
stress concentration and the presence of brittle regions (such as 
high-angle grain boundaries, liquid ﬁlm regions, and other such 
areas) are necessary factors for the initiation of cracks [ 20 , 21 ]. It 
is generally accepted that hot cracking can be avoided during the 
production of EBSL samples, provided that the residual stresses 
that are concentrated in brittle regions can be effectively relieved. 
Research has shown that the ﬁne equiaxed grain microstructure is 
more easily adapted to the strain accumulation within the alloy 
due to thermal cycling during additive manufacturing [22] . To be 
more speciﬁc, equiaxed grain growth makes grain rotation and de- 
formation during strain adaptation much simpler. At present, there 
are two methods to achieve grain reﬁnement. The ﬁrst is to obtain 
ﬁnely equiaxed grains by adding grain reﬁners to the raw material 
[ 23 , 24 ]. The second is to control the rate of solidiﬁcation and the 
temperature gradient at the front of the solid-liquid interface by 
optimizing the intensity of the heat source, the scan rate, the scan 
path, and any other relevant preparation process parameters in or- 
der to force the sample to go through the columnar to equiaxed 
transformation (CET) [24–26] . At the moment, there is neither a 
suitable nor eﬃcient grain reﬁner available for superalloys. Because 
of this, the microstructure of the EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy sample can 
only be optimized by making adjustments to the temperature gra- 
dient and the rate of solidiﬁcation at the solidiﬁcation frontier in 
accordance with Hunt’s CET criterion [25] . As a result, the objec- 
tive of this study is to produce EBSL samples that are composite 
structures of columnar grain and equiaxed grain and devoid of hot 
cracks by basing them on the CET transformation criterion. 
In this study, the EBSL process employs different cooling meth- 
ods in order to control the grain structure of the EBSL-Ni-Co su- 
peralloy that is produced. The preparation of the periodic compos- 
ite structures involves alternately reducing cooling to increase CET 
production. The evolution pattern of the EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy’s 
microstructure has been characterized after extensive research into 
the alloy’s microstructure in a variety of thermal cycle zones. To 
shed light on how internal stress leads to cracking, both the stress 
distribution of the EBSL-prepared samples and the elemental dis- 
tribution of the liquid ﬁlm at the fractures are analyzed and dis- 
cussed. On the basis of this, it is possible to create superalloys with 
exceptional mechanical properties by altering the EBSL parameters, 
which offers a method for high-quality sample preparation.

2.1. Experimental materials 
The nominal chemical composition of the new Ni-Co-based su- 
peralloys is shown in Table 1 . The raw material for the alloy is a 
rod-shaped piece with ϕ90 mm × 800 mm that is produced by 
vacuum induction melting (VIM). This rod-shaped piece is then

3. Results 
3.1. Crystal structure and type 
Samples of EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy were created by employing 
the EBSL process and utilizing the LRC and ARC parameters. The 
LRC cooling method decreases the electron beam’s power in a lin- 
ear manner throughout the cooling process, whereas the ARC cool- 
ing method decreases the electron beam’s power in a periodic and 
alternating manner. Fig. 3 depicts the microstructure observed in 
the cross-sectional analysis of the EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy specimens 
produced by LRC and ARC. Based on the visual representation in 
Fig. 3 (a), it can be observed that the top and middle sections of the 
LRC sample exhibit a distinct pattern of coarse columnar grains, 
which are observed to grow in alignment with the layered solid- 
iﬁcation structure. As can be seen in Fig. 3 (b), the grains in this 
region have an average width of 527 μm. It is noteworthy that the 
grain sizes in the top ( Fig. 3 (b)) and middle ( Fig. 3 (c)) of the LRC 
sample exhibit a gradual increase in coarseness as the direction 
of the layered solidiﬁcation construction arrangement progresses. 
This indicates that some unfavorable grains with non-preferential 
orientation are eliminated due to the competitive development of 
grains during the layered solidiﬁcation construction, resulting in a 
gradual coarsening of the grains. This occurs as a result of the com- 
petitive growth of grains during the layered solidiﬁcation construc- 
tion. The bottom of the LRC sample exhibits ﬁne equiaxed grains,

than those of coarse columnar grains [32] . The presence of ﬁne

the crystal structure in the middle of the ARC sample’s composite 
structure. Additionally, it should be noted that equiaxial grains ex- 
hibit a decreased grain boundary density and possess smaller crys- 
tal sizes. Consequently, this characteristic results in a more homo- 
geneous distribution of stress within the crystal, thereby mitigating 
the impact of grain boundaries on stress transfer.

3.3. Structure of hot cracks in LRC sample 
The EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy samples that have been produced 
using the LRC cooling method exhibit hot cracking at the bottom. 
Fig. 6 shows the morphology and microstructure of hot cracks at 
the bottom of the EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy sample prepared by the 
LRC cooling method. The pseudo-three-dimensional (3D) conﬁgu- 
ration of the hot cracks is located at the base of the LRC sample 
as shown in Fig. 6 (a). The LRC sample exhibits the presence of hot 
cracks, which are located in close proximity to its bottom region. 
These cracks are observable as they extend towards the direction of 
layered solidiﬁcation construction, as depicted in Fig. 6 (a). Fig. 6 (b, 
c) shows the magniﬁed observation of the hot crack areas at the

crack occurs in the bottom region and subsequently propagates in 
the direction of construction. The microstructural composition in

tals that exhibit varying growth orientations (yellow arrows). As 
the process of layered solidiﬁcation construction, the growth di- 
rections of grains compete with each other, displaying a growth 
direction that converges toward the center of the structure. The 
competing growth of grains results in tensile stresses being ap-

4.3. Formation of hot cracks 
The results clearly show that the cracks observed at the bottom 
of the EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy samples produced by the LRC cool- 
ing method are liquefaction cracks. The formation of liquefaction 
cracks has been shown to be primarily caused by the re-melting 
of the low melting point eutectic phase in the heat-affected zone 
due to the high-energy beam, resulting in the formation of holes 
[51] . Splitting or cracking along grain boundaries occurs due to the 
application of tensile stress. 
4.3.1. Formation of liquid ﬁlm 
The formation of a liquid ﬁlm is a crucial prerequisite for the 
development of liquefaction fractures in a material. Research in 
additive manufacturing has revealed that the formation of liquid 
ﬁlms in the heat-affected zone is attributed to the liquefaction of 
the low melting point phase [ 10 , 52 ]. The EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy, 
manufactured using the LRC cooling method, exhibits a distinct 
arrangement of liquid ﬁlm surrounding the hot cracks, as shown 
in Fig. 11 (a). And the identiﬁcation of the γ- γ’ eutectic phase in 
the vicinity of the liquid ﬁlm has been observed. Furthermore, the 
phenomenon of elemental segregation involving Ti and Co was ob- 
served in the vicinity of the hot cracks, as depicted in Fig. 11 (b1–
b8). The ﬁndings of this research diverge from prior research that 
exhibited segregation of the Zr or B elements [ 12 , 18 , 53 , 54 ]. The 
presence of a larger sized γ- γ’ eutectic phase in the LRC samples 
is evident from the results shown in Fig. 11 (a, c). The propensity 
of the precipitated phase to undergo liquid ﬁlm formation is di- 
rectly proportional to its size [18] . Hence, the γ- γ’ eutectic phase 
observed in LRC samples serves as the primary origin of the hot 
cracked liquid ﬁlm.

reaction occurs in the local region due to non-equilibrium rapid so- 
lidiﬁcation during the formation of the initial layer through EBSL. 
As a result, the local area between the inter-dendritic core ex- 
hibits a high eutectic composition, leading to the enrichment of 
the low melting point phase around the inter-dendritic core. Dur- 
ing the continuation of EBSL, the previously solidiﬁed cladding lay- 
ers undergo complex thermal cycling effects once again. As a result 
of subsequent thermal cycles, the low melting point phase origi- 
nally conﬁned within the solidiﬁcation layer undergoes a transfor- 
mation into a liquid ﬁlm. Fig. 13 (c) demonstrates that the residual 
stress near the liquid ﬁlm is compressive stress at this stage. Con- 
sequently, the formation of hot cracks is avoided. The magnitude of 
compressive stress between the newly formed layer and the pre- 
ceding layer increases with the growing number of layer solidiﬁ- 
cation layers in the constructed structure. Fig. 13 (d) demonstrates 
the conversion of previously existing compressive stresses within 
the preceding layers into tensile stresses. Fig. 13 (e) demonstrates 
that cracks develop along the grain boundaries resulting from liq- 
uefaction when the tensile strain surpasses the plastic deformation 
capacity of the liquid ﬁlm at those grain boundaries. This leads to 
the formation of cracks along the ﬂuid ﬁlm. 
4.3.3. Crack extension 
Stress serves as both the driving factor for crack initiation and 
the driving force for subsequent crack propagation [ 62 , 63 ]. Along 
the direction of the layer solidiﬁcation structure, stress progres- 
sively increases during the EBSL process. This observation aligns 
with the distribution of hardness in EBSL-Ni-Co superalloy sam- 
ples. Simultaneously, the contraction of the new layer is con- 
strained by the layer beneath it. Consequently, the compressive 
stress present in the preceding layer gradually diminishes and 
eventually transforms into tensile stress. It has been demonstrated 
that compressive stresses develop near the interface between the 
new layer and the underlying one [64] . As a result, the cracks 
identiﬁed in this study propagate downward towards the under- 
lying layer rather than extending upwards to the surface of the 
new layer, where interlaminar compressive stress exists. This phe- 
nomenon is depicted in Figs. 6 (a) and 13 (f).

As part of the EBSL preparation process, a distinctive chilling 
mechanism is implemented to regulate the sample structure of the 
ESBL-Ni-Co superalloy. The incorporation of a composite structure
