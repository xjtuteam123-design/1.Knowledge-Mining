Low Temperature Hydrogen Effects on the Strength and Ductility of 
Nb-Ta Alloys 
C. V. OWEN and O. BUCK 
Ames Laboratory, Iowa State University, Ames, 114 5(X)ll (U.S.A.) 
(Received February 29.1988; in revised form April 21, 1988) 
Abstract 
Hydrogen effects on the strength and ductifity of 
several Nb-Ta alloys were examined at low tem- 
peratures. The results indicated that hydrides are 
not responsible jor the embrittlement observed or 
for the strengthening found to occur at low temper- 
atures. These results are compared with similar 
investigations performed on other group VA alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Solid solution refractory alloys are being 
extensively evaluated as possible structural 
materials in fusion power reactors. Although 
hydrogen embrittlement is not considered a prob- 
lem in this application, hydrogen in some form 
will always be present in these reactors and could 
become a cause for concern. 
Certain combinations of the group VA ele- 
ments form solid solution alloys across the whole 
system and exhibit a high solubility for hydrogen 
[1]. As part of an extensive study of such alloys, 
the Nb-Ta system and the effects of small hydro- 
gen additions on selected mechanical properties 
have been determined and compared with the 
results obtained earlier on the V-Nb system [2, 3]. 
2. Experimental details 
Tensile specimens with a gauge length of 25.4 
mm and a diameter of 2.03 mm were prepared 
from niobium and tantalum as well as alloy speci- 
mens consisting of niobium plus 10, 25, 50, 75 
and 90 at.% Ta. All specimens were fractured in 
tension at an initial strain rate of 8.3 x 10 -5 s- 
and at test temperatures from 78 to 300 K. Some 
of these specimens were essentially hydrogen free 
whilst others contained 0.3 at.% H. Hydrogen 
was introduced into the specimens after electro- 
polishing to remove any surface defects using a 
modified Sievert's apparatus and thermal decom- 
position of uranium hydride as the hydrogen 
source [2]. Gas extraction hydrogen analyses were 
made on a representative number of samples 
after testing and no loss of hydrogen was found 
compared with the initial charging analysis. 
All the hydrogen-charged material was 
examined by low temperature microscopy 
between 78 and 300 K to determine the possible 
presence of hydride precipitation. Evidence of 
precipitation was found for niobium, tantalum, 
Nb-10at.%Ta and Nb-90at.%Ta. The data pre- 
sented here will be only for pure niobium and 
tantalum, and for 50at.%Nb-50at.%Ta alloy 
since similar results were obtained for all other 
alloys.

When 0.3 at.% H was added to niobium, tanta- 
lum and the alloys, some additional strengthening 
was observed but only at the lower test tempera- 
tures. The incremental strengthening over the 
entire range of test temperatures resulting from 
the addition of hydrogen is depicted in Fig. 2. 
These data show that the strengthening resulting 
from hydrogen is due to a thermally activated 
process. This type of strengthening has been 
reported previously for niobium and tantalum 
[5-7]. It was earlier related to the reorientation of 
hydrides [7], but later results [8] showed that 
hydrides of niobium and tantalum do not reorient 
as do those of vanadium [9]. It was evident from 
the microscopy studies that niobium and tanta- 
lum precipitated hydrides at 175 K and 160 K 
respectively, whilst 50at.%Nb-50at.%Ta alloy 
did not. From the present data it is therefore 
apparent that there is no correlation between 
strengthening and hydride formation as noted 
before [5]. All the other alloys also exhibited 
this same type of low temperature hydrogen 
strengthening whether they precipitated hydride 
(Nb-10at.%Ta and Nb-90at.%Ta) or not 
(Nb-25at.%Ta and Nb-75at.%Ta).

However, when 0.3 at.% H was added the duc- 
tility behavior changed drastically as can be seen 
from the lower curves. Niobium, tantalum and the 
alloy all exhibit a pronounced drop in ductility 
followed by a region of minimum ductility and 
ending with varying amounts of a ductility return 
[3]. Similar results were also observed for all 
other alloys tested. 
The catastrophic loss of ductility would not 
appear to be dependent on hydride formation 
since it occurs, in all cases, at temperature well 
above the equilibrium precipitation temperature 
(about 65 K for niobium and tantalum and above 
125 K in the case of 50at.%Nb-50at.%Ta). Simi- 
lar embrittlement, apparently caused by hydro- 
gen in solution, has also been observed by the 
authors in other refractory metal alloy systems [2, 
10, 11 ] and by other investigators [ 12-14] who 
have worked with pure refractory metals. 
The fracture surfaces of the hydrogenated pure 
metals and all alloys changed from ductile tearing 
for samples with high reduction of area (RA) to 
transgranular cleavage for samples with low RA.
