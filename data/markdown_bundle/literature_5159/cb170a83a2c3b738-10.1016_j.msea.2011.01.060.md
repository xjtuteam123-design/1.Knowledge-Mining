abstract
The effects of Cr addition on the microstructure and stress–rupture property have been investigated
in three experimental Ni-based single crystal superalloys containing various levels of Cr addition
(0–5.7 wt.%). The Re partitioning ratio increased and the lattice misﬁt became more negative with increas-
ing the Cr addition in both dendrite core and interdendritic region. The changes of elemental partitioning
behaviors and the lattice misﬁt show good agreement with the change of /H9253
′ morphology. Cr addition
increased the stress–rupture life at 1100 ◦ C/140 MPa signiﬁcantly due to higher /H9253′volume fraction, more
negative lattice misﬁt and a well rafting structure as well as less width of /H9253channels. High Cr addition
(5.7 wt.%) increased the degree of Re and Cr supersaturation in the /H9253phase and promoted the formation
of topologically close-packed (TCP) phases signiﬁcantly under thermal exposure and creep deformation
at 1100
◦ C.

1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal superalloys have been used extensively
for turbine blades in aircraft engines. Advanced Ni-based single
crystal superalloys typically contain signiﬁcant levels of refractory
alloying additions such as Re, W, Ta and Mo in order to achieve a
remarkable creep resistance at elevated temperature [1,2]. How-
ever, high levels of refractory alloying additions also result in
an increased propensity for the formation of topologically close-
packed (TCP) phases during high temperature thermal exposure
[3,4]. TCP phases are detrimental to high-temperature performance
of superalloys due to their brittle nature and depleting potent solid
solution strengthening elements from the /H9253matrix. Chromium (Cr)
is generally added to Ni-based superalloys because it is beneﬁcial
to hot corrosion and oxidation resistance [5]. However, high levels
of Cr addition also result in detrimental TCP phases. In order to add
more refractory alloying additions with keeping microstructural
stability, modern commercial Ni-based single crystal [1] and direc-
tionally solidiﬁed [6] superalloys typically contain lower levels of
Cr. In addition, Cr is an effective solid solution strengthener [5].
However, there were seldom systematic investigations on whether

2. Experimental procedures
Three experimental Ni-based single crystal superalloys with dif-
ferent levels of Cr addition (0–5.7 wt.%) were directionally solidiﬁed
(DS) as single crystal bars (14 mm in diameter and 150 mm in
length). Their compositions were determined by an atomic absorp-
tion spectroscope and are listed in Table 1 . Alloy USTB-F1 is the
baseline alloy without Cr addition. Alloys USTB-F2 and USTB-F3
contain intermediate level (2.8 wt.%) and high level (5.7 wt.%) of
Cr, respectively. After solution treatment, thermal exposure tests
were carried out at 1100
◦C for different time. After the standard
aging treatment (1140 ◦C/8 h + 870◦C/16 h), the specimens of the
stress–rupture and creep tests were machined and the dimension
of the gauge section was 5 mm in diameter and 25 mm in length.
The stress–rupture testing was conducted at 1100
◦C/140 MPa for
three investigated alloys. Two specimens were tested for the same
alloy. In order to investigate the effect of Cr addition on the ten-
dency of the rafting behavior under the stress condition, the creep
testing at 1100
◦C was interrupted after the creep strain reached
to 1% for three alloys. The maximum creep stress of 130 MPa was
selected due to the limit of creep facilities. The stress–rupture and
creep tests were conducted in the air.
Metallographic specimens for microstructural investigation
were prepared and etched in a solution of 1% HF, 33% CH
3COOH, 33%
HNO3 and 33% H 2O. Microstructural observations were conducted

scope (FE-SEM) equipped with an energy-dispersive spectroscope

entiate between the dendrite core and the interdendritic region.

4. Discussion
The /H9253//H9253
′ lattice misﬁt ( ı) is an important characteristic of Ni-
based single crystal superalloys, and it inﬂuences the morphology
of /H9253’ precipitates [12,13,18] and mechanical properties [19,20]
as well as microstructural stability [18]. It is well accepted that
the elemental partitioning ratio ( /H9253–/H9253
′ composition), the lattice
misﬁt and the precipitate morphology are interrelated. The inﬂu-
ence of the sign and magnitude of lattice misﬁt on the precipitate
morphology has been investigated in details in Ni–Al–Mo ternary
alloys [12]. Spherical precipitates were observed in alloys with a
near zero misﬁt, whereas when the lattice misﬁt deviated from
zero in either the positive or negative magnitude, /H9253
′ precipitates
became cuboidal. Recently, the simulated results using phase-ﬁeld
simulation showed good agreement with the above mentioned
experimental results [13].
4.1. Elemental partitioning ratio, lattice misﬁt and /CR
′morphology
Chromium addition was found to affect the elemental partition-
ing ratios (especially Re), signiﬁcantly [8–11]. Re partitioned much
more strongly to the /H9253phase in a Ni–Al–Cr–Re quaternary alloy,
compared to a Ni–Al–Re ternary alloy [8]. Huang and Chang pre-
dicted by thermodynamic modeling that Re partitioning ratio in the
Ni–Al–Cr–Re quaternary system is close to 1 and it increased with
increasing the Cr content [9]. Furthermore, Dreshﬁeld and Thomas
reported that Re partitioning ratio increased from 10.5 to 19.0 with
increasing the Cr content from 1.6 wt.% to 4.1 wt.% in multicompo-
nent Ni-based single crystal superalloys containing 4.5 wt.% Ru and
2.1 wt.% Mo [10]. Recently, Carroll et al. found that Re partitioning
ratio increased from 5–8 to 12–17 when Cr addition increased from
3.4 wt.% to 6.7 wt.% in UM-F series multicomponent Ni-based single
crystal superalloys containing 5.7 wt.% Ru and 3.0 wt.% W [11]. The
results that Re partitioning ratio increased with increasing levels of
Cr addition are of particular importance in terms of the lattice mis-
ﬁt. According to Eq. (3), the strong Re partitioning to the /H9253matrix
caused by Cr addition results in more negative lattice misﬁt since
Re has a large atomic radius.

alloys [15,21,24].

4.2.4. Effect of solid solution strengthening
Another factor to inﬂuence the stress–rupture life is the solid
solution strengthening, it inhibits dislocation gliding through the
matrix. Re is known to be the most effective solid solution strength-
ening element in Ni-based single crystal superalloys due to its
tendency to form clusters [25] and its low diffusivity [26].I ti s
also well accepted that Cr is an effective solid solution strength-
ening element [5]. In the current study, Re partitioned more
severely to the /H9253phase and the corresponding Re concentration
of the /H9253matrix in the dendrite core and interdendritic region
increased for 4.1 at.% (10.6 wt.%) and 2.9 at.% (7.9 wt.%), respec-
tively, from the Cr-free alloy to the alloy containing 5.7 wt.% Cr
(Table 3 ). In addition, more Cr partitioned to the /H9253matrix with
increasing levels of Cr addition. For these reasons, a stronger con-
tribution of Re and Cr to solid solution strengthening would be
expected in alloy USTB-F3 than that of alloys USTB-F1 and USTB-
F2.
In summary, the combination of more completely rafting struc-
ture, higher Orowan resistance and solid solution strengthening
by Re and Cr contributed to the longer stress–rupture life of alloy
USTB-F3 than that of alloy USTB-F2.

5. Conclusions
(1) The morphology of /H9253
′ precipitates was nearly spherical in the
dendrite core of Cr-free alloy after aging at 1100◦C for 8 h. With
increasing the Cr content, Re partitioned preferentially to the /H9253
phase, which in turn resulted in more negative lattice misﬁt and
more cuboidal /H9253
′precipitates. In the mean time, the /H9253′volume
fraction increased while the precipitate size and the width of /H9253
channel decreased with increasing levels of Cr addition. In the
interdendritic region, the changes of Re partitioning ratio and
lattice misﬁt due to Cr addition showed the similar trend to the
dendrite core. The changes of elemental partitioning behavior
and lattice misﬁt showed good agreement with the change of
/H9253
′morphology.
(2) The average stress–rupture life of the Cr-free baseline alloy
was only 29.5 h at 1100 ◦C/140 MPa. Cr addition increased the
stress–rupture life signiﬁcantly since it increased the /H9253′ vol-
ume fraction, decreased the /H9253channel width and resulted in
more negative lattice misﬁt in both dendrite core and inter-
dendritic region with a well rafting structure perpendicular to
the applied tensile stress. And the average stress–rupture life
of high Cr-containing (5.7 wt.%) alloy was 3.7 times longer than
that of Cr-free baseline alloy.
(3) High level of Cr addition (5.7 wt.%) and the corresponding
high Re and Cr supersaturation in the /H9253phase promoted the
formation of TCP phases under thermal exposure and creep
deformation signiﬁcantly at 1100
◦C.
