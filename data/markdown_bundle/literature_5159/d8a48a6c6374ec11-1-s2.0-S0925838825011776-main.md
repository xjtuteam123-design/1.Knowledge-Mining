ABSTRACT
Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) is extensively used to manufacture Ni-based superalloys, as for aerospace engine 
components and other hot-end materials. Compared to precipitation-strengthened additively manufactured (AM) 
Ni-based superalloys, research on solution-strengthened AM Ni-based superalloys, such as GH3230, remains 
scarce. Even rarer are studies examining the impact of heat treatment (HT) temperature on the microstructure 
and tensile properties across different directions, specifically the building and scanning directions. In this study, 
the effects of HT on the microstructure and high-temperature tensile properties of GH3230 prepared by LPBF 
were investigated using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD), trans -
mission electron microscopy (TEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and thermodynamic modeling. The results indicate 
that an appropriate heat treatment temperature can effectively relieve residual stress and reduce the anisotropy 
of the material ’ s tensile properties. When heat treated at 1220
◦
C, 1250
◦
C, and 1280
◦
C, the grain morphology of 
the samples evolves from columnar grains to equiaxed grains. At 1250
◦
C, the samples exhibit the best overall 
tensile properties. The transverse yield strength is 213 MPa, the tensile strength is 274 MPa, and the elongation is 
49.5 %. The longitudinal yield strength is 203 MPa, the tensile strength is 259 MPa, and the elongation is 70.5 %. 
At 1280
◦
C, excessive growth of recrystallized grains leads to stress redistribution, making the grains more prone 
to deformation, which in turn inhibits the occurrence of recrystallization. This provides a solid theoretical 
foundation for optimizing the heat treatment process and improving the tensile properties of nickel-based 
superalloys.
1. Introduction
GH3230 (with trade name Haynes 230 and UNS N06230) is a solid- 
solution-strengthened nickel-based superalloy strengthened by solid 
solution atoms and carbides, containing abundant Cr, W, and Mo ele -
ments [1 – 3] . The Cr enhances the alloy ’ s resistance to oxidation and 
corrosion, while W and Mo serve as solid solution strengthening agents, 
reinforcing atomic bonds and raising the activation energy for diffusion, 
and slowing down diffusion processes [4] . These mechanisms enhance 
the alloy ’ s thermal strength, demonstrating superior thermal stability at 
elevated temperatures compared to γ’ / γ’’ -precipitation-strengthened 
superalloys [2,5] . Furthermore, GH3230 is widely recognized for its 
exceptional attributes, including high strength, resistance to thermal 
and cold fatigue, oxidation, corrosion, and structural stability [6,7] . As a 
result, it finds extensive applications across aerospace, petrochemical, 
power generation, and other industries [8 – 10] .
As manufacturing technology progresses, the complexity of hot-end 
components such as combustion chambers and fuel nozzles in aircraft 
engines has increased [11 – 14] . Using traditional forming methods to 
manufacture GH3230 as the primary high-temperature structural ma -
terial has become challenging [15 – 17] . Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) 
technology, a type of laser additive manufacturing, presents a promising

based on a 3D slicing model of the parts [18 – 20] . This is achieved by 
selectively melting alloy powder layers point by point, line by line, and 
layer by layer using a laser, eliminating the need for complex molds

During the LPBF manufacturing process, the combination of 
extremely high cooling rate and layer-by-layer formation characteristics 
leads to residual stresses in the as-built (AB) samples. The microstruc -
ture displays significant anisotropy along both the scanning and build -
ing directions, thereby resulting in anisotropy of mechanical properties. 
This presents a barrier to further industrial applications [24,25] . Heat 
treatment (HT), as a widely applied method, effectively adjusts the 
structure and properties of alloys. Heat treatment processes such as so -
lution treatment and tempering enable the adjustment of steel ’ s hard -
ness, strength, and toughness to meet diverse application needs [26,27] . 
Furthermore, heat treatment modifies the grain structure and precipi -
tate phase morphology of aluminum alloys, thereby adapting their 
hardness, strength, and corrosion resistance [28] . Additionally, it im -
proves the mechanical properties and corrosion resistance of magnesium 
alloys, lowers their plastic deformation temperature, and enhances their 
strength and toughness [29,30] . Therefore, implementing appropriate 
heat treatment protocols is of paramount importance in achieving su -
perior performance of the GH3230 alloy.
Extensive research on HT of AM Ni-based superalloys has been 
conducted [31 – 51] . Several studies have explored the effects of HT on 
Ni-based superalloys, including Inconel 718 [31,32] , Hastelloy X 
[33 – 36] , and Inconel 625 [37 – 40] superalloys. These investigations 
reveal that a suitable post-heat treatment can significantly enhance the 
strength and toughness of LMD-processed IN 718 alloys. Moreover, the 
HT temperature plays a crucial role in influencing the dendritic 
morphology and thermal stability of the elemental distribution in the 
LMD-processed IN 738 and IN 625 superalloys.
Yi et al. ’ s research [34] on SLM-Hastelloy X showed that after the 
as-deposited specimens were subjected to aging treatment, affected by 
the precipitation of M ₂₃ C ₆ - type carbides and Fe ₂ Mo as well as the 
presence of voids, the elongation was poor, only 9 %. After solution 
treatment, a large number of high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs) and 
deformation twin boundaries were introduced owing to recrystalliza -
tion, enabling the material to exhibit excellent ductility, reaching 28 %. 
A. Poudel et al. [37] found in their research that after LP-DED IN625 was 
subjected to solution treatment at 1175 
◦
C, the microstructure was 
observed to be completely recrystallized and coarsened, consisting of

high-temperature tensile process (at 870 
◦
C), obvious dynamic recrys -

between the dynamically recrystallized grains improved the ductility. Li 
et al. [39] found that in the LPBF-IN625 alloy, after solution treatment at

attributed to the strengthening of the dislocation network and elimi -

distributed between columnar and cellular grain boundaries, which

C, the 
transverse YS and UTS of the sample decrease to 186 MPa and 241 MPa, 
respectively. Similarly, the longitudinal YS and UTS decrease to 
178 MPa and 230 MPa, respectively. This is attributed to the decrease in 
carbon content as temperature increases, leading to fewer carbides 
precipitating in the material ’ s microstructure. This reduction signifi -
cantly weakens the carbides ’ ability to inhibit grain growth, resulting in 
extensive grain enlargement that impacts the alloy ’ s strength. Further -
more, the carbides ’ influence on distorting the lattice of the solvent 
atoms diminishes, thereby reducing their hindrance effect on 
dislocations.
As shown in Fig. 8 (d), the elongation of as-deposited sample shows 
significant anisotropy between the horizontal and vertical directions, at 
73.1 %. As the heat treatment temperature increases, the anisotropy of 
the material ’ s mechanical properties significantly decreases, reaching 
34.2 %, 30.0 %, and 16.7 % at 1220
◦
C, 1250
◦
C, and 1280
◦
C, respec -
tively. This phenomenon can be attributed to the preferential solidifi -
cation of grains along the (001) crystal plane direction during the as- 
deposited state, resulting in strong grain orientation. However, after 
heat treatment, the grain orientation becomes less pronounced, which is 
also linked to the weakening trend of columnar grains observed in the 
heat-treated samples. This underscores the significant impact of appro -
priate heat treatment processes in reducing the material ’ s anisotropy. As 
shown in Fig. 9 , compared to the high-temperature tensile properties of 
superalloys reported in the literature, the GH3230 alloy prepared in this 
study exhibits excellent comprehensive mechanical properties after HT 
at 1250
◦

3.3. Fracture morphology of tensile specimens
The images in Fig. 10 depict the tensile fracture patterns of samples 
subjected to various heat treatment temperatures, shedding light on the 
alloy ’ s fracture behavior at elevated temperatures (900
◦
C). All samples 
exhibited typical ductile fracture, with a large number of dimples on the 
fracture surfaces. The formation of these dimples typically involves the 
nucleation, growth, and merging of micropores. At 1220
◦
C, the fracture 
surface revealed a columnar grain morphology, outlined by white 
dashed lines, with noticeable cracks observed along the columnar grain 
boundaries. These cracks were likely caused by residual stresses within 
the grain boundaries and microstructure, leading to crack propagation 
along the columnar grains. At 1250
◦
C, the grain morphology transitions 
to an equiaxed structure with equiaxed grain regions, as outlined by 
white dashed lines. As the HT temperature increased, the material un -
derwent recrystallization, during which the original columnar grains 
were fragmented and transformed into equiaxed grains, thereby 
enhancing the toughness of the material. This observation was consis -
tent with the EBSD data. Notably, at 1280
◦
C, the grain morphology 
retained its equiaxed structure; however, the fracture surface exhibited 
numerous voids (indicated by arrows). These voids likely result from 
grain boundary softening, grain coarsening, and reduced interfacial 
bonding strength between the matrix and carbides at high temperatures, 
leading to early nucleation and aggregation of voids, which ultimately 
reduces the strength of the material [28,35] . For example, the phe -
nomenon that typically occurs in the heat-affected zone of weldments. 
Due to the rapid heating and cooling during the welding process, the 
diffusion process of alloy elements becomes unbalanced, making it 
easier for elemental segregation to occur at the grain boundaries. 
Excessively high temperatures can lead to partial liquefaction of the 
grain boundaries, making the material more prone to crack propagation 
at the grain boundaries [64 – 67] .
4. Conclusions
In this study, the effect of HT temperature on the microstructure and 
high-temperature (900
◦
C) mechanical properties of both as-built and 
heat-treated GH3230 alloys fabricated using LPBF technology has been 
systematically investigated. The following conclusions were obtained. 
(1) At the solution treatment temperatures of 1220
◦
C, 1250
◦
C, and 
1280
◦
C, the only precipitated carbide in the samples is the white 
W/Mo-rich primary carbide M6C, which is distributed both 
within the grains and along the grain boundaries. As the tem -
perature increases, the carbide size increases, with the diameter 
rising from 0.67 μ m to 0.98 μ m and 2.18 μ m.
(2) Excessive heat treatment temperatures can inhibit recrystalliza -
tion of the material. At 1250
◦
C, the proportion of recrystallized 
grains is 54.3 %, while the proportion of deformed grains is 
7.1 %. At 1280
◦
C, the proportion of recrystallized grains de -
creases to 5.5 %, and the proportion of deformed grains increases 
to 88 %.
(3) Heat treatment can significantly reduce the anisotropy of the 
mechanical properties of the samples. At 1250
◦
C, the samples 
exhibit the best overall mechanical properties. The transverse 
yield strength is 213 MPa, the tensile strength is 274 MPa, and 
the elongation is 49.5 %. The longitudinal yield strength is 
203 MPa, the tensile strength is 259 MPa, and the elongation is 
70.5 %. Compared with that at 1220
◦
C, the elongation anisotropy 
decreased from 34.2 % to 30 %. In comparison to 1280
◦
C, the 
transverse YS and UTS increased by 14.5 % and 13.7 %, respec -
tively, while the longitudinal YS and UTS increased by 14.0 % 
and 12.6 %, respectively.
(4) Excessive heat treatment temperatures can significantly deterio -
rate the yield strength and tensile strength of the material. At 
1280
◦
C, the transverse yield strength of the sample decreases to 
186 MPa, the tensile strength to 241 MPa, the longitudinal yield 
strength to 178 MPa, and the tensile strength to 230 MPa.
