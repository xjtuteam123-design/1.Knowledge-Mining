Abstract 
This study systematically investigates the unusual tensile mechanical behavior of Mg-RE solid solution (SS) alloys, exhibiting anomalous 
tensile strengths (ATS) and an enhanced strain-hardening rate at high temperature. Both the peak ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and tensile 
yield strength (TYS) values occur at 150–200 °C, which are 12–50% higher compared to those at room temperature (RT). Meanwhile, the 
strain-hardening rate increases with the temperature rising from RT to 200 °C during the plastic deformation process. The results reveal that 
the formation of stacking faults (SFs) and the locking of dislocations, particularly immobile ⟨ c ⟩ partial dislocations, enhance resistance to 
plastic deformation, leading to higher strengths at high temperature. Furthermore, the interactivity between SFs and ⟨ c + a ⟩ dislocations 
intensify with rising of temperature. The presence of RE atoms in the SS plays a critical role in this unique mechanical behavior, as 
they preferentially occupy non-basal planes rather than basal planes, thereby reducing the stacking fault (SF) formation energy. This study 
provides new insights into the high-temperature strengthening mechanisms of Mg-RE based alloys, offering potential guidance for the design 
of advanced lightweight materials with superior mechanical properties.

Mg-RE alloys show an unusual “anomalous tensile strength”
(ATS) behavior: the peak tensile strength occurs at high 
temperature rather than at room temperature (RT), which 
is typically associated with intermetallic compounds (IMCs) 
and rarely observed in other Mg-based alloys and [ 16 ]. For 
these Mg-based alloys with ATS behavior, their peak ten- 
sile strength ( σ T ) occurs at elevated temperate rather than RT 
(room temperature) [ 9 , 17 ]. For example, Mordike B. L., et al. 
[ 9 ] observed a signiﬁcant 40% increase with the temperature 
rising from RT to 150–200 °C in Mg-Gd and Mg-Tb (T6) bi- 
nary alloys [ 17 ]. Ding W.J., et al. also found the similar phe- 
nomenon in Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Zr alloys, the peak strength occurs 
at ∼200 °C [ 18 ]. Numerous studies have been conducted and 
thought that the synergistic strengthening effect of solid solu- 
tion (SS) strengthening and grain boundaries (GB) strength- 
ening was the critical factor for the ATS behavior in Mg-RE 
alloys, as well precipitates phases strengthening [ 19 , 20 ]. 
However, it well-known that both GB and precipitates 
strengthening was gradually decreasing at elevated temper- 
atures [ 5 ]. Additionally, based on the dislocation theory, 
more dislocations were activated with addition of RE ele- 
ments, especially for non-basal dislocations and cross-slip 
[ 21–24 ], like prismatic ({10–10} < 11−20 > ) and pyramidal 
({10–11} < 11−22 > ) slip [ 25 ], which signiﬁcantly enhance 
plasticity rather than strength [ 26 ]. And then the strength- 
ening effect should reduce at high temperature. 
This is because the increase in strength is primarily due to 
dislocation pile-up and multiplication, rather than only the ac- 
tivation of dislocations. And these activated dislocations were 
hard to form the Kear-Wilsdorf (K-W) locking, which is the 
crucial reason for the ATS phenomenon in IMC alloys [ 27 ], 
or to form immobile ⟨ c ⟩ dislocation, which is the main reason 
in Mg single-crystal exhibiting ATS behavior under compres- 
sion conditions [ 28–30 ]. 
In this work, the authors ﬁrstly found that the ATS was 
very closely related to Mg-based solid solution (SS) con- 
taining RE element. Mg-Gd, Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Zr alloys were 
prepared and studied in detailed. Meantime, their strain- 
hardening behavior was investigated during their plastic pro- 
cess. Subsequently, the precise Mg-RE SS molecular dynamic 
(MD) model was built to study the atomic tensile process and 
dislocation movement, based on XRD structure reﬁnement 
and TEM analysis. Finally, the stacking faults (SFs) and the 
dislocation movement were analyzed and the strengthening 
mechanism of SS Mg-RE(-Zn-Zr) alloys with ATS behavior 
revealed, too. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Sample preparation 
Mg-30Gd and Mg-30Zr (wt.%) master alloys and high- 
purity Mg and Zn ( > 99.99 wt.%) were ﬁrstly melted in a 
steel crucible, in a resistance-furnace under a protective atmo- 
sphere (CO2 : SF6 = 99:1, vol%). The melt was then poured 
into a steel mold and cast into an ingot at 720◦C(993 K). 
Their chemical compositions of the alloys were determined by 
an inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectroscopy 
(ICP-AES), designating Mg-5Gd, Mg-10Gd, and Mg-12Gd 
alloy, and the chemical compositions listed in Table S1 in 
Supplemental Data. The above ingots were subsequently con- 
ducted solid solution (SS) heat-treatment: at 500–520 °C for 
6–12 h (T4, their temperature and time were showed in Table 
S2), the SEM images of the homogenization heat-treatment 
alloy were shown in Fig. S1, obtained the fully homoge- 
nization SS microstructure basing the analysis of phases and 
hardness, as shown in Fig. 1 (a). Mg-10Gd ingot was then 
performed by extrusion under the conditions of the temper- 
ature of 350 °C (623 K), with the extrusion ratio of 12:1, 
and the ram rate of 5 mm/s. Subsequently, the extruded piece 
was subsequently forged by a 1250 ton oil-press at 450 °C 
(723 K, E + F piece). Then the ingot conducted aging heat- 
treatment at 220 °C for 24 h. The samples preparation and 
treatment process were shown in Fig. 1 (a). 
2.2. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations 
Large-scale Atomic/Molecular Massively Parallel Simula- 
tor (LAMMPS) were performed to simulate the tensile pro- 
cess. Fig. 1 (b) shows the MD simulation analysis process. 
The forces ﬁeld were modelled using the Modiﬁed Embedded 
Atom Method (MEAM). The box size is (35 ×50 ×50) units 
for each process, respectively. The temperature was controlled 
using the Nosé-Hoover heat bath method with a time step of 
1 f s. All simulations were performed in the NPT integration 
with the temperatures ranging from 25 to 300 °C (or 300–
573 K). The total energy of the system was minimized and 
relaxation 50 ps to ensure that the system reached equilibrium 
before deformation. The strain rate was set to 0.001 A/step. 
The open-source software OVITO was used for visualization 
and analysis the dislocations.

were < a > -type dislocation (1/3 [11–20] (0001)), including

It is well-known that the critical resolved shear stress 
(CRSS) for pyramid dislocation slip is about 150 MPa, and 
the basal plane slip is only 15 MPa for the Mg-RE alloy (Mg- 
1Gd) [ 37 ], which is hard to activated at RT. The addition of 
RE elements signiﬁcantly reduces the pyramidal-to-basal tran- 
sition slip CRSS ratio (P/B CRSS ratio) [ 38 ], which is a key 
reason why RE addition weakens the basal texture. On the 
other hand, the preferential occupation of RE atoms on non- 
basal planes plays a crucial role in the formation and disso- 
ciation of non-basal dislocations, particularly pyramidal-I and 
pyramidal-II ⟨ c + a ⟩ dislocations. However, the energy dif- 
ference between ⟨ c + a ⟩ dislocations on the pyramidal-I and 
pyramidal-II planes is relatively small, and cross-slip transi- 
tions are common [ 32 ], making it challenging to determine 
the dominant slip system between the two dislocations. 
Low SFs formation energy caused by the addition of RE 
atoms to the Mg matrix is beneﬁcial for forming more SFs. 
Fig. 17 shows the SF energy for different crystal planes with 
varying concentrations of solute atoms. The addition of RE 
atoms signiﬁcantly reduces the SF energy on different planes. 
Notably, the non-basal SF energy is even lower, reaching neg- 
ative values, indicating that the non-basal SFs are more stable 
than the ideal crystal structure. Additionally, although high 
temperatures generally increase the SF energy for all crystal 
planes, the energy gap between the basal and non-basal SFs 
is signiﬁcantly reduced.

4. Conclusion 
This work investigated the unusual anomalous tensile 
strength (ATS) and high strain-hardening rates behavior of 
Mg-RE solid solution (SS) alloys at high temperatures. The 
peak tensile yield strengths of these alloys occurred at 150–
200 °C ( ∼0.45 Tm , Tm is the alloy melting point) rather than 
at RT. The following conclusions can be drawn: 
1) Mg-Gd SS alloys exhibit a typical and universal ATS 
phenomenon: both the tensile yield strength (TYS) and 
ultimate tensile strength (UTS) are higher than those 
of RT samples by 12.6–18% and 13–50%, respectively. 
However, the anomalous behavior of Mg-RE alloy is 
vanished after peak-aging treatment. 
2) SFs and non-basal dislocation are crucial for the unusual 
ATS and strain hardening behaviors. 1/3 <−2113 > (10–
11) pyramidal dislocations and immobile < c > -type dis- 
locations were found in the tensile samples basing TEM 
observations, which corroborate the insights obtained 
from the molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. 
3) The ATS behavior of Mg-RE SS alloys has been suc- 
cessfully simulated using MD simulations. The simula- 
tion reveals the formation process of obvious SFs and 
dislocation locking during the tensile process, leading to 
signiﬁcantly higher strain-hardening rate under elevated 
temperatures (150–200 °C). 
4) RE atoms signiﬁcantly changes the crystal structure 
with preferential occupation on the non-basal planes. 
The near-range and far-range atomic stacking increase 
with the addition of RE elements, thus facilitating the 
formation of SFs due to lowering the formation energy 
of SFs. This enhances the interaction with ⟨ c + a ⟩ dis- 
locations and increases the thermostability of the moved 
dislocations.
