ABSTRACT
Microstructure evolution and damage mechanisms during the creep deformation of 
a directionally solidified Ni-based superalloy were evaluated. The creep deformation behaviour 
was studied along the longitudinal direction (i.e. along the solidification direction), at 
a moderate temperature of 870 °C, and applied stresses ranging from 310 to 450 MPa 
concerning the service conditions in industrial gas turbine blades. It was shown that at the 
lower stress levels of about 310 MPa, the average creep strain rate decreased by two orders of 
magnitude, while tertiary creep appeared as the dominant stage. Progressive coarsening of γ’ 
precipitates was also observed, meantime γ’ precipitate shearing was confirmed to occur over 
the evaluated range of stresses.

Introduction
Over the years, the gas turbine inlet temperature (TIT) 
has been increasing to improve gas turbines power and 
efficiency. Owing to the excellent elevated temperature 
capabilities, Ni-based superalloys have been developed 
to design rotary and stationary blades of aero-engines 
and power generation gas turbines. Further enhance -
ments were obtained by eliminating transverse and 
oblique grain boundaries in directionally solidified 
(DS) materials. Moreover, advanced cooling systems 
combined with thermal barrier coatings have been 
applied to prevent the excessive increase of the tem -
perature at the surface of the blades [ 1–3 ].
Classically, it has been shown that the grain 
boundaries have a significant effect on the creep 
deformation behaviour and durability of polycrystal -
line equiaxed alloys. The main creep deformation 
mechanisms are generally recognised as grain bound -
ary sliding, micro-void nucleation generated by the 
stress concentrations, and diminishment of the crystal 
structure integrity [ 4 , 5 ]. A considerable enhancement 
in high temperature mechanical properties can be 
achieved by adjusting the main direction of the 
grain boundaries parallel to the blade’s maximum 
axis [ 6 ]. In the directional solidification process of 
FCC alloys, the <001> direction is the preferential 
growth direction of dendrites along the thermal gra -
dients due to the convergence of four close-packed 
(111) planes towards the <001> direction [ 7–9 ]. It is 
worth noting that it is very challenging to achieve 
a fully <001> oriented structure [ 8 , 10 ] due to the 
complexities existing in the solidification process 
parameters such as furnace specifications, heat extrac -
tion parameters, withdrawal rate. Confining the grain 
boundaries in directionally solidified superalloys has 
brought out the role of other microstructural features, 
such as Ll
2 
γ’ precipitates and carbides, in controlling 
creep properties. Moreover, the effects of the envir -
onment on microstructure degradation can deterio -
rate creep resistance of such superalloys [ 3 , 11 ].
Generally, creep is one of the most dominant 
failure mechanisms during operation of uncooled 
components at high temperatures [ 12 ]. In this 
paper, the creep behaviour and the damage 
mechanisms of a directionally solidified GTD111 
superalloy have been evaluated, and the conformity 
of the results is compared with other studies using 
some creep life equations. Considering that mate -
rial microstructural changes can alter the creep 
behaviour of the alloy over time due to the micro -
structure deterioration and stress redistribution 
[ 13 , 14 ], the study is focused on the effects of the 
initially applied stress at an intermediate tempera -
ture close to the working temperature of the blades, 
and damage mechanisms are discussed.

Creep deformation behaviour
The uniaxial creep tests were performed within an 
applied stress range of 310 MPa to 450 MPa at 870 
°C. All specimens were tested to rupture. The creep 
curves are presented in Figure 4(a) , where the creep 
strains are plotted against the time for four stress 
levels. For each stress level, two specimens were 
tested except for 350 MPa when one of the specimens 
failed during the initial loading. As shown in 
Figure 4(a) , at the higher stress levels of 450 MPa 
and 380 MPa, the tertiary creep was the dominant 
stage that accounted for about 70–90% of creep time 
and 90–95% of creep strain. The rupture times were 
increased from about 8.1 hours to about 51.2 hours. 
It is also evident that at the lower stress levels of 310 
MPa and 350 MPa, the rupture times were increased 
to about 300 hours. The strain rate changes are 
plotted versus the creep strain in Figure 4(b) . At all 
stress levels, the creep strain rate decreases from the 
beginning of the test, reaching a minimum value, and 
then increases continuously in the tertiary region.

Discussion
Experimental creep tests were conducted to elucidate 
the intermediate creep damage mechanisms of direc -
tionally solidified GTD111 superalloy. The observa -
tion of grain structure at the sampling position for 
the creep specimens, Figure 1 , shows that the grains 
have an average diameter of ~ 620 µm increasing to ~ 
1130 µm from side to middle of the specimens. It is 
believed that such columnar coarse grains can 
improve creep properties of DS superalloys in long -
itudinal orientation by limiting grain boundary sliding 
and void nucleation [ 6 ].
The comparison of our experimental creep results 
of GTD111DS with other studies [ 22 , 24 , 32 ] shows 
a good agreement, as shown in Figures 5 and 6 , 
between minimum creep rates and rupture times that 
gives us a chance of comparing deformation mechan -
isms. From the current creep tests, it is suggested that 
surface crack density might correlate with creep strain, 
because most internal cracks had been nucleated at 
pores that are reported to be constant in number 
during the creep deformation and before the final 
fracture at such a creep temperature [ 33 , 34 ]. In fact, 
our creep tests have been performed at a sufficiently 
low temperature and for a sufficiently short duration 
to rule out deformation pore nucleation that would 
arise through a dislocations climb mechanism [ 35 , 36 ]. 
Thus, crack densities at the surface of the specimens 
near the necking area were evaluated and represented

in the inter-dendritic areas. This can be explained by 
considering the fact that casting pores, carbides, and

On the other hand, the involvement of oxidation 
in creep testing in the atmospheric environment is 
inevitable. γ ′ -precipitates depleted layer in DS super -
alloys appears as equiaxed grains that, along with 
oxide scales, have been reported to appear as 
a brittle-weak combination and accelerate crack 
nucleation [ 11 , 37 ]. Microstructural evaluations 
showed that an oxide layer mostly rich in Cr, Ti 
and Al had been formed at the surface of the speci -
mens and along crack lips. However, no noticeable 
γ’ precipitates depleted layer was observed below the 
oxide layer. Diffusion of elements to the surface at 
high temperatures occurs through grain boundaries 
and inter-dendritic areas [ 21 ]. Since there are no 
transverse grain boundaries in DS specimens, Cr, 
Ti, and Al tend to migrate through the second 
easiest diffusion path, namely the inter-dendritic 
areas, making these areas weaker and prone to 
cracking. Also, it has been suggested that some 
carbides in such alloys are sensitive to oxidation 
and may act as crack initiation sites. For example, 
a study on DS200+ Hf superalloy [ 3 , 18 ] showed an 
early cracking of Hf-rich carbides due to oxidation, 
even in the absence of any applied external stress. 
Likewise, cracked Ta-rich carbides were detected at 
all stress levels and both surface and interior of our 
specimens, [ Figures 9-11 ].

In precipitate hardened superalloys like DS

Creep curves in Figures 4(a,b) displayed that ter -
tiary creep was the dominant creep stage at the 
intermediate temperature of 870 °C and stress 
levels of 310 to 450 MPa. Various deformation 
and damage mechanisms during tertiary creep 
have been reported for different superalloys such 
as directional γ’ coarsening and γ’ shearing that 
occur at sufficiently high temperatures and stresses, 
respectively [ 38 ]. Calculation of Norton constants 
and comparison with other superalloys in Figure 6 
confirmed that γ’ shearing should be a dominant 
deformation mechanism at this temperature. 
Moreover, such a creep behaviour dominated by 
the tertiary creep stage is a clear indication that 
the progressive degradation of the γ’ 
microstructure, through coarsening, coalescence, 
and possibly topological inversion for the lowest 
applied stress is creep rate and life-controlling. In 
our case, tests were not long enough to achieve 
such a rafting/topological inversion. Still, the 
absence of any secondary creep stage and 
a continuous increase in strain rate, Figure 4(b) , 
is a clear indication that morphological evolution 
of precipitation is controlling creep properties 
[ 35
, 39–43
]. As observed in Figures 13 and 14 , more 
extended tests (due to lower applied stress) cause 
noticeable γ’ coarsening. It has been recognised 
that directional γ’ coarsening within inter- 
dendritic areas had started for the longest creep 
tests, Figure 13(h) , while γ’ precipitates at dendritic 
areas had only experienced a morphological change 
from cubic to spherical. Therefore, γ’ precipitates 
deterioration occurs faster within inter-dendritic 
areas, at least 3.7 times faster compared to primary 
dendrite arms, according to Figure 14 . Such a faster

degradation within inter-dendritic areas may origi -
nate from (i) a least γ’ morphological regularity as 
observed in Figure 13 , (ii) from a locally higher 
dislocation density after processing (i.e. after soli -
dification and full heat treatment scheme) and (iii) 
from a lower concentration of slow diffusing ele -
ments (i.e. Mo and W in case of GTD-111). It is 
known that after solidification, Mo and W provide 
solid solution strengthening (besides contribution 
in MC carbides) and have a higher concentration 
in dendritic areas. Light elements (i.e. Al and Ti) 
tend to segregate to inter-dendritic areas resulting 
in coarser γ’ precipitates in these areas [ 8 , 44 ]. 
A well-designed heat treatment should be applied 
for a proper distribution of precipitates within the 
dendritic structure after solidification. Caron and 
Khan [ 45 ] have shown that change of solution 
treatment can affect γ’ precipitates size and mor -
phology distribution, resulting in highly different 
creep behaviour. Despite the fact that applying 
a heat treatment schedule (i.e. solution and ageing 
heat treatments) has provided excellent creep prop -
erties compared to previous studies from the litera -
ture, Figure 5 , we cannot entirely rule out minor 
remaining chemical micro-segregations, and hence, 
differences in local diffusivities, that is to say, the 
average diffusivity is higher in inter-dendritic spa -
cings, which is due to the lower amount of the 
refractory elements. Also, Guo et al. have shown 
a higher dislocation density after solidification of 
K465 (a derivative of Mar-M246) superalloy within 
inter-dendritic spacing areas compared to primary 
dendrite arms, leading to possible microstructure 
instabilities such as TCP precipitation and asso -
ciated debit in creep life at 975 °C/225 MPa [ 46 ]. 
Nörtershäuser et al. [ 8 ] have also shown 
a difference in dislocation densities in dendritic 
and inter-dendritic areas after creep testing of 
a single crystal superalloy. This may be due to 
a less organised microstructure in inter-dendritic 
areas, making them prone to creep cracking. Local 
differences in dislocation densities at the dendritic 
scale will affect the transport rate of diffusing ele -
ments, through a pipe-diffusion effect [ 39
, 47–50
]. 
A higher dislocation density within inter-dendritic 
areas could only favour a higher coarsening rate, as 
observed in Figure 14 . Thus, variation of γ’ preci -
pitates morphology and dislocation density in den -
dritic and inter-dendritic areas may cause 
differences in rate of creep deformation within 
these two areas. Although, comparing creep results 
in Figure 5 demonstrated that our results were at 
the upper bound of the GTD111DS Larson-Miller 
diagram, revealing that the solution treatment fol -
lowed fast cooling rate we used led to fine precipi -
tates whose size had then been optimised through 
the ageing stages.
Conclusions
The creep behaviour at 870°C of directionally solidi -
fied GTD111 Ni-based superalloy was investigated 
under different initial applied stresses. In such con -
ditions, the tertiary creep is mainly dominated due to 
a slow and continuous microstructure evolution such 
as precipitate coarsening. Primary γ’ precipitates had 
changed in morphology from cubic to spherical 
showing a coarsening rate in accordance with LSW 
theory. A heterogeneous coarsening rate between 
primary dendritic arms and inter-dendritic spacings 
has been observed due to remaining chemical micro- 
segregations and differences in local dislocation den -
sities. Moreover, final cracking resulted from the 
coalescence of cracks nucleating at internal pores 
and from the surface. The evaluation of surface 
crack formation during creep tests showed that the 
higher the applied stress, the higher the surface crack 
density. It was recognised that surface cracks were 
mostly nucleated at inter-dendritic areas due to:
● Local distribution of the microstructural defects 
i.e. casting pores, carbides, and eutectic phases at 
inter-dendritic areas because these areas are the 
last regions solidify.
● The difference of γ’ precipitates in size, morphol -
ogy and distribution between dendritic and inter- 
dendritic areas results in different creep 
behaviour.
● Higher diffusion rate of constitutive elements from 
inter-dendritic areas to the surface of the speci -
mens and oxides formation weakens these areas.
● Deterioration of γ’ precipitates at inter-dendritic 
areas occurs more rapidly.
