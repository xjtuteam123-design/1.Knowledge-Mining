A W–0.4wt% Y 2 (Zr)O 3 (WYZ) alloy plate with superior mechanical properties and improved high- 
temperature stability was prepared by hot rolling. The tensile properties of WYZ alloys with different 
orientations and recrystallization states were investigated at a temperature range from room temperature 
(RT) to 500 °C. The texture intensity of the original WYZ alloy was weak, but it was signiﬁcantly enhanced 
after annealing. The initial recrystallization temperature of the WYZ alloy was approximately 1400 °C. Af- 
ter recrystallization annealing, the strength of the WYZ alloy decreased signiﬁcantly, but it showed an 
increased ductility. The ultimate tensile strength and fracture elongation of the samples along the rolling 
direction (RD) were always signiﬁcantly higher than those of the samples along the transverse direction 
(TD). Orientation and annealing had no evident effect on ductile-to-brittle transition temperature (DBTT) 
from tensile data, but increasing strain rate would overestimate DBTT. The DBTT of the WYZ alloy from 
tensile data was approximately between 20 0 °C and 30 0 °C. With the increase in tensile test tempera- 
ture, the strengths of α- and γ-ﬁber textures on the side of fracture surface increased signiﬁcantly. The 
increase in α- and γ-ﬁber texture contents was beneﬁcial to ductility enhancement in the WYZ plate.

1. Introduction 
In the extreme working environment of a thermonuclear fu- 
sion reactor, the plasma-facing materials (PFMs) will be subjected 
to the steady-state heat ﬂow and plasma irradiation from the re- 
actor [1] . Tungsten (W) is considered the most promising PFM 
due to its high melting point and thermal conductivity, low sput- 
tering, and deuterium retention [ 2 , 3 ]. However, pure W still has 
some drawbacks in thermal stability and mechanical properties, 
such as low temperature brittleness caused by high ductility to 
brittle transition temperature (DBTT) [ 4 , 5 ], recrystallization embrit- 
tlement [ 6 , 7 ], neutron irradiation induced embrittlement [ 8 , 9 ], etc. 
The DBTT of W alloy is mainly affected by crystal defects, crystal 
orientation, impurity states, preparation routes, etc. [10–13] . While 
the initial recrystallization temperature (RCT) mainly depends on 
the grain structure and foreign impurities (elements/second phase

The low-temperature brittleness of pure W is mainly caused 
by the weak cohesion strength of grain boundaries (GBs) due to 
the segregation of impurities in GBs [16] . The brittleness of W 
will be improved by reducing the segregation of impurities at GBs.

tion of segregated impurities in GBs by reﬁning the grain size, and 
the brittleness of pure W can be well improved [17–20] . The pin-

and GB migration also signiﬁcantly enhances the high-temperature 
strength and thermal stability of W alloys [21] . Among oxide parti- 
cles, Y 2 O 3 possess high melting point (2410 ◦C), and stable physical 
and chemical properties, W–Y 2 O 3 alloys with excellent mechanical 
properties, thermal shock damage resistance and radiation dam- 
age resistance have been widely reported [22–24] . Furthermore, 
the addition of trace alloying elements (Zr, Y, etc.) can purify the 
free oxygen (O) impurities at GBs, improving the GB binding force, 
and the mechanical properties of W alloys can be further improved 
[ 25 , 26 ]. Liu et al. [27] reported the excellent mechanical properties 
of a trace Zr-doped W–Y 2 O 3 alloy. The introduction of Zr element

Sintered W alloys usually have a high ductile-to-brittle transi-

morphology and texture evolution of the WYZ alloy after tensile 
test were analyzed in detail. The effect of tensile strain rate on the 
stress–strain behavior of the WYZ alloy was also investigated. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Preparation of a WYZ alloy plate 
A WYZ alloy precursor powder was fabricated using a 
wet chemical method. Ammonium metatungstate [(NH 4 ) 6 H 2 
W 12 O 40 •xH 2 O)], yttrium nitrate hexahydrate [(Y(NO 3 ) 3 •6H 2 O)], 
and zirconium nitrate pentahydrate [Zr(NO 3 ) 4 •5H 2 O] were dis- 
solved in deionized water, mixed, and added with oxalic acid 
(C 2 H 2 O 4 •2H 2 O) for precipitation. The precursor powder was re- 
duced in a H 2 atmosphere to obtain a WYZ composite powder. 
Cold isostatic pressing was used to shape the powder, then the 
green body was sintered at 2300 ◦C under the H 2 atmosphere for 
6 h. The sintered WYZ alloy bulk was hot-rolled (in Beijing Tian- 
Long Tungsten & Molybdenum Co., Ltd.) at a temperature of 1600 
◦C to improve the microstructure and mechanical properties. The 
rolling reduction was 50%, and the relative density of the obtained 
WYZ alloy plate was 99.3%. 
2.2. Characterization 
The grain structure and texture of the WYZ alloy were eval- 
uated by electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD, using EBSD HKL 
Inca equipped in S-3400 N Hitachi SEM). Prior to the EBSD mea- 
surement, the sample surface was sanded and polished, then char- 
acterized by ion beam thinning (Three Ion Beam Cutting Machine, 
Fuzhou University). EBSD was shooting at 200 × magniﬁcation, the 
EBSD results for all surface samples were obtained close to the 
center region of the original plate, and the fracture samples were 
close to the edge of a side of the fracture surface. EBSD data were 
processed using the Channel 5 software package (Oxford Instru- 
ments), and the important texture types in BCC metals are shown 
in Fig. 1 [32] . 
The annealed samples were sealed in evacuated quartz glass 
tubes to prevent oxidation during high-temperature treatment. The 
samples were subsequently annealed in a tubular furnace for 1 h 
and then cooled to RT by furnace cooling. Rolling direction (RD)–
transverse direction (TD) plane samples were annealed at 1400 °C 
and 1600 °C, hereinafter abbreviated as A1400 and A1600, respec- 
tively. Hardness was measured at RT by using a Vickers hardness 
tester (ML-3L) with a load of 200 gf and a dwell time of 15 s. Ten- 
sile samples of WYZ alloy along the RD and TD were prepared us- 
ing an electric spark linear cutting machine (abbreviated as R-WYZ 
and T-WYZ, respectively. All the surface of the tensile samples were 
polished with sandpaper (from 120 to 1200 mesh), and the side 
of the samples were polished with grinding machine and sandpa- 
per (from 120 to 1200 mesh). For the R-WZY tensile samples an- 
nealed at 1400 ◦C and 1600 ◦C, abbreviated as A1400 R-WYZ and

highest tensile stress measured during the tensile process. Fracture

3.3. Fracture morphology 
Fig. 7 shows the macroscopic appearance of the fracture sur- 
faces of as-rolled WYZ alloys with different orientations and an- 
nealed states. The fracture surfaces of all samples are uneven at 
the test temperature below 300 °C, and large fracture ridges ex- 
ist in local areas. When tested at 400 °C, the fracture surfaces of 
the R-WYZ, A1400 R-WYZ and A1600 T-WYZ samples all show evi- 
dent partial delamination, and cracks penetrate the entire fracture 
surface parallel to the TD direction. By contrast, an obvious de- 
lamination fracture can be observed on the T-WYZ sample when 
tested at 500 °C. As a BCC structural metal, the main plastic defor- 
mation mode in hot-rolled WYZ alloys includes the two types of 
in-grain and inter-grain [41] . In-grain plastic deformation depends 
on dislocation multiplication and slip, which is limited by the grain 
size. Inter-grain plastic deformation relies on the dislocations mov- 
ing along GBs, and this mode is suppressed by chaotic texture [42] . 
Compared with R-WYZ samples, the T-WYZ tensile sample presents 
a smaller capacity (a relative low aspect ratio in TD-ND surface) for 
dislocations ( Fig. 3 a and Fig. 3 b). The torque corresponding to crack 
deﬂection is small in the T-WYZ test sample, and the crack deﬂec- 
tion reaches the limit before the delamination fracture. The crack 
propagation direction of delamination fracture at high temperature 
is parallel to the RD. Therefore, delamination fracture has a signif- 
icant relationship with the direction of grain elongation. Moreover, 
the crack density at the edge is lower than that at the center area, 
which indicates that the cracks are generated from the center of 
the fracture surface and extend to both ends. The fracture of A1600 
R-WYZ shows evident grain deformation, and large tearing and de-

3.4. Texture evolution 
During the tensile tests, the grain orientation starts to rotate, 
which makes the effective dislocation slip direction (in uniaxial 
stretching) parallel to the applied stress direction [43] . Fig. 10 
presents the φ2 = 45 ° section of the ODF from the fracture side 
of the R-WYZ samples at various test temperatures. In accordance 
with the previous description of Fig. 4 , the main texture of the 
RD–TD plane is the weak (112) < 110 > type of α-ﬁber texture and 
(011) < 110 > type of Goss-ﬁber texture. After the tensile test, the 
texture strength increases signiﬁcantly. At RT, the texture type of 
the fracture side is similar to that of the original RD–TD plane (see 
Fig. 4 a). At 200 °C, the main texture evolves into θ-, α-, and γ- 
ﬁber textures. When the temperature increases to 300 °C, no obvi- 
ous texture type changes occur, but α-ﬁber texture [(112) < 110 > )] 
and γ-ﬁber texture [(111) < 112 > , (111) < 110 > ] are signiﬁcantly in- 
creased, indicating that α- and γ-ﬁber textures are enhanced after 
the ductile-to-brittle transition. In BCC crystal metals, {hkl} < 110 > 
is a typical tensile texture. The enhancement of α- and γ-ﬁber tex- 
tures shows that during the tensile process of R-WYZ, the grains 
with various orientations will deﬂect to the < 110 > direction un- 
der the action of tensile stress. The fracture sides of 400 °C and 
500 °C samples show evident (112) < 110 > type of α-ﬁber texture 
and (111) < 110 > type of γ-ﬁber texture. The volume fractions of 
α-, γ-, and θ-ﬁber textures can be calculated in accordance with 
the direction of the typical texture in the ODF map to quantify the 
content of the three typical textures in the WYZ alloy during the 
tensile process. Table 2 presents the volume fraction changes of 
the α-, γ-, and θ-ﬁber textures shown in Fig. 10 with elevated test 
temperature. In general, with the increase in test temperature, α- 
and γ-ﬁber texture strengths are increased signiﬁcantly, whereas 
the volume fraction of θ-ﬁber texture always ﬂuctuates below the 
range of 11%. 
A reasonable explanation for the effect of elevated temperature 
on the transformation of tensile texture is given below. The plastic- 
ity of W alloys is highly dependent on temperature. At a tempera- 
ture below 0.2 Tm (20% of the melting point), the nucleation and 
proliferation of dislocation kinks are the main power sources for 
the movement of screw dislocations [44] . The plasticity of tung- 
sten grains is achieved by dislocation slip. When the temperature 
is suﬃcient to activate the dislocation glide along the < 110 > di- 
rection, the material shows great plasticity. As shown in Fig. 4 , the 
α-ﬁber texture in the original state RD–TD plane is weak. With 
the increased test temperature, the crystal grain orientation turns 
to < 110 > direction and forms enhanced α- and γ-ﬁber textures. 
As the test temperature increases, the fracture deformation gen- 
erally gradually increases (except for the decrease in elongation 
caused by oxidation at 500 °C). A large deformation drives more 
substantial crystals to rotate in the < 110 > direction. As a result, 
α- and γ-ﬁber texture contents increase. The R-WYZ alloys show 
good ductility, which conﬁrms that the contents of α- and γ-ﬁber 
textures have high ductility. The improved ductility of the A1600 
R-WYZ sample should be attributed to the high-level recrystalliza- 
tion texture of α- and γ-ﬁber textures. Controlling the rolling pro- 
cess to increase the contents of α- and γ-ﬁber textures in the W 
matrix may improve the low-temperature toughness of the W al- 
loy. However, for W alloys used in fusion reactors, θ- and Goss- 
ﬁber textures have strong resistance to thermal load and radiation 
damage; thus, the regulation of W alloy microstructure should be 
comprehensively considered [ 32 , 45 ].

the anisotropic polycrystalline W and the loading rate after plastic 
processing. However, there are few studies on the effect of load- 
ing rate on the second phase doping strengthened W alloy DBTT. 
Therefore, it is necessary to understand in detail the stress-strain 
behavior of doped-strengthened W alloys under different tensile 
strain rates. In the range of RT–500 °C, the true stress–strain curves

4. Conclusion 
1) The RD–TD, RD–ND, and TD–ND planes of an as-rolled WYZ 
alloy have different grain aspect ration and texture types, 
which make the WYZ alloy have performance anisotropy 
in various orientations. Low-deformation rolling and Y-Zr-O 
particle doped signiﬁcantly increase the initial RCT (about 
1400 °C) of the WYZ alloy. The texture strength is signiﬁ- 
cantly increased after recrystallization annealing. 
2) Orientation shows an inconsiderable effect on DBTT but it 
seriously affects the strength and elongation of the WYZ al- 
loy. The WYZ alloy exhibits excellent tensile strength and 
ductility in the RD orientation, which is mainly due to the 
larger free movement path and capacity for dislocations on 
the TD–ND plane than those on the RD–ND plane. Recrys- 
tallization reduces the strength of the WYZ alloy but shows 
improved ductility at high temperatures. During the tensile 
test, cracks are generated from the center of the fracture 
plane and extend to both ends along the direction where the 
grains are elongated. 
3) With the increase in test temperature, α- and γ-ﬁber tex- 
ture strengths on the fracture side surface of the R-WYZ al- 
loy are signiﬁcantly enhanced. Increasing the volume frac- 
tion of α- and γ-ﬁber textures is beneﬁcial to improving the 
ductility of the W alloy. Tensile strain rate exerts a great in- 
ﬂuence on the evaluation of DBTT, in which a high tensile 
strain rate overestimates the DBTT of the WYZ alloy. The 
DBTT of the WYZ alloy determined from tensile testing is 
approximately 20 0–30 0 °C. Reducing the tensile strain rate 
at before and after the ductile-brittle transition point has an 
opposite effect on the tensile properties of the WYZ alloy. 
Before the ductile-brittle transition point, the WYZ alloy ex- 
hibits high UTS at low strain rate, whereas shows low UTS 
and high TE above the ductile-brittle transition point.
