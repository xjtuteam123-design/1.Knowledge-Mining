ABSTRACT 
The yield strength (YS) of single crystal Ni-based superalloys is a critical factor for low temperature low cycle 
fatigue and Out-of-phase thermomechanical fatigue durability of turbine blades. The chemical composition of 
this class of alloys appears to have a major role in their low temperature tensile behavior. Indeed, alloying el -
ements modify the antiphase boundary (APB) energy of the γ
′
phase and so its resistance to shearing. To 
investigate the influence of the chemical composition on the YS, 18 superalloys of different generations were 
tensile tested at 650 
◦
C. The results show a significant YS evolution of up to 250 MPa between the first generation 
and the following generations. The YS differences are mainly attributed to the contributions of γ ʹ which in turn is 
dependent on the γ ʹ phase composition. An EDNNB model was adapted to estimate the APB energies for the 
aforementioned set of alloys using Thermo-Calc data. Ta and Ti elements appear to be the most efficient γ
′
strengtheners. The higher their content is, the higher the APB energy is and the stronger the alloy is in terms of 
tensile YS. Several alloys also exhibit an important strain hardening. 
1. Introduction 
The need of high efficiency aero-engines, for both economic and 
environmental reasons, has led to an increase of the turbine inlet tem -
perature over the past decades [ 1 ]. To withstand harsher operating 
conditions, blade design has evolved to include internal cooling systems, 
thermal barrier coatings and, eliminating the grain boundaries via single 
crystal (SX) nickel-based superalloys. 
The development of new chemical compositions for SX application 
has mainly been driven by the need of high temperature creep perfor -
mance. This was achieved by addition of higher rhenium and ruthenium 
amounts through successive SX superalloy generations [ 1 – 4 ]. However, 
much less attention has been given to the low temperature properties 
( < 750 
◦
C) and to the significant yield strength (YS) variations between 
first generation and the following generations of SX alloys [ 5 – 7 ]. A 
lower YS is detrimental to both low temperature low cycle fatigue (LCF) 
[ 6 , 8 ] and out-of-phase thermo-mechanical fatigue (TMF) durability [ 9 , 
10 ], the latter being an issue for internal cooled blades. Indeed, a low YS 
increases the amount of plastic deformation at each cycle and therefore 
reduces the number of cycles to rupture [ 5 , 9 – 11 ]. Moreover, it is well 
known that the creep resistance at high temperature is not the main 
criteria controlling the out-of-phase TMF life of this class of alloys [ 11 ]. 
Several parameters are known to influence the YS of this class of 
alloys, including the microstructure (width of γ channels, the volume 
fraction and size of γ
′
precipitates), the γ / γ

composition [ 12 ]. Refractory elements such as Mo, W, Re and Ru are 
known to be matrix solid solution strengtheners [ 1 , 13 , 14 ]. At low 
temperatures, the YS also relies on γ

2. Experimental procedures 
2.1. SX Ni-based superalloys and chemical composition 
To investigate the influence of chemical composition on YS at 650 
◦
C, 
18 Ni-based SX superalloys of different generations were tested. Their 
nominal chemical composition is reported in Table 1 . SX bars (18 mm in 
diameter or less) were cast using conventional Bridgman process. The 
solidification was performed along a ≈ [001] direction, admitting a 
maximum primary misorientation of 6.5
◦
with respect to the perfect 
[001] crystallographic orientation. MAR-M200 + Hf and CM 247 LC al -
loys were originally developed for directionally solidified applications 
but in the present study, tested samples were machined from SX bars. 
Alloys were received either already fully heat treated or were solu -
tioned and/or aged at Institut Pprime using Nabertherm 1750 and 1800 
furnaces. In all cases, all the alloys were fully heat treated with their 
respective industrial heat treatment ( Table 2 ). 
Further, the composition of the γ
′
phase at 650 
◦
C of each alloy 
presented in the results was determined using Thermo-Calc (TCNi10 
database). 
2.2. Microstructure observations 
Samples for microstructure analysis were polished up to a mirror 
finish and etched with aqua regia . Samples were observed with a scan -
ning electron microscope JEOL JSM-7000F, field emission gun micro -
scope, operating at 25 kV at a working distance of ~10 mm. 
Metallographic observations were analyzed with ImageJ software. Mi -
crostructures were characterized in both primary dendrite arms and 
interdendritic areas. 
Deformation microstructures were observed with a transmission 
electron microscope JEOL 2010. TEM samples were prepared with a 
mechanical grinding and an electrochemical polishing with a solution of 
4% of perchloric acid in methanol.

Interrupted tensile tests at 1% and 0.5% plastic deformation were per -

4.4. Tensile hardening behavior 
The important hardening observed on some alloys is not well un -
derstood yet. In the case of TMS-238, the hardening is most likely 
generated by composition effects. Indeed, a similar yielding is observed 
in TMS-138A [ 43 ] and in others experimental Ru-containing SX super -
alloys [ 44 ]. In those advanced alloys, the high Re and Ru contents 
induce a high misfit and a low stacking fault energy in the matrix. This 
low fault energy promotes the dissociation of perfect dislocations inside 
the γ phase while the misfit prevents dislocation motion at the γ / γ
′
interface. This would result in a low mobility of dislocations in the γ 
matrix and would explain the high hardening rate. However, this hy -
pothesis still fail to explain the hardening observed in PWA 1480, which 
does not contain neither Re nor Ru and does not present a high misfit 
[ 7 ]. TEM observations of the early stages of deformation of PWA 1480 
and CMSX-2 showed that PWA 1480 had a higher density of dislocations 
trapped inside the matrix channel [ 22 ]. This accumulation of disloca -
tions (as evidenced by our TEM observations in Fig. 9 ) would explain the 
work hardening and, given the high Ta content, could be caused by the 
strong strengthening of its γ
′
phase. In all cases, further TEM in -
