Letter to the editor
“Percutaneous coronary intervention underperforms in Takayasu
Arteritis”
We have read the recent article by Wang et al., with great interest
[1]. The authors compared the different management strategies on
long-term outcomes for signi ﬁcant coronary stenosis in patients with
Takayasu arteritis and concluded that PCI is independently associated
with higher risk of MACEs and re-revascularization. We appreciate the
authors for valuable contribution to the literature on management of
coronary stenosis with low syntax score in Takayasu arteritis. However,
there are few concerns which we want to address.
The majority of patients on medical management had LM and ostial
involvement. In the absence of coronary imaging, the etiology of stenosis
being atherosclerosis or underlying disease process (panarteritis) is not
possible to differentiate. If in majority of these patients on medical man-
agement, there is the underlying in ﬂammation, immunosuppressant's
will have good outcomes. A PET-CT scan would also have been useful
in differentiation [2]. One of the limitations of the study is the absence
of assessment of functional signiﬁcance of stenosis by doing stress test
or FFR. It is mentioned that 28% patients had at least one CTO of coronary
arteries. What was the division in the three groups? Was there a statisti-
cally signiﬁcant difference?
In Table 1, though disease activity in all the three groups was similar
at baseline and at the time of intervention, yet signi ﬁcantly more pa-
tients on medical management were on steroids versus the intervention
group. How is this discrepancy explained? In Table 3, the only end-point
different between the three groups, is the increased risk of repeat revas-
cularization in the PCI group. This is similar to many trials in coronary
artery disease comparing CABG versus PCI with multivessel disease
[3]. However, what is important is that myocardial infarction is similar
in the three groups, we can speculate that the increased risk of repeat
revascularisation was probably due to stent restenosis/new onset pro-
gressive stenosis. Decreased use of steroid use might be a contributing
factor in patients with percutaneous coronary intervention.
