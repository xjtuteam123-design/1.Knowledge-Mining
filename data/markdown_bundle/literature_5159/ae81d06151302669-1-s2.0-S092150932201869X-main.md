ABSTRACT 
The mechanical properties of a Ti
2
(CrFeNi)
98 
multi-principal element alloy (MPEA) with heterogeneous mi -
crostructures were systematically investigated at room (293 K) and cryogenic (77 K) temperatures, together with 
the corresponding microstructure evolution. As the temperature decreased from 293 K to 77 K, the yield strength, 
ultimate tensile strength and ductility increased from 800 MPa to 980 MPa, 1.1 GPa – 1.5 GPa and 20% – 25%, 
respectively. Microstructure characterization indicates that the increase of ductility at 77 K is owing to the 
arising of deformation twins (DTs) in the face-centered cubic matrix adjacent to body-centered cubic precipitates. 
Finite element analysis confirm that the DTs is induced by locally concentrated stress which exceeds the critical 
twinning stress, owing to the low stacking fault energy at cryogenic temperature. This work provides a designing 
strategy for promising Co-free MPEAs with both good strength and ductility at extreme low temperature 
conditions. 
1. Introduction 
The development of new alloys which can be used in extreme low 
temperature conditions is significant in space, military and other fields 
[ 1 ]. Traditionally, alloys contain a primary element with minor addi -
tions of other auxiliary elements to enhance specific properties. In 
contrast, multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) are commonly 
composed of at least three principal elements in equal or approximately 
equal ratios [ 2 , 3 ]. Due to the feature of composition, MPEAs mainly 
exhibit a single-phase or multiphase solid-solution structure [ 3 – 6 ]. 
Latterly, single-phase face-centered cubic (FCC) MPEAs have attracted 
tensive attentions owing to the excellent mechanical properties at 
cryogenic temperature (77 K), including outstanding ductility and 
fracture toughness, making them promising for applications in extreme 
low temperature conditions [ 7 – 9 ]. However, single-phase FCC MPEAs 
show low yield strength (YS) because the mutation of local lattice 
distortion only affords Labusch-type weak pinning for dislocation 
motion, which results in a serious limitation of their applications 
[ 10 – 13 ]. An effective solution to improve the YS of single-phase FCC 
MPEAs is to incorporate other strengthening mechanisms, such as grain 
refinement [ 14 – 17 ], precipitation strengthening [ 18 – 21 ], and the 
evolving of heterogeneous microstructures [ 22 – 24 ]. Fortunately, 
single-phase FCC MPEAs have superb ductility, which gives sufficient 
space for such microstructure tailoring to improve YS by minor alloying 
with other elements. For example, the CrFeNi MPEA strengthened by 
(Cr, Si)-rich σ phase, which hindered the slipping of dislocations and 
migration of grain boundaries, achieved higher YS of 550 MPa and ul -
timate tensile strength (UTS) of 795 MPa with remaining 20% ductility 
[ 25 ]. The Ti
2
(CrFeNi)
98 
MPEA with minor alloying of Ti led heteroge -
neous microstructure, which exhibited a high yield strength of 
exceeding 766 MPa and approximately 21% ductility [ 23 ]. For the case 
of Nb addition, Liang et al. found that the formation of Laves phases in 
FCC matrix enhanced the YS to 961 MPa while maintaining good uni -
form elongation (UE) of 10.1% [ 26 ].

Strength and ductility are indispensable mechanical properties. The 
increase of strength of the FCC MPEAs at decreasing temperature usually 
leads to decrease of ductility. Therefore, it is necessary to introduce a 
mechanism to improve ductility of the alloy at cryogenic conditions. It is 
reported that the formation of deformation twins (DTs) can lead to 
improved ductility of single-phase FCC MPEAs composed of 3d transi -
tion metals (e.g., CoCrNi, CrFeNi and CoCrFeNi) at cryogenic condi -
tions, as a result of the reduced stacking fault energy (SFE) at decreased 
temperature [ 27 – 29 ]. A critical shear stress is also required for DT 
nucleation. Schneider et al. found that the DTs in single-phase FCC 
CrFeNi MPEA occurred above a certain critical resolved shear stress 
(165 MPa) comparing well with predictions (180 MPa) based on the 
Kibey ’ s model for twin nucleation [ 30 ]. The presence of multiple phases 
in an alloy should results in stress concentration at the interfaces during 
loading, owing to the mismatch of modulus and strength between pha -
ses. Wang et al. reported DTs were activated in a compositionally 
complex steel with very high SFE during room temperature deformation, 
in which the dual-nanoprecipitation strengthening enhanced the flow 
stress to reach the high critical value for the onset of the DTs [ 31 ]. Ding 
et al. reported that alloying of Ti with a lower valence electron con -
centration leaded to the formation of (Ti, Ni)-rich FCC phase and BCC 
precipitates in the Ti
2
(CrFeNi)
98 
MPEA [ 23 ]. Therefore, the intervention 
of strengthening phases will inevitably increase the stress of the nearby 
FCC matrix in the Ti
2
(CrFeNi)
98 
MPEA, and then facilitates the forma -
tion of DTs, as also implied by the massively activated DTs in a CrFeNi 
MPEA with BCC second phase [ 24 ].

The research is profitable to develop MPEAs with both good strength

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructure and tensile behavior 
Initial microstructure of the alloy before tensile test is shown in 
Fig. 1 a. The alloy consists of two kinds of FCC phases (denoted as FCC 
matrix and (Ti, Ni)-rich FCC II) and Cr-rich BCC precipitates, as 
confirmed by the EDS mapping and SAED results in Fig. 1 b and c. 
Referring to previous research [ 23 ], FCC matrix in the Ti
2
(CrFeNi)
98 
alloy mainly containing coarse grains (more than 30 μ m) and fine grains 
(5 – 15 μ m). The sizes of BCC particles distribute bimodally in the range 
of 0.8 – 1.2 μ m and 100 – 500 nm approximately. Therefore, the alloy 
exhibits a heterogeneous structure. 
Fig. 2 a shows representative engineering stress-strain curves of the 
Ti
2
(CrFeNi)
98

SEM images of fracture surfaces as insets. As the temperature decreases 
from 293 K to 77 K, the YS and UTS increase by ~25% from 798 to 978 
MPa, and ~35% from 1121 MPa to 1511 MPa, respectively. The UE is 
improved by ~25% from 20% to 25%. The SEM images of the fracture 
surfaces at two temperatures both present dimpled features, which 
confirms typical ductile fracture of the samples. 
The true stress-strain curves (solid curves) for tensile tests at 293 K 
and 77 K and the corresponding work hardening rates (WHRs, dash 
curves) are shown in Fig. 2 b. The WHR is defined as the derivative of the 
true stress ( σ ) with respect to the true strain ( ε ), d σ /d ε . The WHR curves 
exhibit multi-stage monotonic decrease for both deformed samples. 
First, the WHR drops rapidly at the onset of plastic deformation until 
~10% strain at 293 K and 77 K, which refers to a typical dislocation- 
strengthening feature [ 32 ]. The deceleration of WHRs for 77 K then 
slows down shows a more pronounced knee point, indicating the 
occurrence of a different strengthening mechanism. At the last stage, 
WHRs decrease at higher rates until they intersect the σ - ε curves, which 
represents necking as suggested by the Consid `ere criterion. It is 
noticeable that the WHR increases with the decreasing temperature, 
which further postpones the onset of necking, and thus demonstrates 
better ductility. Possible rationales for the enhancement of the WHR at 
77 K will be discussed later.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Severe work hardening of BCC precipitates at 77 K 
As shown in Fig. 7 a, a micro-cylinder along the length direction of 
specimen is taken for illustration. During tensile tests, the micro- 
cylinder is elongated, and the cross section shrinks. With the elonga -
tion of the FCC matrix, the BCC particles concurrently deform and the 
amount in the same visual area increases. At 77 K, the number of BCC 
particles is greater than that of 293 K due to the higher UE of the 
specimen at fracture. 
The microstructural evolution of BCC particles is characterized by 
TEM in Fig. 8 during the tensile tests. In the middle stage of deformation, 
dislocations are unevenly distributed and piled up in the BCC particles at 
293 K ( Fig. 8 a). While at 77 K, because the plastic deformation of BCC 
phase is dominated by the cross slip of screw dislocation which is 
thermal activated [ 34 , 35 ], the slipping of screw dislocation is sluggish. 
Much more dislocations are uniformly distributed in the BCC particles 
and intersect with each other ( Fig. 8 b), which inhibits further moving of 
the dislocations because of residual stress and the mechanically induced 
composition fluctuations [ 36 ], resulting in the severe work hardening of 
BCC precipitates and significantly increase of the deformation 
resistance.

5. Conclusions 
The mechanical properties of the Ti
2
(CrFeNi)
98 
MPEA were investi -
gated at 293 K and 77 K, and corresponding microstructure evolution at 
different plastic strains was investigated. FEA and theoretical analysis 
were adopted to reveal the effect of BCC precipitates on DTs. Based on 
these investigations, the following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) The Ti
2
(CrFeNi)
98 
MPEA with a heterogeneous microstructure 
containing BCC precipitates displayed a good combination of 
high yield strength and good ductility at cryogenic temperature. 
Compared to that at 293 K, the alloy exhibited a higher WHR at 
77 K, and the YS, UTS and UE increased by 23%, 35% and 25%, 
respectively. 
(2) At 77 K, the FCC matrix adjacent to the BCC particles involves 
DTs, which can coordinate the deformation and led to larger UE 
of the alloy than that of the 293 K. The intrinsic strengthening of 
an alloy at low temperature and the involving of DTs collabora -
tively achieve the simultaneous improvement of strength and 
ductility of the current MPEA.
