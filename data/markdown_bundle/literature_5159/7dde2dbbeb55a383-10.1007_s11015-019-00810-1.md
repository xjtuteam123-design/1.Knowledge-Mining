The paper presents the results of studying the formation of structure and properties of metals when using 
additive technologies, such as arc and laser surfacing of highly alloyed nickel alloys . The main problem 
of nickel -chromium alloys during treatment with highly concentr ated energy sources (welding, surfa c-
ing, soldering, plasma and laser treatment) consists in low strength and heat resistance after heating to 
high temperatures and rapid cooling, which is typical for these methods . The maximum high -tempera-
ture strength o f such alloys is achieved after quenching and subsequent aging resulting in the formation 
of finely -dispersed ′γ - phase precipitates, which inhibit plastic deformation . However, the achieved 
level of high-temperature strength of nickel alloys is often reduced significantly as a result of thermal e f-
fects and unfavorable structural changes during subsequent treatment (welding, surfacing) . The paper 
presents the results of studying the structure, phase composition and properties of EP648 alloy during 
argon-arc surfacing. A comparison with the EP648 alloy structures obtained by laser surfacing is pr o-
vided. It was established that argon -arc welding of EP648 alloy in combination with ultrasonic trea t-
ment creates an additional effect o f increasing phase dispersion, which leads to an increase in high -
temperature strength of the alloy. 
Keywords: nickel alloys, argon -arc surfacing, ultrasonic effect, laser surfacing, structure formation, 
high-temperature strength. 
Argon-arc and laser surfa cing are some of the effective additive technologies, which combine ample techn o-
logical capabilities of obtaining complex -shape products with a unique mixture of mechanical and special pro p-
erties of the materials, including highly alloyed nickel alloys. 
The main problem during nickel -chromium alloy treatment with highly concentrated power sources (wel d-
ing, surfacing, soldering, plasma and laser treatment) consists in low strength and heat resistance after heating to 
high temperatures and rapid cooling, whic h is typical for such methods. 
One of the basic principles of the theory of alloying high -temperature alloys accepted in metallurgy is the 
principle of multicomponent alloying for the purpose of nanostructuring a hetero -phase alloy composition .

high-temperature (up to 800 –850°C) and high-stress applications. 
The maximum high -temperature strength of such alloys is achieved after quenching and subsequent aging

The quantitative metallographic analysis of the nickel alloy structures has shown that US -treatment causes 
a significant variation in the ratio betwee n strengthening intermetallic ′γ -phase and γ -solid solution of allo y-
ing elements in the FCC lattice of nickel . However, this variation is not proportional to the distance from the 
US-radiation source . The minimum fraction of the ′γ -phase (3%) was observed at a distance of 15 mm from 
the US -radiation source, while the maximum fraction (5%) was observed directly at the source location (Sa m-
ple 5) and at the maximum distance from it (Sample 1) (Fig . 3). Figures 4 and 5 show the ratio between vol u-
metric frac tions of ′γ -phase and γ -solid solution for two most typical situ ations. In addition, these samples 
demonstrate an increased porosity and large -size nonmetallic inclusions (see Fig . 3). However, in general, the 
volumetric fra ction of pores in all samples produced by argon -arc surfacing does not exceed 0.8%. 
US-radiation causes more significant effect on th e degree of dispersion of the ′γ -phase. The degree of 
dispersion and uniformity of the ′γ -phase distribution were estimated based on the fractal dimension value . 
The fractal analysis makes it possible to reveal a single numerical parameter for the description of structure un i-
formity and fragmentariness, as well as define the relation between the fractal dimension and the level of mater i-
al properties. The value of fractal dimension D = 1.05–1.08 corresponds to the maximum dispersion, and D = 
1.01–1.04 – to the minimum dispersion . A fractal analysis program listing is shown in Fig . 6 for Sample 3 . 
At a distance of 25 –30 mm from the US -source, there is a 15 to 20% increase in dispersion of the ′γ -phase in 
comparison with the sample, which was not subjected to US -treatment, or was located at a greater distance (50 
to 60 mm) from the US -radiation source. 
It is necessary to pay special attention to the correlat ion between the degree of ′γ -phase dispersion and m i-
cro-hardness values: the maximum micro -hardness was observed in case of the deposits, the structure of wh ich 
contained about 4 vol . % of highly -dispersed ′γ -phase fractions . Such high value of microhardness (up to 
250 mm/kg2 ) corresponds to the surfacing performed when the US -source was lo cated at a distance of 25 to 
30 mm, which perhaps is the optimal distance based on this criterion.

CONCLUSIONS 
1. It was established that the arc surfacing with nickel alloy EP648 in combination with additional ultraso n-
ic treatment creates a modifying effect of increasing the dispersion of ph ases.

fraction (3%) is observed at 15 mm distance from the US -radiation source, and the maximum content (5%) is
