1. Introduction 
Due to the lattice distortion effect, sluggish effect and high-entropy 
effect [ 1 , 2 ], some refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have high 
melting point and good elevated-temperature strength. Therefore, 
RHEAs are considered to be potentially ideal high-temperature struc -
tural materials. However, most RHEAs exhibit poor room-temperature 
ductility when they have good high-temperature strength. For 
example, though the yield strength of NbMoTaW RHEA is 405 MPa even 
at 1600 
◦
C, the fracture strain of the NbMoTaW RHEA is just only 2.1% 
at room-temperature [ 3 ]. The workability and practical application of 
RHEAs may be significantly restricted by the poor room-temperature 
ductility. In addition, the high-temperature strength of such materials 
needs to be further improved so that they can be used in the field of 
ultra-high-temperature structural materials. Therefore, addressing these 
issues is of both scientific and engineering importance. 
To improve the high-temperature strength of RHEAs, many re -
searchers have introduced C [ 4 , 5 ] or Si [ 6 , 7 ] elements to form carbides 
or silicides in RHEAs. For instance, Luo [ 4 ] et al. reported the effect of 
different carbide content in MoNbW alloys on the mechanical properties 
of the alloy, according to their study, the MoNbW(TaC)
0.5

a yield strength of nearly 800 MPa at 1200 
◦

improve the high-temperature strength of the alloy, it often has a 
negative effect on room-temperature ductility [ 10 – 12 ]. 
In order to improve the room-temperature ductility of RHEAs, many 
researchers have attempted to add ductile metal elements such as Ti 
[ 13 ], Zr [ 14 ], and Hf [ 14 , 15 ] to the RHEAs. Wan et al. [ 13 ] significantly 
improved the room-temperature ductility of NbMoTaW RHEA by 
introducing Ti element. The WTaMoNbTi RHEA prepared by them 
showed a good fracture strain of 14.1% at room-temperature. However, 
its yield strength was only 173 MPa at 1600
