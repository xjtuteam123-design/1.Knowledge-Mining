1. Introduction
In recent years, the problems of energy and the environment are 
becoming more and more intense with the development of science and 
technology, putting forward higher standards for material research. 
Since lightweight materials can reduce the weight of machine parts, and 
solve the contradiction between energy and the environment, they have 
been widely used in automobiles, ships, aerospace, and other fields [ 1 ,
2 ]. Therefore, researchers mainly solve the related contradictions by 
designing lightweight high-strength materials [ 3 ]. Numerous researches 
show that conventional lightweight materials are mainly divided into 
two categories: (i) the traditional lightweight alloys (magnesium alloy, 
aluminum alloy, titanium alloy, etc.) [ 4 – 6 ]; (ii) light-weight multi-ele -
ment alloys (ultra-light-weight multi-element alloys mainly composed 
of Li, Be, Mg, Al, Zn, Sc and other elements [ 7 ] and light-weight 
multi-element alloys (LMPEAs) mainly containing Ti, Al, V, Zr, Nb, Cr 
and other elements [ 8 , 9 ]. However, lightweight alloys are designed 
using traditional alloy phase diagrams, and the strength of these alloys 
have reached their limits [ 10 ]. Ultra-lightweight multi-principal-ele -
ment alloys have ultra-high specific hardness and specific strength, but 
they are limited by the price of raw materials, toxicity, and abundance 
[ 11 ]. Thus, there still exist many limitations to the promotion and 
application of traditional lightweight alloys and ultra-lightweight mul -
ti-principal element alloys in the engineering field. Therefore, it is ur -
gently needed to develop and design LMPEAs with low cost and high 
performance.
In the past two decades, based on the design idea of a multi-principal 
alloy proposed by Cantor [ 12 ] and Yeh [ 13 ], researchers gradually 
shifted the research focus to the design of new LMPEAs [ 14 – 23 ].

2. Materials and methods
Based on the existing TiZrVNbAl alloy with an equal atomic ratio and 
Ti-rich multi-component alloy, we designed the current LMPEAs with a 
high specific strength. We selected Ti as the main element of the alloy 
and V as a strengthening element with a fixed atomic fraction. By 
adjusting the content of Ti we tuning the density of the LMPEAs. To 
balance the relationship between low density and high strength, we 
designed a fixed atomic ratio for Al, Zr, Nb at 2:1:1. Three as-cast alloys

spectrometer. The EBSD system includes the NordlysMax2 probe and 
Aztec 3.1 software for data post-processing. For EBSD measurement, the 
analyzed sample surface was mechanically ground by 400 – 5000# sili -

data points (100 μ m interval distance between adjacent points) in par -
allel paths were collected for each sample to calculate the average 
hardness values. The test was carried out using a testing load of 200 g, 
with a loading time of 10 s. Then, the compressive strength was

4.3. Quasi-in-situ compression deformation mechanism
