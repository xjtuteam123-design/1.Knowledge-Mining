ABSTRACT
It is of great importance to explore alternative alloying elements to replace rhenium (Re) for the design of low-
cost Ni-based single crystal (SC) superalloys. Due to the partitioning of Re to γ matrix, the individual and the
combined eﬀ ects of the single (vacancy, Mo, W and Re) and solute-vacancy complex (Mo-Va, W-Va and Re-Va)
defects on the plasticity of γ-Ni have been studied by the ﬁrst-principles calculations of generalized stacking fault
energies (GSFE). It is found that a single vacancy and an alloying solute Mo/W/Re at the fault plane decreases
the intrinsic stacking fault energy of Ni from 131 mJ/m
2 to 106 mJ/m 2 and ∼97 mJ/m2 respectively. Further
reductions are made by the introduction of a solute-vacancy complex defect ( ∼80 mJ/m2). Furthermore, the
unstable planar fault energies tuned by the Mo-Va and W-Va complex defects are almost identical, which are
both slightly smaller than those by Re-Va. Finally, the calculated values of three twinnability measures elucidate
that W and Mo provide similar enhancements in the twinning propensity of γ-Ni to that by Re. This result
suggests a possibility of partly replacing Re by Mo or W in Ni-based SC superalloys.
1. Introduction
With an outstanding combination of high strength, ductility, oxi-
dation resistance and good thermal conductivity over a broad range of
temperatures, Ni-based single crystal (SC) superalloys are widely ap-
plied in aerospace and petrochemical industries especially in aero-en-
gine combustor, turbine disk and turbine blade [1]. The excellent me-
chanical properties of Ni-based SC superalloys are primarily obtained
from solid solution strengthening of the γ-Ni matrix, precipitation
strengthening of the L1 2-ordered γ′-Ni3Al phase as well as γ/γ′ inter-
facial strengthening [2]. Therein, the solid solution strengthening is
achieved by the alloying of γ-formation elements, such as W, Mo, Re, Cr
and Co. These alloying solutes increase the lattice mis ﬁt and hinder the
dislocation motions due to their larger atomic diameters. Besides, these
alloying solutes can also tune the stacking fault energy (SFE) of γ ma-
trix, which is an intrinsic material parameter related to the plastic de-
formation behaviors and mechanical properties. In face-centered cubic
(fcc) lattice, the glide of a full dislocation may occur by dissociating into
two partial dislocations together with a stacking fault formed in be-
tween. Stacking fault energy is deﬁ ned as the additional energy per unit
area required for this process. Based on the elastic theory, a larger
spacing between the two partial dislocations indicates a lower value of
SFE and a larger resistance for their recombination. Alloying with W,
Mo, Re, Co and other transition elements have been proved to lower the
SFE of γ-Ni [3–5], inhibit the cross-slip behaviors [3] and increase the
creep life of Ni-based superalloys [6].
However, SFE only describes the metastable stacking fault state but
not the whole deformation process. As a result, uniquely using SFE as a
criterion for plastic deformation sometimes comes to incorrect con-
clusions [7]. Based on generalized stacking fault energy (GSFE) pro-
posed by Vitek [8] in 1970s, recent researchers [9,10] dictate the
competition between the full slip and deformation twinning. Using the
combination of GSFE calculations and relative models, a vast number of
phenomena related to dislocations [11,12], plastic deformation regimes
[9–11,13], twinning propensities [14–16] and phase transitions [14,17]
have been investigated. A consistent conclusion has been drawn that
alloying with W, Mo, Co, Re enhances the twinning tendency with a
lower twin stress of Ni [3,5]. Based on the GSFE calculation results,
Siegel et al. [5] found that Ni- x (x = Nb, W, Mn, Fe, Cu) alloys have
similar ductility, but higher tendencies to experience deformation

As an e ﬀective strengthening element, rhenium (Re) receives many
attentions in recent materials design of Ni-based SC superalloys. The
diﬀerent generations of Ni-based superalloys are even characterized by
the content of Re. Previous works reveal that Re additions to SC su-
peralloys could improve the creep strength dramatically [19,20], which
indispensable strengthening e ﬀect of Re is dubbed ‘Rhenium e ﬀect’.
The slow di ﬀusion and high solute-vacancy exchange energy of Re is
considered to support this special e ﬀect to some extent, but the me-
chanism of Re e ﬀect is still unclear. Moreover, the limited reserve of
this expensive element, as well as the high propensity of the formation
of topologically close-packed phases (TCP) caused by Re, both motivate
the development of the low-cost superalloys with lower Re additions.
One eﬀective solution is that exploring the elements for partly replacing
Re with sacri ﬁcing least mechanical properties. Recent reports indicate
that the size of γ′ decreases slightly with increasing Mo content, while
W is irrelative to the TCP precipitation [21] with a low di ﬀusion rate
[22,23] and negative segregation coe ﬃcient in γ′ [24]. Alloying with W
also has similar e ﬀects on the mechanical and thermodynamic prop-
erties of Ni
3Al to those of Re [25]. Besides, Mo, W and Re have similar
atomic radii, valence electronic structures as well as the reductions of
GSFE in Ni alloys [3]. In other words, W and Mo are expected to be the
candidates to partly substitute for Re in Ni SC superalloys.
In this work, the plastic deformation behaviors of di ﬀerent alloyed
γ-Ni systems are studied via the ﬁrst-principles calculations of the
GSFE. We pay a close attention on the combined e ﬀects of a single
vacancy and an alloying solute on the GSFE of γ-Ni. The strengthening
mechanisms behind are discussed further by the possibility evaluation
of replacing Re with other alloying elements in Ni-based solid solution.
The present paper is organized as follows: In the ﬁrst part, the in-
dividual e ﬀect of a single vacancy and an alloying solute W, Mo or Re
on the GSFE of γ-Ni are studied via ﬁrst-principles calculations. Next,
we focus on the combined e ﬀects of the solute-vacancy complex defects,
which both located on the stacking fault plane. Finally, the plastic de-
formation behaviors of the γ-Ni systems are discussed, based on the
evaluation of twinning propensity analysis.
2. Computational methods
In the present work, all the calculations are carried out by the
Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [26,27]. The ion-electron
interaction is described by the projector augmented wave (PAW)
method [28,29] and the Pardew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-cor-
relation function is applied within the generalized gradient approx-
imation (GGA) [30]. The plane wave cuto ﬀ energy of 450 eV is tested to
be enough for the negligible total energy change within 1 meV/atom. A
k-point sampling of 3 × 5 × 1 based on the Monkhorst-Pack scheme is
adopted for the integration of reciprocal-space energy in the Brillouin
zone [31]. Spin polarized calculations are conducted in the case of
ferromagnetic nature of Ni to accurately capture its ferromagnetic
ground state at 0 K. The self-consistent convergence for total energy is
set to be 10
−5 eV/atom.
A slab orthorhombic supercell of 104 atoms (13 layers, 8 atoms per
layer) with perfect stacking sequence of fcc lattice is constructed to
calculate GSFE curves as illustrated in Fig. 1. The primitive lattice
vectors a1, a2 and a3 are set along [112¯], [11¯ 0] and [111 ] directions
respectively, in which =aa 610 , =aa 620 , =aa 33 0 and a0 is the
optimized γ-Ni lattice parameter (3.516 Å) based on our total energy
calculation results. Vacuum spacing of 15 Å is added along the [111 ]
direction (a3) to eliminate the interactions between the stacking faults
in neighboring supercells under the periodic boundary condition.
During the plastic deformation of fcc Ni, the smallest possible per-
fect dislocation with a Burger vector of =b a/ 2[ 101¯] splits into two
Shockley partial dislocations. If the leading partial dislocation
( =b a/ 6[ 112¯]) continues to glide on (1 1 1) plane, an intrinsic stacking
fault (ISF) is created. Otherwise, an extrinsic stacking fault (ESF) forms
if a twinning partial dislocation glides on the (1 1 1) plane adjacent to
the initial one. These two processes are simulated by shifting speci ﬁc
atom planes in the supercell ( Fig. 1 ) and the GSFE curves are obtained
by the total energy calculations of di ﬀerent con ﬁgurations. In the ﬁrst
process (Step 1), the starting con ﬁguration is a perfect supercell in
ABCABCABCABCA sequence, and the upper half is shifted along
[112¯]
direction over a distance of =b a /600 and an ISF is created in CAB-
CABABCABCA sequence. The second process (Step 2) is mimicked by
shifting the lower half an identical distance in the opposite direction to
form an ESF in CABCABACABCAB sequence. Atoms in the supercell are
only allowed to relax along the direction perpendicular to the (1 1 1)
plane to achieve the close packing con ﬁgurations during the shearing.

5. Conclusions
In the present work, a ﬁrst-principles study has been conducted to
calculate the generalized stacking fault energies of γ-Ni systems with
single point defects and solute-vacancy complex defects. The results on
pure γ-Ni are consistent with the previous experimental and theoretical
ones, verifying the high accuracy of the model used. The intrinsic
stacking fault energy of γ-Ni is found to be decreased from 131 mJ/m
2
to 106 mJ/m 2 by a single vacancy and further to ∼97 mJ/m2 by a
single Mo/W/Re alloying solute. Moreover, solute-vacancy complex
defect of Mo-Va/W-Va/Re-Va makes a larger reduction to ∼80 mJ/m2.
However, the combined e ﬀect of solute-vacancy complex defect on the
intrinsic stacking fault energy is smaller than the sum of the two in-
dividual eﬀects due to the interactions between the vacancy and solute.
It is important to note that Mo and W have consistent reduction e ﬀects
on the unstable planar fault energies, while Re has a smaller e ﬀect no
matter they are introduced as a single defect or a complex defect with
vacancy. As a result, the nucleation/formation of leading/twinning
partial dislocations induced by Re is only a bit di ﬃcult in contrast with
that by Mo and W. Finally, the twinning propensity of γ-Ni evaluated by
three twinnability measures of
δus
ut , γd and τcrit increases with the gra-
dual introduction of the point defects from single to complex. Based on
the similar e ﬀects among W, Mo and Re on the planar fault energies
(especially γis) and twinnability of γ-Ni, we have made a preliminary
prediction of the possibility of partly substituting Re by Mo or/and W in
superalloys. The results obtained also shed light on the composition
design of modern low-cost Ni-based SC superalloys.
