Introduction
The strengthening of Ni-based superalloys is mainly obtained by the coherent precipitation of a large
amount of Ni3Al type g9 phase in a Ni-based g matrix (1). The misfit of g/g9 is less than 1% in most
Ni-based superalloys. Moreover, Yamabe-Mitarai et al. (2) found that Ir-based refractory superalloys
had higher 0.2% flow stress even at high temperature and that this good high-temperature strength came
from the fcc/L1
2 coherent structure. They demonstrated that the morphology of L1 2 precipitates
(irregular shape, cuboidal precipitates to plate-like until a three-dimensional maze structure) was the
result of a lattice misfit of less than 0.2 to 0.33 up to about 2%, respectively.
Up to now, in either Ni-based superalloys or Ir-based alloys, only one kind of L1
2 precipitates exists.
Moreover, no lamella structure has been found in these superalloys. It has been demonstrated that two
kinds of L1
2 precipitates, Ni3Al- and Ir 3Nb-typy, existed in the fcc- g matrix in Ni-12.6Al-20Ir-5Nb
superalloys as well as a lamellar structure (3). The present study focuses on the general microstructural
observation of this lamellar structure and the effect of the lamellar structure on the compressive 0.2%
flow stress.
Experimental Procedures
Two kinds of binary alloys, Ir-20at.%Nb and Ni-16.8at.%Al, were selected to prepare the quaternary
superalloys. The method is explained in detail in Ref. (4). The proportion of Ir-Nb and Ni-Al binary
alloys was 25:75 with a nominal composition: Ni-12.6Al-20Ir-5Nb (at. %). The ingot was prepared by
the arc-melting method in an argon atmosphere. To understand the phase evolution at high temperature,
these samples were heated at 1300°C and 1400° for 1hr as well as 1250°C, 1300°C, and 1400°C for
168hrs in a vacuum furnace, respectively. The microstructures of as-cast and heated samples were
observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Some images were taken by the method of
back-scattered electron image (BSI) in order to distinguish Ir
3Nb from Ni3Al and the g matrix. Phase
structure analysis and lattice misfits were carried out by the X-ray method. The equation d 5
(aL12-afcc)/afcc was used to calculate the lattice misfit. Compressive tests were carried out at room
temperature and 1200°C. The initial rate was 4 3 1023/s. The fracture was also observed in a sample
heated at 1300°C for 168hrs.

Results and Analysis
Microstructural Evolution
Figure 1 shows the microstructures of quaternary Ni-12.6Al-20Ir-5Nb superalloys on an as-cast
condition and heated at 1250°C for 168hrs. The as-cast microstructure of the alloy was of dendrite
structure (Fig. 1a). The characteristics of the microstructure were similar to those of Ni-based
superalloys. In the interdendritic regions,
g-fcc dendrite solid solution and precipitates were observed.
Phase analysis results revealed that the precipitated phase in the interdendritic region was the L12-Ni3Al
phase, which contained some Ir and Nb. After the heat treatment, another kind of L12 phase was formed,
and two kinds of lamellar structures, g/Ni3Al 1 Ir3Nb, were mixed (Fig. 1b). The lamellar structure
consisted of alternating lamellae of Ni-rich solid solution and L1 2 phase (Ir3Nb or Ni 3Al).
BSI images of the alloy heated at different temperatures and times are shown in Fig. 2. From these
pictures, we can see that the Ni3Al particles were of medium contrast and larger size; on the other hand,
the larger Ir3Nb particles were of bright contrast, and the darkest one was g solid solution. Two kinds
of lamellar structure formed even when the alloy was heated at 1300°C and 1400°C only for 1hr (Figs.
2a, b). The lamellar structures were precipitated from the
g dendrites (Fig. 2a). After the sample was
heated at 1300°C for 168hrs, coarsening also occurred in front of the lamellar plates (Fig. 2c marked
by arrows). The lamellar structure,
g/Ni3Al, disappeared, but a small lamellar structure, g/Ir3Nb, still
remained after heating at 1400°C for 168hrs (Fig. 2d). This may imply that the lamellar structure of
g/Ni3Al was stable under 1400°C.
The lattice parameters and lattice misfits obtained from X-ray results are shown in Fig. 3. Fig. 3 (a)
reveals that lattice parameter of g in the Ni-12.6Al-20Ir-5Nb alloy is larger than that of Ni-Al binary
alloy and smaller than that of Ir-Nb binary alloy, so do the Ni 3Al and Ir3Nb. However, both of lattice
misfits of Ni3Al and Ir3Nb in the quaternary alloy are larger than that in binary alloys. The lattice misfits
(Fig. 3b) at room temperature are 2.66% for fcc/Ni3Al and 6.39% for fcc/Ir3Nb, respectively. They were
about an order larger than that of g/Ni3Al in the Ni-Al binary alloy (1) and g/Ir3Nb in the Ir-Nb binary
alloy (2).

Discussion
Lattice Misfit and Lamellar Structure
The atomic radius and concentration of alloying elements affect the lattice parameters of a solid solution
with the fcc structure in quaternary refractory superalloys. The L1 2 phases, Ni3Al and Ir3Nb, are of the
ordered fcc structure. The atomic radius and concentration of alloying elements also affect their lattice
parameter.
The lattice parameters, Ir and Ni, are 0.38394 and 0.35238 nm, respectively. The lattice parameter
of fcc, 0.36059nm, in the quaternary Ni-12.6Al-20Ir-5Nb alloy was larger than that of Ni-Al and
smaller than that of Ir-Nb binary alloys (see Fig. 3a), as were Ni
3Al and Ir 3Nb. The morphology of
Ni3Al and Ir3Nb indicated a cuboidal shape in the two kinds of binary alloys because the lattice misfit
of g/Ni3Al and g/Ir3Nb in the two binary alloys was about 0.3%. However, the misfits of g/Ni3Al and

Strength and Lamellar Structure
The strength of quaternary alloys can be estimated by the law of the mixture strength of the composite
materials as a linear combination of the strength of the two types of component binary alloys. From Fig.
4, we can see that the strengths of quaternary alloys are located between the strengths of Ni- and
Ir-based binary alloys. And, the strength mechanism of quaternary refractory superalloys are attributed
to the solid solution hardening, precipitation hardening and interface strengthening (6).

forward by an antiphase boundary (APB) remaining in the precipitate when the leading dislocation
shears a spherical precipitate. The shear stress of moving dislocation,

Conclusions
1. Two kinds of lamellar structure of fcc/Ni 3Al and fcc/Ir 3Nb were observed in quaternary Ir-5Nb-
62.4Ni-12.6Al alloys.
2. Lattice misfits of around 3% for fcc/Ni 3Al and around 6% for fcc/Ir 3Nb assist in the formation of
lamellar structures.
3. There was a increment in compressive strength at 1200°C for the samples heated at 1300 and
1400°C. The increment was attributed to the lamellar structure.
