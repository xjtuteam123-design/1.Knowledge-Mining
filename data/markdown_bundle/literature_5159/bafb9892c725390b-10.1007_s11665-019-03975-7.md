The Hot Bending and Diffusion Bonding of TiAl-Based
Alloy for Corrugated-Core Sandwich Structure
Zhihao Du and Kaifeng Zhang
(Submitted April 8, 2018; in revised form December 4, 2018)
The multi-layer structure of TiAl-based alloys can meet the demand of having lightweight and high-
strength properties at elevated temperature. In this paper, the corrugated-core sandwich structure of a
TiAl-based alloy was fabricated by the hot bending/diffusion bonding process. In order to fabricate this
structure, the high-temperature tensile property, hot bending behavior, and vacuum diffusion bonding of a
Ti-47.5Al-Cr-V alloy were investigated. The ﬂow behavior was evaluated by uniaxial tensile experiment,
and the results showed that the alloy exhibited good plasticity at the given conditions, and the maximum
elongation reached 200% at 1000 /C176C at strain rate of 3 3 10
24 s21. The bending experiments were also
performed at temperatures of 950, 1000, and 1050 /C176C with ﬁllet radii of R = 0, 0.5, and 1 mm, respectively.
No micro-/macro-defects developed. The vacuum diffusion bonding of this alloy was also carried out, and
the shear bonding strength of bonding joints was measured at room temperature. The maximum shear
strength was 398 MPa, and the joint was obtained at 1000 /C176C/30 MPa/3 h. Combining the bonding
interface with the shear strength of different joints, the appropriate bonding pressure and bonding time for
diffusion bonding of the TiAl alloy at 1000 /C176C were 20 MPa and 2 h, respectively. Then, the forming
process of the corrugated-core sheet was conducted at 1000 /C176C with a ﬁllet radius of 1 mm. Finally, the
corrugated-core sandwich structure was manufactured under vacuum atmosphere, and the mechanical
properties and the interface microstructure were investigated.
Keywords compression resistance, corrugated-core structure,
diffusion bonding, hot bending, TiAl-based alloy
1. Introduction
As a result of its low density, high speciﬁc strength,
sufﬁcient resistance to oxidation and corrosion, and good creep
resistance at elevated temperatures, the TiAl-based alloy had
been considered a promising lightweight material candidate for
advanced high-temperature structural applications (Ref 1-5).
The multi-layer structure of TiAl-based alloy can meet the
demand of having lightweight and high-strength properties at
elevated temperatures that are often encountered in the
aerospace ﬁeld (Ref 6) In addition, metallic sandwich structures
have recently been investigated for their lightweight and
multifunctional characteristics, as well as their resistance to
blast (Ref 7, 8). The corrugated-core sandwich construction had
been chosen as a candidate structure for the design of internal
thermal protection systems of space vehicles (Ref 9). The
corrugated-core sandwich structure is comprised of a corruga-
tion sheet that is placed between two thin face sheets (Ref 10).
Several studies have investigated the joining techniques of
TiAl-based alloys, such as fusion welding (Ref 11, 12), brazing
(Ref 13, 14), and diffusion bonding (DB) (Ref 15-23). Some
multi-layer structural components of TiAl-based alloys have
been fabricated by using different joining technologies (Ref 6).
The high crack sensitivity of TiAl alloy and the low mechanical
properties caused by the microstructure between different weld
zones and the base material render it difﬁcult to be joined by
using conventional fusion welding (Ref 24). When brazing is
used, the formation of multiphase interface and variation of
mechanical behavior along the entire interface cannot be
avoided (Ref 25, 26).
Compared with fusion welding and brazing, DB does not
produce drastic local changes in microstructure and related
mechanical properties, and this process partially or completely
eliminates mechanical connection and welding. Moreover,
vacuum DB also avoids brittle cracks caused by vapor and
hydrogen in air (Ref 27), and hence, it is considered as an
appropriate way for the formation of joints of TiAl-based alloy.
Cam et al. (Ref 15, 16) investigated the DB process of cast
c-TAB [Ti-47Al-4.5 (Cr, Mn, Nb, Si, B) at.%] and that of c-
TiAl sheets. at 1000 C for 5 h and 8 h. Wu et al. (Ref 19, 20)
investigated the superplastic diffusion bonding of cast Ti-
46.5Al (at.%) and Ti-45Al (at.%) alloys at lower temperature
and for shorter time by laser treatment of the surface. Shi et al.
(Ref 21) investigated the pulse current-assisted diffusion
bonding (PCDB) behavior of sintered c-TiAl-based alloy
[Ti46.5Al2Cr1.8Nb-(W, B)] with near-gamma microstructure.
And the spark plasma sintering (SPS) was also used to
investigated the effects of bonding parameters on the diffusion
bonding process of a powder metallurgical (PM) Ti-45Al-7Nb-
0.3W (at.%) alloy (Ref 22).
Combining the excellent properties of TiAl-based alloys
with the advantages of the sandwich (three-layer) structure, a
corrugated-core sandwich structure of TiAl-based alloy was
fabricated in this paper. The mechanical properties including
tensile deformation behavior and hot bending property were
investigated at high temperature. The DB process of the TiAl-

3.4 The Forming Process of Corrugate-Core Sandwich
Structure
Superplastic forming/diffusion bonding (SPF/DB) technol-
ogy has been widely used to manufacture multi-layer structures
(including three-layer structure) of titanium alloy in recent
decades (Ref 28). In addition, the SPF/DB process is able to
take full advantage of the superplasticity and diffusion
bondability of titanium alloys (Ref 29).The superplasticity of
TiAl-based alloy has been investigated by many researchers
(Ref 30-33), though it was much lower than that of titanium
alloy. From the investigation of tensile property in section 3.1,
it was shown that the elongation was too low to use SPF/DB
process to fabricate the multi-layer structures of TiAl-based
alloy. Thus, the hot bending combined with diffusion bonding
process was introduced to manufacture the corrugated-core
sandwich structure.
Figure 8 illustrates the hot bending/diffusion bonding
process of corrugated-core structure for a TiAl-based alloy.
This process included the forming of core sheet and diffusion
bond between core sheets and face sheets. Figure 8(a) shows
the forming of the corrugated-core sheet. The temperatures of
hot bending were 950 and 1000 /C176 C, and the ﬁnished core sheet
after hot bending is shown in Fig. 9. It can be seen that the
corrugated-core sheet formed at 1000 /C176 C had better ﬂatness and
forming precision than that at 950 /C176 C. Thus, the forming
temperature for core sheet was selected as 1000 /C176 C. Figure 8(b)
represents the diffusion bonding process of three sheets, and the
diffusion bond parameter was 1000 /C176 C at a load of 20 MPa for
2 h. The cushion block was also made of graphite.
3.5 The Compressive Behavior of Corrugated Structure
The compressive stress curve and corrugated structure after
test are shown in Fig. 10.
The maximum displacement of this structure was 0.15-
0.16 mm as shown in Fig. 10(a). The compressive stress was
calculated from the whole contact area between this structure
and the board. The compressive stress increased with the
displacement increasing, and the maximum compressive stress
was 15.7 MPa. The compressive stress–displacement curve
was linear at the stage when the displacement varied from 0.06

due to the occurrence of the cracks. And the macro-fracture
morphology is shown in Fig. 10(b), and it showed that the
fracture mode was brittle fracture.

4.3 Microstructure Evolution
The ﬁnished corrugated-core sandwich structure and the
microstructure of bonding interface between core sheet and face
sheet are shown in Fig. 13(a) and (b), respectively. There was
no micro-cavity on the bonding interface as shown in
Fig. 13(b), representing an obtained sound joint.
It is shown that there was no obvious difference between the
microstructure of the post-hot bending/DB shown in Fig. 13(b)
and the initial microstructure shown in Fig. 1(a). In addition, it
was different from the microstructure evolution of TC4 alloy
and Ti
2AlNb alloy (Ref 34, 35) in the SPF/DB process, due to a
long duration of thermal exposure. In the SPF/DB process of
TC4 alloy and Ti 2AlNb alloy, the process temperature was near
the phase transformation temperature for each alloy. As such,
the thermal exposure at high temperature resulted in the
remarkable microstructure evolution. In this investigation, the
hot bending/DB temperature was selected to be 1000 /C176 C,
whereas the phase transformation ( c + a
2 ﬁ c + a) temper-
ature was 1120 /C176 C from the binary TiAl phase diagram
calculated by thermodynamic equilibrium (Ref 36). In addition,
the phase transformation temperature for the present alloy
maybe at the temperature from 1170 to 1180 /C176 C, which had
been investigated by the authors (Ref 37). Thus, it was much
higher than the process temperature, and the thermal exposure
had a little effect on the grain growth or the phase transfor-
mation.
4.4 The Failure Process of the Compression Test
The height of this structure was about 9 mm, and the
deformation of the corrugated structure of TiAl-based alloy was
small. Owing to the intrinsic brittleness of the TiAl-based alloy,
the brittle fracture occurred when the compressive displacement
exceeded the maximum value. The compression ratio was
approximately 1.7%.
The fracture morphology is shown in Fig. 14. It is shown
that the fracture mode of compression was the typical
intergranular fracture, and the fracture mode was consistent
with the intrinsic brittle of near- c TiAl alloy. It is also shown
that the fractures mainly developed at the corners near the
bonding areas. The reasons were that the stress concentration
existed at the intersection near the bonding areas, and the core
sheet was also the load-bearing part.

5. Conclusion
In this study, the mechanical properties and diffusion
bonding of TiAl-based alloy were investigated and the
corrugated-core sandwich structure was fabricated by hot
bending/diffusion bonding process. The following conclusions
are drawn:
1. The Ti-47.5Al-Cr-V alloy exhibited good plasticity with
maximum elongation of 200% at 1000 /C176 C and at a strain
rate of 3 9 10
/C04 s/C01.
2. The Ti-47.5Al-Cr-V alloy could be diffusion-bonded well
in vacuum, and the maximum shear strength of bonding
joints obtained at 1000 /C176 C/30 MPa/3 h was 398 MPa.
3. The corrugated-core sandwich structure of Ti-47.5Al-Cr-
V alloy with low superplasticity was manufactured by the
hot bending/DB process, showing that the hot bending/
DB process is an appropriate way to fabricate this struc-
ture.
4. No defect was found in the bonding areas of the struc-
ture, and the compressive strength of this structure was
about 15.7 MPa. The brittleness of the proposed structure
was presented. The compression stress decreased sharply
when cracking developed at the corners near the bonding
areas.
