ABSTRACT
Keeping adequate strain hardening to postpone plastic instability to a larger tensile strain is a stiff challenge in 
alloys with high yield strength. This study showed the feasibility of activating deformation twins (DTs) to 
ductilize an ultra-strong medium-entropy alloy (MEA) through microstructural design. High content (~24 %) γ’’ 
precipitates were introduced into a Ni

Face-centered cubic (fcc) type medium- and high-entropy alloys (M/ 
HEAs) are promising advanced engineering materials owing to their 
outstanding deformability and excellent resistance to corrosion and 
oxidation [ 1-3 ]. However, single fcc phase structured M/HEAs usually 
confront the predicament of excess ductility and insufficient yield 
strength, which seriously hampers their wider utilization. To address 
this issue, second-phase precipitates are extensively exploited for 
strengthening single-fcc M/HEAs while largely retaining their ductility 
[ 4 ]. Among them, the γ’ phase with an L1
2 
superlattice has been widely 
employed with remarkable success [ 5-8 ]. Comparatively, the γ’’ phase 
with a D0
22 
superlattice also shows a significant strengthening effect due 
to its higher anti-phase boundary (APB) energy and larger lattice misfit. 
He et al. designed a NiCoFeCr HEA containing a 7 % volume fraction of 
γ’’ phase, which exhibited a respectable yield stress of ~954 MPa [ 9 ]. Lu 
et al. also achieved a yield stress of ~960 MPa in a NiFeCr MEA by 
introducing 14.8 % volume fraction of γ’’ phase [ 10 ]. Pan et al. obtained 
a yield stress of ~890.6 MPa in a γ’’ -hardened NiCoCr-based MEA [ 11 ]. 
Besides, additively manufactured γ’’ -precipitated M/HEAs also show 
excellent strength and ductility synergies [ 12-14 ]. Nevertheless, the 
yield strength of the currently reported γ’’ -precipitated M/HEAs still 
needs further improvement to meet the increasingly stringent demands 
of engineering parts for safety-critical applications or lightweight 
structural design. Further elevating the strength of γ’’ -precipitated 
M/HEAs generally involves increasing γ’’ phase ’ s content, which how -
ever, will disappointingly cause the early local necking and fractures at 
relatively low tensile strains. Hence, how to efficiently ductilize the 
ultra-strong γ’’ -precipitated M/HEAs with postponed localized necking 
and higher plastic strains remains a critical challenge.
Nano-twinning is well known to act as an effective barrier to mobile 
dislocations [ 15-17 ]. Among them, deformation-induced twinning can 
provide enhanced strain-hardening capacity and associated plasticity to 
ductilize metallic materials [ 18-20 ]. However, inducing such twinning 
in single-fcc M/HEAs at ambient temperature under quasi-static tensile 
loading conditions is not easy since the critical twinning stress is usually 
out of reach [ 21 ]. More significantly, due to the strong hindrance by 
finely dispersed precipitates, deformation twinning is also quite difficult

twinnability of the γ’’ precipitates in IN718 alloy is likely to be improved 
with the increasing γ’’ precipitate size [ 25 ]. Yet, these microtwins were

not only governed by the compositionally influenced SFE but also 
affected by the Frank-Read (FR) source size (usually the grain size of

nanoparticle spacing can be regarded as the FR source size, the move -

spacing [ 24 , 27 ]. Therefore, it is expected that deformation twinning 
could be activated by manipulating the nanometer-scale geometries of 
the precipitate/matrix system and thus effectively ductilize the 
γ’’ -precipitated M/HEAs for achieving enhanced strength-ductility 
synergy.

Fig. 2 a presents the room temperature (RT) tensile curves of the 
studied MEAs. Notably, ultrahigh yield stresses of 1.6 ± 0.02 GPa and 
1.55 ± 0.02 GPa were achieved in the 700-12A and 800-4A alloy, 
respectively, exhibiting a significant increase compared with the 
recrystallized alloy. Such remarkable enhancement in yield strength was 
primarily attributed to the contribution of the coherency and ordering 
strengthening caused by the γ’’ precipitate with a relatively high APB 
energy (~296 mJ/m
2
) [ 10 , 12 ]. Unlike the 700-12A alloy, which ex -
hibits only ~2 % uniform elongation due to premature local necking, the 
800-4A alloy shows a more uniform deformation (see the inserted SEM 
images of the fracture surface) due to its superior strain hardening that 
delays the necking to ~10 % plastic strain, leading to a high UTS of 1.7 
± 0.02 GPa. Surprisingly, the significant increase in fracture elongation 
of the 800-4A alloy was ascribed to its unique strain-hardening capacity 
under such a high-level yield strength, which can be understood from 
the multiple-stage strain-hardening behaviors in Fig. 2 b . The anomalous 
increase and stability of the strain-hardening rate in stages II and III 
collaboratively postponed its plastic instability upon straining. Such an 
excellent combination of high yield strength with good ductility in the 
800-4A alloy at ambient temperature outperforms many other existing 
γ’’ -precipitated M/HEAs [ 9-14 , 29-32 ], as displayed in Fig. 2 c . Addi -
tionally, its tensile yield strength also surpasses many other M/HEAs and 
some superalloys over a wide temperature range, as shown in Fig. S3 , 
suggesting great potential for high-temperature structural applications.
Subsequently, TEM characterizations were conducted on both 
tensile-deformed alloys to clarify their microscopic deformation mech -
anisms at room temperature. Attentions were first paid to the deformed 
grains of the two alloys, as summarized in Fig. 3 . Typical bright-filed 
TEM (BF-TEM) images of the fractured 700-12A alloy are displayed in 
Fig. 3 a and b . It can be seen that the 700-12A alloy was accumulated 
with high-density dislocations, while other planar defects (stacking 
faults and twins) were hardly observed. Fig. 3 c further gives a close-up 
view (highlighted area in Fig. 3 b ) of the extensive curved dislocation 
within the 700-12A alloy. The rapid localized accumulation of disloca -
tions during straining is responsible for premature necking due to the 
relatively small precipitate spacing (i.e., small dislocation mean free 
path). More careful TEM investigations were performed on the deformed 
800-4A alloy to elucidate its deformation microstructures. When the 
alloy deformed to a true strain of ~5 % (Stage II), the BF-TEM image in 
Fig. 3 d shows that pronounced DTs along with discrete stacking faults 
(SFs) are prevalent within the alloy, as evidenced by the inserted SAD 
pattern. Measurements from the corresponding DF-TEM images ( Fig. 3 e 
shows a typical one) reveal that the mechanical twins at this deforma -
tion stage have a mean spacing of ~87.83 nm. Also, they were usually 
accompanied by overlapped SFs since their thickening involves multiple 
SFs propagating on adjacent {111} planes [ 33 ], as detailed in the 
high-resolution TEM image ( Fig. 3 f ). The onset of DTs certainly con -
tributes to the abnormal increase in strain-hardening rate at Stage II. 
When the alloy was further stretched to fracture, as shown in Fig. 3 g , 
extensive DTs were frequently observed within the grain interior and 
their mean spacing decreased to ~27.96 nm. The comparison of the 
HR-TEM images of twins in Figs. 3 h and f reflect the thickening of DTs as 
the strain proceeded, which narrowed their interspacing accordingly. In 
general, the dynamic thickening of DTs may further hinder mobile 
dislocation by refining the dislocation mean free path, thus enhancing 
the strain-hardening capacity [ 34 ]. Additionally, nano-spaced SF net -
works were activated in the fractured 800-4A alloy, as shown in Fig. 3 i , 
the inserted close-up image illustrated the faulted arrangement of atoms. 
The mutual interactions of these SFs may also contribute to the sustained 
uniform deformation [ 35 ]. Consequently, a steady and continuous 
strain-hardening rate at Stage III is identified as a result of the growth of 
DTs and the onset of SF networks.

Fig. 5 a summarizes the deformation micro-mechanisms of the stud -
ied alloys, which indicates that the 800-4A alloy containing spacing- 
increased larger γ’’ precipitates is likely to develop DTs. Deformation 
twinning typically involves the dissociation of perfect dislocations into 
Shockley partial dislocations (i.e. twinning partials) [ 38 , 39 ]. The clas -
sical double-cross-slip mechanism of deformation twinning states that 
the resolved shear stress for twinning nucleation is influenced by the FR 
source size in addition to the SFE [ 26 ]. Here, the γ’’ precipitate spacing 
was considered as the FR source size since the twinning partials can be 
pinned by nanoscale γ’’ particles, as illustrated in Fig. 5 b . Calculations 
were conducted based on the present case to obtain a more quantitative 
understanding of how γ’’ precipitate spacing affect the nucleation of 
DTs. The critical shear stress to initiate the twinning partials can be 
expressed as [ 26 ]:
