1. Introduction
Ni-Co based superalloys are known for their exceptional creep 
resistance, ability to withstand long-term thermal stresses, and strong 
fatigue and oxidation resistance. These properties make them ideal for 
high-temperature applications such as aero-engines and gas turbines 
[1 – 3] . The high strength of nickel-based superalloys at elevated tem -
peratures is primarily attributed to the presence of elements like Cr, Mo, 
and W in the alloy matrix. These elements enhance the alloy ’ s strength 
by inhibiting dislocation slip through the solid solution strengthening 
mechanism [4,5] . In addition, the alloy matrix contains numerous γ ′ 
precipitates. The γ ′ phase forms a coherent interface with the γ matrix 
due to their minimal lattice mismatch [6,7] , which helps reduce stress 
concentrations and discontinuities, thereby minimizing the likelihood of 
crack formation under high-temperature conditions [8,9] . Dislocations 
that slip through the γ phase are obstructed by the γ’ phase, but the 
coherent interface mitigates the tendency to fracture compared to a 
non-coherent interface [10] . This strengthening mechanism ensures 
high-temperature plasticity and elongation while maintaining strength, 
thus enhancing the overall high-temperature performance of the alloy 
[11] . However, despite the γ’ phase effectively hindering dislocation slip 
and improving alloy strength, prolonged exposure to high temperatures 
can cause the γ ′ phase to coarsen or dissolve, leading to a reduction in 
material strength and weakening the overall performance of the alloy 
[12 – 14] . As the working temperature requirements of modern industry 
continue to rise, this presents a significant challenge to the heat resis -
tance and high-temperature strength of superalloys. Therefore, main -
taining the thermal strength of Ni-Co based superalloys has become a 
key focus in their ongoing development.
The conventional approach to enhancing the strength and ductility of 
superalloys involves the incorporation of high melting point solid so -
lution elements or γ ′ phase forming elements. However, the limited solid 
solubility of refractory elements within the nickel matrix often leads to 
the formation of localized supersaturated regions through dendritic 
segregation or grain boundary enrichment. At elevated temperatures, 
these elements tend to interact with Cr, Co, and other alloying

2.1. Feedstock preparation
The Ni-Co superalloy used in this experiment was supplied by 
Jiangsu Vilory Advanced Materials Technology Co., Ltd., with its stan -

2

placed in a rotary tube furnace and heated to 800

10

and dispersibility. The ball milling process is performed using a plane -
tary ball mill under the following operational parameters: zirconia

are mixed with nano-TiO
2

the jar capacity. The milling speed is set to 400 rpm, with alternating

milling duration of 3 h. The high-energy ball milling generates shear 
forces and impact energy that effectively break down nanoparticle ag -
glomerates. Finally, the resulting product is sieved through a 400-mesh 
screen to remove larger agglomerates. To further ensure uniform 
dispersion, the sieved powder is dispersed in anhydrous ethanol and 
subjected to ultrasonic treatment (using a probe-type ultrasonic ho -
mogenizer, model ZDY-001, 500 W, 35 kHz) for 10 min. The resulting 
dispersion is then centrifuged and dried to yield the final product [33,

4. Conclusions
In this work, nano-TiO
2 
reinforced Ni-Co based superalloys with 
varying nanoparticle contents were investigated. Through systematic 
microstructural characterization and mechanical testing, the following 
conclusions were drawn: 
(1) Increasing the content of nano-TiO
2 
effectively refined the grain 
structure, transforming the dendritic columnar grains into fine 
equiaxed grains and reducing dendritic segregation.
(2) The addition of nano-TiO
2 
promoted the intragranular precipi -
tation of the γ ′ phase and reduced its particle size. TEM analysis 
confirmed the in-situ formation of Al
2
TiO
5 
nanoparticles through 
the reaction between TiO ₂ and Al in the matrix.
(3) The addition of 0.2 wt% nano-TiO
2 
led to the optimal mechanical 
performance. At room temperature, the improvement in tensile 
strength and ductility was attributed to grain refinement, γ ′ phase 
strengthening, and dislocation impedance by Al
2
TiO
5 
nanoparticles.
(4) At 800
◦
C, the enhanced ductility was primarily ascribed to 
refined grain structure and the uniform distribution of nano- 
precipitates, which homogenized dislocation motion and sup -
pressed stress concentrations. Excessive nano-TiO
2 
(0.5 wt%) 
caused particle agglomeration and interfacial debonding, leading 
to deterioration in mechanical strength.
These findings suggest that nano-TiO
2 
is an effective reinforcing 
agent for optimizing the microstructure and enhancing both strength 
and ductility in Ni-Co based superalloys.
