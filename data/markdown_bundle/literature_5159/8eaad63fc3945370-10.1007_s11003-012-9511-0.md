It is shown that the electric conductivity of VT1- 0 and Fe–18Cr–14Ni powder a lloys varies within the 
range 6.3– 8.8⋅105 S/m and is 3–5 times higher than the conductiv ity of extensively used YSZ–Ni an-

Keywords: solid-oxide fuel cell, anode, high temperature, hydrogen, electric conductivity, strength. 
Solid-oxide fuel cells (SOFC) can be regarded as a promising and environmentally friendly energy source. 
Their operation is reduced to the dir ect transformation of the chemical energy into the electric energy, which 
guarantees their high efficiency (more than 40%) [1]. The solid-oxide fuel cells in which thin layers of electro-
lytes and the cathode are applied to a thick anode-substrat e deserve special attention. These SOFC can operate 
at temperatures lower than 800 °С, which enables one to reduce the cost of the cell by using less expensive mate-
rials for the production of their structural elements. 
The most extensively used material of substrate anod es is the yttria-stabilized zirconia ceramics modified 
with nickel oxide (YSZ–NiО). After reduction (YSZ–Ni) , this material has satisf actory electric and ionic con-
ductivities and a high catalytic activity [2]. However, it has a number of disadvantages, in particular, sensitivity 
to sulfur, susceptibility to plugging of the pores by deposited carbon in the case of application of hydrocarbon 
types of fuel, which decreases the productivity of the fuel cell [3–5]. Moreover, signi ficant amounts of nickel in 
the massive anode-substrate increase the cost of the SOFC. As one of the methods used to remove the indicated 
disadvantages, we can mention the application of less expe nsive catalytically active metals Mn, Fe, Co, Cu, etc. 
instead of nickel [6, 7]. In recent years, much attenti on has been given to combined anodes in which a thin func-
tional anode layer is applied to a porous metallic substrate [8–10]. The main requirements to the material of the 
substrate are as follows: high electric conductivity, satis factory strength in high-t emperature hydrogen-contain-
ing media, high resistance to sulfur -containing solutions, and low cost. 
The catalytic activity of iron is slightly lower than the catalytic activity of nickel [6]. Therefore, it is often 
used instead of Ni in the anodes of SOFC [11]. In combination with chromium, iron is characterized by a high 
resistance to the action of corrosion-active substances and a high electric conductivity. The Fe–Cr alloys pre-
pared by the method of powder metallurgy are regarded as promising materials for the gas-permeable anode 
layer of the SOFC [12].

Titanium can also be used as a material of the substr ate [13]. As iron, it is characterized by a high electric 
conductivity. Moreover, it is less expensive than Ni. Thus , porous materials based on Ti and Fe can be regarded 
as promising for the production of the anodes (substrates) for SOFC. 
The aim of the present work is to study the influen ce of hydrogen and high temper atures on the strength and 
durability of VT1–0 and Fe–18Cr–14Ni powder alloys. 
Materials and Experimental Procedure 
Specimens of VT1-0 and Fe–18 wt.% Cr–14 wt.% Ni powder alloys were prepared by the method of powder 
metallurgy. The sizes of structural elements of the VT1-0 material were recorded in a fairly broad range: paral-
lel with fine granules (30–100 μm in size), much larger granules (150–220 μm) were detected at some sites 
(Fig. 1a). In the Fe–18Cr– 14Ni material, we observed a much smaller spread of the sizes of granules (150–
250 μm) (Fig. 1b). 
To study the electric conductivity of th e materials, we used a four-poi nt scheme according to which two 
current leads were connected to the ends of a prismatic specimen 1.8 × 3 × 35 mm in size and two potential con-
tacts were connected in the middl e of the specimen at a distance L = 8 mm from each other. For a given 
strength of direct current I and the measured drop of potential V , the electric resistance of the specimen R 
was found from the Ohm law. The electric conductiv ity of a material is given by the formula

The fracture resistance under static and cyclic load s was found for the above-mentioned specimens by using

of the fracture surfaces was carried out by using a Zeiss EVO-40XVP scanning electron microscope.

Results of Investigations and Discussion 
The electric conductivity of VT1-0 and Fe–18Cr–14N i powder alloys varies within the range 6.3–
8.8⋅105 S/m and, hence, is 3–5 times higher than the elect ric conductivity of extensively used anode materials 
of the YSZ–NiO system in the reduced (working) state [14]. Note that, in the investigated materials, it remains 
constant after holding in air (and in hydrogen) for 3 h at 600 °С. This reveals the stability of electric contact be-
tween the granules in high-temperatu re oxidizing-reducing medi a. Thus, in terms of the electric conductivity, 
both the VT1-0 and Fe–18Cr–14Ni alloys are suitabl e for the production of substrates for the SOFC. 
The tests under static loading showed that the maximum values of bending strength σbend max for the Fe–
18Cr–14Ni and VT1-0 powder alloys at room temperature in air are close and vary within the range 270–
280 MPa (Fig. 2). However, the deflection of a specimen δbend max corresponding to σbend max for Fe–18Cr–
14N is three times larger than for VT1-0. This is e xplained by a larger number of slip systems in the face-
centered cubic lattice of the austenitic material, which f acilitates the motion of dislocations and promotes the 
realization of a more plastic dimple fracture mechanism by merging of the pores (Fig. 3a) as compared with the 
hexagonal close-packed titanium lattice which fails according to a brittler quasicleavage mechanism (Fig. 3b). 
At 20°С, the strength of the Fe–18Cr–14N i material in hydrogen increases to 330 MPa with an insignificant 
increase in δbend max (Fig. 2a). A similar influence of hydrogen on the strength characteristics was observed 
for other austenitic steels and, in pa rticular, for AISI 316 steel [15, 16]. It was shown that hydrogen facilitates 
the activation of new slip systems [17] with intense generation and motion of disloca tions under loading. As a 
result, the density of dislocations increases, which l eads to a decrease in their mobility and an increase in the 
deforming stresses. The additional hardening of the material is guaranteed by the formation of atmospheres of 
hydrogen atoms around dislocations [15]. 
In VT1-0 alloy, unlike the Fe–18Cr– 14Ni austenitic material, due to the small solubility of hydrogen in the 
hexagonal close-packed lattice of α -titanium, the process of deformation is accompanied by the formation and 
decomposition of hydrides [18, 19]. In this case, the fracture mechanism changes from more plastic quasicleav-
age in air (Fig. 3b) to brittle cleavage in hydrogen (Fig. 3c).

As the temperature increases to 600 °С in air, the fracture processes in materials are facilitated by the forma-
tion and cracking of oxide films. As a result, the fract ure surface becomes brittle with signs of secondary crack-
ing (Fig. 3d). Moreover, at temperatur es relatively high as compared with 20 °С, we observe the formation of 
favorable thermodynamic conditions for the facilitated moti on of dislocations, which leads to a decrease in the 
load required for the deformation and subsequent fracture of the materials (Fig. 2): for Fe–18Cr–14Ni, the value 
of σbend max becomes twice lower; for VT1-0, the value of this quantity becomes three times lower. 
At the high temperature, the character of the influence of hydrogen on the strength of materials remains un-
changed. Thus, the influence of hydrogen increases the value of σbend max by 17% for Fe–18Cr–14Ni as com-
pared with air but, on the contrary, decreases the value of this quantity for VT1-0 by 67%. The fracture of the 
Fe–18Cr–14Ni material in high-temperature hydrogen runs according to the ductile dimple mechanism (Fig. 3e). 
Unlike the case of room temperature at which hydrogen weakly affects the deflection of the specimens, at 
600°С, the value of deflection becomes twice larger for Fe –18Cr–14Ni alloy and three tim es larger for VT1-0 al-
loy (Fig. 2). A more significant infl uence of hydrogen on the strength and pl asticity characteristics of the VT1-0 
material at high temperatures may be expl ained by the formation of the more plastic β -phase [20]. 
Under cyclic loading, hydrogen increases the fatigue limit (on a base of N = 106 cycles) of the Fe–18Cr–
14Ni alloy at 20 and 600 °С (Fig. 4a) and, vice versa, decreases it for the VT1-0 alloy at 20 °С (Fig. 4b). In view 
of the low static strength of the titanium-based material (Fig. 2b) in the atmosphere of high-temperature hydro-
gen, which is much lower than the critical admissible va lue of strength for the SOFC with anode-substrate [21], 
we do not determine the fatigue characteristics under these conditions.

strength of the VT1-0 material in hydroge n and in air at a temperature of 20 °С varies within the range 230–
