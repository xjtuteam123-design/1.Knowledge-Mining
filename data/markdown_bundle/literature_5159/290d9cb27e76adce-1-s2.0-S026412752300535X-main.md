1. Introduction
With the development of aerospace ﬁeld, low density and high
service temperature gradually become necessary guidelines for
exploring remarkable high-temperature structural material [1].
Currently, nickel-based superalloys have been widely used in key
components of aero-engines and gas turbines due to their superb
high-temperature performance above 650 ℃ [2–4], but large den-
sity greatly limits their space for further improvement. Addition-
ally, intermetallic-based alloys, such as NiAl, Ni 3Al etc.,
possessing lower density and favourable high-temperature
strength, are assumed to be another potential high-temperature
structural material if severe room-temperature embrittlement
can be resolved [5,6]. Although adding trace boron (0.5 at.%) can
signiﬁcantly improve room-temperature plasticity of Ni 3Al (from
0 to 35%) [7], it only works under certain alloy composition and
has little effect on the upgrade of high-temperature performance
[8]. Therefore, it is urgent to ﬁnd a novel material which owns rel-
atively low density and excellent room-temperature ductility and
high-temperature yield strength simultaneously.
As one of the three breakthroughs in alloying theory in recent
decades, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have drawn considerable
attention since the concept was proposed due to their diverse orga-
nization and unique performance [9–13]. At the beginning of
study, HEA was deﬁned as single-phase solid solution alloy with
near/equal atomic ratio of composition [14]. Single-phase solid
solution HEAs exhibit favourable properties relative to conven-
tional metallic materials. Among them, face-centered cubic
(FCC)-type HEAs have been widely studied for excellent ductility
both at room and cryogenic temperature [15]. Besides, the density
of FCC-type single-phase solid solution HEAs is generally lower
than that of nickel-based superalloys, because it is composed of
transition metals with relatively light density and avoids the addi-
tion of refractory metals. However, the poor strength of FCC-type
single-phase solid solution HEAs restricts their further develop-
ment [16].
Secondary phase hardening plays a key role in achieving high
strength [17]. For instance, the strength of solid solution strength-
ened single-phase Ni
3Al alloys is much lower than that of dual-
phase nickel-based superalloys strengthened by coherent c0 phase
(L12-type ordered phase) [18]. More importantly, the elevated-
temperature yield strength of nickel-based superalloys is enhanced
with the increase of c’ phase [19]. Therefore, various secondary
phases are introduced into single-phase solid solution HEAs to
improve strength. Surprisingly, FCC-type HEAs achieved perfect
combination of strength and plasticity from liquid nitrogen tem-
perature (77 K) to room temperature (273 K) with the help of
L12-type nanoprecipitates [20–22], but their high-temperature
performance is still far from practical application [23]. It is under-
standable that deliberate pursuit of near/equal atomic ratio and
high entropy is bound to miss the best composition which can fully
exert strengthening effect of L1 2-type nanoprecipitates so that the
performance cannot meet desired requirement [24,25].
Inspired by above facts, a novel high-entropy intermetallic (HEI)
was proposed in 2017 [26,27]. HEI, also known as HEIC (high-
entropy intermetallic compound) [28–30] or MCI (multicompo-
nent intermetallic) [31], is usually consists of a major intermetallic
phase and small amounts of secondary intermetallic phase with
different structure or solid solution phase, and the volume fraction
of major intermetallic phase is not less than 75% [32]. HEI is
derived from multi-principal-element alloying of sublattice in the
long-range ordered crystalline structure, and the crystalline struc-
ture remains unchanged during alloying. Thus, HEI is essentially
classiﬁed as a novel intermetallic. Unlike traditional intermetallic
where each sublattice is occupied by single element, at least one
sublattice of HEI is randomly occupied by 3 (or more) kinds of sim-
ilar atoms in near/equal atomic ratio. Finally, the mixing entropy of
sublattice in HEI occupied by multiple elements is enhanced rela-
tive to that in conventional intermetallic occupied by single ele-
ment, and the formation process of HEI is denoted as ‘‘entropy
promotion of sublattice” [32]. On the one hand, HEI retains
multi-principal-element composition and high entropy effect
belonging to HEAs through ‘‘entropy promotion of sublattice”. It
endows HEI distinctive performance, which is difﬁcult to be
achieved by traditional intermetallic. On the other hand, HEI lights
up a new idea to solve the problem of poor performance at high
temperature resulted by insufﬁcient strengthening phase in HEAs.
To be speciﬁc, intermetallic phase as effective strengthening phase
of most HEAs evolves from secondary phase in HEAs to main phase
in HEI in terms of content [26], which provides the possibility for
HEI to exhibit better high-temperature performance than HEAs.
The ‘‘dual-phase core–shell” (DPCS) structure is an orderly
assembled microstructure. It can aggregate respective properties
of ‘‘core” phase and ‘‘shell” phase into one alloy to achieve ultimate
integration of multidimensional performance. To avoid room-
temperature embrittlement that commonly occurs in conventional
intermetallic [33], appropriate solid solution phase is necessary to
be introduced into HEI. The DPSC structure can effectively inte-
grate ‘‘hard phase” (intermetallic phase) and ‘‘soft phase” (solid
solution phase) into HEI, which is beneﬁcial to improving plasticity
while maintaining high strength [16]. The DPSC structure has been
found in many materials, such as nanocomposites [34]. ultraﬁne-
grained titanium alloys [35] and HEAs [36], etc. However, it has
not been found in HEI so far. In addition, HEI has attracted exten-
sive attention in the aspects of catalysis, magnetic, room-
temperature mechanical performance and high-temperature sta-
bility [29,30,37,38], but its high-temperature mechanical proper-
ties are rarely reported. Therefore, this paper takes exploring
high-temperature mechanical performance of HEI as main research
goal, and takes the formation of DPSC structure in HEI as main
design idea. The evolution of microstructure and phase constitute
is closely related to the alloy composition, thus investigating the
effect of element addition on microstructure and mechanical prop-
erties of HEI plays an important role in accomplishing above aims.
In this study, we designed three L1 2-type HEIs with different Co
addition to investigate the evolution of DPCS structure and
mechanical properties. First of all, L1 2-type long-range ordered
crystalline structure with A 3B structural formula was selected for
multi-principal-element alloying of sublattice due to higher sym-
metry and more slip systems [39]. Then, Ni and Al elements were
chosen to occupy A site (face-centered position) and B site (cube-
corner position), respectively, according to reported fantastic

The Co element was investigated based following two reasons:
(1) The solid solution phase is requisite for HEI to form DPSC struc-
ture and improve ductility, and Co element is the only one that
promotes the formation of solid solution phase except for Ni ele-
ment; (2) The price of Co element is higher than the other four ele-
ments, thus precise control of Co content can reduce production
cost. The as-cast microstructure and mechanical properties of three
L1
2-type HEIs were carefully examined using SEM, XRD, TEM, elec-
tronic universal material testing machine. The effect of Co addition
on microstructure evolution and mechanical performance develop-
ment of L1 2-type HEI was explored, and the strengthening mecha-
nism and plasticizing mechanism of the L1 2-type HEI with distinct
DPCS structure were systematically analyzed based on microstruc-
ture and deformation micro-mechanism. This work not only clari-
ﬁes the relationship between alloy composition, microstructure
and mechanical properties of L1 2-type HEI, but also provides a
new idea for the exploitation of lightweight high-temperature
structural material.
2. Experimental
The button ingots (120 g) with designed nominal composition
were prepared by vacuum arc-melting furnace under the protec-
tion of a high-purity Ar atmosphere, and detailed chemical compo-
sitions are shown in Table 1 . For convenience, the three L1 2-type
HEI are denoted as Co0, Co0.5, and Co1, respectively, according
to the ration of Co element to Ni element. The purity of all raw
materials is over 99.99 % (wt%), and all ingots were re-melted ﬁve
times to ensure the compositional homogeneity. Due to the low
melting point of Al, it is easy to volatilize during the smelting pro-
cess, and an appropriate amount of compensation for burning loss
should be considered. Besides, the trace amount of oxygen remain-
ing in the furnace atmosphere is absorbed by melting high-purity
titanium ingot. Homogenization annealing was conducted at
1050 ℃ for 36 h to eliminate residual stress in ingot and reduce
composition and microstructure segregation.
Phase identiﬁcation was performed by X-ray diffractometer
(XRD, Empyrean) using Cu K a radiation at the test angles of 20-
100/C176. The XRD patterns were acquired using working voltage of
20 KV and working current of 20 mA, and the step size and step
time was 2 /C176and 0.5 s, respectively. Scanning electron microscopy
(SEM, Quanta 200FEG) equipped with energy-dispersive (EDS)
detector was employed to observe the microstructure and fracture
morphology of the tensile fractured samples. To carry out SEM
observation, the specimens were mechanically polished from
400-grit to 2000-grit SiC paper to eliminate scratches. Then, the
SEM specimens were electro-polished in a mixed solution of 93%
C2H5OH + 7% HClO 4 at 30 V for 20 s and etched using C 2H5OH
(42 ml) + H 2SO4 (34 ml) + HCL (24 ml) mixed solution for 15–
20 s. The microstructure was characterized using transmission
electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Talos F200X) equipped with an
EDS detector. The preparation process of TEM samples is as fol-
lows: Firstly, the samples were ground to 50 lm using 2000-grit
SiC paper. Then, the samples were stamped into U 3 mm disc,
and ion-milling (Gatan 695) was carried out using 90% CH3OH + 10%
HClO4 mixed solution at work voltage of 15 V and work tempera-
ture of 25 ℃. Electronic universal material testing machine
(Instron-5569) was used to conduct room-temperature compres-
sion test with strain rate of 1 /C2 10-3 s/C0 1. The samples for compres-
sive tests were cylindrical in shape and U 4 /C2 6 mm in size, and
experiments were repeated 3 times for each composition to reduce
errors. Electronic universal tensile testing machine (Instron-5569)
and Shimadzu electronic universal testing machine (AG-X plus)
were used for room-temperature and high-temperature tensile
tests, respectively, and the tensile strain rate was 1 /C2 10-3 s/C0 1.
The dog-bone-shaped tensile specimens (gauge length 15 mm,
width 5 mm and thickness 2 mm) were prepared by electro-
discharge machining. The hardness values of alloys were measured
using a Vickers hardness tester (HV-1000IS) under a load of 250 g
applied for 10 s. For microhardness measurements of precipitate
phase, 100 g load was applied. An average value of ﬁve different
points on each sample was obtained.

The crystalline structure of precipitate phase located at grain
boundary was further conﬁrmed by TEM analysis. Fig. 3 c shows
the morphology image of the irregularly-shaped precipitate phase,
concomitant with the efforts of corresponding SAD pattern and
EDS maps. The SAD pattern collected from the zone axis paralleled
to (0,0,1) demonstrates FCC-type crystalline structure, which cor-
responds to the crystalline structure of the unknown phase in
XRD analysis. Moreover, the composition analysis using EDS indi-
cates that Si and Ti atoms have a strong tendency to allocate into
it, whereas Al atoms are largely depleted, consistent with the
chemical composition of grain-boundary precipitate phase deter-

crystalline structure, the grain-boundary precipitate phase is spec-

G phase is a kind of Si-rich intermetallic with FCC crystalline struc-
ture, which is always found in Si-rich alloys [45]. The main reason
of the formation of G phase is the extremely negative enthalpy of
mixing between Si element and Ni, Co, Ti elements, thus they pre-
fer to segregate at grain boundary and form stable intermetallic
with Si element [46].

greatly improves the overall hardness. A further increase in the
Co content results in a signiﬁcant decrease in grain interior hard-
ness (about 306 ± 15 HV), thus the overall hardness of the Co1 alloy
decreases (about 384 ± 12 HV) although the hardness of precipitate
phase remains unchanged (about 518 ± 12 HV). The decrease of
grain interior hardness in Co1 alloy is mainly due to the formation

4. Discussion
4.1. Effect of Co content on the formation of DPCS structure inside
grain and precipitate phase at grain boundary
The main effect of Co addition on as-cast L1 2-type HEIs is the
formation of distinct DPCS structure inside grain and precipitate
phase located at grain boundary. According to the results of
microstructural analysis, slight addition of Co element will lead
to the appearance of Si, Ti-rich precipitate phase at grain boundary,
and the volume fraction of precipitate phase decreases slightly
with the increase of Co content. However, slight addition of Co ele-
ment will not lead to the formation of DPCS structure, this distinct
microstructure will appear only when the Co addition reaches a
critical value that FCC secondary could separate out. Based on
the microstructure characteristic and chemical composition, the
schematic diagram of the formation mechanism of DPCS structure
and precipitate phase with the increase of Co content in as-cast
L12-type HEIs was depicted. As shown in Fig. 7, the essential reason
resulting microstructure evolution is the segregation of elements
caused by the addition of Co element. For Co0 alloy, only two c’-
forming elements Si and Ti are adopted, which has strong tendency
to occupy the Al sublattice. Combined with the designed nominal
chemical composition, an equilibrium atomic molar ratio of Ni:A
l + Si + Ti = 3:1 was acquired inside the grain without any compo-
sition cluster. Therefore, single L1 2-type ordered phase with Ni 3(Al,
Si,Ti) structural formula was formed in Co0 alloy. With the addition
of a small number of c-forming element Co, the content of Ni
atoms was reduced and partially replaced by Co atoms in Co0.5
alloy. At the same time, the contents of Si and Ti atoms remained
unchanged in grain interior. Although Co atoms have the ability
to enter Ni sublattice and replace Ni atoms to form L1
2-type
ordered structure with Si and Ti atoms, the enthalpy of mixing
between Ni element and them are smaller than Co element with
them (Table 5). So the Si and Ti atoms preferentially combine with
Ni atoms to form stable chemical bonds. In this case, the atomic
molar ratio within grain transformed into Ni:Al + Si + Ti less
than 3:1 due to the reduction of Ni atoms. Therefore, extra Si and
Ti atoms are expelled to grain boundary to form Si, Ti-rich precip-
itate phase. As for adopted Co atoms, some segregate at grain
boundary and form precipitate phase with Si and Ti atoms, and
the other stay inside grain to maintain equilibrium atomic molar
ratio of Ni + Co:Al + Si + Ti = 3:1. Therefore, L1 2-type ordered phase
with (Ni,Co)3(Al,Si,Ti) structural formula was formed in grain inte-
rior of Co0.5 alloy. With the further addition of Co element in Co1
alloy, fewer Ni element was remained in grain interior, and fewer
Si, Ti atoms were needed inside grain to form ordered L1 2-type
structure. Therefore, more Si and Ti atoms were expelled to grain

4.2. Strengthening mechanism and plasticizing mechanism dominated
by DPCS structure
4.2.1. Evolution of room-temperature mechanical properties with Co
content
The room-temperature mechanical properties of as-cast L1 2-
type HEI are signiﬁcantly affected by the Co content from compres-
sive engineering stress–strain curves. It is clearly seen that single-
phase Co0 alloy has higher yield strength but the lowest compres-
sive strength and fracture strain. Given the characteristic of HEI,
the good strength along with poor ductility of Co0 alloy is mainly
attribute to the nature of intermetallic with long-range ordered
structure. As well known, the ionic bond and covalent bond
between different sublattice are prevalent in intermetallic, which
results in the hard and brittle nature of intermetallic. As a novel
intermetallic, HEI inevitably has poor plasticity at room tempera-
ture, and premature fracture is bound to cause low compressive
strength. Moreover, distinct grain boundary observed in Co0 alloy
is commonly regarded as a special defect, which can lead to severe
intergranular fracture and deteriorate mechanical performance.
With the increase of Co content, a signiﬁcant improvement of both
strength and ductility was achieved in Co0.5 alloy, which is related
to the microstructure that obvious grain boundary was replaced by
precipitate phase. On the one hand, the harder precipitate phase is

4.2.2. Strengthening mechanism and plasticizing mechanism of Co1
alloy with distinct DPCS structure
From the results of room-temperature compressive testing, the
Co1 alloy achieved the highest compressive strength and fracture
strain simultaneously. At the same time, additional tensile testing
conducted on Co1 alloy indicates the better plasticity than conven-
tional intermetallics at room temperature. More importantly, the
comparison of compressive performance between Co1 alloy and
other as-cast HEAs ( Table 6 ) reﬂects that Co1 alloy possesses the
best comprehensive room-temperature mechanical properties.
Therefore, Co1 alloy shows high potential for application in struc-
tural material and it is necessary to study the strengthening mech-
anism and plasticizing mechanism of Co1 alloy with distinct DPCS
structure.

To uncover the origin of particular anomalous yield effect and
outstanding elevated-temperature yield strength of as-cast Co1
alloy with DPCS structure, detailed TEM characterization was con-
ducted on Co1 alloy with different tensile strain at 450 ℃. It can be
found from Fig. 9 that plastic deformation at room temperature
was dominated by dislocations, and the density of dislocation
became larger with the increase of tensile strain. At the beginning
of room-temperature tensile test, a few of dislocations generated
and accumulated around L1
2-core ( Fig. 9 a). As tensile test pro-
ceeds, some dislocations sheared L1 2-core due to the large stress,
and some dislocations were blocked in the FCC-shell by L1 2-core,
resulting in stress concentration ( Fig. 9 b and c). Different from
the deformation mechanism at room temperature, stacking faults
(SFs) prevailed during the deformation at high temperature (450
℃) in as-cast Co1 alloy. In general, as the strain increases, the num-
ber of SFs became larger and the interaction between SFs in differ-
ent directions became more complicated. It can be seen from
Fig. 10 a that several parallel-structured SFs are embedded in the
form of bundles in Co1 alloy at the beginning of tensile test at
450 ℃. These parallel SFs can shear the L1 2-core by forming the
complex SFs (CSFs), which is common in L1 2 structured alloy
[73–75]. The high-energy CSFs produce strong interaction with
L12-core, thus is beneﬁcial for maintaining stable work-
hardening capacity and improving yield strength [76]. As strain
proceeds to near fracture, more SFs were triggered in Co1 alloy.
It can be seen from Fig. 10b that SFs distributed along other direc-
tion appear, and the mean spacing between adjacent SFs was
reduced. More importantly, the SFs in two directions intersect sig-
niﬁcantly, producing SF networks as shown in Fig. 10 c. The gener-
ation of nano-spaced SF networks can prevent dislocations moving
by decreasing the mean free path of dislocations, resulting
dynamic Hall-Petch effect to improve mechanical properties
[77,78]. Fig. 10 d gives a close-up view of the nano-scale SFs
observed at a tensile strain of 2% and the according FFT pattern
in inset proves the existence of SFs during deformation in as-cast
Co1 alloy at 450 ℃.

erally known as immobile Lomer-Cottrell locks (L-C locks) [79,80].

the anisotropy in the APB energy [71]. In this case, both of the par-

and high temperature, especially the anomalous yield effect, which
makes it a potential competitor for advanced high-temperature
structural material. It should be noted that some studies believe
that the addition of sufﬁcient B element is necessary condition

by B element in other alloys has limited contribution to strength

5. Conclusions
In this study, a novel L1 2-type HEI with distinct DPCS was suc-
cessfully developed by controlling the addition of Co element, and
the effect of Co content on the microstructure and mechanical
properties were carefully studied. The strengthening mechanism
and plasticizing mechanism dominated by DPCS structure were
obtained, and the anomalous yield effect was investigated based
on the deformation micro-mechanism. The main conclusions are
follows:
(1) The Co addition has a signiﬁcant effect on the microstructure
evolution of three as-cast L1 2-type HEIs. The Co0 alloy
formed single-phase L1 2-type HEI with a structural formula
of Ni3(Al,Si,Ti). With the increase of Co content, the Si,Ti-rich
precipitate phase named ‘‘G phase” with a volume fraction of
12% was formed at grain boundary in Co0.5 alloy except for
L12-type ordered phase with a structural formula of (Ni,
Co)3(Al,Si,Ti). With the further increase of Co content, the
distinct DPCS structure composed of 85% L1 2-core and 15%
FCC-shell was formed within grain of Co1 alloy, and the Si,
Ti-rich precipitate phase with a volume fraction of 8% was
also found at grain boundary of Co1 alloy.
(2) The Co addition has a signiﬁcant effect on the room-
temperature mechanical properties of three as-cast L1 2-
type HEIs. With the increase of Co content, the compressive
yield strength ﬁrst increases and then decreases, consistent
with the trend of overall hardness. Both of compressive
strength and fracture strain increase obviously with the
enhancement of Co content, and the Co1 alloy with DPCS
structure achieved the best combination of strength and
ductility at room temperature.
(3) The better room-temperature mechanical performance of
as-cast Co1 alloy is mainly ascribed to the distinct DPCS
structure, which is greatly facilitated by the element segre-
gation resulting from the addition of Co. The disordered
FCC-shell with lower SFE is easy to across, improving ductil-
ity and uniform deformation capacity at room temperature.
The ordered L1
2-core with high volume fraction and APB
energy hinders the movement of dislocation, increasing
strength both at room and high temperature. Therefore,
the Co1 alloy owns the best comprehensive properties
among three as-cast L1 2-type HEIs.
(4) The deformation at elevated temperature is mainly domi-
nated by SFs. The anomalous yield effect was discovered in
as-cast Co1 alloy with DPCS structure due to the formation
of L-C locks, which is promoted by the interaction between
non-coplanar SFs. The strong pinning effect of both K-W
Locks and L-C Locks on dislocation movement contributes
to the anomalous enhancement of yield strength from room
temperature to about 650 ℃. The outstanding elevated-
temperature yield strength of as-cast Co1 alloy comes from
the cooperative effort of dynamic Hall-Petch effect and
anomalous yield effect.
