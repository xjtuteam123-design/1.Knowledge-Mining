Isothermal Oxidation Comparison of Three Ni-Based
Superalloys
H.T. Mallikarjuna, N.L. Richards, and W.F. Caley
(Submitted October 26, 2016; in revised form January 17, 2017)
Ni-based superalloys are used for high-temperature components of gas turbines in both industrial and
aerospace applications due to their ability to maintain dimensional stability under conditions of high stress
and strain. The oxidation resistance of these alloys often dictates their service lifetime. This study focuses on
the isothermal oxidation behavior of three Ni-based superalloys, namely, polycrystalline cast IN738LC,
single-crystal N5, and a ternary Ni-Fe-Cr (TAS) powder metallurgy alloy. The isothermal oxidation tests
were conducted at 900 /C176C in the static air up to 1000 h, and the speciﬁc aspects studied were the oxidation
behavior of these chromia-forming and alumina-forming alloys that are used extensively in industry. In
particular, the behavior of oxide scale growth and subsurface changes were analyzed in detail using various
techniques such as SEM, EDS, and AFM. From the isothermal oxidation kinetics, the oxidation rate
constant, kp, was calculated for each alloy and found to be; kp = 2.79 3 1026 mg2 cm24 s21 for IN738LC,
kp = 1.42 3 1027 mg2 cm24 s21 for N5 and kp = 1.62 3 1027 mg2 cm24 s21 for TAS. Based on a
microstructural analysis, IN738LC exhibited a continuous dense outer scale of Cr 2O3 and discontinuous
inner scale of Al 2O3, whereas N5 and TAS showed a dense outer scale of Al 2O3 alone. The results suggested
that the N5 and PM-TAS alloys are more oxidation resistant than the IN738LC under these conditions.
Keywords microstructure, oxidation, single-crystal, superalloys,
transient oxidation
1. Introduction
The thermodynamic efﬁciency of an aircraft gas turbine
engine depends on the temperature and pressure inside the
engine. For example, a higher inlet temperature and pressure in
the combustion chamber will result in a higher efﬁciency.
However, the tolerance to the turbine entry temperature is based
on the material used in the combustion chamber and in blading
for the turbine section. The material must have good mechan-
ical properties such as fatigue and creep resistance under
temperatures range of 750-1100 /C176 C (Ref 1). Since the material
is exposed to high-temperature exhaust gasses, it should also
possess better oxidation and corrosion resistance (Ref 2, 3).
Ni-based superalloys ﬁnd extensive use for high-tempera-
ture components of gas turbines due to their excellent
mechanical, thermal, and surface stability as well as oxidation
resistance at high temperatures (Ref 4). The best and optimum
performance of the Ni-based superalloys can be adjusted by
adding alloying elements to the material depending on their
application requirement (Ref 5). The Ni-based superalloys,
which are used in the high-temperature zones of a gas turbine
engines, always contain Al and Cr along with Ti, Ta, W, and
Mo. The oxidation of reactive elements such as Al and Cr
forms dense and continuous protective oxide scales of alumina
(Al2O3) and chromia (Cr 2O3), thereby preventing further
removal of the material by separating material and the oxidizing
environment.
Over time, several changes have been made in the manufac-
turing of Ni-based superalloys to improve their chemical
composition and microstructure (Ref 6). In view of the increased
operating conditions over time, developments in materials
processing beyond conventional cast alloys occurred and pro-
cesses such as powder metallurgy and single-crystal technologies
were developed (Ref 7). The fatigue life of single-crystal Ni-
based superalloys is much superior to that of polycrystalline Ni-
based superalloys due to the absence of grain boundaries. The
main disadvantage of powder metallurgy process compared to
other processes is the preserved residual porosity and ﬁne grain
size. The porosity can increase the oxidation rate and may also
have a negative effect on the mechanical properties of the
material such as creep and fatigue (Ref 8).
In the present study, isothermal oxidation tests at high
temperature was undertaken to understand and compare the
kinetics and behavior of the oxide scale in polycrystalline,
single-crystal, and powder metallurgy superalloys used by
major engine suppliers.

2.2 Methodology
The aging heat-treated materials of N5 and IN738LC as well
as PM-TAS were subjected to EDM to produce rectangular
specimens, dimensions of 12 9 10 9 3 mm. The specimens
were ground on silicon carbide papers up to 600 grits and
ultrasonically cleaned in acetone to remove the debris and to
degrease. The polished specimens of IN738LC, N5, and PM-
TAS were checked for dimensions, pre-weighed, placed in
glazed porcelain crucibles and isothermally oxidized in a box
furnace at 900 /C176 C for various times up to 1000 h in static air.

±0.0001 g. Then, the oxidation rate was determined as mass

superalloys at 900 /C176 C followed parabolic oxidation behavior.

diffusion of the reactive species (anions and/or cations) through
the external scale and/or subjected alloy because diffusion-
controlled high-temperature oxidation processes are governed
by the parabolic rate law (Ref 10, 13).
Since no grain boundary oxidation is visible in the IN738LC
and PM-TAS alloys, the oxidation behavior noted in the present
study for all three nickel based superalloys is assumed to be a

According to Giggins and Pettit (Ref 16), the transient stage
appears due to the formation of metastable alumina phases c
and h-Al2O3 at the early stages (1-100 h) during oxidation of
Ni-Cr-Al alloys at 1000 /C176 C, and in the later stages, these phases
will transform to a-Al2O3 which leads to the parabolic behavior
of oxidation. A similar behavior was observed in both single-
crystal N5 and PM-TAS alloys at 900 /C176 C. Since the formation
and growth of the alumina scale is rapid and displacement
reaction processes are reported to be very slow (Ref 13), no
evidence of a displacement reaction was noted nor would be
expected.
From the analysis of the mass change as a function of
oxidation duration experienced by N5 and PM-TAS alloys,
transient oxidation behavior was noted at smaller exposure
duration followed by a steady-state oxidation stage. From
Fig. 4, the N5 specimen showed a high transient stage growth
rate of 1.32 9 10
/C0 06 mg2 cm/C0 4 s/C0 1 for short periods up to
54,000 s, whereas the PM-TAS specimen had a lower
transient stage growth rate of 5.53 9 10/C0 07 mg2 cm/C0 4 s/C0 1 up
to 115,200 s. On the other hand, IN738LC alloys showed
steady oxidation from the beginning of the oxidation due to
the formation of continuous, thick and dense chromia scale on
the surface. The transient oxidation stage represent the
concurrent oxidation of all major alloying elements present
on the alloy surface such as NiO, Cr
2O3,A l 2O3,W 2O5,T a2O5
and complex spinels even though one oxide is usually greatly
favored from a thermodynamic standpoint (Ref 17). In this
study, the EDS analysis of N5 and PM-TAS surfaces at the
end of their transient stages revealed the discrete NiO, Al
2O3
and possible complex spinel (NiCr 2O4) formation on the
surface. In addition to these oxides, PM-TAS alloy also
showed SiO 2 on the surface. The transient stage proceeds until
the initial rapid kinetics have transitioned to a lower rate and
the partial pressure of oxygen at the scale/alloy interface has
been lowered, both of which favor the formation of a
protective layer of the most stable oxides; alumina and
chromia.

4.2 Microstructure of Oxide Scale
The cross-sectional SEM microstructures of 3-h oxidized
specimens of all three Ni-based superalloys revealed no distinct
oxide layer, which is shown in Fig. 4. An inhomogeneous
(discrete) oxide layer formation on the surface of the specimens
and the oxide layer thickness being less than the SEM resolution
may be the reasons for these ﬁndings. However, the surface
analysis by EDS showed the patchy oxide layers of NiO, Cr
2O3,
Al2O3, TiO2, and spinel on all three Ni-based superalloys.
Examination of the surface morphologies of the oxides
formed on IN738LC, N5, and PM-TAS in the later stages by SEM
revealed that the scales were composed of a thick external oxide
scale and discontinuous subscale of oxides for IN738LC and a
thin monolayer in the case of N5 and PM-TAS at 900 /C176 C in static
air. The chromia-forming alloy IN738LC showed internal
discontinuous oxide layer because Ti and Al are thermodynam-
ically more stable than Cr, thus TiO
2 and/or Al2O3 could develop
below the external chromia scale. The partial pressure of oxygen
decreases from the scale/alloy interface into the alloy matrix, thus
internal oxidation generally occurs according to their relative
thermodynamic stability, from TiO
2 and then Al 2O3 to alloy
matrix. The internal oxides formed in the current study obeyed
such a sequence. From Fig. 6, an IN738LC alloy showed
relatively large internal oxidation layers of Ni (Cr,Ti) 2O4,T i O2,
Al2O3, and TiTaO4. As well, the Al 2O3 scale penetration depth
was much larger than the external scale.
The N5 scales exhibit discontinuous voids along the scale/alloy
interface as shown in Fig. 10, which eventually may lead to the
scale spallation. The voids we re expected to become less dis-
cernible with the growth of a denser scale at higher exposure time.
However, the present study showedthat as the oxide scale thickness
increased the average size of the voids also increased from 1 to
2.5 lm. The void formation could have been due to the presence of
6 wt.% tantalum inhomogeneously in the matrix will oxidize before
aluminum to form Ta
2O5 (Ref 18, 19). Once the Ta 2O5 oxide
formed, it will not adhere to the substrate and this may lead to void
formation. In the present study, we can see both mechanisms in
place. The presence of tantalum-rich layer along the alloy (PFZ)/
scale interface and above the void is shown in Fig. 11 agrees with
the studies done by other researchers (Ref18, 19).
The scale kinetics data obtained for the PM-TAS alloy also
support the assumption that the oxide scale growth was
controlled by migration of oxygen ions through the Al
2O3 scale.
With the increase in the scale thickness, potential diffusion paths
for anions (oxygen) and/or cations (aluminum) through the oxide
scale being decreased, accordingly, the growth rate of the oxide
scale also decreased at higher exposure times (Ref 20). The
addition of 0.5 wt.% of Si showed that a transient stage occurred
at about 32 h and followed by the steady-state oxidation behavior

4.3 Effect of alloying Elements
4.3.1 Tantalum. The addition of tantalum to Ni-based
superalloys promotes the selective oxidation of aluminum at high
temperature by third element effect (Ref10, 21, 22). For instance,
the binary alloy Ni-Al shows Type-I oxidation behavior with an
external NiO scale and internal precipitates of alumina in the
subsurface zone. The addition of an alloying element such as
chromium ( P
O2 =1 9 10/C0 25 bar at 900 /C176 C) to a Ni-Al based
alloy can form an oxide layer at lower dissociation pressure
compared to NiO ( P
O2 =8 9 10/C0 12 bar at 900 /C176 C) but higher
than that of alumina ( PO2 =1 9 10/C0 37 bar at 900 /C176 C). This may
result in a decrease in the solubility of oxygen in the scale/sub-
strate interface as well as in the subsurface zone. Hence, the
tendency for internal oxidation of aluminum reduces, thereby
promoting the external alumina scale formation. Similarly, the
dissociation pressure of Ta
2O5 (PO2 =6 9 10/C0 30 bar at 900 /C176 C)
is signiﬁcantly lower than that of Cr 2O3. Therefore, the addition
of tantalum to the Ni-Cr-Al alloy may decrease the oxygen
activity in the solid solution matrix during oxidation and may
encourage the alumina scale formation on the surface of the alloy.
Although the addition of Ta increases the oxidation resistance of
Ni-based superalloys, the present study on the single-crystal N5
superalloy showed defects such as voids at the scale/substrate
interface. To understand the effect of Ta on the oxidation behavior
of alumina-forming Ni-based superalloys, the alloys with and
without Ta have been compared. The single-crystal N5 with
6.4 wt.% Ta revealed voids due to the formation of Ta
2O5 at the
scale/alloy interface, whereas the PM-TAS without any Ta had no
void formation. The oxidation resistance also depends on the
amount of Ta present in the matrix. Increasing the concentration
of Ta more than 3% leads to the non-adhesive Ta
2O5 formation
which could result in spallation of the oxide scale (Ref 19). In
addition, the formation of Al 2O3 is also less favorable in the
presence of higher tantalum concentration.
4.3.2 Silicon. Silica is a thermodynamically more
stable oxide than chromia and if present could form under a
chromia scale during oxidation (Ref 23). The three Ni-based
superalloys had Si; 0.04% with IN738LC, 0.12 wt.% with N5,
and 0.5 wt.% with PM-TAS. However, from cross-sectional
SEM images shown in Fig. 4 all three alloys did not show any
Si-rich layer or precipitates at the scale/alloy interface other
than the possible presence of minor Si in TAS. The combina-
tion of Si-rich oxide phases at the alumina scale/alloy interface
in PM-TAS alloys may reduce the inward diffusion of oxygen
ions and outward diffusion of aluminum, thus lowering the
scale growth rate. Although the silicon is known to promote the
thick chromia scale formation, there has been no evidence in
the present study. The distinct SiO
2 layer formation is decided
by the silicon quantity present in the alloy. It has been reported
that the formation of a SiO
2 layer at the scale/alloy interface can
increase the extent of scale spallation (Ref 24-26). However, no
evidence of this phenomenon was found in this research.
5. Conclusions
An isothermal air oxidation study has been conducted at
900 /C176 C for times up to 1000 h, on the Ni-based alloys
IN738LC, N5, and PM-TAS.
Both the oxidation kinetics and formation of the oxidation
layer of all three alloys were determined to follow the parabolic
rate law.
The magnitude of the k
p values, for N5, 1.42 9 10/C0 7
mg2 cm/C0 4 s/C0 1 and for TAS, 1.64 9 10/C0 7 mg2 cm/C0 4 s/C0 1 were
much lower than the IN738LC, 2.79 9 10/C0 6 mg2 cm/C0 4 s/C0 1.
Hence, N5 and TAS (Ni-Cr-Fe) alloys are more oxidation
resistant than the IN738LC superalloy under these conditions.
The SEM-EDS analysis conﬁrmed that the oxide scale
formed on IN738LC was composed of a continuous external
Cr2O3, with adjacent spinel (Ni(Cr,Ti) 2O4) layer and a discon-
tinuous internal Al 2O3 scale in the subsurface region proving
Type-II oxidation behavior. However, both N5 and PM-TAS
alloys showed Al
2O3-monolayer on the surface suggesting
Type-III oxidation behavior.
The void formation at the scale/alloy interface due to the
tantalum oxide did not lead to spallation of the oxide layer in
the single-crystal N5 superalloy.
