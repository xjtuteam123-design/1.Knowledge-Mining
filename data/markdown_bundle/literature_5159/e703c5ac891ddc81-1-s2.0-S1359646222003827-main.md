ABSTRACT 
Knowledge associated with the alloying effects of Al, Ti, Ta and Nb on phase stability of Ni-Co-based superalloys 
plays a significant role in efficiently exploring the multi-component space for future alloy designs, and thus were 
systematically investigated using a high-throughput dual-anneal multi-component diffusion multiple (MCDM) 
approach. In this work, η phase presenting considerably different morphology and spatial distribution was 
confirmed in the Ta-rich and Nb-rich alloys, and its zero-phase-fraction (ZPF) line at 800 
◦
C was determined that 
suggests a maximum Nb + Ta content of about 2.5 at.% while avoiding the η formation. The composition cri -
terion regarding the stability of η was updated by taking into account the destabilizing effect of Co such that the η 
formation can be significantly suppressed with increasing Co from a critical level of about 12 at.%, providing 
essential guidance to design alloy chemistry for superior microstructural stability at high-temperature service 
conditions. 
Ni-based superalloys are the most commonly used structural mate -
rials for high-temperature applications such as aeroengines and indus -
trial gas turbines, where comprehensive properties of superior strength, 
and significant resistance to creep, fatigue, corrosion and oxidation are 
required [1 – 3] . Over the past few decades, various advanced superal -
loys, such as RR1000 [4] and ME3 [5] , have been developed for turbine 
disks. Due to the high volume fraction of γ
′
phase and the segregation 
problems introduced by elevated levels of refractory elements, most of 
the disk alloys are fabricated by the relatively expensive powder met -
allurgy (P/M) processing route [ 6 , 7 ]. Recently, a series of Ni-Co-based 
superalloys (designated as TMW alloys) were designed for turbine 
disks by combining the features of two types of γ / γ
′
alloys, i.e., Ni-based 
and Co-based superalloys [8 – 10] . Compared to commercial P/M disk 
superalloys, TMW alloys contain higher Co and Ti contents ranging from 
about 21.8 to 31.1 wt.% and 5.4 to 7.4 wt.%, respectively. Fabricated by 
the normal cost-effective cast and wrought (C & W) process route, some 
of those TMW Ni-Co-based alloys were demonstrated to have superior 
phase stability and excellent high-temperature mechanical properties 
that are competitive to the advanced RR1000 and ME3 P/M alloys. 
For further improvement in the performance of disk superalloys, it is 
critical to control compositions for optimal microstructure. It is well 
recognized that modern Ni-based superalloys commonly contain more 
than ten discrete alloying elements [ 2 , 11 ]. Among them, Al is the pri -
mary γ
′
-Ni
3
Al former for precipitation strengthening, while Ti, Nb and 
Ta partition preferentially towards γ
′
phase by substituting onto the Al 
sublattice. At relatively low levels, Ti, Ta and Nb contribute to ensure an 
appropriate γ
′
volume fraction, increase the γ
′
anti-phase boundary 
(APB) energy and greatly strengthen the alloy [12 – 14] . Once the solu -
bility limits are approached, precipitates displaying platelet or 
needle-like morphology, typical for η phase with a D0
24 
hexagonal 
structure and δ phase with a D0
a 
orthorhombic structure [15 – 18] , will 
generate in the matrix and are ordinarily considered deleterious to the 
microstructure stability and mechanical properties of the alloys when 
excessive precipitation occurs.

A recent work by Antonov et al. [16] proposed empirical composi -
tion criteria to predict the tendency of a specific alloy to precipitate η 
and/or δ phases based on various commercial and experimental 
Ni-based superalloys. The atomic ratios of Al/(Nb + Ta + Ti) and (Nb +
Ta)/(Al + Ti) were demonstrated to be closely related to the formation 
of δ and/or η phases. With an Al/(Ti + Nb + Ta) ratio of greater than 
0.85, the alloy will exhibit a γ + γ
′
microstructure with no δ or η pre -
cipitates appear; otherwise, when this ratio is less than 0.85, the alloy 
tends to form δ and/or η precipitates, and the tendency of which phase 
would be preferentially promoted is influenced by the (Nb + Ta)/(Al +
Ti) ratio. More recently, other alloying elements, such as Cr, Co, Fe, were 
also reported to have a considerable effect on the stability of η and δ 
phases, and they were suggested to be taken into account along with Al, 
Nb, Ta and Ti to improve the reliability of the composition criteria [ 17 , 
19 ]. 
The effect of Al, Ti, Nb and Ta on the phase stability of Ni-based 
superalloys with higher Co concentrations is of particular interest to 
the present study, with the purpose of designing advanced disk super -
alloys with better temperature capability. Given the composition 
complexity of modern Ni-based superalloys, the conventional one- 
sample-at-a-time approach is too time-consuming and is insufficient to 
complete this task by exploring such vast composition space [ 20 , 21 ]. In 
our recent work, multi-component diffusion multiples (MCDMs) con -
sisting of several bulk Ni-based superalloys were prepared to quickly and 
efficiently establish composition-phase-structure-property relationships 
for discovering superalloys with superior microstructure stability and 
strength at high-temperature service conditions [ 22 , 23 ]. Herein, this 
novel MCDM technique is therefore applied to fabricate Ni-Co-based 
superalloys with large variations of Al, Ti, Nb and Ta additions. 
A newly developed Ni-Co-based superalloy (named as K0) was used 
as the baseline for designing better alloys. The Co level was selected to 
25 wt.%, within the composition range of Co in the TMW alloys [ 9 , 10 ]. 
The Cr content was slightly lowered to 12 wt.%, since it is reported that 
the decrease of Cr content in superalloys is beneficial for suppressing the 
precipitation of topological close packed (TCP) phases, specifically σ 
phase [ 24 , 25 ]. The alloys K1-K8 were designed by adjusting the 
γ
′
-forming elements of Al, Ti, Nb, Ta and W contents. The nominal 
compositions of the K0 alloy together with its variants are provided in 
Table 1 . Before further experiments, these superalloys were prepared by 
vacuum induction melting and then heat treated at 1180 
◦

ensure homogeneity. 
The geometry of the MCDMs designed in the present work is shown 
schematically in Fig. 1 (a). To fabricate these MCDMs, the annealed su -
peralloys were cut to the required dimensions by electro-discharge 
machining (EDM). The cutting surfaces of the superalloy bricks as well 
as the Ni cartridges were ground to 1500 grit SiC sand paper and then 
ultrasonically cleaned in alcohol. The detailed procedure to fabricate an 
MCDM is illustrated in Fig. 1 (b). After placing the superalloy bricks into 
one Ni cartridge, two pure Ni caps of 3 mm in thickness were welded in 
vacuum onto both the top and bottom of each assembly using an elec -
tron beam (EB) welding. A subsequent hot isostatic pressing (HIP) that 
ran at 1180 
◦
C under an Ar pressure of 150 MPa for 8 h was applied to 
the welded assemblies to achieve good interfacial bonding among each 
superalloy pieces. As shown in Fig. 1 (c), the two assemblies together 
with a piece of Ta foil acting as oxygen getters were then encapsulated 
together into an evacuated quartz tube backfilled with pure Ar for 
annealing at 1180 
◦
C for 592 h, making a total heat treatment duration 
of 600 h. A final annealing at 800 
◦
C for 1000 h was applied to the two 
MCDMs to create various micro-alloys with varying concentration and 
microstructure.

First proposed by Gupta et al. [28] , the concept of zero-phase frac -
tion (ZPF) line provides a direct way to construct complex multicom -
ponent diagrams by dividing a two-dimensional (pseudo-ternary) phase 
diagram into several domains. The principle that on one side of each ZPF 
line a certain phase occurs while on the other side it does not also rep -
resents the solubility limit of a certain phase, and thus can be utilized 
directly for studying the stability of η phase in Ni-based superalloys. In 
order to reveal the exact ZPF line of η phase, quantitative electron probe 
microanalysis (EPMA) was employed to obtain local compositions along 
the phase boundary/interface which separates the η -precipitation region 
from the η -free region, as shown as the white curve in Fig. 2 (a). 
Single-point EPMA measurements using a spot size of 20 μ m were per -
formed on superalloy matrix that is in close proximity to η precipitates to 
provide the solubility information, Supplementary materials Fig. S1. 
Two EPMA points were acquired serially at each selected location to 
guarantee the consistency and integrity of the measured average com -
positions. All the measured compositions are tabulated in the Supple -
mentary materials Table S1 and present as red solid symbols in Fig. 5 (a), 
and accordingly, the corresponding ZPF line of η phase is well defined. 
When plotting those datapoints in atomic percent, Fig. 5 (b), one can

2.0 to 2.5 at.%. This composition range can be referred as the maximum 
amounts of Nb and Ta additions, i.e., the exact limit of γ + γ
′

region, while avoiding the η formation or forming only a very small 
amount of η phase.
