STRUCTURAL TRANSFORMATIONS IN HEATING OF KhN62MKVBTYu 
CAST HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH NICKEL ALLOY 
Yu. G. Veksler, T. M. Maslakova, 
V. P. Lesnikov, M. V. Kukhtin, 
and V. V. Bogaevskii 
UDC 669.245o017.15 
A characteristic feature of nickel-base high-temperature strength alloys is the pre- 
sence of Ni3Al-base hardening T'-phase. The optimum combination of the basic mechanical pro- 
perties in these alloys is obtained as the result of the capability of controlling the pro- 
cesses of precipitation and growth of y'-phase particles since at temperatures above 1000°C 
there is a sharp increase in the solubility of the Ni~Al-base intermetallide. The role of 
the processes of precipitation and solution of the hardening phases in formation of the struc- 
ture of high-temperature strength alloys has been mentioned more than once [1-4]. However, 
in high-temperature strength nickel alloys differing in chemical composition the character 
of the phase and structural transformations in cooling is not the same. 
The purpose of this work is an investigation of the structural transformations in 
KhN62MKVBTYu cast high-temperature strength nickel-base alloy (0.10-0.15% C, 17-19% Cr, 
4-6% Co, 4.5-6.5% Mo, 2-3% Ti, 3-4% AI, 2.5-4% W, <2.5% Fe, 1.4-2% Nb, 0.5% Mn, 0.04-0.12% 
B, 4.0.010% S, 40.015% P, ~0.5% Si) in heating to 950-1275°C, of the processes of solution 
of the hardening T'-phase, and of the change in its parameters in relation to heating temper- 
ature. 
The investigation was made on specimens in two original conditions: after homogenizing 
annealing at I170°C for 3.5 h in argon (lot i) and after aging at 900°C for i00 h (lot 2). 
The processes occurring in heating were investigated on specimens after quenching (in 10% 
aqueous solution of NaOH) from the investigated temperatures of 950-1275°C. The holds were 
primarily 2 h, which corresponded to the holds in production heating, with the exception of 
the temperatures of 1150 (30 min) and 1200 and 1275°C (15 min each). The quenching was used 
to retain the high temperature condition of the alloy and prevent a change in structure in 
cooling. 
The microstructure of the specimens was investigated on an Nu2E light microscope with 
a magnification up to 1250×. The microhardness of the individual structural constituents 
was measured on a PMT-3 tester with a load of 0.5 N. The parameters of the T'-phase (lattice 
parameter a in nm and size of the blocks Def in nm) electrolytically precipitated in the re- 
sidue* were determined by the x-ray method# on a DRON-I instrument in copper K~-radiation 
and the effective size of the blocks Def in the [iii] direction by the approximation method. 
*The electrolytic precipitation of the T'-phase into the residue was done by D. F. Khadyevaya. 
#The x-ray investigations were made by N. A. Manakovaya.

alloy element distribution in the phases was investigated with the use of an MS-46 x-ray

The matrix of the as-cast KhN62MKVBTYu (EP539LM) alloy is a nickel-base y-solid solu- 
tion with a fcc lattice. In the as-cast condition the structure of this alloy is heterophase 
with clearly expressed dendritic segregation revealed in nonuniform distribution of the par- 
ticles of the hardening y'- and carbide-phases (Fig. la). The density of distribution, the 
dimensions, and sometimes the form of the ¥'-phase particles in the interdendritic areas and 
the axes of the dendrites are different. The carbide phase is basically located in the inter 
dendritic areas in the form of skeleton-shaped precipitates and more rarely in the form of 
individual polygonal inclusions in the body of the grains or discontinuous precipitates in 
the grain boundaries. Both the polygonal and the sekelton-shaped precipitates are type MC 
carbides but the polygonal carbides are formed close to the liquidus temperatures and the 
skeleton-shaped as the result of the eutectic reaction and are classified as eutectic [i, 2]. 
The presence of the carbide eutectic is characteristic of the structure of KhN62MKVBTYu alloy. 
A homogenizing anneal at I170°C for 3.5 h leads to the formation of dispered uniformly 
distributed y'-phase particles as the result of equalizing the chemical composition. The 
massive polygonal carbides do not undergo marked changes and the carbides of the eutectic 
phase coagulate, forming accumulations of rounded precipitates in the interdendritic areas. 
Micro-x-ray spectral analysis showed that the eutectic-type carbides contain more Nb, W, and 
Mo and less Ti, Cr, Ni, Co, and A1 than the matrix. 
The original structure of the specimens of lot 2 (after aging at 900°C for i00 h) is 
somewhat different. The y'-phase particles are coarser and oriented in a definite manner 
in the body of the grain, as the result of which the grain structure of the alloy is well 
revealed. Carbide phase of different morphology is observed both at the boundaries and in 
the body of the grains. In addition to carbides dark areas (pores) are observed in the grain 
boundaries. Micro-x-ray spectral analysis showed that the intensity of the x-ray radiation 
of all of the elements in the composition of the alloy is less in these areas than in the 
matrix° 
After rapid hardening from 950°C the microstructure of the specimens of both lots is 
characterized by orientation of the y'-phase particles and some coarsening of them. At the 
grain boundaries there are obserbed carbide precipitates and pores of arbitrary form, the 
quantity of which increases with an increase in heating temperature. After hardening from 
1000°C the density of distribution of the y'-phase particles decreases somewhat although 
their preferential orientation in the individual grains of the solid solution is maintained.

Hardening from 1200°C is accompanied by a significant change in structure. As at lower 
temperatures (ii00 and I150°C) the matrix consists of y-solid solution but at the points of 
accumulations of eutectic carbides there are formed areas of fusion with a "woody" (fan- 
shaped) structure (Fig. Id). An increase in temperature to 1275°C leads to coarsening of 
the fused areas at the points of the carbide eutectic and the apprearance in the body of 
the grains of finer areas of regular form. Frequently formation of the fused areas is ac- 
companied by the appearance of massive inclusions up to 3-5 Dm in size of dull gray color. 
The results of micro-x-ray spectral analysis showed that both the fine and the coarse 
areas of fusion are enriched with the carbide-forming elements Nb, Ti, W, and Mo and depleted 
of Ni, AI, and Co in comparison with the matrix. On the basis of this it may be concluded 
that the fine fused areas in the body of the grain were formed as the result of solution 
of polygonal carbides. The coarse areas of fusion are extremely finely dispersed boron

In air cooling from 1200 and 1275°C a structure similar to the structure of the as-cast

The results of micro-x-ray and x-ray phase analyses confirmed the conclusions drawn 
on the basis of metallographic investigation. For example, x-ray phase analysis did not 
detect y'-phase in the electrolytically precipitated deposits of the specimens rapidly hard- 
ened from ii00, 1150, and 1200°C, which is an indication of either its complete absence or 
of the fact that its quantity is within the limits of measurement error. The error in deter- 
mination of the y'-phase content (from the eleetrolytically precipitated deposit) increases 
with a decrease in its quantity since the possibility of inclusion in the deposit of niobium 
and titanium carbonitrides observed by the x-ray diffraction method is not excluded. 
Micro-x-ray spectral analysis of the deposits showed that while after hardening from 
950-I025°C the electrolytically precipitated deposits consist of y'-phase, after hardening 
from 1050 to I075°C they contain, in addition to this phase, a small quantity of carbides 
and after hardening from II00°C the main portion of the deposit is carbides (carbonitrides) 
of Ti, W, Mo, and Cr with very little y'-phase. After hardening from 1150 and 1200°C carbides 
and carbonitrides of Ti, W, Mo, and Cr are observed in the deposit and there is practically no 
y'-phase.

On the basis of the results of the investigations conducted it was established that the 
temperature of solution of the y'-phase of KhN62MKVBTYu alloy is about II00°C and the tem- 
perature of the nonequilibrium solidus, one of the most important chracteristics of high- 
temperature strength, about 1200°C. This is the result of the presence of the carbide eu- 
tectic and the multicomponent nature of the system. 
The data obtained may be used for determination of the maximum operating temperatures 
in service of alloys of this type.
