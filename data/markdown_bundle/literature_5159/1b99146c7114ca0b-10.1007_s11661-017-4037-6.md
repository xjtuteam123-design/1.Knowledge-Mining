The Microstructure Stability of Precipitation
Strengthened Medium to High Entropy Superalloys
TE-KANG TSAO, AN-CHOU YEH, and HIDEYUKI MURAKAMI
Medium and high entropy superalloys based on the Ni-Co-Fe system with strengthening L1 2 c¢
precipitates have been developed. The present study has shown that by controlling the elemental
partitioning between c/c¢, thermal stability of c¢ can be enhanced in the high entropy c matrix.
Most importantly, high entropy superalloys exhibit stable c–c¢ microstructures with no TCP
phases after long-term exposure at elevated temperatures. Therefore, a new alloy design space
for stable c–c¢ microstructure has been presented. Furthermore, due to relatively high content of
Fe and Ti, their raw materials cost and alloy density can potentially be lower than those of
conventional superalloys.

I. INTRODUCTION
HIGH entropy alloys (HEA) with elevated solution
contents have been a subject under intensive investiga-
tions recently. [1–3] Although several studies have shown
interesting strength and toughness properties in
HEA,[4–7] recent studies have also pointed out that a
single FCC phase HEA is not strong enough for
practical applications. [8] So, additional strengthening
mechanisms are required to achieve desirable mechan-
ical strength. Precipitation strengthening can be one of
the promising methods and has already been applied to
several HEA systems. Liu et al .[9] and He et al .[10] have
shown that CoCrFeNi HEA can be strengthened by
Nb-rich precipitates. In other studies, Al xCoCrFeNi can
be strengthened by nano-sized B2 precipitates to
increase both the yield stress and ultimate tensile
stress[11,12]; however, this increase in strength often
corresponds with a decrease in plasticity.
According to the physical metallurgy of superalloys,
uniformly distributed c¢ particles are coherent with the
FCC c matrix and can render excellent high-temperature
strength.[13,14] Based on the microstructure of superalloys,
several c¢-bearing HEA systems have been studied and
reported.[15–27] Gwalani et al.[15] investigated Al0.3CoCr-
FeNi and Al 0.3CrCuFeNi2 alloys. The precipitation of
L12 c¢ were observed, but the c¢ precipitates in Al0.3CoCr-
FeNi can only be stable up to 823 K (550 /C176 C) and then be
de-stabilized, replaced by B2 phase after annealing at
973 K (700 /C176 C); the thermal stability of c¢ in Al0.3CrCu-
FeNi2 is slightly higher. Borkar et al. [16] reported that by
increasing Al from 0.0 to 1.5 in mole fraction for
AlxCrCuFeNi2, the predominant microstructure would
evolve from FCC solid-solution, to FCC + L12, to mixed
FCC + L12/BCC + B2 and ﬁnally to BCC + B2.[16] Sev-
eral studies on Al 0.5CoCrCuFeNi by Pickering et al .[17]
Xu et al .[18] and Jones et al .[19] revealed the nano-scale
phase separation of microstructure, which is consisted of
one FCC phase rich in Cr, Co, Fe, Ni, one FCC rich in Cu,
and a L12 phase. Daoud et al.[20,21] has further simpliﬁed
the microstructure to be mainly FCC solid-solution phase
as the matrix and with uniformly distributed nano-size
L12 precipitates, e.g., the Al 8Co17Cr17Cu8Fe17Ni33 and
Al10Co25Cr8Fe15Ni36Ti6 alloys containing 20 to 40 pct of
c¢; comparing to those of commercial alloys such as
Inconel 617 and Alloy 800H, the higher tensile strength
can be achieved. Similarly, He et al.[22] reported that with
minor additions of Al and Ti in CoCrFeNi, L1 2 coherent
precipitates can be formed in the alloy matrix, and
well-balanced room temperature tensile properties have
been achieved; [22] however, the excessive addition of the
Al and Ti promotes the formation of brittle (Ni, Co)2TiAl
Heusler phase, and thus greatly degrades the tensile
ductility.[23]
The development of c¢ strengthened HEAs appears to
be promising, but none of these reported studies has
really tested the long-term phase stability of these
HEAs. High-temperature application requires materials
with high-temperature strength and creep resistance, so
it is important to study the microstructure stability of
strengthening precipitates in HEA systems. In this
paper, the thermal stability of several c¢-bearing medium
and high entropy alloys has been investigated. Since
their microstructure resembles that of superalloys, they
have been named as high entropy superalloys
(HESA).[24] In our previous work, HESAs are shown
to possess comparable high temperature hardness and
surface stability to those of conventional Ni-based
superalloys, and with the advantages in lower density
and cost of materials. [25,26] This paper aims to present an
investigation on the microstructure stability of c¢-bear-
ing HESA and also show the eﬀort to increase the
thermal stability of c¢ phase toward 1423 K (1150 /C176 C).

II. MATERIALS AND METHODS
Based on Ni-Al-Co-Fe-Cr-Ti, seven alloys have been
designed and designated as MA-1, MA-2, HA-1, HA-2,
HA-3, HA-3 + Fe, and HA-3 /C0 Co. Table I lists their
nominal and actual compositions. The term of mixing
entropy is calculated by the following equation:
DS
mix ¼/C0 RðXA ln XA þ XB ln XB þ/C1/C1/C1 Þ ;
where DSmix is the mixing entropy, R is the gas constant,
XA means the molar fraction of constituent A in whole
alloy, XB means the molar fraction of constituent B, and
so on. The alloy designs of MA-1, MA-2, HA-1, and
HA-2 are slightly modiﬁed from Ni 1.5Al0.2Co1.5
FeCrTi0.3 HEA, which possesses c-c¢ microstructure,[27]
while their DSmix gradually evolves from medium level
as those of commercial superalloys to high entropy
alloys (The DS
mix of MA-1, MA-2, HA-1, and HA-2 are
1.32, 1.46, 1.55, 1.60 R, respectively). According to the
original deﬁnition, high entropy alloys are in the
category of DS
mix ‡ 1.5 R [28]; MA and HA stand for
medium and high mixing entropy of the alloy system,
respectively. HA-3 is further alloyed with Ti based on
HA-1, while HA-3 + Fe is further alloyed with Fe based
on HA-3. As for HA-3 /C0 Co, the Co content in HA-3 is
substituted by Ni. Consequently, comparisons can be
made between (Ni, Co, Fe)-rich and (Ni, Fe)-rich
matrix.
All alloys have been prepared by vacuum-arc-melting
process. A total of 50 g pure Ni, Al, Co, Fe, Cr, Ti
(purity better than 99 wt pct) were melted according to
each individual ratio in high-purity argon atmosphere
and solidiﬁed in a water-cooled copper mold. Samples
were turned and re-melted more than 4 times to assure
chemical homogeneity. To homogenize the casting
segregations and dissolve back the c¢ precipitates,
solution heat treatments (SHT) on MA-1 to HA-3 and
HA-3/C0 Co alloys were conducted at 1523 K (1250 /C176 C)
for 10 hours. As for HA-3 + Fe, the SHT was at 1443 K
(1170 /C176 C) for 10 hours. Double aging treatment was
then applied; the primary aging allowed the precipita-
tion and growth of c¢, and the secondary aging could
further reﬁne the shape of c¢ phase. Primary aging at
1323 K (1050 /C176 C) for 2 hours and secondary aging at
1073 K (800 /C176 C) for 20 hours were applied for all alloys.
Scanning electron microscopes (SEM, JEOL-5410,
and Hitachi SU-8010) equipped with energy-dispersive
X-ray spectrometry (EDS) were used to observe the
microstructures. The metallographic specimens were
prepared by grinding, polishing, and electrical etching
with 70 pct phosphoric acid (H
3PO4) in deionized water.
The crystal structures were identiﬁed by X-ray diﬀrac-
tometer (Shimadzu XRD 6000) with Cu-target radiation
at 30 kV and 20 mA. The XRD specimens were in plate
form with dimension of 1 cm 9 1c m 9 3 mm and
scanned at 2 h angle from 20 to 100 deg with a scanning
rate of 2 deg/min. A Pyris Diamond TG/DTA was used
to examine the thermal properties of 20 to 50 mg
specimen in an Al
2O3 crucible from room temperature
to 1573 K (1300 /C176 C). The heating rate was 10 /C176 C/min
and with 400 sccm of Ar ﬂow. Strain aging method [29,30]
coupled with SEM-EDS measurement was conducted to
measure the composition and elemental partitioning of c
and c¢ phase. This method was also utilized in our
previous study. [31] During the strain aging process,
samples were cold worked after solution heat treatment,
so plastic deformation was applied, and then followed
by an aging at 1173 K (900 /C176 C) for 50 hours. Therefore,
the straining-annealing-induced cellular growth of c¢
would occur, resulting in coarsen morphology. In
addition, the compositions of c and c¢ phases were
veriﬁed with the lever rule analyses. The diagrams of
C
c¢-Cc vs.C m-Cc were plotted, where C c,C c¢ and C m
were the chemical composition measured from c, c¢, and
bulk alloy, respectively. The gradient of linear ﬁtting of
Cc¢-Cc vs.C m-Cc diagram for each alloy should correlate
with their volume fraction of c¢ phase. CALPHAD-
based simulation package Thermo-Calc (TCNI5 data-
base)[32] and JMatPro (Ni-alloys database) [33] were
utilized in this work.

IV. DISCUSSION
High entropy superalloys with c-c¢ microstructure
have been developed. The present study has found that
the thermal stability of c¢ phase is lowered due to higher
solid-solutions, and the underlying mechanism can be
related to the c¢ ordering energy. The ordering energy is
related to the atomic bonding in order structure, and
stronger bonding can lead to the greater thermal
stability.
[14] According to the binary mixing enthalpy
of elements, [38] when higher concentrations of Co, Cr,
Fe are alloyed in the based Ni 3(Al, Ti) c¢, the main
bonding of Ni-Al ( /C0 22 kJ/mole), Ni-Ti ( /C0 35 kJ/mole)
would be replaced by Co-Al ( /C0 19 kJ/mole), Co-Ti
(/C0 28 kJ/mole) …etc. As a result, the magnitude of
enthalpy can be decreased and results in lower c¢ solvus;
this agrees well with our previous ﬁnding of decreased

V. CONCLUSIONS
Medium and high entropy superalloys based on the
Ni-Co-Fe system with strengthening L1
2 c¢ precipitates
have been developed. Although the solvus temperature
of L1
2 precipitate is decreased with an increase in the
entropy of the overall alloy system, thermal stability of
c¢ can be elevated through the substitution of Ni by Ti,
which enhances the ordering of c¢ phase. Most impor-
tantly, high entropy superalloys exhibit stable c-c¢
microstructures with no TCP phases after 300 hours
exposure at 1173 K (900 /C176 C). Therefore, a Ni-Co-Fe
alloy design space for stable c-c¢ microstructure has been
proposed in this article.
