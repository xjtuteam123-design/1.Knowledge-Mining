ABSTRACT 
We report on the rapid alloy screening of Ni – Co – Ti – Al – Mo superalloys with high thermal stability and specific 
yield stress by means of directed energy deposition. A laser directed energy deposition, a specific type of additive 
manufacturing, was employed using multiple powder feeders and elemental powders. Fifty superalloys of 
different compositions were deposited and the heat-treated microstructure and γ’ solvus temperature were 
examined. The 43Ni – 38Co – 9Ti – 6Al – 4Mo superalloy (atomic percent composition) exhibited a uniform γ / γ’ 
microstructure and a γ’ solvus temperature of 1202
◦
C. The beneficial properties of the superalloy were also found 
in the cast superalloy of identical composition. The cast superalloy exhibited a thermally stable γ / γ’ micro -
structure with cuboidal γ’ precipitates even after long-term aging heat treatments at 800
◦
C, 900
◦
C, and 1000
◦
C 
up to 500 h. The γ’ -coarsening mechanism was evaluated based on the Lifshitz – Slyozov – Wagner model and the 
trans -interface diffusion-controlled model. A transition between these two mechanisms was observed with an 
increase in aging temperature. Atom probe tomography analyses revealed that the sluggish interface diffusion of 
Co and corresponding reduction of the interfacial energy induced the transition of the γ’ -coarsening mechanism. 
Moreover, the cast superalloy showed an enhanced specific yield stress attributed to its exceptionally low alloy 
density of 7.61 g/cm
3
. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys are widely used in gas turbine blades and disks 
[1] , which operate under centrifugal forces at high temperatures owing 
to their superior balance of high mechanical strength, oxidation resis -
tance, and thermal stability [2] . The remarkable high-temperature me -
chanical properties of Ni-based superalloys are attributed to the typical 
microstructure of Ni
3
Al, L1
2
-precipitates ( γ’ ), embedded in a Ni face- 
centered cubic ( γ ) matrix [3] . Since the discovery of Co – Al – W superal -
loys [4] , which have similar γ / γ’ microstructures, the development of 
Co-based superalloys has attracted considerable interest owing to the 
higher melting temperature of Co than that of Ni. 
Representative Co-based systems are Co – Al – W- [4] and Co – Ti-based 
[5] superalloys. However, Co
3
(Al, W) has been reported to be a meta -
stable phase above 900
◦
C [6] with increased alloy density due to W. By 
contrast, the Co – Ti binary superalloy forms thermodynamically stable 
γ / γ’ microstructures [7] , where the Co
3
Ti phase exhibits relatively 
higher yield stress at high temperatures compared to the Ni
3
Al and 
Co
3
(Al, W) phases [8] . Furthermore, Co – Ti-based superalloys have a 
positive γ / γ’ lattice misfit, which indicates an enhanced coherency be -
tween the precipitates and matrix at elevated temperatures [9] ; how -
ever, they have a low γ’ solvus temperature of ~1000
◦
C [8] . By contrast, 
Ni – Al superalloys have a wide γ / γ’ two-phase region range [10] with a 
high γ’ solvus temperature ( > 1200
◦
C) [11] but a negative lattice misfit 
[12] , resulting in a larger mismatch at high temperatures. Given the 
complementary properties of Co – Ti and Ni – Al superalloys, there have 
been systematic studies to design novel Co – Ni and Ni – Co superalloys by 
adjusting the ratio of (Ni/Co) [13 – 15] or (Ni – Al/Co – Ti) [16 – 18] . 
However, there is a need for rapid and systematic screening of 
Ni – Co – Ti – Al-based superalloys for high-temperature applications since 
traditional casting methods are too time-consuming to explore the wide 
compositional range of quaternary systems within a reasonable amount 
of time. Thus far, microstructural evolutions and corresponding prop -
erties have been analyzed by mainly focusing on the changes in Co and 
Ni concentrations [13 – 15] . Otherwise, the ratios between Co – xTi and 
Ni – yAl have been changed [16 – 18] , keeping x, y, and the composition of 
most of the elements fixed. Consequently, high-temperature properties 
have not been optimized, particularly the γ’ solvus temperature, which

The γ’ solvus temperature of Ni – Co-based superalloys was reported 
to be fairly low compared to that of conventional Ni-based superalloys. 
Recently, active researches are being conducted to solve this problem. 
Zenk et al. [13] changed the composition of Co and Ni with fixed Al, W, 
Cr, and B contents and showed that the γ’ solvus temperature decreased 
as the Co composition increased. Llewelyn et al. [14] varied the Ni:Co 
ratio with fixed Al, Ti, and Cr and reported that γ’ solvus temperature 
decreased with increasing Co composition. Christofidou et al. [20] 
examined the effects of varying the Ni:Co and Al:Ti ratios at three Cr 
concentrations and measured the γ’ solvus temperature. Liang et al. [21] 
investigated the effect of refractory elements such as Ta, Nb, W, and Mo 
on the microstructure, γ’ solvus temperature, and high-temperature 
yield stress of Co – Ni – Al – Ti – Cr-based superalloys. Despite their efforts, 
the γ’ solvus temperature of the abovementioned Co – Ni-based superal -

◦
C. Co – Ni – Al – Ta [22] , Co – Ni – Al – W [10] , 
Co – Ni – Al – Ti – W – Ta – (B) [23 – 25] , Co – Ni – Al – Ti – Ta – W – Cr [26] , 
Co – Ni – Al – V – Ta – Ti [27] , and Co – Ni – Al – Ti – W – Ta – Cr – Mo [28] superal -

◦

increase in alloy density (8.46 – 9.35 g/cm
3
) was unavoidable owing to 
the Ta and W additions. As replacements for Ta and W to reduce alloy 
density, Mo and Nb have attracted attention as promising elements 
[15,23] . Compared to Nb, Mo has a relatively higher solubility in Ni – Co 
alloys and can suppress the formation of detrimental intermetallic 
phases [29] . Indeed, Liu et al. [30] and Liang et al. [21] reported that Mo 
increased the γ’ solvus temperature of Co – Ni-based superalloys. There -
fore, Mo is a promising element for increasing the γ’ solvus temperature 
at moderate increases in the density of Co- and Ni-based superalloys. 
However, a limited number of studies have been conducted on the 
Ni – Co – Ti – Al superalloy system. In this paper, we applied a rapid alloy 
screening method that can substantially reduce the time in multicom -
ponent superalloy development. 
We exploited a laser-based directed energy deposition (DED) system 
[31 – 33] equipped with multiple powder feeders for rapid alloy 
screening and high-throughput experiments. The employed DED ma -
chine not only enabled rapid fabrication of multiple alloys in a short 
amount of time (a sample of 3 × 13 × 13 mm
3 
in dimension was finished 
within only 15 min) but also the production of bulk samples of suffi -
ciently large size for mechanical testing. The DED is an advantage to 
thin-film deposition techniques that show some limitations in fabri -
cating samples for macro-scale mechanical testing [34] . 
In this study, 50 superalloys of various compositions were produced 
using a DED metal 3D printer equipped with multiple powder feeders 
and single elemental powders of Ni, Co, Ti, Al, and Mo. The deposited 
superalloys were screened for the presence of γ / γ’ microstructures and 
their respective γ’ solvus temperature. To confirm the reproducibility of 
the properties obtained from additive manufacturing (AM), selected 
superalloys with the most promising properties were fabricated by arc 
melting. Subsequently, the microstructural stability and coarsening 
behavior of the cast sample were examined by long-term aging heat 
treatments at 800
◦
C, 900
◦
C, and 1000
◦
C for up to 500 h. Furthermore, 
atom probe tomography (APT) analyses and calculations of the γ / γ’ 
interface energy were conducted. Finally, high-temperature compres -
sion tests were performed. 
2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Directed energy deposition 
Elemental powders of Ni (MIDAS, Republic of Korea, 99.9%), Co 
(MIDAS, 99.9%), Al (MIDAS, 99.8%), Mo (MIDAS, 99.8%), and Ti 
(KOSWIRE, Republic of Korea, 99.8%) were used for the DED process. 
The powder particles were spherical and showed diameters of 45 – 150 
μ m ( Fig. S1 in supplementary information ), which are suitable for DED. 
A DED machine (InssTek MX-Lab) equipped with an ytterbium fiber 
laser of 1070 nm wavelength and six powder feeders was used to 
fabricate square-shaped samples of 3 × 13 × 13 mm
3 
on a carbon steel 
(S45C) substrate ( Fig. S2 ). To allow complete mixing of all elements, a 
laser power of 400 W, laser scan speed of 0.85 m/min, and powder feed 
rate of 3 g/min were used as deposition parameters using Ar as a powder 
carrier and shield gas. A bidirectional zigzag pattern followed by a 
contour filling was chosen for the laser track. The selection of alloy 
compositions was done as follows. First, referring to the phase diagrams 
of Co – Ti- [35,36] , Ni – Al- [37,38] , and Co – Ni-based [10,29] alloys, Co:Ti 
and Ni:Al ratios expected to yield γ / γ’ two-phase microstructures were 
identified and used for the following experiments. More specifically, 
ratios of 85Co:15Ti, 80Co:20Ti, 75Co:25Ti, 85Ni:15Al, 80Ni:20Al, and 
75Ni:25Al were considered. Subsequently, the selected Co:Ti and Ni:Al 
ratios of each elemental powder and Mo were co-deposited by DED. Mo 
was added up to 7 at.% only to suppress the formation of the detrimental 
Co
3
Mo phase [35] . The measured compositions of all as-deposited 
Ni – Co – Ti – Al – Mo superalloys are summarized in Table S1 . To obtain 
uniform γ / γ’ microstructures, the as-deposited superalloys were ho -
mogenized at 1250
◦
C for 24 h and subsequently aged at 900
◦
C for 24 h, 
similar to heat treatments for cast superalloys [13,14] .

4. Conclusions 
In this study, we demonstrate a novel multicomponent alloy-design 
method and a promising Ni – Co-based superalloy discovered by rapid 
alloy screening using DED. A metal 3D printer equipped with a multi 
powder feeder and single elemental powders was employed in the alloy 
screening process. Fifty superalloys with different compositions were 
fabricated, and the heat-treated microstructure and the γ’ solvus tem -
perature were examined. From the alloy screening process, 
43Ni – 38Co – 9Ti – 6Al – 4Mo (AM_33) superalloy was selected owing to the 
uniform γ / γ’ microstructures and an extremely high γ’ solvus tempera -
ture of 1202
◦
C. The superior properties of the AM_33 superalloy were 
maintained in the Cast_33 superalloy with identical compositions of the 
AM_33 superalloy but different fabrication methods. To investigate the 
high-temperature γ’ phase stability of cast superalloys, long-term aging 
heat treatments at 800
◦
C, 900
◦
C, and 1000
◦
C up to 500 h was applied. 
The Cast_33 superalloy retained cuboidal γ’ precipitates up to 1000
◦
C 
for 500 h. Moreover, the γ’ coarsening was confirmed to follow the LSW 
model at 800
◦
C and the TIDC model at 900
◦
C and 1000
◦
C in terms of 
both the size and number density of γ’ precipitates. By APT analyses, the 
sluggish interface diffusion of Co and corresponding interfacial energy 
reduction induced transition of γ’ -coarsening mechanism from LSW to 
TIDC. The reason for the change in segregation behavior of Co was 
presumed to be a change in partitioning behavior of each element with 
temperature. In addition, the Cast_33 superalloy showed a higher γ’ 
solvus temperature and specific yield stress due to its very low alloy 
density compared to the reported Co – Ni-based superalloys, Co-based 
superalloys, and commercial superalloys. In conclusion, the newly 
developed Ni – Co-based superalloy discovered by DED shows 
outstanding balance of γ’ solvus temperature, microstructural stability, 
and specific yield stress.
