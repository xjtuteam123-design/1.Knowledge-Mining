ABSTRACT
Additive manufacturing (AM) of refractory alloys and high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are gaining 
increasing interest due to their superior high-temperature strength and low buy-to-fly ratio. 
However, most of refractory alloys and RHEAs suffered from room temperature (RT) brittleness 
and hence severe cracking during AM. Here we report the strong yet ductile carbide-strengthened 
niobium alloy (so-called Nb521) fabricated by laser powder bed fusion (LPBF). The LPBF Nb521 
alloy showcases excellent tensile strength-ductility at RT and outstanding specific yield strength 
at 1200 
◦
C, as compared to other promising niobium alloys and RHEAs. The LPBF Nb521 alloy 
exhibits hierarchical microstructures at various length scales, including irregular-shaped grains, 
nano-scale dispersed carbides and carbides-decorated low/high-angle grain boundaries. Trans -
mission electron microscopy investigations revealed that the good tensile ductility originates from 
movement of unusually predominant mixed dislocations. Density functional theory calculations 
uncovered potential reasons for the good ductility, i.e., higher Rice ductility parameter and lower 
unstable stacking fault energies than brittle elements. Further analyses pointed out the reasons of 
high strength of the LPBF niobium alloy and potential directions for pursuing even higher 
strength. Consequently, this study not only deepens the understanding of deformation mecha -
nisms of carbide-strengthened niobium alloys, but also provides a reference for further design of 
strong yet ductile refractory alloys.
1. Introduction
Recently, refractory metals and alloys (e.g., tungsten (W), molybdenum (Mo), niobium (Nb), tantalum (Ta) and their high-entropy 
alloys HEAs) have garnered increasing attention owing to their unique combinations of properties, including high melting points, 
remarkable high-temperature strength and resistance to corrosion and wear ( Cook et al., 2024 ; Feng et al., 2024 ; Liu et al., 2024 ; Zhang 
et al., 2025b ). Among the refractory alloys, Nb-based alloys (such as C103 and Nb521 alloys with composition of Nb-10Hf-1Ti and 
Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr-0.07C in wt.%, respectively) are the most preferred high-temperature alloys for industrial applications due to their 
excellent RT toughness, high-temperature strength retention at temperatures above 1200

4.2. Reasons for ductile behavior
4.2.1. Deformation behavior
The underlying reasons for the two-stage strain hardening behavior and good ductility of the LPBF Nb521 alloy ( Fig. 6 ) can be 
explained based on the observed microstructural features. When the critical shear stress was achieved (or yield occurred), dislocations 
initialized from several possible sources (including LAGBs, HAGBs and Frank-Read source, see Fig. 8 a). With further straining at low 
strains ( < 2.5 %), dislocations multiplied to produce a large amount of new dislocations and intersected with different defects 
(including solutes, dislocations and precipitates), which resulted in formation of various dislocation features (including pinned dis -
locations, dislocation tangles and dislocation loops, see Fig. 8 a-b). These together contributed to a high level of strain hardening ability 
at stage I. At this stage, the number of deformed grains increased and the amount of ‘soft ’ regions in the deformed grains decreased, 
thus resulting in a rapid decrease of strain hardening rate ( Fig. 6 b).
Upon further increasing strains (i.e., > 2.5 %), dislocations further motion were hindered by themselves and precipitates, which 
promoted their glide on other planes via cross slip. The attraction and annihilation of screws with opposite signs via cross slip led to a 
reduction in dislocation density (i.e., so-called dynamic recovery). Meanwhile, non-screw dislocations started to re-organize into low- 
energy substructures (such as dislocation walls, see Fig. 9 c-d). The dislocation-depleted regions between the walls increased the 
dislocation storage and working hardening capacity, hence leading to a slow decrease of strain hardening rate ( Fig. 6 b). This process 
delayed instable necking at stage II and contributed to the high ductility. Here stage II dominated the stress-strain curve (from ~ 2.5 % 
to ~ 16 %, see Fig. 6 b).

4.2.3. Intrinsic ductility criteria
The macroscopic ductility in bcc elements and alloys is usually determined by the intrinsic competition between brittle cleavage 
and ductile dislocation emission mechanisms at an atomistically sharp crack ( Borges et al., 2024 ; Hu et al., 2021 ; Shi et al., 2018 ).

which show brittle behavior. Therefore, this work indicates that the Nb521 alloy retained a sufficient ductility despite its lower D value 
than pure Nb.
It was previously established that an empirical relationship of Rice parameter D > 3.5 is required for ductile behavior in BCC 
crystals ( Borges et al., 2024 ; Rice, 1992 ). However, in this work, it was found that the D value of the current Nb521 alloy is > 2.6, yet it 
also displays ductile behavior. This observation suggests that the empirical relationship may be extended to parameter D > 2.6. 
Similarly, the recently reported MoReW RHEA has a similar D value of ~2.8 ( Borges et al., 2024 ), which does not meet the criterion of 
D > 3.5, yet it also displays ductile behavior ( Senkov et al., 2023 ). Consequently, in this context, further alloying with refractory 
elements (such as Mo) and tuning the parameter D > 2.6 hold promise for developing stronger yet ductile refractory alloys.

were formed in the current LPBF Nb521 alloy. This is correlated to the in-situ formation of precipitates during LPBF process, which 
hindered grains growth and formation of columnar grains along the building direction with the maximum thermal gradient.
5. Conclusions
This work investigated the excellent tensile mechanical properties of a carbide-strengthened Nb521 alloy (Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr-0.07C) 
fabricated by LPBF at RT and 1200 
◦
C. The results were compared to other reported promising Nb-based alloys and refractory high- 
entropy alloys (RHEAs). The underlying reasons behind the excellent properties were revealed via systematic microstructural in -
vestigations and theoretical calculations. The main novel findings are summarized as follows. 
(1) The LPBF Nb521 alloy exhibits hierarchical microstructures at various length scales, including irregular-shaped grains, nano- 
scale dispersed (Zr, Nb)C carbides, and carbides-decorated LAGBs/HAGBs.
