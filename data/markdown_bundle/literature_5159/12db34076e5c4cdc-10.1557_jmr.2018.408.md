ARTICLE
Formation of low-angle grain boundaries under different
solidiﬁcation conditions in the rejoined platforms of Ni-based single
crystal superalloys
Miao Huo, Lin Liu, a) Wenchao Yang, b) Yafeng Li, Songsong Hu, Haijun Su, Jun Zhang, and
Hengzhi Fu
State Key Laboratory of Solidi ﬁcation Processing, Northwestern Polytechnical University, Xi ’an 710072, China
(Received 11 July 2018; accepted 12 October 2018)
The formation of low-angle grain boundaries (LABs) in the rejoined platforms of a Ni-based
single crystal superalloy under different directional solidi ﬁcation rates was investigated by the
experimental investigation and the ProCAST simulation. The results showed that the growth
morphology and orientation evolution of dendrites in the platforms were different under the
withdrawal rates in the range of 60 –100 lm/s and then resulted in different types of LABs. At
lower withdrawal rates, the longitudinal LABs were common in the rejoined platforms. Both the
sliver defects and the orientation deviation of original primary dendrites from two independent
growth paths could cause the longitudinal LABs in the platforms. At higher withdrawal rates, the
dendrite growth patterns were more complex and the secondary branches with lateral growth
tended to deviate from their original orientation, eventually leading to the formation of some
transverse LABs. Finally, some suggestions to prevent the formation of different LABs are
provided.
I. INTRODUCTION
Owing to their excellent properties at elevated temper-
atures, Ni-based single crystal superalloys are widely used
for turbine blades and guide vanes of advanced aero-
engines and industrial gas turbines.
1–4 To obtain higher
turbine entry temperatures, the geometry of blades became
more complex with some complex air-cooling channels.
Besides, more refractory elements were added to improve
the high temperature performance of the superalloys.
5
However, these improvements were always accompanied
by a greater frequency of solidi ﬁcation defects, such as
freckles chains,
6,7 stray grains,8,9 sliver defects10 and low-
angle grain boundaries (LABs).11,12 Among these defects,
LABs is detrimental to the mechanical properties, especially
when the misorientation angle is larger than 9°,
13,14 because
the grain boundaries (GBs) may be the weakest points at
high temperatures due to the absence of GB-strengthening
elements in the Ni-based single crystal superalloys.
Over the past two decades, some studies were carried
out to investigate the formation mechanism of LABs. In
2000, Napolitano et al.
15 developed a tip-growth model to
simulate the dendrite growth, established a convergence-
fault criterion for the location of LABs, and pointed out
that the formation of LABs was due to the gradual
accumulation of slight orientation deviation in the process
of dendrite branching and growth. However, the reasons
for the orientation deviation were not clari ﬁed. After that,
Newell and Souza et al.
11,16,17 used different casting
models to study the formation of LABs in Ni-based
superalloys. The results showed that steady state growth
led to a random misorientation and non-steady state
conditions caused an accumulated misorientation which
subsequently produced LABs when the dendrite fronts
impinged. They attributed this misorientation to the plastic
deformation of the dendrites within the mushy zone. In
fact, as early as in 1993, Siredey et al.
18 found that
crystalline quality could decrease because of the mis-
orientation in initial dendrites themselves and proposed
that the thermomechanical stresses induced by the pre-
cipitation of the c9 could be high enough for the plastic
deformation of the dendrites. In 2014, Bogdanowicz
et al.
12 investigated the subgrain structure in single crystal
turbine blade roots; they reported that the formation of
LABs was caused by the inclined growth of secondary
dendrite arms and concluded that the reason might be the
element segregation around boundaries disturbing the
lattice parameters. In addition, some researchers suggested
that the liquid ﬂow between dendrites might also cause the
dendrite deformation.
19 It was clear that the formation of
LABs was very complicated and the leading reasons might
be different under various solidi ﬁcation conditions. How-
ever, previous studies on the formation mechanism of
LABs were usually under a certain solidiﬁcation condition,
and some investigations about the formation of LABs at

II. EXPERIMENTAL AND SIMULATION METHODS
A. Experiment details
Directional solidiﬁcation experiments were carried out
using the traditional high rate solidiﬁcation (HRS) method.
The Ni-based superalloy DD6 was used in the experi-
ments, with the nominal composition of 4.3 Cr, 9.0 Co,
2.0 Mo, 8.0 W, 7.5 Ta, 5.6 Al, 2.0 Re, 0.5 Nb, 0.1 Hf,
0.006 C, and Ni balance in wt%. The model with rejoined
platforms was used to prepare single crystal castings. From
bottom to top, the platforms were numbered in sequence
and each platform was divided into three areas — left (L),
middle (M), and right (R) — as shown in Fig. 1(a).
The single crystal castings were prepared using
a modi ﬁed Bridgman directional casting furnace. The
furnace consists of a vacuum-induction melting unit,
a graphite heating unit and a cooling zone, as shown in
Fig. 1(b). During the casting process, the ceramic mold
was mounted on a water-cooled copper chill plate and was
then raised into a cylindrical graphite heater, and then the
furnace chamber was evacuated and the mould was
preheated to 1540 °C. After equalizing the mold temper-
ature, the molten superalloy DD6 (1580 °C) was poured
into the mould and held for 15 min. Finally, the ceramic
mold was withdrawn from the furnace at a predetermined
withdrawal rate: 60, 75, and 100 lm/s, respectively.
After the castings were cleaned up, the riser and the
spiral grain selectors were removed by wire electro-
discharge machining, and a transverse cross section
(parallel to the XY surface) was cut at the bottom
position; meanwhile, the rejoined platforms were sec-
tioned from the castings longitudinally (parallel to the ZX
surface) and transversely (parallel to the XY surface).
Then the specimens were polished well and etched with
a solution of HNO
3, HF and C 3H8O3 (volume ratio of
1:2:3.5) for optical microscopy. The microstructure
was observed with an optical microscope (OM; Leica
DM-4000 M, Leica, Berlin, Germany).
Orientation determination of the dendrites was performed
using the electron back-scattering diffraction (EBSD) tech-
nique. After the samples were mechanically polished to
remove stress and obtain a ﬂat surface, EBSD measure-
ments were performed in a Tescan Mira 3 XMU scanning
electron microscope (SEM; TESCAN VEGA, TESCAN,
Brno, Czech Republic) equipped with the HKL system and
Channel 5 analysis software (Oxford Instruments, Oxford,
U.K.). A step size of 40 lm was used for EBSD mapping.
B. Simulation methods
To exhibit the thermal pro ﬁle and assess the transient
stresses and strains evolved during the directional solid-
iﬁcation process, the castings were simulated using ﬁnite
element analysis by ProCAST software (CalcomESI,
Lausanne-EPFL, Switzerland).
22
Finite element mesh models of casting and the ceramic
shell were constructed into a 3D mesh, and the furnace
was constructed into a 2D mesh, as shown in Fig. 1(b).
The initial state of simulation was after the holding
process, the melt temperature was set to 1550 °C, and
the chill-plate temperature was 400 °C. During the
withdrawal process, the heat transfer was assumed to be
heat conduction and radiation and heat convection was
ignored. The radiation heat transfer was calculated by
a radiation algorithm taking into account re ﬂections,
obstructions and shadowing effects. The coef ﬁcients of
thermal emissivity and thermal conductivity were gained
from Refs. 23 –27. The thermophysical parameters were

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS
A. The LABs derived from the orientation
deviation of original primary dendrites at lower
withdrawal rate
Figure 2(a) shows dendritic structures of longitudinal
section (parallel to the ZX surface) of the entire platform
1 at the withdrawal rate of 60 lm/s. The dendritic
structures of regions L and R were similar because they
were from the branching of original dendrites from the
spiral grain selector. The primary dendrite arms (PDA-L
and PDA-R) grew regularly. When the dendrites grew
into the section extension region (SE: SE-L and SE-R),
some long secondary dendrite arms (SDA-L and SDA-R)
formed at the bottom of the platform, as shown as the
dotted line in regions SE-L and SE-R in Fig. 2(a), and
they became the base for ternary dendrite arms TDA-L
and TDA-R. Finally, the TDA from both sides occupied
the entire platform. In this process, a GB formed when
TDA-L and TDA-R encountered, as shown as the dotted
line in Figs. 2(a) and 2(b). If the angle of GB was larger
than 2°, it was a LAB. In platforms 2 and 3, their dendrite
evolutions were similar to that of platform 1, and there
were also GBs at the encounter position of dendrites, as
shown in Figs. 3(a) and 3(c). Their corresponding polar
diagrams in Figs. 3(b) and 3(d) indicated that these GBs
were LABs.
To determine the evolution of original orientation with
height and the relationship between the LABs and the
original dendrites from both sides L and R, the crystal
orientation at the bottom position and the orientations of
PDA-L and PDA-R of the three platforms were all
measured. The misorientation angles are analyzed in
Table I. The results showed that after the process of
crystal selection, the crystal orientation at the bottom was
not a strict h001i direction, and the original orientation of
both sides ﬂuctuated with the increase of height, and the
angles of LABs were close to the deviation angles
between L and R. In other words, the LABs were mainly
caused by the ﬂuctuations of the original orientation of
PDA-L and PDA-R. Therefore, the original orientation
deviations might be a very important concern. From the
data in Table I, it could be seen that the original deviation
angles from h001i were different and there was no
accumulation from platform 1 to 3 as the height in-
creased, which was different from the experimental
results of Siredey,
18 who proposed that misorientation
was due to the thermomechanical stresses. In fact, the
random orientation ﬂuctuations might be natural in the
growth of a single crystal because the complex geometry
might lead to a nonuniform thermal ﬁeld and the
curvature ﬂuctuation of solid –liquid interface, especially
in region SE, as shown by the evolution of the

C. The LABs accompanied by severe lateral
growth of the secondary dendrites at higher
withdrawal rate
The above two types of LABs were easily found on the
longitudinal sections of the casting, especially after
macro-etching. However, in some specimens, the growth
of dendrites in the longitudinal sections was perfect
without defects, while some LABs were found in their
transverse sections, especially when the lateral growth
was severe and the growth paths of the dendrites were
complex. Figure 7(a) showed the dendritic structure of
transversal cross section (parallel to the XY surface) of
the whole platform with LABs accompanied by severe
lateral growth of the secondary dendrites at the with-
drawal rate of 100 lm/s. In regions L and R, the dendrites
were of typical cruciform structure and arraying regu-
larly. After that, the secondary branches in both sides
showed a lateral growth and became gradually serious
until the middle of region M. It might be caused by the
substantial lateral heat extraction in this region. Figure 8(a)
shows the isotherm shapes at the beginning of solidi ﬁ-
cation in region M. Obviously, the isotherm shape was
concave and looks like an ellipsoidal surface, and it was
propitious for lateral heat dissipation. Besides, the tem-
perature gradients in the different regions of the platform
were different, as shown in Fig. 8(b). Previous studies
have shown that the solid –liquid interface would be more
concave and obviously lag under higher withdrawal rate,
and the temperature gradient and its direction would
change accordingly, leading to the dispersion of crystal
orientation.
29,32,33 Furthermore, due to the different
curvature of the isotherm and the inhomogeneous thermal
ﬁeld in Fig. 8(c), the growth rates of dendrites in different
regions were also ﬂuctuating, as shown in Fig. 8(d); thus
the orientation of dendrites in different regions changed
during growth. Finally, these secondary branches of the
lateral growth with different orientations produced
boundaries once they converged, as shown in Figs. 7(b)
and 7(c). Their corresponding EBSD scan maps in
Figs. 7(d) and 7(e) and the polar diagrams in Figs. 7(f)
and 7(g) indicated that the boundaries were LABs. Similar
phenomena can be found in the literature,
20,32,34 especially
for castings with large cross sections which were beneﬁcial
to lateral heat extraction.

IV. CONCLUSION
The formation of LABs in the rejoined platforms of Ni-
based superalloy during directional solidi ﬁcation was
explored using both experiment and simulation method.
According to the results, some main conclusions are
listed as follows:
(1) At lower withdrawal rates, the longitudinal LABs
were common in the rejoined platforms because orienta-
tion deviations between the dendrites growing in in-
dependent channels were inevitable. Fortunately, the
angles of these LABs were very small, generally less
than 5°, and usually in the tolerance range of the
superalloy.
(2) At higher withdrawal rates, the transverse LABs
were common in the rejoined platforms, because the
secondary branches with severe lateral growth in the
middle region of the platforms had complex growth paths
and tend to deviate from orientation in the unstable
temperature ﬁeld.
(3) A special type of LABs was accompanied by the
sliver defects which were caused by the deformation of
secondary dendrites. The deformed dendrites starting at
the section extension region could continue to grow into
the middle region of the platforms to form sliver defects
through competitive growth with their adjacent dendrites.
(4) No accumulation of orientation deviation was
found when the dendrites from different parent arms
growing at different solidi ﬁcation rates converged with
each other.
