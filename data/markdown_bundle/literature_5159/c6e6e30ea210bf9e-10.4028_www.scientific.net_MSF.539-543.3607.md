Abstract. The high-temperature deformation behavior of the single-phase α (Ti-7.0Al-1.5V) and α + 
β (Ti-6Al-4V) alloy were determined and compared within the framework of self-consistent scheme 
at various temperature ranges. For this purpose, isothermal hot compression tests were conducted at 
temperatures between 650 °C ~ 950 °C to determine the effect of α/β phase volume fraction on 
average flow stress under hot-working condition. The flow behavior of α phase was estimated from 
the compression test results of single-phase α alloy whose chemical composition is close to that of α 
phase of Ti-6Al-4V alloy. On the other hand, the flow stress of β phase in Ti-6Al-4V was predicted 
by using self-consistent method. The flow stress of α phase was higher than that of β phase above 
750°C, while the β phase revealed higher flow stress than α phase at 650°C. Also, at temperature 
above 750°C, the predicted strain rate of β phase was higher than that of α phase. It was found that the 
relative strength between α and β phase significantly varied with temperature. 
Introduction 
The wrought processing of conventional two-phase (α+β) titanium alloys often involves a series 
of hot-working steps and final heat treatment. The objective of primary hot working is to produce a 
uniform and fine two-phase microstructure of globular α in a transformed matrix of the α and β 
phases. This is accomplished via initial hot working and heat treatment in the single-phase β field 
followed by breakdown of the transformed microstructure in the two-phase ( α+β) field [1-3]. 
Wrought billet or bar material is then frequently shaped via forging, extrusion, rolling, etc., usually in 
the α+β field. Significant attention has been focused on constitutive modeling and plastic flow 
analysis in this temperature regime [4]. Howeve r, most prior research to establish constitutive 
behavior has neglected the detailed effect of micr ostructure on flow behavior or has concentrated 
primarily on single-phase materials rather than two-phase alloys such as Ti-6Al-4V [5]. 
In an earlier investigation by Kim et al [6], the role of each constituent phase with regard to the 
high-temperature deformation of Ti-6Al-4V was evaluated. They analyzed the deformation 
characteristics of Ti-6Al-4V using the theory of in elastic deformation, and reported that the sliding 
resistance of α/β boundaries was much lower than that of α/α or β/β boundaries. However, the 
specific role of each phase still remained ambiguous because the load-relaxation tests were carried 
out using specimens with volume fractions of α phase which varied as a function of test temperature. 
Therefore the present work was undertaken to exam ine the high temperature flow behavior of each 
constituent phase and their effect on the macroscopi c deformation response of two-phase system. In 
present work, Ti-6Al-4V alloy ( α+β) was selected to analysis aforementioned points. For this 
purpose, a series of high temperature compression tests were conducted using commercial Ti-6Al-4V

Materials and experimental procedure 
Two different titanium alloys were used in th e present work to quantify the individual flow 
characteristic of α and β phase in Ti-6Al-4V alloy at elevat ed temperatures. One was the plate 
material of the commercial Ti-6Al-4V alloy recei ved from VSMPO Corporation, and the chemical 
composition is Ti-6.3Al-4.1V-0.21Fe-0.17O-0.005N- 0.01C-0.003H, balance titanium (in weight 
percent). The other alloy used in this investiga tion was bar material of the single-phase Ti alloy, 
whose chemical compositions were Ti-7.0Al-1.5V-0.15O-low Fe, balance titanium. The composition 
of this alloy is close to that of the α phase in Ti-6Al-4V at hot working temperatures. Fig. 1 shows the 
back-scattered electron (BSE) image of Ti -6Al-4V at various temperatures (650~950 °C). These 
results revealed that the α-phase volume fraction of Ti-6Al-4V were significantly varied with 
temperature. BSE image of single-phase α (Ti-7.0Al-1.5V) is not presented because it doesn’t show 
any micro structural features due to the no β phase existence at the hot-working temperature 
(650~950°C). But its grain size was almost constant at each temperature. The variation of grain size 
and phase volume fraction with temperature is summarized in Table 1 for the two alloys.

Experimental results 
The true stress-true stain curves of Ti-7.0A l-1.5V and Ti-6Al-4V were obtained at various 
temperatures (22°C ~950°C) using constant strain rate of 10 -1s-1. The measured flow curves of 
Ti-7.0Al-1.5V show strain hardening behavior below 650°C, while steady-state behavior occurs due 
to dynamic recovery process above 750°C (Fig. 2a) deformed specimen shows no surface cracking 
even at room temperature. In the case of Ti-6Al-4V, the flow stress of the curve at room temperature 
(below 500°C) decreased rapidly after a strain level of 0.4 (Fig. 2b), which was attributed to surface 
cracking. It is noted that th e flow stress of Ti-6Al-4V ( α+β, two-phase) is higher than that 
Ti-7.0Al-1.5V (α, single-phase) at low temperature (below 650 °C), meanwhile, opposite tendency 
was exhibited at higher temperature (above 750°C). It means that α phase is harder than β phase in 
high temperature, but the former becomes softer th an the latter as temperature decrease. It can be 
assumed that the mode of grain matrix deformation is not iso-stress (i.e. σα ≠ σβ ≠ σα+β).

Prediction of the flow behavior of β phase 
From the series of experiments, the deformation behavior of α+β alloy and single-phase α alloy 
was investigated. However, the flow stress of β phase in Ti-6Al-4V is not available yet. It is because 
there is no single-phase β alloy whose chemical composition is close to that of the β phase in 
Ti-6Al-4V at hot working temperatures. In addition to that, the composition of β phase in Ti-6Al-4V 
significantly varies with temperature, contrary to α phase [9]. Thus, the flow behavior of β phase was 
predicted indirectly using powerlaw viscoplastic self-consistent approach, proposed by Suquet [8].
