Abstract: The generalized stacking fault energies of face centered cubic Ni-Mo solid 
solutions are calculated using the exact muffin-tin orbital method in combination with 
coherent potential approximation . The alloying of Mo in Ni is found to decrease the 
intrinsic stacking fault energy of the solid solution from 150 mJ/m 2 (pure Ni) to 50 
mJ/m2 (17.5 at. % Mo) almost linearly. At the same time , the unstable stacking fault 
energy (the unstable twin fault energy) of the Ni -based solid solution increases 
(decreases) in a small extent with increasing Mo concentration. Three different 
twinnability measures are ad opted and all indicate a substantially enhanced twinning 
mechanism in Ni-Mo solid solutions with increasing concentration of Mo. The weaker 
Ni-Ni bonding at high Mo concentration s is considered to be the main mechanism 
behind the disclosed phenomen a. Segregation of Mo to the fault plane is proved to 
have strong effect on the generalized stacking fault energy of Ni-based solid solution. 
Keywords: Superalloys; Solid solution; Stacking fault energy; Twinning; Ab initio 
calculations

1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys are of great importance for the applications 
in advanced turbine engine blades and vanes, owing to their outstanding mechanical 
properties and oxidation resistance s at high temperature [1]. The new generation of 
Ni-based SX superalloys generally contain different kinds of alloying elements (such 
as Mo, Ta, Re, W) in order to strengthen the disordered γ matrix and ordered γ′ 
precipitates. In fact, the high strength and high creep resistances of Ni-based SX 
superalloys at high temperature are primarily attributed to three aspects, which are the 
solid solution strengthening of γ, the anomalous yield and precipitation strengthening 
of γ′ as well as the γ/γ′ interfacial strengthening [2-4]. However, the initial dislocation 
activity during the creep deformation process of SX superalloy is confined to the γ 
matrix channels, because of the high volume fraction of γ′ precipitates in modern 
superalloys [ 5, 6 ]. The kinetic process es of partial dislocation movements during 
creep deformation, such as cross -slip or climb, are generally considered to be 
controlled by stacking fault energy [7, 8]. Specific to Ni -based superalloys, previous 
experimental studies have proved that lower stacking fault energy of γ matrix could 
facilitate the dislocation dissociation and pr omote the microtwinning process [9, 10]. 
As a result, the dislocation cross -slip becomes difficult and the creep resistance s of 
superalloys are improved . Moreover, alloying elements such as Re, Ru and Co are 
found to decrease the stacking fault energy of γ matrix and strengthen the superalloys 
at high temperature [6, 11, 12]. Consequently, studying the stacking fault energy of γ 
solid solution, which has a close relationship with the steady -state creep rate of SX

For face centered cubic (fcc) structures, the generalized stacking fault energy (GSFE) 
is defined as the energy variation of the material when shearing the crystal within 
{111} planes along <112> and <110> direction s with different displacement vectors 
[13]. Owing to the difficulty in the precise measurement of stacking fault energy 
through electron microscopy [14], atomic simulation based on first -principles theory 
has become a powerful tool for planar fault studies and deformation mode analyses of 
materials [15-18]. Extensive first-principles calculations have revealed that the GSFE 
surface (or γ/Г surface) along the lowest energy path <112> could provide adequate 
information on the deformation modes of fcc elemental metals and alloys [ 19, 20]. 
The elemental study on the stacking - and twin -fault energies of 3d, 4d and 5d 
transition metals shows that the variation trend s of the planar fault energies along the 
row of periodic table are almost consistent with that of d-band width [21]. Aiming at 
pure Ni and Ni -based alloys, existing ab-initio calculation results indicate that all the 
reported alloying elements decrease the stacking fault energy of fcc Ni [ 14, 22-24]. 
Furthermore, the stacking fault energy of Ni decreases more by doping with an 
alloying element farther away from Ni in the periodic table [14]. This finding has also 
been proved by the experimental results [25]. However, most of the studies only focus 
on the intrinsic stacking fault energy of Ni -based binary alloys, and little information 
on the GSFE surfaces of Ni -based solid solutions with different solute concentrations 
is available.

2.2 Total energy calculation 
All the total energ y calculations are performed basing on density functional theory 
(DFT) [26] by using Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional 
within generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [27]. The EMTO method is 
adopted to solve the Kohn -Sham equations and the total energy is computed via the 
full charge -density technique [ 28-31]. In this improved screened 
Korringa-Kohn-Rostoker (KKR) method [32, 3 3], the full potential in EMTO is 
represented more accurately by overlapping muffin -tin potential spheres , which is 
spherically symmetric inside the spheres and constant in between [34]. Due to the fact 
that the meta-stable faulted lattices are close-packed, muffin-tin approaches have been 
proved to perform well in the planar fault energy calculations with minor errors 
associated with the shape approximation [ 17, 35-37]. The EMTO Green’s function is 
calculated self-consistently for 16 complex energy points distributed exponentially on

3.4 Effect of Mo segregation 
The alloying atoms are prone to segregate at the stacking faults due to the chemical 
interaction effect, and their high concentrations in the fault area can even make the 
stacking fault energy of alloys negative as a result. This phenomenon has been found 
in several superalloys [9], which is called Suzuki segregation [47]. The calculation 
result of the apparently decrease of ISF energy in Ni with alloying of Mo suggests 
that similar segregation effect of Mo might also exist in Ni-Mo solid solutions. Two 
different cases are considered in the present study, in which 5 at. % Mo is placed on 
either single side (layer 1/2/3) or both sides (layer 1&9) of the fault plane, in order to 
investigate the effect of Mo segregation on the GSFE surface of Ni-Mo solid solution. 
According to the results shown in Fig. 6, the GSFE surface of Ni-Mo solid solution 
becomes almost the same as that of pure Ni when placing 5 at. % Mo far away from 
the fault plane (layer 3). In other words, Mo only affects the GSFE of Ni within no 
more than two -interlayer distance near the planar fault . This result i s consistent with 
those of our previous convergence tests of the supercell size [16, 17], which prove the 
short-range perturbation induced by a stacking fault. Furthermore, stronger effect on 
the GSFE is found when doping Mo on both sides of the fault plane than that in the 
single-side doping case.

4.2 Twinnability 
The plastic deformation modes of fcc metals are commonly based on the nucleation of 
the subsequent partial dislocation, after the emission of the leading partial dislocation. 
In particular, full dislocation slip will dominate the deformation if a trailing partial 
dislocation nucleates and slip s on the same slip plane as the original one does. 
Otherwise, twinning will become the main deformation mechanism , if the second 
leading (twinning) partial dislocation forms on the adjacent slip plane and creates a 
two-layer twin nucleus [ 49]. The competition between these two main mechanisms 
can be evaluated by the twinning tendency ( i.e. twinnability) of metal, which is 
characterized by the stacking fault energy alone originally. More universal and proper 
measures of twinnability have been proposed involving unstable planar fault energies 
on GSFE surface . Within the Peierls framework for deformation twinning at a crack

positive ISF energy means energetically more stable fcc structure than the hcp one 
[17]. In their pure phases, fcc-Ni and bcc-Mo have approximately 8 and 5 d electrons, 
respectively. Alloying 15 at. % Mo in Ni decrease s the effective d electron number 
from 8 to 7.55 (8×0.85+5×0.15=7.55). This effective number corresponds to the alloy 
system Ni-Co (from the 3 d series), Pd-Rh (4d) or Pt-Ir (5d). All these binary alloys 
have positive ISF energies owing to their stable fcc structures, and the hcp -fcc 
structural energy differences for non-magnetic states slightly increase as reducing the 
d electron number from 8 to 7 [54]. This trend is in perfect line with the larger ISF 
energy of Rh (Ir) than that of Pd (Pt) [21]. However, different from Rh and Ir, the fcc 
phase of Co is stabilized above 700 K by magnetic and phonon effects. The stable hcp

5. Conclusions 
In the present work, t he GSFE surfaces of fcc pure Ni and Ni-Mo solid solutions are 
studied through ab initio calculations basing on the EMTO method in combination 
with CPA. The composition effects of Mo on ISF energy, USF energy and UTF 
energy are all investigated and the variation s in the plastic deformation modes of 
Ni-Mo solid solutions are discussed further. The 17.5 at. % Mo alloying decreases the 
ISF energy of Ni-based solid solution from 150 mJ/m 2 to 50 mJ/m 2 almost linearly. 
Chemical effect is considered to be the main reason of this phenomenon, besides the 
weak volume expansion effect due to the larger atomic radius of Mo than Ni. On the 
contrary, the USF energy which represents the energy barrier for the emission of 
leading partial dislocation in the Ni -based solid solution increases slightly with 
increasing Mo concentration. With further shearing on the adjacent slip plane , the 
energy barrier for the nucleation of the twinning partial dislocation called UTF energy 
drops with increasing Mo concentr ation as that of ISF energy, but in a much smaller 
extent. As a result, the twinnabilit y of Ni-Mo solid solutions is found to be enhanced 
with increasing Mo concentration, via calculating three widely-accepted measures T,
