ABSTRACT 
In this study, to obtain tungsten heavy alloy (WHA, 93W-4.9Ni-2.1Fe) with fine grain, high density and enhanced 
strength, W – Ni – Fe nanocomposite powder with high sintering driving force and small composite scale was 
designed and prepared. The effects of sintering temperature on the microstructure and properties of WHAs were 
systematically investigated. At a relatively low temperature of 1300 
◦
C, WHAs with both high relative density of 
98.0 ± 0.31% and fine grain size of 2.07 μ m were obtained, which was much smaller than that of traditional 
WHAs (30 – 60 μ m). This special microstructure made it simultaneously achieve excellent compressive yield 
strength (1555 MPa), high strain-to-failure (over 40%) and hardness (480 HV
0.5
). This ultrahigh yield strength 
was about 2 times as the conventional WHAs with lager grain size. With the further increase of temperature to 
1450 
◦
C, the grain size of W grew significantly from 2.07 to 6.57 μ m and the yield strength decreased remarkedly 
to 947 MPa. It was found that the W phase had good interfacial bonding with the γ -(Ni, Fe) matrix phase and the 
enhanced mechanical properties were mainly contributed by the small grain size and high ratio of phase 
boundary, which can significantly improve the storage capacity of dislocation. 
1. Introduction 
Tungsten heavy alloys (WHAs) are high-density structural alloys, 
generally composed of 88~99% tungsten and transition metals (such as 
Ni, Fe and Co) with relatively low melting point, in which the body- 
centered cubic W grains are uniformly dispersed in the face-centered 
cubic γ -(Ni, Fe) matrix phase (M) [ 1 – 4 ]. WHAs have a lot of excellent 
properties, such as high density (17~18.5 g/cm
3
), high mechanical 
strength (comprehensive yield strength, 780 – 1060 MPa), good corro -
sion resistance and no radiation contamination. These excellent char -
acteristics make them have a wide range of applications in the 
aerospace, aviation and military fields, such as counterbalance weights, 
rocket ignition tubes, sweating material, kinetic energy penetrator 
candidates and shielding material [ 5 – 9 ]. 
It is well known that the refinement of grain size is one of the most 
efficient pathways to increase the grain/phase boundaries and thus 
improve the storage capacity of dislocation and strength [ 10 ]. However, 
conventionally, WHAs are prepared by powder metallurgy (PM) method 
using W, Ni and Fe powders with large particle sizes. Normally, due to 
large size and low surface-to-volume ratio of coarse powder, it does not 
have abundant surface energy, which cannot provide high driving force 
for sintering [ 11 – 13 ]. Therefore, the liquid phase sintering (LPS) with 
the temperature of 1480~1540 
◦
C and long sintering time (2 – 3 h) [ 14 ] 
were always needed to obtain WHAs with a relatively high density. 
Under these conditions, the W grains of WHAs will grow significantly to 
40 – 60 μ m [ 14 , 15 ], which limits the improvement of strength. 
To reduce the grain size of WHAs, the decrease of sintering tem -
perature is necessary [ 8 , 16 , 17 ]. It was reported that the decrease of W 
particles to nanoscale can significantly improve their sintering activity 
and reduce the sintering temperature from 2500 
◦
C to below 1500 
◦
C 
[ 18 – 23 ]. Therefore, the employ of W nanoparticles is a potential 
pathway to obtain fine-grained WHAs, although it is still not enough. 
Recently, Li et al. prepared WHAs via using nano-sized W and large Ni 
and Fe as raw materials [ 24 – 27 ]. Due to the large composite scale and 
low diffusion driving force between W and Ni/Fe particles, the LPS with 
a very high sintering temperature of 1500 
◦
C was still needed to achieve
