Enhanced Strength of Inconel 718
by High Rate Severe Plastic

Prabhat Chand Yadav and Shashank Shekhar
Abstract Inconel 718 is Ni-based superalloy commonly used for manufacturing
engine components in aerospace industry. Nickel-based super-alloys are particu-
larly well suited for service in high temperature environment where resistance to
creep, corrosion, and thermal shock are of primary requirements. Alloy 718 forms
various nano-precipitates which endow higher strength to the material and thermal
stability to the microstructure up to 650 °C due to grain boundary pinning of these
nano-precipitates. In order to further improve the strength of the alloy at elevated
temperatures, we utilized high rate severe plastic deformation (HRSPD). Machining
was used to impose HRSPD on the alloy, and the chip thus formed was used to
study the response of thermomechanical process. Machining led to signi ﬁcant
microstructural transformation and resulted in “bi-modal” grain size distribution.
Grain re ﬁnement led to signi ﬁcant increase in strength. Further improvement in
strength was attained after extended heat-treatment at 600 °C for 10 h. This
increment in strength can be attributed to the formation of nano-precipitates which
results in obstructing dislocations movement and pinning of grain boundaries.
However, it is known that deformed microstructure with very ﬁne grains saturated
with dislocations, results in brittle behaviour of alloy rendering them unusable for
most applications. In order to alleviate this problem, post-process heat-treatment
was designed. Three different temperatures were selected for short heat-treatment at
700, 800, and 900 °C for 15 min. This short heat-treatment resulted in varying
amount of recrystallization of the matrix and precipitation and ripening of γ″ and γ′
phase. Transformation in microstructure and ensuing properties after thermome-
chanical processing are discussed in this paper.

Introduction
Plastic deformation introduces strain in the materials leading to microstructural
changes that provides improved strength to material. Strain introduced in processed
materials depends upon severity of deformation technique. Various high deformation
techniques, called as severe plastic deformation (SPD) techniques are available that
can easily introduce strain of the order of ∼1–10. The focus on obtaining
nano-structured materials, by way of deformation, is to enhance strength multi-fold by
suppressing dislocation mobility. Ultra-re ﬁned nano-structured materials were cre-
ated by imposing severe plastic strain in several bulk materials [1–7]. Microstructural
reﬁnement in deformed materials was studied in a comprehensive way by Embury and
Fisher [4] and Langford and Cohen [5]. Machining is another such technique that can
cause severe plastic deformation in metals and alloys during the formation of chips
from the work-piece. Plane strain machining has been found to be one of the most
effective SPD techniques in recent years to develop ultra-re ﬁned nano-structured
materials, particularly because of the ability to vary strain-rate and temperature
simultaneously [ 8, 9]. Apart from leading to severe deformation in the chips, ma-
chining can also introduce severe deformation at material surface. This results in
graded microstructure near the surface, withﬁne ultra-reﬁned grains at the surface and
large un-deformed grains away from the surface. These nano-grains are responsible
for larger strength while the large grains endow the material with good toughness. The
modiﬁed properties through this technique can effectively be used for applications
where materials start failing from the surface, i.e. fatigue, stress corrosion cracking,
irradiation assisted applications etc.
The origin of increase in strength of materials after creating nano-structured
grains can be attributed to Hall-Petch relation [ 10]. Strength of precipitation
treatable nano-structured materials (e.g. severely deformed Inconel 718) can further
increase after applying heat-treatment to introduce second-phase strengthening
particle. Second phase (precipitates) is signi ﬁcantly hard, which provides not just
higher strength but also provides thermal stability by continuously pinning the
grains boundaries. As pointed out earlier, severe plastic deformation often makes
the material very brittle due to high saturation of dislocation density. This limited
ductility is a major lacuna in nano-structure materials and stumbling block in
making this process useful for commercial applications. Therefore to improve
ductility, some researchers have recommended short time heat-treatment at higher
temperature which makes strain free grains [ 11, 12]. Still others have recommended
obtaining “bi-modal” microstructure by applying post-process heat-treatment to the
deformed samples [ 12–14]. In this work, we have explored application of
post-process short heat-treatment to improve the ductility of the samples. We have
applied short heat-treatment at 700, 800, and 900 °C for 15 min to yield controlled
recovery of microstructure without causing large extent grain coarsening. Such
microstructure is expected to result in improved ductility without causing signi ﬁ-
cant drop in strength of thermal stability.

Material
Inconel 718 alloy was used to study the effect of intermediate and post
heat-treatment on microstructural evolution. Inconel 718 alloy for present study was
obtained from Katariya Steels Mumbai, India. Nickel based superalloy (Inconel
718) forms various precipitates (i.e. γ′, γ″, δ etc.) in different temperature ranges.
Some of these precipitates viz. γ″ and γ′ appears as nano-precipitates in the matrix.
Formation of these nano-precipitates is known to pin the grain boundaries which
endow higher strength as well as thermal stability to the treated material. Optical
emission spectroscopy (OES) was used to obtain chemical composition of Inconel
718 alloy which is shown in Table 1.

(0.064 mm/rev.), depth of cut (4 mm) and cutting speed (27 m/s) which led to
constant effective strain of ∼5.4 in the chips. Net effective strain in as-machined

Results and Discussion
Microstructural Evolution of Chips
The aim was to understand the kind of grain re ﬁnement and microstructural
transformation taking place in machining followed by heat-treated materials.
Hence, we did microstructural analysis using OIM technique, which is clearly
shown in Fig. 1a–f. After machining, solution treated materials (equiaxed grains)
get converted into severely deformed grains with alternate band of moderately
reﬁned (MZ) and ultra-re ﬁned zone termed as shear zone (SZ), which is obvious
from Fig. 1b. Moderately re ﬁned region consists of grains of the order of few 10 s
of micrometer and can be easily identi ﬁed in the micrographs. On the other hand,
ultra-reﬁned grains are too small to be resolved using OIM and hence appear as
un-indexed points. Therefore based on this fact, we can say that bands seen in
micrographs are alternate layers of MZ and SZ which are inherently ‘bi-modal’ in
nature. It is known that ‘bi-modal’ microstructure leads to improved ductility
compared to materials having shear zone or ultra-re ﬁned grains through out the
microstructure [ 15, 16]. This bi-modality can be seen to be retained up to SH8
sample condition (Fig. 1e). From the micrograph, it is clear that SH9 sample
condition represents the onset of recrystallization. For 700 and 800 °C short
heat-treatment, only recovery takes place and the overall microstructure looks
similar. This is also con ﬁrmed by correlating the microstructure with the
un-indexed points which represent the fraction of shear zone. We found from OIM
micrographs that un-indexed points which are co-related as shear zone remained
approximately constant up to SH8 sample condition.
AM chips were intermediate heat-treated at 600 °C which generates large
fraction of γ″ and γ′ nano-precipitates. These, nano-precipitates are closely spaced

exert a substantial pinning force in both MZ as well as SZ. First two effects of
second-phase particles and severe strain promote the recrystallization while last one
suppress the recrystallization [ 19]. It is important to understand the complex
interplay of these behaviour in order to design proper process for obtaining desired
properties.

Grain Boundary Characterization of Chips
Microstructural modi ﬁcation takes place with transformation in nature of grain
boundary which continuously changes with strain and temperature. We wanted to
examine the change in nature of grain boundary with elevated temperature. Hence,
we did grain boundary analysis of processed samples and observed some nominal
change in the microstructure up to SH8 and drastic change in SH9 in terms of
formation of recrystallized strain free grains. Modi ﬁcation in microstructure directly
affects the mechanical properties i.e. strength and ductility. Hence, we ﬁrstly ana-
lyzed the average grain size of processed sample without considering unindexed
regions which represent the nano-structured grains in SZ shown in Fig. 4a.
Figure 4a shows drastic drop in average grain size for AM condition followed by
approximately constant grain size for all the sample condition except SH9, which
shows marginal increase. We see that the grains in the moderately re ﬁned region is
of the order of few micrometers.

Mechanical Behaviour of Chips
From above microstructural investigation, we observed marginal change up to SH8
sample condition which indicate microstructural stability in terms of formation of
strain free recrystallized grains. Delay in recrystallization up to SH8 is due to
formation of larger fraction of closely spaced ﬁne precipitates which has already
been discussed above. However, SH9 causes transformation of shear zone into
larger fraction of recrystallized grains. Smaller fraction of shear zone still remains
along with recrystallized grain in SH9. These shear zones strongly affect the
mechanical strength of processed sample which is normally un-indexed region.
Therefore, fraction of shear zone was measured in terms of un-indexed points of
processed sample from OIM data which is shown in Fig. 5. Fraction of un-indexed
point was found approximately constant with marginal change up to SH8 which is
depicted in Fig. 5. This marginal change is due to fractional reduction in disloca-
tions during recovery whereas drastic change was noticed for SH9 due to con-
version of shear zone into larger fraction of recrystallized grains. Approximately
constant fraction of un-indexed point up to SH8 can also be related to the brittle
behaviour of the material. On the other hand, SH9 sample condition has much
lower un-indexed point and hence can be expected to be ductile and tough. This
analysis clearly shows that a multiphase system i.e. Inconel 718 offers us much
better handle at microstructural tuning. By selecting suitable temperature, we can
control the fraction of sheared zone which limits the ductility of the processed
sample.
We have already stated that thermal treatment changes the strength of precipi-
tation treated materials i.e. Inconel 718, and the picture will become clear from
hardness plot which is shown in Fig. 5. We observed higher hardness of AM w.r.t.
SA which is due to formation of very ﬁne nano-grains as well as larger fraction of
dislocations tangles. Further increase in hardness was obtained for H6 sample after
introducing heat-treatment due to formation of larger fraction of closely spaced γ″
and γ′ nano-precipitates which leads to strengthening of the material by continu-
ously pinning the grains boundaries. Marginal drop in hardness of SH7 and SH8
sample was also observed, but was found to be still much higher than SA condition.
The marginal drop in hardness can be attributed to some amount of recovery that

Conclusions
After performing plane-strain machining of Inconel 718 alloy, AM chips were
intermediately heat-treated at 600 °C for 10 h followed by post short heat-treatment
at 700, 800 and 900 °C for 15 min which gives following pertinent conclusions:
1. Plane strain machining leads to ‘bi-modal’ microstructure with ultra-re ﬁned
sheared zone and moderately re ﬁned zone.
2. The average grain size for AM chips and various heat-treated samples remained
constant. From misorientation plot, it is clear that primarily recovery took place
for SH7 and SH8 sample condition with some indication of start of recrystal-
lization for SH8. Whereas 900 °C post short heat-treated sample showed
appreciable recrystallization. Recrystallization took place mainly in the
ultra-reﬁned zone which becomes clear after co-relating this data with grain size.
3. Average hardness and strength of AM chips increases drastically, which further
increases after intermediate heat-treatment at 600 °C for 10 h. The average
hardness starts decreasing slightly after applying post short heat-treatment for
15 min but was still much higher than bulk Inconel 718 at the end of 900 °C
heat-treatment.
Based on the above results, we conclude that AM chips intermediate heat-treated
at 600 °C result in largest hardness and AM chips heat-treated at 600 °C then again
post short heat-treated at 900 °C results in microstructure with ultra-re ﬁned grains
