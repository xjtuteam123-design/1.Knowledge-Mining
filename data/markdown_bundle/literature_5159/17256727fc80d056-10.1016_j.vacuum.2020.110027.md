1. Introduction 
Single crystal Ni-based superalloys have been commonly used as the 
hot-section component materials for advanced aeronautic, industrial gas 
turbine blades and vanes due to their excellent high-temperature per -
formance, such as retain strength, resist oxidation and creep at elevated 
temperatures [ 1 – 6 ]. Recently, a class of nanolaminated ternary ceramics

an A-group element, “ X ” represents either carbon or nitrogen) has drawn 
tremendous attention since their good thermal stability and machin -
ability, high electrical and thermal conductivities, which combines the 
excellent properties of both metals and ceramics [ 7 – 10 ]. Among them, 
the Ti
3

excellent oxidation and corrosion resistance in the harsh environment 
with the help of a protective α -Al

structural materials in advanced aerospace, the utilization temperature 
is usually around 800

stability above 1000

the single crystal Ni-based superalloys and the Ti

combine their excellent high-temperature properties, which is of great 
importance for their wide promotion in the high-temperature compo -

casting defects, the single crystal superalloys are difficult to manufac -
ture a large and complicated structure component [ 15 ]. Meanwhile, the 
low room temperature ductility and the poor formability of the MAX 
ceramic have also greatly restricted its extensive application [ 16 ]. 
Hence, the appropriate joining method to obtain a sound bonding be -
tween two materials is an urgent problem for extending their potential 
applications in high-temperature components. 
Owing to the high susceptibility to hot cracking caused by the high 
content of γ’ -former elements (Al and Ti), the reliable joint of single 
crystal Ni-based superalloys through conventional fusion welding 
method is difficult to obtained [ 2 , 17 , 18 ]. Diffusion bonding is an 
effective solid-state joining method to avoid the problems that occurred 
in fusion welding, which has been widely used to prepare the 
high-temperature component, such as hollow vanes [ 19 , 20 ]. Joining of 
single crystal Ni-based superalloys through diffusion bonding has been 
successfully achieved. Li et al. [ 21 ] investigated the bonding behavior of 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys using the Ni – Cr – Co – W – Mo – B alloy as

2. Experimental 
In this work, the single crystal Ni-based superalloy DD3 was provided 
by Institute of Aeronautical Materials, Beijing, China, with the chemical 
composition of Ni-9.5 Cr-5.5 W-5 Co-4.2 Mo-5.8 Al-2.3 Ti (wt%). The 
Ti
3
AlC
2 
ceramic was supplied by Institute for Advanced ceramics, Har -
bin, China. Details on the synthesis of DD3 and Ti
3
AlC
2 
have been 
described in our previous articles [ 32 , 33 ]. A layer of pure Ni foil ( ≥ 99.8 
wt%) with the thickness of 80 μ m was selected as the interlayer, pro -
vided by Tianrui Nonferrous Metal Materials Co. Ltd, Baoji, China. Prior 
to bonding, the DD3 and Ti
3
AlC
2 
substrate were cut into carefully 10 
mm × 20 mm × 3 mm and 5 mm × 5 mm × 5 mm, respectively. Sub -
sequently, all the bonding surfaces of the sample, including the inter -
layer, were ground, polished and then cleaned ultrasonically in acetone. 
The Ni interlayer was sandwiched between the DD3 and Ti
3
AlC
2 
sub -
strate, and then the bonding process was performed in the vacuum 
furnace ((1.3 – 2.0) × 10
 3 
Pa). 
According to our previous study [ 33 , 34 ], the bonding temperature 
and the holding time only changed the morphology of the interface 
without changing the type of reaction phase at the Ni/Ti
3
AlC
2 
interface. 
When the bonding temperature was low or the holding time was short, 
due to the insufficient diffusion and reaction between Ni and Ti
3
AlC
2
, no 
bonding could be observed at the interface. However, with the bonding 
temperature was raised too high, or the holding time was increased too 
long, the thickness of the interface reaction layer became too large. As a 
result of the mismatch of coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) be -
tween the reaction phase and the substrate, considerable residual stress 
would produce at the interface during the cooling stage, resulting in 
many microcracks at the interface. Besides, the uniaxial pressure for the 
diffusion bonding of the Ti
3
AlC
2 
ceramic is often around 30 MPa [ 25 , 
35 ]. Thus, constant pressure of 30 MPa was applied to the assembly 
during the bonding process. At the 900 
◦
C/60 min/30 MPa, a good 
bonding interface was obtained. 
Considering that the emphasis of this study was on the investigation 
of the bonding mechanism of the interface, therefore the typical 
microstructure of the joint bonded at 900 
◦
C for 60 min with a constant 
pressure of 30 MPa was analyzed. More details about the effects of the 
parameters on the interfacial microstructure will be discussed in our 
future study. 
After bonding, the microstructure of the joint was observed by SEM 
(HELIOS Nanolab 600i) equipped with an energy dispersive spectrom -
eter (EDS). The details of reaction phases at the interface were identified 
by TEM (Talos F200x) with a high angle annular dark field (HAADF) 
detector. The samples for TEM analysis were prepared by a focused ion 
beam (FIB, HELIOS NanoLab 600i). The mechanical properties of 
bonding joints were evaluated by the shear tests performed by a uni -
versal mechanical testing machining (1186) with a constant loading rate 
of 0.5 mm/min. 
3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 1 (a) shows the typical interfacial microstructure of the DD3/ 
Ti
3
AlC
2 
joints using a Ni interlayer performed at 900 
◦
C for 60 min. As 
expected, a sound diffusion bonding of DD3 to Ti
3
AlC
2 
was achieved, 
and no obvious defects were observed in the bonding joint. Here, Ni 
interlayer mainly played two essential roles. On the one hand, possess -
ing the good deformation ability, Ni interlayer can significantly alleviate 
the residual stress in the joint induced by the mismatch of CTE between 
the bonded substrates, and thus improve the bonding strength of the 
joint. According to the shear test result, the shear strength of the diffu -
sion bonding joint with the Ni interlayer was 86.4 MPa, increasing by 
124%, compared to the direct diffusion bonding joint with the sheat 
strength of 38.6 MPa. On the other hand, considering that Ni is the main 
element of the DD3 alloy, the best compatibility between the interlayer 
and DD3 alloy substrate can be obtained when using the Ni interlayer. 
Meanwhile, Ni has good diffusion and reaction with Ti
3
AlC
2
. Therefore, 
a good bonding interface at both sides can be obtained with the Ni 
interlayer during the bonding process of DD3 alloy and the Ti
3
AlC
2 
ceramic. 
As shown in Fig. 1 (b), a diffusion zone with a width of 3 μ m between 
DD3 and Ni interlayer was obviously observed due to the interdiffusion 
of elements, labeled as diffusion zone I (DZ I). The EDS analysis showed 
that the atomic percentage of Ni, Al and Ti in the DZ I region was about 
83.68 at%, 3.42 at% and 0.95 at%, respectively. According to the Ni – Al 
[ 36 ]and Ni – Ti binary phase diagram [ 37 ], the solid solubility limit of Al 
and Ti in Ni at the room temperature can still reach 4.2 at% and 1.8 at%, 
respectively. So, Al and Ti mainly existed in the DZ I region in the form 
of solid solution, indicating that the inter-diffusion between the Ni 
interlayer and the DD3 substrate was not intense. The DZ I region was 
consist of the (Ni)
ss 
(ss means solid solution), avoiding the formation of 
brittle intermetallic at the interface, which improved the properties of 
joints. In contrast, a quite complex diffusion zone with a width of more 
than 30 μ m was formed at the Ti
3
AlC
2 
interface, labeled as diffusion 
zone II (DZ II). It is observed that the intense inward-diffusion of Ni from 
the interlayer into Ti
3
AlC
2 
occurred and the apparent new phases were 
formed at the interface. In addition, it is also noted that the diffusion of 
Ni mainly through the grain boundary of the Ti
3
AlC
2 
substrate, as shown 
in Fig. 1 (c). It is concluded that the diffusion of elements between the 
substrate and interlayer is crucial to the formation of the joint, which 
will be discussed in detail in the next sections.
