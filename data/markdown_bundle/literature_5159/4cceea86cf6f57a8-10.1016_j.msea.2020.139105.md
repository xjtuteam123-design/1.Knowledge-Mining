ABSTRACT 
The tensile behavior of single crystal superalloys CMSX-4 was investigated under different strain rates at room 
temperature. The objective of this study is to identify the strain rate sensitivity and understand the damage 
failure mechanism by checking stress-strain curves and microstructure of the deformed sample. Experimental 
results show that the stress-strain curves including strain hardening behavior at both strain rates are different. 
The size of cleavage facets and dimples in the tensile fracture surface is related to the strain rates. The shearing 
lines cross through the γ matrix and γ
’ 
precipitates, which is related to the movement of dislocations. Then, 
Schmid factor and the damage failure mechanism during tensile tests are estimated and discussed. 
1. Introduction 
In the gas turbine engines and aircraft engines, the use of single 
crystal superalloys is increasing due to their good mechanical and fa -
tigue strength as well as good corrosion resistance in both room and high 
temperature [ 1 – 3 ]. The single crystal superalloys are produced by 
directional solidification (DS) process according to controlling the 
undercooling [ 4 ], and followed by a solution and aging heat treatment 
to achieve the desired microstructure and properties [ 5 ]. Then, their 
good performances are related to chemical composition and micro -
structure, including of a high-volume fracture γ
’ 
precipitates embedded 
in the solid solution γ matrix. 
Strain hardening behavior is one of the important methods to check 
and investigate the plastic deformation, especially for different strain 
rates [ 6 , 7 ]. H. J. KLEEMOLA et al. [ 8 ] calculated the strain hardening 
parameters of metal using several empirical equations. Xinhong Xiong 
et al. [ 9 ] showed that the tensile behavior and microstructure properties 
including slip and fracture morphology of single crystal superalloy DD6 
is related to the temperature. Then, in-situ micro-tensile of strain rate 
along different orientations of strain rate response in single crystal 
nickel was recently studied by Alan Xu et al. [ 10 ]. They found that local 
slip on multiple slip systems is activated and caused strain hardening, 
resulting in rapid deformation during tensile tests. And increase in strain 
rate leads to an increase in flow stress for both orientations. 
In the current study, micro-tensile tests under two strain rates were 
performed to investigate the strain hardening behavior and failure 
mechanism. Strain hardening rate and exponent were fitted with 
empirical equations. Then, the fracture surface was observed using 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron micro -
scope (TEM) to define the slip properties and dislocation distribution, as 
well as to understand the deformation behavior and failure mechanism.

5. Conclusions 
1) The stress-strain curves at two strain rates presented different dis -
tribution. Higher strain rate results in greater yield stress, as well as 
higher tensile strength, larger strain hardening exponets and larger 
dislocation density. And strain hardening stages is dependent on the 
strain rate. 
2) Flat fracture surface and dimples can be observed at the deformed 
sample. The size of the cleavage facet and dimple increases with the 
increase of the strain rate. The fracture morphology of tensile sample 
is dependent on the strain rate. 
3) The deformation sample with two strain rates loaded along the [001] 
orientation activates the primary slip system ( 111)[101]. The 
shearing process, which is accomplished by APB-coupled a/2 < 101 >
dislocation pairs, occurs during plastic deformation. This behavior is 
independent of the strain rate and partially responsible for the good 
tensile properties with strain.
