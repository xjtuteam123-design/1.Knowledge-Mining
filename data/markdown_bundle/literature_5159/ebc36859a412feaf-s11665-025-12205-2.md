ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
The Mechanism of Heat Treatment Effects
on the Microstructure and Properties of Additively
Manufactured Cold-Rolled FeCoCrNi High-Entropy Alloys
Yan Cui , Yangchuan Cai, Weihong Li, Yan Zhao, Xiaohua Wang, and Jinyao Mu
Submitted: 21 February 2025 / Revised: 13 June 2025 / Accepted: 11 July 2025
This study investigates the effects of heat treatment on the microstructure and properties of FeCoCrNi
high-entropy alloy (HEA) fabricated by laser additive manufacturing and cold rolling. The alloy underwent
heat treatments at various temperatures (500 to 1300 /C176C), and its microstructural evolution and mechanical
properties were analyzed. Cold rolling introduces high dislocation density and reﬁnes the grain structure,
which facilitates subsequent recovery and recrystallization during heat treatment. The low-temperature
heat treatment (500 /C176C) results in high tensile strength (1114.1 MPa) and microhardness (461.6 HV
0.3), but
low elongation (4.5%). As the heat treatment temperature increases, dislocation density decreases, grain
growth occurs, leading to reduced strength and microhardness but improved ductility. The optimized heat
treatment at 500 /C176C enhances the strength–ductility balance, which can support the use of FeCoCrNi HEA
in high-performance applications.
Keywords cold rolling, FeCoCrNi, HEA, heat treatment, laser
additive manufacturing, strength
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted widespread
attention due to their unique multi-principal element design,
high thermodynamic stability, and excellent mechanical prop-
erties (Ref 1). Among them, FeCoCrNi HEA is a typical single-
phase face-centered cubic (FCC) structure alloy, which exhibits
good ductility and excellent corrosion resistance, making it a
promising material for applications in aerospace, energy, and
high-performance engineering ﬁelds (Ref 2). However, Fe-
CoCrNi alloys typically exhibit relatively low strength, which
is insufﬁcient for engineering applications (Ref 3).
Rolling technology provides a means to control the shape,
size, orientation, and distribution of the FeCoCrNi alloy /C213s
microstructure, offering a method to enhance its mechanical
properties. Hu X et al. (Ref 4) demonstrated that deformation
strengthening and solid solution strengthening induced by large
plastic deformation during rolling can effectively improve the
strength of the material after rolling annealing. The grain
deformation and fracture during the rolling process reﬁne the
microstructure, signiﬁcantly enhancing the overall perfor-
mance. The yield strength can reach up to 1611 MPa, and the
compressive strength can reach 2213 MPa. In a recent study on
CoCrFeNi alloys (Ref 5), partial recrystallization after heavy
rolling was shown to be an effective method for producing
samples with a room-temperature yield strength close to or
slightly above 1 GPa, with total elongation ranging from 21 to
26%.
Recent studies have demonstrated that repeated thermal
cycling and rapid solidiﬁcation during AM not only stabilize
the structure but also reﬁne the grain morphology, resulting in
improved mechanical performance (Ref 6-8). Our previous
research has further shown that introducing these AM-derived
microstructural features as the initial state for cold rolling can
signiﬁcantly enhance recrystallization kinetics and facilitate the
development of favorable textures (Ref 9-12).
The novelty of this study lies in combining laser additive
manufacturing (LAM) with subsequent cold rolling and heat
treatment—a strategy distinct from conventional processing of
cast or wrought HEAs. Unlike traditional methods starting from
coarse, near-equilibrium microstructures, our approach utilizes
the non-equilibrium, ﬁne-grained, and columnar structures from
LAM, which provide high dislocation densities, residual
stresses, and solidiﬁcation textures that promote recrystalliza-
tion, grain reﬁnement, and twin formation during annealing.
In this paper, the effect of heat treatment on the microstruc-
ture and properties of cold-rolled FeCoCrNi high-entropy alloy
was studied. The effect of heat treatment temperature on grain
size, recrystallization behavior, and dislocation density was
revealed through microstructure analysis and mechanical
properties test, and the comprehensive mechanism of tensile
strength, ductility, and reinforcement was explored.

2. Experimental Procedures
2.1 Standard and Process for the Establishment of HEA
In this study, 316 stainless steel plates (300 mm 9 300
mm 9 300 mm) were used as the substrate for additive
manufacturing. Commercially sourced FeCoCrNi HEA powder
with an equiatomic ratio of 1:1:1:1 was employed, featuring a
purity of 99.0–99.5% and particle sizes in the range of 45–
150 lm. The laser metal deposition (LMD) process was carried
out using a HANSGS-RJ0016-F3K laser system (Fig. 1a),
under the following conditions: laser power of 1300 W,
scanning speed of 6 mm/s, beam spot diameter (negative
defocus) of 3 mm, powder feed rate of 3.0 rpm, argon shielding
ﬂow rate of 25 mL/min, and a multi-track overlap of 30%.
Figure 1(b) indicates the sampling region for subsequent
rolling. Samples were cut to 60 mm 9 20 mm 9 2.3 mm
(Fig. 1c), with the rolling direction shown in Fig. 1(d). The
thickness of the samples was reduced to 0.5 mm by cold
rolling, followed by heat treatments at 500, 700, 900, 1100, and
1300 /C176 C for 2 h.
2.2 Microstructure Characterization
Metallographic samples were etched in a mixed solution of
nitric acid and hydrochloric acid (1:3 ratio) for approximately
20 s. The microstructure of the FeCoCrNi HEA before and
after rolling was characterized using a Hitachi S-3400N
scanning electron microscope (SEM). Electron backscatter
diffraction (EBSD) analyses were performed using a TESCAN
MAIA3 system equipped with Channel 5 software.
To minimize strain introduced during sample preparation,
samples were ion-polished at 6.5 V for 30 min. EBSD data
were collected with step sizes of 0.4 lm and 0.175 lm,
respectively, ensuring precise and reliable microstructural
characterization.
2.3 Mechanical Properties
The microhardness of FeCoCrNi HEA before and after
rolling was measured using an MHV2000 digital microhard-
ness tester, following the ISO 6892-1:2009 standard (load:
2.942 N, dwell time: 10 s). Seven data points were recorded for
each group of samples, spaced 0.5 mm apart, and averaged to
obtain the ﬁnal microhardness value, providing a comprehen-
sive assessment of the material /C213s microhardness.
Tensile tests were carried out at room temperature on a CSS-
44100 electronic universal testing machine, in accordance with
ISO 6507-1:2005 MOD. A loading rate of 1 mm/min was
applied. Figure 2 illustrates the sampling geometry of the
tensile test samples. Two tensile samples were tested for each
material condition, and ﬁnal data were obtained through a
ﬁtting procedure.

This difference can be attributed to the following reasons:
First of all, during rolling, deformation primarily accumulated
along the RD, leading to a higher degree of strain and stored
energy in this direction. This higher stored energy drove more
grain nucleation and growth during subsequent heat treatment.
And secondly, the RD samples had a stronger driving force for
recrystallization, causing more pronounced grain growth in this
direction. Finally, the TD experienced less deformation, leading
to lower stored energy compared to the RD. Consequently, the
cumulative effect of grain growth during heat treatment was
weaker in the TD, resulting in a smaller increase in grain size.
In summary, grains in the RD underwent more elongation and
distortion during cold rolling, accumulating higher dislocation
density and strain energy. This stored energy provided a
stronger driving force for recrystallization, making grains in the
RD more prone to growth during heat treatment. Conversely,
TD grains experienced less deformation, accumulating lower
dislocation density and driving force, resulting in a smaller
grain size increase compared to the RD. Overall, as the heat
treatment temperature increased, there was a clear difference in
grain evolution between the RD and TD. This difference
mainly stemmed from the degree of accumulated deformation
and stored energy in each direction. Understanding these grain
size variations is crucial for optimizing the heat treatment
process of high-entropy alloys, allowing better control over
their mechanical properties.

high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs), which is conducive to

3.2 Adjustments of Complete HEA
The tensile properties of the laser additive manufactured
HEA samples after heat treatment are shown in Fig. 10(a).
Additionally, Fig. 10(b) provides the data for yield strength
(YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS), and elongation (EL) as a
function of different heat treatment temperatures. At 500 /C176 C,
both YS (1114.1 MPa) and UTS (1229.1 MPa) reach their
maximum values, but the elongation is only 4.5%, displaying a
typical high-strength, low-ductility characteristic. As the heat
treatment temperature increases, both YS and UTS signiﬁcantly
decrease, dropping to 219.8 MPa and 557 MPa at 1300 /C176 C,
respectively. However, the elongation improves signiﬁcantly to
45.1%, demonstrating excellent ductility. SEM fracture surface
morphology in Fig. 10(c-h) shows that at RT and 500 /C176 C, the
fractures exhibit cleavage fracture characteristics, with ﬂat and
brittle fracture surfaces. Above 700 /C176 C, the fractures are
predominantly characterized by ductile dimples. As the heat
treatment temperature increases, the size of the dimples
gradually increases. Particularly at 1300 /C176 C, the fracture
surface exhibits large, deep, and dense dimples, indicating a
shift in the fracture mechanism toward ductile fracture at high
temperatures.

4. Discussion
Based on experimental results and the schematic diagram of
‘‘strengthening mechanisms’’ (see Fig. 12), an in-depth analysis
was conducted to systematically elucidate the relationship
between microstructural evolution, changes in strengthening
mechanisms, and mechanical properties of the FeCoCrNi high-
entropy alloy under different heat treatment temperatures. In the
low-temperature range (room temperature to 500 /C176 C), the
FeCoCrNi HEA, having undergone cold rolling, exhibits an
extremely high dislocation density (up to approximately
1.9 9 10
15 m/C0 2), with dislocation strengthening being the
dominant mechanism. As shown in Fig. 10(b), the alloy in this
state displays the highest yield and tensile strengths, but with a
limited elongation of only 4.5%, reﬂecting a typical ‘‘high-
strength–low-ductility’’ behavior. The fracture morphologies
(Fig. 10c–d) show clear cleavage features, further conﬁrming
the poor plastic deformation capacity. As the heat treatment
temperature increases to 700–900 /C176 C, partial recovery and
recrystallization occur, signiﬁcantly reducing dislocation den-
sity (to approximately 4.2 9 10
14 m/C0 2, see Fig. 6(a)), along
with the formation of equiaxed grains and annealing twins (see
Fig. 7). During this stage, the dominant strengthening mech-
anisms gradually shift to grain boundary strengthening and twin
boundary strengthening, enhancing the alloy’s plastic deforma-
bility. Tensile tests indicate that the elongation in this stage can
reach 17.4%, while maintaining a relatively high strength
(>800 MPa), reﬂecting a favorable strength–ductility synergy,
driven by the combined effects of recovery softening and twin-
assisted deformation. Under high-temperature heat treatments
(1100–1300 /C176 C), the alloy undergoes full recrystallization
(Fig. 4 and Fig. 6), with signiﬁcant grain coarsening (average
grain size exceeding 2500 lm
2 at 1300 /C176 C), leading to
weakened grain boundary strengthening. Meanwhile, disloca-
tion density further decreases ( <2 9 10
14 m/C0 2), resulting in a
reduction in strength. However, the abundant formation of
annealing twins at this stage enhances the coordination of
plastic deformation, causing elongation to increase substantially
to 45.1%. The fracture morphology (Fig. 10h) exhibits large,
deep dimples, indicating a transition to a typical ductile fracture
mode. At this point, the twin-dominated deformation mecha-
nism becomes the primary contributor to maintaining high
ductility.

increases markedly from 900 ( /C24 82.66 lm
2) to 1300 /C176 C
(/C24 2826.11 lm2), the tensile strength and microhardness
remain relatively stable. This is due to the combined effects
of recrystallization saturation, annealing twin strengthening,
softening plateau, and microstructural homogenization. At
900 /C176 C, full recrystallization signiﬁcantly reduces dislocation

enlarges grains without altering substructures, thus having

hinders dislocation motion, compensating for the loss of grain
boundary strengthening. In addition, after 900 /C176 C, residual
stresses are largely relieved, and further softening is minimal.
The overall texture becomes more randomized, enhancing
deformation compatibility across grains. Although classical

In conclusion, although the FeCoCrNi high-entropy alloy
produced by laser additive manufacturing, followed by cold
rolling and heat treatment, did not achieve ideal mechanical
properties, this does not invalidate the approach taken in this
study. The main reason is that high-temperature heat treatment
fundamentally alters the microstructure by promoting recrys-
tallization, grain growth, and the release of residual stresses.
The enhanced recrystallization behavior signiﬁcantly improves
the uniformity and integrity of the grains, improving the
material/C213s plasticity. Grain growth leads to a decrease in
strength but also signiﬁcantly reduces the residual stresses

fracture mechanism. Therefore, by optimizing heat treatment
temperature, it is possible to tailor the strength and plasticity of
the high-entropy alloy to meet speciﬁc application require-

performance structural materials.
5. Conclusions
1. Microstructural Evolution: Heat treatment promotes
recrystallization and grain growth, with full recrystalliza-
tion and notable grain coarsening occurring above
900 /C176 C. At 500–700 /C176 C, the microstructure retains defor-
mation traces and substructures, while at 1100–1300 /C176 C,
grains become fully recrystallized, equiaxed, and signiﬁ-
cantly larger in size.
2. Mechanical Properties: Peak microhardness (461.6 HV 0.3)
and strength (UTS: 1229.1 MPa) occur at 500 /C176 C, due to
dislocation strengthening. As temperature increases, dis-
location density decreases and annealing twins form,
leading to reduced microhardness and strength but im-
proved ductility, reaching a maximum elongation of
45.1% at 1300 /C176 C.
3. Fracture Behavior and Texture Evolution: At low temper-
atures (RT–500 /C176 C), fractures exhibit brittle cleavage,
while above 700 /C176 C, fracture modes transition to ductile
dimpling, and dimple size increases with temperature.
The RD direction shows more pronounced grain growth
and twin formation, while the TD direction exhibits
greater microstructural homogenization and weaker tex-
ture intensity at high-temperature heat treatment.
4. Optimization for Applications: Low-temperature (500 /C176 C)
heat treatment maintains high strength and microhard-
ness. Intermediate-temperature (700–900 /C176 C) treatments
achieve a strength–ductility synergy through twin-assisted
deformation, while high-temperature ( > 1100 /C176 C) treat-
ment enhances ductility due to twin-dominated mecha-
nisms but reduces strength, necessitating application-
speciﬁc heat treatment strategies.
Furthermore, we demonstrate that the microstructure–prop-
erty evolution in the cold-rolled AM-built FeCoCrNi HEA
follows a unique strengthening path involving dislocation
strengthening ﬁ grain boundary/twin boundary strengthen-
ing ﬁ twin-dominated plasticity, which is less evident in
conventionally processed HEAs. This contributes new under-
standing to the ﬁeld of post-AM thermomechanical processing
of single-phase FCC HEAs.
