ABSTRACT
Tungsten wire with high strength and toughness for silicon wafer cutting in photovoltaic industry is a new hot 
research content. In order to improve the strength and processing performance of tungsten wire, tungsten alloy

1. Introduction
Tungsten is a kind of rare metal with high melting point (3410 ±

and good wear resistance [ 1 – 5 ]. They have been widely used in many 
fields such as electronics and electrical engineering, aviation and aero -

electric lamp filament, electrical contact point, cathode material for 
electron tube, high temperature resistance furnace heating element, 
cemented carbide, aircraft counterweight and shock absorption 
material, armor piercing projectile, etc. [ 6 – 8 ] However, the 
low-temperature brittleness of tungsten alloys is susceptible to cracking 
during high-temperature deformation, which significantly impacts their 
thermal processing performance and impedes the development and 
application of these alloys [ 9 – 11 ]. Therefore, enhancing their thermal 
deformation stability and low-temperature ductility, while preserving 
high-temperature properties, has consistently been a central focus in 
research [ 12 ].

The kinetic model is the first model proposed to draw the thermal 
processing map, but its scope of application is very small, only for 
thermoplastic processing with a small range of temperature and strain 
rate changes [ 35 ]. Therefore, Raj created the atomic model (Atomistic 
Model). The thermal processing map based on this theory can well 
determine the ’ stable zone ’ and ’ unstable zone ’ of metal materials, 
which is better than the previous model. However, it requires a lot of 
parameters when drawing the processing map, which brings a lot of 
work. Due to the intricate microstructure and behavior of complex al -
loys, traditional models may not be adequate. Consequently, Prasad 
et al. [ 36 ]integrated principles from irreversible thermodynamics, large 
plastic deformation mechanics, and physical metallurgy to develop a 
versatile Dynamic Materials Model (DMM). This model offers a broader 
applicability range and is better suited for predicting the behavior of 
complex alloys under various conditions.
