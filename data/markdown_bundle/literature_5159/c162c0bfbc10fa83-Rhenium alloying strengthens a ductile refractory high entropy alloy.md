ABSTRACT
Strengthening alloys at both ambient and elevated temperatures without compromising room temperature 
ductility remains a significant challenge in the structural materials field. Recently, we reported a ductile NbTaTi- 
based refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) capable of maintaining high strength up to 1200
◦
C. In this study, we 
demonstrate that adding 1 at. % Re to this alloy provides further strengthening while preserving excellent cold 
machinability. Additionally, microstructure optimization was achieved through partial recrystallization, result -
ing in a heterogeneous lamella structure with alternating coarse and fine grain layers. This optimized structure 
further enhances the alloy ’ s room temperature tensile strength, exceeding 1 GPa with uniform elongation above 
10%. The designed alloy also retains a high strength of nearly 150 MPa at 1300
◦
C. Our findings demonstrate that 
Re alloying is an effective strategy for improving the mechanical properties of NbTaTi-based RHEAs.
Rapid developments of aerospace and aeronautics technology have 
imposed stringent requirements on novel high temperature structure 
materials [ 1 – 3 ]. Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs), whose melting 
point usually exceeds 2000
◦
C and exhibit excellent strength at elevated 
temperatures, provide a possible pathway for this demand [ 4 – 7 ]. 
However, the majority of RHEAs suffer from poor machinability at room 
temperature and are prone to ductile-brittle transition at reduced tem -
peratures. Therefore, strengthening RHEAs while maintaining excellent 
ductility and machinability is a prominent challenge in this field.
Tremendous research efforts have been devoted to evading the 
strength-ductility trade-off in RHEAs [ 8 – 13 ]. For example, Lei et al. 
designed nanoscale ordered oxygen complexes in TiZrHfNb alloy by 
adding 2 at.% O, which changes the dislocation shear mode from planar 
slip to wavy slip, thereby simultaneously enhancing the tensile strength 
(by 48.5%) and improving ductility (by 95.2%) [ 14 ]. Wei et al. designed

negative mixing enthalpy solid solution strategy in the HfNbTiV system 
through the aluminum alloying, resulting in hierarchical chemical 
fluctuations ranging from sub-micrometer to atomic scale. This micro -

MPa) and remarkable tensile ductility (20%) [ 16 ]. Among these studies, 
alloy composition design and microstructure tailoring turn out to be the 
most effective method. We have previously reported that 
non-equiatomic NbTaTi-based refractory alloys could achieve superior 
high temperature strength, large ductility, and excellent cold machin -
ability [ 17 , 18 ]. Based on this strategy, Cook et al. shows that the 
NbTaTi-based medium entropy alloy (MEA) can obtain a high fracture 
toughness within a wide temperature range. These evidences clearly 
indicate that the NbTaTi-based refractory alloys are promising structure 
materials that are potentially applicable at extreme conditions. The 
main difficulty of this alloy system is further improving its strength 
without compromising its deformation capability.
Re alloying has become a core feature of advanced Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys [ 1 ] due to its low diffusion coefficient [ 19 , 20 ], high 
γ / γ ′ phase segregation ratio [ 21 , 22 ], formation of nanoclusters [ 23 , 24 ] 
and solid solution strengthening. Meanwhile, Re is also an essential re -
fractory element with a melting point over 3000 
◦
C [ 1 , 25 ]. Previous 
research has explored the addition of Re to enhance the properties of 
refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs). However, it has been found that 
Re addition in RHEAs often induces brittleness and compromises uni -
form deformation ability [ 26 – 30 ]. In the current study, we demonstrate 
that adding 1 at.% Re to our previously reported NbTaTi-based alloys 
results in solid solution strengthening up to 1300
◦
C, while preserving 
the excellent cold machinability of the original alloy.

All the specimens involved in this investigation were initially cold 
rolled with a thickness reduction of 80% followed by different annealing 
treatments. Fig. 1 illustrates the corresponding characteristics of the 1Re 
alloy. The as-casted alloy can undergo over 80% cold rolling at room 
temperature without the homogenization step ( Fig. 1 (a)). At the same 
time, calculation of phase diagram in Fig. 1 (b) indicates that the 1Re 
alloy maintains single-phase body centered cubic (BCC) structure over a 
wide temperature range. The XRD results in Fig. 1 (c) confirm that 
different thermomechanical treatments do not alter the phase compo -
sition, demonstrating that the current alloys have excellent thermal 
stability.

Fig. 3 (d – f) show that deformation in the HL-structured material is 
inhomogeneous. Cold-rolled microstructures generally exhibit higher 
strength and lower plasticity. Therefore, the coarse-grained layers in 
1Re alloy have higher kernel average misorientation (KAM) values 
( Fig. 3 (e)), indicating that they undertake the strengthening role during 
deformation, thus accumulating higher density of geometrically neces -
sary dislocations. In contrast, the fine grain layers mainly contribute to 
deformation through uniform plastic flow, with significantly lower KAM 
values than coarse-grained layers. The fracture morphology of Fig. 3 (f) 
reveals that HL-structured 1Re alloy undergoes synergetic deformation 
in the form of interlayer crack nucleation and propagation, as well as 
intralayer crack inhibition. Although no significant necking is observed 
on the fracture surface, the alloy exhibits excellent uniform deformation 
properties, attributed to the synergetic mechanism between the coarse- 
grain and fine-grain layers.
To further investigate the strengthening mechanism of the 1Re alloy, 
nanoindentation and cyclic loading-unloading-reloading tests were 
performed, as shown in Fig. 4 . Fig. 4 (a – d) display hardness mapping 
results along with the corresponding IQ and IPF maps of the 1Re alloy 
with different microstructures. The hardness of the equiaxed structure 
shows an orientation-dependent distribution, while the interior hard -
ness within individual grains remains uniformly distributed. In contrast, 
the hardness of the HL sample shows an alternating distribution of high 
and low hardness layers, corresponding to the lamellar structure 
revealed by the EBSD orientation map. Notably, the HL structure dem -
onstrates higher hardness compared to the equiaxed grain structure, and 
this is due to its higher yield strength.

Fig. 4 (g, h) shows the results of cyclic loading tests for the HL structure 
at room temperature and provides a statistical analysis of the HDI 
strengthening of the 1Re alloy according to Eq. (6) . The data indicates 
that HDI stress increases rapidly in the initial stages of plastic defor -

grained and fine-grained layers. As deformation progresses, the rate of 
HDI stress increase slows down, primarily due to the growth and sliding 
of the interlayer fine grains, which accommodate the plastic flow. This 
contributes to the excellent balance of strength and ductility observed in 
the 1Re alloy.
In conclusion, our study investigates a NbTaTi-based refractory high- 
entropy alloy (RHEA) and explores the effects of alloying with 1 at. % 
Re. This addition enhances the alloy ’ s mechanical properties at both 
room and high temperatures without the brittleness issues noted in 
previous research. This improvement arises mainly from solid solution 
strengthening mechanisms, which enhance the alloy ’ s stability and 
performance over a wide temperature range.
Following partial recrystallization annealing, the alloy forms a 
distinctive heterogeneous lamellar structure with alternating layers of 
coarse and fine grains. This structure effectively refines the micro -
structure, resulting in a notable enhancement in the alloy ’ s strength- 
ductility balance. We demonstrate that, with microstructure optimiza -
tion, the alloy achieves a yield strength exceeding 1 GPa, an elongation 
of 15% and cold rolling capability above 80% at room temperature. It 
also maintains a high yield strength of 150 MPa at 1300
◦
C, reflecting a 
remarkable combination of properties at both ambient and elevated 
temperatures.
This study not only highlights the advantages of Re alloying but also 
provides valuable insights for the design and development of Re- 
containing refractory high-entropy alloys. The findings from this 
research offer a crucial reference for future innovations in materials 
designed to perform exceptionally well at both high and ambient 
temperatures.
