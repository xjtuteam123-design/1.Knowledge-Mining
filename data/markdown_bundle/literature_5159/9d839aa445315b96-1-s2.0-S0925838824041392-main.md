1. Introduction
Medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) and high-entropy alloys (HEAs), also 
be regarded as compositionally complex alloys or multi-principal- 
element alloys, are generally defined as the solid solution alloys 
comprise minimum four primary elements with equiatomic or near- 
equiatomic ratios and high configurational entropy [1 – 4,5] . The high 
mixing entropy, coupled with the distinctive design philosophy of 
MEAs/HEAs lead to some intriguing microstructures, such as the 
intrinsic lattice distortion [6,7] , short-range orderings [8 – 10] , and 
heterostructures [11,12] . These intriguing microstructures engender 
diverse distinctive properties, including high strength and excellent 
ductility [13 – 16] , great radiation resistance [17,18] , high creep resis -
tance [19] , and high corrosion resistance [20,21] . Moreover, the 
aforementioned properties can be achieved in a single MEA/HEA entity 
through specific alloy design and microstructural optimization. Conse -
quently, MEAs/HEAs have garnered significant attention as potential 
materials for demanding engineering applications in extreme service 
environments [22 – 25] .
MEAs/HEAs with a single face-centered-cubic (FCC) structure nor -
mally have good ductility but poor strength, as distinct from body- 
centered-cubic (BCC) structured MEAs/HEAs with high strength but 
poor ductility [26] . To achieve well balanced strength-ductility synergy, 
numerous investigations have demonstrated the efficacy of precipitate 
strengthening in FCC MEAs/HEAs, especially the coherent ordered L1
2 
precipitates [27 – 30] . Notably, when compared to four-component or 
five-component single-phase FCC HEAs, the three-component CoCrNi 
MEA manifests remarkable sustained hardening capability and excellent 
ductility [16,31] . Therefore, it is reasonable to expect that this 
three-component CoCrNi MEA serve as an exceptional base alloy for 
precipitation hardening. By introducing small amounts of Al and Ti into 
the CoCrNi matrix, nanoscale L1
2 
phase precipitates are formed, 
resulting in a significant increase in both yield strength and ultimate 
tensile strength [32] . Moreover, recent investigations demonstrated an

Quasi-static and dynamic mechanical responses of the as-cast, solu -

• The as-cast and aged alloys contain a large number of coherent or -
dered L1
2

volume fraction of ~13.1 % and 19.2 %, respectively, in contrast 
with the single FCC phase solution-treated (CoCrNi)

• The yield strengths of the as-cast and aged samples are significantly 
higher compared to the solution-treated sample due to precipitation 
strengthening. The as-cast and aged MEAs demonstrate a more pro -
nounced dynamic strain rate sensitivity, while the solution-treated 
alloy displays greater temperature sensitivity.
• For all alloys, the dominant deformation mechanism is dislocation 
slips, which leads to the a weak but apparent 〈 110 〉 texture along the 
loading direction. Besides, no deformation twins are detected due to

• The effects of temperature and strain rate on plastic flow of the as-
