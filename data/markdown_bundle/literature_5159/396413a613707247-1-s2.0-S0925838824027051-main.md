ABSTRACT
Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) have garnered significant attention due to their exceptionally 
high yield strengths, yet their practical applications are severely limited by high density and inherent brittleness.

1. Introduction
The development of the aerospace as well as the transportation in -
dustry has placed higher demands on metal structural materials for 
increasing environmental friendliness and fuel efficiency [1] . Over the 
last decades, in the pursuit of superior mechanical properties in a nearly 
infinite space of alloy compositions, a rapidly emerging paradigm, 
known as high entropy alloys (HEAs) or complex concentrated alloys 
(CCAs), removes the limitation of the alloys composed of one primary 
element, which greatly expands the composition space for alloys design 
[2 – 4] . Since the concept was first introduced in 2004, there have been 
many examples of CCAs, some of which have unexpected properties in 
comparison to their dilute counterparts [5 – 10] .
One of the most promising usages for CCAs is related to the pro -
duction of high-temperature structural materials. Among the CCA sys -
tems, refractory complex-concentrated alloys (RCCAs) consisting of high 
melting point elements have been noted for their high thermal stability, 
excellent mechanical strength, and softening resistance at high tem -
peratures [11 – 13] . For example, two RCCAs, MoNbTaW and MoN -
bTaVW with a single body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution phase, 
maintain an attractive yield strength over 400 MPa at 1600 
◦
C, which is 
greatly superior to commercial Inconel 718 [14] . However, their high 
density and undesirable brittleness at room temperature go against their 
malleability as well as practical applications. Considerable efforts have 
been devoted to lightweight RCCAs and the designing of the 
Ti-Zr-Nb-V-Cr (Al) system has been one of the most promising

According to previous reports, the element Vanadium (V) as one of the

Finally, the calculated and experimental results for yield strength 
and the percentage contribution of each strength contribution were 
compared as shown in Fig. 10 . Overall, the calculation results were close 
to the experimental results. However, since the solution strengthening 
model fails to consider the thermal activation effect, inhomogeneity in 
the as-cast microstructure, and local chemical order, the predicted 
values still slightly deviate from the experimental measurements. 
However, the current assessment also confirms that solid solution 
strengthening is the main strengthening mechanism for such BCC 
RCCAs. It can be seen that the solution strengthening strength increment 
increases with the increasing V concentration, which is related to the 
atomic radius of V being smaller than that of Ti and Zr and causing a 
large atomic radius misfit and severe lattice distortion. On the one hand, 
severe lattice distortions can serve to optimize the strength of the alloy, 
but at the same time, excessive atomic size mismatches can lead to 
brittle precipitation. In the V1.0, V1.2, and V1.4 alloys, this relationship 
is relatively well balanced, thus resulting in superior overall mechanical 
properties.
