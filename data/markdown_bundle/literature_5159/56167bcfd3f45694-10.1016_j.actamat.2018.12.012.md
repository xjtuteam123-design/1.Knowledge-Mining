Abstract 15 
The improvement in strength is usually accompanied by ductility loss in structural materials, which 16 
is a long-standing conflict referred as the strengt h-ductility trade-off. Here we present a 17 
heterogeneous-structures-architecting strategy, in which we design bulk high-entropy alloys with 18 
the largely-enhanced strength-ductility trade-off, possessing a yield strength of 711 MPa, a tensile 19 
strength of 928 MPa, and a uniform elongation of 30 .3 %. Such an enhancement of the 20 
strength-ductility trade-off is due to the microstr ucture comprised with a combination of the 21 
non-recrystallized and recrystallized grains arrang ed in complex heterogeneous structures with a 22 
characteristic dimension spanning from the submicro n scale to the coarse-sized scale. The 23 
heterogeneous structures in the high-entropy alloy are produced by cold-rolling, followed by 24 
intermediate-temperature-annealing. Our results dem onstrate that heterogeneous designs can be 25 
accomplished effectively by simple thermal treatments, which offer a design strategy towards a new 26 
generation of high-strength and high-ductility high-entropy alloys. 27

1. Introduction 1 
The development of more efficient and sustainable t echnologies requires novel 2 
high-performance structural materials with excellen tly-comprehensive properties, such as high 3 
strength, high ductility, and high fatigue resistan ce. To achieve this feature, many new alloys are 4 
being sustainably developed through tuning their co mpositions or applying new processing routines 5 
[1]. Usually, conventional alloys are designed, typ ically based on one or two principal elements 6 
with further optimizing performances by adding rela tively-small amounts of other elements [2]. 7 
Recently, a novel philosophy to combine elements fo r a new alloy development, i.e., mixing five or 8 
more elements together in equimolar or near-equimol ar concentrations, is developed [3–6]. A new 9 
class of materials, high-entropy alloys (HEAs), has been synthesized [6–12]. The effect of the 10 
configurational entropy on the phase formation is m ore significant than that of the mixing enthalpy, 11 
which causes some HEAs to form simple solid solutio ns with face-centered-cubic (fcc), or 12 
body-centered-cubic (bcc), or hexagonal-close-packe d (hcp) structures, rather than brittle 13 
intermetallics [4–10]. These HEAs are reported to s how more attractive mechanical performances, 14 
such as ultrahigh fracture toughness [13], high ten sile strength combined high ductility [14,15], 15 
high fatigue resistance [16,17], remarkable high-te mperature strength [18,19], and more resistance 16 
to irradiation or severe wear [20,21]. 17 
Generally, fcc-single-phase HEAs with fully-recrystallized microstructures, such as CoCrFeNi 18 
[22], CoCrFeMnNi [13,14], and Al 0.1 CoCrFeNi alloy [23], exhibit a good ductility but a low yield 19 
strength due to their fcc structural characteristic s [8,61]. For example, the yield strength of a 20 
fully-recrystallized CoCrFeMnNi alloy with a grain size of 4.4 µm was 350 MPa [14], reaching the 21 
limit of Hall-Petch strengthening by grain refineme nt down to the micrometer scale [8–10]. 22 
Regarding the general correlation between the yield strength and fatigue limit [17,24,39], an 23 
important challenge is to increase the yield streng th of HEAs without or less compromising 24 
ductility loss. Such low yield strength could be re markably improved to be higher than 1 GPa 25 
through cold-working or nanocrystallization [25,26] . Unfortunately, as a consequence, the global 26 
elongation dropped rapidly below 5 % because a tens ion instability developed immediately after 27 
yielding, which is a typical behavior in convention al alloys. This trend is a long-standing dilemma 28 
of the strength-ductility trade-off in materials science [13,15,24,27–46]. 29 
Recently, to overcome the strength-ductility trade- off, many measures are developed 30

3. Results 5 
3.1. Experimental approach for heterogeneous structures 6 
After cold-working, HEAs (CW-HEAs) always lose most of their strength when they are 7 
heated to a sufficiently-high temperature because s train-free new grains can be formed and replace 8 
the deformed grains entirely [51]. Intermediate-tem perature-annealing can lead to the CW-HEAs to 9 
be partially-recrystallized, which then may generat e recrystallized grains and non-recrystallized 10 
grains in the CW-HEAs. Usually, strain hardening du e to the non-recrystallized grains can 11 
contribute to a high strength, while the recrystall ized grains can increase ductility. CW-HEAs 12 
usually require a relatively high-temperature annea ling to achieve full recrystallization, and exhibit 13 
a high onset of a grain-coarsening temperature [47– 49]. For instance, the full recrystallization 14 
temperature and the grain-coarsening temperature fo r CoCrFeMnNi alloys are as high as 0.52 ~ 15 
0.60 Tm, and 0.63 ~ 0.67 Tm, respectively [47,48]. These relatively-high tempe ratures enable HEAs 16 
to have a relatively-wide ‘intermediate’ temperatur e range for partial recrystallization. The slow 17 
grain-coarsening behavior resulting from the high v alues of the activation energy, Q, and 18 
grain-growth exponent, n, (for CoCrFeMnNi alloys, Q = 321.7 kJ/mol, and n = 3) [47,49] leads to 19 
the formation of a very fine recrystallized microst ructure. Therefore, in the present study, 20 
CW-HEAs are partially-recrystallized at the interme diate temperatures. After partial 21 
recrystallization, the CW-HEAs are expected to have a complex heterogeneous structure. The idea 22 
to design a heterogeneous structure is motivated by the heterogeneities of the microscopic recovery 23 
and recrystallization kinetics in the deformed HEAs owing to the inhomogeneous nature of plastic 24 
deformation during cold working (such as rolling, e xtrusion, etc.) at a micro scale [51]. We exploit 25 
such heterogeneities, combined with the slow grain- coarsening kinetics to hatch the heterogeneities 26 
in both the non-recrystallized grains (resulting in heterogeneous grain sizes and heterogeneous 27 
dislocation densities), and the recrystallized grai ns (resulting in heterogeneous grains with a wide 28 
continuous size range). 29

3.4. Effective & back stresses 8 
Upon straining, the heterogeneity-arrangement of the grains with th e heterogeneous size in the 9 
873 K-annealed HEA causes an inhomogeneous deformat ion, and thus induces a back stress. 10 
Furthermore, the pre-existed heterogeneous dislocat ions lead to dislocation hardening, and thus 11 
effective stress [42,44,65]. To characterize the re ason of the high yield strength and the high strain 12 
hardening behavior in the 873 K-annealed HEA, and t he contributions of the effective and the back 13 
stresses to the flow stress, loading-unloading-relo ading tests in different strain-hardening regimes 14 
were performed to investigate the Bauchinger effect . Interestingly, both curves for the 873 and 15 
1,073 K-annealed HEAs exhibit obvious hysteresis lo op in each LUR cycle, even near the yielding 16 
point (Figs. 5a – b), meaning a strong Bauchinger e ffect [44,65]. The calculated reverse plastic 17 
strains of the 873 and 1,073 K-annealed HEA increas e with straining to 6 and 10 %, respectively, 18 
then decrease with further straining, which indicat es that the Bauchinger effect is strong in the 19

5. Conclusion 14 
We develop a methodology to design and fabricate HE As with a complex heterogeneous 15 
structure through cold working, followed by interme diate-temperature-annealing. The 16 
heterogeneous-designed 873 K-annealed CW-HEA displa ys a unique combination of high strength 17 
and high ductility, which exceeds, to our knowledge, strength-ductility properties reported on HEAs 18 
so far. Our results demonstrate that heterogeneous structures can be effectively introduced in HEAs, 19 
which have been proven to be difficult to replicate synthetically previously. The method of cold 20 
rolling and then annealing at intermediate temperat ures applied in this study can be used to 21 
synthesize many heterogeneous-structured HEAs as we ll as conventional alloys with the slow 22 
grain-coarsening kinetics upon annealing, and may b e easily adapted to the current industrial 23 
process and, hence, has the great potential for lar ge-scale applications. If the processing parameters 24 
(percent in the thickness reduction, annealing temp erature and time, heat-treatment rate, cooling 25 
rate, etc.) are optimized, the combination of ducti lity and strength can be further improved even 26 
beyond the levels reported in this study. 27
