ABSTRACT
Conventional niobium alloys encounter a bottleneck in synergistically optimizing high-temperature strength and 
room-temperature plasticity. To tackle this issue, considering the positive impacts of Mo and W elements on the 
solid solution strengthening of NbMoWTa alloys and the synergistic strength-plasticity enhancement effect of the 
eutectic structure, this paper integrates multi-principal element alloy design and eutectic carbide strengthening 
to prepare a series of Nb
2
MoWC
x 
alloys with different C addition, and their microstructures and compression 
properties are investigated. All Nb
2
MoWC
x 
niobium alloys are hypoeutectic alloys consisting of body-centered 
cubic (BCC) primary solid solution phase and layered eutectic structure composed of BCC and carbide phases. 
Increasing C addition, the (Mo + W) solid solution amount in the BCC phase increases, the grain size of the BCC 
primary phase decreases, and the volume fraction of the carbide phase increases. The Nb
2
MoWC
0.5 
alloy exhibits 
good plasticity with fracture strain of 11.2 ± 0.3 %, while the Nb
2
MoWC
0.6 
alloy has comprehensive strength- 
plasticity with a yield strength of 1137.6 ± 19.8 MPa and fracture strain of 8.7 ± 0.2 %. The strengthening 
mechanism is contributed by the synthetic of solid solution strengthening, fine grain strengthening, and second- 
phase strengthening, while the plasticity is improved by grain refinement of BCC primary phase and well-bonded 
semi-coherent interface. This work provides a theoretical basis for the design and preparation of high-strength 
and tough refractory alloys.
1. Introduction
Niobium alloys, as a high-temperature structural material, have 
important application values in the fields of aerospace, nuclear energy 
industry, and extreme service environments [1 – 3] . Traditional niobium 
alloys (such as Nb-W and Nb-Zr systems) improve their 
high-temperature strength by adding a single or small amount of 
alloying elements or ceramic phases [4 – 6] . However, the traditional 
alloy design concept is difficult to meet performance requirements — low 
addition of alloy elements and carbides limits the strength of the alloy, 
while excessive increase in brittleness leads to poor plasticity — making 
it difficult to improve the strength and plasticity of the alloy. With the 
increasingly stringent requirements for the comprehensive performance 
of materials such as high-temperature resistance, good 
room-temperature plasticity in cutting-edge fields such as supersonic 
aircraft and nuclear fusion reactors, it is urgent to achieve performance 
improvement through alloy system innovation.
The multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) — usually forming single 
solid solution phase — could suppress the precipitation of harmful phases 
and achieve good performance utilizing the “ cocktail effect ” [7 – 9] . 
Research has shown that NbMoWTa and NbMoWTaV MPEAs can 
maintain excellent high-temperature yield strengths of above 400 MPa 
at 1673 K [10,11] . However, the inherent small number of slip systems 
in BCC alloys and the strong metal bonding of refractory metals result in

2. Experimental procedures
High-purity powders of Nb, Mo, W, and C were prepared to syn -
thesize the Nb
2
MoWC
x 
alloys with different C addition by arc melting 
technology. The molar ratio of added Nb, Mo, W, and C was 2: 1: 1: x. 
The powders were mixed and melted through arc melting in a vacuum 
environment, combined with repeated flipping and remelting for 
4 – 6 times to eliminate component segregation. The size of the prepared 
alloys was 25 mm in diameter and 12 mm in height.
The samples for microstructure observation were cut using wire 
cutting and then polished to observe the microstructure. The phase 
composition was determined by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker D8 
Advance) with a Cu target. The scanning speed is 1 
◦
/min. The 
microstructure and chemical compositions were observed by scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM, Quanta 2250) with a backscatter probe (BSE) 
and energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). To obtain the chemical 
composition of the carbide phase as accurately as possible, the coarse 
carbide particles with a micrometer scale of above 1 μ m were chosen for 
composition measurement. Electron Backscattered Diffraction (EBSD) 
experiments were performed using the SEM. Grind a thin sheet with a 
diameter of 3 mm to below 50 μ m, perform thinning treatment using an 
ion thinning instrument, and observe the fine microstructure and 
composition using transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Talos 
F200S) and EDS. Use a universal testing machine (Instron 5966) to test 
the room-temperature compression performance of a cylindrical object 
with a diameter of 2 mm and a height of 4 mm. The compressive strain 
was tested with an extensometer and the data presented in this study 
have been corrected accordingly [16] .

4. Discussion
The as-cast microstructure of the Nb
2
MoWC
x 
alloys shown in Fig. 2
shows that the compositions are lying in the hypoeutectic region, in 
which the BCC primary phase preferentially nucleates and grows first 
during high-temperature solidification. As the liquidus temperature 
decreases to the eutectic point, BCC and MC phases synergistically 
nucleate and grow, alternately arranged to form a eutectic structure. 
Thus, the microstructure of the Nb
2
MoWC
x 
alloys consists of a primary 
BCC phase and a layered eutectic structure composed of BCC and MC 
phases. Meanwhile, during the solidification process, in order to reduce 
the interfacial energy of the heterogeneous interface, BCC and MC 
phases form a certain K-S orientation relationship for synergistic 
nucleation and growth. In the Nb
2
MoWC
x 
alloys, the fracture strain 
increases first and then decreases with C addition increasing. The 
Nb
2
MoWC
0.5 
alloy exhibits the exceptionally high fracture strain, which 
could be mainly attributable to well-bonded interface and grain refine -
ment of primary BCC phase. The BCC-MC interface has low energy and 
high stability, which is strong bonding and remains stable during high 
stress compression, reducing the premature failure of high-energy het -
erogeneous interfaces. In addition, numerous microcracks originate in 
the brittle MC phase, whereas these microcrack tips are inhibited at the 
BCC phase at the interface ( Fig. 6 ), which effectively relieve the inter -
facial stress and simultaneously slow down crack propagation [12] . 
Thus, the low-energy BCC-carbide interface is conducive to improving 
the strength and plasticity of alloys [22 – 24] . Meanwhile, increasing C 
addition causes grain refinement of the primary BCC phase ( Fig. 2 ), and

5. Conclusions
In this study, a series of Nb
2
MoWC
x 
niobium alloys with different C 
addition were prepared. The microstructure and compression properties 
of the alloys was studied to clarify the structural and performance 
evolution of carbide reinforced multicomponent niobium alloys. The 
conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(1) The Nb
2
MoWC
x 
niobium alloys are composed of BCC primary 
phase and layed eutectic structure composed of BCC and MC 
phases. Increasing C addition, the (Mo + W) addition in the BCC 
phase increases, the grain size of the BCC primary phase de -
creases, and the volume fracture of the MC phase increases.
(2) Increasing C addition, the yield strength of the Nb
2
MoWC
x 
alloys 
increases, while the fracture strain first increases and then de -
creases. The Nb
2
MoWC
0.5 
alloy exhibits good plasticity with 
fracture strain of 11.2 ± 0.3 %, and improved yield strength of 
949.3 ± 15.6 MPa. The Nb
2
MoWC
0.6 
alloy has comprehensive 
strength-plasticity with yield strength of 1137.6 ± 19.8 MPa and 
fracture strain of 8.7 ± 0.2 %.
(3) The Nb
2
MoWC
x 
alloys are reinforced by solid solution strength -
ening, fine grain strengthening, and second-phase strengthening, 
while grain refinement of BCC primary phase and well-bonded 
semi-coherent phase interface simultaneously improve the 
plasticity.
(4) The Nb
2
MoWC
0.5 
and Nb
2
MoWC
0.6 
alloys exhibit excellent high- 
temperature strength and good room-temperature plasticity, 
which could be well processed at room temperature and applied 
as a high-temperature resistance material in key hot-end com -
ponents, such as rocket engine nozzle extensions. Capitalizing on 
the composition-tuning strategies and microstructural engineer -
ing approaches unveiled herein, refractory alloys can surmount 
the long-standing strength – ductility trade-off, thereby broad -
ening their applicability in extreme environments.
