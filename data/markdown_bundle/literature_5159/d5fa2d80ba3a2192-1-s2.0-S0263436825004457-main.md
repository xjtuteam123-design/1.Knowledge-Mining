ABSTRACT
The microstructure evolution, mechanical properties and electrochemical corrosion resistance of Nb-35Ti-6Al- 
5Cr-8V-xC (x = 0, 4, 8, 10, 15 (at.%)) alloys with TiC whiskers addition prepared using spark plasma sinter -
ing (SPS) process were investigated. The Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr-8V alloy exhibited a single-phase Nbss, whereas the 
alloys with TiC whiskers consisted of Nbss and carbide phases. Nb, Cr, and V diffuse into the TiC lattice, forming 
a multi-component MC solid solution (M = Nb, Ti, V, Cr). On the macroscopic scale, the MC phase appears 
uniformly distributed; however, at the microscopic level, it shows inhomogeneities arising from agglomeration 
and grain boundary enrichment. Tensile tests indicated that the ultimate strength of the alloys increased pro -
gressively with TiC whiskers content increase, reaching optimal mechanical performance at 10 at.%. The Nb- 
35Ti-6Al-5Cr-8V-10C alloy exhibited maximum tensile strengths of 525.11 ± 20.65 MPa (room temperature), 
379.79 ± 22.38 MPa (900 
◦
C), and 300.98 ± 15.43 MPa(1000 
◦
C). Compression testing revealed a positive 
correlation between the compressive strength of the alloys and their TiC whiskers content. The Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr- 
8 V-15C alloy demonstrated the maximum compressive strength, with values of 1683.35 ± 37.16 MPa at room 
temperature and 472.31 ± 25.65 MPa at 1000 
◦
C. The increase in compressive strength was attributed to the 
enhanced formation and interconnection of carbides with higher TiC whiskers content. In subsequent electro -
chemical corrosion tests, the Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr-8V-15C alloy shows the highest corrosion resistance among the 
tested alloys at a current density of 0.114 μ A/cm
2
.

elemental diffusion. However, it requires stricter control over powder 
flowability and compositional uniformity. The primary toughening 
mechanisms of the whiskers included whisker-bridging, whisker pullout 
and crack deflection. The macroscopic mechanical properties of the 
material were governed by its microstructure and the dynamic evolution 
of its deformation, damage, and failure mechanisms. The Nb-35Ti-6Al- 
5Cr-8V-xC alloys achieved high-temperature strength owing to the 
synergistic effect of a refined matrix microstructure and a dispersion- 
strengthened reinforcing phase. Therefore, the replacement of carbide 
particles in niobium alloys with submicron TiC whiskers-although 
limited to laboratory could effectively enhance the fracture toughness 
of second-phase reinforced niobium alloys. This improvement was 
attributed to the enhanced toughening capacity of TiC whiskers, which 
alleviated the brittleness of reinforcement particles and decreased their 
tendency to crack during deformation. Based on the design concept of 
multi-phase composition and function sharing, this study employed the 
high-toughness Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr-8V alloy as the matrix and incorpo -
rated submicron one-dimensional TiC whiskers as the reinforcing phase. 
A multi-phase niobium alloy with fine grains and uniformly dispersed 
whiskers was synthesized via powder metallurgy. Meanwhile, the uni -
formly dispersed TiC whiskers improved the strength via second-phase 
strengthening. During alloy deformation, mechanisms such as whisker 
bridging, whisker pullout, and crack deflection helped to mitigate the 
brittleness of the second-phase reinforcements, thereby enhancing the 
overall mechanical performance of the alloy. These mechanisms could 
decrease the detrimental effect of second-phase reinforcements on 
room-temperature fracture toughness and improved the multi-phase 
alloy ’ s overall toughness. By designing the microstructure and eluci -
dating the mechanisms of microstructural regulation and toughening in 
carbide-reinforced niobium alloys, this study aimed to achieve a balance 
between strength and toughness.
Traditional niobium-based alloys are typically strengthened through 
simple binary carbides (such as NbC or TiC) or solid solution strength -
ening. In contrast, the Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr-8V-xC system employs a new 
strengthening strategy: the introduction of laboratory-synthesized TiC 
whiskers via spark plasma sintering (SPS). Unlike conventional casting 
or powder metallurgy, SPS facilitates rapid densification at reduced 
sintering temperatures, thereby preserving a uniform carbide distribu -
tion and inhibiting excessive grain growth. Significantly, the interdif -
fusion of TiC whiskers during sintering encourage the formation of 
multi-component MC-type carbides (where M = Nb, Ti, Cr, V). This 
leads to a unique microstructure comprising a niobium-based solid so -
lution (Nbss) matrix reinforced with composite MC carbides, resulting in 
improved interfacial stability and enhanced load-bearing capacity. This 
study systematically investigates the evolution of microstructure, 
morphology, mechanical properties, and corrosion resistance of com -
posite powders containing varying contents of TiC whiskers and metal 
particles after mechanical alloying and spark plasma sintering (SPS). 
The correlation between microstructural evolution and the macroscopic 
mechanical behavior of the deformed alloy was investigated, enabling 
effective microstructural design.
2. Experimental procedures
2.1. Material synthesis
Laboratory-synthesized TiC whiskers and high-purity Nb, Ti, Al, Cr, 
and V powders (purchased from Hebei Jincaishengte Metal Materials 
Co., Ltd.) were used as raw materials. The powders were homogenized in 
planetary ball mill (ZQM-P2). The designed compositions were Nb-35Ti- 
6Al-5Cr-8V (0C), Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr -8V-4C (4C), Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr-8V-8C 
(8C), Nb-35Ti-6Al- 5Cr-8V-10C (10C) and Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr-8V-15C 
(15C). Precise mass measurements were performed inside an argon- 
filled glove box (MIKROUNA UPURE) to prevent oxidation. Each mill -
ing batch consisted of 180 g of mixed powders loaded into a 250 mL 
stainless steel jar. To minimize oxidation during milling, the ball mill jar 
contained the powder underwent 3 – 5 cycles of argon evacuation and 
back filling. The mass ratio of the balls with diameters of 6 mm and 2 
mm was 3:1. The material of the sphere is GCr15. The ratio of balls-to- 
material is 5:1. The ball mill process was conducted at 300 rpm for 6 h 
under an inert atmosphere. The alloy powder was sintered in a spark 
plasma sintering (SPS, SPS-60) furnace under a vacuum of less than 
10
 2

44 mm. A pre-pressure of 10 MPa was applied in the vacuum chamber 
during sintering. The temperature was initially increased at a rate of

◦

MPa, and the sample was held at that temperature for 10 min. After the 
holding time, the sample was cooled in the furnace.

2.4. Analysis of whisker characteristics after ball milling
Whiskers were not found in either the microscopic morphology or 
the fracture morphology. This absence warrants further discussion in the 
present study. Fig. 3 shows the states of TiC whiskers before and after 
ball milling. It can be seen that the long-neck ratio of TiC whiskers is 
significantly reduced after ball milling (yellow circle). After ball milling, 
TiC changes from whisker form to long strip form, losing its whisker 
characteristics. Ball milling is one of the important reasons for the 
disappearance of whisker characteristics. In addition, the diffusion of 
elements during sintering also leads to the further disappearance of 
whiskers.
3. Results and discussions
3.1. Phase analysis
3.1.1. XRD analysis
Fig. 4 presents the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of five alloys. The 
0C alloy consists of single-phase niobium-based solid solution (Nbss). 
The smaller atoms (Ti, Al, Cr, V) enter the Nb lattice, forming a niobium- 
based solid solution (Nbss). This leads to a reduction in the lattice 
parameter of the Nb. The lattice parameters of the Nbss phase in the five 
alloys were determined to be 0.3233 nm (0C), 0.3231 nm (4C), 0.3234 
nm (8C), 0.3237 nm (10C), and 0.3232 nm (15C). These values are all 
significantly lower than the standard lattice constant of body-centered 
cubic Nb (0.3294 nm, PDF: 01 – 074-7072). Furthermore, the results 
indicate that the addition of TiC whiskers has no significant effect on the 
lattice parameters of the Nbss phase. In addition, it can be found that the 
Nbss phase peak in C-containing alloys is lower than that in 0C alloy. 
The peak intensity of the MC phase is positively correlated with the TiC 
whisker content in the C-containing alloy. These phenomena are closely 
related to the varying proportions of phase content in the alloy [ 28 ]. 
Consequently, it can be inferred that the volume fraction of the MC 
phase increases while that of the Nbss phase decreases with the 
increasing addition of TiC whiskers.
The 4C, 8C, 10C and 15C alloys consists of Nbss and MC-type car -
bides (M = Nb, Ti, Cr, V). The MC carbides are formed through solid- 
solution diffusion between Nb, Cr, and V atoms and TiC [ 29 – 31 ]. TiC 
and NbC are interstitial phases with a simple cubic lattice structure, in 
which C atoms occupy octahedral interstitial sites, forming a face- 
centered cubic (FCC) crystal structure similar to that of sodium chlo -
ride (NaCl). Both TiC and NbC exhibit the same crystal structure and 
similar lattice constants. This similarity in crystal structure bestows 
upon them the property of unlimited solubility during the formation of 
substitutional solid solutions. The calculated lattice parameters of the 
MC phase in the alloys are 0.4266 nm (4C), 0.4285 nm (8C), 0.4292 nm 
(10C), and 0.4311 nm (15C). The increase in the lattice parameter re -
sults in a leftward shift of the XRD diffraction peaks. All values are lower 
than the lattice constant of stoichiometric TiC (0.4326 nm, PDF: 04 – 004- 
8302), This indicates that the smaller elements (Cr, V) are more abun -
dant in the MC phase than the Nb element. With increasing TiC whiskers 
content, the lattice parameter of the MC phase gradually increases and 
approaches that of pure TiC. The theoretical densities of the five alloys 
were calculated as 6.660 g/cm

3.1.2. Microstructure analysis
Fig. 5 displays the microstructure of five alloys at low magnification. 
In the analysis of Fig. 4 , the 0C alloy consists of a single-phase Nbss. This 
single-phase nature is further supported by the uniform microstructure 
observed in Fig. 5 (a). But the microstructure displays two distinct 
contrast zones: white and gray-white phases. In the follow-up analysis, it 
is found that both regions are Nbss phases with different Nb contents. As 
illustrated in Fig. 5 (b-e) and corroborated by XRD analysis in Fig. 4 , the 
introduction of TiC whiskers results in a two-phase structure with the 
Nbss and MC carbides. The 4C alloy exhibits large bright-phase regions 
alongside uniformly distributed fine dark-phase particles. In Fig. 5 (b), 
the minor dark phase is identified as MC carbides at low TiC whiskers 
content. For the alloys of higher TiC whiskers contents, the micro -
structure remains biphasic, with bright Nbss matrix and dark MC car -
bides ( Fig. 5 (c-e)). Notably, an increase in TiC whiskers content results 
in a significant increase in the volume fraction of MC carbides in Fig. 5 . 
The volume fraction of MC carbides increases with the TiC whiskers 
content (approximately 10.06 %, 19.58 %, 22.47 %, and 28.19 % for the 
4C, 8C, 10C, and 15C alloys, respectively). The discontinuously rein -
forced structure is characterized by a non-uniform distribution of TiC 
within the matrix ( Fig. 5 (b-e)); a similar finding has been reported 
previously [ 32 ]. The MC carbides predominantly form blocky pre -
cipitates uniformly distributed within the Nbss phase. Excessive MC 
carbides formation in Fig. 5 (e) results in interconnected blocky car -
bides, ultimately forming a three-dimensional carbide network.

and cooling, short time of heat preservation and pressure preservation, 
and easy to produce high density samples. Therefore, the rapid cooling

allowing the samples to exhibit plastic deformation capability during 
high-temperature tensile testing.

3.3.2. Corrosion surface analysis
Fig. 17 shows the SEM images of the microstructure alloys after 
corrosion in a 3.5 wt% NaCl solution at room temperature. The 0C alloy 
consists of a single-phase Nbss matrix, within which niobium-rich zones 
are observed. Compared with the Nb-rich regions, the normal Nbss areas 
contain relatively higher amounts of Ti and Cr. Such compositional in -
homogeneity alters the local open-circuit potential [ 51 ]. When a po -
tential difference exists between adjacent regions, the more active 
region serves as the anodic site for dissolution, whereas the more inert 
region functions as the cathode [ 52 ]. The Nb-rich zones form a potential 
difference with the surrounding Ti/Cr-enriched regions, thereby giving 
rise to micro-galvanic couples [ 53 ]. Previous studies have reported that 
in alloys with significant Nb segregation, local dissolution preferentially 
occurs within Nb-rich regions, where they act as micro-anodes [ 54 , 55 ]. 
Moreover, the passive films formed on Nb-rich regions exhibit inferior 
protective and self-repairing capabilities compared with those formed 
on Ti/Cr-rich regions. This is attributed to the Pilling-Bedworth (PB) 
ratios of TiO ₂ and Cr ₂ O ₃ (1.73 and 2.07, respectively), which are lower

4. Conclusion
In this study, a series of Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr-8V (0C), Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr- 
8V-4C (4C), Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr-8V-8C (8C), Nb-35Ti-6Al-5Cr-8V-10C 
(10C), Nb-35Ti-6Al- 5Cr-8V-15C (15C) alloys with varying TiC whis -
kers contents were prepared, and microstructural evolution, mechanical 
properties and corrosion resistance of alloys were discussed. Based on 
the results, conclusions are drawn as follows. 
1. The 0C alloy is composed of a single phase of Nbss. The 4C, 8C, 10C 
and 15C alloys consist of Nbss and MC phases. The volume fraction of 
the MC carbides phase increases with increasing TiC whiskers con -
tent, and the formation of MC carbides is accompanied by a carbon 
deficiency phenomenon. In the TEM image, the Nbss phase and 
carbides are observed to be interleaved. The sizes of the carbides 
vary, with the smallest observed carbide being around 0.5 μ m and 
the largest reaching up to 6 μ m.
2. As the content of TiC whiskers increases to 10 at.%, the ultimate 
tensile strength of the alloy rises and reaches its peak. The content of 
TiC whiskers increases to 15 at.%, the compressive strength of the 
alloy reaches its peak. The Nbss phase serves as the primary site for 
energy dissipation, accommodating most plastic deformation, while 
the MC carbide phase breaks after bearing pressure.
3. The corrosion resistance of the material is related to the niobium-rich 
zone. As the content of TiC increases, the niobium-rich zone rela -
tively become smaller, thereby improving the corrosion resistance. 
The 15C alloy exhibits the best corrosion resistance in the electro -
chemical corrosion experiments. Its corrosion potential is 0.501 V 
and the corrosion current density is 0.114 μ A/cm
2
.
