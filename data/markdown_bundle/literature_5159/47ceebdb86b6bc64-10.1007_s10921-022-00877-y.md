Quantifying the Effects of Carbides and Pores on Fatigue Damages
of Ni-Based Single Crystal Superalloys at Elevated Temperature Using
X-Ray CT Scans
Keli Liu 1 · Chi Zhang 1 · Junsheng Wang 1,2 · Chengpeng Xue 1 · Bing Wang 1 · Mingshan Zhang 3 ·
Yanhong Yang 4 · Yizhou Zhou 4
Received: 24 December 2021 / Accepted: 4 May 2022 / Published online: 31 May 2022
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2022
Abstract
Although the effects of pores and carbides on the high temperature fatigue performance of nickel-based single crystal
superalloys have been studied for decades, few studies have statistically compared their damage effects and identiﬁed the
most detrimental factors. X-ray computed tomography has been used to collect the microstructure variations while the fatigue
damage happens at high temperature. Combining image registration and deep learning algorithm, both carbides and pores
have been extracted and quantiﬁed by a new damage factor. It shows that pores are more harmful than carbides during
crack initiation, and carbides are more signiﬁcant than pores during the crack propagation stage at elevated temperatures.
Furthermore, by developing a multiple linear regression model, the damage effects of pore size, morphology, spacing, and
distance to the sample surface on the crack initiation and propagation stage were differentiated. It is found that pore spacing
is the most important factor for crack initiation.
Keywords Ni-based superalloys · High temperature fatigue · X-CT · Carbide · Porosity

properties [1, 2]. However, the carbides and pores that form
during the casting process of nickel alloys often induce
fatigue cracks, resulting in the decrease in mechanical per-

In our work, XCT with the sub-micrometers resolution has
been used to investigate the variations of pores and carbides
in a nickel-based single crystal superalloy. The as-cast speci-
men is scanned before and after the high temperature fatigue
test. The pores are segmented by the interactive thresholding
method and traced by the rigid registration algorithm. The
carbides were segmented by a U-Net CNN model. A new
damage factor is designed to quantify the damage effects of
pores and carbides, For the ﬁrst time, the high temperature
fatigue damage effects of pores and carbides are quantita-
tively compared, and the most signiﬁcant characteristic of
them are identiﬁed.
2 Experimental Methods
2.1 Materials and Samples
In this study, the as-cast nickel-based single crystal DD5
superalloy rod had been used, which was provided by the
Institute of Metal Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences.
The composition of the alloy was listed in Table 1.A ss h o w n

the axial direction of the DD5 rod, and the region of interest
was located in the middle section of the sample. The sam-
ples were grounded with SiC sandpaper and polished with the
1.0 µm diamond paste to remove surface impurities before
the fatigue test.
2.2 Microstructure Characterization
A Zeiss Xradia V ersa 520 was used to perform X-CT scan-
ning on the specimen before and after the high temperature
fatigue test. It was rotated by 180° to obtain 801 projection
images with a spatial resolution of 1.5 µm during the 3.5 h
scanning process. The scanning voltage and the power were
set at 150 kV and 10 W with a 15 s exposure time. The dis-
tance from the sample to the X-ray source and the distance
to the detector was 16.33 mm and 20.51 mm, respectively.
The XCT measurement parameters are listed in Table 2.
2.3 Image Processing
As shown in Fig. 2a, it was difﬁcult to extract carbides
by the threshold segmentation method because of the
blurred boundary of carbides and the unbalanced distribu-
tion of brightness and contrast in two-dimensional XCT
slices. While the U-Net CNN (convolutional neural network)
in ORS Dragonﬂy (Object Research Systems, Montreal,
Canada) [44] could successfully segment the carbide. In this
study, the images were ﬁrstly preprocessed by adjusting the
brightness and contrast of the raw two-dimensional XCT
transverse slices, as shown in Fig. 2a and b. Then, to seg-
ment the carbides, twenty images that had been manually
marked and carbides were selected as input images for train-
ing the U-net CNN model. The input images were enhanced
10 times by some methods, such as scaling (0.9 to 1.1),
shearing (maximum 2°), horizontal and vertical ﬂipping, and
rotation (maximum 180°). A three-layer depth U-Net CNN
was used to train the input image datasets, the batch size,
epochs, path size, and stride ratio were set as 128, 100, 32,
and 0.25, respectively. The Dice Loss and Adadelta algo-
rithms were used to optimize the network. After training, the
carbides of the raw two-dimensional slices were marked and
converted into binary images, and then was reconstructed and
visualized by Avizo software (ThermoFisher Scientiﬁc), as
shown in Fig. 2c and d.
To investigate the variations of the pores before and after
the fatigue test, we aligned the X-CT reconstruction images
with the means of drift correction method. The pores were
extracted by the thresholding method due to the signiﬁcant
difference in the greyscale values of pores and matrix. The
minimum considered pore size was chosen to be > 3.72 µm.

the sample were marked as the boundary of the region of

2.4 High Temperature Fatigue Test
The Instron 8850 biaxial versatile servohydraulic material
testing system had been used to perform the high temperature
fatigue test. This experiment was carried out along the axis
of the DD5 single crystal rod at 900 °C. The constant stress
was 750 MPa with a tensile/compression load ratio of 0.1.
The specimen was loaded at a 15 Hz sine waveform and was
fractured at 126,866 fatigue cycles.

To investigate the effects of pores and carbides on the
fatigue damage behavior in nickel-based single crystal super-
alloy, the fractography observation of the sample was per-
formed by SEM, as shown in Fig. 4. It showed the entire
fracture surface of the failed sample in Fig. 4a. This fracture
surface could be divided into three regions according to the
crack variation stages, which were the crack initiation zone,
crack propagation zone, and ﬁnal fracture zone, respectively.
In the initiation zone in Fig. 4b, it showed that two clusters of
pores and an isolated large pore acted as the crack sources,
and the crack propagation path around them had changed
signiﬁcantly. In the crack propagation zone in Fig. 4c, the
fact that there was no obvious deformation around the pores
and carbides suggested that they had a limited effects on
the crack propagation path. The fracture surface was ﬂat in
the crack propagation zone, and fatigue cracks mainly prop-
agated along the {111} plane, although the carbides were
evenly distributed on the entire fracture surface, and there
were secondary cracks around the pores. These behaviors
indicated that the crystal structure was the main factor affect-
ing the crack propagation path during the high-temperature
fatigue test. In the ﬁnal fracture zone, the fracture surface
in Fig. 4d was smooth. Some pores were present at the ini-
tiation site of the ﬁnal surface and there were no carbides
around the secondary crack or deformed fracture surfaces. In
summary, the fatigue cracks started initially from the pores
around the surface, and propagated along the closed-packed
plane, resulting in interconnecting with the sample surface.
However, based on the fractography observation, it is hard to
quantitatively study the damage mechanism of the carbides
and pores.
3.2 Fracture Surface Roughness Factor
Through two-dimensional observation, the researchers had
found that the size of the pores and the distance to the sam-
ple surface could be the main factors to estimate the effect
of pores on fatigue damage [ 7, 8]. However, these two-
dimensional characteristics could not be directedly used as a
criterion to determine the fatigue damage effects of the pores.
Fortunately, the X-CT technique could extract more charac-
teristics of a fracture surface and quantify the inﬂuence of
microstructure on fatigue damage [ 24]. To further investi-
gate the effects of pores and carbides on the fracture surface
morphology, the fracture surface roughness factor (FSR) was
proposed [36, 37]. For instance, to examine and quantify the
damaging effect of pores, we initially reconstructed the dis-
tribution of pores and extracted the fracture surfaces around
them threedimensionally, as shown in Fig. 5a and b. Subse-
quently, we extracted the projected maximum diameter of the
pore perpendicular to the loading direction, which was used
as the diameter of the projection circle in Fig. 5c. Finally, the
FSR factor was calculated by the ratio of the projection circle
area to the pore-inﬂuenced fracture surface. It was given by:

4 Conclusion
In conclusion, X-CT had been used to observe the variations
of pores and carbides before and after the high temperature
fatigue test and a U-Net CNN was applied to extract the
carbides for a DD5 nickel-based single crystal superalloy.
It showed that the fatigue cracks initiated from the pores.
The effects of carbides and pores on fatigue damage were
quantitatively compared by using the fracture surface rough-
ness factor. Although the carbides had a stronger inﬂuence
than pores on the crack propagation stage, the crack propaga-
tion path was mainly controlled by the close-packed plane of
nickel-based single crystal superalloy. To quantify the most
signiﬁcant pore characteristic of each crack variation stage,
we developed a regression model to differentiate the inﬂu-
ences of pore size, morphology, spacing, and distance to the
sample surface on the crack initiation and propagation stage.
The results showed that pore spacing was the most signiﬁ-
cant pore characteristic in the crack initiation stage, and pore
morphology was the most critical factor in determining the
local deformation of the fracture surface. Moreover, the clus-
tering pores far from the sample surface were the potential
initial site of the ﬁnal fracture zone. According to the new
ﬁndings in this study, we would like to highlight the nega-
tive inﬂuence of clustering pores, which may guide future
optimization and development of the manufacturing process
for controlling porosity defects in nickel-based single crystal
superalloys.
