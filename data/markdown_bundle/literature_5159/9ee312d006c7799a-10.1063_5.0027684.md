ABSTRACT
The influences of co-alloying atom pairs Ta – X (X: Ta, Mo, W, Cr, Co, and Re) on the site preference and elastic properties of γ0-Ni3Al
precipitate phase were studied using first-principles calculations, and the underlying mechanism for the strengthening effect is determined.
It is found that the site preferences of Ta – X co-alloying atom pairs are in the sequence of Al – Al, Al – Ni, Ni – Al, and Ni – Ni sites except for
Ta– Co. The atom pair Ta – Co does not display a strong site preference tendency. The elastic properties for the Al – Al configuration were
examined, and the results were compared with those of single-alloying to investigate the co-alloying effect. It is shown that the elastic
modulus and hardness are obviously improved by co-alloying. Moreover, the effect of co-alloying is stronger than that of single-alloying.
The further analyses by the charge density difference and the electronic density of states reveal that the strong effect of the co-alloying
is attributed to the enrichment of the charge density between X and its nearest-neighbor Ni, as well as the orbital hybridization between
Ta/X-d and Ni- d which acts as covalentlike bonds. Our results can provide an in-depth insight into the physics in the strengthening effect
of co-alloying elements on the mechanical properties of single crystal superalloys.

I. INTRODUCTION
Elastic properties of materials, related to various basic solid-
state properties such as equation of state, phonon spectra, and
interatomic potentials,
1– 3 are mainly influenced by the various
bonding characteristic and strength caused by the interaction of
valence electrons and ions between adjacent atoms. Different atoms
have their own valence electrons and ions. Accordingly, the intrin-
sic electronic structure of the crystal will be changed when the host
atoms are replaced by other alloying atoms, which would affect the
elastic properties of the crystal. Moreover, the variety of electronic
structure originating from the different substitution sites of alloying
atoms could also affect the elastic properties of solid materials.
4
Ni-based superalloys are widely used for aircraft blades in the
aerospace industry due to their excellent high-temperature strength,
ductility, fracture toughness, and oxidation resistance,
5,6 and this
can be attributed to the addition of a large number of refractory
elements in the γ0-Ni3Al precipitate phase. 7 In order to understand
the effect of the alloying elements on the elastic properties, the
underlying mechanism for the replacement of Ni or Al by specific
elements and the strengthening variation should be determined. 8– 10
The site preference of single-alloying elements has been
studied in the γ0 Ni3Al phase by first-principles calculations. 10– 19
It is revealed that some 3 d (Sc, Ti, V, Cr, and Mn), 4 d (Y, Zr, Nb,
Mo, and Tc), and 5 d (Hf, Ta, W, Re, and Os) transition-metal ele-
ments exhibit strong Al-site preference, while other 4 d (Pd) and 5 d
(Pt, Au) elements show strong Ni-site preference. Nevertheless, 3 d
(Fe, Co, Zn, and Cu), 4 d (Ru, Rh, Ag, and Cd), and 5 d (Ir) elements
do not show an apparent preference for substitution. 19 The prefer-
ence for Al-sites decreases with the increase of the d-occupation
number of the alloying element except for the end of a series (i.e.,
Cu, Zn, Ag, Cd, and Au).
17– 19 Moreover, the calculated results of site
preference of Cr, Ta, Ti, Mo, Hf, Co, Nb, and Re are consistent with
the experimental measurements by atom-probe tomography (APT)
and x-ray photoelectron spectra.
9,15,20– 23

doped 3 d,4 d, and 5 d transition-metal elements on the elastic

19,25 For example, the bonding between Ta and Re that substi-

atoms without alloying.
26
The strengthening mechanism of a single-alloying atom in
Ni-based superalloys has been investigated extensively as aforemen-
tioned. When adding two or more elements, the site preference
and the redistribution of the electrons should be complex and the
accumulation of electronic density could be altered. This raises
the question of how this situation influences the strengthening
behavior.
In this paper, the site preference and the elastic properties of
the γ
0-Ni3Al phase co-alloyed by the atom pairs Ta – X (X: Ta, Mo,
W, Cr, Co, and Re) were investigated and compared with those of
single-alloyed. The reason for the selection of Ta – X pairs is as
follows: on one hand, Ta, Mo, W, Cr, Co, and Re are main compo-
nent elements added in Ni-based superalloys; on the other hand,
Ta segregates strongly into the γ
0 phase, whereas Cr, Co, Re, W,
and Mo tend to segregate slightly into the γ0 phase, thus this kind
of co-alloying atom pair could be more likely to exist than others.
For the investigation, the first-principles method based on the
density functional theory (DFT) is adopted. The paper is organized
as follows. Section II describes the details of the calculations. In
Sec. III, the site preference of the co-alloying pairs is first examined
to reveal the stable configuration by the substitution of Ta – X.
Then, the elastic properties are determined for the stable configura-
tion, and the underlying mechanism is elucidated via the calculated
electronic structure. Finally, a brief summary is given in Sec. IV.

V. CONCLUSIONS
In the present work, the site preference and the elastic proper-
ties of Ni 3Al with the co-alloying atom pairs Ta – X (X: Ta, Mo, W,
Cr, Co, and Re) are investigated using first-principles calculations.
The site preference of alloying elements was investigated first by
evaluating the value of the energy factor. For the four kinds of sub-
stitution sites by co-alloying atom pairs, the order of site preference
is Al– Al > Al– Ni > Ni– Al > Ni– Ni except Ta – Co atom pair, suggest-
ing that the Al – Al configuration is most stable. For the stable
Al– Al configuration, the elastic constants, elastic modulus, and H
v
are improved by the co-alloying. Moreover, the strengthening effect
of the co-alloying is stronger than that of sing-alloying. The calcu-
lated CDD and DOS reveal that the strengthening effect of the
co-alloying is caused by the enrichment and accumulation of the
charge density between X and its nearest-neighbor (NN) Ni as well
as the orbital hybridization between Ta/X- d and Ni- d which act as
covalentlike bonds. The enhancement of chemical bonding has a
positive effect on the elastic properties of the Ni
3Al phase.
