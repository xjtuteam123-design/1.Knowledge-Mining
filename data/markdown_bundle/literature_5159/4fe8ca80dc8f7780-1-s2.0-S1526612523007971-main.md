1. Introduction 
High entropy alloy (HEA), one of the hottest and most potential 
metals recently, is composed of five or more elements mixed in equal or 
near-equal atomic ratios [ 1 , 2 ]. Owing to high lattice distortion and low 
stacking faults, this alloy shows excellent strength, ductility and fracture 
toughness [ 3 – 6 ], which breakthrough the barrier of “ strength-toughness 
mismatch ” in traditional alloy. Moreover, with addition of γ’ -formation 
elements of Al, Ti, Ta, high volume fraction of ordered L1
2
-Ni
3
(Al, Ti, 
Ta) can precipitate in the HEA matrix and improve the high-temperature 
properties [ 7 – 11 ], which makes it possible in manufacturing structured 
components serviced in elevated temperature, such as the aerospace or 
aviation engine fields. Currently, Ni-based superalloys have been still 
the dominated material in aviation engine manufacturing [ 12 – 14 ]. New 
facbrication methods such as the PM have been applied for preparing 
superalloys with less defects, higher composition homogenization and 
better performance. One of the third generation PM Ni-based superal -
loys, FGH98, has been widely used to manufacture aero-engine turbine 
disks due to its superior phase stability, excellent creep and oxidation 
resistance [ 15 , 16 ]. Actually, this PM superalloy is similar to L1
2
- 
strengthed HEA (e.g., FeCoNiTiAl), which is mainly composed by γ and 
γ’ phase with little number of carbides or TCP phases. Thus, there is a 
potential application of joining the two kinds of alloys in aviation engine 
field. 
However, due to high volume fraction of intermetallic compounds 
(IMCs) of Ni
3
Al in the two alloys, it is a big challenge for joining them as 
they have characteristics of high yield stress and slow atomic diffusion. 
Transient liquid bonding (TLP) bonding using filler metals with addition 
of melting point depressants (MPDs) such as Si, B, etc. can be taken into 
consideration [ 17 – 19 ]. It has been widely used for bonding Ni-based 
superalloys by BNi type filler metals such as BNi-1, BNi-2, BNi-3 and 
so on [ 20 – 22 ]. While, as the joints usually served in elevated tempera -
ture, this kind of filler material with limited performance (dominated by 
solid solutions) was hard to achieve better joint performances especially 
high-temperature properties [ 19 , 20 ]. The key point is the composition 
design of filler metal, like adding some elements into the initial filler

shear stress of joints under each parameter were calculated and achieved

3.3. Mechanical property of joint 
The microstructure analysis above demonstrates there were multiple 
second phases formed in the TLP joint and constituted the ISZ, ASZ, TBZ 
and DAZ. Actually, a sound joint of excellent mechanical properties is 
required to have homogeneous joint microstructure with no defects or 
continuously distributed brittle phases [ 46 – 49 ]. Thus, it can suffer 
uniform plastic deformation and achieve both high strength and 
ductility. Fig. 9 a shows the nanoindentation results, demonstrating the 
hardness distribution of joints bonded for 30 min and 120 min. And the 
shear strength of joints with a function of bonding time was illustrated in 
Fig. 9 b. When bonded for short time of 10 min, as IS of BNi-2 interlayer 
was insufficient, the brittle Ni-Si-B eutectic phases formed in the ASZ can 
lead to limited shear strength, about 752 MPa. Fracture morphology 
shown in Fig. 10 demonstrates the joint failed in the TBZ and there were 
cleavage steps and tearing edges existed in the fracture surface 
( Fig. 10 b), which can be the quasi-cleavage fracture mode. 
After bonding for 30 min, the interlayer achieved complete IS and 
the eutectic products eliminated. Meanwhile, a small number of AlPd 
phases formed in the joint due to the limited concentration of Al atoms 
diffused from the BMs. Also, the volume fraction of borides in the DAZ 
and TBZ were high enough especially the intragranular TiB
2 
borides, 
which can strengthen the joint excellently. Fig. 9 a displays there was 
little fluctuation of the hardness distribution in joint bonded for 30 min, 
with a range of 5.1 – 7.3 GPa. Hence, the best property of joint was

AlPd compounds, which was detrimental to the mechanical properties of
