Atomic site location by channelling enhanced
microanalysis (ALCHEMI) in c0-strengthened Ni- and Pt-base alloys
C.H. Liebscher, J. Preussner, R. Voelkl, U. Glatzel *
Metals and Alloys, University of Bayreuth, Ludwig-Thoma-Str. 36b, D-95447 Bayreuth, Germany
Received 9 April 2008; received in revised form 21 April 2008; accepted 23 April 2008

Abstract
The additions of alloying elements to Ni- and Pt-base alloys inﬂuence the microstructure and thereby the creep properties, whereas the
mechanism is uncertain. Therefore atomic site location by channelling enhanced microanalysis (ALCHEMI) was used to determine the
site partitioning of ternary and quaternary alloying elements in the L1
2-ordered c0-phase. Two ternary Ni–Al alloys with Cr and Ti addi-
tions were investigated. The measured site partitioning showed that Cr and Ti atoms prefer the Al-sublattice sites. For a ternary Pt–Al–
Cr alloy, it was found that Cr atoms occupy Al sites. The inﬂuence of Ni as a fourth alloying element in a Pt–Al–Cr–Ni alloy on the site
partitioning was also investigated. The detected results give evidence that in the quaternary alloy Cr and Ni atoms prefer the Pt sublat-
tice. First principles calculations were used to support the experimental data.

2. Experimental
Alloy samples were prepared by arc-melting under an
argon pressure of 0.5 mbar. The basic elements were of
99.9% purity. The button ingots of about 15 g were re-
melted at least three times to achieve better homogeniza-
tion. The solid solution heat treatment of the ternary
Ni-base alloys Ni
82Al12Ti6 and Ni79Al15Cr6 was done under
argon atmosphere for 24 h at 1100 /C176C followed by air cool-
ing. The ageing heat treatment was carried out for 120 h at
900 /C176C under air with subsequent water quenching. The Pt-
base alloys Pt 82Al12Cr6 and Pt 77Al12Cr6Ni5 were solution
heat treated for 6 h at 1500 /C176C and 6 h at 1510 /C176C with sub-
sequent air cooling. These samples were aged for 48 h at
1100 /C176C followed by water quenching for both alloys.
TEM foils were prepared through electrolytic thinning
in a Struers Tenupol-5 twin jet. For the Ni samples, a solu-
tion of 10 vol.% perchloric acid in ethanol was used. The Pt
samples were thinned using an aqueous solution of potas-
sium cyanide. The transmission electron microscopes Zeiss
Libra 200FE and a Phillips CM200FEG-TEM operated at
200 kV were used. A low background analytical double tilt
holder was used.
3. Sources of error
Qualitative and quantitative measurements of channel-
ling experiments are eﬀected by many sources of error
including the localization of inelastic excitation, the
absorption of X-rays within the sample, thermal diﬀuse
scattering and the absorption of electrons [19–24,27,30].
The following section emphasizes the limitations of
ALCHEMI analysis in dependence of the measured
systems.
3.1. Localization of inelastic scattering
Observing the variation of ionization cross-sections by
channelling of fast it electrons it is important to imply
the degree of localization of the inner-shell ionization. Pen-
nycook [23] estimated the impact parameter in dependence
of the energy of the incident electrons and the generated X-
ray energy. A more accurate estimation method was given
by Oxley and Allen [24]. The root-mean-square impact
parameters b
rms for an incident electron energy of
200 keV in dependence of the X-ray energy are displayed
in Fig. 1 .
The calculated impact parameter for Al K a shell ioniza-
tion of 25.4 pm according to Oxley and Allen [24] denotes a
strong delocalization of the ionization event. Therefore, the
emission of characteristic Al K-shell X-rays is less sensitive
to changes in dynamical diﬀraction conditions [22].

6. First principles calculations
Total energy calculations of diﬀerent Pt-base com-
pounds were performed using the VASP [38] program. A
32-atomic super cell of the composition 75 at.% Pt and
25 at.% Al with L1
2 structure and a lattice parameter of
386.7 pm was used as basis for calculations. The calculated
enthalpies of the compounds were normalized to the
enthalpies of the pure elements with fcc structure. Lower
enthalpies of formation for the diﬀerent compounds coin-
cide with higher phase stability. Variations in concentra-
tion can take place only in steps of 1/32 = 3.125%. The
following concentrations are rounded to integer percent
values. The basic calculations were performed, allowing a
change in cell volume, whereas the relaxation of the cell
shape was neglected to reduce calculation time. The calcu-
lated normalized formation enthalpies and lattice parame-
ters in dependence of the impurity concentration are
displayed in Fig. 12 .
The calculated formation energy for ternary Pt–Al–Cr
alloys is lower for Cr occupying the Pt sites for both impu-
rity concentrations. This is in contrary to the experimental
ALCHEMI results. The deviation between experiments
and simulations may be referred to the fact that calcula-
tions are performed at T = 0 K neglecting the change of
vibrational and conﬁgurational entropy. Additional calcu-
lations with 3.125 at.% Cr occupying Al and Pt sites were
performed allowing cell shape relaxations. The calculated
results demonstrate that Cr residing on Pt sites leads to a
tetragonal distortion of the L1
2-ordered cell with
a = 390.3 pm and c = 393.2 pm closely to the structure
type Pb 3Sr with a = 383.2 pm and c = 389.4 pm observed
by Stadelmaier et al. [39]. The simulation for Cr residing
on Al sites shows no tetragonal distortion. This indicates
that the enthalpy of formation is lower for Cr occupying
the Pt sublattice but without retention of the L1
2-ordered
structure, i.e. it does not contribute to a stabilization of
the L1
2-ordered Pt 3Al phase. Gornostyrev et al. [40]
showed that the phase instability of the Pt 3Al phase is
directly related to the density of states around the Fermi
level. Therefore electronic calculations were conducted
for the pure L1
2-ordered Pt3Al phase, the Pt 3Al phase with
3.125 at.% Cr on Al and Pt sites, respectively, including lat-
tice relaxation. The density of states for the three com-
pounds normalized to the Fermi energy E
F are displayed
in Fig. 13. Gornostyrev et al. [40] indicated that the density
of states around the Fermi level is mostly formed by d-
states and that the instability of the L1 2-ordered Pt 3Al
phase can be referred to the presence of a peak exactly at
the Fermi energy. This eﬀect is also explained by Chu et
al. [41] for C15 MV
2 (M = Zr, Hf or Ta) intermetallic com-
pounds. Fig. 13a shows the overall total density of states. A
closer look to the Fermi level, Fig. 13 b, exhibits a local
maximum of the total density of states for Pt 3Al and Pt 3Al
with 3.125 at.% Cr atoms occupying the Pt sublattice at the
Fermi level. This is an indication for a structural phase
instability leading to a softening of the lattice which can
be released by the onset of magnetism or a structural trans-
formation [40]. Gornostyrev et al. [40] pointed out that the
density of states at E
F is too small for Pt 3Al to satisfy the
Stoner criterion of magnetism, therefore the phase instabil-
ity can be released by a transformation into a diﬀerent
structure. This statement is supported by the calculations
including lattice relaxation for Cr occupying the Pt lattice
sites, where a tetragonal distortion of the Pt
3Al lattice is
observed.

7. Conclusion
X-ray attenuation, thermal diﬀuse scattering and absorp-
tion of electrons do much stronger aﬀect ALCHEMI mea-
surements in Pt
3Al-based precipitates than in Ni 3Al-based
ones. In general, the above-mentioned limitations may lead
to unphysical values of the quantitative site partitioning
coeﬃcients [19–24,27,30]. Therefore, simulations of ioniza-
tion cross-sections for the given experimental conditions
were used [27] to conﬁrm the measured intensity distribu-
tions. For two ternary Ni
82Al12Ti6 and Ni 79Al15Cr6 alloys
it was qualitatively found that Ti and Cr prefer the Al sub-
lattice in the L1
2-ordered c0-phase. Quantitative calcula-
tions reveal that 70% of the Ti atoms and 116% of the Cr
atoms occupy Al sites showing the same trend. The site pref-
erence of Ti changes to the Ni sublattice, whereas Cr shows
no site preference in the highly alloyed c0-phase of a single
crystal CMSX-4 alloy. The measured site occupancies in
the Ni-based alloys could be veriﬁed with results published
in the literature.
The evaluated site partitioning of Cr onto the Al sublat-
tice in a Pt
82Al12Cr6 alloy was also found to be 90%. The
substitution of 5 at.% Pt with Ni of the Pt 82Al12Cr6 alloy
leads to a change of site occupancy for 37% of the Cr atoms
taking up Al sites and 22% of Ni atoms preferring the Al
sublattice.
First principles calculations were conducted to conﬁrm
the presented site preferences and to discover the conse-
quences of changes in site partitioning of ternary and qua-
ternary alloying elements in the L1
2-ordered Pt 3Al phase.
The calculations allowing cell shape relaxation demon-
strate that Cr occupying Pt places leads to a tetragonal dis-
tortion of the L1
2-ordered lattice. The local maximum at
the Fermi level of the density of states indicates a phase
instability resulting in a softening of the Pt
3Al phase. Cr
atoms taking up Al places implies no lattice distortion
and phase instability out of ab initio calculations and is
thus altering the L1
2-ordered Pt 3Al structure. The combi-
nation of ALCHEMI experiments and the calculated
enthalpy of formation for c0-phases in the Pt 82Al12Cr6
and the Pt 77Al12Cr6Ni5 alloys showed that the addition
of Ni is resulting in a reduction of the formation enthalpy
and the lattice parameter and is therefore additionally sta-
bilizing the L1
2-ordered structure.
