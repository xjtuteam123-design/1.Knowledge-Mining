Modelling of hot rotary draw bending for thin-walled titanium alloy tubes

a b s t r a c t 
Titanium alloys tubes are largely used to manufacture structural parts and piping elements that work at high 
temperature in many applications of transport industry, thanks to their high strength-to-weight ratio and excel- 
lent corrosion resistance. Among the available manufacturing methods, tube rotary draw bending is the most 
promising to achieve challenging shapes with high level of accuracy and process robustness, even if defects, such 
as wrinkling, over thinning, distortion of the cross-sections and springback, are still critical to be controlled. At 
the same time, titanium alloy tubes can be hardly shaped at room temperature due to their high yield strength 
and low ductility. The use of temperature-assisted processes might allow overcoming the above-mentioned lim- 
itations, but, at now, such processes have not been investigated in depth. In this context, the paper aims at 
investigating by numerical simulations the eﬀects of selective heating in the rotary draw bending of thin-walled 
titanium alloy tubes. A numerical model of the process was developed to investigate diﬀerent heating strategies 
and process temperatures. The obtained results show that selective heating is promising and capable to inﬂuence 
the quality of the bent tubes. 
1. Introduction 
The continuous demand of lightness and performances in many sec- 
tors of the transport industry [1] , such as aerospace and automotive [2] , 
is driving the attention of designers and manufacturers towards the use 
of titanium alloy tubes, in particular when high mechanical properties 
are required at high temperatures [3] . Furthermore, the use of tubu- 
lar components is increasing both for structural purposes due to their 
high stiﬀness to weight ratio as well as pipelines for ﬂuid convection 
[4] . Thanks to the relevant advantages in terms of weight reduction, fa- 
tigue and creep resistance at high temperatures [5–7] , near- 𝛼 titanium 
alloys are already extensively used in jet engines as compressor discs 
and blades and for hydraulic tubing despite their limited possibility to 
be subjected to thermo–mechanical processes and heat-treatments in the 
𝛼+ 𝛽 phase ﬁeld [8] to obtain the required microstructure and texture 
[9] . Among the others, the Ti3Al2.5 V titanium alloy (ASTM Grade 9), 
which is borderline between the near − 𝛼and 𝛼+ 𝛽 titanium alloys, cov- 
ers a large number of applications since its mechanical properties are 
near to the conventional Ti6Al4 V titanium alloy, but presents improved 
corrosion resistance [10] thanks to its equiaxed 𝛼phase with very little 
𝛽 phase located mostly at the grain boundaries [11] . 
However, the low ductility of near − 𝛼 alloys, the high strength re- 
quired for bending and shaping operations, and the high material cost 
still represent relevant limitations in the widespread and intensive use

The present paper reports the results of experimental and numeri-

draw bending at high temperature. Having as reference material the 
Ti3Al2.5 V titanium alloy, the focus is on the identiﬁcation of the most

proﬁles by applying selective thermal ﬁelds along the tube proﬁles. Af-

Section 2 , the experimental calibration of the constitutive parameters 
of the material model are presented in Section 3 , based on tensile tests 
carried out up to 700 °C. Section 4 reports details of the numerical model 
that was developed to investigate the diﬀerent heating strategies, as 
well as the process temperatures. Finally, the results are discussed in 
Section 5 , with particular regard to the evaluation of the neutral axis 
position that reveals the risk of defect during the process. 
2. Hot rotary draw bending 
The application case refers to the rotary draw bending of circu- 
lar cross-section tubes, whose dies are shown in Fig. 1 a and schemat- 
ically represented in Fig. 1 b. The deformation is obtained by moving 
the clamped proﬁle around a shaped rotating die, while an inner man- 
drel, which consists of a cylindrical rigid body and idle rings linked by 
spherical joints, maintains a ﬁxed axial position during the tube defor- 
mation and prevents the collapse of the tube extrados as well as the 
cross-section deformation. At now, the process is generally carried out 
at room temperature and consists of: (i) the initial positioning , in which 
the tube is mounted on the mandrel and moved to the initial position, 
(ii) the loading , when the clamp and pressure dies move towards the 
bending and wiper dies gripping the tube, (iii) the bending given by the 
rotation of the bend and clamp dies that determine the plastic defor- 
mation of the tube section and, ﬁnally, (iv) the unloading with the dies 
opening and the mandrel extraction. 
To overcome the material-related limitations of the traditional bend- 
ing processes, namely reduced formability, wrinkling and springback, a 
new rotary bending process at high temperature is proposed, where the 
application of selective thermal ﬁelds in the area of the tube to be bent 
appears both eﬀective and simple to be implemented and controlled. 
Fig. 2 shows the outline of the thermo–mechanical cycle of the new 
process: after the initial positioning and loading of the tube, a selective 
heating is performed on the portion of the tube to be bent, before be- 
ing moved to the dies for the bending operation. The heating step can 
be performed either by using induction heating (as represented in the 
Fig. 2 or other technologies and, in principle, the length and the circum- 
ferential portion of the tube aﬀected by the thermal ﬁeld can be varied

3. Experimental 
3.1. Material 
The chosen material is the Ti3Al2.5 V titanium alloy (ASTM Grade 
9), provided in tubes having an outer diameter (OD) of 60 mm and a 
thickness of 1.15 mm, to be bent until 90°with a bending radius equal 
to 1.5 OD; the diﬃculty factor of the bending is 34.78 [25] . The nom- 
inal chemical composition of the titanium alloy is reported in Table 1 . 
Fig. 3 b shows the microstructure of the as-delivered tubes while Fig. 3 c 
reports the ﬂow stress curve at room temperature carried out at a strain 
rate of 1 s − 1 that shows a yield strength of 845 MPa and an elongation 
at rupture of 8.66%. Fig. 3 a shows the reference surface that was as- 
sumed for all the microstructural observations. The microstructures are 
all referred to the cross section of the tube. 
3.2. Tensile tests at high temperature 
Tensile tests were used to obtain the true stress - true strain curves 
at high temperature. The specimens were cut from the from Ti3Al2.5 V 
tubes according to the standard ASTM E8 with a length equal to 
70.0( ± 0.2) mm and a width equal to 12.0( ± 0.1) mm. Fig. 4 a shows the 
experimental apparatus used for the tensile tests that consists of a hy- 
draulic dynamometer with a maximum capacity of 50 kN and maximum 
ram speed of 180( ± 0.5) mm/s, and a frontal inductor with a maximum 
power of 30 kW to heat the specimen up to the testing temperature. A 
CCD camera (Allied –P i k e Series TM ), having an acquisition speed of 
100 fps, was used to record images of the specimen gauge length during 
the tests, which were then analyzed using the software GOM TM Aramis 
to compute the true strain value. The temperature of the specimen was 
continuously monitored throughout the duration of the test by using a 
thermocouple spot welded in the center of the gauge length.

4. Numerical modelling 
The numerical model of the tube hot bending process was devel- 
oped using the software Forge NXT 2.0 TM with the aim of investigating 
the inﬂuence that the thermal conditions have on the quality of the 
bent proﬁles, see Fig. 5 a. The tube was modelled as an elasto-plastic 
deformable body, while all the dies were modelled as rigid bodies. A 
symmetry condition was assumed along the meridian axis of the tube. 
During the process, both the bend and the clamp dies rotate around the 
rotation axis shown in Fig. 5 b with a constant angular rate of 30°/s. At 
the same time, the pressure die moves along the tube axis direction to 
limit the axial friction force.

5.2. Numerical analysis 
Fig. 9 shows the Von Mises equivalent stress ﬁelds computed through 
numerical simulation for the three heating conditions reported in Fig. 6 , 
in the case of heating temperature equal to 600 °C. The results show that 
the neutral axis position is strongly inﬂuenced by the circumferential 
extension of the thermal ﬁeld, since the plastic deformation of the ﬁbers 
is related to the material ﬂow stress that changes as a function of the 
temperature. 
Fig. 9 a shows the results when the whole cross section of the tube is 
heated before bending: the position of the neutral axis, which is identi- 
ﬁed by the minimum value of the Von Mises equivalent stress, presents 
a slight shift towards the bent tube intrados inducing, as prevailing ef- 
fect, a shift of the minimum stress line toward the intrados. Being the 
temperature almost uniform on the tube circumference during bending, 
such behavior is similar to the one that can be observed in the standard 
tube draw bending at room temperature, but the entity of the shift is dif- 
ferent due to the lower ﬂow stress that the material has when deformed 
at high temperature. 
Fig. 9 b refers to the case in which the tube wall at the extrados is 
locally heated before bending. In this case, the thermal condition of 
the tube section is not uniform at the beginning of the bending, with 
the highest temperature localized at the extrados, while the intrados 
remains at about 100 °C also due to the contact with the bend die that 
is thermally-conditioned to remain at room temperature. In this case, 
the shift of the neutral axis is higher due to the fact that the yield stress 
of the material ﬁbers is not uniform in the tube cross section: the ﬁbers 
at the extrados are plastically deformed before the ones at the intrados, 
thus, the bend allowance of the geometrical axis of the tube is increased. 
Fig. 9 c shows that, in the case where the tube is initially heated at 
the intrados, the temperature and deformation have opposite eﬀects on 
the neutral axis position. This is due to the fact that, having the ﬁbers at 
the intrados a lower yield stress, the plastic deformation is larger with 
the eﬀect of shifting outward the neutral axis, where the strains are null.

the strain distribution along the workpiece cross section. The equiva-

5.3. Thermal validation

terial the Ti3Al2.5 V titanium alloy, the focus was on the evaluation of 
the impact that tailored thermal heating of the to-be-bent zone may 
have on the stresses and strains arising in the deformed cross sections.
