ABSTRACT 
Nb-Si-based alloys, as one of the candidate materials which may replace nickel-based alloys for the next gen -
eration aero-engines, its low fracture toughness (K
Q
) and poor oxidation resistance severely limited their 
application and development. Research has shown that Nb-Si-based alloys with a refined microstructure usually 
have good fracture toughness, whereas B and C elements are good microstructure refinement elements. So, the 
effects of B
4
C on the microstructure evolution and properties of Nb-16Si-20Ti alloys were studied. The results 
showed that the microstructure of Nb-16Si-20Ti was composed of Nb-based solid solution (Nb
ss
) and Nb
3
Si 
phases. With the addition of B
4
C, the coarse Nb
3
Si decomposed and transformed into ( α , γ )-Nb
5
Si
3 
+ Nb
ss 
eutectic structure, refining the microstructure of the alloys. The room temperature fracture toughness and 
compressive strength of Nb-16Si-20Ti alloy were improved by adding B
4
C, among which the fracture toughness 
of 0.50B
4
C alloy was up to 11.5 MPa ⋅ m
1/2
, which increased by 98.3% compared with that of the 0B
4
C alloy. The 
improvement of the fracture toughness was mainly due to the increment of the ductile Nb
ss 
content in the alloy 
and the refinement of the microstructure. In addition, the addition of an appropriate amount of B
4
C ( ≤ 0.5 at.%) 
also improved the oxidation resistance of Nb-Si-based alloys. 
1. Introduction 
The thrust-to-weight ratio of modern aero-engines continues to in -
crease, making the operating temperature of its hot-end components 
increases accordingly. As a key component of the hot-end of an aero- 
engine, the service environments of turbine blades are more severe. It 
is well known, that the iterative upgrading of turbine engines is insep -
arable from updating the turbine blade materials [ 1 – 3 ]. The melting 
point of most nickel-based single crystal superalloys is about 1350 
◦
C, so 
it is difficult to service exceeding 1150 
◦
C (85% of the alloy melting 
point), which has little room for further improvement to meet higher 
usage temperature requirements [ 4 – 6 ]. The Nb-Si-Ti-based alloy has the 
potential to meet the harsh environment and higher performance re -
quirements faced by future aero-engines due to its high melting point 
(~1800 
◦
C), low density (about 6.6 – 7.2 g/cm
3
), and excellent high- 
temperature strength ( σ
b 
> 370 MPa at 1200 
◦
C) [ 7 – 10 ]. At present, 
the main disadvantages of Nb-Si-Ti-based alloys are insufficient fracture 
toughness and poor oxidation resistance [ 11 , 12 ]. 
Alloying is one of the important methods to improve the mechanical 
properties and oxidation resistance of Nb-Si-Ti-based alloys [ 13 ]. After 
decades of research, > 20 alloying elements have been added to Nb-Si-Ti- 
based alloys to enhance properties, such as Ti, Al, Cr, Hf, Fe, Zr, Ge, Ga, 
Co, Ni, Zn, Mg, Sc, and so on [ 14 – 23 ]. However, the commonly used 
alloying elements in Nb-Si-Ti-based alloys are usually low melting point 
elements, which leads to a significant decrease in high-temperature 
mechanical properties. In recent years, some scholars have used C ele -
ments and carbides, with high melting points, such as ZrC, HfC, etc. to 
adjust the microstructure of Nb-Si-Ti-based alloys and improve its K
Q 
value [ 24 – 26 ]. Wang et al. found that the addition of 3 at.% C trans -
formed the microstructure of Nb-16Si-24Ti alloy from Nb

1/2

1/2

2.1. Preparation of alloy ingots 
In this experiment, Nb (~75 μ m), Si (75 – 150 μ m), Ti (~75 μ m), and 
B
4
C (75 – 150 μ m) powders were used as raw materials (all the powders 
were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.), and the 
purity of all powders was > 99%. The design components of the five 
composite materials were Nb-16Si-20Ti-xB
4

1.0 at.%), referred to as 0B

according to the proportions of the components. Then ball milling 
(Al

the WZM-5 L II dry ball mill produced by Haoqiang Machinery Factory 
at a speed of 200 r/min for 8 h to achieve a more uniform powder 
mixture. Particles with a diameter of 10 mm and a height of 10 mm were 
pressed under a pressure of 10 MPa by an electric tablet machine. Five 
ingots were prepared by arc melting in a non-consumable copper cru -
cible under an argon atmosphere (high vacuum arc melting and suction 
casting system-DHL400 produced by Sky Technology Development Co., 
Ltd. Chinese Academy of Sciences). The mass of each prefabricated alloy 
ingot was 65 g, and the weight loss rate was < 0.5%. Each alloy ingot was 
melted 5 times to ensure uniformity. The actual chemical compositions 
of the investigated alloy were determined through EPMA analysis (the 
size of the measured regions were at least 5 areas with diameters of 200 
μ m), and the measured chemical compositions of the alloys were listed 
in Table 1 . The results indicated that all alloy ingots had essentially the 
same composition as the nominal composition.
