Abstract: FeCrAl alloys have been developed as one of the promising candidates of 
accident tolerant fuel (ATF) cladding materials for light water reactor (LWR) to 
replace zirconium-based alloys. In this work, diffe rent contents of Nb (0.5, 1.0, and 
1.5wt.% Nb) are doped into the Fe-13.5Cr-4.5Al-2Mo (in wt.%) alloy to develop 
three Nb-doping FeCrAl alloys and the Nb-free alloy is fabricated for comparison. 
Effects of Nb-doping content on the mechanical prop erties (at room temperature and 
800 ) and high-temperature (1200 ) steam oxidation (HTSO) were investigated. 
Although annealing temperature increased, the grain size decreased with the increase 
of Nb content. The volume fraction of Fe 2Nb-type Laves precipitation increased with 
the increment of Nb content. The strength of FeCrAl alloys at room temperature and 
800  were both enhanced by Nb-doping. At room-temperature, Nb-doping improved 
the mechanical properties through grain matrix stre ngthening, precipitation 
strengthening, grain refining strengthening, and di slocation strengthening, according 
to the theoretical calculation analysis. On the oth er hand, the precipitation of the 
Fe 2Nb phase and the solution of Nb collectively improv ed the yield strength of the 
FeCrAl alloy at 800 . The weight gain rate and the spallation of Al 2O3 scales in

1. Introduction 
Nowadays, the demand for clean energy is increasing because environmental 
pollution has become a tremendous threat to the sur vival and development of 
humanity. Nuclear energy, as one of the most potent ial and practical clean energy, 
plays an increasingly important role in reducing ca rbon dioxide emissions, improving 
the environment quality, saving energy, achieving sustainable development, etc [1, 2]. 
While nuclear energy brings benefits to mankind, it also poses challenges to nuclear 
technology and materials. The current nuclear fuel system (uranium dioxide pellets + 
zirconium alloy clad tubes) still has potential saf ety hazards. For example, in the 
explosion accident of the Fukushima nuclear power p lant on March 11, 2011 [3], 
zirconium alloy cladding failed to protect the core fuel of the reactor because of the 
high-temperature oxidation for loss of coolant acci dent (LOCA), which fully 
explained the important role of cladding materials for the safeness of nuclear energy. 
Considering that the revolutionary design of the fu el cladding system is difficult to 
operate in the current reactor, it is more urgent to develop accident tolerant fuel (ATF) 
cladding materials with good high-temperature perfo rmance compatible with the 
current fuel pellets [4]. 
According to the research and evaluation, the most potential accident tolerant 
fuel cladding materials are divided into the follow ing three types [5]: Coated

zirconium-based cladding, SiC/SiC composite claddin g, and ferritic FeCrAl alloy 
cladding. FeCrAl alloys can produce a protective Al 2O3 film on the surface in a 
high-temperature environment, thus having excellent resistance to high-temperature 
oxidation [6, 7]. Therefore, using FeCrAl alloys as accident tolerance fuel cladding 
instead of Zircaloy alloy in LWR can improve the sa fety of nuclear reactors during 
accidents [8], which has sparked worldwide interest in recent years. Comparison to 
the nuclear grade Zr-based alloys and austenitic st ainless steels, the marked benefits 
for FeCrAl alloys are inherent corrosion resistance , stress corrosion cracking 
resistance, radiation-induced swelling resistance, and high-temperature oxidation 
resistance [9]. As per scholars, the nuclear grade FeCrAl alloy is to establish a mature 
combination of alloy compositions and optimized pro cess pathways for fabricating 
FeCrAl fuel cladding that is suitable for mass prod uction and contribute to the 
development of accident tolerant fuels [10]. Based on the comprehensive properties, 
the FeCrAl based ferritic alloys contained signific ant content of Cr (10 ~ 13 wt.%) 
and Al (4 ~ 6 wt.%) have been tuned to get rid of t he alpha prime phase which is 
thought to be brittle (due to high Cr) [5, 10-12]. 
Quantities of reactive elements (RE), such as Y [13, 14]、Zr [15], Si [16], W [17], 
Nb [18], Mo [19], etc., have been doped into the Fe CrAl alloys to further improve the 
high-temperature strength or/and steam oxidation re sistance of FeCrAl cladding 
alloys. The effect of these element additions on th e potential embrittlement around 
500 °C due to Cr-rich α' formation should be negligible, because the tempe rature 
range of Cr-rich α' formation was predicted to be insensitive to thes e minor alloy 
additions [20]. Fe-13Cr-4Al-2Mo base alloy prepared by Oak Ridge National 
Laboratory has attracted widespread attention due t o its better comprehensive 
properties [21]. Different from the solution streng thening formed by Mo addition, Nb 
can form the Fe 2Nb Laves phase which pins grain boundaries and subgrain boundaries 
during deformation, thus improving precipitation st rengthening, effectively 
controlling structure, and improving high-temperatu re strength [22]. Recently, Sun et 
al. [10] of Oak Ridge National Laboratory further s ystematically studied the effect of

nuclear grade Nb-doping FeCrAl alloys is still in t he initial stage and mainly focuses 
on the rolling and annealing process. Currently, it is not clear whether it can meet the 
requirements of the reactor for accident resistant cladding materials. Therefore, it is 
necessary to further evaluate the properties (espec ially the mechanical and 
high-temperature oxidation resistance) of nuclear g rade Nb-doping FeCrAl based on 
the application requirements of nuclear energy engineering.

microstructure. Effects of Nb content on the mechan ical properties (room temperature 
and 800 ) and 1200  steam oxidation behaviors of the nuclear grade FeC rAl alloys 
were investigated using the tensile test and steam oxidation test, respectively.

2. Experimental materials and methods 
2.1. Materials 
The compositions of the FeCrAl alloys were listed i n Table 1. Ingots were 
prepared by the arc-melting in an industrial ZG-25A vacuum induction melting 
furnace using pure Fe, Cr, Al, Mo, and Nb metals. T he as-cast FeCrAl alloy ingots 
were then homogenized at 1150 °C for 2 h. As shown in Fig. S1, no precipitation 
could be observed after homogenization at 1150 , indicating the Nb solubility in the 
ferritic matrix at the homogenization temperature o f 1150  is greater than 1.5 wt.%. 
After that, they were hot-forged into plates with a thickness of 10 mm. The obtained 
plates were hot-rolled at 800  for 6 passes to achieve a total thickness reductio n of 
about 70%. It should be noted that reheating the pl ates at 800  for 5 minutes is 
needed between each pass. Then, the hot-rolled plat es were cold-rolled into sheets 
with a thickness of about 1.5 mm. Finally, the cold -rolled sheets were annealed at 
600-800  for 1-2 h to achieve a recrystallized homogeneous microstructure, the 
parameters of each alloy were also listed in table 1. The annealing temperature and 
time were designed according to the literature [22].

According to the EBSD analysis of the cold-rolled F eCrAl alloys after annealing 
at different times and temperatures, Nb-doping hind ered the recrystallization process. 
As we all know, the mechanism of recovery is the pr ocess that vacancy and 
dislocation change configuration distribution and q uantity through thermal activation 
[29]. Mechanisms of the recrystallization process for the deformed FeCrAl alloy is the 
nucleation and coarsening of subgrain, in which sub grain boundaries change into 
high-angle grain boundaries through the sliding and climbing of surrounding 
dislocation [30]. The recrystallization process of deformed FeCrAl alloy was inhibited 
after Nb-doping, which indicated that the movement of vacancy and dislocation was 
also hindered. Therefore, the microstructure of Nb- doping FeCrAl alloy can be 
controlled by adjusting the annealing temperature and time.

5. Conclusions 
In this study, effects of Nb content on the mechani cal properties (room 
temperature and 800 ) and high-temperature (1200 ) steam oxidation behaviors of 
the nuclear-grade FeCrAl alloys were studied by dop ing different Nb contents (0.0, 
0.5, 1.0, and 1.5wt.%) in Fe-13.5Cr-4.5Al-2Mo alloy s. The effect of Nb-doping on the 
grains and second phase particles of the annealing FeCrAl alloys was also 
investigated. The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) The content and aggregation of the Fe 2Nb-type Laves phase gradually 
increased with the increase of Nb content. Although the annealing temperature and 
time were increased, the grain size of FeCrAl alloy s gradually decreased as the Nb 
content increased, resulting from the inhibition ef fect of the Fe 2Nb-type Laves on 
recrystallization. 
(2) The strength of FeCrAl alloys at room temperatu re and 800  were both 
enhanced by Nb-doping. At room-temperature, Nb-dopi ng increased the grain matrix 
strengthening and precipitation strengthening by so lid-solution and forming the Fe 2Nb 
Laves phase. Meanwhile, introducing Fe 2Nb precipitation led to refine the grain size 
and pin the dislocations, thus improving the grain refining strengthening and 
dislocation strengthening. For high-temperature, th e precipitation of the Fe 2Nb phase 
and the solution of Nb collectively improved the yi eld strength of the FeCrAl alloys. 
 (3) Nb-doping in FeCrAl alloys increased the weigh t gain rate of 
high-temperature (1200 ) steam oxidation. The oxide scale of the Nb-free a nd 
Nb-doping FeCrAl alloys were mainly Al 2O3, and Nb-doping slightly promoted 
increasing the thickness of the Al 2O3 scale. The weight gain and scale spallation 
significantly increased with increasing Nb content. During high-temperature steam 
oxidation, Nb-doping FeCrAl alloys formed discontin uous Nb oxides at the interface 
of Al 2O3 scales and matrix, which may be caused by the limi ted Nb content and/or the 
Nb oxides (Nb 2O5) were dissolved with alumina according to reaction (9). Besides, 
Nb oxides further increased the stress of Al 2O3 scales, promoting the cracking and the 
peeling in Al 2O3 scales. 
(4) Nb-doping in FeCrAl alloys improved the mechani cal properties but 
Journal Pre-proof

## Page 28

26 
 
deteriorated the HTSO resistance. Therefore, consid ering the balance of these two 
properties, 0.5 wt.% Nb content was advising for Fe CrAl fuel cladding.
