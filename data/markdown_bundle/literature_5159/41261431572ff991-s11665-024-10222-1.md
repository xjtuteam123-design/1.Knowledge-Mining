Effect of Nb Element on Microstructure and Mechanical
Properties of (Co 30Cr25Fe40Ni5)100/C0 xNbx High-Entropy
Alloys
Han Yang, Bo Li, Wanqing Chen, Jinwei Lu, Lei Xu, Xicong Ye, and Dong Fang
Submitted: 17 April 2024 / Revised: 30 July 2024 / Accepted: 15 August 2024 / Published online: 7 October 2024
In this study, new (Co 30Cr25Fe40Ni5)100-xNbx (x =0 ,1 ,2 ,4 ,6 ,8 , x values in molar ratio) high-entropy alloys
(HEAs) were prepared. The effects of Nb element on microstructure and mechanical properties of
Co30Cr25Fe40Ni5 alloy were investigated by using x-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscope
(SEM), universal testing machine and Vickers microhardness tester. The results indicate that Co 30Cr25-
Fe40Ni5 alloy exhibited face-centered cubic (FCC) phase. XRD patterns indicate that the inclusion of Nb
causes lattice distortion. Nb-rich Laves phase precipitates in the studied alloys as the Nb content increases
from 1 to 8%. When the content of Nb is 2%, Laves phase and FCC phase form eutectic structure.
Additionally, the addition of Nb element can enhance hardness and yield strength. The studied alloys
tended to increase and then decrease in plasticity. Strengthening mechanisms include solid solution
strengthening, ﬁne grain strengthening and precipitation strengthening. (Co
30Cr25Fe40Ni5)98Nb2,
(Co30Cr25Fe40Ni5)96Nb4 and (Co 30Cr25Fe40Ni5)92Nb8 have brittle fracture characteristics. Among them,
(Co30Cr25Fe40Ni5)98Nb2 exhibited superior mechanical properties, including a compressive strength, frac-
ture strain and hardness of 1664.88 MPa, 44.58% and 286.28 HV , respectively. Combining theoretical
analysis and experimental statistics, a criterion for the average d orbital energy level (Md) was proposed for
HEAs between single-phase (FCC) and two-phase (FCC and Intermetallic phase (IM phase)). The
boundary between FCC and IM phase is determined to be 0.898.
Keywords high-entropy alloys, mechanical properties,
microstructure, Nb-rich Laves phase, orbital energy
1. Introduction
The concept of HEAs was proposed by Taiwan scholar Y eh
(Ref 1) in 2004. Unlike traditional alloys, HEAs are composed
of four or more principal element and generally form three
kinds of simple solid solutions, such as FCC, body-centered
cubic (BCC), and hexagonal close-packed (HCP). HEAs have
attracted much attention from researchers due to their four core
effects such as high entropy effect, ‘‘cocktail’’ effect, lattice
distortion effect, and hysteresis diffusion effect (Ref 2-5).
Among them, ‘‘cocktail’’ effect enables HEAs to obtain
excellent properties from different elements simultaneously
(Ref 6); the lattice distortion effect enhances the strength and
hardness of HEAs by hindering the movement of dislocations
and producing solid solution strengthening (Ref 7); Reference
(Ref 8) proved that the hysteresis diffusion effect plays an
important role in improving the thermal stability of HEAs.
These above effects have made HEAs a popular topic in
scientiﬁc research and industrial applications.
It is well known that achieving a balance between strength
and plasticity in single-phase HEAs is challenging. The
strength of FeCoNiCrMn (Ref 9) HEAs with FCC phase
cannot meet the requirements of practical applications; mean-
while, (FeCoNiCrMn)
89Al11 (Ref 10) HEAs with BCC phase
exhibit poor plasticity. In addition, compositional segregation
and poor ﬂuidity caused by casting defects severely restrict the
development of HEAs. In order to solve the aforementioned
issues, Prof. Lu Yiping’s (Ref 11) team developed new
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 eutectic high-entropy alloys (EHEAs) with B2
and FCC phase in 2014, which exhibits well plasticity and
strength. The successful preparation of EHEAs offers a original
idea to optimizing the comprehensive properties of HEAs (Ref
12-15). Lei et al. (Ref 16) prepared a CrFeNiB
x EHEAs using
vacuum arc melting technique. The ﬁndings demonstrating that
adding B increased the strength and hardness to varying
degrees. As the earliest reported HEAs, CoCrFeNi HEAs with
FCC phase have high plasticity but low strength (Ref 17, 18).
Therefore, enhancing the strength of CoCrFeNi HEAs is
essential to broaden their practical applications. Current
research employs various strengthening mechanisms, such as
solid solution strengthening (Ref 19), dislocation strengthening
(Ref 20), precipitation strengthening (Ref 21), and ﬁne grain
strengthening (Ref 22), to improve the comprehensive proper-
ties of HEAs. Studies have shown that eutectic structure
consisting of Laves and FCC phase can be formed by adding a

enhance their comprehensive properties. For instance, Zhang

the impact of Nb content on the mechanical properties of
Al

of Nb considerably enhances hardness and wear resistance of
the studied alloys. As is well known, Nb has large atomic
radius, which could be solidly dissolved in FCC matrix. This
contributes to the solid solution strengthening of HEAs by
exacerbating the lattice distortion. Additionally, the low mixing
enthalpy between Nb and other elements facilitates the
formation of intermetallic compounds (IM), achieving sec-
ond-phase strengthening. However, there are few reports on the
inﬂuence of Nb on CoCrFeNi-based HEAs. Thus, this study
investigates the effects of Nb on microstructure and mechanical
properties of Co
30Cr25Fe40Ni5 alloy using XRD, SEM, univer-
sal testing machine and Vickers microhardness tester. This
study aims to establish a theoretical foundation for practical
implementation of EHEAs in the industrial ﬁeld.
2. Experimental Materials and Methods
(Co30Cr25Fe40Ni5)(100/C0 x)Nbx (denoted as Nb0, Nb1, Nb2,
Nb4, Nb6, Nb8) HEAs were prepared by vacuum arc melting.
The elemental parameters are speciﬁed in Table 1. The ingots
were cut into several shapes of the desired size using wire
cutting. The studied alloys were ground and polished sequen-
tially with 240#, 400#, 800#, 1200# and 2000# sandpaper, and
the shapes were etched with a mixture of aqua regia
(HNO
3:HCl = 1:3). The microstructures and compositions
were analyzed utilizing SEM (JSM-6510) and EDS. The phase
analysis was carried out using an XRD (ARL EQUINOX 100
X, Germany), and the target material was Cu target (K a,
k = 0.154 nm), with the scanning range between 20 /C176 and 100/C176 ;
the compression properties of the sample were measured by
means of a universal testing machine (DNS100). The size of the
compression sample was U5 9 6 mm, and the average value of
three samples was selected as the compression properties of the
studied alloys for each group of experiments. The hardness of
the samples was measured by Vickers microhardness tester
(MICRO-586). The pressure was 100KN, and the force
retention time was 15 s. The average value of 10 valid test
points is taken as the Vickers hardness of the samples.
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 X-ray Diffraction Analysis
Figure 1 presents XRD patterns of (Co 30Cr25Fe40Ni5)100-
xNbx HEAs. The results indicate that only FCC diffraction
peaks are observed in Nb0. The studied alloys undergo phase
transition with the addition of Nb. The XRD patterns reveal not
only the diffraction peaks corresponding to the FCC phase but
also the presence of Nb-rich Laves phase peaks. The local
magniﬁcation of Fig. 1(a) from 86/C176 to 94/C176 is shown in Fig. 1(b).
The diffraction peak of (211) crystal plane of FCC phase shifts
to a small angle, which corresponds to the calculation results of
lattice constants (0.3548, 0.3551, 0.3697, 0.3603, 0.3574, and
0.3570 from Nb0 to Nb8, respectively). The above phe-
nomenon conforms to Bragg ’s law (Ref 31). As shown in
Fig. 1(c), the atomic radius of Nb is signiﬁcantly larger than
that of other elements. Part of Nb element is solidly dissolved
in FCC phase to form substitutional solid solution, which leads
to lattice distortion. In other words, when Nb element dissolves
into FCC phase, it will inevitably causes lattice distortion,
resulting in an increase in lattice constant. Thus, FCC phase
diffraction peaks shift toward a smaller angle (Ref 32).
However, when Nb content exceeds 4%, lattice constant does
not continue to increase. The solid solubility of Nb in FCC
phase is inhibited by the generation of more Laves phase,
which reduces the amount of Nb atoms entering the lattice (Ref
33, 34). Thus, lattice constant of FCC phase decreases, which
results in shift of the peak position to a larger angle. In addition,
the half-peak widths of Nb2, Nb4, Nb6, and Nb8 are larger than
the diffraction peaks of Nb1, indicating that the presence of Nb
reﬁnes the eutectic layer. The eutectic structure between the
dendrites comprises both FCC and Nb-rich Laves phase. This
was similar to Fan’s ﬁndings (Ref 35).

3.3 Mechanical Properties
The stress–strain curve of (Co 30Cr25Fe40Ni5)100/C0 xNbx
HEAs is shown in Fig. 4. It is evident that Nb0 exhibits good
plasticity but low strength. The introduction of Nb increases the
yield strength of the alloys, while the plasticity initially
improves and subsequently declines. The mechanical properties
of the studied alloys are shown in Table 3. The yield strength
increased gradually when the Nb content is increased from 1 to
8%. With the Nb content increases, the solid solution of Nb
atoms with a large atomic radius causes a greater degree of
lattice distortion in FCC phase. The interaction between the
stress ﬁeld caused by lattice distortion and the elastic stress ﬁeld
around the dislocation will form an ‘‘air mass’’ that pins the
dislocation. Dislocations must overcome the hindrance of the
‘‘air mass’’ in order to move freely, which increases the shear
stress of dislocation slip and improves the yield strength. For
Nb2, the compressive strength improved by approximately
26% and the elongation is closed to 45%, demonstrating
excellent mechanical properties. The SEM image in Fig. 2 has
shown the reﬁnement of the eutectic layer as Nb content
increases. Grain reﬁnement increases the total number of grain
boundaries, which in turn enhances the resistance to dislocation
movement and improves the strength of the studied alloys. At
the same time, grain reﬁnement could also promote plasticity
deformation dispersed in more grains, reducing stress concen-
tration and enhancing plasticity (Ref 37-39). Fine grain
strengthening is the only way to improve strength and plasticity
of metal materials at the same time (Ref 40). The microstructure
of the studied alloys exhibited a more signiﬁcant reﬁnement
effect as the Nb content increased. From the above, it can be
seen that further increase the Nb content (Nb4) to form a large
number of Laves phases. The precipitation of these phases will
consume part of Nb element, resulting in a decrease in Nb
atoms dissolved in the matrix and a decrease in lattice
distortion. The degree of lattice distortion of the studied alloys
is reduced, and a reduction in the degree of lattice distortion
usually results in a reduction in the strength of the studied
alloys. However, compared with the effect of ﬁne grain
strengthening, the decrease of strength is relatively small (Ref
41). Therefore, the studied alloys exhibit an upward trend in
yield strength. Nb6 compressive strength is as high as
1803.3 MPa, but the excessive precipitation of Laves phase
drastically reduced the plasticity. Although the grains remain
small, too much Laves phase will lead to stress concentration
and affect the toughness and impact resistance of the material.
At Nb8, continued Nb addition results in Laves phase forming
interconnected networks, which increases crack sensitivity and
further embrittles the studied alloys, reducing plasticity and
work-hardening ability. This ﬁnding is more in agreement with
Ai et al. (Ref 42). The excellent compressive properties of the
studied alloys are attributed to solid solution strengthening of
Nb atoms, reﬁnement of the eutectic layer and precipitation
strengthening of Nb-rich Laves phase in FCC phase. CoCrFe-
Ni-based HEAs can be signiﬁcantly strengthened by adding
trace Nb. Figure 4(b) compares the compression properties of
the studied alloys with other HEAs series, demonstrating that
non-equimolar Co-Cr-Fe-Ni HEAs exhibit superior plasticity
and strength compared to equimolar CoCrFeNi HEAs. The
addition of an appropriate amount of Nb element signiﬁcantly
improved the performance of HEAs in terms of plasticity and
strength. This improvement shows promise for the future
development and application of HEAs.
Figure 5(a) shows Vickers hardness of (Co
30Cr25Fe40-
Ni5)100-xNbx HEAs. Nb0 exhibits the lowest hardness value.
The Vickers hardness signiﬁcantly improves with the addition
of Nb, which corresponds to the trend of yield strength. The
increase in hardness can be attributed to the enhancement of
lattice distortion, hindrance of dislocations and slip, and
decrease in lamellar spacing in the eutectic region. This is
consistent with the Hall–Petch relationship (Ref 43). It is found
that the introduction of Nb leads to an increase in the lattice
constant and produces signiﬁcant lattice distortion. The internal
stress ﬁeld caused by this distortion increases the movement
resistance of dislocations, thereby increasing the yield strength
and hardness of the material (Ref 38). Meanwhile, with the
increase of Nb content, the lamellar spacing decreases,
indicating that the grain reﬁnement (see Fig. 2l) further
promotes the hardness of the studied alloy. This is similar to
the literature (Ref 36); the change of Cr content will lead to the
signiﬁcant reﬁnement of the microstructure of WMoNbTiCr
HEAs, thus affecting its mechanical properties. Additionally,
Laves phase continues to increase with the addition of element
Nb. Laves phase is a hard and brittle phase, which plays an
important role in improving the hardness of the studied alloys.

4. Conclusion
In this study, on the basis of Co 30Cr25Fe40Ni5 alloy with
FCC single-phase, the large atomic radius Nb element is added
to improve the comprehensive mechanical properties. The
following main conclusions were obtained:
(1) XRD analysis showed that Co
30Cr25Fe40Ni5 alloy exhi-
bits FCC phase. With the addition of Nb element, Laves
phase precipitated. Furthermore, the inclusion of Nb re-
sults in an increase in lattice constant of FCC phase
compared to that of Co
30Cr25Fe40Ni5 alloy. This sug-
gests that lattice distortion occurs within the studied al-
loys.
(2) With the addition of Nb element, the microstructure of
the studied alloys changed from single-phase FCC
(Nb0) to eutectic structure (Nb1-Nb8) composed of FCC
and IM phase (Nb-rich Laves phase). When the Nb con-
tent is 1%, a small amount of Nb-rich Laves phase was
precipitated. With the continuous increase of Nb content,
FCC and Nb-rich Laves phases gradually form an eutec-
tic network, showing a eutectic structure. At the same
time, it can also be seen that the volume fraction of
FCC phase decreases as the Nb element increases, rang-
ing from 98% for Nb1 to 64% for Nb8. Conversely, the
volume fraction of the eutectic structure increases.
(3) The yield strength and hardness of the studied alloys
gradually increase with the addition of Nb element.
Meanwhile, the plasticity of the studied alloys initially
increases but then decreases. The main strengthening
mechanism of the studied alloys was solid solution
strengthening, ﬁne grain strengthening, and precipitation
strengthening. Notably, the studied alloys exhibit brittle
fracture when the Nb content is 2, 4, and 8%, respec-
tively. The (Co
30Cr25Fe40Ni5)98Nb2 alloy exhibited
excellent performance overall, with a strength of
1664.88 MPa, a strain rate of 44.58%, and a hardness of
286.28 HV .
(4) Combining theoretical analysis and experimental statis-
tics, the proposed Md criterion can effectively predict
the phase formation of single-phase and double-phase
(FCC and IM phase) HEAs. When Md > 0.898, the
studied alloys form a new IM phase.
