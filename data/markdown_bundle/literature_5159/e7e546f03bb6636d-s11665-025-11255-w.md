L.E. Agureev , S.V. Savushkina, E.A. Danilina, S.D. Ivanova, A.V. Ivanov, and S.A. Garibashvili
Submitted: 22 June 2024 / Revised: 30 October 2024 / Accepted: 7 February 2025 / Published online: 2 May 2025
NiAl based materials doped with transition metals are currently receiving a lot of attention due to their high
mechanical properties, including at elevated temperatures. Pseudo-eutectic materials based on the NiAl
system alloyed with Cr, Mo, Co and V in equiatomic proportions were obtained by high-energy mixing of
initial NiAl and metal powders and spark plasma sintering. The alloying components were selected based
on previously obtained extensive research results provided irrefutable evidence of their suitability. Ther-
modynamic modeling by Jmatpro and MAAT software products were used to predict the composition of the
sintered material. In particular, the dependencies of the equilibrium chemical composition on temperature,
the components activities in the ternary systems, and the mixing Gibbs energy were obtained. The study of
the sintered material microstructure showed its multiphase nature with the presence of areas enriched with
alloying elements in different amounts. The microstructure was characterized by the presence of areas with
submicron spherical and micron lamellar phases. According the phase composition modeling at the sin-
tering temperature there are BCC phase, NiAl-phase and topologically close-packed (TCP) phases ( μ and σ)
in alloys. The 3-point ﬂexural strength and Y oung's modulus of the material were determined at room and
elevated temperatures (750, 900, and 1200 °C). A high stability in the mechanical properties of the material
was observed. The material had a strength of 760 MPa at room temperature and 694 MPa at 900 °C.

1. Introduction
NiAl is an intermetallic compound that is highly resistant to
high temperatures and corrosion, making it a promising
material for engineering applications (aerospace, nuclear etc.).
However, its use is limited due to its high brittleness. Materials
based on this intermetallic compound are used for the
manufacture of turbine blades, bearings and other parts
operating under conditions of high temperatures, wear and
corrosion. The polycrystalline NiAl compound transitions from
brittleness to ductility at a temperature range of 300 –500 °C,
which is lower than that of other intermetallic compounds
(only/C24 0.35T
m NiAl). The exact transition temperature depends
on stoichiometry, impurity content, and grain size. However,
the heat resistance and creep resistance of the polycrystalline
NiAl alloy are low at temperatures above /C24 0.5T
m (/C24 700 °C).
Additional strengthening mechanisms have been developed to
enable NiAl alloys high operating temperatures.
The addition of alloying elements to NiAl is a common
method used to improve ductility at low temperatures and reduce
strength at high temperatures. Studies have shown that these
elements can be classiﬁed into three groups based on their impact
on phase stability in the binary phase diagram (Ref 1, 2). For
example, such elements as Ti, Zr, Hf, V , and Nb form ternary
intermetallic compounds and provide strength to intermetallic
precipitates in NiAl alloys. Additives can have a signi ﬁcant
impact on strength through grain re ﬁnement (Ref 2), changes in
grain boundary structure and crack propagation ability, and even
the formation of multiphase structures (Ref 3-5).
Refractory metals such as Cr, Mo, W, Re are insoluble in
NiAl and do not form ternary intermetallic compounds. Instead,
they create a quasi-binary eutectic system with stoichiometric
NiAl. This leads to the hardening of NiAl at high temperatures
and increased ductility at low temperatures (Ref 6). Cr, Mo, V ,
and Ta can enhance the impact toughness at room temperature
and the strength of NiAl at elevated temperatures (Ref 7–9).
The solubility of V in NiAl at elevated temperatures is at least
14 at.% and signi ﬁcantly decreases with temperature decreas-
ing. It increases for Al content above 50 at.% (Ref 10). This
solubility is considerably higher than that for chromium or
molybdenum (Ref 11). According (Ref 12), V , Mo, Fe, and Co
are primarily used as substitutes for nickel, whereas Cr replaces
both nickel and aluminum.
The addition of Cr, Mo, and V reduces the brittleness of the
intermetallic compound and increases its crack resistance.

2. Thermodynamic Modelling
Thermodynamic modelling was carried out using J-MatPro
V ersion 7.0 software with the Ni-Fe database. The software
calculates a wide range of material properties mainly focused
on multi-component alloys used in industry (Ref 45). It is
important to note that the actual results of new alloys synthesis
may differ from those predicted by thermodynamic modelling.
Mechanical alloying and spark plasma sintering are non-
equilibrium processes that are dif ﬁcult fully explained. How-
ever, thermodynamic modelling can provide an estimate of the
physical and chemical limits the system being studied tends.

Figure 2 illustrates the distribution of speci ﬁc alloy elements

3. Experimental part
The starting materials included NiAl (PN70Y u30, 70 μm),
Mo (1 μm), Co (less than 1 μm), Cr (PKh-1S, 140 μm) powders
and vanadium pellets (10 mm). A mixture of NiAl-Cr, Mo, Co,
and V powders was prepared using mechanical alloying in a
vibratory mill and an Activator-2SL planetary mill. The
concentration of each individual element was the same in
equiatomic proportion. The mixing process was carried out in
steel jars with the addition of 0.5 wt.% oleic acid surfactant and
steel balls at a ratio of powder to balls equal to 1:5 in low
vacuum. The sequential mixing process was carried out for 24
hours in the vibromill and an additional 2 hours in the planetary
mill. DSC/TG analyses were conducted using the Netzsch
apparatus within a temperature range of 20 to 1500 °C under an
inert atmosphere.
Sintering was carried out using the FTC Systeme 025 spark
plasma sintering machine. The samples had a diameter of
30 mm and a height of 3 mm. The composition and
microstructure were analyzed using the FEI Quanta SEM.
Density and porosity were determined through hydrostatic
weighing. Rectangular beams measuring 3 95925 mm were
cut from the sintered specimens for three-point bending tests in
a vacuum at room and high temperatures. The Y oung's modulus

5. Physical and Mechanical Properties
The sintered NiAl-CrMoCoV sample had a density of
7.09 g/cm 3 and a total porosity of 0.6% determined by
hydrostatic weighing. The density values of the alloy obtained
by modelling in the JMatPro environment were slightly higher,
by 2%, than those measured by hydrostatic weighing at room
temperature. Figure 14 shows the alloy density versus temper-
ature diagram based on the calculation results.
The strength and Young's modulus of the sintered NiAl-
CrMoCoV sample were measured using the 3-point bending
and ultrasonic methods at room temperature and elevated
temperatures (750, 900, and 1200 °C). Strength values are 760,
723 , 694 , and 100 MPa, respectively, and Young's modulus
values are 185 GPa, 157 GPa, 151 GPa, and less than 40 GPa,
respectively (Table 3). Figure 15 displays the Y oung's modulus
values of the alloy calculated in JMatPro as a function of
temperature for comparison purposes. The calculation values
are slightly overestimated by 5-10%.

The advantages of alloying NiAl with vanadium over
molybdenum, chromium and cobalt are as follows. It is
structural stability. V anadium forms stronger intermetallic
bonds compared to molybdenum and cobalt, resulting in better
alloy stability at high temperatures (Ref 58). It is also improved
ductility. V anadium promotes a more uniform distribution of
dislocations, unlike chromium, which can lead to the formation
of brittle phases (Ref 59). Besides, there electronic effects:
vanadium allows a more ef ﬁcient exchange of electrons,
forming stronger bonds. Molybdenum and cobalt can limit
lattice ﬂexibility resulting in poorer mechanical properties (Ref
60). According to studies in works (Ref 50-64) the addition of
vanadium strengthens the interatomic bonds in NiAl by
forming stronger metallic bonds, via forming solid solutions
in NiAl or CrMo phases or forming intemetallics (Ref 62) This
is explained by the greater tendency of vanadium to delocalise
electrons in the d-sublevels enhanced the interaction with the d-
electrons of nickel, thereby improving the mechanical strength
of the material (Ref 55, 56, 64). The material's hardness and
wear resistance are enhanced by the lamellar phases identi ﬁed
through scanning microscopy and correspond to TCP phases
predicted by thermodynamic modelling. These ﬁndings are
consistent with previous studies.
Therefore, the composite structure of the material comprised
alternating nano- and submicron phases, as well as microphases
containing NiAl and lamellar TCP phases based on alloying
transition metals, exhibits high mechanical properties, includ-
ing at elevated operating temperatures. Such materials may
prove to be a promising option for utilisation in speci ﬁc
components of power plant units.
6. Conclusions
Mechanical alloying and spark plasma sintering were used
to obtain a NiAl-CrMoCoV based material. The activity of
vanadium in the CrMoV system signi ﬁcantly increases with its
concentration. However, its activity in the equiatomic mixture
is lower than that of chromium and molybdenum at sintering
temperature. The CrMoCo system shows extremely high
activity for molybdenum. In VMoCo system molybdenum
exhibits higher activity at sintering temperature compared to
vanadium and cobalt. The activity of components increases
with their concentration in CrVCo system. According to
thermodynamic modelling the material mainly consists of
BCC, NiAl, μ and σ phases.

corresponds to the evaporation of water, surfactants, and
intermetallic phase formation. The endothermic peak at 1267 °
