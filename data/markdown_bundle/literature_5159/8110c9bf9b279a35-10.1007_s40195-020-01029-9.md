Abstract
CrFeNi2V0.5Wx (x = 0, 0.25) alloys based on these parameters of mixing enthalpy (ΔH mix), mixing entropy (ΔSmix), atomic 
radius difference ( δ), valance electron concentration, and electronegativity difference(Δ χ) were designed and prepared. 
The microstructure and room-temperature mechanical behavior of both alloys were investigated. Compressive test results 
showed that the CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy had higher yield strength than that of the W-free CrFeNi2V0.5 alloy, although they 
all exhibited quite larger compressive plasticity (ε > 70%). Compression fracture surface of CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 alloy revealed 
a ductile fracture in the face-centered cubic (FCC) phase and a brittle-like fracture in the σ phase. Moreover, tensile test 
results indicated that the CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 alloy exhibited excellent mechanical property with an ultimate tensile strength 
of 640 MPa and a high tensile elongation of 15.7%. The tensile deformation mode of the FCC phase in the CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 
alloy is dominated by planar glide, relating to dislocation configurations, high-density dislocations, and dislocation wall. 
Therefore, dislocation slip plays a significant role in tensile deformation of CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 high-entropy alloy. The higher 
strength of CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 alloy is predominantly due to the solid solution strengthening of W element and σ phase pre-
cipitation strengthening. Combination of the higher tensile strength and plasticity suggests that the CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 alloy 
can be a promising aerospace material.
Keywords High-entropy alloy · Microstructure · Mechanical property · Deformation behavior
1 Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) [1 ] or multicomponent alloys 
[2] are multicomponent metallic materials, which break the 
design framework of traditional alloys. The coordination of 
multiprincipal elements in HEAs has led to many unique 
combinations of properties, such as high hardness/strength 
[3, 4], wear resistance [5], corrosion resistance [6], excellent 
magnetic properties [7], low-temperature ductility [8, 9], and 
superplasticity [10, 11]. Recently, the new potential applica-
tion of HEAs has attracted the attention of many research-
ers. The mechanical properties of many HEAs have been 
thoroughly investigated. In general, alloys with a single-FCC 
(face-centered cubic)-phase solid solution structure, such 
as CoCrFeNi [ 12, 13] and CoCrFeMnNi [ 14, 15] alloys, 
showed excellent ductility. However, the lower strength 
and operating temperature severely restricted their applica-
tions. Metallic alloys with superior mechanical properties at 
elevated temperatures remain in high demand for the aero-
space industry. Senkov et al. reported Ta
25Nb25W25Mo25, 
 Ta20Nb20W20Mo20V20, and Ta20Nb20Hf20Zr20Ti20 HEAs 
with single-BCC (body-centered cubic)-phase solid solu-
tion structure [16– 18]. Among them, Ta
25Nb25W25Mo25 
and Ta20Nb20W20Mo20V20 alloys containing high W content 
showed ultrahigh strength and higher operating temperature. 
However, their tensile plasticity is quite limited. Therefore, 
the major research interest of elevated temperature alloy has 
been focusing on the attainment of excellent ductility and 
higher operating temperature.

Generally, HEAs with BCC solid solution structure show 
poor plasticity. And the HEAs with FCC solid solution struc-
ture usually own good plasticity and machinability. The 
structure of high-temperature HEAs with fair ductility 
should mainly be FCC solid solution structure. Typical brit-
tle phases (σ, μ, and other intermetallic phases) usually lead 
to the serious embrittlement of alloys. However, some FCC-
based HEAs can be remarkably strengthened by the precipi-
tation of the σ and μ phases [12, 19–21]. Some precipitate-
forming elements of Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf, Mo, Ti, etc., have been 
investigated to improve the yield strength of the CoCrFeNi-
based HEAs [19, 21–25]. Liu et al. [19] added the Mo ele-
ment to the CoCrFeNi matrix and found that CoCrFeNiMo
0.3 
HEA was remarkably strengthened with a tensile strength of 
1.2 GPa by the precipitation of the σ and μ phases. So far, 
several parameters, such as mixing enthalpy (ΔH
mix), mixing 
entropy (ΔS mix ), atomic radius difference (δ ), Ω 
( /u1D6FA= Tm ΔSmix
ΔHmix
 ), valance electron concentration (VEC), and 
electronegativity difference (Δχ ) [26–28], have also been 
proposed to predict the structure stability and phase forma-
tion of HEAs in analogy to the Hume-Rothery rule for con-
ventional solid solution. Zhang et al. [ 26] used parameters 
of ΔH
mix and δ to predict the formation of simple solid solu-
tions: − 15 ≤ ΔHmix ≤ 5 kJ/mol and δ ≤ 8.5%. Guo et al. [27] 
proposed VEC parameter to predict the phase stability of the 
HEAs. The FCC phases will form at VEC ≥ 8, and BCC 
phases will form at VEC ≤ 6.87, while both FCC and BCC 
phases coexist in the region of 6.87 < VEC < 8.0. Dong et al. 
[28] suggested that the topologically close-packed (TCP) 
phases are stable at Δχ > 0.133 in HEAs. As a typical ele-
vated temperature alloy application, Fe–Cr–Ni alloys with 
FCC solid solution structure were widely studied [ 29]. 
Moreover, the vanadium (V) and tungsten (W) elements pos-
sessing excellent high-temperature performance were often 
added to improve the operating temperature [30–35].
In this paper, based on these parameters of ΔH
mix, ΔSmix, 
δ, Ω, VEC, and Δχ, CrFeNi2V0.5Wx (x = 0, 0.25) alloys were 
designed and produced. Also, the effect of W element on 
the microstructure and mechanical properties was inves -
tigated. Furthermore, the deformation mechanism of the 
 CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy at room temperature was also care-
fully studied.
2 Experimental
The CrFeNi2V0.5Wx (x = 0, 0.25) alloy ingots were prepared 
using arc melting under an argon atmosphere. The ingots 
with a total weight of about 35 g were remelted at least five 
times to improve their chemical homogeneity. The crystal-
line structure of samples was characterized by the X-ray dif-
fraction (XRD) using an Empyrean diffractometer with Cu 
Kα radiation scanning from 20° to 100° at a scanning speed 
of 0.215629°/s. The microstructure and the distributions of 
chemical compositions of specimens were analyzed with an 
optical microscope (MEF-3) and a scanning electron micro-
scope (SEM, Zeiss supra 55) equipped with an attached 
X-ray energy-dispersive spectrometer (EDS). Cylindrical 
samples of ϕ 5 mm × 10 mm were prepared for compres-
sive tests with a speed of 10
−3/s. To obtain tensile samples, 
the CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 alloy ingot (100 g) was produced by 
high-frequency induction melting furnace. The BN crucible 
and graphite crucible were preheated at 200 °C to remove 
the water vapor before putting them into the furnace. The 
master alloy with a purity of raw elemental metals higher 
than 99.5 wt% was melted, superheated and poured into a 
graphite crucible with the length of 95 mm, upper inner 
diameter of 16 mm and bottom inner diameter of 13 mm. In 
all cases, the furnace chamber was first evacuated to 5×10
−3 
Pa and then backfilled with high-purity argon gas to reach 
0.05 MPa. Dog-bone-shaped tensile test specimens had a 
diameter of 3 mm and a gauge length of 25 mm. Tensile 
tests were performed on a universal electronic tensile test-
ing machine at a strain rate of 4×10
−3/s. Microstructures of 
the tensile samples were characterized using a transmission 
electron microscope (TEM).

3.3 Compression Properties
The compressive engineering stress–strain curves of the 
 CrFeNi
2V0.5 and CrFeNi 2V0.5W0.25 alloys are given in 
Fig. 3. Both alloys demonstrated significant capability of 
strain and better ductility (ε > 70%), which is higher than 
most reported values. Better ductility of the CrFeNi2V0.5 
and CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 alloys may be attributed to the 
larger volume fracture FCC solid solution phase in both 
alloys. Although both alloys have excellent plasticity, the 
 CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy has a higher yield strength ( σy) of 
278 MPa than that of the W-free alloy (σ y= 78 MPa). The 
yield strength increases significantly due to the transforma-
tion from the single-FCC-phase solid solution to the FCC 
solid solution + σ phase. The W element with the largest 
atomic radius can cause strong lattice distortion when dis-
solved into FCC solid solution phase, which corresponds to 
the shift of the diffraction peaks of (111) to the lower 2θ . 
The σ phase (FeCr type) with a tetragonal structure is a hard/
brittle TCP phase, which enhanced the strength of alloys.
To understand the relationship between mechanical prop-
erty and microstructure as well as the fracture mechanism of 
the CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy, the fractured surface was care-
fully polished, and the post-compression microstructure of 
the CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy observed by SEM is displayed 
in Fig.  4. Two different kinds of fracture modes, namely 
ductile fracture in FCC phase and brittle-like fracture in 
hard σ phase, could clearly be observed. A lot of slip lines 
(indicated by the white arrow in Fig.  4a) were found in the 
dendritic region with FCC structure, which was consistent 
with the extraordinary plasticity. And the direction of slip 
lines is perpendicular to the direction of interdendritic. In 
addition, it was observed that there was a morphological 
change from rounded dendrites (see Fig.  2b) to break den-
drites (see Fig. 4a) after 70% compression deformation. And 
the grains become elongated along the direction, in which 
the specimen was extended (indicated by the black box in 
Fig. 4a). Figure 4b, c presents the enlarged SEM images of 
the deformed sample of CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy. Several dif-
ferent fracture modes with crack nucleation and propagation 
were observed. In the first mode, the cracks were nucleated 
at the tip of σ phase due to the intrinsic brittleness of the σ 
phase and then propagated through the σ phase. For another 
mode, the cracks were nucleated at the FCC/σ phase bounda-
ries in the process of deformation and expanded into macro-
scopic fracture, due to the plasticity mismatch between FCC 
and σ phase. Combined with above analysis, the hard/brit-
tle σ phase exhibits brittle fracture and the soft FCC phase 
shows large plastic deformation.

3.4 Tensile Properties
The tensile stress–strain curve for CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 alloy 
is plotted in Fig.  5a. From the engineering stress–strain 
curve, the yield strength, fracture strength, and elongation 
were 343 MPa, 640 MPa, and 15.7%, respectively. Figure 5b 
presents tensile properties of FCC solid solution HEAs and 
FCC-based HEAs [19, 38–40]. The yield strength of the 
 CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy was higher than that of the single-
FCC-phase HEAs [19, 38, 39], even higher than some of 
second-phase strengthened FeCoNiCr HEAs [19, 38]. 
The yield strength of the σ second precipitation-strength 
 CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 HEAs is 64% higher than the single-
FCC-phase FeCoNiCrMn HEA and 12% higher than the 
 CoCrFeNiMo
0.3 HEA.
In order to understand the deformation mechanism of 
the CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 alloy, SEM and TEM microstruc-
tures were observed. Typical SEM micrographs obtained 
from fractured surfaces of CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy specimen 
at room temperature are shown in Fig.  6. On the fracture 
surface, a high density of dimple-like features can be obvi-
ously observed, which may suggest the microvoid coales-
cence mode fracture. In addition, cast void is observed as 
well, and the crack always initiates from a cast void.
TEM images of the CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy after tensile 
fracture are given in Fig.  7. It can be found that high-
density dislocations and dislocation wall were observed 
in FCC phase. In general, for FCC materials, if they have 
a low stacking fault energy (SFE), dislocations will show 
the minimal three-dimensional mobility and insufficient 
Burgers vector directions for cell formation, tending to 
planar glide. The CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy exhibited high-
density dislocations and dislocation wall structures, which 
indicated that the alloy had low SFE. Moreover, the for -
mation of dislocation wall increased the resistance of 
dislocation movement, enhancing tensile strength of the 
 CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy. The interaction between high-
density dislocations and dislocation wall improved the 
strain hardening ability, delayed the occurrence of neck -
ing shrinkage and finally increased the plasticity of the

4 Conclusions
The CrFeNi2V0.5 and CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 high-entropy alloys 
were designed and fabricated. As well as the corresponding 
microstructure and compressive/tensile deformation behav-
ior were investigated. The conclusions are as follows.
1. The W-free CrFeNi 2V0.5 alloy exhibited a single-
FCC-phase columnar grain microstructure. And the 
 CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy possessed a typical cast dendritic 
structure, where a few lamellae structures appeared in the 
interdendritic region. Furthermore, the dendritic region 
belonged to the FCC structure, while the interdendritic 
region belonged to the mixing of FCC and σ phases.
2. Compressive results indicated that both alloys 
showed significant capability of strain (ε > 70%), and 
 CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy had also higher yield strength than 
that of the W-free CrFeNi2V0.5 alloy. The enhanced strength 
of the CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 alloy resulted from the solid solu-
tion strengthening and precipitation strengthening. Ductile 
fracture in FCC phase and brittle-like fracture in σ phase 
could clearly be observed in the CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy after 
compressive deformation.
3. The CrFeNi2V0.5W0.25 alloy exhibited excellent ten-
sile mechanical property with an ultimate tensile strength of 
640 MPa and a tensile elongation of 15.7%. Obvious ductile 
fracture accompanied with many deep dimples in the tensile 
deformation sample was observed. The tensile deformation 
mode of FCC phase in the CrFeNi
2V0.5W0.25 alloy was domi-
nated by planar glide, which was related to dislocation con-
figuration, high-density dislocations, and dislocation wall.
