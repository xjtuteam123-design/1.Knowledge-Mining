ABSTRACT 
In this paper, the microstructure, fracture toughness, and high-temperature tensile property of the directionally 
solidified Ti – 46Al – 5Nb-0.18C-0.3Si alloy are investigated. The lamellar orientation of the alloy is parallel to the 
growth direction under the action of the seed. Due to the convex shape of the solid-liquid interface, the core grain 
growth preferentially, and a large-size single crystal is formed in the steady-state region. The results show that 
the fracture toughness of the alloy increases with the increase of the orientation angle, and the fracture behavior 
changes from interlamellar to translamellar. The alloy with well-aligned lamellar microstructure possesses the 
excellent high-temperature tensile properties, and the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and elongation of the alloys 
are 636.7 ± 24.2 MPa and 23.9 ± 5.75% at 800 
◦

MPa and 22.6 ± 0.1% at 900 
◦

mechanism are discussed and concluded. 
1. Introduction 
γ -TiAl alloys are considered as an advanced lightweight high- 
temperature material to replace Ni-based superalloy in aero-engine 
blades due to its low density, high specific strength, high specific stiff -
ness, good creep resistance, and oxidation resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. However, 
low room temperature toughness and poor high-temperature strength 
restrict the further industrial application [ 4 ]. Recently, the development 
of the fully lamellar TiAl alloy has attracted wide attention due to their 
excellent comprehensive mechanical properties at room temperature 
and high temperature. With the increase of service temperature of 
aero-engine materials, it is necessary to further improve the mechanical 
properties of the TiAl alloy, especially the tensile and creep properties at 
high temperature. In the past few decades, researchers have been using 
alloying elements and advanced processing technology to continuously 
improve the strength and toughness of the TiAl alloy by controlling the 
alloy composition and corresponding microstructure [ 5 – 7 ]. 
In the literature [ 5 , 6 , 8 , 9 ], researchers have reported the alloying 
element (such as Nb, Cr, B, Y, C and Si) has a strong effect on mechanical 
property of γ -TiAl alloy. A large number of studies have shown that Nb is 
a useful element. When the content of Nb is in the range of 5 – 10 at. %, it 
can improve the oxidation resistance and creep properties of TiAl alloy, 
which can be attributed to the reduction of stacking fault energy, 
refinement of microstructure and reduction of diffusivity [ 10 , 11 ]. 
Recently, some studies have focused on C element, which is an impor -
tant interstitial element. By controlling its proper proportion (0.1 ~ 0.3 
at%), the strength of TiAl alloy can be improved due to its solution 
strengthening effect [ 12 ]. The Si element is inferred to improve the 
creep resistance by reducing the dislocation mobility as well as by a 
stabilization of the microstructure against thermal degradation [ 13 , 14 ]. 
For example, the high temperature creep properties of the typical TNM 
alloy are improved by adding a certain proportion of C and Si. The main 
strengthening mechanisms are solution strengthening and precipitation 
strengthening [ 5 , 15 , 16 ]. However, the existence of multiple phases ( α

them may weaken the strengthening effect of the alloying element at

properties. In the past decades, various hot working technique, such as 
high-pressure torsion, spark plasma sintering, water quenching and low 
temperature aging treatment, have been used to improve mechanical 
properties by refining the lamellar spacing and grain size. For example,

well controlled. Researchers have reported that Φ 20 mm TiAl alloy with 
the oriented full lamellar microstructure show excellent mechanical 
properties by EMCDS with seed method [ 25 , 26 ].

2. Experimental details 
The master ingot of Ti – 46Al – 5Nb-0.18C-0.3Si (at. %) and the seed 
ingot of Ti-43Al-3Si were prepared by cold crucible induction skull 
melting. The raw materials are mainly composed of Titanium (99.9 wt 
%), high pure Al (99.9 wt%), Al-75 wt%Nb matster alloy, TiC (99.9 wt 
%) and high pure Si (99.99 wt%). The actual composition of the master 
alloy was Ti-45.6Al-5.02Nb-0.18C-0.3Si, which measured by EDX and 
Carbon sulfur analyzer. The actual composition of master alloy is basi -
cally consistent with the nominal composition. 
The directional solidification experiments were performed using the 
electromagnetic confinement directional solidification (EMCDS) appa -
ratus equipment, as shown in Fig. 1 . The equipment is mainly composed 
of the induction coil, screen, withdrawing system and liquid coolant 
alloy. High frequency AC power supply is applied to the induction coil to 
heat and melt the seed bar and master bar. The seed and master bars 
with 30 mm in diameter were cut by electro-discharge machining 
(EDM). The seed and the master alloy were melted at a certain pro -
portion to form a melting zone before the directional solidification. The 
whole directional solidification process of TiAl alloy was carried out 
under the protection of high purity argon (99.99%). The specimen was 
pulled down at a growth rate of 10 μ m/s the size of the directionally 
solidified specimen was Φ 30 mm × 100 mm.

4.1. Alignment of Ti-46Al-5Nb-0.18C-0.3Si alloy by EMCDS 
In the process of EMCDS, electromagnetic pressure plays an impor -
tant role in the confined shape of molten metal. Under the action of 
alternating electric field, the confined shape of liquid metal without

metal is determined by a simple balance between static pressure and 
electromagnetic pressure. Only when the electromagnetic pressure and 
static pressure reach the dynamic balance, can the sample keep stable 
shaping, as shown in Fig. 3 (a). To maintain the continuous growth of 
the internal macro columnar crystal, appropriate temperature gradient 
(G) is needed. Generally speaking, the temperature gradient is directly 
proportional to the height of the molten zone during EMCDS. The higher 
the melting zone height, the greater the temperature gradient. When the 
melting zone is too high, the limit of electromagnetic pressure will be 
broken and the molten zone will collapse. Contrarily, if the melting zone 
height is low, the temperature gradient at the core of the sample reduces, 
which will easily lead to nucleation at the front of the solid-liquid 
interface and it is not conducive to the continuous growth of columnar 
crystal. Therefore, the balance of the force and the appropriate melting 
zone height are the key parameters for columnar crystal growth. 
In the directionally solidified TiAl alloy, good macrostructure can 
only obtain general mechanical properties, while the microstructure, 
namely lamellar orientation, determines the ultimate mechanical 
properties in the TiAl alloy with fully lamellar structure [ 21 ]. In direc -

orientation mainly include modifying solidification path method and 
seed method [ 18 , 41 ]. In this paper, the seed method is used to control 
the lamellar orientation in large size Ti-46Al-5Nb-0.18C-0.3Si alloy.

4.3. Strengthening mechanism 
Ti-46Al-5Nb-0.18C-0.3Si alloys with aligned lamellar microstructure 
exhibit remarkable high-temperature mechanical properties, some 
strengthening mechanisms are also discussed in the following lines. 
Firstly, the addition of a small amount of C and Si in the high Nb-TiAl 
alloy helps to inhibit the coarsening of lamellar structure, thus obtain -
ing a fine and uniform lamellar structure. Kim [ 27 ] et al. indicated that 
the addition of carbon element refined α
2
/ γ lamellar spacing. Thus, the 
first type of strengthening mechanism is fine lamellar strengthening. 
Secondly, solution strengthening of carbon in the α
2
/ γ lamellae would 
also increase the strength of the alloy. In general, the mechanical 
property of the TiAl alloy with C is higher than that of TiAl alloy without 
C, which is attribute to solution strengthening to a certain extent [ 27 ]. 
The solid solution of C in TiAl alloy increases the volume fraction of α

This is mainly because the solid solution of carbon in α

spacing will lead to more dislocation stacking to strengthen the alloy. In 
addition, it is found that the plastic deformation at different phases 
during high temperature tension is not uniform. As shown in Fig. 12 (a), 
some dislocations and nanoscale twins can be observed in the γ phase. 
On the one hand, the movement of dislocations is hindered by nanoscale 
twins, which requires higher stress when dislocations pass through the 
nanoscale twins, thus improving the work hardening ability of the alloy. 
On the other hand, some Shockley incomplete dislocations remain in the 
nanoscale twins when the dislocations pass through the nanoscale twins, 
which drives the movement of the nanoscale twins and improves the 
plastic deformation ability of the alloy [ 46 ]. Liu [ 47 ] et al. reported that 
the nanoscale twins not only effectively prevent the movement of 
dislocations, but also provide a lot of space to allow some dislocations to 
pass through, which can significantly improve the mechanical property 
of the TiAl alloys. Therefore, the strength and plasticity of the alloy are 
improved by the interaction between dislocation and nanoscale twins. 
The current researches mainly focus on the microstructure, fracture 
toughness and high temperature tensile properties of the alloy, but the 
research on the room temperature strength, plasticity and creep prop -
erties in high temperature has not been carried out. Therefore, future 
research directions can be outlined. Firstly, it is necessary to further 
study the room temperature mechanical properties and high tempera -
ture creep properties of the alloy in order to understand the application 
potential of the alloy more comprehensively and deeply. Secondly, the 
tensile strain hardening capacity of the Ti-46Al-5Nb-0.18C-0.3Si alloy 
should be improved by compositional and microstructure optimization. 
Adding a small amount of β stable element (Cr and Ta et al.) are two 
perspective strategies. The synergistic strengthening effect of Nb/Cr
