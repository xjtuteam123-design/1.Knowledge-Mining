Abstract 
 
The high temperature mechanical properties of dual phase heavy metal of 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloy 
were investigated in tension. The specimens were prepared by liquid phase sintering. Yield strength 
decreased and ductility increased as the testing temperature was increased to 300°C, reached a 
plateau at between 300 and 500°C and then decreased considerably. The fracture modes of alloys 
when deformed at room temperature were a mixture of intergranular fracture and transgranular 
cleavage. As the temperature was increased, the percentage of intergranular cleavage increased, 
although transgranular fracture also remained. At higher temperatures, substantial reduction in 
ductility and in yield strength was a result of loss of bonding strength between tungsten grains and 
matrix phase. 
 
1. Introduction 
 
Tungsten heavy alloys, with densities between 16.9 and 18.1 g/cm 3, represent the heaviest 
materials generally available to an engineer [1]. These alloys generally contain some combination 
of Ni, Fe, Cu and Co, the balance being W. The most common of these alloys are W-Ni-Fe alloys, 
which contain 85 to 98 wt.% tungsten with Ni: Fe weight ratios between 6:4 and 9:1. The 
mechanical properties of these alloys have been studied extensively [2-10]. This makes them useful 
for several applications and as including kinetic energy penetrators. Although polycrystalline 
tungsten is normally brittle at room temperature, the matrix imparts ductility to the composite [11-
12]. Considering the large number of parameters associated with this material, the variability in 
mechanical properties is not surprising. Generally, the factors influencing mechanical properties can 
be divided into three categories. First are factors which produce different results between studies 
such as composition, sintering temperature, test geometry, sintering atmosphere and heat treatment. 
Second are factors which account for the differing properties of similarly processed alloys, such as 
density, pore size, impurities and particle size. Third are factors which contribute to property 
variations to alloys sintered at the same temperature, such as thermal and gravitational gradients 
[13]. The prime objective of the current research was to evaluate the influence of temperature on the 
strength and ductility of high tungsten content 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloys.

2. Experimental 
 
Elemental powders of tungsten, nickel and iron were mixed to produce tungsten heavy alloys 
with composition 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe. The powders were consolidated into green compacts of tensile 
specimen, with 55-65 % of theoretical density by die pressing. During the heating cycle there was a 
dwell at 1100°C for 1 hour for oxide reduction and then at 1350°C for 1 hour in hydrogen 
atmosphere for solid-state sintering. Solid-state sintered tungsten heavy alloys were subsequently 
sintered at 1500°C for 30 minutes in hydrogen atmosphere. The sintering cycle followed for all 
experiments was 10°C/min up to 800°C and 5°C/min thereafter. During the cooling cycle, at 
1400°C the atmosphere changed from hydrogen to argon. In order to reduce the content of absorbed 
hydrogen and prevent embrittlement, the sintered samples of 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe were subsequently 
annealed in a vacuum at a temperature of 1100°C for 5 hours and then in Argon for 3 hours. They 
were then water quenched to circumvent the impurity segregation problem. The densities of the 
sintered specimens were measured by the Archimedes water immersion method. The tests were 
conducted at a strain rate of 0.5 mm/min in argon atmosphere. The temperature range for testing 
was room temperature to 1100°C and the heating was accomplished by electrical resistance and 
monitored by a thermocouple. In every test, it was brought to temperature and allowed to 
equilibrate for 30 minutes and then tested. At the end of each test, samples were water quenched in 
order to capture the in-situ microstructural parameters and thereby discover their effect on the 
properties of the alloys.

3. Results and discussion 
 
 Figure 1 (a) shows the variation of yield strength of 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloys at various 
temperatures. As can be seen, up to 275°C yield strength decreases as the temperature increases; 
there is then a plateau between 275°C and 500°C; then a gradual decrease up to 700°C and finally a 
sharp decrease beyond this temperature. Variation in the elongation of 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloys in 
relation to temperature is shown in Figure 1 (b). It can be seen that the percentage elongation shows 
an increase in line with the temperature up to 300°C , from 11% at room temperature to 19%. Then 
between 300°C and 500°C an anomalous behavior occurs, and after this temperature the ductility of 
the alloys drops dramatically, suggesting a ductile-brittle transition.

Microhardness indentations tests were run on alloys tested at different temperatures in order to 
find the contribution of W grains and matrix phase in deformation at various temperatures. Figure 2 
shows the hardness of tungsten grain and matrix phase at 10 gf load for a dwell of 10 sec. As can be 
seen, there is no significant change in hardness till 300 and then there is slight increase in hardness, 
which increases significantly after 425°C. It is important to note that the binder hardness does not 
significantly vary in these ranges in 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloys. 
 
 
 
 
Figure 3 Fractographs of tungsten heavy alloys tested at a) 325°C, b) 1100°C 
 
 Figure 3 shows scanning electron micrographs (SEM) of the fracture surfaces of specimens which 
had been tested at different temperatures. It was observed form the fracture morphology that, as the 
temperature increases from room temperature to 275°C, failure occurs by mixed mode, but mainly 
as a result of fractured tungsten grains. From 300 0C to 500 0C failure still occurs by mixed mode but 
now the failure is predominantly in the matrix phase, although fracture is still present in the 
tungsten grains (Figure 3 a). In this temperature range tungsten grains show ductility and there is a 
correlation between fracture surface appearance and ductility levels. The strength of the tungsten

4. Conclusion 
 
 Based on the above results, the following conclusions about the high temperature mechanical 
behavior of 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloys can be drawn: 
 The yield strength decrease and the ductility increase up to 300 0C and then reach a plateau - 
between 300 0C and 500 0C. Three different fracture stages can be observed. From room temperature 
to 3000C, there is failure in both tungsten grains and the matrix phase, but cleavage in the tungsten 
grains is dominant. Between 300 0C and 500 0C, failure occurs almost exclusively through 
intergranular failure. At these temperatures the tungsten grains exhibit ductile qualities. At the 
higher temperature range dramatic decrease in both yield strength and ductility is a result of less 
bonding strength between tungsten and the matrix phase, because the test temperature higher then 
the 0.5 T m of matrix phase changes the characteristics of the matrix phase.
