ABSTRACT
Tungsten-based alloys exhibit poor printability during laser beam powder bed fusion (PBF-LB) due to their high 
crack susceptibility index (CSI) and intrinsic brittleness associated with a high ductile-to-brittle transition 
temperature, leading to severe solidification cracking in additively manufactured components. Addressing this 
challenge requires alloy design strategies that reduce crack susceptibility while maintaining the high- 
temperature capability of tungsten alloys. In this study, two ternary alloys, W – 10Nb – xC ( x = 0.45 and 1.0 wt. 
%), were designed using an integrated computational materials engineering (ICME) framework to investigate the 
role of interstitial carbon in mitigating cracking during PBF-LB processing. The crack susceptibility index was 
evaluated using CALPHAD-based thermodynamic calculations coupled with heat-transfer and material-flow 
simulations representative of PBF-LB conditions. A modified back-diffusion treatment was incorporated to ac -
count for solute redistribution under the high cooling rates associated with variations in laser scanning speed. 
Increasing carbon content promotes a higher volume fraction of carbide phases, which is typically expected to 
increase brittleness and cracking susceptibility. However, CALPHAD-based CSI calculations predict that the 
lower eutectic alloy (0.45 wt.% C) exhibits higher cracking susceptibility than the 1.0 wt.% C alloy, consistent 
with experimental observations. The improved printability of the higher-carbon alloy arises from the formation 
of coarser eutectic structures that enhance liquid backfilling and accommodate tensile strains during solidifi -
cation. Although both alloys exhibit compressive strengths of ~1200 MPa at room temperature, the higher 
fraction of WC and NbC carbides in the 1.0 wt.% C alloy reduces strain relative to 0.45 wt.% C alloy.
1. Introduction
Pure tungsten (W) or tungsten alloys (WAs) are preferred engineer -
ing materials for the plasma facing, heating elements, medical radio -
activity shielding, and rocket ignition tube due to their high melting 
temperature, high density, high stiffness and strength, and low thermal 
expansion among body-centered cubic metallic materials [ 1 – 3 ]. How -
ever, the application is limited due to the poor formability of poly -
crystalline W at room temperature [ 4 ]. The processing and machining of 
W is extremely difficult for making complex geometry due to the very 
high brittle-to-ductile transition temperature (DBTT), around 200 
◦
C – 400 
◦
C, and brittle at room temperature [ 5 , 6 ]. Conventional 
manufacturing processes such as machining, powder metallurgy, and 
powder injection molding have been used for producing WAs parts for 
aerospace structural applications, but with limited success [ 7 ]. The laser 
beam powder bed fusion (PBF-LB) additive manufacturing (AM) process 
can build complex geometry by selectively melting thin layers of metal 
powder layer-by-layer. This capability makes high-temperature re -
fractory WAs a promising candidate for exploration using the PBF-LB 
process.
The research for crack-resistant PBF-LB WAs has been continuously 
growing by controlling the process parameters, substrate heating, 
remelting, and scanning strategies [ 8 – 12 ]. However, fully crack-free 
WAs have not been reported to date [ 13 – 16 ]. Printing WAs parts 
using PBF-LB faces major difficulties like microcracking, brittle oxide 
phase, and lack of fusion [ 17 , 18 ]. Although substrate heating helps

Pre-alloying W with elements such as Cr, Mo, Ni, Ti, V, and Zr has 
shown a modest reduction in microcracking by lowering the DBTT; 
however, this improvement often comes at the cost of reduced high- 
temperature mechanical strength [ 26 – 31 ]. The addition of Re im -
proves ductility by reducing grain size and activating additional {112} 
slip planes through solid solution alloying with W, a phenomenon 
known as solution softening [ 32 , 33 ]. However, as Re has high neutron 
absorption and is an expensive rare-earth metal, alternative alloying 
elements are being explored to achieve similar effects [ 34 ]. The addition 
of Ta to W slightly reduces the grain size, minimizes segregation of 
alloying elements, and mitigates microcracking during constitutional 
undercooling [ 15 ]. Additionally, Ta enhances the strength and hardness 
at higher temperatures; however, it does not suppress the DBTT, 
resulting in lower fracture toughness for an increase in Ta content, ~1 to 
5 wt.%, as reported by Rieth et al. [ 30 ]. Alloying with Ti up to 4% 
increased the grain size during sintering and partially improved 
toughness and strength [ 35 , 36 ]. However, Ti does not reduce the DBTT 
due to a lack of solubility in W. Additionally, Ti forms inclusions that 
segregate at the grain boundaries at 600 
◦

granular cracking [ 36 ]. Recently, dispersion strengthening carbide and

available information is limited [ 39 ]. In the absence of a comprehensive 
thermodynamic database, understanding the mechanisms governing 
crack susceptibility and role of interstitial solutes such as C, N, and O 
remains challenging [ 40 – 42 ].
The selection of alloying elements is crucial for designing PBF-LB 
WAs, as it directly influences the understanding and modeling of their 
crack resistance behavior. In this context, the alloying elements in our 
study were selected based on the following motivation: the Group V 
refractory elements, Nb and Ta have similar properties to increase high- 
temperature strength and creep resistance due to higher self-diffusivity. 
Adding Nb is cost-effective with reduced density (8.58 g/cm
3
) than Ta

Ti, Cr, Ni, and V, and is completely soluble in solid and liquid states, 
facilitating effective alloying. The addition of Nb promotes the forma -

and inhibit the formation of brittle W oxides [ 43 – 45 ]. In our previous 
study for W-Nb-C alloy, the eutectic terminal solidification with C led to 
crack-free build [ 46 ]. Planned addition of interstitial C is essential to 
form hard carbides. The coarsening of carbides is slower than nitrides 
and oxides [ 39 ]. During the repeated thermal cycling inherent to 
PBF-LB, C diffuses and forms stable metal carbides even in the partially 
melted state. The presence of both eutectic and metal carbide phases 
collectively contributes to enhanced crack resistance in PBF-LB pro -
cessed WAs. While carbides have been introduced for strengthening and 
grain refinement with modern tools, the role of interstitial solutes in 
solidification cracking of WAs has not been reported in experimental 
investigations. During solidification from high temperature, C acts as an 
inoculant refining the grain size and breaking columnar grains 
commonly observed in AM into fine equiaxed grains [ 47 – 49 ]. Carbon 
also reacts with excess oxygen to form CO and CO
2

oxide-related defects. Furthermore, it forms a eutectic phase with W, 
thereby improving printability.
In this work, the thermo-chemo-mechanical effects at nanometer to 
micrometer length scale, inherited from rapid solidification, were 
investigated using microscopy and nanoindentation, alongside pro -
cessing parameter optimization, to enable the design and fabrication of 
crack-free W-10Nb-xC ( x = 0.45, 1.0) alloy via PBF-LB. The nano- to 
micro-scale mechanical properties were correlated with dynamic 
microstructural evolution, including constitutional undercooling and 
the formation of secondary metal carbide phases during in-situ alloying 
of the ternary W-Nb-C system. Furthermore, a heat transfer-material 
flow model, coupled with Thermo-Calc database, was employed to un -
derstand the problem of crack susceptibility and role of interstitial C in 
designing new crack-free WAs for PBF-LB process.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Alloy design and powder preparation
Before powder mixing, the Thermo-Calc database (TCHEA6) was 
employed to simulate the solidification path, eutectic phase, and crack 
susceptibility index (CSI) for W-10Nb-xC ( x = 0.2 to 1.4) wt.% alloys 
with total alloying content restricted to < 15% in W. The role of inter -
stitial C on eutectic fraction was simulated as a fast diffuser in liquid to 
replicate the rapid solidification in PBF-LB. The gas-atomized commer -
cially pure W and Nb powders were purchased from Tekna Advanced 
Materials and H.C. Starck GmbH, respectively, for PBF-LB processing. 
High-purity C powder, sourced from Nanoshel UK, was used for alloying 
and printing. The powders were weighed and mixed using the V-shaped 
single-arm mixer for 8 h at 200 rpm to break the agglomerates and 
ensure thorough mixing. The mixed alloy powder was then dried in a 
vacuum oven (740 mm Hg) at 70 
◦
C for 14 h to remove the moisture. 
Powder morphology was examined under a scanning electron micro -
scope (Thermo Scientific Apreo 2C SEM) and detailed shown in sup -
plementary file Fig. S1. The particle size distribution was measured with 
a MICROTRAC MRB – S3500 analyzer, yielding Dv(10) = 9.58 μ m, Dv 
(50) = 22.76 μ m, and Dv(90) = 54.16 μ m, as shown in Fig. 1(a) . The 
mixed alloy powder passed without obstruction through the Carney 
Funnel and Hall Funnel, indicating improved flowability after drying. 
The Hausner ratio, calculated as the ratio of tap density to apparent 
density, was 1.26, based on 3000 taps at 6 Hz, suggesting enhanced 
compaction of the blended elemental powders. Elemental analysis of 
mixed alloy powder using LECO ON736 revealed oxygen and nitrogen 
levels of 0.0896 ± 0.006 (wt.%) and 0.0140 ± 0.003 (wt.%), respectively, 
below the acceptable thresholds for feedstock materials used in AM.

4. Discussion
The innovative design of an alloy using the Thermo-Calc database 
(TCHEA6), combined with optimized process parameters selected, 
enabled the fabrication of near crack-free W-10Nb-xC ( x = 0.45, 1.0) 
alloys via PBF-LB, as shown in Fig. 4 (a)-(d). A high relative density of 
98.16 pct and > 99 pct was achieved, based on a theoretical density of 
16.56 ± 0.1 g/cm
3 
and 16.05 ± 0.2 g /cm
3
, respectively for 0.45 wt.% and 
1.0 wt.% of C with spherical and fine-sized powders contributing 
significantly to densification. This approach enabled building complex 
shapes, such as convergent-divergent nozzles, even with a lower power 
PBF-LB machine (limited to 200 W). The measured density for the 
simple cube to complex nozzles is > 98% which is higher than the ones 
reported in the past for W-Nb alloy systems printed via PBF-LB [ 10 , 46 ]. 
The alloy design and process strategy for W-10Nb-xC ( x = 0.45, 1.0) 
alloys serve as a foundation for (i) crack susceptibility in refractory al -
loys under PBF-LB conditions, (ii) microstructure evolution during so -
lidification and post-solidification, and (iii) corresponding 
strengthening and ductility mechanisms.

4.3. Strengthening mechanism and ductility of W-Nb-C alloy
The heterogeneous phase evolution observed in Figs. 6 and 7 , con -
tributes to enhanced strength in the eutectic phase as compared to pure 
W, as demonstrated in the nanoindentation results shown in Figs. 10 (b) 
and (e) for W-10Nb-xC ( x = 0.45, 1.0) alloy. The strength and limited 
plastic deformation are observed during compression testing of W-Nb-C 
alloy and shown in Figs. 11 (a) and (b). This mechanical response arises 
from several microstructural strengthening mechanisms, including: (i) 
the solid solution strengthening from W-Nb, (ii) elemental segregation 
of Nb and C, (iii) grain refinement induced by C addition, and (iv) 
dispersion strengthening through formation of carbides including W
2
C, 
and NbC. Despite the limited ductility, a certain degree of plasticity is 
observed for post-processing operations such as machining, essential for 
parts printed via PBF-LB. The alloying with Nb and C helps to facilitate 
strengthening by narrowing the dislocation core in BCC materials, which 
in turn increases the Peierls stress – a key barrier to dislocation motion.

From nanoindentation results, the W-Nb matrix region exhibits a 
hardness of ~ 11 GPa, which is significantly higher than pure W, ~ 9 
GPa. The values become significantly higher at the interface of the 
script-like lamellar structure due to modulus mismatch between the 
carbides (e.g., W ₂ C, NbC) and the W-Nb matrix. This indicates that the 
heterogeneous microstructure enhances the properties by eutectic so -
lidification and reduces the microcracking.
Overall, the selected C contents of 0.45 and 1.0 wt.% C in the W-Nb-C 
system, combined with fine spherical powders and reduced layer 
thickness effectively suppress the crack susceptibility during the PBF-LB 
additive manufacturing. While increase in C content lowers the ductility 
and contributes to brittle failure, the outcomes from current research 
point to the importance of material-process co-design to unlock the 
potential of advancing the additive manufacturing of high temperature 
refractory materials. Furthermore, adding scavenging elements and 
controlling high-temperature oxide formation offers further enhance -
ment in structural and mechanical properties and reduces crack sus -
ceptibility. Adding quantitative evaluation of stress accumulation 
during solid bridging, are expected to significantly advance alloy design 
strategies for PBF-LB refractory metals.

5. Conclusions

of PBF-LB tungsten alloy. The conclusions of the current study are as

isomorphous system with C, and is printable over the energy den -
