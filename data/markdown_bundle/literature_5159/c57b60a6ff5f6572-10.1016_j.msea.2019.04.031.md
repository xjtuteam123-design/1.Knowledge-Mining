ABSTRACT
The solid-solution strengthening e ﬀect for the single and simultaneous addition of Zr and Si on the compression
strength and the creep property in Ti-10Al-2Nb based alloys (at%) with an equiaxed α phase designed by us, was
investigated. The compression strengths of these alloys were investigated at temperatures between room tem-
perature and 650 °C. The largest solid-solution strengthening eﬀ ect was obtained for the alloys with Zr and Si
simultaneously added, followed by alloys with the single addition of 2Zr or 0.5Si. For the same amounts of
addition elements, the solid-solution strengthening eﬀ ect of Si was larger than that of Zr. This was attributed to
the larger atomic size mis ﬁt between Ti and Si than that of between Ti and Zr. A creep test was conducted for Ti-
10Al-2Nb-2Zr and Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si within a temperature range of 550 – 650 °C under an applied stress
between 137 and 240 MPa. The alloy with Si exhibited a longer creep life for all tested conditions due to a large
solid-solution strengthening eﬀ ect and high compression strength.
The deformation mechanisms of the compressive deformation and tensile creep deformation were analyzed
using an Arrhenius-type equation. Considering the stress exponent and activation energy, the deformation
mechanism of the compressive deformation was identi ﬁed as low-temperature power-law creep controlled by
dislocation core di ﬀusion. While, the deformation mechanism of the creep deformation was identi ﬁed as high-
temperature power-law creep controlled through lattice di ﬀusion.
1. Introduction
Heat-resistant titanium alloys, particularly near- α titanium alloys,
are light and strong at high temperature and are used for blades and
disks in the compressors of jet engines. Although near- α titanium alloys
have excellent creep properties and an oxidation resistance of up to
600 °C, their properties remarkably deteriorate at a temperature higher
than 600 °C [ 1– 4]. Therefore, the operation temperature of
TIMETAL834 and Ti-1100 alloys, which have the highest durable
temperature among titanium alloys, reaches up to 600 °C. Because the
operation temperature exceeds 600 °C in a high-pressure compressor in
large-sized jet engines, Ti alloys have recently been replaced with Ni
based-superalloys. However, the speci ﬁc gravity of Ni-based super-
alloys is approximately twice that of titanium alloys, which causes a
decrease in the heat e ﬃciency. To improve the heat e ﬃciency of jet
engines, the operation temperature of titanium alloys should be in-
creased to above 600 °C. Recently, TiAl, which is lighter than titanium
alloys and has excellent creep properties at over 700 °C, has been
focused upon as a new jet engine material, although an improvement in
the brittleness remains necessary [ 5– 7], particularly for disk materials
that need more reliability than a blade. Therefore, improvement in the
oxidation resistance and creep of titanium alloys at over 600 °C is im-
portant.
It is well known that the main alloying elements for near- α titanium
alloys are Al, Sn, Zr, Mo, Nb, and Si. Solid-solution strengthening by
such alloying elements and precipitation strengthening by α
2-Ti3Al and
silicide-(Ti, Zr) 5Si3 are the main strengthening mechanisms of α tita-
nium alloys [ 8,9]. Ti alloys are oxidized at high temperature in an air
atmosphere, which forms TiO 2 on the surface of the alloys [ 10,11]. At
high temperature, thin TiO 2 becomes loose and porous, and hence, the
diﬀusion speed of oxygen into Ti alloys increases. The oxidation rate
constants ( kp(g2cm−4s−1)) of pure Ti and TIMETAL834 at 750 °C are
1.04 × 10−11 [12] and 4.92 × 10 −12 [13], respectively. The oxidation
of TIMETAL834 is excellent, with 50 times the oxidation resistance of
pure Ti [ 13], although a further improvement in the oxidation re-
sistance is required.

To improve the heat resistance of titanium alloys, we ﬁrst attempted
to improve the oxidation resistance. As model alloys, Ti-15Al-Sn-Nb
alloys (Sn + Nb = 5at%) designed to have a α and α2 two-phase
structure according to a tertiary phase diagram were prepared, and the
oxidation behavior was investigated at 750 °C [ 14]. The oxidation re-
sistance improved when Nb was increased, and decreased when Sn was
increased [ 14]. To clarify the e ﬀect of Nb, the oxidation behavior was
investigated using Ti-(10 – 15)Al-(2– 5)Nb (at%) alloys [ 15]. The oxida-
tion resistance improved with an increase in Nb, but the e ﬀect was
saturated at approximately 2at%Nb. The e ﬀect of Zr on the oxidation
resistance in Ti-(10 – 15)Al-2Nb alloys was also investigated [ 16]. The
oxidation resistance of Ti-15Al-2Zr alloy was higher than that of Ti-
15Al-2Nb alloy. In contrast, the oxidation resistances of Ti-15Al-2Nb-
2Zr and Ti-15Al-2Zr were almost the same. One advantage of the si-
multaneous addition of Nb and Zr is the improvement in the adhesion of
the oxide layer. The mechanical properties, particularly the e ﬀect of α
2-
Ti3Al precipitates on the strength, were then investigated [ 17]. By
precipitating α2-Ti3Al, the strength was greatly improved in the mod-
erate temperature range of 300 °C – 450 °C, although the e ﬀect of the
precipitates on the strength was weaker at 600 °C or higher. This in-
dicates that precipitation hardening by α2-Ti3Al nearly disappeared at
over 600 °C, and that solid-solution strengthening is the key strength-
ening mechanism at over 600 °C.
In this study, the e ﬀects of single and simultaneous addition of Zr
and Si, on the solid-solution strengthening of a single α phase during
compression test and tensile creep test in Ti-Al-Nb alloys were in-
vestigated. We selected an Al addition of 10 at%, in which α
2-Ti3Al was
not precipitated, and the amount of Nb as 2 at%, which provided ex-
cellent oxidation resistance in a previous study [ 15,16]. The deforma-
tion mechanisms of the compressive deformation and tensile creep
deformation were analyzed using an Arrhenius-type equation.
2. Materials and methods
Ingots of α-Ti alloys (1.1 kg) with nominal compositions of Ti-10Al-
2Nb, Ti-10Al-2Nb-0.5Si, Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr, and Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si
(at%) (Ti-6Al-4Nb, Ti-6Al-4Nb-0.3Si, Ti-6Al-4Nb-4Zr, and Ti-6Al-4Nb-
4Zr-0.3Si (wt%)) were produced using a cold-crucible levitation
melting method. The ingots were forged at 900 °C and groove-rolled to
a 94% reduction at 900 °C in the α+β phase region, forming square
rods of size 14 mm. Small sections of the sample, with a thickness of
6 mm, were cut from the square rod and subjected to heat treatment.
The sections were heat-treated at di ﬀerent temperatures of 800 °C,
900 °C, 950 °C, and 1000 °C for 3 h, followed by water quenching.
Samples of size 2.5 × 2.5 × 5.5 mm
3 were cut from the heat-treated
samples at 800 °C for the compression tests. The compression tests were
carried out at room temperature, 300 °C, 450 °C, 600 °C, and 650 °C
with a strain rate of 3 × 10
−4/s. Samples for the compression test were
held at the test temperature for 20 min prior to testing. Tensile creep
tests were carried out for Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr and Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si
after heat treatment at 800 °C for 3 h followed by water quenching. The
dimensions of the creep test sample were 6 mm gauge diameter and
30 mm gauge length, which are in accordance with of the Japanese
Industrial Standard (JIS) Z 2271. Creep tests were conducted at 600 °C
and 650 °C under 137 MPa and at 550 °C under 137, 190, and 240 MPa
in air.
To investigate the microstructure of the heat-treated samples, X-ray
diﬀraction (XRD, RIGAKU TTR-III) and ﬁeld emission gun – scanning
electron microscopy (FEG-SEM, JEOL JSM 7000 F or 7001 F with
electron backscatter di ﬀraction (EBSD)) were used. XRD was carried
out on a plate with dimensions of 5 × 15 × 1 mm
3 at room temperature
using Cu-K α radiation, at 50 kV and 300 mA. Samples for SEM and
EBSD were embedded in resins and polished using polishing paper and
SiO
2. The microstructure was observed with an acceleration voltage at
15kV.

5. Discussion
To understand the e ﬀects of Zr and Si addition on the compression
strength in Ti-10Al-2Nb, the grain size and mis ﬁt of atomic radius be-
tween the solvent and solute elements are discussed.

solvent and solute elements is e ﬀective for the solid-solution strength.

Table 4 summarizes the lattice parameters of the tested alloys
measured using XRD. Compared to pure Ti, the lattice parameter of the
base alloy (Ti-10Al-2Nb) containing more Al with a smaller atomic
radius than Ti becomes small. The lattice parameter increases through
the addition of Zr and decreases through the addition of Si. This change
can be expected from the atomic radius of the solute elements.
The tendency of the mis ﬁt of the atomic radius is consistent with the
trend regarding the grain size. That is, the grain size of the alloy with a
large mis ﬁt of the atomic radius becomes small. It is considered that a
large misﬁt of the atomic radius causes a large lattice strain, and a large
lattice strain disturbs the grain growth, resulting in a smaller grain size.
Thus, the strengthening mechanism by the addition of Zr or Si is solid-
solution hardening owing to a mis ﬁt of the atomic radius and smaller
grain size compared with Ti-10Al-2Nb.
When Zr and Si are simultaneously added to Ti-10Al-2Nb, the
strength at up to 300 °C is almost the same as that of alloy with single Zr
or Si addition, but is higher at over 300 °C, as shown in Fig. 5. The
lattice parameter of Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si is almost the same as that of
alloy with a single Zr or Si addition; consequently, the larger solid-
solution strengthening e ﬀect may be caused by the multiple e ﬀects by
Zr and Si at over 300 °C. Another strengthening mechanism is silicide
precipitation in Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si, as shown in Fig. 4. Although the

The calculation results are summarized in Table 5. The stress ex-
ponent obtained from the compression test is between 6 and 7. The
activation energy for deformation increases as the number of solute
elements increases from 88 kJ/mol in Ti-10Al-2Nb to 126 kJ/mol in Ti-
10Al-2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si. An increase in the activation energy indicates an
increase in solid-solution hardening owing to a large lattice distortion
due to an increase of the atomic radius. The activation energies for the
deformation of pure Ti for dislocation core di ﬀusion, grain boundary
diﬀusion, and lattice di ﬀusion are 97, 97, and 242 kJ/mol, respectively
[20]. The activation energy for the deformation of Ti-10Al-2Nb, Ti-
10Al-2Nb-2Zr, and Ti-10Al-2Nb-0.5Si is close to 97 kJ/mol, and that of
Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si is lower than 242 kJ/mol, indicating a de-
formation governed by the dislocation core or grain boundary di ﬀusion.
Because the stress exponent is between 5 and 7, it is considered that the
deformation is controlled by a dislocation climb. Thus, the deformation
mechanism of these alloys is considered as low-temperature power-law
creep controlled by dislocation core di ﬀusion, rather than grain
boundary di ﬀusion. The deformation mechanism for compression de-
formation does not change by the addition of Zr and Si although there is
some di ﬀerence in the activation energy for deformation in Ti-10Al-
2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si.
For creep deformation, the stress exponents of Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr and
Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si were 5.9 and 3.4, and the activation energies for
deformation were 290 and 272 kJ/mol, respectively. The activation
energy for deformation was close to the di ﬀusion coe ﬃcient of the
lattice di ﬀusion of pure Ti, 242 kJ/mol. It is thus considered that the
deformation mechanism of these two alloys is high-temperature power-
law creep governed by lattice di ﬀusion, with no signi ﬁcant di ﬀerence
by the addition of Si.
Silicide of approximately 1 μm in size is precipitated in Ti-10Al-
2Nb-2Zr-0.5 Si, as shown in Fig. 4. The stress exponent estimated for Ti

The creep deformation mechanism of α-Ti alloys has been in-
vestigated for many years. For example, creep tests were conducted at
temperature ranges between room temperature and 600 °C for pure Ti
[25]. The grain size of the α phase is approximately 100 μm. The stress
exponent at 500 °C was 4.5, and the activation energy was 240 kJ/mol.
It was concluded that the deformation mechanism of pure Ti is high-
temperature dislocation creep controlled by lattice di ﬀusion. This result
is consistent with the present results.
In another example of Ti-6Al-4V, the stress exponent was 3.6 for a
bimodal structure at 600 °C between 125 and 319 MPa [ 26]. The stress
exponent and activation energy of Ti-6Al-4V were systematically ob-
tained for a creep test at 500 °C within the range of 312 – 520 MPa,
600 °C within the range of 125 – 319 MPa, and 700 °C within the range of
14– 56 MPa [ 26]. The microstructure consisted of equiaxed α grains of
size 10 μm and the β phase at grain boundaries of the α phase, which is
close to the microstructure of our study. The stress exponent was 9.67,
4.25, and 2.62, at 500 °C, 600 °C, and 700 °C, respectively, and the
activation energy was 319 kJ/mol, which is close to the activation en-
ergies of 303 and 329 kJ/mol for the self-di ﬀusion of Ti and inter-
diﬀusion of Al in Ti. It was concluded that the creep was controlled by a
dislocation climb; that is, it was controlled by di ﬀusion. Although the
microstructure of Ti-6Al-4V is similar to that of our alloys, that is, small
equiaxed α grains of size 10 μm, the stress exponent was higher be-
tween 500 °C and 600 °C in Ti-6Al-4V, than in Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr alloys
(Ti-6Al-4Nb-4Zr, wt%). A possible reason for this is that the strength of
Ti-6Al-4V is 521 and 377 MPa at 500 °C and 600 °C, respectively, which
is higher than that of Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr alloys at approximately
300– 350 MPa at 600 °C, as shown in Fig. 4. This indicates a strong solid-
solution hardening e ﬀect of V in Ti-6Al-4V.
The creep behavior of Ti-6Al-4V with a bi-modal microstructure at a
grain size of approximately 30 μm was also investigated systematically
at 450 °C, 500 °C, 550 °C, and 600 °C at between 100 and 600 MPa [ 27].
At 550 °C, the stress exponent was 4.3, and the activation energy was
approximately
250 kJ/mol, which is close to our results. The de-
formation mechanism was con ﬁrmed to be dislocation climb, that is,
the power-law creep, owing to the activation energy being close to that
of the self-di ﬀusion of pure Ti.

6. Conclusions
(1) The compression strength of Ti-10Al-2Nb, Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr, Ti-
10Al-2Nb-0.5Si, and Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si (at%) with an equiaxed
α phase obtained using heat treatment at 800 °C were investigated
at test temperatures between room temperature and 650 °C. The
solid-solution strengthening e ﬀect by the single and simultaneous
addition of Zr and Si in Ti-10Al-2Nb-based alloys (at%) was esti-
mated from the compression strength. The e ﬀect of the solid-solu-
tion strengthening by Si is stronger than that of Zr owing to a large
atomic size mis ﬁt with Ti. The Si addition decreases the grain size
of the α phase, and a small grain size also increases the strength.
(2) The deformation mechanism of the compression test was analyzed
using an Arrhenius-type equation. The stress exponent and activa-
tion energy of the compression test are 5.9 – 7.1 and 88– 126 kJ/mol,
respectively. The deformation mechanism of the compression test is
low-temperature power-law creep controlled by dislocation core
diﬀusion. Although silicide of size 1 μm is formed in Ti-10Al-2Nb-
2Zr-0.5Si, the distribution is extremely sparse, and a small stress
exponent indicates that silicide has no e ﬀect on improving the
strength. This implies that the addition of Zr and Si does not change
the deformation mechanism.
(3) The creep behavior within a temperature range between 550 °C and
650 °C under an applied stress between 137 and 240 MPa was in-
vestigated for Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr and Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr-0.5Si with the
equiaxed α phase obtained through heat treatment at 800 °C. Si
addition increases the creep life under all tested conditions.
(4) The deformation mechanism of the creep test was also analyzed
using an Arrhenius-type equation. The stress exponent and activa-
tion energy of the creep deformation are 3.4 – 5.9 and 272– 290 kJ/
mol, respectively. The deformation mechanism is high-temperature
power-law creep controlled by lattice di ﬀusion, with no di ﬀerence
by the addition of Si. A small stress exponent of Ti-10Al-2Nb-2Zr-
0.5Si indicates that grain boundary sliding also occurs owing to a
small grain size of approximately 9 μm.
