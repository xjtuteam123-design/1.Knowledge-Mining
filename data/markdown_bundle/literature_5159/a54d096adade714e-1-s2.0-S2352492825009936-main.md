1. Introduction
Nuclear reactors structural materials are subjected to extreme service 
conditions, including high temperatures, high-dose irradiation, and se -
vere oxidative corrosion, especially for high-temperature fast reactor 
structural materials that require safe service at 600 ℃ [1 – 3] . Traditional 
reactor structural materials, low-carbon stainless steel, exhibit signifi -
cant performance degradation at elevated temperatures. For instance, at 
the operating temperature of 350 
◦
C in pressurized water reactors, the 
yield strength is only 220 – 400 MPa, which is insufficient to meet the 
strength requirements of structural components in advanced reactor 
designs [4,5] . In recent years, multi-principal element alloy (MPEA) 
have emerged as a promising new class of structural materials, attracting 
considerable attention from researchers worldwide due to their poten -
tial to overcome the challenges posed by these extreme service condi -
tions [6 – 8] . Leveraging unique intrinsic effects, MPEAs exhibit 
exceptional mechanical properties, positioning them as one of the most 
promising candidates for structural components in future Generation IV 
nuclear reactors.
The components flexibility of multi-principal element alloys pro -
vided a broad space for achieving the mechanical properties that bal -
ance both strength and ductility. Compared to FCC MPEAs, BCC MPEAs 
exhibit higher strength and superior high-temperature resistance 
[9 – 11] . Taking into account the requirements for neutron economy and 
reduced radioactive activity in reactor structural materials, traditional 
high-temperature BCC MPEAs often contain high concentrations of re -
fractory elements such as Nb, Mo, Hf, Ta, and W. However, their in -
dustrial applications are constrained by long radioactive decay periods, 
high density, and severe brittleness [12 – 14] . Low activation elements 
such as Ti, V, Cr, and Al can be activated by high-energy neutrons in the 
reactor to reduce their radioactivity to an operable level in a relatively 
short period of time. In addition, previous studies [6,15 – 18] have Pre -
vious studies have confirmed that adding V and Cr elements to Ti-base 
alloy can achieve excellent plasticity while maintaining high strength. 
Moreover, V and Cr are structure stable elements in the BCC Ti-base 
alloy. The addition of Al can improve the bonding strength between 
atoms in Ti-base alloy, significantly enhancing the alloy ’ s specific 
strength concurrently maintaining preeminent plasticity. As a result, 
these elements are considered ideal candidates for reactor structural 
materials.

primarily due to grain boundary strengthening. The grain size of the 
CR800 alloy is significantly reduced to 6 μ m, compared to 379 μ m in the

CR800 alloy. The difference in grain orientation across the grain 
boundaries increases the resistance to dislocation movement. This 
resistance arises from two main factors: On the one hand, for a given 
applied stress, the slip orientations of adjacent grains are different. The 
slip system with the most favorable orientation in one grain may not 
align optimally with that in neighboring grains, increasing the difficulty 
of dislocation motion across the boundary. On the other hand, disloca -
tions gliding within grains are obstructed at grain boundaries, forming 
dislocation pile-ups. These pile-ups generate back stresses near bound -
aries, requiring increased external stress to sustain plastic deformation.

This mechanism critically contributes to material strengthening [48,49] .
Secondly, nano-precipitation strengthening contributes to the 
improved mechanical properties. TEM analysis revealed that the 
nucleation sites for the Ti-rich nanoprecipitates are located at the grain 
boundaries of the BCC matrix. These nanoprecipitates formed at the 
grain boundaries, can impede dislocation emission and motion, thereby 
enhancing the alloy ’ s strength [50,51] . Notably, FCC-Ti is a metastable 
structure, which is uncommon in titanium alloys. Previous studies [52] , 
have shown that independent HCP-Ti can transform into FCC-Ti through 
thermally induced phase transitions. Once the transformation is com -
plete, the FCC structure remains stable even under applied stress and at 
ambient temperatures. This transformation mechanism involves partial 
dislocations sliding to convert the HCP ABAB stacking sequence into the 
FCC ABCAB stacking sequence. When the partial dislocations reside in 
every other close-packed plane, it is referred to as "ordered slip," and the 
HCP structure transitions to the FCC structure, affecting the alloy ’ s 
properties [53] . Compared to HCP-Ti and BCC-Ti, FCC-Ti exhibits the 
superior plasticity, which enhances the alloy ductility [52] .
Finally, the reduction in dislocation density in coarse-grained alloys 
further contributes to improved ductility [25] . Dislocations are the 
primary carriers of plastic deformation in materials. When an external 
force is applied, dislocations move through the crystal by slip or climb, 
causing localized lattice deformation. A lower dislocation density im -
plies fewer dislocations within the material, resulting in less resistance 
to dislocation motion, making the material more susceptible to slip and 
deformation under external stress.

be classified into two categories: grain-boundary strengthening and

5. Conclusions
In summary, a novel lightweight Ti ₄₅ V ₄₅ Cr ₅ Al ₅ MPEA with an excel -
lent balance of strength and ductility has been developed, conducted 
tensile properties, phase structure analysis, microstructure observation. 
The following conclusion can be drawn: 
(1) After rolling and annealing heat treatment, the room-temperature 
tensile yield strength of the Ti ₄₅ V ₄₅ Cr ₅ Al ₅ MPEA is approximately 
1 GPa, with a fracture elongation of about 24 % and a specific 
strength of 257 MPa ⋅ cm³ /g. At 600
◦
C, the tensile yield strength 
is 676 MPa, with a fracture elongation of approximately 41 %.
(2) After annealing, a Ti-rich phase precipitates in the alloy. As the 
annealing temperature increases, the Ti-rich phase undergoes a 
phase transformation from HCP to FCC, and the FCC-Ti precipi -
tate phase was, for the first time, identified in a BCC Ti-based 
MPEA via thermally-induced.
(3) These exceptional mechanical properties are attributed to grain 
refinement, nano-precipitation, and dislocation evolution.
The outstanding mechanical performance of Ti ₄₅ V ₄₅ Cr ₅ Al ₅ MPEA 
demonstrates its great potential for high-temperature structural material 
applications in advanced nuclear reactors.
