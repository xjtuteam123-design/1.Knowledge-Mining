Effect of High-Temperature Annealing on Microstructure
and Mechanical Properties of Extremely Deformed
Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 High-Entropy Alloy Wire
Mengchuan Shi , Hengcheng Liao, Jun Zhou, Guangjing Li, Zhan Cui, Tianrui Yan, and Xuwen Liu
Submitted: 20 July 2023 / Revised: 13 November 2023 / Accepted: 25 November 2023 / Published online: 26 December 2023
Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 high-entropy alloy wire with a diameter of 0.45 mm, prepared by continuous cold-
drawing from a rod with a diameter of 6.34 mm, was subjected to high-temperature annealing at 600, 700,
800, 900, and 1000 /C176C for 1 h. The microstructure and mechanical properties in different states were
investigated by microscopy observations and tensile testing. The wire in the as-drawn state exhibited the
highest strength but the lowest elongation because of its ﬁne multilayer structure and high dislocation
density. Annealing, particularly at higher temperatures, considerably reduced the yield strength and caused
signiﬁcant recovery in both the elongation and strain-hardening exponent. Increasing the annealing tem-
perature signiﬁcantly decreased the dislocation density, notably coarsened the recrystallized grains, and
signiﬁcantly increased the fraction of twin boundaries and mean widths of annealing twins. The yield
strength was found to be positively related to the dislocation density, and the elongation exhibited a negative
relationship. In addition to the decrease in dislocation density, the formation of annealing twins played an
important role in enhancing the elongation.
Keywords cold drawing, high-entropy alloy wire, high-temperature
annealing, mechanical properties, microstructure
1. Introduction
The concept of high-entropy alloys (HEAs), or multicom-
ponent alloys, which was proposed in 2004 by Y e et al. (Ref 1)
and Cantor et al. (Ref 2), represents excellent progress in alloy
design. This differs from conventional alloy design, which is
based on the use of one or two main components. In the design
of HEAs, multiple components are chosen as the principal
elements to obtain novel alloy properties by completely
changing the crystal structure and phase constituents of the
alloy (Ref 3, 4). Because of this unique design concept, HEAs
demonstrate excellent mechanical properties including high
hardness, strength, and fracture toughness. Thus, HEAs have
broad development prospects for industrial applications (Ref 5-
7).
Metallic wire ropes play an important role in the develop-
ment of the Chinese national economy. They have been widely
used in traction and lifting operations, payload transportation,
and other applications owing to their high bearing capacity,
good bending ﬂexibility, and smooth movement without noise
(Ref 8, 9). Rotary swaging is highly efﬁcient for industrial
production of metallic wires with relatively large diameters of a
few millimeters (Ref 10, 11). However, wire rope consists of
many wires twisted together, where the diameter of individual
wires is much smaller (usually less than 1 mm). Thus, the ﬁne
wire produced requires further drawing (Ref 12). However,
further performance improvement of metallic ﬁne wires based
on conventional alloy design has become challenging. For
example, for large-strain pearlite steel wire, the work-hardening
rate decreases signiﬁcantly during the drawing process, thereby
constraining strain-strengthening (Ref 13, 14). This limits
further improvement in the strength of steel wires. Therefore,
wires made of HEAs have attracted considerable attention. Li
et al. (Ref 15) prepared an Al0.3CoCrFeNi HEA wire with a 1-
mm diameter using a hot-drawing process, achieving a tensile
strength of 1207 MPa at room temperature. The high strength
was mainly attributed to the grain reﬁnement and precipitation
of the Al-Ni body-centered cubic phase. Cho et al. (Ref 16)
studied the effect of compression ratio on the microstructure
and mechanical properties of cold-drawn Co10Cr15Fe25Mn10-
Ni30V10 HEA wire. The results showed that nanoscale
ultraﬁne grains and deformation twins were formed during
the multi-pass drawing process. Owing to the combined effect
of these two factors, a 1-mm diameter wire remained intact
even at a sectional compression ratio of 96%. The ultimate
tensile strength of this wire was 1.6 GPa. Ma et al. (Ref 17)
studied the microstructure, mechanical properties, and texture
of a CoCrFeMnNi HEA after cold-drawing deformation. Huo
et al. (Ref 18) prepared CoCrFeNi HEA wires containing
nanotwins using a high-strain drawing process; tensile tests
showed that the yield strength was 1.2 GPa, and the elongation
was 13.6% (at 223 K). Liu et al. (Ref 19) prepared a new type
of CoCrNi alloy wire. At liquid nitrogen temperature, the yield
strength, ultimate tensile strength, and elongation of this wire

3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructure
Figure 1 shows EBSD-inverse pole ﬁgure (IPF) maps of
Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wires along the drawing direction in
the as-drawn and as-annealed states. Grain boundaries with an
orientation difference of less than 2 /C176are identiﬁed as sub-grain
boundaries (SGBs) (Ref 24), which are generally neglected in
statistical analyses of EBSD. Grain boundaries with an orienta-
tion difference between 2/C176and 15/C176are deﬁned as low-angle grain
boundaries (LAGBs) and are represented by black lines in Fig.1.
Grain boundaries with an orientation difference greater than or
equal to 15/C176are deﬁned as high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs)
and are indicated with white lines in Fig. 1. Twin boundaries
(TBs) are characterized by a misorientation angle near 60 /C176(Ref
25) and are marked using red lines in Fig. 1. Grains in the as-
drawn sample underwent severe plastic deformation along the
drawing direction and exhibit a multilayer structure. Approxi-
mately 97.57% of the total grains were deformed, as shown in
Fig. 2(a). The fractions of SGBs, LAGBs, and HAGBs were
34.61, 24.44, and 40.95%, respectively, and the fraction of
deformation TBs was 2.51%. Counting the number of grains in
this sample was challenging because of the severe breakdown of
grain boundaries. In the early stage of drawing, the dislocation
slipping systems of Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA are activated,
which induces the accumulation, entanglement, and proliferation
of dislocations and results in the formation of dislocation
substructures (dislocation cells or walls). Finally, SGBs are
gradually formed. These substructures are thermodynamically
unstable owing to their high deformation energy and high density
of dislocations (Ref 26). However, later in the drawing process,
the SGBs are transformed into interfaces with a large orientation
difference by the continuous plugging and annihilation of
dislocations within the grains or at the sub-grain interfaces (Ref

This result is attributed to the increase in the fraction of
annealing twins (Ref 34, 35). Furthermore, n2 is considerably
greater than n1 for all the wires discussed here, probably due to
the formation of deformation twins during tensile deformation
(Ref 21). Typical n values of most metallic materials are in the
range of 0.1-0.2; for example, the n value of annealed mild steel
is 0.2 (Ref 36). The strain-hardening exponent reﬂects the
ability of metallic materials to be strengthened during plastic
deformation. After annealing at temperatures of 700 /C176C and
higher, the Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wires have high strain-
hardening exponents, meaning that they can harden quickly
during overload accidents. This behavior can ensure the service
safety of components made from the prepared wires.
3.3 Discussion
The mechanical behavior of Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA
wires is closely related to the evolution of the microstructure. In
the as-drawn wire, the ﬁne multilayer structure introduces a
large number of grain boundaries, which can strongly hinder
the movement of dislocations (Ref 37). In addition, abundant
deformation energy is stored in the form of a high density of
dislocations, which prevents the further motion of dislocations.
Consequently, the as-drawn wire exhibited the highest strength
but the lowest elongation. As shown in Fig. 7, annealing,
particularly at higher temperatures, considerably decreased the
strength and resulted in the signiﬁcant recovery of the
elongation. Here, the evolution of dislocations, recovery and
recrystallization of deformed grains, coarsening of crystallized
grains, and formation of twins play important roles (Ref 38).
The effect of dislocations is dominant, as described by the
Bailey-Hirsch relationship (Ref 39, 40). Figure 9 shows the

strength decreased signiﬁcantly, as shown in Fig. 7; conse-
quently, the elongation recovers remarkably.
Complete recrystallization occurred in the wires after
annealing at high temperatures (above 700 /C176C). As shown in
Fig. 2, the fraction of recrystallized grains in the completely
recrystallized samples is signiﬁcantly higher than that in the
samples in the as-recovered state (annealed at 600 /C176C). With
increasing annealing temperature, the recrystallized grains were
obviously coarsened, as shown in Fig. 3. In the sample
annealed at 700 /C176C, the recrystallized grains were ﬁne, and the
average grain size was approximately 1.2 lm (Fig. 3a). The
yield strength of this sample was high (469 MPa), and the
elongation was also good (26.1%). When the annealing
temperature was increased to 800 /C176C, the grains were obviously
coarsened, with an average size of approximately 3.8 lm
(Fig. 3b). This coarsening signiﬁcantly decreased the yield
strength (from 469 to 334 MPa, compared with that of the
sample annealed at 700 /C176C); however, the elongation increased
signiﬁcantly (from 26.1 to 39.6%). After annealing at higher
temperatures (900 and 1000 /C176C), the grains were further
coarsened (to 14.0 and 18.4 lm, respectively). This coarsening
resulted in a large decrease in strength but only a slight increase
in elongation. Grain reﬁnement generally increases both the
strength and elongation (Ref 41); thus, grain coarsening is
expected to decrease both the strength and elongation. In this
study, annealing-induced grain coarsening considerably de-
creased the yield strength but greatly increased the elongation,
as shown in Fig. 11. These changes indicate that other types of
microstructure evolution during annealing promote the plastic
deformation.

high-temperature annealing, the formation of annealing twins is
observed at high densities in the microstructure. Because the
formation of twins can release deformation energy, face-

annealing twins during annealing after severe plastic deforma-
tion (Ref 42). In this study, the fraction of TBs in the as-drawn
wire is 2.51% (Fig. 2a). These twins are deformation twins.

4. Conclusions
The effect of annealing on the microstructure and mechan-
ical properties of Fe35Ni35Cr20Mn10 HEA wires after extreme
deformation was studied. The conclusions are as follows.
(1) The as-drawn wire exhibited the highest strength but the
lowest elongation because of its ﬁne multilayer structure
and high density of dislocations.
(2) Annealing, particularly at higher temperatures, consider-
ably reduced the yield strength and caused the signiﬁ-
cant recovery of both the elongation and strain-
hardening exponents.
(3) Increasing the annealing temperature signiﬁcantly de-
creased the dislocation density, caused notable coarsening
of recrystallized grains, and signiﬁcantly increased both
the fraction of TBs and mean width of annealing twins.
(4) The yield strength is positively related to the dislocation
density, and the elongation exhibits an inverse relation-
ship. Besides the decrease in dislocation density, the for-
mation of annealing twins played an important role in
the improvement of the elongation.
(5) The wire annealed at 700 /C176C for 1 h exhibits a good
combination of strength and elongation, with a yield
strength of 469 MPa and an elongation of 26.1%.
