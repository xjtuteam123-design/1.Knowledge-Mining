abstract
Advanced Cu-Cr alloys with high strength and thermal conductivity are promising heat
dissipation materials but their applications are restricted to low and medium temperatures
(<450 /C14 C) due to the coarsening of Cr nanoparticles and grain recrystallization. In this
study, a Cu-Cr-Y alloy strengthened by nano-Y 2O3 dispersion (ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy) was
prepared by mechanical alloying using atomized Cu-Y alloy, Cr and Cu 2O as Y source,
microalloying element and oxidant material, respectively. The microstructure and high
temperature (600
/C14 C) properties of ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy were investigated. It was found Y 2O3
nanoparticles with ultraﬁne particle size (8.35 nm) and high number density (2.16 /C2 1023/
m3) were distributed homogeneously in Cu matrix and residual Cr particles (50 e100 nm)
were formed on grain boundaries. The high temperature tensile strength and softening
resistance of ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy far exceeded that of traditional Cu-Cr alloys, which can be
attributed to the thermodynamic stability of nano-Y
2O3 dispersions and the inhibition of Cr
nanoparticle growth by Y addition and residual Cr particles. The ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy pro-
vides an optimal combination of strength and thermal conductivity at high temperature,
showing great promises to be used as heat dissipation material under severe conditions
like those in fusion plants and rocket engines.

1. Introduction

materials in many ﬁelds because of their excellent electrical/
thermal conductivity. However, pure copper cannot be
directly used due to its poor mechanical properties. Solution
strengthening and precipitation strengthening are two
mature methods to improve the mechanical strength of cop-
per alloys [ 1e3]. For the typical precipitation-hardened Cu-Cr

added. The solid solubility of these elements in copper matrix
decreases with decreasing temperature, resulting in the pre-
cipitation of ultra-ﬁne coherent second phases such as Cr and
Cr
2Nb to strengthen the matrix [ 4]. Therefore, Cu-Cr alloys
exhibit a good combination of high strength and high con-
ductivity at room temperature. However, application of Cu-Cr
alloys under high temperature conditions is restricted by their
poor softening resistance due to the rapid coarsening of pre-
cipitates and recrystallization of deformed grains. To improve
the mechanical strength of Cu-Cr alloys at elevated temper-
atures, several efforts have been taken. For example, Shan
et al. [5] improved the high temperature microhardness of Cu-
5%Cr alloy by forming residual nano-sized Cr precipitates at
grain boundaries (GBs). W. Wang [ 6] showed the addition of Y
effectively improved the high temperature properties of Cu-
Cr-Zr-Y alloy at 450

conducted on improving the tensile strength of Cu-Cr alloys at
temperatures higher than 500
/C14 C and the reported value of Cu-
Cr-Zr alloy was only 65 MPa at 600 /C14 C[ 7]. To meet the re-
quirements for service under some extreme conditions like
those in fusion plants, rocket engines, etc., it is necessary to
develop copper alloys with satisfying high temperature
properties.
Among recent progresses, the development of oxide-
dispersion-strengthened copper (ODS-Cu) alloys stands out
as the most promising approach to improve the high tem-
perature properties such as mechanical strength and soft-
ening resistance due to the dispersion of insoluble oxide
particles in Cu matrix. As an example, Glidcop ® ODS-Cu alloy
contains a small volume fraction of g-Al
2O3 nano-dispersoids
with an incoherent interface with the Cu matrix, which has a
softening temperature higher than 900
/C14 C[ 8]. Recently, Y 2O3
particles are found to be more promising than Al 2O3 particles.
The limited solubility and diffusivity of rare earth elements in
Cu matrix are appropriate for Y
2O3-containing ODS-Cu alloys
to maintain a high thermal conductivity and microstructural
stability against coarsening at high temperature. In addition,
Y
2O3 particles may form a desirable orientation relationship
with Cu matrix, which would enhance the high temperature
mechanical properties of ODS-Cu alloy [ 9,10]. Mechanical
alloying (MA) is the primary fabrication method for ODS-Cu
alloys due to the low solubility of Y in Cu matrix. In the
traditional MA process, Y
2O3 particles are directly milled with
Cu particles and consolidated by hot extrusion or hot
isostatic pressing. However, due to the ductile nature of
copper, the MA process is ineffective, thus leading to an un-
even distribution of Y
2O3 particles in Cu matrix and inap-
parent improvement in mechanical property of ODS-Cu alloy
[11]. In our previous work, gas atomized Cu(Y) pre-alloyed
powder was used instead of Y
2O3 and it was shown that a
high number density of Y 4Al2O9 nanoparticles ( <5 nm) were
formed in copper matrix after ageing treatment [ 12]. How-
ever, the thermal conductivity of Y 4Al2O9 DS-Cu alloy
was relatively low since a high content of Al (9 wt.%)
was added to minimize the lattice mismatch between
Y
4Al2O9 nanoparticles and the Cu(Al) solid solution matrix,
which increased the lattice distortion and electron/phonon
scattering.
In this work, Cu(Y) pre-alloyed powder, Cr powder and
Cu
2O powder were used to produce ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy by
mechanical alloying and spark plasma sintering. Cu-Cr alloy
with 1.1 wt.% Cr addition was chosen as the matrix in this
work to reduce the content of solute element in copper alloy.
Non ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy was also prepared for comparison. The
microstructures of Cu-Cr-Y and ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloys including
the grain size and secondary phases were studied systemati-
cally. Then the high temperature tensile property, thermal
conductivity and softening resistance of Cu-Cr-Y and ODS Cu-
Cr-Y alloys were determined to reveal the improved high
temperature properties of our ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy. It is hoped
that the current work can provide guidance for the design and
manufacture of novel ODS-Cu alloys suitable for applications
under extreme conditions.
2. Materials and experimental details
2.1. Preparation of ball milled powders and consolidated
samples
Cu-1Y (wt.%) pre-alloyed powder was prepared by gas atomi-
zation method in Central South University (99.9% purity,
mean particle size<74 mm). Cuprous oxide (Cu
2O, 99.9% purity)
and chromium (Cr, 99.9% purity, mean particle size <48 mm)
were purchased from Alfa-Aldrich and used as raw materials.
The designed nominal compositions for two samples were
listed in Table 1 . The detailed ball milling process conditions
can be found in our previous work [ 12].
The consolidation of the milled powders was conducted in
a spark plasma sintering equipment (SPS, SinterLand, LABOX-
1575, Japan). Ball-milled powder was sintered at 500
/C14 C and
900 /C14 C stepwise. In the ﬁrst step, the powders were heated to
500 /C14 C with a heating rate of 100 /C14 C/min and kept at 500 /C14 C
under 40 MPa for 5 min for the escape of gaseous impurities. In
the second step, the temperature was increased to 900
/C14 C with
a heating rate of 100 /C14 C/min and kept at 900 /C14 C for another
10 min, during which the pressure was simultaneously
increased from 40 MPa to 60 MPa to provide enough driving
force to the rearrangement of particles.

3.1.3. Precipitates analyses
The microstructure and precipitates of Cu-Cr-Y and ODS Cu-
Cr-Y alloys were studied by TEM. Fig. 4 shows the TEM image
of Cu-Cr-Y alloy and corresponding elemental mapping. The
grain diameter of Cu matrix ranged from 100 to 500 nm,
which was consistent with EBSD. Dislocation walls and twin
boundaries were found in the microstructure of Cu-Cr-Y
alloy, which was consistent with UFGs Cu alloy produced by
MA [ 18]. In addition, many secondary phase particles can be
observed in the matrix. As can be seen from HAADF image
and elemental mapping results, a high density of Cr particles
was formed in Cu-Cr-Y alloy with larger ones located on GBs
while ﬁner ones located in the grain. The formation of larger
Cr particles on GBs can be expectable for the following
reason. During MA, part of Cr could dissolve into Cu lattice to
form supersaturated Cu-Cr particles while the undissolved Cr
particles, or residual Cr particles, were dispersed on
the surface of Cu-Cr particles, as concluded from XRD
and SEM results. Therefore, the residual Cr particles were
retained on the grain boundaries in the consolidated alloy.
The diameter of residual Cr particle was 30 e100 nm. In
contrast, the diameter of intragranular nanoparticles was
around 5 e10 nm.

3.2. Properties
3.2.1. Tensile properties
Fig. 9 lists the tensile properties of Cu-Cr-Y and ODS-Cu-Cr-Y
alloys. The ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy exhibited an UTS value of
675 MPa and a YS value of 600 MPa at room temperature
(Fig. 9a), which was 10% and 22% higher than that for Cu-Cr-Y

dislocation density was assumed to be much lower than that
of ECAPed Cu-Cr-Zr, so the high strength was mainly attrib-

2O3 nanoparticles
with a high number density. Tested at temperatures above

3.2.2. Softening resistance
The hardness values of Cu-Cr-Y and ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloys were
measured and listed in Fig. 11 to evaluate their softening
resistance after annealing at high temperatures. The hardness
value of ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy increased after annealing at
400
/C14 C/500 /C14 C for 1 h and air quenched. This can be expectable
for the following reason. The alloy sample was sintered by SPS
and used for different tests. Since SPS has a short sintering
period and the diffusion of Y in Cu lattice might be slow, as-
sintered alloy could be under-aged. When annealed at
appropriate temperatures (400 e500
/C14 C), Y 2O3 nanoparticles

3.2.3. Thermal conductivity of ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy
Thermal conductivity is a signiﬁcant parameter to evaluate
the performance of heat dissipation materials. The thermal
conductivities of ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy were measured at
different temperatures and shown in Fig. 13 . The thermal
conductivity of ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy was 221 W m
/C0 1 K/C0 1 at room
temperature. The relatively low thermal conductivity was
mainly attributed to the serious lattice distortion induced by
solute atoms and coherent interface between Y
2O3 and ma-
trix, which impedes the movement of free electrons and
phonons [34]. When tested at 600 /C14 C, the thermal conductivity
of ODS Cu-Cr-Y further increased to 243 W/(m .K), which could
be expectable as Y and Cr precipitated from Cu lattice to
release the lattice distortion. To further improve the thermal
conductivity of ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy, it is necessary to optimize
the size, number density and interfacial property of secondary
particles to achieve a balance between high temperature
properties and thermal conductivity.
Finally, the UTS and thermal conductivity of ODS Cu-Cr-Y
alloy were compared with those of other Cu-Cr alloys and
ODS-Cu alloys as shown in Fig. 14 . The UTS of ODS Cu-Cr-Y
alloy (675 MPa) was among the highest values. In addition,
ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy exhibited an ultrahigh softening tempera-
ture of more than 1000
/C14 C and a medium thermal conductivity
of 243 W/(m .K). Compared with other typical high-
performance Cu alloys, ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy exhibited a good
overall performance and potential to be applied to high-
temperature environment.
4. Conclusion
ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy containing nano-Y 2O3 dispersions and re-
sidual Cr particles was produced by MA using Cu-Y pre-alloy
as the Y source, Cr as the microalloying element and Cu
2Oa s
the oxidation material. The microstructures of ODS Cu-Cr-Y
alloy and its non-ODS counterpart were thoroughly investi-
gated using EBSD, TEM, HRTEM and the corresponding high
temperature properties were tested. Our results suggest that
Y
2O3 nanoparticles with an ultraﬁne particle size (8.35 nm)
and a high number density (2.16 /C2 1023/m3) were homoge-
nously distributed in Cu matrix while residual Cr particles
(50e100 nm) were located mainly on grain boundaries. The
unique microstructure of ODS Cu-Cr-Y alloy provides mani-
fold beneﬁts such as pinning grain boundary and dislocation
movement by nano-Y
2O3 and inhibiting coarsening of Cr
nanoparticle by residual Cr particles and Y solute atoms,
leading to improved high temperature (600
/C14 C) properties. The
tensile strength and thermal conductivity of ODS Cu-Cr-Y
alloy at 600
/C14 C achieved 145 MPa and 243 W/(m .K), respec-
tively, while its softening resistance temperature was higher
than 1000
/C14 C, showing great promises to be used as heat
dissipation materials under severe service conditions like
those in fusion plants and rocket engines.
