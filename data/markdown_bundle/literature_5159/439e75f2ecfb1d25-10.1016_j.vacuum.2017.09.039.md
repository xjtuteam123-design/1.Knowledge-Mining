abstract
In the present study, vacuum brazing was adopted to join carbon ﬁber reinforced silicon carbide (C/SiC)
composite and Nb using Ti-Ni composite foils. The interfacial microstructure as well as mechanical
properties was investigated, and the contact-reaction mechanism was analyzed. The results show that,
(Ti, Nb)C reaction layer, (Ti, Nb)
2Ni and Ti-Ni-Nb-Si compounds, and TiNi-(Nb, Ti) eutectic form in braze
seam. Ti-Ni composite foils turn into liquid eutectic alloy by two stages. Firstly, Ti and Ni foils react to
fabricate TiNi alloy. Secondly, the pseudo-binary eutectic reaction of TiNi and Nb substrate begins to form
the liquid eutectic alloy. The maximum shear strengths of the brazed joints reach 192 MPa, 155 MPa,
1 15 MPa and 82 MPa at room temperature, 600
/C14 C, 800 MPa and 1000 /C14 C, respectively. To enhance the

Because the in ﬁltration of ﬁller alloy into holes can increase the load bearing capacity and decrease the
residual stress of the joint, the shear strengths of laser-drilled joints are signi ﬁcantly improved to
259 MPa, 186 MPa, 161 MPa and 122 MPa at room temperature, 600 /C14 C, 800 /C14 C and 1000 /C14 C, respectively.

1. Introduction
Carbon ﬁber reinforced silicon carbide (C/SiC) composite pos-
sesses many attractive properties, such as low density, high tensile
and ﬂexural strength, enhanced fracture toughness, excellent
impact and thermal shock resistance. In particular, it maintains
superior thermal and mechanical properties under elevated tem-
perature [1e4]. Thus, C/SiC has been extensively used in aerospace
applications [5e7]. However, the joining of geometrically simple C/
SiC parts and metals is involved in the practical applications to
fabricate complex large-scale components [7]. Niobium is an ideal
refractory metal material because of its high melting point
(2477 /C14 C) [8], so the reliable joining of C/SiC and Nb has great po-
tential in aerospace industry.
However, C/SiC composite has a large amount of micro pores
and cracks due to its speci ﬁc structure and manufacturing tech-
nology. Hence, C/SiC tends to oxide at high temperature. Oxidation
can lead holes and cracks form on C ﬁbers, which signi ﬁcantly
decrease the strength of the ﬁbers. Meanwhile, SiC matrix reacts
with oxygen to form SiO 2 during oxidation process [9]. Moreover,
severe oxidation also occurs to Nb at high temperature. Nb can
react with oxygen to form Nb 2O5 powder oxide ﬁlms forms on its
surface. With the increasing of the temperature, the oxide ﬁlms can
crack and peel from Nb surface. As a result, the properties of C/SiC
and Nb can be severely deteriorated due to their oxidation [10e12].
At present, the joining of C/SiC as well as Nb at high temperature
are mainly conducted in vacuum. No joining technology in atmo-
spheric environment is reported. Therefore, the joining of C/SiC and
Nb must be carried out in vacuum condition.
In addition, it has been demonstrated the superiority of vacuum
brazing in fabricating dissimilar materials joints such as ceramic/
composite-metal/alloy joint [13e15]. As a result, vacuum brazing
can be the optimized method to realize the joining of C/SiC com-
posite and Nb. Referring to the literatures, the vacuum brazing of C/
SiC to Ti [7], Ti alloy [16] including TC4 [17e23] and TiAl [24e26],
Ni-base superalloy [7,27],N b [28], and Invar alloy [29] have been
studied. And the Ag-Cu base [16e22], Ti base [23], Ni base and Ti-Ni
base ﬁller alloy are mainly adopted. Nevertheless, service envi-
ronment challenges the application of C/SiC-metal joints. Conse-
quently, C/SiC-metal brazed joints need to guarantee reliable
performance under high-temperature, and maintain good ductility
to avoid the failure. Unfortunately, the reported ﬁller alloys still
have problems to meet the requirements mentioned above. For
example, Ag-Cu base ﬁller alloy can not serve at high temperature.

Ti-base and Ti-Ni-B ﬁller alloy joints possess ideal high-
temperature performance, but the addition of powders like C, SiC
or B in ﬁller alloy may cause the powder aggregations and thus lead
to the formation of large scale debonding voids and compounds in
braze seam. Nevertheless, the joining properties of Ni base joints
were not reported.
In the previous study, we conducted the brazing of C/SiC com-
posite and Nb using arc melting TiNiNb ﬁller [28]. It can be revealed
that the key factor to realize the sound brazing is to form TiNi-(Nb,
Ti) pseudo-binary eutectic structure with good ductility and high
temperature performance in braze seam [30,31]. However, it is hard
for Nb to melt completely in the repeated arc melting process due
to the high melting temperature of Nb. Thus, the uniformity of
TiNiNb ﬁller alloy can not be guaranteed, which could inhibit the
complete melting of the ﬁller under brazing temperature. Addi-
tionally, the graphite crucibles are utilized during arc melting to
fabricate TiNiNb ﬁller. C from the crucibles trends to dissolve into
liquid ﬁller and further deteriorates the composition uniformity of
the ﬁller. In contrast, the contact-reaction has been considered as a
promising method to form liquid during brazing. The contact-
reaction of Ti and Ni foils with Nb substrate can be adopted to
form TiNi-(Nb, Ti) structure and realize the brazing of C/SiC and Nb
[32,33].
At present, several mechanical strengthening methods have
been attempted to improve the reliability of the brazed joints
[34,35]. However, the ef ﬁcient processing method for strength-
ening C/SiC brazed joints is scarcely reported.
In this study, Ti-Ni composite foils were utilized to conduct the
vacuum brazing of C/SiC composite and Nb. The microstructure
evolution as well as brazing mechanism was analyzed. The me-
chanical properties especially high-temperature properties of the
brazed joints were assessed. In addition, an approach to enhance
brazing properties by processing holes on C/SiC surface was carried
out. The effects of holes on brazing properties were investigated.
2. Experimental procedures
Three-dimensional four-directional braided C/SiC fabricating via
precursor in ﬁltration pyrolysis was utilized in this study. The
density and theoretical porosity of the composite is 1.86g/cm
3 and
16 vol%, respectively. The volume fraction of carbonﬁbers in C/SiC is
45 vol%. Fig. 1 shows the microstructure of C/SiC substrate, in which
large amount of pores can be observed. The Nb plate used in this
study is fabricated to 3 mm thick by rolling and its purity is 99.96%.
80 mm Ti foil with purity of 99.6% and 50 mm Ni foil with purity of
99.6% were used as composite ﬁller foils.
C/SiC was sliced into 6 mm /C2 4m m /C2 4 mm. Nb was sliced into
10 mm /C2 10 mm /C2 3 mm for microstructure observation and
15 mm /C2 10 mm /C2 3 mm for shear tests, respectively. Both Ti and Ni
foils were cut into 7 mm /C2 5 mm. Laser drilling method was carried
out to process frustum holes on the surface of C/SiC composite. The
holes are in diameter of 1 mm and depth of 1 mm. The center
distance of the holes is 12 mm. The diameter and depth of the laser
drilling holes were determined by the vemier caliper, which has the
measuring range of 200 mm and the precision of 0.02 mm. Prior to
brazing, the joined surfaces of Nb were polished by SiC paper up to
grit 1000, and all joined materials were ultrasonically cleaned in
acetone for 15 min. The substrates and composite foils were
assembled in order of C/SiC-Ni/Ti/Ni/Ti-Nb, as shown in Fig. 2 (a).
The vacuum brazing was carried out in vacuum furnace (Centorr-
3520) at 1 160e1240 /C14 C for 20min with the vacuum higher than
5 /C2 10/C0 3Pa.
The microstructures of the brazed joints were analyzed by S-
4700 scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped with TN-
4700X energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS), and D/max-rb X-ray
diffractometer (XRD). Shear tests were carried out on Instron-5569
mechanical testing machine to evaluate the shear strength of the
brazed joints, and the samples were pressed with a constant speed
of 0.5 mm/min. The schematic diagram of shear test is shown in
Fig. 2 (b). The fracture surfaces of the brazed joints were observed
by OLYMPUS SZX12 optical microscope.

form liquid phase. The two stages will be analyzed respectively in

3.3. Mechanical evaluation of C/SiC-Nb joint
Shear tests were utilized to evaluate the mechanical properties
of the C/SiC-Nb brazed joints. Fig. 8 (a) shows the room-
temperature (RT) shear strength of the joints brazed at
116 0/C14 Ce1240 /C14 C for 20min. As revealed, the joints strengths in-
crease at ﬁrst and then decrease with the heightening of the
brazing temperature. And the maximum shear strength of 192 MPa
can be obtained when brazing at 1 180 /C14 C for 20min. To meet the
demand of elevated-temperature application, high-temperature
(HT) shear tests of the C/SiC-Nb joints brazed at 1 180 /C14 C for
20min were also carried out. As shown in Fig. 8 (b), 155 MPa at
600 /C14 C, 1 15 MPa at 800/C14 C and 82 MPa at 1000 /C14 C 80%, 60% and 42%
of RT strength) can be obtained, respectively. As a result, the brazed
joints can maintain good performance at elevated temperature.
In order to study the fracture behavior of the brazed joints,
fracture surfaces of the shear tests samples were studied. Fig. 9
shows the typical fracture surface of RT test joint (1 180 /C14 C, 20min).
It can be observed from Fig. 9 (a) that a part of C/SiC substrate is
peeled. And the peeled composite remains on the fracture surface.
Since the maximum residual stress concentrates in ceramic for
ceramic/metal joints due to the coef ﬁcient of thermal expansion
(CTE) mismatch of heterogeneous substrates [36e38], cracks tend
to initiate and propagate inside C/SiC substrate. Moreover, a large
amount of metallic blocks distributes on the surface too. Fig. 9 (b)
shows the SEM image of fracture. The metallic blocks can be
detected as the ﬁller alloy nails in ﬁltrated in the pores of C/SiC.
During the fracture propagating, the breaking and pulling out of the
ﬁller nails occur, leading the de ﬂection of the cracks. Such behavior
can consume more fracture energy and are favorable for the joining
properties.

3.4. Laser drilling strengthening of C/SiC-Nb joint
It can be revealed from the previous discussion that, the in ﬁl-
trating of ﬁller alloy in pores of C/SiC bene ﬁts the brazing proper-
ties. However, the pores obtained during fabricating C/SiC are not
uniform in size and distributing. Consequently, the enhancing ef-
fects of the inﬁltrated ﬁller are not stable and reliable. As a result, in
order to improve the reliability of C/SiC-Nb brazed joints, holes
were processed on the surface of C/SiC composite by laser drilling
method. Fig. 1 1shows the morphology of laser-drilled C/SiC sample.
Four holes were drilled on C/SiC, and the diameter and depth of the
holes are both 1 mm.
The brazing of laser-drilled C/SiC and Nb were carried out at
118 0/C14 C for 20min, and the typical microstructure of the joint are
presented in Fig. 12. Seen from Fig. 12 (a), the laser-drilled hole is
well ﬁlled by the ﬁller alloy, and no defects such as crack or
debonding can be observed. In addition, micro hole can be found on
the bottom of the laser-drilled hole. It is deduced that the micro
hole is fabricated during laser drilling process and in ﬁltrated by
ﬁller alloy. The micro hole can release the residual stress inside C/
SiC and is favorable for the strength of the joints [34].
The microstructures at the bottom (labeled as region I) and on
the wall of the hole (labeled as region II) are magni ﬁed in Fig. 12 (b)
and (c), respectively. The in ﬁltrated ﬁller alloy bonds well with the
C/SiC substrate. The (Ti, Nb)C layer forms on C/SiC surface. The
middle of the hole (labeled as region III) is magni ﬁed in Fig. 12 (d).
Comparing with Fig. 3, the morphology in region III is similar with
that of the braze seam. Some (Ti, Nb) 2Ni and Ti-Ni-Nb-Si
compounds dispersing in TiNi-(Nb, Ti) eutectic structure can be
observed too.
To assess the mechanical properties of laser-drilled joints brazed
at 1 180/C14 C for 20min, RT and HT shear tests were carried out. Fig. 13
compares the strength of laser-drilled joints with direct brazing
joints. The results show that, the shear strengths of laser-drilled
joints reach 259 MPa, 186 MPa, 161 MPa and 122 MPa at room
temperature, 600 /C14 C, 800 /C14 C and 1000 /C14 C, respectively. It is obvious
that the shear strengths of laser-drilled joints are enhanced
signiﬁcantly.
After the shear tests, the fracture surfaces of the joints were
observed. Fig. 14exhibits the fracture of RT test sample. As shown in
Fig. 14 (a), C/SiC layer can be observed remaining on the fracture
surface, demonstrating the fracture occurs inside the composite
substrate. Moreover, the broken sites of the ﬁller alloy nails can be
found on the fracture surface. Fig. 14 (b) shows the three-
dimensional (3D) morphology of the fracture surface. It's
observed that two fracture surfaces of the ﬁller nails are higher
than that of C/SiC, while other two are lower than C/SiC fracture
surface. As a result, the fracture behavior of the laser-drilled joint
can be deduced, as described in Fig. 14 (c). When the fracture oc-
curs, it initiates inside C/SiC, with the propagating of the crack, the
fracture reaches the ﬁller nails inside the laser-drilled holes. Due to
their relatively high strength, crack deﬂects in ﬁller nails, which can
consume fracture energy and prevent the crack propagating, and is
beneﬁcial to the joining properties. After the breaking of the ﬁller
nails under the shear load, crack continues to propagate inside C/
SiC and ﬁnally leads to the failure of the joint.
The strengthening of the laser-drilled joints can be ascribed to
the “nail effect”. The inﬁltrated ﬁller alloy can be considered as nails
inside the holes of C/SiC. The nails have larger strength than C/SiC
substrate, and in which cracks are much harder to propagate. It's
known from Fig. 14 that the fracture of the laser-drilled joints

4. Conclusion
In this study, vacuum brazing of C/SiC composite and Nb was
successfully performed with Ti-Ni composite foils. The interfacial
microstructure as well as mechanical properties of the brazed
joints was investigated. Meanwhile, a method of enhancing joining
strength by laser drilling holes was used. The main results of this
study can be concluded as follows:
(1) The typical interfacial structure of C/SiC-Nb joints is (Ti, Nb)C
