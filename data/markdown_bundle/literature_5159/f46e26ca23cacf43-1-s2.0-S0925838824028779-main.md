1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs), a new class of metallic materials, are 
composed of four or more principal elements [1 – 3] . The multi-principal 
element composition of these alloys significantly expands the design 
space, offering possibilities for creating alloys with varied combinations 
of properties, including excellent strength-ductility synergy [1,4] . 
Among various HEAs, refractory HEAs (RHEAs) are a category consist -
ing of high-melting-point refractory elements such as Ti, V, Cr, Zr, Nb, 
Mo, Hf, Ta, and W [5,6] . Owing to their excellent mechanical properties 
and structural stability at high temperatures over 800 
◦
C and even up to 
1500 
◦
C, RHEAs are promising candidates for high-temperature struc -
tural materials that can rival traditional nickel-based superalloys. 
However, most RHEAs exhibit extremely limited ductility at room 
temperature, for instance, the typical NbMoTaW alloy exhibits only 
1.5 % compression deformation at room temperature [7] , indicative of 
poor cold-workability and severely limiting their commercial applica -
tions. Therefore, developing new RHEAs with a good balance of tensile 
strength and ductility is a paramount challenge [4,8] .
Currently, extensive research efforts are underway to develop RHEAs 
with high strength and excellent ductility. The design of ductile BCC 
HEAs is primarily achieved through the modulation of alloy composi -
tion. One approach is to improve the ductility of the alloy by doping with 
interstitial atoms. Wang et.al [9] successfully increased the ultimate 
compressive strain of NbMoTaW alloy from 1.5 % to 10 % by adding 
0.5 at% boron. Lei et.al [10] improved both the yield strength and ul -
timate elongation of TiZrHfNb by adding 2 at. percent of oxygen, 
achieving a yield strength of 1110 MPa and an ultimate elongation of 
27.66 %. Another approach is to adjust the content and types of the 
principal elements in the alloy. Some ductile RHEAs have been suc -
cessfully developed based on IVB and VB group transition metal ele -
ments (such as Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, etc.). For example, the TaNbHfZrTi 
alloy exhibits a tensile strength of 875 MPa, an ultimate tensile elon -
gation of 16.5 %, and excellent cold workability at room temperature, 
with a cold rolling reduction rate reaching 80 % [11,12] . The tensile 
yield strength and fracture elongation of TiVNbHf at room temperature

However, these alloys mostly contain Ta and Hf elements, resulting in

The concentration of constituent elements significantly affects the 
phase composition and mechanical properties in RHEAs [8] . In the 
Nb-Ti-V-Zr high entropy alloy system, a high V/Zr ratio leads to the 
precipitation of the brittle V
2
Zr Laves phase, which will reduce the 
ductility of the alloy [15] . Therefore, it is necessary to avoid the pre -
cipitation of this phase when adjusting the alloy composition. Further -
more, adjusting the composition of the alloy also affects its intrinsic 
characteristics, which in turn alters its mechanical properties. The 
valence electron concentration (VEC), as an intrinsic charge character -
istic, was found to be closely related to the ductility of RHEAs, as re -
ported in the literature [16] . Chen et.al suggested that the ductility of 
single-phase RHEAs is negatively correlated with the VEC [17] . From 
the perspective of electronic structure, BCC RHEAs will exhibit inherent 
ductility when shear instability occurs before reaching the ideal tensile 
stress. Reducing the number of valence electrons in the alloy lowers the 
Fermi level relative to the band structure, thereby decreasing the critical 
strain for shear instability and causing it to occur earlier [18] . Therefore, 
lowering the VEC is an effective strategy for designing the ductility 
RHEAs. In Nb-Ti-V-Zr alloys, the VEC value of Nb and V is lower than 
that of Ti and Zr. By appropriately adjusting the alloy composition to 
change its VEC, alloys with high ductility can ultimately be designed.

2.3. Molecular statics simulation details
All molecular statics simulations were performed using the Large- 
scale Atomic/Molecular Massively Parallel Simulator (LAMMPS) pack -
age [27] in this work. The structural visualization was performed using 
OVITO [28] . The Embedded Atom Method (EAM) potential, which has 
been widely used in the investigation of the dislocation movement of 
HEAs [29 – 31] , was employed to describe the interatomic interactions of 
the alloy. The four potentials are Nb [32] , Ti [33] , V [34] , Zr [33] . The 
interactions between different elements were described based on the 
formulas by Johnson [35] and Zhou et al. [36] . The reliability of the 
potentials was verified by comparing the lattice parameters of the alloy 
obtained from experiments and simulations. To calculate the LSR of 
dislocations, periodic dislocation array (PAD) model was established by 
inserting single edge/screw dislocations into a random atomic structure 
with appropriate boundary conditions [29,30] . The shear strain was 
applied to the model until the dislocations exhibited noticeable move -
ment, and the corresponding stress was recorded as the LSR. For each 
dislocation on each plane, a set of five LSR values was obtained.

that the formation of kinks can hinder the movement of dislocations and 
effectively accommodate stress concentrations during the plastic 
deformation process. It also indicates that dislocation slip plays a 
dominant role in plastic deformation.
