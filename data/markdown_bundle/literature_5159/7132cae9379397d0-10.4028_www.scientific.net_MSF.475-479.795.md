Recent Work on Alloy and Process Development of Ti 2AlNb based 
Alloys 
Shiqiong Li 1,a , Yunjun Cheng 1,a , Xiaobo Liang 1,a and Jianwei Zhang 1,a 
1Central Iron & Steel Research Institute (CISRI), Beijing P . R. China 100081 
aTiAl@superalloy.cn 
Keywords: Ti 2AlNb based alloy, TMP , microstructure, property 
Abstract . Ti 2AlNb orthorhombic phase based alloys are the promising high tempera ture structural 
materials for aeronautical and aerospace industry because of their low density, high yield strength 
and excellent high temperature performance. In this paper, the r ecent work which has been carried 
out in CISRI on alloy and process of Ti 2AlNb based alloys is presented. The work covers the 
development of the alloy design and microstructure control as well as the processing technology 
applied for producing good quality alloy ingots and fabricating vari ous components. The progress 
obtained in application research of the alloys is addressed. The impor tant role that the combination 
of TMP and the post-TMP treatment affects on the microstructure and thus on the properties of the 
alloys is emphasized. 
Introduction 
The ductility and toughness of Ti 3Al based alloys can be improved by the addition of Nb [1]. In 
exploring the effect of niobium on phase transformations and mechanical properties in Ti 3Al based 
alloys, Banejee [2] identified a ternary intermetallic compound ba sed on the stoichiometry Ti 2AlNb 
which was designated as the O phase on the basis of its orthorhombic structure with Cmcm 
symmetry. Since then the new alloy family, ordered orthorhombic Ti 2AlNb based alloys, has 
received considerable attention [2, 3]. The alloys of this family generally exhibit higher yield 
strength, fracture toughness and creep resistance than other allo ys based on Ti 3Al [4], and thus have 
a great potential for becoming some of the most important aerospace and aeronautical structure 
materials. Two compositions of Ti 2AlNb based alloys, Ti-22Al-24Nb-3Ta (at%) and 
Ti-22Al-20Nb-7Ta (at%) were developed in CISRI in the end of the nineties of the last century in 
order to further improve oxidation resistance and elevated temperat ure performance of this family 
alloys [5,6]. The effects of Ta content and the different paramete rs of thermal mechanical 
processing (TMP) on microstructure and tensile properties were e xtensively analyzed [5, 6]. 
Recently, the composition of Ti-22Al-25Nb (at%) alloy has been developed to satisfy the 
requirement of the relatively low density materials for aerospace and aeronautical engine systems in 
China. Mechanical properties of Ti 2AlNb based alloys are generally very sensitive to the trace 
elements and the microstructure, which are controlled most effecti vely by melting processing and 
by TMP. Therefore, optimization of the melting technology and TMP a re the key issues to make 
optimal use of these alloys. This paper reviews some experimental results achieved and our current 
understanding of Ti 2AlNb based alloys developed in CISRI in such areas as alloy melting 
processing/microstructure relationships, effect of microstructure on properties, and oxidation 
resistance evaluation.

Optimizing Alloy Melting 
Melting of the Ti 2AlNb compositions, while not easy, is an extension of the melting method and 
parameters used for conventional terminal titanium alloys. However , because of the presence of a 
relatively larger difference in density and in melting point of compositions of Ti 2AlNb based alloys, 
as well as the ductility of the alloys being very sensiti ve to the trace elements, therefore the melting 
practices must be carried out with a great deal of caution: control of the purity of the raw and 
processed materials, control of small contents of the trace ele ments, such as O and N, use of the 
master alloys for the higher melting point metals, as well as the use of the combination of V AR and 
ASM (arc skull melting) technologies. All of which are benefi t to improve the homogenization of 
compositions and the purity of ingot. Table 1 shows the chemical analysis of ingots of compositions 
of Ti 2AlNb based alloys studied in CISRI. The data in Table 1 indicate that the compositions of the 
cast ingots are in agreement with the nominal compositions, and the c ontents of the trace elements 
are quite lower. For a comparison, the composition of Ti-22Al-27Nb alloy was also studied. An 
ingot of 300mm in diameter, 300kg in weight of Ti-22Al-25Nb (at%) alloy with good quality was 
successfully prepared as shown in Fig. 1(a). The bars and rings fa bricated well in different sizes 
with Ti-22Al-25Nb alloys are also shown in Fig. 1(b) and (c), some of which have been further 
manufactured to produce engine components for testing.

Processing and Microstructure 
The microstructures of Ti 2AlNb alloys are strongly sensitive to thermomechanical processing (TMP) 
which involves hot working operations such as forging or extrusion follow ed by heat treatments. In 
the present study, the hot working operations were divided into the two s tages. Firstly, canned ingot 
was forged to break down in 
βsingle phase field after a homogenizing treatment. The forged blank 
then was further operated with the second stage of TMP sequences to control the microstructure.

secondary β grains. Table 2 lists three types of TMP sequences applied for the second stage

Oxidation Resistance 
Oxygen in general exhibits two effects in α2 Ti 3Al and orthorhombic Ti 2AlNb based alloys: 
solid solution hardening and oxide formation. When oxygen is in solution, it ra ises the beta 
transformation temperature and the low to intermediate tempera ture strength at the expense of 
ductility. The α2 or O phase based alloys show strong TiO 2 forming kinetics, resulting in the formation of 
surface layer with the mixture of Al 2O3 and Ti 2O [9]. Fig. 6 gives the isothermal oxidation kinetics curves 
of Ti-22Al-25Nb alloy. The mass gains are less than 1mg/cm 2 both at 650 ℃ and 750 ℃ for 100h 
exposure. This result is consistent with that the addition of appropria te amount of Nb can further 
improve oxidation resistance for α 2 and orthorhombic alloys[9]. Fig.7(a) and (b) show the 
micrographs of cross-section of the oxidation layer formed on the surfac es of the specimens of 
Ti-22Al-25Nb alloy after isothermal oxidation at 750 ℃ and 850 ℃ respectively. A very thin (less 
than 5 μm) oxidation layer formed on the surface of the specimen teste d at 750 ℃ for 216h 
exposure (Fig. 7a). However, an about 10 μm of thickness of oxidation layer was obtained for the 
oxidation sample tested at 850 ℃ for 100h and two regions were found on the cross-section in such 
layer. XRD analysis indicated the outer zone is Al-riched, ma inly composed of Al 2O3 oxides and 
inner zone is Al depleted, consisting a mixture of TiO 2, NbO 2, Nb 2O5 and AlNbO 4 oxides. These 
observations are also consistent with the other experimental result s and analysis on Ti 3Al, Ti 2AlNb 
based alloys[9].

Summary 
Extensive progress in Ti 2AlNb based alloy development has been made in CISRI in the recent 
years. By the modification of alloying and TMP sequences to eff ectively control microstructure, the 
alloys not only exhibit excellent mechanical properties at room and elevated temperatures, but also 
show good formability. This progress enhances considerable potential ap plications of this class in 
aeronautic and aerospace industrial fields.
