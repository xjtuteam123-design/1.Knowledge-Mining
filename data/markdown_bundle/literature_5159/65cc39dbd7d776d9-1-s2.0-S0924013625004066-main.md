ABSTRACT
The AlNbTiVZr high-entropy alloy exhibits exceptionally high-temperature specific strength, positioning it as a 
highly promising candidate for hot-section components exposed to intense heat flux and mechanical loading. The 
integrated design requirements for thermal protection and load bearing necessitate dependable bonding between 
the high-entropy alloy and the matrix. However, discrepancies in composition and thermo-physical properties 
between these materials often result in defects and strength degradation. In this investigation, a reliable 
metallurgical bonding was established between the TA15 titanium alloy and the AlNbTiVZr high-entropy alloy 
through strategic composition design of the transition region, followed by successful laser direct energy depo -
sition with power regulation and subsequent post-treatment. By examining the microstructural evolution of the 
TA15/AlNbTiVZr gradient materials and integrating the results with thermal-fluid coupling simulations, the 
impact of temperature gradient and growth rate on microstructural evolution during the laser direct energy 
deposition process was systematically explored. The complex changes in grain morphology were attributed to the 
dynamic variations in the temperature gradient of the melting pool, differences in thermal conductivity, and the 
effects of diffusion coefficient disparities between the materials. The optimized transition region exhibited an 
ultimate tensile strength of 1038 MPa with an elongation of 8.1 %. This study not only enhances the compre -
hension of microstructural evolution in gradient materials but also presents a promising approach for developing 
high-performance materials with tailored properties.

gradient multi-principal element alloy components via the LDED

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure of TA15/AlNbTiVZr with different hybrid ratios
An in-depth examination of TA15/AlNbTiVZr with varying hybrid 
ratios is essential to elucidate their diverse microstructures and resulting 
property differences. Consequently, this section formulated several al -
loys by introducing HEA in 20 % increments and methodically analyzed 
the microstructure of hybrid materials with HEA proportions ranging 
from 20 % to 80 %.
At the macroscopic scale, the deposited specimens demonstrated 
outstanding formability, remaining free from cracks and collapses. Fig. 3
shows the presence of clearly visible unmelted particles when the laser 
power is set at 1200 W. These particles are identifiable as basketweave 
structures with chemical compositions similar to those of the TA15 ti -
tanium alloy. Typically, these unmelted particles have a diameter 
exceeding 80 μ m, a result of inadequate laser power.
To prevent the presence of unmelted particles, the experiments 
employed a strategy of moderately increasing the laser power in 
conjunction with a powder screening method. The TA15/AlNbTiVZr 
powder exceeding 100 μ m in size is sifted out. Fig. 4 illustrates the 
macrostructure of TA15/AlNbTiVZr alloys with varying hybrid ratios 
under a laser power of 1400 W. The amount of unmelted particles in the 
hybrid materials is notably reduced. All four hybrid ratios of the TA15/ 
AlNbTiVZr alloy exhibit exceptional internal quality, showcasing a high 
degree of microstructural uniformity. These findings confirm the effec -
tiveness of slightly elevating the laser power and precisely controlling 
the powder particle dimensions (53 to 100 μ m) as viable approaches, 
which are crucial for improving the quality and efficiency of deposition 
in the LDED process.
Fig. 5 (a) and (b) present the SEM images of the TA15 and AlNbTiVZr 
HEA. The grains in this HEA are fully equiaxed, with an average grain 
size of 60.1 μ m. Preliminary XRD analysis identified the constituent 
phases as a BCC structure. However, previous studies have suggested 
that XRD technology may struggle to accurately distinguish these phases 
when the volume fraction of secondary phases in an alloy is low ( ≤ 5 %) 
or under specific diffraction angles [23] . To further confirm the phase 
composition, Fig. 5 (d) displays the TEM results of the AlNbTiVZr HEA. It 
is evident that the constituent phases are not solely BCC; a significant 
number of B2 phases are present in a dispersed distribution. Statistical 
analysis of the B2 phase particle sizes reveals an average diameter of 
29.5 nm, with detailed statistical results shown in Fig. 5 (f).
The elemental composition of TA15 titanium alloy is Ti-6.5Al-2Zr- 
Mo-V, classified as a near- α titanium alloy with a high aluminum 
equivalent. It combines the benefits of both α -type and α + β -type tita -
nium alloys, primarily strengthened through solid solution strength -
ening using the α -stabilizing element Al. The microstructure of TA15 
post-LDED is shown in Fig. 5 (a), predominantly consisting of α -Ti with 
a minor presence of β phase. The original β -phase grain boundaries are 
largely disrupted, while the α phase forms elongated lamellae within the 
β -phase matrix. The lamellar thickness ranges from approximately 
0.16 – 0.98 μ m (average of 0.38 μ m), and the lamellar length varies from 
around 0.86 – 12.24 μ m (average of 3.45 μ m).
Microstructural characteristics show notable discrepancies based on 
hybrid ratios in the TA15/AlNbTiVZr alloy system. Findings suggest that 
the corrosion resistance of the alloy is enhanced with increasing content 
of HEA. Additionally, variations in corrosion depth were noted in 
transverse sections of the alloy. Detailed examinations of the post- 
corrosion microstructure were conducted using optical microscopy 
and SEM. The investigation unveiled that the BCC phase and β phase 
displayed superior corrosion resistance, while the α phase, characterized 
by a hexagonal close-packed structure, showed greater susceptibility to 
corrosion.

deposition process, HEA efficiently dissipates heat, resulting in a smaller 
temperature gradient and further grain refinement. Moreover, owing to 
HEA ’ s intrinsic characteristics, the alloy tends to exhibit larger consti -

refinement.

3.3. Lamination design and verification in the TA15/AlNbTiVZr 
transition region
Based on the data analysis in Table 3 , the ultimate tensile strength of 
all hybrid materials is lower than that of AlNbTiVZr HEA, but higher 
than that of TA15. Additionally, their elongation is lower than that of 
TA15 but higher than that of AlNbTiVZr HEA. Given that hybrid mate -

4. Discussion
4.1. Thermal flow behavior during LDED of the gradient material
Simulation results indicate a gradual decrease in thermal dissipation 
along the solidification boundary of the melt pool, as observed by the 
decreasing G values at data collection points a1 – a5 along the pool 
boundary, as shown in Fig. 13 (b). The growth rate R at point a1, located 
at the bottom of the melt pool, approaches zero and reaches 9.4 mm/s at 
point a5. The low R value at the bottom of the melt pool is attributed to 
the nearly parallel orientation of the bottom solidification interface and 
the melting interface, resulting in a significant θ angle. Theoretically, R 
can be equal to the scanning speed V , representing the maximum stable 
growth rate of dendrite tips. Progressing from the bottom to the top 
along the solidification boundary, R significantly increases towards the 
scanning speed V as the θ angle decreases. At this juncture, the 
maximum flow velocity in the melt pool is 0.207 m/s. Assuming fluid 
circulates around the Marangoni convection loop at the maximum ve -
locity, it takes approximately 0.0198 s for one complete circulation. 
Meanwhile, based on the maximum R value, the solid-liquid interface 
requires about 0.0609 s to traverse the Marangoni convection. Conse -
quently, the solidification duration exceeds three times that of fluid 
circulation around the Marangoni convection loop, indicating robust 
convection activity and adequate mass transfer, resulting in a broad 
interfacial diffusion zone between TA15 and HEA-80 %. The calcula -
tions of G/R and G × R are shown in Fig. 13 (c). Progressing from the 
bottom to the top along the solidification boundary of the melt pool, G/R 
decreases while G × R increases, with the increased G × R at the top of the 
pool facilitating grain refinement. Additionally, the G values at the 
bottom of the melt pool for different deposition layers were docu -
mented, as shown in Fig. 13 (b). From the second to the fifth layer, the 
temperature gradient G at the bottom of the melt pool gradually di -
minishes with increasing deposition layers.

Furthermore, thermal cycling has also induced recrystallization in 
gradient materials, as shown in Fig. 10 , revealing the formation of a 
small number of equiaxed grains through partial recrystallization. High 
thermal accumulation triggers the activation of the recrystallization 
process, leading to the absorption of a significant amount of stored 
deformation energy by the GBs of the original grains. Once this energy 
surpasses a critical threshold, partial GBs bend and bulge into neigh -
boring grains, promoting the initiation of new grains and causing severe 
distortion in adjacent grains. Additionally, the merging of grain 
boundaries or subgrain boundaries can result in grain nucleation. These 
nucleation events are bounded by high-angle grain boundaries, segre -
gating them from the matrix (the deformed and partially recovered 
matrix). As these high-angle grain boundaries migrate, the nucleation 
sites gradually shift towards orientations with greater misorientations 
compared to the deformed grains to minimize interfacial energy. 
Generally, finer grains lead to a higher density of grain boundaries, 
increasing the prominence of grain boundary sliding during tensile 
deformation. The presence of grain boundaries impedes dislocation 
movement across them, thereby significantly enhancing the mechanical 
properties [27] .

5. Conclusions
A dependable metallurgical bonding between TA15 titanium alloy 
and AlNbTiVZr HEA was successfully established through strategic 
compositional design of the transition region, a controlled LDED process 
involving laser power adjustment, and post-treatment. A precise heat 
flux coupling model for LDED was developed to calculate essential pa -
rameters, including melt pool morphology, temperature field, flow ve -
locity field, temperature gradient ( G ), and solidification growth rate ( R ). 
Subsequent integrated microstructural characterization and simulation 
analyses were conducted to elucidate the evolution of the microstruc -
ture in TA15/AlNbTiVZr gradient materials.
(1) Inadequate laser power resulted in noticeable unmelted particles 
and compositional segregation, while excessive laser power 
posed substantial risks of brittle fracture and internal stresses. To 
address these challenges, a strategy that involved moderately 
raising the laser power and implementing a powder screening 
method was employed. This approach effectively controlled the 
presence of unmelted particles, resulting in the production of 
defect-free and compositionally uniform samples.
(2) The microstructural evolution of TA15/AlNbTiVZr shows a clear 
sequence with increasing hybrid ratio: α + β → α + β + BCC → 
α + BCC → BCC + B2. As the hybrid ratio increases from 20 % to 
80 %, the elongation gradually increases, while the UTS first 
decreases and then increases. Particularly, within the hybrid ratio 
range of 60 – 80 %, a synergistic enhancement of both UTS and 
elongation is evident.
(3) Three gradient transition modes were proposed. When the 
preferred single-lamination transition layer (FGM-1) was 
selected, fracture occurred in the HEA-80 % region with signifi -
cant necking. The corresponding UTS and elongation were 
1039 MPa and 8.1 %, respectively. Subsequent heat treatment 
further increased the UTS to 1089 MPa while maintaining an
