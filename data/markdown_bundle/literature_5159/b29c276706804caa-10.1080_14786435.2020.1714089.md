ABSTRACT
In Ni-based superalloys, it is usually found that borides can
strengthen the grain boundaries, thereby resulting in an
increase in mechanical strength and high-temperature
creep properties. Due to their importance and prevalence in
Ni-based superalloys, this study employs ﬁrst-principles
methods to investigate the crystallographic structure,
anisotropic elastic response, and electronic properties of the
major borides, such as M
2B, M 5B3 and M 3B2 (M: Cr, Mo, W),
respectively, which is necessary for the assessment of
complex mechanical responses of Ni-based superalloys. The
results demonstrate that the studied borides are all
thermodynamically and mechanically stable. Among the
MxBy binary borides analysed, Cr xBy exhibits the largest
shear modulus, Young ’s modulus, and Vicker hardness
values, and these properties increase with the increase of B
contents. The studied borides display nearly isotropic elastic
properties except for W
5B3 and W 3B2. The electronic
structure analysis of M xBy shows that the strong
hybridisation between M-d and B-p orbitals leads to these
borides exhibiting higher theoretical hardness, and the
overlapping peaks of M-d and B-p orbitals move to a lower
energy area with the increase of B contents, which leads to
the increase of shear and Young ’s moduli of M
xBy.
Furthermore, for M 3B2 borides, the Cr-B bonds and Cr – Cr
bonds are much stronger than the W-B & Mo-B bonds, and
W-W & Mo-Mo bonds, respectively, which leads to Cr
xBy
yielding the largest values of elastic moduli.

M2B-type boride was considered to possess a unique tetragonal structure [ 14],

dual-phase structure characteristic [ 7,11]. The crystallographic structure of
these borides has been well documented, while the availability of the mechanical
response of these boride precipitates is still scarce due to the diﬃ culties in
growing adequately sized single-crystal samples required for experimental
measurements. Therefore, theoretical investigations, in terms of ﬁrst-principle
simulations, are employed due to the shortage of experiments [ 15–17 ].
Through these calculations, the phase stability and elastic properties of Cr
5B3
have been reported [ 17,18]. However, there are no reports on the mechanical
response of Mo 5B3 and W 5B3 borides in literature. In addition, investigations
into M3B2 binary borides are lacking, except for a recent study on the site pre-
ference and mechanical properties of M 3B2 ternary system with M referring to a
mixture of Cr and W [ 19]. For the M2B-type boride, only the elasticity and bond
hardness of M 2B (M: Cr, Mo, W) in tetragonal structure had been studied [ 20].
Thus, the mechanical response of M 2B in the orthorhombic structure remains
absent.
The above incompleteness of the fundamental properties motivates the
present work to perform a systematical study of the electronic and elastic prop-
erties of M
5B3-type, M3B2-type, and M 2B-type (M: Cr, Mo, W) borides by ﬁrst-
principles calculations. This is of great signi ﬁcance for the study of complex
mechanical responses of Ni-based superalloys and their structures.

2.2. Elastic constants at 0 K
In an e ﬀort to calculate the second ordered elastic sti ﬀness constants, Cij,a n
eﬃcient stress –strain method [ 21] was employed. Speci ﬁcally, a given set of
strains, εj =( ε1, ε2, ε3, ε4, ε5, ε6), where ε1, ε2 and ε3 refer to the normal strains
and others refer to the shear strains, are applied to a crystal to generate small
elastic deformations. Then the stresses, σ
i =( σ1, σ2, σ3, σ4, σ5, σ6), are determined
for the deformed lattice by ﬁrst-principles. Hooke’ s law governs the elastic
strain–stress behaviour, i.e.
si = Cij1j (1)
where Cij is the 6 × 6 elastic sti ﬀness matrix. The number of independent
elements of Cij is determined by the crystal symmetry. For instance, there are
six independent elastic constants ( C11, C12, C44, C13, C33, and C66) for a tetra-
gonal structure and nine independent elastic constants ( C11, C12, C44, C13, C33,
C66, C22, C33, and C55) for an orthorhombic structure. To obtain the value of
Cij, each set of stains εj are individually applied to the structure. In practice,
we choose six sets of stains εj with values of ± 0.007, ±0.010, and ± 0.013, respect-
ively. This methodology ensures that the elastic constants yield reasonable com-
putational results, and the error between the calculated results of each set of
strains is less than 2%. Based on the obtained elastic constants C
ij, the polycrys-
talline aggregate properties, such as the bulk modulus ( B), shear modulus ( G),
Young’s modulus ( E), and Poisson ’s ratio ( ν) can be evaluated by using the
Voigt-Reuss-Hill (VRH) approximation [ 22].
2.3. First-principles calculations
Based on ﬁrst-principles methods [23,24], all the calculations were performed by
employing the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) [ 25,26] using the
projector augmented wave (PAW) potential [27]. The Perdew–Burke –Ernzerhof
of exchange –correlation functional was used within the generalised gradient
approximation [ 28,29]. The plane-wave basis energy employs a cut-o ﬀ energy
of 400 eV, which yields convergent results. Spin polarisation was considered
due to the ferromagnetic nature of Cr. Brillouin zone integrations were per-
formed by using a Monkhorst – Pack [ 30] k-point mesh with Γ-centered 13 ×
13 × 5, 5 × 5 × 13, 11 × 11 × 11 for the tetragonal structures of M
5B3,M 3B2,
M2B, respectively, and 5 × 9 × 13 for the orthorhombic structure of M 2B. A sen-
sitivity analysis for the k-point meshes based upon the energy was performed to
ensure convergent results. Throughout the calculation, the convergence

4. Conclusions
The crystallographic structure, anisotropic elastic properties, and electronic
properties of M2B, M5B3, and M3B2, where M = Cr, Mo, and W, are investigated
based on the ﬁrst-principles calculations, and the main results are summarised
as follows:
(1) The studied borides are all thermodynamically and mechanically stable, and
the CrxBy exhibits the largest shear modulus (G), Young ’s modulus (E), and
Vicker hardness (Hv), while the elastic properties for the two crystal struc-
tures of M2B reported in literature are nearly the same. The values of G, E,
and Hv each increase with an increasing B atom content. Additionally,
M2B-type, M3B2-type, and M5B3-type borides are all nearly isotropic,
except for W5B3 and W3B2.
(2) The calculated total density of states (TDOS) of MxBy indicates that these
borides exhibit metallic character. Furthermore, the TDOS near the Fermi
level is mainly attributed to the B-p and M-d orbitals. The strong hybridis-
ation between the B-p and M-d orbitals form strong bonds with mixed the
metallic and covalent bonds, thus leading to the observed high values of the
elastic properties. The overlapping peaks of M-d and B-p orbitals move to a
lower energy area with an increasing B atom content, which leads to an
increase of G and E.
