The present work investigated the formation and mechanical behavior of body-centered-cubic
(BCC) Zr(Hf)-Nb-Ti medium entropy alloys (MEAs), in which three series of alloys,
[Zr-Zr14](Zr,Nb)3, [Ti-Zr 14](Ti,Nb)3, and [Ti-(Hf,Zr) 14](Nb)3, were designed by the cluster
formula approach. With increasing the Nb content, the BCC- b structural stability of the
[Zr-Zr14](Zr,Nb)3 alloys would be enhanced, as evidenced by the BCC [Zr-Zr 14](Nb)3
(Zr83.33Nb16.67 in atomic percent at. pct) alloy containing a minor amount of x phase. An
appropriate content of Ti addition can further improve the BCC- b stability of [Ti-Zr 14](Nb3)
(Zr77.77Ti5.56Nb16.67) alloy without any x precipitation. The further substitution of Hf/Ti for the
Zr could also render the [Ti-Zr 8Hf4Ti2](Nb3) (Zr 44.44 Hf22.22Ti16.67Nb16.67) alloy with a single
BCC structure. All these BCC MEAs exhibit prominent mechanical properties, as exempliﬁed
by the [Ti-Zr 8Hf6](Nb3)( Z r44.44Hf33.33Ti5.56Nb16.67) with a higher yield strength of 662 MPa, a
larger elongation to fraction of 15.2 pct, and a lower Young’s modulus of 71 GPa.

I. INTRODUCTION
HIGH-ENTROPY alloys (HEAs), containing multi-
ple principal elements with equimolar, near-equimolar,
or non-equimolar mixing, have attracted more and more
interests due to their simple crystalline structures, promi-
nent mechanical, and corrosion/oxidation-resistant prop-
erties.[1–7] They are generally classiﬁed into three groups,
based on face-centered-cubic (FCC), body-centered-cubic
(BCC), and close-packed-hexagonal (HCP) solid solution
phases, respectively, in which some simple intermetallic
phases (mainly ordered superstructure phases with
L12-Ni3Al and B2-CsCl type) would be expected to be
precipitated to strengthen their solid solution matrix. [5–7]
Especially, the BCC-based refractory HEAs consisting of
early transition metals (Ti, Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta, W, Mo, etc.)
exhibit great potentials for high-temperature (HT) appli-
cations.[8–18] For instance, the BCC WMoNbTaTi HEA
with an equimolar mixing possesses high strength at HTs,
as evidenced by the yield strength and ultimate strength
being 586 MPa and 814 MPa at 1200 /C176 C, respectively,
which is much greater than that (150 to 400 MPa for
yielding) of some widely used Ni-base superalloys such as
Inconel 718 and MAR-M 247 at the temperature above
1000 /C176 C.[3,15] Also, it is noted that two BCC solid solution
phases are indeed presented in this alloy, where the BCC1
phase is enriched by Ta and W, and the BCC2 is enriched
by Ti and Nb, respectively, due to the segregation of
elements.[15] Actually, the separation of two BCC phases is
common in refractory HEAs, even including some qua-
ternary alloys like WMoNbTa, NbTaVW, and NbTaTiV,
which would not be conducive to the improvement of alloy
properties.[16–18] With decreasing the number of consti-
tuted elements, such alloys containing three or four
principle elements are also called medium entropy alloys
(MEAs).[19–21]
Intrinsically, the elemental segregation represents the
preferred atomic nearest neighbors existing in HEAs,
which can be well explained by the local chemical

The CSRO feature is always obvious in alloys consisting
of elements with strong interactions, such as Al1.3CoCr-

alloys constituted of elements with weak interactions,
although it is inconspicuous. [24–30] For instance, the

calculated pair-distribution function (PDF) at short
inter-atomic distances by the cluster model can well
describe that obtained by neutron scattering.[31] It is noted
that the change of local CSROs could induce phase
structure transition of alloys.
For the light-weight refractory BCC-based HEAs and
MEAs consisting of Ti/Zr/Hf and Nb/Ta, [32–34] the BCC
structural stability should be primarily considered since
Ti, Zr, and Hf elements do not exhibit a stable BCC- b
structure. Many existing works have demonstrated that
the equimolar mixing of Ti, Zr, and Hf with other
BCC-stabilized elements of Mo, Nb, Ta, and V could
induce a phase separation of two BCC phases. [15–18,33,34]
Moreover, when the amount of BCC-stabilized elements
is not enough, some metastable phases of a¢ and a¢¢
would like to precipitate from the BCC matrix in some
Zr-Ti-Hf-based HEAs, such as ZrHfTiTa 0.6 and
Ti35Zr27.5Hf27.5Nb5Ta5 (atomic percent, at. pct), leading
to a change in mechanical properties. [32,35] Thus, it is
necessary to stabilize the BCC phase structure through
appropriately tuning the amount of BCC-stabilized
elements. Our cluster model related to CSROs could
give a simple cluster formula to design alloy composi-
tions in multi-component systems, including Al-TM
(transition metals) HEAs and traditional solid solution
alloys, since it can match the constituted elements
optimally.[36–41] Especially in metastable BCC b-Ti
systems, a series of designed alloys with the cluster
formula of [(Mo,Sn)-(Ti,Zr) 14](Nb,Ta)m (m =1 , 3 )
exhibit not only a single BCC- b structure, but also
lower Young’s modulus with E < 60 GPa.[42] Therefore,
the present work will investigate the BCC structural
stability in Zr(Hf)-Nb-Ti MEAs, which are designed by
the cluster formula approach. The mechanical proper-
ties of these designed alloys will also be tested to study
the eﬀect of the phase constitution in alloys on the
mechanical properties.
II. COMPOSITION DESIGN VIA THE CLUSTER
FORMULA APPROACH
The cluster formula approach is composed of the
nearest-neighbor cluster and the second-nearest-neigh-
bor glue atoms, where the cluster is centered by a solute
atom having strong interaction (characterized by a large
negative enthalpy of mixing ( DH)[43]) with the base
solvent atoms to present the strongest CSRO, and the
glue atoms generally have weak interactions with the
base to ﬁll the space among clusters for the balance of
the atomic-packing density. [31,36–41] Thus, it could be
expressed with the formula of [cluster](glue atoms) m (m
being the number of glue atoms). Particularly, in the
BCC structure, the nearest-neighbor cluster is the
rhombi dodecahedron with a coordination number
(CN) of 14, and the glue atom number was ideally
calculated as m =1 o r m = 3 with the guide of the
Friedel oscillation theory for electron-structure stabi-
lization,[36] which corresponds to the fact that whether
the strong BCC-stabilized elements (Mo, V, etc.) or the
weak BCC-stabilized elements (Nb, Ta, etc.) were
added. It was well identiﬁed by experiments in mul-
ti-component Ti alloys, in which both [(Mo 0.5Sn0.5)-(-
Ti13Zr1)]Nb1 (m = 1) and [Sn 1-Ti14]Nb3 (m = 3) alloys
possess not only a single BCC- b structure but also lower
Young’s modulus with E < 56 GPa. [42] Actually, the
cluster formula approach could provide a simpliﬁed
path to select alloy compositions intentionally in mul-
ti-component systems, which could reduce the amount
of experimental veriﬁcation on a large extent. Compared
with the Mo, Nb is a relatively weaker BCC-stabilized
element, the cluster formula with m = 3 will be applied
in the present work to design alloy compositions with
the BCC structure in Zr(Hf)-Ti-Nb systems. First, the
binary Zr-Nb is selected as the basic system since the
BCC structural stability of alloys could be enhanced
with increasing the Nb content. [44,45] However, it is
noted that the metastable phases, such as a¢, a¢¢,a n d x,
are always coexisted with the BCC- b matrix even when
the Nb content is higher than the critical lower limit ( ~
20 at. pct Nb) for BCC stabilization. [45–47] Thus, the
cluster formula of [Zr-Zr 14](Zr,Nb)3 is determined in
Zr-Nb due to the positive enthalpy between Nb and Zr
(DHZr-Nb = 4 kJ mol -1[43]), where three alloys of
N1-[Zr-Zr14](Zr2Nb1) (Zr 94.44Nb5.56, at. pct),
N2-[Zr-Zr14](Zr1Nb2) (Zr 88.89Nb11.11, at. pct), and
N3-[Zr-Zr14](Nb3) (Zr 83.33Nb16.67, at. pct) are given
(designated as the N series). Then, in order to improve
the BCC structural stability, we add the third element Ti
to substitute for the cluster center Zr and glue atom Nb
in N3, respectively, to form the T series of alloys,
T1-[Ti-Zr14](Nb3) (Zr 77.77Ti5.56Nb16.67, at. pct),
T2-[Ti-Zr14](TiNb2) (Zr 77.78Ti11.11Nb11.11, at. pct), and
T3-[Ti-Zr14](Ti2Nb) (Zr 77.77Ti16.67Nb5.56, at. pct).
Finally, another H series of alloys, H1-[Ti-Hf 14](Nb3)
(Hf77.77Ti5.56Nb16.67, at. pct), H2-[Ti-Zr 8Hf6](Nb3)
(Zr44.44Hf33.33Ti5.56Nb16.67, at. pct), and H3-[Ti-Zr 8Hf4-
Ti2](Nb3) (Zr 44.44Hf22.22Ti16.67Nb16.67, at. pct) are also
obtained when the Hf element replaces the Zr in the
cluster shell to form Zr/Hf-based alloys due to that Zr,
Hf, and Ti are in the same group. Table I lists all the
compositions of these three series of designed alloys,
including the cluster formula, atomic percent (at. pct),
and weight percent (wt pct).

were prepared by arc-melting the raw metal materials in
the high-purity argon atmosphere, in which the purities

IV. RESULTS
A. Microstructural Characterization
Figure 1 gives the XRD patterns of these three series
(N, T, and H) of alloys. In the N series of Zr-Nb binary
alloys, the single BCC- b structure appears in the
N3-Zr83.33Nb16.67 alloy alone, while the N1-Zr 94.44Nb5.56
alloy is based on the HCP- a structure. It means that the
BCC-b structure becomes more stable with increasing the
Nb content, as evidenced by the diﬀraction peaks of
BCC-b are much stronger. Also, a small amount of
metastable x phase with a hexagonal structure is accom-
panied with the BCC-b in N2-Zr88.89Nb11.11. Based on the
N3 alloy, the substitution of a small amount of Ti for Zr in
the cluster center of the formula still renders the
T1-Zr77.77Ti5.56Nb16.67 ternary alloy with a single BCC- b
structure. However, the further substitution of Ti for Nb
deteriorates the b structural stability, in which both a and
x phases could precipitate from the b matrix in
T2-Zr77.78Ti11.11Nb11.11 and T3-Zr 77.77Ti16.67Nb5.56
alloys, indicating the weaker BCC- b stability of Ti than
that of Nb. Moreover, when the Zr atoms in T1 are
completely replaced by the Hf, the Hf-based
H1-Hf77.77Ti5.56Nb16.67 ternary alloy is also primarily
composed of the BCC-b structure plus a small amount of
a and x phases, indicating that Nb possesses a much
stronger BCC-stabilized ability to b-Zr than to b-Hf.
While a combination of Zr and Hf with a near-equimolar
mixing renders the H2-Zr44.44Hf33.33Ti5.56Nb16.67 quater-
nary MEA with a single BCC- b structure. A similar case
also exists in the H3-Zr 44.44Hf22.22Ti16.67Nb16.67 MEA
with the further substitution of Ti for Hf.

B. Mechanical Properties
The engineering tensile stress–strain curves of these
three series of alloys are shown in Figure 5, from which
the mechanical property parameters, including the yield
strength ( rYS), ultimate tensile strength ( rUTS), elonga-
tion to fracture ( d), and the Young’s modulus ( E), are
listed in Table I. For the N series of Zr-Nb binary
alloys, both N1-Zr 94.44Nb5.56 and N2-Zr 88.89Nb11.11
possess a higher strength ( rYS > 670 MPa) at the cost
of ductility with d < 6 pct, while the N3-Zr 83.33Nb16.67
alloy exhibits a good ductility with d = 14.2 pct and a
yield strength of rYS = 545 MPa. More interestingly,
the Young’s modulus of this series of alloys decreases
from E = 71 to 75 GPa for N1 and N2 to E = 58 GPa
for N3 with increasing the Nb content. Based on the N3
alloy, the substitution of Ti for Zr in the cluster center of
the formula still renders the T1-Zr 77.77Ti5.56Nb16.67
MEA with similar mechanical property with E =5 7
GPa, rYS = 561 MPa, rUTS = 588 MPa, and d = 15.6
pct. However, the excessive Ti substitution for Nb
would deteriorate the mechanical property, as evidenced
by the fact that the brittle fracture happens in the
T3-Zr77.77Ti16.67Nb5.56 alloy without any ductility,
which results from the precipitation of a large amount
of brittle x phase. Actually, the H1-Hf 77.77Ti5.56Nb16.67
alloy also has a fatal embrittlement with a brittle
fracture due to the x precipitation. However, the
co-existence of Zr and Hf with a near-equimolar mixing
renders the H2-Zr 44.44Hf33.33Ti5.56Nb16.67 quaternary
MEA with a good comprehensive mechanical property
(rYS = 662 MPa, rUTS = 754 MPa, and d = 15.2 pct).
A further substitution of Ti for Hf could slightly
decrease the strength ( rYS = 634 MPa) but still retain
a good ductility with d = 14.5 pct in the
H3-Zr44.44Hf22.22Ti16.67Nb16.67 MEA. Also, the E value
is reduced from 109 to ~70 GPa due to that the x phase
is inhibited on a large extent.
Surfaces of the typical fracture samples were exam-
ined using SEM. The fracture morphology of alloys
with large ductility exhibits obvious necking and is full
of ﬁne dimples, as presented in Figure 6(a), being the
fracture surface of N3-Zr 83.33Nb16.67 alloy. Also, it is
obviously noted that the fracture is induced by the
macro-scaled pore defects produced during rapid
solidiﬁcation. Such alloys would exhibit a much better
ductility if these cast defects are eliminated. While for
brittle alloys, such as the H1-Hf 77.77Ti5.56Nb16.67, the
sample shows intergranular fracture with some tearing
cracks along grain boundaries (Figure 6(b)), indicating
the brittle fracture.

VI. CONCLUSIONS
The BCC structural stability and its eﬀect on the
mechanical property of Zr(Hf)-Nb-Ti medium entropy
alloys (MEAs) were investigated in the present work.
Three series of alloy compositions, [Zr-Zr 14](Zr,Nb)3,
[Ti-Zr14](Ti,Nb)3, and [Ti-(Hf,Zr) 14](Nb)3 were designed
by the cluster formula approach. The following conclu-
sions are drawn.
1. With increasing the Nb content in the
[Zr-Zr14](Zr,Nb)3 series, the matrix changes from
the HCP- a to the BCC- b phase, in which the x
phase easily coexists with the BCC- b, as evidenced
by the BCC [Zr-Zr 14](Nb3) (Zr83.33Nb16.67 in at. pct)
alloy with a minor amount of x.
2. The Ti addition with an appropriate amount of
~ 5.56 at. pct could inhibit the precipitation of x
nanoparticles and retain the BCC- b structure in
[Ti-Zr14](Nb3)( Z r 77.77Ti5.56Nb16.67) alloy, while
excessive Ti would reduce the b structural stability,
resulting in a large amount of x precipitation to
induce an embrittlement in [Ti-Zr 14](Ti2Nb)
(Zr77.77Ti16.67Nb5.56) alloy. A similar case also
occurs in the [Ti-Hf 14](Nb3) (Hf 77.77Ti5.56Nb16.67)
alloy when the Zr is replaced by Hf. However, a
combination of Zr, Hf, Ti, and Nb with a
