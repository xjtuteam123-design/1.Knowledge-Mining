ABSTRACT 
In this study, the creep behaviors of two Ni-based single crystal superalloys with/without Re addition designed 
for industrial gas turbine application were investigated at 900 
◦
C under a range of applied stresses (200 MPa – 330 
MPa), and the examinations of the microstructures and dislocation configurations were performed at the min -
imum creep rate and creep rupture under each condition of both alloys. The results indicated that although the 
creep lives spanned over thousands of hours under different stresses, dislocations underwent the similar 
mechanisms including the proliferation and movements by gliding-climbing process in decelerating stage, fol -
lowed by the progressive increase in dislocations shearing into the γ
′
phases until creep rupture in each alloy. 
This led to a special rafting regime which was characterized by the sluggish formation of the rafting structure in 
the present superalloy, considering the relatively lower γ
′
volume fraction, lower lattice misfit, and lower 
elemental diffusion of the experimental superalloys than those of traditional Ni-based superalloys. Thus, the 
“ duration effect ” was proposed to describe the strong correlation between the creep duration and microstructural 
evolution, which showed insensitivity to the applied stress and accumulation strain, resulting in the consistently 
exponential relationships between the time to the minimum creep rate and the applied stress or creep life of each 
alloy within a wide range of stresses. The addition of Re gave rise to an inversion of the rafting tendency during 
high-temperature creep without changing the deformation mechanism under different stresses. Nevertheless, the 
addition of Re could still enhance the creep property under each stress, which contributes to provide insights for 
optimizing the alloying design and enhancing long-term reliable service of industrial gas turbine blades. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys are widely utilized as the 
materials of choice for manufacturing aero-engine and industrial gas 
turbine (IGT) blades [ 1 ]. The size of an IGT blade is significantly larger 
than that of its aero-engine counterpart, meaning it also weights more, 
while the service temperature of the IGT blades remains consistently 
lower (870 
◦
C – 1020 
◦
C) than the peak temperature of aero-engine 
blades. In addition, the operational requirement for IGT blades is usu -
ally considered significantly longer compared to that of aero-engine 
blades, which imposes strict demands on their long-term operational 
stability [ 2 , 3 ]. 
Ni-based SX superalloys derive their excellent high-temperature 
mechanical properties from the special microstructure consisting of or -
dered γ
′
precipitates (L1
2
)- disordered γ matrix (FCC) [ 4 ]. During high 
temperature creep, a commonly observed microstructural feature is the 
formation of a lamellar γ / γ
′
morphology, so-called rafting, where the 
temperature and strain can affect the rate at which the rafted structure 
develops. Kamaraj et al. [ 5 ] summarized the microstructural develop -
ment after creep conducted at a range of temperatures (760 
◦
C – 1100 
◦
C) 
and under various stresses for some typical Ni-based SX superalloys 
reported in literatures. They recognized that the facile and early for -
mation of a rafted structure occurs when the alloy is subjected to a 
temperature over 900 
◦

It is widely known that the γ / γ

based SX superalloys. The majority of the commercial Ni-based SX su -
peralloys with negative lattice misfit at high temperature usually tend to 
develop a rafted structure perpendicular to the loading axis when 
applying a tensile stress, i.e., N-type rafting [ 7 ]. Previous research has 
indicated that higher temperature ( > 950 
◦

promote even spontaneous formation of a rafting structure in the 
absence of the applied stress, where the considerable negative misfit 
acted as the driving force [ 8 ]. Nevertheless, Ni-based SX superalloys 
with lower lattice misfit exhibit favorable microstructural stability with 
slight coalescence of the γ

stress promotes the occurrence of the precipitate shearing or Orowan 
looping mechanisms, otherwise the dislocations could only climb over 
the γ

the IGT superalloys, considering the elemental and microstructural 
characteristics, especially under a relatively lower temperature 
( < 950 
◦

Additionally, the creep behaviors and deformation mechanisms of 
Ni-based SX superalloys should also be affected by different alloying 
additions even under the same creep condition [ 1 , 15 , 16 ]. Re is widely 
accepted as an effective solid solution strengthener in Ni-based SX su -
peralloys, wherein small Re addition can promote better temperature 
capability, higher microstructural stability and remarkable creep prop -
erties of the alloys [ 17 ]. This can be mainly attributed to the following 
“ Re effects ” : (1) impeding the coarsening behavior of the γ
′
precipitates 
and hindering the deformation mechanisms related to elemental diffu -
sion (i.e., glide or climb) due to the reduced bulk diffusion rates [ 18 , 19 ]; 
(2) ensuring the negative γ / γ
′
lattice misfit which leads to denser 
dislocation networks during creep [ 20 , 21 ]. Re can refine the γ
′
pre -
cipitates with regular morphology and uniform size after heat treatment, 
contributing to the stability of γ
′
precipitates [ 9 , 22 ]. More recent re -
searches found the enrichment of Re on the interfacial dislocations 
provides the drag effect on dislocation movement, thus further improves 
the creep strength [ 23 , 24 ]. Additionally, Re has also been demonstrated 
to improve both the oxidation resistance and the hot-corrosion resis -
tance of the superalloys, making it a potential candidate element in the 
development of next generation superalloys for IGT application [ 25 , 26 ]. 
Our previous research showed that 2 wt% Re addition can effectively 
enhance the microstructural stability during long-term thermal expo -
sure, and significantly improve the long-term creep behavior of an IGT 
superalloy at 900 
◦
C ( > 5000 h) [ 27 , 28 ]. However, few studies have 
investigated the creep deformation mechanism of IGT superalloys at 
900 
◦
C, and little is known about the effect of Re on the long-term creep 
deformation mechanism. 
In the present work, a series of creep tests were conducted on both 
Re-free and Re-containing superalloys, subjected to a range of applied 
stresses (200 MPa – 330MPa) at 900 
◦
C. The microstructures and dislo -
cation configurations at the minimum creep rate and after creep rupture 
were investigated to reveal the effect of stress on the microstructural 
evolution and operative deformation mechanisms. By analyzing the 
creep curves, the features of the long-term creep behavior were eluci -
dated. On this basis, further efforts were made to clarify the Re effect on 
the creep property and deformation mechanisms in the present Ni-based 
SX superalloys designed for IGT application.

3.3. Dislocation configurations 
Considering the different rafting types, STEM images were obtained 
from the longitudinal section ( B = [010] and equivalently [100]) and 
cross section ( B = [001]) for alloys 0Re and 2Re, respectively, to observe 
the complete dislocation configurations. The dislocation configurations 
in the specimens interrupted at the minimum creep rate of both alloys 
are shown in Fig. 5 . The stress axis (red arrows) and the observation 
plane (blue plane) are illustrated in the schematic at the bottom of Fig. 5 . 
In alloy 0Re, the dislocations undergo a sharp proliferation and tend 
to propagate by gliding in the vertical channels (parallel to the loading 
axis) during the very short creep durations under higher stresses (330 
MPa and 250 MPa), as indicated in Fig. 5 (a1) and (a2). Here, the dis -
locations marked by black arrows change their directions by 90
◦
, indi -
cating that dislocation bowing takes place in the γ channels, forming the 
grid-like dislocation network surrounding the γ
′
precipitates. Under 200 
MPa, the dislocations have a lower density which induces less complex 
dislocation reactions that barely form the dense and periodic dislocation 
networks as those under 330 MPa and 250 MPa, as shown in Fig. 5 (a3). 
In addition, dislocations attempt to climb over the γ
′
phase when the 
spreading matrix dislocations encounter the γ / γ
′
interfaces during the 
decelerating stage, leading to the overall bending dislocations at the 
minimum creep rate under different stresses of alloy 0Re, as shown in 
Fig. 5 (a1)~(a3). 
Fig. 5 (b1)~(b3) give the dislocation configurations at the minimum 
creep rate under different stresses of alloy 2Re. Here, the dislocations 
tend to proliferate and glide in the horizontal γ channels (perpendicular 
to the loading axis), while bowing dislocations or periodic bulges cannot 
be readily observed regardless of the applied stress. Although the

4.2. Microstructural evolution during creep under different stresses 
Commonly, the rafting of γ
′
phase during high-temperature creep of 
Ni-based superalloys exhibits the following characteristics: (1) the early 
formation of the rafting structure at the initial creep stage [ 43 ]; (2) the 
onset of the rafting regime once the macroscopic strain exceeds the 
threshold strain [ 6 ]; (3) the accelerated rafting process promoted by 
increasing the applied stress [ 32 ]. However, in this study, sluggish for -
mation of the rafting structure can be observed in both alloys regardless 
of the strain. Specifically, the γ
′
phase remains nearly cuboidal and 
separated at the minimum creep rate when the strain accumulates for 
1.08% under 330 MPa in alloy 2Re ( Fig. 3 (c1)), yet N-type rafting occurs 
at the minimum creep rate when the strain accumulates for only 0.60% 
under 200 MPa ( Fig. 3 (c3)). More evident phenomenon takes place in 
alloy 0Re: rafting structures were not observed when the strain reaches 
1.06% at the minimum creep rate under 330 MPa ( Fig. 3 (a1)); even 
when the strain reaches 28.0% at creep rupture, the γ
′
phase remain 
separated and dispersed ( Fig. 3 (b1)). Nevertheless, substantial P-type 
rafting already occurs at the minimum creep rate under 200 MPa, 
although the creep strain is only 0.47% ( Fig. 3 (a3)). 
The red dotted squares in Fig. 4 circle the microstructural parameters 
(with the occurrence of rafting) with the similar creep times, indicating 
that similar value of every microstructural parameter is obtained once 
the creep time is approached, despite the completely different stress or 
strain in the experimental alloys under a certain range of stresses. These 
observations all suggest that a different rafting regime occurs in the 
present alloying system, exhibiting the following characteristics: (1) the 
sluggish formation of the rafting structure during creep; (2) the absence 
of the rafting structure even at creep rupture with high strain but short 
duration under high stress; (3) the sensitivity of the microstructural 
evolution to creep duration rather than applied stress. Thus, the rafting 
regime occurs when reaching a sufficient creep duration under a range 
of applied stress, and a concept of “ duration effect ” can be proposed to 
describe the direct correlation between the microstructural evolution 
and creep duration instead of the applied stress and accumulated strain. 
The early formation of the rafting structures of commercial Ni-based 
SX superalloys during high-temperature creep can be mainly attributed 
to the higher designed γ
′
volume fraction (65%~70%) and the higher 
elemental diffusional rate at higher creep temperature ( > 950 
◦
C) as well 
as the considerable negative lattice misfit [ 43 – 45 ]. Indeed, the rafting 
regime is essentially related to the elemental directional diffusion [ 46 ]. 
Under relatively lower lattice misfit and lower temperature, the driving 
force of rafting is impeded, leading to the higher dependence of the 
rafting formation on the diffusional duration. Similar sluggish rafting 
regime has also been found in a Re-free Ni-based SX superalloy during 
creep at 900 
◦
C [ 47 ]. Our previous results showed that Cr, which was the 
primary alloying addition in nominal composition and partitioned 
strongly to γ matrix, acted as the rate-limiting element in controlling the 
γ
′
coarsening behavior. The diffusion-controlled effect of Cr was further 
enhanced by the addition of Re, which considerably enabled the sig -
nificant microstructural stability of the experimental alloys during 
long-term thermal exposure at 900 
◦
C [ 27 ]. Additionally, compared with 
commercial Ni-base superalloys, these experimental alloys possess 
relatively lower γ
′
volume fraction (50%~60%) and broader γ channels, 
which create difficulties to form a rafted structure during creep as it 
becomes more difficult for the slowly growing γ
′
precipitates to connect 
with each other over the wider γ channels [ 27 , 47 ]. Consequently, the 
retarded elemental diffusion at 900 
◦
C, combined with the lower lattice 
misfit, lower interfacial energy as well as the lower γ
′
volume fraction, 
mutually impacts the effect of the applied stress on the microstructural 
evolution during creep at 900 
◦
C, giving rise to the sluggish formation of 
the rafted structure. Therefore, sufficient diffusion at longer creep times 
is necessary to facilitate the occurrence of rafting. Also, the consistent 
creep mechanism under different stresses discussed in section 4.1 fa -
cilitates the possibility and realization of the “ duration-affected micro -
structural evolution ” . 
Recently, combined with the plasticity assisted redistribution of 
interacting solutes (PARIS) mechanism [ 48 ], Antonov et al. [ 49 ] further 
recognized that higher applied stress leads to less segregation of solutes 
to the dislocations and consequently lower level of microstructural 
evolution even as substantial strain accumulation occurs. These mech -
anisms contribute to the duration effect on microstructural evolution in 
this study, where the mass transport through pipe-diffusion acts as the 
pathway for rafting governed by the solute-segregated dislocations 
under different stress levels.

5. Conclusions 
In the present work, we systematically compared the creep behaviors 
at 900 
◦
C under different stresses (200 MPa-330 MPa) of two Ni-based 
single crystal superalloys with and without Re addition designed for 
industrial gas turbine applications. The creep deformation mechanisms 
and microstructural evolution as well as the resulting creep behaviors 
were analyzed with the considerations of the microstructural features, 
and the Re effect on creep behaviors and mechanisms was further 
clarified. Thus, the following conclusions can be drawn. 
1. The addition of 2 wt% Re can still effectively enhance the creep 
properties of the superalloys designed for industrial gas turbine 
application by reducing the minimum creep rate and prolonging the 
decelerating creep stage under the same creep condition. The 
strengthening effect of Re is also increasingly stronger with 
decreasing applied stress, where the creep life of over 5000 h can be 
obtained under 200 MPa. 
2. Lower stress facilitates the appearance of the obvious “ transition 
stage ” in the long-term creep behaviors right after the minimum 
creep rate, which is absent under higher stresses. The “ transition 
stage ” with very long duration but limited strain accumulation 
contributes to the reliable long-term creep properties of the experi -
mental alloys, where the addition of Re can also prolong the “ tran -
sition stage ” under the same creep condition. 
3. Although the addition of Re changes the rafting tendency during 
creep, the creep deformation mechanisms of both alloys under either 
stress is similar: (1) The proliferated dislocations glide in γ matrix 
and climb over the γ
′
precipitates when encountering the γ / γ
′
in -
terfaces, and the dislocations tangles in γ matrix or at the γ / γ
′
in -
terfaces before the minimum creep rate. (2) A few dislocations 
shearing into the γ
′
phase is responsible for the onset of the accel -
erating stage. (3) The progressively increasing dislocations shearing 
events facilitate the significantly increasing creep rate until creep 
rupture. 
4. The sluggish formation of the rafting structure during creep displays 
sensitivity to the creep duration rather than loading stress or accu -
mulated strain, which can be proposed as the “ duration effect ” on the 
microstructural evolution. This is mainly associated with the 
consistent creep deformation mechanism as well as the microstruc -
tural characteristics with the comparatively lower γ
′
volume fraction, 
lower lattice misfit and lower elemental diffusion rate than those of 
the traditional Ni-based SX superalloys. 
5. Consistently linear relationships exist between the time to the min -
imum creep rate and the applied stress or the creep life in both alloys 
(in logarithm), where the slopes are even unaffected by the Re 
addition. This is mainly attributed to the duration-affected micro -
structural evolution and similar creep deformation mechanism be -
tween short-term and long-term creep.
