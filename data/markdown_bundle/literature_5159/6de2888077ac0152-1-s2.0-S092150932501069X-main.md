ABSTRACT
The GH3536 pure alloy and nano-WC/GH3536 composite material were fabricated using the circular oscillating 
laser deposition technique. Their microstructure, room temperature (25 
◦
C) and high temperature (980 
◦
C) 
tensile properties, as well as crack and fracture morphology, were comprehensively analyzed. The results showed 
that at 25 
◦
C, compared with the pure alloy, the composite material exhibited a 17 % increase in yield strength, a 
7 % decrease in fracture strength, and a 95 % reduction in fracture elongation. At 980 
◦
C, the WC/GH3536 
composite demonstrated a 9 % increase in yield strength, a 14 % increase in fracture strength, and a 46 % 
improvement in fracture elongation, compared to the pure alloy. Fracture morphology analysis revealed that 
with increasing temperature, the fracture mechanism of the pure alloy transitioned from ductile to brittle, while 
the WC/GH3536 composite toughness has improved. This change is attributed to different grain growth patterns 
in the pure alloy and WC/GH3536 composite at 980 
◦
C. The pure alloy ’ s microstructure, initially composed of 
fine and regular crystal cells, transformed into large grains with different orientations. The precipitation phase is 
discontinuous, leading to intergranular fracture. In contrast, the WC/GH3536 composite ’ s microstructure, 
reinforced by the precipitation of additional W-rich carbides, strengthened the existing precipitate network, thus 
preventing intergranular fracture.
1. Introduction
GH3536 alloy is widely used in the manufacture of components for 
aerospace engines, such as combustion chambers and high-pressure 
turbines[ 1 – 4 ]. Therefore, high-temperature mechanical properties are 
crucial for both the alloy and its composites. The static mechanical 
property at high temperature is the most basic index of high temperature 
performance. Laser metal deposition (LMD) is an advanced additive 
manufacturing technology commonly used for the fabrication or repair 
of aerospace engine parts [ 5 ]. Among these, the circular oscillating laser 
has been shown in previous studies to be a highly effective laser heat 
source. By adding two sets of deflection mirrors, it applies a circular 
oscillating trajectory to the Gaussian laser spot, which disperses the 
energy distribution of the Gaussian beam [ 6 ]. Research in welding and 
additive manufacturing has found that this laser provides better material 
forming effects than traditional Gaussian beams, including improved 
isotropy [ 7 ],finer microstructures [ 8 ] and lower residual stresses 
[ 9 – 11 ].
However, previous studies have generally found that GH3536 alloys 
directly deposited by laser experience significant deterioration in quasi- 
static tensile properties at high temperatures (above 500 
◦
C), with a 
marked reduction in material ductility, especially in horizontal samples 
[ 12 – 14 ] 。

2. Experimental
2.1. WC/GH3536 composite manufacturing
In this study, the Gaussian laser that makes up the circular oscillating 
laser is emitted by a continuous fiber laser with a wavelength of 1064 
nm. The circular oscillating laser with a spot diameter of 1 mm, an 
oscillation radius of 0.3, and an oscillation frequency of 90 Hz was used 
for material preparation. GH3536 spherical alloy powder (particle size 
range 53 – 110 μ m) was mixed with 8 % nano-sized WC particles (particle 
size range 100 – 200 nm) by ball milling for 4 h to obtain the composite

WC/GH3536 composite deposition material. Due to the different 
deposition efficiency, the powder transport rate of pure alloy is about 
13.8 g/min, while the powder transport rate of composite is about 6.6 g/ 
min. The deposition thickness of each layer is controlled at about 1 mm,

during which the C element precipitates discontinuously as carbides 
along the recrystallized grain boundaries. This reduces the intergranular 
bonding strength, allowing cracks to propagate along the newly formed 
grain boundaries. In contrast, the microstructure of the WC/GH3536

precipitate phase at the grain boundaries, when the temperature rises, 
additional precipitates form on the original precipitate phase, causing 
the grain structure to evolve from a regular network-like arrangement to 
a more intricate and complex configuration. The newly formed carbides 
reinforce the pre-existing carbide network at the grain boundaries, 
increased the contact area between the carbide phase and grain, 
resulting in a more interlocked arrangement between the grains. When 
cracks initiate, they are no longer able to propagate smoothly along the 
grain boundaries. Since M
6

at elevated temperatures [ 38 ]. So it is speculated that these carbides, 
functioning like a framework, bear a significant portion of the load 
during material deformation. After fracture, their high hardness effec -
tively traps them at the grain boundaries, obstructing grain slip.
