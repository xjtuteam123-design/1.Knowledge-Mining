ABSTRACT 
Hetero-grain/precipitation engineering was adopted to improve mechanical properties of alloy over a wide 
temperature range. Partial recrystallization and L1
2 
nanoprecipitation were simultaneously introduced into a 
CoCrNi-based medium entropy alloy, resulting in a heterogeneous microstructure. This kind of heterostructured 
alloy achieves an ultrahigh tensile strength of 1.5 GPa at 500 
◦
C, and still maintains a tensile strength above 1.1 
GPa at 600 
◦
C. All elongations exceed 20% at 196 
◦
C and elevated temperatures up to 700 
◦
C, which indicates 
the breakthrough in temperature-dependent embrittlement encountered by many structural materials with 
equiaxed grain structures. Such a superior combination of strength and ductility at elevated temperatures can be 
ascribed to the stable deformed grains and pronounced planar defects, including stacking fault networks and 
deformation twins. This work demonstrates that hetero-grain/precipitation engineering can provide an effective 
strategy to achieve high strengths of alloys without sacrificing ductility over an extended temperature range. 
High-performance structural alloys with excellent mechanical 
properties over a wide temperature range are highly sought-after in 
advanced engineering fields. Recently, it has been revealed that multi- 
principal element alloys (MPEAs) including high/medium entropy al -
loys (HEAs/MEAs) have great potential for applications over an 
extended temperature range [1 – 6] . For example, Cantor alloy with a 
single FCC phase exhibits extraordinary temperature dependences on 
both tensile strength and ductility, increasing with the decrease of 
cryogenic temperatures, until over 1 GPa and 60%, respectively, at 
 196 
◦
C, while its strengths are less sensitive to elevated temperatures 
up to 600 
◦
C [1] . Similar to Ni/Co-based superalloys, MPEAs can be 
significantly strengthened by L1
2 
precipitation to perform a high tensile 
strength of 1.3 GPa with an elongation of 43% at ambient temperature, 
and reach an even higher tensile strength, up to 1.7 GPa, with an 
increased elongation up to 51% at 196 
◦
C [2] . However, the complex 
chemical interactions between many constituent elements could facili -
tate the formation of detrimental phases at grain boundaries, causing 
intermediate-temperature embrittlement. For example, the ductility loss 
of the Cantor alloy at elevated temperatures is ascribed to the rapid 
segregation of elements into grain boundaries [7] , which could be 
further explained by kinetic interpretation [8] and the evolution of 
precipitates [9] . Also, most precipitation-hardened MPEAs become 
brittle at intermediate temperatures (400 ~ 700 
◦
C) with elongations 
less than 10%, even though respectable strengths can be maintained [ 4 , 
5 , 10 – 12 ]. Hence, there is plenty of space left for improving MPEAs 
applied towards elevated temperatures. 
Recently, a few studies strategically tuned partial recrystallization in 
combination of conventional strengthening mechanisms as a kind of 
hetero-grain/precipitation engineering to realize ultrahigh strengths but 
decent ductility in MPEAs [13 – 17] . For example, Du et al. designed the

Given the predicament of intermediate-temperature embrittlement, the

favor the plastic deformation [18] , and the large DGs are expected to

Within the RGs, high-density stacking faults (SFs) multiply and intersect 
frequently along {111} slip planes to form nano-spaced SF networks, as 
shown in Fig. 4 (c). Moreover, deformation twins (DTs) that are absent in 
the previous investigation on the heterostructured Al5Ti5-MEA 
deformed at room and cryogenic temperatures [16] were observed 
( Fig. 4 (d)), the formation mechanism of which will be discussed later. 
Within the DGs, planar traces in Fig. 4 (b) can be further known as highly 
dense dislocation walls along {111} planes, as shown in Fig. 4 (e). 
Similarly, the closeup image of a DG ( Fig. 4 (f)) presents the formation of 
nano-spaced SF networks, reinforcing the slip planarity behavior. In 
view of the above, the achievement of the high YS (~1.4 GPa) at 500

C

the plastic deformation, parallel sets of slip traces can serve as effective 
carriers for dislocation accumulation [39] , contributing to the extra 
strain hardening. Besides, high-density planar defects developed in the 
RGs can give rise to strain hardening to a certain degree or delay the 
plastic instability at 500 
◦

the path of crack propagation could be blocked by the large and strong 
DG, and thus follow the RG region, where plastic deformation could be 
assisted by grain boundary sliding due to the small grain size, to hurdle 
the DG [ 19 , 20 ]. Consequently, the existence of these DGs can effectively 
resist the crack propagation.

In summary, we demonstrated the feasibility of developing strong- 
yet-ductile alloys over a wide temperature range via hetero-grain/ 
precipitation engineering. The heterostructured Al5Ti5-MEA exhibits 
an ultrahigh YS of 1.4 GPa at 500 
◦
C, and still maintains the 1 GPa 
strength level at 600 
◦
C. Meanwhile, its elongations all exceed 20% at 
elevated temperatures up to 700 
◦
C without any intergranular embrit -
tlement. Experimental results indicated that the strengthening effects of 
heterogeneous grain structure could be susceptible to and weakened by 
higher testing temperatures. In other words, DGs play a key role in 
avoiding softening during deformation, assisted by the high-density 
planar defects (SFs and DTs) and associated complex interactions be -
tween them (SF networks and SF-DT intersections). The temperature- 
insensitive ductility can be rationalized by crack propagation impeded 
by the DGs, as well as the plastic deformation of the RGs. Therefore, 
stabilizing the DGs in pursuit of high-temperature performance will be 
critical for similar heterostructured materials in future work, which 
could be accomplished by either alloy design or selective heat treat -
ments. Compared to precipitation hardening and solid-solution 
strengthening adopted by conventional superalloys and other MPEAs, 
hetero-grain/precipitation engineering can be a promising route to 
achieve not only outstanding strengths but also a remarkable break -
through in temperature-dependent embrittlement over a wide temper -
ature range.
