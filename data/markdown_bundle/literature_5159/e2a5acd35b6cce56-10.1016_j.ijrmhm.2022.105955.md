ABSTRACT 
Ultra-high temperature Nb alloys are prospective for practical applications as aircraft materials. Sufficient efforts 
must be made to improve their manufacturing technologies and properties. The Nb-Ti-Al alloys with TiC addition 
were fabricated by laser solid forming (LSF) in this work. LSFed Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloys which were composed of 
Nbss and (Nb,Ti)C had excellent formability and metallurgical quality. There were columnar and equiaxed Nbss 
grains in Nb-37Ti-10Al-3TiC and Nb-37Ti-10Al-5TiC while there were entire equiaxed Nbss grains in Nb-37Ti- 
10Al-8TiC. Expectant columnar-to-equiaxed transition was realized by the addition of TiC. Moreover, the 
grain sizes gradually decreased from the addition of 3TiC to 8TiC, and the average grain size of Nb-37Ti-10Al- 
8TiC decreased to 22.08 μ m. The strip-like and particle-like (Nb,Ti)C precipitation coexisted in Nb-37Ti-10Al- 
3TiC and Nb-37Ti-10Al-5TiC, but only particle-like (Nb,Ti)C formed in Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC. The tensile 
strength, compression strength and fracture toughness of all Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloys increased with the increase of 
TiC content. For Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC, the maximum tensile strength of 754.14 ± 3.84 MPa, compression strength 
of 3310.0 ± 55.28 MPa and fracture toughness of 34.96 MPa ⋅ m
1/2 
were obtained. The simultaneous increase of 
strength and fracture toughness was mainly attributed to fine grain strengthening by TiC addition. Compared 
with Nb alloy with the addition of Ta, W, Mo and Hf, the LSFed Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloy had considerably high 
strength and was of low density. This confirmed the feasibility of preparing Nb alloy by LSF technology and 
adjusting the microstructure and property. 
1. Introduction 
At present, there is adequate demand for structural materials that 
can work as well under high temperatures and severe environments. It is 
therefore particularly urgent to develop the fabrication technology for 
refractory metals and their alloys [ 1 – 3 ]. Compared with other refractory 
metals, Nb has higher melting point and is of lower density, and Nb- 
based alloys had been used to fabricate nozzles, thrust augmenter 
flaps and blades in the aerospace industry [ 4 , 5 ]. To improve the 
comprehensive properties of Nb alloys, many alloying elements were 
selected, such as Ti, Al, Ta, W, Mo and Hf, etc. [ 6 – 11 ]. The addition of 
Ta, W, Mo and Hf had the effect of solid solution strengthening for Nb 
alloy, and generally the density of the Nb alloy with the addition of Ta, 
W, Mo and Hf was greater than 8 g ⋅ cm
 3
, which was contrary to the 
lightweight demand in application. Typical Nb-Ti-Al alloy had low 
density and preferable oxidation resistance, while the addition of Ti and 
Al reduced its high-temperature strength, so it had great significance in 
improving the strength of Nb-Ti-Al alloy. Some studies indicated that 
carbides [ 12 – 18 ], nitrides [ 19 – 21 ], and silicides [ 22 – 24 ] had a signifi -
cant strengthening effect on various alloys. Wei et al. prepared multi- 
phase Nb-25Ti-8C-15Al alloy by arc melting, its room temperature

2. Materials and methods 
The chemical composition of Nb-37Ti-10Al-3TiC, Nb-37Ti-10Al- 
5TiC and Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC (at. %) were designed in this work. Unless 
otherwise stated, all elemental compositions referred to atomic per -

were prepared as feedstock in the process of LSF. They were mixed and 
stirred to ensure the uniform distribution. Fig. 1 (a), (b), (c), and (d) 
showed the morphologies of these powders. Spherical Nb powder and Ti 
powder had the size of 90 – 140 μ m and 40 – 100 μ m respectively. The 
irregular Al and TiC powders had the size of 40 – 100 μ m, where the 
equivalent size was adopted for them. The size distribution of LSF 
powder had an important impact on the fluidity in the LSF process. Fig. 1 
(e) showed the sizes distribution of the four powders, similar 
distribution range of Ti powder, Al powder and TiC powder was indi -
cated, and Nb powder had a slightly larger size. 
The LSF process was conducted employing a continuous wave fiber 
laser, and it was carried out in an Ar atmosphere. The optimum LSF 
parameters of Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloys had been obtained in our previous 
research works. The detailed parameters were listed as follows: laser 
power being 1.8 kW, scan speed being 8 mm/s and spot diameter being 
3 mm. Finally, the samples of the three Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloys were printed. 
The schematic diagram of the deposition process and scanning strategy 
were shown in Fig. 2 (a) and (b). The LSFed Nb-Ti-Al-TiC samples were 
displayed in Fig. 2 (c), all of which had flat and glossy surfaces, and no 
cracks and other defects were observed. It is therefore indicated that 
these LSF parameters were reasonable and stable for Nb alloys. Mean -
while, the good formability of LSFed Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloy was validated. 
The longitudinal section of samples with the size of 10 mm (length) 
× 10 mm (width) × 3 mm (thickness) were cut by wire electrical 
discharge machining for microstructural observation, and were taken 
from similar positions for the three alloys. They were first ground and 
polished to mirror surface, and then etched by the etching solution.

The microstructures of the three alloys were shown in Fig. 4 . There 
were columnar grains and equiaxed grains in Nb-37Ti-10Al-3TiC and 
Nb-37Ti-10Al-5TiC. Entire equiaxed grains existed in Nb-37Ti-10Al- 
8TiC alloy. The solidification process of LSF molten pool belonged to 
the category of rapid solidification, and columnar grains were usually 
formed under the control of laser heating and substrate cooling condi -
tions. The columnar structure had anisotropic characteristics, which was 
prone to micropores, cracks and deformation. For some specific parts, 
few columnar crystals and entire equiaxed grains were expected. 
Therefore, how to adjust the alloy composition, process parameters and 
solidification microstructures, and finally obtain metal part with excel -
lent properties was a concrete issue to be solved. In this work, expectant 
columnar-to-equiaxed transition was realized by the addition of TiC. 
Fig. 5 were the images with higher magnification of the three alloys. 
It could be seen that the grain sizes significantly decreased from Nb- 
37Ti-10Al-3TiC to Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC, being 88.69 μ m, 46.44 μ m and 
22.08 μ m respectively. The content of TiC had a definite effect on the 
crystal size, and the grains were very fine in Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC. Based 
on the microstructural observation, the solidification path of the three 
alloys was shown schematically in Fig. 6 (a). In the initial stage of the 
solidification process, primary Nbss formed in the liquid phase, and the 
remaining liquid had more C content than in the solid Nbss phase, which 
promoted the formation of primary (Nb,Ti)C carbide in the final solid -
ification stage. These (Nb,Ti)C carbides were located in the grain 
boundaries of Nbss. When the solidification was completed, (Nb,Ti)C 
precipitated from supersaturated Nbss in the subsequent cooling stage, 
which was related to the reduced solubility of C in Nbss along with the 
decrease of temperature. The strip-like and particle-like (Nb,Ti)C pre -
cipitations formed in Nb-37Ti-10Al-3TiC and Nb-37Ti-10Al-5TiC, and 
there were more particle-like (Nb,Ti)C in Nb-37Ti-10Al-5TiC. Particle- 
like (Nb,Ti)C precipitation only existed in Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC. In addi -
tion, the appearance of (Nb,Ti)C precipitation was consistent with the 
Nbss + (Nb,Ti)C equilibrium as shown in Fig. 6 (b), where there was the 
two-phase equilibrium region of Nbss + (Nb,Ti)C in the metal side of

Ti-Al-TiC alloys. Besides that, the morphology of (Nb,Ti)C also influ -
enced the strength effect. The strip-like (Nb,Ti)C pinned dislocation in 
the direction which was perpendicular to its length direction, while the 
particle-like (Nb,Ti)C could hinder dislocation movement in each di -
rection, which was conducive to obtaining the maximum strength of Nb- 
37Ti-10Al-8TiC. Secondly, the lattice of Nbss had distorted due to the 
alloying effect of Nb and Ti, which would promote solution strength -
ening and improve the strength of the alloy. And thirdly, the essence of 
the strength improvement was fine grain strengthening. The high cool -
ing rate in the LSF process contributed fast solidification rate and fine 
grains. The addition of TiC also contributed to the CET and realized the 
grain refinement [ 25 ]. The grain sizes decreased from 88.69 μ m to 
22.08 μ m with the increase of 3TiC addition to 8TiC addition in Nb-Ti- 
Al-TiC alloys, and the UTS were improved from 711.39 ± 4.36 MPa to 
754.14 ± 3.84 MPa.

as Nb-10Ta-10 W and Nb-15 W-28Hf-5Mo-1Zr-0.1C, etc. The density of 
Nb-37Ti-10Al-3TiC, Nb-37Ti-10Al-5TiC and Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC was 
6.510 g ⋅ cm

, 6.427 g ⋅ cm
 3 
and 6.233 g ⋅ cm

respectively, the density 
gradually decreasing with the increase of TiC content. The density of Nb 
alloy was determined by the addition of alloy elements, and these Nb 
alloys with the addition of Ta, W, Mo and Hf had higher density than the 
Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloys. It should be emphasized that these LSFed Nb-Ti-Al- 
TiC alloys had high strength and also possessed low density. The 
methodology in this work could meet the demand for high strength and 
lightweight of Nb alloy in the aerospace field. Moreover, as it was 
deduced in our previous research work, the LSFed Nb-37Ti-5Al had UTS 
of 613.58 MPa, which was higher than that of as-cast Nb-37Ti-5Al 
(587.44 MPa), because the equiaxed grains of LSFed Nb-37Ti-5Al were 
finer than that of as-cast Nb-37Ti-5Al. It confirmed the feasibility of 
preparing Nb alloy by LSF technology and adjusting its microstructure 
and property.

TiC addition. This was mainly caused by the following reasons. First, 
though TiC was brittle constitute for Nb alloy, the proportion of TiC was 
relatively low to determine the fracture toughness value. Secondly, the 
addition of TiC refined the grains of LSFed Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloys. Grains 
refinement could not only improve the strength, but also enhance the 
fracture toughness, and the impact of grain refinement was more pro -
nounced. Therefore, more TiC addition led to better fracture toughness 
for Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloys. 
Fig. 12 showed the fracture morphology of the three alloys after the 
three-point bending test. These fracture morphologies were basically 
rough and showed ductile fractures, and there were dimples caused by 
the deformation of ductile Nbss and microcracks caused by brittle car -
bide. There were several flat facets in Nb-37Ti-10Al-3TiC. Big dimples, 
small dimples and flat facets indicated its quasi-cleavage fracture mode, 
and this corresponded with its microstructure shown in Fig. 4 (a). 
Microcracks also existed in Fig. 12 (b), which showed that the extension 
of cracks was rapid and the deformation was uneven. In addition, more 
long and round dimples could be observed in the other two alloys, which 
was the critical factor that they had better fracture roughness than Nb- 
37Ti-10Al-3TiC. Long dimples belonged to transgranular dimples, and 
round dimples belonged to intergranular dimples, their formation being 
related to the distribution of constitute phase and stress. The combina -
tion of Nbss, primary (Nb,Ti)C, and (Nb,Ti)C precipitation coordinated 
together to determine the fracture mechanism of the LSFed Nb alloy. 
It could be concluded that the improvement of fracture toughness 
along with more TiC addition was caused by grain refinement. This was 
consistent with Wang ’ s results, grain refinement acquired intrinsic 
toughness [ 46 ]. Fine grains hindered the generation and propagation of 
cracks. The deflection and branching of the cracks also dispersed the 
stress, which completely offset the reduction of toughness caused by the 
addition of brittle TiC. Therefore, the Nb-Ti-Al alloy had better 
comprehensive properties after TiC strengthening. The fracture mode of 
the three alloys was schematically shown in Fig. 13 . The grain size of 
Nbss decreased with the increment of TiC, and there were (Nb,Ti)C 
precipitations with various morphology. The cracks would penetrate 
straight through large brittle (Nb,Ti)C, while the deflection occurred 
along Nbss and small (Nb,Ti)C. For Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC, the cracks 
encountered more deflection and complex paths due to fine Nbss and 
granular (Nb,Ti)C, so it obtained superior fracture toughness.

4. Conclusions 
The Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloys were successfully fabricated by LSF in this 
work, and their microstructures and mechanical properties had been 
investigated. Here were several brief conclusions. 
(1) All Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloys were mainly composed of a large amount 
of Nbss, a small amount of primary (Nb,Ti)C and some (Nb,Ti)C 
precipitation. There were columnar and equiaxed Nbss grains in 
Nb-37Ti-10Al-3TiC and Nb-37Ti-10Al-5TiC. Only equiaxed Nbss 
grains existed in Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC. 
(2) The grain sizes gradually decreased from Nb-37Ti-10Al-3TiC, Nb- 
37Ti-10Al-5TiC to Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC, and they were 88.69 μ m, 
46.44 μ m and 22.08 μ m respectively. Moreover, the strip-like and 
particle-like (Nb,Ti)C precipitation formed in the Nb-37Ti-10Al- 
3TiC and Nb-37Ti-10Al-5TiC. Only particle-like (Nb,Ti)C exis -
ted in Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC. 
(3) The tensile strength, compression strength and fracture tough -
ness increased significantly with the increase of TiC addition for 
LSFed Nb-Ti-Al-TiC alloys, which was mainly attributed to fine 
grain strengthening due to TiC addition. The maximum tensile 
strength of 754.14 ± 3.84 MPa, the compression strength of 
3310.0 ± 55.28 MPa and fracture toughness of 34.96 MPa ⋅ m
1/2 
were obtained for Nb-37Ti-10Al-8TiC. For these LSFed Nb alloys, 
the improvement of the toughness by fine grain strengthening 
compromised the effect of TiC addition. Compared with Nb alloy 
with the addition of Ta, W, Mo and Hf, the LSFed Nb-Ti-Al-TiC 
alloy had high strength and also possessed low density (~6.5 
g ⋅ cm
 3
).
