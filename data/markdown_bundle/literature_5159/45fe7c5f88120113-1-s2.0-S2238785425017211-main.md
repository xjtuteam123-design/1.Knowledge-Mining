1. Introduction
Sputtering target materials are widely used in sectors such as infor -
mation storage, integrated circuits, display technologies, and automo -
tive rearview mirrors, primarily for magnetron sputtering of various thin 
film materials. As society progresses and the semiconductor industry 
evolves, there is a growing demand for nickel-vanadium target materials 
in electronics, information technology, integrated circuits, and display 
systems. Nickel-vanadium sputtering targets are produced by incorpo -
rating vanadium into molten nickel during the alloy fabrication process. 
This improves the alloy ’ s suitability for magnetron sputtering and 
combines the advantageous properties of both nickel and vanadium. The 
most commonly used composition of nickel-vanadium alloy is NiV7, 
which consists of 7 wt% V added to Ni to mitigate its ferromagnetic 
properties, thus facilitating the sputtering process. Furthermore, the 
NiV7 alloy is recognized as one of the most widely utilized diffusion 
barrier materials [ 1 ].
The ongoing advancements in coating technology have increased the 
demand for target materials, which are essential consumables in the 
magnetron sputtering process, especially in terms of utilization effi -
ciency, cost-effectiveness, and film quality. The microstructural char -
acteristics and properties of these target materials significantly influence 
the performance of the resulting films. Among the various methods of 
plastic processing, rolling deformation is particularly practical. Severe 
Plastic Deformation (SPD) represents a technological process aimed at 
refining the grain size of metallic materials, thereby enabling the pro -
duction of ultrafine or nanocrystalline structures. The common SPD 
techniques encompass Equal Channel Angular Pressing (ECAP) [ 2 , 3 ], 
Twist Extrusion (TE) [ 4 – 6 ], Equal Channel Angular Pressing and Torsion 
(ECAPT) [ 7 , 8 ], and High Pressing Torsion (HPT) [ 9 , 10 ]. In contrast, 
rolling deformation is viewed as more practical, with experimental 
procedures being comparatively straightforward.
However, plastic processing by rolling deformation often results in a 
high dislocation density and significant stress concentration after sub -
stantial deformation. To relieve these internal stresses, annealing ex -
periments were carried on the rolled plates, a practice that has been

Although commercial nickel-vanadium sputtering targets have been 
introduced into industrial applications, research on the control of grain 
size and orientation in these materials remains limited. At present, 
studies focusing on the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
NiV7 alloys after cold rolling and annealing are still relatively scarce. 
Controlling the grain size and microstructural features of NiV7 alloys 
through cold rolling and annealing treatments provides valuable guid -
ance for the development of high-quality nickel-vanadium sputtering 
targets. Therefore, this research is of great significance for optimizing 
the performance of NiV7 alloys in various high-end applications, 
particularly in the electronics and semiconductor industries, where 
advanced thin film materials and diffusion barriers are increasingly in 
demand.

3. Results and discussions
3.1. Microstructure evolution
Fig. 2 presents OM images depicting the microstructure of the NiV7 
alloy subjected to various annealing temperatures. At an annealing 
temperature of 500 
◦
C, recrystallization has not started yet. The defor -
mation bands and fibrous structure characteristic of the cold-rolled 
sample remained evident, with no discernible grain boundaries [ 17 ]. 
At 550 
◦
C, recrystallization initiated, resulting in a transition from a 
fibrous structure to equiaxed grains. At 600 
◦
C, which exceeds the 
recrystallization initiation temperature, grains underwent recrystalli -
zation. Incomplete recrystallization results in variations in grain size and 
the formation of annealing twins. At 700 
◦
C, grain growth continued and 
the recrystallization rate accelerates, increasing the population of 
recrystallized grains and annealing twins. As the annealing temperature 
rises to 800 
◦
C, the average grain size increased, while size heteroge -
neity decreased. At 900 
◦
C, the NiV7 alloy achieved complete recrys -
tallization, Grain size reached its maximum and the number of grain 
boundaries decreasing.
Fig. 3 presents the XRD patterns of the NiV7 alloy annealed at 
different temperatures. Analysis reveals that the (111) peak intensity 
increases initially then decreases with rising annealing temperature, 
whereas the intensity of the (200) peak consistently decreases. A com -
parison with the diffraction peaks of cold-rolled samples indicates that 
the diffraction peaks become narrower; however, there is no significant 
shift in the diffraction angles. This phenomenon can be attributed to the 
substantial plastic deformation experienced by the alloy, which gener -
ates a considerable amount of distortion energy within the grains. 
Furthermore, the relaxation of residual stress following the annealing 
process may result in modifications to the material ’ s texture, subse -
quently affecting grain orientation and the primary diffraction peak [ 18 ,
19 ]. During the annealing process, stored energy release drives recrys -
tallization nucleation and grain growth. The reduction in dislocation 
density accounts for the observed peak narrowing.

3.2. Texture evolution
Fig. 6 illustrates the distribution and statistical content of textures at 
different annealing temperatures. The data show that as the annealing 
temperature increased, the prevalence of cubic texture progressively 
decreased; however, it still constituted a significant proportion of the 
overall texture. Notably, the content of copper-type texture markedly 
increased at an annealing temperature of 800 
◦
C, but then declined as 
the temperature continued to rise. Analysis of the texture distribution 
map revealed that copper-type texture was predominantly located in 
regions with fine grains. At an annealing temperature of 600 
◦
C, the 
quantity of < 111 > //RD direction fiber texture reaches its peak, and this 
texture is frequently associated with areas containing annealing twins, 
which corroborated the observation of a large number of annealing 
twins during grain boundary analysis. As the annealing temperature 
escalates, the quantity of < 111 > //RD texture gradually decreased, 
while the content of < 110 > //RD texture exhibits an initially increased 
and then decreased as the temperature rose. Similarly, the content of 
< 100 > //RD texture increased initially and then decreased with further 
increases in annealing temperature. The ongoing variations in texture 
types are closely related to static recovery, static recrystallization, and 
grain growth that transpire during the annealing process. These phe -
nomena promote the relief of dislocation entanglement within the 
deformed grains[ 23 – 25 ]. This indicates that dislocation slip within 
grains generally takes place along more advantageous slip planes, 
resulting in ongoing crystallographic transformations, specifically al -
terations in texture type [ 24 , 26 , 27 ].
3.3. Mechanical properties
Fig. 7 illustrates the mechanical properties of the NiV7 alloy after 
annealing. The data presented in the figure indicate that as the 
annealing temperature increased, ultimate tensile strength significantly 
decreased; however, the strength remained higher than that of samples 
subjected to homogenization treatment [ 17 ]. The peak tensile strength 
was observed at an annealing temperature of 600 
◦
C, reaching 744 MPa, 
although this was accompanied by the lowest recorded elongation of 
29.1 %. Throughout the heat treatment, the material ’ s microstructure 
experiences several transformations, including grain growth, stress re -
lief, recrystallization, and dislocation rearrangement. Despite these 
changes, the annealed samples still retained some dislocations, as well as 
dislocation bands. Although annealing results in an increase in grain 
size, the final grain dimensions remain relatively moderate. Therefore, 
during plastic deformation, dislocation multiplication is promoted, 
while annealing facilitates dislocation slip, alleviates stress concentra -
tion, reduces crack initiation, and ultimately prolongs the time to fail -
ure. In comparison to the mechanical properties reported in other 
research, the NiV7 alloy exhibited improved mechanical performance 
after extensive cold rolling and subsequent annealing[ 28 – 35 ], as shown 
in Fig. 7 (b).
Fig. 7 (c) presents the fracture morphology at different annealing 
temperatures, revealing that the fracture mode is predominantly ductile. 
During the tensile testing, stress concentration developed rapidly. if 
dislocations multiplied rapidly without sufficient time for slip, the ma -
terial fractured at low strains. Annealing at 600 
◦
C induces grain growth 
and recrystallization, reducing internal stress and dislocation density.
