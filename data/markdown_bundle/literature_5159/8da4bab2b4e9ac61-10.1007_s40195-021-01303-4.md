Abstract
In the present study, a series of AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 (x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0) eutectic high entropy alloys (EHEAs) have been 
designed and prepared. And the effect of Cr content on the microstructures and mechanical properties of the AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 
alloys was systematically investigated. The results indicate that the AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 (x > 0) alloys exhibit almost complete 
lamellar eutectic microstructures with a mixture structure of FCC and B2 phases. And the AlCoFeNi2.1 alloy without Cr 
element exhibited a hypoeutectic microstructure with a primary B2 phase. In addition, the eutectic microstructures for 
 AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 eutectic alloys do not change significantly. The room temperature compressive tests results show that with 
an increase in Cr content (from x = 0 to x = 1.0), the yield strength will first decrease, and thereafter increase. The trend 
is the opposite with the fracture strength and plastic strain. They show an increase trend at first, and then decrease. The 
 AlCoCr0.5FeNi2.1 (Cr0.5) alloy shows the best comprehensive mechanical properties. The tensile yield strength, fracture 
strength, and elongation are 536.5 MPa, 1062 MPa, and 13.8%, respectively. Furthermore, the Cr0.5 alloy also displays a 
high strength with a yield strength of 362 MPa at 700 ºC. In summary, by changing the Cr content, AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 eutectic 
high entropy alloys with excellent comprehensive mechanical properties were obtained and prepared.
Keywords Eutectic · High entropy alloy · Compressive properties · Tensile strength
1 Introduction
Recently, eutectic high entropy alloys (EHEAs) [1 ] have 
become a new research hotspot due to their unique design 
concept, which is combined the concepts of high entropy 
alloy (HEA) and eutectic alloy. Compared to the other 
HEAs, the EHEAs show many obvious advantages, such 
as excellent castability, controllable microstructures, ther -
mal stabilities, mechanical properties, and wide engineer -
ing applications [2 –10]. For instance, the formed eutectic 
microstructure of the CoCrFeNiNb 0.45 EHEA is thermo-
dynamically stable at temperatures as high as their reac-
tion temperatures [7]. Lu et al. designed the AlCoCrFeNi2.1 
EHEA with attractive castability and mechanical properties 
over a wide temperature range [5]. Wang et al. investigated 
that the AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA produced by the directionally 
solidified (DS) method exhibited excellent microstructural 
stability at a low withdrawal rate [9]. How to further improve 
the comprehensive mechanical properties of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 
EHEA and realize the rational allocation of strength, plastic-
ity, and toughness has become a key problem to promote the 
industrial application of EHEAs.
Scientific workers have developed various strategies to 
achieve rational configuration of high strength and larger 
plasticity in AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 EHEA, such as annealing, 
cold-rolled, and preparation method [11 –16]. Wani et al. 
[11] investigated that the AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA after 90% 
cold-rolled and annealing at 800 °C showed remarkable 
high ultimate tensile strength of 1.2 GPa and elongation 
to failure of 12%. Wang et al. [13] proposed the strength 
and ductility of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA via friction stir pro-
cessing were simultaneously enhanced. Reddy et al. [17]

2 Experimental
AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 (x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, and 1.0) alloy ingots 
(about 35 g) were prepared by using a vacuum arc melt-
ing method under argon atmosphere. The AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 
alloys with x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, and 1.0 are denoted as 
Cr0, Cr0.25, Cr0.5, Cr0.75, and Cr1.0, respectively. The 
purity of the Al, Co, Cr, Fe, and Ni metals was higher 
than 99.95%. A bulk AlCoCr0.5FeNi2.1 alloy ingot (about 
2.5 kg) was also synthesized by using a medium frequency 
induction melting furnace. The characterization of the 
crystal structures of the AlCoCr xFeNi2.1 alloys was car -
ried out by using an X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Bruker 
Corporation, Ettlingen, Germany), with Cu K α radiation 
at voltage of 40 kV, and by using a transmission electron 
microscope (TEM, JEOL-2100, JEOL, Japan). The scans 
ranged from 20° to 100°, with a scanning speed of 0.22°/s. 
The microstructures and fracture morphologies were char -
acterized by using a scanning electron microscope (SEM, 
Zeiss merlin compact, Opton Nts Limited, England) and 
a transmission electron microscope (TEM). The thermal 
properties of the AlCoCr0.5FeNi2.1 alloy were measured by 
using a differential scanning calorimeter (DSC) at a heat-
ing/cooling rate of 10 K/min. The room temperature and 
high temperature compressive properties were studied by 
using an 810 Material Test System (MTS), and Gleeble ther-
mal simulation machine, respectively. The strain rate was 
 10–3  s−1, and cylindrical samples of φ5 mm × 10 mm and φ8 
mm × 12 mm, respectively, were used in these studies. The 
tensile performances were also measured by using an 810 
MTS with a diameter of 8 mm and a gauge length of 40 mm 
at strain rate of 10–3  s−1.
3 Results
3.1 Cr‑Induced Microstructure Evolution
The low-magnification SEM images of the AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 
(x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0) alloys are shown in Fig.  1. As 
can be seen in Fig. 1, when decreasing the Cr content from 
x = 1.0 to x = 0.25, the alloys will still show almost complete 
lamellar eutectic morphologies. This indicates that Cr has 
a small influence on the formation of the eutectic structure 
in the AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 alloy system. When further decreas-
ing the Cr content to x = 0, the alloy will be composed of 
a primary phase and a lamellar eutectic structure. Hence, 
when removing Cr from AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 alloys, the alloy 
composition was observed to deviate from the eutectic point, 
leading to the appearance of the primary phase.
To observe the changes of the microstructure in the 
 AlCoCrxFeNi2.1(x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0) alloy system, 
high-magnification SEM images were carefully analyzed 
(see Fig.  2). It was found that the AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 alloy is 
composed of eutectic cells, which are composed of gray 
(denoted as A) and black (denoted as B) two-phase lamellar 
structures. For each cell, there is a regular flat eutectic struc-
ture in the center of the cell, and a coarser eutectic lamella in 
the cell boundary, which is similar to that of the traditional 
NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy [21]. With the decrease in Cr content, 
these alloys are still composed of gray and black two-phase 
lamellar structures. This indicates that a decrease in Cr con-
tent cannot change the morphology of eutectic structure.
The measured compositions of the different phases can 
be observed in Table  1. The Ni element is uniformly dis-
tributed in the Cr0 alloy. In addition, the A phase is rich in 
Co and Fe, and the B phase, as well as the primary α phase 
are rich in Al element. The composition of the primary α 
phase is very close to that of the B phase. They also show 
the same color (as shown in Fig.  1a), which indicates that 
the primary α phase has the same structure as the eutectic B 
phase. Moreover, the element distribution in the Cr0.25 alloy 
is similar with the Cr0 alloy. For AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 (x > 0.25) 
alloys, the A phase is rich in Co, Fe, and Cr elements, while 
the B phase is rich in Al and Ni elements. These results

The specific mechanical properties are listed in Table 2. As 
can be seen in Fig.  1a, a small amount of the hard primary 
B2 phase has appeared in the Cr0 alloy, which is responsi-
ble for the high yield strength of 768 MPa. When adding 
Cr to AlCoCr0FeNi2.1 alloy, the resulting alloys were found 
to consist of almost complete eutectic structures. As the Cr 
content increased to x = 0.5, the yield strength decreased to 
617 MPa, and the compressive plasticity enhanced. For this 
 AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 alloy system, the deformation under room 
temperature compression was generated by a plastic defor -
mation of the FCC phase. As can be understood from the 
EDS results, the main effect by the Cr solid solution in the 
FCC phase is to soften this specific phase. A possible reason 
to the Cr-induced decrease in strength is that the addition of 
Cr will increase the stacking fault energy of the FCC phase 
in the AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 alloys and reduce the separation of the 
Shockley partial dislocation. This will induce the FCC phase 
easier to cross slip, which in turn will reduce the lengths of 
the dislocation pileups [22]. McKamey et al. reported that 
when adding 2%-6% of Cr to Fe-28%Al alloy, it will result 
in a slight decrease in yield strength [23, 24]. Moreover, 
with the increase in Cr content, the plasticity in the alloys 
will increase. This may be attributed to that during the load-
ing process of the FCC + B2 dual-phase eutectic alloys, the 
dislocations stacking in the FCC phase, cracks appear at the 
FCC and B2 interface and finally the alloy breaks [25]. And, 
the addition of Cr will reduce the lengths of the dislocation 
stacking, and delay the crack formations until large strains 
will be formed.
The Cr0.25 and Cr0.5 alloys exhibit excellent com-
pressive mechanical properties. The yield strengths, frac-
ture strengths, and compression ratios of the Cr0.25 and 
Cr0.5 alloys are as high as 617 vs. 617 MPa, 3161 MPa 
vs. 3167 MPa, and 47.5 vs. 47.6%, respectively. As further 
increasing the Cr content, the yield strengths of the alloys 
will slightly increase. This may be due to the solid solution 
strengthening induced by the Cr element. With the increase 
of the Cr content, the lattice parameter of the FCC phase 
increased. For the Cr0.25, Cr0.5, Cr0.75 Cr1.0 alloy, the 
calculated lattice constants of the (111) peak of the FCC 
phase were 3.6010, 3.6016, 3.6037, 3.6115 Å, respectively. 
This indicated that the increased Cr content increases the 
lattice distortion of the FCC phase leading to the increase 
of yield strength. However, the plasticity of the Cr0.75 
and Cr1.0 alloys will slightly decrease. The trend in varia-
tions of yield strength and plasticity, of the AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 
EHEAs, is consistent with those of the FCC + B2 dual-phase 
 Fe30Ni20Mn35Al15 alloy [22]. For the Fe30Ni20Mn35Al15 
alloy, as the Cr content is less than 6 at.%, the yield strength 
will decrease, and the plasticity will increase. On the con-
trary, when the Cr content is as large as 8 at.%, the yield 
strength will increases, and the plasticity will decrease. In 
addition, the compressive fracture strength increases from 
x = 0 to x = 0.75, and then decreases to x = 1.0. The enhanced 
strength is most probably the result of the enhanced Cr-
induced atomic bonding in the EHEAs, which in turn leads 
to increase cohesive strength in the FCC/B2 phase bounda-
ries, and a solid solution strengthening [23, 26, 27]. Thus, 
by changing the Cr content in AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 alloys, not 
only the complete eutectic microstructures will be obtained, 
but also the excellent comprehensive mechanical properties 
will be maintained.

higher temperatures (T ≥ 800 ºC), which leads to a high duc-

3.2.3 Tensile Mechanical Properties at Room Temperature
As is shown in Sect. 3.2.1, the Cr0.5 alloy has the best com-
pressive properties, and the microstructure of the Cr0.5 alloy 
has a more uniform and regular eutectic morphology. To 
further verify the attractive combinatorial properties, like 
strength and ductility, the tensile stress–strain curve for 
the Cr0.5 EHEA was also measured at room temperature 
and shown in Fig. 7. It was found that the Cr0.5 EHEA has 
excellent tensile mechanical properties, engineering yield 
strength, fracture strength, and elongation were 536.5 MPa, 
1062 MPa, and 13.8%, respectively. These excellent mechan-
ical properties are close to, or beyond, some reported values 
for the FCC + B2-dual-phase EHEAs [6, 10, 19, 30], which 
is mainly due to the solid solution strengthening in addition 
to the FCC/BCC regular eutectic composite strengthening 
effect.
In order to study the fracture mechanism of the Cr0.5 
EHEA, the tensile fracture morphology was carefully ana-
lyzed (see Fig. 8). It was found that the surface of this frac-
ture is mainly composed of high density round dimples and 
elongated dimples. This indicates that the tensile fracture 
mechanism of the Cr0.5 alloy is in the ductile dimple-type 
rupture mode. To further characterize the fracture mecha-
nism of the Cr0.5 alloy, the microstructure of the Cr0.5 alloy 
after tensile fracture was studied by using TEM, and the 
results are shown in Fig. 9. The selected area diffraction pat-
terns (SADPs) are shown in Fig. 9b and c. The SADPs show 
FCC and BCC patterns with clear super-lattice reflections 
(enclosed circles), thus indicating the presence of ordered 
 L12 and B2 phases. This consistent with previous investi-
gations, where the L12 phase was depleted in Al, and Ni 
elements, and the B2 phase was enriched in Ni, and Al ele-
ments. As can be seen in Fig.  9d and e, there are two kinds 
of dislocations in the FCC phase. One consisted of regular 
parallel stacking faults, and the other is shown as a messy 
dislocation morphology. Because of the low stacking fault 
energy of the FCC phase, the complete dislocation can be 
decomposed into two incomplete dislocations (in the form 
of stacking fault dislocations). Stacking fault is very impor-
tant crystal defect, which affects the mechanical properties 
of materials at room temperature. The existence of stacking 
faults is similar to the grain size refinement, and the stack -
ing fault spacing can be regarded as the average free path 
of the dislocation movement. So, the existence of stacking 
faults can improve the strength of the alloy (according to 
the Hall–Petch relationship). This may also be an important

4 Conclusions
The microstructure and mechanical properties of the 
 AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 eutectic high entropy alloys have been 
examined in the present study. The Cr-induced alloying 
effects on the crystal phase, microstructure, and mechanical 
properties (at both room temperature and high temperatures) 
were characterized and discussed. Based on the results from 
these characterizations, the following summary can be made.
1. The Cr-free AlCoFeNi2.1 alloy exhibited a hypoeutectic, 
which was composed of a primary phase and lamellar 
eutectic structure. The Cr-containing AlCoCrxFeNi2.1 
(x = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, and 1.0) alloys showed almost com-
plete lamellar eutectic morphologies. Moreover, all 
these alloys consisted of FCC + B2 dual phases.
2. From the room temperature compressive tests, the 
yield strength decreases first and then increases with 
the increase of Cr content (from x = 0 to x = 1.0). On 
the contrary, the fracture strengths and plastic strains 
increase first and then decrease. Wherein, the Cr0.25 
and Cr0.5 alloys exhibited excellent compressive 
mechanical properties, the yield strengths, fracture 
strengths, and plastic strains are as high as 617 MPa vs. 
617 MPa, 3161 MPa vs. 3167 MPa, and 47.5 vs. 47.6%, 
respectively. The analyses also showed that the solution 
strengthening of the primary structure and the eutectic 
composite strengthening of the eutectic structure play 
an important role in strengthening alloys.
3. The Cr0.5 EHEA exhibited excellent tensile mechanical 
properties, with a yield strength of 536.5 MPa, a fracture 
strength of 1062 MPa, and an elongation of 13.8%. The 
low stacking fault energy of the FCC phase in the Cr0.5 
EHEA may induce the formation of stacking faults when 
taking its high ductility and strength into account.
