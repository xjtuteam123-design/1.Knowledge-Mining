ABSTRACT
The present study investigated the influences of Dy-addition on the microstructural character-
ization, functional behaviour and deformation mechanisms in high-temperature Ni
54Mn25Ga21
alloys. The results indicated that adequate Dy-addition contributed to a desirable trade-off
among compressive strength, ductility and functional performance. Ni
54Mn25Ga20.9Dy0.1 alloy
showed the highest shape memory recoverable strain due to the improvement of mechanical
properties and formation of small Dy-rich precipitates. Meanwhile, an obvious pseudoelasticity
was observed over a wide temperature range with a maximum recoverable strain of 3.74% at
320°C in this alloy. All the Dy-containing alloys exhibited low thermal hysteresis. However, it was
revealed that an abnormal relationship between thermal hysteresis and middle eigenvalue λ
2
existed for the investigated alloys with a lattice transition from cubic to tetragonal.

Introduction
Shape memory alloys (SMAs), as promising multi-
functional materials are capable of recovering large
pre-deformed shape triggered by temperature (shape
memory effect, SME) or mechanical stress (pseudoe-
lasticity, PE) via diffusionless austenite to martensite
transformation. These functional alloys have attracted
tremendous interest for technological applications in
transducers and actuators, aerospace engines, solid-
state refrigeration, etc. [1,2]. For the past several
decades, the near equiatomic NiTi alloys have been
exploited as the most commercially successful SMA
systems owing to their enormous recoverable strains,
acceptablecorrosionresistanceandexcellentmalleabil-
ity. However, their practical applications are still quite
limited due to the low martensitic start tempera-
ture (<100°C). Accordingly, several ternary NiTiX
(X = P d,Hf,Zr)alloysystemswithraisedphasetrans-
formation temperatures have been developed [3,4].
Unfortunately, they received negligible commercial
interest due to the high cost of additiveX elements.
Besides, other cost-effective alternatives such as ZrCu-
and NiAl-based alloys still show many drawbacks
includingpoorworkability,largethermalhysteresisand
relativelylowactuationstrains[ 5,6].
