ABSTRACT
Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo refractory multi-principal element alloys typically exhibit high yield strength while tensile 
ductility tends to be poor. In this study, we found that even after solid solution treatment of Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo 
alloys, significant change in ductility occur due to the local chemical fluctuations. Ta ’ s dramatic chemical 
fluctuations cause variations in atomic-scale strain fields, leading to reduced grain boundary strength and brittle 
fracture. First-principles calculations show that (Ti, Zr)-rich at the grain boundaries increases the delocalization 
of valence electrons, leading to longer bond lengths and reduced crystal orbital bond index values, thereby 
causing grain boundary embrittlement. Our findings explore the root causes of brittleness in Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo 
alloys at the atomic and electronic scales, providing not only a method to analyze grain boundary embrittle -
ment using bond length, electronic localization function and crystal orbital bond index, but also a theoretical 
guidance for improving the mechanical properties via grain boundary structure optimization.
Due to their exceptional mechanical properties and distinctive 
structural stability, the multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) [ 1 ] have 
garnered significant interest [ 2-5 ]. Particularly, refractory 
multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs) composed of refractory ele -
ments (e.g., Ti, Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta, Mo) have great potential as 
high-temperature materials [ 3 , 6 , 7 ] and orthopedic implants [ 8-11 ]. 
While a series of high-strength BCC RMPEAs have been reported, most of 
them exhibited low tensile ductility and brittle fractures [ 9 , 12-15 ]. To 
address the inherent trade-off between the strength and ductility char -
acteristics of RMPEAs, extensive research efforts have been undertaken 
by scholars. The ductility of BCC alloys can be modulated by valence 
electron concentration (VEC), enthalpy of mixing and atomic mismatch 
( δ ), and the alloys tend to be ductile when the VEC ≤ 4.5 [ 13 , 16-20 ]. 
Nevertheless, most studies have been analyzed under the assumption of 
an ideal scenario where each site in the lattice is occupied by a random 
major element as it progresses, disregarding the fact that there are 
inhomogeneities in the distribution of elements at the atomic scale in 
reality.
The interaction of constituent elements in RMPEAs results in com -
plex enthalpy dynamics that influence the atomic bonding preference or 
avoidance, leading to diverse local chemical fluctuations (LCFs) [ 21 , 22 ] 
levels. Also, different heat treatment processes can affect the formation 
of LCFs [ 23 ]. In recent studies, both experiments and simulations have 
investigated the impact of LCFs on the mechanical characteristics of 
RMPEAs. This influence manifests across different scales, encompassing 
atomic-level lattice distortion, dislocation movement at the micrometer 
scale, strain hardening at the macroscale, and the overall mechanical 
response [ 24-31 ]. Many studies have shown that the strength and 
ductility of RMPEAs can be effectively improved by modulating LCFs 
[ 24 , 25 , 32-34 ]. The prevailing view holds that LCFs are beneficial for the 
RMPEAs. However, analysis of three Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo alloys with VEC <
4.5 in the as-cast state showed that LCFs affect the local stress

Electronic localized function (ELF) is commonly used to describe the 
chemical bond characteristics by analyzing the bonding features of 
specified electrons [ 52 ]. ELF value ranges from 0 to 1, with higher 
values indicating stronger electron localization, tending to be localized 
in a specific region, and lower values indicating weaker localization, 
allowing for free movement within a vast "electron sea" [ 53-55 ]. The 
three-dimensional isosurfaces of ELF = 0.7 for the two models are shown 
in Fig. 4 (a). The GB-RD model exhibits a greater dispersion of electron 
localization across the supercell, while it is concentrated at GB in the 
GB-LCF model. The atomic pairs that appeared most frequently at the GB 
region of two models were selected, both the ELF values of the first 
nearest neighbor (1NN) and the second nearest neighbor (2NN) atomic 
pairs were analyzed ( Fig. 4 (b and c)). The ELF values between the 1NN 
pairs of atoms are all < 0.2, and the electrons tend to be highly 
out-of-domain. However, the ELF values of the 2NN atomic pairs for the 
GB-RD and GB-LCF are above 0.6, especially for GB-LCF, which show a 
certain degree of electron localization and low kinetic energy density of 
valence electrons. In alloy materials, the higher the degree of electronic 
localization between atomic pairs, the poorer the atomic pair bonding, 
leading to susceptibility to deformation [ 56 ].

Atomic pairs of the type (Nb, Ta, Mo) - (Ti, Zr, Nb, Ta, Mo) tend to have 
higher COBI values and shorter bond lengths. The sum of bond orders 
(SBOs) helps us to understand the relationship between bond lengths, 
bond energies and bond strengths [ 64 ]. As shown in Fig. S4, the larger 
the bond order, the more stable the bonding, so we can find that Ti or Zr 
is poorly bonded to the other elements. Consequently, the enrichment of 
Ti and Zr at GBs leads to longer bond lengths and reduced COBI values of 
interfacial atom pairs, which constitutes a significant factor in the poor 
bonding strength of the GB-LCF model.
The results of microscopic and nanoscopic observations clearly 
indicate the presence of LCFs in N10T15-ST, and the results of the first- 
principles calculations offer a rational explanation for the observed 
embrittlement phenomenon at the GBs. The enrichment of Ti and Zr at 
the GBs results in lower kinetic energy densities of the valence electrons 
and weaker electronic interactions in the neighboring atoms along the 
GBs, leading to weaker bonding and poorer cohesion at the GBs (sup -
ported by ICOBI).
In summary, the ductility and GB strength of Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo 
RMPEAs after solid solution treatment is influenced by the LCFs. Our 
analysis from the atomic, electronic, bond length, and COBI perspectives 
is not only important for understanding the root causes of embrittlement 
in Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo RMPEAs, but also provides theoretical guidance for 
improving the mechanical properties of this type of alloy.
