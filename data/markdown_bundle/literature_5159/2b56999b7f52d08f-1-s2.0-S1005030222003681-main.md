The biocompatible metallic implants with strong osteointegration often lack the ability of anti-infection. 
The biocompatible niobium (Nb) containing the antibacterial copper (Cu), the obtained Nb-Cu alloy, could 
be a potential candidate to solve this issue. To test this hypothesis, ultraﬁne-grained Nb-Cu immiscible 
alloys were fabricated via mechanical alloying and spark plasma sintering. The aim of this study was 
to investigate the microstructure, mechanical properties, magnetic susceptibility, corrosion behavior, ion 
release, and the bactericidal activity, biocompatibility and osteogenic potential of the Nb-Cu alloys in 
vitro and their osteogenesis and osteointegration ability in vivo with a comparison with pure Nb. The 
rat cranial defect model and the bone screws insertion in rabbit femoral bone were used to evaluate 
the osteogenesis and osteointegration ability, respectively. The results showed that after the addition of 
3 wt.% of Cu, the compressive strength was signiﬁcantly improved from 1.57 GPa to 2.21 GPa and the 
magnetic susceptibility slightly decreased. The Nb-3 wt.% Cu (Nb-3Cu) alloy exhibited higher corrosion 
resistance than pure Nb in Hank’s solution and strong bactericidal activity against both E. coli and S. 
aureus. In vitro , the Nb-3Cu alloy showed comparable biocompatibility with pure Nb. The addition of 
3 wt.% Cu also signiﬁcantly enhanced the expression of osteogenesis-related genes (RUNX2, ALP, COLA1 
and OCN) of pre-osteoblasts. In vivo , the Nb-3Cu alloy promoted bone regeneration at the defect sites 
and showed enhanced osteointegration after 12 weeks of implantation. Such a good combination of high 
mechanical strength and corrosion resistance, strong antibacterial activity and improved osteogenesis and 
osseointegration ability enables the present Nb-3Cu alloy a promising candidate for heavy load-bearing 
hard tissue repair.

1. Introduction 
Driven by the aging of the world’s population and the fre- 
quent occurrence of accidental injury and bone diseases, the use of 
metallic implants in orthopedics, dentistry and other load-bearing 
applications has grown dramatically [1–3] . However, the traditional 
metallic implants, like stainless steel and cobalt-based alloys, may 
cause chronic inﬂammatory and hypersensitivity reactions [4–8] . 
Moreover, a typical problem of these orthopedic implants is that 
they show weak osteointegration ability, which would lead to pros- 
thesis loosening and eventually implant failure [9–11] . In addition, 
post-operative infection remains the most concerning and costly

complication following orthopedic ﬁxation procedures. The lack of 
antibacterial properties severely limits the application of metallic 
implants for orthopedic reduction and ﬁxation [ 12 , 13 ]. Hence, an 
ideal load-bearing implant for hard tissue repair should not only 
have a good combination of adequate mechanical properties, high 
corrosion stability, long-lasting antimicrobial activity and good bio- 
compatibility, but also should induce osteogenesis and enhance os- 
teointegration [ 9 , 14 , 15 ]. 
Refractory metal niobium (Nb) has demonstrated high corro- 
sion resistance [16] and excellent biocompatibility [ 17 , 18 ]. In vitro , 
aqueous NaOH-treated Nb could induce bone-like apatite forma- 
tion in simulated body ﬂuid [19] . In vivo , Nb could induce os- 
teogenesis and promote osteointegration [ 20 , 21 ], and showed a 
more "positive biocompatibility" in rabbit bone compared with 
the commercially pure Ti [22] . In addition, Nb was desirable for

avoiding the stress shielding effect owing to its low elastic mod-

repair fails to prevent implant-associated infection. 
Copper (Cu) has been registered by the U.S. Environmental 
Protection Agency as the ﬁrst solid antimicrobial material and is 
known for its high broad-spectrum antimicrobial eﬃcacy and low 
probability for the development of resistant strains [ 26 , 27 ]. Both in 
vitro and in vivo studies demonstrated that Cu had strong antibac- 
terial and antifungal effects [ 24 , 28–31 ]. For example, Cu-containing 
317 L stainless steel showed strong antimicrobial activity against 
Staphylococcus aureus ( S. aureus ) and Escherichia coli ( E. coli ) [32] . 
Cu-bearing Ti alloy showed high antibacterial ability against oral 
speciﬁc bacterial species Streptococcus mutans and Porphyromonas 
gingivalis [29] . Biodegradable Mg-Cu alloy implants showed strong 
antimicrobial properties against Escherichia coli, Staphylococcus epi- 
dermidis and methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) [24] . 
Ta-Cu alloy showed enhanced antibacterial activities against Gram- 
negative bacteria [31] . Furthermore, an appropriate concentration 
of Cu ion could stimulate bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells 
(BMSCs) differentiation, promote osteoblast proliferation and col- 
lagen deposition by mimicking the hypoxia-environment during 
bone metabolism [ 33 , 34 ]. Several studies have also reported that 
Cu-containing biomaterials could signiﬁcantly promote bone for- 
mation in vitro and in vivo [35–40] , such as Cu-containing meso- 
porous bioactive glass scaffolds showed multifunctional properties 
of angiogenesis capacity, osteostimulation and antibacterial activ- 
ity [36] . Thus, adding a minor amount of Cu into the metallic im- 
plants is a promising strategy to introduce bactericidal activity and 
osteogenic function on a sustainable basis and at an effective level, 
but special attention should be paid to the trade-off between the 
bacteria-killing ability and inducing toxic effects to healthy cells in 
the body. 
Based on the high corrosion resistance and biocompatibility of 
Nb and the distinct effects of Cu element on antibacterial prop- 
erties and osteogenesis, it is reasonable to hypothesize that by 
adding a minor amount Cu into Nb matrix, the resulted Nb-Cu al- 
loys, should have great potential for load-bearing hard tissue re- 
placement. Moreover, unlike Ti-Cu [ 29 , 30 , 37 ] and Mg-Cu [ 39 , 41 ] 
alloys which formed intermetallic compounds, the Nb-Cu binary 
phase diagram [42] shows that Nb and Cu are highly immisci- 
ble at room temperature and no intermetallic compounds will be 
formed. 
To test this hypothesis, in this work, we designed and fabri- 
cated ultraﬁne-grained Nb-Cu alloys with a minor amount of Cu 
by a combination of high-energy milling and spark plasma sinter- 
ing. Considering that the Cu content in previous studies on Cu- 
containing biomaterials was mostly below 5 wt.% [ 29 , 31 , 40 , 43–47 ], 
over which cytotoxicity might be induced [48] . In order to make 
sure that the Nb-Cu alloys are at the safety level of Cu content and 
to reveal the effect of Cu content on the alloys’ properties, 0.5 and 
3.0 wt.% of Cu were added in this work, and deﬁned as Nb-0.5Cu 
and Nb-3Cu hereafter, respectively. We ﬁrst examined the mechan- 
ical properties and magnetic susceptibility, corrosion behavior and 
ion release. Then, we evaluated the antibacterial eﬃcacy, cytocom- 
patibility, adhesion and osteogenic potential in vitro . Finally, we 
evaluated the osteogenesis and osteointegration of the Nb-Cu im- 
plants using the rat cranial defect model and bone screws inser- 
tion in rabbit femoral bone, respectively. The obtained results could 
help to evaluate the potential of Nb-Cu alloys for load-bearing hard 
tissue repair. 
2. Results 
2.1. Phase and microstructure 
The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the starting powder 
mixture ( Fig. 1 (a)) of Nb-0.5Cu and Nb-3Cu conﬁrmed the pres- 
ence of Nb and Cu. After 12 h of ball milling, apparent peak broad- 
ening and intensity reduction were observed ( Fig. 1 (b)), suggesting 
grain reﬁnement of the ball-milled powders. However, the Cu re- 
ﬂections were not completely gone and a very weak peak of Cu 
(111) at 2 θ of 44.53 ° was still present. The Nb reﬂections shift to 
smaller 2 θ angles suggested that Cu was partially forced to mix 
with Nb and formed Nb-Cu solid solution. XRD patterns of the 
spark-plasma-sintered bulk Nb-Cu alloys showed the presence of 
Cu reﬂections ( Fig. 1 (c)), suggesting the formation of Cu precipi- 
tates. A trace amount of Nb-oxide was also detected due to the rel- 
atively low vacuum condition during the sintering process. The rel- 
ative densities of sintered Nb-0.5Cu and Nb-3Cu alloys were 92.3% 
and 95.2%, respectively. Both were denser than sintered Nb (91.1%), 
which indicated that the addition of Cu could enhance the sinter- 
ing of Nb-Cu. This can also be revealed by the scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM) images shown in Fig. 1 (d ) , where the area frac- 
tion and the size of the micro-pores were reduced with increas- 
ing Cu amount in the alloys. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) 
maps ( Fig. 1 (e)) revealed that the mean grain size of pure Nb was 
∼200 nm, and after alloying with a minor amount of Cu, the mean 
grain sizes of Nb changed to be 225 and 178 nm for Nb-0.5Cu and 
Nb-3Cu, respectively. The Cu precipitates were located at the grain 
boundaries of Nb, with an average size of 144 nm for Nb-0.5Cu 
and 130 nm for Nb-3Cu, respectively ( Fig. 1 (f)). Overall, the three 
groups of specimens showed ultraﬁne-grained microstructure. 
2.2. Nb-3Cu alloy shows high strength, large fracture strain and low 
magnetic susceptibility 
Fig. 2 (a) showed the representative compressive stress-strain 
curves of Nb-0.5Cu and Nb-3Cu alloys along with pure Nb. Com- 
pared with pure Nb with the yield strength of 1.27 ±0.3 GPa, the 
compressive yield strength of Nb-Cu alloys slightly decreased with 
the amount of Cu ( Table 1 ). However, the ultimate compressive 
strength and fracture strain showed an opposite trend, which in- 
creased with the amount of Cu. The Nb-3Cu alloy showed an ulti- 
mate strength of 2.21 ±0.04 GPa and a fracture strain of 41.1% ±1.2%. 
The magnetization as a function of the induced magnetic ﬁeld 
was shown in Fig. 2 (b). The three groups of specimens showed 
very similar magnetic susceptibility behaviors. All of them showed 
very low magnetic susceptibilities, in the order of 10 −6 cm 3 /g. 
Adding a small amount of Cu could even slightly decrease the mag- 
netic susceptibility due to the diamagnetism characteristics of Cu 
( −1.0785 ×10 −6 cm 3 /g) [49] . 
2.3. Nb-3Cu alloy shows enhanced corrosion resistance 
The potentiodynamic polarization curves of Nb-0.5Cu, Nb-3Cu 
and pure Nb in Hank’s solution at 37 ◦C were shown in Fig. 3 (a).

corrosion resistance with the smallest corrosion current density

of Nb-0.5Cu ( I corr = 2.87 μA/cm 2 ). Passive regions were observed 
in the anodic branch of Nb and Nb-3Cu, indicating the formation 
of protective passive ﬁlms during the polarization process. Partic- 
ularly, the Nb-3Cu alloy had a signiﬁcantly larger passive region 
in the range of 0.098–0.528 V and a more positive transpassiva- 
tion potential than Nb, indicating the formation of stable passive

To investigate the corrosion process of pure Nb and Nb-Cu 
alloys, the electrochemical impedance spectrum (EIS) was mea- 
sured and Nyquist plots were presented in Fig. 3 (b). Larger di- 
ameters of the capacitive loops indicated higher corrosion resis- 
tance [18] . Compared with Nb-0.5Cu and Nb at open circuit po- 
tential (OCP), the capacitive loop of Nb-3Cu showed a much larger 
diameter of the capacitive loop. This revealed that Nb-3Cu alloy 
had much higher corrosion resistance than Nb-0.5Cu and pure Nb. 
On the other hand, diffusion phenomena were observed in the 
low-frequency zone for three groups of samples. The Nb-3Cu al- 
loy showed typical Warburg impendence with the argument of 
π/4. While, the argument of diffusion impendence of pure Nb and 
Nb-0.5Cu alloy was over π/4, indicating a barrier layer occurred 
near the corroded area to block ions inﬂow in the diffusion pro- 
cess. This may be due to the lower relative density of pure Nb and 
Nb-0.5Cu than Nb-3Cu alloy, where the deposited corrosion prod- 
uct from Hank’s solution served as the barrier inside the pores. 
Bode plots were also presented in Fig. 3 (c). It was reported that 
the larger impedance | Z| at the low frequency of 0.1 Hz indicated 
the higher corrosion resistance [50] . The | Z| of Nb, Nb-0.5Cu and 
Nb-3Cu at 0.1 Hz was 763, 1445 and 4276 /Omega1cm 2 , respectively, 
which also conﬁrmed that the corrosion resistance followed the 
order of Nb-3Cu > Nb-0.5Cu > Nb. In addition, the peak of phase 
angle in the intermediate frequency region represented the capac- 
itance effect of the passive ﬁlm during the corrosion process and 
the peak more closed to 90 ° indicated the better capacitance effect 
[51] . The passive ﬁlm of Nb-3Cu showed the strongest capacitance 
effect followed by Nb-0.5Cu, while the pure Nb showed the weak- 
est capacitance effect. This indicated that a denser passive ﬁlm was 
formed on the surface of Nb-3Cu when compared with Nb-0.5Cu 
and Nb. Also, both Nb-3Cu and Nb-0.5Cu had only one time con- 
stant, but pure Nb showed two time constants during corrosion 
process, which revealed that the corrosion process of pure Nb oc- 
curred both on the surface and inside of the pores. In general, the 
Nb-3Cu had the highest corrosion resistance when compared with 
Nb-0.5 Cu and pure Nb. 
2.4. Nb-3Cu alloy shows sustained release of Cu 2 + ions in a safe 
dosage level 
Fig. 4 showed the cumulative amount of Nb and Cu ions re- 
leased from the three groups of specimens during immersion in 
Hank’s solution for 21 days. The released Nb ions from the Nb- 
0.5Cu and Nb-3Cu were apparently lower than those from pure 
Nb, while no signiﬁcant difference was found between the two Nb- 
Cu specimens. During the ﬁrst 14 days, the released amount of Nb 
ions approximately linearly increased with time and then the re- 
leased rate started to decrease. The cumulative released amount 
of Nb ions reached 654 ppb, nearly 33% higher than that of Nb- 
0.5Cu (478 ppb) and Nb-3Cu alloys (499 ppb). Fig. 4 (b) showed 
the Cu ion release proﬁles of Nb-0.5Cu and Nb-3Cu. The concen- 
tration of the cumulative released Cu ions from Nb-3Cu was sig- 
niﬁcantly higher than from Nb-0.5Cu, almost 8 times higher than 
that from Nb-0.5Cu on day 21. The average released rate of Nb- 
3Cu (8.9 ng/(cm 2 day)) during the 21 days of immersion in Hank’s 
solution was ﬁve times that of the Nb-0.5Cu (1.8 ng/(cm 2 day)), 
which was proportional to their Cu contents in the as-sintered al- 
loy. The release proﬁle of Cu ions clearly showed that the sin- 
tered Nb-Cu alloys could sustainably release Cu ions for at least 
21 days. 
2.5. Nb-3Cu alloy shows strong bactericidal activity 
The optical images and the counted numbers of the viable bac- 
teria colony-forming units (CFUs) after 24 h of incubation were in- 
cluded in Fig. 5 . Upon against E. coli. , compared with the number 
of CFUs of pure Nb (1385 ±61), the numbers of Nb-Cu alloys were 
signiﬁcantly reduced, only 176 ±13 CFUs for Nb-0.5Cu and 26 ±13 
CFUs for Nb-3Cu ( Fig. 5 (b)). The calculated antibacterial rate of Nb- 
3Cu reached 98.1%, and the Nb-0.5Cu showed an antibacterial rate 
of 87.2% against E. coli . Similar antibacterial eﬃcacy was observed 
against S. aureus strains. The number of CFUs of pure Nb reached 
up to 1298 ±140, while only 169 ±18 for Nb-0.5Cu and 46 ±14 for 
Nb-3Cu ( Fig. 5 (c) ) . The calculated antibacterial rates of Nb-0.5Cu 
and Nb-3Cu reached 86.3% and 96.4%, respectively. These results 
demonstrated that the Nb-Cu alloys showed strong antibacterial 
activities against both E. coli. and S. aureus and the antibacterial 
rates depended on the Cu content in the as-sintered alloy. 
The bacterial adhesion and bioﬁlm formation of the two strains 
on the three groups of specimens were observed by SEM after 
24 h of co-culture. The clustered E. coli colonies showed normal 
rod shape on the pure Nb control sample, while a much smaller 
number of E. coli colonies were located on the Nb-0.5Cu alloy. A 
higher magniﬁcation SEM image further revealed the membrane 
lysis and rupture. On the surface of Nb-3Cu alloy, only several seg- 
ments of damaged bacteria were observed, and no bioﬁlm was 
formed ( Fig. 5 (d)). Similar results were also observed for S. aureus .

cytocompatibility 
The viability of MC3T3-E1 cells after 1, 3 and 5 days of in-

2.7. In vivo evaluation 
2.7.1. Nb-3Cu alloy could promote bone regeneration in rat cranial 
defects 
The rat cranial defect models were used to investigate the in 
vivo osteogenesis of the Nb-Cu alloys. After 12 weeks of implan- 
tation, the Nb-Cu alloys revealed defect restoration in rat cranial 
defects. During sample collection, it is found that the Nb-Cu al- 
loys implants were tightly bonded to the surrounding tissues but 
pure Nb implant could be easily uncovered, indicating that the 
Nb-Cu alloys could induce the regeneration of surrounding perios- 
teum and connective tissues of defects. The reconstructed micro- 
computed tomography (micro-CT) images revealed a larger amount 
of new bone formation in the defects implanted with Nb-3Cu and 
Nb-0.5Cu, while very few thin new bones were found in the de- 
fects implanted with pure Nb and blank ( Fig. 9 (a) ) . Quantitative 
analysis revealed that the key indices, including bone mineral den- 
sity (BMD) and bone volume (BV)/tissue volume (TV), of the bone-

of the blank control. However, the two Nb-Cu alloy groups showed 
signiﬁcantly higher values than those of pure Nb ( p < 0.05 for 
BMD, p < 0.001 for BV/TV), suggesting that the addition of Cu 
could induce osteogenesis in vivo . Of the two Nb-Cu alloy implants, 
Nb-3Cu alloy group showed higher BMD and BV/TV than Nb-0.5Cu 
( Fig. 9 (b, c)). These data conﬁrmed the superior bone-formation 
ability of Nb-3Cu alloy. 
Histologically, no bone formation was found in the gap of de- 
fects in blank control and pure Nb. A considerable amount of os- 
teonal bone was found to ﬁll the defects and envelope and be in 
direct contact with the Nb-Cu alloy implants ( Fig. 9 (d)). Although 
no signiﬁcant difference was found in the number of osteoblasts 
among the four groups, the numbers of ﬁbroblasts in pure Nb and 
blank groups were signiﬁcantly higher than that of Nb-Cu groups, 
which could be a reason for the relatively poor bone healing in Nb 
and blank groups. Also, the matured bone tissues in Nb-3Cu alloy 
group were apparently thicker than that in Nb-0.5Cu group. The 
defective gap in Nb-3Cu group was linked to new bone, but a vis-

revealed that the Nb-3Cu alloy could promote bone regeneration in 
vivo .

3. Discussion 
3.1. Relationship between microstructure and mechanical properties 
of the Nb-Cu alloys 
To balance the biological functions and cytotoxicity, only a small 
amount of Cu was added to the Nb matrix. For Nb-Cu immiscible 
alloy with a very high heat of mixing, severe plastic deformation 
induced by high energy ball milling at room temperature can be 
extended by the only rather limited amount [52] . In this work, af- 
ter 12 h of ball milling, Cu could still not be completely chemi- 
cally mixed with Nb. The gigapascal level of yield strength should 
be attributed to the ultraﬁne-grained structure. However, the ad- 
dition of Cu did not induce precipitation hardening but the yield 
strength decreased with the adding amount of Cu. This should be 
due to the soft phase characteristics of Cu, which could not effec- 
tively hinder the dislocation movement during plastic deformation. 
The three groups of samples showed strong strain hardening with 
ultimate compressive strength signiﬁcantly higher than their corre- 
sponding yield strength, particularly for Nb-Cu alloys whose com- 
pressive strength was double that of the yield strength. More im- 
portantly, adding a minor amount of Cu dramatically improved the 
fracture strain of the Nb-Cu alloys. This should arise from two fac- 
tors: (i) adding Cu into the Nb matrix improved the relative den- 
sity, and (ii) the soft nature of Cu phase could accommodate more 
plastic strain and the strain gradient between the hard Nb and soft 
Cu phase would help to prevent rapid cracking during compression 
and, thus improve plasticity. 
3.2. Corrosion mechanism of the Nb-Cu alloys 
Since the Nb matrix and Cu precipitates could form micro- 
galvanic couples and Cu ( E °Cu = + 0.34 V for Cu 2 + (aq.) + 2e 
↔ Cu(s)) has a higher standard electrode potential than Nb 
( E 0 
Nb = −0.75 V for Nb 2 O 5(s) + 10H + + 10e − ↔ 2Nb (s) + 5H 2 O), 
it is intuitively believed that Nb-Cu two-phase alloys should cause 
more severe corrosion than pure Nb. However, both I corr and EIS 
suggested that the addition of Cu enhanced the corrosion resis- 
tance as compared with pure Nb. Such enhanced corrosion behav- 
ior of Nb-Cu alloys should probably be contributed to their higher 
relative densities. Although the Nb 2 O 5 passive ﬁlm could form on 
the surface of pure Nb and Nb-Cu alloys to protect the matrix, the 
pores at the sample surface would cause discontinued protection 
of the oxides layer, and thus result in local corrosion around the 
pores. Thus, the Nb-3Cu alloy had a relatively more uniform pas- 
sive ﬁlm than Nb-0.5Cu and pure Nb which helped to prevent local 
corrosion. 
3.3. Ion release of the Nb-Cu alloys 
The released Cu 2 + in aquatic environment has been recognized 
to have the functions of antibacterial activity, osteogenesis and an- 
giogenesis [53–55] . The release rate of Cu 2 + ions is a crucial factor 
in the design of Cu-contained biomedical implants. A low dose of 
Cu 2 + can function in biological activities, while excess cumulative 
Cu 2 + would cause cytotoxicity, allergy and other adverse reactions

[ 33 , 53 ]. Therefore, it is essential to ﬁnd an optimum amount of Cu 
to reach the balance between biocompatibility, osteogenesis, angio- 
genesis and antibacterial properties. The release rates of Cu 2 + ions 
depended on the input concentration of Cu in the as-sintered Nb- 
Cu alloys. Here, even for the Nb-3Cu alloy, the release rate of Cu 2 + 
was only 8.9 ng/(cm 2 day), which is several orders of magnitude 
smaller than the advised maximum intake dose. Note that the tol- 
erable daily upper intake level of Cu for healthy adults is 2–3 mg 
[56] . 
3.4. Antibacterial mechanism 
Cu has been used as a strong antibacterial agent for a long 
time due to its broad antibacterial spectrum [ 29 , 36 , 57 , 58 ]. In this 
study, the ultraﬁne-grained Nb-Cu alloys showed strong antibac- 
terial ability when compared to pure Nb. Nb-3Cu alloys showed 
strong antibacterial activity against both E. coli and S. aureus by 
sustained release of Cu 2 + ions. The strong antimicrobial activity 
of Cu 2 + ions was mainly explained by three mechanisms: (i) Cu 2 + 
could malfunction and destroy the bacteria membrane by binding 
to the membrane then cause the leakages of DNA, RNA and protein 
[ 59 , 60 ]; (ii) Cu 2 + could catalyze the production of reactive oxygen 
species (ROS), and the respiration of bacteria would be inhibited 
once a high amount of ROS was accumulated [ 61 , 62 ]; and (iii) Cu 2 + 
could enter the bacteria and bind to DNA, RNA and protein then 
interfere their transcription and translation [ 24 , 61 ]. In this study, 
almost no bioﬁlm was observed on the surface of Nb-Cu alloys af- 
ter 24 h of incubation, suggesting that the released Cu 2 + ions from 
Nb-Cu discs could suppress the formation of bioﬁlm. The cracked 
E. coli and S. aureus fragments located on the surface of Nb-3Cu al- 
loy disk indicated that the released Cu 2 + could contact and break 
the bacterial membrane, thus resulting in leakage of the bacteria. 
3.5. Biocompatibility 
Cytotoxicity was an important concern for biomedical implants. 
Nb was regarded as one of the biocompatible metals in the chem- 
ical periodic table. However, a high concentration of Cu 2 + could 
cause cytotoxicity [63] . Both cytotoxicity assays and cytoskeleton 
observations conﬁrmed the superior biocompatibility of sintered 
Nb-Cu alloys. Because only a minor amount of Cu was introduced, 
the excellent biocompatibility should mainly be attributed to the 
Nb matrix. The mechanism for excellent biocompatibility of Nb can 
be explained as follows. The surface of Nb was easily oxidized un- 
der the alkalescent body ﬂuid, and then an oxide ﬁlm was formed 
on the alloy surface, which could support the adhesion and migra- 
tion of cells [18] . Then, the Nb-OH functional groups were formed 
during the hydrolysis reaction of Nb, which could bind to the ter- 
minal carboxyl and amino groups of amino acids and proteins and 
absorb the surrounding calcium and phosphate ions to form bone 
mineral-like apatite nuclei [ 17 , 64 ]. In addition, previous studies re- 
ported that the Cu 2 + ions at a concentration of 0.1 mmol/L could 
stimulate cell proliferation but would cause a toxic effect at the 
concentration of 0.5 mmol/L [33] . In our sintered Nb-3Cu alloy, 
the released Cu 2 + concentration was in the range of 70 0–80 0 ppb, 
which was much smaller than the threshold for cytotoxicity. Thus, 
adding a minor amount of Cu into Nb matrix would not cause neg- 
ative effects on the biocompatibility. 
3.6. In vitro and in vivo osteogenesis 
Apart from the antibacterial ability and biocompatibility, Cu 2 + 
also played important roles in skeleton development [65] . Previ- 
ous studies demonstrated that Cu 2 + could also promote the os- 
teogenic differentiation of bone mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) or 
pre-osteoblasts [66] . As a hypoxia-mimicking ion, Cu 2 + could stabi- 
lize the presence of hypoxia-induced factors-1alpha (HIF-1 α), lead- 
ing to the accumulation and continuous activation of HIF- α, which 
could improve the expression of downstream osteogenesis-related 
genes [67] . Furthermore, Cu 2 + could also activate the transcription 
of osteogenesis-related factors by facilitating the formation of the 
HIF-1 transcriptional complex [68] . Therefore, Cu was also used for 
osteogenesis. 
In this study, the Cu 2 + ions released from Nb-Cu alloys signiﬁ- 
cantly promoted the ALP activity and mineralization of MC3T3-E1 
cells after 7- and 14-day of incubation. Besides, the expression of 
osteogenic genes in the initial stage (RUNX2 and ALP) of MC3T3- 
E1 was signiﬁcantly up-regulated after 7 days of incubation with 
Nb-Cu alloys, while the expression of osteogenic genes in the late 
stage (COLA1 and OCN) presented a high level of expression in the 
Nb-Cu alloy after 14 days of culture. Hence, the results demon- 
strated that the Cu 2 + released from Nb-Cu alloys could continu- 
ously promote osteogenesis in both early and late stages in vitro. 
In vivo evaluation using the rat cranial defect model also demon- 
strated the Nb-3Cu alloy had good osteointegration ability . There- 
fore, the Nb-3Cu alloy was selected to investigate the potential 
clinical application of Nb-Cu alloy in hard-tissue repairing. 
3.7. Potential application for bone screws 
Metallic biomaterials were widely used as ﬁxation screws for 
bone fracture, endoprostheses and reconstruction of the Ante- 
rior Cruciate Ligament (ACL). However, most commercially used 
non-degradable bone screws were made of stainless steel or ti- 
tanium alloy, which would easily cause implant-associated infec- 
tion (IAI) and screw loosening due to lack of antibacterial activity 
and poor osteointegration ability. Although various surface modi- 
ﬁcation strategies had been attempted to improve their antibacte- 
rial activity and osteogenesis, the stability, sustained effect and ad- 
hesive strength between the coating and screws still limited their 
clinical application. Herein, the Nb-3Cu alloy exhibited good bio- 
compatibility, strong antibacterial activity and superior osteogene- 
sis. Therefore, the Nb-3Cu alloy was fabricated in the form of bone 
screws for femoral lateral condyle implantation. Nb-3Cu screws 
showed excellent osteointegration ability. The superior mechani- 
cal properties, strong antibacterial activity, excellent biocompati- 
bility, osteogenesis and osteointegration ability enable the present 
Nb-3Cu alloy a promising candidate for load-bearing bone replace- 
ment. 
4. Conclusion 
In this study, ultraﬁne-grained immiscible Nb-Cu alloys with a 
minor amount of Cu were fabricated via mechanical alloying and 
spark plasma sintering and were evaluated for the potential use 
as metallic implants. The ultraﬁne-grained Nb-3Cu alloy not only 
showed high strength, large fracture strain, low magnetic suscepti- 
bility and improved corrosion resistance, but also showed strong 
antibacterial activity and good biocompatibility. Furthermore, by 
sustained release of Cu 2 + , the bulk Nb-3Cu alloy also showed en- 
hanced osteogenesis and osteointegration ability both in vitro and 
in vivo . Together, these results suggest that the present Nb-3Cu al- 
loy should have broad applications in multifunctional implants for 
load-bearing hard-tissue repair.
