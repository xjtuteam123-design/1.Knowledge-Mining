Effect of mechanical alloying on microstructure and mechanical properties of
hot-pressed Nb–16Si alloys

abstract
Nb–16 at.% Si bulk alloys were fabricated by hot pressing from compound powders milled for different
times. The inﬂuences of mechanical alloying on microstructure and mechanical properties of the hot-
pressed alloys were investigated. The results indicate that the particle size of powders was signiﬁcantly
reﬁned and the addition of absolute ethyl alcohol suppressed the agglomeration of powders. When milling
time increased from 6 to 24 h, the constituent phases (Nbss, Nb
5Si3 and Nb3 Si) changed from large size
trips to ﬁne near-equiaxed shape, and the volume fraction of ductile Nbss decreased. In addition, Vickers
hardness and ﬂexural strength of bulk alloys increased gradually owing to the enhancement of the volume
fraction of niobium silicides. Fracture toughness was in range of 10.98–13.23 MPa m
1/2 , which was higher
than that of cast materials. It was attributed to the positive role of ductile phase and ﬁne-grain toughening.

1. Introduction
The advanced turbine designs require airfoils that can operate
successfully at temperatures above 1300 ◦C, which has exceeded
the maximum operating temperature (approximately 1150 ◦C) of
nickel-based superalloys. Nb-based alloys with niobium silicides
have been developed as the new class of heat-resisting structural
materials because of their outstanding properties, i.e. low density
(about 7 g/cm
3), high melting point (>1700 ◦C) and high strength
at high temperatures [1–5]. Nevertheless, the intrinsic brittleness
of silicides at room temperature hinders the practical applications
of niobium silicide-based alloys. To overcome the drawback, many
researchers took various measures including toughening and alloy-
ing. Based on the concept of ductile-phase toughening of brittle
compounds, the fracture toughness of the alloys has been improved
by incorporating ductile Nb solid solution (Nbss) into brittle nio-
bium silicides [6–8]. In addition, many efforts have been devoted
to looking for alloying elements and optimizing their content to
enhance the room temperature deformability or fracture tough-
ness. In previous studies [2,9–13], the effect of alloying elements
such as Hf, Ti, Cr, Al, Ta, Mo, W, and V on microstructure and
mechanical properties of niobium silicide-based alloys have been
investigated, and signiﬁcant improvement of fracture toughness
has been obtained by alloying with Cr, Hf, Ti and Al to form Nb–Ti–Si
[11,12], Nb–Ti–Si–Al [11,12] and Nb–Ti–Hf–Cr–Al–Si [13].

Up to now, niobium silicide-based alloys have been success-
fully produced by a wide variety of processing techniques including
electron beam melting, vacuum arc melting, directional solidiﬁca-
tion and spark plasma sintering [1,2,14,15]. The alloys fabricated
by casting methods possess component segregation and coarse
microstructure, while ﬁne grains can be obtained in dense materi-
als prepared by spark plasma sintering because of the short holding
time. Recently, some researchers have produced Nb–Si–Ti ternary
alloys by reactive hot-pressing sintering [16] and synthesized nio-
bium silicides by ball milling of powder mixtures with different Si
contents of Nb–Si binary system[17–19]. But, so far there have been
rare reports on the effect of mechanical alloying on the microstruc-
ture and mechanical properties of hot-pressed Nb–Si binary alloys.
In this paper, bulk Nb–16Si alloys were prepared by hot press-
ing from compound powders milled for different times. The
microstructure and mechanical properties have also been investi-
gated in detail to understanding the effects of mechanical alloying.
2. Experimental procedures
The raw materials used in this study are element powders of
niobium (99.9% purity, 50 /H9262m) and silicon (99% purity, 50 /H9262m).
Mechanical alloying was conducted at room temperature in a plan-
etary ball-mill (QM-BP) with stainless steel vials and bearing steel
balls. A powder mixture with 47.28 g of Nb and 2.72 g of Si was
added to each vial to obtain the nominal composition of Nb–16Si.
During milling, the weight ratio of ball to powder was maintained
at 12:1, and the rotation speed employed was 250 rpm. The start-
ing and milled powders were performed in a glove box under

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Morphology of MA powders
The SEM micrographs of Nb and Si powders (starting materials)
are shown in Fig. 1 (a) and (b), respectively. The starting powders
showed irregular shapes with an average size of about 50 /H9262m. The
morphologies of Nb–16Si compound powders milled for different
times are presented in Fig. 2, which distinctively exhibits the pro-
gressive changes in morphology of mechanically alloyed particles
from 1 to 24 h. In the early stage of ball milling, particles had more
rounded shapes (Fig. 2(a)) in comparison with initial powders. After
6 h of milling, Nb particles were deformed into slices, spheres and
sticks. It suggests that Nb particles possessed excellent ductility
at the moment and were easily collided into slices and sheared
into spheres and sticks by the grinding balls. In the powder mix-
ture milled for 12 h, ﬁner slices or blocks with the size of about
25 /H9262m were observed. This indicates that work-hardening result-
ing from continuous deformation worked at this period and the
powder particles became brittle. With the milling time increasing
to 18 h, the reﬁning rate of powders became quicker and the aver-
age size of particles reduced to about 5 /H9262m. It can be concluded
that work-hardening became even more predominant during this
milling period. After 24 h milling, the particles became much ﬁner
and distributed more homogeneously. The particle size was even
less than 5 /H9262m for powder mixture milled for 24 h because of the
signiﬁcant brittleness of particles. Moreover, during the milling
process, agglomeration of powders was suppressed because abso-
lute ethyl alcohol covered the ﬂesh surface of particles and reduced
the contacting chance of particle interfaces.
3.2. X-ray diffraction analysis of MA powders
The XRD patterns of Nb–16Si powders milled for different
milling times are presented in Fig. 3 . In general, with the milling
time increasing, it was noted that diffraction peaks of Nb became
broader and their intensities decreased. The peak broadening indi-
cates the formation of ﬁne grain and a high density of defects
associated with large local strains in the powder particles. The grain
size and microstrain of Nb particles milled for different times, which
were calculated from XRD results using Hall method, were shown
in Fig. 4. In addition, Nb peaks also slightly moved toward the direc-
tion of lower diffraction angles and Si peaks reduced in intensities
during milling. Such behavior suggests that the lattice parameter of
Nb increased and a portion of Si atoms dissolved into the Nb lattices
to form interstitial solid solution. Moreover, no clear indication for
the formation of niobium silicides (Nb
5Si3 or Nb3Si) was found.

3.4. Mechanical properties of hot-pressed materials
The mechanical properties of bulk alloys hot pressed from
powders milled for different times are shown in Table 3 . Vick-
ers hardness and ﬂexural strength of bulk alloys exhibited similar
tendency that both of them increased with the milling time increas-
ing from 6 to 24 h. As is known, the strength of a multi-phase
alloy depends on the volume fraction, shape and distribution of
constituent phases, and on the strength of each phase. In the inves-
tigated alloys, the enhancement of both hardness and strength was
attributed to the increased volume fraction of silicides with the
milling time increasing (shown in Table 2 ). Moreover, the mech-
anism of ﬁne-grain strengthening was also thought to contribute
to the improved strength of materials made from powders milled
for the longer time. The highest elastic modulus was observed in
Alloy HC, while alloy HA exhibited the least one. Elastic modulus
mainly depends on the interatomic bonding force of materials, and
niobium–silicon bonds in the intermetallics (Nb
5Si3 and Nb3Si) are
greater than the niobium–niobium bonds in Nbss. Therefore, with
the milling time increasing, the increased volume fraction of nio-
bium silicides was expected to improve the elastic modulus of bulk
alloys.

The fracture mechanisms can be better explained through the
analysis of indentation crack propagation.Fig. 8(a) and (b) show the
backscattered electron (BSE) images of the indention crack paths
in alloy HA and HC, respectively. The investigated alloys showed
different crack initiations and propagation behaviors due to the dis-
tinct fracture mechanisms of Nbss and silicides. Generally, along the
fracture path, cracks propagated uninterruptedly throughout sili-
cides, were blunted and bridged by ductile Nbss phase, and ﬁnally
were arrested at the interfaces between Nbss and silicides or inside
Nbss. Moreover, cracks usually were arrested as meeting the large
Nbss (A in Fig. 8(a)), while blunted and bridged by the ﬁne ductile
phase (B in Fig. 8). It was due to that the ductile Nbss with large size
could bear signiﬁcantly plastic deformation to trap the crack energy
and provide the material with a higher toughness increment via a
crack bridging mechanism. As a result, the crack length in alloy HA
with larger ductile phases was shorter than that in alloy HC. There-
fore, with the elongation of milling time from 6 to 24 h, decrease in
size of ductile phases ( Fig. 6), loss of ductile phases and increase of
brittle phases ( Table 2) inevitably gave rise to the degeneration of
the fracture toughness.

the present study, ﬁner and more homogeneous microstructure

casting techniques for preparation of Nb–16Si alloy unavoidably
induced coarse grains and inhomogeneous microstructure, which
were harmful to the fracture toughness.
4. Conclusions
In the present work, Nb–16Si alloys were hot pressed from pow-
ders milled for various periods of milling time. The results are
summarized as follows.
1. During mechanical alloying, the particles and grains of Nb were
both reﬁned and a portion of Si atoms dissolved into Nb lattices
to form interstitial solid solutions. The particle size of powders
milled for 24 h was reduced to less than 5 /H9262m and agglomer-
ation of powders was suppressed by the addition of absolute
ethyl alcohol in the milling process. No clear indication for the
formation of niobium silicides (Nb
5Si3 or Nb3Si) was found.
2. The investigated Nb–16Si alloys all consisted of three main
phases, i.e. Nbss, Nb5Si3 and Nb3Si, and few NbssII with higher Si
content was also observed in alloys from powder mixtures milled
for the longer time. With milling time increasing from 6 to 24 h,
the microstructure of alloys changed from large size trips to ﬁne
near-equiaxed shape, and meanwhile the constituent phases
distributed more homogeneously. In addition, the volume frac-
tion of ductile Nbss increased with the ball milling proceeding,
while the change of silicides contrasted.
3. Vickers hardness, ﬂexural strength and elastic modulus of hot-
pressed alloys increased with milling time increasing. It was
attributed to that volume fraction of niobium silicides increased
and the microstructure became ﬁner in the alloys made from
longer time milled powders.
4. The fracture toughness of bulk alloys decreased with the elonga-
tion of milling time because of the reduction in volume fraction
of ductile Nbss phase. As for the investigated alloys of this study,
the improvement of fracture toughness was mainly attributed
to the mechanism of ductile-phase toughening. Moreover, the
present fracture toughness was higher than that of as-casted
materials, and it resulted from both ductile-phase toughening
and ﬁne-grain toughening.
