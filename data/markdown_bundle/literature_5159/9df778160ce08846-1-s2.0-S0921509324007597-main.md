ABSTRACT 
Recently, Al-containing refractory high/medium-entropy alloys (RH/MEAs) have attracted special attention from 
materials scientists worldwide. Some of these alloys demonstrated an excellent combination of high-temperature 
strength and room-temperature tensile ductility provided by the formation of a structure consisting of body- 
centred cubic (bcc) matrix and B2 nanodomains/particles. However, oxidation resistance and phase stability, 
and, more importantly, the resulting mechanical properties of the Al-containing RH/MEAs, which are critical 
characteristics for high-temperature structural materials, remain unexplored. Herein, these aspects were 
extensively investigated for the Al
7.5
(NbTiZr)
92.5 
(in at%) alloy having an initial bcc + B2 structure. The alloy 
was subjected to long-term (500 h) annealing or oxidation tests at 600, 700, and 800 
◦
C followed by the eval -

◦
C. Microstructural studies of the speci -
mens exposed to long-term annealing showed the formation of Zr
5

boundaries and within grain interiors. The volume fraction of these particles was the highest (~16 %) after 
annealing at 600

to 10 h at 600

of a set of properties of promising RH/MEAs to critically assess perspectives of these alloys to find applications as 
new high-temperature materials in aircraft and aerospace sectors. 
1. Introduction 
Refractory high/medium-entropy alloys (RH/MEAs) are an object of 
extensive investigations since the inception in 2010 [ 1 , 2 ]. Such an in -
terest in RH/MEAs is attributed to their vast and flexible compositional 
space that helps attain better oxidation [ 3 , 4 ] and wear resistance [ 5 – 8 ], 
higher strength [ 2 , 9 – 11 ], and enhanced malleability [ 12 – 15 ] compared 
to conventional refractory alloys. These features make RH/MEAs 
promising candidates for next-generation aircraft and aerospace 
applications. 
Among different RH/MEAs, special attention has been recently paid 
to Al-containing compositions. Initially added to reduce the density [ 16 ] 
and improve the oxidation resistance [ 17 ], Al surprisingly increased the 
strength yet decreased ductility [ 16 , 18 ]. The reason behind that was 
later rationalised in terms of structure modulation. It was found that Al 
led to B2 (an ordered state of body-centred cubic, bcc, crystal lattice) 
ordering [ 19 – 24 ], and the properties of a B2 phase were highly sensitive 
to the chemical composition [ 21 , 25 – 27 ]. By regulating the Al content,

Besides strength, oxidation resistance is also a vital characteristic for 
materials targeted to the usage at elevated temperatures. Ductile RH/ 
MEAs composed mainly of the elements of IV group are susceptible to 
pesting [ 50 – 52 ]. The latter phenomenon is one manifestation of the 
spallation of the oxide scale associated with a complete disintegration of 
a material into powder due to the formation of the metallic oxides that 
lead to large internal stresses, resulting in propagation of gaps and 
cracks [ 53 ]. Some works showed that the use of protective coatings [ 54 ] 
or modification of chemical composition [ 50 ], including an addition of 
Al and exclusion of the elements, like Hf and/or Zr, allowed to avoid 
pesting in these RH/MEAs [ 50 , 51 ]. For instance, an AlNbTiZr RMEA 
[ 51 ], protectiveness of which relied on the formation of a thick layer 
comprising complex AlNbO

pesting) than an HfNbTiZr alloy in the interval of 600 – 1000

7.5
(NbTiZr)
92.5

show comparable level of oxidation resistance? 
Another important characteristic of high-temperature materials is 
phase stability. Most RH/MEAs are prone to decompose when being 
subjected to annealing at intermediate temperatures [ 12 , 13 , 37 , 55 – 59 ]. 
In the case of Al-containing RH/MEAs, particularly those with simul -
taneous presence of Al and Zr, notable phase transformations are also 
observed [ 24 , 46 , 60 – 70 ]. These alloys can experience (i) phase inversion 
[ 63 , 71 , 72 ] and (ii) precipitation of different Zr
x
Al

compounds [ 24 , 46 , 
63 – 66 , 69 , 70 ]. The first event is accompanied by a structure transition 
from the B2 matrix + bcc particles to the bcc matrix + B2 particles, and 
improves ductility [ 71 , 72 ]. In turn, the emergence of Zr
x
Al

in -
termetallics that precipitate mostly along the grain boundaries embrit -

equilibrium phases by means of CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Dia -
grams) were partially successful due to an incomplete description of 
binary and/or ternary phase diagrams in the databases existing [ 62 , 64 , 
65 ]. Thus, the phase stability of promising Al-containing RH/MEAs is 
still needed to be verified experimentally.

also tried to evaluate the impact of these changes on the mechanical 
performance of the alloy. We believe that the findings obtained allow us 
to critically assess the potential of ductile Al-containing RH/MEAs as 
structural materials in conditions close to real service ones.

3.3. Oxidation behaviour 
In addition to phase stability, we also examined the oxidation 
resistance of the Al
7.5
(NbTiZr)
92.5 
alloy at 600, 700, and 800 
◦

collects data on the semi-logarithmic time-dependent mass gain and

temperatures, the manifestation of pesting phenomena was observed 
that resulted in complex mass gain alterations over time. Specifically, at 
600 
◦
C, the alloy showed continuous yet mild mass gaining up to 25 h, 
albeit could sustain pesting only up to 10 h. Starting from 25 h, an 
abrupt mass loss was detected, which was associated with intensive 
spallation and pulverisation of the remnants of the oxide scale. Note that 
600 
◦
C was the sole temperature, at which the specimens could retain a 
bulk form after 100 h of testing, though with an evident decrease in 
dimensions (see Fig. 8 ). 
At higher temperatures, the pesting phenomena intensified greatly. 
At 700 
◦

of oxidation, the specimen started to spall off and showed a gradual mass 
loss lasting up to 10 h. Then, a switch to a steep mass gain was observed, 
and this trend saved up to 50 h. After 100 h, the specimen transformed 
into powder completely. At 800 
◦

beginning of the testing, leading to total disintegration of the specimen 
already after 5 h.

This study delved into phase stability and oxidation behaviour of the 
ductile Al

1) The Al
7.5
(NbTiZr)
92.5 
alloy showed phase instability accompanied by 
the formation of an additional Zr
5
Al
3 
phase. This phase precipitated 
as grain boundary and grain interior particles, volume fraction of 
which decreased with the temperature from ~16 % at 600 
◦

~0.5 % at 800 
◦

2) The Al
7.5
(NbTiZr)
92.5

aggravated with the temperature. Time to complete disintegration 
into powder decreased from over 100 h at 600 
◦

800 
◦

compositionally complex (Al,Nb,Ti,Zr)O
4

92.5

600 
◦

5

◦

◦
