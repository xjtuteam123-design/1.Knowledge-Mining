Temperature‑Dependent Yield Strength of Nanoprecipitate‑Strengthened 
Face‑Centered Cubic High Entropy Alloys: Prediction and Analysis
Ziyuan Zhao1,2 · Zheng Shen2 · Pan Dong2 · Yi He2 · Jianzuo Ma2 · Yanli Ma2 · Jiabin Yang2 · Weiguo Li1,2

Abstract
Nanoprecipitate-strengthened face-centered cubic high entropy alloys (NSFCC-HEAs) have huge potential for engineering 
applications due to their outstanding mechanical performance at extremely cryogenic temperatures. In this work, a temper -
ature-dependent yield strength predictive theoretical model of NSFCC-HEAs was established by considering the evolution 
of main influence mechanisms with temperature, such as lattice friction stress, grain boundary strengthening, and precipita-
tion strengthening. Only requiring some physical parameters at room temperature, the model can achieve good predictions 
of yield strength of NSFCC-HEAs at different temperatures, especially at extremely cryogenic temperatures, as verified by 
available experimental results. Furthermore, the contribution evolutions of each mechanism with temperature were quanti-
tively analyzed by using the present model. It revealed theoretically that lattice friction and short-range order strengthening 
are the main mechanisms determining the temperature dependence of NSFCC-HEAs. In addition, the influences of grain 
size, precipitate size, and precipitate volume fraction were analyzed. The scope of applicability of the model was discussed. 
The model can provide a method to help save time and resources by reducing cryogenic temperature testing, and deepening 
the understanding of the relationship between microstructure and yield strength of NSFCC-HEAs at different temperatures. 
More importantly, the customized cryogenic-temperature strength of NSFCC-HEAs is expected to be achieved through a 
priori theoretical prediction by selecting the basic material parameters and the corresponding processing technology.

1 Introduction
Due to the special atomic structure of multi-principal ele-
ments with equiatomic or near-equiatomic concentrations, 
some high entropy alloys with face-centered cubic struc-
tures (FCC-HEAs) exhibit excellent ductility and fracture 
toughness [1–7] at extremely cryogenic temperatures com-
pared with traditional alloy. Facing the increasing service 
demand for extreme cryogenic-temperature conditions, 
these unique properties make FCC-HEAs an ideal mate-
rial for deep space exploration, applied superconductiv -
ity, gas industry, etc. However, the FCC-HEAs has the 
characteristics of low yield strength [8 –11], which greatly 
limits its application in actual engineering. To improve 
the yield strength of FCC-HEAs at room and cryogenic 
temperatures, nanoprecipitates induced through thermo-
mechanical processing and substitutional and interstitial 
elements adding was proved to be an effective method [10, 
12–18]. Meanwhile, published results indicated that the 
yield strength of NSFCC-HEAs displays a strong tem-
perature dependence [10, 12, 13]. For example, the yield 
strength of Cr2N-precipitated CoCrFeMnNi high entropy 
alloys(HEAs) annealed at 850 °C doubles at low tem-
perature than at room temperature, which is 1292  and 
556 MPa at 77 and 298 K, respectively [13]. Consequently, 
a sufficient understanding of the yield strength of NSFCC-
HEAs at cryogenic temperature is vital for engineering

Quantitative characterization firstly requires an understand-
ing of the influencing mechanisms. Benefiting from a large 
number of cutting-edge experiments by worldwide scholars 
[21–31], a preliminary understanding of the strengthening 
mechanisms of HEAs at different temperatures has been 
achieved yet. Same as the ordinary alloys, strengthening mech-
anisms such as solid solution strengthening [32, 33], precipita-
tion strengthening [34, 35], dislocation, grain boundary 
strengthening [36], and their evolution with temperature 
existed in HEAs. As for FCC-HEAs, two features were 
observed by the microstructure analysis technologies such as 
X-ray diffraction(XRD) spectra and Backscattered electron 
imaging(BSE) images [37]. One is that initial dislocation den-
sities are very low because the current alloys were processed 
similarly and fully recrystallized to produce comparable grain 
sizes [1, 38]. The other is the FCC-HEAs showed the single-
phase solid solutions with no precipitates [1]. The latter pro-
vided theoretical support for precipitation strengthening [10, 
34, 39, 40]. A large number of models for characterizing the 
quantificational relationship between each strengthening 
mechanism and yield strength at room temperature have been 
proposed based on the understanding of these strengthening 
mechanisms and have many successful applications [24, 41,

as a combination of each strengthening mechanism with a

for characterizing the increment contribution of precipitation 
strengthening for yield strength of NSFCC-HEAs at different 
temperatures has not been reported. These questions are there-
fore the core of this study.
In this paper, a TDYS theoretical model of NSFCC-HEAs 
was established by firstly modifying Wu’s model and further 
considering the temperature dependence of precipitation 
strengthening. The prediction accuracy of modified Wu’s 
model for FCC-HEAs and the final model for NSFCC-HEAs 
were verified. Only some physical parameters at room tem-
perature that can be obtained from literature or material hand-
books are needed in our model to have a preferable predicted 
ability. Further, the analyses of contribution evolutions of each 
mechanism to yield strength with temperature, and some influ-
encing analyses such as the effect of grain size, precipitate 
size, and precipitate volume fraction were conducted based 
on the present model. Finally, the scope of applicability of 
the model was discussed. Except for prediction and quantita-
tive mechanism understanding, this work is expected to lay a 
foundation for the customized cryogenic-temperature strength 
of NSFCC-HEAs.

3.4 CoCrFeMnNi NSFCC‑HEAs
Shim et al. [13] fabricated a series of CoCrFeMnNi HEAs 
specimens after annealing at 750, 850, and 950 °C, and 
tested their microstructure and TDYS. To distinguish from 
the nitrogen-doped CoCrFeMnNi NSFCC-HEAs discussed 
in Sect. 4.3, these specimens merely annealed at 750, 850, 
and 950 °C are referred to as 0 N-750A, 0 N-850A, and 
0 N-950A, respectively. Microstructure showed that all 
specimens are a stable single-phase FCC solid solution 
[13]. The TEM-selected area diffraction pattern (SADP)

at grain boundaries and within grains after annealing at 
750 °C and 850 °C [13]. Figure  4a–c shows the com-

the calculation (Eq.  13) were listed in Tables  1, 3, and

CoCrFeMnNi FCC-HEAs matrix at room temperature are 
0.255 nm and 3.6 Å, respectively [13]. From Fig.  4a–c,

of CoCrFeMnNi FCC-HEAs of 750A, 850A, and 950A,

4 Discussion
4.1 Quantifying Mechanism Evolution
Understanding the contribution of each strengthening 
mechanism to the yield strength of NSFCC-HEAs is vital 
for selecting or improving material design and process-
ing, because the core of the contribution is the relationship 
between microstructure and yield strength. Quantified analy-
ses of the contribution of each strengthening mechanism to 
yield strength, especially their evolution with temperature 
are therefore conducted below based on the present model.
4.1.1 VCrFeNi NSFCC‑HEAs
Figure 5 shows the quantitative strengthening mechanism 
of VCrFeNi NSFCC-HEAs at different temperatures. The 
yield strength of VCrFeNi after annealing at 800 °C was 
calculated with d = 3 μm. Note that theoretical calculations 
indicated that the Orowan strengthening prevails in pre-
cipitation hardening and controlled the YSI of precipitation 
strengthening of VCrFeNi FCC-HEAs of 950A, 900A, and 
800A at different temperatures. The results are also consist-
ent with the recognition that the σ precipitates are hard and 
non-sheared [13, 68]. These figures indicate that the lattice 
friction stress controls the temperature dependence of yield 
strength of VCrFeNi NSFCC-HEAs. The yield strength at 
different temperatures is mainly dependent on the lattice 
friction stress and the grain boundary strengthening. But 
the precipitation strengthening shows remarkable potential 
for improving yield strength at extremely cryogenic tem -
peratures. Its values increase significantly with the decrease 
of annealed temperature, almost doubling from 950 to 750A 
at 77 K.

4.2 The Effect of Grain Size (d), Precipitate Size (r), 
and Precipitate Volume Fraction (f)
Taking the VCrFeNi (800A) NSFCC-HEAs as a sample, 
the influence of d , r, and f on the yield strength of HEAs 
are analyzed, as shown in Fig.  8a–c. The Figure indicates 
that data fluctuations of d have a significant influence on the 
final prediction while the influence of fluctuation of r or f is 
negligible, within the error bounds provided by the litera-
ture. The results further proved that the large fluctuation of d 
can be responsible for the significant difference between the 
prediction results and experimental data of VCrFeNi (800A) 
NSFCC-HEAs in Fig. 2d. Decreasing the d and r, as well as 
increasing the f can improve the yield strength of NSFCC-
HEAs at different temperatures. In particular, the increment 
of yield strength induced by these parameters (d and r) are 
more significant at extremely cryogenic temperatures. Mean-
while, precipitation strengthening shows a huge potential for 
improving the yield strength of NSFCC-HEAs at cryogenic 
temperatures. For instance, an increment reaching nearly 
500 MPa with decreasing r to 20 nm at 4.2 K.

4.3 The Scope of Application of the Model
Only requiring some physical parameters tested at room 
temperature, the present model showed great predictions on 
the temperature dependence of yield strength of NSFCC-
HEAs, especially at extremely cryogenic temperatures, as 
shown in Sect. 3. However, the model has some limitations. 
It cannot be used to predict the TDYS of NSFCC-HEAs 
prepared by the method of interstitial elements adding. This 
was determined by the core thinking of the model, which 
further considers the temperature-dependent precipitation 
strengthening based on the characterization of the TDYS 
of FCC-HEAs. However, it is known that the main con-
tribution of the interstitial elements addition to the TDYS 
of NSFCC-HEAs is not precipitation strengthening, but 
the new mechanisms such as interstitial elements-induced 
short-range order (SRO) strengthening Δ/u1D70ESRO(T) . The lat-
ter was suggested to have a great influence on the cryogenic 
yield strength of NSFCC-HEAs [14, 15]. For example, Soal 
et al. [14] found that the soluble boron-induced SRO led to 
the increased cryogenic yield strength of ∼ 210 MPa; Kli-
mova et al. [15] inferred that nitrogen element may induce 
SRO, and it might be a factor responsible for the significant 
yield strength increment of ∼570 MPa of the nitrogen-doped 
CoCrFeMnNi FCC-HEAs at 77 K. Unfortunately, the new

In addition to the main limitation analyzed above, the 
predictive accuracy of the present model can be influenced 
by the measurement methods of grain size or the grain shape 
and precipitate shape. The first one can cause the scatter of 
average grain size if adopting different measurement meth-

microstructure parameters are important for a better predic-

Despite some shortcomings, the present model has good 
functionality from predicting the cryogenic-temperature 
strength to quantitatively describing the evolution of various 
mechanisms with temperature, within the applicable range. 
According to building the quantitative relationship between 
microstructural parameters and TDYS, the model provides 
a new method for the study of NSFCC-HEAs. The custom-
ized cryogenic-temperature strength of NSFCC-HEAs is 
expected to achieve through a priori theoretical prediction 
by selecting the basic material parameters and the corre-
sponding processing technology.

In this study, a TDYS predictive model for NSFCC-HEAs 
was established by characterizing the evolution of main 
influence mechanisms with temperature. Only requiring 
some physical parameters that can be obtained from lit-

model can achieve a great prediction of yield strength 
for NSFCC-HEAs at wide temperatures, especially at 
extremely cryogenic temperatures. Except predicting,
