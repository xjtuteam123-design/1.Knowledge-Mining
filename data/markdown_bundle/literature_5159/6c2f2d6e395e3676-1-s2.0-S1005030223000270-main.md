Nb-Si based alloys have the greatest potential to replace nickel-based superalloys and become a new gen- 
eration ultra-high temperature structural material with service temperature of 120 0-140 0 °C due to their 
high melting point, high stiffness, low density, and attractive speciﬁc mechanical properties at elevated 
temperatures. This article reviewed the recent progress in the development of Nb-Si based refractory al- 
loys. Special attention was paid to the property characteristics, preparation techniques, alloying effects, 
oxidation resistance and fabrication of Nb-Si based alloy components. Thereafter, the trend of develop- 
ment in the future was forecasted, including: Optimization of the composition and microstructure of Nb- 
Si based alloys to achieve a balance of mechanical properties; Comprehensive properties evaluation for 
Nb-Si based alloys to accumulate suﬃcient data for engineering applications; Development of practical 
preparation technology for Nb-Si based alloy components with complex shapes; New multilayer compos- 
ite coatings for Nb-Si based alloy; Joining technology of Nb-Si alloy to itself and to superalloy.

1. Introduction 
With the rapid development of aerospace technology, the tem- 
perature and thrust-to-weight ratio of gas turbine engines are 
increasing, which puts higher requirements on the temperature- 
bearing capacity of turbine blade materials [1] . At present, the 
nickel-based single crystal superalloy widely used in gas turbine 
engines is close to its maximum operating temperature of about 
1150 °C (reaching 85% of the initial melting temperature of the 
nickel-based superalloy), which cannot meet the future needs of 
high thrust-to-weight ratio aero engines, so it is imminent to de- 
velop ultra-high temperature structural materials with higher ser- 
vice temperature [2] . 
Ceramics and ceramic-based composites, platinum group met- 
als, C/C composites and refractory metal silicides are the main 
candidates for structural materials that can be used at different 
temperature intervals above 1200 °C [3–6] . Among them, refrac- 
tory metal silicides have a temperature-bearing capacity (about 
1400 °C) between high-temperature alloys and ceramics. Also, re-

Generally, the γ-Nb 5 Si 3 phase occurs in Nb-Si alloys with Ti, Hf 
or Zr elements and it is considered metastable.

According to the Nb-Si binary phase diagram ( Fig. 2 ) [14] , a 
dual-phase alloy with Nb-based solid solution (Nb SS ) + silicide 
(Nb 5 Si 3 ) can be formed on the niobium-rich side where the Si con- 
tent does not exceed 37.5%. Within this composition range there 
is a broad dual-phase zone between Nb SS and Nb 5 Si 3 , which are 
connected by the eutectic isotherm at 1667 °C. The composition 
of Nb 5 Si 3 does not vary with the Si content and the solid solu- 
tion of Si in the niobium phase changes only slightly at tempera- 
tures above 1200 °C, indicating the feasibility of preparing a stable 
two-phase alloy, which is the most essential requirement for the 
long-term application at high temperatures. The Nb 5 Si 3 /Nb SS dual- 
phase composites, in which Nb 5 Si 3 provides the high-temperature 
strength and Nb SS supplies room-temperature fracture toughness, 
have better high-temperature strength than single-phase Nb 5 Si 3 , 
and meanwhile own room-temperature toughness to some extent. 
Therefore the Nb 5 Si 3 /Nb SS composites should exhibit a good bal- 
ance of mechanical properties and are regarded as one of the most 
promising new-generation ultra-high-temperature structural mate- 
rials with a service temperature of 120 0-140 0 °C [ 8 , 9 , 15 ]. 
The potential of Nb-Si alloys as high-temperature structural ma- 
terials was ﬁrst recognized more than 30 years ago, when the US 
Air Force Laboratory launched the Integrated High Performance 
Turbine Engine Technology (IHPTET) initiative in 1985, with the 
goal for developing materials that could be used above 1400 °C 
to replace Ni-based superalloys for engines with high thrust-to- 
weight ratio. This has led to deep research into Nb-Si based alloys 
[17] . 
In this paper, the recent effort s and advances in the study of 
the preparation techniques, alloying effects, microstructures and 
mechanical properties, oxidation resistance at high temperatures 
and component fabrication for Nb-Si ultra-high temperature struc- 
tural materials were reviewed, and on this basis an outlook on the 
development directions of Nb-Si alloys is proposed for the future 
work. 
2. Property characteristics of Nb-Si based alloys 
As ultra-high temperature structural materials, Nb-Si based al- 
loys are easier to meet the requirements of high-temperature 
strength. Nb-Si based alloys generally exhibit excellent high- 
temperature strength. Fig. 3(a) shows the strength comparison be- 
tween Nb-Si alloys and second-generation Ni-based single crys- 
tal superalloy (2GSX) [18] . Room-temperature tensile strengths of 
∼830 MPa were obtained by MASC alloy (Nb-16Si-25Ti-8Hf-2Cr- 
2Al) and the compressive strength of Nb-Si alloy C (a Nb-Si-Ti- 
Hf-Cr-Al-Ge-Fe-Zn-W-Mo multi-component alloy developed by GE 
company [ 18 , 19 ]) is maintained at around 1700 MPa from room 
temperature to 800 °C, about 520 MPa at 1200 °C which is more 
than twice that of a typical 2GSX nickel-based superalloy, and 
about 310 MPa at 1350 °C. Furthermore, as show in Fig. 3(b) , The 
speciﬁc strength of Nb-Si alloy at room temperature to 1100 °C is 
also signiﬁcantly higher than that of other materials using at high 
temperatures [18] . Although most Nb-Si based alloys have good 
room-temperature compressive strength, their tensile strength is 
very low. This may be caused by holes and defects within the ma- 
terial. 
Fracture toughness ( K Q ) at room temperature is another im- 
portant indicator to evaluate the performance of high-temperature 
structural materials. When the fracture toughness exceeds 20 MPa 
m 1/2 , it is generally considered that the material can meet the 
design requirements for processing and assembly [20] . Nb-Si al- 
loys mainly rely on the ductile phase (Nb SS ) toughening mecha- 
nism to improve the toughness. However, compared with the ex- 
cellent high-temperature strength, the room-temperature fracture 
toughness of Nb-Si alloy is slightly insuﬃcient. The fracture mech- 
anism of Nb-Si alloy at room temperature is brittle cleavage frac- 
ture, but at 1200 °C or higher temperature, the fracture behavior 
of the alloys changes from cleavage to ductile fracture. Compared 
with the extremely low room-temperature fracture toughness ( < 3 
MPa m 1/2 ) of single-phase Nb 5 Si 3 intermetallic, the toughness of 
Nb/Nb 5 Si 3 composites is signiﬁcantly improved. The Nb-16Si-25Ti- 
8Hf-2Cr-2Al hexagonal alloy prepared by Czochralski directional 
solidiﬁcation has a K Q of up to 23 MPa m 1/2 , while the compres- 
sive strength at 1200 °C can still be maintained at about 400 MPa 
[15] . Decreasing the content of Ti and Hf can also keep the K Q at 
15-22 MPa m 1/2 , while the compressive strength at 1250 °C can be 
increased to above 450 MPa.

In addition, aero-engine blades also require materials with ex- 
cellent high and low cycle fatigue properties and resistance to fa- 
tigue crack growth. Fig. 5 compares fatigue data for Nb-Si alloys, 
single-phase Nb SS , Nb-10Si, and oriented MASC alloys [15] . The 
comparison shows that the fatigue behavior of the Nb-Si alloy is 
close to the metallic type, and the Paris slope of the alloy is slightly 
higher than that of single-phase Nb SS , but compared with the ce- 
ramic (Paris slope greater than 60), its Paris slope is closer to Ni- 
based superalloys. 
In terms of antioxidant properties, two oxidation-resistance 
goals have been established for Nb silicide composites. The ﬁrst, 
short-term goal corresponds to a loss of < 200 μm in 10 h of test 
stand exposure at 1370 °C, the second, long-term goal corresponds 
to a loss of < 25 μm in 100 h at 1315 °C. The basis for these ox- 
idation goals is achieving the same oxidation life at 1315 °C that 
2GSX superalloys currently display at 1150 °C [15] . Fig. 6 shows the 
typical cyclic oxidation performance for uncoated Nb-silicide-based 
composites as described by the material loss as a function of tem- 
perature. The present oxidation behavior satisﬁes the short-term 
goal of a test engine mission with calculated losses of 100-125 μm, 
compared with a tolerant 190 μm of material loss. However, the 
long-term goal requires another tenfold reduction in material loss 
upon the high-temperature oxidation. 
3. Preparation techniques of Nb-Si based alloys 
It is well known that the preparation process has an impor- 
tant inﬂuence on the microstructure and properties of materials. 
With the gradual deepening of the research on Nb-Si based al- 
loys, the preparation techniques are also developing. As can be 
seen from the development history of superalloys, since the late 
1950s, the improvement of superalloy properties often depends 
less on the adjustment of composition and more on the contin- 
uous improvement of preparation techniques. However, due to the 
high melting point, poor oxidation resistance at high temperature 
and high ductile-brittle transition temperature of Nb-Si based al- 
loys, the preparation of the alloys often meets some diﬃculties, 
which limits their application. In the past decades, Nb-Si based al- 
loys have been prepared mainly by vacuum arc melting, directional 
solidiﬁcation, powder metallurgy, etc.

3.1. Vacuum arc melting 
Vacuum arc melting is currently the most widely used method 
for preparing the series of Nb-Si alloys [ 24 , 25 ]. Nb-Si alloy spec- 
imens were mostly prepared by vacuum non-consumable arc 
melting, which is characterized by simple and low-cost process. 
The Nb-Si based alloy prepared by this method has less materi- 
als loss, high purity and accurate composition. However, in this 
case, the alloy ingots are generally small in size ( Fig. 7 ), have 
poor microstructure uniformity, and are prone to defects such 
as shrinkage, making them unsuitable for large-scale engineering 
applications [26] . 
In order to meet the demand for the preparation of large-size 
Nb-Si alloy ingots, vacuum self-consumption arc melting technol- 
ogy has been applied to the preparation of Nb-Si-based alloys: For 
the ﬁrst step, to process the isostatic vacuum sintered raw ma- 
terials including Nb, Ti, Si etc. into self-consumption electrodes; 
For the second step, to remelt the self-consumption electrodes by 
vacuum self-consumption to obtain Nb-Si based alloys ingots. This 
process is more likely to introduce impurities during the melting 
and thus makes it more diﬃcult to control the composition homo- 
geneity and accuracy, resulting in poor repeatability of the melting 
[ 27 , 28 ].

3.2. Directional solidiﬁcation 
Directional solidiﬁcation (DS) is a casting process that makes 
the metal or alloy grow in a speciﬁc crystallographic direction. 
By adjusting the process parameters of DS, the two phases of 
the alloys exhibit alternating directional arrangement to elimi- 
nate transverse grain boundaries, segregation and defects at grain 
boundaries, which can suppress crack initiation of the alloy un- 
der high temperature and thus substantially improve the creep 
properties [29] . Up to now, the two main types of directional so- 
lidiﬁcation methods for fabricating Nb-Si based alloys are ﬂoat- 
ing zone melting directional solidiﬁcation and crucible directional 
solidiﬁcation. 
Floating zone melting directional solidiﬁcation uses the surface 
tension and gravity balance of the melt itself to maintain the sta- 
bility of the melting, which can avoid the pollution of crucible to 
the alloy melt. According to the heating methods, ﬂoating zone 
melting directional solidiﬁcation can be divided into optical ﬂoat- 
ing zone melting (OFZ) [30–32] , electron beam ﬂoating zone melt- 
ing (EBFZ) [33] , and induction ﬂoating melting (IFL) [ 34 , 35 ]. Nb-Si 
based alloys with directional microstructure were prepared with 
OFZ by Beijing Institute of Aeronautical Materials (BIAM) [36] and 
Beihang University [37] . However, this method has small temper- 
ature gradient and shows slow solidiﬁcation eﬃciency, resulting 
in coarse microstructure within the prepared alloys. Guo et al. 
[33] prepared Nb-Si-Ti-Hf-Al alloy through EBFZ and pointed out 
that increasing the drawing speed could reﬁne the microstructure 
and increase the content of eutectic. Kim et al. [34] prepared Nb- 
Si-Mo ternary alloy with directional lamellar structure by IFL. Al- 
though the ﬂoating zone melting technique has signiﬁcant advan- 
tages in avoiding melt contamination, it is diﬃcult to prepare spec- 
imens with large sizes and complex shapes ( Fig. 9(a) ). 
In view of the above problems, researchers began to explore 
crucible directional solidiﬁcation processes, such as cold crucible 
directional solidiﬁcation and crucible integral directional solidiﬁ- 
cation [38–40] . Fig. 9(b) presents the large-sized Nb-Si alloy sam- 
ples prepared by cold crucible directional solidiﬁcation technology

The drawing rate is the most important processing parameter in 
the directional solidiﬁcation process [44–46] . Changing the draw- 
ing rate directly affects the morphological characteristics, growth 
mode and preferred crystal orientation characteristics of the di- 
rectional solidiﬁcation alloys. Guo et al. [47] studied the effect of 
pulling rate on the microstructure of directionally solidiﬁed Nb- 
22Ti-16Si-6Cr-4Al-3Hf alloy. As shown in Fig. 11 , with the increase 
of the pulling rate, the number of eutectic cells increases, and the 
average diameter of the eutectic cells and the interlamellar spac- 
ing of the eutectic cells decrease. When the pulling rate is low (for 
example 2.5 μm/s), the thickness of the Nb SS /Nb 5 Si 3 two-phase 
layer is not uniform, showing only slight coupled growth charac- 
teristics. With the increase of the pulling rate, the two-phase cou- 
pled growth characteristics are gradually enhanced. In particular, 
when the pulling rate reached 100 μm/s, the Nb SS /Nb 5 Si 3 two- 
phase layer showed obvious coupled growth characteristics.

3.4. Additive manufacturing (AM) 
The manufacture of complex-shaped components with is still 
a great challenge for the application of Nb-Si based alloys. Laser 
melting deposition (LMD) technique uses high energy laser beam 
to melt the metal powder and deposit layer by layer as shown 
in Fig. 14 . Near net shape components can be produced by the 
LMD manufacturing process from a computer-aided design model 
[ 63 , 64 ], and the LMD has become the most promising fabrica- 
tion technique for Nb-Si based alloys, showing the following ad- 
vantages: (1) elimination of the composition segregation, uneven 
structure and coarse grains caused by traditional casting technique; 
(2) non-contact processing between the laser and the raw material 
without requiring crucible constraints, and thus no contamination 
of highly active Nb-Si based alloy melts by electrodes or crucibles; 
(3) high laser beam energy density and convenience for the prepa- 
ration of high-melting Nb-Si based alloys. 
Dicks et al. [65] prepared the alloys with the composition of 
Nb-22Si-26Ti-6Cr-3Hf-2Al (at.%) by laser directly forming using 
pre-alloyed powder. As shown in Fig. 15 , the microstructure of the 
as-deposited alloy consisted of bcc niobium, solid-solution (Nb SS )

3.5. Other preparation processes 
Besides the above preparation techniques, physical vapor depo- 
sition (PVD) [72] , shock wave synthesis (SWS) [73] , hot extrusion 
(HE) [74] and other methods are also used to prepare Nb-Si based 
alloy specimens. 
Through PVD or magnetron sputtering methods, Nb SS and Nb- 
silicide can be alternately deposited on the substrate to form Nb-Si 
micro-laminated materials (see Fig. 17(a) ). The thickness and vol- 
ume fraction of each phase is controlled by adjusting the deposi- 
tion time and deposition quantity. However, because the PVD eﬃ- 
ciency is too low to prepare samples with a thickness of more than 
1 mm. Gavens et al. [ 75 , 76 ] prepared Nb/Nb 5 Si 3 laminated mate- 
rials by magnetron sputtering method and studied the microstruc- 
ture, thermal stability and residual stress after deformation of the 
alloys. A thin plate-like Nb-Ti-Hf-Si laminate material was prepared 
by GE company, with a size of ϕ500 mm × 1 mm by electron 
beam PVD [74] . 
The SWS method can be applied to the synthesis of silicides 
[77] . Its reaction range expands with the increase of impact en- 
ergy, temperature and reaction enthalpy. Meyers et al. [78] pro- 
posed a self-sustaining mechanism for forming Nb-Si-based com- 
pounds synthesized by shock waves. The heat generated by the re- 
action is suﬃcient to melt niobium along the interface facilitating 
both the expulsion of the NbSi 2 nodules into the liquid Si and the 
generation of fresh Nb interface for further reaction. In addition, 
the dissolved Nb enriches the surrounding Si liquid, promoting re- 
action formation of more NbSi 2 . 
Due to the relatively coarse solidiﬁcation structure of Nb-Si al- 
loys, the Nb-silicide with the morphology of sharp corners and ir- 
regular greatly damages the mechanical properties at room tem- 
perature and high temperature. Hot extrusion of the Nb-Si alloy 
could produce a strip-like microstructure parallel to the extrusion 
direction ( Fig. 17(b) ), and thus the mechanical properties in this di- 
rection could be improved [74] . An equiaxed microstructure can be 
formed within the Nb-Si alloys by isothermal forging. Although no 
directional microstructure is formed, the microstructure is remark- 
ably reﬁned resulting in the improvement of the fracture tough- 
ness at room temperature. 
3.6. Effect of preparation technique on mechanical property of Nb-Si 
based alloys 
3.6.1. Fracture toughness at room temperature 
In general, it is believed that the fracture toughness value of the 
material should exceed the threshold value of 20 MPa m 1/2 to meet 
the design requirements of processing and assembly. Nb-Si based 
alloys mainly rely on Nb SS to improve their fracture toughness at 
room temperature so that the volume fraction and microstructure 
of Nb SS greatly affect the room temperature toughness. 
Fig. 18 shows the room temperature fracture toughness of Nb-Si 
based alloys prepared by different processes. It is clear that differ- 
ent preparation techniques can affect the room temperature frac- 
ture toughness of Nb-Si alloys signiﬁcantly. The fracture toughness 
of arc-melted Nb-10Si alloy is 8.9-9.5 MPa m 1/2 , and it is slightly 
improved after heat treatment at 1500 °C/100 h. After hot extru- 
sion the fracture toughness of the alloy is obviously elevated to 
about 16.50 MPa m 1/2 , and can be even enhanced further to over 
20 MPa m 1/2 after heat treatment at 1500 °C/100 h [ 20 , 79 ]. The 
main reason for this improvement is that after heat treatment the 
metastable Nb 3 Si phase in the microstructure is decomposed into 
Nb SS and Nb 5 Si 3 , and the volume content of the ductile phase Nb SS 
increases [80] . After hot extrusion, the primary Nb SS phase is de- 
composed along the extrusion direction. Enough elongation makes 
the crack propagation plane parallel to the direction of the elon- 
gated Nb SS phase, and the crack propagation distance increases so 
that the fracture toughness is improved. The room temperature 
fracture toughness of Nb-16Si alloy prepared by hot-pressing sin- 
tering and SPS is signiﬁcantly higher than that of arc-melting alloy 
[ 9 , 20 , 52 ]. This is because the microstructure of hot-pressing sin- 
tering or SPS is very ﬁne releasing the stress concentration at the 
grain boundary and this correspondingly limit the formation and 
prorogation of cracks to toughen the alloy. 
For the OFZ directional solidiﬁcation Nb-22Ti-16Si-7Cr-3Al-3Ta- 
2Hf-0.1Ho alloy, its room temperature fracture toughness is higher 
than that of the arc melting alloy [82] . It is known that the so- 
lidiﬁcation rate of arc melting is very fast, and it is easy to gen- 
erate large internal stress in the alloy, while the OFZ solidiﬁca- 
tion rate of the alloy is slow during the directional solidiﬁcation 
process, which greatly reduces the solidiﬁcation stress, thereby 
inhibiting the stress-accelerated crack propagation process. Addi- 
tionally, compared with the equiaxed Nb SS , the Nb SS with direc- 
tional arrangement provides higher crack propagation resistance.

4.1. Ti and Hf alloying 
In general, the alloying elements such as Ti, Al, Hf, Zr, Mo, and 
W have a large solid solubility in Nb SS , and the solid solution of 
these alloys usually increases the yield strength of the Nb-Si alloys 
but damages the ductility of Nb SS . Available studies showed that 
the elements that can maintain a stable brittle transition temper- 
ature in the Nb SS phase are Ti and Hf. Bewlay et al. [89] observed 
the typical microstructures of longitudinal and transverse sections 
for the DS Nb-27Ti-16Si composite. The structure consisted of non- 
faceted Nb(Ti, Si) dendrites together with large-scale faceted (Nb, 
Ti) 3 Si dendrites; the longitudinal section shows reasonable align- 
ment of both non-faceted metal dendrites and faceted needle-like 
dendrites with the growth direction. Z. Li et al. [90] dealed with 
the Nb-Si based alloys developed by the hot-pressing of Nb-Si-Ti 
ternary powder mixtures with a ﬁxed Ti addition (6 at.%) and Si 
content ranging from hypoeutectic (11 at.%) to near-eutectic com- 
positions (18 at.%). The volume fraction of silicides increased with 
increasing Si content, and most of the Ti atoms dissolved into the 
niobium silicides to form (Nb, Ti) 5 Si 3 solid solutions instead of bi- 
nary titanium silicides. Wang et al. [91] demonstrated that when 
adding 20 at.% Ti into the Nb-20Si-6Mo alloy, Ti-rich Nb 5 Si 3 phase 
was formed in the matrix, in which the silicide morphology be- 
came more square and meanwhile more volume of eutectic was 
formed. It seemed that the Ti addition caused greater segrega- 
tion and promoted the formation of the eutectic microstructure. 
The beneﬁcial effect of Ti addition on enhancing the fracture resis- 
tance of Nb-based in-situ composites could be attributed to the de- 
creased Peierls-Nabarro barrier energy, and thus the increased dis- 
location mobility and plastic ﬂow in the solid solution phase [92] . 
The fracture behavior consists of three stages: (1) the brittle Nb 5 Si 3 
phase initiates a number of microcracks; (2) the tip of the microc- 
rack encounters a solid solution, and the bridging occurs between 
the main crack and some microcracks; (3) the solid solution phase 
is plastically stretched and eventually destroyed. The ratio of γs 
(surface energy) to U P-N (P-N theoretical energy barrier) increases 
with increasing Ti content. Ti is one of the most widely alloying 
elements into Nb-Si based alloys, and its content within the range 
of 20 at.%-35 at.% is considered to be the most important way to 
improve the alloy plasticity. 
Tian et al. [93] pointed out that the Nb-16Si- x Hf ( x = 0,1,3,7) al- 
loys were mainly composed of Nb SS and Nb 3 Si. Hf element had 
no effect on the phase composition but on the microstructure. As 
shown in Fig. 24 , when the content of Hf was 0, 1 at.%, 3 at.%, 
respectively, the eutectic of the alloy was obviously reﬁned with 
the Hf content increasing, and the size of the primary Nb SS phase 
became smaller and its volume content increased. When the Hf 
content reached 7 at.%, the eutectic structure disappeared, mainly 
because the Hf addition increased the mixing entropy of the alloy, 
which led to a decrease in the Gibbs free energy G and ultimately 
to an increase of the solid solution phase and the disappearance 
of the Nbss/Nb 3 Si eutectic structure. With the increase of element 
Hf content the room-temperature fracture toughness K Q value in- 
creased by 34% to 8.5 MPa m 1/2 . The reasons can be attributed to 
the low Peierls-Nabarro energy barrier and the increased primary 
phase. With the increase of primary Nbss phase, cracks will deﬂect 
and bridge when encountering primary Nbss phase. In addition, el- 
ement Hf lowered the Peierls-Nabarro energy barrier and promotes 
more propagation at the crack tip. The fracture mode also changed 
from brittle cleavage fracture to mixed cleavage fracture mode of 
transgranular or intergranular. Wang et al. [91] concluded that sim- 
ilarly for the Nb-Si-Mo alloy with the addition of 3 at.% Hf, the 
eutectic structure became ﬁner and the silicides changed from the 
β-Nb 5 Si 3 to α-Nb 5 Si 3 . Hf, similar to the Ti element, is more likely 
to be enriched in the eutectic structure, and this would increase 
the segregation and promote the formation of eutectic structure. 
Grammenos et al. [94] also found the same transformation when 
adding 5 at.% Hf to Nb-Si-Mo-W system alloys, indicating that a 
small amount of Hf addition in Nb-Si-based alloys could inhibit the 
formation of β-Nb 5 Si 3 phase and stabilize α-Nb 5 Si 3 phase. Zhao 
et al. [95] found that (Nb, Hf) 5 Si 3 distributed around (Nb, Hf) 5 Si 3 
in the Nb-16Si-15Hf alloy, and the Hf could promote the coupling 
growth of (Nb, Hf) 3 Si and Nb SS , thereby forming a better eutec- 
tic layered structure. The addition of 4 at.% Hf would inhibit the 
α-(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 phase in the Nb-22Ti-16Si-3Al-2B-4Hf alloy but pro- 
mote the formation of γ-(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 phase [96] . When the Hf con- 
tent reached 8 at.%, α-(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 was completely suppressed.

5. Oxidation resistance of Nb-Si based alloys and the coatings 
5.1. Effect of alloying elements on oxidation resistance of Nb-Si alloys 
A large number of alloying elements have been proved to in- 
crease the oxidation resistance of Nb-Si based alloys. In general, 
these elements can improve the oxidation resistance in the follow- 
ing ways: providing a glass oxide ﬁlm with a repairing effect, form- 
ing appropriate phases in Nb-Si based alloys with medium thermal 
expansion coeﬃcients between the alloy and the oxide layer and 
thus improving the interface adhesion, cooperating with Nb atoms 
to produce better protective oxidation products. 
In various Nb-Si based alloy systems, titanium and chromium 
are common elements to increase the oxidation resistance [141–
143] . Ti can signiﬁcantly improve the oxidation resistance of Nb- 
Si-Al alloys [144–146] , which is believed to be attributed to an im- 
proved oxidation in Nb solid solutions (Nb SS ) and the fact that Ti 
can produce more protective oxide at the surface [ 144 , 146 ]. It is 
proved that compared with Nb, Cr is easier to combine with O 
and produce Cr 2 O 3 which is beneﬁcial for the stability of the oxide 
scale [ 96 , 147-149 ]. A certain content of Cr in Nb-Si based alloys in- 
troduces Cr 2 Nb phases which can increase the oxidation resistance 
[ 105 , 138-140 ]. Zhou et al. [150] reported the oxidation character-

Nb-Si based alloys for decades and developed various coatings. The 
protection coatings of Nb-Si based alloys can be classiﬁed as fol- 
lows: aluminide coatings, oxide coatings, precious metal coatings 
and silicide coatings.

in a static oxidation atmosphere lower than 1500 °C [163–166] . 
However, due to the poor high-temperature mechanical properties 
of aluminide coatings, it is easy to crack or even peel off dur-

ing in the failure of aluminide coatings. Concerning oxide coat- 
ings, they have excellent high-temperature oxidation resistance 
and have been successfully applied to hot sections of aeroengine 
[167] . However, due to the large difference in thermal expansion 
coeﬃcient between the oxide coating and the Nb-Si based alloy, 
it is easy to peel off when subjected to thermal shock. Precious 
metal coatings have good oxidation resistance and ductility, so it 
can resist the elastic and plastic deformation of the matrix mate- 
rial and the creep deformation at high temperatures [ 168 , 169 ]. But 
the high cost of precious metals restricts their applications. As for 
silicide coatings, they produce a glassy SiO 2 protective ﬁlm during 
the oxidation at high temperatures, which continuously and tightly 
covers the silicide coating and effectively protect the Nb-Si based 
alloys. In addition, the glassy SiO 2 at high temperatures can effec- 
tively make up for the cracks and holes of the coating and pro- 
vide so-called self-healing repair [170–172] . Several types of sili- 
cide coating systems are described below. 
Vishwannadh et al. [173] successfully deposited NbSi 2 coat- 
ing on the surface of niobium alloy by embedding inﬁltration, as 
shown in Fig. 33 . SiO 2 and Nb 2 O 5 were formed at the surface dur- 
ing the oxidation at 1200 °C, and Nb 5 Si 3 intermediate layer was 
formed between the NbSi 2 coating and the matrix. SiO 2 can ef- 
fectively improve the oxidation resistance of the coating at high 
temperature, whereas Nb 5 Si 3 layer reduced the difference in ther- 
mal expansion coeﬃcient between niobium alloy matrix and NbSi 2 
coating, and thus improving the thermal shock resistance of the 
coating. Alam et al. [174] deposited NbSi 2 coating on niobium alloy 
by embedding inﬁltration. The coating could resist oxidation for up 
to 50 h at 1100 °C and 9 h at 1300 °C, and could be subjected to 
the thermal cycle oxidation test from room temperature to 1100 °C 
for 20 cycles and to 1300 °C for 10 ones respectively. Guo et al. 
[175–178] systematically studied the effect of the addition of Al, 
Zr, Y 2 O 3 and Ce on the oxidation resistance of NbSi 2 coating. It ap- 
peared that these additions could improve the adhesion between 
the coating and the substrate, and signiﬁcantly improved the adhe- 
sion between the surface oxide ﬁlm and NbSi 2 coating. A number 
of studies also focused on the protection of Si-Cr-Ti (Fe) coatings 
on Nb-Si based alloys, and these coatings could provide an ade- 
quate oxidation resistance below 1400 °C [ 163 , 179 , 180 ].

MoSi 2 coating. The main problem of these coatings is the pesting 
oxidation. Mo and Si are oxidized to MoO 3 and SiO 2 during the 
oxidation at low temperatures. Since MoO 3 exists in the oxide ﬁlm 
at low temperature, the continuous SiO 2 protective ﬁlm cannot be 
formed at the outermost surface of the coating. Meanwhile, the 
oxide produces concentration of stress due to volume expansion 
at grain boundaries, and it is easy to occur defects and cracks and 
even peeling and fragmentation. For these researchers have studied 
the modiﬁcation of the coating by alloying elements and incorpo- 
rating ceramic particles. The pesting oxidation can be suppressed 
by addition of Al. The combination of Al and MoSi 2 to form Mo(Si, 
Al) 2 phases can signiﬁcantly enhance the toughness of MoSi 2 coat- 
ings and reduce the crack of coatings. During the oxidation of 
Mo(Si, Al) 2 , Al is ﬁrstly oxidized to form a continuous and dense 
Al 2 O 3 protective ﬁlm. As the oxidation proceeds, Al 2 O 3 ﬁnally con-

the high-temperature ﬂuidity just like SiO 2 , and it can provide bet- 
ter self-healing ability and strength [181–187] . Additionally, ele-

7. Outlook 
7.1. Optimization of Nb-Si alloy composition using high-entropy alloy 
design concept 
With the conﬂicting microstructure requirements for high- 
temperature strength and room-temperature plastic toughness of 
Nb-Si based alloys, it is still a great challenge to match the prop- 
erty balance. Alloying should be the effective means to solve the 
problem. However, so far, most of the investigations focused on the 
effects of one or two alloying elements (such as Ti, Al, Cr etc.) on 
the microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb-Si based al- 
loys. Obviously, the understanding of coupling effect of multiple 
elements on Nb-Si based alloys was highly insuﬃcient [207] . 
High-entropy alloys (HEA) consist of ﬁve or more elements in 
equal or approximately equal amounts [208–211] , and they ex- 
hibit excellent properties in terms of mechanical strength, hard- 
ness, toughness, wear resistance, corrosion resistance, thermal re- 
sistance and high-temperature resistance [212–214] . It is worth 
noting that the fracture toughness of HEAs is signiﬁcantly better 
than that of other alloys [ 215 , 216 ]. For example, the room tem- 
perature impact energy of the AlCoCrFeNi based HEAs can reach 
more than 400 J, which is higher than that of most pure met- 
als and alloys [217] . Furthermore, the fracture toughness of CoCr- 
FeNiMn based HEAs is greater than 200 MPa m 1/2 , which is the 
highest level of known materials [218] . These provide an approach 
for improving the fracture toughness of Nb-Si alloys. In addition, 
HEAs also have excellent properties in terms of high-temperature 
strength, thermal stability, high-temperature oxidation resistance, 
high-temperature softening resistance, etc. [219–221] . 
It is noticeable that the main alloying elements Ti, Zr, Hf, Ta, 
Al, Mo, V, etc. in the current Nb-Si alloys highly overlap with the 
common elements in the known refractory HEA systems [222–
225] , Then, it is believed that the alloy composition design con- 
cept of HEAs can be introduced to Nb-Si based alloys for the sub- 
stantial improvement in comprehensive properties [ 226–229,264 ]. 
In other words, the study of Nb 5 Si 3 particle-reinforced refractory 
HEAs should be a promising research direction in this ﬁeld. How- 
ever, the introduction of high entropy alloy design concept leads 
to a huge space for Nb-Si alloy composition optimization. High 
throughput searching and machine learning is an important ap- 
proach to optimize the composition of Nb-Si alloy [230–233] . 
7.2. Development of microstructure control method for Nb-Si based 
alloy 
Microstructure optimization is another technical approach to 
improve the strength at high temperature and toughness at room 
temperature of Nb-Si alloy [ 234 , 235 ]. The ductile phase Nb SS in 
Nb-Si alloys can play a toughening role in various ways such as 
crack deﬂection, bridging, and arresting, but it is easy to lead 
to cleavage fracture at high strain rates or at low temperatures 
[ 236 , 237 ]. Increasing the volume fraction of the ductile phase, im- 
proving the strength and toughness of the ductile phase, mean- 
while, reducing the restraint degree of the brittle matrix to the 
plastic toughening phase are effective ways to improve the fracture 
toughness of Nb-Si alloys at room temperature [93] . 
A large number of studies have shown that the silicides with 
ﬁne-size distribution on the continuous Nb SS matrix can effec- 
tively improve the plasticity and toughness of Nb-Si based alloys 
at room temperature, and could change the fracture mechanism 
from brittle cleavage fracture to dimple fracture [ 238 , 239 ]. For ex- 
ample, adding rare earth element Dy to Nb-Si alloy would reﬁne 
the silicide size, which is believed to be beneﬁcial to both the me- 
chanical strength and plasticity [115] . The Nb-16Si-2Fe alloy pre- 
pared by reactive hot pressing sintering shows an average phase

7.3. Comprehensive properties evaluation for Nb-Si based alloys to 
accumulate suﬃcient data for engineering applications 
The service environment of aero-engine turbine blades is ex- 
tremely harsh, so that they need to be used at high stress in a 
high-temperature environment with long life and reliability [2] . 
The Nb-Si based alloys have to achieve a balance of proper- 
ties in terms of room-temperature strength and toughness, high- 
temperature strength, creep, fatigue properties, oxidation resis- 
tance and corrosion resistance to meet the multi-requirement of 
aero-engine blades [243] . After years of exploration and develop- 
ment, the comprehensive properties of Nb-Si-based alloys have 
been greatly improved. From the current property level, high- 
temperature strength and room-temperature toughness are no 
longer the greatest obstacles to the engineering application of Nb- 
Si based alloys. At present, the main problem is becoming how 
to improve the oxidation resistance, creep and fatigue properties 
of Nb-Si based alloys while maintaining the enough strength and 
toughness level. 
The room-temperature fracture toughness of directionally so- 
lidiﬁed Nb-Si based alloys has reached 15-28 MPa m 1/2 , and the 
strength at 1200 °C is twice than that of the typical 2GSX, and 
the creep properties under the stress of less than 100 MPa are at 
the same level with 2GSX. However, the room-temperature elon- 
gation ( A < 0.5%) of the Nb-Si based alloys is extremely lower than 
that of 2GSX [15] . Attempts were made in Japan aimed to de- 
velop high-melting Nb-Si-based alloys with W and Mo elements 
for gas turbine blades for improving thermal eﬃciency [ 24 , 25 , 85 ], 
with expected goals of speciﬁc strength at 1500 °C ≥50 MPa/(kg 
m −3 ), 100 h creep strength ≥150 MPa and 104 h oxidation loss 
≤250 μm. 
In general, the properties evaluation of Nb-Si based alloys in the 
past years mainly focused on room-temperature fracture tough- 
ness, high-temperature strength and high-temperature oxidation 
resistance. However, so far, the property data for creep and fatigue 
are extremely insuﬃcient. The research concerning the fatigue of 
intermetallic compound system is just beginning. The room tem- 
perature fatigue test results showed that the fatigue crack growth 
threshold of Nb-Si alloy C ranged from 7 to 11 MPa m 1/2 , and 
the Paris slopes from 7 to 12 ( Fig. 5 ) [21] . In contrast, the Parris 
slope of Ni-based superalloy is generally about 5, indicating that 
the fatigue properties of Nb-Si alloy must be improved by adjust- 
ing the alloy composition and preparation process in the future. 
Properties evaluation in all aspects of Nb-Si based alloys should be 
carried out as soon as possible to accumulate suﬃcient properties 
data for engineering applications and provide a technical basis for 
Nb-Si aero-engine blades. In addition, although coatings with ex- 
cellent protective oxidation resistance properties for Nb-Si based 
alloys have been developed, the effect of coatings on the mechan- 
ical properties of Nb-Si alloy substrates also needs to be further 
evaluated. 
7.4. Development of practical preparation technology for Nb-Si based 
alloy component with complex shape 
Whether the Nb-Si based alloys can replace nickel-based sin- 
gle crystal superalloys, to some extent, relies on whether they 
can be prepared into hollow-structure blades. But the current 
research shows that it is still diﬃcult to ﬁnd a suitable shell 
and core for precision casting of high-melting-point Nb-Si al- 
loy hollow blades, and for this aspect new progress is highly 
expected. 
Besides, additive manufacturing uses a high-energy laser beam 
or electron beam to melt metal powder layer-by-layer to com- 
plete manufacturing of complex components with "near-net- 
shape" [ 244 , 245 ]. Therefore, it is one of the most promising prepa- 
ration technologies for Nb-Si based alloys component with com- 
plex shapes. However, the brittleness of the Nb-Si-based alloy and 
the large stresses caused by the high cooling rate during the AM 
process make the Nb-Si alloy structure with larger size easily 
cracked. Therefore, it is still necessary to conduct in-depth explo- 
ration and research on the Nb-Si alloy AM process together with 
the optimization of the chemical composition and the microstruc- 
ture of the alloy. 
There are two main ways to improve the performance of the 
AM process of Nb-Si alloys. Firstly, through alloying design to de- 
crease the intrinsic brittleness of the Nb-Si alloys, so that the alloys 
could exhibit a certain damage tolerance to suppress the initiation 
of microcracks during the AM process. Secondly, to reduce ther- 
mal stresses during the AM process through some methods such 
as preheating [ 246 , 247 ]. It should be pointed out that the electron 
beam selective melting technology can realize the preheating up 
to about 10 0 0 °C during the fabrication process [248–250] , which 
is a better way for Nb-Si alloys additive manufacturing, but it still 
needs to overcome the problems of forming accuracy and surface 
quality. 
7.5. New multilayer composite coatings for Nb-Si based alloys 
With the rapid development of materials for the hot sec- 
tions in the aerospace ﬁeld, new requirements of performances 
have been put forward for high-temperature protective coatings, 
such as oxidation resistance at ultra-high temperatures, resistance 
against gas scour and thermal expansion coeﬃcient mismatch- 
ing with the matrix. At present, the traditional coatings can- 
not meet the strict service requirement. Silicide coatings have 
been widely applied to protect refractory metals from oxida- 
tion because these coatings can form SiO 2 glass ﬁlms with self- 
healing ability at high temperatures [ 251 , 252 ]. At present, the 
service temperature of NbSi 2 coating developed for Nb-Si based 
superalloy generally does not exceed 1300 °C, and the Si-Cr- 
Ti(Fe) coatings which have been successfully applied to hot sec- 
tions, cannot be used for a long time at ultra-high temperature 
[ 180 , 253 ]. 
Therefore, based on evaluation of the advantages and disadvan- 
tages of various coatings, the MoSi 2 coating can be identiﬁed as 
the protective coating with great potentials at high temperatures 
to refractory metals [ 254 , 255 ]. The MoSi 2 coating shows excellent 
oxidation resistance at high temperatures but also the pesting phe- 
nomenon at the medium temperature between 40 0–80 0 °C. On 
the one hand, to improve the oxidation resistance of the MoSi 2 
coatings at the medium temperatures through the optimization of 
compositions will be a research hotspot in this ﬁeld. On the other 
hand, to design scientiﬁc coating structures with improved prepa- 
ration process, and to search for new multilayer composite coat- 
ings should be the development tendency of silicide coatings in 
the future.

7.6. The joining technology for Nb-Si alloy itself and it to superalloy 
Nb SS /Nb 5 Si 3 composite shows great potential for application as 
the next generation turbine blades and vanes with a signiﬁcantly 
higher service temperature (120 0–140 0 °C) than traditional Ni- 
based superalloys [256] . It is known that joining technique is es- 
sential to the manufacture of the blades and vanes in aero-engine 
industry [257] . However, the high melting point ( > 1750 °C) and 
the composite microstructure intend to combine the ductility of 
Nb solid solution phase and superior high-temperature strength 
of Nb 5 Si 3 intermetallic phase, and meanwhile they also pose im- 
mense obstacles to the joining of this composite material [258] . In 
recent years, large number of investigations focused on the fabri- 
cation technique and microstructure optimization of Nb SS /Nb 5 Si 3 
composites. Though the joining technology is an essential technol- 
ogy for Nb-Si alloy to move towards engineering application, the 
joining of this kind of composite is rarely reported and thus the 
basic understanding of its joining characteristic is extremely lack- 
ing. Brazing is regarded as a ﬂexible method for the joining of in- 
termetallics and composites [ 259 , 260,269,270 ]. In our previous re- 
search, traditional Ni-based brazing ﬁller alloys were attempted for 
Nb SS /Nb 5 Si 3 composite joining but the formation of brittle inter- 
metallic phases (e.g. Ni 3 B and Ni 3 Si) at the joining interface greatly 
limited the joint strength [261–263] . In most cases the interfacial 
reactions between the brazing ﬁller metal and the joined parent 
material, and the diffusion effect of the elements from the brazing 
ﬁller metal to the parent material should be considered. Especially 
for the dissimilar joining of Nb-Si based alloy to Ni-or-Co based su- 
peralloy, the joining characteristics will become more complicated. 
Therefore, it is urgent to study the joining technique for Nb-Si al- 
loy itself and Nb-Si alloy with Ni or Co based superalloy, and this 
should be of great importance for providing a theoretical guidance 
and technical basis for engineering application.
