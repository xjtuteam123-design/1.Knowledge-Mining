ABSTRACT
The strength of alloys can be significantly enhanced by the incorporation of second phase. In this work, a novel 
high-strength La-TZM alloy was designed and prepared via multicomponent second phase. It is reported herein 
that the strengthening mechanism of La-TZM alloy is mainly regulated by the Orowan mechanism, where the 
critical phase transformation size (d
C
) of the second phase is inversely proportional to the interfacial energy ( γ ) 
between the matrix and the second phase. Microstructure characterization has revealed that most of the second 
phase particles in La-TZM alloy exceed the critical size, indicating that La-TZM alloy is mainly strengthened 
through the Orowan mechanism. The yield strength (Y
SP
) exhibits a positive correlation with the volume fraction 
of second phase, and it initially increases and subsequently decreases as the size of second phase particles in -
creases. As a result, compared with the conventional TZM alloy, the La-TZM alloy prepared in this study exhibits 
an 85.3 % enhancement in tensile strength. This research not only furnishes a theoretical foundation but also 
offers technical support for the extensive application of high-strength molybdenum (Mo) alloys in major engi -
neering fields, thereby holding significant theoretical and practical implications.
1. Introduction
Mo and its alloys have emerged as pivotal materials in high- 
temperature engineering applications, particularly in electronics, aero -
space and nuclear energy sectors. This prominence stems from their 
exceptional physical properties: high melting point, superior high- 
temperature strength, excellent electrical conductivity, low thermal 
expansion coefficient, as well as remarkable thermal shock and fatigue 
resistance [ 1 – 6 ]. However, due to their inherent brittleness, their 
widespread adoption faces significant challenges, which seriously 
impair processing performance and usability [ 7 ]. Unlike many struc -
tural metals, Mo lacks temperature-dependent crystalline phase trans -
formations, necessitating reliance on alloying for property optimization 
[ 8 – 10 ]. By incorporating some selected alloying elements, researchers 
aim to enhance mechanical performance while optimizing the micro -
structure of Mo alloys, thereby promoting the alloying process of 
molybdenum-based materials [ 11 – 13 ]. To this end, a series of Mo alloys 
have been developed through international cooperation, including but 
not limited to: Mo

After mechanical performance testing, it was found that the La-TZM

compared to the previous TZM alloy. In order to study the second phase

and strengthening mechanism of the second phase on the properties of 
Mo alloys, laying the foundation for multi-scale strengthening design of 
refractory metal systems, and providing practical guidance for the 
development of next-generation high-temperature structural materials.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Tensile fractures and stress-strain curves
The tensile fracture morphologies of La-TZM alloy and TZM alloy 
were observed by SEM. In Fig. 2 (a) and (c), both of two alloys exhibit 
intergranular (rolling direction) fractures, with typical layered fractures 
and clear transverse textures. The grains were elongated and deformed 
after rolling, forming a fibrous structure. In Fig. 2 (b), spherical and 
ellipsoidal dimples caused by the second phase in La-TZM alloy, as well 
as tear ridges caused by large plastic deformation are present, showing 
quasi-cleavage micropore aggregation fracture characteristics. The 
fracture of La-TZM alloy plate also shows that the second phase particles 
mostly exhibit are fine, numerous, and uniformly distributed at the grain 
boundaries. On the contrary, as shown in Fig. 2 (d), the number of second 
phase particles is relatively small in TZM alloy. The stress-strain curves 
are shown in Fig. 2 (e). The tensile strength of La-TZM alloy prepared by 
doping the rare earth element La and introducing C is 1666.39 MPa, 
which is 85.3 % higher than TZM alloy. The tensile strength of La-TZM 
alloy is significantly better than TZM alloy, indicating that the intro -
duction of La element by lanthanum nitrate has a significant improve -
ment effect on the tensile strength of TZM alloy. It can be assumed that 
the second phase particles interact strongly with dislocations in La-TZM 
alloy, causing a large number of dislocations were firmly pinned by the 
second phase particles then needed a greater stress to break free from the 
pinning effect of the particles. Therefore, the room-temperature tensile 
strength of La-TZM alloy is significantly improved compared with TZM 
alloy. Further discussion will be conducted in the following sections.

According to the interaction mechanism between the second phase 
and the gliding dislocations, two different strengthening mechanisms 
can be obtained. One is the Orowan mechanism in which the disloca -
tions bypass the second phase and leave dislocation loops around the 
particles. The other is the cutting mechanism in which the dislocations 
cut through the second phase. The former second phase are called non- 
deformable particles, while the latter are called deformable particles. 
Second phase strengthening is achieved by introducing dispersed hard 
particles into the alloy structure to hinder dislocation movement, 
thereby producing a strengthening effect. The schematic diagram of two 
different reinforcement mechanisms is shown in Fig. 7 .

characteristics that they have a non-coherent relationship with the 
matrix. In the Orowan mechanism, when dislocations bypass the non-

due to the bending of the dislocation lines, so a higher external stress is 
required for the dislocations to cross the particles and continue to slip, 
resulting in strengthening. When interface bonding relationship be -
tween the second phase and the matrix is non-coherent, dislocations 
bypass the particles, and the uncoordinated deformation between the 
particles and the matrix generates additional stress. As the deformation
