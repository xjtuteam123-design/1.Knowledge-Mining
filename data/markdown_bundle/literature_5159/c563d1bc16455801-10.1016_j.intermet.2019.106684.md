ABSTRACT 
High temperature titanium alloy/Ti-43Al-9V metal-intermetallic laminate composite was successfully fabricated 
by hot-pack rolling. The titanium alloy exhibits a lamellar structure and the TiAl alloy consists of B2 phase and 
elongated γ phase. Due to the low rolling and annealing temperature, the TiAl alloy shows a low extent of dy -
namic recrystallization (69.3%) and a high dislocations density (17%). The composite shows an excellent 
combination of the enhanced tensile strength and fracture toughness. The tensile strength of composite at 25

1. Introduction 
TiAl alloys sheets are considered as promising materials to be used as 
thermal protection shields because of their low density, high tempera -
ture strength and high oxidation resistance [ 1 – 4 ]. However, the brit -
tleness at room temperature of TiAl alloy becomes the major obstacles to 
the use of TiAl alloy [ 5 – 7 ]. Inspired by the structure of shell, metal - 
intermetallic laminate (MIL) composites have attracted more attention 
due to the much improved toughness compared with intermetallic ma -
terials [ 8 – 10 ]. This laminate structure could minimize the impact of raw 
micro-cracks thus improving the mechanical properties of the materials. 
So far, many kinds of Ti-Al based MIL composites have been fabri -
cated by varied methods. Vacuum hot pressing is the most common 
approach for preparation of Ti-Al based laminate composites. But the 
formation of Kirkendall voids and the oxide aggregations decrease the 
mechanical properties of Ti-Al based laminated composites. Re -
inforcements such as fibers and whiskers are used to further improve the 
mechanical properties of composites. Nevertheless it makes this method 
more complex and costly [ 11 – 14 ]. The composites fabricated by phys -
ical vaporous deposition and spark plasma sintering show excellent 
mechanical properties. But restricted by equipment, it is rather difficult 
to fabricate large scale composites [ 15 – 17 ]. Explosive welding is 
regarded as a potential method to fabricate large scale Ti-Al based 
laminate composites. However, the inhomogeneous deformation and 
subsequent heat treatment limit its further application [ 18 , 19 ]. 
Hot-pack rolling is an effective way to fabricate Ti-Al based MIL 
composites. Because the deformation between two components are 
more homogeneous at high temperature and it is an economical way to 
fabricate large scale composites. Previous study shows that the com -
posite fabricated by hot-pack rolling exhibits a high bonding strength 
and mechanical properties [ 20 ]. However, the same as the Ti-Al based 
composites fabricated by other methods, the high temperature proper -
ties are the drawbacks of these composites [ 21 , 22 ]. Because most of the 
reinforcements (pure Ti or Ti6Al4V alloy) used in these composites will 
suffer a sharp decrease at high temperature. Therefore, replacing pure Ti 
or Ti6Al4V alloy with high temperature titanium alloy may further 
improve the high temperature properties of Ti-Al based MIL composites. 
In the present work, the Ti-6Al-3Sn-10Zr-(Mo, Nb, W, Si) (wt.%)/Ti- 
43Al-9V (at.%) metal-intermetallic laminate composite was fabricated 
by hot-pack rolling. The microstructure, mechanical properties and 
fracture behavior of composite were investigated. More attention was 
focus on the sub-structure of TiAl alloy and the strengthening and 
toughening mechanism of composite.

4. Conclusions 
Based on the preliminary data and discussions presented above, 
several conclusions are made as follow. 
(1) The Ti-TiAl laminate composite is successfully fabricated by Ti- 
6Al-3Sn-10Zr-(Mo, Nb, W, Si) (wt.%) alloy and Ti-43Al-9V (at. 
%) alloy. The microstructure of titanium alloy and TiAl alloy are 
lamellar structure and elongated γ phase with B2 matrix 
respectively. 
(2) High dislocations density (17%) and low extent of DRX (69.3%) 
are observed in TiAl alloy, which are due to the low rolling 
temperature decreasing the driving force of DRX.
