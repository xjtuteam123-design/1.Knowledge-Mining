Abstract : A serious of diffusion bonding (DB) tests between high Nb containing TiAl alloy and 
Ti 2AlNb alloy were conducted and the bonded joints wer e post-bonded heat treated (PBHT) at 1000°C 
for 10h. The microstructure observation showed that the thickness and microstructure of the bonding 
interface were changed with the bonding parameters. The optimized parameter for direct solid-state DB 
high Nb containing TiAl to Ti 2AlNb alloy was obtained as 1000°C-20MPa-90min. PBHT at 
1000°C/10h can significantly promote elements diffu sion, phase transformation and microstructure 
evolution in bonding interface. The tensile results showed that PBHT can remarkably improve the 
tensile strength of TiAl/Ti 2AlNb joints. After PBHT, the tensile strength can b e increased from 86% to 
146 % compared with TiAl alloy by changing the fail ure mode. However, the Al(Nb,Ti) 2 phase in the 
bonding interface can’t eliminated by PBHT in this study. This work provides a strategy not only 
contributing to control the structure of bonding interface during DB and after PBHT but also improving 
the properties of TiAl/Ti 2AlNb joints. 
Key Words : High Nb containing TiAl alloy, Ti 2AlNb alloy, Diffusion bonding, Microstructure 
evolution, Mechanical properties

1.Introduction 
As one of the critical structural engineering mater ials for high temperature applications, TiAl alloy 
has been deployed in the aerospace industry for yea rs. Their excellent high temperature mechanical 
properties and outstanding corrosion resistance con siderably advance the efficiency of aircraft engine 
under harsh serving conditions [1]-[3]. However, th e poor hot workability, a key factor, limits the 
practical engineering application for fabricating l arge dimension and complex shape structure 
components [4][6]. Therefore, it is necessary to de velop a new material to improve the plasticity with 
good performance at elevated temperature simulaneou sly. The O-phase alloy (based on orthorhombic 
Ti 2AlNb) is designed based on Ti 3Al by adding some element Nb, which improves the ro om 
temperature ductility, fracture toughness and oxida tion resistance apparently [7][9]. Neverthless, 
Ti 2AlNb alloy is denser than TiAl alloy because of hig h content of Nb, which would resultin the fuel 
efficiency reduction of aero engines. One way to ta ke the advantages of both TiAl and Ti 2AlNb is to 
join them soundly to expand them application. 
To date, a large amount of welding techniques have been developed to achieve sound joints of 
advanced alloys. Expressly, the joining of TiAl or Ti 2AlNb alloy were mainly focus on brazing welding, 
friction welding, gas tungsten arc welding and tran sient liquid phase bonding [10][14]. Compared with 
this techniques, the solid diffusion bonding avoids the fusion zone and welding defaults ， reduces the 
thermal stress in the joint concurrently. Hence, it is considered as an appropriate wayto join TiAl or 
Ti 2AlNb alloy. Simões S et. al.[15] bonded a sound TiA l joint using Ni/Ti multilayers by DB. Li H et. 
al.[16] studied the hot press bonding of γ-TiAl and TC17 at low temperature by imposing plast ic 
deformation and post heating. Qian J w et. al.[17] carried out the diffusion bonding of 
Ti 2AlNb/GH4169 at different conditions. Tang et al.[29 ]analyzed the recrystallization behavior at

2.2 Experimental Section 
The diffusion bonding was carried out in a vacuum d iffusion bonding machine under a minimum 
vacuum level of 5.4×10 -3 Pa using the combination of bonding parameters, vi z, the holding time, 
bonding pressure and temperature varied from 60 to 120 min, 15 to 25MPa and 930 to 1000°C, 
respectively. During the diffusion bonding process, the heating rate was set as 10°C/min from room 
temperature to target temperature and furnace cooli ng to the room temperature. Additionally, when 
temperature cooling to 300°C and 600°C,holding 30mi n and 20min to make the temperature more 
homogeneous, respectively. In order to eliminate th e influence of phase transformation on the 
microstructure evolution, PBHT was carried out at 1000°C[23] for 10h. 
After bonding process, the samples were sectioned i nto 8×8×5mm by electro-discharge machine 
followed by grounding and polishing. The interfacia l microstructure and some weld defects of all joint s 
were characterized employing scanning electron micr oscopy (SEM(EBSD), TESCAN VEGA3) and

evaluate joints quality, a nonstandard tensile test was devised to estimate tensile properties by tens ile

and the tensile specimen with the size 8(width)×10( gauge)×2(thickness) mm are shown in Fig.2. After

3.3 Mechanical properties of high Nb containing TiAl /Ti 2AlNb DB joint 
The tensile tests at room temperature was performed to evaluate the bonding quality both in the 
bonding and PBHT conditions. The tensile strength of bonded joints under different bonding conditions 
is illustrated as the histogram in Fig.9. As the bl ack slash shows in Fig.9, the tensile strength of t he 
joints increases with the increasing bonding temper ature, and the tensile strength is the highest abou t 
265MPa at 1000°C. It also indicates that the bondin g temperature has a significant effect on 
mechanical properties of bonded joints from the tre nd of increase. The effect of bonding pressure and 
holding time on the tensile strength also performed at room temperature in Fig.9. It is found that the re 
is a “normal distribution” relationship between tensile strength and bonding pressure as well as holdi ng 
time. Namely, there exist a critical bonding pressure and holding time make the tensile strength highe st. 
The higher tensile strength can be reached under th e 20MPa or 90min. By optimizing the bonding 
parameters, the highest tensile strength of the joi nts obtained is 265MPa, close to 80% of as-received 
TiAl alloy. The corresponding diffusion bonding par ameters are 1000°C-20MPa-90min. A similar 
tensile strength value was also reported by Zou et al[33],but bonding time of our research is longer than 
them, which may be caused by the surface roughness. Our surface roughness is more polished than 
them. Moreover, surface roughness plays an importan t role in the diffusion bonding process, which has 
been researched by our previous investigations [34],[35].

4.Conclusion 
In this work, the investigation of diffusion bondin g high Nb containing TiAl alloy to Ti 2AlNb 
alloy was conducted. Based on the microstructure ob servation, the microstructure evolution at bonding 
interface during DB and after PBHT was revealed. Be sides, the tensile testing at room temperature was 
performed to evaluate bonding quality and optimize diffusion bonding parameters of high Nb 
containing TiAl/Ti 2AlNb joint. Finally, the fracture model was discuss ed. Present analysis leads to the 
following conclusions. 
The thickness and microstructure of the bonding int erface changed with different bonding 
parameters. The optimized parameters for direct sol id-state diffusion bonding Nb containing TiAl alloy 
to Ti 2AlNb alloy in this investigation was obtained is 10 00°C-20MPa-90min,corresponding thickness 
of the bonding interface is approximately 10.82 µm and the bonding interface consists of two parts: gray 
banded microstructure near high Nb containing TiAl alloy and some black axiolitic microstructure 
embedded in the fine white phase close to Ti 2AlNb alloy. 
PBHT at 1000°C/10h can significantly broaden bondin g interface and improve tensile strength of 
DB joint. After PBHT, the tensile strength can be i ncreased from86% to 146 % of the TiAl alloy. 
However, PBHT can’t eliminate the Al(Nb,Ti) 2 phase in this study. Besides, After PBHT, the frac ture 
mode is changed from TiAl surface to phase interface of bonding interface.
