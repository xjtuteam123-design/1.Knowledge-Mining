Abstract—Ti 5Si3 is one of the candidate materials for very-high-temperature applications. Alloying element
additions such as Nb or Cr to Ti 5Si3 result in alloys with di/C128erent morphology and properties depending
on the alloy design. The Ti 5Si3-based stoichiometric alloys which can be abbreviated as (Ti, M) 5Si3 where
M = Nb, Cr, are composed of a monolithic Ti 5Si3 phase and are strengthened by solution hardening. Nb
and Cr atoms dissolve in Ti 5Si3 without changing its phase structure. The solubility of Nb or Cr in Ti 5Si3
is determined to be at least 21 and 12 at.%, respectively. The o/C128-stoichiometric alloys, whose compositions
are Ti
62.5MXSi37.5ÿ X, display a hypereutectic microstructure with Ti 5Si3 as the primary phase. The brittle-
ness of the o/C128-stoichiometric alloys is greatly improved compared with the stoichiometric ones. However,
due to the weak phase involved, o/C128-stoichiometric alloys appear to have low strength at high temperature
and thus are unsuitable for very-high-temperature applications. # 1998 Acta Metallurgica Inc.
1. INTRODUCTION
Silicon compounds, as the functional material, have
received much attention in the production of very
large scale integration electronics over the past dec-
ades because of their unique physical and electrical
properties [1]. Recently, the development of new
materials for very-high-temperature structural appli-
cations requires combined higher properties over
the traditional Ni-base superalloy, and silicon com-
pounds in the means of bulk materials have aroused
great interest again. Titanium silicide, Ti
5Si3, a typi-
cal silicon intermetallic compound in the Ti–Si
binary system is one of the candidate materials in
the current research.
Ti5Si3 bears a complex D8 8 hexagonal structure
(Mn5Si3-type) whose reported lattice parameters are
a = 0.7444 nm and c = 0.5143 nm (JCPDS card
No. 29-1362). The advantages of Ti 5Si3 are its
extremely high melting temperature (2130 8C) and
low density (4.32 g/cm 3) and the main drawback of
this compound, like other intermetallics, is its brit-
tleness. Following the pioneering work of
Frommeyer [2], a few research works on the physi-
cal and mechanical properties of Ti
5Si3 have been
performed. These include elastic modulus, coe-
cient of thermal expansion, fracture strength at high
temperature as well as creep resistance [3–6]. With
these investigations, certain progress has been made
in our understanding of Ti 5Si3. However, these
works limited their scope within the binary system
and further understanding of the fundamental
aspects of the properties of Ti 5Si3 is still needed.
Alloying addition to Ti 5Si3 has been seen as a poss-
ible way to improve the mechanical or physical
properties of this compound and, thus, prompted
the present study. Previous theoretical calculations
showed that Nb or Cr additions to Ti
5Si3 crystal
would enhance the bonding between atoms [7]. In
this paper, experimental approaches were taken on
the alloying behavior of Ti 5Si3 by Nb or Cr ad-
ditions and the relevant evaluation have been car-
ried out in order to gain a further understanding of
Ti5Si3 compound.

these elements (Ti, Nb and Cr), there are three
ways of alloy design for Nb or Cr additions to
Ti
5Si3. This is schematically shown in Fig. 1: (1) by

2.2. Specimen preparation
Alloys were prepared by arc melting high purity
elements in a laboratory furnace under an argon at-
mosphere for four times. The weight loss during
melting can be neglected so that the actual compo-
sitions are close to the nominal ones. After melting,
the ingots were annealed under vacuum at 1000 8C
for 30 h in order to ensure chemical homogeneity.
Oxygen and nitrogen contents were determined to
be 700 and 70 ppm, respectively.
In order to characterize the microstructure, speci-
mens cut from the ingot by spark erosion were ana-
lyzed using optical metallography, scanning electron
microscopy (SEM), energy dispersive X-ray spec-
trum (EDX) and electron probe micro-analyzer
(EPMA).
Specimens for microstructure observation were
metallographically prepared and were ﬁnally etched

obtained by manually milling the small pieces of
ingots, were used for determination of phase consti-
tution by X-ray di/C128raction. Mechanical properties
of the alloys were evaluated by hardness testing and
compression at room temperature as well as elev-
ated temperatures. Fracture surfaces were examined
by SEM.

3.4. Fracture surface
The fracture surfaces of the stoichiometric alloys
are fully cleaved (Fig. 11). River patterns and sec-
ondary cracks can be clearly seen. Some researchers
pointed out that the fracture behavior of binary
Ti
5Si3 was transgranular for large grain and inter-
granular for small grain [4, 6]. We do not think this
is the case in our study. The reported observation
was performed on the powder metallurgy specimens
where oxygen contamination might be high. The
grain boundary was likely to be weakened by oxy-
gen embrittlement thus intergranular fracture
occurred. In this study, the specimens were pre-
pared by arc melting and the oxygen contamination
was low, therefore grain boundary weakening was
eliminated. Taking the above analysis into consider-
ation, the intrinsic fracture behavior of the Ti
5Si3
phase should be cleavage.
Fractographic observations on o/C128-stoichiometric
alloys after compression show a duplex characteriz-
ation of fracture. The fracture surfaces of the pri-
mary Ti
5Si3 phases are plain with many clear ‘‘river
patterns’’ on them [see Fig. 12(a) and (b)]; the
eutectic area displays a ﬁne intergranular fracture
morphology [see Fig. 2 and Fig. 12(b) and (c)].
Fracture seems to occur at both the primary Ti
5Si3
phase and the boundary between the primary Ti 5Si3
phase and the eutectic area, Fig. 12(c).

4. DISCUSSION
4.1. Site occupation of Nb and Cr in Ti 5Si3 crystal
In the past, little was known about the alloying
behavior of Ti 5Si3 since the relevant Ti–Si–M
(M = Nb or Cr) ternary phase diagrams are una-
vailable or incomplete regarding the composition-
phase constitution relationship. The binary systems
of Ti–Si, Nb–Si and Cr–Si are somewhat similar to
one another for each of them forms the M
5Si3-type
compound (Ti 5Si3,N b 5Si3 and Cr 5Si3). Ti–Nb and
Ti–Cr systems, on the other hand, form a fully dis-
solved solution between the pure elements. A
reasonable assumption derived from the above in-
formation is that it is possible to form such a com-
pound as (Ti, M)
5Si3 (M = Nb, Cr) with Nb or Cr
additions to Ti 5Si3. Experimental results have
shown that alloying in two di/C128erent ways in this
study leads to two kinds of alloys: the single-phased
ones and the multi-phased ones. It has also been
shown that Ti
5Si3 phase can dissolve large amount
of Nb or Cr. The stoichiometric alloys are com-
posed of a monolithic Ti 5Si3 phase whose Nb and
Cr contents can be as high as 21 and 11.9 at.%, re-
spectively, while the o/C128-stoichiometric alloys con-

appear to be a reason for this, because the radii of
Nb and Cr atoms are smaller than that of Ti atom.
