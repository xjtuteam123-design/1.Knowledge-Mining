Abstract
Statistical evaluation for strength of die-cast SiC raluminum alloy composites has been performed in order to predict thep
reliability of composites. Die-casting was performed using the preheated die at the pouring temperature of 700 8C. It was

alloy. Furthermore, the tensile strength slightly increased with increasing SiC particulate volume fraction. However, the
strength scattering of composites increased with increasing SiC particulate volume fraction. For the statistical evaluation of
strength, the Weibull modulus of die-cast SiC rAl alloy composites was 29.6 in Al matrix alloy, 22.2 and 14.2 for 10 and

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
Gravity cast SiC rAl alloy composites have
p
shown that SiC particulates are largely segregated in
the intercellular regions and many cast defects are
observed, as shown in Fig. 1. Therefore, new fabrica-
tion methods such as die-casting and squeeze-casting
were needed to obtain sound microstructure and
wxgood mechanical properties. Mochizuki et al. 8
tried to fabricate Al alloy matrix composites by
die-casting. Die-casting improves mechanical proper-
ties by the improvement of particulate distribution
due to rapid solidification and the reduction of cast
defects. Fig. 2 shows microstructures of die-cast
SiC rAl alloy composites with various SiC particu-
p
late volume fraction. The microstructure of SiC rAlp
alloy composites consists of the dendritic structure of
the primary a phase, eutectic Si and SiC particu-
lates. It was also observed that the SiC particulates
were homogeneously dispersed in refined aluminum
matrix and the dendritic cell size of the composites
was about 6.4 mm. According to previous work,
dendritic cell size of the furnace-cooled composites
and gravity mold cast composites were about 350
wxand 45 mm, respectively 9 . Lloyd and Chamberlain
wx10 reported that SiC particulates are rejected ahead
of solidification front and trapped by converging
dendritics in the intercellular regions with decreasing
cooling rate. Since the particulates are intensively
pushed to the intercellular regions at a slower cool-
ing rate condition, the distribution of SiC particulates
in the solidification microstructure is determined
mainly by the cell size, which in turn, depends on
cooling rate. Therefore, it is believed that the SiC
particulates were homogeneously distributed in Al
matrix, resulting from the refinement of cell size due
to rapid cooling rate, as shown in Fig. 2.

SiC particulates were homogeneously distributed
in Al matrix, resulting from the refinement of cell
size due to rapid cooling rate. The tensile strength of
as die-cast composites was higher than that of the
as-die-cast aluminum alloy. Tensile strength in-
creased with an increase in SiC particulate volume
fraction. Strength scattering of composites increased
with increasing SiC particulate volume fraction. For
statistical evaluation of strength, the Weibull modu-
