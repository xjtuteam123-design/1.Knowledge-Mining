ABSTRACT
The physicochemical properties of the Ni-Co-Fe-O system are important to the high-temperature oxidation of the
Ni-Co-Fe based alloy, such as superalloys and high-alloy steels. The equilibrium phase relations between the Ni-
Co-Fe alloy and its oxide at elevated temperature were systematically investigated using experimental and as-
sessment method. The iso-
pO2 lines and iso-concentration lines of oxides in the entire range of the oxygen partial
pressure and composition at 1673 K were evaluated, and the high-temperature oxidation path of the Ni-Co-Fe
alloy was qualitatively examined. Using the thermodynamic ﬁndings allows to ascertain the high-temperature
oxidation phenomena of the Ni-Co-Fe based alloy.
1. Introduction
High-temperature material is critical for aviation, aerospace and
power generation industries. As the most typical high-temperature
materials, superalloys are generally nickel-, cobalt-, or nickel-iron-
based alloys, and they are predominantly used to fabricate turbine
blades for aircraft engines and power turbines [ 1]. The severe operating
environment, always high temperature and corrosive, asks the mate-
rials for various outstanding characteristics, such as excellent me-
chanical strength, resistance to creep and fatigue at elevated tempera-
ture, good surface stability in corrosive atmospheres, and oxidation
resistance [2]. In recent years, large e ﬀorts have been made to improve
the high-temperature oxidation resistance of various conventional su-
peralloys and to develop novel superalloys [ 3–7].
Knowledge of the high-temperature physicochemical properties of
the superalloys at oxidizing atmosphere is of signi ﬁcant scienti ﬁc and
industrial importance for understanding of the high-temperature oxi-
dation behavior and promotion of the high-temperature oxidation re-
sistance of superalloys [ 8–11]. A solid information of the phase equi-
libria of the Ni-Co-Fe-O system, in particularly the Ni-Co-Fe alloy phase
and the oxide phase, is useful to predict and control the high-tem-
perature oxidation behavior of the Ni-Co-Fe based alloy, such as su-
peralloys and high-alloy steels [ 12,13]. Fig. 1 shows the values of the
equilibrium oxygen partial pressure characteristic of pure nickel, co-
balt, and iron and their oxides calculated based on the thermodynamic
database [14], according to which the oxidation behavior of these pure
metals at di ﬀerent temperature and oxygen partial pressure can be es-
timated. However, alloys instead of pure metals are industrially used,
and oxidation of alloy is much more complex [ 15]. Various factors will
inﬂuence the alloy oxidation, such as the di ﬀerent a ﬃnities for oxygen
of the components in alloy, the a ﬃnities among components, and the
formation of ternary or high order oxide phases [ 16]. The thermo-
dynamic equilibrium relations in Ni-Co-Fe-O system will provide fun-
damental idea to ascertain the high-temperature oxidation phenomena
of the Ni-Co-Fe-based alloy under oxidizing atmosphere [ 17].
Information of the phase equilibria of the Ni-Co-Fe-O system is also
essential for controlling the Ni-Co-Fe alloy composition by oxidizing
during the high-temperature processing. The excellent performance of
superalloys is on basis of an extremely careful and strict controlling of
the alloy composition, and thus special technologies are always re-
quired for the high-temperature processing of superalloys, such as va-
cuum induction melting (VIM), vacuum arc re-melting (VAR), and
electro-slag re-melting (ESR), and in these processing the operation
condition must be carefully controlled [ 18]. Controlling of the desired
alloy chemistry composition and elimination of the impurities are two
important targets of the high-temperature processing of superalloys,
which is achieved by a careful adjustment of the oxygen partial pres-
sure. Processing superalloys with advanced characteristics requires
addition of lots of alloy elements, such as aluminium, titanium, chro-
mium, molybdenum, tungsten, and niobium, into the solvent alloy of
Ni/Co/Ni-Fe [ 2,19–24]. Meanwhile, impurities must be removed and
controlled at an acceptable level [ 2]. More attention should be paid on
the impurity issue when the superalloys scrap is recently used as al-
ternative
source of nickel, cobalt or some critical alloy elements during
the melting process, because the composition of these secondary re-
sources is much more variable comparing with primary resource

between maximum elimination of metallic inclusions and minimum

Because of their scienti ﬁc and industrial importance, the thermo-
dynamics of the binary and ternary sub-systems of the Ni-Co-Fe-O
system have been studied for several years. A summary focusing on the
thermodynamic studies on the Ni-Co-Fe-O system in recent years was
carried out and is shown below, and it is shown that only limited in-
formation is available about this quaternary system. In this study, some
key experiments were carried out to directly measure the phase equi-
libria relations between the solid Ni-Co-Fe alloy phase and the oxide
phase in the Ni-Co-Fe-O system at 1673 K using the gas/oxide/metal
equilibrium technique at controlled oxygen partial pressures. Based on
the obtained phase equilibria and thermodynamic properties, the iso-
pO2 lines and iso-concentration lines of oxides in the entire range of the
oxygen partial pressure and composition in the Ni-Co-Fe-O system at
1673 K were evaluated. Further, the high-temperature oxidation path of
the Ni-Co-Fe alloy in an oxidizing atmosphere was quantitively ex-
amined as an application of the obtained thermodynamic ﬁndings.
Using of the thermodynamic ﬁndings allows to easily ascertain the
high-temperature oxidation phenomena of the Ni-Co-Fe based alloy.
1.1. Ni-Co-Fe system
The ternary system of Ni-Co-Fe has been extensively investigated by
many researchers because of its importance as a base material for
commercial alloyed steels, superalloys, and magnetic materials. The
binary sub-systems of Fe-Co [ 29–31], Fe-Ni [ 32–34], and Co-Ni [ 35,36]
have been extensively investigated and systemically assessed. The so-
lid–liquid two-phase ﬁelds for the three binary systems have been found
to be very narrow, and the solidi ﬁcation has been found to place within
a temperature range of only a few degrees. Reviewing of the ternary
system have been carried out by Guillermet [ 37], Raghavan [ 38], and
Tedenac [ 39]. No ternary compounds have been reported in the Ni-Co-
Fe solid phases [ 39]. The solid system is characterized by a continuous
miscible solid solution (fcc) and a narrow single-phase region of ( δ-Fe)
near the iron corner at higher temperatures [ 40]. The liquidus and
solidus in the ternary system are also very close to each other, which
reﬂects the very similar thermodynamic properties of the solid and li-
quid phases near the melting temperature in the ternary system [ 40].
The activity of the components in the molten Ni-Co-Fe system at
1850 K was measured by Knudsen cell mass spectrometry [ 41]. The
results obtained at 1873 K (1600 °C), determined based on the ther-
modynamic properties of the binary alloy system, were found to be in
satisfactory agreement with the experimental results [ 42]. The activity
of the components in the fcc Ni-Co-Fe solid solution at 1600 K was also
measured by Knudsen cell mass spectrometry [ 43]. In both the molten
and fcc solid solution systems, only very small deviations from ideality
were observed for all three components, and the thermodynamic
properties of the systems at 1873 K and 1600 K were found to be very
similar.
1.2. Fe-Co-O system
The Fe-Co-O system was recently reviewed and evaluated by the
CALPHAD method by Zhang et al. [ 44] and Jung et al. [ 45]. It was
found that both CoO and FeO are rock salt-structured phases and that
they can form a continuous solid solution [ 44–46]. The equilibrium
relations of the CoO-FeO solid solution and Co-Fe alloy at 1473 K
(1200 °C) were experimentally investigated[ 47]. Subramanian and
Dieckmann [48] measured the FeO and CoO activity in the oxide phase
coexisting with the alloy phase at 1473 K (1200 °C) by thermogravi-
metric experiments and observed a slightly positive deviation from the
activity predicted by Raoult’ s law for this system.
Based
on the experimental results available, Jung et al. [ 45] as-
sessed the thermodynamic properties of a Fe-Co-O system in which the
FeO-CoO solution was treated as an ideal solution. Zhang et al. [ 44]
carried out the most recent review of the Fe-Co-O system and conducted
a reassessment of this system that yielded new experimental results. In
their work, the compound energy formalism was used to model all the
phases in the Fe-Co-O system, and the activity of CoO was found to
exhibit a slightly positive deviation from ideality.
1.3. Fe-Ni-O system
An early review of this ternary system was carried out by Raghavan
[49], who presented partial isothermal sections at 1813 K (1540 °C) and
1273 K (1000 °C), a schematic liquidus projection, and a reaction
scheme. Schneider [ 50] subsequently determined the phase boundaries
of the wustite and spinel ﬁelds and the nonstoichiometry of the (Ni, Fe)
O using high-temperature in situ coulometric titration with solid
oxygen ion electrolyte. Recently, Rhamdhani [ 51] studied the phase
equilibria of the Fe-Ni-O system in the temperature range of 1073 K
(800 °C) to 1873 K (1600 °C) at various oxygen partial pressures. Using
the CALPHAD method, Pelton et al. [ 52] assessed and Luoma [ 53] re-
assessed this system, improving on the high-temperature phase rela-
tions by using an ionic liquid model and compound energy formalism to
describe the liquid, spinel, wustite, and hematite phases. Later, on the
basis of the results from Luoma [ 53], Kjellqvist et al. [ 54] reassessed the
spinel phase in this system and the magnetic contribution to Gibbs
energy was modelled. In the thermodynamic analysis on SiO
2-NiO-FeO
system carried out by Cruz et al. [ 55], the NiO-FeO system was simply
assumed to be an ideal solution, and the assessment results agreed well
with the experimental results.
1.4. Co-Ni-O system
Only a few studies carried out on the Co-Ni-O system if compared
with the other sub-systems. Moore and White [ 56] determined the
equilibrium relations in the NiO-CoO-O 2 system using a thermo-
gravimetry and constructed an isobaric ternary diagram of the system.
Kuboon and Hu [ 57] investigated the formation of NiO-CoO and Co 3O4-
Ni3O4 solid solutions by calcination of Ni(NO 3)2 and Co(NO 3)2 at dif-
ferent temperatures and found that the NiO-CoO solid solution was

1.5. Ni-Co-Fe-O system
Sarkisyan [59] investigated the reduction process of complex oxides
and oxide mixtures of the Fe-Ni-Co-O system with carbon or hydrogen
used as the reducing agent by the thermogravimetric and di ﬀerential
thermal analysis methods. They found that the mechanism of reduction
of the oxide solid solution di ﬀers substantially depending on the tem-
perature and below 845 K homogeneous Fe-Co or Fe-Ni-Co solid solu-
tions of constant composition can be formed after reduction. Klein et al.
[60] investigated the oxidation behaviour of Fe-Ni-Co alloys in air,
carbon dioxide or water at 758 K and suggested that the oxidation of Fe-
Ni-Co alloys proceeds via outward iron cation migration through the
oxide. Kai et al. [ 61] investigated the oxidation behaviour of Fe-Co-Ni
equi-molar alloy at 1073– 1273 K in dry air and found that the oxidation
kinetics followed the two-stage parabolic rate law with rate constants
generally increasing with temperature. Recently, Dashevskii et al. [ 62]
analysed the oxygen solutions in Fe-Ni-O, Fe-Co-O and Co-Ni-O melts
by thermodynamic method and determined the equilibrium oxygen
concentrations in these melts over the whole range of alloy composi-
tions. However, no systematic study on the equilibrium relations be-
tween oxide and alloy phases in Ni-Co-Fe-O system was reported yet.
2. Experimental
2.1. Starting materials
The starting materials used in this study were metal powders of iron
(99.9% purity, Wako Pure Chemical Industries, Ltd.), cobalt (99.5%
purity, Wako Pure Chemical Industries, Ltd.), nickel (99.9% purity,
KOJUNDO Chemical Laboratory Co., Ltd.) and oxide powders of NiO
(99.9% purity, Wako Pure Chemical Industries, Ltd.) and CoO (99.7%
purity, KOJUNDO Chemical Laboratory Co., Ltd.). FeO powder was
synthesized by heating Fe
2O3 powder (> 99.0% purity, Kanto Chemical
Co., Inc.) at 1200 K for 2 h. The heating atmosphere was controlled by a
gas mixture of CO-50 vol.% CO
2 with a total gas ﬂow rate of 200 ml/
min. The synthesized FeO powder was con ﬁrmed by x-ray di ﬀraction
(XRD).
The Ni-Co-Fe alloy was prepared by melting a mixture of the iron,
nickel, and cobalt powders. The powders of high-purity iron, nickel,
and cobalt with designed compositions were thoroughly mixed in an
agate mortar and then pelletized in a stainless-steel die to form 12-mm-
diameter disks. The pellets were then melted in a high-frequency in-
duction furnace (MU-1700D, Sekisui Chemical Co., Ltd.) using an alu-
mina working crucible. The melting atmosphere was controlled using
argon gas. The molten alloy was then cooled by argon gas ﬂow after
15 min of holding. The Ni-Co-Fe alloy obtained was further processed to
fabricate a crucible (inner diameter: 6.5 mm; outside diameter: 12 mm;
depth: 6 mm; height: 15 mm) and foil circle (diameter: 5 mm; thickness:
0.5 mm) by mechanical means for use in the equilibrium experiments
subsequently conducted.
The powders of high-purity FeO, CoO, and NiO with designed
compositions were thoroughly mixed in an agate mortar. The alloy foil
was embedded into the oxide mixture carefully, and the oxide mixture
containing the alloy foil was then placed into a Ni-Co-Fe alloy working
crucible with the same composition as the alloy foil. The oxide mixture
was compacted and then used in equilibrium experiments.
2.2. Phase equilibrium experiments
Gas/oxide/metal equilibrium experiments were carried out in open
systems at ﬁxed oxygen partial pressures. The Ni-Co-Fe alloy crucible
containing the pre-prepared oxide mixture and alloy foil was placed in
an alumina protecting crucible, and the alumina crucible was placed in
a magnesia crucible with a molybdenum lifting yoke to be set into the
furnace. A vertical tube resistance furnace (furnace core tub: SSA-S
alumina tube, 80 mm × 70 mm × 1000 mm; heater: kanthal superthal,
MoSi
2) provided by Techno Science Co., Ltd., Japan (TSM-1650-3Z)
was used for the equilibrium experiments. When the heating procedure
was ﬁnished, the samples were quickly withdrawn from the heating
area and quenched with ﬂowing helium gas.
The samples were heated in an argon atmosphere to the required
temperature of 1673 K, and the gas was then switched to a mixture of
CO and CO
2 gases with a total volumetric gas ﬂow rate of 200 ml/min.

2.3. Chemical analysis
Following quenching, XRD was used to con ﬁrm the formed solid
solution of the oxide phase. The samples were then mounted in epoxy
resin and cured in a vacuum chamber. Cross sections of the samples
were prepared by cutting the samples vertically, grinding the surfaces
using SiC-grinding papers, and coating them with osmium. The mi-
crostructures of the samples were examined using a scanning electron
microscope (SEM) provided by Oxford Instruments (JSM-6510/INCA
Energy 250). Energy-dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (EDS) was then used
to determine the phase compositions of the equilibrium samples. The
EDS was operated at a 15 kV accelerating voltage, and the WD was set
to 12 mm.
2.4. Initial composition design and equilibrium time
Because the solid-solution Ni-Co-Fe [ 39], FeO-CoO [ 45], FeO-NiO
[55], and CoO-NiO [ 58] systems were all close to ideal, the initial
compositions of the alloy and oxide phases were designed according to
the estimated alloy –oxide equilibrium composition by assuming that
both phases were ideal solid solutions, to reduce the heating time re-
quired to reach equilibrium. For example, in the equilibrium experi-
ments under an oxygen partial pressure log(
pO2)= −7.87 (Nos. 301,
302, and 303, as shown in Table 1), the estimated equilibrium com-
position of the alloy phase under log( pO2)= −8.00 and that of the
oxide phase under log( pO2)= −7.87, assuming an ideal solution, were
used as the initial compositions. In this manner, the required heating
time was reduced dramatically. The achievement of the gas/oxide/
metal equilibrium state was ensured by conducting repeated experi-
ments at extended equilibrium times. Based on the results of pre-
liminary experiments, the alloy and oxide phase of the samples heated
for 12, 24, and 36 h all reached equilibrium, and thus 24 h of heating
time were used for the equilibrium experiments.

3.4. High-temperature oxidation of Ni-Co-Fe alloy
On basis of the thermodynamic ﬁndings shown above, the high-
temperature oxidation path of Ni-Co-Fe alloy can be obtained. The
oxidation path of three Ni-Co-Fe alloy with di ﬀerent composition were
estimated by assuming that the alloy phase is in equilibrium with the
NiO-CoO-FeO oxide phase during the oxidation, and the results are
shown in Fig. 8 . The oxidation path of the alloy and the composition
change of the oxide are drawn based on a series of equilibrium com-
position of alloy and oxide phases under di ﬀerent oxygen partial
pressures. The tie-lines of the oxide and alloy phases intersect at the
point indicating the initial alloy composition because the mass balance
for every metallic element (Ni, Co, and Fe) should be kept. It is noted
that the composition of the oxide phases shown as red squares and that
of the alloy phases shown as purple circles and blue triangles are ac-
tually in di ﬀerent layers, but the top view is shown here.
The results show that the oxidization path of the Ni-Co-Fe alloy
moves quickly toward the Ni-Co binary alloy ﬁrst and then toward the
nickel corner at elevated temperatures under oxidation atmospheres.
The iron contained in the alloy will be oxidized ﬁrst and as the oxi-
dation process proceeds, the cobalt concentration in the alloy decreases
more rapidly when the iron concentration is low. As to the oxide phase,
the FeO is formed ﬁrst and the CoO concentration increases fast as the
oxidation proceeds. Finally, the composition of the oxide moves toward
the position with the same ratio of Ni, Co, and Fe as that i in the initial
alloy if the oxidation keeps proceeding.
The predicted high-temperature oxidation behaviour of the Ni-Co-
Fe alloy agrees well with the results of experimental studies on the
oxidation behaviour of Fe-Co-Ni alloys at 758 K in air, steam, and CO
2
[60] and at 1073 –1273 K in dry air [ 61], although the actual oxidation
mechanism is complex and is also a ﬀected by kinetic factors, such as the
diﬀusivity of cations. From a thermodynamic viewpoint, iron and co-
balt are ﬁrst oxidized and enriched in the outer oxidation layer. Nickel
then appear in the oxide layer, but only after a long period of oxidation.

4. Conclusions
The conclusions are shown as followings.
(1) The equilibrium relations between the oxide and alloy phases in a
Ni-Co-Fe-O system at 1673 K were experimentally measured by the
gas/oxide/metal equilibrium method under di ﬀerent oxygen partial
pressures. Compared with an ideal oxide solution, the composition
of the oxide phase equilibrated with the alloy phase was found to
deviate toward NiO. The observed activity coe ﬃcients of CoO and
NiO in the ternary NiO-CoO-FeO system both exhibit small negative
deviations from ideality.
(2) The iso-
pO2 lines and the iso-concentration lines of oxides in the
entire range of the oxygen partial pressure and composition in the
Ni-Co-Fe-O system at 1673 K were calculated using the assessed
ternary interaction parameters for the NiO-CoO-FeO solid solution
system based on present experimental results.
(3) The equilibrium composition of the Ni-Co-Fe alloy phase and the
NiO-CoO-FeO oxide phases can be easily obtained directly based on
the present thermodynamic ﬁndings.
(4) The oxidation path of the Ni-Co-Fe alloy and the corresponding
composition change of the NiO-CoO-FeO oxide phase at 1673 K
under increasing oxygen partial pressure were quantitively ex-
amined. It was found that the oxidation path of the Ni-Co-Fe alloy
moves toward the Ni-Co binary alloy ﬁrst and then toward the
nickel corner at higher oxygen partial pressures.
(5) Using of the thermodynamic ﬁndings allows to easily and directly
ascertain the high-temperature oxidation phenomena of the Ni-Co-
Fe based alloy.
