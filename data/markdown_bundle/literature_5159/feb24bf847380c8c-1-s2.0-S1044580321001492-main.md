ABSTRACT 
Refractory niobium-based alloy Nb521 (Nb-5 W-2Mo-1Zr) was fabricated by electron beam selective melting 
(EBSM). The microstructure feature of as-built Nb521 alloy was systematically studied in combination with the 
unsteady thermal cycling inherent in the EBSM process. The content of precipitates gradually increased from the 
top region to the bottom region for EBSM-built Nb521 samples. Dot-like or rod-like precipitates with different 
morphologies were distributed inside grains or along grain boundaries in the as-built sample, whereas needle-like 
precipitates with a large aspect ratio existed in Nb521 alloy ingot. All precipitates in the EBSM-built Nb521 
sample mainly contain (Nb, Zr)C and Nb
2
C. With prolonging the thermal balance holding time, the elongated 
precipitates were partially broken, and became fine and uniform. It is expected that the results obtained in this 
study can lay a solid theoretical and technological basis for the fabrication of refractory Nb-based alloy by the 
EBSM process. 
1. Introduction 
The rapid development of aerospace puts forward an urgent need for 
capable materials that withstand extraordinary conditions in hot-end 
components [ 1 ]. Niobium alloys with superior high temperature 
strength [ 2 – 4 ], excellent corrosion resistance, and heat resistance [ 5 – 7 ] 
have become one of the promising materials for fabricating hot-end 
components. However, traditional metalworking processes, such as 
powder injection molding, vacuum-arc melting and welding, introduce 
numerous issues, for instance, long-term process, high tooling and mold 
requirements due to high melting point and poor oxidation resistance of 
niobium alloys [ 8 , 9 ]. Therefore, it is urgent to introduce a new method 
to fabricate niobium alloy rapidly in order to meet the growing needs for 
their components in the aviation industry. 
Additive manufacturing (AM) processes are based on incremental 
layer-by-layer manufacturing, and offer advantages such as delivery 
time-saving and free designing, proposing an attractive route for form -
ing aerospace intricate parts [ 5 , 6 , 10 , 11 ]. Electron beam selective 
melting (EBSM), as a kind of the powder-bed based metal AM processes, 
adopts a high-energy electron beam as the heat source, which has the 
advantages of high vacuum atmosphere [ 12 ], high energy absorption 
rate and low residual stress. A high vacuum degree is suitable for 
forming reactive materials with high affinity for oxygen, such as tita -
nium alloys [ 13 , 14 ], and the high energy absorption rate can enable the 
full melting of refractory materials with high melting point, such as 
tungsten [ 15 , 16 ], molybdenum [ 17 ], zirconium [ 18 ], niobium [ 19 ], etc. 
Low residual stress of components can improve the success rate and 
avoid waste samples caused by cracking. Meanwhile, the feature of rapid 
melting and rapid solidification inherent in the EBSM process will favor 
the formation of finer microstructure and precipitates than the tradi -
tional casting and forging processes. However, the morphologies and 
size of precipitates in EBSM-built samples will vary during layer-by- 
layer cycle heating and cooling, resulting in their heterogeneity of me -
chanical properties [ 20 – 22 ]. Therefore, to fabricate niobium alloys 
using EBSM is a challenging and promising project. To date, scant 
literature available was devoted to manufacturing niobium-based alloys 
by the AM process. Dense Nb components were fabricated by EBSM 
using bimodal powders [ 23 ], and the evolution of grain structure of as- 
built niobium alloys was studied. However, up to now, the precipitates 
are rarely mentioned in the literature, although they have a significant 
impact on mechanical properties of EBSM-built parts [ 24 – 26 ]. 
This study aimed to verify the feasibility of EBSM to produce the 
Nb – W – Mo – Zr system alloy, Nb521 (nominal composition Nb-5 W-2Mo-

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. EBSM processability of Nb521 alloy 
The effect of electron beam current and scanning speed on the sur -
face morphologies of EBSM-built Nb521 samples was shown in Fig. 3 (a), 
and the parameters indicated by the same symbol represented a similar 
upper surface. Porous surface, flat surface and wavy surface were 
involved in these samples, corresponding to the insufficient energy 
input, moderate energy input and excessive energy input during the 
EBSM process, respectively. An appropriate processing window can be 
identified after initial processing trials. As can be seen in Fig. 3 (a), flat 
upper surface of EBSM-built Nb521 alloy samples can be obtained under 
a scanning speed of 0.7 m/s and beam current ranging from 13 mA to 16 
mA, and the outer appearance was shown in Fig. 3 (b). The sample 
fabricated at 15 mA had the smoothest surface as the beam current in -
creases from 13 mA to 16 mA. The 3D surface morphologies of these 
samples in Fig. 3 (b) were measured by CLSM, as shown in Fig. 3 (c). 
Cloud maps in Fig. 3 (c) indicated the flatness of the sample surface, and 
a decrease in the contrast of the cloud map suggested an improvement in 
the flatness. The contrast of the cloud map at 15 mA tended to be 
identical, indicating no significant valleys or peaks on the sample sur -
face [ 33 ]. The relative density of these samples in Fig. 3 (b) fluctuated 
between 96.15% and 99.19%, as shown in Fig. 3 (d), and a near-full 
dense sample was obtained under a beam current of 15 mA. 
The upper surface morphology and internal defects of EBSM-built 
samples were closely related to the flow and solidification of the 
molten pool. The molten pool was strongly dynamic and its fundamental 
behavior was governed by capillary forces in combination with wetting 
[ 34 , 35 ]. The solidification morphology of the molten pool was strongly 
dependent on the wetting characteristics of the molten pool with the 
powders. As the insufficient energy applied, the molten pool was small 
and powders near the molten pool boundary were partially melted. 
Driven by the surface tension, a solidified connection was formed be -
tween the melted portion of the powders and the molten pool [ 36 ], and 
pores between them remained after solidification. Moreover, powders 
close to the molten pool boundary will be dragged into the melted re -
gion, as shown in the insert images in Fig. 3 (d) under 13 mA and 14 mA. 
When the moderate energy input was adopted, the fluctuation of the 
molten pool was relatively stable and powders were fully melted, 
ensuring no pores inside the sample and a flat surface after

3.2. Microstructures under different conditions 
The vertical cross-section micrographs of EBSM-built Nb521 alloy 
samples under various electron beam currents were examined in the top, 
middle and bottom regions, and the results were shown in Fig. 4 . In all 
cases, white precipitates were distributed discretely in rod-like or dot- 
like morphologies on the Nb solid solution (Nbss) matrix. SEM images 
of top region were shown in Fig. 4 (a), (d), (g) and (j), where the pre -
cipitates were rod-like with high aspect ratio. In the middle region, the 
elongated precipitates were bent or broken, and a small amount of dot- 
like precipitates appeared. In the bottom region, the aspect ratio of rod- 
like precipitates decreased whereas the number of dot-like precipitates 
increased, and all the precipitates were homogeneously distributed on 
the Nbss matrix. The content of precipitates gradually increased from 
the top to the bottom regions for the sample under the same parameters. 
The morphology and size of precipitates varied significantly between 
different regions suggesting that there was a significant difference in 
thermal history between different layers. 
Driven by capillary forces, the morphology of precipitates upon 
elevated temperature exposure will change and eventually lead to 
spherical dispersoid shape [ 39 ]. The evolution in precipitates 
morphology was schematically depicted in Fig. 5 . For rod-like pre -
cipitates with a high aspect ratio, the shape was unstable with respect to 
the development and growth of perturbations. When the system was 
heated sufficiently to allow atomic migration, thermal grooves were 
formed in order to establish static equilibrium among surface tensions 
[ 39 , 40 ]. The formed grooves were surrounded by two ridges, and the 
curvature of these ridges promoted the mass transport away from the 
groove, further promoting the deepening of the grooves to achieve the 
re-establishment of tension balance. Precipitates split when the thermal 
grooves on the boundary were thick enough to penetrate the entire 
precipitate. During the long-term thermal cycle, fractured precipitates 
will become larger under the effect of Oswald ripening. Moreover, 
precipitates can be fully precipitated with prolonging the heat preser -
vation time, resulting in an increase in the precipitate content.

there were almost no precipitates in ultrafine grains. Moreover, white 
precipitates were distributed along the fusion boundaries, making the 
fusion boundaries more obvious, as shown in Fig. 6 (c)-(d). The partial 
enrichment of precipitates was mainly due to the fact that in the initial 
stage of molten pool formation, the heat transfer in the molten pool 
mainly depended on heat conduction. Owing to the insufficient mixing 
time and low convection, elements in the molten pool were inhomoge -
neously distributed. With the development of the molten pool, the mass 
transfer in the molten pool was dominated by strong Marangoni con -
vection. Under the combined action of Marangoni tangential force and 
thermal buoyancy, reflux occurred in different regions of the molten 
pool [ 42 ]. Since the temperature gradient in the upper region was larger 
than that in the bottom region of the molten pool, the reflux in the upper 
region was intenser, and elements tended to accumulate on the 
periphery of the molten pool. Elements concentration at the rear edge 
and the periphery of the molten pool were higher than that at other 
positions [ 42 , 43 ]. Meanwhile, an unmixed zone was observed at the 
bottom of the molten pool. In the solidification process of multicom -
ponent alloys system, microsegregation existed in the molten pool due to 
the banding during molten pool solidification, causing perturbations in 
the solidification structure and the solute concentration [ 44 , 45 ]. Due to 
the high cooling rate, the diffusion of elements into the solid phase was 
incomplete in multi-component alloys. During the layer-by-layer accu -
mulation process, the solutes within the molten pool were redistributed. 
For the remelting process, the concentration at the bottom of pre- 
solidified layers remained since the remelting only occurred at the top 
part of the pre-solidified layer. The heterogeneous distribution of these 
elements at the bottom of the molten pool led to the enrichment of 
precipitates along the fusion boundary.

4. Conclusions 
The refractory Nb521 alloy was fabricated by EBSM. The evolution 
mechanism of precipitates in combination with thermal history inherent 
in the EBSM process was elaborated. The following conclusions can be 
drawn: 
(1) The feasibility of refractory Nb521 alloy fabricated by EBSM was 
verified, and EBSM-built Nb521 with a high relative density of 
> 99.19% can be obtained at an electron beam current of 15 mA 
and a scanning speed of 0.7 m/s. 
(2) Long rod-like and dot-like precipitates were distributed intra- 
granular and along the grain boundaries. Ultrafine dot-like pre -
cipitates that enrich in Zr and C elements were determined as 
(Nb, Zr)C and Nb
2
C, and rod-like precipitates enriched in C ele -
ments were determined to be Nb
2
C. 
(3) As the thermal cycle numbers increased and the holding time 
prolonged, the morphology of precipitates at the grain boundary 
changed from the intermittent chain to dot-like, whereas part of 
the elongated precipitates inside grains was broken up and 
became spherical and uniform.
