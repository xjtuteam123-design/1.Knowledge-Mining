abstract
Single-phase body-centered cubic (BCC) lightweight Ti eVeNbeZrx solid-solution alloys
were fabricated by reducing the Zr concentration within the range of 0 e5 at% to improve
the mechanical properties of equimolar TiVNbZr alloy at elevated temperatures. The
fabricated Zr x alloys indeed exhibited greater strength above 800 /C14 C than TiVNbZr, but
some special mechanical responses occurred at 800 /C14 C: (i) superﬁcial cracks with different
lengths and amounts along the high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs) on the compressed
sample surface, and (ii) near-brittle fracture of the tensile stress-strain curves. Based on the
analysis of the deformed and undeformed surface, local stress concentration and phase
transformation were responsible for the cracks and difference in compression behavior,
and atmospheric oxygen, deformation localization and phase transformation were mainly
responsible for the tensile fracture difference at 800
/C14 C. Phase transformation promote
stress dispersion, combined with better oxidation resistance, which make the Zr-
containing alloys fractured via the transgranular mode in tensile test. In particular, the
Zr
3 alloy exhibited work hardening and tensile ductility. Based on the tensile and
compression properties of the Zr x alloys, it was found that at RT and 600 /C14 C, atomic size
difference ( dÞ governed alloy strength; from 800 to 1000 /C14 C, d and melting temperature ( Tm)
co-operatively governed alloy strength; at 1200 /C14 C, alloy strength was independent of d, but
dependent on Tm. The fabricated Ti eVeNbeZrx alloys have medium mechanical properties
but a small strength loss at high temperatures, and are suitable for various RT deformation
processing methods.

1. Introduction
The investigation of lightweight, refractory high-entropy al-
loys (HEAs) for aerospace structural material or hydrogen
storage applications based on Ti eVeZreNb alloys [ 1,2] has
provided in-depth insights into these alloys and broadened
their scope to various alloy systems, such as Al eTieVeNb [3],
AleTieVeCreNb [4,5], and Al eTieVeZreNb [6] with densities
lower than Ti eVeZreNb, as well as Ti-Cr-Zr-Nb [ 1],
CreTieVeZreNb [ 1], Al eTieCreNbeMo [ 7], and Al eTie
ZreNbeTa [ 8,9] with densities slightly higher than
TieVeZreNb. The equimolar Ti eVeZreNb alloy exhibited
good strength from room temperature (RT) to 600
/C14 C, but an
abrupt decrease in strength was observed at 800 /C14 C with ten-
sile and compression strengths below 150 and 187 MPa,
respectively [ 10]. Al or Cr partially improves the high-
temperature strength of alloys, but a signiﬁcant loss in RT
elongation or strength occurs, which is not conducive for the
processing and application of these materials.
Based on our previous research on the microstructural
evolution of the Ti eVeZreNb alloy, the cause of the rapid
decline in the high-temperature strength of this material was
the considerable precipitation of Zr from the alloy at tem-
peratures above 400
/C14 C[ 10]. Moreover, it was reported that
~6e9 vol% and ~20 e30 mm Zr- and V-rich phases precipitated
after the alloy was homogenized at 1200 /C14 C. Therefore, the
current Ti eVeZreNb alloys generally have a composite
structure of 100-micron-scaled equiaxed parent crystals with
internal micron-scaled Zr- and V-rich particles [ 2,11,12],
instead of a stable structure in the entire operating tempera-
ture range. The secondary precipitates have a coarse particle
size with an incoherent lattice compared to the matrix, which
does not have a good dispersion strengthening effect. Mean-
while, decreasing the Zr and V contents considerably reduced
the mixing entropy and lattice distortion in the body-centered
cubic (BCC) solid solution, minimized the diffusion barrier
between atoms, and weakened the solid-solution strength-
ening effect, resulting in a signiﬁcant decrease in the overall
strength of the alloy at high temperatures.
Solid-solution strengthening is the main mechanism of
HEAs. Single-phase alloys have higher strengths than
multiple-phase alloys at high temperatures [ 13]. Therefore,
the stability of the solid-solution phase is an essential factor
for ensuring the stability of entropy and solid-solution
strengthening. For the Ti eVeZreNb system, stability is
likely inﬂuenced by three factors: (i) the eutectic precipitation
reaction of Zr and V [ 14]; (ii) the interaction between Zr and
Nb at approximately 600
/C14 C[ 14]; (iii) the Zr concentration
causes a large atomic radius difference in the system,
because Zr has the largest atomic size among the four ele-
ments [ 15]. Based on the above-mentioned analysis, Zr is
common among the three factors. Both V and Nb are
considered elements with stable elastic parameters (inde-
pendent of temperature), which have signiﬁcant stabilizing
effects on the phase structure and high-temperature perfor-
mance of the alloy [ 16,17]. Therefore, the tailored design and
regulation of Zr content is key for reducing the compositional
ﬂuctuation of Nb and V in the solid solution to obtain a stable
phase and structure in the alloy.
In the equimolar Ti eVeZreNb alloy, 25% Zr addition
resulted in an atomic radius difference d of 6.8%, which
formed an alloy with a BCC composite structure and
nonuniform precipitates. This caused phase decomposition of
the alloy at an intermediate temperature, leading to rapid
softening above 800
/C14 C, as mentioned previously. Based on the
binary phase diagram, reducing the Zr content by 5 at% results
in the formation of a BCC solid solution between the Zr and V
elements in the temperature range of ~500 e1800
/C14 C. For this
range of Zr content, d is below 5.1%, which is 25% lower than
that in the equimolar Ti eVeZreNb alloy. A large d value of
/C21 6.6% leads to phase instability [ 18], whereas an inadequate d
value ( <4%) is generally unfavorable for solid-solution hard-
ening [19]. Based on current research, a desirable d mismatch
has a positive effect on strength and ductility.
Additionally, modulus mismatch is considered a key factor
that affects the mechanical properties of HEAs [ 16], but has an
insigniﬁcant effect on face-centered cubic (FCC) or BCC alloys
[19e21]. Therefore, modulus mismatch is considered negligible
for the design used in this study. Furthermore, the melting
temperature ( T
m) of the alloy signiﬁcantly affects its high-
temperature strength [ 13]. By comparing the mechanical and
physical properties of numerous HEAs, it was demonstrated
that at high temperatures especially above 1000 /C14 C, alloys with
higher Tm values have improved strength. Therefore, alloys
typically achieve a high Tm value and single-phase structure to
maintain a small strength loss at high temperatures.
Based on our design, compared with the equimolar
TiVNbZr alloy (theoretical Tm:1973 /C14 C), Zr-deﬁcient TiVNbeZrx
(x /C20 5 at%) alloys have a single phase, moderate d and higher
Tm values, as listed in Table 1. Thus, it is hypothesized that the
loss in alloy strength with temperature is considerably lower
than that of the equimolar TiVNbZr alloy, which is beneﬁcial
for mitigating softening at high temperatures. Therefore, this
study aims to design and fabricate Ti eVeNbeZr
x (x ¼ 0, 1, 3,
and 5 at%) alloys by controlling the Zr concentration. Addi-
tionally, the phase structures of the alloys are characterized.
Moreover, the strength and plasticity of the alloys in tem-
perature range of RT to 1200
/C14 C is investigated, and the
mechanism is discussed.

3.3. Tensile properties of the TiVNb eZrx alloys
The tensile engineering stress-strain curves of the Zrx alloys at
RT, 600 /C14 C, and 800 /C14 C are shown in Fig. 2 . At RT, the tensile
strength improved gradually by increasing the Zr concentra-
tion from 0 to 5 at%. The Zr 5 alloy exhibited the highest yield
strength ( s0.2) of 840 MPa with a maximum strength ( Rm)o f
870 MPa, but the lowest elongation of approximately 13%. A
positive relationship between strength and d was established.
Thus, in these composition-dependent single-phase HEA al-
loys, solid-solution strengthening at RT is likely controlled by
d. Therefore, a larger d results in a higher strength. At 600 and
800
/C14 C, both s0.2 and Rm exhibited the same trend, that is, they
increased with Zr concentration, but leveled off in the Zr-
containing alloys at 800
/C14 C, exhibiting a similar s0.2 value of
approximately 455 MPa and R m of approximately 460 MPa.
The Zr x alloys with higher Zr concentrations have rela-
tively higher strength, but lower plasticity, which corresponds
to the known compromise between strength and plasticity.
Notably, the Zr
3 alloy has an optimal strength-plasticity bal-
ance at all the temperatures investigated in this study. How-
ever, there are two noteworthy points: (i) tensile elongation of
the Zr
x alloys has a negative relationship with temperature,
exhibiting an almost ductile-brittle transition at 800 /C14 C, which
is in contrast to the common understanding that dislocation
slip is promoted at intermediate temperatures when strength
loss becomes evident; (ii) the strength difference between the
Zr-containing alloys decreased at 800
/C14 C, indicating that the
alloys have similar mechanical properties that are weakly
dependent of the atomic size difference d.

What effect does phase transformation have on tension or
compression? And how do they relate to the bands found after
deformation? The physical parameters of the HCP and A2

their compositions, as listed in Table 2 . The HCP phase has a

During loading, when dislocations meet the generated hcp

making the dislocations accumulate continuously around
these phases, and form dislocation bands [ 33]. The composi-
tion of the HCP phase is dependent on the Zr concentration,
thus there will be a larger fraction of HCP phase and defor-
mation bands present in Zr
5 alloy.

tropy alloys at elevated temperatures, heat transfer between
the edge and central zone is out of sync, causing localization
of deformation, which shows intergranular fracture at the
edge and transgranular at the middle. This is also the main
reason for core cracking in vacuum compression. Cracks were

x
alloys exhibit a strength trend in terms of competition between
the d and Tm values at different temperatures. At RT and 600/C14 C,
d governs alloy strength, and thus alloys with a larger d have

alloy strength is governed by the variation in the Tm.

Zrx alloys and other alloys
A direct comparison of the mechanical properties dependent

limited RT ductility for structural applications. Moreover,
they exhibit thermal stability, and have the highest strength

Compared with the alloys prepared with the same ele-

T

TieVeZreNb alloy which has multiple phases at temperature
above 400 /C14 C. Generally, the Ti eVeNbeZrx HEAs possess good
ductility, moderate strength, and a small strength loss at high
temperatures among the lightweight BCC HEAs. Therefore,
the developed alloys can be applied within 1000
