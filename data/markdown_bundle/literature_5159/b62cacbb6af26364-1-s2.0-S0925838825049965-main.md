ABSTRACT
Light weight, low cost, and superior mechanical strength are the common goals of Mg product development. 
However, it is hardly to synchronously obtain these accomplishments in the high-temperature tensile loading, 
owing to the increased cost brought by rare-earth additions for realizing the strong high-temperature strength. 
This work provides one novel Mg-1.09Bi-0.93Ca dilute alloy with pre-twinned microstructure, which is fabri -
cated by 5-pass multi-directional forging. Compared to the fine-grained and coarse-grained microstructures, the 
pre-twinned microstructure corresponds to the highest yield strengths at both 25
◦
C and 200
◦
C (221 and 
159 MPa). Compared to the previous publications, the present Mg-1.09Bi-0.93Ca alloy exhibits the excellent 
combination of light weight (total alloying weight percents of 2.02 %), low cost (no use of rare-earth) and strong 
high-temperature strength (159 MPa). Such excellent mechanical behavior at 200
◦
C is assisted by the preset 
twins, which are fragmented together with the matrices during tensile loading. These tiny twin/grain fragments 
in turn successfully enhance the high-temperature strength, avoiding the common deterioration on the high- 
temperature strength induced by boundary migration. Thus, the novel Mg-1.09Bi-0.93Ca dilute alloy with 
pre-twinned microstructure can be safely utilized in the engineering fields with complex temperature conditions.
1. Introduction
Nowadays, Mg alloys have been widely used in electronic products, 
aerospace devices, automotive vehicles and other engineering fields due 
to their low density and high specific strength [1 – 4] . However, the 
hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structure inevitably leads to the easy 
twinning at room temperature and the resultant mismatch between 
strength and ductility [5 – 7] . To meet the industrial requirements, some 
trials have been successfully done in the optimization of 
strength-ductility synergy. Grain refinement is an important route for 
simultaneously improving the strength and ductility of Mg alloys 
[8 – 10] . Specifically, grain boundaries can act as hard obstacles for basal 
slip at room temperature, and this strengthening effect can be succes -
sively improved by increasing grain boundaries via further grain 
refinement [11] . Concurrently, non-basal dislocations in fine grains can 
have the opportunity to move across the whole grain interior rather than 
the interrupted movement in coarse grains [12] .
However, the grain boundaries usually become weak at the tem -
peratures approaching to or over 150
◦
C, leading to the invalid grain 
boundary strengthening effect and the sharply reduced strength in fine- 
grained Mg alloys [13] . For examples, the yield strength of fine-grained 
Mg – 1.68Mn – 0.69Ce alloy at 200
◦
C decreased to less than a half of the 
ambient yield strength [14] . More seriously, the tensile strength of 
fine-grained AZ91 Mg alloy sharply decreased from 370 MPa at 25
◦
C to 
110 MPa at 200
◦
C [15] . The strength of coarse-grained Mg alloys de -
creases mildly at high temperatures, but actually the original strength at 
room temperature is non-ideal [12,16] . Therefore, the above-mentioned 
trade-off between room-temperature strength and high-temperature 
strength leads to the application difficulty of Mg alloys in a wide 
range of service temperatures.
To date, the commonly recognized method for improving high- 
temperature strength is adding rare-earth (RE) elements [17 – 20] . 
Nonetheless, the high price and the high weight of RE elements such as 
Gd and Nd inevitably increase the development cost and weaken the 
lightweight advantage of Mg alloys. Then, the development of novel Mg 
alloys with low or no RE additions is strongly required. Zhang et al. [21]
designed one Mg-2.5Nd-1.0Sm-0.4Zn-0.1Ca-0.5Zr-2Ag alloy, with 
increased amounts of β phases triggered by Ag atoms. Their excellent

ductility. Inspired by the advantages of pre-twinning, in this work, one 
Mg-Bi-Ca dilute alloy, with the total element amounts lower than 2 wt%, 
is employed and the effect of its twinned microstructure on the 
high-temperature strength is studied. According to the previous reports

Mg-Bi-Ca series would be greatly enhanced in various engineering 
fields. For this wish, this work focusing on the high-temperature 
strength is performed and relative mechanisms are analyzed.

4.2. Dislocation activation at 200
◦
C
In order to disclose the deformation mechanisms in 200TW sample, 
TEM bright fields are taken using two-beam mode. The incident beam is 
B = [ 2 1 10 ] , and two diffraction vectors are g = [ 0002 ] and g = [ 01 10 ] , 
respectively. The observations for initial TW, deformed 200FG and 
200CG samples are also performed as the comparisons. In Fig. 13 a, the 
incident beam is selected in the upper left grain, in which high-density 
dislocations are observed in initial TW sample fabricated by MDF 
processing. According to the extinction criterion g • b = 0 and the 
impossible existence of independent < c > dislocations [19,51] , both 
pyramidal < c + a > and basal < a > dislocations can be confirmed as 
closely piling up at the TB. Specifically, the < c > components in the 
pyramidal < c + a > dislocations are tilting large angles to the (0002) 
trace, which are the typical dislocation morphologies standing for high 
stored energy [47] . In Fig. 13 b, one ultra-fine grain derived from 
twinning-induced fragmentation is found in 200TW sample at ε = 15%, 
with sharply decreased dislocation densities compared to the initial 
state. Since the tensile temperature at 200
◦
C is still low for the recrys -
tallization nucleation [83] , the disappeared dislocations are probably 
employed for generating sub-grains and evolving into ultra-fine frag -
ments. In this way, the serious stress concentration for deteriorating the 
ductility can also be avoided as shown in the KAM maps in Fig. 11 a.
In Figs. 14 a and 14 c, very few dislocations are observed in initial FG 
sample and almost no dislocations are observed in initial CG sample, 
corresponding to the dynamic recrystallized microstructure after 
extrusion and static recrystallized microstructure after annealing very 
well. Namely, the tensile deformation processes of FG and CG samples 
are finished via dislocation multiplication rather than dislocation 
disappearance in the case of TW sample. In Fig. 14 b, no evident pyra -
midal dislocation traces can be observed in 200FG sample at ε = 15% 
under the g = [ 0002 ] diffraction. The active dislocations are all 
< a > type and cross-slipping from basal planes to the adjacent prismatic 
planes, leading to the grain auto-rotation as shown in Fig. 7 b. The 
absence of pyramidal < c + a > slip can be attributed to the needless ac -
commodation for the plastic deformation induced by the inactive 
twinning [84] . Being one of the soft deformation modes for accelerating 
strain increment [85] , the < a > dislocation cross-slip accounts for the
