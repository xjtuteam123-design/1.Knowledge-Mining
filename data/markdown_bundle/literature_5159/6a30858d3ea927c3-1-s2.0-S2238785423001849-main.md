abstract
Severe intermediate-temperature embrittlement as well as rapid softening and oxidation
at high temperatures are signiﬁcant problems for high-entropy alloys (HEAs) in poly-
crystalline structures, which greatly limit their applications in modern industries. In this
study, a novel Co-rich single-crystal high-entropy alloy (HEA), Co
41Ni35Al11$5Ta2$5Cr4Ti6
(at.%), was innovatively developed. After isothermally aged at 1250 /C14 C, this alloy exhibits an
ultrahigh volume fraction of cuboidal L1 2-type nanoprecipitates (~80%) which is higher
than those of previous Co-based superalloys. More importantly, the present HEA shows
excellent mechanical performance at both ambient and elevated temperatures, and no
intermediate temperature embrittlement and rapid softening were observed. Noticeably,
the yield strength can still maintain at ~800 and ~500 MPa when tested at 900 and 1000
/C14 C,
which are superior to other conventional single-crystal alloys. Moreover, extraordinary
oxidation resistance can be achieved at the high temperature of 1000
/C14 C due to the for-
mation of a compact sandwich-structured oxide layer.

1. Introduction
In the modern power generation industry and aerospace en-
gineering, advanced structural materials with outstanding
high-temperature performance are urgently required to
enhance energy efﬁciency and reliability [ 1e3]. Recently, the
discovery of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) has demonstrated a
new paradigm for designing novel high-performance struc-
tural materials [ 4e8]. Most traditional alloys are developed

based on one principal element (i.e., C > 50 at.%). In contrast,
the HEAs usually contain three or more different elements
where no single element dominates [ 7]. Such a multi-
component-element alloying strategy offers greater possibil-
ities for compositional design to achieve desired performance
combinations. Among them, the bulk HEAs strengthened by
multicomponent L1
2 precipitates has attracted particular in-
terest, which provides a promising pathway to solve thorny
dilemmas in the ﬁeld of structural material [ 9e12]. For
example, by uniquely combining the thermally stable multi-
component L1
2 precipitates with the ductile face-centered-
cubic (FCC) matrix, comprehensive thermal-mechanical prop-
erties superior to those of many conventional Ni and/or Co-
based based superalloys can be potentially achieved [ 2,13,14].
However, like most structural materials, polycrystalline
HEAs usually suffer from serious intergranular embrittlement
issues at elevated temperatures. Speciﬁcally, serious inter-
granular embrittlement is always found in polycrystalline
materials at intermediate temperatures, which is mainly
attributed to the stress-assisted penetration of oxygen along
the grain boundaries resulting in premature failures [13,15,16].
In addition, dynamic recrystallization preferentially takes
place at grain boundaries under high-temperature deforma-
tion process for polycrystalline structural alloys, resulting in a
rapid softening behavior [ 17,18]. These critical issues severely
limit their large-scale applications, especially in some harsh
environments. As a result, extensive efforts have been
devoted to expanding the application temperature range of
HEAs, such as grain-boundary engineering [ 15,19]. Neverthe-
less, the results achieved now are far from satisfactory. On the
other hand, notably, the synthesis of single crystals enables
an effective pathway to eliminate the adverse effects of grain
boundaries, which is expected to greatly improve the tem-
perature capabilities of alloys. However, at present, most
studies are largely limited to the alloys with polycrystalline
structures, research on the single-crystal HEAs as well as their
properties are still very rare.
As a result, in this work, we elaborately designed a novel
single-crystal precipitation-strengthened HEA based on the
CoeNieAleTaeCreTi system, which exhibits a high volume
fraction of multicomponent L1
2 particles. The precipitation
behavior, including size and density, morphology, and
composition of the L1
2 precipitates, was carefully investi-
gated. Subsequently, the mechanical properties and oxidation
behaviors were carefully evaluated.
2. Experimental
The master alloy with a nominal composition of Co 41Ni35-
Al11$5Ta2$5Cr4Ti6 (at.%) was produced in a vacuum induction
furnace and then directionally solidiﬁed into [001] single-
crystal rods by the Bridgman technique at a constant with-
drawal rate of 3.5 mm/min. Afterwards, the alloys were
treated at 1250
/C14 C/48 h and followed by air cooling to ambient
temperature.
Dog-bone-shaped tensile samples with a gauge length of
12.5 mm and a cross-section of 3.2 /C2 2m m 2 were cut by
electrical-discharge machining. All the surfaces of the spec-
imen were ground to 2500 grit by SiC papers. The tensile tests
were performed at 25, 800, 900, and 1000
/C14 C with a strain rate of
1 /C2 10/C0 3 s/C0 1 on a Material Testing System (MTS, Alliance RT30)
tension machine. At least three specimens were measured for
each condition to conﬁrm the data reproducibility.
To evaluate the oxidation behavior of the alloy, the surface
of oxidized samples was ground with abrasive paper up to
2500 grit and then mechanically polished into a mirror. The
isothermal oxidation experiments were performed at 1000
/C14 C
by using a conventional resistance mufﬂe furnace in a labo-
ratory air condition for 100 h.
Scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Quanta FEG450)
equipped with an electron back-scattered diffraction (EBSD)
detector and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEOL
2100 F) equipped with a scanning TEM-energy dispersive X-ray
(STEM-EDX) detector were used for microstructural charac-
terizations. The SEM and EBSD specimens were mechanically
polished and then electro-polished in a solution of HNO
3
(~25%) and CH 3OH (~75%) with a direct voltage of 20 V at a
temperature of /C0 40 /C14 C. For the TEM investigation, the focus
ion beam (FIB) lift-out technique was used to prepare TEM
samples. The average sizes of the precipitates were measured
by Image-J software, and the volume fraction of precipitates
was derived from the lever rule based on the chemical
composition from the EDX results.

3.3. Anti-oxidation properties at 1000 /C14 C
In addition to the superior tensile properties, good oxidation
resistance is also necessarily required for high-temperature
applications. To this end, we further carefully investigated
the cross-sectional microstructure of oxide layer of the alloy
after the oxidation at 1000
/C14 C up to 100 h by the FIB-STEM
technique. Fig. 4 (a) shows a typical STEM image and corre-
sponding EDX maps of the oxide layers, and the chemical
compositions of regions 1 e3 are shown in Table 2. A thin and
sandwich-structured oxide scale with a total thickness of
~1.41 mm can be found clearly, and no apparent internal
oxidation zone is observed. Compared with the oxide scale
thickness of previous commercial superalloys oxidized at the
same condition, such as CMSX-4 (~30 mm) [ 39] and CMSX-10
(~50 mm) [ 40], our novel HEA exhibits extraordinary anti-
oxidation properties at 1000
/C14 C.
The outer layer mainly consists of (Co, Al, Ni)-rich oxides,
with minor Ti-rich oxide embedded in it. Fig. 4 (b) shows the
high-resolution image and related FFT pattern in region 1.
Combined with the EDX results in Table 2 , this outer oxide
layer can be identiﬁed as the (Co, Ni)Al

Compared with the outer layer, the inner layer formed a
continuous compact oxide ﬁlm which is believed to be an
effective barrier for the outward diffusion of metal cations and
inward diffusion of oxygen anions [ 44,45]. The Al

chromia has also been reported to be beneﬁcial for the for-
mation of continuous and compact alumina [ 46,47]. Notice-
ably, Ta can reduce oxygen activity at the surface of a Co-
based alloy [ 48], but has a detrimental effect on the Al diffu-

size suppressing [ 46,47]. The continuous Ta-rich oxide layer
inhibits the outward diffusion of Al, resulting in less Al
element in the outer layer than in the inner layer, thus
forming a sandwich structure. In addition, grain boundaries
are regarded as preferential diffusion paths for oxygen diffu-
sion of polycrystalline Ni eCo-based superalloys [ 42]. There-
fore, the elimination of grain boundaries by designing single-
crystal multicomponent alloys can further improve their
oxidation resistance for critical structural applications under
high temperatures.
4. Conclusion
In summary, a novel CoNiAlTaCrTi-type L1 2-strengthened
single-crystal HEA was elaborately developed. It exhibits an
excellent combination of tensile properties and anti-oxidation
performance at high temperatures. The excellent mechanical
performances are attributed to the high-volume fraction of
multicomponent L1
2 precipitates and the absence of grain
boundaries. In addition, the continuous sandwich-structured
oxide layer formed under the combined action of various el-
ements can effectively protect the alloys from severe oxygen
degradation even at the high temperature of 1000
/C14 C. Most
importantly, our current ﬁndings are expected to provide a
promising pathway to develop high-temperature structural
materials in the future.
