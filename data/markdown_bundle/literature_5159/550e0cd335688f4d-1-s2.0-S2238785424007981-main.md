ABSTRACT 
This work investigates the effect of Ru on the precipitation of the topologically close-packed (TCP) phase from 
the γ - γ ʹ microstructural degradation analysis. The addition of Ru is found to delay the precipitation time of the 
TCP phase from 100 h to 1000 h after thermal exposure at 950 
◦
C. The effect of Ru on the γ - γ ʹ microstructural 
degradation is also investigated. Adding Ru slightly decreases the coarsening rate of the γ ʹ size and promotes the 
reduction of the volume fraction of γ ʹ phase. The reduction of the volume fraction of γ ʹ phase causes the "inverse 
partitioning" effect and increases the solution limit of the TCP forming element in the matrix. These help inhibit 
the precipitation of the TCP phases in Ni-based single crystal superalloys during thermal exposure. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys are extensively used in the aero -
space industry owing to their exceptional comprehensive performance 
at high temperatures [ 1 – 5 ]. These alloys derive their high temperature 
performance from a unique microstructure consisting of L1
2 
ordered γ ʹ 
phase and disordered fcc γ phase matrix. Adding the refractory elements 
is the major strategy to increase the microstructural stability for their 
high melting points and low diffusion rates [ 6 – 10 ]. However, the 
excessive additions of these elements can promote the precipitation of 
the topologically close-packed (TCP) phase [ 11 – 15 ]. The TCP phase is 
the brittle phase, which will consume these strengthened elements, 
promote the internal stress concentrations and initiate cracks at the TCP 
phase precipitate/matrix interfaces [ 16 – 20 ]. Thus, the precipitation of 
the TCP phases will decrease the performance at high temperatures. It 
has been found that adding the Ru element is the main method to delay 
or restrain the precipitation of the TCP phase without sacrificing the 
content of refractory elements [ 21 – 25 ]. 
The incorporation of Ru in the alloy system affects the precipitation 
of the TCP phase through several mechanisms. Firstly, Ru induces an 
"inverse partitioning" effect by reducing the distribution coefficient of 
alloying elements within the γ / γ ʹ phases. This results in a lower content 
of refractory elements in the γ phase [ 25 – 27 ]. Secondly, Ru can impede 
the nucleation of the TCP phase, which is attributed to an elevation in 
the elastic strain energy at the TCP phase precipitate/matrix interfaces 
[ 28 ]. Thirdly, Ru increases the solid solubility of TCP phase-forming 
elements in the γ phase matrix [ 24 , 29 ]. Notably, some studies have re -
ported contradictory findings, suggesting that Ru may also promote the 
precipitation of the TCP phase [ 30 , 31 ]. For example, Chen et al. 
observed that the volume fraction of the TCP phases is 3.1% and 6.3% in 
0 wt.% Ru and 5 wt.% Ru alloys after thermal exposure 800 h at 1100 
◦
C 
[ 30 ]. Therefore, there is still a great controversy about the mechanism of 
Ru. 
In practical situations, the precipitation of the TCP phase is observed 
in the degraded γ - γ ʹ phases microstructure [ 11 , 32 – 35 ]. The researchers 
have also found that adding Ru would influence the degradation of the 
γ - γ ʹ phases. For example, Song et al. found the coarsening rate of the γ ʹ 
phase size is much faster in the 2.5 wt.% Ru alloy than that in the 3.5 wt. 
% Ru alloy after thermal exposure 1000 h at 1100 
◦
C [ 36 ]. Hobbs et al. 
also found a more rapid decrease in the γ ʹ fraction by adding 6 wt.% Ru 
during thermal exposure at 900

4. Discussion

ing Ru inhibits the precipitation of the TCP phase. It slightly decreases 
the coarsening rate of the γ ʹ size and promotes the reduction of the 
volume fraction of γ ʹ phase. To discuss the effect of γ ʹ - γ phase degrada -
tion on the TCP phase, the relationship between the addition of Ru and 
microstructure evolution needs to be clarified.

Based on the model, the size of the γ ʹ phase is determined by the 
volume fraction, equilibrium concentration, diffusion coefficient, and 
interfacial energy. As mentioned above, the 3Ru alloy contains a lower 
volume fraction of the γ ʹ phase, thus leading to a lower refractory 
element in the γ phase. These contribute to a lower coarsening rate of the 
γ ʹ size. The Ru exhibits a lower diffusion rate than most of the adding 
elements in superalloy [ 39 ], decreasing the diffusion coefficient in the 
matrix. This also leads to a lower coarsening rate of the γ ʹ size. Adding 
Ru increases the misfit, leading to a denser of dislocation after the 
thermal exposure. This is the only parameter that leads to a higher 
coarsening rate of the γ ʹ size. The comprehensive effect makes adding Ru 
slightly decrease the coarsening rate of the γ ʹ size. For the heat-treated 
microstructure, the interface energy governs the growth of the γ ʹ size 
at the first stage. The effect of the strain energy is not profound, leading 
to the addition of Ru, which can largely decrease the size of the γ ʹ phase. 
As illustrated in Fig. 3 , the volume fraction of γ ʹ phase in the 3Ru 
alloy is lower than that in the 0Ru alloy after heat treatment, and but its 
decreasing rate during the thermal exposure is slightly faster. The higher 
volume fraction of γ ʹ phase after heat treatment is consistent with the 
Ni – Al – Ru ternary phase diagram, as the addition of Ru will decrease the 
volume fraction of γ ʹ phase [ 40 ]. The 3Ru alloy contains a slighter higher 
density of dislocations, this may promote the decrease of the volume 
fraction of γ ʹ phase, as the dislocation can promote diffusion [ 41 , 42 ]. 
Accompanied by the reduction of the volume fraction of the γ ʹ phase, the 
content of the γ ʹ phase forming elements in the γ phase would increase as 
the content of γ ʹ phase is usually fixed. This leads to the segregation 
efficiency of the γ ʹ phase forming elements moving towards 1. In addi -
tion, increasing the volume fraction of the γ phase increases the 
maximum solubility of refractory elements in it. This makes the segre -
gation efficient of the γ phase forming elements also moving towards 1. 
In these regards, the "inverse partitioning" effect of Ru proceeded, as 
shown in Fig. 5 . 
4.2. Effect of γ - γ ʹ microstructure on TCP phase 
Regarding the size of the γ ʹ phase, due to the larger size of the γ ʹ phase 
in the 0Ru alloy after heat treatment, although its coarsening rate of γ ʹ 
phase is essentially consistent with that of the 3Ru alloy after 1000 h 
treatment, the γ ʹ phase size remains higher than that of the 3Ru alloy, 
leading to more severe microstructural degradation. As for the volume 
fraction of the γ ʹ phase, the reduction of the volume fraction of the γ ʹ 
phase would cause the "inverse partitioning" effect and increase the 
maximum solubility of the TCP forming element as the volume fraction 
of the γ phase increases, this also consequently leads to a decrease in the 
segregation coefficient as depicted in Fig. 5 . These would inhibit the 
precipitation of the TCP phase caused by adding Ru. The "inverse par -
titioning" effect increases with thermal exposure and contradicts the 
TCP phase precipitation during thermal exposure. In addition, as shown 
in Fig. 3 , the precipitation of TCP phases also consumes refractory ele -
ments in the γ phase, resulting in the TCP phases being surrounded by 
large-sized γ ʹ phases, further promoting the process of microstructural 
degradation. 
A claim should be made that the precipitation of the TCP phase is an 
uphill diffusion process [ 43 ]. Even though the TCP forming elements in 
the γ matrix decrease with thermal exposure, it still can be precipitated 
from the matrix for its higher thermodynamic stability [ 44 ]. The lower 
TCP forming elements in the γ matrix can only delay its precipitation 
time. In addition, the similar segregation behavior of the forming ele -
ments in dislocation and the TCP phase may promote the precipitation of 
the TCP phase. The segregation of the elements in the dislocation is well 
recognized in the literature [ 45 – 47 ]. The lattice distortion exists near 
the edge dislocation line, resulting in increased energy and rendering the 
crystal in an unstable state. Solute atoms tend to segregate to the tensile 
portion of the edge dislocation because it reduces lattice distortion, 
making the system more stable, so the dislocation enriches both the 
γ -forming and γ ʹ -forming elements. The γ ʹ -forming elements promote γ ʹ 
phase coarsening and are consequently consumed. In these regards, the 
dislocation is rich in the γ -forming elements. 
5. Conclusion 
This study investigates the effect of Ru on the precipitation of the 
TCP from the γ - γ ʹ microstructural degradation analysis. The experi -
mental evidence and the discussions presented above enable the 
following main conclusions: 
(1) The addition of Ru delays the precipitation time of the TCP phase 
from 100 h to 1000 h after thermal exposure at 950 
◦
C. 
(2) Adding Ru decreases the volume fraction and size of the γ ʹ phase. 
It slightly decreases the coarsening rate of the γ ʹ size and pro -
motes the reduction of the volume fraction of γ ʹ phase. 
(3) The reduction of the volume fraction of γ ʹ phase causes the "in -
verse partitioning" effect and increases the solution limit of the 
TCP forming element in the matrix. 
(4) The reduction of the volume fraction of γ ʹ phase increases with 
thermal exposure, leading to a homogenous distribution of the Ru 
in the γ ʹ and γ phases.
