abstract
Short-term mechanical properties and void swelling were investigated for high-nickel alloys PE-16 and
three compositional variants of Russian alloy EP-753 and in various starting conditions after side-by-side
irradiation in the BN-350 fast reactor at 400, 500, 600 and 650 /C176C to 54 dpa. For both alloys irradiation
resulted in signiﬁcant hardening and ductility reduction dependent on their chemical composition and
initial heat treatment. At test temperatures equal to the irradiation values both alloys exhibited a high
level of strength and satisfactory ductility. In the test temperature range of 550–650 /C176C the phenomenon
of high-temperature irradiation embrittlement was observed.

1. Introduction
Some high-nickel alloys possess a unique combination of phys-
ical and mechanical properties that allow them to serve extended
in-reactor life-times over a wide range of temperature. In contrast
to austenitic or ferritic/martensitic steels many high-nickel alloys
also have a higher level of high temperature strength. These alloys
often have a higher resistance to void swelling and thereby a lower
irradiation creep rate. The higher strength is a result of hardening
via extensive precipitation of the gamma-prime precipitates dur-
ing controlled aging, although irradiation is known to redistribute
these precipitates to different size distributions and often to other
microstructural locations [1,2].
In contrast, the major shortcoming of high-nickel alloys is
their inclination toward the phenomenon of high-temperature
irradiation embrittlement, often designated as HTIE [3,4]. This
embrittlement is thought to arise from the combined action of
gamma-prime precipitation on grain boundaries and helium
bubble accumulation on these boundaries. Helium generation in
fast reactor spectra is known to be directly proportional to the
nickel content of the alloys [5,6].
In spite of the HTIE problem the high-nickel alloy Nimonic PE-
16 is recognized as one of most irradiation-resistant alloys and
has received signiﬁcant attention by other researchers. This alloy
was successfully shown in irradiations in the Dounreay Fast Reac-
tor (DFR) to exhibit improved swelling resistance [7,8]. PE-16 was
used in the solution-treated and aged condition as a structural
material for fuel pin cladding of the Prototype Fast Reactor (PFR)
in the United Kingdom, reaching fuel burn-ups of P15% heavy
atoms at doses at 110 dpa, while one fuel assembly achieved 19%
burn-up at 155 dpa [9–11].
The high-nickel alloy EP-753 (18Cr–40Ni–5Mo–Mn–Nb) has
been used in Russia for similar fast reactor cladding applications
as served by PE-16 in the UK and other western countries. In this
paper the results are presented for investigations of mechanical
properties of alloys PE-16 and EP-753 (the latter in three composi-
tional variations) after neutron irradiation in various metallurgical
starting conditions at temperatures between 400 and 650 /C176C. Void
swelling is also investigated but only for irradiation at 500 /C176C.
In this investigation PE-16 was irradiated side-by-side with EP-
753, providing a standard against which to assess the relative per-
formance of the Russian alloy.
2. Material and experimental details
EP-753 of Russian production and PE-16 of UK production were
investigated in side-by-side irradiations. Three compositional
modiﬁcations of the EP-753 alloy were used. These were EP-753
alloy in the solution treated condition (EP-753 (ST)); EP-753 alloy
doped with boron (0.01 wt.%) in the solution treated condition
(EP-753 + B (ST)); and EP-753 alloyed with titanium, aluminum
and boron in the solution treated + aged condition (EP-753 + B +
Ti + Al (A)). The alloy PE-16 was investigated in two conditions;
solution treated and solution treated + aged. The chemical compo-
sition, initial treatment and the mean grain size of the alloys are
shown in Tables 1 and 2 .
The specimens were in the form of cylindrical tensile bars with
a gauge diameter of 3 mm, a grip section diameter of 6 mm, a
gauge length of 15 mm and a total length of 26 mm. These

3. Results
3.1. Unirradiated alloys
Mechanical properties of the unirradiated alloys are shown in
Figs. 1–4 . The yield strength of the alloy EP-753 (ST) decreases
gradually with increasing test temperature from 20 to 650 /C176C. Fur-
ther increase of the test temperature leads to a steeper decrease in
strength. In the range 20–750 /C176C the ductility of this alloy changes
slightly, with the total elongation never less than 35%.
Alloying of the alloy EP-753 with boron did not inﬂuence its
mechanical properties in the investigated range of test tempera-
tures. However, as anticipated, the combined effect of boron, tita-
nium and aluminum additions plus aging has resulted in a strong
increase of yield strength at all test temperatures. The values of to-
tal elongation of the aged and solution treated alloys differ insig-
niﬁcantly in the 20–650 /C176C range of test temperatures. However,
further increase of the test temperature results in a sharp ductility
reduction in the aged alloy.

4. Discussion
From data shown in Figs. 5 and 7 it is seen that the temperature
dependence of radiation-induced changes in yield strength of the
two high-nickel alloys are rather similar. First, irradiation at low
temperatures (400, 500 /C176C) results in hardening, with the degree
of hardening depending on the initial heat treatment. Second, at
some test temperature the strength properties of irradiated and
unirradiated materials become comparable and a further increase
of temperature results in stronger softening of irradiated material
in comparison with unirradiated material. At irradiation tempera-
tures in the 400 /C176C–600 /C176C range the total elongation was sufﬁ-
ciently high for application in fast reactors, with the exception of
EP-753 + B + Ti + Al (A) alloy irradiated at 600 /C176C.
The HTIE phenomenon in the high-nickel alloys is of special
interest and has been observed in other studies [2,3,12,13–18]. Re-
sults of the present work show that the sharp ductility reduction
with increasing test temperature above a certain value is typical
for all investigated alloys irradiated at temperatures below
650 /C176C. Both the low temperature limit of HTIE and the degree of
ductility reduction depend on alloy chemical composition, heat
treatment and irradiation temperature.
Table 4 summarizes the results of Ref. [12] where specimens at
each irradiation temperature were tested over a wide range of test
temperatures. The low temperature limit of HTIE versus irradiation
temperature is shown for each alloy condition in Table 4 . The test
temperature at which a sharp reduction of total elongation was ob-
served was taken as the lower limit of HTIE.
It is seen from Table 4 that the lowest temperature limit of HTIE
(550–600 /C176C) is observed in both conditions of PE-16 and in EP-
753 + B + Ti + Al (A). An increase of irradiation temperature from
500 to 600 /C176C raises somewhat the low temperature limit of alloy
EP-753 (ST). At the test temperatures at which HTIE is observed,
the degree of ductility reduction is different for the irradiated al-
loys. The most severe embrittlement producing zero values of total
elongation is observed in alloys PE-16 (STA) and EP-
753 + B + Ti + Al (A).
If we focus only on swelling, both PE-16 and EP-753 have opti-
mum nickel contents in light of data presented in Refs. [19–22],
since swelling usually exhibits a minimum in Fe–Cr–Ni alloys con-
taining nickel in the 35–50 wt.% range. On the other hand, differ-
ences of the minor element chemical composition and heat
treatment strongly inﬂuence not only mechanical properties, but
are known to also inﬂuence swelling [21–22]. Indeed, both density
measurements and electron microscopy have demonstrated some
additional resistance to swelling of alloys EP-753 + B + Ti + Al (A)
and PE-16 (ST + A) after irradiation at 500 /C176C to 54 dpa. The differ-
ence between density measurements of 1.4% and microscopy mea-
surements of 0.6% for EP-753 + B + Ti + Al (A) is probably due to a
relatively small lattice parameter change associated with the for-
mation of the gamma-prime phase.
In contrast, swelling values of EP-753 (ST) and EP-753 + B (ST)
alloys are somewhat higher at 3.7 and 2.2%, respectively. It is inter-
esting to note that boron addition might normally be expected to
accelerate swelling via additional generation of helium but boron’s
chemical effect appears to overwhelm its helium effect. Addition-
ally, the impact of nickel and boron on helium production are re-
versed in fast spectra, compared to the early burnout of boron in
thermalized spectra.
The relative resistance to swelling of the EP-753 + B + Ti + Al (A)
alloy was observed at a lower temperature (410 /C176C) in another
study that measured diameter strains and swelling of creep tubes
made of this alloy. Irradiation of creep tubes with hoop stresses
in the range of 0–300 MPa in the BN-350 reactor at 84 dpa resulted
only in a slight densiﬁcation [23].
Although precipitation of gamma prime on grain boundaries is
clearly a major cause of HTIE, the relative contribution of helium
and perhaps even hydrogen has not been fully resolved, and vari-
ous studies have addressed this possibility [24–26]. Ignoring
hydrogen’s possible role for the moment, the possibility has been
raised that helium bubbles forming and growing on grain bound-
aries under applied stress might account for all or some fraction
of the observed embrittlement [27]. Since nickel is the major
source of helium and hydrogen in fast reactor neutron spectra
[5,6,22], it is expected that high-nickel alloys might be particularly
prone to such a process.

Boothby directly addressed the possibility of helium bubble
embrittlement in a study on PE-16 that was irradiated in EBR-II
where grain boundary coating of gamma prime was observed to
coincide with embrittlement while /C24 1.2 appm of helium per dpa
was produced [26]. However, no conclusive evidence of bubble-in-
duced embrittlement under neutron irradiation has been observed
in this study or other studies, including that of Boothby. More work
is required to fully address this issue but the authors’ opinion is
that the dominant cause of HTIE is related to grain boundary
precipitation.
In general it appears that the irradiation experience accumu-
lated using both PE-16 and EP-753 show them to yield comparable
performance. Thus EP-753 can continue to be used successfully for
fast reactor applications, but care must be taken to avoid operation
at conditions where high-temperature irradiation embrittlement
will occur.
5. Conclusions
Side-by-side irradiation of high-nickel alloys EP-753 and PE-16
in the BN-350 fast reactor at temperatures ranging from 400 to
650 /C176C to 54 dpa followed by post-irradiation tensile testing lead
to the following conclusions.
EP-753 with modiﬁcations involving B, Ti and Al appears to be
suitable for applications where PE-16 has already demonstrated
superiority to lower-nickel steels. Both alloys exhibit comparable
behavior following irradiation but are sensitive to the starting
thermal–mechanical condition. At test temperatures equal to the
irradiation temperature both the yield strength and total elonga-
tion of EP-753 are sufﬁciently high for fuel pin application in fast
reactors.
Both PE-16 and EP-753 suffer from high-temperature irradia-
tion embrittlement (HTIE), with the lower temperature limit of
HTIE depending on chemical composition, heat treatment, and
irradiation temperature. The lowest temperature limit of HTIE is
observed in the aged condition for both PE-16 and EP-
753 + B + Ti + Al.
