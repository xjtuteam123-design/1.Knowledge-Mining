Revisiting the Diffusion of Niobium in an As‑Cast Nickel‑Based 
Superalloy During Annealing at Elevated Temperatures
Mohammad Javad Sohrabi1 · Hamed Mirzadeh1 
Received: 21 May 2019 / Accepted: 24 June 2019 
© The Korean Institute of Metals and Materials 2019
Abstract
While understanding the interdiffusion of Nb in the austenite matrix of Ni-based superalloys is important for studying the 
precipitation reactions (Laves phase, γ″ -precipitates and δ-phase) and homogenization of the as-cast structure, there is no 
report on the activation energy (Q) and pre-exponential factor (D
0). Moreover, for the Ni–Nb diffusion couple, the reported 
Q values of 202.6 and 257 kJ/mol are not consistent. In the present work, based on the concept of residual segregation index 
during homogenization treatment and elemental analyses from dendrite cores and interdendritic segregated regions, Q and 
D0 were respectively determined as ~ 236 kJ/mol and 0.0053 cm 2/s for the Ni-based superalloys. Improved analysis of the 
Ni–Nb system resulted in Q and D0 values of ~ 234 kJ/mol and 0.1618 cm2/s, respectively. Therefore, it was concluded that 
the activation energy for interdiffusion of Nb in superalloys and Ni–Nb diffusion couple is the same. However, due to the 
presence of other alloying elements in the superalloys, D
0 for Ni–Nb diffusion couple is ~ 30 times that of the superalloys.
Keywords Nb-bearing Ni-based superalloys · Residual segregation index · Interdiffusion · Activation energy
1 Introduction
Niobium is an essential alloying element in some widely 
used nickel-based superalloys, among them the Inconel 718 
alloy (with Nb in the range of 4.75–5.5 wt%) constitute more 
than 50% of worldwide production of superalloys [1–3].
Age hardening in this alloy is largely related to the pre-
cipitation of the body centered tetragonal Ni
3Nb γ″ phase 
[4–6]. The orthorhombic Ni3Nb δ-phase is also important 
in superalloys due to its effect on the amount of coherent 
γ″ precipitates, inhibition of grain growth, and affecting 
recrystallization processes [7 –12]. On the other hand, Nb 
is responsible for the serious micorsegregation [13–15] and 
formation of Ni2Nb Laves phase [15–17] during solidifica-
tion. Moreover, MC-type carbides (M is Nb, Ti, Ta, Hf, Th, 
Mo, and Zr) are among the common phases in Inconel 718 
alloy.
Therefore, understanding the interdiffusion of Nb in 
the austenite matrix is of upmost importance for studying 
the precipitation reactions, homogenization of the as-cast 
structure, controlling the thermomechanical processing, and 
high-temperature creep behavior.
There are some reported works on the interdiffusion of Nb 
in the matrix of nickel-based superalloy (D
Nb) [18–20]. Pan 
et al. [18] studied the homogenization of as-cast GH742y 
alloy at 1120 °C and 1160 °C and calculated two D
Nb based 
on the concept of residual segregation index [21–23]. Miao 
et al. employed the same concept for homogenization of 
as-cast Inconel 718 alloy at 1190 °C [19]. Campbell et al. 
[20] compared diffusion simulations to two experimental 
multicomponent Ni-base superalloy diffusion couples: Ni/
René-88 and IN718/René-88. However, the main diffu-
sion parameters such as the activation energy (Q ) and the 
pre-exponential factor (D
0) in the Arrhenius equation of 
D = D0 exp(−Q∕RT) have not been reported so far due to 
inadequate experimental data or inappropriateness of the 
analyzing method. As a result, DuPont et al. [24] used the 
diffusion parameters of Nb in γ-Fe for the interdiffusion of 
Nb in Ni-based superalloys. This reveals that this subjects 
needs much more attentions.
There are some reported works on the Nb–Ni diffusion cou-
ple, where Q
Nb–Ni has been reported as 202.6 kJ/mol [25] and 
257 kJ/mol [26]. Moreover, D0 values of 1.04E−02 cm2/s [25] 
and 88E−02 cm2/s [26] have been determined. It can be seen

2 Experimental Material and Procedure
2.1 Processing and Characterization
An as-cast ingot of Inconel 718 superalloy with the chemi-
cal composition shown in Table 1 was annealed at 1050 °C, 
1100 °C, and 1150 °C for holding times in the range of 1–9 h. 
The selection of annealing temperatures was based on the 
incipient melting temperature (IMT) of 1163–1175 °C [13], 
which is the upper limit for the annealing treatment. There-
fore, a high annealing temperature of 1150 °C was chosen for 
quick homogenization, which is important from the industrial 
standpoint. For investigation of the effect of temperature on the 
diffusion parameters, the temperatures of 1050 °C and 1100 °C 
were also considered.
The samples were electrolytically etched in a 3  ml 
HCl—1 ml HNO
3—6 ml glycerol solution at 6 V for ~ 10 s 
[28] and the microstructures were revealed using a FEI Nova 
450 FE-SEM. Elemental analysis was based on a Bruker 
QUANTAX XFlash 6 EDS detector (energy-dispersive 
X-ray spectroscopy) incorporating a true standardless ZAF 
correction.

4 Conclusions
The interdiffusion of Nb in the Ni-based superalloys and 
Ni–Nb diffusion couple was revisited. The following conclu-
sions can be drawn from this study:
1. Based on the concept of residual segregation index 
during homogenization treatment and elemental analy -
ses from dendrite cores and interdendritic segregated 
regions, the interdiffusion coefficient of Nb in the matrix 
was calculated. Based on the experimental results and 
incorporation of the reported data from other works, Q 
and D
0 were respectively determined as ~ 236 kJ/mol 
and 0.0053 cm2/s for the Ni-based superalloys.
2. Improved analysis of the Ni–Nb system via incorpora-
tion of data from two reported works resulted in Q and 
D
0 values of ~ 234 kJ/mol and 0.1618 cm 2/s, respec-
tively. The previously reported Q values of 202.6 and 
257 kJ/mol were not consistent but the combination of 
data showed that both work are consistent with each 
other.
