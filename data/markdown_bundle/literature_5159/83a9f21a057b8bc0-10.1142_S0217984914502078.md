The thermal stability is important for tungsten based alloys as plasma facing materials
to survive against high heat ﬂux in fusion reactors. In this work, the thermal stability of
W-5%V alloy fabricated following a powder metallurgy route by spark plasma sintering
technique has been studied. To investigate the impact of temperature on the mechanical
properties and microstructures, the alloy was subjected to heat treatment for 2 h over the
temperature range 900–1500
◦C in a pure argon furnace. The micro-hardness values of
the heat treated alloys were highly stable as compared to pure tungsten. A slight decrease
ﬂexural strength was observed with increasing annealing temperature. The maximum
change ﬂexural strength at the highest treated temperature was noted about 14% lower.
The morphology analyses of the crack surfaces by scanning electron microscopy did
not identify a drastic change in tungsten grain size, after heat treatment. The results
indicate that the addition of vanadium in tungsten improves the overall thermal stability
of microstructures and mechanical properties.
Keywords: W-5%V alloy; thermal stability; plasma facing materials; mechanical prop-
erties; microstructures.
1. Introduction
Due to the good physical properties, like high melting point, low vapor pressure,
low erosion yield and high thermal conductivity, tungsten (W) is foreseen as one of
the best candidates for plasma facing materials (PFMs) in advanced fusion reac-
tors.
1–3 However, PFMs need to sustain their mechanical performance at evaluated
temperatures. High heat loads and temperature gradients produce thermal stresses
on the PFMs surface, so that the microstructures and mechanical properties are
graduallydegraded during operations. Thus, the study of the thermal stability of W
based alloys is very important in fusion research. The mechanical properties of heat

Figure 1 shows the detailed heating scheme of diﬀerent samples. The samples were

3. Results and Discussions
The ﬂexural stress–strain curves of W-5%V samples after diﬀerent heat treatments
are shown in Fig.2(a). The samples heat treated at 900
◦C and 1100◦C exhibit al-
most identical, nonlinear ﬂexural stress–strainbehavior. The sample heat treated at
1300
◦C shows a minor elastic activity at the start, and its slope after 0.2% ﬂexural
strain becomes almost equal to the one heat treated at 1500◦C. Figure2(b) repre-
sents the ﬂexural strength and stain as a function of heat treatment temperature.
A decreasing trend for both quantities is observed by increasing the temperature.
The degradations in ﬂexural strength and strain due to heat treatment are 14%
and 39%, respectively. It may be due to the fact that V has more than 3 times
heat capacity than W and its enriched phase tries to absorb most of heat. Thus,
heat treatment causes rearrangement of Vparticles in submicron level and aﬀects
the ﬂexural strength and stain. Apparently, the density slightly decreases when
the temperature goes up to 1300
◦C, and then increases with the further increase of
temperature as shown in Fig.2(c). However, this activity in density is not much sig-
niﬁcant and there is small change in the relative density of 0.25% only is observed.
Thus, the alloy can be considered thermally stable, regarding to its stability in
density.

4. Conclusion
Newly developedW-5%Valloyfor plasmafacing materialsby SPS,washeattreated
in a pure argon furnace, for 2 h over the temperature range 900–1500
◦C. The
thermal stability of heat treated materials was evaluated from microstructures and
mechanicalproperties. Density of W-5%V annealed at diﬀerent temperatures shows
an insigniﬁcant change of only 0.25% in its values, indicating high thermal stability.
The W grain size of the W-5%V alloy was stable during heat treatment process. A
slight reduction in ﬂexural strength indicates a minor activity of rearrangementand
reunionof Vmicrostructuresonly.Micro-hardnessvalues ofthe W-5%Vwere highly
stable as compared to pure W. It is concluded that the addition of V improves the
thermal stability of W based materials during annealing process.
