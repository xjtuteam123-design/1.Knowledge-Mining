Microstructure Evolution and Thermal Stability of
 Cu - 15Cr in - situ Composites
TIAN Wei1*, BI Liming2*, DU Jiandi3
(1. College of Science, University of Shanghai for Science and Technology, Shanghai 200093, China; 2. University of Shanghai for Science 
and Technology, Shanghai 200093, China; 3. School of Materials Science and Engineering, Shanghai Jiaotong University, Shanghai 200240, 
China)
Abstract: The Cu-15Cr in-situ fiber-reinforced composites sheets were prepared by cold drawing 
combined with cold rolling process. The evolution process of Cr fibers was studied, and when cold rolling 
reduction ε = 95%, the morphology of Cr fiber at different annealing temperature and the thermal stability of Cu-
15Cr alloy were studied. Microstructure was also studied by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Meanwhile, 
the tensile strength of the alloy was tested by means of a precision universal tester, and the resistance value of 
the alloy was determined by using a digital micro-Euclidean instrument. The experimental results show that, 
with the increase of deformation, Cr dendrites evolve into homogeneous and parallelly arranged Cr fibers, and 
the cross-section of Cr fibers undergoes a "V" shape transition to "一 " shape. In addition, spheroidization of 
the Cr fibers occurs on edges and extends to the center as annealing temperature rises. Moreover, the Cr fibers 
remains stable when the annealing temperature is below 550 ℃. Furthermore, the tensile strength of Cu-15Cr 
alloy decreases gradually as the annealing temperature increases, while the electrical conductivity maximizes 
when annealing at 550 ℃ . Our study also shows that Cu-15Cr alloy has obtained a better comprehensive 
performance with tensile strength of 656 MPa and electrical conductivity of 82%IACS after annealing at 450 ℃.
Key words: Cu-Cr alloy; composites; annealing; spheroidization; tensile strength; electrical 
conductivity

1 Introduction
Cu-BCC and Cu-FCC systems in-situ fiber rein-
forced composites are some kinds of high strength and 
high conductivity materials with excellent comprehen-
sive performance. For example, Cu-Nb alloys have an 
tensile strength of 1 060 MPa and a conductivity of 
65%IACS
[1,2]
, Cu-Ag alloys have that of 800 MPa and 
90%IACS
[3,4]
, Cu-Cr alloys have that of 1 050 MPa 
and 65%IACS
[5,6]
, and Cu-Fe alloys have that of 1 400 
MPa and 60%IACS
[7]
. We have recently developed 
Cu-Cr deformation-processed in-situ composites with 
tensile strength of 1 200 MPa and electrical conduc-
tivity of 70%IACS
[8,9]
. Sakai et al have extended this 
work to Cu-Ag thin-plate alloy with high-strength and 
high-conductivity
[10]
 and we also have extended this 
work to Cu-Fe sheets with high-strength and high-con-
ductivity
[11]
. In practice, these composite material are 
required to have a long service time. Therefore, in or -
der to prolong the service life of the material, it is nec-
essary to further study the micro thermal stability of the 
material and its microcosmic performance
[12]
. 
There are studies on the microstructures and prop-
erties of Cu-Nb, Cu-Ag, and Cu-Cr deformation-pro-
cessed in-situ composites which were cold-drawn at 
different annealing temperatures. These studies show 
that recovery and recrystallization of Cu matrix occurrs 
during the process of annealing, followed by coarsen-
ing, spheroidization, columnar structure, and fracture of 
fibers
[13]
. However, all the studied materials were wires 
fabricated through cold drawing. Thin-plate materials 
have different stress distribution during deformation 
process in fabrication process, resulting in different fi-
ber-reinforced morphologies, thermal stability of fibers, 
and the comprehensive performance of composites. 
Therefore, it is not appropriate to impose the optimal 
parameters obtained for wires to sheet materials in fab-
rication and application. 
Extensive studies

fect of annealing temperature on the thermal stabilities 
stability of wires. However, similar investigations on

2 Experimental
The Cu-15Cr alloy with a purity greater than 
99.95wt% of the cathode copper and a purity of more 
than 99.5wt% of the industrial pure chromium was 
melted in the vacuum induction furnace after the nom-
inal composition is well assembled. The casting ingot 
mould adopts cylindrical cast iron ingot mould, and the 
size is f83 mm×180 mm (83 mm in diameter and 180 
mm in length). The melting is carried out in a magnesia 
crucible, and the pouring temperature is 1 600 to 1 650 
℃. The f83 mm alloy ingot was machined to f80 mm 
and then subjected to subsequent tests. Process flow is 
shown in Fig.1.
The drawing deformation is expressed as loga -
rithm η=ln(A
0/Af). A0 is the cross-sectional area when 
the sample is not deformed, and A f is the cross-sec-
tional area after drawing. Cold rolling reduction ε is 
expressed as ε =(d0-d)/d0. d0 is the original thickness 
of the sample. In this paper, the diameter of the sample 
under final drawing deformation is defined as the orig-
inal thickness of the sample. d is the thickness of the 
sample after each rolling. 
To study the effects of annealing temperatures 
on the phase stability, we annealed these samples (ε = 
95%) under the protection of N
2 at T = 400, 500, 600, 
700, 800, and 900 ℃ for 1 hour, respectively, and then 
cooled them in the air. Taking the high melting point of 
1 857 ℃ of Cr into consideration, the maximum anneal-
ing temperature was elevated to 900 ℃.
The microstructures were observed by field emis -
sion scanning electron microscopy (SEM) after the 
samples without polishing were corroded with HNO
3 at 
concentration of 63%, and then ultrasonic-cleaned with 
alcohol. Tensile tests were carried out at room tempera-
ture for the test specimens using a gauge length of 10 
mm and a cross-head speed of 1mm per minute using 
ZWICK-Z50 test machine. Electrical resistivity were 
measured using a ZY9987 digital micro-ohmmeter, the 
gauge length of the test specimens was 200 mm, and then 
converted to electrical conductivity. The exchange for -
mula of the electrical conductivity is defined as 1.724 1×
L/RS, where L is the effective length of specimen, R is 
measured electrical resistivity and S is the cross section 
of the specimen.

In Fig.3, the Cr fibers after cold drawing deforma-

degree of atomic density of the slip plane of Cr phase 
with BCC structure is less than that of the Cu phase 
with FCC structure. The number of slip direction is 
less than that of Cu as well. Thus, the plasticity of Cr 
phase with BCC structure is much better than that of 
Cu. Therefore, in the process of deformation, the Cu 
phase of matrix is always uniformly deformed in the 
axial direction. At the same time, the Cr phase with 
BCC structure is in order to keep the synchronization 
with the matrix as far as possible, (110) [111] and (110) 
[11

[16]
. The 
above-mentioned characteristics of the slip system of 
the two phases cause the Cr phase with BCC structure 
to be forced to rotate about the stretching axis. After 
the cold drawing deformation reaches a certain degree, 
the orientation of the Cr fibers tends to be uniform 
and forms a <110> silk texture along the direction of 
drawing deformation, showing an irregular twist shape. 
After cold rolling, the test alloy with silk fabric is not 
only affected by the tensile force but also the pressure. 
The previously formed silk texture is transformed into 
a plate texture parallel to the rolling direction. With the 
increase in cold-rolled deformation, the original curved 
and twisted fiber morphology changes to a straight fiber 
morphology.

and fiber straightening. The cross section of dendrites 
Cr is mainly subjected to V-shaped irregularities and 
straightening.
3.2 Thermal stability of Cr fiber
Fig.5 shows the SEM images of Cr fibers parallel 
to the rolling direction after fiber-reinforced Cu-15Cr 
in-situ composites with deformation ε of 95% annealed 
for 1 hour. In the deformation process, grains whose 
crystal orientation are inconsistent with the direction 
of deformation rotated and aligned to the rolling direc -
tion, at last, all Cr fibers aligned parallel to the rolling 
direction. Compared with the unannealed structure in 
Fig.5(a), the Cr fiber morphologies after annealing at 
550 ℃ changed little and remained the smooth cold-
rolled surface except for the horizontal tear of fiber 
as the result of stress release indicated as A zone in 
Fig.5(b). Annealing at 600 ℃ led to the occurrence of 
spheroidization at the edge of thin Cr fibers, as shown 
in Fig.5(c), but the edges were still smooth for the thick 
fibers. Increasing annealing temperature to 700 ℃, as 
shown in Fig.5(d), the fiber spheroidization was inten -
sified and extended from the edges to the center areas. 
After annealing at T = 800 ℃, the grain boundary split-
ed at triple point junctions, indicated by the A, B, and C 
zones in Fig.5(e). The grain boundary splitting created 
holes that is a characteristic failure pattern under large 
compression. Fig.5 (f) shows that most of the fibers 
were edge-spheroidized and fractured, losing almost all 
the original morphologies after annealed at 900 ℃.

3.3 Electrical conductivity and tensile str-
ength
Fig.6 shows the results of electrical conductivity
 
and tensile strength of Cu-15Cr composites as functions 
of annealing temperature. It can be seen that tensile 
strength decreases gradually as the annealing tempera -
tures increases and electrical conductivity maximizes at 
550 ℃.
Specifically, when annealing T < 550 ℃, electri-
cal conductivity increases slowly with the temperature 
and reaches the maximum value 84.4%IACS at 550 
℃. When annealing T > 550 ℃, electrical conductivity 
starts to decreases quickly and drops to 47%IACS at 
900 ℃, about 44% smaller than the maximum. When 
annealing T ≤ 450 ℃, tensile strength changes slightly 
from 700 MPa without annealing to 656 MPa at 450 ℃. 
When T > 500 ℃, the tensile strength drops dramati-
cally to 546 MPa at T = 600 ℃, then decreases to 335 
MPa at T = 900 ℃. The softening temperature can be 
defined as a temperature at which annealing causes ten-
sile strength dropping by 15%. Thus we calculate the 
softening temperature of cold-rolled Cu-15Cr sheet is 
about 450 ℃.
4 Discussion
4.1 Influence of annealing temperature on 
fiber morphology
Sharma et al showed that the thermal instability 
of lamellar microstructures can be attributed to four 
possible mechanisms: edge spheroidization, grain 
boundary splitting, columnar structure, and Rayleigh 
perturbation
[13]
. The four mechanisms of thermal in-
stability do not occur strictly in sequence and the ther -
mal instability is influenced by the size distribution 
of fibers, superposition of diffusion areas, and grain 
coarsening. For the same sample, all four mechanisms 
may occur simultaneously at different areas or any one 
of the mechanisms dominates. Based on our results we 
conclude that the thermal instability of Cr fibers in Cu-
15Cr composites does not involve all the four mecha-
nisms but two mechanisms only: edge spheroidization 
and grain boundary splitting. Fig.7 and Fig.8 are sche-

Kampe et al

 have pointed out that the change 
of fiber morphologies obeys the curvature induction 
mechanism of surface diffusion, and a significant 
influence will be produced on fiber morphology by 
width-thickness ratio of fibers and energy ratio of inter-
face to surface. Deducting from width-thickness ratio 
of the original Cr fiber morphologies, there are two 
possible changes in Cr fibers as follows: For the first 
possibility, when the width-thickness ratio is small, at-
oms would slide from edges to smooth faces because of 
the curvature difference between fiber edges and neigh-
boring faces, then they would pile up at smooth faces 
and finally form columns. If the process was controlled 
by volume diffusion, the big columns would consume 
the small ones and experience Ostwald coarsening. 
If the process was controlled by interface diffusion, 
according to mechanism of Rayleigh perturbation, the 
columns would break apart to spheroids
[18]
. For another 
possibility, big width-thickness ratio and energy ratio 
of interface to surface would lead to edge spheroidiza -
tion, which is the main reason of thermal instability in 
plate-like structure. In this work, edge spheroidization 
was observed for the deformation-processed Cu-15Cr 
composites. At high temperature, there is a local equi-
librium between surface tensions of sub-grain boundary 
and interface, and the different gradients of chemical 
potential due to different curvatures would force the 
atoms to diffuse from the bottom of thermal etching 
groove, breaking the original equilibrium. In order 
to reestablish the equilibrium, sub-grain boundaries 
would be consumed to compensate, which gives rise to 
deepening of the thermal etching grooves. For low an-
gle grain boundaries, the occurrence of edge spheroid-
ization is easier because the driving force of boundary 
splitting is small. 
4.2 Influence of annealing temperature on 
electrical conductivity
The electrical conductivity of Cu-15Cr in-situ 
composites is influenced by microstructures of both Cu 
matrix and Cr fibers. The influence of annealing tem -
perature on electrical conductivity of deformation-pro -
cessed Cu-15Cr in-situ composites can be explained by 
the four mechanisms below. 
When the annealing temperature is relatively low, 
there is no apparent change in Cr fibers that has little 
influence on conductivity. However, Cu matrix is in the 
stage of recovery and recrystallization with density of 
crystal defect declining, so the electronic conductivity 
of composites increases; 
When the annealing temperature rises, solid-solut-
ed Cr atoms segregates and the amount of Cr in Cu ma-
trix decreases, reducing impurity scattering resistance 
and an increase of electronic conductivity; 
At a relatively high annealing temperature, equi-
librium solid solubility of Cr in Cu matrix increas-
es significantly and those segregated Cr can solute 
back into Cu matrix. Meanwhile, fibers undergo the 
spheroidization process, and the conductivity of com-
posites decreases due to impurity scattering resistance; 
High annealing temperature, e g, 800 ℃ in this 
work, leads to fiber spheroidization, grain boundary 
splitting, and fiber fracture. The microstructure changes 
of Cr fibers increases boundary scattering resistance 
significantly.

When annealing T < 400 ℃, the thickness t and 
the spacing λ of fibers as well as microstructure re-
mained unchanged. Therefore, annealing at low T has 
little influence on tensile strength. When annealing T 
= 450 ℃, Cu matrix undergoes temperature recovery 
with slightly decreasing dislocation density. There-
fore, the reduction of σ
dis leads to a decrease in tensile 
strength. When annealing temperature reaches 500 ℃, 
recrystallization of Cu matrix occurs and dislocation 
density declines. Cu grains grow up so that σ
dis and σgrain 
decrease. Elevating annealing temperature to 550-700 
℃, a further declining in dislocation density and coars-
ening in Cu matrix grains lead to continuous declining 
of σdis and σgrain. With increasing annealing temperature, 
Cr fiber coarsening and edge spheroidization extended 
from edges to the center, and spacing between Cr fibers 
kept increasing and gradually changed into equiaxial 
grains, causing t and λ grow continuously and fiber 
strengthening is weakened considerably. Therefore, the 
tensile strength of composites decreases monotonically 
with annealing temperatures. 
5 Conclusions
Based on the test of tensile strength and conduc-
tivity of Cu-15Cr deformed in-situ composite sheet at 
different annealing temperatures, and the observation 
and analysis of Cr fibers under different deformation 
and annealing temperatures, the following main con-
clusions are drawn:
a) The Cr dendrite after cold drawing combined 
with cold-rolled deformation is developed into a homo-
geneous and parallel Cr fiber, and the cross-section of 
Cr fiber undergoes the transformation of “V” shape to 
“一”.
b) We observed the spheroidization occurred at 
the edge of thin Cr fibers and then extended from edges 
to the center, followed by the grain boundary splitting 
and fiber fracture. Our experiments show that the insta-
bility of Cr fibers in cold-rolled Cu-15Cr in-situ com-
posites is attributed to two mechanisms: edge spheroid-
ization and grain boundary splitting.
c) As annealing temperature increases, tensile 
strength of Cu-15Cr in-situ composites decreases 
gradually, whereas electrical conductivity reaches a 
maximal value of 84%IACS at 550 ℃. The optimal 
mechanical and electronic properties can be obtained 
for unannealed alloy with ultimate tensile strength 694 
MPa and electronic conductivity 78%IACS, or 656 
MPa and 82%IACS at softening annealing temperature 
450 ℃.
