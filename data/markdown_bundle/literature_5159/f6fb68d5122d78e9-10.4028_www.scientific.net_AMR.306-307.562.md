Influence of Ru on the Microstructure of Ni-base Single Crystal 
Superalloys

Keywords: Superalloys ；Ruthenium (Ru) ；Microstructure ；TCP phases 
Abstract. Addition of Ru in Ni-base single crystal superalloys had been used to improve the elevated 
temperature strength and other multiple properties. Significant decreases in stacking fault energy of 
the γ phase and the volume fraction of γ′ phase are observed with the addition of Ru. As well as 
serving as an effective solid-solution strengthening element in high refractory content Ni-base single 
crystal superalloys, Ru additions are able to effec tively strengthen both the γ and γ′ phases and 
suppress the formation of TCP phases. Due to the ch anges in the partitioning behavior of elements 
and the slight decrease in the supersaturation of r efractory elements in γ phase associated with Ru 
additions, high temperature creep resistance and the microstructural stability of the alloy are improved 
remarkable. The influence of Ru on the microstructu re of Ni-base single crystal superalloys is 
reviewed. 
Introduction 
The Ni-base single crystal superalloys have made su bstantial contributions to the performance of 
aircraft engines and turbine blades because of high creep strength and low crack-growth rates at high 
temperatures[1-11]. It is necessary to develop Ni-base single crystal superalloys with favorable high 
temperature properties and other multiple propertie s, especially with mechanical properties and 
microstructural stability in order to satisfy the d emands of the sustainable development of 
high-performance aeroengine[12,13]. Modern Ni-base superalloys contain in excess of ten kinds of 
microelements which are extremely potent in improvi ng the solution strengthening effect and the 
elevated temperature strength[14,15]. Based on the development process of single crystal superalloys, 
the refractory elements contents of representative first (CMSX-2), second (CMSX-4) and third 
generation (CMSX-10) alloys has increased from about 14 wt.% to approximately 16.5 wt.% and then 
to greater than 20 wt.%, respectively[16]. If the c oncentrations of refractory elements are too large, 
topologically close-packed (TCP) phases can form du ring the long-term exposures to elevated 
temperatures. The precipitation of most TCP phases causes considerable degradation of the 
mechanical properties and the solution strengthenin g effect because of the depletion of the solid 
solution strengthening elements, such as Mo, Re, Cr, W and so on[17-20]. As the brittle phase, TCP 
phases which are usually act as the initiation also the propagating passageway of cracks, lead to the 
reduction of the stress rupture life and the deterioration of the ductility and toughness of single crystal 
superalloys. Recently, the addition of the platinum group metal Ru in Ni-base single crystal 
superalloys has been reported to increase microstructural stability during high temperature exposures 
and suppress the formation of TCP phases, thus impr oving the oxidation resistance, the creep 
resistance and the corrosion resistance to enable s uperalloys obtain good mechanical properties and 
microstructural stability[20-30]. The Ni-base singl e crystal superalloys are composed mainly of γ 
phase and γ′ phase, furthermore, others contain car bide and boride due to the difference of alloy 
compositions. The application of the platinum group metal Ru in Ni-base single crystal superalloys 
provides superalloys design with important concept in more than a decade, prompting researchers to 
conduct a large number of correlational studies on the improvement of the micro-mechanisms of high 
temperature mechanical properties[31,32]. This arti cle reviews the strengthening effect of Ru on γ 
phase and γ′ phase, the segregation characteristics of refractory elements and the precipitation of TCP 
phases are also analyzed.

γ phase and γ′ phase 
γ′ precipitate of an L12 structure based on Ni 
3Al in a γ matrix (fcc) is the strengthening phase in single 
crystal superalloys, which directly affects mechanical properties by its quantity, size and morphology 
as well as the site preference of alloying elements[33]. γ′ phase contains substantial amounts of Ti, Ta, 
W, Mo, Nb, Hf and high levers of Ni and Al. The str ong partitioning of W and Mo to the matrix γ 
phase results in improving the lever of solid solution strengthening. The alloying extent of γ phase is 
far less than that of γ′ phase which lead to the im balance of the lever of solid solution strengthenin g 
between γ phase and γ′ phase. As a potent strengthener, Ru, which partitions strongly to the matric γ 
phase, indirectly control the balance of the streng thening effect between γ phase and γ′ phase by 
influencing the partitioning behaviors of other refractory elements, resulting in improving the whole 
strength of superalloys and obtaining the stable microstructure. 
Strengthen. Early work on Ru additions to Ni-base single cryst al superalloys found that Ru 
additions appear to provide a certain amount of sol id solution strengthening to both γ phase and γ′ 
phase, along with a significant increase in flow stresses of the single phase γ alloy[34]. R.A. Hobbs et 
al have presented that dislocation propagation between layers of γ/γ′ via the vertical γ channels would 
be impeded by lowering of the γ stacking fault ener gy (γ SFE ) accompanying the addition of Ru, thus 
strengthens the matrix[35]. However, F. Pettinari et al present the addition of Ru has no measurable 
effect on the SFE[36]. 
The Ni-base single crystal superalloys with about 6 5 pct of γ′ phase in a maxture of γ and γ′ phases 
show a maximum of creep rupture life[37,38]. In recent years, some researches about the influence of 
Ru on γ′ volume fraction have been conducted which worked out differently. R.A. Hobbs et al 
indicated that the reduction in γ′ volume fraction upon the addition of 3 and 6wt% Ru was observed in 
AC2001, AC2002, respectively[35]. R.C. Reed et al d educed that the additions of Ru did not 
significantly alter the volume fraction of the γ′ p hase which values were 77.3% for RR2100 and 
77.6% for RR2101 (containing 2wt% Ru) respectively[ 39]. However, A.C. Yeh et al found that 
additions of both Re and Ru to the alloys resulted in a decrease in the volume fraction of γ′ phase. In 
addition, the volume fraction of eutectic decreased significantly with increasing Ru content is pointed 
out by Q. Feng et al[40]. The addition of Ru is central to the composition of γ and γ′ phases, because it 
is directly related to γ-γ′ lattice dislocation and precipitate morphology[41]. This is due to the 
composition of the phase dominated lattice dislocat ion including its sign and magnitude, further 
influence precipitate morphology, the stress-induce d precipitate coarsening (rafting) at high 
temperatures and creep strength of superalloys[41-4 3]. The coarsening of the γ′ precipitates and the 
influence of the coherency stresses between the γ phase and the γ′ phase, which act as driving forces 
for the γ′ coarsening, have been thoroughly investi gated over the last two decades [7,8,44–55]. 
Different alloying elements change the lattice parameters of the γ and γ′ phase and the lattice misfit 
between these two phases[56]. 
Ru preferentially partitions to γ phase[57,58], and has large atomic radius compared to Ni, resulting in 
the increase of negative γ-γ′ lattice misfit, which is beneficial to form rafting microstucture for single 
crystal alloys[59]. Rafting microstucture can incre ase the creep life under the condition of high 
temperature and low stress. The rafting rate is con trolled by the diffusion behaviors of alloying 
elements[60]. Ru promotes the rafting of γ′ phase t hat is related to the increase of negative lattice 
misfit with the addition of Ru at high temperature[61,62]. 
Segregation of elements. Additions of large numbers of refractory elements plays a key role in 
improving the high-temperature strength of superall oys. In order to understand the influence of 
different refractory elements on the strengthening mechanisms of superalloys, it is necessary to have a 
proper understanding about the partitioning behaviors of refractory elements. 
Fig. 1[63] presents that Co, Cr, Mo, W and Re have a preference to partition into the γ phase, and the 
order of partitioning levers is Re > Cr > Mo > Co > W. Al and Ta have a preference to partition into 
the γ′ phase. The partitioning behavior is in agree ment with the results reported by Ohno and 
Yamazaki with EPMA analysis [64] and by Murakami et al. with atom-probe field ion microscopy 
(APFIM)[65].

Additions of the platinum group metal, Ru, was foun d to partition preferentially to the γ matrix in 
Ni-base single crystal superalloys, leading to the imbalance of the lever of solid solution 
strengthening between γ phase and γ′ phase [39,63,6 6]. In the present work, the focus of research is 
whether the partitioning behavior of refractory ele ments will change after the addition of Ru. K.S. 
O’Hara et al put forward that Ru altered the partitioning of elements between the γ and γ′ phases in a 
manner which has been termed ‘reverse partitioning’ , which was in agreement with the results 
reported by L.J. Carroll and co-workers[20]. P. Car on et al[25] also confirmed that Ru additions in 
Ni-base superalloys reversed the partitioning behavior of refractory elements such as W, Re, Cr, Mo, 
causing them partition to the γ′ phase instead of the γ phase. Since then, there has been considerable 
disagreement on whether Ru causes ‘reverse partitio ning’ of alloying elements. A. Volek et al[67] 
pointed out the partitioning behavior of W and Mo w as not affected due to Ru additions. Ru did not 
significantly alter the partitioning behaviors of other elements with EPMA and APT analysis[68]. As 
mentioned earlier[39,63], Ru additions do not greatly affect the partitioning behavior of other solutes, 
such as Cr, Mo, and Co. Another study concluded tha t Ru additions reduced the preferential 
partitioning of Re and other refractory elements to the matrix[69,70]. The partitioning ratio of Re, W 
and Mo decrease with the increasing of Ru content, and significantly partition to the γ′ phase. The 
effect of Ru on partitioning of Al and Ta is not substantial[71,72]. 
Ru is proven to be a key refractory element. Its di stribution in alloys and the influence on the 
partitioning behavior of other refractory elements need to be further investgated, in order to display 
the strengthening effect of solution elements to the largest extent, and provide the theory instruction 
for the designing of Ni-base single crystal superalloys. 
Analog computation . Recently, the influence of Ru on the microstructure of Ni-base single crystal 
superalloys is investigated by analog computation, which is becoming the focus of correlational 
researches. In order to understand the effects of a dding Ru, it is important to know the atomic sites 
occupied by Ru in the superalloy structure[57]. Ana log computation on Ni-Ni 3Al coherent interface 
shows that the strengthening effect of Ru is indire ct, by the atom substitution and lowering the 
binding energy of the system, resulting in the improvement of microstructural stability[19]. Based on 
the atom substitution, most work in this field focu ses on the γ′ phase. Recently, there exists a 
consistent result that Ru substitutes an Al site in the γ′ phase [73,74], and some theoretical works 
illustrate that Ru’s site preference is Ni [57,74-76]. Generally speaking, it is necessary to optimize the 
process of simulation and make a perfect combination of analog computation and concrete research, 
which exist some different results based on the current studies. 
TCP phases 
TCP phases are a kind of intermetallic compound whi ch are composed of transition elements with 
high coordination number of atoms and complicated crystal structure. The basic stacking units are the 
Frank–Kasper coordination polyhedra (Fig. 2) which have exclusively triangular facets. The most

important polyhedron is the 5-fold symmetric icosahedron, Z12, where 12 refers to the coordination 
number Z of the atom at the centre of the polyhedron. Z12 polyhedra can be found in all TCP phases 
where they are combined with higher-coordinated Fra nk–Kasper polyhedra, such as Z14, Z15 and 
Z16[77]. The variety of TCP phases that may potentially form in Ni-base superalloys (σ, P, µ and R) 
are typically characterized by close-packed layers of atoms forming ‘basketweave’ 
sheets[19,78,79].TCP phases are principally compose d of the elements Ni, Cr, Co, Mo, W, Re, and 
unfortunately this list contains the elements which are most effective at solution strengthening. Thus 
the formation of TCP phases depletes the surroundin g matrix of refractory solid-solution 
strengthening elements, and reduces their solid strengthening effect. In addition, the precipitation of 
TCP at high temperatures is frequently associated with the formation of voids which may potentially 
act as initiation sites for fracture[19,79]. As the se brittle intermetallic TCP phases precipitate, th ey 
can initiate or accelerate crack propagation. Morph ology, distribution and quantity of TCP usually 
influence the mechanical properties of superalloys[ 80]. Duing to the low density and weak 
segregation trend of Ru, the phase stability is improved considerably as well as the creep proporties of 
Ni-base single crystal superalloys.

The precipitation of TCP phases . Duing to the low diffusivities of rhenium (Re) in nickel in the 
third generation superalloys at elevated temperatures, solidification and segregation behaviors lead to 
the formation of TCP phases in the dendrite core[82,83]. There has been some researches claimed that 
the TCP precipitates first formed at regions associ ated with the dendrite core and eventually spread 
into interdendritic regions and covered the entire microstructure[29]. 
The factors that influence the precipitation of TCP phases mainly include the diffusion behaviors and 
the contents of elements and aging temperature and time, etc. The diffusion behaviors of elements 
have a close relation with the formation of TCP pha ses. Under the condition of low diffusion 
temperature and a short period of diffusion time, the tendency for Re, Cr, Mo, W to segregate are not 
significant, resulting in γ phase existing as solid solution and no TCP phases precipitates in the 
matrix. Thus, diffusion behaviors of elements incre ase the tendency of precipitating TCP phases 
during exposure to the conditions of high temperature and long time. [19,82-91]. However, this does

Most solid-state phase changes include nucleating p rocess and growing process, and precipitated 
phase is no exception during the high temperature c reep. At present, there are some studies in 
connection with the formation of TCP phases in orde r to understand its influencing factors. Atsushi 
Sato et al found that TCP precipitation rate was substantially reduced when 2 and 2.5 wt.% Ru were 
added to a third generation Ni-base superalloys, TM S-121, and demonstrated that the TCP 
precipitation rate depended on both its nucleation rate and its growth rate [28].

The precipitate area fractions in all alloys increase with time at all exposure temperatures, as shown in 
Fig. 3(a). The addition of Ru decreases the number of precipitates. The TCP precipitate area fraction 
almost reaches its maximum after exposure at 1000°C for1000 h. Even if the equilibrium has not been 
reached, the number of precipitation will be changed insignificantly by further thermal exposure[28]. 
In order to explain the influence of the addition of Ru on Ni-base superalloys, comparative analyses of 
the number of precipitates under various conditions are examined to investigate the nucleation rate. 
Fig.3(b) shows that Ru additions clearly decreases the number of precipitates formed at all exposure 
times and temperatures. The number of precipitates in all alloys are seen to increase with Ru content. 
When the superalloys were exposed to a range of hig h temperatures for 3000 h, the maximum 
precipitate area fraction was found to always be lower for the Ru-containing alloy. The addition of Ru 
decreases the area fraction that is conducive for p recipitate nucleation and further reduces the TCP 
precipitation rate [28]. 
Mechanisms of Ru suppress TCP precipitation . Ru is proven to be an effective element that can 
suppress the formation of TCP phases in superalloys [20,23,26,28-30,93]. The fundamental 
mechanisms by which Ru additions are able to improve the high-temperature phase stability and creep 
resistance of Ni-base superalloys and significantly surppress the precipitation of TCP phases are still 
not well understood [23,26,28,29,32,94,95]. Atsushi Sato et al draw the conclusion that Ru additions 
increased the solubility limits of solid solution s trengthening elements, particularly for Re and W, 
reduced the supersaturation in γ phase and suppressed the precipitation of TCP phases[28]. A.C. Yeh 
et al got some similar conclusions that Ru additions can control the precipitation of both σ phase and 
P phase[26]. Moreover, TEM observation showed that only the P phase precipitated in all specimens 
that were exposed at 1000 ℃[19]. These findings all indicate that Ru increases the solubility limit of 
Re, thus controlling the precipitation of P phase. A.C. Yeh et al presented that Ru additions decreased 
the diffusion rate of refractory elements between t he TCP and matrix interfaces and changed the 
kinetics of TCP precipitation[29]. R.A. Hobbs et al obtained the same conclusion that Ru additions

reduced the solid solubility of Cr, Mo, W, Re in γ phase, thus decreased the precipitation kinetics of 
TCP phases[37]. The presence of Ru in the γ phase and the resulting change in lattice misfit between 
the γ phase and TCP phases may potentially influence the ability of the TCP phases to nucleate. The 
addition of Ru enhances phase stability can be rela ted to the degree of segregation during 
unidirectional solidification. The driving force for precipitation of TCP phases can be minimized by 
controlling the degree of W and Re segregation and preventing the dendrite cores from becoming 
supersaturated with refractory elements[29]. Other reseaches found that the partitioning behaviors of 
Co, Cr, Mo, W, Al, Ti, Ta, Hf and Re were not signi ficantly affected by the 1 at.% platinum group 
metals (PGM) addition. It can be supposed that the PGM atom itself suppresses the formation of TCP 
phases[63]. 
TCP phases are prone to nucleation and growth rapidly in the hot section turbine components during 
exposure to conditions of high temperature and stress, which seriously threaten the aircraft engine and 
gas turbine security. Therefore, proventing the precipitation of TCP phases can improve the toughness 
and plasticity of superalloys, and further increase creep rupture life. Although the experimental results 
clearly demonstrate that Ru additions can be effect ively used to minimize the formation of these 
deleterious precipitates at elevated temperatures, the manner in which Ru additions affect the 
underlying mechanisms associated with TCP precipita tion are not clear. Definitely, more work is 
required to identify the mechanisms and the roles of Ru in superalloys. 
Summary 
The solubilities of refractory elements in γ phase vary with alloying component under different 
temperatures. The change of solubilities results in the precipitation of γ′ phase and the formation of 
carbide and TCP phases. Ru additions can reduce the volume fraction of γ′ phase and the degree of 
supersaturation of refractory elements formed TCP p hases in γ phase, which is important for 
improving the stability of phases at elevated temperatures. In addition, the formation of TCP phases is 
correlated with the distribution of refractory elements between γ and γ′ phases. The composition of γ 
and γ′ phases dominate the sign and magnitude of la ttice dislocation, further influence precipitate 
morphology. It is necessary to make a study of segregation behavior of refractory elements and further 
adjust their adding amount at the time of promising the alloys are of good elevated temperature 
property and phase stability. After the TCP phases nucleate, the growth and coarsening of TCP phases 
result in a gradual depletion of the refractory elements, thereby it changes the segregation behaviors of 
refractory elements between γ and γ′ phases, which is the important factor that affect the performance 
of superalloys. The fundamental mechanisms by which Ru additions are able to surppress the 
precipitation of TCP phases are still unclear. More work is required to explore and identify the 
mechanisms as well as the roles of Ru in Ni-base single crystal superalloys.
