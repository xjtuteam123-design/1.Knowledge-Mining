ABSTRACT 
The CoCrFeNiAl high-entropy alloy, with varying Al compositions, was utilized as the bonding phase in this study 
to fabricate WC-10%CoCrFeNiAl
x 
(where x represents the mole ratio of Al element, ranging from 0 to 1) hard 
alloys using the Spark Plasma Sintering method. The investigation focused on elucidating the influence mech -
anism of microstructure and properties of CoCrFeNiAl
x 
high-entropy alloys on the mechanical properties of WC- 
10%CoCrFeNiAl
x 
hard alloys. Isothermal oxidation testing was subsequently conducted to examine the impact of 
Al content on the high-temperature oxidation resistance of WC-10%CoCrFeNiAl
x 
hard alloys. Finally, a com -
parison was made between the mechanical properties and high temperature oxidation resistance of WC-10% 
CoCrFeNiAl
x 
hard alloys and conventional WC-6Co cemented carbide. The findings suggest that as the Al content 
increases, the CoCrFeNiAl
x 
high-entropy alloy undergoes a transition from a single Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) 
phase to a combination of FCC and Body Centered Cubic (BCC) phases, ultimately transforming into a BCC phase. 
This evolution results in a gradual enhancement in hardness. The mechanical properties of WC-10CoCrFeNi hard 
alloy exhibit exceptional characteristics when the Al content is eliminated, with hardness, fracture toughness, 
and bending strength reaching 1702 MPa, 13.0 MPa ⋅ m
1/2
, and 1378 MPa respectively. As the Al content in -
creases in WC-10%CoCrFeNiAl
x 
hard alloys, there is a corresponding increase in hardness while fracture 
toughness and bending strength gradually decrease. Furthermore, an enhancement in high temperature oxida -
tion resistance is observed. Finally, in comparison to the conventional WC-6Co hard alloy, the mechanical 
properties of WC-10CoCrFeNiAl
x 
hard alloys exhibit similarity while demonstrating a significant enhancement in 
oxidation resistance. 
1. Introduction 
The hard alloys are powder metallurgy-based materials composed of 
metal carbides (such as WC, TiC, and TaC) with high melting points and 
hardness serving as the hard phase, while cobalt, nickel, etc., act as the 
bonding phase. Due to its excellent properties of high hardness, high 
strength and high wear resistance, WC-Co cemented carbide has been 
widely used in various fields such as cutting tools, drilling tools, wear- 
resistant coatings and stamping dies [ 1 , 2 ]. The wettability of metal Co 
to tungsten carbide renders it the most suitable bonding phase in 
cemented carbides based on tungsten carbide. However, limitations 
arise in high-temperature and corrosive environments due to its toxicity 
as well as inadequate resistance to corrosion and oxidation [ 3 , 4 ]. For 
instance, the WC-6Co hard alloy finds extensive application in cold 
working molds owing to its exceptional hardness and wear resistance; 
nevertheless, its limited high-temperature oxidation resistance impedes 
its utilization in hot working molds. Consequently, extensive scholarly 
research has been conducted to explore a more efficacious bonding 
phase capable of replacing or partially substituting Co and surmounting 
these limitations. To date, scholars have developed innovative binder 
phases such as FeAl and Ni
3
Al in an attempt to supplant cobalt. How -
ever, these findings have not yielded satisfactory outcomes, thereby 
presenting a significant challenge in the advancement of high- 
performance WC-based binder phases [ 5 , 6 ]. 
The high entropy alloys (HEAs) are solid solution alloys system 
comprising more than 4 or 4 principal elements in nearly equal molar 
ratios, exhibiting a single-phase microstructure. Owing to its distinctive 
microstructural characteristics, the HEA demonstrates exceptional high 
entropy effect, lattice distortion effect, hysteresis diffusion effect, and 
cocktail effect [ 7 , 8 ]. By employing rational composition design, the HEA

In recent years, Co-Cr-Fe-Ni HEAs have attracted considerable

at both room and elevated temperatures, as well as outstanding corro -

bonding agents in hard alloy materials [ 18 ]. Previous research con -
ducted by our group has demonstrated that WC-HEA hard alloy, utiliz -
ing CoCrFeNiAl as the bonding phase, not only possesses excellent 
mechanical properties but also exhibits superior resistance to high- 
temperature oxidation [ 19 ].

advanced engineering applications.
