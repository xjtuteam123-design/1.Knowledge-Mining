Body-centered-cubic (BCC) refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs)
are being actively pursued due to their potential to outperform
existing superalloys at elevated temperatures. One bottleneck problem,
however, is that these RHEAs lack tensile ductility and, hence, proces-
sability at room temperature. The strategy previously invoked to sustain
ductility in high-strength HEAs isto manage dislocation movementsvia
incorporating dispersed obstacles;this, however, may also have embrit-
tlement ramifications. Here, a new strategy is demonstrated to achieve
ductile BCC HfNbTiV,via decomposing the BCC arrangement (b phase)
into a b(BCC
1)+ b*(BCC2) arrangement via spinodal decomposition,
producing chemical composition modulations and, more importantly,
elastic strain on a length scale of a few tens of nanometers. The
periodically spacedb*, with large lattice distortion, is particularly potent
in heightening the ruggedness of the terrain for the passage of
dislocations. This makes the motion of dislocations sluggish, causing
a traﬃc jam and cross-slip, facilitating dislocation interactions, multi-
plication, and accumulation. Wavy dislocations form walls that entangle
with slip bands, promoting strain h ardening and delocalizing plastic
strain. A simultaneous combination of high yield strength (1.1 GPa) and
tensile strain to failure (28%) is achieved; these values are among the
best reported so far for refractory high-entropy alloys.
Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are currently being developed in large
numbers around the world. These concentrated alloys are
composed of multiple principal elements, unlike traditional alloys
that are based on a single host metal. The exploration of new HEAs
is of course motivated by the possibility of discovering heretofore
unknown alloys that possess unpre cedented properties. In this
regard, a family of HEAs composed of refractory metals, including
titanium, zirconium, hafnium, v anadium, niobium, tantalum,
molybdenum and tungsten, have recently been attracting particular
attention.
1–5 This is because these body -centered cubic (BCC)
refractory HEAs (RHEAs) are expected to withstand higher tempera-
tures than existing (Ni-based) sup eralloys, potentially delivering
unparalleled strength under hot service conditions.

New concepts
We have conceived a facile route to achieve an excellent combination of
strength and ductility, i.e., high yield strength (1.1 GPa) and large ductility
(28%), in a HfNbTiV body-centered-cubic (BCC) refractory high-entropy
alloy (RHEA). The idea is to create contiguous inhomogeneities via
decomposing the BCC ( b phase) structure into b + b* via spinodal
decomposition, producing modulation of the chemical composition
and, more importantly, elastic strain, on a length scale of a few tens of
nanometers. The regularly spaced b* regions, containing unusually large
lattice distortion, create rugged terrain for the passage of dislocations,
thereby making dislocation motion sluggish. The dislocations are
jammed up and are forced to take alternative pathways via massive
cross-slip, forming wavy dislocation walls that inevitably entangle with
other slip bands. The resultant extensive dislocation interactions,
multiplication, and accumulation promote strain hardening and
delocalize plastic strain to increase the tensile ductility. Our success
serves as an illustration of the concept of purposefully designing
heterogeneities to divert dislocations and shows the benefits of this
strategy in sustaining large uniform elongation, even when the flow
stress is at gigapascal levels.

Mechanisms giving rise to the strength–ductility synergy
Before discussing the mechanisms underlying the mechanical
response, we first explain the modulation wavelength (on the
order of 50 nm) we experimentally observed in Fig. 1. Spinodal
decomposition is driven by the positive heat of mixing, which
overpowers the entropy of mixing when the alloy is cooled into
the miscibility gap. The BCC alloy then undergoes phase
separation by developing concentration waves. The wavelength
is selected automatically in the following competition. On the
one hand, fluctuation wave components with extremely small
wavelengths cannot survive, as they would cost too much
gradient energy due to the new ‘‘diﬀuse interfaces’’ incurred.
On the other hand, long wavelength undulations are also
unfavorable because they would require excessive long-range
diﬀusion, which is especially diﬃcult in RHEAs, where the
atomic transport of refractory elements in a heavily concen-
trated alloy is rather limited. The wavelength we have observed
is therefore the critical one at which the amplitude of the
compositional modulation is maximized; it falls in the range
of tens of nanometers, which is in fact typical for spinodal
composition in metallic alloys.

Conclusions

dislocationsvia spinodal decomposition in a BCC HfNbTiV RHEA.
This has generated not just the expected compositional modula-
tions, but, more importantly, also periodic highly lattice-distorted
b* regions tens of nanometers apart. Putting this in a more
general perspective, this allows us to go beyond the normal types
of inhomogeneities, which are ones on the sub-nanometer to
nanometer level that have been reported previously in HEAs.

Experimental section
Material processing
Melts with a nominal composition of Hf 0.1Nb0.4Ti0.4V0.1 and
Hf0.25Nb0.25Ti0.25V0.25 were prepared by arc-melting of a mixture
of pure metals (purity 499.95 at%) in a Ti-gettered high-purity
argon atmosphere. They were re-melted at least ten times to
ensure the initial chemical homogeneity in the melt. Ingots were
obtained through drop-casting of the melt into a water-cooled
copper mould with dimensions of 60 mm /C210 mm /C25m m .
Microstructural and elemental characterization
X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) spectra were conducted with Cu-K a
radiation, scanned in the 2 y range of 20–100 1 at a speed of
0.011 s/C01. Before the XRD scan, the samples were polished with
800 and 2000-grit SiC papers followed by diamond polishing.
The microstructure and morphology at a micro-scale were char-
acterized using an FEI-Quanta 650 field emission scanning
electron microscope equipped with a TSL EBSD system and
EDAX- EDS. The specimens for EBSD analyses were initially
polished with 800, 1200 and 2000-grit SiC papers followed by
diamond polishing, and subsequent ion polishing. The micro-
structure at the nano-scale and atom-scale of the as-cast and
tensile-strained specimens was characterized using high-
resolution TEM with aberration-corrected FEI Titan-G2, Titan-
Themis and Titan-ETEM at 300 kV. HAADF and super four-probe
EDS equipped on the FEI Titan G2 and Themis were used to
analyze the atomic-resolution structure and chemical distribu-
tion. The TEM specimens were first mechanically ground to 30
mm thickness and then ion milled with a GATAN PIPS II 695.
Mechanical property measurements
Room-temperature tensile properties were measured using an
INSTRON-5966 universal tensile testing machine, at an initial
strain rate of 1.0 /C210
/C03 s/C01. Dog bone-shaped tensile samples
with a cross section of 1.0 /C22.5 mm 2 and a gauge length of
20 mm were cut via electrical discharge machining. Five samples
were tested.
