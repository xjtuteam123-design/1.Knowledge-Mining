Abstract 
Severe cold rolling (SCR) and annealing were applie d to a face-centered cubic (FCC) 
Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn high entropy alloy. The crystal structur e, microstructure, and mechanical 
properties of this SCRed Al-containing HEA were studied systematically. The coarse-grained alloy 
is substantially refined after SCR resulting in gre at improvement of the hardness and strength. 
With Al addition into the FeCoCrNiMn alloy, the thermal stability of this alloy is declined, and the 
precipitation of σ and B2 phases in the FCC matrix is greatly promote d. The microstructure 
evolution of the SCRed alloy during annealing can b e divided into three stages: the combined 
precipitation of σ and B2 phases blow 1000 ℃; the dissolution of σ phases and only B2 phase 
precipitates at 1000 ℃; recovering to a single FCC phase at 1100 ℃. The SCRed alloys annealed at 
500~700 ℃ show a high strength but with a low ductility due to formation of a large number of σ 
and B2 phases. With increasing annealing temperatur e, the strength is gradually reduced that is 
attributed to grain growth of the matrix and dissol ution of precipitates. In addition, these 
precipitates effectively suppress the grain growth of the matrix during annealing. A fully 
recrystallized and ultrafine-grained alloy with multiphase structure is obtained as the SCRed alloy 
annealing at 800 ℃, which shows the YS, UTS and the elongation to fai lure of this alloy are 970 
MPa, 1080 MPa and 8%, respectively. The high streng th of this alloy is attributed to the grain

1. Introduction 
Due to the unique composition, structures and prope rties, high entropy alloys 
(HEAs) have received considerable attention [1-10] . HEAs usually contain multiple 
principal elements in equiatomic or near-equiatomic concentration, which tend to 
form a simple solid-solution structure instead of i ntermetallic compounds. High 
configurational entropy is supposed to favor the st abilization of the solid-solution 
structure [2, 3] . The different atomic sizes of multiple elements i n HEAs results in 
severe lattice distortion [4] . Comparing with the conventional alloys, the diffu sion and 
phase transformation kinetics of HEAs usually are s lower owing to the sluggish 
diffusion effect [5, 6] . Among the reported HEA systems, the single FCC-st ructured 
FeCoCrNiMn is the most widely studied alloy [8] . The interesting aspect of this 
FCC-structured HEA is that its outstanding damage t olerance and excellent fracture 
toughness [9] . However, the FeCoCrNiMn alloy usually shows a low strength, which is 
insufficiently strong for engineering applications [8, 10] . Recently, many strengthening 
mechanisms have been used for improving the strength of HEAs. 
Some researches demonstrated that grain refinement is an effective strengthening 
mechanism for the FCC-structured HEAs [11-14] . Many techniques have been applied 
to the HEAs to achieve nanocrystalline (NC) or ultr afine-grained structures (UFG) or 
hierarchical structures to improve the mechanical p roperties. A near-fully dense 
FeCoCrNiMn with hierarchical microstructures, inclu ding melt pools, columnar 
grains, sub-micron cellular structures and dislocat ions was additively manufactured 
by selective laser melting [14] . This additively manufactured HEA exhibits an 
outstanding combination of high strength and excell ent ductility. In addition, it has 
been proved that some conventional severe plastic deformation (SPD) methods have a 
profound effect on strengthening the HEAs [11-13] . The FeCoCrNiMn alloy was

ultrafine-grained alloy with an excellent high stre ngth-ductility combination was

Many studies also have suggested that additions of certain elements into HEAs 
could have strong effects on the microstructure and properties [10, 15-23] . The effect of 
Al element on HEAs has attracted much attentions. T he addition of Al is 
advantageous to decrease the density of HEAs [17] and improve the resistance of the 
oxidation [18] and corrosion [19] . In addition, the addition of Al to HEAs can 
substantially improve the alloy strength [10, 15, 16, 22] . The Al 0.3 FeCoCrNi alloy is 
considered as a highly tunable HEA [15, 16] . The yield strength of this FCC-structured 
HEA can be tuned from 160 to 1800 MPa without changing the composition, owing to 
it contains many phase fields at different temperat ure ranges [15] . Moreover, an 
excellent combination of strength and ductility can be achieved in this Al 0.3 FeCoCrNi 
by coupling refinement strengthening and precipitat ion strengthening [16] . For the 
Al-containing FeCoCrNiMn HEA, the structure tends t o evolve from the single FCC 
to the duplex phases of FCC+BCC and the BCC solid s olution region with increase of 
Al concentration, and corresponding increasing in t ensile properties [10] . A duplex 
Al 0.5 FeCoCrNiMn alloy with FCC and BCC phases was severe ly deformed by HPT, 
the microstructures with the nanostructured FCC gra in containing nano-twinned 
regions and coarser BCC phase was achieved [21] . The single FCC-structured

These ultrafine-grained Al-containing FeCoCrNiMn HE As prepared by powder 
metallurgy exhibit high compressive yield strength [24, 25] and good wear resistance [26] . 
Nevertheless, a limited attention has been paid to the microstructure evolution of this

2.Experimental procedures 
The alloy with a composition Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn were prepared in an arc melting 
furnace by mixing high-purity metals (> 99.9 wt. %) under the high-purity argon 
atmosphere. The ingots were melted four times to en sure compositional homogeneity. 
Then, the ingots were casted into a copper mold wit h dimensions of 80 mm×30 
mm×12 mm. The casted alloys were homogenized at 115 0 ℃ for 6 hours and then 
water cooling. The chemical composition of the homogenized alloy is given in Table 1. 
Finally, the alloy was subjected to severe cold rol ling (SCR) using a laboratory scale 
rolling mill from initial thickness 12 mm to a fina l thickness ~0.48 mm in a reduction 
of 96 %. The further annealing treatments were done in this SCRed alloy at 
temperatures ranging from 500 to 1100 ℃ for 1h. 
The samples from different processing states were cut and mechanically polished 
for microstructural observation. The crystal structure was identified by using a Rigaku 
D/max 2500 X-ray diffraction (XRD) instrument with Cu-K radiation. The 
homogenized and SCRed specimens were chemically etc hed in a solution consisting

3. Results and discussion 
3.1 XRD analysis 
XRD patterns from the Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn HEA in different processing states are 
shown in Fig.1. In homogenized state, the alloy con tains only a single FCC structure, 
which agrees with the results reported in a single FCC Al xFeCoCrNiMn HEA with the 
Al concentration below 8 at. % [10] . In the SCRed state, the alloy still retains a sin gle 
FCC structure. Noticeably, the diffraction peaks of the SCRed alloy are significantly 
broadened as compared to that of the homogenized al loy. Generally, it is believed that 
the alloy comprises of fine grain size, high residu al stresses and high density of 
dislocation resulting in the broadening of diffract ion peaks [27] . There is no obvious 
secondary phase can be detected at annealing temper ature of 500 ℃. With increasing 
annealing temperatures from 600 to 800 ℃, the σ and BCC phases are precipitated. 
However, the σ phases are almost disappeared as annealing tempera ture above 900 ℃, 
and a few BCC phases can be detected. Finally, the microstructure of the alloy reverts 
again to a single FCC structure under annealing at 1100 ℃. In the previous research 
[13] , no obvious phase decomposition takes place in the FeCoCrNiMn HEAs during 
SCR and subsequent annealing at 500~1100 ℃ for 1 h. Apparently, small amount of 
Al addition significantly reduces the stability of ultrafine-structured FeCoCrNiMn 
HEA.

The phase stability of the HEAs has been extensivel y studied. Generally, 
FeCoCrNiMn HEA is considered to be a solid solution alloy with FCC crystal 
structure, and possesses a good stability of micros tructure [36, 37] . However, many 
studies have demonstrated that this single FCC stru ctured HEA occurs the phase 
decomposition below 800 ℃ [11, 34, 38] . The precipitation of Cr-rich phases was 
observed in the coarse-grained FeCoCrNiMn during pr olonged exposure at 700 ℃ [34, 
38] . While a complex chemical decomposition takes plac e in this HEA after prolonged 
exposure at 500 ℃, three different phases were formed, i.e., a Cr-ri ch BCC phase, a 
NiMn-rich tetragonal phase (L1 0 structure), and a FeCo-rich ordered cubic phase (B 2 
structure) [34] . It should be noted that a very long exposure time was used in this 
coarse-grained HEA to formation of secondary phases . The feature of sluggish 
diffusion effect in HEAs is believed to affect the diffusion-controlled precipitation 
process significantly [5] . However, the phase decomposition in the nanocryst alline 
FeCoCrNiMn occurred relatively fast, a NiMn-rich ph ase and a Cr-rich phase were 
observed as annealed at 450 ℃ for 5 min, and then a FeCo-rich phase was formed a s 
annealing time up to 15 h [11] . In present Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn HEA, small amount of Al 
element was added into FeCoCrNiMn alloy. The Al ato m has a larger radius than 
other constituent elements, which increases the lattice distortion, and then destabilizes 
the FCC structure [10] . In addition, Al with the partially filled d-shell transitional

elements, especially, Ni, are easy to form covalent ly bonded intermetallic due to the 
strong formation enthalpy between Al and Ni [39] . It has been proved that Al is a 
strong BCC phases forming element in the Al xFeCoCrNiMn or Al xFeCoCrNi alloy [10, 
22] . Thus, the occurrences of phase decomposition at a n intermediate temperature is 
predictable in present HEA with alloying a minor amount of Al element. 
Our previous work demonstrated that no obvious secondary phases are formed in 
the SCRed FeCoCrNiMn as annealing at 500~1100 ℃ for 1h [13] . However, according 
to the above observations of XRD and TEM, a large n umber of σ and B2 phases are 
precipitated in the present SCRed Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn alloy when annealing at 
500~1100 ℃ for 1h. Thus, the Al element destabilizes the micr ostructure of alloy and 
promotes the secondary phases precipitation. It is reported that the stable temperature 
of Cr-rich σ phase in the FeCoCrNiMn alloy is below 800 ℃ [11, 34] . However, the σ 
phases still can be observed in present Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn alloy at the temperature of 
900 ℃. The σ phase in the FeCoCrNiMn alloy could stand for a hi gher temperature as 
alloying with Al element, which is in agreement with the results of Al xFeCoCrNi alloy 
[22] . It should be noted that the nanocrystalline or ul trafine-grained Al 0.3 FeCoCrNi 
alloy during annealing, the B2 phases were the main precipitates and only a few σ 
phases were detected [15, 40] . Some studies indicated that the Mn additions are 
responsible for destabilizing the phase and promoti ng the formation of the σ phase in 
the FeCoCrNiMn alloy [32, 41] . In the austenite stainless steel, the Mn element has a 
competitive effect with Cr, which promotes the precipitation of the σ phases [42] . It can 
be found that the concentration of Mn in the σ phases is about 20 at. % in present 
alloy, see Table 2. Therefore, both Al and Mn eleme nts have destabilizing influence 
on the single FCC phase FeCoCrNiMn alloy, and speci fically, they will induce the 
precipitation of the σ phase when the concentration of Al and Mn are abov e a certain 
value. Additionally, the present ultrafine-structured Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn contains a high 
density of dislocations and a large numbers of grai n boundaries, they play a key role 
in phase destabilization of the HEA during annealing [11, 35] . The large number of grain 
boundaries serve as fast diffusion pathways and pre ferential nucleation sites for the

dislocations have been observed in the FeCoCrNiMn, suggesting that dislocation

3.3.2 Tensile properties and fracture analysis 
The tensile stress-strain curves of the SCRed Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn at various 
temperatures for 1 h are shown in Fig.10. The SCRed alloy presents high values of 
yield strength (YS:1459 MPa) and ultimate tension s trength (UTS:1534 MPa). 
Unfortunately, the SCRed alloy shows very low ducti lity and disappointedly low 
strain hardening ability. This phenomenon is quite similar to the classical mechanical 
behavior of the ultrafine grained or nanocrystallin e materials produced by SPD 
techniques [29] . On the basis of the above microstructure observat ions, the 
microstructures of the SCRed alloy mainly comprise of ultrafine-structured grain, 
dislocation networks/cells and the substructure wit h a high density of dislocations. 
Thus, the microstructure refinement and dislocation strengthening should be mainly 
responsible for the enhancement in the strength of the SCRed alloy. Compared to the 
SCRed FeCoCrNiMn alloy (YS:1199 MPa; UTS:1335MPa) [13] , the YS and UTS are 
both increased in the Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn alloy. It is reported that the Al-contai ning

FeCoCrNiMn behave like a single FCC solid solution alloy with Al concentration 
below 8 at. %, and the added Al atom acts as the primary strengthener [10] . In addition, 
the stacking fault energy of the alloy is increased with addition Al into the 
FeCoCrNiMn resulting in the planar dislocation glid e as the main deformation 
mechanisms, which increases the dislocation density during severe cold rolling [30] . 
Therefore, the SCRed Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn alloy shows a higher strength than that of the 
SCRed FeCoCrNiMn should be attributed to the Al sol ution strengthening and the 
strong dislocation strengthening. As the sample ann ealed at 500 ℃, the alloy strength 
is substantially increased. The YS and UTS are 1553 and 1735 MPa, respectively, but 
with a poor value in the elongations of failure. Ac cording to XRD and TEM 
observations, no obvious secondary phases are detec ted under 500 ℃, as shown in 
Fig.1 and 4. The annealing-induced abnormal hardeni ng phenomenon is reported in 
the cold rolled FeCoCrNiMn alloy after annealing at 500 ℃ for 1h, which is attributed 
to the formation of long-range ordered structure in the HEA matrix [46] . The alloys 
annealed at 600 and 700 ℃ are very brittle. A large amount of σ and B2 phases 
precipitate in the ultrafine-structured matrix increase the alloy strength, however, they 
seriously cause a decrease in the ductility. With i ncreasing the temperature to 800 ℃, 
this alloy has an elongation of 8%, a YS of 970 MPa and a UTS of 1080 MPa. The 
volume fraction of the σ phases is reduced owing to the dissolution of σ phases at

microstructure with an ultrafine-grained size of ~ 900 nm and consisting of many σ 
(~340 nm) and B2 (~310 nm) phases that still remain a high strength for this alloy. 
With the increasing of annealing temperature, the s trength continues to decline while 
plasticity improves substantially. The alloy anneal ed at 1100 ℃ presents a very high 
elongation but comparable low strength. This is att ributed to the coarsening of the 
recrystallized microstructures and the disappearanc e of the precipitates. Fig.11 shows 
the typical fracture surfaces of the alloys in diff erent processing states. The

4. Conclusions 
(1) An ultrafine-structured Al 0.3 FeCoCrNiMn HEA was prepared by severe cold 
rolling (SCR). The SCRed alloy retains a single FCC structure, and the average grain 
size substantially reduces to ~110 nm. The microhardness, YS and UTS of the SCRed 
alloy are 434 HV , 1459 MPa and 1534MPa, respectivel y. The high-density 
dislocations and ultrafine-grain size that caused b y SCR are responsible for the high 
strength. 
(2) The deformed FCC matrix of this HEA begins to recrystallize at 600 
℃ and is fully 
recrystallized at 800 ℃ for 1h. A Cr-rich σ phase and a NiAl-rich B2 phase precipitate 
during annealing and they are both distributed alon g the grain boundary. The 
microstructure evolution of the SCRed alloy during annealing can be divided into 
three stages: the combined precipitation of σ and B2 phases blow 1000 ℃; the 
dissolution of σ phases and only B2 phase precipitates at 1000 ℃; recovering to a 
single FCC phase at 1100 ℃.

## Page 28

26 
 
(3) With Al addition into the FeCoCrNiMn alloy, the thermal stability of this alloy is 
declined, and the precipitation of σ and B2 phases in the FCC matrix is greatly 
promoted. These precipitates effectively suppress t he grain growth of the matrix 
during recrystallization of the alloy. A fully recr ystallized and ultrafine-grained alloy 
with multiphase structure is obtained as the SCRed alloy annealing at 800 ℃. 
(4) The SCRed alloys annealed at 500~700 ℃ show a high strength but with a low 
ductility due to formation of a large number of σ and B2 phases. With annealing 
temperature up to 800 ℃, the alloy still maintains a relatively high stren gth, the YS, 
UTS and the elongation of failure are 970 MPa, 1080 MPa and 8%, respectively. The 
high strength of this alloy is mainly attributed to the grain refinement strengthening 
and precipitation strengthening. With further incre asing of the temperature, the alloy 
shows significant softening and combine with increa sing of ductility, due to the 
coarsening of the deformed microstructures and the dissolution of the precipitates.
