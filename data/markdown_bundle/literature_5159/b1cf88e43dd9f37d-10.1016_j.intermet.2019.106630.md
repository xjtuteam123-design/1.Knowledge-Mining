ABSTRACT 
The effect of niobium on microstructure and mechanical properties of Ti46Al2.6C x Nb (at. %) alloys with Nb 
content ranging from 0 to 8 at.% have been studied. The alloys have been prepared by vacuum arc melting. The 
content of γ (TiAl) phase increases and content of α
2
(Ti
3
Al) phase decreases with increasing content of Nb. The 
lattice parameters of γ phase increase and parameters of the α
2 
phase remain stable when Nb content is higher 
than 4 at. %. There is no significant segregation of Nb in the studied Ti46Al2.6C x Nb alloys. The size of lamellar 
colonies decreases with increasing content of Nb from 0 to 8 at. %. The increase of the lattice parameters and low 
segregation of Nb without formation of B2 phase are related to a higher solid solubility of Nb in the γ phase. 
Compressive strength and compressive strain increase with increasing content of Nb. Tensile strength of 
Ti46Al2.6C8Nb alloy increases from 330 MPa to 442 MPa and the strain increases from 2.9% to 5.3% with

improvement in mechanical properties of the studied alloys. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl alloys are considered to be promising candidates for replace -
ment of Ni-based alloys in aerospace and automobile industries because 
of their outstanding advantages, such as low density, high strength, 
excellent oxidation resistance. The alloying with Nb leads to an 
improvement of high temperature properties of high-Nb TiAl alloys 
[ 1 – 3 ]. Bean et al. [ 4 ] have shown that high temperature mechanical 
properties can be improved with Nb addition without compromising the 
oxidation resistance by reducing Al content. The content of Al is an 
important factor affecting phase transformations, microstructure for -
mation and content of α
2
(Ti
3
Al) and γ (TiAl) phases in TiAl alloys [ 5 ]. 
However, low room-temperature ductility and high strain at high 
temperatures are obstacles for practical applications of high-Nb TiAl 
alloys, especially for the engine blades requiring low strain and high 
mechanical properties at elevated temperatures. The previous studies 
have been focused mainly to alloy design and microstructure control to 
improve mechanical properties of TiAl alloys [ 6 – 8 ]. The alloying ele -
ments used for improvement of their properties can be divided into three 
groups: increasing ductility (V, Mn, Cr); improving oxidation resistance 
(Ta, W, Mo); microstructure refinement (B, C, Si) [ 9 , 10 ]. Among these 
alloying elements, carbon has an efficient hardening effect on TiAl alloys 
if a fine dispersion of carbide precipitates is obtained [ 11 , 12 ]. The 
composite approach represents an efficient way to improve strength and 
the strain of TiAl alloys by formation of Ti
2
AlC particles. The properties 
of Ti
2
AlC phase, specifically the effect of grain size on the compressive 
strength and hysteretic behavior of has been investigated recently by 
Benitez et al. [ 13 ]. As shown recently by Che et al. [ 14 ], TiAl-based 
matrix composites reinforced with different volume fractions of Ti
2
AlC 
phase prepared by mechanical alloying and hot-pressing have a very 
good wear resistance. The in-situ α
2

low volume fraction of primary Ti
2

ugal casting have shown significantly improved high-temperature creep 
resistance up to 900

and low strain rates promote also plastic deformation of fine grained 
Ti

form the microstructure without the B2 phase.

alloys prepared by casting is beneficial to an improvement of mechan -

Fig. 7 shows the microstructure of Ti46Al2.6C8Nb alloy nearby 
compressive fracture. The microstructure of Ti46Al2.6C8Nb alloy is 
without B2 phase. The crack initiation and growth occur in the γ phase 
regions and lamellar colonies, as seen in Fig. 7(b). In the high-Nb TiAl 
alloys, cracks are usually initiated in the B2 phase and high content of B2 
phase is the main reason for decreasing mechanical properties. How -
ever, there is no B2 phase in the studied Ti46Al2.6CxNb alloys, which is 
beneficial to improve the mechanical properties. The Ti
2
AlC particles 
impede the crack to propagate, cause the crack deflection and crack 
bridging. 
Fig. 8 shows the fracture morphology in the Ti46Al2.6CxNb alloys. 
There are many cleavage surfaces and the morphology of fluvial pattern. 
The morphology of fracture is the mixed fracture mode, which contains 
trans-granular fracture and inter-granular fracture. Deformable bridging 
ligaments and crack-arresting properties of kink boundaries for Ti
2
AlC 
indicate an intrinsic plastic property. Aside from bridging ligament and 
inhibiting cracks, pulling out of reinforcing particles and crack deflec -
tion are observed. The Ti
2
AlC particles with Nb act as the effective short 
fiber reinforcement. The load transfer from the TiAl matrix to high 
Young modulus Ti
2
AlC particles is carried out according to the shear-lag 
model reported by Nardone et al. [29] and Nardone [30].

strength at elevated temperature. When the deformation occurs, the 
influence of grain boundary strengthening becomes weak and the effect 
of solid solution strengthening becomes strong with increasing tested 
temperature. The tensile strain directly relates to the grain boundary 
strengthening. Hence, the Ti46Al2.6C x Nb alloys have higher tensile 
strength and lower tensile strain at high temperature. When the tem -
perature further increases, the deformation occurs also by sliding along 
the grain boundaries under the action of external forces. 
4. Conclusions 
The phase composition, microstructure, mechanical properties and 
fracture behaviors of Ti46Al2.6C x Nb alloys prepared by casting were 
investigated. The achieved results are summarized as follows. 
1. The contents of γ phase increases and α
2 
phase decreases with 
increasing content of Nb. Lattice parameters of the γ phase increases 
with increasing content of Nb and α
2 
phase remains stable over 4 at. 
% Nb. Nb is soluble in the coexisting phases and its solid solubility is 
higher in the γ phase. 
2. Size of lamellar colonies decreases from 36.3 μ m to 17.2 μ m with 
increasing content of Nb from 0 to 8%. The addition of Nb promotes 
formation of heterogeneous nucleation sites and refines the lamellar 
colonies. 
3. The compressive strength and compressive strain increase with 
increasing content of Nb. The tensile strength increases and tensile 
strain is nearly constant with increasing content of Nb at 750 
�
C. The 
tensile strength is 442 MPa and tensile strain is 5.3% at 950 
�
C for the 
Ti46Al2.6C8Nb alloy. 
4. The improvement in mechanical properties are attributed to a 
combined effect of solid solution strengthening by Nb, precipitation 
strengthening by Ti
2
AlC, grain boundary strengthening and 
increasing content of the γ phase.
