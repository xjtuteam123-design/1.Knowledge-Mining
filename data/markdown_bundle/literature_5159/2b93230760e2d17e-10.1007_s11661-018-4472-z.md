Inﬂuence of Annealing on Microstructure and
Mechanical Properties of Refractory CoCrMoNbTi 0.4
High-Entropy Alloy
MINA ZHANG, XIANGLIN ZHOU, WUZHI ZHU, and JINGHAO LI
A novel refractory CoCrMoNbTi 0.4 high-entropy alloy (HEA) was prepared via vacuum arc
melting. After annealing treatment at diﬀerent temperatures, the microstructure evolution,
phase stability, and mechanical properties of the alloy were investigated. The alloy was
composed of two primary body-centered cubic structures (BCC1 and BCC2) and a small
amount of (Co, Cr) 2Nb-type Laves phase under diﬀerent annealing conditions. The micro-
hardness and compressive strength of the heat-treated alloy was signiﬁcantly enhanced by the
solid-solution strengthening of the BCC phase matrix and newborn Laves phase. Especially, the
alloy annealed at 1473 K (1200 /C176 C) achieved the maximum hardness and compressive strength
values of 959 ± 2H V 0.5 and 1790 MPa, respectively, owing to the enhanced volume fraction of
the dispersed Laves phase. In particular, the HEAs exhibited promising high-temperature
mechanical performance, when heated to an elevated temperature of 1473 K (1200 /C176 C), with a
compressive fracture strength higher than 580 MPa without fracture at a strain of more than
20 pct. This study suggests that the present refractory HEAs have immense potential for
engineering applications as a new class of high-temperature structural materials.

I. INTRODUCTION
HIGH-ENTROPY alloys (HEAs) have attracted
extensive research attention owing to their unique phase
constitutes and excellent properties. [1] This new gener-
ation of alloy is composed of at least ﬁve principal
components in equal or close-to-equal atomic percent-
ages, which violates the design concept of conventional
alloy, which are based on one or two major elements as
their principal constituents. Owing to the high mixing
entropy eﬀect, HEAs are favorable for the formation of
simple solid solution phases of body-centered cubic
(BCC), face-centered cubic (FCC), and/or hexagonal
close-packed (HCP) structures. [2–4] In the past several
years, considerable experimental results revealed that
HEAs exhibit unique performance, such as exceptional
thermal stability, high hardness, high strength, out-
standing wear resistance, and corrosion resistance,
attributed to a combination of special structural and
compositional characteristics. [5–8] Thus, HEAs are con-
sidered to be a viable alternative to conventional
high-temperature structural materials in various poten-
tial applications.
Among all reported HEAs, a novel class of HEA
systems—refractory HEAs composed of refractory
metallic elements—has triggered signiﬁcant interest to
date.[9–11] As already reported in the literature, most
refractory HEAs usually form a single solid solution
structure and exhibit intriguing elevated-temperature
properties. These advantages render refractory HEAs
particularly attractive for elevated-temperature applica-
tions. For example, a series of novel refractory HEAs
with a single-phase BCC structure—NbMoTaW,
NbMoTaWV, and TaNbHfZrTi—were ﬁrst proposed
by Senkov et al .[12–14] NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV
exhibit high compression yield strengths up to 405 and
477 MPa at 1873 K (1600 /C176 C), respectively, which are
much superior to those of most of the existing Ni-based
superalloy at high temperature [ > 1273 K (1000 /C176 C)].
The strength of refractory HEAs at high temperature is
attributed not only to the higher melting temperature of
their constitutive elements but also the solid-solu-
tion-like strengthening. [13] However, the two refractory
alloy has high densities—13.75 g/cm 3 for NbMoTaW
and 12.36 g/cm 3 for NbMoTaWV. Subsequently,
another refractory HEA, TaNbHfZrTi alloy with a
reduced density of q = 9.94 g/cm 3, was produced by
replacing heavier elements (W, Mo, and V) with lighter
elements (Hf, Zr, and Ti). This type of alloy under the
as-casting and hot isostatic pressing (HIP) conditions

II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES
A. Preparation of the Alloy
Alloy ingot samples with a nominal composition of
CoCrNbMoTi
0.4 were prepared by vacuum arc melting
a mixture of commercially pure metals (Xing RongYuan
Technology, China) under Ti-gettered high-purity argon
atmosphere on a water-cooled Cu hearth. The weight
purities of the corresponding elements were more than
99.9 pct. The alloy was re-melted at least ﬁve times to
ensure chemical homogeneity, and the ﬁnal HEA ingot
was approximately 120 mm in diameter and 15 mm in
thickness. The actual chemical compositions of the
as-cast alloy are given in Table I. The as-cast samples
were sealed in a vacuum quartz tube and subsequently
annealed in a muﬄed electric resistant furnace at
1073 K, 1273 K, and 1473 K (800 /C176 C, 1000 /C176 C, and
1200 /C176 C) for 20 hours, respectively.
B. Characterization of the Alloy
For microstructural observation, the samples under
the as-cast and annealed conditions were cut into
appropriate shapes, sequentially ground, and mechani-
cally polished. The microstructural characterization of
the specimen was carried out using a scanning electron
microscope (SEM, ZEISS SUPRA 55) operated in the
back-scatter electron (BSE) mode. The observation of
the ﬁner-scale microstructure of the as-cast samples was
performed using a ﬁeld-emission gun transmission
electron microscope (FEI Tecnai G2 F30S-TWIN,
USA). For the preparation of a thin foil for transmis-
sion electron microscopy (TEM), the sample was ﬁrst
ground to a thickness of 60 mm using SiC abrasive
paper, and thereafter punched into circular disks of
diameter 3 mm, followed by ion milling to obtain a
thickness of 40 lm. The chemical compositions of
diﬀerent selected micro-areas were analyzed using
energy-dispersive spectrometry (EDS) equipped with
SEM, and the values were averaged over at least seven
measurements for each area. The crystal structure was

C. Hardness of the As-cast and As-annealed
CoCrMoNbTi0.4 Alloy
Figure 7 shows the microhardness results of the
CoCrMoNbTi0.4 alloy both in the as-cast and annealed
conditions. The hardness value of the as-cast alloy was
829 ± 1H V 0.5. It increased steadily with the rise in
temperature, reaching the maximum value of
959 ± 2H V 0.5 at an annealing temperature at 1473 K
(1200 /C176 C). This indicates that, rather than resulting in
softening, the present alloy possesses the capacity for
age-hardening and structural relaxation in the process of
annealing. A similar phenomenon was also observed in
the reported HEAs, such as CoFeNi
2V0.5Nb0.75,a n d
TiZrNbWMo.[36,37] The high strength of the novel
HEAs can be predominantly ascribed to the solid-solu-
tion strengthening from the multi-principal-element
BCC matrix. Notably, the hardness of the alloy
annealed after 1073 K (800 /C176 C) was slightly higher than
that of the as-cast alloy. The increase resulted from the
formation of Laves phase as shown in Figure 5. More-
over, the hardness of the alloy further increased with an
increase in the annealing temperature to 1273 K and
1473 K (1000 /C176 C and 1200 /C176 C, respectively). It can be
concluded that the increase in annealing temperature
resulted in the increase in the volume fraction (from 1.47
to 2.19 pct) of the hard Laves phases with needle-like
structures scattering in the BCC2 matrix, as mentioned
above in the results of XRD and SEM in Figures 3 and
6. As the Laves phase is a hard phase, the hardness value
naturally increases with the increase in the volume
fraction of the Laves phase in the target alloy. This is
consistent with previously reported References 38 and
39, which also suggested such eﬀective precipitation
strengthening resulting from the secondary phases
formed. Therefore, the increase in microhardness of
the alloy in the as-cast and annealed states can be
attributed to the solid-solution strengthening of the
BCC matrix and precipitation hardening by the Laves
phases with increased volume fraction.

IV. CONCLUSIONS
In this work, the microstructures and mechanical
properties of the refractory CoCrNbMoTi
0.4 HEA were
examined in the as-cast condition and after annealing at
1073 K, 1273 K, and 1473 K (800 /C176 C, 1000 /C176 C, and
1200 /C176 C, respectively) for 20 hours. The following
conclusions can be drawn:
(1) The as-cast alloy exhibited a simple BCC solid
solution phase with typical dendritic and inter-
dendritic structures. The HEA possessed high
thermal stability, sustaining the predominant
BCC1 and BCC2 phase annealed even at
1473 K (1200 /C176 C); further, small amounts of
(Co, Cr)
2Nb-type Laves phase precipitated at a
heat treatment temperature higher than 1073 K
(800 /C176 C), along with the interdendritic regions.
(2) The HEA exhibited enhanced hardness and com-
pressive properties, attributed to the solid-solu-
tion strengthening of the BCC phase, and the
precipitation hardening induced by the distribu-
tion of the fine Laves phase at high temperature.
The hardness of the alloy reached its maximum
value of 959.02 HV
0.5 after annealing at 1473 K
(1200 /C176 C), owing to the increase in the volume
fraction of Laves phase with elevating-tempera-
ture heat treatment. Moreover, the room-temper-
ature compressive strength of the casting samples
increased from 800 to 1790 MPa after annealing
at 1473 K (1200 /C176 C).
(3) The high-temperature compressive strengths
[tested in 1473 K (1200 /C176 C)] of the CoCrMoNb-
Ti0.4 refractory HEA in the as-cast state and
annealing at 1273 K (1000 /C176 C) was as high as 680
and 626 MPa, respectively. Moreover, their com-
pressive strain increased from approximately 7.1
to 50 pct. These results demonstrate that the
present refractory HEAs possess significant
potential for industrial applications to serve as
structure material in a high-temperature environ-
ment, at even more than 1473 K (1200 /C176 C).
