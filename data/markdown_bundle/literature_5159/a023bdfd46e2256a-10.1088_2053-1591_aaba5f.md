Abstract 
The present paper investigates the effect of Co addition on the alloying behaviour, microstructure and the resulting 
properties of cast AlCr 1.5CuFeNi2Cox high-entropy alloys intended to be used at high temperature application. The 
elements Al, Cr, Cu, Fe, Ni and Co (Purity > 99) weighin g approximately 800 g was melted in high temperature 
vacuum induction furnace. The microstructure, phase transformation, density, microhardness and compressive 
strength of the samples were analysed using X -ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscop es (SEM), 
Vickers microhardness tester and Universal Testing machine. The crystalline structure of the alloys exhibits simple 
FCC and BCC phases. The microstructures investigation of the alloys shows the segregation of copper in the 
interdendrite region resulting in Cu -rich FCC phase. The addition of Co further enhances the formation of FCC 
phase resulting in the decrease in micro hardness value of the alloys, which varies from 471 HV to 364 HV with 
increase in the cobalt content from x = 0 to x = 1 (molar ratio). Similar decreasing trend is also observed for the 
compressive strength of the alloys. 
 
1. Introduction 
Metals and alloys are vulnerable to their extreme working environment, which sometimes results in the failure of the 
critical components at high temperature and corrosive environment. Conventionally, minor percentage of various 
elements is combined with cobalt based superalloys in order to enhance its working capability. While, Co based 
superalloys are used extensively in various fields, still they suffer from the influence of high temperature, pressure 
and chemicals, if exposed for longer period of time. High-entropy alloys (HEAs) gave a new dimension to the 
existing supealloys that can sustain mechanical stresses and chemical attack when exposed to such condition. For 
more than a decade, HEAs or multi-component equimolar or near-equimolar alloys has emerged as a potential 
source of materials for providing a number of solutions in various fields [1, 2].Compared to the expected metallic 
alloys based on the combination of specific elements in small quantity, HEAs generally contain five or more 
principal elements with the concentrations range of 5 to 35 at.% [1]. HEAs exhibits exceptional properties such as 
improved hardness [3, 4], high tensile strength [5-6], high compressive strength [7, 8], excellent wear [9, 10] and 
corrosion resistance [11, 12], explaining their potential applications in different areas. 
The selection of material for the design of HEAs is crucial factor if considering an economical alloy [ 13
] for 
a particular application. Element in HEA plays a very vital role in deciding the microstructure and the behaviour of 
the material. A large number of alloys have been reported extensively in the recent years focussing on the effect of

The foremost factor controlling the properties of the HEAs is the fabricating technique and the operating conditions 
during development of the alloys. Further, the proper homogenisation of the material also provides better structure, 
resulting in the enhanced physical and mechanical properties. The formation of the simple stable structures [ 15, 28] 
provides the better performance as compared to the intermetallic or amorphous phase. HEAs not only consist of the 
simple solid solutions but also comprises of the secondary phases and/or intermetallic phases such as β, Laves and σ 
[6, 25, 29, 30]. 
Although various equiatomic HEAs based on the similar system have been investigated [31- 34], there are 
only few studies reporting the near-equiatomic multi-principal alloy system. In addition, there are no detailed 
investigation on the effect of Co on the phase formation and its impact on the properties of the non-equiatomic 
HEAs such as AlCr1.5CuFeNi2. Cobalt is selected as the alloying elements as it falls in the category of transition 
elements which resembles in terms of the chemical nature of the majority of elements in the base alloy and thus 
likely to result in the formation of solid solution, desirable for the high temperature applications. 
In this work, AlCr1.5CuFeNi2Cox
 (x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75,1 in molar ratio) HEAs were cast using high 
temperature vacuum induction furnace and the effect of Co addition on the alloying behaviour, its microstructure 
and the resulting properties were explored. The microstructure, phase transformation and compressive strength of 
the samples were analysed using X -ray diffraction (XRD), Scanning Electron Microscopes (SEM), Vickers 
microhardness tester and U niversal Testing Machine (UTM). The evolved features of the alloy are explained and 
their mechanisms are discussed. 
 
2. Materials and methods 
 
The AlCr1.5CuFeNi2Cox
 (x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75,1 in molar ratio) HEAs samples were cast using high temperature 
vacuum induction furnace under inert gas environment. The elements Al, Cr, Cu, Fe, Ni and Co (Purity> 99.9%) 
were used in granular form for the development of the alloys. The AlCr1.5CuFeNi2Coxalloys are abbreviated as Co 0, 
Co0.25, Co 0.5, Co 0.75 and Co 1 where x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0, respectively for convenience. The elements 
weighing approximately 800 g was melted in the ceramic crucible surrounded by water -cooled chamber under argon 
atmosphere. Each allo y was melted five times with electromagnetic stirring in order to improve the chemical 
uniformity. The alloy exhibited an insignificant decrease in the weight due to the slight evaporation of low melting 
elements. The developed samples of dimension 140 mm× 90 mm×10 mm were then homogenized . The ingots 
obtained from the casting were heat -treated in the furnace at a temperature of 1150 ⁰C for 48 hrs and then furnace 
cooled. The chemical composition of the base alloys as determined experimentally by Energy Dispersive 
Spectroscopy (EDS) is listed in table 1. The composition of each alloy as measured by EDS analysis is close to its 
calculated value.

of mixing slightly increases on addition of the cobalt and then further starts decreasing at x = 1. The value of ∆H mix 
varies from -5.54 kJmol -1 at x = 0 to 5.59 kJmol -1at x=0.5 and then starts increasing to -5.58 kJmol -1 at x=1. The 
negative value of ∆H mix increases the bonding strength between the elements due their proper chemical reaction. 
Also decrease in the absolute value of ∆H mix at x = 1 also predicts the formation of highly stable phase. The similar

 The increase in the value of ΔSmix with addition of the cobalt content confirm to the highly randomised 
distribution of the elements thus assures the formation of the stable simple phase.

The microstructural examination of the cast HEAs are shown in figure 4. The SEM image of the base alloy 
(Figure 4(a)) depicts the presence of interdendritic and the dendritic region indicated by bright and the dark –field. 
The dark region (DR) represents the presence of BCC p hase as compared to the bright region (ID) which is due to 
the formation of FCC phase. Figure 1(b) shows the presence of Cu in the alloys as it is segregated in many regions.

4. Conclusions 
The following conclusions are drawn from the present study: 
1. The AlCr 1.5CuNi2FeCox alloys were successfully cast using high temperature vacuum induction furnace under 
controlled environment. It is found that he base alloy consists of the duplex phase i.e. both BCC and FCC. The 
SEM images of the alloys depict the presence of FCC phase on addition of Co. The AlCr 1.5CuNi2FeCo shows the 
prominent FCC phase as compared to the other alloys. There is a slight increase in the density of the alloys with 
the increase in the Co content in the alloy. 
2. The values of thermodynamic parameters are well within the range of the proposed values for the formation of 
solid solution. All the calculated value and the experimental analysis provide the same information and confirm 
the development of simple structures. 
3. The microhardness of AlCr 1.5CuFeNi2Cox
 alloys decreases on addition of Co content from x=0 to x= 1. Similar 
trend is observed for the compressive strength of the alloys except for the base alloy. The compressive strength 
of the alloy shows gradual decrease for the alloys from x = 0.25 to x = 1. The conversion of BCC phase to FCC 
phase on addition of Co results in the decrease in the hardness hence the compressive strength.
