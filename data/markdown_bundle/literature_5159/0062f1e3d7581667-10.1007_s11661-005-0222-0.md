Microstructure and Properties of Ni 3Si Alloyed with Cr
Fabricated by Self-Propagating High-Temperature
Synthesis Casting Route
QINLING BI, PEIQING LA, WEIMIN LIU, QUNJI XUE, and YUTIAN DING
Ni3Si alloys with 20, 30, and 40 wt pct Cr were fabricated by self-propagating high-temperature
synthesis casting at 543 K. Thermite reaction (Cr 2O3 /H110015CrO3 /H1100112Al /H110057Cr /H110016Al2O3) was used
in Cr alloying. The method is simple and economical when used to prepare Ni 3Si-based alloys. The
process is described in detail. The alloys were analyzed with X-ray diffraction (XRD) and scanning
electron microscopy (SEM) with X-ray energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). The results showed
the alloys mainly consisted of Ni3Si and Ni5Si2 with dissolved Cr and Cr phases. Phases and microstruc-
tures of the alloys varied with Cr content. Microhardness, bending and compressive strength, and
wear rate of the alloys were measured. Microhardness of the alloys was higher than that of Ni 3Si
without Cr and increased with Cr content. Bending and compressive strength of the alloys were better
than those of the Ni
3Si without Cr, and those of the alloy with 30 wt pct Cr were the highest. The
wear rate of the alloys was lower than that of the Ni 3Si without Cr and decreased with Cr content.
I. INTRODUCTION
NI3SI is an Ll 2 structured (ordered face-centered-cubic)
intermetallic compound, possesses anomalous yield strength
as a function of temperature, and shows excellent strength at
temperatures up to 973 K.
[1,2] However, Ni3Si exhibits brittle
fracture and low ductility at room and intermediate tempera-
tures; thus, its use is restricted. [3] Alloying can substantially
improve the ductility and strength at ambient and elevated tem-
peratures.
[4–11] It has been reported that high-temperature duc-
tility and corrosion resistance were improved by a small amount
of Cr element.
[12–15] However, it is complicated and difficult to
fabricate the alloy with Cr by the available methods; for example,
high temperature (1173 to 1373 K) and pressure (about 100 MPa)
are needed in hot pressing.
[13] Therefore, a simple and eco-
nomical new method is needed to promote the applications.
In this article, Ni3Si alloys with 20, 30, and 40 wt pct Cr
were fabricated at 543 K by self-propagating hightemperature
synthesis (SHS) casting. [16,17,18] In comparison to conven-
tional processing (such as hot pressing and hot isostatic press-
ing), this method is simple and economical. The process was
described in detail. Phases, microstructures, and properties
of the alloys were investigated.

When the temperature in the reactor reached 543 K, the
reaction of the igniter was started and the released heat
ignited Reaction [2], and then the high combustion temper-
ature of Reaction [2] ignited Reaction [1]. The theoretical
adiabatic temperature (Tad) of Reactions [1] and [2] at 543 K
was calculated using Eqs. [3] and [4].

In the equations, /H9004Cp is the difference between the heat
capacity of the products and the reactants; Cps and Cpl are
solid and liquid state heat capacities of the products, respec-
tively; and /H9004Hm is the melting enthalpy of the products.[19,20,21]
In these calculations, it is assumed that all the heat generated
by the reaction goes only to raise the temperature of the prod-
ucts and there is no loss of heat to the surroundings, e.g., sup-
pose that the reaction is in a “closed system”. It is also summed
that the difference between the heat capacity of the products
and the reactants caused by the temperature rise is zero (Neu-
mann–Kopp rule).
[22,23] In addition, it is assumed that the reac-
tion goes to completion. Thus, the temperature of Tad is only
a measurement of the exothermic of the reaction and defines
the upper limit for the combustion system.[22,23]
The calculated Tad of Reaction [2] was 3970 K.[19–27,32–34]
It is much higher than the melting temperature of Cr (T m of
Cr is 2173 K, the effect of pressure on Tm is small and not
taken into account) and lower than the boiling temperature
(4865 K) of Cr under 7 MPa (see Table II). So the produced
Cr and Al
2O3 were in liquid state and did not vaporize. Liq-
uid Al2O3 was immiscible with liquid Cr and separated from
it and floated on the top due to its low density.[28] Liquid Cr
was then contacted with the Ni-Si reactants.
The Ni-Si reactants were ignited by the high temperature
of the liquid Cr. The Tad of this reaction was 2250 K, which
was higher than the melting temperature of Ni 3Si. Thus,
the produced Ni3Si was also in liquid state. The two liquids
may be miscible with each other and so spontaneously mixed.
Since the temperature of the liquid Cr was much higher than
that of the liquid Ni 3Si and the fabrication condition of the
alloys was the same in our case, the temperature of the liquid
mixtures increased with Cr content. Figure 10 shows the
variations of exothermic energy and the temperature of
the liquid mixtures with Cr content in the alloys. There-
fore, the superheating extent of the liquid with 20 wt pct Cr
was the lowest, that of the liquid with 40 wt pct Cr was the
highest, and that of the liquid with 30 wt pct Cr was inter-
mediate.
The cooling rate of the liquid mixture in the subsequent
cooling related to the superheating extent. The higher super-
heating extent resulted in the lower cooling rate.

V. CONCLUSIONS
1. Ni 3Si alloys with 20, 30, and 40 wt pct Cr were fabri-
cated using self-propagating high-temperature synthesis
casting at 543 K.
2. Phases and microstructures of the alloys varied with Cr
content. Most Cr elements in the alloys dissolved in the
Ni3Si and Ni 5Si2 phases.
3. The microhardness of the alloys increased with Cr content,
and the alloy with 30 wt pct Cr had the highest strength.
4. The wear rate of the alloys was lower than that of the
Ni3Si without Cr and that of the alloy with 40 wt pct Cr
was the lowest.
