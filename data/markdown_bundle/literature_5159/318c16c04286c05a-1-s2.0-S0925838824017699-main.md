ABSTRACT 
Developing magnesium (Mg) alloys with high strength and ductility at low-cost and high-efficiency is desirable. 
Achieving a uniform fine grains structure is an effective strategy to enhance the strength and ductility of Mg 
alloys. In this study, we propose a novel differential-thermal asymmetric extrusion (DAE) method to achieve 
uniform fine grains during high-speed extrusion. Asymmetric extrusion can introduce additional shear defor -
mation and promote recrystallization. The temperature difference between the billet and the mold can accelerate 
the cooling rate, thereby inhibiting grain growth. Here, by using DAE, we obtained a uniform fine grains 
structure with an average grain size of 1.4 μ m, and the alloy exhibited a high strength-ductility synergy, with 
yield strength (YS) of ~290 MPa and elongation (EL) of ~18.2 %. The excellent mechanical properties 
demonstrate the potential of DAE as a low-cost and high-efficiency method for magnesium alloy production. 
1. Introduction 
Against the backdrop of energy conservation and emission reduction, 
lightweight structural materials have emerged as a pivotal approach [1, 
2] . Magnesium alloys are widely employed in lightweight materials 
owing to low density and impressive specific strength [3] . Nevertheless, 
the improvement of strength and ductility simultaneously remains a 
significant challenge. 
Mg-Al-Sn based alloys have garnered considerable attention owing to 
the synergistic effects of Al and Sn. This combination can effectively 
diminish the discrepancy in critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) be -
tween non-basal and basal slip [4] . Additionally, Ca atoms have been 
observed to decrease the stacking fault energy, thereby enhancing 
non-basal < a > slip in the plastic deformation process of Mg alloys. 
These inherent characteristics ensure the favorable extrudability of 
Mg-Al-Sn-Ca alloys [5] . Furthermore, Ca can form CaMgSn hard 
strengthening phase in Mg-Al-Sn alloy. Zha et al. [6] conducted 
controlled rolling of Mg-4.5Al-1.5Sn-0.5Ca alloy, which exhibits an 
excellent balance of strength (YS: 275 MPa) and ductility (EL: ~20 %). 
This mainly attributed to the formation of nanosized CaMgSn phases 
(~16 nm). Hence, Mg-Al-Sn-Ca alloys offer promising prospects for 
developing high strength and ductility. 
Asymmetric extrusion can induce shear deformation has been 
demonstrated its effectiveness in refining grain size and deviating the 
grain c-axis [7 – 9] . Xu et al. [10] employed asymmetric curve extrusion 
(ACE) to produce AZ31 sheets, leading to significant grain refinement 
and the formation of weak tilted texture. Additionally, adjusting the 
billet deformation can increase accumulated strain, thereby promoting 
the dynamic recrystallization (DRX) [11] . Yang et al. [12] utilized in -
clined extrusion to prepare AZ31 sheets, enhancing accumulated strain 
by introducing inclined interface between billets. The degree of DRX is 
obviously improved, resulting in an impressive ultimate tensile strength 
(UTS) of ~410.7 MPa. In addition, the limited formability of Mg alloys 
presents a significant challenge in achieving high-speed extrusion below

In this study, a novel differential-thermal asymmetric extrusion 
(DAE) was developed for fabricating high strength-ductility synergy Mg- 
3.8Al-1.1Sn-0.4Ca alloys. The microstructure of extruded sheets under 
different differential-thermal conditions was studied, and the DRX 
mechanism and microstructure evolution during DAE were investigated. 
It is worth noting that the application of DAE can eliminate the need for 
repeated deformation, providing new insights for low-cost and high- 
efficiency fabrication of magnesium alloys with excellent strength- 
ductility synergies. 
2. Materials and methods 
The as-cast Mg-3.8Al-1.1Sn-0.4Ca alloys were prepared by melting 
pure Mg (99.85 wt%), Al (99.90 wt%), Sn (99.90 wt%) and Mg-20Ca wt 
% master alloy. Then the as-cast alloys were processed into trapezoid for 
DAE and square-shaped for SE [16] . The as-cast alloys underwent 
multi-stage homogenization (375 ℃ /3 h + 430 ℃ /2 h + 480 ℃ /3 h), 
followed by water cooling. In this work used an asymmetric mold, with 
an asymmetry coefficient of 1.5 (the ratio of length on both sides of the 
extrusion channel is 1.5) [17] . Prior to extrusion, the alloys were pre -
heated at 400 ℃ for 1 h, while the mold was preheated at 400 ℃ , 300 ℃ , 
and 200 ℃ , respectively. The sheets extruded at 400 ℃ was labeled 
IAE400, and the sheets extruded at 300 ℃ , and 200 ℃ were labeled 
DAE300, and DAE200. The extrusion ratio was 21:1, and ram speed was 
16 mm/s. A schematic diagram depicting the process of sheet formation 
is presented in Fig. 1 . 
Microstructure observation was carried out employing scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM; ZEISS, Sigma 500), electron backscattered 
diffraction (EBSD; Oxford Symmetry), and transmission electron mi -
croscopy (TEM; Talos F200X). The corresponding EDS mapping results 
were acquired using double FEI Super-X detectors. The SEM and EBSD 
samples were initially ground with SiC papers of different grit sizes 
(#600 – #7000) and subsequently mechanically polished. The polished 
samples for EBSD analysis were then electropolished in cooled AC

4. Discussion 
4.1. Microstructural evolution 
With mold temperature decreasing, significant microstructure evo -
lution occurred in the DAE sheets. Notably, the DAE200 sheet were 
characterized by uniform fine grains and exhibit a strong texture. To 
investigate the characteristics of microstructure evolution, the DRX 
mechanisms of IAE400 sheet and DAE sheets are studied. Dynamic 
recrystallization divided into continuous dynamic recrystallization 
(CDRX) and discontinuous dynamic recrystallization (DDRX). DDRX 
involves distinct nucleation and growth stages, resulting in the forma -
tion of randomly oriented DRXed grains. In contrast, CDRX typically 
occurs during severe plastic deformation (SPD). Dislocation activation, 
movement and rearrangement lead to the gradual formation of low- 
angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) or subgrains. The resulting DRXed 
grains exhibit a strong basal orientation [54] . Fig. 8 presents the 
different types of grains in the IAE400 sheet and DAE sheets. All sheets 
are entirely composed of DRXed grains and sub-grains, and the sub-grain 
area fraction increased as the mold temperature decreased, indicating 
that a dominant transformation mode occurs from DDRX to CDRX. 
Compared to DDRX, CDRX exhibits a significantly slower grain bound -
ary migration rate by almost three orders of magnitude, leading to a 
finer grain size [55] . Consequently, decreasing the mold temperature 
leads to the formation of fine DRXed grains and induces a transition 
toward a strong texture. 
According to the reported literature, introducing further shear 
deformation through asymmetric extrusion can effectively refine grains 
and deviate the polar axis [17] . In this study, trapezoidal billets are 
utilized to increase the cumulative strain and nucleation rate. To 
investigate the role of trapezoidal billets, the microstructural evolution 
during DAE extrusion is studied for both trapezoidal and square billets. 
Apart from the change in billet shape, all other processing parameters 
are kept consistent with those of the DAE200 sheets. The sheets pro -
duced by the square billet are denoted as SE200.

In this study, the billet undergoes great strain during asymmetric 
extrusion, resulting in an increase in the strain energy and an increase in 
the DRX nucleation rate [57] . However, high-speed extrusion can 
introduce significant friction, leading to the actual mold temperature 
exceeding the preheat temperature. A higher actual extrusion temper -
ature promotes faster growth of DRXed grains [58] . Therefore, a lower 
mold temperature leads to a higher cooling rate that hinders the growth 
of DRXed grains. Due to the abovementioned factors, the DAE200 sheets 
can have uniform fine grain structures. 
4.2. Mechanical property analysis 
In this work, the DAE200 sheets exhibit a high YS of ~290 ±
5.3 MPa. Trapezoidal billets and asymmetric molds introduce further 
shear deformation, increasing the cumulative strain and promoting 
DRXed grain nucleation during extrusion. A high cooling rate transforms 
the recrystallization mechanism and inhibits the growth of DRXed 
grains, while the high-temperature billet retains its plastic deformation 
and precipitation ability. As a result, the DAE process yields fine DRXed 
grains and increasingly dispersed dynamic precipitates. The difference 
in mechanical properties can be attributed to grain boundary strength -
ening, precipitation strengthening and dislocation strengthening.

In this study, the DAE200 sheets achieved high strength while 
retaining good ductility. The favorable ductility is mainly attributed to 
the uniform fine DRXed grain structure. The fine grain structure facili -
tates the coordinated deformation of grain boundaries and enhances the 
cooperative ability during deformation [60]. Furthermore, the tilted 
texture indicates that numerous grains contribute to oriented deforma -
tion. Therefore, DAE is an effective method at improving the strength 
and ductility of Mg-3.8Al-1.1Sn-0.4Ca alloys. 
5. Conclusion 
In this paper, the microstructure evolution and mechanical proper -
ties of DAE process were systematically studied. The results are sum -
marized as follows: 
1. The coupling of trapezoidal billet and asymmetric extrusion can in -
crease the cumulative strain and introduce shear strain, thereby 
promoting recrystallization. Low mold temperature accelerated the 
cooling speed can inhibit grain growth. Therefore, the grain size of 
the DAE200 sheet is significantly refined. 
2. The low mold temperature transforms the dominant mode of dy -
namic recrystallization from discontinuous dynamic recrystallization 
(DDRX) to continuous dynamic recrystallization (CDRX). The tem -
perature difference results in the formation of finer CaMgSn particles 
in the DAE200 sheet compared to the ITE400 sheet. 
3. The DAE200 sheets exhibit an impressive balance of strength and 
ductility, with YS of ~290 MPa and EL of ~18 %. The high YS is 
primarily attributed to grain boundary strengthening, precipitation 
strengthening, and dislocation strengthening. The good ductility is 
mainly related to the uniform microstructure.
