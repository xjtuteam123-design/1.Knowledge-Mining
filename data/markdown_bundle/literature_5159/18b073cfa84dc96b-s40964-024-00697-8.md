Abstract
Additive manufacturing is the manufacture of products with a shape close to a given shape, in which any part with a complex 
geometry is made by layer-by-layer melting of powder material using the energy of a laser or electron beam. CoCrFeNiMnWx 
high-entropy alloys are a promising material as a raw material used in additive manufacturing. In this work, three different 
compositions of CoCrFeNiMnWx (x = 0, 0.125, 0.25) high-entropy alloys powder were obtained by mechanical alloying and 
plasma spheroidization processes. Compact samples were fabricated by L-PBF method. Tests of mechanical characteristics 
in the temperature ranged from − 196 to 600 °C showed practical invariability of fracture energy both at cryogenic and high 
temperatures. When W was added to the alloy in the amount of 0.125 and 0.25 formula units, the tensile strength increased 
on average by 9 and 19%, and the yield strength by 10 and 18%, respectively. When tested at liquid nitrogen temperature, it 
is found that the yield and strength limits increase by 30–35% while maintaining ductility.
Keywords High-entropy alloy · Additive manufacturing · Mechanical alloying · Plasma spheroidization · Powder
1 Introduction
In recent years, the approach to the creation of multicom-
ponent alloys having several elements in equal equiatomic 
proportions as a matrix seems to be very interesting. Particu-
lar attention is paid to alloys in which disordered solid solu-
tions are formed, called high-entropy alloys (HEA). HEA 
are alloys consisting of five or more metallic elements, the 
content of each is in the range of 5–35 at.% in equiatomic or 
almost equiatomic ratio [1 –3]. HEA have unique mechani-
cal, chemical and physical properties compared to conven-
tional alloys, such as a combination of high yield strength 
and ductility [4–6], fatigue strength [7, 8], hardness [9], high 
wear resistance [10–12], corrosion [13], and oxidation [14, 
15]. As a result, they have high potential for use in various 
industries, from aircraft to electronics.
The equiatomic alloy CoCrFeMnNi was one of the first 
synthesized Cantor HEA c FCC solid solution [1 ] and has 
attracted particular attention due to its excellent ductility 
and fracture toughness at room and cryogenic temperatures 
[16–22].
Recently, the possibility of using CoCrFeMnNi HEA 
in additive technologies (AT) has attracted considerable 
interest. Until 2018, only a few papers on the use of HEA 
in SLM technology had been published [23, 24], but the 
number of papers has increased dramatically after 2018. 
Several additive manufacturing (AM) methods have been 
successfully used to fabricate structures with high-entropy 
alloys [ 25]. Among these methods, powder-bed fusion 
(PBF) processes, particularly the selected laser melting 
(SLM) technique, have been widely adopted [ 26]. SLM 
is a powder-bed additive manufacturing technology that 
is also advantageous for producing complex-shaped parts 
[27]. During the selective laser melting (SLM) process, 
rapid melting and solidification occur due to the short-
time interaction between laser energy and metal powder. 
This facilitates the formation of a fine microstructure and 
improves the mechanical properties of the parts [28]. 
Additionally, ultra-fast cooling helps prevent the forma-
tion of intermetallic compounds and dissolution of ele-
ments. SLM has also been used for HEA manufacturing, 
and several studies have reported on the manufacturing

process, microstructure, and tensile properties of these

The paper shows that the relative density of the printed 
samples increases with increasing laser energy density 
and reaches a maximum of 98.2% at an energy density of 
74 J/mm3. Massive clusters of dislocations, nanodoubles, 
tetragonal σ-phase, lattice distortions, and Mn segrega-
tion along the molten bath boundaries are reported as the 
main microstructural features. The tensile strength of the 
SLM-printed specimen is 600 MPa at a relative strain of 
more than 40%, which is superior to the properties of the 
arc-melted specimens due to the fine microstructure and 
the presence of σ-phase. Approximately the same results 
in terms of the influence of laser energy density on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties were obtained 
in other works [30– 34].
Although HEA CoCrFeMnNi, like most HEA with 
FCC structure, has good ductility over a wide tempera-
ture range, its strength at room temperature is insufficient 
for technical applications [23]. Three main strategies are 
most commonly used to improve the mechanical properties 
of HEA [35]. The first strategy is plastic deformation, in 
which the mechanical properties increase with decreasing 
temperature due to the effects of dissolution, twinning, 
and strain hardening [36]. The second strategy involves 
modifying the chemical composition of HEA by adding 
alloying elements, such as Al, Ti, W, Si, Mo, Cu, V, Nb, 
Ta, Y, and B [ 37–48]. In this case, the mechanical prop-
erties can be improved by phase transitions that lead to 
additional barriers for dislocation slip. Shun et al. in their 
work [44] showed that the addition of Ti to CoCrFeNi 
alloy in an amount of 0.5 mol caused the separation of 
intermetallic phases and Laves phases in HEA, which led 
to an increase in hardness from 135 to 515 HV and an 
increase in compressive strength by 75% with a decrease 
in ductility. The alloying of HEA of CoCrFeNi system 
with Nb or Ta induces the formation of eutectic structures 
consisting of FCC solid solution and Laves phase, which 
exhibit a balance of ductility and strength [43, 49–52]. The 
addition of Y to CoCrFeNi HEA, which has a much larger 
atomic size and negative enthalpy of mixing with other 
components of the system, also significantly increased 
the yield strength of CoCrFeNiY x HEA (x = 0 ÷ 0.3) from 
~ 200 to ~ 1400 MPa [53]. It was reported in [54] that 
eutectic microstructure can be formed in CoCrFeNiHf 0.4 
alloys, although the influence of hafnium on the micro-
structure evolution and mechanical properties of CoCr -
FeNi HEA has not been described in detail so far. The 
third strategy involves dispersion strengthening by adding 
a second phase to the HEA matrix, such as SiC [55, 56], 
TiC [ 57–60], Al2O3 [ 61, 62], and TiB2 [ 63]. In such a 
case, the increase in yield strength is due to the presence 
of reinforcing particles in the HEA matrix and grain 
refinement, which prevents dislocation movement and 
deformation.
Basically, the synthesis of modified HEA compositions 
is carried out using classical metallurgical technologies, and 
there are practically no studies of obtaining compact modi-
fied HEA using additive technologies, as well as preparation 
of powder materials for them [ 64–67]. The CoCrFeNiMn 
alloy with W addition was selected for its potential in pro-
ducing high-strength, corrosion-resistant units with good 
plasticity.
The paper presents the results of calculations of the dop-
ing system of CoCrFeNiMnW x, synthesis of powders by 
mechanical alloying followed by plasma spheroidization and 
compacting by L-PBF method. Tests of mechanical char -
acteristics in the temperature ranged from − 196 to 600 °C 
have been carried out and their evaluation has been given.
2 Materials and methods
Elemental powders were used as initial components for syn-
thesis: Chromium (99.9%), Tungsten (99.9%), Iron (99.9%), 
Cobalt (99.9%), Nickel (99.9%), and Manganese (99.9%). 
Mechanical alloying of HEA was carried out using Union 
Process SD-5 attritor with the following parameters: agitator 
rotation speed—270 rpm, weight of the suspension—2000 g, 
loading intensity—15:1, and duration—10, 15, 20 and 25 h.
The powders obtained by mechanical alloying were 
spheroidized in a thermal plasma flow using a Tek-15 unit 
manufactured by Tekna Plasma Systems Inc. (Canada). Tek-
Sphero 15 plasma spheroidization unit is equipped with a 
high-frequency generator with a maximum power of 15 kW. 
Argon–hydrogen mixture was used as a plasma gas. The 
plant power (10–15 kW), reactor pressure (10–15 psia), 
powder feed rate, and powder injection point into the plasma 
flow are the main parameters to be varied.
The phase diagram was calculated using ThermoCalc 
thermodynamic calculation software with the HEA4 data 
package for HEA in cantilever mode [68].
Fabrication of samples from HEA powders was carried 
out using SLM technology. Samples were manufactured 
using the SLM280HL (SLM Solutions GmbH, Lübeck, Ger-
many) selective laser melting system equipped with a YLR-
Laser (wavelength of 1070 nm and focus size about 80 μm). 
To investigate the influence of L-PBF parameters on the 
relative density and chemical composition of the obtained 
alloys, cubic samples with side 8 mm were fabricated. Laser 
power, scanning speed, and laser energy density were varied 
during fabrication. The thickness of the applied powder layer 
was 0.05 mm, and the distance between laser passes was 
0.12 mm. The variation of energy density at different L-PBF 
modes is shown in Fig. 1.

3 Results and discussion
To determine the composition for research, the phase dia-
gram and parameters affecting the formation of single-phase 
solid solutions of HEA of CoCrFeNiMnWx system were cal-
culated. Figure 2 shows the calculated state diagram of HEA 
 CoCrFeNiMnWx. The horizontal axis corresponds to the χW 
content, the increase in the amount of W is compensated by 
the decrease of all elements in equal fractions. The analyzed 
alloy CoCrFeNiMn at equiatomic content of components has 
a narrow crystallization interval (1550–1600 K), accompa-
nied by the formation of a solid solution with an FCC lattice 
(FCC_A1) with a wide homogeneity region. With increasing 
W content, the temperature interval of crystallization broad-
ens. When W is added up to 0.05χ , the broad homogeneity 
region of FCC_A1 is preserved. With the increase of W 
amount above 0.05χ the inhomogeneity of phase composi-
tion increases due to the formation of intermetallic phases. 
The formation of intermetallic phases, in turn, is caused by 
enthalpic (ΔHmix) contribution of W.

In the process of research, it was established that for the 
synthesis of powders of CoCrFeNiMn, CoCrFeNiMnW0.125, 
and CoCrFeNiMnW0.25, with high homogeneity of chemical 
composition, the duration of MA is 15–25 h and depends 
on the initial composition. Therefore, CoCrFeNiMn alloy 
powder already after 5 h of MA is a layered composite with 
uniformly distributed thin layers of initial components. 
When the duration of MA is increased up to 15 h, pow -
ders are obtained with homogeneous distribution along 
the cross-section. When tungsten is added, the dissolution 
pattern of alloying initial components changes. After 10 h 
MA, the powder of CoCrFeNiMnW 0.25 alloy has a pro-
nounced layered distribution of initial components along 
the cross-section of particles with the thickness of lamellae 
up to 2–3 μm (Fig.  3a). To obtain CoCrFeNiMnW0.125 and 
 CoCrFeNiMnW0.25 alloy powders with high homogeneity of 
chemical composition along the cross-section, the duration 
of MA is 20 and 25 h, respectively (Fig.  3b). However, in 
the powder after 25 h MA, there are large lamellar inclu-

milling material from the ball. The formation of inclusions 
is associated with both the increase in the time of MA and 
the increase in the hardness of the powder particles due to 
the increase in the number of microstresses in the material 
during MA, as well as due to the gradual distribution of 
tungsten over the cross-section of the particles.

Mechanical tests at various temperatures have shown 
that alloying with tungsten in the amount of 0.125 and 0.25 
formula units does not unambiguously affect the mechani-
cal properties (Fig.  9, Table 3). The character of the effect 
depends significantly on the test temperature. Therefore, at 
the test temperature − 196 °C, strength properties (σ B and 
σ0.2) practically do not change, and the plasticity is reduced 
by one and a half times. At the same time, the strength prop-
erties reach maximum values exceeding 1000 MPa.
The results of tests at room temperature showed the great-
est dependence of mechanical properties on the W content. 
With an increase in the W content, an increase in strength 
properties and a decrease in plasticity are observed. At the 
same time, the overall level of strength properties is signifi-
cantly reduced. When the test temperature rises to 600 °C, 
the strength characteristics also decrease, practically without 
affecting the plasticity. The analysis of the fractures obtained 
using an electron microscope (Fig. 10) showed that the vis-
cous nature of the fracture remains in all samples of the wind 
turbine during tensile tests.

Analysis of fractures obtained with an electron micro-
scope (Fig. 10) indicates the ductile nature of HEA fracture 
in tensile tests. The SLM HEA is composed of polycrystals, 
with crystal atoms arranged in an orderly manner. When a 
load is applied to the microstructure, the crystals experience 
micro-scale slip, inherent dislocations, and new dislocations, 
resulting in excessive dislocations entangled within the alloy 
and a local strengthening effect. When applied stress exceeds 
the yield stress threshold for local dislocation strengthening, 
the 'dislocation avalanche' phenomenon typically occurs. 
This drives sudden movement of the entangled dislocations, 
weakening the metal strength. The phenomenon is difficult 
to control and has a direct physical effect on the overall mac-
roscopic failure behavior of the materials [71].
4 Conclusion
The calculation of the alloying system, synthesis of 
 CoCrFeNiMnWx HEA powders with subsequent compact-
ing by the L-PBF method, and their properties were tested. 
In the course of calculations, it was found that the addition 
of W in the amount up to 0.024 mol% has practically no 
effect on the region of existence of solid solution with FCC 
lattice, only slightly increasing the crystallization interval. 
Increasing the W content in HEA increases the melting tem-
perature and crystallization interval, and also narrows the 
temperature interval of existence of solid solution. Thus, 
the addition of W in the amount of 0.05 mol% (0.25 for -
mula units) increases the crystallization interval from 40 to 
400 °C, while the temperature interval of existence of solid 
solution with FCC lattice decreases from 450 to 100 °C.
It is shown that for the synthesis of CoCrFeNiMn, 
 CoCrFeNiMnW0.125, and CoCrFeNiMnW 0.25 HEA pow -
ders with high homogeneity of chemical composition the 
duration of MA is 15–25 h. During the MA process, only 
partial dissolution of tungsten in the solid solution occurs. 
Even after 25 h, the peaks corresponding to the BCC lattice 
of tungsten are present on the XRD, which is probably due 
to its high hardness and limited solubility in solid solution. 
Remelting of CoCrFeNiMnWx HEA powders in the process 
of plasma spheroidization allows to obtain spherical pow -
ders with homogeneous microstructure and homogeneous 
distribution of alloying elements over the particle volume.
Compact samples of HEA CoCrFeNiMnW x were 
obtained from powder materials by the L-PBF method. The 
influence of energy density in the L-PBF process on the 
specific density and chemical composition of the fabricated 
samples was investigated. When the energy density increases 
above 76 J/mm3, the manganese content decreases signifi-
cantly compared to the initial powder, which is due to its 
high partial pressure. At volumetric energy densities below 
42 J/mm3, a sharp decrease in the specific gravity of HEA 
is observed.
Mechanical tests of HEA samples on uniaxial tension in 
the temperature range − 196 °C to 600 °C showed practi-
cal invariability of fracture energy both at cryogenic and 
high temperatures. When W was added to the alloy in the 
amount of 0.125 and 0.25 formula units, the tensile strength 
increased on average by 9 and 19%, and the yield strength 
by 10 and 18%, respectively. When tested at liquid nitrogen 
temperature, it is found that the yield and strength limits 
increase by 30–35% while maintaining ductility.
