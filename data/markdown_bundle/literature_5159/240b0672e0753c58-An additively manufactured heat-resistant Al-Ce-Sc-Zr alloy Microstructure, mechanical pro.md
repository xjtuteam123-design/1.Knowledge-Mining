ABSTRACT 
In this study, a high-strength and heat-resistant Al – Ce – Sc – Zr alloy was designed and fabricated by additive 
manufacturing. The as-built alloy shows an excellent combination of strength and ductility compared to other 
Al – Ce-based alloys, including an ultimate tensile strength of 445 MPa, a yield strength of 344 MPa and an 
elongation of 10%. Even at 300 
◦
C, the alloy still retains a superb tensile yield strength of 233 MPa, which is the 
best among the high-temperature aluminum alloys reported so far. The excellent performance is mainly attrib -
uted to a significant amount of fine, homogeneous and coarsening-resistant Al
11
Ce
3 
intermetallic nanoparticles 
formed during the rapid solidification process of laser additive manufacturing. The formation of coherent Al
3
(Sc, 
Zr) nano-precipitates and interfacial segregation at the interface of intermetallic nanoparticles during high 
temperature exposure further improve the high temperature properties of the alloy. Our work has the potential to 
provide design concepts for the development of novel high temperature aluminum alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Aluminum alloys are widely used in industrial fields such as aero -
space and transportation attributing to their excellent properties of 
lightweight, high specific strength and corrosion resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. With 
the development of sustainable green economy, a primary goal for new 
applications of aluminum alloys is to replace titanium and nickel-based 
alloys in moderate temperature applications (200 – 450 
◦
C), such as valve 
filter and engine cylinder head [ 4 ], which will not only reduce the 
weight of components, but also save costs [ 5 ]. However, conventional 
aluminum alloys lose a significant portion of their strength above 
approximately 200 
◦
C due to the rapid coarsening and loss of 
strengthening precipitates [ 6 – 8 ]. Therefore, there is a recognized need 
for development of novel high temperature resistant aluminum alloys. 
The eutectic aluminum – cerium (Al – Ce) alloys are promising candi -
dates for elevated temperature applications due to the high eutectic 
temperature of 642 
◦
C and excellent microstructural thermal stability up 
to 500 
◦
C [ 9 – 12 ]. Cerium has very low solid solubility and diffusion 
coefficient in aluminum matrix, which can both inhibit the coarsening of 
the Al
11
Ce
3 
intermetallic phase based on the Ostwald ripening theory. 
These characteristics make Al – Ce alloys have better high temperature 
properties than existing aluminum alloys. Sims et al. reported that the 
cast binary Al – Ce eutectic alloy has wonderful high temperature sta -
bility, which can remain 80% of the room temperature mechanical 
properties at 240 
◦
C [ 9 ]. Liu et al. reported a cast Al – 12.5 wt%Ce which 
maintained its microhardness after aging at 400 
◦
C for 12 weeks, 
attributed to the excellent thermal stability of Al
11
Ce
3 
strengthening 
precipitates [ 13 ]. Besides, cerium is one of the most abundant rare earth 
elements in the crust and is often considered to be a by-product of rare 
earth mining. The development of Al – Ce alloys is available for solving 
the pervasive issue of excessive by-products in manufacturing [ 14 ]. The 
eutectic Al
11
Ce
3 
phase in alloy processed by conventional casting 
method shows a lath-like morphology with micro-scale dimensions [ 13 ]. 
The strength of the alloy is mainly derived from the load transfer 
mechanism from the soft matrix to the stiff Al
11
Ce
3 
strengthening phase 
[ 9 , 15 ]. However, the cast binary Al – Ce eutectic alloy is not suitable for 
engineering applications because it shows poor mechanical properties at 
room temperature which is mainly due to the lack of solute in aluminum 
matrix and the relatively coarse eutectic structure. 
Recently, additive manufacturing (AM) has gained wide attention 
from academia and industry as an innovative technology [ 16 , 17 ]. The 
rapid cooling rate during AM can greatly refine the microstructure and

strength of Al – Ce alloys [ 35 , 36 ]. Among TM elements, Sc is the most 
suitable element in Al alloys. The Al

crystal structure and similar lattice parameters as α -Al, and the lattice 
parameter mismatch is only 1.3% [ 37 ]. The coherent interface between 
the Al

strength of the alloy without sacrificing the plasticity [ 32 , 38 ]. Mean -

minimizing the interface free energy. Zr is usually added to Al alloys 
together with Sc, the Zr atoms can replace Sc atoms in Al
3

Al

3

since the diffusion coefficient of Zr in Al is smaller than that of Sc, the 
Al

3

3
(Sc, Zr) nano -
precipitates can be formed in cast Al – Ce – Sc – Zr alloys, effectively 
improving the strength at room temperature [ 35 , 36 ]. Nevertheless, 
there is little literature to systematically study the effect of transition 
metal elements addition on the eutectic morphology and alloy strength 
of Al – Ce alloy processed via additive manufacturing. The interaction 
mechanism of Sc/Zr and Ce element under rapid solidification is still not 
clear. 
In this paper, a high-strength and heat-resistant 
Al – 10Ce – 0.4Sc – 0.2Zr (wt.%) alloy processed by AM is reported. The 
response of the eutectic microstructure to different processing tech -
niques was studied. Then the effects of micro-addition Sc and Zr on AM 
Al – Ce alloy were emphatically investigated through multi-scale micro -
structure characterization and mechanical testing. In addition, the 
contribution of different strengthening mechanisms to the strength of 
the Al – Ce – Sc – Zr alloy and the coarsening kinetics of the Al
11
Ce
3 
phase 
were discussed in detail. 
2. Experimental procedures 
2.1. Material processing 
The amount of Ce was selected according to the eutectic point 
composition of the Al – Ce binary phase diagram. In addition, 0.4 wt% Sc 
and 0.2 wt% Zr were added on this basis. Two kinds of pre-alloy pow -
ders, Al – 10 wt% Ce – 0.4 wt% Sc – 0.2 wt% Zr (Al – Ce – Sc – Zr), Al – 10 wt% 
Ce (Al – Ce), were respectively produced by the argon gas atomization. 
Fig. 1 a and d shows the morphology of Al – Ce – Sc – Zr and Al – Ce pre-alloy 
powders, respectively. The powder particles are in spherical shape, 
containing a small number of satellite balls. The Al – Ce pre-alloy pow -
ders contain more fine powder and the agglomeration is more obvious. 
The average particle sizes (d
50

and 29 μ m, respectively, which were characterized by a laser particle 
size analyzer, as exhibited in Fig. 1 b and e. The chemical compositions of 
the powders determined by inductively coupled plasma optical emission 
spectroscopy (ICP – OES) are summarized in Table 1 . It contains trace 
amounts of impurity elements, such as Fe and Si. Prior to the additive 
manufacturing (AM) process, the powders were dried in a vacuum 
drying oven at 120 
◦
C for 4 h. In addition, a piece of Al-10 wt% Ce as-cast 
alloy ingot supplied by Beijing Zhongke Yannuo New Material Tech -
nology Co., LTD was prepared to serve as a control.

of as-built Al – Ce – Sc – Zr and Al – Ce alloy can reach 99.92% and 99.36%,

especially the Al – Ce – Sc – Zr alloy, which can achieve nearly fully dense 
forming. Fig. 2 exhibits the XRD patterns of as-built Al – Ce – Sc – Zr, as-

4.1. Solidification microstructure

the microstructure of the alloy, and finer microstructure can provide 
more incremental strength at room temperature. During the LPBF pro -

as-cast state due to the extreme non-equilibrium process. To charac -
terize the quantitative relationship between microstructure and cooling 
rate, a model established by Trivedi, Magnin and Kurz (TMK model) was 
used to predict the solidification microstructure during non-equilibrium 
solidification, which has already been applied in AM alloys [ 28 , 63 , 64 ].

5. Conclusions 
In summary, the Al – 10Ce – 0.4Sc – 0.2Zr (wt.%) alloy prepared by 
additive manufacturing in this work exhibits excellent mechanical 
properties and heat resistance. The effects of rapid solidification and 
micro-addition Sc/Zr on eutectic microstructure, mechanical properties 
were studied. The strengthening mechanisms and thermal stability were 
preliminarily investigated. The key conclusions are as follows. 
1. The rapid solidification process of additive manufacturing can refine 
the eutectic microstructure and change the morphology of the 
Al
11
Ce
3 
intermetallic compound. The Al
11
Ce
3 
intermetallic formed 
during casting has a lath-like morphology with a width of 0.7 – 1 μ m, 
while the Al
11
Ce
3 
intermetallic formed during AM has a ribbonlike 
morphology with an average width of ~32 nm and is arranged in an 
intricate discontinuous network. 
2. The addition of Sc and Zr has no significant effect on the morphology 
of the as-built microstructure. Sc and Zr elements mainly exist in α -Al 
matrix in the form of supersaturated solid solution. After thermal 
exposure, fine and dispersive Al
3

3. The as-built Al – Ce – Sc – Zr alloy exhibits excellent room temperature 
tensile properties with a high tensile strength of 445 ± 12 MPa, a 
yield strength of 344 ± 9 MPa and an elongation of 10 ± 0.4%. The 
dominant strength contribution of the Al – Ce – Sc – Zr alloy comes from 
the Al
11
Ce

and uniform distribution.
