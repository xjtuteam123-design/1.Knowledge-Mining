ABSTRACT 
An equiatomic CoFeNiMnV high-entropy alloy with a face-centred cubic phase was cast and cold-rolled. The 
influence of recrystallisation and σ phase precipitation on the mechanical properties of this alloy was explored by 
varying the annealing temperature from 700 to 1000 
◦
C. The results revealed that V is more effective than Cr at 
stabilising the σ phase. The precipitation of σ phase occurred before recrystallisation during the annealing 
process. The σ phase precipitation strengthening and recrystallisation softening had a combined effect on me -
chanical behaviour. The strength decreased with increasing annealing temperature, resulting from an increased 
recrystallisation percentage. Moreover, a heterogeneous structure with partial recrystallisation resulted in back 
stress hardening, and the precipitated σ -phase promoted crack nucleation, resulting in premature fracture. 
1. Introduction 
Recently, researchers have developed a unique type of alloy with five 
or more elements in equimolar or near-equimolar ratios, referred to as 
high-entropy alloys (HEAs) [1,2] . HEAs preferentially exhibit simple 
solid solution phases owing to the reduced Gibbs free energy of mixing 
by higher mixing entropy [1 – 4] . They are expected to replace some 
traditional structural materials because of their high strength [5] , high 
plasticity [6] , excellent wear resistance [7] , and other properties [8] . 
Among these HEAs, the representative face-centred cubic (FCC) struc -
ture CoCrFeNiMn HEAs developed by Cantor [1] exhibit strong 
temperature-dependent strength and ductility [5,9 – 11] , making these 
HEAs some of the most widely studied HEAs to date. However, the 
CoCrFeNiMn HEAs exhibit low tensile strength, which is regarded as a 
major drawback for application in the industrial field [12 – 14] . 
To increase the strength of Cantor alloys, several researchers have 
attempted to introduce second-phase strengthening [11,15,16] or 
maximise solid-solution strengthening [17,18] in the FCC single-phase 
microstructure by designing the chemical composition of the alloys. 
For example, the formation of FCC + BCC dual phase structures via the 
addition of Al and the precipitation of the σ phase by Mo microalloying 
are helpful for improving strength [19,20] . The addition of infinitesimal 
interstitial elements, such as carbon or boron, leads to solid-solution 
strengthening and the precipitation-hardening [21 – 23] . Adding V and 
Cr to the CoFeMnNi-based HEA system have increased the strength but 
deteriorated the ductility, which was due to σ phase precipitation 
attributing to the significant distortions in the solid solution caused by 
the poor compatibility of V with other alloying elements [15] . Jo et al. 
[16] controlled the formation temperature of σ phase by the addition of 
V, and the final atomic composition of V20Cr15Fe20Ni45 shows the 
high yield strength and tensile strength (0.82 GPa and 1.37 GPa, 
respectively) with the elongation up to 43 % at cryogenic-temperature. 
As reported, V and Cr are σ -phase-forming elements [16] . However, the 
simultaneous presence of V and Cr makes it impossible to clearly denote 
the effect of V on the microstructure formation of CoFeNiMn-based 
alloys. 
Cold rolling followed by recrystallisation annealing is also an effec -
tive method for tuning the microstructure and optimising the mechan -
ical properties of HEAs. It has been shown that [24] the strength of the 
CoCrFeMnNi HEA is enhanced by the complex heterogeneous structures 
comprising the σ phase and the non-recrystallised and recrystallised 
grains formed by cold rolling and annealing. The FCC phase of the

2. Experimental procedure 
2.1. Material preparation 
Ingots of CoFeNiMnV HEAs were prepared by arc melting high purity 
Co, Fe, Ni, Mn, and V mixtures comprising pure metals (purity > 99.9 wt 
%) under an argon atmosphere. During the smelting process, the alloys 
were re-smelted at least four times to ensure their compositional ho -
mogeneity. The as-cast ingots were encapsulated in quartz ampules, 
homogenised for 4 h at 1000 
◦
C, and then cold-rolled (CR) at room 
temperature with a thickness reduction of 60 % to produce a 1.3-mm- 
thick sheet. The CR sheets were individually annealed at 700, 800, 
900, and 1000 
◦
C for 1 h, denoted as 700A, 800A, 900A and 1000A 
samples, respectively, followed by air cooling. 
2.2. Microstructural characterisation

X-ray diffraction (XRD, D/Max2500 V) with Cu-Ka radiation ( λ = 1.54 
Å) scanning from 20
◦

◦

microstructural evolution was characterised using scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM, Hitachi S4800) with an energy dispersive spectrom -
eter (EDS) and electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD, EDAX-TSL) with 
step sizes of 0.65 µ m. The annealed samples for the EBSD experiments 
were mechanically polished and then electropolished using a mixture of

◦

EBSD data were then exported to TSL-OIM ™ software. The EBSD anal -

average misdirection (KAM) maps. KAM is the angle between adjacent 
points in polycrystalline materials and is a parameter obtained by 
calculating the deviation. It can be used to quantify the strain distribu -
tion and dislocation density [26 – 29] . In the KAM diagram, the lowest 
and highest dislocation densities in the scanned area are shown in blue 
and red, respectively.

ensure the accuracy of the measurement results. Ambient temperature

3.2. Hardness 
Fig. 5 shows the hardness variations of the as-cast, CR, and annealed 
CoFeNiMnV alloy samples. This figure shows that the hardness of the CR 
sample is apparently higher than that of the cast sample from 216 HV to 
342 HV, suggesting that significant work hardening occurs after the 
cold-rolling process. The softening rate was expressed as the ratio of the 
reduced hardness after annealing to the hardness of the CR sample. After 
annealing at 700
◦
C, the softening rate was 11 %, and the alloy 
hardness increased to 379.1 HV. This is mainly because the softening 
effect of recovery in the 700A sample does not cover the precipitation 
strengthening of the σ phase. When the samples were annealed at 800 
and 900
◦
C, the hardness reduced to 284 and 282 HV, respectively. The 
softening effect was increased as a result of recovery and partial 
recrystallisation. When the annealing temperature reached 1000
◦
C, the 
softening rate considerably increased to 34 % and the hardness dropped 
to 227 HV, which can mainly be attributed to the full recrystallisation.

4. Discussion 
The microstructural analyses showed that the as-cast CoFeNiMnV 
HEA exhibited an FCC structure. This is closely related to the fact that 
the constituents of CoFeNiMnV belong to the 3d transition series, which 
contributes to small differences in atomic sizes and facilitates the for -
mation of an FCC solid-solution [30] . The single FCC solid structure in 
HEAs is challenged by certain components or by heat treatment. Tsai 
et al. [31,32] revealed that the tendency for the σ phase to form in Cr- 
and/or V-containing HEAs could be predicted by valence electron con -
centration (VEC) values, that is, alloys with VEC values in the range of 
6.88 – 7.84 are prone to σ phase formation either in the as-cast state or 
during ageing at suitable temperatures. The VEC value of the present 
CoFeNiMnV alloy has a VEC value of 7.80, and the σ phase was formed 
in the annealed conditions. Thus, the above VEC criterion was shown to

be activated. Because both recrystallisation and σ phase precipitation 
are controlled by elemental diffusion, the interactions between the two 
behaviours are unavoidable. Owing to the sluggish diffusion effect in 
HEAs, recrystallisation usually begins at a high annealing temperature. 
In the CoFeNiMnV alloy, the precipitation of the σ phase emerged in the 
700A sample, while the first appearance of recrystallised grains 
happened in the sample annealed at 800

phase precipitation occurred before recrystallisation in the CoFeNiMnV 
alloy. With an increase in annealing temperature, the fraction of

The evolution of recrystallisation and σ phase precipitation resulted 
in the corresponding hardness and tensile behaviour. In the 700A alloy, 
the hardness and strength were higher owing to the deformed micro -
structure and the fine precipitation of σ phase ( Fig. 5 ). It should be noted 
that although the ultimate tensile strength of the 700A alloy was higher 
than the 800A, 900A and 1000A alloys, the yield strength of the 700A 
alloy is still smaller than that of the 800A, 900A and 1000A alloys. The 
smaller yield strength in 700A is possibly due to the very few σ phase. 
When the annealing temperature increased to 800
◦
C and 900
◦

recrystallisation occurred but was not completed, and the σ phase pre -
cipitation was pronounced. Generally, the precipitation of second-phase 
particles could effectively enhance the mechanical properties of alloy. 
Though the 900A alloy showed larger quantities of σ particles, the 
hardness was nearly similar to that of the 800A alloy. This could be 
attributed to the larger number of soft recrystallized grains in 900A 
alloy. Heterogeneous structures with partially recrystallised grains were 
produced in the 800A and 900A alloys. In addition to precipitation 
strengthening, heterogeneity in the FCC matrix plays an important role 
in determining the mechanical properties [39 – 42] . Dislocation slipping 
in heterogeneous alloys is difficult because of the long-range back stress, 
which results from the piled up and blocked mobile dislocations in the 
interfaces between un-recrystallised and recrystallised grains [43] . This 
phenomenon is usually termed back stress hardening, and it increases 
the strength and work-hardening rate of the material [44] . Thus, the 
800A and 900A alloys showed a higher strength and work-hardening 
rate than the those of the 1000A alloy with a fully recrystallised struc -
ture ( Fig. 6 ). The 1000A alloy showed a relatively high ductility because 
of the fully recrystallised microstructures. In conclusion, the micro -
structural characteristics produced by recrystallisation and σ phase 
precipitation in the CoFeNiMnV alloy have a comprehensive impact on 
mechanical behaviour. 
5. Conclusion 
1. The as-cast equiatomic CoFeNiMnV alloy had an FCC structure. The σ 
phase was formed in this alloy after cold rolling and annealing at 
700 – 1000
◦
C. Moreover, V is more effective than Cr at stabilising the 
σ phase resulted from the more negative mixing enthalpy of V with 
other constituents and the wider composition ranges for the σ phase 
in V-containing binary systems. 
2. During the annealing process for the cold-rolling CoFeNiMnV alloy, 
the σ phase precipitation occurred before the recrystallisation. 
3. The strengthening effect provided by σ phase precipitation and the 
softening effect caused by recrystallisation affected the strength and 
ductility of the alloy. The heterogeneous structure of recrystallised 
and non-recrystallised grains offers back stress hardening. Early 
fracture can result from the influence of the σ phase during crack 
initiation.
