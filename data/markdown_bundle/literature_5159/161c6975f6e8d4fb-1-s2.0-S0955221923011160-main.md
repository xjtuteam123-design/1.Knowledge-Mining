ABSTRACT 
In this paper, the “ flash brazing ” of SiC is successfully achieved using FeCoCrNiCu alloy in air for seconds at an 
ultra-low furnace temperature of 400 ℃ . The interface temperature reaches over 1000 ℃ in 1 s at an ultra-fast 
rate via the electric field assistance. The reaction layer of the joint consists mainly of Cr
23
C
6 
and the silicides of 
Fe, Co, Ni, and Cu. With the increase of brazing holding time, Cu gradually precipitates from the alloy, and the 
shear strength of the joints first increases and then decreases. The maximum strength value is 80 MPa at the test 
temperature of 800 ℃ . This approach could help significantly improve the efficiency of ceramic bonding and 
achieve low-temperature joining for high-temperature applications. 
1. Introduction 
Silicon carbide (SiC) ceramic is widely used in nuclear power stations 
and machining because of its high thermostability, wear resistance, and 
good irradiation resistance [1 – 3] . However, the inherent high hardness 
and low toughness severely limit the fabrication of large and complex 
SiC components. Joining is an effective and reliable approach to pre -
paring large and complex ceramic components. Brazing is a widely used 
ceramic joining method [4 – 6] . SiC is an attractive high-temperature 
structural ceramic, however, the major problem in brazing SiC lies in 
its limited application at higher temperatures [7] . The commonly used 
Ag-Cu-Ti active brazing alloy usually could not withstand temperatures 
higher than 600 ℃ [8 – 11] . Therefore, it is imperative to develop a novel 
brazing alloy to improve the high-temperature performance of the joint. 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are currently a hot research topic in 
materials science and engineering [12 – 15] . HEAs comprise multiple 
principal elements and possess excellent mechanical properties at high 
temperatures [16 – 20] . In recent years, HEAs have been widely used for 
joining as brazing alloy [21,22] . However, in the abovementioned 
methods, the joining temperature is generally higher than 1000 ℃ , and 
vacuum or inert gas protection is necessary, reducing efficiency 
[23 – 26] . Moreover, far too little attention has been paid to the 
high-temperature performance of the joints obtained using high entropy 
brazing alloy. Therefore, it is urgent to develop a novel joining approach 
at ultra-low temperatures, and it is necessary to explore the 
high-temperature performance of the joint systematically. 
The electric field could generate high temperatures at an ultrafast 
rate by Joule heating and promote the material bonding process 
[27 – 29] . The electric field has been applied to the processing and 
manufacturing ceramics, such as sintering, joining, etc. The researchers 
have developed a novel electric field-assisted sintering technique for 
ceramics, flash sintering [30 – 32] , which densifies ceramics within sec -
onds at moderate furnace temperatures [33 – 36] . Shen et al. demon -
strated that the current could promote the wetting of the ceramic 
substrate by liquid brazing alloys, and the mechanical property of the 
joint obtained by microcurrent assistance was substantially improved 
[37 – 40] . Moreover, in our previous research, SiC was conductive at 400 
℃ when the electric field applied to the samples was above the 
threshold. Meanwhile, the interface could be heated to over 900 ℃ in 
seconds, proving that local heating is feasible via direct current (DC) 
assistance [41,42] . Therefore, we deduce that the electric current could 
be used to create the high temperature at the interface to melt the alloy 
instantaneously, achieving the ultrafast and ultra-low temperature 
joining of SiC. 
In this work, we successfully achieved the flash brazing of SiC using 
FeCoCrNiCu high-entropy alloy in air at 400 ℃ . The approach is named 
"flash brazing" because the power dissipation trend is similar to the flash 
sintering [43] and flash joining [44,45] . The microstructure and the 
evolution rule of the interface were systematically studied. Meanwhile, 
the effect of the joining time on the joint mechanical properties at high 
temperatures was investigated. The relationship between the interfacial 
microstructures and the properties was expounded.

2. Experimental 
SiC used in this work was prepared by pressureless sintering with 
98.7% purity (Guangzhou Kaihongfei Electronics Co. Ltd., Guangzhou, 
China). The SiC substrates were sliced into 5 mm × 5 mm × 3 mm pieces 
and 5 mm × 10 mm × 3 mm pieces for microstructure analysis and shear 
testing, respectively. The FeCoCrNiCu high-entropy alloy was prepared 
by the arc-melting method with a thickness of 100 µ m (Beijing Yijin New 
Material Technology Co. Ltd., Beijing, China). The data acquisition 
board (USB3200, Beijing ART Technology Development Co. Ltd., Bei -
jing, China) was used to record the voltage and current variations. Two 
sets of 0.2 mm diameter R-type thermocouples were placed at the 
sample interface and substrate surface to monitor the temperature 
changes during the flash brazing. The schematic diagram of the joining 
apparatus and sample assembly is shown in Fig. 1 . Additionally, ther -
mocouple 2 was sacrificial and damaged during the experiments. The 
sample was placed between the electrode sheets, and a pressure of 0.4 
MPa was applied to ensure tight contact. The furnace temperature rose 
to 400 ℃ at a rate of 10 ℃ /min and held for 2 min. The DC power supply 
was subsequently activated, and a constant voltage of 150 V was applied 
to the sample until the sample conductivity was achieved. The sample 
was held at 12 A-0.4 MPa for 1 s, 15 s, and 30 s, respectively. The 
microstructure of the joint was characterized using scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM, FEI Quanta 200 FEG, USA), electron probe X-ray 
micro-analyzer (EPMA, JXA-8230, JEOL, Japan), energy dispersive 
spectrometer (EDS, Merlin Compact, ZEISS, Germany) and transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM, JEM-2100 F, Japan). The shear strength of 
the joints was evaluated at the high temperature of 800 ℃ and under 
room temperature (RT) using a universal testing machine (AG-X Plus, 
Shimadzu, Japan) with a constant loading rate of 0.5 mm/min.

Fig. 4 (a) demonstrates the microstructure of the SiC/ FeCoCrNiCu 
alloy/SiC joint acquired at 400 ℃ 12 A-15 s-0.4 MPa. The joint dis -
plays a tight bonding at the substrate/alloy interface. The brazing seam 
is approximately 30 µ m thicker than the initial FeCoCrNiCu alloy, 
indicating a vigorous reaction between FeCoCrNiCu and SiC during the 
flash brazing. The interface structure is symmetric and primarily con -
sists of three layers. Layer 1 contains numerous irregular black massive 
phases, while layer 2 consists of a continuous striped dark grey phase. 
Layer 3 is predominantly composed of the light grey phase and the 
irregular white phase. Fig. 4 (b-i) shows the element X-ray mapping 
obtained using EPMA to illustrate the element distribution across the 
interface. Layer 1 primarily comprises elements Fe, Co, Ni, and Si, and 
the spatial distribution of the black phase aligns with the mapping of C. 
It indicates that the SiC reacts with the FeCoCrNiCu alloy during flash 
brazing, resulting in the thickening of the interface. Layer 2 only consists 
of Cr and C. The dark gray phase in layer 3 is a Cu-depleted phase 
composed of Co, Ni, Fe, and Si. The white phase is caused by Cu 
segregation, resembling the initial microstructure of the alloy. Notably, 
no obvious oxidation of the joint can be observed. At the flash moment, 
the brazing alloy instantly melted and completely contacted the SiC 
surface to be joined, converting the original solid-gas interface into the 
solid-liquid interface. The interface was isolated from the air, preventing 
oxidation of the interface.

The comparative test was conducted to demonstrate the effect of the 
electric field. The joining of SiC using FeCoCrNiCu alloy was achieved in 
air at 1200 ℃ for 1 min, and the microstructure of the joint is shown in 
Fig. 7 (a). The joint comprises three layers, and the structure of reaction 
layer 1 resembles that obtained by flash brazing. As shown in Fig. 7 (b-i), 
layer 1 mainly contains Co, Ni, and Fe, and the dark phase is the C 
monomer. The dark grey reaction layer 2 is the Cr-rich phase, which 
exhibits a non-uniform and asymmetrical distribution between layer 1 
and the brazing seam. The segregation of Cu can be observed at the 
brazing seam, and the distribution area of Co, Ni, and Fe basically 
matches. Meanwhile, a minor quantity of Si is found in the brazing seam. 
According to Fig. 7 (j), apparent cracks and holes can be found in the 
joint. The shear strength of the joint is 9.8 MPa at room temperature and 
11.6 MPa at 800 ℃ , which falls significantly short of meeting desired 
standards. Comparative test indicated that the electric field played a 
vital role in achieving highly efficient ultra-fast joining. 
The effect of joining time on the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of the join was explored in this work.The flash brazing of SiC 
was achieved at 400 ℃ 0.4 MPa for 1 s, 15 s and 30 s, respectively. 
The microstructure of the joints obtained at different joining times is 
depicted in Fig. 8 (a-c), finding the increase in interface thickness with 
longer joining times. It could be observed that the presence of Cr 
enrichment and Cu segregation becomes evident with extended joining 
times. It is related to the instantaneous melting of the FeCoCrNiCu alloy 
and the element interdiffusion at high temperatures. At the instant of the 
flash, the interface temperature reached 1570 ℃ , which was above the 
melting point of the alloy, resulting in complete contact between the 
alloy and the SiC surface. During the stabilization period, the interface 
temperature ranged from 1080 ℃ to 1120 ℃ , facilitating the interdif -
fusion of elements and reactions between the substrate and the alloy.

4. Conclusions 
In summary, the flash brazing of SiC is achieved using FeCoCrNiCu 
high-entropy alloy in air for seconds. The peak power density is 
1.03 × 10
4 
mW/mm
3
, and the interface temperature instantaneously 
rises to 1570 ℃ at the moment of flash. During the stabilization period, 
the interfacial temperature ranges from 1080 ℃ to 1120 ℃ , which en -
sures adequate interface reaction and element interdiffusion. The reac -
tion layer at the interface consists of silicides, and the continuous and 
uniform Cr
23
C
6 
layer was formed between the reaction layer and the 
brazing seam. With the extension of time, the shear strength of the joints 
increases and then decreases, and the maximum shear strength at room 
temperature is 73 MPa. The high-temperature strength of the joint is 
higher than the room-temperature strength due to the release of residual 
stresses. The maximum value of shear strength is 80 MPa at 800 ℃ . This 
work successfully realized the ultra-fast joining of SiC using FeCoCrNiCu 
high-entropy alloy via electric field assistance and systematically 
explored the high-temperature mechanical properties of the joint. More 
broadly, this study developed an ultra-low temperature brazing 
approach for conductive ceramics and provided a novel strategy for 
high-temperature applications of high-entropy alloys.
