Abstract 
 Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-1Cr-1Fe (Ti-55511), one of the near β-Ti alloys is considered to be the 
candidate material for high strength applications. However, the tendency to excessive grain 
growth under high temperatures limits its potential applications due to drastic reduction in 
strength with temperature and poses difficulties du ring forming. In present study, based on 
the understanding established for the workhorse Ti- 6Al-4V and other Ti alloys, the near- β, 
Ti-55511 alloy was modified with 0.06 and 0.12 wt.% B to investigate the effect of boron on 
its microstructure and high temperature tensile behavior. The boron addition led to significant 
grain refinement and changes in morphology of α-phase from lamellar to globular. The high 
temperature tensile tests conducted over a range of temperatures and strain rates showed that 
the alloy with 0.06 wt.% B displays superplastic ch aracteristics with maximum elongation to 
failure of 385% at 1123 K and an initial strain rat e of 1 × 10 -3 s-1. The bare alloy and the alloy 
with higher boron content (0.12 wt.%) exhibited rel atively higher flow stresses and 
consequently reduced superplastic elongations. Cyclic strain rate tests indicated that the alloy 
exhibits high strain rate sensitivity ( m) values in the range of 0.3-0.8, which suggests th at 
grain boundary sliding (GBS) is the dominant strain contributing mechanism for the observed 
superplasticity at elevated temperatures. Further, the detailed microstructural examination

1. Introduction 
Near β-Ti alloys are known to possess a good combination of high specific strength, 
fracture toughness and hardenability. These propert ies are majorly derived from the 
microstructure obtained by a suitable heat treatmen t, which involves solution treatment to 
produce a metastable β-phase, followed by aging to decompose the metastab le β-phase into α 
precipitates in stable β matrix. These alloys are considered to be good can didate materials for 
high strength applications such as aerospace struct ural and pressure vessel components. 
However, these alloys have found limited use due to some drawbacks like excessive beta 
grain growth during elevated temperature exposure a nd reduced ductility in high strength 
conditions. When the β-grains formed during annealing in the single phase β region are large 
in size and equiaxed, the problem becomes more pred ominant [1]. Further, the presence of 
continuous α layer along the grain boundary can deteriorate the ductility and fracture 
toughness of Ti alloys. There is a need to address these issues to ensure widespread aerospace 
applications of these alloys. An overview on thermo-mechanical processing of β-Ti alloys has 
been presented by Weiss et al. [2] and it was shown that careful control of microstructure is 
essential to tailor the properties of these alloys. The near β-Ti alloys have been widely 
studied, and their microstructural evolution with h eat treatment viz. solution treatment and 
aging, β-annealing and sub- β transus annealing and their compressive deformatio n behavior 
have been reported [2,3]. 
The near β-Ti alloy of Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-1Fe-1Cr, popularly known as VT-22 or BT-22 
(Russian grade notation), undergoes an allotropic t ransformation with a beta transus ( Tβ) of 
~1133-1163 K [4]. The alloy exhibits high yield str ength ( σ y~1100 MPa) and ultimate tensile 
strength ( σ TS ~1250 MPa) and limited elongation to failure ( ef~8%) in solution treated and 
aged (STA) condition [4]. Various studies have been carried out on the Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-1Fe-
1Cr alloy viz. on deformation induced phase transformation [5], h igh strain rate behavior [6], 
high temperature compressive deformation behavior [ 7,8], processing maps [9,10], DRX

Superplasticity is a high temperature deformation p henomenon exhibited by mostly 
fine-grained materials typically with grain sizes l ess than 10 µm, although there are some 
reports demonstrating superplasticity in coarse gra ined metallic alloys [13]. It is important to 
exploit the superplastic characteristics of a Ti al loy, especially under tensile loading 
conditions so as to shape the complex components wi thout any necking/drastic reduction in 
thickness while forming. Many of the Ti alloys exhi bit very coarse grain sizes (in 
millimeters) in as-cast state. However, subsequent conventional thermo-mechanical 
treatments (forging and rolling) can break down the cast structure and produce the alloys in 
wrought form with grain sizes down to ~100 µm only. 
Addition of the grain refiners while melting the al loys is one of the well-established 
strategies to produce microcrystalline cast structu re, and eventually produce the wrought 
alloys with much finer grain sizes. Boron is known to act as a grain refiner in as-cast α+β Ti 
alloys (for example, Ti-6Al-4V) [14,15] and also in some β Ti alloys (Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-3Cr-
0.5Fe and Ti-15Mo-2.6Nb-3Al-0.2Si) [16,17]. Its add ition in trace amounts can have 
significant impact on grain refinement and thereby improves the mechanical properties 
including the retention of strength to elevated tem peratures and superplasticity phenomenon. 
For example, addition of 0.1 wt.% B has led to enha ncement of many mechanical properties 
of Ti-6Al-4V alloy [14,18–20]. Specifically, boron modified Ti-6Al-4V alloy exhibited 
substantial grain refinement and thereby, improved fracture toughness and fatigue resistance 
[19,21,22]. Further, boron containing Ti-6Al-4V all oy exhibited better response to secondary 
processing in the β phase [23] and/or ( α+β)-phase field [24–30]. In addition, Ti-6Al-4V alloy 
with boron showed improved formability during defor mation to large strains without 
cracking as compared to the bare alloy [31,32]. It has also been reported that boron-modified 
Ti-6Al-4V containing 1.6% B exhibits superplasticit y in the β-phase field due to the 
stabilization of a fine-grained microstructure by the presence of TiB [18,33]. 
Review of literature has shown that hot isothermal compression, cold working and 
heat treatment response of commercially available β-Ti alloys in general and Ti-55511 alloy 
in particular have been studied in detail. Our preliminary study [34] on indigenous production 
of Ti-55511 alloy with and without boron indicated that there is significant grain refinement 
of the as-cast microstructure from ~2000 µm down to ~300 µm upon addition of 0.12 wt.%

2.1. Materials 
Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-1Cr-1Fe alloys containing 0.06 wt.%B and 0.12 wt.% B and the bare 
alloy were chosen for the present study. The chemic al compositions of these alloys are given 
in Table 1. These three alloys are designated and r eferred hereafter as Ti-55511-0B alloy, Ti-
55511-0.06B alloy and Ti-55511-0.12B alloy. The all oys obtained in as-cast double vacuum

using an Instron-make UTM of 250 kN capacity equipp ed with a 2.25 kW furnace. The 
tensile behavior of the alloys at elevated temperat ures varying from 1023 to 1173 K at 50 K 
intervals was evaluated at different initial strain rates of 1 × 10 -2, 1× 10 -3 and 1× 10 -4 s -1. The

3.2. High temperature tensile behavior of Ti-55511 alloys 
The tensile response and the evolution of deformed microstructure of Ti-55511 alloys 
are studied as a function of test temperature, stra in rate and boron content. The effect of test 
temperature on flow behavior of the bare Ti-55511 a lloy and the alloy containing 0.06 wt.% 
B at an initial strain rate of 1 × 10 -3 s-1 is presented in Fig. 2a and 2b, respectively. The test 
temperatures were chosen such that they are in the vicinity of the Tβ of these alloys, so that 
the deformation behavior in both single phase β as well as two phase ( α+β) can be evaluated. 
As anticipated, with increase in the test temperature, the peak flow stress ( σ p) was found to be 
decreasing for both the alloys. However, at any tes t temperature, the alloy containing boron 
showed relatively lower flow stresses than the bare alloy. Both the alloys exhibited nearly a 
steady state flow with large true strain prior to f ailure ( ε>1.0) in the temperature range of 
1073-1123 K. 
As compared to the bare alloy, the boron containing alloy exhibited larger elongations to 
failure with a maximum value of ef ~385% at 1123 K. At low temperature i.e. 1023 K, th e 
flow curve of the bare alloy had a typical characte ristic feature of strain hardening, then 
softening, and again hardening. Whereas, the alloy with 0.06 wt.% B displayed rapid strain 
hardening followed by continuous flow softening at 1023 K. At higher temperature of 1173 K 
i.e. in the β-phase region, both the alloys showed slight strain hardening with strain and 
reduced total strain ( ε<0.8). Mild strain hardening was observed in a few samples after some 
amount of strain and all the specimens failed by ne cking thereafter. Similar findings were 
reported in literature for TC-11 and Ti-55211 alloy s [36,37] and were attributed to the 
decrease in the dislocation density by annihilation due to the higher driving force available at 
higher test temperature. 
The effect of initial strain rate on the tensile be havior of Ti-55511 alloy with and 
without boron was studied at various temperatures i n the range of 1023-1173 K. The flow 
curves of the alloys Ti-55511-0B and Ti-55511-0.06B obtained at 1123 K are shown in Fig. 
3a and 3b, respectively as a function of the initia l strain rate. Both the alloys exhibited 
positive strain rate sensitivity, i.e. the flow str ess increased with increase in strain rate. 
During testing at a higher strain rate of 1 × 10 -2 s-1, a distinct peak in flow stress was observed 
which was followed by continuous flow softening in both the alloys (Ti-55511-0B and Ti-
55511-0.06B). At very low strain rate of 1× 10 -4 s-1, both the alloys exhibited reduced ef with a 
slight strain hardening followed by flow softening. The intermediate strain rate of 1 × 10 -3 s-1 
appears to be the optimum at 1123 K for both the alloys.

4. Discussion 
It has been shown that the boron addition leads to significant grain refinement and 
also the change in morphology of α-phase from lamellar in bare alloy to globular in b oron 
added Ti-55511 alloy. Improvement in superplastic c haracteristics have been reported in 
boron modified Ti-6Al-4V and other β-Ti alloys [25,38–40]. Boron addition in Ti alloys 
resulted in β grain refinement by Zener pinning and β texture weakening which in turn results 
in α lath refinement [41,42]. Boron addition is also ex pected to reduce the number of steps 
required to achieve a fine grained microstructure b y thermo-mechanical processing of the Ti 
alloy due to the presence of initially finer as-cast grains.

phenomenological models. The generalized constituti ve equation to describe high

differences in diffusion characteristics with phase change. Under optimum test temperature

curves for elongation and displayed 1 × 10 -3 s-1 as the optimum strain rate. This study indicates

relatively lower test temperatures (1073-1123 K) an d higher strain rates (~1 × 10 -3 s -1) as 
compared to the other near- β Ti alloys. For example, Sun et al. [37] carried ou t a study on a 
(α+β) TC-11 alloy and found that the alloy exhibits sup erplastic elongations with highest 
elongation at a strain rate of 2.2 × 10 -4 s-1. Tensile deformation study was carried out by Sala m 
et al. [43] on a near- β Ti alloy, Ti-3Al-8V-6Cr-4Mo-4Zr and found that sup erplasticity was 
exhibited by the alloy in a narrow temperature rang e of 1123-1138 K and at a strain rate of 
~1 × 10 -4 s-1. 
The failure of a material depends on two factors i. e. the external flow localization and 
concurrent internal cavitation. The strain sensitiv ity ( m) value indicates the resistance to flow 
localization; larger the m value, lesser the flow localization. The Ti-55511- 0.06B alloy was 
also found to exhibit high ‘ m’ values of ~0.3-0.8 under optimum test conditions. Salam et al. 
[43] reported ‘m’ value of ~0.5 for a near- β Ti alloy which exhibited superplastic elongation. 
Morgan et al. [44] carried out strain rate cycling studies on three metastable β-Ti alloys Ti-
8Mn, Ti-15Mo and Ti-13Cr-11V-3Al with coarse grain sizes and Tβ in the range of 923-973 
K. These alloys exhibited ‘m’ values in the range of 0.3-0.5. If m < 0.3, diffusion creep and 
grain elongation occur in place of grain boundary s liding and the deformation mechanism is 
not conducive for maximizing elongation. The value of m should ideally be >0.5 for grain 
boundary sliding to occur. In the intermediate rang e of m=0.3-0.4, deformation occurs by 
dislocation creep mechanism controlled by recovery, which is not desirable [37]. Based on

the high ‘m’ value in the Ti-55511-0.06B alloy in the present s tudy, it can be concluded that

Finer grain size and thereby higher interface areas of β/α and or β/TiB may contribute 
to the higher amount of grain boundary sliding resu lting in their increased contribution to 
superplastic elongations observed in the Ti-55511-0 .06B alloy. At the optimum conditions of 
deformation, the microstructure in the failure regi on of the alloy consists of stable 
microstructure. As the temperature approaches the Tβ of the alloy, the Vf of second phase 
becomes negligible. This results in grain growth an d lowering of ef. Roy et al. [25] showed 
that, the basal plane intensity was higher for the grip region compared to the failure region of 
the deformed samples for both alloys of Ti6Al4V (wi th 0.1% B and without B), suggesting 
that the deformation mechanism of grain/phase bound ary sliding in the bare and boron 
containing alloys are similar albeit to a varying e xtent and contribute to superplastic 
deformation. The observed total elongation to failu re ( ef) values and the ‘ m’ values for the 
boron containing alloys in the present study are significantly high as compared to the bare Ti-
55511 alloy and other common near-β-Ti alloys, which are normally prone to excessive g rain 
growth [1] during exposure to elevated temperatures near Tβ of the alloy. 
The flow softening and premature failure of Ti-5551 1-0.12B alloy compared to Ti-
55511-0.06B alloy can be attributed to cavitation ( Fig. 9) occurring during tensile 
deformation. Cavitation leads to flow softening and thereby early failure and low ductility 
due to the disproportionate grain boundary sliding. Final failure occurs by the coalescence of 
cavities [45]. The presence of triple junctions, gr ain boundary ledges and particles may cause 
stress concentration by impeding GBS [13, 45]. Cavi ties may nucleate if the stress 
concentrations by GBS are not relieved rapidly. The tendency for cavitation can increase with 
increasing strain rate (stress is proportional to s train rate) due to increased stress 
concentration and difficulty of GBS accommodation b ecause of decreased residence time. 
The schematic of cavitation occurring at the boride interface after dislocation pile up during 
deformation is shown in Fig. 11. The mechanism of p remature failure in the Ti-55511-0.12B 
alloy is attributed to the dislocation pile up at t he hard boride-soft matrix interface and 
subsequent cavity growth/ coalescence and ultimatel y separation at these interfaces during 
tensile deformation as observed in Fig. 9e. The loc alized cavitation is associated with strain 
accumulation around the hard borides due to the dif ference in deformation response between 
the ductile matrix and the hard borides [46].

The observed strain hardening under some conditions can be because of several 
reasons such as dislocation storage (higher strain rates), concurrent grain growth (mostly, 
under low strain rates), crystallographic texture changes, dynamic precipitation and decreased 
local temperature on the specimen at large strains. 
Tamirisakandala et al. [15] studied the effect of b oron content in the range of 0.06 -
0.1% on the grain size in Ti-6Al-4V and Ti-6242S an d showed that there exists a critical 
level of boron for grain refinement, beyond which o nly a minor reduction in grain size is 
possible. The critical level of boron was found to be ~ 0.1% in their study. It is required to 
thus find an optimum boron content not only from gr ain refinement perspective, but also to 
obtain large elongations to failure during deformat ion. For the present alloy system Ti-55511 
alloy without and with 0.06, 0.12% B studied, based on the ‘ m’ values and the % elongation, 
it can be concluded that 0.06% B is the optimum con tent required to achieve large 
superplastic elongation at a temperature range of 1 123 K and strain rate of 1 × 10 -3 s -1. This 
can be ascribed to the higher GBS activity and limi ted grain growth during deformation due 
to the finer grain size and presence of controlled amounts of borides, respectively. Therefore, 
the deformation of boron containing alloys can be p erformed at much lower stresses to 
minimize the die wear, which is a main concern in a closed die forging operation. High wear 
and tear of dies is a common problem associated wit h closed die forging operation under 
normal conditions of deformation carried out at hig h stress regime. It is also understood that 
due to grain refinement, the strain rate required t o achieve the same level of deformation can 
be shifted to higher order strain rate regime resul ting in lesser time/ higher productivity for 
realization of superplastically deformed products. 
 
5. Conclusions 
1. The near β-Ti alloy, Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-1Cr-1Fe has undergone sign ificant grain 
refinement and globularization of α-phase upon addition of 0.06 wt.% of boron. 
However, further increase in boron to 0.12% has led to coarsening and agglomeration 
of α-phase and increased volume fraction of boride whiskers. 
2. Ti-55511-0.06B alloy exhibited superplastic charact eristics with maximum elongation 
to failure of 385% at test temperature of 1123 K an d an initial strain rate of 1 × 10 -3 s-1, 
whereas the bare alloy and Ti-55511-0.12B alloy exh ibited relatively higher flow 
stresses initially followed by flow softening and c onsequently reduced total elongation 
to failure.

3. Boron addition improved the strain rate sensitivity ‘ m’ of 0.3-0.8 in Ti-55511-0.06B 
alloy deformed at a temperature of 1123 K as compar ed to 0.3-0.5 in the bare alloy. 
High ‘m’ value suggests that grain boundary sliding could b e the dominant mechanism 
contributing to superplastic elongation in both the alloys albeit to a different extent. 
4. Ti-55511-0.12B alloy failed prematurely compared to the Ti-55511-0.06B alloy due to 
cavitation occurring at the boride particle-matrix interface. The bare alloy failed 
prematurely due to the presence of coarse and elongated lamellar α phase in β-matrix. 
5. The optimum conditions for superplastic deformation were found to be 1123 K and 
1× 10 -3 s-1 for the Ti-55511 alloy containing 0.06 wt.% B.
