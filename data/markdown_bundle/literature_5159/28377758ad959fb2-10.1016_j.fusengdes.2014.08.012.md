1. Introduction
Oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) steel is considered to be
one of the promising candidate structural materials for advanced
nuclear systems due to its excellent corrosion, irradiation resis-
tance, creep strength and oxidation resistance [1,2]. ODS steel
fabricated by powder metallurgy technique, the stable nano-sized
precipitates with high number density formed in the steel matrix
are the reason why ODS steels possess these excellent proper-
ties. Currently, diffusion bonding and friction stir welding (FSW)
are considered as the most promising joint technique in order
to preserve homogeneous distribution of nano-scaled oxide par-
ticles in ODS alloys’ joints. However, the application of diffusion
bonding and FSW is limited due to its poor adaptability to com-
ponents size and shapes, they cannot meet the requirement of
the nuclear structure which is huge and complex [3,4]. The con-
ventional fusion welding techniques to joining ODS steels may
result in considerable pores and agglomeration or coarsening of
the nano-scaled oxide particles, which seriously reduce the quality
and performance of the joint [5,6]. These problems are criti-
cal issues need to solve. MGH956 alloy [5], a kind of ODS steel
and under development in China, which chemical composition
(Fe–20Cr–5Al–0.5Ti–0.5Y2O3, wt%) and mechanical properties are

MA957 (Fe–14Cr–1Ti–0.3Mo–0.25 Y2O3, wt%) [9,10].
Power ultrasound in the metal solidiﬁcation process is the effec-
tive way to degas [11,12]. However, traditional power ultrasonic
vibration was transmitted by mechanical, which is difﬁcult to apply
in welding process. The vibration excited by power ultrasound was
loaded into welding wire and work piece [13], but the experimen-
tal system was complex and inefﬁcient. Arc ultrasonic technology
successfully solves these problems, in which the arc could play the
role of not only a thermal source but also an ultrasonic emitter [14].
So the arc-ultrasonic technology can be used to remove the gases
in ODS steel joint.
In this study, MGH956 alloy were butt-welded by arc-ultrasonic
TIG welding. And the pores distributed in weld and the mechanical
properties are studied with arc ultrasonic function.

The weld section observed under SEM is shown in Fig. 6. There
are many tiny pores which could not be observed by OM. Seen

be conﬁrmed that the particles must be a kind of oxide particles.
As the Ref. [5] reported, the formation of pores is related to the
coarsening of the nanoscale oxide particles during welding process.
Particle growth is a spontaneous process because larger particle
is more stable than smaller one. In fusion welding such as TIG,
the nanoscale oxide particles easily adsorbed gas, and then aggre-
gated, the gas absorbed on the surface of particles aggregated. Small
pores would be formed in the weld, if bubbles could not completely
outﬂow.

3.2. Mechanism of pore formation
The pores of MGH956 alloy in TIG welding are related to the
fabrication process of the alloy. The alloy powders generally are
mechanical alloyed in a high-energy planetary ball mill under a
pure argon atmosphere. As is well known that the argon is inac-
tive gas and low-diffusivity, which makes it difﬁcult to dissipate. In
addition, alloy powders’ surface is hygroscopic and absorbs mois-
ture from atmosphere. Therefore, the compactness of MGH956
alloy is less than the rolled or cast material, it contains a lot of
voids. The condition is quite adequate to form pores. During the TIG
welding, the voids will gather together easily due to metal ﬂow in
welding pool. The voids can provide a place for gas to get together,
and it will expand to form bubbles. Furthermore, oxide particles
are formed in welding pool, they are non-wettable by the melt and
adsorbed gas in surface [11]. As oxide particles grew up, the gas in
surface of particles also aggregated to form bubbles. If bubbles have
not enough time to outﬂow, pores will remain in joint.
3.3. Effect of arc-ultrasonic
3.3.1. The effect of excitation current
Fig. 8 shows the effect of different excitation current on pores
distribution in joints. It seems that excitation current has a great
inﬂuence on pores distribution. When the excitation current is
about 5 A and 10 A, pores grow up obviously at the upper part of
weld. When the excitation current is increased to 20 A or 30 A, the
number of the pores decreases sharply.
In welding process, the nucleus of bubbles occurred on the
surface of non-wettable oxide particles, they gathered and grew
up under arc-ultrasonic condition more easily than without arc-
ultrasonic. When excitation current is about 5 A or 10 A, the excited
ultrasonic intensity is very weak, bubbles gathered and grew up
slowly. It takes bubbles a long time to grow up enough to ﬂoat up.
Bubbles will be sealed inside the weld when the welding pool has
been solidiﬁed, because bubbles need much time to grow up and
ﬂoat outside. This is the reason why most of the pores exist at the
upper part of weld when excitation current is about 5 A or 10 A. If
excitation current is increased to 20 A and 30 A, the excited ultra-
sonic intensity becomes very strong. Bubbles gathered and grew up
more easily, they can ﬂoat outside of the weld before the welding
pool solidiﬁed.

3.4. The effect of arc-ultrasonic on tensile properties of joints
The tensile test of specimens was done at room temperature, the
fracture of the tensile tested samples occurred in the melted zone
and the results were shown in Table 3. It is found that the pores
can be decreased or eliminated to improve the mechanical proper-
ties of joints with applying arc-ultrasonic. Excitation current plays
an important role in improving tensile strength, and frequency has
a certain inﬂuence on it. When arc-ultrasonic is not applied, the
tensile strength of specimens is only 410 MPa; after arc-ultrasonic
was applied in welding process, the tensile strength of specimens is
higher than that without arc-ultrasonic. Tensile strength increases
signiﬁcantly with increasing of excitation current. When the exci-
tation current is increased to 20 A, the tensile strength reaches the
highest, about 550 MPa, which achieves 76% of the tensile strength
of base metal. Tensile strength also increases when frequency is
decreased.
Fig. 10 is SEM of the tensile fracture morphologies. It can
be observed in Fig. 10a that the fracture morphology presents
evidently brittle rupture and many pores. When the excitation
current is increased, the numbers of pores decreased signiﬁ-
cantly, the tensile strength of welded joint increased, but the
weld joint still presented brittle fracture as shown in Fig. 10b
and c.

4. Summary
The pores distribution and mechanical properties in MGH956
alloy TIG welding with or without arc-ultrasonic were studied. The
arc-ultrasonic has quite an effect on the pores distribution and
improving tensile strength. The main conclusions can be summa-
rized as follows:
(1)
 The tensile strength is only about 410 MPa when arc-ultrasonic
was not applied in welding process. Lots of pores remained in
joint is the main reason that tensile strength is weak.
(2) When the excitation current is increased, bubbles gathered and
grew up more easily. Until the excitation current reached 20 A
or 30 A, the pores decreased sharply, and the tensile strength
increased to about 550 MPa.
(3) The bubble size increased when the frequency decreased, which
is beneﬁt to bubbles ﬂoat up. The tensile strength also increased
to 521 MPa. But arc-ultrasonic has not signiﬁcant inﬂuence on
weld joints microhardness change.
