ABSTRACT 
Transformation induced plasticity in multi-principal element alloys involves several types of phase trans -
formation, among which the alloys exhibiting Face-centered cubic (FCC) to Body-centered cubic (BCC) phase 
transformation during deformation have a place. However, the current multi-principal element alloys with single 
FCC phase show relatively low room temperature tensile yield strength, thus usage of these alloys is limited for 
room temperature applications. We report a revised microstructural design by tuning the fractions of FCC and 
other harder phases via annealing according to equilibrium phase diagrams, which gives rise to both higher yield 
strength and tensile strength, and still good ductility at room temperature. The increase of the strength can be 
attributed to the contribution of the harder BCC and Sigma phase particles formed during annealing and the still 
operational FCC to BCC transformation induced plasticity effect. Hence, properly selected annealing treatment to 
produce a multi-phase microstructure is a way to exert both the transformation induced plasticity effect 
meanwhile the composite effect from both soft and hard phases. 
1. Introduction 
The transformation induced plasticity (TRIP) is an effective approach 
to improve the ductility of metastable austenite containing steels [ 1 – 3 ], 
which has also been introduced into multi-principal element alloys 
(MPEAs) [ 4 , 5 ] for ductility improvement in recent years. The types of 
TRIP effects in MPEAs involve Face-centered cubic (FCC) to hexagonal 
close packed (HCP) phase transformation [ 6 – 11 ], Body-centered cubic 
(BCC) to orthorhombic phase transformation [ 12 , 13 ], and BCC to HCP 
phase transformation [ 14 ]. All these different TRIP effects during plastic 
deformation have yielded intensive strain-hardening effect by dynamic 
strain-stress partitioning between the parent phase and freshly formed 
phase and promoted plastic deformation inside grains, which has 
effectively suppressed early cracking and eventually gave rise to 
enhanced combinations of strength and ductility.

TRIP phenomenon in MPEAs. The first TRIP MPEA with deformation 
induced FCC to BCC phase transformation has been reported by Bae 
et al. [ 15 ], who have developed a ferrous Fe

Almost all the MPEAs that present FCC to BCC TRIP effect have been 
annealed prior to tensile tests, with part of them are single FCC phase 
and others have FCC/BCC duplex structure, and the BCC phase in the 
initial microstructure usually form by martensitic transformation during 
cooling instead of isothermal transformation during annealing. It is also 
found that the yield strengths of these alloys are quite low when tested at 
room temperature (less than 600 MPa), although their tensile strength 
could be much higher due to the significant strain hardening behaviors. 
Therefore, further increasing the yield strength of the FCC to BCC TRIP 
MPEAs will be beneficial for those applications which requires both 
adequate yield strength and strain hardening ability. One way to 
improve the yield strength is to introduce a certain amount of hard 
phases which are stronger than the FCC phase. In MPEAs, the extent of 
lattice distortion in BCC phase is more severe than that in FCC [ 21 – 23 ], 
resulting the higher strength of BCC phase than FCC one [ 21 , 24 , 25 ]. It 
can be speculated that adding BCC phase to FCC solid solution could 
help to increase the yield strength, which could be realized by annealing 
rather than redesigning the alloy compositions.

presents FCC to BCC TRIP effect during room temperature deformation,
