ABSTRACT
This study investigates the effects of Nb gradient (5 – 15 wt%) on microstructure and properties of Fe-Al-Nb alloys. 
The addition of Nb element promoted eutectic evolution: lamellar/hypoeutectic (5 %) → quasi-network (10 %) → 
lamellar (15 %). With the increase in Nb content, the hardness and strength of the Fe-Al-Nb alloy gradually 
increase. The alloy ’ s plasticity first increased and then decreased, reaching the best plasticity when the Nb 
content was 5 wt%. The 10 wt% Nb alloy had best wear resistance (minimum wear rate under 5 – 12.5 N) due to 
its high strength/hardness and quasi-network structure. This work demonstrates Nb content critically balances 
mechanical and tribological properties through controlled eutectic architectures.
1. Introduction
Components such as blades and turbine disks in aero-engines are 
subjected to high temperatures, high pressures, and the scouring of high- 
speed gas flows [1,2] . Therefore, the materials used for these compo -
nents must possess extremely high strength and wear resistance. Fe-Al 
intermetallic compounds (such as Fe
3
Al and FeAl) exhibit great appli -
cation potential in the aerospace, energy, and chemical engineering 
fields due to their excellent specific strength, specific stiffness, and 
corrosion resistance. Compared with nickel-based alloys, they have a 
lower density (6.5 – 7.2 g/cm³) and do not contain strategic elements. 
Additionally, they show more outstanding corrosion resistance in 
high-temperature sulfur-containing environments [3 – 6] . However, 
taking Fe
3
Al with a body-centered cubic (BCC) structure as an example, 
its large Burgers vector results in a high dislocation strain energy, 
putting the dislocations in an unstable state [7] . When the superstructure 
dislocations decompose, the nucleation barrier of super-dislocations is 
high. As a consequence, single-dislocation slip is likely to occur at room 
temperature, and it is difficult to achieve multi-system slip for coordi -
nated deformation. Thus, the alloy exhibits significant toughness at 
room temperature. The limited room-temperature toughness of this 
material, characterized by a tensile elongation of merely 2 – 3 %, along 
with the significant reduction in strength and creep resistance above 
600
◦
C, substantially hinder its engineering applications as a 
high-temperature structural material [8 – 10] .
To improve the room-temperature toughness, low strength at high 
temperatures, and poor creep resistance of Fe-Al intermetallic com -
pounds, scholars have conducted extensive research. Methods such as 
grain refinement [11,12] and alloying to form Fe-Al-X (X = Ti, Cr, Ta, 
Nb, V) have yielded various research results with their own character -
istics [13 – 18] . Studies indicate that alloying elements play a crucial role 
in enhancing the mechanical and tribological properties of Fe-Al inter -
metallic compounds [19,20] . The Nb element has become a research 
hotspot due to its unique multi-scale synergistic strengthening effect. Its 
remarkable grain-refining effect can increase the grain-boundary den -
sity, providing more coordination paths for dislocation movement. By 
facilitating multi-system slip, Nb effectively enhances 
room-temperature toughness. This effect directly weakens the restric -
tion on dislocation movement caused by the large Burgers vector in the 
BCC structure and reduces the energy barrier for super - dislocation 
decomposition. At the same time, the difference in atomic radii between 
Nb atoms and Fe and Al atoms induces strong lattice distortion. By 
altering the distribution of electron clouds between atoms and the 
bonding state, it breaks the original crystal symmetry, thereby

solid-solution strengthening and Laves phase precipitation strength -

based alloys at temperatures below 973 K [22 – 24] . The excellent creep

However, the existing preparation techniques for Fe-Al-Nb alloys still 
have significant limitations. Traditional methods such as arc-furnace 
melting [32 – 34] , high frequency induction melting [22,27] , and levi -
tation induction melting [26,35] , not only demand high-purity raw 
materials and considerable energy consumption but, more importantly, 
have difficulty controlling the solidification process to achieve the 
desired eutectic structure. During the melting process, the Laves phase 
tends to precipitate directly from the melt as irregular eutectics, easily 
resulting in a coarse dendritic structure. This causes a severe uneven 
distribution of phases, preventing the full realization of its strengthening 
potential [16] . Moreover, the coarse phase is more likely to become 
crack initiation sources, significantly deteriorating the alloy ’ s toughness 
and fatigue life. In terms of eutectic morphology control, existing 
research faces multiple bottlenecks. The internal structure of the 
eutectic colony shows significant morphological differences between the 
center and the edge. Traditional processes struggle to achieve a uniform 
spatial distribution of eutectic colonies, often resulting in local accu -
mulation or sparsity. Meanwhile, eutectic structures commonly suffer 
from severe elemental segregation and a prominent problem of single 
crystal orientation. These structural inhomogeneities directly restrict 
the improvement of the mechanical and wear properties of Fe-Al-Nb 
eutectic materials. In contrast, Fe-Al-Nb alloys with eutectic structure 
characteristics that are optimized, characterized by nano-scale phase 
interfaces and strong interface-bonding properties, can theoretically 
reach a synergy of high strength and good plasticity through precise 
control of eutectic morphology. However, related research is still 
nascent, especially the structure-property relationship concerning me -
chanical properties and wear behavior, which necessitates thorough 
in-depth analysis.
In recent years, self-propagating high-temperature synthesis (SHS) 
technology has provided a new approach to regulating alloy micro -
structure due to its low energy consumption and flexible process ad -
vantages [36,37] . In previous studies, Fe-Al alloys were prepared using 
SHS technology, and Cr elements were added to achieve grain refine -
ment and alloying. By adding different Cr elements, the morphology of 
the precipitated phase was successfully changed, thereby improving the 
mechanical properties of the material [20,38] . Based on this, this study 
innovatively uses Fe, Fe

3.2. Mechanical property
The load-bearing performance of the alloy is closely related to the 
synergistic effect of the Fe
3
Al matrix phase and the Laves precipitate 
phase. As shown in Fig. 6 , with the Nb content increases from 0 to 15 wt 
%, the microhardness of the alloy exhibits a stepwise increase, with 
values of 354 HV, 421 HV, 504 HV, and 510 HV, respectively, demon -
strating a significant second-phase strengthening effect. The 0 wt% Nb 
alloy exhibits a typical dense single-phase Fe
3
Al structure with excellent 
microstructural uniformity, and the hardness test coefficient of variation 
is only 1.15 %, reflecting the good mechanical uniformity of the single- 
phase matrix. Upon the introduction of Nb, the Laves phase precipitates 
within the alloy system. The Laves phase has a higher hardness than 
Fe
3
Al. When the Nb content increases to 5 wt%, the uneven distribution 
of the Laves phase in the Laves phase/hypoeutectic structure causes the 
hardness to begin exhibiting spatial heterogeneity, with local hardness 
fluctuations expanding to 6.38 %. This phenomenon becomes more 
pronounced when the Nb content reaches 10 wt%, as the formation of 
"network holes" in the quasi-network eutectic structure intensifies the 
organizational heterogeneity, causing the hardness coefficient of vari -
ation to surge to 16.86 %. When the Nb content is further increased to 
15 wt%, the enrichment of Laves in the lamellar eutectic structure re -
duces the hardness dispersion to 5.54 %.

The strengthening mechanism originates from a multi-scale synergistic 
effect: firstly, Nb and Fe
3
Al produce a solid solution strengthening effect, 
causing lattice distortion [21] ; secondly, the in-situ precipitated Laves 
phase Nb enhances performance through a triple mechanism: (1) high 
intrinsic hardness produces a dislocation pinning effect, inhibiting 
dislocation slip/climb [49] ; (2) micron-scale Laves phase acts as a rigid 
obstacle, hindering plastic deformation through geometrically necessary 
dislocation multiplication [50,51] ;
In the 10 wt% Nb alloy, the combination of the lowest grain size and 
the quasi-network distribution of the Laves phase results in significant 
synergistic effects of "grain refinement strengthening" and "dispersion 
strengthening." This leads to a notable increase in hardness and yield 
strength compared to the alloy with 5 wt% Nb. However, when the Nb 
content exceeds 10 wt%, despite the continued increase in the volume 
fraction of the Laves phase, the hardness and yield strength do not 
improve significantly. Compared with the alloy with 10 wt% Nb, the 
alloy with 15 wt% Nb had the larger grain size. According to the Hall- 
Petch relationship, the strength of the alloy matrix containing 15 wt%

3.4. Wear mechanism of quasi-network structure
Fig. 16 illustrates the dynamic evolution process of wear in the quasi- 
network structure of the 10 wt% alloy. In the initial stage ( Fig. 16 (a)), 
the original surface undergoes frictional action, causing the soft Fe
3
Al 
phase to experience elastic deformation and plastic delamination, while 
the quasi-network Laves phase forms micro-protrusions ( Fig. 16 (b)). 
These micro-protrusions effectively bear the load, significantly reducing 
the friction coefficient from an initial μ = 0.54 – 0.85 to μ = 0.13 – 0.23. 
During the running-in stage ( Fig. 16 (c)), the low plasticity of Fe
3
Al 
causes some micro-protrusions to continue wearing, while others endure 
periodic shear loads. When the hard Laves phase is sheared, the friction 
coefficient and noise signal suddenly increase. At this point, the frag -
mented particulate Laves phase participates in local load-bearing 
through rolling and shallow surface grinding [43] . Notably, the strong 
metallurgical bonding interface formed between Fe
3
Al and the Laves 
phase effectively mitigates deep scratch damage caused by abrasive 
particles, while the residual quasi-network micro-protrusions continue 
to function in load transfer and debris retention. In the stable wear stage 
( Fig. 16 (d)), the material surface exhibits adhesive wear morphology 
and pits caused by Laves phase fragmentation, while the friction layer 
composed of Al
2
O
3
, Nb
2
O
5
, and Fe
2
O
3 
has good lubricating effects. At 
this stage, the roughness, oxide layer, grinding, and microstructure of 
the contact surface tend to stabilize, forming a dynamic equilibrium, and 
the friction coefficient eventually stabilizes.
The mechanism of the quasi-network eutectic structure ’ s impact on 
the tribological performance of the material can be summarized as fol -
lows: Firstly, in the early stages of wear, the quasi-network eutectic 
structure achieves friction reduction and wear resistance through the 
synergistic effect of hard and soft phases. The soft Fe
3
Al phase provides 
elastic support, alleviating contact pressure, while the hard Laves phase 
acts as the main load-bearing component, effectively resisting abrasive 
cutting. Together, they reduce surface contact stress, thereby decreasing 
the friction coefficient. Secondly, this structure possesses excellent stress 
homogenization capabilities, significantly alleviating localized shear 
stress concentration and preventing material spalling due to stress 
overload. Simultaneously, its unique network-like porosity can capture 
and store wear debris generated during the wear process, gradually 
forming a self-repairing protective layer on the surface, further slowing 
down the wear process. Thirdly, the particulate Laves phase distributed 
in the quasi-network structure can trigger a "sliding-rolling" friction 
mode transition: during the friction process, the Laves phase particles 
absorb energy through rolling deformation, transforming traditional 
sliding friction into mixed friction, thereby effectively suppressing the 
plowing effect and reducing the degree of abrasive wear on the material.
