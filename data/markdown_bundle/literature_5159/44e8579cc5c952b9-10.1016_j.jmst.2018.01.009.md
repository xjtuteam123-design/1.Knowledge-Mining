Abstract 
A new ly developed severe plastic deformation (SPD) technique, i.e. repetitive 
upsetting (RU), is employed to improve the strength and ductility of a Mg -Gd-Y-Zr 
alloy. During the RU processing, dynamic recrystallization occurs in the Mg alloy, 
which leads to a significant grain refinement from 11.2 μm to 2.8 μm. The yield 
strength (YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and elongation increase simultaneously

with i ncreasing RU passes. The microstructur al evolution is affected by processing 
temperatures. Dynamic recrystallization prevails at low temperatures, while dynamic 
recovery is the main effect factor at high temperature s. Texture character istics 
gradually beco me random during multiple passes of RU processing, which reduces 
the tension-compression asymmetry of the Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloy.

1. Introduction 
Magnesium (Mg) alloys garnered much attention from automotive, aerospace, 
and aircraft industries due to their low density and high specific strength [1-3]. 
However, the hexagonal close packed (hcp) Mg alloys usually exhibit low ductility 
and poor formability, which limits their applications in load-bearing components [4-7]. 
Recent years have witnessed the extensive progress towards the development of new 
alloy compositions and novel plastic forming technologies to enhance the strength and 
ductility of Mg alloys for lightweight applications. Mg -Al-Zn (AZ) [8-12] and 
Mg-Zn-Zr (ZK) [13, 14] are two typical wrought Mg alloy systems which have high 
toughness and high strength, respectively. Y uan et al. [15] reported that AZ91 alloy 
can achieve UTS as high as 408 MPa while retaining a decent ductility of 15.1% by 
multi-passes of ECAP and subsequent aging treatment. Orlov et al. [16] proposed an 
integrated process that combined conventional extrusion and E CAP into one single 
process and successfully produced a fine -grained microstructure with favorable 
texture that promoted basal slip s. ZK60 alloy processed by this method exhibited a

yield stress of 310 MPa and total elongation of 17.1%, which reaches a goo d balance 
between strength and tensile ductility. The main strengthening mechanism of wrought 
Mg alloys is grain refinement, which introduces high density of grain boundaries to 
block dislocation slips [17-19]. However, grain boundary of wrought Mg alloys is not 
stable at elevated temperature s due to grain growth, i.e. the aforementioned alloys 
having poor thermal stability regarding their mechanical performance [20, 21] . In 
order to enhance strength and thermal stability simultaneously, rare earth (RE) 
alloying is employed to develop novel Mg alloys. Since the solubility of REs in Mg 
matrix decreases with decreasing temperature, Mg -RE alloys can be significantly 
hardened by precipitations. It has been reported that the precipitation process in 
Mg-Gd-Y alloy follows the sequence of S.S.S.S (Supersaturated Solid 
solution)→″→T→′→1→ [22]. Precipitates, especially peak aged ′ phase s, 
enhance mechanical strength by blocking dislocation slips [23]. In addition, RE atoms 
tend to segregate at interfaces such as grain boundaries [24, 25], twins boundaries [26, 
27], and stacking faults [28]. Such unique segregation has been believed to be 
effective in pinning dislocations, which leads to further enhancement of mechanic al 
properties and thermal stability [29]. 
However, on the other hand, elongation of peak aged Mg -RE alloys is usually 
less than 5%, which indicates that the strength enhancement by precipitation 
hardening is at the expense of ductility [30-35]. A promising solution to this issue is to 
severely deform Mg -RE alloys before aging treatment, which can well refine Mg 
matrix to improve ductility. Meanwhile, grain refinement also contributes to enhanced

strength. For example, Lu et al. [36] used ECAP t o produce high strength 
Mg-Gd-Zn-Zr alloy with UTS of ~387 MPa and outstanding elongation of 23%. 
Drawbacks of SPD are associated with the difficulty in scaling up the sample size, 
which inhibits its industrial application. Numerical simulation and microhardness tests 
demonstrated that homogeneity of ECAP samples is directly related to sample radius. 
Scaling up of ECAP will cause the deterioration of microstructure homogeneity [37]. 
To solve this problem, a novel SPD technique , i.e. repetitive upsetting (RU ), that 
allows processing samples with size up to the order of 100 mm × 100 mm × 20 mm, is 
developed. It is capable to produce a homogenous microstructure across the sample, 
which is a grand challenge to many other scaling up SPD techniques [38-40]. A 
biodegradable Mg alloy NZ30K was processed by RU processing, which exhibits 
uniform elongation from 8% to 30% [41]. In this paper, a systematic study on the 
deformation pathway of RU and its role in microstructure evolution and 
corresponding mechanical performance of Mg -Gd-Y-Zr alloy is carried out. 
Particularly, texture evolution and its effect on tension -compression asymmetry are 
investigated. 
2. Experimental procedures 
The actual composition of the studied alloy is Mg -9.8Gd-2.7Y-0.4Zr (in wt.%), 
which was determined by an inductively coupled plasma atomic emission 
spectroscopy (ICP-AES) analyzer (PerkinElmer, Plasma 400). An as-cast ingot with a 
diameter of 180 mm was solution treated at 500 °C for 12 h and quenched to room 
temperature in water (T4 treatment). T4 -treated samples were hot extruded

extrusion speed of 5 mm/s. The principle of RU processing is shown schematically in

3. Results and discussion 
3.1 Microstructure analysis 
Due to poor formability of as -cast Mg -Gd-Y-Zr alloy, pre -deformation of 
extrusion was carried out at 400 °C before RU processing. As shown in Fig. 2 (a), 
initial grain size of as -cast Mg -Gd-Y-Zr alloy is ~139.2 μm. After hot extrusion, 
grains are generally equiaxed and significantly refined to ~11.2μm all over the sample. 
A few second phases can be observed in Mg matrix and grain boundaries co -existing 
with some Zr -rich cores as shown in Fig. 2 (b). The observed straight lamellar 
structure in grains probably corresponds to deformation twins and lamellar grain 
boundaries [42, 43]. 
Fig. 3(a-d) show overview of microstructur al evolutions with the increase in RU 
processing passes from 1 to 4 at 350 °C. Clearly, strain accumulation by severe plastic 
deformation give rise to continuous grain refinement in the matrix. To be specific, the 
average grain size follows a descending sequence of 14.3 μm, 6.9 μm, 4.0 μm and 2.8 
μm with increasing passes from 1 to 4. Moreover, the eventual microstructure with 
sufficient refinement at 4 passes is uniformly distributed across the sample, as shown 
in Fig. 3(d). Detailed microstructure in samples after 1 and 4 passes was exami ned by 
TEM in Fig. 3(e) and (f), respectively. The bright-field micrograph after 1 pass shows 
clear contrast of dislocation walls and tangles in the grain, which is probably the 
initial stage of grain refinement by formation of sub -grains. In contrast, gra ins are 
almost clean after 4 passes without remarkable dislocation contrast. Significant

dynamic recrystallization is believed to occur at energetic grain boundaries of the 
initial coarse grain s, where dislocations are accumulated during previous passes of 
deformation. Such recrystallization contributes to the observed sufficient grain 
refinement and transformation of low -angle grain boundary (LAGB) to high -angle 
grain boundary (HAGB) as well [44]. It is interesting that some recrystallized grains

showed severe cracking at very beginning of deformation. It seems that the critical 
recrystallized temperature is between 300 °C and 350 °C. To investigate grain 
refinement of RU processing, the temperatures used in this work are all higher than 
the critical recrystallized temperature. 
Deformation temperature plays an important role in the formation of fine-grained

microstructure of Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloy processed by four passes of RU at 350 °C, 
400 °C and 450 °C, respectively. Grain size of the sample processed at 350 °C is very

3.2 Grain boundary characteristics 
Grain boundary structure is also an important microstructure character. It is clear 
that dynamic recrystallization is achieved by continuous dislocations movement [44]. 
Dislocation multiplication, slipping and piling up occur at the early stage of RU 
deformation, leading to dislocation accumulation and tangles that promote grain 
sub-division. Misorientation across sub -grain boundary is typically lower than 15° 
and is defin ed as low -angle grain boundaries (LAGB). With increasing deformation, 
dislocations are absorbed by sub -grain boundaries, which helps transform LAGB t o 
HAGB of recrystallized grains. In this sense, the percentage of grain boundary angles 
is a good indicator for recrystallization status. 
Grain boundary angles of Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloy processed by various passes and 
temperatures were statistically investigated by EBSD. Fig. 5 shows EBSD mapping of 
grain boundary angles in samples after 1 -4 passes at 350 °C. In the maps, red, green 
and blue lines represent three groups of grain boundaries with misorientation angles 
of 2°-5°, 5°-15°, and larger than 15°, respectively. The boundaries with the angles less

than 2° are excluded as measurement error s. The percenta ge of such three groups of 
grain boundaries is listed in Table 1. It is found that the sample processed by 1-pass is 
dominated by low-angle boundaries, which has a percentage of 67%. This high LAGB 
percentage indicates an insufficient recrystallization after the first pass. Generally as a 
trend, LAGB become less dominated with increasing RU passes, as indicated in Ta ble. 
1 and Fig. 5. Eventually, in the sample after 4 passes, most the grain boundaries have 
been transformed to HAGB during the recrystallization process. Remaining LAGB 
percentage is significantly reduced to 12.8%. This observation agrees with the 
previous rationale in discussion of recrystallization process. As shown in Fig. 3(a) and 
b, grain refinement occurred at grain boundary first, then spread to the whole grain. 
According to the mechanism of Frank -Read source, dislocation is generated inside of 
grains, then slips and piles up at grain boundaries [46]. Thus, the density of 
dislocation at grain boundary is usually higher than that of the interior of grains at the 
beginning of deformation. LAGB in Mg -Gd-Y alloy is formed by dislocations p iling 
up. Its density near grain boundaries shown in Fig. 5 (a) is much higher than inside of 
grains. As shown in Fig. 5 (d), the grain refinement is sufficient , which exhibits 
uniform equiaxed grains with the grain size of less than 1 μm. Comparing to coars e 
grains, it is more difficult to activate dislocation emission and motion in ultrafine 
grains. Owing to the lack of essential dislocations, the density of LAGB in the Mg 
alloy processed by 4 passes of RU is low.

It is well known that dynamic recovery can be accelerated with increasing 
deformation temperature. Dislocation recovery in the sample processed at 400 °C is

temperature increases to 450 °C, the speed of dynamic recovery ris e to a very high 
level which makes dislocations difficult to accumulate in the Mg alloy. The grain 
refining effect is inhibited significantly in the sample processed at 450 °C. Thus, it is 
not recommended to carry out RU processing above 450 °C to produce Mg -Gd-Y-Zr 
alloy.

3.4 Mechanical properties 
Fig. 10 (a) shows engineering strain -stress curves of as -cast, as -extruded, and 
1-pass and 4 -passes RU samples tested at room temperature. Dog-bone shape tensile 
samples were cut from middle of the as -processed chunks, as shown in Fig. 10(a). YS 
and UTS of as -cast Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloy are 100 MPa and 230 MPa, and elongation is 
only 5.5%. After hot extrusion, UTS and elongation increase to 270 MPa and 6.2% , 
respectively. As discussed in prior sessions, grain refinement is the major reason f or 
this simultaneous improvement. YS, UTS and elongation of Mg -Gd-Y-Zr alloy 
processed by 1 -pass RU is further enhanced to 240 MPa, 300 MPa and ~8.5%, 
respectively. Moreover, the sample produced by 4 passes of RU shows YS, UTS and

As temperature increases from 350 °C to 400 °C and 450 °C, yield strength gradually

elongation exhibits a significant increas e from 12% to 18.5 % and 24%. The massive

increased more than four times after RU-4passes at 450 °C. Therefore, RU processing 
is an effective approach to improve the elongation of Mg -RE alloy s before aging

4. Conclusions 
1) Grain size of Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloy is significantly refined with increasing RU passes. 
After four passes of RU processing at 350 °C, grain size is reduced from 11.2μm 
to 2.8 μm. The density of low angle grain boundary in the samples generally 
decreases with increasing RU passes due t o the enhancing recrystallization 
process. As RU pass increases, yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and 
elongation of Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloy are improved simultaneously from 100 MPa, 230

MPa and 5.5% to 250 MPa, 350 MPa and 11.5%, respectively. 
2) Grain refinement is weakened with increasing processing temperature. Density of 
low angle grain boundary in RU -4passes sample processed at 400 °C is higher 
than the one processed at 350 °C, indicating that dynamic recrystallization is 
preferred at low temperature. Density of low angle grain boundary in the sample 
processed at 450 °C is lower than the one processed at 400 °C, which is resulted 
from the dynamic recovery effect at high temperature. YS and UTS of the sample 
processed at 350 °C are higher than the ones pr ocessed at 400 °C and 450 °C, 
while elongation of the sample processed at 450 °C is the highest (＞20%). 
3) Texture intensity of Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloy is affected more by processing passes than 
temperature. After 4 passes of RU at 350 °C, the maximum intensity of t exture is 
reduced from 7.6 to 2.0. Tensile -compression asymmetry of Mg -Gd-Y-Zr alloy is 
almost eliminated based on the observed similar tensile and compression yield 
strengths.
