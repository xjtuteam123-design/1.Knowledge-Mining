ABSTRACT 
This short communication presents a high-throughput experimental approach for screening multicomponent 
alloy compositions with optimal mechanical properties using two-step diffusion multiple, and its successful 
application in Al – Co – Cr – Fe – Ni – Ti high-entropy superalloys. The relation “ composition-microstructure-micro -
hardness ” is then established, from which the compositions with optimal microhardness/tensile properties are 
efficiently screened out and finally validated. 
As a novel class of alloys with great application prospects, high- 
entropy alloys (HEAs) have gathered increasing attention because of 
their wide composition space, complex microstructure and potential for 
diverse performance combinations [ 1 – 7 ]. Among these, high-entropy 
superalloy (HESA), which resembles the microstructure of conven -
tional superalloy and incorporates the four core effects of HEAs, is 
considered to be a candidate material for replacing nickel-based su -
peralloys [ 8 ]. Such novel HESAs own not only low density and cost but 
also good high-temperature properties [ 9 – 18 ]. However, due to the di -
versity and complexity of the components, it presents a challenge to 
simultaneously control the γ’ precipitates while avoiding the formation 
of detrimental phases. With the traditional “ trial-and-error ” method it is 
a time-consuming and labor-intensive process to obtain the optimal 
composition/heat treatment mechanisms over such a wide composition 
space. Thus, there is an urgent need to remedy the situation.

is no specific thermodynamic database for HESAs currently. Under this 
circumvent, the optimized composition of Al – Co – Cr – Fe – Ni – Ti HESAs 
based on the thermodynamic database of either Ni-based superalloys or 
classic HEAs might not be the optimal one. 
Alternatively, high-throughput experimental techniques should be a 
pragmatic choice. The dual-anneal diffusion couple/multiple tech -
niques, originally developed by Cao and Zhao [ 23 , 24 ] for efficient study 
on phase diagrams and phase transformation, has been widely used to 
conduct high-throughput design of composition and/or heat treatment 
mechanisms in different alloys [ 25 – 30 ]. With the first annealing of the 
target diffusion couple/multiple at a higher temperature (i.e., the solu -
tion temperature), a wide range of concerned composition space can be 
efficiently created through the interdiffusion process. After that, a sec -
ond annealing of the target diffusion couple/multiple at a lower tem -
perature (i.e., the aging temperature) is conducted to allow the 
microstructural evolution, e.g., the formation of precipitates.

Accordingly, a large dataset of microstructure information as a function 
of composition can be extensively collected through a single dual-anneal 
diffusion multiple. By further combining with e.g., the nanoindentation 
technique, the “ composition-microstructure-property ” relation of the 
concerned alloy system is readily obtained. Moreover, as already illus -
trated in the reports [ 31 , 32 ], the interdiffusion at a lower temperature 
during the second annealing has a negligible effect on the composition 
gradient formed during the first annealing. 
Consequently, the major objectives of this paper are: ( i ) to conduct a 
high-throughput determination of the relation “ composition-micro -
structure-microhardness ” of Al – Co – Cr – Fe – Ni – Ti HESAs by combining a 
pragmatic two-step sandwich diffusion multiple technique and nano -
indentation, and ( ii ) to efficiently screen the HESA composition(s) with 
optimal mechanical properties, followed by key experimental valida -
tion. The general framework of this paper is schematically shown in 
Fig. 1 .

annealed at 1473 ± 2 K for 10 h in a high-temperature furnace 
(GSL1700X, Hefei Kejing Materials Technology Co., Ltd., Hefei, China),

at 1273 ± 2 K for 3 h, furnace cooling at a speed of 10 K/min to reach 
1073 ± 2 K, and aging at 1073 ± 2 K for 20 h) was also applied to the 
chosen alloy samples. The tensile tests were performed using the 
servo-hydraulic dynamic testing system (Instron 8802, USA) at room 
temperature. Plate-type tensile specimens with a gauge length of 66 mm 
and a cross-section of 2 mm × 1 mm ( Fig. S1 in supplementary file) were 
prepared using wire-electrode cutting. For each alloy, at least three tests 
were performed at a loading speed of 2 mm/min. 
The interdiffusion microstructure of the present Al – Co – Cr – Fe – Ni – Ti 
HESAs diffusion multiple over the entire diffusion path after aging 
treatment due to SEM and TEM characterization is displayed in Fig. 2 . As 
can be seen in the figure, typical γ + γ ′ dual-phase microstructures are 
characterized along the entire diffusion path. Three distinct represen -
tative types of γ + γ ′ microstructures are observed: Type I - γ ′ precipitates 
with a bimodal particle size distribution. The larger γ ′ precipitates 
exhibit nearly cuboidal morphology with a size of approximately 1 μ m, 
while the smaller γ ′ precipitates assume a spherical form with a size of 
around 200 nm. Type II - γ ′ precipitates with a monomodal particle size 
distribution. All γ ′ precipitates in this type are nearly spherical and 
possess a size of approximately 200 nm. Type III - γ ′ precipitates with a 
bimodal particle size distribution. In this type, both larger and smaller γ ′ 
precipitates adopt a spherical morphology. The larger γ ′ precipitates 
measure around 200 nm, whereas the smaller γ’ precipitates are 
approximately 10 nm in size. The formation of monomodal and bimodal 
microstructures is controlled by the competition between the phase

and Ti content, the volume fraction and particle size of the precipitates 
increase, leading to an enhancement in hardness. However, when 
certain precipitate particles exceed 1 μ m, it results in a reduction in 
hardness.

microstructures of γ ′ precipitates in all three alloys behave a monomodal

In summary, a novel two-step sandwich diffusion multiple technique 
successfully screens the alloy composition with optimal mechanical 
properties for Al – Co – Cr – Fe – Ni – Ti HESAs. With the prepared diffusion 
multiple, the relation “ composition-microstructure-microhardness ” of 
the present HESAs over a wide composition range is first established 
efficiently. Three alloy compositions with optimal microhardness values 
are directly extracted, and the corresponding tensile properties are 
finally determined.
