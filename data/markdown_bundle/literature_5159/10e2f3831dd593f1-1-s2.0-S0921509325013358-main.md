ABSTRACT
Due to their excellent mechanical performance at elevated temperatures, TiNbVTa-based alloys have emerged as 
a focal point of research within the field of refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs). However, the intrinsic trade- 
off between strength and ductility in TiNbVTa alloys continues to limit their further development for ambient- 
temperature applications. In this study, a novel microalloying strategy is proposed by introducing chromium (Cr) 
to promote the formation of substitutional solid solutions, thereby enhancing the room-temperature mechanical 
properties of TiNbVTa-based RHEAs. To validate this approach, a series of (TiNbVTa)
100-x
Cr
x 
(x = 0, 0.35, and 
0.7 at.%) RHEAs was systematically designed and synthesized. The results revealed that all the investigated 
alloys possessed a single body-centred cubic (BCC) phase structure. As the Cr content increased from 0 to 0.7 at. 
%, the ultimate tensile strength, yield strength, and elongation of the (TiNbVTa)
100-x
Cr
x 
alloys initially increased, 
followed by a subsequent decline. Notably, the (TiNbVTa)
99.65
Cr
0.35

ature mechanical performance, achieving a tensile yield strength of 903 ± 22 MPa and an elongation of 18.7 ±
2.6 %, both of which surpass previously reported values for TiNbVTa-based RHEAs. The enhancement in me -
chanical properties induced by Cr microalloying was ascribed to the synergistic contributions of the synergistic 
hindrance of dislocation motion by SROs and the lattice distortion field induced by Cr addition, impeded 
dislocation motion through the formation of dislocation rings, and the promotion of dislocation proliferation. 
Furthermore, this study introduces microalloying as an effective strategy for concurrently improving both the 
strength and ductility of RHEAs.

between microstructure and mechanical performance necessitates a 
careful selection of alloying elements to control microstructure evolu -
tion during the design process. In this work, microstructural strength -

TiNbVTa system, promoting the formation of substitutional solid solu -
tions. However, the incorporation of Cr, characterised by a compara -
tively high mixing enthalpy with the constituent elements, may 
potentially destabilise the BCC phase. Therefore, multiple empirical

4.2. Origin of strength and ductility enhancement in the Cr0.35 alloy
Tensile and compression testing revealed that the addition of 0.35 at. 
% Cr simultaneously enhances the strength and ductility of the 
(TiNbVTa)
100-x

mechanism, detailed TEM characterisation was conducted on the Cr0.35 
alloy subjected to a 10 % compressive plastic strain. Fig. 9 (a – b) show 
that the tangled dislocations and dislocation rings are uniformly 
distributed throughout the deformation region. This microstructural 
feature is primarily associated with the characteristic slip behavior of 
< 111 > screw dislocations in the BCC lattice. Due to the non-close- 
packed nature of the BCC structure, dislocations can glide on multiple 
planes within the < 111 > zone, including {110} and {112} [ 56 ]. When 
dislocation motion is impeded on an initial slip plane (e.g., (110)), the 
system accommodates deformation by activating a lower-energy slip 
plane via double cross-slip, thereby sustaining plastic flow [ 57 ]. In this 
process, the screw dislocation transitions to the newly activated slip 
plane while retaining the same glide direction as on the original plane, 
thereby bypassing the obstacle [ 57 ]. The impeded dislocations may 
become pinned, forming dislocation dipoles that can subsequently 
evolve into dislocation rings. These dislocation rings not only impede 
the motion of subsequent dislocations but also act as secondary dislo -
cation sources (analogous to Frank-Read sources), thereby markedly 
accelerating dislocation multiplication [ 58 , 59 ]. In TiNbVTa RHEAs, 
dislocation motion is generally governed by planar glide and single 
cross-slip mechanisms [ 60 , 61 ]. In contrast, TEM observations of the 
(TiNbVTa)
99.65

accommodated by double cross-slip, accompanied by the formation of 
numerous dislocation rings within regions of tangled dislocations. This 
phenomenon suggests that Cr addition lowers the barrier for dislocation 
multiplication [ 62 ]. The observed dislocation behavior further confirms 
that trace Cr incorporation exerts a pronounced influence on dislocation 
dynamics in the (TiNbVTa)
100-x
Cr
