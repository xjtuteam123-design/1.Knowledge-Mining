ABSTRACT
As aerospace and marine engineering advance, alloys face increasingly stringent performance demands, 
particularly in corrosive environments. As a rule, introducing precipitates enhances alloy strength but often 
compromises corrosion resistance. This study achieved a synergistic improvement in both properties by con -
trolling σ -phase precipitation through annealing temperature adjustments. 500 ℃ annealing enhanced me -
chanical strength via precipitation and dislocation strengthening, while optimizing beneficial oxides in the 
passive film improved corrosion resistance. Nevertheless, excessive annealing promoted large-scale precipitates, 
inducing microscopic galvanic corrosion and degrading overall performance. These findings offer insights into 
the composition design and industrial application of precipitation-strengthened high-entropy alloys (HEAs).
1. Introduction
Steel, being one of the most extensively utilized metallic materials 
worldwide, serves as a fundamental component in critical infrastruc -
ture, including bridges and buildings [ 1 ]. Worryingly, the escalating 
severity of corrosion, especially localized deterioration induced by 
corrosive ions (e.g., Cl

leading to catastrophic failures [ 2 , 3 ]. In demanding applications such as 
offshore engineering, the shipbuilding industry, and the nuclear sector, 
conventional steels often fail to achieve the desired balance between 
high strength and superior corrosion resistance [ 4 ].
By moving beyond the traditional alloy design concept centered on 
mixing enthalpy, five or more metallic elements are alloyed in equi -
molar or near-equimolar ratios, leading to the formation of a single- 
phase disordered solid solution with body-centered cubic (BCC), face- 
centered cubic (FCC), or hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structures [ 5 ]. 
Due to the dominant role of configurational entropy in stabilizing its 
solid solution structure, this class of materials is termed high-entropy 
alloys (HEAs). HEAs exhibit distinctive characteristics, including the 
“ high entropy effect ” in thermodynamics [ 6 ], the “ hysteresis diffusion 
effect ” in kinetics [ 7 ], the “ lattice distortion effect ” in microstructure 
[ 8 ], and the “ cocktail effect ” in performance [ 9 ], which collectively 
endow them with exceptional wear resistance, corrosion resistance, and 
radiation tolerance [ 10 – 13 ]. With ongoing research, the discovery of 
phenomena such as local chemical short-range ordering [ 14 ] and ul -
trahigh interstitial solubility [ 15 ] further highlights the profound 
theoretical significance and promising application potential of HEAs.
It is well established that Hall-Petch strengthening, strain hardening, 
precipitation strengthening, and solid solution strengthening are 
commonly employed to enhance the mechanical strength of materials. 
Among these, precipitation strengthening is particularly promising for 
improving the strength of HEAs, as it effectively hinders dislocation 
motion. However, it inevitably led to a loss of plasticity, presenting the 
classic “ strength-ductility ” trade-off dilemma [ 16 ]. Recent advances 
have enabled precise control over precipitate size, distribution, and 
volume fraction, achieving substantial strength improvements with 
minimal or no loss of ductility. For instance, Li et al. [ 17 ] developed a

Beyond mechanical trade-offs, the lifespan and reliability of mate -
rials under actual service conditions are equally crucial. Corrosion 
resistance, therefore, serves as a key indicator of a material ’ s overall 
performance [ 20 ]. Balancing mechanical properties with corrosion 
resistance remains a major challenge in the engineering applications of 
HEAs [ 21 ]. Typically, compositional disparities between precipitates 
and the matrix create potential differences, promoting galvanic corro -
sion [ 22 ]. This process compromises the uniformity and integrity of the 
passive film, ultimately degrading corrosion resistance. Studies have 
demonstrated that modifying alloy composition, such as increasing Cr 
and Nb concentrations, can facilitate the precipitation of corrosion- 
resistant phases while suppressing deleterious ones, thereby enhancing 
overall corrosion resistance [ 23 ]. For instance, Zhang et al. [ 24 ] incor -
porated a Nb-rich, highly corrosion-resistant Z-phase into duplex steel, 
strategically controlling precipitation sites to encapsulate inclusions, 
thereby significantly enhancing the material ’ s electrochemical poten -
tial. Likewise, Cui et al. [ 25 ] optimized the size and spatial distribution 
of the Laves phase in the FeCrVTi

inherent limitations, potentially compromising other properties or 
complicating alloy fabrication. Consequently, instead of directly altering 
the chemical composition, precisely controlling the morphology, size, 
and spatial distribution of precipitates through heat treatment offers a 
more effective and practical approach. Annealing regulates nucleation 
kinetics and growth dynamics, refines precipitate distribution within the 
matrix, and simultaneously enhance both corrosion resistance and me -

The FeCoNiCr-based HEA is among the most extensively studied 
systems. The addition of elements such as Al, Mo, and Ti in equal or 
near-equal atomic ratios significantly improves both mechanical prop -
erties and corrosion resistance [ 29 – 31 ]. Numerous studies indicate that 
the appropriate addition of Mo enhances the mechanical properties of 
FeCoNiCr-based HEAs through solid-solution strengthening while also 
conferring corrosion resistance [ 32 , 33 ]. For instance, Liu et al. [ 34 ] 
conducted a comparative study on the corrosion behavior of FeCoNiCr- 
based HEAs containing Mo, Al, and Ti in equal atomic ratios. The 
findings revealed that Mo addition positively influences the passive film 
structure, reducing the immersion corrosion rate by an order of 
magnitude compared to other alloys. Equally, Chen et al. [ 35 ] optimized

Additive manufacturing (AM), distinguished by its ultrafast cooling 
rate and rapid solidification, presents new possibilities for tailoring the 
microstructure of HEAs. Compared to conventional casting and powder 
metallurgy, AM fosters the formation of fine equiaxed grains due to its 
high cooling rate, significantly improving the mechanical properties of 
HEAs [ 39 ]. Additionally, the rapid solidification inherent to AM sup -
presses the development of thermodynamically stable phases, leading to 
a supersaturated solid solution and expanding the potential for precip -
itate tuning during post-processing heat treatments. Furthermore, the 
non-equilibrium solidification characteristics of AM introduces a 
broader range of nucleation sites, providing greater flexibility in opti -
mizing overall alloy performance. The technology ’ s design adaptability 
further enables the fabrication of intricate structural components, 
extending the application potential of HEAs in aerospace, nuclear en -
ergy, and marine engineering. In summary, AM not only enhances 
microstructural control compared to traditional manufacturing tech -
niques but also facilitates the integrated design of structure and function 
[ 40 ]. This presents novel strategies for simultaneously optimizing the 
corrosion resistance and mechanical performance of HEAs.
Herein, leveraging additive manufacturing (AM) technology with its 
high cooling rates, substantial temperature gradients, and fine-grained 
structures, a supersaturated single-phase solid solution of FeCo -

0.5

size, dimensions, and positioning of precipitates were precisely 
controlled. The study probed the synergistic effects of the σ phase on the 
alloy ’ s mechanical and corrosion properties, utilizing a combination of 
classical electrochemical methods and multi-scale characterization 
techniques.

5. Conclusions
This research has centered on the fabrication of the FeCoCrNiMo
0.5 
HEA using AM techniques, meticulously tuning annealing temperatures 
to manipulate precipitate dimensions and structures. Through a rigorous 
assessment of the mechanical properties and corrosion resistance of 
alloy series, several pivotal conclusions emerged: 
(1) By harnessing annealing to introduce precipitates in supersatu -
rated HEAs, the balance between strength and ductility was 
termed exquisite. Specifically, when annealed at 500 
◦
C, the alloy 
achieved a remarkable UTS of ~ 1.2 GPa, complemented by EL 
exceeding 30 %. This favorable balance was largely ascribed to 
the combined effects of precipitation strengthening, strain hard -
ening, and HDI back stress strengthening. Meanwhile, the speci -
mens under this condition exhibited the best corrosion resistance 
among the series, indicating strong industrial viability.
(2) It was demonstrated that the moderate introduction of precipi -
tated phases at a certain scale could realize the synergistic effect 
of corrosion resistance and comprehensive mechanical proper -
ties. Among them, corrosion resistance initially improved and 
then decreased with escalating annealing temperatures. The 
enhancement of corrosion resistance under low-temperature 
annealing was mainly attributed to the fact that the increasing 
proportion of protective components such as Cr
2
O
3
, MoO
3, 
and 
MoO

in the passive film outweighs the deleterious micro- 
galvanic effects of small-scale precipitates. In addition, the very 
high E

and E

gave the passive film a certain self-healing 
ability.
(3) The passive films of the specimens demonstrated a gradient 
arrangement in composition with the thickness change. The outer 
passive film had a high proportion of hydroxides and oxides in a 
high valence state. Oxides with the increase of the etching depth 
gradually dominated the composition of the passive film. In 
addition, the moderate precipitation of the precipitated phase 
allowed enable the deprotonation within the FCC phase in the 
HEA more significantly, strengthening the corrosion resistance of 
the passive film in high concentration erosive solutions.
