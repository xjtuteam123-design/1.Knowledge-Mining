NATURE OF HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH OF DEFORMED
MAGNESIUM ALLOY OF THE Mg – Zn – Zr – REE SYSTEM
E. F. Volkova, 1 I. V . Mostyaev,1 and M. V . Anikina 1
Translated from Metallovedenie i T ermicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 4, pp. 21 – 27, April, 2021.
Results of a study of the structure, phase composition, and main parameters of high-temperature strength of
forgings from deformed magnesium alloy VMD16 of the Mg – Zn – Zr – REE system in aged condition are
presented. The distinctive features of alloy VMD16 responsible for the thermal stability and high-temperature
strength of the alloy are determined, which make it superior to the commercial refractory alloy MA12. It is
shown that thermally stable LPSO-phases are present in alloy VMD16 in the form of nanoplates in the bulk of
grains and in the form of nanoblocks in multilayer fragments of the eutectic.
Key words: structure, phase composition, parameters of high-temperature strength, deformable
magnesium alloy, LPSO-phases, nanoblocks in multilayer eutectic fragments, aged condition.
INTRODUCTION
Advancement of the aerospace sector of the recent mate-
rials science requires preservation and improvement of me -
tallic-matrix light alloys despite the attractiveness of the use
of novel composite polymer materials. The lightest of the
known structural metallic materials are magnesium alloys.
From the standpoint of economy, application of magne -
sium alloys in parts of the aerospace, aviation, and automo -
tive articles is quite expedient and gives a considerable gain
in the weight, which implies fuel saving.
In some cases, these alloys serve under the conditions of
elevated temperatures (150 – 250°C).
Thus, there is a need for development of new magne -
sium-base refractory alloys with a high and steady level of
properties at elevated temperatures and good strength cha -
racteristics ( /c115
r /c179320 MPa, /c1150.2 /c179250 MPa) at normal tem -
perature.
The presence of hexagonal close-packed (hcp) lattice
with parameters c = 5.199 Å, a = 3.202 Å at a c/c47a ratio of
1.623 is a natural feature of magnesium alloys (containing no
lithium) and distinguishes them from aluminum-base alloys
[1]. The problem is that hexagonal metals and alloys are lia -
ble to manifest lower refractoriness than metals with a cubic
lattice and close melting temperature (magnesium and alumi-
num). Thus, the modern deformable aluminum alloys (spe-
cifically, the refractory structural alloy 1151) can serve at up
to 400 – 450°C (for a short time).
Academician A. A. Bochvar has shown [2] that that the
lower refractoriness in hcp materials is a result of the diffe -
rence in the values of the coefficient of expansion over dif -
ferent directions in the bulk of the material.
Thus, the possibilities of magnesium alloys in this re -
spect are limited as compared to aluminum alloys.
To raise the refractoriness of magnesium alloys, they are
enriched with elements promoting creation of an appropriate
structure and possessing considerable strength of the inter -
atomic bonds with magnesium atoms, for example, manga -
nese and zirconium. Deformable magnesium alloy MA12 de -
veloped at VIAM in the 1960s has shown good performance,
but the level of its high-temperature and strength properties
does not satisfy the requirements of today. At 150°C, the
creep limit of MA12 /c115
0.2/c47100 is only 122 – 127 MPa (depend -
ing on the kind of the semiproduct); the ultimate strength /c115r
and the yield strength /c1150.2 at room temperature do not exceed
255 – 285 and 137 – 190 MPa respectively [1].
Such alloying components as rare-earth elements (neo -
dymium, yttrium etc.) are becoming more and more popular
[3 – 7].

Magnesium alloys with zinc and some REE as alloying
elements have a specific phase composition containing
long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) phases. Formation of
LPSO phases in magnesium alloys has been detected re -
cently by Japanese researchers lead by Y oshihito Kawamura.
As a rule, such phases form in magnesium alloys in the pre -
sence of elements of only the yttrium group, i.e., Y , Gd, Dy,
Tb, Ho, Er, Tm and, necessarily, of zinc [7 – 9]. It is assumed
that these phases make a considerable contribution into ele -
vation of the strength properties of magnesium alloys at
room and elevated temperatures, lower the anisotropy of the
mechanical properties, and improve the corrosion resistance
and the plastic deformability [10 – 14].
We have developed a promising magnesium alloy
VMD16 of the Mg – Zn – Zr – REE system, which differs
from the previous generation in the chemical and phase com -
positions and in the level of properties.
The aim of the present work
2 was to study the nature of
the refractoriness (the special features of the structure and
phase composition) of deformable magnesium alloy VMD16
containing zinc and elements of the yttrium subgroup of
REE.
METHODS OF STUDY
The objects of our study were forgings from alloy
VMD16. The alloy was obtained by a commercial process
involving melting, casting, and subsequent deformation.
Round ingots with diameter 360 mm were first pressed into
bars with diameter 180 mm. After cutting into measured
pieces, the pressed preforms were forged in a vertical hy-
draulic press developing 5000 tonf.
The forgings shaped as rectangular parallelepipeds with
skew vertexes were cut into billets used to prepare specimens
for determining the properties and studying the micro -
structure.
The mechanical properties of the specimens from the de -
formed semiproducts subjected to uniaxial tension in de -
formed and heat treated states were determined according to
the GOST 1497–84 and GOST 25503–80 Standards. The
high-temperature strength parameters were determined as re -
quired for tests of metallic materials, i.e., the long-term
strength according to GOST 10145–81, the creep strength
according to GOST 3248–81 in terms of 0.2% tolerance for
permanent strain.
Special features of the phase composition and
microstructure of the materials were determined with the
help of advanced equipment, i.e.,
– a JSM6490-LV scanning electron microscope with an
INCA450 attachment for x-ray spectrum analysis (XRSA);
– a Tecnai G2 F20 S-TWIN TMP (FEI) transmission
electron microscope at an accelerating voltage of 200 kV .
The specimens for the TEM study were prepared using
an electroerosion mill with subsequent mechanical thinning
against a SiC emery paper to 100 /c109m and then by jet
electropolishing in an acid-alcohol electrolyte at a tempera -
ture of –40°C. The final thinning was conducted in a PIPS II
device for ion thinning in an argon environment at an acce -
lerating voltage of 0.5 kV . Etching was not applied.
The SEM micrographs were obtained in the COMPO
mode in back-scattered electrons. It was important that the
fragments of the phase regions containing alloying elements
with a great number in the Periodic System (in particular, Nd
and La) were colored bright against a light background in the
micrographs. The phase fragments containing elements with
lower numbers (Mg, Zn, Y) were colored darker (a variety of
gray).
Local chemical analysis of the structural components
was performed by the x-ray spectrum method using an
X-max
N 80 (Oxford) energy dispersive spectrometer.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The results of the study of the features of the fine struc-
ture of specimens cut from forgings of alloy VMD16 show
that the microstructure of the alloy without heat treatment (in
the hot-deformed condition) contains oval grains elongated
somewhat over the longitudinal direction. The forgings have
fine grains (7 – 12 /c109m). Fragments of a eutectic phase bear-
ing Zn, Y and Mn with a length of 20 – 25 /c109m are detectable
at junctions of several grains. They have been studied earlier
in [7]. Thin chains of complex-composition microparticles
are arranged over grain boundaries (see Fig. 1 a and b ).
It has also been shown that in the very fine particles are
accompanied by subgrains 200 – 500 nm in diameter. The
subgrain nanosize structure has been detected due to the use
of a scanning electron microscope at large magnifications
(/c18015,000, /c18025,000, Fig. 1 c and d ).
A distinctive feature of the alloy is the fact that the
subgrain structure is preserved in it after the heat treatment
(Fig. 1d ). Prolongation of the aging hold does not cause dis -
appearance of the subgrain structure, but promotes some in -
crease in the total number of subgrains. The SEM study has
also shown formation of numerous randomly distributed dot
intermetallics in the body of grains.
The data of the x-ray spectrum analysis confirm that the
chemical composition of these intermetallic particles con -
tains atoms of the alloying REE (Nd, Y); the linear sizes of
the detected particles do not exceed 400 – 500 nm (Fig. 2).
It also follows from the SEM data (Figs. 1 and 2) that the
phase composition of the alloy and its grain structure remain
virtually invariable after the aging by the variant suggested

pared to the hot-deformed condition.

CONCLUSIONS
The results of the detailed study of the fine structure of
forgings from alloy VMD16 performed with the use of recent
high-resolution methods, analysis of the level of its refrac -
tory properties and of room-temperature properties prove
that the high characteristics of the alloy are formed due to its
specific phase composition and structural features.
The main role in the elevation of the high-temperature
and strength properties of the alloy belongs to the thermally
stable nanosize LPSO phases. The LPSO phases are present
in the bulk of grains and also form blocks in multilayer frag -
ments of the eutectic constituent, i.e., additional hardening
components hindering the dislocation motion and decelerat -
ing the diffusion processes in the alloy.
The nanosize particles of thermally stable phases, i.e.,
zinc zirconides and yttrium hydrides, contribute substantially
into the elevation of the mechanical properties.
The steady subgrain structure detected in the alloy is pre -
served after the heat treatment and hardens the alloy addi -
tionally.
Alloy VMD16 has a level of refractory and strength
properties much higher than those of the commercial refrac -
tory magnesium alloy MA12.
