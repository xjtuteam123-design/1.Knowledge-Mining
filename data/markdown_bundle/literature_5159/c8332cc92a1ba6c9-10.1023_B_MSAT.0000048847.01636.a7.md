SOME FEATURES OF HIGH-TEMPERATURE OXIDATION
OF ALUMINIDE COATINGS ON ALLOY TsNK-7P
A. M. Smyslov, 1 R. R. Nev’yantseva,1 and A. A. Bybin 1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 8, pp. 31 – 36, August, 2004.
Special features of high-temperature oxidation of aluminide coatings (slip-deposited Al – Si and ion-plasma-
deposited VSDP-11) on alloy TsNK-7P are considered. The kinetics of the oxidation process, the element
composition, and the microhardness of coatings with different thicknesses are described. Results of thermal
fatigue tests of alloy TsNK-7P with aluminide diffusion coatings are presented. It is shown that the VSDP-11
coating ensures a higher heat resistance and thermal fatigue strength on alloy TsNK-7P than the Al – Si coat-
ing and can replace the latter on turbine vanes of gas compressor units.
INTRODUCTION
At present, the turbine vanes serving in the AL-31ST
gas-turbine drive are produced from refractory alloy
TsNK-7P. The vanes operate at 750 – 850°C. Under these
conditions the material of the vanes should resist the action
of a high-temperature medium for a long time. It is noted in
[1] that alloy TsNK-7P has a high high-temperature strength,
an optimum combination of strength and ductility, and a high
corrosion resistance exceeding that of the IN738LC foreign
counterpart. However, in the case of long-term service of this
alloy (about 25 – 30 thousand h) at a temperature of about
800°C it requires protection from high-temperature corro -
sion. In this connection, ready vanes are coated with alumi -
num silicide by the slip method. It is shown in [2, 3] that slip
technology does not yield coatings homogeneous over the
thickness and produces finely scattered pores and foreign in -
clusions. Such defects reduce the service life of vanes. Re -
pair of the vanes after a certain time of operation, which in -
volves recoating, does not solve the problem.
According to [4 – 6], the most promising method for de -
positing aluminide protective coatings on the surface of re -
fractory alloys is high-energy vacuum-plasma technology
(HEVPT) based on condensation of a coating from a flow of
vacuum-arc plasma at an energy exceeding 50 eV .
The present work was aimed at studying special features
of high-temperature oxidation of aluminide coatings depo -
sited by this method and the possibility of replacement of
slip-deposited coatings by vacuum ion-plasma ones.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
We will begin our consideration of special features of
high-temperature oxidation with the structure and phase
composition of the coatings and the alloy.
The refractory corrosion-resistant alloy TsNK-7P is a
cast nickel-base alloy [7, 8]. In order to ensure a high corro -
sion resistance the alloy is alloyed with chromium, and in or-
der to raise its high-temperature strength it is alloyed with ti-
tanium and aluminum. Tungsten alloying of the alloy pro -
duces additional growth in the strength characteristics. The
addition of cobalt increases its ductility. The matrix is a
/c103-solid solution of chromium in nickel. The hardening is pri -
marily provided by particles of the /c103/c162-phase based on the
Ni
3(Al, Ti) intermetallic compound formed as a result of de -
composition of the /c103-solid solution of nickel during cooling
of the alloy. Since the grain boundaries of the/c103-solid solution
are reinforced by adding carbon and boron into the composi-
tion of the alloy, its structure also contains carbides (MeC,
Me6C, and Me23C6 ) and borides (MexBy ).
After diffusion annealing, the alumosilicide coating con-
sists of two zones, i.e., external and internal ones. The main
structural component of the external zone is a NiAl /c98-phase
alloyed with chromium [3]. Silicon is present in the layer in
the form of a substitutional solid solution and silicides of re-
fractory elements [9]. Chromium, molybdenum, and tung-
sten carbides are absent in the external layer [10]. A chain of
MeC and Me
6C carbides occurring on the interface of the ex-
ternal and internal zones marks the initial surface of the alloy
[3]. The matrix of the internal zone of the alloy is a mixture
of /c98-NiAl and /c103/c162-Ni
3Al intermetallic phases. Individual
segregations of TiC and Me 6C carbides (for example,
Ni3W3C) occur over the entire thickness of the zone [8].
Coarse segregations of Cr 23C6 carbides occur closer to the
interface with the external zone, and pin-like segregations of
the /c115-phase based on the CoCr compound are encountered at
the interface with the alloy. Silicon is virtually absent in the
internal zone due to the low diffusion mobility [9].
The phase composition of the VSDP-11 aluminide coat -
ing is similar to that of the alumosilicide coating. However,
in contrast to the Al – Si coating it has a finer-grained struc -
ture, and its external zone is alloyed with yttrium in addition
to silicon. It is known [11] that yttrium is poorly soluble in
the nickel-base solid solution, and after diffusion annealing it
chiefly has the form of dispersed particles on grain bound -
aries of the /c98-phase. The internal zone of the VSDP-11 coat-
ing has a heterogeneous structure like the Al – Si coating but
does not contain pin-like segregations of the /c115-phase.

It can be seen from Fig. 1 that the curve describing the
oxidation of uncoated specimens has an almost logarithmic

According to the data of [12] an initial oxide film that
contains oxides of virtually any alloying component of the
alloy, for example, NiO, CoO, Cr
2O3,A l 2O3,T i O2, etc.,
grows in the first period of the oxidation. The growth of the
oxides is determined by the thermodynamic probability of
their formation and by kinetic factors. According to the data
of [11] the thermodynamic potential of formation of the
Al
2O3,C r 2O3, NiO, and CoO oxides at 850°C amounts to
– 1321, – 844, – 140, and – 155 kJ /c47mole, respectively.
Knowing that the Al 2O and C2O3 oxides have a higher ther -
modynamic probability of formation, we assume that they
grow in the first turn and yield an/c97-(Al2O3 ) oxide layer with
an underlying layer of Cr 2O3. Such an oxide layer possesses
a high protective capacity. The formation of NiAl 2O4 and
NiCr2O4 spinels, which have coherent boundaries and in -
crease the protective capacity of the oxide layer, is also pos -
sible. All these factors reduce considerably the oxidation
rate. It should also be taken into account that an isothermal
hold can be accompanied by reactions that cause decomposi-
tion of TiO
2 oxides and NiTiO3 perovskites that are incohe -
rent to the NiAl 2O4 and NiCr2O4 spinels. Defects appearing
on the boundaries of these phases promote oxygen perme -
ation into the depth of the alloy, which explains the inconsid-
erable growth in the mass of the specimens in the last oxida-
tion stages.

Further oxidation is connected with the diffusion of oxy-

over grain boundaries of the oxides and with the diffusion of

Since the diffusivity of aluminum in the /c98-phase is lower
than that of nickel [13], aluminum virtually does not diffuse
into the internal zone of the coating but is chiefly spent for
formation of protective oxides lying in the surface layer.
Nickel diffuses actively into the coating, which is confirmed
by results of x-ray spectral analysis (Table 1).
It is mentioned in [14] that at a temperature of
800 – 850°C the diffusion of tungsten from the alloy can
yield easily sublimating WO
2 and W2O5 oxides. Formation
of tungsten oxides in addition to the possible formation of
defective boundaries between NiTiO
3 perovskites and
NiAl2O4 and NiCr2O4 spinels should promote loosening of
the Al 2O3 oxide film and worsen its protective properties.
However, due to the considerable diffusion of silicon into the
internal zone of the coating, silicides of refractory elements
based on chromium, tungsten, and molybdenum form at the
interface of the internal zone and the alloy. These phases pos-
sess a partially covalent bond and serve as diffusion barriers
for penetration of refractory elements into the external zone
of the coating, which improves its stability.
On the other hand, the process of silicon depletion of the
/c98-phase of the external zone promotes a certain destabili -
zation. The diffusion of nickel from the alloy into the coating
promotes nickel enrichment of the /c98-(NiAl) phase. Accord -
ing to [3], this may lead to formation of a martensitic struc -
ture with enhanced microhardness on the interface of the in -
ternal and external zones of the coating. It is also mentioned
in [13] that a martensitic layer may form under the Al
2O3 ox-
ide film. The presence of a martensitic structure in the coat -
ing seems to explain the growth in the microhardness of the
considered zones of the coating after testing (Table 2). For -
mation of a martensitic layer is responsible for cleavage of
the external zone of the coating in local regions, which has
been confirmed by a study of the surface topography.
It should also be noted that the decrease in the protective
properties of the coating is also promoted by the decomposi -
tion of the /c115-phase, which increases the fraction of Me
23C6
carbides. This is confirmed by the enhanced content of chro -
mium in the internal zone and of cobalt in both external and
internal zones of the coating (see Table 1) and by some in -
crease in the microhardness of the internal zone of the coat -
ing after testing (Table 2). As a result of the considered pro -
cesses the protective capacity of the aluminide coating drops
to some extent, and the oxidation process continues though at
a low rate.
Let is consider the results of high-temperature oxidation
of specimens of the VSDP-11 vacuum-plasma coating. It can
be seen from Fig. 1 that curve 3 reflecting the kinetics of the
process of oxidation of the studied specimens behaves simi -
larly to curves 1 and 2. However, after 100 h of testing the
oxidation rate of the specimens stops to change, and after
300 h of testing /c68m is 2.2 and 1.2 times lower than the values
for uncoated specimens and specimens with an Al – Si coat -
ing respectively. Since the thermodynamic potential of for -
mation of yttrium oxide has a minimum value relative to the
other elements of the coating and is equal to – 1556 kJ /c47mole
[11], we can assume that the rapid growth in the specific
mass of the specimens in the initial stage of the oxidation is
connected with the positive influence of yttrium. Due to the
presence of a high excess of oxygen in the gas medium, the
aluminum of the /c98-phase oxidizes to Al
2O3 like in the slip-
deposited coating. The high affinity of yttrium for oxygen
promotes formation of Y
2O3 oxides which, as shown in [8],
are additional centers of growth of the continuous protective
Al
2O3 coating.
Later, when the oxide film becomes more developed, the
oxidation rate decreases. Yttrium oxides formed primarily
over boundaries of the /c98-phase create zones of physical
blocking, change the amount and geometry of the short
closed paths for oxygen diffusion, and raise the activation
energy of oxygen diffusion over intergrain boundaries [11].
In addition, yttrium causes changes in the physical and
mechanical characteristics of the protective film, which is
connected with the formation of mixed oxide phases on the
oxide-coating interface by the reactions
Al
2O3 +Y 2O3 = 2YAlO3;
Al2O3 + 3YAlO3 =Y 3Al5O12.
Formation of an acicular structure of yttrium oxide parti-
cles in local regions along boundaries of the oxidized
/c98-phase increases the adhesion of the oxide film to the coat -
ing but simultaneously hinders the diffusion of the ions of the
metal and of oxygen.
In high-temperature tests of the VSDP-11 coating we
also observe diffusion of silicon toward the interface of the
external and internal zones, which causes formation of a
layer of silicides. However, in contrast to the Al – Si coating,
where the silicide layer appears chiefly on the bottom of the
internal zone, the layer in the VSDP-11 coating forms in the
external zone, which follows from the results of x-ray spec -
tral analyses (see Table 1). It seems that yttrium present in
the external zone of the coating hinders the diffusion of Si
4+
over cation vacancies and interferes with its penetration into
the internal zone of the coating. This structure of the protec -
tive barrier layer hinders the diffusion of nickel into the ex -
ternal zone of the coating, as a result of which a martensitic

rature strength of a coating depends considerably on its
thickness [11]. For this reason, we subjected specimens with

that of the Al – Si coating. This is another confirmation of
the activating effect of yttrium particles, which are centers of
crystallization, on the process of formation of a protective
Al
2O3 layer.
The endurance of the coatings depends considerably on
their capacity to resist the action of abrupt temperature fluc -
tuations. The test results presented in Table 3 show that
the use of 50- /c109m-thick Al – Si coatings and 55- /c109m-thick
VSDP-11 coatings increases the resistance to thermal fatigue
R by a factor of 1.6 and 1.9, respectively. The higher value of
R for the VSDP-11 coating is connected with the effect of yt-
trium that promotes formation of finer grains in the oxides,
raises the strength and fracture toughness of the transition
layer from the oxide to the metal, smoothes the difference in
the temperature coefficients of linear expansion of the oxide
and the metal, improves the contact between the oxide com -
pounds of the film and the metal, and decreases the stress
concentrations caused by the mismatch between the specific
volumes of the metal and of the oxide on the interface [11].
The results of a study of the effect of the thickness of the
VSDP-11 coating on the thermal fatigue strength show that
with growth in the thickness of the coating from 30 to 55 /c109m
the endurance remains virtually unchanged; when the coat -
ing grows to 70 /c109m, the endurance decreases by a factor of
1.2. This seems to be connected with the decrease in the duc-
tility of the thicker coating due to the higher content of alu -
minum.
CONCLUSIONS
1. A VSDP-11 coating deposited on alloy TsNK-7P by
the method of HEVPT possesses a higher heat resistance
than an Al – Si coatings deposited by the slip method, which
is connected with the special features of formation of protec-
tive barrier silicide layers that hinder the diffusion of the al-
loy components into the coating and with the positive effect
of yttrium that not only increases the adhesion strength of the
Al
2O3 film but also promotes its generation and growth on
the surface on the coating.
2. With growth in the thickness of the VSDP-11 coating
the high-temperature strength of the material increases due to
the higher amount of aluminum in the external zone of the
coating.
3. Deposition of VSDP-11 coating improves the heat re -
sistance of alloy TsNK-7P by 24% relative to an equivalent
Al – Si coating at a thickness of 50 – 55 /c109m. This is explain-
able by the positive effect of yttrium on the physical and me-
chanical characteristics of the protective layer.

a VSDP-11 coating with a thickness of 40 – 60 /c109m due to its
