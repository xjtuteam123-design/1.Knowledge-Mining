abstract
W-based alloys are currently considered promising candidates for high heat ﬂux components in future
fusion reactors. In this work, the strength and fracture toughness of CNTs-doped W–Nb alloys fabricated
by hot pressing were measured by three-point bending tests on smooth and single edge notched beams,
respectively, and the fracture characteristics were observed by scanning electron microscopy. The result
indicated that the bending strength and fracture toughness increased with increasing the Nb content.
Combining with the microstructure analysis on the fracture surface, it was found that the increased
Nb content could reﬁne the microstructure and form the oriented Nb(W) solid solutions, which increases
the number of crack deﬂections and total fracture paths and enhanced the strength and fracture
toughness.

1. Introduction
Tungsten (W) and its alloys have been considered as the most
attractive armour material for high heat ﬂux (HHF) components
in a fusion device due to its extremely high melting point, high
thermal conductivity, low erosion rate and low tritium retention
[1–4]. Key issues for this refractory metal however are its high fab-
rication temperature, high ductile-to-brittle transition tem-
perature (DBTT) and poor ductility [2,3].
Currently, pure W and some oxide and carbide dispersed
strengthened W alloys have been envisaged as potential structural
materials for the divertor of the near-term demonstration reactor
[3–9]. A requirement of the divertor design is the development of
W alloys having DBTT values somewhat below 600 /C176C and recrys-
tallization temperature around 1300 /C176C [3]. The recent work found
that the Y 2O3-doped W–Ti alloys manufactured by powder metal-
lurgy retained their strength and toughness up to 800 /C176C. On this
basis, it has been put forward that sintered W heavy alloys might
fulﬁll the requirements of the current divertor designs due to bet-
ter mechanical properties and more favorable microstructure.
The W heavy alloys containing small amount of sintering addi-
tives such as Ni, Fe, V and Ti, enable major reduction in the sinter-
ing temperature of W alloys [10–12]. However, there are still
existing some limitations for the W alloys by using above-
mentioned elements due to their relatively low melting point,
which affects the high temperature mechanical properties of mate-
rials. Nb has a relatively high melting point (2468 /C176C) and excellent
low temperature ductility. Moreover, attributing to the solid–
solution strengthening of Nb(W) solid solution, the Nb element is
the most probably efﬁcient for improving room and high tem-
perature mechanical properties of W alloys. Additionally, carbon
nano-tubes (CNTs) possess high chemical activity and some unique
physical properties such as ultrahigh mechanical strength, excel-
lent electronic and thermal conductivity. Recently, the CNTs-doped
W–Nb alloys were fabricated by hot pressing [13]. The results
found that the addition of trace CNTs acted as the effective sinter-
ing additive in W alloys. Namely, the CNTs could enhance the dif-
fusion of W atoms along the grain boundaries.
Therefore, the aim of the present work is to investigate the
inﬂuence of Nb content on the mechanical properties and fracture
characteristics of CNTs-doped W–Nb alloys. The mechanical prop-
erties were evaluated by three-point bending test. The fracture
characteristics were analyzed by scanning electron microscopy
(SEM) with energy dispersive spectrum (EDS).

2.2. Materials characterization
The bulk density of specimens was measured by the Archi-
medes methods with distilled water. The theoretical density of
specimens was calculated according to the rule of mixtures. The
sintered specimens were cut by electric discharge machining.
Three-point bending tests were carried out on smooth and
single-edge notched beams to measure the bending strength and
the fracture toughness at room temperature according to ASTM
E290-97a and ASTM E-399-90. The ﬁnal dimensions of the
specimens were 25 l /C22w /C22t mm for the bending test and 25 l -
/C22w /C24t mm for the fracture toughness test, respectively. The sup-
port span was 20 mm. For each test, at least ﬁve samples were used.
For the fracture toughness test, the ratio of the notch depth to speci-
men thickness was about 0.5. Vickers hardness was measured by
micro-indentation technique with a load of 9.8 N for 12 s according
to ASTM 384-89.
The surface morphologies and fracture surface were observed
by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) equipped with energy dis-
persive spectrum (EDS). The mean grain size was determined by
measuring the linear intercept length of the grains on the images
of specimens.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
Fig. 1 shows the microstructure of the W–0.1CNTs–1Nb alloy,
which revealed the presence of two clearly different phases. To
identify the phase composition, the EDS scanning was performed
along the line as shown in Fig. 1 . EDS analysis revealed that the
main composition of gray phase is W and dark one is the Nb(W)
solid solution. As seen in Fig. 1 , the Nb(W) solid solution was
embedded in the W phase.
The formation of Nb(W) solid solution is due to the fact that W
and Nb are solid soluble at high sintering temperature in which W
atoms can move through Nb phase by inter-diffusion, which is
similar to the existence of Ti(W) solid solution in the W–Ti system
[12]. It is worthy noting that the content of W and Nb in Nb(W)
solid solution was dependent on the phase size as shown by the
elemental distribution in Fig. 1 . The larger the phase size of Nb,
the fewer the W content in Nb. The Nb(W) solid solutions became
ﬁne and their distribution appeared to be more homogeneous
when the content of Nb is less than 3%.

surface. Fig. 2 shows the mean grain size of W as a function of Nb
content. For reference, the data for the specimen without Nb plot-
ted in Fig. 2 is corresponding to the pure tungsten fabricated at
same condition. The mean grain size of pure tungsten is about
4.1 lm. However, when the 0.1% CNTs were added to the pure

an enhanced diffusion of W atoms along the GBs induced by CNTs.
Furthermore, with the addition of Nb, the densiﬁcation was

3.2. Mechanical properties and fracture characteristics
Fig. 3 shows the micro-hardness of CNTs-doped W–Nb alloys as
a function of Nb content. The micro-hardness is about 2.18 GPa for
pure W specimen. However, the micro-hardness has a remarkable
increase in the CNTs-doped W–Nb alloys, which is varied from
4.84 GPa to 6.07 GPa when the Nb content changed from 0.5% to
5%. Generally, the micro-hardness is related to the density and
grain size. Thus, the low micro-hardness in pure W could be
attributed to its low density. The high density and small grain size
should be responsible for the increased micro-hardness in the
CNTs-doped W–Nb alloys.

Fig. 4 shows the average bending strength and fracture tough-
ness as a function of Nb content. In case of CNTs-doped W–Nb
alloys, the general tendency was that the bending strength and
fracture toughness increased with increasing the Nb content. This
is similar to those reported in the W–Ti system [12]. The bending
strength was 274.6 MPa for the pure W specimen and 558 MPa
for the W–0.1CNTs–5Nb specimen. The fracture toughness was
3.13 MPa m1/2 for the pure W specimen, 9.76 MPa m 1/2 for the
W–0.1CNTs–5Nb alloy. The high bending strength and fracture
toughness could be attributed to their ﬁne grains ( Fig. 2), high den-
sities (Table 1) and improved grain boundary structure. Particular-
ly, the reduced grain size should be one of the important factors.
Generally, the larger grain size leads to poor mechanical properties.
In order to get better understanding on fracture mechanism, the
side surface and fracture surface of specimens after SENB test were
examined by SEM.
Fig. 5 shows the side views of specimen W–0.1CNTs–0.5Nb and
W–0.1CNTs–3Nb after SENB tests, respectively. For specimen W-
0.1CNTs-0.5Nb, the crack propagation direction is almost parallel
to that of initial notch depth ( Fig. 5 (a)), and the crack propagation
path is straight and short ( Fig. 5(b)), which resulted in the low frac-
ture toughness ( Fig. 4 ). After SENB test, the specimen was broken
into two parts. However, for the specimen W–0.1CNTs–3Nb, it
did not fail completely after SENB test. The crack initiated from
the notch tip and propagated with an obvious crack deﬂection as
shown in Fig. 5 (c) and (d), leading to a high fracture toughness
(Fig. 4 ).
Fig. 6 shows the fracture surface for specimen with different Nb
contents. It is clear that the grain size signiﬁcantly decreased and
had a wide distribution with increasing the Nb content. This is con-
sistent with the result in Fig. 2 . Generally, materials with bimodal
or multimodal grain size distribution can improve the strength and
the ductility [14]. For specimens W–0.1CNTs–0.5Nb and
W–0.1CNTs–1Nb, the Nb(W) solid solutions mainly located at the
triple junctions between the W grains and presented a inter-
granular fracture. For the specimens with content of 3 wt.% and
5 wt.% Nb ( Fig. 6(c) and (d)), the fracture surface was rather rugged
and the oriented distribution of Nb(W) solid solution phase with
small size was clear. The Nb(W) solid solutions were fractured with
evidence of both intergranular cracking and cleavage ( Fig. 6(c) and
(d)), but the cleavage of Nb(W) solid solution is unusual [15]. Prob-
ably, the ﬁne grains and intergranular fracture are main dominate
factors for the improved mechanical properties. The reduced grain
size increases the number of crack deﬂections and total fracture
paths. On the other hand, the improved mechanical properties
with increasing the Nb content could also be attributed to the
favorable mechanical properties of Nb at room temperature. The
high content of Nb in W and the deformation of Nb under stress
might change the stress intensity and reduce the stress concentra-
tion in CNTs-doped W–Nb alloys. Consequently, the crack exten-
sion and deﬂection consumed more fracture energy, leading to
high strength and fracture toughness.
4. Conclusions
CNTs-doped W–Nb alloys were fabricated by hot pressing. The
strength and fracture toughness were measured by three-point
bending tests on smooth and single edge notched beams. The frac-
ture characteristics were observed by SEM observation. The main
results are summarized as follows.
(1) The bending strength and fracture toughness increased with
increasing the Nb content.
(2) The Nb(W) solid solutions were formed, and the W content,
size and distribution of solid solution were varied with the
Nb content in alloys.
