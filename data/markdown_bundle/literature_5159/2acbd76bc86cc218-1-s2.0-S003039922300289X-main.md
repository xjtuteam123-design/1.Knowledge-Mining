ABSTRACT 
In this paper, SLM technique with different laser volume energy density (VED) was used to prepare AlCoCr -
FeNi
2.1 
eutectic high entropy alloy, in order to investigate the effect of different VED on the relative density, 
microstructure, mechanical properties and high temperature oxidation properties of the high entropy alloy. As a 
result, when the VED was raised to 187.5 J/mm
3
, the relative density of the alloy reaches 99.51 %, the alloy 
exhibits a dual-phase (FCC and BCC) lamellar eutectic structure. The sample achieves high strength and ductility 
at the same time, the average microhardness is 479.04 HV, the yield and tensile strength reach 1151 MPa and 
1364 MPa respectively, and the elongation at fracture is 16.37 %. 
1. Introduction 
Traditional alloys are dominated by one or two elements, and the 
performance is enhanced by adding a small number of other elements. 
Due to the design concept of traditional alloys, the number as well as the 
performance tend to be bottlenecked, and new high-performance alloy 
systems need to be designed to meet the higher requirements for the 
performance of alloys in various fields. Yeh et al. [1] of Tsinghua Uni -
versity in Taiwan put forward the concept of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) 
for the first time in 2004. HEAs, also known as multi-principal alloys, are 
composed of five or more major elements, and the molar fraction of each 
element is between 5 % and 35 %. Due to its unique four effects: high 
entropy effect, sluggish diffusion effect, lattice distortion effect and 
“ cocktail ” effect, which give high entropy alloys excellent properties 
such as high strength, high hardness, superior wear and corrosion 
resistance, high temperature softening resistance and oxidation resis -
tance [2] . Therefore, high-entropy alloys have great potential for aero -
space applications. 
Although HEAs have multiple components, they prefer to form 
single-phase solid solutions, such as FCC, BCC and HCP, rather than 
some brittle intermetallic compounds [3] . However, single-phase HEAs 
often do not have both plasticity and strength. Single-phase FCC 
structures have good plasticity but low strength. For example, the 
elongation of CoCrFeMnNi is 35.0 %, but the tensile strength is only 601 
MPa [4] . Single-phase BCC structures have high strength but poor plas -
ticity. For instance, the strength of Nb

FCC phase and B2 phase jointly contribute to high strain hardening,

3.4. Microhardness and tensile property 
Fig. 12 a shows the microhardness distribution of the SLM sample 
cross-section at four different VED. With the VED increasing, it can be 
seen that the microhardness increases and the microhardness fluctuation 
tends to level off. The low VED samples have more defects, and when 
being loaded, these defects will expand under the indenter, resulting in 
larger indentations and thus lower microhardness. As the VED increases, 
it makes the temperature gradient larger and the cooling rate faster, 
obtaining finer grains. According to the Hall-Petch formula, the refine -
ment of grains increases the grains per unit area, so that the external 
forces on the fine grains are dispersed to proceed within more grains, 
reducing the stress concentration and enhancing their microhardness 
[22] . Peng et al. [23] analyzed the relationship between AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
EHEA lamellar spacing and microhardness. The analysis showed that the 
microhardness of the alloy increased with the decrease of lamellar 
spacing. When VED = 187.5 J/mm
3
, the lamellar spacing is the smallest, 
and the maximum average microhardness of the sample is 479.04HV. 
Fig. 12 b shows the hardness comparison between SLM-AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
and AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
prepared by rapid solidification and SPS [24,25] . The 
results show that the microhardness of the samples prepared by SLM is 
significantly higher than that prepared by rapid solidification and spark 
plasma sintering (SPS). In addition, with the increase of VED, the sample 
porosity decreases, the sample microstructure is dispersed uniformly, 
and the deviation of hardness at each hardness point decreases, making 
the microhardness curve become flat [17] . Therefore, the increase in 
relative density and fine grain strengthening are the main reasons for the 
increase in microhardness.
