ABSTRACT
This study successfully develops a novel Al-La-Mg-Fe alloy with a balance of room-temperature and high- 
temperature mechanical properties through precise control of grain structure and intermetallics via hot extru -
sion process. The as-extruded Al-La-Mg-Fe alloys achieve enhanced room-temperature mechanical properties 
(UTS: 293 ± 13 MPa, El: 6.6 ± 0.3 %) coupled with remarkable high-temperature strength-ductility synergy 
(UTS: 158 ± 5 MPa, El: 24.7 ± 3.0 %). The enhanced room-temperature strength-ductility balance originates 
from the synergistic effects of refined grains, finely dispersed intermetallics, and narrowed intermetallic-free 
zones, which collectively enhance dislocation motion resistance and promote dislocation accumulation. More -
over, the combined influence of high volume fraction of coarsening-resistant Al

1. Introduction
Aluminum alloys are extensively utilized in the aerospace and 
transportation industries for their high specific strength, superior 
corrosion resistance, and excellent processability [ 1 , 2 ]. However, the 
mechanical properties of conventional high-strength aluminum alloys 
degrade significantly at elevated temperatures due to the dissolution 
and coarsening of precipitates, making them unable to meet the required 
high-temperature performance standards [ 3 ]. To enhance the 
high-temperature performance of aluminum alloys, current research 
mainly focuses on the following aspects: (i) Introducing alloying ele -
ments with low diffusion coefficients (Sc [ 4 ], Zr [ 5 ], Er [ 6 ], etc.) to in -
crease the volume fraction of strengthening phases and inhibit the 
coarsening and dissolution of precipitates. However, this increases the 
alloy cost (especially for Sc) and has the drawback of insufficient 
strengthening phases. (ii) Incorporating high-melting-point submicron 
ceramic particles and optimizing interfacial effects to form 
heat-resistant aluminum matrix composites [ 3 , 7 ]. This approach can 
significantly increase the strength of Al alloys, but it leads to a dramatic 
decrease in ductility, typically down to around 3 %. (iii) Adding tran -
sition metals (TMs) with low solubility and low diffusion coefficients to 
form eutectic Al-TM alloys [ 8 , 9 ]. This strategy highly depends on rapid 
solidification technologies, and the associated high cost hinders its in -
dustrial application.
In recent years, Al-(La, Ce) alloys have attracted attention due to 
their excellent heat resistance and the economic advantages of La and Ce 
[ 10 – 12 ]. These alloys have potential in the medium-to-high temperature 
range (200 – 400

matrix and their low diffusion rates enable the strengthening phase

Additionally, grain size and intermetallic morphology play crucial 
roles in achieving superior mechanical properties. The Hall-Petch rela -
tionship demonstrates that grain refinement enhances alloy strength by 
impeding dislocation motion. Furthermore, fine-grained structures 
promote more uniform stress distribution, thereby reducing stress con -
centration. Additionally, finely dispersed intermetallics also strengthen 
the alloy through dislocation pinning effects [ 21 ]. Although additive 
manufacturing enables the simultaneous refinement of grain size and 
intermetallics through rapid solidification, its high cost remains a major 
barrier to industrial application [ 22 ]. In contrast, hot extrusion is a more 
cost-effective processing method that can also refine the microstructure 
and enhance the alloy ’ s mechanical properties [ 23 , 24 ]. Furthermore, 
the regulation of hot extrusion parameters enables the adjustment of 
recrystallization behavior, texture intensity, grain size, and 
second-phase morphology, thereby optimizing the alloy ’ s microstruc -
ture to achieve the best combination of strength and toughness [ 25 ].
Based on this objective, this study fabricates a novel Al – La – Mg – Fe 
alloy via hot extrusion and optimizes the extrusion ratio to regulate 
grain size and intermetallic size, achieving an excellent balance of me -
chanical properties at both room and elevated temperatures. By 
comparatively analyzing the microstructure, mechanical properties, and 
fracture morphology of the as-extruded Al – La – Mg – Fe alloy with 
different grain sizes and intermetallic dimensions, this study reveals the 
strengthening and toughening mechanisms at both room and elevated 
temperatures. Thereby providing guidance for the production of heat- 
resistant aluminum alloys and expanding their application scenarios.

4. Discussion
4.1. Microstructural evolution
The coarse intermetallics in the as-cast alloy are effectively refined to 
micron and sub-micron levels and are distributed along the extrusion 
direction after hot extrusion, as shown in Fig. 3 . The progressive in -
crease in extrusion ratio induces intensified plastic deformation, 
resulting in enhanced fragmentation and refinement of the in -
termetallics [ 42 ], thereby rendering the as-extruded 30:1 alloy with the 
finest intermetallic microstructure among the three investigated extru -
sion ratios. Despite the deformation mismatch between the softer matrix 
and the harder intermetallics during plastic deformation, no interface 
cracking between the intermetallics and the matrix was observed after 
hot extrusion. This is validated by the SEM ( Fig. 3 a – f) and TEM ( Fig. 5 a 
and b) images, confirming the good interface bonding between the in -
termetallics and the matrix in this alloy. Nevertheless, microscopic

The as-extruded Al – La – Mg – Fe alloy exhibits tunable strength and 
ductility depending on the extrusion ratio. Adjusting the extrusion ratio 
allows for the regulation of grain size and intermetallic dimension, 
significantly enhancing the alloy ’ s mechanical properties by nearly 100 
MPa (from 272 MPa to 363 MPa). Therefore, it is essential to investigate 
the effect of extrusion ratio on mechanical properties. The strengthening 
mechanisms of the as-extruded Al – La – Mg – Fe alloy mainly include solid 
solution strengthening ( σ

as-extruded 12:1, 20:1, and 30:1 alloys.

4.3. Room temperature toughening mechanisms
Fig. 13 shows the room-temperature longitudinal fractography 
beneath the fracture surface of the as-extruded Al – La – Mg – Fe alloys. 
During static tensile deformation, pre-existing cracks in intermetallics 
progressively propagate and widen, ultimately forming microcracks 
( Fig. 13 d – f, yellow arrows). Fractographic analysis further demonstrates 
that fracture primarily propagates along intermetallics ( Fig. 13 d – f, light 
blue arrows), a phenomenon attributed to the inherent microcracks in 
intermetallics, especially coarse ones. These microcracks act as stress 
concentrators, evolving into primary cracks that initiate fracture 
through strain localization. Additionally, it is noteworthy that all alloys 
exhibit varying degrees of intermetallic-free zones (IFZ), which are 
marked with blue dashed lines in Fig. S4 . According to ImageJ software 
calculations, the IFZ width for the as-extruded 12:1 alloy is 34.8 ± 11.4 
μ m, for the as-extruded 20:1 alloy it is 19.6 ± 7.5 μ m, and for the as- 
extruded 30:1 alloy it is 12.3 ± 2.0 μ m. The variation in IFZ width 
across different alloys stems from the enhanced fragmentation and 
refined dispersion of intermetallics induced by higher extrusion ratios, 
which consequently reduces the IFZ width compared to alloys processed 
with lower extrusion ratios. The IFZ is similar to the often observed 
Precipitation-Free Zone (PFZ) in aluminum alloys. Due to the absence of 
strengthening phases, the IFZ becomes a weak region where plastic 
deformation primarily initiates, making it prone to stress concentration 
[ 53 ]. Therefore, the presence of a wide IFZ is detrimental to the strength 
and ductility of the alloy [ 53 , 54 ]. Moreover, grain size also exerts a

5. Conclusions
This study developed a novel Al – La – Mg – Fe alloy through hot 
extrusion and regulated grain size and intermetallic dimension by 
varying the extrusion ratio. A comprehensive investigation was con -
ducted to elucidate the alloy ’ s mechanical properties, thermal stability 
characteristics, and underlying strengthening-toughening mechanisms 
across different microstructural configurations. The main conclusions 
are as follows. 
(1) Hot extrusion significantly refines the as-cast microstructure, 
with the extent of intermetallic refinement progressively 
increasing with higher extrusion ratios. During the recrystalli -
zation process, the uniformly distributed fine intermetallics serve 
as potent nucleation sites, effectively pinning grain boundaries 
and restricting excessive grain growth through Zener drag 
mechanism.
(2) The Al – La – Mg – Fe alloy achieves a favorable strength-ductility 
balance by adjusting the extrusion ratio. Among them, the as- 
extruded 30:1 alloy, with its narrower intermetallic-free zones 
(IFZ), finer intermetallics, and refined grain size, effectively 
mitigates stress concentration and enhances the Orowan 
strengthening effect, resulting in optimal room-temperature me -
chanical properties.
(3) All as-extruded alloys demonstrate excellent microstructural 
thermal stability, attributed to the exceptional coarsening resis -
tance of the Al
11
La
3 
and Al
13
Fe
4 
phases. The as-extruded 12:1,

temperature strength retention of the 30:1 alloy is primarily 
due to enhanced atomic motion and intensified grain boundary 
sliding at elevated temperatures, which weaken the grain 
boundaries and degrade the alloy ’ s high-temperature perfor -
mance. Moreover, the crack blunting effect during tensile defor -
mation ensures that all as-extruded alloys maintain excellent 
high-temperature ductility.
