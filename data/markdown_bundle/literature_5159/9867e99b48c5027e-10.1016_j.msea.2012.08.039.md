abstract
Deformation behavior of Ti–6Al–4Fe alloys with high strength and formability was investigated by
conducting high temperature tensile test. In tensile test at room temperature, the strength of Ti–6Al–4Fe
alloys was higher than that of a commercial Ti–6Al–4V alloy with a similar level of elongation. In
particular, the high temperature (700 1C) elongation was dramatically increased in Ti–6Al–4Fe alloys
showing /C24 800% superplasticity. Grain boundary sliding and suppression of cavity formation (i.e., easier
accommodation) during gb sliding due to beta phase were found to be possible mechanisms for the
superplasticity of Ti–6Al–4Fe alloys.

1. Introduction
Two-phase (aþ b) titanium alloys have attracted great interest
in research due to their low density, high strength, relatively good
workability, and excellent corrosion resistance [1–6]. Among the
two-phase titanium alloys, Ti–6Al–4V alloy has been most widely
used since it usually shows a good combination of properties. For
better commercial use, however, the cost of the alloy should be
reduced and the formability should be increased as well. These
two requirements can be achieved by replacing vanadium with a
cheaper element such as iron. Ti–Al–Fe alloys as a low cost
substitute for Ti–6Al–4V alloy have been reported to show high
strength and good formability; moreover, they usually have
better high-temperature properties than Ti–6Al–4V alloy [7,8].
In these two-phase titanium alloys, the mechanical properties are
of great complexity and strongly sensitive to the processing para-
meters, i.e. strain rate and temperature [2,3,9]. Therefore, it is very
important to understand the deformation behavior and the relation-
ship between microstructure and t he properties. Moreover, the
fabrication process is also very important for achieving high strength
and ductility. Many titanium alloys can be strengthened by a series
of heat treatment. For example, t he tensile strength of Ti–6Al–4V
alloy can be improved from 900– 1000 MPa to 1100–1200 MPa by
annealing [4]. This strengthening effect by annealing is also true for
the Ti–Al–Fe based two-phase alloys such as Ti–5Al–2.5Fe, TIMETAL
62S and Ti–5.5Al–1Fe alloys[7]. By carefully selecting the fabrication
process, we successfully manufactured Ti–6Al–4Fe alloys with high
strength and great formability at high temperature.
In this study, the high temperature deformation behavior of newly
developed Ti–6Al–4Fe alloys with/without silicon addition was
investigated by conducting tensile test at 700 1C. The commercial
Ti–6Al–4V and Ti–3Al–2.5V alloys were also tested for comparison.
Detailed microstructure was carefully observed by transmission
electron microscopy (TEM) and electron backscatter diffraction
(EBSD). The results were discussed and the possible mechanism of
high formability at high temperature was proposed.

3.2. Microstructures after tensile test
The microstructures of Ti–3Al–2.5V, Ti–6Al–4V and Ti–6Al–
4Fe–0.1Si alloys obtained by EBSD phase analysis before and after
deformation at 700 1C are shown in Fig. 3 (from here on, before
deformation and after deformation indicate microstructures
taken from grip and gauge regions of tensile specimen, respec-
tively). In Fig. 3 , red and green phases indicate alpha and beta
phases, respectively, and black lines indicate high angle grain
boundary ( 4151).
Beta phase (green-colored region) volume fraction in Ti–6Al–
4Fe–0.1Si alloy was found to be /C24 24% while that of Ti–6Al–4V
and Ti–3Al–2.5V alloys was /C24 4% and /C24 1%, respectively. The
initial grain size of alpha phase before deformation ( Fig. 3 a–c)

Bright ﬁeld TEM images of Ti–3Al–2.5V, Ti–6Al–4V and
Ti–6Al–4Fe–0.1Si alloys after deformation at 700 1C were shown
in Fig. 4 . Grain size of the alloys after deformation was reduced
compared to that before deformation ( Fig. 3 a–c); however, grain
boundary structure was quite different in Ti–3Al–2.5V alloy as it
has signiﬁcant sub-grains by dislocations ( Fig. 4a). It may indicate
that grain boundary sliding was suppressed in Ti–3Al–2.5V alloy
while other alloys were mainly deformed by grain boundary
sliding. Grain sizes after deformation at 700 1C of Ti–3Al–2.5V,
Ti–6Al–4V and Ti–6Al–4Fe–0.1Si alloys were 1.03, 0.94, 0.54 mm,
respectively. The grain size from TEM images is the average value
obtained from more than 100 grains including sub-grains, there-
fore, it is lower than that from EBSD scans in which only high
angle grain boundaries were observed ( Fig. 3 ). Comparing higher
magniﬁcation images of Ti–6Al–4V and Ti–6Al–4Fe–0.1Si alloys
(Fig. 4e, f), beta phase grain in Ti–6Al–4Fe–0.1Si alloy (e.g., center
grain in (f)) was observed more frequently than in Ti–6Al–4V
alloy. This was originated from the strong beta stabilizing effect of
Fe addition compared to V. Selected area diffraction pattern
(SADP) image (the right side image of the insets in Fig. 4 f)
conﬁrms that the center grain in Fig. 4f has a body centered cubic
(BCC) structure indicating a beta phase grain.

Another plausible cause is suppression of cavity formation during
deformation.Fig. 6 shows the distribution of cavities formed during
superplastic deformation of Ti–6Al–4V and Ti–6Al–4Fe–0.25Si
alloys. It is noticed that Ti–6Al–4Fe–0.25Si alloys have much lower
number density and volume fraction of cavities than Ti–6Al–4V
alloy. Suppression of cavity formation indicates excellent accom-
modation of grain boundary sliding. The reason for this ﬁnding is
due to the higher volume fraction of beta phase. Accommodation
mechanisms can be explained by (i) triple-edge (point) folds,
(ii) diffusion process or (iii) slip as reported in [12].B e t ap h a s eh a s
a larger number of slip systems than alpha phase and also its
diffusivity is /C24 100 times higher than that of alpha phase [9].
In addition, the boundary characteristic is also important. As shown
in Fig. 3 , Ti–6Al–4Fe–0.1Si alloy has much larger area fraction of
alpha–beta boundaries than Ti–6Al–4V and Ti–3Al–2.5V alloys.
According to [9], internal-strength parameter (
sn) of alpha–alpha
boundaries is /C24 2 times higher than that of alpha–beta boundaries.
Thus, dislocation can overcome the obstacles and easily accommo-
date the geometric mismatch at alpha–beta boundaries. Therefore, it
is suggested that large volume fraction of beta phase retarded cavity
initiation and linkage to others, which results in large elongation
without early cracking. In addition, the friction stress parameter (Sg)
of boundaries was highest in the single-phase alpha alloy and the
lowest in the two-phase alloy[9]. This indicates that grain-boundary
sliding occurs preferentially at alpha–beta boundaries than at
alpha–alpha boundaries. The adv antage of beta–alpha grains in
grain boundary sliding was schematically illustrated in Fig. 7 a,b.
From Fig. 7, grain boundary sliding can easily occur when beta phase
places adjacent to alpha grains due to easier slip along alpha–beta
boundaries. Therefore, Ti–6Al–4Fe alloy which has higher beta phase
volume fraction showed/C24 2 times larger elongation than Ti–6Al–4V
alloy even though grain size of the present alloys was similar.
Besides, Si addition on Ti–6Al–4Fe alloys is not very effective in
the high-temperature behavior since Ti–6Al–4Fe–0.1Si and Ti–6Al–
4Fe–0.25Si alloys showed results similar with Ti–6Al–4Fe alloy
(Fig. 2 b). Strength of Si-added alloys was slightly decreased than
Ti–6Al–4Fe alloy while elongation was similar in three alloys with/
without Si addition. Thus, it can be concluded that strong beta
stabilizer addition such as Fe is most important in high temperature
deformation.
4. Conclusions
The high temperature (700 1C) deformation behavior of
Ti–6Al–4Fe, Ti–6Al–4Fe–0.1Si and Ti–6Al–4Fe–0.25Si alloys as
well as commercial Ti–6Al–4V and Ti–3Al–2.5V alloys was inves-
tigated by conducting tensile test. From the careful observation of
EBSD and TEM, the original grain size of the alloys was quite
similar, however, new alloys in this study with Fe additions have
higher volume fraction of beta phase. Moreover, grain boundary
sliding occurred easily in Ti–6Al–4Fe alloys due to higher beta
phase volume fraction. However, in Ti–3Al–2.5V alloy, grain
boundary sliding was suppressed resulting in different grain
boundary structures with higher density of dislocations. In this
study, superplasticity was achieved using a relatively cheaper
element and new alloys will be possibly used as commercial
alloys.
