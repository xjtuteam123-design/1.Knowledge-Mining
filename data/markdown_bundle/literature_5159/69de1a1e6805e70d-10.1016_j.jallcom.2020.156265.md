Abstract 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) usually have a high strength at elevate temperatures. 
However, their oxidation resistance is inferior for practical applications. Here we proposed a 
eutectic design of compositionally complex eutectic alloy (CCEA) by adding 10 at. % Ta to the 
Al 2Co 28 Cr 20 Fe 25 Ni 25 , the resulting alloy shows a good combination of m echanical properties and 
oxidation resistance, e.g. a yield strength of 764 MPa and an oxidation resistance rate of 0.0031 
mg cm -2h-1 at 800 °C, which outperforms those of RHEAs. The e xcellent oxidation resistance is 
attributable to the formation of protective Cr 2O3 and CrTaO 4 layer at the surface, which prevents 
the matrix from further contact with O 2. 
 
Keyword: Compositionally complex alloy; Eutectic alloy; Oxidation behavior; Phase transition; 
Microstructure 
 
 
1. Introduction 
Recently, high-entropy alloys (HEAs), a new alloyin g concept involving with multiple 
principal elements have been proposed [1-3]. The de signing notion of HEAs is to explore a wide 
range of novel metallic materials with interesting mechanical properties [4, 5]. HEAs generally 
are initially identified as single-phase materials stabilized by their high mixing entropies. 
Nevertheless, the HEAs with a single phase solid so lution structure usually possess a larger 
ductility but lower strength compared with the bcc HEAs which usually have a higher strength

## Page 4

but limited ductility. As the expansion the notion of the HEAs into multiphase structure to 
improve the strength-ductility trade-off has led to the development of various sub-groups of 
HEAs such as non-equiatomic HEAs, eutectic HEAs (EH EAs) and complex concentrated alloys 
(CCAs) [6, 7]. Among these alloy systems, the HEAs comprising of refractory elements 
(hereafter referred as “RHEAs”) have attracted grea t attentions owing to their promising high-
temperature mechanical properties [8-10], which may find applications at elevate temperatures. 
In spite of their high strengths, the RHEAs usually suffer from severe oxidation at high 
temperatures due to the strong chemical affinity be tween refractory elements and oxygen, 
resulting in the formation of volatile and fragile oxide phases at surface, which hinders the 
practical high-temperature applications [11, 12]. A lthough previous works have demonstrated 
alloying elements can improve the oxidation resista nce of the RHEAs. For instance, elements 
such Si enhances the oxidation resistance through f orming oxidation-resist phase in the alloy 
[13]; addition of Al , Cr improves the oxidation resistance through prom oting the formation of 
protective oxide layers [14-16]. However, the impro ved oxidation resistance of the RHEAs by 
minor elemental additions are marginal and insignificant. 
To address the issue of poor oxidation resistance o f the RHEAs, we propose to redesign the 
HEA using eutectic notion by elemental addition of Ta to the non-equimolar Al 2Co 28 Cr 20 Fe 25 Ni 25 
into a compositional complex eutectic alloy. The specific composition of the present alloy was 
selected according to the previous study that incre asing the Co and Ni content in the CoCrFeNi 
alloy is benefit to increase the high temperature p roperties [17]. In addition, adding Ta to the 
base HEAs promotes the formation of an eutectic str ucture that is also beneficial for high 
temperature properties. The additional content of T a is located at the composition of binary 
eutectic point through the calculation of mixing en thalpy, binary phase diagrams and Thermal-
Calculation. We have demonstrated in our previous w ork that the present alloy shows a 
comparable high-temperature strength compared to th ose of RHEAs owing to the formation of 
the eutectic structure [17, 18]. The oxidation resi stance of the current alloy is expected to 
improve significantly compared to those of the RHEA s, because only minor refractory elements 
and significant amounts of Cr are being added. In t his study, we aim to investigate the 
microstructure and oxidation behavior of a non-equimolar Al 1Co 25 Cr 18 Fe 23 Ni 23 Ta 10 alloy.

2. Experiments 
The pre-alloyed high purity Co, Cr, Fe, Ni, Ta and Al powders (>99.99 wt.%) were prepared 
by gas atomization process. The powders were then m ixed with a composition of Al 1Co 25 
Cr 18 Fe 23 Ni 23 Ta 10 and filled into a stainless-steel tank with dimens ions of d 50 mm × h 120 mm 
and extruded at 1423 K with an extrusion ratio of 5 :1. The detailed information on the gas 
atomization and hot extrusion can be found in our previous work [18]. The chemical composition 
of the gas-atomized powders and as-extruded alloy w ere measured by inductively coupled 
plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-OES) and is summarize d in Table 1 . The oxidation tests were 
carried out at 600 °C, 800 °C, 1000 °C and 1200 °C under air atmosphere for 0 h, 25 h, 50 h, 100 
h, respectively. The specimens were then cooled in air after oxidation. X-ray diffraction (XRD, 
ISO-DEBYEFLEX 3003) measurements on the surface and cross section of the samples were 
performed using a Qualitative Phase Analysis system with Co K α ( λ = 1.78897 Å) radiation. 
Microstructure observations were performed using a Zeiss-Crossbeam XB 1540 at an 
acceleration voltage of 30 kV and current of 2 A. The back-scattered electron detector (BSE) was 
used for SEM imaging. The elemental distributions of the powders and as-extruded alloy were 
investigated by using energy-dispersive X-ray spect roscopy (EDS) by the Zeiss-Merlin 
instrument at microscale.

the gas-atomized powders reveals the eutectic struc ture with ultrafine inter-lamellar spacing of

to the reduction in surface energy.

exposure in air at different temperatures. The allo ys exhibit a lustrous and smooth surface at

Figure 3 displays the cross-sectional and surface XRD spect ra of the samples after 
oxidation exposure. The alloy exhibits a similar ph ase constitution to those of the powders and

identified by a Co 0.6 Ta 0.4 phase appeared at 1000 °C. The intensity of the pe aks become more 
pronounced after oxidizing at 1200 °C. Meanwhile, t he peaks of Cr 2O3 can be detected at the 
surface of the alloys in all the samples ( Fig. 3b ), the peak intensity ratio reduces with increasing 
temperature. This peak reduction could be attribute d to the volatilization of Cr 2O3 at higher 
temperatures. Further, the CrTaO 4 phase appears in the alloy at the temperature above 1000 °C 
and a multi-oxide product consisting of the Cr 2O3, CrTaO 4 and Fe(Fe 0.25 Ta 0.75 )2O6 phases were 
identified at 1200 °C.

oxidization at 1000 °C, the Laves phase which was p reviously identified at lower temperatures 
displayed different contrast, with a brighter phase near the surface and a darker phase beneath the 
surface. The brighter phase with a composition of C o 0.6 Ta 0.4 is considered to be transformed 
from the Laves phase according to structural and ch emical analysis using XRD and EDS 
(summarized in Table 4). From the table, the compositions of Ta in the brighter phase and darker 
phase are estimated to be 44.3 at. % and 31.5 at. % , respectively, which are close to 
stoichiometric compositions of Co 0.6 Ta 0.4 and Laves phase (Co 2Ta). Interestingly, the Co 0.6 Ta 0.4 
phase only appears near the surface of the alloys. This suggests that the driving force for the 
phase transition arises from temperature and chemic al effects. The latter could be related to 
enhanced elemental partitioning between Ta and the other elements. To prove this, the ratio of 
the elements in Co 0.6 Ta 0.4 and Laves is compared in the 1200 °C/100 h alloys (Table 4 ). The 
ratio of Co, Ni and Fe is found to be close to 1.3, whereas Cr increases sharply to 1.81. This 
suggests that the Co 0.6 Ta 0.4 phase loses Cr during the oxidation, resulting in the phase transition 
from Laves phase to the Co 0.6 Ta 0.4 . Hereafter, the Laves phase and Co 0.6 Ta 0.4 phase are denoted 
as Ta-rich phases compared with the FCC matrix phas e. Further, a rapid coarsening of the Ta-
rich phase is observed for the alloys oxidized at 1 200 °C. The average grain size of the Ta-rich 
phases increased from 2.3 μm at 1000 °C to 5.4 μm at 1200 °C after oxidation for 100 h. In 
addition, the depth of the Co 0.6 Ta 0.4 zone (367.1 μm) also increases significantly compares to the 
alloys after oxidation at 1000 °C (37.5 μm). A region without Ta-rich phase appears beneath the 
outmost oxidation layer at 1200 °C (Fig. 4d ). 
Table 5 shows a comparison of oxidation behaviors between several typical RHEAs, 
HEAs and presently studied CCEA at different temper atures. The oxidation resistance of the 
presently studied CCEA is superior than those of th e RHEAs [23-29]. Figure 5 displays a figure

considered responsible for the outstanding oxidatio n resistance of CCEA at 600 °C and 800 °C 
(Fig. 5a ). The passivating Cr 2O3 gives a good protection to the alloy by preventing the formation 
of Ta oxides. When the oxidation temperature is inc reased to 1000 °C, the concentration of Cr in 
the FCC phase decreases from 20.57 at. % at the Co 0.6 Ta 0.4 -Laves interface to 16.5 at. % at the 
oxides-matrix interface according to the EDS result s ( Fig. 5b ). This can be attributed to the 
volatilization of the Cr 2O3 at the surface which accelerates the Cr diffusion. As a result, the 
Cr 2O3 is destabilized and not capable to fully cover the alloy surface. The presence of CrTaO 4 is

4. Conclusions 
To conclude, the high temperature oxidation resista nce of a non-equimolar Al 1Co 25 
Cr 18 Fe 23 Ni 23 Ta 10 compositionally complex eutectic alloy was studied . The above results reveal a 
good oxidation performance for the Ta-containing CC EA outperforming many RHEAs during 
oxidation in air at temperature ranging from 600 °C to 1200 °C. The good oxidation behavior is 
attributable to the densely formed Cr 2O3 at lower temperature and protective CrTaO 4 at higher 
temperatures, which prevents the alloys from further oxidation.
