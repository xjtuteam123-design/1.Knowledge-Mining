A novel high entropy alloy with the composition of CoFeNi2V0.5Mo0.2, shows unusual strain hardening ability and
an excellent combination of ductility and strength. Especially at 873 K, the elongation can reach 189.2%, almost
three times that of the common high temperature alloys. Microstructural characterization suggests a coexistence
of planar slip and cross slip. The resistance of planar slip serves to strengthen the alloy, while the cross slip effec-
tively relieves strain concentration, substantially contributing to the uniform elongation. Also, the interaction of
planar slip and cross slip will lead to the formation of two kinds of dislocation loops.

Recently, a new type of alloys, high entropy alloys (HEAs) or multi-
principal-element alloys consisting of at least ﬁve metallic elements
with each amount about 5 –35 at.% [1,2], have seen a research upsurge
due to the possibility of producing a material with combined high
strength, high ductility and good fracture toughness [3,4]. The main de-
formation mechanisms of these HEAs have been researched thoroughly
by several scholars. Zhang et al. [ 5] reported a twinning-induced
plasticity (TWIP) of CoCrNi alloy, in which hierarchical twin network
not only presents substantial barriers for dislocation motion, thus
contributing to its high strength and signi ﬁcant strain hardening, but
also provides multiple pathways for the easy motion of dislocations
which permits the simultaneous generation of signi ﬁcant plastic
deformation. Li et al. [ 6] proposed a transformation induced plasticity
(TRIP) of Fe50Mn30Co10Cr10 HEA, including face-centered cubic
(FCC) matrix and newly emerged hexagonal close-packed (HCP) struc-
tured nanolaminates. The increased density of phase boundaries due to
transformation creates additional obstacles of dislocation slip, thereby
contributing to the strain hardening. While the newly emerged HCP
phase plays an important part in plastic accommodation at later stages
of deformation which conversely improves the ductility.
In this work, we propose a novel CoFeNi
2V0.5Mo0.2 HEA with single-
phase FCC structure, which simultaneously achieves high ductility, good
strength, and continuous strain hardening, even at 1073 K. In order to
investigate the original properties and develop a low-cost engineering
material, all of samples are taken directly from bulk as-cast (AC) ingots.
Observations suggest that the high strength and ductility can be
achieved by simple strain hardening, through cooperation of planar
slip and cross slip of dislocations. We seek to demonstrate that the
resistance of planar slip serves to strengthen the alloy, while the cross
slip effectively relieves strain concentration, contributing to substan-
tially uniform ductility. Furthermore, we note that through careful
adjusting stacking fault energy (SFE) of HEA system, this mechanism
can be ﬁne-tuned, which will shed light on exploring superior proper-
ties of HEAs.
The bulk CoFeNi2V0.5Mo0.2 HEA ingot weighing 2.5 kg was fabricated
by a vacuum medium frequency induction melting furnace. The details
can be found in Supplementary Note 1. The microstructures of the
specimens were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM,
Zeiss supra 55). The SFE was estimated by ﬁrst principle calculations.
The elemental distribution and microstructure were investigated by
micro-beam ﬂuorescence (μ-XRF) and micro-beam diffraction (μ-XRD)
analysis. Tensile tests were performed at six temperatures, i.e., 77,
203, 293, 873, 973, and 1073 K respectively at an initial strain rate
of 1 × 10 −3 s−1. The rod-like tensile specimens with a gauge length of
40 mm and diameter of 8 mm were used for tensile tests. The deformed
microstructures were characterized by a Cs-corrected JEOL 2100F

CoFeNi2V0.5Mo0.2 HEA at six temperatures. The room temperature

strength can still maintain at 455 MPa. However, a strong decrease in
strength with increasing temperature has been observed in most con-

For the RT tensile properties, the current CoFeNi 2V0.5Mo0.2 HEA shows
an unprecedented combination of strength and ductility, which is supe-
rior to the other HEAs and conventional alloys. Importantly, the results
of the other HEAs all come from samples after as-homogenized (AH) or
recrystallization (RC) treatment to eliminate casting defects. While the

the intended combination of strength and ductility can be tailored,
thus enlarging its application ﬁeld. In addition, it is worth mentioning
that the tensile properties at 873 K also exceed the other high tempera-
ture alloys by a large margin [3,12–20].

From above, two kinds of dislocation loops with different size have
been observed in the deformed samples of CoFeNi 2V0.5Mo0.2 HEA. The
large loops observed among long trace lines of edge-on slip planes have
a various size range, from 50 to 200 nm. And around the dislocation
loops, dislocation debris and bow-out con ﬁgurations are detected. On
the basis of these observations, the formation mechanism of large loops
is considered to be the offset double cross-slip mechanism (ODCS) [26].
That is when two dislocations gliding on parallel slip planes, with screw
portions cross-slipping towards each other on the offset plane, will lead
to the formation of a loop as well as two hairpin-like dislocations, the
schematic process is shown inFig. 4d. Thus, the loop size has uncertainty,
which is totally depending on the moving trajectory of screw parts.
Meanwhile, a series of small dislocation loops with diameter about
10 nm distributing regularly in line is observed. It should be noted
that in some deformation regions, the loop string is always found to
appear beside the long forest dislocations. Based on the superjog drag-
truncation mechanism (SJDT) [ 27], i.e., the jog can be cut through by
a forest dislocation on the conjugate plane, and leave behind a dislocation
loop (the schematic mechanism can be seen in Fig. 4e). It is reasonable
that in CoFeNi2V0.5Mo0.2 HEA, the arrays of jog dislocations are together
truncated by the coplanar forest dislocation which will give birth to a
series of loop strings. The deduced formation mechanism of loop strings
of CoFeNi2V0.5Mo0.2 HEA is displayed in Fig. 4f. Apparently, the forma-
tion condition of the two dislocation loops differs from each other.
One with pre-existing jog, and one doesn't need pre-existing jog, but
no matter for which situation high cross slip and planar slip ﬂexibility
are required.
In addition, the strain hardening behavior of the CoFeNi 2V0.5Mo0.2
HEA is also analyzed. As we known, the strain hardening of crystalline
materials is related to the increasing density of obstacles opposing dis-
location slip with increasing deformation. Main mechanisms can be
summarized as TRIP [ 28–30], TWIP [ 31,32] and micro-band induced
plasticity (MBIP) [33,34]. While for the present CoFeNi
2V0.5Mo0.2 HEA,
the excellent strain hardening ability as well as high strength and duc-
tility can be achieved by cooperation of planar slip and cross slip of dis-
locations. Indeed, the results show that neither phase transformation,
twinning nor micro-band can be observed in the tensile samples. It is
worth noting that in previous metal materials, the planar slip and
cross slip of dislocations alternate with each, i.e. planar slip appears at
low strain and cross slip takes over with increasing strain [ 25,35].
While in the present CoFeNi 2V0.5Mo0.2 HEA, even tensile to rupture,
the co-occurrence of cross slip and planar slip can still be observed.
The movement resistance of planar slip can strengthen the alloy,
while the high cross slip ability will relax strain concentration and
improve the ductility. From the other point of view, the dislocation
loop as the product of planar slip and cross slip will also contribute to
the outstanding mechanical properties. The interaction among loops
and the obstruction of loops to the other dislocations will increase the
strain hardening ability as well as the strength. In sharp contrast, the
movement of loops after interaction can coordinate the strain concen-
tration and enhance ductility. Compared with the other three mecha-
nisms, the present mechanism seems to be more effective at high
temperatures. As we known, MRIP mechanism is usually activated by
inhomogeneous plastic deformation at suf ﬁciently low temperatures
[30]. The TWIP mechanism can't work at high temperatures because
of the low possibility of twinning formation [36]. And the MBIP mecha-
nism also becomes ineffective at high temperatures, attributing to the
instability of dislocation band [37].
Taking the previous results in the literature into consideration, the
activation of these hardening mechanisms is strongly dependent
on the SFE. TRIP is observed in steels with very low SFE (below
20 mJ·m −2), TWIP is observed in steels with medium SFE
(20–40 mJ·m −2) and MBIP has been recently reported in steel with
high SFE (90 mJ·m −2). Thereby, the SFE of the CoFeNi 2V0.5Mo0.2 HEA
was calculated and the result turned out to be 50 mJ·m−2. It is reason-
able that high SFE will initiate dislocation band and low SFE can activate
