EFFECT OF SILICIDE COATINGS ON THE MECHANICAL 
PROPERTIES OF ALLOY 5VMTs 
P. I. Glushko,1 B. M. Shirokov,1,2 and A. V. Shiyan1 
UDC 539.3:621793 
The paper examines the effect of silicide coatings and testing media on the strength and plasticity of 
alloy 5VMTs between 25 and 1100°C. It is established that uncoated samples tested in argon have 
higher strength and plasticity over the entire te mperature range of interest than coated samples 
tested in argon and air. The major cause of decrease in the mechanical properties of coated samples 
is the silicide layer whose properties depend on testing temperature. 
Keywords: silicide, ultimate strength, elongation, alloy, temperature, coating. 
INTRODUCTION 
Alloy 5VMTs (grade 5V2MTs-2) of the Nb–W–Mo–Zr system belongs to heat -resistant solid solution-
strengthened low-alloy materials [1, 2]. The optimal doping content of the alloy promotes unique physicochemical 
and processing characteristics: high heat-resistance a nd low-temperature plasticity are combined with good 
weldability and machinability [3]. However, refractory metals and alloys on their basis cannot be used as structural 
materials in redox media without effici ent protective coatings. According to th e relevant literature, coatings based 
on silicides of refractory metals are best suited for the purpose. 
Many papers focus on the formation and stability of coatings [1–11]. A silicide layer may have a significant 
effect on the structure and mechanical properties of material . In this regard, it is of great importance to examine 
how silicide coatings influence the mechanical properties of the alloy. 
EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE 
The mechanical properties of allo y 5VMTs were determined on standard samples using a tensile testing 
machine developed at the Kharkov Institute of Physics an d Technology. The samples were siliconized in a silicon 
powder under a vacuum of 6.7 ⋅ 10–3 Pa at 1250 °C for 6 h [5]. Metallography, x-ray diffraction, and electron 
microprobe analysis were employed to examine the samples. 
Silicide coatings were deposited onto the alloy in two stages. A molybdenum layer (~35–40 µm) was 
deposited through thermal decomposition of Mo(CO) 6 and then the alloy was siliconized under vacuum. The 
coating consisted of a thin Nb 5Si3 layer (~3–5 µm) at the boundary with the alloy and of a basic external MoSi 2 
layer (~100 µm).

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
Figure 1 shows the microstructure of the coated alloy. The columnar structure of molybdenum disilicide is 
clearly seen in polarized light. 
Figure 2 shows how the coating and testing medium infl uence the mechanical prop erties of the alloy. The 
ultimate strength and offset yield stress of the uncoated sa mples are higher than those of the coated samples tested 
in argon and air. The mechanical properties of the coat ed samples mainly decrease because of the silicide layer 
whose properties depend on testing temperature. The coating is brittle at low temperatures and cracks under tension, 
thus decreasing strength. Moreover, the strength and plasticity of the coated samples tested in air are lower because 
oxides form in cracks. This leads to internal stresses that decrease the strength and plasticity of the alloy.

Silicide coatings influence elongation and contract ion and their temperature dependence (Fig. 3). The 
elongation of the samples tested in argon decreases from 16% at 25 °C, reaches its mini mum (~8%) at 700 °C, and 
increases to 20% at 1100 °C (Fig. 3a). The contraction of the samples tested in argon (Fig. 3b) decreases from 24% 
at 25 °C, reaches its minimum (~13%) at 500 °C, and increases to 30% at 1100 °C. Their variation is due to the 
coating that is brittle up to about 800°C, cracks under tension, and decreases the strength of the material. 
With increasing temperature, the elongation of the sa mples tested in air monotonically decreases from 14% 
at 25°C to 2% at 1100 °C and the contraction from 23% at 25 °C to 4% at 1100 °C. The variation in the elongation 
and contraction of the samples tested in air is due to the formation of cracks and oxides in them. 
CONCLUSIONS 
The results demonstrate that the mech anical properties of the alloy so mewhat decrease. This decrease is 
admissible and the silicide-coated alloy may be used in re dox media, while the uncoated alloy cannot be used in 
these media at high temperatures.
