ABSTRACT 
The intrinsic brittleness of tungsten (W) significantly restricts its application as the primary material for plasma- 
facing components. In this study, we propose a novel approach to improve both the strength and ductility of W- 
based alloys based on the synergistic effects between potassium (K) bubbles and high-energy-rate forging. K 
doping was introduced into W, followed by a two-step high-energy-rate forging (HERF) process. This leads to 
laminated matrix grains with refined sizes and deformation textures, as well as an increase in the density of low- 
angle grain boundaries and smaller size K bubbles along the grain boundaries. As a result, the prepared W alloys 
exhibit a substantially reduced ductile-to-brittle transition temperature within the range of room temperature to 
50 
◦
C, along with a notable ultimate tensile strength of up to 1393 MPa. Impressively, these alloys also 
demonstrate remarkable elongation at 200 
◦
C, reaching a value as high as 33.12%. The remarkable mechanical 
strength and exceptional ductility exhibited by these alloys highlight their considerable potential for commercial 
application in the field of nuclear fusion engineering. 
1. Introduction 
Tungsten (W) is considered as a highly promising material for 
serving as the primary plasma facing material (PFM) in the divertor and 
the first wall of fusion reactors. This is primarily due to its favorable 
properties, including high melting point (3410 
◦
C), excellent thermal 
conductivity (174 W/(m ⋅ K)) at room temperature, good creep resistance 
and low sputtering, etc. [ 1 – 3 ]. However, pure W exhibits a grain 
structure known as body-centered cubic (BCC), in which the plasticity is 
governed by the motion of non-planar core 1/2 〈 111 〉 screw dislocations 
[ 4 , 5 ]. This causes the intrinsic brittleness, low fracture toughness and 
high ductile-to-brittle transition temperature (DBTT) (typically 
> 400 
◦
C) [ 6 , 7 ]. As a result, the workability of W in nuclear energy field 
is severely restricted. 
To address the aforementioned challenges associated with W-based 
materials, several strategies have been developed to enhance both 
strength and ductility at lower temperatures. The incorporation of 
rhenium (Re) through solid solution alloying [ 8 , 9 ], as well as the 
reinforcement of carbides [ 10 – 13 ] and refractory oxides [ 14 – 17 ] 
through dispersion strengthening, are well-established methods in the 
field. Nevertheless, the aforementioned approach is costly and also 
generates substantial amounts of solid transmutation waste under 
neutron irradiation. The utilization of letter method has been found to 
have negative implications on thermal conductivity [ 18 ]. Furthermore, 
the presence of impurities such as oxygen (O), nitrogen (N), and phos -
phorus (P) impurities generated by the second-phase particles can lead 
to a reduction in the cohesion of grain boundaries, hence exerting a 
significant influence on the ductility of W-based alloys [ 19 ]. 
In comparison, potassium (K) doping offers a viable solution to 
mitigate the aforementioned side effects. During sintering process, K- 
doped W undergoes volatilization, resulting in the formation of nano- 
bubbles that contain K atoms. These nano-bubbles disperse along the 
grain boundaries. This process provides several advantages to K-doped 
W alloys. It is known that the thermal conductivity of W is determined 
by thermal diffusivity, specific heat, and density. Although K doping 
reduces thermal diffusivity, the specific heat remains high. As a result, K-

within K bubbles increases when the temperature exceeds the boiling 
point of K. The gaseous K inside the bubble generates a stress field at the 
gas/solid interface, enhancing the dispersion strengthening effects [ 23 ]. 
Despite these advantages, further enhancements in the strength and 
ductility of K-doped W at low temperatures are imperative in order to 
satisfy the criteria for its utilization as a structural substance in the 
commercial sector. 
In addition to chemical composition, plastic deformation processes 
such as rolling, hot forging, and swaging have demonstrated their effi -
cacy in enhancing the strength and ductility of W-based alloys [ 24 – 26 ]. 
Recently, high-energy-rate forging (HERF), a upset forging process 
driven by a high-speed pneumatic rammer powered by compressed air 
(tens of MPa), has been employed in W alloys [ 27 – 31 ]. Such striking 
with a high velocity could inhibit cooling of the metal when it flows over 
the cooler die surfaces and leading to greater grain refinement, thereby 
improving the strength and ductility [ 30 , 32 ]. In this study, we employ 
HERF to process K-doped W alloys to further enhance their mechanical 
properties at low temperatures. Our key strategy focuses on refining the 
grain size, adjusting the preferred orientation, and augmenting the 
dislocation density of the alloy through the synergistic effects between 
HERF and K bubbles. The density, hardness, and microstructure of the 
alloy are characterized, and the mechanical properties are evaluated via 
tensile tests. As anticipated, the K-doped W alloy exhibits exceptional 
strength and ductility, surpassing the performance of previously re -
ported W-based materials.

3. Results 
3.1. Density, hardness and grain microstructure 
The density and Vickers hardness values for S-AKS-W and HERF- 
AKS-W are presented in Table 2 . The relative density of S-AKS-W is 
found to be 92.3%, while HERF-AKS-W achieves a significantly higher 
relative density of up to 99.6%. This increase in density can be attributed 
to the reduction of residual pores during the sintering process, facilitated 
by the application of high pressure. Thus, it can be concluded that the 
HERF method effectively prepares high-density bulk AKS-W material. 
Moreover, it is worth noting that the Vickers hardness of HERF-AKS- 
W is approximately 457.4 HV, representing a 39% increase compared to 
S-AKS-W with a hardness of approximately 328.5 HV. This enhanced 
hardness can be attributed to three factors: 
i) The higher relative densities achieved in HERF-AKS-W, as dis -
cussed earlier. 
ii) The increased dislocation density resulting from the high energy 
imparted by the high-speed pneumatic rammer. 
iii) The grain refinement induced by the processing, which contrib -
utes to strengthening effects. 
These factors will be further investigated and substantiated in sub -
sequent sections.

4. Discussion 
As previously mentioned, the HERF-AKS-W alloy demonstrates 
exceptional mechanical properties, including high YS and UTS, as well 
as a low DBTT ranging between RT and 50 
◦
C. These favorable prop -
erties can be attributed to its unique chemical composition and 
microstructure. Notably, HERF-AKS-W differs from other W alloys in 
terms of the presence of a second dispersion phase K bubbles and the 
HERF processing method. Therefore, the synergistic effects of K bubbles 
and HERF on the strength and ductility are discussed in detail. 
4.1. K bubbles characteristics 
In this section, the characteristics of K bubbles after HERF processing 
are investigated to understand their role in the strength and ductility of 
W. SEM images of K bubbles in and S-AKS-W are presented in Fig. 6 (a) 
and (b). It is evident that K bubbles are randomly distributed along grain 
boundaries. Interestingly, no distinct bubble tubes or rows are observed 
in HERF-AKS-W. This distinguishes it from previously reported tradi -
tional K-doped W rods or wires, where spherical sinter bubbles are 
elongate and narrow into long ellipsoids [ 44 , 45 ] or maybe break up into 
bubble rows. This kind of bubbles formation typically involves several 
stages [ 22 ]. 
i) Bubble generation. During sintering, the dopants that have already 
been produced, such as potassium silicates and potassium aluminosili -
cates, migrate to the interparticle necks firstly [ 46 ]. At a temperature of 
approximately 1560 
◦
C, the thermodynamically unstable potassium 
silicates decompose and diffusing out of the matrix[ 47 ]. When the 
temperature exceeds 2100 
◦
C, more stable potassium aluminium sili -
cates decompose rapidly. By this time, the powder compacted has 
already been partly consolidated and the interconnected pores have 
been closed. Thus, only the elements Al and Si, characterized by their 
relatively lower atomic radii and strong solubility in W matrix, exhibit 
diffusion towards the surface. In contrast, K with a larger atomic radii 
and very low solubility remains in W matrix [ 47 , 48 ]. As a result, the 
trapped gaseous K produces nano-sized bubbles. 
ii) Bubble deformation. In subsequent hot deformation steps, such as 
swaging and drawing, the microstructure undergoes significant refine -
ment, resulting in the simultaneous deformation of K bubbles with the W 
matrix. Consequently, the bubbles are deformed into long ellipsoids or

In terms of the formation of K bubbles in HERF-AKS-W, the steps 
differ from those observed in traditional K-doped W rods or wires 
mentioned earlier. In this work, it ’ s notable that the temperature applied 
in sintering is 2045

incandescent lamps [ 46 ]. Consequently, a significant portion of potas -

As a result, the main component in bubbles is potassium aluminium 
silicate instead of K [ 46 ]. Furthermore, to investigate the deformation of 
K bubbles in HERF-AKS-W, their morphology was characterized, and 
corresponding images are given in Fig. 6 (a) and Fig. 6 (b). It can be 
observed that the bubbles in HERF-AKS-W are denser compared to those 
in S-AKS-W. Besides, the bubble sizes are measured and presented in 
Fig. 6 (c) and Fig. 6 (d). Their distributions follow a Gaussian distribu -
tion. The average bubble size in HERF-AKS-W is approximately 53 nm, 
which is comparatively less than the average bubble size seen in S-AKS- 
W, measuring around 149 nm (see Fig. 6 (d)). This suggests that the 
bubbles in the W matrix undergo substantial deformation during the 
HERF process. 
During the first strike, the matrix underwent the majority of the 
deformation, with a height reduction rate of 69.94%. This reduction rate 
accounts for approximately 79% of the overall decrease observed. Under 
such high strain rates, the large-sized K bubbles in the ingot experienced 
significantly deformed. In contrast to swaging or drawing processes that 
primarily lengthen materials in the axial direction, the HERF technique 
compresses spherical bubbles, resulting in the formation of flat ellipsoid 
shapes with aspect ratios exceeding 2 π , as illustrated in Fig. 7 (b). Be -
sides, during this process, the temperature reaches as high as 1550 
◦
C, 
close to the recrystallization temperature (RCT) of the AKS-W alloy [ 49 ]. 
Thus, under high strain rates and elevated temperature, the bubbles 
suffer from “ dynamic break-up ” due to Rayleigh instability. The similar 
phenomenon was also observed in our previous work [ 25 ]. However, the 
smaller bubbles distribute on grain boundaries randomly instead of 
forming bubble rows. Furthermore, under the balance between Laplace 
pressure and the gas pressure in the bubble, bubbles spheroidize and 
stabilize with an equilibrium size. 
In the second strike, the shape of the formed bubbles may undergo 
slight changes, as their size has already been significantly reduced in the 
previous process. It is widely accepted that if the aspect ratio (l/d) of the 
bubbles is far smaller than the critical aspect ratio of 2 π , they can 
maintain a spherical shape during processing [ 50 , 51 ]. While bubbles 
have the ability to maintain their form, their position within the W 
matrix may potentially be altered as a result of additional deformation of 
the matrix during the second step ( Fig. 6 (c)). After two-step HERF, 
numerous small bubbles with dispersed distributions finally appear in 
the HERF-AKS-W matrix as depicted in Fig. 7 (c). 
4.2. Strengthening and ductilizing mechanisms 
Considering the doping of W with K and the subsequent processing 
by HERF in this study, the high strength primarily arises from grain 
boundary strengthening and grain refinement brought by both HERF 
and K bubbles. Xie et al. [ 28 ] fabricated bulk pure W products using low- 
temperature sintering followed by the HERF process. They observed that 
during the initial stages of deformation, dislocation tangling occurred, 
leading to work hardening. However, as the dislocation density 
increased, dynamic recovery became evident due to the temperature 
employed in HERF being close to the RCT of W. Consequently, poly -
gonization of dislocations accelerated, resulting in the formation of 
polygonised dislocation walls (PDWs) within the grains. These PDWs

Thus, it can be concluded that the application of HERF results in a high 
volume of grain boundaries, resulting in the reduction in grain size. This 
observation aligns with the grain size distribution depicted in Fig. 3 . As 
anticipated by the Hall-Petch relationship, this grain refinement leads to 
high strength in HERF-AKS-W. 
The presence of K bubbles contributes to grain refinement by effec -
tively pinning grain boundaries. During processing at high elevated 
temperatures, these bubbles hinder the movement of boundaries, thus 
preventing grain coarsening. It is important to note that the residual 
large K bubbles in the sintered ingot break up into smaller bubbles 
during HERF, resulting in a significant reduction in bubble size and an 
increased number density. This further enhances the strengthening ef -
fect [ 52 ]. The enhancement of grain boundary strength is additionally 
linked to the purification of grain borders through the presence of K 
bubbles. The process of K-doping results in the introduction of a small 
number of impurities at the grain boundaries. These impurities include K 
at a concentration of 83 ppm, O at a concentration of 53 ppm, Al and 
silicon Si at concentrations of ≤ 20 ppm, as indicated in Table 1 . Due to 
the limited presence of impurities and the abundance of grain bound -
aries, the concentration of impurities within the boundaries remains 
very low. This enhances grain boundary cohesion and reduces inter -
granular brittleness [ 14 ]. Consequently, the fracture surfaces exhibit 
transgranular cleavage cracking and knife-like characteristics, as 
depicted in Fig. 5 (a-b) and Fig. 5 (c-e), respectively.

ductility of BCC metals. However, most reported W-based metals exhibit 
improved ductility [ 15 , 25 , 29 , 58 ], contrary to basic work hardening 
theory. The essential reason for this may be the increased density of edge 
or mixed-type dislocations, which are more active and have higher 
mobility[ 28 ]. 
To sum up, both the HERF process and K doping positively impact 
the strength and ductility of W. HERF not only increases the density of 
grain boundaries but also reduces the size of K bubbles. The synergistic 
effects between HERF and K bubbles result in both grain boundary 
strengthening and grain refinement, significantly improving the 
strength of the W matrix. Additionally, HERF and K bubbles lower the 
DBTT, and promote the dislocation slip by increasing the density of edge 
or mixed-type dislocations. This contributes to the improved ductility of 
the W matrix. Moreover, HERF effectively increases the volume of 
LAGBs, alters the preferred orientation, and provides better strain 
compatibility while reducing the resistance to dislocation slip, further 
enhancing the ductility of the W matrix. Through these multi-level 
microstructural optimizations, exceptionally high ductility and 
strength are achieved in HERF-W. 
5. Conclusion 
In conclusion, a high-performance HERF-AKS-W disk has been suc -
cessfully prepared through introduction of K dopant into W, followed by 
a two-step HERF procedure. During HERF, the large K bubbles present in 
the ingot are fragmented into smaller bubbles, leading to a more 
compact arrangement. This phenomenon gives rise to synergistic effects 
that play a role in the distinctive microstructures found in HERF-AKS-W. 
The grains have undergone elongation and refinement along the radial 
directions, accompanied by the appearance of preferred orientations of 
< 111 > and < 100 > . Moreover, there has been a significant increase in 
the density of grain boundaries, particularly low-angle grain boundaries, 
as well as an increase in dislocations with a higher propensity for slip -
ping. As a result, HERF-AKS-W exhibits exceptional strength and 
ductility. Specifically, the ultimate tensile strength at room temperature 
reaches 1393 MPa, while the total elongation is remarkably high at 
19.62% at 100

◦
C. Furthermore, the ductile-to- 
brittle transition temperature is lower than 50

materials hold great potential for use as PFMs in fusion reactors. Addi -
tionally, this study provides a novel strategy for the design of other re -
fractory metals with a combination of excellent strength and ductility.
