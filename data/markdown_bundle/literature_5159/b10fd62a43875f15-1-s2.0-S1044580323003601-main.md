1. Introduction 
Wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) has the advantages of 
wide material sources, low equipment cost and high efficiency of 
forming large size parts [ 1 – 3 ]. Thus, this technique has been used to 
produce parts from numerous metallic alloys including Ni-based su -
peralloys and stainless steels [ 4 – 6 ]. On the basis of WAAM, multi-wire 
arc additive manufacturing (MWAAM) with multiple wire feeder 
further broadens the application range of WAAM [ 7 , 8 ]. Compare with 
WAAM technology of single wire feed, the raw wire of MWAAM can 
have the same composition to increase production efficiency, but also 
can have different composition to synthesize new materials or prepare 
multi-material alloy components [ 9 , 10 ]. In addition, MWAAM tech -
nology can avoid frequent replacement of welding wires to achieve 
continuous deposition of various materials in the integrated additive 
manufacturing of multiple metal materials. Therefore, MWAAM tech -
nology provides a new way for metal multi-material additive 
manufacturing. 
Ti6Al4V (TC4) alloy with high strength, corrosion resistance and 
toughness are widely used in aerospace, medical equipment, national 
military, and industrial automotive fields [ 11 , 12 ]. NiTi alloy has shape 
memory, super elasticity and high damping properties, which can be 
widely used in engineering and medicine because of its three irre -
placeable characteristics [ 13 , 14 ]. In addition to these three character -
istics, NiTi alloy also has good corrosion resistance, durable fatigue 
resistance and non-magnetic properties, and can respond and change at 
human body temperature, suitable for most fields of application [ 15 ]. 
These highly adaptable metal materials are increasingly used in complex 
environments where specific materials are required. Integrating the 
properties of TC4 and NiTi into a single metal component can not only 
improve the multifunctional properties of the material, but also increase 
the utilization rate of the material. However, successfully connecting 
TC4 and NiTi alloys is extremely challenging due to the differences in 
physical and chemical properties between the two materials [ 16 ]. If the 
traditional welding method is adopted, a large amount of intermetallic 
compounds will be formed in the welded joint, and the coefficient of 
thermal expansion between these metal components is different result -
ing in a very large stress concentration of the welded joint, and even the 
failure of cracking [ 17 ]. To date, researchers have made some efforts to 
address the challenges of connecting Ti6Al4V to NiTi. Deng et al. [ 18 ] 
studied the microstructure and mechanical property of NiTi/TC4 joints 
by back-heating assisted friction stir welding. The results showed that

2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Materials and experimental setup 
In this work, TC4, NiTi and pure Nb ( > 99.95%) wires with a 
diameter of 1.2 mm were used as raw materials to fabricate the bionic 
layered heterogeneous alloy. The chemical compositions of TC4, NiTi 
and Nb wires are shown in Table 1 . TC4 plate (150 mm × 150 mm × 5 
mm) with were used as the build substrate. 
The experimental setup in this work was developed by the MWAAM 
system, which was consisted of a 6-axis ABB robot arm with a IRC5 
controller, a shielding gas unit and wire feeders. The MWAAM system is 
presented in Fig. 1 b-c. The other details were introduced in our previous 
publication [ 8 ]. The bionic layered heterogeneous multi-material alloy 
component with dimensions of 80 × 8 × 40 mm

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Compositional analysis of TC4-Nb-NiTi heterogeneous alloy 
In order to study the mutual diffusion behavior of alloy elements at 
the TC4-Nb-NiTi multi-material alloy interface, EDS line scanning was 
analyzed, and the results were shown in Fig. 3 a. The thickness of the Nb 
interlayer of TC4-Nb-NiTi alloy component sample was about 1.2 mm. 
In the Nb layer region, the signals of other elements were very weak, 
indicating that Nb interlayer acts as a good barrier effect. In addition, it 
can be found that the transition of Nb element on the side of TC4 alloy 
was relatively gentle compared with that on the side of NiTi alloy by 
comparing the changed of Nb element on both sides of the Nb inter -
mediate layer. This was due to the addition of a gradient layer between 
TC4 and Nb layers in the previous structural design. The gentle diffusion 
of elements contributed to the metallurgical bonding between dissimilar 
materials, thereby enhancing the bonding strength between interfaces 
[ 25 ]. Moreover, there was a large fluctuation in the Ti and Nb element 
curves in the TC4 alloy side, which meant that a strong mixing occurred 
between the two elements, as shown in Fig. 3 a. In order to more clearly 
observe the distribution of elements between TC4 and Nb layers, the EDS 
maps was carried out on the TC4 alloy side, the results as shown in 
Fig. 3 b. It was obvious from the Fig. 3 b that the four elements presented 
a gradient distribution on the TC4 alloy side. From TC4 layer to Nb layer, 
Ti, Al and V elements gradually decrease, while Nb elements gradually 
increase. In addition, the thickness of gradient layer TC4/Nb was about 
800 μ m. 
In order to investigate the influence of multi wire integrated additive 
manufacturing methods on the composition, electron probe micro -
analyzer (EPMA) was used to study the composition distribution on both 
sides of Nb interlayer. The distribution map of Ti, Al, and Nb elements on 
the TC4 alloy side is shown in Fig. 3 c. It can be seen that component 
segregation occurred locally in the TC4/Nb gradient layer. This was due 
to the two wires were (TC4 wire and Nb wire) fed into the molten pool at 
the same time, resulting in insufficient local metallurgical reaction, 
which caused the phenomenon of composition segregation. However, 
the TC4/Nb gradient layer was thin and the segregation region was

3.3. Microstructure of TC4-Nb-NiTi heterogeneous alloy 
The microstructure evolution on both sides of the Nb interlayer is 
shown in Fig. 5 . Table 3 lists the EDS results at different points in Fig. 5 . 
Fig. 5 a-d shows the microstructure of different regions between the Nb 
layer and TC4 layer. As shown in Fig. 5 d, the microstructure of TC4 layer 
was mainly a basketweave structure formed by the interlacing 
arrangement of lamellar α . As can be seen from the Fig. 5 d, the lamellar 
α was relatively large in length and width, the overall structure was 
typical α and β bi-phase titanium alloy structure. The microstructure of 
MWAAM TC4 alloy had been described in detail in previous articles 
[ 27 ]. Compared with Fig. 5 d, the fraction of the lamellar α phase in the 
lower part of Fig. 5 c gradually decreased, and the short rod-like α phase 
appeared. This difference was due to their location in different areas of 
the TC4 layer, which was closely related to heat dissipation conditions. 
When TC4/Nb layer was deposited in the top region of TC4 layer, Nb 
element in TC4/Nb layer would be diffused around the TC4-TC4/Nb 
interface region due to the remelting effect of TC4/Nb layer, and the 
interface with heterogeneous microstructure was formed, as shown in 
Fig. 5 b. With the increase of Nb content in TC4/Nb layer, α phase size 
gradually decreased and β phase dominated bi-phase microstructure was 
formed. This was due to that Nb element was a β -phase stable element, 
and Nb element can form an infinite solid solution with β -Ti and finite 
solid solution with α -Ti when the amount of Nb in the deposit increased 
[ 28 ]. In addition, no obvious interface or boundary was found in TC4- 
TC4/Nb layer due to the continuous state of Nb content. The micro -
structure of TC4/Nb layer was mainly acicular α phase, and the lamellar 
α phase disappeared. Usually, the upper layer microstructure of the 
sedimentary layer will be coarser than the lower layer due to the thermal 
cycle during multilayer deposition of additive manufacturing alloy 
components process [ 29 ]. However, the α phase underwent a refinement 
process throughout the TC4/Nb layer, from acicular structure ( Fig. 5 c) 
to thin acicular structure ( Fig. 5 b). This may be due to the diffusion 
effect of elements in Nb layer with high Nb content, which made the top 
region of TC4/Nb layer contained more Nb than the bottom region of 
TC4/Nb layer. In addition, this phenomenon further proved that Nb 
element can significantly refine the α microstructure in titanium alloys.

3.4. Crystallographic texture of TC4-Nb-NiTi heterogeneous alloy 
In order to study the crystallographic characteristics of TC4-Nb-NiTi 
multi-material components, five regions were selected from TC4 layer to 
NiTi layer for analysis. The EBSD orientation maps of different regions is 
shown in Fig. 6 . The grain orientation diagram showed the orientation 
relationship between different grains with different colors and different 
orientation. Red represented the 〈 001 〉 direction, blue represented the 
〈 111 〉 direction, and green represented the 〈 101 〉 direction. As can be 
seen from Fig. 6 a, most of the TC4 layers were lamellar α phases. Many 
adjacent and parallel layers fused together to form a larger region, that 
was the colony α structure. It was a relatively large phase in the grain 
orientation diagram, and the size of these colony α had a very important 
effect on the mechanical properties of the alloy component [ 30 ]. In some 
regions, the lamellar α phase grew with a certain orientation, which 
formed the basketweave structure. The phase evolution in the titanium 
alloy component deposited by the WAAM follows the Burgers orienta -
tion relationship [ 8 ], i.e. (0001)
α
// (110)
β
. Based on Burgers orientation 
relationship, it can be known that an individual body centered cubic 
crystal can be transformed into 12 hexagonal close packed crystals with 
different orientation from the original β crystal, that is, any one of the six 
(110) slip planes can be transformed into a (0001) crystallographic 
plane. Meanwhile, there are two different slip directions for the (0001) 
crystallographic plane. Therefore, these possible orientations make it 
possible to repeat the orientation of lamellar α many times, thus forming 
a net-like structure similar to a net basket. The selected location was 
near the TC4/Nb-Nb interface region, and the width of the lamellar α 
phase decreased obviously, as shown in Fig. 6 b. In addition, the large 
colony α phase was also found in this region. Most of the grains in the 
TC4/Nb layer were acicular, as shown in Fig. 6 b. Fig. 6 c shows the grain 
orientation distribution of Nb layer. Compared with TC4 and TC4/Nb 
layers, the grain shape of Nb layer changed significantly, and most of the 
grains were fragmentary. The grain orientation diagram of the region 
near the Nb-NiTi interface is shown in Fig. 6 d. Due to the eutectic 
structure formed there, the grain shape was irregular. Fig. 6 e shows the 
grain orientation diagram of the NiTi region, from which it can be seen 
that the NiTi layer was mainly composed of columnar dendrites. By 
observing the grain morphology in different regions of the alloy, it can 
be seen that the presence of Nb in the intermediate layer affected the 
grain variation at the TC4/Nb-Nb and Nb-NiTi interfaces. As we all 
known, when the grain orientations of polycrystalline metal materials

density of different regions was obtained by EBSD. The polar diagram of

tween TC4 layer and NiTi layer showed the strong {110} texture, and

9.38, respectively. It was reported that when the texture strength dis -

3.6. Compression behavior of TC4-Nb-NiTi heterogeneous alloy 
In order to evaluate the mechanical properties of MWAAM TC4-Nb- 
NiTi alloy, compression test was carried out on the sample. The 
compression stress-strain curves of MWAAM TC4-Nb-NiTi heteroge -
neous alloy were shown in Fig. 9 a. The fracture stress for four specimens 
were 1419.87 MPa, 1211.71 MPa, 1193.33 MPa and 1059.01 MPa, and 
the fracture strain were 22.12%, 20.47%, 19.25 and 17.43%, respec -
tively. The cyclic tensile curve was characterized by a 2% strain incre -
ment followed by a stress release until the strain of the sample at room 
temperature reached 10%. The result was shown in Fig. 9 b. During the 
loading and unloading process of TC4-Nb-NiTi sample, no stress plat -
form was observed in each curve. The disappearance of the platform was 
related to the heterogeneous combination of materials. Xu et al. [ 33 ] 
found a similar phenomenon when studying the effect of rare earth CeO
2 
on laser powder bed fusion of NiTi alloy. They found that when the 
addition of CeO
2 
increased to 3%, the curve stress platform disappeared 
due to stress-induced martensitic transformation.

shown in Fig. 9 c. In the compression test, a total of 10 load/unload

3.7. Tensile property of TC4-Nb-NiTi heterogeneous alloy 
The tensile stress-strain curve of MWAAM TC4-Nb-NiTi multi-ma -
terial is shown in Fig. 10 . The ultimate tensile stress (UTS) was 378.8 
MPa and the elongation (EL) was close to 3.09%. This was a significant 
improvement for TC4/NiTi alloy components with no intermediate 
layer, as they break with a large number of brittle phases when con -
nected directly and cannot be tested. In order to systematically reveal 
the fracture mechanism of tensile specimens, the fracture morphology 
was analyzed by scanning electron microscopy. Fig. 11 shows the frac -
ture morphologies of MWAAM TC4-Nb-NiTi alloy samples. As can be 
seen from Fig. 11 a, the macro fracture morphology of longitudinal 
tensile sample mainly presented a tear shape, and the two ends of the 
fracture showed a step shape. It was difficult to distinguish the fiber 
region, the radical region and the shear lip, and the corresponding 
characteristics were difficult to distinguish. In addition, the necking 
phenomenon of tensile specimens was not obvious. As can be seen from 
Fig. 11 b, there were obvious river patterns and cracks of different sizes 
in the fracture surface. There were also a few cleavage facet features of 
quasi cleavage surfaces, as shown in Fig. 11 c. In addition, some sec -
ondary cracks and dimples were found locally in the fracture surface, as 
shown in Fig. 11 d. Fig. 11 e-h shows the locally enlarged fracture 
morphology of the tensile specimen. The tear characteristics of the 
fracture surface of MWAAM TC4-Nb-NiTi alloy can be observed from 
Fig. 11 f and h. Moreover, the tearing edge had a certain directionality, 
which may be related to the formation of texture [ 36 ]. Therefore, the 
fracture mode of tensile specimens was a mixed fracture dominated by 
brittle fracture. 
Fig. 12 shows EDS maps and crack growth path and corresponding 
schematic diagram of the fracture of representative sample in transverse 
direction. Fig. 12 a shows the SEM image of crack propagation shape in 
MWAAM TC4-Nb-NiTi alloy sample. Combined with the distribution of 
elements in Fig. 12 a-d, the fracture location was in the eutectic region of 
the Nb-NiTi interface, which can be clearly observed. Through SEM 
amplification of the eutectic region locally, it can be observed that the 
crack growth process mainly occurred along the NiTi
2 
phase boundary, 
as shown in Fig. 12 e-h. In addition, microcracks, including secondary 
cracks, were observed in the eutectic region, which can consume large 
amounts of energy in crack propagation and significantly improve 
fracture toughness [ 37 ], as shown in Fig. 12 f. In particular, when the 
crack propagated near the (Nb, Ti) and NiTi eutectic blocks, the crack 
bypassed the NiTi phase by deflecting, as shown in Fig. 12 h. Fig. 12 i-l 
shows the corresponding schematic diagram with SEM, from which it 
can be seen that the crack mainly propagated in the NiTi
2 
phase. This 
can be attributed to the fact that NiTi
2 
phase with high hardness 
intensified the stress concentration phenomenon in eutectic region, 
which was easy to form dislocation plugging, provided favorable con -
ditions for the formation of pores, and accelerated the propagation of 
cracks [ 38 ]. Therefore, the crack growth path in Fig. 12 i-l was relatively 
flat. In this paper, TC4-Nb-NiTi alloy components were fabricated using 
a bionic layered structure, and the microstructure evolution, crystallo -
graphic structure, and mechanical properties of the alloy components 
were tested. In the following work, we will further improve the tensile 
strength of MWAAM TC4-Nb-NiTi alloy components by optimizing the 
thickness of the eutectic region between Nb and NiTi, providing an idea 
for achieving high-strength connections between multi-materials and 
multi-functional heterogeneous alloys. 
4. Conclusions 
In this study, the TC4-Nb-NiTi samples with bionic layered structure 
were successfully prepared by multi-wire arc additive manufacturing. 
The microstructure and mechanical properties of MWAAM TC4-Nb-NiTi 
alloy were revealed in detail. The main conclusions are summarized as 
follows.
