Similarly, the yield strength (00.2) is also as high as 845 MPa at RT and 420 MPa at 473 K. The high values of Of and remain unchanged even after annealing for 100 h at these testing temperatures. The fatigue limit after the cycles of 107 is also as high as 330 MPa at 293 K and 220 MPa at 473 K. The fatigue limit at RT is also 1.2 times as high as the highest value for conventional Al-based alloys. The high mechanical strengths and their good heat resistance are presumably due to the formation of a finely mixed structure con- sisting of A13Ni and Alli(La, Ce)3 particles with a size of 0.05 gm homogeneously embedded in an Al matrix with a grain size of 0.1 to 0.2 gm. In addition, the Al-Ni-Mm alloy also exhibits a low coeffcient of thermal expansion and good wear resistance because of the homogeneous dispersion of a large amount of the intermetallic compounds. The simultaneous achievement of good mechanical and physical properties allows an expectation for practical use of the new type Al-based alloy in various fields. Introduction Since the finding of amorphous alloys with good bending ductility in the Al-Ni-Si system(l), a number of Al-based amorphous alloys with good ductility have been found in a rapidly solidified state in Al-Ln(2), Al-Ml- M2(3) and (Ln =lanthanide metal, MI and M2: transition metal) systems. Furthermore, it has been reported that these Al-based amorphous alloys exhibit high tensile strength above 1000 MPa even at Al-rich com- positions of 84 to 86 at0/o(6) and the highest strength reaches as high as 1250 MPa(7). It has subsequently been that the homogeneous dispersion of nanoscale fcc-Al particles into an Al-based amorphous matrix causes a significant increase in tensile strength to 1560 MPa which is about 1.5 times as high as that of the corre- sponding amorphous single phase alloy. However, these data were obtained from the ribbon samples with a cross section of about 0.02 x 1.0 mm2 produced by the melt spinning technique. In order to develop Al-based alloys with a higher specific strength in practical use by utilizing the amorphous phase with high strength and good ductil- ity, it is essential to accumulate the information on the microstructure and mechanical properties of Al-based alloys in a bulk form produced by extrusion or forging of the amorphous alloy powders. In a series of studies on the microstructure and mechanical properties of Al-bas- ed alloys in bulk form, we have found that high tensile strength above 900 MPa and high fatigue strength above 300 MPa are simultaneously obtained for a bulky Al��Ni- Mm alloy produced by extrusion of the alloy powders containing an amorphous phase as a main phase at tem- peratures above crystallization temperature. This paper is intended to present the microstructure, mechanical properties including tensile strength, fatigue strength and wear resistance, and coemcient of thermal expansion for the bulky A188.5Ni8Mm3.5 alloy. II. Experimental Procedure An alloy with nominal composition A188.5Ni8Mm3.5 (at%) was used because the alloy composition was located at the most Al-rich side in the composition range where an amorphous phase in the Al-Ni-Mm and Al- Ni-Ln systems is formed by melt A mixture of pure Al, Ni and Mm metals was melted by arc melting in a purified argon atmosphere. The Mm metal is compos- ed of 52 mass% Ce, 29 mass% La, 14 mass% Nd and 5 mass% Pr. Three kg of the pre-alloyed ingot was charged into the atomization crucible and remelted under an argon atmosphere. The pre-alloyed ingot was atomized from a temperature of 1473 K with helium gas at an ap- plied pressure of 5.88 MPa in a high-pressure gas atomization equipment. The powders were allowed to cool in the inert atmosphere but were collected and

Fine powders below 25 gm in size were put in a cylindrical Al can with an in- ner diameter of 24 mm. The tap density of the powder was 60%. The powder in the can was outgassed, precom- pacted to 90% density at 673 K by uniaxial hot-pressing and sealed by welding. The canned powder was heated in a resistance furnace at an extrusion temperature of 633 K for 60 s and then extruded to an area reduction of 10:1 in a 150-ton extrusion press. The as-quenched and extruded phases of the powders were examined by X-ray diffrac- tion, optical microscopy and differential scanning calorimetry. The tensile strength and elongation were measured in a temperature range from room temperature to 573 K using an Instron-type testing machine under an argon atmosphere. The sample for the tensile test has a cylindrical shape with the gauge dimensions of 3 mm x 50 mm. The fatigue strength was measured for the cylindrical sample with the gauge dimensions of 8 mm x 15.4 mm at room temperature and 473 K in air using a rotating bending machine. The coemcient of thermal expansion was measured with a dilatometer at a heating rate of 0.33 K/ s in the temperature range of 293 to 623 K. The relative wear resistance against plain carbon steel (S45C) was evaluated at room temperature in air by the pin-on disc method. 111. Results The atomized powder had a fine particle size and 50 wt% of the particles had diameters less than 26 gm. Powder particles in this size range have previously been reported(ll) to exhibit the cooling rates above 105 K/ s. The powder was spherical and was relatively free from satellites. The internal porosity was also very low. Figure I(a) and (b) show the etched cross-sections of two size fractions of powder particles. No distinct contrast can be seen in the optical micrograph (a) of the powder with a particle size fraction below 26 gm and the microstructure of the < 26 um powder is below the resolution limit of the microscope. Figure 2 shows the change with powder size in the X- ray diffraction pattern of the atomized Al-Ni-Mm powders. The structure is composed of amorphous and fcc-Al phases for the powders smaller than 26 gm in diameter and Al +A13Ni + Alli(La, Ce)3 for the powders above 26 gm. It has previously been reported(12) that the melt-spun structure of the A188.5Ni8Mm3.5 alloy is an amor- phous single phase, when the rotation speed of wheel is 41.8 m/ s (4000 rpm), and changes to a mixed structure consisting of amorphous and fcc-Al phases with decreas- ing rotation speed of wheel. Furthermore, the precipita- tion of the Al phase at the Al-rich composition has been presumed to occur during cooling after the formation of the amorphous phase. Consequently, the cooling rate in the present gas atomization is not high enough to sup- press the precipitation of the fcc-Al phase in a rather low temperature range during cooling, though the present equipment has a high cooling capability leading to the formation of the amorphous phase. V. Summary The microstructure and mechanical properties were ex- amined for a bulky A188.5Ni8Mm3.5 alloy produced by ex- trusion of the atomized powders with a particle size frac- tion below 26 gm at an extrusion ratio of 10 and at 633 K. The extruded alloy consists of a mixed structure in which fine A13Ni and Alll(La, Ce)3 compounds with a particle size of about 50 nm are dispersed homogeneous- ly in an fcc-Al matrix with a grain size of about 100 to 200 nm. The tensile fracture strength (Of) of the extruded alloy is as high as 940 MPa at room temperature and keeps high values of 700 MPa at 423 K and 520 MPa at 473 K. The high-strength level is also recognized for the tensile yield strength (00.2) and the values at room temper- ature and 473 K are 845 and 420 MPa, respectively. The high Of and 00.2 values remain constant even after anneal- ing for 100 h at 423 and 473 K. The fatigue limit after the cycles of 107 is also as high as 330 MPa at room temper- ature and 220 MPa at 473 K. Based on the discussion of the mechanism for the achievement of the high mechanical strengths, it is interpreted that the strengthen- ing mechanism is contributed to the combined effect of grain size refinement and dispersion hardening resulting from the formation of the extremely fine mixed structure and the contribution is almost equivalent for both the mechanisms. In addition, the homogeneous dispersion of the intermetallic compounds was also found to cause the low coefficient of thermal expansion as well as good wear resistance.
