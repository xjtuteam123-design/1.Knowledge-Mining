ABSTRACT 
The deformation and fracture behavior of TZM (Mo-0.5% Ti-0.08% Zr-0.03% C) were studied 
after exposures at 825 and 10Q0°C to low-pressure 02, CO, H20, and a simulated radioisotope 
thermoelectric generator environment. TZM showed an uptake of oxygen on exposure to these 
environments. Analysis of the results indicates that a diffusion process controls the kinetics of the 
oxygen absorption at 825°C while both diffusion and chemisorption processes affect the rate at 
1000°C. Exposed TZM also exhibited a small initial increase in carbon content followed by a decrease. 
Decarburization became significant only after extended exposure at 1000°C. 
The mechanical properties of both exposed and heat-treated specimens were determined at 
temperatures to 1316°C (2400°F). TZM specimens showed an increase in strength and a linear 
decreas
e in ductility with oxygen content. Exposed TZM lost its ductility completely at an oxygen 
level of 300 ppm at room temperature, 1093, and 1316°C, but 500 ppm was required at 825°C. The 
ductility of embrittled TZM was increased significantly w?. , leat treatment at high temperatures and 
was almost completely restored after annealing for 15 at 1700°C. The change in mechanical 
properties is discussed in terms of internal oxidation and precipitation of various forms of oxides.

. INTRODUCTION 
The heat source in a 238Pu02-fueled radioisotope 
thermoelectric generator (RTG) for space applications 
requires a cladding not only to contain the radioisotope 
fuel for a normal service life of several years but also to 
survive a launch-pad fire, reentry heating, earth impact, 
and post-impact environments. Materials fulfilling these 
requirements should have a high melting point, proven 
fabricability, compatibility with the heat-source envi-
ronment, and excellent strength and ductility at high 
temperatures. The environment of such a heat source 
operating in the range 800 to 1450°C generally consists 
o
f He and/or Ar plus low-pressure active gases derived 
from decomposition of the oxide fuel, outgassing of 
graphite and insulator materials, and evaporation and 
decomposition of the thermoelectric components. 
As a part of a general research and development effort 
in support of space radioisotope heat-source programs 
sponsored by the Space Nuclear Systems Division of the 
Atomic Energy Commission, we have been studying the 
effects of interstitial contamination on the mechanical 
properties of candidate materials such as T-lll, TZM, 
Ir, and the newly developed alloy1 Pt-3008 (Pt-30 wt 
% Rh-8 wt % W). T-lll, a tantalum-base alloy 
containing 8 wt % W and 2% Hf, is currently used as the 
encapsulating strength member for the RTG heat 
sources. A detailed evaluation of its compatibility with 
low-pressure oxidizing gases at 825, 1000, and 1200°C

3.2 Microstructure 
After exposure and heat treatment the specimens 
were examined metallographically. Figure 3 shows the 
photomicrographs of four specimens: as recrystallized, 
oxidized 2000 hr at 825°C, oxidized 207 hr at 1000°C, 
and annealed 15 min at 1700°C after 207 hr oxidation 
at
 1000°C. Although these specimens have very dif-
ferent mechanical properties as will be shown later, all 
of them exhibit the same recrystallized structure with a 
few particles (presumably carbide) within the grains and 
along the grain boundaries. There was no variation in 
structure from the surface to the center of the exposed 
specimens. Thus, exposure of TZM to 02, H20, CO, 
and heat-source environments up to 2000 hr at 825 and 
1000°
C does not cause any appreciable change in 
optical microstructure. 
3.3 X-Ray Diffraction 
To identify the oxide phase near the specimen 
surface, needle specimens used for x-ray diffraction 
were exposed directly to the oxygen environment at 
1000°C
. The oxidized specimens with or without 
further heat treatment were then cleaned electro-
lytically prior to examination. No reflection lines from 
an oxide phase were detected in the as-oxidized and 
heat-treated specimens. This is possibly due to an 
insufficient volume of diffracting oxide. However, a 
decrease of the bcc lattice parameter dp with oxygen 
content was observed in TZM. For instance, in the 
unexposed TZM is 3.1472 A, while in the specimen 
(heat-treated 4 hr at 1400°C after 207 hr exposure at 
1000°C) containing 480 ppm it is only 3.1470 A. The 
decrease in ap indicates the precipitation of oxide phase 
in the exposed specimens as will be discussed later.

4. DISCUSSION 
4.1 Mechanisms of Gas-Metal Interaction 
It is known that the refractory metals and alloys react 
with low-pressure gases6"9 such as oxygen, water vapor, 
carbon dioxide, and carbon monoxide at elevated 
temperatures. Whether these reactions lead to an 
increase or decrease in the concentration of one or 
more of the interstitial atoms is generally dependent 
upon the gas pressure, reaction temperature, and the 
chemical behavior of the materials. In the case of TZM, 
which contains small amounts of the active elements 
titanium and zirconium dissolved in molybdenum, the 
absorption of gases may lead to the precipitation of 
interstitial compounds, resulting in continuous 
change10 in composition of the base solution. Conse-
quently, the mechanism that governs the gas-metal 
interaction may also change with exposure time. This is 
what we found in the prolonged exposure of TZM to 
the low-pressure gases. 
In this study, TZM was treated at low temperatures 
(825 and 1000°C) and low pressure (1 X 10"s torr). As 
shown in Fig. 2, the oxygen content increased steadily 
with time, indicating that an equilibrium state was not 
reached after 2000 hr exposure at 825°C and 1000 hr 
at 1000°C. TZM also exhibited an initial increase in 
carbon content followed by a decrease (Table 2). 
Decarburization became significant only after extended 
exposure at 1000°C. The mechanism for each respective 
reaction is discussed separately below. 
Internal oxidation. TZM is a material capable of 
internal oxidation. Oxygen and titanium form stable 
oxides, which have a standard free energy of forma-
tion1 1 much more negative than that of molybdenum 
oxide. The diffusivity of oxygen in molybdenum is 
several orders of magnitude greater than that of 
titanium; thus the inward diffusion of oxygen is much 
faster than outward diffusion of titanium in the matrix. 
Both conditions promote internal oxidation in this 
alloy.

4.2 Correlation between Mechanical Properties 
and Structure of Internally Oxidized TZM
