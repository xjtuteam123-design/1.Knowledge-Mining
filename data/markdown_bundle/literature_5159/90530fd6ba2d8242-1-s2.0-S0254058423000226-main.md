1. Introduction 
A new class of advanced metallic materials, which consist of at least 
five principal elements, each contributing 5 – 35 at.%, was put forward in 
2004 [ 1 , 2 ]. Due to the concentrated mixtures of multiple principal 
alloying elements, these alloys are usually called as high entropy alloys 
(HEAs) or complex concentrated alloys [ 1 , 3 , 4 ]. The 
multi-principal-element nature endows the HEAs with large mixing 
entropy which can greatly enhance their phase stability, according to 
the thermodynamic formula ( Δ G
mix 
= Δ H
mix
-T Δ S
mix
, T is the 
temperature, Δ S
mix 
is the mixing entropy, Δ H
mix 
is the mixing enthalpy). 
Therefore, the solid solutions, such as face-centered cubic (FCC), 
body-centered cubic (BCC) and hexagonal closed-packed (HCP), are 
more likely to be formed than intermetallic phases in the HEAs. HEAs 
usually possess the excellent physical, chemical and mechanical prop -
erties, which are mainly related to the high-entropy effects in thermo -
dynamics, sluggish diffusion in structure, severe lattice distortion effect 
in dynamics, and cocktail effect in properties [ 1 , 5 , 6 ].

chanical properties, a series of multi-phase HEAs was developed by

thermophysical parameter, VEC , can critically separate the FCC or BCC 
solid solution formation [ 20 ]. Significantly, the phases of HEAs might 
evolve to a new solid solution or intermetallic phase during the heat 
treatment process. For example, the most widely studied CoCrFeNiMn 
with FCC phase will be decomposed into FCC plus σ phases after 
annealing at the intermediate temperature range [ 21 , 22 ]. However, the 
mechanism of phase evolution of the FCC + BCC dual-phase structured 
HEAs under intermediate temperatures is still inconclusive, and further 
study need to be done for expanding the utilization of HEAs in the field

The dendritic structures of all homogenized Fe

fraction of the smaller-sized grains with much higher content of Fe and 
Cr elements ( β 1) were found adjacent to the FCC/L1
2 
grains. As shown in 
Fig. 2 , this solidification morphology of the Fe

Fe content. In the Fe

FCC/L1

2.5

Considering that the FCC phase was a soft phase with lower strength 
and higher plasticity and BCC phase has higher strength but worse 
ductility [ 41 ]. It is reasonable that the mechanical properties of the Fe
x 
alloys show a trend of increasing strength and decreasing plasticity 
( Fig. 4 a – d) with the decrease of Fe content and annealing treatment for 
the increased BCC phase fraction ( Fig. 3 e – f). The dual-phase structures 
of these alloys make their strain-hardening rate cures present two ascent 
stages (stage II and stage IV, except for the annealed Fe1.5) ( Fig. 5 ). 
During the tensile test, the plastic deformation first occurred in the α 
regions, and the β / β 1 regions did not deform. As the stretching pro -
ceeded, the plastic deformation difference between the α regions and the 
β / β 1 regions became larger and larger, resulting in an increase in strain 
incompatibility. In order to maintain the continuity of the strain at the 
boundary, a large number of GNDs would be accumulated near the 
phase interfaces [ 42 , 43 ]. The GNDs are claimed to block the motion of 
the dislocations and generate a long-range back stress hardening, 
enhancing both strain hardening capability and uniform elongation of 
the alloys [ 44 , 45 ]. It was reported that alloys composed of FCC matrix 
and BCC phase (more than 25%) exhibit inhomogeneous deformation 
behavior on the mesoscopic scale, and there is a very obvious strain 
accumulation in the FCC matrix [ 46 ]. In the later stage of deformation, 
the BCC phase is destroyed by cleavage fracture due to the excessive 
stress of the matrix and rapid crack propagation may lead to the pre -
mature fracture of alloy. It can be deduced that this mechanism might be 
responsibility for the early failure of the annealed Fe0.5 alloy since the 
micro-cracks at the interface of FCC/L12 and BCC/B2 phase was 
observed in the fracture surface ( Fig. 4 a
2
). In addition, the optimization 
of phase distribution in the annealed Fe
x 
alloys played an important role 
in the improvement of mechanical properties. Of course, the grain size 
and dislocation density of the dual-phase alloys might also affect the 
mechanical behavior to some extent [ 47 ], but the effects may be 
negligible compared with the influences of phase constitution and dis -
tribution. In total, the Fe
2.5 
alloy obtained good performance optimi -
zation after annealing at 600 
◦
C for 50 h, which was a possible 
engineering material applied in the intermediate-temperature 
environment. 
5. Conclusions 
The phase stability at intermediate-temperature and mechanical 
properties changes of AlCoCr
0.5
Fe
x
Ni
2.5 
(x = 0.5, 1.5, 2.5 and 3.5) HEAs 
were studied by annealing at 600 
◦
C for 50 h. The following conclusions 
could be drawn. 
1. A FCC-to-BCC phase transformation was occurred in the dual-phase 
structured Fe
x 
alloys during annealing process, resulted in an 
increased fraction of BCC/B2 phase and the optimization of phase 
distribution. 
2. Two parameters ( Ω and δ ) were used to explain the thermal stability 
of Fe
x 
alloys. It indicated that the higher Ω and lower δ were helpful 
to enhance the thermal stability of HEAs under an intermediate 
temperature. 
3. The increased BCC/B2 phase fraction and the optimization of phase 
distribution after annealing improved the mechanical properties of 
the annealed Fe
x 
alloys. The strength of the AlCoCr
0.5
Fe
2.5
Ni
2.5 
alloy 
was significantly improved without sacrificing any plasticity, which 
was expected to develop a new type of engineering material served in 
the intermediate-temperature field.
