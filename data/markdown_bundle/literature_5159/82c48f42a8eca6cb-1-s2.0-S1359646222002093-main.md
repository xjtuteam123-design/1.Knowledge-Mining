ABSTRACT 
The effects of aging precipitation on microstructure, deep drawing formability and tensile properties, were 
investigated for Mg-6Al-1Zn (AZ61) alloy sheets. The alloy aged at 175 
◦
C for 96 h with high strength exhibited a 
significant improvement in deep drawability with an increased limiting drawing ratio (LDR) from 1.5 to 1.9, 
when slightly increasing deformation temperature from room temperature to 100 
◦
C. This LDR (1.9) was even 
larger than that (1.75) of the unaged alloy drawn at 100 
◦
C. The improved deep drawability can be attributed to 
the prevention of cracking at the edge of drawn cup, which is closely related to the restrictions of growth of 
tensile twins and basal slip due to Mg
17
Al
12 
precipitates, resulting in the delayed texture strengthening during 
deformation. The proposed idea of utilizing aging precipitation provides a new strategy for the development of 
Mg alloy sheets with superior deep drawing formability and high strength. 
The development of lightweight Mg alloy sheets with a combination 
of excellent formability and high strength is a challenging task. Deep 
drawing process involving different deformation modes from tension, 
stretch forming, stretch flanging and bending, is of industry significance 
for sheet metal forming. It has been found that microstructural modifi -
cation and the trace addition of rare-earth elements effectively enhance 
deep drawing formability for Mg alloy sheets [1 – 4] . As investigated on 
AZ31 alloy, texture weakening and grain refinement are favored for the 
improvement of deep drawing formability [ 1 , 2 ]. The relatively 
restricted tensile twinning activity can be considered to be one reason 
responsible for the enhanced deep drawability, because tensile twinning 
results in the rapid texture strengthening during deep drawing defor -
mation and in turn induces cracking at the edge of drawn cup [ 1 , 2 ]. 
Compared to the most widely used AZ31 alloy, AZ61 alloy with a larger 
Al content has higher material strength together with aging strength -
ening capability. However, aging precipitation generally deteriorates 
ductility and formability [ 5 , 6 ]. Thus, aging treatment for strengthening 
materials has been considered to be performed after sheet forming. Until 
now, the research on aging precipitation is still mainly focused on pre -
cipitation strengthening, and less attention has been paid to enhance the 
formability of aged alloys. On the other hand, it has been revealed that 
precipitates may increase the stresses required for twin propagation and 
growth, which results in finer twins [7 – 11] . Therefore, it is possible to 
delay texture strengthening during deep drawing through the restriction 
of twin growth associated with precipitates, and in turn to improve deep 
drawing formability. However, the influence of aging precipitation on 
deep drawing formability of Mg alloy sheets has not been reported, even 
though high strength can be expected. 
In this study, the effects of aging precipitation on microstructure, 
deep drawing formability and mechanical properties, were investigated 
for age-hardenable AZ61 alloy sheets. We found that the aggressive 
utilization of aging precipitation, is a novel approach for achieving Mg 
alloy sheets with superior deep drawing formability and high strength 
simultaneously. 
Commercial extruded AZ61 (Mg – 5.94Al – 0.53Zn – 0.33Mn in wt%) 
alloy plates with a thickness of 2 mm were subjected by high tempera -
ture rolling at 480 
◦
C, for obtaining rolled sheets with a well-weakened 
basal texture and thus excellent press formability [ 12 , 13 ]. The plates 
were rolled down to 0.8 mm in thickness by three rolling passes with a 
reduction per pass of 26%, and the rollers were heated to 150 
◦
C during 
rolling. After rolling, the sheets annealed at 350 
◦

unaged alloy) together with those further aged at 175 
◦
C for 96 h 
(denoted as aged alloy) were used for tensile and deep drawing tests at 
room temperature (RT) and 100 
◦
C.

Regarding deep drawing formability, the LDR at RT are 1.7 and 1.5 
for the unaged alloy and the aged alloy, respectively, as shown in Fig. 2 
(c). Compared to the LDR of 1.2 – 1.4 at RT for commercial AZ31 alloy 
sheets [15] , the excellent deep drawing formability (LDR: 1.7) for the 
unaged alloy is originated from the well-weakened basal texture (see 
Fig. 1 (b)). In the case of deep drawing at 100 
◦
C, the LDR slightly in -
creases from 1.7 to 1.75 for the unaged alloy, while the LDR significantly 
increases from 1.5 to 1.9 for the aged alloy. This means that the deep 
drawing formability of the aged alloy is even better than that of the 
unaged alloy (1.9 vs. 1.75 for LDR), when slightly increasing deforma -
tion temperature to 100 
◦
C. This result exactly shows that the precipi -

17
Al
12

drawing formability at a slightly increased temperature of 100 
◦

forming. 
In addition, the fracture behavior is quite different between the 
unaged alloy and the aged alloy. For both deformation temperatures of 
RT and 100 
◦
C, cracking occurs at the edge of cup for the unaged alloy 
(see Fig. 2 (e)), while the fracture site is always the shoulder of cup for 
the aged alloy (see Fig. 2 (f)). For the unaged alloy, even though the 
average FE remarkably increases from 24.7% to 32.9% due to the 
enhancement of PUE with increasing deformation temperature to 100 
◦
C, the improvement of deep drawing formability with a small increase 
in LDR from 1.7 to 1.75 is quite limited. This indicates that the PUE is 
not a dominant influence factor on restricting fracture at the edge of cup, 
where is subjected to compressive stress along the circumferential di -
rection [ 1 , 2 ]. In contrast, the shoulder of cup has a bending deformation 
component (stretch bending) and thus strain in the thickness direction 
(necking) is required [ 1 , 4 , 16 ]. It has been reported that the increase in 
PUE contributes to the improvement of bendability [17 – 20] . It is 
reasonable to assume that the increase from 3.1% to 15.0% in PUE is 
responsible for the enhancement of deformation capability at the 
shoulder and in turn the significantly improved deep drawability for the 
aged alloy. In addition, it is worth noting that no cracking occurs at the 
edge of cup drawn even at a large drawing ratio (DR) of 1.9. This in -
dicates that the precipitation of Mg
17
Al
12 
has the effect on restricting 
fracture at the edge of cup.

Regarding the utilization of aging precipitation for tailoring micro -
structure, it has been reported that the pre-aging treatment can effec -
tively increase the ratio of dynamic recrystallization during thermo- 
mechanical processing, which leads to the improvement of mechanical 
properties due to more refined microstructures [31 – 33] . In this study, 
even though aging precipitation has been considered to deteriorate 
ductility, it is revealed that aging precipitation is an effect approach for 
achieving Mg alloy sheets with the combination of excellent deep 
drawing formability (LDR: 1.9 at 100 
◦
C) and high strength (UTS: 306 
MPa, YS: 177 MPa at RT). This has never been reported to the best of 
authors ’ knowledge. Moreover, as can be confirmed from Fig. 4 , the 
alignment of plate-like Mg
17
Al
12 
precipitates in tensile twins changes 
from parallel to the basal planes to approximately parallel to the pris -
matic planes of the α -Mg matrix due to 86
◦
lattice rotation. It has been 
reported that this type of prismatic plate precipitates can improve 
dispersion strengthening much more effectively compared to basal plate 
precipitates due to more effective barriers to basal slip, based on the 
Orowan strengthening theory and the experimental results [ 14 , 34 , 35 ]. 
Therefore, further contribution to the improvement of strength can be 
expected from the twinned region (the wall and the edge of cup) after 
deep drawing for the aged alloy. In addition, after tensile twinning oc -
curs at the early deformation stage, further formation dominated by 
basal slip rotates basal planes to be perpendicular to the circumferential 
compressive stress and in turn strengthens deformation texture during 
deep drawing [ 1 , 2 ]. Considering the large hindrance to the movement of 
basal slip, it is reasonable to assume that grain rotation driven by basal 
slip can be delayed by the existence of prismatic plate Mg
17
Al
12 
phase 
precipitates. Therefore, the delayed grain rotation and thus the delayed 
texture strengthening can be mainly attributed to the restricted growth 
of tensile twins and the huge hindrance of basal dislocation movement 
caused by prismatic plate precipitates in the twinned region. 
In summary, the combination of excellent deep drawing formability 
(LDR: 1.9) at a slightly increased temperature of 100 
◦
C and high 
strength (UTS: 306 MPa, YS: 177 MPa) was achieved for AZ61 alloy 
sheets using the extensive aging precipitation of Mg
17
Al
12 
phase. The 
LDR (1.9) of the aged alloy was much larger than that (1.75) of the 
unaged alloy, when deep drawing at 100 
◦
C. The improved deep 
drawing formability is closely related to the precipitation of Mg
17
Al
12
, 
which restricts the growth of tensile twins and the basal slip in the 
twinned region, resulting in the delay of texture strengthening during 
deformation.
