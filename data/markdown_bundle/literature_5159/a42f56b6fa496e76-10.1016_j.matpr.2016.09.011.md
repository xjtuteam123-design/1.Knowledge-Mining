evolution and mechanical properties of hypoeutectic Nb-Si-Cr

Abstract 
Among the refractory metals, Nb-based alloys are considered the most promising potential materials in the field of high 
temperature applications ( ≥1150°C). In this regard, novel alloy design strategies are needed to be adopted to obtain a balance 
between strength and creep resistance at elevated temperature and ductility and fracture toughness at low temperature. The 
present investigation is aimed at developing the Nb-rich hypoeutectic quaternary (Nb-Si-Cr-Al) and quinary (Nb-Si-Cr-Al-Mo) 
alloys and study microstructural evolution and mechanical propert ies and thereby studying the effect of addition of Al and Mo. 
The composition of the quaternary (Nb-Si-Cr-Al) and quinary (Nb-Si-Cr-Al-Mo) alloys are chosen using intelligent guess and the 
binary and ternary phase diagrams among the elements. The mechanical properties of alloys were analyzed and correlated with 
the microstructure. Precipitates of hard and brittle silicide ( βNb5Si3) as well as Laves (Cr 2Nb) phase contribute the strength 
whereas (Nb)ss imparts ductility to the alloy. 
 
Keywords: Nb-Si-Cr-Al, Nb-Si-Cr-Al-Mo, Hypoeutectic, Solidification Pathway, Compression test, Oxidation resistance 
 
1. Introduction 
For last two decades, several research works are been carri ed out to make ultra-high temperature materials, which 
would show not only promising combination of low density with impressive mechanical properties at high 
temperature but also should have the property of very good oxidation resistance at high temperature [1, 2, 3]. Nickel 
based super alloys had been the only option for so many years for high temperature applications. But the main 
disadvantages of using these alloys are their relatively high density ( ρ≥ 9 gm/cm 3) and lower liquidus temperature 
(∼ 1350°C) [4]. So to make the better alternative materials for high temperature ( ≥1150°C) applications; the idea of 
design and development of niobium-based in situ composites had come. The melting point of niobium is very high 
(2468°C) and its density (8.57 gm/cm 3) is slightly lower than the density of nickel (8.908 gm/cm 3) [5, 6, 7]. 
Addition of silicon to niobium causes the formation of niobium silicon in situ composites which has the property of 
high spalling resistance at high temperature. The binary phase diagram of Nb-Si provides us the information about 
the formation of different high temperature intermetallic silicide compounds (i.e. Nb 3Si, Nb 5Si3).The balance 
property of strength and ductility can be obtained by controlling volume fraction of these brittle and hard silicide 
phases (Nb 3Si and Nb 5Si3)within the solid solution of niobium and this can be achievedby controlling the 
concentration of silicon and other alloying elements (like Cr ,Al ,Mo ,Ti) to the Nb rich Nb-Si alloys [7, 8, 9]. 
Another speciality of Niobium-Silicon system is that optimum concentration of silicon plays a vital role to enhance

the oxidation resistance at high temperature as silicon readily forms silica (SiO 2) [10, 11]. It is highly stable at the 
high temperature and forms an impervious layer at elevated temperature. But the oxidation resistance property of 
Nb-Si binary alloy is not sufficient enough for high temperature applications and to overcome this obstacle the 
concept of adding some alloying elements to the Nb-Si binary alloys has been developed. Hypoeutectic niobium 
silicon binary alloys consist of brittle intermetallic silicide phases (Nb 3Si and/or Nb5Si3) in a niobium solid solution 
matrix whereas brittle silicide phase acts as a primary phase in the eutectic and hyper eutectic Nb-Si based alloys. 
Certain quantity of Cr addition to the Nb-Si based alloys can lead to form an ordered intermetallic hard and brittle 
compound Cr2Nb laves phase that increases strength, creep resistance as well as oxidation resistance ability of SiO 2 
layer by forming CrNbO 4 [12, 13, 14]. Therefore our main objective is to obtain the balanced properties of high 
temperature strength, creep resistance, oxidation resistance and room temperature ductility, fracture toughness of the 
alloys which would contain brittle intermetallic silicide phases (Nb 3Si and/or Nb 5Si3) and/or Cr 2Nb laves phase 
within the solid solution of niobium. Aluminium addition to the Nb-Si-Cr based hypoeutectic alloys also may 
increase the oxidation resistance by forming their oxides. But to maintain good enough mechanical behaviour, it is 
better not to use higher concentration Al as higher Al co ntent can promote cleavage fracture to the Nb solid solution 
phase [15, 16]. Mo is another refractory metal which not only forms isomorphous system with Nb but also increases 
oxidation resistance of the alloy by forming volatile MoO 3 which reduces the diffusion of oxygen to the alloy [11, 
17]. At high temperature, Mo addition would make the diffusion of Nb sluggish and if the creep 
mechanism is controlled by the diffusion process, then obviously the Mo addition would really enhance the creep 
resistance of the alloy. Relatively smaller atomic radius and high modulus of elasticity of Mo increases the strength 
of the alloy by solid solution strengthening. However, the formation of adherent oxides, solid solution strengthening 
at elevated temperature and isomorphous alloy system of Nb-Mo, makes the addition Mo to the Nb based 
multicomponent alloy. But the concentration of Mo in the alloy should not be high enough otherwise it would form 
brittle silicide phase that would decrease the ductility of alloy. Also higher concentration of Mo forms more volatile 
MoO3 which makes structure porous and affects the mechanical properties of the alloy. 
In this present investigation, a systematic study has b een done (1) by changing Al concentration from 0.5 to 3 
atom% replacing Si in the hypoeutectic Nb 80Si15Cr5 alloys and (2) by changing Mo concentration from 5 to 10 
atom% replacing Nb in the hypoeutectic Nb 80Si12Cr5Al3 alloys. We shall discuss the microstructure evolution during 
suction casting, and mechanical behaviour of the hypoeutectic Nb-Si-Cr-Al and Nb-Si-Cr-Al-Mo alloys. 
 
2. Experimental Method and Characterizations 
 
The in-situ composites of pre-set composition (Nb 80Si15-xCr5Alx (x=0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3) and Nb 80-xSi12Cr5Al3Mox (x=5, 
10)) were synthesized by suction casting vacuum-arc-melting-cum casting technique under high purity argon 
atmosphere. The as-cast alloys were prepared using the high purity (99.9%) elements. To ensure compositional and 
chemical homogeneity, the as-cast buttons were re-melted 4-5 times. Subsequently, the as-cast cylinders were 
synthesized by novel arc melting cum suction casting using the water cooled split copper mould to obtain cylindrical 
rods of diameter (φ) =5mm. 
Identification of the phases were done using BRUKER, D8 FOCUS X-ray diffractometer with Ni-filtered Cu K α 
(λ=0.154056 nm) radiation. The phase distribution and compos itional analysis of all the samples were carried out 
using a Carl Zeiss EVO 50, scanning electron microscope (SEM) coupled with an energy dispersive spectrometer 
(EDS, INCA PENTA FET X3), operated at 20 KV. Detailed fine scale microstructural observations were carried out 
using FEI Tecnai G2 U-Twin TEM, operated at 200 KV. Room temperature compression test of the cylindrical 
specimens (aspect ratio= 1.5) carried out using Instron 1195 at a strain rate 10 -3s-1. 
 
3. Results and discussion 
 
The identification of the phase formation of the eutectic Nb 80Si15-xCr5Alx (x=0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3) alloys were carried out 
by XRD analysis (figure 1).The peaks of the diffraction patterns of all the Nb 80Si15-xCr5Alx (x=0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3) 
alloys can be indexed as primary (Nb)ss (Im3m, a = 0.3306 nm)[18], while rest of the peaks are indexed as β-Nb5Si3

are confirmed by SEM and EDX analysis and finally by TEM characterization. In this alloy system, Cr and Al are 
the alloying elements which are added to the hypoeutectic Nb-Si binary alloys. These alloying elements help to form 
Nb5Si3 and suppress the formation of Nb 3Si. Also the peaks of laves phase Cr 2Nb are found in all the XRD patterns 
of the Nb80Si15-xCr5Alx(x=0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3) alloys.

4. Conclusions 
The following conclusions can be drawn in the present work. 
1. For both the Nb 80Si15-xCr5Alx (x=0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3) and Nb 80-xSi12Cr5Al3Mox (x=5, 10) alloy systems, 
Primary dendritic (Nb)ss phase, dark grey silicide phase β-Nb5Si3, black laves phase Cr 2Nb and two 
different types of eutectic mixtures ((Nb)ss / β-Nb5Si3 and (Nb)ss/Cr 2Nb are observed within the 
microstructure.
