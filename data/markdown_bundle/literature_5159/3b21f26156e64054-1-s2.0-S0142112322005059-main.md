ABSTRACT 
Hot-section components made by single crystal (SC) Ni-based superalloys with geometrical discontinuities such 
as film holes suffer from spatially inhomogeneous rafting of the γ’ / γ microstructure due to harsh circumstances. 
Therefore, low cycle fatigue (LCF) life prediction is critical for ensuring the safety of engineering structures. 
Within this regard, an energy-based LCF life model is proposed based on the concept of the strain energy gradient 
by introducing the effect of rafting into the plastic and elastic parts of the strain energy density. The inhomo -
geneous microstructure degradation of a centre-hole specimen is depicted by stress-assisted rafting and stress- 
free isotropic coarsening, whereas the inelastic deformation behaviour of materials near the central hole is 
simulated by a microstructure-sensitive constitutive model. Combined with the concepts of modified strain en -
ergy density, effective damage zone and weight function, the proposed life model achieves a good prediction 
accuracy in ± 2 scatter bands against the experimental data of the centre-hole specimens with different rafting 
treatments. Finally, the uncertainty of the proposed life prediction model induced by the statistical dispersion of 
the microstructure is estimated. The results show that the uncertainty of the predicted lifetimes decreases with 
the rafting extent and fatigue life, even though the distribution of the γ channel width of the alloy with heavily 
rafting alloys is highly dispersed. 
1. Introduction 
Turbine blades and vanes are critical components for the safe service 
and high efficiency of aero engines. Most of these components are usu -
ally made of single crystal (SC) and directionally solidified (DS) Ni-based 
superallys to improve the temperature capability. The mechanical 
properties of SC and DS Ni-based superalloys are highly dependent on 
the high volume faction of the L1
2
-type γ’ precipitates and the 
morphology, size and distribution of the coherent γ’ / γ microstructures 
[1,2,3] . Operating in the presence of a stress and temperature typically 
in excess of 850 ℃ , the microstructure of the SC and DS Ni-based su -
peralloys forms a rafting morphology, which is controlled by the Ost -
wald ripening and directional coalescence kinetics simultaneously [4,5] . 
This microstructural change needs to be considered in fatigue life 
analysis for the components. However, many stress – strain concentra -
tions occur at geometrical singularities, such as film cooling holes and 
slits of turbine blades, which consequently affects the spatial 
homogeneity of microstructural rafting on the components. This adds a 
new level of difficulty to the notched fatigue analysis of single crystal 
components since the effects of stress distribution and microstructure 
distribution at the geometrical discontinuity need to be considered 
simultaneously. 
Rafting of SC Ni-based superalloys, which is a diffusion-controlled 
process that is highly sensitive to the relative lattice mismatch be -
tween the γ’ precipitates and the γ matrix, applied stress state and 
temperature, has attracted much more attention in recent decades [6,7] . 
For the case of most modern Ni-base superalloys for turbine blades, the 
lattice misfit is designed to be negative such that the rafting aligns 
normal to the stress axis under a tensile load at high temperature ( N - 
rafting), whereas rafting parallel to the applied load occurs when placed 
under a compressive stress (P-rafting) [8] . In addition to the cases under 
uniaxial stress states, several experiments have been carried out to 
investigate rafting under complex stress states [9,10] . Caccuri et al. [11] 
uncovered the correlation between stress triaxiality and rafting

experimental characterization, it is found that the features of rafting 
depend on the position of the region relative to the position of the notch. 
However, their work does not consider the microstructural evolution 
compared with that of Caccuri et al. [11] . 
Due to the high dependency on microstructural features, the effects 
of rafting on the fatigue properties of Ni-based superalloys have aroused 
widespread concern. Some recent experimental results demonstrate that 
N -type rafting and coarsening of the γ’ / γ microstructures pronouncedly 
reduce the yield strength [16] , isothermal and thermal – mechanical fa -
tigue life [17,18,19] , whereas P-type rafting improves the fatigue life in 
some cases [19] . Based on experimental observations, Ott et al. [20] 
pointed out that the layered structure of N -type rafting promotes the 
propagation of cracks in the γ channel, resulting in premature failure. At 
the micro level, rafting and coarsening alter the internal stress of the 
coherent γ’ / γ microstructure so that the degraded materials have a 
higher mean stress under thermal – mechanical loading, as shown in the 
results of Kirka et al. [19] . At high temperature, dislocation mainly 
motioning in the γ channel is the preferential deformation mechanism 
for DS and SC Ni-based suepalloys [21,22] . Therefore, widening of the γ 
channel has a significant contribution to the deterioration of fatigue life. 
Previous works have mainly focused on the effect of microstructural 
degradation of smooth specimens, whereas the influence of inhomoge -
neous microstructures on the fatigue properties induced by stress con -
centrations of components has rarely been investigated either 
experimentally or theoretically. 
Recently, an experimental study was carried out on the influence of 
inhomogeneous rafting on the fatigue properties of an SC Ni-based su -
peralloy [23] . The quantitative rafting distribution near the centre hole 
was carefully characterized through scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM) and digital image processing algorithms. It is found that the 
rafting morphology tends to be uniform, which weakens the effect of the 
inhomogeneous rafting microstructure on fatigue resistance as the 
rafting state exceeds a certain extent. Moreover, uneven microstructural 
rafting affects crack initiation and the path of crack propagation. To the 
best of our knowledge, [23] is the first investigation that focuses on the 
linkage between the inhomogeneous microstructure and fatigue be -
haviours of SC Ni-based superalloys under complex stress states. 
With regard to fatigue life modelling on notched components, many 
nonlocal approaches have been proposed and extended in recent de -
cades. These models can be divided into 5 categories: stress/strain/ 
energy-gradient-based methods [24,25] , highly stress volume-based 
methods [26] , critical zone-based methods [27] , weight function- 
based methods [28,29] and combinations of the above methods 
[30,31,32] . Zhu et al. [31] proposed a strain energy gradient-based 
notched fatigue life model combined with the theory of critical dis -
tance, which shows superior ability for Ni-based superalloys and tita -
nium alloys. Li et al. [29] determined a systematic weight function to 
predict the creep and fatigue life considering geometrically different 
structures, which was validated by different notched components and 
real turbine blades. Based on the formation and growth of short cracks 
for SC Ni-based superalloys, Bourbita and R ´emy [32] proposed a damage 
model based on the plastic strain energy density and dilation energy 
density to predict high-temperature fatigue life in notched single-crystal 
superalloy members. However, few models to date have considered the 
microstructural degradation of the material in service, let alone the in -
homogeneity of the microstructure change in notched members. The 
inhomogeneous distribution of microstructure poses three matters for 
notched fatigue life prediction: (i) microstructural degradation alters the 
stress – strain responses of materials in the notch vicinity; (ii) the spatial 
gradient of the microstructure induced by the stress concentration needs 
to be considered; and (iii) the effect of statistical uncertainty of micro -
structural characteristics on the predicted life needs to be considered. 
In view of the above issues, the present work is devoted to building 
an approach that can quantitatively evaluate the effect of the inhomo -
geneous distribution of rafting on the notched fatigue life of SC Ni-based 
superalloys, which includes two parts: deterministic life modelling and 
uncertainty evaluation of the predicted life caused by micro statistical 
dispersion. First, this paper briefly summarizes the experimental results 
of centre-hole components made by an SC Ni-based superalloy [23] , 
where some new life data are supplemented by the present work. The 
rafting features and the stress – strain responses of the rafted materials of 
the centre-hole specimens are depicted by a stress state-related rafting 
model and a microstructural rafting state-based constitutive model. A 
strain energy density (SED)-based fatigue life model is then proposed by 
combining the concept of the damage zone and weight function, where 
the SED is modified by a rafting state parameter. Finally, the established 
framework calibrated by test data is adopted to evaluate the predicted 
lifetime uncertainty induced by the microstructure statistics.

The finite element (FE) analysis of the centre-hole specimen is con -

predicted lifetime uncertainty induced by microstructure statistics is 
evaluated based on the distribution of the real microstructural features 
and the Monte-Carlo sampling method. The main results are summa -
