Introduction
Operating conditions for details of the nickel-based superalloys under long-term high-temperature
loading necessitate high thermal stability of the alloy structure as a whole and g9-phase especially, as
the latter is an essential factor of the alloy strengthening. The direct investigation of the phase stability
of superalloy specimens in the range of operating temperatures is of major interest.
In the previous high-temperature X-ray investigations (1,2,3) the main attention was concentrated on
the lattice mismatch measurements. In our opinion, examination of the X-ray diffraction line shape and
its qualitative changes as a characteristic of
g9-phase solution process would be informative too (4).
Recently similar superalloy analysis was carried out by Bastie P. et al. (5) on the base of neutron
diffraction, but their investigations were not connected with the role of solidification regimes in the
phase stability. At present time some attempts were undertaken for the industrial improvement of the
superalloy mechanical properties in solid state by an exposure on their melt before solidification to
rearrange a short-order in the liquid metal (6). For some melt overheating regimes creep-rupture live
increases up to 1.5 (1000°C, 270 MPa) for the alloy ZhS 26 single crystal samples, while their growth
structure does not change considerably (7).
In the present work high-temperature X-ray technique was used to study the
g9-phase thermal
stability upon heating in the temperature range from 20°C to 1200°C for a series of ,001. single
crystal specimens of nickel-based superalloy ZhS 26 obtained using various regimes of the melt
overheating before solidification.
Experimental
The ZhS 26 superalloy used as the test material has the following nominal composition: 0.15 C, 5 Cr,
9 Co, 1.1 Mo, 11 W, 1.6 Nb, 1.0 Ti, 5.5 Al, 1.0 V and Ni - the balance (58 vol. % of g9). The single
crystals were grown at a rate of 10 mm/min and a temperature gradient of 100 K/cm using the Bridgman
crystal-growth technique.
Samples for X-ray diffraction examination were {001} plates, 12 mm in diameter and 2 mm thick,
cut from the single crystals normally to growth direction. The surface was electrolytically polished,
approximately 0.15 mm thick surface layer being removed.

In these studies we used DRON-3 X-ray diffractometer supplied with a UVD-2000 high-temperature
setup. The investigations were performed in Co Kb radiation and (004) reflection. Experiments were
carried out in a vacuum of 2 3 1024 mmHg in the temperature range from 20 to 1200°C. Samples were
continuously heated to 1200°C at a rate of 5 K/min. Note that the samples were isothermally held for
10 min before recording of the diffraction line profiles, then the heating was continued. The temperature
was controlled with a W-Re thermocouple attached to the sample.
The physical profile of the total
g 1 g9 diffraction (004) line was separated using the Fourier analysis
of the angular intensity function with subsequent graphic resolution of the individualg and g9 peaks (4).
A nickel single crystal was used as the standard. The lattice parameter a was determined accurate to 6
0.0001 nm, using the maximum intensity of the graphically resolved reflections.
The as-cast and annealed samples (1280°C, 5 h; 870°C, 27 h) were studied. Changes in the profile
of the total g 1 g9 diffraction line with temperature were found to be analogous in many aspects.
Differences reduce to the following: the total (004) line is slightly broadened for as-cast sample and is
initially narrow for the annealed sample. These data are in agreement with the results (5).
Results and Discussion
Single crystal ,001. samples were obtained at different regimes of the melt overheated above liquidus
before the solidification. The melt was overheated up to 1600°C, 1650°C, 1750°C or 1850°C and was
exposed for 10 min holding at all given regimes. The alloy structure exhibits a dendritic-cell solidifi-
cation type. The
g9 particles have regular cuboid morphology with a size about 0.40 mm and a coherent
type of the interphase boundary for all as-cast sample.
Figure 1 demonstrates the results obtained for as-cast single crystal samples of the superalloy for
1650°C and 1750°C, because changes in the shape of the total g 1 g9 line upon heating are identical
for samples produced with preliminary melt overheating to 1600°C, 1650°C and 1850°C.
The X-ray diffraction peaks of the coherent isomorphic g and g9 phases with intimately close lattice
parameters tend to overlap, which calls for their separation. The graphical separation of the peaks is
based on the knowledge that the diffraction peak of the intermetallic phase is sharp and the peak of the
g solid solution is considerably broadened due to the segregation.
All (004) line profiles were normalized to a unit with respect to the maximum intensity. The g phase
peak was approximated with the function: k2 (sin pkx/pkx)2, where k is a constant. All three parameters,
namely, the intensity of the peak normalized to the phase content in the alloy, the peak position in the
x 5 2
u axis, and the value of k were selected in order to obtain the best fit with experimental data.
The lattice mismatch was determined as d 5 (ag9 2 ag)/ag9, where ag and ag9 are the lattice
parameters of the solid solution and the g9 phase, respectively. Note that the mismatch value d is an
average value because of a significant change of the g lattice parameter due to high level of casting
segregation in the dendritic structure (7,8). The lattice mismatch d remains negligibly small (0.04 6
0.01%) upon heating of samples from room temperature to the start temperature of g9 dissolution
(870°C) .
Irrespective of overheating of the melt before samples solidification the g9 phase partially dissolves
with temperature increase above 900°C, which gives an additional g peak on the low angle 2u side near
the total diffraction line, see Fig. 1. This additional g solid solution differs in composition from the
initial one. This new g solution is enriched in aluminum, nickel and niobium, which increase the g
lattice parameter (4). And the total g1g9 line (004) in this case consists of three peaks: one for g9 and
two for g-phase. Heating results in a change of the lattice parameters of the alloy phases and a shift of
the total (004) line along the 2u axis, while the angle distance between maximums of these three peaks
remains unaltered up to 1100°C, to a first approximation. Only at 1100°C we observe some variation

Both the g9 intermetallic phase and the g solid solution have a fcc structure, but they differ in
chemical compositions. At the beginning stage of the g9 dissolution the chemical composition changes
in the solid solution areas near by the g - g9 boundary. Practically, the chemical composition of these
areas is close to that of the intermetallic phase. And two g solid solutions are observed. This metastable
state may exist during long time, and we need to take it into account for the description of the
high-temperature behavior of the superalloy.

Summary
The melt constituent modification before the single crystal solidification may affect the properties of
nickel-based superalloy in the solid state and, particularly, g9- phase thermal stability. In the single
crystal samples of ZhS 26 alloy cast from 1750°C the g9- phase appears to be the most stable to
dissolution on continuous heating to 1200°C. High-temperature X-ray diffraction examination based on
the qualitative analysis of the change of the total
g 1 g9 line shape is useful for studying of theg9-phase
dissolution process.
