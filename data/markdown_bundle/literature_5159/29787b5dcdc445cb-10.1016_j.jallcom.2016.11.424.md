Fabrication and characterization of nano-Y2O3 dispersed W-Ni-Mo and W-Ni-Ti-Nb alloys 
by mechanical alloying and spark plasma sintering 
A. Patra 1,* , R. Saxena 1, S. K. Karak 1, T. Laha 2, S. K. Sahoo 1 
1 National Institute of Technology, Rourkela-769008, Odisha, India. 
2 Indian Institute of Technology, Kharagpur-721302, West Bengal, India. 
Abstract. In the present investigation, nano-Y 2O3 dispersed W-Ni-Mo and W-Ni-Ti-Nb alloys 
with nominal composition of W 79 Ni 10 Mo 10 (Y 2O3)1 (alloy A) and W 74 Ni 10 Ti 5Nb 10 (Y 2O3)1 (alloy 
B) (all in wt.%) were synthesized by mechanical all oying (MA) for 20 h followed by spark 
plasma sintering (SPS) at 1000°C, 1200°C and 1400°C for alloy A and at 1400°C for alloy B, 
respectively for 5 min at 75 MPa pressure. Higher r eduction of crystallite size and increase in 
lattice strain in alloy B is attributed to increase d brittleness caused by Ti addition. Texture 
intensity and residual stress increases and decreas es respectively with increase in SPS 
temperature. However, alloy B had a higher residual stress compared to alloy A, both sintered at 
1400°C. The sinterability, hardness, compressive st rength and wear resistance increased with 
increase in SPS temperature for alloy A. Alloy B si ntered at 1400°C achieved maximum 
hardness (11.89 GPa), compressive strength (2.26 GP a) and wear resistance (wear rate: 
1.14 ×10 -6 mm3/N m). Reduction in grain growth by rapid spark pla sma sintering cycle results in 
substantial improvement in strength, hardness with appreciable ductility as compared to 
conventionally sintered W-Y2O3 alloys. 
Keywords : W based alloys, mechanical alloying, spark plasma s intering, texture, hardness, 
wear.

1. Introduction 
Tungsten possess high melting point (3410°C), excel lent mechanical strength at elevated 
temperature, highest density (19.3 g/ml), hardness of 9.8 GPa, high tensile elastic modulus (411 
GPa at 20°C), low co-efficient of thermal expansion , appreciable thermal conductivity [1,2] and 
therefore widely used in electrical, nuclear reacto r, space vehicle applications. Tungsten based 
alloys are ideal choice as armour material in fourt h generation nuclear reactor application [3]. 
However tungsten applications are limited due to hi gh ductile brittle transition temperature 
(DBTT) (200-500ºC) [2] which leads to fabrication o f tungsten alloys with enhanced properties. 
Recent reports illustrates that transition metal su ch as Ni addition modifies the core of 
dislocation (from symmetric to asymmetric) which re sults in reduction of Peierls stress and 
enhances the ductility [4]. Ni addition facilitates liquid phase sintering to improve densification 
by filling of open pores owing to lower melting poi nt (1455°C) as compared to present sintering 
temperature (1500°C) [5]. Mo and Nb possess high me lting point (Mo: 2623°C, Nb: 2468°C) 
facilitates easy alloying with W and retains high t emperature strength [5, 6]. DBTT of Mo is 
around room temperature whereas DBTT of Nb is aroun d -200°C and therefore reduces the 
DBTT of W [2, 7], whereas Ti eases the densificatio n, restricts the grain growth and improves 
the strength of W, however it increases the DBTT of W [8]. 
Dispersion of ultrafine, thermodynamically stable o xide particles at the interface of tungsten 
matrix provides strong pinning action to the grain boundary and enhances the strength, creep 
resistance at elevated temperature [6]. Mechanical alloying is a potential method to synthesize 
nanostructured alloy as compared to rapid quenching [9], severe plastic deformation (SPD) [10]. 
Fabrication of nanostructured alloy is beneficial i n reducing the sintering temperature of alloy 
and enhancement of hardness and strength [11]. Pres ent study reports the fabrication and 
investigation of microstructure evolution, texture, mechanical behavior of Y 2O3 dispersed 
nanostructured W-Ni-Mo and W-Ni-Ti-Nb alloys.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1 . XRD study 
The XRD pattern of the mechanically alloyed powders of alloy B is displayed in Fig. 1(a). It is 
evident that Nb diffuses into W lattice to form sol id solution (W ss ) with increase in milling time. 
W and Nb shows unrestricted solid solubility owing to similar crystal structure (both BCC) and 
marginal difference between atomic radius (W: 0.193 nm, Nb: 0.198 nm). Crystallite size of W 
reduces with increase in milling time as evident fr om broadening and reduction in the intensity 
of the XRD peak of W. The intensity and broadening of both W and solute elements mainly Nb 
and lesser extend of Ni, Ti peak decreases and incr eases respectively with increase in milling 
time as evident from Fig. 1(a). This is attributed to improved diffusion owing to reduction in the 
interparticle distance between W and solute element s with the progress of milling. Ni shows 
limited solubility in W as evident from the presenc e of Ni diffraction peak even after 20 h of 
milling owing to difference in crystal structure (N i: FCC), and atomic radius (W: 0.193 nm, Ni: 
0.149 nm). According to W-Ti phase diagram, Ti solu bility in W is 3.12 wt. % in mechanically

Fig. 5(a) shows the variation of hardness across th e cross section in alloy A (1000°C, 1200°C,

Conclusion 
Nano Y 
2O3 dispersed W-Ni-Mo and W-Ni-Nb-Ti alloys are fabrica ted by mechanical alloying 
followed by spark plasma sintering. Minimum crystal lite size, maximum lattice strain and 
dislocation density of 23 nm, 0.49%, 26.71×10 16 /m 2 respectively has been achieved in 20 h 
milled W 74 Ni 10 Nb 10 Ti 5(Y 2O3)1 alloy. Texture intensity increases with increase i n SPS 
temperature and Ti addition whereas the residual st ress decreases with increase in SPS 
temperature and increases with Ti addition. The %de nsification of W 74 Ni 10 Nb 10 Ti 5(Y 2O3)1 and 
W79 Ni 10 Mo 10 (Y 2O3)1 alloy is comparable at identical SPS temperature a nd increases with 
increase in SPS temperature in W 79 Ni 10 Mo 10 (Y 2O3)1 alloy due to enhanced interparticle bonding 
at higher temperature. The higher hardness (11.89 G Pa), strength (2.26 GPa) of 
W74 Ni 10 Nb 10 Ti 5(Y 2O3)1 alloy is attributed to finer crystallite size and superior sinterability and 
higher content of hard intermetallic phases. Combin ed addition of Ti and Nb results in marginal 
reduction in elongation of W 74 Ni 10 Nb 10 Ti 5(Y 2O3)1 alloy as compared to W 79 Ni 10 Mo 10 (Y 2O3)1 
alloy SPS at 1400ºC . The wear rate of W 74 Ni 10 Nb 10 Ti 5(Y 2O3)1 alloy is substantially lower as 
compared to W 79 Ni 10 Mo 10 (Y 2O3)1 alloy due to superior hardness, strength and predo minant 
hindrance to dislocation movement by NbNi, Ni 3Ti intermetallic phases.
