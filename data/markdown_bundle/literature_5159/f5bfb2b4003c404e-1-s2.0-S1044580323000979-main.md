ABSTRACT 
Due to compositional complexity, designing superalloys with multiple targeted properties is a great challenge. 
Here, we propose a workflow that incorporates composition and high-temperature mechanical performance into 
machine learning to optimize commercial K403 superalloys for the needs of increasing service temperature of 
hot-end parts in aircraft engine and gas turbine. Moreover, multiple properties including microstructure stability, 
the volume fraction of γ’ precipitates, processing window, freezing range and density were simultaneously 
optimized to select 7 superalloys from 15,625 candidates. One selected superalloy was experimentally synthe -
sized. Compared with the commercial K403 superalloy, the creep rupture life of the newly-designed superalloy is 
improved around three times at 975 
◦
C and even 1025 
◦
C. The predicted high-temperature creep rupture life and 
yield strength using the machine learning model is in excellent agreement with experiments. The current ma -
chine learning approach provides guidance for the rapid design of multi-component superalloys with targeted 
multiple desired functionalities. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based superalloys are the most state-of-the-art high-temper -
ature materials for applications in the hot-end parts of aircraft engine 
and gas turbine [ 1 ]. The increase of service temperature of superalloys is 
always a pursuit for the improvement of engine thrust and combustion 
efficiency. However, engine operation temperatures are reaching limits 
raised by the melting temperatures of superalloys. Designing new su -
peralloys is a great challenge to meet increasing demands of better 
mechanical performance at higher service temperatures. In the design of 
Ni-based superalloys, the compositional complexity causes difficulties in 
balancing multiple properties because the involvement of multiple 
components, including Ni, Cr, Co, W, Mo, Al, Ti, Ta, Nb and other trace 
elements [ 2 ]. To improve high-temperature properties, the influence of 
each component in Ni-based superalloys needs to be considered. Due to 
the complex composition of superalloy system and the complex in -
teractions among different elements, a large amount of experimental 
works needs to be carried out to optimize their properties. To design a 
new superalloy with higher mechanical performance, the corresponding 
experimental cost is huge. This conventional trial-and-error alloy design 
method is extremely time-consuming and largely relies on materials 
designers ’ experiences [ 3 ]. Thus, new efficient approaches have to be 
established to accelerate the discovery of superior superalloys for the 
needs of rapid iterations of engines. 
Materials design empowered by empirical or physics-based modeling 
and genomic materials databases accelerate superior superalloy inno -
vation [ 4 ]. The CALPHAD approach serves as a fundamental technique 
for establishing the materials ’ genomic databases, and is widely recog -
nized as a foundational tool for data-driven material design. Adopting 
CALPHAD (calculation of phase diagrams) database as the thermody -
namics input and microstructure-resistance of creep and oxidation 
models, Reed et al. [ 5 , 6 ] successfully design three single crystal super -
alloys for different application merit targets. Meanwhile, the accuracy of 
this CALPHAD-based modeling approach was validated by experiments. 
By integrating computational simulation models and focused testing 
data, the prediction accuracy of superalloy properties has been 
dramatically improved and alloy processing optimization has been 
realized at the component level. However, modeling and predicting the

3.2. Experimental verification 
3.2.1. Microstructure 
Here, we chose the alloy 1 (denoted as New Alloy below) from the 
screened 7 alloy compositions listed in Table 3 to verify its mechanical 
performance. Also, K403 alloy was fabricated as a comparison [ 1 ]. The 
microstructure of the K403 alloy and New Alloy after heat treated at 
1210 
◦
C for 4 h were examined by back scattered electron (BSE) imaging 
technique, as shown in Fig. 5 . The grain size of K403 and New Alloy is 
1400 μ m and 230 μ m, respectively. A large number of carbides are both 
formed in the boundaries between dendritic and interdendritic regions 
in the K403 and New Alloy, which hinders the element diffusion and 
causes elemental heterogeneity. Still, the cuboidal γ’ phase was formed 
and uniformly distributed in both heat-treated K403 and New Alloy, 
different from the irregular polygon shape found in the as-cast K403. 
The area fraction of the γ’ phase is statistically counted as 68% for K403 
and 76% for New Alloy. The average size of γ’ precipitates is 370 nm in 
K403 and 167 nm in New Alloy. Notice that MC carbides were also 
formed in both K403 and New Alloy. Kontis et al. [ 19 ] pointed that when 
MC carbides are continuously formed along grain boundaries, the ease of 
initiation and growth of grain boundary cracks deteriorates mechanical 
performance at high temperatures. However, isolated MC carbide par -
ticles in both K403 and New Alloy are randomly distributed at grain 
boundaries as revealed in Fig. 5 , and thus the tendency of crack for -
mation and growth is dramatically reduced [ 20 ].

4. Discussion 
To understand the improved creep performance of New Alloy, the 
deformation mechanism is studied by the examination of dislocation 
morphology in the crept samples, as shown in Fig. 8 . In the New Alloy 
and K403 crept at 975 
◦
C/195 MPa, dislocations were only formed in the 
interconnecting matrix channel. Dislocations in the crept New Alloy, 
however, entangle with each other ( Fig.8 a) and form dislocation 
network ( Fig. 8 b), whereas wavy dislocations in the crept the K403 alloy 
barely interact with each other and mainly locates at the γ / γ’ interfaces 
( Fig. 8 c and d). Previous studies have proved that dislocation network 
plays an important role in the improved mechanical performance of 
superalloys [ 21 – 23 ]. The formation of dislocation networks is generally 
formed by two mechanisms. On one hand, parallel dislocations (as 
shown on the right side of the matrix channel in Fig.8 b) continuously 
intersect dislocations on other slip planes (as shown on the left side of 
the matrix channel in Fig. 8 b), and react to form dislocation networks 
[ 2 , 24 ]. On the other hand, dislocation networks are formed by the re -
action of intersecting dislocations at nodes [ 25 , 26 ], as indicated by red 
arrows in Fig. 6 b. Additionally, we calculated stacking fault energy 
(SFE) of New Alloy and K403 by CALPHAD method. The SFE of New 
Alloy, 223.9 mJ/m
2
, is slightly larger than the one of K403, 210.4 mJ/ 
m
2
. And alloys with a high SFE have the tendency of dislocation cross- 
slip and plug [ 27 , 28 ] and thus preferentially form dislocation network 
structures, contributing to the creep performance.

5. Conclusions 
To summarize, a workflow incorporating composition features into 
machine learning model was used to efficiently improve the high- 
temperature mechanical properties by tuning the composition of K403 
superalloy. The success of the property prediction based on the machine 
learning model was verified by experiments conducted at high temper -
atures. The following conclusions can be drawn from our studies: 
1) High accuracy with the R factor larger than 99% was obtained for a 
multi-layer ANN model established by the Levenberg-Marquarelt 
algorithm with the composition feature as input and high- 
temperature strength and creep rupture life as outputs. The input 
and output data were calculated by JMatPro, and its reliability at the 
practical application temperature range was calibrated by experi -
mental data. 
2) With the consideration of optimized multiple properties including 
creep, high-temperature yield strength, microstructure stability and 
processing, seven new compositions were screened out from the 
composition pool predicted by machine learning model. And high- 
temperature mechanical tests were conducted on one newly- 
designed alloy composition, demonstrating the high accuracy of 
the trained machine learning model. 
3) Compared with the commercial K403 superalloy, the newly-designed 
superalloy exhibits an improvement of creep rupture life at both 
975 
◦
C/195 MPa and 1025 
◦
C/195 MPa by a factor of three. These 
experimental creep rupture results agree well with the predications 
from the machine learning model. On the other hand, a reasonable 
agreement was reached between the experimental tensile yield 
strength at 850 
◦
