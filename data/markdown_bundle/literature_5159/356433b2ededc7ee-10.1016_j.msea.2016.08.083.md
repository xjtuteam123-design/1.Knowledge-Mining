abstract
Martensitic transformation in off-stoichiometric Co 2VSi Heusler alloy was ﬁrstly conﬁrmed in this work.
The martensite phase presents an ordered 2M non-modulate D0 22 structure transformed from L2 1
Heusler structure. In the Co 63.5V17.0Si19.5 alloy, the forward and reverse martensitic transformation
temperatures are extremely high which reach up to 683 °C and 693 °C, respectively, higher than most
known Heusler-type high-temperature shape memory alloys. And results also indicate that the single
martensite phase alloy exhibits a good ductility and strength with the compressive strain of over 15% and
strength of about 2300 MPa, respectively. What's more, the recoverable strain being maximal to around
3.1% can be obtained at high temperature (750 – 800 °C) due to its reverse martensitic transformation.

1. Introduction
From AuCd alloys [1] and follow-up NiTi alloys [2], the shape
memory alloys (SMA) have been greatly developed owing to two
unique properties, namely shape memory effect (SME) and su-
perelasticity (SE) [3]. Since then, many attentions have been paid
to this kind of smart materials, especially the researches in the
application ﬁelds of pipe coupling, actuators, antennae and med-
ical implants [3]. However, the operating temperatures of SMA are
usually below 100 °C in the practical service [4]. To extent the
usage of SMA to elevated-temperature applications ( e.g., aero-
space, nuclear industry, and chemical engineering), the develop-
ment of high temperature shape memory alloys (HTSMA) is de-
sired [5]. To date, many SMA systems, such as Ti-, Cu-, Ni-, and Zr-
based [6– 18], are considered as HTSMA, which can be classi ﬁed
into several types based on the different transformation tem-
perature ranges [4]. However, it is found that only a few alloys
possess high martensitic transformation temperatures above
700 °C [19– 21]. Thus, Exploring new HTSMA for higher tempera-
ture applications is signi ﬁcant.
Recently, some Heusler alloys, especially Ni-Mn-Ga based al-
loys, have also been investigated systematically as HTSMA [22– 25].
This kind of alloys exhibits a good high temperature shape
memory effect due to their highly ordered parent phase ( i.e.,L 2 1
phase) [4]. However, the polycrystalline brittleness of ordered
phase prevents its practical applications [25]. On the other hand,
concerning the relatively good mechanical property, processability
and corrosion resistance [26], Co-based alloys might have a broad
prospect in the HTSMA area. Until now, martensitic transforma-
tions have been reported only in a few Co-based Heusler alloys,
such as Co-Nb-Sn [27], Co-Cr-Ga-Si [28] and Co-Cr-Al-Si [29] sys-
tems. (While strictly speaking, the martensite in Co-Ni-Al and Co-
Ni-Ga alloys is transformed from B2, not L2 1 Heusler structure [30–
33].) But the martensitic transformation temperatures in these
alloys are relatively low, making it not applicable at high tem-
perature. Lately, we found that some Co-V-Si Heusler alloys with
L21 structure exhibit martensitic transformation at high tem-
peratures of about 700 °C, higher than most known Heusler-type
HTSMAs. Meanwhile, compared to Ni-Mn-Ga, the martensite al-
loys present good mechanical properties. In the present work, the
crystal structure, phase transformation behaviors, mechanical
property and high temperature shape memory effect in the off-
stoichiometric Co 2VSi Heusler alloys were further investigated.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure and crystal structures
Fig. 1 shows the optical micrograph and BSE image of the stu-
died alloy. It is seen that the alloy exhibits representative plate
martensite microstructure with clear grain boundary, and no
second phase can be observed. It is notable that the size of grain is
relatively big and the width of martensite plates is relatively nar-
row. Fig. 2 illustrates the X-ray diffraction patterns taken from the
studied alloy at room temperature. However, for the sake of a large
grain size as well as a possible preferred orientation in the pre-
pared bulk sample of the Co
63.5V17.0Si19.5 alloy, the number of
peaks in the XRD pattern is limited, even though rudimentary
conclusion can be drawn that it might be L1 0 or D022 structure. At
the same time, there is no peak representing the residual parent
phase retained in the martensitic transformation, maybe due to its
few amount. On the other hand, what's worth mentioned is that in
the Co
63V12Si25 alloy, every peak in the powder XRD patterns can
be indexed and the crystal structure was determined to be single
L21 Heusler structure with aL21¼ 0.5592 nm. In order to con ﬁrm
the crystal structure of the Co 63.5V17. 0Si19.5 alloy precisely, TEM
observations were carried out, with the results shown in Fig. 3 .I t
can be observed in Fig. 3 (a) that the martensite contains a large
amount of twin defects. The corresponding SADP with some faint
ordered spots can be indexed with the tetragonal L1 0 or D0 22
tetragonal structure. On the other hand, the retained parent phase
was found in a very limited region of the same sample as shown in
Fig. 3(b), which can be determined to be the L2
1 Heusler structure.
Generally speaking, if the L2 1 parent phase transforms to a tetra-
gonal martensite, the martensite will become D0 22 structure in-
stead of L10 structure due to the atomic con ﬁguration of the parent
phase [28,29,34]. Actually, the XRD pattern for the Co 63.5V17.0Si19.5
alloy exhibited in Fig. 2 (a) can be indexed as the D0 22 structure
with an estimated lattice constants of aD022 ¼0.3701 nm and
cD022 ¼ 0.6592 nm, which are consistent with the SADP index. So it
is implied that the martensitic transformation is from L2 1 to D0 22
structure in the studied alloy.

3.3. Mechanical property
The mechanical property and pre-deformation were carried out
by compressive tests. It is quite necessary to declare that as dis-
cussed afterwards, this alloy is hard, leading errors of measured
strains caused by the jig's deformation in the axial direction dur-
ing mechanical process could not be ignored, and there is still a
small deviation after calibration. So the residual strains (
εr) of pre-
deformed samples were determined by actual measurements with
micrometer of an accuracy of 0.01 mm, and the strains shown by
the compressive curves turn to be semi-quantitative as well. So,
calibrated strain-stress curves of three typical Co 63.5V17.0Si19.5
polycrystalline samples cut from different parts in different di-
rections of homogenized alloy button are exhibited in Fig. 7 (a),
with strains having deviations of 10 – 20% of themselves, relying on
experience. It can be seen that the samples are extremely hard as
the breaking stresses reach about 2300 MPa, demonstrating a
good strength of this single-phase alloy. And despite of the errors,
the breaking strains of samples reach about 15%. By observing the
broken samples' fracture surfaces which ruptures at an angle of
about 45 ° (Fig. 7 (b– c)), it's found that there are some slip bands
and microcracks (shown by yellow arrows in Fig. 7 (b)) only gen-
erate vertical to stress's direction along the martensite grain
boundary, which both illustrate the integral brittleness and rela-
tively good ductility of Co
63.5V17.0Si19.5 alloy comparing to the Ni-
Mn-based single martensite phase alloys [4,5,36,37]. And the
mechanical properties of this alloy can still enhanced by the
methods of grain boundary strengthening and second phase
toughening.

4. Conclusions
In this study, the martensitic transformation and high tem-
perature shape memory effect of Co 63.5V17. 0Si19.5 alloy were in-
vestigated. The results show that the martensitic transformation is
from L21 parent phase to D0 22 non-modulate martensite (2M) and
the As and Ms temperatures are 693 °C and 683 °C, respectively,
higher than most known Heusler-type HTSMAs, even though the
stability of martensite still needs improved due to the effect of
other phase. In addition, the alloy possesses a good strength of
about 2300 MPa and compressive ductility of about 15% at room
temperature. Meanwhile, it exhibits an apparent shape memory
effect at high temperatures at about 750 – 800 °C, where the re-
covery strain of about 3.1% for residual strain of 7.1% ( i.e., recovery
rate of about 44%) was realized.
