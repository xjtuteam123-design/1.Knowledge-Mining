ABSTRACT
Ti-Al-based alloys have emerged as promising lightweight alternatives to nickel-based superalloys in aerospace, 
energy, and automotive applications. However, their limited room-temperature plasticity and insufficient high- 
temperature strength significantly constrain their broader utilization in aerospace components. Pulsed magnetic 
field treatment (PMT) is a green and efficient method to enhance the mechanical properties of solid-state alloys. 
This study systematically investigates the effects of PMT on the microstructural evolution and mechanical per -
formance of as-cast Ti-Al-X (Cr, V, Zr) alloys at both ambient and elevated temperatures (800 ℃ ), under varying 
magnetic induction intensities (B). Mechanical properties were evaluated through tensile testing. Among the 
tested samples, the alloy treated at B = 3 T exhibited optimal performance, with room-temperature elongation 
increasing by10.7 % to 3.1 % and tensile strength improving by 23.3 % to 379.7 MPa. At 800 ℃ , the tensile 
strength and elongation reached 544.2 MPa and 14.3 %, respectively, corresponding to enhancements of 4.9 % 
and 32.4 %. Fracture morphology analysis revealed a mixed fracture mode, featuring both inter-lamellar and 
trans-lamellar characteristics. The underlying mechanisms of PMT-induced microstructural and mechanical 
property improvements were elucidated, highlighting the roles of magnetic stress and thermal effects in refining 
lamellar spacing, reducing lamellar thickness, enhancing grain orientation, and promoting the precipitation of 
equiaxed γ -phase grains. Furthermore, the exceptional high-temperature performance was attributed to the 
synergistic effects of dislocation jog dragging (DJD) and twin-induced plasticity (TWIP) during deformation. 
These findings provide critical insights into the enhancement of TiAl alloy properties through advanced physical 
field treatments.
1. Introduction
The aerospace industry increasingly demands materials with high 
strength, superior heat resistance, excellent oxidation resistance, and 
low density. TiAl alloys, characterized by a density of 3.9 – 4.2 g/cm³ , 
high specific strength, and outstanding oxidation and creep resistance, 
have garnered significant attention as lightweight structural materials 
[1 – 3] . These alloys are extensively employed in aero-engine turbine 
blades, aerospace structural components, and high-temperature engine 
parts [4,5] . However, their widespread application is hindered by two 
primary limitations: (1) low room-temperature tensile plasticity ( < 2 %), 
which falls short of traditional processing requirements, and (2) a sharp 
decline in strength above 700 ℃ , restricting their potential as substitutes 
for nickel-based superalloys.
High-pulse magnetic fields, which represent an extreme processing 
condition, serve as a crucial approach for enhancing material properties 
and have been utilized across various domains [6,7] . A significant 
number of scholars have effectively applied pulsed magnetic field 
technology to optimize the microstructures and mechanical properties 
of metals and alloys [8 – 13] . Pulsed magnetic fields have shown signif -
icant potential in enhancing material properties through the precise 
control of phase transformations and the modification of dislocation 
dynamics. Phase transformations are influenced by changes in the sys -
tem ’ s free energy. As a material approaches its phase transformation 
critical point, the system enters a metastable state that renders it highly 
sensitive to external perturbations. This phenomenon provides a theo -
retical basis for the intervention of magnetic fields [14] . At the dislo -
cation level, the magnetic field serves as both a driving force for

practically relevant.
This study investigates the microstructure, mechanical properties, 
and fracture morphology of as-cast Ti-Al-X (Cr, V, Zr) alloy after pulsed 
magnetic field treatment (PMT), with a focus on the strengthening 
mechanisms induced by pulsed magnetic fields. Additionally, the factors 
contributing to the enhanced mechanical properties at 800 ℃ and the 
toughening mechanisms during high-temperature deformation are 
explored. The results offer a novel approach for improving the perfor -
mance of Ti-Al-based alloys and other solid materials.
2. Experimental methods and procedures
2.1. Materials and pulsed magnetic field solid-state treatment
The experimental material was as-cast Ti-Al-X (Cr, V, Zr) (denoted as 
TiAlX alloy), with its specific composition detailed in Table 1 . A sche -
matic diagram of the pulsed-magnetic field generator (model: TSK- 
H2060, a voltage-stabilized magnetizer) is illustrated in Fig. 1 . Based 
on prior experimental protocols, samples were treated under magnetic 
induction intensities ( B ) of 3 T, 4 T, 5 T, and 6 T. The pulsed magnetic 
field device automatically performs discharge and magnetization on the 
sample every 30 s, completing one pulse cycle ( N ), with 30 pulses per 
condition ( Table 2 ). The specimens underwent pulsed magnetic field 
treatment, followed by analysis and mechanical property testing.

3.2. Microstructure characterization
Fig. 4 illustrates the optical microstructure of TiAlX alloys subjected 
to different magnetic induction intensities. Figs. 4 (a) to 4 (e) depict the 
metallographic structure of PMT samples at RT. As the magnetic in -
duction intensity increases, there is a marked reduction in the grain size 
of the TiAlX alloy, accompanied by a significant increase in grain count. 
Fig. 4 (k) demonstrates that the PMT effectively refines the grains of the 
TiAlX alloy. The untreated sample exhibited a coarse grain size of 620 
μ m, whereas PMT3 and PMT5 samples showed significant refinement, 
with average grain sizes reduced to 124 μ m and 120 μ m, respectively 
(representing reductions of 80 % and 81 %). Figs. 4 (f) to 4 (j) and Fig. 4
(l) present the metallographic structure and grain sizes of PMT samples 
at 800 ℃ . For the untreated sample and the PMT3 sample, the grain sizes 
are 700 μ m and 182 μ m, respectively, signifying increases of 13 % and 
47 % when compared to the RT samples. The grain growth at elevated 
temperatures is attributed to enhanced atomic activity along with 
accelerated migration and diffusion rates.

presented in Fig. 5 , which depicts the microstructure of the PMT spec -

3.6. The exceptional strength and toughness exhibited by PMT-TiAlX 
alloy at 800 ℃
The mechanical properties of TiAlX alloys are determined by their 
deformation mechanisms, which are influenced by the characteristics of 
the lamellar structure. According to the mechanism of PMT, the syner -
gistic enhancement of strength and plasticity in TiAlX alloys at both RT 
and 800 ℃ is primarily attributed to the interaction among phase 
transformation, grain refinement, texture strengthening, and increased 
dislocation density induced by solid-state processing with strong pulsed 
magnetic fields. The superior strength and plasticity observed during 
tensile testing at 800 ℃ compared to RT can be ascribed to the combined 
effects of dislocation jog dragging (DJD) and twin-induced plasticity 
(TWIP), which are further enhanced by the action of the pulsed magnetic 
field.
During high-temperature deformation, ordinary dislocations in TiAl 
alloys tend to undergo cross-slip due to their compact core structure, 
resulting in the formation of high-density dislocation bends [50] . These 
dislocation bends impede dislocation motion, increase dislocation den -
sity, and consequently enhance the strength of the alloy. Additionally, 
dislocation bends serve as sources for the generation of new dislocations, 
thereby promoting dislocation multiplication and further increasing 
dislocation density. This phenomenon not only imposes additional 
resistance to dislocation movement but also contributes to a higher 
dislocation density, causing the strength of TiAlX alloys at 800 ℃ to 
surpass that at RT and elucidating the underlying mechanism of 
high-temperature strengthening.
Furthermore, during high-temperature tensile testing, the γ phase 
demonstrated not only dislocation slip but also the formation of high- 
density deformation twins and twin intersections. Dislocations and 
twins were identified within the deformed lamellar structure ( Fig. 7 (e)). 
The high temperatures provide sufficient thermal energy for atomic 
movement, facilitating atomic rearrangement and lattice distortion 
associated with twinning. This process promotes mechanical twinning, 
optimizing the balance between strength and plasticity. Specifically, 
deformation twins and twin intersections serve to reduce the average 
free path of dislocations, leading to an increased dislocation density that 
significantly enhances strain hardening capabilities, enabling the alloy 
to withstand greater plastic deformation. Twin intersections function as 
effective locking mechanisms, obstructing the propagation of twin- 
related dislocations [30] ( Fig. 7 (g)). Moreover, twins not only pro -
mote plastic deformation but also interact with dislocations, signifi -
cantly affecting the deformation behavior of materials. Our results align 
with Liu et al. [51] , who reported that high-density deformation twins 
enhance plasticity in γ -TiAl alloys, but our PMT-induced twin in -
tersections show superior strength-ductility synergy. While high-density 
twin structures may obstruct dislocation motion, dislocations are 
capable of circumventing these twins.
Dislocations were also observed within stepped twins ( Fig. 7 (f)), 
indicating an interaction between dislocations and twins that results in 
increased dislocation density and reduced stacking fault energy, thereby 
enhancing the alloy ’ s capacity for plastic deformation. When the 
orientation of twins is conducive to dislocation slip, they promote 
dislocation transmission, further enhancing alloy plasticity. Conversely, 
when the orientation of twins obstructs dislocation slip, they act as 
barriers to dislocation motion or form dislocation blocks through 
dislocation decomposition reactions, thereby strengthening the alloy 
( Fig. 17 ).
4. Conclusions
This study systematically investigated the influence of PMT on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of TiAlX alloys, elucidating 
the strengthening mechanisms and the reasons for enhanced high- 
temperature performance. The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) Phase Transformation Control: PMT at 3 T initiates an atomic 
migration-driven α ₂ →γ transformation in TiAlX alloys, facilitated 
by dislocations and vacancies that promote the precipitation of 
equiaxed γ grains. This transformation is governed by a ledge 
mechanism in which magnetic stress lowers the energy barrier for 
dislocation-mediated nucleation.
(2) Synergistic Strengthening: The refined lamellae structure (from 
588.2 nm to 355.3 nm) and increased α ₂ / γ interfaces suppress 
crack propagation, while magneto-plasticity activates dislocation 
slip across grain boundaries.
(3) Microstructural Refinement: The treatment reduces average grain
