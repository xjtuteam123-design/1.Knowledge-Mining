Inﬂuence of magnetic arc oscillation and current pulsing on microstructure
and high temperature tensile strength of alloy 718 TIG weldments

Abstract
The aim of the present work is to study the effect of magnetic arc oscillation and current pulsing on the microstructure and high temperature
tensile strength of alloy 718 tungsten inert gas weldments. The magnetic arc oscillation technique resulted in reﬁned Laves phase with lesser
interconnectivity. The full beneﬁts of current pulsing in breaking the dendrites could not be realized in the present study due to relatively higher
heat input used in the welding process. In the direct aged condition weldments prepared using magnetic arc oscillation technique exhibited higher
tensile strength due to the presence of reﬁned and lesser-interconnected Laves particles. In the solution treated and aged condition, magnetic arc
oscillated weldments exhibited lower tensile strength compared with the weldments made without arc oscillation due to the presence of large
amounts of ﬁner /H9254needles.

1. Introduction
Alloy 718 is a nickel based precipitation-hardenable super-
alloy, which is a workhorse material in aerospace industry. It
possesses excellent weldability characteristics among all super-
alloys due to its sluggish response to aging treatment. Irrespec-
tive of the pre-weld condition of alloy 718, a post-weld aging
treatment must be given, which not only induces the strengthen-
ing precipitation but also acts as a stress relieving treatment of
the weldment. Direct double aging treatment is a common indus-
trial practice for alloy 718 welds. However, use of a post-weld
solutionizing treatment prior to normal double aging treatment is
also in practice for critical applications[1]. The post-weld aging
treatment serves in reducing unavoidable Laves phase and the
segregation of useful alloying elements like Nb in weld fusion
zones. Mehl and Lippold reported the inﬂuence of/H9254phase along
with Laves volume fraction on repair weldability[2]. Larger vol-
ume fractions of the/H9254phase degrades weldability characteristics
of the alloy 718 and a high temperature post-weld solutionizing

Laves is an unavoidable brittle intermetallic phase (Ni, Fe,
Cr)2 (Nb, Mo, Ti, Si) with a hexagonal close packed crystal
structure in alloy 718 as a consequence of interdendritic Nb
segregation. As the Laves phase consumes signiﬁcant amounts
of useful alloying elements and provides favorable crack initi-
ation sites and ease in propagation, its presence degrades the
mechanical properties of alloy 718 and its weldments. Since
the elemental segregation during solidiﬁcation is strongly inﬂu-
enced by the cooling rates, the extent of Nb segregation and
the amount of Laves phase should vary accordingly in different
welding processes. Radhakrishna and Prasad Rao have shown
that the extent of the Nb segregation was less in electron beam
welds than that in tungsten inert gas (TIG) welds [3].
In TIG welding, techniques like magnetic arc oscillation and
pulsed current are used to increase cooling rate. In magnetic
arc oscillation technique, the severe agitation of the weld pool
breaks down the growing dendrites, which can act as favorable
nucleating sites and results in increased cooling rates. In case of
pulsed current technique the welding current is pulsed between
two levels. The background current ( I

The chemical composition (wt.%) of the material alloy 718

3.1. Microstructure

had a dendritic structure with the interdendritic regions etching
dark. Among the direct aged weld fusion zones, magnetic arc
oscillated welds exhibited reﬁned and less interconnected Laves

phase. The solution treated and aged welds exhibited similar

The magnetic arc oscillation breaks the dendrite branches
that are formed during cooling of the molten weld pool. These
broken dendrites act as favorable nucleating sites resulting in
increased cooling rates of the weld pool. The current pulsing also
should have resulted in a similar effect of increased cooling rates.
However, in the present study, relatively higher heat input was
used in pulsed current technique. Higher the heat input, lower
will be the cooling rate. During slower cooling of welds, the
useful alloying elements diffuse and segregate in interdendritic
regions by forming brittle Laves phase. On one hand, the current
pulsing increased cooling rate and on the other hand, the higher
heat input used in the process lowered the cooling rate. So the
full beneﬁt of current pulsing in breaking the dendrite could not
be realized.
It may be mentioned here that Janaki Ram et al. have reported
signiﬁcant microstructural improvements in terms of reﬁnement,
reduction in size, amount, Nb concentration and interconnectiv-
ity of Laves phase in Inconel 718 TIG welds made by pulsed
current technique [4]. The heat input values used in that study
were 330 J/mm for continuous current and 297 J/mm for pulsed
current techniques. In the present study the heat input values
employed were 873 J/mm for continuous current and 1163 J/mm
for pulsed current techniques. While 2 mm thick sheets were
used in the study made by Janaki Ram et al., 3 mm thick sheets
were employed in the present work, which explains the higher
heat input used to obtain full penetration. As welds were made
in that study with and without current pulsing using almost the
same heat input, the inﬂuence of current pulsing was clearly
evident. As has been explained in the previous paragraph, the
relatively higher heat input used in the pulsed current tech-
nique lowered the cooling rate and so the full beneﬁt of current
pulsing in microstructural improvement could not be realized
in the present study. Also the pulse frequency was 1 Hz in the
present study and 8 Hz in the study reported by Janaki Ram et al.
[4].

welds, the Laves phase appeared to be white in scanning elec-

3.2. Tensile strength
Fig. 3 shows the inﬂuence of test temperature on ultimate
tensile strength of base metal and TIG weldments in both post-
weld heat treated conditions. There was no signiﬁcant variation
in strength of the base metal as the temperature increased from 25
to 500
◦C due to its microstructural stability. The drastic reduc-
tion in strength at 650 ◦C can be attributed to grain coarsening
in the base metal. The presence of Laves phase in all the weld
fusion zones was responsible for the lower strength values of
the welds compared with that of base metal at all test tempera-
tures. The Laves is not only brittle but also weakens the dendrite
core by depleting useful strengthening alloying elements. The/H9254
phase at the grain boundaries of the base metal checked the grain
growth of the base metal up to 500
◦C test temperature; where
as the structural stability was comparatively less in all the weld
fusion zones with increasing temperature. At all temperatures,
solution treated and aged weldments exhibited lower strength
values compared with the direct aged weldments, which may be
attributed to the presence of /H9254phase needles and their quantity
in the solution treated and aged weld fusion zone. The probable
mechanism may be decohesion of/H9254–matrix interface as/H9254is over
aged incoherent phase of Ni
3Nb (/H9253′′).
Among all direct aged weldments, magnetic arc oscillated
weldments exhibited higher strength compared with the other
two weldments due to discrete Laves phase in the microstruc-
ture of magnetic arc oscillated weld fusion zones. Discrete and
dispersed hard phase can resist the dislocation motion by dislo-
cation pileup and increases the back stress, which restricts the
yielding of the material. It can also delay the void coalescence
during tensile plastic deformation, resulting in higher tensile
strength. Magnetic arc oscillated weldments in the solution
treated and aged condition exhibited lower strength compared
with the continuous current weldments, which may be attributed
to higher amount of/H9254phase in the former. Though there is a con-
siderable debate over the inﬂuence of/H9254phase on the mechanical
properties of wrought alloy 718, many investigators reported the
detrimental effects of/H9254phase on mechanical properties when it is
present in needle-like morphology in excessive amounts[9,10].
As more /H9254phase was present in magnetic arc oscillated weld-

4. Conclusions
The magnetic arc oscillation technique used in TIG welding
process resulted in reﬁned Laves phase with lesser intercon-
nectivity. The full beneﬁts of current pulsing in breaking the
dendrites could not be realized in the present study due to rel-
atively higher heat input used in the welding process. In the
direct aged condition weldments prepared using magnetic arc
oscillation technique exhibited higher tensile strength due to the
presence of reﬁned and lesser-interconnected Laves particles. In
the solution treated and aged condition, magnetic arc oscillated
weldments exhibited lower tensile strength compared with the
weldments made without arc oscillation due to the presence of
large amounts of ﬁner /H9254needles.
