Compressive strength and yield asymmetry in extruded
Mg–Zn–Ho alloys containing quasicrystal phase
Alok Singh, * H. Somekawa and T. Mukai
Structural Metals Center, National Institute for Materials Science, 1-2-1 Sengen, Tsukuba 305-0047, Japan
Received 15 December 2006; revised 26 January 2007; accepted 9 February 2007
Available online 8 March 2007
Two alloys, Mg 96.9Zn2.7Ho0.4 and Mg 93Zn6Ho, containing a quasicrystal phase were extruded at various temperatures to pro-
duce diﬀerent grain sizes and tested at room temperature in tension and compression. For larger grains the strength of the
Mg
93Zn6Ho alloy was almost twice that of the Mg 96.9Zn2.7Ho0.4 alloy. Yield stresses approached 300 MPa as the grain sizes
decreased to 1 lm. In the case of the Mg 93Zn6Ho alloy, at grain sizes less than about 2 lm, yield stresses in compression become
higher than in tension.

Keywords: Magnesium alloys; Extrusion; Quasicrystals; Compression test; Yield asymmetry
In the Mg–Zn–RE (RE = Y, Gd, Tb, Dy, Ho or Er)
alloy system a ternary quasicrystalline icosahedral phase
Mg42Zn50RE8 exists in equilibrium with a-Mg [1,2].A
number of studies have been carried out on the strength-
ening eﬀects of the icosahedral phase in Mg–Zn–Y
alloys [3–10]. The icosahedral phase forming in
interdendritic spaces upon the solidiﬁcation of dilute
alloys is dispersed in the matrix by hot deformation,
i.e. methods such as hot rolling [3–6], extrusion [7–9]
and equal channel angular extrusion [10]. In addition,
nanosized precipitates of the icosahedral phase form in
the grains. These alloys show a good combination of
strength and ductility, believed to be due to the strong
interface between the quasicrystal phase and the Mg-
matrix [4,5] because of the very high symmetry and the
quasiperiodicity of the icosahedral phase [11,12].
There are several eﬀects introduced by wrought pro-
cessing, such as grain reﬁnement and texture. A basal
texture introduced in magnesium alloys by wrought pro-
cessing enhances the strength in tension in comparison
with the compressive strength, leading to an asymmetry
in the ratio of the two yield strengths (0.2% proof stress,
YS) in compression and tension. The recrystallized grain
size and texture are inﬂuenced signiﬁcantly by the sec-
ondary phase in the alloy. The eﬀect of icosahedral
quasicrystalline phase on these factors has not yet been
given much attention. We have recently reported on pre-
liminary investigations on the compressive strength of
Mg–Zn–RE alloys [13,14]. Here we report on a system-
atic investigation on the eﬀect of composition and extru-
sion temperature on grain size, texture and strength in
tension and compression at room temperature. It is con-
clusively shown that the icosahedral phase contributes
signiﬁcantly to the strength without compromising the
ductility.
Two alloys, Mg
96.9Zn2.7Ho0.4 and Mg 93Zn6Ho, were
melted and cast in steel moulds. The casting was
machined into rods of diameter 40 mm and extruded at
temperatures between 200 and 430 /C176C with an extrusion
ratio of 25:1. Grain size, orientations and texture were
determined by X-ray diﬀraction, electron backscattered
diﬀraction (EBSD) and transmission electron micros-
copy (TEM). Texture was measured on the longitudinal
section (parallel to the extrusion direction). Tensile and
compression tests were performed at room temperature
on an Instron machine with a strain rate of 10
/C03 s/C01.
Tensile specimens had dimensions of 3 mm diameter
and 15 mm gauge length. The compression specimens
were 8 mm in length and 4 mm in diameter.

Generally, the slopes of Hall–Petch plots in compres-
sion are higher than in tension, for the reason that com-
pressive yielding in wrought magnesium alloys usually
occurs by twinning and the twinning stress increases
with decreasing grain size more rapidly than the stress
required to activate slip [17]. As the grain size becomes
ﬁner (by extrusion at lower temperatures), the grain ori-
entations (texture) become more randomized. This will
aﬀect the plots for tension and compression, making
them converge with ﬁner grain sizes. In addition, as
the grain size becomes ﬁner, the eﬀect of the grain size
will become more dominant than the other strengthen-
ing eﬀects, such as that due to a secondary phase. For
this reason, the plots of alloys with diﬀerent composi-
tions (i.e. with diﬀerent amounts of the second phase)
converge with ﬁner grain sizes, as observed in this plot.
The strengths of the two alloys converge, to near
300 MPa, at a grain size of about 1 lm (as also shown
by severe hot rolling of Mg
97.26Zn2.34Y0.29Zr0.11 [19]).
An exception to this is the Mg 93Zn6Ho alloy, with its
higher fraction of icosahedral phase. This plot does
not converge in the same way, indicating that eﬀects
due to the icosahedral phase still dominate over the
strengthening by grain size, even when the grain size be-
come very ﬁne. Since initiation of twinning is believed to
be more grain size dependent, the compressive YS would

A lower Hall–Petch slope k is often explained by spe-
cial grain misorientation eﬀects, such as in case of AZ91
[20]. In the present study, however, grain boundary mis-
orientations are similar in both the alloys ( Fig. 4 ). The
slope k, therefore, is related to the presence of the icosa-
hedral phase and its matching interface with the matrix
(Fig. 2b). A low slope k can also occur by overageing in
precipitation strengthened alloys, where a large number
of incoherent particles become additional sources of dis-
locations, resulting in easier deformation [21]. The shape
of nanoscale icosahedral particles in the Mg
96.9-
Zn2.7Ho0.4 alloy are mostly rod-shaped ( Fig. 2 ), while
those in the Mg 93Zn6Ho alloy are more varied. How-
ever, the icosahedral phase can show matching inter-
faces in various orientations [22] because of its high
symmetry and several possible orientation relationships
with the matrix [12]. The eﬀects of shape and size of ico-
sahedral phase particles are under further investigation.
In summary, two alloys, Mg 96.9Zn2.7Ho0.4 alloy and
Mg93Zn6Ho, have been extruded at temperatures be-
tween 200 and 430 /C176C to produce diﬀerent grain sizes
and microstructures with an icosahedral quasicrystal
phase. Their tensile and compression strengths were eval-
uated at room temperature. The Mg
93Zn6Ho alloy had
much weaker basal texture than the Mg 96.9Zn2.7Ho0.4
alloy. At large grain sizes the strength of the Mg 93Zn6Ho
alloy is almost twice that of the Mg 96.9Zn2.7Ho0.4 alloy in
tension as well as in compression. As grain sizes ap-
proach 1 lm, the strengths of the two alloys in tension
as well as compression are close to 300 MPa. The
Mg
96.9Zn2.7Ho0.4 alloy showed comparable Hall–Petch
slopes k in tension and compression, of 208 and
257 MPa lm1/2, respectively. The Mg 93Zn6Ho alloy
showed a slope k of 183 in compression but a much lower
one, of about 78 MPa lm1/2, in tension, so that below a
grain size of about 2 lm the compressive YS becomes
higher than the tensile (yield asymmetry ratio >1).
