In order to further enhance the high-temperature compression property of the
AlNb2TiV refractory high-entropy alloy, the AlNb 2TiVWx (x = 0.1, 0.3, 0.5)
alloys have been newly designed. The microstructure and phase composition of
these alloys were investigated, and the compression property tested. The re-
sults show that white dendrite segregation can be observed in the as-cast
AlNb
2TiVW0.1 and AlNb2TiVW0.3 alloys, and is caused by W enrichment. W
addition does not change the BCC phase composition of as-cast alloys, but the
0.3-W and 0.5-W addition can reduce the lattice constant. The AlNb 2TiVW0.3
alloy yields the best compression property with compression strengths of
1482 MPa, 1575 MPa, and 1375 MPa at 600 /C176 C, 700 /C176 C, and 800 /C176 C, respec-
tively. Furthermore, at 200–600 /C176 C, under an initial compression stress of
500 MPa, the stress–time curve of the AlNb 2TiVW0.3 alloy is more
stable compared with the AlNb 2TiV alloy. In the AlNb 2TiV alloy, the HCP
phase forms in the BCC phase matrix during high-temperature compression,
and the two phases have the orientation relationship of <110>
BCC//
<10-10>HCP and <111>BCC//<0001>HCP. However, the AlNb 2TiVW0.3 alloy
yields a good phase stability, which will be the reason for its good compression
stability.
INTRODUCTION
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEA) have been
a research hotspot in recent years. 1–3 In particular,
RHEAs with light weight and high strength are
expected to become substitutes for high-tempera-
ture structural materials in various temperature
ranges.
4,5 In this way, the selection of high-temper-
ature structural materials will be enriched, the cost
may be lower, and the high-temperature properties
may be further improved due to the inherent
advantages of HEAs.
6–8
In 2015, Stepanov 9 and his team members ﬁrst
developed the AlNbTiV RHEA. This alloy has a
lower density ( /C245.59 g/cm3) due to the addition of
some light elements and the non-close-packed body-
centered cubic (BCC) crystal structure (valence
electron concentration, VEC < 6.87). Its compres-
sion strength is as high as 1020 MPa at room
temperature and 685 MPa at 800 /C176 C. Then, the
Stepanov team 10 designed the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy,
which had a slightly improved density ( /C245.64
g/cm3), the compression strength at room tempera-
ture was signiﬁcantly improved (1430 MPa), and
the compressive strength at 800 /C176 C was slightly
reduced (680 MPa). On this basis, Liu et al. 11
designed the AlNb 2TiV alloy, which not only had
almost the equivalent compression strength and(Received January 7, 2025; accepted June 24, 2025;

From the above discussion, we learn that AlNb 2-

order to further improve the high-temperature
property, the element ratio should not be changed;
however a small amount of high-melting point and
high-diffusion activation energy elements can be
properly introduced, such as Mo and W. Neverthe-
less, many studies
14–17 have shown that Mo addi-
tion in HEAs can always lead to the formation of
hard, brittle d and l phases. Adding hard brittle
phases to the intrinsically brittle BCC HEA will not
be conducive to its properties. Therefore, Mo is
unsuitable, but W may be suitable.
In view of the above, the AlNb
2TiVW0.1, AlNb 2-
TiVW0.3, and AlNb2TiVW0.5 alloys have been newly
designed in this paper, and the AlNb 2TiV alloy has
also been used for comparative analysis. The effect
of W addition on the microstructure and compres-
sion property of these alloys has been studied. As a
result, the AlNb
2TiVW0.3 alloy has been found to
yield the best high-temperature compression prop-
erty, especially at 700/C176 C and 800/C176 C. Then, the stress
stability of the AlNb 2TiVW0.3 alloy at 200–600 /C176 C
after an initial compression stress has been found to
be more stable, suggesting that it may be a potential
candidate for high-temperature fastener material.
EXPERIMENTAL
A vacuum non-consumable melting method was
used to fabricate AlNb
2TiV, AlNb 2TiVW0.1, AlNb 2-
TiVW0.3, and AlNb 2TiVW0.5 alloy button ingots by
using high-purity Al, Al-Nb, Ti, Al-V, and Al-W
metal/intermediate alloys. Chemical analysis
proved that the alloy composition met the require-
ments. cylinder samples u 4m m 9 h 6 mm were
prepared by wire cutting for compression experi-
ments, which were carried out at room temperature
(RT), 600/C176 C, 700/C176 C, and 800/C176 C with the compression
speed of 0.5 mm/min. Compression experiments
were also carried out at different initial compression
stresses of 500 MPa and 1000 MPa and at different
temperatures of 200 /C176 C, 400 /C176 C, 500 /C176 C, and 600 /C176 C,
and the stress–time curves were recorded.
The microstructure of the as-cast alloy ingots has
been investigated by scanning electron microscopy
(SEM; GeminiSEM-300), and the composition was
tested by energy disperse spectroscopy (EDS). The
phase composition and lattice distortion were ana-
lyzed by X-ray diffraction (XRD; XRD-7000; Shi-
madzu) using a Cu target from 20 /C176 to 100
/C176 . After
compression experiments with an initial compres-
sion stress, electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD;
NordlysMax2; Oxford) was used to detect the
microstructure of typical samples, with a scanning
step of 1 lm. The SEM samples were electrolytic
polished by using 60%C
4HO + 35%CH2OH + 5%
HClO4 at about – 20 /C176 C for 5–10 s. The EBSD
samples were vibration-polished for 6–8 h after
mechanical polishing.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Figure 1 shows the microstructure and EDS
results of the four alloys. As shown in Fig. 1a and
b, the SEM images of the AlNb 2TiV alloy show a
uniform contrast, but many cavities. These cavities
may be caused by the corrosive solution used in this
study, or may be derived from casting defects.
However, Fig. 1g and h shows the real casting
defects. The casting defects cannot have such a
different shape and contrast under SEM, so we infer
that the cavities in Fig. 1a and b were caused by
corrosion. As shown in Fig. 1b, the EDS results in
the matrix and the cavities indicate that the Al
element in the alloy is more easily corroded. As
shown in Fig. 1c and d, the AlNb
2TiVW0.1 alloy
starts to show micro-segregation with the unclear
white dendritic. As shown in Fig. 1e and f, the
AlNb2TiVW0.3 alloy has a clear white dendritic. The
white dendritic is the primary solidiﬁcation part,
where there should be more high-melting point
elements. However, in the inter-dendritic region,
there should be more low-melting point elements.
Fig. 1f1, f2, f3, f4 and f5 shows the EDS mapping of
the Al, Ti, Nb, W, and V elements, corresponding to
the orange dotted line framed region. On the whole,
the segregation of the elements is relatively weak,
that is, the distribution of the elements is relatively
uniform, but there are still some differences. In
order to focus on the segregation of the elements
more clearly, the distribution of Al, Ti, Nb, W, and V
in Fig. 1f focuses on the blue, yellow, pink, green,
and red dotted line regions, indicating that rela-
tively more W and Ti are distributed in the primary
solidiﬁcation region, i.e., the primary dendritic arm
region, while more Al, Nb, and V are distributed in
the inter-dendritic regions. These segregations are
not very obvious. As shown in Fig. 1g and h, the
AlNb
2TiVW0.5 alloy has many cavities and a poor
metallurgical quality, implying a bad property of
this alloy. These cavities must be casting defects,
not simply corroded holes, presenting a black hole
shape.
Figure 2 shows the XRD pattern of the four as-
cast alloys. Only one kind of BCC phase can be
found according to the XRD results, with peaks
corresponding to (111), (110), (200), (211), and (220)
peaks of the BCC phase, respectively. The four
alloys all consist of the same BCC phase whether
with or without W, indicating that the addition of W
does not change the phase composition. Compared

CONCLUSIONS
The microstructure and phase composition of the
AlNb
2TiVWx (x = 0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5) alloys have been
investigated, and the compression property tested.
The following conclusions can be drawn:
(1) White dendrite morphology can be observed in
the as-cast AlNb 2TiVW0.1 and AlNb 2TiVW0.3
alloys, which is caused by W enrichment.
TheAlNb
2TiVW0.5 alloy has a poor metallur-
gical quality containing many cavities.
(2) W addition does not change the BCC phase
composition of the as-cast alloys. Compared
with the 0.1-W addition, the 0.3-W and 0.5-W
additions can reduce the lattice constant.
(3) The AlNb 2TiVW0.3 alloy yields the best com-
pression property. At 600/C176 C, 700/C176 C and 800/C176 C,
it has the compression strength of 1482 MPa,
1575 MPa and 1375 MPa, respectively. Fur-
thermore, at 200–600 /C176 C, with an initial com-
pression stress of 500 MPa, the stress–time
curve of the AlNb
2TiVW0.3 alloy is more
stable compared with that of the AlNb 2TiV
alloy.
(4) In the AlNb 2TiV alloy, the HCP phase forms in
the BCC phase during high-temperature com-
pression, and they have the orientation rela-
tionship of <110>BCC//<10-10>HCP and
<111>BCC//<0001>HCP. However, the
AlNb2TiVW0.3 alloy yields a good phase sta-
bility, which is the reason for its good com-
pression stability.
