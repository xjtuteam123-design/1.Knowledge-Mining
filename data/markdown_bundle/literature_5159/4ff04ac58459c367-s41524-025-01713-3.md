Low Young’s modulus and high yield strength are concurrently needed to meet the performance
requirements of metallic implant materials. The single-objective performance-oriented alloy design
strategies face challenges in effectively addressing the inherent con ﬂict between Young ’s modulus
and yield strength. In this study, we developed a machine learning model for multi-objective
synergistic optimization of modulus and yield strength, successfully enabling simultaneous prediction
of Young’s modulus and yield strength in the Ti-Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta-Mo-Sn alloy system. The critical features
inﬂuencing the modulus and strength of the alloys were systematically analyzed and identi ﬁed.
Moreover, a series of complex concentrated alloy (CCAs) with low Young ’s modulus and high yield
strength were successfully prepared based on this model. The newly developed alloys exhibited a
stable single-phase BCC (body-centered-cubic) structure with Young ’s modulus in the range of
40– 50 GPa, yield strength of 600 – 915 MPa, and elastic admissible strain of approximately 1.5%. The
multi-objective machine learning model developed in this study can synergistically optimize low
Young’s modulus and high yield strength in complex alloys, providing a novel approach for the design
of advanced biomedical alloys.
Due to their superior mechanical properties, metallic biomaterials are
increasingly recognized as the preferred choice for implant applications in
bones, joints, and teeth that must endure high biological loads
1.I nt h ed e s i g n
and development of metallic biomaterials, the phenomenon of stress
shielding
1– 3 is an important issue which may lead to bone resorption,
accelerated bone degeneration or damage2,4, ultimately resulting in implant
loosening or failure5,6. Achieving comprehensive biocompatibility in terms
of low Young ’s modulus and high yield strength is critical for metallic
implant materials7.Al o wY o u n g’s modulus can effectively mitigate stress

for patients8. Unfortunately, it is widely recognized that modulus and yield
strength are positively correlated, and both are intrinsically linked to the
atomic bonding strength of the material. Stronger interatomic bonds often
correspond to higher modulus and higher yield strength.
Various design principles have been proposed to develop implant
alloys, such as the d-electron alloy design theory
9,10, the electron-to-atom
ratio11,12 and the Mo equivalent (i.e., Moeq)13. These proposals indirectly
estimate alloy modulus by assessing the phase stability, but there are still
signiﬁcant challenges in achieving direct and effective prediction of alloy
modulus, especially for alloys containing multiple principal elements, such
as complex concentrated alloys (CCAs)14– 16. Due to their high compositional
complexity, massive local chemical structures and high solution limits17,18,
CCAs may have relatively weak local atomic bonding, but in a single CCA
multiple strengthening mechanisms can possibly incorporated 19,20,t h u s
endowing the possibility for designi ng alloys simultaneously having low
modulus and high strength. However , the large chemical complexity
resulted from multiple principal elements in CCAs makes it a major chal-
lenge to achieve this goal.

The XGBoost (eXtreme Gradient Boosting) algorithm is a scalable tree
boosting system that can be applied to classiﬁcation and regression tasks22,31.
This algorithm involvesﬁtting multiple iterative models to the same dataset;
during each iteration, it attempts to enhance its predictive capability by
estimating the residuals of the preceding iteration32. We have developed a
multi-objective prediction model based on XGBoost to simultaneously
predict the Young’s modulus and yield strength of alloys. Figure 1 shows
schematic illustration of the multi-objective learning process and frame-

feature construction and subsequentﬁltering, the model with optimal fea-

diction of both modulus and yield strength.

lowest-ranked feature in each iteration until the desired number of features
is reached, thereby simplifying the model and enhancing its interpretability.

Overﬁtting is a prevalent issue in ML and statistical modeling, referring
to models exhibiting excellent performance on the training set but
demonstrating signiﬁcant bias when predicting new or unseen data. The
primary causes for overﬁtting often involve excessively complex models, too
many parameters, or insufﬁcient training data, leading to the model cap-
turing noise and speciﬁc patterns from the training set. Consequently, this
issue hampers its generalization ability and impedes effective extrapolation
to unknown data. Therefore, it is an effective strategy to mitigate the risk of
overﬁtting by simplifying the complexity of the model.
To further ensure the stability of the model, the randomk-fold cross-
validation (k ¼ 10) was employed for more accurately evaluating its per-
formance on diverse datasets (conducted 10 times). The multi-objective
prediction model demonstrated consistent performance, with anR
2 value of
approximately 98% on the training set and slightﬂuctuations around 97%
on the test set. Notably, the model did not exhibit any signs of overﬁtting.
The optimal model training results are depicted in Fig.3b, c, exhibiting R2
values of 97% and 98% in the training set for E andσ0:2, respectively, while in
the testing set, they reach 95% and 97%, respectively.
In this study, the screening of low modulus and high yield strength
CCAs was achieved based on the predictions from a model within a pre-
deﬁned compositional space. Speciﬁcally, for the alloy composition range
(i.e., Ti/Zr: 10– 85 at% and Nb/Mo/Ta/Hf/Sn: 0– 30 at%), the modulus and
yield strength of all alloys in this space were simultaneously predicted using
the model. Subsequently, using the preset dual thresholds (i.e., modulus
≤ 65GPa and yield strength ≥ 600MPa), the predicted compositions were
screened to identify the candidate alloys that satis ﬁed both the low-
modulus and high-yield-strength criteria. A series of CCAs with low
modulus and high yield strength were identiﬁed via our optimized multi-
objective model (see Fig. 4), i.e., Ti
45Zr40Nb4Sn1Mo5Ta4Hf1 (CCA1),
Ti50Zr40Nb4Sn1Mo2Ta2Hf1 (CCA2), Ti45Zr45Nb5Sn1Mo1Ta2Hf1 (CCA3),
Ti50Zr40Nb6Sn1Mo1Ta2 (CCA4), and Ti45Zr45Nb4Sn1Mo2Ta2Hf1 (CCA5).
These CCAs exhibit low Young’s modulus of 40– 50 GPa, yield strength of
600– 915 MPa, high elastic admissible strain of approximately 1.5% and high
tensile ductility larger than 10%. Speciﬁcally, CCA3 has a low modulus of
45 GPa and a high yield strength of 635 MPa, which lies outside the per-
formance range covered by the database (modulus less than 50 GPa while
yield strength greater than 600 MPa). Itis worth noting that visible dimples
were observed on the fracture surface of all theﬁve CCAs, indicating their
ductile deformation characteristics.
Structure of all the designed CCAs were investigated using X-ray dif-
fraction analysis. As illustrated in Fig. 5b, all the ﬁve low-modulus alloys
have a single body-centered-cubic (BCC) lattice with no evidence of any
secondary phases. The corresponding EBSD images (Fig.5e– g) show uni-
formly distributed equiaxed grains with regular shapes, and no dendritic
segregation was observed. In BCC structures, atomic packing is relatively

bonding forces between atoms and consequently a low modulus. Actually,
numerous low modulus bio-medical titanium alloys previously developed
have a BCC lattice, speciﬁcally β Ti alloys, includingβ-type, metastableβ-
type, and near-β type
52– 55.
It was found that both the yield strength and modulus increase with the
content of Mo and Ta, indicating that the modulus and yield strength are
sensitive to the constituent elements of CCAs. In the designed low-modulus
CCAs, the lattice constant exhibits a decreasing trend as the Mo and Ta
contents increase (Fig.6), which is different from the signiﬁcant increase in
lattice constant with Ta addition in binary Ti-Ta alloys. This may be due to
the severe lattice distortion of CCAs. Addition of Mo and Ta increases the
difference in electronegativity, thereby disrupting the stability of the lattice
structure and ultimately leading to adecrease in the lattice constant of CCA
alloys. The decrease in lattice constant indicates a decrease in atomic spa-
cing, leading to strengthening of interaction forces, which contributes to the
increase in modulus from a macroscopic perspective.

Distinct from binary titanium alloys, for the CCAs designed by our ML
model, a single BCC phase was obtained, although it falls within a similar
low VEC range (i.e., 4.09– 4.18). This phenomenon can be attributed to the

modulus at a lower VEC value (VEC = 4.09).

Methods
Modules in the Scikit-learn library were used to build ML models and
perform data processing and analysis. The ML algorithm employed the
xgboost module, feature selection utilized the RFE module, and the clus-
tering algorithm applied the KMeans module. Table 1 explicitly lists the
information of the feature pool used in the multi-objective prediction
model, including the deﬁnitions, calculation formulas, and source references
of 56 features.
Alloy ingots with compositions predicted by ML were fabricated by
arc-melting a mixture of high-purity metals of over 99.9 at% in an argon
atmosphere treated with titanium gettering. All ingots were remelted at least
six times to eliminate chemical inhom ogeneity and eventually drop-cast
into a water-cooled copper mold with a dimension of 10 × 10 × 65 mm
3.
Room-temperature tensile tests were performed on a TSMT universal
electronic tensile machine equipped with a video extensometer at a strain
rate of 1×10−3 s−1. Dog bone-shaped tensile samples of each alloy with a
gauge length of 20 mm and a cross-section of 1.5 × 5.0 mm 2 were cut by
electrical discharging machine. Beforedeformation, all tensile samples were
polished down to 2000-grit SiC paper.
