Abstract. In this paper, we report microstructure and mechanical properties evolut ion of the 
CoCrFeNiMn-type high entropy alloy, containing small amounts of Al and C , during cold rolling 
and subsequent annealing at 700- 1100°C. In the initial as -cast condition the alloy has coarse-
grained single face-centered cubic (fcc) phase structure. Cold rolling and annealing substantially 
refine fcc grains; in addition M23C6 type carbides appear. After annealing at relatively low 
temperatures (≤900°C), these particles are arranged in characteristic arrays aligned with rolling 
directions. The specifi c microstructure of the thermomechanically processed alloy is suggested to 
be the reason of the balanced combination of tensile strength and ductility. 
Introduction 
The so -called high entropy alloys (HEAs) have recently become a major research topic in 
materials science [1,2]. HEAs are usually defined as multicomponent alloys of at least 5 principal 
elements taken in approximately equiatomic concentrations. These alloys have already 
demonstrated promising properties such as high strength at room and elevate d temperatures, 
ductility, fracture and impact toughness, wear resistance, etc [1-4]. However, there is a need for 
better understanding of microstructure -properties relationships in such alloys to improve their 
performance further. 
The most studied type of HEAs is the 3d transition metals based alloys; the typical representative 
of this family is the equiatomic CoCrFeNiMn alloy [5-7]. This alloy has single phase face- centered 
cubic (fcc) phase structure stable at temperatures ≥900°C [8,9], and very high ductility and 
toughness at room and cryogenic temperatures [6,7]. But the strength of the alloy at room 
temperature is rather low [6]. There is a sufficient number of possibilities to increase the strength of 
the alloy; among them there are grain boundary (Hall -Petch) strengthening [10], work hardening 
[11]; and precipitation strengthening [10]. The first two strengthening factors can be introduced by 
proper thermomechanical processing, while additional modifications of chemical composition 
might be required to introduce strengthening precipitates. 
In the present work , we have studied CoCrFeNiMn -type alloy with small additions of Al and C. 
Both Al and C can result in precipitatio n of strengthening phases [ 10,12]. The alloy was subject to 
the cold rolling and further annealed to produce recrystallized grains and (possibly) precipitates. 
Structure and tensile properties of the alloy in different conditions were examined.

Materials and Methods 
The alloy with the composition (in at.%) 3.37Al -0.69C-22.35Co-19.67Cr-22.85Fe-22.44Ni-
8.62Mn was prepared by self -propagating high-temperature synthesis and subsequently remelted in 
vacuum induction furnace to produce starting ingot. The detai ls of alloy preparation procedure are 
reported elsewhere [13]. Furthermore, the specimens were cu t from the produced ingot by electric 
discharge machine and cold rolled to the final thickness reduction of 80% (1 mm thickness). 
Reduction per pass was 5 -10%. Samples with a thickness reduction of 5, 10, 20, 40, 60, and 80% 
were used for the further analysis. In addition, samples after rolling to maximum reduction (80%) 
were annealed at 700-1100°C for 1 h. These samples were also analyzed. 
Microstructural characterization was carried out using scanning (SEM) and transmission (TEM) 
electron microscopy. The cold-rolled specimens were investigated in the plane perpendicular to the 
transversal direction. The samples for the SEM investigation were prepared by careful mechanical 
polishing. FEI Quanta 600 FEG SEM equipped with back- scattered electron (BSE) detector was 
used for the observations. Samples for TEM were prepared by twin- jet electro -polishing of pre -
thinned to 100 μm discs in a mixture of 90% CH 3COOH and 10% HCl04 at 30 V potential at room 
temperatures. TEM observations were performed using JEOL JEM- 2100 microscope equipped with 
energy dispersive spectrometry (EDX) detector. 
Mechanical properties of the alloy were accessed by tensile testing. For this purpose, dog-bone-
shaped flat specimens with gauge dimensions of 5 mm length × 3 mm width × 1 mm thickness were 
machined and pulled to fracture at an initial strain rate of 10 -3 s -1 at room temperature using an 
Instron 5882 test machine. At least two tests per each condition were performed. 
Results and Discussion 
In the as-cast initial condition the alloy had coarse-grained single fcc phase structure. The size of 
the fcc grains was ~250 -400 μm. The grains had an irregular shape. Their boundaries were mostly 
curved. No signs of the second phase(s) had been detected [13].

Pronounced dislocation activity was observed at the initial stages of rolling deformation (Fig. 
3a). Dislocation slip was planar; multiple intersections of slip bands were found. Some individual 
twins appeared at the same time ( not shown; [13] ). The dislocation density rapidly increased from 
~1 × 1011 m-2 in the initial (as -cast) condition to ~2 × 1015 m-2 after 40% reduction and remained 
nearly constant at higher strains. In addition, a t 40% and higher thickness reductions intensive 
twinning was observed (Fig. 3b). High dislocation density inside twins has to be noted. All 
deformation twins belonged to the (111) <112> family. In addition to twin bundles, dislocation

pile-ups and sub- boundaries were found in s ome areas. With the i ncrease of rolling reduction, 
twin/subgrain boundaries became aligned with rolling direction. As the result inhomogeneous 
microstructure composed of twinned and sub-grained areas was formed after rolling. 
In general, the microstructure evolution during rol ling is similar to that observed in the 
equiatomic CoCrFeNiMn alloy [14]; yet the twinning is delayed to latter stages of deformation and 
the dislocation density is higher. This can be potentially attribute d to the increase of the stacking 
fault energy due to Al and C addition [11,13]. 
Annealing at 700- 1100°C resulted in recrystallization of the fcc matrix of the alloy. At lower 
temperatures (700°C and 800°C) the recrystallization was incomplete (Fig. 2a). The recrystallized 
volume fraction was 59% and 82% after annealing at 700°C and 800°C respectively. At higher 
temperature fully recrystallized microstructure was obtained (Fig. 2b). The size of the recrystallized 
grains gradually increased with the increase of the annealing temperature. For instance, the m ean 
grain size after annealing at 700°C was ~1 μm, and after annealing at 1100°C - ~10 μm. The 
recrystallized grains had a polygonal shape and contained numerous annealing twins. 
In addition to recrystallization of the fcc matrix, annealing resulted in precipitation of second 
phase(s) particles visible as fine black dots (Fig. 3b). The visible particles had equiaxed shape and 
are mostly located on grain boundaries. Their size of the second phase(s) particles gradually 
increased with the increase of the annealing temperature. 
TEM studies were performed t o obtain additional information, primary, on the second phase(s) 
particles. It was revealed (selected area electron diffraction pattern in Fig. 2c) that the observed 
particles are M23C6 type carbides. Local EDX analysis had shown that the carbides were mostly 
composed of Cr. After annealing at relatively low temperatures (≤900°C), the carbides were 
arranged in characteristic arrays (Fig. 2c). Note that these arrays were aligned with rolling direction. 
The distance between these arrays was about several hund red nm and increased with the increase of 
the annealing temperature . Some coarser carbides were also found on grain boundaries. With the 
increase of annealing temperature to 1000°C and 1100°C the arrays of second phase particles 
disappeared; most of the carbides were found on grain boundaries (Fig. 2d). Some individual 
carbides can also be found. The size of carbides pronouncedly increased with the increase of the 
annealing temperature. For instance, the mean carbide size after annealing at 700°C and 1100°C 
was 35 nm and 330 nm, respectively.

The precipitation of carbide particles in the carbon -doped alloy after annealing can be 
anticipated in accordance with the results of [12]. The equilibrium solubility of carbon at annealing 
temperatures (700-1100°C) in the CoCrFeNiMn-type alloy is expected to be much lower than the 
amount of carbon dissolved in the fcc matrix of the as -cast alloy. Due to high affinity between 
carbon and Cr, the excessive amount of carbon segregates to the Cr -rich carbide particles. The 
specific arrangement of the carbides in the form of regular arrays (Fig. 2c) after annealing at 
relatively low temperatures (700 -900°C) can be attributed to the ir preferred precipitation at 
twin/sub-grain boundaries aligned with the rolling direction (Fig. 1b). Presumably, at higher 
temperatures the kinetics of the recrystallization process is much faster and deformation -induced 
microstructure does not play any noticeable role in the precipitation of carbides. One has also note 
that carbides are likely to restrict grain growth in the fcc matrix; the grain size in the equiatomic 
CoCrFeNiMn alloy after the same processing is several times higher [9].

Finally, Fig. 3 illustrates the evolution of mechanical properties of the alloy during cold rolling 
(Fig. 3a) and subsequent annealing (Fig. 3b). Apparently, rolling resulted in a gradual increase of 
strength (both yield stress and ultimate tensile strength) together with a decrease of ductility. 
Strength raised monotonically with an increase of strain, for instance the yield strength increased 
from 210 MPa to 1310 MPa when rolling reduction increased from 0% to 80%. Strengthening was

associated both with dislocation density and deformation twinning (Fig. 1) [13]. In turn, ductility 
dropped significantly from 74% to 3.7% when rolling reduction increase d from 0 to 40% and then 
only slightly decreased to 1.3% after 80% reduction. 
Annealing resulted in a decrease of strength and increase of ductility (Fig. 3b). After annealing at 
the lowest temperature of 700°C the ductility already increased to 24%; but the alloy retained high 
strength – yield stress of 870 MPa and ultimate tensile strength of 1060 MPa. The i ncrease of the 
annealing temperature resulted in a gradual increase of ductility and decrease of strength. Loss of 
strength can be associated with (i) decrease of a fraction of recrystallized areas; (ii) coarser 
recrystallized fcc grains; (iii) coarser carbide particles with the higher distance between them 
(Fig. 2). However, the present study clearly indicated that the mechanical properties of the studied 
alloy can be tailored in a wide range by thermomechanical processing via microstructure control 
required to utilize work, grain boundary, and precipitation hardening. 
Summary 
In this work microstructure and mechanical properties of the CoCrFeNiMn -type high entropy 
alloy, containing small amounts of Al and C, after cold rolling and subsequent annealing were 
studied and following conclusions were drawn. 
1) Cold rolling induced the formation of the typical deformed microstructure in the alloy due to 
mostly planar dislocation glide and deformation twinning. Also, rolling resulted in a gradual 
increase of the yield strength of the alloy, but at the cost of reduced ductility. For instance, the yield 
strength and uniform elongation of the as -cast alloy were 210 MPa and 74% respectively, while 
after 80% rolling the corresponding values were 1310 MPa and 1.3%. 
2) Annealing of the cold- rolled alloy resulted in recrystallization of fcc phase. The size of the 
recrystallized grains increased from ~3 µm ( 700°C) to ~10 µm (1100°C). In addition, precipitation 
of M23C6 type carbides occurred. After anne aling at relatively low temperatures (≤900°C), the 
carbides were arranged in characteristic arrays aligned with rolling directions. The annealed alloy 
also demonstrated a balanced combination of strength and ductility. For example, after annealing at 
900°C the alloy has a yield strength of 530 MPa together with uniform elongation of 27%.
