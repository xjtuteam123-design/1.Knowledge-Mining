abstract
Nano-Al2O3 reinforcement’s capability to simultaneously enhance the room temperature (25 1C)
strength and ductility of magnesium alloys has effectively been exploited in ingot metallurgy processed
AZ31/1.5Al2O3 nanocomposite in this study. Tensile characterization revealed that at high temperature
(150–250 1C), instead of strengthening, the thermally stable nano-Al 2O3 reinforcement ironically
exacerbated the softening of AZ31 alloy. However, an incredible increment in AZ31 alloy (with grain
size of /C24 2.3 mm) ductility (up to 184%) has been achieved in the nanocomposite with increasing
temperature due to the incorporation of nano-Al 2O3 as reinforcement. Microstructural characterization
of the nanocomposite revealed that the dynamic recrystallization process has induced a complete
recrystallization in AZ31 alloy matrix at a relatively much lower temperature (150 1C) with tremendous
grain growth near the fracture surface at higher temperature (250 1C). Fractography of the nanocom-
posite revealed that the room temperature mixed ductile mode fracture behavior of AZ31 alloy
transformed to a complete ductile mode at high temperature due to the presence of nano-Al 2O3
particulates.

1. Introduction
Continuing demand of light weight structures with improved
performance by the automobile and aerospace industries for fuel
economy has provided the desired impetus to the global scientiﬁc
community to develop the low-density magnesium based mate-
rials [1,2]. Magnesium is the lightest available metal on our
planet, suitable for performance-critical and non-performance
critical structural applications spanning from automotive, aero-
space to the sports equipment, and electronic industries. Magne-
sium alloys with aluminum content in the amount of 2.5–3.5%
with zinc and manganese addition are most frequently subjected
to hot rolling process to produce sheet metals for industrial
applications. Hence, plastic deformation methods to form semi-
ﬁnished products from these magnesium alloys are intensively
investigated [3,4]. Successful attempts have been made in recent
time to simultaneously increase the strength and formability of
these alloys by using nano-size particulate reinforcements
[1,5–10] and, hence, enhanced their potential to further industrial
applications. Among the nano-size reinforcement used, Al
2O3
particulate was found to be the most promising in simultaneous
improvement of room temperature strength and formability of
magnesium and its alloys. Increasing application of these newly
developed light weight structural nanocomposites involves many
challenges, including understanding of the strengthening mech-
anism and the plastic formability characteristics necessary in
fabrication of members with various shapes. However, review of
the open literature revealed that no work has been reported on
the tensile performance analysis to understand the high tem-
perature strengthening and formability characteristics of these
nanocomposites.
Accordingly, the primary aim of the present study was to
investigate the effect of high temperature on the tensile behavior
of nano-Al
2O3 particulate reinforced AZ31/1.5Al 2O3 nanocompo-
site. The nanocomposite material was processed using solidiﬁca-
tion processing route called disintegrated melt deposition (DMD)
technique [9,11] followed by hot extrusion.

2.2. Primary processing
Monolithic AZ31 was cast using the DMD method [11]. This
involved heating AZ31 sections to 750 1C in an inert Ar gas
atmosphere in a graphite crucible using a resistance heating
furnace. The crucible was equipped with an arrangement for
bottom pouring. Upon reaching the superheat temperature, the
molten slurry was stirred for 2.5 min at 460 rpm using a twin
blade (pitch 45 1) mild steel impeller to facilitate the uniform
distribution of heat. The impeller was coated with Zirtex 25 (86%
ZrO2, 8.8% Y 2O3, 3.6% SiO 2, 1.2% K 2O and Na 2O, and 0.3% trace
inorganics) to avoid iron contamination of the molten metal. The
melt was then released through a 10 mm diameter oriﬁce at the
base of the crucible. The melt was disintegrated by two jets of
argon gas oriented normal to the melt stream and located
265 mm from the melt pouring point. The argon gas ﬂow rate
was maintained at 25l pm. The disintegrated melt slurry was
subsequently deposited onto a metallic substrate located 500 mm
from the disintegration point. An ingot of 40 mm diameter was
obtained following the deposition stage. To form the AZ31/
1.5 vol% Al 2O3 nanocomposite [9],A l 2O3 nanoparticle powder
was isolated by wrapping in Al foil of minimal weight
(o0.50 wt% with respect to AZ31 matrix weight) and arranged
on top of the bundle of AZ31 sections, with all other DMD
parameters unchanged. All deposited ingots were sectioned into
billets.
2.3. Secondary processing
All billets were machined to 35 mm diameter and hot extruded
using 20.25:1 extrusion ratio on a 150 t hydraulic press. The
extrusion temperature was 350 1C. The billets were held at 400 1C
for 60 min in a furnace prior to extrusion. Colloidal graphite was
used as a lubricant. Rods of 8 mm were obtained.
2.4. Microstructural characterization
Microstructural characterization studies were conducted on
metallographically polished extruded monolithic and nanocompo-
site samples to determine nano-reinforcement distribution, grain
morphology and intermetallic particle characteristics. Olympus
metallographic microscope and Hitachi S4300 Field-Emission Scan-
ning Electron Microscope (FESEM) were used. Image analysis using
Scion software was carried out to determine the grain and inter-
metallic particle characteristics. XRD studies were conducted using
CuKa radiation ( l¼1.5406) with a scan speed of 2 1/min in an
automated Shimadzu LAB-X XRD-6 000 diffractometer to identify
the intermetallic compounds.
2.5. Hardness
Microhardness measurements were made on polished mono-
lithic and nanocomposite extruded samples. Vickers microhardness
was measured using Matsuzawa MXT50 automatic digital micro-
hardness tester using 25 gf-indenting load and 15 s dwell time.
2.6. Tensile testing
Smooth bar tensile properties of the extruded nanocomposites
samples at 25 1C, 100 1, 150 1C and 200 1C were determined in
accordance with ASTM test method E8M-05 using Instron 5500
machine with a crosshead speed set at 0.254 mm/min. Specimens
used were with 5 mm diameter and 25 mm gage length. Tests
have been conducted in air circulated resistant heating closed
chamber. Tensile specimens were soaked in testing temperature
for /C24 5 min prior to the test. At the end of the test, one half of the
fracture samples were furnace cooled and the other half quenched
into water to preserve the grain morphology which led to the
fracture of the deformed materials. Metallography was done on
the quenched microstructure using optical microscope to under-
stand the deformed grain morphology and fractography was done
on the furnace cooled fracture surface using JEOL JSM-5800 LV
Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM).
3. Results
3.1. Macrostructural characteristics
The results of macrstructural characterization on the
as-deposited monolithic and nanocomposite samples did not
reveal any macropores or shrinkage cavity. No macrostructural
defects were observed in the extruded rods of monolithic and
nanocomposite materials. Macrostructural characterization also
revealed that there was no visible oxidation on the surfaces of
high-temperature fractures specimens.
3.2. Microstructural characteristics
Microstructural analysis results revealed Al 2O3 reinforcement
distribution in the nanocomposite was reasonably uniform as
shown in Fig. 1 (a and b). The Al 2O3 nanoparticles were located at
the grain boundary as well as within the grain. Microstructural
analysis also revealed that average grain size reduced while
aspect ratio increased in the case of nanocomposite as shown in
Table 1 and Fig. 1 (a and b). Intermetallic particle size and round-
ness ratio (i.e., the sharpness of a particle’s edges and corners
[12]) were smaller in the nanocomposite than in the monolithic
material.
3.3. Hardness
The results of microhardness measurements are listed in
Table 1 . The nanocomposite exhibited higher hardness than the
monolithic magnesium alloy.
3.4. Tensile behavior
Results of ambient temperature tensile testing of the extruded
nanocomposite materials (see Table 2 and Fig. 2 a) revealed sig-
niﬁcant increments in strength, failure strain and work of fracture
(WOF) of AZ31 due to the addition of nano-Al 2O3 reinforcement.
Ductile–brittle mixed mode of fracture was observed in all the
ambient temperature tensile fractured surfaces (see Fig. 3 ).
High temperature tensile test results (see Table 2 and Fig. 2 b)
revealed that the nano-size Al 2O3 reinforcement effectively exa-
cerbated the softening of AZ31 alloy with increasing temperature
used in this study (i.e., 100–250 1C). Results also revealed the
tremendous potential of solid-state formability (ductility prior to
failure was measured up to 184%) of this nanocomposite within
the temperature limit used in this study. Microstructures near the
fracture surfaces of the high temperature fractures samples (see
Fig. 4 ) were found to be completely recrystallized at 150 1C and
experienced tremendous grain growth at 250 1C. Fracture beha-
vior of the AZ31 matrix was changed from brittle for pure Mg to
ductile in the case of Mg/Al 2O3 nanocomposite due to the
incorporation of nano-Al 2O3 (see Fig. 3 ).

4. Discussion
4.1. Macrostructural characteristics of nanocomposite
The results of macrstructural characterization on the as-deposited
monolithic and nanocomposite samples revealed that there was no
detectable metal oxidation or melt reaction with graphite crucible.
The inert atmosphere used during DMD was effective in preventing
oxidation of the Mg melt. No stable carbides of Mg or Al formed due
to reaction with graphite crucible.
4.2. Microstructural characteristics
Microstructural characterization of extruded nanocomposites
samples revealed: (a) reasonably uniform distribution of reinfor-
cement, (b) good reinforcement–matrix interfacial integration,
and (c) grain and intermetallic reﬁnement.
The reasonably uniform distribution of Al 2O3 nanoparticles
reinforcement (seeFig. 1a and b) can be attributed to the cumulative
effect of (a) processing parameters (i.e., uniform arrangement of raw
materials, minimal gravity-associated segregation due to judicious
selection of stirring parameters[11], and argon gas disintegration of
nanocomposite slurry[13]), (b) good wetting of Al2O3 nanoparticles
by the alloy matrix [14].
Nearly equiaxed grains were observed in both extruded
monolithic and reinforced AZ31 nanocomposite as shown in
Fig. 1 (c and d) and Table 1 . However, marginal grain reﬁnement
of AZ31 matrix in nanocomposite suggests the limited ability of
nano-size Al 2O3 reinforcement to serve as either nucleation sites
or obstacles to grain growth during the secondary processing.
Intermetallic particle size and roundness ratio were lower in the
nanocomposite, compared to that of monolithic material. X-ray
diffraction (XRD) analysis revealed the presence of b-Al12Mg17
phase [15]. The intermetallic particles were predominantly
located at grain boundaries. The presence of Al 2O3 nanoparticles
assisted in breaking down the b-Al12Mg17 intermetallic phase.
4.3. Hardness
A signiﬁcant increase in microhardness (i.e., 30%) of the AZ31
alloy matrix in the current study can be attributed to (a) presence
of ﬁner intermetallic particles, (b) reasonably uniform distribu-
tion of harder Al
2O3 nanoparticulates in the matrix, and (c) higher
constraint to localized matrix deformation during indentation due
to the presence of intermetallic particles (having lower size and
roundness ratio) and nanoparticulates [16]. The improvement in
hardness is consistent with earlier observations reported in the
literature [15,16].

4.4. Tensile behavior
Ambient temperature tensile strengths of monolithic material
and nanocomposite (see Table 2 and Fig. 2a) show that the nano-
Al2O3 enhanced the 0.2% YS and UTS of AZ31 by 19% and 21%,
respectively. The strength increment in AZ31/1.5Al 2O3 compared
to monolithic AZ31 can be attributed to the factors such as
(a) dislocation generation due to elastic modulus mismatch and
coefﬁcient of thermal expansion mismatch between the matrix
and reinforcement [8,9,17,18], (b) Orowan strengthening mech-
anism (inclusive of reduced diameter of intermetallic particulates)
[17–19] and (c) load transfer from matrix to reinforcement [6,17].
The tensile strength of AZ31/1.5Al2O3 was similar to or better than
(a) selected wrought/cast Zr-free (or Al-containing) Mg alloys having
similar or higher Al content, (b) selected wrought/cast Zr-containing
(or Al-free) Mg alloys, (c) selected wrought Mg nanocomposites
(extruded at lower temperature) and (d) selected wrought Zr-free
(or Al-containing) Mg alloy microcomposites having higher Al
content, as listed in Table 2 [20–22].
Strength of the AZ31 matrix of the nanocomposite was found
to be diminishing (see Table 2 and Fig. 2b) with increasing testing
temperature (i.e., 150–250 1C) used in this study. The typical
trend of decreasing strength of the matrix with the increasing
testing temperature (i.e., up to 250 1C) [23] is followed in this
study. Decrease in the yield strength (ﬂow stress) of the matrix of
the developed nanocomposite with increasing temperature was
found to be non-linear as shown in the Fig. 5(a). High temperature
stress–strain graphs of nanocomposite (see Fig. 2 b) also revealed
the absence of strain hardening effect on AZ31 matrix which can
be attributed to the complete dynamic recrystallization of the
matrix materials at 150 1C followed by substantial grain growth at
250 1C (see Fig. 4 ). Dynamic recrystallization is attributed to be
one of the leading contributors to the declining strain hardening
effect in magnesium materials at high temperature deformation.
Dynamic recrystallization is dependent on the strain rate and
deformation temperature applied and reported to be always in
very limited amount [24] in the case of AZ31. However, presence
of enormous number of nano-size Al
2O3 reinforcement in the
matrix induced complete recrystallization in the case of the
nanocomposite developed in this study. Strength of the AZ31
matrix further diminished at a higher temperature due to sub-
stantial grain growth. It should be noted that the strain hardening
effect is caused by dislocations interacting with each other and
with barriers which impede their motion [23].

The results also revealed tremendous increment in the room
temperature (i.e., 25 1C) ductility of AZ31 matrix (from 10.4% to
22.2%) in AZ31/1.5Al
2O3 nanocomposite (see Table 2 and shown
in Fig. 2 a) due to incorporation of nano-sized Al 2O3 particulates
reinforcement. The increment of room temperature ductility of
AZ31 matrix in the AZ31/1.5Al 2O3 can be attributed to the
(a) presence of reasonably uniform distribution of ceramic nano-
particles [27–29] and (b) reduction in size and roundness ratio of
intermetallic particles (see Table 1) [23,29]. In the case of reason-
ably uniform distribution of ceramic nanoparticles, it has been
shown in previous studies that the nanoparticles provide sites
where cleavage cracks are opened ahead of the advancing crack
front. This (i) dissipates the stress concentration which would
otherwise exist at the crack front and (ii) alters the local effective
stress state from plane strain to plane stress in the neighborhood
of crack tip [27,28]. In the case of reduction in size and roundness
ratio of intermetallic particles (roundness is a measure of the
sharpness of a particle’s edges and corners [12]) (i) the break-
down of the intermetallic particles (see Table 1 ) located at grain
boundaries and (ii) the change in their distribution from a
predominantly ‘aggregated type’ to ‘dispersed type’ is observed
in the present study, which cumulatively assisted in ductility
improvement [23]. The ductility of AZ31/1.5Al 2O3 was signiﬁ-

Ductility of the AZ31 matrix in the nanocomposite further
increased incredibly up to 184.1% when subjected to high tempera-
tures (i.e., 150–250 1C) (see Table 2 ). Increasing ductility with
decreasing strength in the increasing testing temperature is a well
established phenomenon[23]. Cumulative effect of (a) activation of
non-basal slip system at higher temperature [23], (b) signiﬁcant
grain reﬁnement (see Fig. 4 ), due to the dynamic recrystallization
process induced complete recrystallization (at 150 1C, see Fig. 4 a),
and (c) tremendous grain growth (at 250 1C, see Fig. 4c) apparently
led to this incredible enhancement in the ductility of the AZ31
matrix in the AZ31/1.5Al2O3. Grain reﬁnement particularly beneﬁts
hexagonal closed packed (HCP) metals in terms of ductility [30].
Fracture surface study of the nanocomposite revealed ductile
fracture features, as dimples, are overwhelmingly dominating in
the cases of all the high temperature tested nanocomposites (see
Fig. 3 c and d). There is some indication of intergranular crack
propagation (see Fig. 3e) in the case of the sample tested at 250 1C
where some abnormal grain growth was observed near to the
fracture surface (see Fig. 4 c). In essence, the study revealed that
nano reinforcement used in this study improved the secondary
(solid state) processing potential of AZ31 alloy incredibly.
The work of fracture (WOF) of monolithic material and
nanocomposite are listed in Table 2 . WOF quantiﬁed the ability
of the material to absorb energy up to fracture under load [31].
Compared to monolithic material, WOF was enhanced by 162%
in AZ31/1.5Al 2O3. The signiﬁcantly high increment in WOF
exhibited by AZ31/1.5Al 2O3 shows its potential to be used in
damage tolerant design.
