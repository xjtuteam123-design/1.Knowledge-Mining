ABSTRACT
The influences of phase coarsening and loading direction on the ductility of Ni-based superalloys with phase 
coarsening are investigated, and the relationship between their microstructural and macroscopic mechanical 
properties is also explored in detail. It is found that the precipitation phase and the corresponding phase 
coarsening significantly influence the ductility of alloys. Furthermore, anisotropic features in ductility are 
observed after phase coarsening. Generally, as the volume fraction of precipitation particles or the degree of 
phase coarsening increases, the ductility of alloys gradually decreases; and it initially increases and then de -
creases gradually with the increase of loading angle, presenting an inverted ‘V ’ shape. Comparatively, for the 
alloys with lower volume fraction of precipitation particles and higher degree of phase coarsening, the ductility 
shows different variation trends with the degree of phase coarsening and loading angle compared with that in 
materials with other microstructure features, taking the minimum value at the loading angle of 45
◦
. The vari -
ations of ductility are closely connected to the characteristics of precipitate shape, size and distribution. The 
microstructure evolution and the loading direction significantly affect the initiation and movement of disloca -
tions within the material, and result in various macroscopic ductility characteristics in the Ni-based superalloys.
1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys, as an increasingly vital class of structural 
materials, present an outstanding combination of high strength, good 
ductility, excellent fracture toughness and resistance to creep and fa -
tigue at high temperatures, and thus have been widely used as key 
materials for hot-end components in various industrial fields, e.g., 
aerospace, marine and energy [1 – 5] . During the service life or the 
processing progress of these alloys, the internal precipitation phases 
often undergo coarsening (i.e., Ostwald ripening), resulting in remark -
able changes in precipitate shape, size and distribution (PSD) [6 – 10] . 
The macroscopic properties of alloys are usually closely linked with 
their microstructures. Hence, the effect of phase coarsening would cause 
significant changes in dislocation movement as well as the mechanical 
properties of the alloy [11 – 16] . As the hot-end components often work 
in extreme environments, and the corresponding material microstruc -
tures and mechanical properties (e.g., ductility, strength, etc.) may 
change under the complex environmental loadings [14,17 – 19] , and it 
would lead to a security risk.
Related studies indicate that the γ’ phase within the alloy and the 
interface layer between the γ phase and γ’ phase, usually strongly hinder 
dislocation movement in the material, and the dislocations tend to 
bypass γ’ phase after phase coarsening, and the precipitation strength -
ening effect is weakened progressively [4,20 – 23] . Therefore, the me -
chanical properties of alloys, including ductility, fracture toughness, and 
strength, etc., are closely associated with the exact actual PSD charac -
teristics. Meanwhile, the results of related quasi-static tensile tests for 
Ni-based superalloys indeed demonstrated a remarkable change in 
ductility and strength after phase coarsening, and different deformation 
morphologies and fracture features are also observed in the tests 
[5,12,17,24 – 29] . Generally speaking, experimental observation is usu -
ally limited to the deformation and damage characteristics of materials 
before and after test, and the corresponding measurements are also 
primarily focused on the surface of specimens, thus it is difficult to 
monitor the evolutions of inner structure of alloys as well as the internal 
parameters in real-time, and consequently there is difficulty in

As a good complement for the experimental investigations, the finite 
element method (FEM) simulation has become an important mechanical 
research method. FEM simulation can not only obtain detailed defor -
mation and failure processes within the alloy, but also allow the quali -
tative analysis on the effects of different factors, thus it is helpful for in- 
depth investigation on the mechanical properties of materials [30 – 32] . 
Related experimental observations show that the coarsening of precip -
itation phase exhibits preferential growth features, with their 
morphology generally transformed into elongated shapes, and thus the 
material microstructure is gradually exhibiting anisotropic features 
[33] . This results in significant degradation in mechanical properties, e. 
g., hardness, fracture toughness, etc., and an anisotropic feature usually 
occurs. Though there have been numerous research on the ductility of 
Ni-based superalloys [12,14,18,24 – 26,28,29,34 – 38] , systematic anal -
ysis on the correlation mechanisms between the variation of ductility 
and the phase coarsening is still lacking, especially, little work concerns 
the possible anisotropic features due to the phase coarsening.
In the present work, FEM simulations on quasi-static tensile tests of 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys under different loading directions are 
conducted, regarding to two volume fractions of γ’ precipitates and 
three degrees of phase coarsening in the material. In the corresponding 
simulations FEM models are built according to the microstructural 
characteristics of materials. The variation and anisotropy features of 
ductility in the alloys are systematically investigated, and their re -
lationships with the microstructural evolution are also explored. 
Furthermore, the theoretical guidance for optimizing the microstructure 
of alloys and improving their mechanical properties is provided based 
on related analysis.
2. FEM simulation
In general, three-dimensional (3D) model can capture the internal 
structural features in materials and the actual stress state more accu -
rately. Comparatively, two-dimensional (2D) model is advantageous in 
directly observing the deformation and failure process of materials, also 
it can save the calculation cost. The authors ’ previous work demon -
strated that the 2D model can characterize the main mechanical prop -
erties of alloys relatively well, and it can also reflect the corresponding 
physical mechanism [30 – 32] . Following the similar approach in our 
previous work [30 – 32] . The present investigation will be mainly based 
on the 2D simulations.
2.1. Geometrical model
Similar to author ’ s previous work, the geometrical model is estab -
lished in the pre-processing software [30] . In order to facilitate the 
study, six types of Ni-based single crystal superalloys, including two 
volume fractions of γ’ precipitates combined with three degrees of phase 
coarsening, are selected as samples. Integrated with the quasi-static 
tensile test technology and related FEM simulation method 
[12,14,24,25,35,36,39,40] , the simplified model of the quasi-static 
tensile specimen as well as its fixture is established, as shown in 
Fig. 1 . To cut the calculation amount, the PSD features are only 
considered in representative zone where the deformation and failure of 
the specimen primarily occurs (i.e., the red region in Fig. 1 ). Compar -
atively, other areas are defined as a homogeneous zone where the ma -
terials are considered as homogeneous (i.e., the blue region in Fig. 1 ). In 
addition, the model is meshed with plane strain elements with four- 
node, in which the representative zone contents 600*400 square ele -
ments to balance computational accuracy and cost.
For convenience of discussion, the Ni-based single crystal superal -
loys, which are extensively applied in industry, are selected as the 
research sample, and the average size of the γ’ phase is about 100 – 300 
nm [38,41 – 45] . Therefore, in the representative zone, the size of ele -
ments is configured to be 30 nm, resulting in the overall size of 18*12 
μ m. This element size can present effectively various PSD features in Ni- 
based single crystal superalloys. Besides, loading conditions on the 
specimen are also shown in Fig. 1 , i.e., the left end of Fixture 1 is fixed, 
while the right end of Fixture 2 moves rightward in a constant speed of v , 
and the specimen is connected with the two fixtures.
The geometrical model currently does not yet include the precipi -
tation phase, thus the precipitation particles are needed to further 
construct in the representative zone. In real materials, the precipitation 
phase is bonded to the matrix phase through an interfacial layer, and the 
interfacial layer usually affects the mechanical behavior of alloys [21] . 
Moreover, as dislocations tend to plug in the interfacial layer and then 
micro-voids often initiate, they usually become a weak area within the 
alloy [40,46 – 50] . To better reflect the microstructural features of Ni- 
based superalloys, the geometrical model of precipitation phase and 
interfacial layer will be established simultaneously by the keyword file 
of FEM software. Based on the actual TEM images or the structural 
evolution simulation results on the Ni-based single crystal superalloys, 
the model of precipitation particles and the corresponding interfacial 
layers are established with the help of computer language. The detailed 
establishing process is listed as follow:
(1) Establishment of grids containing precipitate phase and matrix 
phase.
Step 1 : Digitize the image of microstructure in real Ni-based super -
alloys through programming process (e.g., Python), and then transform 
it into a gray image through related functions;
Step 2 : Adjust the resolution of gray image by the nearest neighbor 
interpolation method, and make the number of pixel points equal to that 
of elements in the representative zone (600 × 400 pixels);
Step 3 : Binarize the gray image through the image segmentation 
function, wherein the black and white pixels correspond to the matrix 
and precipitation phases, respectively, and then correlate the Node ID 
number with the Material ID number (i.e., pixel value). Therefore, the 
transition model containing precipitate phase and matrix phase is 
established during this process.
(2) Definition of interface layer in the grids
Step 1 : Check grids under the left – right order. When the point 
property belongs to the matrix phase, if its left point belongs to the 
matrix phase and the right point to the precipitate phase, or if its left 
point belongs to the precipitate phase and the right point to the matrix 
phase, then shift the point property as the interface layer.
Step 2 : Repeat Step 1 until all the 600 × 400 grids are checked. During 
this process the interface layers locating in the left and right sides of 
precipitates are established.

internal movement of dislocations. The initiation and propagation of 
plastic deformation bands are closely linked with the multiplication and 
movement of dislocation. Specially, in the case of precipitation- 
hardening materials like Ni-based superalloys, the precipitation phase 
acts as a series of point barriers. The impediment on the dislocation 
movement is mainly derived from two factors: (a) the interaction among 
dislocations and (b) the interaction between dislocations and precipi -
tation phase [14,15,40,60] . In addition, the authors ’ previous studies 
showed that the effective plastic strain in numerical simulations corre -
sponds to the dislocation density in actual alloys. Therefore, based on 
the evolution features of plastic deformation bands in the numerical 
simulation, the initiation, movement and multiplication behaviors of 
dislocations within the alloys can be deduced [30 – 32] .

5. Discussions
The investigation in this paper indicates that the numerical simula -
tion results effectively capture the primary variation features of ductility 
in Ni-based single crystal superalloys. Although the FEM simulations are 
conducted for Ni-based single crystal superalloys, related analysis ap -
proaches and techniques, as well as the understanding of the corre -
sponding mechanism, are universally applicable to the Ni-based 
superalloys strengthened by γ’ phase. These approaches can be 
employed to conduct in-depth studies on various types of Ni-based su -
peralloys with different microstructural characteristics, prepared 
through different processes.
It is worth noting that because of the limitations in computational 
scale and solution cost, related study is based on the 2D scaled models, 
and only in the representative zone of specimen the microstructural 
characteristics are considered. Therefore, there exist some differences in 
the detailed variation features of ductility between the simulation re -
sults and test results. To investigate the corresponding variations in 
ductility more accurately, it is necessary to establish a larger scale and 
finer mesh for the FEM simulation, such as 3D model. However, this 
would result in a significant rise in the computational cost. Besides, by 
altering the material parameters in the constitutive model (see Table 1 ), 
the effects of various factors on the ductility can also be investigated in 
detail. Furthermore, by establishing a new constitutive model consid -
ering the strain rate effect and carrying out dynamic loading simulation 
analysis under different strain rate conditions, the strain rate effect on 
the ductility of alloys can be further discussed. More refined numerical 
simulations and more detailed mechanism analysis will be conducted in 
the future research.

closely related with the microstructural evolution, especially with the 
PSD within the alloy. The variation in the microstructure will change the 
initiation, movement and multiplication characteristics of dislocations. 
After phase coarsening, the matrix channels for dislocation movement 
become wider, and thus the resistance for the propagation of plastic 
deformation bands and cracks will be reduced, and it results in faster 
crack propagation rate. At the same time, the larger precipitation par -
ticles strengthen the hindering and pinning effect on dislocations, and 
would results in more dislocation plugging, eventually the nucleation 
and multiplication of micro-voids and microcracks become easier, and it 
leads to a significant reduction in the ductility of alloys.
(3) The loading direction also contributes to the ductility of Ni-based 
superalloys. the coarsened precipitation particles and their queue 
arrangement characteristics have different hindering effects on dislo -

initiation and propagation behaviors of plastic deformation bands and 
cracks also differ from each other, and finally it induces complex 
anisotropic features in the ductility of alloys.

optimizing the microstructure and the usage condition, and it could offer 
valuable guidance for enhancing the ductility and reliability of Ni-based
