Cu45Zr45Al5Ag5 Bulk Glassy Alloy with Enhanced Compressive Strength
and Plasticity at Cryogenic Temperature
Asahi Kawashima 1, Takahiro Okuno 1, Hiroaki Kurishita 2, Wei Zhang 1,
Hisamichi Kimura 1 and Akihisa Inoue 1
1Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, Japan
2International Research Center for Nuclear Materials Science, Institute for Materials Research,
Tohoku University, Oarai, Ibaraki 311-1313, Japan
Compressive tests were made for the as-cast Cu 45Zr45Al5Ag5 bulk glassy alloy rods with a diameter of 3 mm at 298 and 77 K, at an initial
strain rate of 5 /C210/C04 s/C01. It was found that at 77 K, the compressive yield strength, maximum strength and plastic strain to fracture of the glassy
alloy rod are higher than those at 298 K. The maximum strength measured at 77 K is about 13% larger than that measured at 298 K. In particular,
its plasticity at 77 K is signiﬁcantly enhanced. SEM observations reveal that more shear bands appear near the fracture plane at 77 K. The higher
strength and ductility at a cryogenic temperature (77 K) are probably due to the lower mobility of the squeezed free volume.

1. Introduction
Since the ﬁrst success of synthesizing bulk glassy alloys in
Ln-1) based alloys in 1989, they have attracted great attention
as a novel bulk metallic material in science and engineering
ﬁelds. In recent years, several families of multicomponent
alloys exhibiting excellent glass-forming ability have been
developed. They include Mg-,
2) Zr-,3,4) Ti-,5) Fe-,6) Pd-,7)
Ni-,8) Co-9) and Cu- 10) based alloys. They possess many
unique properties, such as good corrosion resistance, ex-
tremely high strength, relatively high fracture toughness
11) at
ambient temperature and high strain rate formability and
workability at elevated temperature, applicable to industrial
materials.
12)
Although it is important to evaluate the mechanical
properties of the bulk metallic glasses at cryogenic temper-
atures for wider applications, such as in the ﬁeld of space
exploration and liqueﬁed gas storage,
13) few data associated
with Zr-based metallic glasses have been reported. Tabach-
nikova and Bengus et al.
14,15) investigated the compressive
fracture behavior of a Zr 41:2Ti13:8Cu12:5Ni10Be22:5 alloy at
temperatures of 300 and 77 K and a strain rate of 2:8 /C2
10/C04 s/C01. The fracture stresses ( /C27cf ) of the alloy under
compression at temperatures of 300 and 77 K were 1:75 /C6
0:25 GPa and 1:35 /C60:35 GPa, respectively. On the other
hand, Li et al. 13,16) reported that both /C27cf and plastic strain
("cp)o faZ r 57:4Cu17:9Ni13:4Al10:3Nb1 glassy alloy increased
at 77 K and a strain rate of 2 /C210/C04 s/C01. Accordingly, the
mechanical properties at cryogenic temperature of the glassy
alloys are diﬀerent depending on the alloy system. In
comparison with Zr-based glassy alloys, no information is
available about the mechanical properties at the cryogenic
temperature of a Cu-Zr-based glassy alloy
17) containing
45 at% Cu.
The objective of this work is to investigate the mechanical
properties at 77 K of a Cu 45Zr45Al5Ag5 glassy alloy.
2. Experimental Procedure
The alloy composition of the sample was Cu 45Zr45Al5Ag5
(at%). An alloy ingot was prepared by arc melting the
mixtures of pure Cu, Zr, Al and Ag metals with purity of over
99.5 mass% in a puriﬁed argon atmosphere. The alloy ingot
was re-melted four times to ensure chemical homogeneity.
The bulk glassy alloy of cylindrical rods with a diameter of
3 mm was prepared by a high pressure die casting method
2) in
an argon atmosphere. The copper mold was cooled with
ﬂowing water. The ribbons with a cross section of 0:013 /C2
1:0 mm
2 were also prepared for comparison by a single roller
melt spinning method in an argon atmosphere. The amor-
phicity of the cylindrical and ribbon samples was inves-
tigated by the diﬀractometer X-ray method (XRD) using
Cu K /C11 radiation and thermal stability was examined by
diﬀerential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a heating rate of
0.67 K/s. The compressive tests were conducted at 298 and
77 K using a servo-hydraulic testing machine (Shimadzu
Servopulser of 50-kN shear type load cell) at an initial strain
rate of 5 /C210
/C04 s/C01. The sample dimension for the com-
pressive test was 3 mm in diameter and 6 mm in length. In
order to reduce the frictional stress, both ends of the
specimens were lubricated with lubricating grease. The
fracture and side surfaces were examined by scanning
electron microscopy (SEM).

also reported that at 77 K chevron pattern typical of brittle
failure was present on some areas of the fracture surface.
They prepared the specimens from the ingot with a diameter
of 40 mm, suggesting increasing eﬀects of inhomogeneity in
microstructures and resulting in decrease in fracture stress at
77 K.
It is well known that large homogenous plastic deforma-
tion of glassy alloys by viscous ﬂow takes place under the
condition of the super-cooled liquid region, while at temper-
atures lower than Tg, plastic deformation becomes inhomo-
geneous with highly localized shear bands.
19) Glassy materi-
als start yielding at the onset of formation of shear bands. It
is not certain whether or not glassy alloys become stiﬀ at
cryogenic temperatures. Accordingly, we examined the
temperature dependence of elastic parameters for the
Cu
45Zr45Al5Ag5 bulk glassy alloy using an ultrasonic pulse
method.20) Longitudinal and transverse wave velocities and
eight kinds of the elastic parameters were measured
simultaneously as a function of temperature in the range of
77 and 373 K. The results showed that the Young’s and shear
moduli and the elastic Debye temperature gradually increas-
ed with decreasing temperature from room to cryogenic
temperatures. This means that the glassy alloy becomes stiﬀ
and the eﬀective atomic distance of the alloy decreases at
cryogenic temperatures. Furthermore, Poisson’s ratio de-
creased at cryogenic temperatures, indicating that the rigidity
of the alloy increased. At cryogenic temperature, therefore,
the glassy alloy becomes stiﬀ and rigid, accompanied by the
decrease in the eﬀective atomic distance, which results in
squeezing the free volumes. It is currently accepted that shear
band initiation in glassy alloys is related to free volume
coalescence.
19,21) At cryogenic temperatures, increased stiﬀ-
ness of the atomic bond makes the squeezed free volume

4. Conclusions
The compressive tests were carried out for as-cast
Cu
45Zr45Al5Ag5 bulk glassy alloy rods with a diameter of
3 mm and a length of 6 mm at an initial strain rate of
5 /C210/C04 s/C01 and 298 and 77 K. The main results obtained are
as follows.
(1) The compressive yield strength, maximum strength and
plastic strain to fracture of the glassy alloy rod
measured at 77 K are higher than those at 298 K. The
maximum strength at 77 K is about 13% larger than that
measured at 298 K. The plastic strain at 77 K is about
0.9%, while that at 298 K is 0.14%.
(2) At 77 K, more shear bands are observed near the
fracture plane.
(3) In both cases, the compressive fracture surfaces have an
angle of about 40
/C14with respect to the loading axis and
consist only of a vein-like pattern.
