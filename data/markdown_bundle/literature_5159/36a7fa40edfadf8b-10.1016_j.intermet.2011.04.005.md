abstract
The high temperature compression behavior of TiAl eRu alloys was studied at different temperatures and
strain rates. Ru was found to have a strong strengthening effect on TiAl alloys. However the Ru addition
amount was limited by its low solubility in g-TiAl and a2-Ti3Al, and the detrimental effect of excessive
ternary phase precipitation. Furthermore, the melting temperature decreases when Ru /C21 0.6 at.% as the
alloy composition approaches a ternary eutectic point. The strengthening mechanism is discussed and two
separate mechanisms are proposed, viz. solid solution strengthening and re ﬁned colony strengthening.
Intergranular cracks were found in the alloys with low Ru or no Ru addition, but were barely detected as Ru
content increased to above 0.6 at.%. It was suggested that Ru showed a beneﬁcial effect on both strength and
ductility of TiAl alloys due to the re ﬁned colony size. Three-point bend test results showed that the Ru
addition can also improve the room temperature ductility of TiAl alloys. Hot workability was increased
according to the compression tests. Thermal-mechanically treated TiAl eRu had much smaller grain size
than the heat-treated samples due to dynamic recrystallization. But it did not show superior strength in the
compression test compared to the heat-treated samples. The Zener eHollomon parameter was calculated
from the compression strength of heat-treated TiAl eRu alloys. Its relationship with dynamic recrystalli-
zation and hot work is discussed. The mechanical properties of TiAl eRu alloys are compared with TiAleNb
samples and demonstrate a promising combination of strength and ductility.

1. Introduction
TiAl alloys have attracted great attention in the past decades due
to their potential as high temperature structural material[1e3].T h e y
have relatively low density and high speci ﬁc strength at elevated
temperature. Thus they offer opportunities to be used in modern
turbine engines for substantial weight reduction compared with Ni-
based alloys and better performance compared with Ti-based alloys.
However, their industrial application still suffers from high pro-
cessing cost, low ductility and relatively low creep strength [4,5].
Recent studies on TiAl eNb showed that both the high temperature
strength and the oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys can be substan-
tially improved with relatively large amount of Nb addition[1,6].T h i s
raised interest in the use of other alloying elements that could be
doped into TiAl for better performance. Ru has much lower solubility
in TiAl compared to Nb, less than 1 at.% even at high temperature[7].
It will form the ternary phase
s1 (a ﬁlled variant of the Th 6Mn23
structure) when Ru content exceeds its solubility limit in g and a2
phases. The phase equilibria of Ti eAleRu have been thoroughly
investigated by Gritsiv et al. [7], while the mechanical properties of
TiAleRu alloys have not previously been investigated in detail.
There have been some conclusions on the strengthening
mechanisms for TiAl eNb alloys. Nb has considerable solubility in
binary Ti eAl phases, especially in g-TiAl [8]. Therefore TiAl eNb
alloys usually consist of only two major phases, viz. a2-
Ti3Al þ g-TiAl. They can form a series of structures from duplex to
lamellar depending on the heat treatment [3,9,10]. Recent research
revealed that coarse-grained fully lamellar structure exhibits rela-
tively good fracture toughness and excellent creep resistance, but
poor tensile ductility and strength especially at room temperature
[1 1]. Super- ﬁne lamellar spacing plays the key role in the
strengthening mechanism. Hot isothermal pressing (HIP) is
required before homogenization to remove the voids and porosity
trapped during casting. Additional thermal-mechanical treatment
before solutionization can further strength the material as TiAl
alloys will easily go through dynamic recrystallization during hot
work and form ﬁner grain size [5]. All these processes substantially
increase the mechanical performance of TiAl alloys, but at the same
time they also lead to raised cost and limited application.
The aim of the present study is to investigate the effect of Ru
addition on the microstructure and mechanical properties of TiAl

2. Experiment
The present study was carried out on a series of alloys with
constant Ti to Al ratio z1.26 and increasing Ru amount. The ingots
were made by arc melting using commercially pure materials. A Nb
addition alloy was also made with composition of Ti47 eTi45eNb8

ﬂipped over and re-melted ﬁve times to ensure complete mixing.

used as a getter to further remove oxygen. The as-cast alloys were

with a general purpose strain gage and the strain rate was

In some sense, there is a great beneﬁ t for practical application to
add Ru to TiAl alloys. Second and third generation TiAl alloys are
well known for the requirement of post-processing, such as HIP, hot
extrusion and hot forging. With Ru addition, hot work is less
necessary since its mechanical properties are comparable to the
alloys without thermal-mechanical treatment (viz. HT samples).
Not only does it reduce the processing cost, but it facilitates the
manufacturing of parts with complicated shapes, like discs and
blades. But even after improvement, the ductility of TiAl eRu is still
low compared to other structural materials. Additional alloy
development and microstructure design are required to further
improve its properties.

The strengthening effect of re ﬁned colony size will be canceled
out as Ru content is further increased. One major trade-off is the
reduced amount of super- ﬁne lamellar structure. Also the melting
point of TiAleRu alloys will drop as the composition moves close to
the eutectic point [7], which is detrimental for high temperature
strength.
Therefore, 1e1.4% of Ru is supposed to be the optimal compo-
sition range to be added into TiAl alloys considering both the
technical and cost issues. It improves the grain boundary structure
and reduces the necessity of complicated processing like HIP and
hot work. Thus the processing cost may be reduced offsetting the
added material cost. At the same time the strength is increased by
a large amount with little sacri ﬁce on density, comparable to Nb
addition. As Ru is a
g former, TiAl eRu alloys are more dif ﬁcult to
reach fully lamellar structure which is the key factor for the high
temperature creep strength. Elements that can increase a2 amount
should be considered in combination with Ru.
As-cast structure was not chosen as the candidate for high
temperature application in this work, although it has higher
strength than the heat-treated structure in the compression tests.
The enhanced strength of as-cast alloys is attributed to vacancy
hardening, whose effect will be reduced during high temperature
deformation.
5. Conclusions
(1) Fine lamellae were the major structure of all the samples after
heat treatment. Cracks were found along grain boundaries in
TiAleNb and some TiAl eRu with low Ru amount. These grain
boundary cavities tended to be fewer for TiAl eRu when Ru
/C21 1 at.%.
s1 phase was found in the ingot with Ru /C21 0.6 at.%, and
started to form a network when Ru /C21 1.4 at.%. Dynamic
recrystallization occurred during thermal-mechanical treat-
ment, leading to a reﬁned grain size in the hot-worked structure.
(2) Both Nb and Ru have strong strengthening effects on TiAl. The
strength of TiAl alloys increased with higher Ru addition up to
1.4 at.%. When Ru /C21 1.4at%, the strength started to decrease, as
the composition approached the eutectic point. For the heat-
treated samples, Ru showed better strengthening effect than
Nb. TiAleNb and some TiAl eRu with low Ru content collapsed
by intergranular cracking during high temperature compres-
sion. This is attributed to its weak grain boundary structure.
(3) TiAl eRu showed better ductility than TiAl eNb in room
temperature ﬂexural tests. TiAl eRu had much higher ﬂexural
strain and fracture energy than TiAleNb and its fracture surface
showed some evidence of ductile failure.
(4) Ru addition generated large lattice distortion and modi ﬁed the
electron-atom ratio when it dissolved into
g-TiAl. Thus it
contributed to the solid solution strengthening. When Ru
exceeds its solubility limit in TiAl alloys, re ﬁned colony struc-
ture started to form. This was considered to be the strength-
ening mechanism when Ru can no longer be dissolved in TiAl
alloys.
(5) Zener eHollomon parameter and apparent activation energy
were calculated. It was found that the Zener Hollomon
parameter had a close relationship with the dynamic recrys-
tallization and hot work behavior of TiAl alloys.
(6) A hot-workability map was proposed for HT Ti55eAl43.6eRu1.4.
Ru addition was proven to enhance the hot-workability of TiAl
alloys.
