via Three Principles of Composition Design

Abstract
Composition design is one of the significant methods to break the trade-off relation between strength and ductility of 
medium-/high-entropy alloys (M/HEAs). Herein, we introduced three fundamental principles for the composition design: 
high elastic modulus, low stacking-fault energy (SFE), and appropriate phase stability. Subsequently, based on the three prin-
ciples of component design and the first-principles calculation results, we designed and investigated a non-equiatomic Ni28 
MEA with a single-phase and uniform microstructure. The Ni28 MEA has great mechanical properties with yield strength of 
329.5 MPa, tensile strength of 829.4 MPa, and uniform elongation of 56.9% at ambient temperature, respectively. The high 
ductility of Ni28 MEA may be attributed to the dynamically refined microstructure composed of hexagonal close-packed 
(HCP) lamellas and stacking faults (SFs), which provide extremely high work-hardening ability. This work demonstrates the 
feasibility of the three principles for composition design and can be extended to more M/HEAs in the future.
Keywords Medium-entropy alloys · Strength · Ductility · Stacking-fault energy · Work-hardening rate
1 Introduction
Recently, the development of multi-component medium-/
high-entropy alloys (M/HEAs) consisting of more than 
three or five elements has opened up new regions for the 
alloy composition design [1, 2]. M/HEAs exhibit significant 
potential in various industrial fields, due to the outstanding 
room and low-temperature toughness [ 3, 4], fatigue resist-
ance [5], corrosion resistance [6 ], and radiation resistance 
[7]. Similar to the traditional metallic materials [8 –10], 
the thermal–mechanical process assisted improvement of 
mechanical properties of M/HEAs, via introducing high-
density defects (e.g., grain/twin/phase boundaries and dis-
locations) or nanoprecipitates shows an inverse relation-
ship, that is, the increase in strength generally comes at 
the expense of ductility. In contrast, it has been revealed 
that modifying the alloy composition is an effective means 
to synergistically enhance the strength and the ductility of 
M/HEAs [11– 13]. However, the efficient development of 
novel strong and ductile M/HEAs still remains elusive to 
date attributing to the lack of good principles for composi-
tion design.
To address this issue, our previous work has proposed 
three valid principles for the composition design of face-
centered cubic (FCC) M/HEAs with high strength and good 
ductility [14, 15], including: (1) high elastic modulus: The 
higher elastic modulus is, the better it can suppress the 
occurrence of plastic deformation and crack initiation; (2) 
low stacking-fault energy (SFE): Reducing SFE can facili-
tate the activation of multiple work-hardening mechanisms, 
resulting in a diverse array of deformation characteristic 
structures, including deformation twins (DTs), stacking 
faults (SFs), and, notably, the bidirectional phase transfor -
mation from FCC to hexagonal close-packed (HCP) and 
HCP to FCC. This effectively promotes strain delocalization

2 Simulation Model and Experimental
2.1 Simulation Model
Here, following the three fundamental principles, we have 
designed a novel non-equiatomic CoCrNi MEA with a 
single-phase FCC structure and superior combination of 
strength and plasticity. The design route of this FCC MEA 
is illustrated as follows. Firstly, our previous research has 
shown that the optimal content of Cr element can range from 
30 at.% to 35 at.% in CoCrNi MEAs, and increasing the 
Cr content causes a monotonic decrease of SFE and hence 
improves the work-hardening ability and plasticity [14]. 
Therefore, the Cr element content was determined as 35 at.% 
in the current study. Subsequently, the SFE and phase sta-
bility of CoxCr35Ni65-x MEAs were comprehensively evalu-
ated utilizing the first-principles calculations, namely, the 
exact muffin-tin orbitals (EMTO) method with the coherent 
potential approximation (CPA), to predetermine the optimal 
composition.
Figure 1a displays the schematics of crystal configura-
tions without or with a slice of SF for the calculation of SFE. 
The result indicates that the SFE has a marked decline with 
the increase in Co content in CoxCr35Ni65-x MEAs at 0 K and 
298 K, respectively, as depicted in Fig. 1b. Furthermore, the 
SFE shows mild dependence on the temperature, which is 
different from the substantial decrease of SFE at low tem -
peratures in CoCrFeMnNi HEAs [22]. It demonstrates that 
the improvement of the mechanical properties of CoCrNi 
MEAs at low temperatures may be attributed to the activa-
tion of more deformation twins [4 ] rather than the transi-
tion from sole dislocation motion at room temperature to the 
additional deformation mechanism (e.g., twinning-induced 
plasticity (TWIP) effect) at cryogenic temperatures as in 
CoCrFeMnNi HEAs. Moreover, it is worth mentioning that 
the experimental models for measuring SFE may not be 
applicable in metastable alloys. The SFE values obtained 
through first-principles calculations are often negative but 
have demonstrated better agreement with various deforma-
tion mechanisms [23]. Figure  1c illustrates the function of 
Gibbs free energy difference between HCP and FCC phases 
(ΔEHCP-FCC) at 0 K relative to the variation of Ni content 
in CoxCr35Ni65-x MEAs. It reveals that the FCC structure 
remains metastable at cryogenic temperatures compared 
with the HCP phase. With increasing Ni content or decreas-
ing Co content, the ΔEHCP-ΔEFCC raises monotonically, sug-
gesting that the energy barrier for the phase transition from 
FCC to HCP increases. In other words, the increment of 
Co content causes the deterioration of FCC phase stability, 
and thus, the existence of HCP phase can be inevitable in 
 CoxCr35Ni65-x MEAs with high Co contents.
Moreover, our previous study shows that the rise of Co 
content leads to an evident increase of Young’s modulus and 
shear modulus in CoxCr33Ni67-x MEAs, which contributes to 
the enhancement of both yield and tensile strengths [11]. As 
a result, the increase of Co content has incompatible effects 
on the microstructure and the mechanical properties. Accord-
ing to the three fundamental principles, the decrease of SFE 
and the increment of elastic modulus induced by higher Co 
content promote the activation of TWIP or TRIP effects and 
the improvement of work-hardening ability and strength of

2.2 Experimental
In the current study, the Ni28 MEA was fabricated through 
vacuum induction melting in an Ar-gettered argon atmos-
phere (purity larger than 99.95%). The ingots were remelted 
at least five times to improve the chemical homogeneity. 
Then, the ingot was homogenized at 1200 °C for 2 h fol-
lowed by water quenching and hot rolling at 1100 °C with 
a total reduction of 50%. Finally, the rolled MEAs were 
annealed at 900 °C for 5 min to obtain a fully recrystallized 
microstructure. The dog-bone-shaped tensile samples with 
a gauge section dimension of 6 mm × 3 mm × 1.5 mm were 
prepared using electrical discharge machining. The uniaxial 
tensile tests were carried out on an Instron 8862 instrument 
at room temperature with a strain rate of 1 × 10−3  s−1. The 
digital image correlation (DIC) method with the LaVision 
DaVis data acquisition package was used to study the strain 
field upon tensile deformation. The sample surfaces were 
polished using a 2000# grit SiC paper and electro-chemi-
cally polished using a solution of HClO4:CH3COOH = 1:9 
(vol.) under a voltage of 20 V for 20 s at − 20 °C to obtain 
mirror finish. Electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) 
orientation mappings with step sizes of 0.5 and 0.045 μm 
and electron channeling contrast imaging (ECCI) were con-
ducted using a field emission scanning electron microscope 
(SEM, Sigma-500). High magnification microstructures for 
Ni28 MEA were observed by transmission electron micros-
copy (TEM, JEM-2100P). The high-resolution (HR) TEM 
was conducted by Thermo Fisher Talos F200X. The TEM 
foils were prepared using a twin-jet electropolishing method 
by Tenupole-5 in a solution of 10% perchloric and acid 90% 
alcohol, with a voltage of 20 V and temperature of − 20 °C.
3 Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructural Observation
Figure  2 shows the microstructure before tensile tests of 
Ni28 MEA. The EBSD image in Fig.  2a displays the fully 
recrystallized grains with high-density annealing twins 
(ATs). As illustrated in Fig.  2b, the proportion of twin 
boundaries (TBs) reaches 73.2% after annealing treatment 
in Ni28 MEA, while the grain size is 7.41 ± 5.9 μm. Based 
on the atomistic model of ATs by Gleiter [24], a high propor-
tion of ATs may originate from low energy of the coherent 
twin plane (approximately half the SFE value) high driving 
energy and annealing temperature. In view of this, lower 
SFE of Co-rich Ni28 MEA (Fig.  1b) could facilitate the 
formation of high-density ATs. Figure  2c shows the EBSD 
phase mapping of Fig.  2a. It indicates that the entire Ni28 
sample exhibits a single-phase FCC structure, which proves 
moderate stability of FCC phase. TEM image in Fig. 2d also 
demonstrates obvious ATs extending from the boundary to 
the interior of the grain. The representative SEM images 
and corresponding EDS mapping of a random location in 
Fig. 2e show that no significant segregation and clustering 
was observed in the Ni28 MEA after undergoing homogeni-
zation, rolling, and annealing, respectively.
3.2 Mechanical Properties
Figure  3a displays the tensile engineering stress–strain 
curves of the Ni28 MEA. It exhibits the yield strength (YS) 
of 329.5 MPa, ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 829.4 MPa, 
uniform elongation (UE) of 56.9%, and total elongation (TE) 
of 68.6%. The illustration in Fig. 3a shows the work-harden-
ing rate and true stress–strain curves. The work-hardening 
rate rapidly decreases at the initial plastic deformation, fol-
lowed by a slow reduction until fracture. It implies that the 
occurrence of a particular work-hardening mechanism can 
be induced in the Ni28 MEA with lower SFE, which contrib-
utes to higher work-hardening ability and delays the onset of 
catastrophic fracture [25]. A comparison of the UTS versus 
UE of the non-equiatomic Ni28 MEA designed based on 
the three principles and partially equiatomic CoCrNi MEA 
is shown in Fig.  3b [26–34]. It demonstrates that the three 
principles, i.e., high elastic modulus, low SFE, and suitable 
FCC phase stability, are valid for the efficient development 
of the CoCrNi MEAs with superior balance between high 
tensile strength and adequate tensile ductility.

respectively. TEM image in Fig.  4b and the corresponding 
selected area diffraction pattern (SADP) (Fig.  4c) show 
high-density parallel HCP lamella structures. Meanwhile,

Based on the above results, our Ni28 MEA exhibits high 
plasticity due to both the TRIP and TWIP effects while 
maintaining a certain yield strength. The formation of both 
HCP lamellae and SFs may be attributed to the effect of 
lower SFE, which resulted from our design strategy by 
reducing Ni content and increasing Co and Cr contents. As 
is well known, low SFE can help suppress dislocation cross 
slip and promote planar slip, enhance deformation uniform-
ity, and inhibit stress concentration. Furthermore, twins and 
phase transformations generated by even lower SFE can pro-
vide outstanding work-hardening ability, delaying the occur-
rence of finally catastrophic fracture. It is worth noting that 
both HCP lamellas and SFs form a stable network structure, 
which we speculated is due to the encounter of HCP phases 
and SFs on different slip systems during sufficient deforma-
tion. This structure not only subdivides the microstructure, 
impedes dislocation motion, facilitates dislocation multipli-
cation, but also enhances work-hardening capability. Due to 
the advantages of high elastic modulus [39], and the precise 
control of phase stability by adjusting Ni and Co contents, a 
superior combination of high strength and excellent ductility 
has been achieved in the Ni28 MEA under the guidance of 
our three principles.
4 Conclusion
In this study, in order to provide guidance for the component 
design, we adopted the three principles: high elastic modu-
lus, low SFE, and appropriate phase stability. Based on the 
three principles combined with the simulation results, we 
designed a non-equiatomic Ni28 MEA with single-phase 
and uniform microstructure. The mechanical properties and 
work-hardening ability have been greatly improved com-
pared with the equiatomic CoCrNi MEA. Typically, the 
YS, UTS, and UE of the Ni28 MEA were 329.5 MPa, 829.4 
MPa, and 56.9%, respectively. TEM, EBSD, and ECC inves-
tigations revealed that the high ductility of Ni28 MEA may 
be attributed to the TRIP and TWIP effects. After experi-
encing sufficient deformation, the HCP lamellas and SFs on 
different slip systems meet in motion and form a network 
structure. This structure can not only impede dislocation 
movement and promote dislocation multiplication but also 
effectively enhance the work-hardening ability. Our work 
does not only demonstrate the feasibility of the three prin-
ciples for the quick composition design of FCC CoCrNi 
MEAs, but also has the potential to be further extended to 
other M/HEAs in the future.
