1. Introduction
The
 near equiatomic FeCo-based soft magnetic alloys have
been attracted considerable attention due to their excellent soft
magnetic properties, such as high saturation, high Curie temper-
ature, low coercivity and low core loss [1–5]. The applications of
soft magnetic alloys in many ﬁelds, such as rotor or stator for
power generation working at 500−600 ◦C [1,2,6], require not only
the excellent soft magnetic properties but also good mechanical
properties at high temperatures. Unfortunately, most conventional
strengthening approaches that enhance strength will inevitably
lead to a substantial increase in coercivity [2], namely strength-
coercivity trade-off. Therefore, it is required to develop a soft
magnetic material with high strength and low coercivity at high
temperatures.

It is well
 known that the addition of 2 at.% V can improve the
cold workability of disordered FeCo alloys while remaining good
soft magnetic properties [7–10]. Recently, in addition to conven-
tional preparation processing, laser-based additive manufacturing
was used to process brittle FeCo alloys, which introduces multi-
scale microstructures that simultaneously promote strengthening
and enhance ductility to a level previously unobtainable in the FeCo
alloy [11]. However, the FeCo alloy lacks the required mechanical
properties (high yield strength, good ductility and creep resistance)
for their high temperature applications [1]. In this respect, stud-
ies have focused on improving the mechanical properties of the
alloy by using severe plastic deformation and speciﬁc heat treat-
ment to obtain ultraﬁne-grained microstructure, leading to high
tensile strength [3,12–17]. For instance, Duckham et al. signiﬁcantly
improved the high temperature yield strength of FeCo-2 V alloy
by obtaining ultraﬁne-grained FeCo-2 V alloy [3]. But the mag-
netic materials with ultraﬁne grain have a high coercivity due to
grain boundaries acting as pinning points for magnetic domain wall
movement [18]. In addition, the ultraﬁne-grained alloys usually
exhibit poor thermal stability at high temperatures due to the grain
coarsening [19], which limits their applications. Micro-alloying

approaches for enhancing high-temperature mechanical proper-

will inevitably lead to a signiﬁcant loss of soft magnetic properties
[1].
In this work, we achieve a good combination of high ten-
sile strength and ductility as well as low coercivity at 600 ◦C
in FeCo-2V-0.3Cr-0.2Mo soft magnetic alloy by appropriate heat
treatments. Annealing at 800−850 ◦C leads to the formation of
high-density magnetic nanoprecipitates that are identiﬁed as FeCo-
based phase with disordered body-centered cubic (bcc) structure.
The bcc nanoprecipitates exhibit similar soft magnetic properties
with B2 FeCo matrix and thus enable the alloy to have a low coerciv-
ity. Meanwhile, these nanoprecipitates can impede the dislocation
motion and suppress the brittle fracture, resulting in a high tensile
strength and ductility. This work provides a guideline to design high
strength, high ductility and low coercivity of soft magnetic alloys
for applications at high temperatures via soft magnetic nanopre-
cipitates.
2. Experimental procedures
Fe-49.0Co-2.0 V-0.3Cr-0.2Mo and Fe-49.0Co-2.0 V alloys (at.%)
were prepared via arc-melting under argon atmosphere with purity
of 99.99 wt.% for Fe and 99.95 wt.% for other elements. The ingots
were repeatedly melted for ﬁve times to ensure the homogene-
ity of the alloys. After homogenized annealing at 1200 ◦C for 6 h
and furnace-cooled in a vacuum of 10−4 Pa, the ingots were forged
and hot rolled to plates with a thickness of 3 mm at 1100 ◦C. The
hot-rolled plates were annealed at 950 ◦C, and then quenched in
an ice brine solution in order to retain the disordered phase to
increase the cold workability of the alloys. The plates were then
cold-rolled to 2 mm in thickness with a reduction of 30 %, and the
cold-rolled samples were ﬁnally annealed in a vacuum of 10−4 Pa
at the temperature range from 600 ◦C to 850 ◦C for 2 h followed by
air cooling.
Flat dog-bone-shaped tensile samples with the gauge length of
18 mm and width of 3 mm were machined from the annealed plates
along the rolling direction. Tensile tests were conducted at a strain
rate of 5 × 10−4 s-1 under a temperature of 600 ◦C on the MTS-
880 instrument. The fracture surfaces after the tensile tests were
observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The microstruc-
ture of the alloys was studied by X-ray diffraction (XRD), electron
probe micro-analyzer (EPMA, JAX-8100) and transmission elec-
tron microscopy (TEM, JEM 2100 F). Elemental composition was
analyzed by energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX). High tem-
perature coercivity was measured by SQUID-VSM in a vacuum of 6
Pa at 600 ◦C.

red dashed lines: band-like long precipitates at grain bound-

tates comprise many nanoscale band-like substructures, which are

Fig. 5 shows the effect of annealing temperature on the mechan-
ical properties of FeCo-2V-0.3Cr-0.2Mo alloys at 600 ◦C, together
with FeCo-2 V for comparison. It is clearly seen that both the tensile
strength and total elongation show strong annealing temperature
dependence. In contrast to very low tensile strength for FeCo-2
V alloy annealed at the temperatures in this work, the FeCo-2V-
0.3Cr-0.2Mo alloy exhibits a superior tensile strength, which ﬁrst
slightly decreases due to the elimination of the internal stress and
dislocation density by recrystallization, and then increases signif-
icantly with further increasing the annealing temperature. When
annealed at 850 ◦C, the tensile strength of FeCo-2V-0.3Cr-0.2Mo is
564 MPa, which is much larger than 287 MPa for FeCo-2 V alloy
(Fig. 5(a)). The signiﬁcant increase in tensile strength of FeCo-
2V-0.3Cr-0.2Mo alloy after annealing at >760 ◦C is attributed to
the formation of numerous nanoprecipitates that act as strong
barriers for dislocation motion, which will be discussed below.
Combining the solid solution strengthening of Cr and Mo and pre-
cipitation strengthening, the FeCo-2V-0.3Cr-0.2Mo alloy displays
much higher tensile strength than FeCo-2 V alloy. As shown in

Most importantly, current magnetic bcc nanoprecipitates do
not lead to a sacriﬁce of coercivity and saturated magnetiza-
tion. Fig. 8(a) shows the typical magnetic hysteresis curves of the
annealed FeCo-2V-0.3Cr-0.2Mo alloy measured at 600 ◦C. The vari-
ation of the coercivity with annealing temperature is summarized
in Fig. 8(b). It is clearly seen that the coercivity decreases to a
minimal value with increasing the annealing temperature up to
760 ◦C due to the elimination of residual stress induced by the
cold rolling. After reaching the minima of 6.97 Oe at 760 ◦C, the
coercivity slightly increases to 9.70 Oe with further increasing the
annealing temperature to 850 ◦C. In contrast to coercivity, satu-
rated magnetization is insensitive to the annealing temperature.
When annealing at >760 ◦C, the formation of high-density magnetic
bcc nanoprecipitates results in only a slight increase in coercivity
of the FeCo-2V-0.3Cr-0.2Mo alloy, which suggests that nanopre-
cipitates may relieve the deterioration in coercivity by decreasing
the pinning effect on the domain wall movement. This may be
attributed to the fact that these nanoprecipitates are FeCo-based
soft magnetic phases. Usually, nonmagnetic precipitates in FeCo-
based alloys introduced by alloying, such as /H92532 precipitate with
L12 structure in FeCoVNb alloys, result in a dramatic deteriora-
tion of soft magnetic properties of the alloys due to the pinning
effect [23,24,34]. This is quite different from the present FeCo-2V-
0.3Cr-0.2Mo alloy strengthened by magnetic bcc nanoprecipitates.
In addition, it is well known that the addition of alloying elements
(such as Cr, Mo, Nb et al.) into FeCo-2 V single-phase solid solu-
tion inevitably increases its coercivity [2]. However, the formation
of high-density magnetic bcc nanoprecipitates will absorb solution
elements Cr and Mo from matrix, and therefore relieves their harm-
ful effect on coercivity. From the above analysis, we obtain that
FeCo-based magnetic nanoprecipitates can be used to optimize the
mechanical properties and soft magnetic properties of FeCo-based
alloy at high temperatures.
4. Conclusion
The mechanical and soft magnetic properties of FeCo-2V-0.3Cr-
0.2Mo alloy at 600 ◦C have been studied, together with FeCo-2 V
for comparison. Results
 indicate that a good combination of high
tensile strength and ductility (ultimate tensile strength of 564 MPa
and elongation of ∼ 20 %, respectively) as well as low coercivity
(6.97 Oe) of FeCo-2V-0.3Cr-0.2Mo alloy can be obtained by appro-
priate annealing treatment at 800−850 ◦C, which can be attributed
to the formation of a large number of soft magnetic bcc nanoprecip-
itates. The nanoprecipitates can impede the dislocation motion and
suppress the brittle fracture, leading to a high tensile strength and
ductility. In addition, nanoprecipitates are mainly composed of Fe
and Co elements, indicating that they are soft magnetic phases. This
can relieve the deterioration in coercivity by decreasing the pinning
effect on the domain wall movement, which thus enables the FeCo-
2V-0.3Cr-0.2Mo alloy strengthened by magnetic nanoprecipitates
to have a low coercivity. This work provides a novel approach for
enhancing high-temperature strength and ductility of FeCo-based
soft magnetic alloy without a signiﬁcant increase in coercivity via
magnetic nanoprecipitates.
