1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs), also known as multi-principal element 
alloys, baseless alloys, concentrated solid solution alloys, or composi -
tionally complex alloys [ 1 – 8 ], have attracted tremendous attention in 
the past years. HEAs can be classified into different catalogs according to 
the crystal structure [ 9 – 15 ]. The original concept [ 16 , 17 ] of HEAs is to 
form solid solutions instead of intermetallic or complex phases during 
solidification by five or more principal elements due to the high mixing 
entropy effect [ 18 – 23 ]. With the development of HEAs, strategies such 
as adding minor alloying elements or thermal-mechanical treatments 
are applied to the HEAs for improved performance Some works [ 24 – 26 ] 
reveal a linear relationship between the yield strength and the carbon 
concentration ( < 1.10 at. %) in aged HEAs. 
Among the reported HEA systems [ 27 , 28 ], the Fe – Ni – Co based HEAs 
have attracted great interests for their potential to be an alternative to 
Ni-based superalloys. By attentively trailering the composition and heat 
treatment parameters [ 29 , 30 ], it is possible to control the type and 
content of strengthening phases, the HEAs could possess excellent 
high-temperature strength, superior fracture toughness, and high radi -
ation resistance. For example, Gwalani et al. [ 31 ] studied the precipi -
tation strengthening effect of the L1
2 
phase (Ni
3
Al) and B2 phase (NiAl) 
in the FCC-structured HEA system. Zhao et al. [ 32 ] found that the crystal 
structures of the Al
x
CrCo
2-x
FeNi HEA evolved from FCC + σ + disor -
dered BCC + ordered BCC structures to σ + disordered BCC + ordered 
BCC structures, and then to disordered BCC + ordered BCC structures 
with an increased molar ratio of Al:Co, where the Al
1.6
CrCo
0.4
FeNi alloy 
exhibited good wear resistance and good combinations of strength and 
ductility. In addition, it was proved that introducing the Cu element can 
promote the precipitation and stability of the L1
2

system, and proposed the interdependence between the L1
2 
phase and 
the copper-rich phase [ 33 ]. Even though the precipitation strengthening 
of HEAs has been reported, modified properties are not the sole reason 
for a wide range of applications. Industry is rather sensitive to the cost of 
raw materials.

that of Ni ($28,545 per tonne) (data from the London Metal Exchange 
( https://www.lme.com/ May 27, 2022)), the cost of equal atomic
