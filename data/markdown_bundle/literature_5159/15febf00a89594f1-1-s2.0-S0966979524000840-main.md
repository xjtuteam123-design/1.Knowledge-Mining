1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs), as multi-component alloys, have 
attracted extensive research interest due to their excellent mechanical, 
physical and chemical properties. Among them, single-phase face- 
centered cubic (FCC) HEAs (e.g. CoCrFeMnNi, CoCrFeNi HEAs) have 
received wide attention due to their excellent plasticity and good 
strength over a wide temperature range [ 1 – 3 ]. In particular, 
non-equiatomic single-phase FCC HEAs with the reduced stacking-fault 
energy (SFE) by modulating the alloy composition have been designed 
to further enhance the mechanical properties of the alloys [ 4 – 6 ]. A Low 
SFE contributes to the occurrence of mechanical twinning during plastic 
deformation of single-phase FCC HEAs. At room temperature, the SFE of 
CoCrFeMnNi and CoCrFeNi HEAs is between 27 and 30 mJ/m
2

plastic deformation at this temperature is accompanied by mechanical 
twinning. This can induce the FCC → hexagonal close-packed (HCP) 
martensitic phase transformation (the SFE < 15 mJ/m
2

reducing the SFE [ 7 – 12 ]. However, the complex composition of HEAs 
leads to a complex relationship between the SFE and the plastic defor -
mation mechanisms. Undoubtedly, the twinning-induced plasticity 
(TWIP) and transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) effects induced by 
the low-SFE are responsible for the good mechanical properties of 
non-equiatomic single-phase FCC HEAs [ 13 – 18 ].

3.2. TEM analysis 
Fig. 4 shows the TEM bright-field images and the selected area

of SFE at the low temperature also reduces the critical stress of me -
chanical twins, which further promotes the formation of mechanical 
twins. 
In addition, the motion of dislocations is also influenced by the SFE. 
At low temperature, the SFE of HEAs is low, and in HEAs with low SFE, a 
perfect dislocations may separate into a pair of shockley partials with a 
large separation distance (1/2[-110] → 1/6[-12-1] + 1/6[-211]) [ 55 ], 
which hinders the bunching of shockley pairs, which consequently in -
creases the difficulty of cross-slip [ 56 ], decreases the formation of 
dislocation tangles and dislocation cells, reduces the annihilation and 
rearrangement of dislocations, and causes dislocations to be retained 
( Fig. 4 (c)) and coordinate the plastic deformation in the form of planar 
slips. However, at room temperature, the SFE of the alloy is higher, and 
the dislocations are prone to cross-slip, showing an obvious wave-like 
dislocation slip characteristic, as shown in Fig. 4 (a).
