Abstract: A newly developed low Al containing magnesium alloy , Mg-2Zn-1Al 
-0.2Mn-0.2Ca-0.2Gd (wt.%) shows excellent mechanica l properties. 
 The alloys with 
superior high strength and considerable ductility ( the maximum of tensile strength 
reaches 332MPa and corresponding elongation is 23.1 %) are produced by 
electromagnetic casting and extrusion at relatively low temperatures (110 ˚C, 170 ˚C, 
210 ˚C, 250 ˚C) with an extrusion ratio of 17.4:1. Effects of extrusion temperature on 
the evolution of microstructure, mechanical propert ies and extruded texture are 
investigated by OM, SEM, TEM, and EBSD analysis. The microstructures of the alloys 
extruded at low temperatures mainly consist of the equiaxed dynamically 
recrystallized grains, elongated grains, and regions without dynamic recrystallization. 
And there are only equiaxed dynamically recrystalli zed grains in the alloy extruded at 
250˚C. The increasing extrusion temperature results in grain coarsening and strength 
reduction. When the extrusion temperatures are 110 ˚C and 170 ˚C, the average grain 
size reaches submicron level of 0.65mm and 0.89mm, respectively. Because the 
higher extrusion temperature makes the dynamically recrystallized grains rotate in a 
favorable direction, the basal texture enhances wit h the increase of extrusion 
temperature. The high strength is mainly attributed to the structure of extremely 
refined grains. High ductility benefits from a large amount of basal slip, activation of 
non-basal slip, and grain boundary movement under the action of tensile stress. 
Keywords ᧶ Mg-2Zn-1Al -0.2Mn-0.2Ca-0.2Gd alloy; Extrusion; DRX ; Extremely 
refined grain; Texture; Mechanical properties 
1. Introduction 
Magnesium has a low density of 1.74 g/cm 
3, which is much lower than that of 
conventional structural metallic materials, such as aluminum (2.70 g/cm 3) and steel 
(7.87 g/cm 3), thus, magnesium and its alloys are recognized as the next generation of 
light-weight metallic materials [1]. Moreover, Mg a lloys also possess good 
strength-to-weight ratio (specific strength) and ca st-ability. Nevertheless, the

application of magnesium and its alloys as structural materials is still limited. primarily 
because of their poor mechanical properties at room temperature, such as low 
ultimate tensile strength and ductility. Due to the HCP crystal structure of magnesium 
alloys, it is difficult to achieve the simultaneous improvement of strength and plasticity. 
It has been reported by Xu et al. [2] that the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of the alloy 
sheets rolled at 400 oC reached up to 373 MPa, but the elongation was onl y 6.4% in

deformation, the elongation increases from about 23% to about 48.8%, but the UTS is 
only 267.5MPa. 
In addition, due to the poor formability of magnesi um alloy, the yield of wrought 
magnesium alloy is low and the production cost of w rought magnesium alloy is 
increased. In order to improve the mechanical prope rties of magnesium alloys, 
researchers have made a lot of efforts. The additio n of rare-earth (RE) alloying 
elements (such as Gd, Ce, Y , etc.) to magnesium has been proved to be one of the 
potential ways to improve the formability and mechanical properties of magnesium [4–
9]. Wu et al. [10] have proved that with the addition of 1%Ca and 1%REs to AZ91, the 
ultimate tensile strength increased by 15.9%. Yu et al. [11] have researched the 
effects of Gd addition on microstructures and mecha nical properties of the high 
strain-rate rolled (HSRR) Mg–Zn–Zr alloys and concl uded that the Gd addition can 
reduce the stacking fault energy of the a-Mg matrix and thus promote the dynamic 
recrystallization (DRX) during the high strain rate rolling (HSRR) process. An 
improvement of formability in the extrusion process has been achieved by Bu et al. 
[12] according to add la and Gd to AZ80 due to the fact that many non-basal < a> 
dislocations and few pyramidal <c + a> dislocations could be activated to enhance the 
formability in the extrusion process. 
The solution strengthening and precipitation streng thening of magnesium alloys can 
be strengthened by adding Zn element, and it also c an improve the fluidity of Mg 
alloys [13–15]. The Mg-Zn-Al alloy with higher Zn c ontent than Al has been 
considered as a cheap creep resistant Mg alloy [16] . Coarse

generally considered to be detrimental to the plast ic deformation of Mg alloys. As the 
addition of Ca in the Mg-Al-Zn alloy system will pr eferentially combine with Al to form 
Al 2Ca, so the production of β-Mg 17 Al 12 phase can be inhibited by reducing the content 
of Al and adding Ca element properly [16]. And the alkaline-earth metal Ca is a

many advantages compared with the conventional cast ing techniques, such as the 
refinement, uniformity of as cast microstructure, a nd the reduction of element

Mg-2Zn-1Al alloy ingot. Firstly, pure Mg, Zn, and Al ingots were put into the resistance

temperature is about 700° C, Mg-30 wt.% Ca master alloy is added. And when the melt 
is cooled to 680 ~700° C, electromagnetic semi-conti nuous casting is carried out 
under the protection with a mixed gas atmosphere of CO 2 and SF 6 (CO 2:SF 6 = 99:1,

4. Discussion 
4.1. Extrudability of the Mg -2Zn-1Al alloy 
Due to the difficulty of forming, magnesium alloys are normally extruded at 
300~450 ° C [28]. However, it is difficult to improv e the mechanical properties of Mg 
alloys due to the grain growth caused by high extru sion temperature. In the present 
study, 
 Mg -2Zn-1Al alloy exhibits a good extrudability at relatively low 
temperatures( ≤250° C), and the extruded bars with smooth surface were obtained . 
Because of the decrease of Al content, there is no brittle phase Mg 17 Al 12 in the alloy, 
but Al 2Ca phase formed by the combination of Al and Ca ele ments. This reduces the 
possibility of forming crack sources in Mg alloys d uring deformation. On the other 
hand, due to a small amount of alloying elements ad dition, there are a small quantity 
of secondary phase particle in the alloy, and the h indrance of dislocation motion can 
be reduced. Moreover, this may also be related to t he large initial grain size. The 
larger grain size reduces the number of grain bound aries. The decrease of grain

boundaries makes the hindrance of dislocation movem ent decreases. In the early 
stage of extrusion, the dislocation can move relati vely freely and the stress 
concentration caused by dislocation agglomeration i s reduced. A research by Koike's 
[29] report indicated that when the grain size is v ery small the deformation is 
contributed to not only the basal slip system, but also the slip system on the prismatic 
planes or even the pyramidal planes slip system. In the present study, extremely fine 
DRXed grains are gradually produced during extrusio n, and under the action of 
extrusion force cylindrical slip system and pyramidal slip system will be activated. The 
start-up of cylindrical slip system and pyramidal s lip system will coordinate the 
deformation ability of the alloy. Therefore, the ex cellent extrudability of Mg-2Zn-1Al 
alloy at low temperature can be attributed to the above reasons. 
4.2. Effect of extrusion temperatures on the microstructure of Mg-2Zn-1Al alloy 
Elemental composition, processing methods and processing conditions of magnesium 
alloys will affect their microstructures and mechanical properties. Reducing Al content 
can reduce the formation of Mg 17 Al 12 phase in the alloy, which can cause cracking of 
metal during deformation and affect the forming pro perties of the material. In the 
present study, a new low Al containing Mg-2Zn-1Al a lloy was prepared by 
electromagnetic casting. However, the as-cast alloy contains large grains with size of 
1000-3000mm. During the extrusion processing, the large grains would deform greatly 
and be elongated along the ED, thus generate large stress in the grains. The stress 
and extrusion temperature would lead to DRX along t he grain boundary of these 
elongated grains. In addition, there are some non-D RXed regions due to the large 
deformation resistance and non-uniform deformation at lower temperatures. With the 
increase of extrusion temperature, the area of non- DRX regions decreases. When 
extrusion temperature is 250 ˚C, the alloy exhibits a fully DRXed microstructure. 
MgZn 2 particles are not detected after extrusion, which indicate that MgZn 2 particles 
are dissolved and absorbed by matrix during extrusi on. Moreover, the Al 2Ca phases 
are crushed and dispersive dispersed in the alloys after extrusion at 110˚C (see 
Fig.7a). However, the Al 2Ca phases are mainly distributed along the ED after 
extrusion at 170˚C, 210 ˚C and 250˚C (see Fig.7b, c , d). the grains are obviously 
refined. Especially, the average grain size reaches 0.65mm and 0.89mm 
corresponding to the extrusion temperatures of 110 ˚C and 170 ˚C, respectively. When 
the extrusion temperatures are 210 ˚C and 250 ˚C, the average grain sizes are 1.4 µm 
and 2.3 µm, respectively. The extremely fine grain structure s were caused by low 
extrusion temperature and good extrudability. In general, the DRXed gain size d ( µm)

4.3. Texture evolution 
The fiber texture with (0002) planes parallel to th e ED is a typical texture in Mg alloys 
after extrusion [33]. However, the effect of extrus ion temperature on the texture 
strength is different. It has been reported by Du t hat the Mg-4.5Zn-1.1Ca alloy 
exhibited a stronger basal texture when extruded at lower temperatures due to the 
lower volume fraction of the DRXed region, because the DRXed grains may exhibit a 
weaker texture, as compared to that associated with parent grains [34]. But the result 
is the opposite in Huang's [17] research on extrude AZX912 magnesium alloy at 
different temperatures. Huang's research shows that the extrusion texture, with the 
basal plane and <10

remarkably by increasing the extrusion temperature. And from huang's point of view, 
this result may be attributed to the enhanced activ ity of the prismatic slip at relatively 
high temperatures. In the present study, the maximu m intensity of basal texture 
increases with the increase of extrusion temperature. Because the DRX occurs during 
the extrusion process, and the DRXed grains are sma ller at relatively low 
temperatures, there are more grains in alloy of the same volume, and the orientation 
of these new nucleated DRX grains in the alloy is random. So, the basal texture of the 
bar extruded at low temperatures is weak. In additi on, when extruded at higher

gradually enhanced. With the increase of extrusion temperature, it becomes easier for 
the grains to rotate parallel to the direction of E D. Therefore, With the increase of 
extrusion temperature, the basal texture becomes stronger. 
In addition, the Al 2Ca phase distributed in the martin after extrusion may also affect

distribution are detected in matrix. And the disper sed Al 2Ca particles provide new 
nucleation sites for DRX grains with random orienta tion. With the increase of 
extrusion temperature, Al 2Ca particles are distributed along the ED, and the Al 2Ca 
particles with directional distribution may weaken the PSN effect. Therefore, the basal 
texture of the extruded alloy at 110° C is the weakest.
