ABSTRACT 
The failure mode in the very high cycle fatigue (VHCF) regime of four different nickel-based single-crystal (SX) 
superalloys coated with a slurry aluminide has been investigated at high temperature (1,000 
◦
C) and under fully- 
reversed conditions (R
ε 
= -1). The 3rd generation superalloy CMSX-4 Plus presented a lifetime one to two order 
(s) of magnitude higher than AM1, CMSX-4 and Rene N5 for the same applied alternating stress (180 MPa). The 
crack initiation zones were distinct. CMSX-4 Plus samples failed from the interdiffusion zone (IDZ) while AM1, 
CMSX-4 and Rene N5 samples failed from casting pores close to the IDZ. The interaction between the casting 
pore, the IDZ and the rough zone is believed to control the premature crack initiation. A new crack initiation 
mode in VHCF for coated specimens is proposed in this article and the competition between crack initiation from 
a casting pore or from the surface is considered and discussed. 
1. Introduction 
Recent environmental considerations lead to the development of 
new aero-engines architectures capable to work at higher temperatures 
to minimize CO
2 
and NO
x 
emissions. However, due to the materials 
limitations, airfoil coatings are mandatory to limit oxidation and 
corrosion degradations [1 – 3] . To decrease production and maintenance 
costs, a faster and low-cost coating of high-pressure turbine blades and 
vanes turns out to be an advantage for industrial refurbishment pro -
cesses [4 – 9] . The development of specific deposition processes for 
advanced corrosion and oxidation resistant coatings is hence necessary. 
While coatings can be used for new components, the coating adopted 
in this study was initially developed by the LaSIE laboratory (France) for 
refurbishment purposes using a slurry route. It consists of a mixture of 
aluminum microparticles in suspension in a binder (water + polyvinyl 
alcohol) deposited at the surface of the component/specimen to be 
protected and then heat treated to obtain the coating. This technique 
aims to accelerate the coating process by exothermic reactions between 
Al and Ni occurring during heat treatments and the in-situ formation of a 
protective layer of α -Al
2
O
3 
on top of the internal Al-rich diffusion 
coating of β -NiAl type [10 – 13] . 
The interdiffusion zone (IDZ) between the aluminide coatings and 
the superalloy substrate is a sensitive subject, as it may result in the 
formation of secondary reaction zones (SRZ) [8,9,14 – 17] and topolog -
ically close-packed phases (TCP) [18,19] known to be possibly detri -
mental to mechanical properties. The substrate chemical composition 
can locally influence the diffusion of elements and the development of 
precipitates. The impact of the coating composition on the IDZ/SRZ 
microstructure and mechanical properties has been investigated in 
different works. For instance, the fatigue life of several coated superal -
loys is lowered when compared to the bare superalloys results [20 – 22] . 
Such a lower fatigue life is related to the formation of an interfacial zone 
with less thermal stability than the superalloy, and to the process-related 
residual stress below the surface resulting in a local recrystallization 
(RX) [23] . Recrystallization of a Ni-based SX superalloys is always 
critical from a mechanical point of view by nucleating intergranular 
cracks, especially under high temperatures and cycling loading [24] . 
Very few studies have been focused on the role of a bond-coat on the 
HCF life at high temperature of Ni-based SX superalloys. We may only

2.2. Substrate preparation and coating deposition 
The VHCF specimens were extracted by electric discharge machining 
and subsequent turning from the SX rods. All machining steps were 
performed at Institut Pprime. The specimens were mechanically pol -
ished removing 0.2 mm from the original diameter with an abrasive SiC 
paper to remove residual stresses from the machining. They were ground 
with SiC P180 to decrease the surface residual stresses magnitude 
inherited from turning steps and increase the surface roughness to 
enable the contact between the substrate and the slurry. The metallic 
coating deposition via slurry-route method is described below.

ticles with 3 ––– 5 μ m diameter, and PVA (PolyvinylAlcohol) dissolved in 
water as a binder. The slurry was mixed, sprayed with an aerograph over 
the specimen ’ s gauge length and let dry for one hour to decrease the 
excess of water in the fresh slurry. As proposed in [10] , the diffusion

chosen to cover different states encountered in engine components and

The tests were performed with an induction coil as a heating source. 
The power was controlled by a closed loop with a dual-wavelength py -
rometer measuring the samples ’ central temperature. Before loading, 
the samples were heated for 70 min to stabilize the temperature profile 
and obtain a stable oxide at their surface. The stabilization of the oxide 
scale during this soak time before starting VHCF tests ensures a constant 
emissivity ratio during the fatigue test. 
2.4. Microstructure and failure characterizations 
After mechanical testing, fractographic observations were performed 
using a JEOL JSM-7000F operating at 20 or 25 kV, using the secondary 
(SEI) and backscattered (BSE) electron imaging modes. Observation of 
the γ / γ ′ microstructure evolution in the IDZ after the fatigue tests was 
also performed with longitudinal cross sections previously polished up 
to a mirror finish. 
The energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS) analyses were conducted 
using un-etched samples and an EDAX detector coupled to a FEI Quanta 
200F SEM at 20 kV. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) analyses 
were performed to characterize the crystallographic orientation around 
an internal crack initiation site. EBSD was performed using a JEOL JSM- 
6100 SEM, operating at 25 kV, and the orientation imaging microscopy 
(OIM) software EDAX, version 6.1. The specimens were prepared by 
standard metallographic techniques, and final polishing was made with 
a colloidal silica solution (particle size 0.04 μ m).

IDZ of the diffusion coatings. Therefore, the following discussion is 
divided in two main parts, the first part being dedicated to the compe -
tition in crack initiation between internal (casting) pores and the 
recrystallized layer below the coating. The second part is an analysis of 
the interaction between pores close to the surface and the IDZ.

Fig. 11 illustrates the damage mechanism proposed based on this 
section ’ s observations and analyses. Fig. 11 a represents a longitudinal 
section of a Ni-based SX superalloy NiAl-coated. First, the interaction 
between a (small) casting pore or an homogenization pore close to the 
interface of the substrate/IDZ and a larger casting pore inside the sam -
ple ’ s substrate (i.e., far from the IDZ interface) occurs. Simultaneously, 
the NiAl coating develops cracks during the very first cycles up to the 
IDZ ( Fig. 11 b ) that are arrested at the IDZ/SX substrate interface before 
propagating into the superalloy bulk. Thereafter, oxidation occurs and 
consume the main chemical elements of the γ’ phase, like Al and Ti at the 
substrate/IDZ interface. In such a situation, the small pore is now 
located in a chemically and mechanically (i.e., γ’ dissolution) unstable 
zone, while, simultaneously, slip bands accumulate in the vicinity of the 
casting pores along the favorable slip system [30] ( Fig. 11 c ). 
Then, an earlier development of the rough zone in the vicinity of the 
pore close to the IDZ is obtained compared to the largest internal casting 
pores because of the evolution of the local microstructure and dissolu -
tion of the γ’ phase due to oxidation at the IDZ/SX interface ( Fig. 11 d ). 
Moreover, the local lattice rotation below the IDZ/SRZ extending over 
150 µ m within the substrate (see Fig. 2 ) will clearly induce a higher 
Young ’ s modulus along the loading direction [53] and hence, a higher 
local stress magnitude close to pores located within this area compared 
to if no rotation would be present, like in the bulk, far from the coating. 
Indeed, it is worth recalling that our VHCF tests are conducted in strain- 
controlled mode and a variation in local Young ’ s modulus directly af -
fects at the local stress state. Finally, the crack propagates from the 
smaller pore through the rough zone due to the degradation of the 
microstructure, leading to the sample ’ s failure ( Fig. 11 e ). Consequently, 
it results in a lifetime of at least one order of magnitude lower than the 
one obtained for uncoated specimens (see Fig. 3 ). The microstructure 
evolutions within the IDZ are hence favoring the development of a rough 
zone at a pore smaller than the one that would have led to failure 
without any coating. In summary, with such a coating and in these fa -
tigue conditions, not only internal large casting pore are critical to the 
VHCF as observed in several previous publications 
[29 – 31,33,36,37,42,54,55] , but also smaller ones, possibly including 
homogenization pores. 
5. Conclusions 
The VHCF life of four different Ni-based single crystal superalloys 
(AM1, Rene N5, CMSX-4 and CMSX-4 Plus) coated with a slurry-type 
NiAl bond coat has been investigated in fully reversed conditions at 
1000 
◦
C. From the present work, the following main conclusions can be 
established: 
• The similar VHCF life of coated AM1, Rene N5 and CMSX-4 alloys is 
controlled by an accelerated rough zone development in the vicinity 
of a critical pore in close interaction with the IDZ. The specific 
chemistry of the IDZ accelerates the development of the rough zone. 
• The better VHCF life of coated CMSX-4 Plus compared to all three 
other systems results from a locally higher Al content at the coating/ 
substrate interface, slowing down the propagation of the crack to -
ward the substrate. 
• Sub-surface cracks assisted by oxidation initiate in the very first cy -
cles. Therefore, most of the VHCF lifetime is spent in crack propa -
gation from the surface, which can be delayed by features like 
secondary reaction zone.
