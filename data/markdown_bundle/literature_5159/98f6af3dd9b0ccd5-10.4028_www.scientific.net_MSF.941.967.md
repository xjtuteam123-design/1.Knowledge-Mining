to Their Chemical Compositions

Keywords: Ni superalloy, computational design, composition, microstructure evolution 
Abstract. The degradation of creep resistance in Nickel -based single crystal superalloys is 
essentially ascribed to their microstructure evolutio n. Yet there is a lack of work that manages to 
simulate the effect of alloying element concentrations on microstructure degradation. In this 
research, a computational model is developed to connect the rafting kinetics of Ni superalloys with 
their chemical composition , by combining thermodynamics calculation and an energy-based 
microstructure model. The isotropic coarsening rate and γ/γ ′ misfit stresses have been selected as 
composition related parameter, and the effect of service temperat ure, time and applied stress are 
also taken into consideration to simulate the evolutions of microstructure parameters during creep 
process. The different generations of commercial Ni superalloys are selected and their chemical 
compositions are calculated based on this model. T he simulated microstructure parameters are 
validated by the results from experimental results and the existing analytical model . The capability 
of the model in predicting the microstructure characteristics may provide instructional thought in 
developing a novel computational guided design approach in Ni superalloys. 
Introduction 
The outstanding high -temperature mechanical performance of Nickel based single crystal 
superalloys, in particular their superior creep resistance is what predominantly makes them 
promising candidates as turbine blades in aero engines [1]. Their interesting properties are largely 
dictated by the high-volume fraction (up to 70%) of ordered, L1 2 structured γ′ precipitates, which 
are coherently embedded in a solid solution γ matrix (FCC structure). During the high temperature 
creep process above 900C, the degradation of creep properties of SC alloys also strongly depends 
on the microstructure evolution. Specifically , in the early stage of creep, t he initially adjacent 
cuboidal γ′ particles get coalescent and turn into plate- like structures which are normal to the stress 
direction. The platelets of γ and γ′ phases are stacked alternately in the microstructure and is called 
the rafted structure. The lamellar structure maintains stable and essentially eliminates the 
dislocation bypassing mechanism, thence guarantees a low creep rate during the second creep st age. 
In the later stage of creep, the rafted structure is found to be collapse, resulting in decreasing the 
creep strength. Namely the creep kinetics can be strongly correlated to the microstructure evolution, 
and it is of great importance to identify the main evolution mechanisms and to incorporate their 
effects as a function of service conditions. 
Since the early 1990s, a large number of microstructure evolution models for single crystalline 
superalloys have been derived. Some models focus on the dynamics of dislocation behaviour during 
the creep process, which present the balance within dislocation multiplication and interaction with 
obstacles, and dislocation annihilation and recovery[2, 3] . Some other models put the emphasis on 
the driving force in the microstructure evolution, the energy based anisotropic coarsening of 
precipitates[4, 5]. The microstructure characteristics for Ni superalloys are generally determined by 
three factors: γ′ precipitate volume fraction, isotropic coarsening and directional coarsening, also

As mentioned before, the creep kinetics can be significantly correlated to the process of 
microstructure evolution. However, there is a lack of work that manages to s imulate the effect of 
alloying element concentrations on microstructure degradation, due to the large number of 
interactions between the alloying elements, and the multi effecting factors such as service 
temperature, time and applied stress that need to be evaluated. In principle, theoretical analysis and 
computer modelling tools might conceivably provide a promising method to solve this problems [7, 
8]. In this paper a novel computational design model is developed based on the characteristics of 
microstructure development during creep process , by combining the thermodynamics calculation 
and an energy- based microstructure model . The microstructure features are linked to specific 
composition through calculating the isotropic coarsening rate and misfit stress . In order to valid the 
model, the microstructure evoluti ons of existing Ni grades are calculated with the same 
methodology, and the simulation results have been confirmed by the experimental results from 
literatures. Specifically, the situation in this work corresponds to the coalescence of cubic 
γ′ precipitates at elevated service temperature (above 900C), leading to the lamellar structure that 
perpendicular to one of the crystal axes, as usually observed under uniaxial tensile stress. Other 
loading types (uniaxial compression, multiaxial stress and so on) are beyond the scope of this work.

The Larson -Miller parameter is generally presented as the resistance of creep deformation for 
alloys, where t r is the creep rupture life and T is temperature . In Figure 1, the performance of 
abovementioned Ni commercial grades is compared. The results show that the shift towards the 
right as one moves from SRR99, CMSX -4 to TMS-196 has arisen with increasing creep resistance. 
The CMSX alloys and TMS alloys can outperform the first generation SRR99 at whole stress range, 
while there exist some intersections of Larson- Miller diagram s among 2 nd to 5 th generations, 
especially at the high stress levels. The results indicate that after the development of 2 nd generation 
superalloys, novel superalloys perform closely to the old ones. Within the range with high stress 
level, the rafting process is not remarkably significant , where CMSX alloys have better creep 
deformation resistance compared to TMS -138 alloy. At low stress and at high temperature range, 
these improvements in creep resistance turn out to be very substantial , since the development of 
novel superalloys more focuses on improving the high-temperature capability[1]. 
Predictions and comparison 
The isotropic coarsening rates of Ni commercial grades as a function of service temperature are 
calculated based on equation (9) and the results are shown in Figure 2(a). It can be found that the 
coarsening rate decreases substantially with the updating of new Ni generations. The results are 
reasonable since increasing amount of refractory elements, such as W, Mo, Ta, Re, and Ru, have 
been added as alloying elements in new generation alloys, as shown in Figure 2(b), which can 
significantly retard the diffusion kinetics and correspondingly slow down the coarsening rates. The 
coarsening rate of 1 st generation superalloy SRR99 presents a comparably higher value of 
coarsening rate at the whole temperature range, and the differences increase exceedingly as the 
temperature rises. The results on one hand have a good agreement with the research that the 
addition of alloy element Re can effectively hinder the diffusion process and develop the high 
temperature capability[14]. On the other hand, the result suits well with the Larson- Miller diagram 
in Figure 1 that all other superalloy grades can outperform SRR99 alloy.

When determining the time to reach fully rafted microstructure , the typical way is based on the 
observation of microstructure parameter, i.e., the time for γ channel to reach fully rafted width . 
Epishin[18] has studied the rafting kinetics of CMSX -4 alloy and developed a fitting equation in 
describing the coarsen ing rate of γ channel width as a function of time, temperature and stress. 
However, t he difficulties exist in obtaining the in -situ experimental microstructure results during 
the creep experiments, since huge amount of metallographic observations should be made to 
monitor the changes in dimensions . A more simple but vague wa y in defining rafting time is based 
on the strain-time creep curves. The fully rafted time is set as the point to reach end of the primary 
creep stage, where the lamellar structure has formed and the dynamic equilibrium between creep 
hardening and creep soft ening is reached for the subsequent creep stable stage. In the absence of 
systematic microstructure observation in different Ni superalloy commercial grades, here the 
experimental time to obtain a fully rafted microstructure is evaluated by the second approach, as 
shown by unhashed columns in Figure 6: the calculated values are based on the fully rafted channel 
width, which is shown as dashed columns . The comparison show s that the calculated results can 
well predict the experimental microstructure parameter. Based on capability of this model in 
predicting the microstructure characteristics, it would be possible to optimize the chemical 
composition of Ni superalloys through optimizing the microstructure characteristics. This method 
provides the feasibility in developing a novel computational guided design approach in Ni 
superalloys, which will be the main emphasis of our work in the next step. 
Conclusion 
1. A new computational model for Ni based single crystal superalloys has been built by 
combining the thermodynamics calculation and energy- based microstructure model. The 
chemical compositions are linked to the microstructure parameters when taken the service 
temperature, time and applied stress into consideration. 
2. By empl oying the model, the creep deformation resistance obtained in Larson- Miller 
diagram can be well reflected by t he calculated isotropic coarsening rates of Ni commercial 
grades, while the simulated γ channel width of CMSX-4 alloy are validated by the analytical 
results from Fedelich’s experimental work at high temperature and low stress range. 
3. The time to fully rafted channel width of Ni commercial grades can be well predicted by the 
simulated results from our model. The capability model in predicting the microstructure 
characteristics, which provides the feasibility in developing a novel computational guided 
design approach in Ni superalloys.
