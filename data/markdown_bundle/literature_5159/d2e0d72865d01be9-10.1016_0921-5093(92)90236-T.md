Alloy modification of y-base titanium aluminide for improved oxidation 
resistance, creep strength and fracture toughness 
Seishi Tsuyama, Shinji Mitao and Kuni-nori Minakawa 
Advanced Technology Research Center, NKK Corporation 1-I, Minamiwatarida-cho, Kawasaki-ku, Kawasaki 210 
(Japan) 
Abstract 
An attempt to improve the properties of as-cast y-base titanium aluminides, such as fracture toughness, creep strength 
and oxidation resistance, from low to high temperatures was carried out through microstructural control and alloy modifi- 
cation. Addition of a small amount of silicon from 0.5 to 1% to the TiA1 alloy greatly improved creep strength over binary 
alloys. However, it was revealed that an excess of silicon was harmful not only to creep strength but also to fracture tough- 
ness. Oxidation resistance was dramatically improved by niobium addition. As for fracture toughness improvement, 
optimization of aluminum content and resultant microstruetural control were also important. The Ti-AI-Nb-Si quarter- 
nary alloy with microstructural features of full a2/y lamellae with serated lamellar grain boundaries offered comparable 
oxidation resistance and excellent specific creep strength around 1200 K over the nickel-base superalloy and had a high 
fracture toughness value of 32.5 MPa m ~/2 at room temperature. 
1. Introduction 
The y-base (TiAl) titanium aluminide is a candidate 
material for high temperature structural applications 
around 1200 K because of its high strength-to-density 
ratio [1, 2]. However, poor ductility from low to inter- 
mediate temperatures has prevented this material from 
being used in practical applications. Many fundamental 
studies have been carried out to try to understand the 
relationships between the metallurgical factors and the 
mechanical properties. For the binary y-alloy, it has 
been reported that the greatest tensile ductility at room 
temperature as well as the best creep resistance at 
elevated temperatures is obtained for the alloy contain- 
ing about 48% aluminum [3] (unless otherwise stated, 
compositions will be given in at.%). Many factors 
responsible for this improvement in the properties 
have been pointed out, such as the scavenging of inter- 
stitials by the a2 (Ti3AI) phase [4]. However, numerous 
studies of alloying addition to y alloys have been per- 
formed in recent years, and it has been reported that 
addition of manganese [5] or chromium [6] improves 
room temperature ductility. However, it has also been 
reported that the addition of manganese or chromium 
reduces high temperature properties such as oxidation 
resistance [7]. This suggests that the total improvement 
in properties from low to high temperatures is difficult 
to achieve. 
This paper reports an improvement in properties of 
the y-base titanium aluminide from low to high tem- 
peratures by microstructural control and alloy modifi- 
cation of Si-Nb addition and metallurgical factors 
responsible for the changes in properties are discussed. 
2. Experimental procedures 
Binary y alloys with various aluminum contents 
ranging from 44 to 51%, ternary 48% A1 base alloys 
containing silicon ranging from 0.5 to 3% or niobium 
ranging from 0.5 to 1.7%, and quaternary y-base 
alloys, Ti-AI-Nb-Si, were prepared in 410 g button 
ingots by non-consumable electrode arc melting in an 
argon atmosphere. The levels of impurities (in p.p.m. 
wt.) for all the alloys were as follows: 300 O, 50 C, 
60 N, 10 H. Vickers hardness, fracture toughness, 
creep properties and oxidation resistance of as-cast 
alloys were examined in this study. Fracture toughness 
tests were conducted in accordance with ASTM Stand- 
ard E399 using 1/2T compact type specimens, 12.7 
mm in thickness and 25.4 mm in width, at room tem- 
perature. Creep rupture tests were carried out using 
round bar tensile specimens, 6 mm in diameter and 
30 mm in gage length, in the temperature range 
1033-1273 K. Oxidation resistance was evaluated by 
measuring the depth of the oxidized layer of small 
square plate specimens 2 mm thick after the specimens 
were exposed to the air at 1173 K for 500 h.

3.1. Microstructural control for fracture toughness

can be roughly classified into three groups. (1) Full

equiaxed y grains in the alloys with 50-51% Al [8]. In

increased with a decrease in aluminum content, the 
Ti-48Al alloy corresponding to the second type of 
microstructure exhibited the highest fracture toughness 
value of 30 MPa m 1/2 as shown in Fig. 1. This exceed-

3.3. Niobium addition for oxidation resistance 
Niobium addition drastically enhanced oxidation 
resistance of the Ti-48%AI base alloys, particularly 
over 1% as shown in Fig. 5. 
Figure 6 shows the depth profile of titanium, 
aluminum and niobium in the oxide scale as measured 
by an electron probe micro-analyzer (EPMA) for the 
binary alloy and the 1.65% Nb bearing alloy after 
exposure at 1173 K for 200 h in the air. Although 
the scale thickness of the niobium bearing alloy is much 
smaller than that of the binary alloy, the basic composi- 
tion of the oxide layers appears to be similar in both 
samples, as reported in the previous literature [12], that 
is to say, TiOz(surface)/AlzO3+TiO2/Ti3A1/matrix. 
It should be noted that the distribution of niobium 
shows a similar profile to that of aluminum, but shows 
an opposite trend to that of titanium in the scale. This 
indicates that niobium tends to be absorbed in the 
AI203 oxide and stabilizes the dense AI203 layer in the 
scale. This stable AI203 layer is considered to interrupt 
the growth of the TiO 2 oxide of the surface layer result- 
ing in the enhancement in oxidation resistance. 
Niobium addition up to 1.7% did not significantly 
affect the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
the Ti-48AI base alloy.

4. Conclusions 
A new ),-base titanium aluminide, Ti-AI-Si-Nb, 
with improved fracture toughness, creep strength and 
oxidation resistance has been developed, and the 
following conclusions can be drawn. 
(1) Microstructural control to full a2/), lamellae 
with serrated lamellar grain boundaries is indispens- 
able for an improvement in fracture toughness. 
(2) The addition of a small amount of silicon, up to 
approximately 1%, is effective for an enhancement in 
creep resistance without much deterioration in fracture 
toughness. 
(3) Niobium addition over 1% greatly enhances the 
oxidation resistance. 
(4) The newly developed Ti-AI-Nb-Si quaternary 
y alloy exhibited comparable oxidation resistance and 
superior specific creep strength up to approximately 
1200 K to the nickel base superalloy and had a suffi- 
ciently high fracture toughness of 32.5 MPa m 1/2 at 
room temperature.
