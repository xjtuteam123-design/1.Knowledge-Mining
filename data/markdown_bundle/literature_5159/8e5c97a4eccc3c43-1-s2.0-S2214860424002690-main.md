ABSTRACT 
Cryogenic tensile behaviors of carbon-doped CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy (C-HEA) printed by laser powder 
bed fusion (LPBF) were systematically explored. The LPBFed C-HEA exhibits excellent cryogenic tensile prop -
erties with not only high yield strength but also largely extended elongation as compared to those under room 
temperature deformation. In particular, the elongation of the C-HEA is twice as high at 77 K compared to 298 K. 
The strain hardening rate of LPBFed C-HEA under cryogenic deformation is much higher than that under plastic 
deformation at the room temperature, which contributes to the dramatic enhancement of uniform elongation by 
delaying plastic instability. Because the flow stress of C-HEA is significantly increased by temperature decrease, it 
can exceed the critical twinning stress at the early-stage deformation at 77 K. Deformation twins and nano- 
carbides synergistically contribute to the high back stress evolution of the C-HEA under cryogenic tensile 
deformation. This study can provide a new perspective on developing high-performance alloys for use in additive 
manufacturing in cryogenic applications. 
1. Introduction 
Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF), the most famous metal additive 
manufacturing technique, has led a global manufacturing trend for the 
past decade based on its unique advantages for producing complex- 
shaped metallic parts [1 – 5] . Using the LPBF technique, a 
three-dimensional (3D) product designed by computer-aided design 
(CAD) data can be directly printed by stacking a thin layer of metallic 
powder with a high-energy laser beam [6 – 10] . Based on the unique 
characteristic of LPBF, topology-optimized products can be produced for 
the weight-reduction, enhanced performance, and user-customization 
demanded by various industrial fields (e.g., aerospace, automobile, 
and medical/dental) [11 – 13] . Furthermore, recent studies have shown 
that the extreme thermal cycle during LPBF allows unique microstruc -
tures to be generated (i.e., heterogeneous anisotropic grain structure, 
nano-sized cell structure with dislocation network, and solute 
segregation), resulting in improved strength and ductility compared to 
wrought counterparts [8,14,15] . Therefore, the LPBF process can 
expand the microstructural variables to control the mechanical prop -
erties of metallic materials compared to the traditional 
thermo-mechanical process [8,16] . Therefore, numerous studies have 
deeply investigated the microstructure evolution and resulting me -
chanical properties of the materials processed by the LPBF to tailor the 
mechanical properties of high-performance products [17,18] . 
Recent studies have focused on the utilization of multi-principal 
element alloys, known as high-entropy alloys (HEAs), with no single 
dominant element in contrast to traditional alloys, for the LPBF process 
[19,20] . Beyond the unique merits of HEAs exhibiting multifunctional 
performance, the exploration of microstructure and mechanistic insights 
of the LPBFed HEAs have not only expanded the choice of materials for 
the LPBF but also brought about broad implications for the development 
of alloys with exceptional mechanical properties [21 – 24] . In particular,

CoCrFeMnNi HEA, which is the most famous HEA with a single 
face-centered cubic (FCC) structure, has been intensively investigated 
over the past decade regarding the relationship between microstructure 
and mechanical properties of the LPBFed HEAs [20,25 – 30] . Because 
CoCrFeMnNi HEAs exhibit simple microstructures and good printability 
in the LPBF, the exploration of underlying mechanisms of microstruc -
ture evolution in the processed material during the LPBF can help in 
avoiding factors such as macroscopic defects, multiple phase, and 
martensitic transformation [20,27] . Moreover, because the alloys 
consist of substitutional elements, the fundamental study of the effect of 
interstitial atoms in the LPBFed HEAs can be facilitated. The addition of 
a small amount of carbon allows superior mechanical properties of the 
LPBFed CoCrFeMnNi HEAs via the formation of nano-sized carbide 
finely distributed at the cell boundaries [25] . 
Meanwhile, it has been widely reported that CoCrFeMnNi HEAs 
exhibit excellent cryogenic mechanical properties [30] . While most al -
loys exhibit a significant drop in ductility and toughness when 
decreasing the temperature to cryogenic levels, the CoCrFeMnNi alloy 
exhibits a significantly enhanced strength-ductility combination without 
compromising toughness at liquid nitrogen temperature [31] . In 
particular, the cryogenic fracture toughness of CoCrFeMnNi HEAs is 
almost unrivaled in alloys (e.g., cryogenic steels, nickel alloys, 
aluminum alloys, etc.) reported to date [30,32] . Therefore, this novel 
alloy is highly expected to be used in a range of cryogenic applications 
such as structural materials for the long-distance transportation of 
cryogenic liquefied gas or space exploration [33] . 
On the other hand, because the aerodynamics and weight-optimized 
shape of parts is usually essential for aerospace applications, the LPBF 
process has been attracting considerable attention as a key 
manufacturing technique for producing structural parts used in aero -
space [34] . Moreover, exploring structural materials having good 
cryogenic toughness is essential for advanced rockets and spacecraft that 
need to operate in the frigid temperatures of distant planets. Further -
more, the spacecraft should be capable of serve landing on frozen comets 
and drilling into the regolith for planetary exploration [35] . For 
example, 300 series austenitic stainless steels (e.g., 304 L or 316 L 
stainless steel), the most widely used alloys for cryogenic applications, 
usually exhibit 60% lower toughness at cryogenic temperature than that 
at room temperature [33] . Therefore, the CoCrFeMnNi HEA exhibiting 
not only good LPBF printability but also excellent cryogenic perfor -
mance can be regarded as a potential candidate material for additive 
manufacturing of the structural parts (e.g., engine parts for liquid rocket, 
robot parts for spacecraft) used in future planetary exploration [35,36] . 
However, despite the irreplaceable merits of LPBFed CoCrFeMnNi 
HEAs for cryogenic applications, the cryogenic tensile behavior of them 
has scarcely been investigated. Recent publications [37,38] reported 
that the strength levels of the LPBFed CoCrFeMnNi alloys was signifi -
cantly increased, but ductility nearly maintains when decreasing the 
temperature from 298 K to 77 K. Furthermore, while carbon-doped 
CoCrFeMnNi HEAs printed by LPBF exhibit excellent mechanical prop -
erties at room temperature [25] , the cryogenic tensile properties of them 
have been studied even less. In this study, carbon-doped CoCrFeMnNi 
HEA (hereafter, referred to as C-HEA) was printed by the LPBF, and 
tensile testing was performed on printed samples at 298 and 77 K. The 
tensile properties of the samples were characterized, and the deforma -
tion mechanism was systematically investigated with microstructure 
analysis in various length scales.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Hierarchical heterogeneous microstructure of as-printed C-HEA 
Fig. 1 shows the initial microstructure of the LPBFed C-HEA. Note 
that the EBSD maps were obtained from the middle region of each plane 
in three orthogonal directions of the as-printed samples. As shown in 
Fig. 1 (a), the morphological texture of the grain structure can be 
observed, which is related to the repeated melting/solidification along 
the laser trajectory during LPBF [42,44] . The heterogeneous anisotropic 
grain structure affects the tensile properties of the LPBFed sample by 
promoting strain gradient during plastic deformation [45,46] . The 
average grain size was estimated to be 60.1 μ m from the XY plane of the 
sample (for detailed information, see Supplementary Fig. S3 ). Fig. 1 (b) 
shows the ECCI micrograph obtained within a grain on the YZ plane of 
the C-HEA sample. The solidification cell can be clearly observed with 
the nano-sized precipitates as the black contrast at the boundaries of 
cells. 
Among the various microstructural factors in the LPBFed materials, 
the solidification cell is usually a key microstructure connecting micron- 
sized grains to nano-sized precipitates [43] . When the powder bed is 
exposed to a high-energy laser beam during the LPBF, melt pools are 
created along the laser trajectory, and solidification occurs directly as 
ultrafast cooling in the melt pools. Because the solidification rate is 
extremely high (10

cannot have secondary dendritic arms [47,48] . Therefore, fine cylin -
drical cells with a hexagonal cross-section are grown along the direction 
of the temperature gradient in the melt pools during the LPBF [48] . For 
the present samples, the average cell diameter was measured to be 
430 nm. Moreover, due to the severe thermal-induced stress from ul -
trafast heating and cooling during the LPBF, numerous dislocations are 
profusely generated, and they are arranged into the cell boundaries, as 
indicated in Fig. 1 (c). 
Meanwhile, solute segregation usually occurs at the cell boundaries 
under the constitutional undercooling condition [48] . For the CoCr -
FeMnNi HEAs, the segregation of Mn and Ni at cell boundaries is often 
reported in the LPBFed CoCrFeMnNi HEAs [47 – 49] . Fig. 2 shows a 
scanning TEM (STEM) bright-field (BF) image with EELS maps of sub -
stitutional elements around the solidification cell in the C-HEA sample. 
Mn is abundantly enriched at the cell boundaries, and Ni is dimly 
segregated, while the Co, Fe, and Cr are lean. The results of thermody -
namic calculation using the Calphad software of JMatPro ® version 7.0 
(Sente Software Ltd, UK) are presented in Supplementary Fig. S4 for the 
element concentration in the liquid as a function of the solid fraction 
during solidification in the CoCrFeMnNi system [39,40,50] . During so -

melt, which demonstrates that the Mn and Ni atoms are ejected as solute 
atoms into the melt at the solid-liquid interface under the constitutional 
undercooling condition. It leads to the segregation of Mn and Ni at the 
cell boundaries between cells continuously grown along the solidifica -
tion direction.

3.2. Cryogenic tensile properties of C-HEA 
Fig. 4 shows the results of tensile testing for the C-HEA at 298 and 
77 K. Strikingly, as shown in Fig. 4 (a), while the stress level of the C-HEA 
at 77 K is much higher than that at 298 K, the tensile elongation of the C- 
HEA is significantly enhanced at 77 K compared to that at 298 K. At 
298 K, the yield strength (YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS), and total 
elongation (T. El) of the C-HEA were measured to be 721 ± 12 MPa, 827 
± 21 MPa, and 22.2 ± 2.1%, respectively. At 77 K, the YS, UTS, and T. El 
of the C-HEA were 920 ± 8 MPa, 1220 ± 10 MPa, and 39.2 ± 4.9%, 
respectively. Notably, the uniform elongation (38.9%) of the C-HEA at 
cryogenic temperature is over twice that (14.7%) at room temperature. 
Fig. 3 (b) shows the work hardening rate (WHR) curve with the flow 
curve of the C-HEA at room and cryogenic temperatures. While the WHR 
gradually decreases with increasing strain at 298 K, the WHR is drasti -
cally accelerated with positive slope after the elastic-plastic transition 
region up to fracture at 77 K [51] . This phenomenon of the C-HEA 
printed by the LPBF is quite distinguished from the case of CoCrFeMnNi 
HEAs printed by the LPBF [37] . WHR has a beneficial effect on 
increasing the uniform elongation of the metallic materials by delaying 
plastic instability (i.e., necking) during tensile deformation [53,54] . 
Recent literature [37] reported that the WHR of LPBFed CoCrFeMnNi 
HEAs closely maintained its value after elastic-plastic transition in 
overall tensile deformation at 77 K, which contributed to approximately 
2 times higher uniform elongation at 77 K than that at 298 K. Mean -
while, for the present alloy, the acceleration of WHR under the cryo -
genic condition is superior, with positive slope of WHR as a function of 
true plastic strain, which contributes increasing uniform elongation of 
the C-HEA at 77 K by more than 2 times compared at 298 K. It also 
demonstrates that the addition of a small amount of carbon is advan -
tageous for enhancing the strain hardening ability of the CoCrFeMnNi 
HEAs in cryogenic condition. 
3.3. Deformation mechanisms of C-HEA during cryogenic deformation 
Fig. 5 shows EBSD image quality (IQ) maps of the pre-strained 
samples produced at cryogenic and room temperatures (interrupted 
tensile curves for producing the pre-strained samples; see Supplemen -
tary Fig. S5 ). Notably, while it is difficult to find micro-bundles of twins 
in the sample deformed at room temperature, it can be observed that 
deformation twins are actively evolved at the early stage of deformation 
of the C-HEA at cryogenic temperature (for the EBSD maps at high 
magnification; see Supplementary Fig. S6 ).

exceed critical twinning stress for the onset of deformation twinning 
[55] . The value of critical twinning stress is strongly related to the 
stacking fault energy (SFE) and grain size of metallic materials [55,56] .

decreased, while a smaller the grain size increases the critical twinning

ture. Similarly, for the current C-HEA, the flow stress can exceed the 
critical twinning stress of 860 MPa at the early stage of deformation at 
77 K. Furthermore, it has been widely reported that lowering tempera -

4. Conclusions 
In this study, the cryogenic tensile behavior of carbon-doped CoCr -
FeMnNi HEA printed by the LPBF process was systematically investi -
gated with microstructure analysis in various length scales. The sample 
printed by the LPBF exhibited a hierarchical heterogeneous micro -
structure. In particular, the addition of a small amount of carbon pro -
motes the formation of nano-sized Cr
23
C
6 
carbides finely distributed at 
the cell boundaries in the LPBFed C-HEA. Tensile tests revealed that the 
strength and ductility of the sample are simultaneously increased when 
decreasing the temperature from 298 to 77 K. In particular, the ductility 
of the sample under cryogenic deformation is approximately twice that 
at room temperature. 
Because the flow stress of the C-HEA is increased when temperature 
decreases, the flow stress can easily exceed the critical twinning stress 
for the initiation of deformation twinning. Therefore, deformation twin 
actively evolves at the early stage of deformation of the sample at 77 K, 
while deformation twin scarcely evolves under room temperature 
deformation. Deformation twins act as additional boundaries for dislo -
cation movement, which accelerates strain hardening rate via profuse 
accumulation of GNDs at 77 K. Nano-sized carbides also promote the 
evolution of high-density GNDs around the cell boundaries during 
cryogenic tensile deformation. The deformation twins and carbides 
synergistically boost the GND evolution in the sample, which generates 
high back stress with accelerating strain hardening rate during cryo -
genic deformation. This higher strain hardening rate promotes a sig -
nificant enhancement of the tensile strength and uniform elongation by 
delaying the plastic instability of the LPBFed C-HEA. Moreover, to 
quantify the effect of cryogenic temperature on the flow stress of the 
LPBFed C-HEA, strengthening contributions from various microstruc -
tural factors to YS were quantitatively estimated, considering both 
thermal and athermal components. The predicted value of cryogenic YS 
(956 MPa) shows good agreement with experimentally measured value 
(920 MPa), within an error range of less than 4%. 
We believe that this study offers a potential guideline for alloying 
design in additive manufacturing to produce high-performance products 
with excellent load-bearing capacity for use in cryogenic applications. 
Furthermore, the broader significance of this work lies in the potential 
application of the alloys across various temperature ranges, spanning 
from liquid nitrogen temperature down to extremely low temperatures. 
Depending on the specific temperature requirements of different appli -
cations, these mechanisms of tensile behavior of the LPBFed alloy can be 
adapted accordingly by controlling microstructure via controlling the 
amount of carbon or the LPBF process parameters. The insights gained 
from this study will also facilitate the applicability of the LPBFed ma -
terials in extremely low-temperature in the future.
