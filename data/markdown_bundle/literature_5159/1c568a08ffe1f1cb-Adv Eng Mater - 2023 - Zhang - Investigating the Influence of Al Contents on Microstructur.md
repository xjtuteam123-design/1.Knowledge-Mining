1. Introduction
Owing to the high-volume fraction of γ 0 precipitation hardening
phase ( /C25 80%), Ni 3Al intermetallic superalloys exhibit excellent
mechanical properties and high-temperature resistance. [1,2]
Ni3Al superalloys have been applied to manufacture turbine
blades, civilian transfer rollers in austenitizing furnaces, heat
treatment trays, radiant burner tubes, and hot forging die. [3,4]
These civilian components were characterized by large-diameter
(in meter-scale) and simple shapes of pipes or blocks. Their
manufacturing typically involves casting, [5] cold rolling, [6]
and powder metallurgy technologies. [7,8]
Recently, additive manufacturing has
attracted signi ﬁcant attention because of
its digital, ﬂexible, and controllable fabrica-
tion process for the direct and fast produc-
tion of metal components. [9] Application of
additive manufacturing on Ni-based super-
alloy, e.g., precipitation hardening type
Inconel 738 alloy, provides a new perspec-
tive for prototype manufacturing of Ni 3Al
superalloy components. [10– 12]
Differentiated by raw material, additive
manufacturing was divided into two major
branches. One was powder-based additive
manufacturing, including selective laser
melting, selective electron beam melting,
and direct laser/electron beam energy
deposition (DED) techniques; another
was wire-based additive manufacturing,
including direct laser/electron beam/arc
energy deposition techniques. Ni 3Al super-
alloys have commercially available powders
but lack welding wires. Therefore, powder-
based additive manufacturing of Ni 3Al
superalloys was the ﬁrst to conduct
research. Huang et al. [13] deposited a Ni 3Al superalloy wear-
resistant layer on the 316 L stainless steel using laser melting
Ni3Al powder. By direct laser deposition, Liu et al. [14] deposited
a single-crystal (SX) Ni 3Al-based IC221W superalloy on an SX
Ni-based superalloy substrate but found solidi ﬁcation cracks at
the interface of the deposited alloy and substrate. Yao et al. [15]
employed the selective electron beam melted to prepare Ni 3Al-
based IC21 superalloy. They also observed the solidiﬁcation crack
and detected the enrichment of Si element in the liquid ﬁlm on
the crack surface. Further, the optimization of process parame-
ters helped to obtain crack-free Ni 3Al superalloy. Compared to
powder-based additive manufacturing, wire-based additive
manufacturing, with its nearly 100% material utilization and
high deposition rates, exhibits signi ﬁcant advantages in printing
large-sized and simple-shaped Ni 3Al superalloy components. To
solve the lack of available Ni 3Al superalloy wire, a synchronous
Ni wire – Al powder feeding based on direct plasma arc energy
deposition was developed to prepare a Ni 3Al superalloy. [16] But
low addition of Al (below 12 at.%) led to a limited mechanical
property with an ultimate tensile strength of only 520 MPa.

In situ synthesis of Ni 3Al by simultaneously feeding both Ni and Al wires into the
molten pool is a common wire-based additive manufacturing technology for
Ni3Al superalloys. However, Ni3Al superalloys within a composition gap still have
not yet been synthesized. The optimal composition range has also not been
investigated yet. Herein, Ni 3Al superalloys with Al equivalents between 17 and
25 at.% are fabricated by twin wire-based direct arc energy deposition by
adjusting the relative feeding speed. Microstructural analysis shows that the
solidiﬁcation products generate no γ 0 precipitation when Al content is 17 at.%.
Al promotes the formation of γ 0 phase, as the Al content increases, the deposited
Ni3Al superalloys with 20 –25 at.% Al consist of dendritic γ þ γ 0 dual phase and
interdendritic γ 0 single phase. The proportion of γ þ γ 0 dual phase gradually
increases with decreasing Al contents, whereas the tensile strength and elon-
gation gradually increase. The Ni3Al superalloys (20 at.% Al) with the most γ þ γ 0
dual phase exhibit the highest tensile strength and elongation (939 MPa and
25.7%, respectively). Fracture analysis shows that the dislocation decomposition
and stacking fault shearing mechanisms allow dislocations to continuously cut
through γ 0 precipitations to maintain deformation, which is responsible for high
tensile strength and ductility.

2. Experimental Section
Commercial pure Ni (ERNi1, containing 3 wt% Ti) and pure Al
wires (1A1100) with a diameter of 1.2 mm were utilized as
feedstock in the present work. The equipment setup of the twin
wire-based DED system is illustrated in Figure 1 a. The strategy
employed a tungsten inert gas welding (GTAW) power equipped
with a water-cooled torch (Fronius, Transtig-5000) for heat
resources. The torch was fastened to a robotic arm (ABB, IRB-
2600) and followed the robotic arm in a preset path. The Ni
and Al wires were synchronously fed into the arc molten pool
by two independent wire feeders (Autotig, KHXY) with a feeding
accuracy of /C6 3 mm min/C0 1. Considering the difference between
the melting points of Ni and Al of about 800 °C, the Al wire was
set below the Ni wire in the same vertical plane. The installation
details and droplet transfer behavior of twin wires can be found
in ref. [18]. The critical processing parameters were a GTAW cur-
rent of 120 A, a travel speed of 1.5 mm s /C0 1, an interlayer temper-
ature of 300 °C, and a substrate temperature of 500 °C. The
molten pool was protected with Ar gas.

3. Results and Discussion
3.1. Microstructures of the Deposited Ni 3Al Superalloys with
Different Al Contents
Figure 2 illustrates the XRD patterns of the deposited Ni 3Al
superalloys with varied Al contents. Because the γ 0-Ni3(Al, Ti)
precipitation and the γ-Ni solid solution are both face-centered
cubic lattices with similar lattice constants (3.581 and 3.524 Å,
respectively), it is dif ﬁcult to distinguish the diffraction peaks
of γ 0 and γ phases. And γ/γ 0 phase is marked in Figure 2.
The deposited 83Ni14Al3Ti Ni 3Al superalloy exhibited the γ/γ 0
(200) and (100) of preferred crystallographic planes. As the Al
content increases, the preferred planes of the deposited Ni 3Al
superalloys remained unchanged.
The microscope and SEM microstructure of the deposited
Ni3Al superalloys with varied Al contents are shown in
Figure 3 . The metallographic images of the YOZ section in
Figure 3a,e,i indicated that the deposited superalloys grew
columnar dendrite through multiple layers, which was related
to the high-temperature gradient along the height direction.
Figure 3b exhibits that the dendrite grain consists of a gray den-
dritic phase and gray – black interdendritic phase of the deposited
80Ni17Al3Ti superalloy; the solidi ﬁcation microstructure was
consistent with our previous study about deposited Ni 3Al super-
alloy.[18] Combined with EDS analysis results, the gray dendritic
phase in Figure 3b was determined as dendritic γ þ γ 0 dual-phase
microstructure, and the gray – black interdendritic phase was
interdendritic γ 0 single-phase structure. The magni ﬁed image
of dendritic γ þ γ 0 dual phase in Figure 3c shows the polygonal
γ 0 precipitations connected by γ channels. The γ 0 precipitations
with an average diameter of 0.23 μm were synthesized, which
indicated that the twin wire-based DED could prepare Ni 3Al
superalloy. Figure 3f/C0 g,j/C0 k displays the dendrite grain morphol-
ogy and γ þ γ 0 dual phase of 78Ni19Al3Ti and 75Ni22Al3Ti
superalloys. An obvious difference in the proportion of dendritic
γ þ γ 0 dual phase and average diameter of γ 0 precipitations was
observed with increasing Al contents. Compared to the
80Ni17Al3Ti superalloy (0.23 μm), the 78Ni19Al3Ti found bulk
interdendritic γ 0 phase and an increase in average diameter of γ 0
precipitations (0.26 μm). When the content of Al and Ti met the
stoichiometric ratio (Ni: Al þTi=3:1), the massive interdendritic
γ 0 phase and coarser γ 0 precipitations with an average diameter
of 0.62 μm were observed in the solidi ﬁcation microstructure of
the deposited 75Ni22Al3Ti superalloy. Ref.[17] indicated that
when the Al content exceeded the stoichiometric ratio of
25 at.%, a reticular interdendritic γ 0 phase would form. Even
needle-like brittle NiAl phase appeared when the Al content
was above 30 at.%. Despite the lack of characterization of mechan-
ical performance, it was difﬁcult to coordinate deformation for the
reticular γ 0 phases and needle-like brittle phases to obtain good
mechanical properties. Additionally, Figure S1 (Supporting
Information) shows the microstructure of the 83Ni14Al3Ti alloy.
However, a signi ﬁcant microstructural evolution was observed
and no γ 0 precipitations was generated in theγ matrix, which indi-
cated that the deposited 83Ni14Al3Ti alloy was no longer Ni 3Al
superalloy. Therefore, from a microstructural perspective, the
atomic fraction of Al should lie between 14 and 22 at.% for the
twin wire-based DED binary Ni 3Al superalloys.

3.2. Mechanical Properties of the Deposited Ni 3Al Superalloys
with Different Al Contents
The microstructure of the three deposited Ni 3Al superalloys has
been detailed and compared. The three deposited Ni 3Al super-
alloys were evaluated using the tensile properties as a reference
system. Figure 6a,b displays the tensile stress – strain curves and
all the tensile data of the three deposited Ni 3Al superalloys. The
tensile properties of the deposited superalloys increased with
decreasing Al content. The deposited stoichiometric Ni 3(Al, Ti)
75Ni22Al3Ti superalloy exhibited a fracture strength of 679 MPa
and elongation of 13.5%. The 78Ni19Al3Ti superalloy showed
improved mechanical properties with a fracture strength of
751 MPa and elongation of 18.1%. Further, the deposited
80Ni17Al3Ti superalloy demonstrated an excellent combination
of tensile strength of 947 MPa and ductility of 24.7%. Figure 6c
displays the tensile strength and ductility of the previously
reported cast and directionally solidi ﬁed Ni 3Al superalloys
(IC6: Ni – 8.5Al– 14Mo– 0.1B– C, IC221M: Ni – 8Al– 7.7Cr– 1.4Mo–
1.7Zr– 0.008B, and IC21: Ni – 8.3Al– 2.5Cr– 12.6Mo– 1.5Re– 4Ta,
wt%).[15,23– 25] These Ni 3Al alloys produced by traditional
manufacturing process exhibited the trade-off tensile properties.
Compared to the mechanical properties of AM and cast Ni 3Al
superalloys, the deposited 80Ni17Al3Ti superalloy showed a
synergistic improvement on tensile strength and elongation.
However, compared to DS Ni 3Al superalloys, the deposited
80Ni17Al3Ti superalloy exhibited excellent plasticity but limited
tensile strength. Generally, the twin wire-based direct arc energy
deposited Ni 3Al superalloy demonstrated high tensile strength
and ductility.
Figure 7 shows the corresponding tensile fracture morphology
of the deposited Ni 3Al superalloys. The plastic dimple zone and
brittle cleavage zone were observed simultaneously in
75Ni22Al3Ti superalloy. Previous EBSD characterization on
the tensile sample surface of the 78Ni19Al3Ti superalloy found
the interface between γ þ γ 0 dual phase and interdendritic γ 0
single phase had a high density of geometrically necessary
dislocations and stress concentrations, [18] which would be the
ﬁnal fracture surface. Therefore, the plastic dimple zone and brit-
tle cleavage zone might correspond to γ þ γ 0 dual phase and
interdendritic γ 0 single phase, respectively.
Dislocations originated in the γ solid solution phase of γ þ γ 0
dual phase. Then, in 75Ni22Al3Ti superalloy, the high propor-
tion of bulk interdendritic γ 0 phase would inevitably decrease
the slip distance of dislocations and occurred stress concentra-
tion, as dislocations did not cut through these large-sized bulk
γ 0 phase. Moreover, many secondary cracks appeared at the inter-
face between dimples and cleavage, indicating a weak phase
interface. Thus, the deposited 75Ni22Al3Ti superalloy exhibited
lower strength and ductility. The microstructural evolution in
Figure 3 showed that as the Al content decreased, the proportion
of γ þ γ 0 dual-phase structure in the deposited superalloy
increased, increasing the slip distance. Therefore, the
78Ni19Al3Ti and 80Ni17Al3Ti superalloys exhibited more

1) When the atomic equivalent of Al was 17 at.%, no γ 0 precipi-

the range of 20 – 25 at.% Al, the deposited Ni 3Al superalloys con-

γ þ γ 0 dual phase of the deposited Ni 3Al superalloys gradually
increased, while the average particle size of the γ 0 precipitation
decreased. 2) The tensile strength and elongation of the depos-

20 at.% Al exhibited the best mechanical properties combination

which is similar to those observed in commercial Ni 3Al super-
alloys. 3) The dislocation decomposition and SF shearing mech-
anisms allowed dislocations to continuously cut through γ 0
precipitations to maintain deformation, which was responsible
for high tensile strength and ductility. 4) For the binary
Ni3Al superalloys fabricated by direct arc energy deposited,
Ni-(20– 22 at.%) Al superalloys exhibited promising mechanical
properties for further application.
