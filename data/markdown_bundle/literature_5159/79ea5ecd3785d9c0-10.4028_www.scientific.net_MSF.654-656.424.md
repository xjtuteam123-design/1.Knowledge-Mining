High Temperature Strength of Ir/Ir 2Y Two Phase Alloys 
in the Ir-Pt-Y System 
Nobuaki Sekido a and Yoko Yamabe-Mitarai b 
National Institute for Materials Science, 1-2-1 Sengen, Tsukuba 305-0047 Japan 
 a sekido.nobuaki@nims.go.jp, b mitarai.yoko@nims.go.jp 
Keywords: Iridium, Laves phase, transmission electron microscopy, high temperature strength 
Abstract. Possibilities of heat resistant alloys based on a C15 Laves phase and an FCC phase have been 
examined in the Ir-Pt-Y ternary system. Although the Ir solid solution phase (A1) and the Ir 2Y phase 
(C15) are not in equilibrium in the Ir-Y binary system, this equilibrium is attained by small Pt additions 
to the binary system. High temperature compressive strength of an A1/C15 monovariant eutectic alloy 
was found to be much lower than that of Ir-15Nb, an Ir based γ/γ' alloy. Low strength of the present 
alloys is attributed to the absence of effective strengthening mechanisms that operate in the A1 phase; 
for Y is hardly dissolved within the A1 phase, by which solution hardening and precipitation hardening 
are not available. 
Introduction 
Some of the contemporary heat resistant materials that include Ni based, Fe-Ni based, and Co based 
superalloys take advantage of intermetallic compounds. Although geometrically close packed (GCP) 
phases have been proved to be effective strengthening phases by numerous studies, topologically close 
packed (TCP) phases, such as Laves phases, are often considered as deleterious products that degrade 
the mechanical properties of structural materials. However recent studies have demonstrated that Laves 
phases can be effective strengthening phases for ferritic steels [1,2], austenitic steels [3], Mg alloys 
[4,5], and in-situ composites based on refractory m etals [6,7]. Clearly the Laves phase is a useful 
component of multiphase assemblies under suitable microstructural control and processing. 
Consulting the database for binary phase diagrams, such compounds as A 
5B, A 4B, A 11 B3, A 7B2, 
and A 3B often form between A solid solution phase and A 2B Laves phases. For more than 220 known 
C15 binary Laves phases [8], only 15% of them are i n equilibrium with FCC solid solution phases. 
Moreover most of them are comprised of platinum group metals and rare earth elements. Thus A1/C15 
two-phase alloys have not been studied extensively for potential application to high temperature 
structural materials so far. The purpose in the present study is to examine the possibility of heat resistant 
alloys based on C15-Laves and FCC phases. As a model alloy system, we focus on the Ir-Pt-Y ternary 
system, where the two phase equilibrium between the Ir solid solution (A1) and Ir 2Y (C15) is attained. 
Experimental Procedures 
Alloys used in the present study were prepared by arc-melting of high purity raw materials under an Ar 
atmosphere. The arc-melted alloys were annealed under vacuum (10 
-3 Pa) at 2073 K for 72 hours. The 
constituent phases were identified by XRD and EPMA. The microstructures of the alloys were 
characterized by SEM and TEM. TEM foils were prepar ed by an ion-miller. High temperature 
compression tests were conducted under vacuum at 14 73 and 1773 K. The dimensions of the 
compression test specimens were 2 mm × 2 mm ×5 mm, and the tests were done at the nominal strain 
rate of 3×10 -4 (s -1).

Results and Discussion 
Previous study has demonstrated that the Ir solid solution phase (A1) and the Ir 
2Y phase (C15) are not 
in equilibrium in the Ir-Y binary system due to the existence of Ir 3Y [9]. However Pt addition to the 
Ir-Y binary system is found to destabilize Ir 3Y, by which the two-phase equilibrium between A1 and 
C15 is attained. Figure 1 shows SEM micrographs of as-cast Ir-Pt-Y ternary alloys. An Ir-3Pt-14Y alloy 
forms a two-phase microstructure of A1 and Ir 3Y, as shown in Fig. 1a. For Ir-10Pt-12.3Y (b), 
Ir-20Pt-12.3Y (c), and Ir-40Pt-18.3Y (f), the prima ry solidification phase is A1, followed by the 
co-precipitation of A1 and C15. For Ir-20Pt-18.3Y ( f), on the other hand, C15 is the primary 
solidification phase. An Ir-20Pt-15.5Y alloy exhibi ts a dendritic-eutectic microstructure formed by 
monovariant eutectic solidification of A1 and C15. Based on these observations, the liquidus projection 
of the Ir-Pt-Y ternary system has been determined as in Fig. 2. In this figure, e and p on the binary 
edges represent eutectic and peritectic points. The monovariant lines regarding C15, Pt 3Y and Pt 5Y are 
unsolved in this study, and thus drawn with dashed lines in Fig. 2.

Summary 
Potential applicability of A1/C15 two-phase alloys to heat resistant materials has been examined in the 
Ir-Pt-Y ternary system. Two phases of A1 and C15 ar e in equilibrium in a wide range of alloy 
compositions in the ternary system. An Ir-20Pt-15.5 Y alloy exhibits insufficient high temperature 
strength because of the absence of effective strengthening mechanisms that operate in the A1 phase. 
This is mainly attributed to the limited solubility of Y within the Ir solid solution phase.
