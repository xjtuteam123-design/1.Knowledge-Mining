1 Introduction
Ni-basedsuperalloysareextensivelyusedinthehotsections
of aircraft engines and industrial gas turbines owing to their
excellent mechanical properties and microstructure stability
at high temperatures [1,2]. However, with the elevated
temperature, the critical properties are required for the Ni-
based superalloys. The strengthening of Ni-based super-
alloyscomesfromthesolidsolutionstrengtheningofthefcc-
γ matrix, and the precipitation strengthening of the co-
herently embedded L1
2-γʹ phase [3]. Therefore, the me-
chanicalpropertiesofsuperalloysdependstronglyupontheir
microstructure, which is affected by their chemical compo-
sitionandmorphologyevolutionunderhightemperatureand
stress states.
Compositional optimization is a basic method for devel-
oping high-performance superalloys[4]. The modern com-
mercial Ni-based superalloys contain more than ten
elements,suchasAl,Ru,Co,Hf,Ti,Ta,W,Mo,Nb,Cr,and
Re. These elements tend to be grouped with some com-
monality, the first class of elements includes Al, Ta, and Ti,
andtheyhavebigatomicradiiandpromotetheformationof
an intermetallic compound of Ni
3(Al, Ta, Ti). The elements
Co, Cr, Mo, and Re make up the second group and prefer
partitioning to the γ-matrix[5,6]. Among them, especially
the element Re, which is extremely important for the high-
temperature mechanics and creep properties of superalloys
[7], therefore, the single crystal superalloys are categorized
