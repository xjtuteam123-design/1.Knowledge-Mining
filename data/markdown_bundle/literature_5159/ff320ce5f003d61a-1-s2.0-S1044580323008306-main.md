ABSTRACT 
The number and size of precipitates directly influence the strengthening of the high-temperature alloys. As 
special coherent interfaces, nano-twinned (NT) boundaries are pre-set in CoCrNi-based superalloy to regulate the 
distribution of nano-precipitates for further strengthening the mechanical property. High density of NTs with 
twin spacings of 2 – 200 nm is produced in CoCrNi alloy by surface mechanical rolling treatment (SMRT), and L1
2 
(Ni
3
Ti)-type precipitates are induced by the low-energy NT boundaries, as well as grain boundaries, after aging at 
700 
◦
C for 4 h. The CoCrNi alloy with L1
2 
nano-precipitates exhibits a super-high mechanical property, char -
acterized by 2019 MPa of a yield strength ( σ
y
) and 2300 MPa of an ultimate tensile strength ( σ
u
), much higher 
than the CoCrNi alloy counterpart of 840 ( σ
y
) and 1100 ( σ
u
) MPa, respectively. Meanwhile, the treated CoCrNi 
alloy also delivers a reasonable elongation of 7.0%. The super-high strength is mainly ascribed to the high 
density L1
2 
nanoprecipitates grown on the NT boundaries, while the elongation is sustained by the NTs 
boundaries. 
1. Introduction 
CoCrNi-based superalloys exhibit wide applications in aero-space 
engine blades and nuclear power fields [ 1 – 3 ] due to their prominent 
high-temperature mechanical properties [ 4 – 6 ]. To further enhance the 
strength and durability, the common strategies are involved dislocation 
strengthening by cold work [ 7 ], solid solution [ 8 ], precipitation [ 9 ], 
refined grain [ 10 ], and transformation strengthening [ 10 , 11 ], which 
generally result in the degradation of plasticity. For superalloys, pre -
cipitation strengthening indued by alloying elements can significantly 
increase the strength through controlling the type, size and number of 
the precipitates [ 12 – 14 ]. Kai et al. [ 13 ] found that inhomogeneous L1
2
- 
type ((Ni, Co, Cr)
3
(Ti, Al)) precipitation phases were produced after 
alloying Al and Ti in CoCrNi medium entropy alloy (MEA), which 
resulted in an enhanced yield strength ( σ
y

et al. [ 14 ] proposed a complex procedure to improve the strength by 
incorporating Al into CoCrNi MEA through high-pressure torsion to 
generate σ and B
2 
phases, reaching an ultimate strength ( σ
u
) of 1850 
MPa, and losing the tensile elongation ( ε
e
) to 3%. Factually, the strength 
of the MEA can be obviously improved by precipitation strengthening, 
some new dilemmas are fallen into, such as synthesis process, welding 
and other problems [ 15 , 16 ]. The uniformity and reproducibility of the 
microstructures are hard to control during the synthesis process, 
accompanied with the unpredicted heterogeneous precipitates, such as 
σ , μ , η phases [ 16 – 18 ], which are generally degraded the plasticity and 
high-temperature stability. Meanwhile, these hard and brittle secondary 
phases strongly deteriorate the weldability of superalloys [ 19 ]. These 
problems restrict the serve-life and serve-temperature of these MEA in 
aerospace and nuclear power fields. To avoid the problems caused by the 
introduction of alloying elements with high contents, Jia et al. [ 20 ] 
reported that the CoCrNi-based superalloys, types of MP159 and MP35N 
as objects, exhibited the high strength by producing L1
2 
phases after 
aging treatment. Excitingly, the ultimate strength of both alloys excee -
ded 2100 MPa, but their tensile elongations were less than 2%. 
To balance the strength and plasticity, twin strengthening is pro -
posed by the collaborative improvement of strength and plasticity. Twin 
boundaries (TBs) play the similar function of grain boundaries (GBs) to

mechanisms of twinning and precipitation strengthening, multiscale

rolling condition due to the low stacking fault energy (SFE) [ 31 ]. The 
effect of TBs produced by the SMRT on the precipitation behavior is 
explored. The synergetic actions of twinning strengthening and TBs- 
induced nanoprecipitation in CoCrNi alloy are discussed.

4. Discussion 
4.1. TB-induced precipitation 
The MEAs with low SFEs of 18 – 26 mJ m
 2 
[ 34 ] is susceptible to 
produce deformation twins by special treatments, such as SMRT 
[ 5 , 34 – 37 ]. Local high-energy sites are more likely to nucleate pre -
cipitates, such as GBs, joints of GB-GB, GB-TB, TB-TB, and TBs, and the 
nucleation capacity of the TBs are smaller than the GBs and GB-TB in -
tersections [ 26 ]. Yang et al. [ 38 ] demonstrated that L1
2
-type precipi -
tation phase was most abundant at the GBs in Ni-30Co-13Fe-15Cr-6Al- 
6Ti-0.1B (at.%) superalloy. High density of twins with a multi-scaled 
distribution is generated by SMRT, and the induced GB-TB, TB-TB in -
tersections, and TB interfaces shall be the preferred sites for precipita -
tion [ 39 ]. As shown in Figs. 2 b and 4 b, the SMRT-A is composed of high 
density of NTs and L1
2
-type precipitation phases at the TBs, implying the 
induced precipitation of L1
2
-phases at the TBs. The traditional precipi -
tation process of the CoCrNi alloy involves the precipitation at the GBs 
firstly, and then the interior of the grains, as schemed in Fig. 8 a-c. While 
for the SMRT-A sample, precipitation phases are preferentially produced 
at GBs, or the GB-TB joints, and then the TB-TB joints, and TBs subse -
quently according to the local interface energy, as illustrated in Fig. 8 d- 
g. Accompanied with the twin spacing decreasing and the number of TBs 
increasing, the precipitated phase at the TBs sharply increases ( Fig. 4 b 
and d). Additionally, the precipitates at the TBs have smaller sizes due to 
the burst nucleation, which might inhibit the growth of L1
2 
phases. 
Thus, the generation of NTs by SMRT technology is crucial for the 
regulation of precipitation in the CoCrNi alloy.

resulting from the formation of forest NTs. 
5. Conclusion 
In this work, a collaborative reinforcement strategy is developed in 
CoCrNi-based superalloys, and super-high strength is attained by regu -
lating the distribution of L1
2 
precipitation phase through high-density 
NTs interfaces. The precipitation strengthening induced by NTs are 
explored, and the following conclusions are obtained. 
(1) The CoCrNi-based superalloy was firstly treated by SMRT to 
produce high density of NTs, and then the SMRT sample was 
experienced heat treatment at 700 
◦
C for 4 h to induce the pre -
cipitation along the TBs. Compared to the original CoCrNi-based 
alloy after annealing, the SMRT-A alloy contains more L1
2 
phase 
at the TBs concentrated with Ni and Ti atoms. 
(2) Gradient microhardness distributions are achieved in SMRT and 
SMRT-A samples, while the SMRT-A sample delivers a 725 HV at 
surface, 170% of the As-wrought sample. Impressively, the 
SMRT-A sample exhibits a super high σ
y 
of 2019 MPa and a σ
u 
of 
2300 MPa with an elongation of 7.0%. 
(3) The strengthening of the SMRT-A sample involves the precipita -
tion strengthening, twin strengthening, HDI hardening, while the 
precipitation strengthening induced by the high density of TBs 
contributes the main part among the multiple strengthening ef -
fects. Meanwhile, the NTs improve the plasticity by the inter -
ruption of continuous growth of precipitation phase, and provide 
a large SHR.
