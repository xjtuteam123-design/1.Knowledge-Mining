1. Introduction 
The recently emerging high entropy alloys (HEAs), which originally 
involve the mixing of five or more principal elements in equiatomic 
ratios [ 1 , 2 ], open up a new composition space for designing structural 
materials. Some HEAs have outstanding strength-ductility combinations 
[ 3 , 4 ] and exhibit excellent performance at extreme service conditions 
(e.g. cryogenic temperatures [ 5 , 6 ], ultra-high temperatures [ 7 ] and se -
vere radiation environments [ 8 , 9 ]). It has been recognized that maxi -
mizing the configurational entropy itself cannot stabilize the solid 
solution phase [ 10 , 11 ], and, more importantly, is not the prerequisite to 
acquiring superior mechanical properties [ 6 , 12 , 13 ]. Therefore the 
rigorous compositional criterion of HEAs was relaxed, which generated 
much enthusiasm to design multicomponent alloys with fewer elements 
[ 14 , 15 ] and non-equiatomic compositions [ 12 , 16 , 17 ]. To explore the 
vast compositional space, the microstructure-oriented alloy design [ 12 , 
16 ] is a fruitful approach to realize desired mechanical properties. 
Metastability engineering is an effective strategy to design alloys 
with improved strength and ductility simultaneously. By tuning the 
phase instability [ 18 ], the twinning induced plasticity (TWIP) and/or 
transformation induced plasticity (TRIP) can be activated, which pro -
vides extra strain hardening capacity in addition to the common 
dislocation-mediated hardening. Adjusting the stacking-fault energy 
(SFE) through composition tuning, i.e., different SFE values indicate the 
prevalent deformation mechanisms by the mechanical twinning and/or 
martensite phase [ 16 , 17 , 19 ]. Here, SFE is the critical materials param -
eter related to the thermodynamic stability of the fcc phase and intrin -
sically related to the microscopic mechanisms of twinning and 
martensitic phase transformation [ 20 , 21 ]. On the other hand, in the 
BCC-structured refractory HEAs, HCP or α ʹʹ -martensites have been acti -
vated by decreasing the amount of β -stabilizers (Nb and Ta) in several 
non-equiatomic HEAs (e.g. Ti

2.2. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations 
The DFT calculations were carried out with the exact muffin-tin or -
bitals (EMTO) method [ 26 – 28 ]. The local-density approximation 
parameterized by Perdew and Wang [ 29 ] was adopted to describe the 
exchange and correlation functional, and the total energy was computed 
via the full charge-density technique [ 28 , 30 ]. The basis set included s, p, 
d, and f states. The electronic structure of the substitutionally disordered 
refractory HEAs was computed in the coherent-potential approximation 
(CPA) [ 31 – 33 ].

For Zr30 alloy, the BCC → HCP transformation-induced plasticity 
(TRIP) effect contributes to the steady work hardening and high tensile 
strain. This hardening mechanism might originate from two aspects. 
According to the dynamic Hall-Petch theory [ 39 , 40 ], the transformed 
HCP bands refine the initial BCC grains along with increasing the density 
of phase interface, which effectively reduces the mean free path of dis -
locations and limits dislocation glide [ 12 ]. In addition, the elastic 
moduli contrasts between BCC and HCP phases would produce the dy -
namic strain-stress partitioning and create internal stress and geomet -
rically necessary dislocations, which enhances strain hardening [ 41 ]. 
For Zr20 alloy, a certain amount of residual BCC phase can be 
detected in the as-recrystallized state. Using high-resolution EBSD-TKD 
(Transmission Kikuchi Diffraction) technique, as shown in Fig. 4 (a), 
residual BCC phase with the same crystallographic orientation is 
distributed within HCP phase variants. That untransformed BCC phase is 
also identified in the bright-field images in Fig. 4 (c). After deformation 
with 10% tensile strain, in Fig. 4 (b), no BCC phase can be detected. As 
shown in Fig. 4 (d), high density of dislocations and twins exist in the 
deformed single phase HCP structured Zr20 alloy.

[ 34 ]. For Zr20 alloy, the activation of residual BCC (based on the 
EBSD/TEM results in Fig. 4 ) to HCP phase transformation is considered 
to induce the first yielding around 380 MPa during the tensile test. The 
elastic and plastic deformation of HCP variants contributes to the re -
