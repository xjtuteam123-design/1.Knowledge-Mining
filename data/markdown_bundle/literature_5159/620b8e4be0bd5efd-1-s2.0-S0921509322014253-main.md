ABSTRACT 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have promising applications in industry due to their outstanding high- 
temperature properties, but most of them are brittle and poorly plastic formability at ambient temperature. 
Developing new ambient ductile RHEAs is a challenge in current materials research. In this work, an ambient 
ductility TiHfVNbTa alloy was prepared, and its microstructure, mechanical properties, phase stability, lattice 
distortion, and basic physical properties were investigated by combing experiments and density functional theory 
(DFT) calculations. The obtained results show that TiHfVNbTa alloy has excellent cold-rolled formability with 
> 84% thickness reduction, no crack generation, and ultimate tensile strain up to > 12% at ambient temperature. 
Besides, the tensile yield strength (hardness) of the alloy increases from 997 MPa (363 HV) in the annealed state 
to 1340 MPa (453 HV) in the 84% cold-rolled state, displaying work-hardening behavior. The TiHfVNbTa alloy 
has a single-phase solid solution body-centered cubic (BCC) structure, and no structural transformation occurs 
during rolling and tension, implying its good phase stability. Notably, the TiHfVNbTa alloy exhibits a higher 
specific yield stress and ductility compared to most RHEAs. DFT results reveal that the high strength is mainly 
contributed by severe lattice distortion. Hf and V elements are the major sources of lattice distortion. In addition, 
the physical properties such as metallic bonding, anisotropy and electronic structure are also analyzed based DFT 
calculations. The obtained results not only help to understand the material properties of TiHfVNbTa alloy, but 
also provide design guidelines for novel ductile RHEAs. 
1. Introduction 
Refractory metallic materials are key structural materials for 
manufacturing high-temperature machines used in aerospace, trans -
portation, and petrochemical industries. As the increasing efficiency and 
service temperatures of heat engines, high performance refractory 
metallic materials become more rigorous. However, traditional re -
fractory alloys are approaching their service limits, and there is an ur -
gent need to develop more advanced refractory alloys with high specific 
strength and excellent processability. Over the recent years, refractory 
high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have attracted much attention in the ma -
terials community due to their excellent mechanical properties and 
structural stability at high temperature [ 1 ]. However, the poor ductility 
and deformability of most RHEAs, with a compressive fracture strain 
< 10% at ambient temperature, hinders their engineering applications. 
For example, the compressive yield strength of NbMoTaW alloy can 
reach 1058 MPa, but its compression deformation is only 1.5% at 
ambient temperature [ 2 ]. A recently developed CrMoNbV has high 
compressive yield strength 1518 ± 38 MPa, but its compression strain is 
also only 5% at ambient temperature, making it difficult to perform 
plastic forming [ 3 ]. Therefore, it is urgent to develop new RHEAs with 
high specific strength and good ambient plasticity.

RHEAs with high strength and excellent plasticity. For example, the

These researches encourage us to further explore novel high strength 
and ambient ductile RHEAs. 
More recently, An et al. [ 11 ] synthesized a novel single-phase solid 
solution TiHfVNbTa alloy with high compressive yield stress 1350 MPa 
( > 1 GPa) and ambient compressive fracture strain > 45%. The 
TiHfVNbTa alloy exhibits higher strength and better ambient plasticity 
than most BCC RHEAs. An et al. [ 11 ] calculated the contribution of solid 
solution strengthening to the yield strength of TiHfVNbTa alloy to be 
about 1049 MPa, accounting for 77.7% of the total yield strength (1350 
MPa), and concluded that the high strength of the alloy is due to severe 
lattice distortion. Lattice distortion originates from the atomic size misfit 
of multiple solid solution elements, which is known as one of the four 
core effects of high entropy alloys along with the high entropy effect, 
sluggish diffusion effect, and Cocktail effect [ 1 ]. Lattice distortion is 
closely associated with the properties of HEAs. For example, the lattice 
distortion of MoNiTi alloy results in twisting dislocation lines, triggering 
non-screw dislocation motion on high-index crystal planes, which in 
turn enhances the plastic deformability of the alloy [ 12 ]. The lattice 
distortion also influences the phonon-phonon interactions and lattice 
vibrations of HEAs, and thus affecting their crystal stability and thermal 
properties [ 13 ]. The ambient compressive strain of TiHfVNbTa alloy is 
> 45%, which means that it can be manufactured into commercial 
sheets, rods, and wires by rolling, extruding or drawing at ambient 
temperature. Therefore, the TiHfVNbTa alloy is one of the most prom -
ising RHEAs for industrial applications. However, research on the 
TiHfVNbTa alloy is still in its infancy, and the intrinsic mechanisms 
responsible for many fundamental material properties are still unclear, 
such as phase stability, microstructure evolution, processing form -
ability, tensile properties, mechanical anisotropy, and lattice distortion. 
These properties play a key role in improving the material properties of 
TiHfVNbTa alloy, which are also important for designing processing 
processes and new products. 
This work aims to explore the mechanical properties, plastic prop -
erties, phase stability, lattice distortion, and fundamental physical 
properties of TiHfVNbTa alloy by combing experiments and theoretical 
calculations, and to provide foundations for designing ambient ductile 
RHEAs. To achieve this goal, the TiHfVNbTa alloy was prepared by 
vacuum arc melting. Then the mechanical performances of the alloy are 
detected by cold rolling, tensile, and hardness tests. Besides, the phase 
stability of the alloy is confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD), electron 
back-scattered diffraction (EBSD), and calculation phase diagram 
(CALPHAD). Moreover, DFT calculations are carried out to study the 
electronic properties, anisotropy, ductility, mechanical parameters that 
are difficult to obtain experimentally, and to analyze the correlation 
between lattice distortion and interatomic interaction from the elec -
tronic point of view. This study will systematically reveal the structure 
and material properties of TiHfVNbTa alloys, and also provide new ideas 
for designing RHEAs with good ambient plasticity.

vacuum arc furnace with high purity ( > 99.95%) metal elements within

them over between each step. Finally, spherical cap ingots with a 
diameter of about 30 mm and a thickness of about 7 mm were obtained.

3.5. Lattice distortion 
Extensive studies have shown that the diverse elemental types and 
atomic sizes can cause lattice distortion in HEAs. The lattice distortion 
generates asymmetrical stress field within the alloys, which can twist 
dislocation lines, impede dislocation movement, and thus improving the 
yield strength of HEAs [ 13 ]. In addition, lattice distortions can change 
lattice vibrational modes and phonon dispersion, thus altering the 
thermodynamic and kinetic properties of HEAs [ 15 ]. Therefore, the 
lattice distortion effect, as one of the four main effects of HEAs, has a 
crucial influence on material properties [ 31 ]. However, how to evaluate 
the lattice distortion of HEAs is still an open question. 
In this section, the lattice distortion of TiHfVNbTa is quantitatively 
analyzed by comparing the distorted and undistorted configurations 
from DFT calculations. The distorted configuration is the fully relaxed 
supercell, i.e. , the atomic position, cell shape, and volume allow changes 
during geometric optimization process. The undistorted configuration 
implies that the atoms strictly occupy ideal lattice sites. To build the 
undistorted configuration, a series of energy-volume relations were 
calculated by performing static energy calculations, in which the cell 
shape and atomic positions are not allowed to change. The lattice 
parameter of the undistorted configuration comes from the minimum 
energy point. The calculated lattice parameter of the undistorted 
configuration is 0.3275 nm, which is slightly smaller than the experi -
ment result of 0.3295 nm.

4. Conclusions 
In summary, a single-phase solid solution TiHfVNbTa alloy with 
good ambient plasticity was prepared by vacuum arc melting. The 
fundamental material and physical properties of TiHfVNbTa alloy were 
obtained through experiments and DFT calculations. Experimental re -
sults indicate that the alloy exhibits excellent plastic deformation, phase 
stability, and work hardening features. The tensile yield strength 
(hardness) increases from the 997 MPa (363 HV) of the annealed state to 
the 1340 MPa (453 HV) of the cold-rolled state. DFT calculations reveal 
that the solid solution strengthening roots in severe lattice distortion 
caused by the atomic size misfit, mechanical modulus misfit, and charge 
redistribution of multiple elements. Hf and V elements play key role in 
the solid solution strengthening and lattice distortion of the alloy. This 
work provides important data and references for the commercial 
development and application of the TiHfVNbTa alloy. Subsequently, we 
will focus on the plastic deformation mechanisms of the alloy during 
rolling deformation and explore its application potential in industrial 
fields. Moreover, TiHfVNbTa alloy as a candidate for high temperature 
applications, its high temperature mechanical properties should be 
given attention, especially its creep resistance properties at elevated 
temperature should be worthy of our attention.
