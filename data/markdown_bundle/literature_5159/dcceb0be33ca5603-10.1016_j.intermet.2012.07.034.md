abstract
This paper investigates the strength response of Ir- xHf-yNb alloys ( x ¼ 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10 at.%, and y ¼ 5, 6, 7,
1 1, 13, 15 at.%) with a dual-phase fcc/L1 2 microstructure at room and high temperatures. The experi-
mental strength at 1950 /C14 C was compared with that obtained by the rule of mixture. The results showed
that in the fcc/L1 2 structure the fcc phase always shows higher strength than the L1 2 phase, at both room
and high temperatures. A dual-phase fcc/L1 2 microstructure with different fcc to L1 2 fraction ratio
formed when Hf and Nb contents in the Ir- xHf-yNb ternary alloys were larger than 3 at.% and 5 at.%,
respectively, and the pure L1 2 microstructure was obtained at 10 at.% Hf and 15 at.% Nb. The Ir-5Hf-7Nb
alloy, possessing an fcc-dominant microstructure in which the optimization fcc fraction is about 78%, has
an outstanding 0.2% yield compressive strength of 270 MPa even at 1950 /C14 C. This ultra-high strength is
attributed to strong interface hardening by the large lattice mis ﬁt between the fcc and L1 2 phases. The
failure mode of the Ir eHfeNb ternary alloys is by debonding of the grain boundary.

1. Introduction
Iridium with a high melting point of 2443 /C14 C is one of the most
attractive base metals for future high-temperature structural
materials with an application range in excess of 2000 /C14 C. Ir equil-
ibrates with most transition metals (Me) and forms an Ir solid
solution (fcc) and L1 2 (Ir3Me) dual-phase microstructure [1]. Solid-
solution hardening and coherent hardening [2e6], as well as grain
boundary strengthening [7e9], have been used to improve the
high-temperature strength, which is the primary issue of Ir-based
alloys. Element selection for solid-solution hardening and grain
boundary strengthening, and optimization of the fcc and L1 2
microstructure are effective approaches for maximizing the bulk
strength of Ir-based alloys.
Grain boundary strengthening can be carried out by doping Th,
Ce and Al in the order of less than 100 wppm [7e9]. Those alloying
elements, which have a large atomic size misﬁt with Ir, can produce
excellent solid-solution hardening of the fcc phase and strength-
ening of the fcc/L1
2 interface. This occurs because the large atomic
size misﬁt increases the fcc lattice parameter [10,1 1]and the lattice
misﬁt between the fcc and L1 2 phases [3,4]. Nb, Hf and Zr have
shown the strongest solid-solution hardening effect on Ir [10] since
the atomic size mis ﬁts of these elements to Ir are as high as 0.04 e
0.17. IreHf and Ir eZr binaries with an L1 2-dominant fcc/L12 struc-
ture have outstanding high-temperature strength, which is thought
to be due to a high lattice mis ﬁt of about 2.4 e2.7% between the fcc
and Ir3Hf (L12)o rI r 3Zr (L12) phases [2].
Although Ir-based alloys have shown an fcc/L12 structure, which
phase shows higher strength remains unknown. In addition, the
optimum fraction of the phase that corresponds to the ultimate
strength has not yet been determined in Ir-based alloys. Based on
the atomic size mis ﬁt of the transition metals to Ir, Hf and Nb were
selected as alloying elements for Ir in this research. The nominal
compositions of the Ir eHfeNb ternary alloys were carefully
designed to obtain different microstructures from the monolithic
saturated fcc phase through the fcc/L12 microstructure to the single
L12 phase. The strength dependency of the Ir eHfeNb ternary alloys
on microstructure and temperature was investigated.

were expected to be obtained by increasing the Hf and Nb contents
up to 10 at.% and 15 at.%, respectively; subsequently, alloys with

3.2. Compressive stress estrain characteristics
Typical compressive stress estrain curves of the Ir eHfeNb
ternary alloys at room temperature and 1950 /C14 C are shown in
Fig. 3 . For the Ir-(4 e8)Hf-(6e1 1)Nb alloys with the fcc/L1 2 micro-
structure, the stress estrain curves at room temperature show
a linear portion followed by a long non-linear section ( Fig. 3 (a)).
The stress estrain curves of these speci ﬁc alloys are very close to
each other. The compressive test had to be stopped at a stress close
to 2500 MPa because the SiC platen set between the compressive
sample and the loading hold could not support such a high stress.
With a 78% fcc phase, the Ir-5Hf-7Nb alloy shows a slightly higher
stress than the other three alloys with the fcc/L1 2 microstructure,
and its plastic strain is as large as 7.5% at a stress of 2500 MPa. As for
the monolithic fcc (Ir-3Hf-5Nb), L1 2 (Ir-10Hf-15Nb) and L1 2-
dominant (Ir-9Hf-13Nb alloy with 79% L1 2 phase) alloys, the
compressive stress estrain curve has a small non-linear portion.
After the stress reaches the peak (ranging from 745 to 1440 MPa),
the stress falls rapidly due to the collapse of the samples. The
average Hv of the fcc and L1 2 phases, as well as the bulk 0.2% yield
compressive strength of the six alloys at room temperature, are
summarized in Table 1, where Ff is the volume fraction of the fcc
phase. It should be emphasized that the saturated fcc phase shows
higher compressive strength and hardness than the L1 2 phase, and
the dual-phase fcc/L12 microstructure has both higher strength and
ductility than the monolithic fcc and L1 2 phases at room
temperature.
At 1950 /C14 C, the fcc/L1 2 microstructure still shows an excellent
strength response, better than those of the monolithic fcc and L1 2
alloys, as indicated in Fig. 3(b). This means that the ef ﬁciency of the
interface hardening in the fcc/L1 2 structure at elevated tempera-
tures is much greater than that of the single solid-solution hard-
ening of the monolithic fcc phase. The fcc/L1 2 microstructure
always exhibits strong deformation hardening after yield till the
stress reaches the maximum strength smax of about 300 e500 MPa.
In this region, the three fcc-dominant alloys (Ir-4Hf-6Nb, Ir-5Hf-
7Nb and Ir-8Hf-1 1Nb) show higher stress levels than the L1
2-
dominant alloy (Ir-9Hf-13Nb). The comparison clearly reveals that
the stress level reaches the peak value at an fcc volume fraction of
78% for the Ir-5Hf-7Nb alloy. As for the monolithic fcc and L1 2
alloys, the strength level of the former (the Ir-3Hf-5Nb alloy [1 1])i s
evidently higher than that of the latter (the Ir-10Hf-15Nb alloy).
At 1800 /C14 C, the stress of the single L1 2 alloy (Ir-10Hf-15Nb) falls
rapidly after reaching a peak value of about 220 MPa. The other ﬁve
alloys show deformation hardening behavior until the stress rea-
ches about 500 MPa, at which the Ir-5Hf-7Nb alloy still has the
highest stress level at a constant strain. Table 2 summarizes the
compiled data of the 0.2% yield compressive strength ( s0.2) and/or

1950 /C14 C, s0.2 increases monotonically with the combined total of Hf
and Nb added, and reaches the peak value of about 270 MPa at
a concentration of 5 at.% Hf and 7 at.% Nb ( Ff ¼ 78%) at 1950 /C14 C.

4. Discussion
Previous studies revealed that the s0.2 of pure Ir at 1950 /C14 Ci s
20 MPa [10], and the solid-solution hardening of saturated Ir by the
combined addition of about 3 at.% Hf and 5 at.% Nb enhanced the
s0.2 up to 140 MPa [1 1]. This hardening effect is attributed to an
increment in the lattice parameter by 1.31% when 3 at.% Hf and
5 at.% Nb are added, whereas the lattice parameter of pure Ir is
0.3819 nm [10] and that of the Ir-3Hf-5Nb alloy is 0.3869 nm [1 1].
The increment of the s0.2 from 140 MPa (the Ir-3Hf-5Nb alloy) to
195e270 MPa (the Ir-(4 e9)Hf-(6e13)Nb alloys, in Table 2 )i s
attributed to the two phase fcc/L1 2 microstructures.
The bulk strength of a two-phase structure is estimated to
change as a linear function from 0% to 100% of the constituent
phases according to the rule of mixture (ROM) [16,19]. Fig. 6 shows
the difference of experimental strength from those obtained by
ROM at 1950 /C14 C. In the Ir eHfeNb ternaries the strengths far exceed
the predictions by ROM by about 150 MPa with fcc volume fractions
from 21% to 78%. Tables 1 and 2 conﬁrm this huge difference
between the experimental and predicted strengths at room
temperature and 1800 /C14 C, indicating that further strengthening of Ir
alloys is caused by the interface of g/g0.
Analysis of the X-ray patterns in Fig. 1 shows that in the Ir-4Hf-
6Nb, Ir-5Hf-7Nb, Ir-8Hf-1 1Nb and Ir-9Hf-13Nb alloys, the lattice
misﬁts between the fcc and L12 phases are as high as 0.947%, 1.1 14%,
1.202% and 1.092%, respectively. Our early works also found that in
the IreV, eTi, eTa, eNb, eHf, and eZr binaries [2], the lattice misﬁts
between the fcc and L12 phase are /C0 0.7%, 0.1%, 1.04%, 1.4%, 2.4%, and
2.7%, respectively. The strength increases monotonically in order of
the above lattice mis ﬁts, and reaches a signi ﬁcantly high level for
the Ir eHf and Ir eZr binaries. Higher lattice mis ﬁts of the fcc/L1 2
interfaces may play a key role in the bulk strength of Ir-based alloys
at room and high temperatures. It was found that in Ni-based
superalloys higher lattice mis ﬁt results in denser fcc/L1 2 interfa-
cial dislocation networks, which increases the deformation resis-
tance, i.e., the high-temperature strength [1 7e19]. Here, we should
emphasize that the Ni-based superalloys have an extremely regular
fcc/L12 microstructure, while the? L1 2 phase precipitates from the
fcc matrix during aging. For the Ir-based alloys in this work, the fcc/
L12 microstructure is not regular (see Fig. 2 ) and the L1 2 phase
forms during annealing at 2000 /C14 C for 24 h. Even so, there is also
the possible existence of a dense mis ﬁt dislocation network in the
interface of the Ir-based alloy, which results in such a high strength
at room and high temperatures for the Ir-based alloys with higher
interface lattice mismatch. Careful TEM observations should be
done to test this conjecture in the future.
In the presented Ir eHfeNb alloys, the 78% fcc phase is close to
the experimental optimum for the highest hardening effect by the
fcc/L12 interface. Interestingly, the optimum is about 77% L1 2 phase

5. Conclusions
IreHfeNb ternary alloys were developed according to the
principles of multi-component alloying, saturated solid-solution
hardening and interface hardening, and the following conclusions
were drawn.
1. The solubility limit of the solutes Hf and Nb on the Ir solid
solution in Ir eHfeNb alloys is close to Ir-3Hf-5Nb. The satu-
rated monolithic fcc phase shows higher strength than the L1 2
phase at both room and high temperatures. With a monolithic
saturated fcc microstructure, the Ir-3Hf-5Nb alloy was solid-
solution hardened entirely by Hf and Nb and its 0.2% yield
compressive strength was as high as 140 MPa at 1950
/C14 C.
2. In the Ir eHfeNb alloys, hardening of the fcc/L1 2 interface with
lattice misﬁts of about 0.947%e1.202% makes the bulk strength
of the dual-phase fcc/L1 2 microstructure superior to that of the
monolithic fcc and L1 2 phases. The highest strengths from
room temperature to 1800 /C14 C and 1950 /C14 C were obtained in the
Ir-5Hf-7Nb alloy with an optimum fcc fraction in the vicinity of
78%. At 1950 /C14 C, for example, the s0.2 and smax of the Ir-5Hf-7Nb
alloy with an fcc-dominant microstructure were unusually
high, 270 MPa and 498 MPa, respectively.
3. To obtain outstanding high-temperature strength, Ir-based
alloys should have the fcc-dominant microstructure.
