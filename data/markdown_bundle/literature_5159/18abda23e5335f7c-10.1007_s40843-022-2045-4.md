Simultaneously enhanced oxidation resistance and mechanical properties
in a novel lightweight Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 high-entropy alloy
Zibing An1, Shengcheng Mao 1*, Tao Yang 2, Yinong Liu 3*, Yanhui Chen 1, Xiaomeng Yang 1,
Shanshan Liu1, Xin Wang 1, Qingsong Deng 1, Ze Zhang 1,4 and Xiaodong Han 1*
ABSTRACT Enhanced oxidation resistance is a primary de-
mand for the application of refractory high-entropy alloys
(RHEAs) at elevated temperatures. In this study, Al was added
to a Ti2VZrNb RHEA to partially substitute Nb to improve its
oxidation resistance and mechanical properties. The alloy was
found to have an increased oxidation resistance by forming a
continuous Al2O3 + ZrO2 oxide protective surface. At the same
time, the room-temperature yield strength was also increased
by 66% to 1273 MPa via solid solution strengthening. The low
atomic mass of Al also helped to reduce the density of the alloy
by 8.2% to 5.44 g cm −3. This resulted in a high specific yield
strength of 234 MPa cm 3 g−1 for the alloy. Meanwhile, the
Ti2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy also exhibited a high compressive plas-
ticity of >50%. These values are among the best reported so far
for RHEAs.
Keywords: high-entropy alloy, mechanical property, oxidation
resistance, strength, plasticity
INTRODUCTION
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are a special group of
multiconstituent alloys made of some high-melting-temperature
elements, such as Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Cr, Mo, and W [1,2].
They generally have melting temperatures above 1600°C and are
usually body-centered cubic (BCC) in structure [1,3,4]. These
alloys are able to exhibit high-temperature strengths comparable
to those of Ni-based single-crystal superalloys, making them
potential candidates for turbine blade applications. For example,
NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV alloys are able to retain yield
strengths above 400 MPa at 1600°C [5], which are higher than
those of most Ni-based single-crystal superalloys at similar
temperatures.
High oxidation resistance at elevated temperatures and low
density are also critical or desirable requirements for the appli-
cation as turbine blades. In addition, high plasticity is also a high
benefit for the application of these alloys [6–9]. However, most
RHEAs suffer from poor plasticity, largely due to the inherent
nature of their BCC structure [5,10–13]. They are also generally
heavy, apparently related to the heavy refractory elements they
contain [14,15]. Their oxidation resistance is also generally low
because they do not form protective oxide films [16–18]. These
drawbacks limit the engineering application of RHEAs. Thus, it
has been a goal for material scientists to develop lightweight and
oxidation-resistant RHEAs with high plasticity.
To reduce the density of RHEAs, Al is commonly added to
alloys [19–22]. In addition to lowering the alloy density, Al also
helps to form a protective oxide film on the alloys, improving
their high-temperature oxidation resistance [17,23,24]. A cau-
tion of using Al is the formation of intermetallic phases with
other host elements due to its large negative mixing enthalpies
with most metallic refractory elements. This may increase the
alloy strength via second phase strengthening [25,26] and reduce
the alloy plasticity [13,22,27]. For example, the compressive yield
strength of a MoNbTaTiV RHEA was increased from 1230 to
1390 MPa by the addition of 16.7% Al, but the compressive
plasticity was decreased from 32% to 10% [28]. In this regard,
the formation of second phase intermetallics is undesirable in
Al-containing RHEAs.
This study attempts to fill this gap by developing a novel
single-phase Al-containing RHEA with a low mass density, high
yield strength and high plasticity, and good oxidation resistance.
This is achieved by using ductile and light base elements, ele-
ment substitution by Al, and engineering the valence electron
concentration for alloy plasticity. The effects of Al on the alloy
microstructure, lattice distortion, and oxidation resistance were
investigated.
EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
The alloy design strategy is based on the following considera-
tions (Fig. S1a). (1) First-principles calculations indicate that
elements in group IV (Ti, Zr, Hf) and group V (V, Nb, Ta) are
intrinsically ductile and fail by shear deformation instead of
cleavage under tension [29]. Hence, the base elements were
selected from these groups for high plasticity. (2) To reduce alloy
mass density, heavy elements Ta (ρ
Ta = 16.65 g cm−3) and Hf (ρHf
= 13.31 g cm −3) were eliminated, and Ti (4.54 g cm −3),
V(6.11 g cm−3), Zr (6.50 g cm −3), and Nb (8.57 g cm −3) were
selected as the principal elements. (3) The alloys have valence
electron concentrations (VECs) below 4.6 for high plasticity

[30,31]. A low VEC lowers the threshold lattice strain for dis-

above considerations, a Ti 2VZrNb alloy was designed. To
improve the oxidation resistance and further reduce the density

The two alloys were fabricated from high-purity (99.9 at% or
better) elemental metals by means of vacuum arc melting in a
water-cooled copper crucible in an argon atmosphere. The
densities of the alloys were measured via the Archimedes
method in water using a ME204E balance. The samples were
5 mm × 4 mm × 4 mm in dimension. The samples for oxidation
testing were approximately 4 mm × 4 mm × 4 mm, and all of
their surfaces were ground using SiC metallurgical abrasive
papers to 2000 grit size and then ultrasonically cleaned in
ethanol. Isothermal oxidation tests were conducted at 873 and
1073 K in air. Sample weight gains were measured to evaluate
the extent of oxidation. Cylindrical specimens of Φ 6.0 mm ×
9.0 mm were machined from the ingots by means of electrical
discharge machining for compression testing. Compression tests
were performed using an Instron 8860 mechanical testing
machine under an engineering strain rate of 1 × 10
−3 s−1. At least
three specimens for each alloy were tested to ensure measure-
ment reliability.
The crystalline structures of the alloys were analyzed by X-ray
diffraction (XRD) using a Bruker D8 Advance instrument with
monochromatic Cu Kα radiation. The microstructures of the
alloys were examined in a scanning electron microscope (SEM;
Quanta 650, FEI) equipped with energy-dispersive X-ray spec-
troscopy (EDS), a backscattered electron (BSE), and an electron
backscatter diffraction (EBSD) detector. Samples for transmis-
sion electron microscopy (TEM; FEI-Titan-G2) analysis were
fabricated by means of ion milling using a GATAN PIPS II 695
instrument.

values are much lower than those of many high-temperature

single-crystal superalloys (~8.9 g cm −3) [8].
Mechanical properties
Fig. 2a shows the engineering compressive stress-strain curves of
the two alloys at room temperature. The Ti 2VZrNb alloy showed
a yield strength of 769 MPa, and the Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy
showed a yield strength of 1273 MPa. Both alloys exhibited large
compressive strains of >50%. Fig. 2b presents a comparison of
the room-temperature yield strengths and mass densities of a
number of RHEAs. Ti
2VZrNb and Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 are among
the few lightweight RHEAs with the lowest mass densities [3,38–
40]. Fig. 2c shows a comparison of the specific yield strengths
and compressive fracture strains of these RHEAs. The two alloys
exhibited the best compressive plasticity of all RHEAs shown.

The Ti2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy also exhibited a specific yield strength
comparable to that of all the RHEAs. Other Al-containing
lightweight RHEAs all show low plasticity. This is caused mostly
by the formation of brittle second phases, such as the Laves
phase in AlCrNbTaV [41], μ phase in AlCrMoNbTi [42], and
Al3Zr5 intermetallic phase in AlNbTiVZr [43]. These second
phase inclusions promote nucleation of microcracks at the
interphase boundaries. The Ti
2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy avoided this
problem by having a single phase structure.
Oxidation behavior
Fig. S2 presents the oxidation mass gains of the two alloys after
thermal exposure in air at 873 and 1073 K. It is seen that
Ti2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 experienced a much lower oxidation weight
gain than Ti 2VZrNb (Fig. S2a, b). The oxidation resistance
benefit of Al addition is significant, especially at lower tem-
peratures. The mass gain of the Ti
2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy after 100 h
of exposure at 873 K is only 5.5% of that of the Ti 2VZrNb alloy.
Fig. S2c, d show images of the two alloys after 100 h of exposure
at 873 K. The Ti 2VZrNb alloy showed severe pest effect damage
[17]. The Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy exhibited no such effect, sug-
gesting a protective oxide surface film.
Fig. 3 shows an SEM analysis of the oxide layer formed on the
Ti2VZrNb alloy after thermal exposure. Fig. 3a shows the cross-
section of the sample after exposure at 873 K for 10 h. The
sample surface exhibited two layers, i.e., oxide layer I and oxy-
gen-ingress layer II, in the base metal above unaffected matrix
III. The oxide layer has poor adhesion to the base metal. Fig. 3b
shows another sample after exposure at 1073 K for 2 h. It is
evident that the thicknesses of both the oxide layer and the
oxygen-ingress layer have increased significantly after exposure
at 1073 K (2 h) compared with that at 873 K (10 h).
Fig. 3c shows the cross section of the Ti
2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy
after exposure at 873 K for 10 h. The oxygen-ingress layer is of
similar thickness to that in the Ti 2VZrNb alloy at <10 μm, but
the oxide layer is much thinner, approximately 2–3 μm thick.
The thin oxide layer also seems to have perfect adhesion to the
base metal. Fig. 3d shows the alloy oxidized at 1073 K for 2 h.
The inset shows an enlarged view of the oxidized layer. The
oxidized surface has two sublayers, i.e., a dense inner oxide layer
I and a rough “needle-like” outer layer IV. The dense inner layer
is 7–8 μm thick and has perfect adhesion to the base metal. The
oxygen ingress layer II has become much thicker than that at
873 K, to ~90 μm. In addition, some image contrasts were
observed in layers II and III after exposure at 873 and 1073 K,
suggesting some internal phase changes.
Composition analysis was also conducted on the surface layers
of the samples. Fig. S3 shows a magnified SEM-BSE image and
corresponding EDS point analysis of surface layer I on the
Ti
2VZrNb alloy after exposure at 1073 K for 2 h. Four distinctive
regions may be recognized based on image contrast. As seen in
the data, Region (1) is rich in Zr (brighter contrast), Region (2)
is rich in Ti and V (darker contrast), Region (3), the grey matrix
background, is rich in Nb and Ti, and the needle-like structure
in Region (4) is rich in Zr and Nb (brighter contrast). This
observation suggests that the oxidation of the Ti 2VZrNb alloy is
nondiscriminatory among the elements and that the oxide
formed is a mixture of oxides. Fig. S4 presents an EDS element
mapping analysis of the oxides formed on the Ti
2VZrNb0.5Al0.5
alloy after exposure at 1073 K for 2 h. The rough outer layer (IV)
is rich in Al and Zr, and the dense inner layer (I) is rich in Ti. In
addition, layer II also displayed clear inhomogeneity for Ti, Zr,
V, and Nb. The bright contrast phase appears to be rich in Zr
and V, and the dark-contrast phase appears to be rich in Ti and
Nb.

DISCUSSION
The effect of Al on mechanical strength
As presented in Fig. 2, the yield strength of the Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5
alloy is 166% of that of the Ti 2VZrNb alloy. This is attributed to
the solid solution strengthening effect. Fig. 5 shows high-angle
annular dark-field scanning transmission electron microscopy
(HAADF-STEM) images of the two alloys in the as-cast state
and atomic-scale lattice strain distribution analysis using the
peak pairs algorithm (PPA) approach. Fig. 5a, c show the high-
resolution HAADF-STEM images of the Ti 2VZrNb and
Ti2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloys, and Fig. 5b, d show the lattice strain
distribution maps for the horizontal direction (ε xx) of the
viewing areas of the Ti 2VZrNb and Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloys. It
can be seen that the lattice strain distribution in the Ti 2VZrNb
alloy is much more uniform than that in the Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5
alloy. The average lattice strains for the regions in the dashed
boxes (averaged over the seven rows of atoms contained in the
boxes) of the two alloys are plotted in Fig. 5e. It is seen that the
peak values and the general variations of the lattice strains in the
Ti2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy are clearly larger than those in the
Ti2VZrNb alloy. The mean square deviation of lattice strains is
1.890 for the Ti 2VZrNb0.5 Al0.5 alloy and 0.369 for the Ti 2VZrNb
alloy. The larger lattice strains in the Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy
imply a higher resistance to dislocation movement and a
stronger solid strengthening effect. In addition, Al is a p-element
and is known to form stronger p–d bonds with some refractory
d-elements than d–d bonds [12,28,53]. This may further
increase lattice friction for dislocation movement.

The addition of 10% Al converts the oxide scale into an Al 2O3
+ ZrO 2 outer layer (layer IV) and a TiO 2 inner layer (layer I).
Al2O3 has a low growth constant [56]. This allows and
encourages Zr to oxidize concurrently with Al to form layer IV.
The formation of the dense Al
2O3 + ZrO 2 layer (layer IV)
reduces the oxidation of Nb and V. Furthermore, the increased
lattice distortion of the Ti
2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy also slows down
the inward diffusion of oxygen [57], further reducing the oxi-
dation. This is consistent with the observation of the reduced
mass gain rate of the Ti
2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy shown in Fig. S2b.
The protectiveness of a surface oxide layer against further
oxidation is dependent on its physical integrity and its adhesion
to the base metal, as well as the Pilling-Bedworth (PB) ratio.
Fig. 3 and Fig. S4 show that the oxide scale on the Ti
2
VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy is much more intact and has much better
adhesion to the base metal, in clear contrast to that on the
Ti2VZrNb alloy. The PB ratios of the various oxides are 1.76 for
TiO2, 6.04 for Nb 2Zr6O17, 1.01 for V 2O5, and 2.27 for TiNb 2O7.
For Ti 2VZrNb, the oxide scale (layer I) contains four main
oxides, TiO 2, Nb 2Zr6O17, V 2O5, and TiNb 2O7. Based on the
stoichiometry, the gross PB ratio of the mixed oxides is esti-
mated to be 2.57. This is consistent with the observation of
extensive spalling and poor adhesion to the base metal observed
in the Ti
2VZrNb sample. For the Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy, the
addition of Al converted the surface oxide scale to an outer
Al2O3 + ZrO2 layer and an inner dense TiO 2 layer. The PB ratios
of Al 2O3 and ZrO 2 were 1.28 and 1.56, respectively. Hence, the
mixed oxides for layer IV show a desirable PB ratio in the range
of 1–2 [24], which is consistent with the observation of con-
tinuous and well-adhered layer IV.
CONCLUSIONS
A novel lightweight RHEA was designed in this study to achieve
a combination of high specific yield strength, high room-tem-
perature compressive plasticity, and good oxidation resistance.
The main conclusions are summarized as follows.
(1) Two new lightweight refractory high-entropy alloys,
Ti
2VZrNb and Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5, are designed. Both alloys are in
the single BCC phase.
(2) The Ti 2VZrNb and Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloys exhibit over
50% room-temperature compressive plasticity. The 50% sub-
stitution of the Nb content in the alloy by Al reduces the mass
densities from 5.89 to 5.44 g cm
−3 and enhances the room-
temperature yield strength from 769 to 1273 MPa. These results
translate into an increase in the specific yield strength from 130
to 234 MPa cm 3 g−1.
(3) The Ti 2VZrNb alloy was found to oxidize uniformly
among its constituent elements with a significant rate of mass
gain. The mixed oxide scale experiences excessive spallation and
has poor adhesion to the base metal. The oxides provide no
protection to the metal. With Al addition, the mixed oxide scale
is changed into a dual-layer structure consisting of an Al
2O3 +
ZrO2 outer layer and a dense TiO 2 inner layer for the
Ti2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 alloy. This oxide scale significantly reduces the
oxidation rate of the alloy.

协同提高新型轻质Ti 2VZrNb0.5Al0.5 高熵合金抗氧化
性能与力学性能
安子冰 1, 毛圣成 1*, 杨涛 2, 刘亦农 3*, 陈艳辉 1, 杨晓萌 1, 刘珊珊 1,
汪鑫 1, 邓青松 1, 张泽 1,4, 韩晓东 1*
摘要 提高抗氧化性能是难熔高熵合金高温应用的首要要求. 本研究
中, 我们将Al代替50%Nb添加到Ti 2VZrNb难熔高熵合金中, 以提高其
抗氧化性能与力学性能. 结果表明, 相比于Ti 2VZrNb合金, Ti 2VZrNb 0.5-
Al0.5 合金的抗氧化性得到了显著提升, 这是由于在合金表面形成了连
续的Al 2O3 + ZrO 2氧化层保护膜. 同时, Ti 2VZrNb 0.5Al0.5合金室温屈服
强度高达1273 MPa, 相比于Ti 2VZrNb合金强度提升66%. 结构表征说
明, Ti 2VZrNb 0.5Al0.5合金强度提升得益于其较大的固溶强化效果. 较轻
元素Al的添加使得该合金具有较低的密度(5.44 g cm −3 ), 相比于
Ti 2 VZrNb合金密度降低8.2%, 这使得该合金的比强度高达
234 MPa cm 3 g−1. 同时, Ti 2VZrNb 0.5 Al0.5 合金也具有较高的压缩塑性
(>50%). 综合这些结果, 该合金是迄今为止所报道的性能最好的难熔高
熵合金.
