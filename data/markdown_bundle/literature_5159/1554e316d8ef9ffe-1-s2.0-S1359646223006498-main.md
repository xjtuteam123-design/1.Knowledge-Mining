ABSTRACT 
The lightweight Al
3
Ti intermetallic compound shows superior properties at elevated temperatures but its room- 
temperature brittleness largely limits its usefulness. We propose Al
3
Ti-based lightweight medium-entropy alloys 
(MEAs) with selected alloying elements, which change Al
3
Ti from a tetragonal D0
22 
structure to a ductile cubic 
L1
2 
one with embedded hard B2 and D8a phases. A combination of improved ductility and strength can be 
achieved. Four lightweight AlTiCrMnFeCu MEAs ( ρ = 3.65~4.27 g/cm
3
) with tunable balance between hardness 
and ductility were fabricated. Ti and Fe promoted the formation of hard B2 phase, while Cr and Mn favored the 
formation of D8a phase which strengthened the alloy at the minor expense of ductility. The compressive yield 
and peak strengths of the MEAs e.g., Al

20

5

respectively, with its fracture strain around 17 %. This study provides useful information for designing Al
3

based lightweight MEAs with desirable properties. 
Al-Ti alloys are the most attractive candidate materials for light -
weight components used at elevated temperatures due to their superior 
high-temperature strength, oxidation and corrosion resistance [1] . 
Among Al-Ti alloys, the intermetallic alloy Al
3
Ti displays many advan -
tages, e.g., low density (~3.36 g/cm
3
), high Young ’ s modulus (~216 
GPa), high melting point (~1350 
◦
C), and high oxidation resistance 
[2 – 5] . However, the main deformation mode of Al
3
Ti at room temper -
ature is reported to be (111)[112] twinning in the D0
22 
structure [6] and 
its room-temperature brittleness is generally considered to be caused by 
its low-symmetry tetragonal D0
22 
structure, which largely limits its 
usefulness and practical applications. 
In addition to the grain refinement [7] , three methods are usually 
used to improve the properties of Al
3
Ti intermetallic alloy. First method 
is to add a third element, such as Cr, Mn, Fe, Ni, Cu, Zn, Ag, or V, etc., 
[8 – 13] to substitute Al atoms in Al
3
Ti [14] , leading to a transformation 
from tetragonal D0
22 
to cubic L1
2
. The cubic L1
2 
structure has more slip 
systems for plastic deformation, but it has lower yield strength, gener -
ally less than 500 MPa during compression. Second method is to make 
metallic-intermetallic alloys by mixing Al
3
Ti-based intermetallics with, 
e.g., more Al [ 5 , 15 ] or Ti [ 16 , 17 ], which usually requires several 
fabrication steps, but the achieved improvement is limited. Third 
method is to introduce the dispersive second phases into the L1
2
-struc -
ture Al
3
Ti-based ternary alloys, such as TiB
2 
[18] and SiC [19] , etc., to 
improve room-temperature properties. However, this method is also 
limited due to extra efforts in achieving homogeneous distribution of 
dispersive particles. 
Inspired by the previous studies, we propose a different approach by 
adding excessive elements into Al
3
Ti, attempting not only to transform 
tetragonal D0
22 
structure into cubic L
12 
structure for increased ductility, 
but also for forming hard secondary phases to compensate the loss of 
strength due to the transition from the D0
22 
structure to the ductile cubic 
one. With this approach, a good strength-ductility combination could be 
achieved. From perspective of cost-effectiveness, we selected Cr, Mn, Fe 
and Cu as ternary elements and fabricated the planned alloys using an 
arc melting process. The room-temperature compressive mechanical 
properties of the alloys were investigated, which are also compared with 
those of some lightweight alloys reported in the literature. Relevant 
mechanisms for the microstructure development and corresponding

3
Ti- 
based medium-entropy alloy, Al-Ti-Cr-Mn-Fe-Cu alloy, with impressive 
room-temperature strength-ductility combination. The alloy is achieved 
by adding Cr, Mn, Fe and Cu alloying elements to form (Al, M)
3
Ti type 
L1
2 
matrix phase with harder B2 and D8a secondary phases. Excessive 
Fe is segregated in B2 phase while excessive Cr and Mn are concentrated 
in D8a phase. Dense slip bands in L1

that the L1

strengthen the alloy due to its higher hardness and some deformability. 
D8a phase can be further adjusted in content and morphology for further 
tuning the combination of strength and ductility.
