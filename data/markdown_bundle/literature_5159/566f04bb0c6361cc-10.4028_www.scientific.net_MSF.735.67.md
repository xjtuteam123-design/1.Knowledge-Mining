Evaluation of Plastic Workability Limit of Magnesium Alloy by 
Cavitations in High temperature Uni and Biaxial Test

Keywords: cavitation, multiaxial alternative forging, grain boundary sliding, biaxial deformation, allowable

Abstract. This study examined the critical plastic formability limit of a fine-structure AZ31 magnesium alloy 
plate under warm and high temperature based on the strength of a magnesium alloy that has cavities at room 
temperature. The cyclic hot free-forging process as pre-form working following rolling at a light reduction 
ratio fabricated a fine-structure AZ31 magnesium alloy plate. The appearance of the cavities was examined in 
detail together with changes in the structure and p reparation methods before further damage at high 
temperatures with increasing uni-and biaxial plastic deformation. The allowable deformation limit in the 
super plasticity process can be estimated from the strength of the deformed material and forming limit 
diagram (FLD) at room temperature. During high-temperature deformation, cavities are produced by stress 
concentrations at grain boundary triple points and striation bands due to grain boundary sliding. The 
cavitations growth behavior is dependent upon deformation conditions, and a high percentage of large 
cavities occupy the sample surface as a large amount of grain boundary sliding is present, i.e., as uniform 
elongation grows larger, the cavity size also increases. In a case where 200% uniaxial strain was applied to a 
fine-grained structure material at a temperature of 623K under a strain rate of 10 -4s-1, the tensile strength at 
room temperature decreased about 13%, and elongation was 10% less, compared with that of a material to 
which no load was applied due to the influence of cavities. In a case of biaxial deformation, the values were 
28% lower. It is possible to draw a FLD based on the cavity incidence fraction. 
Introduction 
As magnesium alloy is the lightest metal in practical use, it is an attractive material in terms of offering 
lightweight solutions for vehicles and other transport equipment, and it is seen as a material that contributes to 
weight saving in industrial commodities and lessens environmental problems [1]. Until now, a great deal of 
research into the superplastic forming of magnesium under high temperature has been carried out [2-5]. The 
main focus of such research is the development of high-strength and flame-retardant magnesium alloys, and 
to aid this, an understanding of the relation betwe en the mechanical properties, such as the uniaxial 
deformation behavior at room and high temperature, and the microstructure, as well as an elucidation of the 
superplastic deformation mechanism was examined. 
 However, research is required that connects work forming conditions and structure control and that also 
examines the superplastic-materials processing limit from the position of guaranteeing an allowable design 
strength of processing parts that carry out the superplastic deformation processing. The presence of cavities in 
superplastic-formed material adversely affects the mechanical properties. 
 Therefore, studies of cavitation 
growth and coalescence behavior in superplastic deformation of magnesium alloy have been reported [7-10].

In this paper, to refine the structure of extruded AZ31 magnesium alloy as-received material, a simple 
forging process was applied. By using microstructure materials roll formed under a light reduction rate after 
hot free forging with a small number of passes, the strength of this magnesium alloy at room temperature was 
ascertained after superplastic deformation was carried out at high temperature. The structural and damage 
changes coinciding with increasing uniaxial plastic deformation at high temperature, especially the 
appearance of cavities accompanying these structure, were examined in detail. In addition, we performed an 
intensity evaluation of materials with cavities at room temperature after the relation with processing 
conditions and processing is associated, and the amount of allowable superplastic deformation processing 
was estimated by drawing a forming limit diagram (FLD) based on the cavity incidence fraction.

Figure 2 shows stress- strain behavior of this sample at 623K as a function of strain rate. In order to examine 
the tensile properties at room temperature of samples formed during plastic deformation at temperatures 
573K-623K, large dimensions specimens were used in uni and biaxial tests. After high temperature tests, 
small tensile specimens were cut from these large specimens that contained cavities, and tensile tests were 
carried out at room temperature. 
 To examine the effect of grain boundary sliding and intergranular deformation on macroscopic deformation, 
grain boundary sliding was measured from the shifts angle of scratching lines on the specimen surface. The 
transgranular irregular deformation was calculated from the aspect ratio of the length of the diagonal of the 
Vickers indentation in the grain [12,13]. The size distribution and area ratio of the cavities were then observed 
using an optical microscope and a field emission scanning electron microscope. Cavities in the center of the 
gauge length of the sample were observed within an area of about 2mm 2.

Conclusion 
 As a result of examining the appearance behavior of cavities and the influence of cavities on the mechanical 
properties of material caused by the superplastic deformation of AZ31 magnesium alloy , the following 
conclusion was obtained. 
1) By the combination of three-pass MAF and roll forming with a light reduction ratio at a warm temperature, 
a uniform microstructure material could be produced. At a test temperature of 573K-623K, however, 
superplastic deformation was observed at the low strain rate of 10 
-3 to 10 -4s-1. 
2) During uniaxial deformation at high temperature, cavities occurred in the grain boundary parallel to the 
tensile direction and were produced by grain boundary sliding. However, for biaxial deformation, the 
initiation and growth of cavities can be observed in many directions. 
3) Materials showed a large total elongation that was larger than the amount of grain boundary sliding, which 
is occupied by a larger percentage of the surface cavities that are also a major. 
4) For sample subject to uniaxial strain 200% at 623K and 2.8×10 -4s-1, the tensile strength at room 
temperature were approximately 10% lower than for same heat treatment with no loading. However, in 
bi-axial deformation, the strength was 28% lower for a 50% equivalent strain loading, and the flow stress 
was higher than that in uniaxial deformation, while the elongation was lower. Therefore, the tensile 
strength at room temperature due to the influence of cavities decreased significantly compared with that in 
uniaxial deformation.
