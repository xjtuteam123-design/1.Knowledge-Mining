ABSTRACT 
Precipitation-hardened high/medium entropy alloys (PH-HEAs/MEAs) have attracted extensive interest due to 
their excellent mechanical properties at both room and high temperatures. Strengthening mechanism models can 
be used to guide the discovery and design of new alloys, however, the parameters of the models are often lacking,

1. Introduction 
High-performance structural materials are highly desired for a wide 
range of engineering applications, such as aerospace, transportation, 
and energy sectors. Compared to conventional alloys, high-entropy al -
loys/medium-entropy alloys (HEAs/MEAs) have been demonstrated to 
show preferable properties due to the unique alloying principles [ 1 – 3 ]. 
These properties are often obtained by the advisable manipulation of 
compositions and associated microstructures such as precipitation of L1
2 
phase [ 4 , 5 ]. As a result, composition-based precipitation hardening 
HEAs/MEAs (PH-HEAs/MEAs) are being of particular interest to opti -
mize mechanical properties including yield strength (YTS) [ 6 – 11 ]. 
However, the main obstacle lies in the almost infinite unexplored 
compositional space. Effective strategies to guide the design of 
PH-HEAs/MEAs are in high demand. 
Strengthening mechanism models, including solid solution 
strengthening [ 12 ], grain boundary strengthening [ 13 ], dislocation 
strengthening, and precipitation strengthening [ 14 ], have been 
commonly used to calculate the yield strength. Beyond the calculations, 
insights can be gained by evaluating the contributions from different 
strengthening mechanisms, which are important for understanding the 
mechanical behavior and present clues for developing new alloys. 
However, such clues are often posteriori and qualitative. For example, 
based on the investigation of precipitation strengthening in the 
PH-HEAs/MEAs, high volume fraction of L1
2 
phase, beneficial for me -
chanical properties, can be obtained by doping more Ni, Al and Ti ele -
ments [ 15 , 16 ]. Such posteriori and qualitative knowledges/information 
are not able to enable us to directly use strengthening mechanism 
models to guide new alloys design, due to the following limitations. 
First, the one-to-one mapping from composition to parameters of the 
strengthening models is missing; Second, for unknown alloys, the pa -
rameters of strengthening models are unavailable. 
In this study, we employed machine learning (ML) tools to tackle the 
aforementioned limitations. ML has proven successful in assisting ma -
terials discovery recently [ 17 – 19 ]. It has also been applied to the design 
of HEAs/MEAs, including the high-performance as-cast HEAs [ 20 ], the 
new eutectic HEAs [ 21 ], and the control of the volume fraction of 
nanoprecipitates [ 22 ]. According to the precipitation strengthening 
studies in previous reports [ 7 , 23 ], the change of the yield strength re -
lates to the volume fraction and the antiphase boundary energy of the 
precipitated phase. This motivates us to predict these two key parame -
ters from composition by ML, so that the strengthening models-based

3.2. Tensile properties 
The yield strength of the designed alloys was measured by tensile 
testing. The stress ‒ strain curves of the designed Al7Ti7 and Al5Ti8 
samples are shown in Fig. 5 (a). Both alloys exhibit high yield strengths 
up to gigapascal levels. The YTS of Al5Ti8 is approximately 1.15 GPa, 
which is higher than that of the Al7Ti7 alloy (1.04 GPa). Fig. 5 (b) 
compares the yield strength of the designed alloys to reported ones. All 
the alloys are PH-HEAs/MEAs obtained under similar process conditions 
(aging on the basis of fully recrystallized grains). Clearly, the YTS of the 
reported alloys are below 1 GPa, while the yield strength of the designed 
alloys reaches 1.15 GPa, 10% higher than the best existing L1
2
- 
strengthened AlTiCoCrFeNi HEAs (1.03 GPa). 
The ductility of designed alloys is less than that of reported PH- 
HEAs/MEAs [ 9 , 32 ]. The corresponding fracture morphologies are 
shown in Figs. S3(a) and (b) . Both samples show typical brittle inter -
granular fracture characteristics. Cracks can be observed to propagate 
along grain boundaries, exposing entire stone-like grains. These 
premature fractures may be attributed to the higher volume fraction of 
the L1
2 
phase in the alloy and the inability of the matrix to generate 
sufficient dislocation slip to promote the synergistic deformation be -
tween grains. Compared with the Al5Ti8 alloy, the larger elongation of 
the Al7Ti7 alloy indicates a good deformation ability. This arises from 
the low anti-phase boundary energy particles that are more easily cut by 
dislocations.
