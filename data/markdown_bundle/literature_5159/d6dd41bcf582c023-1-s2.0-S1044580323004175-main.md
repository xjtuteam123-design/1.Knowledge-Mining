ABSTRACT 
The microstructure and mechanical properties of two nitrogen (N) modified Ni-Fe base superalloys subjected to 
long-term thermal exposure were studied. It was found that the M
23
C
6 
phase precipitated discretely at the grain 
boundary even after thermal exposure at 700
◦
C for 7004 h. The inhibition of the continuous precipitation of 
M
23
C
6 
can be attributed to the enhanced formation of intergranular M(C, N) phase due to addition of N. The 
coarsening of γ’ phase during the thermal exposure was found to be gentle in the present Ni-Fe alloys, as 
compared with those without N. The mechanical properties of the Ni-Fe alloys remained relatively stable after 
thermal exposure at 700
◦
C for up to 7004 h, which can be related to the suppressed γ’ coarsening and discrete 
precipitation of M
23
C
6 
at the grain boundary owning to the addition of N. 
1. Introduction 
To improve the thermal efficiencies and reduce the emission of 
greenhouse-gas from coal-fired power generation, the next generation 
advanced ultra-supercritical (A-USC) boiler has been designed with 
steam parameters up to 700 – 760
◦
C/35 MPa [ 1 – 4 ]. Ni-base superalloys 
with preferable creep-rupture strength and oxidation resistance are 
regarded as candidate materials to meet the high-performance re -
quirements at service condition [ 5 , 6 ]. However, the application of Ni- 
base superalloys remains limited due to high price and inferior pro -
cessing capability [ 7 , 8 ]. Hence, Ni-Fe base superalloys have been pro -
posed due to their lower cost and better workability [ 9 – 13 ]. Trace 
elements were found to have significant effects on the microstructure 
and mechanical properties of Ni-Fe base superalloys, e.g., B and C can 
play effective roles in strengthening the grain boundary [ 14 , 15 ]. For the 
application and optimizing the alloy design of the Ni-Fe base superal -
loys, it is urgently needed to study the stability of microstructures and 
properties under thermal exposure conditions, with specially an un -
derstanding of the influence of trace elements. 
Nitrogen (N), as usually a trace element in superalloys, reveals low 
solubility in Ni-base superalloys and nitrides can be easily formed during 
solidification since superalloys contain strong nitride forming elements 
(such as Ti, V, Nb, Hf, Ta), thus affecting the microstructure and prop -
erties of superalloys [ 16 – 18 ]. In cast Ni-base and Co-base superalloys, it 
is generally regarded that the increase of N content will affect the area 
percentage of micro porosity, thereby adversely affect the ductility and 
tensile strength of superalloys [ 18 – 20 ]. However, in Ni-Fe-base super -
alloys, Liu et al. [ 21 ] reported that the moderate increase of N content 
would contribute to form discrete M
23
C
6
, thereby reducing grain 
boundary cracking and resulting in improvement of plasticity of the 
alloy. Previous studies [ 18 – 21 ] have been mainly focused on the effect 
of N on the grain boundary precipitation and mechanical properties of 
cast and short-time aged superalloys. Under high temperature service 
conditions, different microstructure evolutions will occur in Ni-Fe base 
superalloys, including MC phase decomposition, continuous secondary 
carbides precipitation, γ’ coarsening and topologically close-packed 
(TCP) phase formation, which are often deleterious to the service life 
of high temperature boilers [ 22 – 24 ]. The influence of N on the micro -
structure and properties of the Ni-Fe base superalloys will also depend 
on other alloying elements. Moreover, the effect of N on the

2. Experimental 
Two newly-developed Ni – Fe base superalloys were investigated in 
this study. The chemical composition (wt%) of two alloys are 42.0 Fe, 
15.0 Cr, 1.6 W + Mo + Co, 3.6 Ti + Al, 0.04 C, 0.003 B, 0.0011 N with the 
balance Ni (GH4070R(HT700R)), and 30.0 Fe, 20.0 Cr, 1.0 W + Mo +
Co, 3.8 Ti + Al, 0.05 C, 0.002 B, 0.0015 N with the balance Ni (GH4650T

3.2.2. Primary MX phase 
Fig. 3 shows the primary MX phase characteristics of alloy A1 after 
solution treatment and long-term thermal exposure. After solution

( Fig. 3 a). During long-term thermal exposure at 700
◦

Fig. 3 b), the granular precipitates are identified as Ti, C, N-rich car -
bonitride. The selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern provides 
further demonstration of the cubic Ti(C, N) phase ( Fig. 3 c). Compared 
with MC type carbide formed in the superalloy without N addition 
[ 11 , 25 ], it is obvious that addition of N leads to the formation of M(C, N) 
carbonitride. Degeneration of primary MC phase can lead to the initia -
tion and propagation of cracks under service condition [ 23 ]. The M(C, 
N) phase formed in the present alloy presents good thermal stability, 
which is beneficial to the property stability of the Ni-Fe base superalloy. 
As shown in the following results of Fig. 4 , similar results were also 
found in alloy A2. 
As shown in Fig. 5 , it was found that some MX particles present 
uneven element distribution, showing a core-shell structure. Combined 
with the EDS results, it can be confirmed that these blocky phases are 
mainly composed of Ti, W, C, N, in which W and C elements are 
concentrated at the shells of MX precipitates. The preferentially for -
mation of TiN in the alloy may lead to such complex core-shell structure 
[ 21 ]. Similar phenomenon has been reported in superalloys with N 
addition [ 21 , 26 ]. Interestingly, such core-shell structure can exist 
throughout the long-term exposure at 700
◦

discovered in previous studies. However, the influence of MX phase with 
such core-shell structure still needs further studies.
