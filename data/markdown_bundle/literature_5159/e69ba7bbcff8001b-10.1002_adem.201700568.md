Nucleation Crystallography of Ni Grains on CrFeNb
Inoculants Investigated by Edge-to-Edge Matching
Model in an IN718 Superalloy
Wenchao Yang,* Pengfei Qu, Lin Liu, Ziqi Jie, Taiwen Huang, Feng Wang,
and Jun Zhang

Edge Matching model. Three distinguishable orientation relationships

grain refinement at the atomic level is further identified.
1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys servicing at medium and low temperature
condition are always used to fabricate the disc of aeroengine
turbine and low pressure turbine blade.
[1] The operating
temperature of these casting components is usually below
760
/C14 C. In this range, a uniform and ﬁne grain size is desirable to
promote the fatigue properties and reduce the cast defects in Ni-
based superalloys.
[2] Currently, the most widely used approach to
achieve a re ﬁned structure is inoculation by adding nuclei to
liquid.[3] One of the important researches to consider in
understanding grain re ﬁnement is the potency of the nuclei. [4]
Generally, the potency is related to the interfacial free energy,
which drives the phase transformation from liquid to solid. In
order to minimize this interfacial free energy, a maximum atom
matching across the interface with a
speciﬁc orientation relationship (OR) is
required to enhance the nucleation pro-
cess, such as, TiB
2 particles for Al alloys
and Zr particles for Mg alloys without Al
addition, respectively.
[5,6] Clearly, the crys-
tallographic atomic matching between the
particles and the metal matrix is very
important in estimating the nucleation
potency of inoculation.
Several models have been proposed to
describe the atom matching between the
grain re ﬁner and the solid substrate.
[7,8]
One of the most successful models is the
Edge-to-Edge Matching model (E2EM).[9,10]
It has been demonstrated as a powerful tool
in understanding the grain re ﬁnement
efﬁciency and developing new grain re ﬁn-
ers through calculating the crystallographic
matching between inoculants and matrix.
In general, the essential condition for an
effective grain re ﬁner is to achieve the maximum atomic
matching between the inoculants and the matrix across the
interface. T o fulﬁll this requirement, the E2EM model speci ﬁes
that there should be at least one pair of close-packed (CP) atomic
rows, along which the interatomic spacing misﬁt( f
r) between the
particles and the matrix should be less than 10%. And, the
matching rows must be contained in at least one pair of CP
planes that have the interplanar spacing ( d-spacing) mismatch
(f
d) less than 10%. Finally, the relative nucleation potency of
grain reﬁners can be evaluated in terms of the values of fr and fd.
Unlike Al or Mg alloys, the grain re ﬁnement of Ni-based
superalloys is more dif ﬁcult because of their complicated
composition, high melting point, and melted in a vacuum
condition. Therefore, the key is how to select the proper
inoculants with a high effectiveness to promote the nucleation
without producing inclusions in melt. Although some inocu-
lants had been found for Nimonic90, IN713LC, and MAR-M246
alloys, these inoculants (mainly metal oxide and borides) as crack
sources were harmful for the plasticity and fatigue properties of
superalloys.
[11,12] Therefore, Liu proposed some selected criteria
for inoculants in 2005[13]: (i) They were composed of the alloying
elements in superalloys, such as Fe, Ni, Nb, or Cr; (ii) Their
melting point should be higher than 1400
/C14 C, which was above
the liquids of Ni-based superalloy; (iii) The difference of density
between reﬁner and the Ni-based superalloy should be as small

2. Experimental Section
E2EM model was used to calculate the crystallographic matching
between two phases and to predict their OR. Crystal Maker
software was used to construct an interface structure based on
the predict results. The experimental IN718 superalloy was used
to investigate the grain re ﬁnement effect. The IN718 superalloy
ingot was melted in a vacuum induction furnace. After the ingot
was melted, the liquid melt was kept at 1380
/C14 C for 1 min and
then 0.1% CrFeNb intermetallic particles were added into the
melt. After that, the melt was poured into a preheated ceramic
mold with 900
/C14 C and 110 mm in length and 30 mm in diameter.
Finally, the as-cast grains of the etched samples were examined
using Leica DM4000 optical microscopy. The grain size was
measured by the linear intercept method at the center of
transverse section. The specimens were etched in the solution of
5 g CuSO
4, 3.4 ml H 2SO4, and 50 ml HCl.

E2EM matching model as a powerful tool was applied to
explain the heterogeneous nucleation mechanism of CrFeNb
particles on Ni grains in Ni-based superalloys. The crystallo-
graphic calculation predicted that there were three distinguish-
able ORs between CrFeNb intermetallic particles and Ni
grains: 1 /C2210/C138

1/C2210/C138½ Ni//[0001]CrFeNb, (111)Ni 0.72/C14 from 20 /C2220Þð CrFeNb. They

experimentally con ﬁrm these predicted ORs.
