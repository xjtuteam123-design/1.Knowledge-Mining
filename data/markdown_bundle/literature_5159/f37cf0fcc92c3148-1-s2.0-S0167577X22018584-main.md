1. Introduction 
With the increasing demand for lightweight aero-engine components 
in the thermo-mechanical coupling environment, the traditional Al-Cu 
aluminum alloy has been difficult to meet the requirements of its 
work. The main strengthening phase in the traditional Al-Cu alloy is the 
θ -Al
2
Cu phase, but the metastable phase of the coherent or semi- 
coherent phase rapidly changes to the non-coherent stable phase at a 
high temperature above 250 ℃ , which makes it difficult to nail the grain 
boundary, so the high-temperature performance of the alloy is reduced 
[1] . As a result, the maximum tensile strength of the traditional Al-Cu 
alloy at 350 ℃ is only 75 MPa. 
Researchers in Al-Cu alloy add some transition elements and rare 
earth elements, such as Zr, Sc, Ti, Mn, Ni, V, and Er, metastable sediment 
can be stable to a higher temperature to suppress the coarsening and 
transformation, to improve the high-temperature performance [2 – 8] . 
The metal Ni element plays a prominent role in improving the high- 
temperature performance of the aluminum alloy. When Ni element is 
added to Al-Cu alloy, Al

4

the high-temperature performance of the aluminum alloy. A. Shyam [9] 
improved the microstructure stability of cast Al-Cu alloy at a high 
temperature by adding Mn, Zr, Ni, and other elements. The test showed 
that the composition and microstructure formed by microalloying 
allowed the cast Al-Cu alloy to maintain its strength at 350

strain is 9 % at room temperature, and the alloy still maintains high 
strength at 300 ℃ . 
This research adopts the low-pressure sand mold casting method 
prepared Al6.5Cu2Ni0.5Zr0.3Ti0.25V alloy and heat treatment under 
the condition of T7. Analysis Al6.5Cu2Ni0.5Zr0.3Ti0.25V alloy micro -
structure, tensile tests have been carried out under the 350 ℃ , analyzes 
the relations between high-temperature mechanical properties and 
microstructure.

diagram of the alloy. The growth orientations of the alloy are different 
with different grain colors, red represents the direction [0 0 1], blue 
represents the direction [1 1 1], and the green represents the direction 
[1 0 1]. Generally speaking, the color distribution of the samples is 
relatively uniform, indicating that the preferred orientation of grain 
growth is weak. To further determine the texture strength, the {1 0 0} 
polar diagram of the sample was analyzed, and the result was shown in 
Fig. 1 (c). The highest intensity of the polar diagram of the {1 0 0} crystal 
plane was only 3.65, and the high-intensity area was distributed more 
dispersed. Compared with the standard polar projection diagram, there 
was no obvious texture orientation. Fig. 1 (d) shows that grains are 
concentrated in the direction parallel to [1 1 1], with a maximum 
orientation density of 2.21. Fig. 1 (e) shows the distribution law of the 
grain orientation difference of the alloy. Although there is no obvious 
preferred orientation of the grains, the distribution of grain orientation 
difference is not irregular, and the distribution range is wide, but mainly 
concentrated in the range of small Angle grain boundary (less than15
