ABSTRACT 
Introducing Laves phase into refractory multi-principal elements alloys (RMPEAs) is a new exploration direction 
to improve the alloy strength. In this work, we have prepared a series of (TiZr)
65-X
Nb
15
Mo

multi-principal elements alloys, by the addition of Cr to introduce the Laves phase for excellent high-temperature 
performance. The microstructure, phase constituents, and mechanical properties of the refractory high-entropy 
alloy were studied in the present work. Most of these alloys exhibited typical dendrite morphology, except for the 
alloy Cr5. Excessive Cr results in the formation of TiZr-enriched zones. In addition, twins can appear in as-cast 
alloys as a result of a significant lattice mismatch between the matrix and the Laves phase, which is caused by the

deformation reveal that small-sized Laves phases containing nano-twins precipitate in the matrix. The small-sized 
Laves phases and nano-twins restrict the movement of dislocations, leading to a large amount of dislocation 
accumulation, which improves the strength and plasticity of the alloy. Therefore, the current work on designing 
novel alloys provides new ideas for developing RMPEAs with synergistic strength and plasticity. 
1. Introduction 
There are currently many ongoing efforts to develop the next light -
weight high-temperature materials that can endure extreme tempera -
tures and potentially replace traditional TiAl alloys. Multi-principal 
element alloys (MPEAs) have emerged as a highly promising category of 
materials [ 1 , 2 ], drawing significant attention in recent times as a novel 
type of structural material [ 3 , 4 ], and their emergence has provided ideas 
for the development of lightweight high-temperature alloys. 
The combination of high mixing entropy and slow atomic diffusion 
effect [ 5 , 6 ] results in MPEAs having excellent comprehensive proper -
ties, such as a combination of strength and ductility [ 7 – 10 ], impressive 
high-temperature stability [ 11 , 12 ], and outstanding corrosion resis -
tance [ 13 , 14 ]. Refractory multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs), an 
essential branch of MPEAs, were proposed by Senkov in 2010 [ 15 ]. It 
mainly consists of transition metals, such as Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Mo, and 
W [ 16 , 17 ]. These alloys exhibit compressive yield strengths exceeding 
400 MPa at 1600 
◦
C, exemplified by well-known alloys like WNbTaMo 
and WNbTaMoV [ 15 ]. However, their limited ductility at room tem -
perature [ 18 ] has prompted the development of various design strate -
gies, including HfNbTaTiZr [ 19 , 20 ], TiZrHfNb [ 21 , 22 ], TiZrHfTa [ 23 ] 
and TiZrNbV [ 24 , 25 ]. Unfortunately, the strategy of achieving 
strength-ductility synergy with high-cost/density Hf, Ta, and W 
enhancement, presents a challenge for their use as structural materials in

The properties of strength and ductility are vital for metallic mate -
rials [ 26 ]. To achieve exceptional overall properties, traditional mate -
rials rely on various strengthening mechanisms, like solid solution, grain 
refinement, and precipitation strengthening [ 27 , 28 ]. Additionally, 
component modulation is a straightforward and efficient method for 
creating new alloys and changing their mechanical properties [ 26 ]. For 
instance, Wang et al. [ 29 ] developed a novel HfNbTaTiZrW RMPEAs by 
introducing W to the HfNbTaTiZr alloy. This addition significantly 
enhanced the room-temperature yield strength and compressive strain 
of the alloy, while also maintaining high-temperature performance 
stability, with a yield strength of 345 MPa at 1473K. Another example is 
the HfMoNbTaTiZr alloy, which is created by adding the Mo element to 
the HfNbTaTiZr alloy. This alloy has a yield strength of 556 MPa at 
1473K, which is more than six times higher than the original value [ 30 ]. 
However, adding Mo and W also dramatically increased the density and 
reduced the plasticity of the alloy. Therefore, the search for alternative 
elements to include in RHEAs is crucial for their practical utilization.

and valence electron concentration (VEC) in the Laves phase of transi -

most stable for 3.4 ≤ VEC < 4.9 and 6.5 ≤ VEC < 7.7, while C14 is optimal

the rule of the mixture is applied, which aligns with Johannes et al. ’ s 
discovery [ 52 ]. Fig. 7 (e) shows the HRTEM image of the interface be -

shows a noticeable mismatch at the interface. The yellow boxed site in 
Fig. 7 (e)is depicted in the Inverse Fast Fourier Transform (IFFT) image,

addition, the interfacial stress between the matrix and the Laves phase

severe lattice distortion. Therefore, the formation of twins in the as-cast 
alloy may have resulted from the substantial lattice mismatch between 
the matrix and Laves. This mismatch occurred due to the excessive 
cooling rate during the melting process. Additionally, the Laves phase is 
FCC structure with low stacking fault energy, which is conducive to the 
formation of twins.

To gain insight into the deformation behavior of this high-

During the deformation process of the Cr15 alloy with a 10% strain, 
it was observed that numerous dislocations became entangled and 
plugged around the small-sized Laves phase. Despite the dislocation 
being slightly curled, the positional dislocations can still be identified, 
such as dislocations I and II marked in Fig. 8 . The dislocation within the 
BCC crystal structure has a typical Burgers vector of < 111 > , which can 
be determined using the principle of invisibility of g ⋅ b = 0. Further de -

vectors of dislocation I and II are b = 1/2 [ 1 11 ] and b = 1/2 [ 11 1 ] , 
respectively. To identify dislocation types, it is necessary to analyze the 
position of the Burgers vectors under varying work vectors g . This re -
quires calculating the angles between the projections of the Burgers 
vectors in different axial zones and the work vector g . A visual depiction 
of this process can be found in Fig. 8 (i) – (k). It can be seen from Fig. 8 (b) 
(c) that the dislocations I and II are the mixed dislocations and the screw 
dislocations, respectively. The dislocation line vector u can be expressed 
as the cross product of two reciprocal lattice vectors P

different axis zones [ 53 ], thus the slip surface can be expressed as b × u . 
According to the above principle, we can obtain the slip surfaces of 
dislocations I and II as {112} and {110}, respectively. This implies that 
the primary slip systems of Cr15 alloy (at 10% strain) during the initial 
stages of deformation are predominantly {112} < 111 > and {110} <

noticeable change in the behavior of the dislocations. Initially, the dis -

and gather to form parallel slip lines. Some dislocation slip bands take on 
a wavy shape. Multiple slip systems, labeled as dislocations III-IV, can 
also be observed. According to the extinction principle g ⋅ b = 0, we 
determined that the Burgers vectors of dislocation III and IV are b = 1/2
