Abstract 
 
In this study, two similar, commercial polycrystall ine Ni-based disk superalloys (LSHR and 
ME3) were creep tested at 760 °C and 552MPa to approximately 0.3% plastic strain. LSHR 
consistently displayed superior creep properties at this stress/temperature regime even though the 
microstructural characteristics between the two all oys were comparable. High resolution 
structural and chemical analysis, however, revealed significant differences between the two 
alloys among active γ′ shearing modes involving superlattice intrinsic an d extrinsic stacking 
faults. In ME3, Co and Cr segregation and Ni and Al depletion were observed along the intrinsic 
faults - revealing a γ′ to γ phase transformation. Conversely in LSHR, an alloy with a higher W 
content, Co and W segregation was observed along th e intrinsic faults. This observation 
combined with scanning transmission electron micros copy (STEM) simulations confirm a γ′-to-
D0 19 χ phase transformation along the intrinsic faults in LSHR. Using experimental observations 
and density functional theory calculations, a novel local phase transformation strengthening 
mechanism is proposed that could be further utilize d to improve the high temperature creep 
capabilities of Ni-base disk alloys. 
 
 
Keywords: Phase transformation strengthening, Creep, STEM, EDS, Superalloys

1. Introduction 
 
To reduce CO 
2 emissions in power generation and propulsion systems, new high temperature 
capable materials are needed. Currently, Ni-base superalloys are a critical class of materials for 
high temperature applications such as in the hot section of jet turbine engines [1]. These metals 
primarily acquire their superior high temperature mechanical properties from coherent face 
centered cubic (FCC) Ni 3Al γ′ precipitates which can make up to 50-70% of their respective 
volumes [2–4]. As such, decades of research has been spent investigating the varying 
deformation processes that defeat these precipitates in a wide range of temperatures and stresses. 
[5–7]. 
 
To date, the highest operating temperature that jet turbine disks can sustain is around 700 °C [1]. 
Below this temperature the active creep deformation mechanisms primarily consist of dislocation 
glide in the softer γ channels, and anti-phase boundary (APB) shearing or dislocation looping of 
the γ′ precipitates – depending on their size and volume fraction [8]. Above 700 °C, different γ′ 
shearing deformation mechanisms become active [9,10]. Two principal faults that form in γ′ 
precipitates in association with these shearing mechanisms active between 700-800 °C are 
superlattice intrinsic and extrinsic stacking faults (SISF and SESFs) [11–15]. These faults, along 
with APB’s, can form more complex shearing modes such as microtwins and stacking fault 
ribbons [12,16–20]. To incorporate Ni-base superalloys into jet turbines that can operate within 
this higher temperature regime, these mechanisms need to be better understood.

Recent atomic-scale characterization efforts have highlighted the diffusion mechanisms that 
contribute to the formation of these γ′ fault-shearing modes. One prominent feature is the 
presence of solute segregation along the faults during creep at temperatures above 700°C. 
Initially, segregation along both SISFs and SESFs was hypothesized due to the presence of 
higher Z-contrast along the faults when imaged in a high annular angle dark field (HAADF) 
condition using a scanning transmission electron microscope (STEM) [21]. In 2015, the first 
direct observation of segregation along the SISFs in CMSX-4 and ME3 was confirmed by 
Viswanathan et al. [22]. In the years following, numerous observations have been made to 
confirm the presence of segregation along stacking faults in Co and Ni-based superalloys when 
crept at temperatures above 700 °C [23–32]. The segregation effects have only been observed in 
the ordered γ′ precipitates, not in the FCC γ matrix. Generally, these studies report segregation 
of γ-forming elements such as Co and Cr along superlattice stacking faults where it is believed 
the reduction/removal of Al-Al nearest neighbor violations helps reduce the overall stacking fault 
energy and may facilitate the shearing process [10,23,32]. 
 
Titus et al. first hypothesized that the observed segregation may also be driven by an atomic 
phase transformation along stacking faults [32]. Since then this idea has been supported by 
evidence of η phase formation along SESFs in Ni-base superalloys [31] and D0 
19 χ phase 
formation along SISFs in both Co and Ni-based superalloys [30,32,33]. In addition, some studies 
have attributed the solute segregation along faults to represent a localized γ′ to γ phase 
transformation [10,23,29]. After reviewing these segregation studies it may be concluded that 
slight changes in bulk chemistry between the varying superalloys results in significant 
differences in segregation along the faults. A study by Smith et al. was the first to explore if this

properties of the superalloys [34]. In their study, it was discovered that the nucleation of η phase

notably slower creep strain rates. 
 
In a previous effort, an alloy having very similar overall composition and microstructure to ME3 
but with altered refectory element levels, LSHR, was found to have a higher creep resistance 
[35]. However, the deformation mechanisms associated with this high creep resistance were 
unclear. The objective of this study is to determine if different chemical segregation processes 
observed along SISFs and SESFs in ME3 and LSHR may influence their overall creep 
properties. With support from density functional theory (DFT) calculations and HAADF-STEM 
simulations, it is apparent that forming the Co 
3W χ phase along SISFs correlates with enhanced 
creep resistance, suggesting that further γ′ shearing in the form of stacking fault ribbons is 
inhibited. These observations present a new local phase transformation strengthening 
mechanism that can be exploited in development of future superalloys.

2.2 Microscopy Methods 
Post-mortem STEM specimens were extracted from the gauges of the tensile creep specimens 
oriented 45 ° away from the tensile axis to investigate deformation along crystallographic planes 
edge-on. This was done by removing 3mm diameter disks through electrical discharge 
machining (EDM). The disks were polished down to a thickness of 100 µm using SiC polishing 
pads and then electro-polished using a solution of 90% methanol and 10% perchloric acid at -
30 °C and 12V. Deformation substructure analysis was performed on an FEI Talos at 200kV 
utilizing a HAADF detector at high camera lengths (200-300mm) for enhanced diffraction 
contrast [37]. Zone axis STEM imaging at this camera length enables large fields of view to be 
examined and maximizes the probability of multiple dislocation families being viewed in strong 
contrast, and thus is ideal for obtaining an overview of deformation substructures. Atomic 
resolution HAADF imaging of stacking faults was completed using a probe corrected Titan3 at 
300kV. The high resolution HAADF imaging provides atomic number (Z) contrast information 
on atomic columns, where heavier elements present brighter atomic columns [38,39]. High 
resolution energy dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (EDS) was performed using Super-X detectors 
on both a Talos and a Titan 
3 G2 microscope using the FEI Velox and Bruker Esprit software 
packages, respectively. Vertically integrated chemical line scans were quantified through Cliff-
Lorimer analysis [40] using K α energies for Al, Ni, Co, Cr, Ti, Nb, and Mo, while M α energies 
were used for Ta and W as their L α energies were convoluted by the Cu signal from the specimen 
holder.

4. Discussion 
4.1 Rate Limiting Diffusive Mechanisms during Creep Above 700 °° °°C 
 
The two alloys and microstructure conditions used in this study present a compelling case for the 
importance of elemental segregation along stacking faults, and the consequent effect on creep

ACCEPTED
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT
strength. In Section 2, it was demonstrated that the microstructural characteristics between the 
two alloys are almost identical. Despite the microstructural similarities between the two alloys, 
LSHR presents significantly better creep properties. Additionally, in an earlier study, Gabb et al . 
[42] measured the chemistries of the γ phase in LSHR and ME3 using phase extraction 
techniques. Though the amount of W and Mo were different for each alloy, the total amount of 
Mo+W in the γ phase was 9.1 and 8.8 wt%, respectively. Both of these elements are considered 
to provide solid solution strengthening in the γ matrix. Consequently, there is expected to be 
little difference in solid solution strengthening for these two alloys. Furthermore, the same study 
experimentally measured the γ/γʹ lattice misfit values for ME3 and LSHR using X-ray 
diffraction. Their analysis found the γ/γʹ lattice misfit percentage to be almost identical between 
ME3 and LSHR, 0.234% and 0.241%, respectively. Finally, as the amount of C and B in each 
alloy is almost the same, effects from grain boundary strengthening of minor phases (MC 
carbides, M 2B3 borides, etc.) should be negligible as well. Therefore, to explain the differences 
in creep strength, atomic resolution characterization of the active γʹ shearing mechanisms was 
performed. 
 
In ME3, a γ-like state (Co and Cr segregation) was found along all fault types, which is 
consistent with several previous studies [9,29,34]. Remarkably, the slight differences in alloy 
composition in LSHR (increases in W and Nb relative to ME3) resulted in entirely different 
diffusion processes along the different fault types. For the SESF, Ti and Nb segregation is 
detected in LSHR, examined with STEM EDS analysis, consistent with the formation of η phase 
along at least some parts of the fault [31]. The formation of this phase along SESFs has been 
shown to inhibit microtwin formation and improve creep properties at temperatures above 700°C

Recently, Smith et al. [29] made estimates of the rate limiting diffusion process during creep at 
760°C in ME3. The potential rate limiting processes included motion of the Cr-rich Cottrell

5. Conclusion 
This study identifies distinctly different stacking fault segregation and local phase transformation 
phenomena active following creep properties of two similar, polycrystalline Ni-base disk 
superalloys, ME3 and LSHR. From the atomic-scale STEM EDS and HAADF-STEM 
characterization of active γʹ shearing modes following creep at 760°C and 552 MPa, several 
notable conclusions can be drawn. 
 
1. 
 Significant differences between ME3 and LSHR creep strain rates exist even though their 
microstructures (grain size and precipitate structure), solute strengthening contributions, 
and γ/γʹ misfit values are all very similar. 
2. Slight differences in overall chemistry between the two alloys resulted in notable 
differences in stacking fault segregation. LSHR presented the formation of η phase and/or 
W-rich γ phase along SESFs and the Co 3W χ phase along SISFs. In contrast, ME3 
presented the formation of a Co and Cr-rich γ phase along both stacking fault types. 
3. The observed diffusion of W along SESFs presents a new rate limiting process during 
creep, by slowing the formation and extension of twins in LSHR and contributing to its 
superior creep strength. 
4. DFT VASP calculations found W and Mo segregation to γʹ Al-sites significantly raised 
APB energy while Cr segregation reduced or had little effect on the APB energy 
depending on which lattice site it segregated to. 
5. The formation of χ phase along SISFs presents a novel local phase transformation 
strengthening mechanism by inhibiting the shear of 〈112 〉 stacking fault ribbons.
