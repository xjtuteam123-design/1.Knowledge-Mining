Formation of the Laves Phase in Nb-Ti-Cr-Si-X-Based Alloys
Raghvendra Tewari 1,3 • B. Vishwanadh 1,3 • Vijay K. Vasudevan 2
Received: 7 December 2021 / Accepted: 10 January 2022 / Published online: 5 February 2022
/C211 The Indian Institute of Metals - IIM 2022
Abstract The present study reports the formation of Laves
phase in the Nb-Ti-Cr-Si-X-based alloys, which are being
considered for high-temperature applications in aerospace
engines. Depending upon the heat-treatment, various
morphologies of the Laves phases could be induced. Aging
at 1100 /C176 C led to the dissolution of the Laves precipitates
formed within matrix whereas aging at and below 1000 /C176 C
led to their copious precipitation. Crystallographic analysis
has shown that the observed Laves phase had the cubic C15
structure with crystallographic defects whose origin could
be associated to the C14 to the C15 phase transformation.
Current study aims to examine formation of precursor
phases at low ageing temperature, below the typical for-
mation temperature for the Laves phase. Detailed analysis
has shown the low-temperature phase had the orthorhombic
O-phase structure. Based on these observations, a mecha-
nism of the phase transformation, which rationalizes the
occurrence of various phases, is proposed.
Keywords Laves phase /C1C14 phase /C1O-phase
1 Introduction
A structural relationship between the parent and the
daughter phases is of fundamental importance in under-
standing the mechanism of the phase transformation
between the two phases [ 1–3]. In diffusionless transfor-
mations, e.g., martensitic transformation, a one-to-one
relationship between the atoms of the parent and the
daughter phases can be established. Such a relationship is
not only helpful in determining the amount of transfor-
mation strain but also helps in understanding the mor-
phology of the product phase [ 4]. In diffusional
transformations also, structural relationships between the
parent and the daughter phases play an important role [ 5].
However, in transformations, occurring through heteroge-
neous nucleation and involving formation of incoherent
interfaces, the role of the structural relationship does not
have any physical signiﬁcance, though a geometrical
relationship may still exist. In majority of transformations,
which are hybrid in nature, the structural relationship is not
very obvious sometimes, but the role of structural rela-
tionship is still important in establishing the nature of the
interfaces as well as modes of transformation [ 6].
The Laves phases are members of topologically closed
pack compounds which involve complex arrangements of
atoms in a cubic or in a hexagonal lattice [ 7]. The most
common feature of these phases is the occupancy of the
tetrahedral sites by atoms of one of the species. The three
Laves phases (C14, C15 and C36) are well reviewed in
literature [ 7]. C14 and C36 have hexagonal crystal struc-
tures with 12 and 24 atoms in the unit cell whereas C15 has
diamond cubic structure with 24 atoms in the cell [ 7].
In Nb-Ti-Cr-Si-X alloys, which are being considered for
high-temperature applications, the presence of the Laves
phase could help improve the high-temperature strength as

Chemical composition and other crystallographic details

been made to decipher the mode of transformation by

ship between the b matrix and the Lave phases. Based on
the structural relationship possible mechanism has been
proposed.
2 Experimental Procedure
A 20-kg ingot of the Nb-Ti-Cr-Si-X alloy in this study was
prepared from commercially pure elements by vacuum
induction melting. The chemical composition of the alloy
is reported in Table 1. Cut pieces of the as-cast alloys were
vacuum-encapsulated inside quartz tubes partially ﬁlled
with argon prior to heat treatments. Homogenization was
carried out at 1300 /C176 C and aging treatments were carried
over the range of 900–1100 /C176 C for various times. Heat-
treated samples were prepared metallographically to
examine under scanning electron microscope (SEM).
Unetched samples were observed in a Philips XL-30 ESEM
and a Hitachi S-4000 FEG SEM utilizing back-scattered
electron imaging (BSEI) and photographs digitally recor-
ded at appropriate magniﬁcations. The chemical analysis
was also performed using energy-dispersive spectrometer
attached to Hitachi S-4000 SEM and an electron probe
micro-analyzer (EPMA). Philips CM20 transmission elec-
tron microscope equipped with EDS was used to examine
the ﬁner details of the microstructure.
3 Experimental Results
3.1 Homogenized Microstructure
Typical homogenized microstructures of the Nb-Ti-Cr-Si-
X alloys are shown in Fig. 1. Presence of three major
phases could be observed in the homogenized microstruc-
ture. A silicide phase (bright contrast) was found homo-
geneously distributed in the b matrix and the Laves phase
was found present at the interface between the silicide
phase and the b matrix (Fig. 1a). TEM examination of the
homogenized sample showed presence of very small size
Laves phase precipitates distributed into the b matrix
(Fig. 1b). Selected area diffraction patterns (SADPs) from
the b matrix revealed B2 ordering (inset in Fig. 1b).
Chemical composition of various phases determined by
analysis is listed in Table 2.
3.2 Aged Microstructures

Figure 3a–c shows the effect of aging time on the

increase in the volume fraction of Laves phase with time.

with three different morphologies representing three dif-

4 Discussion
Among the three possible structures for the Laves phase,
understanding the atomic arrangement within the C15
(cubic) Laves phase is easiest [ 9]. As this structure is also
observed in the present study, discussion in the present
paper will mainly be conﬁned to the C15 phase. Atomic
occupancies with Wykoff positions are given in Table 3.
The easiest way to visualize the atomic arrangements is to
examine the structure along the \ 111 [ direction, as
tetrahedra of smaller atoms are aligned along these direc-
tions. In this direction, the arrangement will appear in its
layer form (Fig. 8) where the A type and B type atoms can
be separated out. Typical three-layer cubic arrangement is
followed. For example, if the base of tetrahedron is rep-
resented by capital letters it would have …ABCABC…
arrangement and if the top of the tetrahedron is represented
by small letters, it too would have …abcabc… arrange-
ments (Fig. 8a). The large atoms, which are occupying the
center of tetrahedron, will follow the arrangements of
tetrahedron and therefore the entire sequence for a structure
could be written as … aAacbBbacCcb… (Fig. 8c). If the
sequence of four layers aAac is represented by one symbol
A, then the entire sequence could be represented as
…ABC…which is a sequence of simple fcc structure. This
notation is useful in the sense that most of the Laves phases
show C14 to C15 transformation upon cooling which
generates sequence of faults in the structures leading to
presence of dense defects in the structure. Thus, transfor-
mation from C14 to C15 would lead to its own

5 Conclusion
In the present study, Nb-Ti-Cr-Si-X-based alloys were
observed to consist of three phases, namely, bcc matrix
phase, silicide phase and Laves phase. Heat treatment at
various temperatures led to the formation of Laves phases
varying in size and morphology. Aging below 1000 /C176 C
caused profuse precipitation of Laves phase within the
matrix phase. Evolution of the Laves phase as function of
time and temperature was documented. Detailed charac-
terization has shown that the precipitates formed within the
bcc matrix has the cubic C15 Laves phase structure. These
precipitates contain many defects which under TEM have
been established to belong to the family of \ 112 [ {111}
slip system, since these set of defects are generated due to
faults in the stacking sequence change during the C14 to
C15 transformation. It was conjectured that the C15 phase
has not precipitated directly from the matrix phase but
through the C14 phase. Upon aging at lower temperature of
700 /C176 C, formation of a phase relatively leaner in Cr con-
centration than the Laves phase was observed. The SAD
patterns obtained from the precipitate phase could be best
ﬁtted with the O-phase with a distinctive orientation rela-
tionship with the bcc matrix phase. The close matching
between the O-phase and C14 hexagonal structure and the
presence of defects in the latter suggest the following
sequence of structural changes during aging:
bcc ! B2 ! O ! C14 ! C15
