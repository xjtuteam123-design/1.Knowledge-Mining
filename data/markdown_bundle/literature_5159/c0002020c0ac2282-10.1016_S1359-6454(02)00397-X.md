Abstract
The effect of alloy chemistry on single crystal solidiﬁcation has been investigated in a series of model high refractory
Ni-base superalloys with variations in ruthenium, rhenium and aluminum. Over the range of composition investigated,
the initial phase to form during solidiﬁcation was either the γ phase (FCC Ni-solid solution) or the δ phase (HCP Re-
rich phase). In a quaternary Ni-Al-Ta-Re alloy containing 1.7 at.% (5.6 wt.%) Re, δ-Re nucleated at a temperature
well above the liquidus temperature of pure Ni and grew unconstrained into six-fold Re-rich dendrites. These dendrites
served as potent nucleation sites for γ grains as temperature decreased during directional solidiﬁcation. Ruthenium
additions in the range of 2.5~9.0 at.% (4.1~14.1 wt.%) lowered the temperature at which the δ nucleated and eventually
suppressed the formation of this phase during solidiﬁcation. Ru additions also increased the liquidus temperatures of
the multicomponent superalloys. The implications for the design of Ru-containing superalloys are discussed.

Directional solidiﬁcation processing has revol-

the past few decades [1]. Beginning in the 1970s,
the creep and fatigue properties of these nickel-
based materials were dramatically improved by the
removal of weak grain boundaries and low-melting
point boundary strengthening elements, such as
boron [2]. Further improvements in the tempera-

Utilizing alloying additions that increase solidus
and/or liquidus temperatures of these multicompo-
nent alloys, further improvements in the higher
temperature capabilities are still being pursued
[12–14]. Based on binary nickel phase diagrams,
Ru, Re, W and Ir are the only elemental additions
that might be expected to increase the liquidus tem-
perature of a multicomponent superalloy. While Ir
additions are effective [14], the cost of Ir limits Ir-
containing alloys to specialized space applications.
High levels of Re and W are already known to
improve the high temperature creep and fatigue
properties [3,15]. However, to date, there have
only been limited studies on Ru-additions to
nickel-base superalloys, mostly with a focus on the
inﬂuence of Ru additions on mechanical properties
[12–14].
The objective of this study was to investigate
the inﬂuence of Ru-additions on the solidi ﬁcation
characteristics of a series of model high refractory
content alloys. Of particular interest was the in ﬂu-
ence of Ru on the solidi ﬁcation path and grain
defect formation during solidi ﬁcation. Results of
differential thermal analyses and the detailed
characterization of as-cast microstructures by
optical, scanning and transmission electron
microscopy are presented. The behavior of selected
alloys subjected to directional solidi ﬁcation is dis-
cussed, with emphasis on the role of Ru, Re and
Al in the multicomponent dendritic solidi ﬁcation
process.
2. Experimental procedure
The nominal chemical compositions of the
investigated alloys are listed in Table 1. To better
understand the in ﬂuence of Ru, Re and Al on sin-
gle crystal solidi ﬁcation, three sets of alloys were
examined: 1) alloy CM-C2, a Ru-free alloy con-
taining high levels of Re and Al (1.7 and 18.5 at.%,
respectively); 2) alloys CM-A3 and UM-F3, the
intermediate Ru-containing superalloys with simi-
lar levels of Re and Al as CM-C2; 3) a series of
alloys (UM-F7~F9 and UM-F13) with low levels
of Re and Al (1.3 and 14.3 at.%, respectively),
among which the major difference in chemistry is
the Ru content, ranging from 3.5 at% to 9 at.%.
Alloy UM-F9 is selected as representative in this
series of alloys.
The alloys were vacuum induction melted
(VIM) at Sophisticated Alloys, Inc. and cast into
75 mm diameter cylindrical ingots weighing ~2.5
kg each. A series of quantitative composition
analyses were conducted for selected alloys. Com-
position was measured at 225 points in a square
grid using a Cameca CAMEBAX Electron
Microprobe Analyzer equipped with four wave-
length dispersive spectrometers (WDS). Using an
approximate point spacing of 70 µm, the 1.2 mm
2
grid was indexed over a representative region of
the dendritic microstructure. The mean compo-
sition determined from the WDS point measure-
ments was utilized to conﬁrm that alloy chemistries
were very close to aim compositions of the experi-
mental alloys. These data were also utilized to
assess segregation characteristics of elements in
the individual experimental alloys.
Selected alloys were directionally solidi ﬁed
(DS) as cylindrical bars in ceramic cluster molds
in a Bridgman furnace with a withdrawal rate of
200 mm/h. Prior to withdrawal, the charge alloys
were induction melted and poured into the invest-
ment molds preheated to 1550 °C. Conventional
helical starters were utilized to initiate single crys-
tal growth.
The effect of alloying on the liquidus tempera-
ture and other high temperature phase transform-
ations was investigated by a Seteram Setsys 18
Differential Thermal Analysis (DTA) instrument.
The DTA unit was calibrated with high purity Ni
(99.99+%) and Ag (99.9999%) at scanning rates
of 3, 5, 10 and 20 °C/min using a platinum refer-
ence, prior to the experiments. A 5 °C/min scan-
ning rate was determined to yield the best combi-
nation of temperature accuracy and peak
resolution. Once calibrated, the cylindrical DTA
samples (4 mm diameter and ~3 –4 mm height)
with masses ranging from 200 –250 mg were pre-
pared, containing a representative region of the
dendritic microstructure. In order to avoid
undercooling effects, the DTA data were only ana-
lyzed from heating curves. Additional details of the
DTA experiments have been reported elsewhere
[6]. It should also be pointed out that, in a series
of our investigated alloys ( /H1102215 alloys), seven

preferred solidi ﬁcation path. Finally, one DTA
sample of the CM-C2 alloy containing Re den-

which is even higher than the peritectic reaction
temperature (1620 °C) in binary Ni-Re system

corresponding DTA curve. The microstructures of
DTA samples were examined before and after the
DTA experiments. No signi ﬁcant changes were
found in the morphology and the size of Re den-
drites. Thus, the δ phase is apparently stable to a
temperature higher than 1630 °C, which is well
above the liquidus temperature of typical nickel-
base superalloys.

qualitatively consistent with the previous reports

pointing out that the distribution of Ru between

4. Discussion
Within current γ /H11001γ/H11032nickel-based systems,
increased temperature capability has been achieved
with increasing levels of Re and W [3,15]. With
new classes of alloying additions, it is desirable
for the solidus and/or liquidus temperatures to be
increased. In the current study, ruthenium additions
have been shown to increase liquidus temperatures
in multicomponent Ni-base superalloys. This is
consistent with Ni-Ru binary [18] and Ni-Al-Ru
ternary alloys [14], which are compared to the
present study in Fig. 2. There is, however, a
stronger positive in ﬂuence of Ru on the liquidus
temperature in the binary system, compared to the
multicomponent systems. Recently, Murakami et
al. investigated a series of ternary alloys Ni-19Al-
xRu with x ranging from 1 to 9 at.% and also con-
cluded that the addition of Ru increases the liqui-
dus temperature of the ternary alloys [14]. In these
ternary alloys, the liquidus temperatures are sig-
niﬁcantly lower than those in the binary system and
the Ru-containing multicomponent alloys in the
present work due to higher level of Al additions.
The present results also indicate that the Ru-con-
taining superalloys with a low level of Al (14.3
at.%) have signi ﬁcantly higher liquidus tempera-
tures than those of the current commercial Ni-base
superalloys [7,16].
4.1. Solidi ﬁcation paths and the d phase
The δ phase was found to exist in both Ru-free
(CM-C2) and Ru-containing (UM-F3) alloys. It is
interesting that the Ru-containing alloy CM-A3,
based on alloy CM-C2, did not have the Re-rich δ
phase in the as-cast microstructure. This indicates
that Ru additions suppress δ phase formation dur-
ing solidiﬁcation and increase the solubility of Re
in the liquid. Further, it conﬁrms again that the for-
mation of δ dendrites in the as-cast alloy CM-C2
was not caused by incomplete solutioning of the
initial Re addition during melting. While precipi-
tation of refractory-rich TCP phases in multi-
component superalloys is well-known, the pres-
ence of the δ phase is unusual. For a better
understanding of the solidi ﬁcation characteristics
of these alloys, it is necessary to examine the dif-
ferences of the δ phase in the Ru-free alloy com-
pared to Ru-containing superalloys in terms of its
location, composition and morphology within the
microstructure, the temperature at which the δ
phase appears and its in ﬂuence on the solidi ﬁ-
cation path.
In the high Re and Ru-free as-cast alloy CM-
C2, δ dendrites are preferentially located in near-
surface regions and at the bottom of the bars (with
respect to gravity). The morphology of δ phase
shows a completely developed six-fold symmetry
characteristic of free growth within the liquid.
Remelting to a temperature of 1630 °C resulted in
no distinct changes in the δ phase in terms of
location, size and morphology. This indicates that
the temperature at which the δ phase solidiﬁed is
much higher than that of the γ dendrites which sub-
sequently solidify in the temperature range of 1375
°C–1410 °C. The δ dendrites, which have a higher

4.3. The breakdown of single crystal
solidiﬁcation
The solidi ﬁcation parameters, mold and helical
starter design utilized in the present experiments
would result in single crystal solidiﬁcation for most
conventional multicomponent superalloys. In
nickel-based superalloys, single crystal solidi ﬁ-
cation may break down due to the onset of thermo-
solutal convective instabilities that arise due to seg-
regation of refractory alloying elements during
dendritic solidi ﬁcation [7,17]. This involves
remelting and fragmentation of dendritic arms,
which subsequently grow and produce high angle
grain boundaries. Solidi ﬁcation may also break
down in the presence of nucleating agents. For
superalloys, cobalt-aluminates are well-known for
their grain nucleation and grain re ﬁnement proper-
ties [23]. In this study, the incidence of equiaxed
grains was directly correlated with the distribution
of the δ dendrites in alloy CM-C2. It is apparent
that the δ phase dendrites are effective, hetero-
geneous nucleation sites for the dendritic γ phase
(Fig. 3(c)). In alloy UM-F3, the δ phase did not
act as a grain nucleant. This is due to the fact that
the Ru additions depress the temperature at which
this phase solidiﬁes so that it only forms within the
mushy zone during the latter stages of solidi ﬁ-
cation.
Among the three alloys studied in detail, only
alloy UM-F9 solidiﬁed as a single crystal and con-
tained no δ phase in the as-cast condition. This
alloy contained a high level of Ru (6 at.%/9.6
wt.%) with relatively high levels of Re /H11001W (2.8
at.%/ 8.2 wt.%) and low level of Al /H11001Ta com-
pared with the other alloys. While the intermediate
Ru-containing alloy CM-A3 has the same level of
Al and Re as CM-C2 and UM-F3, no δ phase was
observed within the as-cast alloy. Thus, it appears
that Ru may be bene ﬁcial not only because it
eventually suppresses the δ phase formation during
solidiﬁcation, but it also in ﬂuences partitioning of
Re and W during solidi ﬁcation [12]. This aspect
of solidiﬁcation and the in ﬂuence of Ru additions
on partitioning of elements during multicomponent
solidiﬁcation requires further study.
4.4. Solid state precipitation and elemental
segregation
Although the improvements in temperature
capability have been accomplished in advanced
single crystal superalloys, increasing the content of
refractory alloying elements (Re, W, Mo and Ta)
enhances elemental segregation during dendritic
solidiﬁcation and subsequent slows diffusion dur-
ing heat treatment. Further, these additions often
result in the precipitation of undesirable phases
during elevated temperature exposures [9 –11].
Among these elements, Re has the strongest tend-
ency to partition to the dendritic regions and results
in a local enhancement of the Re concentration,
providing an ever greater driving force for the pre-
cipitation of detrimental phases. However, in the
current study (Table 3), the extent of elemental
segregation (such as Re, W, Ta and Al) from den-
dritic to interdendritic regions is somewhat less in
comparison with the conventional high refractory
Ni-base superalloys [17]. Thus, it is important to
understand the in ﬂuence of Ru on the segregation
behavior of Re and other alloying elements in the
multicomponent Ni-base superalloys.
The microstructure in the Ru-free CM-C2 alloy
shows that the δ phase did not dissolve after heat
treatment at 1300 °C. Also, no additional δ precipi-
tates appeared in the matrix after a 1150 °C
annealing treatment. However, the δ phase from
UM-F3 was found to undergo dissolution at a tem-
perature just below the liquidus temperature of the
γ phase. During aging at lower temperatures, ﬁne
Re-rich δ precipitates were observed in the residual
dendritic microstructure, which has an enhanced
Re content relative to the interdendritic regions. No
solid state precipitates of the δ phase were
observed in the Ru-free alloy CM-C2. Apparently,
as the δ phase solidi ﬁes within the liquid, this
depletes the liquid of Re in alloy CM-C2 and
yields a γ matrix without Re supersaturation. The
Ru addition not only depresses the temperature of
δ phase formation in alloy UM-F3, but also
increases the solubility of Re in the liquid and in
the ﬁrst γ phase to solidify. Thus, it would be

expected that Ru additions could enhance strength-
ening, indirectly through their in ﬂuence on Re par-
titioning. This aspect of Ru additions to superalloys
requires further investigation. Finally, it is interest-
ing that there is a range of composition in which
the hcp δ phase can be controlled through the use
of conventional solution treatment and aging pro-

Finally, there are several other interesting
aspects of Ru additions to these Ni-base systems.
Here, it has been observed that the volume fraction
of eutectic decreased signi ﬁcantly with increasing
Ru content. The liquidus temperature also
increased; this is important because these single
crystal materials operate at very high fractions of
their melting points. Additionally, it has been
reported elsewhere that Ru additions may have a
beneﬁcial effect on the partitioning of Re in γ and
γ/H11032phases [12], increasing the solubility of the Re
in the γ/H11032phase. This again would permit greater
control of mechanical properties, since mis ﬁt
between the phases is very sensitive to Re par-
titioning.
5. Conclusions
Ru-free and Ru-containing multicomponent
superalloys were investigated in the present studies
and the conclusions are summarized as follows:
1. Liquidus temperatures of multicomponent
ruthenium-containing Ni-base superalloys
increase with increasing Ru content and
decreasing Al content.
2. The Re-rich δ phase exists in both Ru-free and
Ru-containing as-cast alloys. High levels of Al
and Re promote δ phase formation in the early
stages of solidi ﬁcation.
3. The δ phase is an effective grain nucleant that
results in the breakdown of single crystal solidi-
ﬁcation.
4. Ru additions increase the solubility of Re in the
liquid and in the γ phase which initially solidi-
ﬁes. Ru also depresses the temperature at which
the δ phase nucleates from the liquid.
