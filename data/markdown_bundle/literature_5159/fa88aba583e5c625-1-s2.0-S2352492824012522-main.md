ABSTRACT 
Nickel-based superalloys are widely used in turbine blades and aviation engine due to their outstanding me -
chanical property at elevated temperatures. The superior mechanical property of nickel-based superalloys is 
highly relevant to the morphology evolution of γ ʹ precipitates in the γ matrix phase. At high temperature, the 
directional coarsening of γ ʹ precipitates may result in rafting behavior under applied stress. In addition, defects 
such as the cavities, inclusions and micro-cracks introduced during the manufacturing process in superalloys 
have significant influence on the evolution of the γ ʹ precipitates. In this work, a phase field model with crack and 
precipitate as order parameters has been proposed to investigate the effect of crack on the morphology evolution 
of the γ ʹ precipitates. The morphology evolution and volume fraction evolution of the γ ʹ precipitates in the Nickel- 
based superalloys with different cracks have been investigated under the tensile and shear stresses. It is found 
that the local high equivalent stress and high elastic strain energy near the crack lead to the migration of solute 
atoms from the high stress region to the low stress region. The migration of solute atoms results in the disap -
pearance of the γ’ phases in the vicinity of cracks, which is consistent with the experimental observation. The 
disappearance of the γ’ phases around the crack reduces the volume fraction of the γ ʹ precipitates, which will 
affect the mechanical property of the superalloys. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based superalloys have been widely used in the hottest parts 
of aeroengines or industrial gas turbines over the last several decades, 
such as turbine blades, owing to their good high-temperature tensile, 
creep and fatigue properties [1 – 3] . Their excellent mechanical proper -
ties mostly result from the long-range LI
2
-type ordered γ ʹ phases which 
appear as cubes coherently embedded in the face-centered cubic solid 
solution γ matrix [4,5] . Besides, the volume fraction of the γ ʹ phases and 
the width of the γ matrix channel can also affect the macroscopic 
behavior of the material [6 – 8] . However, the microstructure of the su -
peralloys undergoes the so-called rafting phenomenon under the 
high-temperature service condition [9] . A large number of theoretical 
models has been developed to predict the rafting orientation [10 – 14] . 
Earlier, the rafting behavior has been investigated only with the elastic 
strain [10] . Subsequently, the influence of plastic strain was considered 
[11 – 13] . These studies indicate that there are many factors that affect 
rafting, such as the lattice misfit, the magnitude of the external loading 
and its direction with respect to the microstructure orientation, the 
difference in elastic constants between the two phases, the plastic ac -
tivity in γ channels and so on. The rafting phenomenon is related to the 
distribution of stress which induces the difference in chemical potential, 
which is also verified by phase-field [14] and FEM simulation [15] . 
The rafting phenomenon was successfully predicted by the phase 
field method at the mesoscopic scale [16 – 19] . Different phase field 
models of rafting have been developed during the past few decades, 
from the early elastic framework [16,17] to the current visco-plasticity 
framework [18,19] . In the elastic framework, the driving forces of the 
rafting behavior are always considered as the applied stress and the 
difference of elastic modulus as well as the lattice misfit between γ ʹ and γ 
phases [16] . Although the elastic phase field model is able to predict the 
overall shape and orientation of the raft structure, some detailed

The local regions of the Nickel-based superalloys may form some 
defects during directional solidification process, such as the cavities,

studies have mainly focused on the effects of the applied stress, lattice

morphology evolution of the microstructure. Few studies consider the

morphology evolution of the γ ʹ phases during the creep process of su -

from the experimental result. 
In this article, based on the phase field models of rafting in the elastic 
framework [17] , we consider the effect of cracks on the microstructure 
evolution of the γ ʹ phases. Due to the complexity of the interaction be -
tween crack propagation and the morphological evolution of γ ʹ phases, 
only the influence of static cracks on the morphological evolution of γ ʹ 
phases is considered in this article. By introducing a crack order 
parameter φ ( r ) ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] into the previous theoretical models to charac -
terize the crack, the modulus of elasticity at the crack is modified. The 
purpose of this paper is to study the existence of microcracks will affect 
the local stress distribution around the crack and thus the morphological 
evolution of γ ʹ phases. As for the crack propagation, we will discuss it in 
the next paper. 
2. Phase-field model 
In this article, we mainly study the effect of static cracks on the 
evolution of the γ ʹ phases. The influence of the crack on the model is 
caused by the degradation of the elastic constant of the material at the 
crack. Therefore, by introducing a crack order parameter φ ( r ) ∈ [ 0 , 1 ]
into the previous theoretical model to represent the crack, the elastic 
modulus of the material is modified, so as to simulate the effect of cracks 
on the evolution of the γ ʹ phases.

3.1. Morphological evolution of γ ʹ phases 
The previous study had shown that the γ ʹ phases will be rafted under 
external stress and elevated temperature conditions, mainly because of 
the equivalent stress driving the migration of alloying elements from the 
high equivalent stress area to the low equivalent stress region between 
the γ and γ ʹ phases [35] . In order to investigate the morphological evo -
lution of γ ʹ phases under applied stress and elevated temperature con -
ditions when static crack existing. The dimensions of the model were 
chosen to be 1024 nm X 1024 nm and the model undergoes isothermal 
aging to t = 900 before applying the applied stress. In the simulation, four 
types of cracks are selected as shown in Fig. 2 (a-d). The blue region 
indicates that the material in this region is undamaged, and the corre -
sponding crack ordered parameter φ = 0. The red region represents 
the cracked region, and the corresponding crack ordered parameter φ =
1. In order to distinguish between the different types of cracks, the crack 
represented by the Fig. 2 (a) is defined as type I. Similarly, the crack 
represented by Fig. 2 (b) is defined as type2. The crack represented by 
Fig. 2 (c) is defined as type3, and the crack represented by Fig. 2 (d) is 
defined as type4.

3.2. The driving force of the formed rafted structure 
When the external stress is applied, elastic strain energy gradients of 
γ matrix will be established in the horizontal and vertical γ channels. The 
elastic strain energy gradients drive the movement of solute atoms, 
causing the morphological evolution of γ ʹ phases. There are inevitably 
some micro-cracks during production process of superalloys. When 
there are no cracks, the matrix is continuous and the distribution of 
stress is uniform. But when a crack exists, the matrix is discontinuous in 
the crack region, resulting in a locally extremely an uneven distribution 
of stress and a high stress gradient.
