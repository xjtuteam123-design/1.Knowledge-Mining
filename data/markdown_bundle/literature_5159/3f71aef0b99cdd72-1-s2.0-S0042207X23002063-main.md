1. Introduction 
At present, magnesium alloy is the lightest metal structural material 
available, and due to its low density, high specific strength, and specific 
stiffness, it is ideal for structural use [ 1 – 4 ]. Meanwhile, as a result of 
their good damping and electromagnetic shielding properties, easy 
machining and recycling, and have a wide range of application prospect 
in the areas of national defense, aerospace, lightweight electronic 
products and new energy vehicles, etc. [ 5 , 6 ].

RE alloys, among which Mg-RE alloys are gaining attention for their

In terms of creep mechanism, the n can be used to judge the creep 
mechanism. Generally, the creep mechanism of Mg alloy can be divided 
into four categories [ 25 – 28 ]: 
Class I, the stress exponent n ≈ 1, is diffusion-controlled creep, where 
the process of creep is mainly dominated by the diffusion rate of atoms 
in the crystal, and mostly occurs under lower stress. Class II, n ≈ 2, 
where the process of creep is mainly dominated by the slip of grain 
boundaries, i.e., grain boundary slip creep. Class III, n ≈ 3, the rate of 
steady-state creep is mainly dominated by the slip of dislocation, i.e., 
slip dislocation creep. Class IV, n ≈ 4 – 6, the creep process is mainly 
dominated by dislocation climbing caused by vacancy diffusion, i.e., 
climbing dislocation creep. Generally, it is considered that the disloca -
tion movement is dominated by basal slip in the III and IV creep 
mechanisms, and the difference between the Q
c 
and the self-diffusion 
activation energy of pure Mg is relatively small. Class V, n > 6, the 
power-law creep law is invalid, and the rate of steady-state creep is 
dominated by non-basal dislocation motion and twins. The Q
c 
is higher 
than the self-diffusion activation energy of pure magnesium, which is 
equivalent to the dislocation beam energy.

slip, which is not enough to provide sufficient slip system. The alloy will 
release the stress and energy through twins. The twins can also change 
the grain orientation, so that the slip system that cannot be moved can 
be restarted, which further greatly promotes the deformation and in -
creases the creep rate. However, the activation of the non-basal slip 
system needs to overcome the extremely high prismatic stacking fault 
energy, and the activation of twins needs to overcome the energy in -
crease caused by dislocation propagation and the interface energy 
generated by the formation of stacking faults. Generally, the activation 
of twins requires higher energy than non-basal slip, so the alloy will have 
a relatively high creep activation energy and stress exponent at high 
temperature and high stress, resulting in the failure of the power law.
