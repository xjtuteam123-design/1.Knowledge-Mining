ABSTRACT 
The study of alloys exhibiting noteworthy strength-ductility synergy at ambient and cryogenic 
temperatures has been a persistent area of interest in materials engineering. This interest extends 
to the recent development of high-entropy alloys (HEAs). The current investigation delves into 
the impact of diverse thermo-mechanical treatments on the phase and microstructure evolution in 
a face-centered cubic (FCC) Al

phase originates from nucleation on defect-rich sites, such as deformation bands. However, the 
coherent L1

1. Introduction 
Pursuing an optimal balance between strength and ductility is a longstanding goal in advanced materials science and engineering. 
Conventional alloy design strategies have faced challenges in achieving exceptional strength and ductility combinations in structural 
materials. The recent development of a novel alloy design paradigm, termed high-entropy alloys (HEAs), has provided a solution by 
leveraging their intrinsic lattice distortions to achieve a remarkable balance between strength and ductility synergy ( Kishore et al., 
2023 ; Zhang et al., 2021b ). Generally, HEAs consist of four or more principal elements, each contributing to the atomic composition in 
the range of 5 at.% ~ 35 at.% ( Li et al., 2023 a). Among the family of HEAs, the face-centered cubic (FCC) FeCoNiCr HEA system stands 
out with immense potential in various engineering applications, such as impellers and ship propellers, owing to its exceptional 
ductility, toughness, and remarkable corrosion resistance ( He et al., 2018 ). However, the limited yield strength at ambient temper -
atures significantly hinders their practical engineering application ( Chu et al., 2023 ; Fan et al., 2022a ). 
The introduction of atoms with varying sizes into single-phase (SP) FCC HEAs has been shown to enhance both strength and 
ductility through increased lattice distortion and/or chemical short-range order ( Xia et al., 2023 ; Zhang et al., 2023 a). However, 
reliance solely on solid-solution strengthening presents limitations in optimizing strength. To overcome this constraint, the intro -
duction of precipitated phases into the ductile matrix has proven effective in significantly elevating yield strength ( σ
0 . 2
) ( Han et al., 
2021 ; He et al., 2021 a). Recent investigations on HEAs ( Gwalani et al., 2019 , 2018 ) have revealed the controllability of micro -
structures and mechanical properties through modifications to thermo-mechanical processing (TMP) routes. The TMP treatments not 
only control recovery and recrystallization in alloys but also govern the type of second-phase precipitation. It has been demonstrated 
primarily in HEAs, such as Al
0.3
CoCrFeNi ( Gwalani et al., 2019 ; Shukla et al., 2018 ), and Al
0.5

1.5

interplay between the stability of two ordered intermetallic phases, specifically the L1

fine-tuning microstructure and mechanical properties. The L1
2 
precipitates homogeneously in the FCC matrix due to its low nucleation 
barrier. Conversely, the high nucleation barrier of the B2 phase leads to heterogeneous nucleation at grain boundaries or defects.

mation and even brittleness, and thereby impairing ductility ( Liu et al., 2021 ). Consequently, understanding the pathway of precip -
itated phase transformation in FCC HEAs through TMP becomes of paramount importance. 
More recently, the incorporation of heterogeneous structures has emerged as a promising strategy to address the trade-off between 
strength and ductility in HEAs ( He et al., 2021 b; Zhang et al., 2023 b). Pioneering studies ( Du et al., 2020 ; Fan et al., 2022b ) have 
demonstrated the feasibility of introducing heterogeneous structures through meticulous TMP treatments. Defects, such as subgrain 
boundaries, deformation bands, twins, and dislocation tangles generated during cold deformation, serve as potent sites for hetero -
geneous nucleation due to their inherently high energy content. Subsequent annealing following cold deformation leads to hetero -
geneous microstructures due to differing recrystallization rates and precipitation of equilibrium or metastable phases. Inspired by 
these observations, Ming et al. (2019) produced a Cr

regarding discussions on their deformation-induced microstructures and mechanical behaviors. 
Another critical issue revolves around ensuring the reliable performance of crucial components in demanding environments, 
particularly in the context of contemporary equipment ( Xie et al., 2022 ). While high-performance metals play a pivotal role in energy 
conversion and transportation, they often experience catastrophic intergranular failure in cryogenic conditions ( Seol et al., 2020 ). In 
light of this, FCC-based HEAs have emerged as promising materials for cryogenic engineering due to their distinctive combination of 
fracture toughness and tensile properties at cryogenic temperatures ( Li et al., 2022 , 2023b ). An illustrative study on the cryogenic 
mechanical response, conducted by Gludovatz et al. (2014) highlighted the temperature-dependent mechanical behavior of the 
coarse-grained (CG) FCC FeCoNiCrMn HEA. It exhibited significantly temperature-dependent mechanical properties, becoming 
stronger, tougher, and more ductile as temperatures decreased from room temperature to liquid nitrogen (LN
2

despite these outstanding mechanical properties, CG FCC HEAs generally exhibit insufficient strength, which limits their cryogenic 
applicability. Therefore, to effectively integrate the hierarchical heterogeneous microstructures into cryogenic industries, a compre -
hensive understanding of their mechanical properties and deformation mechanisms at low temperatures is indispensable.

(3) The effects of L -C locks: L -C locks originate from the interaction of the leading partials of the SFs on two {111} planes, forming a 
stair-rod dislocation. Evidently, the CRRA700 deformed at 77 K reveals a distinct presence of L -C locks, although the density is 
comparatively lower when compared to SFs and DTs. These L -C locks are not unique to HEAs, in fact, are commonly observed in 
FCC alloys with low SFE (Saada, 1960). The hardening mechanisms arising from the interactions of mobile dislocations on 
conjugate systems have been systematically investigated and quantified using pair dislocation interaction coefficients (Franciosi 
and Zaoui, 1982). Uzer et al., 2018 have provided evidence that L -C locks not only serve as effective obstacles to dislocation 
motion, but also act as origins for Frank-Read dislocation propagation and growth. The impact of L -C locks on strain-hardening 
has been both validated by experiments (Ma et al., 2018) and atomic simulations (Abu-Odeh et al., 2022). The primary 
mechanism through which L -C locks contribute to strain-hardening involves the accumulation of dislocations. Initially, each 
sessile fault locks four segments of dislocations. This occurs when two dislocations meet to form an L -C lock, with each end of the 
lock immobilizing two dislocation segments. Additionally, the L -C locks not only produce and connect a significant number of 
sessile dislocations but also assist in the formation of SF networks by these L -C locks. This process stabilizes the L -C locks, 
reinforcing their structural stability and ability to withstand dislocation dissociations (Wu et al., 2009). As a result, L -C locks 
offer substantial resistance against dislocation movement and serve as barriers to accumulate more dislocations, making them 
highly effective in inducing sustained high strain-hardening.

(1) Influence of TMP treatment routes: The transition from solid-solution annealing to recrystallization annealing orchestrates the

samples. 
(2) Strength-ductility synergy of heterogeneous structures: Samples displaying heterogeneous structures demonstrate a remarkable 
balance between strength and ductility. Specifically, the sample with a hierarchical precipitate heterogeneous structure exhibits
