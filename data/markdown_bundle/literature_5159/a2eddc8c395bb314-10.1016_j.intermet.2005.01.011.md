Abstract
Ni-based diffusion couples were investigated experimentally at 1000 and 1200 8C. The experimental results were compared with
theoretical calculations. Equilibrium calculations needed were obtained using CALPHAD approach. The diffusion processes were simulated
by means of DICTRA program. The thermodynamic database TCNI was used for equilibrium calculations. User kinetic database was taken
for evaluation of diffusion ﬂuxes. The experimental techniques of light microscopy and analytical electron microscopy (TEM, SEM, EDAX)
were used. An additional microhardness measurement was also performed. Some particular aspects of the joining of the Ni-based superalloys
are discussed. An experimental evidence of validity of local phase equilibrium assumption is given.

1. Introduction
Knowledge of evolution of the diffusion couple micro-
structure at high temperature is essential for advanced weld
engineering. This information enables to design weld joints
with optimised physical, mechanical, and corrosion-resist-
ance properties. Diffusion-controlled processes at high
temperatures determine in many cases the long-term
microstructure stability of the weld joint. This phenomenon
can be observed also in Ni-based weld joints.
Superalloys based on Al–Cr–Ni–X alloy systems rep-
resent established structural materials for Ni-based alloy
development [1,2] because of good mechanical properties
subjected to coexistence of gCg
0 (A1-type disordered C
L12-type ordered) phase microstructure. Study of changes
taking place in diffusion-affected zone of the Ni-based
diffusion couples prepared and annealed under laboratory
conditions is one of effective tools for the investigation of
the microstructure stability, phase transformations, and
element redistribution inside the real weld joints at high
temperatures. In order to increase a reliability of the gained
results, studies are performed using both experimental and
theoretical ways.
A weld joint of dissimilar materials under external stress
usually represents a critical point in many technical
applications covering elevated temperatures. The investi-
gation of the relationships among the element/phase
redistributions, the microstructure at various points across
the weldments, and local mechanical properties represents a
method that is suitable for the evaluation of long-term
mechanical/microstructure stability of weld joints [3]. In the
case of weld joint applications at elevated temperatures, the
mechanical properties can be related to chemical compo-
sition and phase transformation processes in the diffusion-
affected zone [4,5]. The main factors that inﬂuence the
mechanical stability/instability of the Ni-based weld seem
to be, among others, phase transformations in diffusion-
affected zone.
The experimental investigation of the Ni-based labora-
tory diffusion couples can be complemented by advanced
theoretical modelling [6], which is also used for steels, see,
e.g. [7]. This method is based on CALPHAD approach [8],
the presumption of local condition of phase equilibria,
assumption that diffusion is the controlling process of

The aims of this study are to present the results of

2. Experimental
The laboratory diffusion couples were prepared from
high purity Ni-based alloys 0, 1, 2, and 3 (see composition
given in Table 1 ), which were produced by induction
melting of pure components under inert atmosphere. The
alloy No. 0 was combined with alloys Nos. 1, 2, and 3 in
order to obtain three different diffusion couple combi-
nations. The joints were made by electric discharge welding.
The samples (couples 0 j1, 0 j2, and 0 j3) of the Ni-based
heterogeneous welded joints were annealed at different
conditions ((1000G3) 8C/1530 h or (1200 G3) 8C/100 h).
For microstructural studies, a Philips SEM 505 scanning
electron microscope and a Philips CM12 STEM trans-
mission electron microscope both equipped with an EDAX
analyser were used for measuring chemical composition.
The standardless method using a thin foil approximation
was used for spectra evaluation in TEM; standard ZAF
corrections were applied in the case of SEM measurements.
Phases were identiﬁed by means of selected area diffraction
in TEM. The microhardness HV 0.02 measurements were
performed in a standard way.
3. Modelling
The CALPHAD approach [8] and the thermodynamic
database TCNI [14] were used for the solution of both local
and global phase equilibrium problems in the Ni-based
alloys investigated. It enabled to calculate chemical
compositions of the equilibrated phases in all individual
Ni-based alloys at given temperature as well as any of phase
diagram cross-sections for these materials, chemical
potentials/activities of elements, etc.
All elements were included into the thermodynamic
calculations (cf. alloy composition in Table 1). The molar
Gibbs energies of the phases existing in the materials were
described using thermodynamic parameters from the TCNI
database. The g and/or a phase, respectively (i.e. fcc A1 and
bcc A2 phase) were described using a regular solution
model for phases with several components [15] having
magnetic contributions to phase Gibbs energy [13].
Intermetallic g
0 and/or b phase, respectively (i.e. fcc L1 2
and Bcc B 2 phase), were described as ordered phases
involving terms of disordered phasesg, respectively, a [16].
In the case of the joints consisting of dissimilar Ni-based
alloys, the initial state was approximated by two virtual
thermodynamic systems, being in the state of thermodyn-
amic equilibrium, at simulated temperature conditions. The
width of the diffusion zone was set to zero and step-like
initial element proﬁles were supposed for all elements at the
start of heat annealing of the weld sample. All diffusion
couples (excluding 0 j3) were modelled as systems with g
diffusion matrix and assuming no diffusion in g
0 phase for
the case 0 j2.
At simulated temperature, gradients of chemical poten-
tials, ﬂuxes, diffusion coefﬁcients, and other kinetic values
for all elements were treated in the way given in [9]. The
changes in the chemical potentials of the species form the
thermodynamic ground state for phase transformations,
phase precipitation, growth and/or phase dissolution inside
virtual couple. A local equilibrium was assumed in each part
of the virtual diffusion couple. In our paper the DICTRA
program [13] (involving the ThermoCalc sub-code [13])
was used for the simulation of the post-weld heat exposure.
This model deals with element mobilities [17] for diffusion
matrix evaluation. User kinetic database DIF was taken for
evaluation of the diffusion ﬂuxes. The kinetic database DIF
includes, among others, also mobilities published in [18].
The missing parameters for mobility of Al, Co, Cr, and W
were approximated by the same way as used in kinetic FRIB
database [13].

5. Discussion
The theoretical study of the heterogeneous diffusion
couples makes very good complement of the experimental
weld joint investigations also in the case of the Ni-based
weldments. Three diffusion couple combinations (0 j1, 0j2,
0j3) were investigated. Individual combinations reveal
similar qualitative features at 1000 and 1200 8C excluding
the couple 0 j3.
The simplest one-phase diffusion couple 0 j1 investi-
gation reveals good agreement between experimental and
simulated results as can be seen in Fig. 1. The experimental
and simulated element proﬁles after long-time annealing
show small deviations which can be explained by
experimental scatter and/or accuracy of both thermodyn-
amic (database TCNI) and kinetic (database DIF) par-
ameters. The important agreement can be stated for Ni
element proﬁles. The experimental and simulated proﬁles
show the same shape with maximum, inﬂection and
minimum. It can be expected that mechanical properties
will change continuously with no extreme inside diffusion-
affected zone.

material 2, however, the experimental and calculated proﬁles

6. Summary
The experimental and the simulated results enable
understanding of the microstructure processes and phase
transformations occurring in the diffusion-affected zone of
the investigated Ni-based welded joints. It can be generally
concluded that the method used provides with good
accuracy results that reﬂect phase structures and the
redistributions of the elements in diffusion couples. The
results of simulation can be further improved by higher
accuracy of the parameters stored in both kinetic and
thermodynamic databases.
The relation between experimental and theoretical results
show that it is possible to simulate successfully element
proﬁles of welded joints of Ni/based alloys with diffusion
matrix formed by a single phase disordered structure (e.g. g
phase). The similar reasonable results can be attained in the
case that the diffusion matrix involves phases with limited
diffusion, like g
0 ordered intermetallic phase inside g.
The speciﬁc situation arose when the disordered single-
phase g material was joined with intermetallic ordered
phase b with high diffusivity due to the presence of
structural vacancies. The post-weld heat treatment (diffu-
sion couple 0 j3) critically inﬂuences phase formation in
diffusion-affected zone. Different sequences of phase
regions are then formed at 1000 and 1200 8C. Better
mechanical properties can be obtained at higher temperature
of post-weld treatment because the strength of the joint
increases during annealing as well as during cooling due to
primary and secondary gCg
0 microstructure formation.
Similar simulation method can be used also for the other
Ni-based superalloy welded joints and the agreement between
experimental and theoretical phase compositions for diffusion
couple 0 j3a t1 0 0 0 8C conﬁrms the assumption of local
equilibrium condition in the simulation model. This approach
saves time and expenses for weld design of heterogeneous Ni-
based joints with optimised mechanical properties under
external stress during long-term service at high temperatures.
The microstructure and mechanical stabilities are in close
relation with phase transformations in diffusion-affected zone
and the presented approach is suitable for a long-term
stability/instability judgement of the weld joints.
