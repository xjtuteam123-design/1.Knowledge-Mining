ADVANCED MATERIALS FOR EXTREME ENVIRONMENTS
Strength Behavior of Niobium-Based Refractory Systems
KRISHNA JOSHI1 and PANKAJ KUMAR 1,2
1.—Department of Mechanical Engineering, University of New Mexico, Albuquerque, NM 87131, USA.
2.—e-mail: pankaj@unm.edu
A comprehensive assessment of the quasi-static strength behavior of niobium
(Nb)-based refractory systems, including the conventional Nb alloys and
refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), in a broad temperature range has
been performed in this review. The strength and ductility data of the Nb-based
refractory systems in varying temperature ranges manufactured by various
methods have been compiled and discussed to correlate the manufacturing,
material characteristics, deformation, and strength behavior. The
microstructure characteristics and the interstitial contaminations have been
identiﬁed as the dominating factors in controlling strength behavior in the
low-temperature range of the Nb alloys. In the intermediate temperature
range, the dynamic strain recovery plays a signiﬁcant role in dictating the
strength behavior, while, in the high-temperature domain, the diffusion-as-
sisted deformation process leads to signiﬁcant strength reduction. Nb-based
RHEAs have been found to have great potential for applications in extreme
temperatures (> 1200/C176C). A wide range of strength and ductility is possible in
Nb-based RHEAs. Solid-solution strengthening largely controls the variation
in strength, ductility, and thermal stability of RHEAs.
INTRODUCTION
Recently, the increased desire to enhance energy
efﬁciency and the new technological innovations for
aerospace and energy applications have put a high
demand on material performance—increased
strength and stability in an extreme temperature
environment. Refractory alloys are the last viable
metallic option for ultra-high-temperature applica-
tions. Refractory metals are those that exhibit high
melting temperatures and have high strength and
high-temperature resistance. Because of these, they
are relatively higher creep-resistant than any other
metals in the periodic table.
1–3 As per the deﬁnition
of refractory metals, there are only ﬁve acknowl-
edged refractory metals with melting points above
(> 2400/C176C), i.e., tungsten (W), molybdenum (Mo),
niobium (Nb), rhenium (Re), and tantalum (Ta).
3–5
Refractory metals and alloys are highly valued for
their unique combination of properties, such as high
melting points, remarkable mechanical strength at
high temperatures, and resistance to corrosion and
wear.6 These qualities make them essential in
harsh environments such as aerospace, nuclear,
military, and high-temperature manufacturing. 7
Among these, only Nb, Ta, W, and Mo can be of
practical importance largely due to cost considera-
tions.
4 The main downside of these refractory
metals is their high density and ductile-to-brittle
transition (DBT).
8 Among all the recognized refrac-
tory metals, Nb has a low density of /C248.5 g/cc,
comparable to the current state-of-the-art nickel
alloy for high-temperature applications, and a DBT
below room temperature (RT) enables the RT pro-
cessing of Nb and its alloys.
4,8 Thus, among all the
refractory alloys, Nb-based refractory alloys are the
most preferred high-temperature alloys for com-
mercial use in the modern era, owing to their
superior high-temperature mechanical properties,
which differentiates them from all other high-
temperature alloys. Nb alloys stand out as a dis-
tinctive class of materials known for their excep-
tional toughness at RT and strength retention at
temperatures exceeding 1200 /C176C.

istic makes them indispensable for critical high-
temperature structural elements in space explo-

MANUFACTURING METHODS
OF REFRACTORY ALLOYS
Casting
Casting of refractory alloys requires numerous
steps. The ﬁrst crucial step in the process is
choosing the suitable refractory alloy depending on
the application’s needs and considering the major
aspects like the working temperature, mechanical
characteristics, and chemical resistance.
77 Refrac-
tory alloys are difﬁcult to process owing to their
exceptionally high melting temperatures. The cho-
sen alloy is melted in a high-temperature furnace
using processes like vacuum arc melting, EBM, or
induction melting.
78 The composition of the alloy
affects the melting temperature. 79 To attain the
desired characteristics, alloying elements may be
introduced during the melting process.
80 For
instance, the inclusion of substances like tungsten,
rhenium, or hafnium can improve the strength and
durability of materials at high temperatures.
81 A
mold is created based on the required component
form. Since they can survive the high temperatures
and pressures required to cast refractory alloys,
refractory molds composed of materials like gra-
phite or ceramic are often preferred.
82,83 An appro-
priately shaped component is created by pouring the
molten alloy into the prepared mold.
84 Various
casting techniques, including centrifugal, vacuum,
and investment, are used depending on the require-
ments.
85 Once the material has been cast, it must
cool and harden inside the mold. The cooling rate is
meticulously managed to maintain consistency and
reduce the possibility of product errors. After the
alloy has formed, other procedures, such as heat
treatment, machining, and surface ﬁnishing, could
be applied to it in order to match exact technical
requirements. Rigorous quality control techniques
are important to guarantee that the completed
refractory alloy components fulﬁll the required
speciﬁcations and performance criteria.
86 Defects
can be found using non-destructive testing methods,
including X-ray inspection and ultrasonic testing.
The cast refractory alloy parts are incorporated into
the desired applications, which may include rocket
nozzles, jet engine components, furnace elements,
and different aerospace and industrial parts. Due to
the high temperatures and particular characteris-
tics of refractory alloys, casting needs specialized
tools, knowledge, and quality assurance procedures.
It is a crucial process for creating parts that can
survive hostile conditions and has been vital in
improving technology across several sectors.
There are some prominent defects in the casting
process of refractory metals and alloys. One of the
major issues with this process is that the refractory
metals are highly prone to oxidation at high tem-
peratures, which can result in impurities and
brittleness of the alloys.
87,88 Using a controlled
environment (a vacuum or inert gas) can solve this
issue and stop reactions with ambient oxygen.
Coatings or ﬂuxes can also be utilized. Since the
refractory metals have a high melting temperature,
failure to achieve this can result in poor homogene-
ity of metals in an alloy, directly impacting the ﬁnal
alloy’s mechanical properties.
89,90 Figure 1 shows a
schematic of the casting method. 91
Introduction of the electron beam and arc melting
techniques in industries enables the production of
cast Nb alloy ingot reliably with controlled intersti-
tial composition at a commercial scale.
92,93 The cast
structure, however, did not result in the desired
room temperature deformation characteristics. 94
The weak grain boundaries at ambient temperature
cause a signiﬁcant loss in the ambient temperature
ductility, making it difﬁcult to manufacture compo-
nents in the desired shapes.78 Since the early 1960s,
the microstructure of the cast structure of Nb alloys
has been controlled through the thermo-mechanical
processes (or wrought process). The cast method is
still used to purify and manufacture Nb alloys.
94
The cast ingot of Nb alloys is then subjected to the
thermomechanical process to achieve the desired
microstructure, shape, and mechanical properties.92
Currently, commercialized Nb alloys are not avail-
able in cast form but only in wrought form.
Also, although Nb-based RHEAs are yet to be
commercialized, the vacuum arc melting-based cast
method is primarily applied to develop and explore
Nb-based RHEAs. For example, Guo et al. have
successfully manufactured BCC phase NbTaTiV
RHEAs with a high compressive yield strength of
up to 965 MPa and plastic strain of up to 50% using
vacuum arc melting, signifying the importance of the
casting method in the preparation of high-quality
RHEAs.
95 Similarly, Stepanov et al.96 and Liu et al.97
have also prepared high-quality NbAlTiV and
NbHfMo0.5TiV0.5Si0.7 RHEAs using the casting

stresses, 1020 MPa and 2134 MPa, respectively.

The ﬁrst step is to turn the raw material into ﬁne
metal or alloy powders. These powders should have
the required composition and particle size distribu-

99–101 The right alloy composition is
achieved by combining the particles, which can be
mixed using various mixing techniques or in a ball
mill.
101,102 The combined powders are then com-
pressed into a certain form with a hydraulic press or
other compaction apparatus, which create a low-
density, ‘‘green’’ compact.103 In PM, sintering is a
crucial phase. In a controlled environment, the
green compact is heated to a level below the melting
point of the metal in the refractory alloy. 103,104
During the sintering process, the metal particles
connect with one another by diffusion, creating a
solid, dense structure. The majority of the porosity
produced during compaction is eliminated by the
procedure. The alloy being treated determines the
sintering temperature and duration. Extra process-
ing stages may be needed further to enhance the
density and characteristics of the refractory
alloy.
105 HIP is a process that simultaneously
applies high pressure and temperature to get rid
of any remaining porosity and to improve mechan-
ical characteristics.
106 The component may go
through secondary operations like machining and
grinding to get the appropriate ﬁnal dimensions and
surface polish after sintering or any other process-
ing. Throughout the entire process, quality control
is crucial. The components made of the completed
refractory alloy are examined for ﬂaws and tested to
ensure that they have the expected mechanical and
thermal characteristics, such as strength, hardness,
and high-temperature resistance. To further
improve their performance in high-temperature
applications, the components may, if necessary, go
through further ﬁnishing procedures such as coat-
ing or heat treatment. Refractory alloys may be
made precisely, with exact control over composition
and properties, and powder metallurgy can achieve
complex shapes. It is particularly helpful in indus-
tries where dimensional stability, strength, and
resilience to high temperatures are essential
characteristics.

Hot Isostatic Pressing (HIP)
HIP refers to a post-processing part of the PM
process used to improve and densify the properties
of materials, particularly refractory alloys. 106 This
post-processing technique involves heating a mate-
rial to high pressures while an inert gas environ-
ment surrounds it. 112 HIP can be essential for
improving the overall performance of refractory
alloys, eliminating defects, and fortifying their
mechanical properties.106,112 The multi-step process
has been reﬁned over time to increase its effective-
ness and efﬁciency.
113,114 In this process, high-
purity refractory alloy powder is placed into a mold
or other container.113,115 This powder may consist of
a combination of powders or the same material used
in the initial manufacturing process to create
composite products. Enclosing the mold or container
and eliminating all air creates a vacuum or low-
pressure condition. This step in the HIP process is
crucial to remove any impurities and halt oxidation.
Injecting a high-pressure gas, usually argon, occurs
after the sealed container has been heated to a
speciﬁc temperature.
116 Pressure and high temper-
ature work together to sinter and compress the
powder. The isostatic technique produces a more
uniform density since pressure is applied consis-
tently from all directions.
115,116 The material is
exposed to high pressures and temperatures for a
predeﬁned amount of time. This allows for the
consolidation of the powder particles and the
removal of any internal defects or cavities. After
the dwell time, the material is gradually cooled to
room temperature, when the HIP container is
opened and the densiﬁed material is removed. HIP
machines with upgraded gas management systems
have more precise control over the composition and
pressure of the gas, which improves efﬁciency and
performance.
117 Improved temperature monitoring
and control systems that ensure the material is
heated and cooled at the proper rates help to lessen
the risk of thermal stresses and deformation.
118
Some modern HIP machines may reach pressure
levels substantially higher than those of previous
generations, which makes denser and more compact
components.
119 The loading and unloading process
has been automated thanks to sophisticated process
control systems, making the HIP process more
reliable and consistent.
112,113 Developments in mold
design and production have made it feasible to make
complex components with the appropriate quality,
enabling HIP’s capacity to manufacture difﬁcult
shapes.
113,114
The HIP process has been widely investigated for
AM and PM refractory alloy components to increase
their quality and properties. During the HIP pro-
cess, the combined pressure and temperature elim-
inate the internal residual pores in AM and PM
refractory alloys, thereby enhancing their proper-
ties. A study showed a dense C-103 part manufac-
tured by HIP without inclusion and pores with
properties equivalent to the wrought C103.
110 Also,
the RT ductility of HIP-ed C-103 exceeds that of the
wrought counterparts.60 Senkov et al.120 achieved a
signiﬁcantly high yield strength ( > 2000 MPa) and
fracture strain ( > 50%) of the NbAlMo 0.5Ta0.5TiZr
by incorporating the HIP process after casting. 120
Although HIP has the potential to manufacture
high-quality refractory alloys, these studies are
limited to the laboratory scale. Manufacturing a
large volume of refractory alloy parts using HIP at
the industrial scale has yet to be adopted.

REFRACTORY SYSTEMS
Nb-containing refractory systems are usually
meant to be used at elevated temperatures, so these
systems are expected to show superior mechanical
properties in the high-temperature range. For ultra-
high-temperature applications, the mechanical
properties of various refractory alloys are generally
compared at elevated temperatures, and the one
that fulﬁlls the desired requirements is selected
from all other available alloys. The common
mechanical properties of Nb-based refractory alloys
that are of high interest in engineering applications
at the elevated temperature range are tensile
strength, yield strength, elastic modulus, and creep
strength. The unique characteristic of Nb-based
refractory system is its relatively high RT ductility.
Optimization of the alloy composition, control of the
microstructure by thermochemical and heat treat-
ments of the alloy, and control of phase stability are
being utilized. The following section will review and
discuss the tensile properties of the various Nb-
containing refractory systems.

SUMMARY
A detailed review of Nb-based refractory systems
for strength–ductility variation with increasing
temperature has been performed to explore their
temperature-dependent strength behavior. Among
Nb alloys considered here, C103, Cb752, Nb-1Zr, FS
85, and C129Y, RT tensile strengths follow an
increasing trend: Nb-1Zr < C103 < Cb752 <
FS85 < C129Y. The strength variation between
the Nb alloys is due to the dominant strengthening
mechanism in the respective alloy system. Nb-1Zr
and C103 are dispersion-strength alloys, while
Cb752, FS85, and C129Y are solid solution + dis-
persion-strength alloys. Since Nb-1Zr and C103 are
dispersion-strengthened alloys, their strength
behavior is sensitive to the oxygen content in the
alloy. Also, the tensile strength decreases with
increasing temperature in the low-temperature
regime and remains stable up to a certain interme-
diate temperature range. There is a decrease with a
further increase in the temperature in all the Nb
alloys considered except C129Y. The initial decrease
in the strength has been attributed to the ther-
mally-activated recovery process. In the intermedi-
ate range, the strain hardening during tensile
testing is balanced by the dynamical recovery
process, leading to a stable strength in a certain
temperature range. The strength decreases when
the temperature increases to a level where the
diffusion assists the deformation process. In the
ultra-high temperature range, the diffusion-con-
trolled deformation leads to a continuous decrease
in strength with an increase in temperature. In
C129Y, the strength does not change in a low-
temperature range but decreases continuously with
increased temperatures at intermediate and high
temperatures. This could be attributed to this
alloy’s dominant solid-solution strengthening and
limited strain-hardening effect. Also, the ductility
decreases with increased temperature in the inter-
mediate temperature range in all the Nb-based
alloys, which has been attributed to the dynamic
strain aging of the alloy in this temperature regime.
In the high-temperature regime, the ductility
increases rapidly with increased temperature,
reaching much higher values than in the low and
intermediate temperature ranges.
Nb-based RHEAs show a wide range of strength
and ductility, and solid-solution hardening dictates
these characteristics. The potent solid-solution
strengtheners, such as high melting temperature
refractory elements such as W, Mo, Ta, Re, and Hf,
increase the strength and thermal stability over a
wide temperature range of Nb-containing RHEAs.
The absence of these elements in RHEAs shows low
strength and thermal stability in high-temperature
regimes. The strength of these RHEAs was reduced
drastically in diffusion-dominated temperature
regimes. The concepts established in this study
could help steer further research and development
of materials for extreme temperature applications.
