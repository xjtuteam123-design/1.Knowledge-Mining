1. Introduction
Eutectic high-entropy alloys (EHEAs), ﬁrst proposed by Lu et al.
in 2014, [1] have been in the spotlight in the ﬁeld of structural
materials as they combine the advantages of high-entropy alloys
(HEAs) and eutectic alloys, which show excellent mechanical
properties and potential industrial applications. [2–8] In addition,
these EHEAs possess good liquidity and low melting point. They
can be directly cast into industrial-scale ingots with few casting
defects and uniform composition. [9–11] EHEAs have good
liquidity and exceptional properties,
creating considerable prospects for
their application. [12,13] Since their early
days, dozens of EHEAs have been devel-
oped and investigated, such as face-
centered-cubic (FCC) þ B2 EHEAs, [1,11,14]
FCC þ Laves EHEAs, [15–18] FCC þ σ
EHEAs,[19–22] FCC þ body-centered-cubic
(BCC) EHEAs, [23] BCC þ hexagonal-
close-packed (HCP) EHEAs, [24] and
FCC þ BCC þ Laves EHEAs. [25]
As-cast FCC þ B2 (AlCoCrFeNi 2.1,
Fe20Co20Ni41Al19, and Al 0.9CoFeNi2)
EHEAs showed excellent strength –ductility
properties due to the semicoherent phase
interface relationship and low ratio of hard
B2 phase, giving them an advantage
over most reported HEAs and conven-
tional eutectic alloys. [1,11,14] Moreover, the
mechanical properties of such a system
can be improved by thermomechanical treatment and directional
solidiﬁcation.[4,5,26] Shi et al. [4] and Reddy et al. [26] successfully
achieved an ultimate tensile strength in excess of 1.6 GPa and
a plasticity of more than 10% in FCC þ B2 EHEA by introducing
an ultra ﬁne-grained duplex microstructure (via cold rolling)
and a heterogeneous microstructure (via warm rolling), res-
pectively. Shi et al. [5] also developed an FCC þ B2 EHEA with
a hierarchically organized herringbone structure using direc-
tional solidifcation technology. This directionally solidi ﬁed
EHEA exhibited a large uniform elongation of 50% and high
tensile strength of 1060 MPa. Besides, recent research has also
shown that FCC þ B2 EHEAs show good wear resistance, high
oxidation resistance, and excellent thermal stability. [27–29]
Except for the FCC þ B2 EHEAs, many reported EHEAs
showed weak plasticity due to the high volume fraction
of the hard intermetallic compound phase [17–25] such as
CoCrFeNiZr0.55,[17] CoCrFeNiHf0.4,[18] CoFeNi1.4VMo,[19]
Fe2Ni2CrMo1.25,[20] Fe35Ni25Cr25Mo15,[21] Nb25Sc25Ti25Zr25,[24]
MoFeCrTiWAlNb3,[25] and CoFeNi 2V0.5Nb0.75.[19] These
EHEAs show high strength, but their plasticity is close to zero.
Besides, it is worth noting that most reported EHEAs contain
expensive and high density of metal elements, such as Hf, Zr,
Ta, Nb, W, and Pd, [15–22,30] which are not conducive to industrial
applications. Therefore, improving the plasticity, reducing the
manufacturing cost, and decreasing the overall density are key
to the industrial application of such EHEAs.

Eutectic high-entropy alloys (EHEAs), with good castability, compositional
homogeneity, and outstanding properties, are becoming hotpots in materials
ﬁeld. It is worth noting that most reported EHEAs are based on expensive and
high density of metal elements, such as Hf, Zr, Ta, Nb, W, and Pd, which increase
the overall cost and quality. Herein, by selecting the cost-effective and low-density
of Ti and Si as eutectic forming elements, a novel CoCrFeNi 2(TiSi)0.7 EHEA with
face-centered-cubic (FCC) þ M16Ti6Si7-type silicide is designed and prepared. The
evolution of microstructure and mechanical properties is investigated under
different annealing conditions. The CoCrFeNi 2(TiSi)0.7 EHEA shows excellent
high-temperature mechanical properties. At 800 °C, the as-cast and annealed
samples do not fracture under a compressive strain exceeding 50%. In particular,
a long period of high-temperature annealing dramatically increases the high-
temperature yield strength from 510 to 703 MPa, which exceeds that of most
reported EHEAs and some refractory high-entropy alloys.

In this work, by selecting the cost-effective and low density
of Ti and Si as eutectic forming elements, a novel
CoCrFeNi2(TiSi)0.7 EHEA with density of 7.21 g cm /C0 3 was
designed and prepared. Yet, the as-cast CoCrFeNi 2(TiSi)0.7
EHEA with FCC þ M16Ti6Si7-type silicide also showed inherent
brittleness at room temperature. In order to further improve the
mechanical properties of the alloy, the microstructure evolution
and mechanical properties of the CoCrFeNi 2(TiSi)0.7 EHEA
under different annealing condtions were investigated.
2. Results and Discussion
2.1. Microstructure of As-Cast TiSi-EHEA
Figure 1a,b shows the microstructure of the as-cast TiSi-EHEA at
low and high magni ﬁcation, respectively. The SEM-secondary
electrons (SE) images show typical cell eutectic morphologies
with a lamellar structure within the eutectic cell and a network
structure at the eutectic cell boundaries. The XRD pattern in
Figure 1c shows that the crystal structures of the as-cast TiSi-
EHEA are composed of FCC and M 16Ti6Si7-type silicide. The
DSC curve in Figure 1d shows that only one sharp endother-
mic/exothermic peak is observed, which further con ﬁrmed that
the alloy is eutectic in composition. In addition to sharp melting/
solidiﬁcation peaks, a weak exothermic peak can also be found on
the heating curve between 700 and 950 °C, indicating that the
TiSi-EHEA shows a precipitation transformation reaction within
this temperature range.
The scanning TEM (STEM) analysis of the as-cast TiSi-EHEA
is presented in Figure 2 . The STEM-bright- ﬁeld (BF) image in
Figure 2a clearly shows the ﬁne lamellar structure of the two
different phases, with an interlamellar spacing of /C25 300 nm.
The corresponding selected-area electron diffraction (SAED)
patterns in Figure 2b,c further ascertain that the dark and white
phases are the FCC and M 16Ti6Si7-type silicide, respectively.
The M 16Ti6Si7-type silicide has a complex cubic structure. The
STEM–EDS mapping in Figure 2d indicated that the FCC is
mainly enriched with Cr and Fe elements, while the M 16Ti6Si7-
type silicide is mainly enriched with Ni, Ti, and Si elements. The
Co element is uniformly distributed in eutectic dual phases.

Ni-, Ti-enriched phase is η-precipitate with a hexagonal

of SAED and EDS, it can be concluded that the σ-precipitate
and η-precipitate belong to FeCr 2-type phase and Ni 3Ti-type
phase, respectively.
The above SEM images indicated that the eutectic microstruc-
ture of TiSi-EHEA has a critical instability temperature. This is
also observed in traditional eutectic alloys. [33–35] According to
Cline et al., [36] the atomic migration rate V in eutectic alloys is
mainly determined by the ratio of diffusion coef ﬁcient D and
temperature T. The reported results showed that the diffusion
coefﬁcient D of the alloys slowly increased until the critical
temperature was reached, after which it increased exponentially.
In addition, most eutectic microstructures contain “large curva-
ture” positions, such as terminations and ledges, which further
exacerbate the instability of the eutectic microstructure above the
critical temperature. [10,22]
According to Sharma et al. [37] and Kampe et al., [38] the insta-
bility mechanism of the eutectic microstructure was dominated
by a continuous coarsening model and a discontinuous coarsen-

Figure 8 a,b shows the Vickers hardness and room-tempera-
ture compressive stress –strain curves of the TiSi-EHEA at
elevated temperature, respectively. The as-cast TiSi-EHEA has
high yield strength (YS, 2280 MPa) and high hardness value
(/C25 643 HV), but its plasticity is limited (only /C25 1.1%). The brittle
behavior of the as-cast sample may be attributed to the presence
of hard M 16Ti6Si7-type silicide with a higher volume fraction
(53.7%) and more stress concentration positions, which result
in microcracks that are nucleated on the M 16Ti6Si7-type silicide
and then propagate catastrophically along or through the eutectic
microstructure. Compared to the as-cast TiSi-EHEA, the samples
that were annealed at 700, 800, and 900 °C exhibit higher hard-
ness with 735 HV, 687 HV, and 660 HV, respectively, which are
directly related to the high density of precipitates in the eutectic
microstructure. When the annealing temperature is increased
further, the hardness decreases considerably from 643 HV to
567 HV due to the instability of eutectic microstructure and free
of precipitates. However, the plasticity is increased only slightly
from 1.1% to 3.9%.
In order to explore the effect of precipitates on the high-
temperature mechanical properties of the TiSi-EHEA,
high-temperature (800 °C) compression tests were performed
on as-cast and 700 °C /C2 12 h-annealed samples. Figure 8c shows
the high-temperature (800 °C) compressive engineering
stress–strain curves of the TiSi-EHEA at the as-cast and
700 °C /C2 12 h annealed. At 800 °C, the samples did not fracture
under a compressive strain of more than 50%, indicating that
TiSi-EHEA has good high-temperature plasticity. Nevertheless,
the high density of precipitates does not improve the high-tem-
perature strength of the TiSi-EHEA. On the contrary, they have a
deleterious effect on the high-temperature strength, and the YS
is reduced from 510 to 450 MPa after annealing at 700 °C /C2 12 h.
This may be attributed to the fact that the precipitate transforma-
tion reaction temperature is close to the compressive test temper-
ature. The high-temperature compressive strain –hardening
response curves of the TiSi-EHEA at the as-cast and
700 °C /C2 12 h annealed are shown in Figure 8d. After reaching
YS, both samples show similar softening evolution trends as the
true strain increases.

3. Conclusion
In this study, a novel CoCrFeNi 2(TiSi)0.7 EHEA was successfully
designed and prepared using the cost-effective and low-density Ti
and Si as the eutectic phase-forming elements. The evolution of
the eutectic microstructure and mechanical properties under
different annealing condtions was investigated. The main
conclusions drawn from the study are as follows. 1) The as-cast
TiSi-EHEA presented typical cell eutectic morphologies with a
lamellar structure within the eutectic cell and a network structure
at the eutectic cell boundaries. Both crystal structures were
composed of the Cr-, Fe-enriched FCC phase as well as the
Ni-, Ti-, Si-enriched M 16Ti6Si7-type silicide. 2) When annealed

4. Experimental Section
Ingots of CoCrFeNi 2(TiSi)0.7 EHEA (named TiSi-EHEA) were manufac-
tured by vacuum arc melting in an atmosphere of high-purity Ar. The purity
of the selected constituent metal elements (Co, Cr, Fe, Ni, and Ti) was
higher than 99.95 wt%, while the purity of the selected constituent
nonmetallic element Si was higher than 99.99 wt%. To obtain chemical
homogeneity, the ingot was repeatedly melted and ﬂipped at least six
times. After they were completely cooled; the ingots were removed from
the water-cooled copper mold. Then, the ﬁve ingots were heated at a heat-
ing rate of 8 °C min/C0 1 to 700, 800, 900, 1000, and 1100 °C for 12 h, respec-
tively, and subsequently furnace cooled. The eight ingots were heated at a
heating rate of 8 °C min /C0 1 to 1100 °C for 24, 36, 60, 96, 144, 204, 276, and
360 h, respectively, and then furnace cooled.
The thermal behavior of the as-cast EHEA was characterized by differ-
ential scanning calorimetry (DSC, Netzsch F3 404 Germany) under an Ar
jet. The temperature ranged from 25 to 1600 °C with a heating/cooling
speed of 5 °C min/C0 1. The crystal structures of all the samples were investi-
gated by X-ray diffraction (XRD, EMPYREAN Netherlands) using Cu K α
radiation. The scanning range was from 20° to 100° with a step of
0.033°. The eutectic microstructure of all the samples was characterized
using a scanning electron microscope (SEM, Zeiss Supra 55 Germany).
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEM-F200 USA) equipped with
an attached X-ray energy-dispersive spectrometer (EDS) was used to fur-
ther analyze the phase constituents. Compressive tests at room tempera-
ture were carried out using a universal compression tester (DNS-100,
Germany) with sample dimensions of Ф4m m /C2 8 mm at a train speed
of 1 /C2 10/C0 3 s/C0 1, while high-temperature compressive tests were con-
ducted using a thermal simulation machine (Gleeble-3500) with sample
dimensions of Ф6m m /C2 9 mm at a train speed of 1 /C2 10/C0 3 s/C0 1.
Hardness was tested by a Vickers hardness tester with a load force of
500 g and a holding time of 12 s. The average value was obtained at
ten different points on each sample.
