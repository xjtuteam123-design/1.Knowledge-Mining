Effect of Recovery Heat Treatment on Microstructure
and Properties of IN783 Bolts in Service
Shibao Sun, Siyou Tao, Sailong Wang, Jiaqing Wang, Junjian Liu, Yue Wu, and Xiaodong Du
Submitted: 6 June 2022 / Revised: 28 July 2022 / Accepted: 21 September 2022 / Published online: 12 October 2022
Various testing techniques established the association between precipitates and the mechanical character-
istics of IN783 alloy at high temperatures. The results show that the b phase is coarser at the grain
boundary and that the Ni 5Al3 phase precipitates inside the b phase as service time increases, which destroys
the structure of the b phase, weakening the strength of the grain boundary and reducing the ductility of the
alloy at high temperatures. Meanwhile, increasing the size of the c¢ phase coarser from 53 to about 74 nm
enhances the yield strength of the alloy. Following the recovery heat treatment, the grain boundary b phase
is precipitated again with a smaller size and no Ni 5Al3, and the size of the c¢ phase is reﬁned to the unused
level. The alloy material’s high-temperature ductility is restored, which extends the material’s service life.
Keywords b phase, c¢ phase, grain boundary strength,
high-temperature tensile property, IN783, Ni 5Al3
phase
1. Introduction
IN783 alloy was a low thermal expansion, oxidation-
resistant Fe-Ni-Co base superalloy developed by Special Metals
Company in the 1990s (Ref 1, 2). The ﬁrst development of
superalloys originated in 1930 when a British company
developed a low carbon titanium nickel alloy, coded Nimon-
ic75; During the Second World War, due to its wide application
prospects in the ﬁeld of war, superalloys were vigorously
developed. American companies successively developed M-
252, Waspaloy (Ref 3), Udimet500, and other superalloys.
Subsequently, MAR-M, Udimet, and Inconel series alloys were
successively developed, and the superalloy entered the golden
period of development. Compared with other traditional low
thermal expansion superalloys, more aluminum was added to
IN783 to precipitate the b-NiAl phase during aging, which
improved the resistance of stress accelerating grain boundary
oxidation (SAGBO), provided grain boundary strength, and
supported the high-temperature mechanical properties of the
alloy (Ref 4-6). Meanwhile, the chromium level was rigorously
managed to compensate for the rise in thermal expansion
coefﬁcient generated by aluminum. Together, chromium and
aluminum increased the alloy’s oxidation resistance, resulting
in an alloy with outstanding overall mechanical qualities at
650 /C176 C. Due to the beneﬁts of ultra-supercritical thermal power
generating units such as low coal consumption, reduced
pollution emissions, and high electricity production efﬁciency,
thermal power generation has steadily transitioned to ultra-
supercritical power generating units. Under the harsh service
environment of ultra-supercritical power generating units
(steam pressure of 25 MPa, temperature of over 580 /C176 C), the
IN783 alloy nevertheless demonstrated good high-temperature
characteristics. As a result, it may be employed as a bolt
material in ultra-supercritical thermal power units to link the
cylinder ﬂange and the valve ﬂange (Ref 7). However, with the
prolongation of service time and material degradation, a
substantial number of brittle fracture events occur. Long-term
exposure to high temperatures has resulted in considerable
changes in the composition and shape of each precipitate in the
IN783 alloy, resulting in changes in its mechanical character-
istics (Ref 8–10). In general, it is extremely common to
manually manage and alter the heat treatment parameters to
balance the characteristics of alloys and produce outstanding
comprehensive mechanical properties (Ref 11, 12). Tang et al.
studied the thermal deformation behavior of the supersaturated
Inconel 783 superalloy, which demonstrated the strong block-
ing effect of solute elements on the recrystallized grain size
(Ref 13). Xu et al. investigated the oxidation behavior of the
coating and 783 alloy at 650-800 /C176 C for 3850 h, which showed
that the bare 783 alloy was unable to grow external alumina
below 750 /C176 C due to its insufﬁcient Al content and thus
showed bell-shaped oxidation kinetics dependent on tempera-
ture (Ref 14). Han et al. studied the impact, tensile, and fatigue
tests of In783 alloy at 4000, 23,000, and 48,000 h to explore
the relationship between microstructure and properties. The
results showed that a new Ni5Al3 phase was found in the b
phase. As the aging time increased, the Ni5Al3 phase grew
gradually in the phase, which destroyed the martensitic
structure of the b phase and degraded the toughness (Ref 15).

2. Experimental
The samples were obtained from the axial core sections of
IN783 bolts that had been in service for 0 h, 13,000 h, and
39,000 h at 600 /C176 C, respectively. The chemical composition of
the samples was tested using an X-MET7000 portable direct-
reading spectrometer, and the results are reported in Table 1. The
chemical element composition of the samples was within the
standard range. On samples with service times of 13,000 and
39,000 h, recovery heat treatment was performed. The recovery
heat treatment process was separated into solid solutions:
1150 /C176 C, holding for 1 h, air cooling to room temperature; b
aging: 840 /C176 C, holding for 6 h, air cooling to room temperature;
c¢ aging: 720 /C176 C, holding for 8 h, at a speed of 55 /C176 C/h furnace
cooling to 630 /C176 C, holding for 8 h, air cooling to room
temperature. The standard heat treatment process includes solid
solution: 1120 /C176 C, holding for 1 h, air cooling to room temper-
ature; b aging: 840 /C176 C, holding for 4 h, air cooling to room
temperature; c¢ aging: 720 /C176 C, holding for 8 h, at a speed of
55 /C176 C/h furnace cooling to 630 /C176 C, holding for 8 h, air cooling to
room temperature. In comparison to the standard heat treatment,
the recovery heat treatment raises the solution temperature to
make the chemical composition of the supersaturated solid
solution more uniform, and prolonging the aging time causes
more b phase to precipitate, improving the alloy’s high-
temperature performance. As stated in Table 2, the samples were
numbered. The metallographic and high-temperature tensile
specimens were cut along the axial direction of the bolt. Figure 1
shows the diameters of bolts and tensile specimens at high
temperatures. The metallographic sample was 10 * 10 * 10 mm
in size. It was polished using 180 mesh, 400 mesh, 800 mesh,
1000 mesh, and 1500 mesh metallographic sandpaper, and then
polished. An etching solution of 2.5 g CuCl

3. Results and Discussion
The effects of service duration and recovery heat treatment
on the tensile characteristics of materials at high temperatures
are clearly shown in Fig. 2. It can be seen that with the increase
in service time, yield strength increased from 810 to 841 MPa
and 884 MPa, elongation decreased from 21.6 to 11.3 and
10.7%, and tensile strength was maintained at about 1000 MPa
without much change. After recovery heat treatment, yield
strength and tensile strength were also reduced, with yield
strength falling to 740 and 750 MPa and tensile strength falling
to 898 and 942 MPa, respectively. At the same time, the
elongation of sample 4 rose by 27%. And the elongation of
sample 5 grew by 106%, which is greater than the unserved
bolt, demonstrating that the material’s ﬂexibility has been
recovered or even enhanced.
To better understand the mechanical properties of the
material at high temperatures, the microstructure of tensile
fracture at high temperatures is observed in Fig. 3. As the
service time increased from 0 to 13,000 h, and then to
39,000 h, the proportion of intergranular fracture zone (red
dotted line marking area) in high-temperature tensile fracture
continued to rise. The fracture mode evolved from ductile to
brittle with time, indicating that grain boundary strength and
plasticity were decreasing, and cracks occurred preferentially at
grain boundaries during the tensile process. After recovery heat
treatment, the proportion of the intergranular fracture zone in
tensile fracture at high temperature declined, indicating that
grain boundary and plastic toughness were regained. And the
proportion of intergranular fracture zone in the fracture of
sample 5 was the lowest, meaning that the fracture form was a
predominantly ductile fracture, as shown by its elongation of

4. Conclusion
With increasing use time, the size and amount of b phase at
the grain border increase gradually, destroying the original
microstructure and decreasing the amount of b phase in the
grain. Simultaneously, the Ni 5Al3 phase precipitates within the
grain boundary b phase, destroying the martensite structure of
the grain boundary b phase and weakening the b phase and
grain border.
The solid solution of IN783 bolt material precipitates grain
boundary b phase and intergranular b phase of reasonable size
and uniform distribution following recovery heat treatment.
