A Novel Al 0.5CuFeNiV High Entropy Alloy: Phase Analysis, Microstructure and
Compressive Properties
Jiaojiao Yi1,+, Lu Wang 2,+, Fuyang Cao 3, Lin Yang 2 and Mingqin Xu 1
1School of Mechanical Engineering, Jiangsu University of Technology, Changzhou 213001, P .R. China
2School of Materials Science and Engineering, Jiangsu University of Technology, Changzhou 213001, P .R. China
3School of Materials Science and Engineering, Jiangsu University, Zhenjiang 212013, P .R. China
The phase component, microstructure and compressive properties of a novel high entropy alloy, Al 0.5CuFeNiV in as-cast and annealed
conditions were investigated. Its phase structure is composed of FCC +B2 (ordered BCC) phase in both states. The ordered BCC phase is the
major dendritic phase depleted with Cu, while the FCC phase is enriched with Cu, forming a netlike framework. Moreover, the alloys in both
states possess a promising trade-o ﬀ in strength and ductility. Speci ﬁcally, the yield strength and the elongation of the as-cast alloy are 1456 MPa
and 10.0%, respectively. After annealing, the strength decreases to 861 MPa, while the elongation increases to 15.5 %. The combination of phase
constitution, microstructure morphology, and solid solution strengthening was conducted to explain their mechanical behavior.

1. Introduction
The high entropy alloys (HEAs) have gained extensive
research attentions due to their novel design in compositions
since proposed in 2004 by Yeh et al. 1) and Cantor et al. ,2)
which greatly expands the family of metallic alloys. 3)
Initially, HEAs are de ﬁned as the alloys composed of at
least 5 principal elements with the concentration of each
individual element in the range of 5 ­35 at%.
1) Hereby,
associated with unique properties and scienti ﬁc signi ﬁcance,
a large body of works on HEAs were published recently, 4­11)
which had been classi ﬁed into several alloy families. 5)
Thereinto, the majority of reported HEAs compositions
referred to 3d transition metal (3d tm) HEAs, based on the
Co, Cr, Fe and Ni, with addition of elements like Al, Cu, Mn,
V and Ti. According to a recent review of Miracle et al. ,
5) the
AlCuFeNi-based HEAs taken account of around 39 % in the
termed 3d tm HEAs, while the actual compositions of them
with quinary elements just consist of Al, Cr, Cu, Ni and Fe
ﬁve elements.
A compositional change in HEAs has strong e ﬀects on
microstructure and mechanical properties as well as that in
traditional alloys.
12) For instance, the investigation of
Stepanov et al. 13) showed that there is only a single FCC
phase before x < 0.5 while the sigma-phase formed except
FCC phase and its volume fraction increased with an
increased V content after x > 0.5 in CoCrFeMnNiV x alloys.
Meanwhile, the continuous strengthening was obtained as
the volume fraction of sigma-phase increases. Moreover, the
enhancement of overall performance was also con ﬁrmed by
a certain amount of V addition, which is in agreement with
the optimizing hardness and wear resistance caused by V
addition in Al
0.5CoCrCuFeNiV .14) Among the abovemen-
tioned elements, Al is regarded as a candidate for forming
and stabilizing the BCC phase,
5) which is usually added to
form di ﬀerent tm base alloys. For example, Al xCoCrFeNi
alloys can possess FCC, BCC or mixed crystal structure, at
low, high or middle Al content.
15) Among this series,
AlxCoCrCuFeNi alloy with x = 0.5 also possesses a typical
single-phase microstructure. 16,17) Moreover, the alloy with
x = 0.5 possesses a high plasticity ( >30% elongation) and
large strain-hardening capacity (about 160 %) together.
Thus, in the present work, combining the advantage of
V and 0.5Al optimizing mechanical properties, V was thus
added into the AlCuFeNi-baed alloy with the Al content
halved to obtain a new Al
0.5CuFeNiV HEA. Afterwards,
the phase component, microstructure and mechanical proper-
ties of as-cast and annealed Al
0.5CuFeNiV alloys were
investigated.
2. Materials and Methods
Al0.5CuFeNiV ingots were prepared by arc melting high
purity Al (99.999 %), Cu (99.99 %), Fe (99.99 %), Ni
(99.99%) and V (99.9 %) in a water-cooled cooper crucible
under a titanium-gettered argon atmosphere. To ensure
compositional homogeneity, all ingots were turned over and
remelted four times. Before suction casting, the oxidized
surface of each ingot was abraded using an angle grinder.
Cylinder-shaped samples of 4 mm in diameter and 60 mm in
length were fabricated from these ingots using a cylinder-
shaped copper mold. The actual composition of the obtained

3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Phase analysis
To optimize the phase structure in HEAs, several semi-
empirical parameters, such as con ﬁguration entropy, atomic
size di ﬀerence, electronegativity di ﬀerence etc., have been
adopted to predict the phase structure. 18) Thereinto, the
conﬁgurational entropy of high entropy alloys is widely
considered to be the dominant stabilizing mechanism for
single phase formation. However, many experimental
attempts con ﬁrmed that its validity is very ﬁnite. Actually,
only a small fraction of multi-component alloys forms a
single-phase solid solution, while the rest form multiple
phases. Likewise, the validity of other methods and
parameters are also relaxed commonly for certain high
entropy alloys. For instance, according to the Hume-Rothery
rules, the MoNbHfZrTi alloy does not meet the constraints
of forming a disordered solid solution phase. However, the
experiment evidences that its microstructure indeed consists
of a disordered solid solution phase. Thus, in the present
work the phase analysis of the studied alloy was carried out
basing on the XRD results.
XRD patterns of the studied Al
0.5CuFeNiV alloys in as-
cast and annealed conditions were shown in Fig. 1. From
the di ﬀraction peaks, dual-phase belonging to FCC and B2
(ordered BCC) structure was observed in both states. The
B2 phase is generally de ﬁned as one kind of intermetallic
compound with CsCl-type ordered BCC structure. The
diﬀerence between the ordered BCC and other BCC structure
is that the di ﬀractive peaks of (100) and (210) exist around
30°, 73° aside from the usual BCC structure di ﬀractive
peaks of (110), (200) and (211) around 44°, 64° and 81°. In
this work, we uniformly use ordered BCC phase instead of
B2 phase to avoid chaos in the following. Actually, for Al-
containing 3d tm HEAs, the ordered BCC phase widely
exists in the form of AlX (X = Ni, Fe and so on) and FeV
etc,
19) which evidenced the ordered BCC phase observed in
the currently studied alloy. The lattice parameter of the
ordered BCC phase was determined to be 290.5 pm. The
diﬀraction peaks of the FCC phase match well with that of
the FCC Cu, which is in agreement with the Cu-segregation
in the Cu-containing HEAs. The lattice parameter of the FCC
phase was thus determined to be 361.0 pm, almost same to
that of pure Cu, 361.5 pm. We suggested that the positive
mixing enthalpy between Cu and other principal elements
20)
play a vital role for Cu segregation in the solidi ﬁcation
process (as seen in Table 2). Moreover, the XRD patterns
of Al
0.5CuFeNiV in both states are in same feature, implying
that the studied alloy possesses a relatively promising thermal
stability.

4. Conclusions
In summary, a novel 3d tm HEA, Al 0.5CuFeNiV , was
prepared by a vacuum arc-melting. The phase structure of
Al0.5CuFeNiV was composed of FCC + ordered BCC
phases. Hereinto, the ordered BCC phase is the dominant
phase, while the FCC phase corresponds to the Cu-rich
regions, forming a netlike framework. Moreover, this alloy
either under as-cast state or annealed state possesses a
relatively promising trade-o ﬀ in strength and ductility.
Speciﬁcally, the yield strength and the elongation of the
as-cast alloy are 1456 MPa and 10.0 %, respectively. After
annealing, the strength decreases to 861 MPa, while the
ductility increases to 15.5 %.
