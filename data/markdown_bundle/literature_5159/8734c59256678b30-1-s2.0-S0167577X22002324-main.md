ABSTRACT 
A face-centered cubic (FCC) Al
0.3
CoCrFeNiCu high entropy alloy (HEA) is reinforced by combining recrystallized 
fine grains and discrete L1
2 
ordered nano-precipitates. Mechanical processing via cold rolling (90% reduction) 
and subsequent annealing (1000 
◦
C / 5 min + 600 
◦
C / 10 h) has been applied to achieve the strength-ductility 
synergy of Al
0.3
CoCrFeNiCu HEA. This precipitation-hardened HEA with refined grains exhibits high yield and 
ultimate tensile strength of 880 MPa and 970 MPa at room temperature, while a high tensile elongation of 29% is 
maintained at the same time, opening a strategy for the development of HEAs with strength-ductility combi -
nation in engineering applications. 
1. Introduction 
The high entropy alloys (HEAs) with multiple principal elements 
have been a front burner topic in materials science to achieve excellent 
performance of metals in recent years [1] . In particular, the HEA with 
multiphase microstructures consisting of lamellar structure presents 
strength-ductility synergy and high-temperature strengths [2,3] . In 
addition, some HEAs with high irradiation-resistance become candidate 
materials satisfying environments [4] . In general, an optimum me -
chanical property of HEAs can be obtained by mixing several transition 
elements (such as Co, Fe, Cr, Ni) [5] . If a certain amount of aluminum is 
further added to the equimolar HEAs, i.e. , Al
x
CoCrFeNi or Al
x
CoCrFe -
NiCu, they will exhibit an excellent combination of mechanical strength 
and ductility, making a great scientific research point [6] . It is well 
known that grain-refinement strengthening can been adopted to 
enhance the balance between strength and ductility of alloys [7,8] . 
Moreover, precipitation hardening is also an effective method to 
strengthen HEAs by alloying and/or heat treatment [9,10] . Tsai et al. 
reported that the spherical nano-order L1
2 
structure in Al
0.5
CoCrFeNiCu 
HEA contributed to a large precipitation strengthening (~325 MPa) in 
yield strength [11] . On the other hand, cold rolling (CR) and subsequent 
annealing is a common approach to refine the microstructure and ach -
ieve the aim of improving mechanical ductility of HEAs [12] . Further -
more, He et al. had revealed that the applicability and validity of 
precipitation hardening could be obtained in high-entropy alloy during 
thermo-mechanical processing [13] . Therefore, it is reasonable to 
hypothesize that a simultaneous improvement of strength and ductility 
can be achieved in face-centered cubic (FCC) based HEAs if the cold- 
mechanical processing and subsequent annealing is controlled ratio -
nally. In the present case, the coherent intra-granular precipitates 
coupled with grain-refinement strengthening is revealed to improve the 
mechanical strength of Al
0.3
CoCrFeNiCu alloy, while retaining a 
considerable tensile ductility of nearly 30%. 
2. Materials and methods 
The as-cast Al
0.3
CoCrFeNiCu ingot was prepared by induction 
melting with high-purity ( > 99.9% purity) monomers in high-purity 
argon gas, and then they were cold rolled (CR) until a thickness reduc -
tion of 90%. The consequent heat treatment routine was schematically 
showed in Fig. 1 a. The CR alloy was recrystallized at 1000 
◦
C / 5 mins 
(CRR), and then the CRR sheets was annealed at 600 
◦
C / 10 h (CRR- 
600). The phase structures were characterized by X-ray diffractometer 
(XRD), scanning electron microscope (SEM) configurated with energy 
dispersive spectrum (EDS) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). 
Dogbone shaped specimens were prepared along the rolling direction 
(RD) to complete the tensile tests at the strain rate of 10
-3 
s
 1 
at room 
temperature, and then the fracture morphologies were observed by SEM.

The as-cast sample presents a low yield strength (YS, 360 MPa) and 
ultimate tensile strength (UTS, 700 MPa), but high elongation (EL, 60%) 
( Fig. 3 a). The UTS of CR sample is dramatically elevated to 1200 MPa at 
the cost of plasticity, while the YS (750 MPa) and UTS (850 MPa) are 
decreased and EL (31%) is increased for CRR sample. The CRR-600 
sample exhibits enhancement of YS (880 MPa) and UTS (970 MPa), 
and also a considerable EL (29%), implementing the strength-ductility 
synchronization of Al

CoCrFeNiCu alloy.

Based on the Hall-Ptech relationship for CoCrFeNi HEA [23] , the 
grain boundary contribution is calculated to be ~ 840 MPa when the 
average grain size is about 1 μ m. The contributing yield stress of pre -
cipitations is ~ 126 MPa for L1
2 
phases [24] . Consequently, the com -
bined contribution of grain size and L1
2 
precipitates to the CRR-600 
sample can be estimated to be ~ 966 MPa. Furthermore, the size of 
the L1
2 
precipitates in the intergranular region can be further refined, 
resulting in a high mechanical strength due to the existence of disloca -
tions and lattice distortions in these regions [25] . Unfortunately, the YS 
(~880 MPa) obtained from experiment is lower than that of the calcu -
lated value, which can be attributed to the existence of Cu-rich phases 
with larger grain size. The CR sample exhibits a brittle fracture with 
some cleavage plane on the surface ( Fig. 3 c), which is corelated with the 
abundant dislocation accumulation induced by cold rolling [12] , while 
the as-cast alloy shows the typical ductile fracture ( Fig. 3 b). The CRR 
sample presents ductile fracture with abundant dimples ( Fig. 1 d) due to 
the existence of completed recrystallized fine grains. After annealing, a 
lot of tiny dimples are observed on the fracture surface of CRR-600 
sample, indicating a reservation of high ductility ( Fig. 3 e). Due to the 
interaction between dislocations and precipitates, it is rational that the 
deformation mechanism changes from dislocation cutting to bowing, 
resulting in a higher yield strength of CRR-600 sample [22] . On the 
other hand, the order L1
2 
precipitation possesses less slip systems and 
slightly deteriorate the ductility [26 – 28] . 
4. Conclusion 
A study of coupling grain boundary strengthening together with 
precipitation strengthening has been carried out in a fcc based 
Al
0.3
CoCrFeNiCu HEA. The discrete L1
2 
precipitation is uniformly 
distributed within the recrystallized matrix, leading to an excellent 
combination of exceptionally high strength (~970 MPa) and consider -
able tensile elongation (~29%) at room temperature. The ductility of 
this high strength HEA is dominated by dislocation bowing mechanisms 
coupled with grain boundary strengthening. The experimental results 
and their mechanical analysis provide an effective processing paradigm 
to design HEAs with optimum combination of high strength-ductility 
synchronization for the HEA in the future.
