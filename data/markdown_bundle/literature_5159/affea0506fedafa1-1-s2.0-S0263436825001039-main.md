1. Introduction
The refractory multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs) possess high 
melting temperatures ( T
m
), better high temperature oxidation resistance 
than conventional refractory metals, and a vast compositional space for 
microstructure design. Therefore, they are considered as promising 
candidates for high temperature structure materials beyond Ni-based 
superalloys. However, a trade-off between strength and ductility is 
still a main barrier to the application of RMPEAs. It seems that RMPEAs 
comprising of principal elements with relatively lower valence electron 
concentration (VEC) (Ti, Zr and Hf, for instance), usually have superior 
ductility at room temperature but extremely low strength at elevated 
temperatures [ 1 – 4 ]. For example, the yield strength of the ductile 
HfNbTaTiZr is only 92 MPa at 1200 
◦
C. With the addition of principal 
elements possessing high melting temperatures (such as W, Mo, Ta and 
Re etc.), the high temperature strength of RMPEAs can be effectively 
improved, but their ductility is dramatically degraded [ 5 – 8 ]. For 
example, the MoNbTaWTi maintains a high yield strength of 586 MPa at 
1200 
◦
C, while its compressive strain is only 11.5 % at room temperature 
[ 7 , 8 ]. In-situ silicides or carbides can effectively reinforce the RMPEAs 
at elevated temperatures indeed, but the increase in high temperature 
strength would be limited by the soft solid solution phase [ 9 – 11 ]. 
Moreover, the ductility of the multi-phase RMPEAs containing brittle 
reinforced phases also depends on the solid solution matrix [ 11 ]. 
Therefore, a balance between high temperature strength and room 
temperature ductility should be achieved for solid solution RMPEAs, 
which is critical to the development of RMPEAs applied as high tem -
perature structure materials.
NbTiZr alloy possesses superior ductility at room temperature (with 
a tensile elongation of 14.2 %) but really poor strength at high tem -
peratures [ 12 , 13 ]. The addition of Mo is an effective method to improve 
the strength of solid solution RMPEAs at different temperatures without 
obvious increase in their density [ 14 , 15 ]. In addition, an increase in Zr 
content can increase the lattice distortion of solid solution RMPEAs due 
to the obviously larger size of Zr atom (as seen in Table 1 ), which could 
also exert an important influence on the strength and ductility of 
RMPEAs [ 16 , 17 ]. Therefore, the Mo-Nb-Ti-Zr system is expected to 
achieve high strength and good ductility via tuning Mo and Zr contents.

x
NbTiZr 
alloys decomposes into two bcc phases at temperatures below 
700 – 1000

that the bcc phase separation may exist in MoNbTiZr alloy [ 15 ], while
