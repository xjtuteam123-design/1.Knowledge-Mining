a b s t r a c t 
In the context of cast alloy development for high temperature applications, high entropy superalloys 
(HESA) have exhibited superior cost speciﬁc tensile strength than that of superalloys such as CM247LC. 
Compositions of HESA are distinctively different from those of cast superalloys with higher contents of 
Fe and Ti, making HESA cheaper and lighter. Comparing to superalloys, although HESA has adopted the 
template of FCC-structured ( γ) matrix and coherent L1 2 -structured ( γ′ ) precipitates, γ′ is enriched with 
solutes with higher intrinsic strength, rendering positive lattice misﬁt, and the high entropy γ matrix 
may have attributed to a good combination of strength and ductility.

Superalloys are important materials for gas turbine engines in 
aerospace and energy industries [1] . Since the homologous temper- 
ature of superalloys has already reached the limit, effort s on alloy 
development have focused on improving the cost-performance by 
reducing the use of expensive elements such as rhenium (Re) [2] . 
For example, CMSX-8 [2] was developed as a more economical 
version of CMSX-4, which has been widely used in industry, and 
with respect to the cost speciﬁc tensile strength, the advantage of 
CMSX-8 over that of CMSX-4 was about 1.6 times. However, the 
Ni-based system constrains the composition design space for re- 
duction in cost and density. To search for better cost-performance 
alloys, High Entropy Alloy (HEA) allows the exploitation of vast 
composition space [3–8] , among different types of HEA [9] , Re- 
fractory High Entropy Alloys (RHEA) possess high melting points 
and are promising structural materials, however, the development 
of RHEA has yet to reach high-temperature oxidation resistance 
and a balance between high-temperature strength and room tem- 
perature ductility [6 , 10] , on the other hand, 3d transition metals 
(3d-TM) HEA possess the best speciﬁc strength and appear to be 
the most promising HEA for structural application [9] . The most 
well-known 3d-TM HEA is CoCrFeMnNi, although single-phase

CoCrFeMnNi could possess attractive combination of strength 
and toughness from ambient to cryogenic temperatures [11] , the 
strength of single-phase HEA was relatively weak at elevated 
temperatures [6 , 12 , 13] . At high temperatures, diffusion driven 
phase transformation and creep need to be considered [1] , FCC 
phase with lower intrinsic diffusivity than BCC phase should be 
the choice of structure basis for high temperature alloys [14] . Wu 
et al. [15] demonstrated that tensile yield strength of CoCrFeNi, 
CoCrMnNi, CoCrNi, CoNi and Ni dropped sharply with increasing 
temperature, and Otto et al. [12] found that the yield strength 
of CoCrFeMnNi was only 76 MPa at 600 °C. So, strengthening 
mechanisms such as solid solution strengthening, work hardening 
and grain reﬁnement, are not effective at elevated temperatures. 
Conventional superalloys have relied on ordered Ni 3 (Al,Ti)- 
based L1 2 -structured ( γ′ ) precipitates to strengthen the FCC- 
structured ( γ) matrix. And, this type of precipitates has been 
introduced in HEA systems. Shun et al. [16] and Gwalani et al. 
[17-20] investigated Al 0.3 CoCrFeNi, and observed precipitation of 
ﬁne γ′ precipitates in the γ matrix; however, the γ′ phase could 
only be stable up to 550 °C and was destabilized and replaced 
by B2-structured ( β) phase at 700 °C. He et al. [21 , 22] found that 
addition of Al and Ti in CoCrFeNi could induce formation of γ′ 
phase in the γ matrix, however, excessive addition of these two 
elements could promote the formation of brittle L2 1 -structured 
(Ni, Co) 2 TiAl phase. Yeh et al. [23 , 24] systematically studied the

which contained a γ matrix with 46% of γ′ phase and 5% of 
β phase by volumes; its yield strength at 750 °C was 473 MPa, 
although this value appeared to surpass those of solid solution 
strengthened type superalloys such as 800H and Inconel617, it was 
weaker than more advanced cast superalloys such as CM247LC 
[30] , this was mainly due to relatively low fractions and low 
solvus of γ′ phase. During the course of developing wrought HEA 
for high temperature applications [31 , 32] , low γ′ solvus had led 
to chemical instability at the front of mobile grain boundaries 
and resulted the formation of cellular γ- γ′ precipitates at grain 
boundaries during thermo-mechanical processes; this could cause 
severe hot ductility drop at around 750 °C. So, the development 
of HEA for high temperature application has been focused on 
cast type alloy to avoid hot ductility drop associated with cellular 
precipitation at grain boundaries. 
To further increase the elevated temperature strength to com- 
pete with superalloys, both the solvus and fractions of the γ′ 
phase in HEA need to be increased; however, the volume fractions 
and solvus temperature of the γ′ phase could be restricted by 
the increase in entropy of the system within the composition 
range of 5.0 at.% ≤ x ≤ 35.0 at.% [5 , 6] . To break through this 
restriction, alloy design has been shifted to Ni-rich HEA with 
Ni content beyond 35 at.% [5 , 6] . CALPHAD prediction indicates 
that an increase in Ni content beyond 35 at% could allow further 
adjustment in the composition to increase the thermal stability 
of γ matrix and γ′ precipitates, Fig. 1 (a) and 1(b). Furthermore, 
the conﬁgurational entropy of the nominal composition can still 
meet the broader deﬁnition of HEA, i.e. | /Delta1S conf. | > 1.5 R, where R 
is the universal gas constant. So, the development of Ni-rich HEA 
has taken the advantage of a broader deﬁnition of HEA in order 
to further expand the alloy design space, as shown in Fig. 2 (a). 
Since the microstructure of Ni-rich HEA with γ matrix and γ′ 
precipitates resembles that of advanced superalloys, the name 
“High Entropy Superalloys (HESA)” has been given to this class 
of materials [23 , 33-36 ]. Fig. 2 (a) shows that most of Ni-based 
superalloys cluster around 0.60 < X (Ni) < 0.65 in mole fraction 
and 1.2 < | /Delta1S conf. |/ R < 1.3, while HESA are located in the relatively 
lower Ni content and high entropy region ( > 1.5 R).

The excellent phase stability of HESA had been conﬁrmed by 
long term isothermal aging at temperatures from 700 °C to 1100 
°C for up to 10 0 0 h, only γ′ precipitates in γ matrix could be 
observed without formation of detrimental intermetallic phases 
[33] . Furthermore, the γ′ phase with high Ti content resulted 
higher anti-phase boundary (APB) energy than that of conven- 
tional superalloys [38] , which could retard superdislocations and 
strengthen γ′ phase, for example the APB energies of HESA-1 and 
superalloy CM247LC were 0.25 and 0.19 J/m 2 , respectively [33] , 
this had resulted higher hardness of HESA L1 2 -structured γ′ phase 
comparing to that of CM247LC measured by nano-indentation 
tests [33] . On the other hand, high entropy compositions in the γ
matrix of HESAs could lead to lower stacking fault energy, which 
could hinder dislocation cross-slip during deformation by increas- 
ing the spacing between the partial dislocations [34] and enhance 
the toughness by formation of twins [7 , 11 , 39] . By comparing to 
those of conventional HEA (5.0 at.% ≤ x ≤ 35.0 at.%), elevated 
temperature tensile yield strengths of HESA were about 200 MPa 
in average higher than those of reported HEA from room tempera- 
ture to 750 °C [34] , and a positive temperature dependence of the 
yield strength of HESA was identiﬁed due to high fractions of L1 2 - 
structured γ′ phase. Furthermore, the creep rupture life of HESA-3 
in directional solidiﬁed (DS) form under 982 °C and 159 MPa was 
114 h, which was similar to several ﬁrst generation superalloys; 
the steady state creep rate of HESA-3 was competitive against 
CMSX-2 [40] , this was attributed to strengthening from thermally 
stable γ′ precipitates, low stacking fault energy of the γ matrix, 
leading to a creep activation energy of 290 kJ/mol [34] . Due to high 
solute contents in γ′ phase, lattice misﬁts between FCC-structured 
γ matrix and L1 2 -structured γ′ phase in HESA were positive, 
during creep at 982 °C, γ′ phase rafted in parallel to the applied 
stress direction; this was opposite to that of advanced superalloys 
with negative misﬁt between γ matrix and γ′ precipitates [1] .

The concept of HESA has led to several recent studies in 
exploring the strengthening contributions from L1 2 -structured γ′ 
phase in HEAs, however, these works mostly focused on room 
temperature properties and had shown that this approach could be 
effective in increasing the tensile strength while retaining a good 
level of tensile ductility [29 , 42-44 ], e.g. γ′ precipitates strength- 
ened Al(Co, Fe, Ni) 12.3 Ti exhibited a tensile yield strength of 
1028 MPa with an elongation up to 47.8% [42] . On the other hand, 
microstructure optimization through heat treatment can also be 
important in order to balance strength and ductility; Zhang et al. 
[36] studied a Hf containing HESA Al 0.56 CoCrFeNi 3.36 Ti 0.56 Hf 0.015 , in 
as-cast condition, its ultimate tensile strength could reach 960 MPa 
at 750 °C but with only 3% fracture strain; by contrast, the DS 
form of HESA-3 subjected to post heat treatment to optimize the 
microstructure could possess a tensile yield strength of 855 MPa 
at 750 °C with fracture strain greater than 20% [34] . Due to its 
relatively low elemental cost and density, with respect to 750 
°C yield strength, HESA-3 performed better than most reported 
HEA data with the same testing condition and could achieve a 
3 times increase in cost-speciﬁc tensile strength comparing to 
that of CM247LC, Fig. 3 [30 , 31 , 34 , 45] , most importantly, its tensile 
elongation could be 2.7 times that of CM247LC, making HESA very 
attractive for elevated temperature applications. 
Based on the current state of the art, the development of 
HESA technology has combined several alloy design strategies; 
viewpoints of these strategies are described and discussed in the 
following: 
1. Utilize low cost elements such as Fe to lower down the cost of 
material: 
In an alloy system based on Co-Ni, although Co is relatively ex- 
pensive, it is utilized in conventional Ni-based superalloys to stabi- 
lize the γ matrix and lower the stacking fault energy [1] . Co is also 
a common element in HEA, while HEA composition space provides 
the freedom to include signiﬁcant fractions of cheap elements that 
can lower the cost. It is important to balance the cost with desired 
microstructure (FCC-structured γ matrix and L1 2 -structured γ′ 
precipitates) and suﬃcient phase stability. Fe is an ideal choice 
of cheap element serving as FCC γ matrix element, which acts 
mainly as a substitution to Ni or Co in conventional superalloys, 
however, Fe is also known to destabilize γ′ phase [46] ; CALPHAD 
is a useful tool to ensure that Fe-bearing HESA can retain suﬃcient 
fractions of stable γ′ phase. Another potential issue regarding to 
the cost is associated with processing, HESA reported are mostly 
cast alloys, and the yield of casting can be affected by alloy com- 
positions greatly [1 , 47 , 48] . In advanced superalloys, the tendency 
to form freckles defects can be related to both the content of 
refractory elements and their ratios of solid-liquid partitioning, 
since convection caused by density difference with the mushy 
zone can cause nucleation of stray grains with undesired chemical 
compositions. Therefore, HESA with low level of refractories might 
exhibit good castability for complex part, thorough investigations 
on castability of HESA should be conducted in the future. 
2. Utilize light weight elements such as Al, Ti, Cr to lower down the 
density: 
Ni-rich HEA design space presents the freedom to utilize higher 
level of light elements in the system to decrease the density of 
the alloy. However, the contents of light elements need to be 
controlled carefully in order to maintain the phase stability. In 
traditional superalloys metallurgy, Al and Ti are strong γ′ formers, 
and phase instability associated with TCP phases can be related 
to the degree of supersaturation of solutes in γ matrix [1 , 49] ; Cr 
also promotes detrimental intermetallic formations such as σ [1] ; 
CALPHAD can be an extremely useful tool in maintaining phase 
stability while performing alloy design for light weight. And, HESA 
may possess an advantage over superalloys with a higher entropy 
matrix to accommodate more solid solutions, hence maintain 
phase stability for the γ matrix [33 , 50] . 
3. Design through elemental partitioning characteristics between γ
matrix and γ′ precipitate: 
Constituent elements in HESAs possess characteristic partition- 
ing tendencies, e.g. Al and Ti partition to γ′ precipitates and Co, Cr, 
Fe partition to γ matrix, Table II . The partitioning ratios of these 
elements have signiﬁcant meanings to the design of HESA. First 
of all, the degree of entropy in γ matrix and γ′ precipitates can 
be affected; the γ matrix needs to be high entropy to utilize the 
beneﬁcial effects of HEAs, and the γ′ phase has to retain relatively 
lower entropy state to possess thermal stability. This is demon- 
strated in Table I & II , from HESA-0 to HESA-1, the substitution of 
2.0 at% Ni by 2.0 at% Ti affected the partitioning characteristics for 
other elements, resulting an increase in the entropy of γ matrix, 
and a decrease in the entropy for γ′ phase, which in turn could 
enhance its thermal stability. Furthermore, higher Ti content in 
γ′ phase could increase the APB energy to further strengthen the 
γ′ phase. The partitioning ratios can also affect the lattice misﬁt 
between the γ matrix and γ′ phase, it can inﬂuence the morphol- 
ogy of γ′ phase and the evolution of coarsening under stress [1] , 
HESAs developed so far all possess positive misﬁt, e.g. lattice misﬁt 
of HESA-3 was 0.08% at 982 °C [34] . It has been suggested that 
positive misﬁt alloys may possess better cyclic creep resistance 
than negative misﬁt alloys [51 , 52] , since cyclic creep might be 
closer to some actual turbine engine operating conditions, HESA 
should be evaluated against cyclic creep in the future.

the formation of Al 2 O 3 or Cr 2 O 3 layers:

and corrosion, whether Al 2 O 3 or Cr 2 O 3 layers can be protective 
depends on the compactness and continuity of each external oxide 
layer. For the formation of protective Al 2 O 3 , certain level of Al 
content and Al activity are required in the alloy composition, 
for example, HESA-2 and HESA-3 were both Al 2 O 3 formers and

5. Strengthening through hierarchical microstructure development: 
Recent studies have shown that composition complexity 
in HEAs could induce effective heterogeneous microstructure 
strengthening at room temperature through phase transformation 
pathway design [44 , 54-57 ]. HESA may also utilize its distinct 
stoichiometric γ′ compositional complexity to accommodate γ
formers in metastable condition, this could be achieved by quench- 
ing process and subsequent aging to induce γ particles formation 
inside γ′ . Previous studies have shown that this approach could 
be beneﬁcial for elevated temperature strengthening by retarding 
coarsening kinetics of γ′ phase [56] , and compression creep life 
at elevated temperature could be improved [58] . So, strengthen- 
ing through hierarchical microstructures should be explored for 
HESA in the future, since microstructure strengthening can be an 
effective mean to provide further strengthening without the use 
of expensive heavy elements. 
Comparing the tensile strength, ductility, cost, density, and 
surface stability between HESA-3 and CM247LC superalloy, a com- 
bined performances chart has been constructed in Fig. 4 . HESA-3 
can outperform CM247LC superalloy in most of these aspects and 
may be even more useful for applications when cost, density, and 
ductility are more demanding than high temperature strength. 
Recent studies have shown that cast type HESA have shown 
potential for high temperature applications, similar approach may 
also apply to develop wrought HESA in the future, since the 
increase in γ′ solvus can be expected to minimize the cellular 
structure embrittlement at grain boundaries [31] . Recently, artiﬁ- 
cial intelligence machine learning approach has been utilized to 
design HEA compositions and to optimize speciﬁc single property, 
such as hardness [59 , 60] ; the growing interest in the research 
ﬁeld of HESA will generate more experimental data, and artiﬁcial 
intelligence machine learning approach can be employed in the 
future to develop more advanced high entropy superalloys with 
customized and balanced properties.
