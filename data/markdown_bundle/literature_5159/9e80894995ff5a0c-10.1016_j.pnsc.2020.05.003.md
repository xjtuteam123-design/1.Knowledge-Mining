ABSTRACT
The eﬀ ect of trace B on the microstructure and creep properties under 1100 °C/130 MPa in three single crystal
superalloys with various levels of B (0, 0.01 and 0.02 wt %) additions was investigated. Compared with the
boron-free alloy, the creep rupture life decreased slightly for the alloy with 0.01 wt % B, but dropped obviously
for the 0.02 wt% B contained alloy. The low B addition had a slight eﬀ ect on the main element compositions of
γ/γ′ by the high precision atom probe tomography (APT) analysis and no signi ﬁcant change of γ/γ′ misﬁt was
observed. However, the contents of Re, Mo, Cr in γ phase were decreased with the high B addition, resulting in
the decrease of γ/γ′ misﬁt and increase of the spacing of γ/γ′ interfacial dislocation networks. Meanwhile, the
residual ( γ+γ′) eutectics and borides with a large volume fraction obviously decreased the creep rupture
properties in the high B addition alloy. This study is helpful for understanding the boron's role of strengthening
mechanism in high temperature creep of Ni-base single crystal superalloys.
1. Introduction
Over the last decades, considerable interest has been attracted in the
improvement of high temperature creep properties by the addition of
some refractory elements Re, Ta, W and Mo, which can strengthen γ
matrix in single crystal (SX) superalloys. Nevertheless, low angle grain
boundary (LAGB) defects often occur in directional solidi ﬁcation of
complex structure turbine blades made by these superalloys [ 1]. The
LAGB defects with misorientation higher than a certain tolerance can
obviously damage the creep properties of SX superalloys, resulting in
lower casting quali ﬁed rate of turbine blades. Previous work suggested
that the trace alloying additions (C and B) was an e ﬀective way to
improve the damage resistance of grain boundaries in a range of
commercial SX superalloys [ 2,3].
In the past twenty years, a large number of mechanical properties
test results revealed that the tolerance of high angle grain boundaries
(HAGBs) in some SX alloys with trace C and B additions was sig-
niﬁcantly enhanced [2,3]. For instance, Chen et al. [ 3] reported that the
additions of C and B promoted the formation of carbides along the GB,
enhancing creep performance through inhibiting GB sliding. However,
few systematic researches and analyses have been conducted on the
overall impact of these C and B additions on the SX matrix. And in
particular, the in ﬂuence of these elements on the e ﬀect of micro-
structures, heat treatment process and creep properties need to be
synthetically considered. Previous studies showed that the creep
properties of high temperature could be evidently in ﬂuenced by trace C
additions, due to the combined e ﬀects of carbides and casting pores [ 4].
But up to now, the report about the independent e ﬀect of B on the
microstructure and mechanical properties for SX superalloys has hardly
been obtained.
In directionally solidi ﬁed (DS) superalloys, there are some reports
about role of B additions. Seo et al. [ 5] reported that the ﬁnal solidi-
ﬁcation stage of the IN792 + Hf alloy was completed by the formation
of M
5B3 and M3B2 borides from the residual liquid after ( γ+γ′) eutectic
reaction. Yan et al. [ 6] reported that γ′ envelope formed around car-
bides in B containing alloys has a bene ﬁcial eﬀect on the creep life of DS
alloy under 870 °C/250 MPa. Furthermore, the element B promoted the
formation of borides which are considered to induce the incipient
melting of superalloys [ 7]. It must be noted, similar to the roles in
casting DS superalloys, the forming of low melting phases as well as

2. Experimental procedures
Three experimental alloys were directionally solidi ﬁed using a high
rate solidi ﬁcation (HRS) process with the compositions listed in
Table 1 . Alloy B-0 is the base alloy without B additions. Alloys B-1 and
B-2 contain 0.01 and 0.02 wt % B, respectively. The incipient melting
temperature and heat treatment regime of three experimental alloys are
listed in Table 2.
They were all subjected to same twice aging treatment of 1130 °C
for 4 h (air cooling) followed by annealing at 870 °C for 32 h (air
cooling). However, the solid-solution treatment was 1320 °C for 6 h (air
cooling) for both alloys B-0 and B-1, whereas it changed to 1290 °C for
6 h followed by 1305 °C for 8 h (air cooling) for alloy B-2. After fully
heat treatment, the precision machining of the creep specimens was
conducted. Creep tests were conducted at 1100 °C/130 MPa in the air.
The cross-sectional microstructures of as-cast and heat-treated as
well as creep ruptured specimens were carefully checked. The samples
were prepared using standard metallographic techniques and etched
using a solution of 100 ml HCl +80 ml H
2O + 20 g CuSO 4.
Microstructure examination was performed using an optical microscope
(OM) and a ﬁeld-emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM). A
JEOL JXA-8800R electron microprobe analyzer (EPMA) was used to
measure the concentration pro ﬁles of the eutectic region. The standard
point count method by Photoshop software was used to determine the
volume fractions of γ′ precipitates, ( γ + γ′) eutectics and secondary
phases. The Image-Pro software was used to measure the γ′ size, γ
channel width and secondary phase's size. At least ﬁve images were
used for each alloy to obtain reliable statistical results. The precipitated
phases were separated through the electrolytic phase extraction method
in a solution of 90 mL methyl alcohol, 10 ml hydrochloric acid and 1 g
tartaric acid with density of 0.04~0.1 A/cm
2. Extracted phases were
identiﬁed by X-ray di ﬀraction (XRD).
In order to identify the typical dislocation microstructure of dif-
ferent alloys during creep test, TEM observations were also conducted.
The thin foils for TEM were prepared by twin-jet thinning technique in
a solution of 10% perchloric acid and 90% alcohol at −30 °C and 30 V.
To obtain more detailed results and get better understanding about the
distribution of alloying elements around the γ/γ′ phase interface, the
atom probe tomography (APT) analyses were used for the three heat-
treated alloys. The APT analyses were performed with a Cameca LEAP
4000X Si instrument, operating in a UV laser pulsing mode with a pulse
rate of 200 kHz and at a laser energy of 40 pJ. A Cameca IVAS ®-3.6.8
software was used for APT reconstruction.

3.4. High temperature creep properties and creep fracture behavior
High temperature creep properties under the condition of 1100 °C/
130 MPa were tested for all three as-heated alloys, and the results are
present in Fig. 10. Fig. 10 a shows the creep strain-time curves of three
alloys with di ﬀerent B content after fully heat treatment at 1100 °C/
130 MPa. The creep rupture life of alloy B-0 was the longest (366.1 h),
followed by alloy B-1 (321.5 h), and alloy B-2 was the worst (197.0 h).
The creep fracture elongations of alloys B-0 and B-1 were 12.2% and
12.8%, respectively. However, the elongation of alloys B-2 was 20.2%.
This shows that the addition of 0.01 wt % B have no signi ﬁcant eﬀect on
creep fracture plasticity, while the addition of 0.02 wt% B has sig-
niﬁcantly improved the creep fracture plasticity. Fig. 10b shows creep
strain rate-time curves for three alloys. The creep rate of the three al-
loys decreased sharply in the ﬁrst stage (about 25 h ahead) and in-
creased signiﬁcantly in the third stage (about 70 h). All three alloys had
three typical creep stages with distinct boundaries. The second creep
stage of alloy B-0 accounted for 74%, and the second creep stage of
alloy B-1 was slightly shorter than that of alloy B-0, and its proportion
decreased to about 70%. The second stage time of alloy B-2 was sig-
niﬁcantly shortened, and its proportion decreased to about 52%.
Fig. 11 shows TEM images of the three alloys after creep testing at
1100 °C/130 MPa. The beam direction is [001] for all the samples.

4. Discussion
Many research works have demonstrated that high temperature
(950~1100 °C) creep properties mainly depend on the γ′ size and vo-
lume fraction, γ channel width [ 9], solid-solution strengthening e ﬀect
[10], lattice mis ﬁt[ 11,12] and residual eutectics [ 13,14]. In this study,
the γ′ size and volume fraction, γ channel width were not changed by
the B element additions. However, the solid-solution strengthening ef-
fect, lattice mis ﬁt and residual eutectics were obviously changed. The
relationships between microstructure evolution and high creep prop-
erties for the three alloys with di ﬀerent additions of B are analyzed as
follows.

is closely related to the strengthening e ﬀect of both γ matrix and γ′
phases. Meanwhile, in the last twenty years, the precipitation
strengthening of γ′ phases has been developed to maximum e ﬀect in SX
superalloys [ 9,15]. It has been reported that Re, Mo and Cr are all ef-
fective solid-solution hardening elements [ 10]. The changes in the
partitioning behavior of alloying elements Re, Mo and Cr in γ matrix

4.2. E ﬀect of interfacial dislocation networks and lattice mis ﬁt on creep
strength
During the process of high temperature creep, the interfacial dis-
location networks are considered to be a factor contributing to the
improvement of the creep resistance by impeding dislocation glide
through the matrix [ 16,17]. Meanwhile, the denser and more stable
interfacial dislocation networks contribute to lowering the minimum
creep rates and improve the creep rupture life [ 18,19]. But it should be
noted that some researchers believe that the interfacial dislocations
serve as the dislocation sources that eventually cut the γ′ phase during
high temperature creep [ 20]. In this study, the interfacial dislocation
networks after creep in alloy B-2, as a result of creep deformation, were
sparser than that of alloys B-0 and B-1 ( Fig. 9). However, there was no

B-2 had lower creep resistance and exhibited a shorter steady creep

In general, a negative lattice mis ﬁt is achieved in most commercial
SX superalloys because the refractory alloying elements Re, Cr, W and
Mo are more tend to segregate in the γ phase. Moreover, the high ne-
gative lattice mis ﬁt existed in SX superalloys is often associated with
elevated creep resistance at high temperatures [ 16,18,21]. To under-
stand the observed decrease of creep resistance in the Re-containing
experimental alloys the γ/γ′ lattice mis ﬁt that resulted from the varied
compositions should be considered. As described above, the composi-
tions of Re, Mo and Cr in the γ phase decreased obviously in high B
content alloy (Fig. 9). The reduction of the elements Re, Mo and Cr with
large atomic size decreases the lattice parameter of the γ phase in alloy
B-2, which is considered as the main factor responsible for decreasing
the magnitude of the lattice mis ﬁt. These results about the e ﬀect of
misﬁt on high temperature creep resistance of SX superalloys con-
taining the elements Ru and Re are also observed in previous studies
[11,12]. However this phenomenon has not been found in the alloy B-
1due to the small di ﬀerence of concentrations in γ/γ′ phase for alloys B-
0 and B-1 ( Fig. 9). Probably the lower B content (0.01. wt.%) is not
enough to make the di ﬀerence of lattice mis ﬁt in this alloy.
4.3. high temperature fracture behaviors of the tertiary creep
Tertiary creep, which involves the rapid increase of strain and ac-
cumulated damage, leads to necking and ﬁnal failure. Tertiary creep is
an accelerating process due to the development of cavities adjacent to
the eutectics and secondary particles as well as the growth of cracks. It
is generally true that a small quantity of secondary particles with small
size have no deleterious e ﬀect on the high temperature creep fracture
behavior in superalloys [ 7]. However, large size secondary phases,
especially with lamellar or script-like morphology, would result in local
concentration of stress in the matrix around, providing additional
sources for crack initiation and propagation [ 22]. It is important to note
that the coarse eutectics are also nondeformable particles in SX super-
alloy. In the modi ﬁed RR2086 alloy, the large and irregular γ′ phases
resulted in shorter creep rupture life in high temperature [ 13].
 Authors
previous studies [23,24] also suggested that the eutectics with large size
in SX superalloys obviously decreased the creep properties.
In this study, almost no residual eutectics and only a small amount
of carbides were observed in alloys B-0 and B-1 ( Table 5). The cracks
initiated at eutectics and carbides were rarely observed in these two
alloys during creep process ( Fig. 12b and d). In alloy B-2, the volume
fraction of residual eutectics was about 5.8% due to the incomplete
solution temperature heat treatment (1305 °C/8 h). A great number of
cracks initiated and then propagated at these eutectics were obviously
found. It is beleived that the interfaces between the residual eutectics
and matrix can result in large thermal stress due to di ﬀerent thermal
expansion coe ﬃcients. Eventually, these eutectics could be easily se-
parated from the matrix and then lead the initiation at the interfacial
cracks (Fig. 10d). And hence, the duration life of second creep stage was
shortened and third creep stage occurred signi ﬁcantly earlier than al-
loys B-0 and B-1. This harmful e ﬀect by eutectics was also found by
Kong and Wilson [ 13,14].

5. Conclusions
The systematic study on the microstruture and creep behaviors
under 1100 °C/130 MPa of a series of novel Re-containing SX super-
alloy with variations of B additions have been investigated in this study.
Conclusions are summarized as follows:
(1) B additions obviously increased the volume fraction of ( γ+γ′) eu-
tectics. When B content was 0.02 wt%, the precipitation of the M
3B2
borides with script-like morphology has been observed at the edge
of eutectics in the as-cast alloys. These borides signi ﬁcantly de-
crease the incipient melting point.
(2) B additions had no signi ﬁcant impact on the volume fractions, sizes
of γ′ precipitates and γ channel width in the heat-treated alloys.
High B addition obviously increases the volume fraction of the re-
sidual eutectics and script-like M 3B2 borides.
