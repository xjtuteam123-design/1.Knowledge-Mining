ABSTRACT
The microstructure and mechanical properties of weld joints in ethylene pyrolysis furnace (Cr25Ni35NbM alloy 
and Cr35Ni45NbM alloy tube) after service were investigated in this work. The microstructure of the new weld 
joint and base metal of pyrolysis furnace tube vary widely, and the high temperature creep rupture time of the 
weld joint is only reached 50 % of the base metal (1100 
◦
C, 16 MPa). After high-temperature service, the 
microstructure of weld metal and base metal deteriorated and the high temperature creep strength decreased. 
Therefore, the microstructure of weld metal and base metal is tend to uniform, and the creep strength of weld 
joint and base metal reached the same level. After high-temperature aging, the primary carbides coarsen and 
secondary carbides precipitation results in a uniform microstructure and creep property of weld metal and base 
metal. The high temperature creep rupture time of weld joint reached 90 % of base metal (1100 
◦
C, 16 MPa). As 
the dispersion strengthening effect of the secondary carbide disappears, the creep strength of the weld joint no 
longer increases, but begins to decrease. Both high temperature service and elevated temperature aging result in 
the homogenization of the weld joint and base metal, and the high-temperature creep strength also reaches the 
same level.
Introduction
In the petrochemical industry, the high temperature furnace tube 
materials have been developed from HK type alloys (Cr25Ni20 alloy) to 
Cr25Ni35Nb and Cr35Ni45Nb type alloys ( Al-Meshari et al., 2013 ; 
Attarian et al., 2016 ; Chun et al., 2014 ). The ethylene pyrolysis furnace 
is mainly welded by centrifugally cast Cr25Ni35NbM and Cr35Ni45NbM 
alloy tubes (M: Ti/Zr Micro-alloying), which have good high tempera -
ture mechanical properties and excellent resistance to high temperature 
oxidation, carburization and other high temperature corrosion proper -
ties ( Fu et al., 2019 ; Guan and Wang, 2011 ; Gudenko et al., 2023 ). The 
ethylene pyrolysis furnace tubes working at the temperature between 
900 to1100 
◦
C, and the internal pressure of the pyrolysis furnace tube 
vary from 0.3 MPa to 0.5 MPa ( Guglielmino et al., 2015 ). The pyrolysis 
furnace tube and weld joint are in the core position of the whole 
ethylene pyrolysis furnace, which is exposed to the high temperature, 
oxidation, carburization and other harsh environment for several years 
( Guo et al., 2021 ; Han et al., 2019 ). The ethylene pyrolysis furnace tube 
is usually designed for a normal life time of 100,000 h (11.4 years) on 
the basis of API 530 ( API Standard 530 2015 ). However, the life of the 
furnace tube in service varies from 30,000 to 180,000 h, and the design 
life is much lower than the actual service life ( Kondrat ’ ev et al., 2017 ; 
Kraposhin et al., 2017 ). Creep, oxidation and carburizing can easily lead 
to the premature failure of furnace tube and weld joint ( LeMay et al., 
1996 ; McLeod et al., 2015 ).

Experimental
In order to study the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
weld joints, the weld joints of Cr25Ni35NbM alloy and Cr35Ni45NbM 
alloy were selected from ethylene pyrolysis furnace tube after different 
service time, which are cut from the area in the central of the pyrolysis 
furnace with a maximum working temperature (900 – 1100 
◦
C). For 
Cr25Ni35NbM alloy, we collected some furnace tubes and weld joints 
that have been in service for 7 and 8 years (860 – 980 
◦
C); and we also 
collected some Cr35Ni45NbM alloy furnace tubes and weld joints that 
have been in service 5 and 8 years (920 – 1040 
◦
C). Although these weld 
joints have the same service life (in year), those from ethylene pyrolysis 
furnaces of different ethylene manufacturers still exhibit some differ -
ences in their operating environments and temperatures.

Cr35Ni45NbM alloy (wt. %) is listed in Table 1 (HG/T 2601 – 2011). At 
the same time, the weld metal selected in the welding process is a

metal. The weld joint is made of manual Gas Tungsten Arc Welding 
(GTAW). There are three or four weld layers, the interpass temperature 
is about 50

nondestructive welding inspection is carried out to check the imper -

In addition, in order to further analyze the deterioration of the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of the weld joint during ser -

Results and discussion
Microstructure evolution
Weld fusion boundary
In Cr25Ni35NbM and Cr35Ni45NbM alloy new weld joints, the 
fusion boundary is the zone in which carbides size is significantly 
changed from big (base metal) to small (weld metal) and the quantity 
from small (base metal) to large (weld metal) ( Fig 2 ). The fusion 
boundaries of the weld joint in different service states are shown in Fig 1 . 
The fusion boundary is very clearly of Cr25Ni35NbM and Cr35Ni45NbM 
alloy weld joints ( Fig 2 (a) and (b)). There are many columnar crystals 
formed during the solidification of weld metal, and the direction of 
columnar crystals grows along the most favorable direction for heat 
dissipation. During the welding process, the weld pool is small and the 
weld metal has a high cooling rate, therefore the grain size of the weld 
metal is significantly smaller than that of base metal.
In cast heat-resistant alloys with high carbon content, primary car -
bides are located at grain boundaries. After high-temperature service, 
these carbides coarsen into chains or networks along boundaries and 
increase in size. Concurrently, numerous fine secondary carbides pre -
cipitate within the dendrites, exhibiting a high number density and 
small dimensions ( Han et al., 2019 ).
After a long-term service, the outer wall surface of the ethylene py -
rolysis furnace tube and weld joint undergoes oxidation, while the inner 
wall undergoes carburization and oxidation, and the microstructure also 
deteriorates. A large number of fine secondary carbides precipitated in 
base metal and weld metal of weld joint after service. The deterioration 
of the microstructure of weld joint and base metal leads to a decrease in 
mechanical properties and a reduction in the service life of pyrolysis 
furnace tubes. And the microstructure between the weld metal and the 
base metal is gradually similar, the fusion boundary is becoming more 
and more blurred, which is not as clear as the new weld joint ( Fig 2 (c) 
and (d)). The microstructure of the base metal and weld metal becomes 
uniform after long term service, resulting in less and less difference in 
mechanical properties between weld metal and base metal.
Base metal microstructure
The microstructure of Cr25Ni35NbM and Cr35Ni45NbM alloy are 
shown in Fig 3 . In the as-cast Cr25Ni35NbM and Cr35Ni45NbM alloy 
tube, the skeleton shape primary carbides precipitate on the dendrite 
boundaries. The primary carbide is lamellae on the grain boundaries, 
which increases the tortuosity of the grain boundaries, prevents the slip 
of the grain boundary and the expansion of cracks, and improves the 
high temperature creep fracture strength of the alloy ( Zhang, 2010 ).

Weld joint mechanical properties
Hardness
The weld metal hardness of Cr25Ni35NbM and Cr35Ni45NbM alloy 
weld joints is shown in Fig. 5 . In this figure, "7 ″ and "8" represent the 
service life (in year) of weld joints from pyrolysis furnace tubes. Since 
these weld joints come from different ethylene pyrolysis furnaces with 
different operating parameters. Therefore there is no comparability in 
the microstructure and properties between different weld joint. Only 
hardness comparisons between the base metal and weld metal in the 
same weld joint are valid.
The hardness of the weld metal for new weld joint is slightly higher 
than that of base metal ( Fig. 5 ). That is because the grain size in the 
fusion zone of the weld joint is smaller than that of base metal, and there 
are more primary carbides at the grain boundaries ( Fig 2 (c) and (d)). 
However, the hardness of the weld metal for Cr25Ni35NbM alloy weld 
joints are lower than that of base metal after service ( Fig 5 (a)). After 
service, the primary carbide coarsening and secondary carbide precipi -
tation of Cr25Ni35NbM alloy weld joint caused a significant increase in 
the carbide content of base metal and weld metal, but the carbide con -
tent in base metal increased even more, resulting in a higher hardness of 
base metal than weld metal. The Cr35Ni45NbM alloy furnace tube itself 
contains a large amount of carbides. During service, carbides increase 
significantly in both base metal and weld metal of the Cr35Ni45NbM 
alloy weld joint, and the increase amount of carbides is similar in base 
metal and weld metal. Therefore, the hardness of some base metal is still 
lower than that of weld metal after service.
High temperature creep strength
The creep rupture time of Cr25Ni35NbM and Cr35Ni45NbM alloy 
weld joint after different service time at 1100 
◦
C, 17 MPa is shown in Fig 
6 . The Y-axis depicts different weld joints having different service life. 
“ 7 ″ and “ 8 ” indicate the service life (in years) of the weld joint for the 
pyrolysis furnace tube. While some weld joints exhibit identical service 
lives (in years), they originate from ethylene pyrolysis furnaces operated 
by different ethylene manufacturers. These ethylene pyrolysis furnaces 
have distinct operating environments, temperatures, and media condi -
tions for both the tubes and weld joints, rendering comparisons between 
different joints invalid. Therefore, the creep rupture time between 
different weld joints varies considerably, even for identical service life 
(in year). Consequently, this study focuses solely on comparing the weld 
metal and base metal within the same joint. No comparative analysis is 
performed across different weld joints, nor is such a comparison feasible.
The high temperature creep strength of the new weld joint of the 
ethylene pyrolysis furnace tube is much lower than that of base metal. It 
can be seen that the creep fracture time of Cr25Ni35NbM alloy new weld 
joint is only one-third of that of base metal, and the creep rupture time of 
Cr35Ni45NbM alloy new weld joint is also only 40 % of base metal ( Fig 
6 ). And, the fracture region of the new weld joint is the weld metal. Due 
to the significant difference in microstructure between weld metal and 
base metal, the weld fusion line becomes a clear boundary. Therefore, 
there is a significant difference in high-temperature creep property be -
tween weld joint and base metal, and the weld joint is also prone to 
fracture near the fusion line in weld metal[8]. And the previous research 
has also confirmed this conclusion ( Yu et al., 2020 ; Zhao et al., 2016 ).
The ethylene pyrolysis furnaces are worked in high temperature, 
high carbon, and oxidizing environments, resulting in microstructure 
deterioration, carburization, oxidation, and creep damage to the furnace 
tubes. A large number of secondary carbides are precipitated in both 
weld metal and base metal, and the microstructural differences between 
weld metal and base metal are becoming smaller and smaller. Therefore, 
the weld joint fusion boundary becomes more and more blurred, and the 
mechanical properties of weld metal and base metal are tend to 
consistent. After service, the creep strength of both weld joint and base 
metal of the pyrolysis furnace tube decreased, but the creep strength of 
the base metal decreased faster, and finally the creep strength of the base 
metal and weld joint reached the same level. As the service time pro -
longs, the difference in mechanical properties between the weld joint 
and base metal gradually decreases, and the weld joint elevated tem -
perature creep strength reaches the same level.

Conclusion
We have studied and analyzed the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of pyrolysis furnace tube weld joints with different service 
time and aging treatment time. The following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) The microstructure of weld metal and base metal in new weld 
joint vary widely, and the hardness difference between weld 
metal and base metal is small, but the high temperature creep 
rupture time of the weld joint is < 50 % of base metal.
(2) After a long time service, the carbide size and content of weld 
metal and base metal in weld joint are similar, and the micro -
structure of weld metal and base metal is tend to uniform. The 
high temperature creep strength of both base metal and weld 
joint decreases, but the creep strength of base metal decreases 
faster, which reaches the same level of weld joint.
(3) After high temperature aging treatment, the microstructure of 
weld metal and base metal is uniform, the fusion line of the weld 
joint becomes blurred, and the high temperature creep rupture 
time of weld joint reaches 90 % of base metal. When the 
dispersion strengthening effect of the secondary carbide disap -
pears, the creep strength of the weld joint no longer increases, but 
begins to decrease.
Prolonged high-temperature exposure deteriorated the microstruc -
ture of both the weld fusion zone and base metal, leading to similar 
microstructures. Consequently, the high-temperature creep strength of 
the weld joint and base metal reaches the same level.
