abstract
A novel refractory Hf 25Nb25Ti25Zr25 high entropy alloy (HEA) with single body centered cubic (BCC)
structure was prepared by copper mold cast method. This HEA still holds the single BCC phase even after
furnace cooling from homogenization at 1573 K for 6 h. The fracture strength and plastic strain of this
HEA reach about 969 MPa and 14.9%, respectively. There is signi ﬁcant work hardening effect in this HEA,
which is considered as resulting from the movement and multiplication of dislocations.
& 2014 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) are basically de ﬁned as those alloys
which contain multi-principal elements in equal or near equal
atomic percentage [1]. Recently, great attention has been paid to
HEAs due to their high strength, high hardness, excellent softening
resistance, wear and corrosion resistance etc. [1–8]. Two types of
HEA systems with the face centered cubic (FCC) and body centered
cubic (BCC) crystal structure, respectively, have been developed.
The FCC type of HEAs which is represented by FeCoNiCrMn HEA
alloys exhibits high thermodynamic stability and excellent malle-
ability, but has medium high tensile strength [3,5,9]. In contrast,
the BCC type of HEAs has high strength, but shows high brittleness
and low ductility. Very recently, Senkov et al. [10–15] prepared a
series of BCC structure refractory HEAs with high hardness and
compressive yield strength up to 1300 MPa. However, most of
these refractory HEAs only show low compressive plastic strain at
room temperature. It has been also noted that no tensile proper-
ties have been reported for BCC structure HEAs. However, tensile
properties are more valuable for engineering application of HEAs.
In this letter, a new BCC Hf
25Nb25Ti25Zr25 HEA with excellent
structural stability at high temperature, and the combination of
high tensile strength and plasticity is presented for the ﬁrst time.
The structural stability, deformation and fracture feature for this
HEA are examined. The strengthening mechanism for the excellent
tensile properties of this HEA is discussed.
2. Experimental
Alloy ingots with a nominal composition of Hf 25Nb25Ti25Zr25
(at%) were prepared by arc-melting a mixture of pure metals (purity
499.8 wt%) in a Ti-gettered high-purity argon atmosphere. The
ingots were ﬂipped and remelted ﬁve times to ensure the chemical
homogeneity. The melted alloys were then drop-cast into a water-
cooled copper mold with dimensions of 10 mm /C2 10 mm /C2 60 mm.
The as-cast bar was sealed in a vacuum quartz tube and subse-
quently homogenized in a tubular furnace at 1573 K for 6 hours in
ﬂowing Ar atmosphere and subsequently furnace-cooled to room
temperature. The phase formation and stability was characterized
by X-ray diffraction (XRD) and Differential scanning calorimeter
(DSC) at the heating rate of 20 K/min, respectively.
The room temperature mechanical properties were evaluated
using CMT4105 universal electronic tensile testing machine at the
strain rate of 1 /C2 10
/C0 3 s/C0 1. Dog-bone shape specimens with gauge
geometry of 1 mm /C2 5m m /C2 10 mm were used in the test. The
microstructure and fracture morphologies of the samples were
investigated by using scanning electron microscope (SEM) Auriga
produced by Zeiss (BRD). The re ﬁned microstructures of as-cast
and fractured samples were examined by FEI G 2 F20 type of
transmission electron microscope (TEM). The TEM samples were
prepared by ion milling after mechanical grounding down to
40 μm of thickness.

So far, several parameters have been proposed to predict
the structural stability and phase formation of HEAs [16,17]. Zhang
et al. proposed a criterion for the formation of solid-solution
phases in HEAs based on the enthalpy of mixing ðΔHmixÞ and
atomic radius difference ðδÞ [17]. To further include the mixing
entropy effect, an additional parameter ðΩÞ was also proposed as
Ω¼TmΔSmix/∣ΔHmix∣, where Tm is the average melting tempera-
ture and ΔSmix the entropy of mixing of an alloy. From available
data, it was concluded that the criteria for the formation of HEAs
with single solid solution phase are ΩZ 1:1 and δr 6:6%, respec-
tively. As for current HEA, the values of Ω and δ are calculated to
be 10.75 and 4.86%, respectively, which are perfectly in accordance
with the above mentioned criteria.
Guo et al. [18,19] proposed another parameter named valence
electron concentration (VEC) according to the electronic structure
theory to predict the phase stability of HEAs. Guo's statistics
analysis indicates that BCC solid-solution phases were found to
be stable as the VEC value is lower than 6.87. The VEC value of
current HEA is calculated to be 4.25, which is in the range of VEC of
the stable phase.
Fig. 2 shows the tensile true stress-stain curve of Hf25Nb25Ti25Zr25
alloy. It can be derived that this HEA has the yield strength (YS) of
/C24 879 MPa, the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of/C24 969 MPa, and the
elastic and plastic elongation of /C24 0.9%, and /C24 14.9%, respectively.
The fracture strength of this HEA is much higher than most of
reported FCC type of HEAs, which may attributed to the formation
of single BCC phase with strong bonding inherited from the
refractory elements and the high solution hardening effect of
whole-solute matrix. Meanwhile, this HEA exhibits very large
plastic strain, which has been seldom found for BCC type of HEAs.
The SEM images of the fracture surface for this HEA alloy are
shown in Fig. 3. Obvious necking near the fracture surface and the
twirling and surface relief effect of crystalline grains can be found
from Fig. 3(a), demonstrating that typical plastic deformation took
place before fracture. Multiple slip bands can be clearly observed
on the grains in the necking surface ( Fig. 3 (b)). The grain
boundaries have been seriously distorted. From the SEM image

In this work, TEM analysis was also performed to clarify the
strengthening mechanism. As shown in Fig. 4 (a), no secondary
phase precipitated in the microstructure. The dark fringes near the
edge are actually the contrast difference induced by the mechan-
ical stress which was generated during ion milling process. The
selected area diffraction pattern (SADP) of the corresponding area,
as shown in inset of Fig. 4(a), further con ﬁrms that the structure is
of a single BCC structure with the lattice constant of 0.3519 nm.
The structure of this HEA after fracture has been remarkably
changed. From Fig. 4 (b), it is seen that a large amount of short
curved dislocation lines distributes in the whole area of the
sample. No secondary phase was formed even undergoing severe
deformation. It is deduced that during the deformation process,
dislocation motions are gradually retarded, then tangled and
ﬁnally accumulate around grain boundaries and slip bands. There-
fore, the dislocation strengthening via dislocation multiplication is
considered to be the main mechanism for the large plasticity and
signiﬁcant work hardening of this HEA.
4. Conclusions
In summary, a novel refractory HfNbTiZr high-entropy alloy
with a single BCC phase has been successfully developed. This new
HEA alloy exhibits excellent phase stability at high temperature
and no phase separation during furnace cooling from 1573 K
homogenization. In the as-cast state, tensile YS is 879 MPa, UTS
is 969 MPa, and the plastic elongation is 14.9%. The YS and UTS
