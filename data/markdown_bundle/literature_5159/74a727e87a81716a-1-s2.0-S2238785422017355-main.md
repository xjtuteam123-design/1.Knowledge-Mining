abstract
Oxygen solid solution-strengthened titanium-oxygen (Ti eO) alloys with ultrahigh yield
strength and large ductility were designed and synthesized via high-energy ball milling
(BM) combined with spark plasma sintering (SPS) in compression. The morphology,
dispersion state, and solid solution microstructure of the Ti eO alloys were optimized by
regulating the mixing method, sintering temperature, and added oxygen content.
Outstanding properties that include a compressive yield strength of 1220 MPa and large
ductility of 36.3% were achieved after 1 h high-energy BM and 1000
/C14 C SPS with the addition
of about 1.8 at% oxygen. The grain reﬁnement and oxygen solid solution strengthening
indicate that the dense morphology, reﬁned grain size, and oxygen solid solute atoms are
responsible for the balance between the superior strength and ductility of the titanium
alloys.

1. Introduction
Titanium (Ti) and its alloys have recently been considered to
be promising materials for orthopedics and dental implanta-
tion because of their superior mechanical properties
compared with ceramics and polymeric materials [ 1]. The
most commonly used metallic biomaterials include 316 L
stainless steel, cobalt-based alloys, and titanium and its al-
loys. Ti is a lightweight metal with a low speciﬁc gravity
approximately half of that of steel [ 2]; it has better biocom-
patibility than cobalt-based alloys [ 3]. Although Ti e6Ale4V
(TC4) alloys currently account for more than 50% of all alloys
[4], the presence of toxic aluminum (Al) and vanadium (V) in
these alloys limits their applications in biomedical materials.
The use of pure titanium was developed after further reviews
of its biocompatibility. However, pure titanium is prone to
fracture in long-term medical applications in the human body
because of its limited mechanical strength [ 5]. Thus, it re-
mains a challenge to design and fabricate titanium alloys with
improved strength and biocompatibility.
Traditional solid solution strengthening and work-
hardening effects for manufacturing titanium alloys have
become mature technologies. b-type and ( aþb)-type Ti alloys
without Al or V have been developed [ 6]. Niobium (Nb) was
used to replace V in TC4 to reduce cytotoxicity in a new ( aþb)-

The continued advancement of titanium alloys cannot rely
on only the optimization of titanium and its alloys through the
use of rare alloys and complex heat treatments; it also re-
quires the development of methods for solid solution
strengthening through the use of light and safe elements.
Powder metallurgy (PM) is a common process in which light
elements, such as oxygen (O), nitrogen (N), and hydrogen (H),
are introduced into a titanium matrix. The mechanical
strength of titanium enitrogen (Ti eN) alloys that exhibit a
yield strength of 974 MPa and ductility of 21.7% has been
enhanced by a solid solution of N atoms [ 22]. H and O were
introduced into a pure Ti matrix using PM to produce an alloy
with a yield strength of 947 MPa and ductility of 23.9% [ 23].
However, the addition of N, H, and O to the Ti powder using
the gas-solid direct reaction process limited the ability to
continuously increase the added light elements and the ac-
curacy of the addition. To solve these problems, O atoms were
introduced by adding TiO
2 powder in our previous study [ 24].
We found that the compressive yield strength and ductility of
titaniumeoxygen (Ti eO) alloys formed by ball milling (BM)
and spark plasma sintering (SPS) reached 1005 MPa and 37.2%,
respectively. However, only the effect of the BM parameter on
the Ti eO alloys was studied. The sintering temperature and
oxygen content effects will also need to be studied to further
improve the alloys.
To improve the mechanical properties of medical Ti alloys,
TieO alloys with ultra-high compressive yield strength and
large ductility were fabricated using a combination of high-
energy BM and SPS. In this study, we focus on the effects of
the sintering temperature and the added oxygen on the
microstructure and mechanical properties of the fabricated
TieO alloys.

3. Results
3.1. Inﬂuence of process parameters
Fig. 2 (aee) shows the micromorphology of the pure titanium
powder, TiO 2 powder, and their mixture after milling for 1 h.
The unground pure titanium powder had an irregular polyg-
onal shape with a diameter of approximately 30 mm while the
TiO
2 powder was nearly spherical with a size of approximately
1 nm. After the high-energy BM process, the particles in the
mixed powder became non-angular and slightly plate-like
shaped fragments because of the cumulative plastic defor-
mation caused by repeated collisions with the grinding balls.
The particle size of the fragments was slightly larger than that
of the pure titanium powder. In general, high-energy BM in-
volves an alternating process of fragmentation and cold-
welding agglomeration [ 25]. Spherical fragments formed by
the interaction between fragmentation and cold-welding
agglomeration were observed. Fig. 2 (f and g) shows the dis-
tribution of titanium and oxygen elements in the mixed
powder after milling. Titanium is widely distributed, and all
powders are basically covered. The oxygen element is
distributed on most of the powder particles because the tita-
nium dioxide powder particles are very small. After mixed ball
milling, the titanium dioxide particles are dispersed around
and attached to the surface of the larger titanium powder
particles.

3.2. Inﬂuence of oxygen content
Fig. 9(a)e(d) shows the XRD patterns, engineering stress-strain
curves, ductility, and yield strengths of Ti eO alloys (BM) with
different oxygen contents. The XRD results show that only Ti
diffraction peaks are observed in these four samples, indi-
cating that TiO
2 is decomposed after the high-energy BM and
SPS processes. The crystallite sizes of Ti eO alloys with
different oxygen contents of 0 at%, 1 at%, 1.5 at%, and 2 at%
were calculated based on the XRD peaks in Fig. 9 (a). The
crystallite sizes are 27.4 mm, 22.4 mm, 17.7 mm, and 15.5 mm,
respectively. The crystallite size of Ti eO alloy decreases with
increasing oxygen content. Fig. 9(b) shows that the Ti peak of
the (002) crystal plane shifts toward smaller angles whereas
the peak position of the (100) crystal plane remains un-
changed as the oxygen content increases, as shown in
Fig. 9(b). The crystal lattice parameters of the Ti eO alloys with
oxygen contents from 0 to 2.0 at% were calculated from the
XRD results. The crystal lattice parameter along the a-axis
direction is approximately 2.9533 /C23A, whereas those along the
c-axis are 4.6879, 4.7104, 4.7272, and 4.7300 /C23A, respectively. It
can be concluded that oxygen atoms entered the octahedral
sites in the Ti lattice because only the crystal lattice parameter
along the c-axis direction increased with increasing oxygen
content. Mimoto et al. [35] reported that the Ti lattice constant
along the c-axis direction increased by 0.024 /C23A for every 1.0 at
% of added oxygen, which agrees with our XRD results and
calculations. Fig. 9 (c) and (d) shows that with the addition of
oxygen, the yield strength increases whereas the ductility
decreases continuously. When the oxygen contents were 0 at
%, 1.0 at%, 1.5 at%, and 2.0 at%, Ti eO alloys exhibit the
compressive yield strengths of 550 MPa, 1125 MPa, 1220 MPa,
and 1340 MPa, and ductility values of 42.6%, 38%, 36.3%, and
30.5%. Oxygen contributed to the increase in yield strength but
reduced the ductility of the Ti eO alloy. These results are
consistent with those of previous studies [ 36,37], which found
that a wavy-to-planar transition of the dislocation arrange-
ment occurred when the oxygen content in titanium was
increased. However, Chong et al. [ 38] reported that the
increased slip planarity of Ti eO alloys was caused by an
interstitial shufﬂing mechanism that was sensitive to the
temperature, strain rate, and oxygen content and led to the
subsequent alteration of the deformation twinning behavior.

different oxygen contents. The interior structures of the
samples are dense and no obvious holes are observed, which

5. Conclusions
TieO alloys with ultra-high compressive yield strength and
large ductility were synthesized using high-energy BM com-
bined with SPS. The following results were obtained by regu-
lating the mixing methods, sintering temperatures, and added
oxygen content. With the increasing sintering temperature,
the porosity of Ti eO alloys decreased while the grain size of
the samples sintered at 1000
/C14 C in the ( aþb)-Ti region was the
smallest. Oxygen contributed to the increase in the crystal
lattice and yield strength of Ti eO alloy by oxygen solid solu-
tion strengthening effect but reduced its ductility. High equi-
librium strength and ductility were achieved in the Ti eO
alloys. Outstanding properties, which include a high
compressive yield strength of 1220 MPa and ductility of 36.3%,
were obtained through 1 h high-energy BM, sintering at
1000
/C14 C, and adding 1.8 at% oxygen. The excellent compre-
hensive mechanical properties are attributed to the dense
microstructure, grain reﬁnement, and oxygen solid solution.
