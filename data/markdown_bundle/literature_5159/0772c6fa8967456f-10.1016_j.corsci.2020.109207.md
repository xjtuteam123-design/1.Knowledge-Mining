ABSTRACT 
The high-temperature oxidation resistances of Ni-based superalloys with the compositions of Ni – (0 – 15)Co – 
(8 – 15)Cr – (0 – 5)Mo – (0 – 10)W – (3 – 8)Al – (0 – 5)Ti – (0 – 10)Ta – 0.1C – 0.01B were analyzed using an artificial neural 
network (ANN). The oxidation resistances of the alloys were evaluated based on the weight change measured 
during cyclic oxidation tests. An ANN was constructed with the contents of the alloying elements as input and 
mass gains as output. The ANN provided highly accurate regression results. The present results were compared 
with those obtained using response surface methodology (RSM) in a previous study. The regression model of the 
ANN could effectively detect the effect of an additional alloying element explicitly. The main and interaction 
effects of the alloying elements were plotted based on the results with random compositions. The optimum 
composition of superalloys with the highest oxidation resistance at high temperatures was determined using the 
trained ANN. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys have been used in high-temperature applica -
tions owing to their outstanding high-temperature mechanical proper -
ties. These alloys demonstrate high oxidation resistance, which is mainly 
determined by the alloying elements [ 1 ]. The chemical compositions of 
Ni-based superalloys are complex. The main alloying elements of these 
alloys are Co, Cr, Mo, W, Al, and Ta. The roles of these elements have 
been widely studied in terms of their mechanical properties, thermal 
stability, and oxidation. However, the effects of individual elements 
have not been elucidated owing to their complex combinations. 
The addition of Co, Cr, Mo, and W improves the mechanical prop -
erties of Ni-based alloys through solid solution strengthening, whereas 
the addition of Al, Ti, and Ta results in precipitation strengthening. 
Oxides from alloying elements, such as Cr

degraded by excessive alloying. Mo and W are added to obtain high- 
temperature strength, but they may lower the oxidation resistance of 
the alloy [ 2 ]. Recently, researchers have also attempted to add rare 
earth elements to improve the performance of superalloys [ 3 – 5 ]. 
Furthermore, some alloying elements, such as Ti and Ta [ 6 – 10 ], were 
observed to have a controversial effect on the oxidation resistance ac -
cording to the contents of other elements or temperature. 
From a review of existing literature, the effects of the alloying ele -
ments cannot be described straightforwardly due to the interaction of 
the elements. In addition, it is not practical to design experiments with 
full combinations of alloying elements and their concentrations. More -
over, the optimization of an alloy composition with several elements is 
difficult. The effect of alloying elements on the high-temperature 
oxidation resistance was quantitatively analyzed using response sur -
face methodology (RSM) in previous studies [ 6 , 7 , 11 ]. The RSM provides 
an efficiently designed set of experiments to analyze the relationship 
between independent variables and responses. The average effect of an 
alloying element over the entire test range and the synergy or canceling 
out with other elements can be analyzed. However, this method cannot 
reasonably predict the oxidation rate of Ni-based alloys, although it can 
provide a relative ranking of the oxidation resistances of alloys [ 11 ]. 
Recently, deep learning based on artificial neural networks (ANNs) 
has focused on predicting and optimizing experimental designs and re -
sults. Deep learning provides a regression tool for complicated

2.3. Artificial neural network 
The ANN [ 19 , 20 ] used in this study is shown in Fig. 3 . The network 
has one input layer, three hidden layers, and one output layer. The input 
layer has n nodes, where n is the number of input variables. In this study, 
eight input variables were used, namely, the number of oxidation cycles 
and the contents of seven main alloying elements of Co, Cr, Mo, W, Al, Ti 
and Ta. The other were not considered since C and B are impurities and 
Ni is the base metal to balance. The chemical compositions from DOE 
were well-distributed within the intervals, it is expected that ANN pro -
duces reasonable results with minimum uncertainty even for chemical 
compositions which are not considered in the experiment. The output 
layer has only one node, which is the mass gain of the alloys. The three 
hidden layers have 32, 16, and 8 nodes. The numbers of hidden layers 
and their nodes were determined via trial and error. Each node has an 
internal activation function. Rectified linear unit and identity functions 
were used for activation at the nodes of the hidden and output layers, 
respectively. The nodes in a layer were connected to those in other 
adjacent layers with linear functions. The coefficients of the functions 
were initially unknown. Training was performed iteratively to deter -
mine them. An iteration is called an epoch which means one complete 
pass through the training data. The loss function was estimated using the 
mean squared error (MSE =

3.3. Optimization of compositions of alloying elements 
We summed up the statistical results from the ANN to estimate the 
main effects of the alloy components, similar to an analysis tool in the 
RSM. Oxidation mass gains were predicted using the ANN with 1000 
random combinations of alloying compositions. The main effects of the 
individual components were calculated via local averaging and shown in 
Fig. 8 . The results are similar to the graphs obtained using the RSM 
published elsewhere [ 7 ]. The alloy compositions with a minimum mass 
gain lie at the boundaries of the composition ranges. According to the 
RSM analysis [ 7 ], Co, Cr, and W rarely affect the mass gain. Mo and Ti 
increase the mass gain, whereas Al and Ta significantly reduce the mass 
gain. The general trends correspond to the main effects predicted by the 
ANN in this study. Minor differences can be observed from a quantitative 
perspective in that the ANN suggests a nearly linear increase of the 
decrease in the mass gain as the content of Mo increases, but the RSM 
suggested that the effect of Mo is slightly mitigated when its content is 
high. The ANN predicts that the effects of Ta are saturated at 4 wt.% and 
higher, but the RSM showed a greater decrease in the mass gain with an 
increase in the content of Ta. 
Notably, the standard deviation of the prediction of the mass gain by 
the ANN is large, as shown in Fig. 8 . The main effect plot of the RSM is in 
the standard deviation range of the ANN prediction. This is because the 
previous study with RSM [ 7 ] considered data only after 20 cycles of 
high-temperature oxidation. The ANN in this study is trained with data 
over 1 – 20 cycles of oxidation. Thus, the difference in the main effect 
plots obtained using the RSM and ANN can be attributed simply to the 
much smaller data set obtained in the experiments.

within the composition range. According to the plots, Co does not affect 
the mass gain, except when the Ta content is very low. Cr shows very 
weak interactions with Mo and Ti, in that the mass gain slowly increases 
with the increase in Cr content when the Mo and Ti contents are low. It is 
observed from the interaction plot of Mo that a Mo content of 2.5 wt.%

stronger in alloys with low Al or low Ta content. Similar interactions

Cr-Mo and Cr-Ti interactions were not confirmed by the interaction plots 
obtained using the RSM. The interaction of Mo presented by the RSM 
corresponds to the ANN for Mo – Al and Mo – Ta couples but not for the 
Mo – Cr couple. Note that the RSM tools provide an analysis of the 
interaction effect of two variables only. The ANN can be utilized for the 
interaction effects of three or more variables, but graphical represen -
tation may not be simple.

resistance based on the discussion in Figs. 8 and 9 , although it may show

small deviation. Alloy No. 2 has a high amount of Co, Cr, and Ta. The 
small mass gain of this composition can be attributed to the absence of 
Mo and Ti and the high content of Ta. The high oxidation resistance of 
alloy No. 3 can be explained by the high Al content and the absence of Ti. 
For alloys No. 4 and 5, it is apparent that the high Al and Ta contents 
mainly contribute to the small mass gain because Co, Cr, and W do not 
affect the mass gain significantly, as shown in Fig. 8 . The small mass gain 
of alloy No. 6 appears to be mainly related to the high Al content, and 
the addition of Ta, as in alloys Nos. 4 and 5, will further reduce the mass 
gain. 
From a practical point of view, the composition should be optimized 
by considering the mechanical properties and thermal stability of the 
microstructure and the oxidation resistance. The ANN proposed in this 
study may be used in the future for the total optimization of the prop -
erties after training with all the data types required for the alloy design. 
4. Conclusions 
In this study, we analyzed mass gain during the high-temperature 
oxidation tests of Ni-based superalloys using machine learning with an 
ANN and compared the results with those of the RSM. 
1 The ANN provided a highly reliable prediction of the oxidation 
resistance over the RSM. It did not provide explicit forms of equa -
tions to describe the effects of the alloying elements but could pro -
vide accurate predictions of mass gains in the high-temperature 
oxidation tests of Ni-based superalloys. 
2 With the RSM, the effects of the additional alloying elements could 
not be estimated. The effects of these elements were clarified 
explicitly because the ANN results were reliable.
