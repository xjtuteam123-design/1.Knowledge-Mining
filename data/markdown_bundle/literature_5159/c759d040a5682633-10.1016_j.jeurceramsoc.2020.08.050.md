ABSTRACT 
ZrB
2
-SiC ceramic and Nb were brazed by using a novel high entropy alloy CoFeNiCrCu filler. Interfacial 
microstructure, mechanical properties and brazing mechanism of the joint were systematically investigated. It 
was found that the activity of Cr could be maintained during the brazing process due to the high-entropy mixing 
effect of CoFeNiCrCu. The newly generated Cr
2
B had a tooth shape and it improved interfacial bonding between 
ZrB
2
-SiC and Nb. A composite microstructure containing a soft FCC and hard Laves phase was formed in the joint 
due to the presence of Nb and this microstructure significantly enhanced the joint strength. Influences of brazing 
temperature on the interfacial reaction and shear strength of joint were also investigated. After being brazed at 
1160 
◦
C for 60 min, the maximum shear strengths of the joint were 216 and 94 MPa at room temperature and at 
650 
◦
C, respectively, indicating the successful use of the filler. 
1. Introduction 
Joining of ceramics to metals has become a popular and important 
area because after the joining, the resulted product will be endowed 
with merits from both metallic and non-metallic sites [ 1 ]. However, 
structural characteristics and special properties of the materials hinder 
facile connections between metal and ceramics. Main difficulties arisen 
in welding are summarized as follows [ 2 , 3 ]. The bond is difficult to be 
matched due to different microstructures type during joining processes. 
Large discrepancies of coefficients of thermal expansion (CTE) easily 
introduce strains or even cracks between joint sites. For example, ce -
ramics generally have CTE lower than 8 × 10
 6 
/
◦
C, while metals higher 
values [ 4 – 6 ]. Besides, a large number of intermetallic compounds are 
easily formed in the brazed joint, especially at the ceramic side. Cracks 
tend to be formed in the intermetallic compound layer under an external 
load, resulting in the failure of joint. Moreover, high melting point of 
ceramics prohibits the formations of liquid phase when conventional 
fusion welding is applied. 
Among all joining methods, brazing has been considered as one of 
the most promising approaches to get a perfect ceramic-to-metal 
connection [ 7 ]. Up to now, considerable research efforts have been 
focused on reducing the formation of intermetallic compounds and 
relieving residual stresses at brazed joints, such as using soft metallic 
interlayer [ 8 ], adding particles or fibers in the filler [ 9 , 10 ], and using 
foam metal as interlayer [ 11 – 13 ] etc. However, uneven distribution of 
fibers or particles in the filler may result detrimental for the joint. More 
interfaces will be generated after introducing soft metallic interlayers, 
and these will lead to more cracks. Moreover, conventional AgCuTi filler 
and Ni-based filler alloy suffer from many drawbacks when employed to 
braze ceramics [ 14 , 15 ]. AgCuTi filler is incapable to match the ceramics 
requirements at high-temperature. For traditional high-temperature 
Ni-based fillers, a large number of Ni-rich intermetallic compounds 
formed during the brazing process in the joint, leading to the deterio -
ration of joint properties. Qi et all [ 16 ] studied the influence of the Zr 
additive element in Ti-24Ni eutectic filler upon brazing SiC ceramics 
under vacuum and reported the high-temperature performance of the 
joint. It was found that a large amount of Ti
2

formed in the joint. Residual stresses caused by the difference of CTE 
between Ti
2
Ni and SiC matrix were not conducive to the shear strength 
of the joint. The shear strength of the joint, therefore, was only 69 MPa

High-entropy alloys become one of the most attractive filler candi -
dates owning to their special inherent characteristics. They are 
advanced in high configuration entropy, slow atom diffusion and du -
rable in large lattice distortion. These features are expected to enhance 
the formation and stability of the solid solution phase and prevent 
dislocation motion, thereby improving mechanical strength, especially 
at high temperatures [ 17 ]. As filler, their unique thermodynamic and 
kinetic properties are positive for inhibiting the excessive dissolution of 
the base metal and filler elements, reducing the generation of harmful 
substances and improving the solid solution strengthening ability of the 
joint. Unfortunately, high-entropy alloys have been scarcely employed 
as fillers. Zhang et al. [ 18 ] used Ti-modified CoFeNiCrCu high entropy 
alloy to braze ZrB

entropy alloy. A strong joint of 60 MPa was obtained at 1180 
◦

60 min [ 19 ]. Despite these progresses, satisfactory shear strength values

CoCrFeNiCu with a moderate melting point (1140

typical high-entropy alloy with FCC solid solution structure displaying 
also promising strength, plasticity and high temperature performance. 
At the same time, in the fourth period of the sub-group element, the 
atomic fragments of each component are similar, the maximum atomic 
cross-section difference and the low component electronegativity, and 
the alloy has a single FCC solid solution structure, so the structure is 
simple, the properties are stable, and it is easy to prepare [ 20 ]. There -
fore, in the present work, a common CoFeNiCrCu high entropy alloy was 
used as brazing filler to braze ZrB

ceramic and Nb. Impacts of brazing parameters on microstructures and 
shear strength were studied in detail. Especially, the shear strength of 
brazed joints at high temperature were tested and studied. A formation 
mechanism of brazed joint was proposed based on element diffusion 
schemes. This work provides a guideline into the new generation 
brazing filler of advanced ceramic brazing with high performance.

◦

relieving residual stresses. This work provides a guideline into the new 
generation brazing filler of advanced ceramic brazing with high 
performance.
