Microstructures and tensile properties of off-stoichiometric
Ni3Al–Ni3V pseudo-binary alloys
Kazushige Ioroi 1, Satoshi Semboshi 2, Yasuyuki Kaneno 1, Takayuki Takasugi 1,a)
1Department of Materials Science, Osaka Prefecture University, Sakai, Osaka 599-8531, Japan
2Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai, Miyagi 980-8577, Japan
a)Address all correspondence to this author. e-mail: takasugi@mtr.osakafu-u.ac.jp
Received: 3 May 2019; accepted: 21 August 2019
The effect of off-stoichiometry on the microstructures and tensile properties of Ni 3Al–Ni3V pseudo-binary alloys
was investigated by a scanning electron microscope, a transmission electron microscope, Vickers hardness test,
and high-temperature tensile test. As the alloy deviates from a just-stoichiometric composition toward Ni-rich
one, the microstructures constituted by two ordered phases, Ni
3Al and Ni 3V changed to those constituted by
two ordered phases, Ni 3Al and Ni 3V, and one disordered phase, Ni solid solution. Also, the deviation from the
stoichiometric composition resulted in a decrease in ﬂow strength as well as Vickers hardness and conversely
increase in tensile elongation. Higher tensile elongation in the off-stoichiometric alloys was induced by the
transition from intergranular fracturing to transgranular fracturing. The trade-off relation in the yield strength
(or hardness) versus tensile elongation curve, which was drawn plotting the data obtained from the alloys with
different off-stoichiometric compositions, was most excellent at 600 °C but rapidly became worse at high
temperatures beyond 600 °C. It was demonstrated that the deviation to the off-stoichiometric composition in
the two-phase Ni
3Al–Ni3V pseudo-binary alloy system was a useful alloying parameter to improve the balance
of the ﬂow strength and tensile ductility.
Introduction
So-called Ni base dual two-phase intermetallic alloys, which
follow a microstructural feature of Ni base c–c9 type super-
alloys [1, 2], have been developed by the present authors ’
group, based on Ni 3Al (L1 2) and Ni 3V (D0 22) pseudo-binary
alloy system [3, 4, 5]. The D0 22 structure is crystallographically
derived from the L1 2 structure: the lattice parameter a of the
D022 structure is close to that of the L1 2 structure, but the
lattice parameter c of the D0 22 structure is roughly doubled,
accompanied with a slight extension. The longitudinal Ni 3Al
and Ni 3V pseudo-binary phase diagram and Ni-rich corner
of the isothermal (950 °C) section of Ni –Al–V ternary alloy
system are shown in Figs. 1(a) and 1(b), respectively [6]. In
the alloys with hyper-eutectoid compositions in Fig. 1(a), the
microstructures at high temperatures are comprising pri-
mary Ni 3Al phase ( c9) precipitates and Ni solid solution
(A1 phase: fcc) matrix present in a channel form. At low
temperatures, the Ni solid solution is transformed into
a eutectoid microstructure consisting of the Ni 3Al and
Ni3V phases [3, 4, 5, 7]. The microstructures are supposed
to be thermally stable even at hi gh temperatures because the
two constituent phases are crystallographically quite co-
herent. Under loading, the primary Ni 3Al phase is plastically
more deformable than the channel region involving the Ni 3V
phase [8, 9, 10, 11, 12] in contrast to the Ni base c–c9 type
superalloys. It has been so far observed that hardness and
tensile strength of the Ni base dual two-phase intermetallic
alloys are promising for applying to high-temperature struc-
tural materials [13].
Alloying is a generally useful procedure to further improve
mechanical properties of materials. In the Ni base dual two-
phase intermetallic alloys, transition metals including Ti, Nb,
and Ta (groups IV and V in a periodic table) [14, 15, 16, 17, 18,
19]; W and Mo (group VI); and Re (group VII) [7, 18, 20, 21]
have been shown to be effective solid solution strengtheners.
Also, the strengthening of those alloys was generally more
signiﬁcant in the substitution manner of the additive elements
for Al than for Ni and V, via re ﬁning the dual two-phase

of Ni-rich composition. In monolithic Ni 3Al alloys, it has been
shown that as Ni content deviates toward to Ni-rich side, not
only strength (or hardness) increases but also ambient tensile
ductility increases through suppressing intergranular fracture
with the help of boron segregation at grain boundaries [23, 24].
Similar feature has been investigated on other L1

3Al–Ni3V
pseudo-binary alloys with a preferable balance of strength and
tensile ductility, which are crucial for high-temperature struc-
tural materials, are explored and discussed.

Hardness and tensile properties
Figure 7(a) shows the variation of the Vickers hardness with Ni
content in the alloys having a different atomic ratio of Al to V.
The hardness level decreases as the atomic ratio of Al to V
increases, which is attributed to decrease in the volume fraction
of the harder Ni 3V phase. The hardness generally decreases
with increasing Ni content in alloys.
The tensile axis of the prepared tensile specimens was
perpendicular to the axes of the columnar grains developed in
the arc-melted buttons: the tensile specimens were composed
of the so-called bamboo-type grains. Consequently, a majority
portion of grain boundaries should receive normal stress under
tensile loading, implying that brittleness induced by intergran-
ular fracture more severely occurs in the present tensile
specimens than the specimens composed of equi-axed grains
with random orientation.

Relation between the strength (hardness) and
tensile elongation
Regarding the balance of the strength (or hardness) and tensile
elongation for the alloy system investigated in this study,
Fig. 9(a) shows the variation of the RT Vickers hardness with
the tensile elongation for the Ni 751x(Al10V15)25/C0 x alloys. It is
evident that the Vickers hardness decreased as the tensile
elongation increased, indicating trade-off relation between the
both properties. Figure 9(b) shows the relation between the
yield strength and tensile elongation for the Ni
751x(Al10V15)
25/C0 x alloys, plotting the data for the alloys with different off-
stoichiometric compositions. The plots were done at RT, 600,
800, and 900 °C. At all test temperatures, the yield strength
decreased as the tensile elongation increased, again indicating
the trade-off relation between the both properties. As temper-
ature increases from RT to 600 °C, the trade-off curve was
more shifted toward higher tensile elongation region than
toward higher yield strength region. The result implies that
the increase of the tensile elongation via enhancing the grain
boundary cohesion by the temperature increase is much larger
than the increase in the ﬂow strength of the matrix (grain
interior) due to the positive temperature dependence of the
ﬂow strength [29, 30]. At high temperatures beyond 600 °C, the
balance of the yield strength and tensile elongation was
degraded: the trade-off curve was shifted toward lower tensile
elongation region without receiving the merit of the ﬂow
strength increase.
Discussion
From the present TEM observation, it is hard to determine
whether the A1 (Ni solid solution) phase is present or absent in
the channel region, because there is no diffraction spots
uniquely identifying the A1 phase and therefore the technique
using DF image is not applicable to image the A1 phase: all
possible diffraction spots corresponding to the A1 phase are
involved in those corresponding to the Ni
3Al or Ni 3V phase.
However, it is likely that the A1 phase is precipitated in the
channel region of the Ni 76Al10 alloy and more Ni-rich alloys.
Besides, the phase separation into the eutectoid microstructure
appears to be incomplete in the off-stoichiometric alloys. For
the Ni
78Al10 alloy with the highest Ni content, the diffraction
spots from the Ni 3V phase are eventually invisible, thereby
indicating that the Ni 3Al phase is directly equilibrated with the
A1 phase.

off-stoichiometric alloys which were decomposed under the

If assuming that two-ordered Ni
3Al and Ni 3V phases and
one-disordered (Ni solid solution) phase coexists in the
channel region, the disordered phase might be localized
between the different variants of the same ordered Ni 3V phase
or between the different ordered phases (Ni 3Al and Ni 3V). To
resolve the constituent phases in extremely ﬁne microstruc-
tures, for examples, an atom probe- ﬁeld ion microscope as has
been actually done by Zapolsky et al. [28] in the present alloy
system or lattice images using high-resolution TEM will be
potential analysis procedures. An experimental study based on
such atomistic measurements is currently considered to fully
understand the microstructures formed in the channel region
of the off-stoichiometric alloys which were simply cooled down
from high temperature and received the eutectoid reaction.
For the decrease in the strength as well as hardness (Fig. 7)
by the deviation from the just-stoichiometric composition, an
explanation based on the effects of the reduced ordering in the
two-ordered phases [35, 36] and the presence of the disordered
phase are possible. It is suggested that the latter effect more
prevails in the largely off-stoichiometric alloys than the former
effect because the volume fraction of the A1 (Ni solid solution)
phase increases with deviating from the just-stoichiometric
composition.
The observed tensile elongation was strongly correlated
with the propensity of the intergranular fracture: the more the
intergranular fracturing, the lower the tensile elongation
becomes. As shown in Fig. 3, the grain boundaries of the
present alloy system are not only morphologically but also
compositionally complicated. The grain boundaries are consti-
tuted of several types of interface planes between the Ni
3Al
precipitate and channel, the Ni 3Al precipitate and Ni 3Al
precipitate, and the channel region and channel region. The
most preferable (or resistive) interface plane to the intergran-
ular fracture is not explicitly determined in this study.
However, if the ordering of two ordered phases are reduced
and also the Ni content in the two ordered phases becomes
surplus [23, 24, 25], any interface planes mentioned above
should become strong, thereby suppressing the intergranular
fracturing. In the alloys largely deviated from the just-
stoichiometric composition, further increase in the tensile
elongation had to be promoted by preferential precipitation
of the disordered phase at grain boundaries as well as grain
interior, thereby enhancing the grain boundary cohesion.
Further studies particularly involving the effect of the off-
stoichiometry on the mechanical properties of the Ni
3V phase,
which has not been reported until today, are de ﬁnitely required
to justify mechanisms by which the intergranular fracturing is
suppressed in the largely off-stoichiometric alloys.
Conclusions
The microstructures and tensile properties of the off-
stoichiometric Ni
3Al–Ni3V pseudo-binary alloys were investi-
gated by an FESEM, a TEM, Vickers hardness test, and high-
temperature tensile test. The following results were obtained
from the present study.
(1) As the alloy composition deviates from the just-
stoichiometric composition toward the Ni-rich one, the
constituent phases are suggested to be changed from two
ordered phases, Ni
3Al and Ni 3V to two ordered phases
Ni3Al and Ni 3V and one disordered phase (Ni solid
solution).
(2) As the alloy composition deviates from the just-
stoichiometric composition toward the Ni-rich one, the
ﬂow strength as well as Vickers hardness decreased,
whereas the tensile elongation increased.
(3) High tensile elongation in the off-stoichiometric alloys
was induced by the transition from intergranular
fracturing to transgranular fracturing.
(4) The trade-off relation in the yield strength (or hardness)
versus tensile elongation curve, which was drawn
plotting the data for the alloys with different off-
stoichiometric compositions, was improved by
temperature increase from RT to 600 °C but was
degraded at high temperatures beyond 600 °C.
(5) From the observed results, it was shown that the off-
stoichiometry in the two-phase Ni 3Al–Ni3V pseudo-
binary alloy system is useful alloying parameter to
improve the balance of the ﬂow strength and tensile
ductility.
