Experimental data on high-temperature creep of the material of the tubular reaction coils of a unit that
produces ethylene are used to obtain graphs showing the distribution of the stresses that act through the
thickness of the tube wall. The tubes are made of high-strength alloy 45Kh26N33S2B2. The performance
of the tubular elements of the coil during steady-state loading is evaluated using the criterion of rupture
strength, with allowance being made for the nonuniformity of the temperature distribution and the changes
in the parameters of the material of each tube through the thickness of its wall. It is shown that the
temperature gradient which exists in the thickness direction is an important parameter that affects the tube’s
performance.
Key words: reaction coil, high-temperature creep, rupture strength.
According to data compiled by the company Sibur-Neftekhim, the reaction tubes of an EP-300 unit – the main
piece of equipment in oil refineries – operate with a relatively low internal pressure ( p = 0.25 MPa) but also with a fairly
substantial temperature gradient between the tubes’ inside and outside surfaces. It is known that the largest temperature gra-
dient between the inside and outside surfaces of tubes in the outlet section of the reaction coil is 250°C during steady-state
operation.
In accordance with the standards (RTM 26-02-67–84 and GOST 14249–89), calculation of the strength of tubes
reduces to determining the stresses from mechanical loads (especially pressure) by means of the formulas used for thin-walled
tubes with a constant wall temperature and comparing these stresses with the rupture-strength curves of the material.
However, this approach ignores the stresses that act on the material due to a nonuniform temperature distribution and can thus
lead to over-estimation of rupture strength.
The existence of a nonuniform temperature distribution through the thickness of a tube causes two types of addi-
tional stresses. The first type of stresses consists of the stresses due to different degrees of expansion of the material in rela-
tion to temperature (thermal stresses). The maximum value of these stresses is determined by the gradient of the thermal
strains as calculated from the formula εt = α∆t. When α = 18.5·10–6 1/°C and ∆t = 250°C, εt = 0.46%. Since the minimum
ductility exhibited by the given material in delayed fracture is at least δp = 8%, the first type of stresses can be ignored. The
second type of stresses consists of those stresses that are due to different strain rates connected with the fact that the materi-
al has different temperatures. When the temperature gradient is substantial, stresses of this type can have an appreciable effect
on the rupture strength of a structural element.

The goal of this investigation is to evaluate the performance of the tubular elements of reaction-tube assemblies dur-
ing high-temperature creep. Their performance will be evaluated on the basis of the criterion of rupture strength, with
allowance for the nonuniformity of the temperature distribution through the thickness of the wall of the tubes.
Determination of Stresses and Strains in Reaction Tubes During Steady-State Creep with Allowance for the

Conclusions
A method has been developed to calculate the stress-strain state in the tubular elements of reaction-tube assemblies
with allowance for aspects of their thermomechanical loading during service.
The creep resistance of alloy 45Kh26N33S2B2 was thoroughly investigated and its rupture strength was estimated
for the predicted service life of 10 5 h.
It was shown that based on the criterion of rupture strength the coils should operate satisfactorily for at least 10 5 h.
It was also shown that the temperature gradient through the wall thickness of a reaction tube is an important param-
eter that affects the tube’s performance in service.
