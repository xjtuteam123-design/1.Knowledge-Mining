TOPICAL COLLECTION: NEXT GENERATION SUPERALLOYS AND BEYOND
Comparison of Thermodynamic Predictions
and Experimental Observations on B Additions
in Powder-Processed Ni-Based Superalloys
Containing Elevated Concentrations of Nb
STOICHKO ANTONOV , JIAJIE HUO, QIANG FENG, DIETER ISHEIM,
DAVID N. SEIDMAN, EUGENE SUN, and SAMMY TIN
Boron additions to Ni-based superalloys are considered to be beneﬁcial to the creep properties
of the alloy, as boron has often been reported to increase grain boundary cohesion, increase
ductility, and promote the formation of stable boride phases. Despite the importance, it is not
well understood whether these improvements are associated with the presence of elemental
boron or stable borides along the grain boundaries. In this investigation, two experimental
powder-processed Ni-based superalloys containing elevated levels of Nb were found to exhibit
increased solubility for B in the c matrix when compared to similar commercial Ni-based
superalloys. This resulted in an overall lower B concentration at grain boundaries that
suppressed boride formation. As the predictive capability of CALPHAD database models for
Ni-based superalloys have improved over the years, some discrepancies may still persist around
compositionally heterogeneous features such as grain boundaries. Improved quantiﬁcation of
the characteristic partitioning of B as a function of the bulk alloy composition is required for
understanding and predicting the stability of borides.

I. INTRODUCTION
EXCELLENT mechanical properties, such as high
temperature creep and fatigue resistance, along with
high corrosion and oxidation resistance, while operating
at high stress levels and elevated temperatures are the
properties that make nickel-based superalloys the mate-
rial used for the ‘‘hot section’’ components in modern
gas turbine engines. [1–9] These superior high temperature
properties can largely be attributed to the unique
microstructure of Ni-based superalloys, which consists
of ordered L1 2 intermetallic precipitates c¢ (Ni3Al)
distributed coherently within a disordered FCC A1
matrix c (Ni) that provides substantial order and
coherency type strengthening by inhibiting dislocation
motion during plastic deformation. The composition of
modern Ni-based superalloys is complex and tends to
incorporate a variety of elemental alloying additions.
Diﬀerent concentrations of c¢ formers Al, Ti, Nb, and
Ta in modern polycrystalline Ni-based superalloys are
utilized to produce an optimized size and volume
fraction of the c¢ strengthening precipitates, as these
elements will preferentially partition to the c¢ phase and
serve to lower the anti-phase boundary energy as well as
strengthen the precipitate’s resistance to dislocation
shear.[10] However, elevated concentrations of these
elements lead to some residual solubility in the c phase
that will serve as potent solid solution strengthen-
ers.[11–14] In conjunction with conventional refractory
alloying elements, such as W, Mo, Cr, and Co that
strengthen the c matrix phase through solid solution
strengthening, the overall chemistry of the alloy must be
balanced as excessive alloying may promote the forma-
tion of various ordered Ni 3X as well as topologically
closed packed (TCP) phases, which can be detrimental
to the properties due to their brittle nature. [15–17]
Minor additions of grain boundary strengthening
elements, such as C, B, Zr, and Hf, are typically
introduced to the chemistries of polycrystalline Ni-based
superalloys, with the aim of enhancing the overall creep

Speciﬁcally, additions of trace amounts of B to Ni-based

creep performance. [24] As a result, the chemistries of

elemental B forms along the grain boundaries. A study
by Zhou et al . suggested that doping a nickel-rich grain

disordered c phase on both sides of the grain boundary,
through which dislocations can be transmitted and the
strains associated with dislocation pile up can be
dissipated, decreasing the grain boundary hardening.
[20]
On the other hand, such softening of the grain boundary
prevents premature initiation of micro-cracks. It is
generally understood that the elemental boron along
the grain boundaries of Ni-based alloys will eventually
contribute to the formation of some sort of M 5B3 or
M3B2 boride with the available Cr, Mo, and/or W solute
atoms.[23,26,27] However, a recent study by Antonov
et al . has shown that some experimental high Nb-con-
tent powder-processed polycrystalline Ni-based super-
alloys possess an elevated solubility of B in the c
phase.[28] This resulted in a reduced degree of elemental
B segregation to grain boundaries which was inadequate
for boride formation, and permitted the available
boride-forming solute elements Cr, Mo, and W to form
r phase instead of the borides.
[28] This synergistic
interaction between B and Nb appears to be consistent
with other Nb-bearing Ni-based superalloys as Table I
lists the occurrence of boride formation as a function of
Nb, Cr, and B contents for a range of commercial and
experimental alloys. It is apparent that boride formation
tends to be suppressed in the higher Nb alloys.
Despite the many existing commercial Ni-based
superalloys, which utilize B addition in hopes of
improving the creep properties, the role of B in the
microstructure of such alloys is not fully understood and
limited literature exists on the preferred state of B in
these alloys. In order to better understand how to
optimize the B concentration in superalloys, it is
important to understand the characteristic partitioning
behavior of B, as well as its preferred state i.e., solid
solution or discrete precipitation. Thus, the aim of this
study is to elucidate how B atoms behave in high
Nb-content powder-processed superalloys and compare
experimental observations with thermodynamic
predictions.

IV. DISCUSSION
Boron additions to Ni-based superalloys are generally
understood to increase creep performance due to
boron’s favorable eﬀect on grain boundary properties,
ductility, and the ability to form stable borides.
[18,20,43]
Recently, it has been found that an optimized boride
volume fraction can promote superior ductility and
creep performance. [44] Even though minor B additions
are added to most modern superalloys in order to
exploit the aforementioned advantages, few studies exist
on quantifying and understanding the partitioning
behavior and solubility limits of B, partly due to the
complexity of studying such light elements with great
accuracy, when present in trace amounts.
[23,36,45–48] As
such, thermodynamic database models rely on limited
data points for the prediction of B behavior. Further-
more, complex higher-order interactions between B and
other constituent elements present in modern superal-
loys as well as materials processing techniques can result
in characteristically diﬀerent behaviors.
The thermodynamic stability of two experimental
high Nb-content Ni-based superalloys with similar
compositions, given in Table II, was assessed using the
TCNi8 database of Thermo-Calc and the corresponding
phase fraction plots are given in Figures 1(a) and (b).
The main diﬀerences appear to be in the secondary
phase formation as the higher Nb-content superalloy is
predicted to form Ni
3Ta-DOa d phase, while the high
Al-content superalloy is predicted to form r phase, as
well as have a higher volume fraction of c¢ phase. Upon
the suppression of the more thermodynamically
stable M
2B boride, the commonly observed M 3B2 boride
can be predicted. Although detailed comparison of the
predictions shows that minor diﬀerences in the stability
and volume fraction of borides in the two alloys exist,
where RRHT4 has more stable borides, these diﬀerences

can likely be attributed to the slightly higher nominal
content of B in the alloy, Table II. Despite being
predicted to form, detailed microstructural investiga-
tions on RRHT4 have shown that no boride formation
occurred. These contradictory results can likely be
attributed to the unusually low concentration of ele-
mental boron that segregates to the grain boundaries,
~ 1 at. pct corresponding to a ~ 24 lm grain size.
Compared to other boride-forming, powder-processed
Ni-based superalloys, such as RR1000 with ~ 5 at. pct B
measured along the grain boundaries for a ~ 5 lm grain
size,
[28,36] it would appear that the grain boundary B
concentration is below a critical level to induce the
formation of borides. Moreover, a separate APT study
on a Nb-lean alloy ME3, with a comparable grain size of
27.1 to 30.6 lm and a nominal B concentration of
0.11 at. pct, showed that even at a grain boundary B
concentration of 0.98 ± 0.14 at. pct, boride formation
occured. These results appear to suggest that Nb plays a
critical role in inﬂuencing the partitioning behavior of B
[30] within the microstructure of Ni-based superalloys.
Due to the notable diﬀerences in the characteristic
partitioning behavior of B within this class of similar
alloys, it is essential to investigate and understand where
the B atoms reside. The production method of the alloy,
i.e., whether it was cast, wrought, or powder-processed
plays an important part in the concentration of B at
grain boundaries. For example, as-cast alloys typically
exhibit larger grain sizes along with higher degree of
chemical heterogeneity.

Consistent with the predictions shown in Figures 1(a)
and (b), once borides form in cast superalloys, they
persist as thermodynamically stable phases and post-so-
lidiﬁcation heat treatments intended to homogenize or
solutionize the c¢ phases will not aﬀect their phase
fraction or chemistry. Powder-processed alloys, on the
other hand, utilize rapid solidiﬁcation and solid-state
mechanisms during processing to minimize dendritic
segregation and achieve a higher degree of both
microstructural and compositional homogeneity when
compared to cast and wrought alloys. Since the
extremely high cooling rates experienced during spray
atomization processing of Ni-based superalloy powders
have been shown to suppress dendritic segregation and
equilibrium carbide and boride transformations, the
powders contain a high degree of solute supersaturation.
During subsequent consolidation of the powder to form
a billet, solid-state diﬀusion mechanisms serve to redis-
tribute the solute and minimize the chemical potentials
in an eﬀort to move towards a more thermodynamically
stable state. Despite the much reduced length scales
associated with solid-state diﬀusion, chemical segrega-
tion does occur as excess interstitial solutes that have
limited solubility in the parent c and c¢ phases accumu-
late along grain boundaries and eventually lead to the
formation of carbides and borides. An APT study on a
commercial powder-processed Ni-based superalloy,
Astroloy, reported the existence of up to 20 at. pct B
along the grain boundary,
[51] showing that even in
powder-processed alloys elemental B segregates heavily
to the grain boundaries. Typically, both the c and c¢
phases in Ni-based superalloys exhibit limited solubility
for B, and concentrations of 0.08 and 0.04 at. pct
reported for the respective phases in a commercial
Ni-based superalloy RR1000,
[36] are consistent with
thermodynamic predictions showing extremely limited
solubility for boron in any phase other than the borides,
Table III. Gabb et al . looked at the formation of minor

and mostly re-dissolved during the solution heat treat-
ment, as the boride liquation temperature was found to
be slightly higher than the solutioning temperature.

Zhao et al . discuss the challenges with CALPHAD
predictions of superalloys and conclude that limited data
on TCP phases leads to inaccurate phase stability
predictions when alloys are prone to the formation of
such phases.
[54] Challenges with obtaining quantitative
data on elemental B and ﬁne boride particles using
conventional characterization techniques are quite high
due to elemental B being a low atomic number element,
which tends to segregate to grain boundaries. As such, few
data exist on boride formation and boride segregation in
superalloys, leading to discrepancies with thermody-
namic predictions. With the attributes of grain bound-
aries and B additions playing an important role in
determining the physical properties of the alloys there is
a need for both an improved understanding of the
governing mechanisms and high ﬁdelity data to populate
gaps in existing thermodynamic databases to aid eﬀorts in
the computational design of Ni-based superalloys.
V. CONCLUSIONS
Based on the results and observations presented in
this study, the following conclusions can be reported:
METALLURGICAL AND MATERIALS TRANSACTIONS A

## Page 10

1. High Nb-content Ni-based superalloys exhibit a
comparatively higher solubility of B in the c phase,
which leads to an overall lower concentration of B
along grain boundaries.
2. Formation of r phase affects boride formation, as it
depletes the boride-forming elements and dilutes the
elemental B while creating new interfaces.
3. Thermodynamic database models have limited pre-
diction capabilities when it comes to boride forma-
tion and composition in powder-processed Ni-based
superalloys, partly due to the lack of quantitative
data on B behavior.
