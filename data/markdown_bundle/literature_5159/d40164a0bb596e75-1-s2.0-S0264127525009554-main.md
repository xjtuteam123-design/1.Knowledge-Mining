Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys, with excellent mechanical properties in high temperature, are widely 
used as the preferred materials in the aerospace ﬁeld. Atomistic simulations provide an eﬀective means to 
understand the underlying physics of microstructure-property relationship. However, the reliability of atomistic 
simulation results highly depends on the precision of potential. In this work, a machine learning potential 
(MLP) for the ternary Ni-Co-Al system is developed using neuroevolution potential (NEP) framework which 
maintains a high degree of ﬁdelity and computational eﬃciency from ab initio molecular dynamics simulations 
(AIMD). It is demonstrated that the Ni-Co-Al MLP can be used to investigate the temperature and strain-rate 
dependent mechanical behaviors of Ni-based superalloys with the eﬀect of Ni3Al precipitation. Speciﬁcally, 
the following case studies about 𝛾/𝛾′ system are conducted: (1) the temperature and strain rate eﬀect on the 
𝛾/𝛾′ phase boundary characteristics and uniaxial tensile deformation behaviors; (2) the shock-induced phase 
transformation and spallation behaviors at diﬀerent strain rates. The atomistic ﬁndings promote the fundamental 
understanding of the mechanical responses of Ni-based SX superalloys with Ni3Al precipitation at diﬀerent 
temperature and strain rate conditions. Moreover, the MLP development process proposed in this work provides 
a feasible framework to develop diverse multicomponent interatomic potentials.
1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys are widely used as the pre-
ferred materials for various important components such as turbine 
blades for aircraft and gas turbines because of their excellent high tem-
perature creep, fatigue and corrosion resistance properties etc. It is 
important to unveil the underlying physics of mechanical behaviors of 
Ni-based SX superalloys under diﬀerent service conditions so as to guide 
high-performance superalloy design.
Nickel-based superalloys derive their exceptional high-temperature 
mechanical properties primarily from precipitation strengthening, where 
the lattice misﬁt-induced elastic stress ﬁelds and interfacial disloca-
tion networks at 𝛾/𝛾′ phase boundaries (PBs) play a pivotal role. As 
quantitatively demonstrated the minimal spanning tree-based statis-
tical approach, maximum strengthening occurs when precipitate size

on the previous studies on Ni-based SX superalloys, the main micro-

room temperature, 1/2<110> dislocations shear the 𝛾′ phase and 
generate anti-phase boundaries (APBs); (2) At moderate temperatures,

the interface dislocations bypassing the 𝛾′ phase [6–8].
Ni-based SX superalloys will frequently be impacted by high-pressure 
gas during service, and it is also of great signiﬁcance to study their 
ability to withstand shock loading. Shock is a rapid mechanical re-
sponse, and MD simulations, with their short time scales and high strain 
rates, are often used to capture microstructural evolution during shock 
processes. Wu et al. investigated the shock compression behavior of 
helium-containing porous copper through MD simulations, revealing the 
elastic-plastic double-shockwave structure and the protective “surface 
cap” mechanism, providing theoretical insights for the design of porous 
materials under extreme conditions [9]. Li et al. studied the shock re-
sistance of dislocation networks with diﬀerent orientations through MD 
simulations, and the results showed that the (100) PBs had the strongest 
resistance to shock loading [10]. Chen et al. systematically studied the 
mechanical behaviors and deformation mechanisms of [100]-oriented 
Ni-based SX superalloys under diﬀerent shock velocities, ﬁnding two 
types of spallation modes and quantitatively analyzing the relationship 
between shock velocity and spallation strength [11,12]. Sun et al. stud-
ied the microstructure evolution and cracking behaviors of Ni3Alsingle 
crystal models with [100], [110], and [111] orientations under diﬀerent 
shock velocities [13].
The in-depth study of the microscopic deformation mechanisms of 
Ni-based SX superalloys is useful for understanding their macroscopic 
mechanical behaviors. MD simulations can monitor the atom motion 
in real time, allowing the detailed investigation of microstructure evo-
lution. Notably, the reliability of MD simulations is fundamentally de-
termined by the accuracy of the interatomic potential employed. Re-
cently, machine learning potentials (MLP) have been demonstrated to 
be a promising approach to enable reliable MD simulations with a 
reasonable computational cost [14]. Byggmastar et al. used Gaussian 
approximation potential (GAP) [15] to develop a MLP for tungsten, fo-
cusing speciﬁcally on the radiation-induced collision cascades and dam-
age and achieving unprecedented accuracy [16]. Additionally, Nikoulis 
and Byggmastar used GAP to develop a MLP suitable for W𝑥Mo1−𝑥 al-
loys to study the alloying eﬀect on the threshold displacement energy 
[17]. They also developed an accurate MLP for the Mo-Nb-Ta-V-W al-
loy based on the GAP framework and study segregation and defects 
in body-centered cubic (BCC) refractory MoNbTaVW high-entropy al-
loy, demonstrating the evolution of defects are signiﬁcantly diﬀerent 
from those in tungsten [18]. Li et al. developed a MLP for the Ni-Mo 
binary alloy system based on the spectral neighbor analysis potential 
(SNAP) framework [19], which can accurately predict the alloy phase 
diagram of [20]. Ryltsev et al. developed a MLP for the ternary Al-Cu-Ni 
metal melt based on DeepMD [21,22], opening up using MLP to simu-
late the structural and kinetic properties of multicomponent alloys [23]. 
Zhao et al. developed a MLP for the quaternary Pd-Cu-Ni-P metallic 
glass based on the neuroevolution potential (NEP) framework [24–26], 
which can accurately predict the thermodynamic quantities such as the 
glass transition temperature and melting point, providing an accurate 
and eﬀective solution for exploring the thermodynamic, kinetic and mi-
crostructural evolution of Pd-Cu-Ni-P metallic glasses [27]. Liu et al. 
trained an MLP model for tungsten based on NEP and demonstrated 
its accuracy in elastic properties, melting point and various defect en-
ergetics related to radiation damage [28]. This work aims to develop a 
potential for Ni-based superalloys suitable for large-scale atomic simula-
tions, demanding both high accuracy and computational eﬃciency. The 
NEP framework known for its high precision and exceptional computa-
tional eﬃciency [29,30], was thus chosen for the potential training in 
this study. Yuan et al. investigated the eﬀects of L12 precipitate size on 
the mechanical properties of (FeCoNi)86Al7Ti7 medium-entropy alloys 
under varying strain rates and temperatures, revealing a transition from 
dislocation-shearing to Orowan looping mechanisms with increasing 
precipitate size and developing a theoretical model to predict strain-rate 
and temperature dependent strengthening behavior [31].
Although the conventional interatomic potentials for the Ni-Co-Al 
ternary system have been successfully applied to shape memory alloys 
[32] and high-entropy alloys [33], a dedicated potential speciﬁcally de-
veloped for Ni-based superalloy systems remains unavailable. On the 
other hand, the binary Ni-Al embedded atom method (EAM) potentials 
successfully captured the physical properties of stoichiometric NiAl and 
Ni3Al[34]. However, they failed to address the compositional complex-
ity inherent in actual Ni-based superalloys. While some ternary poten-
tials exist for this system, MD studies are often hindered by the absence 
of readily available potentials for more complex multi-element systems 
[5]. Khoei et al. found that adding Re to the 𝛾phase is capable of enhanc-
ing the creep resistance of Ni-based single crystal superalloys that stem 
from the increased dislocation pinning eﬀect and interfacial stress mod-
ulation. [35]. Crucially, Co is one of the main alloying elements with the 
highest content in third-generation low-cost SX superalloys. Yang et al. 
demonstrated that Co content signiﬁcantly aﬀects the stacking fault en-
ergy (SFE) of 𝛾phase [36]. Thereby, neglecting the eﬀect of Co element 
in MD simulations could obscure the key aspects such as multiplication 
mechanisms of dislocations, and their interplay with 𝛾′ precipitates.
In this work, we developed a MLP using the NEP framework for 
the ternary Ni-Co-Al system, speciﬁcally designed to investigate the me-
chanical behaviors of Ni-based superalloys. This MLP enables accurate 
modeling of 𝛾/𝛾′ system with the consideration of Co element. MD sim-
ulations are further conducted to investigate the mechanical responses 
under multifactorial conditions, including: (1) uniaxial tensile deforma-
tion with temperature and strain rate coupling eﬀect, and (2) shock 
response with the coupling eﬀect of crystal orientation and shock ve-
locity. These works could be beneﬁcial to address the combined eﬀects 
(temperature and strain rate etc.) on the mechanical response which 
remain insuﬃciently investigated [37]. The MD simulations simulta-
neously validate the robustness of the developed MLP under extreme 
conditions (elevated temperatures, high strain rates, etc.), meanwhile 
providing novel atomic insights into the multifactor deformation mech-
anisms of single-crystal (SX) superalloys. Moreover, the ternary Ni-Co-Al 
MLP can further be developed to simulate more realistic elemental com-
position of Ni-based superalloys.

2.1.1. DFT calculations
The ﬁrst-principles calculations are performed by Vienna ab initio 
simulation package (VASP) [38] based on density functional theory 
(DFT). The exchange-correlation term is described by the Perdew-Burke-
Ernzerhof (PBE) functional under the generalized gradient approxima-
tion (GGA) [39]. Spin polarization is considered in all calculations. 
For DFT static calculations, KSPACING is set as 0.2 Å−1. The energy 
cutoﬀ is set as 450 eV, and the convergence truncation of electron self-
consistency calculations is set as 10−8 eV. And for AIMD calculations, 
only Γpoints in the Brillouin zone are sampled.

2.1.2. Construction of the dataset
The training dataset consists of 4124 conﬁgurations of Ni-Co-Al al-
loys, with 90% allocated for training and 10% reserved for testing. 
The dataset was systematically constructed to ensure comprehensive 
coverage, including: (1) manually generated structures through elas-
tic deformations, atomic position perturbations, grain boundaries, sur-
faces, and vacancy introductions applied to various elemental composi-
tions; (2) AIMD-sampled structures for heating simulations and ﬁnite-
temperature stability. The elemental compositions of the above struc-
tures include Ni, Co, Al, Ni3Al alloy, and alloys of diﬀerent ratios of 
these elements. To enhance phase space sampling, each structure was 
further expanded through: (i) MD simulations at 300 K, 800 K, 1200 
K, and 1500 K under tensile/compressive loading (2×1 08 s−1), with 
20 uniformly sampled snapshots per temperature; (ii) three volume 
variants (90%, 110%, and 5% random distortion), each contributing 
5 additional conﬁgurations. The dataset spans isolated atoms/dimers, 
bulk phases, defects, and interfaces, with particular emphasis on 𝛾/𝛾′
interfacial structures and high-temperature deformation conﬁgurations 
to capture the essential physics of Ni-based superalloys.

2.2.2. Simulation method
In this work, equilibrium MD simulations are conducted to investi-
gate the inﬂuence of temperature and strain rate on the 𝛾/𝛾′ interface 
structure and the uniaxial tensile behaviors of Ni-based SX superalloys. 
Structure optimization of the cubic model (Fig. 1a) was carried out un-
der the periodic boundary conditions. Speciﬁcally, it was equilibrated 
for 200 ps with a time step of 1 fs under the NPT ensembles at various 
temperatures and 0 bar after energy minimization to minimize the arti-
ﬁcial elastic stress ﬁeld. After the NPT equilibration, tensile loading was 
applied along x-direction at a constant strain rate ̇𝜀=2.5×10 8 s−1. Be-
sides, two other strain rates 5×108 s−1 and 1×109 s−1 were also applied 
to examine the strain rate eﬀect.
On the other hand, nonequilibrium MD simulations were conducted 
to explore the shock induced spallation behaviors with respect to shock 
velocity and crystal orientation. Firstly, the region from 𝑧= 0 to 1 nm 
was set as a rigid body and used as a shock head. The shock head im-
pacted the model at a certain velocity under the NVE ensemble for 2 ps. 
Then, the shock loading was removed and the free evolution continued 
for 20 ps under the NVE ensemble.
The MD simulations are performed using the GPUMD code and the 
NEP of Ni-Co-Al system developed in this work. The EAM potential de-
veloped by Pun et al. [32] is used for some test calculations. OVITO 
[40] and Atomviewer [41,42] were used as visualization tools for the 
microstructure evolution analysis.

3.2. Temperature eﬀect on the phase interface
Since the mechanical properties of materials are closely related to 
the microstructure characteristics which could be temperature-sensitive, 
it is essential to study the 𝛾/𝛾′ interface at diﬀerent temperatures. As 
shown in Fig. 6, the lattice constants for 𝛾and 𝛾′ phases increase linearly 
with the increase in temperature, resulting in a larger volume of the 
system. Based on the lattice mismatch data calculated by Eq. (11), it can 
be found that the lattice mismatch between 𝛾 and 𝛾′ phases decreases 
as the temperature rises, which signiﬁcantly drops with the increase of 
temperature from 300 to 600 K.
The evolution of dislocation networks from 300 to 1200 K re-
veals complex temperature-dependent mechanical behavior, as shown 
in Figs. 7-9. At 300 K, the dislocation network is relatively intact, mainly 
composed of perfect dislocations and Shockley partials (Fig. 7a). As tem-
perature increases, thermal activation promotes dislocation dissociation 
into Shockley partials (Figs. 7a and b), reducing network coherence and 
initially decreasing yield strength. The breakdown of dislocation net-
work integrity facilitates the formation of L-C lock consisting of Shockley 
partials and stair-rod dislocations (Fig. 7e), which create eﬀective bar-
riers to dislocation motion and lead to anomalous yield strengthening. 
Besides, a large quantity of tangled dislocations can be observed at el-
evated temperature (Fig. 7f). Figs. 8a to c illustrates the evolution of 
various dislocation densities with strain during tensile deformation at 
diﬀerent temperatures. Fig. 8d presents the initial dislocation densities 
of diﬀerent types within the models prior to deformation at varying tem-
peratures. The decrease of initial stair-rod dislocation density at 1200 K 
(Fig. 8d) implies that the partials which decompose from perfect dislo-
cations become diﬃcult to evolve into or maintain the L-C lock structure 
at high temperatures. Instead, dislocation entanglement becomes dom-
inant and energetically favorable. On the other hand, it is shown in 
Fig. 8d that the initial density of perfect dislocations and Shockley par-
tials gradually decreases and increases with the increase of temperature, 
respectively. Whereas the initial density of stair-rod dislocations ﬁrst 
increases and then decreases. It indicates that thermal activation pro-
vides the driving force for the decomposition of perfect dislocations into 
Shockley partials and subsequent evolution into stair-rod dislocations. 
But the decreased lattice mismatch and the growing localized stress at 
1200 K (Fig. 9a) make L-C lock unstable although the average stress 
diﬀerence between 𝛾 and 𝛾′ phases decreases with the increase of tem-
perature (Fig. 9b), and thus prefer to form dislocation entanglement 
instead.

3.5. Shock and spallation behaviors
3.5.1. Shock-induced phase transformation
The study of dynamic impact behaviors of superalloys is essential 
for their hostile service conditions, which is conducted by investigating 
the shock and spallation behaviors under diﬀerent shock velocities in 
this work. Under high-speed shock compression, phase transformation 
dominates the main deformation mechanism which is crystal orienta-
tion dependent. Speciﬁcally, when the loading direction is along [100] 
orientation, the FCC to BCC transformation begins to be activated as 
the shock velocity reaches 1.0 km/s and gradually to be dominant with 
the increase of shock velocity (Fig. 16a). However, too fast shock veloc-
ity converts the FCC to BCC towards FCC to amorphous transformation, 
during which an FCC to BCC and FCC to amorphous hybrid transforma-
tion can occur when the shock velocity is around 3.0 km/s. It indicates 
that the energy barrier of FCC to BCC transformation is lower than that 
of FCC to amorphous transformation, yet it is dependent on the crys-
tal orientation relative to loading direction. For the [110] and [111] 
orientations, at lower shock velocities, the primary deformation mech-
anisms involve the generation and slip of SFs and twins (Figs. 16e2 and 
e3), rather than BCC phase transformation, which behavior is common 
in FCC metals [51]. The formation of these irreversible defects signiﬁ-
cantly reduces the spallation strength in the [110] and [111] directions 
compared to the [100] direction, a phenomenon consistently observed 
in experimental studies [52]. As the shock velocity exceeds 1.5 km/s 
(Figs. 16b and c), their shock response then becomes dominated by FCC 
to amorphous transformation.
Regarding the FCC to BCC phase transformation mentioned above, 
it is a common characteristic of FCC crystals under high strain rate com-
pression in the [100] direction. When an FCC crystal is compressed at 
a high strain rate in the [100] direction, it instantly transforms into 
BCC structure, and the higher the shock velocity, the greater the energy

3.5.2. Spallation behavior
Under shock loading, spallation is known as a common failure mode 
for superalloys. When a shock wave reaches the free surface of the ma-
terials, it forms a reﬂected rarefaction wave. At the same time, when 
the shock loading stops, an unloading wave is also generated. These 
two waves interact with each other during wave propagation inside the 
materials, inducing a large tensile stress, which causes the materials 
to undergo spallation behavior. Fig. 18 displays in detail the process 
of spallation behavior caused by shock loading at a shock velocity of 
1.0 km/s. When the model is subjected to shock loading, a compressive 
shock wave starts to propagate from the left end and forms a large com-
pressive stress region (blue region in Fig. 18a). Then the shock loading 
is removed after 2 ps of loading, resulting in an unloading wave at the 
left end which moves forward together with the shock wave (Fig. 18b). 
Afterwards, a reﬂected rarefaction wave generates when the shock wave 
reaches the free surface, moving face-to-face with the unloading wave 
(Fig. 18c). Subsequently, the inevitable encounter of two waves leads 
to an intense tensile stress ﬁeld (red region in Fig. 18d). Consequently, 
spallation occurs in the region where two waves meet initially which 
induce an intense tensile stress ﬁeld (Fig. 18e).

spallation process shown in Fig. 19a is a classical spallation mode at a 
low shock velocity of 1.0 km/s, that is, a pore ﬁrstly forms in the high 
tensile stress area, then the pore gradually grows and results in the fail-

4. Conclusions
By developing a highly precise, reliable and eﬃcient MLP, the tem-
perature and strain-rate dependent mechanical behaviors of Ni-based 
SX superalloys are studied in this work. The main conclusions are as 
follows:
(1) A MLP for ternary Ni-Co-Al system has been developed based on 
the NEP framework combining neural network models and evolutionary 
algorithms. It shows signiﬁcantly higher accuracy than the existing EAM 
potential reported in the literature. Compared with the experiments and 
ﬁrst-principles calculations, it is demonstrated that the developed MLP 
can accurately describe the basic properties such as lattice constants, 
elastic constants and GSFEs, further validating the accuracy and relia-
bility of its description of mechanical behaviors of 𝛾/𝛾′ system.
(2) The dislocation structure at phase interface is temperature-
sensitive. With the increase of temperature, perfect dislocations tend 
to decompose into Shockley partials. The dislocation interaction pro-

(3) During uniaxial tensile deformation, the Shockley partials mainly 
shear precipitate in the form of CSF+APB, and eventually evolves into 
a stable microstructure of SISF. The anomalous yield enhancement at 
900 K may be related to the activation of cross-slip to promote the for-
mation of stair-rod dislocations and {110} APB etc. Besides, the study 
of temperature and strain rate coupling eﬀect reveals that the smaller 
the strain rate, the higher the temperature at which anomalous yield 
enhancement occurs.
(4) The shock loading direction with respect to crystal orientation 
and shock velocities greatly aﬀect the shock and spallation behav-
iors of Ni-based SX superalloys, such as reversible FCC to BCC and 
irreversible FCC to amorphous transformations, classical and micro-
spallation modes. The spallation strength mainly depends on the phase 
transformation mode.
In a word, a NEP framework for high-precision MD simulations 
of temperature and strain-rate dependent mechanical behaviors of Ni-
based superalloys are proposed. Some atomistic insights about disloca-
tion evolution at phase interface, anomalous yield strength enhance-
ment and shock response are successfully revealed. It should be pointed 
out that further studies including the eﬀect of Co content deserve fur-
ther exploration. On the other hand, the ternary Ni-Co-Al MLP can be 
further developed based on NEP framework to simulate more realistic 
elemental composition of Ni-based superalloys to catch the signiﬁcant 
impacts caused by the presence of speciﬁc alloying elements.
