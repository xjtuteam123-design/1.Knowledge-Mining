THERMAL EFFECT ON THE STRUCTURAL AND PHASE CONDITION
AND MECHANICAL PROPERTIES OF ULTRAFINE-GRAINED
TITANIUM ALLOY VT16 IN THE TEMPERATURE RANGE OF 293 – 923 K
G . P. Grabovetskaya,1 I. V . Ratochka,1 I. P. Mishin, 2 O. N. Lykova, 2
and O. V . Zabudchenko 1
Translated from Metallovedenie i T ermicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 9, pp. 28 – 36, September, 2018.
The effect of pre-recrystallization annealing on the evolution of the structural and phase state and the deforma-
tion and fracture behavior of ultrafine-grained titanium alloy VT16 obtained by pressing with change of the
deformation axis and gradual lowering of the temperature within 1023 – 723 K is studied. It is shown that the
process of pre-recrystallization annealing is accompanied by retrogression of the strained structure, which
lowers the strength characteristics, and by a /c98/c174/c97phase transformation and redistribution of the alloying ele-
ments, which keep the strength properties at a high level.
Key words: titanium alloy, ultrafine-grained state, evolution of structure, mechanical properties,
fracture.
INTRODUCTION
Numerous studies conducted in the recent years in Rus -
sia and other countries have shown that creation of an ultra -
fine-grained state in titanium and its alloys by severe plastic
deformation (SPD) improves considerably their mechanical
properties [1 – 6]. However, the ultrafine-grained structure
formed by such methods is characterized by a high density of
deformation-induced defects, nonequilibrium grain bound -
aries and presence of elastic stress fields, which make it un -
stable. In pure metals the processes of retrogression of such
deformed structure and grain growth are observed already at
a temperature of (0.2 – 0.3) T
melt , which lowers the strength
properties to a level corresponding to a coarse-grained condi -
tion [5 – 7]. In addition to retrogression, the stability of the
properties of ultrafine-grained alloys is affected by the stabil -
ity of the phase composition. It has been shown that forma -
tion of an ultrafine-grained state in an alloy by methods of
SPD, may be accompanied by phase transformations at a
temperature other than the equilibrium one and formation of
supersaturated solid solutions [8 – 10]. Exposure of alloys
with ultrafine-grained structure to elevated temperatures may
cause reverse phase transformations and, consequently, a
change in the sizes of the structural components and proper -
ties of the alloy [3, 10, 11].
A possible way for stabilizing the ultrafine-grained state
is to create a structure, where the growth of grains is decele -
rated due to a combination of several phases of different fine -
ness [12, 13]. In ultrafine-grained alloys obtained by meth -
ods of SPD such a structure can be formed by purposeful use
of structural and phase transformations occurring under sub -
sequent heat treatments. In this connection, it is important to
study the thermal effect not only on the sizes of the compo -
nents of the ultrafine-grained structure of an alloy but also on
the evolution of its phase composition with the aim to find
methods for preserving the unique properties.
The aim of the present work was to study the complex ef -
fect of pre-recrystallization annealing on the evolution of the
structural and phase composition, mechanical properties and
fracture behavior of a titanium alloy of the Ti – A l–V–M o
system with an ultrafine-grained structure.

METHODS OF STUDY
The object of our study was commercial titanium alloy
VT16 with composition (in wt.%) 2.9 Al, 4.5 V + 4.8 Mo,
0.03 Zr + 0.05 Si + 0.06 Fe, /c1630.1 C, 0.15 O, 0.05 N, 0.002 H,
the remainder Ti. In the initial condition, alloy VT16 has a
lamellar structure with grains of the initial /c98-phase about
9 /c109m in size. VT6 is a two-phase ( /c97+ /c98) alloy. The volume
of the /c98-phase is 20 /c1771%.
We formed an ultrafine-grained structure in alloy VT16
by pressing with a change of the deformation axis and grad -
ual lowering of the temperature within 1023 – 723 K. Prior
to the pressing, the alloy was quenched from 1173 K. By the
data of the x-ray diffraction analysis, the quenching changed
the composition of the alloy for only /c97/c178-phase. A billet with
diameter 26 mm and a height of 40 mm was pressed in an
IP-2000 press at a rate of about 10
–3 sec –1 in five cycles.
One cycle consisted of three pressing operations. The defor -
mation in one cycle was about 50%. After each pressing, the
billet was cooled in water. The final billet with ultra -
fine-grained structure had a size of about 20 /c18024 /c18014 mm.
The alloy with the ultrafine-grained structure was annealed
in 10
–2 Pa vacuum at 573 – 973 K.
The structure of the alloy was studied using a JEM-2100
transmission electron microscope. The sizes of the structural
components in the ultrafine-grained condition were mea-
sured on dark-background micrographs by the method of se-
cants. A sample consisted of at least 200 elements.
The phase composition, the volume fraction, and the lat-
tice parameters of the phases were determined by standard
methods of x-ray diffraction analysis [14] from diffraction
patterns obtained with the help of a Shimadzu XRD-700
diffractometer in copper K
/c97 radiation. The presence of
phases /c97/c178, /c97and /c98in the alloy was detected by superimposi -
tion of standard diffraction patterns of these phases and the
diffraction patterns of the alloy studied. The volume fraction
of the /c98-phase was determined by the method of reference
standard. The lattice parameters of the phases were deter -
mined using the PowderCell software of the diffractometer.
The level of the internal stresses was determined from broad -
ening of the diffraction maximums at half their height using
the Cauchy approximation technique. To determine the con -
tributions of the microdeformation of the lattice and of the
regions of coherent scattering into the broadening of the dif -
fraction maximums, we used the diffraction maximums of
planes (100)/c97and (110)/c97[14].
The tensile tests were performed in the temperature
range of 293 – 973 K using a PV-3012M device with auto -
matic recording of the tension curve in the load-time coordi -
nates. The initial tension rate was 6.9 /c18010 –3 sec –1 . The
specimens for the tests were cut from billets by the
electrospark method in the shape of a double blade with
functional part 5 /c1801.5 /c1800.7 mm. Before testing, the surface
of the specimens was ground mechanically and polished
electrolytically. After the tension, the surface of the speci -
mens was studied using a Quanta 302D scanning ion-elec -
tron microscope.

after Pressing

Fig. 1. It can be seen from the light-background image
(Fig. 1 a ) that phase contrast is absent and individual grain
boundaries and dislocations are not revealed. The electron
diffraction pattern of such a structure taken from an area of
1.4 /c109m

By the data of the x-ray diffraction studies, the pressing

To study the effect of pre-recrystallization annealing on
the strain behavior and strength characteristics of the ultra-
fine-grained alloy VT16, we conducted annealing at 673 K
for 6 – 30 h. The tensile tests at room temperature showed
that the ultrafine-grained alloy VT16 exhibited susceptibility
to localization of strain both before and after annealing. The
uniform plastic strain of the ultrafine-grained alloy VT16 in
the state right after pressing did not exceed 1%. Virtually the
entire plastic strain localized in the region of two macro -
bands of localized plastic strain with a width of 0.2 – 0.3 mm.
At a stress equal to /c115r the specimens acquired the first
macroband of localized plastic strain at an angle of 60° to the
direction of the load applied. A well manifested neck deve -
loped on the base of this macroband and was surrounded by a
weakly manifested folded relief along the maximum tangen -
tial stresses (see Fig. 5 a). The neck continued to deform until
the appearance of the second macroband of localized plastic
strain directed at an angle of 120°C to the first band. The ap -
pearance of the second macroband caused failure of the spe -
cimen (Fig. 5 b).
After annealing for 6 – 30 h, the homogeneous strain of
the ultrafine-grained alloy increased to 2.5 – 3.5 %. At stress
/c115r , the specimens acquired a neck containing mesobands of
localized strain up to 10 /c109m wide (see Fig. 5 c). Then two
weakly manifested macrobands of localized plastic strain ap-
peared one after another in the region of the neck, and the
specimen failed over these bands (Fig. 5 d ).

ultrafine-grained alloy at room temperature on the time of

after 6-h annealing we observe noticeable decrease of the
values of /c115r , /c1150.2 and growth of /c100. When the annealing time
is increased to 12 – 30 h, the strength characteristics grow
progressively and the plastic characteristics decrease to a
level close to that obtained right after the pressing.

Studying the evolution of the structure of the alloy dur -
ing annealing at 673 K we established that after a 6-h hold,
the mean size of the components of the grain-subgrain struc -
ture and their size distribution remained virtually unchanged.
The alloy preserved a high density of strain-induced defects;
grain boundaries were not detectable on the electron micro-
graphs. However, annealing for 6 h caused partial recovery
of the strained structure of the alloy. This can be inferred
from the decrease of the elastic microdistortions of the crys-
tal lattice of the /c98-phase from 2 /c18010
–3 to 7 /c18010 –4 . The re-
covery of the strained structure seems to be responsible for
the observed decrease of the strength characteristics and
some increase of the plastic characteristics of the ultrafine-
grained alloy after the 6-h annealing.
Prolongation of annealing at 673 K to 12 – 30 h causes
appearance of grains of /c97-phase with reduced dislocation
density and better defined boundaries (Fig. 7 a – c). The
structure contains components with smaller sizes (Fig. 7 d )
and larger (with respect to the minimum and maximum sizes)
components of ultrafine-grained structure after pressing
(Figs. 1f and 7 e). The mean size of the components of the
grain-subgrain structure of the ultrafine-grained alloy re -
mains unchanged, i.e., d
min = 0.03 /c109m, dmax = 0.63 /c109m
(Fig. 7 e). Such size distribution of components in the ultra -
fine-grained structure indicates that annealing of the alloy at
673 K results not only in growth of individual components
but also in appearance of recrystallization nuclei or in preci -
pitation of fine particles of a new phase.

The XRD studies of the ultrafine-grained structure after
annealing of the alloy at 673 K have not shown appearance

diffraction picture reflects lowering of the volume fraction of

sumed that the nanosize particles of /c98-phase form as a result
of a /c98/c174/c97transformation. The elastic microdistortions of

connected with the fact that simultaneously with the reco-

growth of strength characteristics of alloy VT16 in ultrafine-

strong /c98-phase and growth of its strength due to redistribu-
tion of the alloying elements and precipitation of nanosize
particles of a secondary /c97-phase in the volume of the alloy
during annealing.

CONCLUSIONS
1. Formation of ultrafine-grained structure in titanium al -
loy VT16 raises its strength characteristics at room tempera -
ture by about 40% and halves the strain before failure as
compared to the fine-grained condition. At elevated test tem -
peratures the temperature of the appearance of superplastic
properties in ultrafine-grained alloy VT16 decreases to
823 K.
2. The dependence of the ultimate strength and of the
yield strength of ultrafine-grained alloy VT16 on the dura -
tion of pre-recrystallization annealing at 673 K is nonmono -
