ABSTRACT 
The effects of oxygen content on phase transformation, mechanical properties and corrosion resistance of Ti
–
Nb 
based alloys with different Nb contents (15, 32 and 38 wt%), i.e. different β stabilities were investigated. The 
results show that the oxygen does not change the phase constitution of α ’ -type Ti
–
15Nb based alloy with low β 
phase stability, while the oxygen can suppress the α ” martensite and ω phase, acting as β -stabilizer in the higher β 
phase stability of Ti
–
32Nb and Ti
–
38Nb based alloys. The addition of oxygen increases strength through solid 
solution strengthening and/or modification of deformation behavior. It has been found that simultaneous 
improvement in strength and ductility can be achieved in each base alloy by doping a certain content of oxygen. 
Young ’ s modulus of Ti-Nb-O alloys exhibits different trends with oxygen increasing, which is related to the 
different effects of oxygen on phase stability. A desirable balance between low Young ’ s modulus and high 
strength can be obtained through controlling oxygen and Nb content. The Ti-Nb-O alloys exhibit a high degree of 
corrosion resistance within simulated body fluid (SBF) at 310 K. Particularly, the Ti-38Nb-0.5O alloy exhibits low 
Young ’ s modulus (52 GPa), high yield strength (1141 MPa) and good elongation (22%), which has potential in 
biomedical applications. 
1. Introduction 
Titanium and its alloys have been extensively applied as biomaterials 
due to their remarkable strength-to-weight ratio, excellent corrosion 
resistance, and good biocompatibility [ 1 – 3 ]. Until now, Ti-6Al-4 V (wt 
%) has been the most popular alloy for orthopedic implants, which was 
first developed for aeronautic applications [ 4 ]. However, the problems 
in terms of mismatch in modulus between Ti-6Al-4 V and human bone 
[ 5 ] and the release of toxic Al and V [ 6 ] have motivated extensive efforts 
to develop Ti-based alloys with non-toxic elements and lower Young ’ s 
modulus. 
Ti
–
Nb alloys are in the center of on-going research efforts in the 
field of metallic biomaterials based on their low Young ’ s modulus, non- 
toxic, high corrosion resistance, shape memory effect, and super -
elasticity [ 7 – 10 ]. The Young ’ s modulus of Ti-26 at.% (~40 wt%) Nb 
alloy is as low as 61 GPa. However, lowering the Young ’ s modulus of Ti 
alloys is often accompanied by low strength, leading to a low incipient 
yield strength of only 180 MPa observed in Ti-26 at.% Nb alloy [ 11 ], 
which greatly limits its usage in biomedical applications. Interstitial 
oxygen is generally known to increase the strength of Ti alloys [ 12 ]. 
Besides, oxygen addition can suppress the athermal ω phase with the 
highest Young ’ s modulus in Ti alloys during quenching, which is 
beneficial for obtaining low Young ’ s modulus [ 13 ]. Therefore, oxygen 
addition may play an important role in improving the mechanical 
biocompatibility of Ti
–
Nb alloys showing low Young ’ s modulus and 
high strength. Recently, a multifunctional Ti alloy termed as gum metal 
has attracted considerable attention because of its high strength (~1.1 
GPa), low Young ’ s modulus, and high recoverable strain, and these 
special properties are strongly related to the oxygen addition [ 14 , 15 ]. 
Since the appearance of gum metal, research interest in the effect of 
oxygen addition on phase stability and mechanical properties of Ti al -
loys is continuously increasing. 
In general, oxygen is treated as an α -phase stabilizer and the addition 
of 1 at.% oxygen to pure Ti increased the α / β transus temperature by 88 
K [ 16 ]. In β -type Ti-based alloys, oxygen atoms occupy the octahedral 
interstitial sites of the body-center cubic (BCC) lattice and suppress α ”

Fig. 5 shows the change trend of Young ’ s modulus of the Ti-Nb-O

3.2.3. Tensile properties 
Fig. 7 shows the tensile properties of TiNbO alloys. For Ti-15Nb- x O 
alloys, the yield strength increases from 462 MPa to 797 MPa and the 
ultimate tensile strength (UTS) increases from 648 MPa to 923 MPa with 
the oxygen content increases from 0 to 0.5 wt%. While the elongation 
exhibits a decreasing tendency and the value is only 2% when 0.5% 
oxygen is added. For Ti-32Nb- x O alloys, both the yield strength and the 
ultimate tensile strength increase substantially with the increase of ox -
ygen content. Interestingly, simultaneously improved strength and 
ductility are achieved in Ti-32Nb-0.2, 0.5O alloys referring to oxygen- 
free counterpart [ 27 ]. Compared to the Ti-15Nb- x O alloys, the defor -
mation behavior reflected from the stress-strain curves of the Ti-32Nb- 
x O alloys changes with the change of oxygen content. The Ti
–
32Nb and 
Ti-32Nb-0.2O alloys show an obvious double yielding behavior, which is 
the typical stress-induced martensitic transformation of metastable 
β -type Ti alloys. When further increased oxygen to 0.5 wt%, the double 
yielding behavior is suppressed, and a high work hardening rate close to 
2100 MPa is appeared during plastic deformation, resulting in UTS of 
958 MPa and uniform elongation of ~22% in Ti-32Nb-0.5O alloy, which 
is higher than the low Nb-containing Ti-15Nb-0.5O alloy. Similar phe -
nomena were also observed in other metastable β -Ti alloy, such as Ti- 
9Mo-6 W [ 28 ], Ti-8.5Cr-1.5Sn [ 29 ], and Ti-6Cr-4Mo-2Al-2Sn-1Zr [ 30 ]. 
For Ti-38Nb- x O alloys, the yield strength increases dramatically from 
220 MPa to 1141 MPa and the UTS increases from 479 MPa to 1218 MPa 
with the oxygen content increasing from 0 to 0.5 wt%. The Ti
–
38Nb 
and Ti-38Nb-0.2O alloys exhibit a similar double yield phenomenon, 
which indicates the existence of stress-induced martensite trans -
formation. However, the deformation mode of the Ti-38Nb-0.5O alloy is 
dominated by dislocation slipping, with a distinct yield drop limited 
work hardening. The results imply that the deformation mechanisms 
and corresponding mechanical properties of the Ti
–
Nb binary alloys are 
largely dependent on the phase stability and oxygen content, and an 
excellent combination of low Young ’ s modulus, high strength and high 
ductility is obtainable by properly tailoring the oxygen addition.

4. Discussion 
4.1. The role of oxygen on phase transformation 
Different phase constitutions including martensitic phases ( α ’ or α ” ), 
ω phase, β phase, or a combination of them were obtained by controlling 
the content of Nb and O. These changes are closely related to the 
different roles of oxygen on phase transformation in Ti-Nb-O alloys with 
different β stabilities, i.e. different Nb contents. 
It is clearly found that α “ martensite formation is suppressed with the 
addition of oxygen in Ti-32Nb- x O and Ti-38Nb- x O alloys. The result is 
due to the interstitial oxygen stabilizing the β phase and playing a role in 
suppressing β– α ” martensitic transformation, which is consistent with 
previous reports [ 16 , 34 , 35 ]. However, the oxygen addition does not 
change the phase constitution of Ti-15Nb- x O alloys, and all the alloys 
mainly contain α ’ phase, even the oxygen content reaches 0.5%. The 
different roles of oxygen on α ’ and α “ is related to the β stability of alloys. 
The α ’ and α ” phase are two kinds of martensitic phases with different 
crystal structures formed during cooling the β phase, which have the 
same composition with the parent β phase [ 36 ]. The martensitic trans -
formation of Ti-based alloys is a bcc-hcp type transformation, and it can 
be divided into two processes: shuffling of parallel adjacent 〈 110 〉
β 
plans 
in the 〈 110 〉
β 
direction and shearing on the 〈 112 〉
β 
plans in the 〈 111 〉
β 
direction [ 37 ]. And the shuffle and shear magnitude reflects the crystal 
structure of martensite, which depends sensitively on the β stability, i.e. 
content of alloying elements [ 38 ]. The increase of β stability can inhibit 
the shuffling and shearing processes. Orthorhombic martensite α ” forms 
in alloys with higher β stability, whereas hexagonal-close-packed 
martensite α ’ forms in alloys with lower β stability. 
The oxygen atoms occupy the octahedral interstices in lattice of the β 
phase, which results in crystal structure transformation from cubic to 
nearly orthorhombic structure. The transformation is derived from 
atoms which constitute interstices being excluded at 〈 100 〉 direction 
after structure optimization, leading to the decrease in lattice constants 
of a- and b -axis and increase of c -axis [ 39 ]. And the oxygen atoms 
generate local strain fields, which are relaxed by shuffling adjacent 
{110}
β 
planes in the < 110 >
β 
directions corresponds to the nanodomains 
[ 18 ]. These randomly distributed nanodomains suppress the growth of 
long-range α ” martensite, i.e. therefore, the β phase is stabilized by ox -
ygen atoms [ 18 , 34 , 37 , 40 ]. The phenomenon that α ” martensitic trans -
formation suppressed by the addition of oxygen has been widely 
confirmed in β -Ti alloys and its mechanism has been extensively re -
ported and accepted. However, when the β stability of the alloy is lower 
than a certain critical value, the shuffling and shearing processes suffer 
little resistance and occur completely. The equilibrium structure is the 
hexagonal α ’ phase. The addition of oxygen enhances these processes. 
Just like adding oxygen to pure Ti, the HCP α phase is stabilized and that 
oxygen is treated as an α -phase stabilizer. The oxygen atoms occupy 
octahedral sites in hexagonal α ’ martensite and expand the surrounding 
atoms (Ti and Nb) ( Fig. 10 ), similar to that in the HCP α phase.

phases is a competitive process [ 44 ]. Therefore, the phase constitution 
changes from α ” + β to ω + β and further to a single β phase with 
increasing oxygen content in the Ti-32Nb- x O alloys. 
In summary, the suppression of martensite depends on both Nb and 
O content. For the alloy with low β stability, oxygen cannot exhibit or 
very weak β stabilizing effect, but when the β stability is high, the β 
stabilizing element effect of oxygen appears. Consequently, the β -sta -
bilizing effect of oxygen needs to be added in combination with other 
β -stabilizing elements to perform. 
4.2. The effect of oxygen on mechanical properties 
The addition of oxygen has a great influence on the mechanical 
properties, which is related to the phase constitution and phase stability. 
The α ’ and ω phases have a higher hardness than α ” and β . Besides, the 
oxygen has a strong solution strengthening effect. Interstitial oxygen 
atoms create a Cottrell atmosphere around edge dislocations and a 
Snoek atmosphere around screw dislocations while acting as obstacles 
for dislocation motions [ 43 ]. Both of them result in the change of me -
chanical properties. 
The addition of oxygen is shown to be very effective to increase the 
strength, which is attributed to the solid-solution strengthening. How -
ever, the elongation exhibits a different trend with the addition of ox -
ygen. In HCP α -Ti lattice, the O atoms generate a strong but short-range 
repulsive force for moving screw dislocations, which forces them to 
cross slip to neighboring planes or makes them struggle to move with O 
atoms [ 45 ]. This strong oxygen-screw dislocation interaction increases 
strength but often reduces ductility with oxygen increasing. Therefore, 
the increase of strength and decrease of plasticity with the increase of 
oxygen content in Ti-15Nb- x O alloys may due to the similar crystal 
structure of α and α ’ phase and the resulting strong oxygen-screw 
dislocation interaction. The addition of high oxygen content changes 
the fracture behaviors of the Ti-15Nb- x O alloys from plastic fracture to 
brittle fracture. The effect of oxygen on mechanical properties is rather 
complex. The dominant mechanisms of plastic deformation also change 
with increasing content of oxygen. In general, the conventional β -Ti 
alloys can tolerate a relatively high oxygen content compared with ( α +
β )- and α -Ti alloys [ 46 ]. Ti-32Nb-0.5O alloy still shows good ductility 
(~26%) at high oxygen levels (~0.5%). On one hand, the absence or 
reduced presence of the ω phases due to the high level of oxygen may be 
responsible for the less aggressive reduction in ductility. On the other 
hand, the deformation mechanisms of metastable β -Ti alloys, such as 
mechanical twinning and stress-induced phase transformation, 
contribute to good ductility [ 47 ]. The double yielding behavior is 
observed in Ti

A distinct yield drop is observed in the Ti-38Nb-0.5O alloy. And 
similar yielding behavior has been reported in Ti-36Nb-2Ta-3Zr-0.5O

usually due to the strain aging which slightly increases the critical

5. Conclusions 
The influences of oxygen content on the microstructure and me -
chanical properties and corrosion resistance in simulated body fluid of 
Ti-(15, 32, 38)Nb base alloys with different β phase stabilities were 
evaluated, and the following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) The α ’ phases mainly exist in Ti

oxygen content up to 0.5 wt%. For the Ti

β phases to a single β phase. The roles of oxygen in stabilizing β 
phase and suppressing martensite need to be manifested under

(2) The strength and micro-hardness increased with the addition of O 
through solid solution strengthening. With the increase of oxygen 
content in Ti

increased β stability by oxygen addition. For each base alloy, the 
strength and plasticity can be improved simultaneously by adding 
appropriate content of oxygen element. 
(3) The addition of oxygen had a great influence on Young ’ s modulus 
of Ti-Nb-O alloys due to the contribution to bonding and phase 
constitution. In alloys with different Nb contents, the roles of 
oxygen on Young ’ s modulus were different. 
(4) Ti-Nb-O alloys exhibited a high degree of corrosion resistance 
within simulated body fluid at 310 K. The addition of oxygen had 
a positive effect on corrosion resistance. 
(5) A desirable balance between low Young ’ s modulus, high 
strength, and good corrosion resistance was obtainable through 
controlling oxygen and Nb content. The Ti-38Nb-0.5O alloy was 
found to exhibit low Young ’ s modulus (52 GPa), high yield 
strength (1141 MPa), good elongation (22%), and good corrosion 
resistance.
