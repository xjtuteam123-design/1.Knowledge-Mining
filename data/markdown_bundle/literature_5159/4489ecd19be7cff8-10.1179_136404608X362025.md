Development of short alumina fibre and in situ
Mg2Si particle reinforced magnesium alloy
hybrid composite with high temperature
strength
K. Asano * and H. Yoneda
Short alumina fibre and in situ Mg2Si particle reinforced hybrid composites were fabricated by
infiltration with the molten magnesium alloy into the preforms consisting of the fibres having Si
particles attached to their surfaces. P or CaF 2 particles were used as the refiners of the Mg 2Si
particles. All of Si particles reacted with the Mg alloy and melt to form Mg 2Si particles. As the
melting temperature decreased or the cooling rate after the infiltration increased, the Mg 2Si
particles became finer. The introduction of P or CaF 2 further promoted the refinement of the Mg 2Si
particles. When the composite was fabricated by squeeze casting in the permanent mould, fine
Mg2Si particles with a grain size of ,5 mm were formed regardless of introducing the refiners due
to the rapid solidification. The strength of the hybrid composites was higher than that of the
conventional fibre reinforced composite at both room temperature and high temperature.
Keywords: Composite, Alumina fibre, Magnesium, in situ Mg 2Si, Microstructure, High temperature strength
Introduction
Magnesium (Mg) alloys have been recently used in
electrical instruments, mobile instruments and automo-
biles in which their lightweight is an advantage.
However, the strength of the Mg alloy sharply decreases
with the increasing temperature. Although heat resistant
Mg alloys containing alloying elements have been
developed, their use in parts that require high tempera-
ture strength is still limited. The authors fabricated the
AZ91D Mg alloy composite reinforced with short
alumina ﬁbres and clariﬁed that the reinforcement
improved the high temperature strength of the alloy.
1
The high temperature strength of the composite would
be further improved by dispersion of the heat resistant
phases in its matrix. Mg
2Si, a heat resistant intermetallic
compound, is a candidate for the dispersed phase
because it has a low density (2 ?0M gm
23) (Ref. 2) and
can be in situ formed by the introduction of Si into the
Mg alloy melt. However, there are few reports about the
composite reinforced with short alumina ﬁbres and in
situ Mg
2Si particles.
In the present study, the composites were fabricated
by inﬁltration of the preform consisting of ﬁbres and Si
particles with the alloy melt. The effects of the volume
fraction of the Si particles in the preform, the melting
temperature, the cooling rate after the inﬁltration and
the addition of the reﬁners on the reﬁnement of the
Mg
2Si have been investigated, and the formation and
the reﬁnement mechanisms are discussed. Subsequently,
the high temperature tensile strength of the composites
fabricated by squeeze casting was investigated.

Formation, dispersion and refinement
mechanism of Mg 2Si
Mg2Si is formed by the reaction between Mg in the alloy
and Si, as designated by equation (1) (Ref. 4):
2MgzSi?Mg2Si (1)
A previous report showed that this reaction occurs at
,800 K, at which both the Mg alloy and Si are in the
solid state. 4 This indicates that the reaction will occur
under the present condition (958 K) and that Mg 2Si is
formed in a short time. In the present study, the Mg 2Si
particles were homogeneously dispersed. This is prob-
ably due to the fact that the Si particles were
homogeneously supported by the alumina ﬁbres in the
preform, and segmentation of the Mg
2Si particles by
inﬁltration was prevented due to the presence of the
ﬁbres; Mg
2Si was formed near the former locations of
the Si.

Based on these ﬁndings, the formation and dispersion
process of the Mg 2Si particles in the present study is
schematically shown in Fig. 10. After a Si particle
contacts with the Mg alloy melt, Mg 2Si particles are
quickly formed by the reaction near the Si particle. At
the same time, the Si starts to dissolve into the melt.
Unless the Si content exceeds the solubility into Mg, all
of the Si particles dissolve into the melt. While the melt
including the dissolved Si is solidifying, the primary and
eutectic Mg
2Si crystallise out.
Subsequently, the reason why the Mg 2Si particles
were reﬁned using the preform with P or CaF 2 particles
was considered. Kim et al. 5 report that the addition of
elements, such as P or Ca, to the Mg alloy melt reﬁnes
the in situ Mg
2Si particles because these elements
produce the compound [Mg(PO 3)2, etc.] particles, which
promote the heterogeneous nucleation of the Mg 2Si.
Although the nucleation site could not be found in the
present study, the compound formed by the reaction
between the reﬁner and the melt would act as the
nucleation site of the Mg
2Si and promote the reﬁnement.
Because this reﬁnement effect was observed when VSi
was 6 ?5 and 9 ?5 vol.-% as well, it can be said that the
reﬁners are effective for the Mg 2Si formed in a short
time as well.
