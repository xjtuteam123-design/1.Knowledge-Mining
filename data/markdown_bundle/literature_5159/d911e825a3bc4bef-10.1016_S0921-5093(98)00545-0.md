Abstract
The effects of Ir addition on the microstructural characteristics of Ni-based single crystal superalloys have been investigated
using atom probe ﬁeld ion microscopy (APFIM) and the results were compared with numerical estimations obtained from the
Monte Carlo simulations (MCS) and the cluster variation method (CVM). The alloy investigated was TMS-79 (Ni–12.6 at%
Al–7.7 at% Cr–4.6 at% Mo–2.7 at% Ta–1.8 at% Ir). APFIM analysis revealed that Ir atoms have a slight preference to partition
into the g-phase and to substitute for the Al site in the g% precipitates, which showed agreement with numerical estimations by
CVM and MCS. The possible effect of Ir additions on the mechanical properties is brieﬂy discussed. © 1998 Elsevier Science S.A.
All rights reserved.
Keywords: Ni-based superalloys; Microanalysis; Cluster variation method
1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal superalloys, which have re-
markable mechanical properties at elevated tempera-
tures, have been designed for use as turbine blades in
aeroengines. In order to develop higher efﬁciency en-
gines, considerable efforts are being devoted to enhance
the temperature capabilities of superalloys. In addition
to Ni and Al, the superalloys usually contain several
alloying elements to enhance heat resistibility, such as
Cr, Co, Mo, W, Ta. Among the alloying additions, Re
has recently been paid much attention because of its
contribution to improve both the creep and corrosion
properties [1]. However, Re addition also assists the
formation of a topologically close-packed (TCP) phase
[2,3], partly because Re has a low diffusibility, which
results in the segregation where precipitation of the
TCP phase easily occurs [4,5], and partly because the
solubility limit of Re in Ni is rather low due to the
difference in atomic structure between Re (h.c.p.) and
Ni (f.c.c.). On the other hand, addition of Ir in Ni-
based superalloys is of particular interest. Since Ir and
Ni have a complete solid solution system, we expect a
fairly high amount of Ir can be alloyed compared with
Re. It should also be noted that there have been no
reports about Ir-bearing Ni-based superalloys.
The main objective of this study is to investigate the
microscopic characteristics of an Ir-bearing superalloy
using atom probe ﬁeld ion microscopy (APFIM) and
high temperature X-ray diffractometry (HTXRD). The
derived data were also compared with the numerical
estimations obtained from the cluster variation method
(CVM) and the Monte Carlo simulations (MCS).
2. Experimental
The Ir-bearing alloy TMS-79 (Ni–12.6 at% Al–7.7
at% Cr–4.6 at% Mo–2.7 at% Ta–1.8 at% Ir), which
was designed by the authors by adding Ir to the base
material TMS-63 (Ni–12.8 at% Al–7.8 at% Cr–4.6 at%
Mo–2.8 at% Ta) [2,3], was solution heat treated at

TMS-79 has a coherent g and g% two phase structure
without any additional phases (Fig. 1). The heat-treated
material was then examined using APFIM for investi-
gating the atomic location of solute atoms, and
HTXRD for determining the lattice misﬁts between the
g and g% phases, respectively. For APFIM analysis,

and site occupation behaviour of alloying elements

5. Conclusions
The atomic structure of Ir-bearing Ni-based single
crystal superalloy TMS-79 has been investigated. It is
found by APFIM analysis that Ir has a slight prefer-
ence to partition into the g phase and occupy for the Al
sites in the g% phase, which were in agreement with
numerical estimations derived by CVM and MCS.
However, the partitioning preference to the g phase is
less stronger than that of Re and Mo. The typical
conﬁgurational features of Ir, such as enrichment at
interfaces, which were often observed in Re-bearing
alloys, were not observed in this alloy system. The
lattice misﬁt between the two phases in TMS-79 is more
negative than that of the base alloy TMS-63 at less than
1000°C, and similar at 1000°C or more, which may be
attributed to the change in partitioning behaviour of Ir
as a function of temperature. Alloying of Ir is expected
to play a role as a solid solutioning strengthener, for
improving the thermal properties of Ni-based alloys.
