ABSTRACT 
Inconel 718 (In-718) alloy has excellent high-temperature mechanical properties and is a strategic material in the 
military industry, but its high-temperature mechanical properties may decrease due to the comprehensive 
negative effects of grains and Laves phase coarsening during processing and application. In this paper, a fast- 
frequency pulsed tungsten inert gas (FFP-TIG) welding process was proposed, and two different waveforms 
were introduced: fast-frequency double-pulsed (FFDP) waveforms and fast-frequency single-pulsed (FFSP) 
waveforms. By studying the weld morphology, microstructure and mechanical properties of In-718 alloy 
weldments, the results showed that FFP-TIG welding reduces heat input, promotes molten pool flow, refines 
grains and other effects, thereby improving the high-temperature tensile strength of In-718 alloy weldments. 
FFDP waveforms have a better effect on grain refinement than FFSP waveforms due to the reduction in average 
heat input and the enhancement of stirring molten pool. The minimum average grain size and Laves phase size of 
the FFP-TIG weldment were 38.14 μ m and 1.133 μ m, respectively. Similarly, the high-temperature tensile 
strength of FFP-TIG weldments reaches a maximum of 685.54 MPa, which was 90.9 % of the base metal strength. 
1. Introduction 
With the increasingly stringent requirements for material properties 
in the modern military industry, superalloys have attracted much 
attention due to their irreplaceability. Among them, Inconel 718 (In- 
718) is widely used in aviation, aerospace, navigation, petroleum, nu -
clear power, and other fields due to its excellent high-temperature 
comprehensive mechanical properties [1 – 3] . The mechanical proper -
ties of In-718 weld joints at high temperatures are closely associated 
with grain size, grain boundaries (GBs) misorientation, and phase pre -
cipitations (such as Laves phase), and determine the service-life of In- 
718 [4 – 6] . Therefore, refining the grains, reducing the orientation de -
viation of adjacent GBs, and the precipitation of Laves phase to 
strengthen In-718 superalloys is the current mainstream research 
direction. 
The influence of grain characteristics on material properties has been 
widely demonstrated by researchers. Chen et al. [6] proposed the 
concept of detrimental grain boundary (GB) length, and the results 
showed that grain refinement brought about the dispersion of detri -
mental GB and the reduction of detrimental GB length, which reduced 
the plastic tendency cracking susceptibility of Inconel 52 M alloy. In the 
study of Sui et al. [7] , the recrystallization degree of the L-type sample is 
higher than that of the S-type sample, and the twin crystals and the 
grains are more uniform in the L-type sample, which makes it have a 
higher elongation than the S-type sample. In addition, by reducing the 
proportion of low-angle GBs, evenly distributing high-angle GBs can 
prevent crack propagation [8,9] . The strength of GBs mainly affects the 
crack resistance of superalloys, and the initiation of cracks is closely 
related to the dislocation angle of GBs. With the increased dislocation 
angle, the strength loss of GBs is pretty apparent [4] . 
Due to the micro-segregation of niobium, molybdenum, and other 
alloying elements during the welding process, In-718 forms a multitude 
of hard and brittle Laves phases (generally micron-scale) at the end of 
solidification [10,11] . Although a slice of studies had shown that the 
nano-scale granular Laves phases could be used as the strengthening 
phases of some alloys [12,13] , it was generally believed that Laves phase

In this regard, researchers had launched a series of studies around the 
Laves phase. During the room temperature tensile test, the granular 
Laves phases were challenging to be broken, while the long striped Laves 
phases were simple to slip and break. Immediately, micropores were 
formed at the interface with the austenite matrix and Laves phase, which 
promoted the propagation of cracks and eventually led to the fracture of 
the sample [17] . During the high-cycle fatigue test, under low-stress 
amplitude, the unfractured Laves phases played a critical role in hin -
dering crack propagation. In contrast, under high-stress amplitude, the 
Laves phases behaved as weakening phases, favorable for crack initia -
tion and extension [18] . The research of Sui et al. showed that the 
granular Laves phases were more conducive to the plastic deformation 
of In-718 than the elongated Laves phases, and the volume fraction, size, 
and distribution of the Laves phases affect the tensile strength and yield 
strength of In-718 [19] . Based on the above, it could be known that the 
mechanical properties of In-718 could be improved by adjusting the size, 
morphology, and distribution of the Laves phase [20] . 
Researchers typically adopt remelting processing to refine grains and 
Laves phases. The electron beam welding has a lower heat input and a 
higher cooling rate in the melting zone, so it significantly refines the In- 
718 weldment grain and reduces the size and number of Laves phases 
[21] . During tungsten inert gas (TIG) welding, the morphology and 
composition of the Laves phase are closely related to the heat input/ 
cooling rate. Reducing the heat input/increasing the cooling rate can 
decrease the segregation of Nb and change the morphology of the Laves 
phase [16] . In TIG welding, magnetic arc oscillation and pulse current 
are used to increase the cooling rate, which can significantly refine the 
structure of the weldment fusion zone, improve the solid solubility of the 
strengthening alloy elements in the grains, and reduce the percentage of 
Laves phase in the weldment fusion zone, thereby enhancing the fatigue 
resistance of In-718 superalloys [22,23] . In addition, the introduction of 
mechanical vibration and ultrasonic stirring in TIG welding could also 
effectively refine the grains and strengthen the mechanical properties of 
the weldments [24] . 
Recently, a welding method that introduces fast-frequency pulsed 
(FFP) current in TIG (FFP-TIG) welding through an advanced welding 
power system has attracted much attention. Compared with the external 
magnetic stirring device and mechanical ultrasonic vibration in the 
welding process, FFP-TIG welding has the advantages of low cost and 
convenient operation [25] . Researchers have studied FFP-TIG welding 
of aluminum alloys, titanium alloys, etc., and the results showed that the 
introduction of FFP current was beneficial to welding arc shrinkage, 
grain refinement of weld joint structure, reduction of pores or cracks, 
and improvement of mechanical properties of weld joints [25 – 27] . 
However, studies on the microstructure and properties of In-718 su -
peralloys based on FFP-TIG welding are rarely reported. 
Hence, this paper aims to study the weld-forming properties, 
microstructure, and tensile strength evolution of In-718 superalloys and 
their interrelationships. In-718 weldments were fabricated by FFP-TIG 
welding with different waveforms, and the weld formation, grains and 
Laves phase sizes, GB characteristics, and high-temperature tensile 
properties of the weldments were compared. The results showed that 
FFP-TIG welding had provided a new idea for the modern military

2. Experimental method 
2.1. Experimental setup 
Fig. 1 (a) shows the schematic of the FFP-TIG welding system. The 
core part of the FFP-TIG system consists of three parts, namely (1) the

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Weld forming 
During the pulse welding process, pulse current parameters have a 
significant impact on weld formation [9,10] . In order to reveal the effect 
of FFP-TIG waveform on the cross-sectional profile of In-718 superalloys 
weldments, a set of pulsed TIG comparative experiments were designed: 
waveform base/peak current (60/80 A) with a frequency of 50 Hz. The 
measurement results of weld penetration and weld width of In-718 su -
peralloy weldments obtained by pulsed TIG welding and FFP-TIG 
welding are shown in Fig. 4 . Under the influence of FFP waveform, 
the weld width and penetration depth of weldments obtained by FFP- 
TIG welding are significantly reduced. Compared with pulsed TIG 
welding, the weld width obtained by FFSP waveform and FFDP wave -
form is reduced by about 9.3 % and 17.1 %, and the weld depth is 
reduced by about 22.4 % and 29.1 %, respectively. This shows that the 
heat input of FFP-TIG welding is significantly lower than that of TIG 
welding. Based on the calculation of Eq. (1) , the average equivalent 
current was reduced by adjusting the I

heat input of the weldments, which in turn affects the weld topography. 
At the same time, the increase in the peak current value ( I

effect will enhance the arc pressure, and then the arc pressure is in 
close contact with the surface of the molten pool [4,5] , resulting in the 
diffusion of the arc heat source to the inside of the weld concentrated, 
which is the main reason for improving the weld formation. 
3.2. Microstructure 
3.2.1. Grain characteristics 
The effect of grain morphology and size on joint performance has 
been widely demonstrated [20,29,30] . To reveal the influence of the 
FFSP waveform and FFDP waveform on the grain size of In-718 weld -
ments, EBSD analysis was carried out on In-718 weldments under 
different waveform parameters. Fig. 5 shows the grain and orientation 
characteristics of In-718 weldments in FFP-TIG welding with different 
waveforms. It could be seen that coarse dendrites dominated the 
microstructure of the In-718 weldments in FFP-TIG welding. During the 
solidification process, the nuclei grow freely in all directions at 
approximately equal speeds in the molten pool. Thus, the shape of the 
In-718 grains was elliptical or irregular polygonal equiaxed crystals. The 
grain sizes of the weldments obtained by the FFSP and FFDP waveforms 
were 41.69 μ m and 38.14 μ m, respectively. Fig. 5 (h) shows the average 
grain size of the In-718 weldments under different welding waveforms. 
Compared with the original waveform (30/0 A), the weldment grain size 
obtained by the FFSP and FFDP waveforms was reduced by about 9.35 % 
and 17.07 %, respectively. 
The reducing welding heat input and stirring molten pool synergis -
tically refined grain of In-718 weldments with the adopting FFP current. 
The decreasing average equivalent current would reduce welding heat 
input that affects weld solidification process and cause the grain 
refinement of In-718 weldments. An additional factor causing the 
refining microstructure of In-718 weldments is the stirring molten pool

affecting the microstructure of materials [38] . When dendrites growing 
in different directions merge in contact, the merging interface forms a 
GB, while the dendrites growing in the exact directions form large 
austenite grains. Generally, the GB angle types are divided into high- 
angle GBs (HAGBs > 15
◦

Fig. 7 (a1-f1) shows the distribution of the GB deflection angle of In-718 
weldments affected by FFP-TIG with different waveforms, and the cor -
responding statistical graph of the GB deflection angle of the weldments 
is shown in Fig. 8 (a). When the composite waveform base/peak current

3.2.3. Laves phase 
The main component of the Laves phase is a large amount of 
precipitated niobium-rich phase. In the molten pool, niobium atoms 
diffuse in the molten metal and combine with Fe atoms to form Laves 
phase nucleus particles. The distribution characteristics of Laves phase 
of In-718 weldments obtained by waveform evolution test are shown in 
Fig. 9 . It is observed that a large number of Laves phases with continuous 
band-like distribution are gathered at the grain boundaries. With the 
continuous evolution of the waveform, the morphology of the Laves 
phase gradually changed from a thick and long shape to a short strip, 
and the size and density of the Laves phase also gradually decreased. 
During the FFP-TIG welding process, the stirring effect of FFP wave -
forms has greatly improved the instantaneous cooling rate of the 
weldment. The Nb segregation will follow a downward trend with an 
enhanced cooling rate, thus inhibiting the precipitation of Laves phase 
[41] .

weldments under different welding waveforms. When the composite

increase of the I

and spacing of Laves phase in the weldment obtained from the FFDP

moted crack propagation.

4. Conclusions 
This paper developed a novel and high-efficiency fast-frequency 
pulsed tungsten inert gas (FFP-TIG) welding with a fast frequency 
double pulsed (FFDP) waveform. The weld morphology, microstructure, 
and mechanical properties of superalloy In-718 weldments in FFP-TIG 
welding were studied comprehensively and systematically. Some 
outstanding conclusions are as follows: 
(1) FFP-TIG welding improves the weld formation and produces a 
stirring effect in the molten pool, destroys the growing dendrites, 
effectively inhibits the aggregation of niobium atoms, refines the 
grains and the Laves phase, and increases the high-angle grain 
boundary and recrystallization ratio. 
(2) Compared with the FFSP waveform, the FFDP waveform has a 
better effect on grain refinement. The weldment grain size and 
Laves phase size obtained by the FFDP waveform are reduced by 
about 8.5 % and 34.5 %, respectively. 
(3) The tensile strength of the In-718 weldment obtained by FFP-TIG 
welding at 700 
◦

form could improve the tensile strength of the weldment close to 
that of the base metal (90.9 % of the base metal at 700
