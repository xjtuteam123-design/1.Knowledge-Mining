High-Temperature Tensile and Compressive Behavior
of Peak-Aged Sand-Cast Mg –10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr Alloy**
By Xiangjun Chen, Wencai Liu,* Guohua Wu, Yanlei Li, Zhongquan Li, Song Zhang
and Wenjiang Ding
The uniaxial tensile and compressive behavior of the peak-aged sand-cast Mg –10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr alloy
from 25 to 250 /C14 C is investigated. The results show that the alloy exhibits excellent high-temperature
tensile and compressive properties compared with other magnesium alloys. The tensile yield stress and
ultimate tensile strength remain unchanged, while the compressive yield strength slightly increases
when testing temperature rises from 25 to 150
/C14 C. This can be attribute to the metastable b0(cbco) phase
and the ﬁne ductility of the alloy at high temperature. The alloy shows tension-compression yield
asymmetry which could be associated to the pyramidal <cþa> slip and extension twinning. In
addition, the fracture mechanisms of the tested alloy at different temperatures are studied
systematically.
1. Introduction
Magnesium alloys have the potential to be applied widely
in the ﬁeld of aviation and aerospace as light-weight
structural materials for its low density , ﬁne castability , and
high speci ﬁc stiffness. [1] However, the application environ-
ment of the aerospace structural components is so harsh that
conventional magnesium alloys are limited to the working
temperature lower than 120
/C14 C because of its poor high
temperature mechanical properties. [2]
To solve this problem, researchers have found that the
mechanical properties and creep resistance of the magnesium
alloy can be improved by addition of the rare-earth elements
(RE). Among those Mg –RE alloys, the Mg –Gd–Y-based
magnesium alloy system has excellent high-temperature
mechanical properties and creep resistance due to solid
solution hardening and preipitation hardening of the RE-rich
phases.[3,4] Anyanwu et al. and Rokhlin and Nikitina have
developed high –performance heat-resistant Mg –Gd–Y–Zr
alloy which has now been applied widely in the ﬁeld of
aerospace as complex structural material. [5,6] Many studies
were conducted with regard to the microstructure, precipita-
tion sequences, mechanical properties, and forming process of
the extruded Mg –Gd–Y–Zr alloy system.
[7–10] Given the
results in the literatures, [11] the Mg –10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr alloy
exhibits higher strength and better ductility compared with
the other Mg–Gd–Y–Zr alloy . As a matter of fact, sand casting
is a more practicable way in producing complex-shaped
aerospace structural components, and those components
endure complex load and high temperature. However, the
static high-temperature tensile and compressive properties of
peak-aged sand-cast Mg –10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr alloy are rarely
investigated to date.
To have a more comprehensive understanding of the high-
temperature mechanical properties of the alloy for the
succeeded research and application, in the ent work, the
high-temperature tensile and compressive behavior (from
room temperature to 250
/C14 C) of the peak-aged sand-cast Mg –
10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr alloy was investigated.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure
Figure 1 shows the microstructure of the sand-cast Mg –
10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr alloy before and after peak aging treatment. As
can be seen from Figure 1a, the sand-cast alloy consists of
equiaxed a-Mg matrix and networks of intermetallic com-
pounds segregated along the grain boundaries. The stoichi-
ometry of the (Gd, Y)-rich eutectic phase is near Mg
24(Gd, Y)5,
as demonstrated by XRD in Figure 2. After the peak aging heat
treatment, the reticular eutectic phases solves into the a-Mg
matrix leaving only equiaxed a-Mg matrix and small spots
of cuboid-shaped phases scattered near grain boundaries
(Figure 1b). It has been reported that the cuboid-shaped phase
which forms during the solid solution is an fcc (Gd, Y)-rich
sosoloid whose composition is determined to be Mg
5(Gd,
Y).[13] From the SEM images and the corresponding EDS
analysis result in Figure 3, Zr-cores (point B) can be found in
most grains which act as grain re ﬁner in the solidi ﬁcation of
Mg–Gd–Y alloys.
[14] The average grain size of the peak-aged
sand-cast alloy is about 76mm measured by linear interception
method.
3.2. High-T emperature Mechanical Properties
Figure 4a and Table 1 show the temperature dependence of
tensile yield strength (TYS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS),
and elongation (EL). As can be seen in Table 1, when the
temperature rises, the elongation of the alloy increases sharply
from 2.5% at room temperature to 5.2% at 150
/C14 C, and 9.6% at
250 /C14 C, which reveals the ﬁne ductility of the alloy at high
temperatures.
Compared the temperature dependence of the TYS and
UTS of commercial magnesium alloy , namely sand cast WE43
and EZ10,
[15,16] whose mechanical properties decreases
monotonously with the increase of the temperature, the
tested Mg –10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr alloy shows more excellent high-
temperature tensile properties. The UTS increases slightly
with testing temperature from 341 MPa at 25 /C14 C to 350 MPa at
150 /C14 C and then decreases sharply to 307 MPa when the
temperature rises further to 250 /C14 C. Comparatively , the TYS
can be regarded as unchanged from 25 to 150 /C14 C and then
steadily decreases from 232 MPa at 150 /C14 C to 215 MPa at
250 /C14 C. Test results show that the tensile strength of the alloy
reaches the peak value at 150 /C14 C.
Figure 4b and Table 2 show the temperature dependence of
compressive yield stress (CYS) and ultimate compressive
strength (UCS). As seen from Table 2, with the increasing of
the testing temperature, the CYS of the tested alloy ﬁrst
increases slightly from 60 MPa at 25
/C14 C to 276 MPa at 150 /C14 C
with an improvement of 6.2%, and then decreases drastically
from 150 to 250 /C14 C. However, the ultimate compressive

4. Discussion
4.1. T emperature Dependence of Mechanical Properties
To investigate the temperature dependence of UTS, the
tensile stress –strain curves at different temperatures are
compared in Figure 8a. The curves can be divided into
three stages: at ﬁrst, there is an elastic stage (stage I) and it
ends when yield happens. Then, the work hardening stage
follows which can be divided into a work hardening rate
decrease stage (stage II) and a stage with ﬂat and low work
hardening rate (stage III). A rapid decrease of the work
hardening rate can be observed during stage II due to the
initiation and propagation of micro-cracks in the speci-
mens.
[21] For the specimen tested at 25 /C14 C, it has a short
stage II and almost no stage III. This implies that the
fracture occurs shortly after the crack initiation since the
stress concentration near the crack tips cannot be easily
relieved at room temperature due to the lack of deforma-
tion mechanisms. With the increase of tensile temperature
to 150
/C14 C, the promotion of tensile strength can be attribute

4.2. T ension-Compression Yield Asymmetry
Compare the stress –strain curves obtained
under tension and compression at 25 and 150
/C14 C,
the CYS (260 MPa) is 13% higher than the
TYS (231 MPa) at 25 /C14 C, and the asymmetry ratio
(CYS/TYS) is 1.13. At 200 /C14 C, the CYS is 248 MPa,
and the TYS is 225 MPa, which leads to the
asymmetry ratio of 1.10. This demonstrates that
the studied alloy shows a yield asymmetry
between compression and tension.
Although tension-compression yield asymme-
try was frequently observed in strong-textured
wrought magnesium alloy , this phenomenon has
also been reported in other weakly textured Mg –
RE alloys.
[23–26] Earlier works also found that the
yield asymmetry of the weakly textured magne-
sium alloys at high temperature may be related to
the different mobility behavior of <cþa> dis-
locations under tension and compression. [22,27,28]
As explained by Hidalgo-Manrique, [22] dynamic
strain aging (DSA) is related to the yield
asymmetry , and the RE elements will enhance
the phenomena even at higher temperatures.
Since <cþa> dislocations are less mobile in
compression, and more susceptible to be arrested at particles,
it results in further strengthening during compression and
improve the CYS. As the testing temperature further
increases, due to the reduction in the DSA, the difference
between TYS and CYS will decreases. Moreover, due to the
activation of non-basal slip in the alloy at high temperature,
the suppression of twin generation will also reduce the
mechanical anisotropy .
Recently, Kamrani and Fleck studied the effects of
calcium and rare-earth elements on the microstructure,
texture, and tension –compression yield asymmetry of the
ZEK100-based alloys.
[29] The results show that the presence
of the Mg –RE precipitates suppresses the twin generation
and reduces the rate of twinning, there for it increase the
CYS which results in the increase of asymmetry ratio from
0.62 for pure Magnesium to 0.75 for ZEK100. Since the

5. Conclusions
In present paper, the high-temperature tensile and
compressive behavior of the peak-aged sand-cast
Mg–10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr magnesium alloy was investi-
gated. The following conclusions can be drawn:
1) The tensile and compressive yield stress of the peak-
aged sand-cast Mg–10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr alloy improved
slightly with the increase of the testing temperature
from 25 to 150
/C14 C and then decrease with further
increasing temperature from 150 to 250 /C14 C. How-
ever, the ultimate compressive strength decreases
monotonously with the inc reasing of the testing
temperature.
2) The peak-aged sand-cast Mg –10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr
alloy shows a tension-compression yield asym-
metry within a temperature range from 25 to
250
/C14 C. The yield asymmetry can be attribute
to the difference in 10 /C2212g
/C8
twinning charac-
teristics during tension and compression and
the interaction of <cþa> dislocations with
precipitates.
3) The fracture mode of the peak-aged sand-cast
Mg–10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr alloy is cleavage fracture
at room temperature featured by cleavage steps
and remnant grain boundaries. With the eleva-
tion of tensile temperature, the grain boundaries
are weakened and the fracture mode changes
to quasi-cleavage fracture featured by grain
boundaries and tearing ridges, which reveals
the great improvement of ductility of the alloy
at high temperature. The compressive speci-
