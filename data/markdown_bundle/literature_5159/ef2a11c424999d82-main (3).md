ABSTRACT
Tantalum-tungsten (Ta-W) alloys possess exceptional mechanical properties, especially at high temperatures, 
rendering them attractive for advanced structural applications. Conventional preparation routes present inherent 
limitations for Ta-W alloys, while research on the additive manufacturing of these alloys with varying compo -
sitions remains inadequate. In this work, selective laser melting was used to fabricate a series of Ta-W alloys with 
different tungsten contents (specifically, pure Ta, Ta-2.5W, Ta-5W, and Ta-10W). The impact of tungsten content 
on their microstructure, mechanical properties, and deformation behavior was systematically investigated. In all 
studied alloys, W atoms were dissolved into the Ta lattice, forming a single body-centered cubic solid solution. As 
the tungsten content increased from 0 to 10 wt%, the relative density of the alloys experienced a noticeable 
decline but remained consistently above 98%. This change was accompanied by a narrowing of melt pool, 
continuous grain refinement, and a decrease in the intensity of the < 111 > fiber texture. Mechanical tests 
indicated that both hardness and tensile strength increased monotonically with tungsten content, at the expense 
of reduced ductility. Analysis of deformed microstructures manifested more severe strain localization in Ta-W 
alloys with higher tungsten contents, consequently causing earlier necking initiation. Overall, the Ta-10W 
alloy exhibited the optimal balance of mechanical properties, achieving an ultimate tensile strength of 762 
MPa while maintaining an elongation of 32.2%, thereby confirming its superior mechanical performance.
1. Introduction
Refractory metals and their alloys, particularly those based on 
tungsten, tantalum, molybdenum, and niobium, exhibit melting tem -
peratures above 2000 
◦
C, establishing them as essential structural ma -
terials for advanced aerospace and nuclear applications [ 1 ]. Among 
these materials, the tantalum-tungsten (Ta-W) alloy is increasingly 
recognized for its superior high-temperature strength, and exceptional 
corrosion resistance [ 2 , 3 ]. Significant research has been devoted to the 
mechanical behavior, deformation mechanisms, and microstructural 
evolution of various Ta-W alloys [ 4 – 8 ]. Notably, their outstanding 
performance under ultra-high temperatures has attracted extensive 
attention [ 9 , 10 ]. For instance, Ma et al. [ 11 ] demonstrated that Ta-4W 
(wt.%, the same as following) retains continuous work-hardening 
capability up to 2000 
◦
C. Separately, Fan et al. [ 12 ] produced Ta-5W 
by selective laser melting (SLM) and reported a tensile strength of 75 
MPa at this extreme temperature. These outstanding high-temperature 
mechanical properties establish Ta-W alloys as leading candidate ma -
terials for next-generation ultra-high-temperature components.
Conventional methods for fabricating Ta-W alloys, including powder 
metallurgy and vacuum melting, face considerable limitations that 
restrict their broader application [ 13 – 17 ]. In contrast, SLM utilizes a 
layer-by-layer deposition strategy, enabling the production of intricate 
geometries with markedly superior material efficiency [ 18 , 19 ]. The 
rapid solidification inherent to SLM also suppresses elemental segrega -
tion, facilitating a refined and homogeneous microstructure [ 20 , 21 ]. 
Growing research on the additive manufacturing of Ta-W alloys in 
recent years highlights the potential of SLM to address these long -
standing industrial constraints [ 12 , 17 ].
In conventionally processed Ta-W alloys, the strengthening effect of 
tungsten is generally considered relatively predictable, with increasing 
tungsten content leading to enhanced strength but reduced ductility

This unresolved literature divergence highlights a fundamental 
question: how does tungsten concentration intrinsically affect the 
microstructure and mechanical behavior of SLM-fabricated Ta-W alloys 
when processing-induced variations are strictly controlled? To answer 
this, a targeted experimental strategy was implemented. Pure Ta and Ta- 
W alloys with different tungsten contents were fabricated using strictly 
consistent SLM parameters and subjected to identical heat treatments. 
Evaluating these materials under the exact same post-annealed condi -
tions effectively decouples the intrinsic compositional effects from 
processing-induced variations. Through this controlled systematic 
approach, the present work aims to clarify how tungsten additions 
govern microstructural evolution and provide fundamental insights into 
the strength-ductility trade-off in additively manufactured Ta-W alloys.
2. Experiments
2.1. Material preparation methods
The starting powders of pure Ta, Ta-2.5W, Ta-5W, and Ta-10W were 
obtained from Stardust Technology (Guangdong) Co., Ltd. and pro -
cessed by radio-frequency plasma spheroidization (RFPS). These pow -
ders exhibited high sphericity and a consistent particle size distribution 
of 15 – 45 μ m. As shown in Fig. 1 (b – d), cross-sectional scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) 
characterization of the Ta-10W powder confirmed a uniform distribu -
tion of both tantalum and tungsten. SEM images of the remaining 
powders are provided in Fig. S1 , with the chemical compositions of all 
powders verified by ICP-AES. Additionally, the contents of key impurity 
elements (oxygen, hydrogen, and nitrogen) for all raw powders and the 
selected as-printed sample were provided in Table S1 .
All samples were fabricated using an SLM system (MT170H, Beijing 
Avimetalam Co., Ltd.) equipped with a 1064 nm wavelength laser in an 
argon atmosphere with oxygen content below 10 ppm. The optimal 
process window for Ta-W alloys was determined using an orthogonal 
design ( Fig. S2 ). A unified parameter set, derived from the overlapping 
optimal windows of all compositions, was applied to ensure consistent 
fabrication conditions across all samples. The selected SLM parameters 
were as follows: laser power of 200 W, scanning speed of 600 mm/s, 
layer thickness of 30 μ m, and hatch spacing of 50 μ m. The fabrication 
process involved a 67
◦
interlayer rotation strategy, as schematically 
illustrated in Fig. S3(a) . The geometry and dimensions of the tensile 
specimens, which were machined from the as-printed bulk material, are 
depicted in Fig. S3(b) and Fig. S3(c) , where the tensile axis was also 
indicated to be parallel to the scanning direction.

Regarding grain morphology on the XOY plane, the overlapping regions 
between adjacent melt pools (as indicated by the red dashed boxes in 
Fig. 5 (c) and (e)) became increasingly evident with rising tungsten 
content. This phenomenon occurred because the overlapping regions 
experienced extremely high cooling rates resulting from the enhanced 
thermal conductivity of the material, leading to significant constitu -
tional supercooling. Such conditions promoted the heterogeneous 
nucleation and growth of fine equiaxed grains [ 29 ], which clearly 
delineated the individual scan tracks on the XOY plane [ 30 ]. The for -
mation of these equiaxed grains inevitably introduced a large number of 
high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs) between adjacent grains. Conse -
quently, the fraction of randomly oriented equiaxed grains grew 
significantly, thereby increasing the overall proportion of HAGBs, which 
agreed with the statistical results presented in Fig. S4 . On the YOZ plane, 
the grains exhibited arc-shaped columnar morphologies that closely 
followed the melt pool contours, a result of epitaxial growth constrained 
by the melt pool geometry. As the tungsten content rose, the enhanced 
solidification rate suppressed the lateral growth of grains at the solid -
– liquid interface, while the reduced melt fluidity led to narrower melt 
pools. The synergistic effect of these two factors enhanced the geomet -
rical constraint, ultimately making the melt pool boundaries more 
distinct in the resulting microstructure.
Furthermore, a consistent reduction in grain size was observed on 
both XOY and YOZ planes with elevated tungsten content, as shown in 
Fig. 5 (i). This trend was attributed to two underlying mechanisms. On 
one hand, the addition of tungsten notably enhanced the thermal con -
ductivity and cooling rate of the alloy, thereby increasing nucleation 
rate and effectively limiting grain growth. On the other hand, the solid 
solution of W atoms in the Ta matrix created a pronounced solute drag 
effect, substantially hindering grain boundary migration during solidi -
fication and further suppressing grain coarsening [ 31 , 32 ].
Fig. 5 (j – m) showed the pole figures of the YOZ planes of the samples, 
where a strong < 111 > || BD fiber texture was observed in all specimens. 
This observation contrasted clearly with the commonly reported < 100 >
texture typically formed in cubic alloys processed by SLM [ 12 , 33 – 35 ]. 
Under the extremely high temperature gradients and solidification rates, 
the crystal growth behavior of the Ta-W alloys deviated from the con -
ventional < 100 > preferential growth mode governed by the maximum 
heat flow direction [ 36 , 37 ]. As a body-centered cubic (BCC) metal, 
tantalum tended to grow preferentially along the < 111 > direction, 
which possessed the highest atomic attachment efficiency, ultimately 
leading to the formation of the < 111 > fiber texture observed in this 
study. Additionally, the intensity of this texture was found to decrease 
with the increase in tungsten content. This weakening originated from 
the combined influence of higher solidification rates and the solute drag 
effect, which collectively promoted grain refinement and the formation 
of fine, randomly oriented equiaxed grains along melt pool boundaries. 
These microstructural changes progressively disrupted the continuity of 
epitaxial growth, resulting in a notable reduction in the < 111 > texture 
intensity.

the Consid `ere criterion, necking occurs when the true stress – strain curve

which promoted dislocation multiplication and interactions. As the 
tungsten content increased from 2.5 wt% to 10 wt%, although the 
progressively strengthened dislocation – solute interactions continued to 
contribute to strain hardening, the dislocation storage capability 
approached saturation, resulting in a markedly reduced increment in the 
strain hardening rate per unit addition of tungsten content.
Quantitative assessment of the strength – ductility synergy indicated 
that when the tungsten content reached 10 wt%, the alloy maintained 
sufficient work hardening capability and plastic reserve while achieving 
high strength. Extrapolation of the mechanical property trends in Fig. 6
(d) suggested that subsequent tungsten additions beyond 10 wt% would 
produce only marginal gains in strength, demonstrating that the Ta-10W 
composition attained an optimal strength – ductility balance in this alloy 
system, culminating in superior comprehensive mechanical 
performance.
For comparison, the Ta-10W alloy prepared via conventional pro -
cessing routes involving melting followed by rolling and annealing 
exhibited a UTS of approximately 600 MPa with a fracture strain of 
about 31% [ 22 ]. In contrast, the Ta-10W alloy fabricated through spark 
plasma sintering after prolonged mechanical alloying achieved a higher 
UTS of 816 MPa, though the strain at break was reduced to 10.26% [ 39 ]. 
In this study, the SLM-fabricated Ta-10W alloy demonstrated a UTS of 
762 MPa while maintaining a high total elongation of 32.2%. These 
results indicated that the SLM-fabricated Ta-10W alloy achieved a more 
advantageous combination of strength level and tensile ductility relative 
to the Ta-10W counterparts produced by non-additive manufacturing 
routes.
3.3. Fracture mechanism analysis
Fractographic examination of the samples in Fig. 7 confirmed that all 
specimens exhibited typical ductile fracture characteristics, featuring 
numerous dimples on the fracture surfaces. The dimple morphology 
evolved systematically with higher tungsten content. Pure Ta displayed 
large, elongated dimples indicative of excellent plastic deformability. In 
contrast, the Ta-W alloys transitioned to equiaxed dimple morphologies. 
Specifically, Ta-2.5W showed homogeneous and densely distributed 
equiaxed dimples; Ta-5W exhibited pronounced dimple coalescence, 
reflecting a fracture mechanism dominated by microvoid coalescence; 
and Ta-10W maintained the equiaxed dimple pattern but developed 
localized imperfections, including the unmelted particle and 
microcracks.
The evolution of fracture morphology clearly elucidated the under -
lying mechanisms governing ductility as a function of tungsten content. 
The morphological progression from elongated dimples in pure Ta to 
equiaxed dimples in Ta-W alloys demonstrated that the solid solution of 
W atoms significantly modified the plastic deformation mode. The 
pinning effect of W atoms on dislocations restricted long-range dislo -
cation motion, shifting the deformation behavior from concentrated 
shear to homogeneous multi-site void nucleation. As the tungsten con -
tent increased to 10 wt%, defects such as the unmelted particle and 
microcracks induced local stress concentrations, accelerating crack 
initiation and propagation, which consequently promoted earlier 
necking and reduced ductility.
3.4. Deformation mechanisms
As shown in Fig. 8 , EBSD characterization was performed on regions 
with comparable tensile deformation among all specimens. With higher 
tungsten content, the degree of plastic strain localization became 
markedly accentuated. IPF maps revealed that the elongation traces of 
grains became increasingly distinct as the tungsten content rose, indi -
cating a growing heterogeneity in the distribution of plastic strain at the 
grain scale. In the kernel average misorientation (KAM) maps, the color 
distribution transitioned from a relatively uniform state in pure Ta to 
patch-like high-value regions in the Ta-W alloys, and the area fraction of 
these regions expanded progressively with elevated tungsten contents. 
Similarly, the grain reference orientation deviation (GROD) maps 
manifested an enhanced contrast with augmented tungsten addition. In 
the Ta-10W alloy, a large number of hot-colored regions were observed, 
signifying a significant intensification of local lattice rotation and strain 
accumulation.
The quantitative analysis presented in Fig. 9 corroborated the pre -
ceding observations. Both the standard deviations of KAM and GROD 
values increased systematically with enhanced tungsten content, while 
their mean values demonstrated a general upward trend, except for the 
minor deviation in Ta-2.5W. These findings collectively verified the 
transition from relatively homogeneous to highly localized strain dis -
tribution. The elevation in mean values reflected an overall amplifica -
tion of plastic strain, whereas the growth in standard deviation revealed 
an aggravated heterogeneity in strain distribution. The solid-solution W 
atoms exerted a pinning effect on dislocation motion, simultaneously 
amplifying the overall dislocation accumulation density and promoting 
spatial inhomogeneity in dislocation arrangement. This triggered sub -
stantial strain aggregation within intragranular slip bands and acute

The formation of dislocation cells during plastic deformation were 
governed by the ability of dislocations to undergo cross-slip and sub -
sequent rearrangement into low-energy configurations. Previous studies 
on Ta-W alloys reported that increasing tungsten content suppressed 
dislocation cross-slip and refined dislocation cell structures [ 40 , 41 ]. In 
the present alloys, the addition of W strengthened solute – dislocation 
interactions and increased lattice friction, which raised the resistance to 
dislocation motion and reduced dislocation mobility. The reduced 
mobility limited cross-slip and subsequent rearrangement, thereby 
restricting the growth of dislocation cells and leading to the progressive 
refinement of the cellular structure with increasing tungsten content.
When the tungsten content reached 10 wt%, dislocation cross-slip 
and rearrangement were strongly inhibited, preventing the formation 
of stable dislocation cells. As a result, dislocations accumulated rapidly 
in the form of highly localized tangles. The dominance of dense dislo -
cation tangles indicated that dislocations were unable to reorganize 
effectively into low-energy configurations under increased solute resis -
tance. This transition from organized cellular structures to dense dislo -
cation tangles diminished the capacity for homogeneous plastic 
deformation [ 42 ], thereby promoting early strain localization and 
contributing to the observed reduction in ductility.
4. Conclusions
In this work, pure Ta and Ta-W alloys with varying tungsten contents 
were fabricated by SLM and subsequently vacuum-annealed. The 
microstructure and mechanical properties were then systematically 
investigated. The following conclusions were drawn: 
1. W atoms were dissolved within the Ta lattice in all tested Ta-W al -
loys. With increasing tungsten content from 0 to 10 wt%, the relative 
density decreased noticeably and the melt pools narrowed progres -
sively. Furthermore, the grain size underwent continuous refinement 
with higher tungsten content, and the < 111 > fiber texture along the 
build direction was markedly weakened.
2. The strength and hardness of the alloys mounted monotonically with 
rising tungsten content, accompanied by a corresponding reduction 
in ductility. Among all alloys, the Ta-10W alloy demonstrated the 
most superior combination of mechanical properties, achieving a 
UTS of 762 MPa while maintaining a total elongation of approxi -
mately 32.2%.
3. During plastic deformation, the dissolved W atoms effectively pinned 
dislocations and suppressed their long-range motion, promoting a 
transition in the deformation mechanism from localized shear to 
homogeneous void nucleation at multiple sites. In addition, elevated 
tungsten content led to a substantial intensification of strain locali -
zation, coupled with a sustained refinement of dislocation cell 
structures following deformation. When the tungsten content 
reached 10 wt%, only high-density dislocation tangles were 
observed, without distinct dislocation cell structures forming.
