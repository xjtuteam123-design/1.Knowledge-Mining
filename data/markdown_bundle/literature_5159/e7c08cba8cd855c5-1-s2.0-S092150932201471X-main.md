ABSTRACT 
In this paper, the effect of N addition on microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb-16Si-20Ti alloy was 
investigated. The nominal compositions of alloys are Nb-16Si-20Ti- x N ( x = 0, 1, 2, 4, 8 at%). The research results 
show that the addition of N element will make the solidification path transfers from hypoeutectic to hypereu -
tectic. TiN compound appears in all the N-contained alloys and grows up with the increase of concentration of N 
element. The transformation of silicide is verified by the Mediema and Toop thermodynamic model. The room- 
temperature fracture toughness of the alloys is ameliorated by addition of N element. Performance of 4 N alloy is 
the best, which is 50% higher than that of Nb-16Si-20Ti original alloy. Compressive strength increases 18.8% due 
to the grain refinement strengthening and solid solution strengthening caused by N element. 
1. Introduction 
The speed of aviation vehicles is getting faster and faster, and the 
performance requirement of material is also increasing [ 1 , 2 ]. Especially 
the high-temperature structural materials that make up the engine. 
Nowadays, the operating temperature of widely used Ni based alloys has 
reached 85% of its melting point [ 3 ]. Therefore, the development of a 
new generation superalloy is imminent. Nb-Si alloys has a higher 
melting point ( > 1750

applied in industry extensively [ 8 ]. Applying coating on the surface of 
alloy is an effective method to improve the performance stability in high

4.1. Microstructure and constitution 
Primary phase of Nb-16Si-20Ti is Nbss phase, and the solidification 
process is shown in Fig. 9 . When solidification begins, Nbss phase 
precipitated in liquid phase primarily. When temperature reaches the 
eutectic reaction temperature, the Nbss-Nb
3
Si eutectic structure gener -

According to Ti-N and Nb-Si binary phase diagram, melting point of TiN 
(2950

liquid phase primarily as primary phase at the initial stage of solidifi -
cation, as shown in Fig. 9 . Table 3 indicates that in the N-contained 
alloys, N element mainly exists in the form of TiN. The generation of TiN 
consumes almost all of the N element in liquid phase. However, Nb 
element is still in the remaining liquid phase. With the increase of N 
element, Nb in alloy decreases accordingly, as shown in Table 1 . 
Therefore, the more N element in alloy, the more TiN is generated. After 
TiN formed, Nb element in remaining liquid decreases with the increase 
of N element. The reduction of Nb element shifts the solidification path 
of alloy to hypereutectic. In addition, due to the high melting point, TiN 
precipitates earlier, which also provides nucleation particles for subse -
quent solidification and accelerates the solidification rate. 
The shift of solidification path also explains the changes of phases 
morphology. The secondary Nbss phase of 1 N alloy exhibits distinct 
dendritic features, as shown in Fig. 3 (c) and (d). The secondary Nbss 
phase in 2 N alloy shows round shape, the rest of microstructure is fine 
Nbss-Nb
3
Si eutectic structure. This is because solidification path shifts 
from hypoeutectic to eutectic and liquidus temperature decreases, 
which reduces the temperature range for secondary Nbss phase growth.

4.2. Room-temperature fracture toughness 
As shown in Fig. 7 , deflections and bridging appear on the propa -
gation path of cracks. Previous researches have proved that these 
bridging and deflections caused by the existence of Nbss phase and they 
are beneficial to fracture toughness [ 27 ]. Nbss phase has excellent 
plasticity and toughness. When encounters Nbss phase, cracks will 
disappear due to the good ductility of Nbss phase. The propagation of 
stress in Nbss phase consumes more energy. Then the remaining stress 
continue to propagate along the interface between phases nearly form -
ing bridging (as shown in Fig. 6 ), or causing silicide to fracture. It looks 
like cracks always trying to “ by pass ” Nbss phase to proceed. Therefore, 
the more Nbss phase in microstructure, the better for fracture toughness. 
Schematic diagram of crack propagation mechanism is shown in Fig. 11 . 
In addition, deflections could also be observed on the silicide as shown 
in Fig. 6 . Ma [ 28 ] pointed out that the deflection caused by silicide phase 
could also hinder crack propagation which is beneficial to fracture 
toughness. However, toughness of silicide phase is poor, the benefit 
brought by existence of silicides is limited. The morphology, particles 
size and distribution of phases both affect the fracture toughness 
properties. 
Sec.3.1 describes that the composition of phases and microstructure 
of Nb-16Si-20Ti and 1 N alloy are basically same, but a fine TiN phase 
precipitates in 1 N alloy. The present of TiN phase is harmful to fracture 
toughness, and the volume fraction of Nbss phase in 1 N is less than that 
of Nb-16Si-20Ti. The resistance to crack propagation is weakened, 
leading value of K
Q 
decreases. It has been pointed that larger volume 
fraction, larger particles size, more continuous distribution morphology 
will also enhance the buff of Nbss phase on room-temperature fracture 
toughness [ 27 , 29 ]. The fine eutectic structure can increase the de -
flections in the path of crack propagation. The continuous Nbss phase 
makes cracks unable to propagate along the boundaries between phases.
