ABSTRACT 
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
alloy was widely studied as a typical balanced strength-ductility high-entropy alloy (HEA) and 
was declared a promising superalloy. Here, for the first time, a small amount of Mo, Nb, Hf, C elements were 
added to produce multiple carbides to improve further the properties of the alloy. And the nano-scale phase 
formed during annealing was used to strengthen the alloy. Compared with AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
alloy, the tensile 
strength of the new AlCoCrFeNi
1.9
(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) HEA at 973 K increased from 538 to 771 MPa without 
decreasing in ductility. During annealing, a novel nano-scale long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase with 
(Ni, Al)-rich formed. And it increased the yield and tensile strengthes of the alloy at room temperature from 498 
to 1008 MPa and from 858 to 1242 MPa without decreasing in ductility. The tensile properties of the alloy at 973 
K were not significantly improved by the LPSO phase. 
1. Introduction 
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
high-entropy alloy (HEA) with FCC/B2 eutectic 
microstructure was declared as a promising superalloy due to its good 
high temperature property and unprecedented combination of high 
tensile ductility and high fracture strength [1 – 3] . NbC was reported to 
increase significantly the high temperature property in some HEAs 
[4 – 6] . And the multiple or even high entropy carbides have better sta -
bility and strengthening than single carbides [7,8] . At the same time, it 
has been widely reported recently that phase separation usually 
occurred and nano-scale phases formed in most HEAs during annealing 
at intermediate temperature [9,10] . Most of the nano phases can 
improve the room temperature properties of the alloy, but their effect on 
the high temperature properties of the HEAs is unclear. In this paper, 
AlCoCrFeNi
1.9
(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) alloy was developed and multiple carbides 
were obtained by adding Mo, Nb, Hf, C elements to the AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
alloy. At the same time, the effect of annealing on the stability and the 
tensile property of the AlCoCrFeNi
1.9
(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) alloy was 
investigated.

3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 1 shows the as-cast morphology and XRD results of the 
AlCoCrFeNi
1.9
(Mo, Nb, Hf, C) alloy. Fig. 1 (a) indicates that the alloy has 
excellent casting properties. Combined with TEM selected-area electron 
diffraction (SAED) patterns of the as-cast structure in Fig. 2 (a) and the 
EDS results in the Table 1 , one can see the microstructure of the alloy 
met the design expectation. The dendrite was L12 type FCC single solid 
solution rich in Co, Cr, Fe and Ni. The interdendrite consisted of (Ni, Al)- 
rich and (Cr, Mo)-rich BCC phases. The participation in the boundaries 
and dendrite was multiple carbide (Nb, M)C (M = Mo, Hf, Cr), and it was 
almost a high entropy carbide due to the contents of Cr, Mo, Nb, Hf and 
C in the carbide were all relatively high. Figs. 2 (b) and 3 show TEM and 
HRTEM images and SAED patterns of the alloy annealed at 973 K for 2 h. 
One can see that a large number of nano-scale phase was formed in the 
dendrite. From the SAED patterns in Fig. 3 , one can see that streaks 
appeared in the SAED patterns, hinting that stacking faults (SFs) existed 
in the phase. And there were quarter points between the bright spots, 
indicating the phase was a long-period stacking ordered phase (LPSO). 
From Fig. 3 (b), SFs can be seen directly in the lattice fringes obtained by 
the FFT. In combination with Table 1 , one can see that this LPSO phase 
was rich in Ni and Al. LPSO phase was often found in Mg alloys with Gd 
or Y and Zn elements and was considered to be a good strengthening 
phase [11,12] . It can greatly increase the yield strength of the Mg alloy 
without reducing the ductility, and was beneficial to the high temper -
ature performance. Only one kind of LPSO phase with (Ni, Ti)-rich has 
been found in the HEAs recently [13] , suggesting that this phase can 
greatly improve the strength of the alloy without reducing the ductility. 
The (Ni, Al)-rich LPSO phase in this paper was different from others in 
component. And it was a novel LPSO phase. 
Fig. 4 shows the tensile property of the alloy at room and high 
temperature (973 K). From Fig. 4 (a), one can see the strength of the alloy 
increased significantly without decreasing in ductility after annealed at 
973 K. And its yield and tensile strengthes at room temperature 
increased from 498 to 1008 MPa and from 858 to 1242 MPa respectively 
after annealed at 973 K for 48 h. From Fig. 4 (b), one can see the high 
temperature performance of the annealed alloy remained virtually un -
changed compared to the cast alloy. Therefore, the LPSO phase can 
significantly improve the tensile property of the alloy at room temper -
ature, while no obvious improvement was found on the property at 973 
K. Compared the high temperature (973 K) strength of the as-cast alloy 
in this work and the AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
alloy [1] , as shown in the Fig. 4 (b), 
one can see that the tensile strength of the alloy tested at 973 K was 
higher, up to 771 MPa, which reached 90% of that at room temperature 
(858 MPa). Moreover, its elongation was up to 24.5%, which was close 
to AlCoCrFeNi
2.1
, 22%.

4. Conclusion 
(Nb, M)C (M = Mo, Hf, Cr) formed in a new AlCoCrFeNi
1.9
(Mo, Nb, 
Hf, C) HEA by adding Mo, Nb, Hf, C elements to the AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
alloy. 
And it effectively improved the high temperature property of the alloy. 
Tensile strength of the alloy at 973 K is 771 MPa, reaching 90% of that at 
room temperature (858 MPa). And its elongation is up to 24.5%. 
A novel (Ni, Al)-rich LPSO phase formed in the dendrite of the alloy 
during annealing. It can increase the yield and tensile strengthes of the 
alloy at room temperature from 498 to 1008 MPa and from 858 to 1242 
MPa without decreasing in ductility after annealed at 973 K for 48 h. At 
973 K, no improment was found for the LPSO on the high temperature 
property of the alloy annealed 973 K for 2 h.
