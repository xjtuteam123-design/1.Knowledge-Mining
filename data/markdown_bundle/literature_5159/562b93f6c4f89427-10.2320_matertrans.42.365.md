This process is done without brazing and is designed to enhance the reliability of joints at high temperatures. The main feature of the process is the removal of surface oxide films by keeping ODS alloys at high temperature (1523 K) in vacuum (?IO��4 Pa) followed by successive solid state diffusion joining between the cleaned surfaces at 1473 K in the same vacuum chamber. Specimens joined by this new process exhibited both high tensile strength and high ductility comparable to the base metal. However, the ductility of the joints decreased slightly when heated at 1523 K for 2, 8, and 20h in a vacuum, but this additional step substantially increased the tensile strength. Introduction Oxide dispersion strengthened alloys (ODS) have already been used in the aerospace industry, and they are expected to provide the high temperature, high strength materials needed for the next generation of 1773 K class gas-turbines.l) The nozzles and buckets of gas-turbines are precision casted, be- cause they have complex internal cooling holes.2'3) ODS ma- terials are strengthened at high temperature by an ultrafine dispersion of oxides such as Y203 and are made by a mechan- ical alloying method.l) The precision casting process is not applicable to nozzles and buckets made with the ODS alloy, because Y203 coarsens and floats in the melt during the cast- ing process. Therefore, it is mandatory to fabricate complex cooling holes in order to apply the ODS alloy to buckets and nozzles. Hence the following two step process has been de- veloped to facilitate the use of the ODS alloy for nozzles and buckets. The first step is the fabrication of elaborate grooves in the surfaces of the alloys, and the second step is joining the grooved surfaces. It then follows that joining is the technical key for the practical application of ODS. So far, liquid phase diffusion joining, and laser, electron beam, and arc welding have all been examined as possible so- lutions.4'5) The main problem associated with these methods is, however, the deterioration of the high temperature strength of the ODS alloys, because very fine Y203 particles coarsen in the molten region. If the molten region can be substantially reduced during joining, then the high temperature strength of the joint can be improved. In order to minimize the molten region, a vac- uum joining method which uses sputtered 40 gm thick low temperature melting alloy films containing B has been de- veloped.6) However, this causes the Y203 distribution in the joints to become inhomogeneous and agglomerate. Further- more, this causes a low melting point and brittle compounds such as borides to be formed. Joining of Oxide Dispersion Strengthened Ni Based Super Alloys 369 4. Results and Discussions Figure 6 shows a new joining process which we devel- oped based on the preliminary experiment. There are two key points to this process. First, the surfaces of ODS were cleaned by heating at 1473 K in a vacuum of less than (2��5) x 10��4 Pa followed by joining at the same temperature, in a vacuum, and with a compressive stress of 19.6 MPa. Second, the bonded samples were heated in vacuum at 1523K for 2, 8, 20h in a glass tube sealed at both ends. Figures 7, 8, 9, and 10 show the effects of the holding time at 1523 K on the tensile strength, the yield strength, the elon- gation, and the reduction of the area for both joined and base metal specimens. It is clear that the tensile and yield strengths at 1173 K increase with holding time and the strengths are al- most the same for both joined and base metal specimens. In contrast, the ductilities of the joined specimens are similar to those of the base metal after joining, and become less than those of the base metal after heating for 2, 8, and 20 h. Figures II, 12, and 13 show cross-sectional SEM, TEM, and high-resolution TEM images at the interface of as joined specimens. It can be seen from the SEM image that the Y203 panicles did not coarsen and disperse uniformly at the inter- face after joining. It was found from the TEM image that there is partial re-crystallization at the interface and that the average grain size is less than 1 gm. It was also found from high-resolution TEM that both of the (111) planes, which are the preferred orientations of the Ni based ODS samples, joined perfectly with a tilt angle of about 140. Furthermore, there are very fine Y203 particles in the re-crystallized area near the joining interface showing that coarsening did not occur. Figures 14 and 15 show TEM and high-resolution TEM images of the joining interface orthe specimen heated at 1523 K for 8 h after joining. The TEM image shows that re- crystallization and grain growth have progressed further and that the average grain size at the interface grows to about 1��3 gm. The High-resolution TEM image also shows that the (111) planes, i.e., the preferred orientation of Ni based ODS alloy join perfectly. Furthermore, fine Y203 particles lie in both the matrix and the re-crystallized grain boundalies. Hence the reason why the strength increased with the heating time corresponds to the fact that the strength of the base metal also increased with the heating time.
