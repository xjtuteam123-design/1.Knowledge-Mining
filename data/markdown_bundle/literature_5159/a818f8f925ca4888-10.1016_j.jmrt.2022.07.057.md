abstract
The effects of Ti addition and heat treatment on the microstructure evolution and me-
chanical properties of NiAl-Cr(Mo) hypereutectic alloy were studied. The microstructure of
NiAl-32Cr-6Mo alloy consists of Cr(Mo) dendrites and NiAl þ Cr(Mo) eutectic. Ti addition
would lead to the formation of Ni
2AlTi precipitated phase. With the increasing Ti content,
the number of Ni 2AlTi phase increases. After heat treatment, the phase constituent of the
alloy does not change. The hardness, room-temperature and high-temperature yield
strength of the as-cast NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-1Ti alloy obviously increase due to the precipitation
strengthening and solid solution strengthening, but the increasing primary phase and
irregular microstructure result in the decreasing strength of NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-3Ti alloy. The
yield strength and hardness of the alloy decline obviously after heat treatment while the
ductility increases due to the uniform microstructure and increasing number of primary
phase. All alloys show typical brittle quasi-cleavage fracture at room temperature. The
number of cleavage steps and tearing ridges in the Ti-doped alloys increases signiﬁcantly,
and the fracture surface becomes more rugged. In addition, multiple cleavages are
observed in the NiAl phase, which shows that the NiAl phase also plays a role in
strengthening the alloy.

1. Introduction
The development of the aerospace industry has a high de-
mand for the excellent properties of structural materials.
Currently, the widely used materials for hot end components
of aircraft engines are mainly nickel-based single crystal su-
peralloys. However, the use temperature of the superalloys
has reached about 80% of its absolute melting point, it is very
difﬁcult to further increase its use temperature [ 1e4]. NiAl
alloy has the advantages of high melting point, low density,

shortcomings: brittleness at room temperature and insufﬁ-

To improve the high-temperature strength of NiAl alloy,
some strengthening methods based on solution strengthening
and precipitation strengthening mechanisms were proposed
[9]. Especially, Ti is a typical solution strengthening element in
NiAl alloy [10]. Ni

the high-temperature strength of NiAl alloy [ 11,12]. Compared
with the single-phase NiAl and Ni 2AlTi, the creep strength of
Ni-35Al-5Ti alloy is signiﬁcantly improved, which is even
comparable with that of the nickel-base superalloy MARM-
200. However, greater improvement of fracture toughness is
still necessary [ 12].
To overcome the drawback of low fracture toughness of
NiAl alloy, many studies on NiAl-X eutectic alloys (X ¼ Cr, Mo,
V, et al.) have been reported [ 13e20]. Among them, NiAl-
Cr(Mo) eutectic alloys show an obviously increasing room-
temperature fracture toughness and high-temperature
strength due to the Cr(Mo) strengthening phase [ 18e20]. For
example, Raj et al. succeeded in the superior improvement of
the average fracture toughness values for specimens with
planar eutectic and cellular microstructures of 15e17 MPa$m

in NiAl-Cr(Mo) two-phase alloy [ 18].
Combined with the above studies, Ti element was also
introduced into the NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy to expect a further in-
crease in comprehensive properties. The results have shown
that the addition of Ti element to NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy could
obtain a good balance between high-temperature strength
and room-temperature toughness [ 21e23]. For example, Xiao
et al. prepared NiAl-28Cr-3Mo-4Ti alloy and found that the
microstructure of alloy consisted of primary NiAl phase,
NiAl þ Cr(Mo) eutectic and a small amount of Ni
2AlTi phase.
The results showed that Ti alloying was beneﬁcial to the
improvement of the strength of NiAl-Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy [22].
Nevertheless, Hagihara et al. investigated the effect of Ti on
NiAl-Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy and found that the addition of Ti
hindered the formation of lamellar microstructure, leading to
a signiﬁcant increase in high-temperature strength but a sig-
niﬁcant decrease in room-temperature fracture toughness
[24].
Until now, all researches on the Ti addition were only
focused on the NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy with eutectic composition
[21,22]. Our previous studies have shown that a large volume
fraction of Cr(Mo) strengthening phase could lead to the sig-
niﬁcant improvement of fracture toughness and tensile
strength of NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy [ 17,25]. Nb addition in the NiAl-
Cr(Mo) hypereutectic alloy could also further improve the
room-temperature strength due to the large volume fraction
of the Cr(Mo) strengthening phase [ 26]. In addition, heat
treatment can improve the distribution of precipitated phase
and solute atoms as well as the microstructure morphology,
thus increasing the mechanical properties of the alloy [ 27].
Therefore, Ti element was added in the NiAl-Cr(Mo) hyper-
eutectic alloy in this work. The inﬂuences of Ti addition and
heat treatment on the microstructure evolution, hardness,
compression properties at room temperature and 1000
/C14 C,
fracture behavior as well as strengthening mechanism in
NiAl-Cr(Mo) hypereutectic alloy were investigated.
2. Experimental procedure
Ni, Al, Cr, Mo, Ti particles with high purity were used as raw
materials to prepare alloy ingots in the vacuum non-
consumable arc melting furnace. The mass of each alloy
ingot was about 140 g. The compositions of alloys are NiAl-
32Cr-6Mo-xTi (x ¼ 0, 1 and 3, respectively). Unless otherwise
speciﬁed, all alloy compositions refer to atomic percentage.
The actual compositions of alloys were tested by the induc-
tively coupled plasma technique (ICP), and the results were
basically consistent with the designed compositions. Ac-
cording to the previous results [ 27e29], some samples were
heated to 1250
/C14 C in the LSL-1700X high-temperature box
furnace. After holding for 48 h, they were cooled to room
temperature in the furnace.
All the samples for microstructure observation, heat
treatment, hardness test, and compression test were cut from
the as-cast ingots by electrical discharge machining (EDM).
After regular grinding and polishing, the metallographic
specimens were etched in a solution of 80% HCl þ20% HNO
3 by
volume to observe the microstructures by a scanning electron
microscope (SEM). The phase constitute was identiﬁed by an
X-ray diffractometer (XRD) and energy dispersion spectros-
copy (EDS).
The Vickers hardness of alloys was tested by a 2100B
Vickers hardness tester with a load of 100 g and a holding time
of 10 s. Thirty locations were tested in each specimen and the
average value was adopted. The size of compression speci-
mens was F4 /C2 7 mm. The room-temperature compression

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Phase constitute
Fig. 1 shows the XRD patterns of the as-cast NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-
xTi (x ¼ 0, 1 and 3, respectively) hypereutectic alloys. It can be
found that NiAl-32Cr-6Mo alloy is only composed of NiAl
phase and Cr phase but the Ni 2AlTi diffraction peaks are
observed after the addition of Ti. A new phase of Ni 2AlTi is
formed in the NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-1Ti and NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-3Ti al-
loys. Compared with the diffraction peak of pure Cr phase, the
diffraction peak of Cr phase in this work is slightly shifted to
the left, indicating that there may be solid solution atoms in
the Cr phase. The similar result was also obtained by Hagihara
et al. in the Ni-33Al-31Cr-3Mo-xTi (x ¼ 3 and 5 at.%, respec-
tively) alloys [ 24].
3.2. Microstructures of as-cast alloys
Fig. 2 shows the microstructures of as-cast NiAl-32Cr-6Mo,
NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-1Ti and NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-3Ti hypereutectic al-
loys. In the NiAl-32Cr-6Mo alloy, coarse primary dendrites and
irregular cells are observed. From the enlarged microstructure
in Fig. 2 (b), it is found that the cells are composed of alter-
nately arranged two-phase lamellae. Obviously, the lamellae
at the cell center are ﬁner than those at the cell boundary.

can be inﬁnitely mutually soluble to form an inﬁnite solid
solution. So the Cr phase identiﬁed in Fig. 1 actually contains a
large number of Mo atoms, and it is usually called as Cr(Mo)
phase [ 25,26]. Through the XRD result in Fig. 1 and energy
spectrum analysis in Table 1, the primary dendrite in the alloy
is also Cr(Mo) phase, while the black and gray lamellae are the

Al sites by substitution in the NiAl phase, resulting in the solid
solution in the NiAl matrix. During the solidiﬁcation process,
the solid solution of Ti in the NiAl matrix tends to be saturated
as the temperature drops. Then the saturated NiAl(Ti) matrix
decomposes, and the ﬁne Ni
2AlTi phase is precipitated from
the NiAl matrix: NiAl(Ti) /NiAl þ Ni2AlTi.

3.4. Hardness
The hardness of the as-cast and heat-treated alloys measured
by the Vickers hardness pressing method is shown in Table 3.
As the Ti content increases, the hardness value also increases.
Especially, the hardness of NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-1Ti alloy has the
highest value of 575.2HV. According to Figs. 2 and 4 , the Ni 2-
AlTi precipitated phase is formed at the cell boundary and Ti
solubilizes in the NiAl phase after the addition of Ti element,
which has beneﬁcial effects on the hardness of alloy due to the
precipitation strengthening and solid solution strengthening.
When the Ti content increases to 3 at.%, the hardness value is
about 532 HV in the as-cast alloy, which was mainly because
too much Ti element would lead to the increasing number of
primary phase Cr(Mo) in the alloy, and hence resulting in the
decreasing hardness. After heat treatment, the hardness of all
alloys decreases for various reasons. The more uniform and
ﬁner microstructure after heat treatment generally leads to
the increasing hardness, but they could only counteract
partially the negative inﬂuence of the coarsening of NiAl
precipitates and the existence of dislocations in the Cr(Mo)
phase [ 17,31]. Therefore, the hardness of all alloys decreases
after heat treatment.
3.5. Room temperature compression properties
The room temperature compression properties of alloys with
different compositions and conditions are listed in Table 4 .
The room temperature yield strength and compression strain
of NiAl-32Cr-6Mo alloy are 1093 MPa and 18.7%, respectively.
After adding 1 at.% Ti, the yield strength of as-cast NiAl-32Cr-
6Mo-1Ti alloy sharply increases to 1417 MPa, which was
obviously superior to other NiAl-Cr-Mo based alloys. For
example, this value is higher than that of the NiAl-28Cr-3Mo-
4Ti alloy (about 1230 MPa [ 22]). Meanwhile, NiAl-Cr(Mo)-0.5Hf
alloy, which was considered traditionally high-strength alloy,
only has the yield strength of 1380 MPa [ 32]. When 3 at.% Ti
was added, the yield strength is about 1314 MPa. Conversely,
Ti addition leads to a decrease in the compressive strain. In
addition, the effect of Ti addition on the yield strength of the
alloys after heat treatment also experiences the same
changes as the as-cast alloys. The fracture strength of all the
alloys increase while compression strain decreases. To pre-
sent the typical compression behaviors, the stress estrain
curves of the alloys with different conditions are displayed
in Fig. 5. It can be seen from the ﬁgures that all the alloys show
the brittle fracture. The values of compression properties in
the Table 4 and the stress estrain curves in the Fig. 5 are
consistent.

(x ¼ 0, 1, and 3, respectively) alloys at the temperature of
1000 /C14 C are listed in Table 5. The high-temperature strength of

alloy is signiﬁcantly improved to 325.44 MPa due to the larger

adding 1% Ti, the ultimate compression strength of as-cast
NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-1Ti alloy increases to 408.41 MPa, demon-

2AlTi phase in
improving the high-temperature strength. However, when 3%

From the above results, Ti addition has a signiﬁcant effect
on the room temperature compression properties. Compared
to Ti-free alloy, the enhancement of strength in Ti-doped al-
loys is mainly due to the precipitation strengthening by the
existence of Ni
2AlTi phase and the solid solution strength-
ening by the Ti atoms solubilized in the NiAl phase. So the
yield strength of as-cast NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-1Ti alloy increases
obviously. For non-eutectic alloy, cracks will preferentially
extend along the boundary between the primary dendrites
and the eutectic [ 15,25,26]. When the crack begins to generate
and propagate, the resistance of the crack propagation offers
by the Cr(Mo) primary dendritic phase is lower so that it could
not effectively prevent crack propagation [ 37,38]. That is, the
more the volume fraction of primary dendrites, the lower the
overall mechanical properties of the alloy. Furthermore, well-
aligned microstructure is also conducive to the improvement
of properties. So the irregular microstructure in the NiAl-32Cr-
6Mo-3Ti alloy also leads to the decreasing strength. When the
weakening effects are greater than the strengthening effects,
the strength of the NiAl-32Cr-6Mo-3Ti alloy would decrease.
To research the fracture behaviors of the alloys after heat
treatment, the fracture surfaces after compression test are
shown in Fig. 9. Both the as-cast and heat-treated alloys show
brittle fracture. In Fig. 9 (d), there are obvious dimples left by
the shedding of precipitated phases at the fracture. After heat
treatment, the fracture is rougher, and more tearing ridges are
observed, indicating higher compression strength. Compared
with Fig. 7 , the lamellar distribution becomes uniform, the
number of primary phases increases, and the precipitation at
the cell boundary becomes ﬁne and dispersed. From Fig. 9 (d)
and (f), it could be observed that cleavage occurs in the NiAl
phase, which could have a positive effect on the increase in
strength of the alloy. Obviously, the crack propagation
resistance increases and the crack propagation rate reduces
by heat treatment. Therefore, heat treatment could improve
the fracture strength of the alloys.
4. Conclusion
The inﬂuences of Ti addition on the microstructure evolution,
hardness, compression properties at room temperature and
1000
/C14 C, fracture behavior as well as strengthening mecha-
nism in NiAl-Cr(Mo) hypereutectic alloy were investigated in
this paper. The following conclusions could be drawn:
(1) The microstructure of NiAl-Cr(Mo) hypereutectic alloy
contains primary Cr(Mo) dendrite and NiAl þ Cr(Mo)
eutectic. Ti addition generates the Ni
2AlTi precipitated
phase, which is mainly distributed at the cell boundary.
With the increasing Ti content, the number of Cr(Mo)
primary phase increases signiﬁcantly, the eutectic cell
becomes chrysanthemum-like and the lamellar micro-
structure is no longer maintained.
(2) After heat treatment, the phase constituent of all alloys
does not change. The distribution of the Ni
2AlTi
precipitated phase is more uniform and the number of
primary phases increases signiﬁcantly.
(3) Due to the precipitation strengthening and solid solu-
tion strengthening, NiAl-Cr(Mo)-1Ti alloy has the high-
est hardness, room-temperature yield strength and
high-temperature compressive strength. However, the
increasing primary phase and the irregular micro-
structure result in the decreasing strength of NiAl-32Cr-
6Mo-3Ti alloy. After heat treatment, the room-
temperature yield strength of all Ti-doped alloys de-
creases, but the fracture strength and strain are
improved.
(4) All the alloys show brittle quasi-cleavage fracture at
room temperature. The fracture surface of as-cast NiAl-
32Cr-6Mo alloy is very ﬂat and the cleavage plane ap-
pears like a river pattern. After Ti addition, the number
of cleavage steps and tearing ridges increase signiﬁ-
cantly, and the fracture surface becomes more rugged.
In addition, multiple cleavages are observed in the NiAl
phase, which shows that the NiAl phase also plays a
role in strengthening the alloy.
