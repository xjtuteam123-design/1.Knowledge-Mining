in the HTM21 Project

Keywords: Udimet 720LI, Ni-Co-base superalloy, Turbine disk alloy, Tensile strength, Creep. 
Abstract. A new kind of cast & wrought (C&W) Ni-Co ba se superalloys named as “TMW alloys” 
was developed recently for compressor and turbin e disk of gas turbine engines in the High 
Temperature Materials 21 Project at the National In stitute for Materials Science (NIMS) in Japan. 
These Ni-Co base superalloys combine the characters of two kinds of γ- γ’ two-phase alloys (Ni-base 
and Co-base superalloys) and can be fabricated by cheap cast and wrought processing route. The 
results showed that some of these TMW alloys have superior tensile strength at temperatures up to 
750 °C and higher creep resistances up to 725 °C than commercial UDIMET 720 LI alloy. 
Intorduction 
The latest design of high efficiency engines ha s higher requirements for the operating speed and 
temperature of turbine airfoils. Higher engine pe rformances can be achieved by higher turbine inlet 
gas temperature and compression ratio, which main ly depend on the development of the materials 
with higher temperature capabilities for the key components of turbine engines including compressor, 
disk and blade especial the first stages turbine disks and blades where the stress and temperatures are 
the highest. 
For enhancing the turbine entry temperature, much work has been done on the improvement of the 
material temperature capability of turbine blades. Fig. 1 shows a perspective for the alloy and process 
improvement in the temperature capabilities of Ni -base superalloys for turbine blade components 
[1-7]. We choose the creep performance (here take n as the highest temperature at which rupture 
occurs at no less than 1000 hours at 137 MPA) as a measure for the progress. As indicated in Figure 1, 
there is about 200 °C improvement in the latest 40 years and the latest target temperature indicated in 
Figure 1 is the objective of the High Temperature Materials 21 Project (HTM21) led by the National 
Institute for Materials Science of Japan (NIMS) in collaboration with gas-turbine industries [8-10]. 
For TMS-162 alloy, time to 1% creep deformation at 1100°C/137MPa is about 5 times as long as that 
of CMSX-10.

Turbine disks employ to support rotating turbine bl ades in place in engines. The increase in 
operating temperatures of gas turbines blades requires the development of correspondingly improved 
alloys for turbine disks. Comparatively speaking, the thermal capability of disk material is not 
subjected to such extreme temperature as blade. But there has higher requirements for strength, creep 
and dwell fatigue crack growth properties for increasing in the compression ratio [11-15]. Fig. 2 
shows the progress of turbine disk superalloys ranked by creep/rupture performance (the stress for 
1000 h life at 630 MPa) [1-7, 11-15]. For the last forty years, there are only about 50 °C increase in 
the thermal capability for the Ni-base disk superalloys produced by cast and wrought (C&W) process 
and 60 °C improvement by powder metallurgy (P/M) process route. There is work going on 
independently at Pratt & Whitney, General Electri c, Rolls Royce, and SNECMA to develop a new 
disk alloy or an improved process that will allo w rim temperatures to be around 720°C. The latest 
disk alloys, like alloy RR1000 developed by Rolls -Royce Company and alloy ME3 developed by a 
joint contract effort between GE Aircraft E ngines, Pratt & Whitney and NASA Glenn Research 
Center, all use P/M process route and have 
superior tensile and creep properties at 
temperatures up to 720 °C. However, the 
need of making clean powder and using 
expensive thermo-mechanical processing 
steps to improve the fatigue life may limit 
their applications. 
Recently, we proposed a new concept to 
design disk alloy in the HTM21. The 
preliminary results show that this design 
concept may lead up to some promising disk 
alloys that can be processed by C&W route 
and have superior temperature capability 
compared with C&W U720Li superalloy. 
Alloy Design 
Our new concept is based on combin e the characters of two kinds of γ/γ’ two phase superalloys 
(Ni-base and Co-Ti superalloys) as indicated in Fig. 3. The developed disk alloys was designed to be 
able to process by C&W route and have superior strengths by the control of the cobalt and titanium 
contents. The cobalt level is selected to contro l the gamma prime solvus temperature. Increasing 
amounts of cobalt lower the gamma prime solvus temperature, which is desirable to achieve a large 
processing temperature range and reduce the stresses induced by controlled cooling and quenching of 
the alloy [14]. Titanium is a strong gamma prime former and readily substitutes for Al in the gamma 
prime phase [15]. 
Based on our new concept, some Ni-Co base s uperalloys, called TMW alloys, are designed for a 
trial investigation on microstructure and propert y. The nominal chemical compositions of tested 
TMW alloys are listed in Table 1. Here we refer U720Li alloy as TMW-1 alloy as a comparative. In 
the TMW disk alloys, the contents of Co and Ti ar e higher than that of normal disk alloys, and Co 
content is varied from about 22 to 30wt% and Ti content is varied from 5.1 to 7.4 wt%. 
Microstructure and Thermal Stability 
All tested TMW alloys with 50-kg ingots were cas t and then hot rolled in the Special Metals 
Corporation. The hot rolling was conducted smoothly at about 1100 °C with the final reduction of 
75% (from 50.8 mm to 12.7 mm). For microstructure investigation, small samples were cut from 
as-rolled plates and heat treated at 1100°C/4h air cooling (AC) + 650 °C /24h AC+760 °C/16h AC.

tensile strengths with good tensile ductility, high creep resistance are major ones.

about 100 MPa higher than that of TMW-1 alloy

Another important critical property that must be 
considered is creep resistance of new disk 
superalloys. Tensile creep tests were conducted 
at 725 °C and 630 MPa. 
Fig.6 displays the strain-time curves for some 
tested alloys. Time to 0.2% creep strain was 
about 12 hours for TMW-1 alloy (U720LI), 62 
hours for TMW 2 alloy and over 120 hours for 
TMW-4 alloy. So there is 10 times improvement 
in creep resistance of TMW-4 alloy compared 
with TMW-1 alloys (U720 Li) at such high 
temperature and stress. 
Summary 
The results indicate that Ni-Co base TMW alloys are promising alloys which have higher temperature 
capability than current C&W U720 Li disk alloy a nd can be fabricated by the normal C&W process 
route. A systematic and broader study is needed to fully understand the effects of key elements, such 
like Co, Ti and Al, on microstructure, mechani cal properties, thermal stability and processing 
characteristics of these Ni-Co base superalloys.
