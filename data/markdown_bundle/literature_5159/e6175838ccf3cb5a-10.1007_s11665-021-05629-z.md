Effect of Isothermal Aging on Microstructure Evolution
of Ni Claddings on Inconel 617
Lina Gao, Fenfen Han, Shuangjian Chen, Xiang-Xi Ye, Bin Leng, and Xingtai Zhou
Submitted: 4 September 2020 / Revised: 26 February 2021 / Accepted: 1 March 2021 / Published online: 15 March 2021
In this work, gas tungsten arc weld was employed to produce corrosion-resistant Ni claddings on the
surface of Inconel 617. The effect of isothermal aging on the microstructure of Ni claddings was studied at
850 /C176C for up to 3000 h. Results show that isothermal aging can promote the release of welding residual
stress. Recrystallization begins to appear in the Ni claddings when the aging time reaches 2000 h. Hardness
values and chemical compositions of Ni claddings are changed slightly during the whole aging process. TiN
phase is precipitated in the Ni claddings before and after isothermal aging, while their quantity and
morphology do not change signiﬁcantly. Low chromium concentration and extremely low chromium dif-
fusion coefﬁcient in Ni claddings indicate that the Ni claddings have excellent corrosion resistance to molten
salts.
Keywords cladding, coatings, corrosion resistance, hardness,
inorganic, isothermal aging, superalloys, precipitates
1. Introduction
Molten salt reactor (MSR), as one of the most promising
next-generation high-temperature reactors, has many impres-
sive features such as better fuel cycle capabilities, uncompa-
rable safety characteristics, high-temperature hydrogen
production and nuclear non-proliferation (Ref 1). Since the
liquid temperature ranges of ﬂuoride salts are extensive, from
400 to 1400 /C176 C (Ref 2), a common concern for the MSR design
is to increase the operating temperature to exploit the maximum
potential of salt and achieve better thermal efﬁciency. However,
structural materials will be the difﬁculties and barriers in these
higher temperature designs (Ref 3). Up to now, Hastelloy N
alloy is the only available alloy that has been tested in the
molten salt reactor experiment (MSRE) in the 1960s (Ref 4, 5).
According to the American Society of Mechanical Engineers
Boiler and Pressure V essel (ASME -BPV) Code, the codiﬁed
maximum allowable temperature of Hastelloy N alloy is 704 /C176 C
(Ref 4, 5). Although Hastelloy N alloy exhibits excellent
chemical compatibility with the ﬂuoride salt up to 871 /C176 C, its
lower mechanical performance at higher temperatures limits its
application (Ref 4). At present, Inconel 617, which has superior
high-temperature properties, is expected to be a leading
candidate Ni-base superalloy for Generation IV ﬁssion reactors
(Ref 6, 7). Recently, Inconel 617 has become the ﬁrst new
material to get into the volume of ‘‘ASME-BPV III-5 High
Temperature Reactors’’ in the last 30 years, whose codiﬁed
service temperatures can reach 950 /C176 C (Ref 8). Unfortunately,
despite the excellent mechanical performance at high temper-
atures, some researches have shown that Inconel 617 has low
compatibility with molten ﬂuoride (Ref 9, 10) and chloride (Ref
11) salts. Several superalloys, including Inconel 617 alloy,
Hastelloy N, and Ni-201 were tested in molten FLiNaK salts at
850 /C176 C for 500 h (Ref 10). The results show that the 617 alloy
specimens have the highest weight loss (29.7 ± 2.8 mg/cm 2)
which is more than ten times the weight loss of Hastelloy N
(2.3 ± 0.2 mg/cm2) and Ni-201 (0.0 ± 0.5 mg/cm2). The same
series of superalloys were also immersed in molten KCl-MgCl 2
salts at 850 /C176 C for 100 h (Ref 11) . The results show that the
maximum corrosion depth (225 lm) is found in alloy 617,
while the corrosion depths of Hastelloy N alloy and Ni-201 are
much lower ( < 40 lm).
Generally, it is difﬁcult for structural material to meet the
requirements of both corrosion resistance and mechanical
properties. Therefore, to enhance the corrosion resistance of
alloy with excellent mechanical properties at a higher temper-
ature, cladding protective coating on its surface is expected to
be an effective way. Incoloy 800H alloy with nickel claddings
has been studied by Olson (Ref 12) and Dai (Ref 13). The
results show that the Ni cladding is expected to signiﬁcantly
enhance the compatibility of Incoloy 800H alloy with molten
ﬂuoride salt. In ﬂuoride high temperature reactors (FHRs), high
strength alloys with Ni claddings, such as Inconel 617 or
Haynes 230 alloys, have been supposed to be a possible
strategy for structural materials (Ref 14, 15). However, none of
these researches focus on the high-temperature stability of these
materials even though it is an important performance index of
structural materials used in the high-temperature molten salt
environment.
In the present work, Ni claddings were deposited on the
surfaces of Inconel 617 by gas tungsten arc weld (GTAW)
method. The effects of aging temperature and aging time on the
microstructure evolution of Ni claddings on Inconel 617 were
investigated by isothermal aging tests.

2. Experimental
In this study, a Ni cladding layer was coated by multilayer
depositions on the 23-mm-thick Inconel 617 plate by GTAW
with restraint. And ERNi-1 ﬁller was the ﬁller metal. Table 1
shows the main chemistries of the Inconel 617 and the ﬁller
wire, which are provided by the manufacturer. Before welding,
to remove the oxide and impurities, the matrix metal was
cleaned by a grinding wheel followed by wiping with acetone
solution. The welding parameters in this study are listed in
Table 2. The heat input (Q) is calculated by Q = gEI/V (g= 0.7)
(Ref 16), where g is thermal efﬁciency, E is the welding
voltage, I is the current, and V is welding speed. The whole Ni
cladding with a thickness of 4.5 mm consists of three layers.
The specimens with a size of 10 mm 9 10 mm 9 28 mm
were cut by a linear cutting machine. The isothermal aging test
was performed in a high-temperature furnace with a temper-
ature tolerance of ± 5 /C176 C. The aging temperature was set at
850 /C176 C with duration time from 100 to 3000 h (100, 500, 1000,
2000, and 3000, respectively). After the isothermal aging
experiment, all samples were ground with water-proof SiC
abrasive papers from 400 grits to 2000 grits and polished with
0.05 lm alumina powders, followed by cleaning with deionized
water and alcohol.
For metallographic examination, the sample was etched in a
mixed acid (5g FeCl 3 + 50 ml HCl + 100 ml H 2O). The
microstructures were characterized using a Zeiss AX10 optical
microscope (OM) and a scanning electron microscope (SEM,
Zeiss LEO1530VP) equipped with an energy-dispersive spec-
trometer (EDS). The SEM-EDS spectra were analyzed with the
Oxford Instruments AZtec EDS software. The energy line was
calibrated with L- a and K- a from a high purity Mn disk.
Moreover, the crystal structure and chemical compositions of the
precipitates were also analyzed by Tecnai G2 F20 transmission
electron microscope (TEM) equipped with an EDAX AMETEK
EDS detector. The TEM-EDS spectra were analyzed using the
Tecnai Imaging and Analysis (TIA) software with an energy
range of 0-10 keV , allowing all elements to be quantiﬁed using
the K peaks except Mo (L- a). Quantiﬁcation was made using
TIA in standardless mode with a modeled empirical background
correction. TEM samples were mechanically ground to about
50 lm in thickness, and then twin-jet electro-polished in a mixed
solution of 10% perchloric acid and 90% ethanol at the
temperature of /C0 30 /C176 C and a current of 30 mA. The focus
ion beam (FIB) machine (Helios Nanolab 600) was also applied
to prepare the TEM samples to observe the ﬁne precipitation
phase at grain boundaries. The hardness proﬁles were obtained
from the cross section of polished samples with indentations at
intervals of 0.2 mm. The maximum load of 200 gf was applied
with a dwell time of 15 s.
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructure and Chemical Compositions Distribution
of Cladding
Figure 1 shows the microstructure of as-welded 617 plates
with Ni cladding layer (Ni/617) by OM. No visible defects such
as pores, cracks can be observed in the interface between Ni
cladding and 617 substrate, revealing that the interface exhibits
outstanding metallurgical bonding properties. The Ni claddings
mainly consist of columnar crystallites. The axle of the
columnar crystallites is almost perpendicular to the fusion
interface because the molten metal is nucleated based on the
previous layer and its growth direction is consistent with the
previous layer.
Since chromium is the most vulnerable element in molten
salts in common alloying elements, the chromium distribution
of the whole Ni/617 sample from Ni claddings to Inconel 617
substrate was detected using SEM-EDS. As shown in Fig. 2, for
the as-welded Ni/617 sample, it is found that the 1st cladding
layer shows higher chromium concentration, which indicates
that it is greatly affected by the substrate. With the increase in
cladding layers, the chromium concentration decreases gradu-
ally. It can be found that the distribution of chromium changes
little for samples with different isothermal aging time, sug-
gesting that the Ni/617 remains a relatively stable composition
during long-term isothermal aging.
Metallographic images of Ni claddings before and after
isothermal aging are shown in Fig. 3. Compared with the as-
welded sample, no apparent change can be found in the
microstructure of Ni claddings of the samples aged for up to
1000 h. The Ni claddings of these samples show a columnar
dendritic morphology. When the aging time increases to 2000
h, the dendrite structure of the 1st and 2nd cladding layers
partly disappear; instead, equiaxed crystals with irregular
polygon grains are formed, indicating recrystallization occurs
in the 1st and 2nd layers of the Ni claddings. When the aging
time reaches 3000 h, the proportion of recrystallization areas in
the 1st and 2nd cladding layers further increase. Generally,
there are two necessary conditions for recrystallization: reach-
ing critical deformation and recrystallization temperature.

cladding layer partly disappeared, and anisotropy was elimi-
nated after recrystallization. The microstructure of the cladding
layer after long-term aging is consistent with that of the base
metal. Meanwhile, the welding residual stress is released to the
maximum extent.
Figure 4 shows the cross-sectional SEM images of the 1st
cladding layer before and after isothermal aging. It can be seen
that ﬁne precipitates with discontinuous distribution appear at
the grain boundaries (GBs) of Ni claddings as-welded and after
thermal aged 100 and 500h. When isothermal aging time
continues to increase, ﬁne precipitates at the GBs disappear.
Besides, some granular precipitates are always distributed in
the samples with different aging time.
To identify the crystal structure of the precipitates at the
GBs, TEM analysis of the 1st Ni cladding layer thermally aged
for 500 h was conducted, as shown in Fig. 5. The precipitate
possesses a face-centered cubic (FCC) crystal structure, and the
lattice parameter is about 0.44 nm, which is coherent with the
FCC alloy matrix ( cphase) (Fig. 5a and b). The TEM-EDS
result shows that the precipitate consists of titanium, carbon and
molybdenum ((Fig. 5c). Combined with the TEM diffraction
and TEM-EDS results, the precipitate can be conﬁrmed as Mo-
rich TiC (Ref 17).
Figure 6 shows the cross section image of the 2nd and 3rd
cladding layers before and after isothermal aging. For the 2nd
and 3rd Ni cladding layers, there is no signiﬁcant difference in
microstructure in each layer. Fig. 6(b) shows the typical
microstructure of the 3rd Ni cladding layer (20 lm away from
the outermost surface), and the dispersion of ﬁne precipitated
particles exits either near the surface or in the middle part.
Compared to the 1st layer (Fig. 4), very few ﬁne precipitates
can be found at GBs of the as-welded samples, and only
granular precipitates are distributed within the grain. As the
aging time increases, there is no signiﬁcant change in the
morphology and quantity of the precipitates in the 2nd and 3rd
Ni cladding layers, similar to the 1st layer.
Figure 7 shows the TEM analysis results of the granular
precipitates in the 2nd and 3rd cladding layers. The SAED of
the precipitates with [011] zone axis shows a face-centered
cubic (FCC) crystal structure whose lattice parameter is about
0.43 nm. The TEM-EDS results show that all the precipitates
contain titanium and nitrogen, and the molar ratio of metallic
elements to nitrogen is close to one. These results conﬁrm that
the precipitate is TiN (Ref 18). It is known that the trace
elements and their diffusion play a signiﬁcant role in the
production of precipitates. It can be seen from Table 1 that the
titanium concentration in ERNi-1 ﬁller metal is 2.77 wt.%. The
addition of titanium can effectively depress the oxygen
concentration, preventing the cracks and pores in Ni-base
alloys during solidiﬁcation. Nevertheless, titanium is a chem-
ically reactive metal and tends to form oxides, nitrides, sulﬁdes,
and carbides (Ref 19). The TiN precipitates with FCC structure
were also observed in Ni-201 cladding deposited on 304
stainless (Ref 20) and the low carbon steel with extremely low
nitrogen concentration (Ref 21, 22).

3.3 Diffusion of Chromium in Ni Cladding Layer
Corrosion of metals in molten ﬂuoride and chloride salt
systems is a function of the chemistry of the salt and alloy. As
mentioned above, chromium is the most active alloying element
in molten salts. Therefore, as a protective barrier to molten
salts, Ni cladding should be kept as pure as possible in
composition. Dai et al. (Ref 13) tested 800H with Ni cladding
and GH3535 in molten FLiNaK (LiF-NaF-KF: 46.5-11.5-42
mol.%) at 850 /C176 C for 300 h. The results show that, for an alloy
or coating layer, which is resistant to molten salts, its chemistry
criterion should be Fe + Cr £ 11 wt.%. As shown in Fig. 2,
chromium concentration in the entire 3rd Ni cladding layer
remains about 4.0 wt.% (also shown in Table 3). Besides,
chromium concentration in Ni claddings increases due to the
diffusion of chromium from the underlying alloy substrate to Ni
claddings during the long-term high-temperature aging process.
When the aging time reaches 3000 h, the chromium concen-
tration in the 3rd Ni cladding layer is no longer stable and
increases with the distance from the outermost surface. At the
bottom of the 3rd Ni cladding layer, the chromium concentra-
tion reaches 7.9 wt.%, which is still less than 11 wt.%. In other
words, after aging for 3000 h, the chemical composition of the
3rd Ni cladding still meets the requirements of corrosion-
resistant alloy materials.
Given the long-term service of the structural alloys for
nuclear reactors, the depths of chromium diffusion between the
alloy substrate and Ni claddings with different aging times were
measured to evaluate the diffusion coefﬁcient of chromium in
Ni claddings according to the SEM-EDS spectrum. Three
measurements at different locations were done for each aging
time, and the average diffusion depths are listed in Table 4.

diffuses from the alloy substrate to the Ni claddings. The

4. Conclusion
Ni claddings with three layers structure have been success-
fully prepared onto the Inconel 617 substrate using GTAW with
appropriate parameters in this study. We investigated the effect
of long-term isothermal aging on the microstructure evolution of
the Ni claddings at 850 /C176 C for up to 3000 h. Isothermal aging can
promote the release of welding residual stress. And recrystal-
lization begins to appear in the 1st and 2nd layers of the Ni
claddings when the aging time reaches 2000 h. The proportion of
recrystallization areas further increases with the aging time up to
3000 h. The TiN precipitates with FCC crystal structure exist in
the Ni claddings layers before and after long-term high-
temperature thermal aging. There is no obvious change in the
morphology and quantity of the TiN phase during the long-term
heat treatment. The Ni/617 remains stable in composition and
hardness during long-term isothermal aging. The Ni claddings
are expected to have good corrosion resistance to molten salt for
the long term due to its low chromium concentration and
extremely low chromium diffusion rate in cladding layers.
