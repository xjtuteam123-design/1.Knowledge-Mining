abstract
The strengthening phases characteristics in the alloy determine the mechanical properties
of the alloy, but it is a hard task to predict the precipitation of complex alloys. In this work,
we quickly detected 33,484 groups of Ni-based superalloys composition information and
microstructure image by integrating high-throughput experiment and a nested UNet 3 þ
architecture for image recognition, and established a database of g
0 precipitation. Based on
the database, a high-conﬁdence prediction model was established, which could accurately
predict the volume fraction, average size and size distribution of g
0 prediction in different
alloys. Compared with the traditional methods, the proposed approach has a remarkable
advantage in acquiring and analyzing the experimental data, which can also be applied to
other multi-component alloys.

1. Introduction
For many materials, with the improvement of performance
requirements, the inﬂuence of more dimensions makes the
theoretical model of the material too complicated, and the
analytical solution is no longer feasible. Nowadays, data-driven
techniques unify the methods of theory, experiment, and
calculation/simulation, which can produce numerous data-
bases to help the research and development of complex
materials [ 1]. They are becoming more and more popular in
materials science. The high-throughput experimental method
can quickly obtain abundant experimental data which could
provide the necessary basic data for the research of new

high-throughput methods for materials are widely used in the
database establishment and development of functional mate-

preparation and characterization, and the complexity of the
structure of multi-element alloys, the database collection and

For the performance of structural materials, the volume
fraction and size distribution of strengthening phases played a
dominated role in advanced alloys. In the example of Ni-based
superalloys, the main precipitates, generally included, are: g
matrix, g
0 precipitation, the topologically close-packed (TCP)
phases ( s, m, P, Laves), and geometrically close-packed (GCP)
phases (g00 , h, d)[ 7,8]. The g0 precipitation was the strengthening
phase of L 12 ordered phase based on Ni 3Al, which was the key
factor in determining the mechanical properties of the super-
alloys [9]. According to the different heat treatment schedule,g
0
precipitations were divided into different scales: primary gʹ,
secondary gʹ and tertiary gʹ precipitation [10]. Owing to differ-
ential composition of alloy, the composition of g and g0 pre-
cipitation would change, andg0 precipitation exhibited different
morphologies under different lattice mismatch of the g/g0 pre-
cipitation interface, such as nearly spherical, cubic and other
different morphologies [ 9]. The TCP and GCP phases would
signiﬁcantly reduce the service performance, and they would
appear as dot, strip, ﬂake or irregular morphology with differ-
ential composition [ 8,11]. The properties of the alloy mainly
depended on the precipitates, which were very sensitive to the
change of compositions, but the relationship between
strengthening phases and mechanical properties of complex
alloys was still a hard problem. The multi-component solid
solution strengthening model proposed by Gypen and Der-
uyttere et al. [12] cannot be applied well to Ni-based superalloys
on account of the complex composition and shape of the
strengthening phase. And the model proposed by Galindo-Nava
et al. [13] can better predict the yield strength of superalloys, as
it needs basic data such as the size distribution of strengthening
phases. Despite existing methods can approximate the volume
fraction and average size of the strengthening phase, such as
the software DICTRA (diffusion controlled transformation)
combined with the local equilibrium hypothesis and the sharp
interface model [ 14,15], as well as the PanDiffusion (diffusion
simulation) module of the thermodynamic calculation software
Pandat [16], the prediction of complex systems such as super-
alloy was inaccurate [ 17], and its size distribution cannot be
predicted. Although many efforts had been conducted to pre-
dict the precipitates through experiment and calculation [18,19],
it was still an enormous challenge to bridge the size distribution
and compositions of precipitates. Therefore, based on data-
driven techniques and high-throughput experimental method,
many alloy microstructure images were collected and analyzed
to form a reliable database, which was a feasible scheme to
solve the problem of strengthening phase prediction [20].
Fine adjustment of composition would change the charac-
teristics of precipitates for the complexity of the microstructure
of superalloys, which brought challenges to image recognition.
It was not competent for batch image processing by common
software, such as ImageJ [21] and Image-pro which even cannot
automatically divide a single picture and need to be manually
marked. Moreover, it would inevitably be affected by corrosion
and light in the process of sample preparation and image
acquisition, and thus microstructure varied due to different
environmental states including blur and brightness, which
increased more difﬁculties for traditional image recognition
methods such as Otsu [22], Adaptive Threshold [23], watershed
[24], graph cut [25]. To date, deep learning with encoder-decoder
architecture had reported state-of-the-art segmentation accu-
racy. Originated from the Fully Convolutional Networks [ 26],
UNet based encoder-decoder architecture [27] had proved itself
very remarkable simplicity, efﬁciency, and robustness with
limited amounts of data. However, it seemed to be insufﬁcient
when the size of segmentation targets varies dramatically. On
this base, many improved versions such as SegNet [ 28], Atten-
tion UNet [29], UNetþþ [30] and UNet 3þ [31] had been proposed
to improve the segmentation accuracy. Among them, UNet 3 þ
combined the multi-scale features by re-designing skip con-
nections as well as utilizing a full-scale deep supervision, which
provided fewer parameters but yielded a more accurate
position-aware and boundary-enhanced segmentation map. To
this end, we adopted the UNet 3 þ architecture for microstruc-
ture recognition.
In this paper, we proposed a high-throughput preparation
and characterization method combined with machine learning,
which forms an integrated method of experiment design,
database, data-mining technologies and modeling. Through
this method, we established a Ni-based superalloys database
with 33,484 data, and the effect of composition on g
0 phase
characteristics was investigated.

2.2. Characterization
The chemical corrosive agent was 33% H 2Oþ33% acetic

image of each diffusion-triple.
In order to obtain the corresponding relationship between
the component data and the microstructure, we had estab-
lished a coordinate system in each diffusion-triple. The coor-
dinate of each diffusion-triple was established according to a
size of 2000 /C2 2000 mm, setting the ranges of X-axis and Y-axis
as 0e2000 based on the actual dimension. Considering that the
composition changes in the center of the diffusion-triple were
more obvious after the process of diffusion, denser data points
were collected in the center to ensure the uniformity of the
experimental composition distribution. Setting coordinate
points in step of 50 within 500 /C20 x /C20 1500 and 500 /C20 y /C20 1500,
and taking these setting coordinate points as the center and
taking a square with a side length of 50, the central part of the
diffusion ternary node was divided into 441 independent areas.
In the peripheral area, the step size of the center coordinate
points was set to 100, and it was divided into 320 squares with a
side length of 100. In this way, each diffusion-triple was
divided into 761 parts, and the chemical composition and
microstructure diagram of each part were corresponding
based on the coordinates. There were 8371 data for each
diffusion-multiple, and the total data volume was 33,484.

In this paper, we adopted the UNet 3 þ model for identifying

sources, we adopted the common way “divide and conquer”to
face this problem and divided each image into several small
sub-images with size 512 /C2 512. Note that models based on
deep learning require a large number of pixel-level labeled
images for training. During the experiment, 160 representa-
tive and indistinguishable images of different brightness/
contrast and g

3.2. Image recognition of g0 precipitations
In order to explore the microstructure characteristics of Ni-
based superalloys, it is necessary to characterize the main
strengthening phases in detail. As shown in Fig. 4 , these are
representative 4 areas in hundreds of manually labeled pic-
tures. In order to completely eliminate the inﬂuence of harmful
phases ( m, s and h phases) on image recognition of g
0 pre-
cipitations, we have only taken the g0 precipitation as the
foreground (marked as red regions) and the g matrix and
harmful phases as the background.
In order to accurately obtain large amounts of character-
izations of g0 precipitation, UNet 3 þ network was used to
distinguish the various phases of the superalloys and separate
the g0 precipitation. Because the secondary electron image
mainly distinguished the morphological features, and the EBSD
image mainly distinguished the chemical components, in order
to avoid the inﬂuence of other precipitation relative results
with similar morphology to g
0 precipitation, takes the both
image as input for image recognition simultaneously. As illus-
trated in Fig. 5 , the UNet 3 þ comprised the encoder and the
decoder sub-networks that were with skip connections be-
tween the different layers. To be speciﬁc, the encoder sub-
network consisted of ﬁve repeated applications of two 3 /C2 3
convolution layers and one 2 /C2 2 max pooling layer to extract
image features, where the number of feature channels in each
encoder stage were corresponding to 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024.
Meanwhile, the decoder sub-network up-sampled the feature
maps of different layers to origin image size. To seamlessly
merge the shallow exquisite information with deep semantic
information, each decoder stage incorporated both smaller-
and same-scale feature maps from encoder and larger-scale
feature maps from decoder, yielding 320 feature channels. To
learn hierarchical representations from the aggregated feature
maps, the UNet 3 þ yielded a side output from each decoder
stage, which was supervised by the ground truth. To realize
deep supervision, the last layer of each decoder stage was fed
into a plain 3 /C2 3 convolution layer, followed by a bilinear up-
sampling and a sigmoid function. The annotated dataset was

discovery of other outstanding HEAs in the future.
4. Conclusions
In conclusion, this paper has the following innovative points:
1) Developed a high-throughput experiment method to
obtain 33,484 Ni-based superalloys with the composition
information and microstructure.
2) Proposed to utilize deep learning model (UNet 3 þ) for
identifying the g0 precipitation.
3) Built a Ni-based superalloys database on the composition
information, heat treatment schedule and the character-
istics of g
0 precipitation.
4) Established several BP-NN models to accurately predict the
volume fraction, average size and size distribution of g0
precipitation according to composition and heat treatment
schedule.
A substantial challenge in alloy performance prediction
was the size and distribution of strengthening phase, this
method offers a promising opportunity to ﬁnd new generation
Ni-based superalloys with excellent microstructure and
properties for the next step. Moreover, the proposed method
could also be applied to the microstructure recognition and
prediction of other multi-component alloys.
