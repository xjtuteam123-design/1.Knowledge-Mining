Formation of eutectic ( c0 + a) and a transformation in
Ta-bearing high W content cast Ni-base superalloys
Liang Zheng *
National Key Laboratory of Advanced High Temperature Structural Materials, Beijing Institute of Aeronautical Materials,
P.O. Box 81-1, Beijing 100095, China
Received 1 May 2005; received in revised form 17 June 2005; accepted 19 June 2005
Available online 11 July 2005
Abstract
The microstructures of as-cast and heat treated specimens and stress rupture properties for Ta-bearing high W content cast
Ni-base superalloys were investigated. The results indicate that Ta promotes the formation of eutectic ( c0 + a) in late solidiﬁcation.
The a phase is unstable during heating above 1260 /C176C and long term exposure at 1100 /C176C, and can transform to M 6C carbides. To
avoid the a phase formation, it is necessary to modify the compositions of alloys by following the principle of substitution of Ta for
Al at equal atomic ratio.

1. Introduction
Low Cr and high W content cast Ni-base superalloys
are promising candidate materials for manufacturing
gas turbine vanes and high temperature forging dies
due to their characteristics of low cost and high temper-
ature capability [1]. This kind of alloy even with a equi-
axed grain possesses a impressively high stress rupture
strength corresponding to that of the ﬁrst generation
single crystal superalloys at 1100 /C176C. However, they
suﬀer from serious oxidation at elevated temperatures.
Therefore, some attempts such as compositional modiﬁ-
cation to improve the oxidation resistance should be
employed. Of all alloying elements, tantalum was con-
sidered beneﬁcial for increasing the creep and oxidation
resistance of superalloys [2,3]. The content of Ta in
earlier alloys such as B-1900 and TRW-NASA VI is in
the 3–9 wt.% range [4,5], compared to 4–12 wt.% for
recent SC superalloys [6,7]. Because of the importance
of Ta in superalloys, many works have focused on its
eﬀects on the microstructure, stability and strengthening
mechanism in the past [8,9]. Recently the author found
that eutectic ( c
0 + a) formed in the later stages of solid-
iﬁcation because of the excessive amount of Ta plus Al
in total. The purpose of this paper is to understand
the eﬀect of tantalum on the formation of eutectic
(c
0 + a) and the transformation of a phase in cast
Ni-base superalloys with low Cr and high W content.

3.2. Formation of eutectic ( c
0 + a)
The relationship between the amounts of (Al + Ta),
(Ti + Nb) and (W + Mo) and the quantitative metallog-
raphy results of eutectic c
0 and a phase is shown in
Table 3 . It can be seen that the alloys with eutectic
(c0 + a), such as Alloys 3 and 4, are characterized by
their large amounts of eutectic c0 up to 20 vol.%. The
excessive amounts of eutectic c0 correlate to the high con-
tent of (Al + Ta). It was noteworthy that Alloy 4 ought
to have contained a higher amount of a phase than that
of Alloy 3 due to its higher total amount of Al plus Ta
and eutectic c
0, but the result was contrary. This was
caused by the relatively lower W content of Alloy 4.
In high W content cast Ni-base superalloys, the a
phase normally forms at the very onset of solidiﬁcation
as the solid solubility of (W + Mo) in c–c0 matrix
reaches its limit. In this case, the a-(W,Mo) dendrites
were distributed in both c–c0 dendritic core and inter-
dendritic region [10]. However, in this investigation the
a phase was only located within the eutectic c0 block
in the interdendritic region, indicating its formation in
the late stages of solidiﬁcation. Because Ta and Al are
strong positive segregation elements and c
0-formers,
the (Al,Ta)-rich residue liquid can reach the eutectic
(c + c
0) composition and promotes the reaction of
L ! c + c0 in the late stages of solidiﬁcation. It can be
seen from comparing Table 1 with Table 4 that the con-
centration of (W + Mo) in eutectic c0 is 27–43% lower
than that of the average composition of the Alloys.

During the formation of eutectic c0, W and Mo were

The previous work proved that the addition of Ta

In order to avoid the formation of eutectic ( c0 + a), it

excessive Ta level will promote the formation of eutectic
(c

superalloys with high W content. The eutectic
