Abstract 9 
In this study, Re alloying and self-generated hard phase enhancement were used to 10 
improve the strength and toughness of W. The arc-me lting W-based composite with 11 
10 wt% Re and 1 wt% SiC addition is composed of tou ghening W-Re matrix and 12 
ceramic particles (W 2C and W 5Si 3). This composite having high strain capacity 13 
(37.1%) at room temperature (more than 5 times of W ), exhibits the excellent 14 
hardness (5.04 GPa), yield strength (0.92 GPa) and fracture strength (1.78 GPa) due 15 
to the synergetic mechanism, grain refinement, and good interfaces bonding state. The 16 
synergetic mechanism of Re and SiC addition on stre ngth and toughness was 17 
proposed. 18 
Key words: W, Re, SiC, hardness, strength, toughness 19 
1. Introduction 20 
W-based materials feature several advantageous prop erties for structural 21 
materials [1] and plasma-facing components [2-3], e.g. , high melting point, good 22 
thermal conductivity, high creep resistance, good h igh-temperature strength and low 23 
vapor pressure. However, the low ductility of W at room temperature (RT) is one of 24 
the most obstacles for its application [4]. Several approaches have been developed to 25 
increase the ductility, e.g., alloying of W with K [5], Ta [6-7], V [7], Nb [8], Mo [9], 26 
Re [2,9] and Ti [9,10]. Only a few metals (Ta, V , Nb and Mo) form solid solutes in the 27 
complete fraction range. However, recent studies wi th Ta, V , Ti, Nb and Mo, which 28 
should be verified using a different fabrication route, have not led to promising results. 29

Hence, it seems unlikely that the ductility of W wo uld be improved by the formation 1 
of substitution solid solutions other than by Re [1 1]. As reported, Re alloying could 2 
obviously increase the toughness at only high Re co ncentrations close to the limit of 3 
solubility (27%) [11-13]. Base on the density-funct ional theory (DFT) analysis, Re 4 
alloying modifies screw dislocations from a symmetr ic structure to an asymmetric 5 
core structure, and reduces resistance in shear [12 ,14]. Experiments have confirmed 6 
that small amount of Re addition (less than 9 wt%) could “soften” W already [15-17], 7 
because Re alloying could decrease the Peierls stre ss and increase the additional slip 8 
planes [11-12], e.g. , the slip on {112} planes could be promoted by Re addition 9 
[18-19]. Hence, Re alloying is a good approach for improving the ductility of W. On 10 
the other hand, ductility is also enhanced by using grain refinement, which could be 11 
achieved by the addition of ceramic phase and the p rocess treatment of plastic 12 
deformation and thermal treatment. Although more pr omising materials such as 13 
W-Y2O3 [20], W-HfC [21-22], W-TiC [23] and W-ZrC [24] wer e recently produced 14 
by sintering and severe plastic deformation (SPD) i n collaboration, the fabrication 15 
processing with high failure rate and short die lif e is complex and time-consuming 16 
[25-26]. Forming the ductility phase in situ throug h high-temperature reaction is an 17 
available way to enhance the ductility and toughnes s of W. For instance, W 2Zr [27] 18 
and W 5Si 3 [28-29], those formed from the reactions of W, hav e been verified as the 19 
toughness phases. Lee [29] showed that the flexural strength of SiC/W composite at 20 
RT was significantly increased due to the bridging phenomenon by the toughening 21 
W5Si 3. As our previous study shows [30], W-based materia l with 1 wt% SiC addition 22 
significantly enhance the strain capacity due to th e grain refinement and toughening 23 
W5Si 3, as a result of the formation of W 2C and W 5Si 3. Through such studies, 24 
arc-melted tungsten, with high purification effect and low Ductile-brittle transition 25 
temperature (DBTT) (~473 K) [6,31], has the relatively high strain capacity under low 26 
temperature condition. 27 
The combination of various mechanisms may give play to their respective 28 
advantages and achieve the better synergetic effect . Therefore, in this work, both Re 29 
and SiC were added into W powder and melt-solidified by arc melting. The synergetic 30

2. Experimental details 3 
W powder (99.9% in purity, 1 µm in diameter; Aladdin, Shanghai, China), Re 4 
powder (99.9% in purity, 1 µm in diameter; Aladdin, Shanghai, China) and β-SiC 5 
powder (99.9% in purity, 1 µm in diameter; Aladdin, Shanghai, China) were used as 6 
raw materials. Mixed powders (W+10 wt% Re, W+1 wt% SiC, and W+1 wt% SiC+10 7 
wt% Re, abbreviated as R10, S1, and S1R10, respecti vely) were weighed and ball 8 
milled in a low energy ball milling apparatus (QM-A , Xianyang, China). The powder 9 
mixture was dried in a vacuum at 323 K for 12 hours after mixing and then pressed 10 
into pellets (15 mm in diameter and 5 mm in thickne ss) at 30 MPa. Each pellet was 11 
melted 4 times by arc-melting (ACM-SO1, DIA V AC, Japan) to achieve uniformity of 12 
composition, and solidified on a water-cooled coppe r base in an Ar atmosphere at 60 13 
kPa, and obtaining the fully-dense specimen. The me lted specimens were cut into the 14 
specific size, i.e. Φ2 ×5 mm 3 for compressive test, by an electric discharge machine. 15 
The microstructure and compositions were investigat ed by an electron probe 16 
micro-analyzer (EPMA, JXA-8230, Japan) with a wavelength dispersive spectrometer, 17 
and a transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEM 21 00, JEOL, Japan). The grain 18 
size was analyzed by the software of Image-Pro Plus 6.0. The hardnesses of the 19 
W-Si-C multi-phase composites were measured by a Vi ckers micro-hardness tester 20 
(430SVD, Wolpert, USA) using a load of 1 kg. The compressive strength and strain of 21 
the specimens were measured by a mechanical testing machine (AG-100 kN, 22 
Shimadzu, Japan) equipped with an extensometer (M926105BH40-03, Ametek, USA) 23 
using a crosshead speed of 1 mm/min. Both the Vicke rs micro-hardness and strength 24 
values were measured at RT and reported as the averages of five measurement results. 25

4. Conclusions 4 
In summary, Re and SiC were added into pure W to im prove the strength and 5 
ductility via the arc-melting method. The W-based c omposite with 10 wt% Re and 1 6 
wt% SiC additions (S1R10) composed of W-Re metallic matrix and ceramic phases 7 
(W2C and W5Si 3) that either segregated along the GBs or in the grain interior of W-Re. 8 
The grain size of metallic matrix obviously decreas ed due to the formation of W 2C 9 
and W 5Si 3 at the GBs. S1R10 presented a good interfaces bonding state, and exhibited 10 
the excellent hardness and comprehensive properties due to the synergetic effect of Re 11 
alloying and SiC addition. The synergetic mechanism may result from the combined 12 
effect of toughening metallic matrix and internal p article strengthening in a 13 
long-range interaction between particles and disloc ations. Moreover, kinematic strain 14 
gradient contribution is the most effective. In view of that the harder m aterial always 15 
has the lower strain capacity, the synergetic desig n may be a promising approach to 16 
provide the composite with high hardness, strength and strain simultaneously. 17

Highlights 
1. The strain capacity of arc-melted S1R10 is 6 times of W at RT.
