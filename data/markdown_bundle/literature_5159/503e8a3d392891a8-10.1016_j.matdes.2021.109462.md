The high-entropy alloys (HEAs) exhibit outstanding high-temperature properties and promise in a wide range of
applications, such as aviation, nuclear energy and other ﬁelds. However, the problems of formability and defect
control limit the successful realization of HEAs. Herein, we newly developed NbMoTaTi0.5Ni0.5 HEA which renders
a high room-temperature compressive strength of 2297 MPa and high-temperature (1000 °C) compressive
strength of 651 MPa. Meanwhile we present that the addition of both Ni and Ti can suppress the crack formation
and enhance the formability of NbMoTa HEA, prepared by selective laser melting (SLM), without compromising
the mechanical performance. Actually, the fracture is suppressed by the transformation of microcracks due to
grain boundaries with low stacking fault energy (SFE) which can greatly improve the formability of HEAs
while ensuring its high-temperature performance. The current study shall serve as a guideline for the develop-
ment of refractory high-entropy alloys via additive manufacturing, such as selective laser melting.

1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are considered as one of the three
breakthroughs in alloying theory in recent decades [1]. The most impor-
tant refractory high-entropy alloy system mainly includes some ele-
ments with a high melting point, such as Cr, Hf, Mo, Nb, Ta, V, W and
Zr. In general, the HEAs exhibit the body-centered cubic (BCC) structure,
where some of the HEAs possess single-phase BCC structures [ 2–6].

However, the properties of high melting point and HEAs at room tem-
perature are not outstanding, such as limited plasticity and low fracture
strain (<10%) [ 7–11]. For instance, the typical refractory HEA,
i.e., NbMoTaW, exhibits a compressive yield strength of 405 MPa at
1600 °C and yield strength of 1058 MPa at room temperature with a
strain rate of only 2.6%. Therefore, different metals, such as Ti, V, Cr
and other low melting point elements, are added to improve the
room-temperature plasticity and strength of NbMoTaW. Consequently,
the compressive yield strength, fracture strength and plastic strain of
NbMoTaWTi HEA reach 1343 MPa, 2005 MPa and 14.1%, respectively
[5,9,12].
However, the NbMoTaX HEA is mainly prepared by vacuum arc
melting, powder metallurgy and mechanical alloying [ 12–16], which
cannot prepare complex-shape components. Therefore, it is of utmost
signiﬁcance to develop and fabricate the high melting point HEA by
laser melting forming. Unfortunately, it is dif ﬁcult for the BCC-
structured alloy to control different types of defects, such as cracks
and pores, due to the high-temperature gradient [ 7,17–21]. However,
there are few studies on defect suppression of NbMoTaX HEA during
laser augmentation. The current research mainly utilizes the high-
throughput advantage of laser additive manufacturing to realize the
fast traversal of elemental composition and design of functionally gradi-
ent materials [4,22].
Hence, the laser forming of HEAs is mainly dependent on the form-
ability of the target material. Herein, NbMoTa alloy is used as a matrix
[23] and both Ti and Ni are added as strengthening element. The selec-
tive laser melting method is used to fabricate which shape of NbMoTa
HEA. Finally, a novel alloy system of NbMoTaTi
0.5Ni0.5 is established
based on the morphology, structure and properties analysis of
NbMoTaTi and NbMoTaNi HEAs. Meanwhile, the mechanism of grain
boundary microcrack transformation inhibition, induced by low stack-
ing fault energy (SFE), is proposed based on the experimental results,
which has a certain guiding signi ﬁcance for the practical applications
of NbMoTaX HEA.
2. Materials and methods
The Nb, Mo, Ta, Ti and Ni spherical powders (99%) were used with an
average diameter of 15–45 μm. In addition, the equal atomic and near
isoatomic ratios of Nb, Mo, Ta, Ti and Ni powders were mixed to study
the formability of SLM-prepared NbMoTaX system. The theoretical
composition and melting point of NbMoTaX HEA is shown in Table 1.
The self-developed SLM equipment was used for the selective laser
melting of NbMoTaX HEA. The equipment utilized a YLR-500-WC laser
of IPG with a maximum power of 500 W and a laser wavelength of
1064 nm with a spot diameter of 50 μm. The titanium (Ti) alloy
(120 × 120 × 20 mm) substrate was preheated at 200 °C. The process
was carried out at the power (W) of 300 W, scan speed (v) of 300 mm/s,
layer thickness (z) of 0.03 mm and scan distance (d)o f0 . 0 6m m .
The microstructure was characterized by using scanning electron
microscopy (Gemini SEM 500), equipped with an energy dispersive
X-ray spectrometer (EDS) and electron backscattered diffraction
(EBSD) analyzer. The phase analysis was carried out by using an X-ray
diffractometer (XRD, Bruker D8 advance). The grain boundaries and
phase composition were further characterized using transmission elec-
tron microscopy (TEM, JEOL JEM-2100plus) and selected area electron
diffraction (SAED). The compression tests were performed using an
MTS machine (MTS810) at room temperature and Gleeble machine
(Gleeble 1500) at high temperatures with a crosshead speed of
0.3 mm/min. The phase diagram calculation software (Jmpat pro)
were used to estimate the liquid phase temperature of each alloy. The
porosity is calculated as the ratio of the defect area to the total area,
which is obtained by measuring three random micrographs with
image analysis software (Image-Pro Plus 6.0). The CASTEP software
was used to and calculate the SFE based on the analysis of the grain
boundary phase (GB phase) structure.
3. Results
3.1. Additive manufacturing of NbMoTa alloy and material optimization
3.1.1. NbMoTa alloy forming
Compared with NbMoTaW HEA, the NbMoTa alloy exhibits between
high-temperature performance and formability [24], making it a prom-
ising candidate for additive manufacturing processes. Herein, the
NbMoTa alloy was formed by SLM. The morphology and composition
of the as-formed NbMoTa parts are shown in Fig. 1.
The surface energy spectrum indicates that Nb, Mo and Ta are uni-
formly distributed (Fig. 1b), showing that the turbulence of molten pool
under the action of high-energy laser is sufﬁcient to homogenously mix
different elements and form the high-entropy alloy. Fig. 1a shows that
the SLM-prepared NbMoTa alloy still possesses a large number of pores
and micro-cracks (5–10 μm), presenting a uniform grid-like distribution.
In addition, NbMoTa alloy mainly exists in the form of equiaxed crystals,
and the crystal grains and unit cell are tightly arranged without the pre-
cipitation of any secondary phase. The defects are formed due to the
high-temperature gradient during laser-assisted additive manufactur-
ing, which endows large thermal stress to the forming surface in a small
area, resulting in a tendency to crack. Simultaneously, the cracking de-
fects cannot be ﬁlled because the cooling rate is greater than the ﬂow
rate of molten metal, which leads to poor formability of SLM-prepared
NbMoTa alloy and results in the formation of cracks.
3.1.2. Improved formability by elemental addition
To minimize the concentration of cracks in NbMoTa alloy, which are
caused by the high thermal stress during the forming process and the
incapability of the molten metal to ﬁll these cracks, the composition of
NbMoTa alloy is optimized by adding low melting point elements. The
NbMoTaTi alloy and NbMoTaNi alloy were designed based on the pub-
lished literature and phase diagram analysis. The calculated phase dia-
grams are shown in Fig. 2 a and b. In the case of NbMoTaTi alloy
(Fig. 2a), the previous research results demonstrate that the addition
of Ti improves the toughness of WNbMoTa alloy. At the same time,
Fig. 2 a shows that the liquid phase appears at 2300 °C and the as-
obtained NbMoTaTi alloy contains two phases, i.e., α-Ti phase (25%)
and NbMoTaTi BCC phase (75%). The NbMoTaTi alloy exhibits high re-
sistance to thermal stress deformation. In the case of NbMoTaNi alloy
(Fig. 2b), the liquid phase appears at 1200 °C and there are a large num-
ber of secondary phases with FCC structure. Hence, the NbMoTaNi alloy
possesses a wide liquid phase range, rendering betterﬂuidity and ﬁlling
capability at the same temperature. Therefore, the NbMoTaTi alloy is ex-
pected to possess toughness and improve the stress cracking resistance
during the forming process. Therefore, the addition of Ti and Ni is ex-
pected to enhance the formability of NbMoTa alloy. The as-designed
NbMoTaTi and NbMoTaNi alloys were realized by SLM forming experi-
ments, as shown in Fig. 2ca n dd .
Fig. 2c shows that the crack distribution in NbMoTaTi alloy is similar to
the NbMoTa, however, the microcrack size in NbMoTaTi alloy is lower
than the NbMoTa alloy and the crack width is reduced from 5 to 10 μm
to 0.1–2 μm. In addition, the NbMoTaTi alloy also exists in the form of
equiaxed crystals and the enrichment of the secondary phase at grain
boundary results in coarse grains. The surface energy spectrum reveals

3.1.3. Composition optimization of NbMoTaX alloy
The addition of Ti and Ni is beneﬁcial to resist the high thermal stress
during SLM forming process and improve the crack ﬁlling capability of
the molten metal. However, the NbMoTaTi alloy still possesses micro-
cracks due to low proportion of high temperature liquid phase. Though
the micro-cracks in NbMoTaNi alloy are eliminated, the overall fracture
problem of the as-formed component needs to be resolved. One should
note that the addition of Ti only improves the plasticity without enhanc-
ing the high-temperatureﬂuidity, whereas the addition of Ni improves
the high-temperature ﬂuidity and compromises the overall plasticity.
Therefore, the current study added both Ti and Ni to obtain a synergistic
inﬂuence on plasticity and high-temperature ﬂuidity. The calculated
phase diagram is shown in Fig. 3a.
Fig. 3a shows that the addition of both Ti and Ni lowers the liquid
zone temperature to 700 °C, resulting in BCC, BCC2and FCC phases.
Hence, the ﬂuidity of high-temperature liquid metal during the
laser forming process is better than the NbMoTaNi alloy, while en-
suring a certain degree of plasticity. Theoretically, the formability
of NbMoTaTi 0.5Ni0.5 alloy is better than NbMoTaTi and NbMoTaNi.
As shown in Fig. 3b, the structure of NbMoTaTi 0.5Ni0.5 alloy is similar
to the NbMoTaNi alloy, showing a dendritic structure with a speci ﬁc
orientation and both macro- and micro-cracks are completely elimi-
nated ( Fig. 3 c). As shown in Fig. 3 d, the elemental distribution of
NbMoTaTi 0.5Ni0.5 alloy is also similar to the previous results, where
Nb, Mo, Ta and a part of Ti are uniformly distributed in the main
phase, whereas large amounts of Ti and Ni are distributed in the sec-
ondary phase at grain boundaries. However, with the effect of parti-
cle sides and volatilization of elements, the elements Nb, Mo, Ta, in
NbMoTaX alloy, compared with theoretical composition, tend to be
larger but the Ti and Ni are smaller. The XRD results con ﬁrm that
the NbMoTaTi 0.5 Ni0.5 alloy also exhibits a BCC structure, which is
similar to NbMoTaTi and NbMoTaNi ( Fig. 3 e). One should note that
a small impurity peak appeared at 2 θ = 40° due to the segregation
of large amounts of Ti and N i at grain boundaries [ 9,11,13,25–29].
Overall, these results verify that the simultaneous addition of Ti
and Ni signi ﬁcantly improves the formability of NbMoTa alloy.
In summary, the NbMoTaX alloy can be completely mixed and
formed by the SLM method, resulting in a high-entropy alloy with a
BCC structure. At the same time, the addition of Ti and Ni improves
the formability and realizes the high-density molding of the
NbMoTaTi0.5Ni0.5 HEA.

reduced after the addition of Ti into NbMoTa alloy. However, the Ni addi-

macrocrack. The simultaneous addition of Ti and Ni completely removed
the micro- and macro-cracks. The detailed surface analysis reveals that

tension in the mushy zone and tensile deformation during the semi-

et al. have proposed the necessary conditions for solidiﬁcation crack gen-

boundary, indicating that the micro-area (Ω) at the grain boundary and

5. Conclusions
The inﬂuence of low melting point elements on the forming process
and properties of SLM-prepared NbMoTaX alloys has been studied in
detail. Consequently, a novel alloy composition is established by adding
Ti and Ni, NbMoTaTi 0.5Ni0.5, to obtain high forming capability and
strength for SLM. Moreover, without compromising the high-
temperature mechanical performance, the formation of defects during
SLM is suppressed due to grain boundaries with low stacking fault en-
ergy (SFE). The main conclusions can be summarized as:
1. The NbMoTa HEA shows sever cracking defects during SLM process,
where the addition of Ti reduced the crack size, and the addition of
Ni eliminated the microcracks. However, the NbMoTaNi alloy exhib-
ited major penetrating cracks due to the decrease in plasticity of the
main phase. The simultaneous addition of both Ni and Ti suppressed
the formation of cracks and defects in SLM-prepared
NbMoTaTi0.5Ni0.5 alloy as well as ensured high room-temperature
(2297 MPa) and high-temperature compressive strength (651 MPa
at 1000 °C). In terms of mechanical performance, the room-
temperature compressive strength of NbMoTa alloy is increased by
77.5% after the addition of Ni and Ti. Besides, by adjustment the
ratio of Ti and Ni and appropriate heat treatment process, the plastic-
ity and toughness of NbMoTaTi0.5Ni0.5 will be improved in the future
study.
2. A variety of cracks formed during the additive manufacturing of
NbMoTaX alloy. The hot crack index (dT/dfs
1/2) of NbMoTa, NbMoTaTi,
NbMoTaNi and NbMoTaTi0.5Ni0.5 alloys is not consistent with the ac-
tual forming conditions. Hence, the majority of cracks in NbMoTaX
alloys are proved not hot cracks. In the case of NbMoTaTi alloy, the
cold cracks are observed due to excessive internal stress during the
cooling process. The thermal stress during cooling is also found at
the grain boundary of NbMoTaTi 0.5Ni0.5, however, it is different
from the GB phase fracture of NbMoTaTi alloy. The GB phase of
NbMoTaTi0.5Ni0.5 formed a large number of extended dislocations
under the action of thermal stress, which transformed crack defects
into point defects and strengthened the grain boundaries.
3. NbMoTaTi and NbMoTaTi 0.5Ni0.5 alloys exhibited different GB phase
compositions. The α-Ti(Nb,Mo,Ta) phase, with a close-packed hex-
agonal structure, was formed at the grain boundaries of NbMoTaTi
alloy, whereas the monoclinic Ni3Ta(Ti,Nb,Mo) main phase, FCC-
structured (TiNi)3Mo(NbTa)2 GB phase and β-Ti(Nb,Mo,Ta) GB
phase were formed at the grain boundaries of the NbMoTaTi 0.5Ni0.5
alloy.
4. Finally, the transformation mechanism of grain boundary
microcracks is proposed based on the low SFE. In the case of low
SFE GB phase, the dislocation reaction occurs preferentially to form
extended dislocations under the action of GB slip due to thermal
stress. Also, a large number of stacking fault regions consume energy
during GB slip, avoiding the separation of GB phase and main phase
and, consequently, suppressing the formation of cracks. Hence, we
have calculated the SFE of theα-Ti(Nb,Mo,Ta) phase with cold cracks
and Ni3Ta(Ti,Nb,Mo) phase without cold cracks. The result revealed
that the SFE of Ti(Nb,Mo,Ta) phase (471.4 mJ/m 2) was higher than
the SFE of Ni3Ta(Ti,Nb,Mo) phase (357.1 mJ/m 2), indicating the in-
ﬂuence of SFE on microcrack suppression.
