ABSTRACT 
Nickel-based superalloys are in great demand for harsh-service conditions involving high temperatures and 
oxidative environments. Haynes 282 stands out due to its excellent high-temperature properties and easy fab -
ricability. However, the upper operation temperature of Haynes 282 is limited due to its relatively low liquidus 
temperature. Equipped with high-fidelity density-functional theory calculations and high-throughput experi -
mentation methodology, we explored new compositional spaces that exhibit higher liquidus temperature and 
higher strength. While maintaining the manufacturability, the newly designed alloy shows improved strength 
and ductility at room temperature and better oxidation resistance up to 800 
◦
C. The new compositions showcase 
a minor change in the refractory and metalloid content can significantly impact the mechanical and oxidation 
performance of superalloys. 
1. Introduction 
The operating temperatures of Ni-based superalloys used in gas 
turbines [ 1 , 2 ] and steam turbines (such as Advanced Ultra-Supercritical 
(AUSC) power plants [ 3 – 5 ]) are restricted primarily by their creep 
behavior [ 6 ] and oxidation/corrosion resistance [ 7 ], especially in 
moisture-laden environments. We aim to address a critical part of this 
challenge, focusing on understanding the oxidation mechanisms and 
designing suitable alloy chemistries to provide the much-required 
oxidation resistance in such environments without compromising 
strength and fabricability [ 8 ]. The choice of the high-temperature alloys 
is dictated by both its oxidation resistance and mechanical behavior, 
especially creep resistance. The mechanical behavior of these materials 
can be tuned by controlling alloy microstructure in terms of the ϒ’ and 
ϒ’’ precipitation; a higher liquidus temperature also decreases the ho -
mologous temperature, thereby reducing diffusion and creep. Our cen -
tral hypothesis is that tuning the composition, in terms of selected major 
alloying elements, for higher liquidus temperatures will translate to 
lower diffusivities, which is expected to be beneficial from a creep and 
oxidation resistance standpoint. Additionally, we seek to leverage minor 
alloying elements, which migrate preferentially to the oxide scale and 
the scale/alloy interface, to improve the adhesion of the oxide layer, 
resulting in the formation of a thinner, uniform oxide scale. 
Compared to other alloys in its class, Haynes 282 (H282) [ 9 ] alloy 
has high-temperature creep strength surpassing that of Waspaloy and 
comparable to that of R-41 alloy while retaining the excellent fabric -
ability of Haynes 263 alloy [ 10 ]. H282 was shown to have improved 
tensile strength at high temperatures (yield strength of H282, Waspaloy, 
and H263 alloys at 871 
◦

respectively) and much better tensile strength retention [ 11 ] than 
Haynes 263 and Waspaloy (no drop in tensile strength after 760 
◦
C 
exposure for 1000 h for H282, while the strength of others drops at least 
5 %), while exhibiting much better ductility ( ≥ 10 % better) than that of 
R-41 alloy [ 10 ]. The favorable properties of H282 were obtained by 
balancing the amount of strengthening γ’ phase while keeping a refined 
amount of Mo refractory for solid-solution strengthening. With many 
potential high-temperature applications, H282 is a crucial candidate for 
AUSC boilers. However, the yield strength of H282 declines rapidly 
above 871 
◦
C, presumably due to the coarsening or over-aging of γ’ 
precipitates [ 10 , 11 ]. New alloy compositions that stabilize the fcc 
structure and have higher liquidus temperatures may extend the tem -
perature range of H282 and significantly increase the reliability of AUSC 
boilers and similar components. Therefore, our initial efforts focused on 
a first-principles assessment of the fcc phase with the goal of identifying

2. Methods 
The DFT-based KKR-CPA method was used to calculate total energies 
of arbitrary solid-solution alloys [ 34 , 35 ]. The KKR-CPA performs 
chemical configurational averaging simultaneously with the DFT charge 
self-consistency, which properly includes alloy-induced Friedel 
impurity-charge screening. The chemical configurational averaging 
scheme in KKR-CPA allows to handle any fractional composition up to 
the fifth digit “ 0.00001 ″ with utmost accuracy, which allows composi -
tional optimization compared to other typically used DFT approaches. 
We used the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation 
functional for solids [ 37 ] to evaluate relationship between formation 
enthalpy (E
form
) and melting temperature in Haynes family of alloys. In 
all calculations, we employed a site-centered, spherical-harmonic basis 
that includes s, p, d , and f -orbital symmetries (i.e., L
max
= 3). The 
self-consistent charge density was obtained from the Green ’ s function 
using a complex-energy contour integration and Gauss-Laguerre quad -
rature (with 24-point semi-circular mesh enclosing the bottom to the top 
of the valence states) [ 34 ]. An equally spaced Monkhorst-Pack k -space 
mesh [ 38 ] of 24 × 24 × 24 was used for Brillouin zone integrations. Core 
electrons were treated fully relativistically (with spin-orbit coupling), 
while semi-core/valence electrons were treated scalar relativistically 
(neglecting spin-orbit coupling).

3. Results and discussion 
The general alloy design approach utilized in this work consists of 
two key steps – (1) DFT-guided down-selection of best alloy chemistries 
based on phase stability, valence-electron count, and melting tempera -
ture range, and (2) detailed experimental characterization of onset 
melting, Young ’ s modulus, tensile properties, dry-air oxidation and wet- 
air oxidation of down-selected alloys. The holistic design approach 
adopted here allows us to rapidly identify alloy compositions that are 
potentially superior to commercial H282 in terms of mechanical prop -
erties and operating temperature. In the process, we also elucidate the 
role of varying alloying additions and the trade-offs incurred while 
optimizing the final alloy chemistry. 
3.1. Modeling and validation 
Our alloy-design approach is driven by two needs: (a) improving 
liquidus temperatures and (b) attaining enhanced liquidus temperature 
without sacrificing the H282 alloy architecture. Melting temperature 
correlates with cohesive energy, as seen from the universal binding 
curve for pure elements [ 39 ]. Subsequent approaches have also explored 
relations between the cohesive energies and melting temperatures for 
alloys and compounds [ 40 ]. The cohesive energy of an alloy, in turn, is 
related to the elemental cohesive energies and the alloy formation en -

Finally, in effect, we established that slight composition changes can 
bring significant underlying mechanistic changes that affect both sta -
bility and oxidation resistance of modified H282, especially in a wet-air 
environment, which eventually becomes critical in improved alloy 
performance. Alloy down-selection was only made possible by our novel 
DFT-KKR-CPA that addresses finer tuning of elemental compositions, 
unlike typical DFT approaches. This is a key necessary step of our DFT- 
driven holistic design for improving liquidus temperatures via enhanced 
thermodynamic stability, brought about by tuning the valence-electron 
concentration in such a way that mere 2 % composition change results in 
a 17 % decrease in the formation enthalpies leading to a 100 K increase 
in liquidus temperature. The wet-air oxidation experiments performed 
on DFT-selected compositions reveal rather unexpected mechanistic 
phenomena. For instance, NISA_29 shows an improvement over Haynes 
282, despite having lower amounts of the primary scale former (i.e., Cr). 
The series of experiments carried out with different alloy chemistry test 
our hypothesis that wet air oxidation is improved primarily by a com -
bination of lowering the volatilization of MoO
3, 
which disrupts the scale 
formation and dynamic segregation of Ti through the chromia scale, 
which allows the formation of passivating (in wet air) TiO
2 
islands on 
the undisrupted scale. This is evident from a somewhat poorer perfor -
mance of alloys with higher Mo and higher Cr and Ti content (indication 
of the deleterious role of MoO
3 
volatilization) coupled with a superior 
oxidation resistance of alloys with moderate Cr and Ti content but lower 
Mo. When Mo content is kept low, it enables chromia greater dynamic 
segregation and promotes the outward diffusion of Ti, leading to an 
external TiO
2
, which improves the oxidation resistance. Overall, the 
novel computationally aided design pathway shows a light for poten -
tially new discoveries even in a field of research that has seen an 
extensive volume of work for the better part of an entire century.
