Platinum alloys based on Pt–Pt 3Al for ultra-high temperature use
P.J. Hill *, N. Adams, T. Biggs, P. Ellis, J. Hohls, S.S. Taylor, I.M. Wolff

Abstract
Platinum–aluminium alloys based on the L1 2 compound Pt3Al have potential as high-strength alloys with superior environmen-
tal resistance at ultra-high temperatures. Two-phase microstructures, analogous to the nickel-base superalloys, and consisting of
the intermetallic compound Pt
3Al and the (Pt) solid solution, can be engineered to have the attributes of microstructural stability,
environmental resistance, high-temperature strength and room-temperature ductility. Pt 3Al exists as a tetragonal phase below
1290 °C, and ternary alloying is employed both to stabilise the L1 2 crystal form of Pt 3Al and as a solid-solution strengthener of
the (Pt) phase. In this investigation, the phase relations of the Pt-rich comers of eight Pt–Al–X ternary systems (X =Ru, Re, W,
Mo, Ni, Ti, Ta and Cr) are characterised. The solubility of element X in (Pt) and Pt 3Al, and the effect on the phase boundaries,
was assessed using SEM-EDS. XRD was used to assess which crystal variant of Pt 3Al was stabilised. Preliminary room-temper-
ature mechanical testing was carried out on the alloys. This assessment has been used to select the most promising systems for
further characterisation. © 2002 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

1. Introduction
The temperature limitations of modern materials re-
strict the operating performance of jet-turbine engines
and power generators. Nickel-base superalloys are cur-
rently used in these demanding applications and inno-
vative processing and alloying techniques have been
developed to increase their temperature capabilities [1].
Modern nickel-base superalloys now operate at about
90% of their melting temperature [1], which means that
further signiﬁcant improvements in the operating tem-
peratures require that a new generation of materials,
based on higher melting-point elements, be developed.
One approach is to develop alloys with analogous
microstructures to the /p103//p103/p37microstructures of nickel-
base superalloys. In such a microstructure, the face-cen-
tred cubic (fcc) matrix is strengthened by coherent
precipitates of an intermetallic compound with an L1
2
crystal structure [1]. The platinum group metals
(PGMs) are candidates for such an alloy development
programme because many of them have fcc structures
similar to that of Ni. Additionally, the PGMs have
higher melting points than Ni (Pt, Ir, and Rh melt at
1769, 2247 and 1966 °C, respectively, while Ni melts at
1443 °C) and exceptional environmental resistance [2–
5]. Research at the National Research Institute for
Metals (NRIM) in Japan has led to the development of
the so-called ‘refractory superalloys’, based on Ir and
Rh, which are among the strongest high-temperature
metallic materials evaluated to date [2,6] A similar
research programme at Mintek and the University of
Cambridge is focusing on Pt-based alloys for market-
related reasons of availability and supply [4,5]. In the
preliminary stages of this investigation, Pt–Al–X (X =
Ni, Ru, Re) ternary alloys, lying in the two-phase
(Pt) +Pt
3Al ﬁeld, were found to have potential as a
basis for development of ultra-high temperature super-
alloys [4]. In the current work, the effects of other
ternary alloying additions to Pt–Al are investigated.
The Pt-rich end of the currently accepted binary
Pt–Al phase diagram is presented in Fig. 1 [7]. Two
crystal structures have been reported for Pt
3Al. The
high-temperature form of Pt 3Al has been reported to
have an ordered fcc (L1 2) structure, while the low-tem-
perature variant has a tetragonal DO c/p37structure [7–9].
An intermediate form of this phase has also been
reported [9], but is extremely unstable and therefore
unlikely to affect the current investigation. In order to

The Pt-rich corners of several Pt –Al–X ternary sys-
tems were investigated in order to assess the effects of
composition on phase equilibrium. This investigation
had the following objectives:
1. To determine the partitioning of element X between
(Pt) and Pt

2. To assess the effects of element X on the phase
boundaries of (Pt) and Pt 3Al.
3. To determine whether a ﬁne, two-phase mixture of
(Pt) and Pt 3Al can be developed.
4. To assess, using X-ray diffraction (XRD), which
crystal structure of Pt 3Al was stabilised by the
ternary addition.
5. To make a preliminary assessment of the mechani-
cal properties of the alloys using room-temperature
hardness tests.
Ternary additions of Ru, Re, W, Mo, Ni, Ti, Ta, and
Cr were selected for this investigation. Ru, Re, W, and
Mo were expected to partition to the (Pt) phase and
therefore act as solid-solution strengthening elements,
while Ni, Ti, Ta and Cr were expected to play a role in
stabilising Pt
3Al due to evidence of X 3Al-type phases in
these systems [7].

4. Discussion
The partitioning behaviour, and the effects of all the
alloying elements on the (Pt) and Pt 3Al phase
boundaries, are summarised in Table 3.
4.1. Partitioning of element X
The ternary alloying elements investigated here can
be divided into three main groups.(1) Elements that
partition strongly to (Pt) and showed negligible solubil-
ity in Pt
3Al: Ru and Re. (2) Elements that partition
preferentially to (Pt), but showed some solubility in
Pt
3Al: W, Ni, Mo, and Cr. (3) Elements that partition
evenly between (Pt) and Pt 3Al, or showed slight prefer-
ential partitioning to Pt 3Al: Ti and Ta.

and the XRD peaks matched those of the low-tempera-

4.1.3. Elements partitioning preferentially to Pt 3Al
These elements show potential to alter the crystal
structure of Pt 3Al, and its phase boundaries. The high-
temperature L1 2 form of Pt 3Al was observed in the
Pt–Al–Ti and Pt –Al–Ta systems, con ﬁrming that
these elements affect the stability of the L1 2 form of
Pt3Al. The addition of Ta and Ti decreased the Al
content of the Pt 3Al phase relative to that in the binary
Pt–Al system. It is likely that both elements could be
substituting for Al in this phase. A decrease in the Al
content of the (Pt) phase was also observed after the
addition of Ti and Ta.

Fine two-phase microstructures, consisting of a (Pt)
matrix and discrete Pt3Al, were developed in the follow-
ing systems: Pt –Al–Ti, Pt –Al–Ta, Pt –Al–Cr, Pt –Al–

4.3. Mechanical properties
The Pt –Al–Re alloys had room-temperature hard-
ness above 430 HV 10, despite the low Re content. The
highest hardness was recorded for a Pt –Al–Ru alloy
(Pt76:Al22:Ru2), which can be attributed to solid-solu-
tion strengthening of the (Pt) solid solution by Ru, and
also to the high proportion of Pt
3Al in the alloy ’s
microstructure. The tetragonally distorted interface be-
tween (Pt) and Pt
3Al may have also affected the hard-
ness. The hardness of the Pt –Al–Ni and Pt –Al–Cr
alloys were below 400 HV 10, suggesting that only lim-
ited solid-solution strengthening is attained by the addi-
tion of these elements.
The high-temperature compression strength and oxi-
dation resistance of alloys in the Pt –Al–Ru, Pt –Al–
Re, Pt –Al–Ti, Pt –Al–Ta and Pt –Al–Cr systems are
reported elsewhere [12,13].
5. Conclusions
Ru and Re partitioned strongly to (Pt) and showed
potential as solid-solution strengthening elements.
Hardness above 430 HV
10 were recorded after heat
treatment for alloys containing only 2 –3 at.% addi-
tions. These elements did not affect the transformation
of the Pt
3Al phase in the composition range examined,
and higher order alloying will be necessary to stabilise
the L1
2 form of Pt 3Al.
Cr, Ni, Mo, and W partitioned preferentially to (Pt)
with some solubility in Pt 3Al. Cr and Mo appear to
stabilise the high-temperature L1 2 form of Pt 3Al, and
therefore show potential as ternary alloying additions.
The low-temperature form of Pt
3Al was observed in the
Pt–Al–W and Pt –Al–Ni systems, indicating that Ni
and W did not affect the stability of Pt 3Al within the
composition range examined.
Ti partitioned preferentially to Pt 3Al and stabilised
the L12 form of this phase; Ta partitioned fairly evenly
between (Pt) and Pt 3Al, and also stabilised the L1 2
form of Pt 3Al.
Fine two-phase microstructures with a (Pt) matrix
and discrete Pt 3Al were developed in the Pt –Al–Re,
Pt–Al–Ni, Pt –Al–Cr, Pt –Al–Ti and Pt –Al–Ta sys-
tems. These systems, therefore, show potential for de-
velopment of a nickel-base superalloy analogue. A
two-phase microstructure was also developed in the
Pt–Al–Ru system, with a Pt
3Al matrix and discrete
(Pt). Although such a structure is not analogous to a
nickel-base superalloy, useful high-temperature proper-
ties may be expected from such a structure.
From this investigation, Ru and Re were selected as
solid-solution strengthening elements for further study.
Ta, Ti and Cr were selected as alloying additions to
stabilise the L1
2 form of Pt 3Al. The high-temperature
compression strength and oxidation resistance of alloys
in these ﬁve systems are reported elsewhere [12,13]. Mo
