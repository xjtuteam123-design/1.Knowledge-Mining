Keywords: Ni based high temperature alloy, microstructure, alloying, mechanical property 
Abstract. We report on advanced Ni 3Al based high temperature struct ural alloys with refractory 
alloying elements such as Zr and Mo to be apllied in the fields of die- casting and high temperature 
press forming as die materials. The duplex microstructure consisting of L1 2 structured Ni3Al phase 
and Ni5Zr intermetallic dispersoids was observed to display the microstructural feature for the present 
alloys investigated. Depending on alloying elements, the volume fraction of 2nd phase was measured 
to be different, indicating a difference in solid solubility of alloying elements in the matrix γ’ phase. 
Lattice parameter of matrix phase increased with increasing content of alloying elements. In the 
higher temperature region more than 973K, the present alloys appeared to show their higher strength 
compared to those obtained in conventional supe ralloys. On the basis of experimental results 
obtained, it is suggested that refractory alloying elements have an effective role to improve the high 
temperature strength in terms of enhanced thermal stability and solid solution hardening. 
 
Introduction 
Since copper has a high electrical conductivity than aluminum, the use of copper rotor can provide 
us various benefits such as operating cost and weight saving, depending on desirable objectives in the 
field of induction motor industry[1-3]. This is because the electrical energy efficiency of an induction 
motor can be improved in terms of reduction in ohmic loss. Therefore, harmful gas emissions, which 
are directly related to global warming, can be reduced by the application of high efficiency induction 
motor equipped with copper rotor. Using the conventional die materi als such as iron-based alloys, 
however, heat checking would be on e of major issues to be covered in the copper rotor die casting. 
Heat checking is generally arising from insuffici ent thermal fatigue of die materials under cyclic 
thermal stress operating environment resulting in limited life cycle of die materials. Thus, advanced 
die materials to use them more than 1273K would be highly required if one consider copper rotor in 
the replacement of aluminum rotor in the field of induction motor manufacturing using pressure die 
casting. In the presen t work, we chose Ni 3Al(L12 structured ordered pha se) based alloy systems 
containing refractory transition metals(RTMs) such as Zr and Mo. The former intermetallic phase has 
been found to show excellent high temperature strength in terms of strength anomaly especially in the 
high temperature region. The latter alloying elements having high melting points could enhance the 
high temperature thermal stability. 
 
Experimental procedure 
The purities of starting raw materials were 99.9 at% Ni, 99.99 at% Al, 99.9 at% Fe, 99.99 at% Cr, 
99.9 at% Mo, 99.5 at% B and 99.8 at% Zr, respectively. Small alloy buttons of 30 mm in diameter 
were prepared by arc melting on a water-sealed copper hearth under an argon gas atmosphere with a 
non-consumable tungsten electrode. The button ingot s were re-melted at least eight times for 
chemical homogeneity.

Samples for chemical composition analysis, micr ostructural observation and tensile test were 
prepared by electro-discharge machining (EDM) from the arc-melted buttons. The nominal 
compositions for all of the samples studied here are listed in Table 1. X-ray diffraction (XRD) 
analysis was conducted on finely polished samples to determine the crystal structures and lattice 
parameters of constituent phases. Lattice parame ters were determined by the well-known Cohen 
method. Samples for metallographic observations were mounted and mechanically polished with SiC 
paper and Al 2O3 particles with water. Scanning elec tron microscopy (SEM) equipped with 
energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) was used in determin ing the chemical composition of 
each constituent phase. Tensile specimens with 1.5 mm x 3.0 mm cross-section and 13 mm gage 
length were prepared by EDM cut and then mechanically polished. Tensile test was carried out using 
an Instron-type mechanical testing machine in air from room temper ature to 1273K and at an initial 
strain rate of 1 x 10-3 s-1. 
 
Results and discussion 
Fig. 1 shows the XRD profiles on five samples, wh ich was prepared to inve stigate the effect of 
chemical composition that was varied by Zr and Mo content at the same Cr, Al, Fe and B content for 
phase formation behavior. The closed circles in the figure indicate peaks due to L12 structured Ni3Al 
phase, and the closed squares indicate peaks from the Ni5Zr intermetallic phase. No additional peaks 
to define any other phases are characterized in the diffraction spectra. The lattice parameters of Ni3Al 
phase plotted as a function of alloying elements and contents in the two-phase alloys investigated are 
presented in Fig. 2. The lattice parameters increa sed monotonously with increasing content of Mo. 
Interestingly, no change in lattice parameter is seen between TM-free and Zr-added alloy. This may 
correlated with the fact that Zr does not solve in the Ni 3Al matrix phase. Thus the increase of lattice 
parameter can be closely correlated with the fact that the atomic sizes of Zr and Mo being 0.161 nm 
and 0.140 nm, respectively.

It is therefore clear that Zr and Mo replace Ni with increasing Mo content and also Zr addition. This 
explanation is well consistent with the Ni-Al-Mo and Ni-Al-Zr ternary phase diagrams as reported 
previously. On the basis of phase formation result and relative atomic size consideration of Mo and Zr 
would substitute for Ni sites, thereby leading to an enlarged size of unit cell if we assume that there is 
no anisotropy in site occupation of ternary alloying elements Mo and Zr. Optical microscope images 
of various alloys to investigate the effect of alloying elements and their contents at constant Cr, Al, Fe 
and B content are shown in Fig. 3. Besides the Figure 3(a), the lightly and darkly contrasted regions in 
the figures correspond to L1 2 structured Ni 3Al based phase and Ni 5Zr intermetallic phase, 
respectively. By the SEM-EDS analysis, the matrix phase was identified to L1 2 structured Ni 3Al 
phase containing Mo, Cr, and Fe. The dispersoids formed in the inter-dendrite region was analyzed to 
Ni5Zr containing Cr, Mo Zr and Fe. Thus, Zr add ition to the present alloy causes to form an 
intermetallic dispersoid. The ultimate tensile stre ngth from room temperature to 1273K for various 
alloys is presented in Fig 4. With increasing Mo content, the ultimate tensile strength increased 
without showing a strength fluctuation in the temp erature region investigate d. Further in the alloy 
containing Zr only without alloying of Mo, ultim ate tensile strength increase largely from room 
temperature to 1273K. From these results, Mo and Zr additions are very effective to increase the 
strength not only at room temper ature but also even in the high temperature region. Concerning the 
ductility, except for the TM-free sa mple, it increased with increasi ng test temperature up to 1073K 
and then drastically decreased with further increasing temperature for the alloys studied.

The increase in ductility with increasing test temp erature can be explained by thermally activated 
diffusion process of high temperature even though yield stress of Ni 3Al increases with increasing 
temperature, this occurs until the temperature of between 873K and 1073K depending on the alloying 
elements added to the alloy and therefore resulting in decrease in ductility. This explanation seems to 
be well consistent with no strength anomaly in strength in the present alloys. Additionally, at 1273K, 
oxygen may act dominantly as the embrittling agent. Generally, embrittlement of Ni 3Al begins at 
573K in air. The mechanism for em brittlement is generally associ ated with localized high stress 
concentrations, elevated temperat ures and gaseous oxygen. While it is associated with oxygen, it 
may not necessarily be associated with oxide scales. Here, if we are considering several major factors 
to cause an influence on mechanical property, solid solution hardening, volume fraction ratio as well 
as microstructure will be emerged to describe the marked difference in mechanical property shown in 
Fig. 4. Regarding the microstructure, we have observed that there is no drastic change in 
microstructure for four alloys c ontaining Zr and/or Mo within the alloys investigated. Basically, a 
dendritic-like structure was mainta ined even though it was found to be various with a change in 
chemical composition of alloys. Therefore, the microstructural effect on mechanical property may be 
minor to show the difference in ultimate tensile st rength in this study. The in crease in strength with 
increasing Mo content for four kind s of alloys is, therefore, likely associated with the solid solution 
hardening due to elastic interaction arising from the atomic size difference because volume fraction 
of intermetallic dispersoids of the present alloy is found to be very similar. Concerning the effect of 
volume fraction, it can be well understood from the re sult that Zr-added sample showed a higher 
strength than TM-free alloy. This is due to the higher volume fraction of Ni5Zr intermetallic phase in 
Zr-added alloy. 
 
Conclusions 
Zr addition to the Ni 3Al based alloy gave rise to form a Ni 5Zr intermetallic phase, and Mo was 
partitioned into both Ni3Al matrix phase and Ni5Zr intermetallic dispersoids without forming another 
phase. Volume fraction ratio of the present alloy system is primarily dependent on Zr content but not 
Mo. The lattice parameters incr eased monotonously w ith increasing conten t of Mo in the Ni 3Al 
matrix phase. The lattice parameter was not changed with an alloying of Zr in the Ni3Al matrix phase. 
Ultimate tensile strength increases with increasing volume fraction of Ni 5Zr intermetallic phase and 
with increasing Mo content in Ni 3Al matrix phase. It is suggeste d that higher volume fraction of 
Ni5Zr phase and higher Mo content in Ni 3Al matrix phase are favored to increase ultimate tensile 
strength over the whole temperature range investigated in the present two-phase Ni 3Al/Ni5Zr 
intermetallic alloys. Ductility increased with increasing temperature up to 1073K showing the 
maximum and then decreased rapidl y for Mo and/or Zr-added alloys. The increase in ductility with 
increasing temperature can be closely associated with a thermally activated diffusion process.
