1. Introduction 
Researchers' attention has recently been drawn to FeCoNi-based high 
entropy alloys (HEAs) by their appealing mechanical and magnetic 
characteristics. Ferromagnetism is a type of magnetism linked to Fe, Co, 
Ni, and some alloys or compounds that include these elements. The 
primary objective of selecting FeCoNi-based HEAs is to study the impact 
of different alloying elements on the magnetic and mechanical proper -
ties of these materials. Different atom pairs with fixed or varying com -
positions are added to the basic alloy to improve the mechanical and 
physical properties of FeCoNi-based HEAs. The literature states that the 
basic alloy is supplemented with the different atomic elements, either 
alone or frequently in pairs. The basic alloy may be supplemented with

Feng et al. [ 5 ] examined the impact of Mn and Al additions on the

According to their modeling results, the FeCoNi alloy changes from 
having an FCC structure to a BCC structure with a B2 structure when Al

Bonds between the powders are formed during the SPS sintering 
process as a result of processes including surface activation, diffusion, 
surface melting, powder necking, and plastic deformation [ 26 ]. During 
the SPS process, powders join together due to electrical sparks and 
mechanical pressure, resulting in a denser structure. Mechanical pres -
sure strengthens the attraction between powders and decreases their 
distance. Electrical sparks generate localized explosions and heat. These 
hot spots heat surrounding powders, melting or softening their surfaces. 
The substance migrates around the powders, creating local connections 
between particles. The surface of the particle may also melt, acting as a 
strong adhesive. These factors improve the material structure and create 
local linkages. The literature claims that during the SPS process, melting 
occurs at the interparticle interactions [ 27 , 28 ]. The use of pulsed plasma 
facilitates localized melting between the particles at their points of 
contact, which is how densification is accomplished. Then, when uni -
axial pressure is applied, the molten regions flow and fill the pores.

Lattice distortion and slow diffusion effects account for the high 
temperature deformation characteristic of current HEAs. Atomic size 
mismatch, crystal structure, and the bonding preferences of the alloying 
elements in the system all play a role in lattice distortions. The signifi -
cant disparity in atomic sizes between the constituent elements is a 
significant contributor to the exceptional lattice distortion in HEAs. 
Lattice distortion generally has a considerable impact on the strength of 
HEAs, with severe lattice distortion having a notable impact on solid 
solution strengthening and dislocation resistance [ 36 , 37 ]. The intrinsic 
strength of HEA alloys is a result of high lattice friction stress, which is 
highly associated with lattice distortion [ 38 ]. Additionally, the sluggish 
diffusion effect of HEAs may slow atom diffusion [ 39 ]. Higher activation 
energies brought on the greater lattice potential energy fluctuation 
cause HEAs to diffuse slowly [ 40 ]. The misfit parameter increases in the 
present alloys as the proportion of Si rises, and the alloy with the 
greatest value is Si
25
. Thus, it can be deduced that one of the factors 
contributing to Si
25

mismatch value. The Si

which is composed of a BCC and an HCP intermetallic phase, rather than 
the FCC phase found in other alloys with a lower percentage of Si. 
Depending on the processing variables (temperature and strain rate), 
as well as the material characteristics like stacking fault energy (SFE), 
DRX and DRV occur during higher temperature deformation. SFE has an 
impact on the material's work hardening and cross-slipping of partial 
dislocations. As SFE diminishes, partial dislocation recombination be -
comes more challenging, increasing work hardening. Since low SFE 
materials have a lower critical strain for DRX, they can readily attain 
their peak stress. SFEs in HEAs are often lower than those in normal 
alloys, where they can be as low as a few mJm
 2 
[ 41 ]. Due to this, the 
stress-strain curves of the present alloys show evidence of DRX and peak 
stress, which is not surprising.

more effective in preventing recrystallization because slow diffusion of 
HEA is thought to be a factor in recrystallization resistance [ 50 ].
