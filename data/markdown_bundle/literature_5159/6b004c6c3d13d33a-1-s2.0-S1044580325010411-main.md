ABSTRACT
Ni-based alloys with high chromium content are promising candidates for harsh-environment applications due to 
their exceptional strength-ductility synergy and corrosion resistance. This study investigates the influence of Nb 
doping on microstructural evolution, mechanical properties, and corrosion behavior of Ni-40Cr and Ni-40Cr-1Nb 
alloys. The results demonstrate that Nb addition promotes the formation of the intragranular γ ″ phase. By 
reducing the stacking fault energy (SFE) of the matrix, it induces the formation of stacking faults and twins, 
which in turn act as favorable sites for accelerated γ ″ nucleation. Concurrently, this process reduces the chemical 
driving force for discontinuous precipitations (DPs) at grain boundaries, effectively suppressing the formation of 
intergranular discontinuous precipitates. The combined effects of intragranular γ ″ phase, stacking faults, twins, 
and Lomer-Cottrell locks contribute to an improved strength-ductility balance. Furthermore, Nb doping reduces 
pitting susceptibility, increases passive film thickness, promotes the enrichment of Cr
2
O
3 
and Nb
2
O
5 
within the 
film, and significantly enhances both localized and general corrosion resistance. This work provides a viable 
alloy design strategy for developing high-Cr Ni-Cr alloys with superior strength-ductility synergy and corrosion 
performance.
1. Introduction
Face-centered cubic (FCC) Ni-Cr alloys are widely employed in 
aerospace, marine, petrochemical, electric power, and nuclear in -
dustries due to their superior oxidation resistance, corrosion resistance, 
and thermal creep resistance. These properties are strongly influenced 
by the Cr content. Developing high-Cr alloys is therefore essential for 
expanding their application in demanding environments [ 1 – 3 ]. How -
ever, when the chromium content reaches approximately 33 %, two 
forms of α -Cr typically precipitate: discontinuous and continuous pre -
cipitates. Discontinuous precipitates (DPs), appearing as lamellar or 
spherical structures, nucleate and grow near grain boundaries. In 
contrast, continuous precipitates (CPs), which form as needles or 
spheres, develop within the matrix [ 4 – 8 ]. The formation of DPs at grain 
boundaries introduces a brittle phase that severely compromises the 
strength-ductility balance. Additionally, a micro-galvanic couple be -
tween the α -Cr precipitates and the matrix can initiate pitting corrosion. 
These issues significantly limit the use of high-Cr Ni-Cr alloys in harsh 
conditions [ 5 , 9 ]. Consequently, achieving a combination of high 
strength-ductility balance and excellent corrosion resistance in these Ni- 
Cr alloys remains a critical challenge, driving substantial research efforts 
into alloying strategies for the high ‑ chromium Ni-Cr system.
Simultaneously enhancing both the strength-ductility balance and 
corrosion resistance is particularly challenging due to the common 
trade-off between these properties. Yan et al. [ 10 ] demonstrated that 
0.5 wt% Nb doping in Ni-45Cr suppresses grain boundary DPs and 
promotes intragranular continuous precipitation by increasing the 
nucleation activation energy for DPs; however, the mechanical

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure
As shown in Fig. 1 a, both the Ni-40Cr and Ni-40Cr-1Nb alloys exhibit 
a dual-phase microstructure consisting of an FCC matrix and an α -Cr 
phase. The enlargement of the (111)
FCC 
diffraction peak reveals a 
distinct shift to a lower 2 θ value with the addition of Nb ( Fig. 1 b). Since a 
lower 2 θ corresponds to a larger d-spacing, this shift indicates that solute 
atoms are dissolved into the matrix upon Nb incorporation [ 13 ]. 
Furthermore, Cr-rich precipitates (DPs and CPs) are observed both 
within the grains and at the grain boundaries in both alloys ( Fig. 1 c-d). 
Notably, the addition of Nb suppresses the DP at the grain boundary and 
refines its morphology, reducing its volume fraction from 3.3 % to 1.7 % 
as quantified by EBSD ( Fig. 1 e-f).
The EBSD inverse pole figure (IPF) maps of both alloys ( Fig. 2 a-b) 
show that the microstructures consist predominantly of equiaxed grains 
and are fully recrystallized. The average grain sizes of the Nb-free and 
1Nb alloys are approximately 1.38 ± 0.05 μ m and 1.07 ± 0.07 μ m, 
respectively, as determined from the grain orientation maps and fitted 
with a normal distribution ( Fig. 2 c-d). Additionally, the 1Nb alloy has a 
higher twinning ratio (54.4 %) than the Nb-free alloy (50.9 %) ( Fig. 2 e- 
f).
The microstructure of the designed alloys was comprehensively 
characterized by TEM. As shown in Fig. 3 a, aside from DPs and CPs, no 
other nano-precipitates are present in the matrix of the Nb-free alloy. 
SAED analysis indicates that the matrix has an FCC structure, while DP 
has a body-centered cubic (BCC) structure ( Fig. 3 c). The interplanar 
spacing is 0.210 nm for the (111) planes of the FCC matrix and 0.204 nm 
for the (110) planes of DP ( Fig. 3 b). Furthermore, the HRTEM image of 
the DP/matrix interface reveals considerable lattice deformation, indi -
cating an incoherent relationship between them ( Fig. 3 b).

4. Discussion
4.1. Mechanism of Nb-mediated γ ″ phase precipitation and the 
suppression of DP
The work-hardening capabilities of these two alloys (Nb-free and 
1Nb alloys) are evaluated using strain-hardening rate curves. It is 
observed that the 1Nb alloy exhibits an increased strain hardening rate 
in the second stage compared to Nb-free alloy ( Fig. 4 b). This anomalous 
strain hardening behavior is rarely seen in other high stacking fault 
energy (SFE) alloy systems (the SFE of the Ni-40Cr alloy is approxi -
mately 75 mJ/m
2
), such as 7075Al [ 24 ] and Cu [ 25 ] alloys, indicating a 
strong correlation with the microstructural changes induced by Nb 
addition. The previous literature suggested that this anomalous phe -
nomenon is influenced by the short-range ordered structure of high-Cr- 
content Ni-Cr alloy [ 26 , 27 ]. The primary determinants influencing the 
strain hardening of FCC metals are SFE and short-range order [ 27 , 28 ].
Based on combined SEM, TEM, and thermodynamic phase diagram 
analyses, we found that introducing Nb into the Ni-Cr alloy leads to 
profound, multi-scale, and synergistic effects on its microstructure. 
Firstly, driven by a strong thermodynamic tendency (Fig. S1), Nb pro -
motes the formation of the metastable γ ″ phase, which is coherent with 
the matrix and induces intense coherency strain strengthening, serving 
as the primary source of strength enhancement ( Fig. 3 d). Secondly, the 
addition of Nb effectively reduces the SFE of the matrix [ 29 , 30 ]. This 
reduction promotes the formation of a higher density of stacking faults 
and nano-twins in the 1Nb alloy ( Fig. 3 g, i). As a result, the 1Nb alloy 
exhibits not only enhanced work hardening capacity but also abundant 
heterogeneous nucleation sites for the γ ″ phase, thereby accelerating its 
precipitation kinetics.
The synergy of these mechanisms effectively suppresses the detri -
mental DP. On one hand, the rapid and extensive precipitation of the γ ″ 
phase rapidly depletes the supersaturated solutes in the matrix,

4.3. Corrosion mechanism
This study ’ s electrochemical test results indicate that the 1Nb alloy 
displays a reduced corrosion current density, increased pitting potential, 
elevated corrosion potential, larger passivation area, and enhanced 
passive film resistance in comparison to the Nb-free alloy ( Figs. 5, 6, and 
7 ), demonstrating significantly superior corrosion resistance. The pri -
mary factor contributing to the enhanced corrosion resistance of the 1Nb 
alloy is the alteration in the microstructure and the passive films due to 
the Nb content. The polarization curves ( Fig. 5 a) demonstrate that the 
two alloys exhibit distinct passivation behavior, signifying the formation 
of a passive film. The XPS results corroborated the formation of the 
passive film ( Fig. 7 ). Furthermore, grain size and phase composition are 
critical elements affecting the material ’ s corrosion resistance [ 18 , 38 ].

4.4. Comparison of the strength-ductility and corrosion resistance with 
other alloys
Both the Nb-free and 1Nb alloys exhibit superior strength-ductility 
balance and corrosion resistance, with the 1Nb alloy demonstrating a 
more pronounced enhancement in performance ( Figs. 4, and 5 ). In 
practical manufacturing, the strength-ductility product of materials is 
frequently regarded as a significant metric; however, there is a trade-off 
between the strength and ductility of these materials [ 50 , 51 ]. E
pit 
is a 
crucial metric for evaluating the corrosion resistance of materials. 
Particularly in the Cl
 
ion milieu, an elevated E
pit 
signifies that the alloy 
surface possesses a more robust and dense passive film [ 52 , 53 ]. Prior 
research predominantly emphasizes the enhancement of either corro -
sion resistance or mechanical qualities, making it challenging to attain 
simultaneous improvement in both areas. High-Cr-content Ni-Cr alloys, 
characterized by dense DP precipitation, demonstrate elevated strength 
but compromise corrosion resistance and plasticity; FeCoCrNi high- 
entropy alloys with Mo display superior localized corrosion resistance, 
indicated by high E
pit 
values, yet their deformability is frequently con -
strained by the emergence of μ and σ phases [ 54 ]. Fig. 12 illustrates that 
high-Cr-content Ni-Cr alloys, high-entropy alloys, and conventional 
stainless steels were selected for comparison with the two designed al -
loys [ 1 , 12 , 21 , 55 – 64 ], revealing that the Nb-free and 1Nb alloys exhibit 
distinct advantages, achieving an optimal combination of corrosion 
resistance and strength-ductility balance.
5. Conclusion
This study presents a Ni-Cr-1Nb alloy that exhibits superior strength- 
ductility and corrosion resistance. We thoroughly examined its 
strengthening and corrosion mechanisms by multi-scale characteriza -
tion. The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) Trace Nb doping promotes the formation of a high-density γ ″ 
phase, Lomer-Cottrell lock structures, nano-twins, stacking faults, 
and a minor presence of DP. The synergistic effect of these 
components enhances the strength-ductility balance of the 1Nb 
alloy.
(2) The passive films generated on the two alloy systems exhibit a 
bilayer structure, including an outer layer of Cr(OH)
3 
and an 
inner layer of Cr
2

hence considerably improving corrosion resistance.
(3) Trace Nb doping serves to refine the grains, leading to a higher 
density of grain boundaries in the 1Nb alloy. This promotes the 
diffusion of metallic components, enabling the establishment of a 
continuous and stable passive coating on the metal surface, 
thereby improving corrosion resistance.
(4) Trace Nb doping diminishes the potential and height differences 
between the DP and the matrix, postpones the matrix dissolution 
process, and consequently markedly enhances the corrosion 
resistance of the 1Nb alloy relative to the Nb-free alloy.
