ABSTRACT
Cu-Cr-Zr alloy is a candidate material for the heat sink components of divertor in future fusion device, which is 
required for high strength and thermal stability simultaneously. In this study, the Cu-0.76Cr-0.23Zr (wt%) alloy 
is prepared through room temperature rolling-aging-liquid nitrogen rolling (LNR) to form fine precipitates and 
twin/matrix coexisting structures. Besides, room temperature rolling-aging (RTR) method is also adopted as the 
contrastive route. The results indicates that the twin boundary with an average width of 53 nm is achieved in 
LNR. The yield strength of LNR Cu-Cr-Zr are 638, 544, 414 and 213 MPa at 273, 473, 673 and 873 K, which are 
higher than those of RTR sample by 180, 139, 80 and 37 MPa, respectively. The higher strength is attributed to 
the high dislocation density and unique twin boundary. Then both kinds of samples are subjected to isothermal 
annealing at 500, 600, 700, 800 and 900 K for 1 h to study the softening behavior. The LNR sample always shows 
higher hardness than RTR sample. The high thermal stability is related to the newly formed precipitates (on twin 
boundaries), Σ 9 boundaries and stair-rod dislocations during the annealing process. Besides, studies of static 
recrystallization behavior suggest that the low mobility of partial dislocations and the hindrance of twin 
boundaries to dislocation motion led to a high recrystallization activation energy of 111 kJ/mol for newly 
developed Cu-Cr-Zr alloy. These findings underline the important interplay among precipitates, twin structures, 
and mechanical properties, and offer a promising design strategy for developing Cu-Cr-Zr alloy suitable for high- 
temperature applications.
1. Introduction
The heat sink material of International Thermonuclear Experimental 
Reactor (ITER) divertor requires high strength and thermal conductivity 
at operation temperature of 623 K [ 1 , 2 ]. Cu-Cr-Zr alloy is developed as 
the prime candidate for fusion divertor with its high strength and 
thermal conductivity, where Cr precipitates and dislocations contribute 
its high strength [ 3 , 4 ] and the high thermal conductivity is due to the 
low solid solubility of Cr and Zr in Cu, presenting the weak impact on the 
electron motion [ 5 ]. However, when operating at higher temperatures, 
the mechanical properties of Cu-Cr-Zr alloy are significantly reduced 
due to the coarsening of Cr precipitates and the recrystallization of 
deformed microstructure [ 6 – 8 ]. As such, there has been an increasing 
focus on how to improve the strength and stability at high temperature 
of Cu-Cr-Zr alloys.
In order to increase the strength of Cu-Cr-Zr alloys, scientists have 
tried various methods. Severe plastic deformation (SPD), such as high 
pressure torsion [ 9 ] and equal channel angular pressing [ 10 , 11 ], are 
introduced recently to obtain ultrafine grained structure, achieving high 
mechanical properties as described by Hall-Petch relationship [ 12 , 13 ]. 
Besides, the introduction of alloying elements is a common method of 
increasing mechanical properties and softening resistance of Cu alloy. 
Wang and Gong et al. [ 4 ] reported that the addition of Ti element in Cu- 
Cr-Zr alloy increased the structure stability and mechanical properties 
by hindering the nucleation of equilibrium bcc Cr-rich precipitates. By 
adding Si element in Cu-Cr-Zr alloy, Wang and Zhang et al. [ 14 ] 
discovered that the Si atoms participates in the precipitation process of 
Cr and Zr atoms, refined the precipitation particles and increased me -
chanical properties. Sun et al. [ 15 , 16 ] found that the Mg element around 
the Cr precipitate could delay the growth of the precipitate during aging 
or annealing process. Unfortunately, both the more grain boundaries 
induced by SPD and the alloying elements increase the scattering of

Low temperature deformation has been attempted on several metals 
for improving mechanical properties. For metals with different stacking 
fault energy (SFE), deformation at low temperature leads to different 
structural responses. It is widely accepted that low temperature defor -
mation has the effect of suppressing dynamic recovery and increasing 
dislocation density for high SFE metals, and thereby increasing the 
mechanical properties [ 18 ]. In the metals with medium or low SFE, the 
dislocations are restricted to slip on the glid planes at low temperatures 
and tend to form deformation twins [ 19 ]. The twin boundaries (TBs) 
also act as barriers to dislocation motion, while having a negligible effect 
on electrons motion. As a result, the mechanical properties of alloys can 
be improved by twin boundary strengthening with less impact on ther -
mal conductivity [ 20 ]. Furthermore, the stability of twin features that 
deliver high strength is crucial for the applications at high temperatures. 
From a thermodynamic point of view, twin formation reduces the total 
interfacial energy and thus increases high temperature stability, because

For Cu-Cr-Zr alloy, Cr and Zr elements are proved to have contri -
butions on the reduction of the SFE of Cu [ 24 ]. Therefore, the Cu-Cr-Zr 
alloy is considered a promising precursor with good application pros -
pects for forming twin structures. For example, Sun et al. [ 25 ] prepared 
Cu-Cr-Zr alloys consisting of twin structure by dynamic plastic defor -
mation at liquid nitrogen temperature and increased the room temper -
ature (RT) tensile strength. Li [ 26 ] manufactured Cu-Cr-Zr alloy with 
twin structure by rolling at liquid nitrogen temperature (cryorolling). 
Another approach is to add initial rolling and aging prior the cryorolling, 
which could promote the formation of twin structures and consequently 
improve the strength [ 27 ]. So far, most reported studies for twin 
strengthening Cu-Cr-Zr alloys are focused on the RT properties, with 
little attention given to the high-temperature mechanical properties and 
stability of twin structures.
In this work, Cu-Cr-Zr alloy plate were prepared by vacuum induc -
tion melting, followed by thermos-mechanical treatment which 
combined initial room temperature rolling, intermediate aging and 
finial liquid nitrogen rolling. The tensile properties range from RT to 
873 K were tested and the samples were annealed at different temper -
atures to evaluate the high temperature stability. Besides, the micro -
structure of annealed samples was investigated and the interaction 
among TBs, dislocations and precipitates were studied. Finally, the 
softening behavior for annealed specimens was discussed in the view of 
structural evolution and recrystallization kinetics.
2. Experimental procedures
Cu-Cr-Zr alloy ingot (50 kg per ingot) was prepared by vacuum in -
duction melting. The composition of the ingot was analyzed using an 
inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectrometer (ICP-AES), 
revealing a composition of 0.76 wt% Cr, 0.23 wt% Zr and residual Cu. 
The cast ingots were solution-treated (ST) at 1213 K for 4 h and im -
mediate quenched in water. Subsequently, two processing routes were 
adopted as shown in Fig. 1 . For the room temperature rolling (RTR) 
samples, the ingot was cut into 20 mm thick plates and then rolled down 
to 6 mm, achieving a total deformation of 70 % with 10 % deformation 
per pass (7 passes in total) at RT. The diameter of working rolls are 320 
mm. Then, the aging method of 673 K for 2 h were selected for the RTR 
samples to achieve maximum hardness (peak-aged state). For the liquid 
nitrogen rolling (LNR) samples, a two-step rolling process was employed 
to enhance the formation of twins in the matrix. Initially, the ingots were 
cut into 20 mm thick plates and rolled down to 12 mm, achieving a total 
deformation of 40 % with 10 % deformation per pass (4 passes in total) 
at RT. Subsequently, the deformed samples were aged at 673 K for 2 h. 
Finally, the aged samples were immersed in liquid nitrogen for 20 min 
and then rolled from 12 mm to 6 mm, resulting in 30 % total defor -
mation with 5 % deformation per pass (6 passes in total), including re- 
immersion for 5 min after each pass. The final dimensions of the RTR 
and LNR samples were 350 mm (length) × 80 mm (width) × 6 mm 
(thickness).The microstructures were observed using optical microscope 
(OM, SOPTOP) and a scanning electron microscope (SEM, Gemini SEM 
500) equipped with energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS, Esprit 
Compact) after being mechanical polished and chemical etched using a 
solution containing 5 g FeCl3, 25 mL of HCl (12 mol/L) and 75 mL of 
anhydrous ethanol. The electron back-scattered diffraction (EBSD) 
technique under a scanning electron microscope (SEM, ZEISS MERLIN 
COMPACT) was employed to characterize the microstructure. Low angle 
grain boundaries (LAGBs) and HAGBs were differentiated by a misori -

3.1. Initial microstructure
The typical OM micrographs, inverse-pole-figure (IPF) maps and

4. Discussion
4.1. High strength and stability induced by LNR
(I) TBs induced extra precipitation strengthening
Aging at 673 K for 2 h brought the hardness of the RTR sample to a 
maximum (peak-aged), while the LNR sample with 40 % deformation 
cannot reach the peak-aged state under the same aging condition. 
Therefore, given the under-aged state of the LNR sample, Ostwald 
ripening should not play a dominant role in the beginning of annealing 
process, and the alloying elements tend to segregate and precipitate. The 
TBs, formed after aging process, provide abundant fast diffusion paths 
for solute atoms, which facilitates the precipitation on the TBs ( Figs. 7 d 
and 8 d). Thence, LNR sample process more newly formed precipitates 
on TBs and smaller average diameter of Cr precipitates, which is positive 
for precipitation strengthening.
(II) Precipitates inhibit the detwinning process
Higher hardness ( Fig. 5 ) and the stable structure of LNR sample 
( Fig. 6 f) after annealing suggest that precipitation strengthening is not 
the exclusive factor determining the material performance. The high 
temperature behavior of TBs should also be taken into account. Take 
700 K annealed LNR sample as an example, the bright field image of the 
edge region of TBs is shown in Fig. 9 a. Some thin TBs with width about 
15 nm and some thick TBs with width above 50 nm were observed. For 
thin TBs, as indicated by the blue arrow, the detwinning is attributed to 
that the lattice of the twinned region has been transformed back to 
matrix stacking sequence via emitting twinning dislocations into former 
twin paths [ 44 – 46 ]. For thick TBs, red lines and red arrow outline the 
steps on TBs, which are incoherent twin boundaries in f.c.c. metals [ 45 ]. 
The detwinning can also induced by the gliding of steps along the TB 
[ 47 , 48 ]. The observed two detwinning ways are accordance with the 
detwinning models proposed by Wang et al. [ 47 ] and the observation 
results of Chen et al. [ 44 ]. In addition, many precipitates (marked by 
black cycles) were found on both thin and thick TBs, which is same as 
Figs. 7 d and 8 d. Zhang et al. [ 49 ] designed two kinds of Cu alloys ma -
terials with the randomly distributed precipitates and the precipitates on 
nanocrystalline boundaries by switching the order of aging and SPD. The 
results show that the precipitation located on grain boundaries enhances

process, when two partial dislocations on different glide planes meet 
during the annealing process, they can react to form a stair-rod dislo -

that are very resistant to climbing or sliding in either gliding planes, thus 
providing a resistive force against dislocation motion and detwinning 
through dislocation activity.

precipitates, and TBs are not merely additive; rather, significant in -

at TBs increase the precipitation strengthening. The formation of pre -
cipitates at TBs, twin junctions and stair-rod dislocations contribute to 
the stability of the TB structure, thereby sustaining high mechanical 
properties in the LNR sample at temperatures as high as 700 K.

recrystallization.
5. Conclusions
In this study, Cu-Cr-Zr alloys with a twin structure (LNR sample) 
were prepared by 40 % room temperature rolling + aging at 673 K for 2 
h + 30 % liquid nitrogen rolling. The high temperature mechanical 
properties and structural evolution of LNR were investigated through 
systematic microstructural characterizations and static recrystallization 
kinetics analysis. This study aimed to clarify the behavior of twin 
structure in the Cu-Cr-Zr system at elevated temperatures. The most 
important conclusions are summarized as follows:
(1) Liquid nitrogen rolling promotes the formation a twin/matrix 
structure with an average twin width of 53 nm in LNR sample. Besides, 
the Cr and Zr-rich precipitates are distributed in the matrix uniformly.
(2) The yield strengths of LNR sample are 638, 544, 414 and 213 MPa 
at RT, 473, 673 and 873 K, respectively, which is significantly higher 
than the sample processed by room temperature rolling + aging (RTR 
sample). The hardness curves of the samples after annealing at different 
temperatures indicates that LNR sample possesses higher stability than 
RTR sample. The LNR sample exhibits and maintains a higher hardness 
about 189.3 HV
0.2 
after annealing at temperature as high as 700 K.
(3) The improved RT strength of LNR is attributed to its high dislo -
cation strengthening and the twin boundary strengthening. After 
annealing at high temperature, the formation of new precipitates on 
twin boundaries contributes to the precipitation strengthening. Addi -
tionally, the newly formed precipitates, Σ 9 boundaries and stair-rod 
dislocation helps maintain the stability of the twin structure at high 
temperature. All of these factors play a dominant role in ensuring the 
stability of the mechanical properties of LNR sample after annealing.
(4) The recrystallization activation energy of RTR and LNR samples 
are 101 kJ/mol and 111 kJ/mol, respectively. The higher recrystalli -
zation activation energy of LNR Cu-Cr-Zr is related to the low mobility of 
partial dislocations and the hindrance of twin boundaries to dislocation 
motion.
