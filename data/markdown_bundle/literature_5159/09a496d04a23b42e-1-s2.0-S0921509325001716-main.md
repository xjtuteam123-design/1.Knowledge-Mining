ABSTRACT
Here reports a ~50 % increase in yield strength without compromising ductility by aging treatment to produce a 
Nb-modulated α " + β dual-phase microstructure. The novelty arises from the high modulus, ductile α " phase, 
which triggers a modulus misfit strengthening mechanism together with a cooperative plastic deformation 
mechanism with the β phase matrix.
Metastable β -type titanium alloys have garnered attention as load- 
bearing structural materials owing to their tunable microstructures 
and broad range of achievable mechanical properties [ 1 – 5 ]. These alloys 
can be significantly strengthened by aging-induced secondary phases 
such as α and isothermal ω phases via the typical nucleation-and-growth 
mechanism [ 6 , 7 ]. However, the effectively enhanced strength couples 
always with a significant reduction in ductility because these hetero -
geneously nucleated precipitates promote strain localization, facilitating 
crack initiation and embrittlement [ 8 , 9 ].
Besides the nucleation-and-growth mechanism, spinodal decompo -
sition provides an alternative pathway to produce two types of 
compositionally-modulated microstructures in nanoscale [ 10 ]. One is 
the spongy-like modulated microstructure, often observed in alloys with 
small lattice mismatch strains, such as Ti-Nb [ 11 ] and Fe-Cr [ 12 ] alloys. 
Another is the plate-like modulated microstructure, typically observed 
in titanium alloys with a large lattice mismatch strain exceeding about 
0.5 % such as TiZr [ 13 ] and TiMo [ 14 ] alloys. Because the spinodal 
decomposition is free of nucleation energy barriers, both types of the 
modulated microstructures are dense and uniform. These features pro -
vide advantages in enhancing mechanical properties compared to the 
coarse and heterogeneous microstructures produced by the 
nucleation-and-growth mechanisms [ 15 – 17 ]. However, embrittlement 
associated with spinodal decomposition has been frequently reported 
[ 18 ], primarily due to the limited plastic accommodation of the modu -
lated two-phase microstructure and the severe stress concentrations at 
the phase boundaries [ 19 ]. As a result, overcoming the strength-ductility 
trade-off remains a challenge from the perspective of microstructural 
design.
In our recent study on a spinodal decomposition Ti2448 alloy, we 
identified a Nb-modulated α " + β dual-phase microstructure which cou -
ples a microstructural transition from the spongy-like to the plate-like 
with a continuous crystal transition of bcc-to-hcp [ 20 ]. This is in sharp 
contrast with these previously reported alloys that typically maintain 
iso-morphology of either spongy-like or plate-like [ 10 ]. The novel 
decomposition is driven by a continuous atomic shear-and-shuffle 
mechanism along the Burgers pathway toward the formation of hcp 
crystal in the Nb-lean nanodomains [ 21 ]. This results in a 
continuously-varied lattice parameters of orth- α " phase between bcc- β 
and hcp- α phases [ 21 , 22 ]. The transition can be reversible to enable 
functional properties such as Invar [ 23 ], Resinvar [ 24 ], and Elinvar [ 25 ] 
effects across a wide temperature range of about 500 K. Most impor -
tantly, the decomposition-induced α " phase can enhance strength while 
also maintain a good ductility [ 26 ]. This is in contrast to the strength -
ening induced embrittlement of both the nucleation-and-growth and 
spinodal decomposition mechanisms. These findings suggest that the 
nanoscale modulated α " + β dual-phase microstructure offers a new 
strategy to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off. However, the 
origin of its strength-ductility synergy remains unclear and requires 
further investigation.

The initial hot-rolled Ti2448 alloy has a single β -phase microstruc -
ture ( Fig. 1 a). Our previous atom probe tomography (APT) [ 29 ] and 
small-angle neutron scattering (SANS) [ 20 ] analyses have clearly shown 
that the hot-rolled alloy has a nanoscale Nb-modulated spongy-like β 
phase microstructure due to the spinodal decomposition. An aging 
treatment at 748 K for 4 h produces a nanoscale Nb modulated plate-like 
α " + β dual-phase microstructure ( Figs. 1 and 2 ). Rietveld analysis of XRD 
pattern ( Fig. 1 a) further confirms the formation of orth- α " phase. SEM 
observations show that the aged alloy retains fine grains with sizes of 
about 5 μ m ( Fig. 1 b and c), being similar with that of the initial 
hot-rolled alloy [ 29 ]. Furthermore, the decomposition-induced α " + β 
microstructure is markedly finer and more uniform than the conven -
tional α + β microstructures. Specifically, as determined through quan -
titative analysis of the SEM microstructure, the decomposition-induced 
α " phase has a high area number density of approximately 150/ μ m

Contributed by the nanoscale modulated α " + β dual-phase micro -
structure, the aged alloy exhibits higher strength than the initial hot- 
rolled alloy with single β phase ( Fig. 3 a). Specifically, its yield 
strength increases by approximately 50 %, from 700 ± 12 MPa to 1051 
± 10 MPa, while tensile strength rises by about 30 %, from 841 ± 14 
MPa to 1109 ± 11 MPa ( Table 1 ). Interestingly, this substantial 
strengthening has no damage on ductility, as evidenced by its nearly 
constant tensile strain of 19 ± 1 % ( Fig. 3 a) and extremely high reduc -
tion of area (RA) of 75 ± 3 % ( Fig. 3 b) with fine fracture dimples 
( Fig. 3 c). Despite the mechanical softening observed in the aged alloy, 
the exceptional RA combined with high elongation at a strength level 
exceeding 1 GPa demonstrates an excellent strength-ductility synergy 
achieved by the Nb-modulated α " + β dual-phase microstructure. 
Compared to conventional β -type titanium alloys [ 38 , 39 ], the nanoscale 
Nb-modulated α " phase in the Ti2448 alloy not only enhances strength 
effectively but also preserves good ductility ( Fig. 3 d). Additionally, the 
dynamic Young ’ s modulus of the aged Ti2448 alloy is measured to be 76 
± 1 GPa, resulting in a high strength-to-modulus ratio of approximately 
1.5 %. This combination of high strength-to-modulus ratio and good 
elongation of about 19 % ( Fig. 3 e) makes the aged Ti2448 alloy suitable 
for elastic sealing and connection applications that demand large 
recoverable elastic strain and good workability.

Overall, we demonstrate a ~50 % increase in yield strength without 
sacrificing ductility in the aging-treated Ti2448 alloy. This strength- 
ductility synergy is attributed to the Nb-modulated α " + β dual-phase 
microstructure induced by phase decomposition. The high-modulus, 
ductile α " phase induces a modulus misfit strengthening mechanism 
and triggers a cooperative plastic deformation toughening mechanism. 
These findings suggest that the decomposition-induced α " + β dual-phase 
microstructure presents a promising microstructural design strategy for 
developing high-performance titanium alloys.
