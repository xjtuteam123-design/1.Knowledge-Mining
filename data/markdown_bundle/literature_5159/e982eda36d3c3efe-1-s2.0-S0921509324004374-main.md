ABSTRACT 
The comprehensive mechanical behavior of Inconel 625 alloy fabricated by laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) and 
hot isostatic pressing (HIP) was studied. Tensile experiments were performed at six temperatures ranging from 
room temperature to 1000 
◦
C to explore the effect of temperature on the mechanical properties, and the 
microstructure and fracture mechanism was investigated by combining electron backscatter diffraction, trans -
mission electron microscopy, and atom probe tomography. In comparison with the properties of conventionally 
fabricated Inconel 625 alloys, the mechanical properties at room temperature are essentially the same. However, 
the plasticity of the Inconel 625 alloys fabricated by LPBF is drastically decreased with increasing temperature, 
especially when the temperature exceeds 700 
◦
C due to the transformation from transgranular ductile fracture to 
intergranular fracture. Detailed characterization of cracks and grain boundaries (GBs) reveals significant 
segregation of non-metallic elements at GBs, which impedes the movement of GBs and decreases their strength, 
thus enhancing the intergranular embrittlement and resulting in the drastic reduction of ductility. Moreover, the 
irregular grain morphologies result in numerous GBs directional sharp changes and steps, as well as GBs triple 
junctions, in which the stress concentration is further enlarged, thus promoting the nucleation of voids and 
cracks and its associated high temperature embrittlement. The underlying mechanism of ductility deterioration 
of LPBF-fabricated Inconel 625 alloy at elevated temperatures would provide important insights into its per -
formance at high temperatures. 
1. Introduction 
Inconel 625 is a nickel-based superalloy strengthened primarily by 
the addition of chromium, molybdenum and niobium [ 1 , 2 ]. It is widely 
utilized in the aerospace, marine, chemical and petrochemical sectors 
due to its excellent high-temperature mechanical properties and corro -
sion resistance [ 3 , 4 ]. However, it is difficult to meet the requirements of 
integrated molding of complex parts for Inconel 625 alloy by traditional 
material processing, such as powder metallurgy, casting, forging and 
machining, especially for large size and precision components [ 5 ]. 
Moreover, these conventional manufacturing processes are usually 
costly and time-consuming for complex components [ 6 ], which severely 
restrict the applications of Inconel 625 alloy in advanced industrial and 
engineering sectors. 
Additive manufacturing is a unique manufacturing technology, 
which is different from the material removal method in conventional 
manufacturing processes [ 7 ]. Additive manufacturing produces the 
desired workpiece by continuous material addition, shaping and solid -
ification of feedstock. This approach results in a significant reduction in 
material waste and concurrently optimizes part of processing time [ 8 ]. 
Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) is a kind of additive manufacturing 
technology based on powder bed melting technology, which has vast 
industrial applications and attracted considerable attention due to its 
great advantages compared to conventional manufacturing processes 
[ 9 ]. According to the model established by computer-aided design 
(CAD), metal parts with required complex geometric shapes can be

LPBF has been extensively employed in the manufacturing processes of 
metallic parts made of stainless steels, nickel-based superalloys, 
aluminum and titanium alloys, whose mechanical properties are 
essentially comparable to or even superior to those fabricated through 
conventional manufacturing processes [ 13 – 17 ]. 
The high temperature gradient and rapid solidification in the pro -
cesses of LPBF result in the build-up of thermal stresses and the occur -
rence of element segregation. Therefore, the metallic parts fabricated by 
LPBF generally exhibit pores, fine columnar grain structures and micro- 
segregation, all of which can cause severe degradation of mechanical 
properties [ 18 ]. Many investigations have been done to solve the 
fabrication flaws and improve the performance of LPBF manufactured 
materials, most of which focused on the process parameters of LPBF and 
post-processing [ 19 – 23 ]. Huang et al. [ 24 ] investigated the effect of the 
scanning speed (600 – 1200 mm/s) of a laser on the mechanical proper -
ties of Inconel 625 and found at an optimized scanning speed of 900 
mm/s, the fabricated alloy has a higher density and ultimate tensile 
strength. Christopher et al. [ 25 ] studied the effect of laser energy on the 
microstructure of manufactured parts and found that the part density 
increases monotonously with laser energy in the employed energy 
range. Kreitcberga et al. [ 26 ] showed an isotropic equiaxed micro -
structure of LPBF Inconel 625 alloy induced by heat treatment and hot 
isostatic pressing (HIP) with proper process parameters, which enhances 
the elongation at room temperature. Furthermore, Marchesea et al. [ 27 ] 
compared the microstructure and mechanical properties of LPBF Inconel 
625 alloy with different post-treatments, including direct aging, solution 
treatment (ST) and ST + aging, and found that the ST and ST + aging can 
change the microstructure of Inconel 625 alloy and increase its 
plasticity. 
It is noteworthy that the plasticity of LPBF manufactured Inconel 625 
alloy decreases with the increase in temperature, which is opposite to 
the case of Inconel 625 alloys fabricated by conventional machining 
processes and is generally attributed to the weakening of grain bound -
aries (GBs) at high temperature [ 28 – 31 ]. This results in the extremely 
poor high temperature ductility of LPBF-manufactured Inconel 625 
alloy. However, there are no unified interpretations of the mechanism 
that causes the weakening of GBs. Xiao-An Hu et al. [ 32 ] investigated 
the high temperature mechanical properties of LPBF Inconel 625 and 
attributed the loss of plasticity to the inhomogeneous distribution of 
grain size and the presence of carbides at grain boundaries. Lee et al. 
[ 33 ] speculated that the presence of bimodal-sized oxide particles in the 
matrix suppresses twin formation by impeding the extension of stacking 
faults during tensile deformation at 700 
◦
C and the high dislocation 
hardening of grain interior region causes GB embrittlement. Although 
progress has been made in a preliminary understanding of the high 
temperature plasticity loss in LPBF Inconel 625 alloy, the associated 
mechanism remains unclear and lacks robust evidence. 
In the current study, the tensile properties of LPBF Inconel 625 alloy 
heat treated by HIP and ST were studied at six different temperatures 
ranging from 23 
◦
C (room temperature, RT) to 1000 
◦
C to reveal the 
effect of temperature on the mechanical properties of LPBF Inconel 625 
and underlying mechanism for the plasticity loss at high temperature. 
The deformation microstructure, fracture morphologies and cracks were 
characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), electron back -
scatter diffraction (EBSD) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). 
The elemental distribution at GBs was also measured by atom probe 
tomography (APT). The experimental results reveal that the fracture 
behavior of LPBF Inconel 625 transforms from transgranular ductile 
fracture to intergranular fracture with increasing temperature, resulting 
in the drastic loss of ductility at high temperature. Energy dispersive X- 
ray spectroscopy (EDX) and APT results display the enrichment of O, Si, 
C and B at cracks and GBs, demonstrating the effect of segregation of 
non-metallic elements at GB on the plasticity loss at high temperature. 
This work demonstrates the importance of GB segregation of non- 
metallic elements, which would provide crucial insights into the high 
temperature performance of LPBF Inconel 625 alloy.

3.1. Microstructures of AB and HIP + ST LPBF Inconel 625 alloys 
Fig. 1 (c) presents the XRD patterns of both AB and HIP + ST LPBF 
Inconel 625 alloy. It can be observed that both AB and HIP + ST Inconel 
625 alloy have a single face-centered cubic (FCC) structure, which is 
mainly the γ -Ni solid solution. Notably, there are no discernible 
diffraction peaks of δ -phase (Ni
3
Nb), Laves-phase (NbCr
2
), or carbides in 
the XRD patterns, which is due to their low volume fractions and small 
sizes. In addition, all of the diffraction peaks shift towards the higher 2 θ 
side after heat treatment, as shown in Fig. 1 (c), indicating the decrease 
of the lattice parameter. This can be attributed to the stress relief 
induced by HIP + ST, which has also been observed in previous studies 
[ 11 , 39 ]. The lattice parameters calculated from the (111) diffraction 
peak are 3.5929 Å for AB sample and 3.5524 Å for the HIP + ST sample, 
respectively. Table 1 shows the chemical composition of the HIP + ST 
LPBF Inconel 625 alloy measured by ICP-AES and C, H, O, N analyzer, 
which is consistent with other Inconel 625 alloys prepared by conven -
tional manufacturing and additive manufacturing techniques [ 40 – 43 ]. 
The inverse pole figures (IPFs) of different sections of the AB and HIP 
+ ST samples are shown in Fig. 2 (a) and (b). The microstructures of the 
AB specimen on three different planes exhibit distinct variations, with 
the YZ plane revealing a typical melt pool structure. The hardness values 
of the three planes also demonstrate anisotropic behavior, with hardness 
values of 293 HV, 295 HV, and 311 HV for the XY, XZ, and YZ planes, 
respectively. Due to the HIP + ST treatment, recrystallization occurs 
[ 26 , 44 ], leading to a more uniform microstructure. For the HIP + ST 
sample, three different sections exhibit similar microstructure charac -
teristics, which are composed of irregular equiaxed grains with sizes 
ranging from 0.90 μ m to 177.44 μ m. Most of grains are interlaced with 
each other, leading to quite irregular and zigzag GBs, as shown in Fig. 2 
(c) and its inset. As compared with Inconel 625 alloy fabricated by 
conventional manufacturing processes [ 45 , 46 ], the HIP + ST LPBF 
Inconel 625 alloy in the current study displays quite irregular mor -
phologies of grain and GBs, as shown in Fig. 2 and Fig. S2 of the Sup -
plementary Materials, which are also observed in many other studies of 
heat-treated LPBF Inconel 625 alloys [ 26 , 32 , 40 ]. The average grain sizes 
for the three sections are 24.01 μ m (XY plane), 23.90 μ m (XZ plane) and 
24.13 μ m (YZ plane), respectively. No significant textures are present for 
the three sections. The Vickers hardnesses of the three different sections 
are also essentially same, which are 208 HV, 206 HV and 210 HV for XY, 
YZ and XZ sections, respectively. The sample density significantly 
increased after HIP and ST treatment, as shown in Fig. S3 of the Sup -
plementary Materials, and the corresponding porosity decreased from 
4.29 % to 1.64 %. This demonstrates the influence of HIP and ST 
treatment on the microstructure of the LPBF Inconel 625 alloy [ 26 ].

4. Discussion 
The above experimental results display that although the YS and UTS 
of the LPBF Inconel 625 alloy experiencing HIP + ST are similar to that 
of conventional Inconel 625 alloy, the EL is much lower when the 
temperature is higher than 700 
◦
C due to the transformation from 
transgranular ductile fracture to intergranular fracture. The micro -
structural characterization and element distribution analyses show that 
the volume fractions of precipitates are quite low for both the HIP + ST 
sample and tensile tested samples, which suggests that the effect of 
precipitates on the plasticity loss can be excluded. The microstructure of 
the HIP + ST specimen also does not exhibit an inhomogeneous distri -
bution of grain sizes. Therefore, the high temperature embrittlement of 
Inconel 625 alloy is most likely induced by the segregation of non- 
metallic elements at GBs based on the characterization results of frac -
ture morphologies and elemental distributions. 
Many investigations have studied the effect of GB segregation of non- 
metallic elements on the properties of GBs and the mechanical proper -
ties of alloys [ 75 – 79 ]. For example, Jae Bok Seol et al. [ 80 ] studied the 
effect of addition of B on the microstructure and the mechanical prop -
erties of FeCoCrMnNi high entropy alloy and also found the segregation 
of B at GBs, which is believed to enhance grain boundary cohesion and 
retard the movement of GBs. Since the GBs movement is suppressed by 
the segregation of B, the addition of B generally results in grain refine -
ment [ 80 , 81 ]. Previous studies have revealed that the increased plas -
ticity of conventional Inconel 625 alloys at high temperature is mainly 
attributed to the stress relaxation induced by dynamic recrystallization 
(DRX) and the Inconel 625 alloys experienced apparent grain refinement 
under DRX [ 82 , 83 ]. Therefore, it can be expected that the suppression of 
GB movement induced by the segregation of B at GBs would cause the 
drastic loss of plasticity of HIP + ST LPBF Inconel 625 alloy at high 
temperature. Furthermore, Xiao Huang et al. [ 84 ] investigated the effect 
of GBs segregation of B on the weldability of Inconel 718 alloy and found 
that the GBs segregation of B results in microcracks in the heat affected 
zones. It was assumed that B segregation on the GBs changes the inter -
facial energy, which subsequently affects the crack initiation and 
propagation energies [ 84 ]. Considering the maximum concentration of 
B at GB can reach 1.33 at. %, which is much higher than that of other 
non-metallic elements, it can thus be inferred that the effect of B 
segregation at GBs is probably predominant for the plasticity loss of the 
HIP + ST LPBF Inconel 625 alloy at high temperature. 
The effect of O on the high temperature embrittlement of Ni based 
superalloys has also been widely studied. Since no oxides are present at 
GBs, the influence of oxygen on the high temperature plasticity should 
be through the mechanism of gas phase embrittlement [ 85 ]. On the one 
hand, GB are pinned by O and GBs sliding is suppressed, thus causing 
embrittlement in the samples. On the other hand, GBs are weakened by 
adsorption of O and cracks can be initiated more easily by the stress 
concentrations ahead of dislocation pile-ups at GBs [ 85 ]. To further 
demonstrate the effect of O and reveal its sources, the concentrations of 
oxygen of the HIP + ST specimen and tensile tested specimens at 900 
◦
C 
were measured by oxygen and nitrogen hydrogen analyzer and 
compared with that of commercially manufactured Inconel 625 alloy, 
whose plasticity exhibits an increment with rising temperatures [ 46 ]. 
The oxygen concentration of HIP + ST LPBF Inconel 625 alloy is 5.5 
times higher than that of the commercial sample, which is 0.028 wt% 
and 0.0051 wt%, respectively. This supports the effect of oxygen on the 
plasticity loss of LPBF Inconel 625 alloy in the current study. The con -
centration of oxygen increases by ~10 % after tensile test at 900 
◦
C, 
which should be induced by the penetration and diffusion of ambient 
oxygen. The higher concentrations of oxygen have been found in many 
alloys prepared by additive manufacturing, including stainless steels 
[ 86 , 87 ], titanium alloys [ 88 ], aluminum alloys [ 89 ], nickel alloys [ 90 ], 
etc, which generally originate from raw powders and manufacturing

demonstrating that the effect of ambient oxygen is not significant and 
the high temperature embrittlement of HIP + ST LPBF Inconel 625 alloys

characteristics. 
The effect of segregation of Si and C at GBs is believed to be similar to 
that of B [ 75 , 76 , 95 ]. The atomic interactions between these 
non-metallic elements and principal metallic elements are apparent 
characteristics of covalent bonds, including high strong bond strength 
and significant directionality. Therefore, the atomic sliding is sup -
pressed, which makes the movements of GBs difficult [ 75 , 96 ]. 
Moreover, if the concentrations of segregated elements are high, the 
interactions between segregated elements become dominant, which 
would decrease the strength of GBs and facilitate the nucleation and 
propagation of cracks [ 97 , 98 ]. Considering the four non-metallic ele -
ments display apparent GB segregation, the total concentrations of 
segregated elements at GB in the HIP + ST LPBF Inconel 625 alloy are 
higher than those of conventional alloys [ 28 , 99 , 100 ]. Thus, the segre -
gation of non-metallic elements at GB could simultaneously cause the 
suppression of GB movement and decrease the strength of GBs. 
Besides the segregation of non-metallic elements at GBs, the quite 
irregular and zigzag GBs, as shown in Fig. 2 (b) and (c), should also be 
one of the main mechanism for the plastic loss at high temperature. 
Previous investigations have pointed out that GBs triple junctions and 
any irregularities, such as grain boundary directional changes and steps, 
will enlarge stress concentrations and the centers of local stress con -
centrations will serve as preferential nucleation sites of voids and cracks 
[ 86 ]. Fig. 15 shows the SEM images and corresponding KAM maps of the 
HIP + ST LPBF Inconel 625 alloy tensiled to the strains of 5 % ( Fig. 15 (a, 
c)) and 10 % ( Fig. 15 (b, d)) at 900 
◦
C. Numerous voids are located at 
GBs triple junctions, especially between twin boundaries and general 
grain boundaries, as indicated by red circles. The nucleations of voids 
are accompanied with the stress concentrations, as shown in Fig. 15 (c) 
and (d). This demonstrates the effect of irregular GBs on the plasticity 
loss of the HIP + ST LPBF Inconel 625 alloy at high temperature. The 
above experimental results and discussion indicate that controlling 
segregation of nonmetallic elements at GBs and tailoring GB morphol -
ogies are important aspects that might improve the plasticity of HIP + ST 
LPBF Inconel 625 alloy.

5. Conclusions 
In this study, the tensile properties of the LPBF Inconel 625 alloy 
after heat treatment via HIP + ST were investigated at different tem -
peratures ranging from room temperature to 1000 
◦
C. Comprehensive 
characterization of microstructure and chemical composition of AB and 
HIP + ST LPBF Inconel 625 samples were performed by SEM, EBSD, 
TEM, and APT to reveal the key mechanism responsible for the loss of 
ductility with increasing temperature. The summarized findings are as 
follows. 
(1) The microstructure of LPBF Inconel 625 alloy specimen subjected 
to HIP and ST displays equiaxed grains with an average grain size 
of 24 μ m; the morphologies of grains and GBs exhibit apparent 
irregularities. The YS, UTS and TEL at relatively low tempera -
tures are similar to those of conventional Inconel 625 alloys, but 
the ductility is much lower when the temperature is higher than 
700 
◦
C. 
(2) Metallic elements are depleted at GBs except for Mo and Nb, the 
detected four non-metallic elements, including B, C, O, Si, are 
enriched at GBs. The segregation behavior at GBs is enhanced 
after tensile test of high temperature. 
(3) The segregation of non-metallic elements at GBs suppresses the 
GB sliding and decreases the GB strength, thus promoting the 
nucleation and propagation of cracks and reducing the ductility 
of LPBF Inconel 625 at high temperature. Moreover, the irregular 
grain morphologies and GBs provide preferential nucleation sites 
of cracks due to the stress concentration, which is also responsible 
for the high temperature embrittlement of the HIP + ST LPBF 
Inconel 625 alloy.
