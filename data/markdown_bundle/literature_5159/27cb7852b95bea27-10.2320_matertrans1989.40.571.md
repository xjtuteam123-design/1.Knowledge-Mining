Materials Transactions, JIM, Vol. 40, No. 7 (1999), pp. 571 to 577 Mechanical Properties of Nb3Al-Base Intermetallics Prepared by a Ceramic Mold-HIP Process Katsushi Kusaka*, Michihiko Fujine**, Michio Okabe* ** and Hiroshi Endo* ** *Daido Institute of Technology, Nagoya 457-8531, Japan * *Japan Ultra-high Temperature Materials Research Center, Gifu 507-0801, Japan ** *R&D Lab. , Daido Steel Co., Ltd., Nagoya 457-0811, Japan The Nb3Al-type intermetallic is expected as a candidate for high-temperature structural materials because of its high melting point and relatively lower density, though poor in plastic workability at room temperature. Here the Ceramic Mold-HIP process was employed to fabricate the NNS compacts of brittle Nb3Al intermetallic and Nb3Al-base com- posites. Complex-shape compacts such as blades and pyramids were fabricated without any cracks by adjusting the chemical composition of the Nb3AI-base alloy to have high compressive fracture strength at room temperature. High- temperature strength in the range of 1873 to 2073 K was evaluated by compressive and tensile tests. The maximum compressive strength at 2073 K was 151.4 MPa for Nb-20 at%Al-26 at%Mo alloy, while the corresponding maximum tensile strength was only 12.9 MPa for the Nb-19.3 at%AI-44.8 at%Mo alloy. The stram rate sensitivity of compressive stress at high temperatures was also evaluated using the strain rate conversion method. Further, the 5.7% tensile elongation at room temperature was achieved by Nb-25 at%-35 at%Mo plus Nb foil composite, even though the Nb3Al-base alloy itself is brittle at room temperature. Introduction Strong demands for aircraft capable of higher speed and with improved fuel emciency have led to increasingly severe environment for its engine parts. Development of next-generation aircraft engines has intended for use of the aerospace propulsion system exceeding the high-tem- perature performance of superalloys and refractory al- loys. Accordingly, material research has been focused on intermetallic compounds, ceramics, C/ C composite, etc. Some intermetallic compounds have high melting point and low density and hence, can be to offer ductility superior to that of ceramic as a candidate for high-temperature structural materials. In particular, Nb3Al intermetallic?) has the high melting point (2233 K) and relatively low density (7.3 Mg/m3), while its poor ductility and machinability at room temperature man- date urgent development of NNS (Near Net Shape) parts production technology. The authors have already proceeded with R&D study(4) on the specific technique for the production of NNS parts of Nb3Al using powder metallurgical method. This technique is called the Ceramic Mold-HIP process(S), consisting of the lost wax process to fabricate a ceramic mold into which Nb3Al powder is packed and the HIP process to consolidate the ceramic mold (filled up by Nb3Al powder) through the secondary-pressure ceramic media. In the present paper, the authors deal mainly with the mechanical properties and their relationships with proc- ess parameters of Nb3Al intermetallics and Nb3Al-base composites. II. Experimental Procedure Nb3Al-base alloy powder used was mostly spherical with a particle size under 60 mesh produced by the Plasma Melting-Gas Atomization (PMGA) method(6). In some cases, commercial NbA13, Mo, W, Nb and the other bcc metal powder and further, Y203, NbC and the other ceramic fines were added to the Nb3Al-base powder and then were mixed for 1.8 ks in a V-type mixer. The Nb3Al is chemically active and so, Y203 powder highly stable thermodynamically(4) was used to suppress oxide con- tamination during HIP process as the inner layer of the ceramic mold directly contacting the Nb3Al powder and also, as the secondary-pressure media. On the other hand, a high-purity A1203 granular powder was used as the outer layer of the ceramic mold. Figure 1 is a schematic diagram of the Ceramic Mold- HIP process(4). The ceramic mold with the Y203 inner layer and A1203 outer one was produced by the Lost Wax Process(7). The Nb3Al-base powder was packed into this ceramic mold and put in a stainless steel can (114 dia. x 210 mm) and finally surrounded by the Y203 pow- der for the secondary-pressure media. The metal can was sealed under vacuum and treated by HIP (Hot Isostatical Pressing) system in Japan Ultra-high Temperature

Conclusions The Nb3Al-base compacts and composites were fabri- cated mainly by the Ceramic Mold-HIP process and mechanically examined and evaluated as follows: (1) The chemical compositions of the Nb3Al-base al- loys insensitive to cracking were elucidated and allowed the fabrication of complex-shaped parts free of cracks, using the HIP-NNS powder metallurgical technique as stated earlier. (2) It was found that the effective means for im- proving the high-temperature strength are at first to raise the melting point of matrix alloy by Mo, W and other bcc metal (ingredient) and secondly to make composite structure by addition of Y203, NbC and other stable ceramics, and finally to induce crystal grain-coarsening by optimum heat-treatment such as at 2073 K. (3) The highest compressive and tensile strength at 2073 K were 151.4MPa for the Nb-20 at%Al-26 at%Mo alloy and 12.9 MPa for Nb-19.3 at%Al-44.8 at%Mo one (strength ratio SO. 1), respectively. (4) All the Nb3Al-base alloys were brittle at room temperature, while the composite materials consisting of Nb3AI-base alloy plus Nb foil showed room-tempera- ture tensile elongation of 5.7%. In the near future, Nb3Al and other intermetallics are expected to make a remarkable progress in terms of al- loys and composite material, with the new NNS shape- making process and the many data base for alloy design.
