1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys are widely applied in gas turbine engines 
because of their excellent mechanical resistance in high-temperature 
and high-stress environments [1 – 3] . The Ni-Al binary system is tradi -
tionally used to form L1
2 
structure Ni
3

Ni ( γ ) [4,5] . In addition to the L1
2 
γ’ precipitation strengthening phase, 
the ordered-fcc D0
22

I4/mmm ) is also exploited to further improve the strengthening ability. 
The morphology, coarsening kinetics, and strengthening effect of the 
D0

3

Ni-based superalloys that are reinforced mainly by γ’’ usually have a 
high mechanical strength level, which depends on the structure of the 
unit cell of γ’’ . For the three slip directions on the (111) slip surface of γ’ , 
each direction of superdislocation of γ’ only requires two a/2 < 100 >
dislocations. Among the three slip directions on the (111) slip surface of 
γ’’ , only one direction allows two a/2 < 100 > dislocations to pass, and 
superdislocations in the other two directions must include four a/2 <
100 > dislocations [9] . Clearly, the number of directions in which dis -
locations can pass through γ’’ decreases, which can be regarded an 
improvement in the ability to hinder dislocations. In addition, γ’’ is 
coherent with γ’ , and both are coherent with γ . However, the lattice 
mismatch between γ’’ and γ is larger than that between γ’ and γ , which 
results in a larger coherent strain and produces a larger strain energy. 
Thus, a large strain field around γ’’ effectively hinders dislocation to 
strengthen alloys.

To develop a stable and suitable microstructure for Ni-based super -
alloys, computational design based on the CALPHAD method has been 
extensively used. All equilibrium phase fractions, solvus temperatures, 
solidus temperatures, lattice mismatches, and corrosion resistances can 
be obtained using CALPHAD calculations [19 – 23] . High-accuracy 
microstructure prediction can help researchers precisely control mate -
rial composition. Especially for multiple-component Ni-based superal -
loys, the accuracy of the statistical empirical formula [24] has difficulty 
meeting the requirements of material design, whereas CALPHAD can 
accurately calculate the equilibrium phase composition. Meanwhile, the 
use of autoprogramming techniques that are based on the CALPHAD 
method to search for novel materials in expansive material space has 
grown, such as Automatic Execution and Extraction Tasks (AEET), 
which is a Python-based program [25] . Therefore, it is feasible to use the 
CALPHAD method to design novel Ni-based superalloys that are rein -
forced by stable γ’ + γ’’ precipitation. 
In the present work, the CALPHAD method is utilized to develop a γ’ 
+ γ’’ precipitation system that has a stable structure, a high solvus 
temperature, and excellent compatibility with Cr. Then, a high- 
throughput search is performed based on key material criteria to 
design novel Ni-based superalloys that are reinforced by γ’ + γ’’ and 
have the advantages of a sufficient precipitation strengthening phase, a 
small harmful phase, a high solvus temperature, excellent processabil -
ity, and high corrosion resistance. The objective of this work is to 
develop novel high-strength and long-term high temperature service Ni- 
based superalloys that are reinforced by γ’ and γ’’ . In addition, the high- 
throughput CALPHAD search method based on key material criteria can 
provide design guidance to reduce the experimental cost and time 
consumption.

2.2. High-throughput search 
Ni-based superalloys usually have distinct and significant material 
characteristics. According to these characteristics, material design 
criteria can be used to evaluate high-quality Ni-based superalloys. 
2.2.1. Superalloy phase composition 
The mole fraction of the precipitation strengthening phase is a basic 
indicator of the mechanical properties of superalloys. For example, the 
creep life initially increases and subsequently decreases with increasing 
precipitation strengthening phase [30] . Z. Zhu and R.C. Reed proposed a 
design criterion for single crystal superalloys, namely, a 50% 50% 
mixture of the γ and γ’ phases [20] . However, polycrystalline superal -
loys are less dependent on precipitates than single crystal superalloys 
[30] . For traditional γ’ + γ’’ precipitation strengthening Ni-based su -
peralloy IN718, the precipitation phases are 13.5% γ’’ and 4% γ’ at 
650
◦
C by CALPHAD. Synthetically, the γ’ and γ’’ contents of selected 
alloys need to exceed those of IN718. γ is still the matrix phase; hence, 
the content of γ needs to be more than 50%. Furthermore, the use of 
many transition-metal elements may cause superalloys to present 
harmful phases and, thus, destroy the high-temperature mechanical 
properties. The harmful phases include topologically close-packed (TCP) 
phases and some geometrical close-packed (GCP) phases. The TCP 
phases, such as σ , μ and Laves, are brittle phases that will become crack 
formation points. Similarly, some GCP phases, such as δ -Ni
3
Nb and 
B2-NiAl, are incoherent with the matrix; hence, they have no strength -
ening effect and become bases of crack nucleation. Menou proposed that 
a calculated content of less than 6% in the actual long-term exposure at 
the same temperature produces a very small TCP phase [21] . The mole 
fraction of harmful phases (P
h

1%. All phase fraction design criteria at 1000

4. Conclusion 
Ni-Al-V-Nb-Cr systems are potential research systems for the devel -
opment of novel Ni-based superalloys that are reinforced by γ’ and γ’’ 
through CALPHAD calculations. Ni-Al-V-Nb systems have a more stable 
γ’’ than Ni-Al-Nb systems and a higher solvus temperature of γ’’ than Ni- 
Al-V systems. In addition, Ni-Al-V-Nb can enable alloy systems to 
maintain microstructural stability under high Cr addition conditions by 
experimental validation, which is beneficial for improving corrosion 
resistance. Through high-throughput calculation for Ni-Al-V-Nb-Cr, 13 
alloys that have a sufficient precipitation strengthening phase, a small 
harmful phase, a high solvus temperature, excellent processability, and 
high corrosion resistance are selected. By analyzing the effects of Nb and 
V on the activity of Cr and the precipitation phase characteristics, V 
forms γ’’ and Nb controls the precipitation phase and the activity of Cr. 
The design methods can provide design guidance for the development of 
novel Ni-based superalloys that are reinforced by γ’ and γ’’ .
