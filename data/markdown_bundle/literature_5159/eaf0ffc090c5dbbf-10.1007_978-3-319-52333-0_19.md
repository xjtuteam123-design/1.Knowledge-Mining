Abstract The high cycle fatigue performance tests for nickel-based alloy
HAYNES282 at room temperature, 700 and 760 °C had been carried out. The
optical microscopy (OM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission
electron microscopy (TEM) were used to observe the change features of mi-
crostructure and fracture. The results shows that the precipitation of c′ phase which
makes alloy has excellent high cycle fatigue performance is a guarantee of high
strength of the alloy, and fatigue strength reduces slowly with the temperature
increasing. The fracturing pattern was in monophyletic fatigue source, and extended
in the form of cleavage and quasi cleavage and had obvious river pattern charac-
teristics. At the same time, the fatigue cracks propagate in the form of dislocation
slip, and the coarsening of c′ hinder the dislocation sliding and climbing as the
temperature rise, which improved the fatigue strength of the alloy.

Introduction
Advanced ultra-supercritical (hereinafter referred to as A-USC) power plants with
steam temperature of 700 °C is a new technique developed recently for its high
efﬁciency, low emission, easy load regulation and high stability, and become one of
the main research directions of the next generation Coal- ﬁred power generation.
The development of steam turbine of supercritical thermal power generator makes
the claim for the capability of rotor material with the improvement of steam
parameter, such as the thermal stability, fatigue, creep rupture strength, oxidation

A-USC is the development of the high-temperature rotor materials [ 1, 2].
HAYNES® 282® alloy is a new, wrought, gamma-prime strengthened superalloy
developed for high temperature structural applications, especially in aero and
land-based gas turbine engines. It possesses excellent comprehensive properties of
creep strength, thermal stability, weldability and fabricability. Excellent creep
strength in the temperature range of 1200 –1700 °F (649 –927 °C) must be due to
the relative low volume fraction of the strengthening gamma-prime phase which
has outstanding resistance to strain-age cracking (normally a problem with super-
alloys in this creep strength range) in HAYNES282 alloy. Additionally, slow
gamma-prime precipitation kinetics allow for the alloy to have excellent ductility in
the as-annealed condition. Consequently, HAYNES282 alloy exhibits superior
weldability and fabricability [ 3–7]. Researches on HAYNES282 superalloy are
mainly about heat treatment process, steam oxidation, weldability and fatigue
performance, creep rupture strength and so on [ 8–11]. The high cycles fatigue
(hereinafter referred to as HCF) tests of HAYNES282 at room temperature, 700 and
760 °C were performed in our work. And the fatigue fracture and microstructure of
sample were characterized by OM, TEM and SEM, which provides some reference
for the selection of rotor material of A-USC at 700 °C and above.
Samples Preparations and Test Methods
Materials
HAYNES 282 superalloy rod was selected for fatigue tests, the chemical compo-

Method of HCF Tests
The tests were carried out according to the Chinese standard GB/T 3075 “metal
fatigue test axial force control method ”, using reversed loading (stress ratio R = −1)
with sine waveform. The test frequency (symbol f) was in the range of 130 –150 Hz
and the fatigue strength reference cycles were 1 /C2 107. Test temperature was 25,
700 and 760 °C respectively. The test bars were heated by resistance furnace, and
the temperature was closed-loop controlled with three sectionalized thermocouple
to ensure the temperature ﬂuctuation range of specimens was within 2 °C. After
heated to the speci ﬁed test temperature, the specimens were held for 30 min. The
dimensions of fatigue specimens were shown in Fig. 1, and the main test equip-
ments were shown in Table 2.

Observation of the Fatigue Fracture
According to the fractography observation as showed in Figs. 4, 5 and 6, the fatigue
cracks of HAYNES282 alloy were initiated in surface stratum or subsurface stratum
with monophyletic at any temperature. According to the fractography observation,
the inclusions, segregations, cutting tool acted as the stress concentration effect
sensitive area which induced fatigue rupture. At the same time, the fatigue fracture
revealed the similar characteristic at test temperatures. The crack growth areas
appeared periodically cleavage characteristic and the propagation region has
obvious river pattern, indicating that the temperature had a negligible effect on
fatigue strength of this alloy. The typical fatigue striations are observed on the
fatigue propagation regions and align on the vertical direction of the fracture

The Microstructures Analyzed by TEM
(1) The original microstructures of HAYNES282
The original TEM microstructures before fatigue are shown in Fig. 8. After
standard heat treatment, the alloy is mainly composed by granular c′ phase with
sizes of 50 –100 nm, and c′ phase is the main strengthening phase in the alloy.
Moreover, a small amount of precipitates are distributed in the matrix, which were
conﬁrmed as M 23C6 type carbide by the diffraction patterns.
(2) The toughness deformation area of microstructures at room temperature
Figure 9 shows TEM fatigue structures of HAYNES282 at room temperature.
Compared with the original microstructures (Fig. 8), there is no signiﬁcant change in
c′ phase after room temperature fatigue tests. When fatigue deformation occurred,
dislocations move from the c matrix to the c′/c interface and then interfacial dis-
locations formed. Because the stress is relatively low and the deformation is small,
the density of the dislocation is low and stacking faults exist in c matrix and c′ phase
simultaneously. Dislocations in c matrix have a decomposition reaction as follow:

(3) The toughness deformation area of microstructure at 760 °C
Fatigue microstructures at 760 °C observed by TEM are shown in Fig. 10.
Compared with the fatigue at room temperature, c′ phases grew up to a certain
extent and decreased in quantity (Fig. 10b), dislocation density increased and
dislocation network appeared. Moreover, dislocation loops (Fig. 10a) around c′
phases were also observed due to Orowan looping, which weakened the effect of
cyclic softening due to cutting mechanism. Dislocations gathered around the c′
phases interface, which improves the cyclic crack resistance. Meanwhile, the par-
allel slip bands (Fig. 10c) are observed after fatigue at 760 °C. Dislocation loops
and shear bands appeared at the same time, which indicates that the cutting
mechanism and Orowan looping mechanism plays dual roles in fatigue at 760 °C.
In this critical range, the strength of HAYNES282 alloy would be very high and the
plasticity is good in this temperature. So the fatigue strength of HAYNES282 alloy
was not signi ﬁcantly decreased. Moreover, as shown in Fig. 10d, carbide in grains
became a dislocation source and dislocations spread taking the carbide as the center.
Conclusions
The results showed that the HCF strength of HAYNES282 alloy were 367.7, 355 and
357.3 MPa at room temperature, 700 and 760 °C, there was no signi ﬁcant difference
among three temperature conditions. The fatigue strength of alloy at 700 °C
decreased slightly than room temperature. It is interesting to ﬁnd that the fatigue
strength at 760 °C is higher than that at 700 °C, which results from the precipitation c′
phase.
