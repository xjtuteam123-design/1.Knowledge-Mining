HIGH-TEMPERATURE LOW-CYCLE FATIGUE AND CREEP
BEHAVIOUR OF NICKEL-BASED SUPERALLOYS WITH
HEAT-RESISTANT COATINGS
J. Okrajni, M. Cies’la and L. Swadz’ba
Silesian T echnical University, Department of Material Engineering, Metallurgy and T ransport, ul. Krasin´ skiego 8,
40-019 Katowice, Poland
Abstract—The problems associated with nickel-based superalloys with heat-resisting coatings, as used in
aerospace, have been addressed in this paper. The inﬂuence of the heat-resisting coating technology on
both the fatigue and creep behaviour of the alloys has been shown. Low-cycle fatigue and creep tests
have been performed. Lifetime conditions have been determined in relation to low-cycle fatigue, isothermal
creep and creep at cyclically variable temperatures, as a function of the chemical composition of the
coating, parameters of the thermal treatment and thickness of the coating. Possible processes and
mechanisms of fracture are discussed.
Keywords— Fatigue life; Ni-base superalloys; Surface coatings; Fatigue crack behaviour; Creep.

INTRODUCTION
Studies of the mechanical behaviour of materials in and under di Verent conditions constitute
an important aspect of engineering science, because of their practical importance, especially relating
to problems concerning predictions of the durability of mechanical systems that include objects
subjected to mechanical and thermal inﬂuences [1–3]. The development of durability prediction
methods involves progress in ﬁelds, e.g. fracture mechanic, low cycle fatigue, thermal fatigue and
creep [4–8]. When the lifetime of a given object is to be predicted, it is ﬁrst necessary to select
the most appropriate method of investigation, and then to be able to model complex mechanical
systems and their elements using relevant methods in the discipline of the mechanics of solids.
Appropriate criteria are also required with respect to crack initiation and propagation analyses.
As far as macroscopically homogenous materials are concerned, there is a large amount of literature

The problem of coatings is particularly important with reference to creep-resisting alloys used

for a speciﬁc application can be approached by considering relationships between the structure of
materials, their basic physical and mechanical properties, and the behaviour of elements made
from them under in-service operating conditions, the latter by testing the object of major concern.
There is also a need to develop prediction methods in relation to the operating properties of the
critical elements that have an outer layer, these methods being based on material tests, and to
determine criteria for the material-environment state without the need for testing the real compo-
nents. This work presents the problems relating to lifetime investigations of superalloys used in
aerospace, but particularly with regard to the inﬂuence of outer layers on fatigue properties and
creep processes.
DETAILS OF THIS INVESTIGATION
The nickel-base alloys: EI 867 and EI 703, having the chemical compositions given in Table 1,
are the studied materials. The ﬁrst alloy is one used for aircraft engine turbine blades. The EI 703
alloy is used, among other things, as the material for combustion chambers of turbine engines. In
order to increase the high-temperature corrosion resistance of turbine blades and combustion
chambers, the surfaces are covered with creep-resistant coatings. This therefore requires an appro-
priate selection of coatings. This also concerns coating technology, the phase composition and
optimum thickness of coating. In this work, an evaluation of the lifetime of alloys subjected to
mechanical and thermal inﬂuences using industrial coatings has been made.
THE AIM OF THE WORK
The present program is an attempt to use diVerent investigative methods to evaluate the inﬂuence
of outer coated layers on the behaviour of materials with di Verent heat-resisting coatings. In
diVerent approaches that depend on the operating conditions of a speciﬁc material and coating
type combination, the authors aim to obtain preliminary data concerning possibilities, limitations
and the range of application of di Verent tests in relation to life assessment problems of coated
superalloys subjected to mechanical and thermal inﬂuences. It follows that one of the aims of this
work was to specify the factors determining crack initiation and growth processes in the test
materials, and so lifetime conditions under low cycle fatigue, isothermal creep and creep at cyclically
variable temperatures, each as a function of chemical composition of the coating, thermal treatment

THE INVESTIGATION PROGRAMME
The creep test is widely used in industry as one of the basic material tests which enables one to
determine the properties of a material under appropriate mechanical and thermal conditions. In
this paper, the test was used to evaluate the properties of EI867 alloy with di Vusion silico-
aluminizing coatings formed by the pack cementation method. Details of the technological processes
involved in the formation of those coatings, i.e. their thickness and the specimen sizes used in the
tests, are presented in Table 2. Creep tests were also conducted on uncoated specimens which had
been subjected to the same processes of thermal treatment as those specimens with a given
diVusion layer.
The EI 867 alloy specimens, with a coating obtained by the silico-aluminizing method, were
also tested under low cycle fatigue conditions at elevated temperature. These tests were sup-
plemented with tests coatings obtained by the chromo-aluminizing method in order to compare
both types of production technologies. In the case of fatigue tests of a material without a coating,
the prior-coating thermal treatment was performed in order to make justiﬁable comparisons. The
specimens with a circular cross-section were used for the low cycle fatigue tests. The technological
process parameters and coating thickness details are presented in Table 3.
The EI 703 alloy was subjected to creep under a cyclically variable temperature also using
specimens with a circular cross-section. These specimens were covered with protective coatings of
110mm thickness by the plasma spraying method involving Al2O3–Ni powder. The diVerent types
of coatings formed are listed in Table 5. Creep tests at cyclically variable temperatures were also
conducted on the EI 703 alloy without a protective coating.

DISCUSSION
The creep curves of Fig. 2(a) show a signiﬁcant inﬂuence of coating thickness on the lifetime of
specimens with coatings obtained by the silico-aluminizing method. This inﬂuence can be expressed
in mathematical terms as a function of time to failure versus coating thickness [Fig. 2(b)]. The
investigation shows that, in the case of creep tests, the matrix properties control the behaviour of
the specimens. This is probably because, following crack initiation in the coating during the initial
period of creep, the coating then stops transmitting load, which is now borne by the specimen core.
The curves presented in Fig. 2(a) describe the behaviour of coated material under static loading.

test programme included a low-cycle fatigue test which revealed an inﬂuence of overloads (i.e.
during take-oV and landing of an aeroplane) on the properties of the materials used in aircraft
engine elements. The fatigue tests may therefore be considered a more appropriate investigation
method, especially when studying the physical conditions of the crack initiation processes in an
engineering artefact. The fatigue tests showed a great dependence of material behaviour on the
coating thickness. It is seen that the outer layer can have a positive or negative inﬂuence on fatigue
lifetime depending on coating thickness (Fig. 3). This conclusion is for coatings obtained both by
chromo-aluminizing and silico-aluminizing technology. At lower thicknesses of coatings (i.e.
0.06 mm and 0.08 mm), an increase in the fatigue life was obtained in comparison with the alloy
material without a coating. The specimens with coating thicknesses of 0.15 and 0.19 mm showed
a lower number of cycles to failure in comparison with specimens without a coating. Slightly better
fatigue properties were found for the material with a coating obtained by silico-aluminizing
technology (Fig. 3) in comparison with the material covered by chromo-aluminized coatings
(Fig. 3).
Analysis of the fatigue tests together with changes taking place within the material structure
presents the possibility of determining the dominant fracture mechanisms, which is particularly
important for the production engineer with responsibility for the material coating process. The
crack initiation and growth process analyses can be useful in this respect. The creep tests involving
cyclically variable temperatures are an additional attempt to formulate a method of analysis of
the fracture phenomenon for similar purposes. These investigations enabled one to determine crack
initiation sites in the test materials and the manner of their growth. It has been shown that the
adhesive coatings crack earlier than the adhesive–diVusive ones, and the specimens with the former
coatings are also damaged earlier (Table 6). This is due to di Verent crack initiation and growth
processes in the coating itself, and in the substrate. The cracks in the case of an adhesive coating
can be generated in the coating or substrate material [Fig. 5(a)]. The adhesive coatings gave a
lower specimen life under conditions of creep at cyclically variable temperatures (Fig. 4), which
can be related to both the properties of the intermediate layer between the coating and substrate
(in which a crack can be generated), and porosity of the outer layer facilitating the penetration of
oxygen. Crack propagation inside the material in the case of adhesive–diVusive coatings is impeded
on the coating–substrate boundary in the area of the diVusion bond [Fig. 5(b)], thereby causing
an increase in life of specimens with a coating in comparison with material without a coating.
High temperature corrosion is not observed in this case because of the good adherence and density
of the coating, which protects against oxygen penetration. These investigations show that by an
appropriate selection of production processes involved in providing optimal coatings, an additional
protection against the aggressive environment can be obtained along with an increase in the
material lifetime.
CONCLUSIONS
This investigation shows that:
(1) In creep tests, the matrix material properties control the behaviour of the tested specimens.

(2) Since the loading of artefacts made from these nickel-based superalloys usually have a fatigue
character, low-cycle fatigue tests were also conducted. These showed that overloads are an
appropriate method to examine the conditions of fatigue crack initiation.
(3) The fatigue results showed a signiﬁcant diversiﬁcation, which depended on the coating
thickness. It was found that the coating obtained by chromo-aluminizing or silico-aluminiz-
ing can have a positive or negative inﬂuence on fatigue lifetime which depends on coating
thickness.
(4) Crack propagation inside the material, for adhesive–di Vusive coatings, is impeded by the
coating–substrate boundary in the diVusion bond area. This results in an increase in lifetime
of specimens with a coating in comparison to a material without a coating.
(5) For adhesive–di Vusive coatings, a good adhesion to the substrate and a compact structure
impede the penetration of oxygen from the surface into the base material. Hence, the eVects
of stress corrosion which are frequently observed in adhesive coatings were not observed in
this material.
