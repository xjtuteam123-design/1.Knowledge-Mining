Improving the microstructure and high temperature properties of
the Ti–40Al–16Nb alloy by the addition of a minor Sc or La-rich
Misch metal
C.T. Yang, C.H. Koo*
DepartmentofMaterialsScienceandEngineering,NationalTaiwanUniversity,Taipei,Taiwan,RepublicofChina
Received 1 January 2003; accepted 23 January 2003
Abstract
This work investigated the eﬀect of a slight increase in Nb content of approximately 1 at.% and the eﬀect of adding a minor Sc or
La-rich Misch metal (Mm) on the microstructure, phases and high temperature properties of Ti–40Al–15Nb alloy. Microstructural ana-
lysis and phase characterizations of the Ti–40Al–16Nb alloy with 0.39 wt.% Sc or La-rich Misch metal additions are summarized as
follows. (i) The as-cast Ti–40Al–16Nb–0.39 wt.% M (M=Sc or Mm) alloys have an a
2 matrix and contain a-Ti and B2 phase
precipitates but fewg phase precipitates. The Sc2O3 or La2O3 oxide dispersoids, formed by internal oxidation, are observed in this work;
but Ti3(Al,Sc) presented in the dual phases ofg-TiAl were not found. (ii) Adding Sc and La-rich Misch metal decreases thea-transus
temperatures of the TiAl–Nb alloys, possibly stabilizing the a2 phase and inhibiting the B2 or g phase formation. (iii) After
homogenization, thes phase andg phase precipitates in thea2 phase matrix and the B2 phase are absent. The distributed morphology
of the oxide dispersoids in the TiAl–Nb alloy with added La-rich Mm is more uniform and discontinuous than that in the TiAl–Nb alloy
with added Sc, after the homogenization. The compressive peak ﬂow stress increment measured from high temperature compression
testing at 900 /C14C is around 450 MPa and the high temperature strength at temperatures above 900 /C14C is also enhanced. The sig-
niﬁcant improvement in high-temperature mechanical properties is attributed to the precipitation of ﬁne oxide particles with high
thermal stability in the alloys. Furthermore, adding either a minor Sc or Mm to the alloy increases the strength of adhesion between the
oxide layers and the alloys and changes the morphology of the oxide particles formed in the TiAl alloy with a high niobium content.

1. Introduction
Increasing demand for saving energy, increasing
operational eﬃciency and controlling pollution from
vehicle engines, industry and gas turbines in aircraft has
motivated materials scientists and engineers to seek
weight savings, higher high-temperature strength, and
eﬀective resistance to oxidation, corrosion and creep.
Titanium aluminide intermetallic alloys are highly
promising for high-temperature structural applications
[1–3]. Niobium has been shown to be the most eﬀective
element for improving titanium aluminides. In recent
decades, only up to about 2% Nb has been added to
TiAl alloys with Al contents that range from 46 to 49
at.% to improve high-temperature oxidation resistance
and the strength ofg phase [1–6]. Conversely, TiAl alloy
with a high Nb content and an Al from 40 to 45 at.%,
has rarely been reported. Recent investigations have
indicated that TiAl alloys with a high Nb content (such
as Ti–40Al–11Nb) exhibit favorable high-temperature
strength and some ductility at room temperature as well
as a compression ratio of 10–14% [7–11]. Furthermore,
our previous work [9] indicates that the ultimate tensile
strength of as-rolled and homogenized Ti–40Al–15Nb
alloys [with a composition of approximately Ti–40Al–
14.74Nb (at.%)] exhibits a positive temperature depen-
dence at temperatures below 800
/C14C and 850 /C14C,
respectively. The ultimate tensile strength (666.4 Mpa)
of homogenized Ti–40Al–15Nb alloy at 850 /C14C is extre-
mely high because of the stress anomalous phenomenon
caused by the frequent interactions of screw dislocations
in the g phase. However, the matrix of Ti–40Al–15Nb

element to the microstructure of the alloys. However,
no study has elucidated the microstructure of TiAl with
a high niobium content to which has been added a little
Sc or rare earth elements. Besides, the literature [27,28]
indicates that adding niobium to TiAl alloy beyond a
critical content (for example 15 at.%Nb for Ti–40Al
alloy) can generate high thermal stability and a high
melting point of the s phase after long-term isothermal
heat treatment. In an Nb–Al–V alloy system, that thes
phase leads to considerable increases in the high-temp-
erature strength of the alloy [29].
To our knowledge, no study has attempted to clarify
the high-temperature properties of high Nb-containing
TiAl alloys to which has been added a slight amount of
Sc or rare earth elements. Based on the strengthening
mechanism of the precipitates and dispersoids at high
temperature, the Ti–40Al–15Nb alloy [9] with a slightly
increased Nb content of about 1 at.% to which is added
a little Sc or La-rich Misch metal was used to evaluate
in detail the eﬀects on the microstructure and high
temperature properties of the speciﬁed alloy. This study
attempts to improve the microstructure, high-temper-
ature mechanical properties and resistance to oxidation
of such an alloy with a high Nb content, to which a
minor quaternary element, Sc or La-rich Misch metal
has been added.
2. Experimental procedure
In this study, four alloys with nominal compositions
of Ti–40.17Al–14.74Nb (at.%) (referred to as Ti–40Al–
15Nb), Ti–40Al–15.89Nb (at.%) (referred to as Ti–
40Al–16Nb), Ti–40Al–15.89Nb–0.39wt.%Sc (referred
to as Ti–40Al–16Nb–0.39wt.%Sc) and Ti–40Al–
15.89Nb–0.39wt.%Mm (referred to as Ti–40Al–16Nb–
0.39wt.%Mm) were prepared by remelting the alloy in a
non-consumable electrode arc furnace with a water-
cooled copper mold in an atmosphere of argon. Each
alloy button specimen weighed approximately 120 g and
was remelted and turned over for more than ten times to
ensure homogeneity. A slight amount of Sc and Mm
were added as Al–2.13wt.%Sc master alloy and the
La-rich Mm with the composition of 85.7wt.%La,
12wt.%Ce, 2wt.%Pr and 0.3wt.%Nd. The cast alloys
were homogenized at 1100
/C14C for 72 h in Ar at a positive
pressure, and then were left in furnace for cooling. A
TEM (JEOL 100CXII) and a scanning electron micro-
scopy (SEM, Philips XL30 or FEG SEM, LEO 1530)
equipped with an Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectro-
scopy (EDS) were used to analyze the microstructure of
both as-cast and homogenized alloys. The phase of the
alloys were identiﬁed using X-ray diﬀraction (XRD)
with a CuK
a characteristic X-ray and electron probe
microanalyzer (EPMA, JEOL JXA-8600SX) equipped
with Wavelength Dispersive Spectrometer (WDS).
Diﬀerential thermal analysis (DTA) was conducted to
elucidate the eﬀect of adding Sc and Mm on the phase
transformation in the Ti–40Al–16Nb alloy. DTA speci-
mens of 10–20 mg were cut from the ingots and placed
in high purity recrystallized alumina crucibles. The Du
Pont 2000 DTA cell was continually purged with ultra-
high purity argon during analysis runs, which were all
conducted at a heating rate of 10 K/min.
The compression samples were cut using an electro-
discharge machine (EDM) to cylinders with a diameter
of 6 mm and a height of 9 mm. The compression tests
were performed in a Gleeble 2000 hot working simu-
lation machine. All compression tests at high temper-
ature were performed in a protective atmosphere of
argon to minimize surface oxidation. Continuous com-
pression tests were performed at a strain rate of 0.001/s,
to a maximum engineering strain of 0.75 and at a
temperature between 900
/C14C and 1150 /C14C. The speci-
mens were heated to the testing temperature at a heating
rate of 10
/C14C/s using the Gleeble resistance heating
system, and were held constant at that temperature for 5

4. Conclusions
1. Adding a small amount of Sc or La-rich Mm to
the Ti–40Al–16Nb alloy causes the micro-
structure of the as-cast alloy to exhibit an a
2
phase matrix with a small amount of B2 phase,a-
Ti phase, and g phase. The precipitate of oxide
dispersoids (Sc2O3 or La2O3) formed by internal
oxidation are also distributed in the a2 phase
matrix. After homogenization, the phases of
homogenized Ti–40Al–16Nb–0.39wt.%M (M=Sc
or Mm) alloys at room temperature include
oxide dispersoids (Sc
2O3 or La 2O3) and a s
phase with a little a-Ti phase in a g phase-rich
a2 matrix. During homogenization, the a-Ti
phase in the two alloys tends to decompose. The
distribution of cubic Sc
2O3 dispersoids is almost
continuous or cluster-like, but the alloy to
which La-rich Mm is added may present sphe-
rical-like La
2O3 oxides whose distribution is
discontinuous and uniform.
2. The eﬀects of 0.39wt.% Sc or La-rich Mm on the
phase transformation of the TiAl alloy with a
high Nb content, as determined from the DTA
exothermic peaks, can account for the micro-
struntural diﬀerences between the TiAl-Nb alloys
with and without Sc or La-rich Mm. Experi-
mental results indicate that adding a small
amount of Sc or La-rich Mm may reduce the a-
transus temperature of the Ti–40Al–16Nb alloy,
possibly stabilizing and extending the a
2 phase
region in the phase diagram, while decreasing the
volume fraction of the g phase or the B2 phase
under as-cast conditions.
3. The ﬂow stress at 2% and 10% true strain, and
the peak stress of the homogenized Ti–40Al–
16Nb–0.39wt.%M (M=Sc or Mm) alloys are all
superior to those of the homogenized Ti–4Al–
16Nb alloy. In particular, the peak stress of the
former alloys is approximately 450 MPa higher
at 900
/C14C, and the high temperature strength
above 900 /C14C is also clearly improved. This
superiority is mainly attributed to the fact that
adding a little Sc or Mm to the alloy may stabi-
lize the a
2 phase and produce ﬁne oxide dis-
persoids (Sc 2O3 or La 2O3), which are eﬀective
obstacles to motion of dislocations at high tem-
peratures.
