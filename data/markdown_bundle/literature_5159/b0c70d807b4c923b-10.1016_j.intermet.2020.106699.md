ABSTRACT 
The mechanical properties and strain-rate strengthening mechanism of single-phase TiZrHfNbTaMo
x 
(x ¼ 0.25, 
0.5, and 0.75) refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) upon dynamic loading are investigated. Dynamic 
compression with varying strain rates of 2100 – 6000 s
 1 
at room temperature are performed by the split- 
Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB), and the dynamic yielding strength is about 1.3 times of the quasi-static one. 
Positive strain-rate sensitivity (SRS) on the yielding strength is available, and the work-hardening is weakened 
but the yielding strength is improved with increasing strain rates due to the adiabatic heating upon dynamic 
loadings and the dislocation movement resistance, respectively. Considering the adiabatic temperature rise 
converted by plastic deformation work at high strain rates, the flow behavior is characterized by employing the 
modified Johnson-Cook model. A semi-empirical Zerilli-Armstrong constitutive model is proposed to predict 
yielding strengths at various strain rate. 
1. Introduction 
Nowadays, driven by the increasingly more demanding service 
conditions in high-temperature fields, including turbine blade of aero 
engines, rocket nozzles, and hypersonic vehicle leading edges, there has 
been a trend to obtain a new class of refractory materials superior to 
superalloys [ 1 ]. Based on the concept of high entropy alloys (HEAs) 
[ 2 – 4 ], refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) were first put forward in 
2010 by Senkov et al. and immediately caused prodigious impacts 
because of the extraordinary ability to maintain high strength even over 
1600 
�
C [ 5 , 6 ]. RHEAs, compared with superalloys, consisting of four or 
more refractory elements (e.g. W, Ta, Mo, Nb, and Hf), have of late years 
drawn a widely public attention owing to excellent mechanical prop -
erties. During approximate decennium development, extensive studies 
on RHEAs have been widely carried out, including mechanical proper -
ties, antioxidant properties, wear and corrosion resistances, and a 
handful of dynamic properties [ 7 – 9 ]. 
In particular, the studies about the plasticity of RHEAs have attracted 
more and more attention recently. Huang et al. have found that the 
element of Tantalum played a significant role in controlling the phase 
formation, and created a composite structure with interleaved hcp and 
mechanically unstable bcc phases via tailoring the Tantalum content. As 
a result, the ductility of the alloys was greatly improved [ 10 ]. Moreover, 
the ductility of BCC RHEAs was relative to valence-electron concentra -
tion (VEC) [ 11 , 12 ]. Yuan et al. prepared and investigated a series of 
ductile RHEAs, and expounded the connection between the VEC and 
phase stability [ 13 ]. 
Nevertheless, the applications of this novel kind of promising alloys 
require the study of the mechanical performance and microstructure 
evolution during practical services such as rolling, machining, and 
impact loading, which are usually subjected to the strain rate between 1
