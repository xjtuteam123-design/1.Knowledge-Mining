The mechanical behaviour of U-6Nb alloy and AISI 1045 and 1215 
steels at high strain rates 
R. S. ROSEN 
Materials Division, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, Livermore, CA 94550, USA 
M. E. KASSNER, K. E. THIEHSEN 
Department of Mechanical Engineering, Oregon State University, Corvallis, OR 97331, USA 
This study evaluated the mechanical behaviour of 
U-6Nb alloy and AISI 1215 and 1045 steels. The 
mechanical properties were examined at conven- 
tional (e.g. ~ < 10 -1 s -1) and high (e.g. g > 102 s <) 
strain rates. The study was initiated because little 
experimental work has been performed that eval- 
uates the mechanical behaviour of these ailoys at 
high strain rates. The evaluation included measure- 
ments of the yield stress, ultimate tensile strength, 
ductility, strain-rate sensitivity and toughness at low 
and high strain rates. Part of the purpose of the 
study was to determine whether these steels had 
high-strain-rate mechanical properties that can 
simulate U-6Nb. 
Chemical analyses of the AISI 1215 and 1045 that 
we used for mechanical tests are listed in Table I, 
along with the composition specification for U-6Nb. 
The AISI 1215 and U-6Nb were tested in the 
as-received condition; the AISI1045 was oil- 
quenched from 857 °C and tempered at 538 °C. The 
U-6Nb was quenched from the y-phase and tem- 
pered at 204 °C as part of the specification. 
Tensile tests were performed at strain rates from 
3 x 10 -4 to 3 s -1 on an MTS servohydraulic uni- 
versal test machine. Tensile specimens with a 
6.35 mm diameter and 25.4 mm gauge length were 
used. Strain was measured using a 25.4 mm MTS 
extensometer. Tests at strain rates greater than 3 s -1 
were conducted on a Hopkinson split-bar test 
machine to a plastic strain of approximately 0.05. 
The compression specimens were 6.35 mm in dia- 
meter and 6.35 mm long. Strain gauges were at- 
tached to the two elastic bars between which the test 
specimens were inserted. Equations of elastic wave 
propagation, along with the recorded strain gauge 
signals in the bars, were used to compute the 
dynamic stress-strain curve. Strain rates were eval- 
uated from the strain-time data. The details of the 
testing apparatus are described elsewhere [1]. 
Fracture energies were measured using standard 
Charpy V-notch specimens that were tested using 
a Riehle swinging-arm-pendulum impact-testing 
machine. All tests were conducted at ambient 
temperature. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) 
was performed on the tensile and Charpy V-notch 
fracture surfaces of the alloys.
