Abstract 
In the present paper, CoCrFeMnNi(NbC) alloy was des igned based on theoretical calculations and 
proposed as a novel high entropy alloy. The effect of Nb-C microalloy addition on the microstructure 
and mechanical properties of equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloys were studied during 
homogenisation. The chemical composition, microstru ctural evolution, hardness and shear 
deformation behaviour were analysed using XRF, Leco -combustion technique, optical microscopy, 
SEM-WDX, XRD, Vickers hardness and shear punch test ing. Moreover, the thermal behaviour of 
both alloys during heating stage was studied by dif ferential thermal analysis technique at a heating 
rate of 10 °C/min. The experimental results confirm ed theoretical calculations based on Scheil-
Gulliver solidification model and thermodynamic rul es, suggesting a solid solution high entropy 
alloy with FCC-crystal structure after vacuum arc r e-melting and homogenisation. SEM-WDX 
analysis hardly showed any Nb-containing precipitat es in both as-cast and homogenised samples. It 
was also found that Nb-C addition considerably reta rded the growth/coarsening of grain size during 
homogenisation. Moreover, the results suggested tha t the hardness and shear strength of 
CoCrFeMnNiNbC alloy was higher than CoCrFeMnNi allo y, mainly due to solid solution 
strengthening effect. 
 
Key words: High entropy alloys, Homogenisation, Nb-C addition, Microstructure, Mechanical 
properties 
 
 
1. Introduction 
 
High Entropy Alloys (HEA) or multi-component alloys are comprised of at least five 
principal elements and the amount of each elements is between 5 and 35 at% [1, 2]. The concept of

HEAs has been based on, equiatomic or non-equiatomi c chemical composition alloys with a highly 
stable solid solution phase matrix [3, 4, 5]. These alloys have shown excellent mechanical properties 
compared to existing alloys [2, 6]. This has enable d HEAs to be used for intensive environments, 
where high/cryogenic temperature and severe loading conditions are applied, e.g. fasteners, turbines, 
aerospace and military applications [7, 8, 9, 10]. In fact, the outstanding mechanical properties of 
HEAs have been related to four core effects, i.e. s luggish diffusion, high mixing entropy (i.e. 
Smix ≥1.5R), cocktail effect and lattice distortion effect [2]. 
Recent studies have focused on further improvements of HEAs in order to suit them for 
different working conditions. Grain refinement, TRI P/TWIP effect, precipitation behaviour, work 
hardening are some mechanisms that have been widely addressed to improve the mechanical and 
physical properties [11, 12, 13]. This has been ach ieved by employing different chemical 
compositions, and manufacturing processes and parameters. 
Generally, HEAs are cast through Vacuum Arc Re-melting (VAR) and their microstructure is 
manipulated via subsequent thermomechanical processing [14, 15]. In this context, a homogenisation 
heat treatment after casting is usually considered as a necessary step to enhance the homogeneity of 
alloying elements [16, 17]. Many investigations hav e been reported on the effect of homogenisation 
heat treatment on the microstructural evolution and mechanical properties [1]. Nevertheless, there is 
limited knowledge about the microalloyed HEAs during homogenisation. 
The possible precipitation and solute drag effect o f microalloy elements make it possible to 
further control the microstructure, grain size and improve mechanical properties [18, 19, 20, 21]. In 
this way, equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi alloy was chosen as a base alloy. The CoCrFeMnNi alloy (also 
known as Cantor alloy) has shown remarkable ductili ty at room and cryogenic temperatures, though 
a higher strength is still sought [22]. Therefore, the effect of 0.06Nb-0.8C (at%) addition on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of CoCrFeM nNi alloy was studied through 
homogenisation [23]. It was also aimed to assess th e predictive capability of Scheil-Gulliver (SG)

2. Experimental Procedure 
Two quinary CoCrFeMnNi and CoCrFeMnNiNb 0.06 C0.8 at% high entropy alloys (Table 1) 
were designed using Scheil-Gulliver solidification simulations and empirical thermodynamic rules. 
The SG simulations were performed by JMatPro software V7.0 to predict possible phase diagrams of 
studied alloys using Ni-based superalloys database. The SG simulations were carried out by taking 
into consideration of all 5 to 7 elements and all p ossible phases. The designed alloys were melted in 
a water-cooled copper mold from pure metals (purity > 99.8 wt.%) through a Vacuum Arc Re-
melting (VAR) furnace supplied by ALD Vacuum Techno logies GmbH. The ingots with a size of 
15x20x130 mm 3 were re-melted four times to enhance the homogenei ty of alloying elements. Mn 
was placed between the other constituents before me lting to minimise evaporation due to its high 
vapour pressure [21]. To compensate for the evapor ation of Mn during melting, an additional Mn 
(10 wt% ) was added to each ingot. The chemical composition o f each alloy was determined by XRF 
via a Philips X Unique II and also the carbon conte nt was measured by Leco-combustion. The 
studied alloys are hereafter referred to as ‘‘CoCrFeMnNi’’ and ‘‘CoCrFeMnNiNbC’’ alloys.

3.5. SPT 
Fig. 7 compares the normalised SPT curves of studie d alloys before and after 
homogenisation. The results suggested that the shea r strength of CoCrFeMnNiNbC alloy was higher

It has been established that the shear yield streng th is generally equal to 1.73 times of tensile

results showed that the shear yield stress is about three times of tensile yield strength of a 
CoCrFrMnNi alloy in the literature [26]. Note that this alloy has different microstructural 
characteristics compared to our alloys, though the chemical composition is similar.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Microstructure 
 The as-cast microstructure of studied alloys appea red to be a dendritic structure (Figs. 3 and 4). 
The results suggested that the dendrite size was no t influenced by NbC addition and the dendrite size 
was mainly dependent upon the principal elements. 
 It has been well understood that the dendrite stru cture mainly results from micro-segregation 
during casting [27]. Similarly, SEM-WDX results ind icated that the dendritic areas were rich in Fe 
and Mn, while interdendritic areas were rich in Co, Cr and Ni. However, the homogenised structure 
exhibited no dendrite, demonstrating a more homogen eous elemental distribution in the samples 
(Figs. 3 and 4).

 The grain size of CoCrFeMnNi alloy was significant ly larger than CoCrFeMnNiNbC after 
homogenisation. It has been well understood in stee ls that Nb can significantly contribute in 
retarding the growth-coarsening of grain size of au stenite [28]. As all process parameters were 
similar in both alloys, it is deduced that Nb-C add ition was the main reason to retard the 
growth/coarsening of grain size during homogenisati on. In this way, SEM-WDX analysis hardly 
showed Nb-rich precipitates in as-cast and homogeni sed samples. However, the observed difference 
in the grain size of both alloys after homogenisation can be mainly attributed to the solute drag effect 
of Nb. In fact, the segregation of Nb solutes and p ossible Nb-rich nano-scale precipitates might pin 
the advancing boundaries and prevented grain growth /coarsening. Furthermore, Mn rich precipitates 
were widely observed at grain boundaries of homogen ised structure of both alloys (Fig. 4 (e)). The 
results suggested that these precipitates could pin down the grain boundaries and to some extent 
retard the growth/coarsening of grains in both alloys. 
 Recrystallisation twining extensively occurred in both alloys during homogenisation (Fig 4). 
Moreover, step-like shaped recrystallisation twins were observed in both alloys. This is similar to the 
results of other researchers who also reported a co mparable behaviour in HEAs, but with a different 
chemical composition [29]. They found that availabl e precipitates in FCC crystal structure 
CoCrFeNiMo0.15 high entropy alloy can be a reason f or the formation of step-like shaped annealing 
twin. Our SEM-WDX results also showed the presence of Mn-rich precipitates on the interior-side of 
twins (Fig. 4). The observed precipitates might be the reason for the formation of step-like twins in 
both alloys. 
 XRD results showed a single FCC crystal structure in both alloys before and after 
homogenisation (Fig. 5). Similarly, DTA analysis sh owed no phase transition during heating for the 
applied heating rate, i.e. 10 °C/min (Fig. 6). Clea rly, the microstructure was stable and any thermal 
entropy variations did not lead to any phase transition [30]. These results are consistent with JMatPro 
analysis, suggesting the formation of a single-phase solid solution high entropy alloy (Fig. 2).

 From the XRD results, it was also found that Nb-C addition marginally increased the lattice 
parameter after casting (Table 2). Also, further in crease in lattice parameter of CoCrFeMnNiNbC 
was observed after homogenisation. The observed phe nomenon was explained based on Vegard’s 
law. It was assumed that there is a direct relationship between the lattice parameter and stoichiometry 
of elements [31]. Perhaps the dissolution of Nb-ric h precipitates and further homogenisation 
provided free Nb and C atoms and the matrix could a ccommodate more Nb and C atoms in solute, 
resulted in a larger lattice parameter. 
4.2. Mechanical Properties 
Shear punching testing and hardness results showed that the strength of CoCrFeMnNiNbC 
alloy was generally higher than CoCrFeMnNi alloy in both as-cast and homogenised conditions 
(Figs. 7 and 9). It is thought that the observed di fference was mainly ascribed to the effect of Nb-C 
addition. It is similar to the results of other res earchers who also reported strength increases due t o 
the effect of Nb or C addition [4, 20, 32, 33]. The presence of Nb and C could increase the strength 
due to solid solution and precipitate strengthening mechanisms. 
The SPT results also indicated that the homogenisat ion did not considerably decrease the 
strength of CoCrFeMnNi alloy, while CoCrFeMnNiNbC a lloy was slightly softened (Table 4). 
Dendrite boundaries in as-cast structure could impe de dislocation sliding, enhancing the strength of 
materials [23, 32, 34]. Since the as-cast dendrite structure was disappeared during the 
homogenisation, it can be deduced that the shear st rength of these alloys is mainly dependent upon 
their solid solution strengthening (Fig. 3). Howeve r, the most likely reason for the softening of 
CoCrFeMnNiNbC alloy can be attributed to the dissol ution of possible Nb-carbide precipitates 
during the homogenisation. 
SEM analysis also showed the possible formation of rather large Mn-rich precipitates in the 
microstructure of both alloys after homogenisation (Fig. 4). From the SPT and hardness results, it

The fracture surface of both alloys revealed three regions (Fig. 8). It has been well 
established that a plastic deformation occurs durin g the initial stage of punching. This can be 
accompanied by a severe wear between the surface of sample and die. In this case, SEM-WDX 
results also showed smooth oxide worn areas with we ar tracks (scratches), suggesting an 
abrasive/adhesive wear in burnished region. The oxi de layer might be formed as a result of mutual 
interaction between punch surface and sample. Indee d, the interfacial temperature could be raised 
over the flash temperature by frictional heat [35]. A high temperature and ambient humidity 
enhanced the oxide formation on the surface of burn ished region. In the fracture region of all 
samples, a dominant dimple fracture mode was observ ed, suggesting a ductile fracture in both alloys 
(Fig. 8 (d)). Also, no significant difference appea red in the size and depth of dimples in both alloys . 
The results suggested that the Nb-C addition did not influence the fracture mode of studied alloys. 
5. Conclusions 
The present study provides evidence about the effec t of Nb-C addition on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of CoCrFeMnNi high entropy al loy during homogenisation. The main 
conclusions that can be drawn from this study are as follows: 
1. A solid solution high entropy alloy with FCC-crysta l structure appeared in both alloys before 
and after homogenisation, validating SG simulations. 
2. Nb-C addition slightly influenced the melting point and range and also it increased the lattice 
parameter of CoCrFeMnNi alloy. 
3. Nb-C addition significantly contributed in reducing the growth/coarsening of grain size 
during homogenisation compared to CoCrFeMnNi alloy.
