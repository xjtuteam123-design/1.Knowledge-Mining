ABSTRACT 
The equiatomic CoCrFeNiTa high-entropy alloy (HEA) was prepared by mechanical alloying and spark plasma 
sintered at different temperatures in this work. The microstructural evolution of alloy powder and microstructure 
and mechanical properties of the sintered HEA were investigated. The results show that the particle size of HEA 
powder is decreased gradually with the increase of milling time and then tends to be unchanged basically. The 
composition of the HEA powder becomes uniform, and metastable face-centered cubic (FCC) phase and Laves 
phase with Co
2
Ta type are obtained eventually. The HEA sintered at 1100 
◦
C exhibits a near-full density 
(99.15%). Supersaturated FCC and Laves phases with a grain size of 1.32 μ m and 1.26 μ m are formed in the HEA 
blocks, and many Ta
2
[Co, Cr, Fe] precipitates with a size of about 23 nm were observed in the FCC matrix. 
Mechanical properties were measured using Vickers hardness and uniaxial compression testing techniques. The 
HEA sintered at 1100 
◦
C shows an ultra-high hardness of 9.37 GPa and compressive strength of 2177 MPa 
respectively. This is mainly ascribed to the combined effect of the grain refinement strengthening, solid solution 
strengthening, second phase strengthening including Laves phase and nano-sized precipitates. It provides a new 
way to prepare high strength and high hardness materials. 
1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are usually composed of five or more 
elements, each with a content of 5% ~ 35%, and they show unique 
properties, such as high strength [ 1 ], high toughness [ 2 ], high plasticity 
[ 2 ], high hardness [ 3 , 4 ], excellent thermal stability [ 3 ], good wear and 
corrosion resistance [ 5 , 6 ]. Compared with conventional metals, these 
unique properties of HEAs are mainly associated with four core effects at 
the beginning of HEAs research, including high configurational mixing 
entropy, slow diffusion, severe lattice distortion and cocktail effects 
[ 7 , 8 ]. 
In recent years, an increasing number of researchers have paid 
attention to investigating various kinds of HEAs. Among them, CoCr -
FeMnNi HEA is the most widely studied as single-phase face-centered 
cubic (FCC) solid solution alloy. The alloys possess excellent fracture 
toughness at low temperature, however their poor strength has become 
a bottleneck of extensive application [ 9 ]. Meanwhile, NbMoTaW HEA 
has been considered as a single-phase body-centered cubic (BCC) solid 
solution alloy, which exhibits high strength and poor plasticity. In order 
to reconcile the contradictions between strength and plasticity, there are 
usually two methods: strengthening HEAs with BCC solid solution phase 
or improving the plasticity of HEAs by containing a single FCC solid 
solution phase [ 10 ]. Numerous studies have been conducted based on 
the first method, such as solid solution strengthening [ 11 ], nitriding 
strengthening [ 12 ], deformation strengthening [ 13 ] and thermal 
annealing [ 14 ]. The compressive fracture strength and hardness of all 
the modified alloys are higher than those of CoCrFeNi alloys, however 
the strength is still not ideal. For the second method, it is very difficult to

HEAs are usually prepared by vacuum arc melting or induction 
melting methods, however these traditional preparation methods lead to 
the formation of coarse grain and uneven microstructure, resulting in 
poor mechanical properties [ 20 ]. Compared with smelting methods, the 
powder metallurgy process can provide HEAs with more uniform 
microstructure, finer grains and excellent mechanical properties [ 21 ], 
while also avoids volatilizing of some elements with lower melting point 
during heating, which will result in deviation between the actual 
composition and the standard composition [ 22 ]. During the mechanical 
alloying (MA), various elemental powders are ground repeatedly, and 
cold welding and crushing happen for many times. When the two pro -
cesses reach equilibrium, the homogeneous metastable phase is finally 
obtained [ 20 ]. Recently, a novel and effective method for production of 
HEA by a low ball-to-powder ratio (BPR) was proposed by Olejarz et al., 
which shows great advantage in improving the productivity of HEA and 
has received more and more attentions [ 23 ]. 
As for the HEAs powders, spark plasma sintering (SPS) is considered 
as an efficient way to consolidate, which has been widely used for sin -
tering metal, alloy and ceramic powders. The method shows the unique 
advantages of avoiding grain coarsening and non-uniform microstruc -
ture and making the sample dense quickly and available to obtain higher 
mechanical properties. Many researchers have tried to synthesize 
various HEAs by using MA and SPS methods. Varalakshmis et al. first 
published the research results of mechanical alloying of AlFeTiCrZnCu 
high-entropy alloy [ 24 ]. Fu et al. prepared CoCrFeNiTiAl HEA powder 
by using MA technology and obtained HEA blocks by using SPS in a short 
time, and found that a high relative density about 98% and Vickers 
hardness about 432 HV can be reached [ 25 ]. Cabibbo et al. have used 
MA and SPS technology to prepare CoCrFeNiNb
x 
HEAs, which is 
composed of FCC and Laves phases and exhibits a uniform ultrafine- 
grained microstructure [ 26 ]. In recent years, the CoCrFeNiTa HEA has 
been prepared by the vacuum melting method, and the influence of Ta 
addition on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the HEA 
has also been investigated [ 27 ]. Nevertheless, little attention has been 
paid to investigating the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
CoCrFeNiTa HEA prepared by the SPS method. 
Therefore, in this work, CoCrFeNiTa HEA with equiatomic was 
prepared by mechanical alloying and spark plasma sintering. The 
alloying process, phase composition, microstructure and mechanical 
properties were also investigated. 
2. Experimental and methods 
2.1. Preparation of CoCrNiFeTa alloys 
The powders of Cr, Co, Fe, Ni and Ta with high purity ( > 99.9 wt%) 
were used as the raw material of CoCrNiFeTa HEA, which were pro -
duced by Beijing High-tech New Material Technology Co., Ltd. The 
initial particle size of all starting powders was less than 70 μ m. The raw 
material powders were mechanically alloyed by high-energy planetary 
ball mill (QM-QX4L) at a ball-to-powder ratio of 10:1 and a speed of 300 
rpm under high-purity argon atmosphere. The stainless steel balls with 
Φ 5, Φ 10 and Φ 15 in diameter and stainless steel bottles were used for 
mechanical alloying. During milling, a stand-by time of 15 min was used 
to prevent the cold welding. In addition, for the convenience of phase 
analysis, a small amount of milling powder was extracted at regular 
intervals of 5 h, 10 h, 20 h, 30 h, 40 h and 50 h inside a vacuum glove 
box. The powder ground for 50 h was sintered at 900

number of lamellar particles, as shown in Fig. 2 (b). After ball milling at

supersaturated solid solution was formed in the alloy, and the particle 
size did not change significantly, as shown in Fig. 3 . Under the inter- 
diffusion effect of alloying elements, the CoCrFeNiTa HEA powders 
with homogeneous elemental distribution were obtained finally.

The microstructure refinement can be attributed to fast sintering of 
mechanically alloyed HEA powders. On one hand, in the process of 
powder metallurgy, the ground metal powders were subject to severe 
deformation and fragmentation. Due to the intense shearing and plastic 
deformation induced by the mechanical effect of balls, micro defects 
such as dislocations and vacancies can be accumulated in the grain [ 49 ]. 
Prolonged milling time will cause repeated deformation, which leads to 
the dislocations motion, accumulation and tangle, then gradually forms 
dislocation cells, and transforms into sub-grain boundaries afterward. 
Furthermore, with the increase of milling time, the particle size of alloy 
powders will gradually decrease. On the other hand, as characterized by 
high heating rate, short sintering time and high efficiency, SPS tech -
nique can effectively control the grain growth of CoCrFeNiTa HEA 
during sintering. Hence, the grain size of alloys can be refined after rapid 
solidification of SPS [ 20 ]. 
The solid solution strengthening of the CoCrFeNiTa HEA is mainly 
caused by the large atomic radius Ta element. Due to the large difference 
in the atomic radius, the solute atoms Co, Cr, Fe, Ni and Ta are inte -
grated into the solid solution during mechanical alloying and SPS 
preparation. The formation of solid solution leads to lattice distortion, 
which increases the resistance of dislocation movement and makes it 
difficult to slip, thus increasing the strength and hardness of the CoCr -
FeNiTa HEA. 
In addition, the presence of a stiffer and more elastic Laves phase and 
nano-sized precipitation of CoTa2 phase also plays an important role in 
strengthening the CoCrFeNiTa HEA. The strengthening contribution 
depends on the volume fraction and morphology of the two kinds of 
particles. As shown in Figs. 9 and 10 , the hard Laves phase and nano- 
precipitation uniformly disperse in the microstructure of the CoCrFe -
NiTa HEA, including at the trip junctions of FCC grain and in the ductile 
FCC matrix. Both the two second phases could reinforce the HEA by 
carrying partial load form the FCC matrix. 
The strengthening of CoCrFeNiTa HEA in this work is an ideal 
combination of fine grain strengthening, solid solution strengthening 
and precipitation strengthening. However, the ductility of CoCrFeNiTa 
alloy is worse than that of CoCrFeNiTa

adjusted to change the properties of the alloy. 
4. Summary and conclusions 
Mechanical alloyed (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS) were 
applied to prepare the equiatomic CoCrFeNiTa high-entropy alloy. The 
phase composition, microstructure and mechanical properties were 
characterized. The conclusions are shown as follows: 
As the increase of ball milling time, the particle size of HEA powder 
gradually decreases. When milled for 20 h, the average particle size is 
reduced to 25.2 μ m, and all the diffraction peaks of starting element 
nearly disappear. With further increase of milling time, the particle size 
tends to be unchanged basically, and the composition of the powder 
becomes uniform. At last, metastable FCC solid solution and Laves 
phases are formed in the HEA powder. The sintered CoCrFeNiTa HEA 
blocks possess a microstructure composed by supersaturated FCC and 
Laves phases, which shows a size of 1.32 μ m and 1.26 μ m respectively. In 
addition, many precipitation of Ta
2
[Co, Cr, Fe] with average size of 
about 23 nm are observed in the FCC matrix. The hardness and 
compressive strength are increased first and then decreased with the 
increase of sintering temperature ranging from 900 
◦
C to 1200 
◦
C. The 
HEA sintered at 1100 
◦
C shows ultra-high hardness of 9.37 GPa and 
strength of 2177 MPa respectively. This is mainly due to the combined 
effect of near-full density (99.15%) and comprehensive strengthening 
mechanisms, such as fine grain strengthening, solid solution strength -
ening and second phase particle including Laves phase and nano-sized 
precipitation.
