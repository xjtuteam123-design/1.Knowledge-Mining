ABSTRACT
Recrystallization in Ni-based single crystal superalloys (NiSXs) frequently occurs during manufacturing or 
transportation, posing challenges to ensuring the integrity and performance of turbine components. In this study, 
we propose an efficient rejuvenation protocol for a fourth-generation Ni-based single crystal superalloy following 
deformation. By gradually increasing the temperature to just below the sub-solidus temperature and applying 
annealing, we effectively suppress the occurrence of recrystallization. Experimental evidence demonstrates that 
partial dissolution of γ’ precipitates broadens the γ channels, promoting interface migration and facilitating 
dislocation annihilation. This approach reduces the driving force for recrystallization and helps preserve the 
single-crystal structure of the superalloy after solution treatment. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are 
employed to analyze the deformation and recovery mechanisms; results show that increased deformation leads to 
a significant rise in dislocation density. The simulations further reveal that superalloys with wider γ channels 
exhibit enhanced coordinated deformation capability, attributed to the reduced obstruction to dislocation motion 
imparted by these wider channels. This study elucidates the effectiveness of the proposed rejuvenation protocol 
and offers valuable insights for the maintenance and longevity of single-crystal turbine blades.
1. Introduction
NiSXs have been extensively employed in gas turbines and aerospace 
engines due to their superior fatigue and creep resistance under extreme 
operating conditions [ 1 , 2 ]. Their exceptional performance is funda -
mentally attributed to their unique microstructure, which consists of a 
high volume fraction of ordered, cubic γ’ precipitates coherently 
embedded within a γ matrix [ 3 ]. However, during the manufacturing, 
assembly, and transportation of components like turbine blades, local -
ized plastic strain and associated defects inevitably arise [ 1 , 4 ]. The 
stored energy from this plastic deformation can subsequently trigger 
recrystallization during heat treatment or high-temperature service, 
leading to the formation of deleterious high-angle grain boundaries 
[ 5 – 8 ]. These boundaries disrupt the single-crystal integrity and act as 
preferential sites for crack initiation, severely compromising the me -
chanical properties of the component [ 1 , 9 ]. Therefore, a critical chal -
lenge lies in developing a method to rejuvenate pre-strained superalloys 
by eliminating plastic-strain-induced defects (e.g., dislocations) while 
preserving the essential γ / γ’ two-phase microstructure and the single- 
crystal nature of the material [ 10 – 13 ]. An effective recovery strategy 
can mitigate or remove the driving force for recrystallization, thereby 
extending the operational lifetime of turbine blades and reducing 
maintenance costs [ 14 ].
The efficacy of rejuvenation is typically dependent on the level of 
pre-strain, while the treatment duration is governed by temperature. 
Previous research on rejuvenation heat treatment (RHT) has often 
focused on relatively low temperatures (~0.5 homologous temperature) 
[ 8 , 11 ]. However, this low temperature of RHT is inapplicable for reju -
venation of NiSXs due to the complex two-phase microstructures. Even 
the RHT is succeed, it typically requires dozens of hours, which is un -
acceptable for industrial productivity and energy consumption, thus 
necessitating protocol time reduction. For instance, Yao et al. [ 8 ] suc -
cessfully avoided recrystallization of DD6 with a strain of 1.5 %, but its 
ability is limited and requires 24 h. To address this, some researchers 
have attempted annealing above the solution temperature, but unfor -
tunately, this exceeds the recrystallization temperature of NiSXs 
[ 15 , 16 ]. Xiong et al. [ 17 ] considered the standard solution heat treat -
ment for PWA1483 with a compression deformation of 3.5 %,

3. Results
The crystal orientation of a specimen subjected to 2.7 % compression 
was mapped using EBSD with a 15 μ m step size ( Fig. 3 a). While no 
significant change in the primary crystal orientation was observed, the 
corresponding GND map ( Fig. 3 d) reveals high-density dislocation re -
gions and significant stress concentrations, which serve as potent 
nucleation sites for recrystallization. After ST, the microstructures of 
samples with and without the preceding RHT were markedly different. 
The sample without RHT exhibited large, strain-free recrystallized 
grains after ST ( Fig. 3 b, e). In contrast, the sample that underwent the 
RHT successfully suppressed recrystallization, maintaining its single- 
crystal structure ( Fig. 3 c). And the stress concentration phenomena

improvement in the orientation deviation with recovery. The maximum 
deviation of grain orientation in the pre-strained sample is 9.59

4. Discussion
4.1. The TEM analysis and MD simulations of rejuvenation mechanism
The following discussion addresses the mechanisms of plastic 
deformation and the evolution of dislocations structure at the γ / γ’ 
interface during the RHT. The superior creep resistance of NiSXs is 
attributed to the deformation mechanisms associated with γ / γ’ two- 
phase microstructure. For plastic deformation at room temperature, 
dislocations typically nucleate and interact within the narrow γ chan -
nels. With the strain increasing, dislocations move within the channels 
until they are impeded by the harder γ’ particles. Meanwhile, some 
dislocations with insufficient driving force find it difficult to shear into γ’ 
particles, following the Orowan mechanism and bending into adjacent γ 
channels [ 40 , 41 ]. The ultimate result of this bending is the presence of a

by adding two additional columns of γ lattices in the x, y, and z di -

microstructures of the superalloys undergoing the same plastic defor -

annealing. At the onset of plastic deformation, the stacking faults ratio

strain. As strain increases, dislocations begin to move and propagate, 
with dislocation density increasing rapidly at first and then transitioning 
to a smoother, slower increase. Comparing Fig. 7 a and b indicates that 
the wide γ channel structure exhibits better coordinated deformation 
capability. During deformation, dislocations find it easier to move 
within the soft γ phase, although this is not evident at low strains ( < 0.5

sesses a similar wide channel structure, which significantly clarifies the 
rejuvenation mechanism. Then, the proportions of dislocation types and 
their distribution in both structures were calculated and analyzed. It is 
noteworthy that although a large number of dislocations arise within the 
superalloys, most of them are confined to the γ channels ( Fig. 8 a). Dis -
locations frequently accumulate at the γ / γ’ interface, with few disloca -
tions sheared into the γ’ particles. At a strain level of approximately 3 %, 
dislocations are ordinarily difficult to shear into the γ’ particles. 
Furthermore, a high percentage of Shockley dislocations is observed in 
both structures ( Fig. 8 b), and the presence of these highly active dislo -
cations is beneficial for rejuvenation. Anyway, the widened γ channels 
under sub-solidus annealing provide the potential for dislocation reac -
tion, facilitating dislocation movement, annihilation of opposite-sign 
dislocations, significantly reducing dislocation density, and success -
fully avoiding recrystallization.

In a word, after low plastic deformation, dislocations primarily 
nucleate within the γ channels, with few within γ’ particles. At the onset 
of rejuvenation, partial dissolution of γ’ particles occur, the γ / γ’ in -
terfaces begin to move, and the γ matrix channels widen, providing 
ample space for the entangled dislocations within the channels. Subse -
quently, eligible dislocations interact with each other and annihilate 
later, leading to a significant reduction in dislocation density and pro -
portion of stacking faults, ultimately forming a low-energy dislocation 
network configuration that does not meet the conditions to trigger 
recrystallized nucleation. The NiSXs with recovery successfully maintain 
its γ / γ’ two-phase microstructure and single crystal characteristic during 
subsequent heat treatment or high-temperature working conditions, 
whereas recrystallized grains were readily observed without recovery, 
resulting in unfavorable high-angle grain boundaries ( Fig. 11 ). When the 
strain increases, a large number of dislocations are sheared into γ’ par -
ticles, and in addition to the dislocations accumulated at the γ / γ’ 
interface, the center of γ’ phase is filled with low-activity dislocations. 
For such samples, more driving force may be required for central dis -
locations to move and interact. This necessitates more systematic 
research to confirm whether NiSXs with higher plastic strain can be 
recovered through RHT. The high dislocation density and proportion of 
stacking faults significantly increase the difficulty of the rejuvenation 
and may even lead to failure ( Fig. 9 b, c). The present study indicates that 
the key to recovery lies in the partially dissolved γ’ particles providing 
the reaction conditions for the high-density dislocations. When the 
deformation increases, highly active dislocations are more conducive to 
the rejuvenation of stored energy; otherwise, the recrystallization will 
be triggered readily.

5. Conclusion
In this study, an innovative and rapid rejuvenation protocol for a pre- 
strained NiSX was developed and its underlying mechanisms were 
investigated using TEM and MD simulations. The main conclusions are 
summarized as follows: 
(1) A rapid (2 h) RHT protocol was successfully developed that 
suppresses recrystallization in a pre-strained NiSX. This protocol 
significantly elevates the reversible plastic deformation threshold 
to 3.3 %, a marked improvement over the conventional ~2 % 
limit, while drastically reducing processing time and cost;

(2) The success of the protocol is attributed to sub-solidus annealing, 
which causes the partial dissolution of γ’ particles. This promotes 
the movement of the γ / γ’ interface, leading to a widen γ channels. 
Extra space allows dislocations to change their configuration to 
reduce energy or lead to annihilation through dislocation re -
actions, thereby reducing dislocation density;
(3) MD simulations confirm that at low strains, dislocations are pri -
marily confined to the γ matrix and γ / γ’ interface. At high strains, 
however, a significant fraction of dislocations shears into the γ’ 
precipitates, forming sessile dislocation locks (e.g., Hirth and 
stair-rod dislocations). These immobile dislocations trap stored 
energy, hindering recovery and ultimately leading to the failure 
of the rejuvenation process at high pre-strains.
This work provides an efficient RHT methodology that can reduce 
the rejection rate and manufacturing costs of single-crystal turbine 
blades. The elucidated mechanisms offer new insights for the design and 
processing of advanced Ni-based superalloys. Future research should 
focus on optimizing the protocol for even shorter durations or higher 
strain levels, which could further enhance production efficiency.
