Microstructure and high-temperature tensile property of TiAl
alloy produced by selective electron beam melting
Hang-Yu Yue , Hui Peng* , Yong-Jun Su* , Xiao-Peng Wang, Yu-Yong Chen
Received: 23 April 2020 / Revised: 2 June 2020 / Accepted: 11 December 2020
/C211Youke Publishing Co., Ltd. 2021
Abstract The microstructure and high-temperature tensile
property of a Ti-47Al-2Cr-2Nb alloy fabricated via selec-
tive electron beam melting (SEBM) with hatch spacings of
85, 100, and 115 lm were systematically investigated.
When the hatch spacing increased from 85 to 115 lm, the
microstructure comprising the horizontal cross section
changed from coarse lamellar ( c/B2) colonies to an inho-
mogeneous structure and the grain morphology trans-
formed from elongated grains to inhomogeneous and
equiaxed grains along the building direction of the vertical
cross section. The boundary population of the SEBM-
produced TiAl alloy samples was dominated by high-angle
grain boundaries ( C 15/C176), and the volume fraction of these
boundaries decreased with hatch spacing increasing.
Additionally, the as-built TiAl alloy sample produced
under a spacing of 100 lm exhibited the highest room- and
elevated-temperature tensile strengths, with the ultimate
tensile strength at room temperature (642 MPa) increasing
to 674 MPa at 700 /C176C. Furthermore, the mechanism of
anomalous strengthening at 700 /C176C was discussed in detail.
Keywords Selective electron beam melting; TiAl alloy;
Hatch spacing; Microstructure; Tensile property
1 Introduction
TiAl alloys have received considerable interest due to a
combination of unique features, such as low density, high
speciﬁc strength, excellent creep properties, and resistance
to oxidation [ 1–4]. Therefore, TiAl alloys are considered
potential structural materials for replacing the heavier
nickel-based superalloys used in high-temperature appli-
cations in the aerospace ﬁeld [ 5]. However, TiAl alloys
exhibit poor room-temperature ductility and hot workabil-
ity. The fabrication of complex geometry TiAl alloy parts
via traditional processing techniques is therefore difﬁcult.
As a newly developed additive manufacturing (AM)
technique, selective electron beam melting (SEBM) is
highly applicable to the direct fabrication of complex-
shaped parts with nearly full densiﬁcation from a metallic
powder. This technique utilizes user-deﬁned computer-
aided design (CAD) data ﬁles and is superior to the con-
ventional fabricating methods [ 6, 7]. The SEBM process
also affords various advantages, such as fewer production
processes (than conventional methods), high ﬂexibility, and
efﬁciency of material consumption. Moreover, due to the

Preliminary investigations have been performed where
the effect of SEBM processing parameters (e.g., beam
current, scanning speed, and preheating temperature) on
the microstructure and mechanical properties of the mate-
rial is considered. Murr et al. [ 10] characterized the
microstructure of SEBM-produced titanium aluminide
alloy and veriﬁed the feasibility of using SEBM to directly
fabricate near net shape TiAl alloy samples with complex
shapes. Biamino et al. [ 11] manufactured Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb
alloy using SEBM and investigated the microstructure and
tensile properties after SEBM and after heat treatment. The
results indicated that excellent tensile properties of TiAl
alloy can be obtained by means of SEBM. Schwerdtfeger
and Ko¨ rner [12] studied the effect of process parameters on
Al loss and porosity and determined a broad process win-
dow for SEBM of Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb. Kan et al. [ 13]
investigated the effects of preheating parameters on the
microstructure and tensile properties of SEBM-produced
high Nb-containing TiAl alloy and found that different
microstructures could be obtained by adjusting the pre-
heating parameters. The effects of scanning speed and
beam current on the microstructure and mechanical prop-
erties were investigated, and the results showed that pro-
cess parameters had a signiﬁcant inﬂuence on these
characteristics [ 14, 15].
Since SEBM-produced components are built in a layer-
by-layer fashion and each layer is fabricated in a track-by-
track manner, track overlapping has a substantial inﬂuence
on the ultimate density and building rate. This is deter-
mined by the distance between two adjacent tracks and is
generally referred to as the ‘ ‘hatch spacing’ ’ [16]. In gen-
eral, a high building rate can be obtained by increasing the
hatch spacing and decreasing the overlapping level. A low
level of defects (e.g., insufﬁcient melting and porosity) is
required for ensuring the desired density and properties by
increasing the overlapping and decreasing the spacing.
However, to the best of our knowledge, only a few pub-
lished studies have considered the effect of hatch spacing
on the microstructure and high-temperature tensile prop-
erties of SEBM-produced TiAl alloys. Yang et al. [ 17]
investigated the effect of scan line spacing on the texture
and nanohardness of TiAl/TiB
2 metal matrix composites
produced via selective laser melting (SLM). The results
revealed that the spacing had a signiﬁcant inﬂuence on the
microstructure and nanohardness. However, the
microstructure and mechanical properties of SLM-pro-
duced TiAl alloys differ considerably from those of SEBM-
produced TiAl alloys [ 15, 18]. Therefore, an overall
investigation into the inﬂuence of hatch spacing on the
microstructure and mechanical properties, especially the
high-temperature tensile properties, of SEBM-produced
TiAl alloys is warranted.
In the present work, the SEBM technique was used to
fabricate a Ti-47Al-2Cr-2Nb alloy and the inﬂuence of the
hatch spacing on the porosity and microstructure of TiAl
alloys was systematically investigated. Furthermore, tensile
properties at room temperature, 700 and 750 /C176C were
measured and the anomalous strengthening of the SEBM-
produced TiAl alloy was explained in detail. The ﬁndings
will serve as a valuable reference for producing TiAl
components with desired microstructure and tensile prop-
erties under a relatively high building rate.
2 Experimental
The samples were fabricated from a pre-alloyed powder
with a nominal composition of Ti-47Al-2Cr-2Nb. This
powder was prepared by means of electron induction
melting gas atomization. The particle diameter ranged from
45 to 200 lm, as determined by a laser particle size dis-
tribution analyzer (LA-920, HORIBA, Japan) [ 15]. Induc-
tively coupled plasma spectroscopy (ICP, ONH836, LECO,
America) was used to determine the composition of the
pre-alloyed powder. The contents of Ti, Al, Cr, Nb, O, and
N were 48.85 at%, 47.02 at%, 1.98 at% 1.94 at%,
0.16 at% and 0.02 at%, respectively.
SEBM experiments were conducted on an Arcam A2XX
machine in manual mode. Cubes measuring 20 mm
9 20 mm 9 20 mm were built on a stainless-steel sub-
strate. During the SEBM process, scanning was conducted
in a snakelike manner with 90 /C176rotation between adjacent
layers. A detailed description and schematic illustration of
the scanning strategy are provided in Ref. [ 15]. Based on
many previous experiments, the following process param-
eters were employed in this work: beam current of 5.5 mA,
scanning speed of 1800 mm /C1s
-1, layer thickness of 90 lm,
and preheating temperature of 1100 /C176C. The inﬂuence of
the hatch spacing on the microstructure and mechanical
properties was investigated by considering spacings of
85 lm (Sample H1), 100 lm (Sample H2) and 115 lm
(Sample H3). A sketch of the spacing is shown in Fig. 1.

3 Results and discussion
3.1 Melting line morphologies, porosity, and Al loss
Figure 3 shows the effect of hatch spacing on the melting
line morphologies and porosity of SEBM-produced TiAl
alloy samples. The results revealed that, irrespective of
hatch spacing, the samples were characterized by a rela-
tively smooth surface with very few visible pores between
the tracks and discontinuity of the melting tracks. More-
over, the melting lines were quite parallel to the scanning
direction due to the optimized parameters, as shown in
Fig. 3a–c. The l
1, l2, and l3 in Fig. 3 represented the
spacing between neighboring track boundaries of Samples
H1, H2, and H3, and they were 102, 131, and 164 lm,
respectively. This indicated that the overlapping between
neighboring tracks decreased gradually with hatch spacing
increasing from 85 to 115 lm, as shown in Fig. 3a–c. At a
low hatch spacing of 85 lm, the melting tracks were tightly
overlapped, and a dense structure with few visible micro-
pores was obtained (Fig. 3d). When the hatch spacing
increased to 100 lm, the number of visible pores increased
slightly, as shown in Fig. 3e. The largest spacing (115 lm)
led to an increase in the porosity, thereby yielding a rela-
tively unsound structure due to the weak overlapping
between the neighboring tracks (Fig. 3f).
The porosities of Samples H1, H2, and H3 (as deter-
mined via statistical analysis) were 0.17%, 0.24%, and
0.35%, respectively, as shown in Fig. 4. These values
indicated that a high level of overlapping between two
adjacent tracks can reduce the porosity. That is, low hatch
spacing led to overﬂow of some micropores from the
solidiﬁed alloy during scanning of the adjacent melting
line. Furthermore, the effect of hatch spacing on Al content
was determined. Al contents of 45.20 at%, 45.96 at%, and
46.38 at% were measured for as-built Samples H1, H2 and
H3, respectively. Therefore, the low spacing resulted in a
high Al loss due to the high level of overlapping during the
SEBM process. This observation indicated that the selec-
tion of an appropriate hatch spacing is necessary. For
example, an excessively large spacing will yield an
increase in the porosity (although the fabrication efﬁciency
may be improved) and an overly small spacing will lead to
an increase in the Al loss.

3.4 Tensile properties
Figure 9 shows the effect of hatch spacing on the tensile
properties of SEBM-produced TiAl alloy samples at room
temperature, 700 and 750 /C176C (Table 1 [21, 22] for a
summary of the results). Sample H2 exhibited the highest
tensile strength and elongation (663 MPa and 0.5%,
respectively) at room temperature. Although the grain size
of Sample H3 was smaller than that of Sample H1, the
tensile strength and elongation of Sample H1 were slightly
higher than those of Sample H3. That is, the relatively high
porosity and inhomogeneous microstructure of Sample H3
had a negative effect on the tensile properties, leading to
stress concentration and crack nucleation and, conse-
quently, low tensile strength. The greater grain size of
Sample H1 (compared with that of Sample H2) exerted a
negative effect on the tensile properties, in accordance with
the Hall–Petch equation.
When testing at 700 and 750 /C176C, Sample H3 exhibited
slightly higher tensile strength and elongation than Sample
H2. This may have resulted from the fact that, compared
with the grain size of Sample H2, the ﬁne grain size of
Sample H3 had a greater effect on the tensile properties.
That is, the ﬁne grains hindered dislocation motion and
promoted grain boundary sliding at elevated temperatures,
thereby enhancing the tensile strength and elongation.
The elongation level of SEBM-produced TiAl alloy at
700 /C176C was superior to the levels of the alloys fabricated
via traditional methods. This superior elongation resulted
from the ﬁne grain size of the SEBM-produced material,
which was smaller than that of the as-cast TiAl alloy [ 23].
Fine grains promote grain boundary slip and are beneﬁcial

SEBM-produced samples exhibited greater tensile strength
and elongation than those of the room-temperature-tested
samples (Table 1). The tensile strengths at 700 /C176C for

temperature. However, the strength decreased considerably
and the elongation increased sharply when the testing
temperature was increased to 750 /C176C. Kan et al. [ 13]
reported that the tensile strength of SEBM-produced high-
Nb-containing TiAl alloy was 715 MPa at room tempera-
ture and increased to 820 MPa at 800 /C176C. When the testing
temperature was increased to 900 /C176C, the strength
decreased sharply to 500 MPa. Therefore, the experimental
TiAl alloy underwent anomalous strengthening at elevated
temperature.
4 Discussion
TEM microstructures of Sample H2 before and after tensile
testing at room temperature, 700 and 750 /C176C were com-
pared (Fig. 10). Almost no twins were visible for SEBM-
produced TiAl alloy, and a small number of twins and
dislocations were visible in TEM images after ambient-
temperature tensile testing, as shown in Fig. 10a, b. This
indicated that the activation of twins is difﬁcult during
deformation at ambient temperature. However, signiﬁcant
numbers of dislocations and twins occurred after tensile
testing at 700 /C176C, indicating that the twins are easily acti-
vated at elevated temperature, as shown in Fig. 10c–f. Fine
nanotwinned structures (average twin thickness of 8 nm)
were formed. Figure 10g shows high-resolution transmis-
sion electron microscopy (HRTEM) image of the region
enclosed in the red box marked in Fig. 10f. These struc-
tures are an effective barrier to dislocation motion and lead
to improvement in the tensile ductility and strength
[25, 26]. Moreover, numerous dislocations accumulated
along twin boundaries, as shown in Fig. 10c, e. Compared
with other structures, these boundaries exhibit higher
capacity for dislocation generation, accumulation, and
glide, thereby improving the tensile strength with less
degradation of the plasticity [ 27, 28]. Therefore, the tensile

5 Conclusion
The inﬂuence of hatch spacing on the microstructure and
tensile properties of a SEBM-produced Ti-47Al-2Cr-2Nb
alloy was investigated in this work. When the spacing
increased from 85 to 115 lm, the melting lines of the
samples were quite parallel to the scanning direction and
the spacing between neighboring track boundaries
increased gradually. Similarly, the porosity increased
gradually from 0.17% to 0.35% due to the weak overlap-
ping between the neighboring tracks. When the hatch
spacing increased from 85 to 115 lm, the microstructure
transformed from a homogeneous ( c/B2) lamellar colony
structure into an inhomogeneous structure along the
building direction of the vertical cross section. This
transformation resulted from the different overlapping
between adjacent melting lines. The volume fraction of
HAGBs decreased continuously from 95.2% to 91.6%
when the spacing increased from 85 to 115 lm. Sample H2
fabricated with a hatch spacing of 85 lm exhibited the
highest tensile strength among the samples with different
hatch spacings. The sample produced under a spacing of
100 lm underwent anomalous strengthening, where the
tensile strength and elongation increased from 663 MPa
and 0.5% at room temperature to 724 MPa and 19.3%,
respectively, at 700 /C176C. This strengthening is attributed to
nanostructured twin formation and the dislocation cross-
slip pinning mechanism.
