abstract
The Ti-(0e30)Nb-(2e11)Mo-3.5Ta (wt.%) alloys with the composition interval of 5.0 wt.% for
Nb and 3.0 wt.% for Mo were prepared by hot isostatic pressing. The Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys
with high compressive strength and favorable ductility were obtained, which enabled the
alloys to be used as novel structural or biomedical materials. The phase constituent,
microstructure, and compressive properties were investigated. Although the compressive
yield strength and the strain at failure showed different correlations to the Nb and Mo
content, respectively, the two mechanical property parameters both showed distinctly
monotonic correlations to the Mo equivalent. With the increasing Mo equivalent, the
content of the a phase decreased, while that of the b phase increased. Simultaneously, the
yield strength of the Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys decreased, while the strain at failure increased.
Quantitative analyses were then implemented on the grain reﬁnement strengthening, the
second-phase strengthening of the a phase, and the solid solution strengthening of the Nb
and Mo atoms. After comparing the separate contributions of the above determining fac-
tors, it was conﬁrmed that the second-phase strengthening was the dominating mecha-
nism for improving the mechanical properties of the powder metallurgic Ti eNbeMoeTa
alloys.

1. Introduction
Titanium and its alloys have been the key engineering mate-
rials in the aeronautic and astronautic industries, owing to
their high speciﬁc strength and stiffness, excellent corrosion
resistance, and favorable high-temperature mechanical
properties [ 1,2]. Considering the added advantages such as
biocompatibility and the low modulus that approaches
human bones, Ti alloys are also used as promising biomedical
materials; for instance, orthopedic implants [ 3]. With the
aggravating trend of population aging, as well as the
increasing sports injuries, the surging demand for the Ti-alloy
artiﬁcial joints and dental implants is revealed [ 4]. In the
family of the commercial Ti alloys, the b-type Ti alloys possess
lower modulus and better cold forming capacity than the a
and aþb Ti alloys [5]. Nowadays, extensive efforts are made to
design novel b-type Ti alloys for biomedical applications [ 6].
Among them, the Ti-X (X ¼ Nb, Mo, and Ta) alloys [ 7e9]
without carcinogenic vanadium or aluminum that induces
Alzheimer's disease exhibit the great potential to replace the
conventional Ti e6Ale4V alloys, and the modiﬁed Ti eNbeMo
alloy with low elastic modulus [ 10] was afterward developed.
Moreover, another essential concern lay in the strength of the
modiﬁed Ti eNbeMo alloys. Chui et al. [ 11] fabricated a series
of Ti eZreNbeMo alloys by vacuum electro-arc melting. The
highest yield strength reached 834 MPa, due mainly to the
contributions of the reﬁned grains by Mo addition and the
solution strengthening of the alloying elements. The elastic
modulus was 96 e105 GPa. Wei et al. [ 12] reported the me-
chanical properties of the Ti eNbeZreMo alloys prepared by
cold-crucible levitation melting. The tensile strength was in
the range of 592e702 MPa, the elongation was 10e16%, and the
Young's modulus was 68 e73 GPa. Among the designed alloys,
the Ti e27Nbe33Zre2Mo showed the highest compressive
yield strength of 769 MPa. To explore their biomedical appli-
cation as coronary stents, Al-Zain et al. investigated the shape
memory properties and the super-elastic behavior of the
TieNbeMo alloys [
13,14]. The shape memory properties
strongly rely on the reversible phase transformation of b4a00,
as the Ti eNbeMo coronary stent compressed under low
temperature can expand and return to the desired shape at
the body temperature of the patients. The super-elasticity is
associated with the Mo content, the increase of which brings
about the linearly increased critical stress for the slip defor-
mation. Recently, Zhang et al. tailored the deformation mode
to achieve large super-elasticity in the Ti-13.3Nb-4.6Mo (at.%)
alloy by ultra-grain reﬁnement [ 15]. In addition, the corrosion
resistance of the TieNbeMo alloys was evaluated to guarantee
the safety of the biomedical implantation [ 16,17]. A signiﬁcant
improvement in the corrosion resistance of the materials in
the saline solution was achieved by the addition of Nb to
TieMo alloys. Besides, the alloys containing Ta also exhibited
favorable mechanical properties and corrosion resistance.
Plaine et al. [ 18] fabricated Ti e29Nbe13Ta-4.6 Zr (wt.%) alloys
by cold rolling and low temperature aging. The alloys
possessed a low elastic modulus (46 GPa) while maintaining a
high yield strength of 960 MPa, which was attributed to the
homogeneously distributed a precipitates in the b matrix.
Gudkov et al. [ 19] reported the Ti e30Nbe10Tae5Zr alloy with
the tensile strength of 713 MPa and the elastic modulus as low
as 45 GPa, which enabled the Ti eNbeTa alloys to be an
appropriate material for potential medical applications.
Therefore, a limited amount of Ta might be added to the
TieNbeMo alloys to improve the comprehensive properties.
Intensive efforts have been made to improve the me-
chanical properties or to reduce the elastic modulus of the
TieNbeMo alloys for medical application. Till now, the mostly
used fabrication method for the Ti alloys was still melting and
casting. Due to the high melting points of Nb (2468
/C14 C), Mo
(2617 /C14 C) and Ta (3017 /C14 C), arc-melting is a considerably
energy-consuming and environmentally unfriendly fabrica-
tion technique for the Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys. The Ti alloy in-
gots with uniform chemical composition were normally
required to be melted more than 7 times [ 10]. Given this, the
powder metallurgy technique was adopted to reduce the
manufacturing cost of the alloys containing refractory
metallic elements. More importantly, for the joints and dental
implants with an irregular shape and the coronary stent with
hyperﬁne structure, the powder metallurgy method, a near-
net-shape forming technique, signiﬁcantly reduced the pro-
cessing stages in contrast with the arc-melting method. To
date, the Ti alloys were barely fabricated by powder metal-
lurgy from refractory metals [ 20]. The relationship between
the composition and mechanical properties of the Ti alloys
containing Nb, Mo or Ta was only investigated by individually
changing the Nb, Mo or Ta content in ternary alloys. Even for
those cast alloys, the synergistic effect of the Nb and Mo ele-
ments on the mechanical properties was undiscovered.
Compressive stresses would generate on artiﬁcial joints dur-
ing exercise, and coronary stents would undergo severe
deformation before being transported into the human blood
vessels. These are all possible challenges when the applica-
tion of the Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys in the biomedical ﬁeld
considered. Hence, favorable compressive properties are
essential for the Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys to be satisfactory
biomedical materials [ 21]. While ﬁrstly, it is of great signiﬁ-
cance to uncover the Nb/Mo content dependence of the
compressive properties for the Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys.
In this study, the relationship among the alloy composi-
tion, microstructure, and compressive properties is con-
structed ﬁrstly for the Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys fabricated by
powder metallurgy. Aimed at the medical applications of
special-shaped Ti-alloy components, this study provided a
near-net-shape preparation method for a newly developed
TieNbeMoeTa alloys with excellent mechanical properties.
Moreover, the contributions of the solid solution strength-
ening, grain reﬁning strengthening, and second-phase
strengthening on the yield strength are quantitatively elabo-
rated. On this basis, the dominating strengthening mecha-
nism is conﬁrmed for the powder metallurgic Ti eNbeMoeTa
alloys.

4. Discussion
Since the strengthening effects are fundamentally linked with
the microstructural changes, various contributions to the
strength were taken into consideration, based on detailed
characterization of the microstructure for these
TieNbeMoeTa alloys. The grain size of the alloys was ob-
tained from the OM images ( Supplementary Fig. S1), as shown
in Fig. 3(c). The average grain size presented a monotonically
decreasing tendency with the increasing Mo equivalent, in
agreement with the reported grain reﬁnement effects of the
Nb and Mo elements [ 35]. The possible reason was that the
increasing Nb or Mo solute atoms inhibited the migration of
the b-Ti grain boundaries, and the resistance of the grain
growth was increased [ 36,37]. The volume fraction of the a
phase was calculated from the XRD patterns using the Riet-
veld reﬁnement method [ 38], as shown in Fig. 3 (d). The Riet-
veld reﬁnement results of the typical alloys are shown in
Supplementary Fig. S2 . The volume fraction of the a phase
also presented a monotonically decreasing tendency with the
increasing Mo equivalent, in agreement with the reported b-
stabilizing effects of the Nb and Mo elements [35]. The possible
reason was that the increasing Nb or Mo content lowered the
b/a phase transformation temperature, reducing the nuclear
driving force of the a phase [39].
The reﬁnement of the b grains and the reduction of the a
phase would bring the opposite effects on the yield strength of
the studied alloys. One could determine the dominating
strengthening mechanism from the variation trend of the
curves. When the Mo equivalent increased, the reduced grain
size was supposed to enhance the s
s (Fig. 3 (c)), and the
decreasing volume fraction of the a phase was unfavorable to
the enhancement of the ss (Fig. 3 (d)). Given the decreasing ss
with the increasing Mo equivalent ( Fig. 3 (b)), it was prelimi-
narily suggested the second phase strengthening of the a
phase might be the dominant factor in determining the me-
chanical properties of the Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys. In the
following paragraphs, the contributions of the second-phase
precipitation and the grain reﬁnement to the yield strength
were individually evaluated.

5. Conclusions
The relationship between the alloy composition, microstruc-
ture, and compressive properties was investigated for the
TieNbeMoeTa alloys prepared by the HIP. The following
conclusions were drawn.
(1) With the increasing Mo equivalent, the strength of the
TieNbeMoeTa alloys prepared by powder metallurgy
decreased from 1470 MPa to 883 MPa, and the strain at
failure increased from 0.11 to 0.63. The grain size
(ranging from 85.5 to 30.8 mm) and the volume fraction
of a phase (ranging from 0.49 to 0) both decreased with
the increasing Mo equivalent.

(2) Through the quantitative analyses of three strength-
ening mechanisms, the second phase strengthening
was suggested to be the dominating mechanism in
improving the strength of the Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys
with the Mo equivalent of 8.9 e18.0 wt.%. The grain
reﬁning strengthening and the solid solution strength-
ening effects also contributed to the yield strength of
the Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys, playing a minor role in the
strength improvement.
(3) Three types of a phase existed in the Ti eNbeMoeTa
alloys with small Mo equivalent, including net-like a
phase along the grain boundaries, lamellar a phase near
the grain boundaries, and acicular Widmanst €atten a
phase within the b grains. The lamellar and acicular a
phase simultaneously disappeared when the Mo
equivalent was increased to 14.0 e15.0 wt.%, and the
net-like a phase disappeared subsequently at the Mo
equivalent /C21 15.0 wt.%. Accordingly, the strength
decreased and the strain at failure increased with the
decrease in the amount, number of types, and size of
the a phase.
(4) The Ti e25Nbe5Mo-3.5Ta and Ti e25Nbe8Mo-3.5Ta al-
loys, with Mo equivalent falling in the range of
13.0e16.0 wt.%, exhibited favorable comprehensive
mechanical properties as biomaterials. For the alloys
with satisfactory properties, the yield strength falls
within the scope of 900 e1000 MPa, and strain at failure
falls within the scope of 0.45 e0.65. The initial experi-
mental results in this study encourage one to further
optimize the properties of the Ti eNbeMoeTa alloys for
biomedical applications.
