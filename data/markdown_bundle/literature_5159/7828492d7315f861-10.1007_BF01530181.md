An increase in the operating parameters for structures in the cosmlc-rocket, aviation, 
and other fields of technology leads to a situation where many refractory metals exhibiting 
quite a high melting point nevertheless operate at the limit of their potential. This ex- 
plains the intensive search for new, more heat-resistant metals, and correspondingly there 
is an increase in the importance of solving the traditionally vital problem of evaluating 
reliability and long-term operating capacity. 
A series of available procedures at the Institute of Strength Problems of the Academy 
of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR [i, 2] make it possible to obtain quite extensive information 
about the hlgh-temperature strength of refractory metals. Careful analysis of this informa- 
tion indicates that in order to improve prediction of refractory-metal properties it is neces- 
sary to systematize existing experimental data since a strictly theoretical solution for the 
tasks being considered remains a problem. It is sufficient to note that one of the most im- 
portant characteristics of long-term loss of strength -- the exponent n in the theoretically 
substantiated Weertman relationship ~ - o n [3], which is quite closely connected with the 
relationship t.o~ o -n [4, 5] (o is the stress; ~ is the secondary creep rate; t is the 
endurance) -- for pure metals at elevated temperatures and average stress levels should be a 
constant. In fact, as treatment of experimental data indicates, values of n may differ marked- 
ly from values substantiated theoretically. 
In view of this, a basic task for the current study is a search for effective ways of 
carrying out more careful comparative analysis of features of hlgh-temperature long-term loss 
of strength for different refractory metals determined by the time-dependent intensity of 
reduction in long-term strength, and in particular constant n. 
In order to solve this problem we use a previously suggested [6, 7] method of average 
strength relationships intended for rapid evaluation of creep-rupture strength which makes 
it possible not only to ascertain features of long-term high-temperature loss of strength, 
but also to improve the efficiency of evaluation due to consideration of other features.

Thus, it is possible to confirm that in studying the long-term high-temperature loss of 
strength of refractory metals it is not entirely possible to define a specific reduction in 
creep-rupture strength, as follows from the secondary creep theory of Weertman and well-known 
ideas about the correlation of creep and creep-rupture strength. As analysis of data pre- 
sented in Figs. 2 and 3 indicates, the time-dependent intensity of reduction in creep-rupture 
strength may differ markedly for different materials both with similar and markedly different 
test conditions. 
Moreover, in spite of the differences noted in the tlme-dependent intensity of the reduc- 
tion in creep-rupture strength in the cases considered, a quite general tendency is developed 
which is governed by heat treatment, material condition, test temperature level, the degree 
of alloying, etc. Systematization of experimental data by means of average strength depen- 
dences makes it possible to define the effect of different factors on the intensity of the 
tlme-dependent reduction in creep-rupture strength and to increase in this way the efficiency 
of rapid estimation of creep-rupture strength characteristics for refractory materials.
