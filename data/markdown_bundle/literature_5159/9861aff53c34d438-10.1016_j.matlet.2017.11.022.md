abstract
The effect of hydrogen content on room temperature compressive properties of Ti44Al6Nb (at.%) alloy
was studied. The results show that hydrogen promotes a solid solution strengthening in TiAl alloys,
the microhardness increases with increasing of hydrogen content, which is increased by 40% after hydro-
genation with 1.22 at.% H. As hydrogen content increases, the ultimate compressive strength and fracture
strain increases ﬁrstly and then decreases. The Ti44Al6Nb alloy exhibits higher compressive strength and
plasticity after addition of 0.42 at.% H, which is enhanced by 10% and 31% respectively. The distinct effect
of hydrogen on the mechanical properties of TiAl alloys is attributed to the hydrogen-promoted disloca-
tion motion and hydrogen-induced solution strengthening.

1. Introduction
TiAl alloys have been intensively studied for their excellent
mechanical properties, such as high speciﬁc strength, good creep
resistance and excellent oxidation resistance, and thus have the
potential for high temperature applications [1–3]. A foremost
application under consideration for TiAl alloys is in the engines
of hypersonic airplanes and high-speed civil transport airplanes
which are powered by hydrogen fuel [4]. As a result, TiAl alloys
are inevitably exposed to a hydrogen environment and the service
condition becomes complicated. Recently, some reports show that
hydrogen reduces the hardness, the elastic modulus, the yield
strength and enhances the plasticity of TiAl alloys, which is attrib-
uted to the hydrogen-weakened bonds and hydrogen-promoted
dislocation movement [5,6]. The effect of hydrogen on the mechan-
ical properties of TiAl alloys is complicated, including the effect of
hydrogen on microstructure and the effect of hydrogen atoms on
mechanical properties. The objective of the present work is to
study the effect of hydrogen on the mechanical properties of
Ti44Al6Nb alloy, and the mechanism is also discussed.
2. Experimental procedure
The materials used in this study is Ti44Al6Nb alloy prepared by
vacuum self-consuming arc melting, and samples with the size of
U 8m m /C212 mm were cut from the ingot. The hydrogenation
was carried out on a volumetric apparatus which was introduced
detail in previous studies [7,8]. Hydrogen content was calculated
from the variation of hydrogen pressure in the pressure cell and
reaction cell before and after the samples absorption of hydrogen.
The samples were soaked for 1800 s at 673 K and hydrogen content
in the samples was controlled by hydrogen pressure range from 4
MPa to 10 MPa. Vickers hardness was measured on polished cross-
section surfaces using 500 g loading for 10 s, and the microhard-
ness of each sample was the average of 20 points. The compression
was carried out on Instron-5500R machine with a constant com-
pressive velocity of 0.05 mm/min. The microstructures were ana-
lyzed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission
electron microscopy (TEM). The samples used for SEM observation
were ground, polished, and slightly chemically etched by a Kroll
solution (10 vol% HNO3 + 10 vol% HF + 80 vol% H 2O). The TEM spec-
imens were ground to about 80 lm in thickness and then were
twin-jet polished with solution of 6 vol% perchloric acid +34 vol%
butanol +60 vol% methanol at about /C020 /C176C and 25 V.

where the thickness of a2 lamellas and c lamellas is 133 nm and
328 nm respectively based on the statistics results. The
microstructures of hydrogenated alloy are not changed compared

Hydride is not observed by TEM because of low the hydrogenation
temperature (673 K) and the higher Al content which leads to the
interstitial site blocking of hydrogen by Al atoms, consequentially
hydride is not formed [9]. Therefore, it is considered that most of
the hydrogen dissolves in the a2 and c phases. The solubility of
hydrogen in a2 phase and c phase is about 8 at.% and 4 at.% at
923 K and 8 MPa respectively [10]. A. Menand [11] shows that a2
and c phases can absorb interstitial atoms, such as oxygen and
nitrogen. First-principles calculation results reveal that hydrogen
atoms occupy the octahedral sites of a2 phase ﬁrstly and then
occupy the octahedral sites of c phase [12].
The microhardness versus hydrogen content is shown in Fig. 2 .
The microhardness increases with increasing of hydrogen content,
which is increased from 248 ± 3.2 HV to 348 ± 3.1 HV after addition
of 1.22 at.% H. This is attributed to the dissolution of hydrogen
because of the unchanged microstructure. The ﬁrst-principles cal-
culation reveals all the TiAl-H phases experience volume expan-
sion, and the sites hydrogen occupied has a great effect on
volume expansion [12]. The volume expansion leads to formation
of stress ﬁeld which experiences interactions with dislocations
resulting in hindering the dislocation movement. As a result, the
hardness increases with increasing of solute concentration.
The effect of hydrogen content on the compressive properties of
Ti44Al6Nb alloy is shown in Fig. 3 . The compressive strength of
hydrogenated alloys is higher than that of unhydrogenated alloy
during plastic deformation. The ultimate compressive strength
increases ﬁrstly and then decreases with increasing of hydrogen
content, the ultimate compressive strength is increased by 10%
after addition of 0.42 at.% H. The fracture strain increases with
increasing of hydrogen content and reaches the maximum with
0.42 at.% H, which is enhanced by about 30%. It is considered that
a certain amount of hydrogen improves the compressive properties
of TiAl alloy. J.L. Abanto-Bueno reported that hydrogen improved
the tensile strength and ductility of Ti47Al2Cr0.2Si alloy which
agrees well with this study [13]. Hydrogen-induced strengthening
is also observed in TC21 and Ti-6Al-4V alloys by Yuan et al. [14].

induced improvement the mechanical properties of TiAl alloys,

complex mechanisms are expected. The microstructure is not
changed after hydrogenation, thus the improvement of mechanical
property results from the solution of hydrogen. When hydrogen
content is low, hydrogen dissolves in the interstitial sites and the
dislocations, which can activate the single slip system and promote
the dislocation movement resulting in high plasticity and low yield
strength. The effect of hydrogen on yield strength includes two
aspects: hydrogen-induced the decrease of yield strength by pro-
moting dislocation movement and hydrogen-induced the increase
of yield strength by formation of solution strengthening. With
increasing of strain, the dislocation density increases and results
in high ultimate compressive strength. With increasing of hydro-
gen content (>0.42 at.%), the solution strengthening are more obvi-
ous, more slip systems are activated and the dislocation interaction
becomes intensity, the work hardening is obvious and the yield
strength is increased, consequently the plasticity is decreased.
Besides, the hydrogen atoms gather in the grain boundary and
the interface of a2 and c plates leading to large internal stress, it
is considered that hydrogen embrittlement occurs, which is also
observed in TiAl alloys and steels [15,16]. Thus, the ultimate com-
pressive strength is decreased as well as the fracture strain. Fig. 4
shows the typical microstructures of unhydrogenated and hydro-
genated (0.42 at.% H) alloys after compression. The main deforma-
tion mechanisms of TiAl alloys are dislocation gliding and twinning
at room temperature. There are many twins and dislocation tan-
gles in unhydrogenated alloy shown in Fig. 4 (a) and (b), and the
twins with similar morphology are also observed by T. Voisin
[17]. The density of dislocation around the
a2 phase is high because
of the inhomogeneous deformation. However, the dislocation tan-
gles and dislocation around a2 are disappeared in hydrogenated
alloy and the dislocation line is observed in the c phases, indicating
that the deformation is homogeneous as shown in Fig. 4(c) and (d).
Hydrogen reduces dislocation density and dislocation tangles,
which results in increasing of plasticity. Hydrogen promotes
dislocation motion and decreases ﬂow stress of the titanium alloy
and TiAl alloys during hot deformation, which agrees well with this
study [18,19].
4. Conclusions
To summarize, hydrogen plays a major role in solution
strengthening in TiAl alloys rather than softening. The microhard-
ness increases with increasing of hydrogen content, which
increases from 248 ± 3.2 HV to 348 ± 3.1 HV with hydrogen con-
tent increasing to 1.22 at.% due to the hydrogen-induced solution
strengthening. Addition of 0.42 at.% H can enhance the compres-
sive strength and strain of Ti44Al6Nb alloy by 10% and 31% respec-
tively. This is attributed to the hydrogen-induced solution
strengthening and hydrogen-promoted dislocation movement.
