Anterior cervical discectomy and interbody fusion with porous tantalum
implant. Results in a series with long-term follow-up

abstract
Few papers have been published about the anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) with implant
of porous tantalum cages. These studies included patients submitted to operation at a single level. To our
knowledge, we report the results of the largest series of ACDF with implant of porous tantalum cages. Our
series included patients operated at a single or double level with a long follow-up (FU). We also discuss
the pertinent literature. Clinical and outcome data of 99 consecutive patients (47 men, 52 women) sub-
mitted to ACDF with implant of porous tantalum cages (Trabecular Metal TM-S Cervical Fusion Device,
Zimmer Spine, Minneapolis, MN) from June 2007 to September 2012, were retrospectively reviewed.
Mean FU was 67.47 ± 19.63 months. The changes in pain were assessed using the Visual Analogue
Scale (VAS). Patients were evaluated pre-operatively and at FU with the Short Form-36 Score Health
Survey Version 2.0 (SF-36v2) for Physical Health and Mental Health Scores and the Neck Disability
Index. We globally found a statistically signiﬁcant improvement of all evaluated scores. Patients operated
at two levels experienced a statistically signiﬁcant improvement of all scores, with no statistical differ-
ence compared to patients operated at one level. No major complications occurred post-operatively
and at FU. Only one patient (operated at two level) experienced an infection during FU. We conclude that
ACDF with porous tantalum cages is a safe procedure, with long term clinical beneﬁts (also in patients
operated at two levels) and a very low rate of complications.

1. Introduction
Anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) is a widely
accepted procedure in the treatment of degenerative cervical disc
disease [1,2]. Fusion is usually obtained by using autograft from
the iliac crest [3,4] with clinical satisfactory results and good out-
come reported in at least 75–80% of the patients [5–9]. However
the evidence of a non-infrequent complications at the donor-site
[10–12], such as infections, hematomas and longstanding pain,
has promoted the use of allograft [1,2,13–15], some other osteo-
conductive materials [16], and cages of different materials
[7,17,18]. In the last few years a great interest has raised for
Trabecular Metal (TM) which is a porous tantalum biomaterial
with structure and mechanical properties similar to trabecular
bone. It has proved to be more osteoconductive than other com-
mercially available biomaterials [19–21] producing slight artifact
in magnetic resonance imaging and permitting good imaging of
surrounding structures [22,23]. However, despite the increasing
use, few papers have been published reporting the results of ACDF
with implant of porous tantalum cages [24–27] and all these stud-
ies included patients submitted to operation at a single level. Here
we report our large series with a long follow-up (FU) of patients
submitted to ACDF with implant of porous tantalum cages at a sin-
gle or double levels and discuss the pertinent literature.
2. Materials and methods
We retrospectively reviewed clinical and outcome data of 99
consecutive patients (47 men, 52 women) submitted to ACDF with
implant of porous tantalum cages (Trabecular Metal TM-S Cervical
Fusion Device, Zimmer Spine, Minneapolis, MN) from June 2007 to
September 2012, at Institute of Neurosurgery, Catholic University,
Rome. All patients signed a written informed consent. The use of
this implant was approved by our Institution. The approval of IRB
was not required due to retrospective nature of the study. The
mean age was 49.24 ± 11.90 years. The mean follow-up (FU) was
67.47 ± 19.63 months (range: 31–97 months). All patients had no

3. Results
We globally found a statistically signiﬁcant improvement of
VAS (pre-operative 7.77 ± 2.51, at FU 1.80 ± 1.48; p < 0.0001)
(Fig. 1 ), SF-36 (PCS) (pre-operative 32.27 ± 3.79, at FU
43.10 ± 3.92; p < 0.0001) ( Fig. 2 ), SF-36 (MCS) (pre-operative
39.78 ± 4.15, at FU 48.83 ± 3.45; p < 0.0001) ( Fig. 2), and Neck Dis-
ability Index (%; pre-operative 50.5 ± 12.8, at FU 15.6 ± 11.7;
p < 0.0001) ( Fig. 3 ). Patients operated at two levels experienced a
statistically signiﬁcant improvement of VAS (pre-operative
8.05 ± 2.59, at FU 1.94 ± 1.45; p < 0.0001), SF-36 (PCS) (pre-
operative 32.00 ± 3.85, at FU 42.38 ± 4.07; p < 0.0001), SF-36
(MCS) (pre-operative 39.58 ± 3.87, at FU 48.41 ± 3.30; p < 0.0001)
and Neck Disability Index (%; pre-operative 51.7 ± 12.1, at FU
15.4 ± 11.6; p < 0.0001) with no statistically signiﬁcant difference
compared to patients who received surgery at one level.
Moreover, patients with myelopathy experienced a statistically
signiﬁcant improvement of VAS (pre-operative 6.88 ± 3.07, at FU
1.61 ± 1.38; p < 0.0001), SF-36 (PCS) (pre-operative 33.73 ± 4.67,
at FU 43.88 ± 4.16; p < 0.0001), SF-36 (MCS) (pre-operative
40.26 ± 3.76, at FU 49.84 ± 2.89; p < 0.0001) and Neck Disability
Index (%; pre-operative 51.1 ± 13.8, at FU 16.5 ± 13.7; p < 0.0001).
Mean operation length was 73.97 ± 22.17 minutes (59.96 ± 8.14
and 100.73 ± 14.39 minutes for one level and two levels ACDF,
respectively). No major complications occurred post-operatively
and at FU. More speciﬁcally, no cerebrospinal ﬂuid leak, no pullout
of cages and no case of subsidence were observed at FU. In one
patient (operated at two level) an infection occurred after
8 months, requiring cages removal and antibiotic therapy. In
another patient we observed an adjacent segment disease after
24 months, not requiring surgery at latest FU. An illustrative
patient submitted to ADCF at two levels is shown in Figure 4 .
4. Discussion
The aim of our study was to report our experience about ACDF,
using the porous tantalum cages. In our opinion, the main
strengths of this study are the large number of patients, the long
FU and the fact that, for the ﬁrst time, the results of ACDF with por-
ous tantalum cages are reported in patients operated at two cervi-
cal levels. In fact, when looking at the literature, we found that, to
our knowledge, no studies included more patients than ours and
only one paper had a FU of 5 years, [27].
Löfgren et al. [25], comparing ACDF with porous tantalum cages
with traditional procedure using autograft, reported the results in

40 patients with single level ACDF with unﬁlled porous tantalum
ring with a FU of 2 years (two patients had myelopathy). In 79%
of patients an improvement of neck and arm symptoms was
reported. Similarly, Fernandez-Fairen et al. [26] published their
experience in 28 single level cervical ACDF using unﬁlled porous
tantalum ring. In their study, with no patient showing myelopathy,
they reported a good/excellent outcome in 78.7% of patients. These
good results were conﬁrmed at 5-year FU [27]. Kaslival et al. [24],
comparing three groups (11 patients: fusion with iliac autograft;
13 patients: fusion with porous tantalum ring packed with cancel-
lous iliac crest autograft; 15 patients receiving a porous tantalum
block device) reported an improvement of pain, motor and sensory
function in all groups. These good results were conﬁrmed in our
large series in whom we observed an improvement of all consid-
ered scores in all patients, irrespective to the evidence of myelopa-
thy, the number of levels operated and the rate of fusion. It is
important to note that the rate of fusion of porous tantalum cages,
previously extensively investigated [24–26] doesn’t seem to affect
the clinical outcome of patients. In fact, in their comparative study,
Lofgren et al. [25] found no statistically signiﬁcant difference in
clinical outcomes between fusion techniques (autograft vs. allo-
graft) or between patients who appeared radiologically fused or
nonfused after cages placement. Similarly, Kaslival et al. [24] found
that the fusion rate for the tantalum device was very low as com-
pared with the autograft, but all clinical scores were similar among
the three treatment groups. Moreover, Fernandez-Fairen et al. [26]
showed a radiologic and clinical outcomes similar for all groups of
treatment without signiﬁcant difference, reporting a complication
rate considerably higher for the autologous graft plus plating pro-
cedure than for tantalum implant.
It has been previously reported that the presence of artifacts on
post-operative MRI could suggest severe ventral compression of
the operated cervical spinal cord level [28,29]. In our experience
the spinal cord compression can be excellently assessed in the
post-operative setting on T2-weighted sagittal spine MRI, because
there are no artifacts due to the cages on these images ( Fig. 4 B).
These data seem to be conﬁrmed by Elliot et al. [30] who evaluated
the impact of post-operative MRI artifacts on the assessment of
spinal cord or nerve root compression after ACDF, using tantalum
cages. They conﬁrmed that the paramagnetic implant construct
artifact had minimal impact on visualization of post-operative sur-
gical level spinal cord compression. Nonetheless this implant
decreased the visualization of surgical level nerve roots. Thus they
suggest to routinely perform in the post-operative protocol a turbo
spin echo T2-weighted sequences that signiﬁcantly improve nerve
root visualization [30].
Another important factor to take into account when performing
ACDF is the operation length. Previous studies [25,26] reported a
statistically signiﬁcant longer operation time for ACDF with auto-
graft. The reported operation length for single level ACDF with por-
ous tantalum device ranged from 53 [26] to 100 [25] minutes. We
conﬁrm that ACDF with porous tantalum device at a single level is
a fast operation requiring almost 1 hour to be performed. In our
experience an extra time of almost 40 minutes is required to per-
form the procedure at the second level.
Our study has some limitations due to the retrospective nature
of collected data. However, to our knowledge, this is the largest
series on ACDF with porous tantalum cages reported until now.
Moreover, this is the ﬁrst series reporting the results on patients
operated at two levels. Based on our experience and the literature
revision, we think that ACDF with porous tantalum cages is a safe
procedure, with long term clinical beneﬁts (also in patients oper-
ated at two levels) and a very low rate of complications.
