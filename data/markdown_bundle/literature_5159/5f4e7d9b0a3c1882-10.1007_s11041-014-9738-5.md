HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRUCTURAL TRANSFORMATIONS AND
PROPERTIES OF SINGLE CRYSTALS OF INTERMETALLIC ALLOY
VKNA-25-VI UPON MICROALLOYING WITH RARE-EARTH ELEMENTS
V . V . Sidorov,1 O. B. Timofeeva, 1 and A. V . Goryunov1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 5, pp. 7 – 10, May, 2014.
The effect of rare earth elements (lanthanum and scandium) on the endurance of alloy VKNA-25-VI in
long-term strength tests at 1100°C and on its resistance to high-temperature oxidation is determined. The
microstructure of the alloy is studied before and after the tests.
Key words: intermetallic alloy, long-term strength, microalloying, refractoriness, microstructure
INTRODUCTION
In order to obtain stable high properties in cast superal-
loys, it is necessary to ensure that the impurity content of the
metal is ultralow. This goal is achieved by using ultrapure
charge materials obtaining the metal by vacuum refining
[1 – 7]. An equally effective technology for improving the
properties of cast superalloys is their microalloying with
rare-earth elements (REMs). Performing systematic studies
and using experimental data, VIAM has developed a techno-
logy for the REM microalloying of cast and wrought nickel
superalloys ZhS6U-VI, ZhS3DK-VI, ZhS32-VI, EP975-ID,
VZh175-ID, and others. These superalloys are used to make
parts of gas-turbine engines for aircraft (rotor blades and
nozzle vanes, disks, nozzle doors, etc.).
However, no scientific research has been done on the
REM microalloying of intermetallic alloys of the VKNA
group.
The goal of the present investigation is to examine the ef-
fect of REMs on the properties and structural transforma -
tions in intermetallic nickel alloys of the VKNA group by us-
ing intermetallic alloy VKNA-25-VI as an example. This al -
loy is currently under development. Alloy VKNA-25-VI is a
cast structural-grade superalloy based on the compound
Ni
3Al. It is alloyed with small amounts of chromium, cobalt,
molybdenum, tungsten, and rhenium. The alloy has a low
density ( /c103= 8100 kg/c47m3 ) and can perform at gas tempera -
tures up to 1200°C. It was developed to make nozzle vanes
for modern gas-turbine engines. The optimum structure and
properties of alloy VKNA-25-VI, based on intermetallide
Ni
3Al, is realized in castings that have a columnar monocrys-
talline structure with a /c225111/c241crystallographic orientation.
METHODS OF STUDY
The alloy was made in a VIAM 2002 vacuum induction
furnace inside a crucible with a capacity of 20 g. Lanthanum
was used as the main REM, and a small amount of scandium
was used for comparison. After the resulting ingots were ma-
chined, they were remelted by the method of directional
crystallization on a UVNK-9 unit to obtain monocrystalline
semifinished products with the crystallographic orientation
/c225111/c241[11]. The semifinished products were subsequently
used to make specimens that were tested for rupture strength
at 1100°C in accordance with the standard GOST 10145. The
specimens were also tested for scaling resistance (based on
weight gain) in accordance with GOST 6130.
The microstructure was analyzed with the use of a scan-
ning electron microscope made by the company JEOL (Japan).
The specimens were prepared on equipment at metallo -
graphy center of the company Struers. The Labotom cut-off
machine was used to cut specimens in the longitudinal direc-
tion (the working part) and the transverse direction (the
head) from other specimens that had failed during the
stress-rupture test. The resulting specimens were pressed in
polystyrene on a Labopress-3 unit and microsections were
prepared on grinding-polishing machine RotoPol-21.

place in the head of the specimens during the stress-rupture

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
First we studied the effect of varying the nominal content
of lanthanum from 0.015 to 0.50 wt.% on the endurance of
alloy VKNA-25-VI when tested for rupture strength with
t = 1100°C and /c115= 95 MPa. It was established that the rup -
ture-strength curve has a maximum at a nominal lanthanum
content of 0.20 wt.%; rupture strength decreases when the al-
loy contains too much or too little lanthanum.
These results were used to make heats of alloy
VKNA-25-VI with the above-indicated lanthanum content
and test the alloy for rupture strength at t = 1100°C and
stresses of 95, 100, 105, and 110 MPa.
Figure 1 shows the results obtained from stress-rupture
tests of VKNA-25-VN with a 0.20 wt.% addition of lantha -
num. It is apparent that the rupture strength of the alloy is
greater than its certified characteristics when it contains an
REM.
The average rupture strength of alloy VKNA-25-VI with
lanthanum in a 1000-h test at t = 1100°C was 98 MPa. The
certified average rupture strength of the alloy with the same
test parameters is 80 MPa. Thus, microalloying of alloy
VKNA-25-VI with lanthanum increased its rupture strength

Although intermetallic alloy VKNA-25 is more scaling-
resistant than alloys of the ZhS group, that property can be
improved further through microalloying with lanthanum.
Visual inspection of the surface of the specimens be-
tween heating cycles revealed that the scale which formed on
the specimens varied in structure and color:
– on the metal without an REM, the scale that was
formed consisted of large, loose flakes and had a bluish-gray
color;
– on the lanthanum-bearing metal, the scale was a finely
dispersed structure with a bluish-green and green color.
The presence of green scale on the lanthanum-bearing
specimens showed that those specimens contained oxides of
chromium with a dense-packed crystalline lattice, which pre-
vents oxidation of the metal. The coarse flakes that formed
on the specimens without an REM are evidence of the weak
adhesion of the oxide film to the surface of the metal. The
REM prevents separation of the oxide film from the metal in
tests, i.e. it improves the adhesion of the film to the metal.
After alloy VKNA-25-VI was tested for rupture strength,
we performed metallographic studies of the specimens on a
Leica unit. The main indicator of the resistance of the alloy
to high-temperature oxidation is the thickness of the depleted
surface zone. The thickness of this zone was minimal on the
metal with additions of lanthanum and scandium and was
somewhat greater on the metal without an REM. The results
which were obtained are quite consistent with the character
of variation of the curves in Fig. 2 that characterize the unit
change in weight. The results also agree well with the find -
ings in [12, 13].
The microstructure of alloy VKNA-25-VI was studied
with and without additions of lanthanum and scandium. The
microstructure of the alloy in the cast state was cellular-den -
dritic, and a developed network of sub-boundaries was seen
inside the dendrites. The sections between the dendrites con-
tained coarser irregularly shaped /c103/c162-phase particles of differ-
ent sizes and the eutectic (/c103/c162+ /c98).
Study of the microstructure of the alloy after stress-rup-
ture tests at t = 1100°C and /c115= 95 MPa established that in
contrast to the lanthanum-free alloy, in the lanthanum-bear-
ing alloy the dendritic structure undergoes refinement (the
size of the dendrites and the distance between them both de-
crease) and there is an improvement in the morphology of
the strengthening /c103/c162-phase as a result of its refinement. It was
observed that the /c103/c162-phase coalesced and the /c103/c162+ /c98eutectic
dissolved in the failed specimens, with the structure being
coarser near the fracture and the particles of the /c103/c162-phase be-
ing elongated (a “raft” structure was formed). Fine particles
of what was conjectured to be the /c103/c162-phase precipitated in the
intermediate layers of the /c103-solid solution. These particles
were considerably smaller and greater in number in the lan -
thanum-bearing alloy.
The additional precipitation of large quantities of fine
particles of the /c103/c162-phase in the intermediate layers of the
/c103-solid solution strengthens these layers and impedes the
movement of dislocations during high-temperature creep in
stress-rupture tests. These developments stabilize the dislo -
cation structure of the alloy.
Since the intermetallic alloys being discussed fracture
along intermediate layers of the /c103-solid solution, their addi -
tional strengthening by ultrafine particles helps increase the
rupture strength of alloy VKNA-25-VI.

CONCLUSIONS
1. It was determined that rare-earth elements (lanthanum
and scandium) have a positive effect on the rupture-strength
characteristics of single crystals of intermetallic alloy
VKNA-25-VI. Microalloying the alloy with lanthanum in -
creased its rupture strength by 18 MPa in a stress-rupture test
performed at 1100°C over 1000 h.
2. Additional microalloying of alloy VKNA-25-VI with
lanthanum increased its resistance to high-temperature oxi -
dation (scaling resistance): the unit change in the weight of
the specimens was reduced by a factor of 2 – 2.5 in tests per-
formed to determine scaling resistance at 1100°C over 300 h.
3. With lanthanum in the alloy, the morphology of the
/c103/c162-phase on the axes of the dendrites and between dendrites is
finer than in the same alloy with scandium. Fine particles of
a material that is probably the /c103/c162-phase are precipitated from
the intermediate layers of the /c103-solid solution of alloy
VKNA-25-VN when it contains rare-earth elements. These
particles are considerably coarser and greater in number in
the lanthanum-bearing alloy than in the scandium-bearing
alloy.
