Keywords: Nickel based superalloy, Ageing, Fracture toughness, Microstructure, Twinning. 
Abstract. Deformation twinning behaviors have been studied in high Ni alloys, Alloy 28 or Sanicro 
28 and Alloy 625 at RT and at cryogenic temperature. The microstructures were evaluated using 
SEM-EBSD. Some constitutive approach has also been used in the discussion on the deformation 
twinning in high Ni-alloys. The results show that deformation twinning can occur in high Ni alloys 
depending on the chemical composition, strain range and stress conditions. TWIP can occur in the 
Ni based super alloy Alloy 625 at cryogenic temperature, which increases both strength and 
ductility. This is the first report of this phenomena for this alloy. Deformation twinning in high Ni 
alloys occurs heterogeneously in the material, depending on crystallographic parameters such as 
grain orientation and Schmid factor. Formation of deformation twins can lead to high texture in the 
material, which will contribute to the increase of strength. The mechanisms for the formation of 
deformation twins in high Ni alloys have been discussed. 
Introduction 
Workability and mode of deformation of metal alloys with various content of nickel: from 
austenitic stainless steel, up to superalloys will vary depending on chemical composition or stacking 
fault energy (SFE) and the following deformation modes: martenitic phase transformation, twinning 
and subsequent dislocation slip with increasing stacking fault energy of the material [1]. The nickel-
containing alloys can be divided into four groups with increasing content of nickel namely: 
austenitic stainless steels, superaustenitic alloys (non -ferrous elements >50 %), nickel alloys (or 
nickel-based alloys) (nickel is the largest constituent element) and superalloys (nickel >50 %). A 
high Ni alloy defined here is an alloy where Ni is a major alloying element, which includes high Ni 
steels, Ni based alloys and superalloys. These alloys have outstanding corrosion resistances, 
mechanical properties and high heat resistance, and have been extensively used to resist extremely 
conditions in the aerospace, energy, power, chemical and petrochemical industries [ 2]. During the 
last decades, the investigations regarding twinning induced platicity (TWIP) materials have been 
exhaustive [1] since these materials defy the “truth” of the trade of relationship with simultaneo us 
increase of yield strength and ductility. Deformation twinning makes the difference and adds both 
strength and ductility as it is an entirely different deformation mechanism than slip although 
confirmed now that deformation twinning also has its origin in slip [ 3]. Deformation twinning in 
real materials should strictly be considered as a special type of stress -induced martensitic 
transformation [3]. As Cahn has proved that there are no pure elements, which is a prerequisite for 
true deformation twinning [3]. However, in the very most cases that is just curiosity and hereinafter 
the concept of deformation twinning should be used. The Billby and Crocker definition is: “ a 
deformation twin is a region of a crystalline body which has undergone a homogenous shape 
deformation in such a way that the resulting product structure is identical with that of the parent, but 
oriented differently” [4]. The intrinsic stacking fault energy, SFE, of TWIP steels, γ

in the range of 15- 45 mJm-2 at room temperature [3, 5, 6] . The low SFE of the TWIP materials is 
one of the keys to the specific mechanical behavior of the alloys . Ni and high Ni alloys have high 
stacking fault energy, and are difficult to have deformation twinning [7, 8]. For instance, the SFE of

Tensile testing 
Tensile testing was performed using a Roell-Korthaus and an Instron 5982 tensile test machine 
and Magtec PMA-12/2/VV7-1 in air. Round specimens with a diameter of 5 mm and a gauge length 
of 25 mm were used. The testing was carried out at a strain rate of 2x10-2/s at two temperatures: 
room temperature and liquid nitrogen temperature, -196°C.

Microstructure evaluation 
For the microstructural investigation of deformation and fracture behavior. The scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM) technique, electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD), was used to analyze 
the strain or stress localization and grain or twin structure. Crystal misorientation and strain maps 
were performed in a Zeiss Sigma field emission gun -scanning electron microscope (FEG -SEM) 
equipped with ‘Nordlys F’ EBSD detector. EBSD maps were measured at 20 keV acceleration 
voltage. The ‘TSL OIM Analysis 7’ software was used for the microstructure evaluation of the 
acquired EBSD data. 
Results and Discussion 
Influence of chemical composition on deformation twinning in high Ni alloys 
As shown in Table 1, Alloy 625 contains about 60 wt% of Ni and Sanicro 28 contains about 
31wt% of Ni. These two alloys have high stacking fault energy, and may not have deformation 
twinning at room temperature. Fig. 2 shows the grain structure EBSD mappings of the cold-pilgered 
Alloy 625 and Sanicro 28 material. The black lines are high angle grain boundaries; the red lines are 
Σ3 twin boundar ies (60° misorientation angle) and the white ones are low angle grain boundar ies 
(<10° misorietation angle). It can be found that deformation in Alloy 625 is mainly attributed to the 
formation of dislocations (about 370000 observation number) and dislocation slips, but deformation 
twins can also be observed although the density of deformation twins is low (Fig. 2a and c ). In 
Sanicro 28, however, dense deformation twins and less dislocation (about 200000 observation 
number) can be observed. Less dislocatio n slips can be observed (Fig. 2b and d). According to 
Meyers´constitutional model [ 9], formation of deformation twins is a competitive process with 
formation of dislocation. For an alloy with high stacking fault energy, a higher critical stress or 
strain is needed to cause the initiation of twinning in the alloy. Sanicro 28 has a lower stacking fault 
energy, and therefore there are more twinning induced in the material for a given strain. Since Alloy 
625 has a stacking fault energy of 251 mJm
-2, which is to o high to induce deformation twinning 
with plain strain, (see the next chapter), the formation of deformation twins in this alloy means that 
there are other factors that may cause the deformation twinning. The stress conditions during the 
pilgering process are complicated since i t is a multiaxial -stress condition. This may change the 
critical stress to initiate twinning, which causes the formation of deformation twins. More work 
needs to be done.

Influence of temperature on deformation twinning in Ni based alloy 
Fig. 3 shows the stress versus strain curves for Alloy 625 from the tensile testing at room 
temperature and at -196°C. No embrittlement occurred at cryogenic temperature. It is just opposite. 
Alloy 625 shows a combination of high strength and high ductility. The tensile strength increases 
from about 800MPa at RT to about 1120MPa at - 196°C, and the elongation increases from about 
64% at RT to about 87% at -196°C. To study the mechanism for this behavior, EBSD study has 
been performed.

Fig. 4 shows the results of the microstructure mapping from the EBSD study. Fig. 4a shows the 
microstructure in the Alloy 625 deformed at RT. Very few deformation twins can be observed. 
Even less deformation twins can be observed in this specimen comparing with that in the pilgere d 
material (Fig. 2a). This is mainly that the tensile testing is mainly plain strain. At - 196°C, however, 
deformation in Alloy 625 can introduce plenty of deformation twins (Fig. 3b). Σ3 twin boundary is 
18.5%. This indicates that high strength and high ductility of Alloy 625 at cryogenic temperature is 
due to the formation of nano twins during plastic deformation. It is a twin induced plasticity, TWIP. 
A Ni based alloy can also have TWIP depending on the conditions during deformation. As known, 
stacking fault energy of an alloy is correlated to temperature, which will decrease with decreasing 
temperature [9]. This will also decrease the critical strain to initiate twinning.

Influence of crystallographic parameters on the deformation twinning in high Ni alloys 
As shown in Fig. 2b and 4b, formation of deformation in the material is not homogeneous. 
Deformation twins appear only in some grains. To study this behavior, the influence of 
crystallographic parameters on deformation twinning in Sanicro 28 has been investigated as shown 
in Fig. 5. Fig. 5a is an inverse pole figure mapping of a cold pilgered tube with an area reduction of 
55.0 %. Deformation twinning occurs mainly at {111} plane, not much at other crystal planes. Fig. 
5b show a Schmid factor mapping. It is a complicated situation. Deformation twinning may not 
occur at the crystal planes with low or high Schmid factors.

Correlation between deformation twinning and formation of texture in Ni based alloy 
As discussed above, deformation twins in high Ni Alloy are mainly Σ3 twins. This indicates that 
the formation of deformation twins will affect the texture in the material. Fig. 6 shows the IPFs of 
Alloy 625 deformed at RT and at - 196°C. The texture formed in the material deformed in the 
cryogenic temperature is much higher than that in the material deformed at RT. The texture index in 
the cryogenic deformed material is 11.47 (Fig. 6b), but only 4.89 in the RT -deformed material (Fig. 
6a). The high amount of texture in the cryogenic deformed material will also contribute the 
strength.

Summary 
Deformation twinning can occur in high Ni alloys depending on the chemical composition, strain 
range and stress conditions. 
TWIP can occur in the Ni based superalloy Alloy 625 at cryogenic temperature, which increases 
both strength and ductility. 
Deformation twinning in high Ni alloys occurs heterogeneously in the material, depending on 
crystallographic parameters such as grain orientation and Schmid factor. 
Formation of deformation twins can lead to high texture in the material, which will contribute to 
the increase of strength.
