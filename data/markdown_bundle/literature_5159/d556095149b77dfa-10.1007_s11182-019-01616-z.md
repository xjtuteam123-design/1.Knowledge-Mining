THE INFLUENCE OF DIMESIONS AND PHASE STATE OF 
STRUCTURAL ELEMENTS ON MECHANICAL PROPERTIES OF 
BINARY ALLOYS OF THE Ti–Nb AND Zr–Nb SYSTEMS 
A. Yu. Eroshenko,1 Yu. P. Sharkeev,1,2 I. A. Glukhov,1 UDC 539.4; 539.25 
P. V. Uvarkin,1 A. M. Mairambekova,3 and A. I. Tolmachev1 
The results of investigation of the influence of the structural element size and phase state of the Ti – 45 wt.% 
Nb and Zr – 1 wt.% Nb binary alloys on their mechanical properties are presented. The structural states of the 
alloys with the structural elements of different dimensions were formed from the ultrafine-grained state via 
annealing. The curves of dependence of the yield st rength and microhardness on the average size of the 
structural elements, d –1/2, are obtained using the Hall–Petch relation. It is shown that for the Zr – 1 wt.% Nb 
alloy, the Hall–Petch relation is fulfilled in the entire range of sizes under study, from ultrafine-grained to fine-
grained states 0.2–1.9 µm. For the Ti – 45 wt.% Nb alloy, in the analysis of the Hall–Petch relationship within 
the structural element size range 0.43–45 µm, its phase composition is taken into consideration, specifically, 
the presence of dispersion-strengthened 
-phase grains, -phase subgrains, nonequilibrium nanosized -
phase, and the bimodal grain-subgrain structure. 
Keywords: Ti – 45 wt.% Nb and Zr – 1 wt.% Nb alloys, micr ostructure, structure elements size, yield strength, 
microhardness, phase composition. 
INTRODUCTION 
Grain-structure refinement up to nanostructured (NS) and/or ultrafine-grained states by the methods of severe 
plastic deformation (SPD) adds to the metallic materials new specific structure and properties. Some of the properties of 
NS-materials, associated with engineering plasticity, stre ngth, wear resistance, low-temperature and high-speed 
superplasticity, offer new prospects of their practical applications [1]. The NS / UFG-states in metals and alloys, formed 
by SPD, represent a complex microstructure with its own peculiar features. These are th e following: small grain size 
(submicro- and nanoscales), low density of free dislocations , availability of predominantly high-angle grain boundaries 
and their non-equilibrium state. It is well known that the form ation of NS / UFG-states in metals and alloys by the SPD 
methods gives rise to an increase in strength both in alloys and in pure metals. Moreover, an introduction of high 
density of dislocations into NS / UFG-metals during the SPD process can result in their higher strengthening [2]. 
An improvement of strength characteristics accompanying the gr ain refinement is generally attributed to the grain-
boundary strengthening of the material in accordance with the Hall–Petch re lation fulfilled not only for the yield 
strength but also for the flow stress at different strain valu es [3–6]. From this perspec tive, the issues of structure 
stability and mechanical properties of UFG-metals and alloys ar e urgent in a wide temperature interval. An analysis is

Alloys of the Ti–Nb and Zr–Nb systems, which contain bioinert metals only, possess high corrosion resistance 
and bioinertness, which makes them promising materials for manufacturing implants. Of special interest for medical 
applications are titanium -alloys of the Ti–Nb system, which at a niobium concentration of 40–45 wt.% possess a low 
elasticity modulus (50–60 GPa), which is close to that of the cortical bone [7, 8]. A wide application of the Ti–Nb and 
Zr–Nb alloys in medicine is however restrained by their low strength level. The formation of NS / UFG-states in these 
alloys by the SPD methods allows solving this problem [9, 10].

elements, taking into consideration the phase composition, on the mechanical properties of the Ti–Nb and Zr–Nb alloys 
in a wide size range of their structure elements. 
MATERIALS AND EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES 
The experimental materials were two binary alloys in the UFG-states of the following compositions: Ti – 
45 wt.% Nb (Ti45Nb) and Zr – 1 wt.% Nb (Zr1Nb). The UFG-state was formed in them in a two-stage SPD processing, 
which included multiple abc-pressing and multi-pass groove rolling followed by annealing at 300С [10]. 
As a result of combined SPD-processi ng, work-pieces of the Ti45Nb and Zr1Nb alloys were manufactured in 
the form of 400 mm-long square rods measuring 66 mm in the cross section. In order to release internal stresses and to 
increase ductility, the resulting Ti45Nb and Zr1Nb rods were annealed at 300 С for 1 hour in the argon atmosphere. 
After that, in order to form the states with different dimens ions of the structural element (grains, subgrains, fragments) 
in these alloys the UFG-Ti45Nb workpieces were annealed at the temperatures 400, 500, 600, 700 and 800°С, while the 
Zr1Nb alloy workpieces  at 350, 400, 450, 500 and 580 С for 1 hour in the argon atmosphere. The annealing 
temperatures were higher than the upper bound of thermal stability of the UFG-structure for the alloys under study [10, 
11]. 
Specimens in different structural states were formed as a result of anneal ing, whose average structural-element 
sizes were in the ranges from 0.25 to 45 µm and from 0.2 to 1.9 µm for Ti45Nb and Zr1Nb, respectively. The types of 
their structural states were consistent with the grain size ranges of the classification reported in [12]. 
The investigations were performed using the methods of transmission electron microscopy (TEM), energy 
dispersive microanalysis, mechanical te nsile tests, and microhardness measuremen ts. All of these investigations were 
carried out at the NANOTEKH shared use center at the ISPMS SB RAS, using, among other instruments, JEOL JEM 
2100, Duramin-5, and Instron universal testing machine. The av erage size of the structural elements (grains, subgrains, 
and fragments) was calculated using the intercept method [13]. 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
Figure 1 presents typical TEM-images of the microstructure of the Ti45Nb and Zr1Nb alloys in the UFG-state, 
formed by a two-stage SPD pr ocess. The UFG-microstructure patterns in both materials are similar. There are 
fragments and subgrains, as well as completely formed grai ns. The boundaries of grains, subgrains and fragments are 
non-equilibrium. A characteristic feature of the microstructu re is the presence of a large number of extinction contours 
in the bright-field images. The presence of blurred boundaries and extinction contours suggests a high level of SPD-
induced internal stresses. 
The microdiffraction patterns of both alloys, Ti45Nb and Zr1Nb, contain a large number of point reflections of 
differing intensity. These reflections on the microdiffraction patterns are arranged circumferentially (see inserts in 
Fig. 1a, d). This implies a considerable refinement of the in itial structure after the deformation processing and the 
presence of high-angle grain boundaries. The azimuthal blurring of reflections also suggests a high level of residual 
internal stresses.

According to the literature data [4, 15], in a wide range of grain sizes from 10 –8 to 10–2 m such parameters of 
a polycrystal as strain resistance σ0 and coefficient k in the Hall–Petch relationship depend on the average grain size, 
material type (metal or alloy), texture, availability of segregations or precipitates at the grain boundary, volume fraction 
of triple junctions, etc. The results of verification and anal ysis of the Hall–Petch law demonstrate that it is not fulfilled 
in the entire interval of grain sizes in the case of NS-materials and larg ely depends on the process of their 
manufacturing. The behavior of coefficient k in the Hall–Petch relationship as a function of grain size is also 
ambiguous. Its value decreases with the grain size. For pure metals, such as aluminum, nickel, titanium, and iron, it 
approaches zero, and in the case of c opper it even acquires ne gative values. These phenomena are observed in the 
vicinity of the nanometer grain-size range. 
The authors of [3, 15] argue that all deviations from the Hall–Petch relationship reported in UFG-materials in 
the literature are due to the structural state of the grai n boundaries after deformation and subsequent annealing 
processing. Presumably, non-equilibrium boundaries (in contrast to equilibrium ones) generate long-range stress fields, 
which even at large distances retain their high level, suffici ent for them to affect the motio n of intra-grain dislocations. 
A decrease in the grain size gives rise to a qualitative change in the process of plastic deformation  a transition from 
the mechanism of deformation of the grain bulk to a deformation via diffusion-induced grain-boundary sliding. 
Furthermore, the reason of deviations from the Hall–Petch relationship in the nanometric size range might consist in the 
accelerated diffusion, which results in the activation of grain- boundary sliding and alteration of the conditions of strain 
propagation through the grain boundaries.

state in the size interval of the stru ctural elements 0.25–0.43 µm; the region of UFG-to-FG state transition within the

The UFG Ti45Nb alloy consists of the dispersion-hardened ω-phase of -grains in the size range 0.1–0.8 µm 
and finer -grains measuring from 0.2 µm. In the FG- and CG-states, the microstructure is presented by -grains, 
containing precipitates of the nanosized ω-phase. The 0.43–1.3 µm grain size range corresponds to the region of 
transition of the bimodal UFG-structure into FG-structure. It should be noted that this region corresponds not only to the 
structural state transformation but also to the α→β transition, which is accompanied by a considerable deterioration of 
the level of mechanical properties. It is well known that in the case of coarse grains an increase in strength and 
microhardness with a decrease in size is conditioned by the introduction of additional grain boundaries, which represent 
barriers for dislocation motion, while in the case of ultrafine- grains an increase in strength is due to high density of the 
boundaries, which prevent the formation and multiplication of new dislocations [15].

SUMMARY 
Using a combined SPD process, involving abc-pressing, multi-pass rolling and pre-recrystallization annealing, 
UFG-states have been formed in the binary Ti45Nb and Zr1Nb alloys, where the averag e structural element sizes have 
been found to be 0.25 and 0.2 µm, respectively. The Ti45Nb and Zr1Nb Ti45Nb alloy specimens with the average 
structural element sizes with in the intervals 0.25–45 and 0.20–1.9 µm, respectively, have been formed from the 
workpieces of the UFG alloys due to their annealing at the temper atures within 400–800 and 350–580С, respectively. 
Taking into account their phase compositions, the curves of dependence of the yield strength and microhardness on the 
average size of the grain-subgrain structure d –1/2 have been constructed on the basis of the Hall–Petch relationship. 
The UFG Ti45Nb alloy in the range of the structural elemen ts 0.25–0.43 µm has a bimodal structure with -
grains dispersion-hardened by the ω-phase and finer -grains. As a result of annealing in the range of the structural 
element sizes 0.43–1.3 µm, a bimodal UFG-to-FG-structure transition is observed and an α→β phase transition takes 
place, after which the -nanosized particles are retained in the bulk of the -grains. The Ti45Nb alloy microstructure in
