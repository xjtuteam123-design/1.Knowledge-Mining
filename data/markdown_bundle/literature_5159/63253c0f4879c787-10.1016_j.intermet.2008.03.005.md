abstract
The effect of Zr addition on the microstructures and mechanical properties of cast Nb–22Ti–16Si– xZr
(x ¼ 0–4) alloys was investigated. The results show that the Zr addition leads to coarseness of primary
Nbss phase promotes formation and coarseness of Nbss and g(Nb)5Si3 lamellar eutectic structure. In
addition, the Zr addition increases the hardness of Nbss and (Nb) 3Si phases. The yield strength at room
temperature of the alloys improves with increasing Zr content. The fracture strain reaches a peak value in
3 at.% Zr content, and then drops with the increase of Zr content. The high temperature strength de-
creases ﬁrst and reaches a minimal value in 3 at.% Zr content, and then increases again. These variations
are attributed to the microstructure and composition changes of the alloys with increasing Zr content.
/C2112008 Elsevier Ltd. All rights reserved.1. Introduction
Nb5Si3 is thought to be one of the candidates of the next gen-
eration high temperature structural materials because of its high
melting point, low density and high strength at elevated temper-
ature [1–5]. However, the shortcoming of low room temperature
ductility retarded its practical applications. Previous studies [6–8]
have shown that forming Nb/Nb 5Si3 in situ composite by in-
corporating a ductile Nb phase to hard deformable Nb 5Si3 phase
will be one way to enhance room temperature deformability, but
that will result in lowering its high temperature strength. There-
fore, how to improve room temperature fracture resistance without
degrading high temperature strength is one of the important issues
in developing Niobium silicides based alloys as next generation
high temperature structural materials. So, many researchers took
various measures to overcome these drawbacks so as to achieve
a reasonable balance between room temperature ductility and high
temperature strength. One possible procedure used frequently to
meet such requirement is alloying. In previous works [9–18], the
effect of alloying elements such as Hf, Ti, Cr, Al, Ta, Mo, W, and V on
microstructure and mechanical properties of Niobium silicide
based alloys have been investigated. But, so far there haven’t been
any reports on the effect of Zr addition on microstructures and
mechanical properties of Nb–Ti–Si composites. Since Zr additions
to NiAl and Ni 3Al alloys could improve strength and ductility to
a certain extent [19,20], we added Zr element to Nb–22Ti–16Si alloy
and anticipated achieving the same purpose in the present study.
To facilitate description, in the paper, the Nb in solid solution
with Si, Ti and/or Zr is referred to as Nbss, the Nb 3Si with Ti and/or
Zr in solid solution is referred to as (Nb) 3Si and the Nb 5Si3 with Ti
and/or Zr in solid solution is referred to as (Nb) 5Si3.
2. Experimental procedures
The alloys used in this study were arc-melted by a non-
consumable tungsten electrode under an argon atmosphere in
a water cooled copper crucible. The purities of raw materials were
99.9 wt.% of Nb, 99.9 wt.% of Ti, 99.99 wt.% of Si, 99.9 wt.% of Zr,
respectively. Buttons of about 70 g were prepared. Each alloy
button was turned over and remelted at least ﬁve times to get
a homogeneous specimen. Since weight losses were generally less
than 0.5%, the compositions of the alloys were considered to be
equal to their nominal compositions. The nominal compositions of
these alloys were listed in Table 1. X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis
was used to monitor the crystal structure of the individual phases
in a RIGAKU D/Max-r A X-ray diffractometer using Cu K a radiation.
The samples for scanning electron microscopy (SEM) observation
were prepared by conventional metallographic technique. The
volume fractions of the phases were calculated through image
analysis of microstructures from a fairly large number of areas by
means of SISC IAS V80 metallographic image analysis software. The
chemical compositions of the phases were analyzed by using
electrical probe microanalysis (EPMA) technique. The type of the
instrument used for the EPMA work in the present study is EMPA-
1610. EMPA used a 15 kV accelerating voltage, under which the
spatial resolution was about 1

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
The XRD patterns of all the as-cast alloys are presented in Fig. 1.
Individual phases and their crystal structures were identiﬁed by
matching the characteristic XRD peaks against JCPDS databases and
denoted with various marks to indicate the corresponding phases.
XRD results reveal that the as-cast alloys consist of three phases,
which are Nbss, (Nb) 3Si and g(Nb)5Si3. The chemical compositions
of these phases determined by EPMA or TEM–EDS are given in Table
2. The chemical compositions of the ﬁne g(Nb)5Si3 phase were
analyzed by TEM–EDS. The results show that the solubility of Zr in
g(Nb)5Si3 is signiﬁcantly higher than in Nbss and (Nb) 3Si.
Fig. 2 shows the backscatter electron (BSE) photographs of the
as-cast alloys. According to the XRD, SEM, EPMA and TEM–EDS
results, in the as-cast condition the microstructures are composed
of white large Nbss dendrites, gray (Nb) 3Si matrix, black g(Nb)5Si3
phases. Titanium segregation was evident at the edges of Nbss
dendrites. From Fig. 2 , rich-Ti Nbss exhibits darker contrast due to
the increase of Ti content. Zirconium content is also higher in rich-
Ti Nbss than Nbss, as shown in Table 2. The
g(Nb)5Si3 phase tends to
exist at the interface between Nbss and (Nb) 3Si. Also, the g(Nb)5Si3

the eutectic microstructure. As seen in Fig. 2 (c)–(e), the eutectic

increasing Zr content. Furthermore, the eutectic structures tend to
coarsen with increasing Zr content from 2 to 4 at.%, indicating that

rather than the product of rapid decomposition of the (Nb) 3Si
phase. During solidiﬁcation, because the melting points of Ti and Zr
are lower than that of Nb, they will be rejected into the liquid phase,
which leads to the segregation of Ti and Zr in front of the solid–
liquid interface. Furthermore, the crystal structure of Ti 5Si3 and
Zr5Si3 is the same hp16 as g(Nb)5Si3, a large amount of Ti and Zr at
the phase interfaces will contribute to the formation of g(Nb)5Si3.
Therefore on the formation of g(Nb)5Si3 phase other than aNb5Si3,
it seems that a large amount of Ti and Zr at the phase interfaces
plays an important role. This strongly suggests that a large amount
of Ti and Zr stabilizes g(Nb)5Si3. The more the Ti and Zr, the greater
the composition undercooling, and hence the point of solidiﬁcation
commencement approaches the Nbss þ (Nb)5Si3 phase ﬁeld, which
increases the tendency to form the g(Nb)5Si3 phase as a direct so-
lidiﬁcation product.
3.3. Mechanical properties
3.3.1. Vickers hardness
In order to evaluate the strengthening effect of Zr addition on
primary Nbss and (Nb) 3Si, vickers hardness measurements were
carried out on constituent phases of the alloys with various Zr
content. But the microhardness of g(Nb)5Si3 phase hasn’t tested
because its size isn’t large enough. The variations of microhardness
of primary Nbss and (Nb) 3Si with various Zr content additions are
presented in Table 3. It is worthy to note that in 1 at.% Zr alloy the
microhardness of Nbss phase is almost no change, as compared
with that of Nbss phase in Zr-free alloy, which may be attributed to
no appreciable solid solution hardening due to a small amount of Zr
additions. However, it is also evident in Table 3 that the hardness of
Nbss phase signiﬁcantly increases when Zr content is increased
from 1 to 2 at.%. Moreover, it can be seen from Table 3 that the
microhardness of (Nb) 3Si phase monotonously ascents with in-
creasing Zr content. As expected, it is also obvious that micro-
hardness of (Nb) 3Si is much higher than that of Nbss phase due to
its complex crystal structure.
3.3.2. Compressive properties
Compression tests were conducted to investigate the effect of Zr
addition on the mechanical properties of the as-cast alloys at room
temperature and elevated temperature. Yield strength and fracture
strain are presented in Fig. 5 for the as-cast alloys tested at room
temperature at an initial strain rate of 1 /C2 10/C0 4/s. The increase in
yield stress at ambient temperature can mainly be attributed to
solid solution strengthening and the formation of lamellar struc-
ture. When Zr content is less than 1 at.%, Nbss solid solution
strengthening effect by Zr addition is weak, while (Nb) 3Si solid
solution strengthening effect is strong, which can be seen from
Table 3. Therefore, when Zr content is less than 1 at.%, the increase
in yield strength at room temperature is attributed to (Nb) 3Si solid
solution strengthening effect by Zr addition. When Zr addition is
more than 1 at.%, Nbss and (Nb) 3Si solid solution strengthening
effect by Zr increases at the same time, which can also be seen from

eutectic structure forms when Zr content is 2 at.%. The lamellar
eutectic structure has more interfaces to inhibit dislocation
movement, which may cause the further increase of room tem-

with the increase in the thickness and volume fraction of Nbss
phase. The ﬁner the Nbss phase, the bigger constrained force does
the brittle (Nb) 3Si phase apply on the Nbss phase, which leads to
fracture of the Nbss phase easily by means of cleavage. However,
the large scale Nbss phase is subjected to smaller constrained force
from brittle (Nb)
3Si phase, suggesting easier ductile deformation
and interface debonding between Nbss and silicides, which result
in increasing ductility of Nbss. Therefore, the crack propagation can
be effectively retarded by thick Nbss phase, which is in agreement
with viewpoints of Kim [24] and Sekido [25]. Moreover, the for-
mation of more g(Nb)5Si3 phase suggests that the volume fraction
of (Nb)3Si decreases and that of Nbss increases. The increase of Nbss
phase deﬁnitely contributes to the improvement of fracture strain.
But, with increasing Zr content, the beneﬁcial effect on increasing
fracture strain will be partly offset by Zr solid solution strength-
ening effect.
The 0.2% yield stress at 1473 K in vacuum with an initial rate of
1 /C2 10/C0 4/s is also presented in Fig. 6 as a function of Zr content for
the present alloys. It can be seen from Fig. 6 that high temperature
strength signiﬁcantly drops when Zr content is raised from 1 to
2 at.% and exhibits the minimal value at around 3 at.% Zr, and then
increases. The decrease is mainly attributed to the changes of
microstructure and composition. The lamellar eutectic structure
has more interfaces for the diffusion reaction to proceed, which
may cause diffusion process to accelerate. So, the high temperature
strength abruptly drops due to the formation of more interfaces
when Zr content increases from 1 to 2 at.%. In addition, the com-
position change has certain effect on the high temperature
strength. It is well known that Zr has a lower melting point than
Nbss and hence can increase atomic diffusivity in alloys. Thus, Zr
addition may promote high temperature compressive deformation
process controlled by a thermal activation in the present alloys. It
has been reported that the addition of Zr with a melting point that
is lower than Nb will reduce the elevated temperature strength of
Nb-based alloys [26]. However, the high temperature strength
increases again in Zr content more than 3 at.%, which can be due to
the coarseness of lamellar structure and the formation of more
g(Nb)5Si3 phase.
4. Conclusions
1. The microstructure of the as-cast alloys consists of Nbss,
(Nb)3Si and g(Nb)5Si3 phases. The Zr addition leads to coarse-
ness of the primary Nbss phase and promotes the formation
of the lamellar eutectic structure accompanied by coarsening of
the lamellar eutectic structure.
2. The hardness of Nbss and (Nb) 3Si phases improves with
increasing Zr content. The increase of the hardness is mainly
due to Zr solid solution strengthening effect.
3. The yield strength at room temperature of the alloys improves
with increasing Zr content. The fracture strain reaches a peak
value in 3 at.% Zr content, and then drops with the increase of
Zr content. The high temperature strength ﬁrst decreases and
reaches a minimal value in 3 at.% Zr content, and then increases
again. These variations is mainly ascribed to the microstructure
and composition changes of the alloys.
