A. M. Volkov,1,2 D. A. Karyagin,3,4 M. N. Letnikov,1,5 
M. M. Bakradze,1,6 and A. S. Perevozov3,7 UDC 621.762:669.24 
The paper discusses the specifics of chemical composition, hot isostatic pressing (HIP), and heat trea t-
ment of granulated heat -resistant nickel alloys characterized by high long -term strength at 750 °C. 
The results are presented which were obtained by studying EP975 -type alloy during manufacturing of 
large-sized disk blanks for aircraft gas -turbine engines (GTE) using the technology based on metallurgy 
of granules. Changes in the micr ostructure after long -term testing at high temperature were evaluated. 
The phase stability of the material was co nfirmed. 
Keywords: disk blank, gas -turbine engine, granule, heat- resistant nickel alloy, long -term strength, m i-
crostructure, ′γ -phase. 
The mission to create new varieties of aviation equipment, as well as upgrade the existing aircraft fleet r e-
quires improved mechanical properties of the source materials. Higher temperatures associated with “hot” gas 
path represent a steady trend for high-thrust gas- turbine engines (GTE). For high -pressure turbine disks, this 
means an increase in operating temperatures to 750 °C and above [1, 2]. 
Russian material scientists have developed and certified a number of super heat -resistant nickel-based alloys 
for GTE disks characterized by high long- term strength at 750 °C (Table 1). These include both deformable and 
granulated alloys. The blanks based on the granulated alloys are produced by filling capsules with granules fo l-
lowed by hot isostatic pressin g (HIP) [3–6]. 
EP975-ID alloy has the highest operating temperature: up to 850 °С (long-term) or up to 975 °С (with short -
term overshoots). This alloy has been successfully commercialized and used in the man ufacturing of stamped 
“disk” and “disk-shaft” type products of moderate sizes. The scientists at the Federal State Unitary Enterprise 
“VIAM” have also developed a technology for producing small -sized stamp ed products measuring up to 
300 mm in diameter. The approaches utilized during preparation of the s emi-finished products, computer sim u-
lation of the process of isothermal deformation in air, i ncrease in technological plasticity, etc. ensure a set of 
high and stable mechanical properties [7].

The technology based on metallurgy of granules allows solving a number of above -described challenges a s-
sociated with the production of large- sizes disk blanks. However, when using a super heat -resistant EP975P 
alloy, hot isostatic pressing must be performed at quite high temperatures. This is associated with the reduced 
cobalt content and increased niobium a nd aluminum contents in the po wder version of the alloy compared to its 
deformable version (Table 2). As a result, the temperature of complete dissolution (Tcd) of the ′γ -phase of 
EP975P alloy exceeds th at of EP975- ID by 20 to 30° C. Russia has limited technological capabilities in terms 
of large gasostatic equipment (gasostats) with the working space measuring more than 900 mm in diameter to 
facilitate hot isostatic pressing of such material. Procurement and commissioning of mo dern industrial gasostats 
capable of providing the r equired operating parameters (150 MPa at 1,300 °C) is complicated by high cost and 
long lead time. 
Therefore, it became necessary to study and refine the technological conditions of producing large- sized 
disk blanks from EP975 -ID-type alloy using the method of metallurgy of granules. The study was performed 
within the scope of the combined research in the direction 10.2: Isothermal deformation in air of new generation 
of heterogeneous hard -to-deform heat -resistant alloys (“Strategic directions of material and technology deve l-
opment for the period until 2030”) [10].

The first objective was to optimize the alloy composition. As noted above, the chemical compos ition of the 
deformable alloy enables a lower Tcd value. To obtain high -quality material using granular technology, the 
following adjustm ents were made: carbon content was slightly reduced (composition was calculated based on 
the lower limit), and microalloying with active carbide -forming element, such as hafnium, was performed at the 
level of equivalent alloys. Such measures are quite wel l-known and have been successfully tested when creating 
a number of granular versions of heat -resistant nickel alloys based on deformable analogues [11, 12]. 
Castings for atomization were obtained by vacuum induction melting using Consarc equipment with su bse-
quent vacuum arc remelting in a water -cooled crystallizer having a diameter of 90 mm. The microstructure and 
critical points of the alloy with adjusted composition correlated with the similar p arameters of EP975- ID alloy 
(Fig. 1a, b). A relatively low Tcd value and quite large solid solution homogene ity range (between solidus 
and Tcd) made it possible to perform HIP in a single -phase region by using available industrial gasostats wit h-
out the risk of operating them under critical conditions. 
The granules were obtained using the rotary atomization system (UCR -4) under conditions similar to 
those for mass -produced granulated alloys. The quality parameters after physical and mechanical treatment 
(e.g., sc reening, magnetic and electrostatic separation) were consistent with similar param eters for EP741NP 
alloy [13]. The capsules were filled with granules using the ultrasonic generator -type equipment (UZGK) to 
ensure a relative tap density of about 60 %. In this study, “turbine disk” type capsules for producing blanks with 
the developed hub portion were used (Fig. 2). Along with the capsules for producing large -sized disk blanks 
measuring more than 650 mm in diameter and weighing more than 150 kg (as receive d), reference samples were 
made representing cylindrical capsules weig hing ≈ 15 kg. 
Hot isostatic pressing of granule -filled capsules and reference samples was performed in an industrial gas o-
stat at a temperature above Tcd in a single-phase region. After HIP, the grain size of the reference sample m a-
terial was quite small measuring ≈ 25 µm, which corresponds to grade 8 according to GOST 5639. No inherited 
granule boundaries or any other defects were observed (Fig. 3). 
To refine t he heat treatment conditions, reference samples were cut into templates (Fig. 4a). The mater i-
al was treated in accordance with the quenching and aging conditions used in case of deformable EP975 -ID 
alloy. After complete heat treatment, the grain size wa s ≈ 30 µm (grade 7), and the size of the strengthening 
′γ -phase was ≈ 0.5 µm. To determine the required cooling rate during quenching, mate rial samples measuring 
20 × 20 × 70 mm and templates measuring 20 × 100 × 120 mm were used for heat treatment (see Fig. 4b).

An increased attention to the quenching rate is related to the following specific feature of EP975 -ID alloy. 
Due to high content of the strengthening ′γ -phase ( ≈ 60 %), quenching is performed in steps. During the first 
step, material is coo led with the furnace until the temperature reaches 30 to 40 °C below the solvus temperature. 
Not only does this result in the formation of a specific microstructure, but also, from a technological standpoint, 
allows reducing the temperature gradient in the stamped product, lower stresses, and prevent cracking [14] .

Mechanical properties of the material produced from the granules of EP975 -ID alloy after heat treatment i n-
cluding two types of quenching (using samples and templates) are shown in Table 3. The comparative analysis 
of the properties of granular products and small -sized disks obtained by isothe rmal stamping using EP975- ID 
alloy [15] has shown that the level of ultimate strength of the granular material is significantly higher. A 10 % 
increase in σt value is mainly associated with the reduction in grain si ze from grade 4 (for stamped products) to 
grade 7 (for studied granular blanks). The long -term strength of the material is consistent with the guaranteed 
strength level of the deformable alloy an alogue (probability of non -failure was 0.8 at a confidence l evel of 0.9). 
A sample tested for long -term strength at 800 °C for 684 h was used to perform a microstructure study by 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM). It was found that under given conditions, there are no precipitating pha s-
es in the material (e.g., topologically closely -packed needle -shaped particles), which could adversely affect its 
mechanical properties. The strengthening ′γ -phase slightly increases in size, and coagulation of individual pa r-
ticles is observed (Fig. 5). These results along with high level of ob tained properties are signs of phase stability 
of the material and, therefore, allow using the proposed heat treatment conditions for large -sized disk blanks.

The results of mechanical testing of heat -treated samples, namely, high level of plastic characteristics and 
impact toughness, led to a conclusion that no special measures are required to prevent cr acking during quenc h-
ing of large- sized disk blanks made from granules. Perhaps, a low tendency to cracking is also associated with 
the fact that hot isostatic pressing results in significantly lower level of cold working of the material compared to 
hot plastic deformation. The level of mechanical properties of the reference samples after template heat treat-
ment was higher than required. Therefore, large- sized disk blanks were quenched at moderate rates, which add i-
tionally helped eliminating any possibili ty of cracking. 
Heat treatment was performed in the batch -type electric furnaces according to standard conditions for 
EP975-ID alloy. The excess material around the blanks was used to make samples for mechanical testing fo l-
lowed by non -destructive inspect ion (capillary, ultrasonic, macroetching). One of the blanks was subjected to 
a comprehensive testing. The tensile properties, impact toughness, long -term strength (using smooth cylindrical 
and notched samples), and low -cycle fatigue were determined. S ample loc ation (hub, body, rim) and direction 
(tangential, radial) were differentiated to best cover the sections of the blank. The obtained level of properties 
confirms the potential of the utilized technology for pr oducing large -sized disk blanks from g ranules of super 
heat-resistant nickel alloy EP975 -ID (Table 4). 
CONCLUSIONS 
During the study, the technological conditions of manufacturing large -sized disk blanks from super heat -
resistant nickel alloy having an operating temperature above 850°C were ref ined. The results of comprehensive 
testing of deformable EP975 -ID alloy and EP975P alloy, obtained based on the technology of metallurgy of 
granules, made it possible to create the material with a combination of properties exceeding those of the prot o-
type alloys. 
The future direction of study will involve the use of HIP technology with subsequent isothermal defor-
mation, as this would make further increase in the level of strength characteristics and low -cycle fatigue r e-
sistance even more likely [16].
