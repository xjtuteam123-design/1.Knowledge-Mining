Key words: High-entropy alloy; Mechanical property; Plastic deformation; Low temperature. 
Abstract. The phase-formation rule of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) with different microstructures is 
discussed, based on the atom-size difference in mul ticomponent alloys. For the single-phase HEA 
with the composition of AlCoCrFeNi, the yielding st rengths and fracture strengths at cryogenic 
temperatures increase distinguishingly, compared to the corresponding mechanical properties at 
ambient temperature. However, the plasticity at 298 and 77 K changes very gently, while the fracture 
modes are intergranular and transgranular, respectively. 
Introduction 
Recently, many new kinds of metallic alloys have be en developed, which exhibit excellent 
mechanical properties, such as high strengths and l arge plasticity [1-6]. Among these materials, 
high-entropy alloys (HEAs) possess super mechanical performances together with the low-fabrication 
costs, rendering them potential candidates for use as engineering materials. 
The development of HEAs breaks through the cognitiv e strategy of the alloy design [3,7]. 
Conventional strategy for developing the alloys is based on one or two elements as the major 
constituent, and other minor elements as their constituents for the enhancement of definite properties. 
However, HEAs with multiple principal elements are of equimolar or near-equimolar ratios and 
concentrations. Usually, HEAs form simple crystal s tructures, and exhibit super mechanical 
performance [2]. In the past, the mechanical properties and solid-solution-strengthening mechanisms 
of HEAs at ambient temperature have been extensively investigated [8-13]. For example, Zhou et al. 
[2] investigated the room-temperature mechanical properties of AlCoCrFeNiTi 
x alloys with different 
Ti contents, and found that the strengths of the al loys were greatly enhanced, which is due to the 
solid-solution strengthen effect of a large atomic radius of Ti. However, the mechanical behaviors of 
HEAs at cryogenic temperatures are yet to be invest igated in detail. It is known that the 
low-temperature mechanical properties are very important for actual applications of metallic alloys 
[14-16]. The aim of this letter is to study the compressive characteristics of a single-phase HEA with 
the composition of AlCoCrFeNi at 77 K.

Usually, the HEAs form a solid solution with only a body-centered cubic (BCC) or face-centered 
cubic (FCC) structure rather than the complex britt le intermetallics, which makes them possess 
excellent mechanical properties, such as high strength and large plasticity [2]. But some HEAs with 
complex microstructures exhibit brittlement [20]. T he microstructures of the HEAs with the 
compositions of AlCoCrFeNi and AlCoCrFeNiTi 0.5 are shown in Figures 1(a) and 1(b), respectively. 
It has been identified that a single-phase-solid so lution with a BCC structure forms for the 
AlCoCrFeNi HEA [3,10]. From Figure 1(a), it can be seen that a dendritic morphology within the 
grain, indicated by the black arrows, together with the fine grain boundary, indicated by the white 
arrows, constitutes the microstructure. The magnifi ed grain-boundary morphology is shown in the 
inset of Figure 1(a), and no other phases precipita te along the boundaries, indicating a single-phase 
solid solution. As a contrast, for the AlCoCrFeNiTi 0.5 HEA, the microstructure is consisted of the 
dendritic solid solution and Laves phase, as indicated by arrows in Figure 1(b), precipitating along the 
grain boundaries. The Laves phase is identified as Fe 2Ti type [2]. The structure is closely related with 
the composition, and, usually, the component elements in HEAs could be regarded as a solute atom.

To better understand the mechanical behaviors of the HEAs at ambient and cryogenic temperatures, 
the detailed analysis of fractographs is necessary. Figure 4 reveals the morphologies of the 
fractographs of the deformed samples. Figure 4(a) is the fracture morphology at ambient temperature. 
It is clearly seen that the normal fracture occurs since the fracture direction is along the compressive 
direction. Besides, many cracks prevail on the frac ture surface. The magnified fracture surface is 
shown in Figure 4(b), and, microscopically, a cleav age fracture dominates the fracture. The lateral 
surface of the deformed sample at ambient temperature is shown in Figure 4(c). It can be seen that the 
fracture is intercrystalline at ambient temperature. The cracks, indicated by the arrows, are along the 
grain boundary. 
Figure 4(d) shows the morphology of the deformed sample at the cryogenic temperature of 77 K, 
and the sample is splitted into pieces. Generally, part of the plastic-deformation work will transform 
into the surface energy due to the formation of fra cture surfaces upon failure. At 77 K, more work 
during plastic deformation due to the strengthening will transform into the surface energy. As a result, 
more fracture pieces will form to accommodate the surface energy. Figure 4(e) shows the magnified 
fracture surface, and it bears a similar morphology of the fracture surface at ambient temperature. 
Besides, there are many microcracks prevailing on t he cleavage fracture surface, indicated by the 
arrows. Careful examinations on the lateral surface of the deformed sample reveal that the fracture at 
cryogenic temperature is transcrystalline, as shown in Figure 4(f). The cracks, indicated by arrows, 
obviously traverse the grain boundaries. Based on t he analysis on the fractographs at ambient and 
cryogenic temperatures, the fractographs are consis tent with corresponding mechanical properties. 
The higher fracture strength at 77 K than that at 2 98 K induces more fracture pieces and the 
transgranular mode.

Conclusions 
The phase-formation rule of HEAs with the compositions of AlCoCrFeNi and AlCoCrFeNiTi 
0.5 was 
discussed, based on the atom-size difference in multicomponent alloys. A single-phase solid solution 
with a BCC structure can be obtained for AlCoCrFeNi HEA. However, the Laves-phases precipitate 
along the grain boundaries of the AlCoCrFeNiTi 0.5 HEA. For the sigle-phase AlCoCrFeNi HEA, 
comparing with the mechanical properties at different temperatures, it is concluded that the yielding 
strengths and fracture strengths increase by 29.7 % and 19.9 %, respectively, when the temperatures 
decrease from 298 to 77 K. Nevertheless, the fractu re strains change very gently, while the fracture 
modes at 298 and 77 K are intergranular and transgranular, respectively.
