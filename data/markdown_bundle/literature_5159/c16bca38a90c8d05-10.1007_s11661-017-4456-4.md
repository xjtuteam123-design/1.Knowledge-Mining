Effect of the Cr 2Nb Phase on the Tensile Behavior
and Oxidation Resistance of a Two-Step Heat-
Treated Nb-24Ti-12Si-10Cr-2Al-2Hf Alloy
L.N. JIA and H. ZHANG
After a two-step heat treatment, the Cr 2Nb particles in a directionally solidiﬁed Nb-24Ti-12-
Si-10Cr-2Al-2Hf (at pct, unless stated otherwise) alloy were substantially reﬁned into nanoscale
precipitates. These precipitates showed the following orientation relationship with the Nb SS
matrix: (111)Nb//(111)Cr2Nb, [110]Nb//[220]Cr2Nb. The formation of nanoscale Cr 2Nb particles via
an aging heat treatment at 1273 K (1000 /C176 C) improved the tensile strength to 950 MPa and
reduced the alloy weight gain from 218.27 to 144.41 mg/cm 2.

I. INTRODUCTION
ALTHOUGH the operating temperatures of the
hottest section of advanced turbine engines approach
the stability limit of nickel-based superalloys, there is
ongoing demand for further improvements in engine
performance.[1] Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys have gained con-
siderable attention due to their high melting points,
relatively low densities, and excellent high-temperature
mechanical properties. [2] In this alloy system, Nb SS
ensures good room-temperature fracture toughness and
tensile strength, silicides oﬀer high-temperature
strength, and Cr 2Nb provides oxidation resistance. [3]
An excellent combination of high- and low-temperature
mechanical properties has been achieved in the
Nb-24.7Ti-8.2Hf-2.0Cr-1.9Al-16.0Si (MASC) alloy. [4]
Nevertheless, the long-term oxidation resistance of the
Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys is insuﬃcient, which presents a
major bottleneck that restricts their application. [5–8]
Extensive eﬀorts have shown that the addition of
alloying elements such as Cr, Al, Ti, B, and Ge can
improve oxidation resistance. [9–12] Among these ele-
ments, chromium is the most eﬃcient element for
conferring oxidation resistance; therefore, oxidation
resistance is promoted by the formation of the Cr 2Nb
phase. Unfortunately, Cr 2Nb is very brittle, with a
fracture toughness of approximately 1.4 MPa m 1/2 at
ambient temperature, and this brittleness persists even
above 1273 K (1000 /C176 C).[13,14] The results presented by
Zhang indicated that the macrohardness of the
Nb-Si-based alloys increases with the addition of Cr. [15]
A variety of processing schemes have been used to
alleviate the detrimental eﬀects of incorporating Cr 2Nb.
However, modifying the morphology of Cr 2Nb seems
diﬃcult. The size of Cr 2Nb phases in Nb-Cr alloys
obtained via rapid solidiﬁcation techniques is much
smaller than that in alloys obtained via conventional
casting, but this phase exhibits a continuous network
morphology that enables easy crack propagation.
Davidson has demonstrated that slower cooling rates
would result in larger scale Cr 2Nb particles that would
break at a lower stress than smaller particles. [16] This
ﬁnding indicates that smaller Cr 2Nb particles reduce
stress. After heat treatments are applied, the contiguity
of Cr 2Nb blocks, which prefer direct formation from
liquid melting, cannot be altered easily. Fortunately, this
phase can be dissolved during high-temperature heat
treatments. In this study, two-step heat treatments were
employed to obtain ﬁne Cr 2Nb particles in a
Nb-24Ti-12Si-10Cr-2Al-2Hf alloy. The microstructure
of the heat-treated alloys was studied in detail, especially
that of the twice-heat-treated alloys. The purpose of the
ﬁrst heat treatment was to ensure the decomposition of
Nb3Si and dissolve the solidiﬁed Cr 2Nb blocks, while
the aim of the second heat treatment was to obtain ﬁne
Cr2Nb particles.

elsewhere.[2] Two-step heat treatments were performed

B. Tensile Strength of the Alloy After the First Heat
Treatment
After this ﬁrst heat treatment, the tensile strength of
the alloys was tested, and the results are shown in
Table III. All the alloys show good tensile strengths
ranging from 716 to 890 MPa. The tensile strength of
the HT13 alloy is 890 MPa, which is much higher than
that of the HT12 alloy. Since these alloys have similar
phase compositions, the diﬀerence in the tensile strength
may be attributed to the distribution and size of the
constituent phases. Comparing the microstructures of
these alloys (as shown in Figure 1), the HT13 alloy has
vermicular Nb
5Si3 that is well dispersed but not coars-
ened. As a result, the HT13 alloy exhibits the best tensile
strength, approximately 890 MPa. Therefore, heat treat-
ment at 1698 K (1425 /C176 C) for 10 hours is best for this
alloy.

D. Tensile Strength and Macrohardness of the Alloy
After the Second Heat Treatment
The tensile strength and macrohardness of the alloys
after the aging treatments are listed in Table V. A heat
treatment at 1273 K (1000 /C176 C) for 50 hours substan-
tially enhances the tensile strength; thus, the HT21 alloy
exhibits a fairly good tensile strength of approximately
950 MPa. The tensile strengths of the HT22, HT23, and
HT24 alloys were less than that of the HT13 alloy and
decrease as the aging temperature increases. Thus, a
lower aging temperature improves the tensile strength.
The macrohardness of the alloy decreases as the aging
temperature increases.
After the second heat treatment, the alloys display
diﬀerent tensile strengths, despite their identical com-
positions. However, the morphologies and distributions
of the Nb
5Si3 phase in the microstructures of these
alloys are similar. The main distinction between these
alloys lies in the characteristics of the Cr
2Nb and Nb SS
phases. The fractured surfaces of the samples submitted
to tensile tests are investigated and shown in Figures 6.
In the low-magniﬁcation images (Figure 6(a), (d), (g),
and (j)), the surfaces of the HT22, HT23, and HT24
samples are rougher than that of the HT21 sample. The
high-magniﬁcation images reveal that these samples
fractured in a diﬀerent mode. In the HT21 and HT22
samples, Nb
SS exhibited many small dimples and
rugosity. The presence of many dimples indicated that
NbSS failed to exhibit ductile behavior. The rugosity in
the HT23 and HT24 samples seems to be relatively ﬂat,
and some of the Nb
SS phase displays river-like patterns,
indicating a decrease in the degree of deformation of the
NbSS phase in these two samples. The areas fractured in
the dimple and rugosity modes have compositions
similar to that of the Nb SS phase, while that of the area
fractured in the cleavage mode or where the observed
cracks appeared is similar to that of Nb
5Si3. During the
tensile processes, nanoscale Cr 2Nb particles could be
extracted from the matrix in the HT21 and HT22
samples, generating dimples. Additionally, the size of
the gap in the dimples does not match that of the ﬁner
particles. Possibly, the separation of Cr
2Nb from the
matrix occurs due to plastic stretching. In many
instances, extensive decohesion of the Cr
2Nb and matrix
interface has been associated with plastic stretching.
When the volume fraction of ﬁner Cr 2Nb particles is
larger, the tensile strength is increased, which is shown
in Figure 7. Consequently, the tensile strength of HT21
is higher than that of HT22. In the HT23 and HT24
samples, there are few nanoscale Cr
2Nb particles dis-
tributed through the matrix, and only larger Cr 2Nb
particles are located at the interfaces, which may act as
the crack initiation sites. This ﬁnding suggests that
distributing nanoscale Cr
2Nb particles throughout the
NbSS matrix improve the strength at room temperature,
whereas Cr2Nb particles located at the interface of Nb SS
and Cr 2Nb decrease the strength at room temperature.
The relationship between the macrohardness and the
volume fraction of Cr 2Nb is shown in Figure 7. The
microhardness increases as the volume fraction of
Cr2Nb increases, likely because the hardness of inter-
metallic phase Cr 2Nb is higher than that of Nb SS.

IV. CONCLUSIONS
In this work, the eﬀects of two-step heat treatments
on the microstructure, the tensile strength at room
temperature, and the oxidation resistance at high
temperatures of the Nb-12Si-24Ti-10Cr-2Al-2Hf alloy
were studied and the eﬀects of the second heat treat-
ments on these properties were emphasized. The con-
clusions reached by this work are as follows:
(1) After the second heat treatment, the morphology
and size of Nb
5Si3 were unchanged, but Cr 2Nb
particles precipitate at the interfaces of the Nb SS
and Nb 5Si3 phases, and nanoscale particles are
distributed in the Nb SS matrix of the HT21 and
HT22 alloys.
(2) Nb SS and precipitated Cr 2Nb were confirmed to
have the bcc and fcc structures, respectively, and
Cr2Nb frequently has the following orientation
relationships with the matrix: (111) Nb//(111)Cr2Nb,
[110]Nb//[220]Cr2Nb.
(3) The formation of nanoscale Cr 2Nb particles, due
to the aging heat treatment at 1273 K (1000 /C176 C)
for 50 hours, substantiality improved the tensile
strength from 850 to 950 MPa and reduced the
alloy weight gain from 218.27 to 144.41 mg/cm
2.
