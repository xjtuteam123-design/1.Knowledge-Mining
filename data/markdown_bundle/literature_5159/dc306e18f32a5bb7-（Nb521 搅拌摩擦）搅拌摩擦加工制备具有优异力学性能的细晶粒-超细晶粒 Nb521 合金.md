ABSTRACT
High strength-ductility synergy is difficult to achieve in Nb alloys. Although high strength has been achieved 
through severe plastic deformation (SPD) technology, led to low ductility in alloys. In this work, FSP technology 
was applied to treat Nb – 5W – 2Mo – 1Zr-0.1C (Nb521) alloys in preparation of fine-grained (FG)/ultrafine-grained 
(UFG) Nb521 with excellent strength and ductility. The microstructure evolution and mechanical property 
improvement mechanism were systematically studied for Nb521 alloy through various characterization path -
ways. The research results indicated that UFG Nb521 alloy with a grain size of 0.63 ± 0.41 μ m can be prepared 
using low shoulder plunge depth FSP (LPD-FSP), which is the first report of UFG Nb521 alloy. The main reason 
for the formation of onion rings structure in SZ is the periodic wear of the stirring tool, and the onion rings 
structure does not cause mechanical damage. The texture formed by Nb521 alloy under different processing 
parameters is off-axis shear texture, which matches the ideal shear texture of D2 ( 11 2 )[ 111 ] after rotation. In 
addition, this study also elaborated on the refinement mechanism of the second phase particles (Nb, Zr) C in 
Nb521 alloy during FSP. This study also indicated that the increase in yield strength of FSP samples at room 
temperature is mainly determined by grain refinement. These findings provided new ideas for the development 
of high-performance niobium alloys.

affected multiple industries, including but not limited to engineering 
physics, aerospace, optical engineering, electronic engineering, and the 
thriving bio-medical field. Of all promising candidates in Nb alloys, the 
Nb – 5W – 2Mo – 1Zr-0.1C (Nb521) alloy is one well-researched member. It

Friction stir processing (FSP) is a high temperature SPD technique 
that refines the grain sizes of metals and alloys. The pin or shoulder of 
the FSP tool creates heat from friction, generating a soften zone from 
heat, together with dynamic recrystallization (DRX) induced from the 
stirring motion of the pin. The DRX process induced by FSP creates fine 
equiaxed grains in the stir zone (SZ), which leads to the formation of a 
good strength-toughness balance [ 13 , 14 ] and wear resistance. The tis -
sue evolution and mechanical behavior of the stir zone can be manip -
ulated by changing the fabrication parameters of FSP, such as tool 
traverse speed, tool rotation speed, shoulder plunge depth, and tool 
geometry [ 15 ]. Many publications have proved that FSP can fabricate 
FG/UFG Al, Mg, Cu alloys, and steel [ 16 – 20 ], while there lack similar 
report for Nb521 alloy with BCC structure due to its high melting point 
and oxidation sensitivity. According to the existing research in 
FSW/FSP, FSP and its variant Friction stir welding (FSW) were catego -
rized based on the temperature and forces/loads applied. There are 
conventional FSW/FSP [ 21 , 22 ], large load FSW/high-force FSP 
[ 21 – 23 ], and low-temperature FSP [ 24 ]. However, to the best of our 
knowledge, there have been no reports addressing the low shoulder 
plunge depth FSW/FSP (LPD- FSW/FSP). Here, we manipulated several 
aspects of the FSP conditions, including but not limited to materials of 
stirring tools (W – Re alloy for optimal mechanical performance at high 
temperature), heat input (reach the manufacturable temperature of 
Nb521 for FSP), and protecting atmosphere of argon gas, allowing for 
FSP to be applicable for Nb521 alloys. With the processed Nb521, we 
continue to analyze the impact of FSP on the mechanical performance 
and microstructure evolution of Nb521 alloy. Meanwhile, the shoulder 
plunge depth was set as a crucial parameter to obtain UFG Nb521 alloy, 
provide the further strengthening of Nb521 with possibility in terms of 
controlling the microstructure, and provid a new route for improving Nb 
alloy properties in terms of microstructure control and strengthening.

3.5. Taylor factor
The typical Taylor plastic deformation theory shows that general 
deformation requires at least five active sliding systems, and the com -
bined active sliding systems provide the maximum deformation work 
[ 28 ]. Fig. 9 shows the Taylor factor distribution in the BM and the SZs 
obtained by FSP under different conditions. In general, the Taylor factor 
in the figure is divided into three levels. The first level (2.2 – 2.6, blue 
grains) are grains mostly prone to slip. These grains have lower critical 
resolved shear stress (CRSS) values according to the loading direction 
where slip occurs [ 29 ]. The second level (2.6 – 3.2, yellow grains) in -
volves grains rotating in a certain direction and plastically deforming in 
specific slip systems [ 29 , 30 ]. The third level (3.2 – 3.8, red grains), 
consisting of grains that are difficult to activate an appropriate slipping 
system, has a higher Taylor factor. For BM, N1000-100-0.3, 
N1600-100-0.3, N1600-400-0.3, and N1600-100-0.03, the Taylor fac -
tors values are 2.85, 3.11, 3.05, 3.32, and 3.01, respectively.

4. Discussions
4.1. Formation of onion rings shaped stir zone
Onion rings are an important structural feature in FSW/FSP and have 
been reported in aluminum, steel, copper and other materials. However, 
the conditions of generating the onion ring for Nb alloy remains un -
discovered. In previous research on other Nb alloy FSP, no onion ring is 
observed [ 34 ]. In this study, onion rings exist only at the location of SZ 
when the FSP is in low shoulder plunge depth condition ( Fig. 3 ).
The mechanism of onion rings generation is very widely researched 
with multiple studies on different theories from the perspective of ma -
terial flow and microstructure changes (e.g., geometric effects, changes 
in grain size, differences in second phase particle distribution, and 
texture differences). K.N. Krishnan [ 35 ] believed that the appearance of 
onion rings was the result of cyclic deposition of shear layers and 
explained it with geometric effects. A. Gerlich et al. [ 36 ] showed that the 
formation of onion rings was from the introduction of material at the 
position below the shaft shoulder and the bottom of the rotating probe, 
thus produced a spiral vertical rotating flow in the mixing area formed 
around the periphery of the rotating probe. M. W. Mahoney et al. [ 37 ] 
found that for Al alloy after FSW, differences occur in the microstructure 
of grain sizes in the nugget zone (NZ), where large and small grain sizes 
are alternately distributed, resulting in onion rings. Similar result also 
appeared in steel [ 38 ] and Cu [ 39 ]. M.A. Sutton et al. [ 40 ] reported that 
onion rings were produced because of differences in the distribution of 
second phase particles and is later repeated and confirmed by A. 
Esmaeili et al. [ 41 ]. J.A. Schneider and A.C. Nunes [ 42 ] pointed out that 
the onion rings exist with changing in texture caused by the action of the 
stirring tool on the processed material. R.W. Fonda and J.F. Bingert [ 43 ] 
claimed that among the possible reasons for the generation of onion 
rings, the texture changes caused by periodic changes in strain size and 
temperature in the stirring layer should be considered. Some scholars 
believe [ 44 ] that the appearance of onion rings is from the lack of mixing 
of stirring materials. In short, there is still a lack of generally accepted 
conclusions on the origin of onion rings. Specifically, from a micro -
structural point of view, the real cause is still unclear and may be related 
to one or more factors.
As shown in Fig. 4 e, the grain size deviation in the SZ of N1600-100- 
0.03 sample is not considerably large, so grain size is not the main 
reason for the formation of onion rings in Nb521 alloy. Furtherly, the 
SEM morphology of the N1600-100-0.03 sample was acquired 
( Fig. 14 a), it clearly show the presence of bright bands and dark bands in 
the onion rings structure. The bright band is composed of numerous 
small white strip-shaped and point-shaped particles, their distribution 
should be the main reason for the formation of the onion rings structure 
( Fig. 14 b). EDS result shows that the white particles contain 82.04 wt% 
W and 17.96 wt% Re ( Fig. 14 c). Area scanning spectrum analysis 
confirmed that the white particles are rich in Tungsten (W) ( Fig. 14 d), 
indicating that the white particles are W – Re alloys which is the worn 
residue debris of the stirring tool. The results proved that the formation 
of the onion rings structure is caused by the periodic wear of the stirring 
tools.

4.2. Texture evolution

dominant deformation is expected to be simple shear [ 46 ]. Shear tex -
tures are usually defined in terms of crystallographic planes {hkl} and 
directions < uvw > , aligned with the shear plane and shear direction, 
respectively. In bcc metal, some fibers belonging to {hkl} < 111 > and 
{110} < uvw > types are formed by simple shear deformation. A.P. 
Reynolds et al. [ 46 ] studied the texture produced in β -titanium alloy 
β -21S. They found that the texture in all welding zones can be rotated 
and corresponded with the D1 ( 1 12 )[ 111 ] bcc ideal shear texture. K.E. 
Knipling and R.W. Fonda [ 47 , 48 ] found that the D1 ( 1 10 )[ 11 2 ] bcc ideal 
shear texture dominates in Ti-5111 at fast welding speeds. S. Mironov 
et al. [ 49 ] studied FSW for Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy and discovered that the β 
phase texture of FSW could generally be depicted as ideal J ( 1 10 )[ 11 2 ]
bcc shear textures. The same group also [ 50 ] measured the texture 
produced by pure iron during the FSP and observed that it corresponded

4.3. Mechanism for the evolving of second phase
Fig. 6 shows the (Nb, Zr) C particle sizes and shapes vary in SZ of 
different processes, while Fig. 16 shows the SEM result for different zone 
of the N1000-100-0.3 sample. Under the same process, (Nb, Zr)C par -
ticle size and morphology will also be different in different regions, and 
the (Nb, Zr)C particle size in SZ is significantly smaller than TMAZ and 
HAZ.
In FSP process, the forces and moments acting on the second phase 
are rather complicated, causing the second phase particles to exhibit 
various deformation, fragmentation, and refinement mechanisms. 
Categorizing with the characteristics of FSP and the physical properties 
of the second phase particles, the deformation, fragmentation and 
refinement mechanisms of (Nb, Zr)C particles can be summarized into 
the following types.
1) Mechanism from fragmentation of brittle second phase particles due 
to mutual collisions between brittle second phase particles.
The brittleness of (Nb, Zr)C particles in Nb521 alloy is much greater 
than the brittleness of the niobium alloy matrix material. During the FSP 
process, two (Nb, Zr)C particles A and B form irregular fragments after 
collision, and then the (Nb, Zr)C particle fragments are entrained by the 
plastic material to flow together and form a unique distribution. In 
different zones of the processing area, the probability of (Nb, Zr)C par -
ticles colliding and breaking varies. Specifically, the probability is the 
highest in the SZ area. Therefore, the degree of fragmentation and 
refinement of (Nb, Zr)C particles in SZ is significantly higher than that in 
TMAZ.
2) The mechanism from fragmentation and refinement of second phase 
particles resulting from the interaction between the plastic material 
being processed and the second phase particles.

Under high-temperature conditions, dislocation in the Nb521 alloy is 
more likely to cause grain slip, indicating that the material has higher 
plasticity at higher temperatures. Meanwhile, (Nb, Zr)C particles also 
have considerable plasticity under high-temperature conditions. Usu -
ally, the (Nb, Zr)C particles exhibit a nearly spherical morphology. 
During the FSP process, when the stirring tool makes compounded 
rotational and translational motion upon the material being processed, 
second-phase particles are forced into contact with the stirring tool and 
generate interactive compressive force and friction force simulta -
neously. In the vertical direction (loading direction), the (Nb, Zr)C 
particles are affected by the compressive force; in the advancing direc -
tion of the stirring tool, the (Nb, Zr)C particles are affected by friction, 
thrust and resistance. Under the action of multi-directional unbalanced 
stress, (Nb, Zr)C particles undergo plastic deformation due to dislocation 
slip, climb, and grain rotation within the (Nb, Zr)C particle crystal. Since 
the (Nb, Zr)C particles are always surrounded by the base material, 
when the matrix material flows plastically under the action of the stir -
ring tool, the wrapped (Nb, Zr)C particles deform and elongate along the 
flow direction, showing a tadpole shape. Furtherly, during the FSP 
process, the (Nb, Zr)C particles in the SZ area are subject to a large 
compressive force, causing the (Nb, Zr)C particles to be further squeezed 
and elongated on the basis of the tadpole shape, showing strip-like 
shapes. When the stress acting on the (Nb, Zr)C particles exceeds their 
strength, the (Nb, Zr)C particles will crush. The (Nb, Zr)C particles that 
are broken and refined during the FSP process continue to flow and 
disperse while accompanying the plastic material flow.
