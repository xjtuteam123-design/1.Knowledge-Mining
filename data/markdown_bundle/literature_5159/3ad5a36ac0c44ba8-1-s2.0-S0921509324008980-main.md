ABSTRACT
TiAl alloy is a high-temperature and lightweight material with superior properties, which has been widely used
in aerospace industry. and casting is an economical method for its production. However, casting defects has
hindered its direct applications in aerospace turbine blades. To minimize casting defects and achieve alloy
strengthening, this paper proposed a novel single heat treatment process under ultra-high pressure (UHP) to
regulate the microstructure and mechanical properties of the Ti – 47Al – 2Cr – 2Nb alloy. The results indicated that
the microstructure achieved overall grain refinement, high temperature and ultra-high pressure stresses jointly
induced the γ→ α
2
phase transformation. After the UHP heat treatment, the hardness of the alloy increased from
287HV to 381HV, and the yield strength and ultimate strength increased by 53 % and 20 %, respectively, without
significantly affecting the strain to fracture. Based on the phase transformation behavior and strengthening
mechanism, these enhancements in mechanical properties were mainly attributed to the precipitation
strengthening of α
2
grains and the reduction of lamellar thickness. This suggested that UHP heat treatment was
an effective method to improve cast TiAl alloys.
1. Introduction
In the aerospace industry, to enhance the thrust-to-weight ratio of
aircraft engines, it is necessary to use novel high-temperature and
lightweight material instead of heavy Ni-based superalloys [ 1 , 2 ]. TiAl
alloy has become one of the important alternative materials, primarily
due to its low density (3.9 g/cm
3
), high specific strength, specific stiff-
ness, and exceptional oxidation resistance [ 3 – 5 ]. Casting offers the high
cost-effective approach for producing TiAl alloy component [ 6 ].
Currently, TiAl alloy has been successfully implemented in the final two
stages of low-pressure turbine blades in the GEnx engine, resulting in a
weight reduction of approximately 200 lbs per engine and thereby
enhancing aircraft engine propulsion efficiency [ 7 , 8 ].
However, the casting process often results in a coarse microstructure
and is accompanied by common casting defects such as macro-
segregation, cracks, and porosity, which severely compromise the me-
chanical properties [ 9 , 10 ]. Consequently, extensive research has been
carried out in the areas of composition exploration, process
development, microstructure evolution and property improvement to
achieve the optimal trade-off between strength and ductility [ 11 ].
Adding trace elements is one of the commonly used methods. Trace
elements can alter the phase equilibrium, achieving notable enhance-
ments in both microstructure and mechanical properties [ 12 – 14 ]. Li
et al. added varying quantities of B to Ti – 43Al – 6Nb – 1Mo – 1Cr alloy and
demonstrated that the addition of B could refine the α
2
/ γ lamellar col-
onies and transform columnar crystals into equiaxed crystals, thereby
achieving grain refinement. However, the incorporation of B tends to
generate coarse borides, which are typical sources of cracks and can lead
to the premature failure of components [ 15 ]. Wang et al. introduced 0.2
% Er to the Ti – 47Al – 2Nb – 2Cr alloy to eliminate the negative effects of
interstitial oxygen on TiAl-based alloys, ensuring that the concentration
of interstitial oxygen remains below 1000 wt ppm [ 16 ]. Nevertheless,
the substantial addition of trace elements can cause compositional
contamination, reducing material purity.
Heat treatment is another common method to control microstructure
and mechanical properties [ 17 ]. Yim et al. proposed a multi-stage cyclic

comprehensively investigated, and hardness testing and room temper-

provided valuable insights into the phase transformation behavior and
strengthening mechanism under UHP heat treatment.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure evolution
Fig. 3 displays the SEM images of different samples. The red arrows
indicate the lamellar structure, the green arrows highlight the equiaxed
γ phase, and the yellow arrows represent the globular α
2
phase,
respectively. Heat treatment in α + γ phase region leads to different de-
grees of refinement in the lamellar colonies and an increase in the vol-
ume fraction of γ phase. The driving force for grain refinement is mainly
attributed to the reduction of interfacial energy and changes in the
volume fraction of α and γ phase [ 26 , 27 ]. Studies have reported that
heat treatment at temperatures close to equal volume fractions of α and γ
phases is beneficial for grain refinement [ 28 ]. Therefore, a heat treat-
ment temperature of 1250
◦
C is selected, and sufficient holding time of
8h within the α + γ phase region allows for adequate elements diffusion,
achieving thermodynamic stability of the microstructure.
In the case of HT0GPa-FC sample, the initial fully lamellar micro-
structure transforms into a duplex microstructure consisting of α
2
/ γ
lamellar colonies and equiaxed γ grains. Further observations reveal the
presence of smaller-sized lamellar colonies, resulting in an overall
refinement of grain size. The high temperature promotes the simulta-
neous rapid growth of α and γ phases, the competitive growth mecha-
nism leads to the grain refinement [ 29 ].
To explore the impact of cooling method on heat treatment in the
α + γ phase region, water cooling (WC) is employed to enhance the
cooling rate. After HT0GPa-WC, the microstructure remains duplex with
equiaxed γ grains primarily distributed at grain boundaries, and their
volume fractions increase. The growth of equiaxed γ grains exerts a
pinning effect, generating an irregular serrated morphology. Addition-
ally, due to the higher cooling rate compared to the formation rate of
regular lamellar colonies, the element distribution becomes uneven,
resulting in the formation of partial Widmanst ¨atten structures [ 30 ]. The
metastable structures can induce stress concentration and instability at
grain boundaries, leading to diminished toughness and reduced resis-
tance against crack propagation. Hence, the HT4GPa-FC sample is sub-
jected to furnace cooling until reaching room temperature to suppress
the formation of metastable structures.
UHP (4 GPa) leads to the coexistence of the α
2
phase in both equiaxed
and lamellar forms. By utilizing ImageJ software for statistical analysis,
the average size of α
2
equiaxed grains is approximately 18.7 μ m,
resulting in an overall grain refinement. The irregular morphology of the
lamellar colonies primarily results from the segmentation effect of α
2
and γ grains. Most grains are mainly located at grain boundaries, with a
minor presence within the lamellar colonies.

3.3. Effects of heat treatment compression properties at RT
Fig. 6 displays the compression engineering stress-strain curves for
different samples at RT, and the corresponding statistical data on the
compression properties are shown in Table 2 . The compression test re-
sults show that the strain to fracture (SF) for the As-cast, HT0GPa-FC,
HT0GPa-WC, and HT4GPa-FC samples are 26.36 ± 0.24 %, 27.31 ±
0.56 %, 23.12 ± 0.45 % and 25.69 ± 0.23 %, respectively. In the As-cast
condition, the ultimate strength (US) is measured to be 1582.63 ±
12.32 MPa. The HT0GPa-FC and HT0GPa-WC samples exhibit US of
1424.95 ± 15.03 MPa and 1615.56 ± 23.14 MPa respectively. After
UHP heat treatment, the US of HT4GPa-FC sample increase significantly
to 1884.53 ± 17.11 MPa. A similar trend can be observed for the yield
strength (YS), with the highest value of 650.65 ± 12.19 MPa being
achieved for the HT4GPa-FC sample.
After atmospheric pressure heat treatment, the SF of the HT0GPa-FC
sample exhibits a 0.95 % increase comparing to the As-cast sample, but
its strength decreases significantly. The lowest SF is found in the
HT0GPa-WC sample, implying that water cooling reduces the ductility
of the TiAl alloy. Regarding the HT4GPa-FC sample, while the SF shows
a slight decrease of 0.67 % compared to the As-cast sample, there is a
remarkable enhancement in both yield strength and ultimate strength,
with an impressive increase of 53 % and 20 %, respectively.

4. Discussion
4.1. Effect of ultra-high pressure on phase transformation behavior
Ti – 47Al – 2Cr – 2Nb alloy generates obvious phase transformation
during the heat treatment. Fig. 10 shows the phase distribution and
volume fractions for different samples, the volume fractions of α
2
and γ
phases are 9.8 % and 90.2 % for As-cast sample. As shown in Fig. 10 (a) –
(b), heat treatment in the α + γ phase region leads to a decrease in the
volume fraction of the α

treatment, a significant number of α
2
grains and a small amount of γ
grains are formed, leading to an increased volume fraction of the α
2
phase to 8.7 %. This indicates that the TiAl alloy generates γ → α

phase
transformation under UHP heat treatment.
Fig. 11 illustrates the schematic diagrams of phase transformation for
different samples. The HT0GPa-FC sample exhibits a disordered distri-
bution of HAGBs and LAGBs, indicating a relatively uniform distribution
of dislocations. Furthermore, due to the stacking fault energy (SFE) of γ
phase is lower than that of α
2

occurs prior to the α
2

tallized γ grains under atmospheric pressure conditions.
Apart from temperature, studies have shown that stress-induced
factors can promote γ → α
2
phase transformation [ 40 – 43 ]. During
UHP heat treatment, the sample is subjected to significant hydrostatic
pressure stress, which activates slip systems of dislocations and causes
their accumulation at grain boundaries. The high density of dislocations

tallized α

grain boundaries and an increased volume fraction of the α

When temperature and stress satisfy the conditions for γ → α

α
