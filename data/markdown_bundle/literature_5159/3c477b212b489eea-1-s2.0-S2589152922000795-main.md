One of the challenging phenomena in the materials science community is the issue of the strength-ductility trade- 
oﬀ. Pre-strain followed by annealing at low temperatures was used as a novel strategy to overcome the strength- 
ductility trade-oﬀ of CoCrFeNiMn high entropy alloy. The proposed process aims to retain the deformation- 
induced twins after partial recovery. However, the lack of precipitate in the processed alloy bears in mind to 
consider the eﬀect of precipitation. In this paper, for the ﬁrst time, we proposed a simple thermomechanical 
process including light cryogenic rolling (reduction of thickness ∼28%) followed by low-temperature annealing 
(at 500 °C for 180 min) to obtain a unique microstructure. The microstructure with partial recovery contains 
retained deformation-induced twins along with bcc Cr-rich precipitates under optimum conditions (size and vol- 
ume fraction) and no brittle sigma precipitates. This microstructure provides the appropriate balance between 
deformation and precipitation strengthening. The microstructure exhibits much improved mechanical properties 
(UTS ∼1.25 GPa and uniform elongation ∼15%). Additionally, a model was introduced for predicting mechanical 
properties using microstructural parameters. The present results indicated that this thermomechanical process- 
ing could be used as an innovative processing strategy for achieving superior strength–ductility trade-oﬀ by 
microstructural engineering. We believe that a similar concept may be applied for optimizing the mechanical 
properties of other fcc high or medium entropy alloys based on the Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-Mn system. 
1. Introduction 
There has been worldwide special attention to develop high- 
performance alloys with a suitable balance between strength and duc- 
tility; and it has been raised more because of the awareness of energy 
resources, safety issues, and environmental problems. In this regard, the 
major mindset in materials research is about raising the strength of ma- 
terials without the detriment of ductility [1] . High entropy alloys (HEAs) 
development with promising mechanical properties provides a great op- 
portunity to design new alloys with superior strength-ductility trade-oﬀ
[2–5] . Equiatomic CoCrFeNiMn HEA as a pioneer alloy has oﬀered a 
great capability in applications related to structural materials linked to 
its acceptable mechanical properties at low temperatures and its resis- 
tance to hydrogen embrittlement [6–8] . Deformation-induced twinning 
is a well-known deformation mechanism for enhancing uniform plas- 
tic deformation in alloys [ 9 , 10 ]. Residual deformation twins in high

partial recovery (similar to twinning engineering), and also the forma- 
tion of bcc Cr-rich precipitates in optimal conditions (size and volume 
fraction) but preventing the formation of brittle sigma precipitates. 
2. Materials and methods 
The HEA with a nominal composition of CoCrFeNiMn 
(Co 20 Cr 20 Fe 20 Ni 20 Mn 20 in at%) was prepared using a non-consumable 
vacuum arc melting technique in a water-cooled copper crucible. Pure 
titanium was also melted and used as a getter in the chamber. After 
remelting four times to achieve reasonable homogenization, the ingots 
were hot rolled and then homogenized at 1100 °C for 48 h in a vacuum 
atmosphere, which led to an average grain size of ∼330 𝜇m. 
The chemical composition of the fabricated alloy analyzed by Energy 
Dispersive X-Ray Spectroscopy was Co 19.66 Cr 20.12 Fe 20.33 Ni 19.92 Mn 19.97 
(at%), which showed a very good match between the nominal compo- 
sition and the actual composition of the HEA. The homogenized sam- 
ple was rolled at room (RT) and cryogenic (CT) temperatures (25 and 
− 190 °C, respectively) in order to achieve a reduction of the thickness of 
28 and 55%, denoted as small and medium plastic deformation ( Fig. 1 ). 
Post-deformation annealing (PDA) was conducted at 300–1000 °C for 
30 min. Measurements of Vickers microhardness, HV, were performed at 
the center of the longitudinal section of the sheet parallel to the rolling 
direction. For microhardness measurements, a load of 100 gf was ap- 
plied for a dwell time of 10 s, and every point of the reported values for 
HV represents the average of 7 individual hardness values. 
Cylindrical samples of 10 mm length and diameter up to 2 mm were 
cut and inserted into an Adamel DT1000 dilatometer to provide an 
isothermal study of the RT-28%, CT-28%, RT-55%, and CT-55% sam- 
ples. For this thermal analysis, the deformed samples were heated to 
500 °C at a heating rate of 10 °C.min − 1 , held at this temperature for 
180 min.

Microstructural characterizations were carried out using Field 
emission-scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) and transmission elec-

by TEM at 3 mm from the samples were prepared by conventional twin- 
jet electro-polishing of mechanically pre-thinned to 80 mm foils, using 
an electrolyte composed of 95% C 2 H 5 OH and 5% HClO 4 at − 20 °C. 
Field emission-scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) equipped with 
an electron backscatter diﬀraction (EBSD) detector was performed us- 
ing a JEOL 7100F device. EBSD measurements were conducted with a 
step size of 50 nm. The surface of the samples was polished using dia- 
mond paste and colloidal silica suspension for the EBSD analysis. 
Dog bone tensile test samples with a gauge length of 8.0 mm were 
cut from the annealed sheet using electro-discharge machining. Tensile 
testing was conducted at room temperature using an initial strain rate 
of ∼1.0 ×10 − 3 s − 1 with a SANTAM STM-20 universal testing machine, 
and at least two samples were tested for each condition to achieve good 
reproducibility. Stress-strain curves were plotted for each specimen, and 
the UTS values were derived directly from the curves. Elongations were 
estimated by carefully measuring the gauge lengths before and after 
tensile testing.

3.2. A proposed model for structure-hardness correlation after annealing at 
500 °C 
To achieve optimal precipitation eﬀectiveness, dilatometric thermal 
analysis was performed at a temperature of 500 °C for the deformed 
samples ( Fig. 7 a). Phase transformations such as recrystallization, grain 
growth, and the formation of some precipitates are associated with a 
decrease in volume or reverse in some precipitation cases [34] ; there- 
fore, dilatometry can provide valuable information about microstruc- 
tural evolution during heating. The formation of the sigma precipitate 
(with lattice parameters of a ∼8.8 Åand c ∼4.5 Å[15] ) and the formation 
of bcc Cr-rich precipitates (a ∼2.88 Å[15] ) in the fcc matrix (a ∼3.59 Å
[34] ) lead to a decrease ( ∼0.4%) and increase ( ∼2.4%), respectively, in 
the volume [ 19 , 37 ]. Accordingly, it seems the current increments in vol- 
ume at 500 °C are due to the formation of bcc Cr-rich precipitates. These 
results are accompanied by TEM observations which show no recrystal- 
lization and formation of the sigma phase during annealing at 500 °C. 
Close inspection of curves in Fig. 7 a indicates an incubation time for 
precipitations ( 𝜏) about ∼350, ∼250, ∼100, and ∼60 s, for RT-28%, CT- 
28%, RT-55%, and CT-55% samples, respectively. Nevertheless, no in- 
cubation time was observed for precipitation in this HEA after the large 
plastic deformation [19] . The incubation time decreases with reducing 
deformation temperature or increasing deformation value, which intro- 
duces more defects and interfaces such as twins or dislocations, leading 
to an increase in precipitation kinetics.

4. Discussion 
4.1. A unique microstructure by optimizing thermomechanical parameters 
The objective of the present investigation is to obtain the appropri- 
ate parameters of the thermomechanical process in order to overcome 
the mechanical properties shortcoming of the alloy. Accordingly, small 
thickness reduction (only 28%) was utilized to avoid introducing too 
many defects, and the deformation at cryogenic temperature was se- 
lected to increase the volume fraction of DITs in the microstructure. 
Lowering the deformation temperature is beneﬁcial in terms of decreas- 
ing SFE ( ∼21 mJ.m − 2 at RT to ∼10 mJ.m − 2 at CT in CoCrFeNiMn HEA) 
and therefore encourages DIT rather than dislocation slip [40] . It is well 
known that the magnitude of SFE in alloys governs the mechanism of 
deformation ranging from DIT dominated at low values to slip medi- 
ated at large SFE values [41] . The post-deformation annealing at 500 °C 
leads to the formation of four important features in the microstructure: 
(1) partial recovery to improve ductility, (2) sustaining the DITs, and (3) 
formation bcc Cr-rich precipitates to enhance strength, and (4) avoiding 
the formation of brittle sigma Cr-rich precipitates to prevent ductility 
loss. The ability of the dislocation density to increase from a low base 
established by the recovery equips the material with a signiﬁcant strain 
hardening rate capability, which ensures good tensile ductility [13] . 
One of the important features of this heat treatment is sustaining the 
DITs because they can act as strong barriers against dislocation move- 
ment and improve strength [42] . Similarly, DITs in TWIP steels were 
thermally stable up to 625 °C [43] . According to the Temperature-Time- 
Transformation (TTT) diagrams of the CoCrFeNiMn alloy, free sigma 
precipitate alloy containing bcc precipitates can be achieved at 500 °C 
only [ 19 , 44 ]. The absence of sigma precipitates in the microstructure 
can keep ductility because of their extremely brittle nature [15] . Also, 
holding time at 500 °C is one of the inﬂuential parameters for achieving 
optimal precipitation conditions and improving the mechanical prop- 
erties. The hardness results, along with the proposed model at 500 °C, 
showed that a holding time of 180 min provides optimal condition for 
CT-28% sample. The peak hardening reveals that the particles have an 
optimum size and volume fraction in a way that both dislocations cut- 
ting through particles or dislocations bowing around particles are ac- 
tive hardening mechanisms and contribute evenly to the hardness peak

5. Conclusion 
In summary, our work has provided a breakthrough in achieving 
synergy of strength and ductility in CoCrFeNiMn HEA by manipulat- 
ing the simple thermomechanical processing, including light cryogenic 
rolling followed (reduction of thickness ∼28%) by annealing at low tem- 
perature (at 500 °C for 180 min). Extraordinary mechanical properties 
(UTS ∼1.25 GPa and uniform elongation ∼15%) in the present investiga- 
tion are attributed to high area fraction of DITs, partial recovery, nano 
bcc Cr-rich under optimal conditions (size and volume fraction), pre- 
vent to introduce too many defects and absence of brittle sigma phase 
in the microstructure. In addition to these features, a model was pro- 
posed that makes prediction feasible based on microstructural param- 
eters to achieve optimal conditions to reach extraordinary mechanical 
properties.
