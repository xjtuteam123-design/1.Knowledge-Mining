Modelling of the mechanical response of Zr–Nb and Ti–Nb
alloys in a wide temperature range
Vladimir A. Skripnyak . Vladimir V. Skripnyak . Evgeniya G. Skripnyak .
Nataliya V. Skripnyak
Received: 31 October 2018 / Accepted: 28 January 2019
/C211Springer Nature B.V. 2019
Abstract This article presents the results of model-
ing the mechanical behavior of Zr–Nb and Ti–Nb
alloys in a range of strain rates from 0.001 to 1000 1/s
and temperature range 297–1273 K. A modiﬁcation of
constitutive equations describing the mechanical
response of ﬁne-grained and coarse-grained Zr–1Nb
and Ti–13Nb–13Zr alloys in a wide temperature range
is proposed. It was shown that the phase transition
between the hexagonal closed packed and body-
centered cubic crystal structure at elevated tempera-
tures leads to a sharp change in strain rate sensitivity of
the yield strength of Zr–Nb and Ti–Nb alloys. The
proposed modiﬁcations of constitutive equations make
it possible to describe the strain hardening and the
strain rate sensitivity of the plastic ﬂow stress over a
wide temperature range in the coarse-crystalline and
ultraﬁne-grained Zr–Nb and Ti–Nb alloys. The results
can be used for engineering analysis of structural
elements of technical systems and design of manufac-
turing technologies for biomedical products.

1 Introduction
The reliability of complex nuclear power systems,
aerospace transport systems, and chemical reactors is
closely related to the adequacy in predictions of the
mechanical behavior of materials in critical structural
elements in wide ranges of temperatures and strain
rates. The validation of models of mechanical behav-
ior of modern and new structural materials is impor-
tant to ensure that predictions of strength and
deformation of structural elements are correct. In
modern computer-aided design software, a number of
models are used to describe the deformation and
fracture of main classes of materials.
New materials, nanostructured or ultraﬁnegrained
materials require a validation of existing models or the
development of new constitutive models of mechan-
ical behavior. Research in this area has been inten-
sively carried out for various technical applications.
New and modiﬁed models taking into account
structural and temperature effects can be implemented
as a user deﬁned material subroutine in FE solver LS-
DYNA (Dar et al. 2014). Processing conditions and
the nature of microstructure evolution of materials are
important when designing or characterizing

The effect of temperature on the mechanisms of
deformation and fracture should be taken into account
when predicting the resource life of products operating
under unsteady temperature conditions. A number of
studies have shown a signiﬁcant effect of temperature
on the mechanisms and fracture surfaces of metal
alloys (Dafang et al. 2010). Last decades several types
of Zr–Nb and Ti–Nb–Zr alloys were developed and
studied.
Huh et al. ( 2014) conducted an analysis of well-
known models including the Johnson–Cook model,
the Zerilli–Armstrong model and the Preston–Tonks–
Wallace model using results of tests on fcc, bcc and
hcp materials. It was shown, that the appropriate
model taking into account strain rate sensitivity and
temperature for each of materials should be con-
structed by the comparison of predictions and exper-
imental results.
It is important that the adequacy in descriptions of
elastic–plastic behavior of materials with different
crystalline lattices in wide range of temperature, strain
rate and plastic deformation cannot be met by only the
variation of values of coefﬁcients.
Improvement in the technology for the fabrication
of some constructional elements of nuclear reactors is
connected with an adequacy in computer simulation of
speciﬁed process and, as a result, with an adequacy in
the description of the mechanical properties and
structural evolution of radiation-resistant alloys Zr–
Nb (Rodchenkov and Semenov 2005; Motta et al.
2007; Blokhin et al. 2011; Duan et al. 2017). Ti–Nb–
Zr alloys have remarkable properties such as low
density, high melting point, good oxidation resistance
and high speciﬁc strengths, low elastic moduli,
biocompatibility, etc. (Nikonov et al. 2015; Bobbili
and Madhu 2017). In this regard, there is an increasing
need to develop computational models of the mechan-
ical behavior of advanced Zr–Nb in loading conditions
close to operating ones. Understanding of the mechan-
ical behavior of Ti–Nb–Zr alloys in wide temperature
range is extremely essential for the numerical model-
ing of various applications and manufacturing
technologies.
Zr–Nb b Ti–Nb alloys with body centered cubic
lattice may exhibit reduced elastic moduli approach-
ing respective properties of bone tissues (Hynowska
et al. 2012; Yang et al. 2010). Zr–Nb alloys possess
excellent corrosion resistance too. A low elastic
modulus of b-Zr can be achieved at room temperature.
The experimental results suggested that Zr–22Nb
alloy showed the good biocompatibility, thus making
it a promising material for orthopedic devices (Chui
2017). Computer-aided design of orthopedic implants
from these Zr–Nb alloys is associated with the need to
predict their mechanical behavior under dynamic
loads when a person falls or jumps.
It was found that the creation of ultraﬁne-grained
structure of HCP metals and alloys can improve their
strength and corrosion resistance. Therefore, ultraﬁne
grained (UFG) zirconium alloys with a concentration
of Nb below 2.5 weight % and additionally doped with
Mo, Fe, Cr for the stabilization of precipitations of
beta-phase Zr now is in focus of research (Fong2013).
It was established that the formation of ultraﬁne-
grained structures in the alloys of Zr–Nb not only
improves the yield strength of the alloy, but also
prevents the formation of cracks at the mesoscopic
level (Behera et al.2016). But due to the low solubility
limit of Nb in the Zr matrix, coherent and incoherent
precipitations of Nb phases are formed during thermal
annealing (Toyama et al. 2015). The effect of precip-
itates on the mechanical behavior of alloys in a wide
temperature range and under dynamic loading condi-
tions has not been fully studied. This makes it difﬁcult
to create adequate models for predicting the mechan-
ical behavior of alloys with dispersed hardening in
wide ranges of strain rates and temperatures.
Zain-ul-abdein and Nelias ( 2016) showed that
coherent precipitation in the microstructure can be
the cause of the appearance of critical values of peak
stresses, which can cause the formation of microc-
racks. It was founded the plastic strain ﬁeld in the soft
matrix due to the coherent precipitates is more uniform
and less in magnitude than the one due to the
incoherent impurities, since the latter introduces
discontinuities at the micro-scale. This is because the
increase in temperature results in an increased mobil-
ity of dislocations, thereby reducing the overall
strength of the material. However, the hardening
slopes remain the same over a wide the temperature
range 0.32 \ T/T
m \ 0.52. This observation indicates
that hard coherent precipitates impart signiﬁcant
strength to the alloy by interlocking the dislocations.

numerical simulation of coarse grained and ultraﬁne-

2 Computational model
The application of Johnson–Cook and Zerilli–Arm-
strong modiﬁcations models to describe the mechan-
ical behavior of crystalline and ultraﬁne-grained
alloys Zr–Nb and Ti–Nb alloys within the temperature
range from 295 K to 1273 K and the strain rates range
from 10
-3 to 10 4 s-1 was studied by computer
simulation of experiments on a tension ﬂat and
cylindrical alloy samples with constant strain rates.
The computational model was based on the theo-
retical basis of continuum damage mechanics (Zhang
and Cai 2010). Mechanical behaviour was described
by a system of conservation equations (mass, momen-
tum and energy), kinematic equation and the consti-
tutive equation. Initial and boundary conditions were
added to the system of equations. The boundary
conditions correspond to the conditions of loading of
the 3D body. The deformation of specimens working
part was simulated under axial tension with a constant
strain rate.
In performing numerical simulation of tensile tests
carried out on smooth plane and cylindrical speci-
mens, licenced software of ANSYS 14.5 software and
Autodyn were used. The calculations were carried out
by solvers using ﬁnite-difference scheme of second
order accuracy. The grid model of the samples used in
tests is shown in Fig. 1.

4 Summary and conclusions
Mechanical behavior of Zr–1% Nb and Ti–13Nb–
13Zr alloys were numerically simulated using models
taking into account the cumulative plastic strain, strain
rate and temperature within practically important
temperature range from 297 K to 1050 K.
Modiﬁed Johnson–Cook and Zerilli–Armstrong
constitutive equations were used for numerical simu-
lation of mechanical behavior of Zr–Nb and Ti–Nb
alloys with HCP and BCC phases.
It was shown that proposed modiﬁcations enable to
describe the elastic–plastic ﬂow at strain rates from
0.001 to 1000 1/s and temperatures at which alloys are
in the state of HCP lattice and at high temperatures at
which alloys exhibit BCC lattice.
The applicability of modiﬁed Zerilli–Armstrong
model is shown for the bcc ultraﬁne grained alloys Ti–
Nb at range of strain rates from 0.001 to 1000 1/s using
Ti–13Nb–13Zr with reduced elastic moduli as an
example.
The results show a possibility to receive an
adequate description of elastic–plastic behavior of
Zr–Nb and Ti–Nb by a single model under the
temperature range, which includes the hcp to bcc
phase transition.
It is shown that in order to obtain an adequate
prediction of the ﬂow stress of alloys experiencing
eutectoid phase transformations, it is necessary to use
different material parameters of Johnson–Cook
model, within temperature range of the existence of
alpha or beta phases.
At temperatures above the beta-phase transition
temperature of Ti–Nb and Zr–Nb alloys it is advisable
to use the constitutive equation Zerilli–Armstrong for
BCC crystal structure.
It was shown that the dependence of the normalized
yield strength of Zr–1% Nb from normalized temper-
ature can be approximated by a bilinear relation. The
change in slope is due to phase transition a (HCP) ?
