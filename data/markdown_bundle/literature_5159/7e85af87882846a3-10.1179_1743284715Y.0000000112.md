neutron diffraction. Cr is found to predominantly partition to the
partitioning of W to c9. Furthermore, Cr signiﬁcantly enhances the c9 volume fraction, decreases
the c/c9 lattice misﬁt and deteriorates the creep resistance. Addition of Ni to the Cr containing
alloys affects partitioning of W and Al, further decreases the lattice misﬁt and results in the
formation of irregularly shaped precipitates. Al, W and Cr tend to occupy the ‘B’ sublattice

Introduction
Superalloys are characterised by high temperature
strength, creep, oxidation and corrosion resistance, and
are the key materials in aircraft engines and industrial
gas turbines.
1 Ni base alloys are unique high
temperature materials due to their outstanding mech-
anical properties, which can be primarily ascribed to the
characteristic microstructure consisting of coherent L1
2
ordered cuboidal c9 precipitates within a disordered fcc
matrix ( c phase). Additional strengthening is achieved
by solid solution hardening of the c phase with refrac-
tory elements. In case of polycrystalline alloys,
strengthening is also contributed by the formation of
third phases such as carbides or borides located
predominantly at grain boundaries.
Conventional Co base alloys strengthened by
carbides have not been used as widely as Ni base
superalloys due to their lower strength at elevated tem-
perature. However, in 2006, Sato et al.
2 discovered
the formation of a c/c9 microstructure in the ternary
Co–Al–W system, which strongly resembles that of Ni
based superalloys and opens perspectives for further
alloy strengthening. Since then, several research groups
have attempted to develop novel c9 strengthened Co
base superalloys and investigated their properties. 3–7
In order to meet the highly demanding requirements for
high temperature applications, various alloy properties
must be tailored including resistance to oxidation and
corrosion attacks. Cr is known to be an essential
element for corrosion and oxidation resistance of Ni
base alloys.
8 As reported recently, addition of Cr up to
8 at.-% to Co–Al–W alloys signiﬁcantly reduces the
oxidation rate at 800 uC,9 but high Cr contents lead to
destabilisation of the c/c9 microstructure and formation
of additional W rich intermetallic phases. 10 However,
only very limited information exists so far on the inﬂu-
ence of Cr on the microstructure and mechanical prop-
erties of c9 strengthened Co base superalloys.
6,7,10,11
Recently, we reported on elemental partitioning in a
ternary Co–Al–W alloy and its Ti and Ta containing
modiﬁcations.
12 Here, we investigate a set of Cr and Ni
containing Co–Al–W base alloys and characterise their
microstructure, c/c9 lattice misﬁt and elemental parti-
tioning using complementary characterisation tech-
niques such as scanning electron microscopy (SEM),
transmission electron microscopy (TEM), neutron dif-
fraction and atom probe tomography (APT). The use of
APT is essential for analysing elemental partitioning as
it provides three-dimensional elemental mapping with
sub-nanometer resolution and high detection sensitivity
and enables accurate characterisation of c9 precipitates
and nanometer sized c channels. Neutron diffraction
enables investigation of larger gauge volumes compared
to conventional X-ray diffraction, providing better grain
statistics essential for coarse grained materials. Ni is
known to stabilise the c9 phase and expand its existence
region,
13 and was added to the Cr containing alloys
in an attempt to improve their microstructural stability.
We report and discuss the combined effect of these
two elements.

over the entire range of applied stresses, with two orders
of magnitude rise for the 8Cr alloy. The

Conclusions
(i) Cr addition to Co–Al–W base alloys
strongly affects the morphology of the c/c9 micro-
structure. Ni addition enables the formation of
a regular c9 arrangement in the Co–Al–W alloy but
results in an irregular morphology when added
together with Cr (9Ni8Cr and 18Ni8Cr alloys).
(ii) Both Cr and Ni reduce the c/c9 lattice misﬁt from
þ0.43% for Co–9Al–9W alloy to þ0.11% for
18Ni8Cr alloy, resulting in the formation of an
irregular microstructure.
(iii) Cr predominantly partitions to the c
matrix, while Ni shows slight partitioning to the c9
phase. Both elements strongly reduce partitioning
of W to the c9 phase and increase the c9 volume
fraction. Ni signiﬁcantly enhances the parti-
tioning of Al to the c9 phase.
(iv) The phase compositions measured with APT indi-
cate that Co and Ni share the ‘A’ sublattice in the
c9-A
3B phase, while Al, W and Cr occupy the ‘B’
sublattice.
(v) Cr reduces the creep strength in Co base
superalloys. This reduction can only be partially
compensated by the addition of Ni.
