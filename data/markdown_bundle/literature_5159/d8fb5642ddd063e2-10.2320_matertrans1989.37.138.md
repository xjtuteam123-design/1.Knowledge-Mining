China In order to design a new kind of high-temperature Fe-Cr-Ni alloy with good weldability which could be used as structural material of Iron-ore sintering furnaces and Magnesium-smelting tanks, effects of additions of carbon, nickel, and molybdenum to Fe-Cr-Ni alloy on strength at 12500C have been studied. The rupture strength at high-temperature of the alloy is mainly determined by grain-boundaries. Addition of carbon to the alloy can affect the morphology of the precipitates at grain-boundaries apparently. With increasing carbon content, the rupture strength increases significantly at first, and then begins to decrease acutely when carbon content exceeds about 0.43%. The addition of nickel increases the alloy strength until its content reach about 10%, further addition of nickel will slightly decrease the alloy strength. The effect of molybdenum on the strength is similar to that of nickel, the maximum value of the strength is obtained by the addition of 0.30% molybdenum. However, neither nickel nor molybdenum could obviously affect the microstructure of the alloy. It is well-known that nickel can promote the formation and stabilization of austenite in the alloy and thus strengthen the alloy matrix; molybdenum in the alloy can restrain carbides' solution effectively, increases the alloy recrys- tallization temperature, and thus improves the alloy strength at high-temperature. However, there is not enough researches about the effects of the additions of carbon, nickel, and molybdenum to the Fe-Cr-Ni alloy on the mechanical properties above 12000C. In order to design a new kind of low-cost high carbon Fe-Cr-Ni alloy with good weldability, which can be used as the structure material of iron-ore sintering furnaces and magnesium-smelting tanks, these effects will be studied in this paper. IV. Discussions M23C6 carbide is the main type of carbides in the tested Fe-Cr-Ni alloys in which the minimum Cr/C ratio is greater that 40.0, and the chemical composition of M23C6 is (Cr, Fe, M23C6 carbide has a complex f.c.c structure, and has some crystal orientation with austen- ite, such as and Austenite has also a f.c.c structure. Otherwise, ferrite has a b.c.c structure, and there is no special crystal orienta- tion between ferrite and M23C6 carbides. So Austenite may bind with M23C6 carbides more firmly than ferrite. When carbon content ranges from 0.30-0.50%, one hand, the solution of carbon promotes the formation and stabilization of the austenite fiercely. On the other hand, adequate amount of fine and obtuse carbides precipitates on GBs, and GBs become zigzag (see Figs. 6 and 8). The zigzag of GBs increases the resistance to the slip of dislocations(7). So the strength of GBs gets im- proved. When too much carbon was added in to the alloy, carbides begins coarsening, and a great amount of carbides with bad morphology precipitates along GBs (see Figs. 2 and 7) which can cause a large stress concen- tration, and results in the formation of micro-crevices; hence, GBs get weakened remarkably. Nickel has the same f.c.c structure as y-Fe, and can so- lute in it continuously. However, both nickel atom radius (0.124 nm) and cell lattice parameter (0.35168 nm at 250C) are smaller than those of y-Fe (0.127 nm, 0.36468 nm) at 9160C, respectively)(8), so nickel's soluting in the alloy can result in the reduction of the size of the alloy cell and its interstices, and thus the solution of carbon gets suppressed. A certain amount of nickel soluting in the alloy promotes austenite's formation and stabiliza- tion, as well as carbon's precipitation, so both the alloy matrix and GBs get strengthened. When too much nickel is added in to the alloy, which exceeds the amount that needed to stabilize austenite, austenite lattice parameter reduces too much, then the solution of carbon decreases. So the activity of carbon improves apparentely, and too much carbides precipitates out, and the binding between austenite and carbides becomes weak, so the alloy strength decreases. Molybdenum has a b.c.c structure, and its atom radius (0.140 nm) is much larger than that of y-Fe and that of nickel. Molybdenum's solution in the alloy can enlarge austenite lattice parameter, reinforce the granular matrix, and may improve the binding between the matrix and carbides by reducing their mismatch. Conclusions (1) At 12500C, the Fe-27Cr-15Ni alloy mechanical properties is mainly determinted by the alloy GBs. (2) The carbon added into the Fe-27Cr-15Ni alloy can change the alloy structure, especially change GBs apparently. When carbon content ranges from 0.30 to 0.50%, adequate amount of fine carbides precipitates out, and GBs are zigzag; further more carbon will result in the coarseness of carbides, as well as the straightening of GBs. (3) With increasing carbon content from 0.10 to 0.70%, the alloy rupture strength at 12500C increases remarkably until the carbon content reaches about 0.43%, then begins to decrease acutely. The suitable car- bon content to the alloy ranges from 0.30 to 0.50%. (4) Nickel can also influence the alloy rupture strength. As the nickel content increases, the alloy rup- ture strength increases until nickel content reaches about 10.0% and then decreases gradually. (5) Molybdenum has a similar effect on the high-tem- perature strength of the alloy to that of nickel. The alloy rupture strength shows the maximum value when the Mo content is about 0.30%.
