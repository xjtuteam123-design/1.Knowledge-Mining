ABSTRACT
Refractory multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs) exhibit significant potential for applications in aerospace, 
nuclear energy, and petrochemical industries due to their outstanding high-temperature strength, wear resis -
tance, radiation resistance, and thermal stability. However, achieving an optimal balance between strength and 
ductility through compositional design remains a critical challenge for this class of materials. In this study, a 
series Al

experimental techniques and first-principles calculations. The effects of vanadium (V) substitution for zirconium 
(Zr) on microstructural evolution, mechanical properties, and electronic structure were systematically studied to 
uncover the microscopic mechanisms by which V alters the strength-ductility balance through lattice distortion. 
Experimental results showed that all alloys maintained a single-phase BCC solid solution structure, with cold

culations confirmed that V substitution for Zr reduces lattice distortion and improves the elastic modulus of the 
alloys. The V-regulation strategy proposed in this work provides a theoretical foundation for the synergistic 
optimization of strength and ductility in RMPEAs.
1. Introduction
Refractory multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs) are promising 
candidates for high-temperature structural applications due to their 
exceptional thermal stability, wear resistance, and mechanical strength 
[ 1 , 2 ]. For example, the MoNbTaW alloy maintains compressive yield 
strengths of 1058 MPa at room temperature and 405 MPa at 1600 
◦
C. 
The MoNbTaVW alloy achieves 1246 MPa at room temperature and 477 
MPa at 1600 
◦
C, exceeding values of Inconel 718 and Haynes 230 alloys 
[ 3 ]. However, their limited room-temperature ductility has hindered 
practical implementation, as it restricts processability and formability 
under ambient conditions [ 3 , 4 ].
To address this challenge, recent studies have focused on composi -
tional design strategies that balance strength and ductility [ 5 – 8 ]. As a 
representative example, the HfNbTaTiZr alloy developed by Chen et al. 
[ 5 ] achieves a tensile yield strength of 1113 ± 48 MPa and a fracture 
elongation of 16.0 ± 1.6%. Under cyclic loading at a stress ratio (R) of 
0.1, the alloy exhibits a high-cycle fatigue strength of 497 MPa and a 
fatigue ratio of 0.44, demonstrating superior fatigue resistance 
compared to all previously reported RMPEAs and most conventional 
structural alloys [ 5 ]. Wang et al. [ 6 ] introduced trace amounts of oxygen 
(0.5 at.%) and nitrogen (1.5 at.%) into a HfNbTiZr alloy, achieving a 
remarkable enhancement of yield strength from 736.8 MPa to 1412.9 
MPa while maintaining 10% elongation. This strengthening effect

sampled using a 4 × 4 × 4 k-point grid generated by the Monkhorst-Pack 
method. Based on the optimized supercell, the electronic structure and 
elastic properties of the material were further calculated. The elastic 
constants were determined using the finite strain-stress method: for each 
independent elastic deformation mode, seven sets of linear strain per -

erties of crystalline materials [ 31 – 33 ]. Finally, the key mechanical pa -
rameters, such as the bulk modulus and shear modulus for 
polycrystalline materials, were derived from the elastic constants matrix 
C ., using the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximation [ 34 ].

Traditionally, lattice distortion has been regarded as detrimental to 
plasticity, as it impedes dislocation motion and promotes brittleness. 
However, recent studies have revealed that moderate lattice distortion, 
within an optimal range, can in fact enhance ductility by activating non- 
screw dislocation slip and promoting the operation of multiple slip 
systems [ 63 ]. In this work, although V incorporation tends to reduce the 
alloys ’ lattice distortion, its atomic size modulation effect promotes a 
more homogeneous distribution of local strain ( Fig. 13 c), thereby alle -
viating extreme stress concentrations. This facilitates the cooperative 
glide of < 111 > edge dislocations and promotes the formation of kink 
bands ( Fig. 6 ). Such kink bands serve as a localized plastic deformation 
mode that accommodates intragranular strain incompatibility and de -
lays crack initiation, thus contributing to enhanced ductility [ 63 ]. 
Therefore, in this alloy system, the substitution of Zr by V does not 
simply “ increase ” or “ decrease ” lattice distortion; rather, it optimizes 
both the magnitude and spatial uniformity of distortion, enabling a 
synergistic balance between strength and ductility.

(1) All alloys form a single-phase BCC solid solution under vacuum 
arc melting. After V substitutes for Zr, the lattice parameter de -

(2) These alloys exhibit excellent cold rolling formability (with

◦

fully eliminating the rolling texture and restoring plasticity.
(3) Tensile tests show that with increasing V content, the yield

10

the highest yield strength of 719 MPa, while maintaining a 
fracture elongation greater than 20%. Fracture surface analysis 
reveals ductile fracture morphologies, confirming the inherent 
ductility of these alloys. Theoretical calculations indicate that the 
lattice distortion and shear modulus mismatch introduced by V 
are the primary sources of solid solution strengthening.
(4) First-principles calculations show that an appropriate amount of 
V increases the elastic modulus, reduces mechanical anisotropy 
and lattice distortion, and does not significantly alter the overall 
electronic structure.
In conclusion, the Zr/V composition regulation strategy provides a 
new pathway for developing RMPEAs. Future work could further 
explore the synergistic effects of other alloying elements (such as Al and 
Ti) and the mechanical response mechanisms under dynamic loading 
conditions to expand the engineering application potential of RMPEAs.
