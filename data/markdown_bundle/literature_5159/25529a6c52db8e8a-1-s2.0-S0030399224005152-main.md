ABSTRACT 
Additive manufacturing (AM) of tantalum (Ta) and tantalum alloys is increasingly promising as the characteristic 
of AM is capable of melting metals with high melting points and shaping the materials into designed structures 
with the minimum consumption of high-cost materials. The review systematically summarizes the state of art of 
AM of Ta and Ta alloys as well as their applications in detail. Based on the global research focus, processing 
parameters, microstructural evolution, and the resultant mechanical properties received the most attention from 
the academic community. The feedstock quality, selection of processing parameters, and microstructural evo -
lution induced during the processing or post-processing have a critical influence on tailoring the mechanical 
properties of the Ta or Ta alloys fabricated by AM. It is noteworthy that the medical-related research, i.e. 
structural design, biological properties, and development of Ta alloys, also received a noticeable amount of 
research, indicating the significance in medical application. Due to the excellent biological properties and me -
chanical properties, the AM of Ta and Ta alloys have been applied to orthopedic treatment as trauma, spinal, and 
joint implants. This review systematically summarizes the research trend and highlights the real application of 
the AM of Ta and Ta alloys, underscoring the knowledge gap. 
1. Introduction 
Ta, a silver-white metal with high density and high melting point, is a 
member of the refractory metals group [1] . Due to its exceptional 
corrosion resistance, high-temperature mechanical properties, and 
excellent biocompatibility, Ta has gained widespread industrial appli -
cation in areas including chemical engineering, aerospace engineering, 
energy production, biomedical engineering, etc. [2,3] . Based on the 
consumption, the largest application field for Ta is in capacitors as Ta ’ s 
oxide film possesses a high dielectric constant and thus Ta electrolytic 
capacitors have larger capacity with smaller size and better reliability 
[4,5] . From the 1970 s to the present, the consumption by capacitors has 
accounted for more than 1/3 of the total amount of Ta [6] . In addition, 
the oxide film (Ta

) permits Ta to coexist with most acids at room 
temperature [7] , rendering it suitable to be used as a chemical reactor. 
Furthermore, pure Ta exhibits excellent biocompatibility and biological 
stability. Pure Ta is not biodegradable, hence not absorbable to the 
human body in body fluids. In addition, Ta also has a positive effect on 
cell attachment, especially osteoblasts. Therefore, Ta shows great po -
tential in the orthopedic treatment and it is becoming one of the major 
therapies [8] . Moreover, Ta has a high melting point of 2997 ℃ and its 
ability to maintain strength and ductility at high temperature makes it 
an ideal high-temperature structural material in the aerospace industry 
[9] . 
Ta is a costly metal with a high melting point, and traditional pro -
cessing techniques, such as machining and casting, often result in sig -
nificant waste and lengthy processing time, which inevitably increase 
the price of Ta products. AM, colloquially known as three-dimensional 
(3D) printing, has been utilized to process various components with its 
advantages of rapid prototyping and precise component production 
[10] . As two members of AM techniques, laser powder bed melting 
(LPBF) and electron beam powder bed fusion (EBPBF) are superbly apt

2. AM process and post-treatment of Ta alloy 
The quality of the LPBF Ta is highly dependent on the laser system 
properties, scanning parameters, and processing parameters [22] . 
Further, the quality of original materials and the post treatment process 
are core factors impacting the components ’ performances as well 
( Fig. 2 ). Laser system properties include the wave of laser (pulse, 
continuous, etc.), spot size, and laser power. Scanning parameters 
encompass the scanning strategy, scanning speed, and hatch distance. 
Processing parameters involve powder-layer thickness and preheating 
temperature. Among them, laser power, scanning speed, hatching dis -
tance, and powder-layer thickness are core variables influencing the 
components ’ qualities and properties [23 – 25] , which affect the bonding 
between molten metal powder and substrate, interlayer, as well as the 
microstructure and densification behavior of produced parts [26] . All 
changes in the above parameters affect the mechanical properties of 
LPBF components. Therefore, it is necessary to clarify the influencing 
mechanism of various parameters. 
2.1. Effect of scanning speed on density 
Scanning speed is directly related to heat accumulation, which in 
turn affects the microstructure and internal stress of the prepared sam -
ples. In general, an increase in laser scanning speed leads to a shorter 
cooling time and an increase in the temperature gradient, which in -
creases the surface tension gradient and Marangoni convection in the 
molten pool [27] . For a given laser power, increasing scanning speed 
would result in low energy density, and thereby lead to micropores 
caused by Marangoni convection and balling effect. Adversely, at low 
scanning speed and high energy density, the reasons for micropores 
generated might be attributed to spatters and metal vapors. Controlling 
scanning speed under 200 mm/s for pure Ta printing [28] . The results 
showed that the density was not high, which was below 98 % at low 
scanning speed ( Fig. 3 a) as the molten droplets splashed and there were 
micro cracks caused by thermal shrinkage. Gradually increasing the 
scanning speed, the molten droplets will be balled leading to disconti -
nuity of scanning trajectory, resulting in inter-sphere holes ( Fig. 3 b-c). 
The relative density was improved significantly with increasing scan -
ning speed ( Fig. 3 d-f) [29] . Different laser power and scanning speed 
affect the appearance of different types of pores. At low scanning speed, 
excessive heat input entails that the molten pool extends inward deeper, 
exhibiting a keyhole mode ( Fig. 3 g). Under this mode, higher strength 
and ductility were obtained, but the instability and collapse of the 
keyhole led to pore formation, resulting in the density lower than 99 %. 
At high scanning speed, the depth of molten pool was limited and 
showed semi-circular, exhibiting a conduction mode. Under the con -
duction mode, the samples were highly dense ( Fig. 3 h) [30] . To sum up, 
in order to obtain better surface quality of pure Ta and reduce the 
occurrence of splashes and balls, it is recommended to select a moderate

2.2. Effect of laser power on density 
Laser power is an assessment of the energy generated by the laser per 
unit time during the printing process. Excellent cohesion between layer 
and layer requires good bonding of the laser, raw material powders, and 
substrate. When the laser beam is irradiated onto the powder surface, a 
large amount of powders are heated and melted, forming a molten pool 
[31,32] . After the laser scans, the molten pool quickly solidifies to bind 
with the substrate. Excessive laser power may lead to splatter and tur -
bulence of the molten pool, reducing the density ( Fig. 4 a). The keyhole 
will be generated on the surface when follow-up layers collapse 
( Fig. 4 b). Conversely, if the laser power is too low, the powder may not 
completely melt and undergo balling ( Fig. 4 c), resulting in poor binding 
of the molten metal with the substrate and affecting the density 
( Fig. 4 d). To obtain good connections between the powders and sub -
strate and between layers, it is necessary to regulate appropriate laser 
power.

essential impacting factor for continuous layers. The design of the 
scanning strategy directly affects the direction and magnitude of the 
thermal gradient, thereby regulating the size of residual stress and ul -

AMed components, adopting a high-energy electron beam as the heat 
source. The differences between EBPBF and LPBF mainly exist in the 
processing process and environment. EBPBF can maintain a high vac -

complex parts compared to LPBF [46] ( Fig. 8 b). 
The output power of the electron beam in the EBPBF process is 
related to the electron beam current and voltage. The electron beam can 
reach one kilowatt or more, which can fully and quickly melt refractory 
metals. Guo et al. [47] adjusted the electron beam power between 120 
W-1440 W and found that when the power was less than 300 W, the 
sample surface quality was poor, and the molten droplets were spher -

there was excessive melting resulting in protrusions and surface 
warpage. When the power was around 600 W, the surface quality was 
satisfactory, which was consistent with the experimental results of Yang 
et al [48] . The elongation of samples prepared within this power range 
was all above 30 %. The high elongation is mainly due to the extremely 
dense samples with few defects and low oxygen content prepared under 
high energy output and high vacuum.

3. Pure Ta and Ta alloys and their additive manufacturing 
3.1. Pure Ta 
Metal powders are the feedstock materials for AM of Ta. The sphe -
ricity, laser absorptivity, fluidity, and oxygen content of the Ta powders 
will directly affect the printing process and component quality [53] . 
Thus, the influence of the physical features of powders should be iden -
tified. The metal powders used for AM are commonly spherical. The 
sphericity of the powder increases the laser absorptivity and fluidity of 
the metal powders. hence determining the forming accuracy of the 
components. 
There are mainly three preparation methods for spherical powders, 
namely spheroidization, rotating electrodes, and atomization [54] . The 
plasma spheroidization technology has the characteristics of high tem -
perature and fast cooling speed, making it suitable for preparing 
spherical Ta powders [55] . Irregular Ta powders can be prepared by 
reducing fused potassium fluocarbonate with sodium at high tempera -
ture. These irregular powders were further processed by fluidization, jet 
milling, and plasma spheroidization to increase the sphericity of the 
powders [56] . Jet milling and plasma spheroidization can significantly 
improve the fluidity of raw Ta powders. Compared with jet milling 
powders, plasma spheroidization powders had higher sphericity, rela -
tively uniform particle size, uniform oxide film, and higher laser ab -
sorptivity. The researchers further measured the tensile properties of the 
specimens processed using Ta powders with different sphericities. Under 
the same processing parameters, the low laser absorption rate of non- 
spherical powders led to a low density and a lower tensile strength of 
the prepared sample ( Fig. 10 c). The tensile strengths of printed samples 
using spherical powders were greater than 750 MPa, while those using 
non-spherical powders were only about 600 MPa. Therefore, the high 
sphericity of the powders is conducive to the improvement of the me -
chanical properties of the sample [57] . 
Similar to sphericity, the oxygen content in Ta powders also has an 
impact on the performance of the printed samples. In the AM process, 
the continuous flow of inert gas controls the oxygen content in the 
printing chamber within a certain range [58] . The testing results of 
microstructure and mechanical properties showed that by adjusting the 
oxygen content in Ta between 150 – 550 ppm, with the increase of oxy -
gen content, the solid solution of oxygen in the Ta phase increased, 
resulting in lattice distortion and promoting the increase of Vickers 
hardness and ultimate tensile strength of the Ta samples ( Fig. 10 a) [59] . 
In addition, oxygen element was constantly segregated and forming 
oxide at the grain boundary. Micro-cracks were formed in these oxides 
during the rapid solidification process, resulting in a gradual decrease in 
the ductility. The main fracture mode of the LPBF sample changed from 
the typical ductile fracture to the combined fracture mode of cleavage 
fracture and ductile fracture [56] .

The oxygen affinity of Ta powders directly limits its reusability, and 
repeated use of the powders can lead to unstable performance. Song 
et al. [60] used the same process to reuse the same batch of Ta powders 
16 times and found that the average particle size of the Ta powders 
changed from 30.1 μ m to 33.8 μ m, and the oxygen content increased 
from 310 ppm to 450 ppm ( Fig. 11 a). The prepared samples ’ density and 
tensile fracture strain continuously decreased ( Fig. 11 b), while tensile 
strength and microhardness peaked and then decreased. The changes in 
mechanical properties were attributed to oxygen solid solution 
strengthening, as well as the attachment of small powders to large 
powders, resulting in incomplete melting and formation of defects after 
an increase in powder particle size ( Fig. 10 b). Samples with low-oxygen 
content had a high elongation but insufficient tensile strength, while 
with increasing oxygen content, elongation decreased and tensile 
strength increased within a certain range of oxygen content before 
decreasing [59] . The significance of controlling the oxygen content is 
more pronounced when dealing with porous Ta as the fine lattice 
structure increases the sensitivity of the Ta to the defects. Guo et al. [61] 
studied the effect of reusing powders on printing. After repeated use for 
20 times, the compressive yield strength and platform stress of porous Ta 
decreased by approximately 20 % ( Fig. 11 c-d). Porous Ta is composed of 
periodically arranged porous support plates. The support plates had a 
fine structure and were sensitive to defects, so their mechanical prop -
erties varied greatly. However, the particle size and density of the 
powders did not change significantly. To ensure good elongation and 
comprehensive mechanical properties of printed samples, it is necessary 
to control the oxygen content of the powders and the processing 
chamber well.

small and spherical powders. However, excessively small powders e.g. 
nano-sized powders, increase the adhesion force and the decrease the 
fluidity of the melted powders. Viscous molten droplets with poor 
fluidity may not be able to spread the entire forming path, resulting in a 
lack of fusion defects and hence low relative density [62] . To summa -

variation of grain sizes with the different processing parameters, it is 
found that the grain size of the samples is relatively small with a high 
scanning speed because the high scanning speed results in the rapid 
solidification of melted powder droplets and insufficient time to grow. 
However, there are few studies on the relationship between the original 
particle size, processing parameters, and the grain size of subsequent 
samples. In addition, the increase in oxygen content will promote the 
increase of strength and deteriorate the toughness. Therefore, the oxy -
gen content and the physical states in the feedstock powder should be 
controlled and modified for the pursuit of desirable mechanical 
properties.

4.1. Additive manufacturing of porous Ta 
4.1.1. Cell proliferation and attachment 
Biomimetic structure is a decisive factor for the biocompatibility of 
the bone implants. The cancellous bone of the human body presents a 
porous trabeculae structure [91] . Designing the metal implant into a 
porous structure is beneficial for close integration with the cancellous 
bone ( Fig. 16 a, b). Porous structure with extensive surface area offers 
more location for bone cells to adhere and develop ( Fig. 16 c). Mean -
while, beneficial substances, such as nutrition and medicine, inhaled 
into in the human body can be rapidly transported through the porous 
structure to the target region, achieving the improvement of biocom -
patibility [92] . Furthermore, elastic modulus of Ta can be altered into an 
appropriate range through controlling porosity, which improves the 
mechanical compatibility of Ta ( Fig. 16 d). 
To ensure long service life and implant effectiveness, pure Ta im -
plants should possess adequate mechanical properties and have minimal 
effects on human cells [93] . Nevertheless, it is reported that the elastic 
modulus of LPBF pure Ta surpasses 100GPa, which is far beyond the 
elastic modulus of cancellous bone (0.01 – 1.57 GPa) and cortical bone 
(5 – 23 GPa) in the human body [94] . The mismatch of elastic modulus 
between human bones and Ta implants will cause stress shielding effect 
[95] . When subjected to the same stress, different strains are generated 
resulting in relative displacement from bone to bone. Implants with high 
elastic modulus are prone to deform and fracture under large strain, in 
the meantime, the human bone is subjected to small strain for a long 
time, resulting in disuse atrophy [96] . Simulating femoral prosthesis 
implantation and adjusting stress shielding at the range of 0 – 100 %, the 
analysis results showed that stress shielding of 50 % decreased the ac -
tivity of osteoblasts, and stress shielding below 25 % had no significant 
effect on cell proliferation and apoptosis [97] . Therefore, it is a must to 
significantly reduce the elastic modulus of the implant to match that of 
human bone. 
In addition, the friction of Ta with the human body also stimulates 
the growth of the tissue. Ta has a high friction coefficient and its porous 
structure would further increase the friction between porous Ta and 
bone tissue. It is reported that the friction coefficient of porous Ta is 40 
% to 75 % higher than that of traditional porous coatings, which is more 
conducive to the fixation of implants, thus improving the success rate of 
surgery [98] . Dittrick et al. [99] studied the in vitro tribological prop -
erties of Ta coating. Compared with Ti, the wear rate of Ta coating was

Table 4 includes the summarized mechanical properties of porous 
Ta-Ti and Ta-Nb alloys. Comparing the mechanical properties of porous 
Ta-Ti and Ta-Nb alloys, it can be clearly seen that the Ta-Ti alloy 
maintains good mechanical properties and an elastic modulus matching 
human bone despite high porosity. This may be due to the ultra-high 
cooling rate of LPBF, which leads to a large temperature gradient and 
degree of supercooling and in turn causes grain refinement. The fine 
grain strengthening contributes to relatively high strength in Ta-Ti al -
loys [73] . For Ta-Nb alloys, the compressive strength and elastic 
modulus show a trend of first increasing and then decreasing. The main 
reasons are as follows, on the one hand, as the Nb content increases, the 
bonding strength between particles increases, and the material ’ s ability 
to resist bending damage plays a major role. On the other hand, the 
strength of pure Nb is much lower than that of pure Ta. As the Nb 
content increases, the compressive strength of the material continues to 
decrease. Therefore, as the Nb content increases, the mechanical prop -
erties of Ta-Nb alloys decrease compared with pure Ta. Mechanical 
properties changes are the combined effects of two factors [119] . 
In general, the structural design provides an effective way to tailor 
the mechanical properties and biological properties of the AM of porous 
Ta and Ta alloys. By appropriate design of structure, the properties of the 
material itself render the prepared implants with superior biological 
properties. Therefore, Ta and Ta alloys fabricated by AM become 
promising in real applications. 
5. Application of AMed Ta 
The AM of pure Ta and Ta alloys shows excellent performance, 
including mechanical properties, high-temperature properties, and 
biological properties. Therefore, the application of pure Ta and Ta alloys 
fabricated by AM received considerable exploration. To date, most 
AMed pure Ta or Ta alloys are applied to the medical field due to their 
high customization of AM technology and excellent osseointegration of 
Ta. In contrast, the application of AMed pure Ta and Ta alloys in the 
aerospace and power industry is still in its infancy. In this section, the 
case of the application of AMed pure Ta and Ta alloys is introduced in 
detail, presenting their potential in the orthopedic treatment.

5.2. Spinal products 
The clinical cases of AMed Ta spinal products were reported later 
than those of joint implants. With the development of AMed all-Ta spinal 
implant devices, the Ta interbody fusion cage received a Class Ш Med -
ical Device License from the China National Medical Products Admin -
istration (NMPA) in January 2023 [129] . As the first domestic Ta 
orthopedic implant produced through LPBF and approved by NMPA, the 
Ta intervertebral fusion cage marks its success in productization and its 
value in medical applications. This Ta intervertebral fusion product has 
a trabecular structure and can achieve a high porosity of 68 %~78 % to 
promote the fusion of bone tissue and blood vessels. Its elastic modulus 
is equivalent to human cancellous bone, with excellent stability and 
biocompatibility ( Fig. 20 f). 
In July 2021, doctors from Xijing Hospital of the Air Force Medical 
University (China) implanted AMed Ta artificial vertebrae into the pa -
tient ’ s vertebrae defective site to promote rapid healing ( Fig. 20 g). Due 
to a tumor in the patient ’ s spine continuously pressing on the spinal 
column, a three-segmental spinal tumor posterior mass excision and 
body insertion are required to support the defect site. Compared with 
common Ti implants, Ta cones have superior ability to promote bone 
ingrowth, and their elastic modulus is more consistent with human 
bone. Therefore, the hospital finally selected Ta artificial vertebrae as 
the implant. The success of this clinical implant case confirms the per -
formance and practical feasibility of AMed all-Ta implant devices [130] . 
5.3. Trauma products 
In 2019, the AMed trabecular Ta stent developed by Xiangya Hos -
pital doctors was successfully implanted in the body of patients with 
femoral osteonecrosis, which is the first Ta stent implantation surgery in 
the world. The contact point between the top of the Ta scaffold and 
human bone is curved, which is similar to the curvature of human bone. 
There are many contact points between the scaffold and human bone 
when stressed, and the pressure is relatively average, which prevents the 
damage of the implant [131] . 
In one study, eight patients with Crowe Type I developmental hip 
dysplasia were implanted with AMed customized Ta acetabular patches, 
and the implants were guaranteed to match and have superior service 
performance through modeling and finite element analysis, respectively. 
Several months after the successful implantation, the effectiveness of the 
implantation was evaluated, and the results showed that the visual 
analog scale significantly decreased from 2.92 ± 0.79 before surgery to 
0.83 ± 0.72 after surgery, and the Harris score significantly decreased 
from 69.67 ± 4.62 before surgery to 84.25 ± 4.14 after surgery. Imaging 
showed that the acetabulum patch was in close contact with the iliac 
bone without loosening [132] . 
Nowadays, AM has accelerated the development of porous Ta im -
plants and promoted their application in the medical field due to the 
capability of fabricating customized and porous Ta implants, which 
reduce costs and retain the original function while minimizing weight. It 
can be speculated that Ta and Ta alloys can be widely applied in other 
known or unknown fields with the assistance of AM. 
6. Conclusions and prospects 
This review systematically expounds the research hotspots of AMed 
pure Ta and Ta alloys. To date, the research of AM of Ta mainly focuses 
on the process parameters, microstructure, mechanical properties, 
alloying, porous structure design, and biological properties. By 
reviewing and analyzing the research hotspot in this area, the study of 
the AM of Ta and its alloys can be summarized as follows: 
1. The mechanical properties of AMed Ta alloys are the most-concerned 
topic, greatly influenced by raw material quality, processing pa -
rameters, and post-treatment. Powders with optimal characteristics 
like sphericity, uniform particle size, and low impurities ensure good 
fluidity, high laser absorption, and superior component density. 
Choosing the right processing parameters is vital for enhancing 
mechanical properties. Additionally, heat treatment reduces residual 
stress from rapid cooling, improving toughness, and eliminating 
anisotropy. 
2. Alloying of Ta improves the properties of the Ta and thus becomes a 
major concern. Ta-W and Ta-Hf have been used in aerospace high- 
temperature components, while Ta-Nb has better dielectric proper -
ties than pure Ta, making it suitable for the preparation of Ta ca -
pacitors. Ta-Nb and Ta-Ti have also been applied in the medical field. 
3. The biomechanical properties, bone attachment, and bone ingrowth 
of different porous structures are influenced by the porous structure. 
Among the commonly used porous structures, the trabecular struc -
ture is similar to the human cancellous bone structure and thus it is 
more frequently used on the bone implants. In recent years, bionic 
structures such as the saddle-like structure and the arch bridge 
structure have also been gradually developed. 
4. At present, some of the Amed Ta and Ta alloy components have 
already been applied in the medical field. A considerable number of 
porous Ta implants including spinal, joint and trauma types have 
been implanted to facilitate the orthopedic treatment. Nonetheless, 
the application of AMed Ta alloys in other fields still has space for 
development. 
Up to now, the understanding of the AM of pure Ta and Ta alloys is 
still scarce as the metallurgical behavior of the materials during the AM 
process is still not resolved and thus hinder the further development of 
AM of pure Ta and Ta alloys. Based on the information concluded in this 
review, the outstanding knowledge gaps of the AM of pure Ta and Ta 
alloys are listed as follows: 
1. The phase transformation and the solidification behavior of the pure 
Ta or Ta alloys fabricated by AM need to be further realized with the 
aid of phase field simulation and fine element modelling. 
2. The effectiveness and safety of the porous structure still need to be 
further verified through finite element analysis simulations and an -
imal experiments to promote their ultimate application. 
3. How to regulate and control the quality of the recycled Ta powders 
through appropriate processes, decrease the content of impurity el -
ements, and improve sphericity remain to be further explored. 
4. The alloying of Ta should be further developed to ensure improved 
antibacterial properties without sacrificing the biocompatibility of 
Ta implants.
