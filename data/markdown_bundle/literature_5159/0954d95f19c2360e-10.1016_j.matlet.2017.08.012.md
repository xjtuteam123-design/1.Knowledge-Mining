ABSTRACT 
The in-situ laminated Nb/Nb 5Si3 composite was prepared by spark plasma sintering. The 
microstructures and mechanical properties of the la minated Nb/Nb5Si3 composite were investigated in 
present work. It could be found that, alternant dis tribution microstructures of Nb ss and Nb 5Si3 layers 
were observed after sintering. The Nb/Nb5Si3 composite exhibited fracture toughness of 11.2MPa·m1/2, 
and the predicted fracture toughness was estimated by the Ashby model. Moreover, the deviation of the 
experimental value from the predicted value was discussed. In addition, the compressive strength of the 
in-situ laminated Nb/Nb 5Si3 composite was measured at 1400°C and compared with that of the 
unidirectionally solidified Nb-17.5Si alloy . 
Keywords: Laminates; Microstructure; Fracture toughness; Compressive strength 
 
1. Introduction 
Due to excellent strength retention at elevated temperature [1]，Nb5Si3 intermetallic compound has 
been considered as a potential material for high-performance structural application. However, since the 
compound exhibited low fracture toughness of 1-3MPa ·m1/2 at ambient temperature [2], ductile 
niobium based solid solution (Nb ss) was brought in [3-6] to achieve a balance of high -temperature 
strength and fracture toughness. The fracture toughness of 4.5MPa·m 1/2 was reported [7] in the as-cast

In order to further improve the fracture toughness of Nb-Si alloy , the unidirectionally solidiﬁed 
alloys were prepared and exhibited fracture toughness of 10-14.5MPa·m 1/2 in certain directions [8-10]. 
Another method for fracture toughness improvement w as to prepare laminated Nb/Nb 5Si3 composites. 
Kajuch [11] fabricated Nb 5Si3/Nb/Nb5Si3 laminate with a relatively high fracture toughness of 
7.1-11.5MPa·m1/2 by hot pressing the Nb 5Si3 compacts and Nb foil. However, since the thickness of 
Nb5Si3 compacts and Nb foil were 4mm and 0.25mm, respecti vely, it was found that the fracture 
toughness changed apparently depending on the distance of notch from Nb/Nb 5Si3 interface. Thus, the 
thickness of Nb ss and Nb 5Si3 layers should be decreased. The aim of this work w as, therefore, to 
prepare the in-situ laminated Nb/Nb 5Si3 composite with micron-sized multi-layer structure by spark 
plasma sintering, evaluate the microstructure and m echanical properties of the Nb/Nb 5Si3 composite, 
and identify the toughening and strengthening mechanisms. 
2. Materials and methods 
Nb foil (99.99%, 25 µm in thickness), Nb powders (99.99%, 1-3 µm) and Si powders (99.99%, 
1-3µm) were used as the raw materials in present work. A three-step procedure was adopted in the 
current investigation. A mixture with a molar ratio of 1:1 for Nb:Si was selected to prepare the Nb/Si 
slurry by vacuum ball milling for 3h, containing do uble ethanol in volume. Then, the Nb foils were 
covered with Nb/Si slurry by dip-coating method and dried in vacuum. The Nb foils with slurry were 
stacked and wrapped by flexible graphite foil, and then sintered in a graphite mould at 1750°C under 
30MPa for 30min in vacuum, using a heating rate of 100°C/min. Finally the sintered Nb/Nb 5Si3 
composite was furnace-cooled down to room temperatu re. Each sample with a dimension of 
φ30mm× 5mm was initially prepared with about 100 pieces of Nb foils, and then sintered using the FCT

The phase and crystallinity were analyzed via X-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku D/max 2500) using 
CuKα radiation at 40 kV and 250 mA, with a step size of 0.1° and duration of 1 second in each step. 
The microstructures of samples were characterized u sing electron probe microanalysis (EPMA, 
JXA-8230), wave dispersion spectrum (WDS) and scann ing electron microscopy (SEM, Quanta-200). 
Fracture toughness was determined by three-point be nding (TPB) test. The dimension of bending test 
specimen was 2.5mm×5mm×20mm, and the notch was intr oduced perpendicular to, yet sequentially 
through the layer direction using electro-discharge machining (EDM). Fracture toughness tests were 
performed at room temperature and with a speed of 0 .1mm/min. Compression tests were conducted at 
1400°C at a strain rate of 1 × 10−3 s−1 in vacuum. The dimension of the compression test s pecimen was 
φ4mm× 6mm, and the loading direction was parallel to the layer direction. Five specimens were tested 
to obtain an average value. 
3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 1 shows the XRD pattern of prepared Nb/Nb 5Si3 composite. It is seen that the Nb/Nb 5Si3 
composite exhibited a typical XRD pattern of Nbss and α-Nb5Si3, and no other phase, i.e., NbSi2 and Si, 
could be identified in the pattern. Fig. 2 shows th e microstructure of the Nb/Nb 5Si3 composite. In the 
meantime, the average concentrations of elements for each phase were presented along with the figure. 
Alternant distribution of Nb ss and Nb 5Si3 layers could be found in the composite. Since the average 
thickness of Nbss layer were decreased from 25 µm (Nb foil) to 12.6 µm, it is obvious that the diffusion 
of Si element from the sintered layer to the Nb ss layer occurred during sintering. Furthermore, 
according to the WDS results, the oxygen content of Nbss layer is 1.3at.%. The possible mechanism of 
oxygen content on the fracture toughness will be discussed later.

The Ashby model could be used to estimate the toughening effects of aligned microstructure [12]. 
The toughness increment, ∆KC could be expressed via the following equation: 
∆KC = ( C·Vf ·E·σ0·a0)1/2 (1) 
where E (GPa), Vf, σ0 (MPa), and a0 (m) are the Young’s modulus, volume fraction, yiel d strength at 
ambient temperature, and radius of the Nbss phase, respectively . And C is a material constant represents 
the degree of constraint imposed upon a ductile particle from the brittle matrix [8]. In this investigation, 
it is clear that the Nbss phase deformed without interface decohesion (Fig. 3), and thereby the parameter 
C is taken to be 1.6 [8]. The Young’s modulus and yield strength were estimated to be 105GPa [13] and 
670MPa [14], respectively. And the volume fraction (26%) and radius (6.3 µm) of the Nb ss were 
obtained directly from the figures. Table 1 summari zes the actual composition and physical property 
datas of the laminated Nb/Nb5Si3 composite and the unidirectionally solidified Nb-17.5Si alloy [8]. For 
the laminated Nb ss/Nb5Si3 composite, the calculated toughness increment was 13.3MPa·m1/2. It is 
clearly found that, the toughness increment is high er than that experimentally determined for the 
Nbss/Nb5Si3 composite.

predicted value from the experimental value should be associated with the plasticity decreasing of Nbss. 
It was reported that, the plasticity of Nb ss decreased with the increase of oxygen content [15] . In the 
current work, due to high content of oxygen, the Nb ss exhibited lower plasticity than that in the as-cast 
Nb-Si alloys [8]. It is known that, a large amount of energy could be absorbed from the plastic 
deformation of the Nb ss. When the plasticity of Nb ss decreased, the less energy could be consumed, 
resulting in the decrease of the fracture toughness of the Nb/Nb5Si3 composite. 
In the present investigation, the laminated Nb/Nb 5Si3 composite exhibited lower fracture 
toughness than the unidirectionally solidified Nb-17.5Si alloy [8]. This could be related to two reasons. 
First, the plasticity of Nb ss was decreased owing to high content of oxygen. Second, the volume 
fraction of Nb ss was lower than that in the directionally solidifie d sample [8]. Additionally , the 
compressive strength of 380MPa was obtained for the laminated Nb/Nb 5Si3 composite at 1400°C. In 
comparison to the compressive strength (580MPa) of unidirectionally solidified Nb-17.5Si alloy at 
1400°C [8], the value of the Nb/Nb 5Si3 composite was significantly lower. This could be a ttributed to 
the different preparation methods. In the undirecti onally solidified Nb-17.5Si alloy , the growth 
direction of Nb 3Si phase was [001], which was the shortest translat ion vector of Nb 3Si and could 
provide high resistance for slip deformation on the [001] plane. Thereby, excellent compression 
strength was obtained when the loading direction wa s parallel to the growth direction of Nb 3Si [16]. 
However, in the Nb ss/Nb5Si3 composite, Nb 5Si3 grains could be observed in the Nb 5Si3 layer from the 
fracture surface (Fig. 3). Furthermore, it is seen from Fig. 1 that, no preferential orientation of Nb 5Si3 
could be found by XRD. Thus, the Nb5Si3 grains should have a random orientation in the present work. 
As a result, the resistance of Nb 5Si3 phase for slip deformation would be decreased, lea ding to the 
lowered compression strength of the composite.

4. Conclusions 
In the present study , the in-situ laminated Nb/Nb 5Si3 composite was prepared by spark plasma 
sintering. The Nb/Nb5Si3 composite showed fracture toughness of 11.2MPa·m 1/2 at room temperature. 
Due to high content of oxygen in the Nbss layer, the experimental fracture toughness was lower than the 
predicted value. Furthermore, the composite exhibit ed compressive strength of 380MPa at 1400°C, 
which was lower than that obtained in the unidirect ionally solidified Nb-17.5Si alloy . The result could 
be explained by different preparation methods of si ntered Nbss/Nb5Si3 composite and unidirectionally 
solidified Nb-17.5Si alloy .
