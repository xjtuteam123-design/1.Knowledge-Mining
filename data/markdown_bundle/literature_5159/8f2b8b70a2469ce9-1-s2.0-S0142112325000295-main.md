ABSTRACT
Ni-base superalloys used in gas turbine blades are often directionally-solidified (DS) or single-crystal (SX) to 
minimize grain boundaries and enhance creep resistance. However, their anisotropic elastic properties cause 
significant variability in mechanical response depending on the loading direction, particularly for components 
with complex geometries. Predicting thermomechanical fatigue (TMF) and creep-fatigue life across different 
crystallographic orientations is challenging due to numerous influencing parameters. This work extends a pre -
viously developed probabilistic physics-guided neural network (PPgNN), originally limited to 〈 001 〉 orientations, 
by incorporating a normalized elastic modulus as a physics-informed input to account for crystallographic 
orientation. The model further includes transverse loading in DS alloys. For the first time, a general PPgNN 
framework predicts both cycles to failure and life variance, integrating critical parameters such as strain range, 
temperature extremes, dwell durations, cyclic frequency, TMF phasing, and crystallographic orientation. Unlike 
existing empirical models, which address only a subset of these factors, the proposed approach unifies all key 
parameters within a single framework. Physics-informed feature engineering, combined with a novel loss 
function and constrained neural network architecture, enables robust generalization from sparse datasets, 
providing reliable life predictions across all orientations and test conditions.
1. Introduction
Directionally-solidified (DS) and cast single-crystal (SX) Ni-base su -
peralloys are critical materials for gas turbine components operating in 
extreme environments. These materials experience degradation due to 
thermomechanical fatigue (TMF), creep, and oxidation. Designing reli -
able components with these alloys is particularly challenging because 
fatigue and creep life depend on several interdependent factors, 
including crystallographic orientation, thermomechanical loading pa -
rameters, and dwell times at elevated temperatures. Existing empirical 
models, such as those described in Manson and Halford ’ s Fatigue and 
Durability of Metals at High Temperature [1] , address subsets of these 
factors but fail to integrate all critical parameters into a unified pre -
dictive framework.
This work builds on our prior study [2] , which introduced a proba -
bilistic physics-guided neural network (PPgNN) to predict creep-fatigue 
and TMF life for single-crystal and DS Ni-base superalloys under specific 
loading conditions, focusing on 〈 001 〉 orientations. That study demon -
strated the capability of a machine learning (ML)-based framework to 
incorporate key TMF parameters such as strain range, temperature ex -
tremes, phasing, dwell durations, and cyclic frequency into a robust 
predictive model. Importantly, the PPgNN integrated uncertainty 
quantification, allowing for the inclusion of runout data and providing 
confidence intervals alongside fatigue life predictions.
Despite this success, the prior model was limited to 〈 001 〉 orienta -
tions and did not address the influence of crystallographic orientation in 
a generalizable way. In the present work, we extend the PPgNN 
framework by incorporating a physics-informed input based on the 
normalized elastic modulus to capture orientation effects. This addition 
enables the model to predict life for a broader range of crystallographic 
orientations and transverse loading scenarios in DS alloys, significantly 
expanding its predictive scope.
The adoption of ML is critical for tackling the complexity and spar -
sity of TMF datasets. Conventional empirical models often require 
extensive and narrowly focused datasets, limiting their applicability. In 
contrast, ML leverages data-driven approaches to capture nuanced re -
lationships among multiple parameters, even in sparse datasets. By 
embedding physics-informed constraints and input features into the

1.1. Influence of crystallographic orientation
The primary reason for the significant crystallographic orientation 
dependence is associated with the large elastic anisotropy of these alloys 
[3 – 6] . The Young ’ s modulus in the 〈 001 〉 direction at room temperature 
is about 125 GPa, which is the lowest of any orientation. In 〈 011 〉 di -
rection it is 220 GPa and in the 〈 111 〉 direction, it is the greatest at 294 
GPa, over 2.3 times greater than in the 〈 001 〉 [7] . The variations in 
modulus for all orientations are shown in Fig. 1 . These differences in 
modulus with orientation are observable in the cyclic stress – strain 
response [3,8] . Under TMF, because the cycle spends more time at lower 
temperatures where the strength is greater, the macroscopic cyclic 
response is nearly elastic, much more so than observed in isothermal 
low-cycle fatigue (LCF). Consequently, the elastic stiffness is a primary 
factor in determining the TMF life when comparing different orienta -
tions [9] .
When crystallographic crack growth occurs, crystal orientation 
needs to be further considered to understand the variations in life values 
of non – 〈 001 〉 specimens. Crystal orientation influences the activation of 
slip systems. For the orientations closer to 〈 111 〉 and 〈 011 〉 , often just 
one active slip system appears to be dominant, in contrast to 〈 001 〉
loading where multiple slip systems are activated, resulting in energy 
dissipation over multiple slip systems [9] promoting planar crack 
growth [10] . Therefore, another beneficial aspect of 〈 001 〉 -oriented is 
the dissipation of additional energy through deformation on multiple 
slip systems.
While DS alloys are polycrystalline, their grain size when measured 
in the plane transverse to the grain growth direction is relatively large, 
from 0.5 to 3.0 mm typically. In addition, in the grain growth direction, 
denoted as longitudinal (L), the crystal orientation is 〈 001 〉 . Therefore, 
DS alloys are also highly elastically anisotropic with the longitudinal 
orientation having similar elastic behavior as single crystals loaded in 
the 〈 001 〉 direction. The Young ’ s modulus is lowest when loaded in the 
grain growth direction ( θ = 0
◦
), often denoted as the longitudinal- 
oriented (L-oriented) direction, and highest when oriented around 45

from this direction ( θ = 45
◦

direction ( θ = 90

mirrored in the low-cycle fatigue (LCF) life, where the lowest life occurs 
at θ = 45

Most research and testing on DS alloys have considered either L- 
oriented or T-oriented specimens. L-oriented specimens experience 
primarily transgranular cracking, while T-oriented experience inter -
granular cracking along grain boundaries and the interdendritic regions 
[12 – 14] . Therefore, in addition to the increase in Young ’ s modulus 
when loading in the T direction, the lower fatigue life compared to L 
direction has a contribution from the faster fatigue crack growth rate of 
cracks propagating intergranularly and through the interdendritic re -
gions [13] .
It is possible to correlate lives for all crystallographic orientations by 
plotting in terms of stress range and plastic strain range, since total 
strain range does not correlate the data [15] . However, from a compo -
nent design standpoint, this requires having a good viscoplastic model 
for the material to determine stress range and the plastic strain range, 
the latter of which is extremely small in most practical situations making 
life predictions based solely on plastic strain range, as is typically done 
in conventional in LCF analyses, inaccurate.
1.2. Machine learning fatigue life models for high temperature 
applications
ML models are increasingly valuable for fatigue and durability pre -
dictions, particularly for addressing the complexities of TMF and creep- 
fatigue interactions. These interactions involve multiple factors, 
including strain range, temperature, dwell times, microstructural pa -
rameters (e.g., grain size, texture), and material processing parameters 
such as chemistry.
Venkatesh and Rack [16] demonstrated that neural networks (NNs) 
outperform traditional parametric models, such as Coffin – Manson and 
hysteresis energy methods, in creep-fatigue life prediction for Ni-base 
superalloys. Their early work highlighted the potential of NNs to 
improve prediction accuracy with limited datasets. Similarly, Zhang 
et al. [17] integrated fatigue, creep, and creep-fatigue data for austenitic

generalization and accuracy than conventional ML models. Xu et al. [18]
proposed a data-driven approach for LCF life prediction of Ni-base su -
peralloys, combining alloy composition, heat treatment, and experi -

Studies on TMF prediction are fewer. Barto ˇs ´ak [19] incorporated 
mid-life hysteresis behavior into NN models to improve TMF and creep- 
fatigue life predictions for austenitic stainless steels, though the 
approach required extracting or predicting hysteresis data. Chen et al. 
[20] employed NNs to predict TMF damage in turbine engines using 
NASA ’ s gas turbine engine simulator, accounting for operational vari -
ability. Acharya et al. [2] extended a PPgNN to predict creep-fatigue and 
TMF life for DS and SX Ni-base superalloys under 〈 001 〉 loading, 
addressing sparse datasets and incorporating uncertainty quantification.
ML approaches have also been applied to capture microstructure- 
related effects in fatigue and creep predictions. Pinz et al. [21] used a 
Bayesian inference-based ML model to predict fatigue crack nucleation 
in Ren ´e 88DT superalloys, explicitly incorporating grain morphology 
and crystallography. Similarly, Liu et al. [22] employed a divide-and- 
conquer approach for creep rupture life prediction in Ni-base superal -
loys, grouping data by creep mechanisms and using multiple ML models 
to improve accuracy. This approach demonstrated the utility of ML in 
handling alloy-specific datasets. Wang et al. [23] applied a probabilistic 
NN framework for creep-fatigue life prediction in hydrogenation re -
actors, incorporating probabilistic variations to account for material 
variability. Notably, ML frameworks that integrate known physical 
constraints with data-driven approaches show significant promise in 
enhancing generalization and prediction accuracy in creep-fatigue 
modeling [24 – 26] .
Despite these advancements, no existing studies explicitly consider 
the influence of crystallographic orientation, a critical factor in the 
prediction of the fatigue and creep behavior of DS and SX Ni-base su -
peralloys. Furthermore, life prediction for these high temperature ma -
terials is inherently complex due to the interplay of factors such as cyclic 
loading parameters, temperature extremes, dwell durations, dwell 
timing within the cycle, and strain-temperature phasing. Traditional 
empirical models lack the flexibility to simultaneously incorporate these 
interacting variables, underscoring the need for advanced ML 
approaches.
1.3. Machine learning using physics-informed elements
Recent advances in ML for fatigue life prediction have incorporated 
predefined constraints based on existing physical knowledge. These 
physics-informed strategies guide the training process to ensure adher -
ence to known trends and experimental phenomena with underlying, 
often implicit, physical reasoning. By enhancing model robustness 
beyond purely data-driven approaches, these methods enable the use of 
sparse fatigue datasets while mitigating overfitting risks during training 
[27 – 30] . Additionally, they allow the inclusion of incomplete datasets in 
the training process to generate more reliable predictions than would 
otherwise be possible [28] .
Various strategies have been developed to incorporate physical 
constraints into NN models for fatigue life prediction: 
1. Pre-processing Inputs (Feature Engineering) : Feature engineering 
transforms inputs using physically known relationships before 
training. For instance, Liu et al. [22] demonstrated the benefits of 
extracting mechanical, chemical, and thermodynamic features 
derived from alloy composition and processing parameters, partic -
ularly under varying temperature conditions. By embedding physics 
into the input space, feature engineering improves model general -
ization and reduces the reliance on purely data-driven relationships 
[24,30 – 33] .
2. Physics-Informed Loss Functions: These loss functions embed 
physical constraints into the model ’ s optimization process, ensuring 
predictions adhere to known physical laws. Raissi et al. [34] pio -
neered the concept of physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), 
incorporating partial differential equations (PDEs) directly into the 
loss function to guide the learning process. PINNs are primarily 
applied to solve PDE-based problems, ensuring that model outputs 
respect underlying physical equations. This approach is well-suited 
for problems governed by deterministic physical laws and provides 
a rigorous framework for embedding physics into ML models. For 
fatigue life prediction, similar principles have been adapted, but with 
notable distinctions in implementation. Some methods encode non- 
linear partial derivatives related to known physical trends into the 
loss function [26,29,35] . These approaches ensure that the model 
respects specific behaviors, such as fatigue life variability with strain 
range. Loss functions can also enforce adherence to inadmissible 
physical violations such as negative life values [24] .
3. Architecture Shaping : Constraining NN weights and biases to 
reflect known physical relationships enhances reliability and 
generalization. Unlike PINNs, where derivatives are directly incor -
porated into the loss function to enforce physical laws, in PPgNN, 
these derivatives (i.e., trends) are mathematically encoded into 
the neural network design itself through architecture shaping [27] . 
This approach has been applied to predict creep-fatigue and TMF life 
in DS and SX Ni-base superalloys [2] , predict temperature-dependent 
strain-life curves for wrought Ti-6Al-4 V [36] , predict stress-life 
curves for metals and composites under high-cycle fatigue [27,37] , 
and predict stress-life curves for additively manufactured Ti-6Al-4 V, 
incorporating process parameters as inputs to predict the effects of 
processing on fatigue behavior [28] . Modular architectures, such as 
those used by Ciampaglia et al. [38] , split tasks into intermediate 
blocks (e.g., predicting defect characteristics or microstructural 
evolution) before linking these outputs to fatigue life predictions. 
These modular designs enable the incorporation of complex, multi- 
scale physics, bridging the gap between material properties and fa -
tigue behavior.
Probabilistic approaches further enhance physics-informed ML 
models by quantifying prediction uncertainty, which is critical for reli -
ability analysis in creep-fatigue risk assessments [23] . Probabilistic NN 
models typically output both mean life and standard deviation, which 
are vital for risk management [27,29] . Statistical loss functions tailored 
to the distributions of these outputs facilitate the inclusion of runout 
data, significantly improving fatigue life predictions at low stress and 
strain ranges. Repeatedly training the NN with randomized initial 
weights and biases also quantifies inherent model variability, further 
enhancing reliability [2,28] .
In this study, we extend physics-informed methods to thermo -
mechanical fatigue (TMF) and creep-fatigue life prediction. Feature 
engineering is introduced to capture the effect of crystallographic 
orientation on life, addressing the elastic anisotropy of SX and DS Ni- 
base superalloys. Additionally, architecture shaping enhances life pre -
dictions from sparse datasets. These advancements tackle the 
complexity and data limitations of fatigue modeling while bridging the 
gap between empirical models and fully data-driven approaches. This 
framework provides accurate, generalizable life predictions across 
diverse test conditions and orientations, demonstrating its potential to 
address the challenges in TMF and creep-fatigue life prediction of SX and 
DS Ni-base superalloys.

2.3. Input for crystallographic orientation
Defining the input for crystallographic orientation is essential. While 
the orientation vector could be represented by Miller indices ( h , k , l ) or 
Euler angles, these forms do not inherently convey the physical re -
lationships between orientation and mechanical properties, such as 
elastic moduli or yield strengths. These properties are crucial for 
informing the model and enhancing the robustness of both training and 
ML predictions.
Elastic stiffness, for example, plays a significant role in life prediction 
under strain-controlled conditions. Higher elastic stiffness leads to 
greater inelastic strain, resulting in shorter LCF life [3] . Given the sparse 
availability of fatigue life data across different crystallographic orien -
tations, incorporating this known physical relationship into the PPgNN 
is essential.

under strain-controlled conditions, increases with Young ’ s modulus in

For most of the PPgNN inputs, besides life in terms of cycles to

tation can be large. For example, in single crystal experiments oriented 
in the 〈 001 〉 , the 〈 001 〉 crystal axis in the microstructure and the uniaxial 
specimen axis can vary as much as 10

and loading axis is often not reported except stating the maximum

and used to train the model. This amount of variation can also be present

another. It would be ideal to account for this uncertainty in the input, 
though usually not possible since this information is typically not re -

4. Conclusions
This paper presents a modification to the PPgNN model [23] for 
predicting fatigue life under TMF condition in SX and DS Ni-based su -
peralloys that captures the influence of crystallographic orientation and 
solidification direction, respectively. This paper further expands on the 
trends and predictions made by the improved model.
By using an elastic compliance ratio as an input, the model robustly 
predicts the influence of crystallographic orientation under both LCF 
and TMF conditions. The model predicts that 〈 001 〉 orientation has the 
longest fatigue life while 〈 111 〉 orientation has the shortest, which is 
consistent with the inverse relationship between fatigue life and elastic 
modulus. Additionally, the model successfully captures TMF trends 
across different crystallographic orientations and under both IP and OP 
conditions with or without dwells.
This paper ultimately highlights the potential for advancements 
made to machine learning models to capturing and generalizing 
complex relationships for fatigue life predictions when data is expensive 
to obtain and sparse in nature. The PPgNN model can serve as a powerful 
tool for optimal design and maintenance of critical turbine components 
that undergo TMF conditions.
