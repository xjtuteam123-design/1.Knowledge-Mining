ABSTRACT 
Tensile tests of VCoNi medium-entropy alloy with different grain sizes were conducted at 77 K, 194 K and 293 K. 
The results show that the grain boundary strengthening ability characterized by the Hall-Petch slope of the alloy 
increases with decreasing temperature and is evidently higher than that of FCC pure metals, Cu
–
Al alloys, and 
some common FCC high-entropy alloys/medium-entropy alloys at room temperature. A new model correlated to 
intrinsic stacking fault energy and lattice friction stress is derived to describe the high grain boundary 
strengthening ability. The high grain boundary strengthening ability and its temperature dependence of the 
VCoNi medium-entropy alloy are mainly attributed to the high lattice friction stress of the VCoNi alloy. The 
findings provide a possibility for designing the single-phase FCC medium-entropy alloy with ultra-high yield 
strength and considerable plasticity. 
1. Introduction 
A novel category of alloys containing multi-principal elements 
named high-entropy alloy (HEA) has been proposed by Yeh et al. [ 1 ] and 
Cantor et al. [ 2 ] to expand the composition space of alloy design. Among 
the developed HEAs, single-phase FCC solid solution alloys exhibit high 
ultimate tensile strength, sufficient ductility and outstanding fracture 
toughness at room temperature and cryogenic temperature [ 3 – 10 ]. 
CrMnFeCoNi HEA has excellent damage tolerance with a tensile strength 
exceeding 1 GPa, ductility of about 70% and fracture toughness above 
200 MPa ⋅ m
1/2 
at 77 K [ 8 ]. CoCrNi medium-entropy alloy (MEA) also 
exhibits exceptional strength-toughness properties at 77 K with tensile 
strengths of 1.3 GPa, failure strains up to 90% and fracture toughness of 
275 MPa ⋅ m
1/2 
[ 9 ]. The equiatomic VCoNi MEA with a single-phase FCC 
structure possesses excellent mechanical properties at room temperature 
with a tensile strength of 1359 MPa and good ductility of 38% due to the 
severe lattice distortion and slip-band refinement during deformation 
[ 10 ], and the VCoNi MEA exhibits better strength-ductility combination 
at cryogenic temperature than that at room temperature [ 11 – 13 ]. In 
addition, VCoNi MEA possesses high resistance to hydrogen embrittle -
ment and corrosion at 300 K due to the modest internal micro -
mechanical contrast of single FCC structure, formation of nanotwins 
impeded crack propagation, its low hydrogen diffusivity and formation 
of the dense oxide film [ 14 ]. The outstanding mechanical properties and 
functional performance make the single-phase FCC VCoNi a candidate 
for structural applications in harsh environments. However, the yield 
strength of FCC structural alloys is usually relatively low due to their 
essential characteristics. The introduction of various defects, such as 
dislocations and grain boundaries (GBs), into the FCC alloys containing 
multi-principal elements, can effectively strengthen the alloys. High- 
density dislocations introduced by cold-working in alloys will lead to 
a dramatic decrease in ductility, while the introduction of GBs can 
strengthen the alloy without a severe loss of ductility.

strong temperature dependence. A new model is derived to describe the 
GB strengthening ability and the basic physical mechanism is analyzed 
to guide the design of high-strength single-phase FCC HEAs/MEAs. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Material 
Rectangular ingots (100 mm × 25 mm × 12 mm) of the equiatomic 
VCoNi MEA were prepared by vacuum induction melting the elements 
V, Co and Ni with high purity ( > 99.95%) under Ar atmosphere to pre -
vent oxidation and evaporation of the elements. After all melted ele -
ments were mixed evenly, the high-temperature melt dropped cast into a 
rectangular steel mold (100 mm × 25 mm × 15 mm) and cooled to room 
temperature. The cast ingots were sealed in evacuated quartz tubes and 
homogenized at 1473 K for 24 h, followed by water quenching to room 
temperature to ensure chemical homogeneity of the ingots. Blocks with 
dimensions of 100 mm × 8 mm × 8 mm were obtained by cutting the 
homogeneous ingot using electric discharge machining (EDM) to 
remove casting defects. Subsequently, the blocks were cold-rolled with 
an 87% reduction in thickness to break the coarse grains and provide the 
necessary driving force for recrystallization.

3.1. Microstructures of recrystallized alloy 
The actual chemical composition of our VCoNi MEA determined by 
EDS is consistent with the equiatomic composition, as shown in Table 1 . 
Fig. 2 shows the backscattered electron SEM (BSE) images of VCoNi 
MEA after cold rolling and recrystallization at different temperatures. It 
can be seen from the BSE images that all alloys recrystallized at different 
temperatures are all composed of equiaxed grains and contain annealing 
twins within the grains. The average grain size ( d ) of VCoNi MEAs 
recrystallized at 1173, 1223, 1273, 1373 and 1423 K determined by 
linear intercept method, as shown in Fig. 2 (b), are 3.0 ± 0.3 μ m, 11.1 ±
1.3 μ m, 19.3 ± 2.2 μ m, 41.7 ± 3.9 μ m and 138.2 ± 15.2 μ m, 
respectively. 
Fig. 3 shows the EBSD orientation maps of the plane perpendicular to 
the ND and inverse pole figures (IPFs) parallel to the ND and RD of the 
recrystallized MEAs with different grain sizes. In the EBSD orientation 
maps, equiaxed grains containing annealing twins can be observed, 
which is consistent with SEM results. The IPFs suggest that the texture is 
almost random with maximum pole densities of 1.80, 1.70, 1.52, 1.79 
and 1.63 times random for alloys with d = 3.0 μ m, 11.1 μ m, 19.3 μ m, 
41.7 μ m and 138.2 μ m, respectively. The weak texture of the recrys -
tallized specimens is a typical characteristic of recrystallization in FCC 
alloys with low or medium intrinsic SFE [ 34 – 37 ], and the weak texture 
with a maximum pole density smaller than 3.0 times random does not 
affect yield strength [ 29 ]. Thus, the effect of the weak texture on me -
chanical properties and the H
–

VCoNi MEA can be ignored. 
The microstructure of the longitudinal section of the specimens rol -
led and recrystallized at 1173 K (the lowest temperature) for 30 min was 
investigated due to the risk that there may be a temperature gradient in 
the sheet and that the temperature in the center of the sheet is not high 
enough to achieve full recrystallization. The microstructure near the 
surface of the specimen is the same as that at the center of the specimen, 
both of which are fully recrystallized structures, as shown in Fig. 4 . And 
the average grain size at the center of the sample determined by the 
linear intercept method (3.0 ± 0.4 μ m) is almost the same as that of the 
surface (3.0 ± 0.3 μ m), which indicates that the specimen rolled and 
recrystallized at 1173 K for 30 min is fully recrystallized, and the ob -
tained average grain size is reliable. 
Fig. 5 (a) shows the low magnification TEM bright-field images of 
VCoNi MEA with d = 3.0 μ m after recrystallization at 1173 K for 0.5 h. 
Besides the GB and annealing twin boundaries, there are few disloca -
tions in the grains and no dislocation substructure, which is a typical 
feature of recrystallized alloys. Fig. 5 (b) shows a high magnification 
TEM bright-field image and corresponding selected area diffraction 
patterns of the [011]-zone axis. There is no additional diffraction spot 
other than that of FCC lattice can be observed, indicating no precipitates 
appeared in the specimen recrystallized at 1173 K. Combining the XRD 
results in our previous study [ 12 ], it can be determined that the VCoNi 
MEA recrystallized at 1173 K for 0.5 h are fully recrystallized single- 
phase FCC structure, which is consistent with previous studies 
[ 10 – 12 , 38 – 40 ].
