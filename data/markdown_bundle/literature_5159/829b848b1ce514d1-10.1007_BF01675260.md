Evolution of Oxides on Ni-Base ODS Superalloys 
H.-H. Angermann,*f K. Nishi, Y. Aono, M. Inagaki, and H. Kodama 
Received February l, 1996; revised November 18, 1996 
Mechanically-alloyed Ni-base ODS superalloys currently recdve attention for 
application in future gas turbines because they exhibit outstanding mechanical 
properties at high temperatures. They also protect themselves against oxidation 
by forming chromia and/or alumina scales. However, little attention has been 
given so far to the initial stages of oxidation of Ni-base ODS superalloys, 
containing Cr as well as Al in considerable amounts. In the current work, 
oxidation at 1150 °C was studied for different holding times up to 200 hr for 
the commercial materials MA6000 and PM3030 which contain Al in increasing 
amounts. MA754, a chromia former, was investigated as a reference. It was 
shown that for the potential alumina formers MA6000 and PM3030 the conti- 
nuity and therefore protectiveness of the secondary Al203 at later stages is 
indeed determined by the initial stages of oxidation. 
KEY WORDS: Ni-base ODS superalloys; oxidation; initial stages of oxidation; alumina; 
chromia. 
INTRODUCTION 
Mechanically-alloyed, oxide-dispersion-strengthened (ODS) alloys offer 
good creep strength as well as corrosion resistance over a wide temperature 
range, but especially at high temperatures. The latter is due to the presence 
of finely dispersed very small yttria particles, introduced by the mechanical 
alloying process. These alloys are candidate materials for the hottest parts 
of gas turbines. There they may serve, depending on the service conditions 
of the gas turbine, as first stage turbine blades, combustion liners, etc. 
Materials for the strongly exposed parts of gas turbines are often coated 
in order to prolong lifetime with respect to hot corrosion, oxidation resistance,

and creep. However, the coatings can spall off, and then the substrate mat-

the other hand, it is the intention to increase the inlet temperature of gas 
turbines in order to futher increase their efficiency. When the temperature 
of the material is rising above about 950°C oxidation prevails with respect 
to hot corrosion. 
Most of the research work on oxidation of ODS superalloys so far has 
been done on Fe-base alloys, mostly MA956, and Ni-base material of the 
type MA754. The reason may be that these materials are model systems for 
oxidation because they form exclusively A1203 or Cr203, respectively, even 
in the initial stages. The effect of the Y203-dispersions is a considerable 
reduction of the outward diffusion flux of the metallic cations through the 
oxide scale. 1'2 The oxide growth proceeds nearly exclusively by inward trans- 
port of oxygen through oxide grain boundaries 3 which causes a strong retard- 
ation of the oxide growth with respect to non-ODS superalloys. While the 
growth kinetics are believed to be subparabolic for MA956, 3 for MA754 
they are stated to be parabolic I or remain undetermined. 3 In addition to 
reduced growth rates ODS-alloys also show improved scale adherence, better 
selective oxidation and a finer oxide grain size. These phenomena were traced 
back 3 to the altered-oxide-growth mechanism. 
In the present work, the isothermal oxidation of commercial Ni-base 
ODS superalloys has been investigated at the high temperature of 1150°C 
with view to future generations of gas turbines. MA 754 with its low alumin- 
ium content has been selected as a chromia former as well as MA6000 and 
PM3030 which also contain aluminium in increasing amounts in addition 
to the high amount of Cr. The latter alloys possess higher strength in the 
medium temperature regime than MA754 because of the strengthening effect 
by the )"-precipitations. With respect to oxidation they are possible chromia 
and/or alumina formers. The purpose of this work was to explore the evolu- 
tion of the oxides on Ni-base ODS superalloys primarily as a function of 
their aluminium content.

Fifty hours at 1150°C caused a considerable weight loss of MA754 (Fig. 
lb). This is due to (i) partial spallation of the chromia scale and (ii) oxidation 
of Cr203 into volatile CrO3. 
The flat region in the top of Fig. 2g, designated by no. 2, is a spalled 
area. It is composed of Cr203 with small additions of dissolved Ti (EDX; 
Fig. 2g'). The chromia spalls off partially, i.e., parallel to the matrix, but 
the matrix is not exposed. The large knots of chromia in Fig. 2g, designated 
by no. 1, are composed of many small units which is interpreted as an effect 
of the oxidation of Cr203 into volatile CrO3. Due to the selective loss of 
Cr, Ti is considerably enriched there (EDX; Fig. 2g"). 
A metallographic cross section reveals that the average thickness of the 
Cr203 layer has increased to an average value of about 5/~m (Fig. 2h) 
although the weight of MA754 decreased. The weight decrease by loss of 
chromium oxide through spallation and volatilization obviously overcom- 
pensates the weight gain through further oxidation. Furthermore, the layer 
is full of voids and other defects. 
Between 50 and 200 hr there is considerable further weight loss of 
MA754 (Fig. lb), although the surface area, where spallation had occured, 
did not increase much (Fig. 2i; 1 : chromia-knot layer; 2: partially spalled 
chromia layer). However, using the latter observation may lead to mistakes, 
because the flat Cr203-1ayer, created by spallation subsequently oxidizes to 
volatile CrO3. This will roughen up the surface, so that after a certain time 
it is not apparent that there had been spallation at this location. The thick- 
ness of the chromia layer varies strongly between 1 and 12/Ira with an 
average value of about 5/~m (Fig. 2j). 
MA6000 
After heating to 1150°C the surface of MA6000 is completely covered 
by Cr203 with two different morphologies: (i) Cr203 nodules lined up along 
the grinding marks (no. 3 in Fig. 3a) and (ii) chromia knots in between the 
lines of nodules (no. 1 in Fig. 3a). The chromia contains a rather high 
amount of Ti (EDX, Fig. 3a'), probably because of the relatively high-Ti 
content of this alloy. The Au peak stems from the coating for SEM. No 
TiO2 was found by XRD (Fig. 3a"). 
There is very little spallation so far. A metallographic cross-section 
(Fig. 3b) shows that there is internal formation of Al-rich particles down 
to a depth of about 7/lm (no. 4 in Fig. 3b; EDX: Fig. 3b'). This phase can 
be identified in the later stages of oxidation as A1203. The internally-formed 
A120~ is designated in PM3030 (see later) as secondary A1203. The spots, 
homogeneously distributed in lower density further in the interior of 
MA6000 (Fig. 3b), are sometimes rich in A1, Ni, and Cr, sometimes rich in 
Ti and were present in the unoxidized material.

One hour at 1150°C causes spallation of Cr203 knots and nodules

has been exposed by spallation of the initial chromia (no. 5 in Fig. 3c), (ii) 
remaining Cr203 nodules (no. 3), (iii) layer of chromia knots (no. 1.), and 
(iv) a flat chromia layer (no. 2). 
The hollow chromia nodules have mostly spalled off. A portion of such 
a nodule can be seen in the top left quarter of Fig. 3c. Underneath the 
chromia nodules a layer of chromia knots can be seen. The chromia now 
contains a considerable amount of Ti (EDX; Fig. 3c'). 
From the SEM pictures it can be supposed that the flat chromia layer 
(right and top middle of Fig. 3c) is created by partial spallation of the initial 
chromia, i.e., parallel to the matrix, but not down to the matrix. Such a 
chromia layer contains a relatively small amount of Ti (EDX; Fig. 3c")~ 
The internal secondary A1203-particles have grown together now to a 
subscale about 1 ~m thick (no. 6 in Fig. 3d), separated from the chromia 
topscale (no. 2) by a stripe of matrix about 1/~m thick (no. 7) (EDX; Fig. 
3d', Fig. 3d", and Fig. 3d'", respectively). In these cross-sectional pictures 
the chromia and the matrix also exhibits some silicon, although according 
to the nominal composition this element should not be present. 
After 5 hr the surface of MA6000 is composed of three different layers 
(Fig. 3e): (i) 75% is an area rich in Cr (no. 7 in Fig. 3e), (ii) remnants of 
the chromia-knot layer (no. 2), and (iii) singular spots of secondary A1203 
(no. 6). 
Sometimes area (i) is rich in Ni and A1, sometimes rich in Cr. The Cr- 
rich areas are created by spalling of the chromia parallel to the matrix but 
not down to the matrix. The Al-rich areas are created by spallation of the 
chromia down to the matrix. These matrix areas should be oxidized already 
again. 
The few spots of secondary A1203 are exposed by early spallation of 
the thin oxidized matrix layer. The thickness of the secondary A1203 hardly 
changed after one hour (Fig. 3f). 
At 50 hr holding time the surface of MA6000 was covered with two 
different areas (Fig. 3g): (i) the oxidized intermediate matrix layer (about 
75% of the surface; no. 7 in Fig. 3g) and (ii) the secondary AlaO3 (no. 6). 
Layer (i) was exposed by spallation of the chromia which was completed 
now. However, this layer, initially 1 to 2/~m thick, has grown now to a 
thickness of about 2 to 4/~m (no. 7 in Fig. 3h). Correspondingly, the weight 
of MA6000 increased between 5 and 50 hr (Fig. la). The thickness of the 
secondary A1203 is now about 2/~m (no. 6 in Fig. 3h).

PM3030 
After heating to 1150°C nodules containing high Cr formed along the grind- 
ing stripes (no. 3 in Fig. 4a and Fig. 4b; EDX, Fig. 4a). It can be assumed 
that these nodules are composed of chromia although no Cr203 peak was

After one hour the surface of PM3030 was composed of three areas: 
(i) surface covered by convoluted Cr203 nodules (no. 3 in Fig. 4c), (ii) 
primary A1203 covered surface (no. 8), and (iii) surface from which the

much larger than for 0 hr holding time at the final temperature. An estima-

DISCUSSION 
MA754 
This alloy is a chromia former because the amount of aluminium (0.2 wt.%) 
is much too small for A1203 formation. Actually, no A1 could be detected 
in any of the chromia-layers on MA754 by EDX. Also, no Ni-containing 
oxides were detected in either the early stages of oxidation or the later stages. 
This is due to the well-known effect 3 that the presence of Y203 dispersions 
improves the selective oxidation. 
The surface and the cross-section pictures (Fig. 2a-Fig. 2j) together 
with the weight change curve (Fig. la and Fig. lb) indicate an unperturbed

PM3030

SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION 
The current paper presents observations concerning the isothermal oxidation 
of the Ni-base ODS superalloys MA754, MA6000, and PM3030. Owing to a 
low Al-content MA754 is a chromia former, whereas MA6000 and PM3030, 
which contain higher-A1 concentrations, may potentially form alumina as a 
protective layer. A high temperature, HS0°C, was chosen for the oxidation 
experiments in view of the demand for ever increasing application tempera- 
ture of materials, e.g., in gas turbines. 
The growth of Cr203 on MA754 proceeds with a parabolic rate up to 
at least 5 hr; then spallation begins and chromia volatilization influences 
sensibly the sample weight. Interestingly, the spallation does not occur down 
to the matrix but leaves some Cr203 on the alloys surface. This is due to 
pegging of the chromia, an effect which is desired with respect to oxidation 
resistance. 
MA6000 initially forms a continuous chromia scale composed of chro- 
mia knots and nodules. Secondary alumina is created by internal oxidation 
below the chromia, but well seperated from it by a thin Ni-matrix layer. 
After spallation of the chromia and the Ni-matrix layer the secondary alu- 
mina forms a continuous outer scale and protects against fast oxidation. 
PM3030 has a higher A1 concentration than MA6000 and forms in 
addition to chromia (only nodules) also primary alumina on its surface 
during the first stages of oxidation. Internally, A1203 builds again below the 
initial chromia, but does not form below the primary A1203. Therefore, after 
200 hr at 1150°C in air the surface of PM3030 is composed of secondary 
A1203 and spots where the matrix is exposed through spallation of primary 
and secondary A1203 and no (not yet) reformation. 
From the present results it is clear that at the high temperature of 
1150°C the initial stages of oxidation determine the oxidation at later stages 
(at least up to 200 hr holding time) for Ni-base ODS superalloys with sig- 
nificant concentrations of Cr and A1. Formation of primary A1203 like for 
the high M-containing PM3030-alloy is not favorable for a continuous and 
therefore, protective scale of secondary A1203 for times up to 200 hr. The 
reason is that the reformation of A1203 on Ni-base ODS superalloys is, in 
contrast to Fe-base ODS superalloys, not easy due to a low diffusion 
coefficient of A1 in Ni. 
At higher or lower temperature the thermally activated processes which 
cause the oxidation formation and spallation will be accelerated or retarded, 
respectively. Because these processes generally have different activation 
energies their relative significance shifts to some extent. Hence, the evolution 
of the oxides may exhibit some differences at different temperatures. The 
basic phenomena, however, are expected to remain the same.
