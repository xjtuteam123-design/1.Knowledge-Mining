ABSTRACT 
WMoCu alloys doped with Mo particles in the nanoscale or microscale range were prepared by infiltration 
process using the element Ni as an activating element. A comparative study was carried out on the effects of 
doping with nano- and micro-scale Mo particles on the microstructure and mechanical properties of WMoCu 
alloys. The results show that the microstructure and distribution of Mo particles is more uniform in the 
W60Mo25Cu15(M) alloy doped with 3 μ m sized Mo particles. The addition of the activating element Ni promotes 
mutual diffusion between W, Mo, and Cu. A WMo transition layer is formed at the interface between W and Cu, 
and a core-shell structure with a thickness of about 2 μ m is formed around the W particles, which has an 
amorphous structure at the interfaces, which increases the deformation resistance during interfacial sliding and 
improves the bonding strength of the WMoCu alloy. The tensile strength of the W60Mo25Cu15(M) alloy is higher 
than that of the W60Mo25Cu15(N) alloy and the WCu alloy both at room temperature and at elevated tem -
perature. Especially at a high temperature of 600 
◦
C, the tensile strength of the alloys W60Mo25Cu15(M) and 
W60Mo25Cu15(N) is increased by 57.44% and 32.64%, respectively, compared to the alloy W85Cu15. The 
fracture mechanisms of the WMoCu alloy are mainly intergranular fracture and transgranular fracture. 
1. Introduction 
WCu alloys with high W content are widely used in the fields of high- 
voltage electrical contacts, arc extinguishers for electromagnetic rail -
road guns and nozzles for rocket engines, etc. due to their good electrical 
conductivity, high hardness, and arc resistance [ 1 – 6 ]. However, the 
working conditions in the above fields are becoming increasingly harsh, 
and it is difficult to meet the requirements of good arc erosion resistance 
and high strength simply by adjusting the preparation process or ma -
terial composition of WCu alloy [ 7 – 9 ]. Therefore, how to further 
improve the high-temperature strength and ablation resistance of WCu 
alloys has become one of the most important topics in WCu materials 
research [ 3 , 10 – 12 ]. 
In order to improve the high-temperature mechanical properties of 
WCu alloys, various methods have been used in recent years to control 
the microstructure and overall density of the materials, such as me -
chanical alloying by co-reduction, chemical co-deposition and spark 
plasma sintering (SPS) [ 13 – 16 ]. However, the infiltration sintering 
method is the only commercial production method when the W content 
exceeds 85 wt% [ 17 ]. To improve the sinterability of WCu alloy during 
the infiltration process, various options have been investigated, such as 
reducing the particle size [ 18 ], and adding transition elements like Ni, 
Zr, Cr, Co and Fe [ 19 , 20 ]. The particle size of mixed powders has no 
influence on the phase type, but it significantly affects the compaction, 
microstructure and properties. These transition elements can improve 
the wetting of tungsten particles by liquid copper and thus increase the 
infiltration efficiency. For example, Zhang [ 21 ] found that the alloy 
W75Cu25 with 400 nm-sized W powders exhibited excellent homoge -
neity for both sintered W powders and grains, together with the highest 
relative density of 98.9%. Cao [ 22 ] studied the wear resistance of 
coarse-grained W
–
Cu composite (grain size of W phase 5 μ m) and 
nanostructured W-Cu-Cr composites (grain size of W phase 46 nm), the 
results showed that the wear resistance of the W-Cu-6Cr composite was 
an order of magnitude higher than that of the coarse-grained WCu 
composite. Wang [ 23 ] studied the influence of Ni additions on the 
microstructure and tensile property of W
–
Cu composites, and found

studies about the particle ’ s characteristics and mechanical properties of

mechanical properties of WMoCu alloys at different temperatures are 
especially unclear. Therefore, a detailed investigation of the mechanical 
properties and failure mechanisms of WMoCu alloys is urgently 
required. 
The present study aims to explore effects of Mo particle size on the 
mechanical properties of WMoCu alloys at room temperature and 
elevated temperatures. For this purpose, WMoCu alloys doped with 
nanoscale or microscale Mo particles were prepared by the infiltration 
method using Ni as an activating element. A comparative study was 
carried out on the effects of doping with nanoscale and microscale Mo 
particles on the microstructure and mechanical properties of WMoCu 
alloys. The mechanism of WMo solid solution or core-shell structure 
formation at the W/Cu interface was analyzed. The influence of nano -
scale and microscale Mo particles on the mechanical properties of the 
WMoCu alloy at high temperature was investigated. The underlying 
strengthening mechanisms of the WMoCu alloy doped with nano- or 
micro-scale Mo particles were discussed and analyzed. The results can 
serve as a theoretical reference for the material design and potential 
applications of high-performance WCu alloys in electrical, aerospace, 
military, and energy industries. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Raw materials 
The raw materials are W powder (purity 99.9 wt%, average particle 
size 6 μ m and 20 μ m, Xiamen Golden Egret Special Alloy Co. Ltd.), Mo 
powder (purity 99.9 wt%, average particle size 80 nm and 3 μ m, Xiamen 
Golden Egret Special Alloy Co. Ltd.), Ni powder (purity 99.9 wt%, 
average particle size 1 μ m, Shanghai Bu Wei applied Material Tech -
nology Co., LTD), and the oxygen-free Cu plate was used as the source 
for the infiltration process. The composition of WMoCu alloy is shown in 
Table 1 . In addition, two different sized W powders with a mass ratio of 
1:1 were used to obtain a high density of WMoCu alloy [ 30 ]. 
2.2. Preparation of WMoCu alloy 
An infiltration process was used to fabricate the WMoCu alloy. 
Firstly, the W powder, Mo powder and Ni powder were mixed in a home- 
made powder mixer according to the composition in Table 1 and milled 
for 6 h at 60 rpm with the ball-to-powder ratio of 5:1. Secondly, the 
mixed powders were pressed into the W
–

W

placed on the pre-sintered W
–
Mo skeleton, and the infiltration process 
was carried out at 1350 
◦
C for 4 h in the hydrogen atmosphere.

alloy ’ s resistance to deformation and contributes significantly to the 
overall improvement of its mechanical properties.

4. Conclusions 
(1) Compared with nanoscale Mo particle doping, there is a more 
uniform microstructure and Mo particles ’ distribution in 
W60Mo25Cu15(M) alloy doped with micron-scale Mo particles. 
At the interface between W and Cu, a WMo transition layer is 
formed. The formation of a core-shell structure with an approx -
imate thickness of 2 μ m around the W particles, characterized by 
an amorphous structure, enhances the alloy ’ s resistance defor -
mation during the interface sliding and enhances its bonding 
strength. 
(2) The addition of activation element Ni promotes the mutual 
diffusion between W, Mo, and Cu. During the infiltration process, 
the CuNi(L) liquid phase serves as a bridge to connect the diffu -
sion channel between the W and Mo phases, prompting W and Mo 
atoms to diffuse into the adjacent Mo and W phases through grain 
boundary diffusion and bulk diffusion to form a solid solution. 
(3) The tensile strength of WMoCu(M) alloy surpasses that of 
W60Mo25Cu15(N) alloy and WCu alloy, regardless of room 
temperature or 600 
◦
C. Particularly at 600 
◦
C, the tensile strength 
of WMoCu(M) alloy and W60Mo25Cu15(N) alloy increase by 
57.44% and 32.64%, respectively, compared to WCu alloy. This is 
attributed to the high thermal stability inherent to the WMo solid 
solution, which predominantly determines the high-temperature 
strength of the alloy.
