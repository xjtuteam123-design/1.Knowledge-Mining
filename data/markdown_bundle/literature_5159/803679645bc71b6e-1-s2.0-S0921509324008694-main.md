ABSTRACT 
The further application exploration of TZM alloys have been hindered by the limited strength especially at high 
temperatures. Herein, we propose a strategy to enhance the mechanical properties of TZM alloys by introducing 
heterostructures through Y
2
O
3 
doping combined with rotary forging (RF). The particle stimulated nucleation 
(PSN) mechanism promotes fine grains (FGs) nucleate close to the micron-sized second phase particles. The 
mechanical properties of Y
2
O
3
-TZM (YTZM) alloy are improved through the synergistic strengthening influence 
of back stress strengthening and second phase strengthening. The ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of YTZM at 
room temperature and 1000 
◦
C is 820 MPa and 433 MPa, respectively, which are about 15% and 51% higher 
than that of TZM. In addition, the FCC-oxides generated by adding Y
2
O
3 
can trigger twinning, stacking faults, and 
9R structures during high-temperature deformation, and the long-period stacked ordered phase (LPSO) nucleates 
at the stacking faults and twin boundaries through the 9R structures, which is more conducive to alleviate the 
stress concentration at the oxide/matrix interface and improve the dislocation blocking ability. The present 
strategy provides a paradigm for the development of high-performance TZM alloys through creating 
heterostructures. 
1. Introduction 
Emerging fusion reactor applications and reusable hot-working 
moulds require high-temperature materials with exceptional mechani -
cal properties under extreme conditions [ 1 , 2 ]. The TZM alloy, which 
includes 0.5 wt% Ti, 0.06 – 0.12 wt% Zr, and 0.01 – 0.04 wt% C, has 
emerged as the preferred material for high-temperature hot-working 
moulds due to its exceptional high-temperature strength [ 3 – 5 ], small 
thermal expansion coefficient, and good creep resistance ability [ 6 – 8 ]. 
Grain refinement and second phase precipitation via thermomechanical 
processing can increase the strength of TZM alloys through confine -
ments of the dislocation migration. However, the limited strength 
especially at high temperatures limits the further exploration of appli -
cations. Therefore, it remains a tremendous challenge for TZM alloys to 
enhance the mechanical properties [ 9 ]. 
Generally, the mechanical performances of Mo alloys are signifi -
cantly affected by the dispersion strengthening of the ceramic phase 
particles. On one hand, the phase boundaries (PBs) between the second 
phase/matrix exhibit remarkable thermal stability, which can increase 
the deformation resistance ability at high temperatures [ 10 ]. On the 
other hand, carbides and rare earth oxides act as effective O-absorbers 
and suppress grain boundary (GB) embrittlement [ 11 ]. For instance, 
nano-ZrC dispersion-strengthened Mo alloys exhibited a significantly 
increased UTS of ~928 MPa at room temperature, which was greater 
than that of pure Mo (UTS~493 MPa, elongation~33.8%), while 
maintaining a superior ductility of ~34.4% [ 12 ]. NS Mo prepared by 
liquid-liquid mixing, consisting of ultrafine grains (d ≤ 0.5 μ m) and 
dispersed nano-sized oxides, achieved a UTS of ~800 MPa and a 
ductility of ~40% at room temperature [ 13 ]. The La-TZM alloy was 
manufactured through solid-solid mixing with nano-La
2
O
3 
combined 
with thermomechanical processing, and exhibited a room temperature 
UTS of ~1405 MPa, about 40% higher than traditional TZM alloys 
(~925 MPa). However, it exhibited limited room temperature ductility 
(7.9%) and high temperature UTS (348 MPa at 1000 
◦
C) [ 14 ]. Although 
a uniform microstructure can provide good strength and ductility at 
room temperature, it cannot guarantee good high-temperature stability 
due to the degradation of strength and elongation caused by the coars -
ening of ultrafine grains and high degree of localized macroscopic

Recent experience drawn from pure copper with harmonic grain 
structures [ 16 ] and graded precipitation phase high-entropy alloys 
(HEAs) with heterogeneous structures [ 17 ] have indicated that special 
structures such as bimodal grain structures [ 18 – 20 ], multi-phase 
structures [ 21 ], and graded precipitates [ 22 ] can generate heteroge -
neous deformation-induced (HDI) strengthening effects. HDI originates 
from the plastic deformation in soft domain constrained by hard 
domain, which generates back stress opposite to the applied stress at the 
heterogeneous interfaces and forming a lot of geometrically necessary 
dislocations (GNDs) in soft domains, thereby enhancing the mechanical 
properties [ 23 ]. For example, Ti alloys with multimodal grain structures 
exhibited superior UTS~950 MPa and ductility~28.5% compared to 
coarse-grained ones (UTS~680 MPa and ductility~26.5%, respec -
tively). Yuan et al. [ 24 ] prepared heterostructure HEAs through cold 
rolling combined with short-time annealing, which exhibited excellent 
comprehensive mechanical properties over a wide temperature range, 
maintaining a UTS above 1000 MPa under tensile conditions at 700 
◦
C. 
Previous studies reported that the particle stimulated nucleation (PSN) 
mechanism using coarse particles ( > 1 μ m) and the Zener pinning effect 
of fine particles ( < 1 μ m) could control the nucleation and growth of fine 
dyamic recrystallized (DRX) grains. Li et al. [ 25 ] obtained a bimodal 
grain structure in AZ91 alloy through rolling process with addition of Y, 
which increased the FGs volume fraction from 30% to 52%. Meanwhile, 
the optimized AZ91-1Y alloy achieved an excellent combination of large 
UTS (~405 MPa) and good uniform elongation (~9%). 
Inspired by the aforementioned reports, in this work, we highlight a 
strategy to enhance the performance of TZM alloys by creating a 
bimodal grain structure through graded second phases. By adding Y
2
O
3 
along with RF, fine DRX grains can be formed around micro-sized second 
phase particles, thus creating heterostructured YTZM alloys with a 
special core-shell structure in which coarse grains (CGs) serve as soft 
domains and FGs encapsulate the micro-sized second phase particles to 
form hard domains. We disucss the effect of rare earth oxides on the 
microstructures of TZM alloys and discover a unique mechanism of rare 
earth oxides during high temperature deformation. Consequently, the 
microstructure-mechanical property relationship is clarified, providing 
a novel microstructure design solution for TZM alloys with excellent 
mechanical performances.

Metallographic samples for microstructure analysis were processed

(200 kV) were adopted to obtain the TEM bright field (BF), selected area 
electron diffraction (SAED), HRTEM and other related information.

5. Conclusions 
High-strength Y

pared by a powder metallurgy route with the RF, presenting a tensile 
strength of 820 MPa at room temperature and a high temperature 
strength of 433 MPa at 1000

(285 MPa). The performance enhancement can be attributed to the 
synergistic effect of HDI strengthening, GB strengthening, and Orowan 
strengthening. Firstly, the PSN mechanism of micro-oxides promotes the 
nucleation of DRX grains, whereas the secondary carbides with an HCP 
structure reprecipitates and hinder the growth of DRX grains, leading to 
a bimodal grain structure in YTZM and an increased volume fraction 
(20.6%) of FGs ( ≤ 4 μ m) in YTZM-D. Secondly, during high temperature 
deformation, the intergranular and intragranular Y

2
O

alleviate the stress concentration at the PB through twinning, "FCC → 9R" 
transformation, and stacking faults, thereby improving the PB stability.

phases in the oxides. Due to the diffusion of Y and Ti elements with V
o

the high temperature stability of oxides, improve the corresponding 
ability to carry and hinder dislocations, and effectively enhance the high 
temperature performances of YTZM-D.
