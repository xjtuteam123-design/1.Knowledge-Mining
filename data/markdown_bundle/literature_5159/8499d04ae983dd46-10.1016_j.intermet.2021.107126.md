ABSTRACT 
The deformation behavior of VCoNi medium-entropy alloy at the temperatures ranging from 77 K to 573 K was 
experimentally investigated in this study. It showed that the high-density nanosized dislocation substructures 
and deformation twins played a major role in the tensile deformation at 77 K, and their synergistic effects caused 
an excellent combination of strength and ductility. When the temperature is higher than 473 K, the stress-strain 
curve exhibited obvious a clear serrated flow behavior but maintained good uniform elongation. The micro -
structure analysis indicated that a dynamically precipitated V-rich phase had an apparent influence on the 
deformation behavior above 473 K. Present results suggested that although the VCoNi alloy exhibited excellent 
mechanical properties in the present temperature range, the decreased FCC stability should be noticed, which 
could result in some changes of mechanical properties particularly at higher temperatures. 
1. Introduction 
In recent years, a novel design concept of alloy called the high mixing 
entropy and the multiple major elements was proposed by scientists, and 
the produced alloys were referred to as medium and high entropy alloys 
(MEAs and HEAs) [ 1 – 9 ]. Such alloys consist of multiple elements with 
equal or near-equal atomic percentage, and have a simple crystal 
structure, such as face-centered-cubic (FCC) or body-centered-cubic 
(BCC) structure etc . The mechanical properties of HEAs and MEAs 
have been widely studied and reported in the literatures [ 7 – 9 ]. Among 
the various alloying systems, the superior ductility and cryogenic tem -
perature damage tolerance of FCC-HEAs and MEAs give them potential 
advantages in structural applications [ 10 – 12 ]. However, the insufficient 
room temperature strength of FCC-HEAs and MEAs limits their further 
engineering applications. Therefore, great efforts are being made to 
obtain a good combination of strength and ductility in FCC-HEAs and 
MEAs [ 10 , 13 – 15 ]. 
Previous researches suggested that the deformation behavior of 
conventional metals was dependent on the temperature [ 16 , 17 ]. For 
example, the uniform elongation of transformation-induced plasticity 
(TRIP) steels dropped substantially when the temperature exceeded over 
373 K [ 18 ]. This is because the deformation behavior of the TRIP steels 
is dependent on the stacking fault energy (SFE), which is a function of 
temperature. Therefore, the deformation behavior of TRIP steels is 
sensitive to the temperature. Similar result was also observed in 
austenitic Fe – 14Cr – 16Ni alloy [ 19 ]. As compared with conventional 
metals, the multi-element effects of HEAs resulted in the fact that their 
deformation behavior was more sensitive to temperature [ 10 , 11 , 20 ]. 
Many studies on various HEAs and MEAs shown that the strength and 
plasticity change with temperature [ 21 , 22 ]. For example, when 
FCC-HEAs and MEAs were deformed at cryogenic temperatures, the 
deformation twinning was more favorable to form. As a result, more 
superior combination of strength and ductility was obtained [ 12 ]. 
Recently, Sohn et al . reported an ultra-strong FCC-VCoNi equiatomic 
MEA, which exhibited a good combination of strength and ductility at 
room temperature and a strong dependence of strength upon grain size

2. Experimental 
The equiatomic VCoNi MEA were prepared from pure elements with 
purities higher than 99.9% via arc melting under a Ti gettered high 
purity argon atmosphere. The master ingots were flipped and 
completely re-melted at least 4 times to ensure chemical composition 
homogeneity, and then drop casted into a Cu mold with a size of 100 
mm × 20 mm × 10 mm. The rectangular samples were sealed in an 
evacuated and Ar backfilled quartz tubes, and then were homogenized at 
1473 K for 24 h followed by water quenching. Cold rolling was con -
ducted on the homogenized samples with an approximately 0.3 – 0.5 mm 
per pass. The thickness of samples was finally reduced to 1.8 mm after 
20 passes with a total reduction of 82%. After rolling, the rolled samples 
were annealed at 1373 K for 30 min and immediately water quenched to 
obtain fully recrystallized structures. 
The phase of recrystallized samples was identified via X-ray 
diffraction (XRD). The XRD measurement was conducted in a D/MAX 
2400 diffractometer with a Cu- K α using a scanning angle of 20 – 100 deg 
and a scanning rate of 3 deg/min. The microstructure characterization of 
the recrystallized samples was carried out using a field emission scan -
ning electron microscope (SEM, Nova Nano SEM 230) equipped with 
electron backscattering diffraction (EBSD). The samples for EBSD 
observation were ground using SiC papers, followed by polished using a 
silica Nano-suspension solution with the granularity of 0.04 μ m, and 
then electro-polished using a mixture solution of 10 vol % perchloric 
acid + methanol solution at 20 V at room temperature. The details of the 
EBSD measurements can be found in our previous work [ 21 , 28 ]. 
Flat dog-bone-shaped samples with gauge length of 10 mm were 
sliced from the recrystallized sheets using electric discharge machine 
along to the rolling direction of the sheets. All tensile samples were 
ground carefully on each side using SiC paper, and the final samples with 
a thickness of 1.75 mm and a gauge section width of 2 mm were ob -
tained. Tensile tests were performed with a strain rate of 10

3.4. Analysis of fractography 
Fig. 9 shows the SEM images of the fractography views of the spec -
imens after tensile deformation at 77 K, 293 K and 573 K. For the 
specimens deformed at 77 K and 293 K, as illustrated in Fig. 9 a and b, all

dimple rupture of the fracture surface means the ductile nature of

4. Discussion 
4.1. Analysis of deformation behavior of VCoNi alloy at different 
temperature 
The deformation behaviors of the VCoNi alloy presents an excellent 
combination of strength and ductility at 77 K. Additionally, a prominent 
serrated flow is observed in the stress-strain curve above 473 K, almost 
without sacrificing the plasticity. The enhanced strength and improved 
ductility at 77 K is in accordance with the results of previously reported 
alloys with FCC structure and low SFE, such as austenitic stainless steels 
[ 16 ] and Cantor alloy [ 12 ] etc . The underlying mechanism is attributed 
to the synergistic effect of the dislocation motion and deformation twins, 
as illustrated in Fig. 5 . The high-density nanosized dislocation sub -
structure and twin boundaries themselves can act as obstacles, which 
effectively reduce the mean free path of the dislocation movement, 
promoting the dislocation storage and greatly accommodating the 
plasticity. In addition, the deformation twin also leads to continuous 
grain fragmentation, causes dynamic Hall-Petch effect, hence strongly 
refining the grains and blocking the dislocation motion. 
The serrated flow, which macroscopically presents as the dynamic 
avalanche response of flow units to stress during the deformation pro -
cess, has been observed in the stress-strain curves of many HEAs [ 11 , 
33 ]. Generally speaking, the underlying physical origin of serration flow 
is related to dynamic strain aging (DSA), which is resulted from the 
interaction between mobile solute atoms and dislocations during the 
plastic deformation [ 30 , 34 , 35 ]. This is because the dislocation migrates 
intermittently during deformation. The solute atoms diffuse and pin the 
mobile dislocations during waiting time. At the same time, the pined 
dislocations are released under thermal activation. As a result, the 
repeated locking/unlocking effect between the diffusing solutes and 
mobile dislocations causes the serrated flow in the stress – strain curves 
during the plastic deformation. In addition, the deformation twining and 
phase transformation play a crucial role in the serration flow behavior of 
materials [ 11 ]. For example, Liu et al observed that the numerous ser -
rations appeared in the stress-strain curves of the specimens of FCC 
FeCoNiCr HEA when deformed below 77 K [ 11 ]. They suggested that the 
deformation twining and phase transformation (FCC to HCP transition) 
were the dominant factor of the serration behavior. According to the 
theoretical analyses mentioned above and microstructural measure -
ments of the deformed specimens ( Fig. 8 ) in present study, we deduce 
that the serrated flow of VCoNi alloy should be the coupling effect of the 
dynamic strain aging and dynamic precipitation of the V-rich σ phase. 
Here, it should be noted that the serration flow is an inhomogeneous 
plastic deformation, which could result in the localized plastic insta -
bility [ 30 , 34 , 35 ]. However, in this work, although the deformation 
behavior of alloys presents the prominent serrated flow when deformed 
above 473 K, all the specimens have approximate plasticity from 293 K 
to 573 K. Similar results were also observed in a Fe – 25Ni – 20Cr ( wt .%) 
austenitic stainless steel [ 32 ]. Recently, many works reported that the 
phase transformation during deformation was beneficial for the tough -
ening of the metals [ 10 , 36 ]. Obviously, in this work, the dynamic pre -
cipitation of the V-rich σ phase above 473 K played a crucial role in 
keeping good plasticity of VCoNi alloy. The dynamic precipitated σ 
phases effectively prevented the motion of dislocations, as illustrated by 
the dislocation pile-up around the σ phase particle shown in Fig. 8 b, thus 
leading to a strong strain hardening ability. However, it is well known 
that the σ phase is thermodynamically unstable. With the increase of 
temperature, the decomposition of σ phase would occur due to the 
enhanced atom long-distance diffusion. For example, the volume frac -
tion of σ phase apparently decreased with the increase of annealing 
temperatures in FeCoNiCrMn alloy above 1333 K [ 37 ]. This means that

4.2. The mechanism of V-rich phase precipitation 
The σ phase is one of the most frequently observed precipitates in 
HEAs and some steels [ 38 , 39 ]. Although the formation mechanism of σ 
phase in HEAs may be different from the conventional alloys, its for -
mation process is affected by processing conditions and alloying ele -
ments [ 40 , 41 ]. The former includes the aging temperature and aging 
time. For example, it was suggested that the Cr-rich σ phase could be 
obtained during a long time anneal at temperatures between 873 K and 
1073 K in FeCoNiCrMn HEA [ 42 ]. Similar works also have been reported 
in other HEAs [ 43 – 45 ]. Previous works suggested that σ phase was 
prone to form in the alloys containing Cr and V elements [ 46 ]. For 
example, in the CoCrFeNiMnV
x 
HEAs just annealed at 1273 K for a short 
time, the abundant σ phases were observed [ 47 ]. The mechanism is 
ascribed to the high negative mixing enthalpy among V – Co, V – Ni, V – Fe 
and Cr – Mn binary systems, which is favorable for the formation of the 
V-rich or/and Cr-rich phases. In addition, it is noted that the mechanical 
processing can also bring about micro-segregation, hence forming the 
local element fluctuation [ 48 , 49 ]. This is because that: firstly, the 
severely deformed material has high internal energy that supplies the 
driving force for precipitation; secondly, the formed abundant disloca -
tions during deformation also offer fast diffusion pathways of elements 
and more nucleation sites for precipitation. One example of this process 
is that the Cr – Mn rich phase with nano-scale was found in Cantor alloy 
after it was processed by high-pressure torsion [ 50 ]. Based on the ana -
lyses mentioned above, the dynamic precipitation of V-rich σ phase 
during deformation at 573 K can be explained. Firstly, the VCoNi me -
dium entropy alloy itself has a more severe inherent lattice distortion as 
compared to previous MEAs or HEAs and conventional alloys, which can 
worsen the FCC stability of the VCoNi alloy, including thermodynamics 
stability and kinetics stability. Secondly, the abundant dislocations offer 
fast diffusion pathways of elements and more nucleation sites during 
deformation, and the increased temperature also provides additional 
driving force that accelerates the diffusion of elements around disloca -
tions. Consequently, the synergistic effect of the chemical composition, 
temperature and stress results in the dynamic precipitation of V-rich σ 
phase during deformation at 573 K. 
Based on the results in the present study, although VCoNi alloy with 
FCC single phase shows a more excellent combination of strength- 
ductility at present test temperatures than other FCC-HEAs and MEAs, 
the decreased FCC stability should be noticed, because it may result in 
the change of the mechanical properties especially at the higher tem -
perature. Therefore, the realization of the VCoNi alloys exhibiting an 
excellent FCC stability without sacrificing their mechanical properties, 
will not only attract considerable interests in the research circle, but also 
is of great significance in its real engineering application. 
5. Conclusions 
In summary, the tensile deformation behavior of FCC-VCoNi MEA 
was investigated in the temperature range from 77 K to 573 K, and the 
following conclusions can be drawn: 
1. The synergistic effect of the dislocation motion and deformation 
twins caused an excellent combination of strength and ductility 
when the alloy was deformed at 77 K. 
2. A temperature-independent plasticity was observed, especially in the 
temperature range from 293 K to 573 K. 
3. The prominent serrated flow in the stress-strain curves was observed 
when the deformation temperature was above 473 K. Its underlying
