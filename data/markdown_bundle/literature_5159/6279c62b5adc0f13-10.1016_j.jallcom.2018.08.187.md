Abstract 
Several nickel-based superalloys were nitrided at 4 00°C for 4 h in a plasma-assisted low pressure 
gaseous nitriding set-up. Composed of an austenitic γ phase matrix with Ni 3(Al,Ti,Nb)-type γ’ 
precipitates, the studied superalloys include the p olycrystalline Udimet®720Li (U720), the 
directionally solidified MarM200-DS and the single crystal René N4 and MC2 superalloys. The nitrided 
samples were characterized by scanning electronic m icroscopy, energy dispersive spectroscopy and 
glow discharge optical emission spectroscopy to det ermine the nitriding behaviour of the γ’ 
precipitates. The nitrogen incorporation is confirmed in the γ phase, up to 25-30 at.%, corresponding 
to the expanded γ N phase, similar to the one obtained in low temperat ure nitriding of austenitic 
alloys like 316L stainless steel. alloys with a pur e austenitic structure. However various behaviours of 
the γ’ precipitates were observed in the different alloys. In the U720 superalloy, the γ’ precipitates 
are nitrided to the same level than the surrounding γ N phase. On the contrary, the γ’ precipitates in 
the MC2, MarM200-DS and René N4 superalloys are onl y nitrided in the top surface layer, over 0.2 - 
2 µm, whereas the γ phase is nitrided up to 4 -7 µm . Various complementary experiments have 
shown that the γ’ nitriding is not dependent on the size of the γ’ precipitates, on the γ’ crystalline 
orientation nor on the γ/γ’ interface coherency. Th e main influent parameter is the fine intrinsic γ’ 
composition, that is the Al substitution by solute elements like Ti or Nb: the closer to pure Ni 3Al is the 
γ’ composition, the lower is the nitrogen incorpora tion. This influence of the composition on the 
nitriding efficiency is discussed through thermodyn amic and diffusion kinetic considerations. 
However the γ’ nitriding is thought to operate thro ugh be favoured by the propagation of shear 
dislocations in the γ’ precipitates thanks to the h igh compressive stress induced by the surrounding 
γN phase expansion. So, the γ’ chemical composition w ould governing controls the γ’ nitriding ability 
as it influences the shear strength of the γ’ precipitates.

1. Introduction 
By enhancing several mechanical, tribological or ch emical properties, surface nitriding is 
widely employed to improve the durability of materials submitted to various solicitations like fatigue, 
creep, wear, fretting, corrosion, abrasion [1–3]. T hermal or plasma assisted nitriding is for instance 
particularly efficient on steels or other metallic alloys (containing some metallic nitride precursors) to 
improve the hardness and the wear resistance thanks to the formation of nitrided layers or to the 
precipitations of dispersed nitrides (CrN x, MoN x, TiN x…) in the matrix [4–8]. Considering austenitic 
stainless steels (ASS are FCC Fe-based alloys with Cr as main alloying element, called γ phase), low 
temperature (< 450°C) nitriding enables the formati on of a metastable expanded austenitic phase 
austenite (usually called S-phase or γ N phase), corresponding to a solid solution of nitro gen at high 
concentration (up to 30 at.%) with a very high comp ressive residual stress [9,10]. Thanks to the low 
temperature during the treatment, the properties of the “bulk” ASS obtained by the initial 
thermomechanical metallurgy are maintained whereas important improvements are achieved in 
wear or fatigue resistance thanks to the surface γ N layer. With low temperature nitriding, the 
formation of chromium nitrides precipitates is very limited, which is of particular importance to 
maintain the Cr atoms mobility and thus the corrosion resistance. 
Several studies [4,6,7,9,11–19] have then been dedi cated to extend the low temperature 
nitriding to some other alloys with a similar FCC a ustenitic structure (γ phase), like Co- or Ni-based 
alloys. Like in the ASS, the formation of a γN phase layer would aim to improve mechanical behaviour, 
wear or abrasion resistance. In our previous studie s on this subject [20], plasma assisted nitriding a t 
400°C of various nickel-based superalloys has been investigated to determine their ability to produce 
the expected γ N phase. However, some nickel-based superalloys differ from the homogeneous ASS by 
the presence of initial strengthening precipitates dispersed in the γ grains. These precipitates are 
obtained by adjunction of some alloying elements (l ike Al, Ti, Ti or Nb) and appropriate metallurgical 
thermal treatment: they are called γ’, γ” or δ precipitates depending on their chemistry and 
crystallographic structure and they are aimed at en hancing mechanical properties, especially at high 
temperature [21,22]. With shapes and size (from few micrometers to few tens of nanometers) 
depending on the superalloys and on their thermomec hanical preparations, the γ’ precipitates of 
Ni 3Al type chemistry have an ordered L1 2 structure with some substitutional elements to Al (as Ti, 
Ta…) or to Ni (Co…). In references [20,23] and in t he literature [4,6], it was effectively demonstrate d 
that, similarly to the Fe-based ASS, the austenitic -like FCC Ni-based γ matrix was nitrided, giving ri se 
to an expanded γ N phase with nitrogen content up to 25-30 at.%. The γN phase obtained in some 
nickel-based superalloys is however less stable tha n the one in ASS, as some nitrides (CrN) were 
detected with temperature treatments as low as 400° C [9,17]. Various nitriding behaviours were 
evidenced concerning the γ’ precipitates: by macros copic analysis (GDOES chemical profiling) and 
cross-section SEM observations, it was concluded th at the γ’ precipitates in the studied 
polycrystalline nickel-based superalloys (Udimet®720Li, N18, Inconel®718) were nitrided up to 20-30 
at.%, whereas almost no nitrogen was incorporated i n the γ’ precipitates of single crystal superalloys 
(MC2, MC-NG) [20]. The objectives of this present a rticle are then to confirm the nitrogen content, 
by local measurements in the γ’ precipitates, and t o elucidate the observed differences in the γ’ 
nitriding ability. Compared to this previous work [ 20], the present article is providing measurements 
of the local nitrogen content in the γ’ precipitate s, it completes the panel of studied materials by 
introducing other superalloys with an intermediate behaviour (René N4, MarM200-DS) and brings 
additional experimental evidences to discuss the observed differences in the γ’ nitriding ability.

2. Material and methods 
2.1. Materials 
The studied Nickel-based superalloys are the polycr ystalline Udimet®720Li (U720), the 
directionally solidified MarM200-DS, and the single crystals MC2 and René N4 (both with <001> 
orientation). Their chemical composition, the γ and γ’ phases proportions and their respective 
compositions compiled from the literature are prese nted in Table 1 [24–30]. The global composition 
of the materials used in this study was confirmed b y Energy Dispersive Spectroscopy (EDS) and Glow 
Discharge Optical Emission Spectroscopy (GDOES); th e individual composition of the γ phase and of 
the γ’ precipitates could have been verified only w hen the EDS probe size allows it (i.e. for large γ’ 
precipitates or large γ phase zones). 
U720 is characterized by a multimodal γ’ distributi on, with primary (γ’ I, with size of few µm), 
secondary (γ’ II with size of few hundreds of nm) and tertiary (γ’ III size of with few tens of nm) 
precipitates. MC2 is a single crystalline superallo ys with about 400-500 nm large cuboidal shape γ’ 
precipitates characterized by a moderate content in Ti ( < 3 at% in MC2-γ’ ) and by their high 
coherency with the surrounding γ phase matrix chann els [25,31]. The microstructure of the single 
crystal René N4 is similar to the one of MC2 ; it w as chosen because of its higher Ti content (about 6-
7 at.% in γ’) compared to MC2. The superalloy MarM2 00-DS presents an intermediary structure 
between polycrystalline and single crystals superal loys: all the γ grains have one identical 
crystallographic <001> orientation thanks to the di rectionally solidification process. Thanks to the 
solution (at 1230°C) and ageing (870°C) heat treatments, the γ’ phase is coherently dispersed in the γ 
grains, mainly as 250 nm large cuboidal shape preci pitates, with a Ti content of about 3.7 at.%. The 
detailed heat treatments and other characteristics of the studied Nickel-based superalloys can be 
found in the following references as well as information about some other alloys (MC-NG, N18, Ni 3Al) 
used in the discussion for comparison [20,32]. 
2.2. Samples preparation 
Samples were previously polished with increasing gr ade SiC papers in order to eliminate the 
hardened layer resulting from machining. Polishing was completed using solutions of diamond 
powder (6 to 0.25 µm) and a colloidal suspension of alumina (~ 0.1 µm). The samples were thus 
degreased and cleaned in an ultrasonic bath of acet one, then ethanol and finally dried under 
nitrogen gas. Cross-sections were prepared following the same surface polishing, after encapsulating 
pieces of the nitrided samples in conductive resin. 
The nitriding treatments were performed for 4 hours to 16 hours at 400°C in a R.F. plasma 
assisted thermal nitriding reactor previously descr ibed [33]. A gas flow of 100 sccm consisting of 60 
sccm N 2 and 40 sccm H 2 was used to obtain the working pressure regulated at 8 Pa. The forward R.F. 
power was fixed at 700 W. During the whole plasma t reatment, the samples were maintained at the 
floating potential (typically -15 V vs plasma poten tial), avoiding any sputtering by energetic ions. T he 
heating (1 hour) and the cooling processes (requiri ng less than 20 min till 200°C and few hours till 
room temperature) were performed under vacuum (10 -4 Pa). 
2.3. Samples characterizations 
Observations of cross-sections of the samples were realized by Scanning Electron Microscopy 
(SEM) on a JEOL JSM 7001F-TTLS microscope equipped with a field emission gun. Images were 
obtained from secondary electrons (SE) signal with incident electron acceleration ranging from 10 to 
25 kV and working distance of about 10 mm. Energy D ispersive Spectroscopy (EDS) was performed 
on the sample cross-sections using an Oxford Instru ments system equipped with a Silicon Drift 
Detector EDS XMax50, with resolution of 124 eV at t he Mn Kα line. For quantitative analysis, 10 kV

4. Discussion 
Many factors may control the nitrogen incorporation in the γ’ precipitates. If the γ’ 
precipitates localized at the extreme surface in co ntact with the plasma are excluded from the 
discussion, nitrogen has to be introduced in γ’ precipitates from the surrounding nitrided γ phase (γ N) 
and through the γ N/γ’ interfaces. Several nitriding experiments were then performed in order to 
investigate the various material characteristics th at may play a role in the mechanism of nitrogen 
incorporation in γ’. 
4.1. Influence of γ’ size and of the γ/γ’ interface area

same total volume fraction of γ’ (40-45 vol.%) but with different microstructures thanks to specific 
heat treatments before nitriding. The multimodal distribution of γ’ precipitates (γ’I, γ’ II and γ’ III ) in the 
different U720 is described in Table 3 [34,35]. Wha tever the microstructure (sizes and fractions of 
each γ’ population), the nitrogen profiles are rath er similar, indicating the incorporation of nitroge n 
in the γ’ precipitates. The specific shape at the e nd of the profile of sample U720 #4 is relative to the 
anisotropic diffusion in the micrometric γ grains, which depends on the crystallographic orientation,

4.2. Influence of the γ/γ’ interface coherency 
The quality of the γ/γ’ interface (phase coherency) is another difference between the various 
superalloys which depends on the γ/γ’ misfit: thank s to a relatively high misfit, the γ’ shape is cubi c 
and the γ/γ’ interface is highly coherent in single crystalline or directionally solidified superalloys like 
MC2, René N4 or MarM200-DS. On the contrary, with a lower misfit, the γ’ precipitates are more 
spheroidal and the interface is only partially cohe rent in polycrystalline superalloys like U720 [36]. 
Figure 8 compares the nitrogen profiles in a <001> oriented MC2 with the one obtained in a MC2 
specimen with γ’ rafted previously to the nitriding . The loss of the γ/γ’ coherency with the γ’-raftin g 
phenomenon is resulting from a prior creep deformat ion, and hence, a high γ/γ’ interfacial 
dislocations density. The previous rafting did not significantly modify the nitrogen profile shape. 
Indeed the global diffusion length in the γ phase w as slightly reduced, thanks to the altered diffusion 
pathways “inbetween” the γ’ rafts compared to the i nitial MC2 (nitriding of rafted MC2 with γ’ rafts 
parallel to the surface led to a thinner nitrided d epth). On the contrary, the depth with nitrided γ’ 
seems to be slightly thicker in the rafted MC2 spec imen (0.4 µm) than in the original MC2 specimen 
(0.2 µm). However the deeper rafted γ’ precipitates remain unnitrided whereas the surrounding γ 
phase is nitrided. The analysis of the nitrided Ren é N4 (resp. MarM200-DS) confirms the poor 
influence of the γ/γ’ interface coherency on the γ’ nitriding, as part of the γ’ precipitates with 
coherent interface with γ phase are nitrided within the first 2 µm (resp. 500 nm) whereas other γ’ 
located deeper are not. So the crystallographic qua lity (coherency) of the γ/γ’ interface is definitively 
not the determining parameter controlling the nitrogen incorporation in γ’. 
4.3. Kinetics and diffusion limitation of the γ’ precipitates nitriding 
To explore the diffusion and kinetic dependence of the γ’ precipitates nitriding, GDOES 
profiles of Ni 3Al (with 1 at.% Ta) and several superalloys nitride d at 400°C for 4 h and 16 h are 
compared in Figure 9. Although the depth resolution is limited in GDOES, the depth with nitrided γ’ 
precipitates after a 4 h treatment is clearly varyi ng in the different alloys from less than ~ 50 nm ( in 
pure Ni 3Al), about 200 nm (in MC2), 500 nm (in MarM200-DS), 2 µm (in René N4) to 8 µm (in U720). 
After nitriding for 16 h, these depths are almost u nchanged in Ni 3Al, MC2, MarM200-DS and René 
N4. Considering a diffusion-limited process in the γ’ phase (following a √/g1830/g1872 law for instance), the 
increase of time from 4 h to 16 h should have led t o a layer with nitrided γ’ twice thicker, as it is 
mostly observed in U720. 
Moreover, several EDS-SEM observations have shown that the nitrogen diffusion front in the 
γ’ precipitates always remains more or less paralle l to the surface, whatever the crystallographic 
orientation of the γ’ precipitates and whatever the superalloy (see for instance Figure 1, Figure 4 and 
figures in [20,23]). The γ’ cubes in MC2, René N4 o r MarM200-DS are only nitrided from the “front” 
side (face to the external surface). It is also the case of the “second” row of γ’ precipitates which are 
nitrided in René N4 or MarM200-DS: the γ’ cubes are never nitrided from the “back” nor from the 
“lateral” sides (see the zone delimited by the red circle in Figure 4). According to these evidences, the 
γ’ nitriding is not (only) limited by a slower diff usion in γ’ than in surrounding γ, otherwise nitrog en

the nitriding front inside a γ’ precipitate and its occurrence only at limited depth in γ N. However they 
are both controlled by thermal diffusion/nucleation mechanisms and then would imply a significant 
growth of the nitrided γ’ depth with treatment time . This latter is in fact very poor and thus the γ’ 
nitriding involves additional mechanisms and conditions. 
4.4. Influence of the γ’ composition and its proximity to a “perfect” Ni 3Al 
Although a common name is used, the γ’ precipitates are not strictly identical in all the 
studied superalloys. If they are all precipitated w ithin the same cubic L1 2 structure of Ni 3Al type, a 
more or less large amount of Ni or Al atoms are sub stituted by alloying elements: few at.% of Co, Cr, 
W, Re are mainly substituting Ni atoms whereas Al i s mainly replaced by Ti, Ta, Hf or Nb. By 
comparison with the 25 at.% of Al in pure Ni 3Al, γ’ precipitates of MC2, MC-NG [20] and MarM200-DS 
superalloys keep a relatively high Al concentration (15-17 at.%) as it is poorly substituted by Ti (1- 4 
at%). On the contrary, the amount of Ti is higher i n N18 [20] and U720 alloys (8 – 11.5 at.%) and the 
Al content is consequently much lower (10-13 at.%). The single crystalline superalloy René N4 is then 
here particularly interesting as it presents an int ermediary Ti content of about 7 at.%. It was 
observed that the depth with nitrided γ’ precipitates is about 0,2 µm in MC2, 0.5 µm in MarM200-DS, 
2 µm in René N4 and up to 8 µm in U720. Considerin g the previously described results, the γ’ ability 
to be nitrided is clearly increasing with an increa sing Ti or Nb contents or with a decreasing Al 
content (Figure 10). However the question is remain ing: what property is changing with the γ’ 
composition and makes it more or less easier to nitride?

4.4.2. Residual compressive stress and γ’ shearing: influ ence of the mechanical properties 
evolution with γ’ composition 
Another possible parameter that can influences the γ’ nitriding, is the in-plane (biaxial) 
compressive stress induced by the expansion of the cubic cell, from γ to γ N phase [19]. The induced 
anisotropy could explain lower nitrogen incorporation by the γ’ cube lateral sides (under compressive 
stress) compared to the sides parallel to the surfa ce. The difference between the front and the back-
sides of a partially nitrided γ’ precipitate could be related to the decrease of stress with depth. 
The stress induced by the nitriding of the γ phase lead to dislocations generation in the γ N 
phase: it was clearly shown that plastic strain has to complete elastic strain in the γ N phase of 
nitrided austenitic stainless steels [10]. The created dislocations propagates till the γ/γ’ interface and 
also within the γ’ precipitates. At the nitriding t emperature, the shear strength of the γ’ precipitat es, 
linked to the stacking fault energy for APB shearin g [52–54], is relative to their composition and is 
known to be usually lower in René N4 or MarM200-DS alloys than in MC2 or MC-NG alloys [55–58]. 
The ability to be nitrided could then be relative t o the created dislocations density in the γ’ 
precipitates. This mechanism could also explain the slight increase of the depth with γ’ nitrided laye r 
observed in Figure 8, from 0.2 µm (in MC2) to about 0.4 µm (in rafted MC2). Although the rafting was 
operated in such conditions that no shear of the γ’ precipitates occurred, it is corresponding to the 
generation of a high density of dislocations at the γ/γ’ interfaces (i.e. loss of crystalline coherenc y 
without plastic strain of γ’). The compressive stre ss induced by the γ phase nitriding may then 
promote a slightly better γ’ nitriding, thanks to the propagation in γ’ of these dislocations pre-existing

5. Conclusions 
 The plasma assisted nitriding of several Ni-based superalloys at 400°C for 4 hours leads to a 
large incorporation of nitrogen till depth of few m icrometers. Characterizations by SEM and EDX 
allow to confirm that the γ phase is always nitride d up to 25-30 at.% (forming the γ N phase) whereas 
the γ’ precipitates show various behaviours. The fo llowing main conclusions can be established from 
this work: 
• In U720, all type of γ’ precipitates are nitrided a t the same level and till the same depth than 
in the surrounding γ phase, whatever the size of the γ’ precipitates. 
• In directionally-solidified or single crystalline N i-based superalloys like MarM200-DS, MC2 or 
René N4, the γ’ precipitates are only nitrided at t he top surface, till a depth of 200 nm (MC2)

phase remain non-nitrided. 
• This difference between the polycrystalline superal loys U720 and the single crystals 
superalloys is not due to any anisotropy of any effect of the grain orientation on the diffusion 
in the single crystals. 
• The coherency of the γ/γ’ interface has no significant influence on γ’ nitriding. 
• The fine composition of the γ’ phase is in fact the key parameters governing its ability to be 
nitrided. The closer to Ni 3Al composition is the γ’, the higher is the difficu lty to incorporate 
nitrogen. The higher Al is substituted by some nitrides formers elements like Ti, the thicker is 
the depth with nitrided γ’. 
• Variations of the thermodynamic stability of the γ’ phase Ni 3(Al, Ti) with the Ti content hardly 
explain such a difference: the enthalpy of formatio n of Ni 3(Al, Ti) is roughly the same 
whatever the Ti content and remains much lower than the ones of the nitrides (AlN, TiN…). 
• Considering that the nitrogen front in the γ’ preci pitates remains parallel to the surface and 
poorly evolves with nitriding time, the γ’ nitriding is not only kinetics-limited. It likely requires 
a threshold in the nitriding potential and/or a high stress level. 
• The high stress induced by the γ N phase expansion has to be adapted by plastic deformations 
with dislocations formation. Their propagation in t he γ’ precipitates is depending on the γ’ 
shear strength and so on the γ’ composition; these dislocations could act as preferential 
diffusion pathways for nitrogen incorporation in γ’. 
• The poor growth of the layer with nitrided γ’ preci pitates can be explained by the release of 
the stress level when cracks are initiated.
