ABSTRACT 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) composed of Ti, Zr, Hf, and Nb have emerged as promising materials for 
high-temperature aerospace applications due to their exceptional balance of strength and toughness, coupled 
with a reduced density and softening resistance. This study delves into the influence of alloy composition on the 
mechanical properties, a pivotal aspect in the design of RHEAs. We investigate a series of Ti

1. Introduction 
Aerospace engineering has advanced significantly with de -
velopments in aviation propulsion systems, particularly in materials for 
turbine blades. Traditional materials like nickel-based and cobalt-based 
superalloys, used in these components, face limitations due to their 
melting points and alloying capacity. As aviation engines demand higher 
thrust-to-weight ratios, these materials struggle at temperatures above 
1350
◦
C [1] . A promising alternative is high-entropy alloys (HEAs), 
known for their equi-atomic or near-equimolar composition, providing 
high strength, hardness, toughness, fatigue resistance, corrosion resis -
tance, and radiation resistance [2 – 4] . Among HEAs, refractory 
high-entropy alloys, composed of elements like Mo, Nb, Ta, and W, stand 
out for their strength and ductility, even at temperatures up to 1600
◦
C. 
These alloys are particularly promising for aerospace applications, 
including aviation engine turbine blades [5,6] . Despite challenges like 
high density and brittleness at room temperature, newer alloy systems 
incorporating elements like Ti, Zr, Hf, and Nb have been developed, 
offering lower density and better strength at both room and high tem -
peratures [7] . The TiZrHfNb refractory high-entropy alloy system

By incorporating an appropriate amount of oxygen, the ordered 
oxygen-complexes of interstitial atoms can be formed within the 
TiZrHfNb high-entropy alloy [8] . This unique ordered structure of 
interstitial atom complexes significantly enhanced the strength and 
plasticity of the alloy, achieving a remarkable simultaneous improve -
ment in both properties. The oxygen binding and diffusion characteris -
tics are crucial for the mechanical properties of the TiZrHfNb alloys. The 
interaction of oxygen with metal atoms, especially in niobium-rich 
zones, significantly affects the stability of the alloy and reduces inter -
nal stress [9] . Local chemical fluctuations (LCFs) induced dislocation 
pinning, multiplication, and cross-slips in the TiZrHfNb high-entropy 
alloy [10] . The phase stability and strengthening of TiZrHfNb RHEAs 
are significantly influenced by (Ti,Zr)-rich chemical short-range order -
ings (CSROs). Severe lattice distortion and electron transfer under strain 
contribute to the intrinsic strengthening of CSROs in this HEA [11] . The 
significance of CSROs was additionally uncovered through DFT calcu -
lations and molecular dynamics simulations, Ti-Zr and Hf-Nb pairs are 
the dominant factors affecting the mechanical properties of TiZrHfNb 
RHEAs [12] . Research exploring chemical inhomogeneities in 
TiZrNb-based RHEAs revealed CSROs and LCFs on nanoscales, those 
chemical complexities and nano-scale heterogeneities emerge particu -
larly with oxygen/nitrogen additions [13] . 
For an exploration of the activation energy in BCC high-entropy al -
loys, key insights were provided into the instrumented nanoindentation 
tests on TiZrHfNb HEAs [14] , dislocation nucleation requires a 
maximum shear stress and multiple atom cooperative migration. In the 
incipient plasticity of TiZrNbTa HEAs, the maximum shear stress 
required for dislocation nucleation is between 1/16 – 1/12 and 
1/18 – 1/14 of the shear modulus. The dislocation nucleation in these 
alloys occurs through a heterogeneous process involving direct 
atom-vacancy exchange [15] . The local lattice distortion in the 
TiZrHfNb alloy, as revealed by local structure characterization and 
simulations, showed that the charge transfer effect reduces lattice 
distortion but remains significant in local regions with minimal charge 
transfer effect [16] . Planar dislocation slip is the dominant deformation 
mechanism in the BCC-structured high-entropy alloys, the deformation 
behavior can be clarified based on the evolution of lattice strain under 
varying levels of applied stress [17] . The twinning-induced plasticity 
(TWIP) effect has been incorporated into TiZrHfNb RHEAs by lowering 
the temperature to destabilize the BCC phase. The mechanical twins are 
ranging from micro to nanoscale, and progressively segment the BCC 
grains into nanoscale islands during deformation, which enhances both 
the strength and ductility of the alloy [18] .

grounded in the global minimization of Gibbs energies for each phase, 
and it offers an efficient method to design and investigate HEAs [27,28] . 
The surface stability of TiZrHfNb high-entropy alloys in the (100), 
(110), and (111) configurations was investigated using first-principles 
and Monte Carlo methods. The zirconium enrichment on the (100) 
surface enhances stability through Zr-Nb bonding, and the oxygen 
adsorption are significantly influenced by the surface atomic arrange -
ment [29] . Doping TiZrHfNb HEAs with interstitial atoms such as oxy -
gen and nitrogen enhances wear resistance, this improvement is 
attributed to the synergistic effects of the oxide layer, tribo-layer, and 
nanograin layer of alloy [30] . Incorporating Ta and Mo into 
TiZrHfNb-based RHEAs when immersed in a 3.5 wt% sodium chloride 
(NaCl) solution increases lattice distortion and hardness. This 
enhancement improves their tribocorrosion performance by stabilizing 
the OCP (open circuit potential) and boosting corrosion resistance [31] . 
Previous research on TiZrHfNb RHEAs has primarily focused on 
enhancing their strength, ductility, and high-temperature performance. 
The (Ti-Zr) pairs in RHEAs are crucial, significantly influencing their 
mechanical properties through chemical and structural interactions. 
However, a considerable aspect that remains under-investigated in un -
derstanding the impact of Ti-Zr content on the mechanical properties.

interactions with neighboring atoms compared to the 34Zr alloy. This 
suggests that at lower Zr contents, there exists a higher average bond 
strength between atoms, a characteristic beneficial in preventing atomic 
separation and contributing to enhanced toughness [45] . Conversely, 
the 26Zr alloy demonstrates a lower charge density, signifying weaker 
metal bond energy between atoms, thereby exhibiting comparatively 
inferior resistance to fracture compared to other alloys. It is important to 
note that while atomic bonding plays a role, the primary strength of 
TiZrHfNb RHEAs stems from SROs and lattice distortions, with less 
contribution from atomic bonding. The establishment of the charge 
density model clarifies the robust toughness observed in the 14Zr and 
34Zr alloys. Notably, these conclusions derived from first-principles 
calculations are in accordance with the experimental toughness re -
sults, reinforcing the consistency and reliability of the obtained data.
