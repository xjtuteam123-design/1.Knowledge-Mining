ABSTRACT 
In this study, the microstructure and mechanical properties of hot-rolled χ Zr-Ti-5Al-2.5Sn ( χ = 0, 10, 20, 30 and 
35, wt%) alloys were systematically investigated. With the increase of Zr addition, phase constitutions of TiZ -
rAlSn alloys change from single α phase to α + α ´ , α ´ and α ´ + α ʺ + β phases in sequence while the β transformation 
temperature decreases. A lower content of α ” + β phases appears in the 35Zr-Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy. The yield strength 
of TiZrAlSn alloys increased monotonically from 612 ± 15 MPa for basal Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy to an ultra-high 
strength of 1386 ± 11 MPa for 35Zr-Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy, which is ~126% higher than that of basal alloy. The 
high content of Zr solute atoms, high density of dislocations and interfaces/boundaries in α ´ martensite phase 
render the ultra-high yield strength and high strain hardening rate in 35Zr-Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy. With respect to 
ductility, the uniform and total elongations increase initially from Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy to 10Zr-Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy 
and decrease afterward until 35Zr-Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy. The bimodal microstructure of primary α grains and 
secondary α ´ martensite ( β
trans 
region) rendered 20Zr-Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy the best comprehensive mechanical 
properties which included the tensile strength of 992 ± 11 MPa, total elongation of 17.2 ± 1% and a high strain 
hardening rate. This high strain hardening ability of 20Zr-Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy comes from the compatible 
deformation of soft primary α and hard secondary α ´ martensite phases with high density of dislocations and 
boundaries. In addition, the 20Zr-Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy shows a high elongation during tensile necking. The cor -
responding ductile fracture morphologies of TiZrAlSn alloys are also analyzed. These high-strength TiZrAlSn 
alloys could be helpful for developing novel Ti
–
Zr alloys with a high strength and ductility. 
1. Introduction 
Titanium (Ti) and its alloys are important structural materials 
broadly applied in aerospace, marine engineering, petrochemical and 
biomedical industries [ 1 – 3 ]. Specially, Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy is one of the 
oldest developed Ti alloys, which was first used as liquid-fuel engine 
components in space vehicles due to its high fracture toughness and 
good welding capacity [ 4 ]. However, the strength of this alloy is rela -
tively moderate, with a yield strength and ultimate tensile strength 
(UTS) of only 779 MPa and 827 MPa, respectively [ 5 ]. In addition, 
because there are no other β stabilizing elements except impurity ele -
ments of 0.25 – 0.5% iron (Fe), the β phase transformation temperature of 
Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy is high (~1040 
◦
C). It leads to the problematic 
formability and processability of Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy under thermo -
mechanical treatments and thus producing a high manufacturing cost 
[ 6 ]. Therefore, it is significant to develop novel high-performance Ti 
alloys utilizing the advantages of Ti-5Al-2.5Sn alloy. 
Alloy compositions can effectively influence mechanical properties 
of metal materials. In Ti alloys, Zr usually is regarded as a neutral 
element in the text book but has a stronger solution-strengthening effect 
in α phase (hexagonal close-packed lattice, HCP) than in β phase (body- 
centered cubic lattice, BCC) [ 7 ]. According to the Ti
–
Zr binary equi -
librium phase diagram [ 8 ], large amounts of Zr addition would decrease 
the β phase transformation temperature. When the atomic ratio is 
~50%, the alloy acquires the lowest β phase transformation tempera -
ture. However, excessive Zr addition in Ti alloys would promote 
martensitic products transforming from α ´ phase (HCP lattice) to α ” 
phase (orthorhombic lattice) through lattice distortions. For example, Li 
et al. [ 9 ] reported that when the Zr addition was only 20% in the TiZ -
rAlNb alloys, the phase constitutions of the alloys began to appear α ”

strengthening, deformation and fracture mechanisms in TiZrAlSn alloys 
were also discussed deeply.

In order to confirm the phase constitutions of hot-rolled TiZrAlSn 
alloys, XRD measurements were performed on the five hot-rolled TiZ -

4. Discussion 
4.1. Microstructure 
According to different Zr additions (0 – 35 wt%) of TiZrAlSn alloys, 
the phases can be divided into four groups consisting of single α , α + α ´ , 
single α ´ and α ´ + α ” + β phases. The crucial microstructural evolution for 
the TAS and 10ZTAS alloys is the hot deformation of as-cast α lamellae in 
a single α region. During hot rolling processes, the initial microstructural 
evolution of α lamellae is associated with their geometrical orientations 
relative to the normal direction. If the trace of lamellar α is parallel to the 
normal direction of rolling process, it tends to be kinked [ 24 ]. Other -
wise, the lamellar α rotates with strain towards softer orientations and 
progressively realigns its trace perpendicular to normal direction [ 25 ]. 
As the rolling strain increase, dynamic spheroidization or formation of 
equiaxed grains of lamellar α occurs. Many studies [ 26 – 28 ] indicate that 
the spheroidization of lamellar α includes three stages: the formation of 
high-energy defects, the instability of lamellar α induced by high-energy 
defects and the coarsening of split α grains. The first stage introduces 
high-energy defects by dynamic recovery and recrystallization, localized 
shearing, twinning or kinking during deformation. Deformation bands 
are found in the TAS alloys ( Fig. 3 a-b) and the strains in these regions are 
high ( Fig. 3 b-c), indicating that the density of dislocations and sub-grain 
boundaries is high. Compared to TAS alloy, 10ZTAS alloy exhibits a 
microstructure of equiaxed grains showing the excellent effect of dy -
namic recrystallization. It shows that Zr additions improve the dynamic 
spheroidization ability of TiZrAlSn alloys. One reason behind such 
improvement is the decreased prior β grain size and α lamellar size of 
cast alloys due to decreased melted point and β transformation tem -
perature affected by Zr additions. The other reason for the improvement 
in the dynamic spheroidization ability of TiZrAlSn alloys due to Zr ad -
ditions is that the possible influence of Zr additions on the dynamic 
recovery processes, an integral part of nucleation, which may affect the 
recrystallization [ 29 ]. Therefore, 10ZTAS alloy exhibits better dynamic 
recrystallization ability than TAS alloys. 
For 20ZTAS alloy, two crucial factors influence its microstructure: 
hot deformation of high-temperature α and β phases and the following 
martensitic transformation of the β phase. Compared to TAS and 10ZTAS 
alloys with a single α phase in the hot deformation process, the β phase is 
introduced in the hot deformation of 20ZTAS alloy. Due to more slipping

4.3. Work hardening behavior and ductility 
As the Zr content increases, the hot-rolled TiZrAlSn alloys exhibit 
different work-hardening behaviors and total elongations. The strength 
contribution (or the difference between yield strength and true UTS) and 
strain hardening rate ( Fig. 6 c-d) during uniform tensile deformation are 
two critical indicators that can evaluate the work-hardening behavior of 
TiZrAlSn alloys. The difference between yield strength and true UTS of 
TiZrAlSn alloys decreases first from TAS alloy to 10ZTAS alloy and then 
increases monotonously from 10ZTAS alloy to 35ZTAS alloy. Compared 
to 10ZTAS alloy with uniform equiaxed α grains, the base TAS alloy 
reveals a heterogeneous microstructure that includes equiaxed α grains 
and deformation band grains. There are more dislocations in the 
deformation band and thus this region is the harder region that can 
provide higher strength but lower deformation ability. The equiaxed 
recrystallized α grains bear more plastic deformation due to their softer 
hardness due to lower density of dislocations. As the recrystallized α 
grains are surrounded by hard deformation band grains, there is a pile- 
up of GNDs in the soft recrystallized α grains near the boundaries be -
tween recrystallized α grains and deformation band grains during uni -
form deformation. This can be illustrated by the hetero-deformation 
induced (HDI) strengthening [ 42 , 43 ]. The GNDs pile up in the soft 
recrystallized α grains and form back stress making it harder. Then an 
opposite stress would form in the hard deformation band grains induced 
by the GNDs piling up in the soft recrystallized α grains. It is named 
forward stress which can make the deformation band grains behave 
softer. Therefore, this heterogeneous microstructure leads to higher 
work hardening ability and simultaneously keep the higher uniform 
elongations. As Zr content increases to 20% in the 20ZTAS alloy, ~28% 
of the martensitic α ´ phase ( β
trans 
region) appears. Primary α grains 
demonstrate lower strength than the martensitic α ´ phase, which was 
discussed in detail in the above section of strengthening mechanisms. 
Therefore, the martensitic α ´ phase acts a second strengthening phase 
improving the yield strength, and the coordination between primary α 
phase and martensitic α ´ phase improves work hardening ability. For the 
fully martensitic 30ZTAS and 35ZTAS alloys, there are high densities of 
interfaces/boundaries and dislocations and it almost reaches the limited 
density which can induce local failures in the tensile tests. Therefore, 
these two alloys obtain a higher strain hardening rate but lower 
elongations. 
The total elongations of hot-rolled TiZrAlSn alloys increase from TAS 
alloy to 10ZTAS first and then decrease monotonously to 35ZTAS alloys. 
It is essential to divide the total elongation into uniform elongation and 
elongation during necking stage. The primary plastic deformation 
mechanism during the uniform elongation is the coordination by acti -
vation and multiplication of dislocations. The elongation during necking 
stage is mainly decided by the failure of local regions. Unlike the uni -
form elongation, the elongation during necking is related to the tensile 
sample size. The deformation and failure mechanisms during the 
necking stage are only analyzed in the same test condition. Though TAS 
alloy with heterogeneous microstructure keeps higher uniform elonga -
tion, the necking elongation is only 8%. The deformation band grains 
with a high density of dislocations possess limited deformation ability, 
which is easier to provide nucleation sites for micro-cracks. This char -
acteristic decreased the necking elongation and thus decreased the total 
elongation. Cleavage facets with lengths of 30 – 50 μ m in the fracture 
morphology of TAS alloy confirm this ( Fig. 7 a). 10ZTAS alloy displays 
the highest uniform and total elongations due to the uniform equiaxed 
grains by good dynamic recrystallization. Though the uniform elonga -
tion of 20ZTAS alloy is moderate, the total elongation of this alloy is 
high due to the higher necking elongation (~ 11.5%). The α ´ martensite 
phase ( β
trans 
region) was constrained by surrounding primary α grains. 
The α grains can prevent the growth of micro-cracks originating from the 
α ’ phase with high density of interfaces and dislocations. Therefore, like 
10ZTAS alloy, the 20ZTAS alloy exhibits similar ductile fracture with a 
high density of dimples. 
5. Conclusions 
In summary, the microstructural evolution and mechanical proper -
ties of hot-rolled TiZrAlSn alloys with different Zr content have been 
systematically investigated. The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) With the increasing Zr content in hot-rolled TiZrAlSn alloys, the β 
transformation temperature decreases monotonously from 1028 
± 5 
◦
C to 895 ± 4 
◦
C. The phase constitutions are single α phase 
(TAS and 10ZTAS alloys), α + α ´ phases (20ZTAS alloy), single α ´
phase (30ZTAS alloy) and α ´ + α ” + β phases (35ZTAS alloy), 
respectively. 
(2) The TAS alloy possesses a heterogeneous microstructure which 
includes equiaxed α grains and deformation-band grains. The 
10ZTAS alloy is made up of single equiaxed α grains. 20ZTAS 
alloy consists of equiaxed and lamellar α grains and martensitic α ´
laths. 30ZTAS and 35ZTAS alloys are complete martensitic mi -
crostructures and the α ´ lamellar thickness of 35ZTAS alloy is finer 
than that of 30ZTAS alloys. 
(3) The yield strength and UTS of TiZrAlSn alloys increase as the Zr 
content increases due to the synergistic strengthening of Zr so -
lute, grain interfaces/boundaries and dislocations. 35ZTAS alloy 
obtains the highest yield strength of 1386 ± 11 MPa, which is 
~126% higher than the TAS alloy. 
(4) The uniform elongations and total elongations of TiZrAlSn alloys 
increase from TAS alloy to 10ZTAS alloy first and then decrease 
monotonously to 35ZTAS alloy. Due to the heterogeneous 
microstructure, the TAS alloy possesses a high uniform elonga -
tion and better work-hardening ability. 20ZATS alloy obtains the 
best comprehensive mechanical properties with higher yield 
strength and total elongation.
