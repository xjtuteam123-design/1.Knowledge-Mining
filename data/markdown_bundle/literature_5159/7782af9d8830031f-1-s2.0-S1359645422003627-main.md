In this work, we designed heterogeneous (NiCoCr) 88 Al 10 Ta 2 (at.%) duplex medium-entropy alloy (MEA) 
comprising the face-centered-cubic (FCC) matrix and the B2 phase towards outstanding cryogenically 
mechanical responses. The mechanical properties of this MEA are strongly temperature-dependent, i.e. , 
when temperatures decrease from 298 down to 77 K, the yield strength, ultimate strength and tensile 
ductility are increased from ∼1.1 GPa, 1.4 GPa and 20% to ∼1.5 GPa, 1.8 GPa and 25%, respectively. In the 
studied temperature-range, the plasticity mechanisms, involving the cooperation of planar slip, stacking 
fault and twinning, are temperature-independent for this MEA, whereas the low temperature (or high 
stress) promotes stacking fault and twinning at initial plastic deformation assisted by the signiﬁcant B2- 
driven hardening. Multi-strengthening mechanisms coupled with the superior strain hardening capability 
render this alloy excellent strength-ductility synergy, especially for the 77 K stretched MEA involving 
temperature-dependent B2 cracking behavior. This unique B2 cracking behavior was uncovered for the 
ﬁrst time in multi-component alloys, rationalized in terms of the competition between the intensity of 
the stress ﬁeld ahead of the B2-crack tip and the crack-arresting capabilities of the FCC matrix at different 
temperatures.

1. Introduction 
The medium/high entropy alloys (M/HEAs) have drawn exten- 
sive attention due to their promising mechanical performances [1–
4] , particularly, the face-centered-cubic (FCC) M/HEAs manifest ex- 
ceptional mechanical responses under cryogenic conditions [5–8] . 
The coarse-grained (CG) NiCoCr MEA, as a representative FCC MEA, 
exhibits extraordinary mechanical properties at low temperatures 
due to the dominant deformation twinning (DT) [ 8 , 9 ] and occa- 
sional stress-driven transformation [10] . Moreover, the CG NiCoCr 
MEA shows strongly temperature-dependent mechanical proper- 
ties, i.e. , lower temperatures lead to higher strength, larger ductil- 
ity, and greater fracture toughness [8] . Despite the NiCoCr MEA ex- 
hibiting outstanding (ultimate tensile) strength and ductility com- 
bination, it still shows insuﬃcient yield strength ( σy , normally be- 
low 400 MPa at 298 K) [11] . In fact, from an engineering perspec- 
tive, the yield strength σy is more crucial than the ultimate tensile

permanent deformation/failure of structural materials. Thus, it is 
necessary to enhance σy while maintaining suitable ductility (or 
uniform elongation) at the same time. Thus, in this work, we aim 
to develop a novel NiCoCr-based MEA towards ultrahigh strength 
and large ductility within a wide temperature-range from ambient 
( ∼298 K) to liquid-nitrogen ( ∼77 K) temperatures. 
In general, a single strengthening mechanism is insuﬃcient to 
realize desirable σy associated with suitable ductility, thus syner- 
gistic strengthening mechanisms ( e.g. solid solution strengthening, 
precipitation strengthening, dislocation strengthening, grain/twin 
boundary (GB/TB) strengthening and heterogeneous deformation- 
induced (HDI) strengthening) were employed in some FCC M/HEAs 
to achieve their good strength-ductility balance [1] . Utilizing the 
coherent nano-L1 2 phase is one of the most promising path- 
ways to strengthen/toughen M/HEAs without notably costing their 
ductility and altering the ordinary dislocation plasticity (ODP) 
mechanism, such as FeCoCrNiTi0.2 [12] , (FeCoNi) 86 Al 7 Ti 7 (at.%) 
[13] and Al0.5Cr0.9FeNi2.5V0.2 [14] alloys, because the gliding dis- 
locations can cut across these nano-precipitates easily. On the 
other hand, Choudhuri et al. [15] engineered the FCC-B2- σ triplex 
microstructure to realize a good combination of yield strength

( σy ∼ 840 MPa) and ductility in a prototypical Al0.3CoCrFeNi HEA. 
In principle, B2 and σ precipitates in this triplex microstructure 
can act as Eshelby-type inclusion and raise locally high-stress lev- 
els during deformation to trigger DT [15] . 
As a matter of fact, DT is an important internal stress relax- 
ation mechanism during plastic deformation and is quite sensitive 
to temperatures [15] . Thus, different from the local stress concen- 
tration in M/HEAs reinforced with intermetallic, elevating the over- 
all internal stress via the HDI stress caused by the strength discrep- 
ancy between constituents in a heterogeneous material to a high 
level is another pathway to strengthen alloys with the lamellar- 
like structure for superior mechanical properties. This is because 
the long-range HDI stress accommodated by geometrically nec- 
essary dislocations (GNDs) associated with strain gradients con- 
tributes to both strength and strain hardening capability [ 11 , 16 ]. 
Previous studies [ 9 , 10 ] have shown that in the CG NiCoCr MEA 
the deformation mechanism transits from ODP to DT with increas- 
ing strain/stress levels. Moreover, our recent work [5] has veriﬁed 
that the single-phase (NiCoCr) 92 Al 6 Ta 2 (at.%) MEA plastically de- 
forms via DT at 77 K, but via ODP mainly at 298 K and above. 
Thus, it is natural to anticipate that high HDI stresses can promote 
DT during deformation to further enhance the mechanical proper- 
ties of NiCoCr-based MEA by utilizing the temperature-dependent 
dislocation-TB interactions [17] . 
Motivated by the above issues, here synergistic strengthening 
mechanisms and twinning effects were employed in the model 
NiCoCr MEA to realize the above goal via elaborate microstruc- 
tural manipulation, in terms of the chemical composition and the 
thermo-mechanical process as follows. 
(i) From the chemical composition perspective : In addition to 
enhancing the temperature-dependent lattice friction stress 
via the solid solution-strengthening in NiCoCr MEA, the 
hard-but-deformable micron-scaled B2 phase was intro- 
duced into the matrix, in that the B2 phase shows much 
stronger temperature sensitivity of the Peierls-Nabarro bar- 
riers than FCC metals [18] and would further improve the 
cryogenic ultimate tensile strength of materials. In this re- 
gard, the selected Al and Ta elements with larger atomic 
sizes were doped into the NiCoCr MEA [19] . Doping Al is to 
prefabricate the hard B2 phase in the soft FCC matrix of our 
designed NiCoCr-based alloy given the low/negative mixed 
enthalpy of Ni-Al pairs [20] (see Supplementary Table S1). 
While doping Ta could reduce the stacking fault energy (SFE, 
γ) of compositionally complex FCC alloys [ 21 , 22 ], thus en- 
hancing twinnability and the resultant mechanical proper- 
ties. Previously, based on the valence electron concentration 
(VEC) criterion [23] , we have designed a single-phase solid- 
solution hardened NiCoCr-based alloy, i.e. , (NiCoCr) 92 Al 6 Ta 2 
MEA with VEC = 8.0, with a great combination of yield 
strength and ductility[5]. The co-doping effects of Al and 
Ta on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the 
(NiCoCr) 92 Al 6 Ta 2 MEA have also been addressed in our pre- 
vious study [5] . In this work, to realize further hardening via 
the dispersed short-ﬁbrous or lath-shaped B2 phase in the 
matrix (NiCoCr) 92 Al 6 Ta 2 MEA, a duplex (NiCoCr) 88 Al 10 Ta 2 
(at.%) MEA (termed as Al10Ta2 MEA hereafter) with the 
VEC = 7.7 comprising the FCC matrix and the B2 phase was 
designed by adjusting the Al content. Consequently, we can 
investigate the roles of the hard-but-deformable B2 phase 
played in the mechanical response of our designed duplex 
NiCoCr-based alloy as well as the deformation mechanism(s) 
of the matrix under the strong constraint condition. Note 
that although there are numerous studies to enhance σy of 
NiCoCr MEA by Al-alloying, their main mechanism is shear- 
ing or bypassing Al-contained precipitates via the Orowan 
strengthening manner [24–26] rather than the HDI strength- 
ening in terms of the ﬁbrous B2 inclusions as our designed 
alloy in this work. 
(ii) From a thermo-mechanical processing perspective : We fur- 
ther intentionally employ the dislocation strengthening and 
GB strengthening via heavily hot-rolling at 1150 ◦C in the 
present Al10Ta2 MEA. On the one hand, a previous study 
[27] has shown that the high dislocation density in steels 
enhances both σy and ductility by the forest dislocations 
strengthening and the slip of mobile dislocations, respec- 
tively. Thus, the simple hot-rolling with subsequent water 
quenching (WQ) instead of cold-rolling was applied to in- 
troduce a suitable density of dislocations but without severe 
dislocation tangling in our alloy. Concomitantly, we adjust 
suitable grain size via controlling the hot-rolling time to har- 
vest GB strengthening. In particular, hot-rolling can enhance 
the deformability of the hard B2 inclusions to make the 
granular B2 blocks transform into the ﬁbrous B2-laths, given 
the advantage of the hierarchically lamellar (like) structure 
for mechanical properties of alloys [ 28 , 29 ]. Therefore, the 
simple yet eﬃcient hot-rolling method combining compo- 
sition design confers a dual-heterogeneity structure for this 
Al10Ta2 alloy, i.e. , soft FCC + hard B2 mechanically unites 
equiaxed FCC grains + lath-shaped B2 phase morphologi- 
cally, which naturally allows pronounced HDI strengthening 
effect in this alloy. 
Based on the elaborate microstructural design, it is normally 
anticipated that the heterogeneous twinning-assisted Al10Ta2 MEA 
would manifest ultrahigh yield strength and good ductility. At the 
same time, a critical question naturally arises: how the tempera- 
ture and B2 phase inﬂuences the mechanical response, microstruc- 
tural evolution and damage/failure behavior in the Al10Ta2 MEA? 
Therefore, the cryogenically mechanical behavior of this Al10Ta2 
MEA was investigated, uncovering the temperature-dependent 
cracking behavior for the ﬁrst time, as rationalized in terms 
of the temperature-dependent crack-arresting capabilities of the 
matrix. 
2. Experimental material and procedures 
2.1. Materials processing 
The Al10Ta2 MEA was prepared by arc-melting technique 
under a pure Ar atmosphere. The purity for each element is above 
99.95%, and the ingots were re-melted ﬁve times with electromag- 
netic stirring to avoid chemical non-homogeneity. Then the ingots 
with a dimension of 80 × 25 × 15 mm 3 were subjected to the 
homogenization treatment (HT) at 1225 ◦C for 24 h followed by 
WQ. Subsequently, these HT samples were directly hot-rolled (HR) 
at 1150 ◦C to sheets by multi-pass with a thickness reduction of 
∼70% followed by WQ to retain a suitable density of dislocations 
in the fully recrystallized matrix, particularly, making the blocky 
B2 phase turn to lath-shaped one. To optimize the grain size of 
the FCC matrix for acquiring suitable GB strengthening, the overall 
time for hot-rolling is 10 - 12 min. The thermo-mechanical 
processing and microstructure were schematically shown 
in Fig. 1 .

X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker D8 Discover powder) with a scan- 
ning angle from 20 to 100 °. Also, the crystallographic feature was 
characterized by the electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) in- 
stalled on the scanning electron microscope (SEM) of Zeiss Su-

2.3. Mechanical testing 
Flat dog-bone specimens of Al10Ta2 MEA with a gauge length 
of 15 mm, a width of 4.5 mm and 1.5 mm in thickness were trun- 
cated from the HR sheets along the rolling direction (RD). Uni- 
axial tensile tests, at both ambient (298 K) and liquid-nitrogen 
(77 K) temperatures, were performed on an Instron 3699 tensile 
machine under the strain rate of 1 × 10 −3 s −1 . Especially, the 
specimens were immersed in liquid nitrogen and stabilized for 
20 min before the cryogenic tension testing. For the tensile test- 
ing at 298 K, an extensometer was employed to directly monitor 
the strains, while for the testing at 77 K, strains were determined 
indirectly from the crosshead displacement with a correction fac- 
tor for calibration and were further conﬁrmed by measuring the 
change of the gauge length of samples before and after testing. 
This treatment was widely adopted in previous studies and the de- 
tails can be referenced to literatures [30–33] . All the tensile tests 
were repeated at least 3 times for each condition to guarantee 
reliability. 
3. Results 
3.1. Microstructural evolution with processing steps 
Fig. 2 shows the microstructural evolution with processing steps 
after HT and HR for the present Al10Ta2 MEA. The HR Al10Ta2 
MEA has the duplex microstructure, i.e. , the dispersed B2-islands 
( ∼10.4 vol.%) with dozens of micron in several hundred microns 
even millimetric FCC grains ( ∼89.6 vol.%), see Fig. 2 a1 - 2 a3. Also, 
the duplex structure was conﬁrmed by the XRD pattern (Fig. S1). 
As expected, the microstructure of HR samples along the TD view 
is characterized by the lath-shaped B2 phase uniting equiaxed FCC 
grains ( Fig. 2 b). In other words, the HR process turns the dis- 
persed blocky B2 islands with the average aspect ratio ( ω ) ∼1.7 
into aligned ﬁbrous B2 laths with ω ∼9.8 ( Fig. 2 c and 2 d), although 
the B2 morphology along the normal direction (ND) shows little 
change compared with HT specimen (not shown here). The change 
of B2 morphology is indicative of its good plastic deformability 
without cracking during hot-rolling. The HR process results in the 
reﬁned microstructure of the FCC matrix, featured by randomly 
oriented equiaxed grains with the average grain size d on the or- 
der of 5.9 μm (including TBs) by measuring more than 10 0 0 grains. 
In addition, abundant microtwins with the fraction of TBs ∼25.1% 
along with the average twin thickness λ ∼1.0 μm were observed 
in the matrix (indicated by arrows in Fig. 2 e), indicating a rel- 
atively low SFE of the matrix. The above results indicate our al- 
loy design strategy engineering a dual-heterogeneous structure in 
this Al10Ta2 MEA, i.e. , mechanically the soft FCC matrix combining 
hard B2-phase unites morphologically equiaxed FCC grains com- 
bining elongated B2-laths. Moreover, the nanohardness of the B2 
phase is ∼7.5 GPa, while that of the FCC grains is ∼5.1 GPa (see 
Supplementary Section 1 ). In this regard, the strength discrepancy 
between the matrix and B2 phase would cause strain incompatibil- 
ity so as to trigger the generation of massive GNDs in the soft ma- 
trix around the hard B2 phase to accommodate the strain gradient 
(see Fig. 3 below), thus leading to pronounced HDI strengthening 
[16] . 
Further TEM observations were performed on HT and HR 
Al10Ta2 MEA before tension. For the HT sample, there are some 
dislocations with a low density inside the FCC matrix ( Fig. 3 a). The 
selected area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns and the high- 
resolution TEM (HRTEM) images for the FCC matrix along the 
[011] zone axis and the B2 phase along the [001] zone axis were 
presented in Fig. 3 a1 and 3 a2, respectively, conﬁrming the or- 
dered cubic nature of the B2 phase. The feature of atomic res- 
olution B2/matrix phase boundary (PB), as well as the corre- 
sponding fast Fourier transform (FFT) patterns for different re- 
gions, are presented in Fig. 3 b, revealing the existence of interfa- 
cial lattice misﬁt dislocations (inset of Fig. 3 b). Additionally, FCC 
and B2 phases share a Kurdjumov-Sachs (K-S) orientation rela- 
tionship, which was reported in some duplex M/HEAs comprising 
FCC and B2 phases [ 15 , 34 , 35 ]. In this case, the lattice misﬁt be- 
tween FCC and B2 phases is calculated by δ = /Delta1a/ ¯a = 0 . 22 based 
on the lattice constant obtained by the XRD result (a FCC = 0.3593 
nm and a B2 = 0.2875 nm), agreeing well with the dual-phase 
Al0.7CoCrFeNi HEA [36] . Moreover, the SEM-EDS line proﬁle shows 
that the B2 phase is enriched in Al and Ni ( Fig. 3 c). The actual con- 
tents of Al and Ni elements with high SFE in the FCC matrix were 
lower than the nominal compositions caused by elemental parti- 
tioning, quantitated by TEM-EDS ( Table 1 ), which will lower the 
SFE of the matrix thus favoring twin formation [37] .

3.2. Mechanical responses and strain-hardening behavior 
Fig. 4 a displays the engineering stress-strain curves of HR 
Al10Ta2 MEA tested at 298 and 77 K, showing the mechanical 
properties including σy , σUTS and total elongation ( εT ). Given the 
composition of the FCC matrix is close to (NiCoCr) 92 Al 6 Ta 2 [19] in 
the Al10Ta2 MEA, its tensile curve at 298 K and those of the 
model CG NiCoCr MEA [19] and the HT Al10Ta2 MEA are pre- 
sented in Fig. 4 a for comparison. Apparently, this designed Al10Ta2 
MEA at 298 K with ultrahigh σy as well as σUTS is generally much 
stronger than those referenced alloys, simultaneously, maintaining 
large ductility. When the testing temperature drops from 298 to 77 
K, σy of HR sample is improved from 1.08 ± 0.03 to 1.45 ± 0.05 
GPa, and σUTS is increased from 1.38 ± 0.03 to 1.83 ± 0.05 GPa, 
along with the εT increased from ∼20% to 25%. This temperature- 
dependent mechanical response was analogous to other reported 
FCC M/HEAs [ 5 , 6 , 8 ]. It seems that this Al10Ta2 MEA exhibits a 
monotonically reduced strain hardening rate (SHR, θ) at both tem- 
peratures, as shown in Fig. 4 b. Compared with the strain hardening 
behavior at 298 K, the HR Al10Ta2 MEA manifests superior strain- 
hardening capability at 77 K with much higher θ and larger uni- 
form elongation ( εu ) ∼18.8%. 
Next, we compare the mechanical properties of this duplex 
Al10Ta2 MEA, in terms of ( Fig. 4 c1) σy vs . εT , ( Fig. 4 c2) σUTS 
vs . εT and ( Fig. 4 c3) σy vs. σUTS , at 298 K with some reported 
FCC-based M/HEAs reinforced by secondary-phase particles (such 
as B2, laves, μ, σ and ε precipitates [ 33 , 38–45 ]) and the typical 
lamellar AlCoCrFeNi2.1 HEA comprising FCC + B2 phase [46] . It ap- 
pears that our heterostructured Al10Ta2 MEA not only has higher 
strength ( σy and σUTS ) than those of most reported alloys but also 
shows an excellent strength-ductility combination at 298 K. Fur- 
thermore, the Al10Ta2 MEA shows the highest cryogenic strength 
( σy and σUTS ), superior to most of FCC-based M/HEAs reinforced 
by micron- and/or nano-particles [ 8 , 12 , 24 , 33 , 46–59 ]. Particularly, 
the extraordinary strength-ductility combination sets our Al10Ta2 
MEA tested at 77 K apart from some typical advanced traditional 
alloys for cryogenic applications, such as TWIP/TRIP steels [ 63 , 64 ], 
high-Ni steels [62] , 316L and AISI 304 SS [ 60 , 61 ], which gener- 
ally have either low strength or low ductility (or both low), see 
Fig. 4 d. The above results show our designed ultrastrong yet ductile 
Al10Ta2 MEA not only embraces distinguished mechanical proper- 
ties in a wide temperature range guaranteeing a great engineering 
reliability margin but has promising potential for advanced struc- 
tural material candidates under cryogenic service conditions. 
3.3. Deformed substructural evolution 
To illustrate the underlying deformation mechanisms and mi- 
crostructural origins of different strain hardening responses for the 
present Al10Ta2 MEA at 298 and 77 K, the microstructural evo- 
lution upon different interrupted strains was investigated by TEM 
observations. Here, deformed substructures were examined at the 
true strain ε of ∼5, 10 and 16.1% (fractured) for 298 K samples, 
while at ∼5, 12, and 23.1% (fractured) for 77 K samples, as shown 
in Figs. 5 and 6 , respectively. 
For the ε = 5% stretched Al10Ta2 sample at 298 K ( Fig. 5 a1 - 
5 a4), dislocation density evidently increases relative to the unde- 
formed HR sample and the plasticity is mainly mediated by pla- 
nar dislocation slip on the {111} system ( Fig. 5 a1). In addition, 
some SFs can be observed ( Fig. 5 a2 and 5 a4). Meantime, the exten- 
sive (partial) dislocation-TB interactions were discernable along the 
[011] zone axis ( Fig. 5 a2). The stepped TB decorated with multi-SFs 
with a width of ∼2.7 nm ( Fig. 5 a2 and 5 a3) implies the dissocia- 
tion and transmission of glissile dislocations on/across TBs, which 
favors pronounced strain hardening and strength. With increasing 
strains to ε = 10%, a higher density of SFs nano-bundles with a 
spacing of 20 - 60 nm ( Fig. 5 b1 and 5 b2) were formed. Apart from 
the multi-SFs, particularly, plenty of deformation nanotwins with 
the thickness λ in the range of 3 - 35 nm emerged ( Fig. 5 b3 and 
5 b4), which would result in sustainable strain hardening and duc- 
tility [ 5 , 65 ]. Moreover, it appears that some nanotwins and SFs 
primarily emanated in the vicinity of B2 inclusions while termi- 
nated in the grain interior, as evidenced in Figs. 5 b3, 5 b4 and 
S3. This indicated that the B2 phase could act as the stress raiser 
thereby promote twinning in the low SFE matrix. For the fractured 
sample, prevalent nanotwins emitted from the B2/matrix interface 
( Fig. 5 c1) as well as SFs and their interactions ( Fig. 5 c2) were also 
discerned along the [011] zone axis near the B2 phase. For these 
B2 inclusions, their plastic deformation took place via ODP at 298 
K, associated with a much higher density of dislocations inside B2 
interiors compared to the undeformed HR sample ( Fig. 5 c3). 
Similarly, identical deformation products ( e.g. dislocations, SFs 
and nanotwins) but with more complex substructures were gener- 
ated in the cryogenically stretched sample compared with the 298 
K counterpart, as shown in Fig. 6 . With increasing the plastic strain 
ε = 5% ( Fig. 6 a1 - 6 a3) to ε = 12% ( Fig. 6 b1 - 6 b3) even to fracture 
strain ε = 23.1% ( Fig. 6 c1 - 6 c3), the densities/activities of both 
SFs and nanotwins increase, revealing high stress levels promote 
SF/DT-mediated plasticity at low temperatures. Speciﬁcally, DT oc- 
curs earlier (at ε = 5%) than that in the 298 K stretched sample 
( Fig. 6 ), which might be due to the lower SFE at lower tempera- 
tures along with higher stresses [ 30 , 66 ]. Furthermore, in addition 
to the TBs decorated with multilayered-SFs and the prevalence of 
deformation nanotwins ( Fig. 6 b1 and 6 b3), plenty of nano-sized 
SF-networks emerged in the matrix ( Fig. 6 b2) at medium strains. 
For the failure sample, dense parallel single nanotwins ( Fig. 6 c1), 
even the multiple-twins emitted from the B2/matrix interface were 
observed ( Fig. 6 c2). Obviously, intragranular cracking of B2 parti- 
cles rather than interfacial debonding occurred in the 77 K de- 
formed sample, implying the B2/matrix interface with high inter- 
facial adhesion ( Fig. 6 c3). 
3.4. Damage and fracture behavior

4. Discussion 
4.1. Twinning behavior in the present duplex Al10Ta2 MEA 
Above TEM observations for the present Al10Ta2 MEA conﬁrm 
the plasticity mechanisms of the FCC matrix alloy involves ordi- 
nary (full) dislocation slip, SFs/twinning mediated mechanism in a 
wide temperature range from 77 to 298 K, whereas the plasticity is 
only mediated by ODP for the multicomponent B2 phase. Here, the 
appearance of SFs and deformation nanotwins in the 298 K sam- 
ple indicates our Al10Ta2 MEA has relatively low SFE [65] . Indeed, 
our alloy design strategy by tailoring the formation of Ni/Al-riched 
B2 particles modiﬁed the actual composition of the FCC matrix, as 
veriﬁed by the Ni and Al elements (with high SFE) depletion in the 
FCC phase ( Fig. 3 c). Speciﬁcally, the average Al content in the ma- 
trix decreased by ∼36% from the nominal 10.0 at% to the current 
6.4 at% ( Table. 1 ). This elemental partitioning between the matrix 
and B2 particles could notably reduce the SFE of the matrix (in 
the range of ∼30 - 34 mJ/m 2 [19] ) in our designed Al10Ta2 MEA, 
which promotes the partial dislocation-mediated process to gen- 
erate SFs and deformation nanotwins in the matrix. This is simi- 
lar to the ﬁndings in reported Ni/Al-contained FCC HEAs [ 37 , 70 ]. 
Speciﬁcally, compared with the Al10Ta2 MEA deformed at 298 K, 
these DTs emerge at a smaller strain in the stretched sample at 
77 K. This is probably caused by lower SFE at lower temperatures 
[ 9,66,71 ] along with higher applied stresses.

4.3. Origins of the temperature-dependent strain hardenability 
(uniform elongation) 
The Al10Ta2 MEA shows a good strain hardening capability at 
both temperatures, which determines the uniform elongation of a 
material. A lower temperature leads to a higher SHR θ. Here, we 
uncover the origins of the temperature-dependent SHR and uni- 
form plasticity of Al10Ta2 MEA in terms of microstructural fea- 
tures. 
4.3.1. The mechanisms of temperature-dependent strain hardening 
behavior 
In fact, the strain hardening capability of a material is highly 
determined by the interactions between dislocations and deforma- 
tion products. Apart from dislocations, more SFs and nanotwins, 
in particular the SF-networks and multiple-nanotwins, emerge in 
the stretched Al10Ta2 MEA at 77 K. Furthermore, deformation nan- 
otwins appear at a smaller strain and over a more extended strain

Thus, the superior strain hardening capacity of the cryogenically 
deformed Al10Ta2 MEA can be rationalized by the following fac- 
tors. 
(i) SFs : Often, SFs can eﬃciently hinder gliding dislocations on 
a slip plane cutting across SFs [12] . These SFs in a high den- 
sity are beneﬁcial to the ductility, in that these glissile dis-

SF-networks favorably formed at low temperatures facilitate 
remarkable microstructural reﬁnement, leading to a notably 
dynamic (H-P) hardening effect and thus enhanced ductility 
[ 57 , 65 ]. 
(ii) Nanotwins: In fact, deformation nanotwins could introduce 
extra interfaces thereby notably reducing the dislocation 
mean free path, associated with the dynamic H-P effect 
[ 65 , 82 ]. Also, those extra TBs could provide strong resistance 
for dislocation motion so as to gather other dislocations, and 
their subsequent interactions further result in the multipli- 
cation of defects [83] . Obviously, the above twinning-related 
processes signiﬁcantly contribute to the enhanced strain- 
hardening capacity of Al10Ta2 MEA at both temperatures. 
In particular, twinning at smaller strains confers more sus- 
tainable strain hardening in the 77 K stretched sample [9] . 
Meantime, compared with single twins, multiple-twins ob- 
served in the 77 K sample can further lead to more pro- 
nounced strain-hardenability of materials [ 84 , 85 ]. 
4.3.2. Mechanisms of the temperature-dependent tensile ductility 
In this work, we uncovered that a lower temperature leads 
to larger tensile ductility (uniform elongation) in our designed 
Al10Ta2 MEA, similar to reported FCC-based H/MEAs [ 6 , 8 , 12 ]. There 
are three possible reasons for the temperature-dependent ductil- 
ity summarized below. (i) As mentioned above, earlier twinning 
and more defect interactions render much higher strain harden- 
ability in lower temperatures deformed samples. The temperature- 
dependent SHR is one of the most important factors that guar- 
antee the high uniform elongation of the 77 K sample. (ii) Previ- 
ous atomic simulations [17] uncovered the temperature-dependent 
dislocation-TB interactions in twinned FCC alloys, namely, gliding 
dislocations are more preferred to transmit into twin-lamella by 
cutting across TBs rather than to be absorbed by TBs at lower 
temperatures. This dislocation-TB interaction contributes to the in- 
creased ductility of the 77 K sample associated with more nan- 
otwins at low temperatures and profuse microtwins in our Al10Ta2 
MEA, since this dislocation transmission process accommodates 
dislocation motion and thus renders greater plastic deformation 
[17] . (iii) The temperature-dependent cracking behavior in this 
Al10Ta2 MEA shows that B2-cracks are more preferred to render 
localized (necking) damage of the matrix and subsequent fracture 
in the 298 K sample. However, at low temperatures, the strength, 
ductility and strain hardenability of the matrix are strongly en- 
hanced, leading to the superior crack-arresting capability. In this 
regard, although more microcracks initiated in the uniform elon- 
gation region in 77 K sample, the propagation of B2-cracks can 
be strongly suppressed, which delays the premature necking insta- 
bility thus renders enhanced uniform elongation (see Sections 3.4 
and 4.4 ).

B2 phase-related strengthening, plasticity mechanisms, strain- 
hardenability and fracture behavior of Al10Ta2 MEA at 298 and 77 
K were thoroughly investigated. The main conclusions are summa- 
rized as follows. 
(1) This novel hetero-structured duplex Al10Ta2 MEA exhibits 
strongly temperature-dependent mechanical properties, i.e. , 
as the testing temperature decreases from 298 to 77 K, their 
σy and σUTS are increased from ∼1.1 to 1.5 GPa and from 
∼1.4 to ∼1.8 GPa, respectively, along with an apparent duc- 
tility improvement from ∼20% to ∼25%. The ultrahigh σy of 
this alloy arises from the synergistic strengthening, in par- 
ticular the critical role of the lath-shaped B2 phase. 
(2) For the present Al10Ta2 MEA deformed at both tempera- 
tures, the plasticity mechanism of the FCC matrix involves 
cooperative planar-slip, stacking fault and nanotwinning. The 
outstanding strength-ductility synergy and good strain hard- 
enability in this MEA deformed at 77 K were realized via the 
synergy of multiple mechanisms, in particular the B2-driven 
twinning at earlier plastic deformation and the temperature- 
dependent TB-dislocation interactions. 
(3) The unique temperature-dependent fracture behavior uncov- 
ered in this Al10Ta2 MEA originates from the temperature- 
dependent activities/interactions of SFs in the region nearby 
the crack-tip, endowing a superior crack-arrest capability 
and damage tolerance of the Al10Ta2 MEA for cryogenic ap- 
plications.
