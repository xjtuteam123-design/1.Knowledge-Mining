Abstract: Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are promisin g high-temperature 
structural materials due to their high melting poin t and extraordinarily high yield 
strength. However, their industrial application is greatly restricted due to their limited 
room-temperature ductility. In the present investig ation, a ductile and strong 
single-phase NbTaTiV RHEA was synthesized by powder metallurgy method. Effects 
of the sintering temperature on the phase formation , microstructural evolution and the 
mechanical properties of the NbTaTiV RHEA were char acterized. The results show 
that the NbTaTiV RHEA sintered at 1700 oC has an equiaxed single bcc phase 
microstructure, no obvious porosity and composition al segregation can be observed. 
The alloy exhibits a relatively high hardness of 51 0 HV, yield strength of 1.37 GPa, 
and compressive fracture strength of 2.19 GPa with a high fracture strain of 23 % at 
room temperature. Typical strain softening and stea dy state flow occur during 
compressive deformation at high temperatures. Durin g deformation at 1000 oC, the 
alloy still exhibits high yield strength of 437 MPa with a compression strain over than 
40 %. The outstanding mechanical properties is main ly attributed to the homogeneous 
and fine microstructures, and solid solution streng thening effect. It can be concluded 
that the powder metallurgy is a promising way for p reparing ductile RHEAs with 
outstanding comprehensive mechanical properties. 
Keywords: Refractory high-entropy alloys, powder metallurgy, microstructure, 
mechanical properties.

1. Introduction 
The continuing development of advanced gas turbine aeroengines requires 
structural materials capable of withstanding increa sing operating temperatures and 
mechanical loads. Nowadays, the maximum turbine inl et temperature in practice in 
advanced turbines has been rising up to 1600 oC [1], which exceeds the melting points 
of most of the traditional Ni-based superalloys [2] . The limited service temperatures 
of Ni-based superalloys make the further improvemen t of turbine e ﬃciency to be 
extremely di ﬃcult. Therefore, the development of excellent high- temperature 
structural materials is imminent, and it is expecte d to be an alternative material for 
Ni-based superalloys at higher temperatures. 
A recently proposed concept of high-entropy alloys (HEAs), consisting more than 
four principal metallic elements, have received wid e attention due to their unique 
structures and excellent mechanical properties. It is reported that these alloys have 
very high entropy of mixing, which makes HEA has a high tendency to form solid 
solutions instead of intermetallic compounds [3]. F urthermore, these reported 
outstanding physical and mechanical properties also is claimed to be attributed to the 
four core effects of HEAs, including high configura tional entropy, sluggish diffusion, 
severe lattice distortion, and cock-tail effects. B ased on the design concept of high 
entropy alloys, by selecting refractory alloy elements (including group IV (Ti, Zr, Hf), 
V(V, Nb, Ta), and VI (Cr, Mo, W)) as the components , these developed refractory 
HEAs (RHEAs) generally possess high melting tempera ture, outstanding 
room-temperature and high temperature mechanical pr operties, excellent thermal 
stability, which opens up the possibilities in the alloy developments satisfying the 
above demands[4]. 
Numerous recent researches about the RHEAs are orig inated from the MoNbTaW 
and MoNbTaVW HEAs proposed by O.N. Senkov et al. at 2010 [5, 6]. It is reported 
that the BCC MoNbTaW and MoNbTaVW HEAs maintained y ield strengths of 405 
and 477 MPa at 1600 oC respectively, which is noticeably higher than tha t of Inconel 
718 and Haynes 230[6]. Furthermore, Yu Zou et al. [ 7] reported that the MoNbTaW 
thin films also showed substantially enhanced therm al stability for high temperature, 
long-durations conditions (at 1100 oC for 3 days). Unfortunately, these RHEAs are 
generally exhibit high density and brittle at room temperature, fail by cracking at low

compressive strains and suffer from limited formabi lity. These shortcomings have 
become the current bottleneck for industrial applic ations of RHEAs as structural 
materials. In order to enhance their ductility and reduce their strikingly high density, 
Saad Sheikh et al.[8] reported that decreasing the average valence electron numbers, 
or valence electron concentrations, was effective i n ductilizing refractory alloys. As 
reported, group VI (Cr, Mo and W) refractory metals exhibited well-known room 
temperature embrittlement [9, 10], while alloying t he group VI or V (V, Nb, Ta) 
transition metals with group IV (Ti, Zr, Hf) metals can transform these RHEAs into 
intrinsically ductile materials. Therefore, a serie s of BCC HEAs have been designed 
with the additions of lighter elements, such as Ti and Zr, yielding compositions such 
as HfNbTaTiZr [8], NbTaTiV [11], HfMoNbTiZr [12], N bTaTiVW[11] and 
MoNbTaV [13], etc. One exception is the TaNbTiV all oy, which has a high melting 
temperature of 2275 oC[14] and a plasticity of over 50% at room temperat ure[11]. 
However, the yield strength of NbTaTiV is only 965 MPa at room temperature and its 
high temperature strength is insufﬁcient [11]. 
It is well known that the strength of a material is not only significantly influenced 
by their chemical composition, but also inseparable from their microstructure. As 
reported in the literatures, most of the reported H EAs are prepared by the casting 
route [3, 4]. These as-casted RHEAs generally exhib it coarse dendritic structures. In 
addition, component segregation is also difficult t o avoid in these as-cast alloys. 
These intrinsic processing problems are great harm to the mechanical properties of 
HEAs. In order to solve these problems, resent work s [15-17] showed that HEA can 
be prepared by powder metallurgy (P/M) technology, including mechanical alloying 
(MA) and subsequent consolidation process, such as spark plasma sintering (SPS), 
and hot pressing. During the spark plasma sintering (SPS) process, powders can be 
melted and consolidation in few minutes to tens of minutes. Therefore, the grain 
growth can be significantly suppressed. In the prev ious research [15], we prepared 
CoCrFeNi HEA by the P/M process. The alloy exhibite d a high yield strength of 359 
MPa and ultimate strength of 712.5 MPa, which was a lmost 117% and 78% higher 
than that of the as-cast alloy, without causing any embrittlement. The P/M process is 
proved to be an effective approach for the synthesi s of materials with uniform fine 
microstructures and superior compositional homogeneity.

2. Experimental procedures 
High purity (>99.5 wt.%) Nb, Ta, Ti and V powders w ere used in this study. The 
powders in equiatomic composition (Nb:Ta:Ti:V=1:1:1 :1) was ball milled in a 
tungsten carbide vial with argon atmosphere for 12 h. The ball-to-powder mass ratio 
was 10:1 and milling speed was 250 rpm. After milli ng, the mixed NbTaTiV powders 
were consolidated through spark plasma sintering (S PS) ( FCT HP D 25/3, German). 
The sintering temperatures were 1500, 1600, and 170 0 °C, respectively. The holding 
time was 30 minutes and the pressure was 30 MPa. Fi nally, the sample was cooled by 
a circulating water cooling device. 
Cylindrical specimens ( φ6×9 mm) were cut from the as-sintered NbTaTiV RHEA 
for the compression tests. An Instron-3369 and Glee ble-3180 devices were employed 
to accomplish the room temperature and high tempera ture compressive tests 
respectively. The strain rate was 10 −3 s−1 at all cases. The representative data for each 
condition were obtained by averaging three values o f the test results. Room 
temperature compression tests were performed until the fracture of the specimen. 
High temperature compression tests were carried out at 700 °C, 800 °C, 900 °C, and 
1000 °C, respectively. Before the high temperature compression tests, these 
specimens were heated to the set temperature at 10 °C/s and then held for 5 min. High 
temperature compressive tests were performed to a c ompressive engineering strain of 
40 %. 
Particle size distribution of the raw powders and a s-mixed powders was 
characterized by a laser particle size analyzer (MI CRO-PLUS, MALVERN). 
Interstitial impurities (C, H, and O elements) in t he raw powders and mixed powders 
were determined using a LECO TCH 600 analyzer. The microstructure of these 
specimens were characterized by field emission scan ning electron microscope 
(FESEM, JSM-6360LV , Japan), equipped with the energ y dispersive spectrometer

addition, it was found in our previous work that in terstitial element O (~720 ppm) in 
CoCrFeNi HEA also caused a significant increase of yield strength (>100 MPa) [15]. 
In this study, the oxygen content (0.48 wt. %) is m uch higher than that in Ref. [15]. 
Therefore, solid solution strengthening caused by t he interstitial atoms (C and O) can 
also be an important strengthening mechanism for the P/M NbTaTiV RHEA. 
 
5. Conclusions 
NbTaTiV RHEA was synthesized through P/M method. Th e effects of the sintering 
temperature on the phase formation, microstructural evolution and the mechanical 
properties of the NbTaTiV alloys were characterized , the following conclusions are 
drawn: 
(1) Fine and homogeneous microstructure can be obta ined in the NbTaTiV RHEA 
through P/M method. The average grain sizes of thes e NbTaTiV RHEA sintered at 
1500 oC, 1600 oC and 1700 oC are approximately 24 µm, 30 µm and 38 µm, 
respectively. As the sintering temperature increase d up to 1700 oC, no obvious 
porosity and compositional segregation is observed. 
(2) The NbTaTiV RHEA sintered at 1700 oC exhibits a hardness of 510 HV, yield 
strength of 1.37 Gpa, and compressive fracture stre ngth of 2.19 Gpa with a fracture 
strain of 23% at room temperature. Typical strain s oftening and steady state flow 
occur during compressive deformation at high temper atures. During deformation at 
1000 oC, the alloy still exhibits high yield strength of 437 MPa with a compression 
strain over than 40 %.
