Ab Initio Modeling of Bulk and Intragranular Di ﬀusion in Ni Alloys
Vitaly Alexandrov, * Maria L. Sushko, Daniel K. Schreiber, Stephen M. Bruemmer, and Kevin M. Rosso
Paciﬁc Northwest National Laboratory, 902 Battelle Boulevard, Richland, Washington 99354, United States
ABSTRACT: Knowledge of solid-state and interfacial species di ﬀusion kinetics is of paramount
importance for understanding mechanisms of grain boundary (GB) oxidation causing
environmental degradation and cracking of Ni-base structural alloys. In this study, ﬁrst-principles
calculations of vacancy-mediated di ﬀusion are performed across a wide series of alloying elements
commonly used in Ni-based superalloys, as well as interstitial di ﬀusion of atomic oxygen and
sulfur in the bulk, at the (111) surface, ⟨110⟩ symmetric tilt GBs of Ni corresponding to model
low- (Σ = 3/(111)) and high-energy ( Σ = 9/(221)) GBs. A substantial enhancement of di ﬀusion
is found for all species at the high-energy GB as compared with the bulk and the low-energy GB,
with Cr, Mn, and Ti exhibiting remarkably small activation barriers (<0.1 eV; ∼10 times lower
than in the bulk). Calculations also show that the bulk di ﬀusion mechanism and kinetics di ﬀer for
oxygen and sulfur, with oxygen having a faster mobility and preferentially di ﬀusing through the
tetrahedral interstitial sites in Ni matrix, where it can be trapped in a local minimum.
N
ickel-base alloys are used ubiquitously in advanced energy
systems as structural materials able to withstand extreme
conditions involving chemically reactive liquid and gaseous
environments, high temperature and pressure, mechanical
stress, strain and loading, radiation, as well as dramatic changes
in operational conditions. 1−4 These alloys, however, are often
inherently susceptible to localized corrosion and cracking that
substantially limit their performance and operational lifetime in
many technological applications including nuclear reactors,
power-generation turbines, aircraft, and rocket engines. 3−6
A series of recent high-resolution measurements involving
analytical transmission electron microscopy (ATEM) and atom
probe tomography (APT) at buried crack tips of Ni alloys
provided nanometer-scale characterizations of crack-tip struc-
tures and compositions giving critical atomistic insights into
material degradation processes. 5−14 These studies revealed
selective grain boundary oxidation driving intergranular stress
corrosion cracking (SCC) of Ni-base alloys with substantial
compositional and structural changes at the metal grain
boundary leading the crack tip even at low temperatures. In
the past, a number of processes including internal oxidation,
corrosion-enhanced plasticity, and creep have been proposed as
the leading mechanisms of SCC in Ni-base alloys; 15−17
however, until now they have failed to explain all of the
accumulated experimental observations. Also, examinations of
the Ni-base alloy crack-tips have revealed some conceptual
problems with the classical picture of crack-tip continuum
mechanics and electrochemistry. 18 This fundamental disparity
along with the complex nature of multicomponent alloy
compositions, environmental conditions, and characteristics of
the grain boundaries calls for the application of modeling
techniques to enable mechanistic interpretations of the
observed processes.
Recently, analytic models of oxidation kinetics in the
framework of Wagner ’s theory for thick ﬁlm growth have
been applied to relate the rates of oxidation to elemental solid-
state di ﬀusivities in Ni-base binary alloys. 16−21 These models
have provided reasonable predictions for the velocity of an
oxidation front propagating along a boundary in a homoge-
neous binary alloy for the case of planar surface oxidation as
well as the depletion distance of minor elements ahead of the
advancing front. These models, however, are limited in their
capability to incorporate structural heterogeneity due to the
presence of various grain boundaries that are known to play a
crucial role in de ﬁning oxidation kinetics of real alloys and thus
cannot help unravel the microscopic picture of oxidation
mechanisms. In this regard, ﬁrst-principles-based parameter-
free atomistic simulations o ﬀer a way to model reaction and
transport processes by considering elementary steps of species
diﬀusion in both bulk matrix and at experimentally observed
GBs. Some atomistic insights using ab initio techniques have
been recently put forward for point defect energetics,
mechanisms, and kinetics of species di ﬀusion in bulk Ni and
the e ﬀect of thermal expansion on the di ﬀusion process.
22−29
Most previous ﬁrst-principles studies concerning species
diﬀusion were nevertheless restricted to the Ni bulk phase
and only considered a small number of minor elements such as
O,
22,23 Cr, and Fe; 25 however, a large body of experimental
evidence suggests that interfacial reactions and species transport
occurring particularly at the high-energy GBs play a dominant
role in deﬁning oxidation and degradation of Ni-base alloys. 30,31
Furthermore, examining a systematic series of minor elements
could be bene ﬁcial for understanding atomic and electronic
structure e ﬀects underpinning calculated di ﬀusion activation
energies.

All DFT calculations were carried out with the VASP
code
34,35 employing the generalized gradient approximation
(GGA) in conjunction with the Perdew −Burke−Ernzerhof
(PBE) exchange-correlation functional 36 and the projector-
augmented wave (PAW) method. 37 Bulk calculations were
performed using a 3 × 3 × 3 supercell of the host Ni face-
centered cubic (fcc) cell with 108 atoms and applying a 3 × 3 ×
3 Monkhorst−Pack mesh of the Brillouin zone. Supercells for Σ
= 3/(111) and Σ = 9/(221) GBs contain 192 and 160 atoms,
respectively, and k-point meshes were scaled to be consistent
with the supercell used for bulk calculations. Concentrations of
alloying species di ﬀusing via vacancy-mediated mechanism are
therefore 0.9% (bulk), 0.5% ( Σ = 3/(111) GB), and 0.6% ( Σ =
9/(221) GB). Charge neutral cells were used. All calculations
were performed spin-polarized with a plane-wave cuto ﬀ energy
of 400 eV. To generate GB structures that correspond to the
energy minima at 0 K, we ﬁrst constructed initial GB structures
using the GBstudio applet
38,39 and then carried out an ab initio
molecular dynamics equilibration for 2 ps at 2000 K with
subsequent simulated annealing down to 0 K over 1 ps with the
velocities scaled every 20 fs and using a microcanonical
ensemble in the intermediate region and then performed a
DFT optimization of each structure. All ionic relaxations were
allowed within the cell with the volume and shape of each
supercell ﬁxed. It should be noted that the optimized structure
for Σ = 3/(111) GB corresponds to the one found in ref 33,
while the structure for Σ = 9/(221) GB is found to be di ﬀerent.
(See the Figures later.) The optimized Ni bulk lattice constant
used to generate supercells was 3.5196 Å. To model the (111)
Ni surface we used 2 × 2 surface supercell and a slab consisting
of ﬁve atomic layers with a vacuum gap of ∼10 Å. This gives the
80-atom supercell for defect-free case and a distance of ∼9.955
Å between defect periodic images when one metal vacancy is
created on the surface. Both vacancy- and interstitial-mediated
migration barriers were determined by the climbing image NEB
method. Three di ﬀerent vacancy-mediated transport directions
for the Σ = 3/(111) GB (see Figure 1) were found to have
similar activation energies and were averaged.
Vacancy-mediated di ﬀusion was ﬁrst considered for a series
of alloying metal elements in Ni and Ni self-di ﬀusion. It has
been recently shown by ab initio calculations of dilute Ni −Cr
and Ni−Fe alloys that interstitial-mediated di ﬀusion coeﬃcients
are comparable to those for the vacancy-mediated regime only
near the melting temperature and they drop rapidly with
temperature due to the large interstitial formation energy.
26
The elementary step for vacancy-mediated case corresponds to
the di ﬀusion of a minor element atom into a nearest lattice
vacancy in the host matrix of Ni. In the case of Σ = 3/(111) and
Σ = 9/(221) GBs, there are various pathways for metal
transport at the interface. Figure 2 shows the examined
diﬀusion pathway along the Σ = 9/(221) GB that is
characterized by symmetrically equivalent initial and ﬁnal
states. Our calculations of states for other possible pathways
resulted in dramatic restructuring of the GB during
optimization due to the presence of an alloying element and
a vacancy.
Figure 3 summarizes the calculated activation barriers for all
Ni alloying elements in a clustered histogram form, and the
results are listed in Table 1. It is clear that the lowest activation
barriers correspond to the highest-energy Σ = 9/(221) GB,
followed by the low-energy Σ = 3/(111) boundary and the
bulk, with migration barriers for the low-energy GB being only
slightly lower than in the bulk. Among the alloying elements
the most pronounced enhancement of di ﬀusion along high-
energy GBs is found for Cr, Mn, and Ti ( ∼10 times lower
activation barriers), while the least pronounced is for Ni and Fe
(∼3 times lower). Note that our results for the migration
barriers of Cr and Fe di ﬀusion and Ni self-di ﬀusion in Ni bulk
are in full agreement with previous calculations.

The interstitial transport of oxygen and sulfur was next
analyzed. A combination of TEM and APT measurements
11 has
provided evidence of fundamental di ﬀerences in oxidation and
sulﬁdation behaviors in Ni −Cr alloys. These studies have
revealed complex GB oxide microstructures upon exposure to
high-temperature hydrogenated water that are inconsistent with
classical internal oxidation;
13 however, a similar sample with
surface sulfur impurities exposed to the same conditions
exhibited the formation of discrete Cr-rich sul ﬁdes, suggesting
sulfur ingress into metallic GBs and internal sul ﬁdation.
11
These nanoscopic observations show distinct di ﬀerences in
alloy behavior during oxidation and sul ﬁdation and can
potentially generate insights into the mechanism of GB
degradation through modeling di ﬀusion processes. First-
principles information about the elementary processes of
oxygen and sulfur di ﬀusion in Ni is provided here to explain
some of the observed features.
Interstitial oxygen in the face-centered cubic lattice of Ni can
migrate via two di ﬀerent di ﬀusion pathways: from one
octahedral (Oct) site in the center of the Ni octahedron to
an equivalent adjacent Oct site directly along the line
connecting two sites (Oct −Oct pathway) or through a
tetrahedal (Tet) site via a face of the Ni octahedron (Oct −
Tet−Oct pathway). The di ﬀusivities of oxygen in Ni and Ni −
Cr binary alloy along these two pathways have been recently
calculated for a 2 × 2 × 2 supercell.
23 The minimum energy
paths are compared for both oxygen and sulfur (Figure 5a,b) in
Ni along the Oct −Oct and Oct −Tet−Oct pathways choosing a
slightly larger 3 × 3 × 3 supercell to model bulk di ﬀusion. First
of all, sulfur is characterized by higher activation barriers than
oxygen for both migration pathways, which is consistent with
its larger atomic size. It is also apparent that sulfur should
preferentially di ﬀuse along the Oct −Oct, while oxygen should
preferentially di ﬀuse along the Oct −Tet−Oct pathway. More-
over, a deep local minimum is observed at the tetrahedral site
for oxygen migrating along the Oct −Tet−Oct rather than a
transition state, in agreement with previous calculations.
23,24
Therefore, oxygen atoms are expected to have a relatively long
residence time at this metastable state, potentially serving as
precursors for the formation of Cr+O clusters in Ni −Cr alloys.

barrier of oxygen di ﬀusion in Ni −Cr alloy can be much lower
than in pure nickel.

A signiﬁcant lowering of the activation barriers is discovered for

for sulfur, suggesting that di ﬀusion of these elements may be
comparable. Because the presence of Cr was shown to enhance
oxygen diﬀusion in Ni, the barriers for GB di ﬀusion in a Ni −Cr

It should be noted that we have considered metal atom
diﬀusion to be vacancy-assisted; however, the presence of such
metal vacancies in Ni (i.e., Ni-de ﬁcient material) might also
aﬀect the di ﬀusion of oxygen (sulfur). In a previous
investigation, it was shown that if Ni vacancies are present it
becomes more energetically favorable for sulfur to occupy
substitutional rather than interstitial sites.
40 Therefore, we
carried out a series of additional calculations for both oxygen
and sulfur in a 3 × 3 × 3 supercell to estimate the preference
for both species to be at a substitutional (Ni vacancy) site,
relative to an octahedral interstitial sites (as more favorable
than tetrahedral site). We evaluated that oxygen (sulfur) placed
at the substitutional site is ∼1.20 (2.75) eV more favorable than
that at the octahedral interstitial position (keeping the distance
between Ni vacancy and oxygen or sulfur as large as possible in
the given supercell). This ﬁnding is in full agreement with the
previous study on sulfur in Ni bulk.
40 We should note, however,
that the substitutional oxygen atom, unlike sulfur, is found to be
slightly displaced from the lattice Ni vacancy position toward a
nearby Ni atom at the energy minimum, while the structure
with oxygen precisely at the Ni lattice position is found to be a
transition state with an activation barrier of ∼0.7 eV. In any
case, these calculations show that in the presence of metal
vacancies oxygen (sulfur) should prefer to occupy substitutional
rather than interstitial sites. As a result, this will lead to trapping
oxygen (sulfur) atoms by the lattice metal vacancies with a large
potential well when they encounter each other, thus consuming
the metal vacancies needed for transport of alloying elements.
This process is expected to be more important at the surfaces
than in the bulk due to a more facile transport of both metal
vacancies and oxygen (sulfur) species at the surfaces (GBs).
Furthermore, this could possibly comprise a basis for forming
precursors of oxide (sul ﬁde) phases in GBs.
In general, we should point out that our model calculations
have no large body of self-consistent experimental observations
available for comparison; oxidation, corrosion, and SCC studies
of Ni-base alloys involve complex material and environmental
conditions. It is also noteworthy that the vacancy-mediated
element di ﬀusion calculations showed no obvious trend in
calculated activation energy with element atomic radius,
polarizability, or calculated ionic (Bader) charge. Because the
electronic e ﬀects might contribute to the observed di ﬀerences
in properties in bulk and at the GBs of metals and alloys,
41−43
we have also analyzed the projected density of states (PDOS)
for both bulk and GB cases; however, the subtle di ﬀerences
observed across di ﬀerent systems do not allow us to make solid
conclusions about the found trend of enhanced di ﬀusivities
along the high-energy GB. We should note that a combination
of factors such as, for example, elastic, electronic, and magnetic
eﬀects that are not easy to deconvolute might explain the
observed trend. Nevertheless, our calculations allow us to draw
some important conclusions about species di ﬀusivities under
model conditions and generate a mechanistic transport
coeﬃcient database for further mesoscale modeling of transport
processes related to intergranular SCC and oxidation
phenomena in Ni-base alloys.
In summary, we have conducted a systematic study of
vacancy-mediated di ﬀusion in the bulk, at the (111) surface,
and at the model low- ( Σ = 3/(111)) and high- ( Σ = 9/(221))
energy GBs of Ni by estimating their migration energy barriers
using the DFT-based nudged elastic band method. The study
was focused on a series of alloying elements commonly used in
Ni-base structural alloys as well as interstitial oxygen and sulfur.
It has been demonstrated that the high-energy GB can serve as
an e ﬃcient conduit for a rapid di ﬀusion of minor elements
along GBs via vacancy-mediated mechanism, with a remarkable
enhancement for Cr, Mn, and Ti species as compared with the
bulk case ( ∼10 times lower). We have also shown that oxygen
atoms are characterized by a larger mobility than sulfur atoms
in bulk Ni and prefer to di ﬀuse via tetrahedral sites in the Ni
matrix, where they can be trapped in a local potential minimum.
