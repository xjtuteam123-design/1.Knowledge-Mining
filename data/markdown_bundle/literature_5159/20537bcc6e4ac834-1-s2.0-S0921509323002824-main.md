ABSTRACT 
High-temperature stability and strengthening mechanisms of high/medium-entropy alloys (HEAs/MEAs) have 
been studied for Ti-added FeCrV-based MEAs. Experimental results show that FeCrVTi
x 
MEAs can maintain good 
high-temperature stability in chemical element, phase structure and mechanical property. A mixed- 
strengthening-mechanism model dominated by precipitation and fine-grain strengthening has been proposed 
by comparing experimental results and model calculations of the yield strength increments for the FeCrV-based 
MEAs owing to Ti addition. The calculated strength increments with this mixed-strengthening-mechanism model 
closely match the series experimental results. The excellent compressive fracture strain in the FeCrVTi
x 
MEAs 
mainly comes from crack tip plasticity, and Ti addition not only enhances compressive strain by grain refine -
ment, but also improves compressive strain by crack deflection and bridging. Based on this strengthening model, 
the content, size and distribution of precipitates can be designed and controlled to regulate and optimize the 
mechanical properties of HEAs/MEAs. Therefore, this study provides new approaches and ideas for the design 
and development of HEAs/MEAs with high strength and good ductility for advanced nuclear energy systems. 
1. Introduction 
Nuclear energy is a kind of low-carbon clean energy source with 
high-energy density. The development of nuclear energy can effectively 
solve the energy shortage and environmental pollution. However, ma -
terials are one of the main bottlenecks that restrict the usage and 
development of advanced nuclear energy [ 1 – 3 ], especially for the 
Gen-IV nuclear fission reactor, International Thermonuclear Experi -
mental Reactor (ITER) and Accelerator Driven Sub-Critical System 
(ADS). These nuclear energy devices require materials to serve under 
more severe conditions, including high temperature (673 – 1073 K), high 
pressure (1 – 25 MPa), neutron irradiation of 100 – 200 displacements- 
per-atom (dpa), strong corrosion, and severe friction and wear, etc 
[ 2 – 4 ]. Conventional structural materials obviously cannot meet all these 
requirements at the same time and need to be further improved. 
Currently, the strengthening mechanisms of these materials have been 
relatively well established [ 5 – 7 ]. Syarif et al. [ 8 ] found that the yield 
strengths (~400 MPa at 293 K) of ferritic Fe – Cu binary alloys were 
mainly derived from the solid-solution strengthening of Cu element. 
Dhua et al. [ 9 ] discovered that the ultimate strength (1219 MPa at 723 
K) of high-strength low-alloy bearing steel mainly depended on the 
precipitation strengthening of fine precipitates composed of Cu and Nb 
(C, N). Lin et al. [ 10 ] reported that oxide-dispersion-strengthened (ODS) 
steels had superior high-temperature mechanical properties compared 
to conventional ferritic and ferritic/martensitic (F/M) steels due to the 
dispersed-barrier-strengthening of ultra-dense and ultra-fine Y – Ti – O

High/medium-entropy alloys (HEAs/MEAs) are a novel class of 
metallic materials. The four core effects in HEAs/MEAs allow some of 
them to have combined excellent properties such as high strength, good 
ductility, low-activation, high-temperature stability, irradiation resis -
tance and corrosion resistance coexistently [ 3 , 11 – 19 ]. Thus, they have a 
wide range of applications as nuclear structural materials [ 3 , 11 – 16 ]. 
Although some HEAs/MEAs can combine the above-mentioned excel -
lent properties, the origins/mechanisms are still unclear, since these 
excellent properties are usually in conflict with each other [ 20 ]. 
Therefore, study on the origins/mechanisms of coincident high strength, 
good ductility and outstanding high-temperature stability in HEAs/ -
MEAs is a matter of cardinal significance and can guide the design and 
development of high-performance structural materials for future 
advanced nuclear energy systems (Gen-IV reactor, ITER and ADS). 
It has been shown that some HEAs/MEAs can combine high strength 
with good ductility and outstanding high-temperature stability similar 
to conventional alloys through the doping of minimal elements such as 
Al, Mn, Ti, N and C [ 12 , 15 , 16 , 18 , 21 ]. As an example, Yang et al. [ 16 ] 
obtained (FeCoNi)

the addition of minimal elements, resulting in ultra-high strength in 
these HEAs/MEAs through a variety of mechanisms like conventional

twin-boundary strengthening and dispersion strengthening, etc [ 12 , 13 , 
15 ]. Our previous study has revealed that the minimal addition of Ti 
element could indeed significantly improve the strength, fracture strain 
and high-temperature stability of FeCrV-based MEAs simultaneously 
[ 22 ]. In this work, the FeCrV-based MEAs with Laves precipitation by 
adding Ti element will be used as a model material to investigate the 
origins/mechanisms of high-temperature stability and strengthening in 
high-performance HEAs/MEAs with precipitated phases. Most impor -
tantly, we propose that the strengthening mechanism in minimal 
element added HEAs/MEAs with precipitated phases, can be described 
as a mixed-strengthening-mechanism model dominated by precipitation 
strengthening and fine-grain strengthening. This work will focus on the 
strengthening model in FeCrVTi

opment of high-performance HEAs/MEAs (e.g. high strength and good 
ductility, etc.) for potential nuclear applications.

Combining the results of XRD, high-resolution TEM and EDS, it is not 
difficult to find that the lattice constants of BCC phase in the FeCrVTi
x 
MEAs increase with the increase of Ti content, but the two do not 
conform to Vegard ’ s law [ 50 ]. Therefore, in addition to the larger size of 
the Ti atoms themselves relative to Fe, Cr and V atoms, the lattice 
distortion due to Ti addition mainly arises from the atomic size differ -
ence, crystal structure difference and bonding preference difference of 
alloying elements that make up the complex and concentrated BCC 
lattice [ 51 ]. It is clear that the addition of Ti leads to lattice distortion of 
the FeCrV-based MEAs, which in turn increases the microstress of BCC 
lattice and the activation barrier of double kinks, thus hinders disloca -
tion slip deformation [ 52 , 53 ]. As a result, the strength usually increases, 
but the fracture strain may decrease, which affects the deformation 
behavior. Zhao [ 54 ] used the Monte Carlo (MC) method combined with 
density-functional theory (DFT) calculations to predict the presence of 
LCF in the FeCrV MEA. Our experimental results are consistent with his 
theoretical predictions. This suggests that the large lattice strain induced 
by LCF in the FeCrVTi
x 
MEAs BCC-matrix does indeed exist and could 
pin mobile dislocations, and the LCF-induced pinning effect in the MEAs 
is not only beneficial to the strength enhancement, but also to the 
fracture strain. This is mainly due to the rugged energy distribution 
caused by LCF in some HEAs/MEAs, which leads to rugged dislocation 
motion, thus local double cross-slip. The local double cross-slip is a 
decomposition mechanism that can distribute dislocation-multiplication 
in multiple planes, thereby relieving strain location and delaying crack 
nucleation [ 53 , 55 , 56 ]. Therefore, the LCF in FeCrV-based MEAs may

3.3. Potential nuclear applications 
It is well known that the service environment of nuclear structural 
materials for Gen-IV nuclear fission reactor, ITER and ADS is more se -

(e.g. Zr alloys and F/M steels, etc.) cannot meet these requirements at

thermal properties (strength, fracture strain, high-temperature stability

some conventional alloy steels (SS316 and T91), with strength and

nation of experimental results and model calculations, it is found that 
the FeCrV MEA exhibits better resistance to irradiation-induced swelling

target windows and fuel cladding in ADS.

4. Conclusions 
In this work, the high-temperature stability and strengthening 
mechanisms of the FeCrV-based MEAs with Ti addition are discussed 
through modeling calculations and analyzing data. It is noted that the 
initial input parameters used for modeling and calculation are derived 
from our previous published work (microstructural characterization) 
[ 22 ]. The main conclusions can be summarized as follows: 
(1) The good compressive fracture strain in the FeCrVTi
x 
MEAs is 
mainly attributed to dislocation cross-slip and LCF, while the 
addition of Ti can increase the compressive strain of the MEAs by 
grain refinement on the one hand, and improve the compressive 
strain (crack deflection and crack bridging) by the formation of 
Laves precipitation on the other hand, which in turn affects the 
compressive deformation behavior. 
(2) The addition of Ti increases the yield strength of the FeCrV-based 
MEAs mainly due to the precipitation strengthening and fine- 
grain strengthening, with a small contribution from the solid- 
solution strengthening and dislocation strengthening. Based on 
these findings, a mixed-strengthening-mechanism model is pro -
posed, which can well explain the experimental phenomena that 
the yield strength of the MEAs first increases then decreases with 
the increase of Ti content and the details of their microstructural 
evolution. 
(3) Based on the mixed-strengthening-mechanism model, deforma -
tion behavior, phase structure stability and phase evolution path, 
the content, size and distribution of precipitated phases can be 
designed and controlled to regulate and optimize the mechanical 
properties of HEAs/MEAs. This provides new methods and ideas 
for the design and development of high-performance HEAs/MEAs 
for advanced nuclear energy systems. 
(4) The FeCrV-based MEAs have superior strength and fracture 
strain, and can maintain outstanding high-temperature stability 
in terms of chemical element, phase structure and mechanical 
property. Especially their yield strength and fracture strain at 
773 K can reach 604 MPa and 47.3% respectively, which can 
meet the requirements of structural materials for spallation target 
window and core fuel cladding of ADS very well.
