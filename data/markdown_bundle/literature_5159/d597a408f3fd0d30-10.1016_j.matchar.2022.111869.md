ABSTRACT 
Based on high Li content, optimizing the mechanical properties of Mg-Li alloy can bring about the good com -
bination of ultra-low density and high strength, causing high specific strength. Reducing the deformation tem -
perature can effectively maintain the strength improvement of deformed β -based Mg-Li alloy. In this work, the 
microstructures and mechanical properties of room-rolled (RR) and cryogenic-rolled (CR) Mg-16Li-4Zn-1Er 
(LZE1641) alloy were investigated. Results show that cryogenic temperature delays the dynamic recovery 
(DRV) and dynamic recrystallization(DRX). A tensile strength of 267 MPa can be obtained at CR60%, which is 
attributed to the nanocrystalline grains and the significantly higher dislocation density than that of the RR alloy. 
Further CR with 80% reduction makes the form of some alloying elements transform from solid state to pre -
cipitation phases, causing relatively decreased strength 
1. Introduction 
In view of the lightweight demand of aerospace and the concept of 
energy saving, weight loss has always been one of the most concerns in 
the field of metal applications [ 1 ]. Among a multitude of engineering 
metallic materials, Mg alloys stand out because of their low density. 
Furthermore, Mg alloys hold excellent qualities in vibration resistance, 
electromagnetic shielding, and so on, which provides great contribution 
to transportation manufacture and 3C electronic industries [ 2 , 3 ]. 
However, the hexagonal close packed (HCP) crystal structure restricts 
the formability of Mg alloys due to the rare movable slip systems. The 
addition of Li, a structure of body-centered cubic (BCC), makes the 
knotty problem be smoothly solved by activating < c + a > pyramidal 
slip in Mg alloys and altering the structure from HCP to BCC [ 4 , 5 ]. Li 
alloying in Mg brings not only the increased plastic deformation ca -
pacity, but also a further decreased density (1.3 – 1.6 g/cm
3
). However, 
the strength of the alloy is sacrificed to a certain extent. When Li content 
exceeds 11 wt%, there is only β -Li phase in the alloy (called as BCC Mg-Li 
alloy), which makes the strength of as-cast Mg-Li alloys hardly surmount 
180 MPa [ 6 ]. As a matter of fact, even the processed state of them can 
hardly conquer 250 MPa [ 7 ]. 
Severe plastic deformation (SPD), solid solution strengthening and 
second phase strengthening are effective ways to meet the urgent de -
mand for the strengthening of ultra-light Mg-Li alloys [ 8 – 10 ]. The as- 
cast BCC Mg-Li alloys have coarse initial grains, but the grains can be 
refined by inducing the generation of DRX through deformation [ 5 ]. 
Therefore, deformation has a significant effect on improving their 
strength. At the same time, high-Li Mg-Li alloys are extremely sensitive 
to deformation. Large deformation or thermal effects during the defor -
mation process can make the DRV and DRX to appear prematurely or the 
DRX grains to grow up, which lead to premature softening during the 
thermal deformation process [ 11 ]. 
The DRV process is accompanied with the disposition of point defects 
and the cancellation rearrangement with stress relaxation of disloca -
tions, in which the accumulated storage energy of deformation is 
released. DRX primarily fulfils through the accumulation of defective 
structures and storage energy by deformation processing. The defects 
and storage energy act as the driving force for DRX. In BCC structure, the 
stacking energy is so high that the dislocations are easy to climb or cross 
slip away from the original slip surface [ 12 ]. Even at room temperature, 
the DRV can happen due to the dislocation rearrangement which lead to 
a tendency that a small amount of deformation can make the dislocation 
disappear.

2. Method 
LZE1641 alloys were melted and cast in a vacuum medium frequency 
induction furnace under Ar atmosphere in a steel crucible. The alloy was 
prepared from the commercial pure Mg ( > 99.9 wt%), Li ( > 99.9 wt%), 
Zn ( > 99.9 wt%) and Mg-20 wt%Er master alloys. The melt was poured 
into a cylindrical permanent mold. The as-cast specimen was cut into 
sheets with dimensions of 50 mm × 30 mm for the subsequent rolling 
with the thickness of 2.5 mm, 3.4 mm, 5 mm, 10 mm respectively. 
Finally, the sheets were rolled at room or cryogenic temperature, called 
as RR and CR, respectively. For CR, the sheets were pre-refrigerated in 
liquid nitrogen for 10 min before rolling, and they were dipped into 
liquid nitrogen for 5 min at intervals of each rolling pass. The reduction 
per pass was 0.2 mm ~ 0.3 mm, and the final sheets possess the 
reduction rate of 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, respectively. Eight samples with 
different processing states with a thickness of about 2 mm were finally 
obtained.

nitrate alcohol for 4 s and then the microstructure was observed by DM

3. Results 
The stress-strain curves of the RR and CR specimens are shown in 
Fig. 2 . The strength enhances with the increase of the rolling reduction. 
Compared with RR specimens, the strength of CR specimens is signifi -
cantly higher. For further intuitively analysis, the values of ultimate

plotted in Fig. 3 . The curves of UTS show parabolic shape with the in -

diversity in the vertex positions of the parabolas between the two curves. 
The turning point appears at RR40% while it arises at CR60% in CR 
curve. The UTS of RR40% is 201 MPa and the UTS of CR60% reaches the 
highest value of 267 MPa. Despite negative effect of 80% reduction on 
strength of the alloys in both states, CR80% alloy still holds a high value 
of 238 MPa. As for the elongation curves, 20% reduction decreases in 
both RR and CR alloys. With the further increase of reduction, elonga -
tion rises immediately for RR alloys. However, the elongation of CR 
alloy still ordinarily sinks until the reduction is larger than 40%.

CR alloys, the microstructures of them are analyzed. The metallographic

round shape to a flat strip shape accompanying by the appearance of 
cleavage planes. From the macroscopic fractures, it can be observed that 
the cleavage surfaces are similar to the “ river-like ” shape and perpen -
dicular to the applied tension. Rolling deformation causes dislocation 
plugging in the microstructures of the alloys. Brittle fracture originates 
from micro-cracks in stress concentration. This is also the reason why 
cleavage planes appear in the fractures of RR and CR alloys. It is worth 
noting that the cleavage plane is easy to expand inside the grain, but it 
can be hindered by the grain boundaries. Therefore, stress concentration 
and cleavage steps are likely to occur near the grain boundaries.

of the crystal grains which names intergranular fracture. The way along 
the intergranular fracture is gradually derived in CR60% alloy which is 
shown in Fig. 5 (d). From the grain size and shape of the grains, it can be 
judged that there are small-sized grains and elongated grains in the alloy 
proving that CR60% triggered the occurrence of DRX. As we all know, 
Mg-Li alloy is very prone to DRX, but the crystal grains after DRX are 
very easy to grow up quickly. The fine grains of CR60% LZ1641 alloy are 
elongated, indicating that they are involved in deformation without 
rapid growth. It can strongly prove that CR60% may effectively inhibit 
grain growth.

4. Discussion 
Intuitively, slip bands and deformation bands are hard to appear in 
routine deformed Li-rich Mg-Li alloys. When the crystals are subjected to 
shear stress, the micro-steps gradually emerge on the crystal surfaces. 
Slip band is a significant feature of crystal plastic deformation. The 
poorer the plastic deformation ability of the material, the denser and 
longer the slip bands are. The slip band networks caused by multiple 
slips are often observed. The stress propagated to adjacent grains is 
inhomogeneous and there is inherent instability during plastic defor -
mation for grains. They produce rotation in diverse directions to seek 
stable stress orientations. This coordination results in individual grains

hinder the elimination of dislocations [ 26 ]. Its existence is undoubtedly 
the icing on the cake for alloy strengthening. 
4.2. Precipitation and element segregation 
CR80% inputs more external forces to the matrix as compared to 
CR60%. The strengthening functions of CR60% are mainly concerning 
dislocation strengthening and grain refinement strengthening brought 
by nanocrystals. However, when the reduction continues to increase to 
80%, the dislocation strengthening effect of the alloy has no further 
effect. The work hardening rate falls slightly comparing with CR60% 
alloy. The existence of nanocrystals in the CR80% alloy matrix was not 
observed. It can be reflected that more intense deformation triggers 
grain boundary migration. This is because further external force breaks 
two dynamic balances: (1) dislocation proliferation and disappearance 
and (2) grain nucleation and grain boundary migration [ 25 ]. Due to the 
dual pressure of giant degree of undercooling and violent pressure, 
dislocation slip is not the only way to coordinate deformation of the 
CR80% alloy. In order to prevent the dislocations from disappearing, the 
point defects and impurity atoms are concentrated on the dislocation 
lines to pin the dislocations [ 27 ]. Two precipitation species are pre -
sented on the TEM images which are slab precipitated phases and 
element segregation. The segregation of these two does not occur at the 
grain boundaries, but inside the grains. The strip-like precipitates are 
formed under low temperature and high stress, and are different from 
the primary phase structure in the matrix. Accordingly, they may be 
metastable phases which belong to supersaturated solid solution. The 
isothermal kinetics of supersaturated solid solution desolvation con -

However, in addition to the slab phases, the phenomenon of network 
flocculent element segregation also occurs. The network flocculent 
phases are mainly attributed to the segregation of Zn atoms. The results 
show that the atoms are not pushed into a uniform diffusion motion. 
They move along dislocation or other defect channels, resulting in 
element enriched states. In terms of atomic quantum, the components in 
the precipitation phases present a proportion of Zn > Er. This ratio 
differs from our previous researches (the atomic ratio of the second 
phases in the as-cast alloys is Zn < Er) [ 31 ]. To be sure, in the as-cast 
alloy, Zn mainly exists in the matrix as a solid solution atom. In the 
Li-rich environment, Zn plays a very important role as the main solid 
solution strengthening atom, so the formation of this network flocculent 
structure makes the uniformly distributed Zn in the alloy change from 
the status of solid solution to precipitation [ 7 , 10 ]. That is, CR80% 
promotes the transformation of the mechanism from solid solution 
strengthening to precipitation strengthening. Therefore, the convert of

5. Conclusion 
1. Cryogenic temperature can delay the DRV and DRX stages of the 
alloy during deformation. CR retains a higher dislocation density 
under the same deformation reduction than RR. 
2. Nano-scale grains appeared in the alloy at CR60%, indicating that 
cryogenic temperature could effectively inhibit grain boundary 
migration. 
3. The best tensile strength was obtained at CR60% which is 267 MPa. 
4. CR80% transformed the strengthening mechanism of alloying ele -
ments in the matrix from solid solution strengthening to dispersion 
strengthening. Compared with CR60%, strength is lower. But the 
alloy strength was still higher than RR80%.
