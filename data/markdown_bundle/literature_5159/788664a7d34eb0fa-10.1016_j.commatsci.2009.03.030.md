abstract
An analytical method is proposed to investigate the morphological evolution of c0 precipitates in Ni-based
superalloys. Two types of superalloys are considered with different ratio between the Young’s moduli of
the precipitates and the matrix. Based on Eshelby’s equivalent inclusion theory and Mori–Tanaka’s mean
ﬁeld method the elastic energy is calculated as a function of the particle shape. The plastic strain induced
by the formation of dislocation networks at the phase interface is taken into account. The results show
that the external stress and the plastic matrix strain play a signiﬁcant role for shape stability and the raf-
ting mechanism of the precipitates. The elastic–plastic analysis provides a satisfactory explanation for all
available experimental observations superalloys undergoing plastic creep.

1. Introduction
Because of their excellent reliability and mechanical properties,
Ni-based superalloys are used as indispensable materials for the
blades and vane applications in gas turbines. Generally, superalloys
have a naturally developed composite microstructure consisting of
a high volume fraction of hard cubical c0 precipitates embedded
coherently in a softer c matrix. Many researchers have pointed
out that this type of unique geometry of the c=c0 microstructure
substantially inﬂuences the mechanical and creep behavior of sin-
gle crystal superalloys. The mechanical and creep behavior is clo-
sely related to the evolution of their microstructure [1–3]. During
the gas turbine’s operation period, the initially cuboidal
c0 precip-
itates under the acting stress change to disc-like or needle-like
structures. Many experimental works have demonstrated the
appearance of a disc-like or needle-like rafting structure under
an external applied stress during high temperature creep [3–6].
The direction and morphology of these rafts depends on several
parameters such as the signs of the misﬁt between
c and c0 phases,
the applied creep stress and the temperature.
The evolution of the precipitates morphology can qualitatively
be described by using an energy analysis. Several models investi-
gating the change of energy with precipitates shape have been pro-
posed to explain rafting or a shape change of precipitates occurring
under an external applied stress [7–10]. Schmidt and Gross [7] and
Mueller and Gross [8] proposed a boundary element method to
simulate the equilibrium shape of an elastically misﬁtting precipi-
tate in a matrix. Pineau [9] applied Eshbely’s theory to predict the
inﬂuence of an external stress on the morphology of coherent pre-
cipitates during rafting. Ichitsubo et al. [10] discussed the rafting
mechanism by calculating the total mechanical energy in elastic
systems with typical microstructures under external stress. How-
ever, rafting can occur without an external stress when the plastic
strain is pre-introduced before a high temperature annealing
[11–13]. Ratel et al. [13] illuminated the rafting phenomenon by
introducing a plastic matrix strain without an external stress. They
discussed the effect of the precipitation misﬁt strain on the shape
stability of
c0 precipitates after the plastic stain is introduced.
In addition to the effect of the precipitation misﬁt, the stiffness
ratio between matrix and precipitates has an enormous impact on
the shape stability and the rafting behavior of precipitates in Ni-
based superalloys [7,9,14–16]. Experimental results revealed that
there exist some alloys with stiffnesses of c0 precipitates less than
those of the c matrix [5,17,18]. Socrate and Parks [16] used the ﬁ-
nite element method to discuss the rafting behavior of this type of
alloys. Their results indicate that the rafting predictions in this case
do not agree with experimental observations if creep ﬂow in the

3. Energy calculations and results
In this section total potential energies are calculated and com-
pared without taking into account the interfacial energy. This im-
plies that this energy term in a ﬁrst approximation is regarded as
negligible compared with terms associated with plastic matrix
deformation. In Section 3.1 we start the analysis of the shape sta-
bility in an elastic state. In Section 3.2 and Section 3.3, we consider
shape stability associated with plastic deformation caused by the
formation of dislocation networks at the
c—c0 interface. The mate-
rial constants used for the calculations are taken from Refs. [2,18]
and summarized in Table 1 . Two types of Ni-superalloys are se-
lected: type1 with Cp > Cm and type2 with Cp < Cm, where Cm
and Cp are the Young’s modulus of c matrix and c0 precipitates,
respectively.
3.1. Shape stability in the elastic state
For the externally unloaded material the elastic energy is given
by only the initial internal misﬁt energy due to the misﬁt strain. As
an example the calculated elastic misﬁt energy for the type1 mate-
rial is shown in Fig. 2 as a function of the shape parameter. As can
be seen, a shape approximating a sphere has the lowest internal
misﬁt energy. This indicates that the cubic structure is the most
stable one, as observed in the initial microstructure of a material
without any plastic creep deformation. This is because the softer
c matrix is more distorted than the stiffer c0 precipitates in the
cuboidal structure, which reduces the initial internal misﬁt energy.
For the case of an applied external stress in x1 direction, an elas-
tic analysis for both material types has been carried out. Table 2
shows the total potential energy for ﬁve representative precipitate
shapes when an external stress of 200 MPa is applied. In case of
C
p > Cm (type1) and an external tensile stress, the limiting case
a ! 0 for the disc shows the lowest energy. In contrast, for a com-
pressive external stress, the total elastic energy becomes smaller
with increasing shape parameter
a and it reaches the lowest value
for a !1 . These results indicate that for a negative misﬁt alloy the
disc-shaped structure is the most stable one under the tensile
stress, while the needle-shaped structure is the most stable one
under compressive stress. Accordingly, for a type1 alloy the disc-
shaped or needle-shaped rafted structure is favorable under tensile
or compressive stress, respectively.
In case of Cp < Cm (type2) as shown in Table 2 b, the needle-
shaped structure is the most stable one under the tensile stress
and the disc-shaped structure is most stable under the compres-
sive stress. Thus, for a type2 negative misﬁt alloy the needle-
shaped or disc-shaped rafted structure is favorable under tensile
or compressive stress, respectively. This result is exactly opposite
to the result for the type1 alloy. It is also perfectly consistent with
that predicted by Pineau’s [8] stability map based on the elastic
analysis. However, it is not consistent with the experimental re-
sults [3,5].

3.3. Shape stability associated with plastic deformation

plastic strain at the c—c0 interface. For this purpose we compare
the total potential energies of two types of Ni-superalloys with var-

sumed that the plastic strain caused by the formation of disloca-
tions networks at the
c—c0 interface is 50% of the misﬁt strain.

tic analysis do not always agree with experimental results.
Although an elastic analysis provides a good estimation for Ni-
based superalloys with Cp > Cm, it fails for alloys with Cp < Cm.
The essential reason for this failure can be found in the limita-
tions of an elastic analysis which neglects the inelastic response of
the c matrix when evaluating the stress and strain ﬁelds. As we
known, the dislocation motion induces a network of misﬁt dislo-
cations at the
c—c0 interfaces during rafting. The presences of
these dislocations suggest that a localized creep process is reliev-
ing the
c—c0 misﬁt and altering the state of stress and elastic
strain energy. In the explored range of loading conditions, the
predominant creep mechanism involves dislocations motion pri-
marily in the
c matrix. For alloys with Cp > Cm, the c matrix is
softer than the c0 precipitates, thus the elastic analysis can give
a reasonable estimation for the morphological evolution of c0 pre-
cipitates. But for Ni-based superalloys with Cp < Cm, the c matrix
is stiffer than the c0 precipitates. Based on the elastic analysis, the
c0 precipitates should deform and preferentially creep, which is
just contrary to the experimental observation, diagnosing that
the creep mechanism involves dislocations motion primarily in
the
c matrix. Thus, in this case the elastic analysis can not give
reasonable predictions on the shape stability and rafting.
However, if the plastic deformation in the
c matrix is taken into
account, we can obtain reasonable estimates which are consistent
with experimental observations.
Moreover, we found in our calculations that the theoretical pre-
dictions are consistent with experimental results only if the plastic
strain exceeds a threshold value. This threshold value is approxi-
mately equal to the plastic strain caused by dislocations formation.
Thus we conclude that the effect of dislocations networks should
be considered when the morphology evolution of
c0 precipitates
in Ni-based superalloys is analyzed. In Section 3.2, we found by
atomistic simulations that the plastic strain caused by the
formation of dislocation networks is about 50% of the misﬁt strain.
However, it should emphasized that this value is a rough estimate
of the plastic strain relieving the c—c0 misﬁt. It is known that the
structure of c—c0 interface is closely related with the motion of dis-
locations at different temperatures and loading conditions. There-
fore, a more precise description of the plastic strain in the matrix
should include the effect of temperature and external stress on
the dislocation behavior.
5. Conclusions
An analytical method has been proposed to investigate the mor-
phological evolution of c0 precipitates in Ni-based superalloys. Two
types of Ni-superalloys are selected; type1 with Cp > Cm and type2
with Cp < Cm. Eshelby’s inclusion theory and Mori–Tanaka’s mean
ﬁeld method have been used to evaluate the elastic energy caused
by a change in the shape of c0 precipitates. In the calculations, we
simplify the initial and the rafted structures wherever possible by
an ellipsoidal model. Naturally, the results of calculation certainly
depend on the model of initial structure. These simpliﬁcations are
idealized in comparison to the microstructures of
c0 precipitates in
real alloys during creep and constrain the accuracy of the ap-
proach, but retaining the essence of the phenomena and describing
physical effects very efﬁciently.
The calculated results suggest that the predictions based on a
simple elastic analysis do not always agree with the experimental
results regarding shape stability and rafting behavior. However, if
the plastic matrix strain is introduced, caused by the formation
of dislocations at the
c—c0 interface, the results are perfectly con-
sistent with experimental observations for both type1 and type2
materials. Consequently, the plastic strain plays an important role
on the shape stability and rafting behavior during the evolution of
precipitate shape. The effect of dislocations formation and motion
should be considered when the morphology evolution of
c0 precip-
itates in Ni-based superalloys is analyzed.
