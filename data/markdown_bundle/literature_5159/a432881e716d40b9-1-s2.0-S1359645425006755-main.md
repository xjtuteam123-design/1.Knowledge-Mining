ABSTRACT
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) have shown significant potential due to their exceptional mechanical 
properties, including high strength and excellent thermal stability. However, achieving a balance between 
strength and ductility remains a major challenge, particularly for Ti-rich RHEAs, which often exhibit limited 
ductility at room temperature. In this study, we demonstrate that directed energy deposition (DED), a novel 
additive manufacturing technique, can effectively break the strength-ductility trade-off in TiZrNbVAl high en -
tropy alloys. The DEDed TiZrNbVAl thin-wall samples exhibit a yield strength exceeding 900 MPa and a fracture 
elongation of over 27 %, significantly outperforming as-cast samples with a yield strength of 835 MPa and a 
fracture elongation of 18.8 %. The superior mechanical properties of DED samples are attributed to the formation 
of a heterogeneous structure consisting of alternating equiaxed and columnar dendrites, as well as the easier 
formation of α -fiber during the tensile process of DED samples. This promotes the synergistic improvement of 
strength and ductility. During deformation process, the formation of kink bands, multiple slip systems, and high- 
density dislocation walls collectively enhance the plastic deformation coordination between grains, delaying the 
onset of necking. This study provides new insights into the preparation of RHEAs with exceptional strength- 
ductility balance using DED technology, offering valuable guidance for the development of advanced struc -
tural materials for aerospace, energy, and other high-performance applications.
1. Introduction
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) have emerged as a promising 
class of materials for high temperature applications due to their excep -
tional mechanical properties, including high strength, excellent thermal 
stability, and resistance to corrosion [ 1 – 3 ]. RHEAs are typically 
composed of multiple high-melting-point elements, such as Ti, Zr, Nb, V, 
Ta, Mo, and W, which preferentially form a single phase solid solution 
with a body-centered cubic (BCC) crystal structure [ 4 – 7 ]. These alloys 
have shown potential in aerospace, energy, and nuclear applications, 
where high temperature strength and durability are critical [ 8 , 9 ]. 
However, achieving a balance between strength and ductility remains a 
significant challenge, particularly for lightweight Ti-rich RHEAs, which 
often exhibit limited ductility at room temperature [ 10 – 12 ].
The intrinsic brittleness of BCC RHEAs is primarily attributed to their 
complex microstructures, which are prone to the formation of brittle 
intermetallic phases, microcracks, and compositional segregation dur -
ing traditional processing methods such as casting [ 13 ]. For example, 
cast lightweight Ti-rich RHEAs often exhibit coarse grain structures and 
significant compositional inhomogeneity, leading to poor mechanical 
properties and limited ductility [ 14 ]. These challenges have hindered 
the broader application of RHEAs in structural components, where both 
strength and plasticity are essential for reliable performance under dy -
namic loading conditions [ 15 ].
To address these limitations, additive manufacturing (AM) technol -
ogy, such as directed energy deposition (DED), have emerged as trans -
formative approaches for the fabrication of RHEAs. DED offers unique 
advantages, including precise control over microstructure, reduced 
segregation, and the ability to produce complex geometries with mini -
mal material waste [ 16 – 18 ]. The high solidification rates and localized 
heat input during DED result in fine microstructures and homogeneous 
compositional distributions, which are critical for enhancing

The slip bands in adjacent grains (represented by black lines) in Fig. 4 (c)

enhances plastic deformation coordination and delays crack initiation 
and propagation.

4.1. Hetero-deformation induced strengthening
The microstructure of the thin-wall consists of an alternating distri -
bution of small-sized equiaxed dendrites and large-sized columnar 
dendrites, fundamentally different from the fully equiaxed dendrites of 
the as-cast samples. Due to variations in size and morphology, coarse 
grains (columnar dendrites) and fine grains (equiaxed dendrites) exhibit 
distinct mechanical properties, leading to different strain levels during

during DED results in varying temperature gradients ( G ) and solidifi -

formation of equiaxed-columnar dendrites structures along the BD. As 
the number of printed layers increases, the alternating distribution of 
these two types of grains becomes established, as depicted in the sche -

between grains, and features such as grain boundaries and cracks are 
unable to effectively coordinate the deformation. In contrast, hetero -

elastic state. Subsequently, plastic deformation transitions to the hard 
phase, where the hard phase constrains the deformation of the soft 
phase, resulting in a greater degree of plastic deformation in the soft 
phase. The strain distribution of the hard and soft phases is shown in the 
Fig. 10 (a). Coordinating the strain gradient and maintaining continuity

further hinders the motion of GND, resulting in the accumulation of 
more GND and a stronger HDI strengthening effect. The HDI stress 
causes the soft phase to bear higher stress under back stress, thereby 
enhancing the yield strength. Additionally, the hard phase bears higher

5. Conclusions
In this work, Ti
3
Zr
1.5
NbVAl
0.25 
RHEAs were prepared by DED and 
vacuum arc melting. A systematic analysis was conducted on the 
microstructure and plastic deformation behavior. The following con -
clusions were drawn:
1. Ti
3
Zr
1.5
NbVAl
0.25 
RHEAs demonstrate a single phase BCC struc -
ture. The DED samples consist of alternating equiaxed-columnar den -
drites, while the as-cast samples consist of fully equiaxed dendrites. The 
DED samples have a yield strength of over 900 MPa and a fracture 
elongation of over 27 %, and as-cast samples have a yield strength of 835 
MPa and a fracture elongation of over 18.8 %.
2. As the test temperature increases from 400 
◦
C to 800 
◦
C, the high 
temperature tensile yield strength decreases dramatically from 750 MPa 
to 66 MPa, while the plastic strain increases from 24.8 % to 67.6 %. A 
small amount of C14 Laves phase enriched with Zr-V-Al will form in 
HT600, and at higher temperatures, the C14 Laves phase will dissolve 
into the matrix. HT800 exhibits pronounced recrystallization, leading to 
reduced strength and WHR.
3. The KAM value and GND density increase with the increase in 
deformation degree. In order to coordinate the plastic deformation, the 
grains will rotate around a specific axis to form kink bands. The multiple 
slip within the grain and the slip transfer between grains promote the 
movement of dislocations and contribute to the improvement of plas -
ticity. The slip process generates a significant amount of HDDW and MB, 
which plays a role in impeding neck contraction.
4. The unique cooling mode of DED reduces element segregation and 
promotes the formation of equiaxed-columnar dendrites heterogeneous 
structure, in which columnar dendrites are more conducive to the acti -
vation of the slip system, and 〈 001 〉 orientation is more conducive to the 
formation of α -fiber. These factors lead to better comprehensive me -
chanical properties of DED samples.
This work deeply reveals the root of the excellent plasticity of DED 
Ti
3
Zr
1.5
NbVAl
0.25

RHEAs with both strength and plasticity.
