ABSTRACT 
Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) encounter difficulties in balancing room-temperature ductility 
and high-temperature strength, which restricts their applications. Here, a novel NbTaV
0.5
Hf
0.1 
RCCA was pre -
pared through vacuum arc melting for the first time. The as-cast alloy exhibited a stable body-centered cubic 
phase structure with a small amount of micro-scale dispersed Hf-rich oxides. As a result, the designed alloy 
exhibits a room-temperature tensile fracture strain of 14.9 %, and superior compressive yield strength values 
under elevated temperatures (472 MPa under 1200 ℃ , 358 MPa under 1400 ℃ , respectively). A synergy between 
room-temperature tensile ductility and exceptional high-temperature strength was achieved in RCCAs. 
1. Introduction 
Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) encompass re -
fractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), which are considered potential 
materials for high-temperature structural applications [1 – 3] . However, 
the majority of RCCAs or RHEAs exhibit poor room temperature (RT) 
ductility, limiting their practical applications [3] . Senkov et al. [4] 
developed two RHEAs: NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV which demonstrate 
remarkable compressive strength at 600 
◦
C and above, but a limited 
ductility of about 2.0 % and 1.5 % of RT plastic strain. Some RCCAs, 
primarily composed of IVB and VB group elements, exhibit excellent 
ductility. For instance, TaNbHfZrTi exhibits an RT tensile elongation of 
20.8 % [5] , while its yield strength at 1200 
◦
C is only 92 MPa [6] . Han 
et al. [7,8] revealed the exceptional strength and ductility of VNbTa 
alloy at RT or cryogenic temperatures. Yang et al. [9] further revealed its 
super rolling formability ( > 80 % thickness reduction). However, there is 
no reported research on its high-temperature mechanical behavior. Most 
of the previous research has focused on alloys either with better RT 
ductility or extremely high-temperature strength. This work introduces 
for the first time an NbTaV
0.5
Hf
0.1 
RCCA with exceptional RT tensile 
ductility and high-temperature strength concurrently.

The nominal composition NbTaV

measured by using SEM-EDS to be Nb: 37.78 ± 0.01, Ta: 38.79 ± 0.05,

crystal structure of the as-cast alloy was characterized by X-ray 
diffraction (XRD) using Bruker D8 ADVANCE Da Vinci under Cu-K α 
radiation. The microstructure, tensile fracture surface, elements distri -
bution, and crystal orientation maps were analyzed using TESCAN 
Mira3 LHM scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped with back -
scattered electron (BSE) imaging, energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS), 
and electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) detectors. RT tensile me -
chanical tests were performed on the SHIMADZU/AGS-X 10KN using 
dog bone-shaped specimens with a gauge length of 8 mm and a cross- 
section of 2 × 1 mm. High-temperature compression tests were con -
ducted on the Gleeble-3500 using cylindrical specimens of Φ 4 × 6 mm. 
The engineering strain rate for all mechanical tests was set at 1.0 × 10

4. Conclusions 
The microstructure and mechanical properties of NbTaV
0.5
Hf
0.1 
RCCA were investigated. The matrix has a BCC phase, with dispersed Hf- 
rich oxide precipitates located in interdendritic regions. The 
NbTaV
0.5
Hf
0.1 
RCCA exhibited a tensile yield strength of 779 MPa and a 
fracture elongation of 14.9 % at RT. It also retained a high compressive 
yield strength of 472 MPa and 358 MPa at 1200 ℃ and 1400 ℃ , 
respectively, with ductility ε
C 
> 50 %. It exhibits a remarkable combi -
nation of room-temperature ductility and high-temperature strength 
compared to other RCCAs or RHEAs.
