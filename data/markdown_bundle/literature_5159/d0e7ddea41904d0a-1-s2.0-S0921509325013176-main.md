ABSTRACT
Additive manufacturing, particularly laser powder bed fusion (LPBF), offers transformative potential for fabri -
cating complex components in nuclear reactor cores. However, the applicability of LPBF technology to 
specialized nuclear materials such as zirconium alloys, which are designed to withstand the elevated temperature 
extreme environments within reactors, remains insufficiently explored. In this work, Zircaloy-4 alloy was suc -
cessfully fabricated via LPBF technology. The microstructure evolution and deformation behavior of the alloy 
were systematically investigated under uniaxial tensile loading at both room temperature (RT) and an elevated 
temperature of 380 
◦
C. Furthermore, the α lath morphology, second phase precipitates (SPPs), and the type and 
configuration of dislocation were characterized before and after deformation. The results indicate that the 
microstructure in the LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy consists of epitaxially columnar grains containing fine α 
laths. Enrichment of Fe and Cr elements was observed at α lath boundaries, and nanoscale SPPs were distributed 
within α laths. In addition, the LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy exhibits outstanding mechanical strength (such as 
ultimate tensile strength, 700 MPa at RT and 368 MPa at 380 
◦
C), outperforming ASTM standard (415 MPa at 
RT) and sheet samples (204 MPa at 380 
◦
C), respectively. Meanwhile, fracture elongations reached 10.5 % at RT 
and 16.4 % at 380 
◦
C. The moderate ductility is attributed to the activation of multiple slip and cross-slip sys -
tems, with α lath boundaries acting as sources of prismatic < a > and pyramidal < a + c > dislocations to coor -
dinate plastic deformation. The exceptional strength originates primarily from the high density and 
entanglement of < a > and < a + c > dislocations, as well as from dislocation pinning by nanoscale SPPs. Addi -
tionally, the presence of < a > type dislocation loops further contribute to strength enhancement at 380 
◦
C. This 
work demonstrates the feasibility of LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy for potential structural applications in 
380 
◦
C high temperature reactor environments, offering insights into the advancement of AM technologies in the 
nuclear industry.
1. Introduction
As a critical pillar of the global energy system, nuclear power has 
evolved over the past seven decades into a stable, clean, and cost- 
effective source of baseload electricity [ 1 , 2 ]. A long-lasting and safe 
reactor core is paramount to the performance and reliability of nuclear 
reactors [ 2 – 4 ]. Core structural materials are required to exhibit low 
thermal neutron absorption cross-sections, favorable mechanical 
properties, excellent corrosion resistance, good fuel compatibility, and 
high irradiation stability. Compared to nickel-based, iron-based, and 
aluminum-based alloys, nuclear-grade zirconium alloys have been 
widely adopted as core structural materials, for example spacer grid and 
cladding [ 4 ], due to their superior combination of the aforementioned 
properties [ 3 , 5 ]. Among them, Zircaloy-4 has been the most extensively 
used alloy, serving as the primary material for fuel cladding and spacer 
grids in nuclear reactor applications. However, in recent years, the

growing demand for lightweight and miniaturized components in nu -

fabrication routes, creating an urgent need to develop advanced 
manufacturing technologies for complex integrated zirconium alloys 
[ 6 ].
Additive manufacturing (AM), based on a layer-by-layer deposition 
principle, offers exceptional freedom in geometric design and rapid 
prototyping capabilities [ 7 – 9 ], which has been widely used to reliably 
fabricate dense and complex components for various materials. In 
addition, the rapid solidification inherent to AM melt pool can produce 
refined microstructures, potentially enhancing the mechanical proper -
ties [ 10 – 13 ]. Consequently, AM has emerged as a potential approach for 
fabricating high performance structural materials for nuclear applica -
tions. In 2022, Oak Ridge and Argonne National Laboratories [ 14 , 15 ] 
have explored the use of ultrasonic additive manufacturing (UAM) for 
fabricating Zircaloy-4 alloy. In detail, Miao et al. [ 14 ] investigated the 
feasibility of UAM for this alloy using in-situ synchrotron wide-angle 
X-ray scattering, demonstrating that the mechanical properties of the 
UAM-fabricated Zircaloy-4 alloy were comparable to those of forged or 
sheet counterparts. Yan et al. [ 15 ] introduced δ -phase hydrides into 
UAM Zircaloy-4 by hydrogen charging in a controlled atmosphere, 
enhancing the alloy ’ s strength to approximately 650 MPa. However, due 
to the UAM technique is a sheet lamination additive/subtractive 
manufacturing process [ 16 ], the geometric complexity of the fabricated 
components is inherently limited.
In contrast to UAM technology, laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) has 
attracted tremendous attention for its capabilities to fabricate compo -
nents without dimensional and geometric limitations. Song et al. [ 17 ] 
have reported on the microstructural evolution and compressive prop -
erties of as-built and heat-treated Zircaloy-4 alloy fabricated by LPBF, 
achieving a compressive strength of 2130 MPa and a strain to failure of 
36 %, while their tensile performance has received far less attention. In 
fact, due to the extreme operating conditions within reactor cores, the 
tensile properties, particularly at elevated temperatures (~380 
◦
C), are 
of greater practical importance. Accordingly, understanding and 
enhancing the mechanical performance of LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 
alloy at both room and high temperatures has emerged as a research 
focus. In conventionally processed Zircaloy-4 (e.g., rolling), tensile 
behavior at both room and high temperatures can be tailored by 
manipulating grain size and the distribution/morphology of 
second-phase precipitates (SPPs), which strongly influence dislocation 
interactions [ 18 ]. For hexagonal close-packed (HCP) metals such as 
Zircaloy-4, deformation twinning and the activation of pyramidal < a +
c > dislocations play a pivotal role in enhancing deformability and 
strengthening [ 19 ]. Furthermore, the activation of < a + c > dislocations 
generally requires high internal stresses; for instance, Lin et al. [ 20 ] 
demonstrated their activation via thermomechanical deformation. Yan 
et al. [ 21 ] observed pyramidal < a + c > dislocations in laser directed 
energy deposition processed Ti65Zr30Cu5 alloys, suggesting that 
AM-induced residual stresses may facilitate their nucleation and glide. 
Moreover, the effective strengthening achieved by SPPs through dislo -
cation pinning and grain refining, thereby enhancing tensile perfor -
mance at both room- and high-temperature [ 22 ]. However, current 
studies on LPBF-fabricated Zircaloy-4 alloys have mainly focused on the 
effects microstructural on compressive properties at room temperature, 
whereas the influence of microstructural evolution on tensile behavior, 
at both room and elevated temperatures, remains unclear and warrants 
further investigation.
In this work, Zircaloy-4 alloy was fabricated via LPBF and investi -
gated under uniaxial tensile loading at both room temperature (RT) and 
high temperature (HT, 380 
◦
C). Comprehensive microstructural char -
acterizations before and after deformation-including assessments of in -
ternal stress, sub-grain boundaries, and dislocation structures-were 
conducted to elucidate the strengthening and toughening mechanisms 
operative at both RT and HT. The novelty of this work lies in system -
atically correlating LPBF-induced microstructural features, the role of 
SPPs, and dislocation-mediated deformation mechanisms across 
different temperatures. These findings provide valuable guidance for 
tailoring the HT mechanical performance of nuclear-grade zirconium 
alloys and demonstrate the potential of LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 for 
structural applications in 380 
◦
C reactor environments, advancing the 
application of AM in the nuclear industry.

average grain size of ~4.12 μ m.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure characteristics
Fig. 2 presents the XRD pattern of the LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 
alloy. According to the PDF pattern cards, zirconium exhibits two 
distinct crystal structures, where PDF #05 – 0665 corresponds to the 
hexagonal close-packed (HCP) α -Zr phase, and PDF #34 – 0657 corre -
sponds to the body-centered cubic (BCC) β -Zr phase. The diffraction 
peaks of the sample closely match those of the α -Zr phase, while no 
significant alignment with the β -Zr peaks. This indicates that the LPBF- 
processed Zircaloy-4 alloy predominantly consists of the α phase, 
consistent with the findings reported by Song et al. [ 17 ]. Moreover, the 
slight shifts observed in peak positions suggest that the presence of 
alloying elements and the rapid solidification inherent to the LPBF 
process have altered the lattice parameters.
Fig. 3 shows the microstructures of LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy.

the BD. ( Fig. 3 a). High magnification SEM ( Fig. 3 b) and EBSD inverse

4. Discussion
4.1. Microstructure formation mechanism of LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 
alloy
The microstructure of the LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy exhibits 
pronounced epitaxial columnar growth. The formation of such columnar 
grains is governed by the G/R criterion, where G represents the tem -
perature gradient and R denotes the solidification velocity. A high 
thermal gradient combined with a relatively low solidification rate 
stabilizes the solid-liquid interface, thereby favoring columnar grain 
formation [ 38 ]. Due to the high cooling rate inherent in LPBF melt pool, 
α phase precipitates from the metastable β phase in lath like 
morphology, following the BOR ( Fig. 6 h), and consequently presents a 
certain degree of crystallographic alignment ( Fig. 3 c). Similar features 
have also been observed in LPBF-processed titanium alloys [ 39 ].
In Zircaloy-4 alloy, Fe and Cr are the β phase stabilizers. The 
maximum solubilities of Fe and Cr in α -Zr are 24 ppm and 50 ppm, 
respectively [ 24 , 25 ], which are significantly lower than their nominal 
additions in the alloy. Under rapid solidification, both elements are 
prone to segregation, especially along grain boundaries. This behavior is 
consistent with observations in heat treated and quenched Zircaloy-4, 
where second phases of Zr(Fe, Cr)
2 
type SPPs with Fe:Cr atomic ratios 
of approximately 2:1 form either at boundaries or within grains during β 
to α phase transformation. Chai et al. [ 40 ] reported that rapid cooling 
rates suppress the size and number density of SPPs in Zr alloys. In this 
study, nanoscale Fe and Cr enriched precipitates were identified within 
the α laths of LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy, and TEM-EDS analysis 
confirmed an approximate atomic ratio of Fe:Cr ≈ 2:1. Further quanti -
tative analysis indicated a Zr:Fe:Cr ratio of approximately 6:2:1 
( Table 3 ). According to the Zr-Fe binary phase diagram, when the Fe 
content exceeds its maximum solid solubility in Zr, Fe tends to form SPPs 
such as Zr
2
Fe and ZrFe
2
. When temperature drops below 775 
◦
C, Zr
2
Fe 
decomposes into ZrFe
2 
[ 41 ]. The high cooling rate associated with the 
LPBF process can suppress phase decomposition. Based on this, it is 
proposed that the SPPs formed within the lath of LPBF fabricated 
Zircaloy-4 alloy have a nominal composition of Zr
2
(Fe, Cr). However, 
phase identification based solely on TEM-EDS analysis and phase dia -
gram interpretation is limited. Further characterization is necessary to 
determine the structure of this SPPs in future studies.

4.2.1. Deformation behavior
The LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy exhibits a δ

attributed to several factors.
On the one hand, the fracture elongations of 10.5 % at RT and 16.4 % 
at 380 
◦
C can be attributed to the activation of multiple slip systems and 
the cross-slip of both < a > and < a + c > dislocations. Firstly, HCP ma -
terials with a c/a ratio lower than 1.633 are more prone to activate 
multiple slip systems, facilitating to enhanced ductility [ 42 ]. Based on 
the TEM SAED, the calculated lattice parameters of the Zircaloy-4 alloy 
are a = 3.309 Å and c = 5.261 Å, corresponding to a c/a ratio of 1.590. 
Experimental results confirm the presence of prismatic < a > and pyra -
midal < a + c > slip systems in LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy ( Fig. 7 b 
and c). In general, compared with prismatic < a > dislocations, the 
activation of < a + c > dislocations require thermal activation or high 
stresses due to their higher critical resolved shear stress [ 43 ]. Long et al. 
[ 44 ] reported that the multiplication of < a + c > dislocations is closely 
related to the accumulation of < a > dislocations, which can induce 
localized stress concentrations sufficient to activate the more difficult 
< a + c > slip systems. This implies that the high internal stress intro -
duced by the LPBF process can promote the nucleation and glide of < a +
c > dislocations. In the RT tensile condition, the LPBF-induced high in -
ternal residual stress and the fine α -lath structure facilitate extensive 
dislocation storage, leading to significant accumulation of prismatic 
< a > dislocations. The localized stress concentrations generated by this

LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy, the α lath interfaces follow the BOR 
( Fig. 6 h), which further impedes dislocation glide across lath bound -
aries. In addition, elemental segregation of Fe and Cr at α lath bound -
aries ( Fig. 3 d) further obstructs dislocation transmission, thereby 
exacerbating the reduction in ductility [ 45 ].
4.2.2. Strengthening mechanisms
Compared to Zircaloy-4 alloys in different conditions and other 
nuclear-grade zirconium alloys, the LPBF-processed samples exhibit a 
remarkable strength advantage at RT ( Fig. 4 b). Moreover, at 380 
◦
C HT, 
the LPBF-processed samples still demonstrate significantly higher 
strength than its sheet counterpart. It is mainly attributed to the syn -
ergistic effect of fine α lath, high dislocation density, and SPPs.
In general, grain size is inversely correlated with mechanical 
strength of metallic materials, with nanometer-scale grains exhibit 
significantly higher strength than those with micrometer-scale grains. In 
this work, the grain size of the LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy (~1.93 
μ m, Fig. 3 c) is reduced by approximately 50 % compared to its sheet 
counterpart (~4.12 μ m, Fig. 1 c). The fine α lath with submicron widths 
serve as the foundational microstructural units responsible for the high 
strength of Zircaloy-4 alloy under RT and 380 
◦
C HT tensile loading.
Another effective strategy for enhancing alloy strength is the intro -
duction of precipitates or SPPs [ 46 , 47 ]. The precipitates or SPPs 
enhance the strength of alloy by obstructing dislocation motion. 
Therefore, it is reasonable to conclude that nanoscale enriched Fe and Cr 
SPPs hinder dislocation motion and reduce plastic deformation under 
tensile loading, which macroscopically results in a higher strength ( σ

5. Conclusions
In this work, high-strength Zircaloy-4 alloys with reasonably good 
ductility were successfully fabricated via LPBF. By comprehensively 
characterizing the microstructural evolution under RT and 380 
◦

tensile loading, the strengthening and toughening mechanisms were 
elucidated. The key findings are summarized as follows: 
(1) The microstructure of the LPBF-processed Zircaloy-4 alloy con -

lath aligned according to the BOR are present. Elemental segre -
gation of Fe and Cr is observed along the lath boundaries, while 
nanoscale SPPs with an Fe:Cr atomic ratio of approximately 2:1 is 
distributed within the α lath.
