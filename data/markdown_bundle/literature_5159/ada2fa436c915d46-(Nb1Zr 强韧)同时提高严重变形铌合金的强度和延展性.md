abstract
The present study is on the improved monotonic response of a severely deformed niobium-based alloy.
Nb-1Zr alloy was processed at room temperature using equal channel angular extrusion and cold rolling.
After 16 passes, a more than 2.5 times increase in yield strength is accompanied by a ductility level
exceeding the as-received state. Cold rolling of the processed billets resulted in tensile strengths exceed-
ing 1 GPa. The enhancement in both strength and ductility is explained by the microstructural evolution
and the low homologous processing temperature.

1. Introduction
Refractory materials are generally utilized in high temperature
applications owing to their high melting point. Speciﬁcally, nio-
bium is a preferred material with its low density, high ductility
and good formability. Addition of small amounts of zirconium
facilitates solid solution strengthening and is one of the most com-
monly utilized alloying elements for Nb [1,2]. With its stability
against irradiation, earlier studies on Nb-1Zr have mainly been dri-
ven by the ultimate goal of utilization in cladding applications and
structural components [3–5]. Although Zr and its alloys can be
used in biomedical applications [6], interest in using Nb-1Zr as a
potential alloy for orthopedic implants, surgical tools and cardio-
vascular stents due to its high corrosion resistance, favorable bio-
compatibility and osseo-integration properties has surfaced,
albeit its relatively low strength as a restriction [7].
Severe plastic deformation (SPD) via equal channel angular
extrusion/pressing (ECAE/P) has been a subject of great interest
due to its potential in enhancing mechanical properties via grain
reﬁnement. It was also shown that SPD followed by conventional
deformation techniques such as rolling is effective in further grain
size reﬁnement due to abrupt strain path changes involved [8].
However, only a few studies focused on the SPD of refractory mate-
rials [9–12]. From an application point of view both strength and
ductility are instrumental features. As such, the main focus is on
the concurrent enhancement of strength and ductility in severely
deformed Nb-1Zr via hybrid processing routes, while demonstrat-
ing the microstructure and texture evolution.
2. Experimental methods
The as-received material (composition denoted in Table 1 ) had
an average grain size of 70 lm and was severely deformed at room
temperature with a rate of 2.5 mm/s through a 90 /C176die via 8 passes
of route-Bc and 16 passes of route-E. Route-E consists of alternating
rotation of the billet by + 180 /C176and + 90 /C176around its long axis,
between successive passes [13]. Further cold rolling to 80% thick-
ness reduction took place without any intermediate annealing
steps. Room temperature tensile experiments were conducted at
a strain rate of 5 /C210-4 s/C01. Microstructure evolution was revealed
by transmission electron microscopy (TEM), performed using a
Philips CM-200 microscope operating at a voltage of 200 kV. Thin
foils for TEM were prepared from the ﬂow plane of the extruded
billets by twin-jet polishing utilizing a solution of 600 ml metha-
nol, 340 ml butanol and 60 ml perchloric acid. Crystallographic
textures were determined with a Philips X’Pert diffractometer uti-
lizing a Cu-K a source.

Fig. 2 represents the crystallographic texture evolution after
processing via (1 1 0) pole ﬁgures. Other than doubling the texture
strength, ECAE results in the formation of a ﬁber texture tilted
about 30 /C176from the ﬂow direction (FD). Both ECAE routes led to a
similar texture evolution. Though, the location of the (1 1 0) peaks
around the pole ﬁgure rims are slightly different in the two pro-
cessing schemes. There is only a limited number of studies report-
ing the texture evolution of body centered cubic (bcc) materials up
to a high number of ECAE passes and for different routes [16]. The
subtle differences in the textures of the 8B c and the 16E billets dis-
appeared after rolling. Thus, cold rolling to high reductions trans-
forms the ECAE texture to that representative of cold rolled bcc
materials [17].
As shown in Fig. 3 a, room temperature tensile experiments
along the extrusion direction showed substantial increase in both
strength and ductility of the Nb-1Zr after SPD. Strengthening
observed in the 8B c samples is slightly higher than that observed
in the 16E samples. However, 16E samples exhibit a much higher
ductility owing to the presence of strain hardening, governing
the onset of localized deformation. The uniform elongations in
both samples are in excess of 5% which is the required level for
many structural applications. There have been studies on ultra-
ﬁne grained (UFG) materials reporting the mutual enhancement
in strength and ductility by employing strategies such as introduc-
ing a bimodal grain size distribution, second phase precipitation
and low temperature deformation [18,19]. Here, notable improve-
ment in the mechanical response of UFG Nb-1Zr is reported with
the virtue of SPD, resolving the well-known strength-ductility
paradox.

room temperature (low homologous temperature of 0.11) is likely
to be preserved due to the suppressed recovery mechanisms. With
the same token, the dislocation storage ability and thus the strain
hardening is also maintained during room temperature tension
tests. Similar observations have been reported during room tem-
perature tensile behavior of liquid nitrogen rolled pure copper
(Th of 0.06) [19] and liquid nitrogen tensile behavior of pure cop-
per, iron and titanium [18]. Second, a high number of non-
equilibrium boundaries are created during SPD, making these
potential sites for the segregation of the impurity atoms [18].
The addition of Zr to pure Nb promotes a rate dependent behavior
by reducing the mobility of dislocations with increased pinning at
the boundaries [20,21]. These effects will collectively provide a
microstructure resistant to plastic instability and post-necking
softening [19].
As the level of reﬁnement and crystallographic texture evolu-
tions in both 8B c and 16E samples are similar, the contrast in their
plastic instability might arise from the differences in their grain
boundary characteristics such as misorientation and morphology.
It has been shown in a number of materials that the directionality
of dislocation arrangements created during severe deformation,
and their rearrangement by the new dislocations activated during
reloading play a dominant role on the yield and post-yield behav-
ior [22,23]. Also, non-equilibrium boundaries in UFG materials act
as signiﬁcant dislocation sources and accumulation of dislocations
at the boundaries may depend on the boundary character [24]. This
has implications on ductility such that a speciﬁc grain boundary
character distribution may lead to enhanced strain hardening
and facilitate mechanisms such as grain rotation or grain boundary
sliding [25]. In line with the above assertions, the grain boundary
morphology and misorientation should be the main contrasting
features that differentiate the mechanical behavior of 8B
c and
16E samples. Indeed, it is known that that a higher number of ECAE
passes will result in a more stable grain boundary structure con-
sisting of mainly high angle grain boundaries [8,13]. This is espe-
cially true for a hybrid ECAE route such as E where extensive
strain path changes occur.
Upon rolling down to 80% thickness reduction, both 8B c and 16E
samples exhibit improved strengths in comparison to those of the
ECAE processed samples ( Fig. 3b-3c). However, the ductility levels
diminished especially when tested along the rolling direction (RD).
The 8Bc samples demonstrate the highest ultimate tensile strength
(UTS) of over 1 GPa (almost three-fold of the as-received response)
when strained parallel to the RD, and lowest when strained at 45 /C176.
The ductility levels follow an opposite trend. For the 16E samples,
rolling induces similar responses for straining parallel and perpen-
dicular to the RD. As in the 8B c samples, the lowest strength and
the highest ductility is observed for the 45 /C176orientation. The qual-
itative similarities in the orientation dependence of both rolled
samples can be explained by their similar post-ECAE rolling tex-
tures. It is asserted that post-ECAE rolling is effective in maintain-
ing high strength and ductility when tested along speciﬁc
directions. The speciﬁc straining direction would depend on a
number of factors such as processing induced microstructure and
crystallographic texture [8,22].
4. Conclusion
In this study, Nb-1Zr alloy was severely deformed at room tem-
perature for simultaneous high strength and ductility without the
need of additional alloying elements or heat treatments. Decent
mechanical performance was achieved in the samples processed
by 16 passes of ECAE following route E as explained by the favor-
able microstructure obtained via severe plastic deformation at a
low homologous temperature. Abrupt strain paths applied by
post-ECAE rolling were instrumental in further enhancement of
strength levels up to 1 GPa.
