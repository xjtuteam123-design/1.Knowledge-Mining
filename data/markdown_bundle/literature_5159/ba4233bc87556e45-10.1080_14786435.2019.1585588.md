ABSTRACT
A Fe-based multi-component alloy, 60Fe-12Cr-10Mn-15Cu-
3Mo, was designed and fabricated for nuclear applications
in the present study. The crystal structure of the alloy is a
∼85% body – and 15% face –centered cubic. A detailed
microstructure analysis indicated that Cu segregated in the
alloy to form Cu-rich precipitates, and the Cu precipitates
grew during high temperature annealing at up to 773 K.
The high temperature tensile test of the alloys showed that
both the yield stress and tensile strength of the 60Fe-12Cr-
10Mn-15Cu-3Mo were greater than those of typical
austenitic and ferritic stainless steels at 773 K. The Vickers
microhardness of the designed alloy did not change after
high temperature annealing for 1 h at up to 1073 K. The
results indicated that the designed alloy has the potential
to be used for high temperature applications.

1. Introduction
Steel-based materials are often used as nuclear materials because of their abun-
dance in the engineering database. For example, austenitic stainless steels
[crystal structure: face – centered cubic (FCC)] and ferritic/martensitic steels
[crystal structure: body – centered cubic (BCC)] are used as structural materials
for nuclear reactors that are subjected to high ﬂuxes of high –energy neutrons.
However, swelling is an important issue in austenitic stainless steels used as
nuclear materials because only small dimensional changes can be tolerated in
most reactor components [ 1,2]. High activation is another issue a ﬀecting the
performance of austenitic stainless steels. Moreover, the e ﬀect of irradiation
on the toughness of ferritic/martensitic steels is a major concern. Neutron
irradiation greatly increases the ductile –brittle transition temperature (DBTT)
and decreases the upper shelf energy [ 3]. Therefore, it is necessary to develop

a new low –activation material with high performance for reactors that employ
high ﬂuxes of high –energy neutrons. In recent years, high –entropy alloys
(HEAs), which are composed of four or more metallic elements mixed in an
equimolar or near –equimolar ratio [ 4–7], have attracted signi ﬁcant attention
owing to their excellent mechanical properties, such as high strength, high duct-
ility, and good heat and corrosion resistances. HEAs are the potential candidate
structural materials in advanced nuclear reactors. However, their radiation
resistance is not clear. For example, Kumar et al. showed that a Fe-Ni-Mn-Cr
HEA with the composition 27%Fe-28Ni%-27%Mn-18%Cr exhibited a high radi-
ation resistance [ 8]. They irradiated the HEA with Ni ions of energies 3.0 or
5.8 MeV at temperatures ranging from room temperature to 973 K, and up to
10 displacements per atom (dpa). High –density, small dislocation loops were
formed at all temperatures, but voids were not observed for any irradiation con-
dition. Xia et al. also irradiated the Co-Cr-Cu-Fe-Ni HEA, of FCC structure,
with high –energy electrons and found that the structure was stable even after
an irradiance of 40 dpa [ 9]. In addition, they irradiated an Al
1.5CoCrFeNi
HEA of BCC structure with Au ions, which was also stable. Unfortunately,
the Co and Ni (especially Co) components of the Co-Cr-Cu-Fe-Ni and Fe-Ni-
Mn-Cr HEAs acquired higher activations after neutron irradiation. It is there-
fore necessary to design multi-component alloys comprising low –activation
elements for high temperature applications in nuclear structures. In the
present study, a Fe-based multi-component alloy, Fe-Cr-Mn-Cu-Mo, without
the high–activation elements of Co and Ni was developed. Because of the exten-
sive engineering database of Fe-based alloys, Fe-based multi-component alloys
are desired. Low –activation elements of Cr and Mn were selected owing to
their common usage for steel hardening. The purpose of adding Cu into the
alloy was to make it precipitate as Cu-rich clusters, due to the low solubility
of Cu in the Fe-based solid solution. Hence, the precipitation of small sized
Cu-rich phases is expected to enhance the high temperature mechanical proper-
ties of the alloy. A small amount of Mo was also added into the alloy to increase
the alloy strength, although it is a high –activation element. Given the good
irradiation resistance of the BCC phase, and making use of the precipitation
strengthening of the Cu-rich FCC phase for the BCC matrix, a two-phase struc-
ture 60Fe-12Cr-10Mn-15Cu-3Mo (at.%) multi-component alloy was designed
in the present work. The purpose of this work was to investigate the thermal
stability of microstructures and mechanical properties of the 60Fe-12Cr-
10Mn-15Cu-3Mo alloy. The results indicated that the designed alloy is promis-
ing for high temperature applications.

3. Results and discussion

the BCC and FCC structures are 0.2876 ± 0.0001 and 0.3649 ± 0.0001 nm,
respectively. As shown in Figure 1(b), the XRD pattern did not change even

4. Conclusion
A Fe-based multi-component alloy 6 0Fe-12Cr-10Mn-15Cu-3Mo was devel-
oped for use in nuclear reactors employing high ﬂuxes of high-energy neu-
trons. The crystal structure of t he 60Fe-12Cr-10Mn-15Cu-3Mo alloy
comprises both BCC and FCC, in a mass ratio of approximately 85:15. The
yield stress of the 60Fe-12Cr-10M n-15Cu-3Mo alloy at 773 K was much
higher than those of typical stainless steels (such as 304, 316, and 340).
Thermal e ﬀects of the 60Fe-12Cr-10Mn-15Cu-3Mo alloy on its structural
stability and mechanical properties were investigated. The crystal structure
of the 60Fe-12Cr-10Mn-15Cu-3Mo all oy was stable below 773 K, although
Cu segregation occurred at temperatures greater than 473 K. In addition,
the yield stress and tensile strength of the 60Fe-12Cr-10Mn-15Cu-3Mo
alloy at 773 K after annealing at 873 K for 1 h decreased by ∼10% compared
to those of the as-cast alloy.
