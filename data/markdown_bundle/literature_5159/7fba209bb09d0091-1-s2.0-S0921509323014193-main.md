ABSTRACT 
Designing high-entropy alloys with sufficient high temperature bearing capacity is highly expected for practical 
structural applications. In this work, we developed a new class of Ni – Co – Cr – Al system high-entropy superalloys 
(HESAs) with superb high temperature mechanical properties in a wide temperature range by controllably 
introducing stable and coherent nanoscale L1
2 
precipitates into a face-centered-cubic (fcc) structure matrix with 
low stacking fault energy (SFE). The structural features, mechanical properties, underlying deformation and 
strengthening mechanisms at room temperature (RT) and elevated temperatures were systematically investi -
gated. It has been shown that the addition of Ti, Nb, Ta, Mo and W into Ni – Co – Cr – Al base alloy reduces the SFE 
of the fcc matrix and results in the precipitation of nanoscale near-spherical multicomponent particles with 
coherent interface. The HESAs exhibit the RT yield strength (YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and elongation 
up to 1167 MPa, 1632 MPa and 14.8 %, respectively, and the UTS up to ~1100 MPa at 750 
◦
C, which are su -
perior to those of traditional fcc-type HEAs and typical wrought Ni-based superalloys. The superb mechanical 
properties of the HESAs are attributed to multiple work-hardening behavior, resulting from pronounced multisets 
of stacking faults (SFs), deformation-induced microbands, dislocation cells and high density of dislocation walls. 
Specifically, the high temperature strengthening of the HESAs may be attributed to parallel SFs, Lomer-Cottrell 
locks and the dislocation shearing of γ ′ precipitates via the antiphase boundary mechanism. 
1. Introduction 
Microstructurally and mechanically stable alloys that can survive 
over a wide range of temperatures are highly desired to meet the de -
mands of a variety of engineering applications such as aero-turbine 
engines and gas combustion turbines etc. [ 1 ]. In recent years, multi -
component alloys, also named high-entropy alloys (HEAs) [ 2 ], have 
received considerable attention due to their unique merits such as 
high-entropy effect, sluggish diffusion and severe lattice distortion, as 
well as the so-called “ cocktail effect ” [ 3 ]. The HEAs with single fcc-type 
matrix possess excellent cryogenic and RT performance by triggering 
twinning or localized phase transformation [ 4 , 5 ], but lack sufficient 
strength at elevated temperatures, making them far from qualified 
candidates for high-temperature structural applications [ 6 , 7 ].

4. Discussion 
4.1. Deformation mechanism of designed HESAs at RT 
The fracture morphologies of the three HESAs at RT are shown in 
Fig. 7 . It is seen that all alloys exhibit a mixed fracture mode of plastic 
and cleavage fractures. The difference among the three alloys is that RA- 
5 alloy contains most of the toughening dimples with maximum depth, 
and RA-10 alloy contains more cleavage plates on the fracture surface. 
These results are reasonable considering that RA-5 alloy has the lowest 
volume fraction and finest γ ′ nanoparticles. This feature of fracture 
morphologies is consistent with those tensile curves shown in Fig. 5 (a). 
The detailed microstructures of the HESAs after fractured at RT were 
investigated by TEM. As shown in Fig. 8 (a

first set of SFs, which may act as effective barriers to pin the dislocation 
motion and promote the dislocation accumulation as Frank-Read sour -
ces, leading to a steady and progressive plastic deformation under high 
applied stress [ 42 , 43 ]. Therefore, the cross slip of dislocations can be 
regarded as an effective strengthening and toughening pathway. The

5. Conclusion 
Based on experimental results and theoretical modeling, the con -
clusions can be summarized as follows: 
1) A new strategy of designing HESAs with superb mechanical prop -
erties in a wide temperature range was innovated by controllable 
introducing stable and coherent nanoscale L1
2 
precipitates into the 
fcc-type matrix with low SFE. 
2) The microstructures of HESAs are composed of an fcc matrix and 
uniformly distributed near-spherical L1
2 
precipitates in size from 
17.0 ± 1 to 31.7 ± 5 nm. The lattice misfit is nicely in the range from 
 0.22 % to 0.25 %, indicating the coherent feature of the γ / γ ′ 
interface. SFs and twins across the matrix and nanoparticles were 
formed. Al, Ti, Nb, and Ta atoms mainly enrich in L1
2 
phase, while 
Cr, W and Mo atoms mainly enrich in the matrix and are also slightly 
dissolved in γ ′ nanoparticles. 
3) The HESAs exhibit excellent mechanical properties with the YS, UTS 
and elongation up to 1167 MPa, 1632 MPa and 14.8 %, respectively, 
at RT, and the UTS of ~1100 MPa at 750 
◦
C for RA-10 alloy, which 
are superior to those of most fcc HEAs and IN718 wrought Ni-based 
superalloy. 
4) In fractured microstructure at RT, pronounced multisets of SFs, 
deformation-induced microbands, DCs and HDDWs have been 
observed, which result in multiple work-hardening behavior for 
these HESAs. At elevated temperatures, parallel SFs and L-C locks 
were formed. The density of SFs is relatively low, and the APB energy 
of γ ′ precipitate was also reduced, which may account for the 
decreased strain-hardening response as compared to that at RT. The 
paired dislocations are visible in these HESAs, confirming the 
dislocation shearing of γ ′ precipitates via the APB mechanism. 
5) The comprehensive strengthening effects including frictional stress 
effect, solid solution, precipitation and grain boundary strengthening 
on the YS of current HESAs were evaluated. It is shown that pre -
cipitation strengthening accounts for about ~50 % of the YS in RA- 
10 alloy, indicating the dominant role of nanoparticle precipitation.
