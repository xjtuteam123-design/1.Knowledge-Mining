abstract
Phase transformation and crystal structure variety will inﬂuence microstructure evolution and hot defor-
mation behaviour of high niobium containing Ti-Al alloys. These alloys are great attention in research on
industrial and medical sectors. The niobium alloys are lightweight and high strength, increasingly popu-
lar for automotive engine and chassis components. Three novel Ti-Al based alloys have been developed in
present research such as Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr, Ti-43Al-7Nb-3Mo-2Cr and Ti-43Al-8Nb-3Mo-3Cr nio-
bium alloys were fabricated using a hot isostatic press (HIP). The alloys were heat-treated at 1200 /C176C tem-
perature for 5 h in the Ar atmosphere. The mechanical properties and ﬂow curves of Ti-Al alloys are
studied by varying three different temperatures (800 to 1200 /C176C) and strain rates (0.1 to 10 s
/C0 1)o na
servo-controlled universal testing machine. The microstructures of the alloys were analysed using
SEM. The results showed an addition of Nb-Mo-Cr particles to Ti-Al increased material strength by
21.36% (Hardness), 32.08% (compressive), 41.37% (splitting tensile) and 39.23% (ﬂexural) more than as-
cast alloy. In addition, Nb and Cr with Mo will provide excellent oxidation resistance and mechanical
strength for Ti-Al alloys. This study shows a benchmark against compatible alloys and metallic implants.

1. Introduction
Niobium materials have increased day-to-day due to their high
strength and good corrosion resistance. Stainless steels, Nb-Cr
alloys, and titanium alloys are often biomedical implants and aero-
space applications. These metallic materials are often non-toxic,
and the implant, when subjected to chemical and mechanical dam-
ages, replaces the metals, which tends to improve its strength [1].
Usage of aluminium and titanium alloys as lightweight materials
were become increasingly popular for automotive engine and
chassis components because of their ability to reduce fuel con-
sumption and improve engine performance. Low density and good
mechanical strength make aluminium metal matrix composites an
attractive material for lightweight vehicle components. Due to this,
Al-Ti alloys have equal strength, three times the thermal conduc-
tivity and reduced density are excellent for the manufacturing of
vehicle and commercial parts. Hence, the development of niobium
metallic materials plays a crucial role today. Generally, Ti-Al alloys
and 316L stainless steel are used for aerospace applications.
Despite their excellent When compared to other presently used
biomaterials, Ti-based alloys are garnering great attention even
in orthopaedical applications [2]. Recently, low modulus b Ti alloys
with non-toxic elements are being developed, such as pistons, con-
necting rods, wheels of the cars, artiﬁcial hip joints, bone plates,
etc. Ti-alloys are divided into
a alloys, b alloys, and a + b alloys.
When added to Ti alloys in various proportions, the interstitial ele-
ments like oxygen increase the yield stress of the alloy. The addi-
tion of
a stabilizers such as Al and Mo-Nb-Cr to Ti alloys with a
hexagonal close-packed structure at low temperatures stabilize
the a phase. According to previous studies [3], a-Ti alloys are con-
sidered treatable heat alloys. HIP treatment improves the alloy’s
strength, and these alloys are easy to weld. They exhibit interme-
diate strength, considerable creep strength, and excellent corrosion
resistance. It is well known that a + b alloys are the majorly used
commercial Ti alloys for various aerospace, sports, biomedical

Due to optimization of the hot isostatic press (HIP) heat treat-
ments, the alloying elements exhibit different crystal structures
and properties, resulting in different microstructures forming to
achieve the required mechanical properties. In general, various
heat treatments are given to the Ti-Al alloys is: (a) To reduce the
residual stress that is formed during the alloy’s fabrication, (b) Har-
dening aims to produce a better blend of ductility, machinability
and structural stability, (c) Using ageing and solution treatment
to improve strength. (d) To improve qualities such as fracture
toughness, fatigue resistance, and high heat creep resilience. Previ-
ously, the connecting rod was replaced by Co-Cr alloys, but the
strength is not sustained in high pressure. However, an alternative,
commercially pure Ti alloy, is still a problem because aluminium
and vanadium induce a possible cytotoxic effect when released
into the body. So, in the present work, an attempt was made by
combining Ti-Al-Nb-Mo-Cr to overcome defects. Vinod et al. [4]
suggested that the strain softens and the ﬂow stresses level off
before the steady-state is achieved. Increasing the temperature
and decreasing the strain rate tends to downfall peak stress. The
peak contrary raises while increasing strain rate value, leading to
work softening. Hence, stable behaviour such as dynamic recrystal-
lization took place under hot deformation. Xiao et al. [5] concluded
that the ﬂow stress increases as strain rate increases and temper-
ature decreases, stated by the Zener-Hollomon parameter. The
ﬂow softening will take place mainly due to the formation of
dynamic recovery and dynamic recrystallization. While the DRY
is occurred at high InZ values and soften, the mechanism trans-
forms from DRY to DRX at low Z values. However, a grain size of
recrystallization becomes larger when decreasing the Z values,
and deformation bands disappear. Wang et al. [6] predicted the
hot deformation behaviour of base alloy by using ﬂow curves. At
the onset of yielding, two mechanisms such as softening and hard-
ening, are identiﬁed when strain increases. Hence, the restoration
mechanisms such as DRY and DRX act as softening mechanisms,
perhaps dominant over the work hardening mechanism. Hence, it
leads to softening the material and ﬂow stress decrease. Therefore,
the ﬂow stress increases as increasing strain rate. The main objec-
tive of this research is to investigate the effects of hot deformation
behaviour by using different weight percentages and the
microstructure evolution of Ti-Al–Nb–Mo–Cr alloys. The ﬂow
stress curves analysis is evaluated to analyze the material beha-
viour. The mechanical properties are studied for three different
compositions.

The materials used in this investigation consist of pure Ti (99 %),
Al (99 %), Al-Nb (Nb: 68 wt%), Al-Mo (65 wt%), Al-Cr (63 wt%)
alloys. The high novel Ti-Al alloys are chosen with three combina-
tions such as Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr, Ti-43Al-7Nb-3Mo-2Cr and Ti-
43Al-8Nb-3Mo-3Cr alloys were fabricated using a hot isostatic
press (HIP). Before fabrication, the mechanical alloying process
was carried out in a high energy planetary ball milling equipment
utilized for developing Ti-Al-Nb-Mo-Cr was carried by varying
weight fractions within the matrix and samples named S

3.1. Porosity measurement
The amount of porosity found in matrix composites and the dis-
tribution of its dimensions are essential factors in the regulation of
mechanical buildings. When it comes to achieving the desired high
machining efﬁciency, it is necessary that porosity be kept to a min-
imum level. It is possible to calculate porosity as a function of the
sampling weight discrepancies between saturated and fully dry
weights and the weight difference between saturated and put on
hold weights using Porosity & Softness Tester equipment, Model:
GEC-P40113-B. The porosity of a material can be determined by
applying an equation (1) [9] . The porosity of Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-
1Cr, Ti-43Al-7Nb-3Mo-2Cr and Ti-43Al-8Nb-3Mo-3Cr alloys are
shown in Fig. 1 . The observed results show that the addition of
8Nb-3Mo-3Cr particles (2.15%) has considerably reduced open
pores compared to Ti-43Al-6Nb-1Mo-1Cr (2.48%). Increasing the
concentration of niobium, molybdenum, and chromium decreases
the porosity rate for Ti-43Al-7Nb-3Mo-2Cr (2.33%). Reduced poros-
ity of Ti-43Al-8Nb-3Mo-3Cr alloy is observed and compared to the
other three concentrations. The results show that the addition of
8Nb-3Mo-3Cr is effective because pores are reduced, and the pro-
duct’s mechanical qualities are improved as a result.

3.7. Microstructural analysis
SEM examines the fracture surface appearance of a broken spec-
imen. The images of a fracture surface of broken tensile samples
are shown in Fig. 8. Micro-voids cause cracks initiation and forma-
tion in composites, resulting in ductile fracture. Finer dimples
characterize the ductile fracture on the fracture surface due to
the micro-voids joining together. Transganular grain boundary
movement and transgranular cleavage formations cause compos-
ites to fracture in a brittle manner. According to SEM data, compos-
ite sampling fails because of the combination of ductile and fragile
fracture. The SEM (Sample S 1) shows that micro-voids from the ini-
tial cracks and circulate lead to a ﬂexible fracture, as shown by the
crack surface. Ti-Al-enhanced 8Nb-3Mo-3Cr particles cause the
grain boundaries to be smooth and the surface of the specimen
to be smooth. Campos-Quirós et al. [20] found that the resistance
of crossbreed composite cracks to tensile pressure varied depend-
ing on whether or not fracture initiation and propagation led to
brittle or ﬂexible cracks. Nevertheless, the proper combination of
ceramic bits reduces this effect.
4. Conclusions
Ti-Al alloys have attracted much attention from aerospace
researchers because of their less weight and high strength. The
conclusions of present study as follows:.
1. The porosity is reduced in addition to 8% Nb and 3% Cr-Mo,
tends to improved mechanical properties. The lowest porosity
is 2.15% due to low wettability and good uniform distribution
between particles.
2. The hardness, compressive and ﬂexural strength is increased
(451 VH, 898 MPa, 1376 MPa) for Ti-43Al-8Nb-3Mo-3Cr alloy
compare to other alloys.
3. Additionally, the nucleation of DRX grains is identiﬁed in defor-
mation with increasing strain rates (1 s /C0 1) below 1000 /C176C tem-
perature. It also restricts subgrain boundary development at
800 /C176C with the strain rate of 10 s /C0 1 produced by dislocation
motion, which controls deformation.
4. Ti-43Al-Nb-Mo-Cr alloys with strain compensation have a value
of apparent deformation activation energy based on ﬂow stress
curves is considered to incorporate the inﬂuence of plastic
strain on their material properties. The experimental results
are usually by the estimated ﬂow stresses in different deforma-
tion circumstances. Due to strengthening, the ﬂow stress of Ti-
43Al-8Nb-3Mo-3Cr was higher and smoother than that of other
alloys at all deformation conditions.
5. Increasing the temperature and decreasing the strain rate result
in decreasing the ﬂow stress of Ti-43Al-8Nb-3Mo-3Cr alloy.
6. The microstructure of Ti-43Al-8Nb-3Mo-3Cr alloy shows pore-
free compact compare to other alloys.
