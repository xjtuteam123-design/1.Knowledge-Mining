ABSTRACT
A series of novel AlNbTiV
2
Si
x 
(x = 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, and 0.5) refractory high-entropy alloys were fabricated by 
vacuum arc melting. The effects of Si content on the crystal structure, microstructure and high-temperature 
mechanical properties of the alloys were systematically investigated, and the mechanism of properties 
enhancement was analyzed in depth. The results showed that the addition of Si resulted in the formation of a 
hexagonal close-packed M
5
Si
3
-type silicide phase (M = Al, Nb, Ti, and V), transforming the alloy from a single 
BCC structure to a dual-phase structure of BCC + M
5
Si
3
, and increasing the volume fraction of M
5
Si
3 
phase to 
23.1 %. Furthermore, an increase in Si content is beneficial to reduce the density of the alloy. When the Si 
content reaches 0.5, the alloy density decreases by about 7.0 %. The results of high-temperature compression 
tests show that the yield strength of AlNbTiV
2
Si
x 
alloys has increased by nearly 70 % at 873 K, and AlNbTiV
2
Si
0.5 
alloy has the highest yield strength at 1073 K, reaching 1154 MPa. The mechanism of properties enhancement is 
attributed to the second phase strengthening. When the temperature continues to increase to 1273 K, the 
strengthening effect of silicides becomes weaker, and the yield strength of AlNbTiV
2
Si
x 
alloys is not significantly 
different, all around 200 MPa, indicating the dominant role of solid solution strengthening. In addition, the 
solidification path of AlNbTiV
2
Si
x 
alloys was analyzed and discussed. Our current work contributes to the design 
of new high-performance lightweight alloys with controllable structure, and provides a reference for future 
research, development, and production utilization.
1. Introduction
Catering to the rapid development of the aerospace industry and 
industrial production and offering support, materials that can be applied 
at extremely high temperatures and have both high strength and low 
density are currently needed [ 1 – 3 ]. However, the development of 
widely used traditional Ni-based, Ti-Al-based and other superalloys is 
approaching its limit [ 4 , 5 ], and it is difficult to make significant im -
provements. In today ’ s rapidly developing aviation technology, the 
development of high-temperature resistant materials with higher service 
temperature also faces arduous challenges, and the huge development 
space of refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) seems to be a break -
through to solve this problem [ 6 , 7 ]. Refractory high-entropy alloys were 
proposed by Professor O.N. Senkov of the U.S. Air Force Laboratory in 
2010 and have received widespread attention since its advent [ 6 , 7 ]. 
Over the past decade, this type of alloy has been widely studied, and this 
material performs extremely well, especially at high temperatures. 
Therefore, it is considered to have broad development prospects and is 
expected to become a new generation of high-temperature materials 
[ 8 – 13 ].
The typical RHEA of MoNbTaW and MoNbTaVW with a single center 
cubic structure prepared by Senkov et al. [ 6 , 7 ] can achieve a yield 
strength of over 400 MPa at 1673 K, demonstrating an excellent 
high-temperature resistance. Nevertheless, the potential problems of 
those RHEAs are the high density (greater than 12 g/cm
3
) and the low 
fracture strain (about 2 % at room temperature). With the continuous 
exploration of researchers in recent years, a variety of RHEAs with low 
density and different excellent properties have been developed. For

Si elements in this RHEAs with excellent high-temperature mechanical 
properties, and to further explore the specific strengthening mechanism. 
This study not only provides a insight into the mechanical properties of 
RHEAs, but also proposes a theoretical basis to assist in the design of new 
RHEAs.

Si

come from Beijing Dream Material Technology Co., Ltd. (purity of each 
element is > 99.95 %), and the quality of the ingot obtained by smelting 
is about 100 g. All samples are melted at least 5 times to ensure complete 
melting and uniform composition of the ingot. For convenience, ac -
cording to the different Si content in each alloy, they are named as 
RHSi0, RHSi0.1, RHSi0.2, RHSi0.3, RHSi0.4, RHSi0.5 alloy in sequence.
All samples undergo grinding and polishing treatment before testing 
(using SiC sandpaper polish to 2000 #, and then polishing by the 
VibroMet 2 vibration polishing machine from BUEHLER company) to 
reduce surface scratches and residual stress. The crystal structures of the 
alloys were tested using a German Bruker D8 Advance X-ray diffrac -

◦

◦

analyze the distribution of chemical element distribution of each sam -

at a more microscopic scale, also equipped with an EDS detector. The 
sample was prepared using a GATAN 691 ion thinning instrument.

stress and lattice distortion. The high-resolution transmission electron 
microscopy (HRTEM) image of RHSi0.5 alloy BCC matrix is shown in

dislocation slip difficult and increasing the strength of the alloy [ 40 ].
