ABSTRACT 
Uniaxial forging (UAF) and multiaxial forging (MAF) were conducted on a Ti-45Al-7Nb-0.3W (at. %) alloy 
produced by powder metallurgy (PM) technique, and the deformed microstructures and deformation mechanism 
were investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Results 
show that the near-gamma microstructure (NG) transformed into a fine and uniform duplex microstructure (DP) 
after MAF, and the mean grain size of the multiaxial forged alloy was 5 μ m. The microstructure of the uniaxial 
forged alloy was inhomogeneous and anisotropy, while the microstructure of multiaxial forged alloy was very 
uniform and a higher amount of the β phase was distributed at the grain boundaries. During forging, deformation 
induced microstructure changes include dislocation movements, mechanical twinning, layer decomposition and 
dynamic recrystallization (DRX). The DRX occurred repeatedly and more severely during MAF than UAF process. 
Correspondingly, the multiaxial forged TiAl alloy exhibited excellent high temperature mechanical properties 
compared to the uniaxial forged alloy. At a temperature of 750

1. Introduction 
TiAl alloys are considered as novel and attractive high temperature 
structural materials for application in the aerospace and auto industry 
due to their advantages such as low density, high strength, and excellent 
creep resistance [ 1 – 4 ]. However, due to the intrinsic brittleness of TiAl 
based alloys they exhibit low room-temperature ductility and poor 
hot-deformability, which have severely limited the wide applications of 
TiAl alloys [ 5 , 6 ]. 
In recent years, lots of efforts have been made to improve deform -
ability and enhance mechanical properties of TiAl alloys by various 
methods such as alloying, hot working and heat treatments [ 7 – 10 ]. El -
ements to stabilize the β phase such as Nb, Mo and Cr have been added to 
increase the volume fraction of the β phase, which can improve the 
thermal processing performance of TiAl alloys [ 11 – 13 ]. By thermal 
processing, uniform and refined microstructures can be efficiently ob -
tained and thus TiAl alloys are expected to have enhanced mechanical 
performance. In previous studies, powder metallurgical TiAl sheets have 
already been produced by hot pack-rolling [ 14 , 15 ], and the sheets have 
exhibited the ability of superplastic deformation [ 16 ]. The pack-forging 
is an effective method to address the issue of low hot-formability of TiAl 
alloys, and Li et al. [ 17 ] has successfully obtained a pancake with a large 
size of Φ 145 mm � 10 mm by pack-forging. Moreover, the multiaxial 
forging (MAF) is a representative severe plastic deformation technology 
to obtain microstructures with ultra-fine grains and to eliminate “ dead 
zone ” , which has been successfully applied in Ti alloys, Mg alloys, Al 
alloys and so on [ 18 – 20 ]. Imayev et al. [ 21 ] obtained TiAl alloys with 
ultrafine microstructure by MAF at 800

the increase of strain and dislocations are piled up and tangled to form 
dislocation walls ( Fig. 5 (a)), which requires a higher stress to move 
those dislocations. In addition, mechanical twinning can be activated to

Some studies indicated that for high Nb containing TiAl alloys, the 
stacking fault energy (SFE) could be decreased by the alloying element 
Nb, and thus, mechanical twinning could be more easily triggered [ 31 ]. 
As the forging process proceeds, at regions with high stress concentra -
tion, the decomposition of the α
2
/ γ lamellar colonies is likely to occur 
and leads to the formation of equiaxed γ , α
2

twinning play important roles in the early stage of hot-deformation, 
while dynamic recrystallization dominates in the later stage of defor -
mation [ 27 ]. As the forging proceeds to a certain extent, the dynamic 
recrystallization (DRX) could be activated by high density of disloca -
tions, mechanical twins and α
2
/ γ lamellar decomposition. As shown in 
Figs. 4 and 5 , a higher amount of DRX grains can be observed in the 
specimen after multiaxial forging (MAF) than the one after uniaxial 
forging (UAF), indicating that the DRX occurs more severely and thor -
oughly during MAF. Furthermore, the DRX has been considered to be the 
main softening mechanism of TiAl alloys [ 32 ], which plays an important 
role during forging to reduce the dislocation density and release stress 
concentration. During forging, the fine DRX grains can promote the 
movement of grain boundaries and rotation of grains, so that the 
deformation can be conducted more uniformly and coordinately, which 
is beneficial to deform TiAl alloys. 
Multiaxial forging (MAF) is an attractive method that can refine the 
grains and homogenize the microstructure. As shown in Fig. 4 , the 
microstructure of the uniaxial forged specimen is inhomogeneous and 
streamline, while the microstructure of the multiaxial forged specimen 
is very uniform and the grains are very fine. Furthermore, the MAF can 
eliminate “ dead zone ” produced by uniaxial forging (UAF) [ 33 ], and the 
microstructural homogeneity of the TiAl alloy can be dramatically 
improved by MAF. Meanwhile, the DRX occurs repeatedly and more 
severely during MAF than UAF, so that a higher amount of fine equiaxed 
grains can be obtained in the microstructure after MAF than UAF. Ac -
cording to previous studies [ 30 , 34 , 35 ], it is well known that fine equi -
axial grains were mostly caused by dynamical recrystallization (DRX) 
during high temperature deformation. During the MAF, the orientation 
of the deformation zone changes successively with the change of the 
applied load, which is advantageous for accumulation of activation 
energy required for DRX and the formation of DRX grains [ 36 ]. In 
addition, the increase in grain boundary interface due to fine grains 
provides more nucleation sites for the β phase, so that a higher amount 
of the β phase forms at the grain boundaries in the multiaxial forged 
specimen than the uniaxial forged specimen owing to the repeated and 
severe DRX during MAF [ 37 ].

5. Conclusions 
(1) The microstructure of the HIPed Ti-45Al-7Nb-0.3W alloy shows a 
near- γ microstructure with a mean grain size of about 12 μ m, 
which is mainly composed of the γ phase, together with the α
2 
phase and a trace amount of the β phase distributed at the grain 
boundaries. 
(2) The multiaxial forged TiAl alloy exhibits a refined and uniform 
duplex microstructure with a mean grain size of 5 μ m, due to the 
repeatedly and severely DRX. The microstructure of the uniaxial 
forged alloy is inhomogeneous and streamline, while the micro -
structure of the multiaxial forged alloy is uniform, and the β 
phase with a higher amount is distributed at the grain boundaries 
after MAF. 
(3) The multiaxial forged TiAl alloy shows excellent high tempera -
ture mechanical properties with the UTS of 697 MPa at 750 
�

and the elongation of 173% at 900
