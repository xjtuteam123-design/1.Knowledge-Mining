ABSTRACT 
For tungsten (W), doping with second-phase particles is a commonly employed method to enhance its properties. 
Experiments have observed that oxide dispersion-strengthened tungsten alloys (ODS-W) exhibit superior and 
more stable ultimate tensile strength (UTS) at elevated temperatures. To elucidate this phenomenon, in situ 
tensile deformation experiments were conducted on ODS-W using transmission electron microscopy at tem -
peratures up to 1273 K. Under the influence of temperature and tensile stress, a phase transition from BCC to FCC 
occurred near the oxide interfaces in W. This BCC – FCC phase transformation was corroborated by combining 
molecular dynamics simulations (MD) with density functional theory (DFT), confirming that the transformed 
FCC W matrix possesses an exceptional adhesion work, significantly enhancing the bonding strength at the W- 
oxide interfaces. Based on these observations, this work provides a plausible explanation for the high UTS 
retention of ODS-W at high temperatures. These findings offer valuable insights into the development of oxide- 
strengthened alloys for high-temperature applications and present a novel perspective on the mechanisms of 
second-phase reinforcement. 
1. Introduction 
Tungsten (W) is a body-centered cubic (BCC) metal known for its 
excellent mechanical properties at high temperatures, finding wide -
spread applications in various high-temperature environments [ 1 ]. 
However, its ultimate tensile strength (UTS) tends to decrease with 
increasing temperature, which limits its use in high-temperature settings 
[ 2 – 5 ]. To lower tungsten ’ s ductile-to-brittle transition temperature 
(DBTT), rhenium (Re) is commonly added to the tungsten matrix [ 6 , 7 ]; 
however, a reduced DBTT often comes at the expense of the UTS at 
elevated temperatures. Both pure tungsten and W
–
Re alloys inevitably 
experience a significant reduction in UTS at high temperatures [ 8 – 10 ]. 
Research indicates that doping with a second phase can enhance the 
mechanical properties of W [ 11 ]. ODS-W alloys display good high- 
temperature stability, with a gradual decrease in UTS as the tempera -
ture increases. The presence of the second phase plays a crucial role in 
the high-temperature tensile strength. The strengthening mechanisms of 
ODS alloys can be attributed to two factors: first, second-phase particles 
restrict grain growth of the matrix during sintering, leading to grain 
refinement and the associated Hall-Petch strengthening, thereby 
enhancing the alloy ’ s toughness [ 12 , 13 ]. Secondly, the pinning effects 
of uniformly dispersed nanoparticles on dislocation slip and grain 
boundary (GB) migration improve the thermal stability of tungsten al -
loys at high temperatures [ 14 ]. However, there is insufficient research 
and theory regarding the reactions between the second phase and the W 
matrix interfaces under high-temperature stress conditions.

MD with density functional theory (DFT) calculations reveals that the 
FCC phase of W has superior interface bonding energy. By combining the 
observed role of the second phase during high-temperature deformation 
of the matrix, a hypothesis for the second-phase strengthening of the 
ODS-W matrix is proposed, providing new insights into the retention of 
stable UTS in ODS-W at high temperatures.

4. Conclusions 
Here, we employed two different fabrication processes to prepare 
ODS-W samples for tensile testing. This approach helped minimize the 
influence of fabrication techniques on the samples, thereby enhancing 
the reliability of our macroscopic data. Through the two comparative 
sets, it became evident that ODS-W exhibited a more stable ultimate 
tensile strength at high temperatures, providing strong support for our 
theory of high-temperature strengthening. 
In the microscopic experiments, using in situ TEM, we have observed 
for the first time that at high temperatures, the interface between the 
matrix and the oxide has a strong bonding force, which is stronger than 
the interface bonding force of the matrix itself during the tensile process. 
HRTEM analysis during in situ high-temperature stretching shows that 
W transformed from BCC phase to FCC phase has higher activity. It has 
been confirmed, through the integration of Molecular Dynamics (MD) 
and Density Functional Theory (DFT) simulations, that the FCC phase of 
W exhibits stronger interfacial bonding with the secondary phase oxide 
interface. In the stretching process, the crack preferentially selects the 
phase transition area. Thus, W in the FCC phase is separated from the 
matrix and sticks to the rare-earth oxide interface. This process helps 
absorb deformation energy and reduce local stress concentration, which 
prevents crack propagation and causes a ductile fracture. The stable 
ultimate tensile strength (UTS) exhibited by WYO during high- 
temperature tensile testing can be attributed to the strong interfacial 
effects of ODS-W. This observation aligns with our macroscopic data and 
provides a reasonable explanation for this phenomenon.
