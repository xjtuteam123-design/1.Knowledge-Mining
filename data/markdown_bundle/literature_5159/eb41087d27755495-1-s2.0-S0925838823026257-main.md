ABSTRACT 
The microstructures and mechanical properties of 7 at% Al-added VCoNi medium entropy alloy are studied and 
compared with those of the undoped alloy at various temperatures. Doping Al promotes the formation of a 
microstructure composed of the FCC matrix and the BCC-L2
1 
phase, which remains stable until the matrix 
gradually transforms into the HCP- к and tetragonal- σ phases after 600 
◦
C. Compared with the VCoNi alloy, the 
differences involve that the Al addition suppresses the strain-induced FCC-HCP transformation at 25 
◦
C, and 
delays the eutectoid reaction at 700 
◦
C. Corresponding to the microstructure evolution, the doped alloy preserves 
higher strength and strain hardening capacity until 600 
◦
C due to the high-content harder BCC phase but 
comparable values at 700 and 800 
◦
C with the relatively less HCP phase. Besides, the added Al can also reduce 
the susceptibility to the serration flow. The results reveal that the (VCoNi)
93
Al
7 
alloy has high potentials for 
intermediate-temperature applications. 
1. Introduction 
Developing new structural materials with strong microstructural 
stability and thus superior mechanical properties at high temperatures 
has been a consistent pursuit of material researchers [1 – 3] . In this re -
gard, high/medium entropy alloys (H/MEAs) can be highlighted as 
promising candidates due to their unique design strategies and proper -
ties [4,5] . Specifically, the face-centered-cubic (FCC) structured VCoNi 
MEAs exhibit excellent strength-ductility synergy at both ambient and 
cryogenic temperatures in addition to their high corrosion and hydrogen 
embrittlement resistances [6 – 8] . As reported [7] , the mechanical 
properties of the VCoNi MEA are improved with the reduced tempera -
ture and the yield strength and ductility increase from 988 MPa and 
38.4% at 273 K to 1217 MPa and 42.1% at 77 K, respectively. The 
enhanced properties are mainly ascribed into the homogeneous distri -
bution of the deformation structure (Taylor lattice-like structures) 
throughout the grains at 77 K, in contrast to the deformation localization 
near the grain boundaries at 298 K. However, its applications at higher 
temperatures are unfortunately limited by the insufficient strength, with 
a yield strength of ~800 MPa at 25 
◦
C and ~400 MPa at 400 – 700 
◦
C [5, 
9,10] . Thus, other effective methods like the grain/twin boundaries [11, 
12] , precipitation [13] , alloying solutes [11] and high density defects 
[14] are worth examining. Our previous work has found that the addi -
tion of 7 at% Al (termed as (VCoNi)
93
Al
7
, and hereafter the compositions 
are all given in the form of atomic ratio) could effectively improve the 
yield and ultimate tensile strength of this alloy from 787 MPa and 1245 
MPa to 1321 MPa and 1732 MPa, respectively, and the enhanced 
strength was mainly attributed to the refined and heterogenous 
FCC + BCC duplex structure formed after Al alloying [15] . If the micro -
structure is still stable at high temperatures, it is very likely that the 
alloyed MEA can achieve better mechanical performance at elevated 
temperatures. Just as reported in [16,17] , the as-cast AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
HEA with a fine FCC/B2 lamellar microstructure showed an unprece -
dented combination of high tensile strength (538 MPa) and ductility 
(22.9%) at temperatures even up to 700 
◦
C. Meanwhile, it has been 
demonstrated that diverse phase transformations can be stimulated by 
tensile deformation at various temperatures in the VCoNi MEA with the 
strain-induced FCC-HCP phase transformation presented at 25 
◦
C, the 
dynamic precipitation of the σ phase at 400 – 600 
◦
C, and the eutectoid 
decomposition of the matrix into the к and σ phases at 700 – 800 
◦
C [10] . 
These phase transformations greatly affect the properties of the VCoNi 
MEA, however, it remains unknown about the impact of the Al alloying 
on the evolution of both microstructures and properties when the 
deformation temperatures are changed.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Effects of Al on microstructural evolution, deformation mechanism 
and strain hardening capacity 
According to the above results, doping Al to the VCoNi MEA can have 
an important impact on the microstructural evolution, deformation 
mechanisms and strain hardening capacity. Firstly, for the microstruc -
tural evolution behaviors, doping Al promotes the formation of BCC-L2
1 
phase that preserves good thermal stability until 600
◦
C but suppresses 
the strain-induced FCC-HCP phase transformation at 25
◦
C and delays 
the eutectoid phase decomposition of FCC matrix at elevated tempera -
tures. The suppression of the strain-induced FCC-HCP phase is consid -
ered to be related to the stacking fault energy (SFE). Generally, with the 
decreasing SFE, the active plasticity mode will be dislocation gliding and 
shear banding (SFE > 45 mJ/m
2
), twinning-induced-plasticity (TWIP)- 
like pattern (SFE ranging from 20 to 40 mJ/m
2
) and then 
transformation-induced-plasticity (TRIP)-like behavior (SFE < 20 mJ/ 
m
2
) [44] . The formation of SFs is also one of the important deformation 
modes for those materials with the low-to-mediate SFE [45] . The SFE 
can be affected by the temperature and composition. Firstly, the SFE 
generally increases with the temperature [45] , but it exhibits a relative 
constant confirmed via ab initio simulations in the VCoNi MEA [7] . 
Secondly, the alloy composition can also influence the SFE but with 
more complex behavior. In this work, the composition of the FCC matrix 
is V
32
Co
31
Ni
34
Al
3 
determined by the EDS analysis and it displays lower 
V and Co contents and higher Ni and Al contents compared to the matrix 
composition of VCoNi MEA (V
33.4
Co
33.4
Ni
33.2
). It has been reported that 
Co and Ni elements have a prominent role in influencing the SFE and it 
decreases as the Co content increases and Ni content decreases, which 
has been confirmed by the calculated results from the molecular dy -
namic [46] and experiments [41,47,48] . Meanwhile, Al [49] and V [50] 
elements generally tend to reduce the SFE in Ni-based superalloys and 
CoCrNi MEA with the similar constituent elements to the present alloy. 
Thus, with the coupling influences of these elements, the SFE of the FCC 
matrix should be higher than that in VCoNi MEA (~32 – 35 mJ/m
2 
at 
273 K according to the Ab initio calculations in [7,51 – 53] ). As a result, 
the strain-induced FCC-HCP transformation is suppressed in the (VCo -
Ni)
93
Al
7 
MEA. Then, for the delayed eutectoid phase decomposition at 
elevated temperatures, it can be ascribed into the dragging effect of the 
Al solute on the lowered diffusivity of the other elements [17,54,55] . 
Under the further improved temperature of 800
◦
C, the retarding effect 
of Al on the element diffusing is not prominent and the FCC phase 
intensively decomposes to other phases. 
With respect to the deformation mechanism, dislocation gliding is 
the main deformation mode of the BCC-L2
1 
phase in the whole tem -
perature range. Comparatively, the FCC matrix is the one that exhibits 
more complex deformation behaviors. Corresponding to the variation of 
the SFE above, except for the dominant dislocation slip, only few DTs 
and microbands caused by the intensive dislocation reactions are also 
observed at 25
◦
C, which divide the grains into substructures and reduce 
the effective free path for dislocation motion, supporting the superior 
strain hardening capacity [56] . At 400 – 600
◦
C, multiple SFs can be 
activated and act as the primary deformation mechanisms combining 
with the dislocation slip, the complex interactions of which lead to the 
L-C locks and SFs networks [42] to block the transmission of dislocation, 
thereby sustaining the high strain hardening capacity. The trans -
formation of the primary deformation mode from dislocation slip to SF 
with the increased temperature is abnormal but reasonable in the Co-Ni 
based superalloys deformed at intermediated temperatures [57,58] and 
this behavior is related to the segregation of solute atoms on the defects, 
which can alter the local SFE on the nanoscale, as confirmed in Ref. [52] . 
At 700 and 800
◦
C, as the dynamic recrystallization gradually dominates 
the plastic deformation, the strain hardening capacity is significantly 
weakened. Meanwhile, the resultant multi-phase nanograins facilitate 
the grain boundary sliding, supporting the superplastic-like deformation 
behavior at 800
