ABSTRACT 
GH4742 alloy is a kind of precipitation-hardened superalloy, which is widely used in turbine disc, compressor 
disc, bearing ring and other parts due to its excellent high temperature mechanical properties. In this paper, high- 
temperature tensile comparison experiments were carried out at four temperatures, 1050 
◦
C, 1080 
◦
C, 1110 
◦
C 
and 1140 
◦
C, between alloy A containing a fan-shaped structure and alloy B without a fan-shaped structure. The 
results show that the tensile strength and plasticity of alloy A at 1050 
◦
C are superior to those of alloy B. With the 
increase of temperature, the difference of tensile strength and plasticity between alloy A and B decreases 
gradually. By analyzing the fracture, both alloy A and B were found to have a large number of tough dimples, 
which showed ductile fracture characteristics. But alloy B was found to have some icing-like fracture at 1050 
◦
C, 
which showed some brittle fracture characteristics. After 1110 
◦
C, the difference in tensile strength and plasticity 
between alloy A and B almost disappears because γ ′ phase is completely dissolved in the matrix. TEM results 
show that dislocations gather around the large-size γ ′ phase, and for the small-size γ ′ phase, dislocations tend to 
cut into γ ′ phase directly. The results show that the existence of fan-shaped structure is beneficial to the high 
temperature tensile properties of GH4742 alloy, which has a certain reference effect on the production of 
GH4742 alloy in the future. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based superalloys are widely used in aerospace and other 
fields because of their excellent mechanical properties, oxidation resis -
tance and corrosion resistance [ 1 , 2 ]. The microstructure of nickel-based 
deformed superalloy is mainly composed of matrix phase, second phase 
and carbide, in which the matrix phase is γ phase, the second phase is γ ′ 
phase, and the carbide is mainly MC [ 3 ]. γ ′ phase is the most important 
strengthening phase in nickel-based deformed superalloys. It has 
obvious age-hardening ability and belongs to Cu3Al type L1
2 
face-centered cubic ordered structure [ 4 ]. The quantity, size and form of 
γ ′ phase have important effects on the mechanical properties of the alloy 
[ 5 ]. 
Danflou H L et al. [ 6 ] accidently found the formation of acicular γ 
′ phase clusters in a low-mismatch experimental alloy, which is called 
fan-shaped structure because its overall morphology is similar to that of 
fan blades. Since the fan-shaped structure is composed of γ ′ dendrites 
and γ matrix, its formation is mainly affected by temperature and 
mismatch degree. At present, there are four ways to form fan-shaped 
structure in superalloys, such as slow cooling after solution, aging 
after solution, phase-change induced plasticity mechanism and regula -
tion of alloying elements. Based on the research of UDIMET720Li alloy, 
Furrer et al. [ 7 ] found that the morphology of the γ ′ phase changes with 
temperature. When the cooling rate is ≥ 12.7 
◦
C/s, the γ ′ phase is 
spherical, and when the cooling rate is about 1.3 
◦
C/s, the γ ′ phase 
changes into irregular shape, but to form a fan-shaped structure, the 
cooling rate is less than 0.12 
◦
C/s. At present, there are some problems in 
hot working of superalloys, such as narrow deformation temperature 
range, high deformation flow stress and low deformation plasticity 
[ 8 – 10 ], which make the alloy easy to crack during hot working. 
Fan-shaped structure is a form of discontinuous desolventization, which 
is regarded as a harmful phase in many alloys because it usually pro -
duces poor mechanical properties in structural alloys [ 11 – 15 ]. Lu et al. 
[ 9 , 16 ] obtained fan-shaped structures and serrated grain boundaries 
with suitable size and morphology through slow cooling in the γ + γ ′ 
two-phase region of GH4742 alloy. Compared with the original GH4742 
alloy, the GH4742 alloy with precipitated fan-shaped structure effec -
tively reduces the hot deformation flow stress, significantly improves the

accumulate among these long rod-shaped γ ′ phases or cross-slip 
through the γ ′ phases through slip bands. For small-sized γ ′ pha -
ses, due to high Orowan stress, dislocations directly cut into the γ ′ 
phases to form dislocations or wound around the γ ′ phases to form 
Orowan rings.
