Abstract 
 In the present study the microstructure evolutions and compressive strengths of the 
directionally solidified Ti-47Al-2Nb-2Cr-0.2Er alloy undergoing single and cyclic 
loadings are studied. It is found that bending in lamellar structure can be caused by 
compression and the extent of this bend exacerbates with the increase of compression 
strain. For the sample under cyclic -loading, the density of dislocation is improved, 
resulting in a higher compressive strength in comparison with the sample subjected to 
single loading with the same strain. Enhancements of P eierls stress , long -range elastic 
interactions between dislocations, and jog quantity all contribute to the higher strength of 
the cyclic-loading sample. Dislocation loops releasing from the semi -coherent interfaces 
under the applied stress are the main dislocation multiplication mechanism during the 
room temperature compression. 
 
Keywords: TiAl based alloy; cyclic loading; dislocation density; dislocation 
multiplication 
 
 
1. Introduction 
 Titanium aluminide intermetallics composed of γ(TiAl) phase and a small fraction of

## Page 3

α2(Ti3Al) phase have attracted much attention because of high specific strength, low 
density, excellent creep and oxidation resistance [1-4]. They have been regarded as the 
most promising lightweight materials, which show large potential to substitute the 
superalloys partly in automotive and aviation industries[5,6]. In general, the mechanical 
properties depend on the macro/microstructures of the materials, which in turn are 
determined by the chemical composition, interstitials, processing technologies, and so 
on[7,8]. Nowadays, cast TiAl-based alloys show promising application in automobile and 
aircraft industry [9,10]. The composition of intermetalic γ(TiAl)-based alloys of 
engineering interest can be summarized as follows: Ti -(45-48)Al-(1-3)X-(2-5)Y-(＜1)Z, 
where X=Cr, Mn, V; Y=Nb, Ta, W, Mo; Z=Si, B, C[11]. Aluminium content has a 
significant influence on the microstructures and mechanical properties of TiAl -based 
alloys. Previous studies have reported that the optimum volume ratio of γ(TiAl) and 
α2(Ti3Al) are obtained in the TiAl -based alloys containi ng 47 at.% Al, which leads to 
better strength, ductility and fracture toughness[ 12,13]. Also, the research results of Kim 
et al and Koepee et al have shown that about 2 at.% additions of Nb and about 2 at.% of 
some other elements (e.g. Cr , Mn, W ) can boost the tensile ductility at ro om 
temperature[14,15]. Then the compositions of the target TiAl based alloys in the present 
study contain 47 at.% Al, 2 at.% additions of Nb and 2 at.% Cr . Moreover , interstitial 
oxygen in the matrix of TiAl-based alloys is detrimental to the ductility of the TiAl -based 
alloys at room temperature , which restricts the application of structural TiAl alloys[16]. 
The concentration of interstitial oxygen in the TiAl alloys must be kept below 1000 wt. 
ppm for application in aero engine components [17]. In order to minimize the negative 
influence of the interstitial oxygen , 0.2 at.% additions of Er are added into the TiAl based 
alloys which can significantly decrease the oxygen content[18]. So this paper chooses the 
alloy Ti-47Al-2Nb-2Cr-0.2Er as the research subject. 
 The mechanical properties of materials are closely related to the deformation process. 
The plastic deformation of the γ-TiAl is determined by the nature of the lamellar 
microstructure. Lamellar structure is composed of α2 and γ laths where the α2-phase 
crystallizes in the hexagonal DO19 and the γ-phase in the tetragonal L1 0 structure[19]. The 
two phases display different deformation behaviors because of distinct difference existing

## Page 4

in the crystalline structures and lattice parameters [20]. The plastic deformation of the 
(α2+γ) lamellae is a complex process. Detailed analysis of the deformation is necessary, 
which is benefit to further improve the mechanical properties of the TiAl -based alloys. In 
case of the TiAl-based alloys with lamellar structure, the mechanical properties of the two 
phases exhibit considerable anisotropy. The deformation behavior, which can affect the 
strength, ductility, fracture toughness of the two -phase TiAl alloys, is related to the angle 
between the lamellae orientation and loading axis [21]. To facilitate the analysis of 
microstructure evolution with the engineering strain and minimize the influence of the 
lamellae orientation on deformation behavior , all the compression samples in the present 
paper are cut from the ingot prepared by cold crucible directional solidification technology. 
The lamellae orientation variations of all the tested samples are in a narrow range through 
this solidification technology[22,23]. Meanwhile, the ceramic crucibles are not required in 
this solidification technology, which can avoid the reactions between the TiAl alloy melt 
and ceramic crucible, and purify the TiAl alloy[24]. 
 The plastic deformation of titanium aluminum alloys usually takes place in the 
γ(TiAl)-phase and dislocations movements are the primary deformation mode[ 25-28]. In 
the present paper, the compressive strengths of directionally solidified 
Ti-47Al-2Nb-2Cr-0.2Er after single and cyclic loadings are compared. The microstructure 
evolutions of directionally solidified Ti-47Al-2Nb-2Cr-0.2Er during single - and 
cyclic-loading process es are investigated and e ffects of dislocation s on the compressive 
strengths are discussed. The strengthening mechanism of the cyclic -loading sample is the 
high density of dislocations. Increase of dislocation density can be considered as a 
potentially promising method to improve the strength of TiAl based alloys. 
2. Experimental details 
 The experimental ingot of Ti -47Al-2Nb-2Cr-0.2Er was prepared by cold crucible 
directional solidification (CCDS) at the growth rate of 1.0 mm/min and the power of 50 
kW. The details of the solidification process can be found in reference[29]. After 
solidification, the ingot was cut in two along the growth direction. One half was ground, 
polished, and etched in the modified Kroll’s solution (2vol.% HNO3 + 5vol.% HF + 93vol.%

## Page 5

H2O). The macrostructure of the CCDS ingot was recorded by Nikon D7100 Digital 
Single Lens Reflex and observation s of the lamellae orientation were carried out by 
optical microscope (OM). 
 The room temperature compression tests were carried out on the 5569 Instron test 
machine and the loading speed was 1×10-3 s-1. The compression samples were cut in the 
box zone (as shown in Fig. 1(a)) and the size of the compression sample is Ф6 mm×9 mm. 
The axial direction of the sample is along the growth direction. In order to avoid the 
machining effect on the measured data, t he two ends of the compression samples were 
manually ground before testing. For scanning electron microscopy (SEM) observations, 
the specimens were cut along the loading direction and ground, polished. The detailed 
microstructures of the samples were observed by transmission electron microscope (TEM). 
The TEM foils were cut into 300um by wire -electrode machine, followed by manual 
grinding to 70 um. Finally the foils were subjected to twin jet electron polishing in a 
solution of 60 vol.% methanol, 35 vol.% butyl alcohol and 5 vol.% perchloric acid at 15 V 
and -30℃. The dislocation densities of undeformed and deformed samples were estimated 
through X-ray diffraction (XRD) peak profile analysis according to the Williamson -Hall 
model. The scanning degree was in the range of 20°~90° and the scanning rate is 0.05°/s.

4 Discussions 
 In order to withstand the extremely high demanding of the next generation automobile 
and aircraft engines, t he performances of the TiAl based alloys are needed to be further 
improved[9]. Additions of alloys elements and subsequent thermo -mechanical process are 
the two main methods to improve the mechanical properties of TiAl alloys [42-44]. 
However, more alloying additions added to TiAl based alloys can raise the melting point 
and enhance the high-temperature activity of TiAl melt, which makes the casting of TiAl

## Page 11

alloys more difficult [45]. And thermo-mechanical processing, including isothermal 
forging, hot extrusion and rolling, can cause significant creep properties and 
high-temperature stability deterioration although room -temperature of the TiAl based 
alloys are improved. Nevertheless, Creep resistance and structural stability are important 
prerequisites for high temperature applications of TiAl based alloys [46]. In addition, low 
pressure turbine (LPT) blade is one of the important application of TiAl alloys and the DS 
TiAl based alloys with well -aligned crystal orientation can effectively improve the 
performance and service temperature of gas engines [47]. After th ermo-mechanical 
processing, well-aligned crystal orientation is destroyed, thus losing the advantages of DS. 
In the preceding paragraphs, results of CCDS TiAl based alloys undergoing single and 
cyclic loadings indicate that the TiAl alloys can be strengthe ned by enhancing the 
dislocation density of material matrix. It indicates that enhancing the dislocation density of 
TiAl based alloys can be considered as a potential way to improve the mechanical 
properties of as-cast TiAl alloys. Compared with the former two methods (i.e. additions of 
alloying elements and thermo-mechanical processing), introducing large amount of 
dislocations into the TiAl alloys through applications of some technologies during DS 
process can not only strengthen the material but also preserve the crystal orientation of DS 
TiAl alloys. In this section, dislocation strengthening mechanism in the cyclic-loading 
sample will be discussed in details.

considered to be the main strengthening mechanism for the cyclic-loading sample. Based

4.1.3 Formation of the dislocation jogs 
 For a certain plane, there are some dislocations slipping on the plane (termed slipping 
dislocations) and some other dislocations going through the plane (termed forest 
dislocations). The slipping dislocation s usually encounter the forest dislocations during 
deformation process and the dislocation jog is formed if the applied stress is large enough. 
The formation of the jog is illustrated in Fig. 8. Fig. 8(a) shows that the slipping 
dislocations and forest dislocations glide on the orthogonal glide planes, and the Burgers 
vectors and movement directions of the two dislocations are given in Fig. 8(b) . The 
dislocations AB could intersect and pass through the dislocation XY during the 
dislocations movements, then a broken-line PP’ exists on the originally straight 
dislocation line AB (as shown in Fig. 8(c)). The PP’ with the Burgers vectors of b1 would 
slip along the direction of V2 on the plane PXY. As the plane PXY is normal to the glide 
plane of dislocation AB (i.e. PAB), the PP’ is the jog. The mobility of the jog is poor 
because it is normal to the glide plane, so the jogs often act as the barrier to the dislocation 
motion. The resistance to dislocation movement induced by the jogs usually shows the 
characteristics of short -range force . If the jogs are formed by the intersections of edge 
dislocations, the dislocation line length increases and the jogs are difficult to move 
following the dislocation slipping in cry stals. Then the dislocation movement is retarded 
by the dislocation jog. As the jogs are formed by the screw dislocations slips, the jogs are 
immobile in the direction of the motion of the screw dislocations. The jogs can se rve as 
short-range barrier to the other dislocations movement s. Higher stress is needed to 
overcome the jogs. Therefore, it is concluded that the formation of jogs during 
deformation process can impede the dislocation s movements and the deformed sample 
shows higher stress with the increase of dislocation jogs.

engineering strain. Prior to the 2 nd or 3rd loading, the sample S4# has been compressively

of the sample S4# is different from that of the as -CCDS sample and there are a certain 
amount of dislocation jogs in the d eformed microstructure. The prior dislocation jogs can 
act as barriers and hinder the dislocation s movements when the sample S4# is deformed 
under the 2nd and the 3rd loadings. As a result, the flow stress of the sample 4# at strain of 
10% and 15% is higher than those of samples S2# and S3#. 
4.2 Dislocation multiplications 
As described above, the higher flow stress of the sample S4# has been attributed to 
the dislocation strengthening, involving the increments of Peierls stress, long-range elastic 
interactions between dislocations , and dislocation jogs . Dislocation strengthening is 
closely related to the increase of dislocation density. In this section, the dislocation 
multiplication mechanism of the lamellar TiAl alloy during compression will be discussed. 
 Due to the features of development of (α2+γ) lamellae, the semi-coherent interfaces of 
γ/α2 as well as γ/γ (except the true twin relation of adjacent γ lamellae) are involved in the 
lamellar matrix and misfit strain exist in the vicinity of the interface[53-55]. In spite of 
accommodation of interfacial dislocations, the residual lattice misfit can be 
homogeneously accommodated by elastic straining . So there is long-range coherency 
stress at the interfaces. The presence of the coherency stress can be manifested by the 
dislocation loops which emerge from the interface [53,56]. Fig. 10 (a) shows the 
morphologies of dislocations adjacent to the lamellar interface in the undeformed sample 
and it can be seen that a few dislocation loops exist around the interface. Referring to the 
reference[56], the dislocation loops are mostly situated on the {111} γ and the Burgers 
vectors of the dislocation loops is

5. Conclusions 
 The microstructure evolutions and compressive properties of the CCDS 
Ti-47Al-2Nb-2Cr-0.2Er alloy under conditions of single and cyclic loadings are studied. 
The dislocation strengthening and multiplication mechanisms are discussed. The 
conclusions can be summarized as: 
(1) The microstructure characterization reveals that the lamellae bent under both single- 
and cyclic- loading conditions, without cracks or fractures. 
(2) With the same strain, t he sample deformed by applying cyclic loading shows larger 
flow stress compared with the sample undergoing single loading indicating the 
cyclic-loading sample is strengthened. 
(3) Enhancements of Peierls stress, long-range elastic interactions between dislocation and 
jog quantity are caused by applying cyclic loading. Increase of resistance to dislocation 
motion contributes to the larger compressive strength of cyclic-loading sample. 
(4) Dislocation loops induced by interfacial coherency stress can be found at the 
semi-coherent interfaces of the un deformed TiAl alloy. Dislocation loops emissions from 
the semi-coherent interface are proposed to support the dislocation multiplication of the 
γ-TiAl alloy during plastic deformation at room temperature.
