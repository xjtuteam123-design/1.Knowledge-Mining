abstract
We propose a novel strengthening strategy that involves the introduction of a high-entropy nanoscale
phase (HENP) and semicoherent refractory nanoparticle into the A2 matrix of an as-cast equimolar
HfMoNbTaTi refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA). Based on the design concept of low difference in ele-
mental melting points, equiaxed grains with slight segregation were obtained. The HENP was character-
ized as a disordered near-coherent structure with a composition signiﬁcantly similar to the matrix that
differed from traditional coherent intermetallics. The heterogeneous nucleation was attributed to the
aggregation of the largest Hf atoms and interstitial complexes in the locally distorted matrix.
Moreover, the refractory nanoparticle was inferred to be hafnium nitride, possessing high thermal stabil-
ity and establishing a semicoherent interface with the matrix. Notably, the interface was lined with a Ti
ﬁlm of one or two atomic layers. Furthermore, owing to the high melting point and precipitation
strengthening, a highest yield strength (851 MPa) was obtained at 1200 /C176C compared with those reported
cast RHEAs. At room temperature, a high yield strength (1713 MPa) was revealed mainly due to the com-
bined effect of solid-solution strengthening and precipitation strengthening. The present study provides a
new pathway for the future design of HEAs.

1. Introduction
Demands for the improved efﬁciency of energy saving and
emission reduction have driven the research on advanced materi-
als, especially those used in the manufacture of gas-turbine engi-
nes, with speciﬁc properties such as high temperature and
corrosion resistance [1–4]. Nickel-based superalloys are predomi-
nantly used for hot sections and can be employed at high temper-
atures of up to /C24 1000 /C176C. However, the low melting points (MPs)
of pure nickel (1455 /C176C) and nickel-based superalloys (1250 /C176C–1
300 /C176C) limit their application at higher operating temperatures
that are necessary for more efﬁcient propulsion performance [5].
Recently, signiﬁcant attention has been attracted by a novel design
strategy that involves mixing multi-components, based on the con-
cept of the high conﬁgurational entropy favouring the formation of
a single solid-solution phase; the resulting alloys are called high-
entropy alloys (HEAs). HEAs, initially composed of the late transi-
tion elements, exhibit excellent mechanical properties and corro-
sion resistance and show prospects in structural and functional
applications [4,6–8]. Furthermore, in order to explore the alloys
with higher MPs and high temperature performance, refractory
components such as W, Ta, Mo, Nb, and Hf have been introduced
into HEAs; these alloys are referred to as refractory high-entropy
alloys (RHEAs) [9,10]. Studies have revealed that RHEAs, mostly
possessing a body-centered cubic (BCC) matrix, showed signiﬁ-
cantly high strength at both room temperature (RT) and high tem-
perature, primarily due to solid-solution strengthening (SSS) and
their high MPs [11–16]. MoNbTaVW was the ﬁrst developed RHEA
and showed an excellent yield strength of 735 MPa at 1200 /C176C; the
high strength could be retained up to 1600 /C176C [9,11]. However, it
exhibited a high density (12.36 g/cm 3), poor ductility at RT, and
intractable segregation of inner grains. In contrast, another BCC
RHEA system, HfNbTaTiZr, demonstrated excellent ductility (no
fracture beyond 50% compression) at RT; however, unsatisfactory
yield strength was observed at elevated temperatures (92 MPa at
1200 /C176C) [10,17]. Moreover, phase decomposition was demon-
strated across an extensive range of temperatures [18,19].
Recently, strengthening efforts on RHEAs have primarily been
focused on SSS [20–23], grain-boundary strengthening (GBS)
[24,25], dislocation strengthening [26,27], and precipitation
strengthening (PS) [19,28–33]. In particular, analogous to the clas-
sical c-c
0
PS template in superalloys (coherent ordered L12 phase in
disordered A1 matrix), the combination of the coherent ordered B2
compounds, with disordered A2 solid-solution phase, was explored
by the addition of Al [28,29,33–37]. Therefore, a high RT strength
with a decreased density was achieved, and the oxidation resis-
tance can be improved [38,39]. After sacriﬁcing the high MPs, Al-
containing RHEAs were facing the breakthrough of a combination
of ductility and high temperature strength owing to the fragile fea-
ture of B2 compounds and their instability at high temperatures
[33]. In recent reports, disordered nanoscale phases with refractory
constituents in the BCC matrix were observed [18,40], and an
appreciable PS effect could be attained with a homogeneous distri-
bution [19]. In addition, studies on the peculiarities of lattice dis-
tortion and local constituents ﬂuctuation in HEAs assured the
probability of nucleation of the precipitates via atom aggregation
in the locally distorted matrix that differed from the typical inter-
metallic phases ( L1
2 and B2) [22,41–43]. Therefore, based on the
refractory components, the introduction of ﬁne precipitates or par-
ticles without sacriﬁcing the high MP and phase stability is a chal-
lenging alternative pathway in RHEA ﬁeld.
On the contrary, unlike the dilute solutes noted in conventional
alloys, segregation occurred in approximately all the constituents
of multi-principal-element alloys owing to signiﬁcant inter-
component differences in the MPs. In particular, the as-cast RHEAs
generally showed a BCC matrix containing high-MP elements in
the dendritic regions (DR) and low-MP elements in the interden-
dritic regions (IDR) [12,13,23,44–46]. Consequently, the ultimate
non-uniform elemental distribution determined the unequal com-
positions and lattice constants that subsequently led to different
SSS contributions and affected the ductility. Moreover, it is not
conducive to homogeneous precipitation [47,48]. Therefore, the
essential procedures for the elimination of segregation are cumber-
some and obstruct the development of HEAs [4,9,49]. After review-
ing the reports on the microstructure and segregation in the as-
cast RHEAs, we propose a parameter dTm called the elemental
melting point difference (EMPD) as an additional criterion for the
design of HEAs with respect to the segregation tendency.
Following the principle of low dTm, the equimolar HfMoNbTaTi
alloy was designed aiming at developing an RHEA with high MP,
low segregation tendency, good thermo-mechanical property, high
phase stability, and relatively low density. In the as-cast condition,
dendritic structure was signiﬁcantly relieved. Besides, equiaxed
grains with a homogeneous distribution of near-coherent disor-
dered high-entropy nanoscale phase (HENP) were obtained in the
BCC matrix. A semicoherent refractory nanoparticle was also
observed. The present study provides a pathway to develop new
HEAs in future.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Ingot preparation
An equimolar HfMoNbTaTi RHEA was prepared by arc melting
in a water-cooled Cu mould. Pure Ar shielding gas was ﬁlled into
the chamber after a vacuum pressure of up to 3.5 /C2 10
-3 Pa was
achieved. Pure metals were mixed in two groups with the same
equimolar composition for fabrication (90 g each in weight). The
Hf, Nb, and Ta particles with diameters of 2–5 mm were used.
Mo and Ti were used in the form of 1 mm thick slices. Before melt-
ing the mixtures, pure Ti was melted for 2 min to absorb the
remaining oxygen in the chamber. Subsequently, two primary
button-shaped ingots with the same condition were obtained
and melted four times each. The two ingots were then cut into
small blocks using an abrasive wheel cutting machine. Thereafter,
all the blocks were mixed and put into the furnace for repeated
four times melting. Afterwards, the resulting rectangular ingot
underwent another cutting, mixing, and four times melting.
Finally, a rectangular ingot with a lustrous surface was obtained,
approximately, 40 mm in length, 30 mm in width, and 13 mm in
thickness (180 g in weight). In total, the ﬁnal ingot experienced
12 times melting procedure for homogeneity.
2.2. Microstructure and mechanical properties
The microstructure was investigated via X-ray diffraction
(XRD), ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM)
equipped with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), ﬁeld
emission electron probe microanalysis (FE-EPMA), electron
backscatter diffraction (EBSD), atomic force microscopy (AFM),
transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and high angle annular
dark ﬁeld-scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-
STEM). The specimens for TEM and STEM analyses were prepared
using a focused ion beam (FIB)/SEM dual-beam system (Helios
NanoLab
TM 600i, FEI). The Vickers hardness (HV) was measured
at RT in the as-cast condition. Compression tests were conducted
at RT, 800 /C176C, 1000 /C176C, and 1200 /C176C, using a hot deformation simu-
lator (Thermecmastor-Z, Fuji Electronic Industrial Co. Ltd.). Prepa-
ration and characterization procedures for the observations of
microstructure and mechanical properties are detailed in the Sup-

3. Alloy design and theoretical calculations
3.1. Alloy design
Currently, most refractory metals are used for RHEA design,
such as W, Ta, Mo, Nb, Hf, V, Zr, and Ti. To develop RHEAs with high
MPs, low segregation tendency, desirable thermo-mechanical
properties, high phase stabilities, and relatively low densities, the
characteristics of each component, such as MP, density, atomic
radius, shear modulus, phase transformation, and enthalpy of mix-
ing with other constituents, need to be considered. W, with the
highest MP (3422 /C176C) provides high strength and heat resistance
to the alloy while leading to embrittlement and higher probabili-
ties of segregation [13,45,46]. Furthermore, W increases the den-
sity of an RHEA and is difﬁcult to melt completely [45]. Ta, Mo,
Nb, and Hf contribute positively toward SSS and heat resistance
[12]. V demonstrates the ability of BCC phase stabilization and
SSS, and it tends to form the Laves phase (V
2M, M = Hf, Nb, Ti,
and Zr) during annealing at high temperatures [50,51]. Ti exhibits
a lower MP than that of Zr, however, it is more effective in light-
weighting. In addition, Co and Cr are also typically selected for
SSS and Laves phase formation (Cr 2Nb and Co 2Ti) [52,53]. Low-
density elements such as Al and Si might strengthen the alloy
because of the formation of intensive bonds with transition metals
(TMs) [34,54]. Moreover, Al and Cr can effectively be used for oxi-
dation resistance improvement [39,55]. Recently, O and N were
successfully introduced for interstitial solid solution or precipita-
tion to reinforce the matrix [22]. Notably, a large fraction of the
hexagonal close-packed (HCP) elements (Hf, Ti, and Zr at RT) in
RHEAs could reduce the BCC phase stability [12,18,19].
In this study, Hf, Mo, Nb, and Ta were introduced for their SSS
abilities, high MPs, and heat resistances. Ti was selected primarily
to reduce the density. An equimolar composition was designed for
the SSS and phase stability. Furthermore, slight segregation was
expected in the as-cast microstructure because of their close
MPs. Compared with the known HfNbTaTiZr RHEA, the substitu-
tion of Zr by Mo mainly aimed at improving the mechanical prop-
erties and phase stability at high temperatures. Theoretical
calculations for this HfMoNbTaTi RHEA are detailed in the Supple-
mentary material , and the basic elemental data are listed in
Table 1 .

4.1.2. Segregation in the present alloy
In the as-cast HfMoNbTaTi alloy, dendritic structure was
relieved, and equiaxed grains formed with slight segregation in
the grains, as indicated by elemental distribution maps in Fig. 1
(b) and point concentrations in Table 3 . Mo and Ta distributed in
the grain centre, while Hf and Ti were inclined to accumulate close
to the grain boundaries (GBs). Nb, with the smallest absolute value
of DTm
i , was detected to be homogeneous in the grains. Compared
with the typical RHEAs ( Fig. 1(a)), dendritic segregation was signif-
icantly relieved in the present alloy. Besides, the lowest ASI indi-
cated a low segregation tendency. The homogeneous
microstructure gave rise to the formation of precipitates and pro-
moted mechanical property.
It is noted that the microstructure with a uniform elemental
distribution is not only determined via EMPD but also dependent
on the material variables (atomic diffusion rate or other physical
properties) and process factors (sample size, mould shape, melting
method, cooling rate, etc.). As the data were obtained from the dif-
ferent alloy systems reported by researchers that employed vari-
ous equipment and processes, the parameter EMPD ( dTm)i sa n
applicable material variable associated with segregation tendency
in material design.

An equiaxed grain microstructure was obtained in the as-cast
HfMoNbTaTi ingot, and the microstructure was composed of a

and eligible nucleation sites were prepared for nucleation, and sub-

Hf-rich HENP. The high cooling rate, as well as the lattice distortion
effect of HEAs, could impede the diffusion of atoms and hinder the
growth in particle size further.

5. Conclusions
We report a disordered HENP and a semicoherent refractory
nanoparticle strengthening in the as-cast HfMoNbTaTi RHEA that
possessed a low segregation tendency and showed excellent
mechanical properties. The proposed design strategies are valuable
for the future development of HEAs. The following conclusions
were drawn:
(1) EMPD ( dTm), an applicable variable associated with alloy
segregation tendency, was proposed, which promised the
potential for future HEAs design.
(2) The nanoprecipitate in the homogeneous distribution was
characterized as a disordered harder Hf-rich HENP, featuring
an almost similar composition and a near-coherent structure
with the A2 matrix. The heterogeneous nucleation mecha-
