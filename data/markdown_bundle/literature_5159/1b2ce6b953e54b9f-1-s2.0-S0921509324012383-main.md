1. Introduction
With the aging of the population and the expectation of high-quality 
life, the demand for biomedical orthopedic implant materials is growing 
rapidly [ 1 ]. At present, SUS316L stainless steel, commercial pure tita -
nium (CP-Ti), and the Ti-6Al-4V alloy have been widely applied as 
implant materials in biomedical fields [ 2 ]. However, the above materials 
still have some disadvantages. For example, when SUS316L is used in 
vivo, it will be gradually corroded due to wear and corrosion, thus 
releasing nickel ions that can induce toxic effects and cause adverse 
reactions such as cell damage and inflammation [ 3 ]. CP-Ti has the dis -
advantages of low strength, poor wear resistance, and easy fracture after 
implantation, which limits its application in components with a demand 
for large bearing capacity. Ti-6Al-4V alloy has higher strength than 
CP-Ti, and has good processing performance, which is widely used in the 
field of biomedicine. However, the Al and V elements contained in 
Ti-6Al-4V alloy materials are believed to be harmful to organisms [ 4 – 6 ]. 
Therefore, the development of novel biomedical metal materials with 
good biocompatibility, excellent mechanical properties, high wear 
resistance and low modulus is of great significance for the medical field 
[ 7 ].
The multi-principal element alloy (MPEAs) is composed of a variety 
of elements with equiatomic or non-equiatomic ratios, exhibiting 
greater composition flexibility and much better mechanical properties 
compared with traditional alloys [ 8 , 9 ]. The excellent mechanical 
properties can be attributed to the high configuration entropy effect, 
lattice distortion effect, and slow diffusion effect in HEAs [ 10 , 11 ]. It has 
been reported that Ti-based refractory HEAs have excellent physical, 
chemical, and biological properties, which are promising candidates for 
biomedical implant materials. In particular, the TiZr-based refractory 
HEAs with simple body-centered cubic (BCC) phase are capable of 
reducing the risk of “ wear chip disease ” after implant surgery, due to 
their extremely high hardness and excellent wear resistance [ 12 – 14 ].
However, most of the BCC HEAs including the TiZr-based refractory

HEAs have low or no tensile ductility and relatively high Young ’ s 
modulus [ 15 – 17 ]. Some attempts have been made to develop BCC HEAs 
with good biomechanical compatibility. For example, twinning-induced 
plasticity and phase transformation-induced plasticity have been 
demonstrated to be effective in improving the ductility of the BCC HEAs 
by adjusting their phase stability and improving their dislocation storage 
ability and strain hardening stability, while usually resulting in sub -
stantially decreased strength and wear resistance [ 18 , 19 ]. Similarly, 
studies have also shown that by controlling the composition of the alloy 
and reducing the valence electron concentration (VEC) to a certain 
extent ( ≤ 4.4), the intrinsic ductility can be improved at the expense of 
lowered strength [ 20 , 21 ]. So, to date, how to simultaneously improve 
the strength and ductility of BCC HEAs is still an open issue.
In this study, to meet the properties requirement of HEAs for surgical 
implant applications, the TiZrTaNbMo HEAs with non-equiatomic ratio 
were prepared by considering the combined effects of these elements in 
β stability, VEC, and mixing entropy. The resultant microstructures, 
mechanical properties, wear, and corrosion resistance with adjusting 
their constituent were figured out by employing a variety of properties 
and microstructures test approaches. The fundamental mechanisms for 
the improved multiple properties were also deeply discussed. The 
findings can guide the composition design of HEAs for better properties 
and promote the surgical implant application of BCC HEAs.

3.3. Mechanical properties of the TiZrTaNbMo HEAs
The mechanical properties of the TiZrTaNbMo HEAs measured by 
uniaxial tensile test, nanoindentation, and uniaxial compression test are 
shown in Fig. 5 . It can be seen from Fig. 5 a that the yield strength (YS) 
and total tensile elongation of the Ti42.5, Ti45 and Ti47 are 866 ± 28 
MPa and 16.5 ± 1.1 %, 929 ± 13 MPa and 17.8 ± 1.2 %, 899 ± 6 MPa 
and 5.5 ± 0.1 %, respectively. Meanwhile, the higher elongation of Ti45 
can be attributed to the more stable plastic ability, as present in Fig. 5 b. 
Furthermore, as presented in Fig. 5 c, the compressive YS of the Ti42.5, 
Ti45, and Ti47 is ~825 MPa, ~899 MPa, and ~834 MPa, respectively, 
and the three HEAs all have excellent room temperature compression 
ductility. Fig. 5 d shows the nanoindentation load-displacement curves 
of Ti42.5, Ti45, and Ti47, demonstrating that the variation trend of 
Young ’ s modulus of the HEAs is the same as their measured tensile and 
compression YS. The detailed tensile properties, microhardness, and 
Young ’ s modulus data of the three HEAs are also listed in Table 2 . In the 
application of orthopedic implants, Young ’ s modulus is a key biome -
chanical index related to the stress-shielding effect [ 32 ]. As can be seen 
from Table 2 , the Young ’ s modulus of the Ti45 is about 99.9 GPa. 
Nevertheless, it is worth noting that Young ’ s modulus of the TiZr -
TaNbMo HEAs (82 – 99 GPa) is significantly lower than that of 
commercially used biomedical Ti-based materials, such as CP-Ti (104 
GPa) and Ti-6Al-4V (120 GPa) [ 33 , 34 ]. The much lower Young ’ s 
modulus indicates that the TiZrTaNbMo HEAs are more compatible with 
human bone, showing a promising application perspective for ortho -
pedic implants.

play a major role. Studies have shown that elements with negative 
mixing enthalpy form chemical affinity bonds between alloy atoms, 
which not only helps to achieve the strengthening effects but also further 
stimulates dislocation activity inside the grains and promotes disloca -

concentration of the alloy and the grain size can also increase the strain

gation of the alloy. Because the Ti47 has ω brittle second phase, less 
negative mixing enthalpy, and larger grain size, it is difficult to realize 
relatively high ductility. The Ti42.5 and the Ti45 have low valence 
electron concentration and more negative mixing enthalpy, and severe 
lattice distortion leads to a stronger slow diffusion effect, which makes 
them have excellent tensile ductility even in an as-cast state. Finally, the 
tensile properties of the Ti45 alloy are compared with those of other 
alloys containing similar elements (refractory elements) in the litera -

4. Conclusion
In this study, based on the valence electron concentration theory and 
the mixing enthalpy, three kinds of biomedical TiZrTaNbMo HEAs with 
low Young ’ s modulus, high yield strength, and good ductility, wear 
resistance, and corrosion resistance were designed and synthesized by 
adjusting the elemental composition. The conclusion can be drawn as 
follows.
(1) The Ti42.5 and the Ti45 are single BCC structures, and the Ti47 
contains the HCP ω phase. The three alloys all have coarse grain 
size, random grain orientation, homogeneous constituent, and 
severe lattice distortion.
(2) Compared with the other two alloys, the Ti45 has the highest 
tensile yield strength (~931 MPa) and elongation (~17.8 %), and 
the tensile yield strength of the other two alloys is greater than 
860 MPa. Compared with Ti6Al4V, the Ti42.5 and the Ti45 have 
better wear resistance and corrosion resistance.
(4) The high YS strength mainly comes from the solid solution 
strengthening effect, the high ductility can be explained by the 
negative mixing enthalpy induced by the Mo element. Such a 
strategy provides a way for synthesizing high-entropy alloys with 
high strength and ductility.
(4) Better wear resistance of the HEAs than that of Ti6Al4V can be 
realized with specific constituents. Electrochemical tests show 
that the HEAs have lower corrosion tendency and higher corro -
sion resistance than Ti6Al4V. The good corrosion resistance is
