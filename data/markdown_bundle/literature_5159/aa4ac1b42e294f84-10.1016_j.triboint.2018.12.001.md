Abstract: 
Vacuum along with high temperature is typical servi ce condition for machinery 
components operated in aerospace. In this paper, the effects of Al and Mo elements on 
the mechanical and vacuum tribological properties o f the nickel-alloy matrix 
solid-lubricating composites were investigated. Res ults presented that the increased 
addition of Al element noticeably improved the wear resistance and mechanical 
properties of the composites due to the formation o f intermetallic in composites. 
While increasing content of Mo element means higher Vickers hardness and lower 
wear rate at low and moderate temperatures. The wea r mechanism was proposed 
based on microstructure and compositions of sliding surface. 
Key words: Metal matrix composite; Vacuum; High strength; High temperature 
1. Introduction 
High strength solid lubricant composites with super ior vacuum/air tribological 
performance in broad temperature were urgently requ ired in aerospace industries [1, 
2]. The most potential applications include high sp eed air foil bearing, low-heat 
rejection diesel engines, stirling engine cylinders and so on [2-4]. During the past

decades, many effects have been paid to develop the solid lubricant composites, 
including metal matrix, intermetallic matrix and ce ramic matrix, to name a few [5-9]. 
Up to now, introducing solid lubricants, like soft metal (Ag, Au), alkaline halides 
(CaF 2, BaF 2), transition-metal dichalcogenides (MoS 2, WS 2) into the matrix is the 
most effective way to improve lubricity at broad temperatures [10, 11] for composites. 
As reported, the lubricating principles of soft met al was due to the low-shear stress 
with very limited sensitivity of mechanical propert ies to ambient vapor and 
temperatures. For the fluoride, it suffer a brittle -to-ductile transition process at high 
temperatures (500-900 °C), which could form low-she ar-strength surfaces, or glazed 
layer on the worn surface, and provide low friction and wear. Yang et al. presented a 
serious of nickel-alloy matrix composites with Ag a nd BaF 2/CaF 2 as solid lubricants 
that exhibited low friction coefficient (0.1-0.3) f rom RT to 800 °C under both air and 
vacuum conditions [7, 12, 13]. Shi et al. revealed that NiAl-Ti 3SiC 2-Mo/WS 2 
composites also exhibited good solid lubricating pr operties from RT to 800 °C due to 
the synergetic lubricating effect of Ti 3SiC 2 and Mo/WS 2 [14]. Jia et al. depicted that 
both Ag and the formed molybdate on the NiAl-Cr 2O3-Mo-Ag coating worn surface 
during the sliding process resulted in the low wear and friction [15]. 
In addition to tribological properties, it is impor tant to recognize the mechanical 
factors, such as density, microhardness and compres sive strength [7, 16, 17]. As a 
strengthening element, intermetallic has gained con siderable interest in a wide 
application for its high strength and stiffness at high temperatures, low expansion 
coefficient and excellent thermal conductivity, suc h as traditional industries, 
aerospace part manufacturing and cutting-edge elect ronic devices [8, 18, 19]. 
Moreover, the solution strengthening Al is often ad ded into the metal matrix for it is 
easy to in situ form the high strength intermetallic, like TiAl, NiAl, Ni 3Al, etc., during 
the powder metallurgy process [7, 20, 21]. Yang et al [22]. reported that high relative 
density not only improve the wear resistance, but a lso contribute to the high strength 
for the composite. Based on the above discussion, a dding high-strength intermetallic 
into composites may be an effective way to improve the mechanical and 
wear-resistance properties for composites.

2. Experimental details 
Generally, as precipitation strengthening element, Al and Ti are often added in 
nickel-based superalloy to precipitate 
γ' phase. And the content of Al element is 
usually higher than Ti element for the high oxidati on resistance (as it can form stable 
Al 2O3), moreover, Mo element is used primarily to provid e solid-solution 
strengthening at high temperature. In present resea rch, four compositions of 
nickel-alloy matrix composites were fabricated, as shown in Tab.1. The chemical 
compositions (in wt. %) of the nickel-alloy were 15%Cr, 3%Ti, 6%Al, x%Mo (x=0, 5, 
8, 12), and bal. Ni. The weight ratios of solid-lub ricants Ag and fluoride eutectic 
(BaF 2/CaF 2) were 12.5% and, 5%, respectively. The nickel-allo y matrix 
solid-lubricating composites were fabricated by powder metallurgy technique, and the 
details were depicted in other references [26]. 
Density of the solid-lubricant composites was measu red by means of buoyancy 
method and the data were repeated at least for five times. HTV-PHS30 (Archimedes 
Industrial Technology Co., Ltd. England) high tempe rature Vickers hardness tester 
was employed to measure the Vickers microhardness o f the composite in broad 
temperatures, the testing condition was 1 kg applied load and 10 s endurance time and 
the data were the average of ten results. The bendi ng and compressive tests of the 
nickel alloy matrix composites were carried out on a DY-35 universal material testing 
machine with a cross-head speed of 1.7×10 -3 mm/s. The dimension of the bending and

4. Conclusions 
In this work, the influence of Al and Mo elements on mechanical and tribological 
properties of nickel-alloy matrix composites under vacuum from RT to 800 ºC were 
systematically investigated, the main obtained conclusions were as follows. 
(1) The increasing addition of Al elements could dramat ically enhance the 
tribological and mechanical properties of the nickel-alloy matrix composite, while 
the addition of Mo elements shows small effect compared with Al element. 
(2) From RT to 600 ºC, the COF of the nickel-alloy matr ix composites is 0.13-0.26 
and the wear rate is in the range of (0.35-18.7) ×1 0 -6 mm 3N-1m-1. At 800 ºC, great 
decrease of strength results in the relatively high COF and wear rate. 
(3) From RT to 600 ºC, COF of the composites in the mas s descends with the
