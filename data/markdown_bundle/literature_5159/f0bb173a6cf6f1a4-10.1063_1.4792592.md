Sn and Nb modiﬁed ultraﬁne eutectic Ti /C0 Fe alloys with high strength and plasticity prepared by
cold crucible levitation melting were tested in compression at room temperature. (Ti 70.5Fe29.5)93.15
Sn3.85Nb3 alloy exhibited an ultimate compressive strength of 2.36 GPa at 15% plastic strain.
Electron microscopy revealed that lamellar structures in Ti 70.5Fe29.5 alloy could be tailored by the
addition of Sn and Nb to obtain a globular structure. The microstructural reﬁnement, morphology of
phase constituents, and their relationships to the mechanical properties are discussed.

Ultraﬁne grained (ufg) and nanocrystalline (nc) materials
offer record-high strength compared to their coarse-grained
counterparts. However, their applications as structural materi-
als have been hindered by the generally inadequate ductil-
ity.
1–3 Therefore, the improvement of the room temperature
plasticity of ufg and nc materials has been a key topic in the
development of advanced structural materials. Signiﬁcant
work has been done with the intent of tailoring the nanostruc-
tures to achieve coexisting high strength and high ductility at
room temperature. For example, by mixing up the length
scales, specially by creating a bimodal grain size distribution
with micrometre-sized grains embedded inside a matrix of
nanocrystalline and ultraﬁne grains ( <300 nm), a fairly large
uniform elongation of /C24 65% and simultaneously good yield
strength of /C24 400 MPa was achieved in rolled Cu.
4 There are
other approaches to display high strength and ductility includ-
ing materials with dispersions of nanoparticles, nanoprecipi-
tation, and nano-twins, using phase transformation and
twinning-induced plasticity.
2,3,5–7 He et al. 8 reported an in-
situ formed b-Ti dendrite/nanostructured matrix composite
microstructure for a Ti 60Cu14Ni12Sn2Nb10 alloy which has a
remarkably high compressive strength of 2.4 GPa and 14.5%
plastic strain to failure at room temperature. Recently, bulk
binary Ti /C0 Fe alloys consisting of b-Ti and TiFe were
observed to exhibit a high strength of /C24 2 GPa and reasonable
ductility of 4%–7%. These alloys were made by arc-melting
and do not require injection mould casting and rapid solidiﬁ-
cation.
9,10 The additions of third elements such as Sn, 11–14
Ta,15 Co,16 and Nb 17 can further increase the strength and
improve the ductility of eutectic and hypereutectic Ti /C0 Fe
alloys. Investigations have shown that Nb is a very good b-Ti
stabilizer and the formation of a solid solution between Ti
and Nb exists over a wide range of stoichiometry according
to the Ti /C0 Nb binary phase diagram.
18,19 It is expected that
Sn and Nb additions will enhance the strength and improve
the plasticity of the eutectic Ti
70.5Fe29.5 alloys.
So far, information about the Sn and Nb combined
modiﬁed mechanical properties for eutectic Ti /C0 Fe alloys is
still limited. Therefore, it is the objective of the present work
to investigate the deformation and mechanical behaviour of
Ti/C0 Fe eutectic alloys modiﬁed by adding Sn and Nb. Em-
phasis is placed on studying the correlation between the
microstructures and mechanical properties. Such a study is
expected to be helpful in developing high-performance mate-
rials via manipulation of the microstructure.

The high ductility of the Sn and Nb modiﬁed Ti /C0 Fe alloy is
attributed to several factors including ultraﬁne eutectic micro-
structure, morphologies of the eutectics, and compatibility
between the A2 b-Ti and B2 TiFe phases. The globular mor-
phology of the B2 TiFe phase is believed to be another impor-
tant factor improving the ductility of the (Ti 70.5Fe29.5)93.15
Sn3.85Nb3 alloy. For instance, the hypereutectic Ti60Mn40 alloy
consisting of needle-shaped TiMn andb-Ti showed a high ulti-
mate strength of 1.64 GPa but its plastic deformation before
fracture was only 0.1%.16
In summary, Sn and Nb modiﬁed ultraﬁne Ti/C0 Fe eutectic
alloys were prepared by cold crucible levitation melting and
their mechanical properties were tested in compression at am-
bient temperature. SEM and TEM investigations indicated
that lamellar structures which are present in the eutectic
Ti
70.5Fe29.5 alloy could be modiﬁed by addition of Sn and Nb
to obtain a globular structure. The ductility is hereby signiﬁ-
cantly improved which is reﬂected in an increase of the plastic
strain from 5% in the non-modiﬁed Ti 70.5Fe29.5 alloy, to 15%
in the Sn and Nb co-modiﬁed (Ti 70.5Fe29.5)93.15
Sn3.85Nb3 alloy while preserving a ultimate strength of
