ABSTRACT
Efforts to enhance the self-sharpening properties of tungsten heavy alloy based kinetic energy penetrators have 
led to innovations in its compositions and processing techniques. Mo and Nb are the two potential alloying el -
ements for tungsten heavy alloys (WHA) due to their grain refinement capabilities and lower thermal properties 
respectively. Accordingly, this research investigates the influence of Mo and Nb addition to a WHA of compo -
sition 90 W- (6,2,2)(Ni:Fe:Co) in concentrations of 1.25, 2.5 and 3.75 wt%. The alloys were sintered at 1500 
◦
C in 
a reducing hydrogen atmosphere for 60 min. The results demonstrated tungsten grain refinement with Mo 
addition, whereas Nb addition resulted in a slight grain growth. Ni-Nb binary eutectic reactions at 1175 
◦
C lead 
to an early liquid phase formation expanding the time regime for tungsten grain growth. Additionally, Nb 
addition caused NbO
2 
formation and Nb – rich Fe – lean strips and channels to form in the matrix. Increase in 
contiguity and dihedral angle was observed with increase in Mo and Nb concentration due to higher undissolved 
tungsten in the alloy. Mo addition resulted in enhanced compressive strength due to refined tungsten grain size, 
matrix solid solution strengthening and increased tungsten contiguity. Whereas Nb addition resulted in poor 
compressive strength at lower Nb concentrations due to pore formation, however the compressive strength was 
found to increase at higher Nb concentration due to solution strengthening, increase in contiguity and minimized 
porosity.
1. Introduction
Tungsten heavy alloys (WHA) have been used as kinetic energy 
penetrators (KEP) since 1940 owing to their high density ( ≥ 17 g/cc), 
high tensile strength, high hardness, etc. [ 1 ] WHAs were identified as a 
lucrative option for KEP as a replacement to depleted uranium alloys 
(DUA), which had several environmental concerns [ 2 ]. Despite their 
favorable mechanical properties, WHAs possess poor penetration prop -
erties due to higher limit velocity, high tunnel diameter and a lower 
penetration depth compared to DUA [ 1 , 3 , 4 ]. These poor penetration 
properties are a result of mushrooming of the penetrator head when 
impacting and penetrating the armour plate. Mushrooming of the pen -
etrator head is a result of delay in adiabatic shear band (ASB) formation 
which allows for sloughing of the inverted material [ 5 ]. The delay in 
ASB formation is due to high melting point of tungsten, high strain 
hardening and strain rate hardening characteristic of tungsten and 
thermal properties, such as specific heat and thermal conductivity of the 
elements composing WHA [ 6 ]. To achieve suitable performance 
parameters for WHA the mushrooming of the penetrator head has to be 
avoided by inducing early adiabatic shear bands.
Several attempts have been made to induce early shear banding, such 
as adding alloying elements and subjecting the sintered alloys to plastic 
deformation [ 4 ]. Tungsten grain size refinement caused by adding 
alloying elements can help in inducing ASB. Refining the tungsten grain 
size leading to improved penetration properties has been reported pre -
viously. Luo et al. demonstrated a 10.5 % increase in penetration depth 
with a reduction in tungsten grain size in a 95-W-3.75Ni-1.25Fe WHA 
[ 21 ]. Similarly, Fan et al. reported an insensitivity towards work hard -
ening for a fine-grained 93-W – 4.9Ni – 2.1Fe WHA with Y
2
O
3 
addition 
[ 28 ]. Addition of several alloying elements such as Mo, Co, Mn, Ta, Re 
have been studied for their effect on microstructure [ 4 , 7 , 8 ]. Several 
studies have reported that Mo addition resulted in tungsten grain 
refinement due to restricted tungsten diffusion in the matrix, hindering 
its coarsening kinetics [ 9 – 12 ]. Kemp et al. studied the effect of 8 wt% Mo 
in 82-W-8Ni-3Fe composition WHA [ 11 ]. They found that Mo addition 
slows down the W grain growth kinetics with the addition of Mo.

Meanwhile, Co addition increased tungsten grain size due to enhanced 
tungsten grain solubility in the matrix, promoting tungsten grain growth 
[ 13 , 14 ]. On the other hand, Mn addition helps in tungsten grain 
refinement as it helps to reduce the sintering temperature [ 15 , 16 ]. In 
addition to changes in microstructural features, the addition of alloying 
elements also resulted in changes in mechanical properties. Co-addition 
increases strength ranging from 1400 MPa to 1612 MPa (ultimate tensile 
strength after heat treatment + swagging) [ 17 ]. A binder with a 
composition of Ni:Fe:Co 6:2:2 resulted in better strength and ductility 
[ 18 ]. A. Bose and R. M. German demonstrated the tungsten grain 
refinement effect of Mo (4, 8, 12 and 16 wt%) on WHAs causing incre -
ment in mechanical properties [ 19 ].
Thermal properties such as specific heat and thermal conductivity of 
uranium in DUA are lower compared to the elements used to make 
WHA, which is one of the reasons for early formation of ASB in it [ 4 ]. 
Alloying elements studied such as Mo, Mn, Co, and Cu have either of the 
two thermal properties in higher ranges. The addition of alloying 
element with lower specific heat and thermal conductivity can help 
lower the thermal properties, which may help in early ASB formation 
during penetration. Nb is found to have a lower thermal property 
making it one of the favorable materials for alloying in WHA. Nb also has 
an added benefit of having density comparable to that of Ni, Co, Fe while 
Mo has a higher density of 10.28 g/cc. Despite these favorable factors, 
Nb has not been studied for its effect on microstructure and mechanical 
properties in a W-Ni-Fe-Co composition alloy. Existing studies available 
with Nb-added WHA focus more on the effect of mechanical alloying of 
oxide reinforcements [ 20 ]. However, Mo has been studied for its effect 
on WHA but in concentrations higher enough to cause a substantial 
reduction in the WHA density [ 21 – 23 ]. To qualify for KEP the alloy must 
have a density more than 17 g/cc, which is possible if the alloy is 
composed of at least 90 wt% tungsten [ 1 ]. Studies conducted so far were 
on alloys of compositions with a Mo concentration more than 2.5 wt% 
replacing W which reduces the W content to less than 90 %. This 
reduction renders such alloy unsuitable for KEP applications.
The current study examines the effect of Mo and Nb on WHA, 
wherein the tungsten concentration for all the alloys were maintained at 
90 wt% and matrix in ratio of 6:2:2 of Ni, Fe and Co. Mo and Nb were 
added at concentrations of 1.25, 2.5 and 3.75 wt% replacing Ni, Fe and 
Co. All the alloys were subjected to an identical sintering temperature of 
1500 
◦
C for 60 min in hydrogen atmosphere. A comparative study 
among the base alloy, Mo added alloy and Nb added alloy was carried 
out to observe the changes in microstructure, phases formed and me -
chanical properties. Microstructural features such as tungsten grain size, 
phase fractions, contiguity and dihedral angle were analyzed. X-ray 
diffraction and Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy analysis studied 
variations in phase formation at different concentrations of alloying 
elements. Finally, the sintered samples were tested for compressive 
strength and hardness. The microhardness of the phases formed was also 
characterized for changes observed with addition of alloying elements.

2.1. Sample preparation
Tungsten heavy alloys with compositions given in Table 1 were used 
for this study. 1.25 wt%, 2.5 wt% and 3.75 wt% Mo and Nb were added 
independently to a 90-W – 6:2:2 (Ni:Fe:Co) composition base alloy. The 
nomenclature of the alloys is based on the concentration of the alloying 
elements. Therefore, the base composition alloy is here after mentioned 
as WNFC, similarly alloys with Mo and Nb added in are denoted as 
WNFCxMo and WNFCxNb respectively (x = 1.25, 2.5 and 3.75). Further, 
all the alloys with Mo and Nb addition are collectively mentioned as the

compositions were carefully weighed and hand mixed for 20 min in a 
mortar pestle. There after the powders were blended using a turbo mixer 
(Make: Alphie) for 60 min at a speed of 80 RPM without any mixing 
medium. Negligible change in theoretical density is observed with 
addition of Nb replacing Ni, Fe and Co in the WNFCNb series of alloy.

Changes in tungsten morphology from polygonal in elemental state 
to a rounded in liquid phase sintered alloy is a resultant of solution 
reprecipitation [ 4 , 27 ]. Liquid phase sintering comprises three broad 
stages: liquid formation, rearrangement, and finally solution reprecipi -
tation, which causes microstructural coarsening [ 28 ]. During sintering, 
after the liquid phase is formed, tungsten from the powder particles 
diffuses into the nickel-rich liquid phase. Initially, diffusion occurs from 
areas of high energy, such as asperities, small tungsten particles, and 
convex surfaces, leaving a smooth surface behind. Upon cooling below 
the sintering temperature, tungsten begins to reprecipitate from the 
matrix onto the undissolved tungsten grains to attain a shape with the 
least surface energy. Thus, a rounded morphology of tungsten is 
generally obtained.

During the initial stages of liquid formation, the liquid phase tends to 
wet the surface of tungsten grains and rearrange the tungsten grains by 
capillary action to achieve a dense microstructure [ 27 ]. The capillary 
force exerted by liquid over the tungsten grains tends to move the 
tungsten grains closer. This rearrangement results in the elimination of 
vacant spaces in the alloy. To achieve better packing, tungsten grains 
tend to accommodate accordingly while on the other hand matrix tries 
to fill in the pores present in the alloy. Only after most of the pores are 
eliminated, solution reprecipitation starts to cause rounding up of 
tungsten grains, as reduction in surface energy due to closure of pores is 
higher than the reduction of surface energy accompanied by rounding 
up of tungsten grain edges [ 34 ]. But in the case of WNFCNb series of 
alloys, a relatively higher fraction of pores was still visible in the 
microstructure ( Fig. 4 ). As the matrix was still trying to fill up the pores 
in the alloy the tungsten grain rounding was found to a lower extent. 
Parallelly tungsten is getting diffused into the matrix and reprecipitating 
out in regions with no pores in the vicinity. The wavy contours of 
tungsten grains in Fig. 4 (e, f & g) can be explained by the above.

A combined effect of tungsten and matrix hardening or softening was 
found in bulk hardness measurements. Bulk hardness of WNFC and 
WNFCNb series of alloys was found in close vicinity due to softening of 
tungsten grains compensated by strengthening of matrix by Nb addition. 
While WNFCMo series of alloys experienced a gradual increase in 
strength triggered by tungsten grain refinement and matrix strength -
ening with increase in Mo concentration.
Compression testing of the samples revealed the effectiveness of the 
addition of Mo and Nb to the alloy ( Fig. 16 ). Mo was found to help in 
increasing compressive strength of the alloys ( Fig. 16 a). Tungsten grain 
size reduction (as seen from SEM micrographs Fig. 4 and in Table 4 ). 
Furthermore, solid solution strengthening of the γ matrix (as computed 
and given in Table 6 ) due to Mo addition and an increase in tungsten 
phase fraction in the WNFCMo series of alloys lead to the cumulative 
increase in the strength. On the other hand, Nb addition resulted in 
lowering compressive strength. WNFC1.25Nb manifested a strength of 
1751 ± 171 MPa i.e. a 16 % reduction in strength compared to WNFC. 
Strength of the alloys increased with increase in Nb concentrations to 
2079 ± 338 MPa for WNF2.5Nb alloy and 2592 ± 42 MPa for 
WNFC3.75Nb alloy as seen in Fig. 16 b.
Table 7 compiles the compression test results between the present 
study and test results from the literature. There are very few reports on 
the compression test results for WHA and even fewer on compositions 
close to those investigated in the present study. The major factors 
contributing to strength in a liquid phase sintered WHA are solid solu -
tion strengthening of the matrix, tungsten grain size effect and tungsten 
precipitation in the matrix. In the present investigation, the addition of 
Co to the alloy was found to enhance the compressive strength due to 
increment in matrix ’ s solid solution strengthening when compared to 
the 90 W-7Ni-3Fe (alloy without Co content) as reported in an earlier 
study by the authors [ 29 ]. Moreover, with the addition of Mo not only 
solid solution strengthening but tungsten grain effect also played a 
major role in enhancing the alloy ’ s strength. Whereas formation of ox -
ides along with porosity on addition of Nb caused a degradation in the 
alloys strength. A very similar trend was found in one of our previous 
studies on WHA of composition 90 W-7Ni-3Fe, where increment in 
compressive strength was found on addition of Mo due to tungsten grain 
refinement while porosity formed after Nb addition resulted in poor 
compressive strength [ 29 ]. The effect of tungsten grain refinement was 
also observed by Luo et al. where an increment in compressive yield 
strength was observed for fine grained 95 W-3.75Ni-1.25Fe composition 
alloy [ 43 ]. Similarly, in a study by Adhikari et al. on 89 W-6Ni-4Co 
alloyed with ZrB
2 
it was reported that the alloy with smaller tungsten 
size and lower fraction of porosity demonstrated an increase in 
compressive strength similar to that observed in WNFC3.75Nb alloy 
(present study) [ 8 ]. Finally in another study by Adhikari et al. on the 
effect of milling and addition of Cr
2
AlC in 90 W-6:2:2(Ni-Fe-Co) 
composition WHA, the alloy experienced a degradation of compressive 
strength as a result of formation of oxides [ 44 ]. This behavior was found 
similar to that observed for WNFCNb series of alloy in the present study.
The effect of tungsten grain boundary strengthening on the strength 
of the alloy cannot be realized fully as most of the tungsten grains are not 
surrounded by adjacent tungsten grains but the γ matrix. Panchal et al. 
examined alloys of different compositions and stated that solid solution 
strengthening plays an important role in increasing alloys strength [ 45 ]. 
The modulus mismatch, changes in crystal structure (W – BCC and γ 
matrix FCC) and stark difference in composition between the two phases 
(tungsten grains and γ matrix) plays a crucial role in hindering the 
dislocation transmission from one phase to another [ 46 ]. The difference 
in the mechanical properties of the different phases determines the al -
loys strength and toughness. Tungsten has a higher modulus of 310 GPa 
compared to that of γ matrix ( γ matrix comprises of approx. 53 at.% Ni, 
17 at.% Co and 16 at.% Fe, all of which have a modulus lower than that 
of W). The drop in modulus across the grain boundary could have made 
the process of dislocation emittance easier in one direction but not in 
other direction. The detailed mechanism of strain transfer among the 
two phase is a point of further detailed investigation.

5. Conclusion
Addition of Mo and Nb to W-Ni-Fe-Co tungsten heavy alloys has 
provided with some insights regarding their influence on the micro -
structure features, phase formation and mechanical properties. On one 
hand Mo addition helped in tungsten grain refinement due to restricted 
tungsten diffusion into the matrix resulting in finer tungsten grains of 
19.08 ± 10.17 μ m compared to 29.98 ± 11.49 μ m for the WNFC base 
alloy. On the other hand Nb addition resulted in tungsten grain coars -
ening to 32.7 ± 11.54 μ m as a result of a Ni-Nb eutectic reaction at 
temperatures lower than the sintering temperature. Additionally, Nb 
introduction to the alloy lead to formation of Nb rich – Fe lean channels 
and strips in higher concentration alloys. Nb addition was also accom -
panied with NbO
2 
formation due to Nb ’ s high affinity to oxygen. Higher 
undissolved tungsten with Mo and Nb addition led to an increase in 
tungsten fraction resulting in increased tungsten to matrix phase frac -
tion. This increased fraction results in an increase of alloys contiguity 
and dihedral angle. Micro hardness of tungsten grains in the WNFCMo 
series of alloys was found to reduce due to solid solution softening 
caused by introduction of Mo. Micro-hardness test of γ matrix revealed 
an increase in its hardness with Nb addition caused by solid solution 
strengthening and formation of Nb rich strips and channels, while the γ 
matrix hardness with Mo was found to show irregular variation. The 
increased tungsten grain fraction, reduction in tungsten grain size, 
increased contiguity, solid solution strengthening of the γ matrix lead to 
an increase in compressive strength in the Mo added alloy. Whereas high 
fraction of porosity in alloys with lower concentrations of Nb caused a 
reduction in alloys strength. The compressive strength of the WNFCNb 
series of alloys did increase latter with increase in Nb content due to 
reduced pore fraction, solid solution strengthening and formation of Nb 
rich Fe lean phases. As per the observations Nb in its raw form to be used 
as alloying element is not suitable for applications such as kinetic energy 
penetrator for tungsten heavy alloy. This is due to the formation of NbO
2 
causing reduction in alloy density. Lowering of the sintering tempera -
ture with use of Nb could be examined to explore its grain refinement 
capabilities. Whereas increase in compressive strength with addition of 
Mo can be beneficial in its use for kinetic energy penetrator applications.
