ABSTRACT
In this work, the high-temperature tensile properties of in-situ Al
2
O
3 
reinforced Al-20Si alloy fabricated by 
powder metallurgy were systematically investigated. Two types of powders, coarse and fine, were used as var -
iables for comparative study. The results indicate that both particle sizes exhibit an excellent combination of high 
strength and superior elongation at room temperature, with an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of approximately 
325 MPa and elongation ranging from ~8.1 % to ~11.6 %. At an increased test temperature of 200 ℃ , the alloy 
with finer particle size demonstrated a UTS of 207 MPa and an elongation of 14.1 %, whereas the coarser particle 
size alloy showed a UTS of 137 MPa and an elongation of 19.6 %. The enhanced strength of the alloy with finer 
particles at elevated temperatures can be attributed to the presence of highly thermally stable in-situ Al
2
O
3
. The 
ultrafine Si particles, enveloped by a dense Al
2
O
3 
film, resist Ostwald ripening, while the fine Al grain boundaries 
are pinned by the in-situ Al
2
O
3 
particles, which hinders their migration. These factors contribute to stabilizing 
the microstructure at elevated temperatures, resulting in high strength. This work provides an effective approach 
to achieving excellent high-temperature tensile properties in Al-Si alloys.
1. Introduction
Al-20Si alloys, known for their low density and excellent mechanical 
properties at both room and high temperatures, have attracted signifi -
cant attention in industries such as automotive and aerospace [ 1 ]. These 
alloys find applications in air compressors, as well as engine components 
like pistons, cylinder heads, and rotary engine side housings [ 2 ]. How -
ever, conventional cast Al-20Si alloys often contain coarse, irregular 
primary Si and acicular eutectic Si phases, which serve as stress con -
centration points, leading to suboptimal mechanical properties [ 3 , 4 ]. 
Various studies have attempted to address this issue by incorporating 
modifiers such as P, [ 2 , 3 , 5 ], Sr [ 6 ], Y [ 7 ], Er [ 8 ] into the melt to refine Si 
particles through heterogeneous nucleation. However, the improve -
ments in mechanical properties have been limited, with tensile strength 
and elongation typically ranging from 100-300 MPa and 0.5 – 4 %, 
respectively [ 1 , 7 – 12 ].
The limitations of the casting process itself contribute to the inferior 
mechanical properties, as the Si particles tend to grow rapidly and 
irregularly at high casting temperatures. In contrast, powder metallurgy 
(PM) techniques offer promising improvements by producing finer Si 
particles, made possible by lower molding temperatures and the use of 
fine powder. For example, Cai et al. successfully fabricated Al-22Si alloy 
via hot pressing with powder sizes below 74 μ m and a sintering tem -
perature of 565 
◦
C, achieving a tensile strength of 148 MPa and an 
elongation of ~5 % [ 13 ]. Later, Ma et al. refined this approach using 
~25 μ m powder and an extrusion temperature of 450 
◦
C, producing Si 
particles as small as 1.18 ± 0.07 μ m. This resulted in a significantly 
improved tensile strength of ~261.5 ± 2.1 MPa and elongation of ~5.8 
± 0.1 %, surpassing conventional cast alloys [ 14 ].
Despite these advancements, most studies on Al-20Si alloys focus on 
room-temperature tensile properties, with limited research on their 
high-temperature behavior. At elevated temperatures, aluminum

2. Experimental
Gas atomized pre-alloyed powders served as the raw material for the 
preparation of hypereutectic Al-20Si alloy in this study. Two distinct 
types of powders were utilized, differing primarily in their particle size 
distribution. The first type, designated as finer powder (FP), exhibited 
particle sizes ranging from 1 to 2 µ m, whereas the second type, referred 
to as coarser powder (CP), featured particle sizes spanning 20 to 25 µ m . 
Both powder types underwent natural oxidation over a duration of 
approximately three months.
Cylindrical specimens measuring 100 mm in diameter and 200 mm 
in height were fabricated through cold isostatic pressing (CIP) at a 
pressure of 180 MPa for 5 min. Subsequently, these specimens under -
went sintering at 430 
◦
C for 2 h, followed by extrusion at 400 
◦
C with an 
extrusion ratio of 20:1 and a speed of 1 mm/s. The extruded specimens 
derived from FP and CP were designated as E-FP and E-CP, respectively.
The characterization of the samples was conducted using scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM, Zeiss Merlin, Germany) equipped with 
electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) and energy dispersive X-ray 
spectroscopy (EDX) detectors. Additionally, transmission electron mi -
croscopy (TEM, FEI Talos 210, USA) was employed to investigate the 
nano-scaled secondary phases. Sample preparation for TEM analysis 
involved cutting the extruded bars, grinding both sides of the sample to 
achieve a thickness close to 100 µ m , and punching discs with a diameter 
of 3 mm. These discs were further thinned using a precision ion pol -
ishing system (PIPS, Gatan, USA) set at 6.0 kV and an incident angle of 
4
◦
. The oxygen content of the Al-20Si alloy was quantified using an 
oxygen analyzer (Leco, ONH836, USA). Dog bone-shaped cylindrical 
tensile specimens with a gauge length of 37 mm and a diameter of 6 mm 
were machined and subjected to tensile testing using a tensile testing 
machine (Instron, 8032, USA) at a crosshead speed of 1 mm/min. Ten -
sile tests were conducted at temperatures of 25, 100 and 200
◦
C, with 
samples preheated for 10 min prior to testing at each temperature to 
ensure thermal equilibrium. Three samples from each group were tested.

4. Discussion
4.1. Grain refinement behavior during hot extrusion
Although the CP powder exhibits a significantly larger particle size 
(~20 – 25 μ m) compared to the FP powder (~1 – 2 μ m), the E-CP Al-Si 
alloy surprisingly presents a much finer grain structure after hot extru -
sion, whereas the grain size in the E-FP alloy remains nearly unchanged 
( Fig. 3 ). This phenomenon can be rationalized by analyzing the initial 
microstructures of the two powders and their evolution during pro -
cessing. As shown in Fig. 6 a and b, FP powders are composed of nano -
scale primary Si and eutectic Si phases, while CP powders contain 
micron-sized primary Si particles (~2 μ m) along with nanoscale 
eutectic Si exhibiting a lamellar morphology with an average width of 
~50 nm, as further revealed by STEM-EDX imaging in Fig. 6 c. Upon 
heating and hot extrusion, the primary Si particles in both powder types 
grow, and the eutectic Si phases tend to agglomerate and spheroidize. 
This results in average Si particle sizes of approximately 0.82 μ m in the 
E-FP alloy and 2.21 μ m in the E-CP alloy, respectively. The main reason 
for the larger Si particle size in E-CP samples is attributed to the coarser 
initial Si distribution and thermally driven coarsening during extrusion. 
Although plastic deformation during extrusion typically promotes 
fragmentation and refinement of brittle phases like Si, this effect is 
significantly moderated under the present high-temperature conditions. 
The softened Al matrix at 430 
◦
C reduces localized stress concentrations 
and shear forces acting on Si particles, thereby limiting fragmentation 
efficiency. In essence, the high temperature acts as a mechanical buffer, 
allowing thermal-driven coarsening to outweigh deformation-induced 
refinement.
More critically, as shown in Fig. 6 d, EBSD analysis reveals that the 
initial Al grains in the CP powder are already fine, with sizes in the range 
of ~1 – 2 μ m. These fine Al grains, combined with the uniform dispersion 
of eutectic Si particles, provide favorable conditions for dynamic 
recrystallization during hot extrusion. Moreover, the presence of 
numerous eutectic Si interfaces facilitates particle-stimulated nucle -
ation, which further accelerates the dynamic recrystallization process 
and contributes to the formation of a homogeneous, refined micro -
structure in the E-CP alloy. By contrast, the FP powders possess a 
nanocrystalline Al matrix and ultra-fine Si phase, which are already 
close to the stability limit for grain refinement. Thus, the extent of

5. Conclusions
In this study, Al-20Si alloy samples were prepared using cold 
isostatic pressing with fine and coarse pre-alloyed Al-20Si powders, 
followed by hot extrusion. The microstructure and high-temperature 
mechanical properties of the alloys were thoroughly investigated, 
yielding the following key findings: 
(1) The alloys demonstrated superior elongation compared to those 
produced by other methods, while maintaining acceptable 
strength levels. At room temperature, the alloy with finer parti -
cles achieved a UTS of 325 MPa with an elongation of 8.1 %, 
whereas the alloy with coarser particles exhibited a UTS of 325 
MPa and an elongation of 11.6 %.
(2) Throughout all test temperatures, the alloy with finer particle size 
consistently displayed higher strength compared to the coarser 
variant. Notably, at 200 
◦
C, the finer particle alloy showed a 
strength of 207 MPa with an elongation of approximately 14 %.
(3) The enhanced strength of the alloy with finer particles is attrib -
uted to the increased presence of Al
2
O
3 
at the interfaces between 
the Al and Si phases, as well as within the Al matrix. These Al
2
O
3 
particles effectively inhibited grain growth during high- 
temperature tensile testing and contributed to the alloy ’ s 
strengthening by obstructing dislocation movement, resulting in 
improved mechanical properties for the finer grain size alloy.
