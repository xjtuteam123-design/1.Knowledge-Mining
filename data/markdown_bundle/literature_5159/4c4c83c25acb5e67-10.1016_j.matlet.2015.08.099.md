Abstract 
The crystal structure, microstructure, density and compression properties of a novel AlNbTiVZr 
0.5 high 
entropy alloy are reported. After homogenization th e alloy is composed of bcc matrix phase containing 
Laves and Zr 2Al-type second phase particles. The alloy has low d ensity of 5.64 g cm -3. The yield strength 
of the alloy gradually decreases from 1430 MPa at r oom temperature to 680 MPa at 800°C and drops to 
75 MPa at 1000°C. The alloy can be compressed to 50 % height reduction without fracture at temperatures 
from room to 1000°C. Surface cracking is observed o nly at room temperature. The specific yield strengt h 
and ductility of the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy is compared with properties of other high en tropy alloys intended 
for high-temperature usage. 
 
Keywords. 
High entropy alloy; metals and alloys; crystal stru cture; mechanical properties; 
 
Introduction. 
The so-called high entropy alloys (HEAs) – alloys c omposed of 
≥5 principal elements with concentration 
from 5 at.% to 35 at. % - have recently attracted w orldwide attention, mostly due to their encouraging 
properties [1]. For example, some of the HEAs demon strate exceptional strength at elevated temperature s 
which makes them attractive for high temperature ap plications [2]. However, high strength itself is no t 
sufficient: a combination of different properties d etermines potential usage of the alloy. The specifi c 
strength – strength to density ratio – is also impo rtant, i.e. the density of the alloy has to be cons idered. 
The alloys should also have sufficient ductility at ambient temperature. Not many of reported HEAs 
intended for high-temperature application simultane ously have (i) high strength at high temperatures; (ii)

Experimental procedures. 
The alloy with nominal composition of AlNbTiVZr 
0.5 was produced by arc melting of the high-purity 
(≥99.9at.%) elements in an argon atmosphere inside a water-cooled copper cavity. The produced ingot 
with dimensions of ≈6×15×60 mm 3 was re-melted 5 times. The actual chemical composi tion of the alloy 
is given in Table 1. After casting the alloy was ho mogenized at 1200°C for 24 hours in vacuum and 
cooled down on air. 
Phase composition and microstructure of the alloy w ere studied using X-ray diffraction (XRD) and 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The XRD analysi s was performed using RIGAKU diffractometer 
and Cu K 
α radiation. Samples for SEM observations were prepa red by mechanical polishing. SEM 
investigations were performed utilizing Quanta 200 3D microscope equipped with energy-dispersive 
(EDS) detector used for chemical composition measur ements. 
Density of the alloy was measured via hydrostatic weighting. 
Compressive tests were performed on rectangular spe cimens with dimensions of 6×4×4 mm 3 using the 
Instron 300LX machine equipped with radial furnace. The initial strain rate was 10 -4 s -1 and the 
temperatures were 22°C, 600°C, 800°C and 1000°C. Fo r high temperature testing, the specimens were 
placed in preheated furnace and held for 10 minutes before testing. The testing was performed until 
reaching height reduction of 50%. 
 
Results and discussion. 
Figure 1 presents information on structure of the A lNbTiVZr 
0.5 alloy after homogenization. The XRD 
analysis (Fig. 1a) demonstrates that the alloy has multiphase structure. The strongest diffraction pea ks 
belong to bcc lattice with lattice parameter of 0.3 22 nm. Two minor phases are also found – hexagonal 
(C14) Laves and Zr 2Al-type phases. The Laves phase has lattice paramet ers of a=0.535 nm and c=0.818 
nm, while the Zr 2Al-type phase has a=0.493 nm and c=0.599 nm. 
SEM image (Fig. 1b) confirms three-phase structure of the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy: the matrix phase contains 
two types of particles – grey ones and light ones. The average matrix grain size is 22 µm. The grey

particles are predominantly located on bcc grains b oundaries. Some particles are also found inside gra ins. 
The light particles are generally adjacent to the g rey particles; some individual particles are observ ed 
inside bcc grains. The second phase particles have size in range of ≈2-20 µm. The estimated volume 
fraction of grey particles and light particles is 1 0% and 5% respectively. Chemical analysis (Table 1) 
demonstrates that the bcc matrix of the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy has composition closely corresponding to 
actual chemical composition of the alloy, while the grey particles are enriched with Al, Zr and V, and the 
light particles – with Al and Zr. Comparison with X RD results suggests that the grey particles are Lav es 
phase, whereas the light particles are Zr 2Al-type phase. 
The structure of the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy can be compared with that of the Zr-free sin gle bcc phase 
AlNbTiV alloy [13]. Zr addition results in second p hase formation, possibly due to (i) strong chemical 
affinity of Zr to Al ( /uni2206HmixAlZr =-44 kJ/mol [14]), producing the Zr 2Al-type phase, and (ii) high atomic 
radius difference between V and Zr (134 and 158 pm respectively), producing the Laves phase. 
The experimental density of the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy, 5.64 g cm -3, correlates well with the rule of mixture 
predictions (5.63 g cm -3). 
Fig. 2a demonstrates compression stress-strain curv es obtained during testing of the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy 
at different temperatures. At room temperature the alloy reaches yield point at 1480 MPa followed by 
strain hardening stage. At ≈12% strain pronounced drop of stress is observed. S everal drops of stress with 
lower magnitude are observed during further straini ng. The drops are associated with local crack 
formation. Despite cracking, the slope of the curve does not change significantly before reaching 50% 
strain. Macrostructure examination of the specimen compressed to 50% height reduction at room 
temperature (Fig. 2b) demonstrates that although cr ack formation and local fracture occurs on the side 
faces, the middle part of the specimen remains crac k-free. Increase of the testing temperature to 600° C 
results in decrease of the yield strength of the al loy to 1160 MPa. The alloy demonstrates pronounced 
strain hardening after yielding. The alloy is compr essed to 50% strain without any evidences of cracki ng 
at temperatures of 600-1000°C. Pronounced decrease of the yield strength to 680 MPa is observed when 
the testing temperature increases to 800°C. After r eaching peak stress, steady-state flow stage is observed. 
At 1000°C the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy demonstrates low yield strength of 75 MPa an d steady-state flow after 
yielding.

comparison between specific yield strength (SYS) an d ductility of the alloys at room temperature. Most

kPa m3 kg-1 and ductility of ≤10% (marked with circles), (ii) ductile but soft al loys, having SYS<200 kPa

However, at 1000°C the SYS of the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy decreases significantly and becomes inferior to

The high strength of the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy at room and elevated temperatures can be att ributed to (i) 
strengthening effect of Al [7, 8, 12, 13], (ii) pre sence of second phase particles both on grain bound aries 
and inside grains, (iii) fine bcc matrix grain size . But good ductility of the alloy is unexpected. 
Presumably, addition of Zr can make bcc matrix of t he AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy intrinsically ductile [18], 
however, this suggestion has to be verified by addi tional studies. Relatively fine bcc grain size can also 
contribute to good ductility of the alloy. 
 
Conclusions 
In this work, structure, density and compression pr operties of a novel AlNbTiVZr 0.5 high entropy alloy 
were reported. The homogenized alloy is composed of bcc matrix phase containing C14 Laves and Zr 2Al-
type phase particles. The density of the alloy is 5 .64 g cm -3. At room temperature, the alloy demonstrates 
high yield strength of 1430 MPa, and can be compres sed to 50% height reduction with cracking observed 
only on specimen surface. At elevated temperatures no cracking occurs, but yield strength decreases to 
1160, 680 and 75 MPa at temperatures of 600, 800 an d 1000°C respectively. The combination of room 
temperature specific yield strength and ductility o f the AlNbTiVZr 0.5 alloy was found to be superior to 
other high-temperature HEAs.
