abstract
Tensile properties of M951 alloy are shown to be signiﬁcantly dependent on temperatures in this study.
Like in other superalloys, the yield and tensile strengths decreased slightly between room temperature
and 600
◦C, then both strengths showed peak values at intermediate temperatures, and both decreased
sharply above 850 ◦C. The elongation ﬂuctuated with temperature with its minimum at 800 ◦C. The TEM
observations showed that at low temperature the dominant deformation mechanism was /H9253′shearing by
dislocation pairs and slip bands. At high temperature, the deformation was dominated by /H9253′ by-pass. At
intermediate temperature it showed a transition from /H9253′shearing to by-passing.

1. Introduction
It is clearly understood that the high temperature behav-
ior of most Ni-based superalloys are affected by the L1 2 type
ordered /H9253′ phase [1]. At temperature lower than 800 ◦C, cuboidal
/H9253′ precipitates almost maintain their size and shape during creep
deformation because the diffusion rates of alloy elements are rela-
tively low; while above 850
◦C, the /H9253′precipitates become unstable
and link together to form plates [2]. The mechanical properties are
inﬂuenced by the shape and size of /H9253′precipitates [3,4].
Very complicated yielding behavior is exhibited by nickel-based
superalloys containing a high volume fraction of the /H9253′ phase
[5,6]. For example, IN738LC alloy exhibits a ﬂat minimum of yield
strength at 450 ◦C and a maximum at 750 ◦C due to characteristic
deformation mechanisms [7], also this phenomenon was observed
in the single crystal superalloy PWA1480 [8]. The anomalous
increase of the ﬂow stress at high temperature may be attributed
to a change in the mechanism, which involves cross slip of screw
dislocations from (1 1 1) slip planes to the (0 1 0) planes in /H9253
′phase
[9]. The driving force for the change of slip mode with increasing
temperature is provided both by the anisotropy of the anti-phase
boundary (APB) and by the resolved shear stress on the {100 }
⟨110 ⟩system [10]. It has been demonstrated that at high tempera-
ture, dislocations have a lower energy on the (0 1 0) plane than the
(1 1 1) plane because the APB is at a minimum on the (0 1 0) plane
[1 1,12]. Apart from strength, the elongation is an important ductil-
ity parameter regarding the tensile property. There exists an effect
called intermediate temperature brittleness (ITB) with respect to

The alloy with high volume fraction of /H9253

room temperature to 1 100

2. Experimental procedure
The nominal composition of M951 alloy used in this work is
listed in Table 1 .I ti sa /H9253
′-strengthened alloy containing about
58 vol.% /H9253′ phase in the as-cast condition, and Al is the main
/H9253′ stabilizer in this alloy. The master alloy was melted in a
VIM25F vacuum induction furnace, remelted in 10 kg ZG-0.01 vac-
uum induction furnace, and then cast into test bars. The mold
preheating temperature and pouring temperature were 900 and
1450
◦C, respectively. The specimens were directly aged at 1 100 ◦C
for 4 h, and air cooled (AC) to the room temperature. Specimens for
tensile test with a diameter of 5 mm and a gauge length of 25 mm
were machined longitudinally from the heat-treated bars.
The tensile tests were carried out at ten different temperatures
from 20 to 1 100

maintained 3.3 × 10−4 s−1 up to yield and 1.7 × 10−3 s−1 after yield.
Two specimens were tested at each temperature.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructures
TEM images of microstructures for as-cast and heat-treated
M951 alloy are shown in Fig. 1 . As can be seen the unimodal /H9253′
precipitates in the as-cast condition has cuboidal morphology with
an average edge length of 0.649 /H9262m, and the total volume percent
of which is about 58%. After the standard heat treatment (aged at
110 0
◦C for 4 h followed by air cooling), the alloy consists of bimodal
/H9253′ distribution with cuboids particles of 0.730 /H9262m average edge
length and spherical particles of 60–70 nm average diameter. The
cuboidal /H9253
′ particles in the standard heat treatment are less reg-
ular than that of as-cast, and the space between the cuboids are
much larger. Since the solution temperature range of /H9253
′ precipi-
tates is 1 153–1200◦C, some of the /H9253′precipitates may be dissolved
at such a high aging temperature, and cause the precipitation of ﬁne
spherical particles of /H9253

is about 49.4%, less than that of the as-cast condition.
3.2. Mechanical properties
Tensile test results are illustrated in Fig. 2 . The yield strength
(YS) and ultimate tensile strength (UTS) show similar temperature
dependence. As in other superalloys, the yield strength decreases
slightly between room temperature and 600
◦C. It increases with
temperature until it reaches a peak at 800◦C. Beyond this point, the
yield strength decreases sharply. The UTS-temperature curve has
the same trend as that of YS-temperature curve. Similar strength

3.3. Deformation structures
To describe the deformation structure of the fractured speci-
mens, three temperature ranges are considered.
3.3.1. Low temperatures (under 600
◦C)
Fig. 3 shows the TEM micrograph of deformation structures at
room temperature (20 ◦C). High density tangling of dislocations is
observed in the matrix channels where ﬁne /H9253′precipitates are dis-
tributed. There are also presence of dislocation pairs and residual
loops within the /H9253
′, which indicates evidence of /H9253′ shearing [19].
The tangling of dislocations in the matrix channels are related to
the ﬁne spherical /H9253
′ precipitates, which block the further move-
ment of dislocations in to the large /H9253′particles. Dislocations travel
through the /H9253′as closely spaced pairs in order to minimize the APB
area created by the a/2⟨110 ⟩displacement of the superlattice [20].
Both the tangling in the matrix channels and /H9253′shearing lead to the
relatively high strength at 20 ◦C. It is seen that the dislocations are
homogeneously distributed throughout the specimen as indicated
by the uniform contrast in the micrograph, and this is in agreement
with the relatively high elongation as shown in Fig. 2.
Deformation at 600
◦C is different from that of room tempera-
ture. The deformation structure is inhomogeneous, and it consists
of intense single slip bands as shown in Fig. 4 (a). The slip bands

3.3.2. Intermediate temperatures (600–850
◦C)
With increasing temperature to 700◦C, the number of slip bands
increase, and multiple slip is activated on the octahedral {111 }
⟨110 ⟩slip system, the intersecting slip bands are shown in Fig. 5.
The alignment of strong residual contrasts parallel to the projec-
tion of the slip direction is attributed to small loops and debris
which cannot be resolved because of high density. This structure of
slip bands was also observed in two single crystal superalloys [21].
Dislocation substructure in the other regions is similar to that of
600
◦C. As a whole, the slight increase of tensile strength is caused
by the similar dislocation substructures and the increase of ductility
is attributed to the multiple slip.
At peak temperature of ultimate tensile strength, i.e. 760 ◦C, the
dominant deformation mechanism is /H9253′shearing by a/2⟨110 ⟩dis-
locations within the large /H9253′precipitates (Fig. 6(a)), which leads to
the high strength. Also, intersecting slip bands is observed at this
temperature, but their number is much less than that of 700
◦C.
Comparing to low temperatures, the dislocations are mainly dis-
tributed within the /H9253

Another evidence of /H9253′shearing is the array of dislocations, as show
in Fig. 6(b). Actually the single dislocations in the/H9253′are in pairs, they
are constricted within the/H9253′due to the high APB energy, while those
of the same dislocations which emerge out of the/H9253′precipitates are
split into pairs due to the elastic repulsion [19].
At 800 ◦C the yield strength and elongation of the alloy reach
maximum and minimum, respectively. The effect of mid range duc-
tility minimum (MRDM) take place, and this effect is also observed
at the same temperature in M963 alloy and it can be eliminated
by heat treatment [18]. The deformation structure is much more
different than 760

4. Conclusions
The tension test was carried out in the range of 20–1 100
◦C.
The results show that tensile properties of M951 alloy are signif-
icantly dependent on temperature. The yield and tensile strengths
decrease slightly between room temperature and 600
◦C and grad-
ually increase with peak values at intermediate temperatures, then
both strengths decreased sharply with test temperature higher than
850
◦C. The elongation has its minimum at 800◦C. The TEM analysis
of deformation structure shows that the tensile behavior is related
to the deformation mechanisms operating at different temperature
regimes. At low temperatures the dominant deformation mech-
anism is /H9253
′ shearing by dislocation pairs and slip bands. At high
temperatures, the deformation is dominated by /H9253′by-pass includ-
ing Orowan looping and climb-assisted by-passing. At intermediate
temperatures there is a transition from /H9253
′shearing to by-passing.
