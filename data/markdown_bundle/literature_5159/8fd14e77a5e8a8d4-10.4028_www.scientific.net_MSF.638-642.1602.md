Effects of Stacking Faults on High Temperature Creep Behavior in 
Mg-Y-Zn Based Alloys 
Mayumi Suzuki1a and Kouichi Maruyama1b 
1 Ecomaterial Design and Process Engineering Course 
Graduate School of Environmental Studies, Tohoku University, 980-8579, Sendai, Japan 
asmayumi@material.tohoku.ac.jp, bmaruyama@material.tohoku.ac.jp 
Keywords: magnesium alloys, high temperature creep, microstructure, stacking fault energy 
Abstract. Compressive creep behavior of hot -rolled (40%) Mg-Y and Mg-Y-Zn alloys are 
investigated at 480 ~ 650 K. Creep strength is substantially improved by the simultaneous addition 
of yttrium and zinc. The minimum creep rate of Mg-0.9mol%Y-0.04mol%Zn ( WZ301) alloy 
decreases to 1/10 lower than that of Mg-1.1mol%Y (W4) alloy at 650 K. Activation energy for creep 
in W4 and WZ301 alloys are more than 200 kJ/mol at the temperature range of 480 ~ 550 K. These 
values are higher than the activation energy for self-diffusion coefficient in magnesium (135 kJ/mol). 
Many stacking faults (planar defects, PDs) are only observed on the basal planes of the matrix in 
Mg-Y-Zn ternary alloys. Stacking fault energy is considered to decrease by the multiple-addition of 
yttrium and zinc. The size and density of these planar defects depend on solute content, aging 
condition. TEM observation has been revealed that the decreasing of the stacking fault energy 
affects the distribution of dislocations during c reep. Many a-dislocations on basal planes are 
extended significantly. Dislocation motion is restricted significantly by both of these two types of 
stacking faults (planar type and extended dislocations). 
Introduction 
In recent, magnesium based alloys have been applied to portable electronic devices as alternative 
materials for plastic materials for their light -weight and high specific strength . They are also 
attractive in application to the aircraft and automobile parts for increasing the fuel efficiency and the 
transportation speed; however, their poor mechanical properties hinder the wide application of 
magnesium. On the other hand, it has been known that the rare earth (RE) addition is one of the 
effective methods to improve strength of magnesiu m [1-5]. In addition, at the beginning of this 
decade, Kawamura et al. developed new Mg97Y2Zn1 (in mol%) alloy produced by rapid solidification 
of gas-atomized powders [6], which exhibited a high yield stress of 610 MPa at room temperature and 
elongation of 5%. Abe et al. have reported that the long period ordered (LPSO) structure including 
stacking faults is formed in this alloy, and solute atoms of zinc and yttrium are segregated in two 
layers on each stacking fault [ 7]. This long period ordered structure has been reported as a stable 
phase in Mg-Y-Zn-Zr alloy by Luo et al. [8], similar structures have been reported in several kinds of 
Mg-Zn-RE systems [ 9]. However, from the engineering point of view, the content of RE in 
magnesium alloys is expected to decrease for their high cost performance. On the other hand, Suzuki 
et al. have reported that creep strength of an Mg -3mass% Y (about 1mol%Y) solid solution alloy is 
significantly improved by the microalloying of Zn (~0. 1 mass% Zn) [ 10, 11]. They have also 
reported that stacking fault energy decreases in these alloys thereby many stacking faults (not long 
period ordered structure) are formed on (0001) matrix planes [12]. However, the understanding has 
not been enough about the effects o f Zn contents on creep strength and microstructures. T he 
contribution of the stacking fault itself has not been clear yet, too. Furthermore, the creep mechanism 
of Mg-Y based alloys has still been the subject under discussion. 
Therefore, creep behavior of an Mg-Y binary alloys and Mg-Y-Zn ternary alloys containing different 
zinc content has been investigated and the role of zinc in the strengthening is discussed.

Experiments 
The materials used in this investigation were hot-rolled plates at 473 to 673 K by 40 % reduction. 
The alloy designations and their compositions are listed in Table 1. The target contents of yttrium in 
the alloys are almost the same as 3 mass% (~1 mol%). All the alloys were solution -treated in an 
argon atmosphere at 750 ~ 790 K and then water-quenched. Average diameters of these alloys were 
ranging from 50 to 204 m. Compressive creep specimens (2 mm x 2 mm x 3 mm, parallelepipeds) 
were machined and were held at the test temperature for 4 h before creep test to stabilize temperature 
of the testing system. Compressive creep tests were carried out at 480 ~ 650 K under constant stress 
in air. Crept specimens were water -quenched under a full loading condition for microstructural 
observations. The morphology of precipitates and the distribution of stacking faults in these alloys 
were observed by using a transmission electron microscope (TEM) at an acceleration voltage of 200 
kV.

considered to be the effects of stacking fault energy. The understanding of the creep mechanism in

It has been reported that t he activation of the non-basal slip of a-dislocations is suppressed by the 
small addition of Zn in the Mg -3mass%Y based alloys [11]. The change of morphology of the 
dislocations on the basal planes in the WZ301 alloy has also been reported, from perfect a-dislocation 
to partial dislocation pair with large separation wid th [12]. The separation width between partial 
dislocation pair depends on temperature, applied stress, and alloy composition. In Mg -3mass%Y 
based alloy, the separation width increases with increasing of PD density. The “true” stacking fault 
energy in the alloys cannot be estimated by the PD density because this value is also affected by the 
segregation of Y and Zn. However, the “apparent” stacking fault energy including segregation 
effects on stacking faults (PD s and extended area between partial dislocation pairs) has correlation 
with both of PD density and separation width of extended dislocations. Thus the suppression of the 
cross-slip in Mg -Y alloy by the addition of Zn was explained by the decrease of the “apparent” 
stacking fault energy. It is concluded that

Summary 
Creep behavior of Mg -Y-Zn based alloys was investigated. The stacking faults like planar defects 
(PDs) on the basal planes are observed in Mg-Y-Zn ternary alloys. The PD density increases with 
increasing Zn content except forming of the additional compounds including Y. The creep strength 
of Mg-3mass%Y binary alloy is significantly improved by the small addition of zinc. The creep 
deformation mechanism in the alloys including Mg-Y binary alloy above 480 K is assigned to Region 
H, the creep region wit h high activation energy. It is suggested that the transition temperature 
between two different activation energies decreases with decreasing the “apparent” stacking fault 
energy by addition of Y and Zn. The recovery rate of the dislocation climb is si gnificantly 
suppressed by the decrease of the “apparent” stacking fault energy.
