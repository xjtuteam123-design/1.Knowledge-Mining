abstract
A novel high-temperature ﬁller alloy, Co42.5Ti42.5Nb15 (at%) developed with which C f/SiC–Nb joints
were successfully obtained at 1553 –1613 K for 10 min. The effects of brazing temperature on the
microstructure and mechanical properties of the joints were investigated. The results show that the
typical C
f/SiC and Nb joint consist of C f/SiC composite/(Ti,Nb)C reaction layer/(Ti,Nb) 2Co layer/Nb[Ti,
Co]þ (Ti,Nb)Co eutectics with some CoNb 4Si compounds/Nb substrates. With increasing the brazing
temperature, both the thickness of the reaction layers and the content of CoNb 4Si in the brazing layer
increased, whereas the thickness of the brazing layer reduced. The shear strength presented a peak value
of 187 MPa from a brazed at 1593 K.

1. Introduction
Carbon ﬁber reinforced silicon carbide ceramic matrix compo-
site (C f/SiC) is an attractive candidate for many applications,
particularly in aerospace and aircraft thermal structural compo-
nents due to its high speci ﬁc strength and modulus, medium
fracture toughness and excellent oxidation resistance at high
temperature [1–5]. One of the obstacles to expand the application
of Cf/SiC structure depends on the reliable joining of the composite
to high temperature metals [6]. Niobium is regarded as one of the
excellent materials for high temperature application [7,8]. There-
fore, joining of C f/SiC and Nb will be bene ﬁcial to elevate the
operating temperature and further extending their application
scopes. And the C f/SiC and Nb joints are promising new structural
components for a variety of high temperature environments,
including turbo pump, supersonic aircraft thermal structures and
space nuclear reactors.
Several joining techniques, such as brazing [9,10], diffusion
bonding [1 1], spark plasma sintering [12] and reaction synthesis
[13] are viewed as adequate for joining the C f/SiC. Brazing, due to
its simplicity and low cost investment, has been widely used in the
Cf/SiC joining. In recent years, Ag –Cu–Ti based ﬁllers have been
used to join C f/SiC composite to itself or to metal alloys [14–16].
Nevertheless, the service temperature of the joints brazed with
Ag–Cu–Ti system ﬁller alloys cannot surpass 773 K. In order to
improve the service temperature of the joints, high temperature
brazing alloys, such as Au –Ni–Pd–V [17],C u –Pd–V [18] and Cu –
Au–Pd–V [19], have been designed for joining the Si 3N4 or C f/SiC.
However, all these brazing alloys have precious metals being
incorporated, like Au and Pd, thus increasing the cost of the brazes.
Ti-based ﬁller alloys present better high temperature behavior
than Ag-based ﬁller alloys, and they are also cheaper than the
Au-based or Pd-based ﬁller alloys. Recently, Ti –Ni–B [20,21] and
Ti–Ni–Nb [22] ﬁller alloys were applied to the brazing of C f/SiC–
metal, and obtained good joints. However, there were many
intermetallic compounds in the joints, which weakened the joints.
And the joint strength sharply decreased when tested above 873 K.
Therefore, it is necessary to develop other high temperature ﬁller
alloys to join ceramic and metal.
It is well known that both Ti and Nb can be used as active
elements when brazing ceramics, and Co, Ti and Nb all have high
melting point. Due to its high melting point, Co –50Ti alloy was
used as high temperature ﬁller alloy to join the SiC ceramic [23].
Additionally, niobium is a hot metal, and Nb and Ti can be
completely miscible. Therefore, Co –Ti–Nb may also be used as a
suitable high temperature brazing alloy for joining SiC based
ceramics. In present study, Co42.5Ti42.5Nb15 (at%) alloy was
developed and used as the active ﬁller to braze C f/SiC and Nb.
The microstructure, interfacial evolution mechanism and shear
strength were investigated to access the effect of brazing tem-
perature on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the
joints.

2. Experimental procedure
The Co42.5Ti42.5Nb15 brazing alloy used in this study was
prepared by arc melting the mixture of electrolytic Co, sponge Ti
and smelt Nb in a water cooled copper crucible under argon atm-
osphere. The purity of the Co, Ti and Nb are 99.95 wt%, 99.7 wt%
and 99.9 wt%, respectively. The casting was repeated ﬁve times to
ensure a homogeneous alloy. The brazing ﬁller foils of 4 mm /C2
4 mm were spark cut from the ﬁnal casting, and polished to a
thickness of 0.55 mm after being ground with 800-grit SiC paper.
The base materials were two-dimension C f/SiC composite
prepared by precursor in ﬁltration pyrolysis process [24] and pure
niobium. The composite is reinforced with T-300 carbon ﬁbers,
and low pressure I-CVI was employed to deposit a thin pyrolytic
carbon layer and the silicon carbide matrix. The carbon ﬁbers are
distributed in the composite in the form of bundles. The volume
fraction of carbon ﬁbers is about 40% in C f/SiC. The density of the
composite is 1.98 g/cm 3 and its porosity is 21.5% measured by
Archimedes' method. The composite was cut into cubic specimens
with a size of 4 mm /C2 4m m /C2 4 mm. Nb specimens were cut into
5m m /C2 4m m /C2 4 mm pieces for microstructure observation and
10 mm /C2 5m m /C2 4 mm for shear test. All to be joined surfaces
were ground by SiC papers up to grit 800. Co42.5Ti42.5Nb15 foils,
Cf/SiC and Nb pieces were degreased and cleaned ultrasonically in
acetone for at least 15 min and dried at room temperature prior
to brazing experiment. The ﬁller alloy was sandwiched between
Cf/SiC and Nb, and butted by organic glue. Then the assembly was
put into a graphite jig and a pressure of 0.5 kPa was applied on the
top of the assembly to ensure close contact between the brazing
alloy and the substrates. Finally, the graphite jig was pla-
ced in furnace with a vacuum of 1.0 –5.0 /C2 10/C0 3 Pa during brazing.
The temperature was initially increased to 573 K at the rate of
10 K/min, and held for 30 min to make organic glue volatilized.
Subsequently, the temperature further rose to 1473 K at 10 K/min
and then reached the brazing temperature (1553–1613 K) at 5 K/min
and kept steady for 10 min. Finally, the specimens were cooled
down to 573 K at 5 K/min and then cooled inside the furnace wit-
hout power.
The microstructures of the ﬁller alloy Co42.5Ti42.5Nb15 and the
brazed joint were analyzed by a scanning electron microscope (SEM,
Quanta 200FEG) equipped with an energy-dispersive spectroscope
(EDS). Phase composition of ﬁller metal was examined by X-ray
diffraction (XRD, D/max-rb) with Cu K
α radiation, and its melting
temperature was measured by differential thermal analysis (DTA, TGA/
SDTA851e). The detailed microstructure of the joints was also char-
acterized by transmission electron microscopy (TEM, TecnaiG2F30) at
300 kV. The TEM specimens with a thickness of 200 nm were prepared
via focused ion beam (FIB, Helios Nanolab600i) technique. The shear
strength of the brazed joints was tested on a universal testing machine
(instron-5569) with a cross-head speed of 0.5 mm/min. At least three
specimens were tested for each experimental condition.

32.31at% Nb, and owned a similar crystal structure to Ti 2Co
according to the electron diffraction pattern in Fig. 4 (e). Besides,
Ti and Nb have ultimate mutual solubility and similar atomic
radius, it is therefore believed that Ti in the Ti 2Co might be
replaced by Nb, and the phase B was eventually determined as
(Ti,Nb)2Co.
Based on the above experimental results and analyses, the
formation mechanism of the phases in the joint was explored.
During heating, eutectic phase in the ﬁller alloy, which is com-
posed of TiCo and Nb[Ti,Co], began to melt as the temperature
reached the melting point (1540 K) of the ﬁller alloy. With further
increasing the temperature, both the block TiCo compounds in the
ﬁller alloy and substrate Nb dissolved into the molten ﬁller alloy,
and meantime the liquid metal in ﬁltrated into the intrinsic voids
of C f/SiC composite. Simultaneously, Ti and Nb diffused towards
the Cf/SiC composite/ﬁller alloy interface due to the high chemical
afﬁnity of Ti, Nb with C [25], then react with the C f/SiC. The
possible reactions can be expressed as follows [26]:
Ti(l)þ C(s)-TiC(s) (1)
Nb(l)þ C(s)-NbC(s) (2)
Ti(l)þ SiC(s)-TiC(s) þSi(l) (3)
Nb(l)þ SiC(s)-NbC(s) þSi(l) (4)

It is easy to understand that the higher the brazing temperature
is, the better the ﬂowability of the ﬁller alloy becomes [27].
Therefore, with the brazing temperature increasing, more liquid
ﬁller alloy will in ﬁltrate in the inherent void of the C f/SiC, and even
ﬂow out, resulting in the decrease of the brazing layer's thickness.
In addition, higher brazing temperature led to more mass trans-
port across the interface and faster growth of the reaction zones,
favoring the increase of the reaction layer's thickness. Meanwhile,
more Si is released into the melted solder. Moreover, the dissolved
rate of niobium into the ﬁller alloy was also increased. Therefore,
CoNb4Si compound in the joints increased, and the shapes
changed from bulk to strip.
It is interesting to note that there is no reaction layer B but
some big TiCo phases exist in the joint when brazed at 1553 K, as
shown in Fig. 5 (a, e). Due to the brazing temperature (1553 K) is
only 13 K higher than the initial melting point of the ﬁller alloy,
but lower than the melting point of the TiCo compound (1598 K),
and the time of the solder kept liquid is short. Therefore, some
TiCo compounds do not completely melt during the brazing
process, whereas grew gradually with the cooling proceeding. As
a consequence, the melting ﬁller alloy reacted with C f/SiC only to
form reaction layer A without reaction layer B at lower brazing
temperature. It is also explained that the reaction layer B gener-
ated after the reaction layer A formation, rather than forming at
the same time.

that the shear strength of the joints depends signi ﬁcantly on the
brazing temperature. When the brazing temperature is 1553 K, the
average shear strength of the joints is 81 MPa. The joint strength
increases rapidly to 161 MPa and 187 MPa, while the brazing

temperature further to 1613 K, the joint strength decreases to

Inﬂuences of the brazing temperature on the joint strength
mainly are focused on two aspects. Firstly, brazing temperature
inﬂuences the thickness of the ceramic/braze interfacial reaction
layer, which will affect the joint strength directly [28]. In this
study, when the brazing temperature is lower (1553 K), the ﬂuidity
of liquid brazing alloy was poor. Moreover, active elements Ti and
Nb diffused relatively slowly and only a thin reaction layer A
formed by reacting with C f/SiC, resulting in a lower strength. It is
ready to form interfacial reaction layers A and B with suitable
thickness at the C f/SiC and ﬁller alloy interface when elevating the
brazing temperature, which can effectively reduce the thermal
residual stress gradient between C f/SiC and ﬁller alloy, and
improve the load transfer capacity. Nevertheless, the joint strength
will decrease when the thickness of the reaction layers exceed a
certain range, attributing to the brittleness of the reaction layers.
Appropriate reaction layers can be obtained by adjusting the
brazing temperature.
Secondly, brazing temperature also affects the amount and
shape of CoNb 4Si compounds formed in the brazing layer. As
explained in Section 3.3 , with the increase of the brazing tem-
perature, the CoNb 4Si compounds start from scratch to multiple,
and the shapes change from bulk to strip. Large amounts of brittle
compounds in and even cross the brazing layer will impair the
plastic deformation ability of the ﬁller alloy, which will be
detrimental to relax the thermal residual stress, leading to the
deterioration of the bonding strength.
Fig. 7 displays the fractography of the C f/SiC–Nb joints brazed at
different temperatures from 1553 to 1613 K for 10 min. The
fracture model can be divided into two types relying on the
brazing temperature. The ﬁrst type is that the fracture mainly
occurs in the C f/SiC composite/reaction layer A interface and TiCo
intermetallic compounds in the brazing layer, as shown in Fig. 7(a).
This fracture type indicates that the C f/SiC composite/braze inter-
face bonding is not strong enough and the massive TiCo inter-
metallic compounds (big black phases in the brazing layer in
Fig. 5 a) are harmful to the joints. Fig. 7 (b–d) shows the other
typical fracture that the samples fractured in the interface of the
Cf/SiC composite and brazing layer where some carbon ﬁbers were
also detached from the C f/SiC composite. In the fracture surfaces,
some bulges can also be observed. These bulges are corresponding
to the in ﬁltrated ﬁller alloy (circled by ellipses in Fig. 2 a). When
this fracture type is presented, the joints commonly possess higher
bonding strength.
4. Conclusion
The following conclusions can be drawn:
(1) A novel high-temperature brazing alloy Co42.5Ti42.5Nb15 (at
%) with initial melting temperature of 1540 K was developed,
which is composed of TiCo compound and Nb[Ti,Co] solid
solution. C f/SiC was successfully brazed to Nb using the ﬁller
alloy at different brazing temperatures for 10 min.
(2) The typical C f/SiC–Nb joint comprises three parts: thin (Ti,Nb)
C reaction layer close to C f/SiC, subsequent thin (Ti,Nb) 2Co
layer, Nb[Ti,Co] and (Ti,Nb)Co eutectic in the mid of joint with
some CoNb 4Si compounds. With increasing the brazing tem-
perature, the width of the brazing layer decreases, while the
thickness of the reaction layer A ((Ti,Nb)C) and B ((Ti,Nb) 2Co)
increase, and the CoNb 4Si compounds increase and become
banding.
(3) The shear strength presents the trend of ﬁrst increasing and
then decreasing with the increase in brazing temperature. The
maximum shear strength of the joints reached 187 MPa at a
brazing temperature of 1593 K. The high strength was attrib-
uted to a certain thickness of continuous reaction layers and
optimized microstructure of the brazing layer.
