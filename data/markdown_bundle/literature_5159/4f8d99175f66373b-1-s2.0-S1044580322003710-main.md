ABSTRACT 
In this paper, the mechanical properties of the high-performance powder metallurgy (PM) superalloys with Ta 
addition fabricated by the Central Iron and Steel Research Institute (CISRI) were evaluated at room temperature 
(RT). We focus on the reason why the solute element Ta affects RT properties of PM superalloys. To this end, the 
necessary advanced characterization methods were used to study the plastic deformation and fracture mecha -
nisms. The results revealed that the leading cause for the enhancement in RT yield strength with Ta increasing is 
the antiphase boundary (APB) strengthening of the bimodal γ’ precipitates, the extended stacking fault (ESF) 
shear and the interaction between ESF and dislocation. Importantly, the microtwin structure was found in the 
deformed substructure, which is an extraordinary phenomenon in RT plastic deformation for PM superalloys. 
1. Introduction 
Since the alloy performance is strictly required for turbine disks 
applications in modern aero-engines, powder metallurgy (PM) superal -
loys are generally used as key candidate materials for turbine disks 
[ 1 , 2 ]. Compared with C & W superalloys, the powder metallurgy process 
is possible to manufacture a blank with geometry and size close to the 
final product, resulting in a reduction of metal loss during machining 
[ 3 ]. On the other hand, the high alloying and homogenous microstruc -
ture can be obtained through the powder metallurgy technique, which 
means that the alloying element concentrations can be further increased. 
It is generally believed that the precipitation strengthening Ni-based 
superalloys mainly rely on L1
2 
ordered structure γ’ to ensure high- 
temperature performance. With the alloying increases, the distribution 
of the elements in γ and γ’ should be considered when designing the PM 
superalloy composition, even the effect of the specific element on other 
elements. As a refractory 5 d element, Ta has been added to PM super -
alloys in recent years to improve their mechanical properties (such as 
Ren ´e 104, RR1000, LSHR, and ME501 et al. [ 4 – 8 ]). Actually, the in -
fluence of Ta has been reported in superalloys prepared by other pro -
cesses. For instance, Tien et al. [ 9 ] proposed that Ta is an important 
alloying addition in cast Ni-Fe-base or Ni-base superalloys, it is effec -
tively increasing the tensile strength and creep rupture properties of 
these heat resistant alloys. Similar conclusions have also been reported 
in single crystal and C & W superalloys [ 10 – 14 ]. Nevertheless, there are 
few detailed works on Ta in PM superalloys, and deformation mecha -
nisms have not been understood. 
Recently, the Ta-containing PM superalloys prepared by plasma 
rotation electrode process (RPEP) were developed by our team [ 15 ]. The 
current work evaluated in detail the mechanical properties of PM su -
peralloys with different Ta content at room temperature (RT). In addi -
tion, advanced characterization methods were used to reveal the 
alloying elements distribution and the RT deformation mechanism, such 
as electron probe microanalysis, high-resolution transmission electron 
microscope and electron back-Scatter diffraction. Subsequently, ac -
cording to the RT deformation mechanism of Ta-containing alloys, the 
yield strength models were used to estimate the contribution of the 
strengthening mechanism, and the crucial reasons for the strengthening 
of Ta at RT were discussed.

3. Results and analysis 
3.1. Initial microstructures 
After SHT, the EBSD results show that the grain size distribution 
peaks at 20 – 40 μ m for all experimental alloys, and the recrystallization 
fraction reaches > 98%. The typical microstructural orientation distri -
bution is shown in Fig. 2 . Except for the previous particle boundary 
(PPB) observed in 0Ta alloy, the PPB structure was not found in other 
alloys (follow our previous research [ 15 ]). The grain size of the 0Ta alloy 
seems to be slightly smaller than that of the Ta-containing alloys (see 
Fig. 2 (d)). Due to the growth of recrystallized grains being affected by 
PPB particles, some grains in 0Ta alloy can occasionally be observed to 
exhibit the spherical morphology (see Fig. 2 (a)). In addition, as shown in 
Fig. 3 , a bimodal distribution of γ’ precipitates (the secondary γ’ with a 
size of about 200 – 500 nm and the high-density tertiary γ’ with a size of 
< 50 nm dispersed in the γ channel) was obtained by SHT. With the 
addition of Ta, the secondary γ’ gradually splits from a quasi-spherical 
shape to a butterfly shape, which can be clearly observed in Fig. 3 (b- 
c). The STEM-EDS maps of 0Ta and 4.8Ta alloy exhibit the partitioning 
of alloying elements in γ and γ’ phases ( Fig. 3 (d-e)). The Cr, Co and Mo 
are mainly partitioned into γ matrix, while the Al, Ta, Ti, W and Ni are 
mainly partitioned into γ’ precipitates. Remarkably, Ding et al. [ 19 ] 
found that W prefers to segregate into the γ -matrix in SX single crystal 
superalloys with 8Ta, while Zhu et al. [ 14 ] observed that the W segre -
gated in γ’ of Ni-Co-based alloys with 2Ta. In the current work, it is 
clearly revealed that W is enriched in γ’ precipitates. 
3.2. The tensile and impact properties at RT 
Fig. 4 (a) shows the ultimate tensile strength (UTS), yield strength 
(YS), and elongation variation of experimental alloys with increasing Ta 
content. The additive caused a slight increase in UTS, but a significant 
improvement in YS. Compared to 0Ta alloy, the YS of 4.8Ta alloy is 
increased by about 100 MPa. However, the plasticity of the alloy does 
not deteriorate sharply with the increase of Ta. The elongation still 
reaches about 15% for 4.8Ta alloy. There is a positive correlation be -
tween microhardness and strength, as shown in Fig. 4 (b). As the Ta 
content increases from 0 to 3.6%, the impact absorbed energy drops 
slightly. Unfortunately, as the Ta content approaches 4.8%, this value 
drops sharply. The impact absorbed energy decreased from 41 J in 0Ta 
alloy to 18.5 J in 4.8Ta alloy.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Deformed microtwin structure at room temperature 
According to the most recent theories [ 35 ], after a/6 〈 112 〉 disloca -
tion shears γ’ on the adjacent {111} planes, the pseudo-twin can be 
eliminated by atomic diffusion to become true-twins. Smith et al. [ 36 ] 
believe that decorrelation [ 34 ] is not a necessary condition for the for -
mation of microtwins, and two-layer CSFs are formed by two same 
Burgers vector a/6 〈 112 〉 partials traversing the γ’ precipitates. The high- 
energy CSF is transformed into low-energy SESF through reordering, 
and then microtwins are formed. In addition, Smith et al. [ 33 , 37 ] also 
found that the leading dislocations in γ’ particles of ME3 alloy are rich in 
Co and Cr (Cottrell atmosphere). At present, the phenomenon of 
segregation occurring in defects for many superalloys has been reported 
[ 38 ]. Nevertheless, thermally activated atomic reordering is critical to 
creating a true twin structure in all these theories. It would mean that 
these studies were conducted at high temperatures ( > 650 
◦
C, low-strain 
rate conditions, i.e., creep deformation). However, in this study, a twin- 
like structure was found in the initial plastic deformation of the PM 
superalloys at RT. Coincidentally, the microtwins were also found in 
single crystal superalloy during RT deformation in Ref. [ 39 ]. Generally, 
high-energy CSF shearing is not prone to occur during the deformation 
(theoretically, for the fault of Ni
3
Al, CSF has the highest stacking fault 
energy [ 40 ]). If true-twins are formed during RT plastic deformation, it 
seems extremely interesting, because the thermal activation is not 
requisite for the reordering process and this process could be sponta -
neous (the Cottrell atmosphere in leading dislocations or the Suzuki 
effect in superlattice faults). Obviously, this hypothesis needs additional 
research, which will be considered in detail in our subsequent work. 
4.2. The RT yield strength model in PM superalloy with Ta 
The influence of Ta on the other alloying elements partitioning has 
been reported in our work [ 15 ]. Assuming that Ta is only partitioned to 
three phases: MC carbide, γ -matrix and γ’ . The semi-quantitative results 
show that in 2.4Ta and 4.8Ta alloys, the mass fraction of Ta in γ’ pre -
cipitates accounts for 58% and 81% of the total Ta content (it is 
simplified that the chemical composition of secondary and tertiary γ’ are 
identical), and Ta solved in γ -matrix is merely 25 – 34% and 14%, while 
less 8% and 5% are present in MC carbides, respectively. These results 
are visually shown in Fig. 13 (a). The Ta concentration in the matrix is no 
significant change with the increase of Ta addition, and Ta is mainly 
segregated into γ’ precipitates. Therefore, the following results would be 
generated if Ta was added to the alloy: (i) The solid solution hardening 
effect of the γ -matrix is changed, (ii) the γ / γ’ lattice mismatch is 
increased and (iii) the γ -SFE and the L1
2 
ordering energy (APB, CSF 
energy, etc.) are reduced. For (i), the Ta concentration in the matrix 
remains nearly constant. Therefore, the contribution of Ta to the solid 
solution hardening is limited. More Ta atoms are selectively segregated 
into γ’ precipitates, which illustrates that the γ’ strengthening mecha -
nism plays a major role. For coherent lattice mismatch, it shows the 
change in shape and size of γ’ with Ta increases. For (iii), using the 
method of measuring the end-on partial dislocations spacing, we esti -
mated the matrix stacking fault energy of 0Ta, 2.4Ta, and 4.8Ta alloys as

5. Conclusion 
In this study, the performance of the PM superalloys with Ta addition 
was evaluated at room temperature, the fracture and plastic deformation 
mechanisms were discussed. The following main conclusive contents 
were summarized: 
(1) The RT strength of PM superalloy is enhanced due to the Ta ad -
ditive, and the tensile ductility is slightly reduced, but the impact 
absorbed energy of the 4.8Ta alloy drops rapidly. This phenom -
enon is attributed to the fact that Ta promotes Mo and Cr being 
segregated to the grain boundary after standard heat treatment. 
(2) The plastic deformation mechanism is not changed with 
increasing Ta concentration for experimental alloys. APB-coupled 
dislocations shear and ESFs (or microtwins) shear are active in 
the initial plastic deformation. Surprisingly, the 4-layer twin 
structure was found in experimental alloys. According to HRTEM 
observation, the microtwins are likely to be formed by the a/6 
〈 112 〉 Shockley partials shearing precipitates (CSF configuration) 
on adjacent {111} planes. However, it is uncertain whether the 
twin structure maintains an ordered L1
2 
structure, since the lack 
of atomic site information at the fault. Whether the atomic 
reordering process of high-energy fault can be carried out at room 
temperature would be an interesting issue to be studied in our 
future works. 
(3) According to the yield strength models, the addition of Ta reduces 
the solid solution strengthening effect, but the σ
APB 
is indeed 
increased. However, the improvement effect of σ
APB 
is limited 
because the APB energy decreases and the γ’ particles coarsening 
increases with Ta increasing. The contributions of ESFs (or 
microtwins) to strength cannot be ignored, since σ
SF 
is the main 
reason for the increase in yield strength.
