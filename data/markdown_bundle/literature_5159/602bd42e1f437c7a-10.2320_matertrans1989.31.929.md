Introduction It is generally known(l) that the amorphization of an alloy by a liquid quenching technique gives rise to an in- crease of mechanical strengths and toughness. Accord- ingly, an effort to produce an amorphous phase in Al- and Mg-based alloy systems has been made for the last three decades(2), with the aim of developing high-strength materials with light weight in an amorphous state. Recent- ly, the present authors have reported that ductile Al- based amorphous alloys with high strengths are obtained in rapidly solidified Al-lanthanide (Ln) and Al�� Ln-transition metal and Al-early TM(ETM)-late ternary alloys. Here, ETM and LTM repre- sent the transition metals which are located at the left- and right-hand sides in the periodic table, respectively. It has been notable that the highest tensile fracture strength for the Al-based amorphous alloys reaches 1280 MPa(9) which is 2 to 3 times as high as the highest strengths for conventional and presently developing Al-based crystalline alloys. In a subsequent study on the amor- phization of light metal base amorphous alloys, it has been that a Mg-based amorphous phase with a high tensile fracture strength reaching about 970 MPa is formed in a Mg-Ni��Ce system and that the amorphous alloys in Mg��Ni-Ce and Mg��Ni-La systems exhibit a distinct glass transition phenomenon and a wide super- cooled liquid region. II. Experimental Procedure Mg��Y binary and Mg-Ni��Y and Mg��Cu��Y ternary alloys with compositions above 50 at%Mg were used in the present study. The Mg-based binary and ternary alloy ingots were prepared by induction-melting a mixture of pure Mg (99.99 mass%) metal and Ni��Y alloy in a purified argon atmosphere. The compositions are nominally expressed in atomic per cent hereafter. From the master alloy ingots, ribbons with a rectangular cross section of about 0.02 mm x 1 mm were prepared by a single roller melt-spinning technique in an argon at- mosphere. The amorphous nature of the melt-spun rib- bons was examined by X-ray diffractometry and transmis- Sion electron microscopy (TEM) techniques. The thermal stability including glass transition and crystallization was examined by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) with a heating rate of 0.67 K/ s. The reason for the appearance of glass transition and supercooled liquid region It is shown in Figs. 1 and 2 that the glass transition phenomenon is observed in very wide compositional ranges in the Mg��Ni��Y and Mg-Cu-Y systems and the temperature span between Tg and Tx, Tx�� Tg) reaches as large as 41 K for MgsoNi30Y20 and 61 K for Mg65Cu25Y10. The appearance of the wide supercooled liquid region manifests that the supercooled liquid has a high resistance against the nucleation and growth of a crystalline phase. Here, we shall discuss the reason for the high resistance of the supercooled liquid in the Mg�� Ni-Y and Mg-Cu-Y systems. Since the short-range or- dered structure of an amorphous alloy has been pointed out to reflect the structure of equilibrium compounds(18) an equilibrium phase of MgsoNi30Y20 and Mg65C1125Y10 alloys was examined by conventional metallographic techniques. As a result, the equilibrium structure was identified to consist of mixed phases of Mg2Ni, Mg2Y and Ni3Y for the former alloy and Mg2Cu, Mg2Y and Cu3Y for the latter alloy. In addition, the atomic radii of the constituent Mg, Y and Ni or Cu elements are 0.160, 0.182, 0.125 and 0.128 nm, respectively, and hence the atomic size ratio against Mg is in a wide range of 0.78 to 1.14 for the Mg-Ni-Y system and 0.80 to 1.14 for the Mg-Cu��Y system. That is, the Mg-based amorphous alloys are composed of the three elements with a larger size (Y), a medium size (Mg) and a smaller size (Ni or Cu) and the atomic size ratios of Y /Mg, Mg/Ni and Mg/Cu

In spite of the large atomic size ratios, the three kinds of atoms are thought to mix homogeneously in the amorphous solid and the supercooled liquid. Furthermore, the large difference of the atomic sizes is thought to lead to the construction of a more dense random packing structure. When the amorphous phase crystallizes to the Mg2Ni+Mg2Y+Ni3Y or Mg2Cu+Mg2Y+Cu3Y phases, the constituent elements must be redistributed over a long-range scale, implying the necessity of a long-range diffusion of the constituent atoms. The long-range diffu- Sion is believed to be more difficult for an amorphous alloy with a more dense random packing structure. The difficulty gives rise to the suppression of nucleation and growth of their equilibrium compounds in the amor- phous solid and supercooled liquid, leading to the ap- pearance of the wide supercooled liquid region as well as the large glass-forming ability (the wide glass-formation range). 2. Difference in the compositional effect on Tg and Tx between Y and Ni or Cu It is shown in Figs. 3 to 6 that the Tg and Tx values in- crease almost linearly with an increase of Y content while the changes of Tg and Tx as a function of Ni or Cu con- tent show a minimum in the vicinity of 15%Ni or Cu. We discuss the reason for the significantly different composi- tional dependence between Y and Ni or Cu elements. Considering that the melting temperature of Mg-rich (M=Y, Ni or Cu) compounds decreases in the order of Mg2Y > Mg2Ni> Mg2Cu and the minimum solute concentration for the formation of Mg-rich com- pounds is about 150/0 Y and 33%Ni or Cu, the attractive bonding force between Mg and Y, Ni or Cu atoms seems to be the largest for the Mg-Y pair, followed by the Mg- Ni and then Mg��Cu pairs. Consequently, the attractive bonding force of the Mg-Y pairs, which is considerably stronger than that of the Mg-Ni and Mg-Cu pairs, is thought to result in the monotonuous increase in Tg and Tx with an increase of Y content. These amorphous alloys exhibit a wide supercooled liquid region before crystallization and the Tg and Tx increase monotonuously from 398 to 568 K and 413 to 598 K, re- spectively, with increasing Y content while the changes of Tg and Tx with Ni or Cu content show a minimum at about 15% corresponding to the eutectic composition. In addition, the amorphous alloys containing more than about 700/0Mg have good bending ductility and the Of, E and Hv are in the range of 610 to 850 MPa, 40 to 61 GPa and 193 to 237, respec- tively.
