ABSTRACT
Molybdenum (Mo) is an important strengthening element in refractory high-entropy alloys. However, it is
difficult to produce a Mo containing alloy with uniform elemental distribution because of the high melting point
of Mo. This work employed 3D printing, also known as laser additive manufacturing (LAM), technique to prepare
TiZrVNbAl-system refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) with different Mo contents. Due to the high temper-
ature and strong convection in melting pool, uniform elemental distribution is achieved in LAM TiZrVNbAl-
system RHEAs. With increasing Mo, the solid-liquid intervals of alloys increase, which promotes the formation
of fine equiaxed growth, and reducing in the mean grain sizes. Meanwhile, a large difference in the atomic radius
between the Mo and the TiZrVNbAl RHEA results in strong solid-solution strengthening, and hence a 34 % in-
crease in the yield strength. These results imply that the LAM is a promising technique to produce RHEAs with
uniform elemental distribution, as well as good strength ductility balance, which is expected to be able to widen
the potential applications these refractory alloys.
1. Introduction
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are multi-principal elements
alloys, which have been extensively studied due to excellent high tem-
perature softening resistance and high strength [ 1 – 4 ]. The alloy is pri-
marily composed of high melting point elements from IV, V and VI
groups (Ti, Zr, V, Nb, W, Mo, etc.). RHEAs are known to form
single-phase structures due to the high mixing entropy [ 1 , 5 – 8 ].
Lightweight TiZrVNbAl-system RHEAs are single phase BCC alloys
with excellent performance, the main strengthening mechanism is solid
solution strengthening [ 9 , 10 ]. However, the addition of Mo serves to
enhance the solid solution strengthening capabilities of RHEAs, mainly
due to the notable difference in atomic radius between Mo and
TiZrNb-based RHEAs [ 11 , 12 ]. Therefore, the addition of Mo can
improve the yield strength of the alloy [ 7 , 12 ]. Furthermore, the addition
of Mo expands constitutional supercooling zones in the solid-liquid
interface, leading to an increase the growth restriction factor [ 13 , 14 ].
The high concentrations of Mo contents often lead to a larger growth
restriction factor ( Q ). According to Interdependence Theory, increasing
the Q value can effectively promotes grain refinement [ 13 , 15 ]. There-
fore, the addition of Mo can not only facilitate the solid solution
strengthening effect in the RHEAs, but also lead to refinement of the
grain sizes.
However, RHEAs containing high melting point elements are diffi-
cult to obtain uniform distribution of elements thought melting tech-
nology [ 2 , 16 , 17 ]. For example, the RHEA containing the
high-melting-point Mo element, as reported by Zeng et al. while
demonstrating high yield strength, is susceptible to element segregation
during the casting process [ 12 ]. Laser additive manufacturing (LAM), as
a bottom-up preparation technique, not only has greater design freedom
and more efficient material utilization [ 18 – 21 ], but can also signifi-
cantly reduce post-processing and shorten production cycles [ 22 , 23 ].
More importantly, LAM, which employs a laser as a heat source and is
characterized by high melt pool temperatures and strong liquid con-
vection, is regarded as a promising technique for preparing refractory
alloys [ 24 – 27 ]. Xiao et al. [ 28 ] employed LAM to produce WMo-
TaNbNiTi RHEAs with uniformly distributed high-melting-point ele-
ments, including W, Mo, and Ta, within the grains. Furthermore, LAM
exhibits the distinctive attribute of coaxial powder feeding, which en-
ables the concurrent preparation of different alloys through mixing
powders in different proportions. This contrasts with the complex
approach of synthesizing multiple alloys through the use of melting

T50 RHEA and outperforming the majority of documented RHEAs. This

2. Materials and methods
T50-RHEA spherical powders with a size range of 45 μ m – 150 μ m
were prepared by electrode induction melting gas atomization (EIGA)
( Fig. 1 (a) ). Mo spherical powders with a purity of 99.99 % and range of
15 – 53 μ m, were employed ( Fig. 1 (b) ). The sphericity of T50 RHEA and
Mo powders was determined to be 0.957 ± 0.015 and 0.901 ± 0.018,
respectively, through the utilization of dynamic image analysis. Four
powders with different Mo contents were mixed in a centrifuge tube. The
compositions include T50, (T50)97-Mo3, (T50)94-Mo6, and (T50)91-
Mo9 RHEAs.
The T50-Mox RHEAs samples were fabricated using a 4 kW LAM
system (RC-LDM4030, RAYCHAM Laser Technology Group Co., Ltd)
with a 3 mm laser beam, operated in an Ar protective atmosphere with
an oxygen content of less than 50 ppm. The samples were deposited onto
a Ti6Al4V alloys by cyclic method, as illustrated in Fig. 2 (b) . The T50-
Mox RHEAs wall-like plates were prepared by the LAM system, with a
thickness of 5 mm, as depicted in Fig. 2 (c) . Based on the previous
research results [ 32 , 33 ], relevant LAM process parameters were
selected: laser power: 1400 W, scanning speed: 10 mm/s, and powder
feed rate ranging from 10 g/min to 12.5 g/min, a 3 mm beam diameter.
The metallographic sample is polished with SiC paper to particle size
of 5000 and subsequently with SiO

3.2. Tensile mechanical properties
To gain further insight into the role of Mo on the mechanical prop-
erties of T50 RHEA, uniaxial tensile tests were conducted at room
temperature, and the results are presented in Fig. 6 (a) . As the Mo
content increases, there is a notable enhancement in both the yield
strength and ductility. In particular, the yield strength was increased
from 850 MPa for the base Mo0 alloy to 1095 MPa for the Mo6 alloy. It is
noteworthy that the ductility also increased from 22 % for the base Mo0
alloy to approximately 28 % for the Mo6 alloy. This is primarily
attributable to the addition of Mo element, which enhances the strain-
hardening rate of Mo6 alloy ( Fig. 6 (b) ), prevents premature fracture,
and considerably improves its ductility. Consequently, the addition of
Mo not only enhances the yield strength but also improves its ductility of
alloys. It should be noted that the ductility was found to decrease with
an increase in Mo9 alloy. The observed reduction in elongation of the
Mo9 alloy is in accordance with the findings of numerous studies on
single-phase BCC structure RHEAs, predominantly due to the higher
valence electron concentration (VEC) of Mo in comparison to the other
constituent elements [ 39 , 40 ]. The addition of an excess of Mo markedly
elevates the VEC of the alloy, intensifies interatomic bonding forces, and
diminishes the intrinsic plasticity of the alloy [ 41 ]. Fig. 7 illustrates that
the mechanical properties (yield strength, ductility) of the Mo6 alloy are
superior to those of the previously reported RHEAs system.

4.2. The origin of excellent ductility
In comparison to the majority of BCC RHEAs, which sacrifice
ductility to enhance yield strength, the addition of an optimal quantity
of Mo in this work not only enhanced yield strength but also markedly
improved ductility. In order to elucidate this anomalous phenomenon, a
preliminary examination was conducted on the deformation mechanism
at the grain scale. Fig. 11 (a, b, c) illustrate the three-color EBSD

the primary factors influencing the deformation mechanism are grain
size and the formation of kink bands. As shown in Fig. 11 (a, b, c) ,
lenticular structures were observed in all three alloys following defor-
mation. The crystal disorientations of these structures are random, yet
the range of crystal disorientation is approximately between 20

4.3. Strengthening mechanism
In order to reveal the strengthening effect caused by changes in Mo
content, this section analyses the strengthening mechanism of the
RHEAs. According to extant reports [ 18 ], the principal strengthening
mechanisms of the RHEAs comprise solid solution strengthening, grain
refinement strengthening and dislocation strengthening. Furthermore,
the presence of short-range ordering has been identified as a significant
strengthening mechanism in RHEAs, with the capacity to enhance the
yield strength of the RHEAs [ 31 ]. However, when the RHEAs composi-
tion change, the contribution of short-range order to the yield strength
of the RHEA is much smaller than that of solid solution strengthening
[ 71 ]. Furthermore, short-range order is formed through deformation
and low-temperature aging, while the RHEAs have not undergone any
post-treatment, so the effect of short-range order can be ignored.
Consequently, the yield strength of RHEAs is predominantly determined
by solid solution strengthening, grain refinement strengthening, and
dislocation strengthening. The following Eq. (2) can be utilized to esti-

5. Conclusions
This study utilized LAM to prepare Mo-containing TiZrVNbAl-system
RHEA. The main conclusions are as follows.
1. Due to the high temperature and strong convection in the melting
pool. The T50-Mo RHEAs retain a single-phase BCC structure without
precipitation and the high-melting-point Mo is distributed uniformly
in the alloys.
2. With increasing Mo content, the constitutional supercooling zone
ahead of S-L interface increases, promoting the transformation from
columnar growth to equiaxed growth, and hence reducing the grain
sizes.
3. The occurrence of kink behavior in RHEAs facilitates the coordinated
deformation behavior of the RHEAs. Furthermore, an increase in the
Mo content results in a reduction in grain size, which facilitates the
coordinated deformation of the RHEAs (from 22 % to 28 %).
4. The large difference in atomic size between Mo and T50-RHEA,
which achieved strong solid solution strengthening, resulting in a
34 % increase in the yield strength.
It is inevitable that the addition of Mo element in excess will result in
some Mo particles remaining insoluble in the alloy. Particle strength-
ening is an effective method for improving yield strength of RHEAs, but
there is relatively little research in this area. Therefore, it is suggested
that the future research direction is to enhance RHEA with refractory
element particles. The primary objective of this research is to elucidate
the interface relationship between molybdenum particles and the ma-
trix, and to ascertain the hindering effect of molybdenum on dislocations
during deformation.
