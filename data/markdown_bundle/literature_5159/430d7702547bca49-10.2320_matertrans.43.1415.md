Effect of W Alloying and NbC Dispersion on High Temperature Strength at 1773 K
and Room Temperature Fracture Toughness in Nb5Si3/Nb In-situ Composites
Won-Yong Kim1, Hisao Tanaka1 and Shuji Hanada2
1Japan Ultra-High Temperature Materials Research Institute(JUTEM), Ube 755-0001, Japan
2Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, Japan
The effects of W alloying and NbC dispersion on high temperature strength at 1773 K and room temperature fracture toughness are inves-
tigated using Nbss/Nb5Si3 and Nbss/Nb5Si3/NbC in-situ composites with the hypoeutectic composition, where Nbss denotes Nb solid solution.
With increasing W content, 0.2% offset yield stress for both composites increases at 1773 K. The increase in the yield strength is attributable
to the solid solution strengthening in Nb
ss by adding W, low diffusivity of W in Nbss and microstructural change caused by W addition. With
increasing W content, fracture toughness decreases in the Nb ss/Nb5Si3, but no marked change is observed in the Nb ss/Nb5Si3/NbC. A crack
propagates straightly in Nb–16Si–5Mo–15W , while crack deﬂection and branching take place in the Nb ss/Nb5Si3/NbC. Fractography reveals
that Nbss/Nb5Si3 fractures completely in a transgranular mode, while Nb ss/Nb5Si3/NbC fractured in a mixed mode of transgranular fracture
and interface decohesion between NbC and Nb ss or Nb5Si3 and Nbss. It is suggested that the presence of NbC dispersoids in Nb ss/Nb5Si3 is
effective to increase high temperature strength without a signiﬁcant decrease of fracture toughness.

Keywords: niobium-base intermetallic composites, fracture toughness, high temperature strength, solid solution strengthening, crack
propagation, carbides
1. Introduction
In recent years, because of excellent high temperature
strength and low density, Nb-base refractory intermetallic al-
loys have been extensively studied as one of promising candi-
date materials to be used at temperature above 1473 K, which
is maximum available temperature for commercial Ni-base
superalloys.
1–4) Like many other intermetallic alloys, these al-
loys are extremely brittle at room temperature due to com-
plex crystal structure and anisotropy in inter-atomic bond-
ing nature compared to metals and alloys.
5) Nb–Si alloy sys-
tem has a wide two phase region consisting of Nb ss and
Nb5Si3 intermetallic phase. Nbss has bcc structure up to melt-
ing temperature and Nb 5Si3 undergoes phase transformation
from α-Nb5Si3 to β-Nb5Si3 above 2273 K at the stoichiom-
etry composition. 6) Nb–Si binary phase diagram possesses
eutectic and eutectoid reactions, this provides ways to con-
trol microstructure for increasing room temperature fracture
toughness of the brittle silicide.
7–14) In addition, high tem-
perature strength will be achieved by the silicide in equilib-
rium with Nbss up to high temperature. However, incorpora-
tion of ductile Nb ss phase normally decreases the high tem-
perature strength of the in-situ composite since Nb ss phase
is remarkably weakened at elevated temperature.4) Therefore,
one of the major issues in developing Nb–Si base intermetal-
lic alloys as high temperature structural materials is to im-
prove room temperature fracture resistance without sacriﬁc-
ing high temperature strength through incorporation of a duc-
tile Nb
ss phase. One possible way to meet such requirement
is ternary alloying. Recently, we have reported that Mo al-
loying is effective in improving both the fracture toughness
and the high temperature strength in Nb
ss/Nb5Si3 in-situ com-
posites.7, 8, 15) In the present work, we have further studied
the effect of W alloying and carbide dispersion on the high
temperature strength at 1773 K and room temperature frac-
ture toughness of Nb–16 at%Si–5 at%Mo (hereafter denoted
as Nb–16Si–5Mo) in order to understand the relationship be-
tween strength and fracture toughness. W was selected as an
alloying element to strengthen Nb
ss, and Hf and C are cho-
sen as alloying elements to form a highly stable carbide in
the Nb
ss/Nb5Si3 composites. The high temperature strength
at 1773 K and room temperature fracture toughness in the
present alloys have been presented and the obtained results
will be discussed by considering microstructural changes.
2. Experimental Procedure
Purities of starting raw materials were 99.9 mass%Nb,
99.9 mass%Mo, 99.9 mass%W, 99.99 mass%Si and
98 mass%Hf. Carbon was used in a form of Nb
2C with
99 mass% of purity. Alloy ingots were prepared by arc-
melting under an argon gas atmosphere on a water-cooled
copper hearth using a non-consumable tungsten electrode.
The ingots were then heat treated at 1973 K for 48 h, fol-
lowed by furnace cooling to room temperature. Samples
for metallographic observations were mounted and mechan-
ically polished with SiC paper and Al
2O3 particles with
water. Microstructural observation was carried out using
back-scattered electron image (BEI) in a scanning electron
microscope (SEM). Electron probe micro-analysis (EPMA)
equipped with a wavelength dispersive spectroscope (WDS)
was used in determining the chemical compositions of con-
stituent phases. Phase analysis was performed on ﬁnely pol-
ished bulk samples using an X-ray diffractometer (XRD). To
observe the crack propagation behavior, monotonic loading
was applied to notched bending specimens and then inter-
rupted as quickly as possible by detecting a sound signal
that allows us to recognize crack nucleation or propagation.
Compression specimens with 3 mm×3 mm cross-section and
6 mm height were mechanically polished. Compression tests

4. Conclusions
The effects of NbC dispersion and W alloying on high
temperature compressive strength at 1773 K and room tem-
perature fracture toughness have been investigated using in-
situ composites of Nb–16Si–5Mo–xW(Nb5Si3/Nbss) and Nb–
16Si–5Mo–xW–5(Hf+C) (Nb5Si3/Nbss/NbC). At 1773 K the
yield strength for both the alloys increases with increasing
content of W due to the solid solution strengthening of Nb ss
phase, low diffusivity of W in Nbss and microstructural tran-
sition. With increasing W content, Nb–16Si–5Mo– xWe x -
hibits a rapid decrease of room temperature fracture tough-
ness, but Nb–16Si–5Mo–xW–5(Hf + C) exhibits no marked
change of the toughness. This difference can be associated
with crack deﬂection and branching, which are observed only
in the NbC dispersed 5(Hf + C)-added Nb–16Si–5Mo–xW.
The Hf + C addition causes a change in fracture mode from
complete transgranular fracture to a mixed type consisting of
transgranular fracture and decohesion between NbC disper-
soids and Nb
ss phase or Nb5Si3 and Nbss.
