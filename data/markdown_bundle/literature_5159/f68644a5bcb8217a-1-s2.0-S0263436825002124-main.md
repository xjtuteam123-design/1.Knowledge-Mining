1. Introduction
The introduction of refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), also 
known as refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs), in 2010 [ 1 ] 
brought new possibilities for high-temperature applications of re -
fractory alloys, whose development stagnated for nearly forty years 
[ 2 , 3 ]. RCCAs, characterised by their multi-principal element composi -
tions [ 4 , 5 ], demonstrated exceptional strength up to 2000 
◦
C [ 6 – 10 ], 
tensile ductility down to 196 
◦
C [ 11 – 14 ], excellent oxidation resis -
tance up to 1500 
◦
C [ 15 – 18 ], cold workability [ 13 , 19 – 21 ], fracture 
toughness [ 13 , 22 – 24 ], or phase stability [ 8 , 25 ]. However, those RCCAs, 
which exhibit remarkable strength or oxidation resistance, suffer from 
poor ductility, even under uniaxial compression [ 6 , 26 ]. For instance, the 
NbMoTaW and NbMoTaVW alloys having single-phase body-centered 
cubic (bcc) structures show one of the best thermal softening resistances 
among currently reported RCCAs, with the yield strength exceeding 400 
MPa at 1600 
◦
C, while demonstrating limited (below 3 %) room- 
temperature plasticity [ 6 ]. Conversely, other alloys with better form -
ability fail to achieve the necessary high-temperature strength 
[ 13 , 27 , 28 ]. To this group belong the Hf-Nb-Ta-Ti-Zr-based RCCAs, with 
the most well-known example of the single-phase bcc HfNbTaTiZr alloy, 
which demonstrates exceptional cold workability [ 19 ] and good 
cryogenic-to-room-temperature tensile ductility [ 14 , 19 , 29 ] but poor 
creep resistance, uncompetitive to conventional nickel-based superal -
loys [ 27 ]. This dichotomy presents a significant hurdle in developing 
RCCAs with balanced strength-ductility synergy [ 4 ].
The creation of bcc + B2 structures, similar to Fe-based [ 30 , 31 ] or 
Ni/Co-based superalloys [ 32 , 33 ], is considered to be a promising 
pathway to develop high-performance RCCAs [ 34 ]. Still, unlike other 
precipitation-strengthened alloys, the alloying principles, synthesis, and 
processing methods for RCCAs are not established. Most reported bcc +
B2 RCCAs demonstrate relatively low ( < 1100 
◦
C) solvus temperature of 
the B2 phase and/or phase instability [ 35 – 42 ]. Meanwhile, certain bcc 
+ B2 RCCAs, in which the solvus of the B2 phase exceeds 1200

The presented data indicate the need for further research to develop 
more advanced bcc + B2 RCCAs that demand solving a number of issues. 
One of the most important aspects is to find the “ base ” composition that 
would provide reasonable tensile plasticity at room temperature – one of 
the vital characteristics for structural applications [ 50 ]. Though 
numerous bcc + B2 RCCAs have been already designed to satisfy this 
criterion [ 51 – 56 ], sporadic studies showed their rapid softening at T >
800 
◦

simultaneously sustain higher temperatures and remain high form -
ability, is a non-trivial task considering the chemical complexity of 
RCCAs.
In this study, we focus on searching for the “ base ” RCCAs composi -
tion with a single bcc phase structure combining good cold workability, 
tensile ductility, and strength in a wide temperature interval. Despite the 
abundance of works devoted to designing single-phase bcc RCCAs, the 
majority of them focused on checking the validity of proposed design 
principles by subsequent evaluation of the structure [ 58 , 59 ] and/or 
room-temperature properties [ 60 – 65 ]. Mechanical performance at 
elevated temperatures, while being estimated and claimed to be 
exceptional in some cases, was reported mainly under compression 
[ 66 – 68 ], which could not be further reproduced under tension [ 69 ]. 
Moreover, to the best authors ’ knowledge, the information about the 
response of such RCCAs to high-temperature loading in the air, imitating 
the critical in-service conditions when the failure of a protective coating 
can occur, is absent. Therefore the goals of our work were manifold: (i) 
propose and rationalise the design criteria of single-phase bcc RCCAs; 
(ii) systematically investigate the effect of chemical composition varia -
tions on cold workability and tensile properties; (iii) explore the defor -
mation and fracture behaviour in the air for the range of temperatures. 
In a broad sense, this is a beginning of a survey devoted to the devel -
opment of new bcc + B2 RCCAs with a balanced set of properties.
2. Alloy design
Designing of strong RCCAs with high formability requires an inte -
grated approach aimed at finding compositions that meet several re -
quirements simultaneously. This should be a bcc composition with a 
wide single-phase field and a high melting point (over 2000 
◦
C) to 
ensure the absence of undesirable phase transformations, which inevi -
tably degrade the mechanical properties, as confirmed for the Hf-Nb-Ta- 
Ti-Zr alloy system [ 29 , 70 – 74 ], and the operating temperature at the 
level of 0.6T
m 
(at least 1200 
◦
C), when thermal activation processes 
begin to actively occur [ 75 , 76 ]. At the same time, this composition 
should have a relatively low density, since such a parameter is extremely 
important for moving parts, for example, in gas turbine engines. 
Considering the criteria mentioned, we selected the Nb-Mo-Ta-V alloy 
system as a starting point. Due to the close radii of the constituent 
element atoms and identical crystal structures, it is possible to obtain in 
this system a single-phase bcc solid solution with high strength at 
elevated temperatures [ 77 ] and tensile ductility at room temperature 
[ 78 ]. Meanwhile, to ensure adequate ductility and competitive density, 
the content of the most ductile and relatively lightweight Nb should be 
maintained at a minimum of 60 – 70 at.% [ 13 , 21 , 79 – 81 ]. Alloying with 
Mo and V is necessary for solid solution hardening [ 82 – 86 ]; however, 
preliminary studies indicate that the Mo content should not exceed 6 at. 
% to ensure cold workability. Addition of Ta is expected to increase the 
operation temperature by elevating T
m 
without loss of formability.
