Abstract 
In order to investigate the influence of Co on creep deformation anisotropy, some Ni-base single crystal superalloys have been 
creep tested at 800°C and 735 MPa. It is found that. although Co-containing alloys have higher creep resistance when the tensile 
direction is within 5 ° of ~100). they are much more sensitive to tensile orientation than Co-free alloys, which have similar creep 
rupture lives for tensile orientations within 25 ° of (i00). It is revealed that for Co-containing alloys the { i 11 } (I 12) slip system 
is operative during first-stage creep, when heterogeneous deformation occurs. Also, the degree of heterogeneous deformation 
correlates very well with creep rupture lives. It may thus be concluded that Co is not essential in improving creep rupture 
properties of Ni-base superalloys. © 1997 Elsevier Science S.A. 
Keywords: Superalloys: Ni-base single crystal superalloys; Creep deformation anisotropy 
1. Introduction 
Ni-base single crystaI superalloys have unique creep 
rupture characteristics as a function of temperature. 
Several research investigations have revealed that at 
high temperatures over 1000°C, the formation of a 
rafted structure normal to the tensile axis plays a 
dominant role in improving creep properties [1]. This 
rafted structure is formed by the coarsening of ?,' 
precipitates due to the high diffusion rate and by the 
combination of internal and external strain due to the 
difference of Young's moduli between the two phases. 
The creep behaviour at this temperature level is thus 
much less sensitive to tensile orientation and alloys with 
a large negative misfit, which easily form the rafted 
structure, have excellent creep properties at high tem- 
peratures [2]. However, at intermediate temperatures 
between 700 and 850°C, the shape of the ~,' phase does 
not change drastically: thus, the creep behaviour is 
sensitive to the size and shape of ' precipitates, tensile 
strength of ?'/?" both phases and tensile orientation. 
Several studies have shown [3,4] that at intermediate 
temperatures, the {111}(112) slip system is operative

The effect of heat treatment on the precipitate size and

In this study, therefore, the role of Co on creep defor-

the main differences in creep behaviour between these 
two kinds of alloys.

developed at the National Research Institute for Metals 
(NRIM), Japan, have been creep tested at 800°C and 
735 MPa. The creep testing specimens have a 30 mm 
gauge length and a 6 mm diameter. Prior to the creep 
test, a two-step heat treatment was conducted for the 
alloys so that the size of the :~,' precipitates was con-

The reason for the higher steady-state creep resis-

Although Co-containing alloys have slightly higher 
creep rupture lives than Co-free alloys in this study, the 
former are much more sensitive to the tensile orienta- 
tion. In practice, the complex shape of turbine blades 
generates many different tensile orientations; therefore, 
alloys which retain creep resistance for wider tensile 
orientation may be advantageous. It is thus concluded 
that Co is not always essential in improving creep 
rupture properties. For example, Co-free alloy TMS 63, 
developed at NRIM, has excellent creep properties at 
1040°C [2]. To design alloys for practical use, it is 
therefore very important to determine the appropriate 
amount of Co, so that the alloys have both less tensile 
orientation sensitivity and high creep resistance at inter- 
mediate temperatures. 
4. Conclusions 
Creep tests of Co-containing alloys and co-free alloys 
have been conducted and the following results have 
been obtained; 
1. Co-containing alloys are very sensitive to tensile 
orientation. The {111}(112) slip system is operative 
during first-stage creep when inhomogeneous deforma- 
tion occurs. 
2. For Co-free alloys, no distinctive inhomogeneous 
deformations have been observed as long as the tensile 
direction is within 25 ° of {100}. The a/2(l10) type 
matrix dislocations may be propagated during the early 
stages of creep, which results in the homogeneous de- 
formation. These alloys are thus less sensitive to tensile 
orientation. 
3. Although Co-containing alloys generally have 
slightly higher creep resistance at intermediate tempera- 
tures, Co is not always essential for practical use, since 
strict orientation control would be required for fabrica- 
tion. It might be beneficial to develop Co-free alloys 
with high temperature capabilities such as TMS 63.
