Abstract: The powder-metallurgy molybdenum alloys containing @l-1.0 
mass% Ti were carburized in two different conditions, lower and higher carbon 
levels. Tensile tests were performed at temperatures from 77 to 363 K and then 
two parameters, critical stress ( CI,) and temperature (T,), were evaluated. By 
using the relationship between a, and PIF (percent intergranular fracture) the 
apparent intergranular and transgranular strengths were estimated. Then the 
effects of complex additions of Ti and carbon on the low temperature fracture 
strength and ductility were discussed from the viewpoints of the degree of 
interaction between Ti and carbon. Additionally, the optimum carburizing 
condition for improving the low temperature fracture characteristics of Mo-Ti 
alloys was determined. 
The results are summarized as follows: 
(1) MO alloys having O.l-0*2%Ti and lower carbon and MO alloy having 
l*O%Ti and higher carbon indicated the highest a,. These results are due 
to the apparently additive effects of Ti and carbon on the intergranular 
strength. Also they suggest that the amounts of effective carbon and Ti at 
the grain boundaries are still enough for intergranular strengthening. 
(2) The low temperature ductility of Mo-Ti alloys was primarily a linear 
function of a, which corresponds to the intergranular strength. Thereby 
the alloys indicating the highest a, demonstrated the lowest T, or the 
highest ( 1 IT,). 
1 INTRODUCTION 
In our recent study, lv2 the low temperature tensile 
behavior of powder-metallurgy Mo-Ti alloys was 
investigated. Addition of Ti up to 1.0 mass% 
increased the low temperature fracture strength 
and lowered the ductile-to-brittle transition tem- 
perature (DBTT ). By considering that the grain 
sizes are different between alloys and some extent 
of the added Ti forms oxides (TO,), this result 
was interpreted as follows. A small amount of Ti 
in solution increased both the intergranular and 
transgranular strengths and consequently the 
DBTT was lowered, that is the low temperature 
ductility was improved. 
In this study the tensile tests at temperatures 
77-363 K were performed for Mo-Ti alloys 
which had been fully recrystallized and carburized 
in two different conditions. Then the low tem- 
perature fracture characteristics of the carburized 
Mo-Ti alloys +ere discussed from the viewpoints 
of the degree of interaction between Ti and 
carbon. For Mo-Re and Mo-Re-C alloys it was 
demonstrated that the effects of complex addi- 
tions of Re and carbon on the low temperature 
fracture strength of molybdenum were additive.3 
That is, the effect of carbon on the fracture 
strength was constant irrespective of Re content, 
though the effect of carbon was more remarkable 
than that of Re. This result was reasonable, since

2 EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES 
Materials used in this work were powder-metal- 
lurgy molybdenum alloys containing 0.1, O-2, 0.5 
and 1-O mass% Ti which had been supplied by 
Tokyo Tungsten Co., Ltd. In this paper these 
materials were designated as ‘O*l%Ti’, ‘0*2%Ti’, 
‘0*5%Ti’ and ‘l*O%Ti’ alloys. For the reference 
pure molybdenum was also used and designated 
as ‘MO’. Target and residual contents of Ti and 
contents of oxygen and carbon in the alloys are 
summarized in Table 1. In the recrystallized alloys 
the carbon contents were less than 10 ppm 
(referred to as ‘no’ carbon or ‘no’ carbon level) 
and as low as that in MO. 
Tensile specimens with gauge sizes of 
4 mm x 14 mm were cut from sheets of about 
1 mm thickness. The surfaces of the specimens 
were mechanically polished and then heated in a 
vacuum of about 9.3 X lop4 Pa at 1773 K for 
3.6 ks to complete the recrystallization. Average 
grain sizes of the alloys after recrystallization 
tended to decrease from 0.020 mm for MO to 
0.0 13 mm for l*O%Ti alloy.’ The specimens were 
then subjected to carburizing in two different con- 
ditions. First, carbon was vaporized on the 
surfaces of the recrystallized specimens and then 
the specimens were again heated at 1773 K for 
1.2 ks (referred as ‘lower’ carbon or ‘lower’ 
carbon level) or at 1883 K for 1.2 ks (referred to 
as ‘higher’ carbon or ‘higher’ carbon level). Finally 
the specimens were mechanically and electrolytic- 
ally polished in order to remove the carbide layer 
at the surface. The carbon contents in the speci- 
mens after carburizing are also listed in Table 1. 
Here it should be noted that the contents of 
oxygen and Ti and the average grain size in each 
alloy are almost unchanged before and after 
carburizing. 
Tensile tests were performed at temperatures 
from 77 to 363 K at a nominal strain rate of 
1.2 x 10 - .i s - ’ by using an Instron-type testing 
machine. From the load-displacement curve at 
each temperature, yield and fracture (or tensile) 
strengths and total elongation were calculated. 
The fracture surfaces of the specimens failing at 
77 K and/or 173 K were examined by using the 
scanning electron microscope (SEM) and the frac- 
ture mode represented by the parameter, PIF 
(percent intergranular fracture), was determined. 
PIF is the percentage of the intergranular fracture 
area for the total area. For example, when the 
fracture mode is completely intergranular, PIF is 
100% and when the fracture mode is completely 
transgranular, in contrast, PIF is 0%.

The degree of the interaction between Ti and 
carbon was deduced qualitatively as follows. By 
considering the results of Fig. 2 (a, = hi:,,) and Fig.

the grain boundaries and/or in the matrices of the 
alloys can be estimated in the following four cases. 
4.1 The alloy with lower Ti and lower carbon 
The ai, of the carburized alloy was much higher 
than that of the recrystallized alloy and even 
higher, slightly, than that of MO having lower 
carbon. This result suggests that there is a com- 
plex effect of Ti and carbon on the fracture 
strength. It is reported9 that even a small addition 
of carbon is enough for the segregation of carbon 
of several percent to the grain boundaries, which 
is much higher than the Ti content. Thereby it is 
deduced that only a partial extent of carbon inter- 
acted with Ti and there still remained a favorable 
amount of carbon and Ti in order to enhance the 
ai,* 
On the other hand, a,, of the carburized alloy 
was also as high as that of the recrystallized alloy. 
It is easily supposed that only a partial extent of 
carbon might interact with Ti also in the matrices, 
if it occurred there. By considering that a,, of MO 
having lower carbon is almost same as that of the 
recrystallized MO, it is reasonably interpreted that 
the carburized alloy indicated the ots as high as 
that of the recrystallized alloy. 
4.2 The alloy with lower Ti and higher carbon 
The Ois for the carburized alloy was higher than 
that for the recrystallized alloy, but not higher 
than that for the carburized MO. This result sug- 
gests that there was only the effect of carbon on 
the intergranular strengthening. Higher carbon 
means that a higher level of carbon existed at the 
grain boundaries, and hence there still remained 
enough carbon to enhance the yip. However, the 
extent of Ti which interacted with carbon might 
increase, and hence the amount of effective Ti was 
decreased and its favorable effects on the inter- 
granular strengthening was reduced. 
On the other hand, a,, of the carburized alloy 
was much lower than that for the recrystallized 
alloy or the alloy having lower carbon. This result 
is reasonably interpreted by considering that the 
a,, of MO having higher carbon is lower than that 
of MO having lower carbon or no carbon8 and that 
there still remained a sufficient amount of carbon 
in the specimen. 
4.3 The alloy with higher Ti and lower carbon 
The ai, for the carburized alloy was not higher 
than that for the carburized MO. This result sug- 
gests that alternatively the amount of effective 
carbon at the grain boundaries or the amount of 
effective Ti at the grain boundaries is not enough 
for the intergranular strengthening. However, it is 
more likely that the level of Ti at the grain bound- 
aries of the alloy having higher Ti is higher than 
that of the alloy having lower Ti, and hence the 
amount of effective carbon was relatively reduced. 
On the other hand, D,~ for the carburized alloy 
was as high as that for the recrystallized alloy or 
the carburized alloy having lower Ti. This result 
suggests that the amount of carbon in the matrices 
is low and the amount of Ti is enough. 
4.4 The alloy with higher Ti and higher carbon 
The ai, for the carburized alloy was as high as that 
of the alloy having lower Ti and lower carbon. 
This result suggests that there is apparently an 
additive effect of Ti and carbon. It is easily sup- 
posed that more extent of carbon and more extent 
of Ti interacted with each other, but there 
remained still favorable extent of carbon and Ti at 
the grain boundaries for the intergranular streng- 
thening. 
On the other hand, a,, is as high as that for the 
recrystallized alloy or the alloy having lower 
carbon. This result suggests that the amount of 
carbon in the matrices was reduced and con- 
sequently came to be as favorable as that for the 
alloy having lower carbon or no carbon, whilst the 
amount of Ti in the matrices was still enough.

5 SUMMARY 
The effects of carburizing on the low temperature 
fracture strength and ductility of Mo-Ti alloys are 
summarized as follows. 
For the alloy having lower Ti (e.g. less than 
0*2O/,), at lower carbon level, the effects of Ti and 
carbon on the intergranular strength were appar- 
ently additive. At higher carbon level, however, 
only the carbon effect on the intergranular 
strength was demonstrated. The transgranular 
strength was almost unchanged by adding lower 
carbon, whilst the transgranular strength 
decreased substantially by adding higher carbon 
in a manner similar to MO. 
For the alloy having higher Ti (e.g. l*O%), at 
lower carbon level, the favorable effect of carbon 
on the intergranular strength was reduced. At 
higher carbon level, however, the effects of Ti and 
carbon on the intergranular strength were appar- 
ently additive. The transgranular strength of the 
carburized alloys was almost as high as that of the 
recrystallized ones. 
The low temperature ductility of Mo-Ti alloys 
was primarily a function of the critical stress, 
which corresponds to the intergranular strength, 
regardless of carbon level. 
The Mo-O*l%Ti and 0*2%Ti alloys having 
lower carbon, and MO-l*O%Ti alloy having 
higher carbon demonstrated the most favorable 
fracture strength and ductility at low tempera- 
tures.
