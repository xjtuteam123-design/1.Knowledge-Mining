4. Discussion
4.1. Discontinuity of the strain and stress elds
As shown in Fig. 1(a), the eii strain component is discontinuous on the particles
that are perpendicular to the xi direction. Also, Fig. 1(a) shows that the eij strain
component diverges in the cone formed by the faces perpendicular to the xi and xj
directions. In general, it was found that the perpendicular total strain is discontin-
uous at the 
{
0 interface. On the other hand, Figs. 1(a) and 1(b) show that the
strain inside the particle becomes even greater than the eigenstrain. Recent exper-
imental works5,18{21 show that these strain characteristics are present on Ni-based
superalloys.
4.2. Self- and interaction elastic energies
The model allows one to evaluate the strain energy for any precipitates' cong-
uration. In this work, the elastic self-energy was investigated as a function of the
aspect ratio for a parallelepiped particle. It was found that the cubic shape becomes
the energetically more favorable particle geometry. Similar calculations carried out
using the Eshelby equivalent inclusion method for ellipsoidal particles have found
that the disc shape is the geometry with minimum self-energy.11,12 Therefore the
present result for the cubic geometrical shape agrees with experimental observations
in which the cubic geometry of 
 0-particles has been broadly observed in Ni-based
superalloys. Table 2 shows the elastic self-energy per particle unit volume reported
by T. Mori et al.,11 and J. C. Chang and S. M. Allen,12 and those obtained in
this work. The data have been obtained from Fig. 3 of this present work, Fig. 4 of
Ref. 11 and Fig. 1 of Ref. 12. Since each author considers dierent sets of elastic
coecients Cijkl and eigenstrain ", the comparison is limited. In order to com-
pare the self-energy of parallelepiped and ellipsoidal particles, further calculations
considering the present model are required.28

In the case of the interaction energy between two cubic particles, an attractive
interaction along h100i directions and repulsive interaction along h110i directions
were found. The calculations reported here can be used in the analysis of symmet-
ric arrays of two, four, and eight 
 0-particles. Such symmetric arrays have been
observed during the coarsening of Ni-based superalloys, and they have usually been
interpreted as a result of strain-induced particle splitting.29{31 However, Calderon
et al.32 have proposed an alternative mechanism consisting of particle migration
and coalescence. In accordance with present investigation, such migration and co-
alescence processes would be driven by the elastic energy in two steps. In the rst
step, particles are aligned along h100i directions in order to optimize its interaction
energy. In the second step, a particle group tries to coalescence, forming a single
cubic particle. The coalescence process would be inhibited by the short-range re-
pulsive potential which arises from dierent crystallographic ordered domain of the

0-particles.32
4.3. Interaction energy between constitutive particle elements

the same eigenstrain. Thus, the particle self-energy is the total interaction energy

arbitrarily small particles and, therefore, can be considered as the interaction energy
between particle constitutive elements. In this work, the interaction energy between
a source point particle and a probe point particle was investigated as a function of
the relative orientation (Fig. 5).
