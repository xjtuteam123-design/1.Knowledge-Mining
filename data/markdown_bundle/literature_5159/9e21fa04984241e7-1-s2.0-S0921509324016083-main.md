ABSTRACT
Refractory multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs) show promise for high-temperature applications due to their 
superior high temperature strength. To date, the metallurgy community has dedicated considerable efforts to 
developing RMPEAs with strength-ductility synergy at ambient temperature. In this study, the strength-ductility 
trade-off was achieved in NbTa
x
TiV alloys by adjusting the Ta content. The effects of Ta on phase formation, 
microstructure, lattice distortion, and plastic mechanisms in the alloys were systematically studied by combining 
experiments and computations. The results reveal that all designed alloys exhibit excellent tensile fracture 
plasticity and rolling formability. The addition of Ta enhances the alloys ’ elastic moduli, yield strength, hardness 
and tensile fracture elongation, while reducing lattice distortion and elastic anisotropy. The high yield strength 
primarily originates from solid solution strengthening, and the excellent plasticity is due to the mobility of a /2 < 1 
1 1 > -type dislocations and the formation of {1 1 2} < 1 1 1 > twins.
1. Introduction
Refractory multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs), comprising 
three or more high-melting IVB ~ VIB group elements, exhibit superior 
performance compared to traditional alloy, such as high strength, good 
thermal stability, wear resistance, and radiation tolerance at elevated 
temperatures [ 1 , 2 ]. The excellent properties of RMPEAs endow them 
with broad application prospects in aerospace, nuclear engineering, 
weaponry, hydrogen energy fields, thus garnering significant interest 
among researchers [ 3 , 4 ]. In 2010, Senkov et al. first reported two 
RMPEAs, MoNbTaW and MoNbTaVW, which exhibit body-centered 
cubic (BCC) structures [ 5 ]. The MoNbTaW alloy has compressive yield 
strength of 1058 MPa at room temperature (RT), which remains at 405 
MPa even at 1600 
◦
C [ 6 ]. The MoNbTaWV alloy demonstrates high 
compression yield strength 1246 MPa at RT and 477 MPa at 1600 
◦
C, 
surpassing that of most commercial high-temperature alloys, such as 
Inconel 718 and Haynes 230 alloys [ 6 ]. The CrMoNbV alloy displays 
high yield strength 1518 MPa at RT, maintaining 1000 MPa at 1273 K 
[ 7 ]. These early alloys demonstrated high strength but suffered from 
poor ductility at RT. Such as, the compression fracture strain of MoN -
bTaW, MoNbTaWV, and CrMoNbV alloys is just 2.6 %, 1.7 %, and 5 % at 
RT, respectively [ 6 , 7 ].
Subsequently, significant efforts have been devoted to balance 
strength and ductility in RMPEAs by adjusting the composition of 
RMPEAs [ 3 ]. For instance, Senkov et al., in 2015 fabricated a HfNbTa -
TiZr alloy with a single-phase BCC structure and excellent cold work -
ability, which demonstrated a significant reduction in thickness (RIT) of 
up to 86.4 % through cold rolling [ 8 ]. The 86.4 % rolled HfNbTaTiZr 
alloy has a tensile strength of 1295 MPa and plasticity of 4.7 % [ 8 ]. The 
HfNbTaTiZr alloy retains a single-phase structure after rolled 89 % RIT

2. Methods
2.1. Alloy preparation, cold rolling and mechanical tests
Alloy ingots with nominal compositions of NbTa
x
TiV ( x = 0.0, 0.5, 
1.0, 1.5 in relative molar ratio) were fabricated using pure Ti, V, Nb, and 
Ta metals (purity ~99.95 wt%) in a non-consumable vacuum arc 
melting furnace (HVAM-B, China) under a high-purity argon atmo -
sphere. All ingots were subjected to a minimum of six flips and 
remeltings to guarantee chemical homogeneity. The dimensions of the 
as-cast ingots were approximately 25 mm in diameter and 7 mm in 
thickness. The as-cast ingots were rolled into 1 mm-thick sheets using a 
double-high rolling mill with a rolling speed of 150 mm s
 1 
(JH-RZH- 
110, China) at room temperature. Subsequent cold rolling was per -
formed in a multi-pass rolling process with consistent directionality and 
a per-pass deformation of 0.01 mm, culminating in plates approximately 
1 mm in thickness. Multiple dog-bone-shaped samples were extracted 
from the cold-rolled sheets along the rolling direction using an electrical 
discharge machine (DK77, China) for subsequent tensile tests. The ten -
sile samples had dimensions of approximately 16 mm in gauge length, 4 
mm in gauge width, and 1 mm in thickness. The tensile samples were 
encapsulated within a quartz tube under a vacuum pressure of 0.005 Pa, 
followed by annealing at 1200 
◦
C for 45 min to recrystallization and 
relieving residual stress. After annealing, the samples were quenched in 
water to preserve the solid solution phase. Subsequently, the annealed 
samples were polished prior to tensile testing. Tensile tests were con -
ducted at a strain rate of 1 × 10
 3 
s
 1 
at room temperature. Each alloy 
underwent at least three repeated tensile tests for consistency and reli -
ability. The microhardness of the alloys was assessed using a Vickers 
hardness tester (MHV-1000, China) with a 0.3 kgf load applied for 15 s. 
Each sample underwent more than ten hardness tests to ensure statis -
tical reliability. The dynamic elastic modulus and Poisson ’ s ratio of the 
alloys were determined using an ultrasonic pulse-echo measurement 
system (Teclab UMS-100 system, France). Note that, the UMS testing, 
assuming material isotropy, cannot directly determine single-crystal 
elastic constants from polycrystals, potentially overlooking anisotropic 
properties. The mass density of the alloys was measured utilizing the 
Archimedes drainage method (JHY-300W, China) in accordance with 
the international standard ASTM B962-17 .

samples were polished using a broad argon ion beam system (Gatan 685 
PECS II, USA) for approximately 2 h at 7 kV voltage prior to analysis.

4.2. Lattice distortion
Previous investigations have shown that the mixing of multiple 
elements causes atoms to deviate from ideal lattice sites, resulting in 
local lattice distortions within RMPEAs [ 1 ]. Local lattice distortions act 
as barriers to dislocation motion, enhancing solid solution strength -
ening, crucial for maintaining the alloy ’ s high strength and ductility 
[ 14 ]. Moreover, lattice distortions hinder atomic diffusion and alter 
lattice vibrational modes, affecting the alloys ’ thermodynamic proper -
ties [ 13 ]. For traditional alloys, solute atoms can be regarded as “ im -
purities ” embedded within a “ matrix ” of solvent atoms to derive lattice 
distortions. However, for RMPEAs, there are no clear distinction be -
tween solutes and solvents. Accurately quantifying lattice distortions 
caused by different atomic sizes necessitates precise atomic-level sim -
ulations and measurements, posing challenges to traditional methodol -
ogies. Hence, describing and quantifying lattice distortions remain

4.3. Plastic deformation mechanisms

activated plastic mechanisms. Unlike face-centered cubic counterparts, 
there is limited experimental evidence of the underlying plastic mech -

dislocation slip [ 1 ]. The plastic deformation of HfNbTaTiZr alloy is

5. Conclusion
In this work, a series of novel BCC single-phase solid solution 
NbTa
x
TiV RMPEAs with excellent tensile plasticity and cold-rolling 
formability were fabricated successfully by vacuum arc melting. The 
effect of Ta on their phase formation, microstructure, lattice distortion, 
elastic properties and plastic deformation mechanisms were systemati -
cally investigated by integrating experimental and computational ap -
proaches. The results reveal that all designed NbTa
x
TiV alloys exhibit 
excellent ambient ductility, because their tensile fracture plastic strain 
exceeding 10 % and rolling RIT exceeding 80 % at room temperature. 
The addition of Ta appropriately benefits in enhancing the alloys ’ elastic 
moduli, yield strength, hardness, and tensile fracture elongation, while 
also aiding in reducing lattice distortion and elastic anisotropy. The high 
yield strength of the alloys primarily originates from the contribution of 
solid solution strengthening induced by misfits in elastic moduli and 
atom sizes of multiple elements. The excellent ambient plasticity of the 
alloys stems from the movability of a/ 2 < 1 1 1 > type dislocations and the 
formation of {1 1 2} < 1 1 1 > twins. Lattice distortion arises from dif -
ferences in valence electrons and electron affinities among multiple el -
ements. Among these elements, Ti exhibits the most significant impact 
on lattice distortion. The decrease in lattice distortion results from the 
relative decrease in Ti content induced by Ta addition. This study not 
only achieved a strength-ductility trade-off in NbTa
x
TiV alloys by 
adjusting the Ta content, but also provided a distinctive paradigm for 
designing novel RMPEAs.
