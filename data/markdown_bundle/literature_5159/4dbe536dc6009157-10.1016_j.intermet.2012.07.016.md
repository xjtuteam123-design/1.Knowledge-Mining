abstract
A novel high Nb, low Al contained TiAl alloy Ti-43Al-6Nb-1B (at %) was fabricated by induction skull
melting (ISM) technique. The as-cast alloy exhibits fully lamellar (FL) microstructure with mean colony
size of 90 mm. By means of thermophysical simulation, the hot deformation mechanism at 1250 /C14 Ci s
concluded as dislocation slip and twinning in g phase; bending, rotating and elongation of lamellar
colonies are the primary deformation modes. A large-size TiAl pancake was successfully produced by
triple-step canned forging at 1250 /C14 C. The forged alloy displays duplex (DP) microstructure consisting of
elongated lamellar colonies and g grains. Typical DP and FL microstructures were further obtained by
different heat treatments. The tensile strength increases rapidly from 670 MPa in as-cast state to 975 MPa
in as-forged condition at room temperature, with elongation increasing from 0.4% to 1.5%. The as-forged
alloy maintains strength higher than 950 MPa until 750
/C14 C; also the DP and FL materials hold excellent
strength more than 830 MPa and 770 MPa respectively. The superior tensile properties after forging are
mainly ascribed to the microstructure re ﬁnement and homogenization, the lamellar spacing reducing, as
well as the low Al and relatively low Nb additions in this novel high Nb containing TiAl alloy.

1. Introduction
High Nb containing TiAl alloys, termed TNB, have attracted
considerable attention in recent years, due to their superior high
temperature strength and excellent oxidation resistance [1e3].
Many valuable works have been done on TNB alloys, including
oxidation resistance [4], microstructure characteristics [5e7],
relationship between microstructure and properties [3,8,9] and hot
workability [9e11 ]etc. The generally studied alloy compositions of
TNB are Ti-(45 e46)Al-(6e10) Nb-(Y, C, B) [1e11 ]. However, the
strength of TNB alloys is not only determined by solution hardening
of Nb, but also depends on the Al content, i.e. lamellar spacing and
the ratio of
a2 phase [3,12]. “High Nb and lean Al ” seem to be
generally accepted design criteria for TNB alloys. It has been re-
ported by Y.W. Kim et al. [8], that overall strength of Ti-45Al-5Nb
sheet alloy is roughly the same as that of Ti-45Al binary alloy,
which is caused by drastic reduction of
a2 content in Ti-45Al-5Nb
alloy, resulting from the Nb-induced phase-boundary shift.
Lowering Al content increases
a2/g volume ratio and reduces the
lamellar spacing, thereby the alloy becomes harder. Paul and Appel
et al. [13], even deemed that the high strength was solely a result of
the reduced Al content and that Nb played nearly no role in
strengthening. Thus the strengthening of TNB alloys is a synergistic
effect of Nb and Al. Moreover, some problems such as micro-
segregation [6], introduction of low-temperature brittle B2 grains
[6,14], poor hot-workability [15] etc., will be brought about due to
excessively high Nb addition. Therefore, it is of signi ﬁcance to
further lower both the Nb and Al content in TNB alloy. By now, the
recrystallization behavior and the microstructure evolution of
a/g
lamellae, especially a laths, have rarely been examined in detail.
This might be due to the fact that a phase easily transforms into a2/
g lamellae during cooling. So that direct observation of recrystal-
lization in a phase is impossible.
Alloying [3,4,7,16], thermo-mechanical treatment [14], and heat
treatment [17] are effective means to optimize microstructure and
mechanical properties of TiAl alloys. The re ﬁnement effect of B in
TiAl alloy is strongly dependent on Al content and strong boride
formers like W, Ta and Nb etc., and 1 at% addition of B can effec-
tively reﬁne the microstructure of TNB alloys [16].
In this study, a new high Nb and low Al containing TiAl alloy Ti-
43Al-6Nb-1B (at %) was fabricated by induction skull melting (ISM)
method. Using triple-step canned forging process, a large-size
pancake of this alloy was produced. DP and FL microstructures
were obtained by subsequent heat treatments. Microstructure

2. Experimental
The Ti-43Al-6Nb-1B (at %) cast ingot (approximately
ø120 mm /C2 240 mm) was fabricated by induction skull melting
(ISM) technique. Then this ingot was hot isostatically pressed
(HIPed) at 1250 /C14 C for 4 h under a pressure of 150 MPa and was
annealed at 950 /C14 C for 48 h. Cylindrical billet with dimensions of
ø105 mm /C2 145 mm was cut from the ingot by electric-discharge
machining and the surface was ground. Then this alloy billet was
canned by 20 mm thick 304 steel and was sealed by vacuum
welding. Triple-step forging was performed on this canned billet at
1250
/C14 C and at 0.01 s /C0 1, with a total engineering strain of 80%. Prior
to each forging step, an intermediate annealing at 1250 /C14 C for 1 h
was used to ensure the quasi-isothermal forging condition. Finally,
one sound large-size pancake of this TNB alloy was produced, as
shown in Fig. 1. To eliminate residual stress, this forged pancake
was annealed at 950 /C14 C for 24 h.
To investigate hot deformation behavior and microstructure
evolution during hot deformation, thermophysical simulation
experiments were carried out on Gleeble 3500 machine. Cylindrical
specimens with dimensions of ø8mm /C2 12 mm were cut from the
HIPed ingot and the surfaces were ground. Then isothermal
compression tests were performed on these specimens at 1250
/C14 C
and 0.01 s /C0 1. Prior to compressing, a 5-min holding was accom-
plished at 1250 /C14 C. After being compressed to the design strain
respectively, specimens were water quenched immediately to
preserve the as-deformed microstructure.
The microstructures were characterized by scanning electron
microscopy (SEM), transmission electron microscope (TEM) and
electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) techniques. Both TEM and
EBSD specimens were cut close to the core of forged pancake or the
isothermally-compressed samples, then were prepared by standard
mechanical polishing and twin-jet electropolishing using a solution
of 6% perchloric acid þ 34% butanol þ 60% methanol at /C0 20
/C14 C and
25 V. Quanta 200FEG was used for SEM observation. The mean
grain/colony size in cast microstructure was measured by line
interception method. TEM observations were performed on Tecnai
G2 F30. EBSD measurements were implemented on Quanta 200FEG
equipped with EBSD system using scanning intervals of 0.2
mm, the
collected data was processed with TSL OIM 5.31 Analysis software.
Flat tensile test specimens with gauge section of
20 mm /C2 6m m /C2 2 mm were cut from the central area of the
forged pancake. All the tensile tests were conducted on Instron
Electron-Mechanical Universal Testing Machine, driven at cross-
head speed of 0.5 mm min /C0 1. The test temperatures were room
temperature, 700 /C14 C, 750 /C14 C, 800 /C14 C and 850 /C14 C.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. The as-cast microstructure
Fig. 2 shows the cast microstructure of Ti-43Al-6Nb-1B alloy. As
shown in Fig. 2a, current as-cast alloy consists of fully lamellar (FL)
microstructure with mean colony size about 60 mm and several TiB
ribbons distributing within lamellar colonies. Some white-net
domains are clearly observed within lamellar colonies. According
to the research results on Ti-45Al-(8 e9)Nb-(W, B, Y) alloy by Chen
et al. [6], these white domains are caused by microsegregation of
hard-to-diffuse Nb. However, due to the relatively low Nb content
in Ti-43Al-6Nb-1B alloy, together with the advanced ISM melting
technique, no B2 grain was induced within lamellar colony by Nb
segregation. After HIP treatment at 1250
/C14 C for 4 h, both the
lamellar colony size and the lamellar spacing increased obviously.
As shown in Fig.2b, the mean colony size of the HIPed micro-
structure is about 120
mm, coarse g/a2 lamellar structures and ﬁne g
grains around lamellar colony boundaries can be clearly observed.
The TEM image in Fig. 2c shows the HIPed lamellar microstructure,
we can clearly ﬁnd that the lamellar colonies display much coarse
and ununiform lamellar spacing, changing in the range of
0.5 mme1.5 mm. Generally, the lamellar spacing reduces obviously
as the concentration of Al or Nb decreases in TiAl alloys, i.e. the
lamellar spacing reduces with the volume fraction of
a2 increasing
[3]. As a whole, the ﬁne-grained microstructure with ﬁne lamellar
spacing of as-cast Ti-43Al-6Nb-1B alloy is mainly caused by low Al
and low Nb content and re ﬁnement effect of B, while the coars-
ening of lamellar spacing and colony size is induced by HIP treat-
ment at 1250
/C14 C.
The bright-ﬁeld TEM photographs in Fig. 2d and e reveal the TiB
crystal and its SAD patterns respectively. According to analysis of
SAD patterns, the TiB crystal in current alloy has an orthorhombic
B27 structure with lattice parameters of a ¼ 0.612 nm, b ¼ 0.305 nm
and c ¼ 0.456 nm.
3.2. Hot deformation behavior of current alloy
Generally, the hot working of TiAl alloys are performed in (a þ g)
phase region, that is, above the eutectoid temperature, which lies in
a range of 1 100 /C14 Ce1200 /C14 C depending on the alloying additions
[18,19]. Above eutectoid temperature, the a2 phase disorders into
a phase, and the volume fraction of disordered a phase strongly
increases with temperature. Additionally, the width of a lamellae
can be expected to increase as well, as the HIPed microstructure
shown in Fig.2b.

ted to investigate the dynamic recrystallization behavior and
microstructure evolution of Ti-43Al-6Nb-1B alloy at 1250
/C14 C. Fig. 3
shows the water-quenched deformed microstructure after being
compressed to 30% and 60% strain respectively. As shown in Fig.3a,
at 30% compression strain, elongation, rotation and bending of
lamellar colonies arise; it seems that the lamellar structures trend
to switch to hard orientation, with lamellar interfaces perpendic-
ular to the compression direction. However, no obvious dynamic

contrast, all the DRX grains, large or small, exhibit neither LAGBs (or

that the stored strain energy is the driving force for recrystallization
and hence it is to be expected that the dislocation density is
reduced by the recrystallization process. This is the reason why the
DRX areas exhibit low lattice distortion or dislocation density.

detailed results of microstructure characteristics and tensile prop-

shown, the UTS of current alloy is strongly microstructure depen-
dent; at room temperature (RT), the UTS is 670 MPa in the as-cast

alloy maintains much high UTS not less than 950 MPa until 750 /C14 C,

/C14 C, the UTS values of all

to the above microstructure investigation and tensile properties,
three factors can account for the superior properties of this alloy
after canned forging. The ﬁrst, also the main factor is low Al and low
Nb content in this TNB alloy, which is beneﬁ cial to increasing the
ratio of a2 phase and reducing the lamellar spacing. Second, the
reﬁnement and homogenization of microstructure is obtained by
forging, as well as the breakdown of TiB particles. The third is the
reﬁnement of lamellar spacing after forging. It is generally accepted
that increasing the ratio of a2 phase and reducing the lamellar
spacing are favorable to strengthening TiAl alloys [3,8,25].

4. Conclusions
High Nb g-TiAl alloys are attractive due to their excellent high
temperature properties. Based on the study on microstructure
evolution, tensile properties and hot-deformation behavior of Ti-
43Al-6Nb-1B alloy, the mainly conclusions are summarized below.
(1) The high-temperature deformation mechanism is mainly
dislocation slip and twinning of g phase. The deformation at
1250 /C14 C is mainly caused by bending, rotating and elongation of
lamellar structure. Dynamic recrystallization preferentially
proceeds at lamellar boundary areas, resulting in hard-
orientated lamellar structures and recrystallized
g grains.
(2) It is feasible to fabricate large-size Ti-43Al-6Nb-1B alloy
pancake via triple-step canned forging at 1250 /C14 C. After forging,
the FL microstructure of as-cast state converts into DP structure
consisting of elongated lamellar colonies and
g grains. Both the
microstructure and lamellar spacing are re ﬁned.
(3) Hot forging improves the tensile properties signi ﬁcantly, due to
the microstructure re ﬁnement and homogenization, as well as
lamellar spacing decreasing; the as-forged alloy maintains
tensile strength higher than 950 MPa up to 750
/C14 C. Both the as-
