EFFECT OF Nb ADDITION ON GLASS-FORMING ABILITY, STRENGTH, AND

ABSTRACT
Supercooled liquid region ( DTx), tensile fracture strength ( sf), and Vickers
hardness (Hv) of Fe–B–Zr–Nb amorphous alloys were measured. The addi-
tion of Nb to the ternary Fe–B–Zr system was found to lead to signiﬁcant
changes in glass-forming ability (GFA), strength, and hardness. The results
are discussed in the context of the three empirical rules for achievement of
high GFA metallic glass and the change in packing density of the amorphous
phases.

INTRODUCTION
The search for an amorphous alloy with high glass-forming ability (GFA) is of great
importance in the research ﬁeld of amorphous metallic materials. All high GFA amorphous
alloys in Ln–Al–TM [1], Mg–Ln–TM [2], Zr–Al–Ti [3], Zr–(Ti, Nb, Pd)–Al–TM [4],
Zr–Ti–TM–Be [5], Pd–Cu–Ni–P [6], and Fe–(Al, Ga)–(P, C, B, Si) [7] (Ln 5 lanthanide
metal, TM 5 transition metal) system exhibit a wide supercooled liquid region, which
implies high thermal stability of the supercooled liquid against crystallization. High thermal

The glass-forming ability of amorphous alloys can be characterized by the critical cooling
rate Rc. But it is usually difﬁcult to measure R c. Thus, several parameters have been used to
predict the glass-forming ability of a metallic glass. The most often used parameter is the
supercooled liquid region DT
x (5 Tx 2 Tg, where T g is the glass transition and T x is the
crystallization onset temperature of the metallic glass). The Inoue group [3,8,9] has reported
a strong correlation between DT
x and GFA of easy glass-forming alloys. In fact, there is
evidence [10] that DTx may be used to represent the GFA for easy glass-forming metallic
alloys.
We have recently reported [11] that the addition of Zr to the binary Fe–B system results
in the formation of metallic glass with an obvious supercooled liquid region. We also have
concluded [11] that Fe
70B20Zr10 has the best GFA among the Fe 802xB20Zrx (x 5 0 –15)
amorphous alloys. The present study investigated the effect of Nb addition on the ternary
Fe–B–Zr system. In this paper, the GFA of Fe
70B20Zr102xNbx (x 5 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10)
amorphous alloys as determined by their supercooled liquid region DTx is reported. The
relationship of GFA to tensile fracture strength ( sf) and Vickers hardness (Hv) from a
structural point of view is also discussed.
EXPERIMENTAL
Multicomponent alloys with composition Fe 70B20Zr102xNbx (x 5 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10) were
examined in this study because of the wide supercooled liquid regions of these amorphous
alloys before crystallization. Master ingots were prepared by arc melting a mixture of pure
Fe, Zr, and Nb metals and pure B crystal in a water-cooled copper boat under an argon
atmosphere. The purity of the elements ranged from 99.5 to 99.9%. The alloy composition
represents the nominal atomic percentage of the mixture. Rapidly solidiﬁed ribbons with a
cross section of 0.015 3 1.0 mm
2 were prepared by single-roller melt-spinning the master
ingots in an argon atmosphere.
The amorphous structure was examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD) and transmission
electron microscopy (TEM). Thermal stability associated with glass transition and super-
cooled liquid region was examined by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a heating
rate of 0.67 K/s. Tensile fracture strength was measured at a strain rate of 5.0 3 10
21 s21,
using an Instron testing machine. Vickers hardness was examined at a load of 100 g, using
an Akashi hardness tester.

and XRD data clearly indicated that the melt-spun alloys were composed of an amorphous

It is generally believed that the higher the packing density, the higher the thermal stability
and mechanical strength of an amorphous phase, the higher the resistance of the supercooled
liquid against transformation into crystalline phases [3]. The higher packing density found in
the ternary Fe–B–Zr amorphous solid than in the binary Fe–B amorphous alloy can be
explained by difference in atomic size ratio [11]. In the quaternary Fe–B–Zr–Nb system with
an appropriate amount of Nb addition, the atomic size changes more continuously in the
order of Zr . Nb .. Fe .. B with the addition of Nb, which may further increase the
packing density to a larger value than those of the ternary alloys. Therefore, both GFA and
mechanical strength increase with increasing packing density from ternary system to qua-
ternary system with appropriate amount of Nb. The coincidence that both DT
x and mechan-
ical strength varied with Nb addition with the same tendency has veriﬁed the statement above
on packing density of amorphous phase [3].
The reason for the increase in GFA with the addition of 2– 4% Nb can be discussed in the
framework of the three empirical rules [9,12,13] for achievement of large glass-forming
ability: (1) multicomponent alloy systems consisting of more than three elements, (2)
signiﬁcantly different atomic size ratios above about 12% among the main constituent
elements, and (3) negative heats of mixing among the elements. The present study is based

on these three empirical rules. The base composition in these alloys is the Fe–B–Zr system,
which satisﬁes the three rules. The appropriate addition of Nb (2– 4 at%) to the Fe–B–Zr
system is effective for the increase in the degree of satisfaction of the three empirical rules.
That is, the addition of Nb causes the more sequential change in the atomic size, as well as
the generation of new atomic pairs (Nb–Fe, Nb–B) with various negative heats of mixing. In
the amorphous phase, in which the three empirical rules are satisﬁed at a high level, the
topological and chemical short-range orderings are enhanced, leading to the formation of a
highly dense random packed structure, which results in high thermal stability of the super-
cooled liquid against crystallization as well as high resistance to mechanical failure.
The predicted heat of mixing, however, is 23 kJ/mol atom for the Nb–Fe pair and 79
kJ/mol atom for the Nb–B pair [14]. The heats of mixing for the two atomic pairs containing
Nb are considerably smaller than those for the Zr–Fe, Zr–B pairs containing Zr (37 kJ/mol
atom for Zr–Fe pair, 102 kJ/mol atom for Zr–B pair [14]). The weaker bonding nature of the
Nb-contained pairs, as compared with the Zr-contained pairs, seems to result in the decrease
in DT
x for the alloys containing more than 4 at% Nb (Nb6, Nb8, Nb10 alloys). This change
indicates the importance of the attractive bonding nature among the constituent elements for
the achievement of the wide supercooled liquid region before crystallization, in addition to
the signiﬁcantly different atomic size ratios. The decrease in DT
x for the alloys containing
more than 4 at% Nb (Nb6, Nb8, Nb10 alloys) implies the decrease in packing density of these
alloys, which leads to lower tensile fracture strength and Vickers hardness. The relative more
amount (more than 4 at%) of Nb, of which the atomic size is smaller than that of Zr and the
bonding nature is weaker than that of Zr, results in decrease in the degree of satisfaction of
the three empirical rules. On the other hand, compared with Fe
80B20 amorphous alloy (with
no glass transition and relative low sf of 2120 MPa), which does not satisfy the three rules
[11], the Nb10 alloy that contains no Zr element still has DTx and larger sf due to its
satisfaction to the three empirical rules.

strength of 3440 and 3490 MPa, which largely exceeds that for high-carbon high-alloy tool

of high mechanical strength combined with high glass-forming ability allow us to expect that
these two alloys will be used as good engineering materials.
CONCLUSIONS
The close relation of the packing density of Fe
70B20Zr102xNbx (x 5 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10)
amorphous alloys with glass-forming ability as well as mechanical strength of these alloys
was conﬁrmed through the measurement of supercooled liquid region, tensile fracture
strength, and Vickers hardness of the alloys. The alloys all form metallic glass. The
Fe
70B20Zr8Nb2 and Fe70B20Zr6Nb4 alloys have very wide supercooled liquid regions before
crystallization of over 90 K; they are larger than those of any other Fe-based metallic glass
found to date. These alloys all exhibit high tensile fracture strength of more than 2400 MPa,
especially the Fe
70B20Zr8Nb2 and Fe 70B20Zr6Nb4 alloys reach 3440 and 3490 MPa. The
addition of appropriate amount (2– 4 at%) of Nb to the Fe–B–Zr system leads to the increase
of mechanical strength due to the satisfaction of the three empirical rules for achievement of
high GFA metallic glass and increase of packing density of the amorphous phases.
