EFFECT OF CARBON CONTENT ON THE SHORT-TERM 
STRENGTH AND PLASTICITY OF PELLETIZED HIGH- 
TEMPERATURE NICKEL ALLOYS 
E. N. Rudnitskii and N. V. Rogozenkova UDC 669.018.8:681.3.06 
Computer modeling of the mechanical properties, phase composition, and structural parameters of high- 
temperature nickel alloys has recently come into widespread use in their development. In our study, we examine 
the expediency of alloying pelletized high-temperature nickel alloys with carbon. The resulting regression 
curves of mechanical properties versus chemical composition were analyzed from the standpoint of the effect 
attained as a result of increasing the carbon content in three types of alloys, distinguished by the ratio of basic 
"y'-forming elements." aluminum, titanium, and niobium. 
A rather considerable amount of data on the influence exerted by chemical composition on the mechanical properties 
of cast nickel high-temperature alloys has been accumulated to date; this makes it possible to optimize their composition [1]. 
The mechanical properties of different compositions of pelletized nickel alloys produced under identical conditions are less well 
understood. 
Regression analysis is widely used in evaluating the influence exerted by chemical composition on the mechanical 
properties, phase composition, and structural characteristics of high-temperature nickel alloys. Its use is based on the fact that 
such parameters as the volume fraction of phase components, the lattice constants of the 3'- and 3/-phases, the critical points 
(solidus, the temperature of complete dissolution of the ,,/'-phase) under thermodynamic equilibrium are independent of the 
production technology and are universal for cast, wrought, and pelletized alloys. In contrast to these parameters, the mechanical 
properties are determined largely by the conditions under which the alloys are produced and heat-treated. 
Rudnitskii et al. [2] obtained regression curves of the mechanical properties of pelletized nickel alloys versus the 
content of 3/-forming elements (A1, Ti, Nb), and also certain hardeners of the 7-matrix (Mo, W) on the basis of a limited data 
base [1]. To date, the amount of data on experimental pelletized nickel-based alloys has increased; this makes it possible to 
evaluate the influence exerted by a greater amount of alloying elements [3, 4]. The carbon content in alloys recently 
investigated has been varied over a rather broad range (0.03-0.15% of the total mass); this enables us to make a rather reliable 
estimate of the influence exerted by this element on the mechanical properties. In our study, the effect of carbon content was 
determined by deriving regression curves of mechanical properties versus this parameter and the content of other alloying 
elements using a data base that had been expanded in comparison with [1]. 
To obtain adequately reliable curves, it is necessary to observe a number of conditions, which reduce the scatter of 
experimental results relative to the computational equations obtained (dispersion of the goodness of fit) and which eliminates 
outliers: 
1) identical production conditions for the preparation of specimens, the mechanical properties and composition of which 
are entered into the available data base; 
2) identical heat treatment; and, 
3) the absence of structural defects leading to a sharp reduction in mechanical properties. 
We investigated standard (I~I698MP, I~P741NP, I~P962P, and I~P975P) and experimental alloys (a total of 14 melts), 
the chemical composition and properties of which were entered into the available data base. All experimental blanks were 
produced by hot isostatic compaction (HIC) of an alloy powder (pellet) of the + 50-200/xm fraction prepared by dusting by the

In selecting the chemical composition of the specimens investigated and their heat-treatment regime, we also considered 
the possibility of such structural defects as carbide segregations along the pellet boundaries and carbide baskets along the grain 
boundaries, which are formed during aging, and took measures for their prevention. The alloys containing topologically dense- 
packed (TDP) phases, or inclined to their segregation, were not considered. 
The alloys entered into the available data base contained the following amounts of alloying elements: 2.3-6.0% of A1, 
1.1-3.6% of Ti, 1.8-3.9% of Nb, 1.4-8.6% W, 0-6.1% Mo, 0-18.4% Co, and, 0.02-0.15% C (see Table 1). The correlation 
analysis that we conducted indicated the presence of a pronounced relation between the aluminum and chromium contents in 
the alloys analyzed. This is explained by the following. The chromium content in low alloys tends to be held at the maximum 
level to achieve high heat resistance. The chromium content is reduced as the concentration of "),'-forming elements (primarily 
aluminum) is increased. In this connection, chromium was eliminated from the analysis that we conducted, not to mention that 
published data on its influence on the mechanical properties, phase composition, and structure of high-temperature nickel alloys, 
which are available at the present time, are contradictory.

It follows from consideration of the relationships obtained that in the concentration region analyzed, the ultimate rupture 
strength depends on the content of 3,'-forming elements; in that case, an increase in the content of any of these elements leads 
to an increase in %. An increase in the yield point occurs due to an increase in both the content of titanium and niobium, and 
also the content of carbon and molybdenum, which is a vigorous carbide-forming element. The latter fact may most likely be 
governed by hardening of high-temperature alloys with a type of MC carbides (based on Mo for the case in question). As 
follows from the regression equations for 6 and 'I,,, carbide hardening exerts a negative influence on the plasticity 
characteristics. Moreover, the relative elongation increases appreciably with increasing product Ti.Nb -- a parameter largely 
responsible for the level of strength characteristics. 
The analysis that we conducted indicates that the level of impact strength is determined largely by carbon content. The 
niobium content that enters int6 the regression equation probably reflects the role of this alloying element as a 3,'-forming 
element (it is known that the alloys with the maximum niobium content -- the domestic alloy t~P962P and the foreign alloy 
Rene 95 -- exhibit minimum impact strength among the pelletized high-temperature nickel alloys). It is impossible, however, 
to negate the reduction in impact strength as a result of the embrittling effect of the niobium-containing carbides, not to mention 
the fact that this also suggests the very form of the regression equation for a 1. 
To ascertain the feasibility of alloying pelletized nickel alloys containing the maximum amount of alloying elements 
with carbon, let us examine the variation in mechanical properties for three basic groups of high-temperature alloys 
distinguished by ratio of content of 3,'-forming elements. 
High-Strength Alloys. As we have already indicated, these alloys (Rene 95 and I~P962P) are characterized by 
maximum niobium content (3.5%) with a relatively low aluminum content and ratio AI/Ti = 1.4. Let us consider the case of 
the maximum possible alloying of these alloys: 4% of A1, 3% of Ti, and 4% of Nb. A eutectic component is formed in this 
type of alloy with a greater amount of "F-forming elements; this leads to a pronounced reduction in plasticity and impact 
strength, and also to degredation of adaptability to manufacture.

The condition C.Mo < 0.37 (nonhatchured region in Fig. ld) should be met to retain a plasticity 6 > 15%. Superhigh-

strength was set equal to 35 .l/cm 2. In this connection, the maximum possible amount of carbon may be 0.12% for an Nb

fraction of the carbide phase effects a reduction in the content of carbide-forming elements in the -),-matrix; this ensures a 
lowering of the tendency to segregation of the embrittling TDP phases.

The analysis that we conducted indicates that the potential for alloying pelletized nickel alloys with carbon is determined 
largely by the ratio of concentrations of 3"-forming elements, and also by the short-term plasticity and impact strength that can 
be obtained as a result of alloying with basic alloying elements. 
The limiting level of alloying is achieved in all three types of pelletized nickel alloys under consideration, since the 
interval of homogeneity (temperature interval between termination of dissolution of the "),'-phase and solidus) is 20-40°C. The 
various types of alloys have different possibilities for their alloying with carbon, however, depending on the AI, Ti, and Nb 
contents. The maximum possible amount of carbon can be introduced to the superhigh-temperature alloys. It is virtually 
impossible to harden the high-strength pelletized alloys additionally by elevating the carbon content, since this will lead to a 
reduction in plasticity and impact strength. The alloys with elevated high-temperature strength, however, have additional 
potential with respect to alloying with carbon owing to their increased plasticity and impact strength; this allows us to permit 
a certain reduction in these properties as a result of an increase in the volume fraction of carbide phase. It should be pointed 
out that the results obtained are valid only for the case when the carbides are sufficiently uniformly distributed in the structure 
and do not form a carbide-basket type of defect along the grain boundaries, which, as we know, results in embrittlement. 
Regression curves of short-term strength (a u, %.2), plasticity 05, 't'), and impact strength versus content of basic 
alloying elements, including carbon, are therefore obtained on the basis of a data base on the chemical composition and 
mechanical properties of pelletized high-temperature nickel alloys prepared and heat-treated under identical conditions. It is 
established that an increase in carbon content in the concentration region under consideration gives rise to an increase in yield 
point and to a reduction in plasticity characteristics and impact strength most likely as a result of an increase in the volume 
fraction of carbides of the NbC and MoC type. 
The analysis that we conducted relative to the feasibility of improving the strength properties and high-temperature 
strength of three types of alloys containing the maximum amount of alloying elements without altering significantly the amounts 
of other elements indicated that maxinaum carbon content (-0.1%) may be introduced to superhigh-temperature alloys (type 
l~P975P and VZhI37), which are characterized by maximum aluminum content, high A1/Ti ratio, and low niobium content. 
The carbon content can be increased to a lesser degree (to 0.06-0.08%) in type AF-115 alloys, which are characterized by 
approximately the same niobium content, but a lower AI/Ti ratio. Additional carbide hardening of superhigh-temperature type 
t~P962P and Rene 95 alloys, which posses the maximum amount of niobium, is virtually impossible owing to their significant 
embrittlement.
