abstract
Although refractory high-entropy alloys have exceptional strength at high temperatures, they are often
brittle at room temperature. One exception is the HfNbTaTiZr alloy, which has a plasticity of over 50% at
room temperature. However, the strength of HfNbTaTiZr at high temperature is insuf ﬁcient. In this study,
the composition of HfNbTaTiZr is modi ﬁed with an aim to improve its strength at high temperature,
while retaining reasonable toughness at room temperature. Two new alloys with simple BCC structure,
HfMoTaTiZr and HfMoNbTaTiZr, were designed and synthesized. The results show that the yield
strengths of the new alloys are apparently higher than that of HfNbTaTiZr. Moreover, a fracture strain of
12% is successfully retained in the HfMoNbTaTiZr alloy at room temperature.

1. Introduction
In recent years there has been a pressing need to increase the
efﬁciency of gas turbine engines in the aerospace industry. This is
mainly imposed by the reduction of fuel consumption and green-
house gas emission. One of the most effective methods to increase
engine ef ﬁciency is to increase the operating temperature of the
engine. The prevailing engine component material is Ni-based su-
peralloys. Unfortunately, the incipient melting point of Ni clearly
sets a limitation to the operation temperature. Therefore, in
developing the next-generation gas turbines, a suf ﬁciently high
melting point becomes critical. Refractory metals, such as Nb and
Mo, have high melting points and high solubility for solid-solution-
strengthening elements, and therefore are considered as possible
candidates for such applications. For example, Nb eSi alloys have a
good combination of high-temperature strength and low-
temperature damage tolerance [1,2].M o eSieB alloys exhibit
outstanding creep strength and high-temperature strength [3e5].
A new alloy design strategy called high-entropy alloys (HEAs)
has drawn signi ﬁcant attention in the past decade [6e9]. These
alloys can have high hardness, good oxidation and corrosion
resistance, good structural stability, and excellent high temperature
strength [10e24]. These advantages make HEAs particularly
attractive for high-temperature applications. The combination of
the above lines of thinking gives good reasons to synthesize HEAs
using refractory elements e or refractory HEAs e as proposed by
Senkov et al. [24e29]. Refractory HEAs show very encouraging
properties. For example, the MoNbTaVW alloy has a remarkably
high yield strength of 477 MPa at 1600
/C14 C [24]. However, the main
problem of this alloy is its low toughness (fracture strain ¼ 1.7%) at
room temperature (RT) and its high density 12.36 g/cm 3. Another
refractory HEA, HfNbTaTiZr, having very different properties was
developed later [26]. It has a lower density (9.94 g/cm
3) and ex-
hibits high compression yield strength ( s0.2 ¼ 929 MPa) and very
high plasticity ( ε > 50%) at RT, but its yield strength at high tem-
peratures is not pleasing (yield strength < 100 MPa at 1200 /C14 C). In
this study, two new alloys are designed by modifying the compo-
sition of the HfNbTaTiZr alloy. The ﬁrst is HfMoTaTiZr, obtained by
replacing Nb with Mo. The improvement in mechanical properties

purpose of this research is to give a basis for further alloy and
process design in achieving even better properties.
2. Experimental procedures
The two refractory HEAs, HfMoTaTiZr and HfMoNbTaTiZr, were
prepared by vacuum arc melting in an argon atmosphere. The raw
materials had purities higher than 99.9 wt.%. Melting was repeated
for at least 5 times to improve the chemical homogeneity of the
alloy. Crystal structures of the alloys were examined with an X-ray
diffractometer (Shimadzu XRD-6000) operated at 30 kV and 20 mA.
The scanning rate is 4 /C14 /min. Vickers hardness was measured by
Mitutoyo HV-1 15 hardness testing machine with a load of 5 kg. The
duration time in each indentation was 15 s. Compression tests at RT,
800, 1000, and 1200 /C14 C were carried out using a Gleeble-3500
thermal-mechanical simulator. Tests were conducted under argon
atmosphere after purging with argon for three times. Cylindrical
samples for compression test were 3 mm in diameter and 6 mm in
height. The samples were cut by wire electrical discharge
machining and with the sample axis perpendicular to the bottom
surface of the button-shaped ingot. This is intended to let the so-
lidiﬁcation direction and thus columnar zone approximately be
parallel to the sample axis. In the test, the samples were resistance
heated to testing temperature at a heating rate of 20
/C14 C/s by ther-
mocouple feedback-controlled AC current and then holding
isothermally for 180s before deformation was initiated. The sam-
ples were tested at an initial stain rate of 10 /C0 3/s. Microstructures of
as-solidiﬁed and deformed samples were analyzed by using scan-
ning electron microscopes (SEM, JEOL JSM-5410 and JSM-6390).
The chemical compositions of these as-solidi ﬁed alloys were
analyzed by SEM energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS). The pre-
cipitates in deformed samples were examined with transmission
electron microscopy (TEM, JEOL JEM-2010).

3.3. Strength, fracture strain and fracture feature from 25 to
1200 /C14 C
The engineering stress estrain curves of HfMoTaTiZr and
HfMoNbTaTiZr obtained by compression test at various tempera-
tures are shown in Fig. 3. The yield strength and fracture strain are
taken from Fig. 3 and summarized in Table 3. The yield strength of
the HfMoTaTiZr alloy at RT is 1600 MPa. The alloy shows its peak
strength of 1743 MPa atε ¼ 3%, and subsequently fails at ε ¼ 4%. The
yield strength of the HfMoNbTaTiZr alloy at RT is 1512 MPa. The
peak strength of this alloy occurring at ε ¼ 1 1% is 1828 MPa. The
maximum compression strain is 12%. Thus, HfMoNbTaTiZr alloy
exhibits a signi ﬁcantly higher plasticity than HfMoTaTiZr alloy
does. Instead of inclining at ~45 /C14 to the compression direction, the
fracture surfaces of both alloys are parallel to the compression di-
rection. This suggests that the lateral tensile stress induced by
longitudinal loading is the cause for the mode-I fracture along
longitudinal direction. The HfMoNbTaTiZr alloy exhibits a signi ﬁ-
cantly higher plasticity than the HfMoTaTiZr alloy does.Fig. 4 shows
the fracture surfaces of the two alloys. The fracture surfaces of the
two alloys are similar and contain both cleavage and quasi-cleavage
features. Cleavage fracture is featured by either ﬂat surface or river
pattern whereas quasi-cleavage is featured by dense slip lines on
the cleavage surface. Compared with the fracture surface of
HfMoTaTiZr, less cleavage features are observed on the surface of
HfMoNbTaTiZr. In fact, in some areas features of dimpled-rupture
are observed ( Fig. 4 (c)). This means that HfMoNbTaTiZr alloy have
larger shear deformation and thus higher resistance to fracture
than HfMoTaTiZr alloy. Because the main difference between the
two alloys is the addition of Nb, it suggests that Nb can increase the
plasticity of refractory HEAs. However, the mechanism behind the
alloying effect of Nb is not discussed here.

3.4. Strength comparison: base alloy and typical superalloys
Fig. 5(a) compares the strengths of the two modi ﬁed alloys with
that of base alloy, Inconel 718 [36] and CMSX-4 [37]. The
compression tests of two modi ﬁed alloys and the based alloy were
at the same initial strain rate 10 /C0 3/s. However, the data of Inconel
718 and CMSX-4 were obtained by tension test at a strain rate of
0.005/min up to yield and then at another strain rate of 0.05/min
after yielding. Compared with the base alloy, in the as-solidi ﬁed
condition (with the same processing for the modi ﬁed alloys, also
marked as this work) or in the hot-isostatic pressed and vacuum
annealed condition [27], the strengths of two modi ﬁed alloys are
markedly higher. For example, the yield strengths of both alloys are
higher than that of the base alloy across the whole temperature
range covered in this study. At 1200 /C14 C, their yield strengths (404
and 556 MPa) are 4.3 and 6 times that of the base alloy. The speci ﬁc
yield strength (i.e., yield strength to density ratio) shown in
Fig. 5(b) also show the same trend e the modiﬁed alloys apparently
outperform the base alloy. Consequently, the addition of Mo is quite
effective in strengthening the refractory HEAs. Compared with
Inconel 718 and CMSX-4 ( Fig. 5 (a)), both modi ﬁed alloys have
higher yield strengths at all test temperatures except for CMSX-4 at
800 /C14 C. Moreover, the resistances to thermal softening for both
HEAs are higher than those of the superalloys. This is seen by
comparing the transition temperature. According to the theory of
Westbrook [38,39], there are two stages in strength etemperature
curves. At the ﬁrst stage (low temperature region), the softening is
through dislocation slip. At the second stage, the softening mech-
anism is related to diffusional phenomenon such as (1) slip along
more than one slip system due to increased atomic mobility, (2)
dislocation climb, and (3) grain boundary rotation. Indeed, the
transition temperatures between stage I and stage II for HfMoTaTiZr
(~1000 /C14 C) and HfMoNbTaTiZr (probably above 1200 /C14 C) are
markedly higher than those for CMSX-4 (800 /C14 C) and Inconel 718
(700 /C14 C). This indicates that addition of Mo apparently inhibit
diffusional plastic behavior and enhances the resistance to thermal
softening.
Due to the high strengths at high temperatures, the speci ﬁc
yield strengths of both modi ﬁed alloys at 1000 and 1200 /C14 Ca r e
also higher than those of the superalloys. Thus, both alloys have
great potential in high-temperature applications over 1000 /C14 C.
Because of its better combination of strength and plasticity,
HfMoNbTaTiZr is even better than HfMoTaTiZr. However, further
studies on creep properties are needed for applications that
require creep resistance.

HfMoTaTiZr alloy after compression test at different temperatures.
The loading direction is the vertical axis. The observation was made
on the central region of samples to avoid the constraint effect from
outer cold zones. At 800 /C14 C, (Ta, Mo)-rich dendrite (white regions)

after compression. Dendrites and interdendrites are generally
observed to elongate laterally. In some regions, however, the den-

loading direction, which are related to the formation of shear
bands.
Compression at 1000 /C14 C leads to signiﬁcant deformation. Almost
all grains are elongated laterally. In addition, high magni ﬁcation

4. Conclusions
Two new refractory HEAs, HfMoTaTiZr and HfMoNbTaTiZr, were
designed based on the HfNbTaTiZr alloy in order to improve the
overall properties. Several ﬁndings are drawn:
(1) Both alloys have simple BCC structure. The lattice constants
and densities of the two alloys are close to the values ob-
tained by rule-of-mixture. In contrast, the hardnesses of both
alloys are about three times higher than the rule-of-mixture
values. This suggests a strong solid-solution strengthening.
(2) The compression yield strengths of the HfMoTaTiZr alloy at
RT, 800, 1000, and 1200 /C14 C are 1600, 1045, 855, and 339 MPa,
respectively. The corresponding values for the HfMoNbTaTiZr
alloy are 1512, 1007 , 814, and 556 MPa, respectively.
