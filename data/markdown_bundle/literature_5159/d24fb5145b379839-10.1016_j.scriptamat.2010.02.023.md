Toughening and strengthening behavior of an Nb–8Si–20Ti–6Hf
alloy with addition of Cr
J. Sha, * C. Yang and J. Liu
School of Materials Science and Engineering, Beihang University, Xueyuan Road No. 37, Beijing 100191, China
Received 30 October 2009; revised 5 February 2010; accepted 10 February 2010
Available online 13 February 2010
The microstructure, fracture toughness and eﬀect of temperature on the strength of Nb–8Si–20Ti–6Hf–(6,10,14)Cr alloys were
studied. Fracture toughness was decreased by addition of Cr. It is interesting that, up to 1150 /C176C, the hardness or strength was
improved by addition of Cr, whereas above that temperature the strength decreased. As the Cr content increased from 6 to
14 at.%, the yield strength r0.2 increased from 268 to 313 MPa at 1150 /C176C, but decreased from 125 to 85 MPa at 1350 /C176C.

With a relatively high melting point of /C24 2740 K,
Nb is one of the most promising refractory metals for
future ultra-high-temperature structural materials [1–
3]. Based on the eutectic composition Nb–18Si in the
Nb–Si binary, two main systems Nb–W(Mo)–Si [4–7]
and Nb–Si–Cr–Ti–Hf–Al–Ge [8–11] have been devel-
oped so far. The Nb–W(Mo)–Si-based system aims to
improve strength at ultra-high temperatures. It was re-
ported that 0.2% yield strength at 1500 /C176C is quite high
at 365 MPa in Nb–10Mo–15 W–16Si–10Ti alloy with an
Nb
SS/Nb5Si3 microstructure, where Mo and W are the
most eﬃcient solid-solution hardening elements for Nb
[4–7]. Unfortunately, the Nb–W–Mo–Si-based system
has poor toughness of <10 MPa m
1/2 [7]. As for the mul-
ti-component Nb–Si–Cr–Ti–Hf–Al–Ge system [8–11],
Ti and Hf are toughening and ductilizing elements for
Nb; Si and (Si + Ge) concentrations of /C0 17% produced
the Nb
5(Si, Ge) 3 silicide, which is favorable for higher
strength at elevated temperatures; addition of Cr im-
proved the high-temperature oxidation resistance due
to formation of the Laves Cr
2Nb phase. Based on the
above-mentioned alloying principle, a series of alloys
including Nb–25Ti–8Hf–2Cr–2Al–16Si [11] and Nb–
silicide B, C, D alloys, with nominal compositions
given elsewhere [8–11], were investigated. For the direc-
tionally solidiﬁed Nb–25Ti–8Hf–2Cr–2Al–16Si sample,
a range of initiation toughness values between 18 and
23 MPa m 1/2 were obtained. The Nb–silicide C alloy
displayed outstanding compressive strength of /C24 310 MPa
at 1350 /C176C [9]. As for the oxidation resistance, a short-
term goal corresponding to a material thickness loss of
200 lm in 10 h air exposure at 1370 /C176C was successfully
achieved in the Nb–silicide B alloy with higher Cr con-
tents [8].
A review of the literature has shown that no compo-
sition alone can simultaneously meet the requirements
of the high-temperature structural materials in room-
temperature toughness, high-temperature strength and
oxidation resistance. In this work, two approaches for
improving the room-temperature toughness of the Nb–
Si-based alloys were employed. They are (1) reducing
Si content from 18 at.% (eutectic composition) to
8 at.%, i.e., decreasing the volume fraction of silicides,
and (2) doping a ﬁxed composition of Ti and Hf at 20
and 6 at.%, respectively, for toughening. A moderate
strength at room and high temperatures was supported
by addition of 8 at.% Si. Various Cr contents (6, 10,
14 at.%) were added in order to study the comprehensive
eﬀect of Cr on the toughening and strengthening
behavior of Nb–Si-based alloys, which is still unknown.
Investigations on the microstructural evolution and
mechanical properties of multi-component Nb–8Si–
20Ti–6Hf–(6,10,14)Cr alloys at room and high tempera-
tures were carried out in this work.

respectively) were prepared by arc-melting in an argon
atmosphere under a pressure of /C24 0.5 Pa. The purity of

Table 2 summarizes the average values of bulk HV,
the HV of the Nb SS phase and the fracture toughness
(KQ) of the annealed samples. The bulk HV, as well as
the HV of the Nb SS phase, increase monotonically as
the Cr content increases, but the KQ decreases. Solid-
solution hardening of Nb by Cr results in an increase
in the bulk HV. As for the KQ, two factors induce its
decreasing tendency as the Cr content increases: (1)
addition of Cr raises the brittle-to-ductile transition tem-
perature of the Nb SS phase [13], i.e., embrittles the Nb SS
phase; and (2) an increase in the volume fraction of the
brittle phases. A modiﬁed rule of mixtures (ROM) was
proposed to evaluate the bulk toughness of the Nb–Si-
based alloys. The ROM toughness is given by [14,15]:

Figure 3 reveals that the strengthening or weakening
behavior of the multi-phase Nb
SS/Nb3Si/Nb5Si3 or
NbSS/Nb3Si/Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructure is dependent
not only on Cr, but also on temperature. Two disadvan-
tages of the temperature capability of the Nb–Si-based
alloys appear when Cr is added. One is that Cr decreases
the liquidus line (i.e., melting point) of the Nb
SS phase;
the other is that the low melting-point Laves Cr 2Nb
phase (1770 /C176C) forms as the nominal Cr content is be-
yond the solubility of Cr in Nb [15]. With multi-solutes,
the Nb SS and Laves Cr 2Nb phases in this work may
have lower melting points than the binary Nb–Cr solid
solution and Laves Cr 2Nb. Earlier work found that
the 14Cr alloy began to melt at 1500 /C176C, but the 6Cr al-
loy was in the solid state even at 1600 /C176C [16].
In general, the strengthening and weakening mecha-
nisms coexist in the Nb–Si-based alloys with addition
of Cr. Which one is dominant for the bulk strength de-
pends on the testing temperature. It is believed that the
higher testing temperature (closer to the melting point of
the multi-components Laves Cr
2Nb phase or the Nb SS
phase with the higher Cr concentration) may be favor-
able for the weakening mechanism. At 1150 /C176C and be-
low, the solid-solution hardening of the Nb
SS phase and
the stiﬀening phase hardening of the silicides and Cr 2Nb
phase may exceed the weakening eﬃciency caused by
decreasing the liquidus line of the Nb SS by Cr, therefore,
the bulk strength shows an increasing tendency with Cr.
Above 1150 /C176C, the fact that the strength of the 10Cr
and 14Cr alloys decreases with temperature much faster
than that of the 6Cr alloy (see Table 3 and Fig. 3)
strongly suggests that the weakening behavior caused
by the above-mentioned two disadvantages absolutely
dominates the hardening behavior, resulting in a
decreasing tendency of the bulk strength with Cr. How-
ever, further work is still needed to determine the accu-
rate transformation temperature and mechanism from
strengthening to weakening of Nb–Si alloys with addi-
tion of Cr.
The present research investigated the microstructures,
toughness and strength response of Nb–8Si–20Ti–6Hf al-
loy with addition of Cr. At Cr contents of 6 and 10 at.%,
the microstructure consisted of Nb
SS,N b3Si and Nb5Si3,
while at a Cr content of 14 at.% it consisted of the
Nb
SS,N b 3Si, Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb. As the Cr content in-
creases from 6 to 14 at.%, the fracture toughness KQ de-
creases from 17.868 to 13.873 MPa m 1/2. The strength of
the Nb–8Si–22Ti–6Hf–(6,10,14)Cr alloys as a function of
Cr content is dependent on temperature. At 1150 /C176C and
room temperature, the strength increases with Cr content
whereas, at 1250 /C176C and 1350 /C176C, the strength decreases.
