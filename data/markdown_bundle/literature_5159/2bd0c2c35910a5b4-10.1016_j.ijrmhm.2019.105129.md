ABSTRACT
Designing a bimodal α-Mo grain structure in Mo-12Si-8.5B-ZrB 2 alloy was achieved via mechanical alloying
followed by hot pressing. This bimodal structure consisted of a major of nano-scaled α-Mo grains and partial
micron-scaled α-Mo grains. The eﬀ ective cooperation of these ﬁne and coarse α-Mo in the Mo-12Si-8.5B-ZrB 2
alloy was very bene ﬁcial for improving its toughness as well as strength. The high toughness was mainly at-
tributed to the in-situ toughening and crack trapping eﬀ ects originating from the coarse α-Mo. While the high
strength was owing much to the grain boundary strengthening of the ﬁne α-Mo. When the volume fraction ratio
of the ﬁne and coarse α-Mo grains was approximately 6:1, the fracture toughness value reached 13.1 MPa·m 1/2 in
the bimodal alloy, showing a 18% increase compared to an ultra ﬁne-grained alloy. Simultaneously, a high
compressive strength with 2998 MPa was also maintained.
1. Introduction
Mo-Si-B alloys consisting of α-Mo, Mo 3Si and Mo 5SiB2 (T2) have
received considerable attention as potential ultrahigh-temperature
structural materials owing to their high melting temperature, good
oxidation resistance, and excellent high-temperature creep strength
et al. in comparison with Ni-based superalloys [ 1–3]. However, there is
a trade-o ﬀ between these properties: an increase in ductility by im-
proving the α-Mo content is accompanied by a decrease in the oxidation
resistance and strength [ 4,5]. Mo-12Si-8.5B (at. %) alloys contain an
appropriate volume fraction of the intermetallics and metal α-Mo phase
showing a promising balance between the mechanical properties and
oxidation resistance [ 6–8]. The alloys possess enough Si and B con-
centrations to permit an excellent oxidation resistance around 1300 °C
[6]. The toughness achieved, however, remains inadequate. Careful
distribution control of the α-Mo phase in the Mo-Si-B alloys is expected
to be an important factor in improving their fracture resistance. Indeed,
at a given α-Mo volume fraction, the fracture toughness was improved
using the continuous α-Mo matrix rather than the intermetallic matrix
(with isolated α-Mo particles distribution), which is attributed to the
crack trapping by the continuous α-Mo [5,9,10]. Additionally, the alloy
with the coarse α-Mo grains shows a higher toughness than that with
the ﬁner ones due to the higher ductility of coarse α-Mo [ 11,12].
Nevertheless, the coarse α-Mo would result in a serious degradation in
the room temperature strength [ 12] and oxidation resistance [ 4,13]
since the coarse α-Mo is found to su ﬀer the serve evaporation as MoO
3.
Therefore, in order to maximize the toughening e ﬀect of the α-Mo
phase for the Mo-Si-B alloys and simultaneously balance their other
properties, it is desirable for the α-Mo to be distributing as a bimodal
structure (i.e. mixed coarse grain (CG) and ultra ﬁne grain (UFG)).
Researches about Ni, Mg, Cu et al. [ 14–16] have reported that the
bimodal grain size distribution, with the coarser grains embedded in-
side an ultra ﬁne grains matrix, results in a good combination of
strength and ductility. There, the coarse grain ameliorates the ductility
and the ﬁne grain supports the high strength. Like them, the design of
coarse α-Mo grains in Mo-Si-B alloys attempts to enhance the ductility
and fracture toughness of these alloys through in situ ductile-phase
toughening since these CG α-Mo allow the dislocations glide more ea-
sily [ 17]. An excellent strength would also be achieved in the Mo-Si-B
alloys owing to the high strengthening ability of the UFGs [ 18]. On the
other hand, toughening contributions from crack trapping can be en-
hanced by the coarser microstructures, as are seen in a variety of in-
termetallics, composites, and ceramics [ 19–21]. While in the Mo-Si-B
alloys, these coarse α-Mo grains could arrest cracks by local blunting
and further enhance the toughness [ 22]. Thus, producing the bimodal
microstructure for Mo-Si-B alloys would appear to be a promising ap-
proach to improve their fracture toughness as well as ensure the ex-
cellent strength.

2. Experimental procedures
The coarse-grained and ﬁne-grained Mo-Si-B-ZrB
2 powders with a
chemical composition of Mo-12Si-8.5B-1.0 wt% ZrB 2 were used in this
paper. The elemental powder of Mo, Si, B and ZrB 2 of 99.95%, 99.9%,
99.6% and 99.6% purity, respectively, were weighed in appropriate
proportions. The element powders were placed into the planetary ball
milling (QM-3SP2) equipped with the agate and ball, and were mixed
thoroughly with a speed of 400 rpm for 6 h using a ball to powder
weight ratio of 1:1, to obtain physically homogeneous coarse Mo-Si-B-
ZrB2 powders. The milling proceeding of the powders was performed in
argon atmosphere to limit the introduction of oxygen. And the ultra-
ﬁne-grained Mo-Si-B-ZrB
2 powders were achieved by mechanical al-
loying (MA) in a planetary ball mill (Retsch PM 100). During the pro-
cessing, the element powders were placed into the WC vials equipped
with WC balls. The loading/unloading of the powders into/from vials
was performed inside a glove box in a protective (argon) atmosphere.
Ball to powder weight ratio was selected as 10:1, and mechanical al-
loying experiments were conducted for 20 h with a speed of 250 rpm.
Under action of MA, powder particles disintegrate into small ones due
to the cold welding and fracturing, and deformation mainly takes place
in the particle grain leading to a high dislocation density, which com-
bines with each other to form low-angle grain boundaries or high-angle
ones, thus achieving ultra- ﬁned grains. Morphologies of coarse and ﬁne
Mo-Si-B-ZrB
2 powders are presented in Fig. 1. The coarse powders show
that the element powders are uniformly mixed, showing di ﬀerent
morphologies ( Fig. 1(a)). The Si and B powders have irregular shapes,
whereas the Mo powder has a spherical morphology. There, the Mo
particles range from 1.3 to 6.7 μm with an average size of 3.7 μm
(Fig. 1(a)). The Fig. 1(b) shows that the ﬁne powder particles reach
spherical shape and disintegrate into small ones with relative uniform
sizes of 1.4 μm after MA, which promotes the dispersed Si and B pow-
ders distribution within the Mo powders forming small composite
powder particles. Whereafter, the ultra ﬁne Mo-Si-B-ZrB
2 powders were
mixed with 5 or 10 vol% of coarse powders thoroughly using the
planetary ball milling (QM-3SP2) with a speed of 400 rpm, to obtain
physically homogeneous bimodal Mo-Si-B-ZrB 2 powders (named MSBZ-
5, MSBZ-10, respectively).
The bimodal powder mixture was consolidated using hot pressing
technique in which the compaction pressure was applied through gra-
phite rod. The powder was initially isothermally annealed at 1200 °C
for 1 h under a vacuum (< 10
−3 Pa) to eliminate the internal stress and
promote phase precipitation, and then was sintered at 1600 °C for 2 h
with a pressure of 49 MPa. Finally, sintered billets of MSBZ-5 and
MSBZ-10 alloys with 60 mm diameter and 10 mm thick were achieved.
Additionally, the control sample of ultra ﬁne-grained Mo-12Si-8.5B-
1.0 wt% ZrB
2 alloy (UFG-MSBZ) was prepared just using the ultra ﬁne-
grained powders that produced via mechanical alloying for 15 h.
The alloys were characterized using X-ray Di ﬀraction (XRD).
Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) and Transmission Electron
Microscopy (TEM) combined with selected area electron di ﬀraction
(SAED) were used to evaluate the phase formations and micro-
structures. Sizes of grains were measured by TEM images through sta-
tistics. Samples used for microstructural analysis through SEM images
were etched in modi ﬁed reagent (7 g potassium ferricyanide, 2 g NaOH
in 100 mL water) to reveal the phase structure. Since the corrosion
resistance of Mo is stronger than that of intermetallic compound phases,
its grain boundary is not easy to be etched. For TEM sample prepara-
tion, small plates were mechanically ground down to disks with
thicknesses of 50 μm and diameters of 3 mm. Vickers indentation
formed at 50 Kg force was used to produce radial cracks, primarily at
the comers of the indents. The crack traces thus formed reveal the path
of a propagating crack and were used to characterize crack-micro-
structure interactions. Compressive tests and three-point bending tests
were conducted to assess the room temperature mechanical properties
of Mo-12Si-8.5B-1.0 wt% ZrB
2 alloys. Compressive strength was as-
sessed by a computer-controlled machine (HT-2402) at a loading rate of
0.15 mm·min
−1. Samples for compressive tests with a cross-section of
5×5m m 2 and a length of 10 mm were prepared. For three-point
bending tests, rectangular specimens of dimensions
3 mm × 4 mm × 26 mm were cut from the as-sintered alloys, and not-
ches were introduced with a depth of 2 mm in 4 mm thickness (fatigue
pre-cracks could not be introduced because of the brittleness of the
specimens). Three-point bend tests were performed at a 0.05 mm·min
−1
loading rate with a span of 20 mm. The details about the fracture
toughness measurement were shown in Ref. [ 25]. Additionally, the
fracture morphologies were observed with SEM after the bend tests.

3.3. Toughening mechanisms
The improved fracture toughness for the bimodal MSBZ alloys in
comparison with the UFG-MSBZ alloy is signi ﬁcantly associated with
the establishment of coarse α-Mo grains that tend to exhibit higher
ductility and toughness. In fact, the coarse α-Mo grain has stronger
ability to store dislocations and also dislocations can glide more easily
in the CG grain than in the UFG grain due to the larger size and fewer
obstacles of the grain boundaries [ 27], which support evidence for a
higher strain hardening in the coarse α-Mo grain, thereby showing
higher in-situ ductility. And the characteristic of high dislocation ac-
tivities in the coarse α-Mo grain can be easily observed in the deformed
sample, as typically shown in Fig. 8. That is to say the more coarse α-
Mo grains in the alloy, which result in the higher current dislocations
density in the microstructure, would be favorable to the toughness in-
crement. As a result, such increased in-situ ductility of the coarse α-Mo
grain would promote some degree of ductile-phases trapping [ 28], and
that would enhance the fracture toughness of the bimodal MSBZ alloy.
In case of the UFG-MSBZ alloy, SEM image observation of the in-
dentation crack path ( Fig. 9(a)) is primarily con ﬁned to the inter-
metallics transgranular and the α-Mo intergranular. It shows not much
trapping interaction between the ﬁne α-Mo phase and the crack path
and, thus the distance between the α-Mo trapping phases is larger. In
contrast, the typical indentation crack paths in the bimodal MSBZ-5 and
MSBZ-10
alloys have shown clear evidences of crack trapping by the
coarse α-Mo ( Fig. 9(b, c)), which provides appreciable impedance to
the crack advance. The crack trapping involves arresting the crack by
ductile phase, causing it to bow out between them and only renucleate
on the other side. This process is related to the inherent local resistance
of ductile phase to crack, as a result, the crack trapping can be thought
intrinsical toughening [ 29,30]. Fig. 9(c) shows that the crack is trapped
by a row of α-Mo phases. To overcome these barriers of α-Mo phases,
the higher applied driving forces are needed, thereby resulting in higher
overall toughness for the MSBZ-10 alloy.

4. Conclusions
In the present work, Mo-12Si-8.5B-ZrB
2 alloys consisting of α-Mo,
Mo3Si and Mo 5SiB2 (T2) phases were prepared by mechanical alloying
followed by hot pressing. Particularly, a bimodal grain structure was
established in the α-Mo, which consisted of predominant ultra ﬁne α-Mo
grains (average size of 400 nm) and a small quantity of coarse α-Mo
grains (average size of 1.5 μm). The bimodal α-Mo grain structure re-
sulted in the alloys having an obvious disparity in mechanical proper-
ties with the ultra ﬁne-grained alloy. The bimodal one with the ratio of
the ﬁne and coarse α-Mo grains 6:1, presented the best comprehensive
mechanical properties with an enhanced fracture toughness
(13.1 MPa·m
1/2) and a considerable high compressive strength
(2998 MPa). The enhanced toughness was attributed to the in-situ
toughening of the coarse α-Mo grain, specially, which also bene ﬁted the
toughness by aiding crack trapping and rising crack stability. The ﬁne
α-Mo grains however, played an important role in the high strength,
supplemented by a strength contribution due to the Zr-rich particles
that resided in the coarse α-Mo grains.
