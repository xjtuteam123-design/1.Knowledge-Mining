1.Introduction 
NiAl intermetallic has been regarded as a promising candidate for elevated temperature 
structural applications in aggressive environments due to some attractive properties , such as high 
melting point, good thermal conductivity, low density and excellent oxidation resistance [1 , 2]. 
However, low room temperature fracture toughness and elevated temperature strength limit its 
industrial application [3]. Therefore, further studies on the toughening and strengthening of NiAl 
alloy are required, such as alloying, second-phase strengthening , directional solidification and 
single crystal preparation [4-9]. Among these, a n effective way is preparing eutectic in -situ 
composites by directional solidification of eutectic alloy s, which are thermodynamically stable, 
chemically compatible and well aligned [10]. 
Many investigations have shown that directionally solidified NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy could form 
eutectic microstructure and has the best combination of room temperature fracture toughness and 
high temperature strength compared to other NiAl -based eutectic alloys [11-16]. Both X.F. Chen 
et al. [14] and A. Misra et al. [15] found that Cr(Mo) phase could increase fracture toughness and 
high temperature strength in the NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy. But due to the relatively low temperature 
gradient, the studies we re all carried out at the eutectic composition to obtain fully eutectic 
microstructure. For this reason, the volume fraction of Cr(Mo) strengthening phase was limited. In

hypereutectic alloy, the volume fraction of Cr(Mo) phase was larger and fully eutecti c could be 
also obtained [17]. The mechanical properties of in-situ composites are well known to be sensitive 
to the microstructure morphology and volume fraction of constituent phases, which can be 
controlled by changing alloy composition and solidification conditions during the process of 
preparation of eutectic in-situ composites. In previous paper, 6 at.% Mo and different contents of 
Cr were added in the stoichiometric NiAl alloy. The results showed that the volume fraction of 
Cr(Mo) strengthening phase increased with the increasing content of Cr, accordingly, the room 
temperature fracture toughness also increased [1 7]. So, similar effect on the high temperature 
strength is expected. In this paper, different alloy compositions and withdrawal rates were adopted 
to control the volume fraction of Cr(Mo) strengthening phase and microstructure morphology, the 
relationships between them and the high temperature properties of NiAl -Cr(Mo) alloy were 
investigated. In addition, the fracture and strengthening mechanisms were also discussed. 
2. Experimental procedures 
2.1 Alloy preparation and directional solidification 
The master alloys of NiAl-xCr-6Mo ( x=28, 32 and 36 at.%) were prepared by vacuum 
induction melting furnace. Chemical composition analysis was perfor med by the inductive ly 
coupled plasma technique, and the result was mainly consistent with the nominal alloy 
composition. So the nominal composition w as used in this paper. Φ7 mm rod was cut from the 
ingots by electro-discharge machining (EDM) and then placed into high purity alumna crucible 
with 7.1 mm in inner diameter for directional solidification in Bridgeman apparatus with induction 
heating. The vacuum level was about 7 ×10-3 Pa before filling with high purity argon, and the 
sample was heated by a graphite heater to 1680±10℃. After thermal equilibrium was reached, the

2.2 Tensile tests 
The tensile specimens with gage section of 3×3×22 mm were cut from the directionally 
solidified rods by EDM, the long axis of the specimens was parallel to the growth direction. Prior 
to tensile testing, all the surfaces of the specimens were ground by 2000 grit abrasive. Tensile tests

induction heater. The vacuum level was about 1×10-2 Pa, and the specimens were heated to 1000 ℃ 
and kept isothermally for 15 min. All the specimens were tested at a strain rate of 4.2×10-4 /s. The 
specimen lengths before and after fracture were measured to calculate the percent elongation to 
fracture. At least, four samples of each condition were tested to obtain average values of ultimate 
tensile strength and elongation. 
2.3 Metallography and Fractography 
The directionally solidified samples were sectioned transversely by EDM , the location of the 
cross section was 5 mm below the solid/liquid interface. A fter grinding and polishing, the 
metallographic specimens were etched with a solution of HCl + 20% HNO3, the microstructures 
were observed by optical microscope (OM). The fracture surface s of tensile samples were 
examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The dislocations and precipitated phases were 
observed by Tecnai G2F30 transmission electron microscope (TEM). The samples for TEM were 
cut perpendicular to the growth direction and were prepared by conventional procedure.

3.1 Steady solidification microstructures 
The microstructure evolutions of directionally solidified NiAl -Cr(Mo) alloy have been 
systematically discussed in previous papers [1 7-20], so the details are not repeated here, only the 
representative steady-state transverse microstructures are shown in Fig. 1. Fig. 1(a) -(d) show the 
planar eutectic microstructure, coarse cellular eutectic microstructure, perfect cellular eutectic 
microstructure and Cr(Mo) dendrites + eutectic microstructure, respectively. When the withdrawal 
rate was low for the three alloys, the growth interface was planar and the steady microstructure 
was planar eutectic with regular lamallae. At higher withdrawal rate, the growth interface became 
cellular due to the two -phase instability resulting from the constitutional supercooling, and the 
corresponding steady microstructure was cellular eutectic for directionally solidified 
NiAl-28Cr-6Mo eutectic alloy and NiAl -32Cr-6Mo hypereutectic alloy . But for the 
NiAl-36Cr-6Mo hypereutectic alloy, the composition was far away from the eutectic point, the 
solidification condition did not locate in the coupled eutectic growth zone at higher wi thdrawal 
rate; fully eutectic microstructure could not be obtained. The steady microstructure changed to 
Cr(Mo) dendrites + eutectic when the withdrawal rate was 10 μm/s. It is worth noting that the 
perfect cellular microstructure formed under a particular condition [1 9], t he width of the 
intercellular regions was narrowest, no coarse and short plates existed at the cell boundaries ; the 
thickness of the eutectic lamellae was almost uniform, which was beneficial to the increase of the 
room temperature fractu re toughness in directionally solidified NiAl -32Cr-6Mo hypereutectic 
alloy [20]. 
The selected area diffraction pattern of NiAl and Cr(Mo) phase s is shown in Fig. 1(e) . Due to

3.2 High temperature tensile properties 
The tensile properties of directionally solidified NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloys at temperature of 1000 ℃ 
and strain rate of 4.2×10-4/s are listed in Table 1. The ultimate tensile strength of binary NiAl alloy 
at temperature of 980 ℃ and strain rate of 1.67×10-4/s is about 55 MPa [ 23]. In general, if the 
temperature decreases or the strain rate increases, the ultimate tensile strength increases and the 
elongation decreases [22]. Through adding different contents of Cr and Mo element, the ultimate 
tensile strength of directionally solidified NiAl-Cr(Mo) markedly increased to 317.6~513.8 MPa . 
The solid/liquid interfaces of directionally solidified NiAl-28Cr-6Mo eutectic alloy grown at 6 
and 10 μm/s were both planar, and the values of ultimate tensile strength were 317.6 and 374.9 
MPa, respectively. The tensile strength increased by about a factor of 6~7 compared to the binary 
NiAl alloy. When the withdrawal rate increased to 15 μm/s, the microstructure was not fully 
planar eutectic, so the ultimate tensile strength dropped down to 333.3 MPa. Above the 
withdrawal rate, the ultimate tensile strength increased due to the refined microstructure. With the 
increasing withdrawal rate, the microstructure refined, so the elongation gradually increased. For 
the d irectionally solidified hypereutectic alloys , the ultimate tensile strength also 
experienced the same change trend. With the increasing content of Cr, the tensile strength 
increased markedly in the alloy with regularly lamellar microstructure . However, there was an 
exception that NiAl-32Cr-6Mo hypereutectic alloy with perfect cellular microstructure had higher 
tensile strength of 431.7 MPa. Furthermore, the elongation was also higher due to the fast

3.3 Fractography 
In order to research the high temperature tensile behaviors of the directionally solidif ied 
NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy, the fracture surfaces were observed by SEM. Because the microstructure 
evolutions were similar, only the representative fracture morphologies are presented. The fracture 
morphologies of the three alloys solidified at 6 μm/s are shown in Fig. 2. From the fracture surface 
of NiAl-28Cr-6Mo eutectic alloy, the fracture seemed to be ductile, a large number of deep 
dimples were observed. In addition, debonding along phase boundaries occurred occasionally. In 
the NiAl-32Cr-6Mo hypereutectic alloy, the percentage of debonding increased. The fracture 
morphology changed to a combination of debonding along phase boundaries and ductile fracture 
within its constituent phases. With increasing content of Cr, the debonding further increased. The 
fracture behavior of NiAl-36Cr-6Mo hypereutectic alloy was brittle with cleavage and debonding. 
In addition, the fracture surface of NiAl-28Cr-6Mo eutectic alloy was relatively flat, fewer cracks 
were observed. The fracture surface became more rugged in the NiAl-32Cr-6Mo hypereutectic 
alloy, and the number of cracks increased. The microstructures of these three alloys were all planar 
eutectic, the bonding strength between the eutectic lamellae should be almost the same . 
Apparently, the more rugged fracture surface and debonding suggested more fracture resistance 
and less elongation, which are agreement with the experimental results.

 The micracracks generated and propagated during the tensile loading until the specimen 
eventually fracture, which could show the fracture mode of specimen. From Fig. 5(a) -(c), the 
microcracks mainly propagated along 45 °direction with the tensile axis, the fracture direction 
also showed this position relation . During the tensile loading of ductile materials, if the load 
exceeded the yield strength, uniform plastic deformation occurred. With the increasing load, the 
necking appeared, and the actual stress of the necking point gradually improved. At the same time, 
the plastic deformation in the center of the specimen produced transverse stress. The center of the 
specimen was under three-dimensional stress state and was easy to lead to crack initiation. Due to 
the biggest shear stress on the planes along 45° direction with the front of crack propagation, the 
microcracks actually propagated along one of the 45° direction plane. Because crack propagation 
did not deviate from the minimum cross section of necking, the microcracks changed to propagate 
along another 45° direction plane. When the crack s propagated to a certain degree, the heat 
generated by the plastic deformation resulted in local softening of the metal . Together with the 
influence of plane stress state, the s pecimen would be shear fracture along 45° direction with the 
tensile axis. 
From Fig. 5(d)-(e), the microcracks generated in the regions between the primary Cr(Mo) phase 
and the eutectic, and mainly propagated along the phase boundary. During the tensile loading 
process, the resistance of the crack propagation was low and the specimen fractures directly along

significant effect s on the high temperature tensile strength of the directionally solidified

NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy. The reason was that they directly affected the microstructure morphology and 
volume fraction of strengthening phase. In general, well -aligned microstructures, such as planar 
and perfect cellular eutectic microstructures, were better able to improve high temperature tensile 
strength. If the morphology of microstructure was similar, the effect of alloy composition on the 
tensile strength wa s significant, this was mainly due to the different volume fraction s of the 
constitute phases. 
According to Hooke's law, the prediction of tensile strength of composite materials is usually 
analyzed by mixing rule [31]. Obviously, in order to achieve the purpose of strengthening material, 
the strength of the strengthening phase should be greater than the strength of the matrix phase. 
This suggests that the strength of the composite materials will increase with the increasing volume 
fraction of strengthening phase. 
 In the NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy, the yield strength of NiAl phase at 1000℃ is less than 90 MPa [32]. 
As strengthening phase , the strength of Cr(Mo) phase is significantly greater than th at of NiAl 
matrix. The hig hest tensile strength and the corresponding volume fraction of Cr(Mo) 
strengthening phase in the NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloys with different compositions are listed in Table 2. 
The greater the volume fraction of Cr(Mo) strengthening phase, the higher the high temperature 
tensile strength of alloy. 
After high temperature tensile, the dislocation morphologies in the NiAl -Cr(Mo) alloy were 
observed by TEM, and the results are shown in Fig. 6 . A large number of dislocations were found 
in both NiAl phase and Cr(Mo) phase. However, the dislocation density in the NiAl matrix phase 
was more, which suggest ed that NiA l phase ha d experienced a very large plastic deformation 
during the high temperature tensile process. For stoichiometric NiAl alloy, it is very brittle at room

temperature, but the brittle ductile transition happens at 400~600℃. If the temperature is higher 
than the ductile brittle transition temperature, the NiAl phase becomes ductile [1]. In this work, the 
test temperature was 1000℃, the NiA l phase was ductile. Compared to the yield strength of 
Cr(Mo) phase, NiAl phase was much smaller, so the deformation of NiA l phase was greater than 
that of Cr(Mo) phase, resulting in more dislocations. 
As two phase eutectic alloy, the phase interface between NiAl phase and Cr(Mo) phase in the 
NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy has great influence on the motion of dislocation during the tensile process. The 
TEM analysis on the phase interface is shown in Fig. 7. T he HRTEM image indicates that very 
small lattice mismatch was found on the phase interface. The phase interface is semi-coherent, 
which belongs to the strong combination of interface, the bonding strength of phase interface is 
high [33, 34]. In addition, a network of interface dislocations was observed between the NiAl and 
Cr(Mo) phases, which could relieve the long-range internal stresses caused by the lattice mismatch 
[35]. The inhibitions of phase interface are mainly due to (1) the difference in modulus of 
elasticity of NiAl and Cr(Mo) phases; (2) the interface dislocation network between two phases; (3) 
the antiphase domain energy in the ordered phase; (4) dislocations having to change slip systems 
from one side of the interface to the other [24]. 
 With the increasing withdrawal rate, eutectic lamellar spacing gradually decrease d, the number 
of phase interface increased, which could result in more interface dislocation network s. The 
quantity of phase interface s and dislocation networks would hinder the motion of dislocation, so 
increasing with drawal rate always led to the incre ase of the tensile strength under the same 
microstructure morphology.

differences in the strengthening effects caused by solid solution atoms and precipitated phase s. 
Obviously, the differences in the tensile strength caused by the varying withdrawal rates were 
caused by the different microstructure morphologies. When the withdrawal rate increased, it 
would le ad to thinner interlamellar spacing and planar/cellular transition of microstructure. 
Refined interlamellar spacing could hinder the dislocation motion, thus strengthening alloy; but 
low bonding strength in coarse cellular alloy reduced the strength. 
 In a word , among the four strengthening mechanisms, bonding strength of interface was the 
most important. F or planar NiAl -Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy, high interface bonding strength , large 
volume fraction of Cr(Mo) strengthening phase and dispersion strengthening of second phase 
particles were helpful to the improvement of tensile strength. For coarse cellular alloy, low 
bonding strength between eutectic cells made the crack easily propagat e along the cellular 
boundary. Although refined interlamellar spacing had positive effect, but weak cellular boundary 
had greater adverse effect . S trengthen factors were the large volume fraction of C r(Mo) 
strengthening phase , dispersion strengthening of second phase particles and refined eutectic 
lamellar spacing. For perfect cellular alloy, the bonding strength between eutectic cells was high, 
which resulted in high crack propagation resistance and harmonious deformation in the 
intracellular regions and the intercellular regions , so a ll the above strengthening mechanisms 
could work.

(1) Compared to the binary NiAl and other NiAl -based eutectic alloys, the high temperature

relatively low strength.
