Precipitation of an extruded 95W–5(Ni/Fe/Co) alloy at 300–600 ◦C
and its inﬂuence on high temperature plasticity
Fang Wenbin∗, Wang Erde, Yu Yang, He Wenxiong
School of Materials Science and Engineering, Harbin Institute of Technology, Harbin 150001, PR China
Received 30 May 2003; received in revised form 10 February 2004
Abstract
This paper analyzes the conﬁgurations of the binding phases at different temperatures for 95W–5(Ni/Fe/Co) alloys by TEM, the precipitates
of 95W–5(Ni/Fe/Co) alloys distribute dispersively at 300–500◦C. So, it can make up for the fall of strength caused by the rise of temperature.
Deformed at 600◦C, the plasticity of the binding phases falls when the precipitates grow into stripes and distribute layer upon layer. Furthermore,
owing to the discordance of the expansion coefﬁcients of the two phases, internal stress forms, which reduces the integration strength at the
boundary. The abnormity of plasticity change has close connection with temperature and precipitation.

1. Introduction
In 1965, Lasson and Murphy made a conclusion of the
properties and uses of the newly developed material of
tungsten-based heavy alloys [1]. Since then, with the de-
mand of hi-tech and development of military industry and
also the application of new test method, tungsten alloys
appear as the basic theory is developed in depth. Tungsten
heavy alloy has high density high tensile strength good
ductility and good radiation absorbing ability. They also
have good electric transmit, corrosion resistance and lower
thermal expansion coefﬁcient. So, they possess broad fore-
ground of application in radiation shielding and kinetic pen-
etrators, thus enhance the power of the armour piercing core
[2].
German and co-workers [3,4] point out that the fall of
plasticity of W–Ni–Fe tungsten heavy alloys over 550
◦C
is caused by the decrease of the plasticity of the binding
phases and that of the interface between the binding phases
and the tungsten grains. To ﬁnd out the essential cause for
the plasticity change, the change patterns of structure con-
ﬁguration must be studied. Brittle phases will be separated
out if the tungsten alloys are cooled slowly at agglomeration

4. Precipitates and formation law during hot
deformation
To improve the room temperature properties of tungsten
heavy alloys, many scholars have made a lot of research
and have different views on the effect of cooling rate on
mechanical properties [12–14]. By summing up their re-
search results, the effect of cooling rate on the mechanical
properties can be attributed to the precipitation and hydro-
gen brittleness. Though rapid cooling after sintering may
reduce and reﬁne the brittle precipitates, it is not favorable
for the removal of hydrogen, while the hydrogen brittleness
is another important reason for the brittleness of tungsten
heavy alloys at room temperature. If the room temperature
brittleness is caused by precipitation, rapid cooling can
increase the ductility. If the room temperature brittleness

5. Effect of precipitates on plasticity of
95W–5(Ni/Fe/Co) tungsten alloys
Rabin [15] pointed out that the sintering condition of liq-
uid phase is that the liquid phases can wet and dissolve the
solid phases. Liquid phases exist during high temperature
sintering and cause rapid compacting and growth of the
tungsten grains. The growth of the tungsten grains includes
dissolution, separation and combination recrystallization.
Dissolution, separation and crystallization concerns the sol-
ubility of the tungsten in the binding phases, which changes
with temperature. Known from the W–Ni phase diagram
[16], the highest solubility of W in Ni reaches 45% at
1495
◦C. But the addition of Fe can make it decrease. Liang
and Wang have studied the solubility of W in the binding
phases during liquid phase sintering of the W–Ni–Fe alloys
[17]. When the sintering temperature of liquid phase is
1200–1300
◦C, the solubility of W in Ni reaches 17.44%;
1450–1590 ◦C, 19.66%; 1540–1600 ◦C, 20.25%. Super-
saturated solid solution appears because of the drop of
solubility of tungsten in the binding phases. If these kinds
of alloys are cooled rapidly from the sintering temperature
of liquid phase, part of the precipitates will be retained and
become smaller because of the difﬁculty in diffusion. Heat-
ing and preservation of the supersaturated solid solution at
a certain temperature will separate out some new phases
which strengthen the alloys. If the temperature is higher
and the time of heat preservation is longer, the precipitates
will accumulate and grow. Above certain temperature, the
precipitates will dissolve again.
The precipitates at 300
◦C are very tiny, part of them ac-
cumulate at 400 ◦C. They strengthened the binding phases
by dispersive distribution. The 95W–5(Ni/Fe/Co) alloys
are accumulation of only part of the precipitates at 400
◦C.
Dispersive distribution of the precipitates strengthen the
binding phases. Because the mechanical properties depend
strongly on the plasticity and strength of the binding phases,
the result of dispersive strengthening can increase the plas-
ticity and strength of the alloys. On the other hand, because
of the rise of temperature, the resistance of dislocation
movement becomes smaller and the tensile strength of the
alloys decreases. These affect comprehensively the nearly
constant fracture strength. When the deformation tempera-
ture rises to 500
◦C, the accumulation of precipitates is quite
clear, but can be distinguished as composed of many thin
crystalline particles. The precipitates of the binding phases
show themselves with stripes, which can be distinguished
that these stripes are congregated by thin and little tungsten
particles. The particles are only an accumulation, they have
not grown up to an integration. When the temperature rises
to over 600
◦C, the thin precipitates grow up to integrate and
distribute as stripes and patches, which lead to the lower
plasticity of alloys. But the separation aggrandizement
still exists. So 95W–5(Ni/Fe/Co) alloys have the highest

In addition, the W–Ni–Fe tungsten heavy alloys are
a kind of expansion alloy [18], the document [19] point
out that the Curie point of the binding phase material for
95W–5(Ni/Fe/Co) alloys is about 550
◦C, the thermal ex-

when the temperature is below the Curie point. The thermal
expansion coefﬁcients of the binding phases rapidly increase
and the thermal expansion coefﬁcient of the tungsten grains
keeps almost constant when the temperature is above the
Curie point. Consequently internal stress forms between the
tungsten and the binding phases and reduces the combina-
tion strength of the alloy at the interfaces. So, the plasticity
of the alloys declines drastically. This is another reason
why the plasticity of the alloys reduces distinctly when the
temperature rises to over 600
◦C. Further discussion will be
carried out on the effect of internal stress on the plasticity
of the W–Ni–Fe alloys.
6. Conclusions
(1) The fracture strength of the alloy almost keeps constant
at 400–600
◦C. Beyond 600 ◦C, it reduces sharply with
the rise of temperature. When the temperature is be-
low 300
◦C, the plasticity of 95W–5(Ni/Fe/Co) alloys
increases with the rise of temperature. At 400–600 ◦C,
it is the largest. Over 600 ◦C, it falls sharply.
(2) Dispersive distribution of the precipitates strengthens
the binding phases when the deformation temperature is
400–600
◦C, the result of dispersive strengthening can
increase the plasticity and fracture strength of the al-
loys and make up the reduction of strength due to the
rise of temperature, thus keeping the strength of the al-
loys almost constant. When the temperature rises to over
600
◦C, the thin precipitates grow up to integrate and
distribute as stripes and patches. That is an important
reason why the plasticity of the alloys becomes lower.
