Abstract
The effect of two-step heat treatment on the microstructures and room temperature tensile properties of Ti–
47Al–2Cr (at.%) was investigated. A high ductility TiAl alloy, whose room temperature tensile elongation, yield
strength and ultimate tensile strength are 6.7%, 597 and 677 MPa, respectively, was made. The formation
mechanism of high ductility TiAl alloy during two-step heat treatment was discussed.

1. Introduction
Two-phase TiAl alloy has received considerable attention recently as candidate material for relatively
high temperature uses as turbine engine components because of its low density and good high
temperature properties [1]. The mechanical properties of TiAl alloy are sensitive to its microstructure.
The TiAl alloy with coarse fully lamellar structure shows adequate fracture toughness but poor tensile
ductility; on the other hand, the alloy with ﬁne duplex structure exhibits adequate tensile ductility but
poor fracture toughness [2]. This dilemma has not been solved satisfactorily at present. The major
concern for structural use of TiAl alloy is its low ductility at ambient temperatures [3,4]. Adding
alloying elements is an important way to improve its ductility. By adding Cr, B, C, Si, W, and Nb, Liu
et al. [5] have obtained a fully lamellar microstructure TiAl alloy with a room temperature tensile
elongation of 4.7%. By adding Cr and Mo, Chen et al. [6] have obtained a duplex microstructure TiAl
alloy with a room temperature ductility of 5.0%. At the same time, adjusting the microstructure of TiAl

2. Experimental
A two-phase gamma TiAl alloy with composition Ti –47Al–2Cr (at.%) was melted in a vacuum
consumable electrode furnace. Through chemical composition analysis, it is shown that the mass
fractions of oxygen and nitrogen are 6 :4 /C210/C04 and 1 :6 /C210/C04%, respectively. The alpha phase
transition temperature, Ta, of this alloy is determined to be 1326 8C through a series of heat treatments
followed by optical metallography. After homogenization at 1050 8C for 72 h and hot isostatic pressing
at 1250 8C for 4 h under argon pressure of 150 MPa, the two-step canned hot forging[9] was adopted to
deform this alloy. The ﬁrst step canned hot forging deformed the specimens by 50% at 1200 8C using a
deformation rate of 1 :1 /C210/C03 s/C01. After heat treatment at 1250 8C for 4 h, the specimens were
deformed once again by 60% at 1080 8C using a deformation rate of 5 :0 /C210/C02 s/C01. Then, the forged
specimens were heat treated by conventional heat treatment and two-step heat treatment. The heat
treatments are listed in Table 1.
Tensile tests were performed on an Instron testing machine at room temperature in air at a strain rate
of 1 :1 /C210/C03 s/C01. The specimens were mechanically polished to remove surface scratches prior to
testing.
Microstructure features were studied by optical microscopy (OM) and scanning electron microscopy
(SEM). Metallographic samples were etched in a solution of 95% H2O, 3% HNO3, and 1% HF to reveal
microstructures. Semiquantitative methods, such as the point counting method and linear intercept
method, were employed to determine colony size.

3.2. Effect of heat treatment on microstructures
Fig. 1 lists the optical microstructures of specimen 1 (panel a) and specimen 3 (panel b). From Fig. 1,
it can be seen that duplex microstructures are obtained in TiAl specimens after both conventional heat

grain boundaries of specimen 3 is distinct, but that of specimen 1 is not distinct. Thirdly, the lamellae in
specimen 3 precipitate absolutely, but the lamellar colonies in specimen 1 are ﬁbrous.

and that of the specimens after conventional heat treatment. These differences may result from a new
phase transformation mechanism caused by two-step heat treatment.

From the analysis above, it can be seen that a complex phase transformation takes place in TiAl
specimens during the two-step heat treatment, so the two-step heat treatment is superior to conventional
heat treatment. The uniform, ﬁne microstructures can be obtained in TiAl alloy during two-step heat
treatment, so its mechanical properties, particularly ductility are improved. But it is important to control
the processing of two-step heat treatment, especially the temperature of the second step heat treatment.
Because when the temperature of the second step heat treatment is lower, it is difﬁcult for asecond grains
to form lamellar colonies. Brittle a2 particles formed along the boundaries of lamellar colonies and g
grains lead to the marked decrease of room temperature tensile properties.
5. Conclusions
By the heat treatment of 1290 8C, 4 h!FC1240 8C, 4 h, FC þ 900 8C, 24 h, a high ductility TiAl
alloy, whose room temperature tensile elongation, yield strength and ultimate tensile strength are 6.7%,
597 and 677 MPa, respectively, was made.
A complex phase transformation takes place in TiAl specimens during two-step heat treatment, so the
uniform ﬁne microstructures can be obtained in TiAl specimens. Yet, it is important to control the
processing paraments. When the temperature of the second step heat treatment is lower, brittle a
