Adopting the idea of thermal modification of conventional Si-containing eutectic alloys, this study performed heating of the
brittle CoFeNi 2(Ti3Si5)0.16 eutectic high-entropy alloy under various annealing conditions to attempt the fragmentation and
spheroidization of eutectic microstructure and improve fracture plasticity. Results reveal that the as-cast alloy exhibited fine
eutectic microstructures with lamellar + network morphologies, consisting of face-centered cubic (FCC) and M
16Ti6Si7-type
silicide. After annealing at 1100°C × 120 h, the lamellar + network eutectic morphologies were effectively fragmented and
spheroidized into granular + irregular morphologies. The resulting alloy featured excellent mechanical properties with an
ultimate compressive strength (UCS) of 1980 ± 50 MPa, fracture plasticity of ~16.6% ± 1%, and hardness of ~448 ±15 HV .
Compared with the as-cast specimen, the fracture plasticity of the specimen annealed for 120 h increased by 12.7 times, with no
UCS reduction. With a further increase in the annealing time, the hard M
16Ti6Si7-type silicide was seriously coarsened, dete-
riorating the alloy’s room-temperature mechanical properties but improving its high-temperature ones.

1 Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted extensive atten-
tion in the field of metal materials because of their special
physical, chemical, and mechanical properties [1–7]. For
example, they have been reported to exhibit attractive me-
chanical properties, e.g., a good combination of strength-
plasticity, excellent oxidation resistance, and remarkable
wear resistance [8–10]. In addition, the latest research results
also show that HEAs possess specific functionality, e.g.,
good radiation resistance, excellent soft magnetism, and
outstanding antibacterial behavior [11–13]. Although HEAs
exhibit attractive properties, the poor castability and com-
positional inhomogeneity are not conducive to their large-
scale industrial application [14,15].
In order to improve the casting formability and reduce
compositional segregation of HEAs, the concept of eutectic
high-entropy alloys (EHEAs) was first proposed by Lu et al.
[16] in 2014 by combining the advantages of HEAs and
conventional eutectic alloys. Since the concept of EHEAs
was proposed, dozens of EHEAs have been designed and
manufactured, e.g., Al
0.9CoCrNi2.1 [17], Al 19Co20Fe20Ni41
[18], CoCrFeNiNb 0.5 [19], CoCrFeNiTa 0.4 [20], CoCrFe-
NiZr0.55 [21], CoCrFeNiHf 0.45 [22], CoCrFeNiMnPd [23],

Fe35Ni25Cr25Mo15 [24], Co 2Mo0.8Ni2VW0.8 [25], and
Co25Fe25Mn5Ni25Ti20 [26]. Among the reported EHEAs, it
can be found that the selected compositions of the designed
EHEAs were based on metallic elements, while those con-
taining nonmetallic elements have rarely been reported [27].
However, nonmetallic elements, with small atomic radii, low
density, and affordable prices, often participate in the con-
struction of eutectic microstructures in conventional binary
alloys, like Al-Si, Fe-C, and Fe-B alloys [28–33].
Our previous work proposed a new strategy to design
EHEAs containing nonmetallic elements by introducing an
A-(M+N) mode and calculating the thermodynamic phase
diagram [34]. According to this strategy, four novel CoCr-
Ni
2(Mo5C3)0.2, CoCrNi 2(V2B)0.43, CoCrFeNi 2(V6B3Si)0.15,
and CoFeNi 2(Ti3Si5)0.16 EHEAs were successfully designed
and prepared. The as-cast CoCrNi 2(Mo5C3)0.2, CoCrNi 2-
(V2B)0.43, and CoCrFeNi 2(V6B3Si)0.15 EHEAs showed ex-
cellent strength-plasticity balance due to the low volume
fraction of hard carbide/boride and unconnected eutectic
microstructure with rod-like/granular morphology [34,35].
The as-cast CoFeNi
2(Ti3Si5)0.16 EHEA exhibited brittle be-
havior because of the high volume fraction of hard silicide
and connected eutectic microstructures with network + la-
mellar morphologies [34]. Hence, the critical challenge of
CoFeNi2(Ti3Si5)0.16 EHEA to be satisfactory for engineering
applications is enhancing its fracture plasticity.
For conventional eutectic alloys containing Si, heat treat-
ment promoted the decomposition and spheroidization of
eutectic phases to improve the mechanical properties, which
have been widely investigated [36–38]. For example, Li et al.
[36] reported that the effect of solution treatment on the
eutectic Si morphology and mechanical properties of Al-Si
alloy. By heat treatment, the morphology of eutectic Si could
be fully transformed from acicular-shaped to spherical-
shaped, which effectively alleviated the splitting effect of
hard Si particles on the soft Al matrix and remarkably im-
proved the plasticity of the Al-Si alloy. Wu et al. [37] in-
vestigated that the evolution of eutectic microstructures and
mechanical properties of Ti-Si alloy at different heat treat-
ment temperatures. Similar to the effect of solution heat
treatment on the eutectic Si phase of Al-Si alloy, by the
optimized heat treatment process, the Ti
5Si3 eutectic phase
was also entirely changed from lamellar-shaped to spherical-
shaped, which showed the synergistic enhancement of
strength-plasticity. Guo et al. [38] studied that the thermal
stability of Nb-Si alloy under different annealing conditions.
The research results show that the lamellar structure was
broken, and spherical structures were formed when the an-
nealing temperature exceeded the critical instability tem-
perature. With the increased annealing temperature, the
hardness decreased, and the brittle behavior was effectively
alleviated. However, the atomic migration rate is crucial in
the decomposition and spheroidization of eutectic phases.
Compared with conventional eutectic alloys, the micro-
structure of EHEAs usually exhibits excellent thermal sta-
bility due to the sluggish diffusion effect [39–42]. Therefore,
it is worth further exploring the heat modification of brittle
EHEAs.
Adopting the thermal modification process from conven-
tional eutectic alloys containing Si. This work aims to assess
the practicability of fragmentation and spheroidization of the
eutectic microstructures of the brittle CoFeNi
2(Ti3Si5)0.16
EHEA to improve plasticity. The evolution of micro-
structures of CoFeNi 2(Ti3Si5)0.16 EHEA under different an-
nealing conditions was analyzed, and the influence of
evolution microstructures on the mechanical properties was
investigated.
2 Experimental section
All ingots with a nominal composition of CoFeNi 2(Ti3Si5)0.16
EHEA (denoted TiSi0.16-EHEA) were prepared by vacuum
arc melting. The purities of the selected constituent elements
(Co, Fe, Ni, Ti, and Si) exceeded 99.95 wt%. To ensure a
uniform chemical composition, the selected constituent ele-
ments were remelted more than five times in an argon at-
mosphere and finally cooled in a copper mold. Some
specimens were annealed at 700, 800, 900, 1000, and 1100°C
for 12 h, respectively. Other specimens were annealed at
1100°C for 60, 120, 180, 240, 300, and 360 h. All annealed
specimens were protected in an argon atmosphere and finally
cooled in the furnace.
The phase structures were analyzed by X-ray diffraction
(XRD, D8 Advance operating at 40 kV/40 mA) with Cu Kα
radiation from 20° to 100° at a scanning speed of 5°/min. The
microstructures were characterized in a scanning electron
microscope (SEM, JSM-7900F) equipped with an attached
X-ray energy-dispersive spectrometer (EDS). Transmission
electron microscopy (TEM, JEM-F200 operating at 200 kV)
was used to further analyze the microstructure and phase
structures. The samples for TEM were prepared by a focused
ion beam microscope (FIB, Helios G4 UX operating at
15 kV). All samples were thinned to 50 nm by the ion beam,
and then observed by TEM.
Room-temperature compressive tests were conducted on
the as-cast and all annealed specimens, while high-tem-
perature compressive tests were conducted on the as-cast and
annealed 1100°C × 360 h specimens at 600, 800, and
1000°C. All compressive specimens with a working area size
of Ф 6 mm × 9 mm were tested using a universal testing
machine (Gleeble3800) at a strain rate of 1 × 10
−3 s−1. To
ascertain the reproducibility, each compressive test result
reported in this work was the average of three test specimens.
The hardness was tested using a Vickers hardness tester
(MH-50) with a load force of 500 g for 15 s. At least 15 spots

The room-temperature (RT) compressive stress-strain

Vickers hardness of TiSi0.16-EHEA at elevated annealing
temperatures is shown in Figure 5(b). Similar to the evolu-

900°C. When TiSi0.16-EHEA was annealed at 1000°C, the

from 524 ± 20 to 515 ± 25 HV . Up to 1100°C, the hardness
sharply decreased to 482 ± 15 HV due to the instability of the
eutectic microstructure.
The above results indicated that the lamellar + network
morphologies and mechanical properties of TiSi0.16-EHEA
were stable to 1000°C. When annealed at 1100°C, the eu-
tectic microstructures were partially fragmented, and the
comprehensive mechanical properties were effectively im-
proved. This indicates that the suitable fragmentation and
spheroidization temperature of TiSi0.16-EHEA was 1100°C.
Thus, to further enhance the fracture plasticity, annealing at
1100°C for different times was applied to this EHEA.

4 Conclusions
In this study, to investigate the thermal stability of the eu-
tectic microstructure, optimize the room-temperature plasti-
city, improve the high-temperature strength, the evolution of
the microstructure and mechanical properties of the brittle
CoFeNi
2(Ti3Si5)0.16 EHEA under different annealing condi-
tions was investigated. According to the results obtained, the
following conclusions were drawn.
(1) The as-cast TiSi0.16-EHEA exhibit a fine eutectic
microstructure with connected lamellar + network
morphologies, consisting of FCC and M
16Ti6Si7. Although
the as-cast TiSi0.16-EHEA show high UCS of 1995 ±
50 MPa at room temperature, the connected lamellar + net-
work morphologies of hard M
16Ti6Si7 phase seriously dete-
riorated the fracture plasticity (only 1.3% ± 0.2%).
(2) The TiSi0.16-EHEA possesses excellent thermal sta-
bility. Compared with as-cast, the interlamellar spacing and
network cell diameter of the annealed specimens at 700, 800,
and 900°C for 12 h is almost unchanged. Even when an-
nealing at 1000°C × 12 h, the eutectic microstructure still
maintains lamellar + network morphologies, and only the
size of the eutectic cell began to grow
(3) When annealed at 1100°C × 120 h, the connected la-
mellar + network morphologies of the hard M
16Ti6Si7 phase
were effectively thermally modified into unconnected gran-
ular + irregular morphologies, which resulted in an excellent
combination of high UCS (1980 ± 50 MPa) and good frac-
ture plasticity (16.6% ± 1%) at room temperature.
(4) Upon further increasing the annealing process to
1100°C × 360 h, the hard M
16Ti6Si7 phase severely coar-
sened, which effectively improved high-temperature YS and
softening resistance of the TiSi0.16-EHEA. Compared with
as-cast specimens, the high-temperature YS of the 1000°C ×
360 h annealed specimens are increased from 308 ± 10 and
80 ± 5 MPa to 363 ± 10 and 97 ± 5 MPa at 800 and 1000°C,
respectively.
