ABSTRACT
Multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) have garnered significant attention due to their excep -
tional performance under extreme conditions such as high temperatures and irradiation, yet their 
diffusion behavior and mechanisms at elevated temperatures remain elusive. In this work, we 
investigate the diffusivity of a Ni
x
CoCr alloy system under high temperature conditions as a model 
for MPEAs. Our findings reveal that, the alloys with high mixing entropy exhibit unexpectedly 
diffusivity at ultra-high temperatures, challenging the conventional wisdom that diffusion in 
high-entropy alloys is typically sluggish. Based on tight-binging model, it is revealed that severe 
lattice distortion and electron interaction in high-entropy systems markedly weaken the atomic 
bonding strength. This phenomenon significantly reduces the vacancy formation energy and 
substantially increases the vacancy concentration especially at high temperature, thereby coun -
teracting the inhibitory effect of reduced vacancy jump frequency on diffusion due to lattice 
distortion. This discovery not only provides new insights into the diffusion mechanisms of high- 
entropy alloys under extreme conditions but also holds significant implications for the design and 
optimization of high-performance materials suitable for extreme environments.
1. Introduction
The high-temperature performance of metallic materials is crucial for their application in extreme environments ( Cann et al., 2021 ; 
Jiang et al., 2021 ; Tan et al., 2023 ; Liang et al., 2024 ; Ren et al., 2025 ). In fields such as aero-engine and nuclear reactors, materials 
must withstand ultra-high temperatures and high stress over extended periods. For instance, materials used in aircraft-engine turbines 
must maintain their structural integrity at temperatures reaching 1500 
◦

subjected to temperatures up to 1200

within the material, particularly the movement of lattice point defects such as vacancies, are essential in maintaining structural

In recent years, multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) have emerged as promising candidates for high-end applications due to 
their unique design and exceptional mechanical properties under extreme conditions ( Bertoli et al., 2024 ; Cai et al., 2025 ; Fu et al., 
2025 ; Gan et al., 2024 ; Liu et al., 2024 ; Lu et al., 2020 ; Tan et al., 2024 ; Zhou et al., 2023 ). For example, the NiCoFeAlTiB MPEA 
maintains grain stability and high hardness even after being held at 1323 K for 120 h ( Zhou et al., 2016 ). The nanocrystalline AlCoCuNi 
retains its hardness at 1273 K ( Deng et al., 2020 ), and the CrFeMnNi alloy shows significantly lower radiation damage than pure Ni and 
E90 alloy after heavy ion irradiation across a wide temperature range from 50 K to 773 K ( Parkin et al., 2020 ). These properties stem 
from severe lattice distortion in MPEAs, caused by atomic-scale compositional fluctuations, leading to distinct microstructural 
characteristics and motion patterns compared to traditional alloys ( Chen et al., 2021 ; Dai et al., 2022 ; Fang et al., 2022 ; Guo et al., 
2024 ; Kishore et al., 2023 ; Mehranpour et al., 2024 ; Pandey et al., 2024 ; Zhao et al., 2024 ). For examples, dislocation configurations in 
MPEAs are twisted, and their motion patterns exhibit jerky behavior ( Gan et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2022 ; Wang et al., 2020 ). Grain 
boundary (GB) structures are roughened, and migration mechanisms involve highly random atomic rearrangement processes ( Tan 
et al., 2021 ; Yang et al., 2020 ). All of them profoundly affect the microstructural stability and properties.
The severe lattice distortion effect also significantly influences the formation and motion of point defects. Recent studies have 
shown that the energy distribution of point defects in MPEAs is extensive ( Bacurau et al., 2024 ; Roy et al., 2022 ; Shi et al., 2022 ; Zhao, 
2022 ), and their motion patterns are highly susceptible to atomic-scale non-uniform strain fields ( Lu et al., 2016 ; Shi et al., 2022 ; Xu 
et al., 2022 ), a phenomenon rarely observed in traditional alloys. For example, in the CoCrNiFeMn alloy, the variance of vacancy 
migration energy is as high as 0.31 eV, which is close to 38 % of the mean vacancy migration energy of 0.81 eV ( Thomas and Patala, 
2020 ). In the NiFe ( Lu et al., 2016 ) and NbZrTi ( Shi et al., 2022 ) alloys, the motion of interstitials exhibits atypical local 
three-dimensionalization, differing from the one-dimensional motion patterns in conventional metals. Since solid-state diffusion in 
metals essentially relies on the movement of atomic clusters mediated by point defects ( Liu et al., 2023 ; Skamniotis et al., 2025 ), the 
unique energy characteristics and motion patterns of point defects in MPEAs lead to unknown changes in overall diffusion behavior of 
materials, challenging traditional diffusion theories.
Current computational and theoretical analyses consistently reveal that MPEAs exhibit sluggish diffusion kinetics. This phenom -
enon manifests as a strong correlation between configurational entropy and macroscopic diffusion behavior: alloys with higher mixing 
entropy generally demonstrate reduced atomic diffusivity (defined as the material ’ s inherent capacity to enable atomic migration). 
This viewpoint has been supported by some studies. Typical experimental results include measurements of interdiffusion coefficients in 
the CoCrFeMnNi system by Tsai ( Tsai et al., 2013 ) and Vaidya ( Vaidya et al., 2018b ), which indicate that within the temperature range 
of 700 K to 1400 K, the interdiffusion coefficients of CoCrFeMnNi are generally lower than those of low-entropy alloys (such as 
CoCrFeNi alloys, FeCrNi alloys, and pure metal systems). However, other experimental results suggest that the diffusion rates of 
MPEAs are not always lower than those of low-entropy systems. For example, Vaidya et al. studied tracer diffusion in CoCrFeNi and 
CoCrFeMnNi alloys, finding that the diffusion rate of Mn in CoCrFeMnNi is higher than in CoCrFeMn0.5Ni within the temperature 
range of 0.67*T

alloy systems and temperature ranges.
This study aims to address this gap by investigating the diffusion characteristics of MPEAs under high-temperature conditions using 
Ni

systems at ultra-high temperature. This finding challenges the existing sluggish diffusion hypothesis and highlights the critical role of 
severe lattice distortions in regulating the diffusion mechanism. We hope that this study will provide new theoretical support for the 
design and application of MPEAs, especially in extreme environments.

2. Methodology
2.1. Experiments
Fig. 1 illustrates the core experimental procedure of this study. Initially, pure Cr, pure Ni, and NiCoCr alloy button samples were 
prepared using arc melting technology. Subsequently, homogenization annealing was conducted in a vacuum environment to elim -
inate casting defects ( Fig. 1(a) ). After mechanical grinding and polishing pretreatment ( Fig. 1(b) ), the three groups of samples were 
axially stacked and compacted using hot isostatic pressing (HIP) technology to ensure interface integrity ( Fig. 1(c) ). To prevent 
oxidation, the samples were wrapped in Ti foil and sealed in quartz tubes filled with high-purity Ar gas ( Fig. 1(d) ), followed by 
diffusion annealing experiments at specific temperatures ( Fig. 1(e) ). Finally, cross-sections were obtained through wire cutting, and 
the diffusion zones were systematically analyzed using multi-scale characterization techniques ( Fig. 1(f) ). The detailed process pa -
rameters and equipment specifications for each step will be fully elaborated in subsequent chapters.
Materials preparation. The as-cast Cr and Ni with the purity ≥ 99.9 wt. % (weight percent) are obtained by electrolytic deposition, 
and the as-cast NiCoCr are produced by vacuum induction melting the pure Co, Cr and Ni (purity ≥ 99.9 wt. %) under the argon 
atmosphere. Those ingots are remelted five times to homogenize alloying elements as much as possible, and then casted into a size of 
100 × 50 × 10 mm. The as-cast pure Cr, pure Ni, and NiCoCr are homogenized at 1473 K for 48 h to furthest eliminate the difference in 
grain size, followed by quenching in water. The selection of this homogenization process follows previous literature ( Chen et al., 2021 ; 
Ding et al., 2019 ; Zhang et al., 2024 , 2020 ). The chosen temperature (1473 K) is sufficiently high to promote significant grain growth, 
effectively eliminating grain size disparities between the Ni and NiCoCr samples. This treatment ensures that both systems possess a 
similar microstructural foundation for subsequent diffusion experiments.
Diffusion triples fabrication. The homogenized materials were cut into small disc-sharped samples with the radius of 10 mm using 
electro-discharge machining, and the thicknesses of NiCoCr, Ni and Cr were determined to be 3 mm, 3 mm and 5 mm. Hereon, the 
relative larger thickness of Cr discs was selected to ensure there were two completely separate diffusion boundaries. Subsequently, the 
cut samples used to prepare the NiCoCr-Cr-Ni diffusion triples were mechanically ground to 2000 grit using a series of silicon carbide- 
abrasive papers. The HIP under 50 MPa and 1273 K for 30 mins was performed to machine the diffusion triples with ground discs, 
where the relevant parameters have been confirmed to be suitable for tight interfacial bonding forming between different parts. Those 
diffusion triples were then adequately cleaned, wrapped with Ti foil and sealed into quartz tubes with argon to avoid oxidation. The 
diffusion experiments were performed by annealing with electric furnace at 1173 K, 1223 K, and 1273 K for 48 h to reveal the 
dependence of diffusion on temperature, followed by quenching in water. The diffusion temperatures we selected (1173 – 1273 K) are 
currently the commonly used temperature range (1073 – 1373 K) for diffusion experiments on CoCrNi-based MPEAs ( Tsai et al., 2013 ; 
Vaidya et al., 2018b ). These temperature values fall within the commonly reported range in the literature and can significantly 
promote elemental diffusion. After diffusion processes, the diffusion triples were cut into a semi-cylindrical shape by electro-discharge 
machining for the subsequent characterizations.
Measurement of diffusion depth. The electron probe micro-analyzer (EPMA, JXA-8530F) was employed to measure the concen -
tration of the diffused Cr element at various depths within the diffusion triples. The obtained Cr concentration data were subsequently 
analyzed to generate diffusion depth profiles of the Cr element. The EPMA exhibits a detection precision of 0.01 wt % for elemental 
concentration, demonstrating excellent reliability in quantitative analysis. The cut samples used for EPMA were first sealed in epoxy 
resin, and then mechanically ground to 5000 grits using a series of silicon-carbide abrasive papers, and further polished employing 
silica suspension, followed by cleaning in alcohol and drying in air. The surfaces were freshly prepared to get real Cr element dis -
tribution. All the diffusion depth curves were measured at least three times to eliminate human error.
Microstructural characterizations. The microstructure of the diffusion triple samples was investigated by a scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM, Helios Nanolab G3 UC). A field-emission electron microscopy system (Zeiss Sigma 300 VP) equipped with electron 
backscatter diffraction (EBSD, Symmetry S1) and energy disperse spectroscopy (EDS) was employed to identify the grain structure and 
phase distribution. The samples used for EBSD were prepared by the same process of EPMA. Vibrational polishing was additionally 
applied to relieve the surface stress induced by above metallographic preparation. The high-temperature X-ray diffraction (HTXRD) 
was conducted on a RIKAKU diffractometer (Smartlab, 9KW) with a filtered Cu K α to obtain the parameter of lattice structure. With a

2.2. Molecular dynamics simulations
The atomic simulations are carried out using the large-scale atomic/molecular massively parallel simulator (LAMMPS) ( Plimpton, 
1995 ), and an embedded-atom method interatomic potential for the Ni-Co-Cr system ( Cao et al., 2021 ; Li et al., 2019 ). Both bicrystal 
and single-crystal models are created for simulating the GB and bulk diffusion. As a representative high-angle GB, the symmetrical tilt 
GB Σ 5(210)[001] with the orientation angle of θ = 53.1
◦
is created in the bicrystal model. Such GBs are prevalent in materials and have 
been widely used in numerous studies to evaluate material diffusivity ( Dahmen et al., 1994 ; Frolov et al., 2013a , b ; Medlin et al., 2017 ; 
Starikov et al., 2023 ). The simulation block for GB diffusion simulation has the approximate dimensions of 237.47 × 158.31 × 70.8 Å

Starikov et al., 2023 ; Wang and Wang, 2022 ), where comparable atom counts were employed to ensure the convergence and reliability 
of computational results. To build the MPEA model, a pure Ni model with a face-centered-cubic structure is first created. Then, the 
atoms in this model are randomly selected and replaced by other atoms of specified elements, until the targeted composition is ob -
tained. Atomic configurations are visualized, employing the open visualization tool, OVITO ( Stukowski, 2010 ).
The high-temperature diffusion coefficients are determined through MD simulations following the methodology established by 
( Suzuki and Mishin, 2005 ). The computational protocol initiates with structural optimization using conjugate gradient energy 
minimization. Subsequently, the system undergoes a two-stage relaxation process: First, a 0.5 ns isothermal-isobaric ensemble (NPT) 
equilibration at 0 bar pressure with full periodic boundary conditions (PBC) to relieve residual stresses. This is followed by a 1 ns defect 
equilibration phase, where distinct boundary conditions are applied according to the system. For single-crystal models, PBCs are 
maintained in all directions under NPT ensemble. For bicrystal systems, the simulation employs canonical ensemble (NVT) with 
modified boundary conditions: periodic constraints are retained along the GB rotation axis (Z direction), while free boundaries are 
implemented in the transverse directions to accommodate surface relaxation effects ( Frolov and Mishin, 2009 ; Starikov et al., 2023 , 
2020 ).

4. Conclusion
This study systematically investigates the high-temperature diffusion behavior of MPEAs using the Ni
x
CoCr system as a model. 
Based on experimental measurements and atomic simulations, we reveal a transition from sluggish to rapid diffusion in MPEAs at ultra- 
high temperatures, where the diffusion coefficients of high-entropy systems surpass those of low-entropy systems. This phenomenon 
challenges the conventional sluggish diffusion theory and provides new insights into the diffusion mechanisms of MPEAs across a 
broad temperature range.
A comprehensive analysis of lattice distortion, electron interaction, vacancy formation, and vacancy jump processes highlights the 
dual role of severe lattice distortion in MPEAs: 1) Lattice distortion impedes vacancy jumps by raising the migration barrier, which 
supports the classical sluggish diffusion behavior. 2) Due to the lattice distortion dramatically lowers the VFE, the vacancy concen -
tration is very high at elevated temperatures. At ultra-high temperatures, the substantial increase in vacancy concentration com -
pensates for the reduced vacancy jump frequency, resulting in the anomalously rapid diffusivity observed in high-entropy systems. 
This finding not only redefines our understanding of diffusion in MPEAs but also underscores the critical role of vacancy dynamics in 
governing high-temperature diffusion behavior.
These results have important implications for the design and application of MPEAs in extreme environments, where enhanced 
diffusion properties can be leveraged to optimize material performance.
