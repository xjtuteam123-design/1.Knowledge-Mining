Abstract: A dovetail fixture for fretting fatigue test was de signed. Fretting fatigue 
experiments were performed for Ni-based single crystal (NBSX) superalloys at 600 and 700 ℃
in two secondary orientations, [110] and [010]. The influence of secondary orientation and 
temperature on fretting fatigue behavior was investigated. The crystal plasticity finite element 
(CPFE) simulation was used to study the stress field at the contact region. The result indicates 
that the stress distribution is correlated to frett ing damage, which is consistent with the crack

1. Introduction 
Fretting fatigue occurs when two contact surfaces h ave a small relative amplitude 
displacement subjected to an oscillatory loading [1 , 2]. The damage caused by fretting 
fatigue is a severe threat to the industrial applic ation such as aerospace engineering and 
transportation vehicle. The dovetail joint between the turbine blade and disc is a common 
location of fretting damage, where undergo the cent rifugal and aerodynamic force in the 
turbine engine. Many unexpected failures in the dov etail joint results from fretting fatigue 
have been reported [3, 4]. Hence, fretting fatigue of the dovetail joint has attracted more and 
more researchers’ attention. 
Due to the combination of excellent elevated temper ature mechanical properties and 
corrosion resistance [5, 6], Ni-based single crysta l (NBSX) superalloys are widely used for 
turbine blades and vanes in steam and gas engines. In the past two decades, many fretting 
tests for the dovetail specimens were carried out to simulate the fretting condition of dovetail 
joints, a lot of fretting fatigue data for titanium alloys [7, 8] and steel [9, 10] have been 
obtained. However, the investigation about the fret ting fatigue behavior of NBSX 
superalloys is still scarce. Several researchers have indicated that the elevated temperature at 
turbine blade root would cause a significant change in the fretting behavior. One such test 
was designed and performed by Murthy et al. [11], i n order to measure the fretting fatigue 
life of NBSX superalloys at 610 ℃. Further, as observed by Matlik et al., the fretti ng fatigue 
cracks of NBSX superalloys propagate along {111} plane at 649 ℃ [12]. 
It is well known that the stress response of NBSX s uperalloys be influenced by the

crystalline orientation, since the highly anisotrop ic properties. Fretting stress of the blade 
attachment was quite dependent on primary and secon dary crystal orientation, which was 
obtained by three-dimensional finite element model [13]. Huang et al. investigated the effect 
of crystal orientation on the fretting behavior of NBSX superalloys at room temperature. 
This result showed that fretting in the [110] direc tion appeared to be more resistant to crack 
formation than [010] direction at the higher normal load [14]. The fretting data for Ni-based 
polycrystalline superalloys specimens with controll ed primary orientation of NBSX pad at 
high temperature had shown less scatter than the un controlled ones. However, the scatter 
resulting from secondary orientation still existed [15]. As mentioned above, the effects of 
secondary orientation and temperature needs to be e valuated in greater detail by fretting 
fatigue test. 
The fretting fatigue life prediction can provide mo re information for the industrial 
application. An empirical parameter consisting of s hear stress and slip amplitude was used to 
predict crack initiation location in dovetail speci men [8]. In addition, the critical plane 
parameter has been proposed and validated by some i nvestigators for the fretting fatigue life 
prediction. For example, the Smith-Watson-Topper [16], Fatemi-Socie [17], McDiarmid [18] 
and Findley [19] parameters, which are developed as multiaxial fatigue parameters. However, 
the life prediction becomes more complicated and di fficult on account of the high contact 
stress and severe stress gradient at the contact re gion. In the previous work, the contact stress 
field was obtained by the singular integral equatio ns and finite element analysis using the 
isotropic/anisotropic elasticity [15, 20]. More rec ently, some researchers have indicated that 
the local plastic deformation at the edge of contac t region plays a significant role in the 
fretting fatigue [21]. Crystal plasticity finite el ement (CPFE) method has been used to predict 
the deformation and dislocation under conventional conditions (e.g., tensile [22, 23], 
compression [24], creep [25, 26] and plain fatigue [27, 28], etc.). However, few attempts have 
been made to simulate fretting fatigue using the CPFE method. 
In this paper, fretting fatigue tests with varying crystal orientations were conducted at 
elevated temperature. The influence of secondary or ientation and temperature on the fretting 
fatigue of NBSX superalloys was investigated. CPFE simulation have been conducted to 
obtain the stress field. In addition, critical plan e approaches were used to assess the fretting

2. Materials and experimental fixture 
2.1. Experimental setup 
An experimental fixture is designed to simulate the contact condition between the 
dovetail joint. Fig. 1 shows the photograph and sch ematic of the fretting fatigue fixture. The 
fixture and fretting pads are machined from the Ni- based polycrystalline superalloy, which 
represent the turbine disk. And the dovetail specim en is manufactured from the NBSX 
superalloy, which represents the blade. It is worth noting that these materials are consistent 
with the engine component, hence the elevated tempe rature capability of experimental setup 
is also guaranteed. The apparatus uses a pair of pa ds, each of which is 4.82 mm flat contact 
with 1.6 mm radius at each edge, as shown in Fig. 2 a. The fretting pad is 11.5 mm thick to fit 
the dovetail fixture. The pads could be shifted aft er each test to reduce the expense. Before 
testing, the contact surfaces of NBSX specimen and fretting pads are both ground and 
polished to reduce the finish surface roughness (Ra = 0.8 µm). 
There are severe working conditions at the dovetail joint, and the temperature can reach 
about 600-760 ℃ [29]. For this work, the fretting fatigue behavior is studied when the 
temperature change from 600 to 700 ℃. Two type K thermocouples are used to measure and 
control the temperature, which are placed on one si de of the dovetail specimen in all tests, as 
shown in Fig. 1b. 
 
2.2. Material orientation 
The geometry of dovetail specimen is shown in Fig. 2b, which is representative of the 
actual blade. In general, there are two aspects of crystalline orientation need to be considered 
for NBSX turbine blades, due to the highly anisotropic properties. Firstly, [001] orientation of 
NBSX blade is parallel to the growth direction, whi ch is well controlled by directional 
solidification. This orientation has the lowest mod ulus and superior fatigue resistance. 
Secondly, the secondary orientation normal to the g rowth direction, which is defined as a 
reference line parallel to the blade attachment. In most cases, secondary orientation of the

[110] (Orientation B) as shown in Fig. 3a and 3b. I t is clear that the crystal direction of

investigate the effect of crystal orientation on fretting fatigue behavior. 
 
2.3. Experimental procedure 
 The fretting fatigue tests were carried out at 600 and 700 ℃ using a servo-hydraulic 
fatigue testing machine (Instron 8874). A photograp h of the experimental setup is shown in 
Fig. 1a. Two types of NBSX dovetail sample with different orientation were studied in contact 
against the Ni-based polycrystalline fretting pads. The sinusoidal load was applied in the 
longitudinal direction at frequency of 10 Hz and lo ad ratio R = 0.1. Three load levels were 
chosen in the tests. The fracture surfaces were obs erved by scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM, Quanta 450) after fretting fatigue tests.

3.2. Fracture surface investigation 
Fig. 5 shows the typical fretting scar on dovetail specimen and fretting pads. Notice that 
depression only appears at the edge of fretting pad s, due to the material softening of 
polycrystalline superalloys at elevated temperature . In addition, the fracture surface shows 
that cracks are initiated at the trailing edge of d ovetail specimen in all cases. Similar studies 
were carried to evaluate the fretting fatigue behav ior of dovetail specimens [34-36]. For 
example, Lavella and Botto [36] conducted fretting experiment using a dovetail specimen. 
Their work also shows that crack initiates at the t railing edge. In other words, fracture only 
occur in the dovetail specimens in this study, whic h is contrary to in-service observation of 
the engines. It is worth noting that the fretting b ehavior of turbine disc can be studied, when 
the fretting pads are machined from NBSX superalloy s and the dovetail specimen from disc 
material. Similar works have been published by Fleury and Golden [15, 37]. 
Typical fracture surfaces after fretting fatigue te st of the dovetail specimen with 
orientation B at 600 ℃ can be seen in Fig. 6. It is clear that the fractu re surface can be divided 
into two steps as follows: crack initiation (region I) and crack propagation (region II). Overall, 
these fracture surfaces mainly consist of crystallo graphic planes. These dark marks are 
observed in the crack initiation region, due to the longer oxidation time, as seen in Fig. 6b. 
Further, a typical side view of the crack initiatio n region is shown in Fig. 6c. This shows that 
crack initiates at the trailing edge of contact region, as mentioned above. 
The fretting fatigue crack propagation region of or ientation A at 600 ℃ is shown in Fig.

crystallographic planes) is also present in orienta tion B at 600 ℃. In this condition, crack 
grows along the octahedral (11 1) plane. When the crack propagation is hindered, cra ck 
follows the two conjugate fracture surfaces, which is (1 11) and ( 111) slip planes (Fig. 8b).

4.2. Finite element modeling 
The numerical simulation of dovetail joint is carri ed out using the commercial software 
ABAQUS. Fig. 9 shows the geometry and mesh of 3D fi nite element model. To account for 
the geometric symmetry, only one quarter of the ass embly is created in this model. The 
element type is the eight-node brick elements (C3D8I). Due to the highly nonlinear of contact 
analysis, highly refined meshes are created at the contact region. In order to evaluate the mesh 
convergence, different mesh densities are tested in this study. The convergence criterion is 
defined as the stress change less than 8% when the density is doubled. Therefore, the mesh 
size is reaches 1 µm at the contact region. Overall, the dovetail spec imen contains 37570 
elements. And fretting pad contains 51630 elements. The boundary and load conditions are set 
according to the experimental process as shown in F ig. 9. It is mentioned that two load steps 
are used in this paper. First, a small preload is a pplied along z direction to establish contact 
between the pads and dovetail specimen. In the seco nd step, cyclic load is introduced along z 
direction on the dovetail fixture top surface, and the preload is removed. Frictional contact is 
established by the penalty method, which is based o n the finite sliding and surface-to-surface 
contact assumption. A friction coefficient of 0.25 is used, according to the similar work [46, 
47]. This constitutive model defined in section 4.1 was assigned to dovetail specimen by a 
user material subroutine (UMA T). And the pad is ass igned with an isotropic elastic 
constitutive. These computations are carried out fo r all experimental conditions. 
Newton-Raphson iteration is employed to determine the shearing rates on each slip system.

6. Conclusions 
In this paper, an experimental fixture was designed to simulate contact condition at the 
dovetail joint. The influence of secondary orientat ion and temperature on fretting behavior of 
NBSX superalloys were investigated using fretting f atigue tests. The experimental result 
showed that orientation B exhibits more excellent f retting fatigue life, compared with 
orientation A. Consequently, the secondary orientation should be pay more attention in NBSX 
blade design. The crystallographic fracture model w as observed in both orientations. Fretting 
crack always initiates at the trailing edge of cont act surface and propagates along {111} slip 
plane. In addition, the fretting fatigue life of NB SX superalloys decreases when the 
temperature increases from 600 to 700 ℃. But, the difference in fracture model due to 
temperature change from 600 to 700 ℃ was not significantly. Therefore, the effect of 
temperature on fretting fatigue should be investiga ted in detail, considering complex 
temperature gradient at the turbine blade root. 
The numerical analysis of dovetail joint was carrie d out using CPFE method. This result 
confirmed that equivalent plastic strain can be use d to predict crack initiation site. Then,
