ABSTRACT
The fabrication of Ni-based single-crystal (SX) superalloys through laser directed energy deposition (L-DED) is 
hindered by the high susceptibility of SX structures to hot cracking. Therefore, achieving crack-free SX super -
alloys during L-DED is crucial for advancing the application of this technology in SX turbine blade repair. Based 
on solidification shrinkage and solid-bridging theory, this study systematically investigated the formation 
mechanisms of hot cracks in a multi-pass multi-layer DD6 SX prepared by L-DED through microstructure char -
acterisation and coupled thermal-mechanical simulations. The results demonstrate that the initiation and 
propagation of hot cracks are governed by the overlapping characteristics at the inter-pass and interlayer regions, 
which influence the formation of liquid films and localisation of the stress-strain concentration. Furthermore, the 
results revealed that the formation of stray grains and hot cracking mutually amplified each other. To address 
these challenges, a novel strategy for hot crack suppression is to optimise the dwell time at the inter-pass and 
interlayer regions to regulate the dendrite growth and elemental segregation. Consequently, a three-pass five- 
layer Ni-based single-crystal sample with a width of 2 – 3 mm was successfully prepared, which was free of cracks. 
Moreover, the hot crack suppression method was applied to single-pass thin-wall deposition, achieving a single- 
crystal structure with height exceeding 10 mm and a proportion of over 95 %. The repair quality surpassed the 
requirements for single-crystal blade repair. This study provides new insights into the thermal-mechanical 
mechanisms underlying hot cracking and establishes a scientific framework for mitigating the cracks in L-DED 
DD6 SX, thereby advancing their applicability in high-end component repair.
1. Introduction
Ni-based single-crystal (SX) superalloys are the preferred materials 
for high-pressure turbine blades in advanced aircraft engines and gas 
turbines because of their exceptional resistance to high-temperature 
fatigue, creep, and corrosion [ 1 , 2 ]. SX contains a large amount of 
precious elements (e.g., Re, Mo, and W) [ 3 , 4 ], and the internal cooling 
structure of high-pressure turbine blades is complex. Therefore, SX 
turbine blades are difficult to manufacture and are expensive. The cost 
of the SX turbine blade contributes to approximately 10 – 15 % of the 
total aircraft engine cost. However, SX turbine blades are susceptible to 
cracking, wear, dropping, ablation, and localised damage under adverse 
operating conditions [ 5 , 6 ]. In this context, the SX turbine blades must be 
repaired one to three times before the entire machine is scrapped, and 
the comprehensive maintenance cost of the SX turbine blades is only 
approximately 20 % of its replacement [ 7 ]. Accordingly, developing a 
repair technology for SX turbine blades, restoring their aerodynamic 
shape and mechanical properties, and making them capable of re-service 
is crucial for improving the operation and maintenance of aircraft en -
gines and gas turbines. Owing to its controlled heat input, small heat 
affected zone, and easy automation, laser directed energy deposition 
(L-DED) with a high temperature gradient during the deposition process, 
which is conducive to the epitaxial growth of a single crystal, has 
become a trend in component manufacturing and repair [ 8 – 10 ]. How -
ever, SX with an Al + Ti content greater than 6 % is non-weldable. That 
is, it has a high hot cracking susceptibility, which results in the forma -
tion of hot cracks during L-DED SX [ 11 , 12 ], which seriously hinders the 
application of L-DED in the fabrication and repair of SX turbine blades.

Achieving the epitaxial growth of a single crystal without the for -
mation of stray grains or hot cracks is essential to repair the SX com -
ponents. Indeed, a high-temperature gradient is required to promote the 
epitaxial growth of a single crystal, however, the thermal stress 
accompanying a high-temperature gradient can easily cause hot 
cracking in SX. Ensuring the epitaxial growth of single crystals and 
suppressing the hot cracks appears to be contradictory. Optimization of 
the process parameters and deposition strategy is the easiest way to 
solve the above problem; however, it is difficult to find a process win -
dow that can avoid the formation of hot cracks [ 29 , 30 ]. Kontis et al. [ 31 ] 
prevented the cracking of additive manufacturing nonweldable 
nickel-based superalloys by adjusting the build parameters and found 
that a lower thermal stress helps suppress the formation of a liquid film. 
Adjusting the elemental composition is the most direct method for 
inhibiting hot cracking [ 32 , 33 ]; however, it is difficult to adjust the 
elemental composition to ensure the mechanical properties of the alloy, 
which it is expensive as well. Simultaneously, multi-field assistance has 
achieved a positive effect on the suppression of hot cracks and has 
gradually developed into a trend for hot crack regulation. Dang et al. 
[ 19 ] fabricated a nonweldable Ni-based superalloys through additive 
manufacturing with preheating and obtained a steep temperature 
gradient and appropriate cooling rate in different regions, which resul -

tained columnar dendrites by increasing the base cooling during L-DED 
Inconel 718, while Chen et al. [ 35 ] considered that an epitaxial 
crack-free microstructure is formed under air cooling during L-DED SX,

Considering the great potential of SX repair to reduce the operation 
and maintenance costs of turbine blades, it is necessary to further pro -
vide a simple method of hot crack suppression to promote the applica -

are inconvenient for SX repaired by L-DED, and the formation mecha -

different hot cracking theories and microstructure characterisations has 
not been reported. Simultaneously, some studies indicate that the dwell 
time at the inter-pass and interlayer directly affects the temperature 
gradient, cooling rate, and stress relaxation between the successive 
layers, and a properly optimized dwell time can mitigate excessive re -
sidual stress accumulation and inhibit hot cracking [ 37 – 39 ]. Therefore, 
further investigation is required to elucidate the relationships between 
the different hot-cracking theories and the types of hot cracks, and to 
integrate dwell time considerations into SX fabricated by L-DED.
In this study, by combining hot cracking theory and microstructure 
characterisation, the interplay between the thermal gradients, cooling 
rates, and stress distribution during the L-DED process was discussed, 
emphasising their influence on the evolution of hot cracking. We pro -
pose a new approach for suppressing the hot cracks while maintaining 
the epitaxial growth of a single crystal ( Fig. 1 ). Three-pass five-layer 
DD6 SX deposition by L-DED, multi-scale characterisation of hot 
cracking, and coupled thermomechanical simulations of L-DED DD6 SX 
were performed. Considering solidification shrinkage and solid 
bridging, the mechanisms of the initiation and propagation of hot cracks 
have been demonstrated. Additionally, thermal cycle control at the 
inter-pass and interlayer was proposed to suppress the hot cracks, and 
three-pass five-layer and single-pass multi-layer crack-free DD6 SX were 
fabricated. This study is expected to provide a new perspective for 
suppressing hot cracks and maintaining the epitaxial growth of single 
crystals during L-DED SX to repair turbine blades.
2. Experimental procedures
In this study, all the samples of SX superalloys were prepared using 
an LM-S0303 laser directed energy deposition repair system, which 
contained a 3 kW continuous fibre laser (TRUMPF), a computer nu -
merical control centre (Wuhan Huazhong Numerical Control), a water- 
cooled machine, and a coaxial powder feeding system. The fixed laser 
spot size was 1 mm as provided by the manufacturer. However, for the 
specific process in this study, the measurement of the laser spot for 
positioning and the width of the single-path deposition, the beam spot 
diameter at the focal point was approximately 1.2 – 1.5 mm when the 
distance from the substrate surface to the nozzle was 15 mm. Further -
more, the increment in the z-axis was adjusted according to the macro- 
morphology of the deposition area. Ar should be used as a shielding gas 
to avoid the oxidation of the molten pool and ensure that the coaxial 
nozzle is in a lower-temperature environment.

Based on the single-pass multi-layer deposition experiments and 
their corresponding deposition quality [ 40 ], three groups of processes 
were used for the three-pass five-layer DD6 SX deposition by L-DED 
(L-DED 3P5L-DD6), and the processes are listed in Table 2 . The purpose 
of this three-group experiment was to determine the formation mecha -
nism of hot crack during the L-DED 3P5L-DD6.
During the L-DED 3P5L-DD6, there are only two viable sequencing 
strategies: placing the third pass either at the center or at one side. The 
latter approach causing suppression of epitaxial growth in majority of 
dendrites, which significantly limits the ability to regulate dendrite 
orientation across the entire sample. To promote the epitaxial growth of 
columnar dendrites along the [001] direction and to achieve micro -
structural uniformity, it is essential to adopt a sequential three-pass 
deposition strategy with unidirectional scanning within each layer. As 
shown in Fig. 2 a and b, the serial number and colour indicate the 
scanning sequence of the inter-pass and interlayer, and the initial posi -
tion at the interlayer was alternately changed to restrain the uniform 
geometry of the deposition area. Macroscopic morphology of L-DED 
3P5L-DD6 with three types of deposition processes is depicted in Fig. 2 . 
Owing to the limitation of the laser beam diameter, a non-uniform 
surface of the deposition samples is inevitable. However, identifying 
the formation mechanism and suppressing the hot cracks are the pri -
mary objective of this study; therefore, the uniformity of the deposition 
sample was neglected. As shown in Fig. 2 c – e, the overlapping charac -
teristics at the interlayer were obvious, the surface of the sample had a 
metallic lustre, and no discernible nodules were observed, except in the 
bottom region. Long macroscopic cracks were observed on the surfaces 
of the samples obtained using the three groups of processes. Macroscopic 
cracks in 3P5L-1 sample initiated at the bottom and propagated to the 
top surface of the sample. In addition, the side and top cracks of 3P5L-3 
sample may have connected throughout the entire section. To describe 
the complexity of the mechanism of hot cracking with an increase in the 
deposition height, the 3P5L-1 process was used to prepare the three-pass 
fifteen-layer sample (3P15L), as illustrated in Fig. S2 .

3.2. Orientation and microstructure of the longitudinal and transverse 
crack
To ensure a more comprehensive representation and analysis of the 
microstructural characteristics of L-DED 3P5L-DD6, the orientation in -
formation and microstructure near the hot cracks of the longitudinal 
section A-A ′ and transverse section B-B ′ were analysed, as shown in 
Figs. 4 and 5 , which enabled the characteristics of the hot cracks and 
stray grains to be systematically captured from different perspectives. 
According to the inverse pole figure (IPF) of section A-A ′ in Fig. 4 , the 
propagation direction of the two longitudinal cracks (Crack 1, Crack 2) 
was almost vertical to the fusion line: Crack 1 runs through the three 
deposition layers, spreads downward to reach the fusion line, and the 
upward expansion was hindered by the stray grain of the overlapping 
zone at the inter-pass and interlayer; Crack 2 initiated from the inter- 
dendrite between the columnar dendrite and through the subsequent 
overlapping line. With an increase in the deposition height, columnar 
dendrites with a certain orientation deviation (Grains 1, 2, 3) and 
equiaxed dendrites with a sickle-shaped orientation (Grains 4, 5, 6) were 
formed, as well as other equiaxed dendrites with random orientations. 
Simultaneously, the transverse section B-B ’ of Fig. 5 can noticeably 
display the characteristics of overlapping at the inter-pass and inter -
layer: Crack 1 was mainly initiated at the centre of the bottom, and 
smaller-scale hot cracks were found on the side (Crack 2). It is apparent 
that the stray grains near the fusion line of the middle deposition region 
and the number of stray grains in the region where the hot crack existed 
increased with increasing deposition height. Owing to the overlapping 
effect at the inter-pass and interlayer, representative microstructures 
exist near the longitudinal and transverse cracks. Furthermore, the 
characteristics of the longitudinal and transverse cracks were signifi -
cantly different.
In particular, along the propagation path of Crack 1 in Fig. 5 B-B ’ , the 
stray grains were distributed radially, whereas the epitaxial growth of a 
single crystal occurred in the crack-free region. Simultaneously, the 
deflection of the orientation indicates that when there is no hot crack, 
the clusters of columnar dendrites formed by the epitaxial growth of a 
single crystal can inhibit dendrite growth in random directions, thereby 
inhibiting the formation of stray grains. This is similar to Grains 4, 5, 6 
which have a sickle-shaped orientation distribution in Fig. 4 A-A ’ . This 
implies that the distribution of stay grains is closely related to the for -
mation of hot cracks.

the bottom zone was hindered by the planar crystal, and the inclined

5. Conclusions
This study examines the representative characteristics of hot cracks 
in L-DED 3P5L-DD6. The formation mechanisms of hot cracks were 
investigated in relation to the solidification parameters and overlapping 
at the inter-pass and interlayer based on solidification shrinkage and 
solid-bridging theory. Based on the formation mechanism and effect 
factors of hot crack formation, a new perspective to inhibit hot crack 
formation through the regulation of the thermal cycle was proposed by 
changing the dwell time at the inter-pass and interlayer. The following 
conclusions were drawn. 
(1) The dendrite morphology and misorientation near the hot cracks 
are closely associated with the solidification parameters and 
overlap at the inter-pass and interlayer. Stray grains resulting 
from the non-directional growth of dendrites promote hot crack 
initiation and propagation, whereas hot cracking disrupts the 
epitaxial growth of columnar dendrites. This mutual dependence 
highlights the need to maintain the single-crystal formation and 
suppress hot cracking.

(2) The formation of hot cracks primarily depends on the formation 
and tearing of the liquid film, whereas the stability and length of 
the liquid film are governed by the solidification parameters. 
Additionally, the locations of crack formation and propagation 
directions are dictated by the stress-strain concentration areas, 
which provide mechanistic insight into the interplay of coupled 
thermal-mechanical effects during L-DED DD6.
(3) Increasing the cooling rate at the end of solidification effectively 
inhibits hot crack formation. Simultaneously, a higher tempera -
ture gradient ensures the epitaxial growth of columnar dendrites, 
which prevents the formation of stray grains and reduces sus -
ceptibility to hot crack.
(4) Controlling the thermal cycle, achieved by adjusting the dwell 
time at the inter-pass and interlayer, can effectively modulate the 
temperature gradient and influence elemental segregation. 
Through an optimized thermal cycle, crack-free 3P5L-DD6 and 
single-pass thin-walled DD6 samples were successfully prepared 
by L-DED. Notably, the single-pass thin-walled samples achieved 
a single-crystal structure height exceeding 10 mm, with a pro -
portion exceeding 95 %.
Investigating the application of L-DED for repairing complex curved 
surfaces is particularly relevant to SX turbine blade restoration, as non- 
planar geometries significantly affect thermal cycling, residual stress 
distribution, and hot cracking behavior — critical factors in repair pro -
cesses. While this study advances the fundamental understanding of hot 
crack suppression in L-DED SX, developing a robust suppression strategy 
for actual turbine blade repairs persists scientific and engineering 
challenges. Notably, the approach adopted here follows a conventional 
trial-and-error methodology, employing a pseudo-optimization strategy 
where the thermal cycle serves as the input and hot crack suppression 
effectiveness as the output. The applicability of this method to repairing 
SX turbine blades with complex geometries remains uncertain. Future 
research will integrate the Inverse Heat Placement Problem (IHPP) 
framework [ 55 ], combining thermal modeling, algorithmic control, and 
experimental validation to establish a more rigorous and scientifically 
grounded approach to hot crack suppression. By predicting hot cracking 
regions in SX turbine blade repairs, this methodology will enable pre -
cise, quantitative adjustments to the deposition path, process parame -
ters, and thermal cycle, thereby enhancing the effectiveness and 
applicability of L-DED in practical engineering contexts.
