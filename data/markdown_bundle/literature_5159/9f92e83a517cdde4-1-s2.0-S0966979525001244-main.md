ABSTRACT
In this study, a novel Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo medium heavy alloy (X = 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, wt%) was designed, and the 
impact of Co element on the microstructure and mechanical properties was investigated. The results indicated 
that the addition of Co effectively inhibited the formation of topologically close-packed (TCP) phases, which 
decreased with the addition of Co and reached a minimum in the Ni-42W-10Co-1Mo alloy. The yield strength of 
the Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo medium heavy alloy decreased with increasing Co content at both room temperature and 
high temperatures (750 
◦
C). Specifically, the room-temperature yield strength of Ni-42W-1Mo alloy decreased 
from 516 MPa to 368 MPa after addition of 20 wt% Co, as well as the microhardness decreased from 271 HV to 
233 HV. It was attributed to Co weakened precipitated strengthening effect and induced softening effect. 
Moreover, the Portevin-Le Chatelier (PLC) effect was observed in the Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys during high 
temperature compression, which was attributed to the continuous dissolution and precipitation of the TCP phase 
repeatedly, leading to dislocation pinning and unpinning. Moreover, the Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys showcased 
exceptional ductility, withstood up to 60 % strain without fracturing due to the face-centered cubic (FCC) matrix.
1. Introduction
In recent years, medium-heavy alloys have emerged as a research 
hotspot in defense and advanced materials due to their high density, 
superior mechanical properties, and compositional designability. 
Representative systems such as Ni-W alloys achieve a significant in -
crease in material density (e.g., reaching 12 g/cm
3 
at 42 wt% W) by 
addition high content of tungsten ( > 40 wt%) [ 1 , 2 ], which exhibit 
excellent dynamic mechanical performance, demonstrating critical po -
tential in military applications such as armor-piercing projectiles and 
kinetic energy penetrators [ 3 , 4 ]. For instance, Chen et al. [ 5 ] experi -
mentally demonstrated the exceptional energy absorption capability of 
WFeNiMo alloy during steel target penetration. Moreover, Xie et al. [ 6 ] 
reported that the impact energy absorption efficiency of W-Ni-Fe alloy 
can be improved by 25 % further doping with the rare-earth oxide 
Dy
2
O
3
. However, excessive tungsten content promotes the precipitation 
of α -W phases (body-centered cubic structure) and topologically 
close-packed (TCP) phases (e.g., μ and σ phases), with solidification 
segregation exacerbating TCP phase formation, particularly in large-size 
ingots, severely compromising the hot workability and mechanical 
properties [ 7 , 8 ].
To address TCP phase, high-temperature diffusion annealing and 
elemental doping are the existing strategies. While the former eliminates 
segregation-induced TCP phases, it suffers from high energy consump -
tion and low efficiency [ 9 , 10 ]. The latter, involving elements such as Co 
or Cr to stable matrix phase, proves more effective [ 11 , 12 ]. For example, 
Laplanche et al. [ 13 ] revealed that addition of Co element delays 
σ -phase precipitation kinetics in CrMnFeCoNi alloys, but excessive Co 
element conversely promotes formation of TCP phase [ 14 ]. Similarly, 
Dong et al. [ 15 ] emphasized that electronegativity differences signifi -
cantly influence the stability of TCP phase, necessitating precise 
compositional design to balance phase constituents. Nevertheless, sys -
tematic studies on the “ double-edged" effect of Co element in Ni-W-Co 
ternary systems are deficient, especially regarding the critical Co con -
tent threshold under high-W conditions ( > 40 wt%) is unclear, which 
hinders the development of high density alloys [ 16 ].

2. Experimental
Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys (X = 0, 5, 10, 15, and 20, expressed as 0Co, 
5Co, 10Co, 15Co, and 20Co, respectively, depending on the Co content, 
wt.%) were prepared via the vacuum non-consumable arc melting 
method. High-purity materials (purity > 99.99 wt%) were used and 
purchased from Beijing Dream Material Technology Co., Ltd. The button 
ingots were melted under a vacuum of 5 × 10
 4 
MPa and protected by 
argon gas. The current was slowly reduced to 0 at the end of melting to 
eliminate the disturbance of different solidification conditions, and then 
the consistency of the button ingots was ensured. Moreover, all the 
button ingots were melted six times to obtain a homogeneous chemical 
composition. The diameter of the button ingots was 40 mm in diameter, 
with a height of 15 mm.
Microstructural characterization was carried out using an optical 
microscope (OM) and a Hitachi SU8010 field emission scanning electron 
microscope (SEM), and micro chemical composition was analyzed via an 
EDAX energy spectrometer (EDS). The samples were cut from the center 
of the ingot via wire-electrode cutting. The metallographic samples were 
mechanically ground and polished using standard methods. The samples 
were chemically etched using a solution of 200 ml of hydrochloric acid 
+ 200 ml of alcohol + 10 g of CuCl

cast Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys were also analyzed by X-ray diffraction

was carried out on an FEI TECNAI G2 F30 to further characterize 
microstructure and precipitated phase. The samples were mechanically 
ground to 50 μ m and then ion-thinned via a GATAN 691 Precision Ion 
Polishing System.
The Φ 5 × 7.5 mm cylindrical samples were cut from the center of the 
button ingots for compression tests. The tops and bottoms of the cylin -
drical samples were finely ground to eliminate the effects of scaling and

The relevant empirical parameters are calculated through equations 
(1) – (5) , and the results are listed in Table 2 . The effects of mixing en -
tropy and mixing enthalpy on the alloys are completely different. A high 
mixing entropy makes the alloys more likely to generate stable solid 
solutions, whereas a larger negative mixing enthalpy makes the alloys 
more easily to generate intermetallic compounds. The mixing entropy of 
the Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys increases from 4.4242 to 8.7456 as the Co 
content increases to 20 wt%, whereas the enthalpy of mixing decreases 
from 2.0116 to 1.5692. This indicates that the Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo 
alloys tend to form stable solid solutions by the addition of Co 
element, which is consensus with the experimental results [ 25 ].
However, high content of refractory element in Ni-based alloys tends 
to generate the TCP phase, which is detrimental to mechanical proper -
ties because of its high temperature instability. The formation of TCP 
phase can be predicted using the electronegativity difference, and the 
TCP phase forms when the electronegativity difference is greater than 
0.133 [ 26 ]. The electronegativity differences of the Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo 
alloys are all higher than 0.133, indicating the formation of TCP 
phase. Therefore, based on the relevant empirical parameters it can be 
predicted that the TCP phase can be formed as the Co content increases. 
This is consensus with the XRD results and microstructure observations.

The softening effect of Co element in Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys results 
from its weakened solid solution strengthening and second phase 
strengthening. Moreover, the Co element reduces the stacking fault 
energy and improves dislocation plane slip, leading to a softening effect.
Interestingly, the Portevin-Le Chatelier (PLC) effect was observed in 
the Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloy during the high temperature compression, 
and the stress-strain curves presented as a jagged compression curve. 
The A-type PLC-type curve appeared after reaching the yield stage, and 
the PLC effect appeared to stop briefly when the deformation reached 
approximately 19 %. The PLC effect reappeared at approximately 33 % 
and the sawtooth amplitude became intensity as well as the amplitude 
strength, as shown in Fig. 5 (b). It can be seen that the amplitude dis -
tribution was in accordance with the normal distribution and belonged 
to the B-type PLC curve at a strain of 60 % [ 27 , 28 ]. PLC usually mani -
fests as a sawtooth shape on the stress-strain curves at the microscopic 
scale. It is mainly attributed to the dynamic pinning and unpinning in -
teractions between dislocation motivation and solute atom diffusion 
during plastic deformation. However, the dislocation motivation is 
repeatedly blocked and released by the solute, resulting in periodic 
sawtooth motion in the stress-strain curve [ 29 , 30 ]. The precipitates can 
play a similar role to solute atoms in the PLC effect, continuously dis -
solving and precipitating repeatedly leading to the dislocations pinning 
and unpinning. As a result, the continuous dislocations motivation lea -
ded to formation of sawtooth shape compression curves [ 31 ].
The compression microstructures of Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys 
deformed at room temperature are shown in Fig. 6 . The dendritic 
microstructure undergone significant deformation under an external 
force. Moreover, the deformation degree in the vicinity of precipitated 
phase lower than that in the dendritic region without the precipitated 
phase, indicating the obvious precipitated strengthening. Additionally, 
there were no cracks observed on the surface of the Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo 
alloys, indicating the excellent ductility.

The volume fractions of the TCP phase in Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys were 
1.54 %, 0.79 %, 0.24 % 0.46 %, and 0.57 %, respectively, and the TCP 
phases was minimal in the 10Co alloy. It can be concluded that the 
addition of Co element can inhibit the formation of TCP phase. Co 
element promotes the dissolution of W element and mitigates elemental 
segregation, thereby inhibiting the formation of TCP phases. However, 
the Co element is the forming element of TCP phase and excessive Co 
element promotes the formation of TCP phase. TCP phase is usually a 
hard and brittle second phase, with a crystal structure that differs from 
the matrix, forming a strong stress field and hindering dislocation slip. 
Dislocations need to continue moving through the Orowan mechanism 
or cut through the TCP phase, resulting in an increase in material 
strength (precipitation strengthening) but a decrease in plasticity. 
Moreover, when dislocations are obstructed and accumulate in TCP, the 
local stress significantly increases, which may induce nucleation of 
micro-cracks. However, excessive presence can cause stress concentra -
tion, interface failure, and brittle fracture, significantly promoting crack 
initiation and propagation. Overall, optimal content of Co addition in 
Ni-W alloy is determined as 10 %.
4. Conclusions
In the present study, the Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys were successfully 
designed, and the effects of Co element on the microstructure and me -
chanical properties were studied. The conclusions are summarized as 
follows. 
(1) An inhibitory effect of Co content on the formation of TCP phase 
in NiW alloys is revealed. This is because that Co element can 
promote elements dissolution and inhibit the formation of TCP 
phase. The Co element is the formation element of TCP phase at 
the same time, which promotes the formation of TCP phase. The 
optimum Ni-42W-10Co-1Mo alloy was obtained, and the TCP 
phase was determined to be μ -phase.
(2) The Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys tend to form stable solid solutions 
with the increase of Co content from the aspect of mixing entropy 
and mixing enthalpy. The mixing entropy of the Ni-42W-XCo- 
1Mo alloys increases from 4.4242 to 8.7456 as the Co content 
increases to 20 wt%, whereas the mixing enthalpy decreases from 
 2.0116 to 1.5692.
(3) The softening effect of Co element is obvious in Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo 
alloys compressed at room-temperature and high temperature. 
This is attributed to the weakened solid solution strengthening 
and second phase strengthening. Moreover, the Ni-42W-XCo- 
1Mo alloys show excellent ductility, and there is no fracture 
observed after compression of 60 % regardless of the room tem -
perature or high temperature compression.
(4) The PLC effect was observed in the Ni-42W-XCo-1Mo alloys 
during high temperature compression, which was attributed to 
the continuous dissolution and precipitation of the TCP phase 
repeatedly, leading to dislocation pinning and unpinning.
