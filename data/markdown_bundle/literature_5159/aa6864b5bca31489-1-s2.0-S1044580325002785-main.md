ABSTRACT
Due to excellent overall high-temperature performance, CoNi-based superalloys have drawn much attention as 
high-temperature structural materials. In present work, three CoNi-based superalloys with varying Ta contents 
were studied to investigate their impact on the microstructural evolution and mechanical properties, using atom 
probe tomography (APT), high-resolution X-ray diffraction (HR-XRD), and Density functional theory calculation 
(DFT). The results indicated that Ta increased the lattice misfit between γ / γ’ phases, elevated the γ’ phase solvus 
temperature, and maintained the volume fraction over long-term thermal exposure. APT results revealed that Ta 
enhanced the distribution of W to γ’ phase and elevated the content of Al in γ matrix. The high quantity of Ni 
altered the coarsening rate-limiting elements in γ’ phase from W to Al. The increased lattice misfit and volume 
fraction due to Ta addition accelerate γ’ phase coalescence. Meanwhile, Ta addition reduces the interface width 
between γ / γ’ phases, leading to the increases in interfacial energy, and the acceleration in coarsening kinetics. 
Furthermore, microstructure degradation during thermal exposure accelerates γ’ phase coarsening, leading to a 
reduction in yield strength. This study elucidated the Ta effect on the coarsening mechanism of CoNi-based 
superalloys and its influence on the mechanical properties, by integrating diffusion kinetics and thermody -
namics, which provided significant insights for optimizing the composition and designing CoNi-based superalloys 
with excellent microstructural stability.
1. Introduction
The novel Co-Al-W-based superalloys, strengthened by γ’ phase, 
exhibits excellent resistance to thermal corrosion and fatigue, as well as 
high-temperature mechanical properties, making it a current research 
hotspot in the field of superalloys [ 1 – 3 ]. As relevant studies deepen, the 
new phase strengthened Co-based superalloys has gradually evolved 
from the original Co-Al-W alloy system to the current CoNi alloy system, 
which offers improved comprehensive properties for excellent temper -
ature capacity and high-temperature mechanical properties [ 4,5 ].
Similar to Ni-based superalloys, the high-temperature properties of 
γ’ phase strengthened Co-based superalloys are determined by the γ / γ’ 
two phases microstructure features, especially the solvus temperature, 
volume fraction, and morphology of γ’ phase. Nevertheless, the γ’ phase 
in Co-Al-W alloy system is metastable [ 6 , 7 ], and will be transformed into 
Co
–
Al, Co

W and other secondary phases under long-term thermal 
exposure at high temperatures, which cause deterioration to the me -
chanical properties of the superalloys. The temporal evolution of γ’ 
phase under prolonged thermal exposure fundamentally alters the 
deformation mode selection criteria in superalloys. This microstructural 
degradation mechanism, particularly manifested through rafting 
morphology development, has been correlated with accelerated creep 
progression in CMSX-10 [ 8 ]. Therefore, enhancing the stability of the 
γ / γ’ in Co-based superalloys is significant. For more than half a century, 
the coarsening of γ’ phase has been the focus of research in the field of 
superalloys [ 9 – 11 ].

particularly through controlled the content of refractory elements and 
γ’ -forming elements. The addition of refractory elements such as Mo, Ta, 
Re and Ru improve the high temperature mechanical properties of the 
superalloys, especially the creep properties [ 12 – 14 ]. As a key alloying

solvus temperature of γ’ phase with high volume fraction during thermal

improving the comprehensive high temperature properties of the su -

based superalloys γ’ phase is still controversial [ 18 , 19 ]. Qu [ 20 ] et al. 
and Zhang [ 21 ] et al. reported that Ta could reduce the γ’ phase 
coarsening of Co-based superalloys. However, the work of Li [ 22 ] et al. 
and Xue [ 23 ] et al. showed contrary demonstration that Ta could 
accelerate the γ’ phase coarsening via the increase in lattice misfit and 
interfacial energy. With thermal exposure time prolonging, the micro -
structure evolution of γ’ phase at thermodynamic equilibrium state still 
remains challenging, incorporating the reduction of the sum of the 
interfacial and elastic energies (determined by lattice misfit) [ 24 ]. 
Furthermore, lattice misfit as an important interface characteristic 
parameter of superalloy, is affected by multiple factors such as tem -
perature, composition and interface structure as well as the redistribu -
tion of elements due to γ’ solution at high temperatures [ 25 , 26 ]. In 
addition to the interfacial thermodynamics, the influence of complex 
chemical composition on the diffusion kinetics is also challenging 
[ 27,28 ], especially the diffusion mechanism of multiple components 
across the interface between the γ matrix and γ’ phase.
To design new CoNi-base superalloys for high-temperature structural 
material with regard to both temperature capability and long-term 
mechanical performance, a deeper understanding of interactive effects 
between the microstructure stability and the effect of Ta addition are 
required. To characterize the microstructure evolution and elemental 
distribution in each single phase of all alloys in different state, scanning 
and transmission electron microscopy (SEM, TEM) and atom probe to -
mography (APT) were used. The effects of Ta addition on the micro -
structure stability of γ / γ’ was discussed from the perspectives of 
diffusion kinetics and thermodynamics. Mechanical properties with γ / γ’ 
microstructural evolution after thermal exposure have been studied 
systematically. The deeply understanding of this work will be directly 
related to the optimization of the composition of new CoNi-based su -
peralloys strengthened by γ’ phase.

4. Discussion
4.1. γ ′ volume fraction, solvus temperature, solute partitioning and lattice 
misfit
The main strengthening mechanism of Co-based superalloys 
strengthened by γ’ phase is precipitation strengthening, which is 
strongly dependent on the γ’ phase volume fraction The main purpose of 
optimizing the composition of superalloys is to ensure high volume 
fraction of γ’ phase during long-term service and excellent high tem -
perature mechanical properties of the superalloys. The volume fraction 
of γ’ phase exhibits a monotonic decline with prolonged thermal expo -
sure, attributable to the elevated solubility limit of the γ matrix and 
progressive γ’ phase dissolution, ultimately approaching near- 
equilibrium conditions during the later stages [ 40 ] (after 1000 h for 
0Ta, 1Ta, 2Ta). Notably, the 2Ta alloy demonstrates a γ’ phase retention 
exceeding 61 % even after 1500 h of thermal exposure, with a mere 10 % 
reduction in volume fraction, which highlights tantalum ’ s pronounced 
stabilizing effect on γ’ phase coarsening resistance under prolonged 
high-temperature service.

Ta partitions preferentially to the γ’ phase with a large intrinsic

element, resulting in a significant increase in the γ / γ’ misfit with Ta 
content. The increase of misfit implies that there is a large elastic strain

0.4 %, there is a positive correlation between the interface misfit and 
coarsening [ 44 , 45 ]. At the same time, high interface misfit advances the 
coalescence of γ’ phase.

precipitation strengthening. When the size of the γ’ phase is too large, 
the bypassing of the γ’ phase by dislocations through the Orowan 
mechanism may result in less resistance, and at this time, the mechanical 
properties will significantly decline. Meanwhile, the coarsening 
behavior of the γ’ phase follows the Oswald ripening theory, which 
means that as the γ’ phase coarsens, its number density will also 
decrease. The reduction in the number of γ’ phases indicates a decrease 
in the pinning sites for dislocations, which will also lead to a decline in 
the mechanical properties. It is worth noting that the increase in the size 
of the γ’ phase will also significantly reduce the creep life of the alloy. 
Consequently, the mechanical properties of the 1Ta and 2Ta alloys ex -
hibits a more pronounced deterioration compared to that of the 0Ta 
alloy.
In summary, while the strengthening effect of Ta on yield strength 
should be considered, it is also essential to account for its tendency to 
accelerate coarsening of γ’ phase over extended thermal exposure. This 
requires the prudent control of Ta content and the design of Co-based 
superalloys that optimize performance.
5. Conclusion
In this study, a comprehensive investigation was conducted into the 
Ta effect on the microstructure evolution and mechanical properties of 
CoNi-based superalloys subjected to prolonged thermal exposure. The 
following major conclusion could be drawn: 
(1) APT analysis reveals that Ni, Al, Ti, Ta, and W partitioning to γ’ 
phase, whereas Co partitioning to γ matrix. Ta addition contrib -
uted to the partitioning behavior of W from γ matrix to γ’ phase. 
DSC results indicate that Ta elevates the solvus temperature of 
the γ’ phase ， furthermore, DFT results suggest that the substi -
tution of Al by Ta within the γ’ phase may decrease the formation 
energy, thereby enhancing the thermodynamic stability of the γ’ 
phase.
(2) The Ta addition reduces the diffusion distance across the inter -
face for coarsening and elevates the γ / γ’ interfacial energy, the 
thermodynamic driving force for coarsening. The Ta addition 
therefore accelerate the coarsening rate based on both LSW and 
TIDC theory. The diffusion rate constant of the element revealed 
that the coarsening rate-limiting element in CoNi-based super -
alloys switch from W to Al with Ta addition and accelerate 
coarsening of γ ’ phase during thermal exposure.
(3) The γ’ phase coarsening kinetics were investigated using PSD and 
growth index ( n ). Initially, the γ’ phase coarsening behavior is 
dominantly controlled by LSW theory, but TIDC theory becomes 
predominant with the thermal exposure time prolongs. Ta addi -
tion makes the interface narrow and advances the transition time 
of γ’ phase coarsening mechanism. The coarsening kinetics 
experienced a substantial increase when interface reactions 
become predominant with the thermal exposure time prolongs.
(4) Ta enhances the volume fraction and APB energy of γ’ phase and 
improves yield strength. Microstructure degradation during 
thermal exposure, including the increase in γ’ size and decrease in 
γ’ volume fraction, leads to the decline of the precipitation 
strengthening effect, which is reflected in the continuous deteri -
oration of the mechanical properties. Nonetheless, the coarsening 
acceleration phenomenon caused by the γ’ phase coalescence in 
1Ta and 2Ta alloys pronouncedly deteriorate mechanical prop -
erties with extended thermal exposure. Consequently, Ta can 
improve the mechanical properties of the CoNi-based superalloys 
after heat treatment, but as the thermal exposure time prolongs, 
the trend of performance deterioration may become more 
pronounced.
