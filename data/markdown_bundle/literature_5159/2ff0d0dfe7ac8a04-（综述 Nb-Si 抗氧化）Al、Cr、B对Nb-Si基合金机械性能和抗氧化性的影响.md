Abstract
Nb-Si based alloys are considered as a potential candidates to substitute for Ni-based super alloys. However, its poor 
mechanical properties and high temperature oxidation resistance greatly limit its development and application. Alloying can 
effectively improve the mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of Nb-Si based alloy. In this work, the effects of Al, 
Cr, B elements addition on the mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of niobium-silicon ternary alloys are sum -
marized. The microstructure, oxidation behavior and mechanical properties of various alloys were analyzed and compared. 
Moreover, the fracture and oxidation failure mechanism of the alloys are also summarized. Finally, the development trend 
of ternary Nb-Si based oxidation-resistant alloys in the future has been prospected.
Keywords Nb-Si based alloys · Mechanical properties · Oxidation resistance · Alloying · Review
1 Introduction
With the rapid development of aerospace science and tech-
nology, advanced aero engine requires higher performance 
of high temperature resistant structural materials [1–8]. The 
performance of traditional Ni-based superalloys has gradu-
ally failed to meet the requirements of higher temperature 
[9–12]. Therefore, it is urgent to develop a new type of 
high temperature resistant structural material. Niobium and 
niobium-based alloys are ideal candidates due to their low 
density and excellent high temperature strength [13–15]. The 
properties of nickel-based alloy and niobium-based alloy are 
compared as shown in Table 1. It can be seen that niobium-
based superalloy has lower density and higher operating 
temperature and yield strength. Unfortunately, their poor 
oxidation resistance at high temperature has greatly hindered 
their application [16– 19]. Niobium is easily oxidized into 
NbO, NbO2 and Nb2O5 at high temperature, which leads 
to rapid volume expansion, a large number of cracks and 
holes, and finally causes oxidative disintegration and failure 
of the alloy [14, 18, 20]. The researchers found that alloying 
can alleviate this problem to some extent. The addition of 
solute alloying elements can form a solid solution, change 
the diffusion characteristics of the oxide layer, and reduce 
the diffusion velocity of oxygen. When the contents of solute 
elements exceeds the solid solubility of the matrix, the oxide 
and amorphous surface layer with low oxygen contents will 
be formed, which has a beneficial effect on the performance 
of the oxide layer. Furthermore, the alloying elements may 
generate an intermediate phase whose thermal expansion 
coefficient is between the matrix material and the oxide 
layer, which can relieve the internal stress caused by the 
different thermal expansion coefficient of the matrix material 
and oxide layer, thus reducing the generation of cracks and 
improving the oxidation resistance. The study of Lu et al. 
[20] shows that the addition of silicon is beneficial to the 
formation of high hardness Nb5Si3 phase. Besides, the study 
of Deng et al. [21] shows that Si could replace Cr in NbCr2 
due to the similar atomic size of Si and Cr, and ternary Nb-
Si-Cr alloy has better fracture toughness, hardness (HV) and 
thermal stability than Nb-Cr alloy. Therefore, Si element

2 Nb‑Si‑Al ternary alloys
2.1 Microstructure and mechanical properties
The Al element has good ductility, and it is easy to form 
protective Al2O3 film after oxidation [11, 21, 22]. Thus, 
researchers consider adding Al to ameliorate the fracture 
toughness and high temperature oxidation resistance of 
Nb-Si alloys. Kashyap et al. [22] prepared the Nb-18.79Si 
(at%) and Nb-12.7Si-9Al (at%) alloys by vacuum arc melting 
process, and the effect of Al on the mechanical properties of 
the alloy was studied. The backscattered electron images of 
the two alloys are shown in Fig. 1a, d, respectively. It can be 
seen that both of the alloys are composed of white phase and 
light gray phase. The second phase (white) in the eutectic 
of the binary alloy is rod-like, and embedded in niobium 
solid solution (the following are represented by Nbss), as 
shown in Fig. 1b, while the as-cast ternary alloy has a finer 
eutectic mass structure than the binary alloy, as shown in 
Fig. 1d. Figure  1a1, d1 shows the bright-field transmission 
electron microscope images of the as-cast Nb-18.79Si and 
Nb-12.7Si-9Al alloy eutectic region, respectively. It is clear 
that the second phase in eutectic of the former is granular 
or rod-like, while the eutectic precipitation of the latter is 
layered. The EDS (Energy Dispersive Spectrometer) map-
ping of Nb, Si, Al elements in Nb-12.7Si-9Al alloy is shown 
in Fig. 1e, obviously, Nb and Si are distributed in layers in 
the alloy, while aluminum is evenly distributed in the two 
phases. Figure  1c, f presents the Vickers hardness testing 
images of the two alloys at room temperature, and the hard-
ness values of the primary Nb3Si and Nbss phases in the two 
alloys are 1256 ± 50 Hv and 1050 ± 40 Hv, respectively. The 
hardness value of Nbss varies with different solute elements 
[23, 24]. The generally low hardness of Nbss is attributed 
to it is prone to dislocation movement, with the addition of 
aluminum, the hardness of the alloy can be improved by 
solution strengthening.

ment on the fracture toughness of the alloy at different tem-

12.7Si-9Al alloy at room temperature (RT) and 700 °C are

are mainly two forms of cracks, Fig. 2a1 shows a long crack

the strength and toughness of Nb-Si alloy at the tempera-

2.2 Oxidation behavior and mechanism
After the oxidation of aluminum element, a dense Al2O3 film 
will be formed on the surface, which has a certain protec-
tive effect on the substrate. Kashyap et al.[22] prepared Nb-
18.79Si (at%) and Nb-12.7Si-9Al (at%) alloys by vacuum 
arc melting method, and the impact of Al element on the 
antioxidation of Nb-Si alloy was studied. The XRD (X-ray 
diffraction) patterns of Nb-18.79Si and Nb-12.7Si-9Al at 
different oxidation temperatures are shown in Fig.  3a–c, 
respectively. It can be seen that the as-cast Nb-Si binary 
alloy is mainly made up of Nb and Nb3Si, while the ternary 
alloy contains βNb 5Si3 except the former two phases. The 
oxidation products of the binary alloy at 600–700 °C are 
mainly Nb2O5, and the peak of the oxide spectrum is strong, 
indicating that the surface of the alloy is seriously oxidized, 
while the peak of Nb2O5 spectrum of the ternary alloy is 
weak. The predominant phase Nb2O5 and NbO2 are observed 
in the oxide scale of the two alloys at 1000 °C. The weight 
change curves of the two alloys are shown in Fig.  3d, e, 
respectively. Figure 3d shown that the binary alloy has linear 
weight gain at 600 °C and 1000 °C while the weight gain at 
700 °C is a parabola. Furthermore, the weight change curves 
of the ternary alloy at 600–700 °C are small enough even can 
be ignored, as show in Fig. 3e. Moreover, the weight change 
at 1000 °C is linear, but the growth rate is smaller than the 
Nb-18.79Si alloy. Meanwhile, the oxidation resistance of the 
ternary alloy is improved with Al addition at 600–700 °C 
However, the weight change of the two alloys is more than 
100 mg/mm 2 at 1000 °C so the improvement of oxidation 
resistance is not obvious.
Figure  4 shows the element distribution of alloys after 
oxidation at 700 °C for 1 h in air by EPMA (Electron Probe 
Micro-analysis). It is obvious that the sample after oxidation 
consists of an outer oxide layer, an intermediate transition 
layer, and an internal substrate structure, as show in Fig. 4b. 
and the analysis results show that the black oxide composi-
tion of the outer layer is Nb-24Si-13Al-20O (at%), which 
contains a large amount of Si, Al and Nb. The inconsistency 
of thermal expansion coefficient between the oxide layer 
and the substrate leads to the internal stress, and the oxide 
layer cracks, and finally loses its protective effect on the 
substrate [23]. The element distribution of the alloy shows 
in Fig. 4c, d, while the right side of the black dotted line is 
close to the oxidized surface, illustrates that a lot of oxy -
gen is dissolved in the Nb dendritic, and the closer to the 
oxide surface, the higher the contents of Si and O. Moreover, 
the solid solubility of O is improved and the formation of 
Si, Al oxide is accelerated with Al addition. So, the oxida-
tion resistance of the alloy is optimized. Murayama et al.

3 Nb‑Si‑Cr ternary alloys
3.1 Microstructure and mechanical properties
Cr is a solute element that binds to O more readily than Nb. 
[31–38], CrNbO4 and Cr2Nb with better oxidation resistance 
can be formed after Cr is added to Nb alloy. Appropriate 
amount of Cr can also increase the ductile–brittle transi-
tion temperature and improve the machining properties of 
the alloy. Therefore, the researchers consider adding Cr 
element to improve the mechanical properties and oxida-
tion resistance of Nb-Si alloy. Zhang et al. [39] prepared 
ternary Nb-Si-Cr alloy by spark plasma sintering (SPS), and 
studied its fracture behavior by three-point bending test. The 
experimental results are shown in Fig.  5a–c. It is obvious 
that different contents of Nb, Nb5Si3 and Cr2Nb correspond 
to different fracture modes. Due to the good ductility of Nb, 
the alloy has obvious tearing dimples of ductile fracture as 
shown in Fig.  5a. While the contents of Nb5Si3 and Cr2Nb 
phases increase gradually in Fig.  5b, c, the hardness and 
brittleness are enhanced with the solution strengthening of 
Cr, and no dimples were observed.
The strains corresponding to different compressive 
stresses of the Nb-Si-Cr alloy under 1250 °C and 1350 °C 
are shown in Fig.  5d, e, respectively. It is clear that the 
stress–strain curves show a monotonous linear growth at the 
beginning, followed by a short nonlinear hardening stage, 
and the alloy yields slowly after the compressive stress 
exceeds the peak value. The compressive yield strength 
(σ0.2) and the maximum compressive strength (σ max) of 
the alloy increases with the increase of the volume frac-
tions of Nb5Si3 and Cr2Nb, but decreases with the increase 
of temperature. When the volumefrac of Cr2Nb increases 
from 10 to 50%, the Vickers hardness value (H v) increases 
from 367 ± 50 to 700 ± 65, but the fracture toughness (K Q) 
decreases from 6.7 ± 0.6 to 3.6 ± 0.7. Zhang et al. [40] also 
proved that Cr can improve the hardness of Nb-Si based 
alloy but reduce its room temperature ductility. Besides, the

3.2 Oxidation behavior and mechanism
The Cr element is often used to improve the oxidation resist-
ance of alloys [41, 43, 44]. The Cr2Nb phase with low creep 
rate and high oxidation resistance can be formed by adding 
Cr into Nb-Si alloy. [45–51]. Moreover, when Si content is 
sufficient, the oxidation-resistant CrNbO4 phase is more eas-
ily produced [50–55], which can inhibit the diffusion of oxy-
gen and improve the oxidation resistance of Nb-Si alloy[51, 
56]. Jiang et al. [57] prepared the Nb-6Si-xCr (x = 25, 30, 
40, 45 at%) alloy with the method of vacuum non-consum-
able arc melting (VCAM), the alloy was then oxidized at 
1250 °C for 7 h. The backscattered electron (BSE) images of 
the alloy are shown in Fig. 6a–d, it can be seen that there are 
mainly Nb, Cr2Nb and Nb5Si3 phases. Figure 6e–h shows the 
cross-sectional BSE images of the alloy after oxidation, and 
the oxide layer becomes continuous and compact when the 
Cr contents reaches 30 at%, the outer oxide layer is mainly 
a mixture of CrNbO4 and Nb2O5, while the inner layer is 
mainly CrNbO4.
The XRD analysis of these alloys before and after oxi-
dation is shown in Fig.  7a, b, respectively. As can be seen 
from Fig.  7a, the alloy is mainly composed of Nb, Cr2Nb 
and Nb5Si3, and the peak strength of Cr2Nb increases with 
the increase of Cr content. Figure  7b shows that the oxida-
tion products are CrNbO4 and Nb2O5, and the peak intensity 
of CrNbO4 increases with the increase of Cr content. The 
weight change of the alloy after oxidation at 1250 °C in 
static air is shown in Fig. 7c. The weight gain curve is para-
bolic, and the weight gain rate decreases gradually with the 
increase of Cr content, so the oxidation resistance of the 
alloy gradually increases.

4 Nb‑Si‑B ternary alloys
4.1 Microstructure and mechanical properties
The researchers found that the addition of appropriate 
amounts of B to Mo-Si alloys resulted in the formation 
of viscous borosilicate glass, which effectively filled the 
cracks and holes caused by oxidation and volatilization 
[30, 32, 43, 58, 59] and promoted the formation of contin-
uous and dense silica oxide films [31]. At the same time, 
proper amount of boron can improve the room tempera-
ture fracture toughness of the alloy [33, 59–64]. Therefore, 
the addition of B is considered to improve the mechani-
cal properties of Nb-Si alloy. Zhang et al. [30] repared 
Nb-22Ti-16Si-5Cr-4Hf-3Al-xB (x = 0, 2, 5, 10 at%) alloy 
by vacuum non-consumable arc smelting. The BSE micro-
graphs of these alloys are shown in Fig.  10a through (d). 
combined with SEM and XRD analysis, the 0B alloy con-
sists of Nbss, α (Nb, X)5Si3 and eutectic of both, while the 
2B alloy is make up of Nbss, γ (Nb, X)5Si3 and their eutec-
tic, furthermore, the 5B and 10B alloys consist of Nbss, 
(Nb, X) 3Si, γ(Nb, X) 5Si3 and the eutectic of Nbss/γ (Nb, 
X)5Si3. In addition, Nbss, (Nb, X) 5Si3 and Cr2(Nb, X) 
eutectics are present in these alloys, as shown in the white 
box. The fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloy is largely

Nb-Si based superalloys have broad application prospects,

Adding alloying elements (such as Al, Cr and B) can solve 
this problem to a certain extent. Al can significantly improve 
the fracture toughness of the alloy. After oxidation, Al will 
form a dense Al2O3 film, and the alloy has a good oxida -
tion resistance at 600–700 °C. The addition of Cr increases 
the hardness of the alloy, but decreases the fracture tough-
ness and ductility of the alloy. Meanwhile, it can form the 
antioxidation CrNbO4 phase in alloys and inhibit the diffu-
sion and dissolution of O atoms, and enhance the oxidation 
resistance of the alloy. Furthermore, Cr2O3 has a protective 
effect below 1100 °C But, the volatile CrO3 will be gener -
ated and accelerate the oxidation process over 1100 °C The 
contents of B elements in the alloy is less than 2%, the frac-
ture toughness of the alloy improves with the increase of its 
contents, while it has the opposite effect when the contents 
is 5 at% above. Meanwhile, the addition of B elements to 
Nb-Si alloy can form the viscous glassy borosilicate, which 
can fill the cracks or volatile pores, and thus, improve the 
high-temperature oxidation resistance of the alloy.
From what has been discussed above, Al, Cr, B and other 
alloying elements can improve the high temperature oxida-
tion resistance of niobium alloys, but usually at the expense 
of its mechanical properties such as toughness and ductility. 
This means it is difficult to balance the mechanical proper -
ties and oxidation resistance of niobium alloy by alloying. 
Because the performance of ternary niobium-silicon alloy 
still cannot meet the requirements of production and use, 
the future research direction of high temperature resistant 
niobium alloy will turn to multiple niobium alloy or high 
entropy alloy (high entropy alloys with more than five kinds 
of alloying elements and the volume fraction of each alloy-
ing element in the range of 5–35%), by adding other alloying 
elements to improve the mechanical properties and oxidation 
resistance of niobium alloy. The establishment of mathemat-
ical model for numerical simulation and quantitative study 
of the oxidation behavior of the alloy will be very helpful to 
the study of the alloy. It is worth mentioning that the metal 
surface coating technology is an effective method to improve 
the oxidation resistance without damaging the mechanical 
properties of the matrix, which has great research potential 
and will become a major research focus in the future.
