a b s t r a c t 
Eight multi-elemental Nb-Si-based alloys with various Mo, V and Zr contents were prepared by vacuum 
non-consumable arc melting. The cooperative alloying effects of Mo, V and Zr on the arc-melted and 
heat-treated microstructure, mechanical properties as well as oxidation resistance at 1250 °C of the alloys 
were evaluated systematically. The results show that except for adding Mo solely, additions of Mo, V and 
Zr change the microstructure from eutectic to hypereutectic. The additions of Mo, V and Zr suppress the 
formation of α(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 (“X” represents the alloying elements that substitute for Nb in the lattices), 
whilst promoting the formation of γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 . The heat treatment at 1450 °C for 50 h promotes the 
formation of (Nb, X) 3 Si phase in the Zr-containing alloys. Alloying with either Mo or Zr improves, and 
their composite additions more obviously improve the compressive yield strength at 1250 °C as well as 
the microhardness of γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 . The room temperature fracture toughness of the alloys is enhanced 
by sole and composite additions of V and Zr, while it is deteriorated by the addition of Mo. The sole 
addition of Mo, V or Zr improves the oxidation resistance at 1250 °C, the composite additions of V with 
Mo/Zr (especially V-Mo-Zr) degrade the oxidation resistance at 1250 °C.

1. Introduction 
Nb-Si-based alloys, also known as niobium/silicide in-situ com- 
posites, essentially consist of silicide and niobium solid solution 
(Nbss) [1] . The ductile-metallic Nbss phase mainly provides the 
room temperature fracture toughness, and the hard-brittle sili- 
cide can ensure the alloys to service at a temperature as high as 
120 0–140 0 °C that has exceeded the operating limit temperature 
( ∼1150 °C) of the most advanced Ni-based single crystal superal- 
loys [ 1 , 2 ]. The outstanding high-temperature mechanical proper- 
ties of Nb-Si-based ultrahigh temperature alloys encourage them 
to become a potential alternative for the superalloys to apply at 
higher temperatures in the turbine engines. However, the insuf- 
ﬁcient room temperature fracture toughness and inferior high- 
temperature oxidation resistance of Nb-Si-based alloys inhibit their 
practical applications [1–4] . After three decades of alloying re- 
search, promising multi-elemental alloy systems of Nb-Ti-Si-Cr-Al- 
Hf have been developed, but the oxidation resistance and room 
temperature fracture toughness of Nb-Ti-Si-Cr-Al-Hf alloy systems 
still do not meet the demands of aeroengines [ 3 , 5 , 6 ]. Thus, further

Up to now, the alloying elements Mo, V and Zr have been 
substantially investigated due to their positive effects on the me- 
chanical properties and oxidation resistance of ternary/quaternary 
Nb-Si-based alloys [7–17] . It has been reported that [ 13 , 15 , 16 ] al-

dation resistance and mechanical properties at elevated temper- 
ature, but degraded the room temperature fracture toughness of 
ternary/quaternary Nb-Si based alloys. While the addition of Zr 
or V was generally beneﬁcial for the room temperature fracture 
toughness of ternary/quaternary Nb-Si-based alloys [ 7 , 11 , 17 , 18 ]. 
Besides, our previous works [ 19 , 20 ] have also revealed that the 
compositely alloying with V and Zr additions in multi-elemental 
Nb-Si-based alloys improved the room temperature fracture tough- 
ness and high-temperature compressive strength, but degraded the 
oxidation resistance performance of the alloys, while the combined 
additions of Mo and Zr ameliorated the high-temperature oxida- 
tion resistance. Therefore, it is reasonable to expect that the com- 
posite additions of Mo, V and Zr in multi-elemental Nb-Si-based 
alloys have the potential to improve the comprehensive perfor- 
mance including room and high-temperature mechanical proper- 
ties and oxidation resistance. Additionally, in multi-elemental Nb-

2. Experimental procedures 
Eight multi-elemental Nb-Si-based alloys with the additions 
of Mo, V and Zr were designed and prepared by vacuum non- 
consumable arc melting under an argon atmosphere in a water- 
cooled copper crucible. Their nominal chemical compositions have 
been listed in Table 1 . For convenience, those Nb-Si-based alloys 
were brieﬂy described as 0Mo-0V-0Zr, 5Mo-0V-0Zr, 0Mo-3V-0Zr, 
0Mo-0V-4Zr, 5Mo-3V-0Zr, 5Mo-0V-4Zr, 0Mo-3V-4Zr and 5Mo-3V- 
4Zr alloys in the present work, respectively. Each ingot was melted 
four times to ensure its chemical homogeneity. The constant arc 
melting parameters such as maximum electric current, argon pres- 
sure and circulating water pressure in the copper crucible were ap- 
plied during the melting and cooling processes of each ingot. 
Four cubic samples with dimensions of 7 × 7 × 7 mm 3 were 
cut from the same central position of each ingot by electro- 
discharge machining (EDM). Two of them were for arc-melted and 
heat-treated microstructural analysis, respectively. The heat treat- 
ment was conducted at 1450 °C holding for 50 h in a high vacuum 
resistance heating furnace, and then, the furnace cooled down to 
room temperature. The samples for heat treatment were wrapped 
with Ti foils and surrounded by sponge titanium in the furnace to 
avoid oxidation during heat treatment. 
The other two cubic samples were used for examining oxidation 
resistance at 1250 °C for 1 and 20 h in an open-ended tube electric 
furnace. The samples were placed into an alumina crucible so as to 
obtain the total mass changes including the possibly spalled scales. 
For avoiding moisture, the alumina crucibles had been heated at 
800 °C for 3 h before the oxidation test. The mass changes and 
accurate dimensions of samples were measured by an electronic 
balance with an accuracy of 0.0 0 01 g and an vernier caliper with 
an accuracy of 0.02 mm, respectively. 
Vickers microhardness of phases or microstructural constituents 
in the alloys was measured by an HXP-10 0 0TM hardness machine 
with a load of 100 gf or 25 gf for a holding time of 15 s. At 
least ten measurements were conducted for each phase in the al- 
loys. Three single-edge notched bending specimens (SENBs) with 
dimensions of 30 × 5 × 2.5 mm 3 were prepared by Electronic 
Discharge Machining (EDM), and were tested to obtain the av- 
erage values of room temperature fracture toughness, K Q , in an 
electric universal mechanical testing machine (Instron-3382). The 
calculation formula of the K Q value was mentioned in Ref. [22] . 
The notch of SENBs was 2.5 ± 0.1 mm. The cross-head speed was 
0.2 mm/min. The cylindrical specimens with dimensions of 8 mm 
in diameter and 12 mm in length were cut by EDM and were 
tested to obtain the high-temperature compressive yield strength 
in Gleeble 3500 testing machine. The hot compression test was 
conducted at 1250 °C with a strain rate of 0.005 s –1 and a height 
reduction of 30%. 
X-ray diffraction analysis (XRD, Panalytical X’Pert Pro, Cu K α) 
and electron back-scattered diffraction (EBSD, Tescan Mira 3 XMU) 
were used to identify the constituent phases and oxidation prod- 
ucts. Microstructures of the specimens were observed by scan- 
ning electron microscopy (SEM, Tescan Vega 3 LMU). The chemical 
compositions of the constituent phases were analyzed by energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS, Inca X-sight). Quantitative metallo- 
graphic analysis of the amount and size of the constituent phases 
and oxides were performed by Image-Pro Plus version 6.0 high- 
resolution imaging analysis software. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructures 
Fig. 1 (a) shows the XRD patterns of the eight arc-melted Nb-Si- 
based alloys. It can be seen that all arc-melted alloys mainly con- 
sist of Nbss and 5–3 silicides. The 3–1 silicides are not detected in 
the alloys, which is mainly because the alloying elements Ti and Hf 
change the solidiﬁcation path of multi-elemental Nb-Si-based al- 
loys, causing the Nb 5 Si 3 phase directly solidiﬁes from the melt in- 
stead of Nb 3 Si phase [23] . It is observed that the types of 5–3 sili- 
cides in 0Mo-0V-0Zr and 0Mo-3V-0Zr alloys are presented in the 
forms of α(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 (tetragonal, D8 l structure) and γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 
(hexagonal, D8 8 structure), while the other six alloys only contain 
the type of γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 , indicating that adding Mo or Zr solely, 
and cooperatively adding Mo, Zr and V in multi-elemental Nb-Si- 
based alloys enhance the stability of metastable γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 phase 
and suppress the formation of α(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 phase ( γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 
phase is generally regarded as metastable phase and can trans- 
form to α(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 in Nb-Si-based alloys [24] ). Besides, com- 
pared with the diffraction peaks in XRD patterns of α(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 
in 0Mo-0V-0Zr and 0Mo-3V-0Zr alloys, it is obvious that the inten- 
sity and quantity of diffraction peaks of α(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 in the 0Mo- 
3V-0Zr alloy are lower and fewer than those in the 0Mo-0V-0Zr 
alloy, indicating that sole addition of V has a weaker effect on im- 
proving the stability of metastable γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 phase. It is well 
known that element Zr can only form a D8 8 structure of 5–3 sili- 
cides, and Mo and V can form 5–3 silicides with D8 l , D8 m (such as 
βNb 5 Si 3 phase) and D8 8 structure [25–28] . Pan et al. [27] calcu- 
lated the formation enthalpy of three types of Mo 5 Si 3 and demon- 
strated that the Mo 5 Si 3 with hexagonal structure (D8 8 ) was more 
stable than the other two types of Mo 5 Si 3 . Thus, it is reasonable 
to infer that once the metastable γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 form from melt in 
the present work, alloying elements Zr and Mo can occupy the Nb 
sites in γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 and improve their stability. Conversely, based 
on the report of Tao et al. [26] , the V 5 Si 3 with a D8 m structure was 
the stable phase among the three structures of V 5 Si 3 . As expected, 
element V should promote the formation of β(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 or have a 
minor inﬂuence on the other two types of 5–3 silicides. However, 
in this work, the sole addition of V in multi-elemental Nb-Si-based 
alloys also shows more or fewer effects on improving the stability 
of γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 . The interaction effects between V and other alloy- 
ing elements in the present multi-elemental system can arise the 
weaker effect of V on the selection of lattice type of 5–3 silicides 
and will be discussed later.

Fig. 1 (b) shows the XRD patterns of the eight Nb-Si-based al- 
loys after heat treatment at 1450 °C for 50 h. It can be seen that 
the constituent phases in the alloys do not change after heat treat- 
ment. The diffraction peaks of α(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 still are not detected in 
the XRD patterns of the alloys that only contain γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 be- 
fore heat treatment, indicating that the transformation from γ(Nb, 
X) 5 Si 3 to α(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 , which is supposed reasonably to occur in 
Nb-Si-based alloy during heat treatment [10] , does not happen in 
the present multi-elemental Nb-Si-based alloys with the additions 
of Mo, V and Zr, which again demonstrates that the solely and 
compositely alloying with Mo, V and Zr in the multi-elemental Nb- 
Si-based alloys enhance the stability of γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 . 
Fig. 2 shows the backscattered electron (BSE) images of the mi-

that 0Mo-0V-0Zr and 5Mo-0V-0Zr alloys exhibit a fully Nbss/(Nb,

tectic structure mainly consisting of primary γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 blocks

3V-4Zr alloys, indicating that the composite additions of V and 
Zr cause a larger hypereutectic degree than alloying with V or Zr

and γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 , respectively, as indicated in Fig. 2 (a1, c1). The ex- 
istence of γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 and α(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 has also been conﬁrmed 
by their XRD patterns, as shown in Fig. 1 . 
It can be seen from Fig. 2 that the external morphologies of pri- 
mary γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 blocks mainly present near hexagonal shapes in 
all arc-melted alloys. However, some primary γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 blocks

3V-4Zr alloys) show H-shaped or irregularly hexagonal morpholo-

V could cause serious constitutional supercooling and alert the 
growth rates of different crystallographic planes of silicides to form 
H-shaped or irregularly hexagonal γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 during solidiﬁca- 
tion. The eutectic morphologies present more complex changes in

rate of silicides and thus leads to the formation of the denser inner 
layer of scale in the 0Mo-3V-0Zr alloy. 
However, the further addition of Mo in the existence of V dete- 
riorates the oxidation resistance, as shown by the increase in mass 
gain per unit area of 5Mo-3V-0Zr alloy ( Fig. 10 ). The reasons for 
the negative cooperative effect of Mo and V additions on the oxi- 
dation resistance can be conﬁrmed by the change in the crucible’s 
color. It can be seen from Figs. 10 and 12 that the color of cru- 
cibles to place 5Mo-3V-0Zr and 0Mo-3V-4Zr alloys changes from 
white to orange after oxidation at 1250 °C. It is well known that 
the oxide V 2 O 5 general has an orange color and a lower melting 
point (675 °C) [ 36 , 37 ]. Therefore, the crucibles with orange color 
are possibly contaminated by the liquid V 2 O 5 which forms and 
melts during the oxidation of 5Mo-3V-0Zr and 0Mo-3V-4Zr alloys 
at 1250 °C. The formation of liquid V 2 O 5 in the V-containing Nb- 
Si-based alloy systems has also been conﬁrmed by our previous 
work [29] . Compared with the crucible to place 0Mo-3V-0Zr alloy 
showing a consistent color before and after oxidation at 1250 °C 
( Figs. 10 and 12 ), it can be concluded that the cooperative effects 
of Mo-V and V-Zr in Nb-Si-based alloys promote the formation of

In summary, the cooperative additions of Mo and Zr in multi- 
elemental Nb-Si-based alloys are promising to improve the high- 
temperature oxidation resistance, while the cooperative additions 
of V with Mo or/and Zr in the alloys would substantially deteri- 
orate the high-temperature oxidation resistance of the alloys and 
should be avoided. 
4. Conclusions 
(1) The nearly fully eutectic structure of Nb-22Ti-15Si-5Cr-3Al-2Hf 
alloy has been changed to a hypereutectic structure by adding V 
or Zr solely and compositely adding Mo, V and Zr. Sole addition 
of Mo or Zr and composite additions of Mo, V and Zr fully sup- 
press the α(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 phase whilst stabilizing the γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 
phase. The heat treatment at 1450 °C for 50 h promotes the 
formation of (Nb, X) 3 Si phase in the Zr-containing alloys. 
(2) The alloys’ compressive yield strength at 1250 °C is improved 
by adding Mo or Zr solely and further improved by their com- 
posite additions. The alloys’ room temperature fracture tough- 
ness is ameliorated by alloying with V or Zr solely and signif- 
icantly ameliorated by their composite additions. However, in 
any case, Mo addition degrades the room temperature fracture 
toughness of the present Nb-Si-based alloys. 
(3) The microhardness of Nbss is improved by the solid solution 
strengthening effects of Mo addition but is softened by V addi- 
tion. The solid solution softening effect of V on Nbss cannot be 
altered by further additions of Mo or/and Zr. The microhardness 
of γ(Nb, X) 5 Si 3 can be improved by the cooperative solution 
strengthening effects of Zr with V/Mo. 
(4) Sole addition of Mo, V or Zr is favorable to ameliorate the oxi- 
dation resistance at 1250 °C. However, the composite additions 
of V with Mo/Zr (especially V-Mo-Zr) do not improve but de- 
grade it owing to promoting the formation of low melting point 
oxide V 2 O 5 .
