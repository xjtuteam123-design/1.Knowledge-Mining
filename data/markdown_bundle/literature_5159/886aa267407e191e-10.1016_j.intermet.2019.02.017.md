1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys containing numerous alloying elements within
their microstructures possess significant ability to maintain excellent
mechanical performance at elevated service temperatures. Owing to
high strength capacity, these materials are substantially used in aero-
space (turbine blades/discs), marine and land-based applications.

from L12-type ordered γ′-Ni3Al precipitates coherently dispersed in
FCC-type disordered γ matrix phase [1,2]. In recent years, optimization

4.3. Formation and microstructural evolution of γ′-Ni3Al precipitates
High-temperature mechanical strength of Ni-based superalloys is
mostly achieved by long term stability of γ′ precipitates which depends

5. Conclusions
Current study has focused on the atomic size effect of alloying X
elements (X=Co, Cr, Nb, Ta or Ti) on their phase partitioning beha-
viours among γ′ and γ phases, site occupancy tendencies and bonding
characteristics within Ni3Al-X intermetallics as well as microstructural
features of binary Ni80Al20 and ternary Ni80Al15X5 alloy systems to
reveal principal microstructural parameter significantly governing
mechanical strength of Ni-based superalloys. Remarkable outcomes are
summarized as follows:
•In order to reduce the degree of atomic distortions within γ′ and γ
phases, higher atomic radii alloying elements such as X=Ta, Nb or
Ti tend to become enriched in γ′ precipitates, whereas smaller size
X=CrorCoatomspreferentiallyparticipateintoγmatrixphase.In
γ′-Ni3Alprecipitates,relativelybigX=Ta,Nb,TiorCratomsprefer
tooccupyAlsublatticesites,whileX=Coatomshavingsmallersize
substitute for both Al and Ni sublattice sites.
•Mechanical strength of Ni-based superalloys is predominantly af-
fected by bonding strength within γ′ precipitates. As atomic radii of
alloying X elements are closer to that of Al atom, more Alsites of γ′-
Ni
3Al-X phases are preferentially occupied. Accordingly, stronger
directional bondings between X atoms and their NN Ni atoms
along<110>directions of γ′ precipitates and higher micro-hard-
ness properties of Ni-based superalloys are achieved in the order of
X=Co < Cr < Ti < Nb < Ta additions. When these alloying X
elements tend to occupy Ni sites, such a dramatic strengthening
effect would not appear since Al-X bondings are weaker than Ni-X
bondings.
•For the entire aging time period, mechanical strength of alloys de-
teriorateswithincreasingsizeof?'precipitatesanditisindependent
from lattice parameter and volume fraction of γ′ precipitates which
show increasing/decreasing and constant trends, respectively.
