Drastically improved ductility of an Ir-base
alloy by mixing Ir–Nb with Ni–Al
X.H. Yu *, Y. Yamabe-Mitarai, S. Nakazawa, Y. Ro, H. Harada
High-Temperature Materials 21 Project, National Research Institute for Metals , 1-2-1 Sengen, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305-0047, Japan
Abstract
The fcc /L12 coherent structure formed in the quaternary Ir–Nb–Ni–Al alloys. The ductility of quaternary Ir–Nb alloy was
drastically improved by mixing with Ni–Al binary alloy. The two investigated alloys exhibited higher high-temperature 0.2% ﬂow
stress (e.g. above 1000 MPa at 1200 °C) and better ductility ( /p5820% both at room temperature and 1200 °C). An excellent
balance of high-temperature strength and ductility was obtained. The improvement of ductility was attributed to change the
fracture mode from intergranular to transgranular. © 2002 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

1. Introduction
It may be fair to say that the Ni-base superalloys
have almost reached their limit in temperature capabil-
ity as long as Ni ( T
m =1453 °C) is used as the base
metal.
A new class of superalloys called ‘refractory super-
alloys’, which use Ir (Tm =2447 °C) as base metal with
L12 precipitates, was proposed [1–3]. These refractory
superalloys have higher melting temperatures, high-
temperature strength and superior oxidation resistance
compared to the Ni-base superalloys. However, the
weakest points of Ir-base alloys are ductility and den-
sity. Ir-base alloys show intergranular fractures, al-
though Ir has an fcc crystal structure [4]. Ni addition
was the ﬁrst consideration because it has the same fcc
crystal structure as Ir as well as lower density, in
addition to having an effect on improving the ductility
of the Ir-base alloy [5]. However, further improvement
is required. A novel type of refractory superalloys
prepared by mixing an Ir-base alloy with a Ni-base
alloy has been proposed [6]. Some investigations on
quaternary Ir–Nb–Ni–Al alloys have indicated that
some alloys had an fcc /L1
2 coherent structure and those
quaternary alloys located near the Ni-rich corner of the
quaternary Ir–Nb–Ni–Al diagram presented excellent
ductility. However, the behavior of the strength and
ductility of alloys located at an Ir-rich corner has never
been investigated.
In this paper, 5 and 10 at.% Ni–18.9 at.% Al alloys
were mixed with Ir–15 at.% Nb to prepare quaternary
Ir–Nb–Ni–Al alloys. We examined the microstructure
and compressive properties of the two alloys and com-
pared them with Ir–Nb binary alloys and ternary Ir–
Nb–Ni alloys. The possible mechanism of improving
ductility and strength is discussed.
2. Experimental
The 5 and 10 at.% Ni–18.9 at.% Al alloys were
mixed with Ir–15 at.% Nb alloys to prepare the quater-
nary Ir–Nb–Ni–Al alloys. The nominal composition
was: Ir–14.3Nb–4.0Ni–1Al and Ir–13.5Nb–8.1Ni–
1.9Al (at.%). Cylinder samples with a size of 2.5 mm in
diameter and 5 mm in height were cut out from those
ingots prepared by arc melting. Microstructures were
examined by the scanning electron microscopy (SEM)
and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) methods.
The phase characterizations were carried out by X-ray
diffraction (XRD) and differential thermal analysis
(DTA). DTA and a vacuum high-temperature furnace
were used to measure melting temperature. The speciﬁc-
ity of the two alloys was measured by a known density

3.3. Microstructure
The two alloys consisted of an fcc and L1 2 two-phase
structure. Fig. 3 shows the microstructure of the two
alloys after heat treatment. An fcc /L1
2 two-phase struc-
ture formed in the dendrite core and an fcc /L12 eutectic
structure was observed around it. TEM observation
(Fig. 4) indicated that the L1
2 precipitates were present
in a cubic shape with fcc inside L1 2 particles when the
heat treatment was performed up to 1600 °C for 24 h
(Fig. 4a,b). The shape of fcc inside the L1 2 precipitates
was irregular, e.g. plate-like. However, the fcc disap-
peared when the heat treatment went up to 216 h at
1600 °C (Fig. 4c). No effect of the cooling rate on the
morphology of L1
2 precipitates was observed. This is
because the melting temperature of the alloys
(/p922100 °C) exceeds the temperature limit of the DTA
apparatus, so the experiments changing the cooling rate
carried out by DTA could not give a re-precipitation
course from the fcc region entering the fcc /L1
2 two-
phase region. The whole course proceeded in the same
fcc/L1
2 two-phase region.
3.4. Strength beha /p54ior
The compressive test results indicated that the two
alloys exhibited excellent high-temperature strength and
compressive ductility. At room temperature, the alloy
Ir–13.5Nb–8.1Ni–1.9Al (at.%) showed a 0.2% ﬂow
stress of 1477 MPa and a fracture stress of 1998 MPa.
More, the compressive ductility was 21.2% at room
temperature (Fig. 5a). A very good balance between
strength and ductility was obtained. Fig. 5(b) shows the
stress–strain curves of the two alloys at 1200 °C. The
samples were compressed at 1200 °C until the samples
failed. The 0.2% ﬂow stress was also over 1000 MPa,
even at 1200 °C. The most valuable achievement is that
the compressive ductility was drastically improved in
comparison to that of the Ir –Nb binary alloy. In the
near future, it may be possible to machine it into a

In general, the fractured shape of a failed sample
represents its macro-ductility. The fractured shapes of
the two alloys are shown in Fig. 6(a,b). Both presented
a fracture surface with a slope, indicating that the
fracture is a ductile fracture. The micro-fracture (Fig.
6c,d) was observed by SEM after compressive tests at

dominant fracture mode on the surface of the transgran-

5. Summary
In this study it has been shown that a fcc /L1
2 coher-
ent structure was observed in Ir –Nb–Ni–Al alloys.
The mixing of Ni and Al drastically improved the
ductility of Ir –Nb. An excellent balance of high-tem-
perature strength and ductility was obtained and the
fracture mode changed from intergranular to transgran-
ular. The TEM observation on a deformed sample
indicated that the dislocations passed the L1
2 precipi-
tates by shearing at temperatures up to 1500 °C; at
1800 °C, no sheared character was observed. The im-
provement of ductility contributed to re ﬁning the grain
size and changing the fracture mode from intergranular
to transgranular.
