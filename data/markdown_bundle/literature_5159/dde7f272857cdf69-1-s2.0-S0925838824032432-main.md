1. Introduction
Reactive structural materials (RSM) are a new category of energetic 
materials with considerable mechanical properties and chemical sta -
bility but they can release intense chemical energy when suffering high- 
speed impact [1 – 4] . The fragments of RSM projectiles provide not only 
kinetic but also additional chemical energy damage such as ignition
[5 – 7] and overpressure [8 – 11] , thus attracting substantial attention in 
the military domain [12 – 16] .
Recently, there has been a growing interest in W-Zr alloys due to 
their high density, strength, and oxidation reactivity under high-speed 
impact [17 – 19] . W-Zr alloys exhibit potentially enhanced capability in 
penetration and energy-release effects when used as energetic frag -
ments. However, the formation of W
2
Zr intermetallic is inevitable dur -
ing the sintering process for W-Zr binary alloys [1] . During the sintering 
process of W-Zr binary materials, it is easy to form the W
2
Zr interme -
tallic at the W/Zr interface, which seriously hinders the mutual diffusion 
between W and Zr elements. In the solid-phase sintering process of 
powder metallurgy, interdiffusion is crucial for alloy sintering densifi -
cation. Therefore, a large amount of W
2
Zr phase often results in a lower 
sintering relative density of W-Zr alloys. Additionally, the brittle nature 
of W
2
Zr limits the strength and ductility of these alloys. Previous studies 
have reported that the quasi-static compressive strength of W-Zr alloys 
generally falls within the 900 – 1860 MPa range with fracture strain 
usually not exceeding 1 %, making them inferior to other W-based al -
loys. These greatly limit their processing and application [6,18] . Finally, 
a strong atomic bonding in W
2
Zr intermetallic compounds significantly 
may hinder the oxidation reactivity between Zr and oxygen, limiting the 
chemical energy release of the alloys during the high-speed impact 
process. In summary, there are challenges in the application of the W-Zr 
alloys caused by the existence of the W
2
Zr phase, such as hindered 
sintering densification, weakened dynamic ductility, as well as reduced 
energy release efficiency of the W-Zr alloys. Thus, it is essential in 
suppressing the formation of the W
2
Zr phase in W-Zr alloys, to improve

Considerable work has been performed to try to solve the challenging 
problems of sintering densification and brittleness of W-Zr alloys. For 
instance, several researchers have performed to improve the relative 
density of the W-Zr alloy by the hot-pressing sintering process. Although 
the interdiffusion ability between W and Zr elements was enhanced by 
the hot-pressing sintering, the densification effect was still unsatisfac -
tory when preparing W-Zr alloys with W content exceeding 55 % wt% 
[18] . In addition, researchers have employed surface treatment to 
address the brittleness of W-Zr reactive structural materials. For 
example, Wang et al. have studied that, the Ni coating was found to 
effectively preserve the damage potential of the W-Zr alloy bullet under 
detonation loading conditions [17] . However, the authors did not pro -
vide specific explanations about the enhancing plasticity of the Ni 
coating for the W-Zr alloy bullet. Although all of the above studies re -
ported positive results, none of them considered the adverse effect of the 
W
2
Zr phase on the relative density and mechanical properties of the 
alloys and did not fundamentally solve the problem of the brittleness of 
W-Zr alloys.
Inhibiting the formation of the W
2
Zr phase is the most direct and 
effective way to solve the above problems. Based on the binary W-Zr 
alloy, the introduction of alloying elements to design new alloys is a 
potentially effective way [20] . The principle for the selection of alloying 
elements is to promote the formation of W solid solutions instead of 
W
2
Zr. Ti and Nb have attracted the author ’ s attention as preferential 
additions in W-Zr alloys. It is worth noting that Ti is recognized as an 
active element with a high theoretical oxidation heat value [21] , which 
can potentially increase the upper limit of energy release in oxidation 
reactions. In addition, the high diffusion coefficient of Ti is expected to 
enhance the densification of the W-Zr alloy during powder metallurgy 
sintering [22 – 25] . As an additive, Nb can form solid solution phases 
with W, Zr, and Ti elements, respectively, and it is expected to stabilize 
the BCC structured phases to indirectly inhibit the formation of the W
2
Zr 
phase [26 – 30] . In our previous studies, it has been demonstrated that Ti 
addition can effectively reduce the formation of the W
2
Zr phase in W-Zr 
alloys [1] . However, the effect of Nb on the phase composition of W-Zr 
alloys has not been studied yet. Furthermore, when Ti and Nb are 
co-added, the phase composition, the mechanisms of the co-addition on 
the microstructure, and the mechanical properties of W-Zr-Ti-Nb qua -
ternary alloys with different Ti and Nb contents are still unclear.
In this study, a series of 65 W-Zr-Ti, 65 W-Zr-Nb, and 65 W-Zr-Ti-Nb 
alloys were designed and prepared. The study aimed to investigate the 
effect of Ti and Nb additions on the relative density, phase composition, 
and mechanical properties of the designed alloys. The microstructure of 
the alloys was characterized. The stabilized temperature range of the 
W
2
Zr phase was calculated using thermodynamic methods, and the ef -
fect of Ti and Nb additions on the inhibition of W
2
Zr was elucidated. 
Additionally, the quasi-static and dynamic mechanical properties of the 
alloys were tested. The effects of the compositions on the microstructure 
and the mechanical dynamic properties behavior were revealed. Finally, 
the deformed microstructure and P-N stress of the WTi
x 
and WTi
x
Nb

effect of the Ti, Nb contents on the plastic deformation capacity of the 
WTi
x

solid solution phases have been discussed in detail.
2. Experiment
2.1. Preparation
In the present study, W-Zr, W-Zr-Ti, W-Zr-Nb, and W-Zr-Ti-Nb alloys 
with the same 65 wt% W but different Zr, Ti, and Nb content were 
prepared by powder metallurgy. The W with an average particle size of 3 
μ m and Zr, Ti and Nb powders with average particle sizes of 10 μ m were 
used as the raw materials, among which the W powder and Zr, Ti, Nb 
powders were obtained from Xiamen Tungsten Co., Ltd. and Shanghai 
Yunfu Nano Technology Co., Ltd., respectively. The purity of the raw 
powders is listed in Table 1 . Homogeneous powder mixtures were ach -
ieved through a ball milling process utilizing a planetary ball mill with a 
weight ratio of balls to powder of 1:1, a milling time of 6 hours, and a 
rotation rate of 200 rpm. The milling balls and jar were composed of 
cemented carbide. The powder mixtures were subsequently pressed with 
a dwell time of 40 minutes and a pressure of 350 MPa. The powder 
compact was sintered at a temperature of 1773 K for 300 minutes, 
within an Ar atmosphere at standard pressure. The content and the 
theoretical densities of the sintered alloys are listed in Table 2 .
