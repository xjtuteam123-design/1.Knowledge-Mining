Abstract 
Porous TiNi binary and TiNiNb tern ary alloys of four compositions (Ti 50Ni47.5Nb2.5, 
Ti50Ni45Nb5, Ti 50Ni42.5Nb7.5, and Ti 50Ni40Nb10) were fabricated by the elemental powder 
sintering process. The effects of Nb addition on microstructure and mechanical properties of 
TiNi(Nb) alloys were investigated by X-ray di ffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM) and tensile tests, respectively. With the increase of Nb contents, the TiNi matrix as the 
main phase is always retained, while the intens ity of its diffraction peak gradually became 
weak with the appearance of β-Nb and TiNb phases. Most Nb particles are well-distributed in 
TiNi matrix and (Ti,Nb)2Ni phase is found in the binding domain between Nb phase and TiNi 
phase. The tensile strength and elastic modulus of TiNiNb alloys increase with the increase of 
Nb contents, due to the solid solution strengtheni ng effect of Nb-rich particles, the enlarged 
sintering neck and the strengthened bond between particles. Consequently, Nb plays a crucial 
role in adjusting composition and improving microstructure and mechanical properties of 
TiNiNb alloys. 
Keywords 
TiNiNb alloy; elemental powder sintering; Microstructure; Mechanical properties. 
 
1. Introduction 
TiNi alloys, the most important shape memory alloys (SMAs), possessing special shape 
memory effect (SME), superelasticity, high damping capacity, superior corrosion resistance 
and excellent biocompatibility, ha ve been applied in various fields, such as mechanical 
electronics, aeronautics and astronautics, and medical stents and implants [1-4] . Furthermore, 
new features have been found in TiNi-base shape memory alloy when a ternary alloying 
element is added in the TiNi matrix. Among the ternary SMAs, TiNiNb alloys have high 
thermal stability, better damping capacity, and wide thermal and stress hysteresis, that make 
them an ideal candidates for functional materials[4, 5]. 
At present, most works focus on Ti
44Ni47Nb9 alloys fabricated by vacuum melting [6, 7]. 
It is known that Ti 44Ni47Nb9 possess a typical microstructural characteristic, in particular, a 
large number of β-Nb particles dispersively distributing in TiNi matrix. Owing to soft β-Nb 
particles, the as-roll TiNiNb exhibits much higher yield strength than TiNi in tensile tests [8]. 
Notably, better characteristics of TiNiNb alloys can be achieved with lower Nb contents. He

Moreover, Nb exhibits supe rior corrosion resistance and outstanding biocompatibility,

Powder metallurgy is a popular method for the production of porous TiNi-base alloys [3, 17,

porous structure with appropriate mechanical prope rties. Zheng et al. [19] fabricated porous 
Ti

phase structure known from cast material. Howe ver, the systematical studies on porous 
TiNiNb alloys with different Nb contents have not been reported yet to the best of our 
knowledge. 
In the present study, Nb element was the substitution for Ni and a series of TiNiNb alloys, 
the nominal compositions of Ti 50Ni50-xNbx (x=0, 2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10, at.%), were fabricated by 
the elemental powder sintering processes. The relative densities, crystalline phase 
compositions, microstructure and mechanical properties of the obtained alloys were 
investigated. 
2. Materials and experimental procedures 
High purity commercial powders of Ti (General Reseerch Institute for Nonferrous Metals, 
China), Ni (Jiangyou Hebao Nanomaterial Co., Ltd, China) and Nb (Zhuzhou Cemented 
Carbide Group Co., Ltd, China) were used as-received in the present study. The particle size 
distribution, which determined by laser diff raction particle size analyzer (MS Micro, UK), 
and purity of the starting elemental powders ar e shown in Table 1. These elemental powders 
were blended for 4h in a roller mixer to prod uce powder mixtures with different nominal 
compositions of Ti 50Ni50-xNbx (x=0, 2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10, at.%). The powder mixtures were 
compacted under 300MPa pressure, and then the powder compacts were sintered at 1050 ℃ 
for 4 h in high vacuum ( ≤2.0×10-2 Pa) with a heat rate of 5 ℃/min to assure sufficient 
sintering.

TiNi (B2 and/or B19’), Ti2Ni, Ti3Ni4, β-Nb and TiNb phases are identified. It is obvious that 
the phase composition of TiNi(Nb) alloy is sensitiv e to its Nb content. The TiNi matrix as the 
predominant phase is always reta ined, even though Nb contents vary. Additionally, it can be 
seen that no carbide, oxide or free Ti/Ni is detected. When Nb content is 2.5 %, XRD pattern 
of TiNiNb alloy is similar to that of TiNi binary alloy with the exception of less evident peaks 
of Nb. However, the intensity for the peaks of TiNi and Ti
3Ni4 phases decrease with further 
increasing Nb contents, while th e intensities for the peaks of Ti 2Ni, Nb and TiNb increase. 
With the increase of Nb contents, i.e. the decrease of Ni contents, Ti element is excess for the 
formation of TiNi phase. The excess Ti element forms the Ti
2Ni phase, and the rest Ti 
element forms solid solution with Nb element. When the sintering temperature is 1050 ℃, the 
energy is insufficient to make all the excess Ti element dissolved in Nb particles.

The microstructure and phase distribution of TiNi(Nb) alloys with different compositions 
are shown in Fig. 2. A porous structure can be obtained by elemental powder sintering. 
Obviously, except TiNi binary alloy, there are three zones in the TiNiNb ternary alloys (i.e. 
the pores, the bright phases and the dark phases). The pores and the bright phases are well-
distributed in the dark phases. The analyzed co mpositions of the bright and dark zones were 
evaluated by EDS. The results show that the bright zones are primarily β-Nb particles, and the 
dark ones are TiNi matrix phases, as shown in Fig. 3. These results are also demonstrated in 
[9],[10], [19]. After sintering at 1050
℃ the TiNi matrix phases in the TiNi(Nb) with different 
Nb contents are homogeneous compositions. Whereas only a small amount of Nb is dissolved 
in the TiNi matrix by solid-state interdiffusion, while a large amount of Nb is still in the form 
of the isolated β-Nb particles, which almost completely maintain the size and distribution of 
the original Nb powder particles. Meanwhile, it is clearly seen in Fig. 2 that the area of pore 
decreases gradually with the Nb contents increasing from 2.5 % to 10 %.

The relative density and volume shrinkage of the TiNi(Nb) alloys with different Nb 
contents are compared, shown in Fig. 5. The re lative density presents two-stage transform at 
the stable temperature. At first, the relative de nsity decreases with Nb contents increasing at 
low Nb contents, and then it increases significant ly when the Nb content is greater than 5%. 
In addition, the volume shrinkage of the TiNi(N b) alloys continually increases from 15.53 % 
to 27.97 % with Nb contents increasing from 0 % to 10 %. It is concluded that the excess Ti 
element around Nb particles is low with the low Nb contents. In this condition, for Nb 
particles, most energy is used for processing sintering behaviors, including sharp-angle of 
particles becoming smooth and the formation a nd growth of sintering neck. Correspondingly, 
when more Nb addition substitutes for Ni, la rge amounts of Ti element embrace the Nb 
particles. Ti and Nb is a complete solid solu tion system under a certain condition. Nb element 
can dissolve in the excess Ti element even TiNi matrix easily. Consequently, the volume 
shrinkage sharply increases likewise when the addition is above 5 %. The above results can 
be confirmed by the results of XRD analysis as well.

The mechanical behaviors of TiNi(Nb) alloys were tested at room temperature. The 
tensile strength and elastic modulus are shown in Fig. 6. It is clear that Nb element has an 
intense influence on mechanical properties of TiNi(Nb) alloys. When the Nb content is just 
2.5 %, as a small amount of Nb destroyed the stable state of TiNi matrix, both tensile strength 
and elastic modulus are lower than those of TiNi binary alloy. Whereas, with Nb contents 
increasing from 2.5 % to 10 %, the tensile strength and elastic modul us are improved from 
131.21 MPa to 281.81 MPa and from 2.02 GPa to 3.29 GPa, respectively. The trends of 
mechanical behaviors are the same as that of the relative density, shown in Fig. 5.

The “normalized” mechanical properties of the TiNi(Nb) alloys are listed in Fig. 7. It is 
noted that the tensile strength and elastic modu lus still decrease when 2.5 % Nb is added in 
TiNi binary alloy, and then they increase with increasing Nb contents, even though the 
porosity of the TiNi(Nb) alloy is fixed. This can be explained as solid solution strengthening 
by the addition of Nb. As a strengthening element, the dissolving of Nb in TiNi matrix or Ti-
rich zone causes lattice distortion. Thereby the dislocation glide becomes more difficult. As a 
result, the strength and elastic modulus of TiNiNb alloys are enhanced. Meanwhile, the higher 
the proportion of solute atoms (Nb), the reinforcement is more obvious. Large amount of Nb-
rich particles embedded in matrix also plays a role through precipitation strengthening for the 
strength increment. However, the effect is not conspicuous with low Nb content. Besides, the

4. Conclusions 
Porous TiNi(Nb) alloys as potential biomedic al implants were fabricated by elemental 
powder sintering processes. The effects of the addition of Nb in TiNi(Nb) on microstructure 
and mechanical properties 
 were systematically investigated. 
After sintering at 1050 ℃ , β-Nb particles distribute uniformly in TiNi matrix, and 
(Ti,Nb)2Ni is detected in the binding domain between Nb phase and TiNi phase. All of 
relative densities, tensile stre ngth and elastic modulus of TiNiNb alloys decrease and then 
increase with the addition of Nb contents. Furt hermore, the change of tensile strength and 
elastic modulus follow the same trend, though the relative densities of different TiNiNb alloys 
are remained at 83 %.
