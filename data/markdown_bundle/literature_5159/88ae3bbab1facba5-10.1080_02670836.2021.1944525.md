ABSTRACT
Tension and compression tests for a rare-earth-containing alloy sheet, Mg–1.9Mn–0.3Ce (wt-%),
are performed under quasi-static and high-rate loadings along the rolling, transverse and normal
direction, and stress – strain responses exhibit positive strain-rate dependence. Empirically based
Johnson-Cook constitutive model is modified to describe the rate-dependent plastic deforma-
tion in multiple loading directions. A strong strength differential effect persists, and such effect is
shown to be stronger at high strain rates. A weak in-plane anisotropy exists in the initial yielding
and subsequent strain hardening behaviour as well as strength differential effect. In contrast,
the out-of-plane anisotropy in compression is apparent and is approximately rate insensitive.
Microstructural observations indicate that twinning plays the key role for the enhancement of
strain-hardening rate under high strain-rate deformation.

Conclusions
The uniaxial tension and compression stress–strain
responses of the Mg–1.9Mn–0.3Ce (wt-%) alloy in the
RD, TD, and ND was experimentally acquired at var-
ious strain rates up to the order of 10
3 s−1,a n dt h e
influenceofstrainrateonthestrengthdifferentialeffect
andanisotropywasinvestigated.
(1) The in-plane tension responses show a positive
strain-rate dependence, which is greater than the
compression deformation. The strength differen-
tial effect with respect to in-plane loading is
enhanced with the increase of strain rate, but the
asymmetrydecreaseswithincreasingthestrain.
(2) Owing to the addition of Ce, in-plane anisotropy
in both tension and compression is weak. The
out-of-plane anisotropy in compression is notice-
able, and such anisotropy is approximately rate-
independent.
(3) Under high strain-rate loadings, in-plane tensile
plastic deformation mechanisms are mainly pris-
matic and basal slips, and a small number of
10¯12 tension twins are observed. For the in-plane
compressive plastic deformation, the main plastic
deformation mechanisms are basal slip and 10¯12
tension twinning. Basal slip, pyramidal slip and
10¯12 tension twinning jointly dominate compres-
siveplastic deformationalongtheND.
