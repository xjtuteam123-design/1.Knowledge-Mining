ABSTRACT 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) containing well-stabilized nano-precipitates have a huge advantage in elevated 
temperature applications. This study investigated the effects of Mo and V additions on the morphological evo -
lution, the coarsening kinetics of multi-component γ
′
precipitates, and the high-temperature tensile strength of 
HEAs. HEAs with the additions of Mo and V have significantly lower lattice misfit values and exhibit a smaller 
coarsening rate of γ
′
nanoparticles compared to Ni and Co-based superalloys. Moreover, the precipitate 
morphology related to the lattice misfit exhibits completely different effects attributed to Mo and V addition. 
Remarkably, Mo
2.5
V
2.5 
HEA has a yield strength of 496 MPa at 800 
◦
C, which shows great potential for high- 
temperature applications. 
1. Introduction 
In recent years, precipitate hardening has been proven to be an 
effective way to promote the mechanical properties of medium/high- 
entropy alloys (MEAs/HEAs), which exhibit an excellent combination 
of strength and ductility [ 1 – 7 ]. Face-centered cubic (FCC) MEAs/HEAs 
with excellent ductility but limited strength have been strengthened by 
different strengthening precipitates, such as γ
′
, μ , and σ phases [ 1 , 2 , 
8 – 11 ]. He et al. [ 2 ] reported the (FeCoNiCr)
94

temperature owing to the homogeneous dispersion of fine coherent 
precipitates. Yang et al. [ 1 ] designed a high-density L1

ened MEA, which achieved an ultimate tensile strength of 1446 MPa and 
ductility as high as 47.8% at room temperature. In addition, recent 
studies have shown that L1

significant thermal stability at elevated temperatures, showing great 
potential for high-temperature applications [ 12 – 15 ]. Similar to the 
Ni-based superalloys, the service temperature of the 
precipitation-strengthened HEAs is predominantly controlled by the 
coarsening of coherent γ
′
nanoparticles at elevated temperatures. 
The coarsening behavior of coherent γ

nanoparticles in 
precipitation-strengthened HEAs based on the LSW model has been 
widely reported [ 12 , 16 , 17 ], demonstrating that sluggish coarsening 
behavior of the γ

superalloys could be attributed to either the sluggish diffusion of 
component elements or small interfacial energy, as well as a combina -
tion of both [ 4 , 12 , 16 , 18 , 19 ]. Zhao et al. [ 16 ] investigated that the 
coarsening rate of the γ

conventional Ni-based alloys, and their excellent thermal stability could 
be ascribed to the sluggish diffusion of Al or Ti in the high-entropy 
matrix. Zhao et al. [ 4 ] suggested that either the small interfacial en -
