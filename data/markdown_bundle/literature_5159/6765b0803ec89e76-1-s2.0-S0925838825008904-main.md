ABSTRACT
Designing precipitation-strengthened FCC/B2 dual-phase high-entropy alloys (HEAs) is an effective method for 
preparing structural materials with superior strength and lower density at elevated temperatures. However, high- 
density precipitates in FCC/B2 dual-phase HEAs will also cause a sudden drop in ductility, especially for the B2 
phase with insufficient slip systems. Here, by tailoring the precipitates of disordered FCC and ordered L1
2 
in the 
B2 phase, we achieved significant ductilization of the B2 phase. With the ductilized B2 phase, the precipitation- 
strengthened FCC/B2 dual-phase HEAs exhibited superior strength-ductility synergy over a wide temperature 
range. At 650 
◦
C, it was different from traditional understanding that the hard L1
2 
precipitate improved the 
deformability of B2 via sustainable stacking fault shearing, whereas the soft FCC precipitate transformed into the 
hard 18 R phase which reduced the B2 ductility by suppressing the dislocation motion of the B2 phase. By 
tailoring the precipitates in the B2 phase from FCC to L1
2
, the strain localization near the FCC/B2 phase 
boundary was significantly decreased and premature cracking was inhibited, leading to the superior ductility. 
These findings advance the microstructural design of precipitation-strengthened FCC/B2 dual-phase HEAs by 
introducing hard yet ductile precipitates to the B2 phase to enhance ductility, shedding light on the development 
of alloys with exceptional mechanical properties and lower density at elevated temperatures.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are a promising class of metallic mate -
rials for structural application due to their extensive unexplored range of 
alloy compositions and unique microstructures [1 – 3] . Among them, the 
dual-phase HEAs, especially those with face-centered cubic (FCC) and 
ordered body-centered cubic (B2) structures, have exhibited 
outstanding advantages, including low density [4,5] , excellent oxidation 
resistance [6,7] , and thermal stability [8,9] . Their superior 
strength-ductility synergy at room temperature has also drawn 
remarkable attention [10 – 15] . However, up to now, their mechanical 
properties at elevated temperatures are unsatisfactory. To further pro -
mote the application of FCC/B2 dual-phase HEAs over a wide temper -
ature range, introducing precipitation strengthening should be a 
promising approach.
It is widely accepted that the L1
2 
precipitate in the FCC matrix of 
FCC/B2 dual-phase HEAs can effectively strengthen the alloy with an 
acceptable loss of ductility. Previous studies have reported the high- 
density L1
2 
precipitate in the FCC matrix of FCC/B2 dual-phase HEAs 
by adjusting alloy compositions or the thermomechanical treatment 
process [11,16,17] . Owing to the ductile nature of FCC and L1
2 
phases 
and their coherent interface, these alloys maintained good ductility with 
less stress and strain concentrations at local regions and potential 
cracking sites [18 – 20] . Similar to the single-phase FCC HEAs, it is also 
practical to further obtain a better strength-ductility combination by 
achieving a fine and evenly distributed L1
2 
precipitate without hetero -
geneous coarsening [21] .
The precipitation behavior in the B2 phase of FCC/B2 dual-phase 
HEAs and the corresponding effect on mechanical properties at 
elevated temperatures are more complex [22,23] . Typically, Cr-rich 
nano-particles with BCC structure are frequently found in the AlCoCr -
FeNi and FeNiCrAl alloy systems [24 – 26] . The particles formed via 
spinodal decomposition and strengthen the B2 matrix through Orowan 
by-pass mechanisms [27,28] . Besides, in an FCC/B2 hypoeutectic HEA, 
a high-density needle-like phase with a disordered FCC structure was 
detected in the B2 matrix [29] . Generally, given the high critical

3.3. Cracking and fracture behaviors
For the FCC/B2 dual-phase alloys, the sustainable deformability was 
generally strongly related to the cracking behavior of the B2 phase. As 
shown in Fig. 4 , the Ti alloyed SA and DA samples exhibited a similar 
fracture feature after tensile test at 650
◦
C. The cracking over -
whelmingly initiated at the phase boundary and percolated into the B2 
phase, as marked by the yellow triangles in Fig. 4 (a, b), leading to the 
cleavage of the B2 phase. Consequently, these considerable microcracks 
of the B2 phase would propagate and coalesce, resulting in the fracture 
of the alloys.
It is interesting that the DA sample still maintained a more impres -
sive elongation to failure, even with the detrimental B2-cleavage failure 
mode. In the study of ductility enhancement of NiAl alloy, the ductile 
second phase was supposed to suppress crack propagation in the B2 
matrix [37 – 39] . Indeed, as marked by the red arrows in Fig. 4 , crack 
deflection can be observed in both the SA and DA samples with the 
ductile FCC and L1
2 
precipitates. However, after a close inspection, the 
microcracks were not found in the uniform deformation region away 
from the fracture end, implying the B2 phase cracks were still easy and 
quick to propagate under high stress. Therefore, it can be inferred that 
the improved ductility of the DA sample was achieved by restraining the 
crack initiation of B2.
4. Discussion
According to the results above, B2 phases with different precipitates 
determined the ductility of the dual-phase HEAs at elevated tempera -
tures. Therefore, in the discussion section, we will first illustrate the 
reason for different precipitation behaviors in the B2 phases and eval -
uate their influence on the deformability of B2. Then, the deformability 
of the B2 phases with different precipitates and their effects on the 
ductility of the dual-phase HEAs were revealed based on the deforma -
tion mechanisms. Finally, the universality of tailoring precipitation to 
significantly improve the ductility of dual-phase HEAs at elevated 
temperatures was proved.

4.2. Distinct deformation mechanisms of the B2 phase with different 
precipitates
The origin of distinct deformability of the B2 phases with different 
precipitates were revealed by characterizing microstructural evolutions 
of the SA and DA samples under different interrupted strains at 650
◦
C, 
as shown in Fig. 6 . For the SA sample, when strained of ~2 %, dense 
lamellae were observed in the FCC-structured precipitates of the B2 
phase, as marked by the yellow triangles in Fig. 6 (a1). The corre -
sponding HRTEM image shows that the lamellae domain was composed 
of high-density stacking faults (SFs), as confirmed by the streaks in the 
inserted FFT pattern ( Fig. 6 (a2)). In particular, additional spots occurred 
along the < 111 > axis, as marked by the yellow triangles, indicating 
the existence of phase transformation. As the plastic strain increased to 
~8 %, the lamellae in the precipitates became denser ( Fig. 6 (b1)). Ac -
cording to the HRTEM image of the lamellae domain ( Fig. 6 (b2)), the 
lamellae can be distinguished as a striped structure with atom plane 
configuration. Moreover, the diffraction spots on the FFT pattern 
exhibited a six-fold close-packed feature along the < 111 > axis, and the 
angles of the two diffraction spots close to the transmission spot were 
measured as 87.4
◦
. Accordingly, it was confirmed that these lamellae 
domains belong to the long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase with 
18 R structures. Similar LPSO phase transformations are frequently re -
ported in the TiAl alloys [43,44] and rare-earth-containing magnesium 
alloys [45,46] , which originated from the periodic modification of 
stacking sequences by SFs. For the present FCC-structured precipitates, 
which can be seen as a Co-rich solid solution phase, the reduced stacking 
fault energy (SFE) tended to promote Shockley partial dislocations 
dominated plastic deformation. Consequently, the periodic alternating 
gliding of Shockley partial dislocations on every six layers of 
close-packed {111} planes left three SFs and formed the 18 R phase. This 
phase transformation process from the soft FCC to the hard 18 R phase 
could sustainably intensify the resistance of dislocation motion in the B2 
phase, enhancing the precipitation-strengthening effect during defor -
mation. However, the high-density planar defects will also make the 
18 R phase hard to deform and be brittle. As a consequence, it would 
weaken the dislocation mobility within the small spacing between the 
precipitates and induce the premature stress/strain concentration, 
leading to the limited deformability of the B2 phase.
For the 2 % strained DA sample, large numbers of dislocations in the 
B2 phase piled up against the L1
2 
and σ precipitates, as marked by the 
yellow triangles in Fig. 6 (a3, a4), while no obvious deformation sub -
structures can be directly detected in the L1
2 
precipitates. These be -
haviors indicated that the L1
2 
and σ precipitate were harder than the B2 
phase at high temperature, providing the precipitation-strengthening 
effect for the B2 phase. Due to its dispersive distribution and low vol -
ume fraction, the influence of the fine σ particles on the ductility of the 
B2 phase could be relatively weak when compared with the L1
2
. At the 
plastic strain of ~8 %, SF nano-bundles with a spacing of 20 – 80 nm 
formed within the L1
2 
precipitates ( Fig. 6 (b3, b4)), suggesting stacking 
fault shearing dominated deformation mechanism of the L1
2 
precipitate. 
Therefore, the L1
2 
precipitate was a hard yet ductile precipitation- 
strengthening phase for the B2 phase. In the case of the stacking faults 
shearing operating at a large strain, the plastic deformation of the L1
2 
precipitates can help release stress/strain concentration and promote 
extensive activity of dislocations, therefore improving the deformability 
of the B2 phase.

L1

precipitates in the DA sample exhibited enhanced dislocation stor -
age capacity, which contributed to the higher deformability of the B2 
phase.
The different deformability of the B2 phases will fundamentally 
determine the ductility of the FCC/B2 dual-phase HEA at high temper -
atures. For the SA sample with lower deformability of the B2 phase, the 
strains gradually concentrated along the phase boundary during defor -
mation, as revealed by the KAM distributions in Fig. 7 (a1-a3). In com -
parison, although with high KAM values, it distributed relatively 
uniformly in the B2 phase of the DA sample ( Fig. 7 (b1-b3)). To further 
reveal the deform degree near the B2 phase boundary quantitively, the 
local average misorientation (LAM) was analyzed for at least two hun -
dred phase boundaries in each sample at different strains [47] . As shown 
in Fig. 8 (c), the LAM values at the phase boundaries of the SA and DA 
samples increased simultaneously as the strain increased, which origi -
nated from the dislocation pile-up induced by interphase discontinuous 
slip systems. However, the LAM values at the phase boundaries of the SA 
sample were higher than those in the DA sample as the tensile strain 
increased from 2 % to 5 %, indicating the more difficult slip transfer at 
the phase boundaries in the SA sample. On the contrary, the strain 
localization at the phase boundaries of the DA sample was relatively 
relieved in the B2 phase with L1
2 
precipitates. With the better accom -
modation of imposed strains, the crack initiation at the B2 phase 
boundary and subsequent B2 phase cleavage were postponed, leading to 
enhanced tensile ductility of the double-aged FCC/B2 dual-phase HEA.
4.3. The universality of ductilizing B2 phase for high strength-ductility 
synergy
Generally, for the FCC/B2 dual-phase HEAs with higher strength, the 
phase boundary will suffer a more severe stress/strain concentration, 
and the crack incident will occur prematurely, especially at elevated 
temperatures when the strength of the phase boundary becomes weak 
[47] . Therefore, it is significant to ductilizing the B2 phase for better 
strength-ductility synergy. Here we applied the double aging strategy in 
the Ta alloyed FCC/B2 dual phase HEA and achieved better mechanical 
properties. As shown in Fig. 9 (a), with higher precipitation strength -
ening induced by higher volume fractions of precipitates in the B2 
phase, the Ta alloyed SA and DA samples reached yield strengths of 
998 MPa and 1046 MPa at 650
◦
C, respectively. Under such increased 
stress, the elongation the SA sample reduced to 3.8 %, while the DA 
sample showed an impressively higher elongation of 15.6 %, nearly four 
times that of the SA sample, proving the effectiveness of enhancing 
ductility of FCC/B2 dual-phase HEA via tailoring precipitates in the B2 
phase.
Such mechanical performance enhancement would also make the 
studied dual-phase alloys competitive among high-temperature struc -
tural materials. In Fig. 9 (b), the mechanical performances at 650
◦
C in 
this work were compared with other superalloys and HEAs [52 – 59] . As 
indicated by arrows, significant ductilization of DA samples can be 
observed, which achieved better strength-ductility combinations at the 
middle-upper position in the graph and superior to the majority of listed 
HEAs. The DA samples in this work showed close mechanical perfor -
mance to the Inconel 718 and Waspaloy superalloys [48,49] .

In light of the researches on the NiAl alloys, precipitation was

deformation mechanisms discussed above, the soft metallic precipitates

defects and suppress the mobility of dislocations within the B2 during 
deformation, leading to severe strain localization at the FCC/B2 phase 
boundary and earlier fracture. On the contrary, the hard yet ductile 
intermetallic precipitate could play a positive role in ductilizing the B2 
phase by sustainably activating defect activity, contributing to the 
release of strain concentration at the FCC/B2 phase boundary. Even as 
the volume fraction of L1
2 
increases within a certain range, the alloy 
ductility will not decrease ( Supplementary Fig. S4 ). Therefore, for the 
future microstructure design of precipitation-strengthened FCC/B2 
dual-phase HEAs, it could be practical to achieve better 
strength-ductility synergy over a wide temperature range by tailoring 
the precipitation of ductile intermetallic phase in the B2 phase.
