ABSTRACT
Optimizing primary interfaces is key to suppressing hot cracking in additively manufactured Ni
superalloys. This study demonstrates the dual role of primary NbC/ γ interface: optimal NbC den-
sity reduces B enrichment in interdendritic region and suppress low melting point boride formation,
reducing cracking. Conversely, excessive NbC promotes detrimental eutectics that disrupt den-
drite solidification and impede terminal liquid feeding, inducing cracks. Statistical analysis reveals
these mechanisms, enabling quantitative optimization. The alloy with tailored interfaces achieves
excellent printability and strength–ductility synergy. These findings provide a pathway for crack
suppression and compositional design in AM Ni superalloys.
