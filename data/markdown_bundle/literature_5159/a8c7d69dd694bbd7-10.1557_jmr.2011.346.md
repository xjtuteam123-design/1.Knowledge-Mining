Inﬂuence of Co content on stacking fault energy in Ni –Co
base disk superalloys
Yong Yuan,a) Yuefeng Gu, Chuanyong Cui, Toshio Osada, Zhihong Zhong, Toshimitsu Tetsui,
Tadaharu Yokokawa, and Hiroshi Harada
High Temperature Materials Center, National Institute for Materials Science, Ibaraki 305-0047, Japan
(Received 27 April 2011; accepted 19 September 2011)
The inﬂuence of Co content on stacking fault energy (SFE) of the c matrix in four Ni–Co base
superalloys, including newly developed alloys, has been studied by utilizing high-resolution transmission
electron microscopy. The results indicatedthe SFE was not linear with Co content of thec matrix. The
lowest SFE could be attained at around 34.0 at.% Co. This effect was attributed to variation of electron
holes, saturated Co content in the matrix, and the effect of Co on the partition coefﬁcient of other alloying
elements. A high density of twins was related to lowSFE and could improve the mechanical properties.
I. INTRODUCTION
The stacking fault energy (SFE) is an important intrinsic
parameter of materials and is closely associated with the
deformation mechanism, such as dislocation dissociation,
cross-slip, and deformation twinning. Low SFE facilitates
splitting of perfect dislocations and causes planar disloca-
tion slip, whereas high SFE favors cross-slip and non-
planar dislocation structure. It has been reported that
decreasing SFE can simultaneously increase strength and
ductility in ultra ﬁne-grained Cu –Al alloys and the strain
hardening rate of nanocrystalline face-centered cubic
(fcc) metals.
1,2 Apparently, SFE can affect the macroscopic
mechanical properties.
At National Institute for Materials Science, Japan, we
have developed the new cast and wrought Ni –Co base
disc superalloys (registered as TMW alloys); some of them
have superior mechanical properties over the commercial
U720Li alloy.3–6 For example, as concerned creep test at
stress level of 630 MPa, a gain in temperature capability of
76 °C is obtained with TMW-4M3 compared to U720Li
alloy.
5 When compared in creep stress, TMW-4M3 can
survive nearly twice as large stress compared with U720Li.
In our previous study, a high density of annealing and
deformation twins were observed in TMW-4M3 alloy.
7
The annealing twin length per unit area in TMW-4M3
is two times longer than that in U720Li. We have suggested
twin strengthening contributes greatly to the improved
mechanical properties. However, the superior mechanical
performance of TMW alloys at elevated temperatures has
not yet been fully understood. It is therefore signi ﬁcant to
ﬁnd the intrinsic difference between U720Li and TMW
alloys, especially the factors that affect the formation of
twins. Several important factors determine the ability to
form twins, such as grain size, temperature, grain boundary
energy, and SFE.
8,9 Most of all, SFE is a parameter con-
trolling the formation of twinsin materials, and the tendency
for twinning is higher in metals and alloys with low SFE,
and vice versa.
10,11
In the present study, we determined the SFE of the
c matrix (fcc-disordered solid solution) in U720Li and
TMW alloys by high-resolution transmission electron
microscopy (HRTEM). The in ﬂuence of Co on the SFE,
the ability to form twins and mechanical properties, is
discussed.
II. EXPERIMENTAL
The U720Li and TMW alloy ingots were made using
vacuum induction melting. For each alloy, 50-kg ingot was
cast and then hot extruded and hot rolled in the Special
Metals (Huntington, WV). The ingots were hot extruded
successfully intov22-mm billets with extrusion ratio of 12:1
at about 1170 °C. Before hot rolled, the ingots were soaked at
1149 °C for 12 h. Theﬁnal hot rolling reduction of 75% (from
50.8 to 12.7 mm) was reached by rolling two passes at about
1100 °C with InterHeat at 1121 °C for 30 min. The rolled
plates were subjected to the heat treatment (1100 °C/4 h/air
cooling (AC), then aging at 650 °C/24 h/AC followed by
760 °C/16 h/AC). Samples with a 22 mm /C2 v4m mg a u g e
section for tensile and creep tests were machined from the
heat-treated plates. All the samples were polycrystalline. After
creep to rupture at 725 °C/630 MPa, transmission electron
microscopy discs with thickness of around 300lmw e r ec u t
from the samples perpendicular to the stress axis. Then the
discs were manually ground to 50 lm and perforated by
Twin-Jet Electropolisher at 40 V and 0 °C. The electrolyte
consists of 50 mL perchloric acid, 100 mL ethanol, and 300
mL 2-butoxyethanol. The SFE was investigated using a JEM-
2100F operated at 200 kV.

It has been reported that Co has great in ﬂuence on SFE
in Ni –Co binary alloys.
19,20 However, the results even in
Ni–Co binary alloys are not consistent. For example, the
SFE do not have a linear relationship with Co content
as illustrated in Ref. 19, whereas theoretically calculated
SFE has a linear relationship with Co content in Ref. 20.
Xie et al.
21 have reported an empirical formula to calculate
the alloying effect on SFE in superalloys, suggesting the
SFE decrease can be expressed as a coef ﬁcient of re-
duction times the at.% of that alloy addition. Unfortu-
nately, this empirical formula cannot explain the nonlinear
effect of Co content on the SFE in our experiments. Note
that the Co content of c matrix in TMW2, TMW-4M3, and
TMW-26 exceeds the upper limit in Xie ’s empirical
formula, which is 22 at.%. The trend of Co effect on the
SFE we found is similar with the result from Udimet
500 reported by Yang et al.
22 However, there is no W
in Udimet 500, which is clari ﬁed to have an important
effect on the SFE. Meanwhile, Udimet 500 has higher

The different alloy compositions may cause the different
Co content corresponding to the lowest SFE, which is
14.7 at.% in Udimet 500 and 24.0 at.% in TMW alloys.
Yang et al. did not explain the reason for the trend of Co
effect on the SFE and its relationship with the formation of
twins in the alloys.

investigated in the past, and contradictory results were

23 Lee19 used Harris’conclusion to argue

metals will cause a reduction of high density of states at the

with increasing Co content in Ni–Co alloy, as calculated by

20 The electron holes

more addition of Co, the more electron holes (empty d-states)
in Ni–Co alloy. More electron holes may be responsible for

recently, Yu and Wang25 calculated the inﬂuence of alloying
elements, such as Re, Mo, Ru, Cr, W, and Co, on SFE by

bonding of Ni–Co is weak, so Co has less effect on SFE than
other alloying elements under the same concentrations. This
conclusion cannot be simply applied to TMW alloys since
the Co content in TMW alloys is high and there are six
alloying elements in TMW alloys. The effect of high-content
Co on SFE in multiple alloying systems is more complicated

Furthermore, we suggest there are two additional factors
responsible for the increased SFE of the c matrix with
further addition of Co. First, the Co content may gradually
saturate in the c matrix. In Table I, the step of increasing
Co content from U720Li to TMW26 is 47, 15, and 6%.
However, in Table II, the corresponding increment of Co
in the matrix is 51, 14, and 2%, respectively. Apparently,
the increase of Co content in the matrix is not proportional
with increasing Co content in the alloy, but seems to
saturate. So further addition of Co will not decrease SFE
of c matrix any more. Second, when one considers the
interactions of alloying elements, very high Co content
may affect the partition coef ﬁcient of other alloying
elements. For example, Cr content in TMW-4M3 and
TMW-26 is the same, i.e., 14.7 at.%. However, Cr content
in the matrix of TMW-26 is 24.8 at.%, which is lower than
that of TMW-4M3, 27.1 at.%. Comprehensive effect of all
alloying elements may cause the increase of SFE. More
work needs to be done to accurately interpret these
interacting effects.
Now it is clear that Co does have great in ﬂuence on
the SFE in Ni –Co base superalloys, and therefore affects
the ability to form twins as well. Low SFE promotes the
dissociation of perfect dislocations and facilitates the twin
nucleation, resulting in high density of twins. The twinned
structures are effective for improving the mechanical
properties. This implies that mechanical properties can
be tailored by controlling SFE during alloy design.
IV. CONCLUSIONS
In summary, the SFE of c matrix in four Ni –Co base
superalloys has been studied. We found the SFE had
nonlinear relationship against Co content of c matrix. The
lowest SFE could be attained at approximate 34.0 at.% Co
in the matrix of TMW-4M3. Low SFE could enhance the
ability to form twins and introduce more twin structures
into materials, which in turn improved the mechanical
properties.
