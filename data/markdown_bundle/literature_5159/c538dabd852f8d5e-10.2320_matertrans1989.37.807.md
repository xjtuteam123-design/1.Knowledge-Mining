Materials Transactions, JIM, Vol. 37, No. 4 (1996), pp. 807 to 812 Paper from JIM '95 Fall Annual Meeting in Hawaii Microstructure and High-Temperature Property of Reaction HIP-Sintered SiC-AlN Ceramic Alloys Yoshimasa Kobayashi* , Jing-Feng Li, Akira Kawasaki and Ryuzo Watanabe Department of Materials Processing, Faculty of Engineering, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-77, Japan. SiC and AIN are known to form an extensive solid solution, and preferable mechanical properties have been obtained particularly at room temperature for the SiC-AIN alloys than the end number materials. The present study was intended to investigate the high-temperature strength and deformation behavior of the SiC-AIN solid solution. For this purpose, dense SiC-AlN solid solution and its composite samples were prepared by HIP-sinter- ing of Si, C and AIN powder premixtures as a handy process. The microstructures and solid solutioning composi- tions were investigated by STEM-EDX, and the fracture strength and deforrnation behavior of the alloys were evaluated up to 1773K by using a newly-developed Small Punch (SP) testing method. The HIP-sintered SiC-AlN alloys were found to be composed of SiC-rich and AIN-rich solid solutions with ultrafine grained-microstructure of 50 to 100 nm matrix grain size. High fracture strength was obtained in the SiC-AlN alloys up to 1473K, and below this temperature the load-deflection curve was linear, indicating typically brittle behavior. At 1773K, non- linear deformation behavior was observed and it intensified with decreasing strain rate. No such fracture transi- tion behavior was observed in the monolithic SiC and AIN with average grain size of up to 5 gm. The fractographic observation suggested that the grain boundary sliding enhanced by the ultrafine grained-microstruc- ture was predominately responsible for the appearance of the non-linear deformation at high temperature. I. Introduction Silicon carbide (SiC) is an important material for high-temperature structural applications, because of its excellent heat resistance and oxidation resistance supe- rior to those of its contender, silicon nitride (Si3N4). Aluminum nitride (AIN) is mainly used as electronic de- vice substrate materials due to its high thermal conduc- tivity and dielectric properties, but it also receives in- creasing attention for structural uses at high temperatures. Moreover, it was recently found that solid solutions can be formed in the SiC-AlN system over a wide composi- tional range A new series of ceramics with better mechanical properties than the two end-number materials are expected to be developed in the SiC-AlN system by a ceramic alloying approach utilizing the formation of SiC- AIN solid solutions. During the past decade, the micro- structures and solid solutioning composition range of the SiC-AlN system have been considerably investigated. It was found that the grain growth in the SiC-AlN system is remarkably suppressed due to the formation of solid solu- tions, as a result, much higher fracture strength at ambient temperature is obtained mainly due to the effect of super- *Graduate Student, Tohoku University. fine grained-microstructure On the other hand, with increasing attention re- cently devoted to the elevated temperature fracture be- havior of ceramics for high-temperature structural appli- cations, the high-temperature property of the SiC-AlN al- loys became one of our concerns. It is known that non- linear behavior (or plastic deformation) is observed above a specific temperature for fine grained ceramics however, a decrease in the mechanical strength is usually accompanied. As one of the candidate materials to be used at temperature around 1770 K, the high-temperature strength and deformation behavior must be thoroughly in- vestigated. For this purpose, the fracture strength and de- formation behavior of dense SiC-AlN solid solution and its composite samples were evaluated up to 1773 K by using a newly-developed Small Punch (SP) testing method. The temperature dependences of fracture strength and deformation behavior have been examined in connection with the microstructural features. II. Experimental Procedure 1. Material fabrication In the present study, dense SiC-AlN solid solutions and their mixed composites were fabricated by hot-iso- static pressing of Si, C and AIN powder premixtures through the reaction between silicon and carbon. The raw

808 materials were commercial silicon (under 200 mesh, High Purity Chemicals Co., Ltd), carbon black (High Purity Chemicals Co., Ltd) and AIN (average particle size =1.39gm, Toyo Aluminum Co., Ltd). Appropriate amounts of the starting powders were well mixed by ball milling in alcohol solution for 100 h with a pot and balls made of silicon nitride. Three batches were prepared with prospective nominal compositions of SiC-30, 50 and 70 mol% AIN after reaction. After ball-milling and drying, green compacts were formed by uniaxial-pressing in a steel die at 100 MPa and vacuum-encapsulated into a Vycor glass tube at 1573K. The glass-encapsulated speci- mens were HIP-sintered at 2123K and 200 MPa for 2 h to full density. For comparison of mechanical properties, monolithic SiC and AIN specimens were also prepared by glass-encapsulated HIP-sintering method using their commercial powders. 0.5 mass% B4C was added to SiC powder as sintering aid, but nothing was added to AIN. 2. Measurement of mechanical properties The microhardness and fracture toughness were determined by using the Vickers indentation method. The strength was measured from room temperature to 1773 K by means of modified small punch (MSP) test, which had been originally designed for the evaluation of brittle to ductile transition in metallic materials used for nuclear materials The operation principle of this test- ing method is that a disk specimen is loaded by a small puncher and the deflection is monitored (see Fig. 1), and the MSP test has several advantages as compared with the conventional methods. That is, first, a small-sized speci- men ( approximately 10 mm in diameter and 0.5 mm in thickness ) was used. Second, the fixture of specimen to the testing jig is easier. Third, the deformation of a tested specimen can be directly and accurately measured as a deflection in the z-axis in a large deflection range. The present measurement of fracture strength and deforma- tion was carried out in argon with the MSP jig setup in an electric furnace with a tungsten mesh heater. After hold- ing for 1.2 ks when the predetermined peak temperature was reached, the load was applied by a universal testing machine (Instron) mainly at a crosshead speed of 0.05 mm/min (0.83gm/s). In some case, the speed was lowered one order of magnitude to study the effect of displace- ment rate on the deformation at high temperature. Characterization The tensile and fractured cross-section surfaces of the MSP specimens were observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The microstructural observation and microchemical analysis were carried out by scanning and transmission electron microscopy (STEM) equipped with energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometer (EDX). The STEM-EDX samples were prepared by the usual proce- dure for ceramics, i.e., slicing, mechanical grinding/pol- ishing, and ion milling. III. Results and Discussion 1. Fractographic feature Figure 5 shows the change in the fracture surface of the SiC-70mol% AIN specimens tested under different conditions. The specimen tested at room temperature showed a typical fracture surface of brittle materials, with a lot of macroscopic crack branchings and undulations. The fracture mode at room temperature was transgranular as shown at high magnification. The fracture surface at low magnification for the specimen tested at 1773 K ap- pears flatter than that at room temperature. Whereas, un- der this condition the fracture feature almost changed to intergranular mode. Furthermore, at lower crosshead speed the feature of intergranular fracture was more re- markable, and the fracture surface became macroscopi- cally rough as shown at low magnification. The variation in the MSP load-deflection curves was well reflected by the change in the fractographic feature. Figure 6 shows the tensile surface of the MSP specimen failed at 1773 K and at low crosshead speed. Many smaller cracks were formed near the tip of a larger crack. The crack path was zigzag, and the crack opening was relatively large as compared with its length. As is well known, the decrease in grain size greatly enhances the grain boundary sliding, to which the brittle to ductile transition behavior is attributed In the brittle materials at room temperature, the cata- strophic failure is caused by the unstable growth of preex- isting cracks with sharp tip. At high temperatures, the crack tip can be blunted by the local grain boundary slid- ing deformation (14), as a result, the crack opening in- creases without further extension, as shown in Fig. 6. The non-linear deformation was analyzed to be caused by the blunting of a crack tip. Summary and Conclusions Dense SiC-AlN solid solution and its composite samples were fabricated by in-situ reaction HIP-sintering of Si, C and AIN powder premixtures. Their fracture strength and deformation behavior were evaluated up to 1773K by using a newly-developed Small Punch (SP) testing method, in relation with the ultrafine grained-mi- crostructure. The SiC-AlN alloys showed much higher strength than the monolithic SiC and AIN at temperatures up to 1473 K due to the beneficial effect of fine-grained micro- structure. Below 1473 K, the load-deflection curves were elastic, and the typically brittle fracture occurred pre- dominately in a transgranular mode. When the testing temperature was raised to 1773K, non-linear deviation appeared in the load-deflection curves before the final failure; and the amount increased with decreasing strain rate. With the appearance of the non-linear deformation, the fracture mode also changed from transgranular to in- tergranular. The non-linear deformation behavior was discussed in association with the change in the fractographic feature. It was considered that the non-lin-
