Abstract 
Eutectic high entropy alloys possess both excellent plasticity and high strength. 
To explore their potential engineering applications, the diffusion behavior of elements, 
the interfacial microstructure and shear strength of the diffusion bonded joint between 
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic high entropy alloy and GH4169 superalloy were studied. The 
width of diffusion zone increased with bonding temperature increasing and the voids 
gradually disappeared due to the increased diffusion rate of the elements and the 
high-temperature creep. A new layer of face-center-cubic (FCC) phase, which was 
caused by the difference in diffusion rate of the elements between B2 phase and FCC 
phase in the eutectic high entropy alloy, generated in the diffusion zone and increased

## Page 5

2 
 
the shear strength of joints. The width of newly generated FCC phase layer in front of 
the original FCC phase was wider than that in front of the B2 phase. The shear 
strength of the resultant joints increased and the fracture mode transformed from 
brittle fracture to ductile fracture with bonding temperature increasing. The maximum 
shear strength of 528 MPa was obtained and the dimples on fracture surfaces were 
found at the bonding temperature of 1050 oC. 
Key words：Diffusion bonding; High entropy alloy; Superalloy; Microstructure 
evolution; Shear strength 
1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) [1] are composed of at least five elements in equal 
molar or non-equal molar ratios. Due to the unique multiprincipal element 
composition, HEAs exhibit excellent processability, good thermoelectric, outstanding 
properties, corrosion resistance and high temperature stability [2]. However, most 
single-phased FCC structured HEAs are ductile but not strong enough, and 
single-phased BCC structured HEAs could be very strong but brittle. In addition, 
other shortcomings of single-phased HEAs such as low fluidity, poor casting 
properties and severe segregation hinder the engineering application of single-phased 
HEAs. Lu et al. [3] achieved the AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA) 
with hard body-centered-cubic (BCC) phases and soft face-centered-cubic (FCC), 
thus simultaneously obtaining the excellent plasticity and high strength. The 
outstanding castability and remarkable mechanical properties guarantee that EHEA 
can be used in some extreme environments, for example, in high-temperature

## Page 6

3 
 
structural parts by substituting the superalloys [4]. 
The hotspots of current research about HEAs are mainly focused on material 
design rather than material processing. Dong et al. [5], Jiang et al. [6] and Wang et al. 
[7] respectively designed AlCrFeNiMox, CoFeNixVMoy and AlCoCrFeNiTix HEAs, 
and investigated the microstructural evolution and mechanical properties. Zhang et al. 
[8] and Gao et al. [9] found that the excellent strength-ductility combination in 
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA was related to the coupling effect between ductile FCC and 
brittle BCC phases and the migration and interaction of deformation twin. Lu et al. 
[10] further investigated the near-eutectic and eutectic AlCoCrFeNix (x= 2.0, 2.1 and 
2.2) HEAs and pointed out that the deviation from the eutectic composition had no 
remarkably effect on the mechanical properties. It can be seen that in current 
investigations the composition design, mechanical performance, phase formation and 
microstructures of HEAs were comprehensively investigated and the HEAs with 
outstanding mechanical properties were obtained. However, the high level of 
mechanical properties is not the only remarkable feature for the potential engineering 
application of any structural metals, the suitability of HEAs for material processing is 
also necessary. Among all the processing methods, welding is an indispensable step, 
but current researches on welding of HEAs are rare and mostly concentrate on similar 
materials. Wu et al. [11] investigated the gas tungsten arc welding and electron beam 
welding of CrMnFeCoNi HEA, and found that the yield strength of both joints was 
higher than that of the base metal. Jo et al. [12] respectively realized the laser beam 
welding (LBW) and friction stir welding (FSW) of CrMnFeCoNi HEA. Compared

## Page 7

4 
 
with FSW, the LBW joints exhibited lower elongation and tensile strength, which was 
caused by the inclusion of sulfides and oxides in the LBW seam. The result showed 
that the FSW was more suitable for welding HEAs than LBW. Zhu et al. and 
Shaysultanov et al. respectively studied the FSW of CoCrFeNiAl0.3 [13], 
Co16Fe28Ni28Cr28 [14] and carbon-doped CoCrFeNiMn [15] HEAs, and joints with 
high strength and hardness were obtained. Li et al. [16] obtained the sound joint of 
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA by rotary friction welding. At the friction pressure of 200 MPa, 
due to sufficient dynamic recrystallization, the interface disappeared and then the 
tensile strength and elongation of the joint increased. The above studies obtained the 
HEA joints with high strength through a variety of welding methods, which indicated 
that welding processes were suitable for HEAs and could accelerate the engineering 
application of HEAs. However, the homogeneous HEA welded joints cannot meet 
some special requirements in high-temperature structural parts such as engines [4]. 
Therefore, hybrid joints of HEAs to conventional alloys should be studied to 
accelerate the practical application of HEAs and extend their application scopes. 
Sokkalingam et al. [17] investigated the weldability of dissimilar joint between 
AISI304 austenitic stainless steel and Al0.1CoCrFeNi HEA by conventional gas 
tungsten arc welding. The tensile strength of dissimilar joints was higher than that of 
HEA but lower than that of stainless steel due to the coarsening of the grains in 
heat-affected zone. Lei et al. [18] investigated the diffusion bonding of 
Al0.85CoCrFeNi HEA to TiAl, and obtained the maximum shear strength at the 
bonding temperature of 850 °C for 1.5 h under pressure of 30 MPa. Li et al. [19]

## Page 8

5 
 
further realized the diffusion bonding of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA to TiAl. The much 
higher shear strength of 449 MPa was obtained at bonding temperature of 950 oC for 
0.5 h under pressure of 15 MPa, which was contributed to the interlocking effect in 
diffusion zone. These studies showed that diffusion bonding with the advantages of 
little deformation, excellent welding quality and low heat input, could be a more 
suitable method for dissimilar joining of HEAs to conventional alloys. 
Diffusion bonding has been widely applied in manufacturing of some 
aero-engine components, such as hollow vanes [20], which are manufactured with 
superalloy due to its outstanding oxidation resistance, mechanical properties and 
corrosion resistance at high temperatures [21]. The microstructure and properties of 
diffusion bonded superalloys joints have been systematically studied. Zhang et al. [22] 
studied the diffusion bonding of GH3128 superalloy and hydrogenated TC4 alloy by 
designing Nb/Ni intermediate layer and achieved the maximum shear strength of 270 
MPa at bonding temperature of 860 oC for 80 min under pressure of 10 MPa. Xiong et 
al. [23] studied the diffusion bonding of GH4099 using pure nickel as the intermediate 
layer. The maximum tensile strength of 1183 MPa was achieved at bonding 
temperature of 1120 oC for 1.5 h under pressure of 4 MPa. Zhu et al. [24] investigated 
the diffusion behavior of hydrogenated Ti2AlNb-based alloys and Ti-6Al-4V alloys 
during vacuum diffusion bonding process. With the addition of hydrogen, the 
plasticity of the joint and atomic diffusion ability enhanced, and the shear strength 
increased to 711 MPa. As a new material, AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA also might be used in 
high temperature parts in engine of aerospace industry [4]. Therefore, it is necessary

to investigate the diffusion bonding of EHEA to GH4169 superalloy for further 
promoting the application of HEAs. 
In this work, AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA was vacuum diffusion bonded to GH4169 
superalloy. Microstructure evolution and shear strength of diffusion bonded joint were 
studied, and the diffusion behavior of alloying elements and interfacial formation 
mechanism at different bonding temperatures were analyzed in detail.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Microstructure evolution 
As shown in Fig. 5, the precipitation of acicular type or lamellar type δ phase at 
950 oC in GH4169 superalloy is caused by the following two ways: (I) the δ phase 
precipitates non-uniformly on the grain boundaries and twin boundaries through the 
cellular reaction of the grain boundaries; (II) the δ phase precipitates directly from the 
grain interior, which is related to the precipitated γ″ phase. The γ″ phase dissolves in

## Page 20

17 
 
matrix with temperature range of 870 oC-930 oC, and the phase transition of γ″ to δ 
occurs when the temperature exceeded 900 oC [34]. Asadian et al. [29] pointed out 
that the δ phase precipitated in the temperature range of 780-980 oC, and the fastest 
precipitation temperature was about 890-900 oC. Due to the effect of high temperature, 
the long acicular δ phase gradually dissolved first, then transformed to short acicular, 
and finally changed to spherical. When the hard and brittle δ phase precipitated along 
the grain boundaries, the precipitate-free zones formed near the grain boundary 
became the weakness zones with low strength and plasticity, resulting in the 
appearance of cracks and voids during shear test. Short cracks gradually increased 
with the increase in the number of δ phase, and finally induced a decrease in strength 
and plasticity of the joint [35]. The terminal spheroidization of the laths in EHEA 
leads to a certain concentration gradient and atomic diffusion of the lamellar 
microstructure with the increase of temperature. With the bonding time increasing, 
atoms migrated from the terminal of the lamellar to the adjacent lamellar, and the 
melting of the terminal shortens and spheroidizes the lamellar [36]. With the 
temperature increasing, the width of diffusion zone gradually increases and the voids 
decrease, which is related to the diffusion rate. In HEAs, different elements randomly 
occupy the crystal lattice and the different atoms surround each atom, which lead to 
the fact that cooperation of different kinds of elements is required during diffusion 
process. This phenomenon increases the diffusion resistance and reduces the diffusion 
rate, resulting in a low disappearance rate of voids at the interface. However, with the 
temperature increasing, atoms could easily jump out of a lattice site and jump into a

## Page 21

18 
 
vacancy due to higher diffusion rate, and then the diffusion of elements become 
sufficient and the voids decrease. 
4.2. Elemental distribution and diffusion behavior 
As shown in Fig. 6, the content variation of Al near the interface shows a 
gradient from steep to gentle, while the content of Ni in the diffusion zone first 
decreased and then increased, which was related to newly formed FCC phase in front 
of the original phases because of the sluggish diffusion effect of HEAs. In HEAs, the 
growth and migration of one grain requires the coordinated mobility of all elements in 
the system. Therefore, the diffusion rates of elements in different phases should be 
compared to explore the diffusion mechanism during the bonding process [27]. For 
Fe-Co-Cr-Al-Ni system, the sequence of the fastest to slowest element is similar as 
previously reported HEAs: Cr > Fe > Co > Ni. The diffusion coefficient of Ni is 
smaller than the other elements, which leads to the lower diffusion rate of the 
elements in Ni-rich B2 phase than FCC phase. In addition, compared with the 
chemical environment created by adjacent atoms, the structure of the HEAs has a 
greater impact on the values of diffusion coefficients at elevated temperatures [37]. 
Due to the higher activation energy value (349.9 kJ∙mol-1) of the element of Al than 
that of the elements of Fe, Co and Cr (between 268.6 and 280.1 kJ∙mol-1), the average 
level of activation energy of diffusion in the lattice of Al-containing B2 phase 
increases, which lead to a decrease in diffusion rate [38]. On the contrary, sufficient 
diffusion occurred in the FCC phase because the elements of Fe, Co, and Cr diffused 
faster. In addition, GH4169 superalloy with FCC crystal structure tends to have a

## Page 22

19 
 
solid solution reaction with the FCC phase in EHEA. Therefore, during the diffusion 
bonding process, the migration velocity of the elements in FCC phase is faster than 
that of the elements in B2 phase, and a layer of FCC phase forms in front of the 
original phases, which further hinders the migration of the elements in B2 phase. As 
shown in Fig.6 and 7, the diffusion distance of the FCC phase side significantly 
increases compared to that of the B2 phase side at the same temperature, which 
indicates that sufficient diffusion occurs on the FCC side. 
According to the Arrehenius formula, higher is the temperature, larger is the 
diffusion coefficient, which leads to a flat interface and a reduced void number. In 
addition, the decrease in the number of voids is also related to the high-temperature 
creep behavior of base metals (BMs) during diffusion bonding process. Since the 
creep rate is controlled by temperature and stress, the deformation of the BMs near 
the interface is difficult under low temperature and stress, and then the decrease in the 
number of voids is mainly through diffusion rather than creep. Although the effect of 
diffusion closes the voids and reduces the number of voids, the size of voids may 
become larger. The creep rate increases as the temperature rises, and then the voids 
decrease dramatically and disappear eventually due to the coupling effect of diffusion 
and creep. The decrease in the void number is beneficial for improving shear strength. 
However, compared with the diffusion bonding of single-phased FCC structured 
HEAs, the voids in the interface did not completely disappear even at 1050 oC, and 
diffusion distance was shorter in this experiment. Liu et al. [39] studied the diffusion 
bonding of CoCrFeMnNi single-phased FCC structured HEA to 304 stainless steel

## Page 23

20 
 
and found that the original interface completely disappeared at 1000 oC. The width of 
the diffusion zone reached 14.5 μm, which is wider than the maximum distance of 
8.32 μm in this study. This phenomenon further confirms that the diffusion of EHEA 
is not as sufficient as the single-phased HEA due to the severe sluggish diffusion of 
the element of Al [37]. 
4.3. Formation mechanism of the diffusion zone 
Base on the above analysis, the forming process of EHEA/GH4169 superalloy 
joint can be illustrated in Fig. 10. Before diffusion bonding, GH4169 superalloy 
enriches with the elements of Ni, Fe and Cr, while EHEA consists of Al-Ni rich B2 
phase and Fe-Co-Cr rich FCC phase. At the beginning of diffusion bonding, physical 
contact occurs in the interface, and the actual contact area of EHEA/GH4169 
superalloy joint is enlarged due to the plastic deformation under certain pressure and 
temperature. The diffusion of elements is restricted and no diffusion zone formed. As 
the elements continued to diffuse, the diffusion zone forms near the interface and 
gradually widens. Due to the high diffusion rate of the elements in FCC phase of 
EHEA, a layer of FCC phase enriches with the elements of Fe, Co and Cr formed in 
the diffusion zone. The δ phase dissolves and a layer of γ phase without precipitation 
appears on the GH4169 superalloy side. During diffusion bonding process, the 
element of Co diffuses from the EHEA side to the Ni-based solid solution side, and 
since the element of Co is a γ phase forming element, a layer of Ni-rich γ phase with 
the same lattice as the GH4169 superalloy formed near the interface [40]. After 
completion of diffusion bonding, voids on the interface obviously reduce and sound

## Page 24

21 
 
bonding is achieved. The reason for formation of FCC phase layer in the diffusion 
zone is related to the diffusion rate of different elements. The segregation of the 
elements of Fe, Co and Cr at the interface leads to the formation of the FCC phase 
because the elements of Fe, Co, and Cr in FCC phase diffuse faster than the elements 
of Al and Ni in B2 phase. The formation of FCC phase can effectively improve the 
yield strength of the joints since the FCC phase has enough plastic deformation 
capacity. In addition, the width of the FCC phase in front of the original FCC phase 
is wider than that in front of the original B2 phase, which can be explained with the 
following two aspects: (I) the role of the element of Al. Al decreases the diffusion rate 
of the EHEA system [27], thus the Al-rich B2 phase restricts the formation of FCC 
phase; (II) difference in concentration gradient between two phases. Downhill 
diffusion along the direction of the concentration gradient happens to the FCC phase 
near the FCC phase. However, due to the decrease in the activity of the element of Ni 
in FCC phase near the B2 phase, the element of Ni diffuses from the GH4169 
superalloy side into the EHEA side of higher content in the element of Ni, which is 
contrary to the concentration gradient. The uphill diffusion in FCC phase near the B2 
phase decreases the free energy, and then the elements in FCC phase near the FCC 
phase diffuse faster than that in FCC phase near the B2 phase.

4.4. Shear strength and fracture mechanism of bonded joint 
Inadequate mutual diffusion of elements occurs at low bonding temperatures, 
resulting in weak metallurgical bonding and low shear strength. Shear strength 
reached the maximum value at 1050 oC, which was attributed to the decrease in the 
number of voids. Besides, the width of diffusion zone and the ratio of FCC phase 
increased, indicating that sufficient diffusion occurred and thus the shear strength of 
joints increased. The shear strength is closely related to the diffusion ability of EHEA. 
At low temperature, due to the sluggish diffusion effect, the diffusion of elements is 
insufficient, while increased kinetic energy of the elements at elevated temperature 
leads to the sufficient diffusion of elements. The sufficient diffusion of elements 
reduces the number of voids at interface and increases the bonding rate of the 
interface, resulting in an enhancement in shear strength.

The joint at 1050 oC reached the maximum yield strength of 915 MPa with 79.6%

joint will continue to increase due to the weakened sluggish diffusion effect. However, 
the obvious deformation appeared in the joint at 1050 oC and trended to increase

and low deformation rate of the joint, the bonding temperature between 1000 oC and 
1050 oC would be recommended for actual manufacture. 
The failure mode was a brittle fracture at 1000 oC as illustrated in Fig. 9(a) and 
(c). When the bonding temperature is below 1000 oC, the δ phase precipitated at the 
grain boundary of GH4169 superalloy hinders the sliding by pinning effect, thereby 
strengthening the grain boundary. The high-strength grain boundaries make the slip 
easily move to the inner of the grain, thus manifesting as transgranular cleavage 
fracture. However, as shown in Fig. 9(b) and (d), the formation of a large number of 
dimples at 1050 oC indicates that plastic deformation occurs during the fracture 
process, which transforms the fracture mode from brittle fracture to ductile fracture 
and improves the strength and toughness of the joint. Furthermore, the appearance of 
trench-like bands on the EHEA side at 1050 oC could be caused by the following 
reason. The soft FCC phase could be stretched adequately during the deformation 
process, resulting in a better plasticity of the FCC phase than B2 phase under the same 
test conditions. Therefore, during the shear test, an uncoordinated deformation 
between FCC phase and B2 phase results in the fracture in the interface between FCC

5. Conclusions 
(1) Sound diffusion bonded joint of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA to GH4169 superalloy 
was obtained. With the increase of bonding temperature, the shear strength of 
resultant joints increased, and the maximum shear strength of 528 MPa was achieved 
at 1050 oC. The fracture mode gradually transformed from brittle fracture to ductile 
fracture with the temperature increasing, and trench-like bands appeared on the 
fracture surface of EHEA side at 1050 oC. 
(2) A layer of FCC phase formed in the diffusion zone and gradually widened 
with the increase of bonding temperature, which enhanced the shear strength of the 
joints. The width of newly generated FCC phase layer 
in front of the original FCC 
phase is wider than that in front of the B2 phase due to the decreased diffusion rate of 
the element of Al in B2 phase and the uphill diffusion of the element of Ni in newly 
generated FCC phase near the B2 phase. 
(3) With the temperature increasing, the width of diffusion zone increased and 
voids gradually decreased due to the coupling effect of larger diffusion coefficient and
