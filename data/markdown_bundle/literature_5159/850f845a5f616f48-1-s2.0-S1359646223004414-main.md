ABSTRACT 
The extraordinary work hardening ability and fracture toughness of the face-centered cubic medium-entropy 
alloy (MEA) at cryogenic temperature make them good candidates for harsh applications. In this work, we 
developed a hierarchical twin architecture in coarse grained CoCrNi MEA using multidirectional cryoforging 
(MDCF) technique and low-temperature annealing. The unique substructure brought a notable enhancement in 
the cryogenic tensile strength-ductility balance, as well as an impressive 10
6
-cycle fatigue strength of up to 1100 
MPa at cryogenic temperature, which is superior to other cryogenic alloys. The exceptional fatigue property of 
MDCF-MEA is ascribed to the synergistic effect of high-density dislocations and a dense nanotwin/microband 
network, providing high strength and the ability to suppress local surface roughening and crack initiation. Our 
work demonstrates a practical route to further extend CoCrNi MEA for the cryogenic application.

fracture toughness, which has been recorded to 459 MPa ⋅ m

is imperative to conduct further investigations into the fatigue resistance

It is effective to introduce substructures that can enhance overall 
strength or decrease cyclic plastic strain localization to improve fatigue 
property. Notably, an enhanced fatigue endurance limit was achieved 
through introducing a high density of dislocations and oriented growth- 
in nanotwins (NTs) in polycrystalline Cu or high-entropy alloys, which 
stems from high strength and a distinct delocalized fatigue mechanism 
that can mitigate local fatigue damage and does not destroy the stability 
of substructure during cyclic loading [ 2 , 3 ]. Although there have been 
some reports of effectively improving the strength and ductility of 
CoCrNi by severe plastic deformation [ 4 , 5 ]. Considering that extreme 
processing methods are difficult for industrial applications, we still need 
to explore the possibility of new processing methods. Recently, a bulk 
nanostructuring method, multidirectional cryoforging (MDCF) tech -
nique, was proposed to fabricate a multiscale and hierarchical twin ar -
chitecture in the titanium or high-entropy alloy, resulting in significant 
improvements in their cryogenic tensile properties [ 6 , 7 ]. Despite these 
findings, there is currently a lack of knowledge regarding the impact of 
hierarchical nanotwin architecture introduced by MDCF on materials ’ 
fatigue behavior. To address this gap, the authors employed the MDCF 
technique to produce CoCrNi with a hierarchical architecture. An 
extraordinary 10
6

study proceeds by exploring the underlying mechanism behind this 
remarkable fatigue property. 
The fabrication process of the CoCrNi MEA involved arc melting of 
high-purity elements ( > 99.9%) in a high-purity argon atmosphere, at 
least five remelting cycles to promote chemical homogeneity. The 
resulting as-cast ingot was subjected to the MDCF process along x-, y- 
and z- axes at 77 K with high strain rate ( > 10 /s) with a reduction of 
~10%, as illustrated in Fig. 1 a. To relieve the undesirable residual stress

nanoscale twins and microbands within MDCF-MEA. The average NT

Secondly, we have successfully introduced hierarchical NT network 
structure into MDCF-MEA through MDCF process. These TBs/micro -
bands naturally act as barriers for preventing dislocation motion. In this 
case, numerous dislocations are believed to be confined in the short- 
range cell size. Therefore, the possibility of dislocation rearrangement 
are effectively reduced, thus avoiding the drastic change of dislocation 
microstructure even under higher fatigue loading (Supplementary 
Fig. S6). Meanwhile, TBs and microbands are in a state of low excess 
energy. Due to the reduced thermodynamic driving force, the migration 
kinetics of network structure in MDCF-MEA is considerably reduced 
which makes them more stable suffering fatigue load [23] . Thus, the 
network structure can significantly mitigate the evolution of surface 
roughening and fatigue crack initiation, and finally improves the fatigue 
resistance at cryogenic temperature. 
Hence, through MDCF technique and low-temperature annealing, 
CoCrNi MEA achieves a good cryogenic strength-ductility balance. 
Moreover, the synergistic effect of high-density dislocations and the 
hierarchical NT structure brings the high tensile strength and low B , 
which ultimately resulted in the impressive 10
6
-cycle fatigue strength at 
cryogenic temperature in comparison to traditional materials. Our 
findings provide a promising method for elevating high-cycle fatigue 
property of CoCrNi MEA at cryogenic temperature, which is also widely 
applicable to numerous FCC alloys.
