Mechanical Properties and Microstructure Evolution
of Cryorolled AlCoCrFeNi-Reinforced Aluminum
Matrix Composites Tensile Tested at Room
and Cryogenic Temperatures
KAIGUANG LUO, GANG LEI, SHILEI LIU, CHARLIE KONG, and HAILIANG YU
The AlCoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy particles (HEAp)-reinforced aluminum matrix composites
(AMCs) were fabricated by stir casting and subsequent cryorolling. The tensile mechanical
properties of the HEAp/AMCs were investigated at room temperature (298 K) and cryogenic
temperature (173 K). The microstructures of the HEAp/AMCs were studied using scanning
electron microscopy (SEM), electron backscatter diﬀraction, and transmission electron
microscopy (TEM). The tensile tested results show that both the ultimate tensile strength and
elongation of HEAp/AMCs sheets at cryogenic temperature are higher than those at room
temperature. The ultimate tensile strength of 3 wt pct HEAp/AMCs is increased from 204 MPa
(room temperature) to 251 MPa (cryogenic temperature). The improvement in strengths of the
HEAp/AMCs at cryogenic temperature is due to the coeﬃcient of thermal expansion mismatch
reinforcement and the dislocation reinforcement. The HEAp/AMCs have a higher elongation in
the cryogenic environment, in which the particles have a higher length–diameter ratio along the
tensile direction. The molecular dynamics simulation of void nucleation in aluminum alloy
deformed at 298 K and 173 K shows that the void nucleation threshold is higher and the
nucleation rate is slower at cryogenic temperature, which contribute to the higher elongation of
the HEAp/AMCs.

I. INTRODUCTION
ALUMINUM matrix composites (AMCs) are widely
used in a spectrum of ﬁelds, such as aerospace,
automobiles, and military equipment, due to their low
density, high speciﬁc strength, and good corrosion
resistance.
[1–4] The reinforcement materials commonly
used in AMCs include ceramic particles [5–7] and metallic
particles. In the case of the ceramic particles, they can
improve the strength of the AMCs, but generally lead to
a signiﬁcant decrease in machinability.
[8] Also, the weak
bonding interface between ceramic particles and alu-
minum alloy matrix results in diﬃculty in secondary
processing.
[9] Compared to the ceramic particles, the
metallic particles provide a natural interfacial bonding
interaction with the aluminum alloy matrix, [10] which
can signiﬁcantly improve the formability of AMCs, and
then these AMCs have potential to further strengthened
via deformation processing. [11]
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are composed of ﬁve or
more major elements, [12] which have attracted the
attention of scientists worldwide owing to their excellent
mechanical properties. The properties of HEAs, such as
ultra-high strength, good machinability, excellent struc-
tural stability, and super cryogenic mechanical proper-
ties, make the HEA particles (HEAp) an ideal choice for
reinforcement in AMCs.
[13–16] Lu et al. [17] used the
squeeze casting process to synthesize CoNiFeAl 0.4-
Ti0.6Cr0.5 HEAp/AA7050 matrix composites with a
tensile strength of 712 MPa. Karthik et al.[18] fabricated
CoCrFeNi HEAp/AA5083 matrix composites using the
friction deposition process and found that no brittle
intermetallic compounds were formed at the interface
between HEAp and the matrix. Chen et al.
[19] used
powder metallurgy to prepare CoNiFeAl 0.4Ti0.6Cr0.5
HEAp/AA6061 matrix composites with ultimate tensile
strength (UTS) and elongation of 378 MPa and 8.4 pct,
respectively. In addition, some studies show that the
AlCoCrFeNi HEA with BCC structure had higher
mechanical properties and is suitable to be reinforce-
ment particles added to AMCs.
[20,21] Compared to
ceramic particles, the interface wettability between
AlCoCrFeNi particles and Al matrix was more
excellent.
[22]
Previous studies show that Al alloys and HEAs have
excellent cryogenic mechanical properties. Manikandan
et al.
[23] investigated the mechanical properties of
friction stir welded AA2219 joints at room and cryo-
genic temperatures and found that both the strength and
elongation of joints at cryogenic temperatures were
higher than those at room temperature. Similarly, Liu
et al.
[24] reported that both the strength and elongation
of AA6061 sheets at cryogenic temperature were higher
than those at room temperature. Zhong et al.
[25] found
that the Al 1.19Co2CrFeNi1.81 HEA exhibited an excel-
lent strength–plasticity combination due to the defor-
mation dominated by nanotwins as well as dislocation
slip at cryogenic temperature. In addition, Naeem

II. MATERIALS AND METHODS
In the present study, AA1099 alloy was utilized as the
matrix of the AMCs. The chemical composition of the
AA1099 alloy was Al–0.005Cu–0.003Fe–0.002Si.
AlCoCrFeNi HEAp was used as the reinforcement of
AMCs. The HEAp was procured from Yanbang New
Materials Co., LTD, which was prepared by following
steps. Weighing a certain amount of ﬁve metal elements
into an induction heating furnace at 1873 K, and then
spraying the fused elements into fog beads by a
high-pressure inert gas ﬂow with a pressure of
4.5 MPa. Subsequently, the fog beads were processed
into powders. Figure 1 shows the HEAp/AMCs prepa-
ration and processing technology procedure. 1.5, 3, and
4.5 wt pct HEAp/AMCs ingots were prepared by the stir
casting process at 1073 K (Figure 1(a)). Then, the
synthesized ingots were extruded into 3.3 mm thick
sheets at 713 K (Figure 1(b)). To obtain HEAp/AMCs
sheets with identical initial state before cryorolling
except for mass fraction of HEAp, these sheets were
annealed at 713 K for 1 hour. They were cryorolled at
77 K in liquid nitrogen environment. The cryorolled
HEAp/AMCs sheets with rolling reductions of 30, 60,
and 90 pct (Figure 1(c)) were kept to analyze their
mechanical properties. In addition, to determine the
advance of HEAp/AMCs, their mechanical properties
were compared with the pure AA1099 alloy.

A. Microstructure Characterization of High Entropy
Alloy Particles
Figure 2(a) shows the SEM image of HEAp.
Figure 2(b) illustrates the distribution of particle sizes
of HEAp, and the average particle size is 3.8 lm. The

Figures 2(d) and (e) show the EDS diﬀraction and

B. Microstructure of HEAp/AMCs
Figures 3(a) and (b) show the TEM images of the 3 wt
pct HEAp/AMCs before cryorolling. In Figure 3(a), a
higher dislocation density was observed around the
HEAp, and more dislocation entanglement regions were
formed. The dislocation cell structure was gradually
shaped with the upsurge of dislocation entanglement
regions. Figure 3(b) shows the interfacial bonding
between HEAp and the aluminum alloy matrix at high
magniﬁcation. A uniformly distributed interface was
formed without microvoid generation. Figure 3(c)
shows the HAADF image of HEAp/AMCs. It has been
observed that bright white particles are found in the
aluminum alloy matrix. To determine the elemental
distribution, the yellow rectangle area in Figure 3(d) was
analyzed using EDS scanning, as shown in Figure 3(e).
The major peak in EDS spectra depicts the abundance
of Al in the matrix of AMCs. However, the EDS peaks
of Cr, Co, Fe, and Ni elements were also detected. The
peak intensities of Co, Fe, and Ni were similar and
higher than those of Cr. Further, EDS line scanning
analysis was carried out on the labeled straight-line area
in Figure 3(d), and the results are shown in Figure 3(f).
The black curve represents the intensity of the HAADF
diﬀraction peak, whose intensity increased signiﬁcantly
when passing through the particle. The red curve shows
the diﬀraction intensity of the aluminum element, which
decreased slightly as it passed through the particle. In
addition, diﬀraction peaks of Cr, Co, Fe, and Ni
elements were also found, which belong to HEAp.
Figure 4 shows the SEM images of 3 wt pct HEAp/
AMCs. As shown in Figure 4(a), before deformation,
the HEAp has a net-like structure that is uniformly
distributed in the aluminum alloy matrix. At 30 pct
rolling reduction, the reinforcement of HEAp in the
aluminum alloy matrix was aligned along the rolling
direction in a streamlined strip (Figure 4(b)). With a
further increase in rolling reduction, the spacing
between the reinforcements decreases further. At 90
pct rolling reduction, the reinforcement was evenly
distributed in the aluminum alloy matrix, as shown in
Figure 4(d). This is the same with the previous study
that the cryorolling could avoid the void appearance
near the interface between particles and matrix.

C. Mechanical Properties of HEAp/AMCs
The mechanical properties of pure aluminum and
HEAp/AMCs after diﬀerent rolling reductions at room
and cryogenic temperatures were further studied.
Figure 5 shows the tensile stress–strain curves of the
HEAp/AMCs after diﬀerent rolling reductions at room
temperature (298 K) and cryogenic temperature
(173 K). The blue curve shows the stress–strain curve
of the materials after diﬀerent rolling reductions at
cryogenic temperature. Under 30 pct rolling reduction,
the tensile strength of pure aluminum (Figure 5(a)) was
68 ± 2.6 MPa at room temperature and 72 ± 1.3 MPa
in a cryogenic environment. Compared with room
temperature, the cryogenic mechanical properties of
pure aluminum had a certain degree of improvement
(5.9 pct), but the improvement was small. However, the
cryogenic mechanical properties of the HEAp/AMCs
were much better than pure aluminum. The tensile
strengths of 3 wt pct HEAp/AMCs (Figure 5(c)) after
30, 60, and 90 pct rolling reduction were 154 ± 0.9,
179 ± 0.4, and 204 ± 3.2 MPa at room temperature,
respectively. While, as the decrease of temperatures, the
tensile strengths were 185 ± 0.4, 219 ± 0.9, and
251 ± 4.0 MPa, respectively, at cryogenic temperature.
The mechanical properties of the Tensile-173 HEAp/
AMCs were greatly improved in cryogenic temperature,
with an average increase of 21.8 pct. After adding 4.5 wt
pct HEAp, the cryogenic tensile strength of the HEAp/
AMCs (Figure 5(d)) at diﬀerent rolling reductions
increased from 175 ± 2.5, 198 ± 1.6, 216 ± 3.6 to
209 ± 1 MPa, 241 ± 2.3, and 263 ± 1.8 MPa, respec-
tively. The cryogenic mechanical properties of 4.5 wt pct
HEAp/AMCs increased by 21 pct on average under the
three rolling reduction conditions. With the addition of
HEAp, the cryogenic mechanical properties of the
AMCs were greatly improved. In addition, for the
AMCs with the same HEAp mass fraction, the failure
strain of the HEAp/AMCs sheets tensile tested at the
same temperature decreases with increasing in the
rolling reduction. For 3 wt pct HEAp/AMCs samples,
the failure strains decrease from 12.2 ± 0.3, 9.8 ± 0.2 to
4.9 ± 0.2 pct at 298 K with increasing in rolling
reduction from 30, 60 to 90 pct. Therefore, the HEAp/
AMCs still had a good ductility even after a large rolling
reduction. Also, the increase of HEAp particles contents
had a limited eﬀect on the strain of AMCs. When the
rolling reduction was 60 pct, the failure strains of the
1.5, 3, and 4.5 wt pct HEAp/AMCs were 12.3 ± 3.5,
11.3 ± 0.9, and 10.0 ± 0.2 pct, respectively. The failure
strain of the HEAp/AMCs did not signiﬁcantly decrease
with the increase of HEAp contents with the same
rolling reduction.

V. CONCLUSIONS
In the present work, the mechanical properties and
microstructure of HEAp/AMCs at room temperature
and cryogenic temperature were investigated. The main
conclusions are as follows:
(1) The tensile strengths of 3 wt pct HEAp/AMCs at
room temperature were 154, 179, and 204 MPa
under different rolling reductions (30, 60, and 90
pct), and cryogenic tensile strengths were 185,
219, and 251 MPa, respectively. The cryogenic
tensile strength of 3 wt pct HEAp/AMCs
increased by about 21.8 pct compared with room
temperature.
