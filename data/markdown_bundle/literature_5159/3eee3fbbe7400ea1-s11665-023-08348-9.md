TECHNICAL ARTICLE
Tensile and Low-Cycle Fatigue Behavior of Dissimilar
Friction Welds of Alloy 718/Alloy 720Li
P.V. Neminathan, R. Damodaram, G.M. Karthik, and K. Gopinath
Submitted: 3 February 2023 / Revised: 30 April 2023 / Accepted: 15 May 2023 / Published online: 31 May 2023
This study investigates the microstructural and mechanical properties of dissimilar alloy 718/720Li joints,
contemplated for use in hot-end applications of aircraft engine components with service temperatures of up
to 730 /C176C. Microstructural characterization, microhardness, and room-temperature tensile tests were per-
formed, followed by high-temperature tensile tests (650 /C176C) on both as-welded and post-direct aging heat-
treated conditions to analyze the performance of weld joints at high temperature. Results indicate that the
friction welded joint interface in its as-welded condition exhibits lower hardness (240 HV at the heat
affected zone) than the alloy 718 base material due to the dissolution of strengthening precipitates. However,
post-weld direct aging (DA) with an alloy 718 aging cycle has improved the microhardness to be compa-
rable to the parent materials. The peak hardness value of 478 HV achieved at the interface region is 26%
higher than the as-welded condition. The dissimilar 718/720Li FRW joints in direct aging condition show
room and high-temperature tensile strengths of 1374 and 1172 MPa, respectively. Similarly, during low-
cycle fatigue testing, 718/720Li FRW joints in direct aging condition withstand up to 3648 cycles, com-
parable to the low-cycle fatigue life of the alloy 718 parent material, with failures away from the FRW weld
interface and into the alloy 718 base material. The cyclic softening and hysteresis trend obtained during
low-cycle fatigue tests of dissimilar 718/720Li friction welded joints are comparable with alloy 718 parent
material. The dissimilar 718/720Li friction welded joints have a peak stress of 1097 MPa, which falls
between low-strength alloy 718 and high-strength alloy 720Li. These ﬁndings conﬁrm that friction welding
of dissimilar nickel-based superalloys, such as alloy 718 with alloy 720Li, is promising for adaptation in
aero engine applications.
Keywords alloy 720Li, alloy 718, dissimilar welding, low-cycle
fatigue, rotary friction welding
1. Introduction
Heat-resistant superalloys are selected for hot-end applica-
tions of aero-gas turbine engines due to their potential
capabilities of very good tensile, fatigue, and creep properties
at higher service temperatures. Nickel-chromium precipitation
hardenable superalloy 718 is a high-strength and corrosion-
resistant alloy used up to the service temperature of 650 /C176 C,
particularly for critical rotating disk applications in the high-
pressure compressors, where the service temperature exceeds
the capability of the lightweight titanium alloys (Ref 1-3).
Alloy 718 gets high strength mainly due to the presence of ﬁne
nano-size disc-shaped c¢¢ (Ni
3Nb) precipitates, besides, to a
limited extent, from the ﬁne cuboidal shape c¢ (Ni3 (Ti, Al))
(Ref 1). Many investigators have reported that the temperature
capability of alloy 718 is limited to about 650 /C176 C due to the
instability of c¢¢ precipitates, as extended exposure above
650/C176 C would result in the transformation of metastable c¢¢
phase to a stable d phase with cascading effect of reduced
mechanical properties (Ref 2-4). In addition, it is also reported
that the c¢¢ precipitates of alloy 718 are stable up to 627 /C176 C and
its hardness starts decreasing with the further increase in the
temperature (Ref 5).
Considering the above constraints of alloy 718, to improve
the performance of the gas turbine engine, another nickel-based
superalloy 720Li is developed. Alloy 720Li is a one of the most
advanced disk alloys for service temperatures ranging from
650/C176 C to 730 /C176 C (Ref 6-9). Alloy 720Li is strengthened through
solid solution enhancement by the addition of chromium,
cobalt, molybdenum, and tungsten, while additions of titanium
and aluminum provide strengthening by precipitation of c’ (Ref
7). The addition of aluminum gives higher strength which
allows for better load-bearing capability and increased resis-
tance to deformation over time. The gas turbine engine design
and fabrication requirement call for welding of these two
materials in dissimilar combinations to meet the overall
conﬁguration and improvised performance. Particularly, the
high-pressure and high-temperature compressor spools may be
designed with the combination of alloy 718 and alloy 720Li.
Moreover, dissimilar welding of alloy 718 and alloy 720Li can
allow the use of each alloy’s speciﬁc strengths in a single
component. Alloy 718 has excellent corrosion resistance, high-

However, there are various challenges associated with
dissimilar weld joints, such as non-uniform joint microstructure
and different thermal joint properties (Ref 10). Inadequate
melting during welding can lead to unmixed zones on
dissimilar weld joints (Ref 11). Fusion-based welding of
nickel-based superalloys can result in the formation of Laves
phases, which is a serious concern. The segregation of Ti and
Mo from the Nb elements is responsible for the formation of
Laves phase (Ref 12). The depletion of chromium in dissimilar
joints of nickel-based alloys with stainless steel can cause
sensitization corrosion (Ref 13). Hot cracking is also observed
during the fusion welding of alloy 718 due to heat input, grain
size, composition, and thermal contraction (Ref 14). Bexevanis
et al. studied the post-weld heat treatment of electron beam
welded dissimilar alloy 713LC and AISI4140 steel joints. They
found that the main issue associated with the weld was the
Laves eutectic phase, which can be eliminated through a one-
stage post-weld heat treatment (PWHT). However, PWHT
resulted in poor corrosion resistance on the steel side due to ion
dissolution and tempering effect on AISI4140 steel (Ref 15).
Amit Kumar et al. investigated dissimilar gas tungsten arc
weldments of alloy 617 and P92 steel. They found that
macrosegregation between chemical elements resulted in
mechanical failure. The formation of brittle intermetallic NbC
and Laves phases had a detrimental effect on solidiﬁcation
cracking and impact toughness. PWHT was conducted to
reduce the intermetallic phases, which had an impact on the
P92 steel side due to the tempering effect (Ref 16).
Similarly, the fusion welding of alloy 720Li results in micro-
cracking which is encountered during the solidiﬁcation (Ref 17-
21). Lingenfelter et al. (Ref 17) found that the alloy elements,
particularly B, Zr & Mg, exceeding certain limits cause fusion
zone and HAZ micro-ﬁssuring during fusion welding. Further,
the author stated that the material with a ﬁner grain size of less
than ASTM-5 would be less sensitive to HAZ micro-ﬁssuring
than coarse-grained material. It is recommended to use solid-
state welding techniques for alloy 720Li due to its high
concentration of c¢ precipitates (Ref 7). In order to overcome
these limitations, friction welding (FRW) technique is em-
ployed to join dissimilar rod type components.
FRW is a solid-state welding process that provides various
advantages over the fusion welding techniques, such as
eliminating solidiﬁcation defects, developing a narrow HAZ,
generating ﬁne-grain weld microstructures and facilitating
dissimilar metal welding. In addition, FRW requires less
welder skill, obviates edge preparation, and ﬂux shielding gas
requirements. Therefore, FRW has been employed for dissim-
ilar welding alloy 718 with alloy 720Li in this work. Some of
the researchers have contributed to the friction welding of alloy
718 and alloy 720Li.
Damodaram et al. (Ref 18) conducted FRW of alloy 718 and
studied the effect of two different PWHTs such as solution
treatment and aging (STA) & direct aging (DA). Post-weld DA
treatment was recommended after FRW as it leads to a
substantial improvement in microhardness and tensile strength
compared to the as-welded condition. This is attributed to the
re-precipitation of the strengthening phases. Neminathan and
Mohandas (Ref 21) also recommended post-weld DA of inertia
friction welded alloy 718 to regain the weld interface strength
on par with the parent material. Further, it was reported that the
LCF life of alloy 718 inertia friction welds in DA conditions are
comparable to the base material and better than the electron
beam welded alloy 718. Huang et al. (Ref 22) studied the
detailed microstructural analysis of inertia friction welded alloy
720 Li with alloy 718 joints and corelated weldment
microstructure with microhardness. The authors considered
post-weld heat treatment at 760 /C176 C for 2,4,8 and 24 h followed
by air cooling, which have results in over aged condition in the
alloy 718 material.
In the view of the above, the current state of research on
solid-state welding of alloy 718 and alloy 720Li is limited, and
there is a lack of extensive studies on dissimilar welding of
these two alloys. Therefore, there is a need to explore the use of
friction welding as a potential method for joining these two
alloys and characterizing the joint quality through the exam-
ination of microstructures in different weld regions. While
some studies have focused on the microstructure and hardness
properties of dissimilar welding of alloy 718/720Li, there is a
research gap when it comes to thoroughly investigating this
topic in terms of room-temperature tensile, high-temperature
tensile and low-cycle fatigue behavior of dissimilar welding of
alloy 718/720Li. Wide spread of industrial use of dissimilar
friction welding process requires reliable data to be made
available on the microstructure, mechanical properties of
dissimilar 718/720Li friction weld joints. In the current study,
the use of PWHT has been considered as a possible way to
restore the mechanical properties of alloy 718 by limiting
maximum aging temperature not exceeding 732 /C176 C of alloy 718
material. A detailed assessment of the effects of post-weld heat
treatments on weld microstructure and mechanical properties
(room-temperature tensile, high-temperature tensile and room-
temperature fatigue) of welds has not yet been made and so
work is needed. In this study, friction welding of dissimilar
alloy 718 and alloy 720Li is carried out. The post-weld DA is
employed to improve the strength properties even in the present
dissimilar 718/720Li FRW joints. The direct aging as that of
alloy 718 aging cycle is considered, as lower microhardness is
reported in alloy 718 side (Ref 22). The effect of post-weld DA
on the microstructure, microhardness, room-temperature tensile
tests dissimilar 718/720Li FRW joints was investigated. In
order to understand the mechanical behavior of the dissimilar
weld joints at high temperature and cyclic loading conditions,
the high-temperature tensile tests at 650 /C176 C and strain-controlled
LCF for both as-welded and post-weld aged conditions were
studied in the current study.

FRW experiments were performed with a 200 kN capacity
FRW machine that uses the continuous drive method. The
experimental setup is shown in Fig. 1. One of the workpieces is
ﬁxed to a motor-powered unit, while the other is restricted from
rotation (Ref 23, 24). The motor-driven workpiece rotates at a
ﬁxed, predetermined speed. The rotating and the stationary
workpieces are brought in contact with a constant force called
friction force. Due to the rubbing action at the faying surfaces,
frictional heat is generated. This persists for a predetermined
duration until the desired amount of axial shortening has
occurred (to get the nascent surface in contact). Then, the
rotation is stopped, and the friction force is increased (forge
force) to produce a sound, defect-free weld.
The key variables in continuous drive friction welding are
friction force, forge force, burn-off length, and spindle speed.

3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Macrostructure
The cross-section macrograph of dissimilar 718/720Li FRW
joint is shown in Fig. 4. A uniform weld interface, free from
defects such as cracks, pores, and discontinuities are obtained.
The differential etching between alloy 718 and 720Li clearly
distinguishes the dissimilar weld joint. The size of the ﬂash
material on alloy 718 is comparatively large than that of alloy
720Li side due to the inherent low ﬂow stress of alloy 718 at
the FRW compared to the alloy 720Li. Damodaram et al. (Ref
25) measured the weld zone temperature of 1118 /C176 C using an
infrared thermometer during FRW of alloy 718. Monajati et al.
(Ref 26) found that the dissolution of c¢ precipitate occurs in
the range of 1140-1150 /C176 C, and the dissolution c¢¢ occurs at
885/C176 C. In alloy 718, since c¢¢ is the major strengthening phase,
it deforms more at the FRW temperatures and results in a
differential material ﬂow pattern. During inertia friction
welding of alloy 718, Huang et al. (Ref 22) stated that the
material is ejected as ﬂash material due to the ﬂow stress
reduction during friction welding because of the dissolution of
major strengthening precipitates of alloy 718. Billel et al. (Ref
27) observed that the ﬂash is formed only on the lower yield
strength material SS304, while studying the dissimilar FRW of
SS304 and WC-Co cermet alloy. However, the ﬂash can be
machined for real-time application, as it cannot affect the weld
quality.

3.3 Microhardness
The Vickers microhardness plots across the dissimilar 718/
720Li FRW joint in as-welded and post-weld DA conditions
are shown in Fig. 9. The microhardness of alloy 718 and alloy
720Li parent materials are 315 HV and 380 HV , respectively.
The weld interface showed higher hardness (414 HV) than the
both parent materials, mainly due to the ﬁne-grain microstruc-
tures at the weld interface. The drop in hardness in the HAZ/
TMAZ region of alloy 718 is attributed to the dissolution of
strengthening precipitates during FRW. Damodaram et al. (Ref
18) reported that the temperatures generated during FRW of
alloy 718 exceed the dissolution temperature of the strength-
ening precipitates, leading to reduced hardness in the weld
zone. Preuss et al. (Ref 30) attributed the lower hardness close
to the weld interface to the dissolution of strengthening
precipitates during FRW and restoration of strengthening
precipitates did not occur during cooling. Figure 9 shows that
the microhardness of the weld interface, HAZ, and TMAZ
regions is improved after post-weld DA heat treatment and is
comparable to their parent material counterpart. The peak
hardness (478 HV) is observed at the weld interface of post-
aged dissimilar 718/720Li FRW joints. The improvement in
microhardness may be due to the re-precipitation of strength-
ening precipitates as a result of post-weld DA. Formation of
strengthening precipitates after post-aging heat treatment was
conﬁrmed by various authors (Ref 18, 31, 32).

The present experiment also provides data on cyclic
softening phenomena of the FRWed joints & parent material.
Cyclic softening & Hysterias trend obtained on the dissimilar
friction weldment of alloy 718/alloy 720Li are again compa-
rable with that of alloy 718 parent material. Xiao et al. (Ref 35)
observed initial cyclic hardening and softening behavior of
alloy 718 at room temperature and 650 /C176 C until ﬁnal fatigue
fracture due to shearing of strengthening precipitates during
cyclic deformation.
The peak stress observed at maximum strain condition
during the LCF test by alloy 718 and alloy 720Li and also
dissimilar 718/720Li FRW joint at different LCF cycles are
plotted in Fig. 19. Maximum stress value decreases with an
increase in the number of cycles, which indicates the occur-
rence of cyclic softening during cyclic deformation of alloy 718
parent material and post-direct aged alloy 720Li to alloy 718
friction welded joint. The trend of hardening during initial
cycles followed by cyclic softening phenomena seems compa-
rable in alloy 718 and dissimilar friction weld of alloy 718-
alloy 720Li in DA condition. However, the maximum stress
observed during the LCF test of alloy 720Li was consistent
over its life cycles, with a moderate decrease in around 23 MPa
at the latter part of its LCF life.
From the data presented above, it can be noted that the
dissimilar friction welding of alloy 718-alloy 720Li had peak
stress in-between low-strength alloy 718 and high-strength
alloy 720Li. The softening trend on the dissimilar friction weld
was observed from 1129 to 984 MPa (i.e., a decrease in max
stress around 145 MPa during its life of 3400 cycles), whereas
for alloy 718 parent material, it was from 941 to 824 MPa (i.e.,
decrease of max stress around 117 MPa for the same 3400
cycles). Gopinath et al. (Ref 37) reported that cyclic stress
response at all temperatures of alloy 720Li material was
stable under fully constant total strain amplitude less than or
equal to 0.6%, whereas above 0.6%, the material experiences
cyclic hardening followed by softening until fracture at 25 and
650/C176 C. At 400 /C176 C, however, cyclic stress plateau was observed
after initial hardening in the case of the LCF behavior of alloy
718. Sudharshan Rao et.al. (Ref 38) reported that the material
exhibits cyclic softening at all strain amplitudes at 0.4, 0.6 and
1.0% strain rates. Xie et al. (Ref 39) observed that alloy 718 has
cyclic softening behavior even at high temperatures. Fournier
et al. (Ref 40) observed the 718 material experiences rapid
hardening in the early cycles of LCF at room temperature,
followed by softening. Sudharshan Rao et al. (Ref 38) and
Praveen et al. (Ref 41) observed that the deformation during the
LCF test on alloy 718 material occurs by planar slip. While at
low strain amplitude, the slip bands are widely spaced, but at
high strain amplitude, they are more closely spaced. At high
strain amplitude, there is also an intersection of slip bands
because additional slip systems become activated.
Hwang et al. (Ref 42) observed that the coherency loss of
the primary gamma prime aided by shearing and partial
dissolution of the secondary gamma prime induces cyclic
softening in gamma prime strengthened alloys. Chang et al.
(Ref 43) observed that the crack-initiating life of strain-
controlled LCF was of factor 3, 2 and 1.5 at room temperature,
525, and 650/C176 C, respectively. Further, they stated that the cyclic
softening of the standard alloy 718 is due to loss of coherency
by ﬁne c¢¢ disks due to sheared dislocations during cyclic
straining. Cyclic softening of a material is deleterious as it
decreases its load-bearing capability due to the buildup of
cyclic strain. The fracture surface of the tested samples
displayed distinct areas of crack initiation, stable crack prop-
agation, and overload (as seen in Fig. 20). The crack growth
region shows striations on the fracture surface.
4. Conclusions
Microstructure and mechanical properties of dissimilar alloy
718/alloy 720Li FRW joints were investigated and compared
with post-weld DA joints. The following conclusions can be
drawn from the current study.
1. Dissimilar 718/720Li FRW joint interface shows ﬁne-
grain microstructure with no defects.
