ABSTRACT 
The NbMoTaW refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) exhibits excellent high-temperature strength, making it 
suitable for extreme service conditions. However, its brittleness and low ductility at room temperature caused by 
the enrichment of oxygen at the grain boundaries is a major impediment for its industrial applications. In this 
work, in-situ alloyed NbMoTaWTi
x 
(x = 0, 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75) RHEAs were additively manufactured using the 
laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) process. The results show that the LPBF NbMoTaWTi
x 
RHEAs retain a single- 
phase microstructure with the body centered cubic (BCC). With the addition of Ti reduced the oxygen content 
in the alloy, through the formation of, nanoscale TiO
2 
particles appear at the solidification cell boundaries and 
grain boundaries, which mitigated the oxygen induced embrittlement and improved the plastic strain to fracture. 
As a result, the yield and compressive strengths, and the strains to failure, both at room temperature and at 1000 
◦
C, of the NbMoTaWTi
0.75 
RHEA were found to be superior to those of NbMoTaW. The addition of Ti reduced the 
oxygen content at the cell boundaries and grain boundaries to form nanoscale TiO
2 
particles, which mitigated the 
oxygen induced embrittlement and improved the plastic strain to fracture. The dispersion strengthening of TiO
2 
and solid solution strengthening of Ti also contributed toimproved the observed strength enhancement. This 
work illustrates a possible pathway to fabricate near-net shaped RHEA parts with an excellent combination of 
strength and ductility. 
1. Introduction 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are typically comprised of 
equiatomic or near-equiatomic alloy elements with high melting tem -
peratures and single-phase microstructure [1 – 3] . The RHEAs outper -
form conventional high-temperature alloys, such as the Ni-based 
superalloys, in terms of their mechanical properties at elevated tem -
peratures. [4,5] . Therefore, they are especially suitable for heat-resistant 
load-bearing components. They are also the most promising materials 
for the ultra-high temperature and extreme service conditions such as 
the shafts of aero-engines, turbines, and the front ends of 
ultra-high-speed vehicles [6 – 8] . Amongst the various RHEAs that are 
being explored currently, NbMoTaW, which has a compressive yield 
strength of 405 MPa at 1600 
◦
C, attracted significant attention as it 
outperforms Ni-based superalloys such as Inconel 718 and Haynes 230 
above 1000 
◦
C [9 – 13] . However, its low room-temperature ductility,

In the NbMoTaW RHEA, the elements Mo and W are mainly used to 
enhance high-temperature strength and microstructural stability, but 
they are intrinsically brittle [14] . Nb has the lowest density among four 
constituent elements. Ta has a negative ductile-to-brittle transition 
temperature. Both of them are commonly used to improve the ductility 
of the RHEA [15 – 17] . The room-temperature brittleness of NbMoTaW 
RHEA is caused by the segregation of oxygen at the grain boundaries 
(GBs), and, in the process, embrittling them [18,19] . It is intrinsically 
prone to cleavage fracture in tension, i.e. , it ’ s easier to break a bond 
ahead of a crack tip in the NbMoTaW RHEA compared to emitting a 
dislocation. Currently, alloying with elements such as Zr, Hf, Re, Ti is the 
most widely pursued method for improving the ductility of NbMoTaW 
RHEA [20 – 23] . The reported values of compressive yield strengths and 
plastic strains before failure of these alloys are in the ranges of 
1147 – 1340 MPa and 5.7 – 8.9 %, respectively [20,22 – 24] . In contrast, 
alloying with Ti not only reduces the density of the alloy, but also results 
in a single-phase BCC microstructure with a compressive yield strength 
of 1455 MPa and a plastic strain to failure of 11.5 % [19,25 – 27] . There is 
a possibility of optimizing the strength and plasticity of the NbMoTaW 
RHEA by alloying it with V, Cr, Cu elements [24,28] or optimizing the 
alloy composition system [29 – 33] . Additionally, it was demonstrated 
that the intergranular fracture in NbMoTaW can be suppressed by grain 
boundary engineering, such as introducing C and B elements that can 
reduce the oxygen segregation at the GBs [34,35] . However, the 
mechanism of improving the plasticity or the resistance to intrinsic 
cleavability of NbMoTaW by alloying process is still unclear. 
Additive manufacturing (AM) has ability to produce near-net shaped 
parts with complex geometries. Directed energy deposition (DED) and 
laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) processes are the widely used for 
fabricating metal parts [7,36,37] . The LPBF process can produce rela -
tively more precise and complex parts than DED process. Moreover, the 
ultra-fast cooling rate (10
5
– 10
8 
K/s) inherent to LPBF can refine grains 
and suppress element segregation (compared to that of in casting, for 
example), improving materials properties [38] . However, the relatively 
higher residual stresses generated in the LPBF parts make them sus -
ceptible to promote cracking. They can be avoided by selecting a suit -
able substrate material, and preheating the substrate above the ductile 
to brittle transition temperature of the material. [19,20] . Therefore, 
LPBF process can be used to fabricate RHEA parts, which is otherwise 
challenging via conventional means due to their high ductile to brittle 
transition temperature. To demonstrate this in present study, NbMo -

LPBF process to examine (a) the viability of processing RHEAs by LPBF 
through the in-situ alloying approach and (b) examine the effect of Ti 
addition on the strengthening and deformation mechanisms in the 
NbMoTaW RHEA.

4.2. Deformation mechanism 
The strength of NbMoTaWTi
x 
is enhanced by the combined effects of 
dispersion strengthening from the TiO
2 
particles and the solid solution 
strengthening caused by Ti in the solid solution [6] and the interstitial O 
[45] . The low strain to failure of the RHEA is related to the defects 
within the printed specimens and the lower number of slip systems that 
can accommodate the deformation [46] . In some cases, elemental 
segregation at the GBs can cause intergranular embrittlement even when 
adequate intrinsic deformation modes are available [47] . Such grain 
boundary embrittlement is associated with segregation grain boundary 
segregation of interstitials and secondary phases to GBs, which lead to 
brittle intergranular fracture without significant plastic deformation 
within the grains. The poor ductility of NbMoTaW is mainly related to 
the segregation of interstitial O at the GBs [35] . The strain to failure can 
be improved by the enhanced dislocation mobility and multiplication 
[14] . The Mo/W alloying with IV/V transition metals (Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Ta 
and Nb) can enhance the intrinsic ductility due to the decrease of the 
Fermi energy level and the earlier onset of shear instability [14] . 
The fast-cooling process intrinsic to the LPBF process results in two 
effects that contribute to the enhancement of the plasticity of the

5. Summary and conclusions 
In summary, we successfully fabricated nearly-fully dense NbMo -
TaWTi
x 
RHEAs using the LPBF technique and the in-situ alloying 
approach. The microstructure, mechanical properties, formation mech -
anism and effect of nanoscale TiO
2 
precipitates on the mechanical per -
formance were investigated. The followings are the research findings 
and conclusions from this study. 
(1) The rapid cooling during the LPBF process reduced the macro -
scopic segregation of the elements and refined the microstruc -
ture. As a result, the plastic strain to failure of the NbMoTaW was 
improved from 1.9 % to 7.8 %. At the same time, alloying with Ti 
improved the formability of the NbMoTaW and simultaneously 
reduced the density. 
(2) The LPBF NbMoTaWTi
0.75 
RHEA shows excellent compressive 
strength of 1593 MPa and 7.8 % strain to failure, which is supe -
rior to conventionally manufactured NbMoTaW. 
(3) The thermodynamic parameters δ , Δ H , Δ S and VEC predicted 
single BCC phase for NbMoTaWTi
x 
RHEA, which was experi -
mentally verified using XRD. 
(4) Nanoscale TiO
2 
oxide particles formed on the grain boundaries 
and inside the solidification cell boundaries during the LPBF 
processing of NbMoTaWTi
x 
RHEA. The thermodynamic viability 
of TiO
2 
was investigated using VASP DFT simulation and diffu -

which mitigates oxygen assisted embrittlement.
