ABSTRACT
The high-temperature mechanical properties of Cu-Cr alloys were limited, while adding alloying elements could 
inhibit the coarsening of Cr precipitates and improve the high-temperature mechanical properties. The Cu-Cr-Ag 
and Cu-Cr-Ag-Si alloys were designed and fabricated, and their microstructure and mechanical properties were 
investigated to achieve high-strength and high-electrical-conductivity copper alloys. The Cu-Cr-Ag alloy treated 
by two-step cold rolling and aging treatment achieved an ultimate tensile strength of 620 MPa, yield strength of 
600 MPa, electrical conductivity of 85.6 % IACS, and elongation of 15.7 %. Adding Si increased the high- 
temperature strength, room-temperature elongation, and low-temperature elongation of the Cu-Cr-Ag alloy, 
while decreasing the electrical conductivity, room-temperature strength, and high-temperature elongation. 
Nanoscale Cr particles were detected in the Cu-Cr-Ag alloy, while nanoscale Cr and Cr
3
Si particles co-existed in 
the Cu-Cr-Ag-Si alloy, among which Cr particles were the main strengthening precipitates. The curves of pre -
cipitation kinetics indicated that adding Si promoted the precipitation in the Cu-Cr-Ag alloy. Low-, room-, and 
high-temperature mechanical properties measurements showed that the strength variation depended on the 
average size of the main precipitates. The decrease in the high-temperature strength was attributed to the 
coarsening of the Cr precipitates. At low temperature, the average size of the precipitates was essentially the 
same as that at room temperature, thus the low-temperature strength was comparable to the room-temperature 
strength. These findings indicated that Cu-Cr-Ag and Cu-Cr-Ag-Si alloys would exhibit superior electrical and 
mechanical properties.
1. Introduction
Copper alloys are critical in high-speed railway contact wires and 
power electronics [ 1 – 4 ]. Cu-Cr alloys are widely recognized for their 
high EC and strength, making them a prevalent choice for electronic 
connectors [ 5 , 6 ]. Cu-Cr-Zr (CCZ) alloys were employed as a 
new-generation copper alloy with high strength and remarkable EC [ 7 ,
8 ], which was increased to 70 – 90 % IACS. Given the inherent suscep -
tibility of Zr to oxidation [ 9 ], the CCZ alloy is frequently fabricated using 
vacuum melting, which has an expensive production cost and technical 
challenge. Consequently, there is an urgent demand to discover a Cu-Cr 
alloy that integrates excellent electrical conductivity and strength while 
adhering to cost-friendly fabrication methods.
Among the commonly used elements, the Ag element solidly dis -
solved in the Cu matrix has been used in Cu-Cr alloys supplemented with 
resistance to oxidative burnout, excellent electrical conductivity, and 
mechanical properties [ 10 , 11 ]. J.S. Song et al. [ 12 ] fabricated 
Cu-7.0Cr-0.9Ag (wt.%) alloy by melting and casting, following two in -
termediate heat treatments, which achieved an impressive UTS of 798 
MPa. Z.Y. Li et al. [ 10 ] fabricated a Cu-0.19Cr-0.1Ag (wt.%) alloy 
through melting to examine the hot compression deformation behavior 
of the alloy under different temperatures and strain rates. Meanwhile, D. 
Liang et al. [ 13 ] employed the continuous extrusion method to fabricate 
a Cu-Cr-Ag (CCA) alloy. The CCA alloy ’ s UTS, yield strength (YS), and

Based on Cu-Cr-based alloys, researchers have introduced alloying 
elements such as Ti [ 14 ], Mg [ 15 ], and Si [ 16 ] to explore the synergistic 
effects between these alloying elements in enhancing mechanical 
properties. Y.B. Du et al. [ 17 ] added 0.02 wt% Si to Cu-Cr alloys. It was 
shown that trace amounts of Si improved the alloy ’ s stress relaxation 
properties. J.P. Qu et al. [ 18 ] fabricated four CCZ- x Si ( x = 0, 0.02, 0.05, 
and 0.2 wt%) alloys to investigate the effect of Si addition on the me -
chanical properties and microstructure of the CCZ alloy. C.Y. Shi et al. 
[ 14 ] introduced the Ti element to the CCZ alloys. The results implied 
that adding the Ti element assisted the CCZ alloys in improving the 
strength through cold rolling and aging treatments. Z.P. Qu et al. [ 19 ,
20 ] found that adding Si significantly improved the high-temperature 
performance of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy, which was attributed to the fact 
that Si enrichment reduced the interfacial energy of Cu/Cr, thus inhib -
iting the coarsening of the Cr precipitation phase. Among them, Si, as an 
effective alloying element, has attracted much attention because of its 
ability to enhance mechanical properties significantly [ 16 ]. However, 
the role of Si in alloys has not been fully clarified, especially in terms of 
mechanical properties and microstructure at high temperature and low 
temperature.
In this work, the effect of Si addition on the room-temperature, high- 
temperature, and low-temperature mechanical properties and micro -
structure of the CCA alloy was systematically studied. Precipitates that 
existed in the CCA and Cu-Cr-Ag-Si (CCAS) alloys were analyzed in 
terms of type and orientation relationships. The main strengthening 
mechanisms for the CCA and CCAS alloys were discussed. Precipitation 
kinetic curves were constructed to examine the impact of adding Si on 
the transformation of precipitates within the CCA alloy. Based on the 
calculation of strengthening increments, the high-temperature and low- 
temperature strengths of the CCA and CCAS alloys were analyzed. The 
data on the properties of the CCA and CCAS alloys at high and low 
temperatures were supplemented by an in-depth understanding of the 
relationship between Si addition and mechanical properties and 
microstructure.

4. Discussion
In the section of the discussion, the strength mechanism, precipita -
tion, high-temperature and low-temperature strength, and comparison 
of properties were discussed in detail. For the CCA alloy, it was assumed 
that all the precipitated Cr elements formed nanoscale Cr particles. For 
the CCAS alloy, it was assumed that all Si elements formed as the 
nanoscale Cr

4.4. Performance comparisons
The performance comparison of the studied CCA and CCAS alloys 
with CCZ [ 36 – 38 ], Cu-Cr-Nb (CCN) [ 39 , 40 ], and Cu-Cr-Mg (CCM) [ 15 ,
41 , 42 ] alloys are shown in Fig. 19 . At the same electrical conductivity 
(84 – 86 % IACS), the yield strength of the CCA and CCAS alloys was 
50 – 70 MPa and 30 – 50 MPa higher than that of the CCZ alloys, respec -
tively. At the same strength (560 – 600 MPa), the EC of the CCA and CCAS 
alloys was 6 – 10 % IACS and 4 – 8 % IACS higher than that of the CCZ 
alloys, respectively. Compared to the CCN and CCM alloys, the EC and 
YS of the CCA and CCAS alloys exhibited high performance.
