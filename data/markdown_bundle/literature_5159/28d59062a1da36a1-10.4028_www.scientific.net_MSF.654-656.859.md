Keywords: Titanium alloy, aging, nucleation, precipitate 
Abstract: In order to develop a perspective of the new metast able β titanium alloy—— TB-13, the 
influence of ageing treatments on the microstructur e and properties has been studied. The results 
show that TB-13 alloy displays high age strengtheni ng effect and fine precipitation. During the 
one-step aging process, the higher the aging temperature, the coarser the α plate thickness. As a result, 
TB-13 alloy shows the lower the strength and the hi gher the ductility. The best combination of 
strength and ductility is acquired in the TB-13 alloy after solution treatment at 1133K following aging 
at 813K for 16h. The ultimate strength is near 1400 MPa and the specific elongation is 7.0% by 
ageing. At last, the details of the transformation mechanism and microstructural evolution 
accompanying ω-assisted α nucleation during continuous heating are discussed. 
Introduction 
At present, with increasing applications in aircraf ts and nonaerospace military applications, 
metastable β type titanium alloys with superior mec hanical properties have been required plentiful 
[1-4]. It has been reported that the precipitation mechanism of the α phase in β type titanium alloys 
depending on heating rate, aging temperature and ho lding time, thereby resulting in noticeably 
different microstructures[5-7]. TB-13 alloy is a ne w metastable β type titanium alloy which 
possesses an attractive combination of high strengt h and good ductility, making it a potential 
material candidate for aerospace applications [8]. In this work, TB-13 titanium alloy has been 
adopted to explore the characteristics of aging res ponse upon continuous heating. A principal 
objective of the present study is to investigate th e microstructures and corresponding mechanical 
properties of TB-13 titanium alloy after aging proc ess. An additional motivation for the present 
investigation are to understanding the precipitation mechanism occurs under given aging conditions 
and the critical heating rate which fit for industr ial application to acquire the finely dispersed 
intragranular α phase. 
Experimental Procedures 
A Ti-7.5Mo-4.8Nb-3.8Ta-3.6Zr-4Cr-2Al metastable β t itanium alloy named TB-13 alloy was 
received as a rolled round bar of 14 mm in diameter . In order to determine the aging response for 
the TB-13 alloy, specimens were heat-treated above the β-transus temperature (1090K) at 1133K 
for a period of 30 min and subsequently quenched in water. They were then subjected to additional 
aging treatments at 753K, 793K, 813K and 853K for p eriods ranging from 1 h to 16 h upon the 
heating rate of 16K/min. All samples were investiga ted in detail using scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM), transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Tecnai 
® F30). Tensile tests were 
performed at room temperature using a strain rate of 1.5×10 −4 s−1. The gage length of the specimens 
was 25 mm. To analyze the precipitation mechanism o f α phase, high-resolution dilatometry 
(Netzsch ® DIL-402C) has been adopted to explore the length c hange of TB-13 alloy specimens 
upon heating. Dilatometric specimens of 4 mm in dia meter and 25 mm in length were placed in 
contact with the pushrod and heated up to 1133 K at various constant heating rates of 1, 2, 3, 4, 8 
and 16 K/min.

Results and Discussion 
The effect of holding time on the microstructure du ring aging. The rolled material (solution 
treated at 1133K) was aged at 793K using a heating rate of 16K/min for various holding times of 1h, 
4h, 8h, 16h. Microstructures developed during isoth ermal aging are compared in Fig.1. Precipitates 
of the α phase were observed on grain boundaries an d especially inside grains. The morphology of 
the α-precipitates illustrate in Fig.1 is very fine platelet structure. Despite the difference near gr ain 
boundaries, the SEM images (Fig.1a–d) revealed only small differences in microstructure. 
Specifically, the precipitates were slightly coarser after longer holding times.

Aging temperature effects. The scanning electron micrographs in Fig.2 present the features of α 
phase obtained when aging the TB-13 alloy for 16h a t various aging temperature of 753K, 793K, 
813K and 853K , with the heating rate of 16Ks −1 . This micrograph shows a highly refined distribution 
of intragranular α precipitates within the β grains which is as the same as Fig.1. The nanometer-scale 
intragranular α phase shows to be clustered in the form of small triangular shaped clusters. As shown 
in Fig.2, the higher the aging temperature, the coarser the α plate thickness becomes. 
Mechanical properties of TB-13 alloy after aging tr eatment. Fig.3 shows the tensile 
properties of TB-13 alloy specimens aged at four different temperatures for 16h after 1133K solution 
treatment. It can be observed that the TB-13 alloy is with a high aging strength. The strength achieves 
1600MPa when the aging temperature is 753K. Even if the aging temperature is 833K, the yield 
strength is still near 1400MPa. As shown in Fig.2, the higher aging temperature results in a decrease 
in tensile strength and an increase in ductility. A pparently, the higher the aging temperature is, the 
lesser and coarser of α phase becomes (Fig.2). Henc e, thechange of strength and ductility of TB-13 
alloy with aging temperatures can be easily underst ood. From Fig.2, we can easily see that the best 
combination of strength and ductility has been acqu ired in the TB-13 alloy after aging at 813K for 
16h. The ultimate strength is near 1400MPa and the specific elongation is 7.0%. 
ω-assisted nucleation of α plate during continuous heating. In order to analyze the 
precipitation mechanism of α phase, the length chan ge of metastable type TB-13 titanium alloy 
specimens upon heating has been study by thermal di latometer. The dilatometry curves of TB-13 
alloys obtained at different heating rates are shown in Fig.4. Take the sample heated at 1 K/min as an 
example, it is clear that the TB-13 alloy (mainly m etastable β phase) dilates linearly at the initial 
stage under 443K due to the thermal expansion of cr ystal lattice by linear heating. The formation of 
ω phase occurs at 443 K (schematically denoted by P oint A in Fig.4). The dilatometry curve then 
decreases with increasing temperature, reaching its nadir at 651 K (Point B in Fig.4). When the 
temperature is further increased, the length of the sample dilates rapidly due to the progressive

To validate the precipitation sequence which deduce from the dilatometry curve, 703 K is 
selected to ‘freeze’ the transformation at this temperature sta ges at a heating rate of 1 K/min. The 
HRTEM micrographs and electron diffraction patterns of this sample are shown in Fig.5. It is 
clearly shown that the coexistence of the three pha ses β, ω and α are occurrence (Fig. 5(a)) during 
the stage of ‘Point B- Point C’. A detailed view of the structure of these nanometer-scale ω and α 
precipitates and their interface with the surroundi ng β matrix is obtained through a HRTEM study 
which shows in Fig. 5(b). Thus, it can be concluded that the precipitation of ω phase occurs mainly 
in the stage between Point A and B in Fig.1, while the dissolving of ω phase and the precipitation of 
α phase occur in the section between Point B and C. It is also shown that α plates nucleate at the ω/β 
interfaces, as was also reported by Furuhara et al. in β-Ti-15-3 alloy [9].

Conclusions 
1) The effect of holding time on the microstructure during aging is insignificant. Furthermore, the α 
plate thickness increases with the increase of aging temperature. 
2) Due to ω-assisted nucleation during continuous heating, the α phase exhibits very fine and uniform 
platelet structure after aging process. 
3) In TB-13 alloy, α precipitates nucleate on ledges and misfit dislocations at ω/β interfaces.
