ABSTRACT
Conventional post-processing techniques, such as hot isostatic pressing and heat treatment, often encounter 
difficulties in achieving an optimal balance between high strength and ductility in additively manufactured ti -
tanium alloys. This study presents an electropulsing-assisted chemical boundary engineering technique to 
address this challenge in the Ti-6Al-4V alloy prepared via laser powder bed fusion (LPBF). This innovative 
process establishes chemical boundaries within the prior- β grains through controlling element diffusion at 
elevated temperatures, resulting in a novel bi-lamellar martensitic microstructure. The technology yields a high 
yield strength of ~1118 MPa and an elongation of ~11.2 % in LPBF-fabricated Ti-6Al-4V alloy. The enhanced 
yield strength is attributed to the presence of chemical boundaries that impede dislocation slip. Additionally, 
these chemical boundaries restrict the growth of α ′ and inhibit the propagation of microcracks, leading to a 
significant increase in elongation. This innovative process is anticipated to be an effective method for enhancing 
the mechanical properties of various additively manufactured α + β titanium alloys.
1. Introduction
The Ti-6Al-4V alloy is commonly used in marine, chemical, medical, 
and aerospace applications due to its exceptional mechanical properties 
and remarkable corrosion resistance [ 1 ]. One important way to process 
this alloy is through additive manufacturing methods, such as laser 
powder bed fusion (LPBF) and electron beam powder bed fusion 
(EBPBF), which have advantages such as material savings, reduced 
costs, and the ability to create complex geometries compared to sub -
tractive processes like cutting and milling [ 2 ]. However, Ti-6Al-4V alloy 
produced through LPBF may have lower elongation due to increased 
amount of crystallographic defects like dislocations [ 3 ], while EBPBF 
could lead to lower strength levels due to its coarse microstructure [ 4 , 5 ]. 
Therefore, additively manufactured Ti-6Al-4V alloys typically require 
post-processing steps to optimize the microstructure and enhance their 
mechanical properties.
The typical post-processing technologies for additively 
manufactured titanium alloys include hot isostatic pressing (HIP) and 
conventional heat treatment. Hot isostatic pressing, which employs high 
temperature and high pressure, effectively eliminates defects such as 
voids in additively manufactured metal components, enhances their 
density, and improves fatigue life [ 6 ]. However, the widespread appli -
cation of this technique to additively manufactured titanium alloys is 
limited due to its high cost and the tendency to induce significant 
coarsening of the microstructure. Additionally, with appropriate process 
adjustments, it is now possible to achieve the additive manufacturing of 
near-void-free titanium alloys [ 7 ], thereby rendering further HIP pro -
cessing unnecessary. Annealing stages between 800

commonly utilized to enhance the elongation of LPBF-fabricated 
Ti-6Al-4V alloy, as this process can eliminate dislocations and increase 
the β phase content [ 8 ]. Nevertheless, these methods are not ideal for 
LPBF-fabricated Ti-6Al-4V alloys, as a significant increase in plasticity 
inevitably leads to a considerable reduction in yield strength. For 
example, it has been reported that annealing the LPBF-fabricated

The strength and plasticity of the Ti-6Al-4V alloy are closely related 
to microstructural boundaries [ 11 , 12 ], including phase boundaries (PB) 
and grain boundaries (GB), as illustrated in Fig. 1 (a and b). The presence 
of these microstructural boundaries can impede dislocation slip, thereby 
influencing the material ’ s strength and work hardening capacity [ 13 ]. 
Additionally, microstructural boundaries also affect the propagation of 
microcracks. Specifically, the control of phase boundaries (PB) can be 
achieved through processes such as annealing and sub-transus quench -
ing, collectively referred to as phase boundary engineering (PBE). 
However, as previously noted, these processes may not be well suited for 
additively manufactured Ti-6Al-4V alloy, as they could lead to reduced 
strength or plasticity. Grain boundary engineering (GBE) is a proven 
method for enhancing the mechanical properties of metallic structural 
materials [ 13 ]. Nevertheless, this technique often involves plastic pro -
cessing, which is incompatible with the near-net shape characteristics of 
additively manufactured metals. Recent research [ 12 ] has shown that 
chemical boundary engineering (CBE), which employs rapid heating to 
prevent element diffusion and introduce chemical boundaries (CB) 
within the prior austenitic grain, can effectively enhance the strength 
and ductility of low-alloy steel.
Previous studies [ 14 – 16 ] have shown that electropulsing-induced 
rapid heating can inhibit the diffusion of vanadium (V) and aluminum 
(Al) in a mill-annealed Ti-6Al-4V alloy without raising the α →β phase 
transformation temperature. Consequently, electropulsing can facilitate 
a comprehensive phase transformation throughout the entire micro -
structure while approximately preserving the existing elemental distri -
bution due to extremely fast temperature rise ( > 1000 K/s), zero soaking 
time and swift water cooling quenching. This process subsequently in -
troduces chemical boundaries within the β grains, which are formed at 
the highest temperature induced by electropulsing due to the inhibited 
diffusion of alloying elements, as illustrated in Fig. 1 (c). The chemical 
boundaries can impede the growth of α ′ laths and create a unique 
bi-lamellar microstructure, leading to exceptional tensile properties 
[ 17 ]. It is likely that a similar bi-lamellar microstructure can be achieved 
in LPBF-fabricated Ti-6Al-4V alloy through electropulsing-assisted CBE, 
resulting in enhanced mechanical performance. Inspired by this, the 
present work proposes a novel strategy to develop high-performance 
Ti-6Al-4V alloy by subjecting the LPBF-fabricated material to 
pre-sub-transus quenching followed by electropulsing treatment. The 
resultant microstructural transformation and tensile behavior were 
analyzed in detail. Compared to materials without chemical boundaries, 
the strength and ductility of the material were found to increase 
simultaneously.

AR microstructures and applying electropulsing heating above the β

element distribution. In contrast, applying the same electropulsing

will largely preserve the original uneven distribution of elements.

dicular to it [ 40 , 41 ]. As illustrated in the Inverse Pole Figure (IPF) and

The chemical boundary delineates the transition between the soft 
and hard zones in HTEPT. During tensile deformation, dislocations 
initiate in the soft zone (V-rich fine martensite lath region), and subse -
quently slide and accumulate at the chemical boundary. Once the 
accumulation of dislocations reaches a critical threshold, they can tra -
verse the chemical boundary and migrate into the hard zone (Al-rich 
coarse martensite lath region). As dislocations accumulate sufficiently 
around the chemical boundary, microcracks will initiate and begin to 
propagate. During the crack growth stage of the tensile process, 
microcracks tend to propagate along the long axis of the martensite lath

high, as observed in EPT ( Fig. 10 (i)), microcracks can propagate more 
easily, significantly reducing the elongation of the alloy. In contrast, 
when the value of the long axis of the martensite is low, as seen in 
HTEPT ( Fig. 10 (h)), microcracks are more likely to encounter laths with 
different orientations, which can impede their propagation. As a result, 
the presence of shorter martensite laths in HTEPT typically leads to

3.4. Discussion on the stability of the introduced chemical boundaries 
under long-term thermal exposure or cyclic loading
The LPBF Ti-6Al-4V alloy with introduced chemical boundaries is 
primarily composed of martensite, necessitates consideration of its 
thermal stability when utilized in thermally exposed environments. In 
our previous research on electropulsing-assisted chemical boundary 
engineering applied to hot-rolled and annealed Ti-6Al-4V alloy, we 
observed that after aging treatment at 803 K, martensite in both the 
aluminum-rich and vanadium-rich regions decomposed; however, this 
had minimal impact on the chemical boundaries [ 17 ]. Notably, the 
strength of the aged Ti-6Al-4V alloy is significantly enhanced while still 
maintaining high elongation. In our subsequent work, we will conduct 
aging heat treatment on the LPBF Ti-6Al-4V alloy that incorporates a 
chemical boundary to ensure its suitability for thermally exposed envi -
ronments. Cyclic loading in aluminum alloys and high-entropy alloys 
can induce changes in the chemical composition of local nanoscale re -
gions [ 59 , 60 ], potentially affecting the chemical boundary in HTEPT. 
Given the size of the V-rich and Al-rich regions (3 – 5 μ m, as illustrated in 
Fig. 3 ), while simple room temperature cyclic loading can generate 
dislocations and vacancies, it is unlikely to facilitate the long-distance 
diffusion of V or Al elements. In contrast, cyclic loading at elevated 
temperatures significantly enhances the diffusion capacity of elements 
due to the concurrent presence of high temperatures, dislocations, and 
vacancies, which may compromise the stability of the chemical 
boundary. Since the Ti-6Al-4V alloy is typically employed in environ -
ments below 773 K, this temperature is expected to have minimal impact 
on the chemical boundary [ 17 ]. Consequently, we hypothesize that 
under cyclic loading below 773 K, the decomposition of martensite in 
the V-rich and Al-rich regions may be accelerated, although the overall 
impact on the chemical interface is likely to be minimal.
4. Conclusions
By leveraging electropulsing to inhibit element diffusion during 
phase transformation, we were able to establish chemical boundaries 
and create a unique bi-lamellar martensitic microstructure in LPBF- 
fabricated Ti-6Al-4V alloy. This innovative microstructure exhibits 
exceptional strength (~1118 MPa yield strength) and ductility (~11.2 
%, elongation), surpassing outcomes achieved through alternative post- 
processing methods. The high yield strength of the material can be 
attributed to the presence of chemical boundaries, which impede the 
slippage of dislocations. These chemical boundaries restrict the growth 
of α ′ and contribute to impeding propagation of microcracks, ultimately 
improving elongation. This study serves as a guide for implementing 
electropulsing assisted chemical boundaries engineering (CBE) to ach -
ieve a balance between high strength and ductility in additively manu -
factured Ti-6Al-4V alloy. Furthermore, this innovative method shows 
promise for enhancing mechanical properties of other α + β Ti alloys.
