1. Introduction
Currently, with the rapid development of the aerospace industry, 
structural materials with relatively high application temperatures are 
becoming increasingly urgent. At present, the most widely used alloys 
are nickel-based high-temperature alloys, but the working temperature 
of nickel-based high-temperature alloys reaches 80 % of their melting 
point, and further increasing the working temperature is difficult [1,2] . 
NiAl-based alloys have the advantages of low density, high melting 
point, high Young ’ s modulus, high thermal conductivity and good 
thermal stability [3] . It is a high-temperature structural material with 
promising application prospects. However, NiAl alloys exhibit poor 
deformation resistance at room temperature and insufficient strength at 
high temperatures [4] , which limits their further application.
HEAs [5,6] have excellent high- and low-temperature mechanical 
properties [7,8] and wear and corrosion resistance [9 – 12] , which pro -
vides researchers with a new design direction [13 – 15] . Researchers have 
introduced the concept of HEAs into NiAl alloys with comprehensive 
mechanical properties. For example, Ye et al. [16] prepared NiAl-VCrMo 
eutectic HEAs, in which BCC and B2 grow in a lamellar structure and are 
accompanied by nanoscale precipitates. NiAl-20 V-10Cr-5Mo has better 
mechanical properties, with a yield strength of 1363 MPa and a 
compressive strain of 22 %. Xiao et al. [17] investigated a NiAl-CrFeMoV 
lightweight HEA, in which high melting points of Mo and V were added 
to the NiAl matrix, which produced a solid solution strengthening effect. 
The yield strengths of the NiAl-(CrFeMoV)45 alloy are 1739.5 MPa and 
950.7 MPa at room temperature and 1073 K, respectively. Therefore, 
NiAl-based HEAs are promising for balancing room temperature plas -
ticity and high-temperature strength.
The addition of different elements to form lattice distortion as well as 
to promote the precipitation of σ , μ , and γ
ʹ 
and synergistic strengthening 
by solid solution and precipitation are effective methods for improving 
the mechanical properties of alloys [18 – 21] . Zhao et al. [22] investi -
gated the effect of solid solution strengthening of Ti on NiAl-based alloys 
and reported that the addition of Ti induced the precipitation of Ni2AlTi 
phases from NiAl alloys and improved the high-temperature properties 
of the alloys. Raj et al. [23] investigated the effects of Cr and Mo on the 
properties of NiAl-based high-temperature alloys. The results of the 
study revealed that the Cr (Mo)-strengthened phase enhanced the 
high-temperature mechanical properties and fracture toughness of the 
NiAl alloys at room temperature. Zhuang et al. [24] designed an 
Al0.5CoCrFeMoxNi HEA; the addition of Mo promoted the formation of 
Cr-rich and Mo-rich ( σ -phases) materials, and the yield strength of the 
alloy system increased with increasing Mo content.
