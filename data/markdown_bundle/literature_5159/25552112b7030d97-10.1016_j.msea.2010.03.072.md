abstract
Ni–20 wt% Cr matrix composites reinforced by Y 2 O3 particles (Y2 O3 /Ni–Cr composites) have been sepa-
rately prepared by mechanical alloying and powder metallurgy. The effects of mechanical alloying on the
mechanical properties of the composites at room temperature and high temperature have been studied.
It found that nickel and chromium powders become a whole solid solution powder after mechanical
alloying for 20 h. The density, hardness and ultimate tensile strength of the composites prepared by
mechanical alloying is higher than those prepared by powder metallurgy. High temperature simulation
for mechanical properties was carried out based on Larson–Miller function. The simulation results show
that the Vickers hardness of the tested materials is approximately proportional to the heat treatment
parameter P, which indicates that Larson–Miller function is applicable for the life-span design of Ni-
based composites. After heat treatment at high temperature, the 0.8%Y
2 O3 /Ni–Cr composite prepared by
mechanical alloying still maintains good mechanical properties.

1. Introduction
The nickel based composites have high strength, good oxidation
resistance, heat fatigue resistance and heat corrosion resistance at
high temperature; therefore, they are widely used as the important
materials to fabricate the parts subjected to heavy loads, espe-
cially in aircraft engines [1–4]. These composites are strengthened
by many methods, such as solid solution strengthening, precipita-
tion strengthening and dispersion strengthening. Their dispersion
phases must have chemical, crystallographic and micro-structural
stabilities at high temperatures, and the best ones are oxides [12].
There have been some papers [5–8] investigating the fabrication,
microstructure, mechanical properties and creep resistance of the
nickel based composites that are mainly produced by mechanical
alloying. However, there are fewer investigations on the perma-
nence life span [2,9,10], which is a key factor of material design
and hardly to be practiced by test.
In academia and industrial ﬁelds, some synthesis parameters are
brought out to scribe the material life span, such as Larson–Miller
(LM), Orr–Sherby–Dorn (OSD) and Manson–Haferd (MH) parame-
ters [10]. In this paper the authors have prepared the nickel matrix
composites reinforced by yttrium oxide particles by mechanical
alloying (MA) and powder metallurgy (PM), respectively, simu-
lated the high temperature mechanical properties based on the

2.2. High temperature simulation
Holloman and Jaffe have investigated the relationship between
hardness of quenched carbon steel and tempering temperature and
have proposed the Larson–Miller parameter equation [2,9–11]:
P = T (C + lg t) (1)
where T is Kelvin temperature, t is time and C is material constant.
C usually takes the value of 20 in thermal and electric industries
[10]. P is the heat treatment parameter. According to this equa-
tion, the performance of materials will be identical provided that
they are treated under the same P with different combinations of
temperature and holding time.
In general, the life span of structural parts at high temperature is
designed for 1× 10
5 h. Therefore, the life span of materials working
at lower temperature can be simulated at higher temperature for a
comparatively short time, according to the following formula:
P = T
1(C + lg t1) = T2(C + lg t2) (2)

Fig. 5 a shows the relationship between the hardness of the
materials and the simulation parameter P. It is seen that MA
0.8 wt%Y2O3/Ni–Cr composite has the highest hardness values after
the heat treatment. The hardness and the parameter P have a lin-
ear relationship, which agrees well with the empirical equation
proposed by Holloman–Jaffe [2,9,10]:
H = f (P) (4)
where, H is the hardness, P is the heat treatment parameter. This
equation indicates that the hardness is a constant if P does not
change although the temperature changes, coinciding with Fig. 5b.
In fact, Larson–Miller parameter and Holloman–Jaffe (H–J) equa-
tion have the same origin. They both have the similar form but
describe different material processes.

to a size of nanometer, homogeneously distribute in the alloyed
matrix, act as barriers to the movement of the grain boundaries
and make the structure more stable during manufacture process
and the high temperature simulation.
4. Conclusions
The chromium and nickel powders form the 20CrNi alloyed
powders after mechanical alloying for 20 h in a high-energy mill,
which is shown by the chromium peak ﬁnally disappearing after
milled for 20 h in XRD analysis.
The high temperature performance of the materials is simulated
according to Larson–Miller equation. The hardness of the materi-
als has a linear relationship with the heat treatment parameter P.
It indicates that Larson–Miller equation can be used for life-span
design of nickel matrix composites.
