1. Introduction 
The aging population has led to an increase in the demand for novel 
orthopedic implant materials [ 1 , 2 ]. For commonly-used biomedical 
metallic materials (i.e., CP-Ti and Ti6Al4V), there are some clinic 
problems need to be overcome, such as higher Young ’ s modulus than 
that of human bones, low wear resistance, and potential release of toxic 
ions (i.e., V and Al) [ 3 – 5 ]. Thus, the development of novel biomedical 
metallic materials that possess biocompatibility, good bio-mechanical 
compatibility (i.e., low Young ’ s modulus, high strength, and great 
ductility) and high wear resistance is required for meeting the re -
quirements of future medical needs [ 6 , 7 ]. 
Unlike traditional alloys that based on only one dominant element, a 
new type of alloys that consisted of four or more principal metallic el -
ements, termed multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs), exhibit inter -
esting mechanical properties and attract intensive research attention [ 8 , 
9 ]. The high entropy effect, lattice distortion effect, sluggish diffusion 
effect, and cocktail effect of MPEAs give them remarkable mechanical 
properties as well as a stable single solid-solution phase (i.e., 
face-centered cubic (FCC), or body-centered cubic (BCC)) [ 6 , 10 ]. For 
MPEAs, mechanical properties are controlled by their structure types. It 
has been reported that BCC MPEAs usually exhibit high strength/hard -
ness and limited plasticity, while FCC MPEAs have relatively low 
strength/hardness and high plasticity [ 10 ]. In recent years, a new class 
of BCC bio-MPEAs, comprising group IV (Ti, Zr, Hf), V (Nb, Ta), VI (Mo) 
biocompatible elements, are preferred as orthopedic implant materials 
due to their excellent mechanical properties and biocompatibility [ 7 , 
11 – 16 ]. While a series of high-strength bio-MPEAs have been reported, 
most of them exhibited limited tensile ductility and still had a relatively 
high Young ’ s modulus [ 11 , 14 , 15 ]. 
Several efforts have been trialed toward developing BCC MPEAs with 
good bio-mechanical compatibility [ 17 – 21 ]. Previous studies have 
shown that reducing the BCC-phase stability is an effective method to 
design BCC MPEAs with low Young ’ s modulus and good ductility 
[ 22 – 24 ]. The valence electron concentration (VEC) theory plays an 
important role in predicting the phase stability of BCC MPEAs, where the

The wear morphologies features observed in all samples are relatively 
similar, including micro-cracks (in red circle), continued abrasive fur -
rows, and large amounts of adhesion. Visible plastic deformation is 
observed on the worn surface, presenting abrasive furrows parallel to 
the sliding direction, which is a typical feature of abrasive wear [ 45 , 46 ]. 
The wear process usually involves more than one wear mechanism. 
During testing, the contacting areas are sheared and cause detachment 
and attachment to the other surface by the large local pressures, which is 
a typical morphology of adhesive wear [ 47 ]. In conclusion, the results 
indicate that the main wear mechanism of the TiZrNbTa alloy is abrasive 
wear and adhesive wear.
