ABSTRACT 
Niobium (Nb) is considered as candidate material for heat pipe in space nuclear reactors due to high melting 
point, low density, excellent compatibility with alkali liquid metals, irradiation resistance, workability and 
welding performance, but low strength restricts it ’ s engineering application. In this work, yttrium (Y) was 
introduced into Nb matrix to achieve solution strengthening from Y atoms and dispersion strengthening from 
yttrium oxides. The latter one was realized by oxygen carrier route and vacuum electromagnetic levitation 
melting technique. Finally, one Nb521 (Nb-5.0 wt%W-1.7 wt%Mo-1.0 wt%Zr-0.07 wt%C) and four kinds of 
Nb521-xY (x = 0.1, 0.2 0.3 and 0.4 wt%) alloys were prepared. TEM characterization indicated that 80 nm Y
2
O
3 
particles were formed in Nb521-0.1Y, which exhibited the highest yield strength of 673 MPa, ultimate tensile 
strength of 740 MPa as well as larger elongation of 12.8%. Moreover, the solution strengthening of Y atoms and 
Orowan strengthening of yttrium oxides were evaluated quantitatively. 
1. Introduction 
Niobium (Nb) is considered to be one of the most promising candi -
dates for heat pipe in space nuclear reactors due to its excellent prop -
erties, such as high melting point (2468 
◦
C), low density (8.57 g/cm
3
), 
excellent compatibility with alkali liquid metals, irradiation resistance 
of fast neutron, workability and welding performance [ 1 – 3 ]. However, 
some disadvantages like low recrystallization temperature, tensile 
strength and creep resistance, seriously restrict the engineering appli -
cation of Nb alloy in the heat pipe of space reactor [ 4 – 6 ]. For example 
[ 3 ], the recommended temperature window of Nb-1wt.%Zr was about 
800 – 1250 K. The lower temperature limit of 800 K was determined by 
irradiation brittleness of fast neutron, and the temperature upper limit of 
1250 K was influenced by recrystallization brittleness. Furthermore, the 
recommended stresses at 800 – 1100 K and 1100 – 1250 K were only about 
68 – 74 MPa and 20 – 74 MPa, which were determined by 1/3 ultimate 
tensile strength and creep strength, respectively. Thus, it ’ s necessary to 
improve the strength of Nb alloy. Usually, the strength of Nb alloy can be 
increased by dispersion strengthening and solution strengthening. In 
PWC-11 (Nb-1wt.%Zr-0.1 wt%C), the dispersion strengthening was 
mainly from Nb – Zr – C carbides [ 7 ]. In C-103 (Nb-10 wt%Hf-1wt.% 
Ti-0.7 wt%Zr), the solution strengthening was mainly from Zr and Hf as 
well as trace W and Mo [ 8 ]. 
Atomic radius of yttrium (Y) is 1.81 Å, which is larger than that of Nb 
(1.43 Å). Thus, introducing Y into Nb matrix can improve the strength of 
Nb alloy by lattice distortion theoretically. Furthermore, yttrium oxides 
possess significantly low Gibbs free energy and can be acted as disper -
sion strengthening phases [ 9 ]. Recently, oxide dispersion strengthening 
(ODS) was achieved in other refractory metals and steels. In Ref. [ 10 ], 
ODS-Mo exhibited yield strength (YS) over 800 MPa and tensile elon -
gation as large as ~ 40% at room temperature by introducing nano -
metric oxide particles uniformly distributed in the grain interior. In 
Ref. [ 11 ], ODS-W presented YS of 1210 MPa at room temperature and 
the nil ductility temperature is below 25 
◦
C. In Ref. [ 12 ], ODS-Steel 
displayed significant low creep rates and high strength ( > 2 GPa) as 
well as large tensile ductility (RA > 40%) by introducing high number

nomenon will be presented in 3.2 section. Less and larger particles

grain growth, i.e., larger grain size in Nb521-0.3Y and Nb521-0.4Y.

3.3. Tensile properties 
Tensile properties of Nb521 and Nb521 – Y at room temperature are 
shown in Table 4 . The YS, UTS and elongation of Nb521 were 636 MPa, 
680 MPa and 10.8%, respectively. Compared to Nb521, both Nb521- 
0.1Y and Nb521-0.2Y exhibited higher YS, UTS and larger elongation, 
simultaneously. Furthermore, Nb521-0.1Y showed the highest YS of 
673 MPa, UTS of 740 MPa and excellent elongation of 12.8%. For the 
Nb521-0.3Y, although higher UTS was achieved, but lower YS, espe -
cially smaller elongation was also presented. When the content of Y 
addition reached 0.4 wt%, almost the lowest strength and the minimum 
elongation were found. 
Nb521-0.1Y and Nb521-0.2Y displayed higher strength and larger 
elongation simultaneously can be attributed to the uniformly distributed 
yttrium oxides as shown in Fig. 5 (a and b) and Fig. 6 . Nb521-0.3Y and 
Nb521-0.4Y exhibited lower strength, especially smaller elongation can 
be resulted from the large spherical or acicular Y
2
O
3 
as shown in Fig. 5 (c 
and d). As shown in Fig. 5 , the oxide particles in Nb alloys with higher Y 
content are larger in size and tend to be distributed at grain boundaries, 
which leads to poor deformation coordination [ 17 ]. The stress at the 
particle/matrix interface is higher than that at the grain boundary and 
sub-grain boundary, resulting in a greater stress concentration at the 
interface. During the tensile test process, if the stress exceeds the ulti -
mate tensile strength, it will lead to interface debonding and crack 
initiation [ 18 ]. The crack source then continues to expand along the 
particle/matrix interface, resulting in the mechanical properties rapidly 
deteriorating with the addition of Y greater than 0.1%.

4. Discussion 
As mentioned in 3.3 section, 0.1 wt% Y addition facilitated the YS 
and UTS increased by 37 and 60 MPa in Nb521-0.1Y compared to 
Nb521. Thus, it ’ s necessary to explore the strengthening mechanism of 
Nb521-0.1Y. 
Except for the yttrium oxides, the existence form of yttrium element 
in Nb alloy should include solid solution. Fig. 8 (a) shows the XRD of 
Nb521 and Nb521 – Y with various Y additions. There were four peaks of 
Nb matrix, i.e., (110), (200), (211) and (220) peaks but no new 
diffraction peaks can be detected. Fig. 8 (b) presented the enlarged image 
around (110) peak and the peaks of Nb521 – Y shifted to the left 
compared to Nb521, which can be caused by the addition of Y. It is well 
known that the atomic radius of Nb (1.43 Å) is smaller than Y (1.81 Å).

5. Conclusion 
To solve the low strength of niobium (Nb) alloy, yttrium (Y) element 
was introduced into Nb matrix to achieve solution strengthening from Y 
atoms and dispersion strengthening from yttrium oxides. The latter one 
was realized by oxygen carrier route and vacuum electromagnetic 
levitation melting technique. Five kinds of Nb alloys including Nb521, 
Nb521-0.1Y, Nb521-0.2Y, Nb521-0.3Y, Nb521-0.4Y were prepared for 
comparison. Then grain size, second phases, especially yttrium oxides 
were characterized by LSCM, FE-SEM and TEM. Finally, tensile prop -
erties of these Nb alloys were tested and the solution strengthening of Y 
atoms and Orowan strengthening of yttrium oxides were evaluated 
quantitatively. The main conclusion drawn from the results can be 
summarized as follows: 
(1) Y addition facilitated the grain refinement of Nb521. Further -
more, Nb521-0.1Y and Nb521-0.2Y displayed smaller grain size. 
The grain size was 423, 152, 103, 187 and 224 μ m for Nb521, 
Nb521-0.1Y, Nb521-0.2Y, Nb521-0.3Y and Nb521-0.4Y, 
respectively. 
(2) The Y-bearing second phases in Nb521 – Y was Y
2

of which increased with Y additions. In Nb521-0.1Y, the size of 
Y

scale of 80 nm and micron-scale of 1.6 μ m. 
(3) Compared to Nb521, the Nb521-0.1Y exhibited higher YS of 673 
MPa, UTS of 740 MPa and larger elongation of 12.8%. The YS, 
UTS, and elongation of Nb521 were 636 MPa, 680 MPa and
