the comprehensive property.

2. Experimental 
The Nb – 16Si – 10Mo alloy and Nb – 16Si – 10Mo – 10Ti alloy were 
fabricated by SPS. The blended elemental powders were prepared by 
mechanical balling. The high-purity elemental powders of Nb, Si, Mo, 
and Ti were employed as the raw materials, where the purities of Nb, Si, 
and Mo powders were 99.9 % (wt.), and the Ti powder was 99.7 % (wt.). 
The nominal powder sizes of Nb, Si, Mo, and Ti elements were 10.5 μ m, 
5 μ m, 5 μ m and 50 μ m, respectively. The Beijing Gaoke New Materials 
Technology Co., Ltd supplied these high-purity elemental powders. It 
should be noted that the morphology of the as-received elemental 
powders was studied by scanning electron microscope (SEM). Specif -
ically, gold was sprayed on the surface of the powders before 
morphology observation. Fig. 1 illustrates the morphologies of Nb, Si, 
Mo, and Ti powder, revealing that the particle size distribution of initial 
Si, Mo, and Ti element powder is uniform, while the particle size dis -
tribution of initial Nb element powder is uneven. The average particle 
sizes of Ti powder were about 50 μ m, respectively. Moreover, the shape 
of these particles was polyhedron-like, while the particle sizes of Si and 
Mo powders were about 5 μ m and sphere-like. 
The elemental powders of Nb, Si, Mo, and Ti were mixed with the 
molar ratio of Nb – 16Si – 10Mo alloy and Nb – 16Si – 10Mo – 10Ti alloy. The 
mixed powders were mechanically milled in a planetary ball milling 
machine. The speed ratio of revolution to rotation was 1:2, and the ball 
mill tank is stainless steel with a volume of 250 mL. The GCr15 balls with 
diameters of 6 mm, 8 mm, and 10 mm (mass ratio 1:1:2) were added to 
each ball milling tank. Anhydrous ethanol with a volume of 20 mL was 
added to the tank to keep the fluidity and viscosity of the powder 
mixture and prevent the powders from sticking to the tank and the balls.

Before the ball milling process, high-purity argon gas was filled into the

Originally, the blended elemental powder was put into the center of 
the high-strength graphite mold with an inner diameter of 40 mm. In 
order to demold easily, the smooth, flexible carbon papers between 
powders and graphite molds were utilized to segregate them, and a draft 
angle of 1.5 was designed. Then, powder mixtures with a diameter of 40 
mm and a height of 20 mm were pre-compacted at room temperature by 
a manual press to stabilize the assembly. In addition, the powder mix -
tures and the graphite molds were put in a ZLY-60-SPS furnace, and the 
SPS process was carried out in the furnace. During sintering, the vacuum 
level was controlled below 1Pa to prevent powders, mixtures, and mold 
oxidation. An infrared thermometer was applied to measure the tem -
peratures of the sample through the holes in the graphite mold. The 
schematic diagram of the sintering process is illustrated in Fig. 2 .

The density of the sintered alloys was measured by the Archimedes 
method, and the deionized water was used. The surface of the samples 
was polished and then cleaned in anhydrous ethanol to ensure mea -
surement accuracy. One sample was measured at least five times, and 
the average value was used as the final density to ensure accuracy. The 
microstructure, energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS), and fracture
