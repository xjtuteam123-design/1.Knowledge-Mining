Complexly alloyed heat -resistant titanium alloys of the composition Ti –5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr–1Re are 
studied. High -temperature test mechanical properties of these alloys in compression and tension at 
600°C are provided and compared with similar properties of alloy Ti –5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr. It is esta b-
lished that addition of rhenium increases alloy high -temperature strength properties and plastic defo r-
mation processes occur mainly in the β -phase not affecting α -phase. 
Keywords: phase composition, heat -resistant titanium alloys, rhenium, microstructure, plastic defo r-
mation, dislocation sliding. 
Due to high specific strength, good heat and corrosion resistance titanium alloys are used extensively in the 
chemical and aerospace industries [1, 2]. The majority of titanium alloys are two -phase within which low -
temperature α-phase provides high strength and corrosion resist ance, and the high -temperature β -phase demo n-
strates better ductility and formability. Existence of α ↔ β transformation signifies that as a result of heat 
treatment different microstructural combinations may be achieved within the alloy that make s it possible to 
adapt the properties obtained for specific customer requirements [3]. 
One of the successful aircraft structural materials capable of maintaining a prolonged highly loaded cond i-
tion over a wide temperature range is the alloy Ti –5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr. There is a good combined balance b e-
tween alloying elements: Al is an α-stabilizer, reducing alloy de nsity; V and Mo are isomorphic β -stabilizers 
providing ductility and refractory properties; Cr is a eutectoid forming stabi lizer increasing t he temperature 
range for (α + β)-phase existence and gives interesting properties such as high specific strength, good ductility, 
and suitable heat and corrosion resistance [4, 5]. 
One of the important aspects of imp roving titanium alloy properties is alloying elements having a conside r-
able amount of valent electrons changing their phase structure, in particular rhenium that also increases alloy 
operating temperature by 50 –80°C [6–9].

composition is selecte d in order that they have properties most favorable for the best assimilation of alloying 
components. Therefore, during development of production methods for master alloy preparation conditions are

Experimental Procedure and Discussion of Results 
Alloys were melted in an Arcast Arc 200 laboratory vac uum arc furnace with a copper cooled hearth. After 
loading a charge th e furnace w as evacuated to a pressure of 10− 3 Pa and filled with 99.995% pure argon to 
a pressure of 0.6 atm. In order to homogenize the ingot compos ition through the cross section it was rotated five 
times and subsequently remelted . The melt obtained was poured into a metal mold that made it possible to pr e-
pare cylindrical ingots 15 mm in diameter. 
In order to prepare master alloy and alloys iodide titanium ( ТU 48 -4-286–82), aluminum А 99 (GOST 
11069–2001), vanadium VnМ -1 ( ТU48-4-272–73), molybdenum М Ch-1 ( ТU 48- 19-245–84), electrolytic 
chromium ÉRKh -0 (GOST 5905 – 2004), rhenium R е-0 ( ТU 48-19-92–80) were used. Master alloys prepared 
from them, wt.%: Ti –50Mo, Tme = 1945°С; Ti–20Re, Tme = 1910°С; Ti–30V, Tme = 1605°С that were used 
for test alloy preparation. 
After charging the important aspects in technology for preparing titanium alloys are the methods of casting 
and heat treatment since chemical and phase composition, mechanical, and operating properties depend upon 
them. The specimens obtained were heat treated at 850 °C for 3 h, furnace cooled to 760° C with soaking for 3 h, 
air cooling to room temperature, heating to 650 °C with soaking for 4 h, followed by oil quenching. 
Heating and subsequent cooling at a specific rate and under certain conditions were established proc eeding 
from the properties and structure of the original alloy and the corresponding phase composition diagrams for 
heat-resistant titanium all oys [6, 10]. Considering that β -phase decomposition in test alloy proceeds very slo w-
ly, completion of phase formation after cooling was conducted with soaking at 760 °C that provided formation of 
an (α + β)-structure close to equilibrium. 
Similar variation of the heating and soaking temperatures has also been used for heat treatment of similar t i-
tanium alloys from powder materials [11] and this ma kes it possible to control the ratio between the degree of 
chemical homogeneity and the microstructure parameters achieved. Achievement of such of such high -
temperature process with the possibility of changing the metal phase composition provides a flexib le tool for 
creating materials with the properties required. 
The microstructure and phase composition of heat treated specimens were studied using a Tescan Vega 3 
LM scanning electron microscope fitted with an EDX Oxford X -Max 80 attachment. Revelation o f the m i-
crostructure for the test alloys was performed by chemical etching in an aqueous s olution of a mixture of 
2.5% HNO3 + 1% HF + 1.5% НС l acids. 
The mechanical properties of the specimens obtained were determined using a Zwick Z250 universal test 
machine. Testing in compression was conducted at 600 °C on cylindrical specimens 8 mm in diameter, 12 mm 
long with a degree of deformation of 50% at a deformation rate of 4 mm/min. mechanical test results in co m-
pression are provided i n Fig. 1. 
It is seen from the data provided that alloy Ti –5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr starts to deform plastically due to stresses 
of 360 ± 15 MPa, and with addition of rhenium to the alloy of 370 ± 15 MPa. Short -term ultimate strength is 
almost unchanged for all all oys remaining at the level of 655 ± 15 MPa. Therefore, addition of rhenium to alloy 
Ti–5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr leads to a significant improvement in strength properties in compression.

Mechanical properties of test specimens in tension were also performed in a Zwick Z250 test machine 
at 600°C with a similar deformation rate. For this cylindrical specimens of a special shape were used. Results 
of mechanical tests in tension are provided in Fig. 2. 
During tensile tests addition of rhenium to the alloy composition leads to a significant increase in strength 
properties, and in fact shifts the start of plastic deformation from 290 MPa for alloy Ti –5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr to 
375 MPa for alloy Ti –5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr–1Re. Addition of rhenium also increases short -term ultimate strength 
from 317 to 417 MPa and significantly reduces specimen relative elongation before failure from 40 to 24%, 
which agrees with data in [7]. 
Similar behavior of test alloys may be explained by the fact that plastic deformation in metals n ormally pro-
ceeds in the β -region [12 –15] and is a process of sliding of one part of a crystal with respect to another over 
a slip plane where shear resistance is the lowest. Addition of rhenium atoms large with respect to size c ompli-
cates the slip process transforming it partly into a twinning process for which the shear stress is greater than with 
slip, which gives rise to an increase in strength properties [16, 17].

The process of crystalline material plastic deformation at 600 °C (0.3 –0.4Tme ) is determined to a consi d-
erable extent by dislocation movement, which is detected as “wrinkled” bodies in areas of the emergence at 
a crystal surface. With a high degree of deformation dislocation density increases, and their appearance after 
mechanical loading is expressed in the form of etching pits (noted in Figs. 3 and 4 by arrows), formed as a result 
of etching out deformed areas. The dimensions of pits for both alloys are approximately identical and co m-
prise 5–10 µm. 
Deeper extended shaped pits are formed by dislocation movement, directed perpendicular to a specimen 
surface, orientated across the tensile lines (see Figs. 3a, 4a) or along compression lines (see Fig. 4d). Disloc a-
tions emerging at the surface of a crystal at a sharp angle are deeper and more rounded pits in microsections with 
a surface located parallel to the tensile line (see Figs. 3b, 4b) and perpendicular to the compression line 
(Figs. 3c, 4c). Therefore, under tension dislocations move along d eformation lines, thinning and elongating 
a specimen, and under compression shortening and thickening it.

CONCLUSION 
Complexly alloyed rhenium -containing alloys of the composition Ti –5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr–1Re have been 
prepared, and high -temperature studies have been made of their mechanical properties in compression and te n-
sion at 600°C that are comparable with similar properties of alloy Ti –5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr. 
Addition of 1% rhenium to alloy Ti –5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr increases alloy high -temperature strength properties 
in the plastic deformation region, which is especially noted during tests in tension.

The microstructure of the alloys in question has been studied. It has been demonstrated that during heat 
treatment there was formation of a two -phase (α + β)-structure. In this case rhenium, as also chromium, van a-
dium, and molybdenum, is concentrated within β -phase. 
It has been established that in α-phase of the test alloys there are no microstructure distortions after high -
temperature mechanical loading, and all plastic deformation processes in the test titanium al loys proceed mainly 
within the β -region. This is confirmed by presence of etching pits within areas of emergence of highly dense 
sliding dislocations at a specimen surface. In tensi on dislocations move along the deformation line, thinning and 
elongating, and in compression across the deformation line there is specimen shortening and thickening. 
It has been shown that with a sufficient h ot deformation rate (below the β -transition) for alloy Ti –5Al–
5Mo–5V–3Cr within the body of β -phase pr ecipitates of finely dispersed α -phase may be present. Addition 
of rhenium significantly affects titanium alloy propert ies, facilitating formation of la rge areas of finely dispersed 
α -phase, and also transfer of dislocation processes into regions with a lower rhenium content.
