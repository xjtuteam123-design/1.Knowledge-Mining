1. Introduction 
High-strength superalloys can survive at a broad range of tempera -
tures and are likely to satisfy varying rigors of critical application sce -
narios, such as aerospace, petrochemical and nuclear energy. Recently, 
high/medium-entropy alloys (H/MEAs) have been considered potential 
candidates to replace conventional metallic materials owing to their 
superior mechanical, physical and chemical properties [ 1 – 5 ]. The 
excellent ductility and fracture toughness also makes H/MEAs with a 
single face-centered cubic (f.c.c) structure candidates for cryogenic ap -
plications [ 6 – 9 ], while their unsatisfied strength is insufficient at 
elevated temperatures due to their seriously thermal softening [ 10 – 13 ]. 
At present, limited results are not enough to uncover the new effective 
strengthening mechanisms and achieve full potential with wide ranges 
of superior mechanical response at elevated temperatures for the f.c. 
c-system H/MEAs. 
Additive manufacturing (AM) has been proven to be a revolutionary 
technology for preparing metals and alloys [ 14 , 15 ]. Some unique ad -
vantages can be realized in addition to a design flexibility. First, AM 
process offers the possibility to alter solidification sequence. For 
example, the dendritic regions in the L-PBFed Al
0.5
CoCrFeNi alloy only 
consist of the primary f.c.c structure because the B2/body-centered 
cubic (b.c.c) is dynamically restrained by rapid cooling rate ( CR ) [ 16 ]. 
Second, the solid solubility limit tends to be improved greatly; the 
slowly diffusing elements for application at elevated temperatures, such 
as V, W, Mo, Nb, Sc and Zr, can be introduced into solid solution and lead 
to high mechanical response, which is hard to be achieved in the con -
ventional metallurgy processes [ 17 – 21 ]. Third, a high CR normally 
promotes the formation of typical cellular structures, which has been 
reported to be a dominant advantage of AM metallic materials; the

4. Discussion 
4.1. The enhanced stability of cellular structure via elemental segregation 
The strong ability of dislocation storage and strength enhancement 
of the cellular structure at room temperature have been checked via 
micro-pillar compression tests [ 37 , 58 ]. The microstructural character -
istics shown in Fig. 4 reveal that the cellular structure in the CoCrNi 
alloy exhibits low thermally stability, and improving its stability is a 
potentially effective strategy to maintain high strength at elevated 
temperatures. Similar to the other metastable structures, the cellular 
structure in the L-PBFed samples tends toward decomposition during 
post-heat treatments and results in a reduction of dislocation density 
[ 38 – 40 ]. It is mentioning that the kinetics of recovery and/or recrys -
tallization during ageing process are distinct from those of the conven -
tional wrought counterparts, and the recrystallization process in the 
deformed CoCrNi alloy could be initiated with ageing at 600
