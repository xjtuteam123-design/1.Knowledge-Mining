ABSTRACT
Metallic materials exhibiting ultrahigh strength coupled with exceptional ductility play a pivotal role in 
advanced industries, yet enhancing strength typically sacrifices strain hardening and ductility. This study pre -
sents a strategy that activated an innovative deformation mechanism to overcome the long-standing trade-off 
between strength and ductility in an L1
2
-strengthened Al
5
Ti
8
(FeCoNi)

765

with exceptional strength and ductility.
1. Introduction
The development of high-performance metallic materials with 
exceptional strength-ductility balance is critical for improving compo -
nent reliability and driving advancements in industries such as aero -
space, energy, and automotive [ 1 – 3 ]. The inherent trade-off between 
strength and ductility in contemporary alloys, however, remains a 
formidable challenge, limiting the achievement of superior mechanical 
performances [ 4 , 5 ]. This is particularly obvious in traditional alloys, 
where the single-component design strategy limits the fine control of 
alloy composition and microstructure, creating a bottleneck in acti -
vating desirable strengthening mechanisms and improving mechanical 
properties. In recent years, the development of multi-component 
high-entropy alloys (HEAs) has provided unprecedented opportunities 
for the precise tuning of material compositions, microstructures, and 
properties [ 6 – 8 ]. An increasing number of HEAs have been developed 
and exhibit outstanding mechanical properties and significant potential 
for applications in advanced industries [ 9 – 11 ]. Among them, the 
early-developed FCC (face-centered cubic) HEAs exhibit exceptional 
ductility and fracture toughness at both room and cryogenic tempera -
tures [ 12 – 14 ]. However, their relatively low strength presents chal -
lenges in meeting engineering demands [ 15 – 17 ]. Consequently, 
extensive research efforts have focused on exploring and addressing this 
issue.

3.3.1. Dislocation substructure evolution and microbands development
Fig. 7 illustrates the evolution of the microstructure of the 765 – 4 
alloy with increasing engineering strain. When the strain reached 5%, 
planar slip bands aligned along a single direction appeared, interspersed 
with wavy slip bands distributed in a staggered manner ( Fig. 7a ). During 
the deformation of FCC alloys containing the ordered L1
2 
precipitates, 
dislocations cutting through the L1
2 
phase enhanced the ability of sub -
sequent dislocations to traverse the same slip plane, a phenomenon 
termed “ glide plane softening ” , thereby improving planar slip [ 56 , 57 ]. 
Moreover, FeCoNi-based alloys typically feature an FCC matrix with 
medium to high stacking fault energy [ 26 , 29 ], promoting dislocation 
cross-slip due to the reduced separation distance between partial dis -
locations [ 25 ]. Consequently, both characteristics led to the coexistence 
of planar and wavy slip bands in the initially deformed microstructure. 
As deformation reached 12%, the planar slip bands evolved into 
microbands ( Fig. 7b ). The DF-TEM image in Fig. 7c further revealed a 
distinct orientation difference between the microbands and the matrix.

As shown in Fig. 7g , increasing the strain from 20% to 33% resulted 
in a substantial rise in the number of microbands. Fig. 8 illustrates the 
statistically quantified evolution of microband thickness and spacing 
with engineering strain, where both parameters were determined from 
systematic measurements of TEM micrographs. As deformation pro -
gressed, the inter-band spacing decreased significantly, while the 
microband thickness increased substantially. The microstructure of the 
765 – 4 alloy at 33% deformation was also characterized using EBSD, and 
the corresponding IPF map and kernel average misorientation (KAM) 
map are shown in Fig. 9 . The KAM map in Fig. 9b and the point-to-point 
misorientation profile along the white arrow indicated a significant 
orientation difference between the microbands and the surrounding 
matrix. Additionally, a pronounced presence of SISFs was observed 
around the microbands, with the majority aligned parallel to them 
( Figs. 7h and i ). The formation of SISFs reduced the average free path of 
dislocation movement, thereby inducing the dynamic Hall-Petch effect. 
Moreover, SISFs oriented in a different direction facilitated the forma -
tion of Lomer-Cottrel (L-C) locks, which were identified by the HR-TEM 
analysis ( Fig. 7i ). These L-C locks exhibited dual roles: (i) immobilizing 
dislocations through strong pinning effects, and (ii) functioning as 
Frank-Read sources that perpetually emit dislocations, thereby inducing 
sustained strain hardening [ 60 ].
Fig. 10 presents the microstructures of the CRSA, 765 – 0.1, 765 – 0.3, 
and 800 – 4 alloys after tensile testing. The fractured CRSA-F sample 
displayed planar slip bands aligned in the same direction, with dislo -
cation accumulation observed within these bands ( Fig. 10a ). The 
microstructure of the fractured 800 – 4 sample was predominantly 
characterized by planar slip bands, with wavy slip traces emerging be -
tween them ( Fig. 10b ). The insufficient activation of the dislocation 
cross-slip made it difficult to form microbands in the 800 – 4 alloy during 
tensile testing [ 58 , 59 ]. In the fractured 765 – 0.1-F sample, numerous 
well-developed wavy slip bands, resulting from dislocation cross-slip,

number of SISFs compared to the 765 – 0.1-F sample ( Figs. 10e and f ), 
which promoted additional strain hardening in the 765 – 0.3 alloy during 
deformation.

As previously highlighted, the precise modulation of L1

Fig. 13 illustrates the strain-hardening behaviour and corresponding 
deformation mechanism of the alloys aged at 765 
◦
C. During the initial 
deformation stage of the 765 – 4 alloy, both wavy slip and planar slip 
coexisted, with high dislocation density leading to a high strain- 
hardening rate. As deformation continued, planar slip bands became 
unstable under the influence of dislocation cross-slip and evolved into 
microbands. Microbands acted as an additional deformation mechanism 
that facilitated plastic deformation [ 57 , 84 , 85 ]. Moreover, their angular 
misalignment with the matrix obstructed dislocation motion and 
generated long-range back stresses, thereby improving the strain hard -
ening of the alloy [ 20 , 26 ]. Furthermore, an FCC → BCC phase trans -
formation occurred within the microbands of the 765 – 4 alloy during 
deformation. This transformation within the microbands further 
augmented their ability to obstruct dislocation motion, allowing the 
765 – 4 alloy to maintain a high strain-hardening rate during the 
mid-stage of deformation. With continued deformation, the density of 
microbands increased, resulting in a significant reduction in inter-band 
spacing. The increasing number of microbands functioned as low-angle 
grain boundaries, further refining the grains and inducing a continuous 
dynamic Hall-Petch effect, which enhanced the strain hardening of the 
765 – 4 alloy. Moreover, the integration of phase transformation within 
densely arranged microbands facilitated a uniform distribution of phase 
transition throughout the alloy, thereby promoting homogeneous strain 
hardening and plastic deformation. Generally, stacking faults do not 
readily form in the FeCoNi-based matrix with high stacking fault energy. 
However, due to the intrinsic characteristics of the L1
2 
phase in this 
study, SISFs were induced within the L1
2 
phase during the later stages of 
deformation. Small amounts of SISFs began to form in the 765 – 4 alloy at 
20% deformation, and their density increased with further deformation 
up to 33%, which contributed to a further enhancement in strain 
hardening.
