ABSTRACT 
High/medium-entropy alloys (HEAs/MEAs) with simple single-phase face-centered-cubic (fcc) structures have 
exceptional tensile ductility and outstanding toughness, but their room-temperature strengths are moderate. 
Here we introduced a mixed nanostructure, composed of high density nano-scale twins in the form of bundles 
and nanograins in shear bands, in a single-phase fcc FeCrCoNi HEA by means of cryo-rolling at liquid nitrogen 
temperature (LNT), which result in a substantial enhancement of tensile strength. The as-prepared nano -
structured FeCrCoNi HEA exhibits an ultra-high ultimate tensile strength of ~1.8 GPa, an elongation-to-failure of 
~10%. The ultra-high strength originates mainly from the effective blockage of dislocation motion by the ultra- 
high density of internal twin and grain boundaries. The microstructural evolution and formation mechanism of 
the mixed nanostructure in FeCrCoNi HEA were investigated in detail by transmission electron microscopic 
characterization. 
1. Introduction 
Multicomponent alloys, often referred to as high/medium-entropy 
alloys [ 1 – 5 ], have recently gained enormous attention owing to their 
attractive microstructures and interesting mechanical properties. These 
alloys typically contain multiple principal elements in equiatomic or 
near equiatomic concentrations and tend to crystallize as a simple 
single-phase solid solution with simple crystal structure due to the high 
entropy of mixing [ 1 – 5 ]. Among these HEAs/MEAs, the single-phase fcc 
structured CrCoNi-based HEAs/MEAs are most intensively studied 
because of their unique combinations of outstanding fracture toughness, 
exceptional ductility, and superior fatigue resistance especially at 
cryogenic temperatures [ 6 – 11 ]. For example, the fcc, single-phase, 
equiatomic CrCoNi MEA demonstrates excellent mechanical properties 
at LNT with ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 1.4 GPa, tensile ductilities 
of ~90%, and fracture toughness values of 275 MPa

However, owing to the close packed fcc lattice structure, the strength of 
these CrCoNi-based HEAs/MEAs with coarse grain structure is moder -

in the annealed states) at room temperature [ 12 – 14 ], which severely 
limits their practical applications as advanced structural materials. 
Consequently, developing effective strengthening strategies to improve 
the strength of this single-phase fcc CrCoNi-based HEAs/MEAs is highly 
desirable. 
Recently, introduction of high density of nano-scale twins in metals 
has been proposed as an effective strategy to improve strength while 
retaining appreciable ductility. For instance, Lu et al. [ 15 , 16 ] have re -
ported that electro-deposited (EDed) Cu film samples composed of high 
densities of nano-scale growth twins embedded in submicrometer-sized 
grains, achieved an ultrahigh tensile strength (above 1 GPa) about 10 
times higher than that of conventional coarse-grained Cu, while 
retaining considerable ductility and high electrical conductivity com -
parable to that of pure copper. Similar nano-twin strengthening effect 
has been demonstrated in various types of metals and alloys, including 
Au [ 17 ], Ag [ 18 ], Ni [ 19 ], Al [ 20 ], Ni – Mo alloy [ 21 ], austenitic steel 
[ 22 ] et al. Traditionally, these nano-twinned metals (alloys) are gener -
ally fabricated via “ bottom-up ” methods, such as ED [ 15 , 16 ] or other 
sputtering deposition techniques [ 21 ]. However, these deposition 
methods are not suitable for creating high density nano-twins in a bulk

Severe plastic deformation of metals at certain extreme processing 
and deformation conditions, such as cryogenic temperature and high 
strain rates, provides practical approaches for creating high density 
nano-twins in bulk sample for large-scale industrial applications, which 
has been applied successfully to many conventional engineering fcc 
metals and alloys with relatively low stacking fault energy (SFE), such as 
Cu [ 23 – 25 ], Cu-Al [ 26 ], Cu-Ag [ 27 ] and stainless steels [ 28 ] et al. For 
instance, by means of dynamic plastic deformation (DPD) at LNT, high 
density nano-scale deformation twins in the form of nano-twin bundles 
were introduced into Cu – Al alloy, leading to a much higher strength 
than the ultrafine-grained counterparts without nano-twin bundles [ 26 ]. 
The intrinsic low SFE plus the extrinsic deformation conditions (low 
temperature and high strain rates) are responsible for the formation of 
deformation nano-twins. In spite of exciting success in strengthening 
various conventional engineering metals (alloys) by introducing 
nano-twin bundles structures, studies regarding the introduction of 
nano-twin bundles structures for strengthening HEAs/MEAs are still 
missing. For FCC CrCoNi-based HEAs/MEAs, the intrinsic SFE is very 
low and decreases dramatically at cryogenic temperatures, significantly 
promoting the occurrence of deformation twinning [ 6 – 12 ]. Along this 
line of thought, deformation of CrCoNi-based HEAs/MEAs at cryogenic 
temperature and high strain rates is therefore highly expected to pro -
duce a high density of deformation nano-twins, which are beneficial for 
both high strength and ductility. Although deformation twinning is 
known to occur in conventional deformed CrCoNi-based HEAs/MEAs 
[ 6 – 12 ], the anticipant high density nano-twins in the form of nano-twin 
bundles as reported recently in the DPDed Cu [ 26 ], to the authors ’ 
knowledge, are still seldom observed. 
Motivated by the above concerns, in the present study, FeCrCoNi 
HEA was chosen as a model material for this systematic study because its 
ultra-low SFE at cryogenic temperature that promotes the twin pro -
pensity [ 29 ]. Here we demonstrate that a mixed nanostructure, 
composed of high density of nano-scale twins in the form of bundles and 
nanograins in shear bands, can be introduced in a single-phase fcc bulk 
FeCrCoNi HEA by means of a cryo-rolling at LNT. The resultant mixed 
nanostructure results in a substantial enhancement of tensile strength of 
the FeCrCoNi HEA. The microstructural evolution and nanostructure 
formation mechanisms in the cryo-rolled FeCrCoNi HEA were discussed 
in detail. The process used here provides a simple and industrial 
approach to strengthen other single-phase fcc HEAs/MEAs with low SFE 
by generating high-density nano-scale twins.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructure characterization of the cryo-rolled FeCrCoNi HEA 
with 50% rolling reduction 
Fig. 1 a – c shows cross-sectional SEM-BSE images of the FeCrCoNi 
HEA subjected to cryo-rolling to a strain of 50% at LNT. Two distinct 
types of regions with different contrasts: “ gray ” and “ white ” (labeled as 
“ G ” and “ W ” , respectively) are present in Fig. 1 a. Statistical measure -
ments indicated that the “ gray ” regions occupy an area fraction of about 
76%, with the “ white ” regions constituting the remaining area. Higher 
magnification SEM-BSE images in Fig. 1 b and c reveal that a high density 
bundles of lamellae with an extremely fine layer thickness can be 
observed inside the “ gray ” regions. These nano-sized lamellas were 
proved to be nano-scale deformation twins, as identified by the 
following TEM observations in Fig. 2 . The nano-twins within “ gray ” 
regions are aligned roughly parallel to the RD. Except for nano-twins 
“ gray ” regions, the remaining “ white ” regions are characterized by 
intersecting line-like wavy bands, referred as shear bands, which sub -
divided the nano-twin bundle (gray) regions into prism-shaped ( Fig. 1 b) 
and rectangular ( Fig. 1 c) blocks, respectively. These line-like wavy 
bands were actually identified as nano-sized grains from the following 
TEM observations. 
The TEM images in Fig. 2 a – d clearly indicated that a mixed nano -
structure, composed of high density nano-scale twins in the form of 
nano-twins bundles and nano-grains in line-like shear bands, was 
formed in the cryo-rolled FeCrCoNi HEA with 50% reduction. Obvi -
ously, the nano-twin bundle regions were cut into discontinuous prism- 
shaped ( Fig. 2 a) and rectangular ( Fig. 2 b) blocks by intersections be -
tween the line-like shear bands. These resultant discontinuous nano- 
twin bundle blocks have a total width of a few micrometers and a 
length of several micrometers to several tens of micrometers. TEM ob -
servations further confirmed that the nano-twin bundle regions occupy 
an apparently larger area fraction than the shear band regions. Statis -
tical TEM measurements indicated that the average thickness of nano- 
twins lamellae was estimated to be about ~15 nm. The enlarged TEM 
micrograph in Fig. 2 c clearly reveals that plenty of dislocations were 
accumulated at twin boundaries and inside nano-twin interiors, which is 
the typical characteristic of deformation nano-twins. A close examina -
tion of shear bands with higher magnification ( Fig. 2 d) reveal that a 
nanograin structure, in which most nano-sized grains tend to be elon -
gated along the shear direction and some have a nearly equiaxed shape, 
is visible inside the shear bands. The average grain size of the nanograins 
is about 26 nm. In addition, very high dislocation density are found 
inside the nano-sized grains and most grain boundaries are poorly 
defined and displayed a “ diffuse ” contrast, characteristic of utra-fine/ 
nano grain materials processed by equal channel angular pressing 
(ECAP) or high pressure torsion (HPT). Based on the previous studies

3.2. Mechanical properties of the cryo-rolled FeCrCoNi HEA 
The room-temperature tensile property of the cryo-rolled FeCrCoNi 
HEA sample is displayed in Fig. 3 . To emphasize the substantial 
improvement in strength upon the formation of the nano-twin bundles 
architecture, the tensile engineering stress-strain curves for FeCoCrNi 
alloy in the initial condition and after rolling at 293 K are also illus -
trated. The cryo-rolled FeCrCoNi HEA sample exhibits an ultra-high 
tensile strength. The YS (at 0.2% offset) reaches as high as 1815 MPa, 
and the UTS was 1864 MPa. This YS value is more than 7 times higher 
than that of the corresponding coarse-grained FeCrCoNi HEA sample. 
The strength values are also noticeably higher than the sample rolled at 
293 K. Typical microstructure of the HEA after rolling at 293 K was 
exhibited in Fig. 3 b. Apparently, a typical rolling-deformed nano -
structure consisting of lamellae elongated ultra-fine grains with high 
density dislocations is observed in the sample deformed at 293 K. 
Although a small amount of deformation twins were also observed, 
nano-twin bundles as observed in the cryo-rolled sample ( Fig. 2 a – c) not 
formed. Therefore, the unique nano-twin bundle architecture is 
responsible for the observed high strength of cryo-rolled FeCrCoNi HEA. 
It is well known that twin boundaries, as a special type of coherent in -
terfaces, are effective in blocking dislocation motion for strengthening 
[ 15 ]. Moreover, the strengthening contribution of the twin boundaries 
increase with decreasing boundaries spacing in a Hall-Petch type rela -
tionship similar to that of grain boundary strengthening in nano -
crystalline metals [ 16 ]. Specifically, for the nano-twin bundle 
architecture, the higher critical stresses are required to carry plastic 
deformation across the nano-twin bundles structure other than 
individual twins, the strengthening effect is amplified [ 32 ]. In addition, 
the nano-sized grains in shear bands also contribute to an extra 
strengthening because the high density of grain boundaries restricts the 
mean free path of dislocations. Fig. 3 also shows that necking instability 
occurred at the very early stage of tensile deformation in the cryo-rolled 
FeCrCoNi HEA sample, as generally observed in the severely deformed 
metals, indicating poor uniform elongation. Even so, the cryo-rolled 
FeCrCoNi HEA still retains an appreciable tensile ductility with failure 
strain of 10.8%, which is nearly identical to that at 293 K. Such 
ultra-high strength along with a considerable tensile ductility is 
impressive when compared with that of other nanostructured MEAs/ -
HEAs prepared by severe plastic deformation techniques [ 33 ]. Accord -
ing to these results it is noted that the cryo-rolled FeCrCoNi HEA has 
lower work-hardening capacity than that of the electro-deposited 
nano-twinned Cu thin films containing a high density of nanoscale 
growth twins [ 15 , 16 ]. This may be due to the difference in the 
nano-twin structures. Obviously, the accumulation of high density of 
dislocations at twin boundaries ( Fig. 2 ) in the cryo-rolled FeCrCoNi HEA 
has a negligible effect on work-hardening capacity in comparison to that 
of the fully coherent twin boundaries in the EDed Cu at which few dis -
locations exist [ 15 , 16 ]. In addition, as mentioned above, besides the 
presence of high density of dislocations at twin boundaries, other 
nanostructure features, such as the nano-grains embedded with high 
density dislocations, are also responsible for the poor work-hardening 
capability.

Based on the microstructure features presented in Fig. 4 , the 
formation and development of nanostructures can been illustrated 
schematically in Fig. 5 . For metals and alloys, plastic deformation is 
accommodated by means of two competitive modes: dislocation slip and 
deformation twinning. Generally in fcc metals and alloys, deformation 
twinning is promoted by low SFE and/or a low temperatures and/or 
high strain rate [ 34 ]. For the current FeCrCoNi HEA subjected to 
cryo-rolling at LNT, deformation twinning is preferentially activated 
and dominate the plasticity because the intrinsically low SFE value, 
cryogenic temperature and high rolling rate. Thus, as shown in Figs. 1 
and 2 , the plastic deformation of the initial grain is dominated by 
deformation twinning, subdividing the initial coarse grain into 
nano-sized lamellas (nanotwin bundles), as illustrated in Fig. 5 b. Once 
the nano-twin bundles form, localized deformation in the form of 
bending of nano-twin bundles (shear bands) take place to accommodate 
further deformation, as schematically illustrated in Fig. 5 c. Bending of 
nano-twin bundles causes severe localized distortion deformation within 
the nano-twin bundles, which result in the formation of dense disloca -
tion walls or cell walls, as revealed in Fig. 4 b. With an increase of strain, 
these dislocation structures transform into a nano-scale grain structure, 
which is analogous to the grain refinement process established for

4. Conclusions 
In summary, bulk nanostructured FeCoCrNi HEA with a mixed 
nanostructure composed of high density nano-scale twins in the form of 
bundles and nano-sized grains in shear bands, was produced by cryo- 
rolling at LNT, which exhibits an ultra-high tensile strength. The spe -
cial designed nanostructure containing a high density nano-twin 
boundaries and nano-grains boundaries is primarily responsible for 
the observed ultra-high strength. The microstructural evolution and 
formation mechanism of the mixed nanostructure in LNT-cryo-rolled 
FeCrCoNi HEA were discussed based on the nucleation and thickening 
of shear bands. Moreover, the LNT-cryo-rolling process used here pro -
vides a simple and industrial approach to strengthen other fcc HEAs/ 
MEAs with low SFE by generating high-density nano-scale twins.
