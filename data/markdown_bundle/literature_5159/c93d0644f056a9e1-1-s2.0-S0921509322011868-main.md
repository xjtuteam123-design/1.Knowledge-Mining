ABSTRACT 
Mo-0.5 wt%ZrC alloy plate with enhanced mechanical properties and thermal stability was manufactured by 
powder metallurgy and hot rolling. The as-rolled Mo-0.5%ZrC plate shows an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 
725 MPa and a total elongation (TE) of 24.1% at room temperature. After annealing at 1300 
◦
C, the UTS of Mo- 
0.5%ZrC alloy decreased to 676 MPa while the TE increased to 32.8%. After 1600 
◦
C annealing, the UTS of Mo- 
0.5%ZrC alloy is 507 MPa and the TE increased significantly to 56.4%. Additionally, Mo-0.5%ZrC alloy exhibits a 
high recrystallization start temperature of about 1300 
◦
C. The addition of nanoscale ZrC particles can enhance 
the strength and high temperature stability by pining dislocations and grain boundaries. Meanwhile, ZrC 
nanoparticles can also react with the impurity oxygen to form Zr – C – O or ZrO
2 
particles, and thus alleviate the 
oxygen embrittlement of Mo alloy. However, the oxide particles tend to grow into larger particles at high 
temperatures, leading to decreased number density of dispersoids and lower dispersion-strengthening effect. 
1. Introduction 
Molybdenum (Mo) is a typical body-centered cubic metal with high 
melting temperature (2610 
◦
C), high thermal conductivity and low 
thermal expansion, and has been used in machinery, military and 
aerospace industries [ 1 – 3 ]. Mo also has good compatibility with liquid 
alkali metals and is a candidate structural material for space fission re -
actors [ 4 , 5 ]. However, pure Mo exhibits inadequate ductility at room 
temperature, recrystallization embrittlement and irradiation-induced 
embrittlement, which greatly limit its widespread applications [ 6 , 7 ]. 
One of the main reasons for the low ductility of Mo alloys is the 
segregation of some interstitial impurities such as oxygen (O) at grain 
boundaries (GBs) [ 8 – 10 ]. Therefore, the methods that modify the GBs 
and interstitial impurity distribution would affect the strength and 
ductility of Mo alloys [ 11 , 12 ]. Grain refinement through severe plastic 
deformation can decrease the average concentration of O at GBs and 
improve the mechanical properties due to the abundant GBs. However, 
numerous GBs lead to thermal instability of the materials [ 13 , 14 ]. The 
dispersion of thermally stable particles (oxides such as La

O

carbides as, TiC, ZrC, HfC) can effectively refine grains and increase the 
GBs, and enhance the strength and thermal stability by pining disloca -

nanostructured Mo – La
2

perature, high hardness and thermal stability have been used as 
strengthening phases for Mo alloys [ 20 , 24 ]. Furthermore, the smaller

coherent or semi-coherent interface with the Mo matrix, and thus im -

2. Experimental 
2.1. Material preparation 
The Mo-0.5 wt% ZrC plates were prepared by powder metallurgical 
method. Commercial Mo powder (average particle size ~2 μ m) and 
nanosized ZrC powder (average particle size ~50 nm) were ball-milled 
in a planetary ball mill at 260 rpm for 4 h under an argon atmosphere. 
WC balls and jars were used and the ball-to-powder ratio is 8:1. The ball- 
milled powders were subsequently compacted by cold isostatic pressing 
at a pressure of 180 MPa and then sintered in flowing dry hydrogen at 
1900 
◦
C for 4 h. The sintered billet was rolled into 7 mm from an original 
thickness of ~21 mm at 1200 – 1500 
◦
C, and the thickness reduction per 
pass was 15 – 20%. A schematic diagram of the preparation process is 
shown in Fig. 1 a. The chemical compositions of the initial Mo powder 
and the as-rolled Mo-0.5% ZrC alloy are listed in Table 1 . The relative 
density of the Mo-0.5% ZrC alloy measured by Archimedes ’ principle 
was 99.48%.

The Field-emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM, Zeiss

4. Discussion 
The fracture morphologies of as-rolled and annealed Mo-0.5%ZrC 
samples characterized by SEM are shown in Fig. 8 . The hot-rolling 
process greatly reduces the porosity and improves the relative density 
of the alloy. Meanwhile, this hot plastic deformation process leads to a 
delamination structure along the RD (see Fig. 8 a), which may blunt the 
cracks and hinder crack propagation [ 35 , 36 ]. The fracture morphologies 
of the A1200 and A1300 samples are similar to that of the as-rolled one. 
By magnifying the fracture surface of the A1300 sample, as illustrated in 
the inset of Fig. 8 c, several dimples with second-phase particles distri -
bution can be found. These particles can impede the movement of dis -
locations during stretching, thus increasing the strength of the Mo alloy, 
while the dimples are characteristic of ductile fracture. This structure 
well explains the high strength and ductility of the A1300 samples at RT. 
For the A1400 sample ( Fig. 8 d), partially transgranular fracture and 
reduced delamination areas demonstrate that subgrains introduced by 
deformation begin to disappear. Different from intergranular rupture, 
transgranular fracture can be observed in the A1500 and A1600 samples 
( Fig. 8 e and f), suggesting that the addition of ZrC nanoparticles im -
proves the grain boundary bonding. 
To understand the strengthening mechanisms, the as-rolled Mo-0.5% 
ZrC alloy was characterized by TEM and EDS, as shown in Fig. 9 . In the 
as-rolled Mo-0.5%ZrC alloy, most of the smaller particles are in the 
range of 20 – 300 nm and a portion of larger particles with size greater 
than 300 nm are distributed on the GBs ( Fig. 9 a). The average size of 
second-phase particles in the as-rolled Mo-0.5%ZrC sample is about 
0.26 μ m ( Fig. 9 f). As shown in Fig. 9 b and c, small particles dispersed in 
the grains can pin down and accumulate dislocations, thereby improving 
the strength of the alloy according to the Orowan strengthening mech -
anism. The EDS results ( Fig. 9 d and e) suggest that the large particles in 
the as-rolled Mo-0.5%ZrC sample are Zr – C – O or ZrO
2
. These larger 
particles at GBs can hinder the migration of GBs and improve the ther -
mal stability. However, due to the relatively large size of second-phase 
particles and low content of ZrC particles, the strength of the hot- 
rolled Mo-0.5%ZrC plate is much lower than that of the swaged Mo- 
0.6%ZrC alloy, which has a high tensile strength of 928 MPa and a TE of 
34.4% at RT [ 25 ]. In the swaged Mo-0.6%ZrC alloy, the average particle 
size is 54 nm and the average grain size is only 0.67 μ m [ 25 ]. These 
results demonstrate that the distribution and size of the second-phase

For the hot-rolled Mo-0.5%ZrC alloy, the grains were refined and 
numerous dislocations were introduced during hot rolling. Meanwhile, 
nanoscale particles dispersed in the Mo matrix can improve the strength 
by hindering the migration of dislocations and GBs. Therefore, fine- 
grain strengthening, dislocation-strengthening and dispersion strength -
ening are considered as the main strengthening mechanisms in hot- 
rolled Mo-0.5%ZrC alloy. When the T
a 
exceeds the recrystallization 
start temperature, dislocation annihilation leads to a dramatic decrease 
in dislocation strengthening, and grain growth also decreases in the fine- 
grain strengthening effect. In this case, the dispersion-strengthening 
becomes the dominant strengthening mechanism for Mo-0.5%ZrC 
alloy. Therefore, to fabricate Mo – ZrC alloy with both high strength and 
good ductility, the size and distribution of particles should be controlled 
strictly during the preparation process. 
5. Conclusions 
In this study, Mo-0.5% ZrC alloy plates with enhanced mechanical 
properties and high thermal stability were successfully fabricated by 
nanoscale ZrC dispersions. The major findings can be concluded as 
follows. 
(1) The UTS of the as-rolled Mo-0.5%ZrC alloy is 725 MPa and the TE 
is 24.1% at RT. With the increase of annealing temperature, the 
tensile strength of Mo-0.5%ZrC alloy gradually decreases and the 
ductility increases. After 1300 
◦
C annealing, the UTS of Mo-0.5% 
ZrC alloy decreased to 676 MPa and the TE increased to 32.8%. 
(2) The hot-rolled Mo-0.5%ZrC alloy has a high recrystallization start 
temperature of about 1300 
◦
C, which is about 300 
◦
C higher than 
that of pure Mo. After high-temperature annealing at 1600 
◦
C, the 
UTS of Mo-0.5%ZrC alloy decreases to 507 MPa and the TE 
significantly increases to 56.4%, showing excellent room- 
temperature ductility. 
(3) The addition of ZrC nanoparticles can enhance the strength and 
thermal stability of Mo alloy by pinning dislocations and grain 
boundaries. Meanwhile, ZrC can react with impurity O to form 
stable Zr – C – O or ZrO
2 
particles and alleviate the O embrittlement 
of the Mo alloy. However, the oxide particles tend to segregate at 
grain boundaries and grow into larger particles at high temper -
atures, leading to decreased number density of dispersoids and 
lower dispersion-strengthening effect. Therefore, to fabricate 
Mo – ZrC alloys with both high strength and good ductility, the 
size and distribution of ZrC particles should be further optimized.
