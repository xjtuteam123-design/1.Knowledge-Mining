abstract
Magnesiumegadolinium (Mg eGd)-based alloys have excellent high-temperature properties and the
addition of two heterogeneous rare earth (RE) elements may promote the formation of secondary phase
for better-strengthened properties. Herein, novel Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloys were prepared by
synergistic reaction induced by high-speed extrusion (EX) and short-time aging treatment (AT). The
microstructures, textures, and mechanical properties of the resulting alloys were then comprehensively
studied by various analytical methods. The result reveals that the as-cast Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy
is composed of
a-Mg matrix and Mg 5(Gd,Nd) phase with bony and continuous networks at grain
boundaries. The microstructure presents coarse deformed grains and ﬁne recrystallized grains after high-
speed EX, and formation of typical ð0001Þk ED extruded ﬁber texture and [ 1211] RE texture is observed.
The EX22 sample exhibits more Mg 5(Gd,Nd) precipitated phases, fuller DRXed grains, and shorter peak
aging times than the EX9 sample. After the AT at 250 /C14 C, the proportion of deformed grains decreases due
to static recrystallization, which leads to a reduction in the average grain size and weakening of the
texture intensity. Therefore, combined effect of all strengthening mechanisms aids in achieving balance
of high-strength and high-elongation for Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy. The values of yield strength
(YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS), and elongation (EL) of EX22-A sample at room temperature are
found to be 292.5 MPa, 350.6 MPa, and 24.3%, respectively. Overall, this study provides relevant
experimental basis and theoretical guidance for the development of high-strength Mg-RE alloys, which
are useful for future consideration.

1. Introduction
Magnesium (Mg) alloys are characterized by low density, high
speciﬁc strength, and superior speci ﬁc stiffness, thereby attracting
signiﬁcant research attention and possessing potential applications
in many ﬁelds such as aerospace, automotive, biomedical and en-
ergy sectors.1e3 However, the low strength and poor heat resistance
of Mg alloys have limited their practical applications. It is specu-
lated that the addition of rare earth (RE) elements in Mg alloys may
signiﬁcantly improve the mechanical properties at room temper-
ature (RT) and under high-temperature conditions. As a result, Mg

Aging treatment can effectively promote the precipitation of
secondary phases and regulate the mechanical properties of al-
loys.11,12 Extrusion deformation can re ﬁne the grain, generate a
large number of dislocations, and provide the location for second-
ary phase nucleation and nucleation driving energy. Previous
studies have demonstrated that magnesium-gadolinium (MgeGd)-
based alloys can achieve good mechanical properties after extru-
sion and aging treatment.
13e15 For example, Pang et al. 16 subjected
Mg-10Gd(-3Sm)-Zr (wt%) alloy to hot extrusion and aging treat-
ment (10 h), which yielded high strength with TYS of 271 MPa and
failure at elongation of 7.4%. He et al.17 successfully developed high-
strength Mg-10Gd-2Y-0.5Zr alloy by hot extrusion and aging
treatment (10 h). The extruded alloy exhibited TYS of 331 MPa and
EL of 12.8% at RT. Li et al. 18 prepared Mg-14Gd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy
with a remarkably high TYS of 305 MPa by combining hot extrusion
process with aging treatment (36 h). Overall, aging of Mg alloys
after deformation could further improve their mechanical proper-
ties. However, the aging treatment time is relatively long and most
researches dealing with Mg eRE extrusion alloys are based on low
extrusion speed ( <5 mm/s). The increase in extrusion speed
(/C21 5 mm/s) can effectively shorten the aging time and save energy
consumption.7 Therefore, systematic exploration of high-speed
extrusion to shorten the aging time and improve the production
efﬁciency is important for yielding alloys with better properties. 9
However, in-depth research dealing with the microstructural evo-
lution mechanism and mechanical properties of high-speed short-
aging deformed Mg alloys still lacks.
MgeGd-based alloys have received increasing attention due to
their excellent high-temperature properties. Some studies 19 have
shown that adding 7 wt% Gd to Mg produces a ﬁner alloy structure
and increases the strength of Mg. Furthermore, the addition of two
heterogeneous RE elements to Mg alloys may yield more signiﬁcant
strengthening effects. Since Gd and Nd belong to different RE group
systems (cerium group and yttrium group, respectively), 20 their
solid solution degrees may be reduced with respect to each other in
Mg to promote the formation of the secondary phase.
Herein, Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy was extruded at a high
extrusion speed of 16 mm/s followed by a short-time aging treat-
ment. The intrinsic relationships among microstructure, texture,
and mechanical properties under the heat treatment conditions
after deformation were systematically investigated, and the
strengthening mechanism of Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy after
extrusion and aging was analyzed. The results revealed improved
strength and elongation properties of Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%)
alloy.
2. Experimental
2.1. Sample preparation
The Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy was prepared from pure Mg
(99.9%), pure Gd (99.99%), Mg-30Nd (wt%), and Mg e30Zr (wt%)
master alloys supplied by Suzhou Zhengde Rare Earth Material Co.,
Ltd. The mixture was melted in an electrical-resistance furnace
under CO
2/SF6 (99:1) protective gas atmosphere. Pure Mg was
added to the alloy and then melted and heated at 720 /C14 C for 30 min.
Next, Mg eRE master alloy was added to the mixture and the
temperature was raised to 750 /C14 C followed by the addition of
Mge30Zr master alloy. After complete melting and stirring for
5 min, the re ﬁning agent was added and the contents were stirred
for 5 min. Finally, the mixture was left to cool down for 30 min to
710 /C14 C and semi-continuously cast into round bars with diameters
of about 97 mm. The chemical composition of the experimental
alloys determined by inductively-coupled plasma (ICP, MA
02038e3148) spectroscopy revealed the composition to be
7.29 wt% Gd, 2.01 wt% Nd, 0.52 wt% Zr, and bal. Mg.
Subsequently, the as-cast sample was treated at 515 /C14 C for 8 h
followed by water quenching (80 /C14 C). The solution treatment (ST)
was carried out in a vacuum tubular high-temperature furnace
(OTF-1200X) under 99.9% Ar atmosphere. After completion of ST,
the obtained Mg alloy samples were machined into F
90 mm /C2 60 mm cylindrical extrusion billets. The extrusion was
performed at 380 /C14 C using extrusion ratios of 9:1 and 22:1 at a ram
speed of 16 mm/s ( Fig. 1(a and b)). Before extrusion, the mold was
preheated to 400 /C14 C. After extrusion, both EX9 and EX22 extruded
samples (based on extrusion ratios) were aged at 250 /C14 C for 2 and
1h( Fig. 1(c)), respectively.

and d)) indicates that the density of the precipitated phase is
greater under the condition of large extrusion ratio.
3.2. Hardness after aging treatment
The age-hardening curves of the extrusion process of arti ﬁcially
aged Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy at 250 /C14 C are shown in Fig. 6.
The aging-based strengthening originate mainly from precipitated
phases out of the supersaturated matrix. 22 Before peak aging, the
Vickers hardness gradually increases as a function of the aging
time. After peak aging, the precipitated phases grow and increase,
changing from coherent to non-coherent with the matrix, while the
hardness decreases slowly.
The age-hardening behaviors of alloys with different extrusion
ratios seem to be very similar. However, the time to peak aging
varies signiﬁcantly. The hardness value of the solution-treated Mg-
7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy is estimated to be 67 HV. Comparatively,
the extrusion deformation process leads to signi ﬁcant increase in
the hardness values of the Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy to 81 HV
and 83 HV in 2 and 1 h, respectively. During isothermal aging at
250 /C14 C, the extruded Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy reaches
maximum hardness of 88.3 HV and 90.13 HV in 2 and 1 h,
respectively. Compared to a small extrusion ratio, the time for large
extrusion ratio peak aging reduces signi ﬁcantly.
3.3. Microstructure after aging treatment
OM and SEM images of the Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloys with
peak AT time are shown in Fig. 7. The microstructure of the EX9-A
sample consists of numerous ﬁne equiaxial grains and some
irregular coarse grains (red elliptical circle, Fig. 7(a)). The formation
of coarse grains is caused by the heredity of deformed grains of the
extrusion alloys.22 Fig. 7(b) demonstrates the precipitation of large
amount of dispersed and uniform secondary phase after AT.
Compared to EX9-A, more obvious extrusion stripe-like structural
features appear in the EX22-A sample ( Fig. 7 (c)). The deformed
grains basically disappear, and the sample organization consists of
fully recrystallized equiaxed grain organization with ﬁner grain
size. At large extrusion ratios, the grain orientation along the ED is
more pronounced, and the secondary phase mostly precipitates at
higher energy regions, such as GBs, dislocations, and defects. 23
Consequently, more ordered secondary phases along the ED are
similarly produced after AT, as shown in Fig. 7(d).
The XRD results of extruded Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy af-
ter different aging process treatments are presented in Fig. 8 . The
test results are similar to those for only extruded alloy. After AT of
the EX9-A sample, the peak corresponding to Mg
3(Gd,Nd) gradu-

TEM images and the selected area electron diffraction (SAED)

Compared to the TEM morphologies shown in Fig. 5 (c and d), the
density of precipitated phases increases signi ﬁcantly after aging
treatment, as shown in Fig. 9 (c and d).

5. Conclusions
(1) The microstructure of as-cast Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy
is composed of a-Mg matrix and Mg 5(Gd,Nd) phase with
bony and continuous aspects at grain boundaries. After high-
speed extrusion, the metastable phase Mg 3(Gd,Nd) appears,
and then a short aging treatment causes the disappearance of
the metastable phase Mg 3(Gd,Nd) in the EX22-A sample.
(2) After high-speed extrusion, the microstructure transforms
into deformed grains with strong extrusion texture and ﬁne
equiaxial grains and formed typical ﬁber texture-ð0001Þk ED
and [ 1211] RE texture. Moreover, the increase in extrusion
ratio results in more Mg 5(Gd,Nd) precipitated phases, fuller
DRXed grains, and shorter peak aging times.
(3) After the AT at 250 /C14 C, the proportion of deformation grains
decreases due to static recrystallization. Moreover, the grain
size of the EX9-A sample decreases to (1.98 ± 0.5) mm, with a
texture intensity of 1.79. The grain size of the EX22-A sample
declines to (1.89 ± 0.5) mm, with the texture intensity of 1.99.
Further, more Mg 5(Gd,Nd) phases precipitate, effectively
pinning grain boundaries and hindering dislocation
movement.
(4) The combined effect of all strengthening mechanisms en-
dows the Mg-7Gd-2Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) extrusion alloy with
excellent strength, among which the grain re ﬁnement
strengthening mechanism plays a dominant role. The values
of YS, UTS, and EL of EX22-A sample at RT reach 292.5 MPa,
350.6 MPa, and 24.3%, respectively.
In summary, relevant experimental basis and theoretical guid-
ance for the development of high-strength Mg eRE alloys are pro-
vided in this study, which are useful for future considerations.
