1. Introduction
Si et al. (2006) describe that Ti2AlNb alloy has received con-
siderable attention from world aerospace ﬁeld in recent years for
its characteristics of lightweight and high temperature resistance.
Banerjee et al. (1988) found Ti2AlNb alloy with O phase for the ﬁrst
time, when Banerjee’s research group in India’s Defence Metallur-
gical Laboratory was studying the intermetallic compound Ti3Al.
Subsequently, scientists from various countries have studied the
preparation technology of Ti2AlNb alloy with O phase. Boehlert
(2001) describes scientists have deeply analyzed its microstructure,
phase transitions and related mechanical properties. Ti2AlNb alloy
is intermetallic compound consisting of two or three phases from
/H92512, B2 and O phase, with the density between 3.8 and 5.9 g/cm3.
Compared with other alloys, Ti2AlNb alloy can reduce weight by
34–56% when processed into the same component. Meanwhile,
Feng et al. (2011) ﬁnd that Ti2AlNb alloy has good heat resistance
and stands out in terms of creep resistance and oxidation resistance.
Kumpfert (2001) describes that the composition of Ti2AlNb alloy
is usually in the range of Ti–(18–30%)Al–(12.5–30%)Nb(always
atomic ratio unless indicated), with a small amount of other ele-
ments. And Zhang et al. (2001) ﬁnd that with the addition of Nb,
each phase region in Ti2AlNb differs in temperature range. With
further research on Ti2AlNb alloys, Li et al. (2004) divide Ti2AlNb
alloy into two categories according to content of Nb: when con-
tent of Nb is less than 25%, /H9252/B2+O+/H92512 three-phase alloy, the

ﬁrst generation of O-phase alloy is obtained by heat treatment in
three-phase region of Ti2AlNb alloy, with a nominal composition
of Ti–25Al–17Nb, Ti–21Al–23Nb, Ti–22Al–23Nb; when content of
Nb exceeds 25%, O+B2 phase alloy named the second generation
of O-phase alloy, is obtained by heat treatment, and its nominal
composition is Ti–22Al–25Nb, Ti–22Al–27Nb.
The current studies on Ti2AlNb alloy are limited to the material
itself, while relatively less in terms of its forming process. Although
Ti2AlNb alloy has many advantages, formability of Ti2AlNb alloy at
room temperature is not very good. Shi et al. (2013) conducted
crimp test and demonstrate that plasticity of Ti2AlNb alloy at room
temperature is poor and springback is serious, so ordinary form-
ing process is not suitable for the processing of Ti2AlNb alloy.
Because it has superplasticity at high temperature, superplastic
forming is an ideal way to process Ti2AlNb alloy. Zhang and Jiang
(2014) describe superplastic forming/diffusion bonding (SPF/DB)
process is an advanced manufacturing method for producing com-
plex thin-sheet components. And this compound manufacturing
technology can meet process requirement of Ti2AlNb alloy. There-
fore, as Ti2AlNb alloy attracts more and more attention from the
aerospace ﬁeld, it is very necessary to study its processes of super-
plastic forming and diffusion bonding.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Effect of temperature and strain rate on superplasticity of
Ti–22Al–24Nb alloy
Stretched samples and the tensile strain–stress curves at differ-
ent temperatures are shown in Fig. 4. As can be seen from the tensile
strain–stress curves, with the increase of tensile strain, the tensile
stress shows a trend that increases rapidly to the peak ﬁrst and then
decreases gradually. With the stretching temperature increasing,
material’s yield strength decreases, and superplasticity increases
in general. The superplasticity of material at 960 ◦C and 980 ◦C is
signiﬁcantly better than it at 900 ◦C and 930 ◦C. This is because
material’s ﬂow stress is smaller at higher temperature, and it is
relatively easier for material to produce plastic deformation and
have a greater degree of plastic deformation. When the temper-
ature reaches 960 ◦C, material gets a maximum elongation, then
as the temperature continues to rise, the elongation decreases due

After uniaxial tensile experiments of Ti–22Al–24Nb alloy at dif-
ferent high temperatures, the samples near the fracture were taken
for microstructure analysis, as shown in Fig. 6. As is seen from Fig. 6,
plate gets equiaxed grains after unidirectional tensile experiment
at high temperature, which proves that grain boundary sliding
plays a major role in the deformation process. In addition, dynamic
recrystallization reﬁnes the grains, thus increases large-angle grain
boundaries, and further facilitates grain boundary sliding process.
It can be seen from Fig. 6a that when sample stretches at 900 ◦C,
the O and B2 phases distribute evenly in the structure owing to
low elongation. In Fig. 6b–d, with the rise of temperature, the
grains present equiaxed shape, and grain boundary sliding mech-
anism plays a leading role on Ti–22Al–24Nb alloy’s superplastic
deformation. In addition, O phase in the microstructure reduces
signiﬁcantly. And B2 and O phases convert mutually due to the
important coordination mechanism of recrystallization in super-
plastic deformation. As is shown in Fig. 6d, the microstructure of
the plate at 980 ◦C presents directional trait since the grains are
elongated in the stretching direction. Fig. 6e demonstrates that the
tendency that grains extend along the stretching direction grad-
ually weaken with the distance from the tensile fracture surfaces
increases.

In order to know the mechanical properties of Ti–22Al–24Nb
alloy’s formed component, tensile strength test was taken. Ten-

The testing temperatures are set as room temperature, 650 ◦C,
700 ◦C, 750 ◦C and 800 ◦C, and the tensile strain–stress curves are
shown in Fig. 12. It is shown in Fig. 12 that when stretching at
high temperature, with the increasing temperature, plate shows a
downward trend in the tensile strength, and fracture takes place
in the process in which tensile stress decreases gradually after
reaches the peak. Meanwhile plate’s elongation increases in this
process. The elongation at 650 ◦C is only about 7%, while at 800 ◦C
it reached 18%. This is mainly because the ﬂow stress of plate gets
smaller gradually as temperature increases. When stretching at
high temperature, the component has the highest tensile strength
at 650 ◦C, reaching 801 MPa, while has the lowest tensile strength
at 800 ◦C, only 534 MPa, and the tensile strengths in the range of
650–800◦C are between them. Meanwhile, the tensile strength at
room temperature is up to 936 MPa. In conclusion, the component
of Ti–22Al–24Nb alloy has relatively high tensile strength at both
room temperature and high temperature.

4. Conclusions
(1) The uniaxial tensile experiments of Ti–22Al–24Nb alloy at high
temperatures show that the alloy has the best superplasti-
city under the temperature of 960 ◦C and the strain rate of
0.0005 s−1, in which elongation can reach 238%. Samples get
equiaxed grains after uniaxial tensile experiments at high tem-
perature. As temperature increases, grains present a certain
directional trait in the tensile direction. In this process, B2 and
O phases convert mutually, and O phase in the microstructure
signiﬁcantly reduces.
(2) The vacuum diffusion bonding tests of Ti–22Al–24Nb alloy at
different pressures were conducted, shear strength test at room
temperature and microstructure of diffusion bonding interface
demonstrate that within a certain range of pressure, strength
of joint interface will increase with the rise of pressure applied
to diffusion bonding sample, that it has the best quality of diffu-
sion bonding under 20.83 MPa when the temperature is 960 ◦C
and the holding time is 2 h. Under this process parameter, the
shear strength of sample will reach 766.7 MPa, and grains have
the trend of growing continuously across the diffusion bonding
interface.
(3) The box-shaped component was obtained after single plate’s
superplastic forming test, and it ﬁts the die well. Detection on
its wall thickness shows that wall thickness is well distributed
in general. Tests on the tensile strength of Ti–22Al–24Nb alloy’s
formed component show that tensile strength at room tem-
perature is up to 936 MPa. At high temperature, when the
temperature gets higher, samples show lower tensile strength
and higher elongation, and the tensile strengths in the range of
650–800◦C are between 801 MPa and 534 MPa. Thus it proves
that the component of Ti–22Al–24Nb alloy has relatively high
tensile strength both at room temperature and high tempera-
ture. After analyzing the microstructure of the plate, it is found
that a few of grains in deformation zone are stretched, but most
of grains are still equiaxed, whose grain size is about 20 /H9262m.
