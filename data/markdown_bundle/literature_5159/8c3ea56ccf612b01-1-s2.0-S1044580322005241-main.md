ABSTRACT 
High temperature tensile behaviors of two W-free Co-Ni-Al-based γ’ -strengthened superalloys at 773 K ~ 1073 K 
were investigated. The tensile strength and elongation of two superalloys decrease with increasing temperature. 
The fracture mechanism changes from dimple fracture to brittle fracture. A detailed observation of the fracture 
shows that the intermediate temperature embrittlement caused by decohesion of slip plane plays a significant 
role in reducing high temperature performance. Both superalloys reach to peak yield strength at 923 K. 
Occurance of yield strength anomaly (YSA) is derived from the synergistic effects of dislocation locking 
strengthening and dynamic strain aging strengthening. Dislocation cross-slipping hinders the dislocation 
migration. Dislocations ’ unpinning to the moving solutes increases the yield strength at 723 K ~ 973 K. A rapid 
weakening of grain boundaries strength decreases the yield strength at 1073 K.

nents and industrial gas turbines. Compared with traditional Co-based 
superalloys, which are commonly designed with considerations of both 
solid-solution strengthening and carbide strengthening, the Co-based 
superalloys strengthened by γ’ precipitates exhibit better creep resis -
tance due to the stable and coherent strengthening structure such as

the high density of superalloy brought by high content of W results in 
lower strength-to-weight ratio, which limits the application of this su -

[ 8 ], Co-Ti-Cr [ 9 ], Co-Ni-Cr [ 10 ], and so on. The development of W- 
free Co-based superalloys by replacing W with Nb, V, Ta and other 
alloying elements is expected to achieve excellent high temperature 
performances [ 11 ]. Our previous work has also confirmed that the 
addition of 2 at. % Cr or Mo in Co-Ni-Al-based superalloys can increase 
the volume fraction and average size of γ’ precipitates during aging 
treatment [ 12 , 13 ]. 
Except for oxidation resistance, the high temperature mechanical 
properties of superalloys act as an important index to evaluate its

In this study, high temperature tensile tests were carried out on two 
γ’ -strengthened Co-Ni-Al-based superalloys at different temperatures, 
and the tensile behavior was systematically investigated. The fracture 
mechanism and the underlying defect structures of YSA were also dis -
cussed. In addition, a temperature dependent model was employed to 
evaluate the strengthening contributions of 2Cr and 2Mo superalloys 
with different compositions. 
2. Experimental procedures 
The Co-Ni-Al-based superalloys used in this work were fabricated by 
vacuum induction melting with a mixture of pure Co (99.95 wt%), Ni 
(99.8 wt%), Al (99.95 wt%), Ti (99.95 wt%), Cr (99.8 wt%) and Mo 
(99.95 wt%). The melted ingots were homogenized at 1473 K for 4 h and 
then rolled into plates with a thickness of 12 mm in 1423 K. The 
chemical compositions of two superalloys are shown in Table 1 . For 
convenience, the two superalloys were named as 2Cr and 2Mo, 
respectively.

At 923 K, fracture mechanism of the two superalloys began to change to 
brittle fracture. The elongation was the lowest at 923 K and then 
increased with the temperature increasing, indicating the existence of 
intermediate temperature embrittlement (ITB). The ITB is generally 
caused by decohesion of slip plane [ 39 ]. Slip was accelerated by thermal 
activation and the continuous accumulation of dislocations leads to the 
increase in local strain at grain boundaries [ 40 , 41 ], which might result 
in the occurrence of cracks and reduce the strength of grain boundaries 
[ 42 ]. 
At 1073 K, the ductility increased after the intermediate temperature 
trough, the slight recovery of ductility is due to recrystallization soft -
ening, as shown in Fig. 6 . However, the fracture mechanism is still 
mainly intergranular fracture. As shown in Fig. 9 a, many parallel slip 
lines on the planes were revealed, which suggests that the slip plane 
decohesion has occurred during tensile tests. Due to the severeness of 
grain boundaries diffusion, grain/twin boundaries are toilless to pro -
duce local failure when the location of dislocation pile-ups are concen -
trated under stress [ 25 , 27 , 32 , 42 ]. The junction of twinning and grain 
boundaries might be the source of cracks under stress concentration, as 
shown in Fig. 9 b. 
4.2. Synergistic effect of dislocation locks and DSA 
At 773 K, the deformation was sufficient and the stress concentration 
caused by dislocation pile-up results in that the grain boundaries 
become the source of dislocations. Dislocations slip is dominant during 
deformation. Fig. 10 a shows a large number of dislocations pile-ups and 
slip bands at 773 K, indicating that work hardening is intense. In addi -
tion, high density geometrically necessary dislocations (GND) were 
observed near grain boundaries, as shown in Fig. 10 b, demonstrating 
that the deformation was uniform under the coordination of grain 
boundaries [ 20 , 43 , 44 ]. 
In addition, it is worth noting that the interface of grain boundaries 
at the location of pile-ups is not straight, which called “ Non-equilibrium 
grain boundary ” can act as a diffusion channel of solute atoms such as Cr 
and Mo [ 45 ]. The “ Cottrell atmosphere ” will produce at the location of 
dislocations which adsorbed solute atoms and block dislocations 
migration [ 21 ]. What ’ s more, researchers have reported that Cr and Mo 
elements tend to segregate at the location of dislocations under stress 
and hinder the dislocations migration [ 45 ], which is also illustrated by 
the small size γ’ precipitates distributed at the dislocations in Fig. 10 c 
and d. 
Due to dislocation slip as the main deformation mechanism at low 
temperature, the interaction between moving dislocations and solutes 
can ’ t be ignored. Periodic pinning and unpinning between solute atoms 
and dislocations will occur when moving dislocations carrying solute 
atoms are blocked by obstacles such as the γ / γ’ phase interface 
[ 21 , 22 , 26 , 27 ]. This called “ dynamic strain aging (DSA) ” can be char -
acterized by the stress serrations, which is also observed in the grey box 
of Fig. 3 . 
At low temperature, the solute atoms diffuse slowly and the moving 
rates of solute atoms and moving dislocations can match well. However, 
with the increase of temperature, the solutes ’ diffusion is significantly 
accelerated, while the growth of moving dislocations rate is small due to 
the limitation by the fixed strain rate. It leads to the mismatch of the two 
moving rates and no stress serrations occurred at 923 K ~ 1073 K. Our 
previous work has reported that the Al and Ti atoms exist in the form of 
γ’ precipitates in two superalloys, while the Cr and Mo are distributed 
uniformly in the γ matrix and γ’ precipitates phase as replacement atoms 
[ 46 ], which has been confirmed to be more likely to diffuse [ 45 , 47 ]. In 
addition, Neumeier et al. [ 48 ] calculated the activation energies of 
alloying elements in fcc
–
Co, the activation energies of Al (287 KJ/mol) 
and Ti (280 KJ/mol) are larger than that of Cr (254 KJ/mol) and Mo 
(260 KJ/mol), indicating that Cr and Mo elements are more likely to 
participate in the interaction between solute atoms and moving dislo -
cations, and thus the alloys exhibit DSA effect. The DSA effect can 
improve the yield strength of superalloy by releasing the stress during 
tensile tests [ 21 ]. 
Meanwhile, DSA effect was also reported to be characterized by 
micro-twins (large stacking faults) which could act as obstacles and 
significantly block dislocation migration [ 21 , 22 , 25 – 27 ]. Micro-twins 
are frequently produced by shear of γ’ precipitates, but occasionally 
occur in γ matrix [ 18 , 21 , 27 ]. In this study, some micro-twins were 
observed as shown in Fig. 10 e. The High-Angle Annular Dark Field

related to the brittle fracture mechanism of superalloy. With the increase

weaken grain boundaries, and thus this phenomenon plays an important 
role in reducing yield strength. The increase of temperature can also 
improve the diffusion coefficients of solute atoms and weaken the so -
lution strengthening effect of γ matrix [ 26 , 32 ]. It is still possible that 
dislocations will break away from the γ’ precipitates through climbing 
or cross slipping due to the excessive thermal activation, which reduces 
the yield strength [ 21 , 29 , 50 ]. Although there was no evidence for this in 
present work, it can still be corroborated in other research [ 26 ]. 
In total, the YSA is derived from the synergistic effects of multiple 
strengthen mechanisms. On the one hand, the DSA effect which occurred 
at 773 K ~ 923 K increases the deformation resistance. On the other 
hand, the increase of dislocation locks densities lead to the abnormal 
improvement of yield strength. 
4.3. Model of yield strength contribution 
The yield stress of Co-Ni-Al-based superalloys can be divided into 
four strengthening contributions [ 51 , 52 ]: (i) grains refinement; (ii) 
precipitation shearing; (iii) Orowan bypassing and (iv) solid solution in γ 
matrix. In this study, the contribution of grains refinement to yield 
strength was ignored in this calculation because the grain elongation at 
low temperature (773 K) did not significantly change grain size and 
recrystallization at high temperature (1073 K) did not significantly 
refine grains. 
In this work, the anti-phase boundaries (APBs) formed after tensile 
tests at 773 K and 923 K, as shown in Fig. 13 . The APBs generally arise 
from the shearing of dislocations to ordered γ’ precipitates and play an 
important role in the interaction between dislocations and γ’ pre -
cipitates, which can significantly inhibit dislocations cutting into or -
dered phases [ 18 , 27 , 42 ]. Alloying elements affect the moving 
dislocation resistance by changing the γ’ precipitates volume fraction 
and anti-phase boundary energy (APBE). It has been determined that the 
γ’ precipitates in 2Cr and 2Mo are all Ni
3

5. Conclusion 
In summary, high temperature tensile behavior of two γ’ -strength -
ening Co-Ni-Al-based superalloys has been investigated. The high- 
temperature fracture mechanism and the underlying defect structures 
of YSA were investigated in detail. The following conclusions are drawn: 
1. As the temperature increased from 773 K to 1073 K, the tensile 
strengths and elongations of 2Cr and 2Mo superalloys decrease, and 
the fracture mechanism changes from dimple fracture to brittle 
fracture. At 773 K, the formation of small dimples is due to the un -
even deformations of γ matrix and γ’ precipitates. The intergranular 
fracture is caused by both the decrease of grain / twins boundaries 
strengthening and the decohesion of slip plane. 
2. 2Cr and 2Mo superalloys present a peak yield strength at 923 K and 
exhibit YSA effect. At 773 K ~ 873 K, YSA is derived from the syn -
ergistic effects of dislocation locking strengthening, DSA strength -
ening and γ’ -strengthening. On the one hand, dislocation cross-slip 
gets more seriously, which hinders dislocations migration and in -
creases the yield strength and tensile strength. On the other hand, 
dislocations ’ unpinning to the moving solutes decreases deformation 
resistance and increases elongation of superalloys. At 873 K ~ 923 K, 
the yield strength increased with increasing dislocation locks ’ den -
sities while the ductility decreaseed due to ITB effect. 
3. The tensile strength and yield strength of 2Mo superalloy is slightly 
higher than that of 2Cr superalloy, while the ductility is weaker. A 
temperature-dependent model is employed to evaluate the contri -
bution of γ’ precipitates strengthening to yield strength. The yield 
strength contribution of the superalloy is positively correlated with 
the volume fraction of γ’ precipitates and negatively correlated with 
temperature.
