Abstract-A mechanism for “rafting” in Ni-base superalloys under high temperature creep conditions is 
discussed in terms of elastic strain energy. The elastic deformation is analysed by the finite element method 
and approximate analytical expressions are also deduced for it. The difference between the elastic strain

1. INTRODUCTION 
Microstructural stability of blade materials used in 
land based gas turbine systems has become a critical 
issue for the reliability and economy of entire power 
generation systems since the materials must be sound 
during increasingly longer intervals between inspec- 
tions. Ni-base superalloys used in gas turbine blades 
consist of cuboidal precipitates of y’ phase with an 
ordered structure and a matrix of y phase with a 
disordered structure. High resistance to plastic 
deformation of the y’ phase at elevated temperature 
and softer characteristics of the y phase leads the y-y’ 
two phase systems to have high creep strength and 
ductility; however, the microstructure coarsens 
directionally at higher temperatures, typically above 
1100 K, to form rafts [l-4] which are usually 
perpendicular to the tensile direction. 
Though the effects of rafting on the movement of 
dislocations in the y-y’ system and on the creep 
rupture phenomenon are not yet clear, some 
experimental studies show that a rafted specimen has 
a shorter creep rupture lifetime [3, 41, while electron 
microscopy observations depict fractured surfaces 
lying along the rafting direction [5]. Additionally, 
rafted y’ phase is observed to be easily sheared by 
dislocations compared to the cuboidal precipitates of 
y’ phase [3, 61. Hence, understanding the rafting 
phenomenon is not only of scientific interest, but also 
an important technological problem to be solved for 
reliable and economical scheduling of the operation 
of gas turbine plants. 
Physical parameters which control the rafting 
phenomenon include misfits of the lattice parameter 
and elastic constants between y and y’ phases, which 
give rise to non-uniform stress distribution in the 
structure. Pollock and Argon [7-91 made numerical 
stress analyses of the y-y’ two phase materials by a 
finite element method and they showed a stress 
concentration at the corners of the y’ phase. A similar 
result for the stress distribution was also obtained by 
Miiller et al. [IO]. On the other hand. the solution for 
an ellipsoidal inclusion given by Eshelby was applied 
to a y-y’ system by Pineau [ll], Koyama et al. [12] 
and Chang and Allen [ 131 where the stable shape of 
the precipitate was discussed in terms of the total 
elastic strain energy. Gayda and Srolovitz [14] used 
a hybrid Monte Carlo finite element meth’od to 
simulate the rafting process which was driven by 
elastic strain energy. 
More recently, Arrell and Valles [15] and Buffiere 
and Ignat [16] discussed the effect of dislocations 
which accumulate on y-y’ interfaces and generate an 
anisotropic internal stress distribution [17, 181 in y 
channels parallel and perpendicular to the stress axis. 
Also, mechanisms of rafting are discussed in terms of 
matter flow; Pollock and Argon [9] and Svetlov et al. 
[19] discussed the possible diffusion of component 
elements of y and y’ phases driven by local elastic 
stress. Effect of creep dislocations on the matter flow 
was also discussed in terms of non-uniform stress 
relaxation which induces local dissolution of alloying 
elements [20] or in terms of a size effect where 
dislocations interact with matrix atoms and a 
chemical gradient is formed between the y channels 
parallel and perpendicular to the loading axis [ 161. 
However, clearer descriptions are needed of the 
elastic stresses and strain energy, the relationship 
between non-uniform elastic stress and plastic

3.2. A rufting mechanism and elastic strain energies in 
the y-y’ system 
As is well known, the equilibrium concentration of 
chemical elements in alloys is determined by points of 
common tangency to the free energy-concentration 
curves of each phase and the equilibrium concen- 
tration is modified by the coherency strain [28]. We 
consider the local equilibrium concentrations of Al in 
the horizontal and vertical channels of y when the free 
energies in these channels are not the same. In the 
case illustrated in Fig. 6(a), the equilibrium 
concentration in the vertical channel of y is C, and 
that in y’ adjacent to the vertical channel is C,; while 
in the horizontal channel of y, the equilibrium 
concentration is C, and that in y’ adjacent to the 
horizontal channel is Cq. This difference in the local 
equilibrium concentrations results in a concentration 
gradient in y and y’ which drives diffusion of Al and 
makes the y’ phase grow in an anisotropic manner 
[Fig. 6(b)]. Among atomic elements of the Ni-base 
superalloys, elements that mainly form 7’ phase, for 
example Ni or Ta, should diffuse in a similar way to 
Al, while diffusion of atomic elements that mainly 
form y phase, for example Cr or MO. should occur in 
the opposite direction. The driving force for the atom 
flow in this mechanism is analogous to that of 
Ostwald ripening; however. atom flow takes place 
between the horizontal and vertical channels of *J. 
Atom flow in the 7’ phase is also possible between the 
domains adjacent to the horizontal and vertical 
channels of y, even though the flux in “J’ should be 
smaller compared to that in y since the diffusion 
coefficient for y’ is much smaller [29] than that of y.

4. SUMMARY 
We have discussed the elastic deformation in 
microstructures of Ni-base superalloys based on a 
finite element analysis and we derived approximate 
analytical expressions for the elastic stress distri- 
bution. A rafting mechanism in the microstructures 
was proposed and the rate of rafting was estimated 
based on thermodynamic considerations. The results 
are summarized as follows. 
(1) An anisotropy was introduced in the distri- 
bution of the elastic strain energy by mismatches of 
lattice constants and elastic constants of the y and y’ 
phases. The intensity of the anisotropy was a function 
of the lattice constants and elastic constants of y and 
y’ and the volume fraction of y’. 
(2) The non-uniform distribution of the free 
energy in the microstructure caused a local shift in the 
equilibrium concentration of the chemical elements in 
the alloys. Evaluation of the elastic strain energy 
depicted an energy gap between the horizontal and 
vertical channels of y which was about 0.5 MJ/m3. 
The shift in the local equilibrium concentration of Al 
corresponding to this energy gap was evaluated from 
thermodynamic theory to be the order of lo-“. 
(3) The rate of rafting, which was estimated by a 
diffusion mechanism with the local shift in the 
equilibrium concentration of Al, agreed well with the 
observed value.
