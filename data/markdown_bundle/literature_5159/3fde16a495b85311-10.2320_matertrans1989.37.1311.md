Materials Transactions, JIM, Vol. 37, No. 6 (1996), pp. 1311 to 1318 Refinement of Microstructures and Improvement of Mechanical Properties in Intermetallic TiAl Alloys by Rheocasting Kiyoshi Ichikawa and Yoshiji Kinoshita Mechanical Engineering Laboratory, Agency of Industrial Science and Technology, MITI, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305, Japan An investigation was made on the refinement of microstructures and improvement of mechanical properties in inter- metallic TiAl binary alloys containing 44, 49 and 54 at% aluminum produced by the rheocasting in which the solidifying alloy was vigorously agitated at high rotation speeds of 15 to 70 rev / s Of a stirrer immersed in the alloys in argon gas at- mosphere. In the microstructure of the rheocast Ti-44at%AI alloy such a lamellar structure as seen in the alloy solidified without stirring was destroyed completely, and an extremely refined microstructure composed of dispersed agglomera- tion aggregates was formed. The mean sizes of agglomeration aggregates and individual crystal grains of the Ti-44%Al alloy rheocast at a rotation speed of 70 s -l were 6.4��3.7 gm and 2.2��1.3 gm in major axis and 2.0��1.1 gm and 1.0��0.6 urn in minor axis, respectively. However, oval lamellar grains were seen in the microstructure of the rheocast Ti-49at%Al alloy and lamellar substructure was observed in the oval primary crystal grains in the microstructure of the rheocast Ti-54at%AI alloy. In the microanalysis of the Ti-44%Al alloy rheocast at 70 s -l the uniform distribution of Al and Ti elements could be achieved by destruction of the lamellar structure with the mechanical stirring, although lamel- lar segregation was observed in the alloy solidified without stirring. The room temperature elongations of the Ti-44%Al alloy rheocast at 15 and 70 s -l were 2.7% and 3.3%, respectively, being higher than the elongation of the alloy solidified without stirring, i.e. 2.0%. The room temperature tensile strengths of Ti-44%AI alloy rheocast at 15 and 70 s -l were 359 and 468 MPa, respectively. They were also higher than the tensile strength of the alloy solidified without stirring, i.e. 245 MPa. The elevated temperature elongation and tensile strength of the rheocast Ti-44%Al alloys were improved as compared with those of the alloy solidified without stirring, although a fluctuation existed. The tensile strengths of the alloys rheocast at 70 and solidified without stirring were 538 and 363 MPa at 1173 K, and 439 and 389 MPa at 1273 K, respectively. Introduction The semi-solid processing in which the homogeniza- tion of microstructures in the whole parts of an alloy in- got can be achieved by the rotational stirring during the solidification of an alloy, has been well-known to be a promising process for the next generation's continuous casting( The initial work on the processing of semi-solid alloys included the rheocasting process proposed by Flemings(2) and the casting process to scrape cyclically the semi- solid zone at the solidification front by Hagiwara and Takahashi(3). The main theme of their works was the refinement of crystal grains and the elimination of segre- gation of an alloy ingot. They succeeded in the control of macrostructures and the decrease in macrosegregation at the whole parts of the ingot, although irregular crystal grains such as rosette-type primary crystals were seen in the microstructures because of insuffcient agitation dur- ing the solidification The authors tried a further homogeneous refinement of crystal and produced highly ductile materi- by increasing both the rotation speed of a stirrer and the cooling rate of the semi-solid alloy in the rheocasting process. The research on structural intermetallic compounds has recently become one of the most important subjects, so that a National R&D Program named "High-Perfor- mance Materials for Severe Environments" has been performed in Japan on the development of structural intermetallic compounds such as titanium aluminide and niobium aluminide by improving the ductility at room (14) temperature and the strength at elevated temperature In the present work, the homogeneous refinement of microstructures and the improvement of elongation at room temperature and tensile strength at high tempera- ture in the intermetallic TiAl alloys were carried out by a modified rheocasting at high rotation speeds, and remark- able grain refinement and superior mechanical properties of the Ti-44 at%Al alloy were achieved. The heat insulator at the top of the isothermal fur- nace has a hole pass through the output shaft of the mo- tor in the center thereof. To the lower end of the output shaft in the isothermal furnace is attached a stirrer that is thus rotated by the motor (Fig. 2). The lower melting room contains a high-frequency fur- nace to heat the intermetallic alloy in two concentric crucibles whose side is covered with a heat insulator. A high-frequency coil is wound around the insulator. The crucible consists of an inner crucible of calcia and an outer crucible of graphite. A crucible cover that can be opened and closed from the outside of the chamber is mounted on the high- frequency furnace. Under the high-frequency furnace an elevating mechanism that moves the crucible up and down is provided. The elevating mechanism selectively moves the crucible between the high-frequency furnace and the isothermal furnace. With the crucible cover kept in the opened position, the elevating mechanism moves the crucible up into the isothermal furnace. The experimental procedure is as follows. To prepare an intermetallic TiAl alloy, a mixture of granular sponge titanium of not lower than 99.5% purity and small pieces of aluminum of 99.99% purity weighing 380 g in total was inserted in the inner crucible. After placing the crucible containing the mixed raw materials in the high-frequency furnace, the vacuum chamber was evacuated to 1 x 10-3 MPa or below. Then, argon gas was introduced therein until the argon gas pres- sure in the chamber reached the desired level of 0.1 MPa. The raw materials were melted in a short time in the argon gas atmosphere by high-frequency heating. At this point, the crucible cover above the high-fre- quency furnace was opened to confirm that the charge had already been melted. Then, the elevating mechanism was actuated to raise the crucible containing the molten charge into the isothermal furnace where the stirrer was put into the molten charge. The ascent of the crucible

The molten charge was continuous- ly cooled under the condition of no heating of the isother- mal furnace to achieve the maximum cooling rate of the charge. Then, the stirrer began to rotate immediately after the insertion of it into the molten alloy, continued to rotate for the maximum period of 30 s, and was removed from the semi-solid alloy. The rotating speed of the stirrer was a relatively low speed of 15 s -l until the start of solidifica- tion, and was increased not rapidly but at a steady rate to prevent the spatter of the solidifying charge to the out- side of the crucible. By continuing agitation at a constant high speed, the primary crystals formed in the molten charge were broken to realize a homogeneous fine- grained microstructure. The constant rotation speed of the stirrer during solidification is varied from 15 to 70 s-l. After continuing the constant-speed stirring for a given period of time, the elevating mechanism was actuat- ed to lower the crucible into the high-frequency furnace at a point between the fraction of solid of 0.5 and the completion of solidification, thereby preventing the depo- sition of the semi-solid charge on the stirrer. The charge in the high-frequency furnace was then again subjected to high-frequency heating at 1273 K for 600 s. Reheating in the high-frequency furnace released the high strain energy built up in the charge by the forced stirring, which, in turn, prevented the occurrence of cracks in the cooling process of the rheocast intermetallic TiAl alloy. Microscopic observation was then made of the top, middle and bottom sections cut transversely from the alloy ingots rheocast and solidified without stirring. Ten- sile test pieces were also machined from the ingots as illustrated in Fig. 3. The tensile test was carried out in air at room temperature and in an argon gas atmosphere at elevated temperatures, room temperature, 1073, 1173 and 1273 K with a strain rate of 1.1 x 10-3 s -l. The apparatus was equipped with a 2 kW motor hav- ing a capacity to rotate the stirrer at a speed of up to 84 s-l. If the motor is placed outside vacuum chamber, the output shaft of the motor must be passed inside through the wall of the vacuum chamber to rotate the stirrer in- serted in the molten alloy at a high speed. With this construction, the material to seal hole through which the output shaft is passed must be replaced frequently as it is subjected to a heavy load. The construction solves this problem by placing the motor inside the vacuum cham- ber. The inner crucible had an inside diameter of 60 mm, an outside diameter of 83 mm and a depth of 130 mm (Fig. 2). The stirrer made of 99.7% purity titanium had a down-wardly tapering octagonal cross-section, an upper base with a major axis of 38 mm and a minor axis of 30 mm, a lower base with a major axis of 32 mm and a minor axis of 25 mm, and a length of 120 mm (Fig. 2). A new 99.7%Ti stirrer was used one by one in all the rheocasting experiments. Conclusions In order to produce structural titanium aluminides with high ductility at room temperature and high strength at elevated temperature, an investigation was made on the homogeneous refinement of microstructures formed with fragmentation of lamellar structure by rota- tional stirring during the solidification of intermetallic TiAl binary alloys. The tensile testing at room and elevat- ed temperatures was made on the intermetallic TiAl alloys rheocast and solidified without stirring. (1) In the microstructure of the rheocast Ti��44 at% Al alloy such a lamellar structure as seen in the alloy solidified without stirring was destroyed completely, and an extremely refined microstructure composed of dispersed agglomeration aggregates was formed. The average size of agglomeration aggregates and individual crystal grains of the Ti��44%Al alloy rheocast at 15 s -l were 6.6��3.3 gm and 1.6��1.4 gm in the major axis and 2.3��1.0 gm and 0.7��0.5 gm in the minor axis, respec- tively. The average size of agglomeration aggregates and individual crystal grains of the Ti��44%Al alloy rheocast at 70 s -l were 6.4��3.7 gm and 2.2��1.3 gm in the major axis and 2.0��1.1 gm and 1.0��0.6 gm in the minor axis, respectively. However, oval lamellar grains were seen in the microstructure of the rheocast Ti-49 at%Al alloy and a lamellar substructure was observed in the oval primary crystal grains in the microstructure of the rheocast Ti-54 at%Al alloy. (2) In the microanalysis of the Ti��44 at%Al alloy rheocast at a rotation speed of 70 s -l the uniform distri- bution of Al and Ti elements could be achieved by refine- ment with fragmentation of the lamellar structure with rotational stirring, although the lamellar segregation was observed in the alloy solidified without stirring. (3) The room temperature elongations of the Ti-44 at%Al alloy rheocast at rotation speeds of 15 and 70 s -1 were 2.7 and 3.3%, respectively. They were higher than the elongation of the alloy solidified without stirring, i.e. 2.0%. The room temperature ultimate tensile strengths of the Ti-44%Al alloy rheocast at rotation speeds of 15 and 70 s -l were 359 and 468 MPa, respectively. They were also higher than the tensile strength of the alloy solidified without stirring, i.e. 245 MPa. (4) The elevated temperature elongation and ultimate tensile strength of the rheocast Ti-44 at%Al alloys were improved as compared with those of the alloy solidified without stirring, although a fluctuation existed. Especial- ly, the elongation of the alloy rheocast at a rotation speed of 15 s -l was 20.7% at 1273 K. The tensile strengths of the Ti-44%Al alloys rheocast at a rotation speed of 70 s -l and solidified without stirring were 538 and 363 MPa at 1173 K, and 439 and 389 MPa at 1273 K, respectively.
