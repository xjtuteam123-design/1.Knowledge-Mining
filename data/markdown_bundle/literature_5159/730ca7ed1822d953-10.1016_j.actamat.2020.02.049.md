Abstract 
The present paper reports the development of W -, Mo-, and V -free and Ni - and 
Cr-containing Co-Al-Ta-based superalloys. In particular, the presence of a metastable 
L12-γ′ phase is experimentally verified in Co-12Al-2Ta alloy. The results show that the 
γ′ phase stability is improved and the γ/γ′ composition range is broadened by rising the 
Ni content in alloys under study . Moreover, elemental partitioning proves that Cr was a 
γ-phase former , which addition reduces the γ/γ′ lattice misfit and the γ′ precipitate 
coarsening rate. Moreover, the developed Cr-containing alloys exhibit excellent γ′ phase 
stability even at high temperatures of 800-1100 °C. In particular , 
Co-30Ni-12Al-4Ta-12Cr (12Cr) allo y possesses a good combination of high solvus 
temperature (~1152 °C), low density (~8.38 g/cm 3), low γ/γ′ lattice misfit (~+0.33%), 
and low coarsening rate of γ′ precipitate (~1.95 × 10-27 m/s at 900 °C). Besides, nominal

temperatures are no worse than those of other available Cr-containing and W -free 
cobalt-based superalloys.

1. Introduction 
Superalloys (including iron -, cobalt -, and nickel -based alloys) are unique 
materials for applications at elevated temperatures [1]. Among them, the γ/γ′ 
nickel-based alloys with an fcc-γ matrix hardened by the L12-γ′ Ni3Al phase exhibit the 
best high-temperature/strength combination, which imply their growing application in 
critical components of gas turbine engines [1, 2]. The alternative superalloy candidates 
for applications with even higher operating temperature are conventional 
carbide-hardened cobalt -based alloys, which have higher solidus temperatures than 
nickel-based ones. However, a lack of an effective precipitate -hardened mechanism in 
cobalt-based alloys of this class reduced their strength and application prospects [2]. 
This deficiency seemed to be migrated after the emergence of γ/γ′ Co-Al-W-based 
superalloys [3-5], which exhibited very high γ′ volume fractions [6-9] and very 
attractive mechanical properties [10-14]. However, a number of drawbacks still restricts 
their wide applications : (i) a narrow γ/γ′ composition range [4, 7], (ii) the metastable 
nature of the γ′ phase [15-17], (iii) a low solvus temperature (~990 °C) [ 4, 18], and (iv) 
a high mass density (>9.0 g/cm3) [19-21].

Over the past decade, considerable attention has been given to explore alternative 
γ/γ′ alloys in Co-W-(Ge/Ga) [ 22, 23 ], Co -Al-Mo-(Ta/Nb) [ 20, 24 -28], Co -V-(Ta/Nb) 
[29, 30 ], and Co -Ti-(Mo/V/Re/Cr/W) [ 31-35] alloy systems. H owever, either their 
solvus temperatures or incipient melting temperatures were found to be lower than those 
of multicomponent Co-Al-W alloys . More r ecently, novel γ/γ′ Co -Al-V-based 
superalloys with a high γ′ phase stability and a low density were developed by the 
research group including co -authors of this study [36], but their hot-corrosion/oxidation 
resistance may be deteriorated due to the elevated V-content [19, 37]. 
Noteworthy is that the presence of L12-γ′ phase detected in the Co -Al-(W/ 
Mo-Nb/Mo-Ta/V) systems strongly indicates that the γ′-Co3Al phase (which is 
metastable, according to ref [ 38]) can be stabilized by alloying with some of the 
transition metals from VB or VIB group of the periodic table [4, 20, 26, 36]. This 
implies a possibility of developing γ/γ′ alloys in other Co -Al-X systems. Moreover, Ta 
is generally regarded as a strong γ ′ phase forming element in the novel cobalt-based 
alloys [4, 11, 1 8, 20, 36, 39]. These findings proves the expediency of exploring γ/γ′ 
alloys in the Co-Al-Ta system. Although the metastable γ′ phase in Co-Al-Ta alloys was 
first discovered by Lee as early as in 1971 [3], the above reasoning prompts one to 
revisit this issue and perform a more in-depth analysis of this alloy system. 
Therefore, an attempt is made in this study to explore W -, Mo -, and V -free 
Co-Al-Ta-based superalloys with a stable γ′ phase. Additionally, investigations of phase 
equilibria and the effect of alloying additions on the studied alloys are carried out . A

primary consideration is given to Ni and Cr alloying elements, according to the

2. Experimental procedures 
2.1 Sample preparation 
All compositions in the present paper are given in at.%. Small b utton ingots (~30 
g) were arc-melted (under a high -purity Ar atmosphere) using raw materials of Co, Al, 
Ta, Ni, and Cr (a purity of at least 99.9 wt.%). Additionally, samples for mechanical 
property tests were cut from the large arc-melted ingots (~150 g). 
Evacuated silica tubes were used to contain samples for high -temperature heat 
treatment. After heat treatment, samples were quenched into ice water. A solution 
treatment at 1300 °C for 24 h was performed for all samples before annealing at lower 
temperatures. Noteworthy is that the γ′ precipitates are generally very fine when a

short-term aging treatment is performed. Thus, before the equilibrating treatment, some 
γ′-containing samples were cold-rolled, and a 50% reduction rate was used, so that the 
coarsened γ′ precipitates can be obtained. This pre-treatment method was also applied to 
other novel cobalt -based alloys for shortening the equilibrium-annealing time [18, 22, 
23, 32, 36, 40, 42, 43].

The mechanically polished samples were put into an etching solution of HNO3 (75

3.2 Development of Ni-containing alloys 
3.2.1 Effect of Ni content on the γ′ phase stability 
For the recently developed γ/γ′ cobalt-based superalloys, the γ′ phase stability 
appears to b e significantly enhanced by high contents of Ni (usually ~30 at.%) . On the 
one hand, this practice is generally adopted to stabilize the fcc crystal structure of the 
γ-Co matrix [41]. On the other hand, Ni is expected to stabilize the γ′ phase in novel 
cobalt-based alloys [ 20, 2 6, 2 7, 3 6, 40]. Thus, in this section, the Co -xNi-12Al-2Ta 
alloys (x = 0, 10, 20, and 30 , referred to as 0Ni, 10Ni, 20Ni, and 30Ni, respectively ) 
were prepared to explore the effect of Ni content on the γ′ phase stability . Figure 2 
(a)-(d) depicts the microstructure of Co-xNi-12Al-2Ta alloys heat-treated at 800 °C for 
48 h, and the corresponding XRD patterns for Ni -containing alloys are given in Figure 
S3(a). In contrast to the γ+γ′+CoAl microstructure previously obtained in the as -aged 
base alloy (see Figure 1(b)), the γ/γ′ one is readily observed in the Ni -containing alloys. 
This can be attributed to the fact that the phase stability of γ′ precipitate is enhanced in 
the Ni-containing alloys , which issue will be discussed in Section 3 .2.2. The typical

3.2.3 Effect of Ta content in Co-30Ni-12Al-xTa alloys

section, the Co -30Ni-12Al-xTa alloys (x = 2, 4, 6 , and 8, referred to as 2Ta, 4Ta, 6Ta,

3.3 Effect of Cr content 
Chromium is present in almost all superalloys and is generally regarded as an 
indispensable alloying element to increase the oxidation /hot-corrosion resistance. 
However, over the years of superalloy evolution, the Cr content in superalloys has been 
reduced incrementally in advanced nickel-based superalloys [57], insofar as a higher Cr 
content in the alloy results in an increas ing tendency of forming topologically 
close-packed (TCP) phases [58]. Moreover, the Cr addition weakens the γ′ phase 
stability in the novel cobalt -based alloys [18, 1 9, 2 4, 2 5, 3 6]. Thus, we prepared 
Co-30Ni-12Al-4Ta-xCr alloys (x = 0, 4, 8, and 12, referred as 0Cr, 4Cr, 8Cr, and 12Cr, 
respectively). The Co -30Ni-12Al-4Ta alloy was chosen as the base alloy , due to the 
equilibrium of solvus temperature (~1161 °C) and its mass density of ~8.64 g/cm3.

the lattice misfit and allows one to adjust the latter value in novel Cobalt -based alloys 
by adding the respective content of the above alloying elements.

Firstly, using the double logarithmic log(r) vs. log(t) coordinates, the plot slop (1/n) 
was derived, as shown in Figure 7(c). For the studied alloys, 1/n ≈ 0.33, which implies 
that the LSW model can be applied to describe the γ′ coarsening mechanism. Next, t he 
dependences between [r(t)] 3 vs. t at 800 and 900 °C were plotted, as shown in Figure

k were calculated and summarized in Table 4. Noteworthy is a sharply decreasing tren d 
of k versus Cr content, which can be attributed to the following two factors: (i) the 
slow-diffusivity Cr partitioning to the γ-matrix, which hampers the interdiffusion 
process [71], and (ii) a decrease in the γ/γ′ coherency strain energy induced by the γ/γ′ 
lattice misfit reduction. A similar situation was observed in Re -containing Co-Ti- and 
Co-Al-Mo-Nb-based alloys, where the addition of a slow -diffusivity Re resulted in a 
reduced k value. It is noteworthy that the studied alloys exhibit a low k value when 
compared to the Ni-19.3 at.% Al ( ~1.3 × 10 -27 m/s at 800 °C) [72] and Co-10Al-10W 
alloys (0.07 × 10 -27 m/s at 800 °C). Moreover, the k value of the 12Cr alloy (~1.95 × 
10-27 m/s at 900 °C) is lower than that of the nickel-based CMSX-4 superalloy (6.86 × 
10-27 m/s) [73].

3.3.5 Solvus temperature and density comparison 
Figure 9(a) presents the comparative data on solvus temperatures and densities of 
12Cr and other novel cobalt-based superalloys. Overall, a very good combination of 
solvus-temperature/density is achieved in the present 12Cr alloy. T he solvus

(1000 °C) and Co-9Al-10W-2Ta (1098 °C) [ 10] alloys, and also exceeds those of 
Cr-containing Co-Ta-V-based (~965 °C) [30], Co-Al-Mo-Ta-based (1038-1105 °C) [24, 
25], and Co-11Ti-15Cr (1100 °C) alloys [34]. 
Meanwhile, the studied 12Cr alloy exhibits very low mass density (~8.38 g/cm3), 
as compared to those of W -containing Co-Al-W-based [ 20, 74] and commercially

9.54-10.1 and 9.2 g/cm3, respectively. Meanwhile, its density is nearly the same as those 
of available W-free γ/γ′-strengthened cobalt-based alloys (about 8.2-8.5 g/cm3) [24, 25, 
30, 34].

3.3.6 Mechanical properties 
Figure 9(b) depicts the compression yield strength vs. temperature plots of the 
12Cr alloy and other cobalt -based superalloys [10, 24, 34, 56 ]. For the studied 12Cr 
alloy, t he yield strength value drops from ~690 MPa at ambient temperature to ~640 
MPa at 600 °C, and then rises to ~770 MPa at 700 °C, followed by a rapid drop to ~460 
MPa at 900 °C. At temperatures of about 600-700 °C, the yield strength anomaly can be 
clearly ob served in the 12Cr alloy. A similar anomaly was also reported for the 
γ′-strengthened Ni-Al- [75], Co-Ti- [32, 34], and Co -Al-W-based superalloys [10, 11], 
which was attributed to the locking of dislocations at high temperatures via the 
Kear-Wilsdorf mecha nism [76-78]. One may assume that this mechanism is also 
suitable for the studied alloys but further detailed investigations are required, insofar as 
the Co-Al-Ta-based alloys are a new family of γ/γ′ superalloys. As seen in Figure 9(b) 
and (c), it is wort h noticing that the 12Cr alloy exhibits good mechanical properties at 
high temperatures as compared to other recently developed W -free/Cr-containing γ/γ′ 
cobalt-based superalloys. 
4. Summary 
The present study reports the alloy development of the γ/γ′ Co -Al-Ta-based 
superalloys, which are W-, Mo -, and V -free. The results obtained make it possible to 
draw the following conclusions. 
 The metastable γ′-Co3(Al, Ta) phase presence in the Co -12Al-2Ta ( at.%) alloy 
under study was experimentally verified.

## Page 21

20 
 The γ′ phase stability and γ/γ′ composition range were raised by increasing the Ni 
content in Co-Ni-Al-Ta quaternary alloys under study. 
 An increase in Ta content in Co-30Ni-12Al-xTa (x = 2, 4, 6, and 8) alloys 
significantly raised their solvus temperature and mass den sity, whereas in 
Co-30Ni-12Al-4Ta alloy, it provided a mass density of ~8.64 g/cm3 and ensured the 
balance of γ′ solvus temperature (~1161 °C). 
 The Co -30Ni-12Al-4Ta-xCr (x = 0, 4, 8, and 12) alloys were prepared, which 
solvus temperature values changed slightly and densities with the Cr content. 
 Further investigations proved that Cr was a strong γ former, which reduced the γ/γ′ 
lattice misfit and lowers down the γ′ coarsening rate. Moreover, the γ′ phase in the 
Cr-containing alloys was still very stable at high temperatures of 800-1100 °C. 
 The developed Co-30Ni-12Al-4Ta-12Cr alloy exhibits a very good combination of 
high solvus temperature (~1152 °C), low density (~8.38 g/cm 3), low γ/γ′ lattice 
misfit (~+0.33%), and low coarsening rate of γ′ precipitate (~1.95 × 10 -27 m/s at 
900 °C ). At high temperatures, t he 12Cr alloy also has excellent nominal and 
specific yield strength values, as compared to other Cr-containing/W-free 
cobalt-based superalloys.
