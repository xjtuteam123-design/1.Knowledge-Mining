Abstract
The present study focuses on investigating the microstructural evolutions and mechanical properties of energetic 
 Al1(TiZrNbTaMoCr)15 refractory high-entropy alloys with the different heat treatments at low Al content state. It is found 
that even with a reduction in the Al content, the strength of these alloys remains unaffected at room temperature and high 
temperature, while the plasticity improves significantly. In particular, the coherent BCC/B2 microstructure with needle-like 
B2 nanoprecipitates dispersed into the BCC matrix is formed in 873 K-aged Al1Ti6Zr2Nb3Ta3Mo0.5Cr0.5 alloy. Therefore, this 
alloy exhibits the highest compression yield strength (σYS = 1333 and 717 MPa) at room temperature and 1073 K, respectively. 
After 973 K-aged, the coherent BCC/B2 microstructure underwent destabilization, and the B2 phase transforms into the brittle 
 Zr5Al3 phase which then coarsens and dominates the microstructure of S3-AlTa 3 alloy after 1073 K-aged. Moreover, these 
current alloys exhibit exceptionally high theoretical exothermic enthalpy (ΔH ), surpassing 11606 J·g −1, which highlights 
their significant potential as innovative high-performance energetic structural materials.
Keywords Refractory high-entropy alloy · Coherent microstructure · Mechanical property · Exothermic enthalpy
1 Introduction
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) based on the body-
center-cubic (BCC) structure have attracted more attention 
for the great potential in the aerospace applications, and 
other fields, owing to their exceptional mechanical properties 
at room temperature (RT) and high temperature (HT) [1–6]. 
The typical WMoNbTa RHEA with a single BCC structure 
exhibits an outstanding mechanical property with a yield 
strength (σYS) of 405 MPa at 1873 K. However, such kind of 
RHEAs consisting of heavy elements (W, Nb, Ta, etc.) often 
has a relatively higher density (ρ > 10 g·cm−3), which limits 
their potential application on a large extent [7 , 8]. Subse -
quently, Al, Ti, Zr, Cr, V, and Si are introduced to substitute 
for partial heavy elements to form light-weight RHEAs with 
a density of less than 8 g·cm −3 [5, 6], in which the second 
phases, such as hexagonal Laves phase and Zr5Al3 com-
pound, could be precipitated into the BCC matrix [9 –14]. 
Although the alloy strength is further enhanced, the plastic-
ity will be deteriorated seriously due to the precipitation of 
coarse particles, which is ascribed to the semi-coherent or 
non-coherent relationship between the second phases and 
BCC matrix [5 ]. In particular, these alloys with the coher -
ent BCC/B2 microstructure exhibit extremely high strength, 
similar to Ni-based superalloys [15– 23]. For instance, the 
 AlMo0.5NbTa0.5TiZr RHEA possesses significantly elevated 
mechanical properties, with a compressive yield strength 
of σYS = 2193 MPa at 298 K and σ YS = 745 MPa at 1273 K 
[15]. It is emphasized that the presence of the B2 phase pos-
sesses a challenge since B2 is a metastable phase that can 
easily destabilize at high temperatures and transform into a 
brittle Zr5Al3 phase [24]. So, the incorporation of multiple 
components through co-alloying becomes necessary to tailor 
the alloy' s composition and achieve the desirable coherent 
BCC/B2 microstructure.

In our previous work, we have successfully designed a 
series of Al–Ni–Co–Fe–Cr high entropy alloys with coher -
ent BCC/B2 microstructure using the cluster formula of 
 Al2M14, in which M represents different combinations of 
transition metals [25– 29]. It is found that the morpholo-
gies of coherent BCC/B2 microstructure are dependent on 
the lattice misfit where a moderate ε value (0.2%–1.0%) 
contributes to the formation of cuboidal nanoparticles 
base on the Al2M14 [25–31]. However, adjusting the lat-
tice misfit (ε) to the appropriate value is challenging given 
the significant composition difference between the BCC 
and B2 phases. Consequently, BCC/B2-based alloys eas-
ily tend to form a weave-like microstructure of spinodal 
decomposition due to the large lattice misfit ( ε > 0.63%) 
between the BCC and B2 phases, resulting in the brittle-
ness of the alloy [27, 32–34]. We apply this Al2M14 for-
mula to develop lightweight Al–Ti–Zr–Nb–Ta RHEAs, by 
adjusting the combination of M . Luckily, we have obtained 
coherent BCC/B2 microstructure with a cuboidal morphol-
ogy in the Al2Ti5Zr4Nb2.5Ta2.5 and Al2Ti6Zr2Nb3Ta3 alloys 
with the lattice misfit ε (0.2%–1.0%). These alloys exhibit 
remarkable mechanical properties at room temperature, 
with a yield strength (σYS) exceeding 1200 MPa [35, 36]. In 
particular, the Al2Ti6Zr2Nb3Ta3 alloy maintains the coher -
ent BCC/B2 microstructure even after aging at 873 K for 
200 h, exhibiting exceptional thermal stability [35, 36]. 
Moreover, these elements of Al, Ti, Zr, Nb, and Ta have 
high exothermic enthalpy value (ΔH ) indicating that the 
series Al–Ti–Zr–Nb–Ta alloys with the high theoretical 
exothermic enthalpy value hold significant promise to as 
a novel high-strength energetic structure materials [36]. In 
order to improve the oxidation resistance of existing RHEAs, 
the Al2Ti6Zr2Nb3Ta2Mo0.5Cr0.5 with the addition of oxida-
tion-resistant elements of Cr and Mo also obtains the stable 
coherent BCC/B2 microstructure with cuboidal nanoparti-
cles precipitated and shows excellent mechanical properties 
with σYS = 1314 MPa at RT [37].
In fact, the addition of Al with high content would 
bring about the dramatic increase of brittleness RHEAs 
due to the precipitation of B2 phase with high volume 
fraction, which the microstructure with BCC nanopre-
cipitates embedded into the B2 matrix [15– 20, 37]. For -
tunately, phase version phenomenon has been found in 
 Al0.5 Ti1.5 ZrNbTa 0.8 V0.2 and Al20Ti28Zr40Nb12 RHEAs 
through isothermal annealing at 873 K for 120 h and 
923 K for 0.5 h, which the microstructure with spheroi-
dal B2 nanoprecipitates into the BCC matrix [38– 40]. It 
improves the ductility of these alloys at RT obviously, as 
evidenced by the fracture strain from the δ = 2%–4.7% 
to the δ = 5%–30% after annealing, and the strength of 
these alloys keeps at a high level of σ YS > 1500 MPa at RT 
[39, 40]. Therefore, to improve the plasticity by reduc-
ing the volume of B2 phases, the present work designs 
a new series of Al–Ti–Zr–Nb–Ta–Mo–Cr RHEAs with 
low content of Al element using the optimized cluster 
formula Al1M15 based on Al2Ti6Zr2Nb3Ta2Mo0.5 Cr0.5 , 
as a reference alloy (Ref-Al 2). Meanwhile, we add 
the Ti, Nb, and Ta substituting for Al, respectively, to 
avoid the BCC phase separation and the precipitation of 
complex compounds. Consequently, these alloys com-
positions are obtained as Al1Ti7Zr2Nb3Ta2Mo0.5 Cr0.5 
(S1–AlTi 7), Al1Ti6Zr2Nb4Ta2Mo0.5 Cr0.5 (S2–AlNb 4), 
and Al1Ti6Zr2Nb3Ta3Mo0.5Cr0.5 (S3–AlTa 3). Moreover, 
 Al1Ti6.5Zr2Nb3Ta3Mo0.5 (S4–AlMo) alloy is also obtained 
when removing the Cr and adding the Ti based on the 
S3–AlTa3. Table 1 lists related data of the designed series 
of alloys, including cluster formula, composition (at.%), 
density (ρ ), and theoretical exothermic enthalpy (ΔH ). 
The microstructural evolutions of these alloys with the 
aging temperature will be first studied. Then, the RT and 
HT mechanical properties of these alloys will be test, in 
which the effect of low Al content on microstructure and 
mechanical properties will be discussed. Finally, the ener -
getic characteristics of these alloys will also be discussed.

2 Experimental
The current alloy ingots were prepared by arc melting and 
suction cast into a 6-mm-diameter cylindrical copper mold 
under an argon atmosphere. The purities of all raw metals 
are beyond 99.96%. These ingots were remelted at least 
five times to ensure composition uniformity. Then, they 
were sealed in vacuum quartz glass tubes and subjected to 
solid-solutioned process at 1573 K for 2 h in a muffle fur -
nace. Subsequently, these solid-solutioned samples were 
aged at 873, 973, and 1073 K for 24 h, respectively. Water 
quenching was applied following each heat treatment.
The crystalline structures of alloy specimens with 
different treatments were carried out by using a Bruker 
D8 X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Cu K α radiation, 
λ = 0.15406 nm). The microstructures were examined by 
the Olympus optical microscopy (OM) with an etchant 
solution of 21% HF + 21% HNO3 + 58% H2O (volume 
fraction), the SHIMADZU electronic probe microanalyzer 
(EPMA), and the IT800-SHL scanning electron micros-
copy (SEM). The phase structures were further identified 
by an FEI Talos F200X scanning transmission electron 
microscope (STEM), in which the TEM specimens were 
prepared by the FEI Helios NanoLab 600 dual-beam 
focused ion beam (DB-FIB) instrument and the detailed 
description was described elsewhere [41]. The elemental 
distributions and phase composition were analyzed by the 
SHIMADZU electronic probe microanalyzer (EPMA) and 
the Super-X energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometry (EDS) 
detector equipped on FEI Talos F200X STEM operating 
at 200 kV. The statistical analysis of the volume fraction 
and size of precipitated particles and the grain sizes at 
different heat treatment states were measured from the 
SEM/OM morphology images (at least 6 images) using 
the ImageJ software. Uniaxial compressive tests at room 
temperature and high temperatures of 873–1073 K were 
conducted on both the UTM5504 Material Test System 
(MTS). Three cylindrical specimens with the dimensions 
of Φ 5 mm × 8 mm were tested for each heat treatment 
state using a strain rate of 1 × 10–3  s−1, respectively. The 
equilibrium phase diagram of the designed alloy was cal-
culated using Pandat-2022 software with PanRHEA2022 
database.

These findings suggest that the mechanical properties of 
the alloy can be improved by manipulating its microstruc-
ture through the reduction of Al content. In the system of 
 AlNbTa0.5TiZr0.5, a large number of Al-rich and Zr-rich 
intermetallic compounds are formed between the grains, 
which seriously damages the mechanical properties of the 
alloy, resulting in the compressive yield strength that is only 
σYS = 1352 MPa and the compressive plasticity of less than 
1.5% [15]. In addition, the precipitation of the brittle Laves 
phase (such as Cr2Zr and Al2Zr) during aging is an important 
cause for the ductility decrease of the alloys. For instance, 
the reported CrNbTa0.25TiZr alloy after 1273 K-aged for 20 
h exhibits the compression plasticity of less than 8%, due 
to the precipitation of Laves phase [43]. In our previous 
study, Al–Ti-Zr–Nb–Ta series alloys with high Al content 
(Ref Al2) precipitated a significant amount of brittle Zr5Al3 
phase and Laves phase (Cr2Ti) after undergoing aging at 973 
K and 1073 K. This led to a deterioration in the compres -
sion plasticity of the alloy, reducing it from approximately 
δ ~ 50% at solid-solutioned state to less than of δ ~ 10% at 
aged state [37]. Due to the reduction in Al content, the for -

boundaries after 873 K-aged for 24 h, as shown in Fig.  3a, 
d, g. In particular, S3-AlTa3 alloy after 873 K-aged exhibits 
excellent mechanical properties that the compressive yield

at room temperature and the compressive yield strength of 
σYS = 717 MPa at 1073 K, comparing to the performance of 
its predecessor alloys, as shown in Fig. 10a, b, which could 
indicate that the coherent BCC/B2 microstructure has a high 
thermal stability at elevated temperatures up to 1073 K.

5 Conclusion
This study focuses on investigating the microstructural 
evolutions and mechanical properties with the aging tem-
perature of energetic Al1 (TiZrNbTaMoCr) 15 refractory 
high-entropy alloys at low Al content state. The findings 
demonstrate a significant improvement in the plasticity of 
the series alloys without compromising their strength. It is 
found that all these solid-solutioned RHEAs exhibit equi-
axed grains of a single BCC structure, while second phases 
would precipitate from the matrix after aging, evidenced 
by the coarse BCC1, Cr2Zr, and Zr5Al3 phase particles 
in these alloys. Importantly, the S3-AlTa 3 alloy after 873 
K-aged with a composition of Al1Ti6Zr2Nb3Ta3Mo0.5Cr0.5 
forms the coherent BCC/B2 microstructure with needle-
like B2 nanoparticles precipitated. By further improving 
the aging temperature up to 973 K, the B2 phase becomes 
unstable and transforms into stable Zr5Al3 phase due to 
its metastable feature, and then Zr5Al3 phase becomes 
dominant after 1073 K-aged. The excellent mechanical 
properties of the S3-AlTa 3 alloy after 873 K-aged can be 
attributed to the BCC/B2 microstructure with needle-like 
B2 nanoparticles into BCC matrix, showing the highest 
compression yield strength (1333 and 717 MPa) at room 
temperature and 1073 K, respectively, with good plasticity 
(40% at room temperature). Moreover, this series RHEAs 
have ultrahigh exothermic enthalpy value exceeding 11606 
J·g−1, which have significant promise for utilization as 
high-strength energetic structural materials.
