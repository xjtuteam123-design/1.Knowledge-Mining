ABSTRACT
High-temperature structural alloys are urgently desired for refractory applications. In this work, we conceived a 
facile route to achieve an excellent combination of high-temperature strength and room-temperature ductility in 
a NbMoTaWHf refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA). The idea is to break up the stable single body-centered 
cubic (BCC) phase in NbMoTaW RHEA to introduce the tiny secondary phase by alloying appropriate hafnium 
(Hf) that can stall and accumulate dislocations. The dual-BCC-phase structure and the dislocations for the 
NbMoTaWHf RHEA were characterized. The compressive yield strengths of the NbMoTaWHf RHEA at room 
temperature, 1200, 1600, and 1800 
◦
C were 1730, 1088, 390, and 312 MPa, respectively. The alloy displayed 
plastic deformation behaviors and dual-BCC-phase structure stability after compressing at 1600 and 1800 
◦
C. The 
superb high-temperature strength was mainly ascribed to the high softening temperature, second-phase 
strengthening, and solid-solution strengthening. This work provides a promising high-temperature structural 
material with improved room-temperature ductility and high strength at elevated temperatures and enriches the 
database of the NbMoTaW-based RHEAs.
1. Introduction
High-temperature structural alloys with service temperatures above 
1600 
◦
C are urgently desired for refractory applications such as aero- 
engines, gas turbines, and space explorations [ 1 – 6 ]. High-entropy al -
loys containing multi-principal elements in equiatomic or 
near-equiatomic have excellent mechanical properties, wear resistance, 
corrosion resistance, and thermal stability [ 7 – 9 ]. Among them, re -
fractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), mainly consisting of refractory 
elements, exhibit excellent mechanical properties at elevated tempera -
tures with promising application as a new generation of 
high-temperature structural alloys, such as NbMoTaW-based RHEAs 
[ 10 – 16 ] and HfZrTi-based RHEAs [ 17 – 22 ]. RHEAs have expanded to be 
a big family, with more than 180 reported RHEAs since they were re -
ported first in 2010 [ 23 – 31 ]. The NbMoTaW RHEA exhibits superb 
mechanical properties at high temperatures; however, relatively low 
ductility at room temperature [ 32 – 34 ].
Much work has been done to develop new alloys with high high- 
temperature strength and better room-temperature ductility [ 35 – 41 ]. 
The WTaMoNbTi RHEA showed improved ductility of 11.5 % at room 
temperature and considerable strength at 600 – 1600 
◦

strength of the alloy.

deviate from the mixture rule. The strengthening mechanism of 
NbMoTaWHf at room temperature can mainly be ascribed to the com -
bination of solid-solution strengthening and second-phase 
strengthening.
First, the large number of principal elements in the NbMoTaWHf 
RHEA resulted in a large solid solution strengthening effect. The atomic 
radius of Hf is much larger than those of other constituent elements. The 
element Hf solubilized into the lattice of the BCC structure, forming a 
substitutional solid solution. As the difference in the atomic size of the 
elements increases, the lattice misfit parameter increases. Due to the 
large atomic size difference, the stress caused by lattice mismatch in -
creases accounting for the high strength of the alloy.
Second, the secondary BCC2 phase interacted with the BCC1 phase, 
enhancing the alloys by means of second-phase strengthening. The 
massive dispersion of second-phase particles retards the dislocation 
motion, potentially increasing the strength of the alloy.
4.3. Strengthening mechanism at elevated temperature
The high strength of NbMoTaWHf RHEA at elevated temperatures 
was mainly ascribed to the high softening temperature, the second- 
phase strengthening, and solid-solution strengthening.
First, a high softening temperature is beneficial for high-temperature 
mechanical properties [ 41 ]. Generally speaking, the softening temper -
ature for an alloy is about 0.6 T
m 
[ 10 , 42 ]. The calculated T
m 
of NbMo -
TaWHf RHEA is 3027 K (i.e., 2754 
◦
C). Thus, the softening temperature 
of NbMoTaWHf is about 1816 K (i.e., 1543 
◦
C). It is consistent with the 
result that the strength drops rapidly between 1200 and 1600 
◦
C. 
Although it is a little below 1600 
◦
C, the softening temperature some -
how has a specific effect on maintaining the strength at elevated 
temperatures.
Second, the BCC2 phase strengthens the NbMoTaWHf RHEA by the 
second-phase strengthening. The grain size of the BCC2 phase was 
significantly smaller than that of the BCC1 phase. The structurally stable 
BCC2 phase dispersed evenly in the BCC1 matrix, strengthening the 
alloy by the second phase at elevated temperatures. When a moving 
dislocation encounters the grain boundary of the BCC1 and BCC2 pha -
ses, it would be impeded by the phase boundary. Thus, an external force 
is needed to keep deformation, resulting in an increase in stress.
Third, the large number of principal elements in the NbMoTaWHf 
RHEA resulted in a large solid solution strengthening effect [ 58 , 62 ]. Due 
to the size difference between solute atoms and solvent atoms, alloying 
elements cause local lattice distortion, which increases the resistance of 
dislocation movement and makes it difficult to glide, thus increasing the 
strength and hardness. The larger the atomic size difference, the stron -
ger the lattice distortion. By adding Hf, the atomic size difference of 
NbMoTaWHf increased. Thus, the lattice distortion became severe, 
increasing the lattice constant.
4.4. Potential high-temperature applications
Superior high-temperature mechanical properties endow the 
NbMoTaWHf RHEA with promising high-temperature engineering ap -
plications. Fig. 6 shows the yield strength and specific yield strength as a 
function of testing temperature for the NbMoTaWHf RHEA and related 
refractory alloys [ 2 , 10 , 42 , 63 – 67 ]. The traditional superalloys, such as 
Inconel 718, exhibit relatively low strength ( < 150 MPa) above 1000 
◦
C. 
The HfZrTi-based RHEAs exhibit good ductility at room temperature but 
can hardly serve at temperatures above 1400 
◦
C. Outstandingly, the 
NbMoTaW-based RHEAs, including NbMoTaWHf RHEA, exhibit 
strength above 150 MPa at 1600 
◦
C. Besides, the NbMoTaWHf RHEA 
possesses better ductility and higher strength than NbMoTaW and 
NbMoTaWV at room temperature, which benefits industrial applica -
tions. The specific yield strength of NbMoTaWHf at 1200, 1600, and 
1800 
◦
C is 78, 28, and 22 MPa cm
3
/g, respectively, which is relatively 
higher than other alloys at elevated temperatures. The better ductility 
and considerable high-temperature strength make the NbMoTaWHf 
RHEA a potential high-temperature structure alloy.
Fig. 7 shows the reported highest testing temperature and corre -
sponding yield strength of various alloys [ 2 , 10 , 42 , 45 , 53 , 63 – 66 , 68 – 81 ]. 
The highest testing temperature of most alloys is below 1600 
◦
C, which 
drops in the high-temperature range. Traditional tantalum-base and 
tungsten-base refractory alloys exhibit relatively low strength in the 
ultra-high temperature range. In this work, the NbMoTaWHf RHEA 
exhibits an extremely high yield strength of 312 MPa and compressive 
strength of 348 MPa at 1800 
◦
C, classifying it as a UHT-HEA. Assessed 
with high-temperature strength, the NbMoTaWHf RHEA has the po -
tential engineering applications as high-temperature structural alloys in 
aero-engines, gas turbines, nuclear power plants, structural fire engi -
neering, high-temperature cutting tools, and so forth.
