ABSTRACT 
This article reports a thermo-mechanical processing method that enables microstructure tailoring and me -
chanical property improvement in an Al – V – Cr – Mn – Fe – Ni complex concentrated alloy (CCA) with an as-cast 
microstructure consisting of coherent B2 precipitation in a BCC matrix. A large increment (45%) in tensile 
strength has been achieved after the non-uniform as-cast microstructure was changed to a uniform, recrystallized 
structure after processing. Microstructure evolution and the corresponding mechanical properties were also 
studied for this alloy. It was found by selecting the proper temperature at the final annealing step, significant 
improvements in ductility can be further achieved without compromising the high strength; the mechanical 
behavior of this alloy can be effectively tuned by varying the final annealing temperature. Furthermore, to 
validate efficacy and generalizability of the developed thermo-mechanical processing method on CCAs with the 
same type of microstructure, but in a different compositional space, the same method was applied to an 
Al – Cr – Fe – Co – Ni CCA composition found in the literature. Similar to our Al – V – Cr – Mn – Fe – Ni CCA, tensile test of 
the processed Al – Cr – Fe – Co – Ni CCA revealed vast improvement in tensile strength (over 200 MPa) without 
sacrificing any ductility. 
1. Introduction 
Since the discovery of high entropy alloys (HEAs) by Yeh et al. and 
Cantor et al. in 2004 [ 1 , 2 ], a “ renaissance in physical metallurgy ” has 
been ongoing over the past two decades [ 3 ]. Numerous new HEAs and 
their non-equiatomic variants (together referred to as complex concen -
trated alloys (CCAs) or multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs)) have 
been discovered and developed with many showing promising proper -
ties for potential structural applications [ 4 – 8 ]. Due to the vast compo -
sitional spaces that CCAs span, many typical microstructures in various 
traditional alloy systems can also be found in CCAs [ 5 , 7 – 9 ]. One of the 
common types that has been the focus of many research studies is the 
BCC/B2 coherent nanostructure that resembles the γ / γ’ microstructure 
in the commercial Ni-based superalloys [ 10 – 17 ]. A typical superalloy 
microstructure consists of an FCC- γ matrix and cuboidal L1
2
- γ’ nano -
precipitates coherently embedded in the matrix [ 18 ]. The ordered γ’ 
nanoprecipitates in relatively high fraction endow the alloys high 
strength, while the ductile γ matrix ensures decent ductility at room 
temperature. Due to the high solvus temperature of the γ’ phase, the 
alloys can remain strong at elevated temperatures and have excellent 
resistance to creep, and therefore have been widely adopted for various 
critical high temperature structural applications [ 18 , 19 ]. 
Considering the advantage of crystal structure (generally higher 
strength of BCC over FCC) and the extra strengthening effects present in 
CCA systems (e.g., enhanced solid solution strengthening, lattice 
distortion, short-range ordering, etc.) [ 7 , 8 , 20 ], the BCC/B2 nano -
structured CCAs have the potential to perform better than the superal -
loys [ 21 , 22 ]. However, the following disadvantages have greatly 
lowered the competency of these CCAs. For starters, the majority of the 
BCC/B2 nanostructured CCAs reported to date do not possess adequate 
tensile ductility at room temperature [ 13 – 17 ], which is a crucial 
requirement for most structural applications. Secondly, due to the hard 
and brittle nature of the BCC/B2 nanostructure, this type of alloy cannot 
be readily cold-worked, nor does there exist any thermo-mechanical 
processing method previously reported for this kind of microstructure, 
which makes the non-uniform grain morphology and defects retained
