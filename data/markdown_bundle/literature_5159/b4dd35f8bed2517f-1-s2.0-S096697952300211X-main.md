1. Induction 
The rapid development of modern industry, especially in the aero -
space field, puts forward urgent demand for high-temperature materials 
with high-performance. Ni-based and/or Co-based superalloys 
strengthened by ordered L1
2 
( γ ′ ) phase have been widely designed and 
employed to deal with extreme service conditions [ 1 – 3 ]. However, the 
narrow gap between the γ ′ -solvus temperature and the solidus temper -
ature in Ni-based superalloys make them easily approach their tem -
perature limit [ 2 , 4 ]. While in Co-based superalloys, the metastable 
Co
3

elevated temperatures, resulting in severe embrittlement and cata -
strophic brittle fracture [ 5 , 6 ]. Additionally, high mass density, low 
γ ′ -solvus temperature and high-priced of Co element in Co-based su -
peralloys also pose huge obstacles to their structural application [ 7 , 8 ]. 
Consequently, those limitations in Ni-based and Co-based superalloys 
motivate the search for new classes of cost-effective high-temperature 
materials with a good combination of higher upper temperature limits, 
lower mass density, long-term thermal stability and superior 
high-temperature strength. 
Multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs), i.e. high/medium-entropy 
alloys (HEAs/MEAs), have attracted more and more attentions due to 
their unique design strategy, mechanical and physical properties [ 9 – 14 ]. 
Recent researches indicate that γ ʹ precipitation-strengthened HEAs/ -
MEAs show remarkable thermal stability during long-term aging treat -

mass density, γ ′ -solvus temperature, and high-temperature tensile
