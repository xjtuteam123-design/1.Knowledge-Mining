ABSTRACT
To meet the increasingly complex environmental demands, titanium aluminum (TiAl) alloys need to be improved 
in microstructural stability and high temperature strength. However, traditional TiAl alloys exhibit poor 
ductility, which are incompatible with conventional manufacturing techniques such as casting and forging. 
Aiming to overcome these limitations, this work presents a micro/nano multiphase synergistically reinforced 
TiAl-based composite with optimal addition of 0.10 at.% LaB
6 
via directed energy deposition, showing a ho -
mogeneously equiaxed fully lamellar. Meanwhile, the ultimate tensile strength at room temperature is 997 MPa, 
which is 187 MPa higher than the pure TiAl alloy (810 MPa), even at 900 
◦
C it remains at 685 MPa that is over 
100 MPa higher than the pure TiAl alloy (560 MPa). Besides, the multi-stage network structure formed by TiB 
and La
2
O
3 
precipitates significantly improves the stability of the microstructure. The present work offers an 
alternative solution for designing enhanced TiAl-based composites with stable microstructures via additive 
manufacturing.
1. Introduction
Achieving balance between microstructure and mechanical proper -
ties is a persistent goal for TiAl alloys mainly containing α
2

γ phases, which are promising new high-temperature (HT) structural 
materials applied in aerospace and automotive industries owing to their 
low density, good resistance against oxidation and high specific strength 
[ 1 , 2 ]. As the third-generation TiAl alloys, high-Nb TiAl alloys are the 
research focus of the new generation of TiAl alloys. Because of the 
addition of Nb element, it has higher yield strength and service tem -
perature, which satisfy next-generation aircraft engine requirements, 
and the service temperature is higher than that of ordinary TiAl alloys 
[ 3 ]. Meanwhile, the mechanical properties of TiAl alloys are strongly 
dependent on the microstructures [ 4 , 5 ], and a fine fully lamellar (FL) 
exhibits balanced properties. The FL is mainly composed of the L1

room temperature (RT), which seriously deteriorates the mechanical 
properties [ 1 ]. However, TiAl alloys usually suffer from microstructural 
degradation during long-term operation at HTs, which limits their 
application [ 6 ]. Therefore, the strategy has been developed to stabilize 
the matrix microstructure by introducing precipitates to prepare 
TiAl-based composites [ 7 ]. Moreover, directed energy deposition (DED) 
with rapid cooling rate and complex thermal history is a widely devel -
oped additive manufacturing (AM) technology, whose advantages in 
saving production time and material consumption are suitable for the 
development of new materials [ 8 – 10 ]. However, like other 
high-strength brittle materials, TiAl alloys have high crack sensitivity. 
Also, the rapid cooling rate and complex thermal history lead to the 
occurrence of coarse columnar crystals and even cracks during or after 
processing [ 11 – 13 ].
Preparing TiAl-based composites by introducing precipitates is an 
effective way to adjust the microstructure [ 14 ], also expected to alle -
viate hot cracking during AM for brittle TiAl alloys [ 15 ]. In terms of 
TiAl-based composites, previous studies used HT thermochemical stable

The strategy of preparing TiAl-based composites by laser in situ 
substrate combined with pre-depositing TC4 alloy is innovatively pro -
posed. That is: firstly, the substrate is heated in situ by laser; then, TC4 
powder is fed and TC4 alloy is deposited; when depositing to layer 11, 
switch the powder cylinder, feed TiAl powders, and start preparing TiAl- 
based composites. Meanwhile, the schematic and actual process of DED 
is shown in Fig. 1 a and b, respectively. At the initial stage, there are high 
thermal stresses in the interior of the AMed sample and at the top of the 
deposition layer and the interface bonding zone. Meanwhile, the ther -
mal stresses increase first and then tends to balance and finally decrease 
with the increase of the number of deposited layers. Therefore, pre- 
deposited TC4 alloy can effectively release the early thermal stress. 
Compared with traditional depositing methods, this strategy can slow 
down the rapid heat dissipation of the substrate in the initial stage, 
release stress concentration and promote forming. Finally, the optimized 
processing parameters are as follows: laser beam power is 800 W, beam 
diameter is 3 mm; beam scanning speed is 6 mm/s, feeding rate is 6 g/ 
min and layer thickness is 0.45 mm. Notably, the same process param -
eters were used to prepare TC4 alloy and TiAl-based composites. Single- 
track walls with dimensions of 40 × 45 × 4 mm, shown in Fig. 1 c – e, 
were prepared for microstructure characterization and property tests. In 
addition, the chemical composition of the AMed sample is shown in T 
able R1. The result reveals that the chemical compositions are similar to 
the pre-alloyed powders and the Al loss is neglectable.

3. Results
3.1. Powders characterization
The pre-alloyed TiAl powders present a nearly spherical morphology 
( Fig. 2 a), exhibiting numerous microsegregation with evident dendritic 
microstructure ( Fig. 2 b).
Meanwhile, the dendritic segregation phenomenon is closely related 
to the solidification process in which Al was repelled to the interden -
dritic region as the dendrites first solidified from the liquid [ 38 ]. The 
pre-alloyed TiAl powders show a particle size of 45 – 150 μ m ( Fig. 2 c). 
Comparatively, nano-LaB
6 
particles are with a mean diameter about 
100 nm ( Fig. 2 d). Moreover, the nanoparticles adhere to the pre-alloyed 
powders after ball milling ( Fig. 2 e), and the XRD pattern shows that the 
diffraction peak of LaB
6 
is detected compared with the pre-alloyed 
powder ( Fig. 2 f). Also, as confirmed by elemental mapping ( Fig. 2 g), 
the surface of the pre-alloyed powder is significantly rich in La and B. 
This proves that nanoparticles can firmly adhere to pre-alloyed powders 
and provide a basis for the preparation of AMed TiAl-based composites.
3.2. Formability analysis
To further investigate the formability of AMed samples, Micro-CT 
was conducted on pure TiAl alloy and representative TiAl-based com -
posite containing 0.10 at.% LaB
6 
particles, demonstrating the 3D dis -
tribution of defects. For AMed pure TiAl alloys, scattered defects are 
present ( Fig. 3 a). These defects show a peak distribution of diameters 
around 8.5 μ m, and almost all defects have equivalent diameters smaller 
than 15 μ m ( Fig. 3 b). According to the statistical results of Micro-CT, the 
defect volume ratio of the presently AMed pure TiAl alloy is only 0.13 % 
and 99.87 % of relative density, which proves that the DED process is 
feasible. In comparison, after adding 0.10 at.% LaB
6 
particles, the den -
sity of the sample is further improved ( Fig. 3 c), which is manifested as a 
decrease in defects along the BD. Correspondingly, the peak diameter 
distribution of these defects is about 7 μ m, and the equivalent diameter 
of almost all defects is less than 10 μ m ( Fig. 3 d). Meanwhile, the defect 
volume ratio is reduced to 0.085 %, which indicates the improvement of 
the formability of TiAl-based composite by adding of moderate LaB
6 
particles.

3.4. Mechanical properties
