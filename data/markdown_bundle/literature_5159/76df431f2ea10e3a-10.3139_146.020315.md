Finite element analysis of c0 directional
coarsening in Ni-based superalloys
A three-dimensional anisotropic finite element analysis was
performed to calculate the hydrostatic pressure, elastic
strain energy density and von Mises stress associated with
periodically arranged cubic c0 precipitates in the c matrix
of Ni-based superalloys. Inhomogeneous and anisotropic
elastic media are considered, the effects of different lattice
misfit and external loads are investigated. The results show
that the asymmetry of the hydrostatic pressure, the strain
energy density and von Mises stress could be used to predict
the c
0 directional coarsening. The contribution of hydro-
static pressure to the total driving force is smaller than that
of von Mises stress and the elastic strain energy. The reason
was briefly discussed.
Keywords: Finite element analysis; Ni-based superalloys;
Rafting; Coarsening
1 Introduction
Directional coarsening (rafting) of c0 precipitates in nickel-
based superalloys occurs when these materials are sub-
jected to stresses at high temperatures. For the sake of sim-
plification, the rafted morphologies are divided into two
groups: (1) plates aligned perpendicular to the stress axis
are called N-type rafts and (2) plates or needles aligned par-
allel to the stress axis are called P-type rafts [1]. The type of
rafting is determined by several factors such as the sign and
magnitude of the relative lattice mismatch and of the exter-
nal force. The thermodynamic driving force for rafting is
proportional to the applied stress, to the difference between
the lattice parameters of the c matrix and the c0 particles,
and to the difference of their elastic constants in the elastic
regime [2]. Creep deformation-induced changes of the local
lattice parameter, giving rise to residual long-range internal
stresses acting in the phases c and c
0, have been detected by
Mughrabi and co-workers [3, 4].
Theoretical works [5, 6] have treated the energetics and
kinetics of the elastic stress on directional coarsening
through consideration of simplified microstructures. There
have been a number of analytical studies on morphological
evolution of the microstructure of c ± c0 Ni-based superal-
loys by finite element (FE) methods [1, 7 ± 15] and different
experimental techniques have been applied for their meas-
urements [6, 8, 16 ± 19]. For calculating stresses and strains
associated with the c0 precipitates in Ni-based superalloys,
the two-dimensional (2D) FE method has been widely used,
such as by Pollock and Argon [8] and Muller et al. [14] and
they showed a stress concentration at the corners of the c
0
phase. Socrate and Parks [12] and Feng et al. [1] have calcu-
lated the driving force for directional coarsening of the c0
phase by a 2D FE method. The result shows that the driving
force due to the difference between the hydrostatic pres-
sures on the c ± c0 interfaces in the c and c0 phases is an im-
portant contribution to the total driving force, in addition to
the contribution from the elastic strain energy [1]. A 2D FE
method has been used to calculate the strain energy stored
in the c ± c0 microstructure and to determine the equilibrium
shape of the precipitates in the under formed state and under
external load [7, 9 ± 11]. Much progress has been made,
however, the question regarding the mechanism of rafting
is still not quite clear.
Due to the high degree of elastic anisotropy ratios (see
Table 1) of the precipitates and matrix, it is necessary to
use the three-dimensional (3D) anisotropic FE method. Ad-
ditionally, while there is a great deal of experimental data
and computer simulation on directional coarsening in nega-
tive-misfit materials, in order to have a thorough under-
standing of the mechanism of rafting there is a need for par-
allel detailed studies of rafting in positive-misfit crystals.
Therefore, the aim of the present study is to investigate
how the hydrostatic pressure, elastic strain energy density
and von Mises stress are affected by two factors: the lattice
misfit and the external stress, and to analyze their contribu-
tions to the directional coarsening.

4 Results and discussion
Based on the FE calculation, the results are summarized in
Table 2.
4.1 No external stress
When no external stress is applied, the hydrostatic pressure
is symmetrically distributed along the plane y=z in both
negative- and positive-misfit cases (see Figs. 2a and b).
The elastic strain energy density and von Mises stress are
also symmetrical distribution along the plane y=z .
4.2 An external stress along the [001] direction
4.2.1 The hydrostatic pressure distribution
When the external load p = ± 150 MPa, the hydrostatic
pressure has an asymmetrical distribution in the matrix
channels, but in the precipitates, its distribution is similar
to that without external loads, only the sign and magnitude
are changed in some regions (see Figs. 3a ± d). From the re-
sults of the FE calculations it is found that the sign of the
hydrostatic pressure is influenced not only by the external
loads but also by the sign of the misfit. For an alloy with a
negative misfit (the lattice parameter of c
0 phase is smaller
than that of c phase), when the external stress is not large
(p = 150 MPa), evidently, a compressive stress was identi-
fied within the c channels accompanied by a tensile stress
within the precipitates (Figs. 3a, b). When a large tensile
stress is applied ( p = 450 MPa), the compressive stress in
the matrix channels would reduce and turn to tensile stress
in some regions, while the tensile stress in the c0 phase will
enhance (Fig. 4a). Contrarily, when a compressive stress is
applied, the compressive stress in the matrix channels will
increase and the tensile stress in the precipitates will reduce
so that in some regions it changes into compressive stress
(Fig. 4b). At high external stresses ( ± 450 MPa) the asym-
metrical distribution is more evidence as we expected. To
save space, the figures for a positive misfit superalloy under
the external stresses of ± 450 MPa are not included in the
paper.
4.2.2 Inhomogeneous change of strain energy density
and von Mises stress
Application of an external stress p = ± 150 MPa along the
[001] direction leads to differential levels of strain energy
density and von Mises stress in the horizontal and vertical
c channels. Figs. 5a ± d show the distribution of strain en-
ergy density. Figs. 6a and b show the distribution of von
Mises stress for a negative-misfit alloy.
The tensile stress p = 150 MPa was applied to a negative-
misfit superalloy along the [001] direction (Fig. 5a). Within
the matrix, the strain energy density displayed in the hori-
zontal channel is larger than that in the vertical channel.
Within the precipitate, however, the strain energy density
near the horizontal interface is smaller than that near
the vertical interface. When the compressive stress p =
± 150 MPa is applied, the opposite is true, as shown in
Fig. 5b. A similar event occurs in the von Mises stress (see
Figs. 6a, b).

4.4 Application of the elastic strain energy and
von Mises stress to predict the direction of rafting
In the case of a tensile stress is applied to a superalloy with a
negative misfit, although the calculation is in the elastic re-
gime, the largest von Mises stress is » 600 ± 800 MPa and in
the horizontal channels it is higher than that in the vertical
channels (see Fig. 6a). Therefore, in some regions there
would take place plastic deformation even under low external
stress (150 MPa). Due to the higher von Mises stress in the
horizontal channels, the dislocations would generate and dis-
tribute predominantly in that region. At the same time, the
elastic strain energy is larger in this region (see Fig. 5a), and
the driving force for phase transformation is higher in the
same region [2]. Because the Cr and Co atoms in the c phase
have a higher mobility [23], they enter these dislocations so
as to reduce the elastic energy. Therefore, after the first
stages of deformation, a part of the Cr and Co atoms are dif-
fusing along the dislocations and a Cr/Co-depleted zone is
created within the channels filled with dislocations. Cr and
Co are then able to diffuse from matrix channels which are
devoid of dislocations towards matrix channels which are
filled with dislocations. This flux is accompanied by a
crossed flux of c
0 elements (Al, Ti, Ta, Ni) [23].
Therefore, the rafting of c0 occurs. The von Mises stress is
largest at the corners of the c0 precipitates, and the diffusion
along the dislocation pipes is faster than bulk diffusion. This
results in the dumbbell formation during the course of raft-
ing. Concentration gradients in the c phase were also de-
tected by Svetlov et al. [16] and Zhang et al. [17] by experi-
ments. At the horizontal channels near the interfaces the
enrichment of Cr and Co and depletions of Ti and Ta destroy
the original local chemical equilibrium. In order to establish
a new equilibrium, the horizontal interfaces should move to-
wards the c0 side to form more c phase so as to consume the
extra Cr and Co content near the interfaces. As a conse-
quence, the width of the horizontal channels would increase.
This result agrees with the experimental observations [18].
Reversal of the sign of external stress p or of lattice misfit d
will cause the dislocation generation and distribution to alter-
nate between horizontal and vertical channels.
Additionally, when external stress is applied, the differ-
ence of the elastic strain energy density and von Mises
stress between the horizontal and the vertical channels in
the matrix is larger than that in the c
0 phase near the hori-
zontal and vertical interfaces. So long-range diffusion takes
place mainly in the c phase during the course of directional
coarsening of c0 precipitates.
The unsymmetrical distribution of the hydrostatic pres-
sure results in an unsymmetrical variation of the lattice
parameters in the horizontal and vertical channels. Under
the influence of the crystal lattice, the matrix atoms will dif-
fuse from the channels with bigger lattice parameter toward
the channels with smaller lattice parameter. Contrarily, the
precipitate atoms will diffuse from the channels with small-
er lattice parameter towards the channels with bigger lattice
parameter.
From the phenomenological point of view, in each case,
the horizontal channels with bigger strain energy density
and bigger von Mises stress will cause the N-type rafting.
Contrarily, the vertical channels with bigger strain energy
density and bigger von Mises stress will cause the P-type
rafting. These results are summarized in Table 2.
The strain energy is a part of the Gibbs free energy driv-
ing the phase transformation. At microscopic scale, a higher
von Mises stress will cause dislocation generation and
speed up the diffusion along the dislocation pipes. There-
fore, the hydrostatic pressure, the strain energy density,
and the von Mises stress could be used to predict the rafting
process of c
0 in nickel-based superalloys.

5 Conclusions
1. The distribution of hydrostatic pressure, strain energy
density and von Mises stress could be used to predict
the rafting directions of c0 in nickel-based superal-
loys.
2. The contribution of hydrostatic pressure to the total
driving force is smaller than that of von Mises stress
and the elastic strain energy.
