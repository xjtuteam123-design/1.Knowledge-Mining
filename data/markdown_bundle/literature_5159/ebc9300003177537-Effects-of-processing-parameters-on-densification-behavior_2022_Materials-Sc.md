ABSTRACT 
Tungsten and its alloys have been widely used in various industries such as nuclear energy, medical shielding, 
rocket nozzle. However, the intrinsic high melting point and high ductile-to-brittle transition temperature 
(DBTT) normally limit their further applications. In this work, the transition element Ti modified W alloy was 
processed by laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) additive manufacturing process with different laser processing 
parameters. The densification behavior, microstructure evolution, and mechanical performance of LPBF- 
processed W – Ti samples were systematically investigated through the optimization of laser processing param -
eters. It indicated that the highest relative density of 99.1% without obvious pores and balling phenomenon were 
obtained at the applied energy density of 848 J/mm
3 
with laser power of 350 W and scan speed of 275 mm/s. 
The typical microstructure of LPBF-processed W – Ti alloy could be divided into two main regions including W 
bulk region and W dendrite region with the chemical compositions of W – Ti solid solution, which was caused by 
the significant difference of melting point between W and Ti. The high microhardness of 731 HV
0.2 
and 
compressive performance ( σ
bc 
= 1719 MPa, δ = 24.7%) were achieved. Compared with pure tungsten samples of 
our previous work (902 MPa), the compressive strength of W – Ti alloy by LPBF displayed significant improve -
ment of 90%, which could be attributed to the increased densification level and solid solution strengthening 
mechanism. These findings provided the knowledge of the relationship between laser processing parameters and 
properties of LPBF-processed W – Ti alloy, further providing reference values applicable for development of W- 
based alloys by laser additive manufacturing. 
1. Introduction 
As one of the most potential additive manufacturing techniques, 
laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) has experienced rapid development in 
the past decades. During LPBF process, a focused laser beam is utilized to 
melt powder materials layer by layer to form ultimate parts, based on 
computer aided design (CAD) volume model [ 1 – 3 ]. Usually, the molten 
pool of LPBF has a strong heat exchange effect with the surrounding 
substrate or the solidified area due to the laser rapid heating, which can 
lead to a cooling rate of 10
2
– 10
6 
K/s and an unstable non-equilibrium 
thermodynamic evolution in a few microseconds [ 4 , 5 ]. Therefore, the 
metallurgical mechanism containing rapid melting and solidification of 
LPBF gives rise to the non-equilibrium phases with refined microstruc -
ture of the crystal and excellent comprehensive performances [ 6 , 7 ]. As 
compared to conventional manufacturing methods, LPBF technique can 
obtain near-net-shape-production and reduce the restriction on the 
geometric complexity of the molding. Besides, other advantages are also 
provided by it including high material utilization and high dimensional 
accuracy [ 8 – 11 ]. All these advantages make LPBF attractive in the 
aerospace, military, biomedical, and other industries. As reported in the 
previous literature, it is feasible for LPBF to manufacture titanium al -
loys, high entropy alloys, Ni-based superalloys, high melting point ce -
ramics, and even refractory metals [ 12 – 16 ]. 
As a promising refractory material, tungsten (W) is widely used in 
many fields including nuclear energy, medical shielding, and rocket 
nozzle, due to its attractive combinations of attributes such as extremely 
high melting point (3420

threshold energy, appreciable thermal conductivity, and excellent

Fortunately, the performance of tungsten can be improved based on 
alloying, involving solid solution strengthening or dispersion strength -
ening [ 26 ]. The typical material system design of tungsten alloy consists 
of W – Ni – Fe, W – Ni – Cu, W – Ni – Cr, and other more [ 27 ]. Romaner et al. 
[ 28 ] demonstrated that the ductility of tungsten could be improved by 
alloying with group IV or V transition metals theoretically, for the 
reason that the alloying elements enhance the initiation of dislocations. 
Wang et al. [ 29 ] reported on crack-suppressed parts by adding 6 wt% Ta. 
Compared with pure W, cracks in the W – 6Ta alloy were reduced by 80% 
due to the submicron intragranular cellular composed of a large number 
of dislocations. Therefore, it is instructive to investigate that alloying 
tungsten with other metallic elements in order to improve its physical 
properties. Ti element has the properties of relatively high melting 
point, low density, and thermal expansion coefficient similar to that of 
tungsten, which is a good reinforcement material for W-based alloy. 
Meanwhile, since tungsten solidifies firstly during pool cooling, the 
alloying elements Ti could compensate for the resulting volume 
shrinkage to reduce the thermal stress. Jiang et al. [ 30 ] used first prin -
ciples calculations to study the effect of Ti alloying on the mechanical 
properties of bcc W. It was found that the ductility of bcc W could be 
improved through Ti alloying based on the Poisson ’ s ratio and calcu -
lated B/G. Chookajorn et al. [ 31 ] proposed a nanostructure stability map 
and described three different regions based on a thermodynamic 
approach. In this aspect, W-20at% Ti alloy was fabricated by 
high-energy ball milling and subsequently annealed at 1100 
◦
C for 1 
week before being quenched to room temperature. A stable nano -
crystalline structure consisting of tungsten nanograins surrounded by Ti 
segregations at intergranular regions was observed, which was attrib -
uted to the segregation of Ti solute at grain boundaries. Although the 
novel W – Ti alloy system has been reported through the thermodynamic 
approach, there are few relevant detailed studies about fabricating W – Ti 
alloy by laser additive manufacturing have been carried out, and the 
feasibility of the experiment has not been verified. Thus, it is of great 
significance to examine the influence of processing parameters on 
densification behavior, microstructure evolution and mechanical prop -
erties of LPBF-processed W – Ti alloy, which can facilitate its processing 
optimizations and further engineering applications. 
In this work, we added 6 wt% Ti to W powder for mechanical milling, 
and the W – 6Ti samples were successfully fabricated by the optimization 
of parameters using LPBF technique. The effects of processing parame -
ters on the densification, surface morphologies, microstructures, and 
mechanical properties were investigated in detail. A relationship be -
tween the process window and final LPBF-fabricated W – Ti parts was 
established.

The pure W powder (99.9% purity) and pure Ti powder (99.9% 
purity) were served as the raw materials in this study. The spherical

rpm and a milling time of 4 h.

4. Conclusions 
In this study, the densification, surface morphologies, microstruc -
tures, and mechanical properties of the W – 6Ti alloy prepared by LPBF at 
various laser energy densities were characterized and analyzed. The 
main conclusions are summarized as follows: 
(1) The applied laser energy density played an important role in the 
densification behavior of LPBF-processed W-6wt% Ti parts. At 
the suitable energy density of 848 J/mm
3
, pores were almost 
eliminated completely and the highest relative density of 99.1% 
was obtained. The densification decreased at relatively low en -
ergy density, owing to the insufficient melting of W particle and 
limited volume of liquid phase. At a higher energy density of 969 
J/mm
3
, the relative density also decreased, which was caused by 
increased residual thermal stress and evaporation of low melting 
point liquid phase. 
(2) The typical microstructure can be divided into two main regions: 
the W bulk region and the W tungsten dendrite region. As the 
energy density increased, the morphologies of W bulk region 
experienced a change from columnar crystal structure to 
breaking and coarsening of some columnar crystals. EDS results 
indicated that there was a relatively uniform solid solution 
structure in W bulk region. In the W dendrite region, Ti element 
was enriched due to the thermal activation energy is not enough 
to make titanium diffuse into the un-melted tungsten particles. 
The melting mechanism of W – 6Ti system and grain growth 
mechanism for W dendrite region were concluded schematically. 
(3) In the optimal as-fabricated condition with an energy density of 
848 J/mm
3
, the W – 6Ti parts exhibited a considerably high 
microhardness of 731 HV
0.2
, and a great compressive perfor -
mance ( σ
bc 
= 1719 MPa, δ = 24.7%) was obtained in this work. 
Compared with pure tungsten samples of previous work, these 
two properties displayed significant improvement of 54% and 
90% respectively. The strengthening mechanisms can be attrib -
uted to the increased densification level and solid solution 
strengthening.
