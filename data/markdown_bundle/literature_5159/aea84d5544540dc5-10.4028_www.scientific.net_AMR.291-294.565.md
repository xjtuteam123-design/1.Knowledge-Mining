Keywords: Magnesium alloy, lost foam casting (LFC), microstructure, mechanical property 
Abstract. The microstructures and mechanical properties of M g9AlZnGdY alloy after T6 heat 
treatment in lost foam casting (LFC) are compared with that of AZ91D magnesium alloy. The results 
show that the microstructures of Mg9AlZnGdY alloy after T6 heat treatment consist of the α-Mg solid 
solution, β-Mg 17 Al 12 precipitation phase, a small amount of rod Al 2Y and block Al 2Gd phases, which 
are distributed over grain boundaries of the α-Mg p hase. The thermal stability of Al 2Y and Al 2Gd 
phases offers the pinning effect on the grain boundary under high temperature, and therefore, the grain 
boundary sliding is eliminated and the high-tempera ture strength is increased. After the T6 heat 
treatment, the tensile strength of Mg9AlZnGdY alloy was 235MPa at room temperature, and 156MPa 
at 200˚C. Compared with that of AZ91D alloy, the tensile strength was increased by 19.3% and 38.1% 
respectively. 
Introduction 
Lost foam casting (LFC) is a new economical and acc urate casting technology [1], which can be 
extensively used in manufacture of magnesium alloy parts. However, the use of dry sand mold in LFC 
and the broad solidification range of AZ91D magnesium alloy lead to the coarse grain of castings [2]. 
Thus the application of AZ91D magnesium alloy in LFC is limited. 
Some authors added element Y to the AZ91D magnesium alloy [3,4], and this results in the 
refinement of the morphology of β-Mg 
17 Al 12 phase, which was turned into intermittent or granu lar 
forms. Research in recent years [5,6] indicated tha t adding Gd can significantly improve tensile 
properties and the creep resistance of Mg-Y-Zr magnesium alloy under high temperature. In addition, 
Gd can also increase the mechanical properties of M g-2Al-1Zn magnesium alloy under high 
temperature [7]. 
In our earlier experiments, the Mg-9Al-1Zn-0.9Gd-0.5Y alloy was obtained by adding moderate Y 
and Gd to AZ91D magnesium alloy [8]. In this paper, the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
the Mg9AlZnGdY magnesium alloy after T6 heat treatment in LFC are compared with that of AZ91D 
alloy. The benefit of Mg9AlZnGdY magnesium alloy are studied.

Discussion 
The electronegativities of Y and Gd are 1.22 and 1. 20 respectively, while the electronegativities A1 
and Mg are 1.61 and 1.31 respectively. The bigger t he electronegativity difference, the larger the 
binding force between elements. Therefore, Y and Gd prefer to combine with A1, which forms the 
compound of Al-Y / Gd. According to binary phase diagram of Al-Y and Al-Gd [9, 10], the melting 
points A1 
2Y and Al 2Gd are 1485˚C and 1525˚C, respectively. They are compounds of Cu 2Mg form, 
and furthermore, they are stable under high tempera ture. This is consistent with the results of XRD 
and EDS. 
The curves of Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC) of No.1 and No.2 magnesium alloys were 
shown in Fig.4. The fig.4 shows that the precipitation transition temperatures of β-Mg 17 Al 12 phase in 
No.1 and No.2 magnesium alloys are 431.063 ˚C and 429.776 ˚C respectively, and the temperatures 
are almost unchanged. However, the precipitation enthalpy of No. 2 alloy is 9.963J / g, which is less 
than 14.265 J / g of the No.1 alloy by 43.2%. The compounds of Al 2Gd and Al 2Y, which are uniformly 
distributed over the grain boundary, not only obstr uct the diffusion of Al and Mg element, but also 
hinder the precipitation and growth of β-Mg 17 Al 12 phase. The change of the number and morphology 
of β-Mg 17 Al 12 phase results that the β-Mg 17 Al 12 phase is turned from continuous network structure 
into intermittent-like and granular structure. Therefore, the number of precipitation β-Mg 17 Al 12 in No. 
2 alloy after T6 heat treatment is greatly reduced than that of No.1 alloy, as shown in Fig.2(b).

The microhardness of Al 2Y and Al 2Gd phase, which are uniformly distributed on the α-Mg matrix 
grain boundaries, is much higher than that of the precipitation β-Mg 17 Al 12 phase. The Al 2Y and Al 2Gd 
phase effectively hinder the barriers across the gr ain boundaries, preventing grain boundary sliding 
and migration. The β-Mg 17 Al 12 phase is cubic crystal structure, and it is not coherent to α-Mg matrix, 
and it has a poor thermal stability. When the temperature is higher than 125 ˚C, the rapid diffusion of 
Al and Mg results in the coarsening and softening o f β-Mg 17 Al 12 phases, and this leads to the lower 
strength of Mg-Al alloy under high temperature. The melting points of Al 2Y and Al 2Gd compounds 
are as high as 1485 ˚C and 1525 ˚C respectively, w hich is much higher than that (437 ˚C) of the 
β-Mg 17 Al 12 phase. At 200˚C, Al 2Y and Al 2Gd compounds can be used to stabilize the grain 
boundaries and prevent grain boundary sliding, whic h, in turn, significantly improved the thermal

Conclusion 
The microstructures of Mg9AlZnGdY alloy after T6 heat treatment in LFC consist of the α-Mg solid 
solution, β-Mg 
17 Al 12 precipitation phase, a small amount of Al 2Y and Al 2Gd phases. The thermal 
stability of Al 2Y and Al 2Gd phases offers the pinning effect on the grain bo undary under high 
temperature, and therefore, the grain boundary sliding is eliminated and the high-temperature strength 
is increased. After the T6 heat treatment, the tensile strength of Mg9AlZnGdY alloy was 235MPa at 
room temperature, and 156MPa at 200˚C. Compared wit h that of AZ91D alloy, the tensile strength 
was increased by 19.3% and 38.1% respectively.
