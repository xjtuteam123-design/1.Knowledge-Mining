Abstract
Unidirectional solidiﬁcation experiments were performed on the (Nb)/Nb3Si eutectic alloys in the Nb–Si binary and Nb–Ti–Si ternary systems.
The effect of solidiﬁcation rates on microstructure development was studied using an optical ﬂoating zone melting furnace within the solidiﬁcation
rates from 10 to 200 mm/h. For the binary invariant eutectic alloy, a planar eutectic microstructure forms at 10 mm/h, while cellular eutectic
microstructures develop at the higher solidiﬁcation rates. The orientation relationship between Nb and Nb
3Si formed upon planar eutectic solid-
iﬁcation is determined as: {110 }Nb//{110 )Nb3Si, and ⟨112 ⟩Nb//⟨001 ]Nb3Si. For the ternary univariant eutectic alloys, the formation of cellular
eutectic microstructures is prevailing for all over the rates of solidiﬁcation. The growth of Nb 3Si is faster than that of (Nb). The size distribution
of (Nb) particles spreads widely in an asymmetric manner. The cell size variation against solidiﬁcation rates is in a reasonable agreement with the
theoretical prediction.

1. Introduction
There has been increasing demand for the development of
high temperature materials that exhibit superior high tempera-
ture performance to Ni-base superalloys. The major constraints
imposed upon such materials are melting point, oxidation resis-
tance, toughness, and high temperature creep strength. Refrac-
tory metals, and their aluminides and silicides are attractive in
terms of high melting temperatures; however, these materials
in monolithic forms rarely satisfy all the requirements because
of poor oxidation resistance and inadequate creep resistance of
refractory metals, and the brittleness of the aluminides and sili-
cides. Thus, an alloy design to combine these aluminides or
silicides with refractory metals seems to be an effective approach
to strike a balance between low-temperature toughness, oxida-
tion resistance, and high temperature strength. At the same time,

Last decades, alloys based on Nb and Mo silicides have
been extensively studied to develop a new class of heat resis-

control of the lamellar microstructure[12,13], and improves both

Although signiﬁcant improvement in oxidation resistance is still
required [19], this can be modiﬁed by the addition of alloying
elements [6,20], and surface coating [21–23].
For better oxidation resistance and high temperature strength,
Nb–Si alloys with a high volume fraction of silicide phase are
favored. However, such alloys are brittle at room temperature
because of a low volume fraction of the (Nb) phase. It is well
known that the size, morphology, and volume fraction of duc-
tile inclusions have a signiﬁcant impact upon the toughness of
ductile-phase-toughened composites [24,25]. The development
of a microstructure, where columnar or plate-like ductile inclu-
sions are directionally embedded within a brittle matrix, can
induce a frequent occurrence of crack bridging toughening by the
ductile phase, and thereby enhance the toughness of the alloys
[26–28]. Therefore, the control of eutectic microstructures is
beneﬁcial for the toughness improvement of Nb–Si alloys, since
a well aligned (Nb)/Nb
3Si eutectic microstructure can be formed
by applying unidirectional solidiﬁcation technique on the eutec-
tic alloys.
The present paper describes the solidiﬁcation behavior of the
(Nb)/Nb
3Si eutectic alloys in the Nb–Si binary and Nb–Ti–Si
ternary systems. Special emphasis is placed on the effect of
solidiﬁcation rates on microstructure development upon unidi-
rectional solidiﬁcation. The microstructure formation process is
discussed from the instability of solid/liquid interface.
2. Experimental procedures
The compositions of the alloys used in the present study
are Nb– x at%Ti–17.5 at%Si (x = 0, 2, 5, and 10). Rod ingots
for unidirectional solidiﬁcation were preliminary prepared by
arc-melting using high purity raw materials (Nb, Ti > 99.5%,
Si > 99.9%) under an Ar atmosphere. The dimensions of the rod
ingots are 11 mm in diameter and 70 mm in length. Unidirec-
tional solidiﬁcation experiments were performed in an optical
ﬂoating zone (OFZ) melting furnace under an Ar-ﬂowing atmo-
sphere. The rate of solidiﬁcation was controlled by the with-
drawal rates of seed materials ranging from 10 to 200 mm/h.
During unidirectional solidiﬁcation, the amount of ﬂoating liq-
uid was kept constant for all over the rates of solidiﬁcation to
minimize the change in the thermal gradient in liquid phase.
The alloy microstructure was characterized by X-ray diffrac-
tometry, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission
electron microscopy (TEM). The crystallography of the (Nb)
and Nb
3Si phases was studied by TEM using the convergent
beam Kikuchi-line diffraction analysis and the conventional
selected area diffraction technique. Quantitative microstructure
characterization was conducted by using a digital image analysis
software [29].
3. Results and discussions
3.1. Microstructures of unidirectionally solidiﬁed alloys

general trend, the alloy microstructure becomes ﬁner as the
solidiﬁcation rate increases. The alloys solidiﬁed at the rates

shows the development of continuous (Nb) plates within the

(Nb) and Nb3Si has attained at this solidiﬁcation rate. Since few
distinct intercellular products were observed in the solidiﬁca-

attained at this solidiﬁcation rate.

the development of cellular eutectic microstructures for all over
the rates of solidiﬁcation, although slower solidiﬁcation rates
tend to provide increased size and better alignment for (Nb).

Generally there are two types of instability that can develop at
the solid/liquid interface upon eutectic solidiﬁcation: one-phase
instability and two-phase instability [30]. One-phase instability
is a breakdown in the diffusion coupled growth of the two phases,
which leads to the formation of dendrites or discontinuous par-
ticles in either phase of the eutectic. Two-phase instability is the
development of an undulate solidiﬁcation front, which results in
the formation of a cellular or dendritic eutectic microstructure.
In the present study, both types of the instability are found to
develop in the binary alloys solidiﬁed at the higher rates and in
the ternary alloys; the alloys show cellular eutectic microstruc-
tures, and discontinuous (Nb) particles form in the Nb
3Si matrix.
It is well known that two-phase instability is often promoted by
ternary additions or the presence of impurities [30–32]. Thus,
one of the reasons why the binary invariant eutectic alloy exhibits
the development of cellular eutectic microstructures at the higher
solidiﬁcation rates would be the presence of impurities that are
from raw materials or introduced during solidiﬁcation. When a
ternary element or impurity is present, the solidiﬁcation occurs
at a L/(Nb)/Nb
3Si three-phase ﬁeld that exists underneath the
eutectic groove, accompanied by the ternary element built-up
along the direction normal to the solid/liquid interface. This
solute built-up can induce the Mullins–Sekerka type instability
[33] at the solid/liquid interface, as in dilute single phase alloys.
Several studies have shown that the constitutional supercooling
criterion [34] is applicable to explain the transition from planar
to cellular eutectic growth in univariant eutectic alloys[35–37].
This indicates that planar eutectic solidiﬁcation can be attained
in univariant eutectic alloys when a G/V ratio is high enough to
overcome the constitutional supercooling formed ahead of the
solid/liquid interface, where G is the thermal gradient in liquid
phase and V the rate of solidiﬁcation. In the present study, pla-
nar solidiﬁcation is found to be attained by lowering the rate of
solidiﬁcation to 10 mm/h in the binary alloy. On the other hand,
much larger G/V ratio is required for the ternary alloys to attain
planar eutectic solidiﬁcation.
As to the formation of discontinuous (Nb) particles, a large
deference in the growth velocities of the two phases is mainly
responsible. The development of discontinuous (Nb) particles
indicates that Nb
3Si grows faster than (Nb) at the given under-
cooling, so that (Nb) particles are cut off repeatedly from access
to liquid during solidiﬁcation. This fact implies that the coupled
growth of the two phases is attained in the binary alloy solidi-
ﬁed at 10 mm/h, because the solidiﬁcation rate is slow enough
to inhibit the precedent growth of the Nb
3Si phase. In this con-
text, the coupled growth of the two phase could also be attained
in the ternary alloys by employing a larger G/V ratio than that
in the present study. It is worthwhile to mention that the differ-
ence in the growth velocities of the two phases can be modiﬁed,
to a certain extent, by controlling the alloy chemistry [30].I ti s
therefore possible that the optimization of the alloy composition
facilitates the achievement of coupled growth of the two phases
in this eutectic system.

3.2.2. Size distribution of (Nb) in a planar eutectic
microstructure
As depicted inFig. 1, a planar eutectic microstructure exhibits
the development of plate-shaped (Nb) within the Nb
3Si matrix.
Hereafter, the size of the (Nb) plates is characterized with respect
to the lengths of the long and short interfaces that appear in the
transversely sectioned microstructures. Fig. 5 shows the dis-
tributions of the lengths of the long and short interfaces. The
length of short interfaces appears to follow a near-Gaussian dis-
tribution, exhibiting a relatively sharp peak. On the other hand,
the length of the long interfaces shows a multimodal distribu-
tion. One of the possible reasons for this multimodal distribution
would be the branching of (Nb) plates during solidiﬁcation. It
has been suggested in Fe–C eutectic alloys that the branching
graphite plates occur by the neighboring austenite plates to over-
grow the graphite plates in order to cancel the instability at the
groove [39]. In the present study, the growth of Nb
3Si is found
to be faster than that of (Nb) at higher solidiﬁcation rates. It is
therefore speculated that branching of (Nb) plates can take place
in a similar manner even at the solidiﬁcation rate of 10 mm/h.
Another possible reason is the scattering in the OR between (Nb)
and Nb
3Si, as mentioned in the previous section. Some (Nb)
plates may possess an OR that allows to form low energy faceted
interfaces with the Nb
3Si matrix, which yields the formation of
plate-shaped morphology for (Nb), but the others may not, which
results in a rod-shaped morphology. It should be noted that the
thermal gradient in liquid phase (G) has more pronounced effect
on the regularity of eutectic microstructure, in terms of lamellar
faults and defect distribution, than the rate of solidiﬁcation ( V)
even at the same G/V ratio [40,41]. Therefore, the regularity of
the eutectic microstructure can be modiﬁed by controlling the
thermal gradient in liquid phase.

3.3. Characterization of the cellular eutectic microstructure
3.3.1. Spatial variations of (Nb) in cellular eutectic alloys
Contrary to the planar eutectic alloy, the cellular eutectic
alloys exhibit the development of discontinuous (Nb) particles
within the Nb
3Si matrix. The (Nb) particles exhibit irregular
polygonal shapes on the transversely sectioned micrographs. In
the present study, we deﬁned the size of (Nb) as the diameter
of the circle having the same cross section as the (Nb) particle
appeared on the transverse sections. The inter-particle spacing
is deﬁned as the distance between the centers of the neighboring
(Nb) particles.
Fig. 6shows a typical distribution in the size of (Nb) particles
formed in a cellular eutectic alloy. The distribution in the size
of (Nb) particles spreads widely in an asymmetric manner. This
trend is derived from the cellular characteristics of microstruc-
tures, where relatively ﬁne (Nb) particles are formed at the cell
interior, whereas coarse (Nb) particles are formed near the inter-
cellular boundaries. Quantitative analyses on the size of (Nb) and
inter (Nb) particle spacing are conducted in the cellular eutectic
microstructures. Fig. 7 shows the full-logarithmic plots against
solidiﬁcation rates for the average size of (Nb) particles, and
the average inter (Nb) particle spacing. These average values
are found to increase with the decrease of the solidiﬁcation rate,
and the increase of the amount of Ti additions. For the average

hereafter, is deﬁned as the diagonal length of the hexagon, which

shows the full logarithmic plots of the average cell size against
the solidiﬁcation rates. The average cell size increases with the

additions. It has been shown that the variation of the cell sizes in

the same trend as in the cell spacing in a single-phase alloy[42].

4. Conclusions
Unidirectional solidiﬁcation experiments were conducted on
the (Nb)/Nb
3Si eutectic alloys in the Nb–Si binary and Nb–Ti–Si
ternary systems. In the binary Nb–17.5Si alloy, planar solid-
iﬁcation is attained by lowering the solidiﬁcation rate down
to 10 mm/h, whereas cellular eutectic microstructures develop
in the alloys solidiﬁed at the rates over 30 mm/h. Upon pla-
nar eutectic solidiﬁcation (Nb) and Nb
3Si satisfy the orientation
relationship: {110 }Nb//{110 )Nb3Si and ⟨112 ⟩Nb//<0 0 1]Nb3Si.
Multimodal distribution is observed for the length of the long
interface of the (Nb) plates. The ternary alloys show cellular
eutectic microstructures regardless of the solidiﬁcation rates
examined in the present study. The coupled growth of (Nb) and
Nb
3Si was not attained in the ternary univariant eutectic alloys at
the present experimental conditions. The average size and inter-
particle spacing of (Nb) particles are found to increase with the
increase of Ti additions, and with the decrease of the solidiﬁca-
tion rates. The trend of cell size variation against Ti contents and
growth rates is in a reasonable agreement with that of the pri-
mary cell spacing in a single-phase alloy. The controls of alloy
compositions and the thermal gradient in liquid phase would
be required for further modiﬁcation in solidiﬁcation microstruc-
tures.
