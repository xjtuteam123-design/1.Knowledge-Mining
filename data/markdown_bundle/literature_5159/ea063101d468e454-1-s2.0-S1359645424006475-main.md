ABSTRACT
The mechanical properties of the bcc refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) NbMoTaW with columnar and 
equiaxed microstructures and a nanoscale metal oxide layer on the grain boundaries are investigated at ambient 
temperatures (RT) to 1200 
◦
C. Under compression, the alloy shows a yield strength, σ
y
, of ⁓1390 ± 20 MPa at 
RT and retains a high yield strength, σ
y
, of ⁓301.5 MPa at 1200 
◦
C. However, in tension, the fracture strengths, 
σ
f
, of both columnar and equiaxed microstructures are far lower - below 70 MPa - and the material fails in the 
elastic regime without showing any measurable ductility at all temperatures. The fracture mechanisms in tension 
transition from transgranular cleavage at RT to a mixture of transgranular and intergranular fracture at 800 
◦
C to 
a complete intergranular fracture at 1200 
◦
C due to selective weakening of the metal oxide layer on the grain 
boundaries driven by a structural change. The transition in the fracture mechanism in the equiaxed micro -
structure promotes extrinsic toughening at higher temperatures, resulting in a ⁓6.4 times higher fracture 
toughness ( K
Ic 
⁓10.3 MPa √ m) at 1200 
◦
C compared to that at RT. NbMoTaW is a well-known RHEA, but the 
poor tensile strength and fracture toughness measured in this study highlight the critical importance of inves -
tigating the mechanical properties of these high-temperature RHEAs in tension.
1. Introduction
The discovery of structural materials that can withstand higher 
stresses at higher temperatures yet still survive corrosive environments 
is necessary for aerospace applications, hypersonic vehicles, and turbine 
engines with increased energy efficiency [ 1 , 2 ]. Refractory high-entropy 
alloys (RHEAs), a subset of multi-principal element alloys, are poten -
tially suitable for these applications because of their superior strength 
retention under compression up to 1800 
◦
C [ 3 – 6 ]. NbMoTaW, a popular 
body-centered cubic ( bcc ) equiatomic RHEA, retains a yield strength, σ
y
, 
of ~400 MPa at temperatures up to 1600 
◦
C, which is higher than most 
RHEAs and Ni-based superalloys [ 7 – 9 ]. While the mechanical properties 
of NbMoTaW under compression are exceptional, its tensile properties 
have not been investigated, particularly at higher temperatures, which is 
extremely important to evaluate their suitability for safety-critical 
structural applications. NbMoTaW has a very high melting point and 
ductile-to-brittle transition temperature, posing difficulty in processing 
large samples [ 4 ]. For this reason, the fracture resistance of only 
nanocrystalline, single-crystal, and bi-crystal NbMoTaW has been 
investigated (no bulk-scale polycrystalline NbMoTaW has ever been 
tested for fracture). The nano-scale and single/bicrystal alloys show a 
fracture toughness, K
Ic
, of ~3, 2, and 0.2 MPa √ m, respectively, abysmal 
compared to K
Ic 
~75 – 200 MP √ m of structural materials used in 
safety-critical applications [ 2 , 10 , 11 ]. The poor fracture resistance of 
NbMoTaW under tension primarily originates from two factors — (i) 
grain boundary decohesion induced by interstitial segregation [ 12 – 14 ], 
and (ii) the alloy ’ s failure to satisfy the condition of intrinsic cleavability 
( i.e., in principle, it is more favorable for the crack to cleave than to emit

compositional modifications will also affect the high-temperature 
strength of the RHEA. Therefore, exploring alternate mechanisms of 
toughening NbMoTaW is essential. For example, in ceramics and dilute 
group VI (Cr, Mo, W) refractory alloys, where mobile dislocation activity 
is essentially non-existent, extrinsic toughening can improve fracture 
resistance through crack bridging or deflection [ 17 ]. Extrinsic tough -

direction, i.e. , effectively reducing the driving force for crack growth 
[ 18 ].
To address these challenges, in situ heat treatment during the solid -

◦
C were used to 
produce bulk polycrystalline NbMoTaW blocks with equiaxed and 
columnar microstructures. During the process, a nanoscale layer of 
metal oxide forms on the grain boundaries of the alloy, which provides 
an opportunity to investigate the following aspects of this RHEA: (i) the 
microstructure, mechanical properties, and deformation mechanisms of 
NbMoTaW in both tension and compression at temperatures of ambient 
(RT) to 1200 
◦

boundaries and, in turn, their role in the prevailing deformation 
mechanisms, and (iii) the possibility of extrinsically toughening the 
NbMoTaW RHEA if the formation of nanoscale metal oxide layer pref -
erentially weakens the grain boundaries, and the role of columnar and 
equiaxed microstructure in such extrinsic toughening.
2. Materials and experiments
2.1. Materials
The nominally equiatomic NbMoTaW alloy was produced by arc 
melting commercially pure (purity > 99.95 %) Nb, Mo, Ta, and W slugs 
and pieces (Fig. S1a in the Supplementary Material). The arc melting 
process was conducted in a water-cooled Cu crucible placed in a vacuum 
chamber back-filled with Ar gas to introduce an inert atmosphere. The 
buttons were remelted ten times to achieve a homogeneous composi -
tion. During the final remelting process, the melting current was grad -
ually reduced from full power to 60 % at a 30-second interval, 
maintaining the top half of the ingot in the liquid form to heat treat the 
bottom half of the solidified ingot. In situ heat treatment process was 
employed to promote recrystallization in the bottom half of the ingot 
and achieve a near-equiaxed microstructure. Following solidification, 
the buttons were hot isostatically pressed (HIP) at 1850 
◦
C for 4 hr in an 
Ar atmosphere (Fig. S1b). Small pieces of Hf were kept inside the furnace 
to reduce the oxygen available for oxidation during the HIP process. 
However, even after the HIP treatment ( Fig. 1a ), the microstructure did 
not recrystallize and consisted of columnar and equiaxed regions like a 
cast material (Fig. S2). The casting voids were predominantly present in 
the top region of the buttons and persisted even after the HIP treatment. 
Accordingly, the specimens for mechanical testing were machined from 
the equiaxed region, as illustrated in Fig. 1a . However, some machined 
specimens from the equiaxed region still had a columnar microstructure, 
albeit of relatively smaller size, providing an opportunity to investigate 
the mechanical properties of both columnar and equiaxed 
microstructures.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure
Fig. 1a shows the initial microstructure of the cross-section of an 
NbMoTaW button after HIP at 1850 
◦
C. The dashed lines segregate the 
regions with casting voids, columnar grains, equiaxed grains, and the 
heat-affected zone. The presence of these regions in the microstructure 
implies that the HIP process at 1850 
◦
C could not fully recrystallize/ 
homogenize the microstructure or eliminate the casting voids (as inci -
dentally can be a problem with many high-temperature RHEAs). How -
ever, a small region of equiaxed microstructure formed in the bottom 
half of the buttons through recrystallization promoted by the in situ heat 
treatment employed during the remelting process (Fig. S2). An electron 
backscatter diffraction (EBSD) inverse pole figure (IPF) map ( Fig. 1b ) of 
this region shows a bimodal distribution of grains with an average size of 
⁓ 105 ± 66 µ m and smaller recrystallized grains of size ~37 ± 15 µ m on 
the grain-boundary triple points. The majority of these grains (⁓63 %) 
have high-angle grain boundaries ( > 15
◦
), which is also a sign of a 
recrystallized microstructure.
X-ray diffraction maps in Fig. 1c indicate that the as-cast and the HIP 
microstructures consist of a single bcc phase; however, a closer investi -
gation with back-scatter electron (BSE) detection ( Fig. 2b ) shows a 
secondary phase of thickness ⁓66 ± 34 nm formed on the grain 
boundaries of the HIP microstructure. The secondary grain-boundary 
phase was not present in the as-cast microstructure ( Fig. 2a ). An en -
ergy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) line scan across the grain-boundary 
phase ( Fig. 2c , d ) reveals significantly higher O content and the deple -
tion of Mo, Ta, and W elements. The grain-boundary phase consists of Nb 
(~20 – 23 at.%) and O (⁓ 62 – 64 at.%) with a small amount of other 
metallic constituents, implying the formation of a metal oxide complex 
with Nb as the primary constituent [ 22 , 23 ]. Some of these grain 
boundary oxide complexes have Ta as the primary constituent, indi -
cating that oxides of different stoichiometry precipitate along the grain 
boundaries, but mainly Nb and Ta as the primary constituents (Fig. S4 
and S5). The morphology of the nanoscale metal oxide layer on the grain 
boundaries changes from an oval shape to a plate type for different grain 
boundaries, with a thickness that varies between 20 and 60 nm for the 
plate shape to ~124 ± 51 nm (major axis) for the oval shape ( Fig. 2c and 
Fig. S4a). They are largest at the grain-boundary triple points. Metal 
oxides were present on the grain boundaries of all four different buttons 
investigated during the microstructural characterization. The formation 
of the Nb/Ta-rich oxide is primarily controlled by temperature, which 
enables a faster diffusion of O [ 24 ]; therefore, the nanoscale metal oxide 
layer is likely to have formed during the cooling process after the HIP 
treatment, where the exposure to ~1850 
◦
C for 4 hr facilitated the 
diffusion of a large amount of oxygen to the grain boundaries. Such 
interstitial segregations onto the grain boundaries have been primarily 
reported for RHEAs in the as-cast condition [ 3 , 14 ]. Moreover, the dif -
ference in the intensity of the X-ray diffraction peaks is related to the 
texture of the columnar grains in the upper section (columnar region) of 
the buttons ( Fig. 1a ).

4.2. Fracture resistance
In most instances, NbMoTaW fractures intergranularly when loaded 
in tension at RT, primarily because of grain-boundary decohesion pro -
moted by interstitial segregation [ 12 – 14 , 32 ]. Comparatively, the pre -
sent microstructure fails transgranularly at RT because of the metal 
oxide ’ s strong bonding to the grain boundaries; specifically, the 
oval-shaped, grain-boundary oxide particles provide weak points for 
crack initiation for the brittle transgranular fracture ( Fig. 6 ). Even as the 
strong bonding at the grain boundaries promotes transgranular fracture, 
the tensile fracture strength at RT is 96 % lower compared to the 
compressive yield strength. Such a significant difference in the proper -
ties with respect to loading directions highlights the importance of 
evaluating the tensile properties of RHEAs. With increasing tempera -
ture, the compositional and structural changes in the metal oxide pro -
gressively weaken the bonding at the grain boundary, which during 
tensile loading results in a transition from transgranular fracture at RT to 
a mixture of transgranular and intergranular fracture at 800 
◦
C to a 
complete intergranular fracture at 1200 
◦
C ( Fig. 4 ). Regardless of the 
transition in the fracture mode, the material fails without showing any 
plasticity under tension at all temperatures. This is because, under 
tensile loading, the tolerance to defects in the microstructures is essen -
tially non-existent, unlike compression, where the loading direction 
works to close the crack or mask the weak links of the microstructure to 
resist fracture. These results highlight that the plastic activity observed 
under compression will not translate to ductility under tension unless all 
the weak links of the microstructure are eliminated, or the RHEAs are 
intrinsically/extrinsically toughened.
The NbMoTaW microstructure with a metal oxide layer on the grain 
boundaries shows a transition in the fracture mechanism from trans -
granular to intergranular during the fracture toughness measurements at 
RT to 1200 
◦
C. At RT, similar transgranular fracture behavior is observed 
in the binary W-V and W-Ta alloys, where their K
Ic 
values are 2 – 3 times 
higher when the crack-growth direction is perpendicular to the 
columnar grains [ 26 ]. Interestingly, a different trend is observed in the 
case of NbMoTaW, i.e. , the K
Ic 
of the columnar microstructure is nearly 
three times higher than that of the equiaxed microstructure. Conversely, 
at 1200 
◦
C, the K
Ic 
of the equiaxed microstructure is ~6.4 times higher 
compared to that of columnar microstructure. In the case of trans -
granular cleavage fracture, the fracture process is invariably locally 
stress-controlled, i.e. , according to the Ritchie-Knott-Rice (RKR) model, 
at a specific characteristic microstructural distance ahead of the crack 
tip (typically one to two times the grain size, dependent on the highest 
local probability of a crack-initiating event), when the distribution of 
stresses ahead of the crack exceeds the fracture strength of a weak link in 
the microstructure [ 18 , 35 ]. This process is stochastic as the stresses peak 
very close to the crack tip and decay with increasing distance, so the 
lower probability of finding a weak link of the microstructure near the 
crack tip affects the fracture resistance [ 18 ]. The NbMoTaW micro -
structure fractures transgranularly by crack initiation from the metal 
oxide phase on the grain boundaries ( Fig. 6a ). Here, the probability of 
finding the grain boundaries with metal oxides ahead of the crack tip is 
much lower in the case of the columnar microstructure compared to the 
equiaxed microstructure. Therefore, the fracture resistance of the 
columnar microstructure is nearly three times higher than the latter. 
With increasing temperature, as discussed in the previous section, 
intergranular fracture is preferred due to compositional and structural 
changes in the metal oxide phase on the grain boundaries. During 
intergranular fracture, the crack deviates away from the mode-I direc -
tion of the crack growth, effectively reducing the driving force at the 
crack tip, which promotes extrinsic toughening from crack deflection 
[ 18 ]. When the columnar grains are nearly aligned (27 ± 11
◦

direction of the crack growth, the fracture toughness, K
Ic

MPa √ m (Fig. S11c), which increases to ⁓5 MPa √ m when the columnar 
grains are at an angle of ⁓56 ± 10

equiaxed microstructure compared to columnar microstructure result -
ing in a ⁓6.4 times higher K

indicate that while it is possible to extrinsically toughen NbMoTaW with 
a nanoscale metal oxide layer on the grain boundaries, the fracture 
toughness is still extremely low and naturally strongly dependent on the 
microstructure in the vicinity of the crack tip.
5. Summary and conclusions
In summary, the mechanical properties of a NbMoTaW refractory 
high-entropy alloy with a nanoscale metal oxide layer on the grain 
boundaries have been investigated at room temperature to 1200 
◦
C. The 
strength retention of NbMoTaW under compression is unaffected up to 
1200 
◦
C. Nevertheless, the fracture strength, σ
f
, in tension is extremely 
low, with both columnar and equiaxed microstructure fractures showing 
no ductility at all temperatures. The metal oxides on the grain bound -
aries promote transgranular fracture at RT, indicating a stronger 
bonding by the oxides that resist grain-boundary decohesion. But even 
at RT, there is a stark difference in strength and plasticity under 
compression and tension. The nanoscale metal oxide layer on the grain 
boundaries goes through a compositional and polymorphic transition at 
temperatures > 800 
◦
C, which in turn progressively weakens the grain- 
boundary strength, resulting in a transition from transgranular frac -
ture at ambient temperature to intergranular fracture at 1200 
◦
C. Based 
on these results, the following conclusions can be drawn.
(i) The progressive weakening of the grain boundaries promotes 
extrinsic toughening at higher temperatures by generating tor -
tuosity of the crack path, although the fracture resistance is still 
strongly dependent on the microstructure ahead of the crack tip.
(ii) An interesting transition in the fracture resistance is observed 
where the columnar microstructure shows higher fracture 
toughness than the equiaxed microstructure during transgranular 
fracture. In contrast, for intergranular fracture, the equiaxed 
microstructure shows relatively higher fracture toughness than 
the columnar microstructure.
(iii) For a potential structural material, the fracture resistance of
