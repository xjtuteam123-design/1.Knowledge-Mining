Hot corrosion behaviour of D-gun sprayed
NiCoCrAlYTa coated superalloys at 900 uCi n
molten salt environment
S. Kamal, R. Jayaganthan * and S. Prakash
Detonation-gun sprayed NiCoCrAlYTa coatings deposited on superalloys have been investigated
for their hot corrosion behaviour at 900 uC in molten salt environment under cyclic conditions. The
weight change measurements made on the bare and coated superalloys were used to determine
the kinetics of hot corrosion. X-ray diffraction, X-ray mapping and field emission scanning electron
microscope/EDS were used to characterise the corroded products of the coated and bare
superalloys and substantiate the corrosion mechanisms. The formation of scales such as NiCr 2O4,
NiAl2O4 and Al 2O3 on the coated superalloys provided a higher hot corrosion resistance as
observed in the present work.
Keywords: D-gun spray, Hot corrosion, Microstructural characterisation
Introduction
Ni based superalloys are used extensively for the manu-
facture of highly stressed components in the gas turbine
engine, such as turbine blades, turbine discs, and com-
bustion chambers.
1 Also they are widely used in both
aviation and land based gas turbine environments and
are the subject of many studies to improve their micro-
structural properties and life times, due to the harsh
environments in which they are used.
2 Structural alloys
have thus been developed for optimal mechanical
properties
3 and coatings have been developed to provide
the environmental resistance, which includes diffusion
coatings, overlay coatings and thermal barrier coatings. 4
Hot corrosion is the degradation of high temperature
materials in the presence of molten sodium sulphate, the
source of sodium sulphate could be the ingested air or
impurities in fuel cells, hot corrosion has detrimental
effects on the mechanical integrity of the alloys, and
excessive attacks could result in catastrophic failures.
5
Increased temperatures also have an adverse effect
leading towards hot corrosion.
6 NiCoCrAlY alloys are
widely used for high temperature applications due to
their outstanding property proﬁle with good ductility,
high strength and excellent oxidation resistance. 7–10
MCrAlYs are among the most important protective
coating materials applied to counteract hot corrosion
and high temperature oxidation, like any coating
designed to resist oxidative environments at high
temperatures, MCrAlYs should be capable of develop-
ing a thermodynamically stable, slow growing and
adherent surface oxide layer.
11 Recent studies indicated
that the detonation-gun (D-gun) sprayed MCrAlY
coating showed excellent oxidation resistance due to
the formation of a dense and adherent alumina scale on
the coating.
12,13 Detonation-gun spray process is one of
the well established thermal spray techniques used for
the development of wear and corrosion resistant coat-
ings. The D-gun minimises decomposition of the feed
powder, due to a lower heat enthalpy, high velocity, and
shorter duration involved in the deposition as compared
to plasma and high velocity oxygen fuel thermal spray
process. The higher particle velocity during deposition
provides the resulting coatings several advantages such
as lower porosity, good adhesion between the substrate
and coating, higher microhardness, bond strength and
lower oxidation of in-ﬂight particles.
14 Various research-
ers have suggested the use of MCrAlY (M is Ni and/or
Co) overlays and Pt aluminide diffusion coatings as
typical bond coats to protect the superalloy against
oxidation and to reduce the mismatch stresses between
the top coat and the superalloy.
15–20 NiCoCrAlYTa
overlay coatings provide excellent oxidation and corro-
sion resistance at high temperature, and are potential
bond coats for thermal barrier coatings on superalloy
substrates. The corrosion process can be hindered due to
the very slow diffusion rate of oxygen and sulphur into
the coating from the molten salt, consequently, the
base alloys are protected from hot corrosion attack.
21
The hot corrosion behaviour of D-gun sprayed
NiCoCrAlYTa coatings has not been subjected to a
detailed so far in the literature. Owing to the better
properties of D-gun sprayed coatings as discussed in the
earlier work,
22 the present work has been focused to
study the hot corrosion behaviour of NiCoCrAlYTa
coatings deposited on superalloys (namely Superni 75,
Superni 718 and Superfer 800H) exposed to molten salt
(Na
2SO4–60%V2O5) at 900 uC. The corroded products
were analysed by XRD, ﬁeld emission scanning electron

Experimental procedure
Development of coatings
Samples and coating powders

718 and Superfer 800H were used as the substrates,

The nominal compositions of the superalloys are
reported in earlier publications.

Conclusions
D-gun spraying process has been successfully used to
deposit NiCoCrAlYTa coating on superalloys namely
Superni 75, Superni 718 and Superfer 800H.
1. A compact and dense continuous oxide scale
developed on all the coated superalloys.
2. Coated Superfer 800H showed the lowest weight
gain, where as bare Superfer 800H showed highest
weight gain.
3. The phases detected at the surface of the coated
superalloys with the XRD analysis are NiCr 2O4,
NiAl2O4,F e 2O3, SiO 2, NiO, Cr 2O3 and Al 2O3.
4. The elemental mapping for silicon and iron showed
outward diffusion from the substrate to the coating, the
diffusion seems to have almost reached the top surface.
5. Severe hot corrosion suffered by bare Superni 718
may be due the presence of Mo (3 ?05%) in the alloy.
6. The NiCoCrAlYTa coating provides the maximum
hot corrosion resistance to Superfer 800H and has been
found successful in reducing the weight gain by y37%.
7. The formation of oxides along the splat boundaries
and within open pores of the coatings might have acted
as diffusion barrier to the inward diffusion of corroding
species.
