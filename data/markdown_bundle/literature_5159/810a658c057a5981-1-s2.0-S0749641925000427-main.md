ABSTRACT
To meeting the double demands of structural weight reduction and performance improvement of
aerospace vehicle, conventional high-temperature titanium alloys or titanium matrix composites
(TMCs) are encountering a huge challenge that the room-temperature ductility will be inevitably
deteriorated in pursuit of enhancing the elevated high-temperature strength. The present work
proposes a feasible strategy for resolving this contradiction by constructing a novel bimodal ar-

1. Introduction
The burgeoning development of the aerospace industry necessitates the development of diminishing the overall self-weight of
aircraft to augment energy economy ( Khan et al., 2012 ; Ming et al., 2019 ; Williams and Starke, 2003 ; Zhang et al., 2024a , b ).
High-temperature titanium alloys are considered promising candidates owing to their superior specific strength and thermal stability
( Banerjee and Williams, 2013 ). However, the maximum service temperature of high temperature titanium alloys is presently limited to
600
◦
C and their mechanical properties are difficult to be further improved by alloying ( Huang et al., 2021 ). Fortunately, introducing
the reinforcements (e.g. TiB, TiC, etc.) into titanium alloys to produce titanium matrix composites (TMCs) have been proven to be an
effective approach for enhancing the strength at room and elevated temperatures ( Huang et al., 2015 ; Luo et al., 2019 ), unfortunately,
which will result in the inevitable deterioration of the room temperature ductility, namely, the strength-ductility trade-off dilemma
( Ritchie, 2011 ; Wang et al., 2015 ). To avert catastrophic failure in mechanical components or structures, the total elongation to
rupture typically must be at least 5 %, and preferably close to or above 10 % ( Luo et al., 2019 ; Cai et al., 2019 ; Zhu and Wu, 2018 ).
Therefore, achieving sufficient room temperature ductility in pursuit of significantly improving the room and elevated temperature
strength remains a formidable challenge for exploring the high-performance TMCs. Inspired by the high-performance multiscale ar-
chitectures of natural biological materials (e.g. bone, nacre, bamboo, spider silk, etc.), a great deal of researches has focused on
tailoring the spatial distribution of the reinforcements within TMCs to achieve a harmonious balance between exceptional strength and
reasonable ductility ( Chen et al., 2022 ; Lanba et al., 2024 ; Wu et al., 2022 ; Wegst et al., 2015 ). For instance, a novel fiber-like structure
is achieved by tailoring the spatial distribution of TiB whiskers, enabling the fabrication of high-strength TiB/Ti composites without
reduction in ductility ( Wu et al., 2022 ). Furthermore, by introducing the multiscale reinforcements of TiB nano-fibers and La
2
O
3
nano
particles and regulating their spatial distributions, the (TiB + La
2
O
3
)/Ti hybrid-reinforced composite with an ultra-fine network is
achieved and exhibits the enhanced high-temperature strength with minimal ductility loss ( Li et al., 2022 ).
However, usually, the prerequisite for elevating strength-ductility synergy of TMCs by tailoring the spatial distribution of the
reinforcements, is that the volume fraction of the reinforcements should be below 5 %, and the promoting effect of this method remains
limited. Actually, in addition to the reinforcements, the titanium alloy matrixes have a more positive effect on the mechanical
properties ’ improvement of TMCs ( Gu et al., 2023 ; Chen et al., 2022 ; Ma and Zhu, 2017 ; Zhu et al., 2019 ; Huang et al., 2021 ; L. Liu
et al., 2023 ). However, it does not be currently paid enough attention to simultaneously improving the strength and ductility of TMCs
by designing and controlling the microstructure of titanium alloy matrixes. While for titanium alloys, it has been proved that the
simultaneous improvement in strength and ductility can be achieved by constructing the multiscale heterogeneous structures, e.g.
ultrafine/coarse-grain harmonic structure ( Wu et al., 2015 ) and hierarchical nanostructure ( Devaraj et al., 2016 ; Ding et al., 2023 ),
