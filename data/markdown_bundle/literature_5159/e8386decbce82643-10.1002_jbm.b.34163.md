Effects of elastic intramedullary nails composed of low Young’s
modulus Ti-Nb-Sn alloy on healing of tibial osteotomies in rabbits
Atsushi Kogure,1 Yu Mori,1 Hidetatsu Tanaka,1 Masayuki Kamimura,1 Naoya Masahashi,2
Shuji Hanada,2 Eiji Itoi1
1Department of Orthopaedic Surgery, Tohoku University Graduate School of Medicine, Sendai, Japan

Published online 00 Month 2018 in Wiley Online Library (wileyonlinelibrary.com). DOI: 10.1002/jbm.b.34163
Abstract: Intramedullary nailing is widely performed for inter-
nal fixation of fractures. The applicable elasticity of materials
composing intramedullary nails remains unclear. The present
study aimed to evaluate the effects of the elastic property of
b-type titanium alloy nails on fracture healing compared with
conventional Ti-6Al-4V alloy nails using a rabbit tibial osteot-
omy model. Two types of intramedullary nails composed of
b-type Ti-Nb-Sn alloy (Young’s modulus: 37 GPa) or Ti-6Al-
4V alloy (Young’s modulus: 110 GPa) were used for osteot-
omy fixation in the tibiae of rabbits. At 4, 8, and 16 weeks
postoperatively, microcomputed tomography (micro-CT) and
three-point bending tests were performed. Micro-CT images
showed that the callus volume was significantly larger in the
Ti-Nb-Sn alloy group at 4 and 8 weeks. The callus bone
mineral density did not differ at each time point. In mechani-
cal testing, the maximum load was significantly higher at all
time points in the Ti-Nb-Sn alloy group. Taken together, the
elastic intramedullary nails composed of Ti-Nb-Sn alloy
improved the mechanical properties of the bone healing site
from the early phase to the remodeling phase. Adequate
Young’s modulus of the Ti-Nb-Sn alloy enhanced fracture
union and bone strength restoration. The Ti-Nb-Sn alloy is a
promising biomaterial for fracture fixation devices.

INTRODUCTION
Intramedullary nailing is widely performed for internal ﬁxa-
tion of fractures in trauma care. 1,2 Properly inserted nails
prevent shearing and rotational motion, while allowing
transmission of bending force on the fracture site, thus lead-
ing to compression on one side combined with distraction
on the opposing side.
3–5 These axial forces have positive
effects on fracture healing. 6–8 It is also known that an ade-
quate amount of axial motion accelerates bone healing,
while excessive axial motion or shearing and rotational
motion has negative effects on callus bridging despite the
presence of a large callus.
3,9–12 However, the applicable elas-
ticity of materials composing intramedullary nails to induce
an adequate amount of axial motion remains unclear. 13,14
Stainless steel and titanium alloys are the major materi-
als used for current orthopedic implants. 15 In particular, the
use of titanium alloys has been increasing because of their
appropriate mechanical properties, excellent biocompatibil-
ity, and good corrosion resistance. 16,17 Ti-6Al-4V alloy
(a1b type titanium alloy), as the most commonly used bio-
medical titanium alloy, has a lower Young’s modulus (110
GPa) than Co-Cr-Mo alloy (230 GPa) and stainless steel
(205 GPa). 18 However, its Young’s modulus is still much
higher than that of human cortical bone (11–20 GPa). 19
Stems for cementless total hip arthroplasty composed of
alloy materials with high Young’s modulus sometimes cause
stress shielding in the femur and thigh pain because of the
discrepancy in Young’s modulus between the cortical bone
and the metal prosthesis. 20,21 Fracture ﬁxation devices with
too much rigidity are also known to cause problems, such
as suppressed callus formation and non-union. 22,23
To resolve these adverse effects of Ti-6Al-4V alloy on
bone, b-type titanium alloys with much lower Young’s mod-
uli than Ti-6Al-4V have been developed, and good biocom-
patibilities have been reported. 24–27 Our research group
have developed a new b-type Ti-Nb-Sn alloy with a lower
Young’s modulus about 40 GPa. 28,29 We previously reported
the biomedical features of this alloy. The Ti-Nb-Sn alloy is
as safe as commercially pure titanium and has good biocom-
patibility with bone. 30 Surface modiﬁcation with anodic oxi-
dation can add a photocatalytic sterilization function or the
ability for hydroxyapatite formation to induce good bonding

The unique elastic property of our Ti-Nb-Sn alloy might
induce an adequate amount of axial motion on the fracture
site and enhance bone healing. The purpose of the present
study was to investigate differences in the bone healing pro-
cess and bone strength for osteotomized tibiae in rabbits
ﬁxed with intramedullary nails made of the Ti-Nb-Sn alloy
or Ti-6Al-4V alloy. We hypothesized that the Ti-Nb-Sn alloy
group would demonstrate better bone healing and have bet-
ter bone strength than the Ti-6Al-4V alloy group.
MATERIALS AND METHODS
Preparation of intramedullary nails composed of Ti-Nb-
Sn alloy and Ti-6Al-4V alloy
Two kinds of titanium alloys, our recently developed Ti-
33.6Nb-4Sn alloy (nominal composition in mass%) and the
commercially available Ti-6Al-4V extra-low interstitial alloy,
were used in this study. A Ti-33.6Nb-4Sn alloy ingot was
prepared by high-frequency induction melting in a vacuum
using a water-cooled copper hearth, and subsequent vac-
uum arc re-melting.
33 The ingot was hot-forged at 1373 K
to a 42-mm diameter rod and quenched in ﬂowing water .
The rod was then scalped to 34-mm diameter to remove
surface oxide layers, groove-rolled to 20-mm diameter for
simplicity in comparison with multiple steps of rotary swag-
ing, and ﬁnally rotary swaged to 10-mm diameter . This spe-
ciﬁc titanium alloy with low Young’s modulus is referred to
as “Ti-Nb-Sn alloy” hereafter. We designed solid intramedul-
lary nails with diameter of 3.2 mm and length of 95 mm.
The distal length of 30 mm was tapered toward a diameter
of 2.8 mm and the tip was rounded for smooth insertion.
The dimensions were chosen so that the nails could ﬁt
ﬁrmly into the isthmus of the rabbit tibiae. Both types of
nails were machined from Ti-Nb-Sn alloy and Ti-6Al-4V
alloy rods, and had similar surface roughness average (0.27
mm for Ti-Nb-Sn nails; 0.29 mm for Ti-6Al-4V nails). The
measured Young’s moduli of the Ti-Nb-Sn and Ti-6Al-4V
nails were 37 and 105 GPa, respectively, examined by the
free resonance vibration equipment (JE-LT , Nihon Techno-
Plus Co., Ltd., Osaka, Japan), using round bar samples of
3 mm in diameter and 40 mm in length. All implants were
sterilized in an autoclave prior to implantation.
Surgical procedures in rabbit tibiae
Adult male Japanese white rabbits were purchased from
Japan SLC Inc. (Shizuoka, Japan). All rabbits were housed in
the Animal Unit of Tohoku University Medical School, an
environmentally controlled and speciﬁc pathogen-free facil-
ity. The animal protocols were reviewed and approved by
the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the
Tohoku University Environmental and Safety Committee. All
experiments were performed using rabbits weighing 2.8–
3.4 kg. The animals were maintained in individual cages (60
3 51 3 35 cm, W 3 D 3 H) at 22 6 28C and 40 6 20%
humidity, and received water and a speciﬁc animal pellet-
type laboratory-animal food.
The animals were anesthetized by inhalation of 5% sev-
oﬂurane, intramuscular injection of ketamine (15 mg/kg),
and intravenous injection of xylazine (2 mg/kg). The anes-
thesia was maintained with 2% sevoﬂurane, and xylazine
(2 mg/kg) was added just before osteotomy. Cefazolin
(30 mg/kg) was administered by intravenous injection
before surgery.
The right knee of the rabbits was opened through a
medial parapatellar arthrotomy. The medullary canal of the
right tibia was reamed from the proximal surface to the dis-
tal end of the canal up to 3.4 mm in diameter. A 1-cm skin
incision over the medial side of the tibia was performed at
the isthmus level (6 cm distal from proximal entry hole).
Periosteal release was carried out within a 5-mm width, fol-
lowed by transverse osteotomy with an oscillating bone
saw. After the osteotomy, the tibia was reduced manually
without a gap and ﬁxed ﬁrmly with an intramedullary nail.
The rabbits were allowed to walk with full weight bearing
after surgery.
The animals were euthanized by intravenous injection of
pentobarbital (120 mg/kg) at 3, 4, 8, and 16 weeks after
surgery. The bilateral tibiae were removed, wrapped with
saline-moisturized gauze, and cooled in ice, taking great
care not to damage the soft tissue around the callus. The
nail was carefully removed from the proximal side on the
same day. Radiographs of the right tibiae were taken in the
lateral–medial direction using a soft X-ray apparatus (SRO-
M50; Sofron, Tokyo, Japan) and X-ray ﬁlm (BioMax XAR
Film; Carestream Health Co., New York, NY) before and after
removal of the nail. The specimens were preserved at 4 8C
until further tests.

RESULTS
Radiographic observation
Figure 3 shows representative radiographs. At 4 weeks after
surgery, callus formation was progressing. At 8 weeks, bone
union was completed. At 16 weeks, the bone remodeling
process was proceeding at the osteotomy site. There were
no cases of non-union and implant failure in the two
groups. The bone healing process and differences between
the two groups were evaluated precisely by micro-CT analy-
ses and are described below.
Histomorphometric analyses
Figures 4 and 5 show representative histological sections
for each group and the results of quantitative histomorpho-
metric analyses, respectively. The inside of the callus was
mostly ﬁlled with trabecular structures of woven bone,
although the woven bone had rarely bridged the callus at
this time point. Alcian blue staining showed cartilaginous
tissue around the osteotomy site, indicating that endochon-
dral ossiﬁcation had been proceeding. Woven bone around
the cartilage was stained with alcian blue, suggesting that
woven bone had replaced cartilage. The gaps between the
woven bone were ﬁlled with cartilage, ﬁbrous tissue, and
undifferentiated mesenchymal tissue. Histomorphometric
analyses showed that the callus area was signiﬁcantly larger
in the Ti-Nb-Sn alloy group compared with the Ti-6Al-4V
alloy group at 3 weeks after surgery (60.8 6 2.5 mm vs.
50.3 6 2.8 mm, p 5 0.024). The amount of cartilage varied
considerably among the specimens, and there were no sig-

healing of osteotomies in rabbit tibiae. Our results demon-
strated that the bone healing process in the Ti-Nb-Sn alloy
group was associated with increased callus formation, better
newly formed bone structure, and superior bone strength
during the early phase, and furthermore had superior bone
strength and stiffness after bone healing during the late

phase. We considered that adequate interfragmentary
motion and controlled axial motion are provided by the
elastic intramedullary nails composed of the low Young’s
modulus Ti-Nb-Sn alloy, and that these motions might con-

During the early phase of fracture repair , cartilaginous
tissue mainly occupied the callus area. The callus was con-
nected each other by the cartilaginous tissue, and woven
bone had rarely bridged the callus at this time period. The
callus area was signiﬁcantly larger in the Ti-Nb-Sn alloy
group. The mean values of the cartilage area and its ratio
were greater in the Ti-Nb-Sn alloy group, but the differences
were not signiﬁcant. Augat et al. reported that increased
ﬂexibility at the fracture site resulted in enhanced callus
proliferation and greater percentage of uncalciﬁed cartilage
area.
35 Our histological results were compatible with their
ﬁndings. They also documented that a larger callus was not
necessarily associated with an increase in mechanical qual-
ity, and that fracture bridging should be considered an
important factor in determining bone healing quality.
During the middle phase of fracture repair, newly
formed bone replaced the cartilage tissue and bridging by
woven bone was proceeding at the osteotomy site. Micro-CT
analysis showed a signiﬁcantly larger callus volume and
greater callus bridging. These ﬁndings might contribute to
the strength of the callus region in the Ti-Nb-Sn group.
Positive effects of axial intrafragmentary motion on bone
healing have been reported. Adequate intrafragmentary
motion accelerates callus proliferation and produces
mechanical strength. These positive effects of axial intrafrag-
mentary motion have been extensively investigated using
external ﬁxation models.
6–8 Intramedullary nailing has the
same positive effects through adequate bending force, which
induces compression at one side and distraction at the
opposing side.
3–5
Previous studies showed that excessive interfragmentary
motion deteriorates callus mineralization, and causes low
BMD, low bone mineral content, and less bridging at the cal-
lus region.4,10 There was no difference in callus BMD between
the two groups in our study. These ﬁndings indicated that the
Ti-Nb-Sn alloy nails had adequate elastic properties. The
amount of interfragmentary motion appeared sufﬁcient to
induce a larger callus, but was not enough to deteriorate min-
eralization and bridging at the callus region.
The bone remodeling reaction and new cortical shell for-
mation occurred consecutively. Woven bone inside the callus
was partially absorbed. The callus volume decreased gradu-
ally in both groups, but the callus remained signiﬁcantly
larger in the Ti-Nb-Sn alloy group. These effects seemed to
contribute to the higher maximum load in the mechanical
tests, which reached almost the same level as intact tibiae.
However , the BMD of the proximal tibiae did not differ
between the two groups. Stress shielding did not seem to
be important in the bone healing model with intramedullary
ﬁxation by titanium alloy nails. We considered that the axial
interfragmentary motion was a much more important bio-
mechanical factor for fracture repair with intramedullary
nailing than stress shielding, which is an important factor
for a total hip prosthesis.
During the late phase of fracture repair, bone remodel-
ing was proceeding. The woven bone and original cortical
bone at the callus region had been absorbed. The thick new
cortical shell should have a major role in characterizing the
mechanical properties at the osteotomy site during this
stage. Even within this phase, the maximum bending load
was still higher in the Ti-Nb-Sn group, and the low Young’s
modulus intramedullary nailing system might have contrib-
uted to the swift restoration of bone strength after osteot-
omy. The stiffness improved in the Ti-Nb-Sn group during
the middle and late phases after surgery, and there was a
signiﬁcant difference in stiffness between the two groups at
16 weeks.
Previous reports have described similar results that
elastic nails had favorable effects on bone healing on the
remodeling phase. Sha et al.
26 reported that elastic nails
composed of Ti-24Nb-4Zr-7.9Sn alloy (Young’s modulus: 33
GPa) brought higher rigidity to osteotomized femurs in oste-
oporotic rats in the remodeling phase at 12 weeks after sur-
gery, compared with nails composed of Ti-6Al-4V alloy (110
GPa). They also showed a signiﬁcantly higher mineralized
bone volume at the callus region. Utva ˚ g et al.
4 reported that
semi-rigid titanium nails induced a higher maximum bend-
ing load in osteotomized rat femurs at 12 weeks after sur-
gery, compared with rigid stainless-steel nails. They
reported that the BMD at the callus region was signiﬁcantly
higher in the titanium nail group, although the difference
was small.
Although we focused on the new cortical shell together
with the BMD and callus volume, we could not ﬁnd charac-
teristics to explain the difference in mechanical properties
between the groups in the remodeling phase. The factors
for the better mechanical property of the osteotomy site in
the Ti-Nb-Sn alloy group were not the callus volume, BMD,
or new cortical shell formation in the present study. Shefel-
bine et al.
36 reported that ﬁnite element analysis of the cal-
lus could predict the mechanical properties of the fracture
site. It is likely that geometric structures within the callus
or other factors might play roles in the better mechanical
properties of the callus in the remodeling phase by elastic
nail ﬁxation.
In conclusion, our study showed that intramedullary
nails composed of a newly developed low Young’s modulus
Ti-Nb-Sn alloy improved the mechanical strength of the
bone healing site from the early phase to the remodeling
phase in a rabbit model. Adequate Young’s modulus of the
Ti-Nb-Sn alloy enhanced the callus proliferation and bone
strength restoration. Our Ti-Nb-Sn alloy is a promising
orthopedic biomaterial for fracture ﬁxation devices.
