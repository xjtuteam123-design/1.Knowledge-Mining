Effect of Ho additions on the microstructure
and mechanical properties
of Nb-22Ti-16Si-7Cr-3Al-3Ta-2Hf alloys
The effect of Ho additions on the microstructure and com-
pressive properties of the alloy Nb-22Ti-16Si-7Cr-3Al-
3Ta-2Hf (at.%) was investigated by using scanning electron
microscopy, X-ray diffraction, transmission electron micro-
scopy and by performing compression tests. The results
show that Ho additions cause remarkable microstructural
refinement. The addition of 0.1 at.% Ho to the alloy in-
creases its strength and ductility. When the amount of
Ho is increased to 0.2 at.%, the strength and ductility
decreases. Ho addition inhibits the formation of the
(Ti, Nb)
5Si3 phase and the amount of the (Ti, Nb) 5Si3 phase
decreases, even disappears, with increasing Ho content. In
addition, the fracture surfaces of Ho-free alloys deformed
at room temperature in compression tests exhibit cleavage
for both the (Nb, Ti) solid solution and the (Nb, Ti) 5Si3 in-
termetallic compound, while limited local plastic strain of
the (Nb, Ti) solid solution is observed in Ho-doped alloys.
The effect and mechanism of strengthening and plastic de-
formation of Ho additions to the alloy was discussed in
view of the microstructure.
Keywords: Nb5Si3; Microstructure; Mechanical proper-
ties; Ho
1. Introduction
Niobium silicide has been proved to be one of the promis-
ing intermetallic candidates of the next generation of high-
temperature structural materials, since it possesses a high
melting point of more than 2700 K and relatively low den-
sity of 7.16 g cm
–3 which are properties superior to those
of commercial superalloys. However, due to its complex
crystal structure the intrinsic brittleness of Nb
5Si3 at ambi-
ent temperature limits its practical applications [1, 2]. To
overcome this critical issue, the incorporation of a ductile
phase to the almost undeformable Nb 5Si3 phase forming a
two-phase silicide alloy would be one way to improve the
room temperature deformability and fracture toughness [1,
3 – 8]. Although the toughness of such two-phase or multi-
phase alloys has been greatly improved, as compared to
Nb-silicide single-phase alloys, the high-temperature
strength of Nb-silicide is decreased by the incorporation of
Nb solid solution because of its remarkable weakening at
elevated temperatures.
Therefore, one of the major issues in developing Nb-sili-
cide based intermetallic alloys as high temperature materi-
als is to improve the room temperature fracture toughness
without reducing high temperature strength through incor-
porating a ductile niobium solid solution phase [9, 10].
One possible procedure to meet such a requirement is alloy-
ing, by which microstructural and crystal structure modifi-
cations of the constituent phases can be achieved. Many
efforts have been devoted to looking for proper alloying
elements and optimizing their content so as to meet the de-
mands. In previous studies [11 – 18] the effect of alloying
elements such as Hf, Ti, Cr, Al, Ta, Mo, W, and V on the
microstructure and mechanical properties of niobium sili-
cide based materials have been investigated. However,
there are few reports about the effect of Rare Earth (RE) ad-
ditions on Nb-silicide based alloys so far. Previous studies
[19 – 21] showed that appropriate addition of RE elements
to NiAl-based alloys improved the compressive strength
and ductility. Therefore, motivated by the beneficial effect
of RE elements on NiAl-based alloys, we added RE ele-
ments to Nb-silicide based alloys and anticipated them
achieving the purpose of improving strength and ductility.
The aim of the paper is to investigate the effect of Ho ad-
ditions on the microstructure and mechanical properties of
Nb-22Ti-16Si-7Cr-3Al-3Ta-2Hf alloys.
2. Experimental procedures
Alloys of the nominal compositions of Nb-22Ti-16Si-7Cr-
3Al-3Ta-2Hf-xHo ( x = 0, 0.1, 0.2 at.%) were arc-melted by
using a non-consumable tungsten electrode in an argon at-
mosphere in a water-cooled copper crucible. Pure elements
of Nb, Ti, Si, Cr, Al, Ta, Hf and Ho were chosen as starting
materials. Each alloy button was turned over and remelted
more than three times to get a homogeneous specimen. Since
weight losses were generally less than 0.5 %, the composi-
tion of the alloys was considered to be equal to their nominal
compositions. Rectangular compression specimens with the
dimensions of 4 · 4 · 6 mm were cut from the buttons by
electro-discharge machining (EDM) and the major surfaces
were mechanically ground with 600-grit SiC abrasive prior
to compression test. The samples for scanning electron mi-
croscopy (SEM) observation were prepared by conventional
metallographic techniques. Transmission electron micro-
scopy (TEM) was used to characterize the microstructure
and determine the coexisting phase in great detail. The slices
of thickness about 0.5 mm were cut from the alloy buttons by
using EDM. Thinning was performed by mechanical polish-
ing to 50 lm thickness, then shaped into 3 mm in diameter
followed by twin jet electron polishing in a solution of
94 vol.% methanol, 5 vol.% H
2SO4 and 1 vol.% HF at 50 V

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
The SEM microstructures of the Ho-doped and Ho-free
Nb-22Ti-16Si-7Cr-3Al-3Ta-2Hf alloys are presented in
Fig. 1. The microstructure of the matrix alloy is composed
of three phases. These are the continuously distributed
gray-white (Nb, Ti) solid solution matrix, gray (Nb, Ti) 5Si3
and black colored (Ti, Nb) 5Si3 phases, as shown in Fig. 1a.
It can be seen from Fig. 1b that there exist a few fine struc-
tured brightly shining white appearing phases present in
0.1 at.% Ho-doped alloy, which cannot be analyzed by
means of EDS because their size is so small. However,
when the Ho content was increased to 0.2 at.%, a large
amount of bright white cubic phase formed at the interface
between the (Nb, Ti) solid solution and (Nb, Ti)
5Si3 phases.
As shown in Table 1, EDS analysis of the bright white
phase reveals that its composition is 66.10 at.% O,
18.15 at.% Ho, 14.85 at.% Hf, 0.90 at.% Nb, in which the
ratio of Ho : Hf : O is approximately 2 : 2 : 7. By means of
analytical transmission electron microscopy (TEM), the
light white phase is identified to be Ho 2Hf2O7 (see Fig. 3).
The TEM micrograph also shows that the Ho 2Hf2O7 parti-
cles mainly distribute on the interphase boundary of the
(Nb, Ti) solid solution and (Nb, Ti) 5Si3. Figure 4 shows
the selected area diffraction patterns (SADP) of [110] and
[112] zone axis of the Ho 2Hf2O7 particle, which possesses
fcc crystal structure with the lattice parameter of a =
5.206 nm.
In addition, it is evident from Fig. 1 that Ho additions
remarkably refine the microstructure, i. e., the size of the
(Nb, Ti)5Si3 phases becomes much smaller with increasing
Ho content. It was reported in previous studies [19 – 21] that
RE elements, such as Nd and Dy, have a refinement effect
on intermetallics. The RE elements may reduce the surface
tension of the liquid metal and the formation energy of
grain nuclei, thus increasing the nucleation rate during
solidification, owing to their enhanced surface activity. Ho
is also a surface active element similar to Nd and Dy. These
react easily with O and S to form oxides and sulfides in liq-
uid metal, which may act as grain nuclei in the crystalliza-
tion process and prevent the grains from growing. So, the
Ho addition affects the kinetics of nucleation and growth
of the (Nb, Ti)
5Si3 phase during solidification, so as to de-
crease its average particle size. Previous investigation [22]
has shown that these finely dispersed oxide and sulfide par-
ticles in alloys may retard the grain size for further growing.
Simultaneously, the interfaces between (Nb, Ti) solid solu-
tions and (Nb, Ti) 5Si3 phases may be refined due to lower-
ing O and S interstitial content. Therefore, the Ho additions
may be beneficial for the cohesive strength and fracture
toughness of the interface.
It can be seen from Figs. 1 and 2 that Ho additions inhibit
the formation of the (Ti, Nb) 5Si3 compound. The amount of
(Ti, Nb)5Si3 particles decreases, may even disappear, with
the increasing Ho content of the alloy. The (Ti, Nb) 5Si3
phase was not detected in the 0.2 at.% Ho-doped alloy by
means of SEM investigations, as shown in Fig. 1c. As pre-
sented in Fig. 2, X-ray diffraction peaks of the (Ti, Nb)
5Si3
phase become weak in samples with 0.1 at.% Ho and disap-
pear in samples with 0.2 at.% Ho. The formation of the
(Ti, Nb)
5Si3 phase might be related to Ti segregation at
the solid – liquid interface in the solidification process.
During solidification of the (Nb, Ti) solid solutions and
(Nb, Ti)5Si3 phases, Ti atoms are rejected into the liquid
phase and are enriched to form (Ti, Nb) 5Si3 particles in
front of the solid – liquid interface. Therefore, one effect of
Ho additions inhibiting the formation of (Ti, Nb) 5Si3 is re-
lated to hyper cooling and the rapid solidification rate of
liquid metal. The RE Ho can cause greater constitutional
undercooling at the interface between solid and liquid due
to its surface activity. It is well known that the solidification
rate depends on the degree of undercooling at the interface
between solid and liquid. The greater the amount of under-
cooling at the solid – liquid interfaces, the greater is the soli-
dification rate. Thus, the diffusion time decreases sharply
with increasing solidification rate. Therefore, the diffusion
of Ti atoms is retarded, which reduces the formation of
(Ti, Nb)5Si3 due to a lack of Ti segregation. Table 1 shows
that the Ti content in the (Nb, Ti) solid solutions increases
from 20.07 at.% to 26.90 at.% with increasing Ho content.
This suggests that Ho addition enhances Ti solubility in
(Nb, Ti) solid solutions, which may improve the ductility
of the (Nb, Ti) solid solutions because Ti is an effective al-

RE segregation in the grain boundary may weaken the
Ni – Al covalent bounds [29 – 31]. A similar effect of RE ad-
ditions on Nb – Si alloys is anticipated. This work is under
further investigation. If Ho were proved to possess a similar
effect in the present alloy, Ho atoms may offer electrons to
the matrix atoms, which decreases the proportion of cova-
lent bonds and increases that of metallic bonds. This may
result in a ductility improvement in the alloys containing
Ho. Also, the increase in the Ti content in the (Nb, Ti) solid
solution with Ho addition may improve the ductility of the
(Nb, Ti) solid solution matrix. It is now well established
that alloying Nb with Ti results in a lowering of the
Peierls – Nabarro (P – N) barrier energy. The decrease of
the P – N barrier energy increases the dislocation mobility,
compressive ductility and intrinsic fracture resistance in
Nb solid solution alloys [23, 24]. Therefore, the increased
Ti solubility will cause a beneficial effect on the compres-
sive ductility. However, the mechanical properties will be
deteriorated due to the formation of bigger cubic Ho
2Hf2O7
precipitates with increasing of the Ho content to about
0.2 at.%, which may act as the origin of crack initiation.
Figure 6 shows typical SEM micrographs of fracture sur-
face of samples deformed in compression tests at RT. It can
be seen clearly from Fig. 6a that the fracture surface of
Ho-free alloys presents complete cleavage mode for both
(Nb, Ti) solid solution matrix and (Nb, Ti)
5Si3, showing a
large amount of cleavage planes. However, limited plastic
strain of the (Nb, Ti) solid solution matrix is observed in
Ho-doped alloys in Fig. 6b. The fracture exhibits a mixed
fracture mode of cleavage of the (Nb, Ti) 5Si3 compound
and the (Nb, Ti) solid solution matrix and local plastic de-
formation of the partial volume fraction of the (Nb, Ti)
phase. This suggests that local plastic deformation in-
creases the fracture strain of the Ho-doped alloys.
4. Summary
The effect of Ho additions on the microstructure and
mechanical properties determined in compression tests of
Nb-22Ti-16Si-7Cr-3Al-3Ta-2Hf alloys was investigated.
The results can be summarized as follows:
1. The Ho additions remarkably refine the microstructure,
especially the grain or particle of the (Nb, Ti) 5Si3 phase.
Furthermore, with Ho addition, some cubic Ho 2Hf2O7
precipitates are formed at the interface between the
(Nb, Ti) solid solution and (Nb, Ti)
5Si3 phases.
2. Ho addition inhibits the formation of (Ti, Nb) 5Si3. The
(Ti, Nb)5Si3 phase decreases, even disappears, with in-
creasing Ho content.
3. An appropriate Ho addition will improve the strength
properties and ductility simultaneously. However, the
mechanical properties will decrease due to the forma-
tion of bigger cubic Ho 2Hf2O7 precipitates when the
amount of Ho is increased to 0.2 at.%.
4. The fracture surface of Ho-free alloys presents a com-
plete cleavage mode for both the (Nb, Ti) solid solution
and (Nb, Ti) 5Si3 compound. However, limited local
plastic strain of the (Nb, Ti) solid solution was observed
in Ho-doped alloys. The fracture surface exhibits a
mixed fracture mode of cleavage in the (Nb, Ti) solid
solution matrix and (Nb, Ti) 5Si3 compound, and local
plastic deformation occurs partially in the (Nb, Ti) solid
solution matrix.
