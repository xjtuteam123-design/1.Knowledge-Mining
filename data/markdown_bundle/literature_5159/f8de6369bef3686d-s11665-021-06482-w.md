TECHNICAL ARTICLE
Effect of Nb Content on Microstructure and Mechanical
Properties of K4169-Type Superalloy
Liu-kun Zhang, Xiao-dong Wu, Yan Zou, Jin-San Wang, and Ling-fei Cao
Submitted: 18 May 2021 / Revised: 22 October 2021 / Accepted: 17 November 2021 / Published online: 13 January 2022
The inﬂuence of Nb content on the microstructure and mechanical property of K4169-type superalloy was
investigated by using optical microscopy, scanning electron microscopy, transmission electron microscopy
observation and tensile testing, at room temperature and 650 /C176C, respectively. Two alloys with different Nb
contents (4.9 wt.% for Alloy 1 and 8.9 wt.% for Alloy 2) were investigated in this work. The results show
that with the aging temperature increasing from 620 to 720 /C176C, the strength of Alloy 1 and Alloy 2
increases, which is further enhanced during two-stage aging. Comparably, Alloy 2 has higher ultimate
tensile strength at room temperature and 650 /C176C, and better high-temperature resistance while its elon-
gation is signiﬁcantly lower. After aging, the number of carbides does not change signiﬁcantly, but as the
number of Laves and d phases increases, the size and content of c¢¢ phase raises in Alloy 2 as well.
Keywords K4169 superalloy, mechanical property,
microstructure, Nb content
1. Introduction
K4169-type (similar with Inconel718) superalloy, as a
precipitation strengthening Ni-based superalloy, is usually
strengthened by precipitating c¢¢ and c¢ phases in the austenitic
matrix. It is the most widely used Ni-based superalloy due to its
unique creep and fatigue resistance at elevated temperatures up
to 650 /C176 C, which makes it suitable for gas turbine engines and
power generation applications (Ref 1-4). The body-centered
tetragonal precipitate c¢¢ is the primary strengthening precipitate
and the face-centered cubic precipitate c¢ is the secondary
strengthening precipitate (Ref 5), because compared with c¢¢
phase, the c¢ phase possesses a low anti-phase boundary energy
(Ref 6) and the lattice constant is close to the matrix. The
orthorhombic precipitate d phase is a brittle phase. This phase is
an incoherent and Nb-rich phase that precipitates after aging for
an extended time at elevated temperature. In addition, Laves
phase is also a common brittle phase in K4169-type alloy.
When there are a large number of refractory elements in the
alloy, the formation of Laves phase will be promoted, and the
solid solution strengthening elements in the alloy will be
consumed during the formation process of Laves phase,
resulting in the decline of mechanical property (Ref 7). The
details of these phases are shown in Table 1. In addition, the
dissolution temperatures of Laves phase and carbide are
1140 /C176 C and 1277 /C176 C in Inconel 718 alloy, respectively (Ref
8, 9). And the solvus temperature of the Laves phase is
1154 /C176 C and 1169 /C176 C in investigated alloy A1 and A2,
respectively. According to investigation (Ref 10), the dissolu-
tion temperature of Laves phase get higher (over 1165 /C176 C) with
the increase of Nb content (9 wt.%), while the dissolution
temperature of carbide decline to 1230 /C176 C. Besides, it also
indicated that both the initial and ﬁnal melting points of alloy
decrease with the increasing Nb content from 5.2 to 6.0%,
causing a widened solidiﬁcation range (169 /C176 C) .
The c¢¢ phase is prone to transform to d phase that leads to
mechanical properties degradation at elevated temperatures
(Ref 12-14). The precipitation of d phase is related to the
stacking faults of the c¢¢ phase. When the c¢¢ phase is
precipitated from the matrix, there is a great distortion, which
leads to the disorder on the close-packed plane (112) of the c¢¢
phase, and then the stacking faults occur. The new structure
produced by the change of stacking sequence of c¢¢ phase is
consistent with d phase. The d phase formed from the c¢¢ phase
dislocation will grow into the matrix, intersecting with the c¢¢
phase as it grows, and form new d phase in the c¢¢ phase, which
will facilitate the transition from the c¢¢ to the d phase (Ref 15).
This greatly limits the use of the K4169-type alloy. In addition,
as the working environment of aerospace engines and ground
gas turbines become more and more demanding, the alloy
properties combined with high strength and high-temperature
resistance become more critical. Therefore, it is necessary to
increase the service temperature of K4169-type alloy or
increase the mechanical properties at the same temperature to
reduce weight. One of the methods for such improvement is to
change its alloy content (Ref 16).
The composition of K4169-type superalloy is quite complex
(Ref 17) and different alloy elements play different roles in it
(Ref 18, 19). In the past few decades, many scholars have

carbide during the solidiﬁcation process in the Inconel718
superalloy. Masoumi et al. (Ref 22) found that the evolution of
d and c¢¢ phases is mainly controlled by the diffusion of Nb
element, while c¢ phase is controlled by the diffusion of Al, Ti
and Nb. Liu et al. (Ref 23) reported that d phase decreases
greatly and a large amount of Laves phase precipitates along
boundaries when Al content exceeds 1.24%. With increasing Al
content, the impact toughness of the K4169-type alloy is
largely decreased. Collier et al. (Ref 24) proposed that
increasing the Al-to-Ti ratio and the Al+Ti concentration in
the alloy decreases the driving force to form d phase.
Preliminary experimental results (Ref 11) suggest that allowing
the atomic percent of Al and Ti to equal that of Nb may result in
a more thermally stable c¢¢. Collier et al. (Ref 24) proposed that
increasing the Al/Ti ratio and/or the Al+Ti content in alloy
IN718 results in a more thermally and mechanically stable alloy.
This could be due to the higher volume fraction of c¢ or the
smaller c¢¢ particle sizes. Currently, Mo element provides
solution-strengthening effect for the c matrix. The amount of d
phase is also decreased by the increase in Mo addition to 4.00%
and the precipitation of Laves phase is increased by the increase
in Mo content (Ref 25). The excessive local enrichment of Mo
atoms makes alloys prone to the precipitation of brittle phases,
resulting in degradation in mechanical properties (Ref. 26).
Nb element is not only the forming element of the c¢¢ phase,
but also one of the main elements of positive segregation (Ref
27). With the increase in Nb content, a large number of c¢¢
phase will be formed to improve the strength; while serious
segregation will be caused. At the same time, the content of
Laves phase will also be increased, damaging the mechanical
properties of the alloy. The effect of different Nb contents on
the equilibrium precipitated phase can be calculated by using
Thermo-Calc software, but the strengthening precipitate c¢¢ is a
metastable phase, so it is difﬁcult to ﬁgure out the effect of
different Nb contents on the concentration of c¢¢ phase.
Therefore, in this study, the changes in microstructure and
tensile properties of K4169-type alloy are investigated with Nb
content increasing from 4.9 to 8.9 wt.%. Particular attention is
paid to the formation and feature of c¢¢ phase.
2. Experiments
K4169-type superalloy ingot was prepared via vacuum
induction melting, and the nominal composition is given in
Table 2.
The microstructure of casted alloy has three typical zones:
ﬁne grains in the surface, columnar grains in the middle and
equiaxed ones in the innermost zone. In order to avoid the
inﬂuence of different zones on the experimental results, all the
samples were taken from the middle zone. According to the
aviation industry standard HBZ140-2004, the samples were
ﬁrstly homogenized at 1095 /C176 C for 2h, then directly cooled by
air. Subsequently, the solute-treated samples were held for 1h at
955 /C176 C followed by an air quenching. Finally, the samples were
aged ﬁrstly at 720 /C176 C for 8h, then cooled at a cooling rate of 56
/C176 C/h to 620 /C176 C and held it for 8h, followed by an air quenching
(1095+955+720+620). Besides that, in order to investigate the
inﬂuence of aging treatment, different aging temperature was
applied, including 620 /C176 C for 8h (1095+955+620), 670 /C176 C for
8h (1095+955+670), 720 /C176 C for 8h (1095+955+720), respec-
tively.
Since the shape of Laves phase is similar to that of carbides,
it is hard to be distinguished accurately by their appearance.
Hence, color metallography (Ref 28) was used to identify them
by color differences. The sample was held for 30min at 400 /C176 C
followed by an air quenching, the surface of the sample was
oxidized to form a transparent ﬁlm with a thickness of about
400-500nm. When viewed under the optical microscopy (OM),
the incident light will interfere with the ﬁlm to form color. The
content of Cr and Nb elements in Laves phase and carbide is
quite different, and Cr element has strong oxidation resistance,
while Nb element is easy to be oxidized. Therefore, the
thickness of the oxide interference ﬁlm of the two phases is
quite different. When observed with OM, the Laves phase and
carbide have distinct colors and are easy to be distinguished.
The darker one is carbide, and the lighter one is Laves phase.
The tensile tests at room temperature were carried on an
AG-X testing machine at a loading speed of 1 mm/min. The
tensile tests at elevated temperature were carried on a
CMT5305 testing machine at a loading speed of 3 mm/min.
The microstructure was characterized using a FEI Tecnai G
2
F20 transmission electron microscopy (TEM) operating at 200
kV and focus ion beam scanning electron microscopy (SEM).
The foils for TEM were mechanically polished to a thickness of
65lm and then electro-polished in a solution of 10% perchlo-
rate and 90% methanol at temperature between -10 /C176 C and -20
/C176 C. The precipitate phase content is measured by using Image-
pro plus software, and the area measurement method was used
with thresholding techniques in the image analyses. And size of
precipitate phase is measured by using Nano Measure 1.2
software.

3. Results
3.1 Mechanical Property
Figure 1 shows the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of alloys
at room temperature and 650 /C176 C, respectively. For Alloy 1 and
Alloy 2, with the aging temperature increasing from 620 to 720
/C176 C, the UTS is enhanced and the UTS of Alloy 2 is always
higher than Alloy 1 after the same heat treatment. For the same
alloy, the UTS at 650 /C176 C is always lower than that at room
temperature, but the decline range is different after the same
heat treatment. Here, the decline range is deﬁned as the
difference of UTS between room temperature and 650 /C176 C over
the UTS at room temperature. After calculation, the decline
range of Alloy 1 is always higher than that of Alloy 2, in which
the UTS of Alloy 1 decreases by about 20%, while that of
Alloy 2 decreases by only about 10%. This reﬂects that Alloy 2
has better high-temperature resistance than Alloy 1. Compared
with Alloy 1, Alloy 2 has higher strength, but the plasticity is
severely decreased. The elongation of alloys at room temper-
ature is shown in Table 3. It can be found that the elongation of
Alloy 2 is always very low, and the aging temperature has no
obvious effect on the elongation. For Alloy 1, when aged at 620
/C176 C and 670 /C176 C, there is little difference in elongation, but when
aged at 720 /C176 C, it will lead to a decrease in elongation.
Furthermore, when the aging treatment changes from single-
stage aging to two-stage aging, the elongation will further
decrease. Nevertheless, the elongation of Alloy 1 is always
higher than that of Alloy 2.
Figure 2 shows the fracture morphologies of Alloys 1 and 2
after (1095+955+620) heat treatment. For Alloy 1, many
dimples with different sizes and little facets can be observed in
Fig. 2(a), which indicates the good elongation of Alloy 1,
whereas Fig. 2(b) shows that there are still many dendrites in
the fractography of Alloy 2, which causes the poor elongation
of Alloy 2.
3.2 Microstructure investigation
3.2.1 OM. It is well known that the properties of materials
are determined by microstructure. The optical micrographs taken
from center sections of K4169-type ingot are shown in Fig.3 , and
it can be seen that the microstructure presents an obvious dendrite
structure, consisting of dendrite arms and interdendritic. As can
be seen from the enlarged SEM images at the upper right corner,
the content of Laves phase in Alloy 2 (9.8%) is signiﬁcantly
higher than that in Alloy 1 (3.2%), which indicates that alloying
elements in Alloy 2 are more uneven.
Color metallographic diagrams after different heat treat-
ments are shown in Fig .4 . It can be found that the carbide is
uniformly distributed in Alloy 1 and Alloy 2, and there is no
obvious difference in size between them. The carbide is the
intermetallic compound in K4169-type alloy, which has the
properties of high hardness and high melting point. However,
the carbide shows no obvious effect on the properties of the
alloys due to its limited amount. As for the Laves phase, its size
and distribution are different in two alloys. In general, the size
of Laves phase is small and does not connect to each other in
Alloy 1 (Fig .4 a, c, e, g), while it is signiﬁcantly larger and
more continuously distributed to each other in Alloy 2 (Fig .4 b,
d, f, h). In addition, the amount of Laves phase in Alloy 2 is
higher than Alloy 1.
3.2.2 SEM and EDS. Figure 5 shows the SEM diagrams
of the alloys after different heat treatments. As can be seen from
Fig.5 , the content of needle-like d phase in Alloy 2 (Fig .5 b, d,
f, h) is higher than that in Alloy 1 (Fig .5 a, c, e, g) after the
same heat treatment. Zhang et al. (Ref 29) indicated that the
plasticity of the alloy decreases as the content of d phase
increases from 3.79 to 8.21%. In addition, the excessive d
phase will cause local stress concentration and microcrack
generation in the matrix, further initiating premature failure. It
was also found that appropriate d phase (about 3%) at grain
boundary could avoid the notch sensitivity and prolong the
ruptures stress life of the alloy (Ref 6). Hence, it can be inferred
that 3% d phase in K4169-type alloys is beneﬁcial to the
mechanical properties.
The content of each precipitated phases is shown in Table 4.
It can be seen that there is little difference in the content of
carbide in Alloy 1 and Alloy 2, while the Laves phase and d
phase in Alloy 2 are always higher than in Alloy 1. In Alloy 1,
the content of Laves and d phase is 0.4-2.1% and 0.2-2.5%,
respectively, while in Alloy 2, the value is 5.4-9.6% and 9.2-
12.8%, respectively, which can be attributed to the high Nb
content in Alloy 2. The content of d phase changes with the
aging temperature, which might be attributed to the competition
between c/C213/c’’ and d phase.
Composition of precipitated phases is analyzed by EDS, as
shown in Table 5. It can be seen that the compositions of Laves
phase are mainly Ni, Nb and Cr and the main compositions of
matrix are Ni, Cr and Fe. While the main compositions of
carbide are Nb, Ti and C, so the carbide type is (Nb,Ti)C.
Because the size of needle-like d phase in the width direction is
smaller than the EDS beam spot diameter, it is difﬁcult to
accurately measure the composition of d phase.

3.2.3 TEM. The precipitation features of strengthening
phases are observed by TEM. The dark ﬁeld images of alloys
after two-stage aging are shown in Fig .6 . For Alloy 1, the dark
ﬁeld image (Fig. 6a) shows that precipitates have two different
morphologies, which are identiﬁed as c¢¢ and c¢ phases
according to corresponding SADP (Fig. 6c). Besides, c¢¢ is
plate-shaped. This is because the lattice constant of c¢¢ phase in
the c-axis direction is about twice that of a-axis, and the lattice
misﬁt of c¢¢ phase in the c-axis direction will be large when it is
precipitated from the matrix. The misﬁt between the c-axis and
the matrix of c¢¢ phase is about 2.5%, while the misﬁt between
the a-axis, b-axis and the matrix is only 0.8% (Ref 30), which
causes the phase to be distributed in the matrix in a plate-
shaped, resulting in a large number of stacking faults in c¢¢
phase (Ref 25, 31), while the c¢ phases are cuboidal. The
intermetallic precipitation phase c¢, has the composition
Ni
3(Al,Ti) and a cubic L1 2 crystal structure (Ref 5). The
stability of c¢ is high because the c¢ lattice constant is close to
the matrix, with a difference of less than 1%, and the interfacial
energy between c¢ and matrix is low (Ref 32).
It is notable that only plate c¢¢ phases can be observed in
Alloy 2, as shown in Fig. 6(b). That is to say the precipitation
of c¢¢ phase is promoted by the increase in Nb content.
Moreover, Alloy 2 shows higher number density of c¢¢ phase
than that of Alloy 1 and the content of c¢¢ phase in Alloy 1 and
Alloy 2 is 2.9% and 6.5%, respectively. The size of c¢¢ phase is
quantitatively analyzed and shown in Table 6. In Alloy 1, the
length and width of c¢¢ are 14.3nm and 3.2nm, respectively,
while in Alloy 2, the length and width of c¢¢ are 18.6nm and
5.1nm, respectively. So the c¢¢ phase in Alloy 2 is larger than
that in Alloy 1 in both length and width direction. In addition,
the length and width of c¢ are 12.9nm and 12.7 nm, respec-
tively, and the content is about 5.1% in Alloy 1, which is hardly
visible in Alloy 2 with higher Nb content.
4. Discussion
4.1 Microstructure
4.1.1 Laves Phase. In the solidiﬁcation process of
K4169-type alloy, the phase transformation sequence starts
with solidiﬁcation of the matrix c from liquid phase at higher
temperature: liquid phase ﬁ matrix c. As temperature drops,
the following eutectic type reaction liquid phase ﬁ (c+Laves)
will then occur (Ref 33). Nb segregation is serious in the last
stage of solidiﬁcation (Ref 34) and Nb element is the main
positive segregation element. The contents of Nb, Mo and Ti
element in the liquid phase will increase with the solidiﬁcation
process and tend to segregation to inter-dendrites spaces, while
the contents of Cr, Fe and Al element in the liquid phase will
decrease with the solidiﬁcation process and tend to segregation
to dendritic branches.
It can be seen from Table 5 that Laves is a rich-Nb phase. So
the precipitation content of Laves phase in Alloy 2 is much
higher than that of Alloy 1. The increase in Nb content reduces

The inﬂuence of Laves phase on mechanical properties is
complex. On the one hand, Laves phase can strengthen the
alloy, higher content (5.4 /C249.6%) of Laves phase can increase
the strength. But the strengthening effect of Laves phase is
minor when compared with c¢¢ phase. Besides, Laves consumes
a large amount of Nb element, leading to a decrease in the
amount of c¢¢ phase which reduces the strength of the alloy. On
the other hand, Laves is a brittle phase, it is a source of crack
nucleation and propagation, reducing the plasticity and leading
to premature fracture of the alloy (Ref 7) with poor elongation
(lower than 3%). Furthermore, the interface between Laves
phase and the c matrix is often exfoliated under tensile force
(Ref 36). Overall, the increase in Laves phase causes deteri-
oration of strength and elongation of alloy, its content should be
reduced as much as possible.

4.1.3 d Phase. There are two ways to precipitate the d
phase in the alloy: one is precipitated at the grain boundary by
cellular reaction, and the other is precipitated in grains by
inhomogeneous nucleation, in which the nucleation position is
greatly affected by temperature. In addition, d phase can be
precipitated directly from the supersaturated solid solution at
over 900 /C176 C (Ref 40).
According to Table 1, the precipitation temperature of d
phase is in the range of 780-980 /C176 C. However, d phase will be
completely dissolved into the c matrix at about 1020 /C176 C. The
number of d phase has a very important effect on the properties
of the alloy. An appropriate amount of d has a beneﬁcial effect
on improving the plasticity of the alloy and eliminating the
notch sensitivity. In addition, it can control the grain boundary
migration and prevent grain growth from being pinched (Ref
41). However, the excessive precipitation of d will lead to the
deterioration of the performance. The precipitation of d phase
will consume a large number of Nb element, which leads to the
c¢¢-free zone around it, reducing the strength of alloy. When a
large number of d phases distribute along the grain boundary,
under the action of stress, the strain difference between d phase
and the matrix will lead to interface cracking (Ref 42-45).
It can be seen from Table 5 that the content of precipitated d
phase in Alloy 1 is about 1% and that in Alloy 2 is about 10%.
The Nb content forming d phase is between 6 and 8%, so the
content of d phase in Alloy 2 is much higher than Alloy 1.
Zhang et al (Ref 46) found that the plasticity at 950 /C176 C
decreased as the content of d phase increased from 3.79 to
8.21%, and an appropriate d phase (about 3%) at grain
boundary could avoid the notch sensitivity and prolong the
ruptures stress life of the alloy (Ref 47). Hence, it can be
inferred that 1% d phase is beneﬁcial to good plasticity to some
extent, but 10% d phase is too high and will lead to a decline in
elongation. As listed in Table 3, Alloy 2 with a higher content
of d phase shows extremely poor elongation than that of Alloy
1.
4.2 Strength
K4169-type alloy is a precipitation strengthened Ni-based
superalloy. Aging temperature and aging time are important to
the strength of this alloy. It can be seen from Table 1 that the
precipitation temperature range of c¢¢ and c¢ phase is about 600-
800 /C176 C. When aged at 720 /C176 C, the precipitation rate of c¢¢ phase
is the fastest, while the precipitation rate of c¢ phase is the
fastest when aged at 620 /C176 C.
For Alloy 1 and Alloy 2, as the aging temperature increases
from 620 to 720 /C176 C, more and more homogeneously dispersed
second phase c¢¢ will be precipitated of the matrix, resulting in
the increases in the strength. The main forming element of the
c¢¢ is Nb, so the content of c¢¢ phase in Alloy 2 is higher than
that in Alloy 1, which leads to the strength of Alloy 2 higher
than that of Alloy 1 after the same heat treatment. When aged at
620 /C176 C, the strengthening phase is mainly c¢ phase and the UTS
of Alloy 1 and Alloy 2 is 667 MPa and 765 MPa, respectively.
However, when aged at 720 /C176 C, the strengthening phase is
mainly c¢¢ phase and the UTS of Alloy 1 and Alloy 2 is
965 MPa and 1038 MPa. Therefore, when the strengthening
phase type changes from c¢ phase to c¢¢ phase, the UTS of two
alloys increases signiﬁcantly, in which the UTS of Alloy 1
increases by 298 MPa and Alloy 2 increases by 273 MPa.
Compared with aging at 720 /C176 C, the UTS of alloys after two-
stage aging increases slightly, where an increment of 79 MPa
and 54 MPa is obtained for Alloy 1 and Alloy 2, respectively.
The reason for the small increases in strength is that a small
amount of c¢ phase is precipitated in the second aging stage.
Oblak et al. (Ref 42) reported that the strength of Inconel718
alloy after aging depends primarily upon coherency strength-
ening arising from the c¢¢ precipitate. The antiphase boundary
energy of c¢¢ phase is estimated to be 296 mJ/m
2, while that of
c¢ phase is 12 mJ/m 2. Hence, with the increase in c¢ phase
content, the strength of the alloy increases, but the increment is
limited. From the above discussion, it can be seen that the
contribution of c¢¢ phase to the increase in strength is higher
than that of c¢ phase. The content of d phase and Laves phase in
Alloy 2 is much higher than that in Alloy 1 as a result of a
higher Nb content, which can also increase the strength, but
greatly reduce the elongation of Alloy 2.
5. Conclusions
In this work, the effect of Nb content on the microstructure
and tensile properties of K4169-type superalloy were investi-
gated. Two alloys with different Nb contents (4.9wt% for Alloy
1 and 8.9wt% for Alloy 2) and the following conclusions were
drawn:
(1) There is no obvious difference in the content of carbide
precipitation between the Alloy 1 and Alloy 2. In Alloy
2, the content of Laves phase and d phase is higher than
that of Alloy 1. The increase in Nb content promotes
the precipitation and growth of the main strengthening
phase c¢¢.
(2) The ultimate tensile strength of Alloy 2 is higher than
Alloy 1 at room temperature and 650 /C176 C. In addition,
Alloy 2 has the better high-temperature resistance per-
formance. However, the plasticity of Alloy 2 is signiﬁ-
cantly lower than Alloy 1.
