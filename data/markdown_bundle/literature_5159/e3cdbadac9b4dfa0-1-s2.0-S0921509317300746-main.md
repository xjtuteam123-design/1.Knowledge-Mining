ABSTRACT
Mechanical properties of Titanium-zirconium-molybdenum (TZM) and La 2O3 doped TZM alloys under
compression were tested at 1000 °C and 1200 °C. Microstructure of TZM and La 2O3 doped TZM alloys after
compressing was characterized by scanning electron microscopy. The e ﬀects on La 2O3 doping on the high
temperature deformation behavior and microstructure evolution of the TZM alloy were analyzed. Results show
that La 2O3 doping can reﬁ ne the grain size of TZM alloy. La 2O3 doping changes fracture model of TZM alloy.
TZM alloy exhibits mainly intergranular fracture, while the La 2O3 doped TZM alloy exhibits both intergranular
and transgranular fracture mode.
1. Introduction
Titanium-zirconium-molybdenum (TZM) alloy contains 0.5 –0.8 wt
% titanium, 0.08 –0.1 wt% zirconium, 0.016 –0.02 wt% carbon and
molybdenum balance [1–4]. TZM alloys have the similar character-
istics as molybdenum such as high melting point and elastic modulus,
thermal and electrical conductivity, temperature stability, and low
coeﬃcient of thermal expansion [5–8]. Due to the solution and
precipitation strengthening achieved by the additive alloying elements,
TZM alloys have high hardness and creep resistance at high tempera-
tures. Nowadays TZM alloys are highly desirable for a wide range of
critical applications due to their high electrical and thermal conductiv-
ity, strength and creep resistance high temperatures [9–12].
Some studies have shown that doping lanthanide oxide into
traditional TZM alloy can signi ﬁcantly improve its strength and
toughness [13]. Lanthanide oxide content has variable e ﬀects on the
tensile properties of the TZM alloys. The general trend is that the
hardness and the yield strength increase with the lanthanide oxide
mass fraction. This is because the solute atoms segregate around
dislocations and form the Cottrell atmosphere with powerful elastic
interactions. The Cottrell atmosphere is securely pinning dislocations,
leading to the obvious yield strength enhancement. In addition,
deformation mechanism of the body centered cubic refractory metals
generally has three stages: (1) double twist mechanism, which causes
strength reduction at elevated temperature below 0.15T
m, (2) disloca-
tion climb at low stress levels (dislocation creep at low and high
temperatures), (3) deformation controlled by di ﬀusion mass transfer
above 0.5Tm at low stress levels [14].
In this study, the compression tests on high temperature TZM
alloys were carried out at 1000 –1200 °C [15–17]. The e ﬀects of
deformation conditions on the microstructure evolution were studied
by scanning electronic microscopy (SEM). Deformation behavior of the
TZM and La
2O3 doped TZM alloys at high temperature, along with the
corresponding deformation mechanisms responsible for the micro-
structure evolution were also studied.
2. Experimental
Doped titanium hydride, zirconium hydride, lanthanum trioxide
and graphite powders were mixed with the molybdenum powder. The
design mix is listed in Table 1, and the purity of each powder was above
99.5%.
The powders were mixed in the three-dimensional mixer for 1 h.
Then the mixed powders were milled in the planetary ball mill at
40 rpm. The diameter of the molybdenum balls was 10 mm, and the
ball-to-powder weight ratio was 2:1, and the ball milling time was 6 h.
Powder was ﬁlled into the die mold, and pressed using the vertical
500 t YT79-500 hydraulic press (Tianjin Tianduan Hydraulic Co., Ltd.,
China) with 150 MPa pressure for 5 s. The sintering was performed in
the HM3002 intermediate frequency induction furnace (Shanghai

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure characterization
Fig. 2 shows the SEM images of the TZM alloy compressed at room
temperature ( Fig. 2 a), 1000 °C ( Fig. 2 b) and 1200 °C ( Fig. 2 c) respec-
tively. For the room temperature compression test grain boundaries are
more obvious. EDS analysis of the precipitated phases is shown in
Fig. 3 .
In Fig. 3 , the secondary phase precipitates gathered near the grain
boundaries at high temperature compression mechanical properties
test. Since impurity atoms gathered at grain boundaries and the
stabilities of the grain boundaries become worse, while grain bound-
aries melting and oxidizing. The average grain size of room-tempera-
ture compressed TZM alloy is about 23 µm, while the grain sizes are
30 µm and 6 µm for the high-temperature compressed TZM alloy at
1000 °C and 1200 °C respectively. Compared with the same magni ﬁca-
tion SEM images in Fig. 2 , we can ﬁnd that the average grain size
increases with increasing compression temperature. The reason for the
grain size change is undercooling and atomic thermal activation energy
increase with temperature, providing su ﬃcient activation energy for
grain growth [20]. The voids between agglomerated grains without
chemical bonds reduce with compression temperature and thus the
average grain size decreases.
Fig. 4 shows SEM images of the La
2O3 doped TZM alloy after
compression at room temperature ( Fig. 4 a), 1000 °C ( Fig. 4 b) and
1200 °C ( Fig. 4c). Grain boundaries morphology and average grain size
variation are similar to the high temperature compression TZM alloy.
However, there are some distinctions between the two alloys. First, the
amount of oxide precipitation at grain boundaries of the La
2O3 doped
TZM alloy is larger than the TZM alloy. Due to La 2O3 doping, there is
additional oxide in the alloy. The EDS analysis of the precipitated
phases is shown in Fig. 5 .
In Fig. 5, lanthanide oxide can be found in precipitation. Secondly,
the average grain size of the La 2O3 doped TZM alloy is smaller than the
TZM alloy. In La-doped TZM alloy that Lanthanum exists in the form
of La
2O3 in the alloy due to a mix of the TZM alloy with the lanthanum
nitrate solution.

pinned at the grain boundaries. Fine La 2O3 particles have a pinning
eﬀect on dislocations and hinder grain boundary sliding. In addition,
dispersive distribution of the ﬁne La 2O3 particles hinders powder

TZM grain morphology, the La 2O3 doped TZM alloy grains are
elongated, with narrow morphology and directionality.
Under the equilibrium compressive stress, the grains in the close-
packed planes de ﬂect from the compression axis and grow.
Preferentially oriented grains gradually expand and annex neighboring
grains along the compression direction. However, the secondary phase
precipitates around the grain boundaries hinders the intragranular
particles ﬂow to grain boundaries, thereby, providing the driving force
for the preferentially oriented grains to grow. In this case, the La
2O3

3.2. mechanical properties analysis
The compression stress vs strain curves of the La 2O3 doped TZM
tested at 1000 °C and 1200 °C, and TZM alloys tested at 1000 °C and
1200 °C are shown in Fig. 6 . At 1000 °C, elastic limit of the La 2O3
doped TZM alloy is 308 MPa. A plateau appears between the strain
rates of 0.1 s
−1 and 0.2 s −1. The upper yield point is 325 MPa and the
lower yield point is 310 MPa. Yield strength of the La 2O3 doped TZM
alloy is 312 MPa ( σs=312 MPa) and the ultimate strength of the La 2O3
doped TZM alloy is 306 MPa ( σb=306 MPa). The TZM alloy 1000 °C
compression stress-strain curve is di ﬀerent from the La 2O3 doped TZM
alloy. Speciﬁc parameters are as follows: σe=510 MPa, σs=525 MPa,
σb=475 MPa, the upper yield point is 532 MPa and the lower yield
point is 505 MPa. In 1200 ℃, the strength of La 2O3 doped TZM alloy is
also lower than that of TZM. Traditional Hall-Petch relation revealed
that due to dislocation pile up and scattered stress concentration,
starting adjacent dislocation sources is di ﬃcult, and therefore the yield
strength is high [18].L a 2O3 doping re ﬁned the grain, thus the grain
size of the La 2O3 doped TZM alloy is smaller than the TZM alloy.
However, opposite to the Hall-Petch relation, yield strength of La 2O3
doped TZM alloy was apparently reduced. The reason is that the grain
boundaries exhibit viscosity at elevated temperature, leading to rela-
tively easy to start adjacent grains sliding and the signi ﬁcantly decrease
of deformation resistance.

3.3. Fractography analysis
The di ﬀerence of the fracture morphology between the TZM and
La2O3 doped TZM alloys achieved by the high temperature compres-
sion test is shown in Fig. 7 . There are two di ﬀerent fracture modes in
the TZM and La 2O3 doped TZM alloys. In Fig. 7 (a) and (c), TZM alloy
fracture is mainly intergranular with smooth and granular fracture
surfaces, thus the crack extension is along the grain boundaries. TZM
alloy intergranular fracture is of brittle nature. Under the high
temperature compression conditions, the grain boundaries are weak,
where environmental factors cause grain boundary embrittlement or
weakening. For example, high temperature stress or stress corrosion
weaken the grain boundaries [15], which is the cause of the inter-
granular fracture in this case. However, as evident from Fig. 7 (b) and
(d), La 2O3 doped TZM alloy fracture is mixed, including intergranular
and transgranular fracture, with transgranular fracture being the major
fracture mode. The fracture surface of the La
2O3 doped TZM alloy
presents ductile fracture pattern as faint and ﬁber texture. The ﬁber
texture is produced by many extended and joined microcracks, leading
to faint and gray fracture surface.
