The microstructure and phase composition of single crystals of refractory nickel alloys bearing 9% Re are
studied after many-stage heat treatment, long-term high-temperature annealing, and testing for long-term
strength.
INTRODUCTION
The most refractory materials produced today in Russia
and in foreign countries for single crystal gas turbine blades
are nickel alloys with rhenium additives [1]. Rhenium, which
is primarily dissolved in the nickel fcc-solid solution
(/c103-phase), hinders the diffusion processes in the solution and
thus increases the creep resistance of the alloy under the ac-
tion of high temperatures and stresses. Alloying with this ele-
ment raises considerably the cost of refractory nickel alloys
(RNA), but this is compensated by their high-temperature
behavior and service life.
It seems expedient to consider some factors that can af -
fect negatively the operating characteristics of rhenium-bear-
ing RNA. One such factor is the unbalanced alloying of the
metal, which can be described by the parameter /c68E [2], i.e.,

A special feature of the structure of rhenium-bearing
RNA is segregation of lamellar tcp-phases in the process of
long-term high-temperature heating or mechanical testing.
This effect is studied in [3 – 5, 14]. Work [5] seems to be the
most interesting and gives systematic data on the crystal
structure of tcp-phases and the temperature ranges of their
existence. In the CMSX-4, Alloy 800, and RR2071 alloys
that have an unbalanced chemical composition in our opi-
nion (Table 1), the authors of [5] have detected/c115, /c109, R, and P
phases the amount and joint occurrence of which are deter-
mined by the temperature and time of the hold. As a rule, the
tcp-phases have a high content of Re, W, Mo, and Ni in vari-
ous combinations (Table 2). Work [3] presents the results of
a study of the effect of long-term (1000 and 10,000 h) heat -
ing at 950 and 1050°C on the structure and long-term
strength of alloy CMSX-10 bearing 6.3% Re. The authors of
[3] show that the main factor influencing the decrease in the
creep resistance of the alloy after such exposures is the unfa-
vorable shape and size of the particles of the /c103/c162-phase rather
than the segregation of a lamellar phase, which is identified
by them and a /c115-phase. This statement contradicts the known
opinion on the embrittling action of tcp-phases.
The aim of the present work was to study the micro -
structure and the phase composition of single crystals of re -
fractory nickel alloy VZhM-1 (ZhS47) after many-stage heat
treatment, high-temperature heating, and testing for long-
term strength.
METHODS OF STUDY
We studied single crystals of refractory nickel alloy
VZhM-1, the chemical composition of which is presented in
Table 3. VZhM-1 belongs to high-rhenium refractory nickel
alloys of the third generation and is used for casting single
crystal gas turbine blades [1].
It follows from Table 3 that alloy VZhM-1 characterized
by elevated concentration of rhenium (9.0%) as compared to
other known single crystal RNA (Table 1) has a quite ba-
lanced chemical composition ( /c68E = – 0.04). The alloy was
developed using the method of computer design [15]. It has
an ultimately high level of long-term strength characteristics
at operating temperatures ranging within 900 – 1100°C. We
fabricated2 single crystals from alloy VZhM-1 for study by
the method of liquid metal cooling (LMC) on refectory seeds
in a UVNK-9A industrial furnace for directed crystallization.
In order to form an optimum microstructure in single crystals
with uniform distribution of cuboid particles of /c103/c162-phase
about 0.4 /c109m in size, the crystals were subjected to heat
treatment that included long-term (up to 26 h) many-stage
homogenizing at 1285 – 1335°C and two-stage aging.
The amount and the chemical composition of the /c103/c162- and
/c103-phases in the alloy were studied by the method of physico -
chemical phase analysis (PPA) based on electrochemical iso-
lation of the phases and their subsequent analysis by chemi -
cal and x-ray diffraction methods [16]. The same method
was used for studying the lamellar segregations of a new
phase formed in the structure of the alloy under the action of
high temperature; the segregations were isolated in an elec -
trolyte based on ethylene glycol and bearing hydrochloric
acid. The conditions for the electrolysis were chosen so that
the initial /c103/c162- and /c103-phases dissolved, and the lamellar forma-
tions of the new phase remaining on the surface of the speci-
men were isolated. The isolated particles were subjected to
x-ray diffraction analysis in a D /c47Max-2000 diffractometer.
In order to obtain a three-dimensional image of the lamellar
segregations the latter were etched deeply in the mentioned
electrolyte.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The microstructure in the studied single crystals of alloy
VZhM-1 had a form (see Fig. 1) typical for heat-treated sin-
gle crystals of high-rhenium RNA with orientation /c225001/c241.
The first to dissolve during the high-temperature
(1335°C) homogenizing of the alloy is the nonequilibrium
/c103+ /c103/c162eutectic formed in the dendrite spacing of the cast sin -
gle crystal (Fig. 1 a ) due to segregation of the alloying ele -
ments; then the chemical composition levels by diffusion
within the dendrite cells.
A consequence of the dissolution of the eutectic is the
appearance of micropores (homogenization pores) (Fig. 1b ).
The sizes of the particles of the /c103/c162-phase in the matrix /c103-solid
solution of dendrite arms and dendrite spacing become virtu-
ally leveled. After subsequent two-stage aging they acquire
the shape of cubes or parallelepipeds (Fig. 1c and d ).
The data of microscopic x-ray spectrum analysis
(MXRSA) show that after homogenizing and two-stage ag-
ing of alloy VZhM-1 the coefficients of reverse segregation
of refractory rhenium and tungsten decrease from – 3.4 and
– 2.1 to – 1.8 and – 1.3, respectively, whereas the concentra-
tion of the other alloying elements becomes virtually leveled.
Thus, the heat treatment does not remove fully the segrega-
tion of rhenium and tungsten due to the extremely low diffu-
sion mobility of their atoms in the /c103-solid solution, and it is
impossible to make the microstructure homogeneous. This
seems to be the reason behind the inconsiderable size
inhomogeneity of the particles of /c103/c162-phase in dendrite arms
and spacing of the heat-treated single crystal (Fig. 1 c and d )
and behind the presence of a certain amount (about 0.1%) of
segregated lamellar phase in its structure (Fig. 1d ). An x-ray
diffraction analysis of the isolate obtained by the method of
PPA has down that the crystal structure of this phase is close
to the structural type of a Co 7W6 intermetallic compound
(/c109-phase).

single crystals contain micropores with crystallographic fac -

with decrease in the distance to the zone of fracture.
As a result of creep at 1000°C for about 2500 h and at

/c103/c162-phase and penetrating through virtually the entire volume

is an act of adaptation of the system to the rigid conditions of
the tests of the single crystal specimen. This promotes de -
crease in the internal stresses, hinders the processes of frac-
ture of the single crystal alloy, and ensures quite high me-
chanical properties of the alloy under the given testing con-
ditions.

Analysis of the obtained experimental data shows that
despite the formation of a quite high amount of lamellar
/c103c-phase in alloy VZhM-1, the ductility of single crystals of
this alloy due to short-term stretching, their endurance, and
their long-term ductility in the creep tests are preserved at a
high enough level (Tables 5 and 6). Consequently, the lamel-
lar high-rhenium /c103c-phase of structural type A1 does not
cause embrittlement of single crystals of refractory alloy
VZhM-1. However, it can be seen from Table 5 that the en -
durance of the alloy heat treated by a full cycle and then sub-
jected to annealing at 1000°C for 500 h is much lower than
after a full cycle of heat treatment.
Comparing the evolution of the phase composition and
of the /c103/c47/c103/c162structure in single crystals of alloy VZhM-1 in the
process of long-term high-temperature annealing (Fig. 5)
with the results of their tests for long-term strength (Table 5),
we found out that the decrease in the time before failure at
900 – 1100°C in the single crystals annealed at 1000°C for
500 h was caused by coarsening and degradation of particles
of the main hardening /c103/c162-phase.
Thus, the appearance of a woven /c103c-structure in alloy
VZhM-1 in the process of testing for long-term strength or in
long high-temperature annealing allows us to speak of the
mechanism of self-strengthening of the alloy under the ac -
tion of high-temperatures and stresses.
CONCLUSIONS
1. Long-term heating or high-temperature testing for
long-term strength of single crystals of high-rhenium
(9 wt.%) alloy VZhM-1in the temperature range of 1000 –
1100°C yields lamellar segregations of a phase enriched with
rhenium. An x-ray diffraction analysis of lamellas of the new
phase isolated from the /c103/c47/c103/c162-matrix has shown that it belongs
to disordered fcc-structures of type A1 with lattice parameter
equal to 0.361 nm. The lamellar phase has a cluster origin
and has been called /c103c-phase.
2. The method of deep etching has shown that the
/c103c-phase is located over octahedron planes. After long-term
high-temperature testing the phase acquires a woven struc-
ture and has features typical for spinodal decomposition.
3. The /c103c-phase produces no negative effect on the
long-term ductility of single crystals of high-rhenium refrac -
tory nickel alloy VZhM-1 in contrast to the case of segrega -
tion of classical tcp-phases in refractory nickel alloys with
unbalanced alloying.
4. The main factors accelerating the process of softening
of single crystals of refractory nickel alloys are coagulation
and degradation of the cuboid particles of the main harden -
ing /c103/c162-phase and formation of pores.
