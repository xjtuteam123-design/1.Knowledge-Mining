1 Introduction
TA15 titanium alloys are extensively used in aerospace for
their exceptional strength-to-weight ratio, corrosion resis-
tance, and high-temperature endurance (operated at 500 /C176C
for * 5000 h) [ 1–5]. However, their inherent properties,
such as high deformation resistance and poor machinabil-
ity, pose signiﬁcant challenges to their broader application
in structural components [ 6, 7]. In contrast, additive man-
ufacturing offers a superior alternative to traditional sub-
tractive manufacturing, streamlining the fabrication
process, enhancing material utilization, and enabling the
production of intricate titanium alloy components for
aerospace applications [ 8–13].
Numerous researchers have studied TA15 titanium
alloys fabricated using selective laser melting (SLM). The
primary research topics encompass the microstructure,
texture, and mechanical properties of the alloy in as-built
and post-heat-treated conditions [ 14–18]. The ﬁndings
reveal that when subjected to heat treatment, the as-built

the as-built nor the post-heat-treated alloys exhibited pro-

inverted strength-to-ductility ratios. To ensure the safety
and reliability of the structural components, it is imperative
to enhance the comprehensive performance of SLM-fab-

tions. Oxide dispersion strengthening (ODS) has emerged
as a promising approach for this particular application
because it uniformly disperses oxide nanoparticles within

mechanisms, elevating the mechanical performance across
a broad temperature spectrum. In titanium alloys, Y
2O3
reinforcement has signiﬁcant advantages over traditional
reinforcements such as TiBw, TiC, and SiC. In the process
of laser additive manufacturing, Y 2O3 nanoparticles can
reduce the porosity of the molten pool and achieve a nano-
scale uniform distribution without agglomeration via the
Marangoni effect, while TiBw, TiC, and SiC easily form
micron-sized clusters [ 23]. In addition, Y
2O3 nanoparticles
can promote grain nucleation, and pin grain boundaries can
inhibit grain growth, improve isotropy, and synergistically
enhance strength and plasticity [ 19, 24]. However, TiBw
and TiC induce the growth of columnar crystals and
aggravates anisotropy. Because of their agglomeration and
interfacial reactions, early cracking can easily occur,
resulting in extremely low plasticity [ 25–28]. In addition,
Y
2O3 exhibits good high-temperature stability and creep
resistance. It can form a dense oxide ﬁlm at high temper-
atures, whereas TiBw easily dissolves during solid-solution
treatment, resulting in strengthening failure [ 29, 30]. For
example, Han et al. [ 19] and Wang et al. [ 20] investigated
the additive manufacturing process of a ? b titanium
alloys with different Y 2O3 additions. They found that Y 2O3
acted as nucleation sites, promoting a-equiaxed transfor-
mation and enhancing plasticity. A similar result was
reported by Liu [ 24], who added trace Y 2O3 particles
during laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) of a Ti-4Al-4V
alloy. Compared to the as-built Ti-4Al-4V alloy, the
incorporation of Y 2O3 in the as-built Ti-4Al-4V alloy
resulted in a concomitant augmentation of both strength
and ductility. Notably, the as-built Ti-4Al-4V-Y 2O3 alloy
exhibited a strength-ductility combination slightly better
than that of the heat-treated LPBF Ti-6Al-4V alloy. The
tensile strength and elongation of the Y
2O3-bearing TiAl
alloy improved at elevated temperatures [ 31].
In summary, the microstructural characteristics and
superior mechanical properties of additively manufactured
ODS-Ti alloys are closely associated with the incorpora-
tion of Y
2O3. During the melting stage, nanoparticles
inﬂuence the dimensions of the melt pool, its ﬂow
characteristics [32], and keyhole formation [ 33]. Moreover,
these nanoparticulate entities can alter the solidiﬁcation
conditions and function as active nucleation sites, thereby
fostering heterogeneous nucleation and mitigating the
emergence of defects in crack-sensitive alloys, such as Ti
[34]. However, the composition of TA15 is more complex,
and its melting and solidiﬁcation behaviours are inevitably
distinct from those of Ti-Al-V alloys. However, the effects
of trace Y
2O3 on the solidiﬁcation microstructure and
mechanical properties remains unknown. Concurrently,
further investigation of its high-temperature mechanical
properties is warranted, given its prolonged service at
elevated temperatures.
In this study, TA15- xY
2O3 composite powders were
prepared by mixing TA15 alloy powders synthesized via
electrode induction gas atomization (EIGA) with Y
2O3
nanoparticles using a planetary ball mill. The TA15- xY2O3
alloy was fabricated by SLM. The effects of Y 2O3
nanoparticle content on the microstructure, nanoparticle
distribution, tensile strength, plasticity (at room tempera-
ture and 500 /C176C), and strengthening mechanisms of the
TA15-xY2O3 alloy were systematically investigated.

The microstructures of the TA15- xY2O3 alloys after
annealing are shown in Fig. 2. Following a heat treatment

3.3 Morphology of tensile fracture surface
Figure 5 depicts the morphologies of the room and high-
temperature tensile fractures with varying Y 2O3 contents.
Both brittle and ductile fracture regions were observed,
indicating a mixed fracture mode. The fracture surface of
TA15 sample was relatively ﬂat, with a large shear lip at
the edge. Numerous dimples were observed, accompanied
by a small number of microvoids and secondary cracks, as
shown in Fig. 5A. The fracture surface of the TA15-
0.1Y
2O3 sample was uneven, with a signiﬁcant reduction in
both the number and size of dimples. Distinct cleavage
planes and steps were observed at the edges, as shown in
Fig. 5B. Furthermore, the deeper grooves on the fracture
surface indicated the presence of numerous secondary
cracks connected at different positions (Fig. 5E). When the
nano-Y
2O3 content reached 0.4 wt%, the local cleavage
characteristic area and the number of microvoids in the
fracture increased (Fig. 5C). This phenomenon can be
attributed to the formation of microvoids and microcracks.
Figure 5G–L illustrates the tensile fracture morphology
at 500 /C176C. The brittle fracture characteristics were signiﬁ-
cantly reduced compared with those observed at room
temperature. Pronounced shear deformation was observed
at the edge of the TA15 alloy fracture, whereas no distinct
shear deformation bands were present in the fractures of
the TA15- xY
2O3 samples. With an increase in the Y 2O3
content, the fracture pits deepened, and the dimple size
gradually decreased, with the size differences being smaller
than those at room temperature. This resulted in a slower
increase in the alloy brittleness at high temperatures than
that at room temperature. The number of secondary cracks
gradually increased, with microvoids and secondary cracks
forming at the groove depths. Deeper pits offer a larger
surface area for plastic deformation, enabling more effec-
tive stress absorption and dispersion, and preventing and
delaying fracture initiation. The observed increase in sec-
ondary cracks and fracture tortuosity shown in Fig. 5H, I
can also be associated with an increase in the number of
microvoids.
