FEATURES OF THE STRUCTURE OF A HIGH-TEMPERATURE-STRENGTH NICKEL ALLOY 
AFTER LIQUID-PHASE SINTERING 
A. P. Gulyaev, L. P. Sergienko, UDC 669.15'24-192:621.762 
A. V. Logunov, and O. I. Samoilov 
The presence of a liquid phase substantially accelerates the process of sintering a pow- 
der metallurgy alloy as the result of increasing the diffusion rate in the liquid phase and 
also as the result of other factors [i]. As a rule the presence in the structure of trans- 
formation products of the liquid phase has a detremental effect on the properties of the al- 
loy. Nevertheless, in a number of cases the necessity of acceleration of the sintering pro- 
cess necessitates the use of this effect. Normally two production methods providing forma- 
tion of the liquid phase at the sintering temperature are used, addition to the powder mix- 
ture of a lower melting metal such as copper to iron powders or heating above the solidus 
point of the alloy with partial fusion of it [2]. In this work the second method is used. 
High-temperature-strength KhN45MVKBTYu (EP741NP) nickel alloy was investigated [3]. The 
powder of the alloy was obtained in the form of +45-160 ~m granules by fusion of a solid 
blank using the method of a rotating electrode. The subsequent method of preparation of com- 
pact blanks consisted of liquid-phase sintering of the granules, hot isostatic pressing (HIP), 
and heat treatment (HT) [4]. An investigation of the alloy was made after each of these 
stages of the production operation. 
The spherical granules of sprayed EP741NP alloy powder had a dispersed dendritic struc- 
ture (Fig. i) which was uniform y-phase. In metallographic analysis precipitates of other 
phases, including ~'-phase, were not observed, which is the result of rapid cooling of the 
granules (at a rate of about 106°C/see). Consequently the composition of the y-phase in the 
granules was similar to the composition of the molten metal (Table I). 
Micro-x-ray spectral analysis established uniform distribution of the elements along the 
diameter of the granules regardless of their size (Fig. 2)° An increase in aluminum content 
is observed on the surface of the granules, which is related to formation of an oxide film 
on them.

In sintering, passage through the solidus point (T s) leads to formation of liquid phase 
in the structure of the alloy. The quantity of liquid phase in the investigated alloy in 
the solidus-liquidus temperature range is shown in Fig. 3. In [4] it was established that 
optimum quantity of liquid phase for subsequent conversion in EP741NP alloy must be 10-15%, 
which corresponds to a sintering temperature of 1280 ± 5°C. 
Figure 4 shows the microstructure of the sintered alloy. It was established that the 
size of a grain is that of a granule. At the grain boundaries there are pores and products 
of transformation of the liquid phase (Fig. 4a, b), which are fine crystals of solid solution 
with a higher (by 100-130 H) hardness than the grains. The alloy element distribution with- 
in the grain is uniform. 
After hot isostatic pressing there are practically no products of transformation of the 
liquid phase in the structure, obviously as the result of diffusion depletion from the base. 
Dispersed precipitates of 7'-phase and carbides are observed in the whole volume of the 
grains (Fig. 4c). 
Heat treatment of the alloy includes hardening and two successive tempers at 950 and 
760°C. The final structure of EP741NP alloy is coarse grains with a size of D = 0.2-0.4 mm 
(after sintering and hot isostatic pressing D = 0.i mm). Very likely the structure of the 
alloy before and after heat treatment differed only in grain size. At the grain boundaries 
were observed precipitates of carbide phase (Fig. 4d). 
Electron microscopic investigation* (Fig. 5) showed the following change in intragranu- 
lar structure of the alloy. After sintering the y'-phase was cuboids with a size of d = 200- 
500 nm (Fig. 5a). Subsequent production operations (heating, deformation in hot isostatic 
pressing) cause significant changes in the intragranu!ar structure. The formations of X'- 
phase are spheroidized and coarsen to d = 1500-2000 nm (Fig. 5b). In addition to coarse for- 
mations of y'-phase fine particles with d = 10-50 nm are observed. Heat treatment including 
high temperature heating to 1200°C and aging leads to solution of the ~'-phase and a change 
in its morphology. Refinement of the coarse formations to d = 700 nm and also an increase 
in the quantity of fine (d = 20 nm) precipitates occur (Fig. 5c).

According to modern concepts T- and y'-phases are solid solutions with a face-centered 
cubic lattice [5 et al.]. In T-phase the distribution of the atoms is random (statistical) 
while in T'-phase it is definite (ordered). Consequently, so-called precipitation of T'- 
phase is simultaneously a process of ordering and redistribution of alloy elements since the 
T- and T'-phases differ in composition. Therefore while T-phase does not have a definite 
composition (broad area of homogeneity), that is, is a typical solid solution, then T'-phase 
may be considered as a special form of both a solid solution and of a chemical compound which 
has preserved the lattice of one of the components (in this case of nickel). According to 
the latter the ordered solid solution may be expressed by a chemical formula, in this case 
A~B [in a ternary system Ni3(AI , Ti)]* [2]. Having grouped the elements in accordance with 
this formula it may be seen that the sum Ni + Co + Cr is 75-74% and AI + Ti + Mo + W + Nb + 
Hf = 25-26%, which corresponds to this stoichiometric ratio of Ni3AI (that is, 3:1). For 
T-phase the total quantity of the above elements corresponds to 80-86 and 20-14%. Conse- 
quently, the Y + T' ordering process is accompanied by redistribution of all alloy elements, 
enrichment of T'-phase with AI and Ti and also W, Mo, and Nb and by a decrease in Ni, Co, 
and Cr.

The formation of carbides leads to intense impregnation of them by the strong carbide- 
forming elements Mo, W, and Nb. The carbide phase consists primarily of Nb(C, N) carboni- 
tride. In phase analysis oxides pass into the carbide residue and therefore a large quantity 
of A1 was observed in the composition of the carbides (Table i). In addition, method diffi-

As mentioned an the beginning of the article, for liquid-phase sintering heating above 
the solidus point is necessary. In this case the liquid phase is formed as the result of 
fusion of the surfaces of the granules, primarily at the junction of three granules (Fig. 
4b). In cooling the liquid phase crystallizes into a single-phase solid solution with a 
face-centered cubic lattice. The form of the crystals is columnar as a rule (Fig. 4e). The 
higher hardness of these crystals than of the grains (500-530 and 420-400 H, respectively) 
makes it possible to assume that this difference is caused by the different content of the 
dissolved components and by the ordered disposition of their atoms in the crystalline lattice. 
Micro-x-ray spectral analysis showed (Fig. 6) that in comparison with the base in these 
portions there is an increase in the contents of Ni, AI, and Ti and a decrease in Co, Cr, Mo, 
and W, which completely corresponds to the difference in the composition of the ¥- and ¥'- 
phases (Table i). Therefore, it may be stated that the crystallized liquid phase is ¥'-phase. 
Let us attempt to describe the mechanism of formation and subsequent crystallization of 
the liquid phase. 
The original structure of the cast granules is supersaturated y-phase and therefore 
heating must cause precipitation of 7'-phase, which occurs in the 700-800°C temperature 
range. At higher temperatures there occur processes of coagulation and reverse solution of 
y'-phase, which are completely completed at temperatures above 900-I000°C. However, this

The described nonequilibrium structure is observed only in the sintered blank with the 
condition of sufficiently rapid cooling after completion of the sintering process. The last 
production operations are related to heating to a single-phase y-condition. "Cast" y'-phase 
dissolves in y-phase and in slow cooling in the low-temperature area or in tempering (aging) 
ordering occurs with formation even within the grains of the unstable chemical compound 
Ni3AI , which may be assumed to be an ordered solid solution. 
Conclusions. i. Liquid-phase sintering at temperatures somewhat exceeding the solidus 
point of KhN45MVKBTYu alloy leads to the formation of a liquid phase close in composition to 
the compound Ni3AI , that is, y'-phase. 
2. Subsequent production heatings lead to solution of this y'-phase in y-phase. The 
main quantity of y'-phase is formed by the normal method, as the result of intragranular pre- 
cipitation. ~'-phase of both forms has a similar composition and is uniformly distributed 
within the grain. In connection with this it may be assumed that the modern method of pro- 
duction of parts of the powder metallurgy nickel high-temperature-strength alloy eliminates 
the elements of the cast structure unfavorable for the properties.

CONTACT FATIGUE STRENGTH OF POWDER STEEL ShKhl5 
N. V. Abashkina, A. I. Repina, 
V. S. Ivanov, and O. A. Popov 
UDC 669.14.018.298.3:620.178.38 
The reliability and longevity of bearings is governed by the in-service retention of 
the bulk and surface strength of the materials under load and exposure to the medium. The 
processes of the surface failure of the material consist in a number of microscopic failure 
events, which are manifested on three scale levels - macroscopic (frictional force, tempera- 
ture, wear, hardness, etc.), microscopic (microstrueture, microhardness, microgeometry), and 
submicroscopic (secondary structures, dislocation structures). 
The factors that determine the development of failure processes with the material sub- 
jected to friction can be separated into the following: 1 - external mechanical effects 
(type of friction, displacement rate, temperature, load); 2 - the medium; and, 3 - the mate- 
rial (its structure and properties). If factors 1 and 2 are varied, the friction processes 
will be controlled only by the nature, structure, and hardness of the material. Investiga- 
tion of the contact-fatigue strength of steel ShKhl5, which is prepared from powder, is of 
interest.
