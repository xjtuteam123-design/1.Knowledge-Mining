ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
Microstructure and High-Temperature Mechanical
Properties of Ti-6Al-4V/TiAl Matrix Composites using
Powder Metallurgy Technology
Zhenxin Duan, Yuxin Shen, Xiaolei Song, Hao Chen, Liping Liu, Ying Han, and Hua Chen
Submitted: 9 August 2024 / Revised: 29 December 2024 / Accepted: 20 January 2025 / Published online: 23 April 2025
The Ti-6Al-4V/TiAl matrix composites were prepared using Ti-6Al-4V and Ti-45Al-5Nb alloy powders by
powder metallurgy. The results showed that the structure of the TiAl matrix composites with bimodal
structure consisted of ﬁne-grain TiAl matrix, interfaces, and coarse-grain Ti-6Al-4V alloy area. Compared
to the TiAl alloy, the TiAl matrix composites exhibited good high-temperature ductility while maintaining
high strength. The ductility and strength of TiAl matrix composites are 31 ± 1.5% and 178 ± 3.6 MPa at
900 /C176C and 1 3 10
-4 s-1, respectively, which is 25% higher than that of the TiAl alloy. The improved
ductility was attributed to the Ti-6Al-4V alloy particles, which are easily deformable to bear stresses and
coordinate the deformation of composite at high temperature. In the meantime, ﬁne grain of TiAl matrix
can improve co-deformation ability of the composites. The initiation and propagation of the microcracks
are observed in the TiAl matrix and the interfaces boundary during the high-temperature deformation
process. In conclusion, the hot workability of the TiAl matrix composites is improved due to the addition of
Ti-6Al-4V alloy. Finally, the application prospect of the Ti-6Al-4V/TiAl matrix composites is broadened.
Keywords ductility, microstructure, powder metallurgy,
Ti-6Al-4V alloy, TiAl matrix composites
1. Introduction
TiAl alloy is a new lightweight high-temperature structural
material, which has many excellent properties, including low
density (4.40-4.55 g/cm 3), high strength, good creep resistance,
etc. (Ref 1-4). However, TiAl alloys exhibit limited workability
and insufﬁcient high-temperature ductility, which signiﬁcantly
hinders their wide application. These issues are primarily
attributed to the coarse-grained microstructure and inherent
brittleness (Ref 5, 6). Therefore, it is an important scientiﬁc
issue to improve the high-temperature ductility of the TiAl
alloy.
In order to attain better high-temperature properties of TiAl
alloy, many researchers have conducted a lot of studies. Adding
ceramic particles and metal particles/sheets is a simple
technology method to improve the high-temperature properties
of the TiAl alloys. The conventional reinforcements in TiAl
matrix composites include SiC (Ref 7), TiB
2 (Ref 8), Al 2O3
(Ref 9), etc., which can signiﬁcantly improve the high-
temperature properties of TiAl alloys. However, the properties
are obviously different between TiAl matrix and these hard
particles, resulting in the improvement of the strength is
frequently accompanied by reduction of the ductility. Generally,
it is mutually exclusive between the strength and ductility of
structural materials (Ref 10, 11). In addition to high strength
and good ductility, structural materials are also required to
possess excellent properties (Ref 12).
To solve the above problems, the introduction of metal
particles or their alloys (such as Ti (Ref 13) and Ti-6Al-4V
alloy (Ref 4, 14), compared with ceramic hard particles, they
have superior ductility) into composites, which can provide a
new method for manufacturing composites with excellent
strength and good ductility. Pan’s research group (Ref 13, 14)
prepared Ti or Ti-6Al-4V alloy particles reinforced Mg matrix
composites. The tensile testing demonstrated that the introduc-
tion of alloy particles markedly enhances both the tensile
strength and elongation of Mg alloys. The enhanced strength
can be attributed to grain reﬁnement and strong interfacial
bonding between Ti alloy particles and the Mg matrix. The
enhanced ductility is attributed to the reﬁnement of grains and
the synergistic deformation of Ti alloy particles. The results
indicate that the method is viable. In our previous studies (Ref
4), we prepared TiAl matrix composites via powder metallurgy
by adding Ti-6Al-4V alloy particles. The experimental results
show that the soft phase of Ti-6Al-4V alloy is embedded in the
hard phase of the TiAl alloy matrix. This unique microstructure
demonstrates a distinctive deformation mechanism, resulting in
high strength and ductility of the TiAl matrix composites at

2. Experiments
The Ti, Al, and Nb initial powders (the purity ‡ 99.9%,
average sizes £ 100 lm, Beijing Trillion Metals Co., Ltd.,
China) and Ti-6Al-4 V pre-alloy powders (the purity = 99.9%,
average sizes £ 60 lm, Xi /C213an Sailong Metal Materials Co.,
Ltd., China, referred to TC4) were used as the initial powders.
The nominal component of TiAl alloy was Ti-45Al-5Nb (at.%,
average sizes £ 1 lm, referred to TiAl) in this paper. The Ti-
6Al-4V/TiAl matrix composites were synthesized using ﬁne-
grained Ti-45Al-5Nb powders and TC4 pre-alloyed powders.
Figure 1 shows the schematic illustration of procedures to
prepare TiAl matrix composites. And the preparation process of
TiAl matrix composites is as follows: First, three elemental
powders were mixed by planetary ball mill (QM-QX2, Nanjing
Nanda Instrument Co., Ltd., China) at 350 rpm and 30 h with a
ratio of ball to powders of 10:1 under Ar atmosphere, the TiAl
particulates were acquired in a range of 0.5-1 lm. Then, the
alloying TiAl powders and TC4 powders were put into milling
cans in 92:8 (wt.%, referred to previous study (Ref 4)) under Ar
atmosphere, followed by blending at 150 rpm for 1 h. Finally,
the blended powders were transferred into a graphite die, which
was placed in a vacuum hot-pressing furnace with 1200 /C176 C and
30 MPa holding 1 h. For comparison, the TiAl alloy sample
was prepared using the same conditions. The relative density
and actual density were determined using the Archimedes
method with a density balance (FA2104J, China). Three
measurements were conducted for each sample. The relative
density of the TiAl matrix composite and TiAl alloy was over
99.9%. The actual density of alloys and composites was
measured to be 3.94 g/cm
3 and 4.05 g/cm 3, respectively. The
results demonstrated that the densiﬁcation process was pro-
ceeding effectively (Ref 21).
The tensile performances of the TiAl matrix composite and
TiAl alloy were determined using a uniaxial test machine
(WDW-100, Changchun Kexin Text Instruments Co., Ltd.,
China) at 900 /C176 C and 1 9 10
-4 s-1. In order to ensure the
accuracy of the tests, three parallel tests were carried out in this
work. The observation samples were cut using a wire electrical
discharge machining (WEDM, Suzhou Sanguang Science
Technology Co., Ltd., China), and then the samples were
polished with sandpaper and corroded with hydroﬂuoric acid.
The x-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku D/max 2500/PC, Japan)
was used to analyze the phase constituent of powders, alloys,
and composites. The test conditions were: Cu target, 40 kV and
200 mA, and scanning speed of 2 /C176 /min. The microstructure of
the sintered sample was observed by scanning electron
microscope (SEM, Zeiss Supra-40, Germany) and the energy
dispersive spectrometer (EDS).

3.2 High-Temperature Properties of the Composites
Figure 5(a) shows the true stress-true strain curves for the
alloy and the composites at 900 /C176 C and 1 9 10-4 s-1. The
strength and elongation are summarized in Fig. 5(b). The
ultimate tensile strength of the TiAl matrix composite is about
178 ± 3.6 MPa from the curve, which is similar to that of the
TiAl alloy (176 ± 3 MPa). It is worth noting that the tensile
ductility of the TiAl matrix composite is 31 ± 1.5%, which is
higher about 25% than the TiAl alloy (4.9 ± 0.4%), as shown
in Table 2. It is seen the TiAl matrix composite shows better
ductility, which may be attributed to the hetero-deformation
induced strengthening (Ref 23).
The fracture surfaces morphology of the TiAl alloy and the
TiAl matrix composites is shown in Fig. 6(a) and (b). It can be
found that fracture surface morphology of the TiAl alloy was
similar to that of the matrix of the TiAl matrix composites, and
the dimples on tensile fracture are uniform in distribution and
size. The former has smooth fracture morphology (embedded
images in Fig. 6a), while the latter exhibits a large number of
dimples (embedded images in Fig. 6b), it can be concluded that
the TiAl matrix composites have good ductility. For the TiAl
matrix composites, typical ductile morphologies are observed
with abundant ﬁne dimples, which is correlative with better
ductility. Enlarged morphologies of the TiAl matrix composite
are observed in Fig. 6(c-d). Figure 6(c-d) shows many dimples
with some tear ridges, and this proves that ductile fracture is
dominant during tensile deformation. Yellow and blue dotted
arrows mark the location of TC4 alloy particles that are either
broken or pulled out during tensile deformation. It can be found
that many TC4 areas are embedded in the TiAl matrix, and TC4
areas and interface impede the movement of the grains and
dislocations during the deformation process, leading to occur
more largest strain gradient and the back stress strengthening
effect. Moreover, some TC4 particles are still preserved, which
attended with few TiAl matrix on the surface. This indicates
that Ti-6Al-4V/TiAl matrix composites exhibit favorable
mechanical properties at high temperature. In summary, TC4
alloy particles signiﬁcantly enhance the stress-bearing capacity
and coordinate the deformation of the composites. Additionally,
the presence of TC4 particles and well-bonded interfaces
reduces the probability of microcrack initiation and propaga-
tion, hinders dislocation motion, and promotes dislocation
accumulation. Finally, the good ductility of the composite is
obtained. The strengthening reasons are as follows, (1) TiAl

3.3 Fracture Mechanism of the Composites
As shown in Fig. 7(a), it is found that many pores and
microcracks in the longitudinal section of the fracture, which
initiation and expansion formed large cracks, resulted in the
sample to fracture. In Fig. 7(b), it is evident that microcracks in
the tensile direction are formed between the TC4 areas and the
TiAl matrix. These ﬁndings indicate that stress is transferred
from the TiAl matrix to the TC4 alloy areas, resulting in
signiﬁcant plastic deformation and crack formation at the
interface. In summary, the introduction of TC4 alloy particles
hinders plastic deformation of the TiAl matrix composites and
partially enhances their ductility and toughness.
The schematic diagram of the fracture mechanism is shown
in Fig. 8. In Fig. 8(a), the tensile sample was not deformed at
the initial stage of deformation. As shown in Fig. 8(b), when
the stress load is applied, the soft area of TC4 alloy will ﬁrstly
deform, and stress is transferred from matrix to the TC4 alloy
area. At the same time, the TiAl matrix is not deformed, which
occurs dislocation pileup at the interface. With the stress
exceeds the yield strength of the TiAl matrix, leading to slip
along the grain boundaries of the TiAl matrix. When the
interfacial areas are unable to accommodate dislocations or the
TiAl matrix cannot match the deformation rate of the TC4
alloy, pores begin to form at the interface. Subsequently, cracks
develop in the tensile direction, and pores along with micro-
cracks are observed within the TiAl matrix. In Fig. 8(c), with
the deformation of the sample, the pores of the TiAl matrix
expand into microcracks along the tensile paths, and some new
pores and microcracks continue to form. At the fracture stage
(Fig. 8d), when the stress exceeds the bearing limit of the
sample, the sample will break at the formation of crack
location. Due to the TC4 alloy absorbs a lot of stress load in the
matrix, which remarkably enhances the elongation of the TiAl
matrix composites.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, the Ti-6Al-4V alloy particles reinforced TiAl
matrix composites were prepared by high-energy ball milling
method and powder metallurgy technology. The mechanical
properties of TiAl matrix composites were improved. The main
conclusions were drawn as follows:
(1) The TiAl matrix composites showed bimodal structure
in the matrix by introducing Ti-6Al-4V alloy particles.
The composites exhibited equiaxed double phases, inter-
faces around the Ti-6Al-4V alloy particles forming rein-
forcements.
(2) A good interfacial bonding is attended in TiAl matrix
composites. The phase compositions of the interface
were c + a
2 ﬁ a2 ﬁ a + a2 ﬁ a ﬁ a + b from
the TiAl area to the Ti-6Al-4V alloy area.
(3) The TiAl matrix composites exhibited excellent ductility.
The ultimate tensile strength is about 178 ± 3.6 MPa,
which is similar to that of the TiAl alloy, and the tensile
ductility is 31 ± 1.5%, which is higher about 25% than
the TiAl alloy. The good ductility was attributed to the
Ti-6Al-4V alloy particles, which are easily deformable
to bear stresses and coordinate the deformation of com-
posite.
(4) During the deformation, a lot of the stress is transferred
from the TiAl matrix to the interface area and a few the
stress applied to the TiAl matrix, microcracks, and pores
are generated, resulting in the fracture of the sample.
