Stability of Superalloys Doped with Rare Earth Elements 
Walenty Jasiński 1,a and Paweł Zawada 1,b 
1West Pomeranian University of Technology, Al. Piastów 17, 70-310 Szczecin, Poland 
awalenty.jasinski@zut.edu.pl, bpawel.zawada@vp.pl 
Keywords: superalloy, rare-earth metals, microstructure, phase composition 
Abstract. The stability of the microstructure and properties of the iron superalloys of the second 
generation IN 519 (24% Cr, 24% Ni, 1.5% Nb) modifie d with 0.1 and 0.2 [wt.%] rare earth 
elements was investigated. The alloys underwent ann ealing at 825 oC for 10, 100 and 1000 h. The 
following techniques were used to evaluate the phas e transformation: X-ray diffraction on cross-
section and isolates extracted from the as-cast and annealed alloys, microstructure analysis by 
optical microscopy, hardness measurements by Brinel l and nanoindentation with a Berkovich tip 
method. The analysis of changes in hardness distrib ution due to thermal processing of superalloys 
was conducted with respect to the average hardness of matrix and phase precipitates. 
Introduction 
The chemical, petrochemical and energy industries r equirements of materials characterized by a 
combination of good mechanical properties, resistan ce to microstructure degradation at high 
temperatures and the chemical interaction with the environment has led to the development of 
superalloys [1,2]. Economic considerations have led to growing interest in iron-based superalloys. 
The extensive research on iron superalloys gave the technical optimization of their chemical 
composition and properties by the introduction of n iobium, titanium, zirconium and tungsten 
stabilizing elements [1-4]. A further modification of heat resistant alloys is possible through an 
addition of rare earth elements (RE) [5,6]. Literat ure data show that the wide range of metallic 
materials properties can be modified by the introduction of additives such as RE [7-10]. 
In order to check the stability of the microstructu re and properties of the iron superalloys of the 
second generation the alloy IN 519 (24% Cr, 24% Ni , 1.5% Nb) modified with 0.1 and 0.2 [wt.%] 
mischmetal (39 [ wt. %] La, 61 [wt.% ] Ce) was anne aled at 825 C for 10 h, 100 h and 1000 h. The 
following research was carried: X-ray phase analysis (XRD) and hardness measurements by Brinell 
and nanoindentation test. 
Experimental procedures 
The subjects of research were the ingots obtained f rom a crucible furnace with acid lining Silica-
Mix 7 B 0.5. Metal charge weight was 100 kg. Metall ographic examinations were performed on 
cross section specimens using the light microscope Nikon MM 40. The phases present in 
superalloys were identified with use of X'Pert PRO, PANalitical diffractometer (Cu lamp; voltage 
35 kV, current 40 mA) and X'Pert High Score program [11]. In order to increase the sensitivity of 
X-ray phase detection and identification, the elect rolytic dissolution of the was performed. 
Dissolution was carried out in the electrolyte of t he composition: 5 g C
2H2O4, 200 cm 3 HCl and 
1000 cm 3 H2O for 24 h. Phase analysis was carried out on the i solates within the range of 39 ÷ 63 ° 
angle reflections 2Θ . 
Brinell hardness measurements HB2.5/187.5/15 were p erformed using hardness tester HPO 250 
according to DIN EN ISO 6506. Nanoidentation tests with a Berkovich tip were carried out by the 
CSM (Continuous Stiffness Measurement) method at a constant depth of 300 nm with use of 
nanoindenter XP – MTS Nano Instruments. For each al loy a grid of 200 indentations (20 prints in 
each of 10 rows with the step of 8 µm) was performed. The measurements were taken on cr oss-
sections of etched specimens.

Results and discussion 
Metallographic examination of as-cast samples showe d the presence of dendrite austenite grains 
arranged perpendicularly to the walls of the ingot. The microstructure consists of austenitic matrix, 
carbides precipitated in the early stages of crysta llization and networks disperse eutectic at the 
borders dendrites and grains (Fig. 1). Microscopic observations at high magnification revealed a 
decreasing dispersion plate carbide precipitates in eutectic ingots in the order of IN 519 + 0.1 
[wt.%] RE, IN 519 + 0.2 [wt.% ] RE and IN 519.

X-ray diffraction studies of IN 519 and IN 519 dope d with rare earth elements cast superalloys 
show the presence of alloyed austenite and carbide NbC and (Fe,Cr) 23 C6 precipitates. The intensity 
of the reflections did not allow unambiguous identification of phases containing rare earth elements. 
The results of phase analysis of IN 519 isolate sho w the strong reflections of niobium carbide 
Nb 6C5 and chromium-iron carbide Cr 18,93 Fe 4,07 C6. Additionally the weak reflections of chromium 
carbide Cr 7C3 and austenitic matrix were observed (Fig. 3a). Ana lysis of the phase composition of 
isolates obtained from the superalloy IN 519 + 0.1% RE showed a slight decrease in the total 
fraction of carbide phases and a presence of interm etallics Ce(Ni .25 Si .75 )2 and LaNi 4Si precipitates 
containing rare-earth metals with nickel and silicon (Fig. 3b).

transformation is accompanied by the precipitation of several intermetallic phases from the

Summary 
The sensitivity and the explicitness of the X-ray m ethod applied for the superalloys phase 
identification were achieved by the electrolytic di ssolution of the matrix. Phase analysis of the 
obtained isolates gave sharp and intense reflections. 
The 
 addition of 0.1 [wt.%] mischmetal enhances dispersi on of carbide plate precipitates in eutectic 
and increases the Brinell hardness of as-cast IN 51 9 superalloy. Additionaly this also lead to the 
stabilization of alloy microstructure by reducing p recipitation of hard, brittle intermetallic G and σ 
phases. The annealing at 825 °C of each tested superalloys causes the secondary p recipitation 
processes and the Brinell hardness increases with time of annealing. 
The difference in nanohardness of other phase const ituents (H = 4 ÷ 22 GPa) having diverse 
morphology exhibit in macro scale the investigated superalloys different mechanical properties. 
Good repeatability of hardness results for individu al phases gives a possibility of investigation of 
the kinetics of phase transformations taking place in tested superalloys. In order to use the 
nanoindentation measurements to statistic analysis of phase contribution, the number of 
indentations should be increased for dendritic structures.
