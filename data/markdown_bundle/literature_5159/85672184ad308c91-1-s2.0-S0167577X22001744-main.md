ABSTRACT 
Ti-Mo-Nb alloy material, which is a candidate of the zero-scattering material used for neutron diffraction ap -
plications, was designed and prepared by the powder metallurgy method. Ti-Mo-Nb alloy containing 10%Nb 
sintered at 1700 
◦
C for 5 h has a microstructure of single solid solution phase, and has a compressive strength of 
1711 MPa and the compression strain of 13.5%. ( β -Ti-Mo-Nb)/( β -Ti-Mo-Nb) grain boundaries can be gradually 
wetted by the α -Ti phase with the increase of sintering temperature and Nb content. The neutron diffraction test 
of the Ti-Mo-Nb alloy shows that the diffraction intensity is very low and peaks are not obvious, which illustrates 
that no neutron diffraction appears for this alloy. 
1. Introduction 
The development of in-situ high-pressure neutron diffraction tech -
nology has enabled materials science to study crystal structure, lattice 
dynamics, and texture, which have achieved a lot of remarkable results. 
Neutron carries a magnetic moment and a property of high penetra -
bility, which makes it easier to research with a thick vessel at high and 
low temperatures under high pressure. However, the current in-situ 
high-pressure neutron diffraction technology has shortcomings, such 
as a small number of samples and a long data acquisition time [1] . At 
present, Ti-Zr alloy is widely used as neutron transparent materials [2] , 
but it is easier to bond to the diamond anvil after high pressure. To 
obtain a larger sample cavity by increasing the material strength and 
decreasing the bond, we prepared a high-strength W-Ti alloy [3] . Due to 
the low diffusion rate of W, the material structure could not be 
completely homogenized, thus limiting the use of this W-Ti material. 
These problems have not been fundamentally solved for design and 
preparation of the zero-scattering materials. 
In order to increase the strength of material and decrease the bond, 
the Ti-Mo-Nb alloy with different Nb content was prepared by powder 
metallurgy in this paper. The influence of factors such as Nb content, 
sintering time, and sintering temperature on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of Ti-Mo-Nb alloy were studied and a new type of 
Ti-Mo-Nb alloy was successfully prepared for high-pressure gasket 
material.

Zero-scattering material requires atoms with positive and negative 
scattering length to be arranged completely disorderly. To approach this 
state, the elements with positive neutron scattering lengths can be 
infinitely soluble with Ti. The following can affect the solubility of solid 
solution [4] : (1) Crystal structure, the atomic structure of each compo -
nent of the material must be the same to obtain an infinite solid solution; 
(2) Atom size, the favorable range of atom size difference is ± 15%; (3) 
The first ionization energy, the favorable range of the first ionization 
energy difference is generally within ± 20%. 
Elements that have the atom radius and the first ionization energy 
meeting the requirements and has a large range of solid solution with Ti 
can be got on the A-I diagram of Ti alloy ( Fig. 1 ). Finally, the bcc 
structural elements that may form a zero-scattering material with tita -
nium are selected, which are V, Cr, Fe, Nb, Mo, Ta and W. 
To obtain a higher strength of titanium alloy material to withstand 
greater pressure, Mo and W, which have outstanding performance in 
material strength, are the first to be favored. The previous study [3] 
showed that due to the low mutual diffusion coefficient of W and Ti, it is 
hard to obtain an infinite solid solution of W-Ti alloy. Mo has low 
neutron absorption and can maintain the bcc crystal structure from 
room temperature to its melting point 2610 
◦
C [5] , so Mo was finally 
chosen to form an alloy with Ti. The addition of a sufficient number of 
elements (such as Nb) to the binary Ti-Mo alloys, stabilizing the high- 
temperature bcc β -phase, provides the required balance between 
strength and plasticity [6,7] . In addition, Nb has low neutron absorp -
tion. Finally, Nb was added, hoping to improve the plasticity of the alloy 
material while further refining the grains and increasing the alloy 
strength [8] . According to the requirement of zero neutron scattering, 
the calculated powder compositions are shown as followed: (1) Ti:66.8at 
%, Mo:28.2at%, Nb:5.0at%; (2) Ti:66.9at%, Mo:23.1at%, Nb:10.0at%;

Nb:20.0at%.

sintering temperature, and this is more obvious in the alloys with only 
β -Ti-Mo phase. Grain growth of β -Ti-Mo phase was suppressed by α -Ti 
phase. The effect of α -Ti phase is similar to that of the second phase 
particles, which can refine the grains by promoting grain nucleation and 
hindering grain growth.

The phenomenon of “ grain boundary wetting a second solid-phase ” 
was found in Fig. 2 [9] . The ( β -Ti-Mo-Nb) grains (matrix) appear light 
gray. The α -Ti phase grains appear dark gray. The degree of grain

There is almost no wetting of the grain boundary at 1500

alloy completely entered the β -Ti-Mo-Nb phase. At the same time, α -Ti

grain boundary wetting is higher. Most ( β -Ti-Mo-Nb)/( β -Ti-Mo-Nb) 
grain boundaries are wetted by α -Ti phase. The α -Ti phase blocks the 
continuity of β -Ti-Mo-Nb, resulting in lower mechanical properties of 
the alloy and its plasticity is improved. This also corresponds to the data 
in Fig. 2 . 
Fig. 3 shows the neutron diffraction pattern of the Ti-Mo-Nb alloy 
(10% Nb). The diffraction intensity is very low and peaks are not 
obvious, which illustrates that there is no neutron diffraction. This can 
meet the requirements for neutron zero-scattering materials in neutron 
diffraction experiments.

were designed and prepared. The results show that sintering at high 
temperatures and in vacuum is a more effective way for the micro -

alloy containing 10% Nb sintered at 1700
