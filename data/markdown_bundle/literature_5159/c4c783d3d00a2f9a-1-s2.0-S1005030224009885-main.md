Ultra-high strength and ductility of eutectic high-entropy 
alloy with duplex heterostructure at room and cryogenic 
temperatures 
Traditional metals often exhibit a trade-off between strength 
and plasticity, limiting their wide application of metals in 
aerospace, transportation, energy industry and other ﬁelds [ 1–3 ]. 
In order to overcome this dilemma, high-entropy alloys (HEAs), 
proposed by Yeh et al. and Cantor et al., are currently of great in- 
terest in the materials community due to their excellent mechan- 
ical properties [ 4–7 ]. To further promote the wide application of 
HEAs in industrial production, Lu et al. developed a new eutectic 
high-entropy alloy (EHEAs) by combining the potential advantages 
of traditional eutectic alloys and HEAs [ 8–11 ]. 
EHEAs exhibit an excellent combination of high strength and 
ductility at room temperature. Speciﬁcally, CoCrFeNi–Nb6 Ta4 EHEA 
shows a high yield strengths of 1175 MPa and a large elon- 
gation of 30 %, which is attributed to the synergistic effect of 
ﬁne regular microstructure formed by FCC phase and intermetal- 
lic compounds [ 10 ]. The tensile strength and total elongation of 
Fe20 Co20 Ni41 Al19 EHEA at room temperature are up to 1103 MPa 
and 18.7 %, respectively [ 12 ], which is attributed to the ultra- 
ﬁne nanolayered microstructure. High-density phase interfaces in 
nanolamellar microstructure effectively inhibits dislocation mo- 
tion and dramatically enhances its mechanical properties. In ad- 
dition, EHEAs exhibit not only excellent mechanical properties 
at room temperature, but also ultrahigh tensile strength in ex- 
treme environments, such as cryogenic temperatures [ 13 ]. Lu et al. 
[ 14 ] reported that AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA exhibits a high tensile 
strength without sacriﬁcing the elongation at cryogenic tempera- 
ture, which mainly originated from the dislocation accumulation 
and the strong interaction of dense dislocation. The tensile yield 
strength of Co0.96 Cr0.76 Fe0.85 Ni1.01 Hf0.40 EHEA at the cryogenic tem- 
perature increases from 1060 MPa to 1240 MPa compared with 
that at room temperature, which is attributed to the presence of 
deformation twins, SFs and ﬁxed dislocation locks [ 15 ]. Generally, 
the strength improvement of EHEAs at cryogenic temperature is 
mainly attributed to dislocation strengthening [ 16 , 17 ], back stress 
hardening [ 18 ], deformation twins [ 19 ], stacking faults [ 20–22 ], 
nano-precipitation [ 11 , 23 ], etc. 
Ni–Fe binary alloy is often chosen as a target substrate for Ni- 
based HEAs due to their good plasticity [ 24 ]. The addition of Al 
element to the Ni–Fe alloy system generally contributes to the mi- 
crostructure transition from single FCC phase to FCC + BCC dual- 
phases [ 25 ]. Furthermore, the Al element can also induce the for- 
mation of L12 nanoprecipitates in the FCC phase and achieves 
a strong precipitation strengthening [ 26 ]. In addition, the V ele- 
ment with a large atomic radius was introduced into the Ni–Fe–Al 
ternary alloy system due to the large lattice distortion and mis- 
match degree, realizing a strong solid-solution strengthening for 
the Ni–Fe–Al alloy [ 27 ]. In this work, we chosen Ni–Fe–Al–V alloy 
system as target alloy system and designed Ni49 Fe28 Al17 V6 EHEA 
based on the phase diagram simulation. To further strengthen 
the designed Ni49 Fe28 Al17 V6 EHEA, we architected a unique het- 
erostructure in Ni49 Fe28 Al17 V6 EHEA by optimizing the thermal 
processing parameters. The obtained EHEA exhibits excellent me- 
chanical properties at room temperature and cryogenic tempera- 
ture. To reveal the physical origin of the high performance, we 
systematically studied the microstructure and deformation mech- 
anism of this EHEA at room temperature and cryogenic tempera- 
ture, which provides important theoretical guidance for the devel- 
opment of high-performance metals. 
The alloy ingot with the composition of Ni49 Fe8 Al17 V6 (at.%) 
was prepared by melting high-purity (99.95 wt%) alloy elements 
in argon atmosphere by arc melting. The ingot was melted at least 
ﬁve times to ensure its chemical uniformity, and subsequently the 
molten liquid was then drop-cast into a water-cooled copper mold 
with a size of 70 mm × 10 mm × 10 mm. The cast sheet was cold- 
rolled with a thickness reduction of ∼87 %, and then subjected to 
annealing at 750 °C for 1 h. The obtained sample was denoted as 
“CR-A750”. The microstructure of the CR-A750 samples was char- 
acterized by ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM, FEI 
Quanta 250F), electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD, Oxford In- 
struments Aztec 2.0), transmission electron microscopy (TEM, FEI 
Talos F200X), and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX). For 
the EBSD characterization, the samples were mechanically polished 
in a progressive way down to a water-based 0.02 μm colloidal 
silica suspension. For the TEM characterization, the samples were 
mechanically polished to a thickness of 80 μm and then punched 
to circle sheets with radius of Ф 3 mm. Subsequently, the punched 
samples were followed by a twin-jet electro-polishing in the mixed 
solution of HNO3 :CH4 O = 1:4. The dog-bone tensile samples with 
a gauge length of ∼18 mm, a thickness of ∼1.3 mm and a width 
of ∼3 mm, were used for tensile experiments at room tempera- 
ture (298 K) and cryogenic temperature (77 K), respectively. The 
tensile tests with a strain rate of 2 × 10−4 s−1 were performed at 
least three times at room temperature and cryogenic temperature 
to ensure the reproducibility of the obtained results. 
Fig. 1 (a) reveals that the as-cast alloy possesses a typical eutec- 
tic structure, i.e., alternating ultra-ﬁne FCC/L12 and BCC/B2 lamel- 
lar structures. To further conﬁrm the ordered and disordered struc- 
ture of FCC/L12 and BCC/B2 in the as-cast EHEA, detailed TEM 
characterization was performed ( Fig. 1 (b)). The selected electron 
diffraction pattern (SAED) shows that both FCC and BCC lamellae 
have chemical ordering characteristics, as revealed by the superlat- 
tice lattice in Fig. 1 (c, d). Moreover, combined with the calculated 
phase diagram and XRD patterns in Fig. S1 in Supplementary ma- 
terials, the as-cast EHEA was composed of FCC/L12 and B2 phases.

The enlarged TEM image ( Fig. 1 (j)) shows the formation of the L12 
precipitates with a size of ∼230 nm in B2 phase lamellar. TEM- 
EDS ( Fig. 1 (k)) shows that the L12 nanoprecipitates are rich in Ni, 
Fe and V elements. The Bright-ﬁeld TEM image in Fig. 1 (m) re- 
veals typical heterogeneous microstructure of CR-A750 EHEA, such 
as recrystallized grains, deformed grains and substructures. Specif- 
ically, the non-recrystallization region of FCC phase and B2 phase 
mainly presents the typical characteristics of high-density dislo- 
cation walls, which are mainly formed during cold-rolling defor- 
mation and subsequent aging treatment. In addition, high-density 
dislocations can also be observed in the FCC phase and B2 phase 
( Fig. 1 (n, o)). Ultra-ﬁne recrystallization FCC and B2 grains with the 
grain sizes of 200 and 300 nm, respectively, contain low disloca- 
tion densities. Fig. 1 (p, q) shows the SAED patterns of FCC/L12 and 
B2 phases, respectively. Such heterogeneous microstructure with 
different grain sizes and heterogeneous dislocation structure signif- 
icantly contributes to the enhancement of the comprehensive me- 
chanical properties of CR-A750 EHEA. 
The engineering stress–strain curves of CR-A750 EHEA at room 
temperature (298 K) and cryogenic temperature (77 K) in Fig. 2 (a) 
reveal that CR-A750 EHEA not only presents an excellent strength–
ductility combination at room temperature, but also shows ul- 
trahigh strength at cryogenic temperature. Speciﬁcally, CR-A750 
EHEA exhibits a high yield strength of ∼1550 MPa, a high ten- 
sile strength of ∼1772 MPa and a large ductility of ∼16.5 %, re- 
spectively. Furthermore, the yield strength and tensile strength of 
CR-A750 EHEA at cryogenic temperature increased to ∼1877 and 
∼2157 MPa, respectively, and uniform elongation remained ∼10 %.

The bright-ﬁled TEM image ( Fig. 3 (a)) indicates that the tensile- 
fractured CR-A750 EHEA at room temperature still maintains 
FCC/L12 and B2 lamellae structure. FCC lamellar presents higher 
dislocation density than that of B2 phase. Once plastic deformation 
begins, dislocation ﬁrst appears in the softer FCC phase. A large 
number of dislocation walls appear in the soft FCC/L12 lamellar 
( Fig. 3 (b)). As the deformation progresses, dislocations proliferate 
and slip continuously in soft FCC lamellar [ 39 ]. Due to the pres- 
ence of high-density L12 nanoprecipitates in FCC phase, the dislo- 
cation slip is signiﬁcantly hindered, i.e., a pinning effect, resulting 
in the formation of dislocation walls and dislocation cells. Mean- 
while, the strong interaction of high-density dislocations in FCC 
lamellar induces the formation of dislocation tangles, as shown in 
Fig. 3 (c). In addition, dislocations are diﬃcult to slip from soft FCC 
lamellar through the interface to hard B2 lamellar, resulting in the 
high-density dislocation pile-ups at the dual-phase interface. With 
further deformation, a small number of dislocations began to ap- 
pear in B2 lamella, which mainly came from two parts: (i) slip- 
ping from the FCC lamellar through the interface to a partial dis-

high-density dislocations form dislocation entanglement in FCC/L12

FCC and B2 phases, forming a dislocation accumulation at the dual-

The excellent mechanical properties of CR-A750 EHEA at room 
temperature mainly originated from its unique heterostructure, i.e., 
recrystallization structure, deformation structure and substructure 
in soft FCC phase and hard B2 phase. During the deformation of 
CR-A750 EHEA at room temperature, the soft FCC phase ﬁrst began 
to undergo plastic deformation, while the hard B2 phase remained 
elastic. And then, high-density GNDs are accumulated at the inter- 
face between soft FCC phase and hard B2 phase, inducing the for- 
mation of long-range back stress. Such long-range back stress can 
counteract the applied external stress, thus signiﬁcantly strength- 
ening the FCC phase and improving the overall yield strength of 
CR-A750 EHEA. Meanwhile, the GNDs at the dual-phase interface 
can cause strong positive stress in the adjacent B2 hard phase. HDI 
hardening mainly involves back stress and normal stress, which is 
mainly due to the formation of a large plastic strain gradient [ 38–
43 ]. In order to determine the contribution of HDI stress to the 
yield strength of CR-A750 EHEA, a loading–unloading–reloading 
(LUR) test was performed, as shown in Fig. S3. Near the yield 
point of 2 %, the HDI stress is about ∼895 MPa, which accounts 
for about 55 % of the yield strength. When the strain ranges from 
2 % to 5 %, the HDI stress tends to increase slowly, which may be 
due to the strong interaction between GNDs and slip dislocation or 
nano-precipitated phase. And then, it is diﬃcult for GNDs to pro- 
duce a high-density accumulation at the phase interface [ 21 ]. As 
the plastic deformation processes, the HDI stress rises rapidly due 
to the accumulation of dense GNDs at the phase interface. Obvi- 
ously, the HDI stress is always higher than the effective stress dur- 
ing the whole deformation process, indicating that the HDI stress 
plays a dominant role in contributing the mechanical properties of 
CR-A750 EHEA. Moreover, in addition to the contribution of back 
stress to the high yield strength, the presence of deformation twins 
signiﬁcantly strengthens the CR-A750 EHEA at cryogenic tempera- 
ture. Speciﬁcally, deformation twins can introduce additional inter- 
faces and reduce the mean free path of the dislocation, which is 
mainly related to the dynamic Hall–Petch effect [ 34 , 44 ]. These ad-

To summarize, we architected a dual-phase heterostructure 
containing FCC/L12 and B2 phases in CR-A750 EHEA via optimized 
thermal–mechanical processing, showing exceptional mechanical 
properties at room temperature and cryogenic temperature. Specif- 
ically, CR-A750 EHEA demonstrates a high yield strength of 
∼1550 MPa and a high tensile strength of ∼1772 MPa, while con- 
currently possessing a good ductility of ∼16.5 % at room tempera- 
ture. In particular, an ultra-high yield strength of ∼1877 MPa, an 
ultra-high tensile strength of ∼2157 MPa and remained uniform 
elongation of ∼10 % are achieved at cryogenic temperature. Such 
an excellent mechanical properties of CR-A750 EHEA at room tem- 
perature is attributed to the strong HDI hardening, which induced 
by the deformation incompatibility of non-recrystallized grains and 
the formation of ﬁne recrystallized grains in both of soft FCC phase 
and hard B2 phase. Furthermore, ultra-high yield strength of CR- 
A750 EHEA at cryogenic temperature originates not only from the 
strong HDI hardening caused by heterogeneous structure, but also 
from the strong interaction of deformation twins and high-density 
dislocations. Our results provide valuable theoretical guidance for 
the development of high-performance metals.
