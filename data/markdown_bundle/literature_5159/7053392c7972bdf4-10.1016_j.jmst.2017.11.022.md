1. Introduction
Mg alloys have attracted much attention as light weight mate-
rials for vehicle and aerospace applications due to the increasing
demand for reducing carbon dioxide emissions in transportation
applications [1–4]. Wrought Mg-Zn-Y alloy is one of the main high
strength Mg alloy systems [5–10]. Microstructures and mechanical
properties of Mg-Zn-Y alloys mainly depend on the ratio of Zn/Y
[11,12]. When the Zn/Y weight ratio varies between 2 and 7, the
icosahedral quasicrystal phase (I-phase) will be formed in Mg-Zn-
Y alloys [13–15]. The high crystal lattice symmetry of I phase and
low interfacial energy between I phase and /H9251-Mg matrix lead to
a strong I phase//H9251-Mg interfacial bonding [16,17]. Xu et al. [18]
report that an as-extruded Mg-5.5Zn-1.1Y-0.8Zr (wt%) alloy con-
taining I phase exhibits an ultimate tensile strength (UTS, /ESCb) of

When Zn/Y weight ratio is about 1.4–2.5, W phase (Mg3Y2Zn3)
will be the main intermetallic compound in Mg-Zn-Y alloys [16,24].
W phase is reported to deteriorate mechanical properties of Mg-Zn-
Y alloys due to their weak interfacial bonding with /H9251-Mg matrix
[24–26]. It is reported [18] that coarse W phase is too hard to be
deformed during tensile test, therefore, W phase can induce a high
stress concentration at particle/matrix interface, which could trig-
ger the formation of voids and further lead to the nucleation of
cracking, so the W phase has no strengthening effect on the as-cast
Mg-5.5Zn-3.1Y-0.8Zr alloy [18]. After extrusion, coarse W phase is
broken into ﬁne particles and distributed along the extrusion direc-
tion, which contributes to the high performance of the as-extruded
Mg-7Zn-6Y-0.6Zr (wt%) alloy with UTS of 280 MPa, TYS of 210 MPa
and elongation to failure of 12% [12]. This suggests that the homoge-
neously distributed ﬁne broken W-phase particles induced by hot
extrusion may act as an effective dispersive strengthening phase
[8,32]. In addition to the broken W phase, ﬁne W phase can also
precipitate during ageing treatment. For instance, in cast Mg-2Zn-
1Y-0.6Zr (wt%) alloy [27], granule-shape W phase precipitates after
T6 treatment, leading to obvious ageing strengthening.
In our previous research, an ultrahigh strength as-extruded
Mg-10Zn-6Y-0.5Zr-0.3Ca alloy was developed [34]. The ultrahigh
strength of the alloy is mainly attributed to a high density of
broken W phase and nano W dynamic precipitates. In order to fur-
ther investigate the inﬂuence of size and distribution of W phase
on mechanical properties of Mg-Zn-Y alloys, microstructure and
mechanical properties of Mg-5.1Zn-3.2Y-0.4Zr-0.4Ca (wt%) alloy
extruded at different temperatures were investigated to develop
low cost and high strength Mg-Zn-Y alloy.
2. Experimental
Mg-5.1Zn-3.2Y-0.4Zr-0.4Ca alloy was produced from pure Mg,
pure Zn, Mg-30Y and Mg-25Zr and Mg-25Ca master alloys (all in
wt.%). Ca and Zr were added in order to reﬁne microstructure of
Mg-Zn-Y alloy [28–30]. The alloy was prepared by permanent mold
direct-chill casting [31]. Alloy melt was kept at 720 ◦C for 20 min
in a cylinder steel crucible. Then the melt-containing crucible was
immerged into the cooling water at a speed of 40 mm min−1, then
the ingot with a diameter of 60 mm and length of 140 mm was
prepared. The rod billet with a diameter of 43 mm and height of
35 mm was cut from the ingot, then indirect-extruded at 350 ◦C
and 400 ◦C, respectively, under a ram speed of 0.1 mm s−1 and an
extrusion ratio of 12. The billet was hold at extrusion temperature
for 10 min prior to extrusion. The cylindrical extrusion rods with a
diameter of 12 mm and length of 230 mm were produced.

3. Results and discussion

observed at grain boundaries, as shown in Fig. 1(c) and (d). Fig. 1(d)
shows TEM bright ﬁeld image of the eutectic compound. The thick-

In Mg-Zn-Y alloy system, the main ternary intermetallic
compounds vary with Zn/Y weight ratio [11]. In the present Mg-
5.1Zn-3.2Y-0.4Zr-0.4Ca alloy with Zn/Y weight ratio of 1.59, W
phase is the main second phase, which is consistent with our
previous work on Mg-10Zn-6Y-Zr-Ca alloy [34]. In Mg-5.5Zn-3.1Y-
0.82Zr alloy with Zn/Y weight ratio of 1.78, the main second phase
is also W phase [18].

phase particle is more homogenous and the density of particle

at 400 ◦C (Fig. 3(c) and (d)). The effect of large non-deforming par-
ticles in
 promoting recrystallization via the mechanism of particle
stimulated nucleation (PSN) is well known and widely observed
[36]. Deformation zone with lattice rotation and a misorienta-
tion gradient forms around the broken W phase particles during
extrusion, and the accumulation of misorientation by the rapid
sub-boundary migration creates new high-angle grain boundaries
(HAGBs), leading to the formation of DRX nuclei. A higher extrusion
temperature makes the easy ﬂow of the matrix under the applied
stress, leading to more uniform particle distribution. Therefore, the
alloy extruded at 400 ◦C creates more DRX nuclei and a higher grain
growth rate, leading to a higher fraction of DRXed grains (Fig. 2). On
the other hand, the nano precipitates formed during extrusion can-
not induce enough deformation zone and misorientation gradient.
As a consequence, these nano-precipitates will pin the disloca-
tion and create a high density of dislocations which inhibit the
mobility of large angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) and suppress the
transformation from LAGBs to HAGBs, resulting in the formation of
unDRXed regions [33,37]. As shown in Fig. 5, the size and density of
the precipitates vary with extrusion temperature. When Mg-5.1Zn-
3.2Y-0.4Zr-0.4Ca alloy is extruded at 400 ◦C, the precipitates grow
up and their size increases (Fig. 5(b)).

As shown in Fig. 9, the as-extruded Mg-5.1Zn-3.2Y-0.4Zr-0.4Ca
alloy exhibits a microstructure containing multi-scaled second
phases. The micron-scale broken W phases are distributed in the
DRXed regions, while the nano-scale W phases are dynamically
precipitated mainly in the unDRXed regions during extrusion. It
is generally accepted that the distribution, size and space of the
second phases have a great inﬂuence on the ductility of an alloy.
During tensile deformation, the micro-scaled phases easily become
the origin of cracks due to the high stress concentration, while
the nano-scaled precipitates can inhibit the dislocation movement,
leading to the high density dislocations which contribute to the
strength [17,35].
Fig. 10 shows microstructures of the longitudinal section
near the tensile fraction surface of the as-extruded Mg-5.1Zn-
3.2Y-0.4Zr-0.4Ca alloys. It can be seen that the distributions of
micron-scaled particles have a great effect on fracture behavior
of the alloy. In the alloy extruded at 350 ◦C (Fig. 10(a)), a long
crack with more than 200 /H9262m in length is observed to propagate
through the DRXed and unDRXed areas. While in the alloy extruded
at 400 ◦C, many small cracks with 10 /H9262m in length are initiated at
different locations of particle bands, as shown in Fig. 10(b). Dur-
ing tensile test stress concentration is increased near the W phase
particles. When the stress concentration is sufﬁciently high, micro-
cracks will be formed in the areas near the W phase particles, and
then propagate through the matrix, resulting in poor elongation to
failure of the alloy extruded at 350 ◦C. While in the alloy extruded at
400 ◦C, the particle bands are distributed more homogeneously in
the matrix, which can reduce the local stress concentration around
particles. Although many micro-cracks initiate near W phase par-
ticles, no obvious crack propagation is observed (Fig. 10(b)).

precipitates, the nano-scaled precipitates in the unDRXed regions
act as barrier to dislocation motion. When Mg-5.1Zn-3.2Y-0.4Zr-
0.4Ca alloy is extruded at 400 ◦C, the precipitates grow up and
the size and the distance are both increasing than those of 350 ◦C
(Fig. 4). It has been reported that the geometric slip distance which
is the interparticle spacing /H9261 is proportional to the failure strain
[39–42]. In the present as-extruded Mg-5.1Zn-3.2Y-0.4Zr-0.4Ca
alloys, higher extrusion temperature leads to the growth of nano-
scale precipitates and larger /H9261, which may contribute to the higher
ductility of the alloy extruded at 400 ◦C.

4. Conclusion
The as-extruded Mg-5.1Zn-3.2Y-0.4Zr-0.4Ca alloy shows
bimodal microstructure. The alloy extruded at 350 ◦C exhibits
tensile yield strength of 373 MPa, ultimate tensile strength of
403 MPa and elongation to failure of 5.1%. While the alloy extruded
at 400 ◦C shows lower tensile yield strength of 332 MPa, ultimate
tensile strength of 352 MPa and higher elongation to failure of
12.0%. The extrusion temperature has a signiﬁcant inﬂuence on
distribution and size of W phase, which leads to obviously different
strength and ductility of the extruded Mg-5.1Zn-3.2Y-0.4Zr-0.4Ca
alloy. The homogeneous distribution of micron-scale broken W
phase particles in the alloy extruded at 400 ◦C leads to a higher
fraction of DRXed grains, contributing to high ductility of the alloy.
Lower extrusion temperature leads to ﬁner dynamic precipitates.
The dense nano precipitates result in a high fraction of unDRXed
regions, which contributes to higher strength of the alloy extruded
at 350 ◦C.
