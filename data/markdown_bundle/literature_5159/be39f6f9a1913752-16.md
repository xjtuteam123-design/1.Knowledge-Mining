ABSTRACT
Generative AI provides a powerful route for predicting material behavior in data-scarce regimes, an especially 
pressing challenge for refractory alloys where extreme processing environments and high melting points hinder 
large-scale data collection. Here, we introduce a Transformer-based generative framework that models the entire 
deformation trajectory rather than focusing solely on isolated scalar properties (such as YS, UTS), by synthesizing 
full stress – strain curves of Nb-W-based refractory alloys. Applied to Nb521-derived systems, the model captures 
the sequential evolution of tensile deformation and accurately predicts elastic – plastic responses across a wide 
compositional and processing space. The framework demonstrates utility in three downstream tasks: (i) inverse 
design of composition and processing, yielding > 35% strength improvement without loss of ductility; (ii) 
interpretable regime analysis via a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) mechanism, highlighting the beneficial roles of Hf 
and Ta in ductility, consistent with experimental and DFT validation; and (iii) seamless integration of generated 
curves into finite element simulations for structural performance assessment. Together, these results establish a 
coherent generative-analytic-predictive workflow for mechanism-aware, data-efficient design of advanced re -
fractory alloys.
1. Introduction
Refractory alloys, particularly Nb- and W-based systems, are critical 
structural materials for next-generation aerospace, nuclear, and hyper -
sonic applications due to their exceptional high-temperature strength 
and thermal stability [ 1 – 6 ]. Despite decades of development, achieving 
a favorable balance between strength and ductility remains a major 
challenge [ 7 – 9 ]. These multicomponent solid solutions, often further 
strengthened by interstitial dopants such as C, N, and B, present a vast 
design space where alloying, processing, and microstructural evolution 
are strongly interdependent [ 10 – 13 ]. Their mechanical responses are 
inherently path-dependent, governed by evolving internal states such as 
dislocation accumulation, phase transformation, and damage localiza -
tion [ 14 – 17 ]. Consequently, accurate performance prediction requires 
modeling the entire deformation trajectory rather than focusing solely 
on isolated scalar properties.
Among the various descriptors of mechanical performance, the 
tensile stress – strain curve is particularly valuable because it captures not 
only scalar quantities such as yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, 
and ductility, but also the evolution of work hardening and deformation 
history. In Nb-W-based refractory alloys, these responses arise from a 
combination of intrinsic mechanisms (e.g., solid-solution strengthening, 
phase stability) and extrinsic microstructural features (e.g., grain size, 
precipitate morphology) that depend strongly on thermomechanical 
history [ 18 – 20 ]. These mechanisms are highly coupled and 
path-dependent, and conventional scalar-property regression cannot 
capture their full complexity, making Nb-W alloys an ideal system to 
demonstrate the advantages of a generative stress – strain modeling 
framework. To establish a practical benchmark for model development, 
we focus on the widely used Nb521 alloy (Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr-0.07C, wt 
%). In the fully recrystallized condition, Nb521 shows a tensile strength 
of ~500 MPa at room temperature and > 300 MPa at 1200 
◦
C [ 21 , 22 ]. 
While these values demonstrate good thermal stability, the alloy ’ s 
limited strength – ductility balance highlights the need for systematic

Despite decades of progress in constitutive modeling, capturing the 
full mechanical response of materials across diverse chemistries and 
processing regimes remains challenging. Traditional physically based 
approaches — including empirical flow rules [ 23 – 26 ], crystal plasticity 
theory [ 27 – 30 ], and multiscale simulations such as molecular dynamics 
[ 31 – 33 ] — provide mechanistic insight but often require extensive 
parameter calibration, rely on idealized assumptions (e.g., microstruc -
tural homogeneity or simplified loading paths), and lack transferability 
across systems. In response, data-driven surrogate models have been 
proposed to accelerate property prediction by learning from existing 
datasets [ 34 – 39 ]. However, most are formulated as supervised regres -
sion tasks targeting a limited set of scalar outputs (e.g., yield strength, 
Young ’ s modulus), and thus cannot capture the sequential, 
history-sensitive nature of full-field deformation responses. Moreover, 
these models often generalize poorly to unseen composition-process 
regimes. Several recent efforts have applied sequence models (such as 
RNN, LSTM) to the full stress-strain curve prediction. Yet many rely on 
synthetic datasets generated from simplified constitutive models (e.g., 
Ramberg-Osgood, Johnson-Cook), which do not reflect the nonlinear, 
path-dependent complexities observed in experimental deformation 
behavior [ 40 – 43 ]. As a result, such models often miss critical featur -
es — such as yield point phenomena, transient hardening, or 
post-necking softening — and are limited in their utility for performance 
prediction or alloy optimization.
To overcome the limitations of traditional constitutive models and 
largely supervised-based ML approaches, we develop a generative 
modeling framework based on the Transformer architecture for pro -
ducing full-field stress-strain curves of Nb-W-based refractory alloys. 
Trained on experimentally measured data, the model learns to generate 
continuous, path-dependent deformation trajectories conditioned on 
alloy composition and processing parameters. Within this alloy family, 
we demonstrate three representative applications: (i) inverse design of 
Nb-W alloys with enhanced strength – ductility balance, (ii) interpretable 
identification of elemental effects (Ta, Hf, W) on strain hardening and 
ductility, and (iii) finite element integration of generated constitutive 
curves for structural performance assessment. Together, these results 
establish a mechanism-aware and data-efficient framework tailored to 
Nb-W alloys. By leveraging full stress – strain trajectory generation, this 
approach captures path-dependent deformation features, advancing the 
methodological frontier of machine learning in constitutive modeling 
while providing practical guidance for the design of refractory alloys 
with optimized strength – ductility synergy.
2. Methodology
2.1. Multimodal-transformer-enabled constitutive model
As shown in Fig. 1 (a), our multimodal-Transformer-enabled consti -
tutive modeling framework integrates three core components: (i) data 
preprocessing and representation, (ii) a multimodal Transformer-based 
generative model for strain-stress curve (hereafter referred to as 
MTGSS), and (iii) downstream applications for alloy design and analysis.
2.1.1. Data collection and representation
The training dataset consists of 92 tensile stress-strain curves 
collected from two sources: 8 curves were obtained from published 
literature on refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), and 84 were 
generated from our group ’ s experimental studies on Nb-based refractory 
alloys. The data encompass a range of compositions involving the ele -
ments Nb, W, Mo, Zr, Hf, Ti, Ta, Si, V, Sc , Ru, B, Y, C, and N. These curves 
span a wide range of compositions and thermomechanical processing 
conditions, and each is stored as a discrete array of paired strain-stress 
values, typically up to 2 % strain, with 512 uniformly sampled points.
To complement the mechanical response data, we compute a set of 
physically meaningful alloy descriptors for each composition, including 
valence electron concentration (VEC), mixing enthalpy, mixing entropy, 
and atomic size mismatch. These features help embed domain knowl -
edge into the learning process and improve the model's generalizability.

separately encoded using fully connected neural networks and 
concatenated to form the input embedding. The resulting repre -
sentation is processed through a multi-head self-attention 
mechanism, enabling the model to learn temporal and physical 
correlations within the data.
In parallel, the MTGSS model receives a side input in the form of a 
natural language description of the alloy ’ s composition and processing 
route. We design a standardized prompt template to unify descriptions 
across samples (e.g., “ Homogenize at 1800

attention mechanism. This enables the model to align numerical features 
with contextual knowledge, such as thermomechanical processing ef -
fects or alloying strategies.
To account for the distinct mechanical regimes present in stress- 
strain curves — namely elastic deformation, yielding, and plastic flow -
— we incorporate a Mixture of Experts (MoE) layer into the Transformer 
architecture. In the present work, 6 experts of sub-networks are adopted 
to capture the shape of the strain-stress response. Each expert is trained 
to specialize in a particular deformation regime, and the model 
dynamically selects the most 2 appropriate experts for each segment of 
the curve. This mechanism enhances both predictive accuracy and 
physical interpretability by allocating model capacity to regime-specific 
response features.
As illustrated in Fig. 1 (b), the core model architecture consists of a 
stack of six Transformer blocks, each comprising a series of key sub -
modules. The block begins with a multi-head self-attention mechanism, 
which captures both temporal dependencies within the strain sequence 
and correlations among the physics-informed descriptors. This is fol -
lowed by a multi-head cross-attention module that integrates contextual 
information from the textual embeddings, allowing the model to align 
semantic knowledge from alloy composition and processing with the 
numerical features. To ensure stable optimization and effective gradient 
flow, each attention layer is equipped with residual connections fol -
lowed by layer normalization (Add & Norm). Finally, a MoE layer is 
incorporated at the end of each block to dynamically allocate compu -
tational resources to specialized expert subnetworks, each of which fo -
cuses on modeling a specific mechanical regime — such as elastic, 
yielding, or plastic deformation. This modular design enhances both 
prediction accuracy and interpretability by enabling the model to 
adaptively specialize across different stages of the stress-strain response.
The MTGSS model was trained to autoregressively generate the stress 
value at each strain step by minimizing a composite loss function that 
combines mean squared error, Kullback-Leibler divergence, and a cur -
vature penalty, thereby balancing numerical accuracy and physical 
smoothness. Unlike traditional regression models that only predict sca -
lar properties, our framework outputs full stress-strain trajectories, 
enabling rich, path-aware modeling of mechanical behavior under ten -
sion. Model architecture, training parameters, and the loss function are 
detailed in Supplementary Note 1 (Supporting Information).
2.1.3. Downstream tasks
In downstream applications, this generative framework supports 
three representative tasks: (1) Inverse design, where the model is used to 
search for alloy/process combinations that achieve target mechanical 
properties; (2) Mechanism discovery, by analyzing the learned attention 
patterns and expert activations to identify which elements (e.g., Hf, Ta) 
contribute to enhanced room-temperature ductility, and (3) Finite 
element integration, where the generated stress-strain curves are 
directly used in structural simulations for performance evaluation.
Together, this multimodal Transformer framework enables a unified, 
data-efficient, and mechanism-aware approach to modeling and 
designing next-generation refractory alloys. The complete details of the 
dataset, the multimodal-transformer-enabled constitutive model, as well 
as the code for the implementation, have been uploaded to https://githu 
b.com/weidaokuo/MTGSS/tree/master .

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Validation of predictive performance
To systematically assess the predictive performance and extrapola -
tive generalization of the proposed MTGSS model, we conducted a 
stringent pointwise evaluation of predicted versus reference stress 
values using a composition-stratified five-fold cross-validation protocol 
on the Nb-W-based refractory alloy dataset. Each stress – strain curve was 
discretized into stress values at uniformly sampled strain levels, 
enabling direct pointwise comparisons at identical strain steps. To 
enforce a genuine extrapolation setting, the dataset was partitioned into 
five composition strata according to Nb and W contents (19, 19, 18, 18, 
and 18 samples), and five-fold cross-validation was performed by 
holding out one stratum for testing while training on the remaining four. 
This design prevents any test-fold compositions (and their close Nb-W 
neighborhoods under the stratification) from appearing in the corre -
sponding training folds, thereby providing a faithful assessment of out- 
of-distribution extrapolation to unseen alloy chemistries.

our framework achieves superior accuracy by integrating rich compo -

3.3. Interpretable analysis of alloying effects
To gain insight into the influence of individual alloying elements on 
mechanical behavior, we leverage the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) archi -
tecture embedded in the MTGSS model. In this setup, each expert sub -
network is implicitly specialized to model a distinct deformation 
regime — such as elastic response, yield initiation, strain hardening, or 
plastic softening. During stress-strain curve generation, the model 
dynamically allocates varying attention weights to these experts 
depending on the strain state and input features. By tracking the expert 
activation patterns across different alloys and strain intervals, we obtain 
a fine-grained, stage-wise understanding of how different alloying ele -
ments affect mechanical response.

3.4. Structural performance evaluation via finite element integration
To assess the structural applicability of the constitutive model 
generated by our MTGSS framework, we incorporated the predicted 
stress-strain curves into a finite element (FEM) simulation pipeline. As 
shown in Fig. 9 (a), the uniaxial tensile response of a flat dog-bone 
specimen was simulated using the predicted curve of the 
Nb6216 – 1.5Hf alloy. The FEM-predicted stress-strain response (red) 
exhibits excellent agreement with the experimental data (black), accu -
rately capturing the elastic modulus, yield point, and strain hardening 
characteristics. The inset illustrates the geometry and dimensions of the 
specimen, ensuring consistency between computational inputs and 
physical testing configurations.
Fig. 9 (b) further demonstrates the reliability of the MTGSS-derived 
constitutive model by simulating the deformation behavior of a thin 
Nb6216 – 1.5Hf sheet with a central circular hole under uniaxial tension. 
The evolution of von Mises stress contours at various strain stages re -
veals the progressive development of stress localization. Initially, stress 
is uniformly distributed, but with increasing strain, it begins to 
concentrate symmetrically along the transverse axis, forming a 
butterfly-shaped stress field centered at the hole. This localization in -
tensifies during the plastic regime, eventually extending along both 
transverse and longitudinal directions.
Such simulations highlight the model ’ s ability to not only predict 
global mechanical performance but also capture intricate local stress 
redistribution in defect-bearing geometries. This capability is especially 
crucial for the structural integrity assessment of components operating 
in extreme environments, where pre-existing micro-defects and geo -
metric discontinuities are common. Together, Figs. 9 (a) and 9(b) vali -
date that our generative framework serves as a bridge between 
materials-level design and component-level structural analysis.
4. Conclusions
In summary, we developed a multimodal generative modeling 
framework based on the Transformer architecture to predict full-field 
tensile stress-strain responses of Nb-W-based refractory alloys. By inte -
grating structured numerical descriptors, unstructured compositional 
and processing text prompts, and autoregressive sequence learning, the 
model effectively captures the nonlinear, path-dependent mechanical 
behavior of complex alloys. The model has been applied to the Nb – W 
alloy system and shown to be effective for three representative tasks. (1) 
In inverse design, the generative model is coupled with an active 
learning loop using a physically meaningful curve-level metric — the 
expected improvement of stress-strain response. Applying this approach, 
we synthesized the alloy Nb-6W-2Mo-1Zr-6Ta-1.5Hf-0.05C-0.2N (wt %) 
which, under optimized full-recrystallization annealing conditions, 
achieves over 35 % improvement in strength without sacrificing 
ductility compared to commercial Nb521 alloy. (2) The incorporation of 
a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) mechanism enables interpretable decom -
position of deformation regimes within the stress-strain curves. Expert- 
specific attention highlights the positive contributions of Ta and Hf to 
ductility, which are further confirmed by experimental observation and 
DFT calculation. (3) The generated stress-strain curves are seamlessly 
integrated into finite element simulations, enabling high-fidelity struc -
tural performance prediction under complex loading scenarios.
These results highlight the potential of sequential generative 
modeling to accelerate the exploration of composition-process-property 
relationships in refractory alloys. While this study focuses on Nb-W 
systems and composition-driven descriptors, the framework is readily 
extendable to other material classes and to processing path modeling, 
provided sufficient multimodal data are available. This work establishes 
a unified, mechanism-aware workflow for data-efficient constitutive 
modeling and design of refractory alloys.
