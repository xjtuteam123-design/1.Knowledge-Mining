abstract
NieW heterogeneous seed was used to prepare the Ni-based single crystal superalloys and
the microstructure at remelting interface under different conditions was analyzed. The
results showed that there were lots of ﬁne cellular grains with the basically consistent
orientations and compositions at the remelting interface under high rate solidiﬁcation
condition. However, these grains disappeared under the liquid metal cooling condition,
indicating that the formation of ﬁne cellular grains might be controlled by constitutional
undercooling related with the temperature gradient in front of the remelting interface.
Therefore, a relationship between constitutional undercooling and temperature gradient
was calculated to clarify the solidiﬁcation mechanism of ﬁne cellular grains. It was found
that the high rate solidiﬁcation method with a smaller temperature gradient could produce
an enough constitutional undercooling at the front of remelting interface to form ﬁne
cellular grains, and then they competed with each other to adjust the dendrite spacing,
ﬁnally obtained the single crystal microstructure with uniform dendrite spacing, while that
microstructure could only be obtained through the ﬂat-cell-branch transformation under
the LMC condition.

1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal superalloys have been widely used in
aero engine and turbine blades due to their excellent me-
chanical properties, creep and fatigue resistance [ 1,2]. How-
ever, with the increase of the aeroengine inlet temperature,
Ni-based single crystal superalloys with random secondary
orientation are no longer sufﬁcient to meet the needs in some
extreme circumstances where the accurate orientation is
strictly required for properties assurance [ 3]. Generally, there
are two basic ways for preparing Ni-based single crystal su-
peralloys: selection method and seeds technique. The selec-
tion method is simple and efﬁcient but unable to fully utilize
the temperature bearing capacity of Ni-based single crystal
superalloys because it only controls the <001> direction at a
certain angle and the second dendrite orientation is
completely random [4]. Therefore, the seeds method is further
developed, which can accurately control the three-

Traditional seeds have usually a complex alloying
composition and a large crystallization temperature interval,
therefore, it is difﬁcult to avoid the heterogeneous nucleation
of stray grains because of dendrite fragmentations in pasty
zone [ 6,7]. For this reason, Ford et al. [ 8] ﬁrstly used Ni eWo r
NieTa alloy as heterogeneous seed with few pasty regions to
prepare Ni-based single crystal superalloy. Furthermore, Tol-
oraiya and Azhazha et al. [ 9e12] found that Ni eW heteroge-
neous seed could obtain a completely cellular structure at a
large withdraw rate and signiﬁcantly reduce the formation of
stray grains, improving the success rate of preparing single
crystals. In addition, Zhao et al. [ 13] studied the planar-cell-
dendritic instability transition at the remelting interface of
NieW heterogeneous seeds and observed the existence of
fusion zone. And then, Guo et al. [ 14] studied the formation
mechanism of the fusion zone for Ni eW heterogeneous seed
under the high rate solidiﬁcation (HRS) condition. It was found
that compared with traditional homogeneous seeds, although
NieW heterogeneous seed could effectively avoid the forma-
tion of stray grains at the remelting interface, lots of ﬁne
cellular grains were existed in front of the fusion zone. How-
ever, as an important initial microstructure at the remelting
interface, little attention had been devoted to the formation
mechanism and competition relationship of these ﬁne cellular
grains.
In this work, an experiment was designed to investigate
the formation mechanism and competition relationship of
ﬁne cellular grains. The orientation and composition of
different ﬁne cellular grains were analyzed by scanning elec-
tron microscopy (SEM) with an electron backscatter diffrac-
tion (EBSD) and an energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS).
Finally, the formation mechanism of ﬁne cellular grains was
discussed based on a calculation between constitutional
undercooling and temperature gradient.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure and segregation ratio of Ni eW seeds
The microstructure of Ni eW heterogeneous seed was inves-
tigated ﬁrst and the DD3 homogeneous seed was also pre-
pared under the same condition as a comparison, the results
were displayed in Fig. 1. It was seen that Ni eW heterogeneous
seed had a cellular morphology with about 163.7 mm spacing
while DD3 homogeneous seed had a dendrite morphology
with about 238.4 mm as shown in Fig 1 a and c. Furthermore,
the EPMA results of DD3 and Ni eW seeds for the composition
were shown in Fig 1b and d, and then the segregation ratio was
calculated in Table 2 . It was obvious that the W elements in
both seeds were concentrated on the core of branches. How-
ever, the segregation ratio (1.29) of Ni eW heterogeneous seed
was much lower than that (2.03) of DD3 homogeneous seed.
3.2. Morphology of remelting interface of Ni eW seeds
NieW heterogeneous seed was further used to prepare DD3
single crystal superalloy by HRS and LMC conditions, and the
vertical microstructures of remelting interface were shown in
Fig. 2. It was clear that the vertical microstructure at remelting
interface under HRS and LMC conditions could be divided into
three regions: a complete melting zone, a narrow fusion zone
and an original tissue zone. And it was seen that lots of ﬁne
cellular grains competed with each other were present above
the fusion zone under the HRS condition in Fig. 2a, and these
grains which not obtained enough growth space were blocked
by highly differentiated cellular grains, and gradually elimi-
nated during the growth. However, these ﬁne cellular grains
disappeared under the LMC condition. Furthermore, some ﬁne
cellular grains were transformed into dendrites through a cell-
branch transformation and ﬁnally formed the microstructure
with uniform dendrite spacing for DD3 single crystal super-
alloys. In a word, lots of ﬁne cellular grains were existed above
the fusion zone under the HRS condition but disappeared
under the LMC condition.

3.3. Microstructure orientation and composition of ﬁne
cellular grains
Based on the results above, it was clear that these ﬁne cellular
grains were grown from the fusion zone, where the compo-
sition should be close to Ni eW heterogeneous seed. Mean-
while, according to the theory of competitive growth that the
solute interactions at grain boundaries might cause an elim-
ination of dominant orientation grains [ 15,16], these ﬁne
cellular grains with different degrees of differentiation might
differ in compositions and orientations. Therefore, EBSD and
EPMA was used to further investigate the growth process at
remelting interface. The EBSD result was shown in Fig. 3 a,
where a schematic diagram for grains grown from remelting
interface was draw in Fig. 3b for a better understanding. It was
clear that all ﬁne cellular grains and dendrite microstructures
had the same orientation near <001> direction as shown in
Fig. 3 c. In addition, EPMA was used to investigate the
composition distribution of different differentiated cellular
grains marked by some points in Fig. 3 a, and the analyzed
results were displayed in Table 3 . It was seen that the com-
ponents were very consistent in all different differentiated
cell, especially on the points of 1 and 6, 2 and 8, 3 and 9, which
were basically located at the same height as shown in Fig. 3a.
4. Discussion
The results above showed that all of ﬁne cellular grains in
front of remelting interface had basically the same orientation
and composition. In other words, the orientation and
composition between different grains were irrelevant to the
competitive growth. For this reason, it was deduced that the
competition between different ﬁne cellular particles in cur-
rent work might be a method to adjust the spacing after the
appearance of ﬁne cellular grains, since dendritic had always
a certain spacing under the certain condition [ 17]. As an
important initial microstructure at the remelting interface,
the ﬁne cellular grains played an important role in deter-
mining the growth of single crystal. Therefore, their formation
mechanism should be illustrated.
Considering the temperature gradient is the main differ-
ence between HRS and LMC methods and it usually had a
close relationship with constitutional undercooling in front of
remelting interface [ 18]. Therefore, the constitutional

As a result, the formation mechanism of ﬁne cellular grains
could be summarized to constitutional undercooling. When
the solid solution alloy was solidiﬁed under a positive tem-
perature gradient, the melt temperature at the front of the
solideliquid interface would lower than the actual liquidus
temperature. Accompany with the increase of constitutional
undercooling, the interface shape would gradually develop
from straight interface to cell and ﬁnally to dendrite. There-
fore, HRS condition with a much smaller temperature gradient
could produce an enough constitutional undercooling in front
of the remelting interface and generated ﬁne cellular grains.
After that, ﬁne cellular grains emerged in front of the
remelting interface, these grains started to compete with each
other, and then highly differentiated, developed into single
crystal structure with uniform dendrite spacing. However, due
to the inhibition effect from LMC condition on the constitu-
tional undercooling, the remelting interface under LMC con-
dition would appear as ﬂat. The microstructure of Ni-based
single crystal superalloys could only be obtained through the
ﬂat-cell-branch transformation. In summary, the formation of
ﬁne cellular grains was controlled by constitutional under-
cooling, and when constitutional undercooling was small,
such as HRS method, the remelting interface would be
cellular, when under LMC condition where constitutional
undercooling was greatly inhibited, the remelting interface
appeared as ﬂat.

(1) Lots of ﬁne cellular grains with the basically consistent
orientations and compositions were existed above the
fusion zone under HRS condition, while they dis-
appeared under the LMC condition.
(2) A relationship between constitutional undercooling and
temperature gradient was calculated to clarify the

transformation under the LMC condition.
