Abstract. A design of precipitate-containing, ultrafine-grained equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy 
alloy with the addition of nitrogen was introduced. The introduction of nitrogen led to the formation of 
Cr− and N−rich precipitate of Cr 2N on the low temperature-annealed N-CoCrFeMnNi. It was noticed 
that the presence of nitrogen in N-CoCrFeMnNi suppressed the formation of σ−phase precipitate 
generally observed in low temperature-annealed CoCrFeMnNi system. Furthermore, it was observed 
that the addition of nitrogen could be utilized as a viable method in enhancing the mechanical properties 
of ultrafine-grained CoCrFeMnNi matrix, in which the addition of nitrogen successfully led to an 
improved yield strength and ultimate tensile strength of the ultrafine-grained CoCrFeMnNi system. 
Keywords: metals and alloys; high-entropy alloys; nitrogen; precipitate; microstructure; mechanical 
properties.

1. Introduction
Several strengthening mechanisms to enhance the mechanical properties of complex medium- 
and high-entropy alloy (MHEA) systems have been intensively studied in recent times. The exploration of 
additional strengthening mechanisms is required, in which despite MHEAs are generally known for their 
stress−strain balance over a wide temperature range, their mechanical properties were observed to 
decrease under several circumstances [1]. In general, grain boundary strengthening is one of the most 
widely implemented methods, as this strengthening technique has been proved to be effective in 
enhancing the mechanical properties of MHEAs [2]. Precipitation strengthening, which could be 
introduced via either addition of particles or elements or through an applied heat treatment, has also been 
studied to be a feasible method in improving the mechanical properties of MHEAs [3]. As for solid-
solution strengthening which generally carried out by the addition of either substitutional or interstitial 
element, this method was also reported to be a cost-effective method to be implemented as a 
strengthening method in MHEAs [4].
Herein, we proposed a design of precipitation-containing, ultrafine-grained equiatomic 
CoCrFeMnNi with the addition of interstitial nitrogen. The ultrafine-grained CoCrFeMnNi extensively 
studied MHEAs, was also reported to deliver promising mechanical properties [5]. Moreover, nitrogen 
was selected as the strengthening element in enhancing the mechanical properties which is able to form 
Cr2N to deliver precipitation hardening as well as interstitial solid-solution hardening [6]. It is expected 
that the addition of nitrogen could further enhance the mechanical properties of ultrafine-grained 
CoCrFeMnNi system. 
2. Experimental Procedures
Raw materials with high purity (>99.95 wt.%) were arranged in a fused alumina crucible, 
followed by heating to 1773 K in a super kanthal vertical furnace. After the temperature reached 1773 K, 
ultrahigh purity (>99.999%) nitrogen gas was injected for 24 h to saturate the alloy with the nitrogen. 
This was followed by quenching the molten metal in NaCl-saturated water, and the surface was later 
ground. For comparison purpose, N-free CoCrFeMnNi was also prepared by an arc-melting process.
Both ingots initially were homogenized at 1473 K for 24 h under an Ar atmosphere. 
Measurement of nitrogen content by using a LECO combustion analyzer displayed a nitrogen content of

## Page 4

0.95 at.%. This process was followed by a 90% thickness reduction to eliminate any as-cast structure 
using cold-rolling method. To promote ultrafine grain structure, low-temperature annealing at 973 K for 1 
h was performed for both specimens (hereby the specimens will be referred as NC1 for N-CoCrFeMnNi 
and C1 for CoCrFeMnNi), followed by a subsequent water quenching. 
To prepare for microstructural observation, SiC papers was utilized to grind the surfaces, 
followed by an electropolishing with 90% acetic acid + 10% perchloric acid solution. The general 
microstructural observation was performed using a field-emission scanning electron microscope (FE-
SEM) in backscatter electron (BSE) mode. For the precipitate analysis, a transmission electron 
microscope (TEM) was utilized. The TEM disk-shaped specimens with a diameter of 3 mm was polished 
using a twin-jet polisher. Further microstructural observation was carried out by using a FE-SEM with a 
detector of electron back-scattered diffraction (SEM-EBSD). 
To investigate the effect of nitrogen toward mechanical properties, a tensile test using flat dog-
bone-shaped specimens with a gage length of 5 mm, gage width of 2.5 mm, and thickness of 1 mm was 
performed. The tensile test with the initial strain rate of 8.3 × 10 -4 s -1 was performed at ambient 
temperature. For an accurate strain value to be obtained, a digital image correlation (DIC) method 
analyzed using a VIC-2D software was utilized. 
3. Results and Discussions

The general microstructure observation of both specimens is displayed in Fig. 1. Both specimens 
exhibited the formation of ultrafine grain size of 0.97 and 0.94 µm for C1 and NC1, respectively. Both C1 
and NC1 also displayed the presence of precipitate formation, as marked with yellow and red arrows in

Fig. 1a−b, respectively. In C1, this precipitate has been well-recognized to be Cr−rich σ-phase precipitate 
[7], which is commonly formed in low-temperature annealed equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi. 
Fig. 2. TEM observation on NC1. (a) DF image; (b–g) TEM-EDS mapping of each constituent in NC1 
from (a); (h) FFT pattern of the precipitate; (i) SEM-EBSD phase map.
Analysis of the formed precipitate in NC1 was performed by using TEM. The TEM-energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (TEM-EDS) mapping of different constituents from the dark-field (DF) image in 
Fig. 2a showed that the precipitate was mainly enriched in Cr and N in Fig. 2(b–g). This was further 
confirmed by the TEM-EDS point observation displayed as the inset table in Fig. 2 , in which the 
precipitate contains high content of Cr and N combined with small presence of Mn. The Fast-Fourier 
transform (FFT) pattern showed that this precipitate is indexed as Cr 2N precipitate ( Fig. 2h ). By 
employing SEM-EBSD, it was observed that the Cr 2N precipitate was dispersed mainly dispersed in the 
vicinity of the grain boundary and triple junction region of the face-centered cubic (FCC) matrix, while 
there was not any σ-phase formation observed in NC1 (Fig. 2i). Here, due to the lower mixing enthalpy of 
Cr–N binding, the formation of this nitride is more favorable than the Cr−substitutional constituents 
bonding in σ-phase [8]. Meanwhile, the formation of Cr 2N was more favored than other possible

Cr−nitride in this system due to its lower Gibbs free energy of mixing. Here, it could also be concluded 
that the formation of ultrafine grain in both systems of NC1 and C1 was affected by the Zener pinning 
pressure contributed by the Cr2N and σ-phase precipitates, respectively [unpublished work]. 
Fig. 3. (a) Tensile test result; Fractography of (b.1) NC1, and (b.2) C1; (c) Strain hardening rate of NC1 
and C1. 
 Investigation was then performed to analyze the effect of nitrogen addition toward the 
mechanical properties enhancement in equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi. It was observed by tensile test result 
that the addition of nitrogen successfully improved the yield strength (σ y) and ultimate tensile strength 
(σUTS) of the ultrafine-grained matrix of CoCrFeMnNi ( Fig. 3a). Increment of σy by ~57 MPa and σUTS by 
~66.5 MPa with the addition of nitrogen indicates the feasibility of nitrogen to be implemented as a 
strengthening element in MHEAs, particularly in CoCrFeMnNi system. The stress-strain curve also 
displayed a discontinuous yielding phenomenon for both specimens (yellow arrows in Fig. 3a ). This 
phenomenon indicates the presence of the dislocation interlocking phenomenon by the presence of grain 
boundary. Meanwhile, the fractography for both specimens showed a similar tendency of dimples 
formation interspersed with finer dimples ( Fig. 3b.1–2). The dimple depth for both specimens was also

## Page 7

observed to be similar, suggesting the similar engineering strain value and fracture behavior of both 
specimens. As for the strain hardening curve in Fig. 3c, both specimens displayed a similar behavior with 
the presence of small inflection point, caused by the dislocation interlocking phenomenon mentioned 
earlier.
The discussion is then put to observe the behavior of solute interstitial nitrogen in the FCC 
matrix in inhibiting dislocation movement and enhancing the mechanical properties of NC1. This 
discussion is performed, as this aspect is what differentiates NC1 with C1 the most. It was reported earlier 
that the addition of interstitial element in an austenitic stainless steel matrix contributed to a higher 
strength compared to that of substitutional element added in the similar amount [9]. Compared to several 
viable interstitial element, i.e. carbon, nitrogen was reported to contribute to higher local distortion in the 
FCC matrix than carbon. This leads to higher strain fields and atomic misfit, which ultimately contribute 
to the yield strength enhancement [10,11]. It was further observed that nitrogen exhibited a high binding 
energy of dislocation-interstitial atom (H B), in which the H B value for nitrogen-dislocation was observed 
to be higher compared to that of carbon–dislocation [11]. This value of H B also tended to increase with 
higher nitrogen content, indicating the dependence of the presence of nitrogen toward dislocation 
movement inhibition. Moreover, due to the presence of electrostatic force between the negatively charged 
nitrogen atoms and the positively charged dislocation nuclei, high capability of dislocation pinning could 
be achieved [11]. In the case of plastic deformation at ambient temperature, it was observed that this 
plastic deformation more often occurred as the result of nucleation of another dislocation instead of the 
unpinning of interstitial element, in this case interstitial nitrogen atom [12]. All of these aspects suggest 
the feasibility of interstitial nitrogen as a strengthening interstitial element in MHEAs. Further 
observation will then be performed to fully investigate the detailed strengthening mechanism and 
contribution by the presence of nitrogen in MHEAs.
Conclusions
In summary, the strengthening by nitrogen addition in ultrafine-grained equiatomic 
CoCrFeMnNi system was successfully observed. The addition of nitrogen 0.95 at.% of low temperature-
annealed N 0.95-CoCrFeMnNi led to the formation of Cr 2N precipitate. The presence of nitrogen favored 
the formation of Cr 2N precipitate instead of σ-phase generally found in low temperature-annealed

CoCrFeMnNi. Furthermore, higher mechanical properties of yield strength and ultimate tensile strength 
was achieved by nitrogen addition in the ultrafine-grained matrix of CoCrFeMnNi.
