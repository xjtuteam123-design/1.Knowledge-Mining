Ni-Rich Phases in Al-12%Si-4%Cu-1.2%Mn-x%Ni
Heat-Resistant Alloys and Effect of Ni-Alloying on Tensile
Mechanical Properties
Hengcheng Liao, Guangjin Li, and Qu Liu
(Submitted October 18, 2018; Accepted: 20 August 2019)
In this study, formation of Ni-rich phases in Al-12 wt.%Si-4 wt.%Cu-1.2 wt.%Mn-xwt.%Ni ( x = 0.8, 2.0,
2.4, 3.4) alloys and their effect on high temperature strength were investigated by microstructure char-
acterization and tensile test. Three types of Ni-rich phases are observed: d-Al3CuNi, c-Al7Cu4Ni and e-
Al3Ni. The amount of Ni-rich phases is increased with Ni content in the alloy. Ni-rich phases in Ni1-Ni2
alloys ( x = 0.8 and 2.0, respectively) are in the form of d-Al3CuNi and c-Al7Cu4Ni, and however, a great
amount of rod-like or needle-like e-Al3Ni phase is observed in Ni3-Ni4 alloy ( x = 2.4 and 3.4, respectively).
Microstructure observation of the samples after solutionizing at 510 /C176C for 5 h indicates that these Ni-rich
phases have good thermal stability. Ni-alloying in Al-12 wt.%Si-4 wt.%Cu-1.2 wt.%Mn alloy decreases the
strength at room temperature, but proper addition of Ni can remarkably improve the strength at 350 /C176C
and considerably and efﬁciently slow down the softening effect of material at high temperature stage.
0.8 wt.% Ni addition results in a considerable increase in UTS at 350 /C176C from 65 MPa (without Ni
addition) to 97 MPa. During tensile test at room temperature, both Mn-rich phase dendrites and Ni-rich
compounds are the crack originating sites of cleavage fracture. However, during tensile test at 350 /C176C, the
Mn-rich dendrites are the sites for fracture, but Ni-rich compounds are not.
Keywords heat-resistant aluminum alloy, high temperature
strength, microstructure, nickel-rich phase
1. Introduction
For the most key components of an engine, the cylinder
block and cylinder cover, cast-shaping practically becomes the
sole selection because of their quite complex inner/outer
shapes. Thus, the selected materials are required to possess
enough good castability that determines the reliability of engine
to a signiﬁcant extent. Engines are usually working at high
temperature. In particular, high power density engine, which
has been paid more and more attention now, should be working
at a temperature above 300 /C176C. So the developing heat-resistant
aluminum alloys are faced with rather rigorous challenges.
Nowadays, Al-Si-Cu-Mg system heat-resistant alloys, such
as A380, 319 and ZL702A, have been widely used to fabricate
engines for passenger vehicles (Ref 1–5). In these alloys, CuAl
2
compound acts as the main heat-resistant strengthening phase
as well as Al 4CuMg5Si–4 (W-phase), Al 5Mg8Si6Cu2 (Q-phase)
and other complex compound phases from micro-alloying (Ref
1, 6). However, if the working temperature is above 250 /C176C, the
h-CuAl2 strengthening phase formed during aging treatment
will be rapidly coarsened due to its low thermal stability (Ref
6). This will result in a signiﬁcant deterioration of high
temperature strength (Ref 7). Thus, it is very necessary to
develop an aluminum alloy that has enough high strength at
300 /C176C and more.
Ni has been adopted as an alloying element in piston
aluminum alloys. Ni addition in Al-Si-Cu alloys will combine
with Cu to form intermetallic phases with better heat resistance,
usually as Al 3Ni ( e-phase), Al 3CuNi ( d-phase) and Al 7Cu4Ni
(c-phase). Z. Asghar et al. (Ref 8–11) thought that three-
dimensional network structure of these phases possessed great
contribution to mechanical properties at high temperature, and
the respective contribution of three phases is different. The type
and amount of Ni-rich phases are closely related to the Cu/Ni
ratio in the alloy (Ref 6, 10, 12–14). In the alloy with low Cu
content, the Ni-rich phase is mainly as lath-like e-Al
3Ni, and,
with an increase in Cu content, network-like d-Al3CuNi phase
is dominant ﬁrst and then c-Al7Cu4Ni phase is dominant as
closed or semi-closed loops. Li et al. (Ref 15) jointly added
0.8–1.0 wt.%Fe and 0.5 wt.%Cr into Al-13 wt.%Si-
3.7 wt.%Cu-3.2 wt.%Ni-1.1 wt.%Mg alloy and observed that
the closed and semi-closed network-like eutectic a-Al(Mn, Fe,
Cr)Si phase was entangled with Ni-rich phases, which has
strong dragging effect on deformation of Al matrix at high
temperature. And this alloy had high UTS (ultimate tensile
strength) of 98.6 MPa at 350 /C176C by 26% increment. Suwan-
preecha et al. (Ref 16) reported that Al
3Ni phase had good heat
resistance at 300 /C176C. Y ang et al. (Ref 17) investigated the
inﬂuence of Cu addition level on mechanical properties of Al-
Si-Cu-Ni-Mg heat-resistant alloy, in which, when the addition
amount of Cu was at 5.45 wt.%, the ultimate tensile strength at
room temperature and at 350 /C176C was up to 278.9 MPa and
93.5 MPa, respectively. They thought that the improvement of
high temperature strength was mainly from the increase in
phase fraction of Al
3CuNi and Al 7Cu4Ni phases. Li et al. (Ref
6) quantitatively discussed the contribution of e-Al3Ni, d-

experimentally by optical microscope, SEM–EDS and XRD.
And that on high temperature strength was estimated by tensile
test.
2. Materials and Method
Nominal Al-12 wt.%Si-4 wt.%Cu-1.2 wt.%Mn-x%Ni(in
wt.%)(x = 0, 0.8, 2.0, 2.4, 3.4) alloys were prepared in a
graphite crucible of 3 kg capacity using a melting/vacuum
holding furnace with Al-12.5 wt.%Si, Al-10 wt.%Sr, Al-
10 wt.%Mn and Al-10 wt.%Ni master alloys, and commercial
purity Cu (99.8%) ingot and Si (98%) particles. After
processing, the melt was poured at 720 /C176C into an iron mold
(with a plate-like cavity of 170 9 100 9 20 mm
3, preheated at
250 /C176C for 5 h). The chemical composition of the prepared
alloys was measured by MAXx LMF15 spark emission
spectrometer, as listed in Table 1.
The samples for microstructure observation were cut from
the plate castings and were etched with a mixed acid reagent
(20 mL HCl + 20 mL HNO 3 + 5 mL HF + 55 mL H 2O) for
15 s. Microstructural observation was carried out using
OLYMPUS BX60 M metallographic microscope, scanning
electron microscopy (SEM) equipped with EDS. The x-ray
diffraction (XRD, D8-Discover equipped with Cu-K a radia-
tion) was used to analyze phase constitutes with 2 h =1 5 /C176–55/C176
with a scanning step of 0.02
o.
In order to evaluate the effect of Ni addition on high
temperature strength, the castings were ﬁrst solutionized at
510 /C176C for 5 h and further aged at 165 /C176C for 6 h, then
machined into standard tensile samples (GB6397-86) with a
gauge size of 35 9 10 9 3m m
3. Tensile test at room temper-
ature, 300 /C176C and 350 /C176C, was carried out on a CMT5105
electronic universal testing machine with a rate of 1 mm/min.
This provides a strain of the order of about 10
/C04 s/C01. The
ultimate tensile strength is taken as an average of three
replicates.
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Ni-Rich Phases
The formation of Ni-rich phases during the ﬁnal eutectic
reactions is rather complex (Ref 22). It is usually thought that
there are mainly three Ni-rich phases, namely d-Al3CuNi, c-
Al7Cu4Ni and e-Al3Ni phase in the Al-Si-Cu-Mn-Ni or Al-Cu-
Mn-Ni alloys. Because they are formed at the ﬁnal stage of
solidiﬁcation, the size of them is very small. It is difﬁcult to
differentiate them. After many times of SEM–EDS observation,
they can be estimated in rough by their morphologies and EDS
composition. If the morphology is skeleton-like or network-like
and the atomic ratio of Cu/Ni is near 1, this Ni-rich phase is
thought to be d-Al
3CuNi. The c-Al7Cu4Ni phase always
presents as blocky, connected with d-Al3CuNi at its ends, in
which the atomic percentage of Cu is much far more than Ni.
Most of e-Al3Ni are rod-like or needle-like, either as single or
as rows, in which the atomic percentage of Ni is much far more
than Cu.
In Ni1 alloy, two types of Ni-rich phase with different
morphology are observed at the main eutectic boundary zones
even though the amount of them is small. Figure 1(a) and (b)
shows a SEM image of skeleton-like Ni-rich phase in Ni1 alloy
and its EDS results, respectively, illustrating that it is d-
Al
3CuNi phase. Another network-like d-Al3CuNi phase is also
seen beside it. Figure 1(c) shows a SEM image of blocky Ni-
rich phase that is partially in coupled growth with d-Al3CuNi
and CuAl2 phases. Its EDS results (Fig. 1d) are close to that of
c-Al7Cu4Ni, thus it is thought to be c-Al7Cu4Ni. d-Al3CuNi
and c-Al7Cu4Ni and other heat-resistant phase constitute a
closed or semi-closed network structure (Fig. 1a and c), which
would exert dragging effect to gain boundary motion at high
temperature and thus would lead to an improvement of high
temperature strength. The types of Ni-rich phases in Ni2 alloy
are same as in Ni1 alloy, but the amount of them is increased
with Ni content in the alloy (seen from Fig. 2a and b). In Ni3
and Ni4 alloys with high Ni content, besides the above d-
Al
3CuNi and c-Al7Cu4Ni phases, there is a great amount of
rod-like or needle-like Ni-rich phase, either as single or as rows
(Fig. 2c–d). Another morphology of Ni-rich phase with sword-
sagittal shape is observed in Ni3 alloy, as shown in Fig. 3(a). Its
EDS results (Fig. 3b) illustrate that it is d-Al3CuNi too. High-
magniﬁcation rod-like or needle-like Ni-rich phase is shown in
Fig. 3c. It is thought to be e-Al
3Ni phase by the EDS results
(Fig. 3d). Another two e-Al3Ni phases with ﬁshbone or
particulate shapes are also observed in Ni4 alloy, as shown in
Fig. 3(e), (f), (g) and (h), respectively, of which the amount is

With Ni-alloying, a large number of Ni-rich phases with
good thermal stability is induced in the alloy and correspond-
ingly the amount of h(CuAl

thermal stability is decreased (Ref 22). These Ni-rich phases
and other heat-resistant phases constitute a closed or semi-

boundary motion at high temperature and thus would result in
an improvement of high temperature strength. Li et al. (Ref 6)
investigated the inﬂuence of the morphology of Ni-rich phases
on strength at 350 /C176C, and reported that the alloy with d-
Al

than that with c-Al7Cu4Ni, and that containing more e-Al3Ni
was the lowest. For the studied alloy in this article, besides Ni-
rich phases, the main strengthening phase for high temperature
strength is primary Mn-rich phase (Al
15Mn3Si2). With Ni
addition, the amount of Ni-rich phase is increased. And at same
time, the amount of primary Mn-rich phase is increased too

diagram calculation (Ref 22). In the alloy without Ni addition,
the amount of primary Al
15Mn3Si2 phase is small as well as a
relatively coarse size, as seen in Fig. 7(a). The size of the
primary Al 15Mn3Si2 phase is ﬁrst considerably decreased in
Ni1 alloy (as shown in Fig. 7b) and then increased in Ni2-Ni4
alloys (seen in Fig. 7c–e). In Ni2 and Ni3 alloys, Ni addition
not only makes the branches and trunks of primary Mn-rich
phase dendrites reﬁne but also obviously increases the size of
Mn-rich phase dendrites. The shape of most primary Mn-rich
phase, whether the larger ones or the small ones, becomes
trefoil with ﬁne and fragile roots (seen in Fig. 7c–e). This will
considerably increase the structural brittleness of Mn-rich
dendrites. Thus, the UTS of Ni2 and Ni3 alloys at 350 /C176Ci s
less than that of Ni1 alloy. In Ni4 alloy, the coarsening in size
and reﬁning in branches and trunks of primary Mn-rich phase
become more signiﬁcant, and, of importance, the main Ni-rich
phase is e-Al

much less than Ni1 alloy, even lower than N0 alloy. Li (Ref 15)
developed Al-13.0Si-4Cu-4Ni-1.1 Mg-0.8Fe-0.5Cr alloy. After
T6 treatment, its UTS at 350 /C176C is about 98 MPa, almost same
as Ni1 alloy in our study.
3.3 Fractograph
Tensile fractograph could reveal the role of some phases in
cracking to some extent. Figure 8(a), (c), (e) and (g) shows the
fractographs of the alloys with Ni addition (T6 tempered) after
tensile test at room temperature. Dimples and cleavage planes
are coexisted; however, the cleavage planes are dominant.
These fractographs exhibit brittle fracture character of these
alloys during tensile test at room temperature. For Ni1 alloy, the
cleavage planes mainly contain Al, Si and Mn elements
(Fig. 8b), presenting as dendrites of primary Mn-rich phase.
During tensile test at room temperature, stress is concentrated
around the primary Mn-rich dendrites that induce cracking. For
Ni2 and Ni3 alloys, the fraction of cleavage planes is increased,
and d-Al
3CuNi phase is also observed on these planes (Fig. 8c–

From microstructure observation, addition of Ni not only
results in an increase in the amount of the primary Mn-rich
dendrites, but also simultaneously leads to coarsening in size
and reﬁning in branches and trunks of primary Mn-rich phase
dendrites. This will increase the brittleness of the dendrite
structure. With Ni addition, the amount of Ni-rich phases is also
increased. These Ni-rich phases also induce stress concentra-
tion. Especially in Ni4 alloy, there are many e-Al
3Ni rods or
needles. This shape character is prone to stress concentration,
making these e-Al
3Ni rods or needles become the most
potential sites for crack originating. So, Ni-alloying in Al-
12 wt.%Si-4 wt.%Cu-1.2 wt.%Mn base alloy deteriorates its
strength at room temperature.
Figure 9(a), (c), (e) and (g) shows the fractographs of the
alloys with Ni addition (T6 tempered) after tensile test at
350 /C176C. Compared with that at room temperature, the fraction
of dimples on fractographs is remarkably increased, and
coexistence of dimples and cleavage planes does not change.
For Ni1-Ni4 alloy, EDS analysis on cleavage planes (Fig. 9b, d,
f and h) suggests that they are Al 15Mn3Si2 dendrites. No
obvious evidences are found for the existence of d-Al3CuNi, c-
Al7Cu4Ni, e-Al3Ni and h-Al2Cu phases. To some extent, this
indicates that it is the Mn-rich dendrites that bear the tensile
load at high temperature, not the Ni-rich phases. And the
amount of Al
15Mn3Si2 dendrites on fractographs at 350 /C176C
tensile is increased with Ni content in the alloy, indicating that
the dendrites of primary Mn-rich phase are still the crack
originating sites of cleavage fracture. It is concluded that during
tensile test at 350 /C176C, the Mn-rich dendrites are the crack
originating sites for fracture, but Ni-rich compounds are not,
because the Mn-rich dendrites are coarsened in size and reﬁned
in branches and trunks by Ni addition.
4. Conclusions

rich phases changes greatly and its amount of Ni-rich phases is
also increased. After a thermal history of 510 /C176C for 5 h, the
size and morphology of Ni-rich phases do not change,
indicating that they have good thermal stability. Ni-alloying
in Al-12 wt.%Si-4 wt.%Cu-1.2 wt.%Mn alloy decreases the
room temperature strength, but proper addition of Ni remark-
ably improves the high temperature strength at 350 /C176C and
