1. Introduction
Magnesium-base alloys have been attracted attention as
lightweightandhighstrengthstructuralmaterialsduetotheir
low density, high speciﬁc strength and stiﬀness. Further
improvement of the mechanical properties has been required
to extend the application. It has been conﬁrmed that non-
equilibriummaterialsprocessingsuchastherapidsolidiﬁca-
tion (RS) is eﬀective for improvement of mechanical
properties of magnesium alloys.
1) High performance RS
P/M Mg97Zn1Y2 (at%) alloy having the extremely high
tensile yield strength of 610MPa and the elongation of 5%
has been developed. This is due to the hcp-Mg ﬁne grains
with 6H-type long period order structure and the homoge-

Mechanical alloying (MA) is a high-energy ball milling
process with the repeated fracture and welding of powder
particles. In other words, MA is another non-equilibrium
processutilizingsolid-statereaction.Therefore,theimprove-
mentofmechanicalpropertiesintheMAedpowdercompact
is expected to be same as that in the RS P/M process.
However,thereisaninevitablysevereproblemintheMAof
Mg, which is adhesion of Mg powders to milling balls and
container during the processing. This has been overcome by
utilizing of the brittle MgH
2 powder as a starting material,
although the MgH2 powder has been already applied to the
MA process of new hydrogen storage materials. The
hydrogenation-dehydrogenation (HDH) reaction in the Ti–
6Al–4V alloy promotes the grain reﬁnement.
4,5) It is
explained that a number of localizing dislocations are
introduced by the formation of hydrides, which provides
with heterogeneous nucleation sites for recrystallization,
leading to the grain reﬁnement. Consequently, MA-HDH
P/M processing using the MgH
2 powder as the starting
material may be expected as a new process for synthesizing
of high performance magnesium alloys with ﬁne grains.
We have reported recently that the mechanically milled
(MMed) MgH2 powders of almost 100% could be recovered
without the adhesion of powders to milling balls and
container, and MA-HDH P/M process for the production of
Mg bulk metal was established by using MgH
2 powders as
the starting material.6)
In the present study, the MA-HDH P/M process is applied
to fabrication of the Mg 97Zn1Y2 alloy, since the alloy
produced by RS P/M process has high performance. The
mechanical properties of the obtained material are investi-
gated. The microstructure and the thermal stability of the
alloy are also discussed.
2. Experimental Procedure
AM g

<150 mm),Zn(99.9%, <75 mm)andY(99.9%, <850 mm)in
argon atmosphere with a planetary ball milling machine
(Fritsch P-5). The mixed powders were put into a stainless
steel container with SUJ2 steel balls of 10mm in diameter.
Theballtopowderweightratiowas 100 : 1.Millingtimewas

Figure 1 shows the XRD patterns of the as-mixed and

addition, the XRD peaks of Y and Zn are disappeared

grain reﬁnement of MgH
2 powders, and a formation of the
MgH2 solid solution containing Y and Zn. No detectable
amount of other phases is observed. Figure 2 shows the
transmission electron micrographs of the MAed Mg97Zn1Y2
powders.Theparticlesizesarerangingfrom200to800nmin
diameter, as shown in Fig. 2(a). The electron diﬀraction
pattern only consists of hcp-Mg and MgO phases. There are
no diﬀraction rings of MgH

considered to be due to the dehydrogenation of the MAed
powders during producing the TEM specimens and/or TEM
observations.8) A dark-ﬁeld image taken by using the Mg
(10/C22111) diﬀraction spot shows the grain sizes ranging from
several to 30nm in diameter. Figure3 shows the dehydro-

dehydrogenating peaks of the MAed powders appear with
increasing temperatures. It is well known that a partial
tetragonal /C12-MgH

orthorhombic /C13-MgH2 phase under high compressive stress,
and the dehydrogenating temperature of the latter is lower
than that of the former.
9,10) Hence, in this study, the low-
temperature dehydrogenating peak seems to correspond to
the decomposition of the /C13-MgH
2 phase, and the high-
temperature dehydrogenating peak corresponds to the de-
composition of the/C12-MgH
2 phase.

4. Conclusions
(1) MA provided the grain reﬁnement of MgH2 powders:
the particle size and grain size of MAed powders for
50h were ranging from 200 to 800nm and several to
30nm in diameter, respectively.
(2) Two dehydrogenating peaks which seem to correspond
to the decomposition of the/C13-MgH
2 and the /C12-MgH2
phase were appeared in the MAed powders with
increasing temperatures.
(3) The Mg97Zn1Y2 bulk alloy could be produced by MA-
HDH P/M process using MgH2 powder as the starting
material.
(4) Thecompressiveyieldstrengthandplastic strainofthe
as-extruded Mg97Zn1Y2 bulk alloy were 616MPa and
4.2%, respectively. These values were much higher
than those of commercial ingot metallurgy (I/M) Mg
alloys,suchasAZ91andZK60.Thisisoriginatedfrom
the reﬁnement of hcp-Mg grains, the solid solution
hardeningofZnandY,andthedispersionhardeningby
MgO and Y
2O3 ﬁne particles.
(5) No degradation was observed in mechanical properties
of MA-HDH P/M Mg97Zn1Y2 alloy even after anneal-
ing at 673K for prolonging time. This is considered to
be due to the inhibition of the hcp-Mg grain growth by
the densely dispersed MgO and Y
2O3 ﬁne particles.
Therefore, MA-HDH P/M Mg97Zn1Y2 alloy is promis-
ing as the structural material for high temperature
applications.
