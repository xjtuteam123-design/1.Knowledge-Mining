Abstract
Bulk glassy alloys in Ni–Nb–Ti–Hf system were formed at composition of Ni 60Nb20Ti12:5Hf7:5 by copper mold
casting. The supercooled liquid region and reduced glass transition temperature were 60 K and 0.609, respectively. The
bulk glassy alloy exhibits high compressive fracture strength of 3180 MPa and good ductility.

1. Introduction
It is known that amorphous alloys have char-
acteristic physical and chemical properties such as
high strength, high corrosion resistance and good
soft magnetic properties, which are signiﬁcantly
diﬀerent from the corresponding crystalline alloys.
However, the requirement of rapid solidiﬁcation
has lead to strict limitation of their materials shape
to ribbons, wires and powders and its limitation
has restricted further extension of their application
ﬁelds. Since the ﬁndings of glassy-type alloys with
a large supercooled liquid region in Mg–Ln–Ni [1]
and Ln–Al–Ni [2] (Ln ¼ lanthanide metals) sys-
tems, followed by the successes of producing bulk
glassy alloys in the Ln-[3] and Mg-[4] based sys-
tems by the copper mold casting method, much
attention has been paid to the development of new
alloy systems with high glass-forming ability
(GFA). As a result, bulk glassy alloy systems have
been widely extended to Zr-[5,6], Fe-[7], Pd–Cu-
[8], Co-[9], Cu–Ti-[10] and Ni–Zr-[11] based alloys.
Based on the features of the above-mentioned
multi-component systems, the following three
empirical rules [12,13] of alloy components for
the formation of bulk glassy alloys have been
proposed, i.e., (1) multi-components consisting of
more than three elements, (2) signiﬁcant atomic
size mismatches among the main constituent ele-
ments, and (3) suitable negative heats of mixing
among their elements. Besides, in a number of
studies on the thermal properties of the bulk glassy
alloys, it has been revealed that glassy alloys with a
large supercooled liquid region DT

More recently, we have succeeded in ﬁnding new
Ni-based bulk glassy alloys with high compressive
fracture strength of over 3100 MPa in Ni–Nb–Ti–
Hf system. This paper intends to present the GFA,
thermal stability, mechanical properties and frac-
ture behavior of Ni–Nb–Ti–Hf bulk glassy alloys.
2. Experimental procedure
Multi-component Ni-based alloy ingots with
nominal composition of Ni
50–65Nb10–25Ti10–25Hf0–20
(at.%) were prepared by arc melting the mixtures
of pure Ni, Nb, Hf and Ti metals in a Ti-gettered
argon atmosphere. The purity of metals was over
99.9 mass%. Alloy ingots were re-melted ﬁve times
to ensure chemical homogeneity. The mass losses
were measured for each ingot after melting and
were less than 0.1 mass%. The glassy alloy was
produced by copper mold casting for bulk cylin-
drical rods with diameters up to 2 mm and by melt
spinning for ribbons with a cross section of
0:02 /C21:2m m
2. The structure of the rod samples
was examined by X-ray diﬀraction and optical
microscopy (OM). The microstructure of samples
was examined by transmission electron micros-
copy (TEM). The thermal stability was examined
by diﬀerential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a
heating rate of 0.67 K/s. The liquidus temperatures
were measured with a diﬀerential thermal analyzer
(DTA) at a heating rate of 0.17 K/s. Vicker /C213s
hardness of the material was measured using a
microhardness tester. Mechanical properties were
measured with an Instron testing machine. The
gauge dimension of specimens was 1.5 mm in di-
ameter and 3 mm in height for compressive test
and the strain rate was 10

croscopy (SEM).

4. Discussion
The high GFA of the present Ni–Nb–Ti–Hf
alloy is concluded to originate from the high
thermal stability of the supercooled liquid against
crystallization. The reason for the large DT
x and
high Tg=Tl for the Ni–Nb–Ti–Hf glassy alloy is
discussed in the framework of the three compo-
nent rules [12,13] for the achievement of high
GFA. In the Ni–Nb–Hf–Ti system, their atomic
sizes change in the order of Hf > Nb > Ti > Ni.

makes the rearrangement of the constituent ele-

high thermal stability of the supercooled liquid
against crystallization [13].
It is also noticed that the compressive fracture
strength r
c;f is much higher than those for the
other bulk glassy alloys, e.g., 1700–1900 MPa for
Pd–Cu- and Zr-based bulk glassy alloys [16,17],
2000–2500 MPa for Hf- and Cu-based bulk glassy
alloys [19,21–25] and 2770 MPa for Ni–Nb–Ti–Zr
bulk glassy alloys [20]. With the aim of investi-
gating the origin for the high r
c;f of the Ni-based
bulk glassy alloys, the relationship between rc;f
and Tg or Tl for typical bulk glassy alloys including
the present Ni-based glassy alloys are shown in
Fig. 6. One can see a clear tendency for r
c;f to
increase with increasing Tg or Tl. Considering the
previous concept that the Tg and Tl of the glassy
alloys reﬂect the bonding force among the con-
stituent elements [27], it is concluded that the high
r
c;f value of the present Ni-based Ni–Nb–Ti–Hf
bulk glassy alloy originates from the strong
bonding nature among the constituent elements.
5. Conclusions
The Ni60Nb20Ti12:5Hf7:5 glassy alloy was formed
in a rod form with a diameter up to 1.5 mm. The
supercooled liquid region DTx and reduced glass
transition temperature Tg=Tl were 60 K and 0.609,
respectively. The bulk glassy alloy exhibits good
mechanical properties, i.e., Vickers hardness of
824, Young/C213s modulus of 173 GPa, compressive
fracture strength of 3180 MPa, and plastic elon-
gation of 2.5%. The fracture strength shows a
linear function with T
g and Tl. The bonding force
among the constituent elements seems to be a
dominant factor for the high strength.
