ABSTRACT
Significant strength loss at elevated temperatures has always been one of the great challenges for 
the extended application of magnesium (Mg) alloys. This work reports an exceptional increase in 
the elevated-temperature mechanical properties of magnesium rare-earth (Mg-RE) alloys fabri -
cated by direct energy deposition using electric arc (DED-Arc). The tensile strength at 250 
◦
C of 
the DED-Arc Mg-9Gd-3Y-0.5Zr (GW93K) alloy is almost 70 MPa higher than that of the cast 
counterpart, accompanied by desirable ductility. A quasi- in situ approach was employed to 
investigate the dynamic precipitation evolution of both DED-Arc and cast alloys under simple 
thermal effect and thermal-mechanical coupling for a comparative study. Molecular dynamics 
(MD) simulations were utilized to analyze RE diffusion in DED-Arc alloys. Compared to cast al -
loys, DED-Arc alloys exhibit nano-scale precipitates with significantly higher number density 
under thermal-mechanical coupling during tensile test. The residual dislocations from the DED- 
Arc process and uniformly distributed dislocations formed during tensile deformation impeded 
RE solute diffusion toward grain boundaries (GBs), thereby reducing grain boundary precipitation 
(GBP). The inhibited GBP and absence of precipitation-free zones (PFZs) reduce stress concen -
tration at GBs, delaying crack initiation. The effects of microstructural features including grain 
size, texture, and precipitates on deformation modes were systematically compared. Conse -
quently, DED-Arc Mg-RE alloys demonstrate superior strength-ductility synergy: ultimate tensile 
strength: 291.9 ± 7.3 MPa and elongation: 19.2 ± 1.0 % at 250 
◦
C. This research could provide 
new insights into the application of Mg alloys in extreme conditions such as elevated 
temperatures.

1. Introduction
Mg-RE alloys are characterized by lightweight and high specific strength, which makes them extremely attractive for aerospace and 
military applications ( Li et al., 2025 ; Ni et al., 2025 ). Current research typically classifies Mg-Gd-Y alloys as a series of “ heat-resistant ” 
Mg alloys ( Cheng et al., 2024 ; Liu et al., 2019 ; Wang et al., 2024 ; Wu et al., 2023 ; Xiao et al., 2022 ; Zhao et al., 2022 ; Zhou et al., 2023 ). 
However, this assessment is based on a comparison with non-RE Mg alloys, such as the AZ and AM series. For instance, the “ hea -
t-resistant ” as-cast Mg-9Gd-3Y-0.5Zr (GW93K) alloy demonstrates an ambient-temperature strength of ~350 MPa, but at 250 
◦
C, its 
strength decreases to only 200 MPa with a strength loss of over 50 % ( Nie, 2012 ). This indicates that there are great challenges for 
Mg-RE alloys to obtain long-term thermo-stability at elevated temperatures, particularly over 250 
◦
C. Consequently, there is an urgent 
need to further enhance the elevated-temperature mechanical properties of Mg-RE alloys to expand their potential for applications in 
the future.
To improve the elevated-temperature performance of Mg-RE alloys, it is essential to understand the mechanisms of the strength loss 
at elevated temperatures. On the one hand, the increased atomic diffusion rate at elevated temperatures accelerates the coarsening of 
precipitates and leads to a sharp decrease in number density ( Xiao et al., 2024 ). The strength of Mg-RE alloys is increased by pre -
cipitates by hindering dislocation motion ( Nie, 2012 ). At elevated temperatures, the precipitates undergo Ostwald ripening, leading to 
an increase in the particle size ( Yang et al., 2016 ). As a result, the distance between the coarsening precipitates increases and the 
hindering effect on the dislocations is diminished, leading to a decrease in the strength ( Zou et al., 2025 ). Moreover, the coarsening of 
the precipitates may lead to stress concentration and increase the possibility of crack initiation ( Yin et al., 2012 ). On the other hand, 
some researchers suggest that the poor elevated-temperature strength of Mg-RE alloys may result from the recrystallization or recovery 
softening during deformation ( Zhou et al., 2024 ). Recrystallization usually results in decreased grain size, leading to an enhanced grain 
boundary strengthening effect, but incomplete recrystallization or excessive grain growth may result in an opposite impact (A 
Galiyev). Moreover, in some cases, the fine-grain microstructure may lead to a further reduction in strength due to the migration of 
grain boundaries (GBs) ( Zhang et al., 2023 ). Grain size plays a critical role in crack initiation. The representative size effect factor is 
expressed as t / d , where t denotes the sample thickness and d represents the grain radius. A decrease in d or an increase in t leads to a 
higher number fraction and volume fraction of fracture ( Azghandi et al., 2020 ; Feng et al., 2022b ; Shang et al., 2020 ; Tasan et al., 
2014 ). However, the current findings do not account for the influence of grain boundary precipitation on fracture initiation, while the 
severe RE-rich precipitate coarsening readily occur at GBs at elevated temperatures and plays an important role in the mechanical 
behavior of the Mg-Gd-Y alloy.
Based on the above analysis, the elevated-temperature mechanical properties of Mg-RE alloys are primarily governed by two 
factors: grain interior (GI) and GB characteristics. The structural features of GI, such as the size and distribution of precipitates, play a 
significant role in dislocation movement at elevated temperatures. Conversely, the characteristic of GBs, including PFZs and contin -
uous grain boundary precipitates (GBPs), determines the intergranular bonding strength ( Ji et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2024 ; Priester, 2013 ). 
In our previous research ( Tong et al., 2023 ; Zhan et al., 2025b ), the Mg-RE alloys fabricated by directed energy deposition using 
electric arc (DED-Arc) exhibit distinct aging precipitation behaviors compared to cast alloys, which is the first report of such a phe -
nomenon in DED-Arc Mg-RE alloys. Specifically, the number density of precipitates in peak-aged DED-Arc Mg-RE alloys is almost 10 
times higher than that in cast alloys, with a more uniform distribution. Moreover, the extremely rapid cooling rate during the DED-Arc 
process results in a fine-grain structure that provides a higher grain boundary-strengthening effect. As a result, the DED-Arc Mg-RE 
alloys demonstrate exceptional ambient-temperature mechanical properties. However, the thermal stability of such a unique GI 
structure is unclear, and the evolution of GBP at elevated temperatures has not been reported, which is worth in-depth exploration.
As for the engineering application, the DED-Arc method offers several advantages over traditional casting or wrought processes, 
including the ability to produce components with complex geometries using less raw material consumption ( Tong et al., 2023 ). This 
method holds significant potential for expanding the use of Mg-RE alloys in some important applications, such as engine casing and 
missile cabin ( Wu et al., 2022 ). However, these structural components usually serve under elevated temperatures, putting stringent 
demands on the microstructural stability and mechanical properties of the Mg alloys. Thus, it is a critical step toward advancing their 
engineering applications to investigate the microstructural evolution of DED-Arc Mg-RE alloys at elevated temperatures and its impact 
on mechanical properties.
However, due to the complex interaction of temperature and forces involved in elevated-temperature mechanical property testing, 
most of the current studies primarily focus on the analysis of the final failure state of the Mg alloy ( Shi et al., 2023 ; Xiao et al., 2024 ; Xu 
et al., 2022 , 2023 ; Yin et al., 2012 ). The independent effect of the thermal and force on the microstructural evolution of Mg-RE alloys 
remains unclear. To address this issue, the present study innovatively employs a quasi- in-situ method to reveal the microstructural 
evolution of DED-Arc Mg-RE alloys during elevated-temperature tensile testing. The current work first reports the critical role of 
intrinsic dislocations introduced during deposition in governing the dynamic precipitation of Mg-RE alloys at elevated temperatures. 
Furthermore, the deformation and fracture mechanisms of the alloy under thermo-mechanical coupling conditions at 
elevated-temperature tensile test have also been emphasized. This research is expected to contribute to the broader application of 
Mg-RE alloys in heat-resistant aerospace components.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure of the as-deposited alloy
Fig. 2 presents the analysis of oxidation inclusions and pore defects formed during the manufacturing process. It reveals that the 
volume fraction of these defects remains below 0.02 %, a marked improvement compared to the LPBF Mg-RE alloys ( Gangireddy et al., 
2019 ). Notably, over 75 % of the pore defects display a sphericity exceeding 0.75, which indicates that there are fewer unfused defects. 
The analysis further indicates an inverse correlation between pore size and sphericity, with > 90 % of pores measuring < 10 µ m . These 
characteristics suggest that the pore defects are primarily small gas pores. As compared to the DED-Arc alloys, the volume fraction of 
oxidation inclusions and pore defects in cast samples is also extremely low (lower than 0.02 %, as shown in Fig. S2). The above results 
demonstrate that high densities have been achieved in both two alloys. Therefore, the influence of internal defects with an extremely 
low content on the mechanical properties of the alloy can be ignored.

5. Conclusions
In this work, the elevated temperature mechanical properties of DED-Arc Mg-9Gd-3Y-0.5Zr alloy are systematically studied for the 
first time. The distinct microstructure evolution and deformation behaviors compared to the cast alloy are also investigated in detail. 
The main conclusions are drawn as follows: 
(1) The precipitation behavior of DED-Arc alloys is different from that of cast alloys. During the heating stage, as compared to the 
precipitation behavior of cast alloys, the precipitates in DED-Arc alloys are coarsened slightly, with a much higher number 
density. Under the effect of thermal-mechanical coupling, a small increase in number density is observed in DED-Arc alloys, 
which is closely related to the dynamic precipitation behavior during the tensile fracture stage.
(2) During the tensile fracture stage, the GBPs are significantly degraded in DED-Arc alloys compared to cast alloys, which is 
conducive to the reduction of stress concentration at GBs. No obvious PFZs are formed in DED-Arc alloys under the effect of 
thermal-mechanical coupling, which contributes to a uniform stress distribution from GI to GB and thus reduces intergranular 
cracking.
(3) The competition between dislocations and GBs for the segregation of RE atoms is analyzed with MD simulations and TEM 
observation. It is found that the dislocations in DED-Arc alloys significantly reduce the element segregation to GBs. Thus, the 
residual dislocation inherited from the deposition process and the uniformly distributed dislocations in the tensile fracture stage 
both delay the formation of the GBP zone at different stages of the tensile test.
(4) This study compares the different fracture behaviors of DED-Arc and cast GW93K alloys at elevated temperatures. Fewer twins 
in the DED-Arc alloys lead to a more uniform stress distribution. The stress concentration at the GBs is also significantly smaller 
than that of cast alloys. These characteristics lead to uniform plastic deformation and achieve higher elevated-temperature 
strength of DED-Arc alloys.
(5) Superior strength-ductility synergies are obtained in DED-Arc alloys at elevated temperatures of 250
