ABSTRACT 
Increasing strength and preserving high strain hardening rate are of importance to widen the application of the 
metastable β titanium alloys. In this study, Ti – 4Mo – 4Cr – 3Al – 2Nb – 1.2V – 1Zr – 1Sn (wt.%) alloy with four different 
primary α phase contents were prepared by solution treatment at different temperatures. The differences in β 
phase stability, mechanical properties, and deformation modes in these samples were investigated systemati -
cally. With the decrease of solution treatment temperature, the volume fraction of α phase, the stability of β 
matrix, and the tensile strength of the alloy increase, and a high strength and high strain hardening rate can be 
achieved simultaneously when the content of the primary α phase is less than 20%. When the applied stress 
exceeds the activated stress of martensitic transformation, martensite plates can be generated at large β domains 
and caused a dynamic Hall-Petch effect in these samples. With the further increase of strain, substructures are 
generated within the martensite to accommodate the deformation. Our findings provide a possible route for 
strengthening the metastable β titanium alloys and are beneficial to the microstructure design of the alloys with a 
high strength-ductility combination. 
1. Introduction 
As a kind of lightweight high-strength structural material, meta -
stable β titanium alloys have a wide application in aerospace, marine, 
chemical industry and other fields [ 1 – 5 ]. In the past 20 years, many new 
metastable β titanium alloys, such as Ti – 4Al – 7Mo – 3Cr – 3V (Ti-4733) 
[ 6 ], Ti – 7Mo – 3Nb – 3Cr – 3Al (Ti-7333) [ 7 ], Ti – 5Al – 4Zr – 8Mo – 7V 
(Ti-5487) [ 8 ], Ti – 15Nb – 5Zr – 4Sn – 1Fe (Ti-15541) [ 9 ], were developed 
based on the semi-empirical “ molybdenum equivalence ” (Mo
[eq]
) the -
ory, the “ d-electron ” theory, and the average valance electron-to-atom 
ratio ( e/a) theory and others [ 6 – 9 ]. According to these available 
design theories, the mechanical properties and deformation mechanism 
of the newly designed alloys with a single β phase might be predicted, 
and most of them exhibit transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) 
or/and twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) effect [ 10 , 11 ]. However, the 
yield strength of the alloy with a single β phase structure is relatively 
low, often below 500 MPa [ 10 , 12 ]. The yield strength of such alloys is 
generally considered to depend on two main factors: 1) the amount of 
boundaries in the matrix, including the grain boundaries and various 
interphase boundaries, 2) the β phase stability of the matrix, determined 
by the composition of β phase. Therefore, some strategies could be 
proposed based on these two aspects, aiming at increasing the strength 
of the alloy and preserving the TRIP and TWIP effects in the matrix. 
Grain boundary as the most common type of interfaces, its effect has 
been widely studied based on the Hall-Petch theory. Reducing the mean 
free path of dislocation slip by the decrease of the grain size is the most 
common method to improve the strength of metal materials. It has been 
reported that the critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) of twinning in -
creases with the decrease in grain size in TWIP alloys, which results in a 
decrease in strain hardening rate [ 13 ]. In contrast, the CRSS of 
martensite was found to decrease with the decrease in grain size in some 
TRIP alloy [ 14 , 15 ], and it has been suggested that the nano-sized β 
grains were too small for any formation of α " martensite [ 16 ]. Another 
possible strategy to harden the matrix is via introducing precipitate 
phases, such as ω phase and secondary α phase. Sun [ 17 ] employed a 
short time aging treatment to introduce coherent β / ω interphase 
boundaries, which effectively increases the yield strength and preserves 
a high ductility of Ti – 12Mo alloy. Lilensten [ 18 ] proposed that α pre -
cipitates with a 20% volume fraction in Ti – 7Cr – 1.5Sn (wt.%) alloy in -
crease the yield strength of the alloy by more than 200 MPa. However, 
the content and size of these precipitate phases are not easily controlled, 
and the mechanical properties of the alloy are easily affected.

Controlling the content of the primary α phase is also considered to be an 
important route to strengthening metastable β titanium alloys. The 
primary α phase not only introduces β / α phase interfaces, but also leads 
to a re-distribution of alloying elements and then adjust the phase sta -
bility of the β matrix, and thus changes the mechanical properties and 
deformation behavior of the alloys. However, up to now, fewer works 
involving this strengthening strategy were done in titanium alloys. 
In this study, the strengthening strategy of adjusting the primary α 
phase content was applied to our recently developed 
Ti – 4Mo – 4Cr – 3Al – 2Nb – 1.2V – 1Zr – 1Sn (wt.%, designated as Ti-4321) 
alloy. The strength of the Ti-4321 alloy is effectively increased and its 
high strain hardening rate is preserved. The roles of the primary α phase 
in the deformation process and martensite transformation were evalu -
ated, and the deformation mechanism as a function of phase stability 
and deformation strain was proposed based on our observations. This 
work provides a possible strategy to strengthen the metastable β tita -
nium alloys and is beneficial to the microstructure design of the alloys 
with a high strength-ductility combination.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Microstructures and β phase stability 
According to our observations, in Fig. 1 , the volume fraction of α 
phase in the Ti-4321 alloy increases, with the decrease in solution 
treatment temperature. The α to β phase transition belongs to a diffu -
sional transformation, which involves the diffusion of different types of 
stabilizing elements during phase transition [ 27 ]. Such as α stabilizing 
element Al would enrich in the α phase, resulting in the uneven distri -
bution of Al element between two phases. Therefore, the higher volume 
fraction of α phase the alloy has, the higher β stabilizing element and the 
less α stabilizing element remains in the residual matrix. These enriched 
β stabilizing elements in matrix cause the β phase stability to increase. In 
other words, with the increase in solution treatment temperature, the β 
phase stability in the matrix decreases. Generally, the metastable α
′
martensite (HCP), α " martensite (orthorhombic) and athermal ω (hex -
agonal) phase appear sequentially in quenched microstructure with the 
increase in β phase stability in titanium alloys [ 28 ]. Nano-sized athermal 
ω phases were detected in the ST840 sample and the β phase stability in 
this sample is the lowest in all samples. Therefore, it can be speculated 
that the quenching athermal martensite was also absent in other sam -
ples. It means that the martensite plates observed in the fractured 
samples are all deformation martensite instead of the quenching 
martensite. In addition, the athermal ω phases might also appear in the 
other samples, while their presence may not have a significant effect on 
the mechanical properties and β phase stability of the alloys [ 29 , 30 ], so 
their contribution is neglected. 
Bo- Md diagram is usually regarded as an important criterion for 
phase stability evaluation [ 31 ]. It attempts to define the boundaries of 
different transformations using experimental data from a variety of 
alloying systems. Indeed, some boundaries have been built based on the 
previous work [ 3 ], as shown in Fig. 8 . This diagram was divided into 
three regions according to the different deformation products during 
deformation at room temperature. The largest yellow area represents the 
slip region, indicating that only dislocation slip occurs during defor -
mation. The green and orange areas represent the TRIP and TWIP re -
gions, respectively. In particular, some alloys with the Bo- Md value 
located at the boundary of two regions, TRIP and TWIP effects may 
occur simultaneously during deformation. Based on this theory, many 
newly designed alloys exhibit good combinations of strength and 
ductility, such as Ti-5487 [ 8 ], Ti-5321 [ 32 ], Ti-3573 [ 26 ]. In this study, 
the values of Bo and Md in the four samples were calculated and their 
corresponding points are shown in Fig. 8 . For the ST820 and 840 sam -
ples, their points are located in the TRIP region. With the decrease in 
solution treatment temperature, corresponding points of the ST800 and 
ST780 samples are across the TRIP/TWIP boundary and into the TWIP 
region. However, we did not find corresponding evidence of deforma -
tion twinning appearing in ST780 and ST800 samples, as shown in 
Figs. 4 and 5 . Objectively, we do not deny the possibility of deformation 
twins existing in these samples, but twinning has not become the main 
deformation mechanism for the Ti-4321 alloy with these different β 
phase stabilities. Similar results have also been reported in previous 
studies, Xue [ 33 , 34 ]and Ahmed [ 23 ] did not detect the change in 
deformation mechanism when the β phase stability varies cross different 
regions. Ahmed suggested an expansion of stress-induced martensite 
region on Morinaga ’ s Bo- Md map, although an exact expansion border 
needs to be determined in the future. Interestingly, our results strongly 
support the suggested expansion region.

Theoretically, the whole deformation of these samples can be divided 
into several stages, including an initial elastic deformation of β phase, 
followed by a stress-induced martensitic transformation, and finally the 
elastic and plastic deformation stages of these dual-phases [ 35 ]. At the 
first stage, that is, the elastic deformation stage, the elastic deformation 
of β phase is the main deformation mechanism, and the macroscopic 
manifestation is the sharp decrease of strain hardening rate. Then, with 
the increase of applied stress, the yielding of material occurs and 
deformation martensite appears in the matrix, and the main deformation 
mechanism is changed. Generally, the yield strength of the alloy de -
pends on the CRSS of deformation martensite [ 36 ], and the CRSS of 
martensite is closely related to the β grain size and the β phase stability 
[ 37 ]. It has been proposed that the CRSS of martensite increases with 
increasing the β grain size in metastable β titanium alloy in the previous 
works [ 14 , 15 ]. However, in this work, the yield strength, related to the 
CRSS of martensite, decreases with the increase in solution treatment 
temperature, namely the increase in β grain size. Therefore, it is 
reasonable to believe that the phase stability and α phase content have a 
greater effect on martensitic transformation than the grain size. 
At the second stage, where the applied stress exceeds the activated 
stress of the martensite, the deformation martensite would be induced 
and cause a dynamic Hall-Petch effect [ 35 , 38 , 39 ]. At the same time, the 
strain hardening rate starts to increase, because the newly formed 
martensite introduces lots of interphase boundaries in the matrix, and 
the dislocation slip could be hindered by these boundaries. Many pre -
vious works also suggested the interactions between dislocation slips 
and martensite plates are likely responsible for the increased strain 
hardening rate [ 40 , 41 ]. For the ST780 sample, the generation of 
stress-induced martensite is more difficult (its CRSS is higher), and the 
amount of martensite is fewer (only existed in large β domain), so the 
increase of strain hardening rate at this stage is significantly lower than 
that of the other samples. However, large amounts of α phases in the 
ST780 sample provide lots of α / β interfaces in the matrix, which also 
obstruct the dislocation slip. Therefore, the strain hardening rate rea -
ches a relative balance in the ST780 sample at this stage. 
At the last stage, where the amounts of primary martensite bands 
reach the maximum value, the elastic and plastic deformation of dual- 
phases occurs [ 35 ]. As a result, the strain hardening rates start to 
decrease after reaching the maximum value. At this stage, two factors 
determine the decline rate of strain hardening rate curves. On the one 
hand, the dislocation slips occur in the matrix and within the martensite, 
and they are hindered by various boundaries. It is not difficult to un -
derstand that the more phase boundaries including β / α or β / α " bound -
aries in the matrix, the stronger resistance effects to dislocations slip, 
resulting in a lower decline rate of strain hardening rate at this stage. On 
the other hand, the deformation in the martensite plates occurs, and the 
“ secondary martensite ” or “ martensitic variant ” are induced within the 
martensite bands with the further increase in strain. Particularly, Gao 
[ 12 ] recognized these sub-structures as martensitic variants, Ma [ 24 ]. 
considered them as internal twins in Ti-1023 alloy, Ahmed [ 23 ] called 
them as “ secondary martensite ” , Zhang [ 25 ] suggested them as 
martensitic twins and Sadeghpour [ 26 ] reported these structures to be 
the product of interaction between twin and secondary martensite. No 
matter how we name these sub-structures, it is certain that such 
twin-like structures within the martensite are caused by the deformation 
of martensite. These sub-structures may have a twinning relationship 
with each other or a martensitic orientation relationship with the pri -
mary martensite, and they might be the deformation product of ortho -
rhombic martensite due to lack of sufficient slip systems at a high strain 
level. The appearance of these variants could hinder effectively the 
dislocation slip and play an important role in the coordination of 
deformation. Overall, the amounts of boundaries and the newly formed 
“ secondary structures ” within the martensite bands may jointly cause 
the low decline rate of the strain hardening rate at this stage. As shown 
in Figs. 4 – 7 , with the increase in solution treatment temperatures, the α 
phase content decrease, while the amount of deformation martensite 
increase during deformation. Therefore, with the increase in solution 
treatment temperature from 780 
◦
C to 800 
◦
C, the amount of β / α 
boundaries decreases significantly, so the decline rate of strain hard -
ening rate increases. When the solution temperature is further increased, 
more complexly distributed martensite and newly formed “ secondary 
structures ” provide more β / α " and α "/ α " interfaces, resulting in the 
decrease in decline rate once again. 
Based on all the observations and analyses in this study, the variation 
of deformation mode as a function of the phase stability and the 
deformation strain in the Ti-4321 alloy could be proposed, as shown in 
the schematic diagram in Fig. 9 . The content of the primary α phase 
adjusts the β phase stability of the matrix and the CRSS and morphology 
of deformation martensite are both influenced by the phase stability. 
Dislocation slip, martensite transformation, and their interactions 
dominate the whole process of deformation. In particular, the in -
teractions between dislocation slips and other boundaries are likely 
responsible for the variation of strain hardening rate. 
5. Conclusions 
In this study, four samples with different β phase stabilities were 
prepared by changing the volume fraction of the primary α phase in Ti- 
4321 alloy. The differences in microstructures, mechanical properties, 
and deformation modes were investigated systematically. The main 
conclusions can be summarized as follows: 
(1) With the increase in solution treatment temperature, the volume 
fraction of the α phase decreases, and the grain size increases in 
the Ti-4321 alloy. When the solution treatment temperature ex -
ceeds 820 
◦
C, the β single-phase microstructure can be obtained 
in the alloy. The higher volume fraction of the α phase the alloy 
has, the higher phase stability the β matrix has. 
(2) With the decrease in solution treatment temperature, the tensile 
strength of the Ti-4321 alloy increases, and the El decreases. The 
YS of the samples containing part of primary α phase is 12% – 68% 
higher than that of the sample with the β single phase. 
(3) TRIP and dislocation slip are the main deformation mechanism in 
the Ti-4321 alloy with the primary α phase content varying from 
0 to ~20%. The martensite can be produced at large β domains 
and the interactions between dislocation slips and other bound -
aries are likely responsible for the variation of strain hardening 
rate 
(4) The variation of the deformation mechanism as a function of β 
phase stability and deformation strain is proposed in the Ti-4321 
alloy. The morphology and volume fraction of martensite play an 
important role in the deformation of metastable β -Ti alloys.
