Abstract
By substituting Al with Si element, the Fe36Ni36Cr10Mo1Al17−XSiX (X = 0, 1, 2 and 4) alloys were prepared and their mechani-
cal properties were tested at high temperature ranging from 600 to 800 °C. The yield strength and ultimate tensile strength 
of the 4 alloys showed a trend of increasing first and then decreasing when tested at 700 °C. All the alloys with Si added 
showed enhanced strength and elongation compared with the alloy without Si addition. Fe36Ni36Cr10Mo1Al15Si2 exhibited 
the highest yield strength of 516 MPa, ultimate strength of 570 MPa, and elongation of 13.1% at 700 °C. The 2% Si addi-
tion increases the ultimate strength by 102 MPa compared with Si0 alloy (468 MPa), which is attributed to the strengthened 
boundaries with addition of Si element. Moreover, the yield strength and ultimate strength of the Fe36Ni36Cr10Mo1Al15Si2 
alloy represented a decreasing trend while elongation showed an increasing trend as temperature increased from 600 to 
800 °C. The rapid reduction of the strength and increase of the elongation was originated from the rapid softening of the B2 
phase at 800 °C compared with that of FCC phase.
Keywords Silicon · Microalloying · High-temperature mechanical property · Multi-principal element alloy
1 Introduction
Si, one of metalloid elements with small atomic radius, low 
density and affordable price, plays a vital role in enhancing 
the oxidation resistance [1 –3] and corrosion resistance at 
high temperature [4, 5]. However, it was controversial when 
applied in alloys for high temperature mechanical properties 
[6–9]. It was reported that Ni3Si, a kind of ordered precipi-
tate with L12 structure acted as a strengthener in Ni-based 
superalloys [10]. However, Si also acts as an impurity, which 
usually segregates at grain boundaries and thus lowers the 
grain boundary strength [8, 9].
Differently, the Si presents benefit in multi-principal-
component alloys [11]. The effect of Si addition on the 
strength is more significant than that of Al in a CoFeNi 
equal atomic ratio alloy [12] and Si could improve the com-
pressive mechanical properties by solid solution and pre -
cipitation strengthening of nanoscale cellular structure in 
 AlCoCrFeNiSiX alloys [13]. Si can also promote the forma-
tion of the BCC phase and improve the micro-hardness of 
 FeCoCrNiSiX alloys [14]. Therefore, Si works as beneficial 
element when applied in multi-principal-component alloys 
at room temperature. However, the role of Si at the elevated 
temperature is still unclear and clarify its effect to tensile 
strength at elevated temperature is needed.
Fe36Ni36Cr10Mo1Al17−XSiX (X = 0, 1, 2 and 4) alloys had 
been studied in previous work and they exhibited excel-
lent tensile strength and plastic strain at room tempera-
ture [15]. For these alloys, further evaluation of the high 
temperature tensile performance is still needed for future 
applications. In this study, the different Si-containing alloys 
 (Fe36Ni36Cr10Mo1Al17−XSiX, X = 0, 1, 2 and 4) alloys was 
prepared for high temperature tensile and their tensile behav-
ior was investigated. To our surprise, all the alloys after Si 
addition showed enhanced comprehensive properties which 
was completely different from the results at room tem-
perature. Fe36Ni36Cr10Mo1Al15Si2 exhibited the best yield 
strength of 516 MPa, ultimate strength of 570 MPa, and an 
elongation of 13.1% at 700 °C. Characterization and analysis

2 Experimental Section
2.1 Materials Preparation
Raw Al, Cr, Fe, Ni, Mo, and Si pellets with purity ≥ 99.9 wt% 
were melt to prepare the Fe36Ni36Cr10Mo1Al17−XSiX (X = 0, 
1, 2 and 4, named Si0, Si1, Si2 and Si4, respectively) alloys. 
To achieve chemical homogeneity, the alloy samples were 
remelted 4 times and each time kept for 5 min under elec-
tromagnetic stirring. The obtained alloys specimens with 
dimensions of 60 mm × 12 mm × 5 mm were then rolled 
~ 20% and recrystallized at 1200 °C for 20 min, followed 
by water quenching. After repeating the thermomechani-
cal treatment operation 3 times, aging process was con-
ducted at 700 °C for 4 h. Then, the specimens with a size of 
20 mm × 10 mm × 2 mm were cut by electro-spark wire-elec-
trode cutting and polished with 240-, 800-, 1500- and 2500-
grit SiC paper to remove the oxides. The polished specimens 
were then electron-polished in an etchant (10% perchloric 
acid and 90% ethanol) at 30 V for 6 s under ambient tem -
perature. The reference sample of Fe44.5Ni44.5Cr10Mo1 alloy 
was also prepared with the same method and tested at 700 °C 
for clarifying the role of Al element.
2.2 Microstructural Characterization 
and Mechanical Testing
The electron-polished alloys were characterized by scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM, TESCAN MIRA3) equipped 
with an electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) detec-
tor and Transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Talos 
F200X) to characterize the microstructure, grain size and 
element distribution of the Si element. EBSD data of the 
4 alloys with square area of 400 μm 2 were obtained with a 
step size of 0.5 μm and analyzed though AZtecCrystal soft-
ware. The mechanical properties at high temperature (600, 
700 and 800 °C) were tested by a universal testing machine 
(TSMT, EM6.504) equipped with a temperature controller 
and incubator. After reached aim temperature, the tensile 
test was performed with a displacement-controlled speed 
of 0.72 mm/min and the fracture morphology after tensile 
test was also characterized to reveal their fracture behavior.

3.2 Discussion
To reveal the strengthening mechanism, detailed characteri-
zation including high-magnification SEM, EBSD (Electron 
back scatter diffraction) and TEM was conducted. As pre-
sented in Fig. 3, some white needle-like phases with width 
of approximately 10–20 nm precipitated in the FCC phase 
and crossed over each other while abundant black particles 
with diameter of ~ 50 nm appeared in the B2 phase. The 
needle-like structures and particles were proven to be L12 
and BCC phases according to the previous report [15]. Over-
all, there is no significant difference with the Si addition.
Electron back scatter diffraction (EBSD) was performed 
to count their grain sizes. As inset shown in Fig. 4a1–d1, the 
Si0, Si1, Si2 and Si4 alloys exhibited same phase composi-
tion, including a white phase and a gray phase. Combined 
with the phase color derived from the test results, it was 
easy for us to clarify that the white structure corresponded 
to the red color was identified to be FCC phase while the 
other was B2 phase, which was consistent with the SEM 
results. In Fig.  4a2–d2, it showed the grain boundaries and 
grain orientation with different color. It seemed that Si0 had 
a lager grain size compared with Si1, Si2 and Si4 alloys. For 
quantitatively counting the grain size, frequency distribution 
histogram of grain size was drawn (Fig.  4a3–d3). From the 
histograms, the average grain size of the Si0, Si1, Si2 and 
Si4 alloys were calculated to be 4.57 μm, 3.89 μm, 4.02 μm 
and 4.18 μm. Though Si0 had the largest grain size and Si1, 
Si2 and Si4 had close grain size, it was noted that the grain 
size had no correlation with their mechanical properties. 
Meanwhile, their phase volume fraction were also obtained 
and shown in Table  3. The FCC phase volume fraction for 
Si0, Si1, Si2 and Si4 alloys were 45.1, 47.8, 49.6 and 47.4 
while B2 phase volume fraction were 54.9, 52.2, 50.4 and 
52.6, respectively. They showed almost no obvious change. 
Therefore, the grain size and phase volume fraction were 
also not the main reason that enhanced the high temperature 
tensile strength of Fe36Ni36Cr10Mo1Al17 alloy by substitut-
ing Al with Si.
Usually, the improved strength by substituting Al with Si 
may come from solid solution strengthening, precipitation 
strengthening and volume change of different phases. How-
ever, as shown in Table  1, there is no significant strength-
ening for the tensile strength at room temperature. For the 
increased 102 MPa tensile strength in Si2 alloy at 700 °C, 
there must be some other reasons. Very recently, the similar 
behaviors were observed by adding a little amount of Hf 
and B in the eutectic high entropy alloys [23, 24]. These 
elements were supposed to strengthen the boundaries at 
elevated temperatures. To verify this conjecture, transmis-
sion electron microscopy (TEM) and corresponding element 
distribution were conducted to characterize the morphology

4 Conclusions
By substituting Al with Si, the high temperature tensile 
behavior of Fe36Ni36Cr10Mo1Al17-XSiX alloys was studied 
at 600–800℃ . As Si increased from 0 to 4 at%, the yield 
strength and ultimate tensile strength showed a trend of 
increasing first and then decreasing. Fe36Ni36Cr10Mo1Al15Si2 
exhibited the best comprehensive mechanical properties with 
yield strength of 516 MPa, ultimate strength of 570 MPa, 
and an elongation of 13.1% at 700 °C. The 2% Si addition 
not only increases the elongation but the yield strength by 
102 MPa, which is attributed to strengthened boundaries 
with the addition of Si. In addition, the increase in tempera-
ture significantly affected the mechanical properties of the
