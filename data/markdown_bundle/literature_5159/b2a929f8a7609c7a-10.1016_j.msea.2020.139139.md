1. Introduction 
The requirement of higher temperature capabilities in aircraft and power-generation 
turbines is driving the continuous development of a dvanced superalloys [1-4]. In 
recent years, Co-base superalloys is attracting att ention as a competent alloy system 
since Co-Al-W-base superalloys able to form a strengthening metastable phase Co 3(Al, 
W) with an ordered nano-size L1 2 structure similar to that of Ni-base superalloys, 
which were first reported by Sato et al . [5]. This new class of Co-base superalloys has 
higher strength than conventional Co-based superall oys, which rely on solid solution 
strengthening and carbide strengthening at elevated temperature. Meanwhile, with 
higher solidus and liquids temperatures, higher tem perature strength, greater hot

## Page 4

corrosion resistance, Co-based superalloys exhibit less segregation during 
solidification compared with traditional Ni-base su peralloys [6]. Thus, significant 
research efforts have focused on the development of Co-base superalloys that can 
potentially substitute Ni-base superalloys. 
According to previous reports, the L1 2 ordered structure Co 3Al phase has low 
stability at high temperature ( ＞873 K) in Co-Al binary system [7]. Adding an 
appropriate ratio of W and Al to stabilize γ' phase in Co-base superalloys is an 
important milestone[8]. However, the W content in t hese superalloys varies between 
15% and 25% by weight, which significantly increase s the density of the alloys, 
comparing with Ni-base superalloys, and thus limits the application where strength to 
weight ratio is critical [9]. Therefore, there is g reat interest in developing Co-base 
superalloys containing free-W element, mainly repla ced W element with other lower 
density elements and ensuring the stability of the γ' phase. 
Among the reported Co-base binary systems, Co-Ti is the only known system with 
thermodynamically stable L1 2 ordered structure γ'-Co 3Ti and disordered fcc- γ phases 
[10, 11]. Thornton et al. [12] performed that the strength of Co 3Ti exceeds that of 
Ni 3Al and effectively increases the strength of the al loy. Furthermore, Co 3Ti also has 
the advantage of relatively low cost and low density. Thus, Co-Ti-base superalloys are 
promising alternatives to Co-Al-W-base alloys [13, 14]. Whereas, the large misfit 
between γʹ-Co 3Ti phase and γ matrix phase limits the microstructural stability and 
strength at elevated temperatures. At the same time , the addition of excessive Ti 
increases the tendency of η phase formation, which is generally considered to result in

## Page 5

deterioration of alloy properties [15]. This can be easily achieved through adjusting 
the concentration of Ti, and thus, a proper content of Ti is important for Co-base 
superalloys. The recent works reported a new series of lower density and higher yield 
strength γ-γ' Co-base superalloys which is free of W element an d has a relatively 
stable γ'-(Co, Ni) 3X phase after aging treatment by adding of Mo/Nb or Mo/Ta and 
increasing the Ni element content [9, 16, 17]. In f act, the addition of γ'-forming 
elements, such as Al, Ti, Mo, Nb, Ta and so on, has shown an improvement of γ' phase 
stability and high temperature mechanical propertie s [18-21]. Furthermore, the 
addition of Cr imparts oxidation resistance and hot corrosion via the formation of a 
protective (diffusion-resistant) Cr 2O3-rich scale [22, 23]. Therefore, the rational 
alloying adjustment of superalloys should be considered carefully. 
In this work, in order to understand the effects of the relative content of Al and Ti, 
and the addition of alloying elements on the micros tructure and mechanical properties 
of Co-base superalloys, three Co-Ni-Al-Ti quaternar y superalloys with adjusting the 
Al/Ti ratio, and two quinary superalloys adding Cr or Mo were designed, respectively. 
Microstructures were analyzed by application of fie ld-emission scanning electron 
microscope (FESEM), the lattice parameters and latt ice misfits were measured and 
calculated using X-ray diffraction (XRD). Furthermo re, vicker’s hardness 
measurements and tensile tests were also carried ou t to evaluate the mechanical 
properties of the five superalloys.

4. Discussion 
The main strengthening methods to improve mechanica l properties of superalloys 
are γ′ -precipitate hardening and solid solution strengthe ning. In fact, solid solution 
strengthening is associated with the type and the c ontent of alloying elements in γ

## Page 17

matrix phase. As a consideration, Co and Ni element s have similar atomic radius, and 
due to containing 30 at.% Ni in the experimental al loys, the solid solution 
strengthening constants of alloying elements in Nickel are listed in Table 3 to estimate 
the solid solution strengthening of the five aged s uperalloys in this experiment [36]. 
As is shown in Table 3, the solid solution strength ening constant of Ti is slightly 
higher than that of Al, and therefore, although Al content is replaced by Ti content, 
which will increase solid solution strengthening, t he relative contents of Al and Ti 
may have a negligible effect on the solid solution strengthening of the experimental 
superalloys because of the small amount of adjustme nt. On the other hand, replacing 
Co with 2 at.% of Cr or Mo both increase solid solu tion strengthening. Especially, Mo 
has a higher solid solution strengthening constant, which might significantly increase 
solid solution strengthening effect. However, accor ding to the above discussion, the 
variety of hardness does not match the change rule of solid solution strengthening. 
Therefore, it is speculated that solid solution str engthening is not a major factor 
affecting the mechanical properties in this experiment.

In addition to solid solution strengthening, γ′ -precipitate hardening as another 
important factor which affects mechanical propertie s of suprealloys should be 
considered. γ′ -precipitate hardening is determined by γ′ phase size, γ′ volume fraction, 
and intrinsic strength of γ′ phase precipitate. Previously researches showed th at 
decreasing the size of the γ′ precipitates and increasing the γ′ volume fraction both led 
to appreciable strengthening [37, 38]. As can be se en from Fig. 5a, due to the lower

## Page 18

aging temperature and shorter aging time in this experiment, all the test samples of the 
five aged superalloys possessed similar fine γ′ phase with an approximate size, which 
all less than 50 nm. Based on Fig. 7 and Fig. 8, there was no significant correlation 
between γ' phase size and values of vicker’s hardness and strength, thus the effect of γ' 
phase size on mechanical properties of the superalloys should be not significant in this 
experiment. Therefore, the variety in hardness of t he experimental alloy should be 
mainly related to γ' volume fraction and intrinsic strength of γ' phase. Zhang et al. [39] 
reported that the composition of γ'-(Co, Ni) 3(Al, W, Ti) changed from closer to γ'- 
Ni 3Al to closer to γ'-Co 3Ti due to the increase in relative Ti content, and γ'-Co 3Ti had 
higher intrinsic strength than γ'-Ni 3Al according to previous researches [12, 40]. This 
plausibly corresponds to a gradual chemical shift o f γ' phase from Ni 3Al-based to 
Co 3Ti-based with increasing Ti content in the three qu aternary superalloys, 
accompanied by an increase in vicker’s hardness. 
According to the comparison, the vicker’s hardness trends of the five superalloys 
are the same as those of γ' phase during aging period, both reached the maxim um 
value at 100 h aging. Therefore, the changed of vic ker’s hardness during aging can be 
explained by the changed in γ' volume fraction. However, it is worth noting that the γ' 
volume fraction in both 2Cr and 2Mo aged superalloy s increased comparing with the 
3.3Ti aged superalloy in the same aging period, whi le vicker’s hardness decreased. 
Different from the above conclusion, there is no obvious corresponding relationship

aged superalloys of 3.3Ti and 2Cr, which had nearly identical γ′ phase size and γ′

5. Conclusions 
This study investigates the effects of the relative content of Al and Ti, and the 
addition of 2 at.% Cr or Mo to substitute partly Co on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of Co-Ni-Al-Ti-base superallo ys. The following conclusions 
can be drawn: 
(1) The microstructure of the five as-cast superalloys consisted of 
γ-Co matrix and 
γ'-(Co, Ni) 3(Al, Ti) precipitates located on dendrites, and Ni, Al, Ti and Mo atoms 
partitioned preferentially to the γ' phase while Co and Cr atoms partitioned 
preferentially to the γ matrix phase. The microstructure of five aged supe ralloys 
composed of γ-Co matrix and nano-scale γ'-(Co, Ni) 3(Al, Ti) precipitates. 
Substituting Ti for Al, and adding 2% Cr or Mo all increased γ' volume fraction 
and γ' phase size in five aged superalloys. The size of γ′ phase increased with 
aging time, while γ′ phase volume fraction first increased, and then decreased after

## Page 21

reaching the maximum at 100 h 
(2) The lattice misfit increased with the concentration of Ti or the addition of 2% Mo, 
but the addition of 2% Cr had the opposite effect. Meanwhile, the lattice misfit 
increased with aging time. The morphology of γ' phase gradually changed from 
spheric to cuboid due to the increase in the lattice misfit. 
(3) Vicker’s hardness, yield strength and tensile stren gth all increased with the 
concentration of Ti, comparing with 2.5Ti aged supe ralloy, the vicker’s hardness 
and strength of 2Mo aged superalloy increased sligh tly, but decreased for the 2Cr 
aged superalloy. Vicker’s hardness during aging fir st increased, and then 
decreased after reaching the maximum at 100 h. 
(4) Vicker’s hardness, yield strength and tensile stren gth of five aged superalloys 
were strongly correlated with the measured lattice misfit and γ′ phase volume 
fraction in this experiment. The lattice misfit plays a key role on Vicker’s hardness 
in the aging time of 20 h to 100 h aging, while γ′ phase volume fraction plays a 
decisive role in Vicker’s hardness after aging for 100 h.
