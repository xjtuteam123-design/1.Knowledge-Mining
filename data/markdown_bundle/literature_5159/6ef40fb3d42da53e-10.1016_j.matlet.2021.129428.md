abstract
The ambient temperature brittleness and high density of refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) are the
obstacles for their applications as structural materials. A novel ductile body-centered cubic Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3
RHEA with high speciﬁc strength was developed. The tensile speciﬁc yield strength and fracture elonga-
tion of this alloy are 145.5 MPa /C1g
/C01 /C1cm3 and 23.0%, respectively. The speciﬁc yield strength is the high-
est among all other reported RHEAs. The superior mechanical properties of the Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 RHEA arise
from the solid solution strength and cross-slip of dislocations. The ultrahigh speciﬁc yield strength and
superior strength-plasticity synergy may promote the applications of RHEAs.

1. Introduction
The high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have recently attracted inten-
sive research interests because of their exceptional low-
temperature fracture toughness, irradiation resistance and
mechanical performance [1–3]. For face-centered cubic (FCC) HEAs
with intrinsic low yield strength, precipitation strengthening is an
effective way to enhance the yield strength at the expense of plas-
ticity for the appropriate strength-plasticity synergy [4]. While
body-centered cubic (BCC) HEAs possess the intrinsic high yield
strength but inferior plasticity at ambient temperature, especially
the Refractory HEAs (RHEAs) generally exhibit outstanding high-
temperature strength [5]. Therefore, it is of scientiﬁc importance
to improve the plasticity of RHEAs at ambient temperature.
In 2010, Senkov et al. ﬁrstly proposed and fabricated a series of
RHEAs with excellent softening resistance for super high-
temperature applications [6]. However, these reported RHEAs
exhibit negligible tensile ductility at ambient temperature due to
their high ductile-brittle transition temperature (higher than room
temperature) [3,7]. Research efforts have been attempted to over-
coming this shortcoming, and a high content of ductile metals,
including Nb, Hf and Ta, have been added to RHEAs to ameliorate
the tensile plasticity at ambient temperature [3,8–10]. However,
the alloy density increases signiﬁcantly, which severely limits their
applications. Therefore, developing ductile RHEAs with high speci-
ﬁc strength is of scientiﬁc and practical signiﬁcance for their appli-
cations [11–13].
In this work, the light metal Al was introduced into the refrac-
tory Ti
1.5ZrNb alloy, and the ductile Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 RHEA was devel-
oped, which shows the excellent strength-plasticity synergy and
the highest speciﬁc yield strength among the reported RHEAs.
The microstructural origins for the superior mechanical properties
of the Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 were investigated in-depth. The alloy design
strategy in this work might promote the development and the
practical applications of RHEAs.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 a shows the XRD pattern of Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 after annealing,
which has the single-phase body-centered cubic b-structure. This
is because the b phase stability increases with increasing content
of the b-stabilizer element of Nb [15], whose content is as high
as ~26.3 at.% in the Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3. Al element ( ~7.9 at.% in Ti 1.5-
ZrNbAl0.3) is completely dissolved in the Ti 1.5ZrNb matrix after heat
treatment according to the Al-Ti-Zr and Al-Nb-Ti ternary phase
diagrams [16,17]. The TEM micrograph and the selected-area elec-
tron diffraction (SAED) pattern in Fig. 1b further indicate that Ti 1.5-
ZrNbAl0.3 is a disordered BCC structure without precipitation. The
EBSD map in Fig. 1 c shows the recrystallized grains of Ti 1.5-
ZrNbAl0.3, and the grain size is 24.4 lm ± 13.8 lm. The EDS map-
pings in Fig. 1 d demonstrate that the components of
Ti1.5ZrNbAl0.3 are distributed uniformly, but chemical SROs should
exist at atomic scales.
The tensile stress-strain curve of Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 is shown in
Fig. 2a. The yield strength of Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 are 876 ± 6 MPa, which
is higher than most of FCC HEAs. The intrinsic high yield strength
of Ti1.5ZrNbAl0.3 with single BCC phase may attribute to the strong
chemical bonding inherited from the p-d hybrid orbital bonding
between hybridization of p state of Al and d state of transition met-
als [7,10]. And the strong chemical bonding would promote the
formation of SROs, which can affect the dislocation glide resis-
tance, e.g., the classical Peierls–Nabarro resistance is closely
related to atomic bonding [18]. The solid solution strengthening
(SSS) is evaluated in Appendix A of Supplementary data and is only
~22.8 MPa, which is obviously trivial to account for the strengthen-
ing effect. Furthermore, there is also no precipitation in the current
Ti1.5ZrNbAl0.3. Therefore, the SROs may be the dominant strength-
ening mechanism of the Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3.
The fracture strain ( ef)o fT i 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 is 23.0%, which exceeds
the plasticity of most RHEAs as shown in Fig. 2 a. There is a slight
drop in ﬂow stress after reaching the ultimate tensile strength,
and then a stable plastic deformation plateau is maintained until
fracture. The true stress-strain curve and strain hardening rate of
Ti1.5ZrNbAl0.3 have been shown in Fig. 2 b. The alloy underwent
the strain softening ﬁrstly after yield, and then maintained inferior
positive strain hardening rate before necking. Moreover, numerous
dimpled structures on the fracture surface as shown in the inset of
Fig. 2 a. These dimples are characteristic of ductile fracture mode
and exhibit the inherent plastic deformability of Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3.
Comparing the rys/q versus ef properties with other plastic RHEAs
[3,8,10,19–25], the speciﬁc yield strength of Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 is
145.5 MPa g /C01 cm3, which is the highest among all other RMPEAs
as shown in Fig. 2 c. Meanwhile, the rys/q /C0ef synergy of Ti 1.5-
ZrNbAl0.3 is also the best among all other RHEAs. The rys (MPa),
ef (%), q (g cm /C03) and rys/q (MPa g /C01 cm3) of RHEAs are listed in
Table S1 in Appendix B of Supplementary data .

4. Conclusion
In summary, a novel refractory Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 HEA with a single
BCC phase has been developed. This alloy exhibits a superior com-
bination of high yield strength (876 MPa), large plasticity (23.0%),
and the speciﬁc yield strength (145.5 MPa g /C01 cm3)-plasticity syn-
ergy. The speciﬁc yield strength of Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 is the highest
among all other RMPEAs. Strong chemical bonding and the resul-
tant SROs are the intrinsic origins for the high speciﬁc yield
strength. The dominated cross-slip and multiple dislocation inter-
actions, which forms vast dislocation pinning, loops, cell/walls,
account for the durable plastic deformability of the Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3.
The current alloy design strategy of Ti 1.5ZrNbAl0.3 may promote
the applications of RHEAs.
