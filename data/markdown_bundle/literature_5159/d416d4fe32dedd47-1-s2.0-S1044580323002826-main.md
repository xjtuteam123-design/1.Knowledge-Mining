ABSTRACT 
Microstructure modulation is of great significance for the practical application of TiAl alloys. Here, the optimal 
annealing temperature to obtain modulated microstructure containing nanoscale α
2 
+ O lathes for high Nb-TiAl 
alloy is determined as 625 
◦
C by dilatometry (DIL) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis. The 
results show that the compressive strength and plastic strain increased by 5% and 15.8% after annealing at 
625 
◦
C for 5 h, respectively. The improvement of both strength and ductility owing to the improved elastically 
mediated strain transfer ability related to refined microstructure in modulated structure. Geometric phase 
analysis (GPA) and HRTEM results show that the rough interface between tweed laths and γ lamella provides 
nucleation sites for dislocations which release the local elastic strain energy. What ’ s more, the formation of 
nanoscale deformation twins as well as extensive twin intersections also played an important role in the 
improvement of both strength and ductility. 
1. Introduction 
Light and strong materials have been extensively investigated to 
meet the requirements of aerospace and automotive industry, where 
energy efficiency and carbon neutrality are more and more valued 
[ 1 – 3 ]. TiAl intermetallics are an important class of light-weight mate -
rials with high specific strength, excellent creep and fatigue properties 
up to high temperatures, that make them considerable candidates for 
applications in the air craft industry [ 4 – 9 ]. However, the widespread 
applications of TiAl based alloys have been hindered by their poor room- 
temperature (RT) ductility and relatively low strength at high temper -
ature. Fortunately, high Nb-TiAl alloys are developed to further raise 
their high-temperature mechanical properties[ 10 ]. The addition of Nb 
can strengthen the alloy by solid-solution hardening, microstructure 
refinement and precipitation strengthening [ 11 – 13 ]. 
Recently, more efforts have been put to improve the RT ductility of 
high Nb-TiAl alloys because higher ductility is critical to avoid cata -
strophic fracture failure of the engine. One strategy of overcoming the 
brittleness of intermetallic TiAl alloys is to introduce more ductile 
phases. C.T. Liu et al. [ 14 ] revealed that the disordered nanolayer can 
act as a sustainable source of ductility, preventing brittle fracture by 
enhancing dislocation activity. More importantly, the FCC-type disor -
dered nanolayer and L1
2
-type ordered hyperlattice have a low mismatch 
significantly inhibit the grain coarsening behavior of the material under 
high-temperature conditions and improve its thermal stability. In fact, 
this strategy is also effective in TiAl intermetallics. Appel et al. [ 13 , 15 ] 
found a modulated microstructure composed of disordered β -stable 
phase and ordered B19 metastable phase in Ti-(40 – 44)Al-8.5Nb alloy, 
which exhibits good RT ductility and creep resistance. Therefore, it is 
extremely important to identify the precipitation behavior of the 
modulated microstructure and according mechanical behavior of the 
high Nb-TiAl alloys. 
In this work, DIL and TEM were used to determine the optimal 
annealing temperature of obtaining orthorhombic phase for Ti-45Al- 
8Nb alloy. The RT compressive properties of the modulated micro -
structures containing orthorhombic phase were tested. The corre -
sponding deformation mechanisms were analyzed via TEM. The main 
goal is to modulate microstructure, reveal the variation of mechanical 
properties, and demonstrate strengthening-plasticizing mechanism. This 
work provides a basic understanding on how the modulated micro -
structure affect mechanical properties of high Nb-TiAl alloys.

3.4. Compressive properties and deformation mechanism 
Fig. 5 shows the RT compressive stress-strain curves of the HIPed and 
modulated structures. After annealing at 625 
◦
C for 5 h, the compressive 
strength and plastic strain have a value of 1812 ± 31 MPa and 19.0 ±
2.0%, while these of as-HIPed specimens are 1725 ± 37 MPa and 16.4 ±
2.6%. Interestingly, large jagged fluctuations appear in the compressive 
stress-strain curves of both HIPed and modulated structures. However, 
fluctuations appear later in modulated structures (at ~10% compressive 
strain) compared with that of the HIPed structures (at ~9% compressive 
strain). Such large signal fluctuations usually indicate a significant crack 
in the material. It shows in Fig. 6 that large and straight crack initiated in 
the HIPed specimen after deformed at compressive strain of 9%, while 
the crack in the specimen with modulated structure is smaller and 
curved. Therefore, large fluctuations event occurred earlier in the 
compressive stress – strain curve of HIPed specimen. Similar character -
istic features are found as “ pop-in events ” in a three-point bending test 
reported by Appel et al. [ 13 ], which apparently reflect the different 
crack growth resistance of the individual constituents. They attributed 
the sudden drops of the bending force to the trans-lamellar plate failure 
when the crack propagates trough regions that are almost free of tweed 
lamellae. In other words, the fine tweed laths can delay the generation of 
large fluctuations events and large crack as well. 
As reported by D. Hu et al., visible cracking can be identified by an 
acoustic emission system and that cracking occurred at much lower 
stresses than expected [ 19 , 20 ]. While in our compressive stress-strain 
curves, the large fluctuations generated during deformation progress 
reveals that larger cracks initiated. Therefore, we consider that the 
improvement of both strength and ductility owing to the improved 
“ elastically mediated ” strain transfer ability related to refined micro -
structure [ 21 ] rather than to the higher deformability Ti
2
AlNb (O- 
phase). Significant crack is more difficult to initiate in modulated 
structures due to the higher strain delocalization ability brought by the 
presence of the tweed laths. 
To further reveal the mechanisms behind the enhancement of RT 
strength and plastic strain, the deformed structures were characterized 
via TEM. Fig. 7 a shows fine dispersion of mechanical twins in γ lamella 
and thickness of them is in the nanoscale range. The SAED pattern in 
Fig. 7 b confirm that the formation of twins as well as extensive twin 
intersections developed in the deformed γ lamella. While in the tweed 
laths ( α
2 
+ O phase), no significant plastic deformation was observed as 
shown in Fig. 7 a. It is often impossible to shear α
2 
lamellae plastically 
due to the high intrinsic glide resistance of the α
2 
phase. As reported by 
Singh et al. [ 22 ], the strain could be elastically transferred when the α
2 
lamella is very thin as the stress field decreases with increasing distance 
from the twin tip. Therefore, the improved “ elastically mediated ” strain 
transfer ability related to refined microstructure can relax the stress 
concentration at the interface between tweed laths and γ lamella. As a 
result, crack is more difficult to initiate in modulated structures and 
higher strength was obtained as confirmed by Fig. 5 . Furthermore, the 
interface between tweed laths and γ lamella is rough ( Fig. 7 a), we 
consider that the misfit situation provides nucleation sites for disloca -
tions and deformation twins, which supports plastic deformation.

4. Conclusion 
Dilatometry and transmission electron microscopy were used to 
determine the optimal annealing temperature to obtain orthorhombic O- 
phase for Ti-45Al-8Nb alloy. The compressive properties of the modu -
lated microstructures containing orthorhombic O-phase was tested at RT 
and the corresponding deformation mechanisms were analyzed via TEM 
and GPA. The following main conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) The optimal annealing temperature to obtain orthorhombic 
phase for Ti-45Al-8Nb alloy is 625 
◦
C.
