In Situ TiC Particle-Reinforced FeCoCrNiCu High Entropy Alloy
Matrix Composites by Induction Smelting
Haojie Wang 1,3 • Zhiyi Wu 2 • Hao Wu 2 • Heguo Zhu 2 • Wencheng Tang 1
Received: 9 July 2020 / Accepted: 17 November 2020 / Published online: 2 January 2021
/C211The Indian Institute of Metals - IIM 2021
Abstract High entropy alloy is a new type of high-per-
formance metal material with high strength, high wear
resistance and other characteristics, which has been widely
concerned by scholars at home and abroad. In this study,
in situ TiC particle-reinforced FeCoCrNiCu high entropy
alloy matrix composites were prepared successfully via
vacuum induction melting method. X-ray diffraction and
scanning electron microscope equipped with energy-dis-
persive spectrometer were used to analyze the phase,
composition and micromorphology of the samples, and
tensile properties of the samples were tested at room
temperature. The experimental results show that the
FeCoCrNiCu high entropy alloy matrix has an equiaxed
grain structure, and the TiC-reinforced high entropy alloy
matrix composite has a single FCC phase structure. The
generation of in situ TiC-reinforced phase makes the
composite tensile strength increase, but the elongation
decreases. After adding 10 vol% TiC-reinforced phase, the
tensile strength of the sample increases from 560.3 to
705.2 MPa, and the elongation decreases from 53.3 to
23.3%.

1 Introduction
Traditional alloying materials are dominated by one or two
alloying elements, and the properties of materials can be
improved by adding a small amount of other elements.
With the development of science and technology, the study
of traditional alloy materials soon encountered shackles.
Professor Ye [ 1] proposed the concept of multi-principle
high entropy alloys at the end of the last century, that is,
alloy materials are composed of ﬁve or more elements, and
the content of each principal element is between 5 and
35%. Each principal element is basically alloyed according
to the equimolar ratio, and under the effect of high-mixing
entropy, a simple crystal structure is formed, such as FCC
and BCC [ 2–4]. High entropy alloys have dynamic diffu-
sion retardation effect, thermodynamic high-mixing
entropy effect, structural lattice distortion and cocktail
effect on performance [ 5–7]. Compared with other alloy
materials, high entropy alloys have excellent properties
such as high hardness, high wear resistance, fatigue resis-
tance, high temperature stability and corrosion resistance.
Therefore, high entropy alloys have good development
prospects in many ﬁelds of engineering materials and have
been widely concerned by scholars at home and abroad
[8–13].
In recent years, a few scientiﬁc researches on high
entropy alloy matrix composites and ceramic reinforce-
ments have been studied to improve the matrix in terms of
hardness, strength, ductility and so on [ 14–22]. Rogal et al.
[16] prepared 5% Al
2O3/CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy-
based nanocomposites by mechanical alloying and hot
isostatic pressing. Compared with the high entropy alloy
matrix, the hardness of the composite increased from 418
to 545 HV, the Young’s modulus reached 210 GPa, the

At present, the preparation methods of high entropy
alloy matrix composites mainly include solid phase mixing,
liquid phase mixing and gas phase mixing. Liquid mixing
mainly refers to vacuum melting technology, including arc
melting and induction melting. Most of the high entropy
alloy matrix composites are prepared by vacuum melting,
that is, the metal elements are heated and melted uniformly
in a vacuum atmosphere, poured and cooled to form a cast
high entropy alloy material [ 18, 22]. Vacuum induction
smelting refers to the use of alternating magnetic ﬁelds
generated by winding coils under high vacuum to induce an
induction current (eddy current) in the metal material itself,
and the alloys are melted by eddy current heating and then
cast to form a technical means, which has the character-
istics of fast heating rate and high temperature, and can
accurately control the content of active metal elements
(such as aluminum) to ensure the purity of the alloy ingot.
Using composite materials to improve the properties of
high entropy alloys has become a new ﬁeld of research for
scholars. Exogenous metal matrix composites have some
problems such as poor interface bonding and high pro-
cessing costs. The thermodynamics of enhanced phase
generated by in situ reaction is more stable, due to high
binding strength and good wetting property with matrix
metal [23, 24]. The distribution of the reinforcement phase
is more uniform, ﬁnely dispersed, and easy to control.
While ensuring plasticity, it can also effectively improve
the speciﬁc strength and speciﬁc modulus of the material.
Most of the current research revolves around high
entropy alloys, while high entropy alloy matrix composites
are rarely reported. Although there are some researches on
the FeCoCrNiCu high entropy alloy [ 25, 26], there are few
researches on FeCoCrNiCu high entropy alloy matrix
composites. In the present work, TiC particle-reinforced
FeCoCrNiCu high entropy alloy matrix composites were
prepared by in situ reaction via vacuum induction melting
method. The inﬂuence of the in situ TiC particles on the
tensile properties of composites was studied by observing
the microscopic morphology, analyzing the phase struc-
tures and testing the mechanical properties.
2 Experimental Procedures
High purity of Co particles, Cr particles, Ni particles, Cu
particles, Fe powders, Ti powders and C powders was used
as raw materials to prepare high entropy alloy matrix,
5 vol% TiC/FeCoCrNiCu composite material and 10 vol%
TiC/FeCoCrNiCu composite. The content of each compo-
nent of the high entropy alloy matrix in the composites is a
ﬁxed equimolar ratio of 1:1:1:1:1. Physical parameters of
high entropy alloy composites are shown in Table 1.
According to the calculation, the Ti powder and C powder
are required when the volume fraction of the reinforcement
of 5% and 10% are separately conﬁgured, and the iron
powder in the matrix content of the high entropy alloy is
mixed at the same time. Mechanical mixing facilitates the
in situ formation of TiC-reinforced phase during smelting.
To start with, a proportion of 5:1 of mixed powders (Fe, Ti
and C) were ball-milled in the planetary mill (QM-ISP) for
4 h at the speed of 250 rounds/min. Then, the mixed
powders were put in a drying oven to dry for 1 h. After
pressing the powder into the cylindrical block with the oil
press (FLS-50T), the mass loss was calculated and the
corresponding Co, Cr, Ni, Cu particles were weighed. The
mixed particles were put into the ceramic crucible together
with the briquette, and the alloy ingot was smelted in an
induction heating furnace under the protective atmosphere
of argon gas.
The specimens were corroded with aqua regia after
grinding and polishing. The microstructures of the speci-
mens were characterized by means of scanning electron
microscopy (SEM, Quant 250FEG). The phases were
observed by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker-AXS D8
Advance). Tensile tests were operated using an electronic
universal testing machine (UTM5504) at a constant rate of
0.5 mm/min.
3 Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructures and Structures
Figure 1 shows the XRD pattern of high entropy alloy
matrix composites with different TiC contents. It can be
seen that the main phase of FeCoCrNiCu high entropy
alloy matrix composites prepared by induction melting is a
simple FCC structure. With the addition of the TiC rein-
forcement phase, the material still maintains the FCC
structure, but the intensity of each diffraction peak chan-
ges. This may be due to the fact that Ti and C do not all
react to form the TiC enhanced phase, and some atoms are
dissolved into the FCC solid solution phase of the high
entropy alloy, resulting in changes in the crystal orientation
structure involved in diffraction.
Figure 2 reveals the microstructure of TiC/FeCoCrNiCu
with different TiC content. It can be seen that the surface of
the high entropy alloy matrix composite which has not
been corroded by aqua regia is not easy to observe. The
corroded samples have obvious grain boundaries in the
SEM image, and the micromorphology is clearly visible.
Figure 2a, b shows the SEM of high entropy alloy matrix
without TiC. It can be seen that the alloy material has ﬁne
grains, clear grain boundaries, and an equiaxed crystal

3.2 Tensile Properties
The tensile strength and elongation of the TiC/FeCoCr-
NiCu composites are listed in Table 2, and Fig. 4 shows
the tensile curves of the composites with different TiC
contents. It can be seen that with the increase in the content
of TiC, the tensile strength increases, while the elongation
decreases.
Combined with the micromorphology diagram, it can be
seen that the grains of the pure high entropy alloy material
are ﬁne and equiaxed. This ﬁne-grain strengthening effect
not only improves the strength of the material but also
increases the plasticity of the material [ 27, 28]. In the
process of ﬁne-grain strengthening, the plastic deformation
of one grain cannot be propagated directly to the neigh-
boring grains, and the dislocation plug of the plastic grain
is caused. Under the action of external force, the disloca-
tion plug on the grain boundary produces a stress ﬁeld,
which can be used as the driving force to activate the
dislocation source in adjacent grains.
The increase in tensile strength after the addition of TiC
reinforcement phase is mainly due to the TiC reinforce-
ment particles generated in the high entropy alloy matrix,
which are generated directly in the matrix through chem-
ical reaction. The thermodynamically stable and high
interfacial bonding strength, can transfer load more effec-
tively and improve the strength of the material, which is
identiﬁed as load bearing effect [ 29].

Dislocation enhancement is also an important strength-
ening mechanism in high entropy alloys matrix composites
[30]. Combined with the microscopic morphology of the
composite in Fig. 2, it can be seen that the TiC brittle phase
is distributed in a granular form in the high entropy alloy
matrix and is dispersed ﬁnely. When the matrix is plasti-
cally deformed, the TiC-reinforced phase acts as a pinning
dislocation and hinders the dislocation. Movement causes
dislocation jamming and the deformation resistance of the
material increases. Therefore, increasing the TiC volume
fraction within a certain range can increase the tensile
strength of the composite.
It can be seen from the XRD pattern of Fig. 1 that the
high entropy alloy matrix exhibits an FCC structure. The
FCC structure is more likely to slip along the close-packed
direction of the close-packed surface; thus, the FCC
structure has better plasticity, making the high entropy
alloy composites have high strength, as well as good
plasticity and toughness. After adding TiC-reinforced
phase, the grain size of the composites becomes larger, and
the ﬁne-grain strengthening effect is weakened. During the
plastic deformation of the high entropy alloy matrix, stress
concentration occurs around the reinforcement phase par-
ticles, which makes the bonding interface easier to pull
apart, and the shallow dimples and brittleness increase,
which prevents the plastic deformation from continuing
and leads to a decrease in the elongation of the composite.
In a short, the alloy with a very high dislocation density
will be brittle. Therefore, as the volume fraction of the
TiC-reinforced phase increases, the elongation of the high
entropy alloy composite decreases. But the tensile strength
of 10 vol% TiC/FeCoCrNiCu composite is 705.2 MPa and
the elongation of it is still acceptable value (23.3%).

4 Conclusions
The FeCoCrNiCu HEAs prepared by vacuum induction
melting technology form a single FCC phase structure with
an equiaxed crystal structure. TiC reinforcement particles
are generated by in situ reaction in the high entropy alloy
matrix which disperse in the composite material. The
composites still maintain the FCC phase structure, and the
grain size increases microscopically, with Cu element
segregating within the grains and on the grain boundaries.
Due to the effect of several strengthening mechanisms,
10 vol% TiC/FeCoCrNiCu high entropy alloy matrix
composite exhibits an excellent combination of ultimate
tensile strength (705.2 MPa) and ductility (23.3%). The
fracture of samples shows typical plastic fracture.
