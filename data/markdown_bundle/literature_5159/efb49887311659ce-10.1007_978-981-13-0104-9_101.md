Investigation of High-Temperature
Mechanical Property and Fracture
Mechanism of Tungsten Alloy
Guohui Liu, Ling Wang, Yingnan Qin, Guirong Liu and Ning Xiong
Abstract In this study, 93W –5Ni–2Fe tungsten heavy alloy prepared by powder
metallurgy method were tested at 1000, 1100, 1200, 1300 and 1400 °C respectively
for investigating high temperature mechanical properties. Fracture analysis was
carried out by scanning electron microscope and then developing principles of
fracture mechanism obtained. The results showed that as the elevating temperature
in the range of 1000 –1400 °C, mechanical properties of 93W –5Ni–2Fe alloy, such
as tensile strength, elongation, reduction of area and Young ’s module were dra-
matically getting worse. Material becoming brittle, transgranular fracture hardly
found, binder phase tear and interfacial fracture between tungsten grain and binder
phases are domestic fracture mode.

Introduction
Tungsten heavy alloy (WHA) with the unique properties such as high strength, high
hardness, good ductility, good machining performance, low thermal expansion
coefﬁcient, high thermal conductivity, good oxidation and corrosion resistance, is
considered as a reliable material for a wide range of application ﬁelds, for example,
aerospace, electronic information and military ﬁeld [1, 2]. Especially on the ﬁeld of
aerospace so far, tungsten heavy alloy has been more and more frequently applied
to extreme environments, for instance high temperature at 1200 °C and the limi-
tation estimated approximately 1300 °C. In recent years, a loads of researches has
been launched into tungsten alloy mechanical property and fracture mechanism in
room temperature static state at the effort donated by home and abroad scientists.
However, available studies of mechanical behavior at high temperature are rarely
beyond 1100 °C [ 3, 4]. Therefore, in this study, taking 93W –5Ni–2Fe as an
example, the mechanical behaviors and fracture characteristics at temperature span
1000–1400 °C were studied by using the high-temperature tensile test method,
developing principles of tensile strength and fracture mechanism with temperature
in the condition of ultrahigh temperature was explored.
Experimental
93WNiFe alloy adopted in this research was using commercial powders of ﬁne W
powder (3 µm), electrolytic Ni powder and carbonyl Fe powder as raw materials,
prepared by three-dimensional mixer in the light of composition ratio of 93W –5Ni–
2Fe, molded into bar specimen by cold isostatic pressing, and then sintered at
hydrogen atmosphere, ﬁnally vacuum heat treatment. The sample was machined
according to Fig. 1. The specimen was machined following GB/T 228.2-2015 and
with argon atmosphere protection tested by INSTRON 3369 Tensile and
Compression tester. Stretching rate was 2 mm/min. The test temperature were
1000, 1100, 1200, 1300 and 1400 °C respectively. The fracture of specimen
fracture was analyzed by using the model of GSM –6380LV scanning electron
microscope, and the evolution of fracture mode with temperature was observed.

Results and Discussion
Effect of Temperature on Material Mechanical Properties
Table 1 is mechanical property data of 93WNiFe alloy at different temperatures. At
room temperature (RT 30 °C), tensile strength is 936 MPa, yield strength is
670 MPa, elongation is 25%, while in high temperature span of 1000 –1400 °C,
having a signi ﬁcant difference. Figure 2 is 93WNiFe alloy tensile strength with
temperature curve and Fig. 3 is yield strength with temperature curve. As the
ﬁgures shown, both tensile and yield strengths decrease with the increasing tem-
perature when temperature falling the range 1000 –1400 °C, the tensile strength
decreasing from 281 to 102 MPa, yield strength reduced from 200 to 93. 2 MPa.
Moreover, as the temperature increases, tensile and yield strength are getting closer,
yield and fracture tend to be synchronized. Figure 4 is elongation with the tem-
perature curve. The same tendency as tensile and yield strength, the elongation of
the material dramatically decreased with increasing temperature, from 8.5 to 4%,
fracture mode transform into brittle fracture.
Fracture Analysis
Figure 5 is the typical fracture morphology of 93WNiFe alloy at room temperature.
It can be seen that the fracture of tungsten alloy is mainly composed of four kinds of
faults at room temperature, namely tungsten grain transgranular fracture, bonder
phase torn fracture, tungsten-bonder interfacial de-bonding and tungsten-tungsten
interfacial separating. At the room temperature, tungsten grain transgranular frac-
ture and binder phase tearing fracture are mainly fracture mode. Cleavage strength
of tungsten particle is very high, which resulting in material behaving great tensile
strength, and bonding phase tearing also further verifying the high elongation.

particles can be easily separated from the bonding phase, and strength of the binder
phase is also signi ﬁcantly reduced. Tensile strength and the elongation of material
macroscopically behaves sharply reduced.
Conclusion
(1) In 1000 –1400 °C temperature range, the tensile strength, yield strength and
elongation of 93WNiFe alloy are gradually decreasing with the increasing
temperature, and the yield strength and tensile strength are getting closer along
with temperature.
(2) The tensile strength of tungsten alloy depends on the fracture mode. In the
condition of high temperature environment, tungsten-binder phase de-bonding
and tungsten-tungsten interfacial separation are mainly fracture mode.
Transgranular fracture at room temperature disappearing at high temperature.
(3) From 1000 to 1400 °C, with the increase of temperature, W –W interfacial
strength and bonding phase strength decreased sharply. Material exhibits
obvious brittle fracture.
