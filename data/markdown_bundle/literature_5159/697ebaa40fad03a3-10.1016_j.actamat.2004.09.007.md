Determining the site occupancy of Ru in the L1 2 phase of a Ni-base
superalloy using ALCHEMI

Abstract
Small additions of Ru signiﬁcantly improve the high temperature creep properties of Ni-base superalloys, but the mechanism is
uncertain. In order to understand the eﬀects of adding Ru it is important to know the atomic site(s) occupied by Ru in the superalloy
structure. We used the technique ALCHEMI (Atomic site Location by CHannelling Enhanced MIcroanalysis) on a Ni-base super-
alloy containing Ru and encountered unexpected problems. We have explained why the ALCHEMI technique cannot accurately be
used with Ni-base superalloys. We then successfully applied ALCHEMI to the simpler ternary alloy Ni–19at.%Al–3at.%Ru and
found that 60% of the Ru partitions to the Al sublattice in the Ni
3Al c0 phase giving an absolute atomic site preference of 5:1.
Assuming such site preference holds true for more complex alloys, this information can be used to better understand the role that
this platinum group metals plays.

1. Introduction
Nickel-base superalloys are used extensively for tur-
bine blades in gas turbines of aeroengines and power
plants. There are two primary phases present in such
an alloy, the matrix c phase (based on nickel, fcc) which
surrounds the precipitate c
0 phase (based on Ni 3Al,
L12). Typically these materials contain in excess of
twelve elements, their complex composition resulting
from extensive alloy development over many years. This
paper focuses on the eﬀect of the platinum group metal
Ru on the structure of the latest generation of turbine
blade single crystal Ni-base superalloys. In particular
it reports the determination of the sublattice site distri-
bution of Ru in the c
0 phase. Ru partitions preferentially
to the c phase in a c/c0 superalloy [1] with approximately
2 at.% going to c for every 1 at.% that goes to c0. The
addition of Ru is of particular interest due to some re-
cent studies suggesting that this results in a signiﬁcant
improvement in high temperature mechanical properties
of third generation single crystal alloys [2,3].
The phase diagram of the basic c–c
0 system for
Ni-based superalloys is shown in Fig. 1 . The multitude
of alloying additions modify important mechanical
properties such as elastic constants, and physical proper-
ties such as lattice parameter, but the underlying two
phase c–c
0 microstructure needs to be preserved. A fur-
ther limitation for advanced superalloys used in high
temperature turbine blades is that the c
0 volume fraction
has to be kept between approximately 65–75%. This is
required in order to optimise the high temperature
(900–1100 /C176 C) creep behaviour which, at present, deter-
mines the life of the alloy.

Knowledge of the sublattice site that a doping species
within c0 occupies is important in understanding c–c0
lattice misﬁt and microstructural evolution as well as
designing improved alloys. For example, elements that
prefer Al sites in c
0 may give rise to an increase in pre-
cipitate formation; whereas those that prefer Ni sites
may promote matrix formation.
It has recently been established that small additions
of Ru can dramatically improve the creep performance
of alloys at 1100 /C176 C [3], however, the mechanism by
which it does this is uncertain. Determining sublattice
partitioning ratios may be important for explaining this
and other eﬀects.
The atomic site occupancy of an element may in prin-
ciple be determined using ALCHEMI (Atomic site Loca-
tion by CHannelling Enhanced MIcroanalysis) [5].A
reﬁnement of the original Tafto and Spence method is
statistical ALCHEMI [6]. Statistical ALCHEMI has
been successfully applied to ternary through to quater-
nary alloy systems for the L1
0 TiAl-based alloy [6–8],
as well as for the D0 22 and L1 2 variants of titanium tri-
aluminides [9]. In related work, the lattice site of Cu in
b-phase NiAl has been observed to swap from one sublat-
tice site to the other [10], depending on the degree of non-
stoichiometry (Al-deﬁcient or Ni-deﬁcient alloy). This
eﬀect was also studied by
63Cu NMR spectra, which
yielded similar results, i.e. Cu occupies the Al-sublattice
site exclusively in Al-deﬁcient alloys, but is distributed
80:20 over Ni and Al sites respectively in a Ni-deﬁcient
alloy. An alternative method for ALCHEMI, the order-
ing tie-line approach, has been suggested by Hou et al.
[11], and Jiang et al. [12] have suggested ways of optimis-
ing ALCHEMI. Recently, Jones [13] has provided an
extensive review of ALCHEMI.
In this paper, statistical ALCHEMI is used to deter-
mine the sublattice site distribution of Ru in the c
0 phase
of a multi-element Ni-base superalloy, C17, of composi-
tion Ni–14Al–13Co–3W–2Ta–2Re (at.%), which forms a
classic c/c
0 Ni-base superalloy structure. 5 at.% Ru was
then added to C17, producing c0 precipitates with an
approximate composition as shown in Table 2. Note that
the Ru concentration in these precipitates is about
4 at.% because Ru preferentially partitions to the c
phase. Statistical ALCHEMI was used to determine
the atomic site occupancy of the Ru in the c
0 phase.
However, results are shown to be clearly unreliable due
to: small diﬀerences in channelling response from each
sublattice site; similar projected potentials; and weak dif-
ference reﬂections. Diﬀerences in channelling patterns
under these circumstances are dominated by interaction
delocalization, thermal smearing and X-ray absorption.
Turning to the basic c
0 structure, Ni 3Al, the atomic
site occupancy of Ru is determined in this structure
where diﬀerence reﬂections are strong and the channel-
ling response from each sublattice site is clearly
distinguishable. A full analysis of the eﬀects of delocali-
zation, thermal smearing and X-ray absorption on
statistical ALCHEMI is performed.
2. Sources of error in statistical ALCHEMI
Although the overall theory of statistical ALCHEMI
has been published by Rossouw et al. [6], it is rewritten

5. Sublattice site distributions and projected potentials
Fig. 4 shows unit cell projections of the Ni 3Al phase
along the Æ100 æ, Æ110 æ and Æ111 æ directions, sublattice
site a (Al) being on the origin and sublattice site b (Ni) on
the face centres of the cube. This structure has no orien-
tation in which alternate planes are formed by atoms
either on sublattice site a or on sublattice site b. Invaria-
bly, at least one of the planes contains an array of both
sublattice sites, which may in turn project onto similar
atomic columns. Experiment and theory conﬁrm that dif-
ference reﬂections ( hkl mixed) from the 5Ru–C17 alloy
are weak in comparison with the fundamental Ni
3Al al-
loy, which implies that the projected elastic potential
V(r) on both sublattice sites a and b is similar. It is true
that weak diﬀerence reﬂections could also be sympto-
matic of disordering within the system. However, qualita-
tively at least the relative intensities of the fundamental
and superlattice reﬂections from 5Ru–C17 were in agree-
ment with the structure factor calculations based on per-
fect order between Al and Ni. Moderate disorder within
the heavily alloyed system would most likely have caused
the already weak superlattice reﬂections to disappear
completely.

g in volts for various
strong reﬂections ( hkl all odd or all even) and diﬀer-

10. Conclusions
Statistical ALCHEMI has been shown to be of
limited use for determining site occupancies in multi-
element heavily alloyed Ni
3Al. First, the projected
potential is almost the same on the two sublattice sites
in the c
0 phase, hence diﬀerences in channelling pattern
contrast generated from sublattice sites a and b are
small. Second, Al excitations, used to generate a chan-
nelling ﬁngerprint for sublattice site a, have interaction
delocalization, thermal motions and X-ray absorption
that are substantially diﬀerent from those of the alloyed
elements, and it is these atomic species-dependent cor-
rections that dominate diﬀerences in channelling pattern
contrast. A detailed analysis of these eﬀects has shown
that, for this heavily alloyed phase, interaction delocali-
zation provides the major correction.
Since heavily alloyed Ni
3Al cannot be used to deter-
mine the atomic site location of Ru, we have investi-
gated whether we can use ALCHEMI to determine the
atomic site of Ru in binary Ni
3Al. Greater diﬀerences
in channelling patterns occur from Ni 3Al, since it has
a larger diﬀerence in projected potentials. This largely
ameliorates problems associated with the interaction
delocalization of Al. Analysis of simulated data has
shown that sublattice site partitioning of Ru may be
determined with reasonable accuracy. Good overall
agreement occurs between experimental and calculated
X-ray channelling patterns for Ni
3Al.
ALCHEMI experimental data for Ru-doped Ni 3Al
shows that Ru partitions preferentially onto the Al sub-
lattice. Approximately 60% of Ru partitions onto Al
sites with a goodness-of-ﬁt parameter ( v
2
R) of about
unity. This indicates that, on a site-by-site basis, Ru is
about ﬁve times more likely to substitute for an Al atom
than for a Ni atom in Ni 3Al. With the assumption that
the sublattice site distribution of 2 at.% Ru in Ni 3Al is
transferable to the c0 phase in heavily alloyed superal-
loys, a basis for understanding the overall properties,
such as lattice misﬁt, can be laid.
