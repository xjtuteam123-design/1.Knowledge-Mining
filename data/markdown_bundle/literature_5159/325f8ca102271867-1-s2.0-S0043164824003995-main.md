1. Introduction
With the rapid advance of manufacturing technology, there is an 
increasing demand for high-performance materials that exhibit excep -
tional properties for the applications under extreme conditions [ 1 , 2 ]. 
Recently, high-entropy alloys (HEAs), also known as complex compli -
cated alloys (CCAs), have attracted wide research interest in the last two 
decades as a new materials design strategy [ 3 , 4 ]. It has been widely 
reported that HEAs can exhibit excellent mechanical properties [ 5 – 10 ], 
superior fatigue behaviors [ 11 – 14 ], outstanding anti-friction and 
anti-wear performances [ 15 – 20 ], high corrosion resistances [ 21 , 22 ], 
and high oxidation resistances, dependent largely on their chemical 
compositions and phase constitutions. Thus, they have shown a 
considerable promise for practical applications in engineering and 
attracted significant scientific interests across the world.
The HEAs have multi-principal elements and normally contain more 
than three primary elements, different from the conventional alloys that 
only consisted of one principal element [ 4 , 8 ]. Due to their high mixing 
entropy, HEAs primarily form thermodynamically stable or metastable 
solid solution structures, such as face-centered cubic (FCC) solid solution 
phase [ 5 – 7 , 9 – 13 ], body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution phase [ 23 ], 
and dual solid solution phases [ 24 , 25 ]. With the aim of improving the 
mechanical behaviors of FCC-phased HEAs, a number of strategies have 
been reported recently. For instance, Wu et al. [ 6 ] applied an asym -
metric cryorolling process to improve the hardness and tensile strength 
of FeCoNiCr HEA, and Tian et al. [ 9 , 11 ] managed to achieve excellent 
mechanical behaviors of CoCrFeNiMn HEA by fabricating 
ultrafine-grained microstructures or C-doping. In contrast, it is much

HEAs on their plasticity and nano-wear properties.
The AlCoCrFeNi HEA is one of the earliest developed systems and has 
garnered extensive attention because of the stellar mechanical and 
tribological performances and good thermal stabilities [ 53 – 58 ]. Ac -
cording to Geng et al. [ 53 ], the hardness only decreases slightly from 
about 5.5 to 3.7 GPa while temperature is increased from RT to 800 
◦

However, considering the high cost of Co element, developing 
cost-effective HEAs without Co has become a popular target. Recently, a 
number of AlCrFeNi based low-cost HEAs have been reported [ 59 – 62 ]. 
Jiang et al. [ 60 ] has fabricated a Co-free AlCrFeNi HEA by three 
different methods and found that all AlCrFeNi specimens displayed a 
high mechanical strength and good plasticity with nearly the same phase 
compositions. The yield strength of AlCrFeNi HEA was more than 1040 
MPa and the failure strain was higher than 28 % in their report. Dong 
et al. [ 63 ] designed the AlCrFe2Ni2 HEA, which demonstrated a yield 
strength of 796 MPa, a maximum strength of 1437 MPa, and a ductility 
of 15.7 %. Its excellent tensile property is due to the BCC phase spinodal 
decomposition and the composite effect of hard BCC and soft FCC 
phases. Jin et al. [ 64 ] developed the CrFeNi2Al HEA, which exhibited a 
maximum mechanical strength of 1357 MPa and a total ductility of 6.4 
% due to its nano-lamellar microstructure. Chen et al. [ 65 ] has per -
formed a comprehensive study on the influence of Al content on 
microstructure and compressive properties of AlCrFeNi HEA. They 
claimed that the HEA became stronger with a higher content of Al 
because of the enhanced fine microstructure and solid solution 
strengthening effects. Collectively, those studies highlight the potential 
of AlCrFeNi HEA system and their specific properties for 
high-performance engineering applications.
By comparison, HEAs with vanadium (V) element have been given 
much less attention even some of them have exhibited excellent me -
chanical or magnetic properties [ 66 – 71 ]. For example, Dong et al. [ 68 ] 
has reported that introducing the V element into AlCoCrFeNi HEA can 
decrease the elements segregation and homogenize its microstructure. 
With increasing the concentration of V element in AlCoCrFeNi HEA, its 
hardness and yield strength were increased but its plastic strain was 
decreased. According to Salishchev et al. [ 69 ], adding V into CoCrFeNi 
and CoCrFeNiMn HEAs promotes the formation of sigma phase within 
the FCC matrix. Furthermore, Yin et al. [ 70 ] studied the influence of V 
on the mechanical properties of FCC phased (CoCrFeMnNi) and BCC 
phased (CrMoNbTaWHfTiZr) HEAs. They stated that V provided a high 
strengthening effect in both FCC and BCC HEAs and they attributed the 
increased strengths to the atomic volume of V which is large in FCC HEA 
while small in BCC HEA and high misfit volume in both two phases. The 
above-mentioned studies have revealed positive effects of V addition on 
the mechanical performances of several HEAs. However, there is very 
limited knowledge and information relevant to the influence of V 
element on the tribological properties of HEAs [ 72 , 73 ], which is actually 
an ongoing challenge regarding the development of high-performance 
novel materials for applications in mechanical contact systems under 
extreme conditions.
