1. Introduction
Cu alloys have been widely employed in the fields of modern power 
industry and aerospace engineering owing to their outstanding 
comprehensive mechanical properties, excellent electrical conductivity, 
and remarkable wear and corrosion resistance [1 – 3] . With the rapid 
development of high-tech and equipment, Cu alloys are increasingly 
used in extreme and complex working conditions, including high tem -
peratures (HTs) and heavy loads (such as various fasteners and sliding 
components in aero-engines, etc.), which puts forward higher re -
quirements for their mechanical properties. Currently, the majority of 
research on copper alloys centers on those with a high copper content. 
Two primary approaches have been employed to enhance their me -

4.2. Effect of element B on tensile properties of the Cu based muti- 
component alloy
The non-metallic B element can enhance the cohesion within the 
grain boundaries and form homo-polar bonds with metal atoms without 
attracting the charge in the metal-metal bonds, thereby increasing the 
strength of the grain boundaries [37,38] . Additionally, the addition of 
boron also has an impact on elastic strain and the distribution of 
geometrically necessary dislocations. Therefore, the element boron has 
been proven to significantly improve the mechanical properties of alloys 
in various alloy systems, such as nickel-based and cobalt-based super- -
alloys as well as high-entropy alloys [39,40] . Furthermore, during the 
HT tensile process, oxygen atoms penetrate along the grain boundaries, 
causing grain boundary oxidation, thereby weakening the strength of 
the grain boundaries, reducing the plasticity, and triggering brittle 
fracture. Due to the small atomic radius of boron, its segregation at the 
grain boundaries can fill the pores in the grain boundaries and block the 
diffusion path of oxygen, thereby improving the HT oxidation resistance 
of the alloys and preventing brittleness caused by HT oxidation of the 
grain boundaries [41] .

5. Conclusions
In this study, a novel Cu-based multi-component alloy with homo -
geneous precipitation strengthening has been successfully developed. 
This alloy exhibits excellent RT and HT strength along with good 
ductility. A comprehensive investigation into the microstructure, 
deformation mechanisms, and tensile properties of the alloy at both RT 
and HT has been conducted. The key findings are summarized as 
follows: 
(1) In the Cu-based multi-principal element alloy, the blocky and
