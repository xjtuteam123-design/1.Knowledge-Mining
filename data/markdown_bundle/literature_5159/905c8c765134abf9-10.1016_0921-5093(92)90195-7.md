Abstract 
The mechanical properties from 300 to 77 K of an Fe- 
28.3Mn-5.38AI-1.04C-0.24Si alloy were studied. It was found 
that this alloy possesses high strength, good ductility and high 
impact toughness. At 77 K, this alloy has 1112 MPa yield stress, 
1536 MPa ultimate tensile strength, 28% total elongation and 
72 J Charpy V-notch impact energy. After aging, the Charpy 
V-notch impact energy at 300 K is much reduced, and also falls 
continuously with decreasing temperature. The impact energy is 
less than 10 J and the fracture surface becomes mainly inter- 
granular at temperatures below 183 K. 
I. Introduction 
Alloys based on the austenitic Fe-Mn-AI-C system 
have shown some promise as engineering alloys for 
their high strength, light weight and low cost [1-4]. 
Aluminium, a ferrite stabilizer, can increase their 
corrosion and oxidation resistance. Manganese is 
added to stabilize the austenitic structure but too much 
of it, i.e. more than 35 wt.%, causes the precipitation of 
brittle fl-Mn phase [5]. Carbon is a potent austenite 
former and strengthener for this alloy system. Wang 
and Beck [6] suggested a composition range of 20-30 
wt.% Mn, less than 12 wt.% A1 and up to 1 wt.% C, 
while Kayak [7] suggested 8-10 wt.% AI and 25-30 
wt.% Mn. In addition, silicon is a potentially useful 
additive to increase the oxidation resistance and 
mechanical strength of the alloys, but it decreases the 
austenite stability [8]. 
Charles et al. [9] studied the effect of carbon on the 
tensile properties of the Fe-30Mn-5A1 alloy system 
with various amounts of carbon and silicon. Carbon 
can significantly increase the yield stress, by about 34 
MPa at 296 K and 67 MPa at 77 K for every 0.1 wt.% 
increase in carbon content. Reverse ductility, i.e. 
increasing elongation with decreasing temperature, was 
found for alloys with carbon contents less than 0.5 
wt.%. For alloys with 0.9 wt.% C, a fairly good elonga- 
tion of 28% and an impressive Charpy V-notch (CVN) 
impact energy density of 140 J cm 2 were still 
obtained at 77 K [91. For every 1 wt.% increase in 
silicon content, the yield stress was found to increase 
by 54 MPa at 296 K and 96 MPa at 77 K for the 
Fe-29Mn-4.5A1-0.3C system [9]. Kim et al. [10] also 
found reverse ductility in Fe-30Mn-5AI-0.3C alloys 
microalloyed with niobium and vanadium and attrib- 
uted it to the formation of mechanical twinning at low 
temperature. Further study by Kim et al. [11] showed 
that, with 3-5 wt.% AI, the elongation at 77 K in- 
creases with increasing aluminium content. 
Recently, the Fe-Mn-A1 alloys containing 28-32 
wt.% Mn, 8-11 wt.% AI and 0.8-1.0 wt.% C also 
received considerable attention [2, 12-19[. Previous 
work [2] showed that the Fe-30Mn-10AI-IC-1Si 
alloy possesses high strength and good ductility from 
77 to 773 K in a tensile test. However, the CVN impact 
energy decreases from 171 J at 298 K to nearly zero at 
77 K, while at 77 K the Fe-30Mn-5A1-0.3C alloy has 
an impressive CVN impact energy [9]. The yield stress 
of Fe-30Mn-5AI-0.3C alloy (855 MPa [10] and 600 
MPa f9]) is much lower than that of the Fe-30Mn- 
10AI-1C-1Si alloy (1323 MPa [2]) at 77 K. Therefore 
a good combination of impact energy and tensile 
strength may be realized for an Fe-30Mn-5AI-IC 
alloy. As a promising start, Charles et al. [9] showed 
that at 77 K the Fe-25Mn-4.7AI-0.9C alloy has 990 
MPa yield stress and 140 J cm 2 CVN impact energy 
density. The purpose of this report is to study in more 
detail the impact and tensile properties from 300 to 
77 K of an alloy near this composition, i.e. 
Fe-28.3Mn-5.38AI- 1.04C-0.24Si. 
Aging of austenitic Fe-Mn-AI-C alloys at tempera- 
tures from 773 to 973 K generally causes the precipita- 
tion of ~c phase, (Fe, Mn)~A1Cx, along the grain 
boundary or in the austenite matrix [20-26], which is 
considered to be the possible hardening mechanism. 
However, aging of an Fe-30Mn-10AI-IC-1Si alloy at 
823 K for 16 h results in brittle fracture before yielding 
at subzero temperatures [2]. Therefore the effect of 
aging on the tensile and impact properties of the 
Fe-28.3Mn-5.38AI-1.04C-0.24Si alloy will also be 
studied.

2. Experimental procedure 
The alloy used in this study was prepared from high 
purity iron, manganese, aluminium, silicon and graph- 
ite by induction melting in an argon atmosphere. The 
chemical composition (in weight per cent) of this alloy 
was 28.3 Mn, 5.38 A1, 1.04 C, 0.24 Si, 0.04 P, 0.011 S 
and the balance iron. It was hot rolled to plate 12 mm 
thick and then solution treated at 1323 K for 75 rain 
and oil quenched. The average grain size, determined 
by the linear interception method, is 41 btm. To evalu- 
ate the grain size effect on CVN impact toughness, a 
coarse grain (135 ~m) structure was obtained by solu- 
tion treatment at 1323 K for 8 h and oil quenched. 
After solution treatment, aging at 823 K for 16 h was 
used to precipitate the 7¢ phase. 
Cylindrical tensile specimens of 25 mm gauge length 
and 5 mm diameter were machined from the plate with 
the specimen axis parallel to the rolling direction. 
Tensile tests were carried out on an Instron 1125 
universal testing machine with a cross-head speed of 
2 mm min-1. Standard CVN impact specimens were

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Solution-treated specimens 
The tensile properties as functions of temperature 
for the alloy are shown in Fig. 1. At 300 K the yield 
and tensile stresses are 527 MPa and 922 MPa respec- 
tively, and the elongation and reduction of area are 
43% and 65% respectively. The yield and tensile 
stresses continue to increase to 1112 MPa and 1536 
MPa respectively, while the elongation and reduction 
of area continue to decrease to 28% and 25% respec- 
tively, as the temperature decreases to 77 K. Com- 
pared with Fe-25Mn-4.7AI-0.9C [9], about a 10% 
increase in both the yield and tensile stresses at both 
300 and 77 K are observed in this alloy, while tensile 
elongation at each temperature is nearly the same for 
both alloys. No necking was observed for this alloy in 
the tensile test at 77 K, and the specimen fails abruptly 
even though the elongation reaches 28%. Transmission 
electron microscopy study of the specimen tensile 
tested at 77 K shows that the dislocations are evenly 
distributed, similar to in the Fe-29.5Mn-10.0AI- 
1.03C-0.86Si alloy [2]. In contrast to the Fe-30Mn- 
5AI-0.3C alloy [10], no mechanical twinning was 
observed.

ture is shown in Fig. 2. For the 41/~m specimen, the

Charles et al. [9] showed that the impact energy at 
77 K is decreased by about 40 J for every 1 wt.% 
increase in silicon. The decrease in silicon con- 
tent from Fe-29.5Mn-10.0AI-I.03C-0.86Si to Fe- 
28.3Mn-5.38AI-1.04C-0.24Si should increase the 
77 K impact energy by about 25 J. Since the small 
change in manganese content has little effect on the 
impact energy [9] and the carbon content is the same, 
the increase in the impact energy at 77 K from 
Fe-29.5Mn-10.0AI-1.03C-0.86Si to Fe-28.3Mn- 
5.38A1-1.04C-0.24Si alloy should be largely due to 
the reduced aluminium content. Charles et al. [9] also 
determined that, for every 1 wt.% increase in silicon 
content, the yield stress increases by about 54 MPa at 
300 K and 96 MPa at 77 K. Therefore the change in 
silicon content from Fe-28.3Mn-5.38Al-l.04C- 
0.24Si to Fe-29.5Mn-10.0AI-1.03C-0.86Si alloy 
should increase the yield stress by about 30 MPa at 
300 K and 60 MPa at 77 K. After subtracting the 
amounts of silicon and neglecting the small manganese 
effect, there are still yield stress increases of 120 MPa 
and 150 MPa at 300 K and 77 K respectively, which 
can be attributed to the increase in aluminium content. 
These changes correspond to increases of about 27 
and 33 MPa in yield stress for every 1 wt.% increase in 
aluminium content in the composition range discussed. 
For tensile-tested specimens, the fracture surface 
showed mainly ductile dimples throughout the temper- 
ature range. For impact-fractured specimens, it was 
observed that the fracture surface consists mainly of 
ductile dimples for test temperatures above 173 K. 
The fracture surface in the centre region of CVN 
specimens fractured at 77 K consists mainly of trans- 
granular cleavage facets joined by ductile dimples, as 
shown in Fig. 3. Similar transgranular cleavage facets 
were observed in Fe-29.5Mn-10.0AI-I.03C-0.86Si 
alloy impact tested at 77 K and were identified as {111} 
planes [2]. However, the fracture mode of the 
Fe-29.5Mn-10.0Al-l.03C-0.86Si alloy at 77 K is a 
mixture of cleavage and intergranular fracture [2], 
which accounts for the very low impact energy.

253 K, the fracture surface consists of intergranular

some ductile dimples. As the temperature decreases, 
the amount of intergranular fracture increases, and the
