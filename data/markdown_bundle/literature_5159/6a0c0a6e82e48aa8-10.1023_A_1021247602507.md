SINGLE-CRYSTAL RHENIUM-BEARING NICKEL ALLOYS
FOR TURBINE BLADES OF GTE
E. N. Kablov, 1 V . N. Toloraiya,1 and N. G . Orekhov1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 7, pp.7–1 1 , July, 2002.
The compositions of high-temperature single-crystal nickel alloys with rhenium additives (more than 6% Re),
which have strength characteristics corresponding to foreign single-crystal alloys of the third generation, are
determined. The attained level of properties justifies the use of the scarce and expensive element. An alloy
with 9% Re belongs to the fourth generation of single-crystal high-temperature alloys where high-temperature
strength and rhenium content are concerned.
INTRODUCTION
Creation of new aircraft gas turbine engines (GTE) re-
quires advances in their operating characteristics, i.e., thrust,
service life, reliability of operation, and efficiency. These
problems are primarily solved by the use of high-temperature
nickel alloys, especially in the hot duct of the engine. Tur-
bine vanes are the most thermally loaded parts of the hot duct
of the engine because they have to operate for tens of thou-
sands of hours in a wide range of temperatures and stresses.
Single crystal nickel superalloys remain virtually indispens-
able materials for GTE vanes of the fifth and sixth genera-
tions despite the development of fiber-reinforced advanced
ceramics. This primarily concerns single crystal alloys with
rhenium and tantalum additives, which have the highest
high-temperature strength of nickel superalloys.

CHOICE OF THE CHEMICAL COMPOSITION
The main difficulty encountered in the alloys with
growth in the content of rhenium is connected with the fact
that topologically close-packed (TCP) phases are segregated
in the structure of rhenium-bearing alloys in the vanes during
service and markedly soften the material. As a rule, such
TCP phases form in dendrite arms and are represented by
flakes positioned in parallel to the faces of the {111} octa -
hedron. Figure 2 presents the structure of [001] single crystal
of alloy ZhS32 (4% Re) after long-term operation in an en -
gine (about 12,000 h). Flake segregations of the rhenium-
bearing TCP phase lie in two mutually perpendicular systems
corresponding to directions /c225110/c241represented by intersec -
tions of planes {111} with the surface of the (001) section.
Such segregations can also form after heat treatment. In addi-
tion, segregation of rhenium-bearing TCP phases may occur
on the boundaries of a high-temperature alloy with a coating.
In this case they may have a cellular morphology. Such
segregations are frequently encountered in alloys of the third
generation.
It should be noted that rhenium itself does not form inter-
mediate phases with nickel. Therefore, TCP phases form in
the presence of elements of group VI in the alloy, tungsten in
the first turn.
Alloys of the second generation with 2 – 3% Re are usu -
ally structurally stable with respect to the segregation of TCP
phases. However, a microscopic x-ray diffraction analysis of
specimens of alloy ZhS36 [001] ruptured in a test for long-
term strength at 1000°C for about 2500 h showed that indi -
vidual thin flakes of a TCP phase enriched with Ni, W, and
Re segregate in the arms of first- and second-order dendrites.
The appearance of these segregations is connected with the
residual dendritic segregation inhomogeneity not removed in
the process of solution annealing. Since the diffusion mobi -
lity of W and Re in a nickel solid solution is low and their
negative segregation coefficients are high ( K
W /c64– 2.7 and
KRe /c64– 4), they concentrate in dendrite arms, and this inho -
mogeneity is hard to remove by heat treatment. For this rea -
son, the choice of heat treatment modes for rhenium-bearing
high-temperature alloys has special importance, because it
strongly determines their structural stability.

Analyzing domestic and foreign rhenium-bearing alloys
ZhS36, ZhS32, CMSX-10, and Rene N6, we established that
the resistance of the structure of alloys of this type to the for-
mation of embrittling TPC phases of the Ni–W–R e system
is determined by the proportion of elements of group VI
(W, Cr, Mo) to rhenium in the alloy. In the first approxima -
tion the structural stability of an alloy can be evaluated in
terms of the sum of the atomic concentrations of W, Mo, Cr
multiplied by the concentration of rhenium, i.e., [W + Mo +
Cr] /c180[Re]. Accordingly, at a constant sum [Re + W] =
12 – 14% stable compositions will lie in the range of low
content of rhenium and high content of tungsten (for exam -
ple, alloy ZhS36 contains 2% Re and 11% W) or high con -
tent of rhenium and low content of tungsten (i.e., 2% W and
10% Re) at a limited concentration of chromium (2 – 2.5%)
and in the absence of molybdenum.
With allowance for all these facts we chose and melted
two groups of experimental compositions with rhenium,
namely, (1 ) alloys with 6% Re and 5% W (CMSX-10) and
(2 ) alloys with 9 – 10% Re and 2% W.
Taking into account that these alloys are to be used for
the production of single-crystal vanes, the high strength cha -
racteristics and structural stability in them have to be com -
bined with good adaptability to single-crystal casting. In par-
ticular, this concerns the susceptibility of the alloy to “stri -
ated” segregation and formation of foreign crystals in the
process of directed crystallization shaping the single-crystal
casting. Therefore, we added 7 – 8% Ta, which has a positive
segregation coefficient and suppresses the processes of “stri-
ated” segregation, into the composition.
MELTING OF BLEND PREFORMS
Test preforms were melted in a vacuum induction fur -
nace by special regimes providing minimum content of nitro-
gen, oxygen, and carbon in the preform. The blend was com-
posed of pure initial materials.
The presence of nitrogen and oxygen admixtures in -
creases the probability of formation of foreign crystals in
shaping of the single crystal structure of the casting. This ex-
plains the stringent requirements on the purity of the initial
blend preforms of alloys for single-crystal casting. For exam-
ple, for alloy CMSX-10 the level of nitrogen and carbon con-
centration should be below 5 – 8 ppm. The same content of
admixtures was provided in melting experimental alloys with
6 and 9% Re.

STUDY OF THE STRUCTURE OF SPECIMENS
IN CAST AND HEAT TREATED STATES
The study of the macrostructure of cast preforms showed
that the single crystals of the tested compositions were
formed under the conditions of high-gradient directed crys -
tallization without the appearance of “striated” segregation
despite the high total content of rhenium and tungsten in the
alloys. Stripped disordering (columnar grains the off-orien-
tation of which increases with the height of the preform)
were also absent. The radial growth of the structure of
T-shaped specimens imitating blade flanges amounted to
about 25 mm, which indicated good adaptability of the alloys
to single-crystal casting.

CONCLUSIONS
1. We have shown the principal possibility of creating
single-crystal carbonless nickel alloys with 9 – 10% rhe -
nium, which should possess a high adaptability to single-
crystal casting.
2. An alloy with 9% Re has properties exceeding those of
third-generation alloys. The attained level of high-tempera -
ture strength justifies the use of an enhanced rhenium content.
3. The phase stability of high-rhenium alloys is ensured
by the limited content of elements of group VI of the Peri -
odic System (Cr, Mo, W) in the composition, which are re -
sponsible for the formation of TCP phases.
4. The high content of refractory elements in the compo-
sition confers resistance of the alloys to volume recrystalliza-
tion in long-term high-temperature homogenizing annealing.
