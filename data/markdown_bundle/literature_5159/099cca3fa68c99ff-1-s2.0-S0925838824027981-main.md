ABSTRACT
Molybdenum-rhenium alloys has a low recrystallization temperature and insufficient high temperature strength, 
making it unable to serve as structural parts for a long time in high temperature environments above 1300 ℃ for 
a long time. ZrO
2 
dispersion strengthened Mo-Re alloy was prepared by liquid-liquid doping combined with 
hydrogen reduction. The morphology and distribution of ZrO
2 
were adjusted by liquid-liquid doping-copreci -
pitation-codeposition technology, with ZrO
2 
dispersed in the grain of Mo-Re alloy to refine the grain. The 
average grain size is reduced from 14.99 μ m to 8.41 μ m, and the yield strength is 539 MPa, representing an 
increase of 16.2 %. When the strain is 43 %, the high temperature compression performance at 1400 ℃ reaches 
214 MPa, which is 28.1 % higher than that of Mo-Re. The enhanced performance is attributed to the dislocation 
pinning of ZrO
2 
and the formation of a semi-conformal interface between ZrO
2 
and the matrix, which addresses 
the issue of weak interfacial bonding to reinforce Mo-Re alloys. It provides a new idea for the design of Mo-Re 
alloys structural components such as space fission reactors fuel cladding in ultra-high temperature environment.
1. Introduction
Molybdenum-rhenium (Mo-Re) alloys have excellent toughness, 
ductility and a low ductile-brittle transition temperature, and is widely 
used in aerospace, medical equipment, national defense industry and 
other fields [1 – 3] . It exhibits good compatibility with commonly used 
nuclear fuels, such as UN, UC, UO
2
, etc., at high temperatures which can 
reduce the occurrence of critical drop safety accidents in the launch 
phase of reactors, has been identified as a promising candidate for the 
structure material of space fission reactors [4,5] . However, the insuffi -
cient strength of Mo-Re alloys at high temperatures limits their appli -
cation in extremely high temperature environments [6] .
The introduction of second phase particles has been demonstrated to 
enhance the mechanical properties of Mo and its alloys [7 – 9] . The 
second phase particles such as ZrO
2 
can effectively refine Mo grains to 
improve the strength and ductility of the material and decrease the 
ductile-brittle transition temperature [10,11] . Xu et al. [12] prepared 
Mo alloys doped with different contents of ZrO
2 
by hydrothermal syn -
thesis and coprecipitation. The compressive strength of the Mo-2.0 % 
ZrO
2 
alloy at room temperature reached 674 MPa under stress relief 
annealing, which represents a 65.6 % increase in comparison to the 
strength of pure Mo. Jing et al. [13] obtained the unprecedented high 
temperature strength by doping nano-ZrC into Mo alloy by solid-solid 
method, which was as high as 483 MPa at 1200 
◦
C. The strengthening 
mechanism is that ZrC absorbs oxygen at the grain boundaries at high 
temperatures and finally generates ZrO
2
. Therefore, the choice of ZrO
2 
doping to strengthen Mo-Re alloys is an effective way to enhance the 
high temperature strength.

The modulation of oxide particle size and distribution is of critical 
importance in the mechanical properties of alloys [14] . The coarse oxide 
particles predominantly accumulate at the Mo grain boundary, leading 
to stress concentration due to the inconsistency with the Mo matrix 
deformation, thereby serving as the source of cracks [15,16] . The pur -
pose of reinforcement can only be achieved when fine secondary par -
ticles are uniformly dispersed within the grain. Liu et al. [17] prepared 
nanostructured La
2
O
3
-doped molybdenum alloys by liquid-liquid 
doping technique, and the yield strength and elongation were as high 
as 813 MPa and 37.5 %, respectively. Therefore, in-situ oxides have an 
irreplaceable position in strengthening Mo alloys.
The ZrO
2 
doped Mo-Re alloys in this study were prepared through 
liquid-liquid doping and powder metallurgy techniques. The effects of 
ZrO
2 
doping on Mo-Re alloy powder, room temperature and high tem -
perature compression structure and mechanical properties were 
analyzed. The interfacial relationship between the second phase parti -
cles and the matrix was thoroughly investigated, and the strengthening 
and toughening mechanism of Mo-Re enhanced by ZrO
2 
was explored.

4. Conclusion
The nano-sized Mo-Re composite powders and Mo-Re alloys with 
excellent properties were prepared by liquid phase doping combined 
with pressure less sintering. The effects of ZrO
2 
on the microstructure, 
phase interface and room temperature and high temperature compres -
sive properties of Mo-Re alloy were studied. The main conclusions are as 
follows:
(1) The ZrO
2 
particles prepared by liquid-liquid doping significantly 
refine the Mo-Re powder and improve the sintering density. In 
the process of H
2 
reduction, ZrO
2 
particles are attached to the gap 
and surface of Mo-Re particles, which reduces the growth rate 
and refines the powder.
(2) ZrO
2 
particles are uniformly dispersed in the grains of Mo-Re 
alloy, which hinders the migration of grain boundaries. The 
grain size of the prepared Mo-Re-ZrO
2 
alloy is refined from 14.9 
μ m to 8.3 μ m.
(3) ZrO
2 
particles and the matrix form a semi-coherent interface 
reinforced phase boundary. The yield strength of Mo-Re-ZrO
2 
alloy at room temperature is 539 MPa, which is 16.2 % higher 
than that of Mo-Re alloy. At 1400 ℃ , the peak stress of high 
temperature compression reaches 214 MPa, which is 28.1 % 
higher than that of pure Mo-Re.
(4) Mo-Re-ZrO
2 
alloy has higher a higher recrystallization tempera -
ture. ZrO
2 
inhibits the dynamic recrystallization of Mo-Re alloy 
by hindering dislocation movement and increasing dislocation 
density.
