Abstract: The vacancy effects on the site preferences of allo ying elements Re, Mo, 
Ta and Cr at γ/γ' interface are studied using first-principles dens ity functional theory. 
The alloying strengthening mechanism mediated by va cancy is also evaluated via 
calculating the Griffith work of fracture for γ/γ' interface as well as the energy barrier 
of vacancy migration at the interface. The calculat ed results show that a Ni vacancy 
on (001) γ' plane does not change the most preferable substit ution position of the 
alloying elements at corner-point Ni site on (001) γ except Cr. These alloying elements 
in γ' enhance the interfacial bonding strength of γ/γ' interface even with the presence 
of a neighboring vacancy, among which Re exhibits the best strengthening effect. The 
improvement in the γ/γ' interfacial strength is ascribed to the increased chemical 
bonding strength between the alloying atom and the nearest-neighbor host atom. 
Furthermore, a single Ni vacancy has higher migrati on energy barriers around the 
alloying solutes especially Re solute substituting on (001) γ at γ/γ' interface, although 
the alloying solutes at these preferable sites have no contribution to the interfacial 
bonding strength. 
Keywords: Ni-based superalloys; Interface; Vacancies; First- principles calculation; 
Strengthening mechanism.

1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys have been desig ned for application as turbine 
blades in advanced aero-engines, due to their excellent high-temperature strengths and 
creep resistances. Besides the anomalous yield behavior of 
γ'-Ni 3Al (L1 2) precipitates, 
the coherent γ-Ni/ γ'-Ni 3Al interfaces also make crucial contributions to creep strength, 
which could hinder the γ matrix dislocations from cutting into γ' precipitates [1, 2]. 
With the improvements in engine efficiency and service temperature, new generations 
of Ni-based single-crystal superalloys with higher γ' precipitate volume fraction (70 
vol. %) and with many kinds of alloying elements ha ve been developed [3]. The 
narrow γ matrix channel with only several dozens of nanomet ers width makes γ/γ' 
interface play more essential role during the creep deformation process of superalloys 
[4]. 
As a common point defect in metals, vacancy inevita bly exists in Ni-based 
superalloys, especially in the non-stoichiometric N i 
3Al phase with alloying atoms [5, 
6]. More importantly, the dislocation climb process at γ/γ' interface during creep 
deformation relies on the diffusion of vacancy in γ channels, which is absorbed or 
emitted by dislocation cores [7]. Some studies have postulated that the single atom or 
short-range ordering chain of Re might provide resi stance to the localized mass 
transport needed for dislocation climb at γ/γ' interface, due to the high solute-vacancy 
exchange energy and the slow diffusion of Re in γ matrix [8, 9]. Therefore, 
understanding the interaction between vacancy and a lloying solute at γ/γ' interface is

2.2 Total energy calculation 
In the present study, the total-energy calculations within the framework of density 
functional theory (DFT) are performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package 
(V ASP) [19, 20]. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [ 21] exchange-correlation 
functionals within generalized gradient approximati on (GGA) are used. Projector 
augmented wave (PAW) [22] pseudopotentials with a p lane-wave cutoff energy of 
480eV and optimized 8 
×8×2 Monkhorst-Pack k-point meshes [23] are employed in 
both interface models and surface models. The self- consistent convergence for total 
energy is set to be 10 -5 eV/atom and spin-polarization is considered in all the 
calculations due to the ferromagnetism of γ-Ni at 0K. The climbing image elastic 
band (CI-NEB) method [24] is adopted to obtain the energy barrier of vacancy 
migration at interface. Four images between initial and final configurations along 
migration path are used and relaxed until the maxim um residual force is less than 
0.05eV/Å, which has been confirmed to be proper to obtain reliable results.

4. Conclusion 
The influences of vacancy on the site preferences a nd strengthening effects of 
alloying elements (Re, Mo, Ta, Cr) on the 
γ/γ' interface in Ni-based single crystal 
superalloys have been systematically studied by fir st-principles calculations. All the 
alloying elements in the present work exhibit prefe rences to the corner-point Ni site 
on (001) γ plane at perfect interface. The existence of a Ni vacancy on (001) γ' plane 
does not change the most preferable substitution si tes of the alloying elements except 
Cr. The alloying at Al site in γ' enhances the interfacial bonding strength of γ/γ'
