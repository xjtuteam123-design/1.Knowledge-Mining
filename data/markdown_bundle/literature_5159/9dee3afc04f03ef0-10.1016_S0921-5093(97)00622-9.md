Abstract
An investigation was made on the homogenization of microstructures and improvement of mechanical properties in
intermetallic TiAl binary, ternary and quaternary alloys and their composites produced by the rheocasting in which the solidifying
alloy was vigorously agitated at rotation speeds of 15–70 s
1 (900–4200 rpm) by a stirring rod immersed in the alloy in an argon
gas atmosphere. In the microstructures of rheocast Ti–44at.%Al alloy, such a lamellar structure in the alloy cast without stirring
was disappeared completely and an extremely reﬁned microstructure was formed. The crystal grain size of the rheocast
Ti–44at.%Al alloy was 2 mm. The room temperature elongation of rheocast Ti–44%Al alloy exceeded 3%. The tensile strength
of the alloy rheocast at 70 s
1 and was 538 MPa at 1173 K and 439 MPa at 1273 K, respectively. Zirconium-rich lamellar grains
and titanium-rich and carbon-rich precipitates, which were formed in the lamellar grains in a rheocast Ti–44at%Al–5vol.%ZrC
alloy composite, were increased in a rheocast Ti–44%Al–10%ZrC alloy composite. Titanium-rich and carbon-rich precipitates,
which were formed in the lamellar grains in a rheocast Ti–44%Al–10%TiC alloy composite, were increased in a rheocast
Ti–46%Al–10%TiC alloy composite, and were decreased in a rheocast Ti–49%Al–10%TiC alloy composite. The elongation at
room temperature and the tensile strength at 1373 K were 4% and 280 MPa, respectively, in a Ti–44at.%Al–10vol.%ZrC alloy
composite. © 1997 Elsevier Science S.A.

1. Introduction
The rheocasting process in which the homogenization
of microstructures in the whole parts of an alloy ingot
can be achieved by the rotational agitation during the
solidiﬁcation of the alloy, has not yet been applied to
titanium aluminides, although the rheocasting tech-
nique must be one of promising processes for industrial
use of intermetallic compounds [1].
The rheocasting process was initially proposed by
Flemings and coworkers [2]. The main objective of the
process was the homogenization of macrostructures
and the elimination of segregation of an alloy ingot.
They succeeded in the control of macrostructures and
the decrease in macrosegregation for the complete in-
got, although irregular crystal grains such as rosette-
type primary crystals were seen in the microstructures
because of insufﬁcient agitation during the solidiﬁcation
[2].
Ichikawa and coworkers tried further homogeneous
reﬁnement of crystal grains [3–12] and produced highly
ductile materials [4–11] by increasing both the rotation
speed of a stirrer and the cooling rate of the semi-solid
alloy in the rheocasting process.
The National R&D Program named ‘High-Perfor-
mance Materials for Severe Environments’ has been
performed in Japan in order to develop structural inter-
metallic compounds such as titanium aluminide and
niobium aluminide by improving the ductility at room
temperature and the strength at elevated temperatures.
In the present work, the homogeneous reﬁnement of
microstructures and the improvement of elongation
at room temperature and tensile strength at high
temperature in the intermetallic TiAl alloys and their
composites were investigated by a modiﬁed rheocasting
at high rotation speeds, so that remarkable grain re-
ﬁnement and superior mechanical properties were
achieved in Ti–44at%Al and Ti–44at.%Al–
10vol.%ZrC alloys.

chamber was evacuated to 1 10
3 MPa or below.
Then, argon gas was introduced therein until the argon
gas pressure in the chamber reached the desired level of
0.1 MPa. The raw materials except the ceramic particles

At this point, the crucible cover above the high-fre-

mechanism was actuated to raise the crucible contain-
ing the mixture charge made of molten titanium alu-

furnace where the stirrer was put into the mixture

point of 10 mm above the inner bottom ﬂoor of the
crucible. The mixture charge was continuously cooled
under the condition of no heating of the isothermal
furnace to achieve the maximum cooling rate of the
charge.
Then, the stirrer began to rotate immediately after
the insertion of it into the molten alloy of the charge,
continued to rotate for the maximum period of 30 s,
and was removed from the semi-solid alloy. The rotat-
ing speed of the stirrer was a relatively low speed of 15
s
1 until the start of solidiﬁcation, and was increased
not rapidly but at a steady rate to prevent the spatter of
the solidifying charge to the outside of the crucible. By
continuing agitation at a constant high speed, the pri-
mary crystals formed in the molten charge were broken
to realize a homogeneous ﬁne-grained microstructure.
The constant rotation speed of the stirrer during so-
lidiﬁcation varied from 15 to 70 s
1.
After continuing the constant-speed agitation for a
given period of time, the elevating mechanism was
actuated to lower the crucible into the high-frequency
furnace at a point between the fraction of solid of 0.5
and the completion of solidiﬁcation, thereby preventing
the deposition of the semi-solid charge on the stirrer.
Microscopic observation was then made of the top,
middle and bottom sections cut transversely from the
alloy ingots rheocast and solidiﬁed without stirring.

3.2. Microanalysis
In order to compare the distribution of titanium and
aluminum elements of the rheocast intermetallic TiAl
alloy with that of the alloy solidiﬁed without stirring, a
microanalysis was made on the transverse sections at
the top, middle and bottom parts of the charge by a
SEM. As the secondary electron image and X-ray im-
ages were measured, using an EPMA beam of 50 nm in
diameter, in the middle section of a Ti–44%Al alloy
ingot solidiﬁed without stirring, lamellar segregation of
aluminum and titanium elements was observed to be
consistent with such a lamellar structure [13].
On the other hand, as the secondary electron image
and X-ray images were measured, using an EPMA
beam of 50 nm in diameter, in the middle section of a
Ti–44%Al alloy ingot rheocast at a rotation speed of 15
s
1, lamellar segregation of aluminum and titanium
elements was observed.
When the secondary electron image and X-ray im-
ages were measured, using an EPMA beam of 50 nm in
diameter, in the middle section of a Ti–44%Al alloy
ingot rheocast at a rotation speed of 70 s
1, a uniform
distribution of aluminum and titanium elements in the
rheocast alloy could be observed with destruction of the
lamellar segregation seen in the alloy solidiﬁed without
stirring [13].
Next, in order to examine the distribution of tita-
nium, aluminum, zirconium and carbon elements of the

4. Conclusions
In order to produce structural titanium aluminides
and composites with high ductility at room temperature
and high strength at elevated temperature, an investiga-
tion was made on the homogeneous reﬁnement of
microstructures and improvement of mechanical prop-
erties by rotational agitation during the solidiﬁcation of
intermetallic TiAl binary, ternary and quaternary alloys
and their composites. The tensile testing at room and
elevated temperatures was made on the intermetallic
TiAl alloys and composites rheocast and solidiﬁed with-
out stirring.
(1) In the microstructure of the rheocast Ti–
44at.%Al alloy such a lamellar structure as seen in the
alloy solidiﬁed without stirring disappeared completely,
and an extremely reﬁned microstructure composed of
dispersed agglomeration aggregates was formed. The
average size of agglomeration aggregates and individual
crystal grains of the rheocast Ti–44%Al alloy were 6
and 2 mm, respectively.
(2) Zirconium carbide particles were seen to be dis-
tributed homogeneously in the lamellar grains in the
microstructure of a rheocast Ti–44at.%Al–2vol.%ZrC
alloy. Zirconium carbide particles were decomposed,
zirconium-rich lamellar grains and titanium-rich and
carbon-rich precipitates were formed in the lamellar
grains in the microstructure of a rheocast Ti–
44at.%Al–5vol.%ZrC alloy composite. These precipi-
tates were increased with increased zirconium carbide
content from 5 to 10 vol.% in a rheocast Ti–44at.%Al–
10vol.%ZrC alloy composite. Titanium-rich and car-
bon-rich precipitates, which were formed in the lamellar
grains, were increased with increase in aluminum con-
tent from 44 to 46 at.% in the rheocast Ti–46%Al–
10%TiC alloy composite, and were decreased with
increase in aluminum content from 46 to 49 at.% in the
rheocast Ti–49%Al- 10%TiC alloy composite.
(3) It was conﬁrmed in the microanalysis that the
zirconium carbide particles were decomposed with
chemical reaction during the melting and solidiﬁcation
of the alloy and zirconium-rich lamellar grains and
titanium-rich and carbon-rich precipitates were formed
in the comparatively small lamellar grains in the Ti–
44at.%Al–5vol.%ZrC alloy composite rheocast at a
rotation speed of 43 s
1.
(4) It was found that the Ti–44%Al alloy rheocast at
a rotation speed of 70 s 1 had the superior combined
particle-dispersion-strengthened Ti–44%Al alloy com-
posite ingot.
