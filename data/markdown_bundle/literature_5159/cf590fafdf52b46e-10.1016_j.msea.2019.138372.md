ABSTRACT
We engineered an Al 0.3CoCrNi medium-entropy alloy with heterogeneous grain structure and nanoscale pre-
cipitation through thermo-mechanical processing route, with an aim to achieve a remarkable combination of
cryogenic yield strength and ductility. The alloy exhibited an exceptional combination of high cryogenic yield
strength of ∼1.1 GPa and high uniform elongation of ∼37%.1. Introduction
In recent years, alloys that are designed based on multi-principle
elements have attracted wide research interest due to their unique alloy
design concept and remarkable properties [ 1,2]. These multi-principle
elemental alloys (MPEAs) are also widely known as high-entropy alloys
(HEAs), medium-entropy alloys (MEAs), compositionally complex al-
loys (CCAs), or baseless alloys [ 2]. These MPEAs exhibit several su-
perior properties that are rarely demonstrated in conventional alloys
(alloys that are designed based on one principle element with various
alloying elements), such as high strength-high ductility and superior
fracture toughness at cryogenic temperature [ 1–4]. The MPEAs with a
single-phase face-centered-cubic (FCC) structure and low stacking fault
energies (SFEs) have been studied extensively due to their outstanding
mechanical properties at cryogenic temperature, and consequently,
MPEAs are demonstrated to be potential candidates for promising
cryogenic applications [ 3,5–7].
Recently, there are several studies which report the enhancement of
cryogenic yield strength of single-phase FCC alloys by utilizing grain
reﬁnement, heterogeneous grain structure, solid solution strengthening,
and precipitation strengthening [ 5,8–11]. However, the synergetic ef-
fect of grain re ﬁnement, heterogeneous microstructure, and precipita-
tion strengthening on the cryogenic tensile properties has never been
reported so far. Consequently, in the present study, we fabricated an
Al
0.3CoCrNi MEA that consists of heterogeneous microstructure and B2
precipitates through a combination of cold rolling and annealing. Fi-
nally, the microstructure and cryogenic tensile properties of the de-
signed alloy are systematically investigated.
2. Materials and methods
The alloy ingot with a nominal composition of Al
0.3CoCrNi (in mole
fraction) was produced using pure elements of Al, Co, Cr, and Ni (purity
greater than 99.9%) in a vacuum induction melting furnace under an
argon atmosphere. The as-cast ingot was subjected to homogenization
at 1200 °C for 6 h in an argon atmosphere, followed by water
quenching. The purity of argon gas utilized in the present study was
99.999%. The homogenized sample was subjected to cold rolling
through multi-pass rolling with a thickness reduction of 78% (from 7 to
1.5 mm). The cold-rolled plate was recrystallized at 900 °C for 15 min,
followed by water quenching.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1a shows the equilibrium phase diagram of CoCrNi MEA with
varying Al content and Fig. 1b shows the mole fractions of equilibrium
phases in the temperature range of 600 to 1800 °C. The equilibrium
phase diagrams are calculated by Thermo-Calc software with a
TCFE2000 database and its upgraded version [ 12]. The rationale for
selecting the composition with Al content of 0.3-mol fraction is based
on the homogenization (desired equilibrium phase: single FCC phase)
and recrystallization heat treatments (desired equilibrium phase: FCC
and B2). Based on Fig. 1a, decreasing the Al content less than 0.3 would
result in a single FCC phase during recrystallization, while increasing
the Al content will pose problems in getting single phase during
homogenization. In general, the homogenization is carried out at a
temperature where the alloy exhibits a single FCC phase so that the
crack formation by hard secondary phases can be prevented during cold
rolling [ 13]. The recrystallization temperature of 900 °C was chosen
such that the FCC and B2 phases are formed ( Fig. 1a), and it is annealed
for a short duration of 15 min to get heterogeneous microstructure with
ﬁne and coarse recrystallized grains [ 14]. The XRD pattern ( Fig. 1c)
shows the presence of di ﬀraction peaks corresponding to the FCC phase
and B2 phase in the recrystallized sample, illustrating a good agreement
with the equilibrium phase diagram ( Fig. 1 b). The backscattered elec-
tron (BSE) images ( Fig. 1 d and e) and EBSD-inverse pole ﬁgure (IPF)
map images ( Fig. 1f and g) reveal that the recrystallized FCC phase is
composed of ﬁne grains ( ∼2 μm) and coarse grains ( ∼10 μm), whereas
the B2 phase is seen predominantly precipitated along the grain
boundaries of ﬁne grains and within the grains of coarse grains
(Fig. 1e). The B2 precipitates that are formed along the grain bound-
aries are relatively coarser (200 nm– 500 nm) than the B2 precipitates
(< 100 nm) that are formed within the grains. The BSE-EDS mapping
(Fig. 1h) shows that the B2 precipitates are rich in Al –Ni.
The TEM characterization ( Fig. 2 ) further con ﬁrms the presence of
dual phase microstructure in the recrystallized sample. Fig. 2a shows
the bright ﬁeld TEM (BF-TEM) image illustrating the distribution of ﬁne
B2 precipitates within the FCC grains. The SAED patterns shown in

the corresponding SAED pattern of B2 precipitate. Fig. 2d shows the

With the above presented results, we have clearly established that
the microstructure with heterogeneous grain structure and B2 pre-
cipitates can be fabricated by simple thermo-mechanical processing
route. In several alloys, the precipitation of the second phase during
recrystallization of cold rolled material is reported to produce hetero-
geneous microstructure due to heterogeneous precipitation behavior
[14–16]. During cold rolling, the severity of deformation of each grain
varies depending on their grain orientation. Due to this di ﬀerence in the
degree of deformation among di ﬀerent grains, the precipitation be-
comes heterogeneous during annealing of cold-rolled samples [ 15].
During annealing process, the formation of precipitates along the grain
boundaries of recrystallized grains prevents the grain growth by pin-
ning, whereas the absence of precipitation in the recrystallized regions
leads to grain growth [ 17]. Consequently, the recrystallized sample
resulted in the heterogeneous microstructure with B2 precipitates in
Al
0.3CoCrNi MEA.
Fig. 3a shows the cryogenic tensile curves of recrystallized
Al0.3CoCrNi MEA. The alloy exhibits exceptional cryogenic tensile
properties with a high yield strength of ∼1116 MPa, high tensile
strength of ∼1536 MPa, high uniform elongation of ∼37%, and high
ductility of ∼59%. Fig. 3b shows the work hardening behavior as a
function of true strain, illustrating three stages of work hardening rate.
Stage A and Stage B are generally attributed to the dislocation-slip-
mediated deformation process, while stage C where the slop of work
hardening rate decreases can be attributed to the deformation-twin
mediated deformation process [ 18]. The decrease in the slope of the
work hardening rate in stage C could be one of the possible reasons for
enhanced ductility in the present alloy. To highlight the remarkable
combination of cryogenic yield strength and ductility, the cryogenic
tensile properties of the present alloy is compared with the other re-
ported MPEAs ( Fig. 3 c) [ 3,8–10,19–25]. The combination of yield
strength and the uniform elongation achieved in the present alloy at
cryogenic temperature is superior to most of the reported MPEAs, and
on par with ultra- ﬁne grained CoCrNi –W MEA ( Fig. 3c).
Fig. 4 shows the microstructural characterization of the deformed
sample. Fig. 4a shows the fracture surface with numerous ﬁne dimples
and without a macroscopic necking (inset in Fig. 4 a), indicating ex-
cellent plastic stability and ductile nature of the present alloy. The
EBSD-IPF map ( Fig. 4b) reveals the formation of ﬁne deformation twins
(indicated by yellow arrow) in coarse grains. The kernel average mis-
orientation (KAM) map ( Fig. 4c) shows the heterogeneous strain dis-
tributions in coarse and ﬁne grains. The KAM was calculated up to ﬁfth
nearest neighbor with a maximum misorientation angle of 5° [ 9]. The
BF-TEM shows the formation of deformation twins, with the twin width
less than 50 nm ( Fig. 4d). The corresponding DF-TEM and SAED pattern
are shown in Fig. 4e. The presence of high density of dislocations near
deformation twins and B2 precipitates can be seen in the BF-TEM
images ( Fig. 4d and f).
The high cryogenic yield strength achieved in the present study can
be primarily attributed to the synergetic strengthening mechanism from
the presence of ﬁne grains, B2 precipitates, heterogeneous grain
structure, and the formation of nano-scale deformation twins. Based on
the Hall-Petch strengthening e ﬀect, ﬁne grains can e ﬀectively enhance
the strength of the material as the dislocation mean free path decreases
with grain re ﬁnement
[ 26]. In a single-phase CoCrNi MEA processed by
severe plastic deformation, an enhancement in yield strength from
795 MPa to 1363 MPa has been reported by decreasing the grain size
from ∼20 μmt o ∼2 μm[ 5]. The presence of ﬁne B2 precipitates can
act as an e ﬀective barrier to dislocation motion, and enhance the
strength [ 27–30]. It has been shown that the B2 precipitates are not

Recently, several studies on heterogeneous microstructure con-
tained MPEAs have been investigated at room temperature [ 11,15,31].
In most of these studies, a remarkable combination of strength and
ductility has been reported due to the generation of geometrical ne-
cessity dislocations to maintain the strain incompatibility in hetero-
geneous microstructure, leading to back stress strengthening [ 15]. High
magnitude of back stress close to yield strength has been reported in
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic HEA, demonstrating an enhanced strengthening
by heterogeneous microstructure [ 32]. In the present study, the pre-
sence of recrystallized ﬁne and coarse grains can lead to strain het-
erogeneity (Fig. 4c) due to the di ﬀerence in their deformation behavior,
resulting in back stress strengthening. Also, the presence of hard sec-
ondary phase can also contribute to the heterogeneous strain distribu-
tion [ 33]. A detailed study on back stress strengthening needs further
investigation, and it will be discussed in our future work. The high
uniform elongation can be explained based on the formation of nano-
scale deformation twins, and deformation behavior of ﬁne and coarse
grains. In materials with low stacking fault energy, the formation of ﬁne
deformation twins are easily promoted at cryogenic deformation [ 34].
These nano-scale deformation twins can e ﬀectively enhance the strain
hardening ability of the material by accommodating more dislocations
(Fig. 4d), and thereby, enhancing the ductility [ 18,35,36]. The forma-
tion of nano-scale deformation twins would also contribute to the
strengthening through dynamic Hall-Petch e ﬀect [ 36,37]. Thus,
deformation twins can play an e ﬀective role in enhancing both strength
and ductility. Like deformation twins, the presence of ﬁne and coarse
grains can be used e ﬀectively to enhance both strength and ductility. In
addition to the strengthening e ﬀect by back stress generated in het-
erogeneous microstructure, back stress is also reported to enhance the
strain hardening ability, and thereby, ductility [ 11,15]. Consequently,
the present alloy with heterogeneous microstructure and B2 pre-
cipitates shows a remarkable combination of cryogenic yield strength
and uniform elongation.
4. Conclusions
In summary, we engineered Al
0.3CoCrNi MEA with bimodal mi-
crostructure and B2 precipitates through thermodynamic calculations
and thermo-mechanical processing route for cryogenic applications.
The cryogenic tensile behavior showed a remarkable combination of
yield strength ( ∼1.1 GPa) and uniform elongation ( ∼37%), superior to
most of the reported MPEAs. The post-deformation microstructural
characterization revealed the formation of nano-scale deformation
twins, heterogeneous strain distribution, dislocation interactions with
B2 precipitates and deformation twins, and numerous dimple structure,
leading to enhanced cryogenic tensile properties.
