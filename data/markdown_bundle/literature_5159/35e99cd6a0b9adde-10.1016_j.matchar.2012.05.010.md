The 3D reticulated Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foams with 34 –36 wt.% Cr and 20 –24 wt.% Fe were
synthesized by co-deposition of chromium and iron onto open –cell nickel foams, and then
were homogenized to remove concentration gradients. Emphasis was placed on the
research on the oxidation resistance behavior of newly developed Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foams
with changing alloy composition, and the results were compared with those of Ni-25Cr and
bare nickel foams at 800 –1000 °C. The room and high-temperature compression behavior of
Ni–Cr–Fe alloy foams was also examined, respectively. The results show that the Ni –36Cr–
24Fe alloy foam exhibits the best oxidation resistance among the Ni –34Cr–20Fe, Ni –35Cr–
22Fe, Ni –25Cr and bare nickel foams after oxidation at 1000 °C for 120 h. As the Cr and Fe
contents increase, the yield stress and the plateau stress of the Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foams
increase during the room and high-temperature compression tests. Whereas the
compressive strength of the Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foams decreases with the increasing
temperature at the same strain rate.

1. Introduction
Porous metallic materials exhibit many novel properties and
are being mass produced for a multitude of applications at
ambient temperature [1,2]. The nickel-base alloy foams
combined with the high melting point and excellent oxidation
resistance are especially attractive candidates for high-
temperature catalyst substrate, filter and heat exchangers
due to their large specific surface area, low specific weight and
high permeability [3,4]. Recently, nickel-base alloy foams with
3D open-cell network structure have gained more interest on
the foams' properties at high temperature, due to the large
potential application in the burning chamber of gas and steam
turbines and heat shielding for aircraft exhaust [5]. However,
only very limited publications are available on the oxidation
behavior and the high temperature mechanical properties of
open-cell nickel-base alloy foams.
To date, different processes are developed and new high-
temperature resistant nickel-base alloy foams are now being
produced. Queheillalt et al. [6] produced open-cell foams with
Inconel 625 composition by electron beam directed vapor
deposition (with relative densities of <3%), but no oxidation
behavior were investigated. Chyrkin et al. [7] prepared Inconel
625 metal foams by the slip-reaction-foam-sinter-process and
studied the oxidation limited lifetime of the foams in the
temperature range 700–900 °C, which exhibited the accelerated
oxidation behavior after a certain time. Walther et al. [8]
reported the open-cell FeNiCrAl alloy foam by powder metal-
lurgical process. The alloy foam was superior to Inconel 625 in
terms of oxidation resistance and the foam strength after
oxidation was only slightly influenced by temperatures up to
900 °C. Choe and Dunand [9,10] demonstrated that pack
cementation was effective processes to convert 3D open-cell
nickel foams completely into Ni –Cr and Ni –Cr–Al superalloy
foams. The Ni –27.2Cr foam forming a Cr

Despite the quality improvement over the past several
years, the resulting nickel-base alloy foams still suffer from

still a challenging problem on the development of 3D open-
cell nickel-base foams with excellent oxidation resistance and

In the present work, we investigate the feasibility of
synthesis the Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foams based on the composition
of Inconel 690 (Ni: 60 wt.%, Cr: 30 wt.%, Fe: 9.5 wt.%, C:
0.03 wt.%) by co-deposition of Cr and Fe onto open-cell nickel
foams. As a high-performance nickel based alloy, Inconel
alloy 690 has widely used in corrosive and high-temperature
environments [11]. Considering the specificity of the porous
metallic materials, some of the alloying elements are
increased and modified to develop a new kind of Ni –Cr–Fe
single-phase austenitic, oxidation-resistant alloy foam. The
Cr content is increased to enhance the high-temperature
oxidation resistance, and the Fe content is also increased to
raise the melting point. The oxidation behaviors of the Ni –Cr–
Fe alloy foams with different alloy compositions are com-
pared during the high temperature exposure at 800 –1000 °C.
The compressive strengths of Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foams are also
examined at room and high temperature (900 and 1000 °C).

2.1. Synthesis of Open-cell Ni –Cr–Fe Foams
The pure Ni foam (> 99.0% purity) with 20ppi (pores per linear
inch) was supplied by Inco TM and produced by electro-
deposition on a polymer pre-form. The pure Ni foam exhibits
a reticulated structure consisting of hollow struts of wall
thickness of 85 μm, a cell size of 1.3 mm and a relative density
of 3%.
Pack powder mixtures were prepared by accurately weighing
out and thoroughly mixing appropriate amounts of powders of

3.2. Oxidation Behavior of Open-cell Ni –Cr–Fe Alloy Foams
Due to the specific surface area of metallic foams cannot be
defined accurately, the gravimetrical data in mg cm − 2 versus
time do not yield reliable information on the time dependence
of oxide scale formation [7]. A suitable way to present the
oxidation data is to consider mass gain kinetics during the
oxidation process.Fig. 4 illustrates the mass gain kinetics of the
bare nickel, Ni–25Cr and Ni–Cr–Fe foams at 800, 900 and 1000 °C,
respectively. The pure nickel foam exhibits an accelerated mass
gain with almost linear growth kinetics at the beginning, and
then the foam is almost completely oxidized and becomes
brittle, whether at 800, 900 or 1000 °C in Fig. 4. It is because that
porous NiO surface layer, foamed in the air atmosphere, can not
act as an effective barrier to the oxygen transport [16].
As comparison to pure nickel foam, the Ni –Cr–Fe alloy
foams with 34 –36 wt.% Cr and 20 –24 wt.% Fe exhibit excellent
oxidation resistance at 800 °C for 120 h in Fig. 4 (a), and the
mass gains are less than 0.0083 in Table 2. Especially the mass
gains ( K
i) of the Ni –36Cr–24Fe alloy foams are merely 0.006
after oxidation, which are reduced by 18% as compared to that
of the Ni –25Cr alloy foams. It is because that there is a large
amount of Cr in the new alloy foam and Cr can act as an
oxygen getter during the oxidation process. Simultaneously,
among chromia-formers, Ni–Cr based alloys with 18–25.5 wt.%
Fe exhibit excellent oxidation resistance [17]. Therefore, the
formation of a dense oxide layer reduces O diffusion into the
matrix and oxidation is not serious at this temperature.
When the oxidation temperature is increased to 900 °C, the
mass gains ( K

during the initial 3 h compared to that of Ni –25Cr foams in

24Fe alloy foam shows a better oxidation resistance than the
Ni–25Cr alloy foam after 120 h oxidation. Moreover, it is noted
that the oxidation of the Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foams follows the
parabolic-linear law at 900 –1000 °C, indicating that oxidation
behavior is controlled by a diffusion process [18].
It is seen that the difference between Ni –25Cr and Ni–Cr–Fe
alloy foams is more significant at the higher test temperature
(1000 °C) in Fig. 4 (c). Especially the Ni –36Cr–24Fe alloy foam
exhibits 17% lower mass gain kinetics than the Ni –25Cr alloy
foam after oxidation at 1000 °C for 120 h, which confirms its
superior oxidation resistance relative to the chromia-forming
alloy. Compared with those of the reported open-cell Ni-19.4Cr

3.5. Compressive Behavior of Ni –Cr–Fe Alloy Foams at
Ambient Temperature
Fig. 11 shows representative compressive stress –strain curves
of Ni–Cr–Fe alloy foams with different element compositions,
compared to the pure Ni and Ni –25Cr foam. The Ni –25Cr foam
exhibits a stress-strain curve with a near constant (~ 1.5 MPa)
plateau stress characteristic of ductile reticulated foams [21].
As for the Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foam, a stress peak and a stress
valley can be obviously observed in the early deformation
stage, which consists of three phases. Phase I is a linear elastic
region in which the stress value reaches a maximum. In phase
II, the flow stress drops to a valley stress due to collapse in the
cells. Finally, phase III is a densification region wherein a
complete collapse of the cell occurs. It is found that the stress-
strain curve of Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foams has the similar shape
with that of pure Ni foam. However, the yield stress of the Ni –
Cr–Fe alloy foams rises obviously with increasing Cr and Fe
contents. Especially, the yield stress of Ni –36Cr–24Fe alloy
foam is 32 and 2.2 times higher than that of the pure Ni and
Ni–25Cr foams, respectively. The results may be attributed to
increased relative density of the alloy foams and the cell walls
become thicker which subsequently provide greater resis-
tance to buckling and collapse. In addition, Cr and Fe provide
solid-solution strengthening of the γ-Ni matrix, which greatly
increase compressive strength.

4. Conclusions
In this study, the microstructure, oxidation resistance and
high-temperature compressive properties of the 3D open-
celled Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foams are investigated. The following
conclusions can be drawn:
(1) The open-celled Ni –Cr–Fe alloy foams with 34 –36 wt.%
Cr, 20 –24 wt.% Fe can be synthesized by co-deposition
of chromium and iron onto open –cell nickel foams.
After the homogenization treatment, the microstruc-
ture of Ni-Cr-Fe alloy foam consists of the fcc (Fe, Ni)
and (Ni, Cr, Fe) solid solutions with uniform composi-
tion, resulting in a substantial strengthening of the
nickel matrix.
(2) As the Cr and Fe contents increase, the Ni –Cr–Fe alloy
foams show a stable oxidation behavior in the temper-
ature range 800–1000 °C. Especially the mass gain rate of
the Ni –36Cr–24Fe foam is reduced 15% and 17% as
compared to the Ni –25Cr alloy foams at 900 and 1000 °C
for 120 h. Simultaneously, the oxidation mass gain of
Ni–Cr–Fe alloy foams increases with increasing the
temperature.
(3) The formation mechanism of the oxide layers for the
Ni–Cr–Fe alloy foams is as follows: the oxidation
products are mostly mixed oxides containing NiCr
2O4,
NiFe2O4, and Cr 2O3 after oxidation at 800 °C for 50 h. As
the temperature increases, the Cr 2O3 content increases.
When the temperature increase to 1000 °C for 120 h, the
formation of chromium oxide (Cr 2O3) becomes contin-
uous and is rich in the outer layer, which leads to
excellent high-temperature oxidation resistance.
(4) The mechanical properties of 3D open-celled Ni –Cr–Fe
alloy foams are strongly dependent of the foam compo-
sition as well as test temperature. With increasing the Cr
and Fe content, the yield stress and the plateau stress of
alloy foams increase. Simultaneously, as the temperature
increases, the shapes of the curves of the Ni –Cr–Fe alloy
foams change and the length of the plateau of alloy
foams increases.
