ABSTRACT
Achieving high strength and excellent plasticity simultaneously at room temperature has long been a significant 
challenge for NbMoTaW-based refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs). This paper proposes a novel structure of 
heterogeneous phase structure with high concentration interstitials to overcome this dilemma. Through inte -
grated first-principles calculations and experimental synthesis, we demonstrate that 5 at. % C addition enables 
the formation of a carbide-free interstitial into NbMoTaWZr RHEA with significant grain refinement. The 
resulting (NbMoTaWZr)
95
C
5 
RHEA features a highly heterogeneous BCC/FCC microstructure with exceptional 
lattice misfit (~30.2 %) and ultrafine grains ( < 1 μ m). Remarkably, (NbMoTaWZr)
95
C
5 
RHEA exhibits high 
compressive yield strength of 3.2 GPa and excellent plasticity of plastic strain over 65 % in micro-compression. 
High concentration C interstitials and refined grains within the heterogeneous phase contribute significantly to 
alloy ’ s high strength, providing a strength contribution of 0.83 GPa, while the exceptional plasticity stems from 
potent strain hardening enabled by dislocation impediment at phase boundaries and dislocation proliferation 
within the BCC phase. These findings establish a transformative strategy for developing next-generation RHEAs 
with simultaneously ultra-high strength and ductility through controlled heterogeneous phase design and 
interstitial engineering.
1. Introduction
NbMoTaW-based refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are 
increasingly recognized as next-generation structural materials for 
extreme environments due to their exceptional high-temperature 
strength, creep resistance, and microstructural stability [ 1 ]. These 
properties position them as prime candidates for critical components in 
aerospace propulsion systems, including aerospace engines and indus -
trial gas turbines, but struggling with the concurrent achievement of 
high strength and plasticity at room temperature [ 2 , 3 ]. Commonly used 
methods for enhancing alloys with strength and plasticity include 
component optimization [ 4 – 6 ], interstitials or precipitation introduc -
tion [ 7 – 9 ], as well as grain refinement [ 10 ], all of which are difficult to 
achieve the desired results in the field of optimizing the mechanical 
properties of NbMoTaW-based RHEAs whose applications under 
different operating conditions are limited. Heterostructures are the mi -
crostructures formed by the orderly construction of solid solution phases 
or grains with different size, crystal structure or mechanical properties 
[ 11 – 15 ], with the aim of improving the strain hardening capacity and 
ductility of alloys [ 16 ]. This strategy has been applied to a wide range of 
metallic materials and resulted in an excellent match between strength 
and plasticity. For example, the heterogeneous structure RHEA designed

In general, heterogenous zones with dramatically different strength and 
plasticity consist of the basis of heterostructure [ 18 – 20 ], which can be 
composed of the combination with coarse and refined grains or multiple 
phases [ 21 , 22 ]. The difference in mechanical properties divides heter -
ostructures into soft and hard domains, where the former sustains large 
plastic deformation and the latter provides high yield strength [ 23 ]. 
There is a heterostructure boundary between the soft and hard domains, 
which assume the role of coordinating plastic deformation between two 
domains and strain hardening for preventing the early localized insta -
bility [ 24 ]. This is the fundamental reason for heterostructured alloy 
achieving high mechanical properties.
Heterogeneous strategies have been used in NbMoTaW-based 
RHEAs, which is mainly reflected in the heterogeneity of multiple 
solid solution phases [ 25 , 26 ]. Li et al. introduced Zr into NbMoTaW 
RHEA to create a dual-BCC-phase heterogeneous architecture, dramat -
ically enhancing ductility from 2 % to 15.8 % – a breakthrough enabling 
deployment in vehicle leading edges where catastrophic brittle failure 
must be prevented during rapid atmospheric reentry [ 27 ]. High ductility 
in NbMoTaWZr RHEA results from the plastic deformation coordination 
of hard and soft domains, where BCC2 phase (soft domain) is the major

4.4. Deformation mechanism
Another feature for (NbMoTaWZr)
95
C
5 
RHEA is that a promising 
compressive strain over ~ 65 % is achieved in micro-compression. This 
large plastic deformation is originated from the sustained strain hard -
ening of pillar for whole micro-compression. Dislocation structure ob -
servations of alloy after compression loading were used to illustrate the 
effect of heterogeneous phases to strain hardening. There is no obvious 
dislocation movement trace in undeformed sample ( Fig. 5 a). As far as 
the deformed sample, evident tangled dislocations are discernible in the 
BCC phase ( Fig. 11 a), which are derived from the cross-slip of disloca -
tions. The existence of these tangled dislocations would improve strain 
hardening ability via impeding dislocation motion. Regarding the FCC 
phase, the dislocation movement predominantly adheres to a planar slip 
pattern ( Fig. 11 b). This trait could cause the dislocations to migrate 
rapidly towards the boundaries, culminating in boundary deformation. 
As depicted in Fig. 11 c, within the FCC grain boundary, dislocations 
exhibit no pile-up phenomenon. This indicates the limited propensity for 
dislocations to accumulate at the FCC grain boundary. In contrast, in the 
BCC phase adjacent to the phase boundary ( Fig. 11 d), a dislocation pile- 
up is observed. The hetero-deformation mechanism can be invoked to 
elucidate this series of phenomena. The planar slip of dislocations in the 
FCC phase enables their rapid movement to the phase boundary. This 
motion instigates interface deformation, which subsequently triggers 
the nucleation and proliferation of dislocations in the BCC phase. 
Considering that entanglement represents the primary mode of dislo -
cation movement in the BCC phase, high density of dislocations is 
formed at the BCC phase boundary. Such a dislocation structure en -
genders a robust hetero-deformation induced (HDI) stress [ 53 , 54 ]. By 
promoting strain hardening, this stress ultimately enhances the plas -
ticity of alloy.
