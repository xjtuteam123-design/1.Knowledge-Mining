Effect of Zr or Ga Addition and Annealing
on Microstructural Evolution, Deformation
and Fracture Behaviour of Nb-19Si-5Mo-20Ti Based
Hypereutectic Alloy
KASTURI SALA and RAHUL MITRA
The eﬀect of Zr or Ga additions (3 at.pct each) on the evolution of microstructure and
mechanical properties of the Nb-19Si-5Mo-20Ti (base) alloy in as cast or annealed (1500 /C176 C/
100 hours) condition has been examined. The as cast microstructures of both Zr and Ga
containing alloys have exhibited ( b + a)-5-3 silicide phase as primary dendritic phase or as
eutectic constituent besides niobium-rich solid solution (Nb ss). Zr-rich c-5-3 silicide is found in
the eutectic with mixed lamellar or non-lamellar morphology in the Zr containing alloy, whereas
non-lamellar eutectic with b-Tiss at interfaces have been observed in the Ga added alloys. On
annealing, the amount of eutectic surrounding the primary 5-3 silicide dendrites in as-cast
microstructures increases along with increase in the size of Nb
ss, causing a noticeable decrease of
hardness along with increase of dynamic Young’s modulus ( E) to 187–190 GPa ( E = 163 and
179 GPa for as cast Zr and Ga containing alloys, respectively) due to b ﬁ a 5-3 silicide
transformation and partitioning of Mo to Nb ss. The post-anneal fracture toughness is increased
on addition of Zr ( ~ 35.5 pct) and Ga ( ~ 18.2 pct) to ~ 14.9 ± 0.2 and ~ 13 ± 0.1 MPa /C214 m,
respectively, where toughening is facilitated by arrest, bridging or deﬂection of cracks by ductile
Nb
ss phase. The room temperature compressive strengths ( ~ 2600 to 2900 MPa) of the as-cast
alloys, having an inverse relationship with Nb ss content, are reduced by ~ 13 pct with ~ 1.3 and
~ 1.5 times increase in ductility on annealing for Zr and Ga containing alloys, respectively. The
compressive yield strength in the range of 900 /C176 C to 1100 /C176 C is increased more on alloying with
Ga than Zr. However, the strength retention of both the alloys decreases with increasing
temperature by thermally activated deformation of Nb ss. Controlled alloying of hypereutectic
Nb-Si-Mo-Ti with Zr or Ga along with annealing provides a strategy for simultaneous increase
of both strength and fracture toughness.

I. INTRODUCTION
THE present day state-of-art jet turbine engines are
being developed to operate at temperatures beyond
1100 /C176 C. Such an eﬀort is driven by considering that the
speciﬁc core power of a high pressure turbine engine
increases with the inlet temperature of the turbine rotor,
and therefore signiﬁcantly higher eﬃciencies of the
turbine engine can be achieved by increase of the
operating temperature.
[1] The issues which should be
kept in mind while considering the thermal and
mechanical design of any rotating engine component
includes density, microstructural stability, room tem-
perature ductility, high temperature load bearing capac-
ity and elastic properties.
[2] Obviously, the temperature
regime (> 1100 /C176 C) being considered for the operation
of such jet engines is well beyond the upper limit for the
use of Ni-based superalloys, and therefore has inspired
signiﬁcant research for the development of Nb-silicide
based refractory intermetallic alloys since the 1990s. The
typical equilibrium microstructure of a binary Nb-Si
alloy is constituted by a mixture of brittle Nb
5Si3 and
ductile Nb ss phases. To satisfy the requirements for use
in the hot end parts of advanced jet engines, a precise
combination of alloying element addition is needed
which can lead to the microstructural evolution such
that fracture toughness, creep and resistance to oxida-
tion of the alloys are enhanced to the desired level.
Despite alloying and developing Nb-silicide based
intermetallic alloys, achieving adequate fracture resis-
tance at ambient temperature along with high

1765 /C176 C. However, the Nb 3Si is found in the microstruc-

which is stabilized further by addition of alloying
elements like Ti, Zr and Ga.
[6–9] Additionally, the Nb 3Si
is an undesirable brittle phase with poor fracture
toughness of 2.1 ± 1.2 MPa /C214 m,
[10,11] and its presence
is also expected to be detrimental to the creep proper-
ties.[12] It is also intuitive that the formation of Nb 3Si (a
Nb-rich silicide) would also lower the amount of ductile
Nb
ss in the microstructure, which in turn would
adversely aﬀect the fracture toughness and compressive
ductility.[13] By avoiding the formation of Nb 3Si, it has
been possible to obtain microstructures with primarily
two phases, Nb ss and Nb 5Si3, whose amount and
morphology can be tailored with ease to achieve
desirable mechanical properties. Further, speciﬁc alloy-
ing elements like those from Group 4A (Ti, Hf and Zr),
Group 5A (V and Ta) and 6A (Mo, Cr and W) with a
limited range of concentrations are added for solid
solution strengthening apart from stabilizing the desir-
able forms of silicides. In addition to his, the group 3B
elements (Al and In) are also reported to enhance
compressive strength, ductility and oxidation resistance
apart from stabilizing the b-5-3 silicide.
[14] The presence
of Ti as an alloying element helps in enhancement of
fracture toughness as well as improving high tempera-
ture strength retention of the alloys with hypoeutectic
composition.
[15,16]
Recently, a study involving 1.5 at.pct Zr addition to
binary Nb-25 at.pct Si system has shown enhanced rate
of decomposition of the Nb 3Si phase to form Nb/Nb 5Si3
lamellar microstructure. [17] Another study has reported
improved compressive strengths at room temperature
and 1250 /C176 C (278.89 to 293.08 MPa) and oxidation
resistance for 1 to 8 at.pct Zr addition to Nb-22Ti-15-
Si-5Cr-3Hf-3Al system.
[18] Apart from the positive eﬀect
of Zr addition as mentioned above, the synergetic eﬀect
of Ti and Zr addition is believed to reduce the
Peierls–Nabarro barrier energy, such that dislocations
are emitted at the tip of a propagating crack to form a
plastic zone, and therefore enhance the fracture tough-
ness.
[18] The addition of 1 to 4 at.pct Zr to the ternary
Nb-Si-Ti based alloys has been found to alter the
coarsening behaviour of the Nb ss phase, or promote the
formation of lamellar eutectic leading to increase in
hardness and yield strength at room temperature. [19]
Sankar et al. have examined the eﬀect of Zr addition and
have reported fracture toughness of ~ 14.3 MPa /C214 m
along with compressive strength of ~ 2160 MPa for
the hypoeutectic Nb-16Si-4Zr alloy. [20] Similar to Al and
In, addition of Ga as an alloying element which belongs
to Group 3B of the periodic table is believed to impart
strength, toughness as well as oxidation resistance.
[14,21]
Though the melting temperature of the refractory
Nb-silicide based alloy may decrease after addition of
Ga due to its low melting temperature, its addition needs
to be limited in amount to improve the strength of the
alloys. A recent study by Kashyap et al .
[6] on the
inﬂuence of Ga addition in a hypereutectic ternary
Nb-16.5Si-4Ga (at. pct) alloy has shown that Ga
addition can modify the solidiﬁcation path thereby
decreasing the eutectic spacing, which causes increase in
both compressive strength and fracture toughness sig-
niﬁcantly. It has been reported that the presence of Ga
(0.1 or 1 at. pct) as an alloying element in the annealed
Nb-17Si-10Mo is beneﬁcial for fracture resistance, but
its concentration should be kept suﬃciently low to avoid
interfacial embrittlement.
[22] As reported by Guo et al .,
Ga addition (1 and 5 at.pct) to binary and multicom-
ponent system Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Cr-2Al-2Hf (at.pct) pro-
motes the formation of Nb
5Si3 phases.[10] It has been
further shown by Guo et al . that the addition of Ga
decreases the compressive strength, obtained from the
micropillar compression tests of the Nb ss phase, while
increasing it in case of both Nb 3Si and (Nb,Ti) 5Si3.
Although the aforementioned studies have been
carried out on additions of Zr or Ga to the Nb-Si
based refractory intermetallic alloys, yet the understand-
ing of their eﬀect on microstructural evolution, fracture
and deformation mechanisms of speciﬁc multicompo-
nent systems in the hypereutectic composition range is
very limited. The detailed results of earlier studies on
microstructural evolution and its inﬂuence on the
mechanical properties and oxidation behaviour of the
hypereutectic quaternary Nb-19Si-5Mo-20Ti alloy in
as-cast or annealed conditions (1500 /C176 C/100 hours) may
be found in the earlier publications by the
authors.
[16,23,24] It has been noticed that although the
hypereutectic alloys possess superior compressive
strength compared to hypoeutectic compositions, yet
an opposite trend is noticed for fracture toughness. On
the other hand, the oxidation resistance of hypereutectic
Nb-Si-Mo and Nb-19Si-5Mo-20Ti alloys have been
found to be superior to those with hypoeutectic com-
positions in previous studies.
[24,25] Therefore, it is
desirable to try selective quinary alloying additions with
optimum concentrations based on the information
available in literature for further improvement of the
fracture toughness of the Nb-19Si-5Mo-20Ti alloy,
without compromising its strength at both ambient
and high temperatures. In view of the aforementioned
ideas for further development, the present study has
been carried out with the objective of the fundamental
understanding of the eﬀect of minor addition (3 at.pct)
of Zr and Ga to Nb-19Si-5Mo-20Ti alloy on the

II. MATERIALS AND METHODS
A. Alloy Preparation
Two alloys have been prepared by adding 3 at pct Zr
or Ga to Nb-19Si-5Mo-20Ti based quaternary alloys by
arc melting using high purity Nb (99.7 pct), Mo
(99.5 pct), Si (98.6 pct), Ti (99.5 pct), Ga (99.9 pct)
and Zr (99.2 pct) powders. The powders were mixed in
appropriate proportions for 1 h to obtain the afore-
mentioned compositions using mortar and pestle. Sub-
sequently, the powder mixtures were separated into
batches of 25 g, which were cold-compacted to form
pellets having nearly 10 mm diameter and approxi-
mately 8 mm height at a pressure of 16 MPa. The pellets
were melted in water-cooled copper crucibles with the
help of non-consumable tungsten electrode inside an
arc-melter, which was ﬁrst evacuated to high vacuum
(10
-6 Torr) and then ﬁlled with Ar. The process of
arc-melting was carried out using a current in the range
of~ 250 to 280 A. The ingots were turned and remelted
at least ﬁve times inside the crucible to achieve chemical
homogeneity. Some of the as-cast specimens were
annealed in Ar atmosphere inside a horizontal tube
furnace at 150 /C176 C for 100 hours. The annealing temper-
ature was kept at 1500 /C176 C( ~ 0.81T
M, considering the
eutectic melting point, 1915 /C176 C) in order to obtain
microstructures closer to equilibrium and homogeneity
of composition within the constituent phases. Consid-
ering the formation of metastable phases such as Nb 3Si
and b-Nb5Si3 (as discussed in Section 1), whose trans-
formation is incomplete during solidiﬁcation, the choice
of 1500 /C176 C for annealing has been considered as
appropriate in the present study for evolution of
stable phases. Moreover, none of the phases present in
the microstructure is expected to melt at 1500 /C176 C. For
the purpose of annealing, the specimens encapsulated in
a Ta foil were placed in an alumina boat, and then kept
inside the hot zone of the tube furnace, where the
temperature distribution was uniform. Additionally, an
alumina boat ﬁlled with Ti-sponge was placed at the
entrance of the tube furnace during the annealing heat
treatment to function as the oxygen getter. The nominal
compositions of the present investigation in both as-cast
and annealed (1500 /C176 C/100 hours) conditions are listed
in Table I.
B. Microstructural Characterization
The procedures used for microstructural characteri-
zation have been well-described in the earlier publica-
tions by the authors.
[23,26] The samples were examined
using a scanning electron microscope (SEM) (Carl Zeiss,
Germany, Model: Supra 40 FESEM) operated with
accelerating voltage of 20 kV, and the chemical compo-
sitions of the constituent phases were analyzed simulta-
neously with the help of an attached energy dispersive
X-ray (EDX) micro-analyzer (Oxford Instruments Ltd.,
UK Model: ISIS300). For the measurement of volume
fractions of the phases present, representative
microstructures recorded on the SEM were chosen and
subsequently analyzed using the point-counting method
as discussed in detail elsewhere.
[23,26] Identiﬁcation of
the phases present in the microstructure was carried out
by X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) analysis on a diﬀractometer
(Bruker AXS, Germany, model: D8 Advance) by using
Cu K
a radiation with the diﬀraction angle selected to be
in the range of 20 to120 deg. The XRD scan was carried
out at a step size of 0.02 /C176 with the time per step being
equal to 0.3 seconds. In-depth microstructural study of
each alloy was carried out using a transmission electron
microscope (TEM) (JEOL, Japan, Model: JEM-2100F)
having a ﬁeld emission gun being operated at an
acceleration voltage of 200 kV, where chemical compo-
sitions were analyzed by using the attached EDX facility
(JEOL, Japan, Model: JED-2300). The samples for
TEM studies were prepared on a dual beam focused ion
beam (FIB) microscope (Carl Zeiss, Germany, Model:
Auriga Compact) to achieve site-speciﬁc electron
transparency.

The present study has shown that it is possible to
achieve a stable microstructure with Nb ss and 5-3 silicide
by adding 5 at. pct Mo to the Nb-Si based alloy, in spite
of alloying with Ti + Zr or Ti + Ga, which are known
to stabilize the Nb 3Si, which is metastable at tempera-

that annealing at 1500 /C176 C for 100 hours leads to a more
equilibrium microstructure with considerable increase of
Young¢s modulus from 163 to ~187 GPa for the Zr
containing alloy, and from ~ 179 to ~ 190 GPa for the
Ga containing alloy. A more interesting observation is
the signiﬁcant increase in fracture toughness from
11 ± 0.17 MPa /C214 m obtained for the base alloy to
~ 14.9 ± 0.2 MPa /C214 m on alloying of the base alloy with
3 at. pct Zr, in spite of hypereutectic composition. On
the other hand, addition of 3 at. pct Ga has led to
fracture toughness of ~13 ± 0.1 MPa /C214 m, along with a
much higher value of compressive strength

tionally, greater compressive yield strength has been
observed at high temperature up to 1100 /C176 C, with the
ranking of the values following the order, Ga containing
alloy > Zr containing alloy > base alloy. Moreover, the
strength retention of the Zr-containing alloy in the range
of 900 to 1100 /C176 C is comparable to that of the base alloy
in as cast or annealed condition, whereas that of the Ga
containing alloy is marginally superior. The present
study has also shown that annealing at 1500 /C176 C for
100 hour leads to an increase of compressive ductility of
the investigated alloys by 1.3 to 1.5 times, with a modest
(~13 pct) decrease in the compressive strength at room
temperature. Therefore, a limited addition of Zr or Ga

V. CONCLUSIONS
The present study carried out to examine the inﬂuence
of adding 3 at. pct Zr or Ga as well as annealing at
1500 /C176 C for 100 hours on microstructure and mechan-
ical properties of a hypereutectic Nb-19Si-20Ti-5Mo
alloy leads to the following conclusions:
1. The presence of 5-3 silicide ( a and b) and Nb ss
phases has been found in as cast or annealed
microstructures of both Zr and Ga containing
alloys. Additionally, a eutectic containing Zr-rich
c-5-3 silicide phase has been observed in both as cast
and annealed Nb-19Si-5Mo-20Ti-3Zr alloy. On the
other hand, b-Ti
ss has been found at the Nb ss/5-3
silicide interfaces in the microstructures of both as
cast and annealed Nb-19Si-5Mo-20Ti-3Ga alloy,
while it is found to be absent in the Nb-19Si-5-
Mo-20Ti-3Zr alloy.
2. Annealing has resulted in moderate coarsening of
the Nb
ss phase present as a part of non-lamellar
eutectic. Moreover, partial transformation of
b-! a-5-3 silicide has occurred on annealing.
3. The Young ¢s modulus of the examined alloys have
been found to scale with the net amount of 5-3
silicide phase and is found to increase on annealing
in spite of decrease in the amount of primary 5-3
silicide, owing to increase in the Mo concentration
of Nb
ss, and also due to the transformation of
b- ﬁ a-5-3 silicide. The Poisson ¢s ratio lies in the
range from 0.2 to 0.28, depending on composition,
and it increases with annealing due to the increase in
the amount of ductile Nb ss in the microstructure.
4. In the as-cast alloys the average microhardness of
the 5-3 silicide remains more or less similar for the
investigated Zr and Ga added alloys, while it is
found to be lower for the eutectic mixture. The
transformation of b-t o a-5-3 silicide with lower
microhardness value contributes to decrease in
microhardness of eutectic mixture after annealing
apart from spherodization of non-lamellar eutectic
followed by a modest amount of coarsening. On
annealing, the hardness of Zr or Ga containing
alloys has decreased, due to a significant reduction
in the amount of primary 5-3 silicide with simulta-
neous increase in the amount of non-lamellar
eutectic and Nb
ss along with its coarsening.
5. The post-anneal room temperature fracture tough-
ness found as 11 ± 0.17 MPa /C214 m for the base alloy
is increased by 35.5 pct to 15 ± 0.2 MP /C214 mo n
alloying with Zr, whereas a relatively moderate
increase of 18.2 pct is observed on addition of Ga.
The fracture toughness has been found to scale with
the net amount of the ductile Nb
ss phase, probably
because of its role in damage tolerance by increasing
the tortuosity of crack path through arrest and
deflection of cracks formed in the neighbouring 5-3
silicide phase. The river patterns appearing in the
Nbss phase along with facets formed in 5-3 silicide
phases reveal the predominance of cleavage mode of
failure.
6. The room temperature compressive strength of the
as cast alloys found to be in the range of ~ 2600 to
2900 MPa, appears to have an inverse relationship
with the net Nb
ss content of their microstructures,
such that the order of decrease is: Ga-containing
alloy > Zr-containing alloy > base alloy. Further,
the compressive yield strengths have been found to
decrease upon annealing due to increase in the net
volume fraction of the non-lamellar eutectic and
Nb
ss at the expense of 5-3 silicide, as well as
coarsening of the Nb ss phase, which lowers the
constraint to its deformation.
7. In the temperature range of 900 to 1100 /C176 C, the
compressive yield strength has been found to be the
highest for the Ga-containing alloy, followed by
those of Zr-containing alloy and the base alloy.
Both compressive yield strength and strength reten-
tion decrease with increase in the test temperature,
due to the thermally activated deformation of the
ductile Nb
ss phase. Additionally, the compression
tests at 1100 /C176 C have shown reduction of strength
retention on annealing due to the increase in size
and volume fraction of the Nb
ss.
