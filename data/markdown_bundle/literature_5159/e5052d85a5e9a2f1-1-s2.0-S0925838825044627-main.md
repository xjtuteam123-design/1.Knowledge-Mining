ABSTRACT
To overcome the long-term problem of impossible achievement in both strong high-temperature strength and fast 
dissolution rate in low-cost Mg alloys, this work provides one novel Mg-0.76Y-0.79Ni dilute alloy using common 
extrusion method. Two alloys Mg-1.97Y-0.22Ni and Mg-5.53Y are also studied as the comparisons. The tensile 
testing at 473 K shows the highest yield strength is realized in Mg-0.76Y-0.79Ni alloy, with the collaborative 
combinations of fine grains and abundant blocky particles. Meanwhile, the block distortion replacing block 
cracking has positive effects on the high-temperature mechanical property of Mg-0.76Y-0.79Ni alloy. The 
dissolution testing at 366 K in 3 % KCl solution shows the fastest dissolution rate is synchronously realized in Mg- 
0.76Y-0.79Ni alloy, with the collaborative combinations of high amounts of blocky particles at triple grain 
boundaries and inhomogeneous dissolution induced by lath-like phases in grain interiors. Thus, with the 
excellent combination of high-temperature strength and dissolution rate, the novel Mg-0.76Y-0.79Ni dilute alloy 
successfully exhibits the great potential in the wide applications to engineering fields.
1. Introduction
Currently, green mining has been put forward all over the world, in 
order to protect the environment and prohibit the energy cost [1 – 2] . 
Among the various eco-friendly mining tools, the different types of 
bridge plug, fracturing ball and tension packer made by Mg alloys are 
showing the great potential in the shale oil engineering field [3 – 4] . The 
advantages of Mg mining products locate in many aspects, such as the 
light weight decreasing energy cost and the good dissolubility short -
ening construction period via avoiding final backflow process [5 – 6] . 
With the attractive characteristics above, the outdated viewpoint on the 
poor corrosion-resistance of Mg alloys can be totally reconsidered to -
wards the opposite but one quite positive direction [7] . Namely, the 
dissoluble tools fabricated by Mg alloys with fast dissolution rate are 
reasonably expected to replace the traditional steel products and to act 
as the crucial role in the environment protection.
The practical applications are facing one serious trade-off between 
the development cost and the service performance. Since the internal 
temperatures of most shale oil mines range from room temperature to 
366 K or even up to 396 K [8 – 9] , the high-temperature strength is 
commonly required apart from the room-temperature strength. To avoid 
the premature failure before reaching the ideal pipeline expansion, the 
present Mg dissoluble tools with acceptable high-temperature strength 
are usually alloyed by large amounts of rare-earth (RE) elements [10] . 
Due to the high solubility of RE atoms in Mg matrix and the rapid aging 
response of Mg-RE alloys, the solution strengthening and precipitation 
strengthening effects can be easily achieved [11 – 14] . For examples, Yu 
et al. [15] prepared one Mg-11Gd-4.5Y-1.5Zn-1Nd-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy via 
hot extrusion and aging treatment, showing the yield strength of 
343 MPa at 473 K. Yan et al. [16] studied the tensile deformation of 
extruded Mg-8Gd-4Y-1Sm-0.5Zr alloy at 473 K. Their results indicated 
that the β ʹ precipitates composed by Mg and Gd elements could suppress 
the grain boundary sliding and could strengthen the alloy significantly. 
However, the above-mentioned excellent high-temperature strength was 
achieved via sacrificing the lightweight advantage of Mg and consuming 
the high-cost RE resources. Moreover, extremely low or even no dis -
solubility could be observed in these highly RE-alloyed Mg products 
owing to the strong corrosion-resistant characteristic brought by fruitful 
RE elements [17 – 18] . Consequently, the present high-strength Mg-RE 
alloys could hardly be applied to the oil and gas engineering field.
To increase the micro-battery amounts, transition metal (TM) 
elements including Ni, Fe and Zn are usually added into Mg and 
MgTM compounds with complex compositions are generated to 
induce galvanic corrosion in the electrolytic medium like the

weight, strong high-temperature strength and fast dissolution rate 
should be concerned carefully in the design of dissoluble Mg-RE-TM 
alloys. Moreover, most of the previous studies used room-temperature 
mechanical properties to evaluate the applicability in the high- 
temperature environment of shale oil wells [19 – 26] . The actual 
high-temperature mechanical strength and its balance with dissolution 
rate were neglected for a long time. Therefore, in response to the 
comprehensive considerations and the material plain concept [30 – 31] , 
one novel Mg-Y-Ni dilute alloy with both strong high-temperature 
strength and fast dissolution rate is put forward in this study. Two as -
pects are considered for the element selection: one is the price of Y is 
cheaper than Gd and Nd, and the other is the weight of Ni is lower than 
Zn and Cu. Since the external pressure may increase the actual tem -
perature of Mg tools higher than the electrolyte temperature 
(298 – 366 K) [32] , the mechanical strength is evaluated at 473 K. In this 
way, the Mg-based dissoluble tools can be safely applied in most of the 
shale oil wells. Meanwhile, comparative experiments are carried out to 
research the microstructure characteristics and to confirm the excellent 
service performance of this dilute alloy. We sincerely hope that this 
novel Mg-Y-Ni dilute alloy reported in this study can greatly contribute 
to the advancement of lightweight and low-cost dissoluble tools in the 
related engineering fields.
2. Experimental procedures
2.1. Alloy preparation
The three alloys used in this study included Mg-0.76Y-0.79Ni, Mg- 
1.97Y-0.22Ni, and Mg-5.53Y, in weight percents (wt%). These alloys 
were prepared by mixing high-purity Mg and master alloys including 
Mg-30wt%Y and Mg-5wt%Ni, melting in resistance furnaces at 1053 K, 
and casting followed by air cooling. The whole process was protected by 
an atmosphere of CO
2 
and SF
6 
with a volume ratio of 100:1, so the 
oxidation could be successfully avoided. Then, the cast alloys were 
machined into cylinders and polished to 250 mm in diameter. The ho -
mogenization treatment was performed at 693 K for 24 h, and the 
extrusion was performed at 623 K with the extrusion speed at 
0.5 mm ⋅ s
 1 
and the extrusion ratio as 9:1. The extrusion parameters fell 
in the common ones for extruded Mg since the hexagonal close-packed 
materials with low deformability should be processed at high tempera -
ture and low strain rate [15] . Specifically, for the sake of avoiding any 
extrusion cracks, the extrusion ratio was relatively lower compared to 
16:1 – 25:1 in spite of the larger extrusion ratio decreasing the particle 
scales. But if the extrusion ratio was reduced to 6:1 or lower (or the 
extrusion temperature was increased over 673 K for such one dilute 
alloy, the abnormal grain growth might happen during extrusion [1] . All 
the extruded alloys were promptly cooled with water to preserve the 
instantaneous microstructures. The actual chemical compositions of 
three extruded alloys were detected by means of energy dispersive 
spectrometer and listed in Table 1 as well as their abbreviations. Spe -
cifically, the first part stands for the Y content and the second part

To further check the excellent high-temperature strength, the tensile 
creep tests are performed at 473 K and one comparative Mg-2.22 wt%Y 
binary alloy (Y2.2) is also included. After creep loading for 96 h at 
120 MPa, as shown in Fig. 3 (median curves of multiple trials), no 
obvious rupture occurs in all four alloys. However, the accumulative 
creep strain and steady creep rate are quite different among the crept 
alloys. The poorest creep performance lies in Y2.2 alloy while the creep 
performance of YN20 alloy is sharply enhanced using the dilute 0.2 wt% 
Ni addition. Moreover, with the Y/Ni weight ratio decreasing and 
continuously dilute alloying in YN11 alloy, the creep performance is 
further enhanced to the level close to the creep performance of Y5.5 
alloy. The high-temperature strength and even the long-time safety of 
YN11 dilute alloy are confirmed again, and the creep properties of YN11 
dilute is worthy of studying in the future work.

3.4. Deformed microstructure
The microstructural information of deformed alloys is shown in 
Fig. 7 , after tensile loading to the strain ε = 8% at 473 K. No twins are 
observed and the texture intensities are increased slightly, indicating the 
grain scales of all three alloys are fine enough for avoiding the twinning- 
induced deterioration in mechanical properties [46 – 47] . Meanwhile, 
compared to the initial state, the deformed alloys have quite different 
changes in the KAM values. Specifically, the average KAM value in -
creases slightly in YN11 alloy whereas it increases sharply in YN20 alloy. 
It is suggested that the dislocation slips in YN11 alloy can be well 
accommodated whereas the dislocation slips in YN20 alloy inevitably 
induce the deformation incompatibility and stress concentration as 
implied by the KAM map [48] . For the mildly increased KAM value and 
slightly increased texture intensity in Y5.5 alloy, the transverse defor -
mation induced by prismatic slip may account for its microstructure 
evolution [49] .

on mechanical property, TEM observations for initial and deformed al -
loys are shown in Fig. 10 . For the initial YN11 alloy, in Fig. 10 a, one

considered as promoting plastic strain increment with reduced possi -
bility for dislocation tangling [62] . Thus, the elongation of Y5.5 alloy is 
a little higher than the elongation of YN11 alloy, and much higher than 
the elongation of YN20 alloy.

4.3. Dissolution route
Although previous reports have pointed out that grain boundaries 
and strong texture are two negative factors for dissolution rate incre -
ment [86 – 87] , the fine-grained and strong-textured YN11 alloy still 
shows the fastest dissolution rate among the three alloys. To illustrate 
this issue and to understand the comprehensive performance clearly, the 
post-dissolution micrographs are provided in Fig. 12 and the corre -
sponding schematic diagrams are drawn in Fig. 13 . The observation 
directions in Fig. 12 are performed along the upper surface of cylinder 
specimens in order to avoid any damages on the corroded morphologies 
during electro-discharge machining. In the highly RE-alloyed Mg as 
reported [88 – 89] , the RE-containing precipitates formed in grain in -
teriors have few opportunities to contact the KCl solution. Even if the 
precipitates could be corroded, the oxides containing RE elements would 
serve as protective films and decrease the dissolution rate in turn. For 
this reason, Y5.5 alloy with no precipitates even has no obvious dis -
solubility as shown in Figs. 4 and 12 c.
Inversely, the ternary blocky particles at triple grain boundaries of 
YN11 and YN20 alloys (see Fig. 9 ) can result in totally different disso -
lution behaviors after reacting with KCl solution. Once the galvanic 
reactions happen in the blocks as the cathodes [90 – 94] , the contact 
surface areas between KCl solution and blocky particles would increase. 
Then, the KCl solution could easily penetrate into the grain interiors 
through the destroyed grain boundaries, leading to the sharp expansion 
of contact areas between KCl solution and grains. Since the block frac -
tions are lower in YN20 alloy, the dissolution rate is much faster in YN11 
alloy. Thus, with the catalyzing role of blocky particles, the finer grains 
with higher grain boundary densities provide more chances for the 
contact between KCl solution and grain interiors in YN11 alloy.
Meanwhile, although the ternary lath-like phases in grain interiors

with the inhomogeneous lath distributions leads to the fast dissolution 
rate of YN11 alloy. Such excellent comprehensive service performance 
in the novel YN11 dilute alloy is quite suitable for serving as the low-cost 
Mg dissoluble tools in the oil and gas engineering field and other relative 
fields.
5. Conclusions
To overcome the dilemma in the present Mg dissoluble tools with 
high cost but weak high-temperature strength and slow dissolution rate, 
one novel Mg-0.76Y-0.79Ni (YN11) dilute alloy is developed in this 
work. Through evaluating the high-temperature strength at 473 K and 
the dissolution rate at both 298 K and 366 K, the great advantages of this 
YN11 dilute alloy are revealed and analyzed. Meanwhile, one ternary 
alloy Mg-1.97Y-0.22Ni (YN20) and one binary alloy Mg-5.53Y (Y5.5) 
are also studied as the comparisons. The main conclusions are drawn as 
follows: 
(1) With the lowest total element contents of only 1.55 wt%, YN11 
alloy demonstrates the best service performance. The yield 
strength at 473 K reaches 149.6 MPa, even stronger than some 
highly alloyed Mg. The dissolution rate at 366 K reaches

(2) The grain boundary strengthening corrected by texture hard -
ening significantly contributes to the strength of YN11 alloy 
despite the high tensile temperature. The frequent pyramidal 
< c + a > slip and the independent prismatic < a > slip during ten -
sile loading effectively accommodates the plastic deformation.
(3) Despite the weak solution strengthening in YN11 alloy, the high 
fractions and the distortion of blocky particles at triple grain 
boundaries drastically improve the high-temperature strength 
and avoid the deterioration in elongation. Meanwhile, the lath- 
like phases in grain interiors provide the further optimization in 
the mechanical property.
(4) The high fractions of blocky particles increase the dissolution 
routes of YN11 alloy. The lath-like phases induce the inhomo -
geneous dissolution which further accelerate the dissolution rate. 
With the excellent combination of strong high-temperature 
strength and fast dissolution rate, the novel YN11 dilute alloy is 
greatly expected to be widely utilized in the oil and gas engi -
neering field.
