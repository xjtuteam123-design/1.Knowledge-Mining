ABSTRACT 
Ti-xNb-4Zr – 8Sn (x = 16, 20, 24, 28, 36 wt%) alloys were fabricated using vacuum arc furnace melting with 
subsequent hot rolling and solution treatment. Tensile tests were conducted to evaluate the mechanical prop -
erties of the Ti-xNb-4Zr – 8Sn alloys. The microstructural and phase composition were examined using scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM) equipped with an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDX), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and 
transmission electron microscope (TEM). The results show that the Nb content strongly affects the microstructure 
and the mechanical properties of the Ti-xNb-4Zr – 8Sn alloys. With the increase of Nb content, more β phase 
occurs in the alloy due to the stabilizing effect of Nb, and the alloys evolve from α ’’ + β (Nb: 16, 20, 24 wt%) 
duplex-phase structure to a single β (Nb: 28, 36 wt%) phase structure. The Young ’ s modulus decreases first with 
the increase of Nb and then increases when the Nb content increases over 24 wt%. The Ti – 24Nb – 4Zr – 8Sn alloy 
shows the minimum Young ’ s modules of 45 GPa. The tensile strength (UTS) of the alloys decreases with the 
increase of Nb content. The effect of Nb content on the microstructure and the mechanical properties of the 
quaternary alloys are elucidated, and the affecting mechanism is discussed in this study. 
1. Introduction 
Titanium alloy has become one of the most important materials in 
biomedical applications because of its low density, high specific 
strength, excellent corrosion resistance, and good biocompatibility [ 1 , 
2 ]. In the past decades, several titanium alloys have been developed for 
orthopedic implant applications, such as Ti – 6Al – 4V [ 3 ], Ti – 6Al – 7Nb [ 4 , 
5 ], Ti – 5Al-2.5Fe [ 6 ], etc. However, challenges still exist in developing 
Ti-based biomedical alloys, especially for their mechanical and biolog -
ical compatibility with the human body. First, the Young ’ s modulus of 
some Ti alloys developed, including Ti – 6Al – 4V (~110 GPa) [ 3 ], 
Ti – 6Al – 7Nb (~108 GPa) [ 4 , 5 ], Ti – 5Al-2.5Fe (~128 GPa) [ 6 ] is higher 
than that of human bone (10 – 40 GPa) [ 7 , 8 ]. The addition of Al and V 
can improve the strength of Ti alloys, but the Young ’ s modulus of the 
alloys also increased. The mismatch of Young ’ s modulus between 
Ti-based alloy and the bone could cause the so-called “ stress shielding ” 
effect [ 9 ], leading to the degradation or failure of implant materials [ 1 , 
10 – 12 ]. Second, some alloying elements, such as Al and V, are associ -
ated with cytotoxicity, and hence should be avoided in alloy design. 
Therefore, most of the current research on biomedical Ti-based al -
loys are focusing on the two aspects: (i) Developing near β -type or β -type 
titanium alloys possessing a high strength with a low Young ’ s modulus 
that is compatible with human bone; (ii) Using nontoxic alloying ele -
ments, such as Ti, Nb, Mo, Ta, Zr, Sn, to improve the biocompatibility 
[ 13 , 14 ]. A series of new metastable β -Ti alloys, including Ti-Nb [ 9 ], 
Ti-Nb-Zr [ 1 , 13 ], Ti-Nb-Sn [ 12 ], Ti-Mo [ 15 ], Ti-Nb-Ta-Zr [ 16 ], have been 
investigated. C.H.Wang et al. [ 15 ] studied the mechanical properties of 
Ti-Mo alloys. Their results showed that although a single β -type Ti-based 
alloy can be obtained by the addition of Mo, the Young ’ s modulus 
(83 – 104 GPa) of Ti-Mo alloys is still much higher than that of human 
bone. Peng Qi et al. [ 17 ] studied the effect of Fe element on mechanical 
properties of Ti – 6Zr-xFe (x = 4, 5, 6, 7) Alloy. The research indicated 
that Fe, like Nb, can improve the stability of β phase. The alloy is mainly 
composed of α and β phases and its tensile strength (748 – 994 MPa) and 
Young ’ s modulus (90 – 94 GPa) increase with the addition of Fe. Different 
processing techniques are also utilized to optimize the mechanical 
properties of Ti-based alloys. Q.K.Meng et al. [ 1 ] studied the effect of 
thermo-mechanical treatment on the mechanical properties of a ternary 
Ti – 36Nb – 5Zr alloy. They found that the tensile strength of the alloy can 
be improved by introducing nano-scale hexagonal α precipitates through 
short-term low-temperature heat treatment. However, the Young ’ s 
modulus was still obviously higher (60 GPa) than that of human bone.

Srijan Acharya et al. [ 16 ] studied the mechanical properties of 
Ti – Nb – Ta – O and Ti – Nb – Ta – Zr – O alloys. And their results showed that 
although the tensile strength of those Ti-based alloys could be improved 
by introducing interstitial O element, the Young ’ s modulus could not 
reach a desired level. 
In recent years, a novel quaternary Ti – Nb – Zr – Sn alloy has attracted 
great attention because of its excellent mechanical properties and 
biocompatibility. Y.L.Hao et al. [ 18 ] developed a Ti – 24Nb – 4Zr – 8Sn 
(Ti2448) alloy with a record low Young ’ s modulus of 42 GPa [ 19 , 20 ] 
and excellent strength of over 850 MPa. In the quaternary alloy, Nb and 
Ti can be infinitely dissolved in the β phase because of the alike lattice 
parameters. Meanwhile, Nb, Zr, and Sn can stabilize the β phase and 
prevent the formation of brittle α ’’ martensite and ω phase. Hence, an 
optimized mechanical property without comprising the biocompati -
bility is successfully derived. Niobium is a critical alloying element for 
Ti-based alloys, and in most cases, it works as the β phase stabilizing 
element to be added. Few studies have been conducted to systematically 
investigate the effect of the variation of Nb content on the microstruc -
ture and mechanical properties of the quaternary Ti – Nb – Zr – Sn alloy. In 
the current study, the effects of Nb content on the microstructure, phase 
composition, and mechanical properties of quaternary Ti-xNb-4Zr – 8Sn 
(x = 16, 20, 24, 28, 36 wt%) alloys were investigated. The affecting 
mechanism of the alloying element on the mechanical properties was 
discussed. The findings of this research would be beneficial to the 
development of novel biomedical Ti alloys with excellent mechanical 
and biological compatibility with the human body.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Effect of Nb content on phase composition 
Fig. 2 (a) and (b) show the XRD patterns of Ti-xNb-4Zr-8Sn alloys 
with different Nb contents in as-cast and solution-treated (ST) states, 
respectively. As shown in Fig. 2 (a), when the Nb content is 16 wt% and 
20 wt%, the as-cast alloy is composed of hexagonal α phase and β phase. 
When the Nb content increases to 24 wt%, the hexagonal α phase 
changes to the orthogonal α ’’ phase. The phase evolution observed with 
the increase of the Nb content can be attributed to the β phase stabili -
zation effect of Nb. 
XRD results ( Fig. 2 (b)) show that ST alloys are composed of α ’’ + β 
phases for the Ti-(16, 20, 24 wt %)Nb – 4Zr – 8Sn alloys, indicating that 
the actual martensitic transformation starting temperature (Ms) is 
higher than room temperature and the martensitic transformation 
( β→ α ’’ ) occur [ 21 ]. When the Nb content is higher than 24 wt%, a 
complete single β phase composition is formed with a preferred orien -
tation of (110)
β
. All the above results show that increasing the Nb 
content in Ti-xNb-4Zr – 8Sn alloy can improve the stability of the β phase, 
hence promotes the formation of the β phase in the alloy, which is 
consistent with that reported by other researchers [ 22 , 23 ]. 
The volume fraction of the phases that appear in ST alloys is quan -
titatively analyzed by the K-value method based on the XRD results by 
Jade software, and the results are shown in Fig. 2 (c). It can be seen that 
when the Nb content increases from 16 wt% to 20 wt%, no obvious 
changes are observed for the volume fraction of each phase. However, 
when the Nb content increases from 20 wt% to 24 wt%, the volume 
fraction of the α ’’ phase decreases from 76.3% to 44.4%. Further in -
crease the Nb content to 28 wt%, the alloy consists of a single β phase. 
Hu et al. [ 24 ] reported that in a binary Ti – Nb system, a single β phase 
alloy could not be formed until Nb content reached 38 wt%. In the

3.2. Microstructure characterization 
Fig. 3 shows the microstructure images of Ti-xNb-4Zr – 8Sn alloys 
after the solution treatment. When the Nb content is lower than 24 wt%, 
the alloy consists of coarse equiaxed β grain with amounts of needle-like 
α ’’ phase precipitated inside grains. When the Nb content increases to 
24 wt%, equiaxed β phase grains ( Fig. 3 (c)) and randomly distributed 
need-like α ’’ phase are present ( Fig. 3 (d)). Further increasing the Nb 
content to 28 wt%, a single β phase structure with equiaxed grains ap -
pears as shown in Fig. 3 (d). The mean grain sizes of β phase present in Ti- 
xNb-4Zr – 8Sn (x = 16, 20, 24, 28, 36, wt.%) alloys are determined using 
Image-Pro Plus software, which is 143 μ m, 121 μ m, 67 μ m, 55 μ m, and 
33 μ m, respectively. Hence, the grain size of the β phase decreases 
gradually with the increase of the Nb content. Microstructure charac -
terization reveals that the Ti alloys after the solution treatment possess 
much finer grains with a more homogeneous structure comparing to the 
as-cast alloys. As the α ’’ phase possesses a smaller Young ’ s modulus than 
the α phase, more desirable mechanical properties, i.e. low modulus, 
could be derived in ST alloys [ 25 , 26 ]. Therefore, the following 
evaluations were mainly performed on ST alloys. 
3.3. Evaluation of mechanical properties 
The mechanical behavior of the Ti-xNb-4Zr – 8Sn alloys under tensile 
testing conditions is strongly affected by the Nb content. The stress- 
strain curves of the tensile tests are shown in Fig. 4 and the results are 
listed in Table 2 . Among all the samples tested, Ti – 16Nb – 4Zr – 8Sn alloy 
shows the highest tensile strength (752 Mpa) with an elongation of 
19.6%. A typical tensile stress-strain curve features with a first elastic 
deformation stage and then a plastic deformation stage is observed for 
Ti – 16Nb – 4Zr – 8Sn alloy. Further increasing the Nb content to 20 wt%, 
both the tensile strength and the elongation decrease. A plateau related 
to the so-called “ double yield ” phenomenon appears in the stress-strain 
curve. The “ double yield ” is also observed for the Ti – 24Nb – 4Zr – 8Sn 
alloy. The “ double yield ” should be attributed to the stress-induced 
martensitic transformation from the parent phase ( β ) to the martens -
itic phase ( α ’’ ) [ 27 ]. It is worth noting that the critical loading stress for 
the occurrence of the first yield shown in the “ double yield ” curve de -
creases from 410 MPa to 200 MPa when the Nb content increased from 
20 wt% to 24 wt%. The reduction of the critical stress induced in the 
martensitic transformation should be related to the variation of the 
Gibbs free energy. As suggested in Ref. [ 28 ], the free energy change for 
the stress-induced martensitic (SIM) transformation could be calculated

Liu et al. used in-situ synchrotron-based high-energy X-ray 
diffuse-scattering (HE-XRDS) technique to investigate the deformation 
mechanism of Ti – 24Nb – 4Zr – 8Sn-0.1O single crystal under loading

formation process by tracing the change of diffuse scattering signals in 
the analysis using the in-situ HE-XRDS, which demonstrated that two 
mechanisms, i.e. the switch of oriented martensitic domains and the 
reversible martensitic transformation involving nanoscale lattice mod -
ulation, resulted in the low elastic modulus and the nonlinear super -
plastic behavior. 
Fig. 6 shows the fracture morphology of the alloy samples after the 
tensile tests. Typical ductile failure morphologies were observed for all 
the samples. When the Nb content is 16 wt%, uniformly distributed 
dimples with a diameter of 8.5 μ m appear on the fractured surface. River 
patterns are also observed on the fractured surface indicating that brittle 
failure also occurs at several spots probably at the locations where the 
martensite α ’’ existed. When the Nb content increases to 20 wt% and 24 
wt%, the alloys exhibit a typical tearing fracture surface with flat shaped 
dimples of about 17.8 μ m. The flatness of the fracture surface can be 
attributed to the damage process developed inside localized shearing 
bands. With further increase of the Nb content, the dimple sizes which 
appear on the fracture surface decrease to about 8.0 μ m. Meanwhile, the 
elongation of alloy samples increases. Fig. 5 (d) illustrates the 
relationships between the elongation and the dimple sizes. It can be seen 
that the alloys (Nb: 20 wt%, 24 wt%) with “ double yielding ” possess 
lower elongation and larger dimples than other alloys. The deduction of 
the plasticity for those two alloys should be related to the stress-induced 
martensitic transformation which caused the formation of a more 
martensitic α ’’ phase leading to enhanced work hardening response.

4. Conclusion 
In this work, the effect of Nb on the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of Ti-xNb-4Zr – 8Sn alloys is systematically studied, and the 
following conclusions are drawn: 
(1) The change of Nb content has a significant influence on the phase 
composition of the alloy. When the Nb content increases from 16 
wt% to 36 wt%, the phase composition of ST Ti-xNb-4Zr – 8Sn 
alloy changes from two-phase ( α ’’ + β ) to single-phase ( β ) 
composition. Namely, the volume content of the β phase in the 
alloy increases with the increase of Nb content. 
(2) The tensile properties of Ti-xNb-4Zr – 8Sn are strongly affected by 
the Nb content. “ Doule-yielding ” caused by the stress-induced 
martensitic transformation is observed for the alloys with the 
Nb contents of 20 wt% and 24 wt%. The ultimate tensile strength 
of the alloys generally decreases with the increase of Nb content, 
which should be attributed to the volume reduction of the α ’’ 
phase in the alloys. The Young ’ s modulus of Ti – 24Nb – 4Zr – 8Sn 
alloy is the lowest, which is mainly attributed to the decrease of 
α ’’ volume content and the stress-induced martensitic trans -
formation. Hence, among all the alloys evaluated, 
Ti – 24Nb – 4Zr – 8Sn alloy demonstrates the greatest potential to be 
used as biomedical material.
