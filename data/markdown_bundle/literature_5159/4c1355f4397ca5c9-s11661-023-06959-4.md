XIAOLI ZHUANG, STOICHKO ANTONOV, LONGFEI LI, and QIANG FENG
In order to accelerate the adoption of c¢-strengthened CoNi-based wrought superalloys in
engineering applications, two alloys that were previously designed using a framework
combining data from a multicomponent diﬀusion-multiple and machine learning. In this study,
we evaluated the comprehensive properties of these alloys, one with spherical c¢ morphology
and the other with cuboidal, including the alloy density, phase transformation temperatures,
microstructural stability, oxidation resistance and mechanical properties. The properties were
discussed with respect to alloying eﬀects and in regard to other CoNi-based and Ni-based
wrought superalloys. The results show that the designed alloys have relatively low density,
decent microstructural stability, good oxidation resistance and mechanical properties. This
study will provide guidance for further design and optimization of c¢-strengthened CoNi-based
wrought superalloys.

I. INTRODUCTION
IN 2006, Sato et al.[1] re-discovered the c/c¢ two-phase
microstructure in Co–Al–W-based alloys, and these
alloys were subsequently shown to possess higher
strength than traditional Co-based and Ni-based super-
alloys.
[2–5] Due to the instability of the c¢ phase in
Co–Al–W ternary alloys, [6,7] current multicomponent
CoNi-based superalloys have been gradually developed
to incorporate more Ni for improved microstructural
stability among other properties.
These c¢-strengthened CoNi-based superalloys, such
as CoWAlloy1 and CoWAlloy2, tend to have a higher c¢
volume fraction and a lower c¢ solvus temperature
compared to similar Ni-based wrought superalloys, e.g.
U720Li,
[8] which results in a good combination of higher
high-temperature strength and a wider hot processing
window. Therefore, it has been suggested that
c¢-strengthened CoNi-based superalloys may be suit-
able to be further developed as wrought superalloys.
Neumeier et al. ,
[8,9] Knop et al. [10,11] and Forsik
et al.[12,13] investigated and developed several multicom-
ponent CoNi-based wrought superalloys for engineering
applications. The alloys developed by Neumeier et al.[8,9]
exhibited lower compressive creep strain rate and
comparable oxidation resistance compared to those of
alloys Waspaloy and U720Li. The alloys developed by
Forsik et al.
[12,13] possessed oxidation resistance superior
to that of alloy Waspaloy, while the tensile strength and
creep property of the ﬁne-grained and coarse-grained
alloys were comparable to those of alloy U720. These
results suggest that CoNi-based wrought superalloys
could be potential alloys for future gas turbine applica-
tions, and further optimization of their comprehensive
properties, such as alloy density, microstructural stabil-
ity, oxidation resistance and mechanical properties, is
desired.
Recently, our group used a framework combining a
multicomponent diﬀusion-multiple and machine learn-
ing to design two multicomponent CoNi-based wrought
superalloys, namely alloys A1 and A2, with spherical
and cuboidal c¢ morphologies, respectively.
[14] The
design of alloy A1 with spherical c¢ morphology was
primarily to meet the requirements of good long-term
microstructural stability, since a spherical c¢ morphol-
ogy represents a low c/c¢ lattice misﬁt and interfacial
energy, which is beneﬁcial for controlling the c¢ precip-
itate coarsening rate.
[15–18] The design of alloy A2 with
cuboidal c¢ morphology was primarily aimed at the
service environment requiring high strength, as a

The average grain size of alloy A1 was about 6 lm,
while alloy A2 had slightly bigger gains of about 8 lm.
Samples with this recrystallized microstructure were be
used to evaluate the oxidation resistance and mechanical
properties in the following parts.
Figure 2 shows the densities and DSC heating curves
of alloys Al and A2. Alloys A1 and A2 have comparable
densities, which are lower than those reported for the
CoNi-based wrought superalloys CoWAlloy1 and
V208C as shown in Figure 2(a). However, alloys A1
and A2 have slightly higher densities compared to
Waspaloy and U720Li. The DSC heating curves in
Figure 2(b) show that alloy A1 has a lower c¢ solvus
temperature, but a higher solidus and liquidus temper-
atures, which are 1050 /C176 C, 1335 /C176 C and 1389 /C176 C,
respectively. Meanwhile, alloy A2 has a relatively higher
c¢ solvus temperature, but a relatively lower solidus and
liquidus temperatures, which are 1095 /C176 C, 1284 /C176 C and
1365 /C176 C, respectively. Thus, the hot processing windows
of alloys Al and A2 are 285 /C176 C and 189 /C176 C, and the
freezing ranges are 54 /C176 C and 81 /C176 C, respectively. The
above diﬀerences in phase transformation temperatures,
hot processing windows and freezing ranges are mainly
related to the contents of Ti, Ta and Nb in alloys Al and
A2. It has been shown that the addition of Ti, Ta and
Nb can signiﬁcantly increase the c

but decrease the solidus and liquidus temperatures. [20–23]

C. Partitioning Behavior
Figures 5 and 6 show the STEM-HAADF images and
the corresponding EDS maps of the c/c¢ two-phase
microstructures of alloys A1 and A2 after aging at
850 /C176 C for 1000 hours, respectively. The EDS maps
indicate that Co, Cr and Mo partition preferentially to
the c matrix, while Ni, Al, W, Ti, Ta and Nb partition to
the c¢ precipitates to diﬀerent extents. Since W and Ta,
which have higher atomic mass, partition to the c¢
precipitates, the c¢ precipitates show a bright contrast in
the STEM-HAADF images, while the c matrix shows a
dark gray contrast.
Figure 7 shows the composition proﬁles from the line
scans across the c/c¢ interfaces shown in Figures 5 and 6.
Alloy A1 has a higher Al content in both the c and c¢
phases compared to alloy A2, which is due to higher
nominal Al content in alloy A1 (Table I). Similarly,
alloy A2 has higher Ti, Ta and Nb contents in both the c
and c¢ phases, compared to alloy A1, due to its higher
nominal Ti, Ta and Nb contents. However, although
alloys A1 and A2 have the same content of Cr (14 at.
pct), the Cr content in their c and c¢ phases are diﬀerent.
The Cr content in the c and c¢ phases of alloy A1 are
about 20 and 5 at. pct, respectively, while it is about 25
and 7.5 at. pct, respectively, in alloy A2. The Cr content
in both the c and c¢ phases of alloy A2 is higher than
that in the respective phase of alloy A1, and is
particularly apparent for the c matrix. On one hand,
this may be related to the higher c¢ volume fraction of
alloy A2, which concentrates the Cr in the c matrix. On
the other hand, it was found that the c matrix of alloy
A1 also contains a relatively high Mo with the content
of about 3 at. pct. Therefore, the lower Cr content in the
c matrix of alloy A1 may also be attributed to the
addition of 2.5 at. pct Mo in the alloy A1.

IV. DISCUSSION
A. Physical Properties of the Investigated Alloys
Alloy density is an important factor for wrought
superalloys as lighter component weight is beneﬁcial in
terms of improving the fuel eﬃciency and decreasing the
cost of aero-engines.
[18] W (19.35 g/cm 3)a n dT a
(16.60 g/cm 3) are the heaviest elements typically alloyed
in wrought superalloys, and their nominal levels basi-
cally determine the density of the alloy. The total W and
Ta content in alloys A1, A2, V208C and CoWAlloy1 are
3.0, 3.5, 4.0 and 4.5 at. pct respectively, while alloys
Waspaloy and U720Li are not alloyed with W and Ta
(Table I). Therefore, alloys A1 and A2 exhibit lower
densities than those reported for alloys V208C and
CoWAlloy1, but higher than alloys Waspaloy and
U720Li (Figure 2(a)).
The phase transformation temperatures are also of
great importance to wrought superalloys, particularly
the c¢ solvus and c solidus temperatures, which
determine the hot processing windows of wrought
superalloys. Table III lists the phase transformation
temperatures and hot processing windows of alloys Al
and A2, as well as those of some CoNi-based and
Ni-based wrought superalloys for comparison. The c¢
solvus and solidus temperatures of alloys A1 and A2
are comparable to those of other reported CoNi-based
wrought superalloys, in which the c¢ solvus tempera-
tures are below 1100 /C176 C and the solidus temperatures
are about 1300 /C176 C or even higher. This allows
CoNi-based wrought superalloys, such as alloys A1,
CoWAlloy1, CoWAlloy2 and CoEXP, to have wide
hot processing windows of about 200 /C176 C to 350 /C176 C.
The c¢ solvus and solidus temperatures of alloy A1 are
similar to those of alloy Waspaloy, and hence they
have comparable hot processing windows. Meanwhile,
the hot processing window of alloy A2 is smaller than
that of alloy Waspaloy due to its higher c¢ solvus and
lower solidus temperatures. It should be pointed out
that alloy Waspaloy has a low c¢ volume fraction
(~ 22 pct at 650 /C176 C),
[18] while alloys A1 and A2 have a
much higher c¢ volume fraction (Figure 4(a)). In
comparison with alloys U720Li ( ~ 45 pct at
760 /C176 C)[30] and GH4151 ( ~ 50 pct at 800 /C176 C)[31] which
have equivalent c¢ volume fractions, alloys A1 and A2
have signiﬁcantly larger hot processing windows. This
enables good processability of these two alloys, while
maintaining a suﬃciently high c¢ volume fraction for
good creep performance.

two-phase microstructure after aging at 850 /C176 C for
1000 hours (Figure 3), the microstructural characteris-

morphology of alloy A1 was spherical, while that of
alloy A2 was cuboidal. The c¢ morphology is closely
related to the c/c¢ lattice misﬁt, d, which is determined by
the lattice parameters of the c and c¢ phases, and

[18] CoNi-based superalloys commonly have a
positive lattice misﬁt, that is, the lattice parameter of the
c¢ phase is bigger than that of the c phase.
[33] The
composition proﬁles across the c/c¢ interfaces of alloys
A1 and A2 in Figure 7 show that Ni, Al, W, Ti, Ta and
Nb are primarily enriched in the c¢ precipitates, while
Co, Cr and Mo are primarily enriched in the c matrix.
Therefore, it is reasonable to infer that the diﬀerence in
lattice parameters of the c/c¢ phases of alloy A2 will be
larger than that of alloy A1, since alloy A2 has higher
contents of Ti, Ta and Nb while alloy A1 has a higher
content of Mo. As a result, alloy A2 has a larger c/c¢
lattice misﬁt and exhibits a cuboidal c¢ morphology,
while alloy A1 has a lower c/c¢ lattice misﬁt and exhibits
spherical c¢ morphology.

the c¢ coarsening rate in CoNi-based superalloys.
C. Alloying Effects on the Oxidation Resistance
and Mechanical Properties
The diﬀerence in oxidation mass gains of alloys Al
and A2 and Waspaloy and U720Li is associated with the
compositions and oxide scales of the alloys. The
oxidation resistance of alloys Waspaloy and U720Li at
750 /C176 C are mainly attributed to the dense Cr
2O3 layer
due to their low Al and high Cr contents. [26] Meanwhile
the higher Al contents in alloys A1 and A2, as shown in
Table I, results in the formation of a continuous Al
2O3
layer at 750 /C176 C (Figure 9). A continuous Al 2O3 layer
oﬀers a better oxidation protection compared to a dense
Cr2O3 layer.[12,13,36] Therefore, alloys that can form a
continuous Al 2O3 layer generally have excellent
high-temperature oxidation resistance.
Furthermore, although both alloys A1 and A2 can
form continuous Al 2O3 layers and alloy A1 has a higher
Al content (9 at. pct), the oxidation resistance of alloy
A2 is better than that of alloy A1 (Figure 8). This is
associated with the higher Cr contents in the c and c¢
phases of alloy A2 (Figure 7), which is beneﬁcial to the
formation of dense Cr 2O3 layer faster, and a decrease of
the oxygen partial pressure in the substrate alloy A2.
This is of importance because it promoted an earlier
formation of a continuous Al
2O3 layer and improved the
oxidation resistance of alloy A2. [37] In addition, the
comparatively lower oxidation resistance of alloy A1
may be also related to its higher Mo content (2.5 at. pct).
Studies have shown that the addition of Mo is detri-
mental to the oxidation resistance of Co–Al–W-based
and Ni-based superalloys, which is associated with the

pct) and a lower Ti + Ta + Nb content (2.5 at. pct),

inferior to alloy A2 due to the addition of 2.5 at. pct
Mo. In the subsequent alloy design and optimization of
these alloys’ compositions, the content of Mo should be
appropriately reduced and the content of Cr can be
increased in order to further improve the long-term
microstructural stability as well as the oxidation and
corrosion resistance of alloy A1. In contrast, alloy A2
has a higher Ti + Ta + Nb content (5.0 at. pct) and no

properly increased on the basis of alloy A2 to further
improve the oxidation and corrosion resistance as well
as mechanical properties in the subsequent design and
optimization of alloy composition.
V. CONCLUSIONS
The physical properties (density and hot processing
window), microstructures, oxidation resistance, tensile
and creep properties of two c¢-strengthened CoNi-based
wrought superalloys, A1 and A2, with diﬀerent mor-
phologies, volume fractions and coarsening rates of c¢
phases were evaluated and the alloying eﬀects were
discussed. The following speciﬁc conclusions can be
drawn from the present work:
1. Alloys A1 and A2 have relatively low densities at
8.47 ± 0.01 and 8.43 ± 0.04 g/cm
3 respectively.
Compared to alloy A1 (285 /C176 C), alloy A2 (189 /C176 C)
has a smaller hot processing window due to its
higher Ti, Ta and Nb content.
2. Alloy Al containing 2.5 at. pct Mo has a spherical
morphology, a relatively low volume fraction ( ~ 46
pct) and a faster coarsening rate (863 ± 25 nm
3/
hour) of c¢ precipitate at 850 /C176 C. Meanwhile, alloy
A2 with higher Ti + Ta + Nb content has a
cuboidal morphology, a relatively high volume
fraction ( ~ 49 pct) and a slower coarsening rate
(330 ± 34 nm
3/hour) of c¢ precipitate at 850 /C176 C.
The addition of Mo is unfavorable in terms of
minimizing the c¢ coarsening rate, since Mo parti-
tions to the c phase and decreases the matrix Cr
content, which is the rate-limiting element for the c¢
coarsening rate in the investigated alloys.
3. The oxidation resistance of alloys A1 and A2 at
750 /C176 C is mainly provided by a continuous Al
2O3
layer, and is superior to that of alloys Waspaloy and
U720Li which are protected by a dense Cr 2O3 layer.
The oxidation resistance of alloy A2 is better than
that of alloy Al which has a higher Al content,
demonstrating that the addition of Mo is unfavor-
able to the oxidation resistance of CoNi-based
wrought superalloys.
4. The tensile and creep properties of alloy A1 are
superior to those of alloy Waspaloy and compara-
ble to those of alloy U720Li. Alloy A2 has superior
tensile and creep properties compared to both alloys
Waspaloy and U720Li due to its higher Ti + Ta +
Nb content.
