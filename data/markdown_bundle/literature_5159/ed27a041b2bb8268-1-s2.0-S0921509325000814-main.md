ABSTRACT
Lightweight high-entropy alloys (LHEAs) have garnered significant attention as potential solutions to the density 
and strength challenges posed by conventional single-principal element alloys. However, the content of 
aluminum, a resourceful and cost-effective element, has been limited in the design of LHEAs. In this study, we 
propose a high aluminum content Al
2
TiNbV LHEAs, and investigate the effect of annealing treatments at various 
temperatures (600 – 900 
◦
C) on the strength-plasticity trade-off. Notably, after annealing at 800 
◦
C for 1 h, the 
Al
2
TiNbV alloy exhibits an exceptional strength-plasticity synergy, achieving a compressive strength of 1758.5 
MPa and a plasticity of 6.6 %. This represents a substantial improvement over the equiatomic AlTiNbV LHEA, 
with a 19.1 % increase in yield strength and a 16.5 % reduction in density. Microstructural analysis reveals that 
the as-cast Al
2
TiNbV alloy possesses a dual-phase structure comprising BCC and L1
0 
phases, and the enhanced 
proportion of the refined lamellar L1
0 
phase after annealing at 800 
◦
C for 1 h contributes to the improved 
strength-plasticity trade-off. Furthermore, annealing at 900 
◦
C for 1 h is found to negatively impact the plasticity 
due to an increase in the proportion of the brittle σ phase. Overall, this study provides valuable insights into the 
development of advanced lightweight LHEAs with high-aluminum content.
1. Introduction
Reducing the density while enhancing strength is a paramount goal 
in the design of structural materials. Traditional metal alloys, relying on 
single or binary principal elements, have faced a challenge in breaking 
through inherent material strength limitations. In recent years, high- 
entropy alloys (HEAs) have emerged as a promising solution, featuring 
multi-principal elements in their composition. Among these, lightweight 
high-entropy alloys (LHEAs) have garnered significant attention due to 
their unique combination of low density, high strength, and exceptional 
high-temperature performance [ 1 – 7 ]. LHEAs are typically crafted from 
low-density metallic elements such as titanium (Ti), aluminum (Al), 
vanadium (V), and zirconium (Zr), and have evolved into a diverse array 
of alloys, including AlNbTiV, AlTiVCr, NbTiVZr, CrNbTiZr, and others 
[ 8 – 22 ].
Aluminum is an important metal due to its lightweight, abundance, 
and affordability. In the context of LHEAs, aluminum plays a pivotal role 
in enhancing the material ’ s properties. It is demonstrated that 
increasing the aluminum content in LHEAs can lead to significant 
improvements in material strength, both at room and high temperatures 
[ 21 – 24 ]. Xu et al. [ 22 ] showed that by raising the aluminum content in 
AlTiVNbMo alloys from 10 to 20 atomic percent (at%), there was a 
notable reduction in metal density, coupled with improved strength at 
both room and high temperatures. Similarly, Yurchenko et al. [ 23 ] 
observed an increase in both microhardness and high-temperature 
strength in AlCrNbTiVZr alloys as the aluminum content was incre -
mentally increased from 0 to 1 at%, which is attributed to the rise in the 
percentage of hexagonal phase within the body-centered cubic (BCC) 
matrix. Liao et al. [ 21 ] further confirmed the beneficial effects of 
aluminum on strength by demonstrating that in AlCrNbTiV LHEAs, an 
increase in aluminum concentration within the range of 12 – 18 at% led 
to strengthened material due to the precipitation of B2 nanoparticles. 
However, it is noteworthy that the amount of aluminum in LHEA designs 
is often constrained, typically not exceeding 25 at%. There remains a 
significant potential for exploring the effects of higher aluminum con -
tent in LHEAs, particularly in terms of its influence on further reducing 
density and enhancing material properties.

2. Method
In this study, two LHEAs with varying aluminum content were 
designed and investigated. The first alloy, denoted as AlTiNbV, had an 
equiatomic composition with a molar ratio of Al:Ti:Nb:V = 1:1:1:1. The 
second alloy, denoted as Al
2
TiNbV, had a higher Al content with a molar 
ratio of Al:Ti:Nb:V = 2:1:1:1. Theoretical density of high-aluminum 
Al
2
TiNbV is 4.74 g/cm
3 
which is less than the 5.52 g/cm
3 
of AlTiNbV. 
Both alloys were prepared using a magnetic levitation vacuum smelting 
furnace, employing 99.9 wt% pure metals of Al, Ti, Nb, and V. The ingots 
were remelted at least five times to ensure homogeneity and were then 
cast into a water-cooled copper cavity. The as-cast alloys were subjected 
to annealing treatments at various temperatures ranging from 600 
◦
C to 
900 
◦
C for 1 h each, followed by quenching in water.
Compression testing specimens with dimensions of ϕ 5.5 mm × 8.5 
mm were cut from the ingots using wire-cut electrical discharge 
machining (EDM). These specimens were then tested on a Zwickell 
tensile testing machine at room temperature, with a strain rate of 0.05 
s
 1
, to analyze their mechanical properties. The crystal structures of the 
alloys were investigated using X-ray diffraction (XRD) with Cu K α ra -
diation on a Bruker D8 discover diffractometer. The morphologies of the 
alloys were characterized using a Zeiss GeminiSEM 360 field emission 
scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped with electron back- 
scattering diffraction (EBSD) and energy-dispersive spectroscopy 
(EDS) spectrometers (Oxford Symmetry S2). Finally, the microstructure 
of the alloys was examined in detail using a Tecnai G2 F20 S-TWIN 
transmission electron microscope (TEM).

4. Conclusion
In this paper, the design of AlTiNbV lightweight high-entropy alloys 
(LHEAs) incorporating high aluminum content is investigated, with a 
focus on studying the annealing process for achieving a breakthrough in 
strength-plasticity synergy. Comprehensive compressive mechanical 
property tests are conducted under various conditions of aluminum 
content and annealing temperature. Subsequently, microstructural 
evolutions are analyzed based on XRD and TEM results. Ultimately, the 
mechanism underlying the improvement in strength-plasticity synergy 
with high aluminum content and specific annealing temperatures is 
elucidated using scanning electron microscopy coupled with SEM/ 
EBSD. The main conclusions are as follows.
