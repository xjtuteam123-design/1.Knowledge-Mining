based alloys simultaneously posse ssing high strength properties at operating temper atures and high resistance to marine salt 
corrosion. The paper presents the results of work on creation of a new high-strength nickel-based alloy which exhibits both a high 
level of heat resistance typical for aviation alloys (

Complexity of the problem lies in that heat-resistant ni ckel alloys applied for conventional aviation gas-turbine 
engines and possessing high strength properties at (900-1100)°С lack resistance to sea-water salt corrosion, and even 
application of protective coatings does not ensure the required operating life and deteriorate the operating conditions 
of marine gas-turbine units (GTU) [1]. 
One of the most important tasks in this sphere is development of heat-resistant nickel-based alloys 
simultaneously possessing high strength properties under operating temperatures and high resistance to sea salt 
corrosion. 
The main difficulty is that chromium en suring resistance of nickel alloys to salt corrosion affects heat resistance.

Second, the impact of W, Re and Ta on the value and nature of corrosion damages caused by sea salt has not 
been sufficiently studied. 
Thirdly, W, Re and Ta are ‘heavy’ elements and increase of their content in the alloy results in the alloy density 
increase, which imposes additional requirements on the turbine disks strength properties. 
Fourthly, increase of the Ta content in the alloy above th e definite value results in precipitation of the plate-like 
η-phases of Ni3(Ti, Та) type from the γ'-phase on the basis of Ni 3(Al, Ti), which also significantly reduce the alloy 
strength. 
Therefore, solving the mentioned problems can be achieved by optimization of models which link the chemical 
compositions of the alloys with the strength, physical -chemical, structural, therm odynamical and other factors 
defining the levels of their operational performance (including the environmental loads). 
Herewith most important is accuracy and dependability of the created models, since they directly affect the alloy 
levels and quality of newly developed alloys.

Preliminary examinations of corrosion effe ct in the molten salt mixture (10%NaCl+90%Na 2SO4) at 900°С show

Fig. 4. Mass variation of the specimen in molten salt 10% NaCl + 90% Na2SO4 at 900°С 
The tests were conducted simultaneously on the specimens of SLGS-5.1, 5.2 and 5.3, and ChS70 and ChS88 
alloys with equiaxed structure. Detailed examination of the salt a ttack traces after exposure over 200 hours showed 
that there were neither damages nor erosion on the surface of the specimens (Fig. 5). At the same time a rather deep 
salt penetration in the intergranular channels was fo und, e.g. up to 0,9 mm deep down the ChS88 and ChS70 
specimens. Such channels appear between the crystal grains with the material polycrystalline structure. However, 
these channels did not increase under the influence of salt. Therefore, we can say that th e crystals themselves have 
high resistance to sulfide corrosion.

The developed alloy possesses the pe rfect structure, i.e. fine cubic γ'-phase precipitates and absence of TCP 
brittle formations, which are not formed either during melting or under the influence of high-temperature rupture 
stresses. 
The new alloy features a higher heat-re sistance level and rather high sulf ide corrosion resistance compared to 
alloys of similar application. It is also characterized by an efficient structure, which ensures conditions for 
elimination of the TCP brittle phases formation in unloading and complex high-temperature loading conditions.
