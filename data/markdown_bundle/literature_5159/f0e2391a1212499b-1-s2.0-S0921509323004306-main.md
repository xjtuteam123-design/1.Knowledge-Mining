1. Introduction 
Aerospace, the equipment field for lightweight demand is more and 
more urgent. As the most promising lightweight material, magnesium 
(Mg) alloy has the advantages of low density, high specific strength, 
excellent vibration damping, and electromagnetic shielding perfor -
mance [ 1 – 6 ]. As the saying goes, a generation of equipment, a genera -
tion of materials. Aerospace, the field of equipment required by the 
high-strength Mg alloy service environment, is diverse, which puts for -
ward higher requirements for the strength of Mg alloy, especially 
elevated temperature (ET) strength [ 1 – 4 ]. Therefore, how to obtain 
excellent ET strength is the current barrier for Mg alloys to achieve 
further industrial applications. 
Rare earth (RE) Mg system alloy has become the leading research 
object for developing heat-resistant Mg alloy, mainly due to the low 
diffusion ability of RE elements, which can delay the recrystallization 
process to achieve the effect of grain refinement [ 7 – 9 ]. At the same time, 
the Mg-RE system alloy can form heat-resistant second phases. Studies 
have shown that RE elements such as Gd and Y can produce significant 
solid-solution strengthening and ageing strengthening effects [ 7 , 10 – 12 ].

resulting in excellent RT/ET mechanical properties. At the same time, 
the cast alloy is extruded and aged to take full advantage of the 
contribution of grain refinement and second phase RT/ET mechanical 
properties. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Materials preparation 
A high-frequency induction melting furnace was used to prepare the 
alloy required for the experiments. Firstly, pure Mg (99.8%), Mg – 30Y 
(99.9%) intermediate alloy, Mg-30Gd (99.9%) intermediate alloy, and 
pure Sn (99.9%) were cut into suitable sizes, and the surface of the alloy 
was polished to remove surface impurities and oxidation layers. Then 
the raw material was put into the high-frequency induction melting 
furnace, and the CO

spectrometer (XRF-1800 CCDE), as shown in Table 1 . Mg-12Gd-3Y is
