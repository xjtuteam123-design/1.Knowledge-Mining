1. Introduction 
The development of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) has initiated a 
brand-new cognition to design high-performing materials [1 – 4] . Despite 
the complex composition, high configurational entropy of HEAs nor -
mally contributes to stabilize solid solutions rather than harmful inter -
metallic phases, which, consequently, endows these types of alloys with 
tunable properties [5 – 11] . As a subgroup of multi-component systems, 
refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) based on high-melting-point 
elements has garnered increasing attention for their great potential to 
be promising candidates for elevated-temperature applications on ac -
count of the desirable resistance to softening [12 – 17] . Specifically, the 
classical MoNbTaW and MoNbTaVW RHEAs with single-phase solid 
solution of body-centered-cubic (BCC) structure retain ultrahigh yield 
strength above 400 MPa up to 1600 ℃ which greatly surpasses the 
service temperatures of conventional Nickel-based superalloys [18] . 
Nevertheless, insufficient ambient-temperature ductility faced by a 
majority of RHEAs remains one of the practical challenges for industrial 
applications [18 – 20] . To date, considerable efforts have been made to 
surmount the strength-ductility trade-off of RHEAs via tuning chemical 
compositions and microstructures [21 – 27] . For instance, Lei et al. pro -
posed a novel strategy to enhance strength and ductility simultaneously 
by forming ordered oxygen complexes in the oxygen-doped TiZrHfNb 
RHEA [28] . The metastability-engineering strategy was utilized to 
achieve an outstanding combination of strength and ductility in

In order to elucidate the hot deformed features, microstructures after 
compression tests at 800 ℃ and 1000 ℃ are characterized. As can be 
seen from XRD pattern in Fig. 6 , the alloy maintains a single-phase BCC 
crystal structure after compression at 1000 ℃ , which implies the 
absence of phase transformation during deformation. The EBSD analysis 
was also conducted to obtain deformed microstructures after compres -
sion at elevated temperatures. From the inverse pole figure (IPF) maps in 
Fig. 7 (a) and (d), initial grains are elongated perpendicular to the 
compression axis (CA), ˂ 001 ˃ and ˂ 101 ˃ fiber textures can be

severely elongated grains, exhibiting a salient characteristic of dynamic 
recrystallization (DRX) [53] . Fig. 7 (a) and (d) provide the grain orien -
tation spread (GOS) maps of hot deformed specimens. Note that the 
grains with small values of GOS, blue region in GOS maps, are consid -
ered to be recrystallized grains. It is evident that the fraction of recrys -
tallized grains increases with increasing of deformed temperature, 
which indicates a greater degree of DRX. Fig. 7 (c) and (f) show the grain 
boundary (GB) maps, where black and red lines represent the high-angle 
grain boundaries (HAGBs) and the low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs), 
respectively. As can be seen from Fig. 7 (c), a mass of LAGBs are observed 
along the initial grain boundaries, indicating the accumulation of dis -
locations during deformation. The fraction of LAGBs decreases from 
74.8 % for 800 ℃ to 56.8 % for 1000 ℃ , because DRX consumes some 
dislocations to form recrystallized grains with HAGBs. As a whole, the 
decrease in yield strength of the alloy at elevated temperatures is mainly 
attributed to the DRX during compression.
