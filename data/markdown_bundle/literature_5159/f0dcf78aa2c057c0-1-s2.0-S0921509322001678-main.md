ABSTRACT 
In this study, the Al
0.3
CoCrFeNi high entropy alloy (HEA) with various heterogeneous microstructures was 
designed and fabricated through different process histories, such as severely cold-rolled plus annealing at low 
temperature for a long time (S1), cryo-rolled plus annealing at intermediate temperature (S2), cold-rolled plus 
annealing at intermediate temperature (S3). The microstructures were analyzed by SEM, EBSD, and TEM. The 
results showed that the heterogeneous grain structure of S1 alloy was composed of stretched original grains and 
fine recrystallized grains. Besides, the L1
2
-(Ni, Co)
3
Al type phase was also observed within the face-centered 
cubic (FCC) matrix of the S1 alloy. However, the S2 and S3 alloys consisted of stretched original grains, sur -
vived original grains, fully recrystallized equiaxed grains, and B
2 
precipitates. Moreover, a mount of nano- 
deformation twins was also incorporated in the matrix of S2 alloy. Furthermore, the S2 alloy exhibited a su -
perior balance of high ultimate tensile strength of ~1019 MPa and high elongation of ~28% as compared to S1 
and S3 alloys due to its complex heterogeneous microstructure. This research would provide guidance for 
designing the heterogeneous structure in the FCC HEA and facilitate establishing the relationships between the 
heterogeneous structure and mechanical properties. 
1. Introduction 
In recent years, with the development of new industries and tech -
nology, more and more novel metallic structural materials that possess 
excellent comprehensive performance such as high strength and high 
toughness are required. In order to achieve this feature, many new alloys 
are being engineered and fabricated by changing their composition and 
processing history [ 1 ]. Generally, the traditional alloys based on one or 
two principal elements have been further used and developed through 
the addition of other minor elements containing one or more elements 
[ 2 ]. Moreover, the atomic fraction of each minor elements is less than 5 
at.%. Recently, a novel alloy, i.e., high entropy alloys (HEAs) and me -
dium entropy alloys (MEAs) are designed which are based on three or 
more principal elements with equimolar or near-equimolar atomic 
fractions, each with concentrations between 5 and 35 at% [ 3 ], this 
strategy is different from a design concept of the conventional 
multi-component alloys [ 4 , 5 ]. These HEAs and MEAs have attracted 
many researchers due to their unique properties, such as superior 
fracture toughness [ 6 ], remarkable cryogenic temperature ultimate 
strength-ductility synergy [ 7 ], excellent anti-oxidation and superior 
wear resistances et al. [ 8 ]. In addition, most of the HEAs and MEAs are 
generally simple solid solutions, i.e., face-centered cubic (FCC), 
body-centered cubic (BCC) crystal structures, or both, rather than 
complex intermetallic compounds phases [ 9 – 12 ], in which the FCC-type 
HEAs have been widely studied for academic research and engineering 
applications. 
However, these FCC-type HEAs that have a fully recrystallized 
microstructure exhibit a good ductility but low strength owing to their 
structural characteristics [ 13 , 14 ]. To improve their low yield strength, 
an effective conventional strengthening mechanisms such as solid so -
lution strengthening, precipitation hardening, dislocation hardening, 
and grain boundary strengthening are incorporated, but the ductility of 
alloys is decreased [ 15 , 16 ]. Recently, a number of design strategies such 
as new alloys, processing, and tailored microstructure are implemented 
to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off of different metals and al -
loys [ 16 – 18 ]. Among them, it is noted that heterogeneous alloy

Base on the above results of S1 alloy, it should be mentioned that the 
three stages in the deformation process for S1 alloy [ 24 ]. At the initial 
stage of deformation (Stage I), elastic deformation occurs in all domains. 
With increasing deformation, the dislocation ship occurs preferentially 
in non-recrystallized grains because a higher pre-existed dislocations 
can provide more nucleation sites for the multiplication of dislocation 
[ 45 ]. With further increasing strain (Stage II), a strain incompatibility at 
the interface between recrystallized and non-recrystallized grains in -
creases. Thus, to maintain the continuity of strain, GNDs are blocked and 
pile up at GBs, and thereby, back stress is enhanced and consequently 
high strength. However, the hard recrystallized domains with a volume 
fraction of 30% are embedded in a soft matrix, the weak strain parti -
tioning during deformation Stage III renders low strain hardening, 
which leads to low ductility. Moreover, the strain localization and stress 
concentration at the interface may be lead to the formation of a void at 
the interface, and thereby, accelerating the fracture of the alloy 
( Fig. 10 a – d). These results further demonstrate the importance of 
reasonable segmentation in both hard and soft domains for hetero -
structured materials (HSMs). That is to say, if the strength difference in 
both hard and soft domains is too large or too small, additional HDI 
stress cannot achieve a remarkable combination of strength and 
ductility. Therefore, it is a reasonable deduction that the hard domains 
constrained by soft domains have lower ductility than the unconstrained 
soft and hard domains in HSMs.
