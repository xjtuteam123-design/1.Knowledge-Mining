INTERCONNECTION BETWEEN SHORT-TERM STRENGTH, 
STATIC STRESS-RUPTURE STRENGTH, AND CREEP RESISTANCE 
OF TUNGSTEN, MOLYBDENUM, AND ALLOYS BASED ON THEM 
V. V. Bukhanovskii 
UDC 539.4 
Comprehensive analysis of experimental data for the high-temperature mechanical properties of commercial 
puri~ tungsten, molybdenum, and their alloys with solid solution, dispersion, and mixed hardening prepared 
by powder metallurgy with different forms of uniaxial tension is provided. It was established that for materials 
of this class at high temperature (0.5-0.8 Tin) there is a close correlation between short-term and static stress- 
rupture strength, and creep resistance, which is described by unified functional dependences that are common 
for all of the metals and alloys studied. 
Refractory alloys based on tungsten and molybdenum prepared by powder metallurgy are used in order to prepare 
articles and structural elements for power installations that operate under high temperature conditions, the action of corrosive 
gas atmospheres, and erosive wear. These materials exhibit a unique set of physicomechanical and operating properties that 
combine high heat resistance, erosion and corrosion resistance with satisfactory ductility and technological efficiency during 
machining [1-3]. 
Extensive experimental material has been accumulated for the strength and failure of these refractory metals and 
alloys under short-term and prolonged static tension over a wide temperature range. It has been established that the 
temperature dependences tbr strength and ductility characteristics of these materials and the nature of their failure under the 
loading conditions studied are qualitatively similar [4-9]. 
Information available about the mechanical properties of tungsten, molybdenum, and their alloys make it possible to 
establish a correlation between the short-term, static stress-rapture, and creep resistance of this class of materials. As a rule 
these relationships are entirely described by specific functional dependences based on both empirically and physically 
substantiated approaches that make it possible to perform a rapid evaluation of material heat resistance characteristics with 
minimum expenditure [ 10, 11 ]. 
Approaches used in treating the data obtained were based on the assumption that in the specific temperature-force 
ranges the strength of metals and alloys is controlled by the same fundamental mechanisms of plastic deformation and failure. 
Then the effect of the temperature factor on the intensity of material loss of strength under short-term and static stress-rapture 
loading conditions appears to be at the same level. Experimental data were presented in the form of curves specifying the 
dependence of secondary creep rate k and time to failure zf for a material at the test temperatures on the ratio of applied 
stresses ~ to material ultimate strength at the corresponding temperature ~tt- [ 12]. 
In the mathematical treatment data were used for short-term strength, static stress-rupture strength (base 24 h) and 
instantaneous creep in the range 1770-2770 K for commercial purity tungsten, molybdenum, and alloys based on them with 
solid-solution, dispersion, and mixed hardening. The alloys W - HfC - HfO2, W - Y203 - HfO2, W - Y203 - HfO2- Cu, 
Mo - 30% W, and Mo - 30% W - NbC - C were prep.ared by powder metallurgy. The average particle diameter in 
dispersion-hardened alloys was 0.8-2.0 gm (hafnium and yttrium oxides) and 0.03-0.05 [am (hafnium and niobium carbides). 
The level of material deformation E in relation to the form of semifinished product was within the limits from 50 to 90%. 
Short-term strength and ductility characteristics for the metals and alloys in the high-temperature region are provided 
in Tables 1 and 2. The ultimate strength and nominal yield point at similar temperatures differs by a factor from 1.8 to 5.5. 
The strength of the alloys depends not only on chemical composition, but also on the level of semifinished product 
deformation.

Thus, in the high-temperature region for tungsten, molybdenum, and alloys based on them and prepared by powder 
metallurgy there is a close correlation between creep resistance, short-term and static stress-rupture strengths. These are 
described by functional dependences that are common for all materials of this class. In view of the fact that the dependences 
in question embrace both pure metals and also their alloys with solid-solution, dispersion, and mixed hardening, the practical 
importance of the results obtained is obvious for rapid evaluation of the heat resistance characteristics of well-known and also 
new alloys in the process of their creation and preparation technology.
