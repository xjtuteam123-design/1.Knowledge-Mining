INTRODUCTION
The alloys based on the Fe–Cr–Co system that are
alloyed with tungsten and gallium represent a new
direction in the development of hard magnetic mate-
rials that have high strength comparable to high-
strength maraging steels and simultaneously possess a
reserve of plasticity sufficient for preventing the brittle
character of failure, which is of prime importance
when using these materials for rotors of high-speed
hysteresis motors with rotation speeds of more than
100–150000 rpm [1].
As a rule, the high-coercivity state in the alloys of
this system is obtained due to the precipitation decom-
position of the high-temperature 
α-Fe–based solid
solution with the formation of a modulated structure
consisting of a strong magnetic phase enriched in
FeCo ( α1) and a weak magnetic phase enriched in
FeCr (α2) [2].
In our previous work [3], we performed a prelimi-
nary investigation of Fe–Cr–Co alloys alloyed with
tungsten and gallium after a treatment that results in
enhanced mechanical properties; in the course of this
study , the structure of a high-temperature state was
identified, which to a significant extent determines
processes of the formation of postdeformation struc-
tures. In [3], some factors leading to the high-coerciv-
ity state of an alloy possessing the required strength
and plasticity were also considered.
In this work, we investigated structural transforma-
tions occurring at different stages of treatment of the
alloys in more detail, using various methods. The pur-
pose of this work was to clarify the role of alloying ele-
ments and cold plastic deformation preceding low-
temperature aging in the formation of magnetic and
mechanical properties of these alloys.
EXPERIMENTAL
The alloys were melted in an induction furnace in
an argon atmosphere from pure charge materials (with
a purity no less than 99.95%). After homogenization at
1200°С for 2 h, the ingots were hot-forged at 1100–
1150°. The hot-rolled sheets were quenched from
1150°С for α solid solution, and then the alloys were
subjected to cold rolling with deformations to 60%. The
heat treatment for the high-coercivity state was per-
formed at temperatures characteristic of the alloys of
this type [4], i.e., at 
620–640°С for 20–45 min and
then at 600°С for 30–60 min. For comparison, the
samples that did not undergo cold rolling were also
heat-treated.
The mechanical properties (the yield stress σ0.2 and
the maximum relative elongation δ) were determined
from the stress–strain diagrams measured using stan-
dard samples on a UME-10TM testing machine. The
magnetic characteristics were measured on a vibrat-
ing-sample magnetometer. The phase composition of
the samples was determined by X-ray diffraction; since
the samples aged immediately after quenching from
1150°C were coarse-grained, their oscillating-crystal
X-ray patterns were recorded on a film.
The electron-microscopic and electron-diffraction
investigations were conducted using a JEM-200CX
microscope. The foils were prepared by electrolytic thin-
ning in a mixture of chromium anhydride and ortho-
phosphoric acid. The Mössbauer spectra were measured
at 300 K using the regime of constant accelerations
with a

Abstract—Processes leading to a high-coercivity state of W and Ga containing Fe–Cr–Co alloys that possess
a required strength and plasticity have been investigated. The effect of W and Ga additions on the structural
constituents formed due to the decomposition of a high -temperature solid solution has been studied by the
methods of X-ray diffraction, electron microscopy , and Mössbauer spectroscopy . The role of cold deforma-
tion preceding low-temperature annealings has been clarified.
Key words: structure, mechanical properties, magnetic properties

1. Magnetic and Mechanical Properties
Table 1 contains the results of measurements of
magnetic and mechanical properties of Fe–22 wt %
Cr–15 wt % Co alloys with additions of W and Ga
after quenching, cold deformation, and subsequent
aging. It is seen that introduction of 9 wt % W into the
alloy leads to a sharp enhancement in both the magnetic
(
Hc = 100 A/cm) and mechanical ( σ0.2 = 1450 MPa)
characteristics, as compared to those of the alloy with-
out alloying additions. Simultaneously , the magnitude of
the relative elongation abruptly decreases to 1–1.5%,
which results in the brittle failure of the samples as the
loads increase.
When even as little as 0.5 wt % Ga is added into the
alloy , its plastic properties improve significantly; a
threefold increase in the relative elongation of the
alloy is observed. A further enhancement in the gal-
lium content up to 4 wt % is accompanied by a gradual
deterioration of the plastic properties and a drop in the
coercive force.
2. X-ray Diffraction Analysis
After quenching from 1150°С, the high-tempera-
ture α-Fe–based solid solution was the only phase in
all the alloys except for the alloy containing 0.5 wt %
Ga, in which a γ-Fe–based solid solution was present as
well. The lattice parameter of the α phase in the ternary
Fe–Cr–Co alloy was 2.870 Å; the addition of W was
accompanied by its enhancement to 2.882–2.883 Å.
The lattice parameter of the γ phase was 3.606–3.609 Å.
The samples aged after quenching from 1150°С
without preliminary deformation were coarse-grained
(100–200 μm). In the X-ray diffraction patterns taken
from them, reflections of an “average lattice” of the
α-phase with satellites were observed, which points to
the formation of a modulated structure in the regions
of this phase. The lattice parameter of the “average lat-
tice” was equal to 2.880–2.881 Å.
The cold rolling of the samples quenched from
1150°С led to a strong grain refinement and a sharp
drop in the amount of the γ phase or even to its com-
plete disappearance . No formation of new phases was
noted. A subsequent aging of alloys containing 
W was
accompanied by a decrease in the lattice parameter of
the α phase to 2.874–2.876 Å; in the X-ray diffraction
patterns, the lines of this phase were very broad, and
no satellites were found near them .

tungsten subjected to postdeformation aging, weak
lines of an unknown phase were also present. Figure 1

3. Electron-Microscopic Investigation
Figure 2 shows bright-field electron micrographs of
the structure of the samples of an Fe–22Cr–15Co–
9W–3Ga alloy in two states: aged immediately after
quenching from 1150° C (Fig. 2a) and subjected to
cold deformation with subsequent aging (Fig. 2b). In
Fig. 2a, there is seen a modulated structure with mod-
ulation along the <100> directions of the reciprocal
lattice, with a modulation period of ≈(40–50) nm. Fig-
ure 2b shows that in the cold-rolled samples the high-
temperature solid solution underwent decomposition
without the appearance of a well-formed modulated
structure.
In the insets containing electron diffraction pat-
terns in Fig. 2, we find, along with reflections of the
α-phase, reflections of the above-mentioned
unknown phase; one of them is enclosed in a circle.
The dark-field image obtained in this reflection
(Fig. 2c) shows that this phase exists as nanoparticles
(50–100 nm). The reflections of this phase in the elec-
tron diffraction patterns occupy definite positions
with respect to the reflections of the α-phase, which
indicates the existence of an orientation relationship
between the lattices of these two phases. In the electron-
micrographs of the samples aged immediately after
quenching from 1150°C, there are also observed parti-
cles of an unknown phase (dark particles in Fig. 2a).

It was of interest to trace changes in the spectral
contributions from structural constituents depending
on the alloy composition; the results of such an inves-
tigation are shown in Fig. 5. It is seen that the ternary
alloy contains a strong magnetic phase and a weak
magnetic phases. After introduction of W, regions cor-
responding to the spectral contribution in close-to-
zero fields are formed. An analysis showed that this
contribution is connected with a paramagnetic dou-
blet. The shape of the high-field distribution becomes
more distinct, which indicates a deeper phase separa-

leads to an increase in the intensity of contributions in
a field range of 200–300 kOe.
It follows from Fig. 5 that in the distribution P(H)
for an Fe–22Cr–15Co–9W alloy , we can distinguish
three contributions related to the high-field distribu-
tion, one contribution related to the low-field distri-
bution, and a nonmagnetic contribution. Accordingly ,
we carried out a resolution of the spectra of all alloys
into four sextets and one doublet in terms of the direct
problem (Fig. 6). Then, we subtracted the low-field
and nonmagnetic contributions from the spectra of
the alloys and analyzed the remained spectrum in the
P(H) representation. The results are shown in Fig. 7.
Structural changes in the regions corresponding to this
range of hyperfine fields are well seen. When the Ga
content in the alloy increases, the intensity of the peak
with the maximum field is reduced; this peak is related
to the configuration of a surroundings of iron atoms
that have no other impurities except for cobalt in the
first coordination shell; the position of this peak is
shifted toward lower fields; the width of the distribu-
tion grows at the expense of the enhancement in the
intensity of the lower-field part of the distribution. It
ma y be said that the strong magnetic phase becomes
more inhomogeneous in composition.

DISCUSSION
(1) All alloys aged immediately after quenching from
1150°С were very coarse-grained; the X-ray diffraction
analysis and microstructural investigations (Fig. 2a)
showed that, due to the decomposition of the high-tem-
perature homogeneous solid solution, in these alloys
there forms a modulated structure consisting of coher-
ently connected regions of a strong magnetic α1-phase
enriched in FeCo and a weak magnetic α2-phase
enriched in FeCr, just in the same way as in the previ-
ously known alloys treated using the conventional tech-
nology (quenching from high temperature + aging) [2].
A cold rolling after quenching from 1150°C leads to
grain refinement and a sharp drop in the amount of the
γ phase or even to its complete disappearance. The
microstructure (Fig. 2b) and the shape of the distribu-
tion P(H) (see Figs. 3–5) indicate that during subse-
quent aging the high-temperature solid solution like-
wise decomposes into α1 and α2 phases. Y et, no mod-
ulated structure is formed. It seems likely that upon
aging of the cold-rolled samples containing numerous
defects, chromium diffuses rapidly into regions adja-
cent to defects; in the gaps between these regions,
there remain regions enriched in iron and cobalt.
Thus, the morphology formed is caused by the spatial
arrangement of existing defects. Owing to the faster
diffusion, in the cold-rolled samples there takes place
a deeper decomposition of the high-temperature solid
solution as compared to the samples aged immediately
after quenching from 1150°С (see Fig. 4).
The P(H) distributions (see Figs. 4–6) show that
compositionally both phases are inhomogeneous; this
is consistent with a corresponding spatial nonunifor-
mity of lattice-parameter magnitudes, which brings
about a broadening of diffraction lines. Since the dif-
ference in the lattice parameters between α1-(Fe–Co)
and α2-(Fe–Cr) is not very large [8], the peaks in the
X-ray diffraction patterns are not resolved and merge
into one wide peak.
(2) In the alloys with W during annealings at 640–
600° there forms a dispersed phase enriched in tung-
sten. This nanophase can serve as an obstacle for
motion of dislocations and lead to a strengthening of
the alloy , increasing stresses, and, hence, to an
enhancement in the contribution of elastic energy to
the high-coercivity state. The magnitude of the rela-
tive elongation in this case, however, is strongly
reduced.
Since in aged alloys no γ phase is found and the
Mössbauer spectra contain a weak paramagnetic peak,
it may be assumed that this peak belongs to the phase
under discussion. The values of the isomeric shift of
the paramagnetic phase agree with this assumption. As
is seen from Table 2, its amount is independent of the
Ga content and it is only as little as about 4% Fe that
falls onto this phase; in the X-ray diffraction pattern
(see Fig. 1), the peaks belonging to this phase likewise
are very weak.
The composition and crystal structure of the tung-
sten-rich phase has not been established to date. Tak-
ing into account the positions of the peaks of this
phase in the X-ray diffraction patterns and the mutual
arrangement of its reflections in the electron diffrac-
tion patterns, it may be assumed that this phase has a
tetragonal lattice with lattice parameters a = 5.35 Å
and c = 4.28 Å. However, no data on the existence of a
compound with such a crystal structure in alloys con-
sisting of W , Fe(Cr, Co) are now available.
The dispersed phase is easily formed during aging
of the samples subjected to cold deformation, but its
formation is hindered upon aging immediately after
quenching from 1150°C. This phase appears to easily
nucleate at numerous defects and is uniformly distrib-
uted in the bulk of the samples. Thus, one more

(3) The processing of the Mössbauer spectra made
it possible to perform a comparative analysis of the
effect of Ga on the phase compositions. The data given
in Table 2 show within the error that an increase in the
Ga content in the alloy leads to an increase in the frac-
tion of iron in the strong magnetic phase and its
decrease in the weak magnetic phase with a simulta-
neous enhancement in the Cr content in the strong
magnetic phase. This means that gallium hinders dif-
fusion of chromium from the strong magnetic phase.
The reason for this can be the fact that Ga forms cova-
lent–metallic bonds in the alloys [9], which hampers
diffusion of atoms. It is seen from Table 2 that with an
increase in the gallium content in the alloy the amount
of chromium in the strong magnetic phase grows faster
than the amount of iron does. Consequently , the
strong magnetic phase is enriched in chromium. This
results in a decrease in its specific saturation magneti-
zation (see Table 1).
(4) It is seen from Table 1 that the alloy with 0.5 wt %
Ga possesses the best plastic properties. Although the
Ga content in the alloy is small, its effect on the plastic
properties is great. It may be assumed that its atoms are
nonuniformly distributed over the volume; they can,
for example, be accumulated in peripheral regions of
precipitates, decorating them, which improves the
alloy plasticity . With an increase in the concentration
of Ga, it is possible that this element forms segregates
and holds more chromium in the strong magnetic
phases, which leads to a deterioration of the plasticity
and magnetic properties.
CONCLUSIONS
(1) In the Fe–22Cr–15Co–9W–(0–4)Ga alloys
during annealings at 640–600°С the high-temperature
solid solution decomposes into three phases: an
α1 phase enriched in Fe and Co; an α2 phase enriched
in Fe and Cr; and a W-rich paramagnetic phase. The
first two phases yield a high-coercivity state. The
W-rich phase is dispersed and can serve as an obstacle
for motion of dislocations, thereby favoring strength-
ening of the alloy , increasing stresses, and, hence,
enhancing contribution of elastic energy to the high-
coercivity state.
(2) Cold rolling of samples preceding low-tempera-
ture annealings reduces the amount of the γ phase to its
complete disappearance and refines grains in the alloys.
Owing to numerous deformation-induced defects, dif-
fusion is accelerated, which favors an enhancement in
the decomposition into the α1 and α2 phases (without
the appearance of a well-formed modulated structure)
and the formation of a large amount of a tungsten-rich
nanophase.
(3) It has been established that, although the Ga
content in the alloy is small, its influence on the plastic
properties is significant. The mechanism of this influ-
ence has not been clarified in detail; it was assumed
that Ga atoms are nonuniformly distributed over the
volume; they can, for example, be localized in periph-
eral regions of precipitates, decorating them, which
improves the plasticity .
