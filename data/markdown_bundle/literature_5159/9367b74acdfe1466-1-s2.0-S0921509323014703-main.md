ABSTRACT 
Metallic materials with high mechanical performance are in constant demand by various engineering applica -
tions. However, most conventional strengthening mechanisms inevitably suffer from fiercely sacrificing ductility, 
which severely limits service safety of metallic alloys, especially under various harsh circumstances. In this work, 
a novel ultra-strong (CoCrNi)
94.5
W
3
Ta
2.5 
medium entropy alloy (MEA) with dual nanoprecipitates is successfully 
developed. By conducting multiple thermomechanical processes, not only quantities of coherent γ " nano -
precipitates with D0
22 
superlattice are introduced, but also massive semi-coherent η nanoprecipitates with 
hexagonal D0
24 
superlattice are reconstructed with appropriate sizes and distributions, meanwhile ultrafine 
grains are achieved, together enabling superior strength-ductility synergies. This MEA exhibits not only high 
tensile strength of about 1.7 GPa and ultimate elongation of 23.8 % at room temperature, but also ultra-high 
tensile strength of about 2.2 GPa and still sufficient ultimate elongation of 13.2 % at cryogenic temperature. 
In-depth microstructure characterization indicates that the nanoprecipitates are effective barriers to dislocation 
motion at the early stages of plastic deformation. Intriguingly, the slip and deformation twinning could be 
transmitted across the phase boundaries with increasing applied strain, ensuring both strength enhancement and 
plasticity maintenance. At cryogenic temperature, much more complex deformation mechanisms are activated, e. 
g., multiple slip systems and high-density deformation twins, facilitating even better mechanical properties. The 
design concept of strengthening materials via tailoring dual coherent nanoprecipitates may afford a paradigm to 
develop advanced metallic materials with ultrahigh strength. 
1. Introduction 
High-strength metallic materials are always highly desirable for 
modern engineering applications, not only to save material cost, but also 
to provide adequate supports for most challenged structural applica -
tions, such as hypersonic aircraft, deep sea probe, aerospace industry 
and so on. During past decades, the emerging category of high entropy 
alloys (HEAs) or complex concentrated alloys (CCAs) has attracted a 
constant upsurge [ 1 – 18 ]. The unique design concepts, i.e., material 
systems with multiple principal elements in equi-molar or 
near-equimolar ratio, open huge compositional space to modulate me -
chanical properties of structural materials [ 19 – 21 ], especially under 
various harsh circumstances, which broaden our horizons to develop 
advanced metallic alloys. So far, there have been several HEAs with 
unique structural designs achieving excellent strength-ductility bal -
ances, such as transformation-induced plasticity-assisted dual-phase 
HEAs [ 22 , 23 ], interstitial-solid-solution-strengthened HEAs [ 24 , 25 ], 
heterogeneous-structure-strengthened HEAs [ 26 – 29 ] and 
precipitation-hardening (PH) HEAs [ 30 – 35 ]. However, since the 
strength and ductility are usually considered to be contradictory 
[ 36 – 41 ], most conventional strengthening mechanisms inevitably suffer 
from fiercely sacrificing ductility, known as trade-off dilemma. Thus, 
how to achieve strength-ductility synergies has always been a concern of 
scientists. 
Among varieties of advanced HEAs, precipitation hardening stands 
out to be one of the most promising strategies owing to its comparable

3.3. Tensile properties 
Fig. 4 a shows a series of tensile engineering stress-strain curves of 
designed PH-MEAs subjected to different processes. First, the yield 
strength (YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and fracture strain of AC 
are about 340 MPa, 650 MPa and 48.4 %, respectively. After cold rolling, 
the CR shows much higher YS of ~1.72 GPa and UTS of ~1.82 GPa, but 
its total tensile elongation is much limited (~1.4 %). Fortunately, the 
situation gets better after a short-time annealing, the CRA exhibits still 
high YS of ~1.07 GPa and UTS of ~1.32 GPa, meanwhile maintaining 
high fracture strain of ~21.9 %. Intriguingly, after a long-time aging, the 
CRAA shows not only a great enhancement in YS and UTS, but also a 
slightly increment in its ductility at room temperature, whose YS, UTS 
and fracture strain are about 1.50 GPa, 1.69 GPa and 23.8 %, respec -
tively. Evidently, the excellent mechanical performance of CRAA suc -
cessfully verifies the effectiveness of tailoring nanoprecipitates. When 
the temperature drops from 293 K to 77 K, the CRA possesses much 
higher strength without significant sacrifice of its ductility, whose YS, 
UTS and fracture strain are 1.43 GPa, 1.82 GPa and 17.8 %, respectively. 
By contrast, the CRAA exhibits even exceptional mechanical properties 
at 77 K, with ultrahigh YS (~1.96 GPa) and UTS (~2.19 GPa). Moreover, 
current γ " precipitates-strengthened MEA demonstrates still sufficient 
fracture strain (~13.2 %), validating its capability to operate reliably 
under extreme conditions.

3.4. Deformation mechanisms 
Then, detailed TEM observations were performed again to further 
reveal the deformation mechanisms underlying superior properties of 
CRAA at both room and cryogenic temperatures. In order to obtain a 
better understanding of how the coherent γ " and η nanoprecipitates 
interact with dislocations and deformation twins (DTs), and uncover the 
evolution of deformation substructure of CRAA with increasing defor -
mation at room temperature, we investigated multiple material states 
that applied different levels of accumulated strain. 
As shown in Fig. 5 , at a true stain of 3 %, the initial stage of plasticity 
in current alloy is characterized by the glide of full dislocations disso -
ciated into two partials on {111} planes, with a formation of stacking 
faults in between these partials, consistent with based CoCrNi MEA [ 53 ]. 
These relatively wide dissociations are anticipated to impede cross-slip 
and facilitate planar slip during the early stages of plastic deforma -
tion. Besides, the stacking faults would act as precursors for the 
formation of deformation twins or hcp martensite via nucleation and 
glide of Shockley partials during subsequent deformation. 
As shown in Fig. 6 , at a true strain of 5 %, the microstructures of 
CRAA show an increase in dislocation density relative to initial plastic 
state. It is noted that, some dislocations are pinned and shaped like ar -
rows, marked by red arrows ( Fig. 6 a). According to HRTEM image and 
corresponding FFT image ( Fig. 6 b), coherent γ " nanoprecipitates are 
clearly detected at the pinning point, marked by white circle. Thus, it is 
believed that the existence of γ " nanoprecipitates could hinder the mo -
tion of dislocations and thus cause pinning. As shown in Fig. 6 c and d, 
planar slip gradually generates obvious slip bands, and massive dislo -
cations pile up at annealing twin boundaries and η - γ interfaces, marked 
by blue and green arrows, respectively.

tions and coherent η precipitates occur, as shown in Fig. 7 a. Almost all 
slip bands could directly shear the η precipitates, among which massive 
dislocations are gliding. Therefore, it is the particle shearing mechanism 
rather than the dislocation bypassing mechanism that dominates the 
interactions between dislocations and η precipitates. As shown in 
Fig. 7 b, at a true strain of 15 %, the interactions between dislocations 
and η precipitates become even stronger, and some slips have

4.1.2. Deformation mechanisms at different temperatures 
At 293 K, dislocations interact with dual nanoprecipitates in a rela -
tively mild manner. Typically, the phase boundaries are effective bar -
riers to dislocations motion. However, upon application of strain, the 
coherent or semi-coherent interfaces between nano-precipitates and 
matrix facilitate dislocation transmission and relieve the stress concen -
trations that can lead to premature crack initiation, ensuring the sig -
nificant improvement of strength without seriously compromising their 
tensile ductility. Then, the widely distributed nano-DTs induce promi -
nent work-hardening ability, because numerous internal boundaries 
generated during twinning could severely refine grains and block 
dislocation motion [ 54 ], enabling dynamic Hall-Petch effect, such as 
nano-twinned copper [ 55 ]. Besides, the 3D SF-DT structure networks 
can further divide grains into more sub-grains so that induce much 
stronger dynamic Hall-Petch effect [ 56 ]. Moreover, due to the strong 
interactions between dislocations and SFs, the work hardening ability 
might be enhanced by the impeding effect of required additional stress 
on dislocation motion [ 54 ]. Finally, the interactions between SFs and 
twin boundaries may result in Lomer-Cottrell (L-C) locks or evolve into 
secondary DTs and thereby induce multiple twinning systems. On the 
one hand, as the products of the interaction between two leading partial 
dislocations from different slip planes, the L-C locks have a good stability 
to resist dissociation and a good capability to accumulate different types 
of dislocations [ 57 , 58 ], which could finally induce pronounced work 
hardening ability. On the other hand, the existence of twinning network 
could not only impede dislocation motion, but also offer adequate 
pathways for dislocation glide, so that the strength and ductility could 
be enhanced simultaneously [ 59 , 60 ].

mainly including much higher density of DTs and multiple slip systems, 
which are thought to be two main factors contributing to extraordinary 
cryogenic tension properties. Since the deformation mode of current

mode is planar dislocation slip, and the γ " nanoprecipitates could 
hinder the motion of dislocations as well as cause pinning. At a strain 
level of 10 %, massive slip bands could directly shear the η pre -
cipitates, among which strong interactions between dislocations and 
η precipitates occur. With increasing strain, DTs gradually become a 
predominant aspect of the substructure, which could partition grains 
into much smaller structural units and facilitate high work hardening 
ability. 
4. At 77 K, not only much more DTs are activated due to the lower SFE 
and ultrahigh yield stress, but also individual DTs are activated be -
tween two η precipitates, becoming effective ways to accommodate 
severe non-uniform plastic deformation. Besides, multiple slip sys -
tems induce pronounced dynamic microstructure refinement and 
thus further ensure plastic flow stability. 
5. By calculation, the precipitation hardening plays the most significant 
role in enhancing yield strength, especially the strengthening effect 
provided by γ " precipitates. Moreover, the γ " precipitates even show
