ABSTRACT 
The isothermal oxidation behavior of high-Nb-containing TiAl alloys doped with W, B, Y, and C (WBYC) /Si 
(WBYS) was investigated via oxidation tests at 900 
◦
C in ambient air. Both alloys exhibited outstanding high- 
temperature oxidation resistance, with unit weight gains of < 1 mg/cm
2 
after 200 h of oxidation at 900 
◦
C. 
The inclusion of alloying elements could refine the crystalline particle size and reduce oxygen activity, conse -
quently suppressing TiO
2 
growth and promoting Al
2
O
3 
formation. Furthermore, the generated oxide pegs 
improved the adhesive strength between the oxide scale and the substrate and the spalling resistance of the oxide 
scale. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl-based alloys are characterized by excellent properties such as 
low density, high strength and stiffness, and good creep resistance at 
elevated temperatures; thus, they are one of the most effective high- 
temperature structural materials that can partially replace heavy Ni- 
based alloys for the manufacture of turbine blades in the aerospace, 
automotive, and gas turbine industries [1 – 3] . However, their limited 
ductility at room temperature, poor hot working ability, and limited 
oxidation resistance at > 800 
◦
C severely limit their application [4,5] . 
Although the Ti-48Al-2Cr-2 Nb (GE 4822) alloy has been successfully 
used for the manufacture of the low-pressure turbine of the GEnx ™ 
engine, its maximum service temperature is only 700 
◦
C [6 – 9] . 
Ti-43.5Al-4 Nb-1Mo-0.1B (TNM) is regarded as a new-generation 
TiAl-based alloy, owing to its good high-temperature performance and 
hot workability; however, its operating temperature is only 750 
◦
C [10, 
11] . In recent years, numerous studies have been conducted to improve 
the oxidation resistance of TiAl-based alloys. The oxidation resistance of 
TiAl alloys can be improved through the introduction of ternary and 
multicomponent alloying elements, such as W, B, Y, Cr, Nb, B, C, and Si 
[2,12 – 14] . However, not all alloying elements are beneficial to the 
mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys. 
Chen et al. [15 – 17] reported that Nb addition (6 – 10 at%) to TiAl 
(high-Nb-containing TiAl) alloys can improve the oxidation resistance of 
the alloy and greatly enhance the strength and creep resistance at high 
temperatures. Studies have found that the addition of appropriate rare 
elements to TiAl alloys improved oxide scale adhesion [18,19] . Xiang 
et al. [20] reported that the addition of excessive Y to 
high-Nb-containing TiAl alloys was detrimental to oxidation resistance 
owing to the coarsening of Y-rich precipitations and the increase in 
lattice defects. A certain amount of B can refine the grain size and 
improve the mechanical properties of TiAl alloys [21,22] . While 
numerous studies have investigated the effect of a single alloying 
element on the properties of TiAl alloy, studies on the synergistic effects 
of multiple elements are few. 
Therefore, the oxidation resistance of high-Nb-containing TiAl alloys 
modified with W, B, Y, and C/Si was investigated in this study. 
Isothermal oxidation experiments of Nb-containing TiAl alloys were 
conducted at 900 
◦
C for 200 h. The synergistic effect of multiple ele -
ments on the microstructure and oxidation behavior of Nb-containing 
TiAl alloys was investigated. 
2. Experimental procedures 
Two high-Nb-containing TiAl alloys with nominal chemical compo -
sitions of Ti 45Al8Nb0.2W0.2B0.1Y 0.2 C and Ti45Al8Nb0.2W0.2 -
B0.1Y0.2Si (at%) (hereinafter referred to as WBYC and WBYS alloys, 
respectively) were carefully considered in this study. The alloy ingots 
with a size of φ 50 × 140 mm were prepared via the induction skull 
melting in an argon atmosphere, using raw materials such as Ti sponge 
(99.9%); high-purity Al ingot; Al-Y, Al-Nb, Al-Ti-B, and Al-Ti-C master 
alloys; and high-purity tungsten powder with an average size of 50 nm.

carbon oxides, vanadium oxides, etc. Gong et al. [29] found that many 
oxide clusters are observed in the scale during oxidation of

them can be attributed to the evaporation of vanadium oxides. In this 
study, the oxide cluster formed in WBYC alloy may be related to the 
volatilization of carbon oxides on the substrate surface, which may 
interrupt the growth of the continuous oxide scale. Meanwhile, the 
formation of oxide clusters also increased the oxygen transport rate and 
the possibility of scale spalling, which deteriorated the oxidation resis -
tance of the WBYC alloy. For the WBYS alloy, oxide clusters were not 
formed on the oxide scale, but the alloy exhibited a fine and dense TiO

improve the spalling resistance of the oxide scale [29] .

4. Discussion 
According to the study of the microstructure and oxidation behavior 
in Sections 3.2 – 3.4 , the investigated alloys all exhibited outstanding 
oxidation resistance compared with previous TiAl-based alloys ( Fig. 4 ), 
attributable to the synergistic effects of W, B, Y, and C/Si. The subtle 
difference in the effects of the different alloying elements in Ti45Al8Nb 
alloys is explored. The mass gain rate of the WBYS alloy over oxidation 
was lower than that of the WBYC alloy, related to the favorable effect of 
Si; that is, Si doping into the TiAl alloy may facilitate Al
2
O
3 
formation by 
enhancing the activity of aluminum ion [35] . As demonstrated in 
Fig. 9 d, the (Al, O)-rich layer of the WBYS alloy was more intact. The 
oxidation mechanisms of both alloys after isothermal oxidation for 
200 h are illustrated in Fig. 11 . Owing to the discontinuous Al
2
O
3
-rich 
layer in the WBYC alloy, the micro-paths for the inward diffusion of O 
were more than those in the WBYS alloy, which facilitated the oxidation 
of the alloy. The formation of more micro-paths in the WBYC alloy may 
be due to the rapid consumption of C atom [36] , which resulted in oxide 
cluster formation on the alloy surface ( Fig. 8 b). Similar oxide cluster was 
also found during oxidation of Ti44Al6Nb1Cr2V alloy due to the evap -
oration of vanadium oxides, which decrease the resistance of spallation 
and is harmful to the oxide scale adhesion [29] . Moreover, the occur -
rence of more precipitates in the WBYS alloy not only improved the 
grain refinement effect but also resulted in the formation of more oxide 
pegs ( Fig. 11 b). Li et al. [28] thought that the adhesive strength between 
the oxide scale and substrate was inversely proportional to the grain 
size. Oxide pegs formed near the interface functioned as anchors to 
significantly improve the adhesion between oxide scale and substrate, 
which enhances the spalling resistance of the oxide scale. Therefore, in 
the present study, the oxidation resistance of WBYS is slight better than 
that of WBYC. 
Comparing with previous TiAl-based alloys ( Fig. 4 ), the presence of 
alloyed W, B, Y, and Si/C has a positive effect on improving the oxida -
tion resistance of Ti45Al8Nb alloy. The co-effect of elements on the 
oxidation of TiAl alloy can be analyzed by the schematic diagram in 
Fig. 11 . The effect of W, B, Y, and Si/C on the oxidation of TiAl alloy can

oxidation resistance, the co-doping of W, B, Y, and C/Si can signifi -

5. Conclusions 
The oxidation behavior of high-Nb-containing TiAl alloy doped with 
W, B, Y, and C/Si was investigated through isothermal oxidation tests at 
900
◦
C for 200 h. The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) The oxidation resistance of Ti45Al8Nb alloys was effectively 
improved by the addition of W, B, Y, and C/Si. After oxidation at 
900
◦
C for 200 h, the oxidation-induced mass gain values of the 
WBYC and WBYS alloys were 0.903 and 0.874 mg/cm
2
, respec -
tively, indicating that the oxidation resistance of the WBYS alloy 
was slightly greater than that of the WBYC alloy. 
(2) The oxide scales of the two alloys exhibited a similar structure: 
TiO

2

from outside to inside. The presence of Si in the high-Nb- 
containing TiAl alloy promoted the formation of the Al

(3) Y addition significantly affected the oxidation resistance of the 
TiAl alloy. The presence of rare earth Y promotes the formation of 
oxide pegs to enhance the adhesion of the oxide scale, while the 
alloyed Y close to the oxide scale improved the oxygen partial 
pressure and deteriorated the oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys.
