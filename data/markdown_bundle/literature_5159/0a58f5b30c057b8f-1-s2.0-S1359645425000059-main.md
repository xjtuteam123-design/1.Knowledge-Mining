ABSTRACT
A reactive metallic force field, ReaxFF
NiAlRe
-S23, has been developed to simulate the mechanical behavior of Ni- 
Al-Re systems. This force field accurately reproduces density functional theory (DFT) results, including various 
energies, geometries, and charge distributions, providing a robust platform for computational exploration of 
alloying effects. Utilizing ReaxFF
NiAlRe
-S23, we conducted tensile molecular dynamics simulations and found that 
rhenium (Re) significantly enhances mechanical properties, especially when strategically positioned around 
dislocation cores within the Ni matrix. This improvement is attributed to Re ’ s ability to induce localized atomic 
disorder, effectively resisting dislocation propagation under external deformation. These findings underscore the 
critical importance of Re addition and its spatial distribution in the Ni matrix for optimizing the mechanical 
performance of Ni-based superalloys. Additionally, our study examines the evolution of atomic charges during 
tensile loading, providing insights into the electronic factors contributing to mechanical strengthening mecha -
nisms. ReaxFF
NiAlRe
-S23 emerges as a powerful computational tool for advancing our understanding of alloying 
effects in superalloys, facilitating the design of materials with mechanical properties tailored for high- 
temperature applications.
1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys are the materials of choice for commercial 
turbine blade materials due to their exceptional strength, oxidation 
resistance, and resistance to gas corrosion at temperatures ranging from 
650 to 1000 
◦
C [ 1 ]. The single crystal Ni-based superalloys are primarily 
composed of a γ -Ni phase and a γ ′ -Ni
3
Al phase [ 2 ]. The substantial 
strength of the alloy is attributed to the precipitation of an L1
2
-ordered 
γ ′ -Ni
3
Al phase [ 3 ]. Moreover, these superalloys generally contain more 
than 10 different alloying elements, with Rhenium (Re) playing a pivotal 
role in enhancing their mechanical properties [ 4 ]. In the second and 
third-generation superalloys, Re concentrations reach up to 3 wt.% [ 5 ] 
and 6 wt.% [ 6 ], respectively, underscoring its significance in the alloy 
composition.
The Re enhancement effects are crucial mechanisms to 
understanding alloying effects. There are three main hypotheses 
regarding the effects of Re: (a) Re enrichment at the γ / γ ′ interface may 
impede dislocation movement [ 7 , 8 ]; (b) Re may reduce stacking fault 
energy, making it harder for Schottky partial dislocations to climb [ 9 ]; 
and (c) due to Re ’ s low diffusion coefficient, its interaction with dislo -
cations within the lattice may hinder dislocation motion [ 10 ]. Specif -
ically, Mottura et al. suggested that Re atoms, when positioned near 
dislocation cores, can impede dislocation movement by obstructing the 
climb/glide processes during creep, thereby enhancing the alloy ’ s me -
chanical properties [ 11 ]. This hypothesis has been supported by sub -
sequent studies, including Ge ’ s work, which utilized scanning 
transmission electron microscopy and energy dispersive X-ray spec -
troscopy to observe Re ’ s role in improving high-temperature perfor -
mance by limiting dislocation mobility near interfaces [ 12 ]. Similarly, 
Wu ’ s research provided direct evidence of Re ’ s enrichment at crystalline

2. Computational methods and models
2.1. Construction of the DFT training dataset
The parameters for the ReaxFF force field are meticulously crafted to 
emulate experimental or DFT computational values. Our approach uti -
lizes DFT calculations via both the CASTEP program [ 29 ] and the Vienna 
ab initio simulation package (VASP) [ 30 , 31 ] to establish the training 
datasets. CASTEP is employed for calculating Mulliken charges [ 32 – 34 ] 
for alloys, while VASP optimizes geometry through energy minimiza -
tion. In VASP calculations, the ion-electron interaction leverages the 
comprehensive plane-wave basis projector augmented wave (PAW) 
method [ 35 ]. Meanwhile, the exchange-correlation functional employs 
the Perdew Burke Ernzerhof (PBE) formula under the general 
gradient approximation (GGA) [ 36 ]. Parameters include an energy 
cutoff of 520 eV and a 0.03 Å
 1 
grid spacing between k-points, with 
convergence criteria for energy and force set at 10
 5 
eV and 0.02 eV/Å, 
respectively. The spin polarizations of the systems were also considered.

bulk Re (30 structures), surface structures (3 structures), and bulk

In this work the cohesive energy is calculated as the difference be -
tween the sum of total energies of the isolated constitute atoms and the 
total energy of the composite system. The cohesive energy represents the 
energy change during the transition from gas phase constitute atoms to a 
solid compound. On the other hand, the formation energy is defined as 
the difference between the total energy of a system and the sum of the 
total energies of the corresponding constitute atoms in the form of 
crystal. The formation energy serves as a more effective measure of the 
stability or likelihood of a structure formation from pure constitute 
solids to a compound solid.

4. Conclusions
In this study, we developed a reactive metallic force field, ReaxFF -
NiAlRe
-S23, specifically tailored for Ni-Al-Re alloys, parameterized from a 
comprehensive DFT dataset. Based on a meticulous parameter optimi -
zation to match DFT, the ReaxFF
NiAlRe
-S23 force field demonstrates high 
accuracy for replicating DFT results, including various formation en -
ergies and geometric properties, across a range of Ni-Al-Re structures. 
This force field is particularly effective in capturing the distribution of 
Re within the γ -Ni phase, making it a robust tool for modeling the 
intricate interactions and structural dynamics of Ni-based superalloys 
containing Re.
Our tensile molecular dynamics simulations, which involved various 
configurations of Re-added structures, reveal that the strategic addition 
of Re atoms at dislocation cores rather than at the Ni/Ni ₃ Al interface in 
the interface and interface-dislocation structures significantly enhances 
the tensile strength of the system via drag effects on dislocation move -
ment. The MD results indicate that the tensile strength improvement is 
more closely related to the Re addition location than to the number or 
separation of Re atoms. Specifically, Re atoms contribute to the 
resistance against external deformation by inducing atomic disorder 
near dislocations as well as electrostatic interactions, thereby enhancing 
the mechanical properties of the alloy. Our simulations support the 
mechanism of dislocation hindrance by Re through local disorder tran -
sitions, rather than through interface strengthening.
Overall, these findings underscore the importance of Re addition in 
Ni-based superalloys, particularly in terms of its strategic placement 
near dislocation cores within Ni solid solution matrix, to optimize the 
mechanical performance. Thus, the ReaxFF
NiAlRe
-S23 force field has 
proved to be a valuable computational tool, offering insights into the 
charge transfer dynamics during deformation and illustrating Re 
enhancement effects in Ni-based superalloys.
