ABSTRACT
In this work, pure Nb, Nb 5Si3 and Laves Cr 2Nb compound powders were used as raw materials to prepare Nb-Si-
Cr ternary alloys by spark plasma sintering (SPS). A comprehensive estimation of the microstructure and
properties, including room temperature fracture toughness, high temperature strength and oxidation resistance,
of the Nb-Si-Cr ternary alloys as a function of the Nb/Nb 5Si3/Cr2Nb phase volume fraction combinations was
conducted. The results showed that Nb-Si-Cr ternary samples with the relative density larger than 98.42% were
obtained by SPS processing, and the samples all consisted of Nb, Nb 5Si3 and Cr2Nb phases that were distributed
homogeneously. The fracture toughness KQ of the Nb/Nb 5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructure, which was dominated by
the Nb phase, naturally increased with the Nb fraction. As expected, the room-temperature Vickers hardness and
the high-temperature strength of the bulk alloys increased monotonically with the increasing of the sti ﬀening
Nb
5Si3 fraction. Interestingly, the binary Cr 2Nb phase played a positive role in the high temperature strength and
oxidation resistance. Finally, the fracture modes of the typical Nb/Nb 5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructures under bending
and compression conditions at room and high temperatures as well as the oxidation mechanism are described
and discussed.
1. Introduction
In searching for high-temperature structural materials for the hot-
test components of high thrust-to-weight ratio aero-engines, other than
nickel-based superalloys, Nb-Si-based alloys with a dual-phase Nb/
Nb5Si3 microstructure have attracted considerable interest because they
possess a high melting point 2520 °C, high stiﬀ ness, relatively low
density (7.1 g/cm 3) and excellent mechanical properties at elevated
temperatures [1–8]. For high-temperature structural materials, ba-
lanced properties, including room-temperature toughness, high-tem-
perature strength and oxidation resistance, are the most important re-
quirements for applications. For the dual-phase Nb/Nb 5Si3 alloys, the
room-temperature toughness and the high-temperature strength and
creep resistance can be supplied by the ductile Nb phase and the stif-
fening Nb 5Si3 silicide [3–6], respectively; however, no phase can resist
oxidation, which can result in disastrous consequences when Nb/Nb 5Si3
alloys are in service in oxygen atmosphere at high temperatures. The
addition of Cr to Nb –Si based alloys forms oxidation against Laves
Cr2Nb phase and reduces oxygen di ﬀusion and solubility in Nb, both of
which enhance the environmental stability of Nb-Si-based alloys at high
temperatures [9–13]. A short-term goal, corresponding to a material
thickness loss of 200 µm in 10 h air exposure at 1370 °C, has been
successfully achieved for Nb-Si-Cr ternary-based alloys with a tri-phase
Nb/Nb
5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructure [9,10]. Therefore, the Nb, Nb 5Si3 and
Cr2Nb phases are the basic phases for a designed Nb-Si based alloy.
Subsequently, optimisation of the microstructure and determination of
the properties dependent on the Nb, Nb 5Si3 and Cr2Nb fractions become
urgent issues in developing Nb-Si-based alloys.
To date, researchers have successfully fabricated Nb-Si-based alloys
by vacuum arc melting [7,8,14,15] and directional solidi ﬁcation
[4,8,16,17]. However, Nb-Si alloys fabricated by the above-mentioned
casting methods usually possess element segregation and a coarse mi-
crostructure. Fabricated by powder metallurgy technology, Nb-Si-based
alloys can obtain a homogeneous and controlled microstructure (in
morphology, fraction and size of the constituent phase) through ad-
justing the powder's metallurgical parameters, such as milling time and

such as milling time and sintering temperature, on the microstructural

the pure element powders, however, uncertainty exists in the phase

ample, in the Nb-16Si binary alloy, three phases, Nb, Nb

were observed in the as-sintered bulk alloys, and, as the milling time

morphology of the constituent phases (Nb, Nb 5Si3 and Nb 3Si) changed
from large size strips to a ﬁne near-equiaxed shape [18]. A similar result
is found for multi-component Nb-Si-Ti-Al-Hf-Cr alloys [21]. Another
phenomenon, that is, a new phase in addition to the designed phase and
microstructure, would sometimes arise in multi-component Nb-Si-based
alloys due to incomplete solid reaction [18,19]. It has been reported
that Ti, the popular toughening element for Nb [22], is usually added to
Nb-Si-based alloys, leading to the formation of the Ti solid solution
phase in sintered Nb-Si based alloys [19,21], whereas no Ti solid so-
lution appears in as-cast Nb-Si based samples [4,8,16–18]. Prolonged
mechanical alloying is expected to enhance the solid reaction of Nb and
Ti during ball milling and sintering. This suggests that for a given Nb-Si
composition, the microstructure, including the phase constitution and
fraction, is uncertain and relates to the powder's metallurgical para-
meters and results in di ﬀerent property responses. A large amount of
work in adjusting and optimising the milling time and sintering tem-
perature is needed to obtain a homogeneous and controlled Nb-Si-based
microstructure.
To solve the uncertainty of the phase constituents and the micro-
structure of a sintered Nb-Si alloy made from pure element powders and
to reduce the work in the investigation of a microstructure dependent
on the milling time and sintering temperature, Nb
5Si3 and Cr 2Nb
compound powders were ﬁrst employed as the raw materials in this
work to obtain the accurately controlled phase fractions of the tri-phase
Nb/Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructure. In this case, the solid reaction is
avoided, and it is expected that no new phase or undesired phase forms
during the sintering process. Button samples, with various volume
fraction combinations of the Nb, Nb
5Si3 and Cr 2Nb phases, were fab-
ricated by spark plasma sintering (SPS) technology, which can facilitate
completion of the densiﬁ cation process of the sample without in-
complete solid reaction in a very short period of time under the com-
bined e ﬀect of a mechanical shock wave and external pressure. The
mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of the SPS Nb/Nb
5Si3/
Cr2Nb alloys have been quantitatively investigated as a function of the
three phase fractions.
2. Experimental procedures
Powdered Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb compounds and pure elemental Nb
were employed as the raw materials for preparing the tri-phase Nb/
Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb alloys. Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb ingots were prepared by arc
melting, and then the compound powders were obtained by mechanical
disruption of the ingots. By using various volume fraction combinations
of the Nb, Nb
5Si3 and Cr 2Nb powders, eight types of tri-phase Nb/
Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb alloys were developed. Table 1 lists the sample names
and the volume fraction combinations of the Nb, Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb
powders of the eight designed Nb-Si samples. For terminology, the ﬁrst
number in each sample name refers to the volume fraction signal of Nb,
the second number represents the volume fraction of the Nb
5Si3 phase,
and the last number represents the fraction of the Cr 2Nb phase. For
example, the symbol 50-10-40 refers to the sample made from a powder
mixture with 50% Nb, 10% Nb 5Si3 and 40% Cr 2Nb (in vol% fraction).
The powder mixture was mixed in a planetary mill machine with
stainless steel vials under argon atmosphere at a rotation speed of
400 rpm for 2 h at room temperature. The mixed powders were subse-
quently placed into a BN-coated graphite die and sintered at 1350 °C for
5 min under 50 MPa in an argon atmosphere using an SPS-1050 ma-
chine, followed by furnace cooling. Secondary electron images (SEI) of
the powder morphologies and fracture surfaces of the sintered samples
were taken using a scanning electron microscope (FEI Quanta 200F),
back-scattered electron (BSE) images were taken to investigate the
microstructures, crack propagation path and oxide morphology of the
tested samples (SEM, JEOL JXA-8100). The chemical composition of
each phase was analysed using energy dispersive X-ray (EDX) spectro-
scopy. TEM image was taken to investigate the interface between
phases using JEOL-2100F. Three-point bending tests were conducted on
an Instron testing machine 1186 at a constant rate of 1 mm/min to
measure the fracture toughness K
Q. The bending specimen was 30 mm
in length and 3 mm × 6 mm in cross-section; the outer span in the
three-point bending was 24 mm. A straight notch of approximately
3 mm in depth was inserted into each bending specimen at the mid-
point of the length using electro-discharge machining (EDM) with a 0.1-
mm diameter Cu wire. The Vickers hardness ( Hv) was measured using
an HXZ-1000 digital micro-hardness tester under a load of 1 kg with a
load-maintaining time of 15 s, and the average Hv value was obtained
from at least 11 indents on each sample. The compressive tests at
1250 °C and 1350 °C were conducted in an argon atmosphere at a strain
rate of 3 × 10
−4 s−1 using a Gleeble 1500 testing machine. To
homogenise the temperature throughout the heating area, the speci-
mens were held for 5 min at the testing temperature prior to testing.
The compressive cylinders were 6 mm in diameter and 9 mm in length.
Isothermal oxidation tests were performed at 1250 °C in static air. The
weight changes of the 15 mm × 10 mm × 2 mm samples were mea-
sured continuously using a thermal balance with an accuracy of
0.0001 g. All specimens for the three-point-bending, high-temperature
compression and isothermal oxidation tests were mechanically polished
using SiC paper (1000 grit) with water before testing.

3.2. Microstructures of the as-sintered samples
Fig. 2 displays the BSE images of the eight as-sintered samples with
diﬀerent phase volume fraction combinations, in which relative density
of each sample is inserted. The samples with the relative density in a
range of 98.42~99.52% were obtained by SPS processing, and three
phases existed with clear contrasts (bright, gray and black) in all eight
alloys. The as-sintered Nb/Nb
5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructures in Fig. 2 show
that the Nb (bright), Nb 5Si3 (gery) and Cr 2Nb (black) phases (see
Fig. 2(f)), which were determined by EDX, display near-equiaxed
morphology and they had a basically homogeneous distribution. It is
natural that with a decrease in the Nb fraction, that is, an increase of
the Nb5Si3 and Cr2Nb fractions, the Nb 5Si3 and Cr2Nb phases distribute
from islands ( Fig. 2(a) and (b)) to a discontinuous network ( Figs. 2(e),
2(f)) and ﬁnally to the matrix ( Fig. 2(g) and (h)). A TEM image
(Fig. 2(i)) shows a triple point consisting of Nb, Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb
phases. The interfaces of the Nb/Nb 5Si3, Nb/ Cr 2Nb and Cr 2Nb/Nb5Si3
couples were found to be straight, without any precipitates.
3.3. Mechanical properties of the SPS samples at room and high
temperatures
Table 2 summarises the Vickers hardness ( Hv) and fracture tough-
ness ( KQ) values of the bulk SPS alloys. The Hv of the bulk samples
naturally increased monotonically with the stiﬀ ening Nb5Si3 and Cr2Nb
phase proportion, whereas it is the contrary for the KQ values. For ex-
ample, the Hv increased from 367 to 637 –700 as the Nb 5Si3 +C r2Nb
fraction increased from 30% to 70%. The contribution of the Cr 2Nb
phase to the Hv is little higher than that of the Nb 5Si3 phase. As ob-
served in Table 2, when the total fraction of the Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb
phases was at a constant of 50%, the Hv of the alloys presented an
increasing tendency, from 400 to 523, as the Cr 2Nb fraction changed
from 10% to 40%. A similar tendency arised in samples with the 70%
Nb5Si3 +C r2Nb fraction.
It is also demonstrated that the Nb fraction played a key role in the
fracture toughness KQ. For the samples with 70% Nb, the KQ was
6.7 MPa m1/2, whereas the KQ was only 3.6 –4.0 MPa m1/2 at an Nb
fraction of 30%. For a ﬁxed Nb 5Si3 +C r2Nb fraction, the variation of
the Nb5Si3 and Cr2Nb fraction did not a ﬀect the KQ of the tri-phase Nb/
Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructures, that is, the KQ changed by amplitude of
less than 10%. The reason is that the fracture toughness of the mono-
lithic Nb
5Si3 and Cr2Nb phases are both at the same level and as low as
1.5–3 MPa m1/2[22].
To understand the failure modes of the Nb SS/Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb micro-
structure, a series of fractographic observations were performed on the
fracture surfaces of the three-point-bent samples. Fig. 3 shows a typical
feature of the crack propagation path in the Nb 5Si3 +C r2Nb dominant
sample 30-35-35. In general, the main crack propagates in a straight
line without large deﬂ ection, and the brittle Nb 5Si3 silicide and Cr 2Nb
compound is the favourite path of the cracking, as observed in Fig. 3(a).
The crack propagated uninterruptedly throughout the Nb 5Si3 and
Cr2Nb phases, revealing that both the Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb phases cannot
eﬀectively stop the crack propagation, on the other hand, is harmful for
the toughness from the viewpoint of the toughening mechanisms
[22–24].
As the crack meets the Nb phase, however, many mechanisms ob-
struct the crack growth in the SPS samples, bene ﬁting the toughness.
The crack may go along the Nb SS/Nb5Si3 interface (arrow A in
Fig. 3(b)), pass through the Nb phase (arrow B in Fig. 3(d)), bridge the
Nb phase (arrow C in Fig. 3(c)), bypass the Nb phase (arrow D in
Fig. 3 (d)) and sometimes be arrested by the Nb phase (arrow E in
Fig. 3(d)). The interaction between the cracks and the constituent
phases, shown in Fig. 3,was also found in the Nb-dominant samples (not
shown here) and the cast Nb-Si-based alloys [22–24], suggesting the
universality of the fracture behaviour in the Nb SS/Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb mi-
crostructure. The fractographic morphologies, shown in Fig. 4, reveal
the di ﬀerent failure modes of the Nb, Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb phases under
bending. On the fractured Nb phase, river patterns, so-called active
facets, appeared, illustrating the cleavage failure manner of the Nb
phase. For the Nb
5Si3 and Cr2Nb compounds, they showed very smooth
fracture surfaces without river patterns, suggesting that they fail in a
brittle mode.

3.4. Oxidation behaviour of the sintered samples
Representative oxidation kinetics curves of the sintered alloys oxi-
dized at 1250 °C in static air are shown in Fig. 7. The kinetics curves can
be divided into three groups depending on the Nb fraction. Group one
contained the samples 70-20-10 and 70-10-20 with 70% Nb, and its
oxidation kinetics was not totally parabolic. At the beginning of oxi-
dation, two alloys showed a parabolic plot, and the mass gain increased
rapidly with time. The mass gain reached the top value at approxi-
mately 10 h and then remained almost constant at 250 –270 mg/cm
2
during further oxidation. The second group includes the samples 50-40-
10, 50-25-25 and 30-50-20, in which the Cr
2Nb content was small, in a
range of 10– 25%, but the Nb 5Si3 +C r2Nb content was as high as
50–70%. In this group, the oxidation kinetics curves were similar to
that of group one, but the time for the mass gain to reach the top value
of 250 –270 mg/cm
2 was extended to 20 h for the sample 50-40-10 and
50 h for the samples 50-25-25 and 30-50-20. The third group involves
the samples 50-10-40, 30-35-35 and 30-20-50, with the Cr
2Nb fraction
of 35– 50%. After a transient period, the kinetics curves showed basi-
cally parabolic characteristics during air exposure for 100 h, and with
the increases in the Nb 5Si3 +C r2Nb content, the mass gain decreased;
therefore, the oxidation resistance was clearly improved. With air ex-
posure at 1250 °C for 100 h, sample 30-20-50, with the highest Cr
2Nb
fraction had the lowest mass gain of approximately 135 mg/cm 2.
Fig. 8 shows the macro-morphologies and oxide layers of two ty-
pical SPS samples 70-20-10 and 30-20-50 isothermally oxidized at
1250 °C for 75 h. The representation of the two alloys is that the 70-20-
10 sample had the highest Nb fraction and the highest mass gain (the
poorest oxidation resistance), whereas the 30-20-50 sample had the
highest Cr
2Nb fraction but the lowest mass gain (the best oxidation
resistance). During air exposure, heavy spallation of the oxide produced
occurred for the sample 70-20-10 with the 10% Cr
2Nb fraction at an air
exposure time of 5 h, as observed in Fig. 8(a), it was found that the
disastrous oxidation generated due to the spallation in the re-exposure
of the fresh substrate to oxygen and lead to a higher mass gain. The
spallation was found to be prolonged as the exposure time increased
(~75 h). With the highest Cr 2Nb fraction of 50%, the sample 30-20-50,
however, had a dense oxide layer with no spallation and almost
maintained the whole origination shape at an exposure time of 75 h, as
observed in Fig. 8(b). This dense oxide layer may be a barrier to impede
the diﬀusion of the oxygen in the atmosphere into the substrate, which
is bene ﬁcial to improve the oxidation resistance of the Nb/Nb
5Si3/
Cr2Nb microstructure. The macro-morphologies in Fig. 8 are consistent
with the mass gain depending on the fraction combinations of the Nb,
Nb
5Si3 and Cr2Nb phases, especially the Cr 2Nb fraction shown in Fig. 7.

4. Discussion
With di ﬀerent combinations of Nb, Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb powder vo-
lume fractions, the eight samples actually contain three elements, Nb, Si
and Cr, and they can be marked as Nb-Si-Cr ternary alloys. The prop-
erties, those are, the room-temperature Vickers hardness ( Hv) and
fracture toughness ( KQ), high-temperature strength ( σ 0.2 and σ max),

A comprehensive investigation of the properties reveals that the
room-temperature toughness and high-temperature oxidation re-
sistance of the SPS Nb-Si-Cr ternaries are insu ﬃcient to meet the re-
quirements of the high-temperature structural materials. In the devel-
oping Nb-Si-Cr-based alloys, alloying is one of the most e ﬀective
approaches to improve the toughness, strength and oxidation re-
sistance. It has been qualitatively shown that Ti and Hf are the only
alloying elements that do not increase the brittle-to-ductile transition
temperature of Nb [22]; on the other hand, Ti and Hf are toughening
and ductilising elements for Nb. Ti, Al and Fe are also found to reduce
oxygen diﬀusion and solubility in Nb [14,15], which might increase the
environmental stability of Nb at high temperatures. It has been reported
that the K
Q of as-cast Nb-16Si alloy is only 5.4 MPa m 1/2 [30], it then
increases up to 11.3 MPa m 1/2 [31] when is alloyed with 27 at% Ti (Nb-
16Si-27Ti alloy). With a three-phase of Nb SS/Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb micro-
structure, a cast Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-17Cr alloy composed of 30%
NbSS, 48% Nb 5Si3 and 22% Cr 2Nb has a fracture toughness of
10.8 MPa m1/2 [32], approximately three times that of the SPS samples
30-20-50, 30-50-20 and 30-35-35. The higher toughness in the as-cast
sample is strongly believed to be contributed by Nb with 22 –27 at%
toughening element Ti [22].
Unfortunately, in this multi-component alloy, the Cr 2Nb phase also
solidiﬁes elements Ti, Al and Si, and these elements signi ﬁcantly de-
crease the melting point of the Cr 2Nb phase from 1770 °C [33] of the
Cr2Nb binary to lower than 1500 °C [25]. With such a low melting
point, the multi-component Cr 2Nb phase might become a soft phase
compared with the Cr 2Nb binary (300 MPa at 1400 °C [29]) and, thus,
decrease the bulk 0.2% yield strength to 250 MPa at 1250 °C and
85 MPa at 1350 °C [32]. However, in the Nb-Si-Cr ternary, the Cr 2Nb is
the strengthening phase and the sample 30-50-20 (with the similar
phase fractions to the cast multi-component Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-
17Cr alloy) has a bulk 0.2% yield strength of 440 MPa at 1250 °C and
308 MPa at 1350 °C ( Table 2), much higher than those of the Nb-16Si-
22Ti-2Al-2Hf-17Cr alloy. This strongly con ﬁrms that alloying cannot
completely solve the incompatibility of these parameters in the Nb-Si-
Cr-based alloys with the tri-phase Nb/Nb
5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructure.
Further work on balancing these properties of the tri-phase Nb/Nb 5Si3/
Cr2Nb microstructure through combination of macro- or micro-alloying
[1–5,21,32,34], grain controlling [35], microstructural optimisation, as
well as processing technologies [4,8,16–21] is still needed, which
would be the theoretical base for engineering service of the Nb-Si based
alloys.

1) The samples all consist of the Nb, Nb 5Si3 and Cr2Nb phases; no new

SPS samples have the relative densities greater than 98.42%.
2) The Nb phase dominates the room temperature fracture toughness
(K

is improved from 3.6 to 4.0 MPa m 1/2 to 6.7 MPa m 1/2, as a result,
the Vickers hardness ( Hv) decreases from 637 to 700 –367.
3) In the tri-phase Nb/Nb 5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructure, the Nb 5Si3 and
Cr2Nb compounds both play positive roles in the high-temperature
strength and oxidation resistance. With an Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb
dominant microstructure, the 30-20-50 sample has the highest yield
strength of 351 MPa at 1350 °C and the lowest mass gain of 135 mg/
cm2, that is, the best oxidation resistance when exposed in static air
at 1250 °C for 100 h.
4) Under room temperature bending, the sti ﬀening Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb
phases fracture in a brittle mode, whereas the slight river pattern
appearance on the Nb
SS phase surfaces reveals a cleavage mode of
failure. Under high-temperature compression, collapse of the Nb 5Si3
and Cr 2Nb phase dominates failure of the Nb-Si-Cr alloys.
5) For the Nb-dominant microstructure, Nb 2O5 is the main oxide pro-
duct, and cracks usually initiate within the oxide layer, resulting in
spallation of the oxide layer and poor oxidation resistance. As for the
Cr2Nb dominant microstructure, the CrNbO 4 oxide is the main oxide
produced in the oxide layer. The oxide layer is dense with no
spallation, which protects the substrate and results in good oxida-
tion resistance.
