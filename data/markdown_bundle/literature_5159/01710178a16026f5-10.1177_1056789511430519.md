ABSTRACT: In the power generation industry, the goal of increased gas turbine
efficiency has led to increased operating temperatures and pressures necessitating
nickel-base superalloy components. Under these conditions, the tertiary creep
regime can become the dominant form of creep deformation. In response, the
classical Kachanov /C151Rabotnov coupled creep-damage constitutive model is often
used to predict the creep deformation and damage of Ni-base superalloys. In this
model, the secondary creep behavior can be determined through analytical methods
while the tertiary creep behavior is often found using trial and error or
numerical optimization. Trial and error may produce no constants. Numerical
optimization can be computationally expensive. In this study, a strain-based and
damage-based approach to determine the tertiary creep behavior of nickel-base
superalloys has been developed. Analytically determined constants are found
for a given nickel-base superalloy. Creep deformation and damage evolution
curves are compared. Methods to deal with stress dependence are introduced and
studied.
KEY WORDS: continuum damage mechanics, constant determination,
Kachanov/C151Rabotnov, master curve, Ni-base superalloys, Norton’s power law,
rupture prediction, stress-independent, Waspaloy.

INTRODUCTION
N
ICKEL-BASE SUPERALLOYS ARE often used in the aerospace, power gener-
ation, and pressure vessel and piping industries where operating
temperatures and loads necessitate materials with strong creep resistance.
Under some conditions, the creep behavior of Ni-base superalloys is dom-
inated by the tertiary creep regime, where primary and secondary regimes
are subordinate (National Research Council, 1986). Under these conditions,
a constitutive model is needed, which couples creep deformation with micro-
structural damage evolution. One such model is that developed by
Kachanov (1967) and Rabotnov (1969) based on continuum damage
mechanics (CDM).
Creep deformation is defined in three distinct stages: primary, secondary,
and tertiary as depicted in Figure 1. Creep is sensitive to stress and temper-
ature. For Ni-base superalloys, diffusion flow, dislocation slip and climb,
and grain boundary sliding are all basic mechanisms of creep. Diffusion flow
occurs under low stress and high temperature where atomic diffusion and
the applied load lead to elongated grains. Dislocation slip and climb is the
fundamental mechanism for primary and secondary creep stages where ini-
tially strain hardening occurs until a saturation of dislocation density
together with high temperature provides a balanced recovery mechanism.
Grain boundary sliding is the fundamental mechanism for polycrystalline
tertiary creep where small cavities coalesce into large voids causing a non-
linear reduction in creep strength ultimately leading to rupture (Penny and
Marriot, 1995; Stewart and Gordon, 2010a).

Under low stress and temperature conditions, a majority of life is spent in
the secondary creep regime. Early work focused on modeling the secondary
creep regime and predicting creep rupture using Norton’s power law and
either the Larson-Miller parameter or Monkman-Grant method, respec-
tively (Norton, 1929; Larson and Miller, 1952; Monkman and Grant,
1956). In all three, the required material constants can be analytical deter-
mined from experimental data (Stewart and Gordon, 2010b).
Under high stress and temperature, the tertiary creep regime is dominant,
thus Kachanov (1967) with modifications by Rabotnov (1969) developed a
coupled creep-damage constitutive equation, which model both secondary
and tertiary creep. The Kachanov /C151Rabotnov equations have been found to
accurately model the creep behavior of a number of Ni-base alloys; however,
an approach to determine the tertiary creep-damage constants analytically
from experimental data has yet to be developed. Researchers often resort to
dexterous trial and error or computationally expensive numerical optimiza-
tion to determine appropriate material constants (Stewart and Gordon,
2010c; Stewart et al., 2011a). Global optimization requires an extensive
number of iterations such that large time-steps are needed to reduce com-
puter time. Large time-steps lead to constants that can diverge when time-
step size decreases and greatly underestimate deformation and damage
when time-step size increases. This has limited the application of the
Kachanov/C151Rabotnov equations.
To that end, the Kachanov /C151Rabotnov equations have been extensively
exercised and both a strain-based and damage-based approach to determine
the tertiary creep-damage constants has been developed. Methods to deal
with the stress-dependence of these constants are also introduced. Using a
suitable Ni-base superalloy, a comparison of the creep deformation and
damage evolution of these approaches per test and collectively are provided
and discussed.

Under high stress and temperature conditions, a majority of life occurs in
the tertiary creep regime. The tertiary creep regime is where most creep
damage occurs. Creep damage takes the form of defects such as micro-

. Strain Approach (SA) /C151where the damage evolution equation is incor-

approximate damage evolution.

CONCLUSION
Both the strain and the damage approaches were successful in finding
tertiary creep-damage constants that fit the creep and damage evolution
curves of the Waspaloy superalloy. In the per test fits, the SA was better
at predicting strain than damage, while the DA was better at predicting
damage than strain. The importance of strain or damage evolution within
a design application should be used to determine which approach to apply.
Using the normalized equations, a singular set of constants was found to fit
the creep and damage evolution curves for the given stress range.
Considering variability in materials these constants generally fit well to
the data.
