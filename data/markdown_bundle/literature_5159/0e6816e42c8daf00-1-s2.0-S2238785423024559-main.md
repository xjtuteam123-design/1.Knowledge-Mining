abstract
In this work, a novel Co eCreFeeNieMoeSi multi-principal element alloy (MPEA)-based
Laves phase alloy (T-MPEA) is designed and fabricated. The microstructure, mechanical
properties and dry sliding wear properties of the new alloy are investigated, and compared
to those of T-800 (Co-based) and T-700 (Ni-based) Tribaloy alloys. Hard intermetallic Laves
phases disperse in face-centered-cubic solid solution in T-MPEA, and the Mo- and Si-rich
Laves phase reaches a fraction of ~60%. The compressive strength of T-MPEA reaches
1912 MPa, which is higher than that of T-700 and close to that of T-800. T-MPEA exhibits a
mean hardness of 624 HV
0.3, and maintains a high hardness above 500 HV 0.3 even at 800 /C14 C.
Owing to its high strength and hardness, T-MPEA exhibits higher wear-resistance,
compared to that of T-700 in all dry sliding wear tests, from room temperature to 600 /C14 C.
Owing to the high resistance to softening and dense oxide ﬁlm formation at high tem-
peratures, T-MPEA exhibits excellent high-temperature wear-resistance.

1. Introduction
Industrial components of various systems operating under
the harsh working environments often experience mechani-
cal wear and corrosion degradation due to contacts between
elements and interactions with the environment, which
severely limit their service life. Tribaloy is a series of wear-
resistant Laves-based alloys, usually recommended for high-
temperature and corrosion/oxidation resistant applications
[1,2]. Based on either Co or Ni, they typically comprise
approximately 30 weight percent (wt.%) Mo, 18 wt.% Cr, and
3 wt.% Si. Tribaloy has a structure comprising of
30e70 volume percent (vol.%) primary intermetallic Laves
phases evenly dispersed in a soft matrix of Ni- or Co-based
solid solution [ 3]. The hard primary intermetallic phases,
which are ternary Laves phases of the C-14 (MgZn
2) type with
compositions of approximately M 3Mo2Si or MMoSi (M is Co or
Ni), stabilize the strength and hardness of Tribaloy, even up to
a temperature of 1000
/C14 C[ 4,5]. The Co-based Laves phase alloy,

T-800 [ 6], and Ni-based Laves phase alloy, T-700 [ 7], are two
common Tribaloy alloys. T-800 exhibits high hardness and
wear resistance; however, its application is limited by fracture
brittleness and cost. Compared to T-800, T-700 costs less, but
exhibits lower hardness and wear resistance.
To overcome the above problems, new alloy compositions
based on that of Tribaloy alloys have been explored. In recent
decades, the multi-principal element alloy (MPEA) has pro-
vided a new design strategy, which is superior to the previous
alloy design strategies of adding relatively small amounts of
secondary elements to a primary element [ 8]. Unlike tradi-
tional alloys that have a single principal element, MPEAs
usually contain at least four alloy elements in similar pro-
portions. The mixing of elements in this composition results
in severe lattice torsion, sluggish diffusion, cocktail effect, and
high mixing entropy, which produces outstanding mechanical
strength, high thermal stability, superior wear resistance, and
excellent corrosion resistance [ 9]. The inherent concentration
inhomogeneity created through tailoring of their chemical
complexity, which enhances both strength and ductility.
Controllably introducing second-phase intermetallic com-
pounds [ 10], nanoscale spinodal structure [ 11], gradient
nanoscaled dislocation cell structures [ 12], can further
enhance the strength and ductility. MPEAs have shown
promising results in terms of corrosion and oxide resistance.
The combination of multiple elements helps to form a pro-
tective oxide scale on the surface, which acts as a barrier
against further corrosion and/or oxidation [ 13e16]. MPEAs
also have shown attractive wear resistance result from good
structure stability and high hot hardness, making them prime
candidates for high-temperature wear resistant parts in tur-
bine disks, high-temperature bearings, and other high-tech
ﬁelds that demand superior oxidation, wear, and corrosion
resistance at high temperatures [ 17e20].
In this work, an MPEA-based Laves phase alloy is designed
based on MPEAs, referring to the microstructural character-
istics of Tribaloy alloys. The microstructure, mechanical
properties, and dry sliding wear properties of the new alloy, T-
MPEA, were investigated, and compared with that of Co-based
Laves phase alloy, T-800, and Ni-based Laves phase alloy, T-
700. The introduction of Co eCreFeeNi MPEA solid solutions,
based on Tribaloy alloys, improves the wear resistance and
reduces the alloy cost while reducing fracture brittleness.

3. Results
3.1. Phase and microstructure

3.2. Mechanical properties
Nano-indentation tests were carried out to study the me-
chanical properties of the micro-zones in each alloy. The
calculation results of average hardness and elastic modulus
are shown in Table 3. The hardness of the Laves phase in the
three alloys are about 15 GPa, and the elastic modulus are
greater than 300 GPa. The hardness of the FCC phase in T-700
and T-MPEA are 4.67 and 5.28 GPa, respectively, and the
average elastic modulus of both are greater than 200 GPa. The
average hardness of the eutectic phases in T-800 is 9.88 GPa,
and the Young 's modulus is 262.63 GPa. According to the
mixing rules, the Young 's modulus and strength of the layers
are highly related to the properties and modulation rate (ratio
of mono-layer thickness) of the monolayers, and the hardness
(H

VLaves ¼ 0.5, obtaining a hardness of approximately 4.0 GPa for

(i) Ni in the solid solution can improve the plastic deformation
ability of the Laves phases through synchronous shear and by
promoting matrix slip [ 39]. The Laves phase load curve of T-
MPEA shows an obvious pop-up under the load of 30 mN,
indicating the activation of dislocations, as shown Fig. 11 . (ii)
The increase in alloying elements will result in alloying
toughening. Owing to the close packing nature of the Laves
phase, the free atom volume of the variable interlayer is
highly limited. The increase in elements will enlarge the alloy
site, promote the up and down movement of plastic defor-
mation, and produce the toughening effect [ 40,41].
In short, the strength and toughness of the Laves phase T-
MPEA are both enhanced through the solid solution strength-
ening caused by lattice friction and the alloy toughening
caused by the increase in alloying elements. Although the
room temperature brittleness of Laves phases is not improved
by breakthrough, it provides guidance for the design of alloys
in the future, according to the relationship between micro-
structure and strength.
4.3. Wear mechanisms
According to the analysis in the previous section, the wear
resistance variation of the three alloys at different tempera-
tures are summarized, and the corresponding wear models
of T-MPEAs at different temperatures are established in
Fig. 12.
According to the Hertz contact theory, the maximum shear
force occurs at a certain depth from the contact surface (i.e.,
the subsurface area), where many dislocation pile-ups
appeared and led to the formation of holes and wear debris
[42]. Macroscopically, ﬂake-like wear debris and shallow
grooves formed on the worn surface as shown in Fig. 8 a1ec1.
Alternating stresses accumulate at the Laves eFCC interface
until it exceeds the bonding strength of the two phases with
increasing friction time, which leads to the exclusion of the
Laves phase and the formation of wear debris. The wear debris
move together with the friction pair, resulting in shallow
grooves on the worn surface, which cause severe three-body
abrasive wear ( Fig. 12 a). Owing to the extremely high instan-
taneous temperature of the worn surface during friction, an
incomplete oxidized layer generated through the oxidation
reaction and the oxidation degree of the wear debris increases
signiﬁcantly, as the oxygen content in wear debris increases
obviously ( Fig. 12 b). At 200
/C14 C, although the improvement in
the wear resistance is limited by the oxidized glaze layer, it is
beneﬁcial to reduce the friction coefﬁcient by reducing the
worn surface roughness. In conclusion, abrasive wear and
micro-cutting are the main wear mechanisms at room tem-
perature and 200
/C14 C.
The edges of the wear marks are squeezed and undergo
severe deformation under the action of normal load as the
high temperature microhardness of T-MPEA decreases obvi-
ously at 600
/C14 C( Fig. 12 c). Meanwhile, the oxygen content on
the surface of the alloy gradually increases with the increase
in temperature, as shown in Table 5. T-MPEA exhibits shallow
grooves parallel to the sliding direction and an incomplete
oxide ﬁlm. During the wear process, the oxide layer is
continuously compacted, forming a new protective layer on
the surface. The wear resistance is contributed by the inter-
metallic strengthening and the oxide lubrication at high
temperature. With the slight decreasing in hardness and
prevailing oxides generated by tribo-chemical reaction at high
temperature, the three-body abrasive wear turn to oxide-
wear. The oxidized layer can signiﬁcantly improve the high
temperature tribological properties of the materials. On the
one hand, the oxide layer generated by the tribo-chemical
reaction and high temperature oxidation reaction not only
reduces the roughness of the worn surface but also prevents
direct contact between the friction pairs, which greatly im-
proves the tribological properties of the materials. On the
other hand, the oxide layer replaces the strong atomic or ionic
binding forces between the friction pairs with weaker van der
Waals forces, which reduces the surface molecular forces at
the friction interface, thereby reducing the friction coefﬁcient
and wear rate [ 43,44].
