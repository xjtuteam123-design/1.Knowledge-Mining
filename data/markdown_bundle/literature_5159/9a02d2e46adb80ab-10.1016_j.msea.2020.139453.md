ABSTRACT 
The strengthening mechanism of a laser welded fusion zone between two dissimilar Ni-based superalloys is 
studied. The microstructure of as-welded fusion zone is columnar grain featured with a micron scale dislocation 
cellular substructure. Strengthening component analysis of aged fusion zone at 650 
�
C reveals the hardening 
contribution of gamma prime phase (~52%), dislocation cellular substructure (~28%) and solid solution ele -
ments (~19%). Our results indicate that the formation of dislocation cellular structure endows fusion zone with 
higher strength particularly at 600 and 650 
�
C, which provides novel insights into strengthening of welded Ni- 
based superalloy. 
1. Introduction 
The development of lightweight and high strength materials has 
become a focal point for aero-engine components. A blisk, referred as 
bladed disk, is fabricated by welding blades to the disk and renders 
higher energy efficiency than the conventional blade/disk joint realized 
by tenon and mortise [ 1 – 3 ]. In general, laser welding is utilized to 
fabricate turbine blisk and repair damaged components because of its 
high quality, high precision and low distortion [ 4 – 6 ]. It has been re -
ported that the strength and hardness of fusion zone (FZ) are higher than 
the base metals [ 7 – 10 ]. The strengthening mechanisms of cast and 
wrought Ni-based superalloys, including precipitation strengthening ( γ
0
and γ
0 0
phase), grain boundary strengthening and solid solution 
strengthening, have been extensively studied [ 11 – 14 ]. Owing to the 
high power density and low heat input of laser beam [ 15 ], the FZ is 
characterized by fine cellular/dendritic substructure with elemental 
segregation and fine particles in the cellular walls/interdendrites, which 
is quite different from the microstructure of conventional cast and 
wrought alloys [ 8 , 10 , 16 , 17 ]. However, the increased strength of the FZ 
is not well understood and has often been attributed to a single 
strengthening source such as microstructure refinement or carbide 
particles. 
Herein, the dissimilar welded joint between Inconel 718 alloy and 
DZ125L alloy was employed to investigate the strengthening mechanism 
of the fusion zone. Inconel 718 alloy is one of the most widely used Ni- 
based superalloys developed for turbine disk due to its exceptional 
strength at elevated temperatures up to 650 
�
C [ 18 ]. DZ125L alloy is one 
of the first generation of directional solidified Ni-based superalloy which 
can be used as turbine blade up to approximately 1000 
�
C [ 19 ]. It is also 
one of the advanced directionally solidified Ni-based superalloys in 
China. The strengthening components of fusion zone and the contribu -
tion of each strengthening component are analyzed through a series of 
heat treatment procedures and tensile measurements. Moreover, the 
factors contributing to the higher strength of the fusion zone are dis -
cussed and compared with the Inconel 718 alloy. These results provide 
novel insights into the strengthening mechanism of welded Ni-based 
superalloy.

3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 1 summarizes the yield strength of aged FZ and Inconel 718 in 
the temperature range of 25 – 650 
�

strength of aged FZ is higher than aged Inconel 718, especially at 600 
and 650

The as-welded FZ is composed of columnar γ grains with a diameter 
of a few to hundred microns and length of hundreds of microns. The 
transverse and longitudinal sections show planar, cellular and dendritic 
substructures inside the columnar grains ( Fig. 2 ). The FZ is mainly 
composed of grains with cellular and dendritic substructures. The cell 
spacing and primary dendrite spacing are in the range of 2 – 6 μ m. 
Moreover, discrete particles, i.e., Laves phase and MC carbide, with an 
average size of 0.86 μ m, can be observed in the intercellular/interden -
dritic regions, which are parallel to the thermal flow direction, as shown 
in Fig. 2 b and c. Therefore, the spatial distribution of these discrete 
particles is cellular even though the matrix is dendritic. The planar 
crystals are located at the areas where the FZ meets two base metals on 
both sides. They display low contrast owing to the lack of sufficient 
element segregation and precipitation. Since the two planar crystal re -
gions are very narrow (less than 6 μ m, as shown in Fig. 2 b and c) and 
close to base metals, they do not participate in plastic deformation, 
which indicates that the yield strength of FZ is independent on the 
contribution of planar substructure. Therefore, the influence of planar 
substructure on the yield strength of FZ is not considered in the present 
study.

observed in the interior of cellular substructure. A possible zigzag slip 
mechanism is sketched in Fig. 6 b. One should note that the gliding of 
screw dislocations on (11 1) plane is blocked by stress field due to 
tangled dislocations in the dislocation cellular wall and cross-slips from 
(11 1) plane to ( 11 1) plane. After gliding on ( 11 1) plane for a short 
distance, the screw dislocations are blocked by stress field generated by 
tangled dislocations at the other side of dislocation cellular wall and 
cross-slips back to (11 1) plane. The repetitive dislocation cross slip 
movement causes zigzag slip traces on the surface. Zigzag slip in the 
interior of cellular substructure reflects that the dislocation cellular wall 
is an effective barrier to dislocation movement. It is worth emphasizing 
that the zigzag slip has not been reported in such a cellular substructure 
probably because TEM was usually used for observation while the 
limited field view cannot distinguish the zigzag morphology. Fig. 6 c 
shows the TEM microstructure of the dislocation cellular wall after 
deformation. The higher dislocation density indicates that some dislo -
cations from cellular interior are trapped and retained in the cellular 
wall [ 26 , 28 ]. The trapping and retention of dislocations make the wall 
denser and more effective in preventing dislocation movement from 
cellular interior. Moreover, since the interactions between dislocations

4. Conclusions 
In summary, the FZ strengthening mechanism of a laser welded joint 
between Inconel 718 and directionally solidified superalloy DZ125L is 
reported. The yield strength of aged FZ, which is contributed by the γ
0
phase (~52%), dislocation cellular substructure (~28%) and solid so -
lution elements (~19%), is found to be higher than aged Inconel 718 in 
the temperature range of 25 – 650 
�
C, The dislocation cellular substruc -
ture is mainly composed of dislocation tangles and discrete particles, i. 
e., Laves phases and MC carbides, which can be seen as a framework. 
Zigzag slip of dislocations inside the cell suggests an effective barrier of 
dislocation cellular substructure to dislocation movement from cellular 
interior. The formation of dislocation cellular substructure endows 
higher strength to the FZ over a wide temperature range.
