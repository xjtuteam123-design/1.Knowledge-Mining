Abstract 
The microstructure evolution of Mg 
96 Y3Ni 1 alloy with different Ti content was 
investigated. Mg 95.8 Y3Ni 1Ti 0.2 alloy achieved finest grains, which was composed o f 
α-Mg and long period stacking ordered phase. The ult imate tensile strength of 
Mg 95.8 Y3Ni 1Ti 0.2 alloy reached to 435 MPa with elongation of 21% af ter hot rolling 
and aging treatment.

1. Introduction 
Recently, magnesium alloys with long period stackin g ordered (LPSO) structure 
have attracted much attention due to their excellen t mechanical properties. Mg-RE-Zn 
alloys with LPSO phase can be classified into two t ypes [1]: for type I alloys such as 
Mg-Y-Zn, 18R-LPSO phase formed in as-cast alloy and changed to 14H after solution 
treatment; for type II alloys such as Mg-Gd-Zn, no LPSO phase formed in as-cast 
alloy, but 14H-LPSO phase precipitated during solut ion treatment. Itoi et al. [2] 
reported that 18R changed to 14H after heat treatme nt at 773 K for 5 h in Mg 97 Y2Zn 1 
alloy. Compared with Mg-Y-Zn alloys, 18R-LPSO phase in Mg-Y-Ni alloy had higher 
thermal stability and did not change in high-temper ature solution treatment [3]. 
Moreover, Ni improved the thermal stability of 18R- LPSO phase in Mg 94 Y4Zn 2 alloy 
[4] and promoted the formation of LPSO phase in Mg 96 Gd 3Zn 1 alloy [5]. 
In order to reinforce mechanical properties of magn esium alloys with LPSO 
phase, grain refinement and deformation processing, aging treatment were introduced. 
The study showed that Zr can effectively refine the grain and homogenize the 
microstructure, thereby improving the deformability of magnesium alloys [6]. 
Furthermore, Zr promoted the formation of LPSO phas e in as-cast Mg-Gd-Zn alloy 
[7]. Ti also can be used as the grain refiner in ma gnesium alloys. Wang et al. [8] have 
studied the effect of Ti on AZ31 magnesium alloy and reported that small Ti additions 
can effectively refine the grains. The same result can also be obtained through the 
works of Chen et al. [9] and Zhang et al. [10]. How ever, there was little research 
about the effect of Ti on Mg-Y-Ni alloy containing LPSO phase in the as-cast state. 
Aging treatment was an important means to improve m echanical properties of the 
alloy. Xu et al. [11] and Honma et al. [12] found t hat the mechanical properties of the 
deformed magnesium alloys significantly increased a fter aging treatment. Therefore, 
the aim of this work was to investigate the effect of Ti on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of Mg 96 Y3Ni 1 alloy. The relationship between the 
microstructure evolution and mechanical properties of Mg-Y-Ni-Ti alloys after hot 
rolling and aging treatment were revealed.

2. Experimental procedures 
Mg 96-xY3Ni 1Ti x (at.%) (x=0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4) alloys named as alloy A, B, C and D, 
respectively, were prepared from pure Mg (99.95 wt. %), Y (99.95 wt.%), Ni (99.95 
wt.%), Ti (99.95 wt.%). The metals were weighed on the basis of alloy composition 
and melted in an electric resistance furnace under the protective atmosphere of N 2 and 
CH 2FCF 3 at 1033 K for 20~30 min. When the temperature decr eased to 993 K, they 
were casted into a preheated mold with the temperat ure of 473 K. The samples for 
rolling were taken from the cast ingots with dimens ions of 50 mm × 60 mm × 12 mm 
and held at 773 K for 1 h in a resistance furnace before rolling. Then the samples were 
rolled through 8 passes with total reduction of 72% and 15% reduction for per pass. 
During each pass the samples were reheated at 773 K for 20 min. The rollers were 
heated to 473 K with a speed of 6 rpm. Some of rolled sheets were subjected to ageing 
treatment at 473 K for 30 h. 
The microstructures of the alloys were investigated using an optical microscope 
(OM, Leica DM2500M), a scanning electron microscope (SEM, MIRA3) equipped 
with an X-ray energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS), transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM, JEOL 2010) and X-ray diffraction ( XRD, Cu-K α, Y -2000). The 
samples for OM and SEM examination were polished an d etched with a solution of 4 
ml nitric acid and 96 ml ethanol. Thin foils for TE M observation were prepared by 
cutting the bulk samples into about 0.3 mm slices a nd grinding to the thickness of 
about 50 µm, then by electropolishing with a solution of 30 m l nitric acid and 70 ml 
ethanol, and finally using ion milling. Tensile spe cimens were cut into sheet with a 
gauge dimension of 18 mm × 4 mm × 2.5 mm and perfor med by a DNS100 electronic 
universal testing machine with a crosshead speed of 0.5 mm/min at room temperature. 
The grain size was measured by the liner intercept method. 
 
3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 1a-d shows the microstructures of as-cast Mg-Y -Ni-Ti alloys with different 
Ti content. With increasing Ti content, the α-Mg grains were significantly refined and 
the finest grains were obtained when Ti content was 0.2 at.% (Fig. 1c). Although the

mean grain size was increased at 0.4 at.% Ti content, the grain size of alloy D was still 
smaller than that of alloy A. Fig. 1e shows the mea n grain size of Mg-Y-Ni-Ti alloys 
with different Ti content. It can be concluded that moderate Ti added to Mg-Y-Ni 
alloy could refine the grains, which can be attribu ted to the following factors. On one 
hand, based on the Mg-Ti binary phase diagram, the peritectic reaction occurred at 
924 K: L + ( α-Ti) → ( α-Mg), when Ti content increased to 0.02 wt.%. Mg 
(aMg =0.32094 nm, cMg =0.52107 nm) and Ti ( aTi =0.29512 nm, cTi =0.46845 nm) with 
hexagonal close-packed possessed similar lattice co nstants. According to the 
achievements of Bramfitt [13], the lattice mismatch between Ti and Mg ( δa = 8.05%, 
δc= 10%) was much less than 15%. Therefore, α-Ti can cause the heterogeneous 
nucleation of the α-Mg phase theoretically. On the other hand, as can be seen from Fig. 
2, the majority of Ti was concentrated at the grain boundary. According to the analysis 
of crystal growth restriction theory, Ti possessed greater constitutional undercooling 
ability, which promoted nucleation and limited the growth of α-Mg grains [14-15]. 
The SEM images and corresponding EDS results of all oy A and C are shown in 
Fig. 3a-d. It can be seen that alloy A and C were c omposed of α-Mg and the network 
second phase. The EDS results of second phase in al loy A and C were 
Mg-5.36Y-3.92Ni (at.%) and Mg-6.4Y-5.2Ni-0.2Ti (at.%), as shown in Fig. 3c and 3d. 
According to the diffraction spots in Fig. 3e and 3 f, the second phase in both alloys 
was identified to be 18R-LPSO phase. Besides, the m ain diffraction peaks remained 
α-Mg and LPSO phase by comparing the XRD of A and C alloy given in Fig. 1f. It 
was indicated that the addition of Ti had barely ef fect on the formation and type of 
LPSO phase. 
The microstructure of as-rolled Mg 95.8 Y3Ni 1Ti 0.2 alloy was shown in Fig. 4a, 
which was the typical fibrous morphology. LPSO phas e and α-Mg matrix were 
stretched along the rolling direction, and arranged as a long strip shape. The slender 
LPSO phase like fiber-reinforced phase was embedded in the α-Mg matrix. Part of the 
LPSO phase was crushed into small particles (shown in Fig. 4c). The corresponding 
EDS result of small particles is shown in Fig. 4d. Twins can be observed in α-Mg 
matrix from Fig. 4b. Because of the limited slip sy stem in Mg, magnesium alloys

deformed in the way of twinning when crystal orient ation was not conducive to slip. 
Xiao et al. [16] reported that twinning was involve d in deformation in the temperature 
range of 573 K-673 K when the strain rate was 0.1 s -1. Due to the low roller 
temperature in hot rolling process, the actual roll ing temperature of the samples may 
be lower than 673 K, thus leading to the generation of twins. 
Fig. 5 presents tensile stress-strain curves of the alloys. After adding Ti, both the 
strength and elongation of alloy A were improved, w hich mainly can be attributed to 
the grain refinement effect of Ti. Because the fine and uniform grains not only 
enhanced the strength, but also improved the plasti city of the alloy. The tensile 
strength and elongation of as-cast alloy C were 201 MPa and 6.5% respectively, 
which were improved remarkably by 91% and 185% to 3 85 MPa and 18.5% 
respectively after hot rolling. As can be seen from Fig. 6a, a large quantity of 
dislocations pile-up was generated in α-Mg matrix after hot rolling. During 
deformation process, the major slip system in Mg al loys was the basal plane 
dislocation slip. When the slipping of the dislocat ion was hindered, dislocations piled 
up in front of the obstacle. In order to continue t he dislocations slip, greater force was 
needed, i.e. the increment of dislocations density accompanying continued 
deformation was responsible for strain hardening [1 7-18]. Thereby, abundant 
dislocations pile-up in α-Mg matrix strengthened the alloy. The phase interf ace 
between LPSO phase and α-Mg matrix was a coherent one, which was stable and 
difficult to be destructed [19]. The LPSO phase emb edded in α-Mg matrix like 
fiber-reinforced phase and increased the interfacia l area of α-Mg and LPSO phase in 
RD-ND direction, thus strengthening the alloy. In a ddition, the ultimate stress that the 
alloy bore would be improved through transferring s tress from α-Mg matrix to LPSO 
phase. Some LPSO phase was crushed into small parti cles and dispersed in α-Mg 
matrix, which could induce dispersion strengthening. Due to the diffusion of Y and Ni 
elements in the hot rolling process, tiny granular 18R-LPSO phase precipitated in 
α-Mg matrix and hindered the movement of the disloca tions, as shown in Fig. 6c. 
Furthermore, as shown in Fig. 6b, kinking of the LPSO phase with angle of 46˚ can be 
observed. Kinking of the LPSO phase could strengthe n the alloy as a result of

refinement. Meanwhile, kinking of the LPSO phase co uld afford considerable amount 
of plastic deformation to the alloy, which was impo rtant for the ductility of the alloy 
[20]. The granular LPSO phase and the kinking defor mation jointly refined the 
microstructure of alloy effectively, which increased both strength and toughness of the 
Mg 95.8 Y3Ni 1Ti 0.2 alloy. Because there was limited slip system in Mg, twins stimulated 
further slip of the crystal and improved deformabil ity of the alloy. More deformation 
can be obtained caused by alternating between slip and twinning, which significantly 
improved elongation of the alloy. 
The tensile strength and elongation of alloy C reac hed to 435 MPa and 21% after 
aging treatment, which were improved by 13% and 13. 5% respectively compared 
with as-rolled alloy. As shown in Fig. 6d, aging pr ecipitates were generated after 
aging treatment. According to the corresponding SEA D patterns, these precipitates 
were determined to be β/uni02CA precipitates [21]. Fig. 6e shows that β/uni02CA precipitates impede 
the mobility of the dislocations (as pointed by whi te arrows), accordingly result in 
dislocations piled up in front of the β/uni02CA precipitates. Thus, aging precipitates played an 
important role in improving the properties of the a lloy. Fig. 5e-f shows the fracture 
morphology of alloy C in as-cast and as-rolled stat es. It can be seen that brittle 
fracture was the main fracture mechanisms for the a s-cast alloy, as many cleavages 
and holes were observed. A lot of dimples were observed for the as-rolled alloy, which 
indicated that the ductility of the alloy was increased. 
 
4. Conclusions 
The microstructure evolution of Mg-Y-Ni-Ti alloys with different Ti content was 
investigated. The results indicated that moderate T i addition into Mg-Y-Ni alloy 
refined and homogenized α-Mg grains without affecting the formation and type of 
LPSO phase, and hence improved the deformability of Mg-Y-Ni alloy. When the 
content of Ti was 0.2 at.%, the grain size of Mg-Y- Ni alloy achieved the best 
refinement. As-rolled Mg 95.8 Y3Ni 1Ti 0.2 alloy obtained a large quantity of dislocations 
pile-up, kinking of LPSO phase, granular LPSO phase , and twins, which can 
contribute to the strengthening of the alloy. After aging treatment, Mg 95.8 Y3Ni 1Ti 0.2

which could be attributed to aging strengthening.
