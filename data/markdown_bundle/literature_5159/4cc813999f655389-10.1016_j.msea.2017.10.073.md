Abstract 
Mechanical properties of the Ti 30Zr25Hf15Nb20Ta10 high entropy bcc alloy have been studied in 
wide range of low temperatures (4.2 -350 K). Values of microhardness and Young modulus at 
300 K, temperature dependenc ies of the yield strength, deforming stress and strain rate 
sensitivity have been registered and analyzed in framework of thermally activated deformation 
models. Empirical estimates have been made of the microscopical parameters of plasticity, such 
as Peierls stress, activation energy for dislocations, as well as level of internal stresses. 
 
Keywords: High entropy alloy; Low temperature; Thermally activated plasticity 
 
1. Introduction 
Multicomponent high entropy alloys (HEA) is a new class of materials, which have higher 
strength and thermodynamic stability in comparison with the traditional multicomponent 
multiphase alloys [1 -14]. The structure of HEAs consist of five or more elements with the near 
equiatomic concentration, which typically have solid solution phase with fcc or bcc crystal 
lattice. Difference in the atomic radii of the element in HEA [8, 9] leads to distortion of the 
crystal lattice providing improved strength characteri stics. While, decreased free energy of HEA 
provides stability of the solid alloy during the following thermal treatment [6, 7], but still 
possible phase segregation remains one of the HEA´s weakness [10], and in this sense the bcc 
HEAs have advantage over the fcc HEA due to much higher t hermal stability. Mechanical 
properties of the HEA are actively studied in different experimental conditions, including wide 
temperature range. By now, mechanical properties of the HEA with fcc and bcc lattice are 
studied ra ther well at high temperatures, whereas in temperature range of 300 – 4.2 K such

studies were carried out mainly for fcc alloys. Consequently, study of mechanical properties of

strength. 
 
1. Materials and Methods 
Alloy was produced in arc furnace MIFI-9 by melting of the components (total weight 200 g) 
in high-purity argon by tungsten electrode inside a water cooled copper cavity. The ingots are re-
melted 6-7 times to ensure homogeneity, the melt was cooled at a rate of 80-100 K /s. Rolling of 
the metal sheets was carried out by the DUO-500 rolling mill at room temperature with a degree 
of deformation per pass from 15% to 50%. Annealing of the deformed metal was carri ed out in 
vacuum at temperature of 1270 K for 1 hour. 
Three structural states are studied in this work: 
- initial as-solidified, 
- rolled up to 40% of deformation 
- annealed at 1270 K after rolling. 
The microstructure of the alloy was studied by means of X-ray diffractometers (DRON-UM1 
and Ultima IV) using Cu Kα radiation, and transmission electron microscope JEM-2100F. 
Hardness and elastic modulus were measured using automatic microindentation method by 
"Micron-gamma" indentomet er using Berkovich pyramid under a 3 N load at ambient 
temperature. The values of the Young modulus (E r), normalized hardness (HIT/Er), elastic 
deformation of the material during indentation (ε es), the yield strength ( es) in indentation were 
measured according to [15, 16]. 
Compression samples (3.8x2.2x1.8 mm) were cut from the billet along and across the rolling 
direction. The mechanical characteristics of the alloy were studied at ambient temperature of 300 
K and cryogenic temperatures achieved with liquid nitrogen and helium in compression of the 
sample with a initial strain rate of 3·10−4 s−1 in an MRK-3 deformation machine.

that this is the main system of the dislocation sliding of the Ti 30Zr25Hf15Nb20Ta10 high entropy

Conclusions 
The Ti30Zr25Hf15Nb20Ta10 high entropy alloy demonstrates exceptional combination of high 
strength and reasonable plasticity even at cryogenic temperatures, indicating its good application 
perspectives as structural material. Additional improvement of mechanical properties probabl y 
can be achieved by decreasing of grain sizes. 
The temperature dependences of the flow stress, strain rate sensitivity and the activation volume 
in wide range of low temperatures indicate thermally activated plasticity of the 
Ti30Zr25Hf15Nb20Ta10 high entropy alloy. The thermal activation analysis of experimental data 
has shown that the process of plastic deformation at low temperatures is controlled by thermally 
activated motion of dislocations in the Peierls potential relief, which is realized b y nucleation 
and expansion of the kink pairs at dislocation lines. The e mpirical estimates of the microscopic 
parameters of dislocation interaction with the Peierls barriers(such as activation energy (energy 
of kink pair nucleation) and height of the Peierls barrier) and estimates of internal stresses are
