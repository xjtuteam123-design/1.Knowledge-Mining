ABSTRACT
Low-cost and high-performance are main research directions of structural tita-
nium alloys. In this study, a two-phase structural Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe alloy was
designed and prepared through elemental powder metallurgy (P/M) process,
including cold isostatic processing, vacuum sintering, hot rolling and heat
treatment. Results indicate that the P/M Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe alloy has a multi-
phase microstructure with equiaxial primary a phase, needlelike secondary a
phase and retained b phase. The room-temperature yield and ultimate strength
reach to 1303 MPa and 1422 MPa, respectively, with a balanced elongation of
8.5%. At 400 /C176C, the alloy still has a high yield strength, ultimate strength and
elongation of 850 MPa, 935 MPa and 17%, respectively. The Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe
alloy was successfully processed into intake valves of automobile engine, which
passed the engine test and meet the serving requirement.
Introduction
Titanium and titanium alloys are widely applied in
aerospace, automobile industry, marine engineering
and biomedical ﬁelds because of their unique prop-
erties, such as excellent corrosion resistance, high
speciﬁc strength, high fatigue performance and good
biocompatibility [1, 2]. However, the cost of titanium
alloys, including the high costs associated with pro-
cessing and alloying elements (e.g., V, Nb and Ta),
severely limits their applications. Therefore, simul-
taneously minimizing the costs of processing and raw
materials is essential [ 3, 4].
To decrease the processing costs of titanium alloys,
application of powder metallurgy (P/M) technology,
especially blended elemental powder metallurgy
technology, is highly effective. The well-known
advantages of this technology include fewer pro-
cessing steps, low buy-to-ﬂy rate, direct alloying,
uniform microstructure, no segregation of alloying
elements and lower energy consumption [ 5, 6]. To
diminish cost of raw materials, cheap alloying ele-
ments (e.g., Al, Fe and Fe–Mo master alloy) can also
be used [ 7, 8]. For titanium alloys prepared via the
P/M method, aside from cost, the selected alloying
elements should facilitate the powder sintering pro-
cess and mechanical properties. Al can increase

P/M titanium alloys often have residual pores,

subgrain boundary within the a phase. Then, the b

boundary, resulting a broken a phase, eventually
leading to the spheroidization of the microstructure.
They also found that comparing to lamellar structure,
the equiaxed structure has the advantage of increas-
ing the ductility and the resistance for fatigue crack
initiation of the Ti–6Al–4V alloy.
In the present study, a novel low-cost two-phase
structural Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe, wt% alloy was designed
and prepared through elemental powder metallurgy
(P/M) process. Hot rolling in the a ? b phase ﬁeld
and subsequent annealing were used to further
optimize the microstructure and improve mechanical
properties. The microstructure and ambient/elevated
mechanical properties were studied, and the inherent
strengthening mechanisms were also discussed in
detail.

Results
Microstructure of the P/M Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe
alloy
Figure 3 shows the microstructure of the sintered Ti–
5Al–3Mo–2Fe alloy. The as-sintered sample exhibits a
typical Widmanstatten structure with primary b
grain size of approximately 50–80 lm. The uniformly
oriented a phase precipitates within the initial b
phase and the intervals of a plate are approximately
3–5 lm (Fig. 3b). The volume fraction of the b phase
is approximately 12%. Several residual pores with
sizes of approximately 5–20 lm retain on the matrix,
and the shapes of most pores are circular. The cir-
cular pores in sintered Ti–6Al–4V alloy could lead to
a higher fatigue life compared to the irregularly
shaped pores [ 21]. However, stress concentration is
easy to appear around the residual pores, which will
greatly decrease the tensile properties of the materi-
als. This as-sintered alloy has to be processed to
eliminate residual pores. Table 2 presents the
detailed chemical composition of the as-sintered Ti–
5Al–3Mo–2Fe alloy. The actual composition and
designed composition are consistent within the error
range.
Figure 4 exhibits the optical image of the as-rolled
Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe alloy along the rolling direction.
After rolling, the residual pores are effectively elim-
inated, and the porosity decreases to about 0.2%. The
original lamellar microstructure is destroyed, and the
ﬁbrous grains are elongated along with rolling
direction. The as-rolled alloy is almost fully dense,
and the stress concentration around the pores will be
eliminated, which is one of the most important rea-
sons to obtain a higher plasticity of the alloy. Figure 5

Discussion
The present rolled ? annealed Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe alloy
exhibits high yield strength (1303 MPa) and high
ultimate strength (1422 MPa) with acceptable ductil-
ity (8.5%). The inherent strengthening effects are
mainly due to the following three mechanisms: solid
solution strengthening, microtexture and ﬁne-grain
strengthening.
Solid–solution strengthening occurred in metallic
materials due to the additions of other elements as
solute atoms (like Al, Mo and Fe in the present work).
These atoms were different with the matrix Ti atoms
in size and shear modulus, and lead to a severe local
strain ﬁeld by entering the Ti lattice, which would
interact with dislocations and restrain the dislocation
movement [ 32, 33]. The interstitial elements in the
P/M Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe alloy also played a large role
in improving the yield strength. Interstitial solid
solution strengthening in titanium alloy was believed
to be controlled by the Labusch model, and the con-
tribution of O interstitial solute on yield strength in
titanium alloy was found to be 769.8 MPa/mass%
[34]. A relatively high O content appeared in the
present rolled ? annealed Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe alloy
(about 0.35 wt%), which was mainly introduced by
raw materials and the hot-rolling process. The solid
solution strengthening effect caused by interstitial
element oxygen can signiﬁcantly improve the
strength of the alloy, which could be calculated as
269 MPa. Therefore, the solid solution strengthening
of O atoms also made an important contribution to
the strength of Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe alloy.
The anisotropic behavior of hexagonal crystal
structural after hot deformation could lead to differ-
ent mechanical properties in titanium alloys [ 35].

Conclusions
In summary, a low-cost and high-strength Ti–5Al–
3Mo–2Fe alloy was prepared via the P/M method.
The microstructure and tensile properties were
characterized, and strengthening mechanisms were
discussed. The following conclusions are drawn:
1. The as-sintered Ti–5Al–3Mo–2Fe alloy exhibited
a primary b grain size of 50–80 lm and a lamellar
spacing of 3–5 lm. In rolled and annealed state,

exhibited a length of approximately 1 lm and
width of approximately 300 nm.

3Mo–2Fe alloy reached to 1303 MPa and
1422 MPa, respectively, with a balanced elonga-
