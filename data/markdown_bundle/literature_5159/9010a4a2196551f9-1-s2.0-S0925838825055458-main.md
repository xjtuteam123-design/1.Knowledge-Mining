1. Introduction
The past few decades have witnessed remarkable advancements in 
aerospace industry, driving sustaining demands on aeronautical mate -
rials candidates with reduced weight and improved mechanical prop -
erties. Titanium alloys, characterized by their exceptional specific 
strength, low density and high-temperature, have been recognized as 
one of the most promising aviation materials and are widely used to 
manufacture aircraft core components such as fan discs and landing 
gears [1 – 4] .
The mechanical properties of titanium alloys are significantly 
influenced by their microstructural features. Generally, the bimodal 
microstructure (BM) composed of equiaxed primary α phase ( α
p
) and 
transformed β matrix ( β
t
, β matrix precipitated with secondary α ( α
s
)) 
exhibits the best strength-ductility synergy [5 – 7] . Micrometer-sized α
p 
obtained by hot deformation and solution in α + β region serves as 
dislocation source and ensure the basic strain hardening ability [8] . 
Subsequently, by adjusting the aging parameter (temperature, time), 
different-sized α
s 
can be precipitated in β matrix, thereby further con -
trolling the dislocation activity and resultant strength-ductility synergy 
of the alloy [7,9] . It has been widely reported that the refinement of α
s 
in 
β
t 
will increase strength but simultaneously deteriorate ductility in

influence of aging temperature on strength-ductility evolution of 
Ti-5Al-5Mo-5V-3Cr-1Zr alloy with BM. Under the solution temperature 
of 800 ℃ , when the aging temperature increased from 580 ℃ to 640 ℃ ,

Ti-5Al-4Zr-8Mo-7V alloy with BM, the spacing between adjacent α
s

resulted in the dislocation pile up at α
s
/ β interface . The refinement of α
s 
would reduce dislocation mean free path λ , which increased the 
Hall-Petch strengthening effect but simultaneously deteriorate strain 
hardening ability. Normally, when solution temperature is held con -
stant, variations in aging temperature typically produce inverse trends 
in strength and ductility, theoretically providing significant mechanical 
property regulation range of high-strength titanium alloys with BM.
However, it is noteworthy that some apparently abnormal findings 
have also been reported. For instance, Wang et al. [12] reported that the 
strength of Ti-6Al-2Sn-2Zr-3Mo-1Cr-2Nb-0.1Si alloy with BM decreased 
with the increase of aging temperature due to the weakened dislocation 
impedance in β
t
, yet the ductility did not improve. Church et al. [13]
found that both the strength and ductility of Ti-5Al-7.5 V alloy with BM 
decreased when aging temperature raised from 500 ℃ to 600 ℃ and 
660 ℃ , which was contrary to the results from Wu et al. [10] , Fan et al. 
[11] , and Zhu et al. [7] . These counterintuitive results suggest that while 
the influence of aging temperature on the strength of BM follows a 
generally consistent trend, the mechanism underlying its effect on 
ductility remains to be explored. It is widely recognized that the tensile 
ductility is intrinsically associated with the stress/strain compatibility 
during tension [14,15] . For titanium alloy with BM, module mismatch 
exists between α
p 
and β
t
, leading to inevitably strain incompatibility 
during tension [16 – 18] . Therefore, understanding the strength-ductility 
evolution of titanium alloy with BM requires consideration of not only 
the deformation behavior of β
t
, but also the deformation coordination 
between α
p 
and β
t
. While numerous studies focus on the effect of aging 
temperature on β
t 
parameters and resultant tensile property evolution, 
few researches on the deformation incompatibility between α
p 
and β
t 
under various aging temperatures have been carried out, which arise 
practical difficulty for the mechanical property optimization of titanium 
alloy with BM.
The purpose of this work is therefore aimed at revealing the effect of 
aging temperature on micro deformation incompatibility and strength- 
ductility evolution of titanium alloy with BM. To achieve this, a novel 
high-strength α + β titanium alloy Ti-5Al-7.5V-0.5Mo-0.5Zr-0.5Si alloy 
(hereafter referred to TC5751S) with exceptional mechanical property 
proposed in our previous work was selected [19,20] . TC5751S alloy was 
solution treated and subsequent aged in various temperatures to obtain 
BM with disparate microstructural features. The deformation behavior 
and strength-ductility evolution of TC5751S under different aging 
temperature were investigated by associating tensile test, fracture 
analysis and nano-indentation. Our work facilitates the understanding of 
deformation incompatibility and strengthening-ductilizing mechanism 
of titanium alloy with BM.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials processing
The novel high-strength α + β TC5751S alloy used in this work was a 
φ 130 mm forging bar provided by Western Superconducting Materials 
Technology Co., Ltd, China. The microstructure of the as-received alloy, 
as illustrated in Fig. 1 (a), was a typical equiaxed microstructure con -
sisting equiaxed primary α phase ( α
p

precipitate. The actual composition of the current TC5751S alloy was 
measured to be 5.34 Al, 7.56 V, 0.518 Mo, 0.515 Zr, 0.422 Si, 0.242 Fe, 
0.153 O, 0.00 06 H, 0.017 N, and balance Ti (wt%). The β -transus 
temperature measured by metallographic method was about 912 ℃ . A 
series of φ 12 mm × 75 mm cylinder samples were cut from the 1/2 ratio

chanical testing. Samples for microstructure characterization and me -
chanical testing were solution treated in 880 
◦

then aged in 500/550/600 ℃ for 8 h, air cooled (referred to as 880 – 500, 
880 – 550 and 880 – 600, as shown in Fig. 1 (b)).
2.2. Mechanical testing
The specimen used for tensile test was a standard cylinder specimen 
fabricated according to Chinese standard GB/T228.1 – 2021, with a 
parallel section length of 35 mm, a diameter of 5 mm and a total length 
of 71 mm. Detailed specimen blueprint can be referred in our previous 
research [21] . Tensile test was performed on a CMT-5105GL tension 
machine equipped with a mechanical extensometer at room tempera -
ture. The strain rate during tension was set to be 1 × 10
 3

hardness of α

TI980). Displacement-controlling model was adopted with the

to be 5 s and the holding time was set to be 2 s. At least 10 
nano-indentation data of each microstructural features were recorded to 
obtain the average hardness.
2.3. Microstructure characterization
The microstructure features of different stated TC5751S alloy were 
characterized using field-emission scanning electron microscope (SEM, 
CLARA GMH, TESCAN, Czech Republic), electron backscatter diffrac -
tion (EBSD, Oxford, Symmetry S2, The United Kingdom) and trans -
mission electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Talos F200X, The United States). 
SEM samples were mechanically polished and subsequently etched 
using an etchant consists of 10 vol% nitric acid, 10 vol% hydrofluoric 
acid and 80 vol% water. EBSD samples were electro polished in an 
electrolyte consists of 60 vol% methanol, 35 vol% normal butanol and 
5 vol% perchloric acid at 25 V, 3 ℃ for 20 s, and the EBSD character -
izations were performed at an acceleration voltage of 20 kV with a step 
size of 0.1 μ m. TEM samples were prepared by twin-jet polishing at a 
voltage of 30 V and temperature of 25 
◦
C, using an electrolyte consists 
of 60 vol% methanol, 35 vol % normal butanol and 5 vol% perchloric 
acid, and the TEM characterizations were performed at an acceleration 
voltage of 200 kV. For volume fraction statistics, at least 5 randomly 
selected SEM images were recorded, and the average volume fraction for 
each microstructural feature were used as the reference value. For size 
statistics, at least 200 samples for each microstructural feature were 
recorded, and the average size were used as the reference value.

5. Conclusion
In this work, the effect of aging temperature on deformation in -
compatibility and strength-ductility evolution of a novel high-strength 
TC5751S alloy with bimodal microstructure were investigated. The 
underlying strengthening-ductilizing mechanism was deciphered via 
fracture analysis and nano-indentation. Dominant conclusions are 
drawn as follows: 
(1) With an increase in aging temperature from 500 
◦
C to 600 
◦
C, the 
average yield strength of TC5751S significantly decreases from 
1218 MPa to 1131 MPa, while the average fracture strain re -
mains changeless, fluctuating within a narrow range of 10 
± 0.1 %.
(2) With aging temperature increases, the α
s 
is significantly coars -
ened, which weakens the impedance of dislocation motion inside 
β
t 
( α
s
+ β ), resulting in the softening of β
t 
and decrease of tensile 
strength in TC5751S alloy. A Hall-Petch-like equation is applied 
to quantify the criterion of α
s 
precipitation on strength evolution 
during aging.
(3) Voids preferentially nucleate at α
p
/ β
t 
interface or within β
t
. With 
aging temperature increases, void nucleation within β
t 
reduces 
owing to the softening of β
t
. Simultaneously, the softening of β
t 
exacerbates the strain mismatch between hard α
p 
and soft β
t, 
leading to increased void nucleation at α
p
/ β
t 
interface. The 
detrimental effect of the α
p
/ β
t 
interface damage on ductility is 
counterbalanced by the positive effect of the reduced damage 
tendency within β
t
, thus maintaining the ductility of TC5751S 
alloy unchanged across various aging temperatures.
