ABSTRACT
γ -TiAl alloys generally require tedious processing steps to eliminate the influence of coarse cast microstructure 
and refine grains before applications, thereby hindering the progress of manufacturing high-performance γ -TiAl 
alloys. Here, a V, B-contained β -solidified γ -TiAl alloy with a composition of Ti-44Al-5Nb-1Mo-2V-0.2B, with 
unique banded heterostructures (BHS), has been successfully developed by variable-temperature hot-pack rolling 
(VT-HPR) and short-time annealing. This BHS is composed of segregative multiphase layers with high-density 
dislocations, twins, and multiscale precipitates, which significantly contributes to the excellent tensile 
strength of 642~762 MPa and outstanding ductility at 800 
◦
C. In addition, the synergistic strengthening effects 
of the dislocation-twin and the heterogeneous interface were revealed.
1. Introduction
γ -TiAl alloys are currently employed to replace heavier nickel-based 
superalloys for aerospace components in the temperature range of 
650 – 850 
◦
C, due to their low density (3.9 – 4.2 g/cm
3
), high strength, and 
creep resistance [ 1 , 2 ]. They effect a substantial weight reduction of ~50 
% compared to their congeneric nickel-based superalloys. For instance, 
in the commercial GEnxTM 1B (Boeing 787) and GEnxTM 2B (Boeing 
747-8) engines, over 40,000 low-pressure turbine blades fabricated from 
Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb alloy (at.%, 4822 alloy) have been utilized to replace 
the original nickel-based superalloy blades [ 3 , 4 ]. As a result, the weight 
of a single engine is reduced by about 200 pounds, and the emissions of 
gases like nitrides are decreased by 80 %. The Airbus 320neo jet engine 
has been equipped with turbine blades made of the third-generation 
forged TiAl alloy [ 5 ]. However, these alloys generally possess the 
limited hot workability and tedious processing steps, which mainly 
originates from the coarse α
2
/ γ lamellar microstructure and restricted 
dislocations gliding. Moreover, the mechanical strength of these alloys 
appear to restrict the maximum applicable temperature to 700 
◦
C, which 
will conversely limit their commercial applications [ 1 , 5 ]. For the 
large-scale engineering application of γ -TiAl alloys, it is still necessary to 
optimize the suitable microstructure in the hot-work process and the 
mechanical properties at the operating temperatures. To overcome the 
constraints of commercial applications, great attentions have been 
placed on achieving a good balance between strength and ductility in 
TiAl alloys [ 6 , 7 ].
The mechanical properties of TiAl alloys are extremely dependent on 
their microstructures, whereas, microstructures are closely related to the 
chemical composition, process method and heat treatment. In order to 
improve comprehensive performance of traditional TiAl alloys, various 
specific measures are introduced, such as adding β -stable elements [ 8 , 9 ], 
toughening elements (microalloying) [ 10 ], plastic deformation (forging 
[ 11 ], extrusion [ 7 , 12 ] and rolling [ 13 ]), cast-rolling [ 14 ], powder 
metallurgy [ 15 ], multi-stage mechanical-heat treatment [ 16 , 17 ], trig -
gering nanotwin or a single crystal structure [ 18 , 19 ], etc. The rein -
forcement core of above methods is almost expanded around the 
recrystallization and grain refinements. However, from the Hall-Petch 
effect, blindly reducing the grain size can not continuously improve 
the strength synchronizing with large ductility.

microstructure (BHS). Based on this design strategy, an ordered coor -

phase transformation, high-density dislocation strengthening, TWIP ef -
fect, HDI strengthening/hardening, and multiscale precipitates. This 
mechanism leads to a significant improvement in the overall mechanical 
properties in operating temperature.

The microstructure of the cast Ti-44Al-5Nb-1Mo-(2V-0.2B) alloy is 
shown in Fig. 2 a and b, which compare the effects of the addition of V

α
2

0

an α →γ phase transformation, resulting in lamellar colonies with 
different lamellar orientations, and the B addition refines the colonies to 
below ~70 μ m. The phase diagram shown in Fig. 2 c verifies the β -phase 
solidification path of the Ti-44Al-5Nb-1Mo-2V-0.2B alloy [ 28 ].

3.3. Deformation behaviour of VT-HPR5 specimen (50 % reduction)
To gain insight into the distinct performance and deformation 
mechanisms governing the control of the alloys, a detailed examination 
of the banded microstructural evolution and deformation behaviour is 
performed by EBSD and TEM.
In VT-HPR5 specimen, the complete softening of multiphase grains is 
not achieved, and a small amount of hard residual α
2
/ γ lamellar colonies 
(~30 nm) is preserved ( Fig. 4 a – c). This outcome can be attributed to the 
decomposition of initial α
2
/ γ lamellar structure ( Fig. 2 b). Moreover, VT- 
HPR process has also produced a banded heterostructure (BHS), with 
alternating distributions of fine α
2
, γ , and β
0 
grains (or subgrains) 
ranging from < 1 to 10 μ m along the RD (within the red line region of 
Fig. 4 a). Essentially, BHS is composed of three kinds of grains with 
different deformation tendencies through multi-level construction. This 
multi-level heterostructure is conducive to enlarging the strain gradient, 
resulting in additional HDI strengthening, thereby enhancing the 
working hardening ability of VT-HPR5 specimen. During the VT-HPR 
process, the stress concentration can also be significantly relieved to 
prevent cracking and the formation of residual lamellae.
Based on the EBSD results of BHS in Fig. 4d1-d3 , it is evident that the 
β and α grains contain numerous low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) 
and medium-angle grain boundaries (MAGBs) (L2, L3 in Fig. 5 b and c). 
While the γ grains internalize a higher concentration of high-angle grain 
boundaries (HAGBs) and twin boundaries (TBs) (L1 in Fig. 5 a). This 
indicates that dynamic recovery (DRV) process primarily occurs within 
β ( β
0
) and α ( α
2
) phases to form fine substructures [ 10 , 12 ]. While γ phase, 
with a low stacking fault energy, mainly undergoes dynamic recrystal -
lization (DRX) and mechanical twins [ 32 ]. At this stage, the energy 
stored in γ phase is relatively low, placing it in a more stable state. This 
stable γ grain region plays a crucial role in restraining the plastic 
deformation of β
0 
and α
2 
layers, which in turn suppresses DRV/DRX of β 
and α grains, thereby preserving the metastable state of β and α phases 
(with large amounts of LAGBs and MAGBs). Fig. 4d4 shows that the 
schmid factor (SF) values to describe the difficulty of grain deformation. 
Generally, grains with SF value more than 0.4 are prone to dislocation 
gliding and plastic deformation [ 33 ]. It can be observed that SF values of 
BHS grains are almost located between 0.3 and 0.5 (bright red and or -
ange colors). High-SF grains are more conducive to the subsequent 
plastic deformation. Thus, the present BHS TiAl alloy possesses the key 
combination of three characteristics, i.e., multiple phases, metastability, 
and lamination.
TEM images in Fig. 4 e display submicron and nanoscale banded 
structures of three phases, with an average thickness of 100 nm – 1 μ m, 
and fine twin structures with thicknesses of 30~150 nm along with 
high-density dislocation network are discovered in γ layer ( Fig. 4e1-e3 ). 
Furthermore, some dislocations induced by VT-HPR process are blocked 
and accumulated at the γ / β interface, and then cause the local stress 
concentration to form the strengthened curved interfaces ( Fig. 4e4 ). This 
observation also points to the superior dislocation storage capability of 
the heterogeneous interface [ 34 , 35 ].

3.5. Texture of VT-HPR5 and VT-HPR7 specimens
γ phase VT-HPR and cooling process promote the formation of γ 
phase, either in the form of nano-lamellae, or as precipitates in the β 
phase, or DRY γ grain layers. Some specific phase transformation paths 
affect the change of γ texture. As can be seen from the features of pole 
figures, γ phase (including DRX γ ) must have been deformed signifi -
cantly ( Fig. 8 b and e).
After rolling 4 passes (VT-HPR5), γ presents a modified Cube texture 
( Fig. 8 b), which usually occurs as the result of recrystallization of γ in 
the hot-rolled TiAl alloy [ 44 , 45 ]. This texture has been reported to be 
relatively stable, due to occupying the favorable direction during 
deformation [ 46 ]. Even after further rolling (VT-HPR7), γ still maintain 
the obvious Cube texture component, and the texture strength becomes 
stronger. Some small deviations can be observed in the divergence of the 
pole figures, originating from the texture inheritance of β phase. In 
addition, the sub-micron-scale γ precipitates in the inner β phase may 
also contribute to the formation of the Cube texture. β undergoes a 
transformation from the recovery process to recrystallization process,

in the VT-HPR process and interacts with the multiple twin structure; 
simultaneously the phase transformation of β and α grains is inhibited to 
ensure the stability of BHS with the alternating distribution of multi -
phase; thus BHS is retained during the subsequent annealing. Addi -
tionally, γ precipitates in the β phase ( Fig. 12 d) provides additional γ / β 
heterogrnrous interfaces and HDI strengthening effects. These ulti -
mately results in a three-metastable-phase BHS TiAl alloy with dense 
twins and precipitates.
5. Conclusions
In summary, VT-HPR and short-time annealing were performed to 
obtained a novel BHS on a β -solidified γ -TiAl alloy in this investigation. 
The excellent high-temperature mechanical properties with a high 
strength of 642~762 MPa and elongation of 51.6 – 61 % mainly come 
from the synergistic mechanical behavior of the dislocation-twins and 
heterogeneous interface. Firstly, during the initial rolling, the rapid 
decomposition of α
2
/ γ lamellar colonies, and DRX of γ grains leads to the 
main deformation. At this time, the multiplication of dislocations pro -
vides the driving force for subsequent deformation. Then, by regulating 
the stability of α and β phases, the phase transformation, multiphase 
recrystallization, and the interaction between mechanical twins and 
dislocations cause the change of the multiscale heterogeneous charac -
teristics, activate multiple strengthening mechanisms simultaneously. 
Finally, the later rolling and annealing processes promote the formation 
of twin nano-lamellae and submicron/nano-scale γ precipitates at the 
heterogeneous boundaries, it is possible to avoid the formation of brittle- 
broad α
2
/ γ lamellar colonies while maintaining the alloy composition 
unchanged. This can significantly enhance the strength and ductility of 
γ -TiAl alloy by making the ordered superimposition of ODP, TRIP or 
TWIP, HDI and precipitation strengthening mechanisms. This 
manufacturing strategy offers a new approach for developing high- 
strength and ductile TiAl alloys, or other intermetallic compound 
materials.
