Effect of V addition on microstructure and mechanical
property in the Nb–Si alloy system
Won-Yong Kima,∗, In-Dong Yeoa, Tae-Yeub Raa, Gue-Serb Cho a, Mok-Soon Kim b
a Korea Institute of Industrial Technology (KITECH), Incheon 404-254, South Korea

Abstract
Microstructure and mechanical property of vanadium-added two-phase alloys consisting of /H9251-Nb5Si3 phase and Nb solid solution (Nb ss)
were investigated in terms of a variation of chemical composition and compression test at room temperature and at 1773 K. The equilibrium
phase ﬁeld of
/H9251-Nb5Si3 and Nbss in the Nb–Si–V ternary alloy system was examined at 1773 K using scanning electron microscopy equipped
with wavelength dispersive X-ray spectroscopy and X-ray diffraction. The/H9251-Nb5Si3 phase formed in the Nb–Si–V system was extended along
the Nb5Si3–V5Si3 pseudo-binary line without a solubility range of Si at 1773 K, while vanadium is soluble continuously along the Nb–V
phase boundary with Si solubility of about 0.7 at%. It is observed that volume fraction ratio of the present alloy is predominantly dependent on
Si content but exhibited no dependence on vanadium content. With increasing vanadium content, primary
/H9251-Nb5Si3 and Nbss phases became
ﬁner through the whole composition studied. The 0.2% offset yield strength at room temperature increased with increasing volume fraction of
/H9251-Nb5Si3 phase and with increasing vanadium content in Nbss. In contrast, the 0.2% offset yield strength at 1773 K decreased with increasing
vanadium content in Nbss. Details will be discussed in correlation with microstructure, phase relation and mechanical property.

in the ﬁeld of electric power system [1]. If we could de-

an argon gas atmosphere with a non-consumable tungsten
electrode. The button ingots were re-melted at least ﬁve
times for chemical homogeneity. The ingots were then heat
treated under an argon atmosphere at 1773 K for 100 h
followed by cooling to room temperature. The cooling rate

Two kinds of chain structures, strongly bonded –Si–Mo–Si–

the [100] direction and [001] direction. The selective sub-

in an increase of c/a ratio in the present Nb–Si–V alloy sys-
tem. Therefore, the selective substitution behavior of V for
Nb sites may lead to an increase in c/a ratio, and there-
fore enhance the phase stability of /H9251-Nb
5Si3 in the present
Nb–Si–V alloy system.

3.3. Mechanical property
Mechanical tests were carried out in compression using
various samples at room temperature and at 1773 K. Among
the samples prepared, two systematic variations were em-
ployed to clarify the effects of V and Si content in the
present alloys. At constant V content, volume fraction of
constituent phases could be veriﬁed by changing the Si
content, while at constant Nb and Si content volume fract
ion ratio of constituent phases could be kept constantly.
Thereby the inﬂuence of volume fraction could be negli-
gible to describe the mechanical property obtained at con-
stant Nb and Si content in the present alloy. The 0.2% off-
set yield stresses at room temperature and at 1773 K plot-
ted as a function of V content are presented in Fig. 5 .
With increasing V content, yield stresses for the samples
tested at room temperature increased for both hypo-eutectic
Nb–18Si–xV and hyper-eutectic Nb–20Si–xV alloys, where
x is a variable and at%. For the overall V content, it is ob-
served that Nb–20Si– xV alloys display higher yield stress
than Nb–18Si–xV at room temperature.
Here, if we are considering several major factors to cause
an inﬂuence on mechanical property, solid solution hard-
ening, volume fraction ratio as well as microstructure will

volume fraction of /H9251–Nb

temperature strength and, (ii) the increasing V content in
Nb

in easy glide or climb motion of dislocations, which gives
rise to a decrease of high temperature strength. At room tem-
perature or in the low temperature region, the former effect
as well as solid solution strengthening may play a dominant
role to increase the yield stress. The latter would become
prevailing with increasing test temperature. Therefore, the
balance between (i) and (ii) terms described just above may
be responsible for the no apparent change in yield stress at
1773 K.

4. Conclusions
The effect of V addition on microstructure and mechani-
cal property at room temperature and at 1773 K was investi-
gated in the Nb–Si alloy system. The following results were
obtained from the study.
1.
/H9251-Nb5Si3 phase is extended along the Nb 5Si3–V5Si3
pseudo-binary line without having a solubility range of
Si at 1773 K, suggesting that V substitutes for Nb sites in
/H9251-Nb
5Si3 phase, while V is soluble continuously along
the Nb–V phase boundary with Si solubility of about
0.7 at%. Ternary element V is predominantly partitioned
into Nb solid solution than /H9251-Nb
5Si3 phase.
2. The c/a axis ratio of /H9251-Nb5Si3 phase increases with in-
creasing V content up to about 8.7 at% and exhibits al-
most no change with furthermore increasing content of V
in /H9251-Nb
5Si3. The lattice parameter of Nb solid solution
decreases linearly with increasing content of V .
3. V olume fraction ratio of the present alloy system is pri-
marily dependent on Si content but not V . With increasing
V content the microstructure of primary /H9251-Nb
5Si3 and
Nb solid solution becomes ﬁner at hyper-eutectic com-
position and at hypo-eutectic composition, respectively.
4. Offset yield strength (0.2%) at room temperature in-
creases with increasing volume fraction of /H9251-Nb
5Si3
phase and with increasing V content in Nb solid solu-
tion. In contrast, 0.2% offset yield strength at 1773 K
decreases with increasing V content in Nb solid solution.
It is suggested that higher volume fraction of /H9251-Nb
5Si3
phase and higher V content in Nb solid solution are
favored to increase the room temperature yield strength
in the present two-phase Nb
5Si3/Nb solid solution inter-
metallic alloys. The decrease in yield strength at 1773
K is closely associated with the solid solution softening
due to a thermally activated diffusion process, which
is promoted with increasing V content, in the present
alloys system.
