ABSTRACT
In order to obtain more knowledge to replace or lower the content of expensive and heavy elements Re and Ru in
advanced Ni-based single crystal superalloys, the role of Ru playing in the microstructure and creep performance
at 1100 °C and 140 MPa was evaluated in this study by considering its individual eﬀ ect and also synergizing it
with Mo to maximize the bene ﬁcial eﬀect from each element. A series of single crystal superalloys with varying
alloying elements were investigated and their initial microstructure and microstructural evolution during the
creep deformation were characterized. It was shown that both Mo and Ru could increase the γ/γ' lattice mis ﬁt
and density of interfacial dislocations, that contributed to improve the creep resistance at high temperature and
low stress condition. The inferior microstructural stability regarding to the TCP phase formation in alloys with
more Mo addition lowered creep performance to some extent, however, that could be restrained by the addition
of Ru. The high temperature and low stress creep performance could be enhanced by the co-addition of Mo and
Ru since their synergistic eﬀ ect could be able to enhance the γ/γ' interfacial strengthening and retain the mi-
crostructural stability.
1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal superalloys are widely used for turbine
blades in aircraft engines. Considering their service condition, the creep
performance of single crystal superalloys at high temperature
(> 1000 °C) and low stress (100 –150 MPa) level attracts much atten-
tion of researchers. The enhanced creep performance of single crystal
superalloys in multiple generations could be partially attributed to the
constant increase of refractory alloying elements such as Re, W, Mo and
Ru [1]. These elements a ﬀect the microstructural features and evolution
in di ﬀerent ways, including γ channel width, γ' size and volume frac-
tion, γ/γ' lattice mis ﬁt, the formation of topologically close-packed
(TCP) phases as well as the dislocation substructure during creep de-
formation, etc.
Re has always been considered as the most e ﬀective element for the
enhancement of creep performance since it was added into second
generation single crystal superalloys. It was reported that Re could slow
the coarsening kinetics of γ′ precipitates and increase the γ/γ′ lattice
misﬁt [2]. Earlier atom-probe microanalysis revealed the occurrence of
small Re clusters in size of 10 nm in Re-modi ﬁed superalloys, which
could act as more e ﬃcient obstacles against the dislocation motion [3].
However, Re addition endangers the microstructural stability by ser-
ving as a strong TCP phase-former [4]. In addition, the high price of Re
and its importance as a strategic element limits the practical application
of superalloys with high Re content [5]. Mo with a large di ﬀerence in
the electronic structure and atomic radius from Ni is added into Ni-
based single crystal superalloys for its signi ﬁcant solid solution
strengthening in the γ matrix [1]. The earlier studies indicated that
more Mo addition could bene ﬁt the creep property by increasing the
density of γ/γ′ interfacial dislocation networks [6], although the ne-
gative eﬀect of Mo on the microstructural stability was also found [1,7].
In order to explore more potential of Mo on increasing the creep re-
sistance, the e ﬀects of Mo addition during the creep deformation at
high temperature and low stress condition was investigated in this
study. It is expected to provide the possibility to decrease the addition
of expensive Re.
Microstructural instability caused by the high content of solid
strengthening elements in single crystal superalloys needs to be sup-
pressed
for the better creep performance. As a strong TCP phase-sup-
pressor, Ru was added into the fourth generation single crystal super-
alloys [8,9]. Ru has been reported to enhance the microstructural
stability through inhibiting the nucleation and growth of TCP phases

2. Materials and experimental procedures
Seven alloys in a series of Ni-based single crystal superalloys with
diﬀerent Co (~ 7.0 and 15.0 wt%), Cr (~ 3.5 and 6.0 wt%), Mo (~ 1.0
and 2.5 wt%) and Ru (~ 2.5 and 4.0 wt%) additions were examined in
this study. Single crystal bars with the growth orientation < 001 >
(15 mm in diameter and 150 mm in length) were directionally solidi-
ﬁed at the Beijing Institute of Aeronautical Materials. These alloys were
named as SC plus four numbers representing the level of Co, Cr, Mo and
Ru additions in turn ( “1” and “2” denoting the low and high level of
each element, respectively). Based on the chemical composition, in-
vestigated alloys in current study were classi ﬁed into three sets, as
listed in Table 1. For alloys in Set I, alloys SC1121 and SC1112 have
higher level of Mo or Ru, compared to the baseline alloy SC1111 with
the low level of Co, Cr, Mo and Ru. In Set II and Set III, both Mo and Ru
contents were increased from the low level in baseline alloys SC2111
and SC1211 to the high level in alloys SC2122 and SC1222. Standard
heat treatment (solution treatment at elevated temperature in the range
of 1300 –1335 °C for 8– 16 h followed by the two-stage aging at 1150 °C
for 4 h and 870 °C for 24 h) was performed on the directionally solidi-
ﬁed single crystal bars. Tensile creep rupture tests and interrupted tests
with 1.0% creep strain were conducted at 1100 °C and 140 MPa by
using specimens employed the standard heat treatment. The loading
direction was parallel to the single crystal growth direction < 001 > .
Meanwhile, thermal exposure without stress at 1100 °C for di ﬀerent
times was also performed following the standard heat treatments.

3. Results
3.1. Initial microstructure
As the initial condition for following creep tests, γ/γ′ micro-
structures in experimental alloys after the standard heat treatment were
examined. The size, area fraction, volume fraction and feret ratio of γ′
precipitates as well as the γ channel width in dendrite centers of ex-
perimental alloys were measured and listed in Table 2. γ′ precipitates in
all alloys presented cuboidal, near cuboidal or rectangular morpholo-
gies with the average feret ratio in a range from 1.5 to 2.0, larger than
2 . Fig. 2 shows the typical microstructures and distributions of γ′
morphology in alloy SC1211 with the lowest average ferret ratio and
alloy SC1222 with the highest average ferret ratio. By comparing the
feret ratios of experimental alloys in each alloy set, it was suggested
that Mo and Ru additions slightly increased the feret ratio. In general,
the average γ′ volume fraction of experimental alloys was ~ 60% and γ′
size was ~ 300 nm. For alloys SC1211 and SC1222 in Set III with the
higher Cr content, the γ′ size and γ′ volume fraction were smaller and γ
width was larger than other alloys in Set I and Set II with the lower Cr
content. No signi ﬁcant inﬂuence of Mo and Ru additions on the γ′ size,
γ′ volume fraction and γ channel width was found in experimental al-
loys.
3.2. γ/γ′ elemental partitioning behavior
Compositions of γ and γ′ phases in experimental alloys after the
standard heat treatment were measured by the chemical analysis after
the electrolytic phase extraction procedure, as shown in Table 3. Si-
milar to the elemental partitioning behavior in the majority of Ni-based
single crystal superalloys, it was showed that Al and Ta partitioned into
γ′ phase (partitioning ratios were less than 1), while Cr, Mo, Re and Ru
enriched γ phase (partitioning ratios were greater than 1). Additionally,
it should be mentioned that the partitioning ratio of W between γ and γ′
phases was slightly less than or near to unity in these experimental
alloys, which could be attributed to the presence of Re and Ru. Di ﬀerent
additions of Mo and Ru in ﬂuenced the partitioning behaviors of
themselves and other alloying elements, especially those elements with
a large atomic radius (Re, W, Mo and Cr). In comparison to alloy
SC1111, the high level of Ru addition in alloy SC1112 decreased the γ/
γ′ partitioning ratios of Cr, Mo and Re to di ﬀerent extent, while more
Mo content in alloy SC1121 increased γ/γ′ partitioning ratios of Mo, Re
and W. Moreover, increasing both Mo and Ru additions promoted the
Cr, Mo and Re to concentrate more in γ phase, as indicated by com-
paring alloys SC2111 and SC2122 in Set II containing high Co content.
However, no signi ﬁcant e ﬀect of Mo and Ru additions on the γ/γ′
partitioning
ratios was found in alloys SC1211 and SC1222 in Set III
with the high level of Cr addition.
3.3. γ/γ′ lattice mis ﬁt
The lattice mis ﬁts of γ and γ′ phases in all experimental alloys at
room temperature (~ 25 °C, measured by synchrotron X-ray di ﬀraction)
and 1100 °C (calculated by Eq. (3)) are listed in Table 4. The magni-
tudes of γ/γ′ lattice mis ﬁt at 1100 °C were larger than those at room
temperature, and they showed the same trend with increasing the ad-
ditions of Mo and Ru. In comparison of three alloys in Set I, more Mo
addition was found to increase the magnitude of γ/γ′ lattice misﬁt from
−0.17 at 25 °C and −0.37 at 1100 °C in alloy SC1111 to −0.25 at 25 °C
and −0.46 at 1100 °C in alloy SC1121, and higher Ru content increased
the lattice mis ﬁt magnitude to −0.33 at 25 °C and −0.52 at 1100 °C in
alloy SC1112. The lattice mis ﬁts of alloys in Set II with high level of Co
addition and alloys in Set III with high level of Cr addition at 25 °C and
1100 °C also became more negative with increasing both Mo and Ru.

The creep properties of all investigated alloys at 1100 °C/140 MPa

3.5.2. Formation of TCP phases
Due to the di ﬀerent alloying additions, experimental alloys had the
diﬀerent tendency to form TCP phases during the creep deformation at
1100 °C/140 MPa. It was found that TCP precipitates with the di ﬀerent
volume fraction formed in four experimental alloys SC1121, SC2122,
SC1211 and SC1222 after 1.0% interrupted creep testing and the vo-
lume fraction of TCP precipitates in alloys SC1111, SC1112 and SC2111
was at the near-zero level, as listed in Table 6 . This is consistent with
the TCP phase formation tendency in alloys after the long term stress-
free thermal exposure reported in the earlier article [24]. Fig. 5 de-
monstrated the transverse microstructures (perpendicular to the
loading direction) in the gauge sections of four experimental alloys with
the TCP phase formation after the interrupted creep testing. For alloys
in Set I, a large volume fraction of TCP precipitates ((10.8 ± 1.4) %,
shown in Table 6 and Fig. 5(a)) appeared in alloy SC1121 in compar-
ison to alloys SC1111 and SC1112, indicating that higher Mo content
strongly promoted the TCP phase formation. For alloys in Set II, the
volume fraction of TCP precipitates increased slightly from ~ 0 in alloy
SC2111 to ~ 0.3% in alloy SC2122 ( Fig. 5(b)) with increasing both Mo
and Ru additions. Moreover, the formation tendency of TCP phases was
raised by a small degree from ~ 9% in the volume fraction in alloy
SC1211 ( Fig. 5(c)) and to ~ 11% in alloy SC1222 ( Fig. 5(d)) by in-
creasing both Mo and Ru additions in Set III alloys. It was suggested
that the strong promotive e ﬀect of Mo on the formation of TCP phases
could be restrained by the addition of Ru. It should be mentioned that
the high volume fraction of TCP phases formed in Set III alloys was
attributed to the high level of Cr content.

microstructural stability regarding the formation of TCP phases and γ/
γ′ rafting contribute to the creep performance. These contributors are

4.1.1. γ′ phase and secondary phase strengthening
As the main secondary phase in Ni-based superalloys, the initial size
and volume fraction of γ′ precipitates after the standard heat treatment
inﬂuence the strengthening e ﬀect and creep behavior signi ﬁcantly
[32–34]. It was reported that the creep property at 1100 °C/137 MPa
was enhanced ﬁrstly and then decreased with increasing the initial size
of γ′ phase. Meanwhile, the optimum γ′ size depended on the γ/γ′ lattice
misﬁt: for alloys with the lower lattice mis ﬁt magnitude, the optimum
initial γ′ size for high temperature and low stress creep behavior was
larger compared to alloys with greater lattice mis ﬁt [33]. γ′ size in
current experimental alloys after the standard heat treatment was in the
range of 280– 340 nm with no signi ﬁcant change with di ﬀerent alloying
additions, as the same to the optimum γ′ size range (~ 320 nm)
reported for Ni-based single crystal superalloys containing both Re and
Ru additions [33]. Therefore, the initial size of γ' phase in this study is
expected to have minor e ﬀect on the creep behavior at high tempera-
ture and low stress condition. Murakumo et al. [34] reported that the
creep behavior of Re-containing TMS-75 single crystal superalloy at
1100 °C/137 MPa showed a tendency to be enhanced with increasing
the γ' fraction to ~ 55% and then the creep performance dropped down.

performance (longest creep rupture time and time to 1.0% creep strain

4.2. E ﬀect of Mo addition
The increase in Mo content from 1.0 wt% in alloy SC1111 to 2.4 wt
% in alloy SC1121 caused the minor e ﬀect on the volume fraction of γ'
phase (Table 2) and the concentrations of Re and Ru with low di ﬀusion
coeﬃcients in γ phase ( Table 3), therefore, no signi ﬁcant e ﬀect of Mo
on the precipitation strengthening and solid solution strengthening was
expected at this point. As shown in Table 3, Mo itself with the parti-
tioning ratio of greater than 2 and the atomic radius 12% larger than Ni
was strongly concentrated in the γ phase, which would result in the
enhancement of γ/γ' lattice mis ﬁt and interfacial dislocation density.
Therefore, Mo could act as an important creep enhancer from this
perspective. However, the results showed that the creep property at
high temperature and low stress decreased with increasing the Mo
addition as comparing alloys SC1111 and SC1121. This could be at-
tributed to the e ﬀect of Mo on the microstructural instability, especially
the precipitation of TCP phases. As shown in Table 6, high Mo content
in alloy SC1121 strongly promoted the formation of TCP phases during
the creep deformation. The e ﬀect of Mo content on the precipitation of
TCP phase in this series of single crystal superalloys was also shown
through the experimental results after long term stress-free thermal
exposure [24]. This harmful e ﬀect of more Mo addition on the micro-
structural stability overweighed its bene ﬁcial eﬀect
on the lattice mis ﬁt
and interfacial dislocation structure, and consequently lowered the
creep performance of the alloy with high Mo content.
4.3. E ﬀect of Ru
As Ru content increased from 2.6 wt% in alloy SC1111 to 4.0 wt% in
alloy SC1112, no signi ﬁcant eﬀect was found on the fraction and size of
γ' phase ( Table 2). Due to the low di ﬀusion coe ﬃcient of Ru, high Ru
content in alloy SC1112 was expected to decrease the interdi ﬀusion
coeﬃcient ∼D and thus enhance the solid solution strengthening by a
certain extent. No TCP phase precipitation in either alloy SC1111 or
SC1112 occurred during the creep deformation ( Table 6). In addition,
more Ru content could increase the γ/γ' lattice mis ﬁt and the density of
interfacial dislocation structure ( Table 4). These bene ﬁcial eﬀects of Ru
on the microstructural factors contributed to the creep performance
improvement of alloy SC1112 with high content of Ru compared to
alloy SC1111. It should be emphasized that although the partitioning
ratio of Re slightly decreased from 13.4 in alloy SC1111 to 13.0 in alloy
SC1112 ( Table 3), the larger γ/γ' lattice mis ﬁt and denser interfacial
dislocations in alloy SC1112 could be more attributed to the fact that
Ru itself strongly enriched the γ phase and the atomic radius of the Ru
atom is 7.2% greater than Ni. Therefore, similar to Mo from this aspect,
Ru could play an important role in the creep strengthening at the high
temperature and low stress conditions.
4.4. Synergistic eﬀ ect of Mo and Ru
Extrapolating from each individual e ﬀect of Mo and Ru discussed as
the above, it could be considered that the γ/γ' interfacial dislocation
structure and the formation of TCP phases were two main micro-
structural factors which Mo and Ru additions a ﬀected, and conse-
quently the high temperature and low stress creep performance was
inﬂuenced in this study. Mo could serve as a creep performance en-
hancing element through lowering γ/γ' interfacial dislocation network
spacing. However, the negative e ﬀect of Mo on the microstructural
stability played a greater role and thus the decrease of creep perfor-
mance was caused by the high level of Mo addition. Actually, the
double-side eﬀect of Mo could be balanced by the addition of Ru, since
Ru is such an excellent microstructural stabilizer as found in this study
and others [10,11,24]. Furthermore, Ru was also able to improve the γ/
γ' interfacial strengthening e ﬀect. The synergistic e ﬀect of Mo and Ru
could be revealed from the investigated alloys in Set II and Set III in this
study. Compared to the baseline alloys SC2111 and SC1211 for each
alloy set, the formation tendency of TCP phases was slightly increased
by more co-addition of Mo and Ru in alloys SC2122 and SC1222
(Table 6), which was resulted from the balance of opposite e ﬀects of Mo
and Ru on the microstructural stability. In addition, the experimental
results showed that the dislocation network spacings in the baseline
alloys SC2111 and SC1211 were decreased dramatically by more co-
addition of Mo and Ru ( Table 4), indicating the stronger γ/γ' interfacial
strengthening
eﬀect. As a result, the creep performance was enhanced
as increasing both Mo and Ru contents could play their roles sy-
nergistically to improve the γ/γ' interfacial strengthening and retain the
microstructural stability.
5. Conclusion
The individual and synergistic e ﬀects of Mo and Ru playing in the
microstructure and creep resistance at high temperature and low stress
condition were evaluated in this study. Following conclusions can be
drawn:
(1) For the initial microstructures in investigated alloys, no signi ﬁcant
inﬂuence of Mo and Ru additions on the γ′ size, γ′ volume fraction
and γ channel width was found. Increasing each and both of Mo and
Ru additions resulted in more negative lattice mis ﬁt.
(2) The creep property at 1100 °C/140 MPa was decreased with in-
creasing the Mo addition from 1.0 wt% to 2.4 wt% and improved
with increasing the Ru addition from 2.6 wt% to 4.0 wt%.
Meanwhile, it was indicated that increasing both Mo and Ru ad-
ditions extended the creep rupture time and the time to 1.0% strain
and lowered the minimum creep rate.
(3) It is suggested that the γ/γ' interfacial dislocation structure and the
formation of TCP phases are two main microstructural factors that
signiﬁcantly in ﬂuence the high temperature and low stress creep
performance in this study. The harmful e ﬀect of more Mo addition
on the microstructural stability overweighed its bene ﬁcial eﬀect on
the interfacial dislocation structure, and consequently lowered the
creep performance of the alloy with high Mo content. Ru could
serve as not only the microstructural stabilizer but also the γ/γ'
interfacial strengthener. The strong promotive e ﬀect of Mo on the
formation of TCP phases was restrained by the addition of Ru. The
creep performance was enhanced by the increase of both Mo and Ru
contents because of their synergistic e ﬀect to improve the γ/γ' in-
terfacial strengthening and retain the microstructural stability.
