MARTIN DETROIS,1,3 STOICHKO ANTONOV,1

1.—Illinois Institute of Technology, Chicago, IL 60616, USA. 2.—Rolls Royce Corporation,

In response to the increasing temperature capability of the structural mate-
rials required for advanced gas turbine engines, new alloying concepts are
required to develop materials with properties that are signiﬁcantly better
than existing nickel-base superalloys. Recent investigations have focused on
the development of polycrystalline, ternary eutectic c-c¢-d Ni-base superalloys
that use large volume fractions of the intermetallic d phase to provide com-
posite strengthening. While compositional changes enabled the formation of
the d phase precipitates, in some alloys an additional precipitate phase g was
formed. As the effects of these phases on high-temperature mechanical prop-
erties are not well quantiﬁed, a better understanding of the thermodynamics
and kinetics associated with the formation of these d and g phase precipitates
is required for future designs of Ni-base superalloys. A set of experimental
alloys was investigated to understand the formation of the d and g phase
precipitates in Ni-base superalloys. When the alloy chemistry was observed to
exhibit a compositional ratio of Al/(Nb+Ta+Ti) less than 1, d and/or g phase
precipitates formed, whereas a ratio greater than 1 resulted in conventional
c-c¢ microstructures. For alloys in which d and/or g phase precipitates were
formed, the prevalent phase could be determined by evaluating the composi-
tional ratio for (Nb+Ta)/(Al+Ti). Alloys that had ratios greater than 1 were
largely composed of d phase precipitates, whereas a ratio less than 1 resulted
in the predominance of the g phase precipitates.
INTRODUCTION
As a material of choice for turbine engines and
power generation application, Ni-base superalloys
have been under constant development to achieve
higher strength and fatigue resistance when sub-
jected to elevated temperatures, corrosive environ-
ments, or high stress levels.
1–9 Most polycrystalline
Ni-base superalloys possess microstructures con-
sisting of ordered L1
2 intermetallic precipitates c¢
(Ni3Al) distributed coherently within a disordered
face-centered cubic matrix c (Ni). The superior high-
temperature mechanical properties exhibited by
these alloys rely heavily on the two-phase micro-
structure that provides substantial order and
coherency type strengthening by obstructing the
movement of dislocations during plastic deforma-
tion. Modern polycrystalline Ni-base superalloys
often contain Al, Ti, Nb, and Ta to yield an opti-
mized volume fraction of the c¢ strengthening phase
as well as controlled concentrations of refractory
elements (W, Mo, Cr, and Co) to provide solid-solu-
tion strengthening to the c matrix and the precipi-
tates. As the property limits associated with Ni-base
superalloys exhibiting conventional c-c¢ phase
microstructures are approached, innovative alloy-
ing concepts and new classes of materials need to be
considered to meet the design requirements of
future gas turbine engines.
Initially considered as candidate materials for gas
turbine airfoil, directionally solidiﬁed c/c¢-d alloys
were originally developed in the 1970s and 1980s.
These alloys have been shown to exhibit superior
longitudinal tensile strength and creep resistance
when compared with the best performing c-c¢
Ni-base superalloys available at the time by addi-
tion of the precipitate phase d (Ni
3Nb) of D0a crystal
structure. Despite the attractive properties, the
application of these directionally solidiﬁed c/c¢-d
eutectic alloys for airfoil components has been

In addition to the d phase, g phase precipitates

of how various elemental additions inﬂuence the
microstructure and properties of the alloy is neces-
sary for the development of high-performance
polycrystalline c-c¢-d eutectic alloys. Considering
that thermodynamic database model predictions are
not yet accurate enough to predict the phase sta-
bility of alloys that lie outside of the bounds of
conventional alloy systems, the present investiga-
tion was aimed at quantifying the compositional
dependence on the formation of g and d phases in a
series of experimental alloys and developing rela-
tionships that could be used to predict the long-term
stability of the d and g phase precipitates.
PROCEDURE
A set of experimental alloys was designed to
assess the effect of Ti and Ta alloying additions on
the d and g precipitate phase stability in Ni-base
superalloys. A total of nine alloys were prepared
with compositions compiled in Table I.
While the atomic percent of Cr, Mo, and W were
kept constant and the ratio of Nb over Al equal to 1,
the atomic percent of Ti and Ta followed the relation
Ti[at.%] + Ta[at.%] = 1, 2, or 3%. All of the alloys
were prepared using pure Ni (99.95%), Cr (99.5%),
Al (99.9%), Ti (99.9%), Nb (99.98%), Ta (99.9%), Mo
(99.8%), and W (99.9%) melted in a MRF ABJ-900
(MRF Inc., Suncook, NH) arc melter under an argon
atmosphere. Prior to the melting of each of the
alloys, the melting chamber was purged under
vacuum to a pressure of 10
/C03 mbar and then back-
ﬁlled with high-purity argon (99.998%). The process
was repeated three times prior to melting to ensure
cleanliness of the arc melt ingots. Additionally, each
ingot was melted and remelted up to six times to
convectively mix and uniformly distribute the
alloying additions throughout the ingot. The ingots
were then homogenized at 1200/C176 C for 48 h and aged
at 1100/C176 C for 38 h.
After preparation of the ingots, samples were
metallographically prepared for observation under a
JEOL JSM-5900LV (JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan)

10 mm, 46, and 60 s, respectively. EDS analyses

RESULTS
The SEM micrographs taken at a magniﬁcation of
1,000 times are shown in Fig. 1 for all of the
experimental alloys. The two precipitate phases, d
and g, present within the microstructures were
observed to exhibit a variety of different morpholo-
gies. Because of the characteristic compositional
differences between the phases, contrast differences
from the backscattered mode on the SEM were ini-
tially used to differentiate the precipitates. How-
ever, as some of the d precipitates were found to
present a similar morphology as the g phase, semi-
quantitative EDS measurements of the precipitate
chemistries were performed and the results were
compiled in Table II.
The results were averaged over three to ﬁve pre-
cipitates on each precipitate phase for each alloy.
One primary difference in composition between the
d and g phase precipitates is that a relatively high
fraction of Nb was present in the d phase: /C2416 at.%
on average compared with /C248 at.% in the g phase.
Cr was found to have preferential solubility in the g
phase with an average concentration of /C247a t . %
compared with /C244 at.% in the d phase. Additionally,
Ta partitioned preferentially to the d phase with an
average concentration of /C244 at.%, whereas Ti pre-
ferred to partition to the g phase. These composi-
tional differences between the d and g phase
precipitates were responsible for the atomic number
contrast differences that resulted in the d phase
being brighter than the g phase when observed
using backscattered electrons. Morphological dif-
ferences were also observed as the d phase precipi-
tates appeared as either small blocky particles or
elongated rods depending on the composition,
whereas the g phase precipitates were exclusively
present in the form of elongated thin rods.
For the 1Ti, 2Ti, and 3Ti alloys, the corresponding
microstructures were largely composed of g phase
precipitates with the size and area fraction of the
precipitates increasing with Ti content (Fig. 1).
Conversely, the alloys with additions of Ta only
(1Ta, 2Ta, and 3Ta) exhibited microstructures con-
taining exclusively d phase precipitates with both
blocky and needle morphologies and no
g phase.
From the micrographs in Fig. 1, increasing the Ta
content in these alloys resulted in a higher density
of blocky d precipitates. Finally, the microstructures
of two of the alloys with both Ta and Ti additions,
1Ta-1Ti and 2Ta-1Ti, were observed to form d phase
precipitates only, whereas the alloy 2Ti-1Ta con-
tained both d and g phase precipitates.

DISCUSSION
Based on the characterization of these resulting
microstructures and their precipitate phase at
equilibrium, compositional relationships were
drawn to predict the precipitate phase stability of
c-c¢-d-g Ni-base superalloys. In addition to the
experimental alloys, commercial c-c¢, and c-c¢-(d/g)
alloys listed in Table III were considered for vali-
dation of these compositional relationships.
At relatively low alloying levels, Nb, Ti and Ta are
all considered to be c¢ forming elements in Ni-base
superalloys. These alloying additions are soluble in
the c¢ phase and substitute for Al in the Ni
3Al
lattice. When an excess amount of Nb, Ti and Ta are
added to the Ni-base alloy system, phases other
than c and c¢ may form. By considering the overall
ratio of Al to Ti+Ta+Nb, where the concentrations
are in at.%, the likelihood of the alloy formingd and/
or g phase precipitates can be assessed. For the
alloys listed in Table III, when Al/(Ti+Ta+Nb) was
less than 1, the microstructure exhibited a tendency
to form d and/or g phase precipitates. This was
observed for all of the alloys investigated and it
applies for commercial alloys Inconel 718 and Allvac
718Plus. Moreover, this ratio was validated against
and is consistent with the microstructural stability
of another set of experimental alloys (RCH53,
RCH49, RCH48, and LN8) in which the elevated
levels of Nb+Ta+Ti resulted in the formation of both
d and g phases
16 as well as experimental alloys
containing d phase precipitates only (V204A,
V204B, V204G, and V204H). 13,14 Conversely, in
alloys where the Al/(Ti+Ta+Nb) is greater than 1,
the c-c¢ microstructure is stable and no d or g phase
precipitates are present. A comparison of the com-
positions of several commercial c-c¢ alloys in
Table IV, such as RR1000 (containing Ta but no
Nb), Rene´95 (containing Nb but no Ta), Rene ´104
(containing both Nb and Ta), and Inconel 100 (that
does not contain either Nb or Ta), supports this
compositional relationship.
For the alloys in which d and/or g precipitate
phases form, a separate compositional relationship
was deﬁned to predict whether d or g would be the
preferred phase. Again, based on the bulk alloy
composition, the ratio of the atomic percent of the d
formers (Nb and Ta) over the concentrations in
atomic percent of the g formers (Al and Ti) was
considered. From Table IV, when the ratio of
(Nb+Ta)/(Al+Ti) was greater than 1, precipitation of
the d phase precipitates was preferred over the g
phase, whereas a ratio less than 1 resulted in a
microstructure predominantly composed of g phase
precipitates. When the ratio approached /C241,

CONCLUSION
Similarities and differences between the d and g
phases were observed in multicomponent Ni-base
alloy systems. Based on the results and observa-
tions presented in this study, the following conclu-
sions can be reported:
(1) The morphologies of the g phase precipitates
were observed to occur as needles or elongated
rods while the d phase precipitates existed with
both blocky and needle-like morphologies.
(2) Within the range of alloy compositions investi-
gated, Ta additions were found to stabilize the d
phase while Ti additions tended to stabilize the
g phase.
(3) The d phase possess a chemical composition
deﬁned by Ni
3(Nb,Ta) with a orthorhombic D0 a
crystal structure. The proportion of Nb and Ta
in the precipitate is deﬁned by the overall alloy
composition. The concentration of Nb was mea-
sured to be modestly higher in the blocky or
plate-like d precipitates when compared with
those with needle-like morphologies. The com-
position of the g phase can be expressed as
Ni
3(Al,Ti)0.5Nb0.5 with a hexagonal D0 24 crystal
structure, where the Nb content is proportional
to the Al+Ti content.
(4) A compositional ratio of Al/(Nb+Ta+Ti) less than
1 resulted in the formation of d and/or g phase
precipitates. Conversely, if greater than 1, no d
and/or g phase precipitates were formed and the
alloy exhibited a conventional c-c¢ microstructure.
(5) For the alloys containing d or g precipitates, a
second compositional ratio of (Nb+Ta)/(Al+Ti)
predicted the prevalent phase. The g phase will
predominate if the ratio is <1 whereas a ratio
>1 resulted in the prevalence of the d phase. A
ratio /C241 resulted in an equal mixture of the two
phases.
