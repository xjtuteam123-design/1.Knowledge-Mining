Grain boundary hardening is an important mechanism for improving the strength and ductility of metal 
materials. However, the industrial fabrication of ﬁne-grained FeCrAl alloys was limited by the interaction 
between the recrystallization and precipitation. Here, we report the facile mass production of ﬁne-grained 
FeCrAl alloys by Si alloying and manipulation of the recrystallization process through introducing hetero- 
geneous Si-rich Laves precipitates. The pre-precipitation of heterogeneous Laves phase not only promotes 
subsequent recrystallization grain nucleation by the PSN (Particles simultaneous nucleation) and SIBM 
(Strain-induced grain boundary migration) mechanisms, but also provides resistance to grain growth by 
the Zener pinning mechanism. Moreover, continuous grain reﬁnement can be achieved by intensifying 
the heterogeneous Laves precipitates through decreasing their formation energy. This approach enables 
the preparation of a fully recrystallized ﬁne-grain structure with a grain size of 4.6 μm without the in- 
troduction of segregated boundaries. Consequently, an unprecedented synergy enhancement of strength 
( σy = 625 MPa, σuts = 867 MPa,) and ductility ( εu = 13.8%) is achieved in the ﬁne-grain structured 
FeCrAl alloys compared with the coarse grain counterpart. The experimental results prove that the pro- 
posed strategy is appropriate for developing high strength and ductility FeCrAl alloys, and further boost- 
ing its potential applications as accident-tolerant-fuel cladding in nuclear reactors. In addition, this grain- 
reﬁnement strategy should be extendable to other alloy systems, where there is a signiﬁcant difference 
between precipitation and recrystallization temperatures.

1. Introduction 
Developing steel structure materials e.g. advanced FeCrAl alloys 
used as heat-resistant and nuclear materials with high strength 
and suﬃcient ductility have always been desirable for expanding 
their application [ 1–6 ]. Unfortunately, these properties are gener- 
ally mutually exclusive, which would induce the strength-ductility 
trade-off dilemma in materials. Among the defects in polycrys- 
talline metals, grain boundaries (GBs) and phase boundaries (PBs), 
working as a type of planar discontinuities in alloys, are remark- 
ably effective in restraining the sacriﬁce of ductility with enhanc- 
ing strength [ 6–12 ]. Thus, GB engineering, e.g., modulating the 
quantity or arrangement of GBs/PBs, was widely used for the 
strength and ductility design of polycrystalline alloys [ 13 ].

Currently, a popular strategy to produce high strength and duc-

the ultraﬁne recrystallized grain [ 12 ]. Under this condition, a large 
number of dislocations can be expected to accumulate in the grain 
interior due to the existence of dense GBs and PBs, resulting in 
enhanced strength, work hardening ability and ductility [ 14 , 15 ]. 
However, there often exists a great difference between the precip- 
itation kinetics and recrystallization kinetics in FeCrAl alloys due 
to the unideal chemical composition and precipitate type, which 
make the nano precipitates and ﬁne recrystallized grains cannot 
be obtained simultaneously, and even be mutually exclusive [ 16–
18 ]. Speciﬁcally, Mo and Nb elements usually were added to form 
high-temperature resistant Fe2 (Nb, Mo) type Laves precipitates in 
FeCrAl alloys [ 19 ], and nano-Laves precipitates would form ﬁrstly 
in the deformed matrix during annealing, which will produce great 
Zener pinning force on GBs, and thus retard the recrystallization 
[ 20 ]. Because there often exists a strong texture in the deformed 
alloys that would induce mechanical anisotropy for the alloys. The

One of the general strategies employed until now in the mi- 
crostructure design of FeCrAl alloys is to make a compromise be- 
tween the precipitation and recrystallization by adjusting the an- 
nealing temperature and time after hot/cold rolling [ 24 , 25 ]. How- 
ever, not only ﬁne Laves precipitates but also fully recrystallized 
grains cannot be obtained under this condition. The lower re- 
crystallization kinetic and higher recrystallization temperature are 
still the fundamental reasons for the diﬃculty in obtaining small 
equiaxed grains, and the problems need to be solved through novel 
structure design or chemical composition optimization. Based on 
this thought, Liu et al. [ 20 ] introduced coarse precipitate parti- 
cles in the alloy matrix before recrystallization annealing, which 
resulted in ∼32.7 μm grain size in the FeCrAl alloys due to the 
particle-stimulated nucleation (PSN) mechanism. As we all know 
that recrystallization grain size of alloys is not only related to grain 
nucleation, but also to grain growth, the grain growth rate will be 
fast due to the drastic loss of Zener pinning force induced by the 
ﬁne precipitates. Thus, the ∼32.7 μm grain size in the coarse pre- 
cipitate decorated FeCrAl alloy deserved to be further reﬁned. 
It is desirable to develop a microstructure design that recrys- 
tallization is affected by both the acceleration effect caused by the 
coarse precipitate particles and by the retarding kinetics due to the 
ﬁne precipitate particles [ 26 , 27 ]. Such heterogeneous Laves precip- 
itates can be obtained by processing route design [ 28 ]. However, 
the size and content of the coarse precipitate and ﬁne precipi- 
tate need to be investigated. In addition, to avoid the compromise 
dilemma between promoting grain nucleation controlled by coarse 
precipitate and inhibiting grain growth controlled by ﬁne precipi- 
tate, the total content of the heterogeneous Laves precipitates also 
needed to be increased. 
In this work, the bimodal distributed Laves precipitate particles 
in the alloy matrix were successfully constructed by a two-step hot 
rolling technology, the kinetics process of subsequent recrystalliza- 
tion thus was well controlled and ﬁne recrystallized grains were 
obtained. Moreover, with the addition of Si element (Laves precip- 
itation promoter) [ 29 , 30 ], the precipitation behavior of Laves pre- 
cipitates was promoted, which not only leads to continuous recrys- 
tallization grain reﬁnement but also protects the ﬁne Laves precip- 
itates due to the continuously decreased recrystallization tempera- 
ture [ 16 ]. With the strategy, the synergic increase of strength and 
ductility was achieved in the Si-alloyed FeCrAl alloys. This work 
may enlighten a novel microstructure design strategy for alloys 
that cannot achieve synergic control of recrystallization and pre- 
cipitation. 
2. Experimental procedures 
2.1. Alloys preparation and microstructure design 
In this study, we used the Fe-13%Cr-4.5%Al (weight percent, 
wt.%) alloy as the base alloy due to its higher oxidation resis- 
tance, to form Fe2 Nb-type Laves strengthening phase, 2 wt.% Mo 
and 1 wt.% Nb were added into the FeCrAl base alloy [ 31 ]. In or- 
der to break through the strengthening limits of Mo and Nb ele- 
ments, Si element was also selected and added to the base alloy to 
promote further Laves precipitation [ 31 , 32 ]. Therefore, four FeCrAl 
alloys with a nominal composition of Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-1Nb-2Mo- x Si 
( x = 0, 0.5, 1, and 2 wt.%) were synthesized by arc-melting pure Fe, 
Cr, Al, Nb, Mo and Si in an argon atmosphere using a vacuum elec- 
tric arc furnace. And each ingot was repeatedly melted at least six 
times to ensure chemical homogeneity. For convenience, the four 
different alloys are hereafter referred to as Si0, Si 0.5, Si1, and Si2, 
respectively. 
The novel processing route was designed, as shown in Fig. 1 (a), 
the 15 mm thick samples were ﬁrstly subjected to a normal hot- 
rolling (15 mm to 10 mm) at a temperature of 1100 °C followed 
by water quenching (WQ). And then they were subjected to a 
low-temperature hot-rolling (HR) (10 mm to 5 mm) at a temper- 
ature of 800 °C followed by WQ. Such double hot-rolling is de- 
signed to obtain the heterogeneous Laves precipitates, which can 
effectively promote the formation of ﬁne recrystallized grains dur- 
ing subsequent recrystallization annealing. Then, the samples were 
warm rolled (WR) at 300 °C (5 mm to 1.5 mm) to produce more 
distortion energy in the alloy matrix and enhance the recrystal- 
lization driving force. Finally, the samples with different Si con- 
tents were recrystallization annealed at different tem peratures for 
0.5 h. The recrystallization temperatures of Si0, Si0.5, Si1 and Si2 
samples were determined by metallographic analysis, which are 
90 0, 80 0,770 and 750 °C, respectively. Beneﬁcial from the inten- 
siﬁed heterogeneous Laves precipitates, the recrystallization tem- 
peratures of the samples gradually decrease with the increase of Si 
content. 
The conventional thermomechanical processing of the same Si- 
alloyed FeCrAl samples ( Fig. 1 (b)) was ﬁrstly subjected to 1300 °C 
high-temperature HR (15 mm to 5 mm) followed by WQ, and the 
Laves phase cannot precipitate at such high temperature. And then, 
WR with 70% thickness reduction was conducted at 300 °C. Fi- 
nally, the recrystallization temperatures of the Si0, Si0.5, Si1 and 
Si2 samples processed by the conventional method also were in- 
vestigated and found to gradually increase from 1100 °C to 1130, 
1170 and 1250 °C, which is obviously higher than that of samples 
processed by the new route. This also shows the effectiveness of 
the new process route in reducing the recrystallization tempera- 
ture of Si-alloyed samples. For convenience, the samples that expe- 
rience two steps HR and contain heterogeneous Laves precipitates 
were designated as Si x @HL ( x = 0, 0.5, 1 and 2) ( Fig. 1 (a)), while 
that processed by conventional method and do not have any pre- 
precipitated Laves phase before recrystallization annealing were la- 
beled as Si x @NL ( x = 0, 0.5, 1 and 2) ( Fig. 1 (b)). 
2.2. Microstructure characterization 
X-ray diffraction (XRD) experiments were conducted by 
a Bruker D8 Discover powder X-ray diffractometer with Cu 
Kα( λ= 1.5406 ˚A) radiation to determine the crystallographic phase 
of the Si alloyed FeCrAl alloys. Laves precipitates and fracture mor- 
phology of tensile specimen characterizations were carried out on 
a ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscope (SEM, Zeiss Gem- 
iniSEM 500) equipped with an energy dispersive spectrometer 
(EDS). Grain orientation and recrystallization of the samples were 
analyzed by electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD). The scan- 
ning step was 0.2 μm. For the EBSD scan, the samples were elec- 
tropolished at −10 °C in a solution containing 10 vol.% perchlo- 
ric acid and 90 vol.% absolute alcohol. Transmission electron mi- 
croscopy (TEM, JEM-F200) observations were conducted on a foil 
sample at 200 kV to characterize the Laves precipitates. The TEM 
samples were prepared by twin-jet electropolishing using the so- 
lution with electropolishing, conducted at a temperature of −35 °C 
with a voltage of 22 V. The sizes of grain and precipitates and 
volume fractions of precipitates were measured using the Image 
J software.

3.2.2. Reﬁned recrystallized grains 
Fig. 6 shows the EBSD inverse pole ﬁgure (IPF) of the recrys- 
tallization annealed Si x @HL and Si x @NL ( x = 0, 0.5, 1 and 2) sam- 
ples. Notably, accompanied by the introduction of heterogeneous 
Laves precipitates, the recrystallized grain sizes of the Si x @HL sam- 
ples were effectively reﬁned compared with the Si x @NL samples. 
The average grain size of Si0@HL samples ( ∼10.7 μm) was reduced 
to one-tenth of the grain size of Si0@NL samples ( ∼97.5 μm), as 
shown in Fig. 6 (a) and (e). With the increase of Si content, i.e., 
the increase of heterogeneous Laves precipitates, the grain size of 
Si2@HL sample ( ∼4.6 μm) even was reduced to one-twentieth of 
its without pre-precipitation counterparts ( ∼83.5 μm), as shown in 
Fig. 6 (d) and (h). In another hand, the monotonically decreasing 
grain size (from 10.7 μm to 4.6 μm) of the Si x @HL samples with 
the increase of Si content also indicates the beneﬁcial effects that 
intensiﬁed heterogeneous Laves precipitates on grain reﬁnement. 
This is beneﬁcial for improving the mechanical properties of FeCrAl 
alloys, and also for promoting the application of thin wall cladding 
tubes ( ∼400 μm) made of FeCrAl alloys. 
3.3. Tensile properties 
The change of alloy microstructure usually leads to different 
mechanical properties. The engineering stress–strain curves of the 
Si x @NL and Si x @HL samples at room temperature are shown in 
Fig. 7 (a) and (b). Both types of alloys exhibit increased strength 
(including the yield strength σ0.2 and the ultimate tensile strength 
σUTS ) with increased Si content, the σY and σUTS are about 405 
and 536 MPa for the Si0@NL, and increase to 565 and 684 MPa 
for the Si2@NL ( Fig. 7 (a)). Similarly, σY and σUTS increase from 
about 411 and 555 MPa in the Si0@HL to 625 and 867 MPa in the 
Si2@HL ( Fig. 7 (b)), proving the effectiveness of adding Si in improv- 
ing the mechanical properties of FeCrAl alloy. Due to the additional 
strengthening effect produced by the reﬁned grain size and Laves 
precipitates, the strength of Si x @HL alloys always is higher than 
that of Si x @NL alloys under the same Si content. Unexpectedly, the 
Si content dependence of alloy ductility for the two types of al- 
loys exhibits a completely distinct evolution trend. In the Si x @NL 
alloys, the uniform elongation decreased sharply from 18.7% in 
the Si0@NL to 3.2% in the Si2@NL, exhibiting a severe strength- 
ductility trade-off dilemma ( Fig. 7 (a) and (c)). The inset image 
in Fig. 7 (a) shows that Si2@NL alloy experiences brittle cleavage 
fracture during tensile deformation. However, in the Si x @HL al- 
loys, the uniform elongation decreases slightly from 17.2% in the 
Si0@HL to 13.8% in the Si2@HL accompanied with a strength in- 
crease ( Fig. 7 (a) and (c)). The ductile fracture surface in the in- 
set image of Fig. 7 (b) proves that the failure mechanism of Si2 
alloy has changed. By comparison, the microstructure reﬁnement 
in the Si x @HL alloys clearly provides a desirable strength-ductility 
balance. A mechanical performance comparation of our alloys with 
the reported FeCrAl alloys was conducted and plotted in σUTS vs. 
uniform elongation [ 34 , 35 ], as shown in Fig. 7 (d). Apparently, the 
result proves the advantage of grain reﬁnement in simultaneously 
enhancing the strength and ductility of FeCrAl alloys. 
4. Discussion 
4.1. Inﬂuence of Si alloying on the precipitation behavior of Laves 
phase 
Reﬁned grain structure and improved mechanical properties of 
the FeCrAl alloys in this work primarily steaming from the inten- 
siﬁed heterogeneous Laves precipitates. The precipitation behavior 
of Laves phase in the Si-alloyed FeCrAl alloys is strongly inﬂuenced 
by the composition of Laves phase itself according to the experi- 
mental results. Meanwhile, we can also notice that the (Nb + Mo) 
concentration in the Fe2 (Nb,Mo) phase exhibits a gradual devia- 
tion from the stoichiometric ratio with the increase of Si concen- 
tration ( Table 2 ). For example, in the Si0 alloy, the concentration of 
(Nb + Mo) elements is 27.3 at.%, slightly deviate from the stoichio- 
metric ratio 33 at.%. However, in the Si2 alloy, the concentration 
(Nb + Mo) element was further reduced to 24.9 at.%, and the gap 
between 33 at.% was enlarged. That may be the reason that numer- 
ous SF defects were formed in the Laves precipitates. Because the 
alloy undergoes HR and WR in sequence during processing, the SFs 
in the Laves precipitates (forming during the HR stage) may orig- 
inate from plastic deformation. To solve the confusion, the Laves 
precipitates nucleation process was characterized, as shown in Fig. 
S2. Numerous SFs still can be observed in the nano-Laves precip- 
itates, showing the Laves precipitates try to capture some defects 
to stabilize the Nb and Mo-poor non-stoichiometric phase. How- 
ever, the relationship between SFs forming and Laves precipitation 
behavior still is unclear.

4.2. Grain reﬁnement mechanisms 
During recrystallization annealing, the recrystallized grain size 
was mainly controlled by two processes: nucleation and grain 
growth [ 37 ]. Thus, for effectively reﬁning the recrystallized grain 
size, it is crucial to increase nucleation density and suppress sub- 
sequent grain growth. In this work, the introduction of heteroge- 
neous Laves successfully achieves the synergic control of nucle- 
ation and grain growth, and that will be discussed in detail as fol- 
lows. 
4.2.1. Particle stimulated nucleation (PSN) mechanism 
To conﬁrm the impact of coarse Laves precipitates on grain nu- 
cleation during recrystallization annealing, we investigate the mi- 
crostructure evolution of the Si2@HL alloy at the early stage of re- 
crystallization annealing. Fig. 9 exhibited the SEM and EBSD im- 
ages of the alloy after annealing at 600 °C for 10 min. Firstly, ac- 
cording to the high kernel average misorientation (KAM) values 
at the interface between coarse Laves particles and alloy matrix 
( Fig. 9 (a) and (b)), we can know where high-density dislocations 
have been accumulated. During the rolling process of the alloys, 
dislocations cannot cross the coarse particles through climbing or 
cross slipping, and then were pinned by the coarse Laves particles. 
Because dislocations have high strain energy as a type of lattice 
defect, which can provide a great driving force for recrystalliza- 
tion nucleation. The behavior of coarse particles working as addi- 
tional nucleation sites is referred to as particle stimulated nucle- 
ation (PSN) [ 38 ]. Fig. 9 (c) shows the IPF map of the same zone as 
Fig. 9 (b), where the recrystallized grain nuclei, exhibiting different 
colors with the green alloy matrix, can be observed. Selecting the 
blue recrystallized grain as the representer, the misorientation be- 
tween the recrystallized grains and alloy matrix was quantitatively 
characterized, the misorientation change along the yellow arrow in 
Fig. 9 (c) was shown in Fig. 9 (d), it can be seen that the misorienta- 
tion between recrystallized grains and alloy matrix was more than 
20 °, larger than the 15 ° of high angle grain boundaries [ 39 ]. Thus, 
it can be proved that extensive recrystallization has happened at 
the interface of coarse Laves particles due to the PSN mechanism. 
4.2.2. Strain-induced grain boundary migration (SIBM) mechanism 
Decreased hot rolling temperature in the present new process- 
ing route not only can produce heterogeneous Laves precipitate, 
but also increase the dislocations density of alloy matrix, then in- 
creasing strain energy of alloy and promoting grain nucleation. For 
investigating the recrystallization process of the alloy matrix, the 
microstructure evolution of the alloy matrix in the early stage of 
recrystallization annealing was investigated, as shown in Fig. 10 . 
Fig. 10 (a) exhibits the EBSD images of the samples after isothermal 
annealing at 500 °C for 30 min. As we can see the alloy remains a 
banded structure composed of elongated grain, decorated by in- 
ternal shear bands, aligned at ∼30 °-35 ° to the rolling direction 
and marked by white arrows in Fig. 10 (a1). Speciﬁcally, the shear 
bands exhibit higher KAM values, i.e., high internal strain, due 
to the strong localized shearing during rolling [ 40 ], as shown in

4.2.3. Grain boundaries pinning during grain growth 
The introduction of coarse Laves precipitates and the decreased 
hot rolling temperature both increase the dislocation density and 
strain energy of the alloy matrix, which effectively promotes the 
recrystallized grains nucleation. Meanwhile, the decreased recrys- 
tallization temperature and therefore maintained ﬁne Laves precip- 
itates play a positive effect on inhibiting subsequent recrystalliza- 
tion grain growth. The strong drag effect of ﬁne Laves precipitates 
on GBs of the recrystallized grains can be seen in this work, as 
shown in Fig. S3. Thus, the heterogeneous Laves precipitates in this 
work can be considered as playing a dual effect on grain reﬁne- 
ment. Of course, the grain nucleation behavior and grain growth 
behavior must be carefully designed so as to achieve a better grain 
reﬁnement effect.

than any of the HR temperatures, the ﬁne Laves precipitates will 
not coarsen during recrystallization annealing. Both ﬁne recrystal-

in the Si2@HL alloy, completely different from the Si2@NL alloy

4.3. Strengthening mechanisms 
As elaborated earlier, simultaneous enhancement of strength 
and ductility was achieved in the Si x @HL samples. To understand 
the underlying reasons, taking the Si2 alloys for example, we in- 
vestigate the strengthening mechanisms of the Si2@HL and Si2@NL 
alloys, separately. The strength enhancement of poly-crystalline 
materials usually can be ascribed to four strengthening mecha- 
nisms [ 42 ]: solid solution strengthening ( σss ), dislocation strength- 
ening ( σd ), grain boundary strengthening ( σgb ) and precipitation 
strengthening ( σp ). Then, the yield strength of alloys can be cal- 
culated by summing the strength contributions from each of these 
strengthening strategies, as expressed below: 
σy = σ0 + σss + σgb + σd + σp (2) 
where σ0 is the intrinsic lattice friction stress (45 MPa) [ 16 ]. The 
solid strengthening of different solid solution elements can be ex- 
pressed as [ 43–45 ]: 
σss = 75Al + 80Nb + 11Mo + 83Si −31Cr (3) 
where the strengthening coeﬃcient of different elements is 
matched with the mass fraction. By adding the element concen- 
tration of Si2@HL and Si2@NL alloy matrix (see Table S1 in Sup- 
plementary Information) into Eq. (3) , the solid solution strength- 
ening of Si2@HL and Si2@NL alloys can be calculated as 170 and 
205 MPa, respectively. 
The contribution of grain boundaries strengthening can be cal- 
culated by the well-known Hall-Petch relationship: 
σgb = k · d−1 / 2 (4) 
where k is the Hall-Petch coeﬃcient, and d is the average grain 
size. For the Si2@HL alloy, k is ∼500 MPa μm1/2 [ 46 ]. However, 
for the Si2@NL alloy, numerous grain boundaries Laves precipi- 
tates can act as strong barriers to slip propagation across the grain 
boundaries and raise k , thus k in Si2@NL alloy was determined 
to be ∼930 MPa μm1/2 [ 47 , 48 ]. Taking the grain size of Si2@HL 
and Si2@NL alloys into Eq. (4) , the strength contribution of grain 
boundaries can be obtained as 233 and 101 MPa, respectively. 
Dislocation strengthening is strongly relative to the dislocation 
density ρ, which can be calculated according to the Taylor harden- 
ing law [ 49 ]:

4.4. Deformation behavior 
To understand the difference in tensile ductility between the 
Si x @HL and Si x @NL alloys, we investigate the microstructure of the 
tensile deformed samples using EBSD characterization (see Fig. 13 ). 
As we can see that no deformation mechanisms such as twins can 
be found in the ferrite matrix, leaving dislocation activity as the 
sole source to provide strain hardening for the alloys ( Fig. 13 (a) 
and (c)). The KAM can be considered as a dislocation density indi- 
cator [ 54 ]. In the coarse grain structured Si2@NL alloy, the distribu- 
tion of KAM values is not uniform, higher KAM values are mainly 
located in the grain boundaries zone ( Fig. 13 (b)), indicating that 
grain boundary Laves phase actually can suppress the dislocation 
annihilation or slip cross grain boundaries. The stress concentra- 
tion caused by the dislocation pile-up can be seen as the main 
reason for the brittle fracture of Si2@NL alloy. However, for the 
Si2@HL alloy, accompanied by the elimination of grain boundary 
Laves precipitates and grain size reﬁnement, dislocations distribu- 
tion becomes more homogeneous (see Fig. 13 (d)), which provides 
continuous work hardening for the alloy during tensile deforma- 
tion. 
5. Conclusions 
(1) In this work, a novel thermomechanical processing route was 
proposed to develop high strength and high ductility FeCrAl al- 
loys by forming heterogeneous Laves precipitates decorated ﬁne 
grain structure. Finally, 625 MPa yield strength, 867 MPa tensile 
strength and 13.8% uniform elongation are obtained in Si2@HL 
sample. 
(2) Si element addition promotes the decrease of nucleation en- 
ergy of the Laves precipitates, it contributes to the formation 
of intensiﬁed heterogeneous Laves precipitates. Among that, 
coarse Laves particles stimulated recrystallization through the 
PSN mechanism, while ﬁne Laves particles retard the recrys- 
tallized grain growth and thus promote the formation of ﬁne 
grains structure. 
(3) The results demonstrated that grain boundary strengthening 
is mainly responsible for the yield strength improvement of 
present FeCrAl alloys. The elimination of grain boundaries Laves 
precipitates and uniform strain distribution due to grain re- 
ﬁnement promotes work hardening ability enhancement of the 
present FeCrAl alloys.
