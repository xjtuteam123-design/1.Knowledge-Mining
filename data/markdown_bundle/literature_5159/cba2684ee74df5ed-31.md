The effect of tantalum (Ta) doping on mechanical properties of
tungsten (W): A ﬁrst-principles study
Diyou Jiang, Qingling Wang, Wei Hu, and Ziqing Wei
Department of Physics, Jiangxi University of Technology, Nanchang 330098, China

The effect of Ta concentration on the fundamental mechanical properties of W –Ta alloys has been
studied from ﬁrst principles study. The lattice constants, the cell volumes, and the formation
energies of the W 1/C0 xTax (x 5 0.0625, 0.125, 0.1875, 0.25, 0.3125, 0.5, 0.5625, 0.625, 0.75)
alloys were calculated. It is shown that Ta alloying in bcc W lattice is an in ﬁnite solid solution
and the W 0.5Ta0.5 have the lowest formation energy. With the optimized geometry and lattice, the
elastic constants are calculated and then the elastic moduli and other mechanical parameters are
derived. Results show that although the mechanical strength of the W –Ta alloys is lower than that
of pure W metal, it is much higher than that of pure Ta metal. On the other hand, the B/G ratio
and the Poisson ’s ratio of the W –Ta alloys is much higher than that of pure W, and even higher
than that of pure Ta, indicating that Ta alloying can improve the ductility of bcc W substantially.
I. INTRODUCTION
In the International Thermonuclear Experimental
Reactor (ITER), pure W is considered to be one of the
most promising candidates for plasma facing materials
(PFMs) and divertor plates in fusion power reactors, due
to its high melting point (3695 K), high strength at high
temperatures, high sputtering threshold energy, good ther-
mal conductivity, and low thermal expansion coefﬁcient.
1–3
In addition, tungsten is also used as a shielding material in
fusion reactors and other systems involving nuclear
reactions.4 Unfortunately, pure tungsten metal exhibits
poor radiation stability and fracture toughness, and low
ductility associated with a high ductile-to-brittle transi-
tion temperature (DBTT). These properties are strongly
dependent on the chemical composition and the micro-
structural state.
5 Alloying is a good method to improve
the physical properties of tungsten. For example, the
thermodynamic properties of tungsten are expected to be
enhanced by alloying with other elements.
6–8
Among various alloying strategies of W metal, W –Ta
alloy is a typical one. To our knowledge, both experi-
mental and theoretical aspects focus on understanding the
mechanical properties of W –Ta alloy. For example, Vani
Shankar et al. studied the in ﬂuence of varying W and Ta
on low cycle fatigue and creep-fatigue interaction
behavior of reduced activation ferritic/martensitic steels.
9
Zhang et al. studied the effect of thickness ratio
on toughening mechanisms of Ta/W multilayers. 10
Bercegeay et al. studied second-nearest-neighbor modi-
ﬁed embedded-atom potential for binary Ta –W alloys
based on ﬁrst-principles calculations. 11 Turchi et al.
studied stability and local order in substitutional Ta –W
alloys by ﬁrst-principles calculations.12 So far, only a few
work has been reported on the effect of Ta-alloying and
the Ta concentration on the fundamental mechanical
properties such as elastic properties and ductile/brittle
behaviors. We know that the mechanical properties are
very important for the ﬁrst wall materials. The density-
functional theory (DFT) calculation can be used to
predict the mechanical performance of materials with
relatively low cost and high ef ﬁciency, comparing with
the experimental evaluations. Since conventional exper-
imental alloy design processes can be very expensive and
time-consuming, DFT calculations can be used to study
the structure –property relationships and help to design
these materials. Computer simulation focus on improving
speciﬁc alloy properties that can narrow down the
number of compositions that are needed to be prepared
and characterized.

mechanical properties of W

using ﬁrst principles calculations. The lattice constants,
the cell volume, the formation energies and the elastic
constants of the W

13,14 The core ion and valence electron

and Ta-6 s25d3, respectively. The exchange-correlation
part was described with the generalized gradient approx-
imation (GGA) by Perdew and Wang (PW91).
16 All

According to Pugh theory, the bulk modulus to shear

ductility of metal materials. The larger the B/G ratio is,
the better ductility the material exhibits. 28 Fig. 9 presents
the B/G ratio and the Poisson ’s ratio ( m) as functions
of the Ta concentration x. With increasing Ta concen-
trations, the B/G value increases gradually and reaches
a value of 3.66 at x 5 62.5%. This value is already higher
than that of pure Ta metal (3.41), indicating that the
ductility of the W 0.375Ta0.625 alloy is better than that of
pure Ta metal. Poisson ’s ratio can also characterize the
ductility of bulk materials. As it is shown in Fig. 9, the
variation of the Poisson ’s ratio to the Ta concentration
exhibits similar trend to that of the B/G ratio. When the
Ta concentration x reaches 0.625, the Poisson ’s ratio is
0.375 and larger than that of pure Ta (0.367).
Qualitatively, the metallic bonding (ductile metals) and
covalent bonding (directionally bonded solids) behaviors
of the W –Ta alloy can be characterized by the calculated
Cauchy pressure,
29 which is shown in Fig. 10. When the
Cauchy pressure ( C9) has a positive value, metallic

ductility properties.
29,30 On the contrary, as for brittle
materials, the Cauchy pressure is negative. The smaller
the negative value is, the stronger covalent bonding and
the more serious degree of brittleness of materials.
29,30

binary W
1/C0 xTax alloys are positive, because metallic
