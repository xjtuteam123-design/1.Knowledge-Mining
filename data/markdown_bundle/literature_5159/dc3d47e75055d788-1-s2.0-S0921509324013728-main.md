Light-weight refractory high-entropy alloys with low density and good ductility are widely used in various en -
gineering fields. Despite their potential, achieving an optimal strength-ductility balance in these lightweight

1. Introduction
The term “ high-entropy alloys ” (HEAs) was proposed by Yeh et al. 
[ 1 ]. HEAs are considered promising candidates for a wide range of po -
tential applications [ 2 ]. HEAs may surpass traditional alloys or bridge 
the gap between existing alloys due to their unique mechanical prop -
erties. Several recent studies showed that refractory HEAs (RHEAs) 
based on refractory elements exhibit extraordinary thermal stability and 
resistance to high-temperature softening [ 3 – 7 ]. MoNbTaW and MoN -
bTaVW alloys were shown to retain their high yield strengths of 405 and 
477 MPa, respectively, at 1873 K [ 4 ]. Therefore, RHEAs are widely used 
in high-temperature applications owing to their exceptional mechanical 
properties at elevated temperatures [ 8 ].
The combination of different properties, such as the specific strength, 
and the synergistic effect of strength and toughness of an alloy, deter -
mine its potential uses. A high strength at high-temperatures alone does 
not meet most application requirements. Furthermore, the density needs 
to be considered in the design of HEAs [ 8 ]. Therefore, lightweight HEAs 
(LHEAs) have been developed [ 9 ]. For example, the densities of 
Mg x MnAlZnCu and AlLiMgSiCa LHEAs are 2.2 – 4.29 and 1.4 – 1.7 g/cm
3
, 
respectively [ 10 , 11 ]. Unfortunately, these low-density LHEAs do not 
exhibit excellent mechanical properties at room temperature (RT). For 
instance, the AlNbTiV HEA with a density of 5.59 g/cm
3 
had a 
compressive fracture strain of 5.2 % [ 12 ]. In contrast, the TaNbHfZrTi 
HEA with a high density of 9.94 g/cm
3 
was deformed to 50 % 
compressive strain without any evidence of fracture [ 13 ]. Therefore, the 
specific yield strength (SYS) plays an important role in determining the 
practical application potential of HEAs.
The AlNbTiV HEA has a low density and high SYS, but its ductility is 
limited [ 14 ]. In contrast, the NbTiVZr HEA exhibits good ductility, but 
its SYS is quite low [ 15 ]. The lightweight RHEA AlNbTiVZr exhibited 
excellent mechanical properties at high-temperatures. Several studies 
showed that adding Zr to AlNbTiV HEA produces a lattice distortion 
effect, which significantly improves its properties [ 16 ]. The

unfolds and the applied external force escalates incrementally, the dis -
locations accumulate sufficient momentum to bypass the second-phase 
particles. This dynamic interaction within the Zr0.3 alloy not only bol -
sters its compressive strength by impeding dislocation slip but also en -
hances its ductility.
