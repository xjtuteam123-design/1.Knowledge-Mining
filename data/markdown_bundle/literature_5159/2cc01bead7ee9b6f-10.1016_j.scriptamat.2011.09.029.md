A novel route to prepare an ultrahigh-strength commercial Mg–3Al–1Zn (AZ31) alloy was established by optimizing the pro-
cessing parameters of high-ratio diﬀerential speed rolling (HRDSR). After HRDSR at 423 K followed by immediate water quench-
ing, a grain size of 0.6 lm was obtained. The ultraﬁne-grained AZ31 exhibited a very high yield stress of 382 MPa. The eﬀects of the
HRDSR conditions on the microstructures and mechanical properties of this alloy were examined and discussed.

Grain reﬁnement is known to be an eﬀective
method of improving the strength of Mg alloys. The
equal channel angular pressing (ECAP) method has
been extensively studied as a technique for reducing
the grain sizes of Mg alloys [1–5]. Highly reﬁned micro-
structures with grain sizes of 1–3 lm can be obtained
after multiple pressings using ECAP, but the rotation
of basal planes upon shear deformation at the ECAP
die corner often decreases in strength to below that of
the starting material [2,3]. This decrease in strength is
due to the texture-softening eﬀect that overwhelms the
grain-size strengthening eﬀect in Mg alloys with a lim-
ited number of slip systems. The texture-softening eﬀect,
however, was shown to be compensated for or overcome
by signiﬁcant grain reﬁnement to the submicron size.
Yoshida et al. [4] reported that the grain size of the
AZ61 alloy could be reduced to 0.5 lm by using ECAP
processing at 448 K, which is noticeably lower than con-
ventional ECAP temperatures ( P473 K). The ECAP-
processed AZ61 alloy exhibited a high yield stress (YS)
of 340 MPa. Unlike AZ61, AZ31 has a low fraction of
second phase ( b-Mg
17Al12), so the reﬁnement of grain
size to the submicrometer level is relatively diﬃcult.
Ding et al. [5], however, demonstrated that, by adopt-
ing the A route (no rotation of the sample between
passes, unlike other routes, such as B
c and C) in ECAP
with 12 passes processed at multiple processing temper-
atures between 473 and 388 K, an extremely small grain
size of 0.37 lm could be obtained in AZ31. The pro-
cessed AZ31 alloy exhibited a YS of 372 MPa. There
is also a report of the attainment of an extremely ﬁne-
grained microstructure in AZ31 plates with a grain size
of 0.1–0.3 lm using friction stir processing with a rapid
heat sink (liquid nitrogen) [6]. These results suggest that
a combination of a high degree of severe plastic defor-
mation at low temperatures, a rapid heat release of
deformation and friction heats, and suppression of tex-
ture softening can signiﬁcantly increase the strength of
AZ31.
Kim et al. [7,8] demonstrated that high-ratio diﬀeren-
tial speed rolling (HRDSR), where a large shear strain is
induced during asymmetric rolling at high roll speed ra-
tios of P2, is quite eﬀective for grain reﬁnement. In
HRDSR, the roll speed ratio, the roll speed and the
working temperature strongly aﬀect the amount and dis-
tribution of eﬀective (shear) strain, the deformation rate

the ﬁnal grain size and the deformation texture. In the
present study, we report the achievement of an excep-
tionally strong AZ31 with a YS of about 400 MPa by
optimizing the HRDSR rolling parameters.
The material used in this study was extruded AZ31
(3 wt.% Al, 0.8 wt.% Zn, 0.2 wt.% Mn) plates, 2 mm in
thickness, 180 mm in width and 200 mm in length. Roll-
ing was conducted using a rolling mill with roll diame-
ters of 300 mm. The speed ratio between the upper
and lower rolls was set at 2. The top roll rotated in
the counterclockwise direction at a speed of 6 rpm, while
the bottom roll rotated in the clockwise direction at a
speed of 3 rpm. The roll surfaces were maintained at
423 or 473 K throughout the rolling process using heat-
ing elements embedded within the rolls. The rolling was
conducted for a total thickness reduction of 66% under
non-lubricated conditions. The cold plate was initially
rolled from 2 mm to 1.65 mm in thickness (17.5% in
thickness reduction) at a roll temperature of 473 K, then
cooled in air. This material is referred to hereafter as the
FR. The FR was rolled again to a ﬁnal thickness of
0.68 mm at the same roll temperature or at the lower
temperature of 423 K and then cooled in air, producing
two materials, SC473 and SC423, respectively. The sec-
ond pass was performed without rotation of the sample
and the rolling direction of the second pass was kept
same as that in the ﬁrst pass. Immediately after the sec-
ond rolling pass, some of the samples rolled at 473 or
423 K were placed into cold water for rapid quenching;
these two materials obtained after water quenching
(WQ) are referred to as SC473WQ and SC423WQ,
respectively. A piece was taken from SC423 and then an-
nealed at 523 K for 3–5 min to examine the annealing ef-
fect on the microstructure and tensile properties.
For tensile testing, dog bone-shaped specimens with a
gauge length of 32 mm and gauge width of 6 mm were
cut from the rolled materials along the planes coinciding
with the rolling direction (RD) and extrusion direction.

processed materials ( Fig. 1 (c) and (d)) exhibited homo-

Figure 2 (c)–(e) shows the representative transmission
electron microscopy (TEM) micrographs of SC473WQ
and SC423WQ. Most of the grain boundaries in
SC473WQ were sharp ( Fig. 2 (c)). Dislocations were ob-
served in the interiors of many grains, but the density
was low. In SC423WQ ( Fig. 2 (d) and (e)), some grain
boundaries were sharp while others were not, indicating
a mix of high- and low-angle boundaries. There were
also many fringes ( Fig. 2 (d)) and network structures
(Fig. 2 (d) and (e)) within the grains, which correspond
to a high density of dislocations arranged into cell and
subgrain boundaries or dislocation networks in the
grains [9]. Few twins were spotted in either material.
The grain sizes of SC473WQ and SC423WQ determined
based on their low-magniﬁcation TEM micrographs
were 0.92 and 0.42 lm, respectively. The grain size of
SC423WQ measured by TEM is notably smaller than
that measured by SEM, suggesting the presence of smal-
ler subgrains with low-angle boundaries inside some of
the large grains with high-angle grain boundaries
(clearly etched boundaries) revealed by SEM. Figure
2(c) and (e) shows many very small spherical or rod-
shaped particles homogeneously dispersed over the
matrices of SC473WQ and SC423WQ. Energy-disper-
sive spectroscopy elemental mapping indicated that
most of the particles in the two materials were Al–Mn
phase. The Al/Mn atomic ratios were in range between
1.6 and 1.8, implying that the particles were most likely
to be Al
8Mn5. As well as the Al–Mn particles,
b-Mg17Al12 particles were also occasionally encoun-
tered. They had a spherical morphology, with sizes of
100–150 nm, and were located mainly on the grain
boundaries ( Fig. 2 (f)). b particles with similar size and
shape were also found in SC423 and SC473. There were
no noticeable diﬀerences in the amount and distribution
of b particles between the samples with and without
WQ.
The evolution of the macrotexture was visible with
the aid of the (0002) X-ray pole ﬁgures shown in Figure
3. The as-received material showed a weak basal texture,
with some basal poles spreading toward the TD. After
the ﬁrst rolling pass, the basal pole spreading toward
the TD decreased, and the maximum basal texture
intensity increased drastically. The maximum basal tex-
ture intensity was 18–27 at s =9t o s = /C0 9. During the
second rolling pass, the basal texture intensity increased
further. The textures of SC473 (30–35) and SC423 (26–
33), which are not shown here, were similar to those of
SC473WQ (28–33) and SC423WQ (26–32), respectively.
The decrease in basal pole spreading toward the TD and
its increase toward the RD, which occurred during the
ﬁrst rolling, might have been a result of shear strain to-
ward the RD imposed by HRDSR. However, rotation
of the basal poles from the normal direction toward
the RD and the subsequent basal texture weakening,
which have been reported in Mg alloys after diﬀerential
speed rolling with low roll speed ratios (<1.2) [10], did
not occur under the current experimental conditions.
A low processing temperature may also strengthen the
basal texture and suppress the tilting of the basal poles
by limiting non-basal slip systems, compression and
double twinning. In the current study, the cold samples
were deformed using rolls heated to 423 or 473 K,
whereas the samples preheated at 573 K were deformed
using rolls heated to the same temperature used in Ref.
[10].
Figure 4 (a) shows the stress–strain curves of the as-
received and rolled samples. A signiﬁcant improvement
in strength occurred after the second rolling. The mate-
rial rolled at the lower roll temperature exhibited a high-
er strength. The YS of SC423 was 353 MPa, which was
58 MPa higher than that of SC473. The application of
WQ after the second rolling increased the strength fur-
ther by preserving the ﬁner microstructure. The
SC423WQ exhibited a YS of 382 MPa and an ultimate
tensile strength of 401 MPa. This strength is comparable
to that (372 MPa) of UFG AZ31 fabricated using 12
passes in ECAP with a complicated combination of tem-
peratures and pressing rates [5], and that (379 MPa) of
UFG AZ31 fabricated by the consolidation of ball-
milled nanocrystalline powders [11]. The tensile ductil-
ity, however, decreased considerably after the second
rolling. This decrease was due to a signiﬁcant reduction
in strain-hardening ability.
Short-time annealing was found to be eﬀective at
improving the ductility. After annealing SC423 for
3 min, the total elongation increased from 6.4 to
13.6%. The uniform elongation also doubled (from 5
to 10%). However, the strength loss during the anneal-
ing was very small, suggesting that the improvement in
ductility was a result of the relief of the residual stress
developed during rolling. After annealing for 4 min,
the YS decreased to 298 MPa, but the uniform and total
elongations increased further, to 15 and 21%, respec-
tively. The strain-hardening exponent of SC423 that
was annealed for 4 min (0.12) was identical to that of
the starting material. This result suggests the possibility
of achieving a good balance between strength and duc-
tility through proper annealing of ultraﬁne-grained
AZ31 fabricated by HRDSR. The (0 0 0 2) X-ray pole

In summary, we demonstrated that an ultrahigh-
strength AZ31 alloy could be fabricated by employing
HRDSR followed immediately by WQ. The processed
alloy with a grain size of 0.6 lm exhibited an exception-
ally high YS of 382 MPa. This strength is the highest
ever achieved in AZ31 in sheet form. It is believed that
this technique could be readily applied to other Mg alloy
systems.
