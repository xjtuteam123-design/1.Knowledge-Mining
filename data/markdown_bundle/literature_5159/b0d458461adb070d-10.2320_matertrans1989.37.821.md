Introduction Nickel aluminides are considered to become one of the most promising intermetallics for high temperature struc- tural applications such as turbine blades, because of their higher melting points and higher oxidation resistance than ordinary superalloys and other intermetallics which do not contain Al(l). In particular, Ni3Al(y'; LI 2-ordered) and NiAl(?; B2-ordered) are considered to be near-future candidates for practical use. Ni3Al, which has positive temperature dependence of yield strength, is known as the strengthening phase of commercial Ni-base superalloys. The recent however, have revealed that the creep strength of monolithic Ni3Al is lower than that of the conventional superalloys with y'/y dual phase structure. This is considered that the y'/y interface contributes to increase in creep resistance as a barrier against dislocation motion across the interface. In fact, IC218 one of the commercial Ni3Al alloys developed, also has the y'/y dual phase to achieve higher creep strength than the previous alloys. Although these Ni3Al alloys are considered to have superior properties such as specific strength to weight and oxidation resistance to the conventional superalloys, they show only limited hot workability. NiAl is considered to have superior properties to Ni3Al for advanced high temperature applications. It has higher melting point, lower density, better thermal conductivity and superior oxidation resistance. However, poor creep resistance is considered to be one of the major drawbacks in MAI It is known that multiphase structure is often effective on improving high temperature strength with enough ductility or toughness. Therefore, in the present work, high tempera- ture deformation in ?+Y'+disordered Y triple phase alloys has been studied in order to clarify their potentiality for high temperature structural applications.
