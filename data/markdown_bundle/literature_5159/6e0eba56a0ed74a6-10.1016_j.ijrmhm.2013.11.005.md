Multi-element alloy “Al0.5CoCrCuFeNi” is a brand new novel metal material. It has high working hardness, high
high-temperature strength, and anti-oxidation properties. Multi-element alloy powders can be prepared by
blending various pure metal powders using a mechanical alloying method. Different ratios of WC and
multi-element alloys can be sequentially mixed and alloyed using a mechanical alloying method. WC particles
were dispersed homogeneously in ductile multi-element alloy without forming any second phase. After press
forming, the admixed powders were debound at 500 °C and then liquid-phase sintered at 1300 –1450 °C. The
sintering process of WC/Al
0.5CoCrCuFeNi multi-element alloy is similar to the traditional WC/Co. The hardness
of the sintering body of WC/Al 0.5CoCrCuFeNi alloy is Hv 200 –300 higher than the traditional WC/Co sintering
body measured at room temperature. Its high-temperature hardness is Hv 200 higher than the traditional
WC/Co measured at 900 °C. Substitution of multi-element alloy for Co as the base phase can enormously improve
the material properties of WC super-hard composite material.

1. Introduction
Since 1920, super-hard composite materials have been developed.
Because of their high hardness, high wear-resistance, good fracture
resistance and high temperature strength, they are attractive and
successful composite materials that have been widely used in industry.
One of the most super-hard composite materials is a carbide composite
material that can be divided into two classes where the primary phase is
either tungsten carbide (WC) or titanium carbide (TiC). Super-hard
composite material is composed of two different components. One
component is a ceramic phase that has a high melting point, high
hardness and brittleness. The ceramic particle involves tungsten carbide
and titanium carbide. The other component is binder metals that have
low hardness and high toughness. During the last several decades, the
binder metals of WC grains bound with a Co phase have been used.
Powder metallurgy is used for manufacturing this composite material.
During the sintering process, a binder metal is melted to liquid phase
together with carbide to form eutectic liquid. The particles of carbide
are surrounded liquid phase through capillarity phenomenon and result
in a relatively high density. Therefore, the function of carbide is to
provide high hardness and high wear-resistance, while the binder
metal provides good fracture toughness. Because the cobalt used in
the process has magnetic properties and is a valuable resource, many
recent studies have focused on ﬁnding a new metal to substitute for
cobalt [1–4].
Multi-element alloy is a newly developed alloy comprising many
elements of metal. Due to the high entropy effect of multi-element
alloy, a simple crystal microstructure is formed. Multi-element alloy
has some special properties including high hardness, temper softening
resistance, anti-corrosiveness, anti-oxidation and precipitation harden-
ing at high temperatures [5–8].
In the previous studies of multi-element alloy [9–14] ,
Al
0.5CoCrCuFeNi, unitary face center cubic (FCC) structure was formed
and nanocrystalline precipitate phase was found in the matrix during
the casting state. There are six attractive properties of multi-element
alloy: (1) it has good elongation properties at room temperature;
(2) it can be deformed by a nano-twin mechanism and appears as
nano-grains during deformation; (3) it has a high working hardness
from room temperature to 900 °C and does not soften after high
temperature annealing; (4) it has a high temperature strength up to
900 °C; (5) good wear resistance; and (6) good anti-oxidation proper-
ties at high temperature. Due to the superiority of multi-element alloy
over Co in terms of the aforementioned properties, it ﬁts the working
requirements of high temperature dies and tools. Therefore, in this
study, we used multi-element alloy to replace Co for the manufacturing
of a super-hard WC/multi-element alloy composite material.

2.1. Starting materials
High purity ( N 99.9%) starting materials (WC, Al, Cr, Co, Cu, Fe, Ni)
were used for the preparation of alloys. The particle size of WC powder
was about 2.5 μm. And the particle sizes of other pure metals were less
than 325 mesh.
2.2. Powder preparation of multi-element alloy
An Al 0.5CrCoCuFeNi multi-element alloy system was used in this
work. Multi-element alloy powder mixtures together with stainless
steel balls (diameter of 1 mm) were sealed in a stainless steel vial inside
a glove box in a nitrogen atmosphere. The ball to powder weight ratio
was controlled at 2:1. A mechanical alloying process was carried out
on a high energy ball mill (Retsch PM4) with a selected rotation speed
of 250 rpm. After ball milling, multi-element alloy powder with a
unitary FCC structure was formed.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. The sintering body of WC/Al0.5CrCoCuFeNi multi-element alloy
Six pure metal powders (Al, Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Ni) were mixed and
ground to form multi-element alloy using a high energy ball milling
process. Fig. 2 shows the phase structural change of multi-element
alloy with increases in the ball milling time. According to the XRD
analysis, many pure metal powders became alloy powder when the
ball milling time exceeded 24 h. After the ball milling process, multi-
element alloy of Al 0.5CrCoCuFeNi will become a unitary phase with
FCC structure. The surface morphology of multi-element alloy after
ball milling for 48 h is shown in Fig. 3(a). It can be seen that the particle
sizes of multi-element alloy of Al 0.5CrCoCuFeNi are about 50 –100 μm.
20 wt.% of multi-element alloy powder mixtures and 80 wt.% WC
were mixed to form uniform powder mixtures using a ball milling
method. The X-ray diffraction patterns of the powder mixtures of
multi-element alloy and WC after ball milling for 48 h are shown in
Fig. 4. The sample contains both FCC structure of multi-element alloy
and HCP structure of WC. Powder mixtures of multi-element alloy and
WC were not oxidized and indeed became a metal compound during
ball milling. The SEM image of powder mixtures of multi-element
alloy and WC after ball milling for 48 h is shown in Fig. 3(b). There are
two ranges of particle size distributions. The average sizes of large par-
ticles and small particles were about 0.8–1.2 μma n da b o u t0 . 1–0.3 μm
respectively. The particle size of the starting material of WC is about
2.5 μm and the size of multi-element alloys is about 50 –100 μm. How-
ever, the powder mixtures of WC/multi-element alloy are below
1.2 μm after the ball milling process. This means that the particle can

two distributive ranges: a larger grain size of about 0.66μm and a small-

continuous and discontinuous grain growth during the sintering pro-
cess. In addition, the grain growth of WC was relatively slow. Traditional
WC/Co super-hard composite must add carbide [15] that includes TaC,
VC, Cr3C2 and NbC to inhibit grain growth. In this study, we found that
WC grain growth can be considerably inhibited by the multi-element
alloy.
The grain growth of WC must pass through the separation steps of
dissolution and proliferation, so that the grain growth can be primarily
affected by the chemical composition environment surrounding the WC
grains during the sintering process. Different chemical compositions
created different interfacial energy between WC and the matrix resulting
in a different energy barrier of diffusion for W and C in the matrix. There-
fore, the multi-element alloy has a better inhibitive ability for WC grain
growth than the Co. Accordingly, the hardness of WC/Co composite can
be reduced by the WC grain growth. When the composite is comprised
of the same binder metal, the hardness of WC/multi-element composite
is higher than WC/Co composite. This phenomenon can be attributed to
three factors: (1) the hardness of multi-element alloy is higher than Co;
(2) the multi-element alloy can inhibit the grain growth of WC; and
(3) the distance between WC grains increases with the increase of the
content ratio of multi-element alloy.

3.2. Fracture toughness
The WC/multi-element alloy composite exhibits not only hardness
at high temperatures, but also good fracture toughness compared to
traditional WC/Co composite. The hardness and fracture toughness of
commercial WC/Co composites and WC/multi-element alloy compos-
ites are shown inTable 3. The hardness of commercial WC/Co composite
and WC/multi-element alloy composite is almost the same, but the frac-
ture toughness of WC/20 wt.% multi-element alloy composite is 30%
higher than the fracture toughness of WC/Co composite. Our results
demonstrate that the WC/multi-element alloy composite has higher
fracture toughness than commercial WC/Co composites.
Prakash et al. [17,18] reported that ultraﬁne-grained (particle size -
0.4 μm) hardmetals with pure Co binder exhibit high bending strength
(fracture toughness) values, and the hardmetals with complex binder
system (Fe, Co, Ni) showed lower bending strength (fracture tough-
ness) values. However, our WC/multi-element alloy composite has
high bending strength values (K 1C: 17.4) compared to commercial
WC/Co composites (K1C:1 3 . 0 ) .
4. Conclusions
The present investigation of WC/multi-element alloy has revealed
several new and important aspects:
(1) WC grains of WC/Al 0.5CrCoCuFeNi composite exhibit continuous
and discontinuous grain growth that were relatively slow during
the sintering process. So, WC grain growth can be considerable
inhibited by the multi-element alloy.
(2) WC particles were dispersed homogeneously in ductile multi-
element alloy without formation of second phase.
(3) The hardness of WC/20 wt.% multi-element alloy composite is
Hv 300 higher than WC/Co composite at 700 °C. This ef ﬁciency
attributed positive temperature effects to the multi-element
alloy.
(4) The WC/multi-element alloy composite has higher fracture
toughness than commercial WC/Co composites with the same
hardness.
