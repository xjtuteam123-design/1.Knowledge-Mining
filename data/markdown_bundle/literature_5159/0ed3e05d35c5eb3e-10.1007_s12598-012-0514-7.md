High temperature tensile properties of directionally solidified 
Ni-43Ti-4Al-2Nb-2Hf alloy 
 
JIANG Dongwen, ZHENG Lijing, ZHOU Lei, PAN Liwen, TANG Xiaoxia, and ZHANG Hu 
School of Materials Science and Engineering, Beijing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Beijing 100191, China 
Received 31 October 2011; received in revised form 22 February 2012; accepted 21 March 2012

Abstract 
By liquid metal cooling (LMC) process, the Ni-43Ti-4Al-2Nb-2Hf (%, atomic fraction) alloy was directionally solidified (DS). The micro-
structure and tensile properties at room and elevated temperature were investigated. It was found that the DS process significantly improves 
the room temperature tensile strength, increasing by 70% compared with the as-cast alloy. After appropriate heat treatment (HT), the average 
tensile strength reaches above 1900 MPa, nearly twice of the as-cast one. At 800 and 900 °C, the tensile strengths are about 308 and 169 MPa, 
respectively. 
Keywords: NiTi-Al based alloy; directional solidification; heat treatment; tensile prosperity 
 
 
1 Introduction 
NiTi-based alloys are well known for their excellent 
shape memory effect, which has been successfully applied 
to the medical field as functional materials [1−4]. Different 
from most brittle intermetallic alloys, the NiTi alloy is of 
great interest as a new class of high-temperature structural 
materials, for its outstanding room temperature plasticity [5]. 
In order to overcome low strength at high temperature, 
various routes were tried, such as microalloying through 
adding Al, Nb, Mo, and Hf elements [6 −10] and adjusting 
the Ni/Ti atomic ratio[11]. The addition of Al to NiTi alloys 
was verified to remarkably improve the compressive 
strength to 2300 MPa [12−13]. Moreover, the addition of Nb 
improved the high-temperature oxidation resistance [14−15]. 
Based on these results mentioned above, a series of NiTi-Al 
based alloys including Ni-43Ti-4Al-2Nb-2Hf alloys were 
investigated. 
However, because of containing a lot of highly active 
elements for NiTi-Al based alloys, the casting defects and 
impurities elements are unavoidable by traditional casting 
method, which seriously affect ed the alloy performance. 
Zhang et al. prepared NiTi-Al based alloy by using Bridg-
man technique with LMC and self-made Y
2O3/Al2O3 dou-
ble-layer ceramic tube [16 −20], successfully reducing the 
content of casting defects. Thus, this paper further explores 
the tensile mechanical properties of the DS NiTi-Al based 
alloy at room temperature and 800~900 °C. Besides, the ef-
fect of HT process on the DS microstructure and the me-
chanical properties were also investigated. 
2 Experimental 
High purity Ti (99.76%), Ni (99.95%), Nb (99.98%), Al 
(99.99%), and Hf (99.90%) were used as the raw materials 
to prepare the ingots of alloy with nominal compositions of 
Ni-43Ti-4Al-2Nb-2Hf (at.%). Because Al element, as the 
solid solution atom, mainly occupies Ti atomic positions in 
the crystal lattice of matrix NiTi alloy and Ti 2Ni precipita-
tion, without forming the new phase containing Al, this al-
loy will be referred to as the NiTi-Al based alloy. The mas-
ter alloy was melted in a vacuum electromagnetic levitation 
melting furnace on a water-cooled copper crucible under 
high purity argon atmosphere and casted in a graphite mould. 
Before the melting, the furnace chamber was evacuated 
down to 8×10
–3 Pa and backfilled with high purity argon up 
to 0.05 MPa. The ingot weighing 5 kg was melted and 
turned four times to promote homogeneity. Before DS ex-
periments, the ingot was cut to give master alloy samples 
with a size of 14 mm in diameter using electrical discharge 
machining (EDM). Then, these samples were ground, 
cleaned, and dried. The DS process was performed using a 
laboratory–scale Bridgman furnace (LMC, liquid metal 
cooling technology) equipped with an Y

3 Results and discussion 
3.1 Microstructures analysis 
The back scattered electron (BSE) image of the micro-
structure of as-cast sample is shown in Fig. 1 (a). It can be 
observed that the microstructure consists of the dark gray 
phase (arrow 1) and the light gray phase (arrow 2), the white 
strip line phase distributing above the light gray phase (ar-
row 3), and a small amount of black phase (arrow 4). Based 
on the EDS results, the dark and light gray region all repre-
sented the near equiatomic NiTi matrix phase, in which 
small amounts of Al, Nb, and Hf were dissolved, and the 
difference was the degree of segregation. The light gray re-
gion had more Nb and Hf content but less Ti content than 
dark gray region. The black region represented the Ti
2Ni 
phase, and the white region was the Nb-rich phase.

as-cast samples had almost the same phase composition, but 
the microstructure morphologies were obviously different. It 
can be seen from the longitudinal section microstructure, the 
NiTi matrix grew as dendrite along the preferential orienta-
tion, the white Nb-rich, and black Ti

3.2 Tensile properties 
Tensile strength-displacement curves of HT samples at 
different temperatures are shown in Fig. 3, and tensile prop-
erties of as-cast, DS, and HT samples are shown in Table 1. 
It indicates that the tensile ultimate and yield strength of 
as-cast sample at room temperature can reach 1000 and 960 
MPa, respectively. Compared with the as-cast samples, the 
tensile strength of DS ones at room temperature increased 
from 1000 to 1739 MPa, increased by nearly 74%, and 
the yield strength increased from 960 to 1526 MPa, in-
creased by nearly 60%. Obviously, DS process had greatly 
improved the mechanical properties of the alloy. The tensile 
strength of HT samples at room temperature further in-
creased to 1913 MPa, which was twice as many as the 
as-cast sample. Furthermore, the tensile strength of HT 
samples at 800 °C is above 300 MPa, and the tensile 
strength at 850 °C is about 233 MPa, while above 160 MPa 
at 900 °C. By the increase of temp erature, the plasticity of 
the alloy is obviously improved. In the stretching process at 
elevated temperature, the alloy experienced elastic deforma-
tion, yield softening, work hardening, and local necking. 
Figure 4 shows the fracture surface morphologies of HT 
samples at 800, 850, and 900 °C, which present that there 
are many dimples in the fracture surface. With the increase 
of temperature, the dimples became bigger and deeper and 
began to polymerize between each other and formed larger 
holes. The surface morphology presented the ductile fracture 
mode at 800~900 °C.

4 Conclusion 
DS process has greatly improved the tensile properties of 
Ni-43Ti-4Al-2Nb-2Hf alloy at room temperature, and the 
tensile strength has increased by 74% comparing with the 
as-cast alloy. After appropriate heat treatment, the average 
tensile strength reaches above 1900 MPa, nearly twice of 
the as-cast one. The tensile strengths of Ni-43Ti-4Al-
