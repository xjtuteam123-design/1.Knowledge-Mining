A novel high-Cr CoNi-based superalloy with superior high-temperature micro-
structural stability, oxidation resistance and mechanical properties
Xiaorui Zhang, Min Zou, Song Lu, Longfei Li ✉, Xiaoli Zhuang, and Qiang Feng ✉
Beijing Advanced Innovation Center for Materials Genome Engineering, State Key Laboratory for Advanced Metals, University of Science and Technology
Beijing, Beijing 100083, China
(Received: 5 November 2023; revised: 26 January 2024; accepted: 29 January 2024)
Abstract: A novel multicomponent high-Cr CoNi-based superalloy with superior comprehensive performance was prepared, and the
evaluation of its high-temperature microstructural stability, oxidation resistance, and mechanical properties was conducted mainly using
its cast polycrystalline alloy. The results disclosed that the morphology of the γ′ phase remained stable, and the coarsening rate was slow
during the long-term aging at 900–1000°C. The activation energy for γ′ precipitate coarsening of alloy 9CoNi-Cr was (402 ± 51) kJ/mol,
which is higher compared with those of CMSX-4 and some other Ni-based and Co-based superalloys. Importantly, there was no indica-
tion of the formation of topologically close-packed phases during this process. All these factors demonstrated the superior microstructural
stability of the alloy. The mass gain of alloy 9CoNi-Cr was 0.6 mg/cm2 after oxidation at 1000°C for 100 h, and the oxidation resistance
was comparable to advanced Ni-based superalloys CMSX-4, which can be attributed to the formation of a continuous Al2
O3 protective
layer. Moreover, the compressive yield strength of this cast polycrystalline alloy at high temperatures is clearly higher than that of the
conventional Ni-based cast superalloy and the compressive minimum creep rate at 950°C is comparable to that of the conventional Ni-
based cast superalloy, demonstrating the alloy’s good mechanical properties at high temperature. This is partially because high Cr is bene-
ficial in improving the γ and γ′ phase strengths of alloy 9CoNi-Cr.
Keywords: CoNi-based superalloys; microstructure; coarsening; oxidation; mechanical properties
 
 
1. Introduction
γ′-strengthened Co-
based superalloys have attracted signi-
ficant attention since Sato et al. re-discovered the L12-
ordered γ′-Co3(Al,W) phase in the Co–Al–W alloy system in
2006 [1]. Currently, with a good understanding of the effects
of different elements [2–6], γ′-strengthened Co-based super-
alloys have gradually evolved from the initial Co–Al–W
ternary alloys to the multicomponent CoNi-based superal-
loys to balance the comprehensive properties and find engin-
eering application [7–15].
However, most of the current research has been aimed at
developing new alloys with high γ
′ solvus temperature
(Tγ′-solvus) and high creep resistance, which may be more suit-
able in aeroengine turbines [12–13]. Compared with aeroen-
gine turbines, industrial gas turbines have lower service tem-
peratures but longer steady-state service time; thus, the ma-
terials should have better long-
term microstructural stability
and basically stable mechanical properties. The diffusion
coefficients of alloying elements in Co-based alloys are
lower than those in Ni-based alloys [16]; therefore, Co-based
alloys are superior in terms of diffusion-related microstruc-
tural stability and creep properties and more suitable for in-
dustrial gas turbines.
Because of the hot corrosion environment, the materials
used in industrial gas turbines commonly have a high Cr con-
tent [17–18]. However, developing multicomponent CoNi-
based superalloys with high Cr content remains an immense
task because Cr is a strong deleterious topological close-
packed (TCP) phase former [7,19]. Ng et al. [7] have 
re-
cently described the influence of Cr addition on multicom-
ponent CoNi-based alloy with 12at% Cr, displaying an ex-
tensive secondary phase within the grains on aging. Another
problem is that Cr addition decreases the γ′ solvus temperat-
ure of Co-based superalloys, which is essential for the high-
temperature capability of superalloys. Moreover, adding Cr
to Co-based alloys is one approach to improving the oxida-
tion resistance by promoting the formation of Cr2
O3 and
Al2O3 protective oxide layer [20]. Our recent work has shown
that a higher Cr content is beneficial to the yield strength of
CoNi-based superalloys [21]. So far, most reports on high-Cr
CoNi-based superalloys are focused on polycrystalline
wrought superalloys for turbine discs, which mainly aim at
relatively lower service temperatures (<850°C) [9
–11]. The
development of high-Cr CoNi-based superalloys with superi-
or high-temperature (>900°C) microstructural stability, oxid-
ation resistance, and mechanical properties remains a great
challenge.

superalloy has been developed by our group by “diffusion-

2. Experimental
The alloy is denoted as 9CoNi-Cr because it contains nine
elements and has a high Cr content. Table 1 summarizes the
nominal and measured compositions by inductively coupled
plasma-optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES) of alloy
9CoNi-Cr. Moreover, 0.15at% of C and B are added to this
alloy to strengthen grain boundaries. The cast polycrystalline
alloy was prepared from the appropriate amounts of element-
al metals by arc melting in Ar. The mass of Al was increased
by a factor of 1.05 and added in the ﬁnal melting stage to
compensate for the loss of Al due to the evaporation during
the melting process. The alloy was inverted and melted more
than eight times to attain a homogeneous composition. Ho-
mogenization heat treatment was conducted at 1250°C for 12
h, followed by air cooling to reduce the segregation of as-cast
alloy. Tγ′-solvus was determined using a Setsys Evolution 2400
differential scanning calorimeter with a heating rate of
10°C/min. The density of the developed alloy was calculated
by the Archimedes principle. The γ/γ′ lattice misfit at 1000°C
was determined using a Bruker D8 ADVANCE X-ray dif-
fractometer (XRD) on samples aged at 1000°C for 100 h.
Due to the presence of tetragonal lattice distortion in high γ′
volume fraction alloys, a three-peak fitting method was se-
lected to split the peaks. Among these physical properties, the
Tγ′-solvus is 1147°C, the density is 8.77 g/cm3, and the lattice
misfit is 0.31%.
The alloy was aged at 900–1000°C for 24–1200 h to ex-
amine its high-temperature long-term microstructural stabil-
ity. γ/γ′ microstructures were characterized using a ZEISS
SUPRA 55 field-emission scanning electron microscope
(SEM) in secondary electron mode. The samples were chem-
ically etched in a solution containing 33vol% HNO3 +
33vol% CH3COOH + 33vol% H2O + 1vol% HF. The volume
fraction (Vγ′) and γ′ equivalent radius (r) were calculated by
the standard point count method and ImagePro software, re-
spectively. The partitioning behavior of γ/γ′ elements was
studied on an FEI Talos transmission electron microscope
(TEM) equipped with super energy dispersive spectroscope
(EDS) and high-angle annular dark-field (HAADF) detect-
ors. Samples for TEM analysis were mechanically ground to
~50 μm thickness, followed by twin-jet electrochemical pol-
ishing in an electrolyte solution containing 8vol% perchloric
acid, 4vol% acetic acid, and 88vol% ethanol at −30°C.

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructure and coarsening behavior of γ′ pre-
cipitates
Alloy 9CoNi-Cr has superior microstructural stability,
which is beneficial for its stability of mechanical properties.
Fig. 1 presents the typical microstructures of alloy 9CoNi-Cr
after homogenization and aging at 900–1000°C for 24–1200
h (obtained from the near [100] oriented grains). The γ′ pre-
cipitates keep a near-cuboidal shape without the precipitation
of TCP phases, and r increases obviously with increasing
temperature. The evolution of the Vγ′ of alloy 9CoNi-Cr is
plotted in Fig. 2(a). Vγ′ increases steadily during the aging up
to 1200 h at 900°C, after which it remains relatively constant
from 600 h. A similar case is also found at 950°C while Vγ′
remains stable at 200 h. At 1000°C, Vγ′ maintains ~58vol%
during the whole aging process (24–1200 h). An equilibrium
state is predicted before 24 h.

3.2. Comparison of γ′ coarsening rates and activation en-
ergy
Fig. 3 depicts the γ′ coarsening rates of alloy 9CoNi-Cr
compared with other typical superalloys at 900–1000°C
[10,19,25–28]. It is worth noting that the K for all alloys was
calculated by r and coarsening exponent value n = 3. At
1000°C, the γ′ coarsening rate of alloy 9CoNi-Cr was much
lower than those of Ni-based superalloy CMSX-2 and low-
component Co–Al–W-based alloy [19,25,27] and compar-
able to novel CoNi-based wrought alloy with high Cr content
[10] while slightly higher than Ni-based superalloy CMSX-4
[26]. The lower γ′ coarsening rate of alloy CMSX-4 may be
ascribed to the addition of refractory element Re, which has a
much lower diffusion coefficient than the other elements in
the γ matrix [29]. However, alloy 9CoNi-Cr has a significant
advantage over Ni-based alloys in terms of the stability of the
γ′ phase, as it keeps its original morphology after 1000°C
aging for 1200 h, whereas the γ′ phase in alloy CMSX-4
would coarsen and interconnect and even form the rafting
structure after 1000°C aging for 1000 h [26,30]. The
coarsening rates of 9CoNi-Cr at 900 and 950°C are lower
than those of CMSX-4 and some other Ni- and Co-based su-

high temperatures.

3.4. Oxidation properties
However, not only superior microstructural stability but
also good oxidation resistance is required for industrial gas
turbine materials. Fig. 6(a) shows the typical morphology of
oxide scales, while Fig. 6(b) presents the XRD pattern of al-
loy 9CoNi-Cr after isothermal oxidation at 1000°C for 50 h.
Combining the element distributions in oxide scales (Fig.
6(c)), it can be determined that the oxide scales of alloy
9CoNi-Cr are composed of the external CoCr2O4 layer, the
intermediate phase of the CoCr2O4 layer, which is TiTaO4,
and the internal continuous Al2O3 layer. The massive blocky
(Co,Ni)3(Ta,Ti) and the needle-like TiN precipitates exist in
the γ′-depleted zone below the continuous Al2O3 layer. The
mass gain per unit area of alloy 9CoNi-Cr is 0.6 mg/cm2 after
oxidation at 1000°C for 100 h (Fig. 7), which is much lower
than those of γ′ -strengthened Co-based superalloys and Ni-
based superalloys MarM247, and comparable to that of ad-
vanced Ni-based superalloys CMSX-4 and others [11,
13,33–37]. 
3.5. Mechanical properties
Alloy 9CoNi-Cr displays good mechanical properties at
high temperatures. Fig. 8(a) compares the compressive yield
strengths of alloy 9CoNi-Cr and other superalloys at
20–950°C. The compressive yield strength of alloy 9CoNi-
Cr using cast polycrystalline materials decreases slowly from
20 to 600°C, followed by the abnormal yield strength peaks
in the 600–800°C range, and finally decreases rapidly in
800–950°C. The overall compressive yield strength of alloy
9CoNi-Cr is higher than those of Co-based superalloys
MarM509 and Co–8.8Al–9.8W–2Ta, and its high-temperat-
ure yield strength (>700°C) is better than those of the con-
ventional Ni-based cast superalloy MarM247 and CoNi-
based wrought alloy [11,38–40]. It is worth noting that the
compressive yield strength of alloy 9CoNi-Cr is significantly
higher than that of the high-Al low-Cr alloy of similar com-
position [22]. Fig. 8(b) compares the minimum creep rates of
alloy 9CoNi-Cr and other typical superalloys using polycrys-
talline materials at 950°C under different stresses [41–42].
The minimum compressive creep rate of alloy 9CoNi-Cr at
950°C is lower than those of Co-based superalloys MarM509
and Co–9Al–9W–2Ti–0.12B as well as conventional Ni-
based cast superalloy IN100 and slightly higher than the min-
imum tensile creep rates of conventional Ni-based cast su-
peralloy MarM247 for similar grain size.

4. Discussion
High-temperature microstructural stability, oxidation res-
istance, and mechanical properties are important for assess-
ing newly developed CoNi-based superalloys. With the aim
of elucidating the reason for the superior comprehensive per-
formance of alloy 9CoNi-Cr, the following is addressed. 
4.1. Microstructural stability
The high temperature service conditions of superalloys
can result in microstructural instabilities such as the morpho-
logical evolution of γ′ precipitates (the morphology, size, and
distribution). During stress-free thermal exposure at high
temperatures, in general, the γ′ morphology evolution can be
associated with the combination of the elastic strain energy
and interfacial energy and has been evidenced by numerical
results conducted for multiple particles [46]. Alloy 9CoNi-Cr
has a significant advantage over Ni-based alloys in terms of γ′
phase stability, which has stable morphology and superior γ′
precipitate coarsening resistance.
The elastic strain ﬁeld induced by the lattice misﬁt
between the γ matrix and the coherent γ′ precipitates drive the
formation of γ′ precipitates with the lowest strain energy after
heat treatment. First, Cr addition can significantly lower the
lattice misfit of the Co-based superalloy. The lattice misﬁt at
1000°C of alloy 9CoNi-Cr is relatively low, which is benefi-
cial to decrease the elastic strain energy of γ/γ
′ phases and the
driving force of the γ′ phase deformation. Moreover, the ad-
dition of Cr can significantly decrease the Vγ′ of the alloy,
which results in less interconnect and rafting phenomena [7].
All of these facilitate the stability of the γ′ phase morphology
of the 9CoNi-Cr.

concentration gradient on the γ/γ′ interface and slow effect-
ive diffusivity in the γ matrix. From Fig. 5, the “rate-limiting
element Cr” in alloy 9CoNi-Cr is strongly partitioned into the
γ phase, and the concentration gradient across the γ/γ′ inter-
face is large, which has the advantage of decreasing the inter-
facial energy of the alloy. A similar aspect was also de-
scribed for the high-Cr Ni-based superalloy René 88 DT [48].
In this case, the “rate-limiting element Cr” is more difficult to
diffuse at the γ/γ' interface, and the activation energy of Cr is
enhanced (compared with its activation energy in the γ mat-
rix), which makes the γ′ coarsening slower [16]. 
4.2. Oxidation resistance and mechanism
Oxidation resistance is essential for the engineering ap-
plication of novel Co-based superalloys. The oxidation res-
istance of alloy 9CoNi-Cr is comparable to that of advanced
Ni-based superalloy CMSX-4, which is due to the formation
of a continuous Al2O3 protective layer [36].
Compared with these superalloys in Fig. 7, alloy 9CoNi-
Cr has higher Cr content, which is beneficial to provide a
lower oxygen partial pressure in the transient oxidation stage
and facilitate the formation of continuous Al2O3 layers earli-
er [17,49]. This should be the primary reason for its good ox-
idation resistance at high temperatures. Moreover, the oxida-
tion resistance of alloy 9CoNi-Cr is significantly better than
that of high-Cr Ni-based superalloys with a Cr2O3 protective
layer [50], which is ascribed to the formation of a continuous
Al2O3 protective layer at 1000°C. Meanwhile, at 1000°C, the
Cr2O3 layer is unstable and will evaporate due to the forma-
tion of gaseous chromium oxides CrO3(g). Also, the appro-
priate contents of Ta and Ti in alloy 9CoNi-Cr improve the
oxidation resistance of the alloy [51]. 
4.3. Mechanical properties
Alloy 9CoNi-Cr displays good mechanical properties at
high temperatures. As the principal strengthening phase in
superalloys, the strength of the γ′ phase significantly follows
the creep properties of alloys, its strength mainly dictated by
the compositional characteristics within the γ' phase. Re-
search on Co–Ni–Al–W-based series alloys 30Ni-A(0Cr),
5Cr-A, and 9Cr-A, as illustrated in Fig. 8(d) [13,43], reveals
that the hardness of the γ' phase would be promoted with in-
creasing Cr content. Due to the strong enrichment of Cr in the
γ phase, Cr addition will result in the increase of Ta and Ti
contents in the γ′ phase, thus increasing the γ′ strength [13].

5. Conclusion
In this work, a novel CoNi-based superalloy with superior
comprehensive performance, alloy 9CoNi-Cr, was de-
veloped. This alloy retained a high volume fraction, stable
morphology, and slow coarsening of γ′ precipitates during the
long-term aging at 900–1000°C. Importantly, no indication
of the formation of TCP phases was found during this pro-
cess. A continuous Al2O3 layer could form during 1000°C
oxidation, and the oxidation resistance is superior to conven-
tional Ni-based cast superalloy MarM247. Moreover, the
yield strength of the polycrystalline alloy at high temperat-
ures is higher than that of superalloy MarM247, and the com-
pressive minimum creep rate at 950°C is similar to conven-
tional Ni-based cast superalloys, demonstrating good mech-
anical properties of the alloy at high temperatures. In this
work, the Cr element in the high-Cr CoNi-based superalloy is
the “rate-limiting element”. This also confirmed that high Cr
content is beneficial to enhancing the oxidation and mechan-
ical properties of the alloy. Thus, this work offers a reference
for the composition design of CoNi-based cast superalloys
for industrial gas turbines.
