ABSTRACT
Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) have emerged as promising candidates for high-temperature 
structural applications due to their high melting points and potential for exceptional strength, whereas their 
creep properties remain relatively underexplored. Here, we investigate the tensile creep behavior of NbTaTi and 
NbTaTiV in high vacuum to elucidate the influence of alloying on creep mechanisms. NbTaTiV exhibits higher 
creep resistance than NbTaTi, which is attributed to V-induced edge-dislocation-glide-controlled deformation. 
Microstructural analysis reveals the formation of Ti- and interstitial impurity-rich secondary phases during creep, 
which enhances the creep resistance without significant embrittlement. Stress exponents (~3) and activation 
energies (~those for vacancy diffusion) align with thermally activated dislocation glide-controlled creep. Despite 
NbTaTiV surpassing Ni-based superalloys in yield strength at elevated temperatures, its single-phase BCC 
structure limits creep resistance under engineering-relevant conditions, highlighting the necessity of deliberate 
secondary phase design to achieve competitive high-temperature performance.
1. Introduction
1.1. Refractory complex concentrated alloys
The pursuit of alloys capable of operating at temperatures exceeding 
those of Ni-based superalloys, aimed at improving turbine engine effi -
ciency, has recently directed research attention toward refractory sys -
tems with inherently higher melting points [ 1 ]. A newly developed and 
important class of refractory alloys designed for this purpose is known as 
refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs), also referred to as re -
fractory multiple principal element alloys (RMPEAs) or refractory 
high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) [ 2 ]. These alloys are characterized by 
containing substantial concentrations ( > 10 at %) of at least three re -
fractory elements, typically drawn from Groups IV to VI of the periodic 
table (e.g., Ti, V, Cr, Zr, Nb, Mo, Hf, Ta, and W). Initial studies on RCCAs 
have identified compositions with promising compressive strength 
across 1073 – 1873 K. For example, the AlMo

1.2. Creep properties of RCCAs
Resistance to creep deformation is a critical criterion for the selection 
of structural materials intended for high-temperature applications. 
Although extensive literature exists on the strain rate – controlled 
compressive and tensile behaviors of RCCAs as reviewed and compiled 
by Miracle et al. [ 18 ], experimental investigations of their tensile creep 
properties remain scarce [ 19 – 21 ]. Specifically, the HfNbTaTiZr system 
is the only RCCAs that has been systematically studied in tensile creep. It 
was initially selected as a model alloy because of its nominally 
single-phase BCC structure stable up to 1523 K and its demonstrated 
tensile ductility, which enables reliable tensile creep testing. Stresses 
were varied from 5 MPa to 100 MPa from temperatures between 1253 K 
and 1523 K, and this revealed a stress exponent ranging from 2.0 to 2.8, 
and an activation energy of 273 ± 15 kJ/mol [ 19 , 20 ]. However, 
HfNbTaTiZr was found to be 25 times weaker than the Ni-based super -

1.3. Creep mechanisms of RCCAs
Currently, it has been observed that tensile creep deformation in the 
above RCCAs is controlled by screw dislocations. In the HfNbTaTiZr 
system, screw dislocations were identified via g-dot-b analysis [ 20 ]. In 
the Nb
45
Ta
25
Ti
15
Hf
15 
system, post-creep microstructures displayed 
long, straight dislocations that showed signs of bowing, pinning, and 
debris, features characteristic of screw-dislocation-controlled deforma -
tion involving cross-kink unpinning and jog-dragging mechanisms 
[ 21 – 24 ]. Recently, Rao et al. [ 25 ] employed a modified Suzuki model 
[ 23 ] to rationalize the measured secondary creep rates based on the 
aforementioned screw dislocation glide mechanisms. Screw-controlled 
deformation leads to high strength in RCCAs at lower temperatures 
[ 26 ], but these improvements to creep properties are largely eliminated 
at high temperatures when diffusion becomes active [ 23 ]. In order to 
design RCCAs with better performance at increasingly elevated tem -
peratures, different deformation mechanisms must be considered. One 
such mechanism may be present in the composition of interest in this 
work, NbTaTiV. As mentioned above, it is observed that edge disloca -
tions control elevated-temperature deformation due to the presence of V 
[ 14 ]. The large lattice distortion caused by V forces dislocations to move 
across high energy barriers, which requires high thermal or stress acti -
vation, especially for edge dislocations that are less 
temperature-sensitive [ 12 , 27 ]. It has been established that the 
edge-strengthening effect of V contributes to high yield strength up to 
1173 K. However, whether this mechanism also enhances creep resis -
tance remains to be investigated in detail.

controlled deformation, as induced by V alloying, can provide 
improved creep resistance compared to conventional screw dislocation- 
controlled mechanisms prevalent in most RCCAs. The insights gained 
from this comparison will provide design principles for developing next- 
generation refractory alloys with enhanced high-temperature strength 
and creep resistance. Other critical factors for structural applications at 
elevated temperatures, such as oxidation resistance, is beyond the scope 
of the current paper.
