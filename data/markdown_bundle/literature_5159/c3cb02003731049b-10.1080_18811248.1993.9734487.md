lnconel alloys have been used extensively as heat exchange tubing materials in the power 
generation industry because of their excellent mechanical properties at elevated temperatures. 
Evaluation of the alloys for High Temperature Gas-Cooled Reactor (HTGR) applications has 
proved that Ni-Cr-Co alloys have good high temperature strength, creep properties and weld­
ability. 
In this work, the cyclic fatigue behavior and creep crack growth rate of lnconel 617 alloy 
were tested for analysis of time-dependent crack propagation characteristics at 650"C. Micro­
structural examination was performed for observation of grain boundary carbide precipitation 
growth and the coalescence of intergranular microcracks that would enhance the crack propa­
gation rate. It was observed that at higher temperatures. low fatigue frequency, ami higher 
R-ratio (mean-stress), the effects of dynamic recovery tended to become stronger because the 
mobility of dislocation increased with increasing temperature. As a result, the subgrain grew 
to a larger size. Eventually, the influence of time-dependent processes on crack growth rate 
can effectively represent the overall effects on a creep ductile material at elevated temperatures.

I. INTRODUCTION 
Inconei alloys have been used extensively 
as heat exchange tubing materials in the 
power generation industry because of their 
excellent mechanical properties at elevated 
temperatures<'>. Evaluation of the alloys for 
High Temperature Gas-Cooled Reactor (HTGR) 
applications has proved that Ni-Cr-Co alloys 
have good high temperature strength, creep 
properties and weldability<2>. 
It is important to understand the crack 
propagation mechanism in high temperature 
environments under the conditions of time 
varying loads. Because both time-dependent 
and time-independent processes can control 
the crack advance, both damage mechanisms 
have to be considered. Depending on the 
fatigue frequency and other parameters, it is 
often found that the time-dependent or time­
independent process dominates, and analysis 
of the dominant process is sufficient to de­
scribe the crack growth rate. Nevertheless, 
the regime of dominance of either of the two 
mechanisms must be established so that the 
correct approach is applied to predicting con­
ditions for crack advance. It is also possible 
that neither of these two damage mechanisms 
dominate, but that they interact. In this case, 
an appropriate analysis is also needed to 
describe crack growth behavior. Several 
parameters influence high temperature fatigue 
crack growth behavior. They include tem­
perature, R-ratio (ratio of minimum to maximum 
stress), wave shape, cycle frequency, environ­
ment and microstructure.

1. Effects of Frequency 
The effects of frequency on fatigue crack 
growth rate can most easily be seen if the 
crack growth rate is plotted as a function of 
frequency at a fixed value of the stress inten­
sity factor. The results for crack growth 
rates at R-ratio values of 0.05 and 0.6 are 
shown in Fig. 5. Some extrapolation is neces­
sary for the R=0.6 case, since the crack 
growth rates are selected at a constant stress 
intensity factor range (JK ~ 19 MPa/m) for both 
cases. At high frequencies, fatigue crack 
growth rate is independent of frequency, that 
is, the crack growth per cycle is constant. 
It can be seen that in the present study, crack 
growth rates at frequencies higher than 0.5 Hz 
are independent of the cycle rate for both 
R-ratio values. This indicates that at high 
frequencies crack growth rate is frequency 
independent even though time-dependent pro­
cesses are important at R=0.6. Crack growth 
rate is found to be constant for frequencies 
in the range of 0.1 Hz and to slight deviation 
from 0.1 liz down to 0.016 Hz for R=0.05 case.

The crack growth characteristics of Inconel 
617 at 650"C is studied to evaluate the appli­
cability of time-dependent parameters to low 
frequency and high R-ratio environments, and 
the following results were obtained. 
(1) High R-ratio and low frequency can 
significantly increase time-dependent pro­
cesses, resulting in an intergranular crack 
growth mode.
