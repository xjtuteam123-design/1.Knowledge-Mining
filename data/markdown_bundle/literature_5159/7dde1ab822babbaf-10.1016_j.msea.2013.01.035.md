abstract
The micromechanical behavior under compression for a cube-oriented single crystal Ni-base superalloy
was in-situ studied by synchrotron high energy X-ray diffraction at room temperature. Both elastic and
plastic deformation altered the mismatch strain between g and g0 phases with different variances. The
deformation-induced interface coherency strain relaxation was observed after yielding as evidenced by
an increment of lattice mismatch and an abnormal decrease in full width at half maximum (FWHM),
which is attributed to aggregating of dislocations toward phase boundaries. A dislocation-mismatch
model is proposed to correlate the relative change in lattice mismatch and FWHM during deformation,
which ﬁts the experiment well.

1. Introduction
Single crystal Ni-base superalloys are widely used as high-
temperature structural materials in land-based gas turbines, as
well as in aircraft turbines. Because of their importance for
industrial applications, the evolution of microstructures and their
related mechanical behaviors of single crystal Ni-base superalloys
are the subject of many studies [1–5]. Their excellent mechanical
properties at high temperature are related to a structural hard-
ening induced by the ordered g0 phase (with a L1 2 structure)
precipitated inside the disordered g phase matrix (solid solution
with a face centered cube structure). These precipitates are
coherent with the matrix and have a cuboidal shape after solid
solution and consequent aging treatments [6]. Owning to a small
difference in lattice parameters between the two phases char-
acterized by lattice mismatch, coherency lattice strain are present
in these alloys [7]. The lattice mismatch between the g phase
matrix and the precipitated g0 phase of single crystal Ni-base
superalloys has a great inﬂuence on the internal stress distribu-
tion, which affects the microstructural evolution and deformation
mechanisms under room and high temperature environments
including rafting, recrystallization and failure [8–11]. Therefore,
it is important to understand the lattice strain and the lattice
mismatch variation during deformation.
The distribution of the lattice parameters within the material can
be characterized by the diffraction proﬁles of {hkl}-type reﬂections
using either X-rays or neutrons [11]. The peak broadening of X-ray
proﬁle is closely related to the particle size (or coherent domain
size) and the density and arrangement of dislocations[12].T h ef u l l -
width at half-maximum (FWHM) of diffraction peaks is usually used
to characterize the internal stra in caused by dislocations. While
previous investigations have been made on the change in FWHM
after creep deformation for revealing the evolution of microstrain
[1], the in-depth understanding of micromechanical behavior, parti-
cularly on the change in the multiscale stress ﬁeld during deforma-
tion, of the single-crystal Ni-base superalloys still lacks in. In the
present article, synchrotron high-energy X-ray diffraction (HE-XRD)
technique with high reciprocal-space resolution was mainly
employed to study the evolution of lattice strains, lattice mismatch,
and FWHM of g/g0 phases in a single crystal Ni-base superalloy
during in-situ compression at room temperature. The present
experiment provides important evidence on the interface coherency
strain relaxation induced by plastic deformation in single crystal Ni-
base superalloys.

4. Discussion
When Ni-base superalloys are subjected to plastic deforma-
tion, g phase usually yields before g0 phase. Deformation is
generally conﬁned within the g matrix at low stress level and g0
precipitates are sheared by matrix dislocations at a higher load[14].
The lattice parameter for g phase is slightly larger than that for
g0 precipitates, and the g and g0 phases undertake initial com-
pressive and tensile stress, respectively. The coherent mismatch
increases at earlier elastic stage due to the difference in elastic
modulus between g and g0. On application of a uniaxial external
load at room temperature, deformation occurs in the matrix by
dislocation slipping on the {111} /110S slipping system, which
ﬁts well with the orthotropic slip lines on sample surfaces. The
stress to bow dislocations within the g-channels is smaller than
that to generate dislocations in the g0 precipitates, so the plastic
deformation of g/g0 microstructure proceeds by the motion of
dislocations within g-channels [15]. Dislocations are aggregating
at the g/g0 phase boundaries with the result of relieving the
coherency stress between g/g0 phases, which causes the large
minishing of ag, reducing the lattice mismatch. The dislocation
networks at the phase boundaries form additional obstacles to
penetrate the g0 phase; hence, the lattice strain increases con-
tinuingly. At stress level larger than 700 MPa, the bending of

break the coherency mismatch between g and g0 phases, which
leads to a great increase in lattice mismatch.
Generally, the peak broadening of X-ray proﬁle is mainly
caused by the crystallite size or coherent domain size, lattice
strain and/or the density and arrangement of dislocations inside
grains or particles [12,17]. Some investigations [1,18] have been
performed on the qualitative changes in FWHM of superalloys
during creep deformation and annealing. In the present experi-
ment the crystal size could not be changed under such a low
stress. The dislocations generated inside both g and g0 phases
during plastic deformation would enlarge the FWHM, rather than
reduce the FWHM. Therefore the reduction in FWHM, as seen in
Fig. 3 (d), should be related to coherency strains between the g
phase and g0 phase. The change of coherency strain at the phase
boundaries during compression can be explained schematically in
Fig. 5 . There is no dislocation at phase boundaries before defor-
mation, and the interface coherency strain is mainly caused by
equivalent dislocations as shown in Fig. 5 (a). Since the g and g0
phases are coherent, even small lattice mismatch can cause large
strains at the interface boundaries between two phases.
Fig. 5 (b) shows the array of edge dislocations after yielding of g
phase. It can be seen that motion of dislocations in the g phase
toward the phase boundaries breaks the strain coherency partly,
which induces an increase in lattice mismatch and a decrease
in FWHMs.

Fig. 6 (a) shows the evolution of integrated intensity for (200)
reﬂections as a function of applied stress. The integrated intensity
is constant in elastic regime. The increase in the integrated
intensity after plastic deformation in a single crystal is caused
by the formation of mosaic domains that could reduce the
extinction of X-ray diffraction and lead to increase in FWHM
[21]. Williamson and Hall [22] suggested that the FWHM of
diffraction peaks can be written as DK¼0.9/dþDKD, where DKD
is the strain contribution to line broadening and d is the average
grain size or particle size. K¼2sin(y)/l, DK¼2cos(y)(Dy)/l, y and
l are the diffraction angle and the wavelength of x-rays, respec-
tively, and diffraction vector g¼K at the exact Bragg position. In
the ‘‘conventional Williamson–Hall plot’’ it is assumed that DKD is
either a linear or a quadratic function of DK. The WH plot, as
shown in Fig. 6 (b), is used to separate the two kinds of contribu-
tions, i.e., the ﬁnite size of crystallites and microstrain, to the peak
width [22,23]. It can be seen from the WH plot that the crystallite
size after deformation does not change, which indicates that the
decrease in FWHM observed in our case is not due to the so-called
stress-induced grain growth as recently found in the deformed
nanocrystalline metals [23,24]. The microstrain is determined by
the slope of a straight line from the (111) and (222) reﬂection
[23,25]. The difference in the slope of the WH plot for the
undeformed and deformed samples strongly veriﬁes the
deformation-induced strain relaxation, which is in agreement
with the observation of increased intensity.
5. Conclusion
In summary, a cube-oriented single crystal Ni-base superalloy
was in-situ deformed by compression at room temperature. The
changes in lattice strain, lattice mismatch and FWHM for g/g0
phases in the alloy were successfully characterized by synchro-
tron X-ray diffraction during deformation. Two yielding points
were found during compression and the g phase yields before
g0 phase. Along loading direction, the lattice mismatch increases
linearly in the elastic regime, then decreases after the yielding
of g phase and increases again wheng0 phase is yielded. The increase
