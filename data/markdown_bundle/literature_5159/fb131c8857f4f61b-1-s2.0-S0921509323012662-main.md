ABSTRACT 
For ultralight Mg – Li alloys, the improvement of strength is the most concerned issue. In this work, we suc -
cessfully realize the incredible enhancement of strength in the Mg-8.5Li-7.5Zn-1.5Y alloy via a series of prep -
aration processes, and the excellent tensile properties containing high yield strength of 312.4 MPa and good 
elongation of 14.2 % are obtained. Yield strength increment is as high as 213 MPa through deformation pro -
cessing, which is rarely reported in Mg – Li alloys. Qualitative and quantitative studies reveal that the dislocation 
cutting of semi-coherent MgLi
2
Zn nano-precipitates is mainly responsible for such a high yield strength. The new 
finding for strengthening way is expected to contribute to the design of high-performance Mg – Li alloys. 
1. Introduction 
As the lightest of the structural alloys with potentially high specific 
strength, magnesium (Mg) alloys have attracted extensive attention due 
to the increasing demand for light weighting of structural components in 
automotive, aerospace, electronics, medical and other fields [ 1 – 4 ]. 
However, most Mg alloys are not strong enough due to the low inter -
atomic cohesion and especially the poor strengthening effect [ 5 ]. In 
addition, the activation of limited slip systems in the hexagonal 
close-packed (HCP) structure lead to the poor plasticity of common Mg 
alloys [ 6 , 7 ]. It is well known that the addition of Li to Mg alloys can not 
only further decrease the alloy density, but also effectively improve their 
plasticity by varying the lattice parameters and crystal structure [ 8 ]. 
Unfortunately, the addition of Li further reduces the strength of Mg al -
loys [ 9 ]. According to current reports, the yield strength (YS) of Mg – Li 
alloys is rarely higher than 270 MPa [ 10 ]. 
Grain refinement is recognized as an effective means of improving 
the strength and plasticity of Mg alloys [ 11 ]. Some severe plastic 
deformation (SPD) processing technologies, such as accumulative roll 
bonding (ARB), high pressure torsion (HPT), and low-temperature 
deformation, can refine Mg alloy (without Li) grains to submicron or 
even nanoscale. Somekawa et al. [ 12 ] reported that the average grain 
size of AZ31 alloy extruded at cryogenic temperature is about 1 μ m. Zhai 
et al. [ 13 ] obtained ultrafine grain structure (~0.8 μ m) in the 
Mg – 3Bi – 1Ca alloy extruded at low temperature. However, due to the 
addition of Li, the dynamic recrystallization (DRX) temperature of 
Mg – Li alloys decreases, which makes the grain growth easy and the 
realization of fine grain difficult during the deformation process [ 14 ]. 
Tang et al. [ 15 ] obtained an average grain size of 16 μ m in the 
Mg – 5Li – 5Al – 2Zn-0.5Y alloy extruded at 350 
◦
C. Wu et al. [ 16 ] reported 
that the average grain size of Mg – 5Li – 1Al (LA51) alloy after ARB at 
300 
◦
C with a total reduction of 96.8 % is still around 10 μ m. 
Precipitation strengthening is another important strengthening way 
for Mg alloys [ 17 ]. The formation of nanoscale prismatic β’ precipitates 
in Mg-13Gd alloy increases the yield strength by 120 MPa, and results in 
ultra-high strength in the Mg – Gd alloy [ 18 ]. Yu et al. [ 19 ] reported that 
Mg-6.0Zn-1.78 Yb alloy achieves the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 
417.5 MPa due to the formation of Mg – Zn precipitates. Some reports 
also involved precipitates such as Al
3
Li and MgLi
2
Al in Mg – Li – Al alloys, 
MgLi
2
Zn and MgLiZn in Mg – Li – Zn alloys [ 20 – 22 ]. But unfortunately, 
these precipitates are inferior in nature including interface, size, distri -
bution and especially their number density is too low to provide a 
compelling strengthening contribution. So far, few studies have focused 
on Mg – Li alloys strengthened predominantly by nano-precipitation.

deformation processing to obtain the ultra-light and high-strength Mg

2. Experimental procedure 
Commercial pure Mg ingots (99.9 wt%), commercial pure Li ingots 
(99.9 wt%), commercial pure Zn ingots (99.9 wt%), and Mg-20 wt% Y 
master alloy were used to prepare Mg-8.5Li-7.5Zn-1.5Y alloy. The 
chamber for smelting was first pumped to vacuum condition (less than 
10
-1 
Pa) and then filled with argon gas to achieve a vacuum of 6 × 10
-2 
MPa. The ingots were melted at 750 
◦
C for 25 min. The melt was poured 
into a permanent mold to obtain the as-cast ingot (ASTM B 403 – 2012 
standard). The obtained ingots were homogenized at 200 
◦
C for 24 h in a 
heat-treatment furnace (SX2-2.5 – 10A) under normal air atmosphere 
rather than in any inert atmosphere. Then the homogenized ingot was 
extruded into a sheet with 3 mm thickness at 350 
◦
C, and the extrusion 
ratio is 30. The extruded sheet was cut into samples with dimensions of 
45 mm × 30 mm × 3 mm for the subsequent rolling. These samples were 
soaked into liquid nitrogen for 10 min before rolling. The cryogenic 
rolling (CR) of sheets was carried out in a single pass with a reduction of 
50 %. After rolling, the sheets were soaked in the liquid nitrogen for 5 
min. 
The microstructure was characterized by a scanning electron mi -
croscope (SEM, JSM-6480A) equipped with an energy dispersive X-ray 
spectrometer (EDS) and a transmission electron microscope (TEM, FEI 
Talos F200X) equipped with EDS. The samples used for SEM observation 
were mechanically polished and etched with 4 % nitric acid alcohol 
solution. The specimens for TEM were prepared by ion-milling on a 
Gatan 695 machine. The average grain size was determined by the mean 
linear intercept method. 
The tensile specimens with gauge length of 48 mm, width of 8 mm 
and thickness of 1.5 mm (ASTM E8 (E8M)-2013a standard) were tested 
by an electronic universal testing machine (WDW3050) with an initial 
strain rate of 1 × 10
-3 
s
-1 
at room temperature. Five specimens at least for 
each condition were tested to ensure data accuracy.

3.1. Microstructure of the as-cast alloy 
Fig. 1 (a) shows that the as-cast Mg-8.5Li-7.5Zn-1.5Y alloy consists of 
a duplex matrix, i.e., a major β -Li matrix (gray color) and a minor α -Mg 
matrix (white color), and the initial grain size is about 90 μ m. It is noted 
that there are also massive eutectic pockets along grain boundaries, and 
dispersed particles in β -Li matrix, as shown in Fig. 1 (b). Fig. 1 (c) shows 
the macroscopic image of the as-cast sample subjected to a 50 % rolling 
reduction at low temperature. The obvious cracking indicates that the 
as-cast alloy has limited deformation ability at low temperature. It is 
considered that this may be related to the coarse grain size and massive 
eutectic pockets of the as-cast alloy. Consequently, the hot extrusion- 
assisted CR processing route was designed to process this Mg – Li alloy, 
based on hot extrusion refining grains and crushing large eutectic 
pockets.

4. Discussion 
In this work, we successfully prepared an ultralight and high- 
strength Mg – Li alloy by casting, pre-hot-extrusion followed by cryo -
genic rolling, and the strength of the alloy is superior to that of most 
Mg – Li alloys reported so far. Two important factors need to be empha -
sized in the preparation process of this high strength Mg-8.5Li-7.5Zn- 
1.5Y alloy. First, the process of cryogenic rolling is assisted by hot 
extrusion. The results show that even the α -Mg + β -Li duplex matrix 
Mg – Li alloy can not achieve large deformation at low temperature when 
coarse and hard eutectics exist in the coarse-grained matrix. After pre- 
hot-extrusion treatment, the alloy can realize the process. But

2

near the grain boundaries (areas with more dislocations), lines appear in 
the MgLi
2

particle, as shown in Fig. 8 . This phenomenon is similar to the lines on θ 
precipitates in a deformed Al – Cu alloy [ 63 ], and it has been suggested as 
evidence of steps induced by dislocation cutting in the semi-coherent 
second phase [ 63 ]. 
For the coherent particles, an advancing dislocation either bypasses 
them or cuts through them, and cutting increases phase interface due to 
formation of antiphase boundary. However, the dislocation shearing 
occurs within semi-coherent precipitates, such as MgLi
2
Zn in β -Li ma -
trix, the slip directions of two crystals do not align in the same orien -
tation and the slip magnitudes are also not the same in the two crystals. 
This indicates that the mismatched interface is generated between the 
precipitates and the matrix and the dislocation needs to change its di -
rection when it would enter the MgLi
2
Zn precipitate from the matrix, 
leading to the cutting-induced steps ( Fig. 8 ) [ 64 ]. It is worth empha -
sizing that this phenomenon is only observed in the CR-processed alloy, 
as the MgLi
2
Zn particles in the cast alloy are relatively large, making it 
difficult for moving dislocations to cut through the second-phase 
particles. 
There is no doubt that the increment in critical resolved shear stress 
(CRSS) can be used to describe the effect of precipitates on strengthening 
[ 65 ]. For the CR Mg-8.5Li-7.5Zn-1.5Y alloy, the larger the CRSS in the 
system, the stronger the resistance to dislocation motion, resulting in a 
higher YS of the CR alloy. CRSS vaules of varying slip systems in BCC 
structure β -Li matrix are relatively small [ 65 ]. The introduction of a 
large number of MgLi
2
Zn nano-particles with an average spacing of 
about 65 nm would significantly increase the CRSS values in the system. 
In addition, the introduction of semi-coherent interfaces leads to 
discontinuity of the CRSS [ 66 ]. This causes an elevation of the overall 
energy of the system, resulting in a further augmented resistance to 
dislocation movement [ 66 ].

5. Conclusions 
In this study, we successfully prepared a Mg-8.5Li-7.5Zn-1.5Y alloy 
with remarkable strength, plasticity, and lightweight characteristics. 
This new Mg – Li alloy exhibits an extremely high YS of 312.4 MPa, a 
good EL of 14.2 %, and a low density of 1.55 g/cm
3
. The microstructure 
and tensile properties were thoroughly investigated, leading to the 
following key findings: 
(1) MgLi
2
Zn nano-precipitates with average size of 30 nm and 
spacing of about 65 nm can be formed in the β -Li matrix of Mg- 
8.5Li-7.5Zn-1.5Y alloy. The relatively high Zn alloying in the 
Mg – Li alloys with β -Li matrix, supplemented by deformation 
processing at low temperature, is one of the methods to develop 
profuse nano-precipitates in Mg – Li alloys. 
(2) The CR-processed alloy presents the superhigh YS (312.4 MPa) in 
the reported Mg – Li alloys. This remarkable increase in YS can be 
primarily attributed to the dislocations cutting through semi- 
coherent MgLi
2
Zn precipitates. 
(3) Despite undergoing deformation processing at low temperature, 
the average grain size of the Mg-8.5Li-7.5Zn-1.5Y alloy remains 
larger than 10 μ m, signifying that the formation of ultrafine 
grains cannot be achieved through this process. In view of the fact 
that the process in this work is single pass large reduction, it is 
considered that multi-pass small reduction treatment for each 
pass at low temperature is more favorable for obtaining ultrafine 
grained.
