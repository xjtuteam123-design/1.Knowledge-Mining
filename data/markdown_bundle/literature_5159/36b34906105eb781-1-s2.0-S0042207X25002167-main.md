ABSTRACT
CoCrFeNiMn high-entropy alloy thin films (HEAFs) were fabricated on TA15 (near- α Ti alloy) and 304 stainless 
steel (SS) substrates using magnetron sputtering. The films were used as an interlayer in vacuum diffusion 
welding to enhance the mechanical properties of the welded joints. It was observed that higher sputtering 
temperatures resulted in increased surface roughness, while significantly enhancing the crystallinity of the 
crystal structure. During process optimization, it was found that the increase in sputtering temperature had a 
significant impact on the joint strength. The shear strength of the joint exhibited a trend of initially increasing 
and then decreasing, with the peak shear strength approximately 76.30 % higher than that of joints without an 
interlayer. Fracture surface morphology analysis revealed a brittle fracture characteristic. Joints with interlayer 
exhibited superior mechanical properties compared to those without, owing to the synergistic effect of high 
entropy and sluggish diffusion, which slowed down element diffusion and effectively suppressed the formation of 
brittle phases in the weld seam. The results demonstrate that the use of CoCrFeNiMn HEAF as an interlayer 
significantly enhances the mechanical properties and welding quality of TA15 and 304SS joints, providing a new 
technical solution for high-strength welding.
1. Introduction
TA15, a near- α titanium alloy, combines the dual advantages of 
α -titanium alloys and ( α + β ) titanium alloys, demonstrating excellent 
thermal stability and weldability [ 1 – 3 ]. However, the fabrication pro -
cess for TA15 is complex and costly, and its service temperature 
generally does not exceed 600 
◦
C [ 4 ], which limits its application in 
broader fields. In contrast, 304 stainless steel (304SS), a widely used 
Cr-Ni based stainless steel, exhibits lower cost and excellent corrosion 
resistance due to its inner chromium oxide layer and outer mixed 
iron-nickel oxide layer [ 5 ]. Welding TA15 with 304SS to form 
TA15/304SS joints enables the combination of the outstanding corro -
sion resistance of titanium alloys and the economic benefits of stainless 
steel, fully exploiting the synergistic effects of both materials. Such 
dissimilar metal joints have promising applications in industries such as 
petrochemical, marine engineering, and nuclear power equipment 
[ 6 – 10 ]. Researchers have attempted to directly join titanium alloy and 
stainless steel using traditional fusion welding methods such as arc 
welding [ 11 ], laser welding [ 12 ], and electron beam welding [ 13 , 14 ]. 
However, due to the low solubility of Fe atoms in Ti, brittle Fe-Ti 
intermetallic compounds (IMCs) such as FeTi and Fe
2
Ti tend to form 
at the weld interface, significantly reducing the strength and ductility of 
the joint [ 15 ]. Moreover, the large differences in thermal conductivity 
and thermal expansion coefficients between titanium and steel often 
lead to uneven thermal stress during welding, resulting in large residual 
stresses and various defects, making it challenging to achieve 
high-quality welded joints. Therefore, exploring more effective welding 
techniques to address these issues is essential for improving the quality 
of TA15/304SS welded joints.
Vacuum diffusion welding, as a solid-state joining technique, has 
become the preferred method for joining dissimilar metals, ceramics, 
and composite materials due to its ability to achieve high-precision 
connections and avoid the adverse effects of atmospheric components 
on the joint in a vacuum environment [ 16 , 17 ]. However, during the

High-entropy alloys (HEAs), which are solid solutions formed by at 
least five metal elements in nearly equal atomic concentrations [ 26 , 27 ], 
typically exhibit a simple single-phase crystal structure. Compared to 
conventional alloys and pure metals, the self-diffusion coefficients of 
elements in HEAs are typically an order of magnitude lower, resulting in 
significantly slower diffusion rates of the alloying elements. This char -
acteristic contributes to the superior diffusion resistance of HEAs rela -
tive to other alloys or pure metals [ 28 , 29 ]. Furthermore, HEAs exhibit 
excellent high-temperature phase stability [ 30 ]. Based on the unique 
properties of HEAs, scholars have employed them as interlayers for 
dissimilar metal welding. This approach has been explored in dissimilar 
metal welding such as Al/steel and Ti/Al. Lei et al. [ 31 ] achieved 
diffusion welding between the Al
0.85

TiAl, attaining a maximum shear strength of 70 MPa. Saray et al. [ 32 ] 
used Al
0.5

of 6061 aluminum/St-12 steel, and all joints exhibited smooth Fe/HEA 
interfaces, indicating a good metallurgical fusion between the two ma -
terials. Furthermore, Li et al. [ 33 ] used the AlCoCrCuNi
2 
dual-phase 
HEA as an interlayer to achieve a strong connection between TC4 tita -
nium alloy and 316L stainless steel, with a maximum shear strength of 
214 MPa.
The CoCrFeNi-based HEAs system has attracted significant attention 
due to its unique solid solution characteristics. Among them, the face- 
centered cubic (FCC) structured CoCrFeMnNi alloy (also known as the 
Cantor alloy) [ 34 ] exhibits excellent metallurgical compatibility and 
mechanical property advantages. This alloy system, with atomic sizes 
similar to those of stainless steel constituents and a relatively low mixing 
enthalpy, favors the formation of a stable FCC single-phase solid solu -
tion, which exhibits good ductility [ 35 , 36 ]. In this study, Mn is intro -
duced as the fifth element into the classic quaternary system, adjusting 
the phase composition through atomic size matching and the negative 
mixing enthalpy effect. Theoretical calculations indicate that the addi -
tion of Mn not only enhances the mechanical properties of the interlayer 
through lattice distortion strengthening effects, and its similar atomic 
number also contributes to maintaining the stability of the solid solution 
phase [ 37 ]. Based on this, Wang et al. [ 38 ] applied the CoCrFeNiMn 
high-entropy alloy as an interlayer in a NiTi shape memory alloy/304 
stainless steel laser welding system. Experimental results confirmed that 
this interlayer reduced the formation of brittle Fe-Ti intermetallic 
compounds by 62 % while promoting the formation of (Fe,Ni) solid 
solution phases (with a volume fraction reaching 83 %), leading to a 
reduction in joint hardness to 225 HV and an increase in tensile shear 
strength to 518 MPa. Nevertheless, the application of this type of 
high-entropy alloy thin films as an interlayer in TA15/304SS dissimilar 
metal welding remains scarcely explored.
In this study, the CoCrFeNiMn high-entropy alloy thin films (HEAFs) 
were deposited on the surfaces of TA15 titanium alloy and 304SS using 
magnetron sputtering as interlayers for vacuum diffusion welding. This 
research further investigates the impact of HEAF sputtering temperature 
on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the welded joints. 
Ultimately, this study will reveal the influence of the presence or 
absence of an interlayer on the microstructure, mechanical properties, 
and IMC evolution of TA15/304SS vacuum diffusion-welded joints, 
providing both theoretical and practical insights for dissimilar metal 
welding between titanium alloys and stainless steel.
2. Materials and experimental methods
2.1. Preparation and characterization of CoCrFeNiMn HEAFs
TA15 titanium alloy and 304SS were selected as the substrate ma -
terials for the coating, with their chemical compositions listed in Ta -
bles 1 and 2 , respectively. Both materials had dimensions of 30 mm ×
30 mm × 5 mm. The samples were first ground, polished, and cleaned 
with ethanol until no visible scratches were observed under a metal -
lurgical microscope. They were then immersed in anhydrous ethanol 
and cleaned using a KQ2200DE ultrasonic cleaner for 10 – 15 min. After 
that, the samples were rinsed with deionized water and dried with ni -
trogen gas. Subsequently, the CoCrFeNiMn target was placed into the 
target holder inside the chamber of the magnetron sputtering system, 
and the tray loaded with substrates was placed into the transfer cham -
ber. After the substrates were transferred to the main chamber, rough 
and fine vacuum pumping were carried out using a mechanical pump 
and a molecular pump, respectively. Once the vacuum in the main 
chamber reached 5 × 10
 8 
Torr, a high vacuum environment was 
established, which is beneficial for surface cleanliness and significantly 
improves the compactness and adhesion strength of the films [ 39 ]. 
High-purity argon gas (Ar, 99.999 %) was introduced as the working 
gas, with a flow rate of 30 – 35 sccm. At a working pressure of 6 mTorr, 
the substrate tray was rotated at 20 rpm, and a pre-sputtering mode was 
used for 20 min to further clean the CoCrFeNiMn target and remove 
surface impurities from the substrates. During the formal sputtering 
process, a sputtering power of 100 W was applied, with a working 
pressure of 3 mTorr, and the sputtering was continued for 5 h. The 
temperatures were set to four different conditions: 25 
◦

◦

generally involve high-temperature environments. The sputtering tem -
perature applied in this study is consistent with that reported in other 
literature [ 40 , 41 ]. As for characterization, the surface and 
cross-sectional microstructures of the film samples were observed using 
a QUANTA 250 scanning electron microscope (SEM) and atomic force 
microscope (AFM, Bruker Dimension Edge American). The phase 
structure of the films was analyzed using a D8 ADVANCE X-ray 
diffractometer (XRD).

In addition to the joint morphology, the metallographic structure of 
the weld joints was examined using OM, as shown in Fig. 5 . It should be 
noted that, due to the different corrosion solution formulations for ti -
tanium alloy and stainless steel, the corrosion solution used in this study 
primarily exposed the metallographic structure of the titanium alloy 
material. The joints were divided into six regions, from left to right. 
Region I corresponds to the 304SS base metal. Region II represents the 
high-temperature diffusion reaction zone near the weld in the 304SS. 
Region III is the weld fusion zone, which appears black under OM due to 
the presence of potentially corrosive compounds or unbonded voids. 
Region IV is the smooth, flat β -Ti zone. Region V is the biphasic α -Ti and 
β -Ti coexistence zone. Region VI corresponds to the initial equiaxed α -Ti 
phase of the TA15 base material. In the TA15/304SS diffusion welding 
process, since Fe, Ni, and Cr are strong β -Ti stabilizing elements, the 
width of the β -Ti region can reflect the extent of elemental diffusion 
between the two base materials. Measurements show that the width of 
the IV region in the five samples are 29.17 μ m, 33.76 μ m, 30.04 μ m, 
39.60 μ m, and 34.76 μ m, with the β -Ti layer widths being similar. The 
process conditions in this study ensure sufficient elemental diffusion and 
metallurgical bonding between the two base materials. However, 
compared to the joints without an interlayer ( Fig. 5 (e)), the weld fusion 
zone (region III) in joints with an interlayer is noticeably thinner, indi -
cating that the CoCrFeNiMn interlayer plays an active role in sup -
pressing the formation of compounds and voids during the TA15 and 
304SS diffusion welding process. Considering the hysteretic diffusion 
effect of high-entropy alloys [ 53 ], where the unique crystal structure 
and lattice distortion of high-entropy alloys can effectively slow down 
the atomic diffusion rate, the interlayer in the film reduces the atomic 
diffusion rate during welding. This inhibition of compound phase for -
mation improves the metallurgical bonding quality of the joint.

3.3. Mechanical properties and fracture morphologies
As shown in Fig. 7 , the hardness distribution of joints with and 
without an interlayer was compared. Multiple measurement points were 
selected for analysis along a direction perpendicular to the weld, with an 
interval of 5 μ m between each point. The results indicate that, in all 
conditions, the weld seam region exhibit higher hardness compared to 
the base metal. Specifically, in the joint without an interlayer, the weld 
seam exhibits a peak hardness of 675 HV due to the formation of a 
higher amount of Fe-Ti and Fe-Ti-Cr IMCs. In contrast, for the joint with 
an interlayer, the maximum hardness increases with the rise in sput -
tering temperature, reaching a peak value of 600 HV. This increase is 
due to the higher content of Fe-Ti, Cr-Ti, and Ni-Ti IMCs. Furthermore, 
the IMC layer in the joint without an interlayer has a significantly higher 
peak hardness and a broader affected region, while the joint with an 
interlayer, likely containing more Ni-Ti IMCs, shows a relatively lower 
hardness.

which was welded without interlayer, demonstrates the lowest shear

interlayer deposited at 200 
◦

4. Conclusion
CoCrFeNiMn high-entropy alloy films were deposited via magnetron 
sputtering at various temperatures and employed as interlayers between 
TA15 and 304SS. Subsequently, vacuum diffusion welding of TA15 and 
304SS was performed under the welding conditions of 950 
◦
C, a holding 
time of 170 min, and a welding pressure of 10 MPa. For comparative 
analysis, joints without an interlayer were also prepared using the same 
welding parameters, to further investigate the strengthening mechanism 
of the HEAF interlayer on the welded joints. The results are summarized 
as follows. 
1) As the sputtering temperature increased, the surface roughness of the 
HEAFs gradually became higher, while their crystallinity signifi -
cantly improved, exhibiting an FCC single-phase structure. When the 
sputtering temperature reached 600 
◦
C, a noticeable increase in 
porosity was observed on the film surface, and the grain morphology 
underwent significant changes. The surface roughness was two or -
ders of magnitude greater than that of the base material.
2) Under the welding parameters of 900 
◦
C, a holding time of 170 min, 
and a welding pressure of 10 MPa, as the sputtering temperature 
increased, the high-entropy effect and sluggish diffusion effect of the 
interlayer effectively reduced the rate of element diffusion and 
suppressed the formation of brittle phases in the TA15/304SS joint. 
As a result, the weld seam width decreased accordingly. Further -
more, the maximum shear strength of this joint reached approxi -
mately 152.5 MPa, marking an increase of about 76 % increase 
compared to the 86.5 MPa of the joint without an interlayer.
3) With the same welding parameters, the fracture surface of directly 
diffusion-bonded joints mainly contained brittle intermetallic com -
pounds, such as Fe
2
Ti, Cr
2
Ti, and FeTi. In contrast, the joint fracture 
surface with a high-entropy interlayer primarily consists of the 
ductile FCC phase, along with Ni
3
Ti and NiTi compounds. This re -
sults in a significantly improved joint quality compared to directly 
welded joints.
In summary, the introduction of CoCrFeNiMn HEAF interlayers 
significantly reduced the formation of brittle phases and improved the 
mechanical properties of TA15/304SS joints, offering an effective
