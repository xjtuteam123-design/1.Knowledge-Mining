1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) consisting of at least five chemical ele -
ments, have attracted significant research interest as structural materials 
due to their exceptional mechanical properties [ 1 – 5 ]. The strength of 
single-phase FCC-structured HEAs is usually insufficient, which prevents 
their potential application prospects [ 6 – 8 ]. Strengthening can be ach -
ieved by several methods, such as grain refinement, severe plastic 
deformation, and precipitation hardening [ 9 – 13 ]. In previous studies, 
nanoscale L1
2
-type precipitates were found to be particularly effective in 
balancing the mechanical properties of FCC-structured HEAs because 
the dislocation was primarily impeded by the coherent phase boundary 
[ 4 , 5 , 14 – 18 ]. L1
2
-strengthened HEAs have many toughening mecha -
nisms, such as dynamic-refined stacking faults and dislocation-induced 
microbands [ 14 , 15 ]. Noteworthy, controlling the geometry of lamellar 
nanoscale precipitates can endow the material with excellent properties, 
which are different from those of spherical precipitates [ 4 , 5 ]. 
A lot more systematic explorations are demanded to fully exploit the 
plasticity advantages of FCC-structured HEAs, while enhancing its 
strength. Therefore, the formation of multiscale grain structures, such as 
bimodal grain structures and harmonic structures, has attracted signif -
icant research attention in different alloy systems [ 19 – 22 ]. In these 
unique structures, the high strength originates from ultrafine grains 
(UFGs), while larger grains (LGs) are designed to provide continuous 
strain-hardening capability, resulting in a good combination of strength 
and ductility [ 23 – 25 ]. Yuan et al. [ 26 ] showed that the nanoscale 
spherical L1
2 
precipitates-strengthened partially recrystallized HEA was 
achieved with the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 1335 MPa and total 
elongation (EL) of 14% at room temperature. Fan et al. [ 27 ] fabricated 
nanorods-shaped L1
2 
precipitates in fully recrystallized HEAs, which 
exhibited a high UTS of ~1.8 GPa and EL of ~18.2% at room temper -
ature. Such strategies are very effective, and the experimental results are 
encouraging. However, the recent studies have not yet thoroughly dis -
cussed the phenomenon of rolling inhomogeneous deformations, but 
merely described the morphology of the shear bands [ 27 , 28 ]. Although 
the microstructure evolution and the texture formation process of HEAs 
after rolling deformation were elaborated by Kaushik et al. [ 29 ], it is still 
necessary to propose a strategy to control inhomogeneous deformation

It is well known that the deformation of single crystals is not uniform. 
Many different alloys and even ceramics exhibit discontinuous slip 
deformation with Schmid factors close to 0.5 in micro-compression 
testing [ 30 – 33 ]. Similarly, the deformation of several grains with the 
same grain orientation is also discontinuous because the grains have the 
same slip direction, which can be extended to columnar crystalline 
materials with unified crystal orientation. Based on the aforementioned 
analysis, in this study, a strategy was proposed to stably obtain materials 
with an inhomogeneously deformed microstructure by rolling the alloy 
perpendicular to the growth direction of the columnar crystals. Subse -
quently, a bimodal multiscale grain structure was obtained by recrys -
tallization annealing of inhomogeneously deformed HEA. This method 
is theoretically applicable to almost all alloys that can be directionally 
solidified and rolled. Furthermore, in order to obtain lamellar L1
2 
nano-precipitates during the annealing process, a novel Cu-containing 
HEA with FCC structure was designed to control the precipitation 
behavior. In our previous study, Cu promoted L1
2
-type precipitation 
from the matrix [ 34 ], which was attributed to the large mixing enthalpy 
between Cu and other alloying elements [ 35 – 39 ]. The strategies used in 
the current study, including the mechanical process design for obtaining 
inhomogeneous deformed structures and the composition design for 
controlling precipitates, offer a new approach for the development of 
promising high-performance structural materials.

In order to further analyze the strong strain hardening, the true stress 
and corresponding strain work-hardening rate (SHR) (d σ /d ε ) vs. true 
strain curve of the T700 sample was obtained and presented in Fig. 8 . 
The result indicates that the strain-hardening behavior is similar to that 
of L1
2
-precipitate strengthened HEAs [ 4 , 5 ], and can be divided into 
three main stages. The work-hardening rate first presents a significant 
initial drop in the true strain range of 0 – 1.8% (labeled as stage I), and 
then the work-hardening rate decreases and increases many times till a 
strain of ~4.8% (stage II), and finally slightly decreases until tensile 
failure over the strain of ~13% (stage III). Three distinct deformation 
stages are recognized in SHR responses of the heterogeneous bimodal 
structure. 
It is well known that deformation behavior of larger grains is 
different from that of the fine grains, because higher stress is needed for 
the plastic deformation of the fine grains [ 50 – 52 ]. In the stage I (strain 
≤ 1.8%), LGs exhibit a relatively strong capacity to accommodate dis -
locations. Furthermore, many stacking faults (SFs) and nanotwins (NTs) 
are formed inside the LGs, which results in the decrease in the path 
length of dislocations and impedes dislocation slip [ 27 ]. During the 
stage II, the strain gradients arise at the boundaries of larger grains and 
fine grains, corresponding to the hard-soft boundary regions, the stress 
develops and subsequently activates new slip systems or induces for -
ward stress in the ultra-fine grains [ 50 – 52 ], thus realizing the dynamic 
Hall-Petch effect [ 53 ]. Moreover, the lamellar L1
2 
nano-precipitates act 
as an obstacle to dislocation movement, resulting in the increased 
work-hardening rate [ 4 , 5 , 27 ]. The synergy of heterogeneous bimodal 
structure and L1
2 
nano-precipitates results in the fluctuation of the work 
hardening rate. During the stage III (strain ≧ 4.8%)), with development 
of the stresses, LGs are continuously divided into fine grains. Subse -
quently activates new slip systems actived in both LGs and UFGs as well 
as SFs and NTs lead to the gradual decrease in the strain hardening 
ability of the HEAs [ 27 , 54 ]. Thus, the heterogeneous bimodal structure 
led to the strengthening and continuous work hardening by generating 
the back stress and forward stress at hard-soft domains [ 50 – 52 ].

Thus, the ultrahigh strength originates mainly from the lamellar

overcomes the strength loss caused by dislocation recovery and recrys -
tallization during the annealing process. 
To sum up, the features of the HEA microstructures during the cold 
rolling and annealing processes are schematically depicted in Fig. 12 . 
The columnar crystal (marked as CC) of the HEAs was obtained after 
solid solution treatment ( Fig. 12 a). After 90% cold rolling, both macro- 
shear band and non-shear band regions appeared, and the distribution of 
the macro-shear bands was observed as a network ( Fig. 12 b). After 
annealing at 700 
◦

UFGs and LGs, respectively. The LGs enclosed with UFGs assisted the 
coordination of grain deformation, resulting in enhanced plasticity. 
Furthermore, the lamellar nano-precipitates both at UFGs and LGs 
generated excellent strengthening effects ( Fig. 12 c). Thus, the above- 
mentioned characteristics of the evolved microstructure led to HEAs 
with excellent strength and elongation. 
4. Conclusions 
In this paper, we performed a new strategy for developing HEAs with 
exceptional strength and elongation by cold-rolling of columnar grains 
and subsequent annealing. The design and process route of the manu -
factured HEA enable the modification of the size and distribution of 
grains and the lamellar coherent nano-precipitates, resulting in a sig -
nificant balance of strength (~1.68 GPa) and elongation (~13.5%), 
superior to that of previously reported HEAs. The ultrahigh strength 
originates mainly from the lamellar nano-precipitates strengthening and 
the bimodal sized grains; however, the large elongation correlates to a 
progressive work-hardening mechanism regulated by the collaborative 
deformation of the UFGs and LGs and the unique nano-lamellar pre -
cipitates. This study suggests a new strategy to utilize bimodal grains 
and coherent nano-precipitates in the development of HEAs or other 
similar alloys.
