ABSTRACT 
Creep rupture life is a key target for designing Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys. Machine learning (ML) 
has the potential to accelerate alloy design by accurately predicting the creep rupture life of potential superal -
loys. In this work, a physical-constrained neural network named Superalloy Transformer-based Network for 
Creep (SaTNC) is established, and the representation of superalloy is automatically learned based on deep 
representation learning. As core physical bias in the SaTNC model, the information associated with interaction 
among alloying elements achieved by Transformer-based neural network is introduced to constrain the repre -
sentation of the superalloy. The SaTNC model exhibits competitive generalization performance and the results 
have verified the high capability of the SaTNC model to capture the sensitivities of creep rupture life on alloy 
composition and service conditions. Moreover, Transformer-based deep representation for superalloy with both 
effectiveness and interpretability is obtained. The material insights including the interactions among alloying 
elements and their combined effects on the creep rupture life of superalloy are extracted from this deep repre -
sentation. Finally, with the aid of the SaTNC model, a potential alloy composition space with low density and 
excellent creep resistance at high temperatures is identified, which will be experimentally verified in the future. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys are typical materials applied 
for aero-engine turbine blades [1] . Material fracture caused by creep 
deformation exceeding design tolerance is one of the main failure modes 
of crucial heated end components in aero engines, which limits their 
service life. To satisfy the increasing requirements of service perfor -
mance for the aero-engine turbine, continuously improving creep 
rupture life is one of the important goals in the design of modern Ni- 
based SX superalloys. Ni-based SX superalloy is composed of γ (FCC) 
matrix and γ’ (L1
2
) phase, which contains more than 10 elements, such 
as γ’ strengthening elements Al, Ta, Ti and γ strengthening elements Mo, 
W, Re, Ru. A small change in the content of alloying elements could 
cause a significant influence on creep resistance, which leads to the alloy 
composition space being huge and complex [2,3] . However, the process 
of the traditional design approach - “ trial and error ” requires high cost 
and a long development cycle, and it is difficult to quickly discover the 
alloy compositions with desired creep resistance from the huge 
composition space. Therefore, establishing a model that can rapidly and 
accurately predict the creep rupture life of superalloys is of great sig -
nificance to accelerating the composition design of Ni-based SX super -
alloys with low costs and high performances. 
In the past, the time – temperature parameter model represented by 
the Larson-Miller criterion [4] and the physical-based constitutive 
models [5,6] based on the behavior of dislocation movement and 
microstructural evolution during the creep deformation are mostly used

to estimate the creep rupture life of Ni-based SX superalloys. The

applications of creep constitutive models are limited to narrow ranges of 
composition, temperature and stress because the creep constitutive 
models are more used to predict the creep rupture life of the superalloys 
with the same creep mechanism, while creep mechanisms usually vary 
with alloy composition and service conditions. It is hard to utilize a 
theoretical model to screen the vast alloy space efficiently. 
In recent years, materials informatics has shown great advantages in 
accelerating material design [7–11]. Machine learning (ML) has become 
a core method in materials informatics, for its versatility in mining the 
rules contained in high-dimensional material data and discovering po -
tential new materials driven by data. It has been successfully applied in 
the design of perovskites [12], high-entropy alloys [13,14], and amor -
phous materials [15]. For the design of superalloy, by combining the 
multielement diffusion coefficient database obtained by high- 
throughput experiments with unsupervised ML algorithms, Feng Liu 
et al. [16] propose a novel design strategy and designed alloys with 
outstanding creep resistance and structural stability. Similarly, Zi Wang 
et al. [17] construct the relationships between composition and micro -
structural features including γ’ fraction, γ’ size, and TCP fraction of Ni- 
based superalloys with the aid of high-throughput experiments. And for 
the alloy designs that point directly to the mechanical properties of the 
target, ML is widely applied to estimate mechanical properties from an 
initial of the chemical composition. Typically, Conduit et al. [18] 
develop a neural network tool to predict fatigue life, yield stress and 
elongation of superalloys, and a superalloy that satisfies multiple 
physical criteria is designed. In order to improve the generalization of 
ML models and further reduce the uncertainty in the design process of 
superalloys, more works focus on the constructing descriptors of su -
peralloys including microstructure [19–21], thermodynamic properties 
[22,23] and defect energies [24,25]. Naturally, several ML models 
[26–29] are developed by combining ML algorithms with physical- 
informed descriptors for predicting the creep rupture life of superal -
loys. Although these established ML models have extended the gener -
alized composition space of superalloys over the traditional theoretical 
models, the development of ML models for superalloys is still upgrade -
able. A key issue is that the representation of superalloys has always 
been neglected. For the ML models applied in materials science, the 
main inputs are chosen as the descriptions for materials, which de -
termines the generalization performance of ML. However, the repre -
sentation of superalloys used in ML models is limited because ML models 
heavily rely on manually constructed features. Currently, simple 
chemical compositions or composition-based statistical vectors (CBFV) 
[30] are widely used to represent superalloys. CBFV is the representative 
manually constructed features, the key atomic properties are introduced 
and the statistical features are constructed by using the composition as 
the weights. But, neither the compositions nor the CBFV features lack 
the necessary physical information. The insufficient information in the 
input further limits the generalization performance of subsequent ML 
models. For the superalloy, it is not only the contents and properties of 
the alloying elements but also the elemental interactions that need to be 
considered. The elemental interactions are existing in the superalloy 
system [31–33], especially, the elemental interaction on diffusion under 
the thermal–mechanical coupling conditions could affect the micro -
structural evolution of the alloy and thus the creep resistance. The ex -
istence of elemental interactions implies that the elemental inter- 
linkages need to be represented for superalloy. In this case, the costly 
manual constructed features heavily relying on specific empirical 
knowledge or even intuition cannot provide the specific descriptor for 
the elemental inter-linkages. Benefiting from great ability in represen -
tation learning and high efficiency of automatic feature learning, deep 
learning (DL) provides a new opportunity to solve the above problems. A 
cutting-edge network architecture – Transformer [34] is worth noting 
because the internal Self-attention mechanism in Transformer could 
represent the interdependence among nodes as well as the dependencies 
between a single node and global information [35,36]. It is a natural 
choice for the representation of information sharing among alloying 
elements for superalloys. Besides, considering some elements play a 
critical role in elemental interactions in creep, such as Re, and Ru 
[37–39]. The Self-attention mechanism provides a suitable hypothesis 
allowing these critical elements could be focused in the representation of 
elemental interactions. Therefore, the Transformer architecture could 
constrain the neural network to learn the representation of superalloy 
with materialistic meaning. 
In this work, we have established a deep learning model - SaTNC 
(Superalloy Transformer-based Net for Creep) for predicting the creep 
rupture life of Ni-based SX superalloy. With the particular aid of 
Transformer, the intrinsic properties and information sharing among the 
alloy system are integrated into the representation of superalloy with 
multiple elements to restrict the SaTNC model to learn more key prin -
ciples about complex interactions of alloying elements. The micro -
structural features are integrated into the model in order to improve its 
performance further. Our research results show that SaTNC could 
accurately predict the creep rupture lives of Ni-based SX superalloys 
with different compositions and under various service conditions, the 
accuracy of predicted Larson-Miller (LM) curve (R
2
) greater than 0.97 
for several typical Ni-based SX superalloys, and the prediction error 
(MAPE) less than 0.25 for unseen alloy compositions. More importantly, 
the representation of superalloys provided by the Transformer-based 
neural network is proved to be effective, and we obtain extra inter -
pretable information and extract some material insights related to multi- 
component effects from this representation. We have also found that the 
elemental embedding extracted from SaTNC encodes materials science 
knowledge related to creep behavior. Finally, we apply the SaTNC 
model to the composition design of the novel Ni-based SX superalloy. A 
new superalloy system with potential low-density requirements and 
high creep resistance at high temperatures is discovered and will be 
experimentally verified in the future. 
2. Construction of the SaTNC model 
2.1. Data collection and processing 
The creep datasets are composed of the creep life results of 1675 Ni- 
based SX superalloys with [0 0 1] orientation under different creep 
conditions of temperature and stress. Data are collected from published 
literature and patents, as well as the experimental data obtained by our 
group. All data collected from open literature, and patents, except un -
published data from our group, can be viewed on GitHub 
(fanYang-X/SaTNC-A-Transformer-baed-neural-network-f 
or-creep-rupture-life-prediction (github.com)). An overview of this 
dataset can be found in Supplementary Table 1 and Fig. 1. 
Considering the important influence of phase structure features and 
defects on creep properties, we attempt to expand the descriptions of 
microstructure in the feature space [40,41]. It should be clarified that 
some information on the microscopic scale that determines the creep 
deformation behavior is difficult to be introduced, such as initial dislo -
cation density in γ, interface dislocation density, etc. Although this in -
formation could be obtained experimentally or by simulations including 
first principles and molecular dynamics, this process is extremely cost 
intensive. Therefore, from a cost-effectiveness point of view, the 
microstructural features introduced in datasets need to satisfy the 
following premises: 1. the features can be estimated from the given alloy 
composition and service conditions to ensure that the database can be 
expanded effectively; 2. the estimation of these features needs to be 
sufficiently accurate. Based on this, microstructure features related to 
creep rupture life including volume fraction of γ’ phase, γ/γ’ lattice 
mismatch, stacking fault energy (SFE) of γ and antiphase boundary en -
ergy (APBE) of γ’ are added to the dataset. These microstructural fea -
tures are calculated at creep temperature, the volume fraction of γ’

3.2. Effectiveness of deep representation for superalloy, microstructural 
features and elemental properties 
In order to investigate the roles of different parts played in a deep 
learning model, ablation experiments are often used to evaluate the 
changes in model performances caused by removing or altering corre -
sponding network structures. To clarify the roles of the prior physical 
information including the representation of superalloys based on the 
Transformer and key microstructure features in SaTNC, we conduct two 
parts of ablation experiments. Firstly, we demonstrate the impacts of the 
Transformer-based neural network in the SaTNC model, the role of 
Conditional Self-attention and Elemental FeedForward in it is also 
investigated. Four comparison models are settled and named SaTNC_NT 
(the SaTNC without the Transformer), SaTNC_T (standard Transformer), 
SaTNC_E (replacing the FeedForward in standard Transformer with 
Elemental FeedForward), SaTNC_C (replacing the Self-attention in 
standard Transformer with Conditional Self-attention). For the 
SaTNC_NT model, we use the features consistent with the ML model 
(compositions and CBFV features) to ensure the consistency of the input 
information. As shown in Table 2 , when the Transformer-based neural 
network is replaced by the compositions and CBFV features, the per -
formance of the SaTNC model decreases significantly. The MAPE and 
SMAPE scores increase by 13.59% and 7.38% respectively, which con -
firms the effectiveness of Transformer-based deep representation 
learning compared with manually constructed features for the repre -
sentation of superalloys. Besides, the generalization performance of 
SaTNC_E, SaTNC_C and SaTNC_T is decreased compared with that of 
SaTNC. It indicates that the Conditional Self-attention and Elemental 
FeedForward in the Transformer-based neural network could further 
promote the SaTNC model to learn the superalloy representation that is 
conducive to improving the generalization performance. 
Further, an additional study has also demonstrated that Transformer- 
based networks provide effective representations for superalloys. The 
representations extracted from the SaTNC model are used as input to the 
LR (Lasso) [52] , LR (poly + Lasso), SVR, and MLP models, LR (poly +
Lasso) is the LR (Lasso) model with polynomial expansion features. The 
variation in the prediction accuracy of these ML models is obtained. 
Although LR does not apply to model the nonlinear relationship between 
alloy composition and creep rupture life under thermodynamic coupling 
conditions, it can still be utilized to explore whether the deep repre -
sentation in Transformer-based networks provides extra valuable in -
formation, since simple linear models are more dependent on the quality 
of the input features. Furthermore, the LR (poly + Lasso) with higher 
accuracy can also ensure the credibility of the information obtained in 
the LR model. (The parameter settings of the LR model can be found in 
Supplementary Table 5 .) Note that since the Transformer-based network 
consists of two layers and the [CLS] vector can be used as a compression 
representation for the representation of superalloys, as described in Sec 
2.2 . The [CLS] vectors in two layers are both extracted for input into the 
ML models. Compared to Model (LR (Lasso), LR (poly + Lasso), SVR, 
MLP)-base, the prediction errors (MAPE) of Model-layer1 are reduced by 
14.1%, 44.75%, 24.4%, and 2.3%, respectively, the prediction errors 
(MAPE) of Model-layer2 are reduced by 16.7%, 50.9%, 37.9%, and 
11.9%, respectively, as shown in Fig. 3 . The deep representations in two 
layers of the Transformer-based network are conducive to the 
improvement of the LR (Lasso), LR (poly + Lasso), SVR and MLP, and 
that these representations become more effective as the Transformer- 
based network progresses, the improvement in prediction performance 
caused by [CLS] in Layer2 is higher than that of [CLS] in Layer1. The 
improved performance of the LR (Lasso) and sufficient prediction ac -
curacy of the LR (poly + Lasso) also imply that the deep representation 
in the SaTNC model provides features directly related to the creep 
rupture life. The additional intrinsic element interaction information 
learned from data could extend the effective feature space. In addition, a

4. Discussion 
4.1. Insights related to complex interactions among alloying elements 
Due to the “ black box ” nature of ML, interpretability is always a 
desired property of ML models. Specific to materials informatics, 
insufficient interpretability has become a major barrier limiting the 
application of ML in materials design which needs conducive insights. In 
this section, we try to extract the embedded interpretable information 
via the visualization of the Self-attention layer in the model. We mainly 
focus on two aspects: 1 the key alloying elements that directly effecting 
the creep rupture life of SX superalloy; 2 the chemical information 
sharing among the elements in SX superalloy. Firstly, considering that 
the [CLS] vector connects the alloy representations and the predicted 
creep rupture life in the SaTNC model, we visualize it by using t- 
Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding [59] (TSNE) as shown in 
Fig. 6 a . It can be seen that the dark purple symbols represent the su -
peralloys with low creep rupture lives are mainly located in the top right 
corner part of Fig. 6 a . This indicates that the spatial distribution of 
visualized [CLS] vector could roughly distinguish the creep rupture life 
of superalloy. Moreover, considering that [CLS] plays a global attention 
role in the representation learning of superalloy systems, some key el -
ements focused by [CLS] could directly affect the creep rupture life. As 
illustrated in Fig. 6 b
1

Nasair100 and CMSX-10 respectively. And this information captured by 
the ML model is consistent with previous experimental results. W is 
considered a critical γ -strengthening element in the first-generation SX 
superalloys because its low diffusion rate could retard the dislocation 
motion in γ according to Cottrell theory [60 – 62] . While for the third- 
generation SX superalloy CMSX-10, which has a much higher content 
of Re (6 wt%), lower microstructural stability has become a noteworthy 
issue restricting its creep resistance [63] . Co has been proven to delay 
the coarsening of γ’ phases [64] as well as the precipitation of detri -
mental Topologically Close-packed Phases (TCP) [65,66] , which is 
similar to the Ru effect [67] . In other words, Co does have an important 
influence on the creep rupture life of high-Re no-Ru SX superalloys.

compared with those between Cr, Mo and other elements, which in -

mation. Interestingly, recent research has also suggested the important 
interactions between Mo/Cr and Ru in superalloys [68] . The presence of 
Ru is considered to mitigate the precipitation trend of TCP owing to the 
higher contents of Cr and Mo. Because Ru changes the partitioning 
behavior of Mo, Cr in γ / γ’ phases [69] , it weakens the tendency of Mo, 
Cr to be segregated in the γ phase, which in turn reduces the TCP phase 
nucleation rate in γ and enhances the creep resistance of the alloy. The 
above discussion demonstrates that the SaTNC model can capture 
interpretable information consistent with materials science insights, 
which further implies that we could extract new materials insights to 
guide the design of advanced SX superalloys. On the other hand, we have 
to mention that not all the interpretable information contained in the ML 
model could be translated into materials insights, some information 
might be over-interpreted due to the limitations of inductive bias. 
Filtration of the interpretable information can be conducted by further 
experimental and simulation verifications, in order to enhance the 
reliability of the inherent inference logic of the SaTNC model and cap -
ture the real patterns underlined in material data. For example, Atom 
Probe Tomography (APT) can be used to measure alloying element 
partitioning behavior in γ / γ’ , which could directly provide information 
about the interaction from the perspective of diffusion, and kinetic 
Monte Carlo (KMC) simulations can also verify the interaction on 
diffusion [70,71] . In addition, we encourage to utilize first principles to 
verify the existence and mechanism of interatomic interactions at the 
level of electronic structure. 
4.2. Materials information contained in elemental embedding 
In material informatics, any numerical representation has an ideal 
characteristic, i.e. this representation conforms to the intuition of ma -
terial science and is interpretable [72] . Inspired by this idea, we add 
elemental embedding into the SaTNC model when learning the repre -
sentation of elements. Here, we extract the elemental embedding from 
the SaTNC model in order to explore the contained material knowledge 
concerning creep. The elemental embedding in this work is a 62-dimen -
sional vector, we use TSNE [59] algorithm to reduce it to 3-dimensional 
for better visualization. As shown in Fig. 7 a , the γ forming elements and 
γ’ forming elements can be easily distinguished by their spatial distri -
butions. Specifically, the γ forming elements such as Re, W, Co, Cr, Mo, 
Ru are concentrated in the blue area, while the γ’ forming elements such 
as Al, Ti and Ta are concentrated in the orange area. In addition, the 
distribution of elemental embedding could also encode the electronic 
structure information of elements. It can be seen from Fig. 7 b that the 
line among Ta-Ti-Al, Ru-Co, Re-Cr and Mo-W are nearly parallel on 
TSNE1 and TSNE2, which indicates the vector represented by the line 
encodes some elemental property. This elemental property could be 
d electron numbers since the elements distributed on a line have similar 
d electron numbers. Furthermore, those elements are clustered on 
TSNE1 and TSNE3 as shown in Fig. 7 b . The above discussions clarify 
that the information related to d electron numbers could be reflected in 
the characteristics of the spatial distribution of the elemental embed -
ding. The visualization results show that the elemental embedding is not 
as incomprehensible as a “ black box ” , the spatial distribution of the 
elemental embedding codes the segregation preference in γ / γ’ as well as 
the electronic structures information of refractory elements. It further 
suggests that the established material deep learning model could ab -
stract material knowledge from data and provide representations with 
material science significance.
