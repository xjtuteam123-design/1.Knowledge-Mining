ABSTRACT 
Based on the results of many studies devoted to exploring the effect of adding different elements to CoCrFeNi 
alloys in the field of high-entropy alloys, in this study, Ge is included in CoCrFeNi alloys to form CoCrFeNiGe
x 
alloys (molar ratio, x = 0, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3). Ge is used to improve mechanical properties and corrosion resis -
tance of traditional alloys, but its application in high-entropy alloys has been less discussed. CoCrFeNiGe
x 
alloys 
(x ≤ 0.3) exhibit a single face-centered cubic phase. Tensile testing of these alloys revealed that the CoCrFe -
NiGe
0.3 
alloy exhibited slightly better mechanical properties — a yield strength of 223 MPa, ultimate tensile 
strength of 617 MPa, and elongation of 63.2% — due to solid-solution hardening. A potentiodynamic polarization 
test in deaerated 3.5% NaCl solution showed that Ge addition can effectively inhibit pitting corrosion and can 
improve the corrosion resistance of CoCrFeNi alloys by reducing the corrosion and passive current density. 
1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are a new alloy design concept reported 
by Yeh et al., in 2004 [ 1 ]. According to recent studies [ 2 , 3 ], HEAs 
exhibit numerous properties that traditional alloys do not; for example, 
(1) high-entropy effects, making single-phase solid solutions thermo -
dynamically stable; (2) lattice distortion effects that enhance 
solid-solution hardening and degrades electrical and thermal conduc -
tivity; (3) sluggish effects that limit the effective diffusion rate and often 
generates nanoprecipitates in the alloys; and (4) cocktail effects, making 
the alloy properties variable. Unlike traditional alloys that are primarily 
based on one or two elements, HEAs are composed of five to thirteen 
main elements, with atomic percentages that vary between 5% and 35%. 
Along with several unusual properties, HEAs exhibit high strengths 
[ 4 – 8 ], excellent thermal stabilities [ 9 , 10 ], large damage-tolerances 
[ 11 – 14 ], good wear [ 15 – 18 ], and corrosion resistance [ 19 – 23 ], and 
this field has significant potential for further development. 
CoCrFeMnNi alloys are one of the earliest developed HEAs and 
exhibit a single-phase face-centered cubic (fcc) solid solution structure 
after solution treatment [ 12 , 24 – 32 ]. Since the first report of CoCr -
FeMnNi alloys, research on single-phase fcc HEAs has blossomed, with 
over 30 different HEA systems reported in the literature [ 33 – 39 ]. Among 
them, the CoCrFeNi system with additions of other elements such as Al 
[ 22 , 40 , 41 ], Ti [ 37 , 42 ], Mo [ 43 , 44 ], C [ 45 – 49 ], and N [ 50 , 51 ] are 
prominent. Through the use of elements with different atomic radii, the 
mechanical properties of the alloys can be increased by solid-solution 
hardening or interstitial strengthening. The various characteristics of 
each element can provide additional benefits to alloys. For example, 
addition of Al can improve the oxidation resistance [ 52 ], and Mo 
addition can enhance the corrosion resistance [ 53 , 54 ]. In addition to 
metallic elements, metalloid elements are often discussed, such as Si 
[ 55 – 57 ]. Kumar et al. [ 56 ] mentioned that Si addition can enhance the 
hardness, wear resistance, and corrosion resistance for alloys with fcc 
matrices and intermetallic compounds rich in Ni – Si. 
Ge has rarely been discussed in the HEA field. Lam et al. [ 58 ] stated 
that tuning the minor element compositions of CoCrFeMnNi-based HEAs 
using Ge may result in improved strength and ductility. However, 
several studies have explored the addition of Ge to traditional alloys. 
Yao et al. [ 59 ] pointed out that adding a reasonable content of Ge can 
increase the corrosion resistance of Zr – 1Nb alloys. Rosalbino et al. [ 60 , 
61 ] also stated that the addition of Ge to intermetallic Fe
3
Al can enhance 
the corrosion resistance.

5. Conclusions 
1. The as-homogenized CoCrFeNiGe
x 
alloys (x = 0.1 – 0.3) are all single- 
phase fcc solid-solution structures. The Ω parameter and atomic size 
difference allow good prediction of the phase composition. However, 
the mixing enthalpy and valence electron concentration deviate 
slightly from those of single-phase fcc, indicating that the predictions 
require adjustment in case of Ge addition. 
2. The mechanical properties of the alloys improve upon Ge addition. 
This is ascribed to solid-solution hardening because Ge possesses a 
larger atomic radius than the other elements comprising the alloys. 
CoCrFeNiGe
0.3 
exhibits the best mechanical properties among the 
prepared alloys, reaching a yield strength of 223 MPa, an ultimate 
tensile strength of 617 MPa, and an elongation of 63.2%. The results 
agree with the prediction of solid-solution hardening. 
3. CoCrFeNiGe
x 
alloys exhibit superior corrosion resistance, particu -
larly pitting resistance, compared to CoCrFeNi alloys. Although all 
these alloys present similar corrosion and breakdown potentials, the 
alloys with higher Ge contents exhibit lower corrosion and passive 
current densities and wider passivation regions. Therefore, we can 
conclude that Ge addition improves the corrosion resistance of HEAs.
