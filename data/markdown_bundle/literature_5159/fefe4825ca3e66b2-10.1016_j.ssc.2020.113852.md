Abstract 
The site occupancy, lattice misfit and elastic modu lus of Ni-based single crystal 
superalloys are investigated by combining the first principles exact muffin-tin orbital 
method and coherent-potential approximation. The si te preference of alloying 
elements in γ'-Ni 3Al can be classified into four types: Co, Fe, Ru and Ir occupy Ni site 
or Cr, Mo, Re, Ti, V occupy Al anti-site (namely al loying elements occupy Al site 
while the removed Al occupy Ni site). Ti and V are the alloying elements which 
stabilize the γ'-Ni 3Al. The lattice misfit and the difference of elasti c properties 
between γ and γ' phases are the causes of γ' rafting, while the alloying elements will 
improve the differences. The effects of 1 at. % ~ 5 at. % alloying elements on lattice 
constant and misfit are studied, because of biggish atomic radius, Ir, Mo, Ti, Re and 
Ru increase the lattice constant of γ/γ' phase much faster, and the alloying elements 
have some impacts on the lattice misfit of γ/γ' phase. Re is the most remarkable 
alloying element which improves the elastic modulus of Ni-based superalloys. 
 
Keyword : Ni-based single crystal superalloys, site occupan cy, lattice misfit, 
elasticities 
 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys are ideal candidate for vanes of aero-engine and

## Page 4

industrial gas turbine due to their excellent high temperature mechanical performance 
and oxidation resistance [1-3]. The outstanding fea tures are mainly derived from 
solid-solution strengthening of γ phase (matrix phase) and precipitation strengtheni ng 
of γ' phase (foremost precipitated phase). In general, there are more than 10 elements 
alloying in Ni-based single crystal superalloys. Al loying elements either improve the 
stability of the coherently precipitated γ' phase or provide solid-solution strengthening 
in γ phase. As a solid-solution strengthen element, Re improves the creep resistance of 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys [4]. The additi on of Ru boosts the 
high-temperature mechanical property of superalloys and restrains the precipitation of 
topologically close packed (TCP) phase [5]. Cr impr oves the inoxidizability of 
Ni-based superalloys and lowers the embrittlement o f superalloys at high temperature 
[6]. Co addition reduces the solubility of Al and Ti in γ phase such that more Al and Ti 
are distributed into the precipitated γ' phase. A directional coarsening of the γ' phase 
breaks the superiority of Ni-based single crystal s uperalloys. The rafting is usually 
driven by the applied stress, the high temperature, the lattice misfit and the difference 
of elastic constants between the γ and γ′ phases [7]. Therefore, the current 
optimization of the physical and mechanical propert ies of the Ni-based superalloys 
requires a detailed understanding of the lattice mi sfit and elasticity of γ/γ' phase with 
alloying elements. 
In present work, we firstly classify and discuss th e site preference of alloying 
elements in γ'-Ni 3Al, and analyze the effect of alloying elements on the stability of 
γ'-Ni 3Al. Then, the lattice constant, lattice misfit and elastic modulus of γ and γ' phase 
with alloying elements are investigated. 
 
2. Methods and calculation details 
The first-principles methods used in the present wo rk is based on the density 
functional theory (DFT) [8]. The one electron Kohn- Sham equation is solved by the 
use of a Green’s function technique. The Green’s fu nction was calculated for 16 
complex energy points distributed exponentially on a semicircular contour. The 
effective potential in the one electron equation is treated with optimized overlapping

## Page 5

muffin-tin approximation but the total energy is co rrected with the so-called full 
charge-density (FCD) method [9]. The total energies were calculated through 
combining the exact muffin-tin orbitals (EMTO) meth od [9,10] with the coherent 
potential approximation (CPA) [11,12]. The s, p, d and f orbitals are included in the 
EMTO basis sets. The electronic exchange-correlatio n potential is described with the 
generalized-gradient approximation (GGA) parametriz ed by Perdew et al [13]. The 
Brillouin zone is sampled employing 17×17×17 k-point meshes for all calculations.

3.3 Lattice misfit of γ/γ' phase 
The correlations of lattice constant of γ/γ' phase and composition of alloying 
elements are shown in Fig. 1-2. Except that Co nearly has no effect on lattice constant

of γ/γ' phase, all the other elements increase the lattic e constant. Nevertheless, Ir, Mo, 
Ti, Re and Ru increase much faster, because they ha ve larger atomic radius ( Table 4 ). 
Fig. 3 show the lattice misfit of γ and γ' phase due to alloying elements. As known to 
us, the lattice constant of Ni is smaller than Ni 3Al, and the lattice misfit is about +10 -2. 
Nevertheless, the lattice misfit is about -10 -3 in Ni-based single crystal superalloy at a 
room temperature. It is resulted from the lattice m isfit of γ/γ' phase caused by 
preferential partitioning to γ/γ' phase of alloying elements. Since the atomic radi us of 
Cr, V , Fe and Co are similar to which of Ni, they a re not candidates to have obvious 
effect on the lattice misfit. Because Mo, Ru and Re tend to partition strongly into γ 
phase, increasing their levels could result in increasingly negative lattice misfit. While 
Ti tends to partition to γ' phase, its addition has increased positive lattic e misfit. Ir 
nearly even partition into γ/γ' phase, so it nearly has no effect on lattice misfit. 
 
3.4 Elastic modulus of γ and γ' phase 
Calculated and experimentally elastic properties of γ/γ' phase are shown in Table 5 , 
including the bulk modulus (B), shear modulus (G), Young’s modulus (E) and 
Poisson’s ratio ( ν). The B, G, E of γ phase are greater than γ' phase, while the 
Poisson’s ratio of γ/γ' phase are approximately equal. Therefore, the all oying elements 
are required to improve the elastic properties, on the other hand must reduce the 
elastic difference of γ/γ' phase for fear of generating γ' rafting. Elastic modulus and 
Poisson’s ratio of γ and γ’ phase with alloying elements are shown in Fig. 4-5. Mo, Cr, 
Ti and V increase the E and G of γ phase, while decrease the γ' phase’s. These 
elements equalize the elastic difference between th e γ and γ' phase. Re could increase 
all modulus of γ/γ' phase, especially obviously increase the bulk mod ulus of γ phase 
and all modulus of γ' phase. Re is the important element to improve the elasticity of 
Ni-based single crystal superalloy, because it impr ove the elastic modulus of γ/γ' 
phase. Ru decrease the shear modulus and Yong’s mod ulus of γ' phase, because its 
effect is increase stability of micro phase and res train the precipitation of TCP phase, 
rather than improve the mechanical property. On the basis of empirical rule of Pugh 
[17], the tenacity or brittleness of materials is b ound up with Poisson’s ratio, and the

higher Poisson’s ratio is corresponding to the bett er tenacity. Re will increase the 
brittleness of Ni-based superalloy because of the decrease of the Poisson’s ratio of γ/γ' 
phase. In general, the increase of brittleness is u sually accompanied with 
improvement of elastic modulus. 
 
4. Conclusions 
In this paper, the site occupancy, lattice misfit a nd elastic modulus of Ni-based 
single crystal superalloys are investigated by the first-principle EMTO-CPA method. 
The main results and conclusions were summarized as follows: 
(1) The site preference of alloying elements in γ'-Ni 
3Al can be classified into four 
types, Ni site, Al anti-site, Al site and Ni anti-site. The γ' phase with alloying elements 
is more stable when Co, Fe, Ru and Ir occupy Ni sit e or Cr, Fe, Mo, Re, Ti, V , W 
occupy Al anti-site. Ti and V are the alloying elements which stabilize the γ'-Ni 3Al. 
(2) Because of biggish atomic radius, Ir, Mo, Ti, R e and Ru increase the lattice 
constant of γ/γ' phase much faster, and the alloying elements have some impacts on 
the lattice misfit of γ/γ' phase. 
(3) Mo, Cr, Ti and V equalize the elastic difference between the γ and γ' phase. Re is 
the most remarkable alloying element which improves the elastic modulus of 
Ni-based superalloys. However, it will increase the brittleness of Ni-based superalloy.
