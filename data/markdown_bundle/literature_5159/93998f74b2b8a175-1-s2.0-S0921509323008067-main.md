1. Introduction 
With entry temperature of advanced aero engines and industrial gas 
turbines (IGT) reaching over 1500 
◦
C, metal structural materials with 
higher temperature capacity are therefore required. Ni-based single 
crystal (Ni-SX) superalloys have become key materials for the blades 
used in aero engines and industrial gas turbines blades (IGT) [ 1 , 2 ]. 
Superior holistic high-temperate properties, long-term microstructure 
stability, corrosion and oxidation resistance become the pursuit for the 
latest generation Ni-SX superalloys [ 2 , 3 ]. As a development trend in 
alloy design, increasing the content of refractory elements (such as Ta, 
W, Re, and Ru) is being explored to improve high-temperature proper -
ties [ 4 , 5 ]. 
During service or thermal exposure at high temperature, the Ni-SX 
superalloys undergo varying degrees of microstructure degradation, 
which further deteriorates the mechanical properties [ 6 , 7 ]. The micro -
structure degradations are mainly attributed to γ ′ -phase coarsening and 
topologically close-packed (TCP) phase precipitation in Ni-SX 
superalloys. The former is the major strengthening phase while the latter 
is detrimental. γ ′ -phase coarsening has a significant impact on the 
deformation mechanism of superalloys and has been identified as the 
primary cause of creep performance degradation in CMSX-10 during 
thermal exposure [ 8 ]. Additionally, Sujata et al. found that the presence 
of a large quantity of TCP phase in the γ -matrix is responsible for crack 
initiation and premature blade fracture [ 9 ]. As for equiaxed and direc -
tionally solidified superalloys, the grains and carbides must also be 
considered [ 6 , 10 ]. 
The high-temperature mechanical properties of Ni-SX superalloys 
have been significantly improved by microstructure and chemical 
composition optimization. However, achieving further improvements 
through modulation of the γ ʹ -phase morphology or the diffusivity in 
γ -matrix has reached a bottleneck. Researchers from NIMS ( National 
Institute for Material Science, Japan ) focused on the γ / γ ′ coherent inter -
face, which is a crystal defect, with the goal of utilizing the nature of 
dislocation to limit its mobility. Dislocations require additional energy 
to pass through the coherent interface and shear the γ ′ -phase [ 11 , 12 ].

4. Discussion 
The superior high-temperature mechanical properties of Ni-SX su -
peralloys are provided by the γ / γ ′ two-phase microstructure, with their 
minimum creep rates typically being lower than those of single-phase 
alloys. The exceptional creep resistance can be attributed to three 
main sources: precipitation strengthening caused by high volume frac -
tion of L1
2 
ordered γ ′ -phase, solid solution strengthening resulting from 
the high content of refractory elements in the FCC disordered γ -matrix, 
the interface strengthening provided by the γ / γ ′ coherent interface. This 
section will explore the correlations between microstructure degrada -
tion and creep rupture properties in more detail, focusing on the 
γ ′ -phase, γ -matrix, and γ / γ ′ coherent interface. Specifically, we will focus 
on the 900 
◦
C/10,000h and 1000 
◦
C/2000h samples in order to elimi -
nate the interference from variations in γ ′ -phase size and volume frac -
tion. Finally, we will discuss Interface Segregation Manipulation to 
enhance interface strengthening. Through this engineering, we can 
further improve the mechanical properties of Ni-SX superalloys, making 
them even more suitable for high-temperature applications.

The results of TMS-75 and TMS-82 superalloys shows that the 
optimal γ ′ -phase volume fraction for maximizing creep life is between 60 
and 70% [ 30 ]. Reed concludes that γ ′ -phase volume fraction within 
60-70% confines the dislocation movement to a narrow matrix channel, 
maintaining the continuity of the γ -matrix. If the γ ′ -phase volume frac -
tion exceeds 70%, the continuity of the γ -matrix channel is disrupted 
and the creep properties are compromised [ 1 ]. Conversely, a lower 
volume fraction of the γ ′ -phase results in insufficient precipitation 
strengthening effect, leading to a reduction in creep properties.

size via coarsening process. A study by Nathal on the typical 1st gen -

The interfacial dislocation network is prominent microstructural 
feature in Ni-SX superalloys. Its density is primarily influenced by the 
misfit, with dislocations attaching to the γ / γ ′ interface to releasing the 
misfit stress [ 12 ]. Field et al. [ 35 ] suggest that the formation and 
maintenance of dislocation networks at the γ / γ ʹ interface is a dynamic 
process, which includes dislocation motion characterized by slipping, 
climbing, kinking, and unkinking. Interfacial dislocation networks have 
been observed in several early Ni-based superalloys. They are consid -
ered to be a consequence of plastic deformation and have subsequently 
been intentionally utilized to guide alloy design by NIMS. Zhang et al. 
found a correspondence between the interfacial dislocation network 
density and the steady-state strain rate [ 12 ]. Higher interfacial dislo -
cation network density corresponds to lower minimum creep rate and 
extended creep rupture life. In general, a smaller γ / γ ʹ interface misfit, 
namely a larger and negative value of misfit, results in a more dense and 
regular dislocation network, which leads to a significant improvement of 
creep properties.

Finally, manipulation and decoration of the interface at nanometer- 
scale through appropriate heat treatment or alloy composition design 
are feasible, and can further improve the microstructure and property 
stability of advanced metal structures. This is called “ Interface Segre -
gation Manipulation ” , which involves manipulation of solutes to the 
lattice defects (grain boundary or phase interface) for strengthening 
based on chemical Gibbs segregation. In this case, the “ Interface 
Segregation ” effect is restricted to the nanometer-scale interface or 
boundary, leading to the following outcomes: (1) The generation of local 
strain fields, such as misfit for dislocation networks. (2) The modifica -
tion of local chemical composition at the interface, which can stabilize 
the interface, improve the cohesion between phases, impede element 
diffusion, and reduce the coarsening kinetics of precipitates or grains 
[ 57 ]. “ Interface Segregation Manipulation ” is particularly effective for 
high-temperature alloys with simple coherent two-phase structure, it 
can significantly improve the stability of the thermodynamic micro -
structure and mechanical properties at high temperatures. Therefore, 
the “ Interface Segregation Manipulation ” can be used as an effective 
method to design and fabricate high strength metal materials as well as 
maintain stability of microstructure and mechanical properties.
