Abstract 
 Nano crystalline High Entropy Alloys (HEA) with e qui-atomic compositions were 
synthesized through mechanical alloying followed by Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS). Alloys with 
nominal compositions of TiAlNiCo, TiAlNiFe and TiAl NiCoFe were selected for the study. 
Microstructure, hardness and room temperature compressive strengths of the alloys were studied.

1. Introduction 
Conventional alloy systems contain one or two eleme nts as the main components and the 
secondary elements are added for getting the desire d properties. A new class of alloys was 
developed by Yeh et al. [1] in 2004 called High Ent ropy Alloys (HEA). Unlike conventional 
alloys, HEAs contain 5 or more elements in equi-ato mic or near equi-atomic ratios with element 
concentrations between 5 – 35 wt.% [1]. These alloy s are found to be stabilized as a single phase 
having simple crystal structures due to the increas e in the configurational entropy and are 
reported to be stable at high temperatures [2]–[10] . The configuration entropy of a n component 
alloy having equiatomic composition (= R ln n) increases with increasing number of components

which stabilizes single phase solid solution. Over the last decade a lot of research has been 
carried out in understanding the criteria governing the formation of simple solid solution with 
multiple components. Takeuchi et al. [11] showed th at some thermodynamic factors promote the 
formation of simple solid solution phases in multi component systems. They suggested that the 
enthalpy of mixing ( ∆Hmix ) in the range of 7-22 kJ/mol and an atomic size di fference factor ( δ)

several HEA compositions having good mechanical pro perties from ambient to high 
temperatures as well as cryogenic temperatures [13] –[17] HEAs can find several applications as 
a structural material and as coatings. 
Most of the HEAs reported in literature are process ed through casting route. Detailed analysis of 
these microstructures reveals typical dendritic mic rostructure with segregations in the inter-
dendritic regions [18]–[20]. Compared to casting, m echanical alloying produces more 
homogeneous microstructure and offers the advantage of generating nano-crystalline and 
metastable microstructures. Mechanical alloying can be used to alloy elements having different 
melting points and even those which are immiscible in the liquid state. Murty et al. have worked 
extensively on mechanical alloying followed by spar k plasma sintering and developed a new 
class of HEAs termed as nano-HEAs [5], [21], [22]. They have also shown that the 
nanocrystalline nature is extremely stable at high temperatures [5]. In their studies 
CuNiCoZnAlTi HEA were subjected to annealing at tem peratures of 1073 K and they found out 
that the BCC phase before annealing remains unalter ed after annealing. This indicates the phase 
stability of these alloys. High hardness and yield strength values were reported for the 
Al 
xCoCrCuFeNi HEAs processed through mechanical alloyi ng followed by Spark Plasma 
Sintering (SPS)[5]. Varalaksmi et al.[23] reported that AlFeTiCrZnCu HEA exhibits a high 
hardness value of 9.50 GPa and a good compressive s trength of 2.19 GPa before fracture. They 
also studied the wear resistance of these alloys an d reported that the wear resistance is higher 
than Ni hard faced alloys. Senkov et al.[24] studie d room temperature compressive properties of 
Ta 20 Nb 20 Hf 20 Zr 20 Ti 20 alloy and reported a value of 929±15 MPa as yield strength. Alloy shows 
50% deformation before failure.

There has been a lot of interest in HEA composition s having BCC structure [25], [26]. BCC 
HEAs have been observed to have higher yield streng th than FCC HEAs. BCC phases form 
when binary pairs constituting the alloys crystalli ze in BCC lattice. Studies [27]–[31] have 
shown that AlCoCrFeNiTi alloys produces BCC phase i rrespective of the processing route. 
Murty et al. [32] mentioned that the HEAs with cons iderable amount of Ni and Al tend to form 
NiAl phase since it has the highest ordering energy among the binary pairs considered. 
Varalakshmi et al. [33] studied the processing and properties of nanocrystalline CuNiCoZnAlTi 
HEAs. These alloys were processed through mechanica l alloying followed by vaccum hot 
pressing. The major phase observed in these alloy i s a BCC phase along with two minor FCC 
phases. Also these alloys develop a nanocrystalline microstructure after mechanical alloying with 
crystallite size less than 10 nm after 20 h of mill ing. These alloys were shown to have 
homogeneous composition throughout. 
In the present study, HEAs with nominal composition s of TiAlNiCo, TiAlNiFe and TiAlNiCoFe 
were processed through mechanical alloying followed by Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS). The 
composition were chosen since the Ti-Al, Fe-Al, Ni- Al, Ti-Ni systems form an intermetallic 
having equiatomic composition and B2 crystal struct ure. So the probability of forming a B2 or a 
disordered BCC structure is high with these compositions. 
 
2. Experimental details 
High purity powders of Ti (99.5% pure, -325 mesh si ze), Al (99.5% pure, -325 mesh size), Ni 
(99.5% pure, 200 mesh size), Co (99.5% pure, -325 mesh size), and Fe (99.5% pure, < 10 micron 
) procured from Alfa Aeser, India have been used in the present work to synthesize equiatomic 
TiAlNiCo, TiAlNiFe and TiAlNiCoFe alloy. These powd ers were ball milled in a high energy 
planetary ball mill (Fritsch Pulverisette P5) using tungsten carbide lined vials and 10 mm dia. 
tungsten carbide balls under a wet media of Toluene to minimize the oxidation of the powders. 
The ball to powder weight ratio was 10: 1 and milling speed was 300 RPM. Milling was done for 
8 hours and powders were extracted at the interval of two hours for XRD analysis. All the 
blended powders were consolidated using Spark Plasm a Sintering equipment (Model SPS-625, 
Fuji Electronic Co. Ltd., Japan). Samples were sint ered at a temperature of 1000 °C with a

cross section was ground and polished for XRD and S EM analysis. Phase analysis of the ball 
milled powders and SPS compacts was carried out usi ng an X-ray diffractometer (X’pert Pro 
PANalytical) using Cu k α radiation (1.5405 A°) with a step size of 0.02 o and time of 20 s/step. 
Crystallite size of the phases were calculated usin g single peak analysis program of Xpert High 
Score Plus software by Lorentzian distribution fitt ing. Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC) 
was carried out using a Simultaneous Thermal Analys er (Netzsch STA 409 DSC Germany) to 
study the phase stability and the melting point of the alloys. The samples were heated in alumina 
crucibles at a heating rate of 20 °C/min up to a te mperature of 1400 °C in argon atmosphere 
(flow rate 20 ml.min -1). Microstructure of the alloys and the milled powd ers were analyzed using 
Quanta 200 and Inspect F Scanning Electron Microsco pe equipped with Energy Dispersive 
Spectrometer (EDS). Transmission electron microscop e (FEI Tecnai T20) operating at 200 kV 
was used for microstructural characterization. Samp les for TEM investigation were prepared by 
ion milling. Micro hardness of the samples were mea sured using Vickers indenter (Wolpert 
Wilson) under a load of 1 kg with a dwell time of 1 0 s. Room temperature compression test were 
carried out using cylindrical samples (3 mm dia. an d 4 mm height) at a strain rate of 10 -3 s-1 with 
a Zwick Roell Z100 mechanical testing machine.

TiAlNiCo, TiAlNiFe and TiAlNiCoFe multicomponent al loys were successfully synthesized

patterns indicated the presence of a major BCC and minor BCC phase. SEM of the 
microstructure indicated a fine dispersion of the t wo BCC phases along with fine dispersion of
