ABSTRACT 
A novel ultra-high temperature Pt-15Ir-3Hf-0.5Y (wt.%) alloy was developed for the first time by high -
vacuum arc-melting, hot rolling and ageing treatment. The microstructures of the as-cast and hot-rolled and then 
post-aged samples were investigated. The alloy consists of the (Pt,Ir)
5
Y phase and matrix. Through transmission 
electron microscopy atomic resolution imaging, three types of nanoclusters, clusters containing stacking fault, 
clusters at 1/2 〈 0 1 1 〉 dislocation core and clusters with short-range order were observed in the matrix. The 
hardness at the ambient temperature of the alloy after hot rolling and ageing treatment was up to 443 HV and the 
tensile strength at 1500 
◦
C was up to 47.8 MPa. Compared with the solid solution strengthened Pt-Ir binary 
alloys, the addition of Hf and Y can effectively improve the mechanical properties, which were achieved by 
introducing the combined effects of nanocluster strengthening and grain boundary strengthening of (Pt,Ir)
5
Y 
phase. Our findings provided new insights into designing the Pt-based superalloy with high strength and 
ductility. 
1. Introduction 
With the development of the aerospace industry, high-temperature 
structural components such as rocket engine nozzles are required to 
withstand temperatures higher than 1500 
◦
C [1] . The Pt-based alloy has 
a high melting point (~1774 
◦
C), excellent thermal stability and 
oxidation resistance, which are promising candidates in such applica -
tions [2,3] . For instance, the Pt-Ir alloys can be used in high temperature 
and stress environments. Though the Pt-Rh alloys have higher oxidation 
resistance, the price of Rh is too expensive. The improvement of the 
high-temperature strength of Pt-Ir alloys largely depends on the solid 
strengthening of the γ phase matrix. The mechanical properties of Pt-Ir 
alloys can be enhanced with the increase of Ir content [4] . But higher Ir 
content will deteriorate the machinability and accelerate the volatili -
zation rate of Ir at high temperatures. When the Ir content is higher than 
25 %, the alloy will be difficult to deform plastically [5] . To further 
improve the high-temperature mechanical properties, we need a com -
bination of more strengthening effects such as precipitation 
strengthening. 
By drawing on the successful experience of Ni-based superalloys with 
γ’ / γ structure, Pt-based superalloys with L1
2
-ordered Pt
3
Al precipitates 
were developed [6,7] . Though the Pt
3

have excellent oxidation resistance, fatigue resistance and fracture 
toughness, they can be used only below 1350 
◦
C. This is because the 
Pt
3
Al melts at 1556 
◦
C and the eutectic reaction Pt-Pt
3
Al occurs at 
1507 
◦

chanical properties, higher melting-point elements need to be added to 
replace the Al. Hf can form the L1

3
Hf phase with Pt. The Pt
3
Hf phase 
has a higher melting point of 2250 
◦

ening phase of Pt-Ir alloys [10 – 13] . It should be noted that Hf can cause 
embrittlement due to the formation of brittle oxides when exposed to 
oxygen at high temperatures. The addition of Hf needs to be carefully 
selected to maintain oxidation resistance. 
In addition to strengthening the matrix, the grain boundaries also 
need to be enhanced. Because the grain boundary sliding proceeds easily 
during deformation at high temperatures. The grain boundaries of Ni- 
based superalloys are usually enhanced through minor additions of 
carbon and boron to form carbides and borides at grain boundaries 
[14 – 16] . Similarly, we can also introduce precipitates at the grain 
boundary of Pt-based alloys to hinder the high-temperature grain

2. Experimental procedures 
A Pt-15Ir-3Hf-0.5Y (wt.%) alloy was prepared from pure elements 
(Pt, Ir, Hf and Y with 99.99 % purity). The mixture with the desired 
composition was melted and alloyed in a high vacuum arc-melting 
furnace. The furnace was evacuated to 5 × 10
-3 
Pa during smelting 
process to minimize evaporation and oxidation losses. To enhance the 
homogeneity, the ingot was flipped over and re-melted 4 – 6 times. The

about 6.5 mm and a diameter of 20 mm was fabricated successfully. In

type cluster core 
( Fig. 11 b). Thus, both 1/4[0 0 1]
M 
type and 1/8[1 1 2]
M 
type clusters can 
be treated as clusters at 1/2 〈 0 1 1 〉 dislocation core. These clusters will 
hinder dislocation motion and improve alloy strength. 
For SF-containing clusters, the SF with Burger ’ s vector of 1/6 〈 1 1 2 〉
in the cluster can impede dislocation migration to enhance alloy 
strength [25] . Moreover, SF can promote nanoprecipitate formation, 
inducing a strong strengthening effect, similar to that in Ni-based su -
peralloys [33 – 35] . Pt and Ir are metals with high SF energy. Hf was 
found to decrease the intrinsic stacking fault energy (SFE) of Pt, but Ir 
increase it [36] . Then the Pt-Ir-Hf matrix still has a high SFE. Therefore, 
the SF is present in the Hf-rich cluster rather than the matrix. And the Hf 
atoms with a larger atomic radius than Pt and Ir in the clusters will 
further lock the SF. Then nanoprecipitates will tend to form based on the 
SF-containing clusters at high temperatures. In addition to the SF- 
containing clusters and clusters at 1/2 〈 0 1 1 〉 dislocation core, short- 
range ordered clusters also exist in great quantity in the alloy matrix. 
Short-range ordered clusters can also slow down the dislocation 
migrating, as reported in medium-entropy or multi-principle element 
alloys [27,28,37] . Consequently, the alloy matrix can achieve high 
strength. 
In addition, Hf and Y can also refine the grain size of Pt-15Ir alloy, 
resulting in grain refinement strengthening in the Pt-15Ir-3Hf and Pt- 
15Ir-0.5Y alloys [29] . Therefore, the strengthening contributions from 
cluster strengthening and grain boundary strengthening should be lower 
than 130 HV and 60 HV, respectively. Then the hardness of the hot- 
rolled Pt-15Ir-3Hf-0.5Y alloy is 386 HV, which is comparable to that 
of the hot-rolled Pt-30Ir alloy [38] . This is because hot rolling further 
refined the grains. Grain refinement strengthening should contribute 
about 54 HV to the hardness. 
After ageing treatment (700 
◦
C/10 h), the hardness of as-cast and 
hot-rolled samples are increased by 54 HV and 57 HV, respectively. It 
indicates that the alloy has a strong ageing strengthening effect. But the 
hot rolling did not appear to affect the ageing behavior. The hardness 
increase of as-cast and hot-rolled samples are almost equal. After ageing 
treatment, more and larger nanoclusters formed, which results in higher 
hardness. More and larger nanoclusters will also lead to a higher 
strengthening effect at high temperatures. 
Moreover, the high-temperature tensile strength and ductility can be 
enhanced simultaneously after hot rolling and ageing treatment. As 
shown in Fig. 12 . the fracture surfaces of the tensile samples tested at 
1500 
◦
C were observed. The hot-rolled sample exhibited the fracture 
features of a quasi-cleavage-like fracture. Both secondary micro-cracks 
and flat cleavage planes can be found on the fracture surfaces 
( Fig. 12 a and b). After ageing treatment, the sample showed a ductile 
fracture mode. No large-area cleavage planes can be found on the 
fracture surface ( Fig. 12 c and d). It is indicated that the fracture mode 
changed from brittle fracture to ductile fracture. After hot rolling, the 
Pt
5
Y phases were broken and microcracks formed ( Fig. 8 b). And the hot 
rolling can introduce internal stress in the sample. The microcracks and 
internal stress will reduce the ductility at high temperatures. Then the 
hot-rolled sample exhibits brittle fracture. After ageing treatment, 
microcracks formed during hot rolling can be filled and the internal 
stress can be relieved. The refined Pt
5
Y phase will significantly enhance 
the grain boundaries. And more and larger nanoclusters will further 
strengthen the matrix. Consequently, the strength and ductility of the 
hot-rolled sample were enhanced apparently after ageing treatment. 
Therefore, based on the solid solution strengthening Pt-15Ir alloy, we 
introduced nanocluster strengthening and grain boundary strengthening 
by the addition of Hf and Y, respectively. And then after hot rolling and 
ageing treatment, high hardness and good ductility were achieved by a 
combination of solid solution strengthening, nanocluster strengthening, 
grain boundary strengthening and grain refinement strengthening. With 
a comparable high-temperature property, the Pt-15Ir-3Hf-0.5Y alloy can 
be used for rocket engine nozzle in place of Pt-25Rh alloy to reduce the 
Rh use and lower the material cost. 
4. Conclusion 
In this work, we explored the hot rolling and heat treatment to 
develop an ultra-high temperature Pt-15Ir-3Hf-0.5Y alloy. Through hot 
rolling and ageing, the strength and ductility at 1500 
◦
C were improved 
significantly. The major conclusions were made as follows. 
(1) The Pt-15Ir-3Hf-0.5Y alloy with excellent high-temperature me -
chanical properties was achieved through hot rolling and ageing 
treatment. The tensile strength ( σ
b
) and elongation ( δ ) increased 
from 24.9 MPa and 3.18 % to 47.8 MPa and 9.23 % after ageing 
treatment, respectively. 
(2) The hot-rolled and ageing treated alloy contains orthorhombic 
Pt
5
Y particles and FCC matrix, and a high density of nanoclusters 
in the matrix. These clusters can be classified into three types, 
clusters containing SF, clusters at 1/2 〈 0 1 1 〉 dislocation and 
clusters with short-range order. After hot-rolling and ageing 
treatment, Pt
5
Y particles were refined and the nanoclusters 
increased to 7 nm, which resulted in the simultaneous improve -
ment of strength and ductility. 
(3) This investigation provides a feasible approach to developing 
ultra-high temperature Pt-based alloy. Based on the solid solution 
strengthening Pt-15Ir alloy, combining Hf and Y addition, hot 
deformation and ageing treatment to enhance grain boundaries 
by secondary phase and strengthen the matrix by solid solution 
strengthening and nanoclusters hardening, high hardness and 
excellent high-temperature strength achieved in Pt-based alloys.
