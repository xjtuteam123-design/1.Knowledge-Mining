Abstract 
The CoCrFeNiMn high entropy alloy (HEA) bulk alloy was successfully 
manufactured using laser aided additive manufacturing (LAAM). The microstructure 
and mechanical behaviors of the as -fabricated HEAs were investigated. The as -built 
HEAs exhibit directional solidification at regions close to the melt-pool boundaries, 
forming dendritic columnar grains and transiting to equiaxed grains further away from 
the boundaries . Compared with the conventionally cast HEAs, the CoCrFeNiMn 
fabricated using LAAM possesses significantly higher yield strength (518 MPa) and 
ultimate tensile strength (660 MPa). The strength ening effect is attributed to finer 
grains and could be explained quantitatively through grain boundary strengthening. 
The as-built HEA shows a simultaneous enhancement in yield strength and ductility 
with decreasing testing temperature. The improved low temperature tensile properties 
could be ascribed to the formation of deformation twins at low temperature, which 
results in a steady strain hardening behavior. This work demonstrat es the potential of 
using LAAM technology to extend the application of HEAs with fabrication of larger 
and more complex parts with good mechanical properties.

1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs), also termed as multicomponent alloys , have gained 
increasing attention, especially amongst materials researchers [1-8]. The upward trend 
of the HEAs is mainly associated with their special alloy design concept and attractive 
mechanical behaviors. Unlike conventional alloys, which consist of one major 
element with additional elements to tailor the microstructure and properties, the HEAs 
usually consist of at least four elements with equal or near equal atomic ratio [2]. The 
HEA design concept provides a vast compositional space to develop novel alloys. The 
HEAs normally ha ve four core effects, including high entropy, sluggish diffusion, 
severe lattice distortion and cocktail effects [3]. With these four core effects, the 
HEAs can exhibit outstanding properties, such as high phase stability and excellent 
mechanical property. As the first HEA reported by Cantor [1], the CoCrFeNiMn HEA 
(or Cantor alloy) is one of the most extensively inves tigated materials in the HEA 
family [ 9-15]. The CoCrFeNiMn HEA shows a high phase stability of a single 
face-centered-cubic (FCC) structure [11]. Besides, it exhibits outstanding combination 
of high strength and ductility at low temperatures, as well as high fracture toughness 
at cryogenic temperature which can be even higher than some cryogenic steels [15]. 
These attractive properties make the CoCrFeNiMn HEA a suitable low tempera ture 
material. The development of materials processing and fabrication methodologies are 
key drivers for more practical applications. Currently, the fabrication me thods of 
HEAs mainly rely on arc -melting and casting [16], though different fabrication 
methods have been reported, such as the spark plasma sintering [17] and mechanical 
alloying [18], which have the disadvantages in terms of cost and efficiency, especially 
when the complex geometry is required. To bridge this gap, more research efforts are 
necessary to drive the application of HEAs through development of novel

manufacturing technologies. 
The additive manufacturing (AM), which is based on the philosophy of manufacturing 
through layer-by-layer method, has gained broad attention arising from its advantages, 
such as the design freedom and time efficiency [18,19]. Up till now, the widely 
documented AM technologies for metals are powder-bed based selective laser melting 
(SLM) and electron beam melting (EBM) , in which the metal powder s are spread in 
thin layers [ 18]. Besides the powder -bed AM technology, the laser aided additive 
manufacturing (LAAM), or also named as laser -based directed energy deposition 
(DED) and laser engineered net shaping (LENS), shows promising capabilities for 3D 
AM applications. The LAAM technology uses blown powder delivery method 
whereby the powder jets are fed through a coaxial nozzle into a laser melt -pool. In 
addition, wire feeding can also be used as additive materials for LAAM process. 
Compared to the powder -bed process, LAAM has flexibility in terms of build size , 
wider material selection and higher deposition rate. With its advantages, the LAAM 
has demonstrated the ability to process variety of materials, including Inconel alloys 
[20-23], stainless steel [ 24,25] and titanium alloy [ 26,27]. Therefore, LAAM is well 
suited for efficient materials development and characterization, requiring less starting 
powder for experimental studies (without having to fill up the powder bed), faster 
build r ate and can be used to print 3D structure with moderate geometric 
complexities. 
With the abovementioned strengths of the AM technologies, there are some reported 
investigations on the AM of HEAs, including the powder -based SLM [28-32] and 
EBM [33] technologies. In addition, the direct metal deposition has also been used to 
fabricate the MoNbTaW [ 34] HEA. However, as a typical HEA with outstanding 
mechanical properties and potential low temperature applications, the investigation of 
fabricating CoCrF eNiMn with LAAM has not or rarely been reported and 
investigated rigorously. Thus, it is necessary to study the feasibility of fabricating 
CoCrFeNiMn HEA using LAAM, with in-depth investigation of its microstructure 
and mechanical behaviors.

2. Experimental 
The CoCrFeNiMn powders used for LAAM were produced by gas atomization in the 
Ar atmosphere. The size of the powder s was measured using A HORIBA LA -960 
laser particle size analyzer. The samples were fabricated using the LAAM system 
developed in Singapore institute of manufacturing technology (SIMTech) as shown in 
Fig. 1. The laser system used for the experiments is the 6 kW IPG Ytterbium laser 
System 6000. The additive materials were delivered using discrete coaxial blown 
powder jets. Argon carrier gas at flow rate of 4 𝑙/min at the pressure of 0.2 MPa was 
used for the coaxial powder delivery. In addition, argon shielding gas at flow rate of 
20 l/min and pressure of 0.1 MPa was used at the base of the nozzle to protect the 
laser optics and to minimize melt -pool oxidation. Based on a series of printing 
parameter optimizations, the utilized laser process parameters were laser power of 880 
W, with a beam size of 2.5 mm, lase r scanning velocity of 10 mm/s, and powder 
feeding rate of 8.6 g/min. 
Rectangular blocks with a dimension of 108 mm (length) × 70mm (width) × 6.6mm 
(height) were deposited on a mild steel substrate using the raster scan strategy. The 
building geometry and coordinate systems for the samples are shown in the Fig. 2a. 
The raster scan pattern will alternate between laser scanning in the width -wise (x-axis) 
and length -wise (y -axis) directions. The hatch distance is 1 .2 mm and the layer 
thickness for the AM HEA sample is approximately 0.6 mm. The scanning direction 
rotates 90 between the adjacent layers. After the deposition process, each rectangular 
block will be wire-cut and machined into 6 tensile coupons in accordance with ASTM 
standard E8 as shown in Fig. 2b. The gauge dimension of the tensile coupons is 32 
mm × 6 mm × 4 mm. A total of 2 such rectangular blocks were built for fabricating 
the tensile coupons. The tensile property was performed at a strain rate of 10-3/s using 
a universal Instron -5982 testing system. The tensile test temperatures are 25C, 0C, 
and -130C, respectively. 
The as -deposited HEAs were processed by cutting, grinding and polis hing. The

3. Results and discussion 
3.1 Microstructure analysis of the as built HEA 
Figure 3 illustrates the morphology, particle size distribution and cross section of the 
powders. Fig. 3(a) reveals that the powders show spherical morphology with some 
satellite structures adhering to the surface of the powder s. The particle size 
distribution in Fig. 3(b) indicates that the size of the powder s ranges from about 30 
m to 100 m with the mean powder size of about 70 m. The powder particle 
cross-section in Fig. 1(c) shows some internal pores within the powder particle and a 
typical example is given in the inset. The existence of pores inside some of the 
powders may arise from the entrapped gas during gas atomization [35] and could have 
detrimental effects on the quality of the printing samples [36]. 
Figure 4 shows the microstructure of as-built HEA using OM. Fig. 4(a) shows the OM 
images in the YZ plane while the OM image at a higher magnification is presented in 
Fig. 4(b) . The OM images reveal some pores in the samples , as indicated by the 
arrows. The melt -pool profiles produced by laser deposition in the direction 
perpendicular to the YZ plane can be clearly observed and they are indicated by the 
blue broken lines. Besides, the laser movement direction was also labeled using black 
broken lines. Within the mentioned melt-pools, two types of microstructure s are 
visible. The as-built HEA exhibits equiaxed grains and dendritic columnar grains. The

higher magnification of equiaxed grains and dendrite columnar grains are shown in 
Figs. 4( c) and ( d). SEM images showing clearer details on the equiaxed grains and 
dendritic columnar grains are given in Fig. 5 for the as-built sample in the YZ plane. 
The average diameter of the equiaxed grains is about 3.68  0.85 m and the width of 
the dendrites is about 3.11  0.60 m. The formation of equiaxed grains or dendrite 
columnar grains is dependent on temperature gradient ( G) and solidification rate ( R). 
This phenomenon was also elaborated in the authors’ previous work [37]. For instance, 
consider the semi-elliptically shaped melt -pool boundary formed by laser moving in 
the direction perpendicular to YZ plane (as indicated by blue broken lines in Fig. 4(b)), 
the regions near the melt -pool boundaries were formed when the melt -pools were 
close to the maximum size when viewed in the reference stationary YZ plane. At this 
instance, the G is high while R is low, resulting in formation of dendrite columnar 
grains. As the advancing laser continued to move further away from the reference YZ 
plane, the melt -pool retreated and the solidification front moved upwards. The 
temperature field transited to low G and high R, resulting in formation of equiaxed or 
cellular grains. The formation of cellular structures has also been reported for the 
CoCrFeNiMn HEA fabricated using SLM [29], and the cell size is about several 
hundreds of nanometers. The much larger cell size in the sample with LAAM is most 
likely due to the relatively lower cooling rate during LAAM process. 
Figure 6 illustrates the EBSD images of the as -built HEA in the YZ plane. Fig. 6(a) 
shows the inverse pole figure ( IPF) map of the as built sample, and there is no 
dominant texture. Fig. 6(b) presents the IPF map of the as built sample in the 
rectangular area in Fig. 6(a) at a higher magnification and the corresponding Kernel 
average misorientation (KAM) map is presented in Fig. 6(c). The KAM map shows 
the local misorientation across grains and could reflect the dislocation density [38, 39]. 
It is obvious that the KAM value of all the grains is between 0.2-0.7. Furthermore, 
the as-built HEA shows a high percentage of LAGBs, which is about 21.6% from the 
grain boundary analysis. Fig. 6 (d)-(i) show the corresponding band contrast map and 
EDS mapping of the rectangular area . The grain boundaries are rich in Ni and Mn, 
with lower contents of Co, Cr, and Fe. For instance, this is evident in Fig. 6(g) where

the regions rich in Fe have lower content of Mn in Fig. 6(i). The chemical segregation

3.2 Mechanical properties of the as built HEA 
The tensile properties of the as -built HEA samples at different temperatures (25C, 
0C, and -130C) were plotted in the Fig. 7. Fig. 7(a) shows the representative 
engineering stress vs. engineering strain curves while the corresponding true stress vs. 
true strain curves are shown in the Fig. 7(b). The yield strength (y), ultimate tensile 
strength (UTS), and tensil e elongation ( UTS) are marked in the Fig. 7(a). The tensile 
properties of CoCrFeNiMn HEA fabricated by LAAM, SLM and conventional casting 
methods are summarized in Table 1. The y of the as-built HEA at room temperature 
(25C) is about 518 MPa, which is approximately 2.5 times higher than the 
conventional casted parts (about 209 MPa) [41]. The y of the LAAM fabricated HEA 
is very close to the reported values for tested HEA coupons fabricated using SLM 
process. However, the UTS (660MPa) is higher than that fabricated using SLM and 
casting method [29, 41]. The true UTS of the as-built HEA at 25C is about 789 MPa, 
which is comparable to that of the as -cast samples [4 2]. In the meantime, the UTS of 
the as built HEA is about 19.8%, which is significantly lower than that fabricated by 
casting method (about 60%). The tensile property at room temperature indicates that 
the CoCrFeNiMn HEA fabricated using LAAM shows a significant strengthening 
effect with a reduction of ductility. 
The strengthening effect is a common phenomenon for the alloys fabricated using 
LAAM [43] or SLM [44-46]. While the origin of strengthening effect requires more

extensive investigation , it is likely attributed to the cellular structure [45], the 
dislocation hardening [29,47,48], and nanoprecipitates [ 49]. It had been reported that 
the y of the stainless steel 316L fabricated by SLM could be estimated from the size 
of the cellular structure based on the Hall -Petch relationship [48, 50]. The Hall-Petch 
relationship of the CoCrFeNiMn HEA has been established previously and given as 
follows [51]: 
y = 194 + 490/d-1/2 (1) 
Where d is the average grain size. For the present as-fabricated HEA, the average size 
of the equiaxed grains is about 3.68  0.85 m while it is about 3.11  0.85 m for the 
dendrite columnar grains. The calculated y based on the Hall -Petch relationship is 
about 449 MPa and 472 MPa, which occupy a substantial part of the measured y of 
518 MPa. This suggests that the enhanced strength is largely contributed by the finer 
equiaxed grains and dendritic columnar grains. Besides, the initial dislocation in the 
sample could also contribute to the enhanced strength [47]. 
When the testing temperature decreases, the as built HEA shows a simultaneous 
enhancement of y, UTS, and UTS. This trend is similar to the previous reports for 
casted HEA [52]. For the LAAM as-fabricated HEAs tested at -130C, the y could 
reach up to 710 MPa and the tensile elongation is about 40%. The true UTS of the 
as-built HEA at 25 C is about 789 MPa, which is comparable to that of the cast 
samples. Fig. 7(c) presents the work hardening behaviors of the as -built HEA at 
different temperatures. Three typical stages could be observed. The first stage shows a 
rapid decrease in work hardening rate till about strain of 3% for the samples deformed 
at all temperatures, which is a typical trend for the met als [53]. In the second stage, 
the work hardening rate of the samples deformed at 25 C and 0 C also show a 
monotonic decrease, at a relatively lower gradient ( of the strain hardening curve) for 
samples tested at 0C. For the samples deformed at -130C, the strain hardening rate 
maintains relatively constant at -130C, displaying a steady strain hardening in this 
stage. In the final stage, the work hardening rate decreases until fracture for the 
samples deformed at all temperatures . The detailed mechanisms of different work 
hardening behaviors will be discussed from the microstructure evolution perspective

To understand the deformation mechanisms of the as -built HEAs, Fig. 8 shows the 
representative fracture surface. Fig. 8(a) indicates slight macrosc opic necking, while

formation of Mn -rich particles c an be associated with oxide formation during the

3.3 Structural evolution during tensile deformation 
To further evaluate the different deformation behaviors at different testing 
temperatures, Fig. 9 show s the microstructure evolution using EBSD after tensile 
fracture at 25  and -130, respectively. All the EBSD images were taken in the YZ 
plane, which is along the tensile axis (TA). Figs. 9 (a) and (c) show the EBSD IPF 
images after fracture at 25 C and -130C. It is observed that the grains are dominated 
by red and blue colors, indicating that the texture components are characterized by 
<100>//TA and <111> //TA. Similar textures have been reported for the twinning 
induced Plasticity (TWIP) steel [55]. Figs. 9 (b) and (d) show the K ernel average 
misorientation (KAM) map the corresponding rectangular areas in Figs. 9 (a) and (c), 
respectively. The KAM distribution deformed at 25 C, 0C, and -130C is shown in 
Fig. 9(g). The results show increasing KAM val ue with increasing tensile elongation. 
The increasing KAM values reflect increase in dislocation density. This suggests that 
the dislocation motion contributes to part deformation behavior. For the HEA 
fractured at -130C, the KAM value could reach as high as 3, indicating significant 
dislocation trapping. Besides, c ompared with the sample deformed at 25 C, the 
sample deformed at -130C shows multiple deformation induced twins while there are 
quite few deformation twins for the sample deformed at 25 C. An example is shown 
in the Figs. 9 (e) (EBSD IPF map) and (f) (EBSD grain boundary map). In Fig. 9(f),

the blue lines and red lines represent the HAGBs and deformation twins, respectively. 
The result indicates that dislocation motion contributes to the d eformation at 25 C 
and -130C. However, there is an ad ditional deformation mechanism; deformation 
twinning at -130C, which made the HEA behave in a similar manner as the twinning 
induced plasticity (TWIP) steel [56], leading to a simultaneous enhancement of 
strength and ductility at low temperature. The formation of the deformation twins of 
the HEA at -130C could explain the steady strain hardening behavior which could 
enhance the tensile elongation till fracture. 
 
Conclusion 
In summary, LAAM process was successfully applied to fabricate bulk CoCrFeNiMn 
HEA part with reasonably good mechanical strength . The as -built HEA exhibits 
features of overlapping melt-pools, with the fine equiaxed grains and dendritic 
columnar grains approximately 3 -4 𝜇𝑚 in size . The as -built HEAs exhibit an 
enhanced y with a reduction in ductility compared to the casted parts . The 
strengthening effect is largely contributed by the finer grains through grain boundary 
strengthening. The as -fabricated HEA shows a simultaneous enhancement of yield 
strength and ductility when the tensile testing temperature decreases. The improved 
low temperature tensile properties could be ascribed to the formation of deformation 
twins. This work has demonstrated that LAAM can be employed to fabricat e HEA 
bulk material, with potential of using this technology to build larger and more 
complex parts. For future work, the focus will be to reduce porosity level to improve 
ductility as well as mechanical strength and to obtain better understand ing on the 
mechanism of strengthening effect in the CoCrFeNiMn HEA.
