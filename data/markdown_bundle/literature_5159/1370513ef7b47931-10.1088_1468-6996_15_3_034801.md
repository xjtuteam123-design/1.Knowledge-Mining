Abstract
The high-spin states of 140Pm have been investigated through the reaction
126Te(19F, 5n) at a beam energy of 90 MeV . A previous level scheme based
on the 8 − isomer has been updated with spin up to 23 ¯ h. A total of 22
new levels and 41 new transitions were identiﬁed. Six collective bands were
observed. Five of them were expanded or re-constructed, and one of them was
newly identiﬁed. The systematic signature splitting and inversion of the yrast
πh11/2 ⊗νh11/2 band in Pr and Pm odd–odd isotopes has been discussed. Based
on the systematic comparison, two /Delta1I= 2 bands were proposed as double-
decoupled bands; other two bands with strong /Delta1I= 1 M1 transitions inside
the bands were suggested as oblate bands with γ ∼− 60◦; another band with
large signature splitting has been proposed with oblate-triaxial deformation
with γ ∼− 90◦. The characteristics for these bands have been discussed.
1. Introduction
The light rare earth nucleus 140Pm with N = 79 and Z = 61 is located at a transitional
region and closed to the doubly closed-shell nucleus 146Gd. In this region, at low spin, the
nuclei show a small quadrupole deformation and γ -soft triaxial deformation. When the spin
becomes higher, the collective character will be predominant. According to the mechanism of
γ -deformation driving of rotating quasiparticle orbits inγ -soft nuclei [1], the alignment of the
proton of the lower h
11/2 orbits will drive the nucleus toward a prolate shape (γ ∼ 0◦), while
the alignment of the neutron of the upperh11/2 orbits tends to drive the nucleus toward an oblate
shape (γ ∼− 60◦) in the Lund convention [2]. The competitions between the single particle
and collective characters and between the different shape driving effects by the neutron and
the proton alignments have made the nuclear structural research in this region very interesting,

2. Experimental methods
High-spin states of 140Pm have been populated via the 126Te(19F, 5n) fusion–evaporation
reaction at a bombarding energy of 90 MeV . The 19F beam current of 7.0 nA was obtained
by the HI-13 tandem accelerator at the China Institute of Atomic Energy (CIAE). The target
of 2.85 mg cm −2 thick enriched 126Te was prepared by evaporating tellurium metal powder
on a 21.75 mg cm −2 thick gold backing where the thick backing was used to stop the recoil
nuclei quickly for minimizing the doppler shift. The in-beamγ -rays have been detected by 12
Compton-suppressed high-purity Ge (HPGe) detectors and 1 clover detector which was used
as four Ge detectors in this experiment. The detailed information of the detector array is shown
in table 1, including the detector numbers, the angles, the distances, the relative efﬁciencies
and the energy resolutions. Here the angles are with respect to the beam direction and the
distances are from the detectors to the center of target. The energy resolutions of the detectors
11 and 12 were measured at 121.8 keV γ -ray energy and the other ones were obtained at
1332.5 keV γ -ray energy. Total absolute detector efﬁciency of this array is around 1% for
1332.5 keV γ -ray energy. The relative efﬁciencies were calibrated using
152Eu and 133Ba
sources. The data acquisition is the KODAQ system based on CAMAC modules. In our
experiment, a total of 1.9×10
8 γ –γ coincidence events were collected where the coincidence
resolving time was set as 50 ns and sorted out into aγ –γ coincidence matrix. The multipolarity
of theγ -transitions is determined by the average directional correlation of oriented state (DCO)
intensity ratios. To obtain the DCO ratios, an asymmetry two-dimensional angular-correlation
matrix was produced with the events collected by the detectors at around 90 ◦ along one axis
and the events collected by other detectors along the other axis as discussed in [ 5]. The γ –γ
coincidence data were analyzed with the RADW ARE software package [29].

5. Summary
In this work, the high-spin states of the
140Pm nucleus have been re-investigated. The previous
level scheme has been updated and many new levels and transitions have been identiﬁed. Six
collective band structures are observed. The TRS calculation indicates that the yrast band
of
140Pm has β2 ∼ 0.13 and γ ∼− 32◦ deformation at low spins. Signature splitting and
inversion in the yrast πh11/2 ⊗ νh11/2 band in 140Pm have been discussed and systematically
compared with the neighboring nuclei. Two bands based on the 9 − state at the low spins
and the 14− state at the high spins are suggested as the decoupled bands probably with two-
and four-quasiparticle conﬁgurations respectively. Other three collective bands based on high
excited levels are proposed as the four-quasiparticle conﬁgurations. Two of them are proposed
as oblate deformation with γ ∼− 60
◦, and one of them is proposed with oblate-triaxial
deformation with γ ∼− 90◦. The other characteristics for the observed bands have been
discussed.
