Fe-9Cr, Fe-9Cr-1Mo and Fe-9Cr-0.5Mo-0.5Ni (wt%) alloys have been prepared for studying the effect of (Mo, 
Mo/Ni) on carbide precipitation, oxidation behavior and high-temperature tensile property of Fe-9Cr alloy. Rod-

1. Introduction 
Due to the high viscosity of heavy oil, in-situ combustion (ISC) has 
begun to be widely used in oil fields in recent years. In the ISC process, 
air is typically injected through a central vertical tubing (injection well) 
surrounded by many production wells [ 1 ]. Combustion is initiated at the 
injection well and heats the heavy oil, which results in the low-viscosity 
heavy oil flowing toward the producer. During the service life of the 
tubing, the working temperature is below 600 ℃ for a long time [ 2 , 3 ]. At 
present, the common tubing is still used for injection and production 
wells. Although the common tubing usually exhibits good electro -
chemical corrosion resistance in oil fields, it generally does not have 
excellent high-temperature oxidation resistance, leading to severe 
oxidation. It is well known that the oil field ’ s normal production de -
pends on the reliability, service life and cost of the tubing. To meet the 
needs of injection and production wells in the ISC environment, special 
tubing materials should be developed. 
During air exposure in the temperature range 550 650 ℃ , 
martensitic 9 wt% Cr steels, such as P91 or P92, can form the protective 
oxide scale over time periods of several thousands of hours [ 4 ]. Clearly, 
Fe-9Cr alloy becomes an excellent candidate for the tubing materials in 
the ISC environment. Although the various properties of Fe-9Cr can 
basically meet the needs of ISC, better oxidation resistance will signifi -
cantly extend its service life and greatly reduce the cost of oil produc -
tion. Therefore, it is hoped that the oxidation resistance of Fe-9Cr alloy 
can be improved significantly by small additions of alloying elements. 
According to the above analysis, the tubing should have excellent 
oxidation resistance, good electrochemical corrosion resistance and 
sufficient high-temperature strength in the ISC environment. The se -
lection of alloying elements should consider these properties. As well 
known, Mo as an alloying element can improve electrochemical corro -
sion of the tubing in oil fields [ 5 – 7 ] and enhance high-temperature 
tensile properties, such as high-temperature strength, creep strength 
and so on [ 8 , 9 ]. Numerous researchers have suggested that Mo addition 
will damage the protective scale due to the formation of liquid and/or 
volatile MoO
3 
[ 10 – 14 ]. However, other papers have reported that Mo is 
beneficial to the oxidation resistance of Mo-containing alloys [ 15 , 16 ]. It 
is therefore concluded that the effect of Mo on oxidation behavior de -
pends on the material (microstructure and alloy composition) and the 
oxidation conditions (gas composition, temperature and so on). The 
production of liquid and volatile MoO
3 
will be significantly inhibited at 
temperatures below 600 
◦

impact on the oxidation resistance of the tubing in the ISC environment. 
In addition, alloying element Ni is also commonly used to improve the 
electrochemical corrosion resistance and mechanical properties of the 
tubing in oil fields [ 18 , 19 ]. Although Ni element has little effect on the 
oxidation resistance in air [ 19 – 22 ], the NiMoO
4 
formation has a bene -
ficial impact on Mo-containing alloys [ 15 ]. Therefore, in the present 
study, the overall effects of alloying elements (Mo, Mo/Ni) on the

2. Materials and methods 
Alloys were provided by Baoshan Iron & Steel Co., Ltd., and the 
compositions were listed in Table 1 . Alloys were melted by a vacuum 
furnace, casted into ingots and hot-rolled into 10 mm thick plates. The 
heat-treatment methods of these plates were 900 ℃ oil quenched and 
650 ℃ tempered. The samples (15 × 10 × 2 mm) were ground to a 1200 
grit and then ultrasonically cleaned in ethanol. After drying in hot air, 
samples were weighed by an analytic balance (precision 0.01 mg) and 
then stored in a vacuum-desiccator for use. 
The isothermal oxidation test was performed in a muffle furnace at 
550 ℃ for 2650 h. Samples were placed in Al
2
O
3 
crucibles which were 
heated along with the furnace. After that, the crucibles were taken out of 
the furnace at different time intervals, cooled to room temperature in air 
and weighted by an analytic balance. At least three samples confirmed 
the data reproducibility. High-temperature tensile properties of round 
specimens with a gauge diameter of 8 mm and length of 50 mm were 
investigated at 550 ℃ . Each value of the tensile property was the average 
of two samples. 
Microstructural evaluations of tested samples were investigated by 
optical microscope (OM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM) equipped with energy- 
dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX). The morphologies of the oxide 
scales were examined by OM and SEM, and then the reaction product 
was identified by X-ray diffraction (XRD) with Cu Ka radiation.

4.1. Role of alloying elements (Mo, Mo/Ni) on the microstructure and 
tensile properties of 9Cr

and shape of precipitates, as shown in Fig. 1 . The precise role of alloying

the NiMoO
4 
phase at the alloy-scale interface. 
As well known, chromia scale growth depends on outward diffusion 
of cations, inward diffusion of oxygen and grain boundary diffusion 
[ 39 ]. In this case, although Mo addition had no influence on grain 
boundary diffusion, it decreased the diffusion of cations and oxygen, 
thereby reducing the growth rate of an inner Cr-rich layer. In the case of 
co-doping of Mo and Ni, the addition of Ni in the ferritic matrix would 
reduce the diffusion coefficient of Cr which further slowed down the 
chromia forming kinetics. The observation of relatively thinner Cr-rich 
layers in both Mo and MoNi doped 9Cr alloys ( Fig. 6 ) was in line with 
the above analysis. The rapid growth of an inner Cr-rich layer in 9Cr 
resulted in the high consumption of Cr at the subsurface region of the 
alloy, which could not be replenished from the matrix according to the 
above analysis. However, the relatively slow growth of the Cr-rich layers 
in both 9Cr-Mo and 9Cr-MoNi decreased the rate of Cr consumption and 
therefore maintained the Cr-rich layers for a longer time. As a result, the 
oxidation resistance of 9Cr was improved by alloy elements (Mo, Mo/Ni) 
during the long-term oxidation process. 
Based on the above experimental results, small additions of alloying 
elements (1.0 %Mo, 0.5 %Mo-0.5 %/Ni) remarkably improved the 
oxidation resistance and high-temperature strength of 9Cr in the simu -
lated ISC environment at 550 ℃ . However, the simulated ISC environ -
ment is not very accurate and further research is still needed. 
5. Conclusions 
In the present study, adding common alloying elements (Mo, Mo/Ni) 
to 9Cr obtained the tubing with excellent comprehensive performance, 
especially the oxidation resistance. The following conclusions have been 
drawn: 
(1) The rod-shaped Cr
23
C
6 
precipitates in 9Cr were refined and 
spheroidized by Mo addition, while the size and shape of Cr
23
C
6 
precipitates were restored by Mo/Ni addition. Meanwhile, high- 
temperature strength of 9Cr was enhanced by alloying elements 
(Mo, Mo/Ni). 
(2) Adding (Mo, Mo/Ni) to 9Cr reduced inward oxygen diffusion and 
the growth rate of an inner Cr-rich layer, thereby accelerating 
chromia formation and improving the oxidation resistance. 
However, the cracks in the scale of 9Cr-Mo were induced by the 
MoO
3 
phase with the large PBR. 
(3) The formation of spinel-type NiMoO
4 
at the alloy-scale interface 
could effectively block the diffusion of Fe, thereby reducing the 
thickness of an outer iron-rich layer. 
(4) The partial replacement of Mo by Ni had little effect on the 
oxidation resistance, but a massive impact on the oxidation 
mechanism.
