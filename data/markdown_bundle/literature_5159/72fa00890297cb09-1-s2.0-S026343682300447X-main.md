ABSTRACT 
In this study, we prepared Ti
x
ZrNbMoV (x = 1, 1.5, and 2) refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) with varying 
amounts of Ti by vacuum arc melting. The microstructures of RHEAs are investigated by neutron diffraction, 
electron backscatter diffraction and transmission electron microscope. The results showed that the Ti
x
ZrNbMoV 
alloys mainly comprise body centered cubic phases and hexagonal close packed phases and the average grain size 
of alloys decreases as the Ti element content increases. Room-temperature compressive tests showed that 
increasing the Ti content of the alloys improved their ductility, although their strength decreased. Compared 
with some previously reported RHEAs, the Ti
x
ZrNbMoV RHEAs prepared in this study possessed better 
comprehensive mechanical properties, with the specific yield strength of the TiZrNbMoV alloy being as high as 
312 kPa ⋅ m
3
/kg. Finally, the deformation behavior of Ti
x
ZrNbMoV RHEAs was studied by nanoindentation. 
1. Introduction 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), a type of multi-component 
alloy composed mainly of refractory metal elements, were first reported 
by Senkov [ 1 ] in 2010. Their use is expected to solve problems associ -
ated with traditional alloys in industries such as the aerospace and nu -
clear industries. Compared with conventional single-principal element 
alloys, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) exhibit high strength [ 2 , 3 ], high- 
temperature resistance [ 4 ], wear resistance [ 5 – 7 ], and radiation resis -
tance [ 8 ], and therefore, have great potential for various industrial ap -
plications. However, with their high strength, most previously reported 
RHEAs have a high density and poor ductility at room temperature. 
Some RHEAs also exhibit negligible plasticity [ 9 ]. In addition, RHEAs 
have insufficient specific yield strength (SYS), which severely restricts 
their industrial applications. 
The element proportions and types of RHEAs are commonly adjusted 
to improve their mechanical properties. Wang [ 10 ] increased the con -
tent of V in the RHEAs that inhibit impurities and brittle compounds at 
grain boundaries, making the alloy prone to transgranular fracture, and 
thus improving their plasticity. Liu [ 11 ] prepared a series of RHEAs by 
adding Si elements, the yield strength increased from 1260 MPa without 
Si to maximum value of 2134 MPa, while the density decreased from 
9.02 g/cm
3 
to minimum value of 8.49 g/cm
3
. Sheikh [ 12 ] proposed that 
the ductility of RHEAs can be improved by adding Ti and Zr elements, 
and TiZrNbMoV alloy system is a potential RHEAs that possesses the 
characteristics of low density and good ductility. Huang [ 13 ] found that 
increasing the Ti content in an alloy can inhibits the formation of the 
detrimental V

tion, Ti is beneficial to refine the grain and improve the plasticity of the 
alloys [ 14 – 16 ]. Wu [ 17 ] et al. obtained the TiZrNbMo

with the yield strength and plastic strain of 1312 MPa and > 50%, 
respectively. Meanwhile the plasticity of RHEAs can be improved by 
adjusting the proportion of Mo and V in the TiZrNbMoV alloy system. 
Most reported RHEAs have a single-phase body centered cubic (BCC) 
structure [ 17 – 19 ] because of the formation of solid-solution phases. 
Multiphase alloys usually have higher strength but too much second 
phase will seriously deteriorate the plasticity [ 20 ]. Therefore, it is ex -
pected to obtain RHEAs with good comprehensive mechanical proper -
ties by introducing and regulating the second phase in RHEAs.

2. Materials and methods 
Ti
x
ZrNbMoV (x = 1,1.5 and 2) alloys were prepared by vacuum arc 
melting using Ti, Zr, Nb, Mo, and V raw materials with > 99.99 wt% 
purity. The vacuum chamber was evacuated to 3 × 10
 4 
Pa and then 
flushed with Ar gas to 0.05 MPa to prevent oxidation of the alloys during 
the melting process. The evacuation and flushing steps were repeated 
three times. All the resulting ingots were remelted and flipped five times 
during the arc-melting process to ensure homogeneous distribution of 
the elements. 
The microstructures of the Ti
x
ZrNbMoV (x = 1,1.5 and 2) alloys were 
analyzed using ND, EBSD and TEM. The ND experiments were per -
formed using the Multi-Physics Instrument at the China Spallation 
Neutron Source (CSNS). EBSD analyses were performed using an EDAX- 
TSL system and a Nordlys Max3 EBSD detector. TEM were performed in 
a FEI Tecnai G2 F20 microscope, operated at 200 Kv, and the TEM 
samples were prepared by ion-beam thinning. 
Alloy specimens were prepared with dimensions of Ф 4 mm × 6 mm 
from the as-cast alloy ingot using electric discharge machining. The 
room-temperature compressive tests were performed on a universal 
testing machine. The compressive strain rate was set to 1 × 10
 3
/s in all 
the mechanical property tests. The test was repeated three times for each 
alloy. 
Nanoindentation tests (Nano Indenter G200) were conducted with a 
Berkovich-type indentation tip at room temperature. Before performing 
the nanoindentation creep tests, the surface of each sample was polished 
to smoothness and a loading rate of 2 mN/s and a load of 400 mN were 
maintained for 60 s. To ensure data reliability, four nanoindentation 
experiments were performed for each condition, with each indentation 
point located at least 50 μ m apart.

4. Conclusions 
In this study, we investigated the effects of varying the Ti content on 
the microstructures and mechanical properties of Ti
x
ZrNbMoV (x = 1, 
1.5, 2) RHEAs. Based on the results of our study, we present the 
following conclusions: 
(1) The Ti
x
ZrNbMoV alloys mainly comprised a BCC phase and a few 
secondary HCP phases. Second-phase nanocrystals existed pri -
marily at the grain boundaries of the BCC phase. The proportion 
of the second phase decreased with increasing Ti content, and the 
microstructure of the Ti
x
ZrNbMoV alloys underwent dendritic to 
equiaxed-dendritic transformation. Meanwhile, the average gain 
size of RHEAs decreased from 81 to 16 μ m with increased Ti 
content. 
(2) The Ti
x
ZrNbMoV alloys possessed excellent mechanical proper -
ties, as well as a good balance between strength and ductility, low 
density (6.69 – 7.13 g/cm
3
), and high SYS (237 – 312 kPa ⋅ m
3
/kg). 
The high strength of the Ti
x
ZrNbMoV alloys is attributed mainly 
to solution strengthening and second-phase strengthening 
mechanisms. The reduction in the degree of lattice distortion and 
the secondary phase resulted in a decrease in strength. In addi -
tion, the grain refinement of RHEAs gave rise to an improvement 
in ductility with an increase in Ti content. 
(3) The strain rate sensitivity of the RHEAs was 0.00952 – 0.02941 at 
the steady-state creep stage in the nanoindentation creep test.The 
low strain-rate sensitivity of the Ti
