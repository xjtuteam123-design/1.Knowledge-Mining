We developed a novel low-activation, ultraﬁne-grained W-Cr-V multicomponent alloy (MCA) with excel- 
lent thermal stability and desirable high-temperature strength. The as-sintered W70 Cr15 V15 (at.%) alloy 
was mainly composed of a body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution matrix with an average grain size 
of ∼0.45 μm, minor hexagonal close-packed (HCP) phase, and ultraﬁne oxides at grain boundary (GB) 
regions. The average grain size of the MCA was < 2 μm after heating at 1500 °C for 1 h, showing a 
high thermal stability of the microstructure. Accordingly, the estimated grain growth exponent n ( ∼7) 
and the corresponding activation energy ( ∼433 kJ mol–1 ) of the MCA indicate that diffusion during the 
grain growth in the present W-Cr-V alloy is dominated by the GB diffusion. Such high thermal stability 
can be mainly attributed to the signiﬁcant pinning effects from the in-situ formed oxides at GBs. Besides, 
the nonequilibrium segregation of Cr and V at GBs also contributes to the thermal stability of the alloy at 
temperatures of 1200 °C and below. Furthermore, the average high-temperature compressive strength of 
the alloy was over 1376 MPa at 1100 °C, mainly due to the prominent solid solution and GB strengthening 
which were still effective at the high temperature. The results indicate that the present low-activation W- 
Cr-V alloy system with exceptional thermal stability and high-temperature mechanical properties could 
be a promising candidate for structural materials in future fusion reactors.

1. Introduction 
Tungsten and its alloys with high melting points and excep- 
tional resistance to ion sputtering and irradiation, hold promise for 
real applications in fusion power plants [ 1 , 2 ]. For the fusion reac- 
tor, a low-activation alloy design is necessary to accelerate the de- 
cay of radioactivity induced by irradiation and then exclude severe 
accidents in a fusion environment [3] . Due to the limitations of al- 
loy compositions, it is a great challenge to simultaneously improve 
the mechanical performance and radiation resistance of traditional 
tungsten alloys and their oxide/carbide dispersion-strengthened 
counterparts [ 4 , 5 ]. Noteworthily, the concept of multicomponent 
alloys (MCAs) containing multiple principal elements proves to 
be an eﬃcient way to improve the comprehensive performance 
of tungsten-containing alloys [6–8] . Random solid solutions with

mixing entropies, and a series of tungsten-containing MCAs with

It has been well recognized that ﬁner grains beneﬁt the syn- 
ergy of high strength and good ductility for most metallic mate- 
rials. Noteworthily, the plastic deformation of tungsten and most 
of its alloys are highly determined by the non-planar mobility of 
1/2 < 111 > screw dislocations in the body-centered cubic (BCC) 
structure, which need a very high Peierls stress [12] . Moreover, the 
spreading of a 1/2 < 111 > screw dislocation core readily constructs 
a special three-dimensional structure that is diﬃcult to move along 
any slip system in a BCC-structured crystal [13] . Additionally, tung- 
sten and most of its alloys usually suffer from poor GB cohesion 
and signiﬁcant intrinsic brittleness can be readily caused by GB de- 
cohesion triggered by external stress conditions, e.g., in the previ- 
ously reported NbMoTaW [14] , CrMoNbWTi-C [15] , and W-1 wt.% 
Ta [16] . Therefore, tungsten and most of its alloys commonly pos-

sess poor deformability (even called intrinsic brittleness). Given 
the above issues, grain reﬁnement has been regarded as an eﬃ- 
cient method to improve the fracture toughness of these tungsten- 
rich alloys at room temperature [ 13 , 17 ]. Additionally, high-density 
grain boundaries (GBs) in ultraﬁne-grain alloys can absorb irra- 
diation defects like vacancies and self-interstitial atoms, thereby 
enhancing the eﬃciency of defect recombination in a fusion en- 
vironment [18–20] . Besides, irradiation-induced dislocation loops 
are also readily annihilated at GBs, thus the radiation tolerance of 
tungsten alloy is further improved by ultraﬁne grains [21] . How- 
ever, atom diffusion is inevitably accelerated within the GB re- 
gions [22] and the total interfacial energy is accordingly increased 
by high-density GBs in ultraﬁne-grain tungsten alloy, which conse- 
quently enhances the driving force for grain growth and thus de- 
teriorates the thermal stability of the alloy at high temperatures 
[ 23 , 24 ]. In addition, it is found that a considerable thermo-stress 
ﬁeld during hot deformation can promote the GB slip and grain ro- 
tation [ 25 , 26 ], further weakening the high-temperature mechanical 
properties of tungsten alloys with ﬁne grains. In a word, it is of sig- 
niﬁcance to control the thermal stability of GBs when the strategy 
of grain reﬁnement is applied to produce superior tungsten alloys 
feasible for complicated parts of nuclear industries. 
Note that the nature of sluggish diffusion of MCAs is found 
to suppress the atomic diffusion and stabilize the conﬁguration 
of GBs at high temperatures [27] . Moreover, the in-situ formation 
of composite microstructures containing dispersive second phases 
is also reported in a number of MCA systems, which eﬃciently 
hinders the migration of GBs and maintains the ﬁne-grain ma- 
trix within a wide temperature range [ 28 , 29 ]. Therefore, the de- 
sign concept of MCA is promising for attaining thermally stable 
microstructures of high-strength tungsten alloys available for a 
fusion environment. From the above considerations, a new low- 
activation W-Cr-V MCA composed of ultraﬁne grains was pro- 
posed in this work. To ensure the high solution concentration of 
each primary element without signiﬁcantly lowering the melting 
point, the total atomic ratio of Cr and V was kept below 30%, 
and the chemical composition of the alloy was determined as 
W70 Cr15 V15 (at.%) using a thermodynamic calculation. The pristine 
elemental powders were alloyed using high-energy ball milling 
and subsequently sintered via spark plasma sintering (SPS) to ob- 
tain a dense ultraﬁne-grained alloy bulk. The microstructural fea- 
tures of the attained MCA specimens were characterized using 
X-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The kinetic mechanism 
of grain growth in this alloy was quantitatively analyzed, and its 
room- and high-temperature mechanical properties were evalu- 
ated. The W70 Cr15 V15 alloy is composed of an ultraﬁne crystalline 
BCC-disordered solid solution and nano-micron second phases dis- 
persedly distributed at GBs. The alloy exhibits excellent thermal 
stability with signiﬁcantly high strength (the value can exceed 
1496 MPa) at 1100 °C, and is expected to be employed for struc- 
tural components of future nuclear reactors. 
2. Materials and methods 
High-purity ( > 99.95 wt.%) W, Cr, and V powders (Shanghai ST- 
nano Science and Technology Co., Ltd., China) with particle sizes < 
45 μm were used as raw materials in this study (their detailed 
information can be seen in Table S1 and Fig. S1 in Supplemen- 
tal Material). The powders were mixed to a chemical composition 
of W70 Cr15 V15 (at.%) using planetary ball millers for 100 min at 
100 rpm in an argon atmosphere and then milled for 2000 min 
at 300 r min–1 for preliminary alloying. Tungsten carbide vials and 
balls were used as milling media with a ball-to-powder mass ratio 
of 5:1. After milling, the mixed pre-alloyed powders were consol- 
idated through spark plasma sintering (SPS) (LABOX-325R, SINTER 
LAND INC.) at 1400 °C for 5 min at 40 MPa under vacuum, and the 
heating rate was 100 °C min–1 . A bulk /Phi120 mm × 10 mm sample 
with a density of 15.67 g cm–3 was obtained. The carbon content 
of the bulk sample was measured using an infrared carbon sulfur 
analyzer (CS744, LECO Corporation). 
Cylindrical samples with a dimension of 9 mm in height and 
6 mm in diameter were prepared for compressive tests. Room- 
temperature compression was performed by an Instron-8802 de- 
vice at a nominal strain rate of 0.001 s–1 . Due to the small size 
of the cylindrical samples, a strain gauge was not used. Instead, 
the displacement of the indenter was used as a reference for the 
calculation of nominal engineering strain. Isothermal compression 
experiments were performed on a Gleeble-3500D thermal simula- 
tion machine at 1100 and 1200 °C in a vacuum, with a ﬁxed strain 
rate of 0.001 s–1 . The samples were heated to the target tempera- 
ture at a rate of 5 °C s–1 and held for 1 min. The temperature was 
measured using a thermocouple spot-welded in the center of the 
compression samples. 
The mass density of the sintered sample was measured using a 
method of Archimedes’ principle. The microstructure and elemen- 
tal distribution were characterized by a TESCAN CLARA GMH scan- 
ning electron microscopy (SEM) system equipped with the energy 
dispersive spectrometer (EDS) and electron backscatter diffraction 
(EBSD). The phase composition was identiﬁed by X-ray diffrac- 
tion (XRD, D/MAX-2550, Rigaku, Japan) with Cu K α radiation at 
40 kV and 200 mA. Vickers hardness was measured using a Vick- 
ers microhardness tester (HMAS-D10 0 0Z) under an indenter load 
of 0.3 kg applied for 10 s. The microstructural features of the alloys 
were studied using a transmission electron microscope (TEM, Talos 
F200X). The TEM specimens were prepared from a wafer sample 
with a dimension of 3 mm in diameter and 60–90 μm in thick- 
ness using a twin-jet electro-polisher in the solution 1 wt.% NaOH 
aqueous at a constant current of ∼100 mA at ∼5 °C. Then an ion- 
thinning system (model 695, Gatan) was used to further thin the 
disk for the requirement of standard TEM specimens. 
3. Results 
3.1. Thermodynamic phase-diagram calculation of potential W-Cr-V 
alloy systems 
Note that the experimental trial-and-error methods for attain- 
ing feasible chemical compositions of target W-Cr-V MCA systems 
with a solid solution free of brittle secondary precipitates were 
extremely time-consuming and economically costly. Hence, con- 
ventional thermodynamic phase-diagram calculation of potential 
W-Cr-V alloy systems was applied in this work. Given that the 
powders direct contact with C-containing vessels and mediums 
(such as tungsten carbide vials, balls, and graphite die) during the 
high-energy ball milling and SPS sintering processes. During ball 
milling and sintering, carbon and oxygen impurities are inevitably 
introduced into the fabricated specimens and accordingly alter the 
phase content of the alloy. Thus, the effects of C and O on the 
phase stability were also involved in our calculation. 
Fig. 1 illustrates the phase diagram of the W-Cr-V (-C- 
O) alloy system calculated using the Pandat software with a 
PanNi2022_TH + MB database. It is evident that a signiﬁcant equi- 
librium region of a single BCC phase exists in the alloy system 
within the temperature range from ∼1500 °C to the liquid phase 
line. In addition, a new BCC phase is formed from the parent phase 
upon cooling. The volume fractions of the BCC_2 phase increase 
with more chromium and vanadium contents. In the W-Cr-V sys- 
tem, a high content of Cr and V can increase the lattice distor- 
tion of the solid solutions, which commonly contributes to their 
strength and potential radiation resistance [ 30 , 31 ]. However, the 
increased content of Cr and V would lower the melting point and

The morphologies and element distributions of W70 Cr15 V15 al-

20 0 0 min are displayed in Fig. S2. The diameter of powder parti-

3.3. Thermal stability of W70 Cr15 V15 alloy 
As aforementioned, the extremely high temperature in a fusion 
environment would signiﬁcantly deteriorate the mechanical prop- 
erties of materials by altering the alloy microstructures. Therefore, 
the thermal stability of the sintered W-Cr-V specimen should be 
further evaluated. Fig. 4 shows the variations of morphology and 
grain size (GS) of the W70 Cr15 V15 specimens annealed at differ-

the BCC-structured matrix was increased from ∼0.45 to ∼0.74 μm.

when the annealing temperature was below 1200 °C, the dimen- 
sion of HCP-structured grains remained quite stable and their area 
fraction was not signiﬁcantly altered, either. The size distribution 
and area fraction of the second phases (including the HCP phase 
grains and oxide particles) for the as-sintered and annealed alloys 
are presented in Fig. S3 for reference. The second phases started to

3.4. Mechanical properties and deformation microstructures 
The average Vickers microhardness of the bulk W70 Cr15 V15 alloy 
was 1012 ±7 HV ( ∼9918 MPa), higher than most of the tungsten- 
containing MCAs reported in previous works [32] . Fig. 7 (a) shows 
the representative stress-strain curves of as-sintered alloy speci- 
mens compressed at room temperature (RT), 1100 °C, and 1200 °C, 
respectively. The specimen’s compressive strength at RT was 2535 
± 324 MPa, which is less than one-third of its hardness value, 
namely the theoretical yield strength according to classic contact 
mechanics [33] . Noticeable wobbles were also visible in the en- 
gineering stress-strain curve of the specimen compressed at RT, 
indicating continuous micro-crack propagation. This result sug- 
gests that a brittle fracture occurred before the yielding in this 
specimen. Unexpectedly, the as-sintered alloy shows an ultrahigh 
yield strength (the average value is over 1376 MPa) at 1100 °C, 
and the value rapidly decreased when the specimen was de- 
formed at higher temperatures over 1200 °C. It is also seen in 
Fig. 7 (a) that the ﬂow stress at high temperatures would progres- 
sively reduce after the deformation exceeded the yielding point, 
indicating a strain-softening scenario in that specimen. Neverthe- 
less, it is noted that the present W70 Cr15 V15 alloy presents a 
remarkably higher strength at elevated temperatures, especially 
when compared to the W-rich MCAs reported in previous works

atures. Noteworthily, a large number of chemically ordered nan-

energy [45] were observed inside several HCP-structured grains 
(see Fig. 3 ), which would enhance the radiation resistance of the 
present W-Cr-V MCAs [46–48] and promote their real applications 
in fusion environments.

partly offset the loss of pinning effects caused by the alleviated

4.3. Deformational behavior and strengthening mechanisms 
As aforementioned, the tungsten-rich BCC-structured refractory 
solid solution alloys are commonly brittle at room temperature 
and their plasticity increases with a temperature rise [14] . Within 
a large temperature regime, dislocation movements in a BCC- 
structured matrix were quite restricted, mainly due to the sig- 
niﬁcantly higher Peierls-Nabarro stress compared with the FCC- 
phase counterpart [ 71 , 72 ], readily leading to transgranular and in- 
tergranular crack propagation as the primary failure mode of the 
present alloy (see Fig. S8). At high temperatures above the DBTT, 
the deformability of the BCC-structured alloys was improved, due 
to the enhanced thermally activated motion of screw dislocations 
(see Fig. 8 ). Dislocation pile-up and tangles were also released 
by dynamic recovery (see Fig. 9 ), thereby enabling the continu- 
ous plastic ﬂow inside the deformed specimen. Accordingly, the 
high ﬂow stress at room temperature led by the immobility of 
screw dislocations was inevitably reduced [73] . Nonetheless, the 
W70 Cr15 V15 alloy retained a remarkable compressive strength (the 
average value is over 1376 MPa) at the temperature of 1100 °C, still 
much higher than most of the tungsten-rich MCAs previously re- 
ported [ 7 , 14 , 15 , 34–38 ].

5. Conclusions 
In this study, a refractory low-activation multicomponent alloy 
with an ultraﬁne grain structure was proposed. High-energy ball 
milling and SPS sintering methods were employed to produce a 
solid solution with a chemical composition of W70 Cr15 V15 (at.%) fa- 
voring the generation of a BCC-structured matrix. The phase com- 
position, microstructural features, and mechanical properties of the 
alloy were systematically investigated. The main conclusions are as 
follows: 
(1) The as-sintered alloy was mainly composed of a BCC-structured 
solid solution matrix, a minor HCP second phase, and ultraﬁne 
oxides, in accordance with the theoretical prediction from the 
phase diagram predicted using the Pandat software. Inevitable 
oxygen impurity from the ambient environment reacted with V 
due to the limited vacuum degree and then formed dispersive 
nano-sized oxide particles along GBs, which could hinder grain 
growth during the sintering process. 
(2) The dispersive oxide particles effectively block the GB migration 
and grain rotation, suppressing the grain growth at the high- 
temperature range from 1200 to 1500 °C. The non-equilibrium 
segregation of Cr and V at GBs also contributes to the thermal 
stability of the alloy at temperatures of 1200 °C and below. The 
grain growth exponent of the W70 Cr15 V15 alloy was estimated 
as ∼7 and the corresponding activation energy was ∼433 kJ 
mol–1 , indicating that diffusion during the grain growth in the 
present W-Cr-V alloy is dominated by the GB diffusion and ex- 
ceptional thermal stability of the present MCA at high temper- 
atures. 
(3) The W70 Cr15 V15 alloy exhibits high compressive strength at 
both room temperature (the average value is over 2535 MPa) 
and high temperature (1100 °C, the average value is over 
1376 MPa). When the deformation temperature of this alloy ex- 
ceeded 1200 °C, GB activities became more apparent, causing 
dynamic strain softening and lowering the ﬂow stress on defor- 
mation. 
(4) The present W-Cr-V MCA system was promising for structural 
materials employed in nuclear reactors due to its exceptional 
thermal stability and high yield strength at elevated tempera- 
tures (especially below 1200 °C).
