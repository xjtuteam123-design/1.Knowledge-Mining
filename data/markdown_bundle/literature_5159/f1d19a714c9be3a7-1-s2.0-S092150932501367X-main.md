ABSTRACT
This study investigates the influence of annealing, solution treatment, and aging heat treatment on the room- 
temperature and high-temperature tensile strength of TC17 alloy fabricated by laser powder bed fusion 
(LPBF). The TC17 alloy prepared by LPBF predominantly exhibits α lamellae in both the solution-treated and 
aged states. The optimization of alloy strength is achieved by inducing the precipitation of the secondary α phase. 
Analysis of room-temperature tensile properties identifies 910 
◦
C as the optimal annealing temperature. Under 
the final heat treatment regime of 910 
◦

400

compared to the standard, while maintaining the required tensile strength, thereby optimizing their mechanical 
properties.
1. Introduction
Titanium and its alloys are renowned for their exceptional proper -
ties, including high specific strength, excellent fatigue resistance, and 
outstanding corrosion resistance. These characteristics make them 
highly versatile and widely applicable in industries such as aerospace, 
chemical engineering, shipbuilding, and beyond. Among the various 
types of titanium alloys, near-beta titanium alloys stand out due to their 
superior thermal strength, deep hardening capability, and enhanced 
fatigue resistance compared to α or α + β titanium alloys [ 1 – 4 ]. TC17 
alloy, a near-beta titanium alloy with the composition 
Ti-5Al-2Sn-2Zr-4Mo-4Cr, is distinguished by its high strength, tough -
ness, and hardenability. It is also commonly referred to as Ti17, 
reflecting the total sum of its alloying elements. Thanks to its remarkable 
properties — including heat resistance, corrosion resistance, robust 
toughness, and structural stability — TC17 has emerged as a critical 
structural material in the aerospace industry. It is extensively utilized in 
components such as blades, compressor disks, and integral bladed rotors 
for advanced aircraft engines that operate at temperatures of up to 
400 
◦
C [ 5 – 7 ]. However, the traditional forging process for 
manufacturing large and critical titanium alloy structural components 
often leads to extended processing times, low material utilization rates, 
and low yield rates. Additionally, this method is not well-suited for 
producing components with complex shapes. LPBF technology offers a 
solution by enabling the 3D printing of parts with intricate geometries 
and large dimensions. This process provides enhanced forming preci -
sion, shorter manufacturing cycles, and improved material utilization 
[ 8 – 11 ]. As additive manufacturing technology continues to advance and 
manufacturing equipment becomes more sophisticated, there is signifi -
cant potential for increased demand for complex large titanium alloy 
components.

composition but different morphologies in the longitudinal (L) and 
transverse (T) directions. Compared to the T-direction samples, the 
L-direction samples displayed lower strength but higher plasticity. After 
annealing treatment, the strength of the L-direction samples increased 
significantly, while plasticity decreased. For the T-direction samples, 
strength showed minimal change, but plasticity decreased by nearly 50 
%. Wang et al. [ 22 ] analyzed samples of TC17 alloy fabricated via LPBF. 
Their investigation into the microstructure and mechanical properties 
found, during heat treatment, the Ostwald ripening mechanism pri -

lamellar thickness growth reaches its peak between 650

The main dislocation distribution of LPBF-TC17 lies at α lamellae edges, 
adjacent to the β phase, and dislocation density diminishes with rising 
heat treatment temperature. Concurrently, the termination migration 
mechanism causes α lamellae ends to become progressively rounded 
rather than sharp with increasing heat treatment temperature. The in -
crease in α lamellar thickness and reduction in dislocation density 
collectively contribute to the effective enhancement of TC17 plasticity. 
Despite the existing studies on TC17 alloy produced through additive 
manufacturing, research specifically focused on the heat treatment of 
TC17 alloy fabricated by LPBF remains limited. The as-deposited TC17 
alloy demonstrates relatively high ductility but limited strength, which 
renders it unsuitable for engineering applications. Consequently, it is 
essential to apply appropriate heat treatment processes aimed at 
enhancing its strength while ensuring adequate room-temperature per -
formance to meet the engineering requirements under operating con -
ditions of 400 
◦
C. Implementing effective heat treatment strategies will 
be crucial for optimizing the mechanical properties of this alloy for 
practical applications.
Due to the unique forming process of the LPBF technique, the alloy 
undergoes a complex thermal cycle during fabrication. The as-deposited 
LPBF near- β titanium alloys typically exhibit coarse original β grains, 
including columnar and equiaxed grains, which are significantly 
different from the microstructure of conventionally forged components. 
Since mechanical properties are highly sensitive to microstructural 
characteristics, factors such as the fraction, morphology, and orientation 
distribution of the α phase significantly influence the alloy ’ s mechanical 
performance [ 23 – 27 ]. Meanwhile, solution treatment, aging time, and 
cooling rates have a pronounced effect on the microstructural evolution 
during the heat treatment process of titanium alloys [ 28 – 30 ]. Due to the 
significant differences in the microstructures of LPBF titanium alloys 
and forged components, traditional heat treatment methods may yield 
results that differ markedly from those of forged alloys. By exper -
imenting with traditional heat treatment methods and adjusting the 
temperature and duration of the heat treatment, it is possible to optimize 
the strength properties of the alloy. Furthermore, previous studies have 
not investigated the high-temperature mechanical properties of TC17 
alloy produced by additive manufacturing. This research explores the 
relationship between heat treatment conditions and microstructure 
evolution of TC17 alloy fabricated via LPBF. Specifically, we analyze 
how different heat treatment protocols influence the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of the alloy. Based on this analysis, we identify 
suitable room-temperature tensile heat treatment processes. Building 
upon these results, we further adjust the aging time to develop an 
optimal heat treatment schedule for high-temperature tensile perfor -
mance at 400 
◦
C. This approach achieves a balanced enhancement of 
strength and plasticity, which is of great significance for the develop -
ment of high-performance, high-temperature LPBF near- β titanium 
alloys.
2. Materials and experimental details
2.1. LPBF process and post heat treatment
The Ti-5Al-2Sn-2Zr-4Mo-4Cr gas-atomized powder was utilized as 
the raw materials, featuring a particle size range of 15 – 55 μ m and an 
average particle size of 32 μ m. Cube samples and cylindrical test bars 
were fabricated using a Hanbang HBD E400 machine equipped with a 
500W fiber laser and a beam diameter of 50 μ m. To enhance the flow -
ability of the powder and minimize moisture absorption prior to the 
LPBF process, the powder was dried in a vacuum oven at 110 
◦
C for 4 h. 
During the forming process, the forming chamber was purged with 
argon gas, ensuring that the oxygen concentration remained below 0.2 
%. After printing, the components were separated from the substrate 
using a wire electrical discharge machine for subsequent mechanical 
performance evaluation and microstructure analysis. The designs of the 
cube samples and the finished samples are illustrated in Fig. 1 , while the 
chemical composition of the powder is detailed in Table 1 .

of 0.03 mm, laser power set to 250 W, scanning speed of 1000 mm/s,

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure characterization
The near- β α + β titanium alloy fabricated via LPBF typically exhibits 
coarse primary columnar grains and equiaxed grains that grow along the 
build direction. As shown in Fig. 2 , the three-dimensional morphology of 
the as-deposited TC17 alloy sample reveals that the top surface is 
composed of a series of regularly arranged equiaxed grains, while the 
side surface consists of columnar grains whose growth direction is par -
allel to the build direction. During the LPBF process, the significant 
temperature gradient oriented toward the substrate causes both large 
and small columnar grains to grow parallel to the printing direction. The 
thickness of each printed layer and the height of the small columnar 
grains are determined by specific process parameters.
Integrating metallographic Fig. 3 optical microscopy (OM) images 
with Fig. 4 the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the as-deposited 
material, the TC17 titanium alloy fabricated by LPBF exhibits a 
macroscopic morphology characterized by a series of coarse β columnar 
grains, which extend across multiple build layers along the build di -
rection. These columnar grains are arranged in a relatively regular 
pattern, with their principal axes predominantly perpendicular to the 
laser scanning direction. Layer banding phenomena are also evident 
across different build layers, manifested as alternating light and dark 
features in the macrostructure, which arise from variations in the crys -
tallographic orientations of the grains. Metallographic analysis reveals 
that the as-deposited samples have a regularly arranged structure of 
equiaxed β phases, with an average grain size of approximately 100 μ m. 
No ultrafine α phase was detected upon detailed examination of the β 
phase, which contrasts with the findings of Zhu et al. [ 18 ], who reported 
that TC17 samples produced via laser deposition exhibit a fine net-like 
microstructure containing ultrafine α phases. This difference can be 
attributed to the fact that in titanium alloys, the α phase has a hexagonal 
close-packed (hcp) crystal structure that provides good resistance to 
plastic deformation, thereby enhancing the material ’ s strength. 
Conversely, the β phase has a body-centered cubic (bcc) structure, which 
facilitates plastic deformation and imparts good ductility. Consequently,

3.2. Tensile properties
3.2.1. Room-temperature tensile properties
According to the sampling method shown in Fig. 1 , the obtained 
room temperature tensile performance data of the test bars after 
annealing in the single β phase region are shown in Table 3 . Compared to 
the aviation standard for TC17 titanium alloy (GJB 2744A-2019), the as- 
deposited LPBF TC17 samples exhibit low strength and high ductility, 
which can be attributed to the rapid solidification process and the 
dispersion strengthening effect of the ultra-fine α
s 
during the LPBF 
process. The low strength may be due to sampling along the X-direction 
and the coarse original β grains in the as-deposited microstructure. The 
high plasticity is likely a result of the relatively uniform distribution of 
the original β grains, fewer defects in the as-deposited state, and a stable 
β phase structure. After annealing treatment in the single β phase region 
(Sample 8), the elongation (EL) and reduction in area (RA) at fracture 
are 14 and 17, respectively. These values are reduced by 28 % and 22 % 
compared to the as-deposited state, but still meet the aviation standard. 
The ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and yield strength (YS) increase by 
321 and 339 MPa, respectively, which are 35.9 % and 40.8 % higher 
than the as-deposited state. Both strength and ductility are higher than 
the aviation standard. These results indicate the effectiveness of single β 
phase annealing treatment in improving the mechanical properties of 
LPBF TC17 alloy.

During high-temperature deformation of titanium alloys, slip re -
mains the dominant deformation mechanism. As the temperature in -
creases, the energy of dislocations in the system also rises, leading to a 
reduction in the critical resolved shear stress required for slip [ 42 ]. 
Under high-temperature conditions, the dislocation motion mechanisms 
undergo significant changes: interactions between dislocations and so -
lute atoms weaken, resulting in a reduction of solid-solution strength -
ening effects, an increase in cross-slip frequency, and the prominence of 
high-temperature-specific deformation mechanisms such as dislocation 
climb and grain boundary sliding. These factors contribute to a macro -
scopic behavior where the alloy exhibits decreased strength but 
enhanced ductility at elevated temperatures [ 43 , 44 ].

decreasing, while high-temperature plasticity gradually diminishes. The
