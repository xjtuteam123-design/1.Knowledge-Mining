ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
Study of the Crack and Porosity Regulation
of a Non-weldable Ni-Based Superalloy Fabricated
by Laser Powder Bed Fusion
Shuijin Li, Shuang Gao, Weiguang Zhang, Guoxin Lu, Luqing Cui, Linzi Li, Yinghui Ma, and Sai Wang
Submitted: 28 June 2024 / Revised: 14 August 2024 / Accepted: 24 August 2024 / Published online: 18 November 2024
It is very challenging to prepare non-weldable Ni-based superalloys without defects by laser powder bed
fusion (LPBF) technology since they are prone to cracking due to the comparatively high content of Al and
Ti. This paper studied the reduction of the crack and porosity of a non-weldable superalloy, IN939 Ni-based
alloy, by preheating substrate and reﬁning laser volume energy density of LPBF process. The results
showed that the crack density increased with the laser volume energy density increasing; while, the porosity
decreased. The range of laser volume energy density suitable for IN939 Ni-based superalloy was very
narrow, which was from 37.5 to 58.3 J/mm
3 considering the synchronized reduction of crack and porosity.
The alloying element segregation and the high residual stress were two essential reasons for forming cracks.
Further, the substrate preheating at 180 /C176C inhibited the formation of cracks; and hence, the mechanical
property was slightly improved. This study will provide a reference for the preparation of LPBF non-
weldable Ni-based superalloys.
Keywords crack, ﬁnite element modeling, laser powder bed
fusion, non-weldable Ni-based superalloy, porosity
1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys with outstanding corrosion–oxidation
resistance and creep resistance above 650 /C176 C, are essential
materials for manufacturing aero-engine blades, turbines,
combustion chambers, and other high-temperature parts with
complex structures (Ref 1-4). With the development of
topology structure design and integrated design for the
lightweight of aero-engine, the structure complexity of many
high-temperature parts has increased in recent years. These
components with more complex structures are difﬁcult to be
prepared by traditional manufacturing processes, such as cast
and wrought (Ref 5, 6). Laser Powder Bed Fusion (LPBF), a
metal additive manufacturing technology, has gained signiﬁcant
attention for its high precision and ﬂexibility in producing hot-
end components of nickel-based superalloys with high-perfor-
mance, complex geometry (Ref 7-10). A successful application
example, that is, General Electric Company has successfully
used LPBF technology to prepare fuel nozzles by integrating 20
parts, as a result, the weight of fuel nozzle is reduced by 25%
(Ref 11). CM247, IN738 and IN939 Ni-based superalloys have
the outstanding high-temperature strength and microstructural
stability above the higher temperature of 850 /C176 C. Unfortu-
nately, they are classiﬁed as non-weldable superalloys with a
high crack sensitivity due to comparatively high contents of Al
and Ti (Ref 10, 12, 13). The LPBF process, similar to welding,
involves rapid heating and cooling process, leading to serious
thermal stress and even hot cracks (Ref 14). These defects
undoubtedly impair the mechanical properties of LPBF alloys.
Kangazian et al. (Ref 15 and 16) also proved that element
segregation and carbide aggregation at grain boundaries (GBs)
were the reasons for the ductility loss of LPBF superalloys.
Therefore, it is a challenge to manufacture non-weldable
superalloys by LPBF process.
In order to realize the fabrication of crack-free LPBF non-
weldable superalloys, many researchers have made efforts in
this ﬁeld. For instance, Liu et al. (Ref 17) obtained nearly
crack-free CM247LC specimens by controlling the hatch
distance of melt pools. They believed that serrated GBs with
the reduced aspect ratio of grains alleviated stress concentration
at GBs and delays crack propagation. Carter et al. (Ref 18)
found that the /C212island/C213scanning strategy strongly inﬂuenced the
grain structure of LPBF CM247LC alloy. They deemed that
ﬁne grains without a distinct texture had a high level of
misorientation at GBs that greatly inhibited the formation of
cracks. Wan (Ref 19) and Dai et al. (Ref 20) also demonstrated
that the scanning strategy changes the orientation and texture of
grains to affect the formation of defects and mechanical
properties of alloys. Chen et al. (Ref 21) prepared nearly crack-
free IN738LC alloy with almost 99.9% density through the
substrate preheated at 700 /C176 C. They found that the formation of
high angle grain boundaries (HAGBs) is suppressed by

IN939 Ni-based superalloy is one of the most popular

Recently, LPBF IN939 alloy attracted the attention of many

the Si addition will change the design composition of the alloy,
which is thought to impact the microstructural stability during
long-term and high-temperature service. In addition, some
researchers chose pulsed wave emission laser (Ref 24) and
electron beam (Ref 25) instead of common continuous wave
laser as energy input sources, to print high-quality IN939 alloy.
Marchese et al. (Ref 26 studied the effect of laser volume

that the effect of P on Ev is more important. However, there are
few reports on the effect of Ev changes due to different P
matching with different v on the pores and cracks of LPBF

process by designing a wider range of P and v. Additionally, the
synergic substrate preheating was also studied to expand the
fabrication window to produce LPBF IN939 alloy with fewer
defects. The formation and control mechanisms of defects in
LPBF IN939 alloy were summarized in this paper. This above
work will provide a reference for the fabrication of LPBF non-
weldable superalloys.

3.3 Porosity
Figure 9 shows pores distribution at XY planes of LPBF
IN939 alloy samples with various laser powers and scanning
speeds. It is evident that irregular pores are concentrated in the
upper right corner of the parameter region where P is in the
range of 120-160 W. When P is relatively low, insufﬁcient
input energy results in poor interlayer bonding and un-melted
regions, which lots of irregular pores distribute. When P is in
the range of 180-280 W and v is above 1000 mm/s, the porosity
is effectively controlled. The area marked by red dotted lines in
Fig. 9 shows the porosity below 0.1%. On the contrary, the high
P cooperating with relatively low v results in excessive energy
input, which causes over-melting, evaporating and entraining
gas (Ref 32, 33). This entraining gas will become micropores
and keyholes, which are circle pores as shown in the bottom left
corner of Fig. 9. It can be seen that when P is ﬁxed, there is an
optimal value for v. The v determines the interaction time
between laser and powder, and can directly determines the
amount of energy input. In other words, the effect of v on pores
formation is the same as that of P. E
v delineates the combined
effect of P and v. According to Fig. 9, the porosity is basically
suppressed when Ev is in the range of 37.5-83.3 J/mm 3.
3.4 Crack Density
Crack density is counted based on ten OM images, as shown
in Fig. 10. It can be found from Fig. 10 that the minimum crack
density is 0.34 mm/mm 2 and the maximum is 6.85 mm/mm 2.
When v is more than 1400 mm/s, the crack density increases
with the increase in P. This signiﬁes that the crack density
increases with the increase in Ev. When v is less than 1400 mm/
s, the crack density ﬂuctuates at the high level, and most crack
densities are larger than 3.00 mm/mm 2. This indicates that
1400 mm/s is the threshold of v where the corresponding
maximum Ev is 58.3 J/mm 3. Although lower energy density
can reduce the crack density, it increases the porosity, as
reﬂected in Fig. 11. When E
v £ 37.5 J/mm 3, the porosity
sharply increases. When Ev is between 37.5 and 58.3 J/mm 3,
the porosity is stable below 0.1%, and the crack density is also
controlled at the low level. Once Ev ‡ 58.3 J/mm 3, the crack
density ﬂuctuates at the high level. It is concluded that the
suitable fabrication window becomes narrow, limited between
37.5 and 58.3 J/mm 3. The Ev has a great inﬂuence on the
accumulation of residual stress, which triggers different crack
formation mechanisms (Ref 34). The crack formation mecha-
nism in this paper will be is revealed in the next section, which
is useful for selecting suitable parameters to fabricating defect-
free LPBF samples.
3.5 Crack Formation Mechanism
The morphology of defect-free molten pool is shown in
Fig. 12(a), and the microstructure is composed of columnar
dendrites and cellular dendrites. The morphologies of two
typical cracks show the tearing pattern and the dendrite shape,
which are found in Fig. 12(b). There is a crack kink between
two neighboring cracks. The morphologies of cracks indicate
that the cracks happen in the interdendritic region, where some
alloying elements segregate affecting the solidiﬁcation behavior
of local alloys. The chemical component analysis is performed
at the matrix and crack tip, as marked by Point 1 and Point 2 in
Fig. 12(c), and the results are shown in Table 3. It can be found
that the aggregation of Ti, Ta, Zr, Nb and C on the crack tip.

The aggregation of Ti, Ta, Nb and C will facilitate the
formation of carbides with high hardness (Ref 35, 36) at the
crack tip. These carbides will cause stress concentration by
impeding dislocation motion. In addition, the aggregation of Zr
will reduce the local melting temperature and signiﬁcantly
promote the formation of low melting point phases (Ref 37-39).
During the repeated thermal cycle of the LPBF process, the
low-melting phase may also melt to form liquid ﬁlm which is a
weak spot (Ref 40). The high concentration of Zr is mainly
responsible for increasing crack sensitivity and broadening the
solidiﬁcation temperature range. It prolongs the existence time
of the liquid ﬁlm during the solidiﬁcation process and repeated
heating process (Ref 41, 42). Therefore, liquid ﬁlms will crack
when the stress concentration is serious. Figure 12(d) shows the
EBSD map near the cracks in LPBF IN939 alloy. It can be
found that cracks almost distribute along HAGBs with a
misorientation difference greater than 15 /C176 , as marked by the
blue lines in Fig. 12(d). The liquid phase at HAGBs has better
wetting and spreading ability; and thus, low melting point
phase is easy to precipitate at HAGBs (Ref 43). When the stress
accumulation at HAGBs exceeds the critical strength, it will
become the main driving force of crack formation and
extension along HAGBs (Ref 44, 45).
Based on the results of previous study, it is thought that the
high E
v will result in the increasing residual stress, as a result,
this leads to the high crack density. The infer is veriﬁed by
ﬁnite element modeling and the results are shown in Fig. 13.
The parameters used for simulation are different P value
matching the same v value, and correspond to 50 J/mm
3 and
100 J/mm 3, respectively. The residual stress of two samples
increases with increasing the deposited layer during LPBF
process, as shown in Fig. 13. The sample with P = 120 W, its
residual stress accumulates up to 408.1 MPa when t = 4.4 s
and cooldown for 300 s. When P = 240 W, the residual stress
value dramatically increases to 909.4 MPa. Therefore, cracks
are more likely to generate at GBs with liquid ﬁlms when the Ev
is high. In conclusion, the alloying element segregation and
high residual stress are two essential reasons for the crack
formation.

substrate preheating (Fig. 15b and e), and the dimples become
larger and deeper (Fig. 15c and f). The results indicate that the
plasticity of LPBF IN939 alloy is improved after substrate
preheating, which agrees with tensile test results in Fig. 14. The
substrate preheating is an important control method of cracks
(Ref 49, 50). It has been proved that the substrate preheating
can decrease temperature gradient which is the essential reason
of the formation of high residual stress in LPBF alloy.
Therefore, the residual stress in the deposited layer is not easy
to reach the critical value for liquid ﬁlm rupture when the
substrate is preheated. As a result, the formation and propaga-
tion of the original crack can be controlled effectively (Ref 51,
52). This explains the decreased crack density of LPBF IN939
alloy after the substrate preheating at 180 /C176 C. However, the
preheating temperature of 180 /C176 C (the limit temperature of
HBD-150 equipment) is not enough to decrease obviously
temperature gradient and residual stress of LPBF IN939 alloy
in this paper; and hence, the increase amplitude of mechanical
properties is not obvious. Further, the preparation of LPBF
IN939 alloy subjected to substrate preheating at higher
temperature will be carried out to obtain high-quality LPBF
IN939 alloy in our future work.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, the laser volume energy density Ev is
controlled by adjusting laser power and scanning speed, and
the effect of Ev and substrate preheating on the crack and
porosity of a non-weldable superalloy, IN939 Ni-based alloy, is
studied. The main conclusions are as follows:
1. The surface roughness of LPBF IN939 alloy decreases
and the relative density increases with the increase in E
v.
However, splattering and combustion losses lead to
incomplete forming when Ev is excessive.
2. The porosity and crack density show the opposite trend
with the increase in Ev. The suitable range of Ev for
LPBF IN939 alloy is very narrow, which is from 37.5 to
58.3 J/mm
3 considering the synchronized reduction of
crack and porosity.
3. The aggregation of Ti, Ta, Zr, Nb and C facilitates the
formation of carbides and liquid ﬁlms. The ﬁnite element
modeling result demonstrates that the residual stress will
increase with E
v increasing. The alloying element segre-
gation and the high residual stress are two essential rea-
sons for the formation of cracks.
4. The strength and the ductility of LPBF IN939 alloy are
slightly improved due to the cracks reduction after the
substrate preheating at 180 /C176 C.
