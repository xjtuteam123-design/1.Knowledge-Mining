Fatigue behaviour of Ni3Al(B) alloys at room temperature 
Gang Li, Jian-Ting Guo, Zhong-Guang Wang and Chang-Xu Shi 
The high cycle fatigue behaviour of high boron polycrystalline Ni3AI alloys is studied. A single phase Ni3AI alloy with a mole fraction of 0.6% 
B and a Ni3AI alloy containing a mole fraction of 1 .0% B with a small amount of the boride eutectic at the grain boundaries are selected for 
investigation. High cycle fatigue tests at room temperature with R (minimum stress/maximum stress) 0.1 are conducted in air and at 30 Hz. 
The results show that the Ni3Al(0.6% B) alloy is better than the Ni3Al(1.0% B) alloy in the fatigue resistance, although, the latter is much 
higher than the former in static strengths. The fatigue fracture surfaces are observed by SEM.

Considerable research effect has been concentrated on 
the investigation of an intermetallic compound Ni3Al which 
is a candidate material for high temperature structural ap­
plications. However, the brittleness limits the fabrication 
and application of polycrystalline Ni3Al. Since the ductility 
of polycrystalline Ni3Al at room temperature can be sub­
stantially improved by a small boron addition and hyposto­
ichiometric deviation [l], it is an attractive field to investi­
gate the mechanical properties of Ni3Al(B) alloys. Numer­
ous investigations have focused on NbAl(B) alloy in which 
the mole fraction of boron was usually less than 0.5% [2; 3]. 
Very little attention has been paid to the mechanical prop­
erties of high boron NbAl alloys. Recently, several papers 
investigated the influence of boron concentration on the 
microstructure and tensile properties of Ni3Al alloy [4; 5]. 
The fatigue behaviour of this intermetallic is also one of the 
most crucial factors, which can determine whether NbAl 
could be put into practical service or not. Additionally, the 
influence of Al content on high cycle fatigue behaviour of 
Ni3Al alloy has been studied [6]. In the present paper, the 
emphasis is placed on the differences of the microstructure, 
tensile properties and high cycle fatigue behaviour of two 
high boron Ni3Al alloys. 
Experimental 
Two Ni3Al(B) alloys used in the present investigation 
were prepared from pure Ni (mass content of 99.9%) and 
pure Al (mass content of 99.99%) and a Ni-B master alloy. 
The starting materials were induction melted and then cast 
in vacuum. The chemical compositions of the two alloys are 
given in table 1. The bars ( 11 mm in diameter by 60 mm 
in length) were electrodischarge machined from the two 
Ni3Al(B) alloy castings, respectively. The bars were ho­
mogenized at 1200 °C for 24 h, furnace cooled to 1100 °C 
and held there for 1 h, then cooled in air to room tem­
perature. Specimens of the Ni3Al(0.6% B) alloy with a 
gauge section of 3.00 mm in diameter and 10.00 mm in 
length were machined from the bars while specimens of

Load-controlled tension-tension high cycle fatigue tests 
were conducted in a closed loop hydraulic servo machine 
at room temperature, with a frequency of 30 Hz and in a 
laboratory air environment. In all tests, the stress ratio, R 
(minimum stress/maximum stress), was fixed at 0.1, and 
the wave form of the stress pattern was a sinusoidal shape. 
The number of cycle to fracture for the specimens was 
recorded. 
Tensile tests of the two Ni3Al(B) alloys were performed 
at a strain rate of 2-10-3 s- 1. The tensile properties of the 
two Ni3Al(B) alloys are also shown in table 1. 
The microstructure and fracture surfaces of the two 
Ni3Al(B) alloys were observed by optical microscopy (OM) 
and SEM. X-ray diffraction, energy dispersive X-ray spec­
troscopy (EDS) and TEM were utilized to analyze the con­
stituent phases in the Ni3Al(B) alloys. 
Results and discussion 
Microstructure. The X-ray diffraction trace, OM and 
SEM examination indicate that the Ni3Al(0.6% B) alloy 
is a single phase polycrystalline alloy with the L12 crys­
tal structure. On the other hand, there is a small amount 
of the second phase particles at the grain boundary in the 
Ni3Al(l.0% B) alloy. After being separated by extraction 
method, the extract from the Ni3Al(l.0% B) alloy was an­
alyzed by X-ray diffraction to determine the crystal struc­
tures of constituent phases. The X-ray diffraction result 
shows that there is a boride in the second phase particles. 
The boride exhibits a M23C6 type crystal structure.

Strength. It can be clearly seen from table 1 that the 
0.2% offset yield strength and ultimate tensile strength 
of the Ni3Al(0.6% B) alloy are lower than those of the 
Ni3Al(l.O% B) alloy. In the Ni)Al(l.0% B) alloy, high 
boron concentration is one of the reasons for the occur­
rence of the boride eutectic. The boride eutectic is also a 
contributor to the 0.2% offset yield strength and ultimate 
tensile strength. That means, it is partly due to the second 
phase particles strengthening that the Ni3Al(l.0% B) alloy 
exhibits higher static strengths. 
Fatigue life. The results of the fatigue tests are illus­
trated in figure 2 in the form of cyclic stress amplitude 
versus the number of cycle to fracture. From this figure, it 
can be seen that the S-N curves of the two Ni3Al(B) alloys 
are similar to those of other metallic materials. However, 
it is surprising to note that the fatigue lives of Ni3Al(0.6% 
B) are longer than those of Ni3Al(l.0% B) over the overall 
stress levels adopted in the present study. The differences 
in life between the two alloys are the most pronounced at 
low stress levels, that is, in the high cycle fatigue regime. 
At high stress levels the differences are minimized and the 
fatigue lives are generally similar at a given stress level. As 
compared with Ni3Al(0.6% B), the alloy of Ni3Al(1.0% B) 
exhibits higher static strengths and lower ductility (table 1). 
According to the general concept of fatigue life under stress 
controlled conditions [7], the fatigue lives of Ni)Al(l.0% 
B) should be longer at lower stress levels and shorter at 
higher stress levels than those of Ni3Al(0.6% B). Hence, 
the results in figure 2 show an abnormally high cycle fa­
tigue behaviour of the alloys. The cyclic stress amplitudes 
were normalized by the yield strengths in figure 3. It is 
also found obviously in figure 3 that all ratio values of 
cyclic stress amplitude to yield strength of the Ni3Al(0.6% 
B) alloy are markedly over those of the Ni3Al(l.0% B) al­
loy on the same scale of cycle to fracture. Thus, it can be 
concluded that the fatigue resistance of the Ni3Al(0.6% B) 
alloy is much higher than that of the Ni)Al(l.0% B) alloy.

Effect of casting temperature. Gieseke et al. [10] inves­
tigated the effect of casting temperature on the high cy­
cle fatigue properties of cast IC-221M at 650 °C. IC-221M 
was cast in vacuum using castings poured at three different 
temperatures spanning a 56 °C range. IC-221M is a multi­
phase alloy with ,', 1 , (3 and Ni5Zr3 phases. Their results 
show that casting temperature, or the cooling rate of cast­
ing, has strongly influenced size, shape, distribution and 
volume of solidification microporosity. The microporosity 
can be divided into three categories, including fluid flow 
macroporosity, gas porosity and shrinkage microporosity. 
In those IC-221M castings, macroporosity was not encoun­
tered and fatigue crack initiation occurred almost exclu­
sively at shrinkage microporosity whereas no instances of 
initiation at gas pores and/or at the Ni5Zr3 were observed. 
In the present investigation, although casting in vac­
uum is also utilized to produce the Ni)Al(B) alloys, un­
like Gieseke et al. results, the casting defect is not a main 
contributor to fatigue crack initiation. This situation may be 
associated with shrinkage. Examination by SEM confirmed 
that there is hardly any shrinkage porosity. The casting pro­
cess variables have strongly influenced the microporosity 
and microstructure which can conversely influence the fa­
tigue properties of Ni)Al alloy. On the basis of the compar­
ison of the fatigue fracture features, the significant differ­
ences are shown between Ni3Al(0.6% B) and Ni3Al(l .0% 
B). It implies that, in the Ni3Al(B) alloys, fatigue crack ini­
tiation is strongly dependent on microstructure. The factors 
which govern the fatigue fracture are strikingly different. 
From a standpoint of obtaining higher fatigue resistance of 
Ni3Al(B) alloy, it is not appropriate to strengthen Ni)Al(B) 
alloy using the second phase particles in Ni3Al(B)-matrix, 
which may change the conditions of local stress and pro­
mote the initiation of fatigue crack, thus decreasing the 
fatigue resistance of materials. 
Cyclic deformation. Under strain controlled tension­
compression conditions, several papers [ 11...14] have re­
ported the cyclical deformation behaviour of Ni3Al, both 
single crystal and polycrystalline, at room temperature. It 
was believed that fatigue crack initiation was intimately 
related to an accumulation of dislocation dipoles during 
fatigue. Dislocation structures of Ni3Al single crystals fa­
tigued have been observed. A mechanism for fatigue crack 
initiation was proposed. TEM investigation on dislocation 
structures of the Ni3Al(B) alloys fatigued is underway. A 
detailed report will be presented in another paper.

In a process to put the advanced materials, such as the 
intermetallic compound, into practical service for struc­
tural applications, it is a fundamental rule to highlight the 
mechanical properties not only on static strengths, but also 
on fatigue behaviour. It is extremely necessary to investi­
gate various aspects of Ni3Al(B) fatigue behaviour because 
higher static strength and better fatigue resistance do not 
correlate. It should take the point of view into account 
both in designing the composition and in controlling the 
microstructure of Ni3Al. The results of the present investi­
gation are good examples. 
Conclusion 
The high cycle fatigue resistance of the single phase 
Ni3Al(0.6% B) alloy is better than that of the Ni3Al(l.0% 
B) alloy with a small amount of the boride eutectic at the 
grain boundary, on the same scale of cyclic stress amplitude 
and even so that the cyclic stress amplitude is normalized 
by the 0.2% yield strength, although, Ni3Al(l.0% B) has 
higher static strengths than NiJAl(0.6% B) in tensile test. 
These results can be explained in terms of the existence of 
the boride eutectic in Ni3 Al(l.0% B) alloy. The boride eu­
tectic is the strengthening phase, behaves as the more easily 
initiating fatigue crack site and decreases the fatigue resist­
ance. The two Ni3Al(B) alloys show the different mode of 
fatigue crack initiation and propagation. The surface is the 
exclusive site of fatigue crack initiation in Ni3Al(0.6% B), 
whereas the boride eutectic predominant in Ni3Al(l.0% B).

The non-metallic element boron is of special interest as alloying element in steels 
due to its hardenability enhancing effect since decades. One advantage of the addi­
tion of boron to steels in the low strengthening effect on the solid solution comp­
ared with other hard ability enhancing elements, like chromium or manganese. 
Therefore boron containing steels are easy to be cold reduced in the as-annealed 
or as-rolled condition. On the other hand, the necessary amount of boron as alloy­
ing element with up to 70 ppm is low. However, in processing of boron containing 
steels, f. i. during annealing, boron causes difficulty in that even under inert gas or 
under vacuum it evaporates from the steel product. Thus at least the surface of the 
product often becomes impoverished from boron. Just in this area, however, us­
ually the highest hardness and abraision resistance are required. When boron is 
missing it cannot exert its hardenability enhancing effect. 
The author has summarized the knowledge about boron and its influence on the 
properties from the rare and widely spread literature and has presented it in a con­
centrated survey.
