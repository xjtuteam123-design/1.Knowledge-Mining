Abstract
Superelasticity has been widely used in various fields. High elastic limit simultaneously with large superelasticity is urgent in 
the critical applications. In this study, we have employed the high entropy alloy (HEA) concept to develop a new TiHfNiFe-
Nb eutectic HEA (EHEA) based on the TiNi-Nb eutectic alloy. The results show the directional catkin-like microstructure 
with the fiber eutectic in the center and the lamella eutectic in the outside form along the directional solidification direction 
when the growth rate is 60 mm/h. The corresponding specimens display the high strength of 1409 MPa and superelasticity 
of 3.1% in tension at room temperature, which is attributed to the synergetic effect of high entropy, preferred orientation, 
ordered body-centered cubic (B2) phase and disordered body-centered cubic phase in the eutectic structure. This work sheds 
light on designing the high-performance superelastic function materials with EHEAs.
Keywords High-entropy alloy · Eutectic alloy · Directional solidification · Superelasticity · Tensile strength
1 Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) have been developed as a new 
concept of the metallic material designed by Yeh et al. [1 ], 
which refer to the alloys containing complex compositions 
of multi-principal element constituents. Since then, numer -
ous alloys with unique properties have been discovered and 
investigated [2 –10]. For example, the FeCoNiCrMn HEA 
displays enhanced mechanical properties of tensile strength 
over 1280 MPa and fracture toughness value exceeding 
200 MPa m1/2 at cryogenic temperature [11]; He et al. [2 ] 
reported that the (CoNi)50(TiZrHf)50 HEA shows an extraor-
dinary Elinvar effect between room temperature and 900 K, 
along with an superelastic limit of ~ 2%; and W-bearing 
HEAs exhibit an outstanding radiation resistance [6]. Many 
works have shown that this multicomponent “cocktail” will 
make some unexpected and magical breakthroughs in many 
aspects, mainly due to its core effects of high entropy, slug-
gish diffusion and lattice distortion, etc., by comparison to 
the conventional alloy [12].
Very recently, this state-of-the-art alloy design con-
cept has been applied to shape memory alloys (SMAs) to 
overcome the degradation of shape memory effect (SME) 
induced by the irreversible plastic deformation. Firstov 
et al. firstly reported that the (TiZrHf) 50 (CoNiCu)50 HEAs 
exhibited a perfect shape memory at temperatures higher 
than 400 K based on the NiTi intermetallic [13]. It is attrib-
uted to high elastic modulus and high yield strength, which 
are originated from the core effects of HEAs, such as high 
entropy, sluggish diffusion and lattice distortion, etc. Sub-
sequently, SME at an ultrahigh temperature, large elastic-
ity at an elevated temperature and superelasticity at a low 
temperature have also been reported in the Ni–Pd–Ti–Zr–Hf 
[14], Ti–Hf–Zr–Cu–Ni [15, 16] and Ti–Zr–Hf–Cu–Ni–Co 
[17] alloy systems, respectively. The interaction of multi-
components effectively suppresses the plastic deformation 
during the process of martensitic transformation, improving 
the performance of SME [18]. Apparently, it is convinc-
ing that mutual applications of SME and HEA will give

In this article, we employed the HEA concept to develop 
a new Ti–Hf–Ni–Fe–Nb eutectic HEA (EHEA) based on 
the TiNi-Nb eutectic alloy [19–22]. As known, Ni40Ti40Nb20 
alloy has been widely studied as a eutectic alloy composed 
of body-centered cubic (BCC) and B2 phases. Based on this 
alloy, use Fe and Hf partially to replace Ni and Ti, respec-
tively. Fe and Hf are both elements used to stabilize the B2 
phase in the previous work. Accordingly, the nominal com-
position of Ti30Ni30Fe10Hf10Nb20 was obtained. Directional 
solidification technique was used to optimize the microstruc-
ture. The relationship between microstructures and mechani-
cal properties was studied for a directionally solidified 
 Ti30Ni30Fe10Hf10Nb20 (at.%) EHEA. It is indicated that the 
EHEA sample directionally solidified at 60 mm/h exhibits 
a directional eutectic microstructure composed of ordered 
body-centered cubic (B2) phase Ti–Ni–Fe–Hf–Nb matrix 
and body-centered cubic (BCC) phase Nb solid solution, 
accordingly showing a high tensile strength over 1400 MPa 
and super elasticity over 3%.
2 Materials and Methods
Ingots with a nominal composition of Ti30Ni30Fe10Hf10Nb20 
(at.%) EHEA were prepared by arc melting the mixture of 
elements (purities higher than 99.9 wt%) in a Ti-gettered 
high-purity argon atmosphere. In order to obtain the chemi-
cal homogeneity, the TiHfNb intermediate alloy was firstly 
prepared, and final ingots were remitted five times. The 
cylindrical rods with a diameter of 8 mm and a length of 
150 mm were prepared using a copper mold tilting-cast 
method in Edmund-Buhler AM/0.5 high vacuum arc-melt-
ing system. The as-cast rods were used for the directional 
solidification conducted in an FZ-T-12000-X-VP-S type 
optical floating zone furnace at different growth rates. The 
microstructures were characterized by means of X-ray dif-
fractometer (XRD; Rigaku D/max-2500PC) with Cu- Kα 
radiation, electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD; Tescan 
MIRA3) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM; ZEISS 
Supra 55 and Tescan MIRA3) equipped with an energy dis-
perse spectrometer (EDS) and backscattered electron image 
(BSE). Tensile samples with a gauge length of 14 mm and a 
cross-section dimension of 2.5 mm × 1.2 mm were cut from 
the tilting-cast and direction-solidified samples and tested 
on an electronic universal testing machine (Instron 5582) 
with contact extensometer (Instron 2620-601) operated at a 
strain rate of 10  s−4.
3 Results and Discussion
Figure 1 shows the XRD patterns and SEM images of four 
as-cast specimens prepared using tilting-cast (TC) and 
direction-solidification (DS) methods, respectively. All 
the specimens contain B2 and BCC phases with no other 
phases detected in Fig. 1a. As shown in Fig. 1b–h, all SEM 
images display the white and black phases. Meanwhile, 
EDS technology further confirmed that Nb is enriched in 
the white phase while Ti, Ni, Hf and Fe are segregated in 
the black one, as shown in Fig.  1i. Combining XRD and 
EDS results, it can be inferred that the white phase is BCC 
phase and the black one is B2 phase. As a comparison, the 
TC EHEA specimens display a few white dendrites and 
typical lamellar eutectic microstructure, as shown in the 
inset of Fig.  1b. The width of the BCC phase lamellar is 
determined to be approximately 20 nm due to rapid solid-
ification. However, different microstructures are shown 
in the DS EHEA specimens prepared at a growth rate 
of 60 mm/h, as shown in Fig.  1c–h. In the cross-section, 
many clusters can be seen at low magnification (Fig.  1d). 
The details in one cluster with a size of ~ 240 µm can be 
seen in Fig.  1e at high magnification, where the center 
contains Nb fibers and the outside is in the lamellar eutec-
tic microstructure with different lengths [23]. The longi-
tudinal section was observed for this specimen. It is seen 
that the columnar grains were aligned along the DS direc-
tion. Figure  1f shows the details of one columnar grain, 
which looks like catkin. Such directional microstructure is 
regarded as catkin-like microstructure (CLMS), hereafter 
the same.
It is carefully found out that the width or diameter of 
BCC phase is about 200 nm in DS specimens, far larger 
than that of BCC phase in TC specimens. It is implied that 
the Nb BCC-phase coarsens significantly in the directional 
solidification at a growth rate of 60 mm/h, which leads 
to the variation of growth condition at the solid/liquid 
interface. Accordingly, the microstructure evolves into 
the lamella eutectic patterns. Such the fiber-lamella tran-
sition is clearly shown in Fig.  1c. In this transition, some 
perturbation occurred to induce the dissolution of the Nb 
BCC-phase as marked by purple cycles in Fig.  1c. The 
lamella eutectic and fiber one, as marked by red outline 
and yellow outline, respectively, in Fig.  1c, were further 
confirmed from the different cutting angles, as shown in 
Fig. 1g and h, respectively.

cation, as shown in Fig. 2. Figure 2a–c shows the morphol-
ogies parallel to the growth direction at different growth 
rates of 1 mm/h, 60 mm/h and 180 mm/h, respectively.

Since the first HESMA was reported by Firstov et al. 
[13], up to now, most reported HESMAs have been tested 
in compression [14, 15, 17, 18]. With our knowledge, the 
present Ti30Ni30Fe10Hf10Nb20 EHEA was firstly reported to 
possess high strength and superelasticity in tension, which 
exhibits tensile strength as high as 1409 MPa and superelas-
ticity as large as 3.1%. Regardless of measurement meth-
ods, the mechanical properties displayed herein are superior 
to those of most reported HESMAs [13– 15, 17, 18]. The 
unique columnar eutectic microstructure is responsible, 
which formed in the directional solidification at a growth 
rate of 60 mm/h. First of all, Saghaian et al. found that crys-
tal orientation largely determines the superelasticity and the 
strength of shape memory alloys [26]. In their work, among 
the three crystal orientations, [001], [011] and [111], the 
[011] orientation displayed the largest superelasticity and the 
highest strength in compression cycle tests at 140–220 °C 
temperatures, meaning that the [011] orientation is the pre-
ferred orientation in Ni50.3Ti29.7Hf20 SMA and greatly rein-
force superelasticity and strength. In addition, the 60 mm/h 
DS alloy exhibits the largest superelasticity and the high-
est strength in the tensile test, because only in the 60 mm/h 
DS alloy do the crystals take the [011] as the orientation 
whatever in BCC or B2 phases, (Fig.  4e). Thus, the large

As discussed in other previous works, the introduction 
of multicomponent makes NiTi-like B2 phase with the ele-
vated activation energy for deformation-induced martensitic 
transformation due to HEA’s core effects, which contributes 
to high strength as well as superelasticity and SME [18]. 
Furthermore, the involvement of Nb-rich BCC phase has a 
strong plastic deformation ability, which can stabilize the 
deformation behavior and avoid catastrophically premature 
fracture caused by brittleness of high-strength B2 phase. In 
addition, it is well-known that the B2 phase is quite brittle, 
which usually shows a premature fracture under loading, 
while the Nb-rich BCC phase is soft to possess the strong 
plastic deformation capacity. In the column-like eutectic 
microstructure involving B2 and Nb-rich BCC phases, the 
soft BCC phase will prevent the B2 phase catastrophically 
fracturing because it is beneficial to release the stress con -
centration through its plastic deformation. Meanwhile, the 
strength of BCC phase will enhance due to the confinement 
of B2 phase. In such way, the deformation and phase trans-
formation of BCC phase and B2 phase are well matched. 
Thus, synergetic effect of lamellar or fiber eutectic micro-
structure involving soft BCC phase and high strength B2 
phase enables the specimens to show excellent mechanical 
properties [19]. Besides, the morphology of Nb-rich BCC 
phase is crucial to maintaining high strength and superelas-
ticity, as described above. Big-size Nb-rich BCC phase is 
prone to plastic deformation, leading to the alloy losing the 
superelasticity. Herein, size effect and confinement effect 
in eutectic structure are presented in Nb-rich lamella or 
fibers, which make it difficult to plastic deformation so as 
to show its own ultra-elasticity. Directional solidification 
is an important technology to obtain a directional and fine 
eutectic structure, as directional microstructure of CLMS 
formed in the present work. The present results imply that 
the formation of the directional eutectic structure is very 
sensitive to the growth condition for EHEAs due to the com-
plicated element diffusion at the solidification interface. It 
needs the in-depth investigation to understand the underlying 
mechanism. In a word, directional solidified eutectic high 
entropy alloy provides a novel and effective way to design 
high performance shape memory alloys with enhanced SME 
and superelasticity.
4 Conclusion
In summary, a novel Ti30Ni30Fe10Hf10Nb20 directionally 
solidified eutectic high-entropy alloy with large elastic 
strength and high elastic limit was studied. The samples were 
prepared in the range of growth rate from 1 to 180 mm/h. 
The microstructure is dependent on the growth rate. When 
the growth rates were 1 mm/h and 180 mm/h, the samples 
presented equiaxed eutectic clusters and formed large Nb-
rich BCC phase particles between the eutectic clusters. The 
samples at a growth rate of 60 mm/h show a directional 
columnar eutectic microstructure parallel to the DS direc-
tion, named catkin-like microstructure with fiber eutectic in 
the center and lamella eutectic in the outside. In the tension 
tests, the 60 mm/h specimens show the highest elastic limit 
of 1409 MPa and the largest elastic strain of 3.1% without 
yield phenomenon, while the 1 mm/h alloy and 180 mm/h 
specimens as well as the tilting-cast specimens yield at 
708–881 MPa with maximum elastic strains of 0.6–1.2%. 
The achievement of high tensile strength and large super -
elasticity is attributed to the synergy effect of BCC/B2 in the 
directional, fine eutectic structure and preferred orientation. 
Big-size Nb-rich BCC phase is prone to plastic deformation 
so that apparent yielding occurs at a relatively low stress to 
deteriorate the superelasticity. These findings show a novel 
and effective way to prepare the high-performance shape 
memory alloy using the directionally solidified EHEAs.
