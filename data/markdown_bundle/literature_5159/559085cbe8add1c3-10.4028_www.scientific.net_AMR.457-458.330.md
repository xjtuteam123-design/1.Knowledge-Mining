Effect of Aluminide Coating on Rapture Behavior of Ni-Based 
Superalloy GTD-111 in high temperature

Keywords: Ni-based superalloy GTD-111, Aluminide coating, Rapture 
Abstract. GTD-111 superalloys are of the newest Ni-based sup eralloys which because of having 
good stability and strength in high temperatures ar e used in manufacturing of hot-gas-path parts of 
the gas turbines. Because of severe working conditi ons, application of these superalloys without 
coating isn't recommended. For this purpose, firstly the superalloy is coated and then is used. In this 
paper, the effect of Aluminide coating on rapture b ehavior of the GTD-111 superalloy is 
investigated. For this purpose, a number of samples made from GTD-111 superalloy have been 
coated by powder cementation method and have been s ubjected to rapture test and have been 
compared to uncoated samples. The results suggest t hat the presence of coating causes increase of 
Al content in the surface and contributes in stabil ity of γ´ which is the most important factor of 
superalloy strength and also causes more life cycle of this superalloy with respect to similar 
uncoated samples. 
Introduction 
The Ni-based superalloys GTD-111 were designed in m id-1970s by GE company and have been 
considered as a replacement for IN738LC superalloy [1]. The rapture resistance of this alloy is about 
20 
oC higher compared to IN738LC. Moreover, the low cyc le fatigue resistance and the corrosion 
resistance of the GTD-111 are also better than that in IN738LC [1]. The major constituent elements 
of the GTD-111 superalloy are Mo, W, Ta, Cr and Co which raise the mechanical and corrosion 
properties of the alloy and its microstructure is m ultiphase and includes γ matrix, first and second 
γ´sedimentary phases, different carbides, γ´-γ eute ctic and harmful TCP phases [3]. This superalloy 
is used in first row blades of the gas turbine and engines of the advanced airplanes like jet engines, 
such that nowadays about 50% of engine weight in th ese airplanes is made of this superalloy. But 
regarding the use of these superalloys in high temp eratures and for long duration, the employment 
of these superalloys without coating is not economi cal. For this purpose, they are coated and then 
are used [4]. Aluminide coatings are amongst the mo st common coatings used on these superalloys 
which are manufactured using the powder cementation method and have a good connection to the 
sublayer [5]. The studies of fusion aluminide coati ngs suggest that there are only two mechanisms 
for formation of the coating. First, the inward fus ion of the Al and then outward fusion of the Ni in 
next heat treatment, and the second type, the outwa rd fusion of the Ni in entire of the coating 
process duration. The main reason is that only the Ni or Al atoms can penetrate inside the aluminide 
Nickel phase [6]. On this basis, the aluminide coat ings are divided into two categories based on 
their morphology: 
The low-activity high-temperature aluminizing process (LAHT) 
The high-activity low-temperature aluminizing process (HALT) 
In HALT, the coating temperature is about 1223 °C a nd the main phase is β-NIAL with outward 
growth mechanism and in LTHA the coating temperatur e is 1323 °C and the main phase is 
NI2AL3 δ - with inward growth mechanism [5].

One of the most important practical disadvantages o f the aluminide coating is its low resistance 
against the corrosion resulted from the sulfide com pounds. These coatings are decayed gradually 
after losing constituent elements of the protecting shell because of the fusion towards the sub layer 
and oxidation. Hence, the modification of the alumi nide coatings with addition of the elements like 
Cr, Pt, Si and other noble gases has caused many investigations [7]. 
In this paper, the impact of the applied coating on rapture behavior of the GTD-111 superalloy 
was investigated. 
Materials and Method 
The investigated samples were made of Ni-based supe ralloy GTD-111 and were casted according 
the standard ASTM E139 by the method of vacuum prec ision casting with chemical composition

Then, to solve the sedimentary phases and homogeniz e the microstructure, a heat treatment was 
done as follows on the samples: 
Solution: keeping the samples in temperature 1100-1 120°C for 2 hours and reducing the 
temperature until 700°C with the rate of 30°c/m in neutral environment (Ar gas) and cooling down 
the samples until room temperature. 
Aging: to warm up the samples in temperature 845°C for 24 hours and then cooling down until 
room temperature for the purpose of the growth and nucleation of the secondary sedimentary phase. 
Then, the surface of the samples was cleaned routin ely. For this purpose, all the parts were 
washed in a mixture of ethylene and white alcohol u sing ultrasonic device with controlled 
conditions and then were placed in the degreasing bath for 10 minutes. 
In order to investigate the microstructure of the p rovided superalloy, the samples were mounted 
after heat treatment and polished electromechanical ly and then for unveiling the microstructure, the 
Marble etching solution with following composition was used. 
HCL 37 %( 50ml) + CuSO4 (10gr) + H2O (50ml) 
The aluminide coating was of low activity high temp erature (LAHT) type and was implemented 
using powder cementation method as follows: 
The coating materials include 6-7.5 Wt% Al, 0.5-0.7 Wt% aluminium chloride as activator and 
the remaining content was alumina powder (with cert ain size and form) as the filler. Then, the 
samples and the powder mixture which had been subje cted to mixing process for 24 hours were 
placed inside metal boxes made of refractory steel. Finally, these packs were placed in the furnace 
in temperature 1050-1090°C for 4 hours under noble gas atmosphere for fusion treatment and then 
the samples were cooled down in the air via sandblast and were cleaned via ultrasonic method. 
The rapture test was provided in temperature 1000°C and in stress 200MPa according to ASTM 
E139 and was implemented for coated and uncoated sa mples. Also the microscopic investigations 
of the surfaces of samples were carried on using op tical microscope (Olympus CK40M) and SEM 
microscope (WEGA/TESCAN). 
The phase analysis of the samples was performed by X-ray diffraction method using Bruker 
device made of Germany in the angular range of 35-1 00 and using K αCu beam and Ni filter in 
ambient temperature.

In Fig.3 some images of the sample surface which ar e captured using optical microscope have 
been shown. As one can see in this Fig., two differ ent aluminide coatings have been formed on the 
samples. The investigations suggest the outer layer of the coating contains β-NIAL phase with

According to investigations performed by optical mi croscope, the thickness of the obtained 
coating is about 55-58 µm which is suitable for thi s coating and also no warm crack was observed 
in the coating and fusion area of the coating. The warm crack phenomenon is one of the important 
issues in casting of some superalloys which causes severe and sometimes irreparable defects. The 
superalloy GTD-111 is also amongst the superalloys which are sensitive to warm crack defect. And 
fortunately, regarding the microscopic studies, no warm crack was observed in the coating.

To obtain the rapture life, the samples were subjec ted into rapture test which the results are 
shown in Fig. 4. 
 
Fig. 5-The results of rapture test of coated and uncoated samples in temperature of 1000°C and stress of 300MPa 
Regarding the results, one can see that the coated samples (sample 3, 4) have sustained much 
more rapture life than similar uncoated samples (sample 1, 2). In fact, the reason is that the presence 
of the coating on the superalloy surface causes inc rease of Al percent on the surface and contributes 
in stability of the phase γ´, and since presence of this phase in austenite matrix is the most important 
factor for strength of this alloy we will observe h igher life in coated samples. Also this layer is 
capable of protecting the sublayer through oxidation of the aluminide nickel phase and formation of 
aluminium oxide on the superalloy surface which pro tects the superalloy surface against the 
oxidation and hot corrosion and raises the rapture resistance of the superalloy. 
Summary 
Application of the aluminide coating with LAHT meth od causes an increase in rapture strength and 
life cycle of the GTD-111 superalloy with respect to uncoated state. 
The maximum mechanical strength of the superalloy was obtained at temperature 750°C. 
The implementation of the aluminide coating on the GTD-111 Ni-based superalloy causes an 
increase in the mechanical properties, specially the rapture in temperatures higher than 1000°C. 
The fusion aluminide coating causes an increase in stability of the strength-giving phase of γ´ and 
hence causes an increase in mechanical properties of the GTD-111 superalloy.
