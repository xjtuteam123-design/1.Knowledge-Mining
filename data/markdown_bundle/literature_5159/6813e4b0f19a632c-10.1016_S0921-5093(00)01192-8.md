Formation, microstructure and mechanical properties of
Al–Fe base quasicrystalline alloys
H.M. Kimura, K. Sasamori, A. Inoue
Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, Japan
Received 20 September 1999; accepted 17 February 2000
Abstract
An icosahedral (I) phase in coexistence with Al-phase was formed in the size fraction range up to 125 mm of atomized Al93Fe3Cr2Ti2
powder. The decomposition temperature of the I-phase is about 790 K. As per energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy analysis, the constituent
composition of the I-phase is Al 84:2Fe7:0Cr6:3Ti2:5. It is found that the Al 84:2Fe7:0Cr6:3Ti2:5 alloy forms a mostly single I-phase in the
melt-spun state. Bulk I-alloys in a rod form were produced by extrusion of the atomized powders at 673 K and an extrusion ratio of 10.
The extruded Al
93Fe3Cr2Ti2 alloys have good mechanical properties, i.e.,  0:2 of 550 MPa,  UTS of 660 MPa and "p of 4.5% at room
temperature and  0:2 of 330 MPa, UTS of 350 MPa and"p of 1.5% at 573 K. The I-based Al93Fe3Cr2Ti2 alloy is expected to be useful as
a new type of high-elevated temperature strength material. © 2000 Elsevier Science B.V . All rights reserved.

1. Introduction
Since the ﬁrst synthesis of an I-phase in a rapidly solidiﬁed
Al86Mn14 alloy in 1984 [1], followed by interpretation of the
I-phase as a quasicrystal [2], great efforts have been devoted
to the synthesis, structure, mechanical strength, deformation,
physical properties and chemical properties of the quasicrys-
talline (QC) alloys. Although a number of QC-alloys without
second phase have been found as metastable and thermody-
namically stable phases in Al-, Mg-, Ga- and Ti-based sys-
tems [3], little is known about useful mechanical properties
of the I-based alloys. The stoichiometric I-alloys have high
hardness and extremely brittle nature owing to the difﬁculty
of dislocation movement in the quasiperiodic lattice with-
out long-range periodicity [4]. However, if the nanoscale
I-particles are dispersed homogeneously in a ductile matrix,
the mixed phase alloy is expected to exhibit high strength
combined with good ductility. Furthermore, it has been re-
ported that the stoichiometric I-phase has higher resistance
to plastic deformation in the temperature range up to 1200 K
[5]. The high resistance also indicates the possibility of
the mixed phase alloy having a high-elevated temperature
strength. We have performed a series of studies on the forma-
tion, structure and mechanical properties of Al-based alloys
containing nanoscale I-particles [6–9]. It is important to ﬁnd
an I-phase in a new Al-based system containing inexpensive

structure and properties of Al–Fe based alloys containing Ti,

Al–Fe base system is important for future development as

have found that a mostly single I-phase is formed in rapidly
solidiﬁed Al–Fe–Cr–Ti and Al–Fe–Mn–Ti alloys. This paper
is intended to present the formation, structure, thermal stabi-

2. Experimental procedure
Al-based alloy ingots in Al–Fe–Cr–Ti system were pre-
pared by arc melting the mixture of pure metals in an argon
atmosphere. Rapidly solidiﬁed ribbons with a cross-section
of 0.2 mm1.5 mm were produced by melt spinning. At-
omized alloy powders were produced by high-pressure gas
atomization. The bulk rod alloys were produced by warm
extrusion of atomized powders with a particle size frac-
tion below 26, 75 and 125 mm at 673 K and an extrusion
ratio of 10. The structure was examined by X-ray diffrac-
tion and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Tensile
strength and elongation were measured at a strain rate of
5.010
−4 s−1 with an Instron testing machine. Hardness
was measured with a Vickers hardness tester. Thermal sta-
bility of the prepared alloys was measured by differential
scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a heating rate of 0.67 K/s.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 shows the X-ray diffraction patterns of the atomized
Al93Fe3Cr2Ti2 powders with particle size fractions of 26 and
125 mm, together with the data of a melt-spun Al 84:6Cr15:4
ribbon [10] with a mostly single I-phase. The as-atomized
structure consists of Al and I-phases for the 26 mm powder
and AlCICAl23Ti9 phases for the 125 mm powder. The pre-
cipitation tendency of the Al23Ti9 compound increases with
decreasing cooling rate and hence the contribution of Ti to
the formation of the I-phase seems to be lower as compared
with Fe and Cr. The diffraction peaks of the I-phase agree
with those for the Al–Cr alloy, suggesting that the I-phase in
the Al
93Fe3Cr2Ti2 powder reﬂects the formation tendency
of the Al–Cr I-phase. We measured the thermal stability of
the I-phase in the <26 and <125 mm powders by DSC. An
exothermic peak is clearly seen for both powders and the on-
set temperature (T
x) is in the vicinity of 790 K. No distinct
change in the onset temperature with powder size fraction
is seen. The <26 mm powder annealed for 3.6 ks at 900 K
has a mixed structure of Al CAl
13Cr2CAl13Fe4CAl23Ti9
and hence the exothermic reaction is due to the transition
from the I-phase to Al13Cr2CAl13Fe4CAl23Ti9 phases. The
Tx of Al-based amorphous alloys with solute contents of
7–10 at.% lies in the range of 450–530 K [11] and hence the
decomposition temperature of the I-phase is much higher
than that of the amorphous phase. The signiﬁcant difference
in the thermal stability indicates the possibility of achieving
high-elevated temperature strength for the I-based Al alloys.
Fig. 2. TEM images and selected-area electron diffraction patterns of
atomized Al93Fe3Cr2Ti2 powders with particle size fractions of <26 mm
(a–d) and <125 mm (e and f) [8].
Fig. 2 shows the bright-ﬁeld TEM images and selected-
area electron diffraction patterns of the Al93Fe3Cr2Ti2 pow-
ders with particle sizes of <26 mm (a–d) and <125 mm( e
and f ) [8]. The diffraction patterns of the nearly spheri-
cal particles have the ﬁve- and two-fold symmetries of the
I-phase. The particle size and interparticle spacing of the
I-phase are about 380 and 250 nm for the 26 mm powder
and about 420 and 270 nm for the 125 mm powder. The in-
crease in the particle size leads to the more distinct elec-
tron diffraction patterns of the ordered (F) type I-phase.
Furthermore, distinct contrast revealing a high density of
internal faults is observed in all I-particles. Considering
that the I-phase in Al–Cr binary system has the disordered
(P) type I structure, the addition of Fe and Ti appears to
cause the structural change of the I-phase from the P- to
the F-type.

5. Summary
We examined the possibility of developing a high-strength
material through the formation of a mixed structure of ICAl
phases in rapidly solidiﬁed Al
93Fe3Cr2Ti2 alloy. The mixed
ICAl phases were formed in the atomized Al 93Fe3Cr2Ti2
powders with a particle size fraction of<26 mm. The I-phase
has a high decomposition temperature of about 790 K. By
the nanobeam energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) spectroscopy
analyses, the alloy compositions of the I-particles were de-
termined to be Al
84:2Fe7:0Cr6:3Ti2:5. The melt spinning of
the alloy caused a mostly single I-phase with a grain size of
250 nm. By extruding the atomized I-based powders, bulk
I-based alloys in a cylindrical rod form were prepared. The
bulk I-based Al
93Fe3Cr2Ti2 alloy exhibits good mechani-
cal properties exceeding 550 MPa for  0:2 and 660 MPa for
 UTS at room temperature. Furthermore, the alloy has the
 UTS of 350 MPa at 573 K, which is maintained even af-
ter annealing for 100 h at 573 K. The high-elevated temper-
ature strength is promising for the development of a new
high-strength material at elevated temperatures.
