A thorough TEM analysis has been carried out to study the dislocations
cutting into γ′ phase in a single-crystal superalloy during uniaxial tensile creep
under high-temperature and low-stress conditions. It is proved that the a 〈100〉
edge superdislocation originates from the interfacial a 〈100〉 dislocations and
moves into the γ′ phase by pure climbing. And the dissociation of the a 〈100〉
superdislocation core into two a/2 〈101〉 superpartial dislocations during uniax-
ial tensile creep has been identi ﬁed by HRTEM method for the ﬁrst time.
Keywords: Ni-based single-crystal superalloys; creep; HRTEM; dislocation
structures; atomic structure; superdislocation
1. Introduction
Nickel-based single-crystal superalloys are key materials for advanced aeroengines due
to their excellent high-temperature mechanical properties [ 1]. For the blades fabricated
by nickel-based single-crystal superalloys, creep deformation due to the centrifugal
force along the [0 0 1] blade axis is one of the most main origins of failure during
high-temperature service, thus many studies have been carried out to elucidate the rele-
vant creep mechanism. The evolution of dislocations and γ/γ′ conﬁguration, and their
relation to creep properties under conditions of high temperature and low stress have
been studied in a number of researches [
2–8]. Both dislocation networks and rafting
structure are completely formed at the early stage of creep and they may remain for a
long time during secondary creep. Once the rafting structure was formed, the horizontal
γ matrix channels are isolated from each other, thus recovery process by annihilation of
interfacial dislocations with opposite Burgers vectors was hindered [
6]. Considering
these effects, there must be some other mechanisms for the recovery process during
creep of superalloys so that creep deformation could go on, and cutting of the γ′ phase
is an obvious way to facilitate the recovery process.
Nickel-based single-crystal superalloy is composed of L1
2-ordered γ′ hardening
phase and disordered FCC γ matrix [ 1]. Many previous studies showed that the

been found during creep, where the temperature is not too high and the applied stress is
not too low [
9–12]. During creep under conditions of high temperature and low stress,
a new cutting mechanism by a 〈100〉 superdislocations has received extensive attention

In 1985, cutting of L1 2-ordered γ′ phase by a 〈100〉 superdislocations was ﬁrst
reported by V eyssiere and Douin [ 13] in single-crystal Ni 3Al. Soon afterwards, Louchet
and Ignat [ 14] observed a prismatic dislocation in nickel-based single-crystal superalloy
CMSX-2 after compression deformation at 975 °C; they considered these sessile edge
dislocations as b =2 a〈001〉 and proposed a non-planar core structure. In the following
years, a series of studies on a 〈100〉 dislocations have been conducted by Eggeler et al.
[
15–18] on the CMSX-4 and CMSX-6 single-crystal superalloys under double-shear
creep conditions. Conventional TEM studies con ﬁrmed that a 〈100〉 superdislocation cut-
ting into the γ′ phase is formed by a combination of two matrix a/2 〈101〉 dislocations.
And the only high-resolution TEM observation [ 18] revealed the non-compacted core
structure of these a 〈100〉 superdislocations which were considered to move by gliding,
thus a ‘self-fed’ vacancy equilibrium mechanism and a ‘non-self-fed’ vacancy equilib-
rium mechanism were proposed for the combined process of glide and climb during
superdislocation motion.
Though the macroscopic deformations are the same for shear and uniaxial creep
testing, it is not clear whether the micromechanisms of creep are the same for these two
deformation modes; therefore, the a 〈100〉 superdislocations should be studied individu-
ally during [001] tensile creep. Up to now, many studies [
19–26] have been conducted
to con ﬁrm the formation of a 〈100〉 superdislocations during uniaxial tensile creep under
high-temperature and low-stress conditions, but the dislocation con ﬁgurations are com-
plicated and the motion mechanisms of the superdislocations are still unclear. In the
LEK94 [
22], single-crystal superalloy, a 〈100〉 (the notation, introduced by Link and
Epishin [ 21]), is used because when [001] is the tensile axis, the third index plays a
special role. Accordingly, {h k l} (or 〈uv w〉) superdislocations were formed by the
reaction of matrix dislocations. While in the CMSX-10 [ 21] and the TMS-138 [ 23]
single-crystal superalloys, edge type a 〈100〉 and a 〈001〉 superdislocations have been
found, respectively, and it was considered that these edge superdislocations move by
pure climb. In addition, the latest study by Sarosi [
25] showed that both types of a
〈100〉 dislocations are formed during [001] tensile creep under 1100 °C/140 MPa. The
dislocation core structure of the a 〈100〉 superdislocations has been studied through mod-
elling; however, only Kohler et al. [ 27] have conducted simulation on edge a 〈100〉
superdislocations, and the MD simulation results showed that the a 〈100〉 edge superdis-
locations along the 〈011〉 direction dissociate asymmetrically in L1 2 structure. The
asymmetrical dissociation corresponds to a dissociation of the a 〈100〉 superdislocation
into two a/2 〈101〉 superpartial dislocations on two intersecting 〈111〉 glide planes that
enclose an acute angle, and the superpartial dislocations could further dissociate into
two Shockley partial dislocations with stacking faults in between.
Based on these previous researches, the dislocation type and motion of a 〈100〉
superdislocations have been studied widely in different superalloys. However, different
single-crystal superalloys after [001] tensile creep show different behaviours of a 〈100〉
superdislocations and the experimental study of a 〈100〉 superdislocation core structure

The nominal composition of the DD6 single-crystal superalloy used in the present study

3. Results and discussion
During high-temperature and low-stress creep, rafting structure was well formed even at
the initial stage of secondary creep, and some dislocations could be observed in the
lamellar γ′ phase. Figure 1 shows the TEM images of a typical superdislocation cutting
into the γ′ phase, and the dislocation line is right-angle shaped with the line vector u1 =
[0/C2211], u2 = [001] and u3 = [011] as shown in Figure 1a. Previous studies have con ﬁrmed
the diffraction contrast of a 〈100〉 edge-type dislocations: for g·b = 0 and g·(b × u)=0 ,
i.e. g//u, the dislocation is fully out of contrast; while for g·b = 0 but g·(b × u) ≠ 0, a
double-line residual contrast could be observed; and for g·b ≠ 0 and g·(b × u) ≠ 0, we
can get non-vanishing main contrast and the dislocation appears as a single line. The
behaviour of diffraction contrast in Figure
1b–f matches well with b = a[100], which is
perpendicular to the three line segments, so the superdislocation is an edge a[100] dislo-
cation. The three segments lie along different directions on the same (100) plane, thus
it is suggested that the edge a[100] superdislocation can only move by climbing in the
(100) plane. Additionally, it can be observed that the two ends of superdislocation are
connected to the γ/γ′ interfacial dislocation networks, so the motion of the a[100]
superdislocation in γ′ may lead to the recovery of interfacial dislocations during creep.

Based on these results, we can infer that a 〈100〉 superdislocations cutting into the γ′
phase are formed by bowing out of interfacial a 〈100〉 dislocations, and the a 〈100〉
superdislocations move in γ′ by pure climbing. The structure of the interfacial disloca-
tion network has been studied in our previous work [ 7], 60° mixed a/2 〈101〉 interfacial
dislocations will gradually rotate to edge orientation and react together to form edge a/2
〈110〉 dislocations, and the following reaction between two mutually perpendicular a/2
〈110〉 edge dislocations forms the a 〈100〉 edge dislocations. During the climb of a 〈100〉
superdislocation, the two ends of the dislocation are still connected to the γ/γ′ interfacial
dislocation network, so the climb of a 〈100〉 superdislocation may lead to the annihila-
tion of interfacial dislocations and relax the back stress. As a result, the climb of a
〈100〉 superdislocations could reduce the interfacial dislocation density and cause
additional dislocation motion in the γ matrix which contributes to the increase in creep
strain.

4. Conclusions
In summary, the a 〈100〉 edge superdislocation cutting into the γ′ phase has been
identiﬁed in single-crystal superalloy DD6 during uniaxial tensile creep under high-
temperature and low-stress conditions and the core structure of the a 〈100〉 superdisloca-
tion has been studied by HRTEM for the ﬁrst time. It is proved that the a 〈100〉 edge
superdislocation originates from interfacial a 〈100〉 dislocations and could move only by
pure climbing. In addition, the HRTEM results indicate a dissociation of the a 〈100〉
superdislocation into two a/2 〈101〉 superpartial dislocations and a combined process of
coupled glide and climb of individual a/2 〈101〉 superpartial dislocations has been
suggested for the pure climb of the whole a 〈100〉 edge superdislocation.
