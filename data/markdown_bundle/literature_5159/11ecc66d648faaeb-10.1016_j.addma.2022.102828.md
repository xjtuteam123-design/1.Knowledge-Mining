ABSTRACT 
This research aims to study the laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) processability, the microstructure and the tensile 
mechanical properties of an AlFeCr alloy at a wide range of temperatures. With this goal, Al-5Fe-6Cr (wt%) pre- 
alloyed powder was produced by casting and gas atomization. The microstructure of the as-atomized powders 
and the as-built specimens was characterized via scanning electron microscopy, x-ray diffraction, and trans -
mission electron microscopy. Following a parameter optimization study, dense as-built specimens with a high 
relative density of 99.8% and a Vickers hardness at room temperature of 192 HV were fabricated. In addition, the 
newly developed AlFeCr alloy exhibits yield strength values of 273 ± 5 MPa and 179 ± 3 MPa at 300 º C and 
400 º C, respectively. The exceptional strength of this alloy at high temperature is attributed to the homogeneous 
precipitation during processing of large density of nanoscale icosahedral (i-phase) and intermetallic phases, 
which are endowed with high thermal stability. These values are significantly higher than those corresponding to 
the conventional wrought Al7075 aluminum alloy in the T6 condition and to all Al alloys manufactured by L-PBF 
to date. The current research sheds guidelines for the design of high strength aluminum alloys containing 
transition alloying elements that are suitable to be processed by L-PBF for high temperature structural appli -
cations in a cost effective way. 
1. Introduction 
Laser-powder-bed based fusion (L-PBF), an additive manufacturing 
(AM) layer-by layer build-up process, is emerging as a promising 
fabrication technology for metallic products in many industrial sectors, 
including aerospace, automotive, electronics and biomedicine [1] . The 
main advantages of L-PBF are that it allows fabricating complex, lighter 
components with geometries that once seemed impossible and without 
the need of expensive molds or tooling. Other advantage includes the 
possibility to consolidate components into one single part with overall 
better structural performance [2] . Additionally, L-PBF processes, 
involving high cooling rates, of the order of 10
4 
to 10
7 
K/s [3] , and a 
complex multi-cycle thermal history, can produce unprecedented mi -
crostructures and properties, that are not achievable via conventional 
routes. 
However, the number of metallic materials that can be processed by 
L-PBF into components with a robust structural behavior is still rather 
limited [4] . In particular, among the commercial aluminum alloys, only 
the low performance AlSi casting system, including AlSi7Mg, AlSi10Mg, 
and AlSi12, can be reliably printed [5] . The high strength 
age-hardenable aluminium alloys belonging to the 7xxx and 2xxx series 
can in general not be efficiently processed by L-PBF, as the 
non-equilibrium melting and solidification processes involved often 
result in undesirable microstructures with large columnar grains and 
with a propensity for cracking [6] . In addition, during L-PBF processing 
under vacuum conditions, common alloying elements such as Zn, Mg, 
and Li, which form the age hardening phases, are highly volatile and 
evaporate preferentially, thus decreasing their high precipitation 
strengthening potential [1] . Other challenges associated to the additive 
manufacturing of Al alloys are the high reflectivity of the aluminium 
powder as well as its high reactivity with oxygen, which leads to the 
growth of surface oxide films that give rise to an increased density of 
defects in the printed samples [7] . The high demand for lightweighting 
of structural and engine components in transportation vehicles, trig -
gered by the urgent need to reduce energy consumption and CO
2 
emissions, requires developing new aluminium alloy systems with high 
thermal stability and improved strength at a wide range of temperatures, 
and which are printable and affordable.

A traditional approach for improving the thermal stability and thus 
the high temperature mechanical behavior of Al-base alloys is alloying 
with transition metals (TMs). TMs with low diffusion coefficients in solid 
aluminum are particularly effective as their slow diffusivity retards 
undesirable high temperature microstructure transformations, such as 
grain growth and coarsening of intermetallic phases, which lead to a 
deteriorioration of the mechanical behavior in most Al alloy systems. 
[8] . TMs like Fe, Cr, Zr, and Mn present considerably slower diffusivity 
in Al than Cu, Mg, Zn and Si, and are thus good candidates for the 
fabrication of high temperature Al alloys. In particular, AlFeCr based 
alloys have attracted great interest [8 – 10] , because both Fe and Cr are 
inexpensive and easily available. In addition, it has been reported [11] 
that the thermal stability of AlFeCr alloys can be noticeably further 
improved by alloying with other TMs, such as Ti, V, Nb and Ta, leading 
to a stability of the mechanical behavior even after long-time annealing 
at 400 
◦
C. The outstanding mechanical performance of these Al-TMs 
alloys is due to the presence of quasi-spherical metastable icosahedral 
particles embedded in the α -Al solid matrix [12,13] . These quasicrys -
talline phases have been extensively studied due to their unique atomic 
structures, which effectively offer a high resistance to be sheared by 
dislocations at room and high temperatures, as shearing would involve 
the destruction of the quasiperiodic lattice [14,15] . 
Al alloys containing high concentrations of TMs cannot be manu -
factured by conventional casting or by standard thermomechanical 
processing, because the large volume fraction of hard and brittle in -
termetallics that form when such fabrication routes are utilized are very 
detrimental to mechanical properties. Thus, these TM-containing alloys 
have been traditionally processed by rapid solidification techniques 
(melt spinning, gas atomization), which lead to a fine dispersion of 
phases that, in turn, give rise to very high strength and thermal stability 
[9,16 – 19] . Additive manufacturing appears as a suitable method to 
process Al-TMs alloys as the cooling rates involved are of the same order 
of magnitude as those typical of rapid solidification techniques [20 – 23] . 
The possibility of processing Al-TMs materials via additive 
manufacturing methods, which has still not been sufficiently explored, 
would open new applications in the automotive and aeronautical in -
dustries (tribological, thermal protection, heat exchangers, radiators) 
where commercial Al alloys cannot currently be used [15,24] . 
This study aims to investigate the possibility to process an AlFeCr 
based alloy with good mechanical performance by L-PBF. The proposed 
AlFeCr alloy is specifically designed to capture the benefits of the rapid 
solidification rate from the L-PBF process. The processing parameters 
were optimised to achieve simultaneously maximum density and to form 
the hardening quasicrystalline phases. The microstructure and the ten -
sile mechanical properties at room and high temperature have been 
analyzed and compared with the properties of reference alloys. 
2. Experimental procedure 
The material investigated in the present work, with a target chemical 
composition of Al-5 wt% Fe-6 wt% Cr, will be termed, for simplicity, 
AlFeCr alloy. It was processed in an induction furnace (VSG 002 DS, PVA 
TePla) from a mixture of high-purity Al (99.95 wt%), Fe (99.9 wt%) and 
Cr (99.9 wt%) pellets, which were mixed in a clay-graphite crucible and 
heated to ~1000 º C under argon atmosphere. Powders were produced 
from the as-cast ingots by gas atomization using a semi industrial Ley -
bold VIGA 2S atomizer at CENIM-CSIC (Madrid, Spain) and sieved into 
four different size ranges in a CISA RP200 N sieving machine. The 
flowability and apparent density of the powders were measured using a 
Hall flowmeter under the ASTM B-213 and ASTM B-212 – 17 standard 
test methods, respectively. 
Additive manufacturing of the AlFeCr samples was carried out using 
a Renishaw AM400 system (Renishaw plc, UK) equipped with a pulsed 
laser. The chamber was prefilled with argon in order to minimize oxygen 
contamination. A parameter optimization study was first performed 
with the aim of selecting the L-PBF processing conditions rendering both 
the high cooling rates necessary to stabilize the quasicrystalline hard -
ening phases and also the high density required to avoid catastrophic 
failure. Eight 10 × 10 × 5 mm

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructural characterization of the as-cast ingots and gas- 
atomized powder 
Fig. 2 illustrates the microstructure of the as-cast AlFeCr alloy ingots. 
EDX analysis revealed average contents of Fe and Cr of ~4.9 ± 0.2 wt% 
and 6.1 ± 0.2 wt% (Al balance), respectively. These values are very 
similar to those of the nominal target composition. The microstructure 
of the AlFeCr alloy in the as-cast condition consists of the primary α -Al 
matrix and of a distribution of large needle and plate-shaped irregular 
particles, rich in Cr and Fe, with dimensions ranging between 100 and 
700 µ m. The formation of these large particles is consistent with the 
slow cooling rate inside the induction furnace, that is estimated to be 
approximately 0.3 º C/s [27] . The presence of these second phases, which 
is consistent with earlier reports on similar systems [28,29] , is known to 
be detrimental to the mechanical properties [8] , as the large and brittle 
intermetallic particles constitute favorable sites for crack nucleation and 
propagation. Thus, although these alloys containing high concentrations 
of transition elements cannot be produced by conventional casting or 
ingot metallurgy, rapid solidification like gas atomization and additive 
manufacturing removes the problems associated to large intermetallic 
particles because, as will be discussed below, it produces finely 
dispersed phases that give rise to excellent mechanical properties. 
AlFeCr powders were produced from the as-cast ingots by gas at -
omization. Powder particles with diameters (d) belonging to different 
intervals ( < 20 µ m, 20 – 63 µ m , 63 – 100 µ m and 100 – 150 µ m) were 
separated by sieving and the distribution of phases in each group was 
measured by XRD. It is expected that, since smaller particles cool down 
at higher rates than larger particles, the phase distribution may vary in 
different powder fractions. Fig. 3 illustrates the portions of the dif -

phase is clearly apparent in all the powder fractions, as indicated by the

and 44.7 º . The full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the α -Al (111)

potential to be manufactured by L-PBF for demanding high temperature 
applications. The performance of this alloy could be enhanced by further

and a higher fraction of i-phase particles. 
3.7. Outlook 
Our results suggest that fabrication of Al-TM alloys by L-PBF is an 
effective approach to develop light Al alloys with outstanding high 
temperature behavior and that can be formed into complex shapes. In 
addition, these alloys do not require post-L-PBF ageing treatments, 
which is industrially desirable. However, further research is needed to 
develop heat-resistant Al-TM alloys that, additionally, exhibit a RT 
behavior that is comparable to that of the strongest age hardenable Al 
alloys and that can be manufactured by AM methods. It has been re -
ported [9,13] that addition of other TM elements such as Ti, V, Nb or Ta 
to a gas atomized and extruded AlFeCr alloy resulted in a more homo -
geneous distribution of finer icosahedral particles (with sizes around 
100 nm in the whole component), and thus in a better combination of 
properties over a wider temperature range. Research on similar material 
compositions constitutes therefore a high potential avenue for the 
fabrication of high temperature Al alloys with unprecedented mechan -
ical behavior by additive manufacturing.

this study are listed below. 
1. An optimum L-PBF processing window rendering, simultaneously, 
very high density (~99.8%) and outstanding microhardness (~192 
HV) was defined. The hardness values achieved are higher than that 
of the high-strength wrought Al 7075 alloy in the T6 condition. 
2. The outstanding hardness values achieved under optimized L-PBF 
conditions are attributed to the precipitation during L-PBF process -
ing of a high volume fraction of fine metastable i-phases. 
3. The L-PBF AlFeCr alloy exhibits an outstanding thermal stability and 
excellent mechanical performance up to temperatures as high as 
400 º C. In particular, yield strengths of ~300 MPa and ~200 MPa 
were obtained at 300 º C and 400 º C, respectively. These values are 
considerably higher than those achieved to date in any other L-PBF 
alloy and those corresponding to the high strength Al7075-T6 alloy. 
The outstanding mechanical behavior of the L-PBF AlFeCr alloy is 
attributed to the high thermal stability of the fine quasicrystalline i- 
phase particles that precipitate during processing. 
4. An additional advantage of the L-PBF AlFeCr alloy is that no post- 
processing ageing treatments are required, as all the hardening 
agents precipitate as a consequence of the rapid solidification con -
ditions associated to the additive manufacturing process.
