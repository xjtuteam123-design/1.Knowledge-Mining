1. Introduction 
Refractory multicomponent alloys (RMCAs) are advanced materials 
that consist of two or more refractory elements and possess distin -
guished properties compared to conventional alloys [ 1 – 4 ]. MoNbTaW 
and MoNbTaWV are the first RMCAs introduced by Senkov. et al. [ 5 , 6 ] 
and the high-temperature mechanical properties of these alloys are 
better than IN718 and Haynes 230 superalloys but the limitations of 
both these alloys are low plasticity and high density. In order to improve 
plasticity at ambient temperature, several attempts have been made by 
designing novel alloys. Subsequent alloys were developed by selecting 
elements from group IV of the periodic table (Ti, Zr, and Hf), group V (V, 
Nb, and Ta), and group VI (Cr, Mo, and W) [ 7 ]. Various alloys were 
processed by replacing V, Mo, and W high-density refractory elements 
with Hf, Ti, and Zr to obtain good plasticity and to reduce density. It may 
be relevant to mention here that materials with higher density are not 
desirable in the aerospace industry, a target application area for these 
alloys, since an increase in density amounts to an increase in mass for the

were shifted towards lower 2 θ as the Nb content increased, indi -

compared to that of the other three constituent elements. 
2. Dendritic microstructure was observed for all alloys. The interden -
dritic regions were enriched with Nb and Mo and dendrites were 
augmented with W and Ta. Heat treatment lowered the micro- 
segregation of elements in as-cast alloys, which was evaluated by 
calculating the segregation ratio. 
3. Increased addition of Nb led to the decrease in overall density of the 
alloys. 
4. Hardness decreased while toughness increased as Nb concentration 
increased. 
5. Enhancement of Nb reduced the compressive yield strength from 
1251 to 945 MPa and increased the fracture strain from 10 to 33 %, 
indicating a decrease in the flow stress and an improvement in 
plasticity due to increase in average grain size and broader grain size
