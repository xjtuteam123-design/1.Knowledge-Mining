Abstract 
The light and heavy rare earth elements are added to the magnesium alloys to improve 
the strengths and the creep resistance. The age hardening behaviors of the extruded 
Mg-6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr alloy aged at 200, 225 and 250 ℃ were investigated. Tensile 
tests and c reep tests of the extruded and extruded -T5 Mg-6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr were 
carried out at 150-300℃. The relationship between the microstructure and the 
properties of the extruded -T5 Mg-6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr alloy was studied . The result 
shows that the extruded Mg-6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr (contained less than 10 wt.% Gd) peak 
aged at 225 ℃ for 72h has the excellent creep resistance and high strengths with the 
UTS more than 350MPa from room temperature to 200 ℃, which are correlative with 
the precipitates. The high dense and uniform distribution of β' phase with good heat 
stability precipitates inhibit ing the dislocation motion contributes to age hardening, 
accelerates the ageing hardening response and increase s the creep resistance. The 
artificially aged (T5) at low temperature further creep tested and t ensile tested at

1. Introduction 
The magnesium alloys have attracted increasing interest in the light -weight 
component because of the superior properties, such as low density, high strength and 
stiffness [1-3]. However, the low creep resistance and poor strength s of the 
magnesium alloys at elevated temperature s limit the extensive application in the 
aerospace and automobile industry [4, 5]. 
The addition of Rare earth (RE) e lements (like Gd and Y) according to their 
solubility can improve the strengths at elevated temperatures due to solution 
hardening and precipitation hardening [6, 7]. It has been reported that the creep 
resistance of the binary Mg -Gd alloys is better than those of the WE43 alloy and 
QE22 alloy [8]. However, the Mg-Gd alloys show little or no precipitation hardening 
effect when contained less than 10 wt.% Gd. The solubility of the heavy RE elements 
(Gd) increases rapidly with increasing temperature and is large at elevated 
temperatures [6-8]. Much more content of the heavy RE elements (Gd) is needed to 
keep the precipitation hardenin g effect, which increases the cost and density of 
magnesium alloys. The addition of the ligh t RE elements like Nd can reduce the 
solubility of Gd, promote the precipitation and reduce the cost [ 9]. Meanwhile, t he 
addition of Y elements in Mg-Gd alloys could improve the aging response, which is in

## Page 4

3 
general the same as the Gd elements. Recently, the combination of different RE 
elements is an attempt to improve the heat resistance and tensile properties for the 
wide application of magnesium alloys. Most of the papers deal with the ageing 
hardening behaviors of Mg -Gd-Nd-Zr, Mg -Gd-Y-Zn-Zr and Mg -Y-Nd [1 0-12]. 
However, relatively few studies have been related to the relationship between the 
precipitates and tensile properties at elevated temperatures of the Mg -Gd-Y-Nd-Zr 
(contained less than 10 wt.% Gd ) extruded followed by ageing treatment . More 
recently, it is still unclear the effect of the precipitates on the creep behavior especially 
the creep temperature over the artificially aged temperature. 
In this study, the age hardening behaviors of the extruded Mg -6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr 
alloy aged at 200, 225 and 250℃ were investigated. Tensile tests and creep tests of the 
extruded-T5 ( peak-aged at 225 ℃) Mg -6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr were carried out . The 
precipitate behavior and microstructure evolution were investigated. The study also 
discussed the effect of the precipitates on the creep resistance and tensile properties at 
elevated temperatures of the extruded and extruded-T5 Mg-6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr alloy. 
 
2. Experimental procedures 
The magnesium alloy used in this paper was an extruded Mg-6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr 
(wt.%) (GWN6 41K). The artificially aged (T5) of the extruded GWN 641K were 
carried out at 200-250℃ with different aged times. Vickers hardness was measured by 
100g load for 15s. Tensile tests of the extruded and extruded-T5 peak-aged 
GWN641K were carried out using an Instron -5965 universal testing machine 
equipped with a three-zone split furnace at a cross-head speed of 1 mm/min at room 
temperature and 150-250℃. The creep tests were carried out using RDL1000 
electronic creep testing machine at 150-300℃ under different stresses. The tensile

3.1. Age hardening behaviors of the extruded GWN641K 
The ageing hardening behaviors of the extruded GWN641K aged at 200, 225 and 
250℃ are showed in Fig.1. The hardness value of the extruded GWN641K is 92.4Hv. 
The extruded GWN641K aged at 200℃ has no distinct hardening response until 80h. 
After 80h, t he ageing hardness increases obviously, with the peak hardness value 
about 116.1Hv obtained at 96h and increased by 25.6% compared with that of the 
extruded GWN641K . The extruded GWN641K aged at 225 ℃ has the highest peak

GWN641K exhibits the large age hardening response with the highest initial hardness 
at the early stage of ageing. Due to the increment in ageing temperature improving the 
diffusion rate of elements and accelerating the ageing hardening response, the time to 
peak hardness decreases. The peak hardness value of the extruded GWN641K aged at 
250℃ for 60h is about 118.5Hv, which is lower than that of the extruded GWN641K 
aged at 225℃ for 72h.

3.4. Discussion 
The ageing hardening behaviors of the extruded GWN641K aged at 200, 225 and 
250℃ have been investigated. The extruded GWN641K aged at 225℃ for 72h has the 
excellent high strengths from room temperature to 200 ℃, which are correlative with 
the precipitates. During ageing process, the stress concentration and the dislocations 
introduced by hot extrusion (Fig.5a) improve the diffusion rate of solute atoms and 
facilitate the nucleation of precipitates, resulting in the formation of precipitates. The 
precipitates in the extruded -T5 GWN641K (Fig. 5b) are more than those in the 
extruded GWN641K (Fig.5a). 
At the early stage of ageing, due to the formation of β'' precipitate phase [6, 17], the 
hardness of the extruded -T5 GWN641K is higher than that of the extruded 
GWN641K. As time goes on, the β'' phase is transformed to β' phase. The high dense 
and uniform distribution of β' phase keeping one orientation (Fig.5c) p rovides 
resistance to the dislocation motion and contributes to age hardening. With increasing 
the aged temperature, the ageing hardening response is accelerated and the formation 
of β'' phase is faster [18, 19]. The higher aged temperature, the highe r hardness at the 
early stage of ageing and the shorter time to peak hardness is (Fig.1). In addition, the 
secondary phase s with the higher dislocation density introduced by hot extrusion 
(Fig.5a) promote the diffusion rate of solute atoms . As reported by S.S. Vagarali [20], 
during ageing process due to the depletion of solute atoms the precipitate-free zone

At higher ageing temperature (above 225℃), due to the higher diffusion rates of the 
solute atom and grain boundaries, the phases get coarse as fast as they can, resulting 
in the reduction in the volume fraction of those in peak-aged condition (Fig.6d). As 
shown in Fig.6d, both of the grains and the secondary phases with the lowest density

the growth of the strengthening phases and the grains lead to the red uction in 
strengths of the extruded GWN641K aged at 250℃ (Fig.1 and 2).

4. Conclusions 
In the present study, the combination of different RE elements are added to the 
magnesium alloys to improve the creep resistance and the strength s. The excellent 
creep resistance and high strengths of the extruded Mg-6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr alloy are 
achieved by peak aged at 225 ℃ for 72h. The UTS of the extruded 
Mg-6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr (contained less than 10 wt.% Gd) aged at 225 ℃ for 72h is

## Page 13

more than 3 50MPa from room temperature to 2 00℃. The extruded-T5 GWN641K 
with the lower steady creep rate shows the better creep resistance than the extruded 
GWN641K below 200℃. The precipitates contribute to age hardening, accelerate the 
ageing hardeni ng response and increase the creep resistance. The creep rate of the 
extruded-T5 GWN641K is higher than that of the extruded GWN641K above 250 ℃ 
and increases with increasing stress. Dynamic precipitation in the extruded 
GWN641K during creep increases creep resistance. The artificially aged (T5) at low 
temperature further creep tested and tensile tested at higher temperatures leads to the 
small precipitates dissolving into the matrix and decreases the resistance to the 
dislocation motion, resulting in the reduction in the creep and tensile properties of the 
extruded-T5 Mg-6Gd-4Y-Nd-0.7Zr above 225℃.
