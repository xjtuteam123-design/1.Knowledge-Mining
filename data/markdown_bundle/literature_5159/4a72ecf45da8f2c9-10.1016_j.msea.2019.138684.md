Abstract 10

1. Introduction 8 
Mo-rich multiphase Mo-Si-B alloys pioneered by Berc zik [1,2] are potential 9 
candidates for high-temperature structural applicat ions beyond the capability of 10 
Ni-base superalloys since they possess high melting points (around 2000 oC), good 11 
creep resistance and oxidation resistance, and acce ptable damage tolerance [3-7]. 12 
Among the Mo-Si-B systems, the Mo-12Si-8.5B (at.%) alloy involving a Mo solid 13 
solution ( α-Mo, A2 structure) and two intermetallic compound p hases Mo 3Si (A15 14 
structure) and Mo 5SiB 2 (T2, D81 structure) was investigated widely [8-11] . An ideal 15 
microstructure consisting of a continuous α-Mo matrix with homogeneously dispersed 16 
Mo3Si/T2 particles could be achieved by powder metallurgical (PM) processing [10]. 17 
The fine-grained (FG) Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy exhibited high strength resulted from 18 
Hall-Petch effect. Its compression strength achieve d ~2.4-2.8 GPa [11,12] at room 19 
temperature and ~0.37-0.52 GPa [13] at 1300 oC. Moreover, the FG microstructure 20

However, like many nanocrystalline (NC) and ultrafi ne-grained (UFG) metal 4 
materials, although the relatively ductile α-Mo phase formed a continuous and 5 
uniform matrix, the Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy exhibited in sufficient room-temperature 6

extensive applications in the engineering field. Th e intrinsic low ductility of alloy has 8 
been attributed to the limited dislocation storage capability inside the FG α-Mo and 9 
intermetallics [13,15,16]. Therefore, improving the ductility and toughness without 10 
drastically losing the strength and oxidation resistance has been a major objective over 11 
recent decades for the FG Mo-Si-B alloys. 12 
In order to achieve good comprehensive performance and especially improve the 13 
poor ductility of NC and UFG materials, a series of methods, such as developing 14 
bimodal and multimodal structure, gradient structur e, and laminated structure were 15 
proposed [17-20]. Among them, a bimodal grain size distribution microstructure with 16 
micrometer-sized coarse grains embedded inside a ma trix of NC and UFG grains 17 
should be regarded as a reliable method. The NC and UFG matrix provided high 18 
strength meanwhile the coarse-grained (CG) grains i mproved ductility and retained 19 
high extent of deformations. Wang et al. fabricated a Cu sample with a bimodal 20

The FG 0.57 wt% La 2O3 doped Mo-12Si-8.5B alloys with bi-modally structur ed 6
