ABSTRACT 
The Ni – Cr – W based Haynes 230 alloy, is considered to have important applications in the hot-end components of 
aerospace engines due to its excellent high-temperature properties and oxidation resistance. However, Haynes 
230 alloy presents a high hot-cracking tendency during the laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) process. In this study, 
a novel composition optimized Ni – Cr – W superalloy based on Haynes 230 alloy was fabricated by the LPBF 
method. The metallurgical defects, microstructure and tensile properties at room temperature (RT) of the LPBF 
Ni – Cr – W based superalloy printed under different volume energy densities (VEDs) were systematically 
researched. The results show that the novel Ni – Cr – W superalloy presents excellent processability, and a near- 
fully dense specimen without any metallurgical defects was obtained under optimal VED of 83.33 J/mm
3
. 
However, solidification cracks also occurred in the samples with higher VEDs (exceeding 100 J/mm
3
). The main 
reason is the increase of thermal stress and liquid-feeding resistance as VED increases. The rapid scanning speed 
and steep temperature gradient during the LPBF lead to the occurrence of the multi-scale heterogeneous mi -
crostructures, including the epitaxial columnar grains with a mixing of {100} and {110} crystallographic 
alignment along the building direction (BD) and cellular substructures entangled with high-density dislocation. 
The cellular substructure strengthening and solution strengthening are the dominant factors for the excellent 
ultimate tensile strength (1100 MPa), yield strength (850 MPa), and ductility (29.3%) of the LPBF alloy at RT. 
With the increase of VED, the yield strength of the LPBF alloy decreases. The main reason is the reduction of 
cellular substructure strengthening. The ultimate tensile strength of the LPBF alloy at 1000 
◦
C reaches 200 MPa, 
which is superior to that of the LPBF Haynes 230. 
1. Introduction 
Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) is perhaps the most potential addi -
tive manufacturing (AM) technique and has attracted much more 
attention in recent years for its capacity to prepare high-precision, high- 
level densification and high-performance metal parts [ 1 , 2 ]. A series of 
metallic materials have been explored and developed in nickel-base al -
loys, such as Inconel 738LC, Inconel 625, Inconel 718, and so on [ 3 – 5 ]. 
Haynes 230 alloy (GH3230) as a typical solid-solution strengthened 
Ni – Cr – W based superalloy, has excellent oxidation resistance and high- 
temperature performance for up to 900 
◦
C [ 6 – 9 ]. Because of the 
outstanding comprehensive performance, Haynes 230 alloy has 
tremendous potential in the application of aerospace hot-end compo -
nents, but in fact, due to the extremely complex shape of these compo -
nents, it exists difficulties in processing them efficiently and easily using 
traditional methods such as casting and forging. Laser powder bed 
fusion might provide a potential pathway to solve this issue based on the 
layer-by-layer fabricating principle. 
However, due to the rapid scanning speed and steep temperature 
gradient in the LPBF process as well as the intrinsic high thermal con -
ductivity and thermal expansion coefficient of the alloy itself [ 3 , 5 ], 
there are also challenges existing in the LPBF Ni – Cr – W based superalloy. 
Zhang et al. [ 10 ] found that the LPBF pure GH3230 alloy has a high 
tendency of hot cracking. The addition of sub-micrometer TiB
2 
particles 
could eliminate hot cracking and improve the tensile strength of the 
alloy. But the addition of ceramic particles would also lead to a relatively 
lower elongation ( < 20%). Therefore, optimizing alloy composition 
might be a feasible method to improve the printability and mechanical 
properties of the LPBF Ni – Cr – W alloy.

is no consistent conclusion on the internal mechanism of cellular sub -
structures for the simultaneous improvement of strength and ductility in 
LPBF alloys [ 17 ]. After carefully exploring the microstructure and 
properties of laser powder-bed-fusion 316L stainless steel, Morris et al. 
[ 18 ] believed that the dominant contribution to yield strength is the 
cellular substructure strengthening, because the size of cellular 
substructures can better match the Hall-Petch equation rather than the 
grain sizes. Kong et al. [ 19 ] also reached the same conclusion. However, 
Li et al. [ 20 ] argued that dislocation strengthening is the dominant 
factor in determining the yield strength of additively manufactured 
stainless steels. In addition, Sui et al. [ 21 ] also emphasized the Orowan 
bypass strengthening of precipitated particles at the cellular boundaries. 
Therefore, it can be seen from the description above that the internal 
mechanism of excellent strength and elongation of the LPBF materials is 
still unclear, and the role of cellular substructures in enhancing yield 
strength is still controversial. 
As is mentioned above, the studied alloy in this paper is a novel 
composition optimized Ni – Cr – W based superalloy with Al, Ti and Nb 
added on the basis of Haynes 230 alloy. The content of C element is 
decreased. The purpose of the current work is to optimize the process 
parameters to prepare a near-fully dense LPBF Ni – Cr – W alloy, and then 
comprehensively research the microstructure and mechanical properties 
of the alloy. The evolution of metallurgical defects and their formation 
mechanisms obtained under different energy densities were investi -
gated. Determining strengthening mechanisms of the LPBF Ni – Cr – W 
samples with outstanding tensile strength and plasticity at room tem -
perature (RT) and high temperature (1000 
◦
C)were also discussed. This 
study would provide significant guidance for composition optimization 
and performance improvement of the LPBF Ni – Cr – W alloy.

4. Discussion 
4.1. The formation mechanisms of metallurgical defects 
It has been mentioned above that improper laser processing would 
lead to metallurgical defects in the LPBF alloy. With the increase of VED, 
the dominant defects in the LPBF alloy change from unfused pores to 
spherical holes and microcracks. Since the formation mechanisms of 
unfused defects and spherical holes have been widely reported in pre -
vious studies [ 15 , 33 ], only the formation mechanism of cracks will be 
discussed in detail in this paper. 
Fig. 11 (a – d) illustrate the typical crack morphology in LPBF spec -
imen with laser power of 350 W and scanning speed of 800 mm/s (VED 
= 155.55 J/mm
3
). It can be seen that hot cracks with typical dendritic 
fracture surfaces were mainly located in the middle of molten pools, 
meaning that these cracks were formed in the final stage of solidification 
and thus can be considered to be solidification cracks [ 34 ]. 
The crack rates in LPBF samples with different VEDs have been 
illustrated in Fig. 12 (a). The tendency of hot-cracking increases with the 
increase of VED. It is known that solidification cracks mainly occur in 
the mushy zone of the solidification process [ 35 ]. In this region, A so -
lidified dendritic framework appears, in which a certain percentage of 
the liquid film is trapped. The transverse shear strength of the material is 
very low and liquid feeding is quite difficult [ 36 ]. The formation of the 
interdendritic granular Laves phase would also increase the 
liquid-feeding resistance. Therefore, when the development of thermal 
shrinkage strain cannot be accommodated by liquid flow or solid, the 
dendrites would be pulled apart, and solidification cracks would appear. 
The specific formation diagram is shown in Fig. 13 . From the formation 
mechanisms of solidification crack, it can be determined that the ten -
dency of solidification crack mainly depends on the thermal stress and 
liquid-feeding resistance. Thermal stress generated during the LPBF 
process can be evaluated by the formula [ 37 ]:

4.2. The formation mechanisms of crystallographic texture 
It has been mentioned above that columnar crystal structures along

occurrence of preferential orientation will be discussed combined with 
the growth pattern of cellular substructures. 
In general, the maximum temperature gradient inside the molten 
pool is perpendicular to the pool boundary, pointing to the center of the 
molten pool, as shown in Fig. 14 (a). But in fact, the growth direction of

heat dissipation direction inside a molten pool would change because of

heat dissipation direction [ 41 ]. At the same time, for FCC crystals, [100] 
direction is the preferred growth orientation. Therefore, cellular sub -

5. Conclusions 
In this study, a near-fully dense Ni – Cr – W alloy has been fabricated by 
the LPBF method by adjusting laser energy densities. The microstructure 
evolution and tensile properties of the LPBF alloy with different VEDs 
were explored in detail. The crack formation mechanisms and the 
dominant strengthening mechanisms in the LPBF alloy were discussed. 
The main conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(1) Laser energy density plays an important role in the formation of 
metallurgical defects. With the increase of VED, the dominant 
defects in the LPBF alloy change from unfused defects to spherical 
holes and microcracks. When VED reaches 83.33 J/mm
3
, a near- 
fully dense Ni – Cr – W alloy has been fabricated. 
(2) A multi-scale heterogeneous microstructure appears in the LPBF 
Ni – Cr – W alloy, including epitaxial columnar grains with a mixing 
of {100} and {110} crystallographic alignment along the BD and 
cellular substructures entangled with high-density-dislocation. 
Nano-scale Laves particles are also formed at the cellular 
boundaries. With the increase of VED, the dimension of cellular 
substructures and columnar grains increase and the dislocation 
density in the LPBF alloy decreases. 
(3) The formation of ultrafine cellular substructures during the LPBF 
process is the dominant factor for the excellent strength (1100 
MPa) and ductility (29.3%) of the LPBF alloy at room tempera -
ture. With the increase of VED, the yield strength of the LPBF 
alloy decrease. The main reason is the reduction of cellular sub -
structure strengthening. The ultimate tensile strength of the LPBF 
alloy at 1000 
◦
C is up to 200 MPa, which is superior to that of the 
LPBF Haynes 230.
