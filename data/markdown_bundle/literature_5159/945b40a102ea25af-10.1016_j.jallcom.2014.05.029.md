Abstract 
Highly-dense ultrafine grain Mg-7.4% Al bulk samples with gra in size of about 300 nm have 
been produced by powder metallurgy through hot consolidation of mechanically alloyed 
powders. Room temperature compression tests of the consol idated bulk material reveal 
remarkable mechanical properties: high compressive strength of about 690 MPa combined with a 
plastic strain exceeding 9 %. The high strength of the a lloy can be attributed to the combination 
of three different strengthening mechanisms: grain size ref inement, dispersion strengthening and

2 
 
solid solution strengthening. The fracture surface shows trans-granular quasi-cleavage fracture in 
nature. The MgO rich regions often acted as the microcrack initiation source and thereby stop the 
main crack. Uniformly distributed intermetallic ( γ-Al12Mg17) particles in the matrix deflected 
main crack and resulted a tortuous crack path. These results demonstrate that powder metallurgy, 
mechanical alloying combined with hot consolidation is a s uitable method for the production of 
nanostructured Mg-based materials characterized by high str ength and considerable plastic 
deformation. 
1. Introduction 
Magnesium alloys have attracted the interest of the scientific community as lightweight 
structural materials due to their low density combined wit h high specific strength. However, 
relatively low strength and poor ductility limits their application [1]. Recent studies show that 
grain reﬁnement of Mg alloys is an efficient approach for enhancing both the strength and 
ductility of the material [2]. Small grains possess a lar ge fraction of grain boundary areas, which 
may impede dislocation motion, leading to the strengtheni ng of the material as described by the 
Hall–Petch relationship [3, 4]. In addition Mg has larger Tal or factor (K y value) compared to 
other metals, which also increases the strengthening effe ct [5]. The grain refinement also 
increases the ductility of Mg alloys due to the activation of non-basal slip deformation modes 
based on compatibility stresses near the grain boundaries [6, 7]. Other mechanical properties, 
such as hardness, fracture toughness and wear resistanc e also show a significant improvement 
due to the presence of ultrafine/nano-sized grains or particles [8-11]. 
Powder metallurgy is one of the techniques that can produce n ear net shape components 
from nano-crystalline powders. The tolerance of compone nts produced by powder metallurgy is

quite high, therefore no further machining or minimal machi ning is required. It can produce 
components of complicated shapes with a uniform microstr ucture with defined physical and 
mechanical properties like hardness, strength, density and porosity [9-12]. In addition, the size of 
the components are not restricted and the process leads to no or minimal scrap during the 
process. It has been demonstrated that powder metallurgy can lead to smaller grain size, 
compared to a conventional ingot metallurgy process [11-14] dem onstrating the production of 
nanocrystalline/ultrafine grain Mg-based alloys by powder metallurgy route. 
MA combined with powder consolidation is one of the solid state processing techniques 
that can be used to synthesize equilibrium, metastable p hases and nano-crystalline phases of 
commercially useful and scientifically interesting mate rials [15] from metal powders. In this 
technique, simultaneous application of pressure and temperat ure helps in achieving high density 
with minimum grain growth. The grain growth in pure Mg can al so restricted by alloying with 
Al. But beyond the solid solubility limit of Al in Mg i t forms hard and brittle intermetallic ( γ-
Al12Mg17) phase, which discontinuously precipitated along the grain boundary and leads to 
decrease the mechanical properties [16]. Through powder metal lurgy it is possible to reduce 
grain boundary precipitation/segregation of intermetallic phase, but in this process also a balance 
amount of Al is required to reduce coarsening of interme tallic phase as well as its homogeneous 
distribution in the alloy matrix [12, 13]. Accordingly i n the present work Mg-7.4%Al alloy 
composition was taken for this study because; as stated by the phase diagram this composition 
has excellent scope for solid solution as well as precipitation strengthening. 
This work is focused on the synthesis of ultrafine grain Mg-7.4%Al bulk sample by hot 
pressing followed by hot extrusion of mechanically alloyed powders. The mechanical properties 
of the bulk specimens were investigated by room temperature co mpression tests. In order to

to investigate the crack propagation mechanism. 
 
2. Experimental 
The starting alloy powder with a nominal composition Mg-7. 4%Al (wt. %) was prepared 
by mechanical alloying, details of the powder preparation and characterization is reported 
elsewhere [17]. Consolidation of the milled powders was c arried out by uni-axial hot pressing at 
673 K and 400 MPa for 10 min followed by hot extrusion under argon atmosphere at temperature 
of 673 K and pressure of 500 MPa with an extrusion ratio of 6: 1. The phases of the milled 
powder as well as of the consolidated samples were studied by X-ray diffraction (XRD) using a 
D3290 PANalytical X’pert PRO with Co-K α radiation ( λ = 0.17889 nm) in Bragg-Brentano 
configuration. The microstructure of the samples was studi ed by scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM) using a Gemini 1530 microscope and by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) using a 
FEI Tecnai F30 electron microscope. The density of the c onsolidated samples was evaluated 
using the Archimedes principle. According to the ASTM standa rd for compression testing [18], 
cylinders with an aspect ratio (length/diameter) of 2.0 (8 mm length and 4 mm diameter) were 
prepared from the extruded samples. The specimens were test ed at room temperature using an 
Instron 8562 testing facility under quasistatic compressive loading (strain rate of 8×10 -4 s -1). 
Both ends of the specimens were polished to make them parall el to each other prior to the 
compression test. The strain during tests was measured di rectly on the specimens using a Fiedler 
laser-extensometer. After the compression test the fr acture surface and microstructure of the 
fractured sample was studied using SEM and TEM.

3.1 Phase analysis and microstructure 
 The XRD pattern of the extruded Mg-7.4%Al specimen is sho wn in Fig. 1 along with the 
pattern of the parent as-milled powder. The extruded samp le consists of a Mg(Al) solid solution 
(ss) together with the γ-Al12Mg17 and MgO phases, which are already present in the as-mil led 
powder. In contrast to the pattern of the as-milled powder, no trace of residual Al is visible in the 
extruded sample. In addition, the amount of γ-Al12Mg17 phase is larger in the extruded sample, 
which suggests that the residual Al in the as-milled powder is transformed into the γ-Al12Mg17 
phase during consolidation. The diffraction peaks of the Mg(Al)ss in the extruded specimen are 
much narrower than those observed for the as-milled powde r (Fig. 1(a)). This suggests that 
significant grain coarsening takes place during hot consolidation. 
 
 The microstructure of the consolidated bulk material wa s studied by SEM, and the 
corresponding micrographs are shown in Fig. 2. The images reveal the presence of a 
homogeneous microstructure consisting of bright particles , which can be identified as the γ -
Al12Mg17 phase, dispersed in a continuous dark-gray matrix (the Mg(Al)ss). These results are 
confirmed by the EDX-SEM composition maps in Figs. 2(b)-(e ), which clearly indicates that the 
bright particles are enriched in Al with respect to th e dark matrix. Only few pores are visible 
[indicated by arrows in Fig. 2(a)], corroborating the high density of the sintered samples 
evaluated by density measurements (about 98%).

and interfacial characteristics of the γ -Al12Mg17 and MgO phases in the extruded Mg-7.4%Al

distinguish the phases the micrograph was investigated in scanning transmission electron

STEM micrograph in Fig. 3(a) shows the overview of the m icrostructure of the extruded sample. 
The microstructure consists of ultrafine grains of hcp Mg(Al)ss (dark-gray areas) as well as γ -

orientation of the grains. The nature of the MgO particle s was confirmed by EDX analysis. The 
MgO phase is distributed throughout the matrix; however, it s concentration is higher at the

giving an average grain size of 318 nm. In contrast, the size distribution of the γ -Al12Mg17 is 
much narrower, with grain size mostly ranging between 200 and 400 nm and average size of 301 
nm. Finally, the grain size of MgO is in the range of 3 to 20 nm. 
 The bright field TEM micrograph in Fig. 4(a) shows two n eighboring Mg(Al)ss grains

grain contains colonies of MgO particles starting from t he edge of the grain. The SAED pattern

arrows in Fig. 4(b)) belonging to the (110) reflection of the Mg(Al)ss matrix. Except for the (022)

3.2 Mechanical properties 
Attempts were made to test the samples in tension. Howe ver, the sample, perhaps due to 
brittleness, leads to fracture of the sample at various stages like sample preparation, fixing the 
sample in the tensile grip of instron etc. Hence only co mpression results were discussed here. 
The room temperature compression stress-strain curve for the extruded material is shown in Fig. 
5.The specimen exhibits a yield strength (offset = 0.2%) of about 450 MPa. After yielding, the 
stress increases with increasing strain and the sample exhibits pronounced work hardening up to 
690 MPa, where fracture occurs at ~ 9.6% strain. The observe d compressive strength is 
consistent with the behavior reported for different nanos tructured Mg-Al alloys produced by 
extrusion of MA powders (inset in Fig. 5), where the stre ngth increases with increasing the Al 
content. However, in contrast to previous reports [19, 20 ], the present nanostructured Mg-
7.4%Al alloy does not show any work-softening behavior and, i nstead, it displays distinct work 
hardening with increasing strain (Fig. 5). Work softening has bee n ascribed to distributed 
internal damage resulting from grain-boundary decohesion i n the material and, therefore, it can 
be considered as an inherent aspect of nanostructured Mg-bas ed alloys produced by powder 
consolidation [20]. Although additional investigations (e.g. on the effect of texture) are needed to 
clarify the reasons for this contrasting behavior, the pr esent results clearly indicate that work 
softening is not an intrinsic characteristic of ultrafine grain Mg-based materials.

A high specific strength (tensile or compressive strength divided by the density) is one of 
the most important requirements of lightweight materia ls for engineering applications. The high 
strength (690 MPa) of the present ultrafine grain material together with the reduced density (1.89 
g/cm3) gives rise to a specific strength of about 365 kNm/kg. T his value of specific strength is 
higher than those reported for other lightweight materi als such as high strength nanostructured 
Al-based alloys [23] (280 kNm/kg) and Mg-based metallic glasses [24] (290 kNm/kg). 
 
In general, the strength of Mg alloys may be influenced by the average grain size, by the 
Schmid factor for basal slip or basal texture through its effect on the activation of the slip system, 
and by the second phase through dispersion and precipitatio n strengthening [2–4]. The 
evaluation of the mechanical properties by compressive te sting shows a good combination of 
strength and plastic deformation for the present Mg-7.4%Al alloy. The attractive mechanical 
properties of this alloy can be principally attributed to the ultrafine-grained microstructure that 
results in inhibiting dislocation mobility across the grains and leads to dislocation pile-up, which 
strengthens the alloy (as described by the Hall–Petch rela tionship). Moreover, the small grain 
size promotes the activation of non-basal dislocation modes enhancing the ductility of the alloys, 
as seen also in [6, 7]. Secondly, the presence of the s econd phase intermetallic particles ( γ-
Al12Mg17) of different morphologies may lead to effective pinn ing of the dislocations. In 
addition, the presence of the homogeneously distributed nano scale MgO with average size of 7 
nm may also be an important strengthening factor. The nano MgO particles most likely originate 
from the fracture of the oxide layer formed on the surfac e of the powder particles during milling. 
The presence of fine dispersoids in powder metallurgical processed materials can help to pin or

inhibit dislocations rather than deteriorating the mech anical properties in cast alloys [5]. 
Consequently, the dislocation mobility is lowered and the strength is enhanced. 
 The influence of the grain size on the yield stress can be estimated using a standard Hall–
Petch equation of the form [3]: 
∆σ y = kd -1/2 , (1) 
where ∆ σ y is the increase in yield stress due to grain refinement, k is a constant and d is the grain 
size. From Eq. (1) an increase in the yield stress due to gr ain refinement in the extruded Mg-
7.4%Al alloy is estimated to be 384 MPa, assuming that the value of k for the Mg-7.4%Al alloy 
is the same as that of the AZ91 Mg alloys (210 MPa µm -1/2 [25]). This suggests that the 
contribution of the fine-grain strengthening mechanism to t he high strength of the extruded Mg-
7.4%Al alloy is significantly large. 
 
3.3 Fracture analysis 
Typical SEM micrographs of the fracture surface of the extruded Mg-7.4%Al alloy after 
compression test are shown in Fig. 6. The fracture morph ology of the present material is similar 
to that observed in conventional Mg alloys. The fracture surface shows elongated dimples 
indicating that significant plastic deformation takes pl ace during compressive deformation. The 
regions containing the ductile dimples are surrounded by trans-granular quasi -cleavage fracture 
areas, as can be seen in Fig. 6(b). 
 
After the compression test, the fractured sample was po lished parallel to the loading 
direction to study the probable causes of failure. The pol ished surface was observed by SEM in 
compositional contrast mode (backscattered mode). The im ages at lower magnification (Fig. 7)

## Page 11

10 
 
reveal a large number of rough regions and a crack propagati ng through these regions. The crack 
is not continuous but it consists of segments and these segments are oriented approximately 
parallel to the compressive direction due to the shear st ress component during compression test. 
The rough regions are frequently observed in the regions ahea d of the crack segments, as well as 
adjacently to the growing dominant crack. Images taken at higher magnifications in (Fig. 7) 
reveal that these rough regions are actually microcracked regions. 
 
 Further investigation at higher magnification (Fig. 8) indic ates that the main cracks as 
well as the microcracks are locally deflected by the γ -Al12Mg17 particles and the linkage of the 
microcracks with the main crack is also prevented by the γ -Al12Mg17 particles. Additionally, in 
some places of the crack the matrix act as crack bri dging, which can be attributed to the more 
ductile nature of the matrix as compared to the γ -Al12Mg17 particles. 
 TEM bright field (BF) (Fig. 9(a)) and dark field (DF) im ages (Fig. 9(b)) show a 
microcrack (white region in BF and dark region in DF) in th e fractured sample. The BF image 
reveals that the microcrack starts at the interface between matrix and γ -Al12Mg17 phase. In 
addition, the γ -Al12Mg17 particles deflect the crack from its path. This occurs due to the presence 
of body centered cubic (bcc) structure of intermetallic c ompound Al 12Mg17 which is 
incompatible with the hcp structure of the Mg(Al)ss matrix. This leads to fragility of the matrix-
Al12Mg17 interface during deformation [26]. Furthermore, the DF mic rograph reveals that the 
microcrack is surrounded by nano-size MgO particles and that the crack tip is blunted and finally 
stops in regions densely populated with MgO particles. The M gO particles help to form the 
microcracks [27] the reasons could be : (i) mismatch of thermal coefficient between Mg-Al 
matrix (26.4×10 -6 K -1) [26] and MgO (15.1×10 -6 K -1 between 300 and 1873 K) [28], which

11 
 
builds large residual stress in areas with higher MgO conce ntration during cooling, (ii) MgO is 
more brittle than the magnesium matrix and γ - Al 12Mg17phase [29], hence the interface is more 
prone to crack formation and weaken the coherence of the magnesium alloy matrix. 
 
The crack deflection, crack bridging and microcrack formati on all reduce the elastic 
energy of the main crack, which tends to stop the main crack, increasing the fracture toughness 
of the material. Increased fracture toughness was report ed by many authors [30-32] in Ti-Al 
alloys. The fracture toughness of the Ti-Al alloys increa sed because of various reasons (i.e. 
deflection of main crack, torturous crack path, reduction o f stress intensity factor at crack tip, 
formation of shear ligament & microcracks). Similar behavior was reported for Al-Si alloys 
which undergo ductile fracture of ligament and enhance fract ure toughness [33]. The degree of 
toughening depends on the ligament length, ligament toughness parameter, area fraction of the 
ligament, and the area fraction of frictional contact [33]. Chiu and Mao also found toughness 
enhancement by the ligament fracturing in Fe 3Al intermetallics and estimated the enhancement 
in the toughness of the material [34]. Microstructural in-h omogeneity is also one of the factors 
for microcrack formation and that leads to improve the toughness of the material. This has been 
observed in ultrafine grained copper alloy produced by equal channel angular pressing [35]. 
 
4. Conclusions 
High-strength Mg-7.4%Al alloys with ultrafine microstruct ure have been successfully produced 
by powder metallurgy techniques. The microstructure and mecha nical properties have been 
investigated. The following conclusions can be drawn from this study

## Page 13

12 
 
• Phase analysis and microstructural investigations reveal that the microstructure of the 
consolidated material consists of γ -Al12Mg17 particles with size below 1 µ m and MgO 
particles with average size of about 7 nm homogeneously di spersed in a continuous 
Mg(Al)ss matrix. 
• The microstructure of the consolidated samples leads to a remarkable mechanical 
properties. Room temperature compression tests of the c onsolidated material reveal high 
compressive strength of about 690 MPa combined with plastic strain exceeding 9%. 
• The fracture surface shows elongated dimples indicating significant plasticity in the 
sample. It further reveals formation of microcracks a t the tip of the main crack as a result 
decreases the strain energy of the main crack and there fore increases the toughness of the 
material.
