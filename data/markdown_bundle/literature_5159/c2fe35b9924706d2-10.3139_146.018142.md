The temporal evolution of the nanostructures of
model Ni – Al – Cr and Ni – Al – Cr – Re superalloys
The temporal evolution of c0(L12)-precipitates in Ni-5.2 Al-
14.2 Cr at.% and Ni-9.5 Al-8.6 Cr-2.0 Re at.% alloys are
studied utilizing three-dimensional atom-probe micros-
copy. For the times investigated (0.0833 to 1024 h), these
alloys exhibit a high number density (10 22 –1 024 m –3 )o f
spheroidal c0-precipitates, 0.45 – 30 nm in radius. For the
Ni 5.2 Al-14.2 Cr at.% alloy, nucleation, growth, and
coarsening of the c0-precipitates is observed during the iso-
thermal transformation at 873 K. Precipitate coalescence is
present, and its influence on the power-law dependencies
of the number density and mean radius during coarsening
is discussed and compared to classical models. For Ni-9.5
Al-8.6 Cr-2.0 Re at.%, phase separation occurs during the
initial quench leading to a highly interconnected c0-nanos-
tructure. The temporal evolution of the elemental partition-
ing behavior at 1073 K between the c-matrix and c0-preci-
pitates is studied in detail.
Keywords: Ni-based superalloys; Three dimensional atom
probe microscopy; Temporal evolution; nanostructures
1. Introduction
The precipitation of c0(L12)-precipitates from a supersatu-
rated solution, c(fcc)-matrix, in a temperature range where
nucleation and growth is observable, has been studied in
binary Ni-Al alloys by both direct [1, 2] and indirect [3]
imaging techniques. In the present investigation, three-di-
mensional atom-probe (3DAP) microscopy is employed to
characterize the temporal evolution of Ni-5.2 Al-14.2
Cr at.% aged at 873 K [4]. 3DAP microscopy allows the di-
rect spatial characterization of the chemical composition on
a subnano- to nanometer scale by reconstructing a volume
of material, typically 10
5 nm3 and 107 nm3 for the conven-
tional 3DAP and the local electrode atom-probe ( LEAP)
[5, 6] microscopes, respectively. Power-law time dependen-
cies of the number density and mean radius are discussed in
light of model predictions for Ostwald ripening (coarsen-
ing). In addition, a Ni-9.5 Al-8.6 Cr-2.0 Re at.% alloy is in-
vestigated to examine the effects of a Re addition [7, 8] on
the nanostructures of a Ni-based superalloy, the temporal
evolution of the c0-precipitates, partitioning between the
c- and c0-phases, and the segregation behavior of alloying
elements at the c/c0 heterophase interface. The results of
this study are compared with our results on Ni-9.8 Al-8.3
Cr at.% and Ni-9.7 Al-8.5 Cr-2.0 W at.% alloys [9, 10].
2. Experimental
High-purity constituent elements were induction-melted
under an Ar atmosphere, and then chill-cast in a 19 mm
diameter copper mold. Bulk chemical analyses were per-
formed by inductively coupled plasma atomic-emission
spectroscopy, and the alloys’ elemental compositions were
measured to be 80.5 Ni-14.2 Al-5.2 Cr and 79.9 Ni-9.5
Al-8.6 Cr-2.0 Re, in at.%. To ensure chemical homogeneity,
cast ingots were annealed at 1573 K for 20 and 24 h, respec-
tively. The temperature was then decreased to 1123 K and
held for 3 h for the Ni – Al – Cr alloy, and for the Ni – Al –
Cr – Re alloy it was held for 0.5 h at 1253 K, in the single-
phase region. Ingot sections were placed in a pre-heated
furnace and aged at 873 K (Ni – Al – Cr) and 1073 K (Ni –
Al – Cr – Re) for times,t, varying from 1/30 to 1024 h under
flowing argon. 3DAP microscopy was performed utilizing a
conventional 3DAP microscope (Northwestern University)
and a LEAP
1 (Imago Scientific Instruments) microscope.
Further experimental and analytical details are found in [5,
10 – 12].
3. Results and discussions

3.2. Temporal evolution of Ni-5.2 Al-14.2 Cr at.% at
873 K
As shown in Fig. 2, the Ni-5.2 Al-14.2 Cr at.% alloy de-
composes at 873 K into a high number density, Nv , of nan-
ometer-sized, spheroidal c0-precipitates. The lattice para-
meter misfit between the c and c0 phases is near zero [10,
13], and the c0-precipitates are coherent and spheroidal up
to 1024 h as confirmed by transmission electron micro-
scopy [10, 11, 14]. The nanostructural properties character-
ized by 3DAP microscopy are summarized in Table 1 and
are displayed in Fig. 5. Three regimes are identified: (i) nu-
cleation ( t = 0.17 – 0.25 h); (ii) concurrent nucleation and
growth (t = 0.25 – 4 h); and (iii) concurrent growth and coar-
sening (t ≥ 4 h).

0.25 h aging times, and nucleation is the dominant mechan-
ism over this time scale. The constant < R>, 0.75 nm (Ta-

4. Conclusions
Three-dimensional atom probe (3DAP) microscopy is
utilized to investigate the temporal evolution of a Ni-14.2
Cr-5.2 Al at.% alloy, aged at 873 K from 0.03 to 1024 h,
leading to the following results:
• After aging for 0.17 h, c0 precipitation is evident, with
precipitates as small as 0.45 nm in radius detected at a
number density of (3.6 ± 1.3) × 1023 m –3 . The peak
number density, (3.2 ± 0.6) × 1024 m –3 , is achieved
after 4 h, and the phase transformation enters a quasi-
steady-state regime between 4 and 16 h (Table 1 and
Fig. 5).
• In quasi-steady-state, the time exponent of 0.29 ± 0.05
obtained from 3DAP microscopy for <R> is in approxi-
mate agreement with the prediction of 1/3 found in
coarsening models [15], while the time exponent for Nv
of – 0.64 ± 0.06 deviates significantly from the pre-
dicted value of – 1 [15]. The latter result is attributed to
the fact that the phase transformation is not complete,
as the precipitated volume fraction is steadily evolving
in this regime (Fig. 5).
• The morphology of the coherentc0-precipitates is a mix-
ture of spheroidal c0-precipitates and c0-precipitates in
various stages of coagulation and coalescence (Figs. 1
and 2), where coagulation and coalescence is detected
in specimens aged between 0.25 ≤ t ≤ 256 h. The per-
centage of c0-precipitates, f, interconnected by necks
has a maximum value of 30 ± 4 % (Fig. 6), which coin-
cides with the smallest edge-to-edge interprecipitate
spacing, 5.9 nm, at 4 h of aging at 873 K.
The nanostructure, chemistry, and coarsening kinetics of a
Ni-9.5 Al-8.6 Cr-2.0 Re at.% alloy aged at 1073 K for 0 to
264 h is investigated with 3DAP microscopy, leading to
the following results:
• In the as-quenched state, spheroidal c0-precipitates, with
a mean radius, <R>, of 3.89 nm, are present, which is in
contrast to the as-quenched state of Ni-14.2 Cr-5.2
Al at.% alloy, where no precipitates are observed.
• The <R> value of the c
0-precipitates increases and num-
ber density, Nv, decreases concomitantly with increasing
aging time, as anticipated.
• Three-dimensional reconstructions obtained using
3DAP microscopy display alternating Al-rich planes in-
side the c0-precipitates, along the [100] direction of the
ordered L1 2-structure, even in the as-quenched state
sample.
• 3DAP microscopy results demonstrate that the addition
of Re increases the partitioning of Al to the c0-precipi-
tates and Cr to the c-matrix, compared to the model
Ni – Cr – Al alloy and delays the coarsening kinetics.
