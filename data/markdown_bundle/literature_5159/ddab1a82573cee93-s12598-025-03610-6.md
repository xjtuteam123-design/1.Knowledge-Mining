Low-energy semi-coherent interfaces deliver strength-plasticity
synergy and softening resistance in a lightweight refractory
high-entropy alloy
Wei-Jian Shen, Rui-Xin Wang*, Shun Li, Yu Tang*, Shu-Xin Bai
Received: 29 May 2025 / Revised: 10 August 2025 / Accepted: 10 August 2025
/C211 Youke Publishing Co., Ltd. 2025
Abstract Lightweight refractory high-entropy alloys
(LRHEAs) show signiﬁcant promise for high-temperature
structural applications, yet often exhibit limited room-
temperature deformability and insufﬁcient high-tempera-
ture strength. In this study, we address these challenges by
engineering low-energy semi-coherent interfaces between
the body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution matrix and
C14 intermetallics in the TiZrNbV
0.6Al0.75 LRHEA (den-
sity = 5.5 g cm -3). These interfaces provide exceptional
dislocation accommodation capability, while the C14
intermetallics contribute 679 ± 57 MPa precipitation
strengthening, resulting in a remarkable speciﬁc yield
strength of * 282 MPa cm
3 g-1 with 27.5% plasticity at
room temperature. The semi-coherent interfaces exhibit
outstanding thermal stability at 1073 and 1173 K, pre-
serving the C14 morphology, effectively impeding dislo-
cation motion and grain boundary migration. This stability
enables exceptional high-temperature strength retention:
1305 ± 12 MPa (84% of room-temperature strength) at
1073 K (0.6 T
m) and 1062 ± 15 MPa (68% of room-tem-
perature strength) at 1173 K (0.65 Tm). This work estab-
lishes a novel LRHEA design paradigm that synergistically
integrates strength, plasticity, and softening resistance
across both ambient and high-temperature regimes.
Keywords Lightweight refractory high-entropy alloy;
Semi-coherent interfaces; Strength-plasticity synergy;
Softening resistance; C14 intermetallics
1 Introduction
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), which are com-
posed of multiple high-melting-point refractory metals
[1, 2], are considered potential structural materials for
elevated-temperature applications due to their high strength
across a broad temperature range [ 3, 4]. For instance, the
MoNbVTa
0.5 RHEA with a body-centered cubic (BCC)
structure demonstrates yield strength of 1505 MPa at
298 K and 697 MPa at 1473 K, attributed to severe lattice
distortion and sluggish diffusion effect stabilizing grain
boundaries [ 5]. However, the high density (9–15 g cm
-3)
of most refractory metals and their corresponding RHEAs
poses a signiﬁcant challenge in meeting the speciﬁc yield
strength requirements for structural components. Addi-
tionally, similar to conventional metals, RHEAs tend to
soften at temperatures exceeding 0.5–0.6 T
m (where Tm is
the melting point) due to the activation of diffusion-con-
trolled deformation mechanisms [ 6, 7]. This softening
behavior, coupled with their high density, severely restricts
the application of RHEAs as high-temperature structural
materials.
The development of lightweight refractory high-entropy
alloys (LRHEAs) incorporating elements such as Ti, Zr, V,
and Al offers a promising strategy to overcome these
limitations [ 8]. By integrating these relatively low-density

[9, 10].
On the one hand, thermally stable intermetallics like the
C14 Laves phase can enhance high-temperature strength
[11] but create incoherent interfaces with the matrix due to
crystallographic mismatch. These incoherent boundaries
facilitate atomic diffusion at elevated temperatures while
induce dislocation pile-up during room-temperature
deformation, ultimately compromising ductility at room
temperature. For example, C14-containing AlNbTiVZr and
Al
0.6Nb0.5TiV2Zr0.5 LRHEAs demonstrate respective
strengths of 550 and 596 MPa at 1073 K, and a limited
fracture strains of 3% and 14.3% at 298 K [ 12, 13].
On the other hand, ordered B2 intermetallics within
disordered BCC matrix form coherent interfaces in
LRHEAs. These coherent interfaces offer one potential
solution by exhibiting exceptional dislocation accommo-
dation capacity, thereby enabling superior room-tempera-
ture plasticity [ 14]. However, alloys leveraging this
approach typically undergo rapid high-temperature soft-
ening due to B2 phase degradation. For instance, the B2-
containing (TiNbZr)
89(AlTa)11 and Al 10Nb15Ta5Ti30Zr40
alloys demonstrate compressive strains [ 50% at 298 K,
yet retain only 124 and 210 MPa yield strength at 1073 K
[15, 16], respectively.
Consequently, achieving synergistic integration of ele-
vated-temperature strength and room-temperature
deformability remains a fundamental challenge for high-
temperature structural materials, as both properties are
critical for performance and processability.
The present study develops a novel TiZrNbV
0.6Al0.75
LRHEA through compositional optimization of the previ-
ously reported TiZrNbV
0.6 system [ 17]. Al alloying was
strategically implemented to concurrently reduce density
and induce intermetallic phase formation. Combined
experimental analysis and density functional theory (DFT)
calculations conﬁrm the existence of thermodynamically
stabilized semi-coherent interfaces between the BCC
matrix and intermetallic phases. These semi-coherent
interfaces exhibit exceptional tolerance for dislocations,
effectively prevent dislocation pile-up, while the C14
intermetallics provide signiﬁcant precipitation strengthen-
ing effect. As a result, the alloy achieves high speciﬁc yield
strengths and good plasticity at room temperature. More-
over, these low-energy semi-coherent interfaces effectively
suppress the diffusion-induced deformation at elevated
temperatures. Consequently, the TiZrNbV
0.6Al0.75 LRHEA
demonstrates remarkable retention of yield strength at high
temperatures, maintaining 84% of its room-temperature
strength at 0.6 T
m and 68% at 0.65 Tm. Through systematic
investigation of the microstructure and mechanical
behavior, the underlying role of the low-energy semi-co-
herent interfaces, the strengthening and deformation
mechanisms were subsequently discussed and elucidated.
This research provides theoretical insights for the devel-
opment of next-generation high-temperature structural
materials, catering to the stringent demands of aerospace
and military applications.

analyses were conducted to reveal the substructures formed
during deformation. The specimens with 5%, 10% and 20%
strains were investigated, and the results are illustrated in
Fig. 5. No twinning, stacking faults, or phase transforma-

Instead, a signiﬁcant number of slip dislocations were

4 Discussion
4.1 Formation of chain-like C14 intermetallics
As depicted in Fig. S5, the mixing enthalpy, which is a
thermodynamic parameter indicating element compatibil-
ity, is negative for Al with Zr and V [ 44]. More negative
values normally mean elements aggregate more readily.
Thus, the negative mixing enthalpy implies Zr, V, and Al
have a strong tendency to cluster and form C14 inter-
metallics in TiZrNbV 0.6Al0.75 LRHEA. This stronger
directional bonding result in the higher melting tempera-
ture of C14 intermetallics compared to the BCC matrix,
leading to earlier precipitation of C14 phases. Subsequently
solid–liquid interfaces are established and facilitated
heterogeneous nucleation of subsequent C14 particles
through interface templating effects during the process of
solidiﬁcation. Meanwhile, as shown in Fig. S2, the pre-
cipitation process induces localized solute depletion near
the growing interfaces due to the rapid cooling hindering
timely element replenishment from distant liquid areas, and
thus restricting irregular growth of C14 intermetallics
phase. The competition between these interface-mediated
nucleation events and diffusion-limited growth ultimately
governs the characteristic chain-like morphology observed
in Fig. 2A.

4.4 The deformation behavior at room temperature
For most high-strength alloys containing intermetallics, the
ultimate strain before fracture is generally small (Fig. 4B)
due to the poor deformation capacity of intermetallics and
the stress concentration induced by highly mismatched
interfaces. The appreciable plasticity for TiZrNbV
0.6Al0.75
LRHEA deformed at room temperature mainly originates
from the low-energy semi-coherent interfaces between
BCC matrix and C14 intermetallics. In the initial stage of
deformation, considerable dislocations are activated to slip
with planar mode in the BCC matrix (Fig. 5A) while no
deformation occurs in the hard C14 intermetallics
(Fig. 5D). Both the lattice distortion [ 61] and the hard C14
intermetallics would pin dislocations; thus, the activation
of dislocations is pretty difﬁcult. With increasing strain,
Taylor lattice with characteristic of coplanar dislocation
arrays, which is common in RHEAs [ 62–65], appears in the
matrix (Fig. 5B). The interaction between the multiple slip
systems would boost dislocation multiplication then
homogenize the dislocation substructure. When the dislo-
cations approach to C14 intermetallics, it is too hard to
shear or by-pass the large and hard phase to further slip.
But the semi-coherent interfaces possess fairly large tol-
erance for dislocation [ 4] since the relatively low mismatch
(Fig. 5E), thus avoiding the pile-up of dislocations and
consequent stress concentration then suppressing the initi-
ation of crack. Notably, the C14 intermetallics are also
capable of undergoing deformation, eliminating the brittle-
fracture possibility of C14 intermetallics. The improved
deformation ability of C14 intermetallics may beneﬁt from
the chemically complex nature of multi-elements in those
precipitates [ 66]. As the dislocations further multiplicate
and slip, the Taylor lattice composed of high-density dis-
locations fully develops (Fig. 5C), while the tolerance of
boundary for dislocations and the deformation of C14
intermetallics reach limit. As a result, cracks initiate in the
C14 intermetallics (Fig. S4) then fracture appears. In all,
the multiple slip in matrix and the semi-coherent interfaces
could effectively alleviate strain localization, appreciable
plasticity is achieved in the high-strength TiZrNbV
0.6Al0.75
LRHEA. The above deformation mechanism is schemati-
cally shown in Fig. 10A.

5 Conclusion
In this study, semi-coherent interfaces between the BCC
solid solution and C14 intermetallics in the TiZrNb-
V
0.6Al0.75 LRHEA were designed to achieve an optimal
balance of strength, plasticity, and resistance to high-tem-
perature softening. These interfaces exhibit a low interfa-
cial energy of -3.8715 eV ( c = -0.2849 J m
-2), a key
characteristic that facilitates their preferential formation,
enables effective dislocation accommodation at room
temperature, and ensures robust thermal stability at ele-
vated temperatures. Such unique interfacial properties
directly contribute to the alloy’s exceptional mechanical
performance at ambient conditions. The alloy demonstrates
a remarkable speciﬁc yield strength of * 282 MPa
cm
3 g-1 accompanied by plasticity of 27.5%. This high
strength arises from the solid solution strengthening for
BCC matrix and precipitation strengthening for C14
intermetallics, while the moderate plasticity results from
the dislocation accommodation of the semi-coherent
interfaces. Notably, the beneﬁts of these interfaces extend
beyond room-temperature performance to high-tempera-
ture environments. At 1073 and 1173 K (0.6 and 0.65 T
m,
respectively), the alloy retains exceptional strength, with
yield strengths of 1305 ± 12 and 1062 ± 15 MPa, corre-
sponding to 84% and 68% retention relative to its room-
temperature strength. This resistance to thermal softening
stems from the inherent stability of the semi-coherent
interfaces, which suppresses dislocation motion and grain
boundary migration even under elevated temperatures.
