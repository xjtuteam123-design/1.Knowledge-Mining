Development of Near β Titanium Alloy with High Strength 
and Superplastic Properties 
E. V. Naydenkina), I. V. Ratochka, I. P. Mishin, and O. N. Lykova 
Institute of Strength Physics and Materials Science SB RAS, Tomsk, 634055 Russia 
 
a) Corresponding author: nev@ispms.tsc.ru 
Abstract. Ultrafine-grained (UFG) structure with an average element size of about 0.25 µm was obtained in a near β 
titanium alloy under severe plastic deformation by abc pressing. It is shown that the formation of such structure greatly 
increases the ultimate strength of the alloy compared to its coarse-grained state (up to 1610 MPa). In addition, the UFG 
alloy features improved superplastic characteristics under tension at temperatures of 973–1073 K: its elongation to failure 
exceeds 1300% and the flow stress decreases to several MPa. 
INTRODUCTION 
Titanium alloys, due to their high specific strength and corrosion resistance, find wide technological applications 
[1, 2]. Numerous studies have shown that tensile α + β Ti alloys with a globular fine-grained structure (grain size 
d < 10 µm) display superplasticity normally at temperatures above 1073 K (>0.5 Tm) and strain rates lower than 
10–3 s–1 [3]. Severe plastic deformation (SPD) provides grain structure refinement and increases the extent and role 
of grain and phase boundaries in plastic deformation and fracture of metal materials [4, 5]. In Ti alloys, the process 
can provide not only an increase in strength but also a shift in superplasticity toward lower temperatures and/or 
higher strain rates [4, 6–8]. By now, there are scarcely any research data on the formation and properties of 
ultrafine-grained (UFG) structure in near β Ti alloys. Here we present a study of the SPD effect on the structure, 
mechanical properties, and superplasticity of a near β Ti–5Al–5Mo–5V–1Cr–1Fe alloy with a high content of β-
stabilizing elements (Mo, V) [9]. For SPD we used multi-directional or so-called abc pressing, which makes it 
possible to obtain a structure with an element size of 0.1–0.5 µm in titanium alloys [4]. 
MATERIAL AND METHODS 
The test material was a commercial near β Ti alloy of composition Ti–4.74Al–5.57Mo–5.04V–0.81Cr–0.98Fe 
shaped as rods of diameter 40 mm. The material was exposed to severe plastic deformation by abc pressing with a 
change of its strain axis [4] on a IP-2000 press at 1073–723 K. The initial strain temperature was 50 deg lower than 
the temperature of complete polymorphic transformation for the alloy [9]. The strain was about 0.5 per pass, and the 
total accumulated strain was no less than 7. For structural and phase analysis, the material was spark cut in a plane 
perpendicular to the rod axis (cross-section). Preliminarily, a layer about 100 µm thick was removed from the 
specimen surface through mechanical grinding and electrolytic polishing. The specimens were examined by 
transmission electron microscopy of thin foils on a Jeol JEM-2100 microscope at the Nanotech Shared Use Center 
(ISPMS SB RAS, Tomsk). The foils for electron microscopy were prepared by the standard method using a Mikron-
103 jet-polishing unit with an electrolyte containing 20% HClO
4 + 80% CH 3CO2H. The phase composition of the 
material was analyzed in CuKα radiation on a Shimadzu XRD-6000 diffractometer equipped with a monochromator. 
The material was tested for tension in a vacuum of 10–2 Pa over a wide temperature range using a PV-3012M system 
(equipped with a strain gauge measuring system) with automatic flow curve recording in load–time coordinates at an

RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS 
The results of transmission electron microscopy show that abc pressing greatly decreases the grain size in the 
alloy (Fig. 1). The average grain size (regions bounded by high angle boundaries) is ∼0.25 µm. No original grain 
structure is revealed. A complex deformation contrast is observed inside the grains (Fig. 1a). The size of grain-
subgrain structure elements determined by dark-field imaging varies from 0.1 to 0.5 µm (Fig. 1b). Microdiffraction 
patterns from an area of ∼1.6 µm2 reveal a large number of individual circumferentially arranged reflections, being 
evidence for high angle grain misorientations (Fig. 1a). 
Our X-ray diffraction analysis of the phase composition shows that the β phase volume fraction in the alloy after 
abc pressing reaches 45%. In addition, broadening of Bragg peaks is observed with decreasing peak intensities for 
both phases (the intensity decreases almost to one half), which is indicative of an increase in the internal stress and 
of a decrease in the size of structural elements limited by coherent scattering regions. 
The results of tensile tests suggest that compared to the coarse-grained state, the UFG alloy displays higher 
mechanical properties: increased strength and plasticity (Fig. 2). The yield stress and the ultimate strength increase 
from 1210 to 1540 MPa and from 280 to 1610 MPa, respectively. The values are much higher than those reported 
for cold rolling with subsequent aging [10]. The ductility of the UFG alloy increases from 7 to 10% (Fig. 2), which 
is not typical for metals and alloys obtained by SPD methods [4, 6]. In addition, the nature of flow changes greatly. 
While the alloy with a coarse-grained structure shows strain hardening almost up to failure ( ∼5%), the UFG alloy 
demonstrates short-term strain hardening (∼1%) with long subsequent softening characteristic for SPD materials [4]. 
The so short strain hardening stage in UFG Ti alloy can be due to rapidly developing plastic strain localization [11]. 
The deformation behavior of Ti–5Al–5Mo–5V–1Cr–1Fe with ultrafine-grained structure at high temperatures 
suggests that its maximum plasticity (>1300%) falls on the temperature range 973–1023 (Fig. 3a). The material at 
these temperatures was not brought to failure because of the limits imposed by the equipment and length of the 
specimen gage used (Fig. 3b). It is seen form Fig. 3a that the alloy in the temperature range of superplasticity (823–
1023 K) reveals a sharp drop in its yield stress. For the alloy at tensile temperatures of 973 and 1023 K, the flow 
stress at the stage of steady superplasticity decreases up to several MPa (Fig. 3a). Such low flow stresses correspond 
in magnitude to the shear modulus of rubber. Thus, in Ti–5Al–5Mo–5V–1Cr–1Fe alloy with ultrafine grained 
structure, the flow stress at these temperatures for superplasticity decreases almost three orders of magnitude 
compared to its values at room temperature. Such a decrease in plastic strain resistance in combination with 
increased plasticity makes it possible to treat the UFG alloy under superplastic forming conditions.

Our study shows that the abc pressed Ti–5Al–5Mo–5V–1Cr–1Fe alloy with ultrafine grained structure 
demonstrates high strength at room temperature and increased superplastic properties at elevated temperatures. It is 
a good prerequisite for using this UFG alloy in practice, including its use for reliable elements and constructions in 
modern aerospace technologies. 
CONCLUSION 
Thus, our TEM and X-ray study of ultrafine-grained Ti–5Al–5Mo–5V–1Cr–1Fe alloy obtained by abc pressing 
shows that the treatment provides a structure with an average grain size of ∼0.25 µm whose elements are related to 
both α- and β-phases with a volume fraction of the latter of ∼45 %. The formation of such a micro-duplex ultrafine 
grained structure provides a substantial increase in the strength of the alloy at room temperature, compared to values 
in its initial coarse-grained state (from 1280 to 1610 MPa), and a simultaneous increase in deformation to fracture.
