Abstract 
The effects of minor rare earths, such as Sc, Er, and Y, on the microstructure and properties 
of Cu-Cr-Zr alloys, were studied systematically. Microstructure evolutions and properties 
variations of Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys indicate that the hot-rolling + solid solution treatment+ 
cold-rolling + aging treatment is an appropriate method to fabricate studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys 
with high ultimate tensile strength, high electrical conductivity, excellent plasticity, and large 
strength-elongation products. The addition of Y effectively suppressed the precipitation and 
growth of Cr particles in the Cu-Cr-Zr-Y alloy. The high-temperature ultimate tensile strength of 
the peak-aged Cu-Cr-Zr-Y alloy was 381 MPa at 450 °C, and that of the over-aged one was 341 
MPa, which are much higher than those of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy in the peak-aged temper (320 MPa) 
and the over-aged temper (300 MPa). The primary strengthening mechanism in the high strength 
Cu-Cr-Zr-Sc alloys is the grain boundary strengthening following the Hall-Petch relationship. The 
mainly strengthening mechanisms in the Cu-Cr-Zr, Cu-Cr-Zr-Y, and Cu-Cr-Zr-Er alloys are 
precipitation strengthening and dislocation strengthening. These findings indicate rare earth 
elements play important roles in improving the high/room temperature properties of Cu-Cr-Zr 
system alloys. 
Keywords: rare earth; copper alloy; electrical conductivity; strength.

1 Introduction 
Copper and copper alloys are the earliest metallic materials used by human beings. Copper 
alloys have excellent electrical and thermal conductivity, high mechanical properties, 
processability, and a range of other advanced properties. Copper-based products are widely used 
in aerospace, power lead frame materials, heat exchange equipment, high-speed railway contact 
materials, overhead wires, and equipment manufacturing [1, 2]. With the development of modern 
industry, more and more new copper alloys were developed to replacing old ones due to the 
progressiveness of the high-tech. For example, the Cu-Al-Mn shape memory alloy with high 
ductility and plasticity was fabricated to replace Cu-Al-Ni and Cu-Zn-Al alloys [3, 4]. Since the 
Fe has unique electromagnetic shielding properties and the Cu has high electrical and thermal 
conductivity, Cu-Fe composites would like to be widely used in high-tech fields such as 
particle-accelerated electromagnetic transmitters and pulsed magnetic field systems [5-7]. A small 
amount of Ni and Si makes the Cu-Ni-Si alloys a tensile strength of 800 MPa and an electrical 
conductivity of 45 %IACS, resulting in primary lead-frame materials [8, 9]. The developing 
high-strength and high-conductivity copper alloys, such as Cu-Fe, Cu-Ag, Cu-Cr, Cu-Cr-Zr, 
Cu-Ni-Si, Cu-Ni-Sn, and et al , will be widely used in motor manufacturing, transportation, and 
other fields. Among these alloys, the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy has attracted much attention because of its 
excellent comprehensive properties (electrical conductivity of 80 %IACS and tensile strength of 
600 MPa). The solubilities of Cr and Zr in Cu-Cr-Zr alloys at room temperature are very low (less 
than 0.03% for Cr and 0.01% Zr). Thus, these excess Cr and Zr atoms will precipitate out from the 
supersaturated solid solution, resulting in strengthening the matrix and enhancing the electrical 
conductivity. Wang et al. believed that the Cu-0.75Cr-0.13Zr alloy precipitated either FCC or

The addition of trace amounts of La and Y to Cu-0.81 Cr-0.12 Zr increases the hardness and 
slightly reduce the electrical conductivity [17]. After cold-rolled by 80% and aged at 300 °C for 
16 hours, the hardness of Cu-0.4 Cr-0.15 Zr-0.05 Ce alloy reached 170 HV in hardness and 
66 %IACS in electrical conductivity [18]. After hot-rolled for 500 times, the ultimate tensile 
strength, yield strength, and elongation of the Cu-0.5Cr-0.04Zr-0.03Ti alloy reached 349 MPa,

could effectively reduce the stacking fault energy of the Cu-0.4Cr-0.2Zr-0.2Hf alloy and introduce

Zr-rich, and Hesuler phases in the Cu-0.43Cr-0.17Zr-0.05Mg-0.1RE alloy with a high tensile 
strength of 525 MPa, a hardness of 118 HV, and an electrical conductivity of 82 %IACS,

during aging treatment. Thus, additions of Zr and other elements have been attempted in Cu-Cr 
alloys to suppress this undesired phenomenon. Generally, adding a small number of rare earth 
elements can purify the matrix, remove impurities, and refine grains in copper alloys, resulted in

In this work, we will focus on: (1) exploring the effects of Sc, Er and Y elements on the 
microstructure and properties of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy; (2) optimizing the processing method for the 
Cu-Cr-Zr-X (X=Sc, Er, or Y) alloys; (3) evaluating the high-temperature properties of the 
Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys. Besides, the physical properties variations and microstructure evolutions of 
the studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys will be characterized. The mechanisms for strengthening the 
matrix, enhancing the electrical conductivity, and improving the thermal stability will also be 
discussed. 
2 Materials and experimental procedure 
The designed Cu-Cr-Zr alloys with different additional alloying elements (Y, Er, or Sc) 
were produced in a vacuum induction furnace. The raw materials include electrolytic copper 
(99.99 wt.%, 4N), pure chromium (4N), pure zirconium (4N), pure scandium (4N), pure erbium 
(4N), and pure yttrium (4N). Argon was employed in a protective atmosphere to avoid oxidation. 
Table 1 gives the nominal components of the four ingots. Three thermomechanical treatment 
processes were conducted to optimize the appropriate process by testing the physical properties.

Fig. 1 shows the schematic illustration of the three thermomechanical treatment processes: (1) 
HR+AG: the as-cast ingots were hot-rolled (HR) at 900 °C by 50%, followed by quenched with 
water, and then aged at temperatures from 400 °C to 500 °C for various periods; (2) HR+SST+AG: 
the as-cast ingots were hot-rolled (HR) at 900 °C, followed by solid solution treated (SST) in 
960 °C for 4 h and quenched with water, and then aged at temperatures from 400 °C to 500 °C for 
various periods; (3) HR+SST+CR+AG: the as-cast ingots were hot-rolled (HR) at 900 °C, 
followed by solid solution treated (SST) in 970 °C for 4 h and quenched with water, cold-rolled 
(CR) by 50%, and then and then aged at temperatures from 400 °C to 500 °C for various periods. 
Metallographic observations were carried out using a Leica optical microscope. Vickers hardness 
measurements were performed on an HV-5 hardness tester with a load of 3 kg and a loading time 
of 10 seconds. Each sample was measured by seven times, and the average hardness was recorded. 
Electrical conductivity was measured at a DK60 eddy current conductivity meter. At least three 
measurements were conducted, and the average was recorded as samples’ electrical conductivity 
(%IACS, International Annealed Copper Standard). Tensile tests were performed on a 
high-temperature tensile testing machine at both 20 °C and 450 °C with a strain rate of 0.008 s-1. 
Fractures of the tensile test samples were captured by an FEI Quanta-200 scanning electron 
microscope (SEM) with an operating voltage of 15 kV. An FEI G20 field-emission transmission 
electron microscope (TEM) was employed to characterize the microstructure of the foil samples 
with an operating voltage of 200 kV.

4.1 Influence of thermomechanical heat treatments on properties 
Three processes were conducted to compare their influence on the properties. Fig. 2 to Fig. 
5 show variations of the hardness and electrical conductivity in the studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys 
during aging treatments. After hot rolling (HR), the hardness of the four studied alloys is around 
120 HV, and their electrical conductivities are around 50 %IACS. This can be attributed to the 
partial precipitation of solute atoms during the hot rolling process, the same to the aging process, 
which increased the initial conductivity. It is generally believed that the electrical conductivity of 
Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys is related to the concentration of alloying elements in the matrix. The greater 
the solid solubility, the stronger the scattering effect of lattice distortion on the electron 
transmission process and the lower the conductivity. Compared with the HR process, the 
combined performance of samples after solid solution and cold rolling can be improved much 
more. The electrical conductivity of studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys decreased to 40 %IACS after 
solid solution treatments and cold rolling (HR+SST+CR), which can be improved by the 
following aging treatments. After a solid solution, the microscale precipitated Cr particles during 
hot-rolling were re-dissolved into the matrix, and they would be precipitated as nanoscale particles 
during aging treatment, providing a large amount of precipitation hardening. Furthermore, the cold 
rolling deformation before aging treatment can significantly refine the grains and introduce 
substructures and dislocations, which could prevent dislocations motion and then work hardening.

well as in the other studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys. Fig. 9 to Fig. 12 show the microstructure

The addition of minor rare earth elements increased the hardness of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloys, as

the hardness of the peak-aged Cu-Cr-Zr alloy was 127.6 HV, while those of the Cu-Cr-Zr-Sc and

On the other hand, for the optimal processing (HR+SST+CR), the peak hardness of the Cu-Cr-Zr 
alloy was 159.7 HV, while, those of the Cu-Cr-Zr-Sc, Cu-Cr-Zr-Er, and Cu-Cr-Zr-Y alloys were 
5.07%, 6.76%, and 7.20% higher than that of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy, respectively. The ultimate 
tensile strength of peak-aged Cu-Cr-Zr alloy tested at room temperature was 540 MPa, while those 
of the Cu-Cr-Zr-Sc and Cu-Cr-Zr-Y alloys are 5.02% and 1.81% higher. Although the effects of 
Sc, Er, and Y elements on the microstructure of Cu-Cr-Zr alloys are not completely consistent, it 
is certain that the addition of these rare earth elements have refined the microstructure, increased

4.5 Properties comparisons 
Figure 13 shows the tensile strength and elongation of the studied alloys as well as other 
Cu-Cr system and Cu-Cr-Zr system alloys. Table 7 shows the comparison of the electrical 
conductivity and mechanical properties of the reported Cu-Cr-Zr system alloys. The addition of Hf 
in the Cu-0.4Cr-0.2Zr-0.2Hf alloy increases the tensile strength [21], while the addition of Ag in 
the Cu-0.5Cr-0.15Zr-0.1Ag increases the electrical conductivity [31]. The addition of minor Fe in 
the Cu-1.0Cr-0.2Zr-0.03Fe alloy does not enhance either strength or electrical conductivity [32], 
while the addition of Y in the Cu-0.8Cr-0.2Zr-0.05Y alloy increases the electrical conductivity 
and elongation [33]. With the increasing content of Cr and Zr, the physical properties of the 
reported Cu-Cr-Zr system alloys did not change significantly. For high-strength and 
high-conductivity copper alloys, both the strength and elongation are important. It is difficult to 
improve the tensile strength and elongation at the same time, which is a problem that many 
researchers want to solve. The tensile strength of the Cu-0.4Cr-0.2Zr-0.2Hf alloy is as high as 628 
MPa, but the elongation is only 5% [21]. While the elongation of the Cu-0.5Cr-0.04Zr-0.03Ti is 
up to 26%, while the tensile strength is only 349 MPa [19]. The previous Cu-Cr and Cu-Cr-Zr 
system alloys either show high strength and small elongation or show large elongation and low 
strength. Compared with previous reports, the HR+SST+CR process introduced a lot of 
substructure in the studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys, and the addition of minor rare earth elements 
contributed purifying the matrix during melting and casting. Substructure, dislocations, and

ultrafine precipitates were detected in the Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys, resulting in high tensile strength 
and large elongation were approached at the same time. The studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys showed 
higher elongation than other reported Cu-Cr-Zr alloys except for the Cu-1Cr-0.65Zr alloys [29]. 
Moreover, the additions of Sc and Y significantly increase the high-temperature properties of the 
Cu-Cr-Zr system alloys, as given above. 
5 Conclusions 
(1) Hot-rolling + solid solution treatment+ cold-rolling + aging treatment has been proved to 
be an appropriate method to fabricated the studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys. The hardness and ultimate 
tensile strength of the studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys having been treated by HR+SST+CR+AG-ed 
are higher than those of the other two methods increasing by about 30 HV in hardness and 100 
MPa in ultimate tensile strength, respectively. Moreover, this optimized method could maintain 
high electrical conductivity and large elongation for all studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys. 
(2) The addition of minor Y effectively improved the high-temperature properties of the 
studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys. Compared with the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy, the Cu-Cr-Zr-Y alloy has the 
best high-temperature performance. The high-temperature ultimate tensile strength of the 
peak-aged Cu-Cr-Zr-Y alloy was 381 MPa at 450 °C, and that of the over-aged one was 341 MPa, 
which are much higher than those of the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy in the peak-aged temper (320 MPa) and 
the over-aged temper (300 MPa). The addition of Y effectively suppressed the precipitation and 
growth of Cr particles in the Cu-Cr-Zr-Y alloy. 
(3) The primary strengthening mechanism in the high strength Cu-Cr-Zr-Sc alloys is the 
grain boundary strengthening following the Hall-Petch relationship. The mainly strengthening

## Page 19

18 18 18 18 / 26 26 26 26 
mechanisms in the Cu-Cr-Zr, Cu-Cr-Zr-Y, and Cu-Cr-Zr-Er alloys are precipitation strengthening 
and dislocation strengthening. The studied Cu-Cr-Zr-(X) alloys showed excellent plasticity and 
large strength-elongation product, indicating high formability and creep properties, which will be 
further studied.
