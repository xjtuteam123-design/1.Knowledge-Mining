Mechanical Performance of the Al xCoCrCuFeNi
High-Entropy Alloy System with Multiprincipal Elements
CHUNG-JIN TONG, MIN-RUI CHEN, SWE-KAI CHEN, JIEN-WEI YEH, 
TAO-TSUNG SHUN, SU-JIEN LIN, and SHOU-YI CHANG
The AlxCoCrCuFeNi alloys with multiprincipal elements (x /H11005the aluminum content in molar ratio,
from 0 to 3.0) were synthesized using a well-developed arc-melting and casting method, and their
mechanical properties were investigated. These alloys exhibited promising mechanical properties,
including excellent elevated-temperature strength and good wear resistance. With the addition of alu-
minum from x /H110050 to 3.0, the hardness of the alloys increased from HV 133 to 655, mainly attributed
to the increased portion of strong bcc phase to ductile fcc phase, both of which were strengthened by
the solid solution of aluminum atoms and the precipitation of nanophases. The alloys exhibited supe-
rior high-temperature strengths up to 800 °C, among which the Al0.5CoCrCuFeNi alloy, especially, had
enhanced plasticity and a large strain-hardening capacity. Moreover, the wear resistance of these alloys
was similar to that of ferrous alloys at the same hardness level, while the alloys with lower hardness
exhibited relatively higher resistance because of their large strain-hardening capacity.
I. INTRODUCTION
CONVENTIONALferrous tool alloys have been devel-
oped for centuries and have provided abundant practical con-
tributions. However, they are usually stable and robust below
some specific temperatures ranging from 350 °C to 550 °C,
depending on their content of alloying elements.[1,2] In order to
achieve a higher elevated-temperature strength and better ther-
mal resistance, superalloys have been widely developed since
the 1930s.
[3] The superalloys can sustain excellent elevated-
temperature strength and thermal resistance up to 700 °C to
900 °C, and their yield strengths in wrought product forms at
800 °C are mostly 350 MPa, at least. Since the 1970s, inter-
metallic compounds based on Ti-Al, Ni-Al, and Fe-Al binary
systems have attracted much attention because of their high spe-
cific strengths and thermal resistance.
[4] However, the most
severe disadvantage for them to overcome is their brittleness,
rendering their application limited. In the last two decades, many
researchers have explored a wide range of bulk amorphous
alloys, including Pd-, Ln-, Zr-, Fe-, and Mg-based alloys.[5–13]
Bulk amorphous alloys also provide very high mechanical
strengths, but their strengths and toughness at elevated tem-
peratures drastically drop due to the crystallization of the amor-
phous matrix at temperatures higher than 400 °C to 600 °C.
The development of practical alloy systems, including fer-
rous alloys, nickel-based and cobalt-based superalloys, and
even bulk amorphous alloys, etc., is mainly based on one prin-
cipal element as the matrix, limiting the number of applica-
ble alloy systems. Recently, some other metallic glasses with
multicomponents have been prepared by melt spinning, but,
however, still based on one principal group of transition met-
als.
[14–17] To overcome the limitations on design and applica-
tion, the alloys containing multiprincipal elements (thus, with
high mixing entropies) have been developed and investigated
recently.[18–23] Under the effects of a high mixing entropy and
the accompanied sluggish cooperative diffusion, they easily
yield the formation of simple solid-solution phases and nanos-
tructures during solidification, rather than many binary and
ternary intermetallic compounds or other complicated
phases.[24,25] The simplification of their structures avoids the
disadvantages of difficulty in microstructure analyses and the
inherent brittleness of ordered compounds. Moreover, we may
design the alloys with multiprincipal elements to exploit the
merits of each element, to generate a cocktail effect in addi-
tion to the virtue of nanostructure.[18–23]
The present study is focused on the investigation of the
mechanical properties of AlxCoCrCuFeNi (x/H11005the aluminum
content in molar ratio, from 0 to 3.0) high-entropy alloys
with six principal elements, including their hardness, high-
temperature strength, and wear resistance, with an aim to
understanding their potential for the requirements of high-
temperature alloys. Typical cast dendrite and interdendrite
structures were observed in all of the cast alloys with grain
sizes of about 50 to 100 
/H9262m. However, these cast alloys pos-
sessed simple fcc/bcc solid-solution phases constructed of sub-
micronic structures and nanoprecipitation, which changed with
the aluminum content.

minum (x /H113490.8), the alloys were mainly composed of a sim-
ple fcc solid-solution structure. As xincreased to 1.0, an ordered
bcc phase (similar to a B2 NiAl structure) appeared and con-

the eutectic and modulated plate structures gradually disap-

formed and dispersed in the ordered bcc phase. With x higher
than 2.0, the strongly ordered bcc phase tended to dominate
the matrix structure, and a single ordered bcc phase was
obtained for x values larger than 2.8. Based on the varied
structures of this alloy system, alloy and process modification

II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES
The multiprincipal-element AlxCoCrCuFeNi alloy system,
with different aluminum contents (x /H110050 to 3.0), was prepared
in this study by arc melting and casting in a vacuum of 0.01
atm.[21,22,23] The solidified ingots were about 50 mm in diam-
eter and 20-mm thick. Repeated melting for at least 5 times
was carried out to improve the chemical homogeneity of the
alloy. The weight loss of the alloys after arc melting and solid-
ification was lower than 0.2 at. pct, because no volatile ele-
ment was used for alloy preparation. The precise chemical
compositions of the cast alloys are rather close to those to be
prepared.
[21,22,23] For high-temperature mechanical tests, the
alloy specimens were prepared by vacuum induction melting
and casting, then water-knife cut along the direction of thick-
ness, and then polished to obtain cylinders of 10 mm in diam-
eter and 15 mm in height. Hardness measurements of the
Al

ness tester (Matsuzawa Seiki MV-1) under a load of 49 N and
at a loading speed of 70 /H9262m/s for 20 seconds. Scattering errors
were within 3 pct. A Gleeble 2000 testing machine (Dynamic
Systems Inc., Poestenkill, NY) was used for high-temperature
compressive tests, which were conducted from room
temperature to 1200 °C at different strain rates of 10/s and
10
/H110023/s. The crack morphologies and fracture surfaces of the
alloy specimens after Vickers hardness and Gleeble compressive
tests were observed using a JEOL* JSM-5410 scanning elec-

The wear resistance of the Al xCoCrCuFeNi alloys was

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A. Hardness and Crack Length
Figure 1 shows the Vickers hardness of the AlxCoCrCuFeNi
alloy system with different aluminum contents and the crack
lengths around the indent marks from the hardness tests. The
hardness of the alloys exhibited a strong correlation with their
aluminum content and increased from HV 133 to 655. Since
the Al
xCoCrCuFeNi alloy system had been identified as being
mostly composed of fcc and bcc solid-solution phases,[21,22,23]
the contribution of aluminum atoms to the hardness increase
was related to a solution-hardening mechanism. It was appar-
ent that aluminum not only had strong binding forces with
other metallic atoms, but also a much larger atomic radius
(1.4317 Å) than the others.
[26,27,28] Thus, both the increased
Young’s modulus and the larger lattice-strain effect were
believed to be crucial for strengthening and, therefore, were
expected to enhance the hardness of the alloys. Notably, the
transformation of the crystal structure of the alloys from fcc
into bcc phase with increasing aluminum content to about x /H11005
1.0
[21,22,23] resulted in dramatically increased hardness val-
ues, indicating that the bcc phase was stronger than the fcc
phase. Furthermore, dispersion of nanocrystallites might pro-
vide an effective precipitation strengthening, based on the
microstructure examination.[21,22,23] A nanospaced spinodal
structure might also provide a nanocomposite-strengthening
effect.
[21,22,23] These strengthening mechanisms were expected
to be responsible for the increased hardness of the alloys.
With a small addition of aluminum up to x /H110050.8, the
AlxCoCrCuFeNi alloys did not reveal any cracks around the
indent marks during the hardness tests, as seen in Figure 1,
because of the consisting ductile fcc phase. As the aluminum
content increased to about x /H110051.0, the appearance of strong
but brittle ordered bcc phase accompanied by eutectic and
modulated plate structures led to crack formation. However,
when the aluminum content continued to increase from x /H11005
1.5 to 2.0, the crack length decreased because the brittle
eutectic and modulated structures gradually disappeared.
With aluminum contents higher than x /H110052.0, the strongly
ordered bcc phase tended to dominate the matrix structure,
thus, once again enlarging the crack lengths.

C. Wear Resistance
Khruschov et al. showed that the wear resistance of mate-
rials is, in general, proportional to their Vickers hardness.
[29]
In addition, metals have much better abrasive wear resis-
tance than ceramics at the same hardness level, because of
their toughness and work-hardening ability. In this study, as
shown in Figure 11, we found that the wear resistance of
AlxCoCrCuFeNi alloys was similar to that of ferrous alloys
at the same hardness level, but seemingly insensitive to their
hardness. The wear resistance, contrary to ferrous alloys,
did not follow linearly the hardness, as seen in this figure.
The wear resistance of the Al
0.5CoCrCuFeNi alloy with a
lower hardness was relatively better than that of ferrous
alloys, whereas the wear resistance of the Al
2.0CoCrCuFeNi
alloy with a higher hardness was a little inferior.

IV. CONCLUSIONS
The Al xCoCrCuFeNi (x /H110050 to 3.0) alloys with multi-
principal elements were developed in this research, and their
mechanical properties were investigated. Due to the increased
proportion of strong bcc phase to ductile fcc phase, the solid-
solution strengthening of aluminum atoms, and the precip-
itation strengthening of nanophases, the hardnesses of the
alloys drastically increased to HV 655 with increasing alu-
minum content. The superior high-temperature strengths of
the alloys were sustained up to 800 °C, and the Al
0.5CoCr-
CuFeNi alloy, especially, exhibited an obviously positive
temperature coefficient of strength, enhanced plasticity, and
large work-hardening capability. Moreover, the wear resis-
tance of these alloys was similar to that of ferrous alloys at
the same hardness level, while the Al 0.5CoCrCuFeNi alloy
with a lower hardness exhibited relatively higher resistance
because of its large work hardening. These alloys with mul-
tiprincipal elements exhibited promising mechanical prop-
erties, including excellent elevated-temperature strength and
good wear resistance. They reveal a high potential for appli-
cation in the tool-alloy and high-temperature alloy industry.
