ABSTRACT 
Single-phase face-centered cubic (FCC) medium/high-entropy alloys (M/HEAs) have drawn much attention 
recently regarding their intriguing mechanical properties such as a good combination of strength, ductility, and 
fracture toughness. But the yield strength levels of these alloys are still low as compared to other high strength 
metallic materials. Here, both static plastic deformation (SPD) and dynamic plastic deformation (DPD) were 
employed to improve the yield strength of CrCoNi MEA. A very high yield strength of more than 900 MPa was 
obtained after SPD/DPD. The elongation rates were reduced dramatically after SPD, while a combination of 
ultrahigh yield strength of 930 MPa with a high elongation of 27% was achieved after DPD. When compared to 
SPD, a high density of nanoscale deformation twins can be observed inside the grains in the DPD sample, which 
could be responsible for the outstanding tensile properties after DPD. Our results can provide a new strategy to 
significantly increase the yield strength of FCC M/HEAs while maintaining a high elongation. 
1. Introduction 
Medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) and high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have 
generated considerable interest all over the world owing to their 
excellent mechanical properties [ 1 ]. For example, the face-centered 
cubic (FCC) CrCoNi MEA exhibits a tensile strength of nearly 1 GPa, a 
failure strain of more than 65%, and a fracture toughness value of above 
200 MPa m
1/2 
at room temperature [ 2 ]. More interestingly, their me -
chanical properties are strongly temperature/strain rate dependent with 
a simultaneous enhancement in tensile strength and ductility upon 
cryogenic/dynamic tension, due to the transition of dominated defor -
mation mechanism from dislocation slip to twinning [ 3 – 5 ]. 
However, the M/HEAs with coarse grains (CG) have a drawback of 
their relatively low yield strength, usually 150 – 450 MPa at room tem -
perature, which limits their engineering applications [ 6 , 7 ]. Thus, 
severely plastic deformation methods such as cold rolling (CR), 
high-pressure torsion (HPT), equal channel angular pressing (ECAP), 
surface mechanical attrition treatment (SMAT) and ultrasonic nano -
crystal surface modification (UNSM) have been applied to realize the 
grain refinement and therefore strengthening the M/HEA [ 8 – 12 ]. For 
example, Picak et al. [ 10 ] produced an ultrafine-grained CoCrFeMnNi 
HEA with a yield strength of as high as 925 MPa by using ECAP, but its 
elongation-to-failure dropped to only 10% since the work-hardening 
capability diminished considerably. In fact, such serious 
strength-ductility trade-off resulting from severely plastic deformation 
has already been a focusing issue for most metallic materials, including 
the M/HEAs as well. 
Besides the grain refinement, it was documented that embedded 
nano-scale twins inside the grains can effectively strengthen the metallic 
materials while maintaining considerable plasticity [ 13 , 14 ]. On the one 
hand, twin boundaries (TBs) can act as effective obstacles for dislocation 
motion. On the other hand, they can act as additional slip planes to 
accommodate the mobile dislocations. If a large number of nano-scale 
twins are embedded in grains, the materials may possess high strength 
as the TBs blocking dislocation motion, and it may have considerable 
plasticity at the same time as TBs provide additional room for disloca -
tion storage and slip. Zhang et al. [ 15 ] successfully embedded 
nano-scale deformation twins into refined grains of bulk Cu and Cu al -
loys through dynamic plastic deformation (DPD) and found that they 
exhibited much higher strength than their counterparts without

The main objective of this present work is strengthening M/HEA via

MEA as an example to generate a high density of deformation twins by

been applied in some FCC metals with medium/low stacking fault en -

It is well known that twinning and dislocation slip usually are two 
competitive mechanisms during the deformation of metallic materials. 
The dominating deformation mechanism is determined by both the 
intrinsic nature of materials, such as SFE and grain orientations, and the 
loading conditions like temperature and strain rate. Twinning can be 
facilitated by lower SFEs and/or stimulative deformation conditions, 
such as cryogenic temperature and/or high strain rate for FCC metals 
[ 29 – 31 ]. The SFE of the present CrCoNi MEA was estimated to be 22 ± 4 
mJ/m
2 
in Ref. [ 22 ], which is relatively low. Therefore, DPD with a high 
strain rate is dominated by twinning. On the one hand, due to the effect 
of viscous phonon drag on the dislocation motion, the stress values of 
FCC M/HEA should increase dramatically with an increasing strain rate 
from quasi-static to dynamic, which can promote the nucleation of

These high-density nanoscale twins are expected to promote a better 
mechanical property of CrCoNi MEA. Twin boundaries can effectively 
reduce the mean free path of dislocations and produce a high degree of 
strain hardening rate, i.e., the so-called “ dynamic Hall-Petch effect ” [ 3 ]. 
The high strain hardening rate can postpone the occurrence of necking 
to a larger strain and increase the elongation. In addition, unlike tradi -
tional strong interfaces such as grain boundaries, twin boundaries will 
not only hinder the movement of dislocations but also provide channels 
for dislocations movement, thereby avoiding stress concentration while 
remaining strengthening [ 14 ]. Therefore, a good combination of 
ultra-high strength and ductility is expected to be observed in the 
CrCoNi MEA after DPD. The following mechanical analysis confirms this 
expectation. 
3.2. Mechanical properties of the DPD and SPD samples 
The hardness values of four specimens treated in various processing 
conditions are shown in Fig. 5 . They increase significantly after the 
plastic deformation, and the hardness value of the DPD specimen is 
much higher than that of the SPD one with the same strain. Uniaxial 
tensile engineering stress-strain curves in Fig. 6 (a) show that the as- 
prepared sample exhibits a yield strength of ~430 MPa, an ultimate 
tensile strength of ~800 MPa, and an elongation rate of ~52%. An 
obvious increase in yield strength and a decrease in tensile ductility are 
observed after DPD or SPD treatments. The ultimate tensile strength of 
the sample after DPD straining to 35% is about 1065 MPa, which is 10% 
higher than that of 35% SPD-strained sample. But the elongations are 
reduced dramatically to about 20% and 18% for the DPD and SPD 
samples strained to 35%, respectively. For the sample after SPD strain -
ing to 28%, although it exhibits a good elongation of ~25%, its yield 
strength only increases to about 750 MPa. It is worth noting that a 
combination of remarkable yield strength of ~930 MPa with a high 
elongation of ~27% is achieved in the DPD sample strained to 28%. In 
addition, all samples show the typical characteristics of ductile fracture, 
as shown in the inset of Fig. 6 (a), necking and a large number of ductile 
dimples can be observed after the tensile test for the DPD sample 
strained to 28%. 
To quantify the notable properties of the current DPD treated MEA, 
the tensile strength and elongation are put in perspective in Fig. 7 , with a 
direct comparison with traditional metallic materials, conventional 
solid-solution M/HEAs as well as the H/MEAs that treated by other 
plastic deformation techniques including ECAP, CR and HPT [ 8 – 10 , 
33 – 49 ]. Clearly, for the H/MEAs treated by other methods, their 
strength can be elevated indeed, but the ductility of them are still very 
limited due to the absent dislocation accumulation ability. Owing to the 
embedded high-density nanoscale deformation twins in advance, the 
CrCoNi MEA exhibits a good combination of strength and plasticity.

4. Conclusions 
Despite having many remarkable mechanical properties, single- 
phase FCC M/HEAs are limited by a modest yield strength. In this 
work, SPD and DPD techniques were employed to produce nanotwinned 
CrCoNi MEA with a very high yield strength. Synchrotron XRD, EBSD, 
and TEM were performed to reveal the microstructure evolution of SPD/ 
DPD-treated samples. Key findings can be summarized as follows: 
1. A very high yield strength of more than 900 MPa can be obtained 
after SPD/DPD processing. Compared with SPD treatment, defor -
mation twins can be easily activated during DPD processing, which 
promotes a more significant improvement of the mechanical prop -
erties of CrCoNi MEA. 
2. The high-density deformation twins with a lower dislocation density 
can provide sufficient room to accommodate more dislocations while 
obstructing the movement of dislocations. Therefore, a good com -
bination of strength and plasticity can be obtained after DPD 
straining to 28%. 
Our work provides a new possible strategy for improving the me -
chanical properties of FCC M/HEAs via embedding nanotwins. 
Furthermore, considering that the formation of deformation twins is 
strongly temperature dependent, cryogenic DPD processing can be 
further studied in the future.
