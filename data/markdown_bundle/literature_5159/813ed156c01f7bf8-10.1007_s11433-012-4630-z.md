Evolution of misfit dislocation network and tensile properties 
in Ni-based superalloys: a molecular dynamics simulation 
WU WenPing1,2*, GUO YaFang3* & WANG YueSheng3 
1 Department of Engineering Mechanics, Wuhan University, Wuhan 430072, China;

The structural evolution of dislocation network is closely related to ′ rafting and tensile properties. In this work, the effects of 
strain rate and temperature on the structural evolution of interface dislocation netw ork in Ni-based superalloys are studied by 
molecular dynamics simulations. The correlation between the evol ution of dislocation network a nd tensile properties is also 
explored. The results indicate that the dislocation network shows different degrees of deformation and damage at various strain 
rates and temperatures. The ′ rafting depends on the damage structure of dislocation network at various strain rates and tem-
peratures. Moreover, the tensile properties of interface in Ni-bas ed superalloys are closely related to the evolution of disloc a-
tion network and dislocation motion mechanisms.

Ni-based single crystal superalloys have been used as in-
evitable materials for the blades in gas turbines due to their 
outstanding high temperature mechanical properties. The 
dislocation networks at the /′ phase interface can effec-
tively strengthen the interfac e and enhance the creep resis-
tance. The structure of dislocation network and its evolution 
under the influence of loading and temperature are linked to 
mechanical properties [1–4]. Uniaxial tension test is the 
most common mechanical test since it can provide not only 
the strength properties of the material but also the ductility 
data. The tensile behavior of Ni-based single crystal super-
alloys primarily depends on the following factors: (1) the 
morphology of ′ precipitates, (2) temperature and (3) strain 
rate [5–7]. Many researchers ha ve investigated the tensile 
deformation behavior of Ni-based superalloys at various 
strain rates and temperatures by experimental observations 
[7–15]. The effect of strain rate and temperature on flow 
stress, strain hardening and elongation behavior were ob-
served in these materials, and the correlation between de-
formation microstructure and high temperature tensile 
properties was discussed. 
As discussed above, it is important to document the me-
chanical response of Ni-based superalloys at various strain 
rates and temperatures in order to satisfy the demanding 
conditions required in the proposed applications for these 
materials. However, it is not still clear about the relation 
between tensile properties and the evolution of dislocation 
network at /′ phase interface at various strain rates and 
temperatures. Generally, it is difficult to observe the evolu-
tion of dislocation network and dislocation motion at the 
atomic scale by direct experiments. Molecular dynamics 
(MD) is the method of choice for exploring the deformation

1 Modeling and simulation 
In this work, MD simulations are performed for the ideal-
ized Ni-based superalloys composed of Ni
3Al cuboidal pre-
cipitates (L12 ′ phase) and the pure Ni matrix (fcc  phase) 
[3,16–19]. It is natural for the real Ni-based superalloys that 
the  phase is not pure Ni and the /′ microstructure has 
small amounts of other elements such as Re and Co; the 
model presented is simplified in order to extract the funda-
mentals of misfit dislocation network behavior. Both Ni and 
Ni
3Al are face-centered cubic structures, with very similar 
lattice parameters. The lattice parameter is 3.52 Å for Ni 
and 3.573 Å for Ni
3Al [20]. Because the lattice parameters 
of the two phases are not identical, the lattice mismatch is 
present. When the mismatch exceeds the limit of elasticity, 
the misfit dislocation will form on the phase interface to 
reduce the distorted energy of the system. Considering the 
concept of coincidence site lattice (CSL) on the misfit phase 
interface, we can write such a relation as [19]:

(100) phase interface model. The model is divided into two

atoms. The top half-part is Ni, with box vectors 67[010], 
67[001] and 24[100], respectively; and the bottom half-part 
is Ni
3Al, with box vectors 66[010], 66[001] and 24[100], 
respectively. In the simulation, periodic boundary condi-
tions are applied in the directions parallel to the phase in-
terface ( X and Y directions), while the fixed-displacement 
boundary condition is applied perpendicular to the phase

interface in the loading direction ( Z direction). The outmost 
four atom layers in the Z direction are identified as the

Since the lattice misfit causes th e stress field at the inter-
face, the phase interface beco mes unstable. Based on the 
principle of minimum energy, the atoms on the phase inter-
face will rearrange in order to minimize the elastic stress 
field between the  phase and the ′ phase. So a full relaxa-
tion should be performed before the tensile load is applied. 
The model is relaxed by MD calculation of 10000 time 
steps, and the relaxation is carried out by integrating New-
ton’s equations of motion for all atoms using a time step of 
6×10
15 s. The misfit dislocation networks are formed on the 
phase interface after relaxation. Then, the tensile load is 
applied along the Z direction every on e time step through 
moving the boundary atoms, which results in the specimen 
elongated along the Z direction. This simulation procedure 
is the same as that in our prev ious work [3]. In the present 
study, three different strain rates (10
9, 0.5×10 10, 10 10 s1) 
and three different temperatures (0, 300, 800 K) are applied 
to simulate the evolution of dislocation networks and de-
formation mechanisms. The temperature is kept invariant 
during the simulations, which is achieved by scaling all at-
oms instantaneous velocities with the appropriate Max-
well-Boltzmann distribution at a specified temperature. The 
XMD program is used in the atomistic simulations [21].

3 Discussion 
3.1 The effect of strain rate and temperature on ′ 
rafting 
Researchers have investigated ′ directional coarsening 
(rafting) behavior of the [001] Ni-based superalloys at 
various stresses and temperatures [22]. Their experimental 
results verify the effect of the stress and temperature on the 
′ directional coarsening (raftin g). It is well known that the 
irregular coarsened ′ precipitates are enveloped by the mis-
fit dislocation networks over the /′ interfaces during creep 
[2–4]. The damage structure of dislocation networks under 
the influence of loading and temperature has close relation-
ship with the ′ rafting of Ni-based superalloys [2–4]. 
For the (100) interface, at the lower strain rate of 10 9 s1, 
the irregular square dislocation network is found to be lo-
cally damaged at one node. The locally damaged network 
loses the co-coordinating role of maintaining the dynamic 
equilibrium, so that a large number of dislocations are piled 
up at some regions where the network has been damaged.

promote the ′ rafting. 
In contrast, from the simulation results in sect. 2.2, it can 
be seen that the four nodes of the irregular square disloca-
tion network are almost simultaneously damaged at the 
higher strain rate of 10 10 s1. The dislocations are not piled 
up at some local regions while the dislocation network is 
fully and synchronously damaged. The dislocation network 
will not cause the local stress concentration and will not 
lead to the stress difference in two matrix channels, so it is 
difficult for ′ rafting to occur under this condition.

dislocation network are different at various strain rates, the 
yield strength and plasticity of alloy are different. In sect.

strain condition, for a lower strain rate, the dislocation net-
work shows a greater deformati on while for a higher strain 
rate, the dislocation network shows a smaller deformation. 
In other words, the damage and destruction of dislocation 
network need to achieve a greater strain at a higher strain 
rate than a lower strain rate. Therefore, the alloy yield stress 
and tensile strength increase with the increase of the strain

4 Conclusions 
Based on the above simulation, it can be concluded that the 
effect of strain rate and temperature on the tensile properties 
is closely related to the damage features of dislocation net-
work. Some key points are summarized as follows. 
(1) The dislocation network shows different degrees of 
damage at various strain rates and temperatures. For a lower 
strain rate, the square dislocation network is found to be 
locally damaged at one node. The deformation mechanism 
is characterized by a/2 <110> dislocations cut into the ′ 
phase. For a higher strain rate, the four nodes of dislocation 
network are almost simultaneously damaged, and the dislo-
cations have not directly cut into the ′ phase, while a small 
void is found at the interface. The interface strength de-
creases with the temperature increasing and the dislocation 
network is easier to damage. 
(2) The  ′ rafting shows different difficulty levels at 
various strain rates and temperatures. The ′ rafting is prone 
to occur at a lower strain rate and becomes easier at a higher 
temperature. 
(3) The dislocation motion and deformation mechanisms 
are different at various strain rates and temperatures, which 
leads to the different tensile properties of interfaces. The 
yield strength and ultimate tensile strength increase with the 
increase of strain rate, while decreasing with the increase of 
the temperature. The plastic ity and ductility decrease with 
the increase of strain rate, while increasing with the increase 
of the temperature.
