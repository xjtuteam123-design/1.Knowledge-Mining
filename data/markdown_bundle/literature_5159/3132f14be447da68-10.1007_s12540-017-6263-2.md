In this study, A series of the high strength (T82Sn18)100-xNbx (x=0, 1, 3, 5, and 9 at%) ultrafine eutectic alloys
with large plasticity are developed by suction casting method. The Ti82Sn18 binary eutectic alloy consists of a
mixture of a hcp Ti3Sn and a α-Ti phases having the plate-like lamellar type duplex structure with micro scaled
eutectic colony. From the (T82Sn18)97Nb3, the alloy display structural heterogeneous distribution of ultrafine-
scaled phases composed of β-Ti(Nb) solid solution surrounded by alternating plate-like shaped Ti 3Sn and
α-Ti phases. With increasing Nb content, the volume fraction of β-Ti is continuously increased, which
induced improving mechanical properties both strength and plasticity. Especially, (Ti 82Sn18)91Nb9 alloy has
the outstanding combination of the high strength ( σy1.1 GPa) and large plasticity ( εp36%) at room tem-
perature.
Keywords: alloys, microstructure, casting, mechanical properties, Ti-based eutectic alloy
1. INTRODUCTION
Nano-/ultrafine eutectic composites have been highlighted
as novel application because of their remarkably high strength
for decades [1-6]. However, these materials commonly show
a limited room temperature plasticity and toughness by highly
localized shear bands before fracture [5-12]. For that reason,
these materials are limited to use in commercial application.
So, improving the room temperature plasticity of nano-/
ultrafine eutectic composites has been important issue in
field of structural materials [13,14]. In order to overcome the
drawback, nanostructure-dendrite composite having micron-
scale dendrites and ultrafine eutectic matrix have been devel-
oped to enhance the plasticity and toughness during defor-
mation [15-19]. High strength combined with considerable
plasticity can be obtained in these composites [19,39,40]. More-
over, modulating the microstructure by tuning the composition
can control the strength and plasticity of the nano-/ultrafine
eutectic composites, which result from change of volume
fraction, morphology and length-scale of the individual phases
[13,20-23,41]. For example, the nano-/ultrafine-structure and
the phases formed in Ti-based ultrafine eutectic composites
present a superior ultimate compressive strength of 2.4 GPa
and a large plastic strain of more than 10% with control of
the Cu + Ni and Nb + Sn contents [13]. However, it definitely
causes that the enhancement of plasticity by the structural
heterogeneity comes along the deteriorating the strength.
Recently, it has been reported that bimodal eutectic struc-
ture consisting of different constitutive phases and length scale
on the individual eutectic structure, introducing the chemical
heterogeneity in the eutectic structure, have been developed
to overcome the tradeoff between the strength and plasticity of
the nano-/ultrafine eutectic-dendrite composites [24-29]. The
bimodal eutectic is formed by a pseudo quasi-peritectic reaction
on solidification, which reveals the homogeneous behavior
upon deformation without the decrease of strength [15,26].
Furthermore, Lee et al. demonstrated that the deformation
mechanisms of bimodal ultrafine eutectic composites is
possible to propose three different interface-controlled deforma-
tion modes to release the stress concentration; (I) rotational
boundary mode at the colony boundaries between the pri-
mary and matrix eutectic structure, (II) accumulated interface
mode considered as an unique way to accommodate the
strain inside the primary colonies and (III) individual inter-

2. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
A series of (Ti82Sn18)100-xNbx (x = 0, 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 at%)
ingots was prepared by arc melting a mixture of pure ele-
ments with high purity (>99.9%, supplied by the Research
and Development Korea Corp.) under Ti-gettered high-purity
argon atmosphere. The master ingots were re-melted five times
to insure compositional homogeneity. Rod-type samples with
a diameter of 3 mm and height of 50 mm were fabricated using
a suction casting facility. Phase analysis of the alloys was
examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD: Rigaku-D/MAX-2500/
PC) using Cu Kα radiation. Scanning electron microscope (SEM:
JEOL JSM-6390) and transmission electron microscope (TEM:
Tecnai-F20) coupled with energy-dispersive spectrometry (EDX)
of the as-cast samples were utilized for structural investigation.
Thin foil of TEM specimens were prepared by conventional
ion polishing system with liquid nitrogen cooling (Gatan Model
691). Mechanical properties were obtained from uniaxial
compressive test with 2:1 aspect ratio cylindrical specimens
using an initial strain rate of 10
-3
/s at room temperature. Indenta-
tion tests were carried out using a Nanoindentation tester
(CSM, NHT-X) with a standard Berkovich diamond tip. The
results of indentations are obtained over 50 times of indents.
3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Figure 1 shows XRD patterns of the as-cast (Ti82Sn18)100-xNbx
(x = 0, 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 at%) alloys. From the XRD patterns,
it is identified that the Ti82Sn18 alloy consists of a mixture of
an hcp Ti3Sn (P63/mmc) intermetallic compound and a α-Ti
(P63/mmc) solid solution phase [31]. For the addition of Nb
1 at%, there is no change on the diffraction peaks compared to
Ti-Sn eutectic alloy. With increasing Nb content up to 9 at%,
the diffraction peak indicated a bcc β-Ti (Im3m) solid solu-
tion phase is observed and the peak intensity of β-Ti phase
is increased, continuously. These results reveal that the addi-
tion of Nb content results in the formation of β-Ti solid solu-
tion phase and strongly suppresses phase transformation from
β-Ti to α-Ti phase.

4. CONCLUSIONS
In the present study, a series of (Ti 82Sn18)100-xNbx alloys
with x = 0, 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9 at% have been developed. The
Ti-Sn eutectic alloy is composed of lamellar type duplex structure
of α-Ti solid solution phase and Ti
3Sn intermetallic com-
pound surrounded by Ti3Sn boundary. For Nb 1 at% addition
alloy, the refinement of grain size can be effectively induced
without change of phases. With increasing the addition of
Nb contents, Ti-Sn-Nb ultraf ine eutectic alloys are com-
posed of β-Ti(Nb) solid solution, Ti
3Sn and α-Ti phases and
the volume fraction of β-Ti(Nb) phase is continuously increased,
which induced improving mechanical properties both strength
and plasticity. The structural heterogeneous distribution of
ultrafine-scaled phases in Ti-Sn-Nb ultrafine eutectic alloys is
effective to improve both strength and plasticity. Especially,
the (Ti
82Sn18)91Nb9 alloy which has near 84% of volume
fraction of β-Ti(Nb) phase reveals excellent combination of
the high yield strength (~1.1 GPa) and good plasticity (~36%).
