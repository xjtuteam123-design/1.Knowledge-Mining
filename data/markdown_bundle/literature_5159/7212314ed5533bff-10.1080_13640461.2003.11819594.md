Introduction 
High chromium cast irons have been applied to high 
temperature abrasion resistant parts for energy plants, 
steel plants, mineral plants etcu. However, the recent 
environmental problem demands the development of high 
performance alloys to attain the longer service life at 
higher operation temperature in practical applications. 
Many research works on the dry and wet abrasion 
resistance of high alloy cast irons indicate that a high 
oxidization resistance, high hardness at high temperature 
and proper distribution of hard phases are essential to the 
high temperature abrasion resistant alloy. The preliminary

experiments show that the oxidization of conventional 
high Cr cast iron proceeds by the selective oxidation of 
matrix, and the eutectic and primary carbides with a higher 
amount of Cr suppress the oxidization. Since lower Cr 
content of matrix leads to a severe oxidation, the Cr 
content of high performance alloy should be adjusted to a 
higher level corresponding to the C level (the amount of 
carbide). Higher amount ofNi might also be desirable to 
get an austenitic matrix similar to heat resistant steels. 
Based on the estimation of Cr distribution in carbide and 
matrix, the composition of 35-40%Cr-9%Ni is selected in 
this study. To develop new heat and wear resistant alloy, 
the influences of Mo (alloy elements of heat resistant 
steel) as well as V and Nb (hard MC type carbide formers) 
on the structure, the oxidization behavior and the 
high-temperature compressive strength of a series of alloy 
were investigated. 
Experimental Procedures 
As a preliminary experiment, hypo-eutectic, eutectic and 
hyper-eutectic 20% and 25%Cr irons, shown in Table I, 
are prepared. The oxidation behaviors were investigated 
by holding the 20mmx30mmx5mm specimens in an 
atmospheric kanthal furnace at 923K, 1073K and 1223K 
for 10 to 300hr. The compressive strengths were also 
measured at temperature from 298K to 1 083K. Cr contents 
and volume ratios of matrix and carbide were estimated 
with Thermo-calc and related with the measured 
properties. The same experiments were conducted on 
conventional stainless steels (JIS SUS403, SUS430, 
SUS304), and the results were compared with those of 
high chromium cast irons.

Results and Discussions 
Oxidation Behaviors of High Chromium Cast Iron 
The measurement of weight gain due to oxidation, the 
analysis of oxide film by both SEM and X-ray diffraction, 
and the EPMA analysis on the oxidized scale were carried 
out each oxidized specimen. The amount of carbides as 
well as the Cr contents of both carbide and matrix are 
evaluated on 20%Cr and 25%Cr cast irons with 
Thermo-Calc3• Fig. I shows an example of Thermo-Calc 
evaluations at 1223K. The amounts of M7C3 in the 20%Cr 
cast irons are a little lower than those of25%Cr cast irons, 
because the solubility ofC and Cr becomes lower with the 
increase in Cr content of alloy. The Cr contents of y in 
20%Cr cast irons are in the range of 3-7% and those in 
25%Cr cast irons are 5-l 0%, and they are considerably 
lower than the average Cr content of alloy. On the other 
hand, the Cr contents ofM 7C3 are 43-63% in 20%Cr cast 
irons and 56-72% in 25%Cr cast irons. To verify the 
calculated values, the 25%Cr eutectic specimen was kept 
at 1223K for 36ks and then quenched into ice water. The 
matrix of alloy was extracted by the iodine-methanol 
method. The chemical analysis revealed that the 
chromium content of the matrix of this heat treated iron 
was 5.9%, which was well coincident with the values 
evaluated by Thermo-Calc.

content of austenite matrix of the cast iron is much lower.

matrix. However, the oxidation resistance of 20%Cr cast

Though similar oxidation characteristics are observed on

Secondary carbide particles precipitate during heating 
the specimen at high temperature. These fine M23C6 
particles might suppress the oxidation of matrix. The 
lower the heating temperature is, the more secondary 
M23C6 particles precipitates, which leads to the lower Cr

distribution of carbides are essential to improve the 
oxidation resistance of high Cr cast iron. EPMA analysis 
revealed that a smaller amount of carbide particles 
distribute especially in the inner layer of oxide film, 
indicating they are gradually oxidized in oxide film. 
The oxidation rate of 25%Cr cast iron is much smaller

stainless steels. Because the eutectic is a kind of composite 
composed of fine austenite and carbide, the oxidation of 
matrix is suppressed by the carbide. Cr ions might diffuse 
from Cr-rich oxide formed on carbide to the oxide on

Compressive strength of high chromium cast 
irons at high temperature 
The compressive strength of high chromium cast irons in 
Table I and stainless steels were examined at temperatures 
from 298K to 1073K, by using the specimens with the 
dimensions of 3mmx3mmx7mm. The tests were 
conducted at strain rate of 1. 7x 10"2 /sec with an 
lnstron-type machine in Ar atmosphere. 
Fig. 7 shows the compressive strength in relation to the 
test temperature. The Vickers hardness of the specimens 
are also shown in the figure. The almost parallel changes 
of the compressive strength and the Vickers hardness 
indicate that the compressive strength can be a reasonable 
index of hardness. High chromium cast irons show higher 
strength above I ,OOOMPa until 773K, however at higher 
temperature the values significantly drop to the similar 
level as those of stainless steels at I 083K. Therefore, the 
enhancement of strength at higher temperature is 
necessary for the high temperature abrasion resistant 
irons.

developed alloys, static oxidation experiments were also 
conducted at air atmosphere. Fig. 9 shows the results 
under the temperatures of 1073K and 1223K. All the 
Nb-free specimens showed excellent oxidation resistance. 
The weight gains are about 0.3mg/cm2 at 1073K, almost

Conclusions 
The following conclusions are obtained from this study. 
I. Primary and eutectic M7C3 suppress the oxidation of 
high Cr cast iron. Both the 20%Cr and 25%Cr cast irons 
exhibits higher oxidation resistance than stainless steels 
at 1223K. 
2. The internal oxidation of high Cr cast iron proceeds 
along the carbide-matrix interface. 
3. In the case of 25%Cr cast iron, the continuous Cr20 3 
film forms on the alloy surface, and contribute to the 
superior oxidation resistance of the alloy. 
4. Primary and eutectic M7C3 carbide in high Cr-Y cast 
iron, containing higher amount of V, are selectively 
oxidized. 
5. Fe-36%Cr-9%Ni-5%Mo-2.3%/3.5%C alloys show a 
superior oxidation resistance and high compressive 
strength.
