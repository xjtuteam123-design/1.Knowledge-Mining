High Strength, Ductility and Superplasticity Mg-
6Zn-1Y-0.6Ce-0.6Zr Alloy Prepared by Rapid 
Solidification and Reciprocating Extrusion
YANG Wenpeng1, GUO Xuefeng1*, REN Fang2
(1. School of Materials Science and Engineering, Henan Polytechnic University, Jiaozuo 45000, China; 2. School of Economics and 
Management, Henan Polytechnic University, Jiaozuo 454000, China)
Abstract: High strength, ductility, and superplasticity Mg-6.0%Zn-1.0%Y-0.6%Ce-0.6%Zr (wt%) 
alloy was prepared by sequentially applying rapid solidification, extrusion, and reciprocating extrusion (REX). 
The microstructure of the alloy after 2-pass REX consisted of fine grains smaller than 0.7 μm and nanometer 
strengthening particles. The refined grains resulted from recrystallization during which the nanometer particles 
played an important role in restrain grain growth. The mechanical properties of the material were investigated 
at room and elevated temperatures. High tensile yield strength of 336 MPa and elongation of 27% were 
obtained at room temperature. At elevated temperatures, the highest elongation of 270% was obtained at 250 
℃ and an initial strain rate of 3.3×10
-3 s-1, and LTS and HSRS were achieved. The high strength, ductility, and 
superplasticity were attributed to the refined unique microstructure.
Key words: magnesium alloy; rapid solidification; reciprocating extrusion; superplasticity

1 Introduction
Magnesium and its alloys are very attractive for 
transportation
[1]
 and aerospace
[2]
 industries for energy 
saving. Casting, specifically die-casting
[3-5]
, is the 
dominant fabrication method. The variety of products 
made of wrought magnesium alloys is limited because 
magnesium alloys are difficult to be deformed into 
different shapes needed for structural applications
[6]
. 
Therefore, improving the ductility and strength of 
wrought magnesium alloys is the key to increase their 
applications. As a consequence, rapid solidification 
(RS)
[7-10]
 and severe plastic deformation
[11-15]
 have 
been adopted to achieve the goal. Recent researches 
revealed that superplasticity can be achieved for 
magnesium alloys with fine grains smaller than 10 μm 
at elevated temperatures
[14]
. High-strain-rate ( 10-2
 
s-1
) superplasticity (HSRS) and low-temperature (T
0.5 T m, Tm the melting point of Mg) superplasticity 
(LTS) have also been reported
[11,13,16]
 in some alloys 
prepared by combined processes which give fine grains 
no more than 1 μm. It is well established that high-
strength ZK60 alloy with grain size smaller than 2 μm 
exhibits LTS and HSRS
[16,17]
. Recently, it is suggested 
that the addition of rare earth elements could improve 
the mechanical properties both at room temperature 
and elevated temperature
[18]
. We previously reported 
the microstructural evolution of Mg-6.0Zn-1.0Y-
0.6Ce-0.6Zr alloy, which was designed based on ZK60 
alloy by adding 1% Y and 0.6% Ce, and found that 
the alloy prepared by reciprocating extrusion (REX) 
and extrusion (EX) from rapidly solidified ribbons 
had an average grain size of about 0.7 μm and densely 
dispersed nano-dimension precipitate of about 80 
nm
[19]
. In the present paper, we report the excellent 
mechanical properties of Mg-6.0Zn-1.0Y-0.6Ce-0.6Zr 
alloy at room and elevated temperatures, as well as the 
HSRS and LTS.

prepared by pouring Mg-6Zn-1Y-0.6Ce-0.6Zr alloy 
melt into a copper mould with diameter of about 52

Rapidly solidified ribbons were produced by remelting 
the cast ingots in a single-roller melt-spinning machine. 
The thickness of the RS ribbon was about 80 μm, the 
cooling rate was estimated to be about 2.571 06 ℃/s
according to the cooling rate formula
[20]
. Before 
extrusion RS ribbons were comminuted into chips with 
a dimension of 2 mm×2 mm, and then hot-compressed 
into a cylindrical billet with a diameter of 200 mm at 
150 ℃. The billet was extruded into cylindrical bar 
with diameter of 50 mm at 350 ℃. The bar was cut 
into 55 mm in length for REX. REX was carried out at 
330 ℃ by different passes. The detailed processes were 
reported elsewhere
[21]
. Finally, the REXed material was 
extruded into cylindrical bar with diameter of 10 mm 
at 300 ℃, the material was designated as REX-n -EX-
RS66, where n is the REX pass number. The sample 
photos after each procedure are shown in Fig.1.
2.2 Microstructures
Specimens for microstructural analysis were 
prepared from bars and etched with 5% HNO
3 in 
95% ethanol. Microstructures were observed using 
optical microscopy and scanning electron microscope 
(SEM). The grain size was measured on a Philips 
CM30 transmission electron microscope (TEM) at an 
acceleration voltage of 300 kV . The detailed preparing 
procedure of the TEM specimen was reported in 
reference
[21]
.

Room temperature tensile tests were conducted at

4.0×10
-4
 s -1

The dog-bone tensile specimens for elevated

with tensile axes parallel to the extruded direction. The 
specimens had gauge length of 5 mm and cross-section

2

at a constant cross-head speed in air. The initial strain 
rate range was from 3.3×10
-4
 to 3.3×10 -2
 s -1
. The 
cross-head speed of the 810 material test system (MTS) 
and the temperature of the furnace attached on the MTS 
were controlled by a computer system via Test Star IIs 
software. The heating times prior to tests were 30 min 
to 150 ℃ and 170 ℃, 40 min to 200 ℃ and 60 min to 
250 ℃. The fluctuations of the temperature during tests 
were 2 ℃ between 150 and 200 ℃, and 5 ℃ at 250 ℃.

In addition to grain size, the mechanical proper-
ties of wrought Mg alloy also depended on the shape, 
size and distribution of strengthening particles. 
As-cast 66 alloy contained continuous network of 
intermetallics that formed at grain boundaries
[19]
. After 
severe plastic deformation, the intermetallics was 
broken into small particles along extrusion direction. 
However, comparing with the strengthening particles in 
the alloy prepared from RS ribbon, the particles in as-
cast counterpart were coarse and had sharp boundaries, 
which could cause stress concentration and result in 
limited ductility. Furthermore, the Mg alloy processed 
by REX has random texture that is beneficial to achieve 
high tensile ductility
[22]
. Therefore, the outstanding 
mechanical properties of REX-EX-RS66 were 
attributed to the uniform microstructure. 
4 Conclusions
a) Fine grain Mg-6Zn-1Y-0.6Ce-0.6Zr 
alloy with an average grain size of 0.7 μm and 
nanometer strengthening particles was prepared by a 
combinational process of RS, EX and REX. 
b) Room temperature mechanical properties 
were tested at an initial strain rate of 4.0×10
-4
 s -1
. 
The highest ultimate tensile strength of 340 MPa was 
achieved in the REX-2-EX-RS66 alloy, and the highest 
elongation of 27% was obtained in REX-4-EX-RS66 
alloy.
c) For REX-2-EX-RS66 alloy, when tested at 
elevated temperatures, a high ultimate tensile strength 
of 208 MPa was obtained at 150 ℃ and an initial strain 
rate 3.3×10
-3

obtained at 250 ℃ and 3.3×10 -3
 s -1
 strain rate; The 
material had LTS and HSRS.
