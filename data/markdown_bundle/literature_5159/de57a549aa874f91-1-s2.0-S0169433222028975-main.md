1. Introduction 
Iridium-based refractory superalloys have been pursued as the next 
generation structural materials after nickel-based materials owing to 
their irreplaceable ultrahigh temperature properties [1 – 5] . The Ir-based 
superalloys are used as key mechanical components in extreme envi -
ronments such as rocket combustion chambers and nuclear fuel con -
tainers for use on space stations [6] . New Ir-based alloys continue to 
attract research interest because of their tremendous potential in mul -
tiple fields such as aviation and nuclear materials. Yamabe et al. [7,8] 
developed a series of Ir-based superalloys possessing dual-phase γ / γ’ 
structures, with γ’ alloy precipitates (L1
2
– Ir
3
X , X = Hf, Nb, Ta, Ti, V, and 
Zr) coherently embedded in a face-centered (Ir) γ matrix. These dual- 
phase materials exhibit remarkable mechanical properties, super-high 
melting temperatures ( > 2300 K), and corrosion resistance at elevated 
temperatures. These γ / γ’ structures impart strength to the alloy through 
several mechanisms, such as order and coherency strengthening [9 – 11] . 
Lattice misfit and coherency between the γ and γ’ phases at elevated 
temperatures are key parameters for these dual-phase materials [12,13] . 
The coarsening of the γ’ phase caused by a large misfit adversely impact 
the high-temperature mechanical properties of superalloys. Meanwhile, 
a low misfit is preferred for high-temperature creep resistance and the 
stress rupture life [14] . Therefore, a lattice misfit as small as possible is 
considered desirable for most commercial alloys. Earlier experimental 
studies on Ir-based alloys focused on the microstructural features [15] , 
compressive strength [16] as well as the plastic flow behavior and creep 
properties [17] . However, reports on high-temperature lattice misfit and 
coherence at the Ir/Ir

2. Methods and details 
The density functional theory calculations were conducted using the 
projector-augmented wave method [23,24] as implemented in the 
Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [25,26] . The Per -
dew – Burke – Ernzerhof (PBE) electronic exchange – correlation functional 
within the generalized gradient approximation was adopted [27] . A 
plane-wave basis set for electron wave function with a cutoff energy of 
600 eV was employed. The interaction between the ions and electrons 
was described by the ultra-soft pseudopotential. The Monkhorst – Pack 
scheme [28] was used for the numerical integration in the first Brillouin 
zone with a grid of 0.02 Å
 1 
for all the cases. The total energy and the 
Hellmann – Feymann force of the atom were set to 1 meV/atom and 
0.001 eV/Å, respectively. Phonon frequencies were calculated using the 
PHONOPY code [29] . The temperature dependence of cell volume was 
evaluated by fitting the energy versus isothermal volume to the Vinet 
equation of state [30,31] .
