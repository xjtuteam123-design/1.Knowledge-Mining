1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) with multiple principal elements have 
emerged as an innovative design approach to alloy design with great 
potential for industrial applications, due to their distinct properties [1] . 
Current HEAs, however, still suffer from the long-standing strength- -
ductility trade-off dilemma, e.g., these increasing strength is often 
accompanied by a loss in ductility [2] . To tackle this dilemma, efforts 
have been extensively made through incorporating various strength -
ening strategies, such as metastability-engineering [3] and solid solution 
strengthening [4] . Metastability-engineering strategy was applied in 
non-equiatomic Fe

(700 MPa ~ 900 MPa) and uniform elongation (23 % ~ 70 %) [3,5,6] . 
Then, existing relatively low yield strength (less than 400 MPa) in these 
MHEAs becomes a key scientific issue.
Enhancing yield strength of the HEAs through solid solution 
strengthening strategy was proved to be effective and feasible [7] . 
Moreover, interstitial elements (e.g., small-size non-metallic B, C, N, and 
O) provide a comparable or even greater solid solution strengthening 
effect to the HEAs than intrinsic substitutional atoms (e.g., Fe, Mn, Co, 
Cr), because of severe lattice distortions caused by atomic-size differ -
ences between non-metallic and metallic elements [8] . This approach 
not only enables precise control over phase stability but also triggers

4. Discussion
4.1. Carbon interstitial-supersaturated solid solution
Dissolution of carbon in FCC structure was extensively demonstrated 
to strengthen HEAs [8,38,72] . The existence of carbon as an interstitial 
solid solution can be attributed to atomic size considerations: carbon has 
a significantly smaller atomic radius (C: 0.07 nm) than the matrix ele -
ments (Fe: 0.14 nm, Mn: 0.14 nm, Co: 0.135 nm, Cr: 0.14 nm, Si: 
0.11 nm) in present Fe-Mn-Co-Cr-Si alloy system [73] . Due to this size 
mismatch, carbon atoms tend to dissolve as interstitial atoms in the FCC 
lattice and preferentially occupy octahedral interstitial sites, as pro -
posed by previous studies on carbon-containing CrMnFeCoNi and 
Fe
50
Mn
30
Co
10
Cr
10 
alloys [24,74] . In present SLMed alloys with carbon 
content ≤ 3 at%, no carbides can be detected in XRD patterns ( Fig. 3 ) 
and observed by SEM-BSE ( Fig. 4 (a

( Fig. 5 ), confirming the formation of carbon interstitial supersaturated 
solid solutions.
It is well known that the solubility of interstitial solid solutions is 
predominantly governed by the cooling rates employed in the fabrica -
tion process. For example, the solid solubility of carbon in Fe
