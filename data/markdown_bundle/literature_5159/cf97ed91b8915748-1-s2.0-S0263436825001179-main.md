ABSTRACT
Preparing Cu
–
Nb alloys on copper substrate by laser directed energy deposition (LDED) can improve the 
ablative resistance effectively. However, the mechanical properties of the Cu
–
Nb alloys deteriorate due to the 
weak Cu
–
Nb interfacial bonding. To address this challenge, we propose a strategy that an interphase TiO is 
introduced to optimize the interface structure. Adding Ti element can significantly transform the morphology of 
the Nb from cellular structure to fine dendrite. It was found that the formation of TiO interphase can transform 
the incoherent Cu/Nb interface into the semi-coherent Cu/TiO/Nb interface that the dislocations can transmit 
across more easily. Therefore, the Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloys exhibit higher total elongation. In addition, the Cu- 
Nb-Ti deposited alloys exhibit higher strength due to the grain boundary strengthening and solute solution 
strengthening, and the Cu-Nb-1.5Ti deposited alloy achieves the most excellent synthetic performance.
1. Introduction
To improve the ablative resistance of the copper alloys, refractory 
metal such as W, Mo and Nb will be added [ 1 – 3 ]. In recent years, copper 
alloys with addition of the refractory metals have been widely used in 
the electricity and aerospace industries due to their excellent conduc -
tivity and ablative resistance [ 4 , 5 ]. By adjusting the contents of re -
fractory metal and Cu, the excellent properties of refractory metal and 
copper can be combined perfectly [ 6 , 7 ].
At present, the fabrication of Cu-X (X stands for W, Mo and Nb) 
components is mainly through powder metallurgy (PM) technology 
which requires not only dedicated tools, but also complicated process 
[ 8 , 9 ]. Generally, the sintering temperature in the process of PM tech -
nology is lower than 2200 
◦
C [ 10 ]. The melting points of W and Mo are 
3420 
◦
C and 2622 
◦
C [ 11 , 12 ], which is even higher than the boiling 
point of Cu, so the W or Mo in the copper alloy is in a state of granular. 
The poor wettability and mutual immiscibility between refractory 
metals and Cu would result in the weak interfacial bonding, thus the 
mechanical properties deteriorate, especially in elongation [ 13 – 15 ].
Laser directed energy deposition (LDED) is one kind of laser-based 
additive manufacturing (AM) technologies that implements a high- 
energy laser beam to melt deposited materials delivered by a powder 
feeder system [ 16 , 17 ]. Comparing with PM technology, LDED can 
achieve the fraction of large-scale and complex metal parts [ 18 , 19 ]. Due 
to the high laser reflectivity of Cu and the high melting point of the 
refractory metal, the LDED of copper alloys composed of high- 
conductivity and refractory metals is more challenging. Su et al. fabri -
cated a densified W
–
Cu composite free of apparent cracks and pores via 
LDED, but the mechanical property was not performed [ 20 ]. Wang et al. 
utilized LDED to obtain W
–
Cu composites on the surface of copper alloy 
with the elongation no more than 5 % [ 19 ].
Different from W and Mo, Nb is miscible with Cu in the liquid phase. 
The melting point of Nb is 2468 
◦
C lower than the boiling point of Cu 
[ 21 ], making it possible that Nb can be in a state of dendrite in the 
copper alloy. At present, most studies relevant to Cu
–
Nb materials were 
based on simulations [ 22 , 23 ]. The methods to fabricate the Cu
–
Nb al -
loys are accumulative roll bonding [ 24 , 25 ] and magnetron sputtering 
[ 26 ]. The Nb and Cu are not mutually soluble in solid phase, and the Cu/ 
Nb interface has a weak bonding strength. The Cu/Nb interfaces are very 
strong traps for glide dislocations and effective barriers for slip trans -
mission [ 23 , 27 ]. Researches have confirmed that interface is pivotal in 
overcoming the deformation incompatibility of composite [ 28 , 29 ].

results will be helpful for the further development of the copper alloys

2. Experimental procedures
2.1. Materials and specimen preparation
Nb and CuCrZr or CuTiCrZr, seen in in the reference [ 31 ], were 
designed as the raw materials, respectively. A copper alloy (1.8 – 3.0 wt% 
Ni, 0.4 – 1.0 wt% Si, 0.2 – 0.8 wt% Cr and bal. Cu) blocked with a 
dimension of 200 mm × 100 mm × 80 mm was selected as the substrate. 
The CuCrZr and CuTiCrZr powders were mechanically mixed to regulate 
the content of Ti using a planetary ball mill (Turbula T2F Mixer) at 200 
rpm for 5 h, and the powders were dried in a vacuum oven at 110 
◦
C for 
2 h to remove the moisture before the experiment.
As shown in Fig. 1 (a), the LDED system employed for the processing 
consists of a 20 kW fiber laser, a 6-axis robot, a homemade laser head 
and a double hopper powder feeder. The optimized processing param -
eters for samples are listed in Table 1 . As the laser scanned, the deposited 
alloys with a dimension of 120 mm × 60 mm × 3.5 mm were manu -
factured. The chemical composition of the deposited alloys measured by 
using the inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectrometry 
(ICP-OES) is shown in Table 2 . The deposited alloys were defined as 
Cu
–
Nb, Cu-Nb-1.5Ti and Cu-Nb-3Ti based on the content of Ti, 
respectively.

2.3. Property testing

4. Discussion
4.1. Evolution of the microstructure
Fig. 11 shows the schematic diagram of the deposited alloys forma -
tion mechanism. In terms of Cu
–
Nb deposited alloy without Ti addition, 
the mixed powders were melted during the process of LDED. According 
to the phase diagram of the Cu

the Cu

the liquid phase at high temperature, Nb and Cu atoms were infinitely 
miscible and uniformly distributed, as shown in Fig. 11 (a). With the 
movement of laser beam, leading to a sharp decrease temperature, 
Cu
–

the Nb-rich droplets L

Due to the collision and coalescence of the moving droplets by Mar -
angoni motion and natural convection, some of the Nb-rich droplets 
grew in size [ 33 , 34 ]. As the molten pool temperature was dropped to 
1948 K, the Nb-rich droplets underwent a monotectic reaction L

the solidification of the Cu-rich liquid finished.
For Cu-Nb-1.5Ti and Cu-Nb-3Ti deposited alloy, as shown in Fig. 11
(b). On the one hand, Ti was solubilized in the Cu and Nb at high tem -
perature, and the Ti was released from Nb with the temperature 
decreasing. As the whole process of LDED was in the air, despite powders

formation of TiO in preference to other Titanium oxides in low oxygen 
environment [ 35 ]. The TiO is the first to form and can directly serve as

4.2. Influences of Ti addition on ductility
Fig. 13 presents the TEM analysis of the Cu
–
Nb and Cu-Nb-3Ti 
deposited alloys after tensile test. The plastic deformation of the crys -
tal is aided by the motion and multiplication of glide dislocations under 
stress. It has been demonstrated that the Cu/Nb interface is an inco -
herent interface [ 23 ]. The interfaces are able to act as the barrier to 
dislocations glide. The incoherent Cu
–
Nb interfaces are weak interfaces 
with low shear strength, and the Cu
–
Nb interfaces are easily sheared 
under the stress field of the glide dislocations during the plastic defor -
mation [ 23 ]. The sheared Cu
–
Nb interfaces can generate an attractive 
force on the glide dislocation [ 23 ]. Therefore, large quantities of glide 
dislocations are trapped within the Cu/Nb interfaces and then pile up at 
the interfaces, as shown in Fig. 13 (a), which further hinder the motion of 
the subsequent glide dislocations.
After Ti addition, the newly formed interphase TiO can improve the 
coherency between Cu and Nb by transforming the incoherent Cu/Nb 
interface into semi-coherent Cu/TiO/Nb interface. As shown in Fig. 13
(b-c), compared with the incoherent Cu/Nb interfaces, it is energetically 
favorable for the glide dislocations to pass through the semi-coherent 
Cu/TiO/Nb interfaces [ 38 ], which can relieve the stress concentration 
at the Cu/Nb interface, so the Cu-Nb-1.5Ti and Cu-Nb-3Ti deposited 
alloys exhibit higher TE.
The results of mass fraction of Ti atoms in the Cu and Nb phases in 
deposited alloys acquired by EDS are listed in Table 4 . The mass fraction 
of Ti element in Cu and Nb phases in the Cu-Nb-3Ti deposited alloy is 
higher than that in the Cu-Nb-1.5Ti deposited alloy. The Cu and Nb

5. Conclusions
In this study, the Cu
–
Nb deposited alloys with different Ti addition 
were successfully prepared by LDED. We clarified the mechanism that 
the introduction of Ti influences the microstructure and the properties of 
the deposited alloys. The main conclusions are summarized as follows: 
(1) The Ti addition can transform the grain morphology of Nb phase 
from cellular structure to dendrite. The formation of TiO can 
improve the coherency between Cu and Nb. The Cu/TiO and Nb/ 
TiO interface show low mismatch of 0.172 and 0.052, thus both 
the Cu/TiO and Nb/TiO are semi-coherent relations.
(2) The semi-coherent Cu/TiO/Nb interfaces are benefit for the mo -
tion of the dislocations that the Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloys exhibit 
higher TE and the Cu-Nb-1.5Ti achieves the highest TE of 18.6 ±
1.9 %.
(3) The grain boundary strengthening, solute solution strengthening 
and the motion of the dislocations at the interface are combined 
to improve the strength of the deposited alloys and the Cu-Nb- 
1.5Ti deposited alloy achieves the most excellent synthetic 
performance.
