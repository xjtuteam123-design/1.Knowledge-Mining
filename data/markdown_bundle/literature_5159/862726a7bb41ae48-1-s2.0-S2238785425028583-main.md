ABSTRACT
Molybdenum-rhenium (Mo – Re) alloys are promising candidates for fuel cladding in advanced nuclear reactors 
owing to their attractive combination of neutronics and physical properties, including low neutron absorption 
and tailorable density. However, its insufficient high-temperature strength seriously restricts its long-term ser -
vice as a reactor core in high temperature environment. In this study, the thermal compression properties of 
sintered bodies, which can strip away the interference from complex processing, were compared. The effects of 
different particles (ZrO
2
, ZrC, Ti
3
AlC
2
) on the evolution law and deformation mechanism were clearly revealed. 
The results showed that the Mo – Re alloy doped with ZrO
2
, ZrC, and Ti
3
AlC
2 
achieved compressive strengths of 
209 MPa, 217 MPa, and 230 MPa at 1400 
◦
C, which significantly exceeded the corresponding values for pure 
Mo – Re by 17.4 %, 21.9 %, and 29.2 %. The Ti
3
AlC
2 
phase had the best strengthening effect because of its unique 
layered structure, which could effectively anchor grain boundaries and hinder dislocation movement. The 
introduction of three types of second phase particles could all hinder the movement of dislocations in Mo – Re 
alloys and increase their dynamic recrystallization temperature, which provided a new research direction for 
designing low-rhenium molybdenum-based alloys for a high temperature environment.
1. Introduction
Molybdenum (Mo)-based alloys are widely regarded as a leading 
material candidate for use in advanced reactor designs, including fast 
neutron reactors and nuclear fusion reactors, owing to their exceptional 
combination of high melting point, superior high-temperature me -
chanical strength, resistance to liquid metal corrosion, and neutron 
irradiation stability [ 1 , 2 ]. However, the reliable deployment of pure Mo 
in the extreme service environments that are characteristic of reactors is 
severely constrained by its intrinsic shortcomings, notably inherent 
room-temperature brittleness, proneness to oxidation, and recrystalli -
zation embrittlement [ 3 ]. Rhenium (Re) alloying has a beneficial effect 
on Mo, effectively enhancing its strength and plasticity while simulta -
neously suppressing the ductile-brittle transition temperature. However, 
rhenium is expensive and the production cost is high, and its strength -
ening effect ( “ Re effect ” ) is obviously weakened with decreasing Re 
content [ 4 ], which leads to the lack of high-temperature strength of 
Mo – Re alloys, further limiting its application in an ultra-high tempera -
ture environment [ 5 , 6 ].
The addition of rare earth oxide particles effectively strengthens 
Mo – Re alloys, offering a viable solution to the aforementioned chal -
lenges [ 7 – 10 ]. For instance, Zhang et al. [ 11 ] fabricated a Mo-14R -
e-1CeO
2

along with mechanical properties. The obtained tensile strength was 
657 MPa with an elongation of 35.2 %, markedly outperforming pure 
Mo (453 MPa and 7.01 %). Moreover, under compression at 1200 
◦
C, the 
alloy exhibited a strength of 355 MPa, still surpassing that of pure Mo 
(221 MPa). Wei et al. [ 12 ] synthesized a Mo-5Re alloy reinforced with

compression

exhibited a pronounced temperature dependence. As shown, the stress- 
strain curves of the Mo – Re alloys which were strengthened by different 
second phases display similar trends. An inverse relationship was 
observed between the flow stress and the compression temperature 
under a constant strain rate. Elevated temperatures intensify atomic 
thermal motion, which enhances the material ’ s thermal activation en -
ergy. This enhancement significantly facilitates dislocation slip and 
climb. Consequently, it activates softening mechanisms like dynamic 
recovery (DRV) and dynamic recrystallization (DRX), thereby effec -
tively lowering the material ’ s deformation resistance [ 32 , 33 ]. It is worth 
noting that the strengthening effect of doped Ti

mechanisms such as inhibiting dislocation motion, enhancing grain

size [ 20 , 34 ].

4. Conclusion
In this study, three types of second phase particle-doped Mo – Re al -
loys were fabricated via solid-solid mixing and powder metallurgy. The 
influence of the second phase particle type on the high-temperature 
strength and microstructural evolution of the alloys following hot 
deformation was investigated. The main conclusions are as follows: 
1. The stress-strain curves of Mo – Re alloys doped with various second 
phase particles exhibit two dominant types across different defor -
mation temperatures: work hardening and high-temperature dy -
namic softening. The flow stress declined with increasing 
deformation temperature. Among the strengthening particles, 
Ti
3
AlC
2
, ZrC, and ZrO
2 
enhanced the deformation resistance of the 
Mo – Re alloys in descending order of effectiveness. The Ti
3
AlC
2 
demonstrated unique advantages in coordinated deformation owing 
to its layered structure.
2. The effect of deformation temperature on the recrystallization 
microstructure of the four Mo – Re alloys revealed that their struc -
tures were predominantly composed of heavily deformed grains at 
lower temperatures. As the temperature increased, the average grain 
size increased gradually, accompanied by a rise in the number of 
dynamically recrystallized grains. Moreover, the addition of second 
phase particles led to significant microstructural refinement in the 
Mo – Re alloys.
3. When Mo-Re-ZrO
2
