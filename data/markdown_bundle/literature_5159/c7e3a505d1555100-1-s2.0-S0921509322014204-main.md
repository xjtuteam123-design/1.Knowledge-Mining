ABSTRACT 
High-strength metallic wire is a vital bearing structure used in many industrial fields. Impact loads often chal -
lenge the service safety of metal wire in engineering applications. However, few studies have been made on the 
dynamic mechanical behavior of metallic wires, especially for newly developed high-entropy alloy wires. By 
equipping split Hopkinson tension bar (SHTB) with specially designed test fixtures, we have carried out a sys -
tematic study on the dynamic deformation behavior of the heavily-drawn CoCrNi medium-entropy alloy (MEA) 
wire in impact tension at both room and cryogenic temperatures. We show that these millimeter-diameter MEA 
wires with nano-scale grains can achieve an excellent combination of impact tensile strength and ductility at 293 
K and 77 K. More interestingly, we find that the strength and ductility of the MEA wire were enhanced simul -
taneously with decreasing temperature and increasing strain rate. Detailed microstructure characterizations and 
molecular dynamics simulations reveal that the increased strength and ductility at coupled high strain and low 
temperature resulted from the multiplication and thinning of nanoscale twins, which further caused additional 
strengthening and toughening mechanisms such as stack faulting net and secondary twin. This study highlights 
the advantage of CrCoNi MEA wire for cryogenic temperature and impact applications and provides an exper -
imental reference for the design and evaluation of high-strength metal wires under such extreme conditions. 
1. Introduction 
High-strength metallic wires are used as heavy-duty structural 
components in many engineering industries, such as long-span bridges, 
special lifting equipment, and impact protection nets [ 1 – 7 ]. Wires are 
usually wound and braided into wire ropes and mesh, for which high 
strength is requested ( > 1 GPa) to allow for a reduction in the diameter of 
solid wire while maintaining its load-bearing ability. The typical model 
is cold drawn pearlitic steel wire, which exhibits extremely high strength 
benefiting from the strong confinement of the cementite layer to dislo -
cations [ 8 , 9 ]. However, this strengthening effect, along with other 
strengthening mechanisms usually accompanied by a reduction of 
ductility (called the strength-plastic trade-off dilemma), which not only 
limits the shaping of industrial components but also reduces service 
safety. Hence, new metallic wires that possess high strength and suffi -
cient ductility should be developed. 
In recent years, high/medium entropy alloys (HEAs/MEAs) have 
received extensive attention due to their novel design concepts and 
unique properties [ 10 – 20 ]. Numerous studies have shown that HEAs 
exhibit extraordinary mechanical properties, such as the great potential 
to overcome traditional strength-ductility trade-off [ 21 – 23 ], high 
hydrogen embrittlement resistance [ 24 – 26 ], and self-sharpening [ 27 ]. 
Particularly, CoCrNi-based HEAs/MEAs exhibit a perfect combination of 
strength and plasticity [ 28 – 35 ], showing a great potential to prepare 
high-strength and high-plastic alloy wires. For example, Zhang et al. 
found that Al

Although the deformation behaviors of CoCrNi MEA are extensively 
researched in both ambient and cryogenic temperatures under quasi- 
static conditions, the underlying deformation mechanisms have not 
yet been fully understood under impact loadings, especially for heavy 
cold-drawn wires that usually exhibit an ultra-fine even nanoscale grain 
structure like severe plastic deformation materials [ 42 , 43 ]. This is 
important in engineering applications where wire and its products are 
often subjected to dynamic loads. For example, in ultra-deep coal mines, 
the multilayer winding hoist often has friction and impact between wire 
ropes during the winding process [ 44 ]. Furthermore, the local wires of 
the structure usually undergo deformation at a higher strain rate when 
the wire-rope fence under the vehicle or rockfall impacts [ 45 , 46 ]. The 
service safety of alloys under high load rate has always been a critical 
issue in engineering applications. To accurately evaluate the service 
safety of HEAs/MEAs wires in impact engineering applications, it is 
necessary to explore their mechanical behaviors at high strain rates. 
Stable and damage-free clamping is a critical technical challenge for 
wire mechanics testing techniques, especially testing the dynamic me -
chanical properties of wires on Hopkinson tie rods. In wire-related me -
chanical experiments, wire fixation devices often have large sizes and 
complex structures [ 3 , 46 – 49 ], such as the fixation device used by B. 
Hopkinson in his early research on the impact tensile of iron wires [ 47 ]. 
Traditional fixtures are difficult to apply directly to Hopkinson bars. 
This may be one of the reasons for the lack of studies on the dynamic 
tension of the MEA/HEA and even metal wires, although increasing 
researches are devoted to the high-speed deformation behaviors of bulk 
materials of MEA/HEAs [ 17 , 50 , 51 ]. 
In this study, CoCrNi MEA wires were successfully fabricated to a 
diameter of 1 mm using mechanical drawing techniques. By developing 
a wire clamp and experimental method for split Hopkinson tension bars, 
we carried out dynamic tensile tests at room temperature and low 
temperature (77 K). Mechanical responses and microstructure evolu -
tions under different loading conditions are characterized to identify the 
effects of strain rate and temperature on the deformation mechanism of 
CoCrNi MEA wires, as detailed in the Results section. In the discussion 
section, the deformation behaviors of these CoCrNi MEA wires are 
compared with bulk CoCrNi materials. In addition, the strain rate and 
temperature effect are analyzed on atomic scale by MD simulations. 
2. Material and methods 
2.1. Alloy preparation 
The equiatomic CrCoNi MEA ingot was produced by smelting high- 
purity metal mixtures (purity of each component > 99.9 wt%) in the 
vacuum magnetic levitation melting furnace. After being homogenized 
at 1473 K for 24 h, the ingot was hot forged and swaged at 1423 K more 
than eight times to a round bar with a diameter of 15 mm. These round 
bars were further hot-rolled at 1123 K to a diameter of 8 mm, hot-drawn 
at 1073 K to 2 mm, and subsequently cold-drawn to 1 mm at a speed of 
0.5 mm/min. Part of them was further heat-treated at 873 K for 1 h after 
low temperature recovery at 673 K for 2 h.

Fig. 4 shows the bright-field TEM images of cold-drawn MEA wires 
after quasi-static and dynamic tensile at room temperature (293 K). 
Besides the heavily entangled high-density dislocations inherited from 
the as-drawn state, some deformation twin is observed in the MEA wire 
after quasi-static tensile. A close inspection reveals two kinds of twin 
morphology, including mature twins with a width of ~40 nm (marked 
by red arrows) and very thin twin embryos (marked by yellow arrows). 
The high-resolution TEM image of one of these thin twins indicates a 
width of ~4 nm with about 20 atomic layers ( Fig. 4 b). Considering the 
minimal plastic deformation of cold-drawn MEA wires, which are 
unable to provide sufficient plasticity for twin growing, these mature 
twins may be formed during the cold-drawn process instead of quasi- 
static tensile deformation. Nevertheless, the tensile strength of cold- 
drawn wire is much higher than critical twin stress, allowing the 
nucleation of new twins. These newly nucleated twins are limited by the 
negligible plastic strain, manifesting as very thin twin embryos. 
Similar to quasi-static tensile, a high density of clustered dislocation 
structures and some deformation twins are also found in the dynamic 
tensile samples, as shown in Fig. 4 c. Comparing the microstructure after 
dynamic tensile and quasi-static tensile ( Fig. 4 a and c ), we find that the 
tension induced thin twins after dynamic tension is denser than quasi- 
static condition. The magnified image of the twinning region shown in 
Fig. 4 d presents some stacking faults, suggesting a dislocation-twin 
interaction during dynamic loading. High strain rate loading often 
leads to an inhomogeneous strain within the sample due to the short 
response time, wherein some regions would exhibit localized plastic 
events such as dislocation-twin interactions, which widen the twins and 
increase the strength of cold drawn wire simultaneously. 
The high-density entangled dislocations inhibit dislocation motion 
and thus bring these cold-drawn MEA wires to an ultra-high strength at 
both quasi-static and dynamic tensile. However, the high-density 
entangled dislocations in the cold-drawn wires often serve as prefer -
ence sites for microcrack nucleation [ 62 , 63 ], which leads to fracture at 
low strain as manifested by the poor ductility at both quasi-static and 
dynamic tensile. In turn, it suggests that the plasticity of cold-drawn 
wires can be improved by reducing dislocations. 
This inference can be proved by the strain stress behaviors of heat- 
treated wires tensiled at dynamic and static conditions (seen Fig. 3 ). 
Significantly, the ultimate strain of the heat-treated MEA wire rea -
ches~15%, much higher than cold-drawn wire. This suggests that the 
high plasticity of cold-drawn wire can be obtained by lowing dislocation 
accumulation, which increases the mobility and free path of disloca -
tions. Although the reduction of dislocation density by heat treatment 
results in a significant loss of strength, the strength of heat-treated 
CoCrNi wire remains a relatively high level with a yield strength of 
1014 MPa and ultimate strength of 1189 MPa at 293 K. Comparing the 
quasi-static tensile microstructure of heat-treated (see Fig. 5 a) and cold- 
drawn wire (see Fig. 4 a) reveals that the twin lamella in the heat-treated 
wire is significantly wider than that of cold-drawn wire. These wider 
twins may originate from the widening of inherited mature twins during 
heat treatment. Besides, abundant newly tiny twins are present in the 
heat-treated wire. These suggest that heat-treated microstructure with 
less entangled dislocation cells allows sufficient nucleation and devel -
opment of twinning during plastic deformation, which can improve 
ductility and strength simultaneously. 
Similar to the cold-drawn wire, the heat-treated CoCrNi MEA wire 
also exhibits a noticeable strain rate effect under dynamic tension. As 
shown, under dynamic stretching at room temperature, the yield 
strength of heat-treated MEA wire was increased to 1350 MPa, ~30% 
higher than the quasi-static condition. Besides, the elongation of heat- 
treated wire is about 16%, slightly higher than that under quasi-static 
conditions. In short, the heat-treated MEA wire exhibit a significant 
strain rate strengthing effect without loss of plasticity under dynamic 
loading.

3.3. Experimental tensile and deformation behaviors at cryogenic 
temperature 
Besides strain rate, temperature is another important factor affecting 
mechanical properties. Fig. 6 demonstrates the quasi-static and dynamic 
mechanical properties of both cold-drawn and heat-treated CoCrNi 
wires at 77 K. Compared with room temperature, the strength has 
increased significantly at 77 k for both dynamic and quasi-static loading. 
For example, the ultimate strength of cold-drawn MEA wire at 77 K was 
increased by 27.7% – 2475 MPa, and the yield strength was increased by 
19.9% – 2126 MPa, relative to 273 K. 
The thermally activated dislocation behavior is known to be sup -
pressed at low-temperature environments and requires larger stress to 
promote dislocation movement [ 65 ], which results in the higher 
strengths of the wires studied here. Fig. 7 presents photomicrographs of 
cold-drawn wires under quasi-static tensile at 77 K. As shown, the 
nanoscale twin lamellas at low temperatures are thinner and more 
closely arranged than at room temperature. This suggests that twin 
appear to be more favorable at cryogenics temperature, which is associte 
with the lower stacking fault energy at low temperature [ 66 ]. Note that 
this low-temperature strengthening effect is amplified for the 
heat-treated wires, in which the yield and ultimate strength at 77 K are 
increased to 1259 MPa and 1617 MPa, respectively, 24.2% and 36.0% 
higher than the cold-drawn state. Astonishingly, the ultimate strain of 
heat-treated MEA wire is ~0.3 at 77 K, nearly twice over the elongation 
of room temperature condition. This excellent strength and plasticity are 
related to the thinner and denser nano deformation twin bundles (the 
collection of dense nano-scale twin lamellas) in the heat-treated wire. 
The mechanical properties shown in Figs. 3 and 6 confirmed that 
both high strain rate and low temperature can increase the strength of 
CoCrNi wires. As expected, the yield and tensile strength are further 
improved at both high strain rate and cryogenic temperature conditions.

3.4. Atomistic insights into rate and temperature effect 
The above experimental results clearly indicate that increase strain 
rate and decrease temperature can enhance strength significantly 
without ductility loss. Although strain rate and temperature-related 
strengthing effects can be comprehended by the thermally activated 
plastic deformation process, the detailed deformation mechanisms of 
these nanocrystalline MEA wires are still poorly understood. MD simu -
lations have been widely used to investigate the mechanical properties 
and plastic deformation mechanisms of nanocrystalline metals, although 
the inherently high strain rates of MD simulations make it challenging to 
determine the whole deformation mechanisms occurring in experiments 
timescale [ 70 – 72 ]. In this section, the dependence of deformation be -
haviors on strain rate and temperature is discussed based on MD simu -
lation results, which are also compared with experimental results.

4. Discussion 
Thanks to the high-density entangled dislocations after heavy cold 
drawing, the cold-drawn wire has achieved an ultrahigh-strength of 
~1.9 GPa at room temperature, which can further improve by 
decreasing to cryogenic temperature or increasing strain rate. This 
strength is much higher than that of CoCrNi bulk materials, as demon -
strated in Table 1 where the quasi-static mechanical properties of our 
CoCrNi wires and reported bulk CoCrNi materials at both room tem -
perature and 77 K are listed. Table 1 also shows that the tensile prop -
erties of both CoCrNi wires and CoCrNi bulk materials are sensitive to 
the hot working conditions that determine the microstructure. Slone 
et al. [ 74 , 75 ] found a strong relationship between the tensile properties 
and recrystallized fraction in the equiatomic CoCrNi alloy. By producing 
partially recrystallized heterogeneous microstructures, they have 
dramatically increased the yield strength to ~1100 MPa with only 
modest compromises to ductility in the CoCrNi alloy. This value is 
comparable to the strength of our heat-treated wires under the same 
loading conditions. Unlike these partially recrystallized heterogeneous 
microstructures, our heat treated wire retains the nanocrystalline 
structure without obvious recrystallization benefiting by the dislocation 
recovery treatment, as mentioned earlier ( Fig. 2 c). In this case, the high 
strength of the heat-treated CoCrNi wire originates from its entangled 
dislocation cells and nanotwins formed during cold drawing. 
As expected, CoCrNi wires with nanocrystalline have better strength 
and toughness at 77 K compared to room temperature, which are 
consistent well with bulk CoCrNi materials with micron grain size or 
heterogeneous grain structures [ 30 , 32 , 41 , 76 ]. Both experimental and 
atomic simulations demonstrated an enhanced twinning activity at 
cryogenic temperature than at room temperature, which improves the 
work hardening ability and thus strength and plasticity. Besides 
nano-twin, HCP phase has been also observed in CoCrNi MEA during 
quasi-static deformation which are more prominent at cryogenic tem -
perature [ 74 , 75 ]. Miao et al. [ 76 ] indicated that the enhanced 
nanotwin-HCP lamellae may be responsible for the improved strength 
and ductility at cryogenic temperature. In our study, HCP phases can

5. Conclusion 
In the present study, deformation behaviors of the 1 mm CoCrNi 
MEA wires upon quasi-static and dynamic tension at both room and 
cryogenic temperature were investigated by using a novel SHTB based 
impact tension method for high-strength metallic wire. The TEM char -
acterization was employed to investigate dislocation and twin sub -
structures to uncover micro-mechanisms under different tension 
conditions. The main conclusions are summarized as follows: 
(1) Accurate dynamic stress-strain curves of the wires can be ob -
tained by employing the especially-designed two types of wedge 
clamps. The sleeve-type wedge-shaped clamp is suitable for 
holding high-strength and low-plastic wire samples, and the 
square bolt-type wedge-shaped clamp can clamp high-plastic 
wire samples. 
(2) The cold-drawn wire exhibits an ultrahigh-strength of ~2662 
MPa at cryogenic temperature and dynamic tension, resulting 
from the high-density entangled dislocations and deformation 
twins. However, high-density dislocations inhibit dislocation 
motion and lead to poor ductility.
