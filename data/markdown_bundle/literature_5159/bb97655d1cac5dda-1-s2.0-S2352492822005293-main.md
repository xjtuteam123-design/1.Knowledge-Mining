ABSTRACT 
Mg alloys have been attracting particular interests in industrial manufacture. Nevertheless, the poor high- 
temperature mechanical properties of Mg alloys limit their further practical applications. Thus, as-cast Mg- 
2.5Nd-1.0Sm-0.4Zn-0.1Ca-0.5Zr-xAg (x = 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 wt%) alloys were designed and fabricated to 
overcome this shortcoming in this study. The influences of Ag addition on the microstructures and mechanical 
properties of Mg alloys were analyzed in detail. The results indicated that the microstructures of the alloy were 
composed of equiaxed α -Mg grains and β phase. The content of the β phase increased from 5.56% to 8.91% upon 
increasing the content of Ag from 0 wt% to 2.0 wt%, indicating that Ag can promote the formation of the β phase 
significantly. Moreover, the first-principles calculations supported that the formation enthalpy for the β phase 
was reduced with the doping of Ag resulting in increased stability of the β phase. Ag also enhanced the bulk 
modulus and ductility of the β phase. Meanwhile, the Young ’ s modulus and elastic anisotropy of the β phase was 
diminished by Ag addition. The addition of Ag increased the strength and decreased the elongation of the as-cast 
alloys. The as-cast alloy with 2.0 wt% Ag addition achieved comprehensive mechanical properties. Furthermore, 
the as-cast alloy with 2.0 wt% Ag addition showed the least decrease in ultimate tensile strength with increasing 
temperature compared to the other alloys. The ultimate tensile strength of the as-cast alloy with 2.0 wt% Ag 
addition decreased from 196.0 MPa to 188.8 MPa as the temperature changed from 25 
◦
C to 275 
◦
C. The increase 
in strength and heat resistance of the alloys is mainly attributed to the increased β phase content and stability. 
Overall, this work developed a new heat resistant Mg-Nd-Sm-Zn-Ca-Zr-Ag alloy with excellent high-temperature 
mechanical properties. This suggests that it helps to improve the high-temperature mechanical properties and 
reliability of new aero engines and promote applications of lightweight engines. 
1. Introduction 
Magnesium (Mg) is a promising lightweight material [1] . The high 
specific strength of Mg contributes to improving fuel efficiency and 
economic efficiency in industrial manufacture [2 – 4] . However, insuffi -
cient high-temperature mechanical properties of pure Mg significantly 
limit its aerospace applications. Luckily, rare earth (RE) alloying is a 
very effective strategy to enhance the high-temperature mechanical 
properties of Mg alloys [5,6] . To date, research on Mg-RE alloys tends to 
focus on the heavy RE elements, such as Mg-Gd, Mg-Y and Mg-Er series 
alloys [4,7] , which possess excellent high-temperature strength. 
However, the high content of the heavy RE elements is detrimental to 
lightweight, which may limit their applications in aerospace [8 – 10] . 
Conversely, a small amount of the light RE elements can significantly 
strengthen the high-temperature strength of Mg alloys. The rational 
introduction of the light RE elements could expand the range of appli -
cations of Mg alloys. In particular, Mg-Nd series alloys possess good 
high-temperature mechanical properties and have been widely applied 
in aerospace applications [11 – 13] . Recent studies have revealed that Sm 
has a good strengthening effect in Mg alloys and low cost [14,15] . 
Therefore, Sm is a promising light RE element for strengthening Mg 
alloys. Cai et al. [15] designed a novel Mg-4Sm-2Zn-0.5Zr alloy. This

alloys were studied in this work. Meanwhile, the influences of Ag on the 
enthalpies of formation and the elastic properties of the strengthening 
phase in the as-cast alloys were studied by first-principles calculations.

3.4. Mechanical properties 
The Young ’ s modulus of β phase was obtained by the nano- 
indentation tests. The load-depth curves and corresponding Young ’ s 
modulus values of β phase in the 0Ag and 2.0Ag alloys present in Fig. 8 . 
The Young ’ s modulus of the β phase in the 0Ag and 2.0Ag alloys are 
61.2 GPa and 60.0 GPa, respectively. The results of nano-indentation 
test revealed that the effect of Ag on the Young ’ s modulus of β phase 
was consistent with the theoretical calculations, and Ag addition can 
decrease the Young ’ s modulus of β phase. In addition, we also find that 
the theoretical calculation results were higher than the actual test re -
sults. This is because the theoretical calculations usually ignore internal 
defects, impurities and compactness, etc. Meanwhile, the presence of Sm 
and Zn in the β phase could also affect the elastic modulus of the β phase. 
The hardness curve of the Mg-2.5Nd-1.0Sm-0.4Zn-0.1Ca-0.5Zr-xAg 
alloys (x = 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 wt%) was shown in Fig. 9 . The results 
indicated that the hardness increased remarkably with increasing Ag 
content. As the Ag content increased from 0 wt% to 2.0 wt%, the 
hardness of the alloy increased by 13.1% from 53.6 HB to 60.6 HB. The 
increase in the hardness of the alloy attributed to the increase in β phase 
content. The β phase exhibited more excellent hardness than α -Mg, 
while being able to restrict grain boundary sliding [46,47] . Thus, the 
increased β phase content enhanced the effect of the second phase 
strengthening.

4. Conclusions 
1. Ag doping is an effective strategy to promote the formation of the β 
phase, and the composition of the β phase is (MgAg)
12
(NdSmZn). 
First-principles calculations revealed that Ag doping could enhance 
the stability, bulk modulus and ductility of the β phase, as well as 
reduce the Young ’ s modulus and elastic anisotropy of the β phase. 
Furthermore, the β phase is harder than the α -Mg matrix. 
2. As the Ag content increased, the strength of the alloy increased, and 
the elongation decreased. The increased β phase could effectively 
hinder the dislocation motion. Meanwhile, the coarsening β phase 
also generates more stress concentration, which promotes the initi -
ation and propagation of cracks. 
3. The as-cast alloy with 2.0 wt% Ag addition has achieved compre -
hensive mechanical properties. Furthermore, compared to the other 
alloys, the as-cast alloy with 2.0 wt% Ag addition showed the least 
decrease in ultimate tensile strength with increasing the tempera -
ture. The thermostable β phase effectively enhances the micro -
structure stability of the alloy.
