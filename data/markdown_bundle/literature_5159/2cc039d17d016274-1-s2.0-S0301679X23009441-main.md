ABSTRACT 
This study investigates the influence of Zr on the microstructural evolution, mechanical and tribological prop -
erties of CoFeNi
1.5
VZr
x 
(x = 0 – 0.6) eutectic high entropy alloys (EHEAs). The main composition evolutes from 
single FCC to FCC and Laves phase with increasing Zr content. Simultaneously, the microstructure changed from 
hypoeutectic to eutectic and then to hypereutectic with decreasing in grain size. The increase in Zr content 
improves the wear resistance of EHEAs at 25 – 800 
◦
C. The rise in strength and hardness is the main reason for 
improved room-temperature wear resistance of EHEAs. The formation of gradient nano-tribo-layer and the 
transformation of the wear subsurface layer components from the FCC phase to the highly wear-resistant Laves 
phase help to enhance the high-temperature wear resistance of the EHEAs. 
1. Introduction 
Eutectic high entropy alloys (EHEAs) are a high entropy alloy (HEA) 
concept first proposed by Lu et al. [1] . It has the advantages of excellent 
properties of high entropy alloys [2,3] and easy casting of eutectic alloys 
[4] . EHEA normally consists of two phases, some of the EHEAs 
composed of two hard phases (e.g., BCC + B2 or BCC + intermetallic 
compound) usually have high hardness, but their plasticity is poor. In 
contrast, EHEA consisting of one soft phase (FCC phase) and one hard 
phase has better overall mechanical properties and more potential 
applications. 
Significant progress has been made in EHEA research, leading to the 
discovery of many high-performance EHEAs. Lu et al. [1] found that the 
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
EHEA consists of FCC and B2 phases, forming a regular 
lamellar structure that provides excellent mechanical properties [5 – 7] . 
Jiang et al. [8] developed an industrial-scale method for producing 
CoCrFeNiNb
0.45

from 600 
◦
C to 1000 ℃ due to its stable lamellar structure. 
Additionally, EHEAs can serve as effective wear-resistant materials 
in various temperature ranges because of their high strength, hardness, 
oxidation resistance, and thermodynamic stability. Yu et al. [9] inves -
tigated the tribological properties of CoCrFeNiNb
x 
EHEA and compared 
them with Inconel 718 alloy. They found that EHEA has higher hardness 
than Inconel 718 alloy at both room and elevated temperatures, and that 
EHEA has a better wear resistance especially at 800

researchers have already studied the tribological properties of EHEAs, 
there have been relatively few studies on their high temperature wear 
resistance, and the mechanism of high-temperature wear resistance of 
EHEAs remains unclear. 
Alloying element addition is a method to improve the performance of 
HEA. Deng et al. [11] prepared CoCrFeNiMo HEAs by spark plasma 
sintering and studied the effect of Mo addition on the friction and wear 
properties of the HEAs, The results showed that the addition of a tiny

and wear rate. Zhang et al. [12] studied the effect of Al content on the 
tribological properties of Al

and Mo oxides significantly reduced the wear rate. Alloying element 
addition is also a method to produce EHEA, the conventional addition of

In order to design EHEA, there are requirements for the selection of 
elements and preparation methods. Lu et al. proposed a design method 
for EHEA by adding eutectic-forming elements to a single-phase FCC 
solid solution [13] , where a single-phase solid solution requires a mixing

hand, and eutectic-forming elements need to have larger atomic radius

other hand. Jiang et al. utilized this design approach and investigated 
the effect of Mo and Nb on the CoFeNiV system HEA [14,15] , found that 
the addition of Mo and Nb promoted the transformation of the micro -
structure from single phase to eutectic structure, and significantly 
improved the hardness and strength of HEAs.

The EBSD analysis provides a deeper understanding of the impact of 
Zr addition on the microstructure of EHEAs. As shown in Fig. 4 a – c, the 
Zr00 HEA consists of equiaxed crystals of the FCC phase, with grain size 
ranging from 50 to 950 µ m and varying grain orientations between 
adjacent grains. When the Zr content of the HEA is 0.3 ( Fig. 4 d – f), the 
HEA is composed of the FCC phase and the Laves phase, which is in 
agreement with the SEM results. Smaller grains of the Laves phase 
appear in the relatively large grains of the FCC phase and split the FCC 
phase grains into parts with the same orientation. When the molar 
content of Zr was increased to 0.6 ( Fig. 4 g – i), the EHEA exhibited a more 
refined microstructure, comprising alternating layers of FCC and Laves 
phases. The Laves phase is finer grained than Zr03 EHEA and splits the 
FCC phase into lamellae with a spacing of about 3 – 6 µ m. The length of 
the FCC phase was reduced to below 20 µ m in the Zr06 EHEA, which is a 
significant decrease compared to the Zr00 and Zr03 HEAs. This evidence 
strongly suggests that adding Zr contributes to the refinement of the 
HEA grains, leading to a more intricate microstructure. The generated 
Laves phase refines the grains, and this stabilized Laves phase prevents 
subsequent grain growth, resulting in fine grains. These observations are 
consistent with the previous findings obtained from the XRD and SEM 
analyses.

The more detailed analysis of the cross-sectional structure of wear 
marks in the Zr03 and Zr06 EHEAs is illustrated in Fig. 14 and Fig. 15 . 
Simultaneously oxidation and wear produce a tribo-oxide layer on the 
worn surface during the high-temperature friction [35] . The thickness of 
the tribo-oxide layer for both Zr03 and Zr06 EHEAs is approximately 
5.1 µ m, and beneath this layer is a subsurface friction layer. Micro -
structural deformation occurs within the subsurface layer, exhibiting 
deformation layers of roughly 30.4 µ m for Zr03 EHEA and 12.6 µ m for 
Zr06 EHEA. The Inverse Pole Figure ( Fig. 15 a and c) reveals grain 
refinement characterized by a concentration gradient extending from 
the worn surface to the matrix. Finer grains were observed closer to the 
worn surface, while and coarser grains were found nearer to the matrix 
EHEA. In the grain refinement zone, stress accumulates ( Fig. 15 b and d) 
and the FCC phase becomes more dispersed when compared to the 
matrix EHEA (Fig14b and d). Notably, the worn surface becomes 
enriched with Laves phase. 
Under longitudinal loading as well as shear forces during sliding, the 
gradient stress induces the generation of gradient plastic deformation 
below the sliding surface, which is particularly evident in the Zr03 EHEA 
of Fig. 15 a. The thickness of the deformed layer of Zr06 EHEA is smaller 
than that of Zr03 EHEA, which indicates that Zr06 EHEA, which is 
dominated by Laves phase, has better support for the tribo-layer than 
Zr03 EHEA. The grains in this gradient plastic deformation zone are 
finer, and the FCC phase grains exhibit this characteristic more pro -
nouncedly. This is a typical phenomenon of grain recrystallization at 
high temperatures and high stress [36 – 38] . Since the stress is gradually 
decaying from the surface to the substrate, dynamic recrystallization 
produces a gradient nanostructure with a gradual increase in grain size 
from the surface to the substrate. The stress seek to return the HEA from 
its post-deformation state to its pre-deformation state, which makes the 
resistance to sliding increase. The generation of gradient nanostructures 
also reduces the sliding resistance and provides a friction reduction ef -
fect. Gradient nanostructures can better accommodate the gradient 
stresses in the subsequent friction process and inhibit strain localization 
[39,40] , thus enhancing the wear resistance of the EHEAs. The FCC 
phase on the EHEA surface transforms into the Laves phase during 
friction. The phase structure of the EHEAs is also altered, and its worn

4. Conclusion 
A series of CoFeNi
1.5
VZr
x 
EHEAs were successfully produced by 
vacuum arc melting. This study investigates the microstructure, me -
chanical characteristics, and tribological properties of CoFeNi
1.5
VZr
x 
EHEA. 
1. As the Zr content in the HEAs increased, the composition transi -
tioned from a single FCC structure to a combination of FCC and Laves 
phase. Concurrently, the microstructure evolved from a hypoeutectic 
structure to a eutectic structure and subsequently to a hypereutectic 
structure, accompanied by a reduction in grain size. 
2. The addition of Zr enhanced the strength and hardness of the 
EHEAs due to the combined effects of lattice distortion, secondary phase 
strengthening, grain refinement and eutectic structure strengthening. 
However, when the Zr content exceeded 0.3, both the strength and 
plastic strain of the EHEAs decreased. The CoFeNi
1.5
VZr
0.6 
EHEA frac -
tured before yielding, primarily attributed to the brittleness of the Laves 
phase. 
3. The addition of Zr significantly improves the tribological proper -
ties of EHEAs. The wear rate of Zr06 EHEA was 10 times lower than that 
of Zr00 HEA at room temperature, the reasons for the rise in wear 
resistance include the hardness increase and the emergence of friction 
transfer. The main wear mechanism of EHEAs at room temperature is 
abrasive wear. 
4. The improvement in wear resistance of the EHEAs at high tem -
peratures can be attributed to several factors, including the formation of 
an oxide layer that has a lubricating effect at high temperatures, the 
hardening of the surfaces through grain refinement and phase transi -
tions, and the development of gradient nanostructures that prevent the 
localization of stresses. The wear mechanisms of EHEAs at high tem -
peratures are mainly adhesive wear and oxidative wear.
