ABSTRACT
An anisotropic elastic–plastic model was developed to investigate the rafting behavior of Ni-based single crystal
superalloys. This model was developed by introducing the plastic constitutive equation of face-centered cubic
(FCC) single crystal in the framework of Eshelby's equivalent inclusion theory. The Hill's equivalent stress was
calculated when applying a tensile or compressive loading along the [001] direction. The calculated results
successfullypredicttheraftingdirectionforalloyswithapositiveornegativemismatch,consistentwithpervious
experimental and theoretical studies. Moreover, based on this model, the mismatch degree and the elastic-
constant differences of the matrix and precipitate phases and their effects on the speed of rafting are carefully
discussed. Regardless of a positive or negative mismatch alloy, the larger absolute value of mismatch degree can
more effectively accelerate the process of rafting in Ni-based single crystal superalloys. However, when the
alloys enter the plastic deformation stage, the variation in mismatch degree slightly affects the speed of rafting.

1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal superalloys are widely used as advanced
aircraft turbine blade materials because of their excellent creep re-
sistance behavior. The material is strengthened by a high volume
fraction of hard cubical γ′ precipitate phase embedded coherently in a
softer γ matrix, where the γ′ phase volume fraction may reach 70% or
even more. Various studies observed that the cubical γ′ precipitates
tend to coarsen directionally, leading to the formation of γ′ rafts under
the combined influence of stress and temperature (Nabarro, 1996;
Pollock and Argon, 1994; Tang et al., 2016; Tien and Copley, 1971;
Saitoetal.,1996),andthisraftingbehaviorcandirectlyaffectthecreep
and fatigue life of Ni-based superalloys and eventually lead to the
failure of the alloys (Fan et al., 2015; Fedelich et al., 2009; Sakaguchi
and Okazaki, 2007). Therefore, rafting behavior has always been the
focus of studies on precipitation strengthening mechanism and plays a
crucial role in the mechanical properties of Ni-based single crystal su-
peralloys.
Except for experimental studies on the rafting behavior of γ′ pre-
cipitates in Ni-based single crystal superalloys, some theoretical models
have been proposed to describe and predict the rafting behavior of γ′
precipitates. For example, Buffiere and Ignat (1995)proposed a dis-
location model based criterion;Louchet (1995)proposed a model for
negative creep based on the release of interfacial energy;Arrell and
Valles (1996) and Yue et al. (1999) developed a rafting prediction
criterion based on the relief of interfacial misfit strains by dislocation
generation at the γ/γ′ interfaces under multiaxial stress states.
Zhou et al. (2007),Kundin et al. (2012), andTsukada et al. (2017)used
three-dimensional phase field modeling of directional coarsening
(rafting) of γ′ precipitates in single crystal Ni–Al alloys under external
load. Gaubert et al. (2010)and Cottura et al. (2012)further combined
the model with phasefield andviscoplasticityto studythe rafting in Ni-
based superalloys. Furthermore, numerical models in the framework of
thefinite elementmethod(SocrateandParks,1993;Mülleretal.,1993;
Zhou et al., 2003) and some micromechanical models based on Eshel-
by's equivalent inclusion theory (Pineau, 1976; Chang and Allen, 1991;
Miyazaki et al., 1979; Ratel et al., 2006; Ratel et al., 2009) have also
been proposed to describe and predict the rafting behavior at high
temperatures. These models effectively predicted the directional coar-
sening (rafting) behavior from a different perspective, mainly

analysis

γ′ precipitates with a very high volume fraction. Based on our previous

4. Predicted rafting behavior at different mismatches or external
stresses
The driving force of rafting mainly originates from the stress dif-
ference in different matrix channels. In our previous studies (Wu et al.,
2008; Li et al., 2016), it was understood that the directional coarsening
(rafting) direction of γ′ precipitates can be predicted by calculating the
Von Mises equivalent stress in different inclusions (horizontal inclusion
(I) and vertical inclusion (II)). Here, in view of the anisotropic char-
acteristics of γ′ matrix and γ′ precipitates, the Hill's equivalent stress of
different inclusions was calculated to determine the rafting direction of
γ′ precipitates.Fig. 4shows the relationship between Hill's equivalent
stress in different inclusions with external stress (tensile or compres-
sive) along the [001] direction. In the calculation, two types of mis-
match degreeδ = −0.38% andδ = 0.56% were selected. The elastic
constants of Ni-based single crystal superalloys are shown inTable 1,
and the parameters (A, B, C) of Hill's equivalent stress at different
temperatures are shown inTable 2.
When a tensile stress is applied to a positive mismatch alloy
(δ = 0.56%) along the [001] direction, the Hill equivalent stress in the
inclusion (I) is larger than that in inclusion (II), and inclusion (I) first
enters the yield state when the tensile stress is ∼ 850MPa, as shown in
Fig. 3a. Because the inclusion (I) preferentially occur a plastic de-
formation, it mainly induces the mixed dislocation generation and
distribution predominantly in that region. As a result, the rafting di-
rection is parallel to the [001] stress axes (vertical matrix channel). In
contrast, for a negative mismatch alloy (δ= −0.38%) application of a
tensile stress along the [001] direction, the situation is reversed. The
Hill's equivalent stress in the inclusion (II) is larger than that in the
inclusion (I), and the inclusion (II) first enters the yield state when the
tensile stress is ∼ 400MPa. As a result, the inclusion (II) preferentially
generates plastic deformation. The mixed dislocations are pre-
dominantly generated and distributed in that region, the rafting di-
rection is vertical to the [001] stress axes (parallel matrix channel).
Similarly, the rafting direction is also predicted successfully when the
external stress is compressive along the [001] direction. The Hill's
equivalent stress calculation results are shown inFig. 3b. When com-
pared with the tensile stress case, the compressive results are reversed.
By taking a negative mismatch alloy (δ= −0.38%) application of a
tensileorcompressivestressalongthe[001]directionasanexample,to

microstructural evolution, a two-dimensional (2D) schematic diagram

the calculation of this model, the inclusion (II) has a higher von Mises
stress when a tensile stress is applied along the [001] direction, the
mixed dislocations would be predominantly generated and distributed
inthatregion.BecausetheCrandCoatomsintheγphasehaveahigher
mobility (Buffiere and Ignat, 1995), the vertical (V) channel (inclusion
(II))ofγphaseformingelements(CrandCo)alongthedislocationpipes
diffuses into the horizontal (H) channel (inclusion (I)) to destroy the
original local chemical equilibrium. To establish a new equilibrium, the
γ′ phase forming elements (Al, Ni, Ta, and Ti) should move towards the
declined phase interface to supple the demand for diffusive substances
due to phase interface migration. Thus, the width of inclusion (I) in-
creases, whereas the width of inclusion (II) decreases, the rafting be-
havior is perpendicular to the [001] stress axes (horizontal matrix
channel) (See Fig. 4a). In contrast, when the external load is com-
pressive stress, the width of inclusion I decreases and the width of in-
clusion II increases. Thus, the rafting behavior is parallel to the [0 0 1]
stress axis (vertical matrix channel) (seeFig. 4b).
The reversal of lattice mismatch and external stress will both cause
dislocation generation and distribution to alternate between the hor-
izontal channel (inclusion (I)) and vertical channel (inclusion (II)).
Therefore, the change in external stress direction (tensile or compres-
sive) or mismatch degree sign (positive or negative) will convert the
rafting behavior of Ni-based single crystal superalloys into another di-
rection. Based on the above analysis, when a tensile stress is applied to
an alloy with a positive mismatch or a compressive stress is applied to
an alloy with a negative mismatch, the rafting direction is parallel to
the [001] stress axes (vertical matrix channel). When a compressive
stress is applied to an alloy with a positive mismatch or a tensile stress
is applied to an alloy with a negative mismatch, the rafting direction is
vertical to the [001] stress axes (horizontal matrix channel). These re-
sults are consistent with the previous experimental observations
(Nabarro, 1996; Pollock and Argon, 1994; Tang et al., 2016; Tien and

5. Discussion
5.1. Effect of mismatch degree on rafting behavior
In Section 4, the relationship curves between Hill's equivalent stress
and external stress in different inclusions were used to successfully
determine the rafting direction of γ′ precipitates, and the effects of sign
of mismatch (positive and negative) and external stress direction (ten-
sile or compressive) on the rafting direction are analyzed. In this sec-
tion, the influencing factors (mismatch degree and elastic-constant
differences) of Hill's equivalent stress are discussed, and then their ef-
fects on the process of rafting (speed up or slow down rafting) are
analyzed.
For Ni-based single crystal superalloys, the directional coarsening
(rafting) is generally caused by stress nonhomogeneity and plastic de-
formation in the γ matrix. Therefore, in the calculation, we only cal-
culate the inclusion with higher Hill's equivalent stress and pre-
ferentially generate a plastic deformation based on the results shown in
Section 4, namely, inclusion (I) preferentially generates a plastic de-
formation for a positive alloy application of tensile stress or a negative
alloy application of a compressive stress; inclusion (II) preferentially
generates a plastic deformation for a positive alloy application of a
compressive stress or a negative alloy application of a tensile stress.
Fig. 5a shows the Hill's equivalent stress variations of inclusions (I)
in four different types of positive mismatch (δ= 0.15%, 0.30%, 0.45%,
and 0.60%) alloys when the external stress is tensile along the [001]
direction. Clearly, the equivalent stresses increase with the increase of
external tensile stress, and the larger the positive mismatch degree is,
the larger the Hill's equivalent stress at the same external tensile stress.
The positive alloy with the largest mismatch degree (δ= 0.60%) has
the largest equivalent stress and first enters the plastic state in the in-
clusion (I). However, after entering the plastic stage, the difference in
equivalent stresses among these four positive mismatch alloys almost
disappears. InFig. 5b, a similar conclusion can be drawn that the larger

6. Conclusions
An anisotropic elastic–plastic model based on the plastic deforma-
tion of FCC single crystal in the framework of Eshelby's equivalent
inclusion theory was developed to predict the rafting direction in Ni-
based single crystal superalloys. Hill's equivalent stresses of inclusions
(matrix of original material) were calculated when the external stresses
are applied along the [001] direction. The calculated results indicate
that the developed anisotropic elastic–plastic model can effectively
predict the rafting direction of Ni-based single crystal superalloys. This
is further proved from the micromechanical theory that the rafting di-
rection depends on the sign of mismatch degree (positive or negative)
and the type of external stress (tensile or compressive), and the pre-
diction results are consistent with previous theoretical predictions and
experimental observations. The proposed micromechanical model is
more beneficial for achieving the transformation between macroscopic
and microscopic physical quantities and determining the relationships
among different physical quantities.
