Abstract 
Chemical composition and heat treatments of spray-deposited AlCuMgAg-base alloys for use at ambient and moderately elevated 
temperatures have been optimized for high strength and high fracture toughness. This alloy development has led in particular to two 
new age hardenable aluminium alloys (N213 and N232) with excellent property combinations. 
Alloy N213 (AlCu5Mg0.4Ag0.4Ti0.4Zr0.15Mn0.2) clearly outperforms commercial 2XXX series alloys such as 26 18 in terms of 
fracture toughness and yield strength at temperatures up to 180 “C. 
Alloy N232 (AlCu4Mg0.8Ag0.4ZrOSMnO.4) shows the highest product of yield strength and fracture toughness ever achieved for 
aluminium alloys; at 23 “C yield strength and fracture toughness values are R,,,, = 378 MPa, K,, = 100 MPa ml’2 (T4 temper), and 
R PO.? = 452 MPa, K,, = 77 MPa ml’* (T6 temper). Fatigue strength of this experimental alloy (T6 temper) is also obviously superior to 
that of commercial aluminium alloys such as 747.5~T7351 and 2219-T8. 
Alloys N213 and N232 show stress corrosion cracking behaviour comparable with that of commercial 2XXX alloys; plateau crack 
growth rates around 10-s m s-l have been measured. 
As revealed by transmission electron microscopy studies, the excellent property combinations of the new alloys can be related to 
microstructural characteristics, such as type, size, density and distribution of the precipitates. Moreover, these studies explain the 
nature of the microstructure tending towards overaging during extended exposure at temperatures above 150 “C; in particular the 
harmful impact of higher Mg contents on the microstructural stability at elevated temperatures is evidenced.

1. Introduction 
The review of recent literature evidences the need of 
the aerospace industry for a new class of high strength 
damage-tolerant aluminium alloys with moderately ele- 
vated temperature capabilities. As demonstrated by 
Wanhill et al. [I], alloy 2024-T3, since its introduction 
in the 1930s still remains the only damage-tolerant 
aluminium alloy candidate for primary structures in 
civil aircraft. Although showing the best compromise in 
terms of fracture toughness, crack and corrosion resist- 
ance, alloy 2024-T3 has relatively poor tensile proper- 
ties and fracture toughness. Improvements in fracture 
toughness have already been achieved by reducing the 
contents of impurities, which led to alloys 2124 and 
2324. It seems, however, that the 2X24 alloy family 
has found a rival in Alcoa’s new damage-tolerant alloy 
C-l 88. As a matter of fact C-188 has been selected as 
the new fuselage skin alloy for the Boeing 777 aircraft 
[2,3]. Unfortunately, little information concerning com- 
position and processing details of C-188 is available at 
present. Hyatt and Axter indicate some properties to 
meet the Boeing’s future long-range high strength com- 
pact tension candidate aluminium alloys [3]. Since the 
cruise speed at Mach 2.0-2.4 generates skin tempera- 
tures in the range loo-160 “C, with possible limited 
exposure at 205 “C, the new skin material will have to 
show long-term (1.2 X lo5 h) elevated temperature 
capabilities. These goals cannot be met either by the 
2X24 alloys or by alloy C-188 in the thermally 
unstable T3 tempers. However, alloys with improved 
elevated temperature strength such as 2219-TS or 
26 18-T6 show poor fracture toughness values. 
A few years ago Alusuisse-Lonza developed the 
spray-deposited alloy N202 [4]. This AlCuMgAg-base 
alloy, derived from an experimental alloy developed by

2. Alloy development 
The dominant strengthening phase in alloy N202 
has been shown to be the S2 phase [6]. Since 52 has 
already been extensively described elsewhere [ 9- 141, it 
may just be mentioned here that & is an orthorhombic 
AlCu phase with traces of Mg and Ag which forms as 
small, very thin platelets on the { 111) matrix planes in 
aluminium alloys with high Cu:Mg solute ratios and 
small additions of Ag. The most important feature of & 
is its thermal stability at temperatures up to 200 “C, 
which can be related to the high misfit strains normal to 
the & plates [ 141. This characteristic makes Q a pre- 
ferred strengthening phase in aluminium alloys at ele- 
vated temperature applications. 
As a matter of fact, when compared with alloy 26 18, 
alloy N202 proves to show superior tensile properties 
up to 170 “C [6,7]. However, the elevated yield tensile 
strength of alloy N202 is impaired by poor fracture 
toughness; as shown in Fig. 4, scanning electron mi- 
croscopy studies of fracture surfaces of the compact 
specimens used for fracture toughness measurements 
revealed large fractions of coarse and brittle constitu- 
ents acting as crack initiators and embrittling the 
material (Fig. 1). Energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) 
analysis of these particles revealed compositions sug- 
gesting them to be of the (AlCu),(Cu,Mn, Cr,V) type. 
The high concentration of the transition elements Mn, 
Cr and V in these phases is confirmed by the element 
concentration profile in Fig. 2. Some of the particles 
from Fig. 1 have also been shown to be of the 
A&( Zr,Ti) type. 
From these observations it can be concluded that the 
practical cooling rates achieved by spray deposition are 
too low to retain a supersaturated solid solution of the 
high transition metal contents of N202. Instead of 
forming thermally stable dispersoids, large amounts of 
these elements are precipitated as coarse constituents 
and thus degrade fracture toughness. Reducing the 
content of the transition metals allows an improvement

potential. The Cu content in N232.was lowered to 4

0.4 wt.% Mn and 0.5 wt.% Zr. 
It is noted at this point that for alloys N213 and 
N232 only the elements Zr and Ti are added in excess 
of their equilibrium solubility.

Since it is well known that AlCuMg-alloys are prone 
to SCC [20,23], the susceptibility to SCC of alloys 
N213-T6, N232-T4 and N232-T6 has been analysed. 
Indeed, as illustrated in Fig. 7, both alloys show SCC.

Alloy N213-T6 shows a plateau crack growth rate of 
4 x 10 WY m s- ‘; this is obviously ten times lower than 
the crack growth rate of alloy N232. No influence of 
heat treatment on SCC behaviour is observed for alloy 
N232. As a matter of fact, the values for the T4 and T6 
tempers are in the same scatter band between 4 X lo-” 
and 3 x 10pR m s- ’ . This scatter band is also typical 
for 2XXX series aluminium alloys [24]; thus it can be 
stated at this point that the new spray-deposited 
AlCuMgAg-base alloys do not show any exceptional 
susceptibility to SCC. It is also shown in Fig. 7 that the 
crack growth rate at high stress intensities is even lower 
than at intermediate values of K,. This is due to crack 
branching that occurs at high stress intensities: Speidel 
showed that the effective stress intensity K, at the crack 
tip of a branched crack is inversely proportional to the 
square root of the number y1 of the crack branches 
(K, CC l/n1’2) [25], and thus a lower effective crack 
growth rate results. At lower stress intensities the driv- 
ing force for crack propagation becomes insufficient to 
sustain the growth of each branched crack and the 
number of the “active” cracks diminishes until there is 
only one single active crack left; at this stage a marked 
increase in crack growth rate is noticed as a conse- 
quence of the higher effective stress intensity. 
The SCC threshold values K,,,, are very low (below 
5 MPa m’i2); however, it should be pointed out that 
heat treatments for the spray-deposited alloys were 
optimized in view of highest combinations of yield 
strength and fracture toughness and not for improved 
SCC resistance.

7. Discussion 
Two new spray-deposited AlCuMgAg-base alloys 
with excellent mechanical properties have been 
developed. As shown by TEM investigations, these 
properties rely on complex structures consisting of 
various fine precipitates and dispersoids. These 
microstructures are influenced by alloy composition 
and heat treatment, as well as by extended exposures at 
temperatures above 150 “C. 
In alloy N2 13 (AlCu5MgO.4Ag0.4Mn0.2Ti0.4- 
Zr0.15) the existence of the phases Q, 8’, T, a’- 
Al,(Zr,Ti) and Al,,Mn,Cu, was established. Q and 19’ 
are the predominant hardening phases, and T phase is 
formed by a phase reaction during prolonged exposure 
at 180 “C, whereas a' and Al,,Mn,Cu, are relatively 
coarse, heterogeneously distributed dispersoids. The 
latter phases (only observed in small quantities) contri- 
bute to stabilize grain and subgrain boundaries. 
Alloy N213 shows an outstanding combination of 
yield strength and fracture toughness up to 180 “C.

especially up to 150 “C, relies on the thermal stability 
of P in this alloy. However, during extended exposure

Since the latter observation is specific for alloy 
N232 containing 0.5 wt.% Zr and does not apply for 
alloy N2 13 containing 0.15 wt.% Zr plus 0.4 wt.% Ti, it 
can be concluded that if a fine and dense precipitation 
of a’-A1,Zr is aimed at, Zr cannot simply be substi-

produced by spray deposition but must not necessarily 
concern alloys produced by means of rapid solidifica- 
tion. 
The relatively high density of a’ in alloy N232 is 
believed to account for the very fine, pronounced and 
stable grain substructure in this alloy. 
The excellent fatigue properties of alloy N232 may 
be related to this fine substructure; in addition to a fine 
and homogeneously dispersed slip distribution result- 
ing from the numerous various precipitates and disper- 
soids (various phases with different crystallographic 
structures and with different precipitate-matrix coher- 
ency relationships) the fine grain substructure inhibits 
crack initiation proceeding by protrusion of coarse slip 
bands on the specimen’s surface. 
Although showing excellent mechanical properties 
up to 120 “C (GP solvus temperature), alloy N232 
suffers from a significant loss in yield strength when 
exposed at higher temperatures, especially above 
150 “C. It has been shown by TEM investigations that 
this is due to the formation of S phase that proceeds at 
the expense of the strengthening phases Q and 8’; both 
of the latter phases are gradually dissolved, and thus 
the original S2 -strengthened microstructure is heavily 
degraded. 
A somewhat similar result was shown by Vietz and 
Polmear in an earlier work on the aging behaviour of an 
alloy AlCu2.5Mg1.5Ag0.5 [26]: when peak aged at 
200 “C (3.6 h) this alloy was predominantly strength- 
ened by a fine and homogeneous precipitation of T 
phase; however, prolonged aging at 200 “C beyond 
peak hardness caused progressive precipitation of S 
phase (possibly S’), and T phase became less promi- 
nent. 
Nevertheless, the main quality of alloy N232 is its 
unrivalled high fracture toughness (100 MPa m”* in 
the T4 and 77 MPa ml’* in the T6 tempers). The 50% 
advantage compared with alloy N213 cannot be 
explained by microstructural features on their own,

While being obviously beneficial for excellent 
mechanical properties at ambient temperature, high 
Mg contents have been shown to be detrimental for 
good elevated temperature tensile properties. This is 
due to the partition of magnesium in the T phase 
(Al,CuMg,) as well as in the S phase (Al,CuMg) during 
extended exposure at temperatures higher than 150 “C. 
This phase reaction involves the dissolution of the 
main strengthening phases Q and 8’; thus, the micro- 
structure is destabilized and degraded. 
For further alloy development the Mg content in 
AlCuMgAg-base alloys must be considered if high ele- 
vated temperature strength is required.
