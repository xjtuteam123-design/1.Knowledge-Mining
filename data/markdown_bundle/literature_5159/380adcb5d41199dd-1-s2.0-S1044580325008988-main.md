ABSTRACT
Titanium alloys are widely used in aerospace applications due to their excellent heat resistance and high specific 
strength. However, their high-temperature strength enhancement is constrained by grain boundary slip phe -
nomena at elevated temperatures. In this study, a TA0-(9 wt%) NiAlCrMoZr titanium alloy was fabricated via 
laser-directed energy deposition (LDED) to systematically investigate the formation mechanism of interfacial 
element enrichment and its influence on high-temperature tensile properties. Owing to the different diffusion 
rates of elements in β -Ti, Ni and Mo were enriched at the grain boundary of α phase and β phase interfaces by 
tailoring the heat treatment temperature. Notably, the specimen heat-treated at 900 
◦
C demonstrated a 9.0 % 
enhancement in high-temperature tensile strength and an 88.6 % improvement in elongation at 600 
◦
C compared 
to its counterpart treated at 800 
◦
C. These significant improvements in high-temperature mechanical properties 
are primarily attributed to interfacial element enrichment. On one hand, the strengthening effect by grain 
boundary element enrichment on grain boundaries is greater than the weakening effect caused by grain 
boundary coarsening, thereby increasing the high-temperature strength. On the other hand, the strengthening of 
grain boundaries by element enrichment promotes the synergistic deformation of grain boundaries and grain 
interiors, significantly increasing the elongation of the alloy. Furthermore, the interfacial enrichment of Ni and 
Mo is ascribed to the epitaxial growth of the α phase during high-temperature processing and the mismatch in 
atomic diffusion rates during rapid cooling. This work may provide valuable insights into microstructure design 
strategies for optimizing the high-temperature performance of titanium alloys.
1. Introduction
Titanium alloys are widely utilized in critical components of aero -
space components, such as turbine engine parts, aerospace wing leading 
edge and spacecraft cabins, due to their low density, high specific 
strength, and excellent corrosion resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. With the develop -
ment of aircraft with high thrust-to-weight ratio, there is an increasing 
demand for titanium alloys with high tensile strength at high tempera -
tures to mitigate security risks [ 4 ]. The relatively low tensile strength of 
titanium alloy blades in the engine may lead to component deformation 
under high temperatures, bringing about friction and ignition with the 
casing, thereby resulting in catastrophic failures of structural compo -
nents or even air crashes [ 5 ]. Therefore, improving the high- 
temperature tensile strength of titanium alloys is essential to meet 
aerospace industry requirements.
In order to improve the tensile strength of titanium alloys at high 
temperatures, much attention has been drawn into the strengthening 
effect to grain boundary α phase ( α GB) [ 6 , 7 ]. During cooling, the α phase 
preferentially nucleates along β grain boundaries, gradually growing 
into continuous α GB [ 8 ].The previous studies indicated that, the 
strengthening effect of grain boundaries (GBs) decreases with increasing 
temperature [ 9 , 10 ]. To address the weak defect problem of grain 
boundaries, researchers have primarily focused on two approaches: 
increasing grain size and introducing precipitates at grain boundaries 
[ 11 ]. Heat treatment is an important factor influencing the micro -
structure and grain size of titanium alloys [ 12 ]. Although this process 
can effectively decrease the number of grain boundary phases and weak 
defects, it leads to the growth and spheroidization of α phase in the

α GB/ β interfaces and its impact on high-temperature tensile properties 
in titanium alloys. A TA0-based alloy with 9 wt% NiAlCrMoZr was

and preserve α GB morphology. Transmission electron microscopy

synergistic effects of (1) epitaxial growth of α phase and (2) atomic

processing. High-temperature tensile testing demonstrated that grain

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Grain boundary coarsening and element enrichment
Fig. 3 shows the microstructure of the as-printed sample produced by 
LDED. The sample consists of basketweave α laths, irregular primary α 
phase ( α p), continuous α GB, and retained β phase. Fig. 3 c shows the 
microstructure at α GB, it can be observed from the diagram that α GB is 
columnar, and no obvious α branches are grown. In addition, there is a 
clear interface between the phases. The EDS analysis indicates that Ni, 
Cr and Mo are significantly segregated in the β phase, Al is segregated in 
the α phase, while Zr does not show any significant segregation. Both 
characterization analyses demonstrate that the high-entropy powder's 
alloying elements are well dissolved within the titanium alloy matrix, 
confirming the successful in-situ synthesis achieved via LDED.
Fig. 4 (a) shows the XRD patterns of the samples after HT1, HT2, HT3, 
and HT4 treatments. The XRD analysis confirms that all samples main -
tain a typical α + β dual-phase titanium alloy microstructure across 
different heat treatments. Quantitative analysis of the XRD patterns, 
illustrated in Fig. 4 (b), reveals that the α phase content slight decline 
from 91.6 % to 90.4 % for heat treatment temperatures of 800

heat treatment temperature accelerates the α → β phase transition, 
resulting in the retention of a larger fraction of the high-temperature β 
phase upon cooling.

The fracture behavior of the HT1 sample conforms to the typical 
characteristics of high-temperature failure in titanium alloys: in high- 
temperature environments, the disappearance of grain boundary 
strengthening leads to significantly reduced grain boundary strength 
relative to the grain interior, making grain boundaries weak defects 
under high-temperature conditions. Combined with stress concentration 
caused by grain boundary sliding, this ultimately results in intergranular 
fracture. However, the transition in fracture mode of HT2-HT4 samples 
indicates a change in the internal strength distribution of the material.
At elevated temperatures, grain boundary softening becomes the 
primary mechanism driving plastic deformation through enhanced 
sliding. This softening facilitates the release of internal stresses via grain 
boundary sliding, resulting in reduced tensile strength [ 6 ]. Theoreti -
cally, coarser grain boundaries promote sliding and deformation, reduce 
the high-temperature resistance [ 55 ]. The strength variations observed 
in HT1 and HT2 samples solely reflect a decrease in grain strength, 
without indicating any changes in grain boundary strength. The high- 
temperature strength of the HT3 sample is higher than that of the HT1 
sample and its fracture behavior shifts from intergranular to trans -
granular. This transformation indicates that the changes in the strength 
difference between grain boundaries and grain interiors have occurred. 
Such a change implies an improvement in the high-temperature strength 
of α GB in the HT3 sample, attributed to the enrichment of Ni and Mo at 
the α GB/ β interface outweighing the detrimental effects of grain 
boundary coarsening. Concurrently, grain boundary strengthening leads 
to a significant change in the fracture mode of the material, synergistic 
deformation of grain boundaries and grain interiors, which greatly in -
creases the plasticity of the material. Building on the HT3 sample, the 
increased temperature leads to an expansion of the critical grain size 
(Dc) associated with the high-temperature inverse Hall-Petch effect. The 
grain size in HT4 more closely approximates Dc, which is helpful to 
improve the strength, but this approximation reduces plasticity. It can be 
seen that the enrichment of elements at the α GB/ β interface has a sig -
nificant strengthening effect on the high temperature strength of the 
grain boundary, which not only improves the strength of the material, 
but also enhances its ductility, thus achieving the synergistic improve -
ment of mechanical properties.
In-situ atomic resolution experiments show that the movement of 
grain boundary slip phase at high temperature is more dependent on the 
diffusion and migration process of atoms than at room temperature 
[ 56 – 58 ]. In addition, the atomic diffusion rate of β -stable elements is 
higher than that of α -stable elements, so the diffusion efficiency of 
β -stable elements determines the ease of grain boundary sliding motion. 
As a result, restricting atomic diffusion at the α GB/ β interface to limit 
grain boundary sliding may be an effective approach to improving the 
high-temperature strength of titanium alloys. As shown in Fig. 15 , when 
the tensile stress is parallel to α GB and β -stable elements are enriched on 
the side of β phase at the α GB/ β interface, they occupy lattice vacancies, 
thereby reducing the number of vacancies, which can effectively reduce 
the long-range diffusion ability of atoms and the slip rate of grain 
boundary, thus realizing the pinning effect of grain boundary. When the 
tensile stress is perpendicular to α GB, the accumulation of elements at 
the α GB/ β interface raises the diffusion barrier, thus reducing atomic 
mobility. Without element enrichment at the grain boundary, the 
driving force for segregation is balanced by the chemical potential from 
the concentration gradient between the two phases, which means atoms 
only need to overcome the energy barrier related to phase trans -
formation. However, when elements accumulate at the α GB/ β interface, 
the larger concentration difference significantly increases the diffusion 
barrier, effectively impeding atomic movement.
4. Conclusions
In this study, TA0-(9 wt%) NiAlCrMoZr alloy was prepared by LDED. 
The coarsening mechanism of α GB, the enrichment mechanism of ele -
ments at the α GB/ β interface and its effect on high temperature tensile 
properties were studied. The main conclusions include: 
(1) The enrichment of elements at the α GB/ β interface significantly 
enhances the high temperature tensile properties of titanium al -
loys. Compared to the sample heat-treated at 800 
◦
C, the 900 
◦
C 
heat-treated sample exhibited a 9.0 % increase in tensile strength 
and an 88.6 % improvement in elongation.
(2) At 600 
◦

effectively suppresses grain boundary slip by inhibiting the 
interface diffusion of atoms, thereby enhancing the high tem -
perature tensile strength. Simultaneously, the strengthened grain 
boundaries promote coordinated deformation between grain 
boundaries and grain interiors, ultimately improving the plas -
ticity of the titanium alloy.

process dominated by interface reaction, and rapid cooling can
