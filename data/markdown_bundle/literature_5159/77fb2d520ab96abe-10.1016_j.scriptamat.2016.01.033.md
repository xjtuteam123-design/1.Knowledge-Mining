The high temperature deformation behavior of an optimized Nb –Si based ultrahigh temperature alloy was
investigated by isothermal compression. The constitutive equation as well as the deformation microstructure
has been evaluated and discussed. The activation energy of deformation during the steady softening stage is
366–437 kJ/mol. Dynamic recovery of Nbss, brittle fracture of large silicide blocks and reﬁnement of small silicide
particles have been recognized as the softening mechanisms of the Nb–Si based alloy under present deformation
conditions.

Nb–Si based ultrahigh temperature alloys, due to their high strength
at elevated temperatures and proper densities, have been considered as
alternatives applied at higher temperatures (1200 –1350 °C) [1,2].
However, the intrinsic brittleness of constituent niobium silicides
((Nb,X)
5Si3 or (Nb,X)3Si, X represents the alloying elements Ti, Cr and
Hf, ect., similarly hereinafter) and poor oxidation resistance of constitu-
ent niobium solid solutions (Nbss) are major barriers to their applica-
tion as high temperature structural materials. A great amount of
researches have been carried out on effects of alloying elements and
processing technologies on microstructures and properties of Nb –Si
based ultrahigh temperature alloys, aiming at a balance of improved
room-temperature fracture toughness and high temperature oxidation
resistance, with little loss of high temperature strength[3–6].
Multi-alloying has been employed as an essential solution to opti-
mize the integrated property of Nb–Si based ultrahigh temperature al-
loys via changing the type, stability and amount of their constituent
phases. Ti, Cr and Al have been proved to be bene ﬁcial to oxidation re-
sistance. Furthermore, Ti is able to enhance room-temperature fracture
toughness, but to signiﬁcantly degrade high temperature strength be-
yond a critical content [7–10]. Cr and Al alloying also embrittles Nbss
at high concentrations [7,10–13]. Hf is assumed to improve high tem-
perature strength by solid solution strengthening and is reported to
slightly increase oxidation-resistant performance [14–17].T h u s ,a
multi-component system Nb–Ti–Si–Cr–Hf–Al has been selected in the
present study considering an optimum synthetical performance in
both oxidation and mechanical behavior.
Up to now, directional solidiﬁcation and hot pressed sintering have
generally been explored in the processing of Nb–Si based ultrahigh tem-
perature alloys [18–19]. But hot processing (hot extrusion or hot forg-
ing, ect.), which is a convenient and effective method for metal
forming, has rarely been investigated for Nb –Si based alloys. Further-
more, the hot deformation of Nb–Si based alloys, which is essential for
the investigation of hot processing, has only been researched in binary
or ternary systems (such as Nb –10Si and Nb –18.1Si–1.5Zr, at.%)
[20–23]. However, hot deformation behavior of a multi-component
Nb–Si based ultrahigh temperature alloy which possesses better bal-
anced integrated properties (including both oxidation resistance and
room-temperature fracture toughness) and is therefore more ap-
proaching to practical application, has been rarely evaluated and
discussed. Moreover, no constitutive equations have been constructed,
which are critical to the prediction of ﬂow curves and numerical simu-
lation of hot working for Nb–Si based ultrahigh temperature alloys.
In the present work, the high-temperature deformation behaviors of
an o v e lN b–15Si–22
Ti –5Cr–2.5Hf–3Al (at.%, similarly hereinafter) alloy
have been investigated in terms of both constitutive model and micro-
structure evolution, and the softening mechanisms have been discussed
according to deformation microstructures, strain rate sensitivity and the
activation energy of deformation.

8 mm in diameter and 12 mm in height were cut from the ingot for com-
pressive tests. Isothermal compression tests were conducted on a
Gleeble-3500 thermo-mechanical simulator at temperatures ranging
from 1200 to 1300 °C and strain rates of 0.001 –0.05 s

The microstructures in the center of vertical sections of the de-
formed samples are shown in Fig. 4. It can be observed from Fig. 4a-d
that similar microstructures have been evolved after deformation
under different conditions. The morphologies of large silicide blocks al-
most keep the same as those in the as-cast microstructure while multi-
ple cracks have originated and propagated through most of these blocks,
which has resulted in a signi ﬁcant amount of holes in deformation
microstructures. The brittle fracture of large silicide blocks can be
interpreted as that the present deformation temperature is lower than
the brittle- to-ductile temperature of silicides which has been reported
to be over 1300 °C [26,27], so that the stress in (Nb,X)
5Si3 during defor-
mation has been released mainly by crack initiation and propagation,
instead of slipping or twinning. Moreover, the brittle fracture of silicide
blocks tends to be retarded by higher temperatures and lower strain
rates (Fig. 4a–e).
In contrast, visible plastic deformation occurs in the eutectics so that
their morphology becomes less coupled than that prior to deformation.
The original Nbss branches have stretching along the axis vertical to the
compressive direction (CD), which is accompanied by the deﬂection of
small silicide particles (distributed between Nbss branches in original
eutectics) to the same direction ( Fig. 4 a–d). Moreover, some Ti, Si
and Cr-rich bands with dark contrast (originating from the Nbss/
(Nb,X)
5Si3/Cr2(Nb,X) eutectics in the connecting regions of Nbss/
(Nb,X)5Si3 eutectic cells under as-cast condition) have been observed
along the direction vertical to CD. In addition, adjacent Nbss phases
have merged together at the edges in the process of being elongated
so that a few boundaries between original eutectic cells become blurry
and some ﬁne silicide particles are isolated by continuous Nbss phase
in some local regions ( Fig. 4a–d). Also fewer eutectic cells have been
recognized and continuous Nbss phase has formed in more regions at
higher temperature. However, Nbss surrounded by silicides has almost
kept the as-cast morphology and its plasticﬂow has hardly been recog-
nized (as indicated by rectangles inFig. 4), indicating that silicides have
blocked the plastic deformation of Nbss. It is worth noting that
prominent ﬂow localization occurs along a speci ﬁc direction ( Fig. 4e)
during deformation at the low temperature and high strain rate
(1200 °C/0.05 s
−1), giving rise to macro-cracking in this direction
(Fig. 4e’).
It can be concluded that plastic ﬂow is major in Nbss while only
cracks and holes are observed in large silicide blocks with little plastic
deformation. Therefore the microstructure evolution during hot
deformation has mainly been achieved by the plastic ﬂow of Nbss,
which is closely related to the variation of the activation energy of defor-
mation. Because of their high strength and poor compatible deforma-
tion with Nbss, silicides have a tendency to impede the plastic ﬂow of
Nbss (thus blocking microstructure evolution), which is enhanced
with continuous work hardening. This comes to a result that consider-
able concentration of stress at the Nbss/(Nb,X)
5Si3 boundaries occurs
and promotes the crack initiation and propagation in (Nb,X)5Si3 blocks,
which is conﬁrmed by the deformation microstructure observed under
different conditions. It should also be noticed that theQ value keeps in-
creasing up to the strain of 0.07 (even exceeding the self-diffusion acti-
vation energy of Nb), indicating that the work hardening (such as
dislocation multiplication) probably continues in Nbss during this
stage. However, there exists the most rapid drop of ﬂow stresses at
the strains of 0.05 to 0.07 ( Fig. 2). It can be deduced that the obvious
softening in the strain range of 0.05 to 0.07 is achieved by the crack ini-
tiation and propagation in silicide blocks. When the strain rises from
0.07 to 0.1, the Q value exhibits a sharp decrease, indicating that Nbss
restoration has started to control the softening of the Nb –Si based
alloy. This corresponds to the relatively slow drop of ﬂow stress com-
pared with that at the strain of 0.05 –0.07 (Fig. 2). At the strain larger
than 0.1, the Q value decreases slowly and is inclined to reach a steady
value at relatively large strain of 0.35, which is accompanied with the
much slower and relatively constant softening rate of ﬂow curves. It
can be interpreted as that further ﬂow softening of Nbss plays a domi-
nant role in plastic deformation.

Moreover, a large volume fraction of hard particles (such as silicides
with an amount comparable to that of Nbss in the present study) are re-
ported to retard the restoration during ﬂow softening by reducing the
rate of dynamic recovery and moving the dynamic recrystallization do-

erally considered as the dominant restoration of hot deformation when
the activation energy of deformation is similar to that of self-diffusion
[32,33]. At the low deformation temperature (1200 –1300 °C) and
the low strain (0.35) in this study, the strain rate sensitivity has
not exceeded 0.2 and the activation energy of deformation (366 –
437 kJ/mol) is equivalent to the self-diffusion activation energy of Nb
(350–450 kJ/mol [23]). Considering that the plastic deformation has
mainly occurs in Nbss which is still elongated along the axis vertical to
CD (Fig. 4a-d, as discussed above), it can reasonably be deduced from
the values ofm and Q that the dynamic recovery has dominated the soft-
ening process of Nbss and thus the alloy.
Though large silicide blocks exhibit obvious brittle fracture due to
their poor capability of plastic deformation, the ﬁne silicide particles
with larger speci ﬁc interfacial area possess a lower inclination to
crack, indicating that the stress delivered through them is lower. It can
be interpreted as that Nbss prefers to circumvent the silicides (obstacles
for Nbss deformation) through the diffusion of Nb in short distance
along the phase boundary between Nbss and the small silicide particles.
It is interesting that Nbss is observed to wedge into the silicide par-
ticle and even penetrate through them in some local regions, while no
cracks is discernible nearby, as indicated by circles in Fig. 4 d’. Nbss
wedge has been accompanied with the formation of new Nbss/
(Nb,X)
5Si3 boundary (the interfacial energy of these boundaries is
most probably derived from the high distortion energy in silicides),
which is beneﬁcial to relieve the interior stress and suppress the crack
initiation in silicides. It is also suggested that the Nbss wedge and pene-
tration through the silicides have a tendency to split them into smaller
ones, so that they have been isolated and surrounded by Nbss. More-
over, the adjacent Nbss phases have been merging together at edges
during plastic ﬂow, as mentioned above. All of these have promoted
the elimination of Nbss/(Nb,X)
5Si3 eutectic morphology and the forma-
tion of a relatively homogeneous microstructure with (Nb,X) 5Si3 dis-
persed in the continuous Nbss matrix.
In the present work, the ﬂow behavior of the Nb –15Si–22Ti–5Cr–
2.5Hf–3Al alloy has been researched upon hot deformation at tempera-
tures of 1200 –1300 °C and strain rates of 0.001 –0.05 s−1. A modiﬁ ed
constitutive model based on the hyperbolic sine relationship has been
established and has successfully predictedﬂow stresses under different
conditions. The plastic deformation of the alloy has been achieved
mainly by the plastic ﬂow of Nbss, while cracks and holes have concen-
trated in large silicide blocks with little plastic deformation. The activa-
tion energy of deformation decreases from 437 to 366 kJ/mol during the
steady softening stage and the strain rate sensitivity has not exceeded
0.2. The softening mechanisms of the Nb –Si based alloy is recognized
as brittle fracture of large silicide blocks initially after the peak stress,
dynamic recovery of Nbss at the strain larger than 0.07, and reﬁnement
of small silicide particles by Nbss wedging.
