ABSTRACT
Al
–
Cu alloys are widely employed in the automotive and aerospace industries owing to their exceptional me -
chanical properties. However, their performance degradation at elevated temperatures restricts broader appli -
cations. Departing from conventional alloying approaches, this study introduces a novel strategy to enhance the 
high-temperature mechanical properties of Al
–
Cu alloys through grain boundary stabilization and precipitation 
manipulation via the incorporation of trace meticulously designed TiC – TiB ₂ nanoceramic particles. These par -
ticles inhibit element segregation and facilitate the transformation of intergranular second phases into a finer, 
reticular structure, thereby stabilizing the grain boundaries. Additionally, the nanoparticles increase dislocation 
density, serving as preferential nucleation sites for precipitation. The larger lattice distortion around the pre -
cipitates also triggered the formation of stacking faults. Consequently, a more refined and uniform distribution of 
nano-precipitates enhances interactions with dislocations, imparting greater deformation resistance., The ulti -
mate strength (310 MPa) and fracture strain (10.2 %) of 0.15 wt% particle reinforced Al
–
Cu alloy achieves a 
synergistic improvement in both high-temperature strength and toughness, which are improved by 28 % and 64 
% at 473 K compared with the matrix alloy. This work offers valuable insights for developing high-performance, 
heat-resistant Al
–
Cu alloys, paving the way for advanced industrial applications.
1. Introduction
In order to meet the requirements of low-carbon economy and 
environmental protection, aluminum alloys are widely used in the 
transportation and aerospace industries due to their low cost and 
excellent mechanical properties [ 1 , 2 ]. However, some of the key com -
ponents, such as engine crankshafts and pistons are exposed to extreme 
high-temperature working conditions, which put forward more 
stringent requirements on the high-temperature performance of 
aluminum alloys [ 3 , 4 ].
As a representative precipitation-hardening aluminum alloy, Al
–
Cu 
alloy system exhibits excellent high-temperature strength primary from 
the large number of θ ’ -A l
2

[ 5 , 6 ]. However, most studies have confirmed that these precipitates 
exhibit limited thermal stability, typically deteriorating at temperatures 
exceeding 200 
◦
C [ 7 ]. During high temperature service, the substable

These nano θ -Al

reduces the energy barrier for dislocations bypass. This insufficient

promoted due to the enhanced atomic diffusion and dissolution of sec -

improving the high-temperature performance lies in the introduction of 
thermal stable nano-precipitates and the optimization of intergranular 
second phase distribution in Al

In general, synergetic manipulation of trace elements is an effective 
strategy for enhancing the high-temperature resistance of aluminum 
alloys. Alloying elements always stabilize the matrix by forming heat- 
resistant precipitates and intergranular reticular microstructures while 
promoting the aging process [ 13 – 15 ]. For instance, Dorin et al. [ 16 ] 
found that the addition of Sc/Zr elements promote the formation of 
Al

rate of θ ’ -Al

reported that the intergranular heat-resistant Ce/Mn-rich phase in 
Al
–
Cu alloys maintain a relatively reticulation after high-temperature 
re-aging treatment, achieving a synergistic multiscale strengthening 
effect with intragranular nanoprecipitates. However, because of the high 
aging temperatures required for some rare earth elements, they often 
prove incompatible with conventional heat treatment, thus resulting in 
performance degradation. Jiang et al. [ 18 ] demonstrated that segrega -
tion of Mn elements at the semi-coherent Al/ θ ’ -Al
2

icantly improve the coarsening resistance of nano-precipitate phases 
during thermal exposure. Meanwhile, Chen et al. [ 19 ] found that the Ni- 
rich phases at the grain boundaries evolve into an equiaxial after solid 
solution, effectively constraining grain torsion and grain boundary 
sliding at elevated temperatures. Nevertheless, excessive elements al -
ways increase the room-temperature brittleness and reduce casting 
performance by changing melt viscosity. Therefore, despite the obvious 
advantages of alloying, the concentration and selection of alloying ele -
ments still need to be strictly controlled [ 20 , 21 ]. Given these findings, 
obtaining aluminum alloys with excellent high-temperature perfor -
mance though optimized composition design has become a research 
hotspot.
Ceramic nanoparticles such as SiC, TiB
2
, AlN are considered as ideal 
reinforcements for aluminum alloys due to their excellent mechanical 
properties and thermo-chemical stability [ 22 – 25 ]. By optimizing re -
actants ratio, refining synthesis pathways, and manipulating the grain 
size and distribution, ceramic particle reinforced alloys can achieve a 
synergistic enhancement in both strength and toughness [ 26 , 27 ]. For 
example, Xie et al. [ 28 ] introduced dual-phase AlN – Al
2
O
3 
particles into 
Al
–
Cu alloys, achieving ultra-high strength at 350 
◦
C. The result 
revealed that the AlN nanoparticles effectively prevent dislocation 
movement, pin grain boundary slip, and coordinate strain hardening 
within GP zones, resulting in excellent high-temperature strength-plas -
ticity. Besides stabilizing the microstructure, nanoparticles also achieve 
phase transformation modulation and microstructural reconfiguration 
by manipulating solidification behavior. Peng et al. [ 29 ] prepared syn -
ergistically reinforced Al
–
Cu alloy with in-situ synthesized TiC – TiB ₂ 
nanoparticles. They found that the nanoparticles significantly refined 
the microstructure and mitigated elemental segregation, thus enhancing 
high-temperature performance. However, current reinforced techniques 
often rely on high-volume fractions of particles, which typically sacrifice 
plasticity for high strength and may induce anomalous precipitation at 
the particle/matrix heterogeneous interface [ 30 – 33 ]. A large number of 
studies have shown that trace nanoparticles reinforced aluminum alloys 
are considered an effective way to overcome the strength-plasticity 
contradiction [ 34 – 36 ]. These materials achieve a comprehensive 
manipulation of the microstructure and mechanical performance by 
promoting heterogeneous nucleation and optimizing the precipitation 
strategy. Nevertheless, the underlying mechanisms by which trace 
nanoparticles improve high-temperature resistance of Al
–
Cu alloys 
remain incompletely revealed.
Our previous work has proven that the dual-phase TiC – TiB
2 
nano -
particles can effectively manipulate the microstructure of aluminum 
alloys [ 37 – 39 ]. This study incorporates precisely engineered TiC – TiB ₂ 
nanoparticles into Al
–
Cu alloy system. The master alloy containing pre- 
dispersed nanoparticles was gradually melted and slowly released in the 
Al
–
Cu melt assisted by ultrasonic vibration. The reconfiguration of the 
second phase and the enhancement of high-temperature properties were 
systematically investigated. Furthermore, the microstructure manipu -
lation mechanisms of trace TiC – TiB
2 
particle reinforced Al
–
Cu alloys 
during liquid-solid phase transition, heat treatment and high- 
temperature deformation were comprehensively revealed. This work 
provides a guidance for the development of high-performance heat 
resistance Al
–
Cu alloys.

4.2. Microstructure evolution during the high-temperature deformation

instability. Dislocations tend to accumulate at grain boundaries, forming 
dislocation walls that eventually merge and rearrange into lower energy, 
more stable sub-grain boundaries. As the primary carrier for disloca -
tions, the interaction between precipitates and dislocations plays a 
pivotal role in improving high-temperature properties of Al

Fig. 11 (a – b) exhibit TEM micrographs of the unmanipulated and

pin grain boundaries, obstructing dislocation movement causing dislo -

these particles promotes further precipitation, leading to more dispersed 
and finer precipitates that efficiently pin and reinforce grain boundary. 
As illustrated in schematic diagrams, the manipulation of the particles 
significantly increases the density of inherent dislocations in the matrix.

4.3. Strengthening and toughening mechanism
Fig. 12 (a – b) shows the fracture surfaces of the unmanipulated and 
manipulated alloys after high-temperature tensile. The unmanipulated 
alloy exhibits the evident white tear ridge with quasi-cleavage fracture, 
indicative of mixed ductile-brittle fracture behavior characterized by 
small planes, micro-voids and river patterns. The poor plasticity pri -
marily results from stress concentration caused by large sized and uni -
formly dispersed intergranular second phase. After being manipulated, 
there are numerous deep dimples with rough pits on the fracture surface. 
The fracture mechanism was transformed into a ductile fracture without 
apparent brittle fracture characteristic. This is attributed to improved 
microstructural homogeneity and refined dispersion of secondary pha -
ses, thereby effectively mitigating stress concentration.
According to comprehensive microstructure and fracture surface 
analysis, the synergistic improvement mechanism of high-temperature 
plasticity of particle reinforced alloy are explored. The schematic illus -
trations of crack propagation are demonstrated. The introduced parti -
cles significantly improve the distribution pattern of second phases, 
promoting a finer and more homogeneous dispersion. The second phase 
efficiently coordinates the deformation of the matrix, reducing the stress 
concentration and preventing the premature cracking, thus improving 
the high-temperature plasticity. Meanwhile, the high density second 
phase particles and nanoparticles distributed at the grain boundary 
provide more resistance and deflection paths for crack propagation. 
Besides, microstructural refinement also induces increased lattice 
distortion and enhanced torsion between the contiguous grains, leading 
to more uniform dislocations distribution in the matrix after high- 
temperature deformation. Residual stresses are mainly concentrated 
around the grain boundary during the deformation. As mentioned in 
previous analysis, higher density of the grain boundary is induced by 
particles. More intense lattice torsion occurs around these grain 
boundaries, thus improving the uniformity and coordination between 
the grain boundaries during the plasticity deformation, and finally 
inhibiting the crack initiation caused by stress concentration.

reduced at high temperatures. At temperatures exceeding the aging 
temperature, the balanced volume fraction of precipitates decreases 
significantly. This indicates that some smaller and insufficient thermal 
stability precipitates dissolve back into the matrix, reducing the effective 
strengthening phases. Conversely, atoms from small precipitates tend to 
diffuse towards the surfaces of larger precipitates, leading to the 
disappearance of smaller phases and the growth of larger ones — a pro -
cess known as Ostwald ripening — which markedly reduces the density of 
precipitates. Furthermore, grain boundaries soften further at high 
temperature, the intersection of grain boundaries forms stress concen -
tration points more conveniently, and voids are more readily formed 
during high-temperature deformation. These act as crack initiation sites 
and propagate rapidly into the surrounding area, accelerating inter -
granular damage accumulation and ultimately inducing fracture. 
Meanwhile, the mechanism of dislocation motion during high- 
temperature deformation is more complex. Due to the higher thermal 
activation energy at high temperature, dislocations bypass precipitates 
by absorbing vacancies through the synergy of slip and climb. The 
resistance of the precipitates to dislocations movement is weakened, and 
the Orowan stress is further reduced, thus leading to a high-temperature 
strength lower than the theoretical value.
In summary, the incorporation of nanoparticles significantly in -
creases the dislocation density in the matrix, refine the nano-precipitates 
and optimize the distribution of the intergranular second phase. The 
enhanced high-temperature strength of Al
–

from grain boundary pinning by second phases, high density disloca -
tions strengthening, as well as composite strengthening involving 
nanoprecipitates and SFs. Among them, the precipitate strengthening 
dominates the overall strengthening effect, contributing approximately 
52 % of the total strength.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, trace nanoparticle-reinforced Al
–
Cu alloys were pre -
pared by master alloy containing pre-dispersed TiC – TiB
2 
particles as the 
reinforcement carrier, achieving synergetic enhancement of high- 
temperature strength and toughness. The microstructure manipulation 
mechanism of TiC – TiB
2 
particles during liquid-solid phase transition, 
heat treatment and high-temperature deformation were systematically 
explored. The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) The TiC – TiB
2 
nanoparticles significantly refined the as-cast 
microstructure by promoting heterogeneous nucleation. In 
addition, these nanoparticles distributed at the solid-liquid 
interface front effectively inhibited the element segregation and 
refined intergranular second phase. After heat treatment, the 
nanoparticles introduced more dislocations that provided more 
nucleation sites for precipitation and promoted the trans -
formation from irregular blocks to uniform reticular distribution.
(2) During the high-temperature deformation, the finer θ ’ -Al
2
Cu 
precipitates in the manipulated alloy and uniform dispersion of 
intergranular second phases provided more resistance to dislo -
cation motion. The enhanced interaction between dislocation and 
precipitates in the manipulated alloy led to more favorable dis -
tribution of stress. The larger lattice distortion around the pre -
cipitates also triggered the formation of SFs, which effectively 
hindered dislocation climb and cross slip.
(3) The 0.15 wt% TiC – TiB
2 
particles reinforced Al
–
Cu alloy 
exhibited significantly enhanced mechanical properties, with YS 
of 188 MPa, UTS of 310 MPa and FS of 10.2 %, which are 
improved by 18 %, 28 % and 64 %, respectively, compared to the 
unmanipulated alloy. The simultaneous enhancement of both 
strength and plasticity is mainly owing to microstructural 
improvement. More precipitates and hard TiC – TiB
2 
particles 
inhibited the crack propagation, thereby improving plasticity. 
The high-temperature strength enhancement was achieved by 
multiscale strengthening, including intergranular second phase, 
precipitate, dislocations and SFs. Among them, precipitate 
strengthening accounts for approximately 52 % of the total 
strengthening effect.
