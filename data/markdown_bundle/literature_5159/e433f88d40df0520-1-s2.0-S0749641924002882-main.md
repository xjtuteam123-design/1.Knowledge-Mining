ABSTRACT
The low-temperature tensile brittleness of body-centered cubic (BCC) metals and alloys can
seriously compromise their service applications. In this study, we prepared a BCC structured
China low activation martensitic steel (CLAM) steel with lamellar grains by regulating the rolling
and heat-treatment processes, successfully reversing the decreasing trend of ductility in the steel
with decrease in temperature. Compared with current face-centered cubic (FCC) structural steels
and high-entropy alloys, the lamellar grained CLAM steel exhibits an excellent synergy of strength
and ductility at 77K, but with lower raw material costs. The superior low temperature ductility of
the lamellar grained steel can be attributed to an increase in grain strength at low temperatures
which promotes the propagation of layered tearing cracks; this in turn leads to a significant in-
crease in the necking area of the steel, thereby compensating for the decrease in ductility. We
conclude that our lamellar grain structures can be utilized to significantly enhance the low-
temperature tensile ductility of BCC metals and alloys, thereby expanding their service range
to cryogenic temperatures.

However, as a BCC structure, the poor low-temperature ductility of RAFM steels makes them susceptible to irradiation embrittlement,
that can result a significant reduction in room temperature ductility ( Yuan et al., 2022 ). As the ductility of metals and alloys is related
to the motion and density of dislocations ( Lai et al., 2022 ; Z.J. Li et al., 2022b ; Li et al., 2022a ; Khantha et al., 1994 ; Hartmaier et al.,
1997 ), in BCC structures the Peierls-Nabarro force required to initiate screw dislocations increases significantly with decreasing
temperature, making dislocation slip much more difficult at low temperatures ( Zhang et al., 2018 ; Xu et al., 2020 ; Monnet et al., 2009 ).
The research of Lu et al. (2021) showed that under applied stress the bowing of edge dislocations can reach up to 180
◦
; at this point
screw dislocations are required to participate, thus forming Frank-Read dislocation sources. Therefore, the fewer screw dislocations at
low temperatures also significantly reduces the dislocation multiplication rate. The low dislocation density and low rate of slip of screw
dislocation make it difficult for BCC metals to easily plastically deform at low temperatures, thus promoting brittle fracture
characteristics.
Under irradiation conditions, a series of defects such as vacancies and interstitial atoms will be generated in BCC metals and alloys,
which increases the slip resistance of dislocations and causes irradiation embrittlement of the metals ( Saleh et al., 2016 ; Vo et al.,
2023 ). Therefore, irradiation will act to promote a higher ductile-brittle transition temperature (DBTT) in BCC materials; once the
DBTT value exceeds ambient temperatures, there is a risk of brittle fracture. ( Li et al., 2008 ; Chatterjee et al., 2018 ), Thus,
low-temperature ductility is particularly important for RAFM steels. Excellent low temperature ductility can delay the potency of
irradiation embrittlement and extend the service life of the materials.
The current primary methodology for improving the low-temperature ductility of BCC metals is to promote the mobility and
multiplication of dislocations. For example, the mobility of dislocations in Cr, Mo, and W alloys can be increased by the addition of Re;
this serves to change the symmetry of the dislocation core structure, reducing the Peierls-Nabarro force required for the initiation of
screw dislocations and thereby increasing the ductility ( Romaner et al., 2010 ; Li et al., 2022b ). Pre-strain has also been used to increase
the number of mobile dislocations in BCC metals, which can enhance the low temperature deformation ability of BCC metals
( Chatterjee et al., 2018 ; Homayounfard et al., 2022 ). The multiplication of dislocations can also be promoted through the introduction
of a nanoscale second phase; these phases can act as dislocation sources to promote deformation ability ( Deng et al., 2018 ). In addition,
the research of Ding et al .( Ding et al., 2016 ) has indicated that the nano-bubbles of helium can also act as sources to emit dislocations
into the grains, thereby promoting plastic deformation in BCC metals.
Although the low temperature ductility of BCC metals can be effectively improved by regulating dislocation motion and their
multiplication, the fact that ductility still decreases at lower temperatures has not been solved. In face-centered cubic (FCC) structure
metals, transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) ( Chandan et al., 2022 ; Li et al., 2023 ) and twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) effects
( Guo et al., 2021 ; Li et al., 2022c ; Zhao et al., 2021 ) can effectively improve their low temperature ductility, because the sliding
resistance of dislocations increases significantly with the decrease of temperature, while the activation stress of the TRIP and TWIP
effects remains almost unchanged ( Zhong et al., 2023 ; Wu et al., 2018 ). Due to its effect in delaying plastic instability, strong
strain-hardening behavior caused by phase transformation and twinning can significantly improve ductility at low temperatures.
Therefore, the lack of mechanisms similar to the TRIP and TWIP effects in many BCC metals serves to compensate for their reduced
ductility resulting from the high force for dislocation initiation at low temperature; this is the main reason for their poor
low-temperature ductility.
In our previous research, we found that a layered grain structure can increase the plastic deformation (necking) area during tensile
loading by longitudinal layered tearing; this serves to improve the room temperature and high-temperature ductility in a China low
activation martensitic (CLAM) steel ( Zhou et al., 2023 ). In order to further increase the longitudinal tearing effect, we prepared a
CLAM steel with layered grain structure by optimizing the rolling and heat treatment process. The tensile properties of this layered
steel were then studied at room temperature and 77K, where it was found that with decreasing temperature, the layered tearing effect
of the layered grain structure was markedly enhanced, making the necking area of the steel at 77K much larger than that at room
temperature. The larger necking area compensates for the high initiation force of dislocations at low temperatures, successfully
reversing the decreasing trend of tensile ductility in CLAM steel as the temperature is decreased.

4. Discussion
4.1. Yield strength mechanisms
The yield strengthening mechanisms of metallic materials are usually related to the grain-boundary, subgrain boundary, precip-
itation, dislocation, and solid-solution strengthening. However, in this experiment after tempering, the alloy elements in the steel were
basically fully precipitated, and dislocations were highly recovered ( Fig. 1 d,e). As solid-solution and dislocation strengthening thus do
not need to be considered, the strength in the lamellar CLAM steel can be deemed to be primarily generated from grain boundaries,
subgrain boundaries, and precipitation. The contributions to the yield strength at room temperatures from these mechanisms are
estimated below.

4.1.3. Yield strengthening mechanisms at 77K
A schematic diagram of the dislocation structures in the lamellar grain CLAM steel after room temperature and 77 K tensile fracture
is shown in Fig. 7 . One can see that at room temperature, there are large number of mixed dislocations on the (110) slip planes ( Fig. 7 a,
c,e), whereas at 77 K, the mixed dislocations essentially disappear and only straight edge dislocations are distributed on the (110) slip
planes ( Fig. 7 b,d,f). With a reduction in temperature, the main reason for the decrease in the number of mixed dislocations is that the
nucleation and slip of screw dislocations are likely suppressed ( Zhang et al., 2018 ; Xu et al., 2020 ; Monnet et al., 2009 ), due to the fact
that screw dislocations can cross-slip and edge dislocations can only slip, such that the driving force for the edge dislocations is much
greater than that for screw dislocations ( Monnet et al., 2009 ; Lu et al., 2021 ). Therefore, the lack of screw dislocation motion at low
temperatures is likely to be the main reason for the increase in yield strength of the CLAM steel.

4.2.1. Lamellar crack formation mechanism
To discern the initiation and propagation mechanisms of the layered tearing cracks, an in situ tensile test was carried at room
temperature in the scanning electron microscope (SEM) on the RD orientation of the lamellar grain CLAM steel. Fig. 8 shows the
morphology near the preset crack, its locally magnified morphology, an EBSD orientation map, and the main slip system (Schmid

lamellar grain structure CLAM steel exhibits a layered grain distribution in the RD orientation ( Fig. 8 c), with the main slip systems in
the grain being {110} 〈 111 〉 , {112} 〈 111 〉 and {123} 〈 111 〉 respectively ( Fig. 8 d). When the strain is 0.075, the direction of the layered
grains gradually parallels the stretching direction ( Fig. 8 g), and the {123} 〈 111 〉 slip system starts to initiate and generate the slip
system cracks in the grains (as shown by the white arrow in Fig. 8 f with the slip system determined through Fig. 8 h). When the strain is
0.100, the grain orientation changes, and the grains become elongated along the stretching direction, resulting in a significant decrease
in the thickness of the layered grains ( Fig. 8 k). Additionally, the number of {123} 〈 111 〉 slip band cracks is significantly increased, with
observations indicating that grain boundaries can effectively hinder the propagation of the slip-band cracks ( Fig. 8 j). When the strain is
0.125, the grains continue to be elongated along the stretching direction ( Fig. 8 o), with the slip-band cracks contributing to an increase

propagate along these boundaries, ultimately forming layered grain-boundary cracks (as shown by the yellow arrow in Fig. 8 m).
Subsequently, with increasing strain, the layered grain-boundary cracks continue to propagate along the boundaries (as depicted in
Movie S1). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) of the fracture process in the in situ tensile test sample shows that these slip-band
cracks actually are initiated from the subgrain boundaries and M

5. Summary and conclusions
This study provides a design approach involving a lamellar grain structure to achieving attractive ductility at cryogenic temper-
atures and ultrahigh strength in a BCC structured nuclear CLAM steel through control of the rolling and heat treatment processes. In
general, this work successfully provides one solution for the problem of the low-temperature tensile brittleness of BCC steels, which
potentially can further extend the service temperature of such materials to 77K. The key results of this work are summarized below:
