ABSTRACT
To obtain a medium-entropy alloy (MEA) having a body-centered cubic (BCC) structure with relatively high 
specific strength and room-temperature fracture strain, a NbTiCrAl alloy is manufactured by the vacuum arc 
melting method. The NbTiCrAl alloy is developed by adding Ti to improve elongation and reduce weight, Al to 
enhance strength and reduce weight, and Cr to increase oxidation resistance. However, Al and Cr can form 
intermetallic compounds, which may reduce the mechanical properties of the alloy. To optimize strength and 
fracture strain, the contents of Al and Cr are varied. All the (NbTi)

1. Introduction
Metallic materials are highly demanded in various industries, such as 
aerospace and structural industries, owing to their excellent mechanical 
and functional properties. Among metallic materials, high-entropy al -
loys (HEAs) have garnered significant attention recently. These HEAs 
are generally defined as alloys with a configurational entropy (or mixing 
entropy) exceeding 1.5 R , where R is a gas constant. Most of these alloys 
are composed of at least five principal elements with equimolar or 
concentrations ranging from 5 at.% to 35 at.%, but some alloys are 
composed of less than 5 elements [ 1 ]. Owing to such elemental com -
positions, HEAs exhibit four core effects, including the high-entropy, 
severe lattice distortion, sluggish diffusion, and cocktail effects, as 
well as high phase stability [ 2 ]. Based on these key characteristics, many 
HEAs have been reported to exhibit outstanding mechanical properties, 
such as high strength at room and high temperatures, exceptional creep 
resistance, superior stability at elevated temperatures, and excellent 
corrosion resistance [ 3 – 6 ].
Generally, the component atoms of HEAs are randomly dispersed 
throughout the lattice. These HEA systems typically exhibit single-phase 
solid solutions with face-centered cubic (FCC), body-centered cubic 
(BCC), or hexagonal close-packed (HCP) crystal structures, owing to 
their high configurational entropy [ 7 – 11 ]. However, recent studies 
showed that maximizing configurational entropy is not the primary 
factor for stabilizing solid solutions in HEAs [ 12 ]. Medium-entropy al -
loys (MEAs), which exhibit better properties than conventional HEAs, 
have been investigated as an alternative alloy. As a representative 
example, by eliminating Mn and Fe from CrMnFeCoNi HEA, Gludovatz 
et al. examined CrCoNi MEA, which has a single-phase FCC structure 
and exhibits much higher fracture toughness [ 13 ]. However, some of 
BCC structured HEAs [ 14 , 15 ] are composed of high-density elements 
and exhibit low ductility at room temperatures, which limits their uti -
lization in engineering applications. For example, a BCC single 
solid-solution NbMoTaW HEA exhibited an extremely high compressive

In general, in Nb-based solid solution alloys, Ti can improve both the 
tensile ductility and fracture toughness [ 19 , 20 ]. Al has been reported to 
be fundamental in reducing the density of alloys and improving oxida -
tion resistance [ 21 , 22 ]. By increasing the Al content, the strength of 
M/HEAs can be improved; however, when the Al content exceeds a 
certain amount, severe lattice deformation occurs and intermetallic 
compounds are generated, which adversely affects ductility [ 23 ]. In 
addition, Al stabilizes the BCC phase and promotes transformation to the 
short-range ordered (SRO) B2 phase [ 24 , 25 ]. Although Cr is used to 
improve the intrinsic creep strength with protective oxide scale forma -

structured M/HEAs, which generally exhibit low ductility. Senkov et al.

4. Conclusions
To achieve BCC structured MEAs with a relatively low density and 
high fracture strain at room temperature, (NbTi)
100-x
(CrAl)
x 
alloys were 
manufactured via a vacuum arc melting method. Subsequently, the ef -
fects of Cr and Al contents on the microstructure and compressive me -
chanical properties were analyzed. Based on phase analysis, all the 
(NbTi)
100-x
(CrAl)
x 
alloys were MEAs, with a BCC-structured solid-solu -
tion phase as the main phase. When the total Cr and Al contents 
exceeded 30 at.%, an SRO B2 phase was formed. In particular, when it 
exceeded 40 at.%, an intermetallic compound Laves C14 phase was 
formed. Based on the compression tests at room temperature, as the Cr 
and Al contents decreased, the yield strength decreased but the elon -
gation increased. The increase in yield strength with increasing Cr and 
Al contents can be attributed to the strengthening effect due to the larger 
atomic size difference, lattice mismatch, and the formation of a hard 
phase, such as an SRO B2 phase and an intermetallic compound. 
Compared with other BCC structured MEAs, HEAs and Ni-based super -
alloys, the (NbTi)
100-x
(CrAl)
x 
MEA exhibits superior properties in terms 
of specific yield strength and elongation.
