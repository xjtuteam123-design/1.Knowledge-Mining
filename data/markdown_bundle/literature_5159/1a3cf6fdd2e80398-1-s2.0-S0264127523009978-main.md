ABSTRACT 
Brittle defects, such as continuous intermetallic compounds and straight bonding line composed of grain or phase 
boundaries, often form at the interface of dissimilar metals joints, resulting in the premature cracking at the 
interface. Therefore, bonding interfaces with excellent strength and ductility are attractive for application in 
welding structural components. However, increasing strength usually comes at the expense of ductility, which is 
known as strength-ductility trade-off. In this work, we explored a novel interface design strategy by introducing 
multi-scale grains into the bonding interface of the Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy using a heterogeneous refractory high 
entropy alloy. The interface was composed of the multi-sized grains, which successfully eliminated the brittle 
defects. Through this approach, the strength and ductility of the joint synergistically improved to 877 MPa and 
11.54 %, respectively. Both strength and ductility exceeded 98 % of the mechanical performances of the base 
metal. The exceptional strength-ductility combination was benefiting from cooperative strain hardening mech -
anisms, which increased the interfacial hardening capability via in situ refinement of free path of dislocations 
motion. This hetero-interface strategy could expand the design ideas for the bonding interface of intermetallic 
compound-based alloys, and provides a new approach to synergistically increase the mechanical performance.

Pursuing ultra-high strength metals with low density ( < 8 g/mm

in aero-engine. Orthorhombic phase (O phase) Ti

were hence developed and are becoming a promising substitute for 
conventional superalloys, specially for Inconel 718. The density of 
Ti

20 % over Inconel 718. Additionally, in Ti

orthorhombic phase (O phase) with high volume fraction strongly ar -

strength-ductility combination and excellent high-temperature proper -

instead of Inconel 718 in aircraft lightweight parts with precise struc -
ture, such as the compressor blades and casing parts [4] . 
During the manufacturing of the complex parts above, welding of 
Ti
2
AlNb alloys has played an irreplaceable role due to its high structural 
integrity and relatively low cost. Among the welding methods, diffusion 
bonding (DB) bonds the metals through the diffusion of solid state atoms 
at a given temperature and pressure. Compared with normal fusion 
welding, it effectually avoids the embrittlement of matrix grain 
boundaries caused by O phase segregation [3] . In addition, DB has 
advantage in remarkable bonding strength and ultrahigh welding 
shaping precision. Thereby, DB has been suggested as one of the best 
way to manufacture the above Ti2AlNb parts. However, the diffusion- 
inhibiting O phase still brings challenge to DB. There is a straight line 
throughout the welding interface, i.e., bonding line (BL) due to the se -
vere pinning effect of O phase on Ti atoms. To eliminate the BL, many 
measures have been developed, such as increasing the bonding tem -
perature to B2 phase field [5] or introducing the electric field to assist 
heating [6] . Effectual as these measures were, the microstructure of 
forged Ti
2
AlNb alloy was degraded, characterized as the dissolution of O 
phase [7] and resultant irreversible growth of B2 grains [8] . Adding an 
interlayer to drive atoms diffusion is an anther promising solution to 
surmount this contradiction. Wang [9] and Li [10] eliminated the BL in 
Ti
2
AlNb diffusion bonding joints using pure Ti foil interlayer at rela -
tively lower temperature to avoid damaging the base metal. Multilayer 
metal foils, such as Ti-Ni-Nb [11] were also superposed as the interlayer.

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructure of multi-scale heterogeneous interface 
The morphology of bonded interface was characterized by SEM. 
Fig. 2 (c) displays the magnification photograph of bonding interface 
marked in Fig. 2 (b). There are no obvious macroscopic bonding defects, 
such as voids and cracks, at welded interface according to the macro -
scopic morphology of the joint ( Fig. 2 c). The streamline structure pro -
duced by cold rolling still remains in the interlayer even after being 
heated at 970 ℃ for 2 h. The whole interface can be divided into three 
regions according to various microstructures. The RHEA interlayer 
marked as region I is uniform bright white in color, indicating that no 
obvious phase transformation has occurred. The region III is the Ti
2
AlNb 
base metal containing the dark gray B2 matrix and numerous black 
lamellar O phase. The base metal almost retains the pre-welding 
microstructure without obvious thermal damage during the welding. 
In region II, a dark gray layer is formed, which is referred to as diffusion 
reaction layer (DRL). The relative uniform contrast indicates that the 
DRL is composed of single phase without the precipitation of brittle and 
agglomerated IMCs, such as Al-rich participles in the joint using the Ti 
foil [9] or TiZrHfNb as interlayer [25] . 
The detailed grain-scale investigation of bonded interface was car -
ried through EBSD analysis, as shown in Fig. 2 (d,e). The heterogeneous 
composite interface is formed, characterized by the staggered distribu -

average diameter of 3.8 μ m. At the interface between DRL and Ti
2
AlNb 
base metal, DRL grains interdigitate with B2 grains, which has the 
similar orientation on account of the same color in IPF image ( Fig. 2 e). 
At the DRL-RHEA interlayer, the the coarser grains with the average 
diameter of 12.5 μ m interlocked with nano-sized grains (0.6 μ m). 
Interestingly, individual grains have grown across the bonded interface

recrystallized grain, hard O phase and nanosized grain) during the

strengthening effect at interface. As the applied stress exceeded the yield

The planar slip dominates the plastic deformation. As the strain in -
creases, the mobile dislocations are suppressed by the neighboring

are activated, and dislocation networks are formed near the grain 
boundaries. This contributes to a further increase in dislocation in -
teractions and the strain hardening ability of the interface, which is 
known as hetero-deformation induced (HDI) hardening. Further on, the 
nanoscale O phase precipitated at the interface hinders dislocation 
motion. Dislocations strengthen by cutting through the O phase via 
cross-slip mechanism, further enhancing the strain hardening. 
On the other hand, the high strain hardening effect is attributed to 
the gradually refined free path for dislocation movement due to the 
transformation of strengthening mechanism [55] .Various structural el -
ements, such as slip bands, dislocation network and nano-sized O phase, 
divide the grains into multi-scale ‘cell ’ for dislocations movement during 
the deformation. Fig. 14 (e) shows the refined free path for dislocations

recrystallized grains with lower dislocation density provide micrometer- 
scale paths for dislocation motion. At a strain of 2.5 %, the dislocations 
slip bands refine the recrystallized grains, reducing the free path of 
dislocations motion to ~500 nm. As the strain increases to 5.5 %, the 
‘cell ’ is composed of dislocations network induced by the long-range 
back stress field, which has the size of ~100 nm. When the strain rea -
ches to 6.8 %, the nano-sized O phase further refines the size of ‘cell ’ to 
~20 nm. As a result, the strain hardening rate of heterogeneous interface 
is significantly improved due to the multi-scale constraint of dislocation 
motion, leading to a higher ductility of interface and preventing 
cracking at interface. 
5. Conclusion 
This study investigated a novel interface design strategy to surmount 
the trade-off of strength and ductility via introducing heterogeneous 
high entropy microstructure into the bonding process of Ti
2
AlNb 
interface. The findings lead to the following conclusions. 
(1) The heterogeneous bonding interface is composed of the multi- 
scale grains with average diameters ranging from sub -
micrometres to nanometres and dispersed nano-sized O phase. 
The interfacial heterogeneous microstructure successfully elimi -
nates the brittle defects, such as bonding line and IMC segrega -
tion along the interface. 
(2) The heterogeneous microstructure at bonding interface results in 
a higher strain hardening rate of the joint compared with the base 
material undergoing the same thermal process, thereby 
improving synergistically the bonding strength (877 MPa ±
12 MPa) and ductility (11.54 % ± 1.2 %). The exceptional combi -
nation of bonding strength and ductility promotes the fracture of 
the parent material rather than interface, indicating an equal 
strength and toughness bonding with the parent material through 
the heterogeneous microstructure. 
(3) The higher strain hardening rate is attributed to refinement 
hardening, HDI hardening and precipitation hardening. The HDI 
hardening is mainly related to the long-range back stress field 
caused by the piling-up of GNDs at CG/FG interface. The 
sequential activation of these strengthening mechanisms and 
interactive effects induced the formation of slip bands and dis -
locations network. These various structural elements gradually 
reduce the free path for dislocation motion, resulting in contin -
uous strain hardening and thus preventing early cracking at the
