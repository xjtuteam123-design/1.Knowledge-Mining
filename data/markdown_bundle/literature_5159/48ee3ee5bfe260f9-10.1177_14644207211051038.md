Non-equiatomic W 10Mo27Cr21Ti22Al20
high-entropy alloy produced by mechanical
alloying and spark plasma sintering: Phase
evolution and mechanical properties
Hamed Naser-Zoshki , Ali-Reza Kiani-Rashid and Jalil Vahdati-
Khaki
Abstract
In this work, non-equiatomic W 10Mo27Cr21Ti22Al20 refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) was produced using mechanical
alloying followed by spark plasma sintering. The phase formation, microstructure, and compressive mechanical properties
of the alloy were studied. During mechanical alloying, a Body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution phase with a particle
size of less than 1 µm was obtained after 18 h ball milling. The microstructure of the sintered sample exhibits three dis-
tinct phases consisting of two solid solution phases BCC1 and BCC2 as well as ﬁne TiC
xOy precipitates distributed in
them. The volume fractions of each phase were about 79%, 8%, and 13%, respectively. The sintered
W
10Mo27Cr21Ti22Al20 showed yield strengths of 2465, 1506, 405, and 290 MPa at room temperature 600, 1000, and
1200°C, respectively, which are about twice that of the same refractory high-entropy alloy produced by vacuum arc melt-
ing. At 1000 and 1200°C, the strength after yielding gradually increased to 970 and 718 MPa at a compressive strain of
60%. The studied refractory high-entropy alloy can have good potential in high-temperature applications due to its high
speciﬁc strength at elevated temperatures compared to conventional Ni-based superalloys and most as-reported refrac-
tory high-entropy alloys.
Keywords
Refractory high-entropy alloy; Mechanical alloying; Spark plasma sintering; Phase evolution; Compressive strength.

Introduction
In comparison to conventional alloys with one or rarely
two principal elements, high-entropy alloys (HEA) typi-
cally contain more than ﬁve principal elements with
equiatomic or close-to-equiatomic composition.
1–3 This
type of alloy composition maximizes the entropy of
mixing and, by its nature, stabilizes the solid solution
phase in HEAs.
4–7 In general, compared to conventional
alloys, HEAs have shown favorable properties such as
better oxidation and corrosion resistance and mechanical
properties, especially at elevated temperatures. 8,9
For high-temperature applications, refractory HEAs
(RHEAs) have been proposed due to their high melting tem-
perature and high-temperature strength.10–13 For the ﬁrst, in
2010, Senkov et al. 11 reported NbMoTaW and
VNbMoTaW RHEAs exhibited high yield strength of 500
and 735 MPa at 1200°C, which is signi ﬁcantly higher than
that of conventional superalloys. However, RHEAs contain-
ing refractory elements have two major disadvantages: poor
oxidation resistance and very high density.14–17 Subsequent
studies have shown that adding or replacing low-density ele-
ments with higher oxidation resistance with some refractory
elements can greatly reduce these disadvantages. 18–24
Accordingly, Gorr et al.14 designed and prepared the equia-
tomic WMoCrTiAl RHEA via vacuum arc melting (VAM)
method to improve the oxidation resistance and reduce the
density.
On the other hand, it has been shown that maximiza-
tion of mixing entropy through equiatomic ratios of ele-
ments is not a precise criterion for the formation of a
solid solution phase and that it can be formed in a wider
range of compositions outside the equiatomic.
25,26 Many
properties of HEAs, including density reduction, can be
improved by extending the range of alloy composition
from equiatomic to non-equiatomic and subsequently
optimizing the elemental composition.
26–28 Therefore,
the non-equiatomic approach can open up a wider range
of compositions for designing HEAs to achieve the
desired properties.
Melting and casting methods, especially VAM method,
are mostly used to fabricate bulk HEAs.
1,29–31 However,

the formation of coarse grains, evaporation of low melting
point elements, and dendritic segregation are the major
drawbacks of these methods. 32–35 To overcome these
drawbacks, HEAs can be prepared via powder metallurgy
(PM) methods such as mechanical alloying (MA)
followed by spark plasma sintering (SPS). In PM
methods, due to the synthesis of materials in the solid
state, there are no problems of evaporation of elements
with low melting point and also segregation of elements
due to their melting temperature difference. However, to
obtain a solid soluble alloy, these methods generally
involve two steps: ﬁrst, alloying in powder state with a
high-energy milling, and second, sintering.
32,35–37 The
SPS process can consolidate the MA powders in a short
sintering time, which suppresses the grain growth and
therefore a ﬁner microstructure can be achieved. 12,38
In this research, non-equiatomic W 10Mo27Cr21Ti22
Al20 refractory HEA was designed and successfully
produced by MA and SPS. Phase evolution and micro-
structure of the alloy in the as-milled and sintered states
and the compressive properties of the sintered alloy
were subsequently investigated.
Experimental procedure
High purity powders of W, Mo, Cr, Ti, and Al were mixed
in the composition of W
10Mo27Cr21Ti22Al20. The choice
of this alloy composition is based on alloy design calcula-
tions that are described in detail in our previous study.39 In
summary, it should be noted that typically HEAs have an
equiatomic composition to form a solid solution phase
when the mixing entropy is maximized. However, by con-
sidering non-equiatomic compositions, a wide range of
compositions opens up for the design of the HEAs.
Therefore, in addition to obtaining the solid solution,
some desired properties such as the density can be opti-
mized. In the previous study, W
10Mo27Cr21Ti22Al20
alloy was obtained as the optimal composition in
W-Mo-Cr-Ti-Al system using calculations of empirical
parameters (including mixing entropy ΔS
mix, mixing
enthalpy ΔHmix, entropy to enthalpy ratio Ω, atomic
size difference δ, and density ρ). In addition to forming
a solid solution phase ( Ω > 1.1 and δ < 6.6%), this alloy
had a lower density than the equiatomic alloy (7.6 vs.
8.7 g/cm
3).
Mechanical alloying was performed by milling the
mixed powders in a high-energy planetary ball mill for
0, 3, 6, 12, and 18 h at 270 rpm and a ball to powder
weight ratio of 10:1. Hardened steel balls and vials were
used as the milling media. The process of charging and
discharging powders was done in argon atmosphere
inside the glove box. About 2wt% ethanol was used as
a process control agent (PCA) to avoid cold welding of
the powder to the walls and balls during milling.
Subsequently, the 18 h milled powder was sintered
using spark plasma sintering equipment at 1650°C for
15 min under 60 MPa axial pressure in a vacuum of
10
−3torr. Heating rate was 100 °C/min and pressure was
increased gradually with temperature up to 60 MPa.
Phase analysis of the MAed powders and the SPSed
sample was examined by EXPLORER GNR X-ray dif-
fractometer (XRD) using CuK α radiation at the range
of 2 θ = 20–90°. The microstructure of MAed powders
was observed by a scanning electron microscope
(SEM, LEO 1450VP). A ﬁeld emission scanning electron
microscope (FE-SEM, TESCAN-MIRA3) with energy-
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS, SAMx) was used to char-
acterize the microstructure and elemental composition of
the sintered sample. Carbon content was determined
using an LECO CS-230 analyzer (LECO Corporation,
USA). Archimedes ’ principle based on ASTM-B962
was used to measure the experimental density of sintered
alloy. Microhardness tests were conducted on the polished
surfaces of sample using a BUEHLER Vickers micro-
hardness tester under a 500 g load for 15 s according to
ASTM E384-16. For compression testing, the cylindrical
samples of φ 5 × 10 mm were prepared from the sintered
alloy. The samples were tested at various temperatures
from room temperature to 1200°C using a Zwick/Roell
(Z250) machine at a strain rate of 10
−3s−1. All tests
were performed up to 60% compressive strain or up to
fracture, whichever occurred ﬁrst.

Microstructure and phase evolution after SPS
The bulk sample was prepared by sintering the 18 h milled
powder at 1650°C by SPS technique. The XRD patterns
of W 10Mo27Cr21Ti22Al20 RHEA after 18 h ball milling
and after SPS are shown in Figure 4. Compared with
the XRD patterns of the milled powders, the broad diffrac-
tion peaks are narrowed due to the increase in crystallite
size and the release of internal energy during SPS
process. It was found that the sintered sample consisted
of a BCC solid solution as the main phase, and a minor
FCC phase corresponding to titanium carbide (TiC,
ICDD PDF No.00-032-1383) and titanium monoxide
(TiO, ICDD PDF No.01-086-2352). It has been shown
that titanium carbide (TiC) and titanium monoxide
(TiO) can form a solid solution with general formula
TiC
xOy. This phase results from the reaction of carbon
and oxygen with titanium at high temperatures during

Mechanical properties
The sintered RHEA exhibits a high Vickers microhard-
ness of 1210 HV with a high relative density above
99%. Figure 7 shows the stress-strain curves after com-
pression test of bulk sintered W
10Mo27Cr21Ti22Al20
alloys from room temperature to 1200°C. Compressive
properties including fracture strain, ϵ
f, yield strength,
σ0.2, and maximum strength, σmax are taken from
Figure 7 and listed in Table 2. This alloy exhibits the
yield strength of 2465 MPa and fracture strain of 11.2%
at room temperature. During testing at 600°C, yield
strength decreases to 1506 MPa and the sample fractures
at 13% strain without noticeable plastic strain. The yield
strength at 1000°C and 1200°C decreases further to 450
MPa and 290 MPa, respectively. At these temperatures,
the strength after yielding increases to maximum
strength of 970 MPa and 718 MPa during strain hardening
at compressive strain of 60%. Compared with the same
RHEA fabricated by VAM method,
39 the SPS sample
has higher strengths at RT-1200 °C (about twice).
Therefore, in addition to solid solution strengthening,
the high strengths of the SPSed alloy can be attributed
to the ﬁne grain strengthening obtained by mechanical
alloying, as well as precipitation strengthening caused
by the formation of TiC
xOy precipitates.
It is worth noting that ﬁner microstructure and the
presence of TiC xOy particles can increase the strength
of the SPSed alloy by blocking the movement of
dislocations as well as restricting the migration of grain
boundaries.
56,57,61
Figure 8(a) shows the variation of yield strength with
temperature for bulk sintered W 10Mo27Cr21Ti22Al20 com-
pared with commercial Ni-based superalloys Haynes 230,
Hastaloy X, Waspaloy and Inconel 718, 62–68 and several
other RHEAs. 11,69–71 As can be seen, the yield strength
of RHEAs is much higher than that of conventional super-
alloys. The main reason for this can be the solid solution
strengthening caused by strong lattice distortion of HEAs,
which is the most important strengthening factor in these
alloys.
69,71,72 In addition, at high temperatures, this may
also be related to the low amounts of high melting point
elements in superalloys, which means weaker bonding
under elevated temperatures. 73 In our RHEA, in addition
to solid solution strengthening, precipitation strengthen-
ing due to hard TiC xOy particles is also effective in the
high strength of the sample. In addition, due to the
lower density of this alloy than conventional superalloys,
this difference is more noticeable in terms of speci ﬁc yield
strength (Figure 8(b)). At all studied temperatures, both
the strength and the speci ﬁc strength of the alloy are
higher than those of superalloys. At elevated temperatures
(1000°C and 1200°C), the strength of the alloy is some-
what lower than that of NbMoTaW and HfNbTaTiZrW.
However, at these temperatures, its strength is approxi-
mately equal to that of NbMoTaW and greater than that
of HfNbTaTiZrW.
Conclusion
Non-equiatomic Rhea W 10Mo27Cr21Ti22Al20 was suc-
cessfully produced by MA and SPS. The phase evolution,
microstructure, density, hardness, and compressive prop-
erties of the alloy were evaluated after MA and SPS pro-
cesses. The conclusions of the present study are
summarized below:
1. A BCC solid solution phase with crystallite size 23.1
nm and particle size of less than 1 µm was formed in
the 18 h as-milled RHEA powder during MA process.
2. After SPS at 1650°C, the alloy consisted of two BCC
solid solution phases, BCC1 and BCC2, and homoge-
neously distributed TiC
xOy precipitates.
3. The bulk sintered W 10Mo27Cr21Ti22Al20 showed high
Vickers hardness of 1210HV and high yield strengths
of 2465 MPa at RT, 1506 MPa at 600°C, 405 MPa at
1000°C, and 290 MPa at1200°C.
4. These strength values are about twice those of the same
RHEA fabricated by VAM method, which can be
attributed to the ﬁner microstructure as well as the pre-
sence of ﬁne precipitates in addition to solid solution
strengthening.
5. During deformation at 1000°C and 1200°C, the
strength after yielding gradually increased to 970
MPa and 718 MPa, respectively, at ϵ = 60% without
fracture.
6. The speci ﬁc strength of sintered W
10Mo27Cr21
Ti22Al20 is comparable to high-strength RHEAs and
signiﬁcantly higher than conventional Ni-based
superalloys.
