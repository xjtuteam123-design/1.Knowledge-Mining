Abstract
First-principles spin polarized calculations have been implemented by using
the Discrete Molecular (DMol3) package to investigate the strengthening
effects of alloying addition including Nb, Mo, V and Co in a cubic solid
solution of Fe–Ga alloys. Mayer bond order values were used to evaluate
the atomic bond strengths in the alloys and were then used to assess the
alloying strengthening characteristics. Results from the calculations suggest
that the transition metal Nb achieves the best strengthening effect in Fe–Ga
alloys. The solid solution strengthening follows a decreasing order trend
from larger to smaller from Nb, Mo, V to Co. The effect of Ga on individual
bond strength variations between Fe and alloying elements and also on
alloying strengthening was examined.
1. Introduction
Magnetostrictive materials are widely used for sensor and
actuator device applications. It has been recognized that
substituting a small amount of non-magnetic Ga element for
Fe in a bcc solid solution results in an unprecedented ten-
fold increase in magnetostriction, 3/2λ
100, of Fe and twice the
increase compared with FeAl alloys [ 1, 2]. Fe–Ga alloys are
found to become elastically very soft at a certain composition
range, and the softening of the shear elastic constant 1/2
(C
11-C12) is believed to explain the novel magnetostrictive
behaviour of the alloys [3]. In addition to the magnetostrictive
properties, an enhancement of mechanical properties, such as
solid solution strengthening in Fe–Ga alloys with speciﬁcally
chosen alloying elements, is another important issue in
the development of Fe–Ga alloys. Thus, a thorough
investigation of the underlying mechanism responsible for
alloy strengthening is essential to the research on Fe–Ga alloys.
Currently, obtaining an alloy with both good toughness and
novel magnetostrictive properties still remains a challenge. To
our knowledge, there are no direct experimental or theoretical
studies on the strengthening mechanism associated with
alloying additions of Nb, Mo, V and Co in Fe–Ga alloys. In
this study, we make an effort to investigate the mechanism of

2. Calculational methods
In general, molecular cluster approach is suitable for studying
electronic properties that are primarily a function of local
environment [ 4], such as the local strengthening effects
induced by alloying additions. In this study, the DMol3
package, a molecular cluster approach [ 5] based on density
functional theory [6], was implemented. The double numerical
basis sets are employed in spin polarized calculations.
The degree of convergence of the self-consistent iterations,
measured by (rms) changes in the charge density, was set to
be 10
−5, which allowed the energy to converge to 10 −5 Ry.
All calculations in the present research were performed with
the generalized-gradient approximation (GGA) proposed by
Perdew and Wang [ 7], and the cluster model was fully
relaxed before examining the bond characteristics. In order
to quantitatively investigate the alloy strengthening effect, the
atomic bond strength between atoms was calculated. In this
study, the atomic bond strength is measured by the Mayer

3. Results and discussions
To study solid solution strengthening, the effect of the
individual bond strength and the alloying addition on bond
strength variation were examined. It was found that the
surface effect on BO values due to the selection of the small
cluster size could be minimized by using a large supercell
[9–11]. In addition, the bond orders were evaluated within
the FNN distance of atoms. The calculations indicate that
the contributions from the second nearest atoms to BO values
are around 2–3% of those from the ﬁrst nearest atoms.
Thus, beyond the FNN range, the BO
AB value is small and
can be neglected without altering the conclusions. This
approach has recently been successfully applied in the alloying
strengthening study of Ni-based alloys [9–11]. Figure 1 shows
a supercell cluster model of Fe with 35 atoms in size used
for BO calculations. There are eight basic bcc unit cells that
build up this supercell. Accordingly, there are eight centre
atoms (small grey circles) used as potential Ga locations for
the substitutions. In the centre of the supercell, a relative large
grey circle is used to represent either Fe( 1) or an alloying
element. This model is large enough so that the surface
effect on the bond strength between Fe( 1) and its FNN can
be neglected. For a cluster with bcc symmetry as shown in
ﬁgure 1, there are eight FNN atoms surrounding the Fe(1) site.

4. Conclusions
By carrying out ﬁrst-principles spin polarized calculations
using the DMol3 package, we observed that, among Nb, Mo,
V and Co, transition metal Nb achieves the best solid solution
strengthening effect in cubic Fe. Although the addition of Ga
modiﬁes the individual bond strength between Fe and alloying
elements to some extent, the trend of strengthening due to
alloying addition remains the same.
