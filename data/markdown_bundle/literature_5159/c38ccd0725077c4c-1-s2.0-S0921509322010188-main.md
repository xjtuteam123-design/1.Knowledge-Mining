ABSTRACT 
The tensile behavior at ( 100)-300 
◦
C of the extruded and peak-aged Mg-5Gd-3Y-xZn-0.5Zr (x = 1 and 2 wt%, 
designated as VWZ531K and VWZ532K) alloys was investigated in the aspects of microstructural evolution, 
deformation modes, mechanical properties, etc. The higher Zn content endows the VWZ532K alloy with more 
intragranular lamellar LPSO phases and larger interdendritic ones at the initial homogenized status, which in -
duces the lower DRX ratio, finer DRXed grain size, and negligible aging hardening response subsequently. During 
extrusion, precipitation of intragranular LPSO phases occurred in the VWZ531K alloy and partial DRXed grains 
formed a special texture component with the c-axis aligning parallel to the extrusion direction. However, the 
VWZ532K alloy experienced the dissolution of intragranular LPSO and dynamic precipitation of β phases. During 
tensile tests, deformation modes (including twinning, basal slip and non-basal slip) were modified by the testing 
temperature, grain orientation, LPSO morphology, etc. Compared with the β
′
precipitates, LPSO phases are more 
thermostable to maintain the high-temperature strength, which induce the higher tensile yield strength of the 
peak-aged VWZ532K alloy (182 MPa) at 300 
◦
C. The larger volume fraction of tension twins and lamellar LPSO 
phases result in the abnormal high elongation (17.9%) at 100 
◦
C for the VWZ531K alloy. 
1. Introduction 
Mg-RE (rare earth)-TM (transition metal) alloys with long-period 
stacking ordered (LPSO) phases have drawn sufficient attention in past 
decades since they possess excellent mechanical properties to be applied 
in the lightweight and energy-efficient engineering systems [ 1 – 3 ]. The 
stacking structures [ 4 , 5 ], formation conditions [ 3 , 6 , 7 ], growth and 
transformation mechanisms [ 8 , 9 ], deformation modes [ 10 – 14 ], and 
properties [ 15 – 18 ] of the LPSO phases have been widely investigated. 
Recently, attention has been paid to the effect of LPSO phase 
morphology on the deformation behavior and mechanical properties 
[ 19 – 24 ]. Varies morphologies of LPSO phases, including interdendritic 
or intragranular ones, were controlled by the modification of alloy 
composition [ 20 ], heat treatment [ 19 , 21 , 23 , 24 ], pre-deformation [ 22 ], 
etc. And the LPSO morphology was reported to influence the dynamic 
recrystallization (DRX) behavior, workability, and mechanical proper -
ties of the Mg-RE-TM alloys [ 19 – 24 ]. 
As a typical representative of the Mg-RE-TM series alloys, the Mg-Gd- 
Y-Zn-Zr alloys with a high RE content exhibit outstanding strength and 
hardness after thermomechanical processes [ 25 , 26 ]. To overcome the 
economic drawback, recently more effort has been devoted to devel -
oping wrought Mg alloys containing less RE atoms with relatively high 
mechanical properties [ 27 , 28 ]. Among the plastic deformation methods, 
extrusion constructs compressive stress condition [ 29 ], which can 
overcome the shortcoming of low workability of Mg alloys, especially 
those containing LPSO phases [ 27 , 30 , 31 ]. In the previous study, the 
effects of extrusion ratio and heat treatment on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of the extruded Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Zr alloys (with RE

as VWZ531K and VWZ532K) alloys with different morphologies of LPSO

during extrusion and aging was investigated. Additionally, the defor -

2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Material preparation 
The as-cast ingots with a size of Φ 70 mm × 200 mm were prepared 
by melting pure Mg and Zn, Mg-25Gd, Mg – 25Y and Mg – 25Zr (wt.%) 
mater alloys in an electrical resistance furnace and then pouring into a 
water-cooling iron mold, under the protection atmosphere of CO
2 
and 
Ar. Their actual composition (as listed in Table 1 ) was examined by an 
inductively coupled plasma (ICP, SPECTRO BLUE SOP) analyzer. 
After homogenization at 510 
◦
C for 24 h, cylindrical billets with a 
diameter of Φ 50 mm were machined from the center of the ingots. After 
being held at 400 
◦
C for 1 h, the cylindrical billets were extruded in an 
extrusion container of Φ 52 mm into bars of Φ 10 mm, at the ram speed 
of 1 mm/s and with the extrusion ratio of ~27, followed by air cooling. 
Aging treatment after extrusion was conducted at 200 
◦
C in an air 
furnace. 
2.2. Mechanical property tests 
Aging-hardening response was examined by the Vickers hardness 
tests with a load of 4.9 N and a dwelling time of 15 s. Tensile tests were 
conducted on an SUST-CMT-5105GL machine at a crosshead speed of 1 
mm/min at ( 100)-300 
◦
C. Before testing, the corresponding specimens 
were held at the target temperature for 15 min. Flat specimens, instead 
of rod ones, were used for tensile tests for easy capture and observation 
of the slip trace as reported in Refs. [ 33 , 34 ]. Specimens with a gauge 
length of 20 mm and cross section of 6 mm × 2 mm were machined from 
the center of the extrusion bars. The 0.2% proof strength was applied as 
the tensile yield strength (TYS). Each status was tested along the 
extrusion direction (ED) using at least three specimens to ensure the 
reliability of the results. 
2.3. Microstructure characterization 
Microstructure was characterized by the scanning electron micro -
scope (SEM), electron back-scatter diffraction (EBSD), and transmission 
electron microscope (TEM). A Phenom G2 scanning electron microscope 
equipped with an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS) was used to 
acquire SEM images. Specimens were ground, polished, and then etched 
in a solution of 6 g picric acid, 40 mL acetic acid, 40 mL water, and 100 
mL ethanol. EBSD was conducted on a Helios Nanolab 600i SEM at the 
scanning step ranging from 1/10 to 1/5 of the average grain size of each 
sample. Inverse pole figure (IPF) coloring maps, pole figures, in-grain 
misorientation axis (IGMA) distribution, etc. were derived from the 
EBSD data. In the IPF coloring maps, grain orientation spread (GOS) was 
chosen as the criterion to distinguish the DRXed and deformed grains, 
and those with the GOS less than 2
◦
were considered as the DRXed ones. 
Misorientation angles in the range of 2
◦
– 5
◦
were included in the IGMA 
analyses. All specimens for EBSD examinations were firstly ground and 
then electro-polished at 35 
◦
C using a solution of 15 ml perchloric acid 
and 285 ml ethyl alcohol. It should be noted that, before tensile testing, 
the gauge regions of the aforementioned tensile specimens were ground 
and electro-polished by the same method in order to clearly observe the 
slip trace after fracture. Bright-field TEM images and selected area 
electron diffraction (SAED) patterns were obtained on an FEI Tecnai G30 
transmission electron microscope. Specimens for TEM examinations 
were twin-jet electron-polished at 35 
◦
C and 0.01 A, using the same 
solution as electro-polishing. 
3. Results 
3.1. Microstructure of the homogenized and extruded alloys 
Fig. 1 shows the microstructure of the homogenized VWZ531K and 
VWZ532K alloys. Combined with the EDS results ( Table 2 ) of the tested 
points marked by the red arrows ( Fig. 1 c and d), it can be inferred that 
both alloys contain α -Mg matrix, Zr-rich clusters (with cores and rod- 
like/spherical slices, as indicated by the ellipses), interdendritic block 
LPSO phases, and intragranular lamellar LPSO (whose composition was 
failed to be examined by the EDS due to their limited width). 
The quantity of intragranular lamellar LPSO phases is significantly 
improved in the VWZ532K alloy, since the increased Zn content in the 
α -Mg matrix (compare B2 with B1 in Table 2 ) should provide the 
favorable condition for their formation [ 6 , 35 ]. Besides the lamellar 
LPSO, another microstructural difference lies in the volume fraction of 
interdendritic LPSO. Both the length and width of the interdendritic 
LPSO phases in the VWZ532K alloy increase. Apparently, different 
initial LPSO morphologies can be achieved by the modification of Zn 
content. 
After extrusion, DRX occurs and LPSO phases tend to align along ED 
as shown in Fig. 2 a and b. It is interesting to note that numerous 
intragranular lamellar LPSO phases precipitate in the majority of both 
DRXed and deformed grains in the VWZ531K extruded alloy. However, 
in the VWZ532K extruded alloy, although lamellar LPSO phases inherit 
from the homogenized state and remain in the deformed region, 
spherical particles rather than lamellar LPSO phases precipitate in the 
DRX region (as indicated by the ellipse in Fig. 2 d). Detailed phase in -
formation will be presented in the subsequent TEM images. 
Fig. 3 shows the IPF coloring maps, local misorientation maps, and 
pole figures of the extruded VWZ531K and VWZ532K alloys. The 
VWZ531K alloy owns a larger average grain size than that of the 
VWZ532K (7.9 versus 5.3 μ m). Based on the GOS criterion, the DRX ratio 
(i.e. the proportion of DRXed grain in α -Mg matrix) of the VWZ531K and 
VWZ532K alloy is detected as 87.3% and 71.2%, respectively. For 
brevity, the corresponding EBSD data of the DRXed and deformed grains 
are shown in Figs. S1 and S2 in the supplementary material. Deformed 
regions own a larger local residual plastic strain concentration (i.e. 
dislocation density [ 36 , 37 ]) compared with the DRX regions, as indi -
cated by Fig. 3 c and d. The VWZ532K alloy exhibits a typical basal fiber 
texture with (0002) plane and < 01 10 > axis parallel to ED, with the 
intensity of 6.1. However, the VWZ531K alloy additionally exhibits 
another special texture component with the c-axis almost aligning par -
allel to ED, as indicated by the arrow in Fig. 3 e. A similar texture 
component was also found in the extruded Mg-4.7Gd-3.4Y-1.2Zn-0.5Zr 
alloy sheet [ 38 ]. DRX regions rather than deformed regions account for

In order to investigate the tensile behavior, IPF coloring maps of the 
regions near the fracture of the peak-aged VWZ531K and VWZ532K 
tensile specimens are exhibited in Fig. 8 and Fig. 9 , respectively. Tension 
twins (identified by the EBSD software) can be detected in the VWZ531K 
specimens (as indicated by the white rectangular frames), and their 
volume fraction increases with the decrease of testing temperature. 
However, twinning deformation is negligible in the VWZ532K speci -
mens. Moreover, it is noteworthy that many elongated grains with the 
color gradient can be detected in the VWZ531K specimen at the high 
testing temperature of 300 
◦
C ( Fig. 8 d), which keeps in accordance with 
its large elongation (41.7%). 
Statistical IGMA analysis of all the detected grains in each tensile 
specimen is exhibited at the lower left corner of Figs. 8 and 9 , which acts 
as an effective method to determine the dominant slip mode during 
tensile deformation [ 44 ]. Therein, IGMA distribution with concentra -
tion around < 0001 > direction was often considered to result from the 
prismatic < a > slip, while the < utv0 > -type concentration was related to 
the basal < a > slip (the major slip mode for α -Mg) or < c + a > slip [ 44 , 
45 ]. The majority of specimens possess the combination of < 0001 > and 
< utv0 > distribution, except for Fig. 8 d (with a single < 0001 > con -
centration) and Fig. 9 a (with a single < utv0 > concentration). It should 
be noted that statistical IGMA analysis cannot represent the slip mode of 
all individual grains. Actually, the IGMA analyses performed on each 
individual randomly selected grain (see Figs. S3 and S4 in supplemen -
tary material) from Fig. 8 a (grain A-L) and Fig. 9 a (grain 1-12) reveal the 
diverse distribution of the concentration, under the premise of keeping 
with the aforementioned overall trend. It can be inferred that basal and 
non-basal slip were both activated during tensile tests at all tempera -
tures (( 100)-300 
◦
C) for both the VWZ531K and VWZ532K alloys, 
which can be further proved by the slip trace analysis shown in Figs. 10 
and 11 . 
Since the intragranular LPSO phases are parallel to the basal planes 
of α -Mg grains [ 9 , 34 , 46 , 47 ], the slip modes can be divided into basal slip 
and non-basal one according to the orientation between the intra -
granular LPSO phases and slip traces. Although the lack of lamellar LPSO 
phases in the VWZ532K specimens disturb the differentiation of slip 
modes, sufficient identified slip traces and special microstructures (e.g. 
raised grain boundary, crack in interdendritic LPSO) are labeled and 
shown in Figs. 10 and 11 .

4.1. Microstructural evolution during extrusion and aging 
The main microstructural evolution during extrusion involved the 
DRX, dissolution or precipitation of secondary phases. Both the lower

DRX ratio and finer grain size were achieved in the VWZ532K alloy. 
Although the increased interdendritic LPSO phases may promote DRX 
nucleation via the particle-simulation nucleation (PSN) mechanism 
[ 19 ], the initial intragranular LPSO phases possess a strong inhibition 
effect on DRX growth [ 30 ]. Moreover, the spherical β precipitates could 
also pin the movement of DRXed grain boundaries [ 43 ]. Finally, the 
lower DRX ratio and finer grain size can be obtained. The formation of 
the special texture component in the VWZ531K alloy might result from 
the preferential grain growth of certain crystallographic orientations 
during recrystallization. The supposition of the higher boundary 
migration ability of the < 0001 > ‖ ED component was applied to explain 
the transition from < 0001 > ⊥ ED to < 0001 > ‖ ED during annealing for a 
Mg-4.3Gd-3.2Y-1.2Zn-0.5Zr (i.e. VWZ431K) alloy [ 32 ]. In the present 
work, more growth of DRXed grains indeed occurs in the VWZ531K 
alloy (see Fig. 3 ), which satisfies the basic condition for the above 
supposition. However, the exact mechanism remains unclear and needs 
future work. 
Numerous lamellar LPSO phases appear in the α -Mg matrix of the 
VWZ531K alloy. Similar phenomenon was found in other wrought Mg- 
Gd-Y-Zn-Zr alloys which also own few intragranular LPSO phases at 
the homogenized status [ 19 , 48 ]. This can be explained by the increased 
defects (e.g. dislocations, stacking faults) due to the plastic deformation 
which could promote the diffusion of solute atoms and construct the 
stacking condition for the formation of LPSO phases [ 19 ]. Moreover, the 
air-cooling process after extrusion could also decrease the solid solubi -
lity to promote precipitation. 
On the contrary, dissolution rather than dynamic precipitation of the 
intragranular LPSO occurred during extrusion in the DRX regions of the 
VWZ532K alloy like that reported in Refs. [ 49 – 51 ]. The dissolution of 
initial intragranular LPSO phases should be related to the operation of 
DRX at the early stage of extrusion, since DRXed grain boundaries could 
provide pressure (or energy) on these phases for this endothermic re -
action [ 32 , 51 ]. Subsequently, dynamic precipitation of the previously 
dissolved atoms dominated at the later stage of deformation or 
air-cooling process, forming dynamic precipitates of β phases. It can be 
inferred that there might exist a competition or balance between the 
dissolution and precipitation of secondary phases during this thermo -
mechanical process. The dynamic precipitation of intragranular LPSO or 
β phases, and the dissolution of LPSO phases should be determined by 
the initial microstructure and deformation parameters. Although the 
VWZ531K and VWZ532K alloys contain different kinds of dynamic 
precipitates (lamellar LPSO versus β phases), their distinction in aging 
hardening response should mainly be attributed to the Zn content which 
consumes the resource of β
′
aging precipitates (i.e. RE atoms) to form 
interdendritic LPSO phases.

4.3. Comparison on strength and ductility 
The TYS of the Mg alloys containing LPSO phases was mainly 
affected by the following aspects: grain boundary, solid atoms, LPSO 
phases, aging or dynamic precipitates, texture, dislocation density, etc 
[ 21 , 27 , 32 ]. At the as-extruded state, the VWZ531K alloy contains a 
much higher solid solution strengthening effect due to the less volume 
fraction of block interdendritic LPSO phases which consume solute 
atoms and result in the less RE and Zn content in the α -Mg matrix (see 
Table 2 ). It compensates for the disadvantage of the VWZ531K alloy in 
the aspects of larger grain size (7.9 versus 5.3 μ m), higher Schmid factor 
for basal slip (0.30 versus 0.24), less deformed grains with the higher 
dislocation density, fewer interdendritic LPSO phases as the short-fiber 
reinforcements, etc. As a consequence, both the extruded alloys exhibit 
the approaching mechanical properties at room temperature.
