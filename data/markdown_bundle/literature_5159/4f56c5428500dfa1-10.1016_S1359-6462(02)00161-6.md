1. Introduction
The intermetallic compound, NiAl, has been
studiedforanumberofyearsforhightemperature
structural applications. The main purpose of the
research is to improve ductility at room tempera-
ture and creep strength, which are major draw-
backs of monolithic cast NiAl. In an eﬀort to
addressbothproblems,weadoptedthemechanical
alloying (MA) process followed by hot extrusion
or hot pressing to produce oxide dispersion
strengthened (ODS) NiAl with ﬁne submicron
grain size [1–3]. The utilization of this processing
technique results in improvement in both room
temperature compressive ductility and strength at
elevated temperatures as well as decrease in mini-
mum creep rate by two orders of magnitude from
that in cast NiAl [1–4]. Improved creep resistance
in MA NiAl was found to result mainly from the
ﬁnely distributed Al
2O3 dispersoids which act as
signiﬁcant obstacles to dislocation motion [2–5].
Dispersoids are also known to prevent grain
boundary sliding [6]. Large grains are preferred in
high temperature structural materials such as MA
superalloystodecreasediﬀusionalcreeprates[6,7].
Largegrainscontainingﬁnelydispersedoxidescan
be eﬀectively developed in MA NiAl and superal-
loys by secondary recrystallization (SRx) [7,8]. In
this case, the elevated temperature strength can be
further improved by producing elongated grains

4. Summary
NiAl based ODS alloy has been produced by
MA and consolidated by hot extrusion. Thermo-
mechanical treatments have been performed to
induce SRx. It was shown that SRx led to pro-
nounced grain coarsening and high grain aspect
ratio combined with ﬁne dispersoids, resulting in
improved high temperature properties in compar-
ison to both as-extruded and NGG conditions.
The improved high temperature properties of
SRxedMANiAlmaybepostulatedtoresultfrom
themaintenanceofaﬁnedispersoiddistributionas
well as reduced grain boundary sliding and diﬀu-
sional creep. SRx of dispersion strengthened in-
termetallics oﬀers a potential processing route for
optimization of the high temperature mechanical
properties after net shape processing with a ﬁne
grain size.
