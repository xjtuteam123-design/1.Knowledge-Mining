ABSTRACT
The CoCrFeNi multi-principal element alloy (MPEA) is well known for its remarkable ductility; however, its low 
room-temperature strength limits its broader practical applications. In this study, a partially recrystallized het -

1. Introduction
Multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) have garnered significant 
attention due to their excellent properties, which arise from their unique 
alloy design concept. These properties include remarkable room- 
temperature plasticity [ 1 – 3 ], enhanced corrosion resistance [ 4 – 6 ], su -
perior fracture toughness [ 7 ], and outstanding low-temperature per -
formance [ 8 ]. Such characteristics make MPEAs highly promising for 
applications in various industries, including automotive, shipbuilding, 
and aerospace manufacturing. Among these, CoCrFeNi alloy stands out 
as a typical example of MPEAs, exhibiting excellent tensile plasticity and 
achieving up to 62 % plastic strain at room temperature [ 9 ]. Despite 
these impressive ductility characteristics, CoCrFeNi alloy suffers from 
relatively low strength, which significantly limits their practical use in 
structural applications [ 10 – 12 ].
The development of heterogeneous structure is widely regarded as an 
effective strategy for enhancing the mechanical properties of alloys 
[ 13 – 15 ]. By constructing such structure, heterogeneous-induced 
strengthening and hardening are promoted, enabling a balanced 
trade-off between strength and ductility [ 16 ]. For example, when a 
partially recrystallized heterogeneous structure is introduced into 
CoCrFeNi alloy, it creates a good balance between strength and ductility 
[ 17 ]. In this structure, some grains undergo complete recrystallization, 
forming new fine grains, while others retain their original morphology, 
preserving their unique characteristics such as amorphous phases or 
localized grain boundaries. This mixture of different grain types creates 
non-uniform deformation during plastic deformation, which in turn 
triggers hetero-deformation induced strengthening and hardening effect 
that improves the strength and ductility of the alloy [ 18 ]. However, 
despite these improvements, the yield strength of the heterogeneous 
CoCrFeNi alloy remains below 1 GPa, which highlights the limitation of 
this approach. This observation suggests that a single strengthening

4. Discussion
4.1. Formation of heterogeneous structures
In our experiments, the regulation of heterogeneous structure in -
volves two key factors: alloying elements and recrystallization fraction. 
Fig. 8 provides a schematic diagram to clarify the formation process of 
the heterogeneous structure.
First, the addition of Mo element improves the plastic deformation 
ability of the alloy. This is because Mo has a large atomic radius, which 
can produce a large solid solution strengthening. As shown in Fig. 8 (a), 
the mixing enthalpies ( Δ H
mix
) between Mo and Co, Cr, Ni, and Fe are 5 
kJ/mol, 0 kJ/mol, 7 kJ/mol, and 2 kJ/mol, respectively. The close 
mixing enthalpies make it easier for the elements in the alloy to dissolve 
into each other, which reduces phase segregation and intermetallic 
compounds formation and helps stabilize the alloy phase structure. In 
addition, alloying Mo can also reduce the stacking fault energy of the 
alloy, stimulate more stacking faults to coordinate the plastic deforma -
tion. Therefore, single-phase Co

Secondly, the optimal recrystallization ratio is obtained by regu -
lating the heat-treatment temperature, as shown in Fig. 8 (c – d). When 
annealed at 700 
◦

a recrystallization transformation, resulting in a 67.3 % recrystallized

excellent property is primarily attributed to the formation of partially 
recrystallized heterogeneous structure with a non-recrystallized region 
volume fraction of 24.3 %. This structure can induce the heterogeneous 
deformation induced strengthening and hardening, which contributes to 
the yield strength of 691 MPa. During the deformation process, the 
plastic deformation mechanism is initially controlled by dislocation slip 
along the primary slip planes in both the recrystallized and non- 
recrystallized regions. As deformation proceeds, this mechanism 
evolves to involve a coordinated interaction of the stacking fault 
network, composed of L-C locks and deformation twins. This transition 
enables a dynamic Hall-Petch effect and reduces the dislocation mean 
free path, which achieves the high strain-hardening capacity of the A750 
alloy. These findings revealed in partially recrystallized heterostructures 
provide valuable insights for designing strong and ductile advanced 
structural materials for modern industrial applications.
