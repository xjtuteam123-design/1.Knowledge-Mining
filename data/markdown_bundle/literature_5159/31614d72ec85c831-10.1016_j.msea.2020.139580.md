1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs), firstly reported in 2004 [ 1 ], have 
attracted increasing interest from researchers in recent years due to their 
extraordinary properties, including exceptional low-temperature 
ductility, good corrosion resistance and high-temperature properties, 
etc. HEAs were originally defined as “ those composed of five or more 
principal elements in equiatomic ratios ” [ 1 ] and the term HEA usually 
refers to equiatomic multi-principal elements alloy with a single-phase 
solid solution (SS). To exploit more HEAs with superior properties, the 
current definition of HEAs is no longer restricted to equiatomic and 
single-phase SS. Some non-equiatomic alloys with multi-phases or even 
minor additions are also considered as HEAs [ 2 – 6 ]. Other terms, such as 
complex concentrated alloys or compositionally complicated alloys 
(CCAs) and multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) [ 2 , 4 , 7 , 8 ], are also 
frequently used for this type of alloys. 
Compared with the equiatomic single phase HEAs, non-equiatomic 
HEAs containing multi-phases generally exhibit superior mechanical 
properties resulted from the integrated strengthening mechanisms 
[ 9 – 11 ]. Hence, most recent work has focused on the multi-phase 
HEAs/CCAs. Non-equiatomic HEAs/CCAs usually consist of supersatu -
rated metastable solid solution matrix and intermetallic compounds at 
specific temperatures [ 12 ]. Thus, they possess higher potential for aging 
hardening [ 8 , 13 , 14 ] as compared with both the traditional alloys and 
equiatomic HEAs composed of stable single phase. However, most of the 
currently reported HEAs with age-hardening ability are near-equiatomic 
and generally contain high content of expensive metals (e.g. Co or Mo), 
such as the Al

3. Experimental procedure 
3.1. Alloy preparation 
The Fe-enriched HEA was prepared in an arc-melting furnace using a 
water-cooled copper mould in an argon atmosphere. Commercial metal 
powders (Fe, Ni, Cr and Mo) with 99.9 wt% purity were mixed according 
to the designed compositions and mechanically agitated for half an hour 
to ensure homogeneity. Then the blended powders were compacted into 
small cylinders of 14 mm in diameter and 20 mm long. To ensure the 
chemical homogeneity, these cylinders was remelted four times with 
processing parameters of 250 A current and 28.5 V voltage. Commercial 
316L stainless steel (SS) and Inconel 625 superalloy powders were used 
to make the counterpart ingots in the arc-melting furnace for compari -
