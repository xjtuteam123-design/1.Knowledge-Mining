ABSTRACT
This study investigates the influence of minor alloying elements on the phase stability, microstructure, and 
mechanical properties of Zr-Nb alloys through integrated first-principles calculations and experimental ap -
proaches. Special quasirandom structure (SQS) modeling revealed that Mo doping significantly can stabilize the β 
phase by inducing strong electron interactions with the Zr/Nb matrix, whereas V, Ta, and Hf additions failed to 
achieve comparable stabilization. The Zr-Nb-X alloys were prepared by powder metallurgy. It demonstrated that 
alloys with Mo addition exhibited enhanced β phase proportion, suppressed continuous grain-boundary α -phase 
formation, and refined grain structures, leading to simultaneous improvements in dynamic strength and 
ductility. In contrast, V doping promoted grain coarsening and compromised dynamic plasticity despite 
strengthening effects, while Hf and Ta additions induced specific microstructural deterioration modes, including 
blocky α -phase aggregation along grain boundary and transgranular cracking. The electrons interaction, phase 
distribution, and fracture behavior were systematically elucidated, highlighting Mo as the optimal microalloying 
candidate for balancing β stability and mechanical performance. These findings provide a computational- 
experimental framework for designing Zr alloys with tailored microstructures and superior load-bearing capa -
bilities under dynamic conditions.
1. Introduction
Owing to excellent corrosion resistance, small thermal neutron ab -
sorption cross section, low thermal expansion coefficient, good 
biocompatibility, and mechanical properties, Zr-Nb alloys are widely 
applied in nuclear, chemical, medical and defense industries [1 – 8] . In 
recent years, powder metallurgy (PM) Zr-Nb alloys have received 
increasing attention from researchers because of their advantages in 
uniform and fine grain structure, excellent strength and formability 
[9 – 13] . By leveraging the non-equilibrium process in powder metal -
lurgy, a relatively large quantity of high- temperature phases, such as 
the β phase, can be effectively retained. This is conducive to achieving 
an optimal balance between strength and plasticity of powder metal -
lurgy (PM) Zr alloys [9,14 – 17] .
For the Zr-Nb alloys, Nb always acts as a β phase stabilizer to reduce 
the transformation of β -Zr (bcc), by which the alloys can contain a lower 
elastic modulus [18,19] compared to that with α -Zr (hcp) [2,3,20] . 
Traditionally, the Nb content is usually under 30 wt% in the Zr-Nb alloys 
[1,4,14,21,22] . A large amount of α phase that was often regarded as the 
embrittlement factor is commonly formed along the boundaries in a 
continuous form that may cause great harm to mechanical reliability of 
the material [14,23 – 25] . Thus, it is necessary to conduct composition 
design for Zr-Nb alloys to achieve a higher β -phase proportion. Adjusting 
the content of existing Zr alloys or introducing new alloying elements 
represents the most efficient approach to enhancing their performance.
The properties of Zr alloys are intricately linked to their phase sta -
bility. When alloying elements are added, compounds or solid solutions 
form within the matrix, thereby improving the overall performance of 
the Zr alloys [26 – 28] . With the evolution of the alloy design concepts, in 
recent years, there has been a gradual exploration of multi-element 
zirconium alloys, such as high entropy alloy, medium entropy alloy 
and complex composition alloys [29,30] . This exploration takes into 
account the diverse contributions of various elements to the mechanical 
and physical properties, processability, and cost of the alloys. However, 
due to the complex interaction among alloying elements in zirconium 
alloys, the specific role of each element in stabilizing the phase structure

In order to improve the strength and plasticity of the materials while 
ensuring their physical and chemical properties, microalloying/minor 
addition has become an important method [31 – 33] . By trace element 
addition, a desired phase constitution and properties can be obtained 
due to the special electronic interaction between additions and matrix. 
Except for Nb element, V, Mo, and Ta are also β phase stabilizers 
[34 – 36] , where V has a higher solubility in the Zr matrix [37] , Mo is a 
β -Zr eutectoid element with limited solid solubility in β -Zr. Besides, Hf 
addition is often used in simultaneously enhancing strength and 
toughness of Zr alloy [38] . As is known, a comprehensive understanding 
of the theories that govern the structural and electronic properties of 
compounds facilitates effective manipulation of these properties. How -
ever, there have been few theoretical works on Zr-Nb systems, where the 
different atomic sizes and entropy effect in microregion caused by minor 
elements addition may strongly affect the electronic and structural 
features and further the mechanical properties. Moreover, a prevalent 
challenge in modeling solid solution through first-principles calculations 
is predicting or analyzing random configurations within a limited 
number of atoms [39] . Recently, special quasi-random structure (SQS) 
has been successfully applied to simulate random atomic distribution of 
solid solutions or high entropy alloys [39 – 41] . In this model, random -
ness of atomic distribution is achieved by simulating the correlation 
functions of an infinite random atomic site occupancy within a finite 
supercell. However, the mechanism underlying the stability of the bcc 
phase under different solid solution elements remain ambiguous, and 
the effect of the phase composition and microstructure formed at the 
matrix and interface on the mechanical properties of the material is still 
unclear, especially under high strain rate conditions.
In this study, Zr-30Nb was selected as the alloy matrix, and a variety 
of powder metallurgy Zr-27Nb-3X alloys (X = V, Mo, Ta and Hf) were 
prepared by microalloying. The phase stability of the bcc phase was 
described using the SQS method. Furthermore, the effects of alloying 
elements on phase structure, microstructure, mechanical properties and 
failure behaviors were systematically investigated.

3.1. Phases constituent and distribution
XRD patterns in Fig. 2 show that the phases of the Zr-Nb and Zr-Nb-X 
alloys consist of α , β and β’ phases. Notably, the peak intensity of the β 
phase is higher than that of the α phase, indicating that β phase con -
stitutes a larger proportion. Based on previous research results, the β’ 
phase is supposed to form during the eutectoid process of the original β 
phase [9] . Table 1 summarizes the phase abundance and lattice con -
stants for all alloys as determined by Rietveld ’ s refinement. It could be 
seen that the β phase abundance increased with V and Mo doping and 
decreased with Ta and Hf doping. However, due to the overlapping of 
the primary diffraction peaks of α and β phases at approximately 36
◦
, the 
absolute phase proportions obtained from the refinement results may 
exhibit uncertainty.
The proportion of each phase was also separately counted by SEM 
image analysis, as depicted in Fig. 3 . It can be found that the proportion 
of β phase (bcc) increases by over 70 % after adding V and Mo, while the 
proportion of α phase (hcp) in the Zr-27Nb-3Mo alloy decreases signif -
icantly. The patterns of influence of various alloying elements on phase 
proportions obtained from the Rietveld ’ s refinement are consistent with 
the results from image analysis. Additionally, based on the lattice pa -
rameters, it is speculated that the solid solubility of Ta element in the β 
phase may be relatively high, as significant lattice expansion occurs. In 
contrast, the phase proportion of alloys after adding Ta and Hf shows an 
opposite trend. This is because the addition of different alloying ele -
ments affects β phase stability. Mo is a potent β phase stabilizer, which 
can reduce the transition temperature of β → α and then stabilize the 
high-temperature β phase to room temperature. The β stabilization ef -
fects of V and Ta are not as strong, and Hf is a neutral element, making it 
difficult to retain the high-temperature β phase. The increase in β con -
tent is conducive to enhancing the plasticity of the alloy, yet it may also 
result in decreased strength.

3.3. Mechanical properties
Fig. 10 (a) exhibits the quasi-static compressive stress-strain curves 
of the Zr-Nb and Zr-Nb-X alloys at room temperature. The yield strength 
and plasticity were shown in Table 5, and the mechanical properties 
graphs of the X addition were plotted in Fig. 10 (b). The yield strength of 
the alloys ranges above 900 MPa, and the compressive strength exceeds 
1200 MPa. The addition of V, Ta, or Hf effectively enhances the yield 
strength of the Zr-Nb alloys. Among the alloys, the Zr-27Nb-3V alloy 
demonstrates the highest yield strength, reaching 1099 ± 6 MPa. This 
value represents a 21.2 % increase compared to Zr-30Nb alloy. Based on 
the microstructure of the alloy and the fine-grain strengthening theory, 
the Zr-27Nb-3V alloy exhibits a remarkable increase in grain size. 
However, its strength does not decline. This can be attributed to the 
solid-solution strengthening of V, which has high solubility in the β

3.4. Dynamic fracture mechanism
To further study the influence mechanism of alloying element 
addition on the strength and plasticity of the Zr-30Nb alloy, the fracture 
morphology and crack propagation path after dynamic compressive test 
were analyzed. Fig. 11 represents the longitudinal section morphology 
of Zr-Nb and Zr-Nb-X alloys after compression failure. Evidently, grains 
exhibit varying degrees of plastic deformation in the direction perpen -
dicular to the loading axis. By observing the crack front end, the cracks 
primarily nucleate and propagate within the α phase. Significantly, the α 
phase distributes predominantly and continuously along the grain 
boundaries. This distribution pattern leads to the occurrence of an 
intergranular fracture mode. From Fig. 11 (a, c, and d), Zr-30Nb, Zr- 
27Nb-3Mo, and Zr-27Nb-3Ta alloys show main cracks that extend and 
penetrate 45
◦
to the compression axis, indicating that the alloy has shear 
fracture. Adiabatic shear band begins to develop from the edge to the 
center in the sample. Microcracks or micro-holes initiate at the inho -
mogeneous microstructure of the shear band and then propagate along 
the direction of the adiabatic shear band, resulting in the failure of 
samples. Meanwhile, grains near the crack undergo severe deformation. 
In Fig. 10 (d), it can be seen that Zr-27Nb-3Ta also exhibits transgranular 
fracture, and its propagation path goes through both the β grain 
boundary and grain interior. This is because there are a large number of 
lath α phases in β grains, leading to stress concentrations inside the grain 
between the phase boundaries or in α phase, which is extremely unfa -
vorable for the continuous plastic deformation of the β phase.
It can be found that the distribution state of grain boundary α 
significantly affects the crack propagation. The narrow grain boundary α 
with discontinuous distribution in the Zr-27Nb-3Mo alloy causes less 
damage to the plasticity of the alloy. β phase embedded in discontinuous 
grain boundary α phase effectively releases the potential stress con -
centration at grain boundary, inhibiting the crack nucleation and 
propagation, further contributing to high strength and plasticity. How -
ever, in the Zr-27Nb-Hf alloy, the α phase at the grain boundaries is 
distributed in large blocks. This leads to severe stress concentration. As 
can be seen from Fig. 11 (e), a large number of microcracks form in GB α , 
leading to premature failure under dynamic loading condition. Fig. 12
schematically illustrates the alterations in the microstructure and dy -
namic failure mechanism of the alloy resulting from microalloying ad -
ditions. The primary failure mode of the alloy is intergranular fracture. V 
addition causes grain coarsening but reduces the plastic development 
around the crack-wake significantly. In contrast, Mo addition refines the 
grains, suppresses grain boundary α formation and coarsening, as well as 
promotes a large consecutive β phase. These changes make it easier to 
prevent crack propagation and accumulate continuous plastic defor -
mation in Zr-Nb-Mo alloy. Ta addition increases the lath α content 
within matrix and causes partial transgranular fracture. While Hf addi -
tion causes the alloy to form block grain boundary α , leading to micro- 
holes and microcracks nucleation and coalescence along the grain 
boundaries.
4. Conclusions
Based on the Zr-30Nb alloy system, Zr-27Nb-3X (X = V, Mo, Ta, Hf) 
alloys were fabricated via powder metallurgy. Integrated first-principles 
calculations and experimental studies elucidated the following key 
findings: The following conclusions were drawn: 
(1) Mo addition suppressed lath α formation, narrows grain bound -
ary α into a discontinuous morphology, and increased β phase 
proportion from 69.3 % to 75.3 %. V doping inhibits eutectoid 
structure formation, while Ta and Hf additions elevated α -phase 
proportions (29.6 % and 39.4 %, respectively). Ta promoted 
eutectoid structures, whereas Hf favored blocky grain-boundary 
α -phase development.

phase, with each formation energies reaching 1.775, 1.146, and
