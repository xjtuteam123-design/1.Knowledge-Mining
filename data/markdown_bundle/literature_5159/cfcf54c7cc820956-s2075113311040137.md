Abstract—The article presents an overview of the projects aimed at development of a new class of high-tem-
perature Nb–Si composites consisting of a niobium matr ix and a strengthener in the form of niobium sili-
cides. Nb–Si binary diagrams and Nb–Cr–Si, Nb–H f–Si, Nb–Ti–Si, and Nb–Si–Mo ternary diagrams,
as well as Nb–Si–Ti–Cr and Nb–Si–Ti–Hf quaternary diagrams, are analyzed, on the basis of which the
compositions of the composite materials are selected. Major methods of obtaining of the composites are con-
sidered, including directional solicitation. The main physicomechanical properties are analyzed: short- and
long-term strength, destruction viscosity , elasticity modulus, thermal expansion, high-temperature strength,
and protective coatings. The density of Nb–Si composites is 6.6–7.2 g/cm3, and the operating temperature
is 1350°C, which is 200°C higher than temperature ability of monocrystals of nickel high-temperature alloys
of the last generations.
Keywords: high-temperature composites, Nb–Si composites , directional solidification, high-temperature
strength.

TEMPERATURE CREEPAGE
Long-term strength and resistance against creep-
age are the most important characteristics of high-
temperature structural mate rials. The main target in
the field of materials science was development of a
material with a 1% creepage limit after 125 h at
1200
°C and stress of 170 MPa. The classical creepage
curves of Nb–Si composites are not available in the
scientific literature; mainly , there are data concerning
creepage rates either at extension or at compression.
Bewlay [11] studied the creepage of DS MASC com-
posite at extension and compression in the tempera-
ture range of 1000–1200
°C [11]. Figure 18a illustrates
the curves of the Larson–Miller parameter relation for
this composite and NHTA monocrystals, generation
II. It can be seen that, at low stresses (high tempera-
tures), the creepage properties of the DS MASC com-
posite are the same as that of CMSX-4 monocrystals,
but lower than that of CMSX-10 monocrystals at high
stresses. However, in terms of specific high-tempera-
ture strength, the DS MASC composite is superior
than the monocrystals at all temperatures owing to
lower density (Fig. 18b). Distinctive conclusions con-
cerning the dominancy of such composites are prema-
ture right now , since extended tests are required with
databases tenfold greater than ones available at
present.
In the course of studies of the effect of doping ele-
ments Hf, Ti, Cr, Al, and Mo on creepage upon com-
pression at 1200
°C, it has been demonstrated that,
with an increase in titanium and hafnium concentra-
tions, the creepage rate increases. From the point of
view of optimal creepage rate, the ratio Ti/Hf should
be <3, and the Ti concentration should be <21%. At
high concentration of titanium or hafnium, there
occurs formation of silicides hP16 Ti
5Si3, which
become more stable in comparison with silicides tI32
Nb
5Si3 and tP32 Nb3Si. Silicide hP16 Ti5Si3 has lower
resistance against creepage; thus, it reduces the overall
creepage properties of the composite.

HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH
It is a well-known fact that niobium and its alloys
are characterized by low resistance against oxidation.
The high diffusion rate of oxygen through oxide layers
with its subsequent dissolution in niobium phases
results in strengthening and embrittlement of such
phases in alloys. Therefore, numerous studies and pat-
ents are devoted to determination of the high-temper-
ature strength of Nb–Si composites as a function of
their chemical composition, testing temperature,
compositions of protective coatings, and technology
of their application. It is quite obvious that, without a
positive solution of these problems, it is impossible to
use niobium composites for operation at temperatures
exceeding 1200
°C.
High-temperature tests are performed using the
standard method of determining the weight variations
of a specimen (mg/cm2) or the decrease in its diameter
(μm) as a result of detachment of scaling at a predeter-
mined temperature. With regard to the resistance
against isothermal oxidation of Nb–Si composites
without coatings, two goals were pursued: short-term
and long-term. The short-term goal was intended for
development of a composite a specimen of which
would be reduced in diameter by <200 
μm in the
course of annealing for 10 h at 1370°C. The long-term
goal stipulated development of a material for which
the reduction in specimen diameter would not be
higher than 25 
μm in annealing for 100 h at 1315°C. It
should be noted that monocrystalline NHTAs, gene-
ration II, have the same loss of ignition, but at 1150
°C.
The composites based on the Nb–Si binary system
are characterized by low high-temperature strength;
however, this parameter at 1200 and 1300
°C increases
significantly as a result of doping with Ti, Al, Cr, and
Hf, as can be seen in Fig. 20. Also, the optimal resis-
tance against oxidation and resistance against creep-
age are achieved at the ratio Nb/(Ti + Hf) 
≈ 1.8–2.1
and Si content from 17 to 19 at %.

doping with Ge, B and Fe; at the same time, Ge and B

HEAT-PROTECTIVE COATINGS
In order to protect Nb–Si composites against oxi-
dation up to 1400
°C, thermal barrier coatings (TBCs)
were developed, which are widely applied for protec-
tion of NHTA monocrystals against oxidation. The
TBC is a ceramic layer of zirconium oxide, stabilized
by 7% yttrium dioxide. This 300-
μm-thick layer is
characterized by low thermal conductivity , providing a
decrease in substrate temperature. Owing to the
columnar structure of the TBC grains (maizelike
structure), the coatings are capable to prevent oxygen
diffusion over the grain boundaries and oxidation of
the substrate on which they are applied. As a result of
the difference between the TCLE of the high-temper-
ature substrate and ceramic coating under thermal
cycling, stresses occur, which finally result in destruc-
tion of the TBC by fracture. Therefore, to prevent oxi-
dation of the substrate and to provide its compatibility
with the ceramic layer, a so-called bond coating is
applied. The term TBC refers to the combination of
the ceramic thermal protective layer and bond coating.
However, even after application of bond coating, the
fracture of the TBC will occur after long-term opera-
tion. The bond coating is made of niobium alloy con-
taining silicon, titanium, and chromium with the con-
centrations exceeding the respective values in the main
alloy . In particular, the composition of one such coating is
patented: 66% Si, 10% Ti, 5% Cr, and remaining 19% Nb.
The phase composition of such coating is as follows: 50%

CONCLUSIONS
The current state of the problem of developing high-
temperature Nb–Si composites and manufacturing
GTE parts on their basis, mainly blades, can be judged
by the brief results published in 2008 after completion of
the European project titled UL TMAT [36]. The most
important results of this project are as follows:
(i) Compositions of innovative composites have
been determined with a satisfactory balance of
mechanical properties in the temperature range of
20–1300
°C and resistance against oxidation. The
capabilities and limitations of the innovative high-
temperature composites have been established.
(ii) Cost-efficient technologies of production of
these composites have been developed using powder
metallurgy and conventional metallurgy of ingot pro-
duction. Investment casting technology has been
developed and the capability of producing large-scale
blades (up to 320 mm in length) based on Nb–Si com-
posites has been demonstrated.
(iii) Systems of protective coatings and the tech-
nology of their application have been developed, and
their high-temperature resistance in the range of 800
and 1100–1300
°C has been tested. The developed
heat-protective coatings reveal reliable protective
properties; however, for higher operating lifetimes, it is
necessary to increase the resistance against oxidation
of the composites.
(iv) A database has been developed for the proper-
ties required for resource-oriented design of high-
pressure turbine parts.
(v) The technology of producing turbine parts has
been preliminarily estimated, concerning mainly
machinability and connections.
The main scientific and technological results can be
considered as a significant stage in understanding the
synergetic impact of doping elements on phase stability ,
segregation effects, phase transformations and equilibria,
oxidation process, microstructure, and mechanical
properties of high-temperature composites.
