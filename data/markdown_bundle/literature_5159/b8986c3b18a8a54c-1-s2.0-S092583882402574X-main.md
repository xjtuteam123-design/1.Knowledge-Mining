1. Introduction
The advancement in aerospace, automotive, and energy industries
has stimulated a pronounced desire for materials exhibiting superior
high-temperature mechanical properties and reduced density [1,2] .
Conventional nickel-based superalloys are renowned for their
high-temperature strength, creep- and fatigue resistance; however, their
density is still rather high, typically surpassing 8.0 g/cm
3
, which limits
their application in scenarios where weight reduction is crucial [3] . In
contrast, iron-based superalloys boast a relatively low density, but suffer
from low high-temperature strength and structural stability, confining
their service temperature to below 700 ℃ [4,5] . Therefore, exploring
innovative superalloys with low density has recently become a priority.
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) provide a novel pathway for developing
lightweight alloy materials. The focus on eutectic high-entropy alloys
(EHEAs) is well-founded due to their unique eutectic lamellar structure,
which endows them with exceptional mechanical properties, excellent
liquidity, and castability. A variety of EHEAs, including AlCoCrFeNi

4. Conclusions
The main conclusions can be summarized as follows:
(1) Novel Ni
70- x
Co
10
Cr
20
Al
x
( x = 17, 18, 19 at%) EHESs with the
density of 7.3 – 7.5 g/cm
3
have been designed. As the Al content
increases, the alloy transforms from hypo-eutectic to eutectic and
then to hyper-eutectic. Al18 alloy processes the lamellar hetero-
geneous structure consisting of FCC and B2 phases with the nano-
scale L1
2
and disordered Cr-rich BCC phase precipitated from the
FCC matrix and the B2 phase, respectively. After annealing at 750
℃ for 7 d, the FCC and B2 phases underwent phase decomposi-
tion, but no additional phases were detected.
(2) The present EHESs exhibit an excellent combination of yield
strength and elongation at ambient temperature and yield
strength and density at 750 ℃ . Especially, Al18 alloy exhibited a
yield and ultimate tensile strength of ~787 MPa and
~1133 MPa, and elongation of ~7.0 % at ambient temperature; a
yield and ultimate tensile strength of ~563 MPa and ~618 MPa
at 750 ℃ , which is even higher than those of equiaxed crystal cast
nickel-based superalloys at 700 ℃ . The density of Al18 alloy is
7.41 g/cm
3
, which has attained a ~13 % reduction in density
compared to the conventional superalloys.
(3) The ambient and high temperature strengthening effect can be
attributed to three possible factors: the solid solution strength-
ening of Al, HDI hardening of finer eutectic lamellar structure,
and precipitation strengthening of high-density thermally stable
ordered L1
2
and disordered BCC nano-precipitates. However, HDI
hardening is greatly weakened under high temperatures.
(4) The ambient-temperature deformation behavior of these EHESs is
predominantly governed by dislocations slip. At a high temper-
