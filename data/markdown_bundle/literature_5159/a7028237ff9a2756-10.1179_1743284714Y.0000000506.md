Room temperature mechanical strength and
fracture behaviour of Nb–Ti–Si–Cr based alloy
L. Su, L. Jia, S. Yuan, L. Zhou, H. Zhang and H. Zhang *
The effect of heat treatment on the mechanical strength of a three-phase Nb SS/Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb alloy
with composition Nb–22Ti–12Si–14Cr–2Al–2Hf was investigated. This included the evaluation of
microstructure, room temperature mechanical strength and the fracture behaviours. The
microstructures of heat treated samples were composed of Nb SS,N b 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb, and the
phase size changed under different heat treatment schemes. The results showed that the
microstructure composed of large Nb SS and fine Nb 5Si3 exhibited best mechanical strength due
to the deformation of Nb SS in these samples. Moreover, fractographic analysis indicated that the
Nb5Si3 and Cr 2Nb fractured in cleavage manner in all heat treated samples.
Keywords: Nb–Si–Cr based alloy, Heat treatment, Microstructure, Mechanical strength
Introduction
Advances in turbine engines are paced by the continuing
research for lightweight durable materials with good high
temperature strength and stiffness. In recent years, It is
interesting to study Nb–Si based alloys as one of potential
high temperature materials due to their higher melting
point, excellent high temperature strength and low
density.
1–3 However, the low room temperature fracture
toughness and poor oxidation resistance of Nb–Si based
alloys have been the major barriers to their practical
applications. Many studies reveal that the balance of the
mechanical properties and oxidation resistance could be
achieved by the addition of the alloying element
4–10 and
by adjusting processing technologies. 11–13 The effects of
alloying element have been reported in many research
papers, and Nb–Ti–Si–Cr–Al–Hf is considered to be one
of the most potential systems to obtain the balanced
properties.
2 Moreover, directional solidiﬁcation is a
useful approach to obtain the well aligned microstructure
and improve the mechanical properties of Nb–Si based
alloy.6,14 In our previous study, 15 the Nb–12Si–22Ti–
14Cr–2Al–2Hf alloys fabricated by directional solidiﬁca-
tion were investigated. The higher withdrawal rate plus
heat treatment (HT) led to the microstructure consisting
of ﬁne intermetallic phases (Nb
5Si3 and Cr 2Nb) and
continuous Nb SS, which resulted in the increase in
fracture toughness. In addition, the subsequent HT can
cause the disappearance of the metastable phases and
reduce the solute segregation in the alloy, 16 which is
beneﬁcial to the improvement of room temperature
mechanical properties.
In order to optimise the microstructure, different
HT schemes were carried out in the present study, and
the relationship among microstructure, mechanical
properties (tensile properties, fracture toughness and
hardness) and fracture behaviour of Nb–Si based alloy
fabricated by directional solidiﬁcation plus different HT
schemes was investigated.

Results and discussion
Microstructure of HT samples
In our previous study, the microstructure of this DS
sample was investigated.15 The microstructure consisted
of Nb SS dendrites, lath shaped Nb 3Si and Ti rich
NbSSzCr2Nb eutectics, which aligned nearly along the
growth direction. Figure 1 is the XRD patterns of HT
samples. As shown in Fig. 1, the phase constituents of
all HT samples are Nb
SS,N b5Si3 and Cr2Nb. No Nb3Si
peak is detected in the XRD patterns of HT samples,
suggesting that the Nb 3Si phase has completely con-
verted into Nb SS and Nb5Si3 after HT.
Figures 2 and 3 show the typical longitudinal and
transverse microstructures of the HT samples respec-
tively. As shown in Fig. 2, the microstructures of HT
samples consist of light grey Nb
SS, dark grey Nb5Si3 and
black Cr 2Nb. The Nb 5Si3 decomposed from Nb 3Si
exhibits a network sharp with blunt interface. The
morphologies of NbSS present two types: one is dendrite
morphology preserved from the morphology of Nb SS of
DS sample, and the other is small particle-like morphology
decomposed from Nb3Si. These two types of NbSS tend to
be connected with each other and form the continuous
matrix after HT. The volume fractions of the NbSS,N b5Si3
and Cr2Nb are similar in all HT samples and with the
percentage ofy63, y28 and y9% respectively. Although
some research indicated that the decomposition kinetic of
Nb3Si was very sluggish, 10,14 the Nb 3Si has completely
decomposed after HT at 1375 uC for 5 h in the present
study. This result suggests that the decomposition kinetics
of the Nb3Si could be accelerated by alloying the element
of Cr, which is in agreement with the study of Sekido et
al.14 Moreover, Zelenitsaset al. indicated that alloying Cr
could stabilise Nb5Si3 and destabilise the Nb3Si.10 Hence,
owing to the addition of high Cr content, the HT
temperature and time for the decomposition of Nb
3Si are
both reduced in the present study.
Table 2 shows the phase size in the HT samples. The
secondary arm spacing of Nb SS and the size of Nb 5Si3
and Cr2Nb are listed in Table 2. As shown in Table 2,
the secondary arm spacing of Nb SS and the size of
Nb5Si3 are 7–12 and 4–5 mm respectively for the
HT1375-5 sample, while those for the HT1375-50
sample are 17–40 and 5–10 mm respectively. Moreover,
when the HT is carried out at 1400uC, the secondary arm
spacing of NbSS and the size of Nb5Si3 are 12–20 and 2–
5 mm respectively for the HT1400-5 sample. It could be
concluded that both the secondary arm spacing of Nb SS
and the size of Nb 5Si3 increase with increasing HT
temperature or HT time. However, the Cr 2Nb coarsens
slightly under different HT schemes, and the size of
Cr
2Nb is y5 mm in all HT samples.

Fracture behaviour
From the results of tensile tests, the HT samples exhibit
brittle fracture at room temperature. The details are
revealed by fractographic investigation through SEM.
Typical images of local fracture surface are presented in
Fig. 4. It could be found that the fractographies are
different when the samples are heat treated under
different schemes.
As for the HT1375-5, HT1375-50 and HT1400-10
samples (as shown in Fig. 4 a, c and e), the Nb
5Si3 and
Cr2Nb fracture in a brittle cleavage manner exhibiting
cleavage step. The Nb SS mainly fractures in a cleavage
manner demonstrating a ‘hair river’ pattern. In addition,
only a little Nb
SS fractures in a plastic stretching manner
showing a sharp edge (as shown in Fig. 4 a). Moreover,
little cracks are found in these samples. It could be
concluded that the fractures of these samples are mainly
attribute to the cleavage of Nb
5Si3,N b SS and Cr 2Nb,
thus leading to a smoother over appearances.
In terms of HT1375-10 and HT1400-5, the overall
appearances of these two samples are rougher, which
may be due to a mixed mechanism of cleavage of Nb
5Si3
and Cr 2Nb, and plastic stretching of Nb SS occurred in
these two samples. The plastic stretching of Nb SS leads
to the tearing appearance, suggesting that the Nb SS is
signiﬁcant ductility. Moreover, there are many cracks
that originate from Nb
5Si3 and terminate in NbSS, which
suggests that the Nb 5Si3 is the origin of crack initiation
