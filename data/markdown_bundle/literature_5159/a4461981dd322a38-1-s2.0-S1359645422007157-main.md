Rhenium (Re) is a paramount strengthening element in nickel-based single crystal superalloys because of 
its low diffusion coeﬃcient, high melting point and high partition coeﬃcient between γ and γ’ phases. 
However, its detrimental inﬂuence on phase stability has remained a long-standing problem to be solved. 
In this paper, we report a new design strategy to improve creep resistance and phase stability of nickel- 
based single crystal superalloys by replacing Re by osmium (Os), which contradicts the common belief. 
The experimental observations clearly show that the creep rate of newly designed CSU-Os-2 alloy is lower 
than that of commercial CMSX-4 alloy at 980 °C/200 MPa/100 h. Moreover, the CSU-Os-2 alloy also owns 
the better phase stability after exposure at 1100 °C for 10 0 0 h, compared with CMSX-4 alloy. The striking 
properties can be well explained by higher partition coeﬃcient between γ and γ’ phases and very likely 
lower diffusion coeﬃcient of Os in γ matrix than Re.

1. Introduction 
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys, exhibiting a typical mi- 
crostructure of a high fraction of coherently precipitated γ’ (or- 
dered fcc-L1 2 structure) cubes separated by thin channels of γ- 
matrix (disordered fcc-A1 structure), have excellent mechanical 
properties and high resistance to creep and fatigue at elevated 
temperatures, and are widely used as the essential material of 
high-pressure turbine blade and vane [1–3] . To meet the ad- 
vancement of constantly increasing the turbine entry temperature, 
higher resistance to the creep deformation and microstructural sta- 
bility are of necessity for Ni-based single-crystal superalloys [ 4 , 5 ]. 
For the past decades, intensive effort s have been devoted to con- 
stant introduction of higher percentage of refractory alloying ele- 
ments, like Mo, W, Ta, Re, etc., into Ni-based single-crystal super- 
alloys to improve their creep resistance and phase stability [ 6 , 7 ]. 
For instance, the total amounts of W + Mo + Ta + Re increase from 
16.4 wt.% in the second generation to 20.1 wt.% in the third gen- 
eration of nickel-base single crystal superalloys. Among them, Re

Accordingly, substantial efforts all over the world have been 
making to address the concern of TCP precipitations. One vi- 
able measure is to introduce the alloying element Ru (ruthenium) 
[ 11 , 12 ] because its addition can reverse the partitioning behavior 
[ 13 , 14 ], increase the solubility of Re and W within the γ-matrix 
phase [ 15 , 16 ], lower the TCP/ γ interface energy [ 15 , 17 ] and γ/ γ’

induce the occurrence of topological inversion, especially at high 
temperatures, which in turn inﬂuences the creep rupture life of 
nickel-based single-crystal superalloys [ 20 , 21 ]. Therefore, there is 
an incentive to hunt for other possible elements replacing Re or 
Re/Ru, aiming at exploration of the novel nickel-base single crys- 
tal superalloys [ 22 , 23 ]. One effective strategy is to search the el- 
ements with interdiffusion coeﬃcients in γ-matrix of Ni-based 
single-crystal superalloys comparable to and even lower than Re.

by Os in Ni-based alloys in terms of more consideration, including 
solid solution strengthening, creep resistance and microstructure

optimizing the heat treatment routine, aiming at better creep re- 
sistance and phase stability. 
2. Experimental 
Based on the above analysis, a novel Re-free Ni-based single- 
crystal superalloy was designed by completely replacing Re by Os 
in the present work. The chemical composition of the designed Os- 
containing nickel-based single crystal superalloy, namely CSU-Os- 
2, is shown in Table 1 . The alloy ingot with 60 mm in diameter 
and 160 mm in length, were prepared by vacuum induction melt- 
ing (VIM) and the elemental compositions were measured by in- 
ductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES). 
Single crystal rods with a diameter of 15 mm and a length of 
170 mm were directionally solidiﬁed by Bridgman method (ALD 
GmbH, Germany) with a withdrawal rate of 3.0 mm/min. So- 
lution and two-step aging heat treatment were applied as fol- 
lows: 1315 °C/2 h + 1320 °C/2 h + 1325 °C/2 h + 1330 °C/10 h, 
AC + 1120 °C/4 h, AC + 870 °C/24 h, AC. The heat treatment was 
carried out in air with a heating rate of 10 °C/min up to 900 °C, 
and then 5 °C/min till solution temperature (tube furnace, Carbo- 
lite Gero). 
Standard metallography process was carried out to obtain high- 
quality surface ﬁnish for microstructure observation, and was 
then chemically etched with a solution of 33% HNO 3 + 33% acetic 
acid + 33% H 2 O + 1% HF. Scanning electron microscope (SEM, FEI 
Quanta 650) was used to observe microstructures. The JXA-8530F 
electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA) equipped with 4 wavelength 
dispersive spectrometers (WDS) was used for microprobe analysis. 
The WDS were conﬁgured with multiple crystal databases such as 
TAP (for Al, W, Os, and Hf), PETJ (for Ta, Mo and Ti) and LIFH (for 
Ni, Cr, Co). 
Long-term exposure up to 10 0 0 h was carried out of fully heat- 
treated alloy at 1100 °C, and the experimental alloys were metal- 
lographically grinded, polished and chemically etched. Creep tests 
were performed at 980 °C/200 MPa for 100 h (Specimen geome- 
try was provided in Supplementary materials). Subsequently, the 
gage section was cut to observe the deformed microstructures on 
both (001) plane and cross sections. Transmission electron micro- 
scope (TEM, FEI Titan G2 60–300) with an objective spherical aber- 
ration corrector and a super-X high resolution energy dispersive 
X-ray (EDX) spectrometry system operated at 300 kV was used to 
observe elemental distribution and lattice image. 
To capture three-dimensional (3D) spatial imaging and chem- 
ical composition measurements, nanoscale needle-shaped speci- 
mens for atom probe tomography (APT) observation were pre- 
pared using lift-out and annular milled in a FEI Scios focused ion 
beam/scanning electron microscope (FIB/SEM). The APT characteri- 
zations were performed in a local electrode atom probe (CAMEACA 
LEAP 50 0 0 XR). The specimens were analyzed at 70 K in voltage 
mode, at a pulse repetition rate of 200 kHz, a pulse fraction of 
20%, and an evaporation detection rate of 0.2% atom per pulse. The 
data analysis workstations AP Suite 6.1 were used for creating the 
3D reconstructions and data analysis. 
3. Results and discussion 
During the design of novel Ni-based single-crystal superalloys, 
their high-temperature yield strength and creep resistance are the 
two foremost properties. Since Re preferentially goes in the γ- 
matrix, the contribution of Re/Os to the yield strength of Ni-based 
single-crystal superalloys majorly comes from the solid solution 
strengthening mechanism, which heavily relates to the solid sol- 
ubility of Re/Os in γ phase at elevated temperatures. While for 
the creep resistance, the interdiffusion coeﬃcients of Re/Os in γ
matrix and the inﬂuential factors on the microstructure stability, 
including the fraction of γ’ precipitates, the lattice misﬁt of γ/ γ’, 
and the susceptibility to formation of TCP phase, should be taken 
into account. 
First, the relatively large solubility of Re/Os acts as the guaran- 
tee for its contribution to the solid solution strengthening in Ni- 
based single-crystal superalloys. Very recently, Lin et al. [ 36 , 37 ]. 
experimentally measured the phase equilibria of ternary Ni-Al-Os 
system in Ni-rich corner at 10 0 0 °C and 1200 °C, as well as the 
phase equilibria of quaternary Ni-Al-Cr-Os system in Ni-rich corner 
at 1200 °C. According to the measured phase equilibria of ternary 
Ni-Al-Os system [36] , the maximum solubility of Os in γ phase 
at 10 0 0 °C and 1200 °C are 5.1 wt.% and 7.5 wt.%, respectively. 
Moreover, based on the measured phase equilibria of quaternary 
Ni-Al-Cr-Os system [37] , the maximum solubility of Os in γ phase 
at 1200 °C is 7.8 wt.%, indicating that the addition of fourth ele- 
ment may not largely affect the maximum solubility of Os. Accord- 
ing to the available literature report [38] , the experimental max- 
imum solubility of Re in γ phase of ternary Ni-Al-Re system at 
1040 °C is 6.91 wt.%. Thus, Os has a comparable maximum solid 
solubility in the γ phase as Re ( Fig. 1 a). Moreover, the atomic ra- 
dius of Os and Re is 0.135 nm and 0.137 nm, respectively, both of 
which are slightly larger than Ni (0.124 nm) (Table 5 in Ref. [37] ), it 
can be concluded that Os might have a comparable solid solution 
strengthening as Re in Ni-based alloys.

4. Conclusions 
A detailed theoretical analysis on the possibility of substitu- 
tion of Re by Os in Ni-based single crystal alloys was performed 
in terms of solid solution strengthening, creep resistance and mi- 
crostructure stability. With a positive conﬁrmation, a novel Re-free 
nickel-based superalloy, named as CSU-Os-2, was designed by re- 
placing Re with Os in the commercial CMSX-4 alloy, and then suc- 
cessfully prepared in single crystal rods. By means of proper so- 
lution and aging heat treatment, the segregation was almost alle- 
viated and typical γ/ γ’ microarchitectures were produced like the 
commercial alloys. Under exposure at 1100 °C/10 0 0 h, the coales- 
cence of γ’ precipitates of CSU-Os-2 alloy is slower than CMSX- 
4 alloy, and no TCP phase was observed. After creep deforma- 
tion at 980 °C/20 0 MPa/10 0 h, the CSU-Os-2 alloy demonstrates 
lower creep rate than in CMSX-4 alloy. All the attractive proper- 
ties/performance can be explained by higher partition coeﬃcient 
between γ and γ’ phases and very likely lower diffusion coeﬃ- 
cient of Os in γ phase than Re. The successful development of a 
novel Re-free but Os-containing Ni-based single-crystal superalloy 
with enhanced creep resistance and microstructure stability deﬁ- 
nitely explores the new direction of next-generation Ni-based sin- 
gle crystal superalloys.
