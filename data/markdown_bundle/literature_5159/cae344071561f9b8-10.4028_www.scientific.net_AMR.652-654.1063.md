Keywords: the Cast TiAl alloy, tensile properties, fracture surface. 
Abstract. A cast TiAl alloy with directional γ-TiAl/ α2-Ti 3Al lamellar microstructure showed 
excellent strength retention at high temperatures and distinguished ambient ductility level as well. But 
both the ductility and strength at room temperature exhibited somewhat undulation. According to the 
microstructure and fracture surface observations, t he negative diversification of the test results is 
attributed to the presence of mis-oriented lamellar colonies. The tilted lamellar colonies larger than a 
critical value can trigger the tensile fractures and therefore diminish the tensile strength and ductility 
significantly. 
Introduction 
Light-weight TiAl intermetallics alloys are considered as promising materials of turbine blades for 
their good gravity-specific strength and stiffness at high temperatures. The roadblock includes 
undesirable ambient ductility and not yet satisfact ory cost control [1]. What the studies using 
polysynthetically twinned (PST) crystals have typically clarified the effect of lamellar orientation on 
the mechanical properties in TiAl alloys suggested that a good balance of strength and ductility can be 
obtained when the loading axis is parallel to the l amellar boundaries [2]. Therefore, forming 
directional lamellar microstructures in the turbine blades should be a potent way to fulfill the 
designers’ expectation, especially the increased en durance to the intermetallics’ brittleness. In 
principle, seeding of the α phase in such casting operations is necessary because the lamellar structure 
of TiAl-base alloys consisting of γ-TiAl and α2-Ti3Al phases with the (111) γ // (0001) α2 orientation 
relationship while the preferred growth direction of α dendrites is parallel to the [0001] direction [3]. 
It is therefore much cost-plus of course although i t has shown a glimmer success in both lamellar 
orientation control and excellent mechanical properties [4]. It is recently noticed that simple castin g 
operations that produce fully columnar macrostructures through α solidification should also result in 
the directional lamellar microstructure with the la mellae perpendicular to the α columnar growth 
direction, i.e., parallel to the turbine blades’ su rfaces [5]. It is reasonable to believe that such 
directional lamellar microstructure should be beneficial to bear the service load in the turbine blades 
as well since the mechanical properties of TiAl all oys mainly depend on the lamellar orientation. 
However, there are still concerns on principle abou t the detrimental influence of final solidification 
area to the mechanical properties, especially to th e possible deviation of tensile test results for th e 
intermetallics-based materials. 
This study conducted a number of tensile tests of a cast TiAl alloy with directional lamellar 
microstructure to statistically examine the strength retention and ductility undulation. The influences 
of macro- and microstructure as well as the cast fl aw on the tensile results are thereafter assessed 
according to their effects on cracks initiation and propagation on the fracture surfaces.

Experimental procedures 
 The cast Ti-47.5Al-2.5V-1.0Cr alloys was re-melt ed in a vacuum cold crucible induction 
levitation-melting furnace and cast into an investm ent ceramic mould to produce 100 ×80 ×10 mm3 
platy specimens. The hot isostatic pressing was carried out at 1250 °C in 120MPa Argon for 3.0 hours 
to remove the porosity in the cast specimens while remain the directional lamellar microstructure. The 
tensile tests were then carried out at room temperatures and 800 °C. Tensile specimens were taken by 
spark erosion technique along the cast plates’ long itudinal direction and then wheel ground to 
25.0mm gauge length matching with 5.0mm diameter. T he fracture surfaces were then examined 
using scanning electron microscopy. 
 The metallographic specimens were mechanically p olished, and those for microstructure 
observation were dip-etched by a hydro-solution containing 2%HF and 10%HNO3 (by volume). The 
microstructure and porosity examinations were carri ed out under optical and scanning electronic 
microscopes.

Detailed observation found that the critical micro- cracks are composed of several coarse 
interlamellar microcracks characterized by smooth f acet surfaces without obvious river pattern on 
them and the angles between the cleavage cracking planes of these sub-microcracks are comparatively 
small (Fig.5). No evidence from the fracture surfac es exhibited the detrimental influence of the 
remainder tiny pores to the ambient tensile properties. 
According to the fact that inter-lamellar strength is less than a third of the translamellar strength in 
TiAl lamellar structure [7], the size of the interlamellar crack should be the controlling factor for the 
failure of the studied TiAl tensile specimens. The statistic section measurement of initial cracking 
facets indicates that the tilted lamellar colonies in the specimens with desirable ambient tensile 
properties are mostly smaller than 600 µm. So that, the lamellar colonies tilted to the mai n fracture 
surface are fragile in the test process. 
In principle, those mis-oriented lamellar colonies are transformed from the equiaxed α grains that 
precipitate during final solidification due to not yet well optimized heat radiation condition. It is 
therefore necessary to further intensify the heat radiation gradient for minimize the size of the random 
oriented lamellar colonies. Such cast technology modification is on-going. 
Summary 
The tensile properties of cast TiAl alloys containi ng directional γ-TiAl/ α
2-Ti 3Al lamellar 
microstructure can reach a surpassing level at both room and elevated temperatures even though still 
present certain amount of undulation. The unfavoura ble diversification of ambient ductility and 
strength was induced by the mis-oriented lamellar colonies that are larger enough to trigger the tensile 
fracture. Accordingly, the technical approach to eliminate such detrimental influence is suggested.
