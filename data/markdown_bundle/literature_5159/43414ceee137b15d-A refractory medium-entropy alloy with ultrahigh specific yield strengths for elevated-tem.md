ABSTRACT
Refractory high- or medium-entropy alloys (RHEAs or RMEAs) with excellent high-temperature mechanical properties and softening resis-
tance have been proven to be the potential candidates for advanced engineering applications. However, room temperature brittleness and
high density have become an important challenge that needs to be addressed. In this work, the tensile mechanical behavior and the underly-
ing deformation mechanisms of lightweight Ti 40Zr20Nb13.33V26.67 RMEA at 298 and 873 K were investigated systematically. The results
showed that the as-cast RMEA has a single body-centered cubic phase and low density (5.88 g/cm 3) and exhibits excellent mechanical proper-
ties at 298 K, with yield strength of 1033.9 MPa, specific yield strength of 175.8 MPa /C1 cm3/g, and tensile fracture strain of 5.3%. More impor-
tantly, it also exhibits ultrahigh strength and sufficient ductility at 873 K, with yield strength of 783.2 MPa, specific yield strength of
133.2 MPa/C1 cm3/g, and tensile fracture strain of 5.7%. It showed that a large number of slip bands and dislocation bands are the main deforma-
tion products at 298 K, leading to excellent ductility. In comparison, high dislocation density was found between the slip bands in the samples
deformed at 873 K, which can effectively hinder the motion of dislocations, resulting in strain hardening and the increase in strength. This
work can provide a route for the design and fabrication of high-performance lightweight alloys, which would be beneficial for engineering
applications.

Lightweight structural materials with excellent combinations of
ultrahigh strength and ductility over a wide range of temperatures are
urgently demanded for advanced engineering applications, such as avi-
ation engines, gas turbines, and nuclear reactor systems. In the past
two decades, the newly emerged high- or medium-entropy alloys
(HEAs or MEAs) and complex concentrated alloys (CCAs) have
completely changed the traditional alloy design strategy. There are at
least five (three to four) principal elements in an HEA (MEA), which
may provide diverse routes for exploring and designing high-
performance metallic materials.
1–4 Refractory HEAs or MEAs (RHEAs
or RMEAs), which are composed of multiple principal refractory ele-
ments with high melting points, have drawn considerable attention
because of their outstanding high-temperature strength and thermal
stability.
5–7 For example, the typical MoNbTaW and MoNbTaWV
RHEAs have yield strengths of 405 and 477 MPa at 1600 /C14 C, respec-
tively, which are significantly higher than traditional alloys. 8
Nevertheless, the apparent room temperature brittleness and high
density of the two RHEAs remain important challenges for their proc-
essing and practical engineering applications.
To solve the above-mentioned problems, a series of RHEAs or
RMEAs with excellent mechanical properties have been developed in
the past years. However, the mechanical properties of most RHEAs or
RMEAs were investigated using compression tests.
9–12 Only a few ten-
sile properties of RHEAs or RMEAs were reported so far, among
which the equiatomic TiZrHfNbTa RHEA and its non-equiatomic
derivatives have received extensive and in-depth research atten-
tion.
13–17 For example, Wanget al.14 investigated the mechanical prop-
erties of the equiatomic TiZrHfNbTa RHEA at room and cryogenic
temperatures and found that its yield strength and tensile fracture
strain are 875 MPa and 22.8% at 277 K and 1549 MPa and 20.8% at
77 K, respectively, better than that of most RHEAs or RMEAs reported
previously. Unfortunately, it was found in some investigations that
the TiZrHfNbTa system has poor phase stability at intermediate tem-
peratures, which causes the formation of detrimental phases and

21,22 For example,

RHEAs or RMEAs reported. However, the tensile mechanical behavior
and the underlying deformation mechanism of the lightweight

Inspired by the above-mentioned findings, in this work, a light-
weight Ti
40Zr20Nb13.33V26.67 RMEA with ultrahigh specific yield
strengths is designed and prepared based on the following consider-
ations: (1) increasing the content of element V can improve the
strength of an alloy by solid solution strengthening due to its small
atomic radius (r Ti:1 4 1 . 8 p m ,rZr:1 5 5 . 1 p m ,rNb: 142.9 pm, and r V:
135.0 pm);24 (2) increasing the content of Ti can avoid the formation
of intermetallic compounds in the TiZrVNb system;25 (3) the designed
RMEA possesses inherent ductility because its valence electron con-
centration (VEC) is smaller than 4.5.26 Then, the mechanical proper-
ties and deformation mechanisms of the RMEA under tension at both
room and elevated temperatures are studied and discussed.
The as-cast ingot with a nominal composition of Ti40Zr20Nb13.33V26.67
RMEA is prepared with non-consumable vacuum arc melting and a
mixture of high-purity raw materials (better than 99.95 wt. %) under a
Ti-gettered argon atmosphere. The ingot is re-melted and flipped at least
six times to ensure the chemical homogeneity. All samples used for test
and analysis are cut from the internal part of the as-cast ingot without
any subsequent processing. Dog-bone-shaped specimens with a gauge
length of 12 mm and a cross section dimension of 2.5 /C2 1.5 mm2 are
fabricated with wire electric discharge machining for uniaxial tensile
tests. The uniaxial tensile tests at atmosphere temperatures of 298 and
873 K are conducted with a universal testing machine (AG-X plus,
Shimadzu, Japan) at a fixed deformation rate of 0.72 mm/min. Before
testing, the specimen surfaces are mechanically ground and polished
with SiC papers up to 2000 grit. The phase constitution and microstruc-
ture of the ingot are characterized with an x-ray diffractometer (XRD,
Bruker D8, Germany), a field emission scanning electron microscope
(FESEM, Zeiss Gemini 500, Germany) equipped with electron backscat-
ter diffraction (EBSD), and a transmission electron microscopy (TEM,
Tecnai G2 F30, America). The EBSD specimens are mechanically
ground with SiC papers and then electrochemically polished in a solu-
tion of 10% perchloric acid and 90% methanol at /C0 30
/C14 C. For TEM
characterization, the specimens are thinned and polished to less than
100lm with SiC papers and then punched to disks of 3 mm in diameter
and, finally, perforated using an ion-thinning machine (Gatan 691,
America).

elevated temperatures is mainly ductile. In addition, it can be found
that the dimples on the fracture surface of the specimen tested at 873 K
are distinctly larger and deeper than that at 298 K, indicating better
ductility of the former. For polycrystalline metallic materials, the pro-
cess of ductile fracture usually consists mainly of the nucleation,
growth and coalescence of voids, the nucleation and growth of voids
would absorb energy because of the formation of new surface.28,29 On
the other hand, voids also have the function of blunting cracks, leading
to ductile fracture of a material.30,31 The dimples on the fracture surfa-
ces are caused by the voids. The larger and deeper the dimples, the
larger the voids and the more the energy absorbed for the formation of
more new surface. Therefore, with the dimples becoming larger and
deeper, the material would become more ductile. Similar phenomena
and conclusions were also obtained by Zhanget al.32 and Zhao et al.33

Based on the experimental and analytical results, the RMEA
exhibits excellent mechanical properties at 298 and 873 K, which can
mainly be attributed to the following reasons. First, it can be found in
Figs. 4 and 5 that the deformation mechanisms of the RMEA is planar
slip and cross slip, which plays an important role in achieving both
high strength and ductility. Second, as shown in Figs. 4 and 5,n os e c -
ond phase can be found at grain boundaries after deformation at 298
or 873 K, which can avoid intergranular fracture. Third, increasing the
content of element V can further improve the strength of the RMEA
by solid solution strengthening due to its smallest atomic radius. It has
been reported that solid solution strengthening is an effective method
to improve the mechanical properties of most RHEAs or RMEAs.
41,59
Finally, it can be seen in Fig. 5 that a large number of dislocations are
observed between the slip bands after deformation at 873 K, which can
effectively hinder the movement of dislocations and contribute to
strain strengthening.
4,50
In summary, a lightweight Ti 40Zr20Nb13.33V26.67 RMEA with
ultrahigh specific yield strengths and sufficient ductility at both
room and elevated temperatures was developed using vacuum arc
melting. The observation of the deformed microstructure showed
that a large number of slip bands and dislocation bands are the
main deformation product at 298 K, leading to excellent ductility.
In comparison, high dislocation density was found between slip
bands in the sample after deformation at 873 K, which can effec-
tively hinder the motion of dislocations and contribute to strain
strengthening. These findings can not only unravel the tensile
mechanical behavior and the underlying deformation mechanisms
of the RMEA at room and elevated temperatures but also provide
guidance for the design of lightweight high-performance alloys for
advanced engineering applications.
