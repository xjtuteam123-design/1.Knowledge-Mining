Abstract 
Structure and mechanical properties of a new Al0.5CrNbTi2V0.5 high entropy alloy in the as-cast 
and annealed at 1200°C for 24 hours conditions are reported. In the as -cast condition the alloy 
has single body-centered cubic (bcc) phase structure . After annealing relatively fine and 
homogeneously distributed particles of Laves phase form in the bcc matrix. The precipitation of 
the second phase particles is accompanied by an increase in the yield strength and decrease in 
ductility determined in compression at room temperature . With increase of the testing 
temperature to 1000°C, the strength of the alloy decreases and ductility increases. The 
experimental phase composition of the alloy is compared with predicted equilibrium phase s and 
possible ways to tailor the structure of the alloy to increase the mechanical properties further are 
discussed. 
 
Keywords: High entropy alloy; metals and alloys; cast; crystal structure; mechanical properties; 
 
Introduction 
The so-called high-entropy alloys (HEAs ), usually defined as alloys composed of multiple (≥5) 
principle elements with concentrations close to equiatomic (5 -35 at.%), have attracted 
considerable attention in recent years particularly due to their encouraging mechanical properties 
[1, 2]. Earlier efforts in the alloy development were mostly focused on obtaining a single solid 
solution phase structure [ 3]. However, it was suggested that the HEAs for high -performance 
structural applications require strengthening by second phases [ 4, 5]. Based on a Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-
Mn system, s everal precipitation-strengthened HEA s with high mechanical properties were 
developed [6, 7]. 
Another an attractive class of HEAs is the refractory element based alloy s which can be used at 
elevated temperatures [8-14]. A lot of the refractory HEAs contain second phases and 
demonstrate high strength at elevated temperatures; however, most of these alloys have limited 
ductility at ambient temperature [8, 9, 11]. A balanced combination of properties can be achieved 
in those alloys, where the second phase particles can be precipitated in a controlled way in an 
initially single phase structure. Although such possibility has been predicted by CALPHAD 
(CALculation of PHAse Diagrams ) calculations in some alloys [ 11, 13 ], it has never been 
realized experimentally. Here we report structure and properties of a novel precipitation-
strengthened Al0.5CrNbTi2V0.5 high entropy alloy. 
 
Materials and methods 
The alloy with a nominal composition of Al0.5CrNbTi2V0.5 (the subscripts indicate atomic 
fractions) was produced by arc melting of the high -purity (≥99.9 at.%) elements in an argon

6×12×40mm3 was re-melted 5 times. The actual c hemical composition of the alloy is given in 
Table 1. The cast alloy was annealed at 1200°C for 24 hours in vacuum and cooled down on air. 
The s tructure and properties of the Al0.5CrNbTi2V0.5 alloy were studied in the as-cast and 
annealed conditions. Samples for the structural investigations via x -ray diffraction (XRD) 
analysis and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) were prepared by mechanical polishing. The 
XRD analysis was performed using a RIGAKU diffractometer and Cu Kα radiation. SEM 
investigations were carried out on a Quanta 600 FEG microscope equipped with energy-
dispersive (EDS) detector used for chemical composition measurements. The density of the alloy 
was measured via hydrostatic weighting. Compressive tests were per formed rectangular 
specimens with dimensions of 6×4×4mm 3 on an Instron 300LX machine equipped with a radial 
furnace. The initial strain rate was 10 -4s-1 and the temperatures were 22°C, 600°C, 800°C and 
1000°C. For high-temperature testing, only the annealed specimens were us ed. The specimens 
were placed in the preheated furnace and held for 10 minutes before testing. The testing was 
performed until reaching a height reduction of 50%. 
CALPHAD modeling of the equilibrium phase diagram was conducted using Thermo -Calc 
(version 5) software employing TTTI3 (titanium alloys) database. 
 
Results 
The s tructure of the Al0.5CrNbTi2V0.5 alloy is shown in Fig. 1. The XRD data for the as -cast 
condition (Fig. 1a) suggests single body-centered cubic (bcc) phase structure in the alloy that is 
confirmed by the SEM observations (Fig. 1b). Inside the bcc grains with size of ~100 -300µm, 
dendritic segregations are observed; however , deviations from actual composition of the alloy 
are insignificant (Table 1). After annealing at 1200°C for 24 hours, XRD detects the presence of 
a second phase (Fig. 1a) identified as a hexagonal close packed (hcp) C14 Laves phase. The C14 
Laves phase was already observed in an Al-Cr-Nb-Ti-V-Zr HEA system [15]. SEM image (Fig. 
1c) shows that the Laves phase precipitates are mostly located inside the bcc grains as 
homogeneously distributed rectangular particles with an average length and width of 8 and 5µm, 
respectively, and the volume fraction of 5%. Some individual Laves phase particles are found on 
bcc grain boundaries. Note that thin continuous layer of a second phase observed in grain 
boundaries is, most probably, the same Laves phase. Chemical analysis (Table 1) demonstrates 
that the Laves phase particles are composed mostly of Cr, Nb, and Ti. 
The as-cast alloy has the yield strength of 1240 MPa and a pronounced strain hardening capacity, 
which results in a good ductility – the alloy survives 50% reduction without any signs of 
fracture. A nnealing at 1200°C for 24 hours significantly affects the room temperature 
compression behavior of the Al0.5CrNbTi2V0.5 alloy (Fig. 2) ; the yield strength reaches 
1340MPa, but the alloy becomes less ductile and fractures after 18.5 % height reduction. At 
600°C, the annealed alloy demonstrates yield strength of 930 MPa and ductility ~30%. Taking 
into account the measured density of the alloy (5.76g/cm3), the specific strength of the annealed 
alloy is 233 kPam3kg-1 at room temperature and 163 kPam3kg-1 at 600°C. At higher 
temperatures of 800 and 1000°C the alloy softens considerably – the respective values of the 
yield strength are 445MPa and 90MPa. It becomes very ductile and can be compressed to 50% 
reduction without fracture. 
Figure 3 demonstrates the simulated equilibrium phase diagram of the Al0.5CrNbTi2V0.5 alloy. 
The diagram suggests that the alloy solidifies through a single bcc phase; the solidus temperature 
is 1558°C. A Laves phase precipitates at temperatures 1146°C. Its volume fraction increases 
with a decrease in temperature. Finally, at T<700°C, a Ti3Al phase appear. The predictions 
correlate with the experimental findings (like the single bcc structure in the as -solidified 
condition (Fig. 1a and b) ) nicely. The com parison of the simulated phase diagram and the 
structure of the alloy anneal ed at 1200°C suggests that the Laves phase observed after annealing 
(Fig. 1a and c) has precipitated during cooling from the annealing temperature. Nevertheless,

note that the thermodynamic predictions usually do not provide quantitatively accurate results in 
the case of HEAs due to the limitations of the existing databases [11, 13, 15]. 
The presented data shows that the annealing at 1200°C has a pronounced effect on the structure 
and mechanical properties of the as -cast Al0.5CrNbTi2V0.5 alloy. Namely, it precipitat es the 
relatively fine Laves phase particles (Fig. 1c) which are likely to impede the dislocation motion 
in the bcc matrix of the alloy and thus increases stren gth (Fig. 2). However, the alloy becomes 
less ductile, probably, due to the presence of the second phase on the bcc grain boundaries. 
According to simulate phase diagram (Fig. 3) the alloy can be heat treated in the single-phase 
field (homogenization; the annealing temperature and cooling conditions required to obtain the 
single phase structure after cooling are yet to be identified) and then annealed in two -phase field 
(“aging”) to precipitate Laves phase particles. A high ductility of the alloy in the singe phase (as-
cast or homogenized) condition suggests that it can be cold -worked, thus providing additional 
nucleation sites for precipitates. By changing process parameters – cold working strain, 
annealing temperature, duration, cooling condition etc, it seems possibl e to vary the parameters 
of the produced structure, including bcc grain size, fraction, morphology and dimensions of 
Laves precipitates and to modify the mechanical properties of the alloy in a wide range. 
 
Conclusions 
In this work, structure and mechanical properties of the Al0.5CrNbTi2V0.5 high entropy alloy 
were examined. In the initially (in as -cast condition) single bcc phase alloy relatively fine, 
homogeneously distributed C14 Laves phase particles precipitate as a result of a nnealing at 
1200°C for 24 hours . The precipitation of Laves phas e particle increased room temperature 
compression yield strength of the alloy from 1240 MPa to 1340 MPa, but at a cost of a reduced 
ductility. The yield strength of the annealed alloy decrease s to 910, 445 and 90 MPa with an 
increase in testing temperature to 600, 800 and 1000 °C, respectively . Reasonable correlation 
between the experimental phase composition of the alloy and the predicted equilibrium phase 
diagram was established.
