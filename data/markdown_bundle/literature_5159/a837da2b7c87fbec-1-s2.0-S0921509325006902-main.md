ABSTRACT
The improvement of tensile strength at elevated temperatures in light metallic alloys often compromises room 
temperature (RT) ductility. In this study, we focus on Mg-RE alloys to demonstrate a strategy for simultaneously 
achieving excellent high-temperature strength and decent RT ductility. This is accomplished by integrating a 
heterogeneous lamella-structure with dispersed nano-spaced stacking faults. Specifically, the small deformation 
but multipass hot-rolled Mg-8.5Gd-3Y-2Cu-0.5Zr (wt.%) alloys exhibit a distinctive heterogeneous lamella- 
structure. This structure features coarse grains interspersed with fine-grained layers and plate-shaped long- 
period stacking ordered (LPSO) precipitates. Such a microstructure grants the alloy a yield strength of 228 MPa 
at 300 
◦
C, RT ultimate tensile strength of 538 MPa, and RT elongation of 12.2 %. The good combination of ultra- 
high strength and substantial ductility at RT is primarily due to the hetero-deformation induced back stress and 
significant activation of < c + a > dislocations. In addition, the dislocation cellular walls developed during the 
multipass hot-rolling process, can encourage mechanisms such as twinning and stacking faults formation. The 
excellent strength at high temperatures is as a result of the thermally stable LPSO particles which hinder grain- 
coarsening in the fine-grained layers.
1. Introduction
With the increasing attention to the lightweight metals, many 
essential fields have put forward higher requirements for the magnesium 
(Mg) alloys with both good room-temperature (RT) and high- 
temperature mechanical properties. Yet, the rapid diffusion of atoms 
at high temperatures may also cause abnormal growth of strengthening 
phases and grain boundary sliding, thereby compromising the material ’ s 
strength at elevated temperatures [ 1 , 2 ]. Adding rare-earth (RE) solid 
solutions to magnesium alloys is widely acknowledged as a highly 
effective method to improve their mechanical properties at elevated 
temperatures while the tensile strength above 250 
◦
C is still far from 
satisfactory [ 3 ]. Introducing considerable intermetallic precipitates or 
eutectic phase of high melting temperature could further improve the 
heat-resistant properties [ 4 – 6 ]. However, the high density of pre -
cipitates or second phase in Mg alloys can lead to a substantial loss of RT 
ductility, leading to the trade-off between high-temperature strength 
and RT ductility [ 7 ]. For instance, the squeeze-cast Mg-10Gd-3Y-0.5Zr 
(wt.%) alloys exhibit a remarkable ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 
282 MPa at 300 
◦
C but low elongation (EL) to failure of 2.4 % [ 8 ]. There 
is therefore a need to explore new methods that can guide the design of 
Mg alloys with high elevated temperature strength but good RT tensile 
ductility.
Engineering heterogeneous microstructures in metallic materials has 
emerged as an innovative approach to enhance overall mechanical 
properties, offering a potential solution to the strength-ductility trade- 
off [ 9 ]. This approach easily integrates with other strengthening 
mechanisms, offering excellent tunability and flexibility. It has been 
successfully applied to improve the mechanical performance of Mg al -
loys in several aspects [ 10 , 11 ]. Typically, the heterostructure of these 
alloys exhibits multi-grain inhomogeneity, featuring a mix of fine grains 
(FGs) and coarse grains (CGs), leading to display dramatic strength 
difference according to the well-known Hall-Petch relationship [ 12 ]. In 
such cases, hetero-deformation induced (HDI) hardening contribute to 
increased tensile elongation in Mg alloys compared to their solely FG 
counterparts [ 13 ]. For instance, bimodal structured Mg-8Gd-3Y-0.5Zr

nonequilibrium or unstable, i.e., fine grain boundaries (FGBs) possess

For heat-resistant Mg alloys, introducing nano-spaced stacking faults 
(SFs) or LPSO precipitates can significantly improve the

low rare-earth content. These layered stacking structures are renowned 
for their excellent thermal stability, effectively inhibiting grain bound -

nano-spaced stacking structures into the heterostructure of Mg alloys 
could potentially enhance the synergy between high-temperature 
strength and RT ductility [ 19 ]. Previous studies have suggested that 
the synergistic application of thermomechanical processing and heat 
treatment plays a critical role in tailoring the morphology, including the 
reshape of LPSO precipitates [ 20 , 21 ]. For example, severe plastic 
deformation (SPD) and hot rolling have been demonstrated to effec -
tively fragment and refine the coarse, blocky LPSO phases present, 
resulting in a more homogeneous and dispersed distribution [ 22 ]. The 
motivation of the present work is to establish a connection between 
LPSO dispersed heterostructures and the optimal balance of 
high-temperature strength and RT ductility.
In this study, we take Mg-8.5Gd-3Y-2Cu-0.5Zr (wt.%) extruded alloy 
as an example, and investigate the effect of LPSO dispersed hetero -
structures on the mechanial performance at both RT and high temper -
atures. With help of multipass hot-rolling and double-ageing treatment, 
we plan to fabricate lamellar heterostructure with fragmented LPSO 
phases uniformly dispersed within the fine-grained regions. Then, the 
mechanical performance at different temperatures are tested, and the 
corresponding microstructure evolution is revealed. The outline of the 
paper is as follows. We describe in Section 2 the experimental meth -
odology. Section 3 presents the mechanical properties, along with a 
detailed microstructure characterization. Section 4 discussion provides 
the mechanisms underlying the exceptional mechanical properties, 
especially the coupling between lamellated heterostructure and thin 
plate-shaped LPSO phases.
2. Methodology
The Mg-8.5Gd-3Y-2Cu-0.5Zr (wt.%) alloy ingot, measuring 90 mm in 
diameter and 1200 mm in length, was successfully produced through 
semi-continuous casting. The ingot was subjected to a homogenization 
treatment at 500 
◦
C for 12 h and then extruded at 375 
◦
C. The extrusion 
process was performed at a ratio of 28:1 and a rate of 0.6 mm/s, followed 
immediately by air quenching to room temperature. This produced cy -
lindrical extruded rods with a diameter of 18 mm and a length of 1000 
mm. Plates 5 mm thick were subsequently cut from these rods along the 
extrusion direction. Before undergoing multi-pass hot rolling, these 
plates were pre-heated to 200 
◦
C for 30 min in a resistance furnace, with 
the mill rolls heated to the same temperature. The rolling involved 
multiple passes, reducing plate thickness by 2 % – 5 % each time, 
accompanied by a 90
◦
in-plane rotation after each pass, resulting in 4 
mm thick sheets. After each pass, to maintain the recrystallization 
temperature and activate the PSN mechanism [ 23 ], the samples were 
reheated to 200 
◦
C for 5 min and cooled in room-temperature water after 
rolling was completed. This unique multi-pass rolling (MR) process was 
designed to ensure mostly equiaxed grains within the lamellar hetero -
structure. The 4 mm thick sheets were then subjected to a double-ageing 
treatment, achieving peak-aged conditions at 100 
◦
C for 8 h and 200 
◦
C 
for 36 h. The final peak-aged samples are referred to as MR + A samples.
Tensile specimens with a gauge length of 17 mm and a cross-section 
of 3.5 × 2 mm
2
, were cut from the sheets with their tensile axes aligned 
parallel to the rolling direction. The tensile deformation tests were 
carried out at both room temperature and 300 
◦
C on an Instron 5569 
testing machine, with a strain rate of 1 mm/min. For tests at 300 
◦
C, the 
samples were pre-incubated for 15 min. The microstructures of the 
samples were examined using an Olympus BX60M optical microscope 
(OM), Oxford Symmetry S2 Electron Backscatter Diffraction (EBSD) 
detectors, and a JEOL JEM-F200 FE-TEM. The EBSD maps were acquired 
using a step size of 0.12 μ m. The analysis of EBSD and TEM data was 
carried out using AZtec software, Channel 5.0 software, and Digital 
Micrograph software, respectively.

We started by comparing the mechanical performance of the Mg- 
8.5Gd-3Y-2Cu-0.5Zr (wt.%) alloy with and without undergone the

4. Discussion
It is commonly accepted that there exits a trade-off between high 
temperature strength and RT ductility for Mg alloys [ 8 , 26 , 27 , 30 ]. Yes, as 
mentioned above that the lamellated heterostructure here endows the 
Mg-8.5Gd-3Y-2Cu-0.5Zr (wt.%) alloys with a good combination of 
remarkable RT and elevated temperature mechanical properties. 
Unraveling the specific deformation mechanisms responsible for the 
alloy ’ s superior mechanical performance is essential. Furthermore, 
previous studies have suggested that the high strength of metallic alloys 
at high temperature is closely linked to the robust stability of micro -
structure at elevated temperatures [ 38 ]. In the following, we will first 
investigate the deformation mechanisms of the present alloy. We then 
analyze the microstructure evolution of the alloy subjected to thermal 
exposure, aiming to shed light on the stability of its microstructure at 
high temperatures.
4.1. Deformation mechanisms at high stress level
The remarkable improvement of strength for the MR + A samples is 
first studied. The microstructures depicted in Figs. 2 and 4 reveal that 
the alloy has a fine grain size of only several micrometers and a signif -
icant number of SFs within the grains. These features are crucial for 
strengthening the alloy, as supported by the Hall-Petch relationship 
[ 39 ]. Additionally, the nano-scale dynamically precipitated LPSO pha -
ses effectively pin the grain boundaries, contributing to dispersion 
strengthening. Furthermore, the high solid solubility of Gd/Y atoms in 
the α -Mg matrix plays a significant role in enhancing the alloy ’ s strength 
through solution strengthening. Besides these, the bimodal microstruc -
ture endows the MR + A samples with additional HDI-stress strength -
ening or hardening. The HDI-stress ( σ

the average of σ u and σ r, where σ u is the yield stress upon unloading and 
σ r is the yield stress upon reloading at the unloading – reloading loop.

were generated and entangled upon tensile loading. The corresponding 
high resolution electron microscopy image (HRTEM) confirms a high 
density of dislocations in the walls or cellular structure boundaries 
( Fig. 7 (b)). Upon further magnification, numerous sub-cellar walls were 
observed( Fig. 7 (c)). Also, the cellular walls are found to trap disloca -
tions, and it should give rise to additional strain harderning. Morever, 
the onset of considerable stacking faults (SFs) and deformation twins is 
observed and penetrate through the cellular walls, as shown in Fig. 7 (d) 
and (e). Deformation twins traversing the cellular walls subdivides these 
cellular structures, facilitating interactions between dislocations and 
twins/stacking faults as well as dislocations and cellular walls. This 
process provides a progressive work-hardening mechanism. In addition, 
the synergistic activation and interaction of multiple deformation 
mechanisms, including dislocations, SFs, and deformation twins, should 
also play a critical role [ 43 ]. Specifically, GNDs at heterogeneous in -
terfaces facilitate strain coordination between FG and CG layers, while 
the activation of < c + a > dislocations, along with the interactions among 
SFs, twins, and dislocation cells, collectively contribute to the coordi -
nated enhancement of strength and ductility.

contributes to slowing down the reduction rate of strength in Mg alloys 
during heating, thereby achieving improved high-temperature me -

Before closing, we further explored the mechanisms contributing to 
the exceptional high-temperature strength of the MR + A alloys. Firstly, 
the presence of finely recrystallized (DRXed) grains significantly en -
hances yield strength through refinement strengthening, as explained by 
the Hall-Petch equation. It is important to note, however, that 
nonequilibrium DRXed grain boundaries are highly energetic, which 
makes them particularly active in movement at elevated temperatures. 
Consequently, GB motion becomes a prominent deformation mode in 
ultrafine-grained materials at high temperatures. Previous studies have 
highlighted that dispersed precipitates at grain boundaries can reduce 
grain boundary energy, thereby inhibiting intergranular deformation 
[ 45 ]. In the case of MR + A alloys, the plane-shaped LPSO precipitates 
exhibit lower interfacial curvature and energy, which translates to better 
thermal stability at high temperatures, effectively restraining the 
coarsening of dispersed LPSO phases. These dispersed LPSO phases act 
as stable pinning points, significantly inhibiting the movement of 
nanocrystalline grain boundaries within the fine grain layers when 
tensile force is applied along the rolling direction. Additionally, a 
notable quantity of SFs is present within the coarse grains (as seen in 
Fig. 4 ). Previous studies have demonstrated that grains containing SFs 
can stabilize intragranular deformation and resist lattice rotation [ 18 ,
46 ]. During deformation, the interaction between mobile dislocations 
and SFs also play a role in high-temperature strengthening. Based on the 
points discussed above, we conclude that the fine microstructure and SFs 
in grain interiors, along with the abundance of LPSO particles at fine 
grain boundaries, are responsible for the exceptional high-temperature 
strength of the MR + A alloy.

5. Conclusion
In summary, we have fabricated a heat-resistant Mg-8.5Gd-3Y-2Cu- 
0.5Zr (wt.%) alloy with excellent elevated-temperature strength yet 
decent RT ductility. The alloy demonstrates a RT tensile elongation of 
12.2 %, coupled with an ultimate tensile strength of approximately 538 
MPa at 25 
◦
C and a yield strength around 228 MPa at 300 
◦
C. The 
comprehensive mechanical property is significantly higher than most 
Mg-Gd-Y-based wrought alloys. Microstructural analysis reveals that the 
alloy exhibits a heterogeneous lamellar microstructure, characterized by 
alternating layers of submicron-sized fine grains and larger coarse 
equiaxed grains at the micron scale. Dispersed among these fine-grain 
layers is a significant proportion of fragmented LPSO phases. Such 
unique microsturcture was generated during the DRX process that is 
triggered by our multipass hot-rolling with small deformation. The 
microstructure provides considerable refinement strengthening, pre -
cipitation strength and hetero-deformation induced hardening, response 
for the high strength of our alloys. Furthermore, the respectable ductility 
is facilitated by collective effects of hierarchically heterogeneous mi -
crostructures, including the activation of various non-basal dislocation 
modes, subsequently followed by the formation of deformation twins 
and stacking faults. We believe the strategy holds great potential for 
application in the development of heat-resistant Mg alloys in general.
