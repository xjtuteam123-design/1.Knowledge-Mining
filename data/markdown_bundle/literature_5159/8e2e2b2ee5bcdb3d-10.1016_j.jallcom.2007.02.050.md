Abstract
Materials may be strong or ductile, but rarely both at once. However, the combination of high strength and good ductility is signiﬁcant for
application of materials as structural materials. In this work, we report a Ni–Nb alloy, which exhibits ultrahigh compressive strength of up to
3500 MPa together with good compressive ductility of 5.3%. To the best of our knowledge, the plastic strain is larger than previous values reported
for any bulk ultrahigh strength (over 3000 MPa) alloys. The good ductility is due to the heterogeneous microstructure of the alloy.

1. Introduction
Bulk glassy alloys (BGAs) usually exhibit higher strength
than their crystalline counterparts [1,2]. Many BGAs with high
strength up to 2000 MPa have been successfully fabricated in Cu-
based [3–5], Ni-based [6–13], Fe-based [14–17] and Co-based
[18,19] alloy systems. Especially, several Ni-based[10–13], Fe-
based [15–17] and Co-based [18,19] BGAs exhibit ultrahigh
compressive strength over 3000 MPa. Nevertheless, the plastic
strain of BGAs is usually less than 1% at room temperature,
which limits the application of BGAs as structural materials.
Recently, great efforts have been devoted to circumvent the
limited ductility of BGAs [20–27]. However, ductile BGAs
or BGA composites with compressive strength over 3000 MPa
were rarely reported [9,10]. Very recently, parallel to our work,
Zhu et al. reported the fabrication of binary Ni–Nb bulk metallic
glass with high strength of 3.4 GPa and compressive ductil-
ity of about 2% [28]. In this paper, we report a Ni–Nb binary
BGA composite, which exhibits ultrahigh compressive strength

2. Experimental
Binary alloy ingots with nominal composition of Ni 60.25Nb39.75 were pre-
pared by arc melting the mixtures of Ni (99.98 wt.%) and Nb (99.7 wt.%)
elements in Zr-gettered high purity argon atmosphere. Cylindrical alloy rods of
1 mm in diameter were prepared by injecting alloy melt into copper mold. Struc-
tures of the samples were examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD, using a Thermo
ARL X’Tra diffractometer with Cu K /H9251radiation at 45 kV) and transmission
electron microscope (TEM, JEM-2010). The TEM specimens were prepared
by ion milling with liquid nitrogen cooling. Glass transition and crystalliza-
tion behavior were studied using a NETZSCH DSC 404 C differential scanning
calorimeter under a continuous argon ﬂow at a heating rate of 0.33 K/s. Com-
pression tests were carried out on six cylindrical rods (1 mm (diameter)× 2m m
(height)) using a universal testing machine (CMT5205 SANS, China) at a strain
rate of 4 × 10
−4 s−1. The morphology of the side surface of fractured sam-
ples was observed by ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM,
SIRION-100).

crystallites are formed, which were not detected by XRD due to
(most likely) low volume fraction of the crystalline component.
In general, it is assumed that nanometer-sized heterogeneities
in BGAs act as nucleation sites for the multiplication of shear
bands [24–27]. We believe that the nanometer-sized crystallites
observed in the present Ni
60.25Nb39.75 alloy could interact with
shear bands to branch and hinder the movement of shear bands.
Consequently, large plasticity during compression is detected.
More investigation is required to understand how the shear bands
interact with nanometer-sized crystallites.
In addition, the sudden decrease of the stress was observed
after 5.5% total strain. The declining serrated stress–strain curve
after 5.5% may be resulted from the thin shear band thickening,
which causes strain softening [29,30].
4. Conclusions
In summary, a Ni
60.25Nb39.75 alloy, which exhibits ultrahigh
compressive strength of up to 3500 MPa together with good
compressive ductility of 5.3%, was synthesized. To the best of
our knowledge, the plastic strain is larger than previous values
reported for any bulk ultrahigh strength (over 3000 MPa) alloys.
The good ductility is resulted from existence of nanometer-sized
crystallites randomly distributed in glassy matrix. This ultrahigh
strength Ni–Nb alloy combined with good ductility is promising
for application as a high strength material. The result obtained
here indicates that nanocrystallization might be a promising way
to enhance the ductility of ultrahigh strength BGAs.
