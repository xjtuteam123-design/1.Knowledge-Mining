ABSTRACT
Mo/ZrO2–Y2O3 composites were successfully prepared through high-energy ball milling and powder metallurgy
technique. The morphologies of the composite powders, the microstructures and properties of the sintered
compacts and rotary swaged compacts were studied. The results show that ZrO 2 addition re ﬁned the grains of
composite powders and Mo alloys by promoting grains nucleation and inhibiting grains growth. Tetragonal
zirconia (t-ZrO
2) was completely stabilized at room temperatures by Y 2O3 and evenly distributed in the mo-
lybdenum matrix. Moreover, ZrO 2 was bonded with Mo matrix by an interfacial bonding zone. All above mi-
crostructures improve the mechanical properties and recrystallization temperature of the composites. The added
values of yield strength, tensile strength and elongation of the doped Mo alloys after annealing all exceed 30% at
room temperature, compared with pure Mo. ZrO
2 particles make an e ﬀect on dispersion strengthening and
improving recrystallization temperature by hindering dislocations movement.
1. Introduction
Molybdenum (Mo) and its alloys have long been the high tem-
perature structural materials of choice in metallurgy, machinery, elec-
tronics, aerospace and other ﬁelds, providing high melting point, low
thermal expansion coe ﬃcient, good electrical conductivity and ex-
cellent high-temperature mechanical properties [ 1–3]. However, the
vulnerabilities of molybdenum, such as low temperature brittleness and
recrystallization brittleness, restrict its applications [ 4–6]. The
strengthening-toughening of Mo was, is, and remains to be a vital topic
in the research of Mo alloys.
The strength and ductility of Mo alloys can only be simultaneously
improved through grain re ﬁnement. Ivanov [ 7] investigated the char-
acteristic features and thermal stability of pure molybdenum processed
by severe plastic deformation, and showed that high pressure torsion
dramatically increases strength-ductility and recrystallization tem-
perature of pure molybdenum. However, the improvements in strength-
ductility obtained through grain re ﬁnement (down to 0.2 μm) will ra-
pidly deteriorate at high temperature, especially above the re-
crystallization temperature [ 8]. Alloying elements, carbides and oxides
were generally introduced into pure Mo to develop TZM, TZC, Mo/
La
2O3 alloys [ 9–12] etc. The second phase particles in Mo matrix can
reﬁne grains and hinder the grain growth, thus enhancing strength
greatly and improving ductility appropriately. Liu et al. [ 13,14]
developed nanosized La 2O3 doped Mo alloys prepared by molecular-
level liquid-liquid mixing/doping technique with yield strength over
800 MPa and tensile elongation as large as 40% at room temperature.
Recently, the addition of ZrO
2 particles as ideal reinforcing phase has
attracted increasing attention due to their high hardness and high
melting point. PLANSEE [ 15] developed MoZrO
2 alloys by adding
1.7 wt% ZrO 2 to Mo matrix. The MoZrO 2 alloys have better corrosion
resistance against glass melts, greater stability at high temperatures and
better creep resistance than pure molybdenum. Cui et al. [ 16] re-
searched on the properties of Mo/ZrO 2 composites prepared via the
hydrothermal method. The tensile strength and yield strength increased
by 32.33% and 53.76% compared with those of the pure Mo. However,
ZrO
2 distributed in the Mo matrix is prone to crystal transformation
between the monoclinic zirconia (m-ZrO 2) and tetragonal zirconia (t-
ZrO2) as operating temperature changes. These transitions will reduce
the ductility of Mo/ZrO 2 alloys. In this study, the stabilizing agent yttria
(Y2O3) was introduced in Mo/ZrO 2 alloys. Mo/ZrO 2–Y2O3 composites
were fabricated by the high-energy ball milling and powder metallurgy
technique. And the e ﬀect of ZrO
2 particles on the morphologies of
composite powders, the microstructures and properties of compacts
were also investigated.

2. Material and experimental
2.1. Material and processing
Composites with ZrO 2 volume fractions of 0, 3, and 5 vol% and
corresponding Y 2O3 volume fractions of 0, 0.2, and 0.35 vol%, respec-
tively, were prepared to investigate the e ﬀect of ZrO 2 on the properties
of Mo alloys ( Table 1). The AR-grade raw materials used for the pre-
paration of Mo/ZrO 2–Y2O3 are as follows: (NH 4)2Mo4O13·2H2O, Zr
(NO3)4·3H2O and Y(NO 3)3·6H2O.
The preparation of Mo/ZrO 2–Y2O3 composites is illustrated in
Fig. 1. Ammonium tetramolybdate [(NH4) 2Mo4O13·2H2O] was heated
at 520 °C for 4 h in a mu ﬄe furnace and sequentially treated by dry
milling for 8 h, to convert (NH4) 2Mo4O13·2H2O into MoO 3. Meanwhile,
a certain amount of Zr(NO 3)4·3H2O and Y(NO 3)3·6H2O were put into a
beaker. Then, distilled water and ethanol were added to the materials at
a volume ratio of approximately 2:1. The mixture was then heated in a
water bath at 80 °C and stirred using an electric mixer until the solids
were completely dissolved. The prepared solution was mixed evenly
with MoO 3 powders through wet milling for 8 h. MoO 3 composite
powders were obtained after drying. The composite powders were re-
duced in hydrogen atmosphere at 520 °C for 4 h to convert MoO
3 into
MoO2. Then, the Mo/ZrO 2–Y2O3 composite powders were obtained by
reducing MoO 2 to Mo at 960 °C for 5 h.
The composite powders were ﬁlled into a rubber can and pressed by
cold isostatic pressing (CIP) at 280 MPa for 20 min, and then sintered in
an intermediate frequency induction sintering furnace under H 2 atmo-
sphere at 1920 °C for 3 h. The cylindrical sintered compacts with di-
mensions of Φ20 mm × 60 mm were obtained.
The cylindrical sintered compacts were heated for 20 min at 1350 °C
in a molybdenum wire furnace (ZMY-50-15) and sequentially subjected
to multistep rotary swaging, causing total deformation to exceed 84%.
The cylindrical rotary swaging compacts with a diameter of Φ8m m
were obtained after surface polishing.

particles provide higher speci ﬁc surface area, which will promote the
grain nucleation. Thus, the particles are ﬁner and more homogeneous
with the increase of ZrO 2.

4. Conclusions
(1) Mo/ZrO
2–Y2O3 composites were successfully developed by a com-
bination of solid- liquid doping method, high energy ball milling
and powder metallurgy.
(2) ZrO 2 particles act as a foundation for nucleation, and as hindrances
to grain growth and dislocations moving. ZrO 2 particles thus ef-
fectively re ﬁne the grains of composite powders and Mo alloys.
(3) In Mo/ZrO 2–Y2O3 composites, t-ZrO 2 is stabilized at room tem-
peratures by Y 2O3. Moreover, ZrO 2 was bonded with Mo matrix by
an interfacial bonding zone. The atomic arrangement of interfacial
bonding zone is similar to the structure of t-ZrO 2, but it is dis-
ordered. There existed a crystallographic relationship between t-
ZrO
2 and Mo matrix. The crystallographic orientations at the in-
terface can be illustrated as t-ZrO 2[010]//Mo[111] and t-
ZrO2(002)//Mo(011).
(4) The room temperature tension testing results show that Mo/
ZrO2–Y2O3 composites exhibit excellent strength-elongation com-
bination and high recrystallization temperature. ZrO 2 particles
make e ﬀect on dispersion strengthening and recrystallization tem-
perature improving by hindering dislocations movement.
