Selective Laser Melted Ti-6Al-4V Alloy after Post-Heat
Treatments: Microstructure, Mechanical Properties
and Fatigue Behavior
Minghui Wang, Hua Yan , Qinghua Lu, Peilei Zhang, and Kaiwei Liu
Submitted: 31 March 2023 / Revised: 18 February 2024 / Accepted: 20 March 2024 / Published online: 6 May 2024
In the present work, an investigation is conducted into the effects of heat treatment (700, 800, 900 and 1000/C176C) on
the microstructure, mechanical properties and fatigue behavior of Ti-6Al-4V alloy fabricated by selective laser
melting (SLM). According to the results of microstructure analysis, compared with the Ti-6Al-4Valloy under the
initial SLM condition, the sample shows a completely different microstructure after heat treatment. With the
increase in heat treatment temperature, the dominant aciculara’ martensite in Ti-6Al-4V alloy is decomposed
into ﬁne (a + b) lamellae, and the microstructure is signiﬁcantly coarsened. When the heat treatment temper-
ature exceeds 800 /C176C, the heat treatment sample develops a ﬁner (a + b) dual-phase matrix microstructure. As
revealed by the test of mechanical properties, the samples heat treatment at 900/C176C exhibits higher yield strength,
higher tensile strength, ﬁner microstructure and greater elongation at break. T o be speciﬁc, the tensile strength,
yield strength and elongation are 921 Mpa, 819 MPa and 16.1%, respectively. According to the results of high
cycle fatigue test, the fatigue performance of SLM Ti-6Al-4Valloy is signiﬁcantly improved by conducting stress
relief heat treatment at 900 /C176Cf o r1 0

1. Introduction
As a duplex titanium alloy with a + b two-phase
microstructure at high temperatures, Ti-6Al-4V alloy is widely
used in aerospace, military, biomedical and other industries
because of its excellent physical properties such as lightweight,
high strength and strong biocompatibility (Ref 1-3). It is also
used in various maritime activities and the chemical industry
due to its strong resistance to corrosion (Ref 4). However, it
performs poorly in machinability, which hinders it from being
machined into more complex parts, thus limiting its applica-
bility in practice. Selective laser melting (SLM) refers to an
additive manufacturing technology applied to selectively melt
metal powders and build parts layer-by-layer with high-
dimensional accuracy. It enables the production of complex
or customized parts, which removes the need for expensive
machining (Ref 5-10). Typically, the molten pool has a faster
cooling rate during the SLM forming process, as a result of
which the microstructure of the formed titanium alloy is mostly
metastable martensite (Ref 11, 12). This makes SLM-processed
titanium alloys ideal for producing various high-performance
parts such as impellers and blades (Ref 13, 14).
Despite the advantages of SLM, there remain metallurgical
differences between the SLM-fabricated parts and those
fabricated through conventional means, such as mechanical
anisotropy, residual stresses and additive manufacturing-speci-
ﬁc defects (Ref 15, 16). These distinctions impose demanding
requirements on core aerospace applications, particularly when
the use of high-temperature fatigue components is required,
such as the fuel injectors and other complex components
subjected to multi-directional loading (Ref 17). Therefore, post-
heat treatment is crucial to improving the ductility of selected
zone laser melted Ti-6Al-4V alloys and to reducing their
anisotropy (Ref 18-20).
During the SLM process, residual stress reaches a high level
in the part. Therefore, heat treatment is a key step in achieving
stress relief during the production based on SLM technology
(Ref 21-23). Metal heat treatment is a process through which
the performance of metal workpiece is manipulated by heating,
holding and cooling in a certain medium and by altering the
surface or internal structure of metals. Many studies on the
optimization of heat treatment of Ti-6Al-4V parts fabricated by
SLM, with large differences in part performance (Ref 24).
Abolfazl Azarniya et al. (Ref 25) suggested that the elongation
of Ti is related to the heat treatment temperature and that it
increases signiﬁcantly after the part is heat treatment at 843 /C176 C
for 2 h. According to the study of Vrencken et al. (Ref 26), the
elongation of Ti-6Al-4V fabricated by SLM under the same
conditions is 7.3 ± 1.1%, while that of Ti-6Al-4V + 10Mo is
20.1 ± 2.0%. As revealed by the study of Emanuelli et al. (Ref
27), the elongation is merely 11.5% after the heat treatment
conducted at 800 /C176 C, while the rise in temperature to 850 /C176 C
results in a signiﬁcant increase in elongation. Leuders et al.

The microstructure, tension and fatigue fracture morphology

2.4 Heat Treatment
In this paper, their effects on the microstructure and
properties of Ti-6Al-4V alloy were studied at four different
heat treatment (HT) temperatures (700, 800, 900 and 1000 /C176 C).
According to the relevant literature, annealing refers to the heat
treatment of Ti-6Al-4V alloy in the range of 700 /C176 Ct o b-
transition temperature, so the minimum heat treatment temper-
ature of 700 /C176 C is set in this paper (Ref 32). The recrystalliza-
tion temperature of Ti-6Al-4V alloy is 750 /C176 C, and the
recrystallization annealing temperature is generally selected
above the recrystallization temperature, so the heat treatment
temperature of 800 /C176 C is set (Ref 33, 34). Then, the heat
treatment temperature of 900 /C176 C was selected to help stabilize
the b-phase to the greatest extent (Ref 32). Finally, because the
b-transus transition temperature is 995 /C176 C, the 1000 /C176 C heat
treatment temperature is selected (Ref 35, 36). In order to
prevent surface oxidation, the samples were heat treatment in a
WHQ-200 vacuum high-pressure gas quenching furnace. In
order to ensure that the alloy is completely transformed and
does not cause a waste of energy consumption, the holding time
is set to 2 hours and then cooled to 200 /C176 C. The heating rate
was 15 /C176 C/min, the cooling rate was about 3 /C176 C/min and the
vacuum in the vacuum furnace was maintained at 0.0001 bar.
The samples treated at different holding temperatures were
denoted as HT700, HT800, HT900 and HT1000, respectively.

3.1 Phase Identiﬁcation
Figure 4(a) shows the XRD patterns of the heat treatment

3.4 Tensile Test
The results of tensile test are detailed in Table 4, and the
stress–strain curves are presented in Fig. 9. By observing the
properties of the sample throughout the tensile phase, it can be
discovered that elastic modulus remained unchanged before
and after heat treatment, which is around 120 GPa. Notably,
there are noticeable variations found in yield strength, ultimate
tensile strength and elongation after different heat treatments.
Figure 10 shows the ultimate tensile strength and elongation
characteristics of Ti-6Al-4V as examined after various heat
treatments. The as-built sample exhibits the highest UTS and
yield strength, but the lowest total elongation and overall trend
of plastic deformation. The HT700 sample shows similar
tensile behaviors to the as-built sample, indicating a less
signiﬁcant change in microstructure. However, a slightly higher
ductility is exhibited due to reduced residual stress. Given the
heat treatment at 900 /C176 C, the lamellar microstructure of a + b
shows an increase in elongation, but a decline in yield stress
and tensile strength. When the heat treatment temperature
reaches 1000 /C176 C, the development of plate-like microstructure
leads to a signiﬁcant deterioration of performance in plastic
deformation.
The best ductility behaviors can be observed in the sample
annealed at 900 /C176 C, and the largest dimples take shape. The
reason why the as-built sample and that heat treatment at
700 /C176 C show high tensile strength and low ductility lies in the
more complete martensitic a¢ structure relative to the a + b of
the annealed sample. The a/a¢ with hexagonal close-packed
(HCP) crystal structure has fewer slip systems than the b-phase
of BCC. In addition, the higher V content in a¢ enhances the
solid solution strengthening effect while hindering the move-
ment of dislocations, which requires a larger driving force for
dislocation. When fracture occurs, the sample accumulates lots
of dislocations, which initiates cracking and breaking given less
severe deformation. The heat treatment sample at 900 /C176 C
exhibits the highest proportion of b-phase and an appropriate
amount of layered a-phase, resulting in optimal ductility and
ultimate tensile stress without being excessively low. Accord-

4. Conclusions
In this study, an investigation is conducted into the
microstructure evolution, mechanical properties and fatigue
behavior of SLM Ti-6Al-4V alloy after different heat treat-
ments. Then, the effects of heat treatment temperature on
microstructure, phase composition, hardness, tensile and
fatigue properties were analyzed. Finally, the following con-
clusions are drawn.
(1) The microstructure of Ti-6Al-4V sample fabricated by
SLM is comprised of acicular a¢ martensite. With the in-
crease in heat treatment temperature, a¢ martensite is
decomposed into stable a + b-phase.
(2) The mechanical properties of Ti-6Al-4V sample fabri-
cated by SLM can be improved by heat treatment. With
the increase in heat treatment temperature from 700 to
1000 /C176 C, the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength
decline from 1022 to 774 MPa and from 1133 to
882 MPa, respectively. The elongation increases ﬁrst
and then decreases, reaching a maximum of 16.1%
(900 /C176 C). Comparatively, the elongation increases by
67.7%.
(3) The fatigue behavior of Ti-6Al-4V alloy fabricated by
SLM is closely related to its microstructure. Defects
have a great impact on the fatigue behavior of the alloy.
The optimal microstructure ( a + b phase) can extend the
crack initiation time, and the fatigue properties of the
material can be improved by setting an appropriate heat
treatment temperature (900 /C176 C).
