1. Introduction
Metals and alloys are necessary materials for fabrication and load- 
bearing applications. For structural materials, strength and ductility at 
room temperature (RT) are two essential mechanical properties [1] . 
However, conventional strengthening methods such as fine grain 
strengthening, precipitation strengthening, solid solution strengthening, 
and dislocation strengthening tend to increase strength at the expense of 
ductility and toughness [2 – 4] . Unlike traditional alloys which are 
composed of one major element and other alloying elements, high en -
tropy alloys (HEAs) consist of multiple components, with each element ’ s 
atomic ratio ranging from 5 at% to 35 at%, provide a novel way to solve 
this problem [5,6] .
HEAs usually possess face-center-cubic (FCC), body-center-cubic 
(BCC), and/or close-rowed-hexagonal (HCP) crystal structures [7] . 
Single-phase FCC HEAs have excellent formability and plasticity at RT, 
although they exhibit low strength [5,8] . FCC HEAs prepared by 
combining heterogeneous structures (HS) and precipitated phases show 
an superior strength-plasticity synergy. Heterogeneous alloys generally 
include alternating soft and hard regions, which acts as a heterogeneous 
deformation-induced (HDI) strengthening during deformation [9] . In 
terms of the morphology, HS is mainly classified into the types of 
lamellar heterostructures, multi-scale heterostructures, gradient heter -
ostructures, etc [10 – 12] . For example, Fan et al. [13] prepared

Numerous studies have shown that SFE is one of the key factors 
influencing the deformation mechanisms of FCC alloys [13,79] . When 
SFE is at a low level ( < 20 mJ/m
2

(TRIP) is dominant. The intermediate SFE (20 ~ 45 mJ/m

20 – 45 mJ/m
2
, within which partial dislocations separation with SF 
bonds are thermodynamically preferred, resulting in easy activation of 
SF under plastic deformation [53] . For FCC alloys with low SFEs, a 
1/2 < 110 > perfect dislocation readily decompose into a pair of 
1/6 < 112 > Shockley partial dislocations bonded to SF during plastic 
deformation. However, dislocation cross-slip and microbands are the 
preferred deformation modes for materials with high SFEs. Therefore, 
the SF-dominated deformation in the Al5Si5 alloy originates from its low 
SFE. This leads to a high tendency for dislocations to dissociate into SFs 
and thus hinder the cross-slip process. In addition, the formation of 
cross-SFs (L-C locks) as shown in Fig. 7 (a) further limits the movement of 
dislocations, resulting in a significant enhancement of the 
work-hardening capacity [80] . Thus, the Al5Si5 – 800 HEA exhibits high 
strength.
Combined with previous studies, the following reasonable assump -
tions are obtained for the coordinated plastic deformation process of 
Al5Si5 – 800 alloy [81,82] . When uniaxial tensile stress is applied to the 
alloy, a high stress concentration will be generated at the interface be -
tween the FCC phase (soft phase) and the ordered B2 phase (hard phase). 
Dislocations preferentially initiate in the soft phase with lower lattice 
stress, resulting in plastic deformation of the soft phase first [83] . 
However, due to the high strength and elastic modulus of the B2 phase, 
the B2 phase is still in the elastic deformation stage [84] . Thus the dis -
locations will pile up at the FCC / B2 phase interface and cause stress 
concentration in some specific planes and crystal directions of the B2 
phase, represented by < 111 > {110} slip system [85] . Furthermore, 
when the external tensile stress and interfacial stress concentration 
reach a certain level, the slip system of the ordered B2 phase is active. 
When dislocations accumulate at the phase boundary, the dislocation 
energy generated by dislocations in the FCC phase is higher than the 
energy per unit dislocation length of the alloy. In order to reduce the 
overall energy, the partial piled dislocations near the FCC / B2 interface 
in the FCC phase can be transferred to the B2 phase [32,86] . Thus 
forming the overall coordinated deformation of the soft and hard regions 
of the heterogeneous Al5Si5 – 800 alloy and obtaining a higher elonga -
tion. In addition, the yield strength of the B2 precipitation phase is 
higher than that of the matrix FCC phase, playing the role of the sec -
ondary phase strengthening. On the one hand, B2 phase blocks the 
movement of dislocations and improves the strength of the alloy. On the 
other hand, as a new dislocation source, it promotes dislocation prop -
agation and ensures good plasticity.
