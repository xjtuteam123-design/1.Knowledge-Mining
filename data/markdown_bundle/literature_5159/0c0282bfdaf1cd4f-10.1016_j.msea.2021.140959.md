1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys, so-called multi-principal-component alloys, 
were first proposed by Yeh et al. [ 1 ] and Contor et al. [ 2 ] in 2004. High 
entropy alloys were originally defined as consisting of five or more el -
ements in an equimolar ratio [ 3 ]. Compared with traditional alloys, high 
entropy alloys display four core effects: high entropy effect, sluggish 
diffusion, severe lattice distortion and cocktail effects [ 4 ], which bring 
about interesting characteristics in alloy properties, such as high 
strength [ 5 – 8 ] and hardness [ 9 , 10 ], good resistance to abrasion [ 11 ] 
and corrosion [ 12 – 14 ], and excellent electrical and thermal conductiv -
ity [ 15 , 16 ], etc. In particular, the promising mechanical properties of 
high entropy alloys have attracted widespread attention in the last 
decade. 
The mechanical properties of high entropy alloys can be improved by 
changing the content of principal elements. In a previous study of high 
entropy alloy [ 17 ], Jin et al. proposed a unique insight that broke the 
traditional VEC standard and judged the alloy structure and phase with 
the distribution ratio of Cr element. That is, the Cr content promoted the 
formation of FCC phase and BCC phase at low concentration and high 
concentration, respectively, so the strength and ductility and Cr content 
did not follow a monotonous change. With the increase of Cr content, 
the ultimate strength of AlCoCr

Various strengthening mechanisms have been identified in high en -
tropy alloys [ 35 , 36 ]. From them can be found that the factors that 
prevent the dislocation slipping can enhance the tensile strength of the 
samples. 
Solid solution strengthening is a commonly strengthening mecha -
nism for metal materials. The use of solvent atoms to dissolve in the 
solute causes lattice distortion, which produces a certain lattice stress 
field and increases the resistance to dislocation movement. Although 
there is no distinction between solutes and solvents in high entropy al -
loys, it is possible to increase the content of some major elements in the 
system to study their effects on alloy structure and properties. Lattice 
distortion occurs when Nb atoms dissolve in the matrix to form a solid 
solution, making slippage difficult, thereby increasing the strength and 
hardness of the high entropy alloy. In addition, Nb atoms tend to gather 
on the dislocation line to form Cottrell atmosphere, which result in the 
anchoring of dislocation and increases the resistance to dislocation 
movement. The formula related is as follows [ 41 ]:

5. Conclusions 
Nb-doped CrMnFeCoNi
x 
(x = 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 1) HEAs were 
fabricated by vacuum electromagnetic induction melting. Based on the 
analysis of microstructure and tensile properties, the conclusions can be 
drawn as follows: 
(1) With the increase of Ni content, the CrMnFeCoNi
x 
alloys changed 
its structure from FCC + σ phases to a single FCC phase. For the 
CrMnFeCoNi
0.8

appeared and its volume fraction increased with Nb content. 
(2) With the increase of Ni content, MnNi-enrich particles appeared 
in the CrMnFeCoNi
0.8

0.8 
high entropy alloys exhibits a different dendritic morphology 
from that of the Nb-free high entropy alloys, especially the rime- 
like eutectic morphology shown in Ni

Nb
0.2

properties, with the yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and 
fracture elongation being 406.9 MPa, 662.8 MPa and 15.8%,
