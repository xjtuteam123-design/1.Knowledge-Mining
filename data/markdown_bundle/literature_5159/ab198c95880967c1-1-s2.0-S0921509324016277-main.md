ABSTRACT
How to optimize the strength-plasticity matching of refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) by simple strategies 
has been widely discussed. Generally, the strengthening mechanism of RHEAs mainly comes from the solid 
solution strengthening (SSS) caused by atomic radius mismatch and modulus mismatch. However, excessive 
atomic radius mismatch can increase the distortion energy of the RHEA and decrease its plasticity. Therefore, we 
propose a strategy to enhance modulus mismatch without altering atomic radius mismatch, thereby improve the 
strength-plasticity of RHEAs. We selected the ZrTiNb system as the model and increased the modulus mismatch 
by adding Ta, resulting in a 23.8 % increase in strength and an 82.3 % increase in plasticity. More importantly, 
the addition of Ta can decrease the diffusion rate of elements within the RHEAs and serve to refine the grains. 
This provides a significant avenue for developing RHEAs with outstanding properties.
1. Introduction
Alloys with multiple-principal components, commonly referred to as 
multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs), high-entropy alloys (HEAs), 
and complex concentrated alloys (CCAs), represent a novel alloy design 
strategy and have been extensively explored for their ability to achieve 
excellent mechanical properties [ 1 – 3 ]. Among these alloys, refractory 
high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) featuring single-phase body-centered cubic 
(BCC) structures composed of refractory elements such as Ti, Zr, Nb, Mo, 
Ta, Hf, W, and V, have recently attracted much attention for their high 
yield strength [ 4 – 7 ]. Senkov et al. [ 8 ] first highlighted that the solid 
solution strengthening resulting from the synergistic effects of atomic 
size mismatch and shear modulus mismatch is an intrinsic factor 
contributing to the high strength of RHEAs. For instance, solid solution 
strengthening contributes more than 60 % to the total strength of 
TiZrHfNb and TiZrVNbAl RHEAs [ 9 , 10 ]. Therefore, most researchers 
employ solution-strengthening strategies to enhance the strength of 
RHEAs.

with higher Ta concentrations, the distance from the dendrite tip (i.e., 
the solid-liquid interface) to the free growth of other nuclei is signifi -

refinement ( Fig. 7 ).
