Effect of Nb and Nb 5Si3 Powder Size on Microstructure and
Fracture Behavior of an Nb-16Si Alloy Fabricated by Spark
Plasma Sintering
WEI LIU and JIANGBO SHA
The inﬂuence of Nb and Nb 5Si3 powder sizes (Nb: 83.8, 37.4, 4.9 lm; Nb 5Si3: 86.4, 43.6,
2.9 lm) on microstructure and fracture behavior of an Nb-16Si alloy fabricated by spark
plasma sintering (SPS) was investigated. The results revealed that all as-sintered samples con-
sisted of Nb and Nb 5Si3 phases, with no new phases formed during SPS. The morphologies of
the Nb and Nb 5Si3 phases in the as-sintered samples depended on the original Nb and Nb 5Si3
powder sizes. Large Nb 5Si3 powders 86.4 and 43.6 lm in combination with Nb powder of
described sizes resulted in an Nb matrix plus Nb 5Si3 island biphase microstructure, which
supplied excellent fracture toughness. The microstructure made from the ﬁnest Nb powder
(4.9 lm) and the largest Nb 5Si3 powder (86.4 lm) had a high fracture toughness of
12.4 MPa m 1/2 through a mixed fracture mechanism of dimple, tear, and cleavage from the
small Nb grains. When the Nb 5Si3 powder size was decreased to 2.9 lm, the sample tended to
form an Nb 5Si3 matrix plus Nb island biphase microstructure, which exhibited a poor fracture
toughness value of between 5.6 and 8.0 MPa m 1/2 due to the crack propagation mainly within
the brittle Nb 5Si3 phase.

I. INTRODUCTION
WITH a high melting point close to 2793 K
(2520 /C176 C), high stiﬀness, and attractive speciﬁc mechan-
ical properties at elevated temperatures, [1–3] Nb-Si-based
alloys have great potential to be used in the hottest
sections of future higher thrust aircraft engines. The Nb-
Si binaries usually comprise of ductile Nb solid solutions
(NbSS) and stiﬀ Nb/Si silicides (Nb 3Si and Nb 5Si3), in
which the metallic Nb SS oﬀers room-temperature duc-
tility while the reinforcing silicides supply the strength
and creep resistance at elevated temperatures. [4,5] How-
ever, in the as-cast Nb-Si binaries, the Nb SS grain
usually has a size of a few tens of micrometers and
generally fails in a cleavage mode, because the river-
pattern is the dominant morphology found on the
fractured Nb SS surface. This damage mode cannot fully
utilize the toughness of Nb. [6–8]
Many eﬀorts have been devoted to alloy Nb-Si binary
by Ti, Hf, Cr, Al, B, W, and Mo, and optimizing their
contents to enhance the room-temperature fracture
toughness. The elements Ti and Hf [1,8,9] can decrease
the brittle-ductile transition temperature (BDTT) of
Nb,[7] while on the other hand they are toughening and
ductilizating elements for Nb. Jackson et al. [10] reported
that the fracture toughness of typical as-cast Nb-(12-
16)Si alloys fell in the range of about 5 to 7.5 MPa m 1/2.
However, it then increased up to the range of between 13
and 16 MPa m 1/2 when about 30 to 40 at. pct of Ti was
introduced to these alloys. In addition, thermo-mechan-
ical processes, such as directional solidiﬁcation [1,3,8] and
hot extrusion, [11,12] have frequently been used in the
fabrication of Nb-Si-based alloys, in order to improve
the ambient temperature fracture toughness. For the as-
cast Nb-10Si alloy, for example, its fracture toughness is
typically about 9.6 MPa m 1/2, which increases up to 14
and 20 to 22 MPa m 1/2 when prepared by directional
solidiﬁcation and hot-extrusion, respectively. [11] In com-
parison with the as-sintered Nb-10Si-2Fe ternary, an
improvement in the fracture toughness of the hot-
extruded + heat-treated sample by about 90 pct has
been obtained. [12] However, so far there have been rare
reports on what the optimized microstructure is for
toughness of Nb-Si-based alloy.
Recently, it has been reported that in a bimodal
microstructure-containing micrometer-scale Cu grains
embedded in a Cu nanocrystalline matrix, the Cu
nanocrystalline showed extraordinary intrinsic tensile
plasticity with no degradation of strength, [13] despite it
being well known that the nanocrystalline Cu often
exhibits low tensile ductility at room temperature. The
inhomogeneous microstructure induced strain harden-
ing mechanisms that restrained the localized deforma-
tion and necking, and stabilized the uniform tensile
deformation.[14] This strongly suggests that a bimodal
microstructure may improve the ductility of the nano-
crystalline phase by changing the fracture mechanism.
In this research, the concept of diﬀerent phase size
combinations (from a few to several tens of micrometers
scale) was used to design Nb-16Si alloys with biphase

III. RESULTS
A. Phase Constitution and Microstructural
Characteristics of the As-Sintered Samples
Typical X-ray diﬀraction patterns of the nine as-
sintered Nb-16Si samples made from diﬀerent Nb and
Nb
5Si3 powder size combinations are shown in Figure 2,
which exhibited similar X-ray diﬀraction proﬁles. It can
be seen from Figure 2 that in the as-sintered condition,
no characteristic diﬀraction peaks of intermetallic com-
pounds between Nb and Si were observed other than the
Nb and a-Nb
5Si3 silicide peaks. This suggested that the
as-sintered Nb-16Si samples had an identical phase
constitution to the powder mixture, and that the Nb and
Nb
5Si3 powder size combinations did not inﬂuence the
phase constitutions.
Figure 3 shows the back-scattered electron images
BSE of nine SPS Nb-16Si samples. Two phases with clear
contrast were observed in all samples, with the Nb phase
being white and Nb
5Si3 being grayish. When the Nb 5Si3
powder size was relatively larger (86.4 and 43.6 lm), the
microstructure was composed of a continuous Nb matrix
with Nb
5Si3 islands distributing uniformly within the
matrix (see Figures 3(a), (b), (d), (e), (g) and (h)).
However, the samples prepared from the 2.9 lmN b5Si3
powders showed an inverse microstructure. The Nb 5Si3
phase became a continuous matrix network with decreas-
ing Nb powder size, while the Nb phase almost adopted
an island-like morphology (Figures 3(c), (f) and (i)).
These observations suggested that the phase size, distri-
bution pattern, and grain size of the biphase Nb/Nb 5Si3
microstructures were apparently governed by the size of
the Nb and Nb5Si3 powders, with the Nb5Si3 powder size
being a dominant factor. It was interesting to note that
for the samples prepared with the 86.4 lmN b 5Si3
powder, the microstructure did not show any noticeable
change, even as the Nb powder size signiﬁcantly
decreased from 83.8 to 4.9 lm (see Figures 3(a), (d) and
(g)). In these samples, the Nb 5Si3 phase was basically its
original size of the powder form. To identify the
microstructural evolution with respect to the Nb powder
size, the crystal orientation maps (COMs) of Nb in
samples 1-1, 2-1 and 3-1 were measured using EBSD and
are shown in Figures 4(a), (b) and (c), respectively, in
which the average Nb grain size is also displayed. It was
clear that the feature of Nb grain among the SPS samples
1-1, 2-1 and 3-1 was very diﬀerent, and that this depended
on the Nb powder size. As expected, the Nb grain size in
each sample had a normal distribution (see Figures 4(a¢)
through (c ¢)) which was more or less consistent with the
size distribution of the original Nb powder used in this
sample (Table I). As an example, in the biphase micro-
structure of the sample 3-1 (i.e., the sample made from the
4.9 lm Nb powder), the average grain size of Nb was
about 9.2 lm. This small diﬀerence in size between the
Nb grain and its original powder form was believed to be
associated with the SPS process. Figures 3 and 4 strongly
indicate that the biphase Nb/Nb
5Si3 microstructure
could be tailored to a given phase fraction, distribution
pattern, and grain size, using the SPS-based approach.

IV. DISCUSSION
In this paper, nine kinds of dense Nb-16Si samples were
fabricated by the Nb and Nb 5Si3 powders with diﬀerent
size combinations. The original Nb and Nb 5Si3 powder
sizes aﬀect the phase size and distribution in the
as-sintered samples, which ﬁnally dominate the fracture
toughness of the alloys. Despite the three samples, 1-1,
2-1, and 3-1 all having a similar biphase microstructure
(See Figures 3(a), (d) and (g)), their K
Q values increased
dramatically (see Table II). To understand the underlying
reason behind why samples with the similar microstruc-
ture but diﬀerent Nb grain size showed such a larger
diﬀerence in K
Q, fracture surfaces of the samples 1-1 and
3-1 were compared and these can be seen in Figure 7.A s
expected, the same size and smooth fracture surface on the
Nb
5Si3 phase was observed in both the samples, but on the
fractured Nb surface in sample 1-1 (68.9 lm in the Nb
grain size), many sections with the independent river
pattern were found (see Figure 7(a)). When the Nb grain
size decreased to 9.2 lm (sample 3-1), a variety of fracture
modes appeared on the Nb matrix (see Figure 7(b)),
instead of the single river pattern observed in Figure 7(a).
The mixed fracture morphologies containing river pat-
terns, dimple (arrows A), and tear ridge (arrows B) were
found on the ﬁner Nb grains of the typical biphase Nb/
Nb
5Si3 microstructure, as seen in Figure 7(c), an enlarged
SEM image of Figure 7(b). Dimple and tear are typical
characteristics associated with a ductile fracture-behav-
ior
[7]; therefore, the KQ of the biphase Nb/Nb 5Si3 micro-
structure was undoubtedly improved when the fracture
mode of the Nb matrix changed from a singly cleavage
mode to a mixed dimple, tear, and cleavage mode.

In the as-cast Nb-Si binary, the Nb grain size in
either the Nb matrix or in the Nb 5Si3 matrix sample is
about a few tens of micrometers. [6–9,20,21] With this
relatively large size, the Nb grains usually fracture in a
cleavage mode and exhibit little or no dimple and
plastic stretching modes that are beneﬁcial to the
improving of the fracture toughness. For this reason,
the as-cast Nb-Si binary has low fracture tough-
ness. For example, the K
Q is about 9.2 MPa m 1/2 for
Nb-10Si alloy (30 pct Nb 5Si3 fraction[15]), which
decreases to about 5.4 MPa m 1/2 when the Si content
increases up to 16 at. pct. [1] This KQ value was the
same as that of sample 1-3, because the microstructures
(Nb5Si3 matrix and larger Nb grains) of the as-cast and
SPS Nb-16Si samples were similar to each other. Even
by thermomechanical processing, such as directional
solidiﬁcation, the K
Q of the Nb-16Si alloy is only
slightly improved to 7.8 MPa m 1/2.[16] By hot-extrusion

V. CONCLUSIONS
The eﬀect of Nb and Nb 5Si3 powders’ size combina-
tions on microstructure, including phase distribution
patterns, grain size, and fracture toughness, and mode
of SPS Nb-16Si alloy has been investigated. The
conclusions are summarized as follows:
1. Dense Nb/Nb
5Si3 biphase microstructures were
obtained in Nb-16Si alloy by the SPS technique.
The samples tended to form an Nb matrix micro-
structure when the Nb powder size was smaller
than the Nb
5Si3 powder size; conversely, they
formed an Nb 5Si3 matrix microstructure.
2. Even with a ﬁxed Nb to Nb 5Si3 volume fraction ra-
tio of 0.6:0.4, the KQ of the Nb-16Si samples with
biphase microstructure depended strongly on the
phase distribution pattern and the Nb grain size. A
finer Nb grain matrix with larger Nb 5Si3 islands is
the optimized Nb/Nb 5Si3 microstructure, and con-
tributed to a mixed mode of failure consisting of
dimple, tear, and cleavage for Nb, resulting in a
considerable increment in the K
Q.
3. For the Nb-16Si samples with the Nb 5Si3 matrix
microstructure, brittle fracture of the Nb 5Si3 phase
was the dominant failure mode. Such a microstruc-
ture showed poor fracture toughness.
