The behavior of hydrogen in high temperature

Abstract
The solubility of hydrogen in Ti-60 alloy obeys the Sivert’s Law and the Arrhenius relationship. The heat of dissolution of hydrogen in this
alloy is found to be 42.3 kJ/mol. The experimental results also show that the /H9251+ /H9252→ /H9252transformation temperature and high temperature yield
strength of Ti-60 alloy are continuously decreased with increasing hydrogen contents. The minimum yield strength of the alloy at high temperature
occurs at the hydrogen concentration corresponding to the /H9251+ /H9252→ /H9252transition and further increase in yield strength is due to the hardening
effect of hydrogen addition on the /H9252phase. Tensile at 900
◦C will make the yield strength reduce about 70% for the alloy containing 0.3% of
hydrogen.

1. Introduction
Hydrogen is so called temporary alloying element in tita-
nium alloys. Hydrogen can readily be charged into titanium
alloy, and it also can be easily removed by vacuum anneal-
ing. The addition of hydrogen as an alloying element has a
beneﬁcial effect on workability[1–8] because it leads to a signif-
icant increase in ductility and a decrease in the strength at high
temperature. The status and applications of thermohydrogen
processing to titanium alloys have been reviewed[9]. Therefore,
the hot processing of titanium alloys, such as hot forging, isother-
mal forging, superplastic forming and powder consolidation are
usually operated with the hydrogen as a temporary alloying
element.
Ti-60 alloy is a near-/H9251high temperature titanium alloy, which
can be used as the components of aircraft engines at tempera-
ture as high as 600
◦C. However, the operation of hot processing
of this alloy such as isothermal forging should be conducted at
temperature more than 980
◦C. Therefore, it is necessary to use
the more expensive steel as the mould material. The use of ther-
mohydrogen treatment is highly efﬁcient for decreasing the ﬂow

2. Experimental procedure
The investigation was made on specimens of Ti-60 alloy
(Ti–6Al–5Sn–2Zr–1Mo–1Nd, wt.%), cut from the hot rolled
bar. All the specimens were solution treated at 980
◦C for 2 h
air cooled and then aged at 700◦C for 2 h, air cooled. The cylin-
drical tensile specimens 5 mm in diameter and 25 mm in length
were prepared. The hydrogenation was conducted in a quartz
tube 25 mm in diameter 750 mm in length mounted in a labo-
ratory furnace. Pure hydrogen gas released from the hydrogen
storage materials was introduced to a glass reservoir. Before
hydrogenation the tube was evacuated to a pressure of approx-
imately 10
−2 Pa, the hydrogen gas was introduced from the
reservoir to the quartz tube at room temperature. When the
hydrogenation was started, the quartz tube was pushed into the
furnace, which has been heated to desired temperature. During

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Hydrogen solubility in Ti-60 alloy
Hydrogen solubility tests were carried out under constant
pressure and temperature. Fig. 1 shows the hydrogen solubility
in Ti-60 alloy as a function of pressure at various temperatures.
It is clear that all the date points are located on a straight line at
constant temperature. The solubility of hydrogen in the alloy
is directly proportional to the square root of hydrogen pres-
sure (i.e., C

in Ti-60 alloy as a function of reciprocal absolute temperature
at constant pressure. From the slope of straight lines the average
heat of solution (/Delta1H) of hydrogen in Ti-60 alloy is found to be
−42.3 kJ/mol. It is well known that the solubility of hydrogen
in the /H9252phase is much greater than that in the /H9251phase, in the
two phase region the hydrogen concentration in the /H9251and /H9252
phase do not change with increasing hydrogen content at given

properties of Ti-60 alloy at high temperature
Fig. 6 shows the yield strength of hydrogenated Ti-60 spec-
imens as a function of hydrogen contents. Tensile testing was
conducted at 850, 900 and 950

It should be noticed that at 900 or 850 ◦C the yield strength is
at ﬁrst decreased with increasing hydrogen contents and then
increased with hydrogen content. Comparing the data in Fig. 6
with data in Fig. 4, it clearly indicates that the minimum yield
strength occurs at a hydrogen concentration which corresponds
to the /H9251+ /H9252→ /H9252transition. This phenomena is attributed to
the fact that the volume fraction of /H9252phase is continuously
increased with increasing hydrogen contents, as a result, the
yield strength of the alloy is decreased with increasing hydrogen
contents, as the /H9251phase is completely transformed to /H9252phase,
the yield strength at this time reaches to a minimum point, the
further increase in yield strength is due to the hardening effect
of hydrogen on the /H9252phase. From Fig. 6 it also can be seen that
the hydrogen concentration that corresponds to the minimum
in the yield strength increases when the tensile temperature is
decreased. When tensile tests are conducted at 900

tensile temperature is decreased to 850

increased to 0.5% of hydrogen content.
4. Conclusions
(1) The solubility of hydrogen in Ti-60 alloy obeys the Sievert’s
Law and the Arrhenius relationship, and the heat of disso-
lution of hydrogen in this alloy is found to be 42.3 kJ/mol.
(2) The /H9251+ /H9252→ /H9252transformation temperature is continu-
ously decreased with increasing hydrogen contents. The
maximum extent of decreasing /H9251+ /H9252→ /H9252transformation
temperature for hydrogenated Ti-60 alloy is about 175
◦C.
The /H9251+ /H9252→ /H9252transformation temperature as a function
of hydrogen contents can be expressed as: Tα+β→β (◦C) =
815 + 210 exp[−3CH].
(3) The high temperature yield strength of hydrogenated spec-
imens is continuously decreased with increasing hydrogen
contents. The minimum yield strength of the hydrogenated
Ti-60 alloy occurs at the hydrogen concentration corre-
sponding to the /H9251+ /H9252→ /H9252transition, and further increase
in yield strength is attributed to the hardening effect of the
hydrogen on the /H9252phase.
