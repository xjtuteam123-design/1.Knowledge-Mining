ABSTRACT
The sigma ( σ ) phase formation has a key role in the mechanical properties of alloys. In this study, we studied the 
effects of aging temperature and time on the σ -phase formation and plastic deformation behavior of Fe
40
Mn
40 -
Co
10
Cr
10 
medium-entropy alloy (MEA). The σ -phase begins to form when the aging temperature is higher than 
700 
◦
C and gradually grows as the aging temperature and time increases. After aging at 800 
◦
C for 24 h, the 
tensile strength and elongation of the MEA with the σ -phase size of 108 nm were 662 MPa and 32 %, respectively. 
The σ -phase forms within the grains rather than at the grain boundaries. Dislocations are strongly hindered by

1. Introduction
The concepts of high- and medium-entropy alloys (HEAs and MEAs) 
were proposed in 2004, expanding not only the design range of alloys 
but also resulting in excellent mechanical properties, particularly high 
strength and ductility [1 – 4] . The crystal structure of HEAs is generally 
face-centered cubic (fcc), body-centered cubic (bcc), and hexagonal 
close-packed cubic (hcp) due to high mixing entropy stabilizing the solid 
solution phase. However, fcc single-phase MEAs have relatively low 
strength although exhibiting excellent ductility [5 – 7] , which limits their 
widespread application. Grain refinement strengthening [8 – 11] and 
solution strengthening [12 – 15] are frequently used to enhance the 
strength of single-phase fcc MEAs, but often result in a loss of ductility. 
Additionally, precipitation strengthening is another effective strategy 
for increasing the strength of single-phase fcc MEAs while maintaining 
decent ductility [16 – 18] .
The sigma ( σ )-phase is a common precipitate in fcc MEAs. The 
σ -phase with a tetragonal lattice frequently precipitates in Cr-containing 
fcc MEAs having valence electron concentration (VEC) between 6.88 to 
7.84, and an electronegativity difference Δ χ greater than 0.133 [19 – 21] . 
In CrMnFeCoNi
x 
and CrMnFeCo
x
Ni HEAs, the elements Ni and Co (fcc 
stabilizers) inhibit the formation of σ -phase [22,23] . Mn and especially 
Cr destabilize the fcc solid solution and promote the formation of the 
σ -phase [24,25] . The hard intermetallic σ -phase hinders dislocation 
sliding, which increases the strength of the MEAs [26,27] . However, the 
σ -phase is prone to precipitate at grain boundaries, leading to a severe 
loss of ductility. Han et al. [28] reported precipitation of σ -phase at grain 
boundaries in CoCrFeNiMo HEAs. The elastic and plastic deformation 
between the σ -phase and the matrix during deformation is highly 
incompatible, and high local stress and strain concentrations are easily 
generated at the interface between the σ -phase and the matrix. The in -
crease in local stress levels lead to microcracks and a decrease in the 
ductility of the alloys.
In addition, recent studies have suggested that by effectively con -
trolling the size and distribution of the intermetallic compound phases, 
it is possible to increase the strength of the alloy without causing severe

The A800 – 24 sample exhibits the best combination of high strength 
and good ductility. First, precipitation strengthening depends on the size 
and distribution of precipitates in alloys [56 – 58] . The σ -phase, being a 
hard intermetallic compound, forms an incoherent interface with the fcc 
matrix, making Orowan strengthening the dominant mechanism [59] . 
During deformation, dislocations are hindered and bent due to the dif -
ficulty of passing through the σ -phase. The bent dislocations further 
develop to form dislocation loops, while remaining dislocations 
continue to move under higher stresses and dislocation-to-dislocation 
entanglement occurs, leading to a pinning effect as interactions in -
crease [60] . The σ -phase interacts with dislocations and increases the 
strength of MEAs. As the σ -phase is distributed within the grains and 
rather than the grain boundaries, good ductility is maintained at high 
strengths. Secondly, high-density nanotwins also enhance the strength 
of the MEAs. The low energy and high stability of twins lead to regular 
arrangement of atoms at the twin boundaries. The formation of nano -
twins introduces stable and coherent internal interfaces within the 
grains, reducing the mean free path of dislocation motion and increasing

4. Conclusions
In summary, the effect of aging treatment on the precipitation of the 
σ -phase in fully recrystallized Fe
40
Mn
40
Co
10
Cr
10 
MEA was investigated. 
Prior to the aging treatment, cold rolling and high temperature recrys -
tallization pretreatments were carried out to facilitate grains growth and 
inhibit the precipitation of σ -phase at grain boundaries. Aging at 600 
◦
C 
does not promote the formation of σ -phase, while σ -phase was observed 
after aging at 700
◦
C for 24 h. When the aging temperature is increased 
to 800 
◦
C, the time required for σ -phase formation was shortened to 
10 h. As the aging temperature increases, the σ -phase precipitates faster. 
After aging at 800 
◦
C for 24 h, hundreds of nanometer of σ -phase par -
ticles are formed within the grains, which improves the strength of the 
MEA with little loss to ductility. The σ -phase acts as a strong barrier to 
dislocation motion, where dislocations bypass the σ -phase and form 
dislocation loops, increasing strength and strain-hardening ability. The 
low SFE of this MEA leads to the formation of a high-density of twins, 
which introduce additional interfaces within the grains, effectively 
relieving stress concentrations. In addition, a large number of twin 
boundaries are formed which can act as nucleation sites for the σ -phase 
within the grains. The formation of σ -phase within the grains can be 
controlled by adjusting the heat treatment process, which is beneficial to 
improve the strength and ductility.
