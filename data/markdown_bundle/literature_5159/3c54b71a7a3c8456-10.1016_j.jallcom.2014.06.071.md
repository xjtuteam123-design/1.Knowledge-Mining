abstract
A 300 lm Ni-rich Ni 3Al sheet was produced by electron beam physical vapor deposition (EB-PVD) and
followed by different heat treatments to obtain ﬁne c0/c two-phase structures with large elongation.
Tensile testing was performed at room-temperature, and the corresponding mechanisms were investi-
gated in detail. Results indicated that the as-deposited Ni 3Al alloy exhibited non-equilibrium directional
columnar crystal, and transited to equiaxed crystal with uniformly distributed tough c phase after heat
treatment. Meanwhile, the fracture mechanism transited from brittleness to a mixture of ductility and
brittleness modes. With an appropriate heat treatment, high strength (ultimate tensile strength obtained
828 MPa) and high ductility (elongation obtained 14.6%) Ni 3Al alloy has been achieved, which was due to
the mesh network microstructure. A series of transmission electron microscope (TEM) characterizations
conﬁrmed that the increasing ﬂow stress of Ni 3Al alloy was attributed to the cubical secondary c0 phase
precipitates (25–50 nm) within the c phase. This work provides a potential strategy for repairing the
worn tip of single crystal engine blades using Ni-rich Ni 3Al alloy by EB-PVD.

1. Introduction
To obtain additional service life and higher operating efﬁciency,
nickel-based single crystals superalloys, which contain increasing
amounts of refractory and precious elements, are widely used in
the high-temperature turbine blades [1,2]. During operation, the
rotating turbine blades withstand wear damage by turbine shroud,
which results in an increase of tip clearance [3–7]. This form of
damage, if not repaired, will obviously reduce engine operating
efﬁciency and service life. Therefore, an effective method for
repairing worn blade tips of single crystal blades, rather than
replacing the whole costly blades, is desired, which could preserve
scarce and valuable metals, reduce the maintenance costs and
extend the service time [8–11]. Advances in repair procedures
and repair materials are expected to be the focus of future develop-
ment efforts.
Numerous procedures for repair and refurbishing blade tips
have been developed in the past, such as gas tungsten arc welding
(GTAW), plasma transferred arc welding (PTAW) and laser beam
welding (LBW) techniques. However, these procedures must
employ under complicated process control and high energy input,
which greatly increase the difﬁculty of repairing the single crystal
blades [12–19]. Compared with above-mentioned method, elec-
tron beam physical vapor deposition (EB-PVD), which has been
successfully used in the metal coatings and ceramic thermal
barrier coatings on blades and vanes, offers many desirable charac-
teristics, such as low energy input, ﬂexible deposition parameters,
high deposition rates (up to 150 lm/min), controlled composition,
tailored microstructure, and high interfacial bond strength. During
the deposition of EB-PVD, substrates are heated far below its melt-
ing point, which can effectively avoid the emergence of thermal
cracking during repairing [20–23]. All of these make EB-PVD a
potential method to repair the worn tips of single crystal engine
blades.
Ni3Al alloys have been selected as an attractive candidate for
repair materials because of their high strength and excellent wear
resistance in high temperature environment. However, serious
environmental embrittlement of polycrystalline Ni 3Al alloys limits
their application as repair materials [24–26]. Alloying elements,
such as boron, could signiﬁcantly improve tensile ductility
[27–29]. However, the tensile strength of Ni 3Al alloy drops at
elevated temperature when more than 0.01 wt.% boron is doped
[30–32]. Fortunately, microstructural control could improve
tensile ductility of Ni3Al alloy without sacriﬁcing the tensile strength
in high temperature environment, which could be attributed to
tough c phase existing at grain boundaries [33–35]. According
the nickel–aluminum phase diagram, microstructures containing

3. Results and discussion
3.1. c phase toughening in c0/c two-phase structures
The sheets heat treated at 900 /C176C, 1000 /C176C and 1100 /C176C are
labeled as HT900, HT1000 and HT1100 respectively. The tensile
stress–strain curves of as-deposited and heat-treated Ni 3Al sheets
are compared in Fig. 1. The as-deposited Ni 3Al sheet exhibits elas-

the tensile elongation increases signiﬁcantly with increasing heat

increasing heat treatment temperatures, more volume fraction of
tough c phase is obtained in Ni-rich Ni 3Al system, which decreases
the thermodynamic energy of the system [36]. The volume fraction

c
phase, there are increasing number of additional tearing ridges in
grain boundaries ( Fig. 6 d, f and h), which enhances the effective
slip length for dislocation motion and promotes the ductility of
the heat treated samples. A mixture of ductility and brittleness

of c phase effectively enhances the ductility and the elongation

and distribution of tough c phase. As shown in Fig. 5d, the distribu-

interface area and effectively suppresses crack propagation during

3.2. Grain size dependence of yield strength
The tensile strength of the as-deposited sample is higher than
900 MPa in Fig. 1 . Compared with recrystallization after cold roll-
ing the single crystal (RACRS) Ni 3Al alloy or self-propagation
high-temperature synthesis and hot extrusion (SHS/HE) Ni 3Al alloy
[33,39], the yield strengths of EB-PVD Ni 3Al sheets are signiﬁcantly
higher, as shown in Table 1 . The yield strength of the sheets
decreases with increasing heat treatment temperature. The yield
strength values of HT900, HT1000, and HT1100 are 844 MPa,
682 MPa and 512 MPa, respectively.

3.3. The role of secondary c0 precipitate in the fracture strength
The ﬂow stress of HT1000 shows little change after yielding,
while that of HT1100 increases from 512 MPa to 828 MPa. The dif-
ference in ﬂow stress between HT1000 and HT1100 samples could
be associated with secondary c0 precipitation strengthening, which
is similar like nickel based superalloys [42].
Numerous ﬁne c0 phase particles precipitate (denoted as sec-
ondary c0 phase) within c phase, and show ﬁxed orientation rela-
tionships with primary c0 phase in the grain, as shown in Fig. 9 .
The effect of precipitation strengthening is related to the precipi-
tate size [43]. The sizes of secondary c0 phase particles in
HT1000 and HT1100 are /C24 20 nm and /C24 50 nm, respectively, which
indicates that the critical precipitate size should be in the range
20 nm to 50 nm. Once the critical size is reached, obvious increase

4. Conclusions
High strength and high ductility of polycrystalline Ni-rich Ni 3Al
alloy sheets with ﬁne c0/c two-phase structures were produced by
EB-PVD and followed by appropriate heat treatments. The c phase
toughening could play an important role in the elongation of Ni 3Al
alloy with the c0/c two-phase structures. The elongation of Ni 3Al
alloy could be effectively enhanced by increasing the volume frac-
tion and controlling the spatial distribution of c phase. Beneﬁt
from continuous mesh network microstructure, the heat treated
sample obtained large elongation, the highest of which reached
14.6%. The yield strength of Ni 3Al sheets obeyed Hall–Petch rela-
tionship and reached /C24 844 MPa due to ﬁne-grain strengthening.
Moreover, the fracture strength of the Ni 3Al sheets, which
exceeded 820 MPa, was enhanced by the precipitation strengthen-
ing of the secondary c0 phase without sacriﬁcing the elongation
when the size of secondary precipitate arrived at a critical size. This
work provides a general processing for high strength and high duc-
tility of polycrystalline Ni-rich Ni 3Al alloy, which offers the poten-
tial of repair and refurbishment of worn single crystal blades.
