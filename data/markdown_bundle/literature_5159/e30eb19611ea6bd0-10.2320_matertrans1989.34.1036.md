Materials Transactions, JIM, vol. 34, No. 11 (1993), pp. 1036 to 1041 High Temperature Strength and Aging Behavior of 12%Cr-15%Mn Austenitic Steels Kazuya Miyahara, Dong-Su Baet, Hidenori Sakait and Y uzo Hosoi" Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Nagoya University, Furoh-cho, Chikusa-ku, Nagoya 464, Japan High Mn��Cr austenitic steels are still considered to be an important high temperature structural material from the point of view of reduced radio-activation. The objective of the present study is to make a fundamental research of mechanical properties and microstructure of 12%Cr-15%Mn austenitic steels. Especially the effects of alloying elements of V and Ti on the mechanical properties and microstructure evolution of high Mn��Cr steels were studied. Precipitation behaviors of carbides, nitrides and a phase are investigated and their remarkable effects on the high temperature strength are found. The addition of V was very effective for strengthening the materials with the precipitation of fine VN. Ti was also found to be beneficial for the improvement of high temperature strength properties. The results of high temperature strengthes of the 12Cr-15Mn austenitic steels were compared with those of the other candidate and/or reference materials, for example, JFMS (modified 9Cr��2Mo ferritic stainless steel) and JPCAs (modified 316 austenitic stainless Steels). (Received February l, 1993) Keywords: high manganese-chromium austenitic steels, reduced radio-activation materials, fusion reactor materials, heat resisting steel, high temperature strength, aging behavior, creep-rupture strength, sigma phase I. Introduction High Mn steels have been developed for the high strength and toughness and/or non-magnetic steels which are usually utilized at low As the heat resisting alloys, high Mn steels have been developed as modified Cr-Ni steels in which a part of Ni content is substituted by Mn. For example, Kromarc(4), Tenelon(7), etc. are known as such high Mn heat resisting steels. Recently, however, much attention has been paid on the high Mn heat resisting austenitic steels with very low content of Ni from the viewpoint of reduced radio-ac- tivation of fusion reactor materials, because the substitu- tion of Ni with Mn reduces the long-term radio-activity(S). Many fundamental researches are being done on the various aspects of the high Mn-Cr heat resisting steels, that is, the effects of neutron irradiation on microstruc- and phase high temperature ture low temperature toughness 07) and electro- (18) chemical property or corrosion resistance In the present paper, we will discuss the strength properties and microstructure of high Mn-Cr austenitic steels. In the present research, the precipitation behaviors of carbide, nitride and other phases and their contribution to the strength properties of the high Mn- Cr alloy were studied. The results of the high tempera- ture strength of the 12Cr-15Mn alloys were compared with that of modified 316 austenitic steels (JPCAs) and modified 9Cr-2Mo ferritic steel (JFMS) as the reference alloys for the development research of the first wall materials in Japan. Aging behavior and microstructure evolution Figure 1 shows the Vickers hardness change during the aging treatment at 873 K. The materials containing vanadium showed large age-hardening peak at about 1000 h aging and the hardness increased slightly again after 3000 h aging. On the other hand, vanadium-free materials indicated small hardening peak at early stage of aging (at about 1 to 10 h) and then large increase of hard- ness after aging for 3000 h. Creep-rupture strength Figure 8 shows the creep curves for the materials tested at 873 K under the stress of 216 MPa. The addition of V is effective for increasing the creep strength of 2CINW and IC2NW and the combined addition of V and Ti is also very effective for improvement of creep strength. Such strengthening effects of V and Ti are considered to be due to the formation of fine VN or slightly coarse precipitates of Ti(C, N). Figure 9 shows the creep rupture strength of various 12Cr-15Mn materials at 873 K. The 2CINWV, IC2NWV and 2CINWVTi show the largest creep-rupture strengthes among the 12Cr-15Mn ma- terials and they are between those of 316 stainless steel(21) and JPCA-2(22) (including JPCA(23)). The data on JPCA- 2 was obtained from the Larson-Miller plotting of Asakura's data(22) with rupture time of 5 to 2 x 104 h at 923 K test. IV. Conclusions The conclusions of the present study are as follows, (1) The addition of V to 2CINW or IC2NW materials increased hardening peaks and tensile strengthes but decreased the elongations after the aging at 873 K for 1000 h. By the addition of Ti to the 2ClNWV or IC2NWV materials, however, the elonga- tion was improved without deteriorating the tensile strengthes. (2) Secondary age-hardening were observed during the aging at 873 K for about 3000 to 8000 h for all the 12Cr-15Mn steels. From TEM observation and X-ray diffraction analysis, this large hardening was caused mainly by the coarse precipitates of Mn C6 carbides in the interior of grains. (3) 2CINWV, IC2NWV and 2CINWVTi steels showed the largest creep-rupture strengthes among the 12Cr-15Mn steels. These largest strengthes are less than that of JPCA or JPCA-2, but higher than that of JFMS. Acknowledgment The present authors are grateful to Dr. T.
