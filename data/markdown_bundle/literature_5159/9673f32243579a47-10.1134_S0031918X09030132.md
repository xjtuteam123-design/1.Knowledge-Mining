INTRODUCTION
The creation of next-generation engineering, espe-
cially aviation and aerospace engineering, requires new
nontraditional structural materials that can ensure a
higher level of service properties compared to the exist-
ing materials and/or a decrease in the construction
weight due to a decrease in the material density. The
application of light intermetallic alloys based on the

TiAl,

temperature steels and nickel alloys, which have a sub-
stantially higher density. However, the key problem of
titanium aluminide–based intermetallic alloys consists
in their low plasticity and insufﬁcient fracture tough-
ness over a wide temperature range. However, low-den-
sity materials such as titanium alloys have high heat
resistance at relatively low temperatures (
 
400–600°C
 
),
good plasticity, and a high strength and signiﬁcantly
excel intermetallic alloys in technological characteris-
tics. Therefore, a titanium alloy–intermetallic com-
pound composite material could have a favorable com-
bination of service and technological properties. The
development of effective methods for the production of
such composite materials is one of the promising ways
to design new high-temperature materials and to extend
the ﬁelds of the application of sheet semiproducts of
intermetallic and titanium alloys as the components of
layered composite materials.
It should be noted that metallic layered composite
materials have received the most study, whereas the

Alloy–Orthorhombic Titanium Aluminide Layered Composite
 
R. M. Galeev, O. R. Valiakhmetov, R. V. Safiullin, V. M. Imaev, and R. M. Imaev

Abstract
 
—The microstructure and tensile properties of a layered composite material fabricated by solid-state
bonding of its components using pressure welding are studied at room and elevated temperatures. The compo-
nents are made of a high-temperature VT25U titanium alloy and an intermetallic alloy (
 
O
 
 alloy) based on
orthorhombic titanium aluminide of the composition Ti–23Al–22.7Nb–1.1V–0.6Zr–0.2Si–0.3C (at %). The
study of the microstructure and chemical composition of the composite by scanning electron microscopy and
energy dispersive X-ray analysis demonstrates that this method of producing a layered composite provides
high-quality poreless bonding of materials of different types. The solid-state bonding zone has an intermediate
chemical composition. Mechanical tests demonstrate that the room-temperature strength of the composite is
comparable with that of the 
 
O
 
 alloy and is higher than that of the titanium alloy; as the fraction of the titanium
alloy in the composite decreases, this strength increases. The relative elongation of the layered composite is
found to be higher than that of the 
 
O
 
 alloy and lower than that of the titanium alloy. In the temperature range
500–
 
700°C
 
, the strength of the composite material is 25% higher than that of the titanium alloy, and its plastic-
ity is lower than that of the titanium alloy. Our method is shown to be promising for producing layered com-
posite materials that have high mechanical properties over a wide temperature range.

CONCLUSIONS
Using a high-temperature VT25U titanium alloy and
an orthorhombic alloy based on the é-Ti2AlNb phase,
we developed a method for the production of metal–
intermetallic compound layered composite materials
and fabricated layered composite sheets of laboratory
dimensions. We studied the zone of bonding of these
different materials by scanning electron microscopy
and showed that the joint has a high quality and has no
pores. The preliminary study of the tensile properties of
the fabricated composite material demonstrates that
they are higher than those of the titanium alloy and are
at least comparable with those of the orthorhombic
alloy. Our method of producing layered composite
materials was shown to be promising, and the metallic
component/intermetallic component ratio should be
optimized to improve the mechanical properties of the
composite.
