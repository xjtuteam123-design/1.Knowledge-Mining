abstract
With the aim of synthesizing a new ferromagnetic CoFe-based glassy alloy with a large supercooled
liquid region, we studied the (Co xFe1 /C0 x)62Nb8B30 (x¼0–1) alloy system. The alloy corresponding to
x¼0.5 exhibits a large supercooled liquid region of 101 K, together with a high fracture strength of
approximately 5000 MPa at room temperature and superplasticity near its glass transition temperature.
In addition, the x¼ 0.5 alloy as a soft magnetic material shows a low coercivity below 2 A/m, a high
effective permeability of about 20,000 at 1 kHz and 3400 at 1 MHz as well as a giant magnetoimpedance
ratio of 62%. The combination of superplasticity and soft magnetic properties makes this alloy promising
for future functional applications.
& 2013 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.1. Introduction
CoFe-based ferromagnetic glassy alloys have been widely used
in choke coils, converters and giant magnetoimpedance (GMI)
sensors [1,2]. However, conventional techniques such as melt
spinning and in-rotating water spinning have limited the shape
and dimension of these alloys to thin ribbons with a thickness less
than 50 μm and wires with a diameter less than 150 μm [3].I ti s
therefore expected that eliminating the limitation of the sample
shape and dimension could expand the ﬁelds of application [3,4].
Recently, thermoplastic processing and spark-plasma sintering has
been developed for devices with high dimensional accuracy and
complex geometries on metallic glasses, and such devices are
expected to enter commercial production [3,5]. From a processing
point of view, glassy alloys suitable for thermoplastic forming and
spark-plasma sintering are supposed to possess a large super-
cooled liquid region (SCLR), which not only implies high resistance
against crystallization [6] but also gives access to low forming
viscosity [3,5].
Since 1995, various types of soft magnetic alloys with a SCLR
over 50 K were found in many systems [1,3]. Aiming at synthesiz-
ing a new ferromagnetic CoFe-based glassy alloy with a larger
SCLR, we focused on Fe
62Nb8B30 metallic glass, because of its large
SCLR exceeding 70 K and the absence of rare earth elements [7].
To further increase the SCLR, Fe was partially replaced by Co,
because the coexistence of similar atoms is reportedly bene ﬁcial to
frustrate the process of crystallization [8]. Consequently, a new
alloy system (Co xFe1–x)62Nb8B30 (x¼0–1) with a SCLR of 101 K was
synthesized.
2. Experimental procedure
Multi-component alloy ingots with nominal compositions
(CoxFe1/C0 x)62Nb8B30 (x¼ 0–1) were made by arc-melting high-purity
elements in an argon atmosphere. Ribbon samples were prepared by
melt spinning. The glassy and crystallized structures were examined
by X-ray diffraction with Cu Kα radiation. Thermal stability associated
with glass transition temperature (Tg), crystallization temperature (Tx)
and supercooled liquid region (ΔTx¼Tx /C0 Tg) was examined by diffe-
rential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a heating rate of 40 K/min.
The compressive strength was measured on glassy rods with a
diameter of 1 mm using an Instron testing machine at a strain rate
of 5 /C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1. Saturation magnetization (Ms) was measured with a
vibrating sample magnetometer under an appliedﬁeld of 10,000 Oe.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 (a) shows DSC curves of the as-spun (Co xFe1–x)62Nb8B30
(x¼0–1) glassy ribbons, and all the compositions exhibit a SCLR
before crystallization. In the case of x¼ 0–0.5, crystallization takes
place through a single-stage exothermic reaction. However, in the
case of x¼0.6–1, crystallization takes place through a two-stage
reaction, and the increase of Co content expands the interval
between the two crystallization reactions. Tg decreases from 919 K
to 897 K as x increases from 0 to 0.5. With the further increase of x
from 0.6 to 1, Tg remains almost unchanged. On the other hand,
Tx maintains a nearly constant value of approximately 995 K at
x¼0–0.5 and then decreases from 998 K to 933 K as x increases
from 0.6 to 1. As a result, the alloy with x¼ 0.5 exhibits the largest
SCLR of 101 K. Previous studies have shown that the coexistence of
similar atoms at an equiatomic ratio can minimize the Gibbs free
energy difference between the solid glass and its liquid state [8],
which re ﬂects the stability of the glassy state [9]. Fe and Co with
comparable atomic radii (0.124 and 0.125 nm respectively) have a
small enthalpy of mixing ( /C0 1 kJ/mol) [10], and their coexistence
as similar atoms increases the disorder and generates a complex
crystallization process from the metastable supercooled liquid
state, hence an enlarged SCLR [9]. In line with this, we explored
the crystallization phases of (Co 0.5Fe0.5)62Nb8B30 as a way to
explain its large SCLR. Fig. 1 (b) displays the XRD patterns of as-
spun and annealed (Co 0.5Fe0.5)62Nb8B30 ribbons. The XRD pattern
of the as-spun ribbon shows a broad halo peak typical of fully
glassy structure. The glassy state is preserved after annealing at
957 K (Tg þ 60 K) for 3 min, indicating its resistance against crystal-
lization at high temperature. By increasing the annealing time at
957 K to 5 min, crystalline peak with weak intensity is super-
imposed on the glassy halo. After annealing at the same tempera-
ture for 20 min, peaks corresponding to Fe (Co) and (Fe, Co) 23B6
phases could be identi ﬁed, which are different from what have
been identiﬁed as the primary crystallization phases for Co62Nb8B30
and Fe 62Nb8B30 alloys [7]. The simultaneous precipitation of
(Fe, Co) 23B6 and other phases through a single-stage exothermic
reaction requires long-range atomic diffusion due to the large unit
cell of (Fe, Co) 23B6 phase containing 92 metallic atoms and 24
metalloid atoms [1 1], hence the high resistance against crystal-
lization represented by its wide SCLR.
As introduced earlier, a large SCLR indicates superplasticity in a
large temperature range [5]. In this context, compressive mechan-
ical tests were conducted to evaluate this property. Fig. 2
(a) presents the stress –strain curves obtained from the compres-
sive tests. At room temperature, the glassy rod exhibits elastic
deformation before fracture, which occurs at about 5000 MPa. This
high value is interpreted to result from the strong bonding in the
glassy structure among the constituent elements as is expected
from the large negative enthalpy of mixing from 16 kJ/mol to
54 kJ/mol for Fe –Nb, Co –B, Co –Nb, Fe –B and Nb –B pairs [10].
Nonetheless, in the temperature range of 857 K ( Tg–40 K) to 917 K
(Tg þ20 K), the deformation occurs in homogeneous mode where
stress initially increases and attains a maximum value before
decreasing to a steady-state ﬂow stress. Such a stress overshoot
phenomena was observed previously in Pd-based glassy alloys and
is attributed to the transition from the Newtonian to non-
Newtonian deformation mode [12]. With further increase of
temperature to 937 K ( Tg þ40 K), the stress overshoot disappears
and the stress increases monotonously before attaining a steady-
state value. One can see that the diameter of the rod is twice as
large after compression without cracking (the inset of Fig. 2 (a)),
demonstrating good ﬂow workability in the SCLR.

4. Conclusions
In this letter, we report a glassy alloy system (Co xFe1–x)62Nb8B30
(x¼0–1) exhibiting a large SCLR up to 101 K at x¼ 0.5. This alloy
system also shows good superplasticity and soft magnetic proper-
ties. The combination of these properties makes this CoFe-based
glassy alloy worthy of further investigations for its potential
engineering applications.
