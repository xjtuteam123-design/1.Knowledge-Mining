ABSTRACT 
Additive manufacturing of lightweight and heat-resistant aluminum-based materials has received ever increasing 
interest for obtaining high specific strength and complex-shaped components serving at elevated temperatures in 
aerospace industry. In this study, an in-situ TiB
2 
particle reinforced Al – Cu – Mg – Fe – Ni (Al2618) alloy was additive 
manufactured by laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) with excellent printability. The as-printed TiB
2
/Al2618 
composite presented a fully equiaxed and remarkably refined grain structure with an average size of ~1 μ m. The 
intermetallic phases in the Al matrix exhibited an ultrafine cellular structure owing to the rapid solidification by 
L-PBF. The ultrafine microstructure showed excellent thermal stability at elevated temperatures due to the 
synergic effects of TiB
2 
particles and Fe, Ni-rich coarsening-resistant intermetallic phases. The L-PBF TiB
2
/ 
Al2618 composite has shown superior mechanical performance at elevated temperatures both in tensile strength 
and in strength retention after thermal exposure among typical L-PBF and wrought Al alloys. This work inspires 
the design of heat-resistant metal matrix composites (MMCs) with advanced high-temperature mechanical 
performance enabled by additive manufacturing for potential industrial applications. 
1. Introduction 
Laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) of aluminum (Al) alloys has shown 
huge application opportunities in aerospace and automotive industries 
for additive manufacturing (AM) high specific strength and complex- 
shaped components [ 1 – 3 ]. Recent attentions have been drawn on the 
opportunity space on AM heat-resistant Al alloys in replacement of the 
titanium and ferrous alloys for lightweighting and improved energy 
efficiency at elevated temperatures [ 4 – 6 ]. However, Al alloys typically 
suffer from an evident loss of strength above 200 
◦
C (~0.5T
m 
of Al) [ 7 ]. 
At present, the most commonly applied L-PBF Al alloys are Al – Si series 
alloys, such as the AlSi10Mg alloy [ 2 , 8 , 9 ]. Despite their outstanding 
room-temperature mechanical performance, the degradation of strength 
is severe under high temperature loading or annealing [ 10 – 12 ]. It is 
highly worthwhile but still challenging to AM Al alloys with agreeable 
high-temperature mechanical performances, including both excellent 
high-temperature strength and high strength retention after thermal 
exposure, for potential industrial applications. 
Due to the intrinsic high cooling rate during the solidification of the 
melt pool, the L-PBF printed microstructure features, such as the grains 
and intermetallics, are greatly refined. The refined microstructure leads 
to a high strength at ambient temperature compared with the conven -
tional casting condition [ 13 , 14 ]. However, the refined microstructure is 
vulnerable to get coarsening at elevated temperatures, resulting in a 
severe loss of strength therefore. For instance, for the L-PBF Al – Si alloys, 
the submicron-sized Si cellular network is broken into Si particles and 
get coarsened at elevated temperatures [ 12 , 15 ]. The tensile strength of 
the L-PBF AlSi10Mg alloy at 200 
◦
C and 300 
◦
C were reported as 189 
MPa and 73 MPa respectively, which were only 50 % and 20 % of that at 
ambient temperature [ 11 ]. Moreover, the hardness and tensile strength 
of the L-PBF AlSi10Mg alloy exhibited a severe degradation from HV128 
to HV77 and from 415 MPa to 263 MPa after annealing at 300 
◦
C for 
only 2 h, respectively [ 15 ]. To enhance the high-temperature mechan -
ical performance of the L-PBF Al alloys, the key point is to improve the

coarsening-resistant intermetallic phases and 2) ceramic particle re -

phases are typically formed from elements with low solubility and low 
diffusivity in Al matrix. The low solubility and low diffusivity provide 
the thermodynamic and kinetic barrier for coarsening [ 16 ]. Ceramic 
particles are inherently thermal stable and can improve the 
high-temperature strength of the matrix through the particle strength -
ening mechanisms [ 17 ]. Meanwhile, particles at grain boundaries can 
limit the grain growth effectively via the Zener pinning mechanism [ 18 ]. 
The commercial Al2618 (Al – Cu – Mg – Fe – Ni) alloy is a typical heat- 
resistant Al alloy that has been broadly applied in heavily loaded en -
gine pistons, aircraft engines components, and the cladding of the 
Concorde supersonic aircraft at elevated temperatures of ~150 
◦
C [ 19 , 
20 ]. The alloying elements Fe and Ni have very low solubility and low

21 ]. The Fe, Ni-rich intermetallic compounds are highly 
coarsening-resistant up to 400 
◦
C to retain the microstructural stability 
[ 22 ]. Besides, compared to some novel developed AM heat-resistant Al 
alloys, such as Al-Ce [ 5 , 23 , 24 ] and Al – Mg-Sc-Zr series alloys [ 25 , 26 ] 
with high amount of rare-earth elements, the commercial Al2618 alloy 
is advantageous for its reliability, repeatability, and low-cost for po -
tential industrial applications. 
Amongst the ceramic particle reinforcements, TiB
2 
is advantageous 
for the high hardness, modulus as well as the strong bonding with Al 
matrix, providing evident particle strengthening effects at both ambient 
and elevated temperatures [ 27 – 29 ]. TiB
2 
is also thermal stable for its 
high melting point during L-PBF processing [ 30 ]. Moreover, commercial 
Al alloys commonly suffer from the high hot-crack susceptibility (HCS) 
during L-PBF. The high HCS is owing to the large solidification tem -
perature range of the alloys and the formation of coarse columnar grains 
under the extremely high thermal gradient in the melt pool [ 8 , 31 ]. TiB
2 
is a typical inoculant to trigger the columnar to equiaxed grain transition 
(CET) during solidification, which alleviates the HCS and therefore en -
ables the L-PBF of many unprintable Al alloys [ 32 ]. Besides, TiB
2 
par -
ticles can be in-situ fabricated into Al matrix in nano or submicron size 
with well dispersion, providing strong Zener pinning force to restrict the 
grain growth under long-time thermal exposure [ 33 ]. 
Exploiting the synergic effect of thermal stable intermetallics and 
ceramic particles, TiB
2 
particles can be in-situ introduced into a heat- 
resistant alloy matrix as Al2618 via the salt-melt reaction, pursuing 
for enhanced high-temperature mechanical properties and heat resis -
tance. Our recent work has fabricated an in-situ TiB
2
/Al – Cu – Mg – Fe – Ni 
composite for electron beam powder bed fusion (EB-PBF). The coexis -
tence of TiB
2 
particles and coarsening-resistant intermetallics in Al 
matrix resulted in excellent microstructure stability under high tem -
perature environment ( > 400 
◦
C) during EB-PBF [ 34 ]. However, such 
strategy has been scarcely studied so far by introducing particle re -
inforcements into heat-resistant Al matrix for L-PBF, except for limited 
research such as [ 35 , 36 ] by Belelli et al. Their studies have revealed 
satisfactory mechanical properties at room and elevated temperatures of 
the L-PBF Ti/B modified Al2618 alloys. However, the role of particle 
reinforcements on the solidification microstructure, microstructure 
thermal stability and temperature-dependent strengthening mecha -
nisms were not discussed in detail. As a result, systematic investigations 
are still expected on the association of the mechanical performance with 
the microstructure features of the L-PBF Al matrix composites at the 
elevated temperatures. 
In this work, TiB
2 
particles were pre-alloyed into an Al2618 matrix 
via in-situ reaction and atomized as TiB
2
/Al2618 composite powders for 
AM by L-PBF. The TiB
2
/Al2618 composite presented excellent print -
ability in a wide processing window. With optimized processing pa -
rameters, the as-printed composite exhibited a significantly refined 
microstructure with ultrafine grains and intermetallic cellular network, 
which also showed excellent thermal stability under long-time thermal 
exposure. The ultrafine microstructural L-PBF composite presented 
superior high-temperature strength and strength retention after long- 
time thermal exposure. The solidification behavior, microstructure sta -
bility and high-temperature strengthening mechanisms were investi -
gated in detail. This work inspires the design and fabrication of 
advanced high-temperature mechanical performance Al matrix com -
posites by AM for potential industrial applications serving at elevated 
temperatures. 
2. Material and methods 
2.1. Material and L-PBF process 
An Al – Cu – Mg – Fe – Ni (Al2618) alloy reinforced with TiB
2 
particles 
was fabricated via the in-situ mixed salt-metal reaction, as described in 
previous work [ 34 ]. The composite ingot was subsequently 
gas-atomized into TiB
2
-decorated composite feedstock powders. Pow -
ders ranging from 15 to 53 μ m in size were selected for the following 
L-PBF processing as Fig. 1 (a). The L-PBF was conducted by a TruPrint 
1000 metal 3D-printing machine equipped with a 200 W fiber laser with 
a spot size of 30 μ m under a flowing argon (Ar) atmosphere. The pro -
portion of oxygen was maintained below 0.02 vol %. The laser scanning 
direction was alternated by 67
◦
between the consecutive layers without 
the preheating of the Al baseplate. The hatching distance and the layer 
thickness were set as 100 μ m and 30 μ m, respectively. The composite 
showed excellent L-PBF printability in a wide processing window 
(scanning power in 90~170 W; scanning velocity in 1000~1400 mm/s) 
with good surface quality and without cracking as shown in Fig. A1(a) . 
Hardness tests were conducted on the Vickers hardness testing machine 
(EZ-mat, CARAT 930) with a load of 10 kg and a dwell time of 15 s on 
nine test points. The density of the samples was measured using the 
Archimedes drainage method. The scanning power and velocity were 
optimized as 170 W and 1000 mm/s, respectively ( Fig. A1(b, c) ). With 
the optimized L-PBF processing parameters, crack free and near fully 
dense bulk samples (a relative density of ~98.5 %) were built up for 
further mechanical property tests and microstructure characterizations.

5. Conclusions 
An in-situ TiB
2 
particle reinforced Al – Cu – Mg – Fe – Ni (Al2618) was 
fabricated and additive manufactured by L-PBF, showing superior me -
chanical performance at elevated temperatures. The correlation be -
tween the enhanced high-temperature mechanical performance and 
microstructure was systematically investigated. The main conclusions 
are drawn as follows: 
The TiB
2
/Al2618 composite presents good printability in a wide 
processing window and a high relative density without cracking. The as- 
printed composite exhibits a fully equiaxed grain structure with a very 
fine grain size of ~1 μ m in average, owing to the inoculation by TiB
2 
particles and the rapid solidification by L-PBF. Meanwhile, the in -
termetallics are significantly refined as an ultrafine cellular structure in 
Al matrix. An excellent microstructure stability is presented for the L- 
PBF TiB
2
/Al2618 composite after long-time heat exposure. The grain 
size remains almost unchanged exposed at 190 
◦
C and slightly gets 
coarsening at 300 
◦
C, attributed to Zener pinning effect from TiB
2 
par -
ticles and the refined intermetallics. The ultrafine intermetallics in Al 
matrix, especially the Fe, Ni-rich phases, show remarkable coarsening 
resistance under a thermal exposure of 150 h up to 300 
◦
C, despite the 
coarsening of Cu, Mg-rich phase. 
The L-PBF TiB
2
/Al2618 composite exhibits a much superior high- 
temperature strength up to 300 
◦
C compared to the typical L-PBF 
Al – Si alloys and wrought Al alloys, which is advantageous over the L-
