Abstract
The microstructure and mechanical properties of ethylene cracking furnace tube(HPNb alloy) are
investigated by scanning electronic microscopy(SEM), tensile tests and Charpy impact tests at room
temperature, tensile tests and creep tests at high temperature in this paper. The primary carbides of
HPNb alloy coarsened and formed a continuous network after aﬁve-year service. Furthermore, a lot
of ﬁne secondary carbides precipitated in the dendrite interior. The primary carbides M
7C3 and NbC
transformed into M23C6 and G phase after service, respectively. The furnace tube after service exhibits
higher yield strength, lower tensile strength, worse ductility and toughness than as-cast tube at room
temperature. At high temperature, the tensile strength and yield strength of service tube are higher
than as-cast tube, but its tensile elongation is lower. The creep strength of HPNb alloy at high
temperature decreases after aﬁve-year service. Both microstructure and mechanical properties of
ethylene cracking furnace tube have deteriorated after aﬁve-year service.
1. Introduction
The ethylene cracking furnace, which is welded by centrifugal casting heat-resistant steel tubes, is the critical
component in petrochemical system. Naphtha and ethane feed to produce ethylene are almost exclusively
carried out in steam crackers operating around 1173 K∼1373 K [1]. Hence, the materials of ethylene cracking
furnace tube should exhibit excellent creep strength, superior oxidation and carburization resistance at high
temperature. HPNb alloy is suitable to be used in cracking furnaces for its outstanding high-temperature
strength and good corrosion resistance. The ethylene cracking furnace tubes are designed for a life time of
100 000 h (11.4 years) of service at the operating temperature[2]. Overheating [3], oxidization and carburization
[1, 4– 7], creep and fatigue[8] contribute to a premature failure of ethylene cracking furnace tube.
Microstructure degradation and creep ductility exhaustion are considered to be the typical failures in ethylene
cracking furnace tubes[9].
Due to prolonged exposure to high temperature(1173 K∼1373 K), the microstructure and mechanical
properties of the materials are subjected to degradation. The primary eutectic carbides of M
7C3 and NbC will
transform into M23C6 and G phase after a long-term high-temperature exposure[10– 12]. Besides the carbide
transformation, the mechanical properties also vary greatly after service[3, 13, 14]. Nakarin Srisuwan[15]
research shows that the ductility of the ethylene cracking furnace tube decreases and the hardness increases after
aging. The high temperature creep strength decreases after a long-term high-temperature service according to M
N Ilman [13], Ashok Kumar Ray[16] and Antonello Alvino[17]. The microstructure and mechanical properties
degraded after service, which could lead to a premature failure of ethylene cracking furnace. So the study of
ethylene cracking furnace tube degradation after service is important for assessing the remaining life of ethylene
cracking furnace and ensure continued safe operation.

2. Experimental
The specimens were cut from an ethylene cracking furnace tube after aﬁve-year service. The ethylene cracking
furnace tubes were exposed at 1223 K– 1373 K with aﬂowing naphtha at a total pressure close to 0.5 MPa. The
chemical composition of two type tubes is listed in table1, which is tested by Optical Emission Spectrometer(Q8
MAGELLAN).
The microstructure was characterized by scanning electron microscopy(SEM, SUPARR 55). The specimens
were grounded with silicon carbide papers from 200 to 2000 grit and polished on woollen cloth with 1μm
diamond paste. The specimens which are used for observation of microstructure were electrolytically etched in a
solution of oxalic acid(wt10%) at 5 V, and 3 A for 10 s. Electron back-scattered diffraction(EBSD) analyses were
performed in order to identify the phases present in the service tube. After polishing with diamond paste down
to 1 μm, the specimens were further polished with a 0.03μm colloidal silica suspension for 6 h. The EBSD
measurements were carried out with aﬁeld emission gun scanning electron microscope operating at 20 kV and a
working distance of 13 mm. The step size was 0.5μm.
Tensile tests of standard specimens(ISO 6892-1:2009 MOD) with a gauge length of 35 mm were carried out
at room temperature at a strain rate of 2.5×10
−4 s−1. Tensile tests of standard specimens(ISO 6892-2:2011
MOD) at elevated temperature were carried out at 1173 K at a strain rate of 0.1 min−1. To evaluate the impact
toughness, the absorption energy of the standard specimens(ISO 128-1:2006 MOD,
55 mm×10 mm×5m m) with a V-notch were measured at room temperature by using the Charpy impact
testing equipment at an input energy of 30 J. To evaluate the creep strength at high-temperature, uniaxial creep
testing in tension of standard specimens(ISO 204:2009 MOD) was conducted at 1173 K, 55 MPa and 1273 K,
25 MPa.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
Figure 1 shows the microstructure of as-cast tube and service tube. The microstructure of as-cast HPNb alloy
consists of a cellularγ-matrix and the skeleton shape eutectic carbide: eutectic carbides on the dendrite
boundaries and γ-matrix in grains, which is a typical microstructure of as-cast materials of alloys with high
chromium and nickel contents[4, 18] (ﬁgure 1(a)). After service, there are manyﬁne secondary carbides
precipitated in dendrite interior. The primary carbides coarsened and formed a continuous network in the
furnace tube after service(ﬁgure 1(b)). The primary carbides width is about 5μm, and the secondary carbides
with a mean size is approximately 1μm and disperse rather homogenously in dendrite interior. It is a sign of
early creep damage that some creep voids appeared at the boundary of the matrix and eutectic carbide.
Meanwhile, the condition of carbide phases after aﬁve-year service also varies. The eutectic chromium carbide
of M
7C3 with HCP crystalline structure partly transforms to M23C6 with FCC structure(ﬁgure 1(c)). At the
boundary of the matrix phase and eutectic chromium carbides, G phases are being formed in the HPNb alloy
after a long-term service(ﬁgure 1(c)). The precipitated carbides are M23C6 dispersed in the dendrite interior
(ﬁgure 1(c)). There is no M7C3 and NbC type carbides in the tube after aﬁve-year service. The primary carbides
coarsened and formed a continuous network, secondary carbides precipitated which caused the deterioration of
HPNb alloy microstructure after service.
3.2. Tensile test and Charpy impact test at room temperature
The mechanical properties measured by tensile test and Charpy impact test at room temperature are
summarized in table2. The service tube tensile tests demonstrate that the tensile strength decreased from
510 MPa to 380 MPa, the yield strength increased from 240 MPa to 310 MPa and the elongation decreased from
18.5% to 3.0%. The absorption energy reduced from 21 J cm
−2 to 4 J cm−2. The tensile strength, elongation and
absorption energy decreased after service. A great deal ofﬁne secondary carbide precipitated in in the grain
interior. The service tube exhibited a higher yield strength owing to the high-density precipitates ofﬁne
secondary carbides in the grain interior. Meanwhile, since the primary carbides coarsened and formed a
continuous network at grain boundary, so the tensile strength decreases after service. The primary carbide

3.3. Tensile test at elevated temperature
Figure 2 shows the stress-strain curves of specimens of as-cast tube and service tube at 1173 K. The tensile
strength and yield strength of as-cast tube are 120 MPa and 108 MPa, which are below the strength of the service
tube (200 MPa and 139 MPa). The elongation of as-cast tube(42.4%) is higher than the service tube(25.4%).
The strength of service tube increased and the elongation decreased at high temperature. The coarser primary
carbide at grain boundary andﬁne secondary carbide in the grain as the high-temperature reinforce phase
improve the tensile strength and yield strength of service tube at elevated temperature. But the bigger and more
carbides could reduce the ductility of service tube at high temperature. So the ductility of HPNb alloy at high
temperature is very low. The primary carbides coarseness and secondary carbides precipitation have a signiﬁcant
effect on the room temperature and high temperature strength of the HPNb alloy, which could reduce the room
temperature strength and improve the elevated temperature strength.
3.4. Creep test at elevated temperature
Figure 3 shows the creep strength of HPNb alloy. The creep strength of as-cast tube is higher than the minimum
rupture strength of HPNb alloy, however the creep strength of service tube at 1173 K and 1273 K are all below it
(ﬁgure 3(a)). Typical creep strain and creep strain rate curves of as-cast tube and service tube at 1173 K, 1273 K
are respectively plotted inﬁgures 3(b) and(c). The as-cast tube and service tube exhibit an extended secondary or
steady creep with signiﬁcantly lower minimum creep rates inﬁgures 3(b) and (c). The secondary creep life in
service tube accounted for more than 70 pct of its total creep life. These clearly show that the limited extent of
primary creep and the protracted secondary stage. The creep rupture time of as-cast tube is longer than service
tube at the 1173 K and 1273 K, though the stress of as-cast tube is higher than service tube. The results of these
two tests show that the creep strength of the furnace tube decreases after a long-term service. Consequently, the
creep strength of as-cast HPNb alloy is higher than service tube at high temperature. Figure3(c) shows that the
minimum creep rate of service tube is less than as-cast tube, but the rupture time is shorter. It’s suggests that the
service tube is apt to sudden failure at service.
These results show that the creep strength of service tube at high temperature is lower than as-cast tube.
That’s because of the primary carbides of as-cast tube are skeleton shape at the grain boundary, and the primary
carbides of service tube coarsened and formed a continuous network. The creep voids appeared in the service
tube also reduced the creep strength. Because of the grain boundary twists and turns can prevent grain boundary
slip and crack propagation, the skeleton shape carbide could improve the creep rupture strength. In generally,
when the grain boundary carbide coverage increases, the creep strength increases, but after formed a continuous
network at grain boundary, the carbides are beneﬁcial to crack propagation and plays a negative effect on the
creep strength. Therefore, there are numerous carbides at grain boundary of the service tube, but its creep
strength is still lower than that of as-cast tube.
From above results, it leads to the ductility and toughness decrease and material embrittlement that the
primary carbides of HPNb alloy coarsened and formed a continuous network. There are many creep voids in the
ethylene cracking furnace tube after aﬁve-year service, which is a sign of early creep damage. The material

4. Conclusion
The microstructure evolution and mechanical properties degradation of ethylene cracking furnace tube after a
ﬁve-year service were studied in this research. The key results are summarized as follows:
(1) The primary carbides of HPNb alloy tube after a ﬁve-year service coarsened and formed a continuous
network, a lot ofﬁne secondary carbides precipitated in the dendrite interior. Meanwhile, the primary
carbide M
7C3 and NbC transformed into M23C6 and G phase, respectively.
(2) The ductility and toughness of HPNb alloy at room temperature are reduced and the furnace tubes become
brittle after a long-term service.
(3) For HPNb alloy tube, the tensile and yield strength increased and the ductility decreased at high temperature
after a long-term service.
(4) The high temperature creep strength of HPNb alloy tube after aﬁve-year service is drastically reduced.
