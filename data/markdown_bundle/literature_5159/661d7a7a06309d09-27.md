ABSTRACT
Most refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are susceptible to brittle fracture when subjected to tensile loading. 
The TiZrHfNb RHEA and its derivative possess good tensile ductility, but still encounter issues with low yield 
strength. In this study, we propose an Oxygen-Nitrogen Synergistic Effect (ONSE) for reinforcing the TiZrHfNb 
RHEA. Oxygen atoms create local chemical order (LCO), seeding more dislocation nucleation sites, promoting 
dislocation multiplication and ensuring uniform deformation. Nitrogen atoms induce lattice distortion, hinder 
dislocation motion, and generate a significant solid solution strengthening effect. The introduction of ONSE 
results in high-density mobile dislocations distributed throughout the alloy, as opposed to the conventional belief 
that dislocations are limited to narrow slip bands by LCOs. The model alloy (TiZrHfNb)

1. Introduction
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), which include refractory 
elements such as W, Ta, Mo, Nb, and Hf, demonstrate exceptional 
resistance to softening and phase stability at high temperatures, making 
them promising candidates for aerospace, nuclear reactor, and other 
high-stress applications [ 1 ]. However, RHEAs still face significant 
challenges, primarily due to their low strength or inherent brittleness. 
The TiZrHfNb RHEA, known for its stable body-centered-cubic (BCC) 
structure [ 2 ], has received attention for its superior tensile ductility [ 1 ,
3 ], but has a low yield strength below 1 GPa. Improving the yield 
strength of the TiZrHfNb RHEA continues to be a significant challenge in 
the field.
Given the inherent high melting point, sluggish atomic diffusion ef -
fect, and pronounced lattice distortion characteristics of RHEAs, these 
systems inherently develop multiscale chemical composition fluctua -
tions that create localized strain gradients [ 4 ]. Such chemical hetero -
geneities profoundly influence dislocation dynamics through solute 
interaction drag and elastic modulus mismatch effects, ultimately gov -
erning plastic deformation mechanisms. Bu et al. observed dislocation 
pinning induced by local chemical fluctuations in the TiZrHfNb RHEA 
using in-situ transmission electron microscopy (TEM) [ 5 ]. Pozuelo et al. 
demonstrated that chemical composition is indeed the best quantitative 
predictor of yield strength in the NbMoTaW RHEA. The yield strength in 
the W-rich micropillar regions (~ 1500 MPa) is 2.1 times higher than 
that in the Nb-rich regions (~ 700 MPa) [ 6 ]. Therefore, the

Based on the study of the intrinsic local chemical fluctuations of 
RHEAs, the deliberate doping of small-sized interstitial atoms to engi -
neer local chemical order (LCO) provides a new approach to overcoming 
the strength-ductility trade-off in alloys. These interstitial atoms typi -
cally exist in crystal structures in two forms: localized ordering and 
random distribution [ 7 ]. The different forms of distributions have 
varying impacts on the alloy properties. Lei et al. introduced 2.0 at. % 
nitrogen atoms into TiZrHfNb RHEA, and no significant ordered struc -
ture was formed. The yield strength increased by 73.3 %, but at the cost 
of ductility. In contrast, the introduction of an equivalent proportion of 
oxygen atoms formed the (Ti, Zr, O)-based LCO, which led to a 95.2 % 
improvement in the alloy ’ s ductility. However, the enhancement in 
yield strength was less significant than that achieved with nitrogen [ 7 ]. 
The presence of LCO has a significant positive effect on ductility, as the 
LCO-dislocation coupling increases the local strain during tensile 
deformation [ 8 ]. This mechanism promotes dislocation interactions and 
multiplication, thereby improving plasticity [ 9 ]. Nevertheless, its effect 
on strength enhancement is relatively limited.
To address the inherent limitations of the single-type interstitial 
atom doping strategy, we have innovatively proposed the Oxygen- 
Nitrogen Synergistic Effect (ONSE) and successfully applied it to 
TiZrHfNb RHEA. Experimental results confirm that oxygen atoms form 
the LCO structure, providing low-energy nucleation sites for dislocations 
on the slip plane, which in turn promotes dislocation multiplication. 
Meanwhile, the strong lattice distortion effect induced by nitrogen 
atoms effectively suppresses long-range dislocation slip, leading to an 
enhancement in strength.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructures
The structure of the as-cast samples was characterized using EPMA, 
EBSD, and XRD, which confirmed that the alloy system maintained a 
single-phase BCC structure without any secondary phase precipitation 
( Fig. 1 , Fig. S1 in supplementary materials). It is reasonable to speculate 
that the nitrogen and oxygen atoms exist as interstitial solute atoms of 
the BCC lattice. In Fig. S1(b), the diffraction peak of the NO-2 alloy shifts 
towards lower angles, indicating that the introduction of interstitial 
atoms causes more significant lattice distortion and an increase in the 
lattice constant. Fig. 1 (c-d) show the inverse pole figures (IPF) of NO- 
0 and NO-2 alloys in their as-cast state. The NO-0 alloy exhibits coarse 
grains with an average size of approximately 521.4 μ m, while the NO-2 
alloy features equiaxed grains of around 277.1 μ m with random orien -
tations and no specific texture detected. This grain-size refinement 
contributes to the strength increase [ 10 ], as evidenced by subsequent 
tensile test results.
The corresponding WDS mapping (Figs. S2, S3 in supplementary 
materials) illustrated significant compositional segregation, i.e., areas 
with dark contrast indicate elements with lower atomic numbers and 
vice versa. The segregation observed in the NO-2 alloy was more pro -
nounced than that in the NO-0 alloy, possibly due to the presence of 
nitrogen and oxygen interstitial atoms. Furthermore, WDS point scan -
ning (Table S1 in supplementary materials) reveals that Hf and Nb el -
ements exhibit higher concentrations in the bright contrast dendritic 
areas, while Ti and Zr are more prominent in the dark contrast inter -
dendritic regions. This observation supports the notion that the LCO in 
TiZrHfNb-based RHEAs arises from the segregation of Ti and Zr elements 
[ 7 ]. The density functional theory (DFT)-based Monte Carlo simulation 
of the NO-0 alloy confirms formation of the Ti-Zr and Hf-Nb pairs is 
favored [ 5 ], which is consistent with the experimental observation. Such 
(Ti, Zr)-rich and (Hf, Nb)-rich regions could alter local generalized 
stacking fault energy and then exert a significant impact on the dislo -
cation behavior and mechanical properties. To verify whether phase 
separation occurs between dendritic and interdendritic regions in the 
alloy, high-resolution EBSD characterization was performed on multiple 
regions of the NO-2 alloy. The results indicate that all analyzed areas 
exhibit a clearly single-phase BCC structure, with no evidence of phase 
separation detected. The consistency of phase composition between 
dendritic and interdendritic regions is further confirmed as shown in 
Fig. S4 (supplementary materials).

4. Discussion
4.1. The impact of LCO on deformation behavior
To understand the ductilization mechanism of the NO-2 alloy, 
dislocation configurations were captured in the samples at the strain of 2 
%, 4 %, and fracture, as shown in Fig. 6 . In the NO-2 alloy, we observed 
that the primary deformation mechanism involves planar-slips, with 
some contribution from local double cross-slips. Under a strain of 2 %, 
the dislocation density remains relatively low, as shown in Fig. 6 (a). The 
TEM observations reveal the presence of dislocation pinning and 
numerous insoluble spots with an average size of 1 – 3 nm in the alloy. As 
previously mentioned, the existence of LCO in the NO-2 alloy leads to 
uneven element distribution and atomic radius differences, which in 
turn induce local lattice strain fluctuations. These fluctuations cause 
significant lattice distortions that effectively increase lattice friction, 
thereby enhancing the alloy ’ s strength [ 5 ]. Additionally, the presence of 
a large number of insoluble spots in the alloy, a phenomenon commonly 
observed in oxygen-containing titanium alloys [ 26 , 27 ], serves as 
dislocation-nucleation sites formed by the LCO during deformation.
When the strain reaches 4 %, the interplay between dislocations and 
local chemical fluctuations induces a second-order pinning effect, i.e., 
the formation of a large number of dislocation loops within the slip 
bands, which are usually perceived as an outcome of cross-slip. The LCO 
is one of the critical factors activating cross-slips in the current alloy, 
which has also been reported in previous HEAs [ 7 , 28 ]. In terms of 
overall morphology, although most dislocations slide in the same di -
rection, their sliding trajectories are also wavy at the nanoscale, 
attributed to local double cross-slips. This phenomenon transfers 
planar-slips to various atomic planes, effectively preventing dislocation 
entanglement and stress concentration on the plane slip, and reducing 
the dislocation density on a certain atomic plane, which is beneficial for 
the growth and expansion of dislocation loops [ 29 ].
After tensile fracture, the TEM analysis revealed that ONSE signifi -
cantly enhanced the dislocation density in the NO-2 alloy and triggered 
the synergistic activation of secondary slip systems to coordinate the 
plastic flow. The staggered dislocation forest formed in the deformation 
process presents a band-like distribution, and the formation of high- 
density short dislocations within it unveiled two key mechanisms 
[ Fig. 6 (f)]: (1) Dislocation regulation by LCO: The LCO regions, through 
modulus mismatch effects, provide low-energy barrier nucleation sites 
for dislocations on the slip planes, thereby promoting dislocation pro -
liferation; (2) Lattice distortion pinning effect: The strong lattice 
distortion induced by solute atoms generates an elastic stress field, 
hindering the long-range slip of dislocations and leading to an increased 
dislocation storage density. This LCO-mediated dynamic evolution of 
dislocations triggers the “ deformation delocalization ” phenomenon. 
After yielding, high-density mobile dislocations efficiently propagated 
within the local strain region and quickly transferred to the undeformed 
areas. This finding breaks through the cognitive framework of tradi -
tional theories that dislocation motion is limited to narrow slip bands 
only, and reveals a more complex dislocation behavior with profound 
implications for the alloy ’ s plasticity. A comparative experiment was 
conducted on the fractured NO-0 alloy, with the results presented in 
Fig. S6 of the supplementary materials. Plastic deformation in the NO- 
0 alloy primarily proceeds via planar-slip, with dislocation motion 
tending to accumulate within dislocation bands. No obvious “ deforma -
tion delocalization ” phenomenon was observed between the intersecting 
dislocation bands, and the dislocation density was relatively low. These 
findings further support the successful implementation of ONSE in the 
NO-2 alloy.
The slip mechanism utilized in our design not only enhances strength 
but also preserves plasticity by leveraging the typically less favored 
planar sliding mode. A key factor contributing to the effectiveness of our 
strategy is the incorporation of suitable interstitial atoms, which inte -
grate LCO with solid-solution strengthening. The formation of a wide 
range of LCO regions due to notable differences in chemical affinity 
among the constituent elements. Additionally, the severe atomic-size 
mismatch induces significant lattice distortions when LCO dissociates 
into random solutions. As dislocations propagate through these distorted 
regions, the intensified lattice strain fields generate fluctuating strain 
barriers that effectively impede dislocation motion. The plastic- 
deformation mechanism of the NO-2 alloy is illustrated in Fig. 7 .

The above results indicate that LCO promotes planar-slip in the alloy. 
On one hand, this is due to the slip-plane softening effect caused by the 
localized disruption of the LCO [ 30 ]; on the other hand, it may be closely 
linked to the interstitial atom occupation mechanism. In each BCC unit 
cell, the radius (R) of the tetrahedral-site (~0.291 R) being approxi -
mately twice that of the octahedral-site (~0.155 R). From the crystal 
structure model of the tetrahedral and octahedral interstices in the BCC 
lattice [ 31 ], it is determined that the interstitial atoms in NO-2 alloy 
occupy the octahedral-site [ Fig. 4 (e, f)], rather than the larger 
tetrahedral-site. This is primarily because the octahedral-site is coordi -
nated by only two nearest-neighbor host atoms, leading to a lower strain 
energy from atomic displacement compared to the tetrahedral-site, 
which is coordinated by four nearest-neighbor host atoms [ 31 ]. Never -
theless, it is precisely because the octahedral interstitial site is smaller 
that it may cause more pronounced lattice distortion. The existing 
research suggests that at 2.0 at. % oxygen doping, oxygen atoms occupy 
both tetrahedral and octahedral interstitial sites, forming ordered oxy -
gen complexes that enhance strength and plasticity [ 7 ]. Liu et al. found 
that in a system with 12 at. % oxygen, oxygen atoms predominantly 
occupy tetrahedral interstitial sites [ 31 ]. This suggests a significant 
concentration-dependent pattern of interstitial site occupancy, where 
oxygen atoms may dynamically migrate from octahedral to tetrahedral 
interstitial sites along the concentration gradient. Whether this migra -
tion process serves as a key controlling factor for the synergistic 
enhancement of strength and plasticity remains to be further explored. 
This study clarifies that in the low-oxygen system of NO-2 alloy, the 
stable occupancy of octahedral-site by oxygen atoms significantly pro -
motes dislocation planar-slip behavior, providing a new theoretical 
perspective for understanding the structure-activity relationship be -
tween the spatial distribution of interstitial atoms and plastic deforma -
tion mechanisms.

5. Conclusions
This study presents an innovative Oxygen-Nitrogen Synergistic Effect 
for significantly enhancing the yield strength of RHEAs, as demonstrated 
through its implementation in the (TiZrHfNb)
98

1.5
O
0.5 
RHEA. A syn -
ergistic effect of solid-solution strengthening from nitrogen/oxygen and 
ductilization from ordered oxygen was obtained. Several key conclu -
sions were drawn from the study: 
(1) The study reveals the unique atomic distribution pattern induced 
by ONSE. In the as-cast NO-2 alloy with a single-phase BCC 
structure, oxygen atoms are ordered in (Ti, Zr)-rich regions, 
preferentially occupying octahedral interstitial sites and forming 
a high-density LCO structure (1920.71 nm
-3
) with a diameter 
range of 1.5 to 3.2 nm. In contrast, nitrogen atoms exhibit a 
random distribution, thereby establishing an ordered-disordered 
coupling mechanism.
(2) PALS demonstrates the correlation between ONSE and the evo -
lution of internal defects in the alloy. Compared to the NO- 
0 alloy, the characteristic lifetime ( τ
2
) of positrons is signifi -
cantly shorter in the NO-2 alloy. This reduction is likely due to the 
capture of interstitial atoms by vacancy-type defects. Moreover, 
the introduction of interstitial atoms reduces the vacancy for -
mation energy, resulting in an increased vacancy concentration.
(3) ONSE exerts a dual regulatory effect on dislocation behavior. The 
LCO region provides low-energy barrier nucleation sites for dis -
locations on the slip plane, thereby promoting dislocation 
multiplication. After fracture, the dislocation density of the NO-2 
alloy is approximately twice that of the NO-0 alloy. The intense 
lattice distortion caused by interstitial solute atoms impedes the 
long-range motion of dislocations, leading to an enhancement in 
strength.
(4) The NO-2 alloy exhibits a significant synergy between strength 
and ductility. The yield strength of 1412.9 ± 13.5 MPa was 
achieved, representing a 92 % improvement over the NO- 
0 RHEA, while maintaining a fracture elongation of 10 %. This 
improvement can be attributed to the solid-solution strength -
ening effect of interstitial atoms and the strong lattice distortion 
effect when the LCO dissociates towards a random solution.
(5) Different alloy systems exhibit significant variations in their 
ability to accommodate interstitial atoms, which may result in a 
narrowed solubility window for oxygen/nitrogen, potentially 
leading to element segregation or instability of ordered struc -
tures. Therefore, it is necessary to rationally select and optimize 
matrix compositions based on the interaction characteristics 
among elements, in order to develop more suitable alloy systems 
for ONSE.
