Formation, corrosion behavior, and mechanical properties of
bulk glassy Zr–Al–Co–Nb alloys
Shujie Pang, Tao Zhang, Katsuhiko Asami, and Akihisa Inoue
Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, J apan
(Received 29 December 2002; accepted 14 April 2003)
Bulk glassy Zr 55Al20−xCo25Nbx (x /H115050 to 5 at.%) alloys with critical diameters of
2.5–5 mm and supercooled liquid region of 48–75 K were produced by copper-m old
casting. These alloys were spontaneously passivated in 3 mass% NaCl and 1 N H2SO4
solutions. The addition of Nb to the alloy enhanced the pitting corrosion r esistance in
the NaCl solution and enabled the open-circuit potential in the H 2SO4 solution. Zr- and
Al-enriched passive films formed on the bulk glassy Zr–Al–Co–Nb alloys ex posed to
air and on those immersed in the solutions. The increase in Nb content of the alloys
resulted in an increase in Zr and Nb concentrations and a decrease in Al conc entration
of the passive film. The Vickers hardness, Young’s modulus, compressive f racture
strength, fracture strain, and plastic strain of the bulk glassy Zr–Al–Co –Nb alloys were
550, 98 GPa, 2100 MPa, 2.9%, and 0.9%, respectively.
I. INTRODUCTION
Much attention has recently been paid to Zr-based
bulk glassy alloys such as Zr–Al–Ni, 1 Zr–Al–Ni–Cu,2
Zr–(Ti, Nb)–Al–Ni–Cu, 3 Zr–Ti–Be–Ni–Cu, 4 Zr–(Pd,
Ta)–Al–Ni–Cu,5 and Zr–Al–Be–Ni–Cu6 systems, as they
possess a large supercooled liquid region prior to crys-
tallization and good mechanical properties. Zr-based
bulk glassy alloys have been used as sporting-goods ma-
terials and connecting tubes for optical communication
fibers.
7–10 Because of their easy formability in the su-
percooled liquid region, high strength, and low Young’s
modulus, Zr-based bulk glassy alloys are expected to
have medical applications as biomaterials. The corrosion
characteristics of biomaterials are important because the
corrosion in body or mouth may cause health hazards. It
has been demonstrated that the Zr–Al–Ni–Cu bulk glassy
alloys with large supercooled liquid region exhibit good
corrosion resistance in a variety of environments.
11–14
However, nickel is known to be highly toxic/allergic to
cellular metabolism.
15 Considering chemical and me-
chanical biocompatibility, Zr-based bulk glassy alloys
with less hazardous elements are required. Recently, the
Inoue group in Sendai has developed a nickel-free bulk
glassy Zr–Al–Co ternary alloy with a large supercooled
liquid region prior to crystallization and good mechanical
properties.
16 For medical and other applications, the cor-
rosion resistance of the glassy Zr–Al–Co alloy needs to
be evaluated and further improved by substitution of
strongly passivating elements for aluminum, which is the
weakest constituent from the view of corrosion. This pa-
per presents the corrosion behavior of the bulk glassy
Zr
55Al20Co25 alloy and the effects of additional elements
on the glass-forming ability, thermal properties, corro-
sion resistance, and mechanical properties.
II. EXPERIMENTAL
Master alloys with nominal compositions of
Zr55Al20−xCo25(Nb, Ti, or Ta) x (x /H115050, 2.5, 5, 7.5, and
10 at.%) were prepared by arc melting mixtures of the
pure metals in an argon atmosphere. From the alloy in-
gots, ribbon samples with a cross section of 0.02×1m m
2
were fabricated by melt spinning. Bulk alloys in a rod
form with a length of about 40 mm and diameters up to
6 mm were prepared by copper-mold casting. The struc-
ture of the specimens was examined by x-ray diffraction
(XRD) using Cu K
/H9251radiation at tube voltage of 40 kV
and tube current of 150 mA. The thermal stability asso-
ciated with glass transition, supercooled liquid, and crys-
tallization for the glassy alloys was investigated by
differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a heating rate
of 0.67 K/s. The temperature and heat release of the
calorimeter were calibrated within ±0.2 K and ±0.1 J/g
by melting indium.
For corrosion tests, bulk glassy alloys in a rod form with
a diameter of 2.5 mm were used. Prior to corrosion tests,
the specimens were mechanically polished in cyclohex-
ane with silicon carbide paper up to grit 2000, degreased
in acetone, washed in distilled water, dried in air, and
further exposed to air for 24 h for good reproducibility.
Electrolytes of 3 mass% NaCl an d1NH
2SO4 solutions
open to air were used at room temperature (about 298 K).

IV. CONCLUSIONS
Glassy alloys with a supercooled liquid region
/H9004Tx /H11505Tx − Tg of 37 –75 K were formed in the
Zr55Al20−xCo25Nbx (x /H115050 to 10 at.%) system by melt
spinning. The Zr 55Al20−xCo25Nbx (x /H115050 to 5 at.%)
alloys can be synthesized in a bulk glassy form with
critical diameters of 2.5 –5 mm by copper-mold casting,
although the glass-forming ability and thermal stability
exhibit a slight decrease with addition of Nb. The addi-
tion of Ti or Ta to the Zr
55Al20Co25 alloy for replacing a
portion of Al is detrimental for glass formation. Bulk
glassy Zr
55Al20−xCo25Nbx (x /H115050 to 5 at.%) alloys are
spontaneously passivated in 3 mass% NaCl and 1 N
H
2SO4 solutions. The addition of Nb enhances pitting
corrosion resistance in the NaCl solution and enables the
open-circuit potential in the H
2SO4 solution. A Zr- and
Al-enriched passive film is formed on the bulk glassy
Zr–Al–Co–Nb alloys by air exposure and by immersion
in the solutions and is responsible for the high corro-
sion resistance of the alloys. The increase in Nb content
of the alloys results in an increase in Zr and Nb concen-
trations and a decrease in Al concentration of the passive
film and therefore improves the protective nature of the
