HIGH-TEMPERATURE DEFORMATION OF ALLOYS OF THE
MULTICOMPONENT Nb – Si – X (X = Mo, Ti, Hf, Zr, Cr, Al) SYSTEM
AFTER HIP AND AFTER INDUCTION MELTING IN THE MODE
OF DIRECTED CRYSTALLIZATION
T. S. Stroganova, 1 M. I. Karpov, 1 D. V . Prokhorov,1 and A. I. Logacheva 2
Translated from Metallovedenie i T ermicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 1, pp. 26 – 30, January, 2020.
The results of creep tests in the bending mode at 1150 – 1200°C for the Nb – Si – X alloy (X = Mo, Ti, Hf, Zr,
Cr, Al) after hot isostatic pressing and after induction melting in the mode of directed crystallization are ana -
lyzed. It was shown that the creep rate of powder alloys is an order of magnitude higher than the creep rate of
an alloy with a directed structure, which is related to the microstructural features of the alloy and the type of
silicide formed in its structure. In all the alloys tested, the short-term bending strength at 20°C weakly depends
on their composition and method of fabrication, while at 1300°C the alloy with a directed structure has the
highest strength.
Key words: eutectic alloys of the Nb – Si system; hot isostatic pressing; directed crystallization;
strength; creep.
INTRODUCTION
Multicomponent alloys based on the Nb – Si system
have long been considered as substitutes for heat-resistant
nickel alloys due to their lower density by 10% and higher
strength at temperatures above 1150°C. These alloys are
most commonly fabricated by metallurgical smelting me -
thods, such as high-temperature arc melting, electron beam
melting, and induction melting in the mode of directed crys -
tallization. However, high melting points and the active in -
teraction of the melt with casting molds complicate the pro -
cess of their fabrication. As a result, powder metallurgy
methods are considered as an alternative, in which the fusion
temperatures of these alloys are much lower.
A detailed review of short-term compressive and tensile
strengths for alloys of the Nb – Si system with equiaxed and
directed structures is presented in [1]. It was shown that at
room and medium temperatures, the tensile strengths of di -
rected Nb – Si composites significantly exceed the strength
of equiaxed composites. With the temperature increase, the
difference is leveled and at temperatures above 1200°C, the
type of structure does not affect the strength of the alloy. The
compressive ultimate strengths of equiaxed and directed
composites slightly differ, and at 20°C they exceed the ten -
sile strength by 2.5 times.
In [2] the creep rate was compared in the temperature
range 1200 – 1400°C for a binary alloy Nb – 18 at.% Si, fab -
ricated by the powder metallurgy and directed crystallization
methods. The microstructure of the alloy was a niobium ma -
trix reinforced with Nb
3Si silicide. It was shown that the
steady creep rate of an alloy with a directed structure is three
orders of magnitude lower than that of a powder alloy at
1200°C. The authors suggested that the difference in rates
may be related to microstructural features, namely, the size
of the phases in the powder alloy and the size of the cells in
the cast alloy.
The creep of niobium alloys with directed crystallization
during compression and tensile tests in the temperature range
from 1000°C to 1200°C was studied in [3, 4]. The creep

Al, Mo, and Si, on the creep of materials in comparison with

In contrast to two-component alloys, the creep characte -
ristics of multicomponent alloys vary widely. For example,
the exponent n, which characterizes the sensitivity of the
strain rate to the load, varies from 1.08 to 11 for alloys based
on the Nb – Ti – Hf – Si system, while for two-component
Nb – Si alloys its values are 2 – 3, and for Nb
5Si3 it is close
to 1 [5].
The objective of this work is to evaluate and compare
mechanical properties (short-term strength and high-tempe -
rature creep in bending mode) of an alloy based on the
Nb – Si system fabricated by two methods: hot isostatic
pressing and directed crystallization method.
METHODS OF STUDY
Samples of the Nb – 15Si – 9Mo – 13Ti – 4.5Hf – 4.5Zr –
3.5Cr – 2.5Al (at.%) alloy were fabricated by powder metal -
lurgy (PM) and casting methods [6]. Alloy ingots after
crucibleless induction melting in suspended state (levitation
melting) were used as the ingoing material.
In the first case, the ingots were subjected to bucking in a
steel press die to particles, the sizes of which did not exceed
350 – 400 /c109m, and subsequent grinding in a planetary globe
mill in a high-purity argon atmosphere. The powders fabri-
cated by this method were separated using vibrating screens
into fractions with a particle size of 25 – 50 and > 50 /c109m.
Hot isostatic pressing (HIP) of the powders was carried out
in titanium sleeves at 1400 /c17710°C. After extraction from the
sleeve, the ingots had a diameter of 12 mm and a length of
50 mm.
In the second case, the ingots were remelted in an instal-
lation with induction heating by the Bridgman method with a
crystallization rate of 5 mm /c47min in a high-purity argon at -
mosphere.
From the ingots obtained, transverse and longitudinal
sections for structural studies and mechanical tests were pro -
duced by electrical discharge machining method. Micro -
structural studies, including acquisition of images of objects
in backscattered electrons and x-ray microanalysis, were per -
formed using a Tescan VEGA-II XMU digital electron scan -
ning microscope. X-ray structure analysis was carried out us -
ing a modernized DRON-3.0 diffractometer in molybdenic
K
/c97-radiation.
The ultimate strength of samples 2 /c1803 /c18020 mm in size
was determined during short-term mechanical three-point
bending tests at room temperature and at temperatures up to
1300°C in the vacuum chamber of the INSTRON 1195 in -
stallation with a loading speed of 0.5 mm /c47min.
To determine the creep characteristics, such as the creep
strain rate, the 100-hour creep limit (the value of the stress
causing 1% strain for 100 h in steady creep mode), the expo -
nent n (the indicator of the sensitivity of the creep strain rate
to an increase in stress), a series of tests was performed on al -
loy samples in the temperature range 1150 – 1200°C in an ar -
gon atmosphere. During bending tests, the strain of the sam -
ple was measured under the action of a stepwise increasing
load during equal periods of time (at constant load after 10 h)
at a specified test temperature. According to the data ob -
tained, the bending deflection rate was determined. The cal -
culation of the relative strain rate in the region of steady
creep was carried out according to the method given in [7].

The fabrication of alloy powders by the ingot grinding
method is inevitably associated with the loss of the initial al -
loy composition in the final product. One of the possible rea -
sons for this is a significant difference in the mechanical
properties of the matrix and silicide phases. The particles of
the silicide phase, being more fragile, grind into small frac -
tion fines faster than the particles of the matrix. In this re -
gard, small fraction fines are enriched in silicon and depleted
in niobium.

Short-term bending strength of alloy samples at room

From the data presented in Fig. 4, one can see that with the

The samples of powder alloys demonstrate an increase in
creep rate and in exponent n with an increase in test tempera -
ture (Fig. 5). The samples of the Nb – Si – X (50+) alloy at

Particles of silicides, hafnium oxides, and matrix layers
in the samples of both alloys have the same sizes. Alloys dif -

CONCLUSIONS
1. The structure and mechanical characteristics of experi -
mental alloys based on the Nb – Si system fabricated by vari -
ous methods were studied. These methods were the hot iso -
static pressing of alloy powders of various fractions and the
Bridgman method.
2. The short-term strength of the samples at room tem -
perature weakly depends on their composition and fabrica -
tion method. With the temperature increase above 1150°C,
the strength of alloys fabricated by the HIP method sharply
decreases and at 1300°C is 180 MPa, while the strength of
the alloy after DC is about 820 MPa.
3 . N b–S i– X alloy fabricated from a mixture of pow -
ders with a dispersion of > 50 /c109m at temperatures of 1150
and 1200°C has a lower creep rate than that prepared from a
mixture of powders with a dispersion of 25 – 50 /c109m. Forma-
tion of colonies in the first alloy may contribute to an in -
crease in creep resistance. The creep rate of powder alloys is
an order of magnitude higher than the creep rate of alloys
with directed structure.
