abstract
The circumferential V-type notched specimen has been designed to investigate the effect of multiaxial
stress state on creep rupture behavior of Ni-based single crystal superalloys at 980 1C. The stress on the
minimum net-section is chosen to be 400 MPa. Tests on smooth specimens were also carried out under
400 MPa for comparison. Time to creep fracture was found to be higher in the notched specimens.
Scanning Electrical Microscope (SEM) analysis indicated that rupture initiates at the notch root in the
notched specimen and at the inner in the smooth specimen. Finite element analysis was carried out
to understand the stress distribution and the creep damage evolution under multiaxial stress state.
The morphology the evolution of γ
0-phase proved that directional coarsening was strongly dependent on
the local principal stress ﬁeld. These ﬁndings can be helpful to understand the effect of notch on the
creep rupture behavior and mechanism, especially on the γ0-phase morphology evolution.

1. Introduction
Due to the superior fatigue and creep properties at high
temperatures, Ni-based single crystal superalloys have been widely
used in components for advanced turbine blades. The blade is
subjected predominantly to centrifugal load at high temperature
under the operating conditions. However, the blade consists of
complex structures: airfoil, root and platform. These components
can be subjected to a complex stress system in service. Extensive
literatures have been dedicated to the analysis of the uniaxial
creep behavior of smooth specimens. This does not suf ﬁce for the
description of the component behavior under complex loading.
Therefore, it is necessary to carry out the investigation of the creep
behavior of notched specimens.
Previous studies indicate that notch effects can be both notch
softening [1,2] and notch strengthening [3– 6] depending on three
factors: material properties, notch geometry and testing condi-
tions. A number of studies [5,7– 9] have mainly focused on the
numerical studies on the multi-axial stress effect by simulating a
series of notched specimens with various notch shapes. They
found that the most decisive factor is the degree of triaxiality
due to the presence of the notch. In the above works, the
investigations are rather limited in the description of the rupture
behavior and failure mechanism in notched specimens. Further
study on the notched effect on microstructure evolution needs to
be employed. Therefore, the main purpose of this work is to study
the microstructure evolution around the notch.
In this paper, we performed the uniaxial creep tests in a second
generation Ni-based single crystal (DD6) with smooth and V-type
notched bars under 980 1C and 400 MPa. Fracture morphology and
microstructure evolution in both specimens were observed by
SEM. Subsequently, FE analysis with a modi ﬁed Kachanov – Rabot-
nov (K – R) damage law was carried out to evaluate the stress and
damage distribution during the creeping process of the notched
specimens. Finally, the correlation between microstructure eleva-
tion and max principal stress was pointed out.

3.2. Microstructure evolutions
Fig. 5 shows the microstructure evolution of the smooth
specimen. In Figs. 5 (a, c, e and f) and (b and d), the SEM samples
cut parallel and perpendicular to the loading axis are shown,
respectively.
In the vicinity of the fracture surface, cracks and voids can be
observed in the longitudina l cross-section, as shown in Fig. 5 (a).
Under the test stress (400 MPa), the directions of both voids growing
and cracks propagating are perpendicular to the loading axis. The
cracks have an average length of about 58 – 251 mm and tend to
coalesce into a line. The microstructure heterogeneity displays many
round and elliptic pores with average equivalent size of about 6.7mm
in Fig. 5(b), where the sample was cut perpendicular to the loading
axis. These pores and cracks distributed near the fracture surface
could reduce the effective load- bearing area during the creeping
process, which could also result in the increase in true stress and the
decrease in section areas (necking).
Fig. 5 (c) illustrates γ
0-precipitates (black region) that form rafts
with ‘wavy’ γ/γ0 interfaces after 29 h. For DD6 is a single crystal
superalloy with negative lattice mis ﬁt, the γ0 composed elements,
e.g., Al and Ta, diffuse from the regions of high hydrostatic
pressure to those of low hydrostatic pressure under the applied
stress and γ/γ0 interface mismatch stress. They move from the edge
of γ0-phase to the γ matrix channels which are parallel to the
tension axis. This elements transferring progress would lead to a
growth of the γ0-phase along the direction perpendicular to the
stress axes, which is usually called P-type rafting. In the transverse
cross-section ( Fig. 5 (d)), recast layer with a thickness of about
15 mm can be observed in the free surface and the γ0 precipitate's
cuboidal shape can still be recognized in the matrix adjacent to the
recast layer. Fig. 5 (e) shows the growing progress of a pore with a
length of about 80 mm after 29 h creep test: initial casting defects,
e.g., microcracks and microvoid, deformed under the interaction of
thermal stress and applied stress and then the microcrack/micro-
void gradually propagated along the direction perpendicular to the
applied stress axis. Fig. 5 (f) indicates that location B is the initial
defect position where the vertical matrix channel can still be
recognized due to the low stress. Location A presents the inter-
mediate creep state with fully rafted structure, which indicates
that this position was subjected to very high tension stress during
creep and before the crack crossed to this location the material
had fully rafted. Besides, it can be seen that the coarsening of γ
0 is
parallel to the crack face. The same coarsening result has already
been observed by Cormier et al. [13].
Fig. 6 shows the SEM photos of the notched specimen P3. Fig. 6
(a) illustrates that the stress concentration results in many cracks
near the notch throat where the material is subjected to a long time
oxidation. A more detailed analysis near the notch and far away
from the fracture surface shows that the morphologies can be
clearly different (see Fig. 6 (b)). The area in the center of the
specimen (position A in Fig. 6 (b)) presents perfect P-type rafting

3.3.2. Damage evolution
In this study, the damage distributions were calculated by the
modiﬁed K– R creep damage model, as shown in Fig. 9. The results
show that the notch geometry has a great effect on the creep
damage distributions. When the calculation was terminated, the
maximum creep damage reached 0.21 at the point notch root. The
creep damage development with time in the radial direction of the
minimum notch plane is plotted in Fig. 10. It can be seen that the
creep damage increases quickly with the creep time at the
maximum damage point, while the damage keeps to a very low
value (close to zero) in the notch section center ( R 40.5 mm). This
FEA result is consistent with the conclusion in Section 3.1 that the
notched specimen fracture initiates at the root and ﬁnally to the
center (see the fracture morphology in Fig. 4 ).
4. Conclusions
Creep tests and numerical simulation for smooth specimens
and notched specimens were conducted to study the notch effect
on the creep behavior in Ni-based single crystal superalloys. The
observations of macro/micro-structure evolutions and stress –
strain ﬁeld of a notched model based on a modi ﬁed damage creep
law were considered here. Some principal conclusions can be
drawn as follows:
1. Damages initiate at the inner material and propagate to the free
surface in the smooth specimen, while cracks initiate at the
notch root and ﬁnal fracture occurs at the center in the notched
specimen. The FE results show consistent damage evolution
with the fracture morphology.
2. Macro/micro-analysis indicates that the creep failure mechan-
ism is the growth and coalescence of microcracks and micro-
voids. The growing directions of microcrack and microvoid are
perpendicular to the applied stress axis. The microstructure
evolution in the pore displays different creep stages during the
propagation of microvoid.
3. The γ
0 particles' microstructure evolution in the vicinity of
notch root is different from that in the low stress concentration
locations. Such a difference is strongly dependent on the
orientation with respect to the local max principal stress axis.
Results realized in this study can be easily applied to the
general structural parts such as Ni-based single crystal blade using
the K – R creep model linked within ABAQUS. Further study on an
in-service turbine blade could be extremely promising.
