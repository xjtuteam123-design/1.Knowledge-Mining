It is known that in high-temperature tests the rate of softening in nickel alloys after termomechanical 
treatment (TMT) is higher than after the standard heat treatment (SHT). For low-alloyed alloys of the EI437B 
type this is associated with the development of recrystallization at operating temperatures of 650 - 750°C. 
Complexly alloyed high-temperature nickel alloys are characterized by a higher content of the y'-phase, a 
wide range of the double-phase (7 + y')-state, and, as a consequence, a considerably higher recrystallization 
temperature compared to alloy EI437B. However, these alloys often lose strength after TMT more intensely 
than after SHT. The causes of this phenomenon have not been determined so far. This investigation is devoted 
to the solution of this problem.

The literature contains data on the diffusion activation 
energy of pure nickel in alloy 1~I698 [2]. We could not find 
such data on I~P741 alloy. The diffusion activation energy Qd 
of nickel in 1~I698 is 18,200 J/mole. It is known that the acti- 
vation energy depends on the diffusion mechanism (diffusion 
through the crystal lattice or over grain boundaries). If the 
diffusion activation energy in the lattice (Qt) is taken to be 1, 
the diffusion activation energies over grain boundaries and 
dislocations, according to [3, 4], can be chosen as 0.5 and 
0.7 - 0.8, respectively. For 1~I698 alloy Q/--- 13,000 J/mole, 
Qd = 18,200 J/mole, and QI/Qd = 0.7. These data show that 
the mechanism of softening of an alloy is controlled by the 
density of the dislocations over which the diffusion occurs. 
Therefore the softening rate in t~I698 alloy after TMT is 
higher than after SHT because of the elevated dislocation 
density. Structural investigations showed that this is mani- 
fested by the difference in the grain growth rate of the 
strengthening ?,'-phase in testing. 
Results of a statistical analysis of the size of particles of 
the ,/'-phase before and after testing for long-term strength at 
650 and 750°C for alloy t~I698 are presented in Table 4. 
In order to determine the size of particles of the ?"-phase, 
replicas taken from microsections prepared on heads of the 
tested specimens were subjected to electron microscopic ex- 
amination. At least 500 particles of the ?,'-phase were ana- 
lyzed for each specimen. 
Analysis of the data in Table 4 shows that the mean size 
of particles of the ?,'-phase after testing specimens in the 
TMT state is larger than that in the SHT state. This indicates 
that particles of the ?,'-phase have a high coagulation rate at 
650- 750°C, which leads to earlier softening of the speci- 
mens subjected to TMT. 
Thus, under conditions where diffusion processes limit 
the softening mechanism the increase in the dislocation den- 
sity due to TMT leads to lower long-term strength compared 
to SHT. It can be assumed that at lower temperatures (when 
the diffusion processes do not control the softening mecha- 
nism) TMT should be preferred. We can predict from the

CONCLUSION 
Worsening of the high-temperature strength in long-term 
high-temperature tests of complexly alloyed nickel alloys of 
the types 1~I698 and 1~P741 that have been subjected to TMT 
should be associated with processes of coagulation of the 
strengthening y'-phase that develop due to the elevated dislo- 
cation density, which intensifies the diffusion of the alloying 
elements to particles of the y'-phase, rather than with recrys- 
tallization as in 1~I437B alloy.
