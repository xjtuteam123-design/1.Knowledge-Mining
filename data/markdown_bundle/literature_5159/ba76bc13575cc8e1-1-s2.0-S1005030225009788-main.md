This study presented a powder metallurgy (PM) strategy combining mechanical alloying (MA) and spark 
plasma sintering (SPS) for fabricating the high-performance AlMoNbTaTiZr refractory high-entropy alloys 
(RHEAs), which are composed of BCC phases, ordered B2 phases and grain boundary Al4 Zr5 intermetallics. 
By optimizing ball milling processes, SPS sintering temperatures, and Al/Zr content, a relatively high con- 
tent of B2 phase with a high degree of structural ordering and desired discontinuous Al4 Zr5 intermetallics 
at grain boundaries was achieved. And it was noteworthy that the average grain size of AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr 
RHEA was only 14.8 μm, an order of magnitude smaller than that of as-cast AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEA. 
These endowed the PM AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEAs with a low density (7.4 g/cm3 ) and signiﬁcantly im- 
proved mechanical properties, especially at 10 0 0 °C, with the yield strength of 853 MPa and compressive 
strength of 929 MPa. Moreover, with a decrease in the content of Al and Zr elements, the yield strength, 
fracture strength and fracture strain at room temperature for the present PM Al0.5 Mo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr0.5 
RHEA were up to 2408 MPa, 2783 MPa and 20.8 %, which were approximately 400 MPa and 108 % higher 
than that of as-cast AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEA, respectively. More importantly, the speciﬁc strengths of 
present PM AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr and Al0.5 Mo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr0.5 RHEAs were far higher than that of publicly 
reported Ni-based superalloys, particularly above 10 0 0 °C, having a potential for partial substitution of 
conventional Ni-based superalloys to meet the dual demands for aerospace’s weight reduction and perfor- 
mance improvement. Finally, the microstructure evolution characteristics and strengthening-toughening 
mechanisms of PM AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEAs were discussed.

1. Introduction 
With the rapid advancement of aerospace technology, next- 
generation aero-engines with higher thrust-to-weight ratios have 
an urgent need for lightweight high-strength high-temperature- 
resistant structural metal alloys [ 1 , 2 ]. Currently, commonly 
used Ni-based superalloys cannot meet the dual demands for 
aerospace’s weight reduction and performance improvement, due 
to the high density ( ≥ 8.4 g/cm3 ) and insuﬃcient mechanical

tory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), as a new generation of high- 
temperature structural materials, are composed of high-melting- 
point elements, e.g., Nb, Mo, Hf, Ta, W, Zr, Cr, etc. [ 5 ]. Their multi- 
principal element design concept and unique microstructural char- 
acteristics endow them with exceptional mechanical properties un- 
der high-temperature conditions [ 6 , 7 ]. Various RHEAs have been 
developed, including WReTaMo, TaNbHfZrTi, CrMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr, 
AlMoNbTaTiZr, etc. [ 8–11 ], in which dual-phase AlMoNbTaTiZr 
RHEAs exhibit a unique microstructure resembling the γ/ γ’ struc- 
ture of conventional superalloys and thus possess enhanced high- 
temperature performances [ 12 , 13 ]. Besides, their melting points are

based superalloys [ 14 , 15 ]. More importantly, the relatively lower 
densities due to the addition of light Al and Ti elements make the 
AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs have a greater advantage in speciﬁc strength 
at high-temperature compared to Co-/Ni-based superalloys [ 10 , 11 ]. 
So far, the arc-melting and casting is the most widely used fabri- 
cation method of the AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs, leading to two main 
drawbacks containing inhomogeneous microstructure of segrega- 
tion and coarse grain sizes, which remarkably deteriorate these al- 
loys’ mechanical properties and limit their practical applications. 
Additionally, the corresponding room-temperature plasticity is lim- 
ited due to the inevitable presence of large-sized continuous grain 
boundary precipitates Al-Zr-rich phases with a hexagonal close- 
packed structure. Therefore, it is particularly essential to exploit 
a feasible method for producing the AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs with a 
good combination of high strength and high plasticity. 
In comparison with traditional casting techniques, powder met- 
allurgy (PM) e.g., the combined techniques of mechanical alloy- 
ing (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS) [ 16–18 ], displays no- 
table advantages in microstructural control and performance opti- 
mization. This is because MA utilizes high-energy ball milling to 
make multiple elements uniformly mix and form target solid solu- 
tions with ultraﬁne grains, which can effectively avoid the chemi- 
cal composition segregation caused by the traditional casting [ 19–
21 ]. Subsequently, the MA’s uniform microstructures with ultra- 
ﬁne grains can be retained by SPS due to its rapid heating and 
short densiﬁcation process [ 22–25 ]. Based on these beneﬁts, Fu 
et al. [ 26 ] synthesized an ultraﬁne-grained Fe25 Ni25 Co25 Ti15 Al10 
HEA, with a hierarchical nanoprecipitate structure consisting of co- 
existing BCC and FCC phases by MA and SPS. This alloy exhib- 
ited an exceptional tensile strength, up to 2.52 GPa. Joo et al. [ 27 ] 
also demonstrated a powder metallurgical CoCrFeMnNi HEA by the 
low-temperature SPS sintering at 900 °C, whose compressive frac- 
ture strength was three times higher than that produced by cast- 
ing methods. These studies suggest that MA and SPS will offer a 
promising strategy for precisely controlling the microstructure and 
simultaneously improving the strength and plasticity of the target 
AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs. 
In addition, as stated above, the AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs’ lim- 
ited plasticity below 600 °C can be attributed to the existence of 
continuous grain boundary precipitates Al-Zr-rich phases [ 11 , 28 ]. 
Recent research has shown that adjusting processing methods 
and chemical compositions can regulate the grain boundary pre- 
cipitates Al-Zr-rich phases and thus the AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs’ 
mechanical properties can be improved signiﬁcantly [ 11 , 29–32 ]. 
Suárez Ocaño et al. [ 29 ] concluded that the AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs’ 
plasticity could be enhanced by reducing the volume fraction of 
grain boundary precipitates Al-Zr-rich phases to < 1 % through 
rapid cooling. Furthermore, Senkov et al. [ 11 ] also reported that 
a reduction in the Mo content of the AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEAs 
was in favor of the reﬁnement of the Al-Zr-rich phases precipi- 
tated at grain boundaries, thereby promoting the plasticity at room 
temperature. On the contrary, this adjustment would disrupt the 
BCC/B2 coherent structure within equiaxed grains, resulting in a 
decrease in the AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs’ strength. Consequently, fur- 
ther researches need to focus on regulating the morphology, size 
and distribution of the Al-Zr-rich phases at grain boundaries while 
maintaining the BCC/B2 coherent structure. 
In the present work, a powder metallurgy strategy combining 
MA and SPS was employed to produce the bulk AlMoNbTaTiZr 
RHEAs. The emphasis was on optimizing the relative content and 
morphologies of BCC, B2 and grain boundary precipitates, Al-Zr- 
rich phases within AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs, by precisely controlling 
the ball milling parameters, SPS sintering temperatures and ad- 
justing the proportion of component elements. Moreover, the for- 
mation and regulating mechanisms of ﬁne-grained AlMoNbTaTiZr 
RHEAs with controlled discontinuous grain boundary precipitates 
were clariﬁed. The corresponding mechanical properties at room 
and elevated temperatures were evaluated. Finally, the strengthen- 
ing mechanism and fracture characteristics of the powder metal- 
lurgy AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs were discussed. 
2. Experimental procedures 
2.1. Materials preparation 
The objective of the present work is to fabricate the 
high-performance AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr and Al0.5 Mo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr0.5 
RHEAs with the corresponding theoretical densities of 7.4 g/cm3 
and 7.85 g/cm3 by powder metallurgy. Phase formation rules 
for these two alloys based on the empirical formula were 
provided in Table 1 , indicating that AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr and 
Al0.5 Mo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr0.5 RHEAs primarily consisted of BCC solid so- 
lution phases [ 33 ]. The density ( ρ), Theoretical melting point ( Tm ), 
mixing entropy ( /Delta1Smix ), mixing enthalpy ( /Delta1Hmix ), atomic size mis- 
match ( δ), valence electron concentration (VEC), and thermody- 
namic parameter ( /Omega1) are also listed in Table 1 . For simplicity, 
the term “AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEA” is used as a collective designa- 
tion for these two compositions. When referring to individual al- 
loys, their full chemical formulas are explicitly stated to avoid 
ambiguity. Speciﬁcally, the starting materials were selected to be 
high-purity metal element powders provided by Beijing Xing Rong 
Yuan Technology Co. Ltd, including Al (99.7 wt. %, 48 μm), Mo 
(99.9 wt. %, 48 μm), Nb (99.9 wt. %, 45 μm), Ta (99.9 wt. %, 45 μm), 
Ti (99.5 wt. %, 45 μm) and Zr (99.97 wt. %, 48 μm). The mechan- 
ical alloying of these different metal element powders was con- 
ducted in a planetary ball mill (QM-3SP4, Nanjing NanDa Instru- 
ment Plant) with a ball-to-powder weight ratio of 12:1 at a speed 
of 360 rpm under an argon atmosphere. To avoid overheating, the 
ball milling was interrupted every 50 min for a 10-min cooling in- 
terval. No process control agent was used to prevent contamination 
by impurity elements. Subsequently, the mechanically alloyed Al- 
MoNbTaTiZr powders were consolidated via SPS in a graphite mold 
with a 40 mm inner diameter. The SPS process was carried out at 
temperatures ranging from 1400 °C to 1600 °C for 20 min, with an 
axial pressure of 50 MPa applied in vacuum ( < 1.3 × 10−2 Pa). The 
SPS sintering temperature was monitored with an infrared pyrom- 
eter, and the mechanically alloyed AlMoNbTaTiZr powders were 
initially heated to 360 °C for 5 min, followed by a controlled heat- 
ing rate of 75 °C/min to the ﬁnal sintering temperature. Finally, 
the bulk AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr and Al0.5 Mo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr0.5 RHEAs 
with 40 mm in diameter and 10 mm in height were achieved. 
The schematic diagram of the fabrication processes is shown in 
Fig. 1 (a–c).

2.3. Mechanical properties test 
Compression specimens with 5 mm in diameter and 8 mm in 
height were machined from the as-SPSed bulk AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr 
and Al0.5 Mo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr0.5 RHEAs using electric discharge ma- 
chining. The compression tests were performed at both 25 °C and 
10 0 0 °C, respectively, with the strain rate of 10−3 s−1 . The speci- 
mens were preheated at 10 0 0 °C for 5 min before the compression 
testing. All compression tests were carried out until the specimens 
achieved a 40 % reduction in height and were repeated three times 
for each condition to obtain the average values. 
Microhardness measurements across several grains were con- 
ducted using an HV-10 0 0 Vickers hardness tester with a 9.8 N 
load applied for 15 s. The average microhardness was obtained 
based on three measurements for each representative region. The 
nanoindentation experiments were performed using an Agilent 
G200 Nano Indenter to evaluate the nanohardness of the BCC/B2 
phases within grains. At least ten indentations were made to en- 
sure reliable results.

3.2. Bulk AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEAs prepared by MA and SPS 
3.2.1. Effect of sintering temperature on ﬁnal microstructures 
In order to produce the desired bulk AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEA, 
the mechanically alloyed AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr powders were sub- 
jected to SPS at 1400 °C, 150 0 °C and 160 0 °C under the pressure 
of 50 MPa. As shown in Fig. 3 (a), the bulk alloys mainly consisted 
of BCC and Al4 Zr5 phases. The enhanced sharpness and intensity 
of the diffraction peaks were due to increased grain size and the 
release of the lattice strain. Notably, the bulk AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr 
RHEA sintering at 1500 °C exhibited the stabilization of two dis- 
tinct BCC phases: disordered BCC and ordered B2 phases ( Fig. 3 (b)). 
In addition, the diffraction peaks of the ordered phases in XRD may 
be obscured by the strong peaks of disordered phases sintered at 
140 0 °C and 160 0 °C. Therefore, transmission electron microscopy 
may be required to further observe and conﬁrm the presence of 
ordered structures.

AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEA sintered at 1400 °C consisted of light

ular particles with 2–10 μm in length and 1.3 μm in width dis-

within grains.

3.3. Microstructure formation mechanism of PM AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr 
RHEAs 
The equilibrium phase diagram of the theoretically designed 
AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr alloy was calculated using the Thermo-Calc 
software according to the TCHEA3 database, which ultimately de- 
picted the respective volume fractions of the constituent phases 
under equilibrium conditions as a function of temperature, as 
shown in Fig. 9 (a). Furthermore, based on the calculated phase di- 
agram and the experimental results regarding the morphologies, 
sizes and distributions of the constituent phases mentioned above, 
the schematic diagram of the mechanism of microstructural forma- 
tion of PM AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEAs at different sintering temper- 
atures was illustrated in Fig. 9 (b–d). 
Firstly, the mechanically alloyed AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr powders 
were subjected to the spark plasma sintering (SPS) at 1400–
1600 °C. During the heating process, all the Al-Mo-Nb-Ta-Ti-Zr ele- 
ments diffused more evenly and tended to become more homo- 
geneous. When the sintering temperature reached 1400 °C, dis- 
ordered BCC phases enriched in Nb, Mo and Ta and ordered B2 
phases enriched in Al, Ti and Zr appeared because BCC phases and 
B2 phases could form below 1895 °C and 1536 °C, respectively, 
as shown in Fig. 9 (a). The ordered B2 phases exhibited a regu- 
lar periodic grid distribution within disordered BCC phases, namely 
BCC/B2 coherent structure, as shown in Fig. 9 (b). However, the me- 
chanical alloying would inevitably introduce a small number of 
O elements and the highly active Zr possessed a strong aﬃnity 
towards O, resulting in the formation of ZrO2 phases during the 
spark plasma sintering. In addition, Zr-rich phases remained due 
to the insuﬃcient diffusion of Zr elements at 1400 °C [ 42 , 43 ]. 
Subsequently, when the temperature was lowered to 1205 °C, 
continuous Al4 Zr5 intermetallics started to precipitate on the grain 
boundaries. Differences in diffusion kinetics within various grain 
boundary regions led to the different morphologies and sizes of 
Al4 Zr5 intermetallics. Coarse massive Al4 Zr5 intermetallics formed 
in high-curvature triple-junction grain boundaries due to higher 
diffusion ﬂux, while continuous striplike Al4 Zr5 intermetallics pre- 
cipitated on the other low-curvature grain boundaries. This would 
be likely to provide crucial guidance for optimizing the morphol- 
ogy and distribution of grain boundary Al4 Zr5 intermetallics. As 
the temperature gradually reduced to the ambient temperature, 
the ﬁnal microstructure of the AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEA sintered at 
1400 °C composed of BCC/B2 coherent structure, continuous grain 
boundary Al4 Zr5 intermetallics, ZrO2 phases and residual Zr-rich 
phases. 
When the sintering temperature rose to 1500 °C, the diffusion 
rate of Zr atoms became faster, leading to the disappearance of Zr- 
rich phases and an increase in the content of Al4 Zr5 intermetallics 
and ordered B2-enriched Al/Zr/Ti elements. It was noteworthy that 
Al4 Zr5 intermetallics became desired discontinuous distribution at 
the grain boundaries due to the enhanced Zr diffusivity and Ost- 
wald ripening, which was in favor of improving the plasticity of the 
AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEA sintered at 1500 °C. The average channel 
width of B2 phases slightly increased and B2 phases possessed the 
highest degree of ordering, and thus BCC/B2 coherent structure be- 
came more perfect, as shown in Fig. 9 (c). The microstructure of the 
AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEA sintered at 1500 °C consisted of BCC/B2 
coherent structure with the enhanced B2 ordering, discontinuous 
grain boundary Al4 Zr5 intermetallics and ZrO2 phases. 
Markedly different from that sintered at 1400 °C and 1500 °C, 
while the sintering temperature reached 1600 °C, a certain pro- 
portion of liquid phases appeared, and disordered BCC phases and 
ZrO2 phases still remained, as shown in Fig. 9 (d). When the tem- 
perature dropped to 1536 °C, nanosized B2 phases with a low de- 
gree of ordering precipitated within disordered BCC phases, instead 
of a BCC/B2 coherent structure. With decreasing temperature, liq- 
uid phases gradually transformed into solid disordered BCC and 
ordered B2 phases until all the liquid phases disappeared. As the 
temperature continued to decrease, equiaxed Al4 Zr5 intermetallics 
precipitated and their content signiﬁcantly increased compared 
with that sintered at 140 0–150 0 °C due to the faster diffusion rate 
and enhanced Ostwald ripening at 1600 °C. The notable grain re- 
ﬁnement observed at the sintering temperature of 1600 °C was 
considered to be associated with the proximity to the localized 
melting point, at which partial melting of low-melting elements 
occurred, leading to a minor liquid phase sintering process. This 
promoted rapid particle rearrangement and densiﬁcation, effec- 
tively suppressing grain growth and resulting in ﬁner microstruc- 
tures. Finally, the microstructure of the AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEA 
sintered at 1600 °C contained equiaxed BCC phases with nano- 
sized ordered B2 phases, randomly dispersed equiaxed Al4 Zr5 in- 
termetallics and a small number of ZrO2 phases.

4. Conclusions 
In the present work, the bulk AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs were suc- 
cessfully fabricated via MA and subsequent SPS. The effects of fab- 
rication processes and Al/Zr content on the microstructure and me- 
chanical properties were systematically investigated. Finally, the 
strengthening mechanism and fracture characteristics of PM Al- 
MoNbTaTiZr RHEAs were discussed. The main conclusions were as 
follows: 
(1) By adjusting the ball milling parameters, the mechanically al- 
loyed AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr spherical powders with uniform dis- 
tribution of elements could be ﬁrst produced. And subsequent 
SPS sintering temperatures were optimized to successfully fab- 
ricate the bulk AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEAs, composed of BCC 
phases, B2 phases with a high degree of structural ordering, 
and Al4 Zr5 intermetallics with a desired discontinuous distri- 
bution at grain boundaries. By decreasing the Al and Zr con- 
tent to obtain the Al0.5 Mo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr0.5 RHEA, the content 
of Al4 Zr5 intermetallics was reduced by 65.9 %, and their mor- 
phology became ﬁner and more dispersed, while a high degree 
of structural ordering in the B2 phase was retained. More cru- 
cially, the average grain size of the ﬁnal PM AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr 
RHEAs was only 14.8 μm, an order of magnitude smaller than 
that of as-cast AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEAs. 
(2) The present PM AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr RHEA exhibited a low den- 
sity of 7.4 g/cm3 and a signiﬁcant improvement in mechani- 
cal properties, especially at 10 0 0 °C, with the yield strength 
of 853 MPa and compressive strength of 929 MPa. Moreover, 
as the content of Al and Zr elements decreased, the yield 
strength, fracture strength and fracture strain at room tem- 
perature simultaneously increased up to 2408 MPa, 2783 MPa 
and 20.8 %, respectively, approximately 400 MPa and 108 % 
higher than that of the reported as-cast AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr 
RHEA. More importantly, the speciﬁc strengths of the present 
PM AlMo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr and Al0.5 Mo0.5 NbTa0.5 TiZr0.5 RHEAs far 
exceeded those of Ni-based superalloys, making them promis- 
ing candidates for aerospace applications requiring both weight 
reduction and performance enhancement. 
(3) The strength improvement of the present PM AlMoNbTaTiZr 
RHEAs could be attributed to the synergistic effects of multi- 
element solid solution strengthening, precipitation strength- 
ening caused by ordered B2 phases and grain boundary 
Al4 Zr5 intermetallics, and grain boundary strengthening re- 
sulting from grain reﬁnement by powder metallurgy. While 
the grain reﬁnement of the present PM AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs, 
and the reasonably discontinuous distribution at grain bound- 
aries and the reduction in content/size of Al4 Zr5 intermetallics 
largely contributed to the signiﬁcant enhancement in the 
fracture strain of present PM AlMoNbTaTiZr RHEAs at room 
temperature.
