ABSTRACT 7 
Several Nb-based alloys containing Ti, Mo and W were produced and their microstructure, phase 8 
composition and mechanical properties in the temperature range of 23 to 1200°C were reported 9 
and compared with a high-strength commercial Nb alloy C-3009, which contains Hf and W 10 
alloying elements. Replacement of Hf with Ti and W with Mo led to alloys with considerably 11 
reduced density and cost, as well as with reduced micro-segregation of the alloying elements. 12 
The strengthening effect from W was higher than from Mo at temperatures above ~600°C and 13 
more amount of Mo was required to compensate the strength decrease. The strengthening effects 14 
from Hf and Ti were comparable at temperatures up to 1000°C; however, at 1200°C the effect 15 
from Hf was superior. It was concluded that the Nb-Mo-Ti system can be used as a baseline for 16 
the development of high strength, high-temperature alloys using alloying with other elements. 17

1 INTRODUCTION 21 
Nb-based refractory alloys currently used in some high-temperature engine applications contain 22 
expensive and dense alloying elements [1]. For example, C-103, which is one of the most 23 
commonly used medium-strength Nb alloys, contains (by wt.%) 10 Hf, 0.5 Ta, 0.5 W, 0.7 Zr and 24 
1 Ti; and a high strength C-3009 contains 30 Hf and 9 W. Hf, Ta and Zr are expensive, costing 25 
approximately $1200, $290 and $150 per kilogram, respectively, and Hf, W and Ta have high 26 
density of, respectively, 13.21, 16.65 and 19.25 g/cm 3 (Table 1). Moreover, these alloys have 27 
poor oxidation resistance above 600°C. It would therefore be beneficial to develop less 28 
expensive and lower density Nb alloys, which would have structural properties, such as high 29 
temperature strength and oxidation resistance, equivalent or better than those of the currently 30 
used commercial Nb alloys. There has been extensive research on Nb alloys containing Si, 31

whose main goal is to improve both high temperature strength and oxidation resistance [2-6]; 32 
however, Nb-Si alloys are generally brittle at temperatures ≤ 1000°C and they have not found 33 
practical use yet. Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) is another promising 34 
direction of research resulting in developing several multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) 35 
with attractive high temperature mechanical properties and improved oxidation resistance [7]. 36 
The main challenge is the lack of experimental data, as well as valid theoretical models, on 37 
thermodynamic, mechanical and physical properties of these complex MPEA systems. RCCAs 38 
experience slow kinetics of phase transformations and may contain metastable phases. Thus 39 
extensive studies are required to identify equilibrium phase diagrams, kinetics of phase 40 
transformations and phase stability in these alloys in the time-temperature-stress conditions of 41 
practical interest [8, 9]. 42

In this work, we used conventional methods for alloy development and produced several Nb-49 
based alloys, which contain Mo, W and Ti. A high-strength commercial Nb alloy C-3009 was 50 
used as a baseline material and the new alloys were produced by replacing heavy W and Hf with 51 
lower density Mo and Ti, respectively. Mo belongs to the same group (VI) of the periodic table 52 
as W and Ti to the same group (IV) as Hf. In addition to decreasing density, Ti is known to 53 
noticeably improve oxidation resistance of Nb [12, 13]. However, Mo and Ti have lower melting 54 
temperatures than W and Hf. In addition, Mo has smaller elastic moduli than W but Ti has higher 55

## Page 4

elastic moduli than Hf. Because the strength of the alloys generally increases with increasing in 56 
melting temperature and elastic moduli, replacement of W with Mo may reduce high temperature 57 
strength relative to C-3009, while the effect from replacement of Hf with Ti is not 58 
straightforward. Thus, the effect of the concentration of the alloying elements on high 59 
temperature properties has also been studied in this work. 60

and good agreement between these two methods was found. The room temperature elastic 95 
modulus values reported in this work were measured using Vic-Gauge software. 96

3.2.2 Deformation Behavior at Elevated Temperatures 226 
The true stress vs. true strain deformation curves for the studied alloys at 600°C, 800°C, 1000°C 227 
and 1200°C are shown in Figure 4. At these temperatures, W1, M1, M2, M3 and M4 did not 228 
fracture during deformation. MW1 had limited compressive ductility at 600°C, 800°C and 229 
1000°C, but it did not fracture at 1200°C. C-3009 was ductile (did not fracture) at 600-1000°C, 230 
but it fractured after true strain of 0.3 at 1200°C. 231 
At T = 600°C and 800°C, deformation of C-3009, M1, M2 and M3 occurred with continuous 232 
strain hardening, while steady-state flow at almost constant flow stress was observed in W1 and 233 
M4 (Figure 4 a, b). MW1 showed continuous decrease in true stress after reaching a peak stress 234 
σp = 1227 MPa at εp = 0.12 (T=600°C) or σp = 1115 MPa at εp = 0.16 (T=800°C), which resulted 235 
in fracture at εf = 0.3 (600°C) or 0.36 (800°C). At 1000°C and 1200°C, all the alloys showed 236 
strain hardening only at the beginning of deformation, up to ~0.1-0.18 true strain; then strain 237 
softening and/or steady state flow followed (Figure 4 c, d). A considerable loss in compressive 238 
ductility was observed in C-3009 at 1200°C (Figure 4 d). Visual observation of the tested C-239 
3009 samples showed that this loss in compressive ductility was due to strain localization in a 240 
single ~45°-inclined shear band and fracturing along this band. Such behavior indicates 241 
deformation instability of C-3009 at 1200°C. Fine-grained structure and multiple cracks were 242 
observed inside this shear band. It is likely that formation of fine-grained structure within the 243 
shear band was responsible for strain localization and cracking. The fine-grain structure initiated 244 
extensive grain boundary sliding, which, however, was not yet accommodated by dislocations 245 
and diffusion inside grains, and this resulted in crack formation at triple junctions and along 246 
grain boundaries. By growing and merging, these micro-cracks led to macroscopic fracture. 247 
Obviously, the strain hardening capability of the alloy is not sufficient to initiate plastic 248 
deformation on 5 independent slip systems needed for homogeneous shape change. 249

3.2.4 Improving Specific Strength 342 
Although the yield stress of C-3009 was superior to the developmental alloys W1, M1, M2 and 343 
M3 at T ≥ 600°C, its specific yield stress, due to higher density, lagged up to 1000°C (Table 6, 344 
Figure 5). For example, at room temperature, σ0.2 /ρ of C-3009 and M1 are the same (=65.5 MPa 345 
cm 3/g) and much smaller than those of W1, M2 and M3. At 600°C, σ0.2 /ρ of C-3009 is similar to 346 
M2 but smaller than that of M3 and W1. At 800°C, σ0.2 /ρ of C-3009 is almost similar to that of 347 
M3; but at T ≥ 1000°C, C-3009 became superior to these three Mo-containing alloys. At the 348 
same time, σ0.2 /ρ of W1 is superior to all the studied alloys, including C-3009, up to 1000°C. The 349 
specific yield stress values of M4 and MW1 are considerably higher than those of all other 350 
studied alloys, including C-3009. It is apparent that solution strengthening of Nb by Mo is 351 
noticeably weaker than by W. Alloying with higher amount of Mo is required to achieve the 352 
strengthening effect similar to W. At the same time, Ti provides stronger strengthening effect 353 
than Hf at room temperature; however, the advantage is lost above 800°C. 354

4 CONCLUSIONS 356 
Six new Nb-based refractory alloys containing W and Ti (W1) or Mo and Ti (M1, M2, M3 and 357 
M4) or Mo, Ti and W (MW1) were produced and their microstructure and compression 358

## Page 15

14 
 
properties in the temperature range of 23 to 1200°C were reported and compared with 359 
commercial C-3009. 360 
The produced alloys, except MW1, had single-phase BCC crystal structures. MW1 had 361 
small fraction of Ti-rich precipitates located at grain boundaries. While chemical micro-362 
segregation was clearly observed in the cast C-3009, replacement of Hf with Ti and W with Mo 363 
led to more homogeneous distribution of the alloying elements in the developmental alloys. In 364 
Mo-containing alloys, the average grain size decreased with an increase in the amount of Mo. 365 
The densities of the produced Nb alloys were considerably smaller than the density of C-366 
3009 (10.3 g/cm 3). Alloy M4 had the smallest density (=7.70 g/cm 3) and MW1 had the largest 367 
density (=8.94 g.cm 3) among the reported alloys. 368 
W-containing alloys (C-3009, W1 and MW1) were stronger than Mo-containing alloys at 369 
T = 600 – 1200°C. With increasing temperature in this temperature range, Mo-containing alloys 370 
lost their strength quicker than W-containing alloys. 371 
Alloying with Hf had beneficial effect on high-temperature strength relative to alloying 372 
with Ti; however, the effect from Ti was stronger at room temperature. On the other hand, 373 
alloying with Ti reduced the alloy density and increased specific strength at higher temperatures. 374 
At all the studied temperatures, the strength of Mo-containing alloys increased almost 375 
linearly with increasing Mo concentration evidencing solid-solution strengthening. 376 
Atomistic simulation based on Suzuki’s model can satisfactory predict yield stress of the 377 
Nb refractory alloys at temperatures ≤ 1000°C, but it fails at higher temperatures, when the 378 
diffusion-controlled processes start to dominate. 379 
Due to reduced density, the room temperature specific strengths of the developmental 380 
alloys were superior to that of C-3009. Specific strengths of M4 and MW1 were superior to C-381 
3009 and all other reported alloys at all the studied temperatures. 382
