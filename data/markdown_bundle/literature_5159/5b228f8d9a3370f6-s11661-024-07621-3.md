ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
Investigating the Dissolution and Evolution Behavior
of Oxides in FGH96 Superalloys
YANG LIU, YUFENG LIU, LIN ZHANG, PENG ZHANG, XINGYU LI,
SHAORONG ZHANG, NA LIU, ZHOU LI, ZEJING LI, and XUANHUI QU
The dissolution and evolution behavior of oxides in FGH96 superalloys was systematically
investigated through the diﬀusion couples and heat treatment experiments. Results reveal that
two distinct interfaces structures were identiﬁed at diﬀusion couple interface of the Ni-based
superalloys during heat treatment, namely the contact interface and the oxide particle interface,
both exhibiting notable oxygen (O) enrichment. During the heat treatment, the contact interface
exhibited a gradual healing process, while the oxide particles were partly dissolute, caused by the
decrease of the equilibrium O partial pressure and the diﬀusion of elements. This results in a
reduction of O content at diﬀusion couple interface to a concentration equivalent to that of
matrix. In the diﬀusion process, a gradient distribution of O concentration was observed from
the diﬀusion couple interface to the matrix, and an apparent O segregation within c matrix was
revealed, indicating a heightened aﬃnity between O and c matrix. First-principles calculations
demonstrated a lower formation energy for O solid solution into Ni ( /C0 5.12 eV) than that into
Ni
3Al (/C0 5.00 eV). Additionally, the expansion of the cell parameter was inﬂuenced by a higher
concentration of O. Consequently, the c matrix provided a conducive diﬀusion pathway for O
migration from the diﬀusion couple interface to the matrix. Eventually, O atoms solidly
dissolved within c matrix, leading to the lattice expansion.

I. INTRODUCTION
POWDER metallurgy (PM) Ni-based superalloys
oﬀer signiﬁcant advantages over traditional cast and
forged counterparts in aerospace applications, primarily
due to their homogenous composition and ﬁne
microstructure.
[1–3] These PM Ni-based superalloys
exhibit excellent mechanical properties at elevated
temperatures, including high yield strength and favor-
able fatigue characteristics, rendering them highly
promising for further development.
[4–8]
Unfortunately, the mechanical properties of these
superalloys are often limited by the formation of prior
particle boundaries (PPBs), consisting of detrimental
oxide and carbide particles.
[9,10] Among the unfavorable
inﬂuence of oxide particles on the performance of PM
Ni-based superalloys, the stress concentration between
the oxide particles and the matrix during deformation is
widely recognized as a critical failure mechanism.
[11,12]
Additionally, considering the initially homogeneous
distribution of O on the powder surface, the formation
of oxide particles, such as Al
2O3 and ZrO2, is facilitated
by the high surface energy of the alloy powders during
hot isostatic pressing (HIP). [13–16] While apart from the
presence of oxide particles observed at PPBs, the
remaining areas on the powder surface exhibit signs of
O dissolution and diﬀusion. Previous study reported
that the dissolution behavior of the diﬀusion couple
interface is mainly divided into two parts: the decom-
position of interfacial oxides and the diﬀusion of O into
the matrix.
[17] For example, Xie et al . revealed the
gradual decomposition of interfacial oxides ( e.g.,
MnCr2O4) at the bonding joints of 316L stainless steel
with prolonged holding time. [18] Meanwhile, oxide
particles precipitate on both sides of the interface as O
ions diﬀuse into the matrix, forming a particle precip-
itation zone. Actually, the diﬀusion of O in metal/alloys
is similar to the oxidation behavior.
[19] Nevertheless,
Zhai et al . observed the oxidation behavior of Ni-based
superalloys from room temperature to 700 /C176 Cb y in situ
environmental transmission electron microscopy and
found the oxidation behavior in Ni-based superalloys
may not occur under a low O pressure even at high
temperatures, while complex oxides could nucleate and
grow at the c and c¢ interfaces as the O pressure rises.
[20]
Herein, the low O content on the powder surface of
Ni-based superalloys hinders a comprehensive under-
standing of the dissolution and diﬀusion behavior of O
at PPBs during subsequent manufacturing pro-
cesses.
[21–23] In our previous study, interrupted sintering
experiments have corroborated the dissolution of the
surface oxide layer on powder particles as the sintering
process advances towards densiﬁcation. Nonetheless,
elucidating the diﬀusion pathways of O and its speciﬁc
form within the substrate has been impeded by the
inherently low O content at the powder surfaces.
[24] This

presents a signiﬁcant challenge in understanding the
underlying mechanisms of O transport and its inﬂuence
on the microstructure of sintered alloys. To address this
limitation, this study incorporates a pre-formed oxide
layer on the surface of Ni-based superalloys to increase
the O content. Moreover, a diﬀusion couple interface is
prepared to control the starting position of oxide
particle diﬀusion during heat treatment. The dissolution
and diﬀusion behavior of O at the diﬀusion couple
interface exhibit sensitivity to the heat treatment time.
In this work, the dissolution and diﬀusion behavior of
O at PPBs is elucidated by analyzing the diﬀusion
behaviors of oxides at the diﬀusion couple interface,
including the diﬀusion path and the ﬁnal form of O in
terms of structure and composition. Furthermore, the
dissolution behavior of alloy elements and O into the
matrix following diﬀusion is analyzed using ﬁrst prin-
ciple computations. These analyses provide valuable
insights into the diﬀusion mechanisms of O at PPBs in
low O content alloys during high-temperature deforma-
tion and heat treatment, oﬀering a comprehensive
understanding of this aspect.
II. EXPERIMENTAL METHOD
A. Materials and Experimental
The FGH96 superalloy ingot was provided by AECC
Beijing Institute of Aeronautical Materials. The com-
position of the alloy is shown in Table I. Figure 1
presents the ﬂow diagram of the experimental procedure
for the diﬀusion coupling of FGH96 alloy. The exper-
iment is divided into the following four key steps: (1)
Pre-oxidation: The initial as-cast cylindrical FGH96
alloy ingots were heated at 500 /C176 C for 30 minutes to
form oxide ﬁlm on their surface. (2) Sintering: Two
ingots with ﬂat oxide-coated surfaces contacted were
bonded together by Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS) at
50 MPa and 1000 /C176 C for 10 minutes. (3) Vacuum
packaging: The bonded sample with an oxide interface
was cut into four parts along axial direction. And the
dissected samples were vacuum-sealed within quartz
tubes, respectively, where the pressure is below 10 Pa, to
prevent further oxidation during the following heat
treatment. (4) Heat treatment: Three vacuum-sealed
samples were heat treated at 1000 /C176 C for 0, 10, 24, and
120 hours, respectively, to observe the O diﬀusion
process and oxides evolution behavior.
B. Characterization
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Talos
F200X G2) equipped with a high angle annular dark
ﬁeld (HAADF) detector were applied to characterize the
morphology of the phases on the diﬀusion couple
interface. The test environment of TEM is vacuum less
than 1.4*10
/C0 5 Pa at room temperature, and the accel-
eration voltage is 200 kV. Selected area electron diﬀrac-
tion (SAED) was used to determine the crystal structure
of the phases observed in TEM/STEM. Notably, a
super-X Energy dispersive spectroscope (EDS) was
adopted to observe the distribution of elements in the
diﬀusion process, and an outstanding output count rates
for a given beam current could be obtained even for
low-intensity EDS signals. This helps to collect signals
from light elements such as carbon, nitrogen, and
oxygen, thus reducing the error eﬀect. Herein, EDS
mapping mode was employed to obtain the qualitative
analysis of phase, while EDS points mode was used to
get semi-quantitative analysis of the distribution of O
beside diﬀusion couple interface.
TEM samples were prepared using focused ion beam

To calculate the formation energy of O in c and

used to calculate the electronic wave function and
charge densities. The ﬁne k-point was set for the
Brillouin zone sampling for all the cell models studied.

E. Diffusion Mechanism of O at Diffusion Couple
Interface
Figure 15 provides a comprehensive overview of the
diﬀusion process at the diﬀusion couple interface. As
depicted in Figure 15(a), the initial diﬀusion couple
interface is characterized by the presence of large oxide
particles and the contact interface. Due to the short
sintering time, a segregation of alloy elements and O is
observed at the diﬀusion couple interface, while the
matrix regions near and far from the interface consist
solely of Ni and Ni
3Al [Figure 15(a1)]. Upon the
initiation of heat treatment [Figure 15(b)], the dissolu-
tion behavior of the oxide particles primarily motivated
the diﬀusion of segregated elements present at the
diﬀusion couple interface. The outer diﬀusion of impu-
rity elements such as Cr, Co, and W within aAl
2O3 leads
to a reduction in particle size and lattice parameter. This
provides the suﬃcient element diﬀusion for the growth
of c¢ phase during heat treatment. Concurrently, O,
concentrated at the diﬀusion couple interface, diﬀuses
into the matrix and solidly dissolves in the c and c¢ phase
near the interface, resulting in the formation of Ni–O
and Ni
3Al–O compounds. However, due to insuﬃcient
diﬀusion, the matrix phases located far from the
diﬀusion couple interface remain unaﬀected by O and
retain their composition as Ni and Ni
3Al. Consequently,
a gradient distribution of Ni–O and Ni 3Al–O com-
pounds is observed from the diﬀusion couple interface
towards the matrix regions [Figure 15(b1)]. After a
suﬃcient duration of heat treatment [Figure 15(c)], the
alloy and O elements fully diﬀuse into both sides of the
diﬀusion couple interface, leaving only pure aAl2O3 at
the interface. At this stage, the contact interface is
healed, and O diﬀusion becomes homogeneous through-
out the matrix. The matrix phases are composed of
Ni–O and Ni
3Al–O compounds [Figure 15(c1)].

IV. CONCLUSION
The dissolution and diﬀusion behavior of the O was
investigated by designing a diﬀusion couple experiment
with the help of ﬁrst-principles calculations. Following
conclusions can be addressed:
(1) O atoms diffused form the diffusion couple
interface into the matrix and dissolved into the
matrix during heat treatment, driven by the
decrease of the equilibrium O partial pressure.
(2) The gradient distribution of O concentration from
the diffusion couple interface to the matrix results
in the gradient impact on the lattice parameter of
the matrix.
(3) With the gradual weakening of the diffusion of O
into the matrix during heat treatment, the
uniform existence of O atoms solid solution in
the c phase is finally achieved.
(4) First-principles calculations further demonstrate
a lower formation energy ( /C0 5.12 eV) associated
with O solid solution in the Ni crystal cell
compared to that observed in Ni
3Al
(/C0 5.00 eV), resulting in the O concentration
difference between the c and c¢ phases.
By analyzing the diﬀusion behavior of high content
alumina in the alloy, it provides an analytical idea for
the diﬀusion mechanism of O at PPB in low O content
Ni-based superalloys.
