ABSTRACT 
Shock-induced dynamic mechanical behavior and spallation failure of Ni-based single crystal 
superalloys are studied via non-equilibrium molecular dynamic simulations. The results show that 
the spallation failure of Ni-based single crystal superalloys exhibits two modes of classical 
spallation and micro-spallation. At low shock velocity, the deformation mechanism is dominated 
by slip and drag of dislocations. As the shock velocity increases, the atomic structure undergoes 
complex phase transitions from FCC to BCC or disordered structures, and the spallation strength 
gradually decreases. The decreasing tendency of the spallation strength corresponds to the 
characteristics of the deformation process. Moreover, the spallation strength and spallation 
threshold velocity drop significantly with increasing initial temperature, which is due to the 
softening of Ni-based single crystal superalloys at high temperatures. In addition, the plastic 
mechanism dominated by shear stress leads to void collapse along the vertical direction at lower 
shock velocities, while the internal jetting mechanism, controlled by normal stress, induces void 
collapse in the horizontal direction under higher shock velocities. The existence of voids signif -
icantly lowers the spallation strength, and the amplitude reduction of the strength is directly 
proportional to the radius of the voids. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-base single crystal superalloys are widely used as primary materials in turbine blades of aero-engines and gas turbines due to 
their excellent high temperature creep, fatigue and corrosion resistances ( Reed, 2006 ; Pollock et al., 2006 ; Wu et al., 2020 ; Zhang et al., 
2019 ).Turbine blades are subjected to strong dynamic loadings during operation, such as extremely high-intensity centrifugal forces 
and scouring of extremely high-speed, high-temperature and high-pressure gas during sudden acceleration or steering of the aircraft. 
Under the action of these strong dynamic loadings, the turbine blades are prone to shock damage and even failure. Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys play an essential role in the mechanical properties of turbine blades, and the shock resistance of turbine blades

the dynamic mechanical response and shock resistance of Ni-based single crystal superalloys under shock loading. 
Spallation is a common dynamic fracture phenomenon under shock loading, which is caused by the superposition of the rarefaction 
wave formed by the incident compression wave on the free surface and the unloading stress wave to produce high tensile stresses 
(Kanel, 2010; Nguyen et al., 2019). According to whether the material is melted or not when micro-voids are formed, the spallation 
phenomenon can be divided into micro-spallation and classical spallation. When the shock strength is large enough, it will lead to 
melting before spallation, which is called micro-spallation. Conversely, no melting will occur in case of classic spallation (Soulard, 
2008; Xiang et al., 2013). The spallation phenomenon under shock loading has been described in detail by some experimental tests 
(Bronkhorst et al., 2016; Cerreta et al., 2005; Hong et al., 2017; Steinhauser et al., 2009) and theoretical models (Colvin et al., 2009; 
Feng et al., 2020; Nair et al., 2013; Nguyen et al., 2019, 2020), and the influences of relevant factors on spallation strength and 
spallation failure have been discussed (Bronkhorst et al., 2016; Cerreta et al., 2005; Nguyen et al., 2019, 2020). The spallation phe -
nomenon involves complex processes such as wave release, propagation and reflection, and the shock process needs to achieve a state 
of ultra-high strain rate and ultra-high shock pressure in a short time (Austin et al., 2012; Nguyen et al., 2020). With the rapid 
development of high-performance computer systems, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations based on atomic interactions are an ideal 
method to match the above-mentioned complex extreme experimental conditions (Agarwal et al., 2016; Bisht et al., 2019; Fensin et al., 
2013; Kanel et al., 2014; Mackenchery et al., 2016; Neogi and Mitra, 2017a, 2017b; Vogler et al., 2006; Xiong et al., 2019). MD 
simulations could reveal the atomic-level deformation mechanisms and allow to obtain the microstructural evolution characteristics 
and thermodynamic changes that are difficult to be directly observed via in-situ experimental testing (Kadau et al., 2002; Reed et al., 
2003; Reed et al., 2010). 
So far, the spallation of metals such as Al (Shao et al., 2013; Xiang et al., 2017; Tian et al., 2021; Yanilkin et al., 2014), Cu (Fensin 
et al., 2013; Luo et al., 2009; Mackenchery et al., 2016; Shao et al., 2019; Razorenov et al., 2014; Tian et al., 2020), Mg (Garkushin 
et al., 2014), and Pb (Xiang et al., 2013) was investigated and the deformation characteristics of the spallation process under shock 
loading were revealed. MD studies on nanoporous Al, Mg and Cu found that the spallation strength under shock loading is affected by 
shock velocity and void size, and decreases with increasing shock velocity (Garkushin et al., 2014; Xiang et al., 2017; Luo et al., 2009). 
In addition, when the shock velocity is lower, smaller voids can effectively reduce the internal stress and slightly increase the spallation 
strength, while larger voids will significantly reduce the spallation strength (Li et al., 2018b, 2022; Xiang et al., 2017). Investigations of 
the shock spallation of copper showed that the spallation strength of copper single crystals is generally higher than that of poly -
crystalline copper, while the spallation strength decreases with rising temperature (Razorenov et al., 2014). Moreover, the spallation 
strength of copper is related to the initial temperature of shock loading. At lower shock velocities, the spallation strength begins to 
decrease when the initial temperature exceeds 900 K, but at higher shock velocities, the spallation strength decreases monotonously 
with increasing initial temperature (Tian et al., 2020). Besides studies on the spallation of metals, the spallation behavior of other 
materials, such as silicon carbide (Li et al., 2017), ceramics (Branicio et al., 2013), metallic glasses (Demaske et al., 2019; Song et al., 
2022; Wen et al., 2019) or composites (Austin et al., 2012; Xiong et al., 2019), have also received extensive attention. The shock 
simulation results of single crystalline silicon carbide showed that the spallation strength first increases, followed by a decline with 
increasing shock velocity. Branicio et al. (2013) found the spallation strength of the AlN ceramics depends on the crystallographic 
directions. Spallation studies on metallic glasses have also found that softening occurs near the voids collapse region due to localized 
temperature increase. Therefore, spallation occurs in high temperature regions instead of locations associated with maximum tensile 
stress (Demaske et al., 2019). The above studies show that factors such as shock velocity, temperature and voids have important effects 
on the spallation strength and microstructural deformation mechanism of the material. However, there is no research on the spallation 
of Ni-based single crystal superalloys and the effects of these factors on the spallation strength and microstructural evolution of these 
superalloys are poorly understood. The previous research mainly focused on the tensile (Ma et al., 2016; Wu et al., 2012, 2021), creep 
(Khoei et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022), friction and wear (Zhu et al., 2022), and fatigue (Chen et al., 2020) mechanical properties of nickel 
base single crystal superalloys by MD simulations. At present, part of the research on superalloys has paid attention to the mechanical 
properties and plastic deformation behavior under shock loading. For example, Lee et al. (2005) discovered that the work hardening 
rate of superalloys increases and deformation twins become more active under high strain rate shock loading. Experimental studies 
have also shown that the mechanical properties of Ni-based single crystal superalloys exhibit a non-monotonic temperature depen -
dence under high strain rate shock loading, which is caused by structural rearrangements at different temperatures (Zaretsky et al., 
2005). In addition, some studies have combined experimental results with numerical simulations to predict the failure mode and 
resistance characteristics of superalloys under shock loading (Erice et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2019) . To sum up, the effects of shock 
velocity, temperature and voids on the deformation process and mechanism of the spallation of superalloys are still unclear and remain 
a major ongoing issue. 
Therefore, in order to deeply reveal the dynamic mechanical behavior and spallation deformation mechanism of Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys under shock loading, we preformed MD to simulate the shock loading process and analyze the microstructure 
evolution characteristics, spallation modes and spallation strength of superalloys. In addition, one has to consider that voids cannot be 
completely eliminated in the actual material due to the immature manufacturing process, and that the voids have an attenuation effect 
on the shock wave stress. Hence, the model is divided into two types, with voids and without voids, and simulations and discussions are 
carried out for both scenarios, respectively. The obtained results shed light on the understanding of the failure mechanism and the 
dynamic mechanical behavior of superalloys under shock loading.

3.1. Validation of MD simulations 
Before performing the shock-induced spallation simulations, the Hugoniot relationship curves of single crystalline nickel and single 
crystalline aluminum were calculated to verify the correctness of the simulation method and the interaction potential function. The 
reason for choosing the two prototype materials was the lack of experimental results for the shock Hugoniot curve of Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys in the current literature. The calculation of the Hugoniot curve adopts the non-equilibrium MD simulation method 
with absorbing boundary conditions. This method can significantly eliminate the reflection of the shock wave on the boundary, so as to 
realize long-term shock loading simulations, which are widely used in the calculation of shock Hugoniot curves ( Bolesta et al., 2007 ; 
Guan et al., 2020 , 2021 ). Fig. 2 shows the calculated and experimental P - V/V

3.2. Void collapse mechanisms 
The collapse of voids will attenuate and dissipate the energy of the shock loading, and has an important influence on the spallation 
deformation mechanism under shock loading ( Guan et al., 2019 ; Liao et al., 2018 ; Wen et al., 2019 ). Fig. 3 displays the distribution of 
the local von Mises shear strain invariant and the evolution of the void shape when the shock velocities are 0.5 and 1 km/s, 
respectively. Fig. 3 (a) reveals that the void is continuously squashed in the vertical direction at a shock velocity of 0.5 km/s, which 
indicates that the void collapses in the vertical direction of the shock direction. In order to explain the above-mentioned void collapse 
mechanism, the distribution of the local von Mises shear strain invariant was calculated as shown in Fig. 3 (a). The maximum value of 
the von Mises shear strain invariant appears at the upper and lower ends of the void, which results in void shrinkage in the vertical 
direction, as shown by the black arrows in Fig. 3 (a). When the shock velocity increases to 1 km/s the void is continuously squashed in 
the horizontal direction and the maximum value of the local von Mises shear strain invariant appears on the left side of the void, 
leading to void shrinkage in the horizontal direction (horizontal collapse mechanism of the void). When the shock velocity is larger 
than 1 km/s, the void still shows a horizontal collapse mechanism. The characteristics of the vertical collapse mechanism and the 
horizontal collapse mechanism of superalloys are essentially consistent with the plastic mechanism and the internal jetting mechanism 
of void collapse for nano-porous metals ( Li et al., 2018b ; Neogi and Mitra, 2014 ; Neogi et al., 2019 ; Tian et al., 2021 ; Xiang et al., 2017 ) 
. In the study of the void collapse process of nano-porous metals, it was found that there is a plastic mechanism that leads to void 
collapse vertical to the shock direction under relatively lower shock velocity. When the shock velocity is higher, the internal jetting 
mechanism causes void collapse horizontal to the shock direction. The difference between these two mechanisms is that the collapse in 
the plastic mechanism is caused by shear stress, while the collapse in the internal jetting mechanism is dominated by normal stress. 
The existence of the void induces the reflection, refraction and other interactions of the stress wave during the stress wave 
propagation near the void. In order to explore whether a previous void will affect the collapse of subsequent voids, the distribution of 
the local von Mises shear strain invariant and the evolution of the void shape of the entire superalloys sample at 0.5 km/s and 1 km/s 
were also characterized, as shown in Fig. 4 . It can be seen that the existence of a preceding void does not affect the collapse mechanism 
of the subsequent voids. In summary, the void collapse mechanism is only related to the shock velocity. At a lower shock velocity, the 
plastic mechanism controlled by shear stress causes void collapse in the vertical direction, while at a higher shock velocity, the internal 
jetting mechanism dominated by normal stress leads to void collapse in the horizontal direction. 
3.3. Deformation mechanism under shock loading 
Studying the microstructure evolution under shock loading aims to lay a foundation for a better understanding of the spallation 
mechanism ( Li et al., 2017 , 2019 ; Liao et al., 2015 ; Neogi and Mitra, 2017a ; Guan et al., 2019 , 2020 , 2021 ; Shao et al., 2013 , 2019 ; 
Song et al., 2022 ). To clearly show the evolution characteristics of microstructure under shock loading, only one cubic structure unit of 
the superalloy at the front end of the shock loading in the Fig. 1 (c) is shown for observation. Fig. 5 shows the microstructure evolution 
of a sample with the void radius of 1.5 nm under shock velocities of 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 km/s. When the shock velocity is 0.5 km/s, it can

face-centered cubic (FCC) crystals. As shown in the Fig. 5 (b), the Shockley dislocations shear the γ’ precipitates by slipping along the

Further, the atomic configuration and evolution of the superalloy samples under shock loading were analyzed, as shown in Figs. 7 
and 8 . The analysis of the atomic configuration shows that a complex structural phase transition gradually appears in the samples as 
the shock velocity increases, and the fraction of closely packed hexagonal (HCP) atoms in the structure gradually decreases and finally 
disappears. When the shock velocity is 0.5 km/s, there is basically no obvious phase transition. When the shock velocity is greater than

Mitra, 2017c ). When the shock velocity is greater than 2.5 km/s, the atomic configuration transforms from the FCC structure to a 
disordered structure.

3.4. Shock induced spallation behavior 
Spallation is a classic failure mode under shock loading. The interaction between the rarefaction wave reflected by the free surface 
and the unloading wave generates a strong tensile stress, resulting in spallation failure. The detailed spallation process is illustrated in 
Fig. 9 . When the superalloy model is subjected to shock loading, a compressive shock wave will be formed and propagate from the left 
end of the sample. The atomic compressive stress increases in the area (the blue area) where the shock wave passes, as shown in Fig. 9 
(a) and (b). In addition, the shock wave has a double-wave structure, i.e., the upper part of the shock wave front is an elastic wave, and 
the lower part is a plastic wave (see Fig. 9 ), a consequence of the larger propagation velocity of the elastic wave as compared to the 
plastic wave. The double-wave structure is a typical characteristic observed under the action of one-dimensional elastoplastic shock 
waves ( Li et al., 2019 ; Renou et al., 2017 ; Song et al., 2022 ; Wen et al., 2019 ; Zhao et al., 2021 ). 
After loading for 5 ps, the piston is removed to produce an unloading wave. Fig. 9 (d) illustrates the simultaneous propagation of the 
stress wave and the unloading wave at t = 6 ps. At t = 7 ps, the shock wave reaches the free surface and reflects a back elastic 
rarefaction wave and plastic rarefaction waves, as shown in Fig. 9 (f). Then, the elastic reflection rarefaction wave and the unloading 
wave meet at t = 7.8 ps to form a triangular wave, as shown in Fig. 9 (h). On account of both, the rarefaction wave and the unloading 
wave, tensile forces are generated. An area with high tensile stress forms after the two waves meet, as indicated by the black dashed 
frame in Fig. 9 (i) and (j). As the loading progresses, the tensile stress gradually increases and spallation occurs when it exceeds the 
ultimate strength of the material, as shown in Fig. 9 (k). These considerations determine the shock induced spallation behavior.

4. Conclusions 
Molecular dynamics simulations were performed to study the dynamic mechanical behavior and spallation deformation mecha -
nism of superalloys under shock loading. The void collapse mechanisms, the microstructural evolution and the spallation behavior 
under different shock velocities have been analyzed in detail. The major conclusions are as follows: 
(1) Void collapse mechanisms: There are two collapse mechanisms of the voids under shock loading. The plastic mechanism 
dominated by shear stress causes the voids to collapse along the vertical direction at a lower shock intensity. When the shock 
intensity is higher, the internal jetting mechanism controlled by normal stress leads to void collapse in the horizontal direction. 
(2) Deformation mechanisms: The deformation mechanism of the superalloys under shock loading is dominated by dislocations slip 
and dragging effect at a shock velocity of 0.5 km/s. When the shock velocity varies between 0.5 and 2.5 km/s, the atomic 
structure mainly undergoes a phase transition from FCC structure to BCC structure. For shock velocities higher than 2.5 km/s, 
the atomic structure transforms from the FCC structure to a disordered structure. 
(3) Spallation failure: Under a shock velocity of 0.5 km/s no spallation occurs due to the resistance of the γ / γ’ interfacial dislocation 
network. Classical spallation and micro-spallation occur at shock velocities beyond 0.5 km/s, and the spallation failure shows a 
ductile failure mode. With the increase of the shock velocity, the spallation mode gradually changes from classic spallation at 
low shock velocity to micro-spallation at high shock velocity. 
(4) Spallation strength analysis: The spallation strength of the superalloys shows three stages with increasing shock velocity. The 
three stages correspond to the microstructure deformation characteristics of the superalloy under different shock velocities. The 
existence of voids reduces the spallation strength, and the reduction amplitude of the strength is directly proportional to the 
radius of the voids. 
(5) Temperature effects: An initial high temperature environment reduces the threshold of the spallation velocity and spallation 
strength of the Ni-based single crystal superalloys, which is mainly related to high temperature softening of superalloys.
