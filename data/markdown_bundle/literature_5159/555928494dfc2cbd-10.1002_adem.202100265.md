Microstructure and Mechanical Property of Ti –5Al–5Mo–
5V–3Cr–1Zr Alloy Fabricated by Selective Laser Melting
with a Preheated Substrate
Hao Deng, Wenbin Qiu, Longqing Chen, Sheng Cao, Yongqiang Wei, Zhendong Hu,
Xuidong Cui,* and Jun Tang*
1. Introduction
Selective laser melting (SLM) is a promising and rapid
manufacturing technology as it offers design ﬂexibility to metal-
lic parts, reduces raw material waste and shortens the lead
time.[1,2] Based on the versatility of SLM, an enormous number
of studies have been conducted to explore the feasibility of this
technology in fabricating different alloys, including Al alloys, [3,4]
steels[5] and Ti alloys. [6,7] However, in SLM process, the high
energy laser continuously interacts with
powder layers, resulting in some unique
processing features, namely, complex ther-
mal history and extremely high cooling
rates.[7,8]
According to previous studies, [9– 14] the
microstructure of SLM-fabricated Ti alloys
is nonequilibrium, owing to the aforemen-
tioned high cooling rates. For instance, α’
martensite formed in some SLM-fabricated
near α and α þ β Ti alloys,[11,14,15] because
the solidi ﬁcation rate of SLM is much
higher than the critical cooling rate
required for martensite formation. The
metastable α’ martensite structure causes
a high strength but a low ductility of
as-SLMed Ti-alloy. For the case of SLM-
built metastable Ti alloys, [12,13,16] the
combined effect of the high cooling rate
and the high concentration of β stabilizers leads to the formation
of metastable β solid solution. Therefore, the absence of α phase
results in a low strength of SLM-built metastable Ti alloys.
Undoubtedly, the nonequilibrium microstructure and corre-
spondingly unreliable mechanical property may not meet the
aeronautical standard. In addition, the repeated heating and cool-
ing processes induce a complex thermal effect. Consequently, the
formation of residual stress is inevitable during SLM process,
which increases the risk of manufacturing failure. [17,18]

The high solidi ﬁcation rate of selective laser melting (SLM) induces a non-
equilibrium microstructure in as-fabricated Ti alloys. Herein, a metastable β
Ti–5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr–1Zr alloy is successfully fabricated by SLM on a preheated
substrate at 700 /C14 C. The effects of preheating temperature on microstructure and
mechanical properties of as-fabricated specimens is systematically studied.
The phase identi ﬁcation and electron microscopic results demonstrate that the
700 /C14 C preheating temperature enables the transformation of metastable β
structure into an α þ β structure with massive amounts of nanosized α pre-
cipitates. Consequently, the SLM-fabricated specimen after preheating process
possesses higher ultimate tensile strength and hardness at 1440 MPa and
8.24 GPa, respectively. This work shows an alternative for SLM to tackle non-
equilibrium microstructure and to increase the strength of metastable Ti alloys by
introducing high temperature preheating.

Postprocess heat treatment is an effectively way to manipulate
the nonequilibrium microstructure and improve the mechanical
property of SLM-fabricated Ti alloys. [13,19,20] However, as an
ex situ treatment conducted after the SLM process, the postpro-
cess heat treatment would increase the processing steps and
lead times. If the problems of nonequilibrium microstructure
and residual stress can be addressed through an in situ route,
the processability and manufacturing ef ﬁciency of Ti alloys
can be improved.
High temperature preheating is a potential in situ heat treat-
ment method in SLM. Currently, the preheating upper limit of
most commercial SLM equipments is 200 /C14 C due to the chal-
lenge of machine design and sealing quality. At this temperature,
it is very dif ﬁcult to achieve the equilibrium phase transforma-
tion and the elimination of residual stress for Ti alloys. [21]
Recently, some studies have successfully demonstrated that high
temperature preheating ( >300 /C14 C) of SLM is desirable for print-
ing high-quality parts. Ali et al. [22] preheated the SLM substrate
temperature to 570 /C14 C for Ti – 6Al– 4V alloy, which promoted the
phase transformation from a martensitic microstructure to an
equilibrium αþβ microstructure. Schwab et al.[23] have fabricated
Ti5553 alloy with αþβ structure on a preheated substrate of
550 /C14 C. Müller et al. employed a preheated substrate up to
1000 /C14 C for printing pure tungsten, [24] and Mertens et al. fabri-
cated 7075 Al-alloy with a substrate preheated to 400 /C14 C,[25] both
works demonstrated that the substrate preheating is an effective
approach to eliminate thermal cracks induced by residual stress.
Ti– 5Al– 5Mo– 5V– 3Cr– 1Zr (Ti-55531) alloy is a typical metasta-
ble Ti-alloy, which has been widely used in the aerospace
industry. This alloy shows a good heat treatability, and the
heat treatment temperature of Ti-55531 is in the range of
600– 880 /C14 C.[26,27] Similar to the other SLMed metastable Ti
alloys, our previous works has showed that the microstructure
the SLM-fabricated Ti-55531 (without substrate preheating) is
a nonequilibrium β structure. To explore the feasibility of high
temperature preheating in the SLM manufacturing of Ti-55531
alloy, this work aims to investigate the effect high temperature
preheating on microstructure and mechanical properties of
Ti-55531 alloy.
2. Experimental Section
The Ti-55531 spherical powder feedstock was produced by elec-
trode induction melting inert gas atomization (Chengdu Luster
Advanced Materials Technology Co., Ltd.). The information
regarding morphology, size distribution and chemical composi-
tion of Ti-55531 powder particles have been described in our
previous study. [10] Ti-55531 cylindrical samples with dimension
of Φ10 /C2 60 (Z ) mm was manufactured by a SLM-Solutions 280
system with a high temperature platform module which can heat
the substrate up to 800 /C14 C. The SLM parameters were set as the
laser power of 280 W, the scanning speed of 1100 mm s /C0 1,
the hatch spacing of 120 μm and the layer thickness of 30 μm.
To evaluate the effect of platform temperature on the microstruc-
ture and mechanical property of as-built Ti-55531, we investi-
gated two types of platform temperatures: one was at 200 /C14 C,
which was commonly used in fabricating SLM Ti alloys (named
S200), and the other one was 700 /C14 C (named S700).
The microstructure characterizations and mechanical prop-
erty evaluations were performed at different deposition heights,
i.e., bottom region ( Z ¼ 5 mm), middle region ( Z ¼ 30 mm), and
top region ( Z ¼ 58 mm), as schematically shown in Figure 1 .
X-ray diffraction (XRD; D8 Advance, Bruker; step size of 0.01 /C14 ),
ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscope (SEM; FEI-Inspect
F50), and transmission electron microscopy (TEM; FEI-Tecnai
G2 F20) were used to analyze the phase and microstructure of
as-fabricated Ti-55531 at different substrate temperatures.
SEM and nanoindentation samples were prepared by using a
Buehler AutoMet 250 polisher. SEM samples were etched by
Kroll’s agent (1 vol% HF þ 4 vol% HNO 3 þ 95 vol% H 2O).
TEM foils were prepared by ion beam milling (Gatan-PIPS II
695). Nanoindentation tests were carried out on a Nano
Indenter G200 apparatus equipped with a diamond Berkovich
indenter. Arrays of indentations were made at each specimen
height with a 30 μm spacing between adjacent indentations,
and the loading depth was 1500 nm. The compression tests were
conducted on cylindrical specimens ( Φ3 /C2 4.5 mm3) by using an
INSTRON 8801 machine under a loading speed of 0.3 mm
min/C0 1. Tensile tests were also performed on the same
INSTRON 8801 machine with strain rate of 10 /C0 3 s/C0 1, according
to ASTM-E8 standard.

extremely high ( >103 /C14 Cs /C0 1),[8] which is similar to the quench-

β!α transition. Moreover, we can deduce that the substrate tem-
perature of 200 /C14 C is not high enough to allow the β!α phase
transformation to occur in Ti-55531 alloys during the SLM
process.

for α precipitates once the temperature is below 600 /C14 C.
The top region, which is far away from the substrate compared
with the middle and bottom regions, should experiences a lower
in situ “aging” treatment temperature, so an even slow kinetics
for α precipitations is expected in the top specimen.
Based on the microstructural analyses of S200 and S700 sam-
ples, we can deduce the possible α precipitation mechanism in
S700: the metastable β structure is resulted from the rapid cool-
ing, and the α precipitates formed from metastable β during the
in situ aging process induced by the high temperature preheat-
ing. It is well accepted that the precipitation of α is due to element
diffusion. The metastable β solid solution is in a supersaturated
condition, which means elemental diffusion will be enabled dur-
ing the in situ heat treatment below the β transus temperature,
giving rise to the precipitation of α. According to our previous
work,[13] a considerable amount of low-angle grain boundaries
was found in SLM-prepared metastable β Ti-55531 alloys.
A number of factors, such as the convection of melts, surface
tension, residual stress, etc., are responsible for the formation
of low-angle grain boundaries. The disordered arrangement of
atoms in low-angle grain boundaries provides nucleation sites
for α precipitates. In addition, the growth of α precipitates is
related to elemental diffusion. Previous investigation about
Ti-55531 alloys clari ﬁed that the size of α precipitates is in
micrometer-scale once the aging temperature is beyond
640 /C14 C.[30] However, the size of α precipitates is much smaller
at a nominal preheating temperature of 700 /C14 C in this work,
which indicates that the component is actually subject to an aging
temperature lower than 640 /C14 C. In SLM, the heat transfer
through the substrate mainly consists of three aspects: the heat
conduction between the substrate and the beneath components,
the heat conduction between the substrate and the above pow-
ders and specimens, and the thermal convection of circulating
gas. Although the nominal substrate temperature is at 700 /C14 C,
there will be heat dissipation due to the Ar gas circulation

3.2. Mechanical Property
The mechanical properties of Ti alloys are controlled by the
microstructures. To reveal the relationship between the micro-
structure and property, we perform nanoindentation, tensile,
and compression tests to evaluate the mechanical performances
of SLM-fabricated Ti-55531 specimens with different substrate
preheating temperatures. Considering that microstructural inho-
mogeneity is absent along building direction in S200, only one
specimen is taken from the middle part of the sample for all the
relevant tests. Figure 5 shows the nanoindentation curves of
load– displacement (Figure 5a) and hardness – displacement
(Figure 5b) samples. During the loading process, the load on
S700 specimen (500 mN) is higher than that of S200
(130 mN) for the same displacement. As a result, S700 shows
a much higher average hardness value of 8.24 GPa than that
of S200 (2.21 GPa).
Figure 6 depicts the tensile – displacement and compression –
displacement curves of these samples. As shown in Figure 6a,
the S200 sample exhibits excellent performance in elongation
with an engineering strain to failure 16.9%. However, the corre-
sponding yield strength (YS) and ultimate tensile strength (UTS)
of S200 are 801 and 816 MPa, respectively, which are much lower
than those of a forged Ti-55531 with a YS of 1200 MPa. In con-
trast, S700 has a high fracture strength of 1440 MPa. However,
there is an extremely low elongation to failure and no obvious
yielding, indicating the absence of plastic deformation. To eval-
uate the mechanical performances of S700 samples at different
building height, we conduct compression tests at the bottom,
middle and top regions. For comparison, the same test is also
carried out on the S200 sample (Figure 6c). The results show
similar compression strain (about 11 – 12%) and yield strength
(1942, 1950, and 1955 MPa for the bottom, middle, and top
regions) in the three heights of S700 sample, which implies sim-
ilar microstructures at different building heights. S200 is much
lower in compression strength than S700, but it exhibits good
compression ductility, which is similar to the results in tensile
tests. quite well in elongation as its behaviors in tensile tests.
Figure 6d,e display the tensile fracture surface of S200 and
S700 samples. Dimples are observed in S200, evidencing a
ductile fracture resulted from microvoid coalescence, which is
consistent with the good ductility in tensile and compression
tests. On the contrary, S700 shows a transgranular transcrystal-
line fracture mode according to the brittle quasi-cleavages.
The mechanical properties of Ti alloys are highly sensitive to
the morphology, size and volume fraction of α phases. In the case
of S200 specimen, the microstructure consists of metastable β
phases without α precipitates. The tensile deformation behavior
is inﬂuenced by the bcc metastable β. Considering the increased
number of slip systems in bcc comparing with that in hcp, meta-
stable bcc β should has a better ductility than hcp α. In addition,
there is no α/β interface in the S200 specimen, which can impede
dislocation glide. Therefore, S200 shows a high ductility but low
strength, which was also observed in our previous works includ-
ing the Ti-55531 samples prepared by laser melting deposited [29]
and EOS-M290 SLM. [13] For S700, typical β-transformed micro-
structure with α nanoprecipitates is observed. It has been ﬁrmly
proved that nanosized α is considered as a strengthening phase
in Ti alloys. [31] First, the size of α is not large enough to allow
distinct dislocation slipping. [31] Second, a large amount of α
phases introduces a considerable amount of α/β interfaces,
which effectively hinders the dislocation slip, but it is also prone
to cause stress concentration and even early fracture. Hence, it is
difﬁcult for S700 to deform during either tensile or compression
tests, resulting in an obvious hardening effect with a high
strength and a poor ductility.
4. Conclusions
This work demonstrated the microstructure evolution and its
inﬂuence on mechanical properties of metastable Ti-55531 alloy
fabricated by selective laser melting with a high temperature sub-
strate preheating. The microstructure of the sample subjected to
the normal 200 /C14 C preheating is metastable β phase without α
