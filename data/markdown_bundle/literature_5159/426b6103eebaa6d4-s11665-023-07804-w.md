Effects of Al Content on the Microstructure, Mechanical
Properties, and Oxidation Behavior of CoCrNiAl xY Middle
Entropy Alloys
Dunying Deng, Hanqing Wen, Shengli Ling, Hai Shan, Yonggang Tong, Jian Liu, and Zhihai Cai
Submitted: 3 August 2022 / Revised: 3 November 2022 / Accepted: 20 December 2022 / Published online: 30 January 2023
Developing advanced materials with excellent mechanical properties and high-temperature oxidation
performance are important to the potential application in high-temperature structural parts. In the paper,
CoCrNiAlxY ( x = 0.2, 0.6, 1.0 and 1.4) middle entropy alloys were prepared, and the effects of Al content
on the microstructure, mechanical properties and oxidation behavior at 1200 /C176C of CoCrNiAlxY middle
entropy alloys were studied. The phase of CoCrNiAl xY alloys is changing from FCC to BCC with increasing
Al content; thereinto, CoCrNiAl 0.6Y alloy forms duplex FCC plus BCC phase structure. The hardness and
fracture strength of CoCrNiAl xY( x = 0.2, 0.6, 1.0) alloys increase with increasing Al content. When Al
content is 1.4, the hardness and fracture strength of CoCrNiAl 1.4Y alloy decrease, which result from the tiny
variation of alloy microstructure. The oxidation behavior at 1200 /C176C of CoCrNiAl xY alloys conﬁrms that
compact aluminum oxide ﬁlm can be formed on the CoCrNiAl 0.6Y alloy, which slows down the oxidation
rate of the alloy. The CoCrNiAl 0.6Y alloy has the best oxidation resistance with lower oxidation rate of
1.467 3 10211 g2/(cm4 s).
Keywords CoCrNi middle entropy alloy, high-temperature
oxidation, mechanical properties, microstructure
1. Introduction
Since high-entropy alloys (HEAs) ﬁrstly proposed by Y eh
et al. (Ref 1), a lot of research has been performed on studying
different kinds of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) (Ref 2-5) and
middle entropy alloys (MEAs) (Ref 6-9) in recent years.
CoCrNi MEAs composed of three kinds of elements with equal
atomic ratios have become a research hotspot in metallic
materials ﬁeld (Ref 10). CoCrNi MEAs exhibit better mechan-
ical properties than some typical HEAs and most multiple-
principal-component alloys (MPCAs), the tensile strengths and
failure strains can be achieved 1 GPa and 70% at room
temperature, and K
JIc fracture-toughness values are higher than
200 MPa m 1/2(Ref 11). Although the room- and high-temper-
ature tensile strength is not satisfactory, then ternary CoCoNi
MEAs have better work hardening capacity and plasticity than
some four and ﬁve components HEAs/MEAs (Ref 12-14).
Based on the mentioned above, CoCrNi MEAs are an excellent
alloy, which can be used as the matrix alloy to design different
alloys with required performance by adding other elements.
Generally speaking, the mechanical properties of CoCrNi
MEAs can be improved after adding the particular elements
because of solid solution strengthening, grain boundary
strengthening or precipitation strengthening mechanism. Re-
cent publications (Ref 6, 15-19) have conﬁrmed that adding
some elements such as N, B, Si, Al, Mo and Ti can increase
overall strength of the CoCrNi MEAs. For example, by adding
Al and Ti, the tensile strength of CoCrNi MEAs at room
temperature can reach 1.3 GPa while maintaining 45%
elongation (Ref 15). CoCrNi MEAs with Al, Ti and Ta present
an ultimate strength of 1012 MPa at room temperature and a
yield strength of 695 MPa at 700 /C176 C (Ref 10). Compared with
CoCrNi MEAs, the hardness of CoCrNiMo

an increment from 187.5 HV to 309.5 HV , and the yield
strength and ultimate tensile strength also increase from 339 to
644 MPa and from 810 to 1071 MPa, respectively.

due to its strong interaction with transition metal elements. The

several strengthening mechanisms by the addition of Al via

2. Experimental Methods
2.1 Alloy Preparation
High-purity elements of Co, Cr, Ni, Al and Y ( ‡ 99.9
wt.%) were used to prepare CoCrNiAl xY MEAs. Nominal
chemical components are displayed in Table 1. All ingots were
prepared in a vacuum arc melting furnace with an argon
atmosphere. To ensure the uniformity of the alloy composition,
each sample was turned over and remelted ﬁve times. Finally,
the button shape ingots were prepared.
2.2 Cyclic Oxidation Test
The ingots were cut into 5 9 5 9 2 mm square plates by
using an electronic wire-cutting machine (DK77 EDM). All
specimens were prepared by grinding with silicon carbide
abrasive papers and polishing with a polisher. These samples
were cleaned using deionized water and alcohol in an ultrasonic
machine. Before the cyclic oxidation test, the weight of these
samples was recorded using an electronic balance with one-
thousand scale. The cyclic oxidation experiment was conducted
at 1200 /C176 C with air ﬂow rate of 400 ml min
/C0 1, and these
samples ﬁxed in individual crucible were placed in the center of
tube furnace. During cyclic oxidation experiment, these
samples were taken out from the tube furnace after high-
temperature oxidation of 10 h, air-cooled at room temperature
and weighed the weight. The total cyclic times were 6 times,
and the total oxidation time was 60 h.
2.3 Characterization
An electronic universal tensile machine (CMT5105GL) was
used to measure the compressive mechanical properties. Each
compression performance testing was repeated three times, and
the samples used for the test were cut into a cylindrical shape
with a length of 7.5 and a diameter of 5 mm. Hardness tests
were conducted with a semi-automatic micro-hardness tester
(HVST-1000Z), and the loading force and duration time were
100 N and 15 s.
A D8A x-ray diffractometer (XRD, German BRUKER
company) was used for the phase identiﬁcation. XRD was
performed with the diffraction angle from 20 /C176 to 80 /C176 at a
scanning speed of 5 /C176 /min. Scanning electron microscope (SEM,
JSM-7900F) and energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS, EDAX
Octance Elect Super) were used to observe and analyze the
microstructure and element distribution.

In the early oxidation stage, abundant oxygens are dis-
tributed around the alloy matrix, and the oxidation reaction is
controlled by the interface metal. As the oxidation reaction goes
on, the oxidation ﬁlm forms. The further oxidation reaction is
controlled by the diffusion of oxygen and metal element, and
the diffusion rate of element will decide the oxidation rate of
alloys. As conﬁrmed by calculation (Ref 38), Al in Ni matrix
has the largest interdiffusion coefﬁcient in most cases, and the
order of interdiffusion coefﬁcients in Ni matrix is Al, Cr and
Co. As the oxidation ﬁlm forms, the compact oxidation ﬁlm can
slow down the element diffusion and, thus, improve the
oxidation resistance of alloys.
In the CoCrNiAl
xY MEAs, Al and Cr elements distributed
on the interface are easily be oxidized based on the lower
activation energy of metal oxides in the early oxidation stage.
CoCrNiAl0.2Y alloy with the lower Al content, which cannot
form the compact oxidation ﬁlm. As the oxidation reaction goes
on, the elements of oxygen, chromium and aluminum in the
metal matrix diffuse continuously, which form a near 30 lm
discontinuous oxidation layer. With enough Al content, the
compact oxidation ﬁlm can be formed quickly in the
CoCrNiAl0.6Y alloy, which slows down the element diffusion.
So, the CoCrNiAl 0.6Y alloy has better oxidation resistance. The
thicker oxidation ﬁlm is formed on the surface of CoCrNiAlY
and CoCrNiAl 1.4Y alloys with the higher Al content, but the
difference of thermal expansion coefﬁcient between the oxida-
tion ﬁlm and matrix which makes the cracks and spallation of
oxidation ﬁlm. The element will accelerate to diffuse into the
matrix; the discontinuous thicker aluminum oxide can be found
in the matrix of CoCrNiAl
1.4Y alloy.
4. Conclusions
In this study, effects of Al content on the microstructure,
mechanical properties and oxidation behavior at 1200 /C176 Co f
CoCrNiAlxY ( x = 0.2, 0.6, 1.0 and 1.4) MEAs were investi-
gated. The following conclusions were made:
1. The phase of CoCrNiAl xY alloys changes from FCC to
BCC with increasing Al content. CoCrNiAl 0.6Y alloy
forms duplex FCC plus BCC phase structure; thereinto,
Co is distributed uniformly in the alloy, Al and Ni are
majorly distributed in the BCC phase, and Cr is dis-
tributed in the FCC phase.
2. The hardness and compression strength of CoCrNiAlxY
(x = 0.2, 0.6, 1.0) alloys increase with increasing Al con-
tent. When Al content is 1.4, the hardness and fracture
strength of CoCrNiAl

CoCrNiAl0.6Y alloy is about 418.5 HV . The yield
strength, fracture strength and compressibility of CoCr-
NiAl
0.6Y are 482.4( ± 5) MPa, 1758.7( ± 5) MPa and
23%, respectively.
