Fe–Co based alloys were strengthened through Al2O3 homogenous dispersion within the matrix, using
mechanical alloying method (MA). The structure and properties of alloys were investigated by
X-ray diffraction (XRD), ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscopy, Vickers hardness, tensile,
compression and soft magnetic test. By Al
2O3 nanoparticle reinforcing, the Fe–Co alloys have shown
outstanding mechanical properties besides the high saturation magnetisation and high curie
temperature, which can beneﬁt their industrial application.
Keywords: mechanical alloying, mechanical characterisation, dispersion strengthening, Fe – Co alloy
Introduction
Fe–Co alloys were discovered with high saturation mag-
netisation and high initial permeability. 1 However, the
alloys are very brittle and not easy for cold-processing,
limiting the applications of these alloys. For example, the
next generation aircraft and automobiles require advanced
magnetic materials in smaller sizes that can stand up high
torque or high speeds, heavy loads and high tempera-
tures.
2,3 Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop ma-
terials for excellent magnetic and mechanical properties.
Numerous studies have been expanded for these two alloys,
mainly focused on improving mechanical properties by
adding alloying elements. Material strengthening mech-
anisms mainly include: solid solution strengthening; phase
transformation; ﬁne grain; precipitation hardening;
dispersion; deformation strengthening, etc.
4 Currently, the
industrial production of Fe–Co alloys are mainly
Fe-(27–35) Co with 0 ?5–0?6% Cr and Fe-49Co with 2%
V. Particularly Fe 48Co50V2 alloy has the maximum

Results and discussion
Characterisation of composites
X-ray diffraction analysis of the sintered Fe 50Co50–
x%Al2O3 (x50, 0 ?25, 0 ?5, 0 ?75, 1) materials, in the 2 h
scan range 20 u–130u, revealed the presence of cubic
structure with (110) preferentially oriented plane as
shown in Fig. 1. The materials with different contents of
Al
2O3 all formed Fe-50 Co phase during sintering. The
XRD pattern displayed Bragg reﬂection peaks at
2h544?82u,6 5 ?06u,8 2 ?70u,9 9 ?42u and 116 ?96u corre-
sponding to the (110), (200), (211), (220) and (310)
planes, respectively. No oxide formation or contami-
nation was detected. Forty hours of high-energy milling
leads to the disappearance of Fe, Co peaks but the
formation of Fe-50Co solid solution, suggesting that Fe-
50Co bcc structure was formed.
The powder particles were repeatedly ﬂattened, cold
welded, fractured and re-welded by high-energy milling.
This process increased the chances to get a homogeneous
mixture. The initial spherical particles disappeared and
Fe–Co–Al
2O3 powder aggregated into nanostructured
particles. The composite particles at this time had a
characteristic layered structure consisting of various
combinations of the initial constituents.
The inﬂuence of Al
2O3 on long range ordering of the
matrix alloy was analysed using the XRD patterns
obtained from very slow scan (0 ?02
u 2h min21) exper-
iments. For different contents of Al 2O3, there were no
signiﬁcant changes in the peak positions and the FWHM
of the super lattice lines to be observed, which veriﬁed
that there was no change in the ordering fractions.
Characterisation of composite constituents
The SEM images of Fe 50Co50 melting alloy and the
sintered Fe 50Co50–x%Al2O3 (x50, 0 ?25, 0 ?5, 0 ?75, 1)
materials were shown in Fig. 2 a–f.
The melting alloy was very compact, where the grain
sizes were very small, about only a few micrometres, mainly
pure Fe-50Co phase. The sintered alloys grains had fully
grown, so the sizes were much bigger, about a few milli-
metres owing to longer high temperature insulation. The
alloys all emerged small pits, which formed by oxides
shedding. Fe
50Co50–0%Al2O3 and Fe50Co50–0?75%Al2O3
alloys had lower density because of the porosity. Fe50Co50–
0?25%Al2O3,F e 50Co50–0?5%Al2O3 and Fe 50Co50–
1%Al2O3 alloys almost had no porosity. These alloys all
appeared small oxide particles except some block Fe 2O3.
The particle sizes increased with the increasing content of
Al2O3. By analysing, the spherical particles were Al 2O3.
Al2O3 particles in Fe50Co50–0?5%Al2O3 alloys were trivial
and dispersed uniformly. However, parts of Al2O3 particles
in Fe50Co50–1%Al2O3 had aggregated to about 1 mm. The
adding amount of Al 2O3 cannot be too much in order to
prevent the aggregation, which can inevitably affect the
magnetic properties.
