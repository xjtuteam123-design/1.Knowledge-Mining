ABSTRACT

Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr (at.%) alloy was explored in detail. The microstructural evolution of these alloys con -
taining Ge was characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD), 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The mechanical properties including room-temperature fracture 
toughness and high-temperature compression strength were measured. Only Nbss and β -Nb
5

1. Introduction 
The niobium ‑ silicon (Nb
–
Si)-based alloys have attracted significant 
research attention as the potential candidate for the hot-end components 
of next-generation aero engine due to their excellent properties. The Nb- 
Si-based alloys have higher melting point ( > 1750 
◦
C), low density 
(6.6 – 7.2 g/cm
3
) and outstanding strength at high temperature [ 1 – 3 ], 
which guarantee their use at higher temperature (1200 – 1400 
◦

well known that Nb-Si-based alloys belong to the class of in-situ com -
posites consisting of Nb-based solid solution (Nbss) and silicides (Nb
5

3

Nb

room-temperature fracture toughness to the alloys, and the brittle sili -
cides phase offers the high-temperature strength [ 2 , 4 ]. 
Although the Nb-Si-based alloys exhibit excellent high-temperature 
strength, the inferior room-temperature fracture toughness and poor 
high-temperature oxidation resistance restrict their applications. 
Numerous alloying elements mostly belonging to the groups IVB, VB, 
VIB, IIIA and IVA in the periodic table are added to Nb
–
Si binary alloys 
for improving their toughness and oxidation resistance. As a result, the 
Nb-Si-based alloys have been developed from binary to multielement 
systems, among which the ternary Nb-Si-Ti system alloys are widely 
applied because these alloys can availably achieve a balance between 
room-temperature toughness and high-temperature oxidation resis -
tance. Titanium (Ti) can efficaciously improve the toughness and 
oxidation resistance. However, excessive Ti can decrease the melting 
point and ductile-brittle transition temperature of Nb
–
Si alloys. Thus, 
the content of added Ti should not exceed 24 at.% [ 5 , 6 ]. Among the 
alloying elements additions for improving the high-temperature oxida -

oxidation resistance of Nb-Si-based alloys [ 8 , 9 ]. At present, the studies 
on addition of Ge are mostly focused on improving the high-temperature

2. Experimental procedures 
The five nominal compositions of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr- x Ge ( x = 1, 
3, 5, 7 and 9 at.%, respectively) button ingots were prepared in a vac -
uum non-consumable arc furnace with a tungsten electrode under an 
argon atmosphere in a water-cooled copper crucible. These six-element 
alloys are named as 1Ge, 3Ge, 5Ge, 7Ge and 9Ge alloys. The raw ma -
terials with high purities were selected, i.e., Nb (99.9 wt%), Si (99.99 wt 
%), Ti (99.4 wt%), Al (99.9 wt%), Cr (99.4 wt%) and Ge (99.9 wt%). 
Each button ingot (100 g) was melted five times to guarantee the 
chemical homogeneity of alloys. 
The cuboid specimens with dimension of 6 × 6 × 3 mm
3 
were cut by 
electro-discharge machining (EDM) in the middle of the button ingots. 
Then the specimens were polished with SiC sandpapers for microstruc -
ture analysis. The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with energy- 
dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) analysis was used to characterize 
the microstructure and analyze the solid solution of alloying elements in 
different phases. The constituent phases of the Ge-added alloys were 
determined by X-ray diffraction (XRD). The volume fraction and grain 
size of phases were measured by using ImagePro 6.0 software. The 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM, talos f200x) at an accelerating 
voltage of 200 kV was used to acquire selected area electron diffraction 
(SAED) patterns of constituent phases, high-resolution images of phase 
interface and dislocation distribution. The ion-milled specimens were 
used for electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) analysis to explore the 
orientation relationships among constituent phases. 
The three-point bending test was used to test the room-temperature 
fracture toughness of experimental alloys by the single-side notch 
cantilever beam method [ 12 , 13 ]. The 2 × 4 × 20 mm
3 
cuboid specimens 
were cut by EDM from the middle of the button ingots. Each surface of 
specimen subjected to three-point bending needed to be polished with 
SiC sandpaper to achieve a scratch-free surface with desired smoothness. 
The testing equipment was an electronic universal testing machine 
(Instron5569) with the loading ratio of 0.1 mm/min. The room- 
temperature fracture toughness ( K
Q
) could be calculated by using the 
equations, presented in our previous study [ 14 ]. 
The Berkovich diamond indenter tip with curvature radius of 20 nm 
was equipped in nanoindentation instrument G200. The elastic modulus 
and nanohardness of constituent phases in the alloys with added Ge 
were acquired through nanoindentation test. The high-temperature 
compression specimens were cylinder with Φ 6 mm × 9 mm (diameter 
of 6 mm and length of 9 mm). The dynamic thermal simulation test 
machine (Gleeble-1500, US) was utilized to test the high-temperature 
compression strength at 1200 
◦
C at a strain rate of 1 × 10
 3 
s
 1
. The 
cylindrical specimens were first polished to remove scales produced 
during EDM process and then they were subjected to the compression 
test. The specimens were quenched into water promptly after high- 
temperature test to maintain the deformed microstructure. Further, 
the orientation relationships among constituent phases and dislocation 
density in high-temperature deformed microstructure were measured by 
EBSD method.

reasons for this phenomenon are revealed later.

tural images of of the as-cast Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr- x Ge ( x = 1, 3, 5, 7

The primary β -Nb

main reason for the change in position of the highest diffraction peaks

respectively, 11.11 and 7.98 at.% in 3Ge and 5Ge alloys, which are 
lower than those in 1Ge, 7Ge and 9Ge alloys presented in Table 1 . The Ti 
(1.45 Å) and Nb (1.47 Å) have almost the same radii. Therefore, high 
content of Ti dissolved in Nbss results in the contraction of Nb lattice, 
which is attributed to the change in chemical bonding in Nbss [ 15 ]. In 
contrast, lower Ti content in Nbss leads to smaller contraction of Nbss 
lattice. Furthermore, the solid solution of Ge in Nbss also results in 
contraction of the Nbss lattice, because of lower atomic radius of Ge 
(1.39 Å) than that of Nb (1.47 Å) [ 16 ]. According to the Bragg equation 
of 2 d • sin θ = n λ , the interplanar spacing ( d ) and diffraction angles ( θ ) 
exhibit the inverse proportional relationship, when λ is constant. 
Therefore, the position of the highest diffraction peaks for Nbss first 
shifts toward left when the Ge content increases from 1 to 5 at.%. Then, 
it shifts toward right with the further increase in the Ge content.

5. Conclusions 
The effects of different Ge contents on microstructure, room- 
temperature fracture toughness and high-temperature compression 
strength of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy are systematically investigated. 
The following conclusions are drawn: 
1. All Ge-added alloys consist of primary β -Nb
5
Si
3 
and chaotic Nbss/ 
β -Nb
5
Si
3 
eutectics. The result indicates that Ge intensively promotes 
the formation of β -Nb
5
Si
3
. 
The metastable Nb
3
Si phase is not present in the Ge-added alloys, 
indicating that Ge can restrain the formation of Nb
3
Si. 
2. Compared with the fracture toughness of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy 
(13.49 MPa • m
1/2
), the fracture toughness of the alloys is elevated 
when the Ge content is below 9 at.%. In particular, the 1Ge (Nb-16Si- 
22Ti-2Al-2Cr-1Ge) alloy shows the highest fracture toughness 
(18.47 MPa • m
1/2
), due to plentiful chaotic Nbss/ β -Nb
5
Si
3 
eutectics. 
With the further increase in the content of Ge, the fracture toughness 
of Ge-containing alloys decreases, because of the increase in the 
volume fraction of brittle β -Nb
5
Si
3 
phase. 
3. The elastic modulus and nanohardness of constituent phases are 
increased with Ge addition due to strengthening the bonding in the 
constituent phases by Ge. 
4. The high-temperature compression strength increases when Ge is 
added to Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloys, which is mainly attributed to 
the increase in volume fraction of primary β -Nb
5
Si
3
. Moreover, the 
dynamic recrystallization of constituent phases is limited by the low 
strains during high-temperature deformation.
