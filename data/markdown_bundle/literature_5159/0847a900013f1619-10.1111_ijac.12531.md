Ti3SiC2 Reinforced ZA27 Alloy Composites with Enhanced
Mechanical Properties
Hai-Yan Li, Yang Zhou,* Ao Cui, Yong Zheng, Zhen-Ying Huang, Hong-Xiang Zhai, and
Shi-Bo Li
Center of Materials Science and Engineering, School of Mechanical & Electronic Control Engineering, Beijing Jiaotong
University, Beijing 100044, China
This work reports the fabrication and mechanical properties of Ti 3SiC2 reinforced Zn-27 wt. % Al alloy (denoted as
ZA27). A total of 10 –40 vol. % Ti 3SiC2 reinforced ZA27 alloy composites were synthesized by hot pressing mechanically
alloyed mixtures of Ti 3SiC2 and ZA27 powders. Among the fabricated composites, 20 vol. % Ti 3SiC2/ZA27 composite possesses
the highest room temperature tensile strength, bending strength and Vickers hardness of 339 MPa, 593 MPa, and 1.13 GPa,
respectively. The improved mechanical properties of the 20 vol. % Ti
3SiC2/ZA27 composite are mainly attributed to the effects
of ﬁne-grain strengthening and dispersion strengthening.
Keywords: ZA27 alloy; Ti 3SiC2; composites; mechanical properties; microstructure
Introduction
Zn-27 wt. % Al alloy (denoted as ZA27) belonging to
the family of zinc alloys possesses good wear resistance,
low-melting point, exceptional castability, super plasticity,
easy machinability, and moderate strength compared with
common cast aluminum alloys.
1–3 So far, ZA27 alloy
matrix composites reinforced with different ceramic parti-
cles (such as SiC, Al
2O3, graphite)4,5 and short ﬁbers (such
as Al2O3 short ﬁber and ZnO whiskers)6,7 have been studied.
These composites exhibit excellent wear resistance,
enhanced hardness and modulus, and greatly reduced
creep rate compared with their parent matrix alloy.
4,8 The
improved mechanical properties indicate efﬁcient second
particle strengthening mechanism.
However, enhancing the wettability between the
ZA27 matrix and the reinforcements, and improved
mechanical properties of ZA27 alloy composites are still
challenging.
9
Recently, MAX phases (M is a transition metal, A is a
group IIIA to VIA element, and X is C or N) have
attracted much attention due to their unique combination
of properties of metals and ceramics.
10 Ti3SiC2 is one of
the most fascinating members of the MAX phases because
of its low density, high strength and modulus, damage tol-
erance, high electrical conductivity, machinability, thermal
shock resistance and oxidation resistance.
11 The above
unique properties make Ti 3SiC2 attractive as a reinforce-
ment for metals. So far, many studies have been performed
on the fabrication of MAX phases reinforced metal com-
posites, such as Ti 3SiC2/Cu,12,13 Ti3SiC2/Al,14 Ti3AlC2/
Cu,15 Ti3SiC2/NiAl,16,17 MAX/Mg.18 But no work has
been reported on MAX/ZA27 composites according to the
literature. Besides, comparing with other reinforcements
of ZA27, as shown in Table I,
19–21 the thermal expansion
coefﬁcient of Ti 3SiC2 is closest to that of ZA27. Hence,
Ti3SiC2 has the potential to become an ideal enhancement
phase of ZA27.
In this study, Ti 3SiC2 was selected as a reinforce-
ment to prepare Ti 3SiC2/ZA27 composites. The effect of
volume fractions of Ti 3SiC2 on the mechanical proper-
ties of Ti 3SiC2/ZA27 composites was studied. Phase
composition and microstructure were characterized with
X-ray diffraction, scanning electron microscopy, and
transmission election microscopy.

The starting powders were mechanically alloyed (MAed)
in a planetary mill (QM-3SP4; Nanjing Nanda Instru-

under vacuum with a speed of 300 rpm, and a ball-to-
powder weight ratio of 10:1. The MAed powders were
sieved with a /C080 mesh.
The MAed powders were ﬁrstly cold-compacted in a
graphite die using a uniaxial pressure of 5 MPa,. and
then pressurelessly sintered at 870 °C for 60 min in Ar
atmosphere with a heating rate of 30 °C/min and hot-
pressed at 430 °C for 90 min with a uniaxial pressure of
30 MPa in a hot pressing furnace (ZT-30-22y; Shanghai
Chenhua Furnace Plant, Shanghai, China). Hereinafter,
it is referred to as a two-step sintering technology.
Phase composition of the four composites was identi-

Bruker, Karlsruhe, Germany). The density of the compos-

average values. Both the tensile(GB/T 228- 2002) and the

Results and Discussion
SEM images of the raw ZA27 powder and the as-
MAed 20Ti 3SiC2/ZA27 powder are demonstrated in
Fig. 2. The raw ZA27 powder is about ~20 lm in size
(Fig. 2a), whereas the MAed powder is about 60 lm due
to agglomeration (Fig. 2b). An enlarged image as shown
in Fig. 2c clearly shows that the agglomerated particles
were composed of ﬁne grains with sizes ranging from 3 to
5 lm. It suggests that the size of the starting powders
greatly decreased after MAing. The MAed particles with
enhanced reactivity should promote sintering activity.

Besides, the test results for tensile strength and bending

contents of Ti 3SiC2 are shown in Fig. 4b. It is seen that
20Ti3SiC2/ZA27 exhibited both the highest ultimate

40Ti
3SiC2/ZA27 showed the lowest tensile strength of
212 MPa and bending strength of 422 MPa. According
to the references,
24,25 the UBS of SiC and Al 2O3 rein-
forced ZA27 matrix composites, which fabricated by stir
casting technique decreases with reinforcement contents.
The maximum UBS of SiC
p/ZA27 and Al 2O3/ZA27
composites is 387 MPa and 483 MPa, respectively,
when the reinforcement contents are up to 3 wt. %. The
UBS decreased with increasing reinforcement contents.
One of the main reasons should be ascribed to the poor
wettability between matrix and reinforcing phases. On
the contrary, the UBS of Ti
3SiC2/ZA27 composite
increases with increasing Ti 3SiC2 contents in a range of
0–20 vol. %. The UBS of 20Ti 3SiC2/ZA27 (18.4 wt. %
Ti3SiC2) is much higher than that of SiCp/ZA27 and
Al2O3/ZA27 composites, suggesting a good bonding
between ZA27 and Ti 3SiC2. As the content of Ti 3SiC2
increased to 30 –40%, both UBS and UTS decreased cor-
respondingly, possibly due to the relatively more defects
of composites.

The hardness test revealed that there is a peak hard-

The hardness of the composites decreased as the Ti 3SiC2
content was up to 40 vol. %, being inﬂuenced by the
low density. The increased hardness is attributed to the
ﬁne-grained microstructure and the relatively hard
Ti
3SiC2 particles, which act as barriers to the movement
of dislocations within the matrix.
Mechanical strength and hardness of the composite
are dependent on the content of reinforcement. Theoret-
ically, the more the Ti 3SiC2 particles were added, the
stronger strength and hardness were achieved in crys-
talline material. But in fact defects are also increased
with increasing the Ti
3SiC2 content. Mechanical strength
is more sensitive to defects than hardness, so 20Ti 3SiC2/
ZA27 composite exhibits a much higher strength com-
pared to 30Ti
3SiC2/ZA27, but a lower hardness. In
addition, some macro pores were observed in 40Ti 3SiC2/
ZA27 (Fig. 9d). So its hardness is lower than
30Ti3SiC2/ZA27. From the above analysis, it can be
concluded that the 20Ti 3SiC2/ZA27 composite exhibits
the enhanced mechanical properties.
Conclusion
The Ti3SiC2/ZA27 composites with different Ti 3SiC2
contents have been obtained through the two-step sinter-
ing technique. The effect of Ti 3SiC2 as an effective rein-
forcement phase on the mechanical properties of Ti 3SiC2/
ZA27 composites was investigated. No new phases were
detected in the Ti
3SiC2/ZA27 composites after sintering
at 870 °C. The results showed that the 20Ti 3SiC2/ZA27
composite possesses the enhanced mechanical properties
with tensile strength, bending strength and Vickers hard-
ness of 339 MPa, 593 MPa, and 1.13 GPa, respectively.
The improved mechanical properties are mainly attributed
to high-density, ﬁne-grained microstructure, and second-
phase strengthening.
