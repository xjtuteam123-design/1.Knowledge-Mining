abstract
To enhance the mechanical properties of carbon ﬁber (CF) reinforced 7075 Al alloy material
at high temperature conditions, TiO
2 coating was prepared on the surface of CF by
chemical vapor deposition. The composites were prepared by hot pressing sintering and
hot extrusion methods. The evolution of the compositional components of the composite
interface was investigated. The density, hardness, and tensile strength of the samples were
measured, and the strength/density ratio of the samples was calculated at 150
/C14 C. The
results indicate that the tensile strength of coated CF reinforced composite (535 MPa) was
increased by 21.3% and 5.7% compared to the tensile strength of 7075 Al alloy (441 MPa) and
uncoated CF reinforced composite (506 MPa), respectively. Furthermore, the strength/
density ratio of the composite also increased with the addition of TiO
2 coating. The
enhancement of coating on CF on tensile strength is attributed to the transformation of CF
failure mode.

1. Introduction
7075 Al alloy is widely used in automotive [ 1], aerospace [ 2],
and deep drilling [ 3] industries due to its high strength/weight
ratio, which can also be described as lightweight and high
strength. However, the mechanical properties of 7075 Al alloy
decrease with increasing service temperature [ 4]. Within the
past decade or so, researchers have moved from monolithic
materials to composites where reinforcing materials can be
easily added to the 7075 Al alloy matrix [ 5]. Common rein-
forcing materials include silicon carbide (SiC) [ 6], graphene [7],
and carbon ﬁber (CF) [8]. Although Kulkarni and Robi [9] as well
as Razaghian et al. [ 10] have conﬁrmed that SiC can improve
the tensile strength of composites at 150
/C14 C and even at 400 /C14 C,

However, it has been shown that the rod-shaped form of
Al
4C3 is generated on the surface of carbon materials such as
carbon nanotubes [16], graphene [17], and diamond [18] during
the sintering process of Al alloy. Due to its natural brittleness,
Al
4C3 always causes cracks [ 19]. This will harm the CF and
make it less effective in strengthening [ 20]. Under this crack,
the CF does not debond and pull out; instead, a ﬂat shear
surface appears [ 21].
Surface modiﬁcation of CF is one of the means to solve
such problems. Mu et al. [ 22] used the electroplating method
to coat Ni on the surface of CF, which formed intermetallic
compounds Al
3Ni2 and Al3Ni with the Al matrix and prevented
the formation of Al 4C3. However, when the heat treatment
time increased, the intermetallic interface almost dis-
appeared and Al
4C3 in the brittle phase was generated [ 22].
Obviously, a more stable interface helps to further improve
the performance of composite materials. Tang et al. [ 23]
investigated the properties of CF coated with Ni, Cu, and Al 2O3
coated CF reinforced 6061 Al alloy and showed that coating
Al
2O3 on CF resulted in the highest mechanical properties of
the corresponding alloy. However, in Mg-containing Al alloys,
Mg accumulates around CF and undergoes chemical reactions
with Al
2O3 [24,25]. Similar phenomena have also been
observed in Mg alloys where this interfacial reaction leads to
signiﬁcant expansion of the interface and a decrease in the
strengthening effect [ 26]. TiO
2, which is also an oxide, has
received attention due to its good physical properties and
chemical stability and low cost [ 27,28]. Research works have
found that TiO 2 interfacial zone is clean between CF and Mg
matrix without any intermetallic compounds as reaction
products and obstructs continuous interfacial reaction [ 29,30].
Coating TiO
2 on the surface of CF can form self-limiting in-
terfaces in Mg-containing Al alloys (such as 2024 Al alloy),
effectively improving the performance of 2024 Al alloy [ 24].
Therefore, in this study, CF reinforced 7075 Al alloy
(AleZneMgeCu alloy) materials were prepared using powder
metallurgy. The effect of TiO
2 coating on the mechanical
properties of the composites at 150 /C14 C was investigated, and
the interfacial evolution and fracture mechanism were dis-
cussed in detail.
2. Material and method
The 7075 Al alloy powder used in this experiment was pro-
duced by Shanghai Chaowei Nano Technology Co., Ltd. It had
a D50 particle size of approximately 20 mm and consisted of
5.720 wt % Zn, 2.324 wt % Mg, 1.533 wt % Cu, 0.280 wt % Si,
0.153 wt % Fe, 0.130 wt % Mn, 0.050 wt % Ni, and the balance
Al. The T700 polyacrylonitrile-based CF used in this study
was produced by Toray Co., Ltd. The titanium (Ti) powder
and iodine (I) powder used in the coating were 99.0% and
99.8% pure respectively and were produced by Sinopharm
Co., Ltd.
A tube furnace equipped with a quartz tube which was
used as a reaction vessel for chemical vapor deposition (CVD).
The reaction vessel had a halogenated zone and a deposition
zone [ 31]. The halogenation zone contained mechanically
mixed Ti powder and I powder in a molar ratio of 1:2 while the
deposition zone contained CF after degumming [ 31]. Argon
mixed with oxygen was chosen as the carrier gas and atmo-
sphere for the experiment [ 24]. Four different coating times
were used for CF at 900
/C14 C: CT30 (30 min), CT60 (60 min), CT90
(90 min), and CT120 (120 min). The corresponding coating
thicknesses for these four coating times were 100, 145, 180,
and 210 nm respectively [ 24].
The CF and 7075 Al alloy powder were mixed evenly in
anhydrous by mechanical stirring until complete volatiliza-
tion of the solvent. The mixed powder was then placed in a
vacuum drying oven for 4 h at 80
/C14 C. The thoroughly dried
mixed powder was placed in a graphite mold of a speciﬁc size
for hot pressing sintering. The schematic diagram of the
graphite mold, the temperature, and the pressure curve dur-
ing sintering is shown in Fig. 1b. The as-sintered samples were
then placed at 470
/C14 C for 4 h for homogenization followed by
air cooling. The as-sintered sample was placed again at 470 /C14 C
for 30 min and extruded under appropriate pressure. The
schematic diagram of hot extrusion is shown in Fig. 1 c.
Through this process, the CF in the 7075 Al alloy matrix is
oriented along the extrusion direction [ 32]. The surface of all
samples was polished and then immersed in anhydrous
ethanol for ultrasonic cleaning before conducting T6 heat
treatment (solid solution at 470
/C14 C for 120 min, water
quenched and aged at 120 /C14 C for 24 h) after drying. The sam-
ples were cut to the size required for the tensile test using
electrical discharge wire-cutting and then polished carefully.
The size of samples for the tensile test is shown in Fig. 1 d.
Tensile tests were carried out at the temperature required for
the experiment, and subsequent tests were conducted. In the
following text, for example, the sample of CF with a volume
content of 8% and a coating time of 30 min is named 8%-CT30-
7075. In addition, this experiment prepared 8%-CT0-7075 as a
control sample compared with reference 15.
The microstructures of samples ' tensile fracture were
characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, S-
4800, Tokyo, Japan). The interphase between the composite
and CF was characterized using transmission electron mi-
croscopy (TEM, Titan G2 60 e300, Hillsboro, USA) equipped
with scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) and
energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). The phase constituents
of samples were characterized using X-ray diffraction (XRD,
DX2700BH, Dandong, China) with Cu K a radiation in step
mode from 20
/C14 to 80/C14 . The Vickers hardness was measured at
room temperature via a microhardness tester (HXD-1000TM,
Shanghai, China), and the density of the composite was
measured through Archimedes’ principle. High-temperature
tensile tests were performed on the INSTRON-1121 material

4. Discussion
Interfacial reaction mainly occurs during the sintering process
(at 560
/C14 C). Table 1 shows the Gibbs free energy of some re-
actions at 560 /C14 C. According to Table 1, Zn and Cu elements do
not react with TiO 2 under 560 /C14 C. However, Al element would
react with TiO 2 producing Ti element and Al 2O3. Additionally,
Mg elements gather on the surface between Al and CF [ 24].
According to the thermomechanical analysis, Mg element
reacts with Al
2O3 producing MgO and Al. Mg element also
reacts with TiO2 producing Ti and MgO. Therefore, TiO 2 is ﬁrst
reduced by the 7075 Al alloy matrix to Ti and formed by MgO at
the interface. Thermodynamic calculations indicate that Ti
can form intermetallic compounds with Al but they were not
found by TEM because the diffusion of Ti into the 7075 Al alloy
matrix was effectively hindered by MgO [ 33]. Furthermore, C
element from CF diffuses into the 7075 Al alloy matrix during
the sintering process causing C element to react with Ti
element limited by MgO generating TiC. Following C elements
diffuse across the interfacial layer reacted with Al element
generating Al
4C3. Above process leads to the formation of the
MgO and TiC interface and the separation of rod-shaped Al 4C3
from CF, which is consistent with the TEM and EDS results.
Tensile tests generate stress concentrations forming crack
paths around brittle Al 4C3 [34]. Cracks propagate in the matrix
and the matrix tends to separate into two parts. CF is affected
by Al
4C3 causing cracks to pass through CF and samples to
shear failure. In this study, MgO and TiC were added to the
interfacial composition separating rod-shaped Al
4C3 from CF.
This reduces the percentage of CF that is damaged by shear
which is consistent with the results in Fig. 10.

of TiO

in the 7075 Al alloy matrix and increase its percentage that
breaks in tensile failure, thus allowing for a more fully
exploited stress transfer effect from CF.
5. Conclusion
Apply TiO 2 coating on the surface of CF using the chemical
vapor deposition method. After hot-pressing sintering and hot
extrusion preparation process, the TiO 2 reaction on the sur-
face of CF generates MgO and TiC when it is coated with 7075
Al alloy powder. The sample 's density, hardness, and tensile
strength were measured. The experimental results showed
that adding TiO
2 coating on the CF surface improved tensile
strength and strength/density ratio of 7075 Al alloy at 150 /C14 C.
By adding CF with TiO 2 coating to 7075 Al alloy, the sample 's
tensile strength increased by 21.3% from 441 MPa to 535 MPa
while its strength/density ratio increased from 158 (MPa/(g/
cm
3)) to 198 (MPa/(g/cm 3)). These results are consistent with
lightweight and high-strength CF reinforced 7075 Al alloy.
The tensile fracture of the composite revealed three forms
of CF failure: shear failure, tensile failure, and end pulled-out.
The interface layer can separate Al
4C3 and CF, preventing
direct crack propagation from Al 4C3 to CF and resulting in
direct cutting off of CF. Deﬂection of cracks at the interface
effectively reduces the shear failure proportion and increases
the tensile failure proportion of CF. However, an excessively
thick interface can cause cracks to propagate along its interior
and pull out CF.
