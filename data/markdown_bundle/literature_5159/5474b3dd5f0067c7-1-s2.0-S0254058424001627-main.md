ABSTRACT 
This study introduced a novel method for incorporating microstructural and mechanical heterogeneity into 
Ti – Zr – Nb alloy to improve its strength without significantly affecting the elastic modulus. Ti – Zr – Nb alloy powder 
was milled at different ball-milling energies using a planetary ball mill, mixed with parent pristine powder in 
equal weight fractions using a horizontal ball mill, and sintered using spark plasma sintering. The resulting 
bimodal-like microstructure showed an increase in tensile yield strength (up to 410 MPa) without significant 
changes in the elastic modulus ( < 6 GPa). Milling followed by sintering caused grain refinement and precipitation 
in the relevant zones of the sintered samples, resulting in a heterogeneous structure due to the unique spread of 
α -precipitates. The strength of the heterogeneity-incorporated alloys was improved as a function of milling while 
maintaining high plasticity and a low elastic modulus.

1. Introduction 
β -Ti alloys have grasped the attention of researchers in biomedical 
prostheses owing to their outstanding mechanical properties, corrosion 
resistance, and biocompatibility [ 1 ]. Initially, Al- and V-like elements 
were used as β -stabilizer in these alloys; however, they were declared 
neurotoxic and responsible for Alzheimer ’ s and osteomalacia [ 2 , 3 ]. 
Therefore, most β -Ti alloys investigated for biomedical applications in 
the last decade are free of Al and V [ 4 ]. Currently, the most suitable 
β -stabilizers are Ta, Nb, and Mo, which are accepted by the host body 
[ 5 – 7 ]. 
The appropriate content of these alloying elements defines the 
deformation modes of β -Ti alloys and their mechanical properties 
especially the elastic modulus, which is a crucial property of biomedical 
implants [ 8 – 10 ]. The d -electron concept developed by considering the 
mean bond order and mean d-orbital energy level provides an accept -
able approach to designing the composition for Ti – Zr – Nb 
low-elastic-modulus alloys [ 11 – 14 ]. This concept defines that an alloy, 
primarily composed of Ti – Zr – Nb systems, deforms by slip when it has 
high β -phase stabilit. However, alloys with lower phase stability may 
exhibit transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP), twinning-induced 
plasticity (TWIP), or both. This results in significant strain hardening 
in the alloy [ 15 , 16 ]. 
Strengthening is required for β -Ti alloys to increase their strength 
comparable to that of Ti6Al4V, which is approximately 800 MPa [ 14 ]. 
This can be achieved by compositional design, however, it may increase 
the elastic modulus of the alloys, causing stress-shielding problems. 
Likewise, the higher alloying element content can also decrease the 
plasticity of the system as the TRIP or TWIP behaviors that cause strain 
hardening may vanish [ 2 ]. 
This research is focused on fabricating heterogeneous materials [ 17 ] 
characterized by distinct hard and soft domains, aiming to enhance 
overall strength while maintaining the elongation of β -Ti alloys. How -
ever, this work aims to have consistent low elastic modulus along with 
improved strength. The strategy employed involves the deliberate par -
titioning of powders into soft and hard grains. The soft grains refer to the 
pristine material and the hard grains are achieved by varying degrees of 
work hardening among them through milling. This approach allows for 
the development of a unique distribution of strength among the grains, 
contributing to an optimized material structure that balances strength 
and elastic modulus. To achieve this, Ti – 20Zr – 23Nb (TZN), using atomic 
percent nomenclature, alloy powder that can be considered as a 
medium-entropy alloy is subjected to high-energy ball milling, thereby 
increasing the defect density of the powder through continuous cold 
welding and fracturing in a controlled atmosphere. As previously stud -
ied, the unavoidable oxygen contamination during ball milling can in -
crease the α -precipitation stability with considerably finer morphology 
[ 18 ]. The defects during the ball milling process can initiate the α -pre -
cipitation in the β matrix during heat treatment or sintering [ 15 , 19 ]. In 
this work, conventional solution treatment followed by aging was not 
employed, and SPS was considered sufficient for the required precipi -
tation. A higher Nb content, which works as a β stabilizer in the selected 
composition, and a higher cooling rate of the SPS were used to suppress 
the formation of the excess ω phase, thereby avoiding brittleness [ 20 , 
21 ]. 
2. Materials and methods 
Pre-alloyed Ti – 20Zr – 23Nb (TZN) powder, obtained from Guangzhou 
Sailong Additive Manufacturing Co., China, with an average particle size 
of 50 μ m, was utilized in this research work. The oxygen content in TZN 
was found as 0.083 ± 0.01%, measured by using the ONH determinator 
(ONH-2000 ELTRA, Germany). Pristine TZN powder was milled at 100, 
150, 200, and 300 rpm for 3 h using a planetary ball mill (Pulverisette 5, 
Fritsch). WC balls and vials were used with a 10:1 ball-to-powder ratio, 
while keeping the argon atmosphere inside the vials tightly closed to 
prevent air from entering. Subsequently, the milled TZN powders were 
blended with pristine powder in equal-weight proportions (1:1) through 
a 3-h milling session at 125 rpm using a low-energy horizontal ball mill. 
This secondary milling operation, performed in an air atmosphere with 
plastic bottles and WC balls at a 5:1 ball-to-powder ratio, was specif -
ically employed for the purpose of uniform powder mixing. The pristine 
and milled-mixed (1:1 mixing of as-received and milled powders) TZN 
powders were sintered using spark plasma sintering (SPS) at 1200 
◦
C for 
10 min with a heating rate of 100 
◦
C/min, using a 13 mm graphite die for 
SPS. Tensile and compression sub-size samples were taken from the 
SPSed pallets, following ASTM-E8 and E9 standards, and tested using a 
universal testing machine (Instron 8848) at 0.005 mm/s, accompanied 
by digital image correlation for precise strain measurement. Oxygen 
content was measured by the ONH determinator (ONH p, Eltra). 
Nanoindentation (Hysitron TI Premier, Bruker) was performed in load 
control mode at 1000 μ N maximum load with a trapezoidal loading 
profile using a 5-s loading, holding, and unholding time. Elastic modulus 
was measured using the ultrasonic pulse-echo technique (ASTM E494 ) 
which is based on measuring the ultrasonic velocity in materials. 
Microstructural and phase analysis was conducted using scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM, SU5000), electron backscattered diffraction 
(EBSD, TSL Hikari Super), transmission electron microscopy (TEM, 
Tecnai F-30 S -Twin), and X-ray diffraction (XRD, Smartlab). Further 
details about the experimental work and instruments are available in the 
referenced literature [ 22 , 23 ]. 
3. Results and discussion 
The milling or cold working of TZN alloy can lead to grain refinement 
and precipitation in the matrix upon heat treatment by providing more 
nucleation sites for the α phase [ 24 ]. In this work, only half of the matrix 
is subjected to cold working. Fig. 1 (a) presents the changes in the shape 
of pristine TZN powder as a function of the ball-milling energy with the 
speed of 100 – 300 rpm for 3 h. The powder size increases as a function of 
the milling energy due to the wrapping of highly ductile powder around 
the other particles. Initially, the highly spherical powder particles 
changed into plates, which started wrapping up themselves, thereby 
changing the particle size upon further milling. Both the topological and 
cross-sectional views of the powder exhibit the wrapping of extremely 
thin sheets. 
The EBSD and SEM images in Fig. 1 (b) show the cross-sectional 
microstructures of sintered samples of TZN powder mixed in equal 
weight fractions using pristine powder and the cold worked powders 
milled at 100, 150, and 200 for 3 h (hereafter 100-RPM-TZN, 150-RPM- 
TZN, 200-RPM-TZN). The alloy mixed with the powder milled at 300 
rpm was excluded from the study owing to its accumulated porosity 
during milling. The grain size of 100-RPM-TZN is considerably refined 
when compared to the pristine TZN sample, from ~297 μ m to 122 μ m; 
however, the average grain size is not much changed with further 
milling as the average grain size was 111 μ m for 150-RPM-TZN and 96 
μ m for 200-RPM-TZN samples. On the other hand, the size of pre -
cipitates in the 100-RPM-TZN sample is big (~5 μ m) compared to other 
milled samples (~300 nm) as the deformed sites in the 100-RPM-TZN 
sample are less in comparison to the samples incorporating more 
severely milled powder. 
The grain size distribution as a function of the milling speed is pre -
sented in Fig. 1 (c). The proportion of the finer grain size introduced by 
milled powder slightly increases as the milling energy increases. A 
marginal bimodal distribution can be noted in the figure. This bimodal 
distribution promotes the fine grain size as a function of the milling 
energy even with slight changes in the average grain size. The volume 
fraction of α -phase precipitates, as shown by the SEM and EBSD images, 
increases as a function of the milling conditions. In particular, higher 
milling energy generates more defects and dislocations, increasing the 
number of nucleation sites for the α -phase precipitation.

The deformation mechanisms of the pristine TZN and 100-RPM-TZN 
samples were compared using EBSD images; the results are shown in 
Fig. 3 (d). The TRIP phenomenon is observed in both cases where 
martensitic transformation is induced with applied stress, and the level 
of the TRIP region is growing at higher strain levels. For the 100-RPM- 
TZN sample, TRIP is mostly associated with the finer grains that have 
higher stress concentration due to the presence of precipitates, and 
hence are responding to the applied stress, thereby depicting strain 
localization. This localization is causing dislocation pile-up in the 
smaller grains hence triggering TRIP within these grains. As stress is 
concentrated on the finer grains, this affirms the role of the heteroge -
neous distribution of the refined grains inside the TZN sample. The TRIP 
phenomenon is confirmed by TEM for the 100-RPM-TZN sample 
strained at 10%, as shown in Fig. 3 (e) where a TRIP region sandwiched 
between the β matrix is presented. The selected area diffraction pattern 
of both the matrix and martensitic phases is presented above the high- 
resolution TEM image of the β /martensite interface. This explains that 
the deformation in the system occurs by TRIP, similar to previous studies 
[ 20 ]. 
In a typical bimodal heterogeneous microstructure, finer grains 
impart higher strength to the system and deformation occurs by stabi -
lizing the plasticity in finer and harder grains through their strain 
gradient [ 30 ]. In this work, slight differences in the grain size, except for 
the pristine sample, are noted with increasing milling energy. However, 
a larger difference in the grain strength is expected owing to the fine α 
precipitation inside these grains. The nanoindentation hardness maps 
( Fig. 3 (b)) support the case of aforementioned spatial variations of the 
hardness in the matrix, affiliated with a distribution of α -precipitation. 
The grains are categorized into soft and hard regions based on the dif -
ference in the precipitation fraction. The increase in strength observed in 
the 100-RPM-TZN sample, as compared to the pristine sample, can 
primarily be attributed to changes in grain size. In contrast, the 
strengthening observed in the 150-RPM-TZN and 200-RPM-TZN sam -
ples results from a higher fraction of fine precipitates. In addition to the 
precipitation, oxygen inclusion also plays an important role in 
strengthening. As it can be observed that the volume fraction of the 
precipitates in the 150-RPM-TZN sample does not significantly differ 
from that of the 100-RPM-TZN sample, henceforth, the presence of a 
greater amount of oxygen inclusions can explain the increase in 
strength.

4. Conclusions 
In conclusion, this study has successfully introduced a novel 
approach for enhancing the strength of the system while maintaining a 
high level of plasticity and minimal impact on the elastic modulus. The 
following key findings have been derived from this work: 
i. Microstructural studies have revealed a unique morphology 
achieved through milling the half quantity of the total powder at 
different RPMs before mixing with the other half of the pristine 
powder and subsequent sintering. 
ii. The yield strength increased from 590 MPa (in the case of TZN) 
up to 1000 MPa through precipitation strengthening in the 200- 
RPM-TZN sample. 
iii. The plasticity of the system under compression remains consis -
tently above 50% for all sintered samples examined. 
iv. Deformation predominantly occurred by the TRIP mechanism in 
the pristine and 100-RPM-TZN samples. However, in the other 
two samples, this effect was absent, resulting in a reduction in 
elongation.
