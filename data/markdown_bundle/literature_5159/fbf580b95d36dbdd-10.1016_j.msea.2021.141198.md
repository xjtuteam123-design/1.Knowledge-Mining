ABSTRACT 
W – 3Re alloys reinforced with various HfC particles contents (0 wt%, 0.5 wt%, 1 wt%, 5 wt%, 10 wt%) were 
fabricated using spark plasma sintering (SPS) at 2050 
◦
C for 10 min. Microstructure, Vickers hardness and high 
temperature compression properties of the sintered W-3Re- x HfC alloys were investigated. Spherical HfC nano -
particles and micron scale clusters are distributed at the grain boundaries of the W – Re matrix. These nanoscale 
HfC particles pin dislocations and grains boundary as well as refine grain, thus enhancing the strength of 
composites. Furthermore, the HfC-W interfaces are well bonded semi-coherently without apparent interfacial 
gaps. The high temperature strength and micro-hardness of sintered composites are significantly increased with 
an increase of HfC contents. The micro-hardness of W – 3Re alloy was 659.4 HV when 10 wt % HfC was added, 
which is enhanced up to 92.5 % compared with matrix. Its compressive strength is 850 MPa, increased by ~286 
% compared with that of W – Re matrix. Quantitative analysis indicates that the main strength mechanisms are 
grain refinement, Orowan strengthening and interfacial thermal mismatch strengthening, which are influenced 
significantly by the HfC contents. This study provides new insights into composites performance optimization, 
reinforcement designs and strengthening mechanisms of particles reinforced W matrix alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Tungsten (W) and W-base alloys have the advantages of high melting 
point, excellent high temperature strength, good thermal conductivity 
and corrosion resistance, and are widely used in nozzles, turbines and 
combustors of rocket engines, nuclear reactor, kinetic energy penetrator 
[ 1 – 4 ] etc. However, their low temperature ductility and high tempera -
ture strength are the most obstacles for their successful applications. To 
solve these issues, solid solution strengthening and second phase parti -
cles have been extensively explored. Alloying with rhenium (Re) can 
refine the grain size of W and improve the room mechanical properties 
(ductility and strength) and high temperature stability of tungsten ma -
terials, i.e. “ "rhenium effect ” [ 5 ]. L. Romaner et al. reported that there 
were two reasons for this phenomenon, one is an improved mobility of 
1/2 < 111 > screw dislocations, and the other is change in slip plane from 
{110} to {112}. The former reduces the peierls force of plastic defor -
mation, while the latter increases the number of available slip planes 
from 6 to 12, resulting in “ solution softening ” effect [ 6 ]. For example, 
Kang reported that the arc-melting W-based composite shows a high 
strain capacity of 37.1 % at room temperature, excellent hardness (5.04 
GPa), yield strength (0.92 GPa) and fracture strength (1.78 GPa) when 
10 wt % Re and 1 wt % SiC were added [ 7 ]. 
However, Re alloying could only increase the high-temperature 
performance of W – Re alloy when the Re concentrations is increased to 
nearly the limit of solubility [ 8 , 9 ], thus leading to the cost of W – Re 
materials. The main reason is that the rarity of Re renders it a very 
expensive metal. 
Severe high temperature working conditions require the W – Re alloys 
with superior high temperature strength. In order to solve this problem, 
various strategies have been applied to improve the properties of W – Re 
alloys [ 12 – 14 ]. As we all known, the use of composite systems gives us 
the additional advantage of tailoring the various properties of the 
composites by controlling the compositions or the fabrication processes 
of the system. Hence, refractory carbides/nitrides (TiC, HfC, ZrC, TiN

higher than that of W – 25Re alloy (2 – 3 %). However, in the mentioned 
studies, the high amount of Re was employed, leading to the high cost of 
W – Re alloys. 
Owing to the higher sintering densification temperature of W matrix 
composites, spark plasma sintering (SPS) technology are developed to 
fabricate refractory metal matrix composites. The main reason is the SPS 
has many advantages that distinguish it from the conventional sintering 
methods [ 19 ]. Hence, W – 3Re alloys reinforced with various HfC con -
tents (0 wt%, 0.5 wt%, 1 wt%, 5 wt%, 10 wt%) were fabricated using 
SPS at 2050 
◦
C for 10 min. HfC concentrations effect on the micro -
structure and elevated temperature mechanical properties of W – 3Re 
composites was studied. Furthermore, the interfacial characteristics of 
HfC and W – 3Re matrix will be thoroughly studied, and the strength -
ening mechanisms to increase strength of W-3Re- x HfC composites are 
discussed. We show that the W – 3Re composites exhibit a compressive 
strength of 850 MPa, increased by ~286 % compared with W – 3Re 
matrix when the 10 wt% HfC was added. The strength mechanisms are a 
combination of grain refinement, Orowan strengthening and interfacial 
thermal mismatch strengthening. 
2. Experimental procedures 
2.1. Raw materials 
Commercial W, Re and HfC powders were used as starting materials 
to fabricate the W-3Re- x HfC composites. The W powders had 99.90 % 
purity and an average particle size of 5 μ m ( Fig. 1 a). The Re powder 
(99.95 %) with a size of 2 μ m was used. A scanning electron microscope 
(SEM, Zeiss Gemini500, Germany) image in Fig. 1 b shows the Re powder 
has a dendritic shape. HfC powders (99.90 wt% purity) with a mean size 
of 1 μ m was utilized ( Fig. 1 c). The detailed characteristics of raw pow -
ders used in this work are listed in Table 1 . 
2.2. Fabrication of W-3Re-xHfC composites 
Herein, W-3Re- x HfC (x = 0, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0 and 10 wt%) composites 
was fabricated using a powder metallurgy method ( Fig. 2 a). Firstly, the 
designed powders were weighed and ball milled at 350 rpm for 6 h in an 
Ar atmosphere with a ball-to-powder weight ratio of 3:1, using a plan -
etary ball milling apparatus (QM-3SP2) ( Fig. 2 a). After the ball milling 
process, the Re and HfC powders were uniformly dispersed into the W 
powder shown in Fig. 1 d. 
Finally, the obtained W-3Re- x HfC mixtures were densified using a 
spark plasma sintering (HPD250, FCT, Germany) in a graphite mold at a 
temperature of 2050 
◦
C for 10 min under a pressure of 40 MPa in an Ar 
protective atmosphere ( Fig. 2 a). Before the sintering, the mixed powders 
were loaded into a graphite grinder with an internal diameter of 30 mm 
and an external diameter of 40 mm. The graphite paper with a thickness 
of 1 – 2 mm were placed surrounding the powders to prevent the reaction 
and bonding of W – Re powders with the graphite mold. The size of the 
SPS processed cylinder composites billets was ϕ 30 mm × 40 mm. For 
simplicity, the sintered composites were named as WR0H – S, WR0.5H – S, 
WR1H – S, WR5H – S, WR10H – S, respectively.

3.2. Mechanical properties of the composites 
The relative density and Vickers micro-hardness of the fabricated W- 
3Re- x HfC composites are plotted in Fig. 6 a. The detailed mechanical 
properties values are listed in Table 2 . It can be seen from Fig. 6 a and 
Table 2 that the presence of HfC particles slightly increases the relative 
density, and the effect is more pronounced with increasing HfC particles 
content from 0 to 5 wt%. However, the relative density of W-3Re- x HfC 
composites decreases a lot with the further increase of HfC contents. For 
examples, the relative density of W – 3Re alloy is 96.2% when the HfC is 
10 wt%. In fact, it is difficult to obtain a fully dense W matrix composites 
using a conventional powder metallurgy method without any sintering 
aids or sintering activators such as Pt, Ni, Pd, etc. [ 33 ]. However, as the 
HfC content is increased to ~10 wt%, and the relative density is 
decreased slightly. As the HfC content is increased, uniform dispersion of 
HfC particles in the W matrix becomes difficult, and aggregation of HfC 
particles at W grain boundaries occurs. Therefore some of HfC particles 
can not contact closely with the W particles. Additionally, the sintering 
of HfC aggregation is not easy at 2050 
◦
C due to its chemical bond 
characteristics. Hence the densification rate of W-3Re- x HfC composites 
with a high HfC contents is decreased which results in a lower relative 
density. The relative density of the obtained composites increases first 
and then decreases with the increase of HfC content from 0 wt% to 10 wt 
%. The main reasons are (1) a small number of HfC particles can activate 
the GBs and promote the GBs migration; (2) the agglomeration of HfC 
particles is increased with increasing HfC volume fraction ( Fig. 3 e). 
The micro-hardness of the as-received pure W – 3Re alloy is ~342.6 
HV. As shown in Fig. 6 a, micro-hardness values of composites are 
significantly increased with an increase of HfC contents, which are 
attributed to second-phase dispersion strengthening. The low error bar 
in the micro-hardness value in Fig. 6 a reveals that the microstructural 
morphology of the obtained composites is relatively homogeneous. The 
micro-hardness of W – 3Re alloy is about 659.4 HV when the HfC content 
is 10 wt%, which is corresponding to an enhancement of 92.5% 
compared with matrix.

3.4. Strengthening mechanism 
For the strengthen mechanisms of the metal matrix composites, 
microstructure and properties are greatly dependent on the shape, size 
and distributional characteristic of reinforcement in matrix [ 19 , 34 ]. For 
the W-3Re- x HfC composites in this study, there are three main mecha -
nisms which can be used to explain the strength improvement. (1) Grain 
refinement ( Fig. 3 and Tables 2) and (2 ) Orowan strengthening, and (3) 
Prismatic punching of dislocation at the HfC/W interface which lead to 
work hardening of the W matrix owing to thermal mismatch between 
HfC particles and W-3Re alloys matrix. These factors should be simul -
taneously taken into consideration because of their effects on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties.
