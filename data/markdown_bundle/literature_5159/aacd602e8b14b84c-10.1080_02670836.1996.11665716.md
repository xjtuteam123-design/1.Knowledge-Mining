High temperature tensile tests were carried out on L12 type C03 Ti alloys, both undoped and doped with 1-4 at.-%Fe. There
were anomalous increases of the 0·2% yield stress (yield strength) with increasing test temperature from 473 to 1073 K (or
1173 K, depending on the composition). The elongation and ultimate tensile stress (UTS) monotonically decreased with
increasing temperature. The fracture surfaces of specimens showed a variety offracture modes which were dependent on the test
temperature and composition. There was a correlation between the ductility and the fracture mode: the more transgranular the
fracture mode, the higher the ductility. It wasfound that C03 Ti with 2 at.-%Fe exhibited improved ductility and it exhibited the
highest peak value of yield strength and peak temperature. The alloys were also hydrogen charged to investigate their hydrogen
embrittlement behaviour. Room temperature tests indicated that the addition of2 at.-%Fe decreased the hydrogen related
embrittlement. MST/3479

Introduction
There is currently considerable interest in the L1z type
intermetallic compound C03 Ti owing to its attractive
properties for high temperature applications and its good
fabricability.1 Compared with other intermetallic com-
pounds such as Ni3Al and Ni3 Ge, etc., its peak value of
yield strength is lower and it also exhibits obvious
embrittlement at higher temperatures.1,z Meanwhile, its
mechanical properties are very sensitive to environmental
factors and strain rate. The C03 Ti alloy shows a severe loss
of ductility in an atmosphere containing a trace amount of
hydrogen.3,4 Takasugi et al.s have investigated the mechan-
ical properties of C03 Ti alloys but, so far, there has been
less systematic work on the effect of alloying elements on
the mechanical properties of these alloys. Iron is a typical
element which can substitute for both cobalt and titanium
and Takasugi et al. have found that iron increases the
ductility of Ni3Al (Refs. 5-7). The present paper investigates
the tensile properties of C03 Ti alloy doped with different
amounts of iron over a range of temperatures and hydrogen
embrittlement at ambient temperature.

above phenomena suggest that the ductility of C03 Ti alloy
can be improved by adding a small amount of iron.

HYDROGEN EMBRITTLEMENT BEHAVIOUR
The present result that the hydrogen charged specimens
showed more brittle intergranular fracture and cracks along
the perimeter of specimens suggests that hydrogen pen-
etrates into the specimen through grain boundaries and
microcracks in the surface of the specimen and then creates
'hydrogen damage'. Planar slip during deformation is
characteristic of intermetallic compounds where the super-
lattice dislocation can be visualised as two unit dislocations
that move together, separated by strip of antiphase
boundary.12 This unique dislocation structure implies that
cross slip is restricted and therefore a high stress concen-
tration is created near the grain boundary region. Hydrogen,
which exists at the surface, diffuses and segregates to this
region of stress concentration reducing the cohesive strength
of the grain boundary. Alternatively, a microcrack can act
as a 'transport path'. Hydrogen atoms are postulated to be

Conclusions
Polycrystalline C03 Ti and C03 Ti alloys doped with 1, 2,
and 4 at.-%Fe were tensile tested at temperatures from 473
to 1323 K. The mechanical properties were evaluated by
the yield strength, UTS, and elongation. It was found that
the yield strength showed a positive temperature depend-
ence regardless of composition. The UTS and elongation
decreased monotonically with increasing temperature. A
variety of fracture modes were observed, depending on the
testing temperature and composition of the alloy. There
was a correlation between the ductility and fracture mode
of the intermetallics. The occurrence of a trans granular
fracture mode resulted in higher ductility. With increasing
temperature, the area of intergranular fracture increased.
The addition of 2 at.-%Fe enhanced the grain boundary
strength, improving the ductility of C03 Ti alloy significantly.
The elongation and UTS of the C03 Ti-2 at.-%Fe were
greater than for the other C03 Ti alloys investigated in the
range of testing temperature.
