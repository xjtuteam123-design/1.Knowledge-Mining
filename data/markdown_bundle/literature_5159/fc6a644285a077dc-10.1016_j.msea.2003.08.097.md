Abstract
The damping property of Fe–12 wt.% Cr–(6–30) wt.% Mn alloys has been investigated intending to develop high damping and high strength
alloys. Particularly, the effects of the phases of alpha, gamma and epsilon martensite, which constitute the structure of the alloys Fe–12 wt.%
Cr–(6–30) wt.% Mn alloys, on the damping capacity at room temperature has been investigated. Various fraction of these phases were formed
depending on the Mn content and cold work degree. The combination of 22 wt.% Mn and 50% cold rolling formed the largest volume fraction
of epsilon martensite phase and showed the highest damping capacity. The other phases of alpha and gamma had actually no effect on the
damping capacity. Fifty percent cold worked Fe–12 wt.% Cr–22 wt.% Mn alloy indicated the largest tensile strength of more than 1000 MPa.

develop structural materials with high strength and high

4. Conclusion
The effects of the /H9251(/H9251
′), /H9253, and ε phases on the damp-
ing property of cold rolled Fe–12 wt.% Cr–(6–30) wt.% Mn
alloys have been clariﬁed. The contribution of ε phase is
very large, while that of /H9251(/H9251′), /H9253phases is very small: Q−1
value at room temperature increases monotonously with the
increasing of the volume fraction of ε phase. The addition
of nitrogen decreases the Q−1 value and the volume frac-
tion of ε martensite phase in Fe–12 wt.% Cr–22 wt.% Mn
alloys. The largest damping ( Q−1 = 9 × 10−3 at a strain
amplitude of 5 × 10−6) was obtained at the smallest content
(0.009%) of nitrogen, that is, at the largest volume fraction
of ε phase. The Fe–12 wt.% Cr–22 wt.% Mn alloys have also
high strength properties and particularly the ultimate tensile
strength at room temperature is higher than 1.2 GPa.
