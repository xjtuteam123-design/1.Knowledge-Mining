ABSTRACT 
Rapid degradation of the mechanical properties of conventional Mg alloys with temperature precludes their wide 
application in industry. The LPSO (long period stacking ordered) Mg alloys, on the other hand, show consid -
erable potential to achieve excellent high-temperature mechanical performance. In this study, a Mg-5.1Y-2.4Zn 
(wt.%) extrusion alloy with the microstructure comprising of α -Mg matrix and LPSO phase was prepared. It is 
shown that the yield strength of the alloy at 200 
◦
C retained ~83% of the yield strength of 198 MPa at room 
temperature (RT). Importantly, the ductility also improved, owing to the increased non-basal slip activities at 
elevated temperatures. In contrast to RT and 200 
◦
C, the alloy exhibited softening tensile behavior with an 
apparent loss of yield strength at 300 
◦
C. In-situ lattice strain analysis revealed that load transfer from α -Mg to 
LPSO remains effective at temperatures up to 200 
◦
C, governing the high yield strength of the alloy. The post- 
mortem analysis by means of electron microscopy confirmed that the LPSO phase is structurally stable at 
200 
◦
C. In particular, the high strain hardenability is rationalized by the sandwiched LPSO structures together 
with stacking faults serving as effective obstacles to the motion of non-basal dislocations. The load transfer effect 
disappears at the temperature of 300 
◦
C, resulting in the fast deterioration of strength of the alloy. The cracking 
of the LPSO phase along with the recrystallizing of α -Mg grains were found to be the main causes for the soft -
ening behavior of the alloy at 300 
◦
C. 
1. Introduction 
With low density and high specific strength, Mg alloys offer advan -
tages over other metallic materials and have been increasingly evaluated 
for applications in defense, automobile, railway, aerospace and other 
industries, where weight reduction is a matter of particular concern 
[ 1 – 4 ]. However, the relatively low strength and ductility of Mg alloys 
remain major bottlenecks against their practical application. Improving 
the mechanical properties has since been the subject of intense research 
to expand the usage of Mg alloys [ 5 , 6 ]. Recently developed Mg-RE-TM 
alloys (where RE refers to as rare earth elements, such as Y, Gd, and 
Dy while TM represents transitional metals, such as Zn, Ni, and Cu) 
containing long-period stacking order (LPSO) structures show 
considerable potential to achieve excellent mechanical performance at 
both, ambient and elevated temperatures [ 7 – 11 ]. For instance, an 
extruded Mg-Y-Ni alloy with 74.8 vol% of LPSO phase exhibited a tensile 
yield strength of above 500 MPa and an elongation of ~5%, which is 
attractive as structural alloys for a wide range of potential applications 
[ 12 ]. 
The LPSO structures are periodically stacked along the c-axis of the 
hexagonal crystal structure where RE/TM atoms occupy the Mg posi -
tions in the neighboring {0001} planes [ 13 ]. Due to the unique ordering 
structure along the crystallographic c -axis, the deformation of LPSO 
structures is strongly anisotropic, depends on the crystallographic 
texture and the loading direction [ 14 – 16 ]. Similar to Mg, basal slip can 
be operative in the LPSO structures [ 17 ]. In the case where stress is

because of its complicated stacking structures [ 16 ]. Instead, unique kink

along basal plane [ 15 , 18 , 19 ]. The underlying tension-compression 
asymmetry of kink band formation has been the subject of a number 
of studies [ 15 , 20 ]. It is shown that the formed kink bands act as effective 
obstacles to the motion of basal dislocations, contributing to the 
strengthening of the LPSO phase [ 8 , 21 , 22 ]. In tension along the basal 
plane, where both basal slip and kinking formation are geometrically 
suppressed, non-basal slip was observed to operate in LPSO structures 
[ 14 ]. The corresponding deformation modes in LPSO phase are more 
difficult to be activated than those of in counterpart Mg [ 23 ]. This could 
give rise to a load transfer/partitioning effect from α -Mg to LPSO during 
plastic deformation, strengthening the alloy as a result. [ 21 – 23 ]. 
The technological developments since the early 20th century have 
required materials that resist higher and higher temperatures [ 27 ], but 
improving the mechanical strengths of Mg alloys at elevated tempera -

temperatures, leading to a loss of strengthening [ 33 ]. The LPSO- 
containing Mg alloys, on the contrary, have been reported as excellent 
to withstand the strength-loss at elevated temperatures, in both tension

3. Results 
3.1. Initial Microstructure 
Fig. 2 shows the initial microstructure of the investigated Mg-Y-Zn 
extrusion alloy. The 3D reconstructed X-ray computed tomography in 
Fig. 2 (a-c) shows the alloy with a duplex microstructure comprising of 
α -Mg phase and LPSO fibers. The volume fraction of LPSO phase in this 
extrusion alloy is ~18.5 vol.%, measured with the Micro X-ray CT sys -
tem. The LPSO phase displays a morphology of fiber-shape stretched 
along the ED with a dimension of dozens to hundreds of micrometers. 
The LPSO phases appeared to be irregular in the transverse direction 
with a dimension of roughly a few to dozens of micrometers. Fig. 2 (d) 
shows the EBSD-based image quality map of the alloy, where the α -Mg 
phase exhibits a fully dynamic recrystallized (DRXed) grain structure. 
The grain size spans a range with a mean value of about 8.9 μ m, as 
shown by the histogram in Fig. 2 (e). 
TEM was used to further reveal the microstructure of the alloy. The 
bright field (BF) images of LPSO shown in Fig. 3 (a) and (b) demonstrate 
the sandwiched structure of LPSO that was sliced by multiple layers of

5. Conclusions 
In this work, a Mg-Y-Zn alloy containing α -Mg and LPSO phases was 
processed by casting, homogenization, and extrusion. The mechanical 
behavior in tension at elevated temperatures and the accompanied 
microstructure evolution were studied by means of in-situ high-energy 
X-ray diffraction and electron microscopy. The main conclusions drawn 
from the study are as follows: 
(1) The dual-phase Mg-5.1Y-2.4Zn extrusion alloy with ~18 vol.% 
LPSO phase exhibited superior high-temperature mechanical 
properties of yield strength and strain hardening over most other 
Mg alloys at elevated temperatures up to around 200 
◦
C. The 
yield strength of the alloy falls dramatically at 300 
◦
C concurrent 
with a prolonged softening behavior. 
(2) Measurements of lattice strain evolution with plastic strain 
revealed that the load transfer from Mg to LPSO remains effective 
up to 200 
◦
C, giving rise to the high yield strength. In addition to 
high strength, the alloy exhibited a great deal of strain hardening. 
The high strain hardenability of the alloy at 200 
◦
C originated 
from the α -Mg/LPSO phase interfaces, the thin LPSO lamellae, 
and the stacking faults, which act as obstacles to the gliding of 
non-basal dislocations. 
(2) (3) Load partitioning between the phases of Mg and LPSO dis -
appeared at 300 
◦
C, as the LPSO phase developed a comparable 
lattice strain with the α -Mg phase, which is in contrast to the 
behavior at RT and 200 
◦
C. The loss of strengthening was pri -
marily due to the damage accumulation caused by cracking of 
LPSO fibers into particles at high temperatures. The cracking of 
LPSO phase along with the increased dynamic recovery and 
recrystallization of α -Mg phase were responsible for the softening 
behavior of the alloy at 300 
◦
C.
