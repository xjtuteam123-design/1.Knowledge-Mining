ABSTRACT 
This study is dedicated to the development of Co-free eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA) with potential appli -
cations in nuclear power. An Al
0 ⋅ 9
Cr
0 ⋅ 8
FeMn
0 ⋅ 8

2.0

varying temperatures to investigate microstructural transformations. In its as-cast state, the alloy displayed 
dendritic (FCC), sunflower petal-like eutectic (FCC + BCC), and sunflower disc-like matrix (BCC) structures. 
Following annealing at 600 
◦

dered B2 phase (rich in Al and Ni) precipitates and diminished mechanical properties. The temperature- 
microstructure-mechanical linkages identified in this investigation provide valuable insights for future 
research in EHEAs tailored for nuclear applications. 
1. Introduction 
Eutectic alloys possess the advantages of controllable phase 
composition, stable defect structure, high fracture strength, and low- 
energy phase boundaries, making them an area of great interest in 
materials science. Building upon the concepts of eutectic alloys and 
high-entropy alloys (HEAs), the concept of eutectic high-entropy alloys 
(EHEAs) was first proposed by Lu et al. [ 1 ]. The AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
alloy was 
the first reported EHEA, exhibiting excellent mechanical properties and 
attracting significant research attention [ 2 , 3 ]. Subsequently, several 
EHEAs with exceptional mechanical or other unique properties have 
also been reported [ 4 – 7 ]. In the field of nuclear power, the long-term 
stable operation of nuclear power plants requires effective control of 
high-radioactive elements in structural materials, and Co is not feasible 
element for the long-term operation in nuclear reactor due to its high 
radioactivity, thus Co should be replaced to lower activation elements in 
terms of the material cost [ 8 , 9 ]. Therefore, the development of reduced 
activation EHEAs, particularly those without cobalt, is of great 
importance. 
The annealing process can induce changes in the sunflower-like 
morphology, grain size, and phase structure of EHEAs, which can 
significantly affect their mechanical properties [ 10 ]. For example, the 
sunflower-like morphology in EHEAs, resembling the petals of a sun -
flower, is a result of component segregation during solidification. This 
unique morphology enhances the mechanical properties, hardness, wear 
resistance, and corrosion resistance of the alloys [ 11 ]. Hence, the 
objective of this study is to develop novel Co-free EHEAs and investigate 
the influence of different annealing temperatures on the sunflower-like 
morphology and grain refinement in EHEAs. By gaining a deep under -
standing of the relationship between these microstructural evolution 
and mechanical properties, this research could promote the application 
and development of EHEAs in the field of nuclear power.

In the as-cast state, microstructural morphologies with significant dif -

structure reorganization and phase transformation within this specific 
temperature range, with the phenomenon being particularly pro -

◦
C. Notably, after annealing at 600 
◦
C, the alloy exhibits 
the microstructural characteristics of dual phases similar to the as-cast 
state. Specifically, the eutectic structure in the petal region is retained, 
but both the eutectic structure in the petal region and the matrix 
structure in the disc region exhibit significant growth ( Fig. 1 e). With a 
continued rise in annealing temperature, the dendritic and eutectic 
structures undergo a gradual dissolution, resulting in a notable 
compromise of their structural integrity. At annealing temperatures of 
700 
◦

transforming into the matrix structure. Simultaneously, needle-like 
precipitates become apparent within the dendrites, as illustrated in 
Fig. 1 f and g. 
In order to further analyze the crystal structure of the precipitates 
and matrix phase, TEM bright-field images are obtained in Fig. 1 h. 
Notably, the eutectic structure in the petal region manifests as an 
alternating arrangement of rod-like precipitates extending outwardly 
from both the matrix of the disc region and the dendritic structure. 
Analysis of the Selected Area Electron Diffraction (SAED) patterns re -
veals distinct structural identities. The matrix in the disc region is 
identified as the Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) phase (P1), while the 
dendritic structure is recognized as the Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) phase 
(P2). Within the FCC structure, the presence of a spherical disordered 
BCC structured phase is evident (P3), and concomitantly, a needle-like 
B2 phase with an ordered BCC structure precipitate (P4). 
The EPMA observations were performed to reveal the elemental 
distribution of the alloy in the as-cast state and annealed at different 
temperatures ( Fig. 2 ). In the as-cast alloy ( Fig. 2 a), the dendritic struc -
ture exhibits an enrichment of Fe and Cr in the FCC phase, whereas the 
matrix in the disc region shows an enrichment of Ni and Al in the BCC 
phase. The eutectic microstructure in the petal region displays alter -
nating enrichments of Ni – Al and Fe – Cr, further providing evidence that 
this eutectic structure consists of alternating arrangements of BCC and 
FCC phases. Indeed, the distribution patterns of the mentioned elements 
remain consistent in the samples annealed at different temperatures, 
even if the microstructure evolves differently.

The stability of the dendritic FCC phase further diminishes at higher 
temperatures, with part of the FCC phase transforming into the BCC 
phase. Simultaneously, a large amount of needle-like B2 phase pre -
cipitates from the residual FCC phase. Upon annealing at the high 
temperature, the atoms possessed sufficient thermal energy to migrate 
and rearrange themselves, leading to segregation of different atoms 
during the annealing process. This resulted in the appearance of phases 
with different chemical compositions and crystal structures in eutectic 
alloys. During annealing, Al and Ni atoms exhibited higher miscibility 
and affinity, favoring the formation of the BCC structure. Such a struc -
ture exhibited high strength and hardness, contributing to improved 
mechanical properties [ 18 ]. Conversely, Fe and Cr atoms tended to form 
the FCC structure, which offered good ductility and toughness. This 
characteristic is advantageous for enhancing corrosion resistance and 
deformability in the alloy [ 19 ]. 
The combined effect of the evolving microstructure and the influence 
on recrystallization highlights the complex interplay between temper -
ature, phase transition, and recrystallization behavior in the alloy during 
annealing. These evolutions produce significant effects on the mechan -
ical properties of the alloys. Tensile properties of the as-cast EHEAs as a 
function of the annealing temperature were obtained using uniaxial 
tensile tests. The small size of our tensile samples posed challenges, 
making it difficult to employ a traditional extensometer in our experi -
mental setup. Given these constraints, our study focused on a lateral 
comparison of the tensile properties of EHEAs under different heat 
treatment temperatures. Fig. 5 a shows the engineering stress-strain 
curves of the as-cast and annealed EHEAs. The variation curves of ten -
sile strength and elongation with annealing temperature are shown in 
Fig. 5 b.

with a high tensile strength over 1000 MPa and a good elongation of

was improved through grain refinement caused by recrystallization. 
Even more, the increase in the number of grain boundaries has a hin -
dering effect on dislocation and crack movement, which limits crack 
propagation and further improves the ductility of the material. This 
study preliminarily shows that the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of this EHEA make it promising for use in nuclear power. 
However, the high-temperature stability and irradiation resistance of 
alloy is important in nuclear materials. Hence, in future work, we plan to 
conduct long-term aging studies and ion irradiation and neutron irra -
diation experiments at different temperatures, and systematically study 
the high temperature stability and irradiation resistance of this EHEA. 
4. Conclusion 
This study explored the microstructure evolution and mechanical 
properties of novel Al
0 ⋅ 9
Cr
0 ⋅ 8
FeMn
0 ⋅ 8
Ni
2.0 
high-entropy alloy, which 
meets the design concept of EHEAs [ 12 ], during annealing at different 
temperatures. The findings in this study enhanced understanding of 
EHEAs and had implications for advanced materials in demanding ap -
plications like nuclear power. The results were summarized as follows. 
(1) In the as-cast state, the alloy exhibited dendritic (FCC), sunflower 
petal-like eutectic (FCC + BCC), and sunflower disc-like matrix 
(BCC) structures. Annealing at 300 – 500 
◦
C led to the dissolution 
of the eutectic structure and gradual coarsening of FCC dendrites. 
(2) At 600 
◦
C, the BCC phase became dominant, initiating static 
recrystallization and refining the grain size. At higher annealing 
temperatures (700 – 800 
◦
C), the FCC phase continued to dissolve, 
leading to the formation of needle-like B2 phase precipitates. 
(3) The annealed samples exhibited varying mechanical properties, 
with the alloy annealed at 600 
◦
C showing a good balance be -
tween strength and ductility, offering a high tensile strength of 
over 1000 MPa and an elongation of 7.3%.
