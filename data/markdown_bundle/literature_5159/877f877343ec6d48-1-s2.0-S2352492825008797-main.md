ABSTRACT
Refractory High-Entropy Alloys (RHEAs) exhibit exceptional mechanical properties, making them promising 
candidates for aerospace and biomedical applications. This study investigates the elastic and mechanical prop -
erties of TiZrNbTa-based RHEAs with alloying elements (X = Mo, Hf, V, W, Re) using a hybrid machine learning 
(ML) and density functional theory (DFT) approach. A dataset of 370 RHEAs was used to train ten ML models, 
with CatBoost Regressor demonstrating the highest accuracy (R² > 0.9) and lowest error metrics, making it the 
optimal model for predicting elastic properties. The CatBoost model accurately predicted elastic constants (C11, 
C12, and C44), validated against DFT calculations with deviations < 5 %. Key mechanical properties, including 
bulk modulus (B), shear modulus (G), Young ’ s modulus (E), and hardness (Hv), were systematically analyzed. 
TiZrNbTaRe exhibited the highest strength, achieving a yield strength exceeding 2 GPa, making it ideal for ultra- 
high-strength applications. In contrast, TiZrNbTaHf demonstrated the greatest ductility, with the lowest yield 
strength of 1.18 GPa (DFT) and 1.10 GPa (ML), attributed to easier nucleation of spiral dislocations, enhancing 
plastic deformation. SHAP analysis identified valence electron concentration (VEC) and melting temperature 
(Tm) as the most influential descriptors governing elastic behavior. This study demonstrates that integrating ML 
with DFT offers a computationally efficient strategy for accelerating alloy design while maintaining high pre -
dictive accuracy. The results provide a framework for tailoring RHEAs with optimized strength and ductility, 
paving the way for next-generation high-performance materials for demanding structural applications.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) represent a unique class of materials 
whose compositional versatility can allow for the development of new 
alloy design paradigms for advanced applications [1] . HEAs enable the 
unique combination of high strength with ductility and even corrosion 
resistance for various applications [2] . One of these is Refractory 
High-entropy Alloys (RHEAs) that retain superior strength-and-stiffness 
characteristics at high temperatures, and are of interest to aerospace and 
medical industries including orthopedic implants [3] . WNbMoTaV, 
developed in 2010, displays much better high temperature stability than 
the conventional nickel-based superalloys of Inconel 718 type [4] . Re -
fractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) represent a novel class of materials 
that are known to possess remarkable mechanical properties such as 
high strength, ductility and thermal stability at high temperatures. 
These characteristics make RHEAs suitable for use in aerospace and even 
biomedical engineering, which requires materials that can withstand 
high mechanical and environmental conditions. Equimolar composi -
tions such as HfNbTaTiZr and HfMoNbTaTiZr have enhanced phase 
stability and chemical segregation resistance owing to their homoge -
nous elemental distribution [5,6] . On the other hand, non-equimolar 
compositions like Zr-Ti-Nb-Ta and Ti-Zr-Hf-Nb provide a platform for 
tailoring some properties like hardness, corrosion resistance and tensile 
strength through changing the concentration of the elements [7 – 9] . 
These RHEAs have performed exceptionally well compared to biomed -
ical alloys such as Ti6Al – 4 V, especially inhibiting corrosion and

However, the development of RHEAs via conventional trial-and-

experimental difficulties. As a result, the computational methods, such 
as first principles calculations and machine learning have become 
pivotal tools to screen and narrow down alloy candidates and accelerate

using advanced computational methods for many years. For example, 
Bhandari et al. [14] performed the structural, mechanical, and ther -
modynamic properties of HfNbTaTiZr and Hf0.5Nb0.5Ta0.5Ti1.5Zr by 
first principles calculations and the special quasi-random structure 
(SQS) method. However, the SQS method assumes a fully randomized 
atomic distribution and does not account for short-range ordering ef -
fects, which could influence mechanical properties in real systems. 
Similarly, Gao et al. [15] utilized the virtual crystal approximation 
(VCA) method to construct a BCC solid solution model, revealing the 
influence of Cr and Ti concentrations on the elastic properties and uni -
formity of RHEAs. Despite its computational efficiency, the VCA method 
oversimplifies atomic interactions by assuming a continuous medium, 
limiting its accuracy in predicting localized structural distortions and 
phase segregation effects [16] . Rare Earth-Based High-Entropy Alloys 
show great potential for optical applications, renewable energy, and 
environmental remediation. Their high strength, thermal stability, and 
corrosion resistance make them ideal for energy storage systems, clean 
energy technologies, and addressing environmental challenges [17 – 23] .
Machine learning has emerged as a transformative approach in ma -
terial design, uncovering complex relationships between input variables 
and material properties without explicitly defining physical equations. 
ML models have successfully predicted mechanical properties in 
medium-entropy alloys, optimized nickel-based superalloys for high 
hardness, and established correlations between valence electron count 
and HEA hardness. Notably, integrating ML with experimental data has 
dramatically improved predictive accuracy, enabling the rapid explo -
ration of novel materials. However, most ML models rely on training 
datasets that are limited in size and diversity, which may restrict their 
generalizability to unexplored alloy compositions. Furthermore, many 
existing ML models function as black boxes, providing predictions 
without physical interpretability, making it difficult to extract funda -
mental insights into alloy behavior. Additionally, while first-principles 
calculations offer an atomistic understanding of material properties, 
they remain computationally expensive, particularly for high- 
dimensional composition spaces.
This study investigates the TiZrNbTa-based refractory high-entropy 
alloy (RHEA) system by introducing an additional element, ‘X, ’ to 
design equimolar compositions. A hybrid framework integrating ma -
chine learning (ML) and density functional theory (DFT) is developed to 
predict the mechanical properties of TiZrNbTaX (X = Mo, Hf, V, W, Re) 
RHEAs. The key innovation of this approach lies in the seamless inte -
gration of ML and DFT, which significantly enhances prediction accu -
racy while reducing computational costs, thereby enabling a more 
efficient and data-driven alloy design process. The results indicate that 
Re plays a critical role in strengthening RHEAs by enhancing yield 
strength and dislocation resistance, making it a promising candidate for 
high-strength applications. In contrast, Hf contributes to improved 
ductility by facilitating enhanced plastic deformation mechanisms. 
These findings provide valuable guidelines for tailoring RHEAs to ach -
ieve an optimal balance of mechanical properties. Furthermore, SHapley 
Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis identifies valence electron con -
centration (VEC) and melting temperature (Tm) as the most influential 
descriptors governing the elastic and mechanical behavior of RHEAs. 
This study not only offers a fundamental understanding of the key 
factors influencing alloy properties but also establishes a scalable and 
systematic methodology for accelerating the discovery of high- 
performance RHEAs. The proposed computational framework has 
broad applicability in designing alloys for demanding environments, 
including aerospace and biomedical applications such as high- 
temperature structural components and next-generation orthopedic 
implants. By addressing these aspects, this work lays the foundation for a 
rational and computationally efficient approach to designing advanced 
RHEAs with tailored mechanical properties for real-world applications.

The database collected from previously reported in the literature 
[24 – 27,28] . The database includes binary, ternary, quaternary, and

step in developing machine learning (ML) models is selecting appro -
priate physical descriptors (variables chosen based on their relevance to 
the target variable), and this is the key contribution of this study. De -
scriptors that could be computed employing density functional theory 
(DFT) calculations were picked to cut computational expenses. Firstly, 
ten numerical descriptors and one categorical feature (alloy ’ s name) 
were chosen for ML models, in which numerical descriptors were 
calculated using Python. The elemental constituents of these alloys were 
assigned attributes using mechanical and thermal attributes. Key de -
scriptors such as the entropy of mixing ( Δ Smix) [29] , enthalpy of mixing 
( Δ Hmix) [30] , melting temperature (Tm) , average atomic radius ( r ),

are presented. The parameter Ω is defined as the ratio of Δ Smix to Δ Hmix 
times the alloy ’ s average melting temperature (Tm) [31] . Due to the 
difficulties in representing material properties accurately by averaging 
(when constituent properties vary), variations among constituent 
properties need to be considered [32] . Additional descriptors, however, 
such as atomic size difference ( δ ) , electronegativity difference ( Δ χ ) , and 
valence electron concentration (VEC) [33] were added. However, pre -
vious studies [34 – 40] have shown that these descriptors predict alloy ’ s 
stability and properties effectively. For example, Islam et al. [35] pre -
dicted phases of multi-principal element alloys using a dataset of 118 
alloys with five descriptors: VEC, Δ Smix, Δ Hmix, δ , and Δ χ . Additionally, 
Khakurel et al. [41] used ML models to estimate Young ’ s modulus of 
RHEAs, showing that VEC, δ , and melting temperature (Tm) are essential 
factors. In addition, we introduce a descriptor, geometrical parameter 
( λ ) , the ratio of Δ Smix to the square of δ . The ten numerical descriptors

2.2. Machine learning model selection
There are 370 data points of RHEA from the DFT calculation were 
collected and a total of 80 % of the data were utilized for training, and 
20 % were used to test and validate the results, which is also used for 
data preparation and model assessment. In this study, ten ML models 
used through the Scikit-learn library [44] were employed to analyze the 
dataset and predict the target variable accurately. The Random Forest 
Regressor (RFR) is an ensemble-based model that aggregates predictions 
from multiple decision trees to improve accuracy and mitigate over -
fitting by averaging tree outputs. It uses bootstrapping, allowing sam -
pling with replacement, making it robust and effective for various data 
distributions. Another ensemble model, similar to Random Forest R, is 
the AdaBoost Regressor (AdaBoost R), which combines weak learners 
(like shallow decision trees) through iterations and successive models to 
fix their previous collective model ’ s error by emphasizing the error 
parts, therefore creating a better robust and accurate model. The 
Gradient Boosting Regressor (GBR) is an ensemble learning method that 
iteratively learns a decision tree to minimize the residual errors of the 
previous model, making it especially suited to rough relationship data. 
To the same extent, the Extreme Gradient Boosting Regressor (XGBR) is 
also an optimized implementation of gradient boosting, focused on 
maximizing speed and efficiency in the traversal of large datasets, with 
regularization techniques built to avoid overfitting and parallel 
computing to improve operational speed. The Light Gradient Boosting 
Machine Regressor (LGBM) is built with speed in mind: it builds tree ’ s 
leaf-wise instead of level-wise, has much better predictive performance 
at deeper nodes, and works faster on large datasets. In this study, The 
CatBoost Regressor (CatBoost R) showed the highest accuracy in the 
training and testing phases.
The CatBoost Regressor a model based on gradient boosting is 
formulated to process the categorical features without complicated 
preprocessing and, hence, is more friendly to users. Its performance is 
greatly enhanced due to its advanced algorithms and regularization 
techniques, and it has the ability to handle overfitting effectively. The 
neural network-based model, Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), preserves 
complex nonlinear data mappings by architectures with multiple layers 
of interconnected neurons. Backpropagation learns patterns iteratively 
using weights, suitable for problems with nonlinear decision bound -
aries. A simple, interpretable model called the Decision Tree Regressor 
(DTR) splits data into subsets by creating feature thresholds to make 
predictions but tends to overfit in its natural state. The Ridge Regressor 
(Ridge R), a linear regression model with L2 regularization, punishes 
significant coefficients and acts to reduce the effects of multicollinearity 
because it improves generalizability. The K nearest Neighbors Regressor 
(KNNR) predicts outcomes by taking the average or weighted average of 
the K nearest data points in the feature space. It is effective for problems 
where local proximity significantly predicts outcomes. The 370 datasets 
of elastic constants were used to train the 10 ML models, which used all 
finalized calculated descriptors. The Grid Search CV function of the 
Sklearn library was used on LOOCV, 3-fold, 5-fold, and 10-fold cross- 
validation (CV) to improve the machine learning model. Hyper -
parameter tuning is a critical step in optimizing the performance of 
machine learning models by facilitating effective cross-validation 
between training and testing datasets. In this study, hyperparameter 
optimization significantly improved the predictive accuracy of the 
models for elastic constants. The performance of these models was 
assessed using standard evaluation metrics, including mean squared 
error (MSE), coefficient of determination (R²), mean absolute error 
(MAE), and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE). The overall 
workflow of this study, as outlined in the preceding sections, is depicted 
in Fig. 1 to provide a clear and comprehensive understanding of the 
methodology.
2.3. Design strategy
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have emerged as a 
powerful machine learning approach for the discovery and optimization 
of novel alloy compositions. Unlike conventional alloy design methods 
that rely on empirical rules and trial-and-error experimentation, GANs 
enable the systematic exploration of vast compositional spaces, accel -
erating the identification of materials with optimized properties. GANs 
operate through an adversarial framework comprising two neural net -
works: a Generator and a Discriminator. The Generator learns under -
lying patterns from existing datasets and synthesizes new alloy 
compositions, while the Discriminator evaluates whether a given 
composition is real (sourced from experimental or computational data) 
or synthetic (generated by the Generator). Through iterative adversarial 
training, the Generator progressively improves its ability to produce 
realistic compositions, ultimately generating novel alloys with tailored 
properties. The application of GANs in alloy design follows a structured 
methodology. Initially, a dataset containing experimentally validated 
alloy compositions and their corresponding properties is compiled. The 
GAN model is trained on this dataset, allowing the Generator to learn the 
statistical distributions of feasible compositions. Once trained, the 
Generator can be conditioned to design alloys with specific mechanical, 
thermal, or electrical properties, making it a valuable tool for inverse 
materials design as shown in Fig. 2 . The generated compositions can be 
further refined using additional machine learning models or physics- 
based simulations, such as Density Functional Theory (DFT) calcula -
tions, to assess their stability and feasibility. The most promising can -
didates are subsequently validated through experimental synthesis and 
testing.
GANs offer several advantages over conventional alloy design ap -
proaches. They enable rapid exploration of compositional space, iden -
tifying novel materials that might not be considered through traditional 
methods. This significantly reduces the time and cost associated with 
alloy development. Additionally, GANs facilitate multi-objective opti -
mization, allowing the simultaneous enhancement of multiple perfor -
mance criteria, such as high strength, corrosion resistance, and thermal 
stability. Unlike conventional methods that rely heavily on human 
intuition and iterative experimentation, GANs provide a systematic, 
data-driven approach to alloy discovery, improving both efficiency and 
accuracy. Furthermore, GANs can be integrated with physics-based 
models to refine and validate generated compositions, ensuring their 
practical applicability. Recent studies have demonstrated the effective -
ness of GANs in designing high-entropy alloys (HEAs) and other complex 
multi-component systems. For instance, GAN-based models have been 
successfully employed to predict stable HEAs with superior mechanical 
properties, optimize compositions for targeted functionalities, and 
automate the discovery of novel magnesium alloys with enhanced 
corrosion resistance. These applications underscore the transformative 
potential of GANs in materials science, paving the way for the acceler -
ated development of next-generation alloys for aerospace, biomedical, 
and energy applications. The integration of GANs with high-throughput 
simulations and experimental validation further enhances their reli -
ability, establishing them as a promising tool for advancing computa -
tional materials design [45 – 49] .

2.4. DFT calculations
The Special Quasirandom Structure (SQS) [50] approach was 
employed to model chemical disorder in RHEAs. Using the Alloy 
Theoretic Automated Toolkit (ATAT) algorithm [51,52] , multiple 
2 × 2 × 5 supercells with a fixed unit cell containing 40 atoms were 
generated. The optimized total free energies and lattice constants of 
these supercells were evaluated, and the configurations with lower en -
ergies and lattice constants closely matching experimental values were 
selected for mechanical property calculations. The specific structures are 
given in supplementary document. First-principles calculations were 
performed using the CASTEP package within the framework of Density 
Functional Theory (DFT) [53] . The approximation of Generalized 
Gradient Approximation (GGA) of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) was 
chosen as the exchange – correlation functional for describing electron 
interactions, while electron-ion interactions were treated with the 
projector-augmented-wave (PAW) method [54] . A plane-wave cutoff 
energy of 600 eV was used, and the Brillouin zone was sampled using the 
Monkhorst-Pack scheme with a 4 × 4 × 2 k-points mesh. Convergence 
criteria were set to a maximum tolerance 10

3.2. Feature selection criteria
To better understand our descriptor space, we calculated the Pearson 
correlation coefficients (p) of each pair of descriptors before using the 
ML models, and visualization of the results is presented as a heatmap in 
Fig. 4 . The relationships among features are shown by the numeric 
values of p, with p = 0 meaning that there is no correlation, p = 1 
meaning there is a strong positive correlation, and p = 1 a strong 
negative correlation. ML models tend to overfit when they have highly 
correlated features that do not make robust predictions. The red and 
blue color indicates strong positive and negative correlations respec -
tively in the heatmap. The descriptor "AE" (average electronegativity) 
was dropped from the dataset to optimize the machine learning model 
and increase its predictive accuracy. This showed a strong positive 
correlation (a correlation coefficient of 0.803) between "AE" and "VEC" 
(valence electron concentration). Generally, highly correlated de -
scriptors are retained, resulting in redundancy in the dataset, causing 
multicollinearity, which complicates the learning task and raises the risk 
of overfitting. Removing "AE" simplifies the dataset, ensuring that only 
unique and independent descriptors are utilized. This step reduces 
redundancy and enhances the model ’ s ability to generalize to unseen 
data, improving its overall performance. Therefore, the number of de -
scriptors was reduced from 8 to 7.
3.3. ML model performance
The elastic constant for RHEAs was predicted from the dataset by 
employing ten models: RFR, XGBoost R, GBR, Ridge R, Adaboost R, 
MLP, LGBM, KNNR, DTR, and CatBoost R. The dataset is randomly 
divided into a training and a testing set with 8 – 2 ratio. For each 
approach, hyperparameter tunning is done with LOOCV, 3-fold, 5-fold, 
and 10-fold CV. Cross-validation (CV) is a crucial technique employed in 
machine learning, as it assists in the effective partitioning of data into a 
training and a validation set for the evaluation of the model performance 
in order to enhance bias and overfitting that may occur as a result of a 
single exposure to training and testing data. This study used four CV 
strategies as leave-one-out CV (LOOCV), 3-fold CV, 5-fold CV, and 10- 
fold CV to the data set in order to identify and apply the most optimal 
method for the provided data set. LOOCV Cross Validation that is 
exhaustively computationally intensive as it considers one observation 
for testing in each round which may results into high variance because of 
outlier variation.

3.4. Descriptor importance and visualization
The Shapley Additive explanations (SHAP) method elucidates the 
influence of each descriptor on the machine learning (ML) model by 
computing the Shapley values. The SHAP bar graph uses all dataset in -
stances to calculate the mean absolute value of each descriptor. Fig. 7
displays the relevance ranking of seven descriptors and their contribu -
tion to the elastic constants, with VEC and MT occupying the top two 
positions. Interestingly, prior findings highlight the significant role of 
VEC, ranking highest in descriptor importance for predicting crystal 
structure [35 – 37,39,56] , Young ’ s modulus [41,57,58] and hardness 
[59] of HEAs. Previous studies affirm VEC as a critical feature for pre -
dicting Young ’ s modulus in High Entropy Alloys using ML models [41] . 
Similarly, Roy et al. [60] demonstrated that MT is a crucial parameter 
for predicting Young ’ s modulus of HEAs via the gradient boosting 
method. Moreover, analyzing SHAP values across different compositions 
can help to detect nonlinear interactions between features, which can 
inform advanced modeling strategies such as polynomial regressions or 
interaction terms.
Additionally, discrepancies between SHAP-based feature importance 
and physical intuition may indicate missing descriptors that influence 
mechanical properties, guiding further feature engineering. While SHAP 
itself does not enhance model performance directly, its insights can be 
leveraged to refine feature selection, hyperparameter tuning, and model 
architecture, ultimately improving predictive accuracy. Another key 
aspect not explicitly considered in this study is the effect of temperature 
on the mechanical properties of the studied RHEAs. The ML models were 
trained on density functional theory (DFT) calculations performed at 
0 K, which is a widely adopted approach in computational materials 
science for establishing baseline mechanical properties. However, me -
chanical properties such as elastic moduli can be temperature- 
dependent due to thermal expansion, anharmonic effects, and phase 
transformations. Incorporating temperature effects would require 
additional computational methodologies, such as finite-temperature 
molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, quasi-harmonic approximation 
(QHA), or experimental validation, which were beyond the scope of this 
study. Nonetheless, future work could integrate temperature-dependent 
effects by coupling ML models with thermodynamic and phonon-based 
calculations to capture the mechanical behavior of these alloys at 
elevated temperatures. This extension would enhance the applicability 
of the ML framework in predicting the performance of RHEAs under 
realistic operating conditions. Fig. 8 illustrates comparison of elastic 
constants (C11, C12, and C44 in GPa) for TiZrNbTa, TiZrNbTaMo, 
TiZrNbTaHf, TiZrNbTaV, TiZrNbTa W and TiZrNbTaRe RHEAs as pre -

lowest error metrics. Therefore, CatBoost R was taken as the optimal 
model for future predictions. With this model, different alloy composi -

compositions were chosen from the composition space within the 
TiZrNbTa system, From the additional testing by density functional

TiZrNbTaV, TiZrNbTaW, and TiZrNbTaRe are considered as potential

3.6. Mechanical parameters
The shear, bulk, and young moduli (B, G, and E) are critical pa -
rameters to assess the capacity of a material to sustain deformation 
through the applied stress. Fig. 10 shows the comparisons of the DFT and 
ML-prediction to the elastic moduli of TiZrNbTaX (X = V, Mo, Hf, W and 
Re) RHEAs. The elastic modulus further exhibits consistent trends using 
both methods, thus supporting their use as complementary predictive 
engineering tools for the mechanical properties.
The moduli were computed using the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approxima -
tion [67] , ensuring an accurate representation of material behavior. 
Adding Mo, W, and Re significantly enhances B, G, and E, indicating an 
increase in deformation resistance. Among these, Re alloying imparts 
the most pronounced strengthening effect due to its inherently high 
elastic modulus, making TiZrNbTaRe the most deformation-resistant 
alloy. Conversely, Hf alloying drastically reduces the elastic moduli, 
with E values of 74.52 GPa (DFT) and 67.64 GPa (ML), owing to severe 
lattice distortion induced by the large atomic radius of Hf. The addition 
of Re increases resistance to deformation due to its strong atomic 
bonding and solid solution strengthening. Re ’ s large atomic radius and 
high atomic number enhance interatomic forces, making dislocation 
motion more difficult. It also disrupts the crystal lattice, creating bar -
riers to dislocation movement, which strengthens the material. Addi -
tionally, Re stabilizes high-temperature phases, further improving 
strength at elevated temperatures. In contrast, Hf enhances ductility by 
forming a solid solution with the base elements and reducing internal 
stresses. This promotes a more homogeneous microstructure, improving 
dislocation mobility and reducing grain boundary defects, which allows 
for more uniform deformation and better plasticity.
These trends align with experimental results, where the elastic 
moduli for TiZrNbTa and TiZrNbTaHf are reported to range between 
59 – 85 GPa and 80 – 116 GPa, respectively [67 – 71] . Hardness (Hv) and 
yield strength ( σ y), key indicators of resistance to plastic deformation, 
were derived using Eq. (1) proposed by Zhao et al. [42] , hardness (Hv) 
values correlate with G and E as shown in Fig. 10 , with Re alloying 
yielding the highest hardness (6.64 GPa for DFT and 7.36 GPa for ML). 
On the other hand, Hf alloying results in the lowest hardness (3.54 GPa 
for DFT and 3.30 GPa for ML), these predictions agree with experi -
