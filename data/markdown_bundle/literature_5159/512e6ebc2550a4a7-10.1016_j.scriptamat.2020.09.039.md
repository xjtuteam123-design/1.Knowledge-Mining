a b s t r a c t 
We report that in contrast to the anticipation, the macroscopic mechanical strength of single crystals 
of the face-centered-cubic single-phase equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni high entropy alloy is not inﬂuenced 
by thermal annealing during which short-range ordering is expected to develop. Experimental evidence 
shows that hardness at room temperature, critical resolved shear stress at room temperature and at 77 
K and activation volume values of the alloys do not signiﬁcantly vary with the annealing temperature in 
the range from 900 to 1200 °C. The stacking fault energies in the specimens deformed at 77 K following 
annealing at 10 0 0 and 120 0 °C respectively are essentially identical to each other. These results clearly 
indicate that the impact of the formation of chemical short-range order on the macroscopic yield strength 
of the equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni high entropy alloy is insigniﬁcant, even if it exists.

Since the discovery of quinary Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni high entropy al- 
loy (HEA) system in 2004 [ 1 , 2 ], this compositionally complex solid 
solution with the face-centered cubic (FCC) structure and its qua- 
ternary and ternary subsystems, such as Cr-Co-Ni medium en- 
tropy alloy (MEA), have attracted considerable interests. One of 
the reasons for this is that the unique alloy systems of HEAs and 
MEAs have aroused the revolutionary transition of alloy design 
space from the edges and corners in the phase diagram based on 
the conventional single-principal element (solvent-solute) concept 
to the near central regions of multi-component phase diagrams 
based on the innovative multiple-principal-elements concept [ 3 , 4 ]. 
In addition, HEA/MEAs have exhibited exceptional combination 
of high strength, high tensile ductility and high fracture tough- 
ness, particularly at cryogenic temperatures [5–8] . For example, 
an outstanding fracture toughness greater than 200 MPa /22c5m 1/2 has 
been attained in an equiatomic single-phase Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA 
through forming twinned nanobridges between the near-tip crack 
faces [8] .

As can be inferred from the occurrence of deformation twin- 
ning, the stacking fault energy (SFE), γSFE , of most HEA/MEAs is 
expected to be relatively low. Indeed, the SFE of the equiatomic 
Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni and Cr-Co-Ni solid solutions are respectively re- 
ported to be ~30 mJ/m 2 [9] and ~22 mJ/m 2 [10] , both of which dif- 
fer drastically from those obtained from density-functional theory 
(DFT) calculations that predict near-zero or negative SFEs [ 11 , 12 ]. 
Computational modelling has further demonstrated that the SFE of 
HEA/MEAs can be tuned by manipulating their local atomic con- 
ﬁgurations through the formation of chemical short-range order 
(SRO) while keeping the overall FCC structure [12] . Since the for- 
mation of SRO is thermodynamically driven to form bondings be- 
tween speciﬁc constituent atomic pairs for the reduction of the 
overall free energy, the extent of SRO is expected to vary depend- 
ing on temperature, i.e. from a virtually random structure at higher 
temperatures to structures with varying extents of SRO at lower 
temperatures [ 13 , 14 ]. The formation of SRO in the equiatomic Cr- 
Co-Ni MEA was experimentally proved ﬁrst by Zhang et al. [15] us- 
ing the extended x-ray absorption ﬁne structure (EXAFS) tech- 
nique, recently followed by Zhang et al. [16] with the energy- 
ﬁltered transmission electron microscopy (EF-TEM) imaging and 
diffraction techniques. The SFE of the equiatomic Cr-Co-Ni MEA is

The formation of SRO that provides additional resistance to dis- 
location motion has been known to strengthen the alloys [17–19] . 
Such strengthening is claimed to occur in low-temperature an- 
nealed Ti-6 wt.% Al [20] and in slow furnace-cooled equiatomic 
single-phase Cr-Co-Ni MEA [16] . However, the substantially differ- 
ent claim of no measurable yield strength increment, in the Cr- 
Co-Ni MEA annealed at temperatures ranging from 600 to 1200 °C 
followed by water-quenching, is also made recently [ 21 , 22 ]. On top 
of that almost nothing is known about the formation of SRO in 
the equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA and its effects on mechanical 
properties. 
In this study, we systematically investigate hardness, yield 
strength and dislocation structure (dissociation) in single crystals 
of the equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA as a function of annealing 
temperature (ranging from 900 to 1200 °C) to shed lights on the 
impacts of SRO on mechanical properties of the equiatomic Cr-Mn- 
Fe-Co-Ni HEA. 
A single crystal of the equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA was 
grown in a modiﬁed Bridgman furnace with an Al 2 O 3 crucible in 
a vacuum of 1.1 × 10 −6 Torr and a growth rate of 82 mm/h. The 
as-grown single crystal encapsulated in an argon-ﬁlled quartz tube 
was ﬁrst annealed at 1200 °C for one week followed by water- 
quenching, and then cut into four slabs of ~2.5 mm in thickness 
with the [ ¯1 23 ] direction aligned along its long side. To obtain spec- 
imens with four different initial states, three slabs of the four were 
ground slightly, re-encapsulated and re-annealed respectively at 
90 0, 10 0 0 and 1100 °C for one week to promote the formation of 
SRO (if exists) and water-quenched. 
Vickers microhardness measurements were performed on the 
polished (111) plane of the single crystal annealed at 90 0, 10 0 0, 
1100 and 1200 °C respectively with a load of 50 g and a dwelling 
time of 15 s. The dimension of 2 mm × 2 mm × 5 mm for com- 
pression specimens and a cross-sectional area of 2 mm × 2 mm 
and a gauge length of 4.5 mm for dog-bone-shaped tensile spec- 
imens were utilized. The loading-axis orientation for both com- 
pression and tensile tests was set to be [ ¯1 23 ] . Uniaxial compres- 
sion and tensile tests were conducted at 77 K and room temper- 
ature (RT) at a strain-rate of 1 × 10 −4 s −1 . Strain-rate jump tests 
were carried out in tension at RT to estimate the activation vol- 
ume for deformation of specimens annealed at 10 0 0 and 1200 °C. 
Microstructures before and after deformation were examined us- 
ing a transmission electron microscope (TEM) (JEM-20 0 0FX TEM) 
operated at 200 kV. 
Fig. 1 shows phase decomposition observed in a single crystal 
of the equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA upon annealing at 850 °C 
for one week followed by water-quenching. Platelet precipitates 
are observed presumably on {111} planes of the single crystal 
( Fig. 1 (a)). In Fig. 1 (b), these precipitates are conﬁrmed to be a 
Cr-rich phase by energy dispersive X-ray spectrometry (EDS) analy- 
sis. According to the previous study by Otto et al. [23] , this Cr-rich 
phase must be the sigma phase. Since such phase decomposition 
was never observed in this alloy annealed at a temperature above 
900 °C, we evaluate the effects of the annealing temperature on 
mechanical properties by annealing crystals at temperatures over 
900 °C. 
Fig. 2 (a) shows Vickers hardness values measured at RT on the 
(111) plane of single crystals as a function of annealing tempera- 
ture. The hardness value does not vary signiﬁcantly with the an- 
nealing temperature, and stays almost constant (~145 Hv). Fig. 2 (b) 
displays critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) in compression plotted 
as a function of annealing temperature. The CRSS was calculated 
for the (111)[ ¯1 01] slip system with the maximum Schmid factor 
based on the results of slip trace analysis. The CRSS values at both 
RT (~45) and 77 K (~110) also does not change signiﬁcantly with 
the annealing temperature, and are consistent with those obtained 
in our previous study [9] .

( Fig. 2 (c)) and is in line with that obtained in compression 
( Fig. 2 (b)). The activation volume for deformation was also evalu-

Fig. 2 (d) presents the activation volumes of single crystals an- 
nealed at 10 0 0 and 120 0 °C. The activation volumes are 202 ±42 
and 192 ±42 b 3 after annealing at 10 0 0 and 1200 °C, respectively 
and slightly lower than that (232 b 3 ) obtained recently by com- 
pression tests of single crystals annealed at 1200 °C [24] . The mea- 
sured activation volumes for both crystals keep nearly constant, 
being in accordance with the hardness and CRSS values shown in 
Figs. 2 (a)-(c). 
Figs. 3 (a) and (b) show typical dislocation structures observed 
in [ ¯1 23 ] -oriented single crystals deformed to about 4% plastic 
strain in compression at 77 K after annealing at 1200 and 10 0 0 °C, 
respectively. Dislocations are seen to be smoothly curved forming 
planar dislocation arrays on the slip planes in both specimens. This 
is consistent with our previous observation [9] . We believe that the 
low SFE of the HEA alloy is one of the major reasons for the planar 
arrays of dislocations on their slip planes. Although the presence of 
SRO is proposed to be a more plausible reason for the planar array 
of dislocations in some FCC alloys [25] , we do not see any clear 
difference in the planarity of dislocation arrays for specimens an- 
nealed at 10 0 0 and 1200 °C ( Figs. 3 (a) and (b)) even if there is a 
variation in the extent of SRO for these two specimens. 
As in other FCC metals, a 1/2 [ ¯1 01 ] perfect dislocation in the 
Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA is known to dissociate into two 1/6 < 112 > 
Shockley partials separated by a stacking fault [9] . Figs. 3 (c) and 
(d) demonstrate typical weak-beam dark-ﬁeld TEM images show- 
ing dislocation dissociation in specimens deformed at 77 K in com- 
pression after annealing at 1200 and 1000 °C, respectively. The sep- 
aration distances measured for several coupled Shockley partials 
are plotted in Figs. 3 (e) and (f) as a function of angle, θ, between 
the Burgers vector and line direction of the perfect dislocations [9] . 
The SFEs are estimated to be 28 mJ/m 2 for both specimens, which 
is essentially identical to those (26.5 mJ/m 2 [26] and 30.0 mJ/m 2 
[9] ) previously reported for the Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA. Surprisingly, 
the SFE here does not depend on the annealing temperature at 
all. Expectedly, it should be higher for the specimen annealed at 
a lower temperature (at 10 0 0 °C in this study) due to the higher 
extent of SRO developed during annealing at the lower tempera- 
ture [ 16 , 19 ]. This will be further discussed later.

In conventional concentrated solid-solution alloys, the CRSS can 
be described as the two-thirds power of solute concentration, i.e. 
the Labusch model [30] . This model considers the fact that a dis- 
location moving in the slip plane would encounter an array of so- 
lute atoms with varying interaction strengths, not a single solute 
atom proposed by Fleischer [31] for dilute solid-solutions. These 
two models are built on the premise that a dislocation glides in 
a solvent lattice containing solute atoms, and interacts with so- 
lute atoms. However, this solvent-solute distinction will become 
ambiguous in HEA/MEAs in which most components appear in 
(nearly) equiatomic concentration. In addition, due to the nature 
of compositional complexity, the unique atomic-scale randomness 
of constituting elements in HEA/MEAs would profoundly affect the 
nucleation and motion of dislocations [32–35] . Hence, the Fleischer 
and Labusch models are not appropriately utilized to predict the 
CRSS in HEA/MEAs. 
The randomness of different atomic species at the atomic 
level in HEAs creates a stochastic spatial distribution of Peierls 
stress across a slip plane. Such local ﬂuctuation in the Peierls 
stress originating from the spatial randomness of compositions 
renders an HEA with a CRSS exceeding that associated with any 
of its individual component metals [32] . Apart from the random 
spatial distribution of complex compositions, the enthalpy-driven

which SRO is expected to develop. Slow cooling and annealing at 
lower temperatures should not be used here due to the propensity 
of precipitation, see Fig. 1 . This further implies that the impact of 
SRO formed on the macroscopic yield strength of the equiatomic 
Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA is insigniﬁcant even if SRO does exist, i.e. the 
yield strength of the HEA here is governed by its atomic-scale com- 
positional randomness. 
The extent of SRO in the equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA is ex- 
pected to be less signiﬁcant than that in the equiatomic Cr-Co-Ni 
MEA. Chemical SRO in the equiatomic Cr-Co-Ni MEA is believed 
to form since there exists strong repulsive interaction among Cr 
atoms and attractive interaction between Cr and Co/Ni atoms [12] . 
The quinary HEA contains Mn and Fe atoms that do not contribute 
to the formation of SRO, leading to the reduced absolute fractions 
of Cr, Co and Ni atoms in the quinary HEA. This composition- 
dependent SRO favors that a signiﬁcant increase in yield strength 
by SRO is not anticipated for the quinary HEA. 
As locally randomly distributed elements evolve into SRO the 
local SFE will be distinct from one site to another, causing variable 
dislocation dissociation distance. As seen in Figs. 3 (e) and (f), no 
clearly changing partial separation distance is observed in speci- 
mens deformed at 77 K after annealing at 10 0 0 and 120 0 °C, and 
their SFEs are essentially identical. This contradicts the simulation 
results on the equiatomic Cr-Co-Ni MEA where the SFE is pre- 
dicted to increase upon the formation of SRO [ 12 , 16 , 19 ]. According 
to the results of recent computer simulations [ 16 , 36–38 ], chemical 
SRO would be completely destroyed after the passage of a certain 
number ( < 7) of dislocations. Massive dislocation motion occurs at 
macroscopic yielding. This means that chemical SRO (if exists) is 
entirely destructed at the stress level of macroscopic yielding, and 
thus the dissociation width of dislocations is not affected by SRO 
after yielding. Although the varying chemical elements randomly 
present in the vicinity of a dislocation core will generate locally 
variable partial splitting distance [39] , this rarely affects the mea- 
sured average SFE ( Figs. 3 (e) and (f)). 
Our results show that the hardness (at RT), CRSS in tension and 
compression (at RT and at 77 K) and activation volume values (at 
RT) of single crystals of the equiatomic Cr-Mn-Fe-Co-Ni HEA an- 
nealed for one week respectively at 90 0, 10 0 0, 110 0 and 120 0 °C 
followed by water-quenching all remain almost unchanged. In ad- 
dition, the SFEs in specimens deformed at 77 K after annealing at 
10 0 0 and 1200 °C are essentially equivalent. This supports that the 
macroscopic yield strength of the single-phase equiatomic Cr-Mn- 
Fe-Co-Ni HEA is not affected by heat-treatment during which SRO 
is expected to develop, the inﬂuence of SRO on its macroscopic 
yield strength is trivial even if SRO does exist.
