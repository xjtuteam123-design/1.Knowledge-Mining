Mechanical Properties and Energetic Characteristics
of AlTiZrNbTa Refractory High-Entropy Alloy
Shenghui Zeng, Shengde Zhang, Fangju Zhang, Honglei Wang, Junhong Chen, and Xicheng Huang
Submitted: 30 May 2025 / Revised: 25 July 2025 / Accepted: 31 July 2025
The compressive mechanical behavior of the AlTiZrNbTa refractory high-entropy alloy was systematically
investigated under both quasi-static (1 3 10-3 s-1) and dynamic (2.2 3 103 s-1) conditions, and its fracture
mechanisms and energetic characteristics were analyzed. Results reveal that the AlTiZrNbTa alloy exhibits
outstanding mechanical properties with a yield strength of 1899.4 MPa, ultimate compressive strength of
2102.8 MPa, and fracture strain of 12.3%. Moreover, the alloy shows pronounced positive strain-rate
sensitivity, demonstrating signiﬁcantly enhanced strength and ductility under dynamic compression.
Fractographic analysis reveals a strain-rate-dependent transition in failure mode. With increasing strain
rate, temperature effects on deformation become pronounced, shifting the failure mechanism from brittle-
ductile fracture to a viscous fracture mode. Due to its low thermal conductivity, dynamic deformation
induces signiﬁcant adiabatic temperature rises. Furthermore, this localized heating accelerates oxidation of
the reactive metallic elements in the fragments upon exposure to air, thereby signiﬁcantly enhancing the
material/C213 s energy release performance. These ﬁndings highlight the alloy /C213 s combination of high strength,
ductility, and energy release capability, suggesting its potential for advanced energetic structural materials
and high-velocity penetrator applications.

1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are a novel class of metallic
materials that use multiple principal elements in near-equal
proportions (Ref 1). This compositional strategy exploits high
conﬁgurational entropy to stabilize solid-solution phases,
thereby overcoming the limitations of conventional alloy
design. In other words, HEAs depart from conventional
single-principal-element alloy design, enabling the exploration
of novel multicomponent alloys with enhanced properties (Ref
2).
In recent years, refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs)
have garnered signiﬁcant attention for their potential in
energetic structural materials (ESMs) (Ref 3). Their combina-
tion of exceptional strength and energy-release capability under
dynamic deformation makes them promising candidates for
applications in extreme environments. For instance, tailoring
the Ta concentration in HfZrTi alloys has been shown to
enhance penetration performance and exothermic energy
release, achieving a synergistic improvement in strength,
ductility, and theoretical combustion enthalpy (Ref 4). These
ﬁndings have spurred extensive research into RHEAs as
materials capable of simultaneously possessing superior
mechanical performance and energy release capabilities (Ref
5-11).
To serve effectively as ESMs, RHEAs must exhibit high
load-bearing ductility while enabling controllable fragmenta-
tion behavior and predictable energy release under dynamic
impact loading. Notably, Nb, Zr, Ti, and Ta possess high
theoretical combustion enthalpies and exhibit pronounced
exothermic upon oxidation in air. In addition, these elements
have a body-centered cubic (BCC) crystal structure at high
temperature. This has made the TiZrNbTa-based alloys a key
baseline system that forms a single-phase BCC solid solution
and exhibits both very high strength and good ductility (Ref 9-
14). For example, TiZrNbTa exhibits a compressive strength
approximately 1389 MPa and a failure strain of /C24 36% (Ref
10). Consequently, the TiZrNbTa system serves as an ideal
foundation for the subsequent development of advanced ESMs
(Ref 15).
Incorporation of Al into RHEAs further enhances perfor-
mance by promoting solid-solution strengthening and
microstructural reﬁnement (Ref 16). Moreover, the high
exothermic potential upon oxidation of Al makes it particularly
valuable for energetic applications (Ref 4). Thus, incorporating
Al into the TiZrNbTa RHEA can enhance the alloy /C213 s theoretical
energy release potential and penetration capability, suggesting a
viable strategy for developing higher-performance ESMs.
However, the mechanical properties and fracture behavior of
AlTiZrNbTa remain poorly understood. In particular, the failure
mechanisms and associated energy release processes under
high-strain-rate loading remain unclariﬁed. Therefore, a sys-
tematic investigation of the strain-rate-dependent mechanical
behavior and energy release characteristics of the AlTiZrNbTa
alloy is imperative. Elucidating these strain-rate-dependent

2. Experimental
2.1 Material Preparation
The AlTiZrNbTa alloy was prepared via vacuum arc
melting. Due to the difference in melting points between Al
and refractory metals, Al tends to volatilize during the melting
process, potentially leading to deviations from the target alloy
composition. To mitigate this, a two-step melting process was
used in this study. Firstly, high-purity Al (99.99 wt.%) and Ti
(99.99 wt.%) were melted together in a predetermined ratio to
create an AlTi intermediate alloy. subsequently, this interme-
diate alloy was remelted with high-purity Zr, Ni, and Ta
( ‡ 99.99 wt.%) in the vacuum arc furnace to synthesize the
initial alloy ingot. During melting, the furnace pressure was
kept below 5 9 10
-3 Pa and high-purity argon gas was
employed as a protective atmosphere to minimize oxidation
and further volatilization. The ingot was repeatedly melted ﬁve
times. The ﬁnal AlTiZrNbTa alloy was a bowl-shaped ingot
approximately 30 mm high and 40 mm in diameter.

3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructural Analysis
The optical micrograph of the AlTiZrNbTa alloy is shown in
Fig. 3. It can be seen that the alloy’s surface exhibits a clear
dendritic morphology. The dark dendrites create an intercon-
nected network that partitions the lighter areas between them
into small interdendritic regions. EDS was performed on
dendritic and interdendritic regions, and the compositional data
are summarized in Table 1. It can be seen that Al, Ti, and Zr are
preferentially enriched in the interdendritic region, while Nb
and Ta are relatively enriched within the dendrites. This
distribution suggests that Al tends to form stable bonds with Ti
and Zr, which signiﬁcantly reduces the compatibility between
group IV elements (Ti, Zr) and group V elements (Nb, Ta),
leading to elemental segregation and dendritic growth of the
alloy.

3.2.2 Fracture Behavior. Under dynamic loading condi-
tion, the fracture of the specimen resulted in the generation of
exothermic reaction sparks. Fig. 6 records this process using a
high-speed camera. The specimen exhibited catastrophic frag-
mentation failure after experiencing a limited compressive
deformation (Fig. 6b) and localized sparks appeared at crack
sites (Fig. 6c). Along with fragment ejection, micron-sized
particles formed drifting dust clouds in local space. When the
concentration reached a critical level, the thermal energy
released during fragmentation ignited the dust cloud, producing
a spherical ﬂame front that propagated outward (Fig. 6d). This
observation illustrates the rapid energy release behavior of
AlTiZrNbTa under dynamic compression.
Figure 7 shows the recovered AlTiZrNbTa specimens after
quasi-static and dynamic compression. In both cases, the
AlTiZrNbTa alloy fractures by a multi-shear failure mechanism,
with cracks nucleating near the specimen mid-section. Under
quasi-static condition, the fracture surface exhibits one primary
shear zone accompanied by a secondary shear zone. In contrast,
dynamic resulted in numerous intersecting shear zones and the
formation of smaller fragments. In this case, instead of strain
localizing into a single catastrophic shear zone, the plastic
deformation is spread across many zones. As a result,
catastrophic crack propagation is suppressed and the alloy
accommodates much larger strains (Ref 27), which demon-
strates its good ductility.
Fragmentation morphology is highly strain-rate dependent.
After quasi-static testing (Fig. 7a), the alloy fractured into
multiple angular fragments ranging from 3 to 8 mm. In contrast,
under dynamic compression (Fig. 7b), the alloy disintegrated
into ﬁner fragments (1 /C0 3 mm) and micron-sized powder.
Macroscopic analysis of the recovered specimens indicates that
increased strain rates amplify plastic deformation and produce
ﬁner fragmentation, reﬂecting a transition in the dominant
fracture mechanism.
To elucidate the fracture mechanism of the AlTiZrNbTa
alloy, SEM was employed to investigate the fracture surfaces of
the recovered specimens. Figure 8 illustrates the multi-scale
fracture features of the alloy under quasi-static compression.
Fig. 8(b) reveals distinct microstructural contrasts between
primary and secondary shear zones, illustrating the complex
fracture response of the AlTiZrNbTa alloy under quasi-static
loading. The primary shear zone displays cleavage facets
characterized by river patterns and shear separation traces
(Fig. 8b). At higher magniﬁcation (Fig. 8c), microcracks were
observed at the intersections of cleavage facets and grain
boundaries. Notably, Fig. 8(d) reveals vein-like patterns devoid
of dimples and crystalline facets, indicating the presence of
localized molten regions. This phenomenon parallels the frac-
ture characteristics observed in amorphous alloys (Ref 28)-
(Ref 30) and suggests that rapid adiabatic heating at crack tips
induces transient amorphization followed by resolidiﬁcation,
which will be discussed in section 3.3.
The secondary shear zone exhibits a coexistence of brittle
and ductile microstructural features. As Fig. 8(e) shows, the
fracture surface displays a quasi-cleavage morphology charac-
terized by ﬁne cleavage facets interspersed with tear ridges, slip
steps, and Chevron patterns. A rock-candy morphology was
also observed, indicating brittle grain-boundary failure. At
higher magniﬁcation (Fig. 8f), localized microvoid coalescence
dimples were observed along grain boundaries, indicating the
occurrence of ductile fracture in this area. The formation of
these intergranular dimples can be attributed to a pronounced
thermal softening effect within the AlTiZrNbTa alloy, which is
proposed to induce dynamic recrystallization at critically
stressed grain boundaries (Ref 31, 32).

brittle-ductile mode to a viscous fracture mechanism, thereby
enhancing its plasticity and energy dissipation.

4. Conclusions
In this study, the AlTiZrNbTa RHEA was prepared via
vacuum arc melting. The alloy /C213 s potential as a novel, high-
strength energetic structural material was demonstrated through
systematic evaluation of its mechanical properties and energetic
performance. The principal ﬁndings are summarized as follows:
1. In comparison with conventional energetic structural al-
loys, AlTiZrNbTa demonstrates exceptional mechanical
properties, achieving a compressive strength of 2108.8
MPa and a fracture strain of 12.3%, along with pro-
nounced positive strain-rate sensitivity.
2. The fracture mode of AlTiZrNbTa is strain-rate depen-
dent. Under quasi-static compression, the alloy shows a
mixed fracture mechanism of brittle and ductile. In con-
trast, under the dynamic regime, signiﬁcant thermal soft-
ening triggers a transition to a viscous fracture
mechanism, thereby enhancing localized plasticity during
high-strain-rate deformation.
3. The low thermal conductivity of AlTiZrNbTa promotes
thermomechanical instability under high-strain-rate load-
ing. Consequently, AlTiZrNbTa undergoes more pro-
nounced thermal softening and displays increased
susceptibility to adiabatic shear band formation under dy-
namic loading.
4. AlTiZrNbTa shows a rapid energy release under dynamic
compression, while its exceptional mechanical strength
enables effective penetration. This energy release mecha-
nism proceeds through three stages, including mechanical
fragmentation into ﬁne particles; rapid heating of these
fragments; and subsequent exothermic oxidation. The
combination of high mechanical strength and oxidation-
driven energy release under dynamic loading highlights
AlTiZrNbTa alloy as a promising candidate for energetic
structural applications.
