New Mg-based amorphous alloys in Mg-Y-misch metal systems 
H. Horikiri, A. Kato, A. Inoue and T. Masumoto 
Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980 (Japan) 
Abstract 
New amorphous alloys in the Mg-Y-Mm (where Mm denotes misch metal) system were found to form in the composition 
range 2.5-15 at.% Y and 0%-8% Mm by melt spinning. The highest tensile fracture strength is 530 MPa for 
Mgss.sY75Mm 4 and the amorphous alloy also exhibits good corrosion resistance which is superior to that for the commer- 
cial AZ91 (Mg-AI-Zn) alloy. The application of powder metallurgy processing consisting of high pressure gas atomiza- 
tion and warm extrusion to an Mg95.sY2.5Mm 2 alloy with good bending ductility even in a crystallized state caused the 
formation of a bulk alloy with a yield strength of 560 MPa at room temperature and 410 MPa at 523 K. The elevated- 
temperature strength is much superior to that for the heat-resistant type of Mg-Y-Nd alloy (WE54A-T6). The high 
strength is presumably due to the formation of a finely mixed structure consisting of Mg, MglE(Ce, Nd), Mgl7La 2 and 
Mg24Y 5. Thus the Mg-Y-Mm alloy is attractive because of the features of the high elevated-temperature strength 
combined with its light weight and good corrosion resistance. 
1. Introduction 
Recently, Inoue and co-workers have reported [1-5] 
that Mg-based amorphous alloys are formed in wide 
composition ranges and their amorphous alloys exhibit 
high mechanical strengths as well as a wide super- 
cooled liquid region before crystallization. In these Mg- 
based amorphous alloys, Mg-Ln-TM (Ln = lanthanide 
metal, TM = transition metal) systems were thought to 
be the most important, because the highest tensile 
strength was obtained in these alloy systems. However, 
the existence of TM elements was recognized to be 
harmful for corrosion resistance even in an amorphous 
state [6]. Accordingly, we searched for a new high 
strength Mg-based amorphous alloy without TM 
elements. As a result, amorphous alloys with rather 
high tensile strength were found to form in rapidly 
solidified Mg-Y-Ln ternary alloys. The first aim of this 
paper is to present the composition ranges in which an 
amorphous phase is formed in rapidly solidified 
Mg-Y-Mm (Mm = misch metal) alloys and the thermal 
stability, crystallization process, mechanical properties 
and corrosion resistance for the Mg-Y-Mm amor- 
phous alloys. The second is to examine the microstruc- 
ture and mechanical properties of bulk Mg-Y-Mm 
alloys produced by warm extrusion of their atomized 
powders.

tion melting a mixture of prealloyed Mg-Mm alloys

From the master alloy ingots, rapidly solidified ribbons

diffractometry and transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM) techniques. The crystallization temperature T x

properties were examined by a Vickers microhardness 
tester with 0.49 N (50 gf) load and an Instron-type 
tensile testing machine at a strain rate of 5.6 x 10 -4 s- 
respectively. 
Based on the experimental data obtained from the 
melt-spun ribbons, the prealloyed ingot with an appro- 
priate alloy composition (Mg95.sY2.sMm2) was 
atomized with argon gas at an applied pressure of 9.8 
MPa in a specially designed high pressure gas atomiza- 
tion apparatus [6] in which each of the components 
oxygen and moisture can be controlled to be less than 
1 p.p.m. The powders were sieved and precompacted 
into a Cu can in the same well-controlled argon 
atmosphere with oxygen and moisture contents less 
than 1 p.p.m. Subsequently, the precompacted powder 
was outgassed for 900 s at 300 K and sealed by weld- 
ing. The sealed powders were extruded to an area 
reduction of 10:1 in a 100 ton extrusion press at differ- 
ent temperatures between 573 and 698 K. The micro- 
structure and mechanical properties of the extruded

Mg95.sY2.sMm2 alloy as the fundamental composition
