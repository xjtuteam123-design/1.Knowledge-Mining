ABSTRACT 
Existing additively manufactured aluminum alloys exhibit unfavorable tensile strength above 200 
◦
C due to the 
strengthening phase coarsening, microstructure instability and cracking. This paper designed a novel Al-5Fe- 
1Mg-0.6Sc-0.3Zr alloy for powder bed fusion using laser beam (PBF-LB) with tensile strength of 189 MPa at 
400 
◦
C, which is the highest value reported. The main strengthening phases of the alloy were Al
6
Fe particle and 
nano-scaled L1
2
-Al
3
(Sc, Zr) precipitates. The exceptional high-temperature performance is attributed to high 
volume fraction (~35%) of nanoprecipitates Al
6
Fe modified by Sc and Zr elements, showing enhanced thermal 
stability and refined particle dimension. The addition of Sc and Zr elements also significantly retards the phase 
transformation from Al
6
Fe to θ -Al
13
Fe
4 
and inhibits brittle θ phase growth during thermal exposure. 
1. Introduction 
Recently, lightweight components with complex geometry and able 
to withstand higher service temperature are urgently needed in 
increased thrust-weight ratio aircraft [ 1 ]. Using heat-resistant 
aluminum alloy (density of 2.8 g/cm
3

(density of 4.05 g/cm

◦

40%. Additionally, additive manufacturing (AM) is an ideal method for 
produce complex lightweight parts [ 2 , 3 ]. By printing heat-resistant Al 
alloys, parts can achieve exceptional "complex structure & 
heat-resistance" performance [ 4 , 5 ]. Unfortunately, conventional 
heat-resistant aluminum alloy compositions based on casting and spray 
deposition are not suitable for AM, thus the development of AM-specific 
alloys is a key focus in the AM aluminum materials field [ 6 ]. For this 
purpose, adding low-diffusivity elements to form thermal stable pre -
cipitates is an effective way, such as Al-Fe [ 7 – 9 ], Al-Ce [ 10 – 12 ], Al-Cu 
[ 13 – 15 ] and Al-Ni [ 16 , 17 ] alloys. For example, a 200

Currently, low-cost recyclable AM Al-Fe [ 7 , 8 , 18 ] based alloys have

2. Experimental materials and procedure 
The Al-5Fe-1Mg-0.8Sc-0.7Zr (wt. %) alloy was fabricated by PBF-LB 
using prepared atomized powders. 15 × 70 × 15 mm
3 
cube-shaped 
samples were manufactured on a FS271M Farsoon PBF-LB machine 
operating with the optimum parameter (parameter exploration in Sup -
plementary Fig. S2), laser power 300 W, laser scan speed of 1000 mm/s, 
hatch spacing 0.1 mm, and layer thickness of 0.05 mm. Microstructural 
characterization and Mechanical testing were performed on PBF-LB 
printed samples at different test temperatures, as well as after long 
term thermal exposure. Specimens for scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM, Helios Nanolab G3 UC) and electron backscattered diffraction 
(EBSD, FEI Nano LAB, 600i) were prepared using standard metallo -
graphic techniques. The NETZSCH DSC204F1 analyzer conducts dif -
ferential scanning calorimetry (DSC) tests within the temperature range 
of 25 
◦
C – 600 
◦
C. Phase analysis using X-ray diffraction (XRD, XRD- 
7000S). Thin flake sample for TEM were prepared in a twin-jet elec -
tropolishing device (TJ100-SE, LEBO Science). To reveal the phase 
distribution, specimens were observed in the transmission electron mi -
croscopy (TEM, Talos F200X), which integrating high-resolution STEM 
and TEM imaging. The lattice strain near the interface was analyzed 
using Geometric Phase Analysis (GPA) based on the HRTEM image. 
Electronic universal material testing machine (EUTM, Instron 3369) was 
used for ambient temperature tension. High-temperature tensile tests 
were conducted on a hydraulic servo dynamic testing machine (8802, 
UK). Vickers hardness testing of the alloy in the as-fabricated and 
thermal exposure conditions was used a 100g load with a 15s dwell time. 
The first-principles calculations have been performed within the 
framework of density functional theory (DFT), as implemented in the 
Vienna ab initio Simulation Package code.

4. Conclusions

Zr alloy for both high-temperature strength and sufficient PBF-LB- 
processability. The alloys exhibited attractive tensile strength of 497 
MPa at ambient temperature, and the strength approximately 376 MPa 
at 200
