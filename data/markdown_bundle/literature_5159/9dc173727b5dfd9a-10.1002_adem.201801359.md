1 5. Concluding Remarks
2 The microstructure of metallic materials determines their
3 mechanical properties. Therefore, one of the main research
4 directions in the production of titanium alloys is the tailoring of
5 the microstructure by controlling the impurity content (such as
6 oxygen, nitrogen, and carbon) and optimizing preparation
7 techniques and post treatments to achieve various morpholo-
8 gies, including lamellar , equiaxed, basket-weave, and bimodal
9 structures, grain sizes ranging from fine to ultrafine to
10 nanosized, and phase components of α , β , and α þ β . The goal
11 is to achieve the desired mechanical properties for specific
12 requirements via specific strengthening mechanisms.
13 V arious length scales from the micron to the nano level result
14 in different contributions to the strength and ductility of metallic
15 materials. H ence, a multimodal structure consisting of various
16 length scales in specific phases is desirable to achieve excellent
17 mechanical properties. An understanding of the stress and strain
18 characteristics of alloys and the fracture and strengthening
19 mechanism of multimodal structures facilitates the design and
20 development of the optimum microstructure.
21 A ccording to crystallographic behavior , the grain-boundary
22 interconnection, texture, or crystal orientation can dominate the
23 microstructure and affect the dislocations, free volume, and the
24 faceting mode of the grain boundary . The manipulation of the
25 characteristics of the grain boundaries and texture can change
26 the grain-boundary interconnection and fracture modes and
27 thus improve the mechanical properties of metallic materials
28 such as titanium and titanium alloys, as demonstrated and
29 formulated in our previous studies.
