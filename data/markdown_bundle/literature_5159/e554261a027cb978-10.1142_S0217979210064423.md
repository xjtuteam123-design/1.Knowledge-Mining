MECHANICAL PROPERTIES OF P/M MATERIALS OF RAPIDLY 
SOLIDIFIED Al-Co-Mg ALLOYS AT ELEVATED TEMPERATURES 
MAKOTO SUGAMATA and OHKI 
College of Industrial Technology, Nihon University, Narashino, Chiba 275-8575, Japan 
sugamata.makoto@nihon-u.ac.jp 
 
Received 30 August 2009 
Revised 1 February 2010 
With an aim of obtaining aluminum P/M materials str engthened by dispersion of transition metal 
compounds and solid solution of Mg, Al-2mass%Co and Al-5mass%Co alloys with varied Mg 
additions of 0, 1 and 5 mass% were prepared by rapi d solidification techniques. Rapidly solidified 
flakes were produced by argon gas atomization and s ubsequent splat quenching on a water-cooled 
copper roll. The flakes were consolidated to the P/ M (Powder metallurgy process is named as P/M) 
materials by hot extrusion after vacuum degassing. Cast ingots of these alloys were also hot-
extruded under the same conditions to the I/M (Ingo t metallurgy process is named as I/M) reference 
materials. Uniform dispersion of fine intermetallic compounds (Co 2Al9) was observed in all the as-
extruded P/M materials. Added Mg was present as the solute in the P/M and I/M materials alloy 
even after annealing at 773K. The P/M materials con taining Mg exhibited higher hardness and 
strength than those without Mg at room temperature. Tensile strength increased with increasing 
amount of Mg in the I/M materials at elevated temperatures. However, strength of the P/M materials 
decreased with addition of Mg at 573K and 673K. According to the steady state creep rate and creep 
rapture time, the creep resistance of the P/M mater ials containing Mg was clearly inferior to that of 
Mg-free alloys. Thus the positive effects of Mg add itions on mechanical properties of the P/M 
materials of Al-Co-Mg alloys disappeared at high temperature. 
Keywords : Al-Co-Mg alloy; rapid solidification; powder meta llurgy; elevated temperature; tensile 
strength; creep strength; dispersion strengthening; solid solution strengthening. 
1. Introduction 
Rapid solidification is one of the promising techni ques to develop metallic materials of 
high strength. In the P/M materials consolidated from rapidly solidified powder or ribbon, 
uniform dispersion of fine intermetallic compound c rystallized under high cooling rate 
contributes to increase their strength. In the alum inum based materials by rapid 
solidification (RS), transition metals
1-3 such as Fe, Mn, V and rare earth metals 4,5 such as 
Ce, Y are selected as the alloying element because their solubility limit in aluminum is 
low and their intermetallic compounds are thermally stable. On the other hand, there is 
the solid solution hardening as a material strength ening mechanism. In the RS-P/M 
materials of Al-Mg-Si alloys of high alloying addit ions, the alloys containing excess Mg 
to Mg 2Si composition showed higher hardness and tensile s trength than those without 
excess Mg at the same Mg+Si content 6. It was considered that both Mg solid solution and

2. Experimental Part 
Nominal and analyzed compositions of the tested all oys are listed in Table 1. Alloy 
ingots were prepared from pure aluminum and pure ma gnesium and Al-5mass%Co 
master alloy in ambient atmosphere. After dissolvin g master alloy in molten aluminum, 
Mg was added into the melt by the amount with an es timated loss of three per cent. The 
RS flakes of about 2 kg for each alloy were prepare d by remelting the alloy ingots. RS 
was carried out by argon gas atomizing the alloy me lt and subsequent splat quenching 
onto a water-cooled copper roll. The cooling rate d uring rapid solidification was 
estimated to be about 5×10 
5 K s -1 from lamellar eutectic structure of Al-33%Cu alloy . 
This cooling rate was higher than those of gas atom ized powders. More details of the 
rapid solidification apparatus have been reported elsewhere 9.

Hardness changes of the RS-P/M and I/M materials af ter isochronal annealing at 
various temperatures were measured at more than 10 points and the averaged values were 
obtained. The tensile test for as-extruded P/M and I/M materials was carried out at room 
temperature, 473 K and 573 K at an initial strain r ate of 1.67×10 -3 s-1 for the specimens 
machined to 4mm in diameter and 30mm in gauge lengt h. The holding time at the test 
temperature was 300 s. Furthermore, tensile propert ies of P/M materials annealed at 773 
K for 18 ks were obtained at the same initial strai n rates. The tensile strength and 
elongation were obtained by averaging the values of three specimens for each condition. 
Creep test specimens of 4 mm in diameter with M6 sc rew on both sides were 
machined from extruded rod. Screw grip parts made o f strainless steel were attached to 
the both end of specimen as shown in Figure1. The r ods for detecting the creep 
elongation were installed on small ribs. Creep stra in was calculated by the judgment that 
only the aluminum specimen was elongated. Creep tes ts were carried out at 573K and 
673K and various constant loads (initial stress) in air. Specimen was held in the electric 
furnace of creep test machine at the testing temper ature for 20 h before creep test to 
stabilize temperature of the testing system. The te mperature was kept within ±3 K of the 
test temperature, and creep elongation was obtained by averaging the values from two 
dial gauges placed on both side of the specimen. Fo r each condition, two creep specimens 
were tested. Creep rupture time and steady state cr eep rate were obtained by averaging 
the values of two creep curves.

3. Results and Discussion 
3.1. Microstructure and hardness of extruded materials 
As an example, optical micrographs of as-extruded P /M and I/M materials of A-2Co-
5Mg alloy are shown in Figure 2. After extrusion, f ine particles of Co compounds are 
dispersed uniformly in the P/M material. Coarse rod shaped Co compounds of which 
length is 20-30 µ m are observed in the I/M counterparts. The amount of compound 
increased with increasing Co addition in P/M and I/ M materials, however, the size of the 
compound was unchanged. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Figure 3. shows X-ray diffraction patterns of as-RS ed flake, as-extruded and 
annealed P/M materials of A-5Co-5Mg alloy. Weak dif fraction peaks of Co 2Al 9 are 
observed in the RS flakes, and they become sharp in the P/M material after annealing. 
Mg compounds (e.g. Mg 2Al 3) are not detected in the P/M materials even after annealing 
at high temperatures. The amounts of solute Mg in the as-extruded P/M and I/M materials 
of the alloys are shown in Table 2. The lattice con stants of α -Al becomes large with 
increase of the amount of solute Mg, on the other h and, it becomes smaller by solid 
solution of Co. Here, assuming the small amount of solute of Co can be neglected, the 
amount of Mg solute is calculated from the change o f lattice constant of α -aluminum. In 
all extruded materials, almost all the added Mg was dissolved as the solute in the 
aluminum matrix. 
The hardness of as-extruded P/M and I/M materials o f tested alloys at room 
temperature are shown in Figure 4. An increase in h ardness by the addition of Mg is 
clearly observed for the P/M and I/M materials in e ach Co-bearing alloy. Hardness of 
P/M materials is higher than that of the I/M materials for all the tested alloys. Hardness of 
Al-5Co-5Mg alloy is highest (142HV) for the P/M mat erial whereas it is not so for the 
I/M material. The effect of the Co addition for the increase in the hardness of I/M 
materials is observed slightly.

3.2. Tensile properties of P/M and I/M materials 
Figure 5 shows the tensile strength of as-extruded P/M and I/M materials at room 
temperature. Tensile strength of the P/M materials is higher than that of the I/M 
counterparts for all the tested alloys, thereby the effect of rapid solidification is clearly 
shown. For the P/M materials, tensile strength incr eases with increasing addition of Mg 
and Co. However, tensile strength of the I/M materi als decreases with increasing amount 
of Co, whereas hardness increased as shown in Figur e 3. Because of poor ductility of the 
I/M materials involving coarse Co compounds, an imp rovement of the tensile strength 
was not achieved in the I/M materials of Al-Co-Mg b ased alloys bearing higher amounts 
of Co. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
. 
 
 
 
 
Figure 6 shows tensile strength of the P/M and I/M materials at various testing 
temperatures plotted against amount of Mg for Al-5C o-XMg alloy. Here, “X” means the 
variation of Mg in the alloys. Tensile strength of I/M materials clearly increases with 
increasing Mg addition at room temperature and 473 K. However, an increment of 
increase of strength by addition of Mg decreases wi th testing temperature. An increase of 
the strength is slightly observed at 673 K. 
The tensile strength of P/M materials is high in co mparison with that of I/M 
materials at each testing temperature. Addition of Mg produces a large increase in tensile 
strength of the P/M materials of Al-Co-Mg alloy at room temperature. However, the 
effect on increase in strength by Mg addition decre ases at higher testing temperature. The 
tensile strength of Al-5Co-5Mg alloy rapidly decrea ses with testing temperature, and 
drops below that of the Mg-free alloys at 673K. Thu s, high temperature strength of 
dispersion strengthened aluminum based materials decreases with addition of Mg.
