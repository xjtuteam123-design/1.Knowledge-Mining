ABSTRACT
The high-temperature phase structure stability and mechanical property evolution of Mo
0.5
NbTiZrAl
x 
(x = 0.3, 
0.5, 0.7) RHEAs were investigated. The experimental results show that with the increase of Al content, the alloy 
maintains a stable BCC single-phase structure before and after deformation at 1100 ℃ , exhibiting excellent high- 
temperature phase constitution stability. At the same time, increasing Al content significantly enhanced the yield 
strength and fracture strength of the alloys but reduced the plasticity. The fundamental reasons are the lattice 
distortion caused by atomic solid solution and the hindering effect of grain boundaries on dislocation movement. 
Simultaneously, Al addition promoted grain refinement and orientation randomization of crystallographic 
orientation, contributing to improved microstructural homogeneity and high-temperature performance.
1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) are composed of five or more elements, 
with a simple microstructure and excellent comprehensive properties, 
and have received widespread attention since their proposal [1 – 3] . 
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) are an important branch in their 
development process, providing a new approach for the development of 
new high-temperature structural alloys [4] . The RHEAs, mainly 
composed of refractory metal elements such as W, Hf, Ta, V, Mo, Zr, and 
Nb, maintain the original properties of the HEAs while also possessing 
various high-temperature characteristics such as high melting point and 
high strength [5,6] . However, RHEAs composed of elements with high 
atomic numbers typically result in higher density and cost, limiting their 
specific engineering applications.
Consequently, reducing the density and cost of RHEAs is currently a 
key issue that needs to be addressed. At present, scholars have proposed 
that the density of RHEAs can be reduced by adding light elements such 
as Al, Ti, or Zr, and have made certain breakthroughs [7,8] . Preliminary 
work on Mo
0.5
NbTiZrAl
x 
alloys showed that the addition of Al can 
effectively enhance the hardness and wear resistance of the alloy 
without altering its phase composition [9] . However, in such RHEA, the 
evolution of phase structure and mechanical properties at 
high-temperatures has scarcely been explored.
Therefore, based on the previously designed Mo
0.5
NbTiZrAl
x 
(x = 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) RHEAs, this study further investigates the evolution of 
high-temperature phase structure and mechanical properties of the al -
loys, aiming to provide theoretical foundations and experimental bases 
for the development of lightweight, high-performance RHEAs.

a single BCC phase ( Fig. 1 (a)). The increase in Al content did not alter 
the phase constitution of the alloys, however, the compressed sample 
also exhibits a BCC single-phase structure ( Fig. 1 (b)). This observation 
indicates that high-temperature conditions (at 1100 
◦
C) do not affect the 
phase constitution of the alloys, suggesting that these alloys possess 
good phase stability at high-temperatures (at 1100 
◦
C). Tang et al. [10]
have demonstrated that the presence of Al can modify the lattice con -
stant or structural type, often resulting in the formation of an ordered B2 
phase or a stable BCC phase in conjunction with elements such as Ni, Ti, 
and Nb in high-entropy alloys (HEAs), thereby contributing to structural 
stability at elevated temperatures. In this study, the atomic radius of Al 
is relatively smaller than that of Ti and Zr in the alloy, and it exhibits 
high solubility in Zr and Ti [11] . Consequently, Al can occupy the 
interstitial sites of other elements in the form of an interstitial solid 
solution, leading to significant lattice distortion and a reduction in 
interplanar spacing. This observation indirectly confirms that the addi -
tion of Al can alter the lattice constant of the alloy, thus stabilizing the 
BCC phase. Furthermore, a comparison of the displacement of diffrac -
tion peaks of the alloys before and after compression reveals that the 
diffraction peaks of samples with identical parameters have shifted to -
wards higher angles post-compression. The rightward shift of the 
diffraction peaks indicates a decrease in the interplanar spacing of the 
alloy, suggesting further solid solution of atoms at elevated tempera -
tures, which can enhance strength through solid solution strengthening 
[12] .

the addition of Al can refine the alloys grains, improving the stability of

stronger hindering effect on dislocations, which can lead to early

The EBSD data of as-cast and compressed samples were analyzed 
using inverse pole figure (IPF) and pole figure (PF), with the results 
presented in Fig. 3 . Consistent with previous research [9] , the increase in 
Al content in as-cast samples effectively promotes grain refinement 
( Fig. 3 (a – c)), which is clearly reflected in the average grain size pre -
sented in Table 2 . Zhang et al. [16] demonstrated that as the grain size 
decreases, the distance of dislocation movement shortens, facilitating 
the formation of pile-ups at the grain boundaries. Consequently, a 
greater external force is required to extend to adjacent grains, resulting 
in a macroscopically increased strength [16] . Therefore, Al
0.7 
alloy has 
relatively high strength. Notably, the distribution direction of the grains 
in the compressed samples is perpendicular to the compression direction 
( Fig. 3 (d – f)), showing a flattening and broken morphology, which is a 
typical characteristic of grain compression. According to Table 2 , the 
average grain size at this stage is significantly reduced compared to the 
as-cast sample, indicating that the microstructural changes in the com -
pressed state alloy are related to compression deformation, 
high-strength external forces cause both deformation and fragmentation 
of the grains. Moreover, as the Al content increases, the average size of 
the compressed grains shows a decrease and significant refinement. This 
is attributed to the enormous plastic strain introduced during the 
compression process, which results in a high density of dislocations. 
These dislocations entangle with one another during motion, causing the 
original grain to subdivide into numerous small subgrains, ultimately 
leading to a fragmented and refined crystal morphology [17] .
Furthermore, the PF diagram reveals significant differences in the 
grain orientation characteristics among the various as-cast and com -
pressed samples. It can be seen that the change trend for crystal orien -
tation of the two samples is basically consistent. However, in contrast to 
the as-cast samples, the grains of Al
0.3 
and Al
0.5 
alloys display higher and 
more concentrated contour strength in the {111} directions, indicating 
that both alloys possess stronger preferred orientations in the {100} and 
{111} directions. Additionally, among all samples, the Al
0.7 
alloy dem -
onstrates a distinct orientation in the {110} direction, suggesting a more 
random orientation for this alloy. In summary, increasing Al content is 
advantageous for maintaining the randomness of crystal orientation, 
refinement and homogenization of alloy grains in Mo
0.5
NbTiZrAl
x 
RHEAs at high-temperature (at 1100 ℃ ), thereby effectively promoting 
the improvement of alloy strength.
To sum up, an increase in Al content enhances the strength of the 
alloy while reducing its plasticity. This phenomenon can be primarily 
attributed to the lattice distortion induced by atomic solid solution and 
the hindering effect of grain boundaries on dislocation movement. The 
increased presence of Al promotes a significant solid solution of atoms, 
intensifies the degree of lattice distortion within the alloy, and raises the 
lattice friction stress required for dislocation movement. Consequently, 
this results in an enhancement of the yield strength of the alloy through 
solid solution strengthening. At the same time, The addition of Al pro -
motes grain refinement, the fine grain structures and stable grain 
boundaries jointly suppress the migration and grain coarsening of grain 
boundaries at high-temperatures, prolong the stability of the structure, 
and maintain the strengthening effect of fine grains. However, plastic 
deformation relies on the continuous slip and proliferation of disloca -
tions, but the multitudinous Al solid solution atoms and numerous grain 
boundaries form a dual constraint on dislocation movement. The plenty 
solute atoms are pinned to dislocations, severely limiting their mobility 
and making plastic deformation difficult to coordinate. More impor -
tantly, smaller grains mean multitudinous grain boundaries, leading to 
rapid accumulation of dislocations at these boundaries and resulting in 
highly localized stress concentration. Under compressive stress condi -
tions, these localized stress concentrations are challenging to dissipate 
effectively through dislocation motion, making microcracks more likely 
to develop at grain boundaries or phase boundaries, which lead to 
premature brittle fracture ultimately.
4. Conclusion
This study investigated the high-temperature mechanical properties 
of Mo
0.5
NbTiZrAl
x 
(x = 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) RHEAs obtained by vacuum arc 
melting using Gleeble-3500 thermal simulation testing machine. The 
results indicated that the alloy system maintains a stable BCC single- 
phase structure under high-temperature deformation conditions of 
1100 ℃ , and has excellent high-temperature phase stability. With the 
increase of Al content, the yield strength and fracture strength of the 
alloys significantly improve, among which Al
0.7 
alloy exhibits the 
highest high-temperature strength, but the plasticity slightly decreases, 
showing a typical trade-off effect between strength and ductility. The 
fundamental reason is the lattice distortion caused by atomic solid so -
lution and the hindering effect of grain boundaries on dislocation 
movement. Simultaneously, Al addition, particularly at x = 0.7, signif -
icantly promotes grain refinement and randomization of crystal orien -
tation, contributing to improved microstructural homogeneity and high- 
temperature mechanical properties of the alloys.
