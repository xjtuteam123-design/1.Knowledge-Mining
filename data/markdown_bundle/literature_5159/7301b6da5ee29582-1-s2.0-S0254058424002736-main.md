ABSTRACT 
In this study, small button-shaped ingots were prepared by a non-self-consuming vacuum arc melting method. 
The effect of the content of Nb and Mo elements on the microstructure, mechanical properties and oxidation 
resistance of TiAl alloys were investigated. The as-cast TiAl alloy presented laminar microstructure. When only 
adding Nb element, a small amount of β phase was distributed in the form of dots at the grain boundaries of α
2
/ γ 
lamellar structure. When adding both Mo and Nb, the precipitated β phase started to precipitate in lines inside 
the α
2
/ γ lamellar structure. The microhardness of the alloy increased with the increasing Nb and Mo content. The 
compressive strength of the alloys increased firstly with the increasing Nb content and decreased when the 
content of Nb exceeded 7 wt%. However, with the addition of Mo, the compressive strength of the alloys 
decreased drastically. The optimal compressive strength of the alloy, i.e. 2250 MPa, was obtained with 5 wt% Nb 
and 5 wt% Mo addition. The oxidation resistance of the alloys were enhanced with the addition of Nb and Mo 
elements and the TiAl – 5Nb – 5Mo alloy also showed the best oxidation resistance at 800 
◦
C. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl alloy has low density, high strength, good high-temperature 
oxidation resistance and creep resistance. Therefore, the TiAl alloy is 
widely used in the aerospace and automotive industries involved in 
high-temperature structural components, such as aircraft engine blades 
[ 1 – 4 ]. However, TiAl alloys also have their disadvantages, such as poor 
plasticity at room temperature, low fracture toughness and low strength 
at elevated temperature, which are not conducive to room temperature 
processing and limit their application temperature [ 5 , 6 ]. Among these 
drawbacks, the low plasticity is one of the main problems of TiAl alloy, 
which can be improved by adjusting Al content or adding other alloying 
elements [ 5 – 7 ]. 
It is essential to understand how TiAl alloy ’ s properties are affected 
by Al concentration. Firstly, it determines the liquid alloy ’ s initial phase 
during solidification and the sequence of phase transformation during 
subsequent cooling, which has an important effect on the composition of 
the alloy in the room temperature cast state [ 8 ]. With increasing Al 
content, the strength of the alloy decreases, and the plasticity and 
oxidation resistance of the alloy increase [ 5 , 9 ]. Paul et al. [ 10 ] inves -
tigated the strengthening mechanisms of niobium alloyed γ titanium 
aluminides, which presented that the strength improvement of the alloy 
was caused by the aluminium content decreasing and the Nb plays no 
role in strengthening or work hardening. Brotzu et al. [ 11 ] found that 
obtaining an alloy with high oxidation resistance and good fracture 
toughness, the Al/(Al + Ti) ratio should be no less than 50% and the

The addition of alloying elements to TiAl alloys also have an 
important effect on the microstructure, mechanical properties and 
oxidation resistance. Nb and Mo are both β -phase stabilizing elements, 
and Mo has stronger β -phase stability than Nb [ 13 – 15 ]. Nb solutes both 
in the α

strengthening and increases the volume fractions of γ phase [ 16 ]. 
Meanwhile, the grain refinement caused by the addition of Nb was also 
reported in Ref. [ 16 ]. The above effects caused by Nb addition in TiAl 
alloys contribute to the improvement of strength and plasticity [ 16 ]. 
Besides, it has been shown that Nb plays a positive role in the 
improvement of oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys [ 17 – 20 ]. The addi -
tion of Nb also leads to the generation of β -phase and the refinement of 
grains by changing the solidification path [ 21 ]. Similarly, the addition of 
Mo as a β -phase stabilizing element to the TiAl alloy matrix results in 
solid solution strengthening and refinement of the matrix grains, 
resulting in superior mechanical properties [ 22 ]. Mo has a more positive 
effect on the oxidation resistance of the alloy based on the effect on the 
ion diffusion and solubility of the matrix [ 19 , 23 ]. 
In this study, TiAl alloys were prepared using a vacuum non-self- 
consuming arc melting furnace. The effect of Nb and Mo alloying ele -
ments on the microstructure, mechanical properties and oxidation 
resistance of the alloys were investigated. Meanwhile, the strengthening 
and oxidation mechanisms were discussed and unveiled. 
2. Experimental methods

MSM20-7 (non-self-consuming) under argon atmosphere to obtain 
button-shaped ingots of TiAl alloys with a diameter of 25 mm. The

2.3. Mechanical properties 
In this study, Vickers hardness and room temperature compression

on the polished specimens using a Vickers hardness tester (HXD-1000

3.1. Effect of Nb and Mo elements on the microstructure of the alloys 
XRD patterns of the alloys are shown in Fig. 1 . The main phases of the 
alloys are γ and α
2 
phases. With the increasing content of Nb, both the 
γ -phase peaks and the α
2

increase in the content of γ and α
2 
phases. Based on the diffraction 
theory [ 24 ], the phase content is proportional to the intensity of the 
diffraction peak. With the increasing content of Mo, the intensity of α
2 
peak gradually decreases and the intensity of γ peak gradually increases. 
Therefore, the content of α
2 
phase decreases and the content of γ phase 
gradually increases with the increase of Mo content. 
The addition of Nb and Mo could introduce β phase into the matrix of 
TiAl alloys [ 13 – 15 ]. Noted that the β phase is not detected in the XRD 
patterns in this study, because the volume fraction of β phase in the 
prepared alloys is relatively low. Using the vacuum arc melting tech -
nology and water-cooled copper crucible in the preparation process of 
the alloys make the cooling rate of the alloys quickly, which is the reason 
for the low content of β -phase in the alloys, and a similar phenomenon 
has been reported in Ref. [ 25 ]. 
The microstructure of the alloys is shown in Fig. 2 , which shows 
typical ( α

analysis, Fig. 2 (a) is the SEM image of as-cast Ti-50 at.%Al alloy (i.e. 1# 
specimen), which consists of ( α

Fig. 2 (b – e) are SEM images of 2#-5# specimens, which adding various 
Nb into Ti-50 at.%Al alloys. With Nb addition, some brighter dotted 
precipitation phases appear in the network, which is further identified as 
β phase. Fig. 2 (f – h) are SEM images of 6#-8# specimens with addition of 
different contents of Mo. Some bright irregular lines of precipitation 
phases appear in the ( α

phase. Network like Al-rich γ phase appears brighter dotted precipita -
tion phase. Meanwhile, the content of bright irregular line precipitation 
phase is increasing obviously with the increase of Mo. 
With reference to the TiAl binary phase diagram, the addition of 
β -phase stabilizing elements cause a change in the solidification path of 
TiAl alloys [ 25 ]. The main reason is that Nb and Mo could shift the 
α -transit line to the Al-rich side, while the ( α + γ ) phase field becomes 
narrower [ 27 ]. So the solidification of TiAl binary alloys (Ti = 50%, at 
%) change from α -phase solidification to solidification with precipita -
tion of β phase and the β phase is formed in matrix. 
TiAl alloys tend to undergo segregation during rapid cooling and 
solidification affecting the microstructure of the matrix. When the β 
phase is transformed into α phase, the β phase will discharge Al atoms 
through the phase boundary, and Al atoms will diffuse into the α -phase. 
While Nb and Ti atoms will diffuse into the β -phase, resulting in the 
β -phase being Al-lean, Nb-rich and Ti-rich to produce β -segregation. The 
dotted brighter phase at the boundary of the lamellae colonies is the 
production of β -segregation. When the TiAl alloys contain high Al con -
tent ( > 44 at.%) and low β -phase stable element content, S -segregation 
occurs during the transition from the L -phase to the β -phase. If there are 
excess β -phase stable elements in α phase, the α -phase will transform 
into α
2

results in a lamellar structure composed of α

the β -phase of bright irregular lines is distributed within the lamellar

produced by S -segregation. As shown in Fig. 2 (a – h), the bright dotted

3.2. Effect of Nb and Mo elements on the mechanical properties of the 
alloys 
Fig. 4 shows the microhardness of 1#-8# specimens. The micro -
hardness of the alloys improves with the increase of Nb and Mo content. 
The 8# specimen has the highest microhardness of 381.7 H V. The main 
reason is due to the solid solution strengthening of Nb and Mo. 
Fig. 5 shows the results of compressive stress-strain curves of 1#-8# 
specimens at room temperature. The compressive strength value and 
fracture strain value of the specimens are given in Table 2 . With the 
addition of Nb, ranging from 0 wt% to 5 wt%, the compressive strength 
of 1#-4# specimens gradually increase from 2010 MPa to 2440 MPa, 
and the fracture strain increase from 30.9% to 39.6%. The main reason is 
due to the Hall-Petch strengthening and solid solution strengthening of 
Nb [ 17 ]. Meanwhile, the Nb has a beneficial effect on the plasticity [ 16 ]. 
When addition of 7 wt% Nb, the compressive strength decreases to 2070 
MPa and the fracture strain decreases to 34.6%. Previous investigations 
[ 28 ] shown that adding too much Nb could produce the brittle β
0 
phase, 
and decrease the strength and plasticity of the alloys. So the compressive 
strength and fracture strain of 5# alloy are lower than that of 4# alloy. 
The compressive strength and fracture strain of 6#-8# specimens 
increase firstly and then decrease. The 7# specimen has the highest 
compressive strength and fracture strain of 2250 MPa and 37.1%. 
Compared with 7# specimen, the compressive strength of 8# specimen 
decreases to 1870 MPa and the fracture strain decreases to 28.9%. The 
main reason is due to the brittle β
0 
phase generation with adding too 
much Mo element [ 29 ].

alloys 
Fig. 6 shows the isothermal oxidation weight gain curves of 1#-8# 
specimens at 800

from 0 to 10 h and starts to slow down subsequently. Because the thin

becomes worsen. The oxidation resistance of 1#-5# specimens are 
gradually improved with the increase of Nb. According to previous lit -
eratures [ 12 , 18 ], Nb is beneficial to improve the oxidation resistance of 
TiAl alloys, and the reasons are as follows: (1) when ions with valence 
+ 5 or + 6, such as Nb, are incorporated in the scale, oxygen vacancy 
concentration decreases and the growth of TiO
2 
may be suppressed [ 12 ]. 
(2) the activity of Al can be increased and promotes the diffusion of Al, 
which is very advantageous to form a continuous Al
2
O
3 
oxide film [ 18 ]. 
It is fully demonstrated in the present experimental study that Mo 
also plays a role in improving the oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys. Mo 
can reduce the concentration of O in the matrix, impede the diffusion of 
Ti, and promote the diffusion of Al to induce the generation of contin -
uous Al
2
O
3 
oxide film, resulting in the improvement of oxidation 
resistance [ 19 , 23 ]. Therefore, the oxidation resistance of the alloy is 
further enhanced with the addition of Mo. However, adding too much 
Nb and Mo will generate complex oxides containing alloying elements, 
which will make the oxide film loose and reduce the oxidation resistance 
of the alloys [ 20 ]. So the oxidation resistance of 8# specimen is weak -
ened than that of 7# specimen.

4. Conclusion

consuming arc melting furnace. The effect of Nb and Mo alloying ele -
ments on the microstructure, mechanical properties and oxidation 
resistance of the alloys were investigated. The main results can be 
summarized as follows. 
(1) The microstructure of TiAl alloys were typical lamellar structure. 
The main phases of the alloys were γ , α

the alloys was gradually increased, which was mainly due the 
solid solution strengthening. The TiAl – 5Nb – 7Mo alloy had the 
highest microhardness of 381.7 H V. 
(3) The compressive strength and fracture strain of the alloys were 
improved with addition of Nb and Mo, which was due to the solid
