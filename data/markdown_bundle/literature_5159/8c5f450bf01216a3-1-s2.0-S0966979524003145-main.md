ABSTRACT
Ta-10W alloy has the advantages of excellent high temperature strength, high ductility, good weldability and
excellent corrosion resistance, and is widely used in chemical and aerospace fields. At present, the commerical
tantalum-tungsten alloy products are mainly prepared by vacuum electron beam melting method, but the pro-
cessing costs a lot which is attributable to its cumbersome processing process. Compared with it, although the
tantalum alloy prepared by powder metallurgy is slightly less plastic, it has higher strength. In this work,
Tantalum-tungsten (10 wt% W) mixed powder was used as raw material, and Ta-10W alloy was prepared by
spark plasma sintering (SPS) technology, and high-performance Ta-10W alloy was prepared by changing the
sintering parameters. The test results indicated that the sample had the best mechanical properties at the sin-
tering process 2 (1900
◦
C, 40 MPa, 10 min). Its hardness was 434.38 HV
0.2
, the yield strength of compression at
room temperature was 1370 MPa, and it had a good elongation (44.8 %). Its average friction coefficient was
0.677 and abrasive wear was the dominant wear mechanism. Besides, the Ta-10W alloy prepared by SPS
exhibited brittle fractures, and the presence of brittle phases, W phase, Ta
2
O

and Ta
2
C further deteriorated
sintered Ta-10W ’ s mechanical properties.
1. Introduction
Due to the solid solution strengthening effect, in addition to
obtaining the merits of high melting point and corrosion resistance, the
strength of tantalum-tungsten alloy is higher than that of pure tantalum,
and can be used as a high-temperature structural part. Common models
of tantalum-tungsten alloy are Ta-2.5W, Ta-7.5W, Ta-10W, etc. Studies
have shown that although appropriately increasing the content of W is
expected to further improve the high temperature mechanical proper-
ties, it may face the problem of significantly reducing the plasticity at
room temperature [ 1 – 3 ]. Due to its high density, high strength, high
temperature resistance and stable chemical properties, Ta-10W has been
successfully applied to high-temperature components such as liquid
rocket nozzles with gas temperature above 3300
◦
C and heating ele-
ments in high-temperature vacuum furnaces, and has good application
prospects [ 4 ].
The preparation methods of Ta-10W alloy can be roughly divided
into two types, melting method and powder metallurgy method. The
melting method can be divided into vacuum arc melting and vacuum
electron beam melting. Arc melting is the use of electric energy gener-
ated by electric energy between electrodes or electrodes and melting
materials to melt metals; Electron beam melting is the use of high-speed
electron bombardment of the material emitted by the cathode to
continuously melt the material, so as to realize the melting process. The
preparation of tantalum-tungsten alloy by smelting method often re-
quires multiple smelting to make billets, and the obtained ingot grains
are coarse, and multiple processing deformation and recrystallization
annealing are required for grain refinement in the later stage, hence the
process is complicated. The preparation of Ta-10W alloy by powder
metallurgy method is achieved through tantalum-tungsten alloy powder
sintered by discharge plasma, which has the characteristics of fast
heating rate, uniform heating, low sintering temperature, short sintering
time and high production efficiency [ 5 – 7 ] though it also has disadvan-
tages, such as low thermal efficiency, difficult to control the sintering
environment and high equipment costs. The working principle of SPS is
shown in Fig. 1 , it utilizes the pulse current and axial pressure to prepare

Zhang et al. [ 8 ] designed and prepared tantalum-tungsten alloys
with different tungsten contents by vacuum arc smelting. They found its
grain size decreased when the W content increased. Li et al. [ 4 ] studied
the recrystallization annealing temperature process of Ta-10W alloy
prepared by electron bombardment smelting. Seyyedin et al. [ 9 ] studied
the influence of mechanical alloying time with regard to the micro-
structure, hardness, friction and wear (F & W) and tensile properties of
spark plasma sintered Ta-10W alloy. They found that the fracture
mechanism was changed from facet fracture to dimple fracture by
increasing mechanically alloying time. Nemat-Nasse et al. [ 10 ] studied
the deformation behavior of Ta and Ta-2.5W alloy, and compared the
sensitivity of the rheological stresses of the two to temperature and
strain rate. The results showed that the addition of tungsten could
reduce the rheological stress at high temperatures, and at the same time
reduce the sensitivity to temperature and strain rate. Yan [ 11 ] and
Zhang et al. [ 12 ] studied the mechanical properties of different tantalum
tungsten alloys at room temperature, Yan explored the influence of Y
2
O
3
content on the microstructure and properities of tantalum tungsten alloy
while Zhang probed the effect of tungsten content on quasi-static and
dynamic deformation behavior of alloy.
At present, there are still relatively few reports on the spark plasma
sintering of Tantalum-tungsten alloy. Therefore, in this work, Tantalum-
tungsten powder was used to prepare Ta-10W alloy by spark plasma
sintering (SPS), and the samples with different properties were prepared
by regulated the sintering temperature, sintering pressure and holding
time. What ’ s more, the influence of process parameters on microstruc-
ture was investigated. Its mechanical properties and corresponding
deformation fracture mechanism were studied as well.
2. Experimental
2.1. Materials preparation sintering procedure
The Ta-10W alloy powder (10 wt% W)was developed by Jiujiang
Nonferrous Metal Smelting Co., Ltd., China and the preparation sinter-
ing was performed by SPS technology (Mini SPS Furnace, Shanghai
Chenhua Co., Ltd., China). The prepared Ta-10W alloy powder wrapped
in carbon paper was placed in the graphite mold, a pre-pressure was
given to the mold, and then under the set single-axis pressure, it was
heated to the set sintering temperature and kept to the set time by pulse
current, and the whole process heating rate was 100
◦
C/min, and then
cooled to room temperature with the furnace to take it out. The sintered
specimen was a cylinder with a diameter of 10 mm and a height of 8 mm.
The SPS process parameters are shown in Table 1 below.
2.2. Micro-structure and phase characterization
To observe the microstructure of the sintered Ta-10W specimen and
to understand its phase composition, the sample was sliced into coin-
shaped pieces with a diameter of 10 mm and a thickness of 1 mm, and
then embedded in resin. The specimen was first used with diamond slab
to remove the carbide on the surface, then polished step by step with 600
#, 800 #, 1000 #, 1500 # and 2000 # sandpaper, and finally polished
with a water-soluble polishing paste with a particle size of 0.5 mm to
remove fine scratches. The density of the specimen was measured using
Archimedes drainage. In order to observe the microstructure of the
sample, the polished specimen was corroded with a strong acid solution,
the ratio of H

graphic microscope for observation. In order to understand the phase
composition of the specimen, it is sanded, cleaned, and dried. Then the
phase identification was measured by X-ray diffractometer (XRD, D/max

2.3. Hardness measurement, wear test and compression test
Microhardness tester, friction and wear tester and universal testing
machine were utilized to measure the hardness and mechanical prop-
erties of the sample. For the hardness test, this test was implemented by
a Vickers hardness tester (Falcon 400G2, Innovatest), the load was 200
g, the holding time was 10 s, and 5 positions were randomly chosen in
the middle of the samples ’ upper or lower surface for hardness mea-
surement. The wear resistance of the samples was studied using a
multifunctional friction and wear machine (HSR-2M). The slip speed
was 500 rpm and reciprocating distance was 5 mm. A 5-mm-diameter
GCr15 bearing steel ball was served as the rubbing-pair. All wear tests
were conducted at room temperature (~25
◦
C) with a 5 N load for 30
min. The coefficient of friction was continuously transcribed by the
built-in experimental system of the machine. When the compression test
was carried out with a universal testing machine (Instron 6800), a
compression test was executed on a cylindrical sample with a length of 7
mm and a diameter of 6 mm using a compression rate of 0.1 mm/min.
When the pressure reached 75 KN, the compression procedure ended.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Original mixed powder analysis
The microscopic morphology of the mixed powder is shown in Fig. 2 -
a, tungsten powder shows a multi-angle prism, and tantalum powder
represents ellipsoidal shape, and a certain agglomeration phenomenon
occurs between the two powders. The X-ray diffraction pattern of the
mixed powder is shown in Fig. 2 -b, the four diffraction peaks of tantalum
correspond to the four crystal faces of tantalum (BCC) (110), (220),
(211) and (200), and the three diffraction peaks of tungsten correspond
to the three crystal faces of tungsten (BCC) (110), (211) and (220). We
can find that the particle size of the powder ranges from 10 μ m to 125
μ m and the distribution of different sizes is relatively uniform according
to Fig. 2 -c. The reason may be that the mixed powder undergoes varying
degrees of agglomeration so that large sized powder was detected which
was consistent with Fig. 2 -a.
3.2. Sample density analysis
The density of Ta-10W specimens at different sintering temperatures
measured by Archimedes drainage method was depicted in Fig. 3 . It can
be seen from Fig. 3 -a that as the sintering temperature increased, the
density of the specimen increased, because the increased temperature
promoted the formation and growth of the sintered neck which would
favor the viscous flow of the liquid metal [ 13 ]. When the sintering
temperature was 1800
◦
C, the density of the sample was only 93.9 %,
and as the temperature increased to 1900
◦
C, the density increased
significantly, reaching 97.3 %. Furthermore, the density of the sample
did not increase much, yet the sintering temperature rose to 2000
◦
C.
This could be explained as when the temperature was too high, the
carbon-oxygen reaction would be intensified, and the shrinkage effect
would be hindered, resulting in a decrease in density. Fig. 3 -b showed
the effect of sintering pressure on the density of Ta-10W specimen. It
represented plainly that the sintering pressure had much less affect on
the density of the specimen than what the temperature did. Under the
sintering pressure of 30 MPa, the density of the sample reached 97.1 %,
and the density of the sample could reach about 98 % despite continuing
to increase the sintering pressure. The effect of holding time on the
sintered specimens ’ density was demonstrated in Fig. 3 -c, and it can be
seen from the figure that when the holding time was shortened to 5 min,
the density of the sample was almost unchanged. Nevertheless, it had an
exact reduction under the 3 min holding time, which signified that it
would be difficult to ensure the density of the sample if the holding time
was too short.

obtain better mechanical properties. However, it seemed that there were

properties of the alloy to a certain extent [ 15 ]. Fig. 6 -c indicated that

the graphite mold reacted with it [ 16 ], and the brittle carbide formed
may deteriorate its mechanical properties and became the root cause of
defects ultimately. Actually, the introduction of carbon may be related
to carbon paper, which is placed between sintering powder and mold for
better demolding. Therefore, the content of carbon will reduced by
reducing the use of carbon paper and increasing the amount of sintering
powder. In addition, lowering the sintering temperature appropriately is
also a solution.

3.5.2. Wear test
According to Fig. 8 -a and Table 3 , we could see that the average
coefficient of friction for all samples ranged from 0.6 to 0.7 and did not
change much except sample 1. In general, according to the Archard
equation [ 21 ] V = KH / ρ , where V is the wear volume of material per
unit time, K is the wear coefficient, and H is the average hardness,and ρ
is the stress during friction. This meaned that the greater the hardness,
the better the wear resistance. But sample 1 performed the best in the
wear test. The reason that may explicate was that at 1800
◦
C, tungsten
could not be completely dissolved in tantalum, thereby it was diffusely
distributed in the matrix as the second phase, which could be served as
an effective carrier in the friction process to improve the plastic
deformation ability of the material. Observing the worn surface was
essential for a better understanding of wear mechanism. Fig. 8 -b, c
showed the morphology of sample 2. As shown in Fig. 8 -b, there were
many grooves on the worn surface, which were formed when wear
debris was pressed into the material surface under a normal load and
subsequently scratched. This suggested that the dominant wear mech-
anism of sintered alloy was abrasive wear. At the same time, delami-
nation and few pits were observed in Fig. 8 -c, illustrating that the wear
mechanisms included adhesive wear and fatigue wear [ 22 ].

4. Conclusions
In summary, this study used different sintering processes, and suc-
cessfully prepared dense and good mechanical properties of Ta-10W
alloy by adjusting the three influencing factors of sintering tempera-
ture, sintering pressure and holding time. The conclusions from the
study are as follows.
(1) Among the three influencing factors, the sintering temperature
had the greatest effect on the density of the spark plasma sintered
Ta-10W alloy while the sintering pressure and holding time were
followed by it. When the sintering temperature was 1800
◦
C, the
sample was obviously not dense enough. However, when the
temperature rised to 1900
◦
C, 2000
◦
C, obviously the density was
close to saturation. The adjustment of sintering pressure had little
effect on the density. Too short holding time is not conducive to
improving the density of the sample due to air that cannot escape
timely.
(2) Adjusting the sintering parameters affected the phase composi-
tion of the specimens. Under the condition that the sintering
pressure and holding time remain unchanged, increasing the
sintering temperature was conducive to the gradual solid solution
of tungsten in the tantalum lattice, and the solution strengthening
