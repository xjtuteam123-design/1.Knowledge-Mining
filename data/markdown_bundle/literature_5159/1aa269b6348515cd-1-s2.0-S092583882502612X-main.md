1. Introduction
Complex concentrated alloys (CCAs), also known as high/medium- 
entropy (H/MEAs), multi-principal component/element alloys (MPCA/ 
EAs), represent a new alloying paradigm aimed at overcoming the 
limitations of traditional materials [1 – 7] . Particularly, due to better 
malleability [8,9] , higher strength [10,11] , improved oxidation 
[12 – 14] , wear and corrosion resistance [15 – 17] , CCAs based on re -
fractory metals (RCCAs) appear to be more attractive for potential ap -
plications in harsh environments compared to conventional refractory 
alloys.
Numerous RCCAs have been already designed and analysed [10,18] , 
however the effects of certain elements remain in dispute, and, among 
them, Al is of special interest. Reducing the density and increasing the 
oxidation resistance were the primary reasons for adding Al to RCCAs 
[19 – 24] . While the density reduction due to Al was experimentally 
validated [19,20] , the direct influence of Al on the oxidation resistance 
was not determined in pioneering studies [23] . Instead, surprising 
structural modulations were observed. Early works claimed that Al 
stabilises the body-centred cubic (bcc) phase [19,20,22,23,25] , yet 
further reports revealed the Al-induced B2 ordering [26 – 30] . The B2 
ordering manifests itself in the continuous (matrix) or discrete (particles 
[31 – 36] , domains [37 – 40] , local chemical fluctuations (LCOs) [41,42] ) 
form, depending on the Al content or heat treatment. Along with the B2 
ordering, Al also leads to the formation of different intermetallics, such 
as Zr
x
Al
y 
[28,43 – 48] , Nb
x
Al [44,49] , Mo
x
Al [50] , or transition of Laves 
phase polytypes [51,52] .
All the structural modulations affect the mechanical properties 
greatly. In early studies, where the structure was considered to be single- 
phase bcc solid solution, Al was shown to increase the room-temperature 
yield strength linearly, with the strengthening coefficient of ~ 30 MPa 
per 1 at% of Al [24,53] . The origin for that was solid solution 
strengthening. Later, when the Al-induced B2 ordering was elucidated 
for different alloy systems, the similar strengthening coefficients were

the Al-induced B2 ordering, at comparative grain sizes, controls the

In regards to oxidation resistance, Al was initially added to RCCAs to 
create a continuous Al
2
O
3 
scale. Yet, the alumina scale is extremely rare 
to appear [14,23,69] . Still, the effect of Al on the oxidation resistance is 
mostly positive: Al reduces the mass gain and eliminates the oxide scale 
spallation [54,70 – 76] . In many cases, these changes occur due to the 
formation of a mixture of complex oxides, including Al-rich ones, which 
are suggested to create barriers against oxidation [13,14,71] . However, 
the efficiency of Al additions depends on the alloy composition. For 
instance, 4.8 at% of Al is found to be sufficient to depress the oxide scale 
spallation in the Al-Hf-Nb-Ta-Ti-Zr system [77] , but if an alloy contains 
reactive elements, such as V, even an equiatomic or close to that Al 
content does not guarantee the best oxidation resistance [78] . Moreover, 
at high amounts of Al, the presence of Al-rich intermetallics can lead to 
stress-induced spallation or complete disintegration of the material 
[79] .
Despite the diversity and abundance of the data obtained, it is still 
difficult to establish the composition-structure-property relationships in 
Al-containing RCCAs. Most studies focus on the narrow concentration 
ranges of Al or choose the alloys systems, in which the formation of the 
bcc/B2 structure is only observed [37,40,53 – 55,57,61] . While in those 
works, where Al-containing intermetallics are reported [45,51,52,65, 
73] , analysis of their homogeneity ranges is absent. As related to me -
chanical performance, there is limited information on how the 
strengthening ability of Al depends on the phase composition and tem -
perature [47] . Lastly, it is still not well understood whether Al offers a 
better protection than Cr - another desirable element for improving the 
oxidation resistance of RCCAs [14,80] .
This study pursued two interconnected goals: (i) to address the issues 
mentioned above related to Al effects and (ii) to complete our survey on 
the influence of different elements, such as Cr, Ta, Mo, and V, on the 
structure, mechanical properties, and oxidation behaviour of the well- 
known single-phase bcc NbTiZr alloy [81,82] . To be consistent, we 
chose the Al content similar to our previous works and focused on the 
comparison of how Al, Cr, Ta, Mo, and V affect the strength and 
oxidation resistance of the NbTiZr alloy. The results of this study are 
believed to be useful guidelines to design new RCCAs with improved 
properties.

4. Discussion
4.1. Effect of Al alloying on the structure
We found a dependence between the Al content and phase 
transformations it induced when added to the single-phase bcc NbTiZr 
alloy. At 10 and 25 at% of Al, the B2 phase was formed. With the in -
crease in the Al concentration to 30 and 35 at%, the additional Zr
5
Al
3
- 
type phase appeared. Lastly, when the Al content reached 40 at%, two 
new phases, namely C14 Laves and σ , emerged, while the Zr
5
Al
3
-type 
phase disappeared.
Based on these observations, some Al-related trends can be revealed. 
Along with the bcc phase, the B2 phase demonstrates the largest ho -
mogeneity range, lasting from 10 to 40 at% of Al. If we take into account 
our previous data [37] , this interval starts already from 5 at% of Al. Note 
that, up to 35 at% of Al, the bcc/B2 phase structure dominates, with the 
volume fraction of ~98 %, while these phases become minor abruptly at 
40 at% of Al. In turn, the Zr
5
Al
3
-type phase exists in a much narrower 
interval - from 30 to 35 at% of Al, if no heat treatment is applied [87, 
88] .

Differences in the strengthening effectivenesses of Al, Cr, Ta, Mo, and

is added, the strength of the NbTiZr alloy has only one source - solid

and relatively small a = 0.317 nm. Meanwhile, moderate strengthening 
provided by Ta ( G = 69 GPa and a = 0.331 nm) or V ( G = 47 GPa and a 
= 0.299 nm) is due to domination of only one contribution – shear 
modulus mismatch or lattice distortions, respectively. In the case of Al or 
Cr additions, the NbTiZr alloy benefits from synergistic solid solution 
and secondary phase strengthening, resulting in the highest strength -

4.3. Effect of Al alloying on the oxidation resistance
Additions of Al to the NbTiZr alloy had a notable influence on the 
oxidation behaviour. From the mass gain curves and overall views of the 
oxidised specimens, it could be found that Al: (i) did not affect the 
oxidation mechanism; (ii) reduced oxidation rate constant and the mass 
gain; (iii) suppressed the spallation/disintegration and edge swelling 
starting from 25 at%.

Laves phase networks was observed. The experimental Al-induced phase 
transformations were in a reasonable agreement with the CALPHAD
