abstract
12 wt.% Cr-ODS ferritic alloys were fabricated by mechanical alloying combined with hipping under dif-
ferent sintering pressure and then forging deformation work. The microstructures of the hipped and
forged samples after annealing at different temperatures were investigated and compared. No obvious
recrystallization occurred even at an annealing temperature of 1100 /C176C, both for as-hipped and as-forged
materials. When hipping under high pressure (200 MPa), the ODS material is very dense, and almost no
cavities could be found after annealing at high temperature. While when hipping under low pressure
(70 MPa), obvious pores could be found for this material after forging, the pores assembled and formed
big cavities up to /C2410
lm after annealing at temperature of 1300 /C176C.

1. Introduction
Oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) steels based on a Fe–Cr
ferritic or martensitic matrix are considered as one of the most
promising high temperature structural materials for future nuclear
ﬁssion and fusion applications [1–4]. The ODS steels show excel-
lent high temperature mechanical properties and neutron irradia-
tion resistance ability due to the high number density of nano sized
particles [5,6] or/and nanoclusters [7–10] dispersed in the ODS
steel matrix. The typical process for producing ODS steels is by
mechanical alloying (MA) combined with consolidation, deforma-
tion work and heat treatment. Hot isostatic pressing (HIP) is re-
garded as one of the most promising consolidation process for
nearly end-shaped structures. HIP also has the additional advan-
tage that no anisotropy is induced as in the case of hot extrusion
process. Although most ODS alloys are consolidated either by hip-
ping or hot extrusion, which are processes believed to be able to
attain full density, they still contain a relatively high fraction of
porosities after annealing [11]. These porosities are not only due
to the MA process, but also may due to the consolidation/deforma-
tion parameters.
In this work, a 12 wt.% Cr-ODS ferritic alloy powders were pre-
pared by mechanical alloying. The as-received MA powders were
then consolidated by hipping under different pressure combined
with forging process. The fabricated ODS alloys were annealed at
different temperatures. The microstructure and micro-hardness
of these materials were examined and compared.
2. Experimental
2.1. Material
The 12 wt.% Cr-ODS ferritic alloys investigated in this study
were prepared from nitrogen gas atomized pre-alloyed powders
with an average particle size of 45
lm. The pre-alloyed powders
were mechanical alloyed with 0.35 wt.% Y 2O3 with an average par-
ticles size of 30 nm and 0.5 wt.% Ti with a particles size less than
74 lm. The chemical composition (in wt.%) of the milled powders
is Fe–0.11C–0.16Si–0.51Mn–11.2Cr–1.21W–0.5Ti–0.35Y2O3.
2.2. Experimental methods
The mechanical alloying was conducted by a high-energy plan-
etary ball mill in an argon gas atmosphere. A ball-to-powder mass
ratio of 10:1 and a rotational speed of 380 rpm, milling time for
30 h were adopted for performing this study.
After ball milling, the powders were transferred to a mild steel
can, then degassed in a vacuum of /C243 Pa at 450 /C176C for 15 h, and
sealed. The MA powders were consolidated by hipping the sealed
cans for 3 h at 1150 /C176C under pressure of 200 MPa (designated as
sample of ODS-200) or 70 MPa (designated as sample of ODS-70).
After hipping, deformation work was performed only on the sam-
ple of ODS-70 by forging at 850–1150 /C176C with 70% deformation le-
vel (designated as sample of ODS-70F). All samples were annealed
at 1100 /C176C or 1300 /C176C for 2 h in vacuum.
A SUPRA 55 scanning electron microscope (SEM) and JEOL JEM-
2100 TEM with an accelerating voltage of 200 kV both furnished
with X-ray energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS) analysers were
used for chemical analyses and electron microscope examinations.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure observation
Fig. 1 shows the TEM morphologies of the ODS-200 and ODS-
70 samples. Very ﬁne Y-, Ti-oxide particles with a homogeneous
distribution could be found for the ODS-200 sample, as shown
in Fig. 1 a. The particle sizes are less than 20 nm. While for the
ODS-70 sample, coarse particles with a size up to 200 nm were
observed, as shown in Fig. 1 b. The result of EDS indicated that
these coarse particles were Al-, N-, O-enriched particles. Alumi-
num is not in the designed nominal compositions, which may
be introduced due to the contamination of the powders during
ball milling.
Fig. 2 shows the microstructures of the ODS-70F sample (the
forged sample of 70 MPa HIPed specimen) and ODS-70F samples
after annealing at different temperatures. Two types of grains were
found for the ODS-70F samples. The coarse grains with 1–2
lm
aggregated aligned with the forging direction in the matrix and
the equiaxed ﬁne grains with the size of /C24200 nm distributed
around the coarse grains. EDS indicated that the equiaxed ﬁne
grains were Ti-enriched particles. After annealing at temperature
of 1100 /C176C very slight growth occurred on the coarse grains, and
no grain growth occurred for the ﬁne grains. When annealing at
1300 /C176C, obvious grain growth occurred on the coarse grains, but
no grain growth occurred for the ﬁne grains.
3.2. Development of cavities during annealing test
Fig. 3 shows the evolution of cavities in the ODS-200, ODS-70
and ODS-70F samples after annealing tests. For the ODS-200 sam-
ple, a rather dense microstructure could be found, as shown in
Fig. 3 a. After annealing at 1100 /C176C, only a few equiaxed cavities
with size about 2 lm were found, as shown in Fig. 3b. The cavities
disappeared when annealing temperature increased to 1300 /C176C, as
shown in Fig. 3 c, but a Cr-enriched phase appeared in the matrix.
For the ODS-70 sample, also a rather dense microstructure was
found, as shown in Fig. 3 d. After annealing at 1100 /C176C, only a
few equiaxed cavities with size about 2 lm were found, as shown
in Fig. 3 e. But big cavities appeared when the annealing tempera-
ture increased to 1300 /C176C and their sizes are about 10 lm. This
indicated that although dense microstructure was observed by
SEM for the ODS-70 sample, micro-pores may exist in the sample
due to the low sintering pressure. For the ODS-70F sample, it can
be seen that, a few cavities with size about 2 lm appeared in the
matrix after forging work on the ODS-70 sample, as shown in

These interesting phenomena mentioned above might be due to
the precipitates and the procession conditions. Very ﬁne nano Y–
Ti-oxide particles formed in the ODS-200 sample. These particles
exert a pinning force on grain boundaries to retard boundary
migration, and thus inhibit the aggregation of small pores. While
the precipitates in the ODS-70 sample are much larger than that
in the ODS-200 sample, due to the contamination during ball mill-
ing. This make the grain boundary migration of the ODS-70 sam-
ples occurred more easily than that of ODS-200 sample, which
would accelerate the cavity aggregation. On the other hand, the
sintering pressure of the ODS-70 sample is far less than that of
the ODS-200 sample, which indicates that the micro-porosity of
the ODS-70 sample should be higher than that of the ODS-200
sample. This also explain why cavities could be observed by SEM
for ODS-70 after forging work. The evolution of these micro-poros-
ities are very sensitive to annealing temperature. When annealing
at temperature of 1300 /C176C, these micro-porosities would be easy to
assemble to large cavities.
Fig. 4 shows the micro-hardness of the three ODS alloy samples
as a function of annealing temperatures. The results are quite
accordance with Figs. 2 and 3 . The micro-hardness are not only re-
lated to the recrystallization of the material but also related to the
cavities evolution after annealing tests. Before annealing tests, all
the ODS alloy samples have rather high hardness. After annealing
at 1100 /C176C, the hardness of the ODS-200 and ODS-70 samples only
show a slight drop, which indicates that almost no recrystallization
occurred at this temperature. While the hardness of the ODS-70F
samples decreased obviously, which should be due to the forma-
tion of pores, as seen in Fig. 3 h. After annealing at 1300 /C176C, the
hardness of all the three ODS alloy samples decreased signiﬁcantly,
less than two-thirds of the original hardness, due to the recrystal-
lization at this temperature. While the ODS-70 sample has the low-
est hardness due to the formation of large cavities, as seen in
Fig. 3 f.

4. Conclusions
The effect of annealing temperatures and forging process on the
porosity evaluation of ODS ferritic alloys fabricated by the process
of MA combined with hipping has been investigated. The results
indicate that the sintering pressure during HIP is a critical factor
for the cavities formation and evaluation after annealing tests.
The specimens hipped under pressure of 200 MPa possess a few
cavities even after annealing at 1300 /C176C, while for the specimen
hipped under pressure of 70 MPa, cavities formed after forging.
The cavities aggregated and linked up obviously when the anneal-
ing temperature increased to 1300 /C176C.
