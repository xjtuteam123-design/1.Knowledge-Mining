An ion-plasma method for depositing alloy diffusion aluminide coatings on high-temperature alloys, which
favorably differs from the traditional processes, is considered. The special features of formation of ion-plasma
diffusion coatings on the surface of high-temperature alloys in single-stage and double-stage processes are
considered. The process of formation of such coatings and their effect on the long-term high-temperature
strength of nickel-base alloys is studied. Examples of advantages of the new method are presented.
INTRODUCTION
Diffusion aluminide coatings are very widely used for
protecting the external surface of turbine blades fabricated
from high-temperature alloys from high-temperature oxida-
tion. The high protective properties of these coatings are de-
termined by the properties of NiAl ( /c98-phase) nickel alu -
minide that constitutes the base of the external layer of the
coating. By enriching this layer with elements raising its re -
fractoriness, strength, and thermal stability at 1050 – 1100°C
the service life of protective diffusion coatings on turbine
blades can be substantially increased.
VIAM specialists have developed a new method for
forming alloyed diffusion aluminide coatings (ADAC) on
high-temperature alloys, which is based on a high-energy
vacuum plasma process. As compared to conventional calo -
rizing techniques, i.e., powder, slip, and gas circulation ones
[1 – 7], this method has the following advantages: ( 1 ) high
accuracy of formation of the coating with respect to the
thickness, the chemical and phase compositions, and the in -
tegral content of aluminum and alloying elements in the
coating and (2 ) possibility of wide-range control of the con -
tent of aluminum and other alloying elements in the coating,
which is impossible in diffusion formation of coatings by the
known methods.
An ion-plasma diffusion coating can be formed on the
surface of high-temperature alloys by single- and double-
stage processes.
In the single-stage process the coating is formed in the
plasma of an aluminum alloy bearing elements that alloy the
coating and during the subsequent vacuum heat treatment of
the coated part. The process is characterized by the following
accuracy of reproduction of the parameters of the coating:
/c1635% with respect to the thickness and /c1635% with respect to
the content of aluminum and alloying elements in the coat -
ing. Such a process provides ADAC with a thickness of
1 0–8 0 /c109m with 16 – 26%
2 aluminum and makes it possible
to reproduce and precisely shape these layers on the func -
tional surface of GTE blades, which cannot be provided by
any of the known methods of deposition of such coatings. In
addition to the mentioned advantages the single-stage pro -
cess of formation of ADAC has a disadvantage, i.e., a limited
possibility of alloying with elements enhancing the protec -
tive properties of the coating.

METHODS OF STUDY
We formed coatings in a MAP-1M ion-plasma installa -
tion using a commercial method [8]. The surface of the spe -
cimens of high-temperature alloys for depositing the coat -
ings was prepared by degreasing them in spirit and acetone,
sandblasting with dry electrocorundum grains 40 – 60 /c109mi n
size, and finishing treatment by ion etching in the plasma of a
vacuum arc under negative electrical potential of the sub -
strate of 250 – 300 V for 3 – 5 min directly in the working
chamber of the MAP-1M installation before forming the
coating. Single-stage ADAC on specimens and blades fabri -
cated from high-temperature alloys were formed in the
plasma of an aluminum alloy. Double-stage ADAC were ob-
tained in the following manner: (I) deposition of a condensed
layer from a nickel-base alloy (for example, from alloy
SDP-2 of the Ni – Cr – Al – Y system) onto the surface of
specimens and blades at a bias voltage of 10 – 20 V and vac-
uum arc current of 700 A and (II) subsequent formation of a
diffusion layer in the plasma of aluminum alloy with parame-
ters chosen for stage I. The final formation of the coating oc-
curred in the process of vacuum annealing with parameters
specially chosen for each high-temperature alloy (the tem -
perature and the hold time).
The coatings were studied under a “Versamet-2”
metallographic microscope and DRON-3M, DRON-4, and
DRON-UM1 x-ray diffractometers. The phase analysis was
performed with the help of the “PHAN” x-ray phase bank.
For the phase analysis panoramic micrographs were taken in
the 20 – 85° angle range and micrographs and micrograms
were taken in the most informative range of 10 – 45° at
which the thickness of the studied layer varied from 5 to
17 /c109m. The chemical composition and the microstructure of
the coatings were studied by methods of microscopic x-ray
spectral analysis and scanning electron microscopy using a
“Superprobe” apparatus (JCMA-733, JEOL, Japan). The
range of elements detected by the microanalyzer varied from
B( Z =5 )t oU( Z = 92). The area of analysis was 1 /c109m
2 and
the depth was about 1 /c109m. The characteristics of the
long-term strength were determined according to the GOST
10145 standard, and the fatigue resistance was determined by
the GOST 25.502 standard.

Ion-plasma diffusion coatings were formed due to the
diffusion of aluminum and alloying elements into the surface
of the alloy. The external layer of the /c98-phase coating con -
tained a considerable amount of refractory elements that
lowered the protective properties of the coating but raised its
high-temperature strength, which is required from GTE
blade coatings because it makes it possible to increase the re-
sistance of the ADAC to the formation of thermal fatigue
cracks. The lowering of the protective properties of the coat -
ing due to the presence of refractory elements in the external
layer was compensated by alloying with elements contained
in the aluminum alloy (for example, Si and Y , Cr and Y , or Si
and B). The new process doubled the service life of the tur -
bine blades relative to that of blades with traditional diffu -
sion coatings deposited by the method of powder calorizing
(according to the results of tests of ZhS6U blades with coat -
ings). On the whole, the ion-plasma process of formation of
ADAC is similar to the process occurring in the slip method
of deposition of diffusion coatings, where the diffusion layer
on the base of the NiAl intermetallic system forms through a
liquid phase as a result of chemical interaction between alu -
minum and the surface layer of the high-temperature alloy
and subsequent diffusion interaction between the coating and
the substrate.

The external layer of the coating is divided into two
zones. The surface zone bears inclusions of an /c97-solid solu-
tion based on Cr, Mo, W and other elements, which is a con-
sequence of predominant diffusion of aluminum from the
surface into the depth of the alloy and of the change in the
dissolution of the alloying elements in the intermetallic com-
pound relative to their dissolution in the high-temperature al-
loy. The internal zone of the external coating consists of a
/c98-phase, which is free of segregations of excess phases, and
contains refractory elements in the solid solution.
The internal layer of the coating has a heterophase struc-
ture. Phases based on refractory elements, i.e., carbides,
/c109-phase, and other compounds, segregate on the intermetal -
lic matrix of this layer. The content of refractory elements in
the internal layer of the coating is higher than in the substrate
material. The internal layer of the coating forms due to nickel
diffusion from the surface layers of the high-temperature al -
loy towards aluminum.
There is no principal difference in the structure of coat -
ings on alloys ZhS6U, VZhL12U, and ZhS26. However, the
amount of the segregated excess phase in the surface layer of
the coating on alloy ZhS26 is lower than on alloys ZhS6U
and VZhL12U due to the difference in their alloying systems.
The single-stage process makes it possible to alloy the
coating within the ranges of solubility of the alloying ele -
ments in the /c98-phase at the temperature of final formation of
the coating. In the coating obtained on alloys ZhS6U
and VZhU12U by the standard heat treatment mode
(1010 /c17710°C) the content of Si, Cr, Y(B) does not exceed
0.8 – 1.2%, 1 – 1.5%, and 0.1 – 0.3% respectively. For this
reason, the possibility of alloying of single-stage ion-plasma
diffusion coatings by way of introduction of alloying ele -
ments into the aluminum alloy is limited.
For high-temperature alloys with a low content of chro -
mium like ZhS26VNK chromium can be introduced addi -
tionally from the aluminum alloy into the external layer of
the diffusion coating.

The ion-plasma method of forming diffusion ADAC pro-
vides a high (about 100%) reproducibility of the parameters
(thickness and composition) of the deposited coatings and
makes it possible to shape the diffusion layers to the requisite
thickness at different regions of a blade and to deposit coat -
ings on ready blades, which simplifies the production of
GTE blades and permits purposeful alloying of the aluminide
coating with elements increasing its en -
durance and resistance to various kinds
of high-temperature gas corrosion [3].
Today, single-stage ion-plasma
ADAC are widely used in aircraft enter -
prises instead of coatings produced ear -
lier by powder or slip calorizing. The
method gives good results in the repair
of diffusion coatings on turbine blades.
Ion-plasma coatings increase the service
life of the blades by a factor of 1.5 – 2 as
compared to slip coatings commonly
used in the repair of turbine blades.
Double- and multistage ion-plasma
diffusion coatings. The condensed layer
deposited in the first stage of the deposi -
tion of double-stage ADAC provides
complex alloying of the diffusion coating
with elements that enhance the protective properties of the
coating (Cr, Y , Si, Co, Pt, Ta, etc.) and elements simulta -
neously increasing its high-temperature strength and heat re -
sistance in contact with refractory alloys (Ta, Re, W, etc.).
The presence of a condensed layer on the surface of a
high-temperature alloy in the process of formation of ADAC
prevents uncontrolled penetration of alloying refractory ele-
ments into the external layer of the coating. At the same
time, this makes it possible to eliminate undesirable “de-
fects” in the surface layer of the substrate (the high-tempera-
ture alloy), for example, carbides, the presence of which
causes structural inhomogeneity of the single-stage ADAC
on the surface of the protected part.
Formation of single- and double-stage ADAC is possible
if the thickness of the condensed layer is less than that of the
diffusion layer, which allows us to classify the coating as a
whole as a diffusion one. Then the structure of the formed
surface layer is typical for a diffusion coating with a devel -
oped internal transition layer. Otherwise, the structure
formed on the surface of the high-temperature alloys has a
pattern typical for high-temperature condensation-diffusion
coatings [8]. The composition of the condensed layer, its

Double- and multistage ion-plasma ADAC noticeably
differ from single-stage coatings due to their alloying. For
comparison, we present in Table 4 the elements contained in
some ADAC obtained by single-stage and multistage meth -
ods. It can be seen that the double-stage and multistage
ADAC virtually do not have refractory elements, which alloy
the high-temperature alloys, in the external zone but have
considerable amounts of Cr and other elements (Ta, Pt) pres-
ent in the condensed layer. The proportion of alloying ele -
ments in the layers of double-stage ADAC is presented in
Table 5.
Double-stage ADAC provide high heat resistance of
high-temperature alloys at the operational temperature
(Fig. 6), raise their high-temperature strength by 10 – 15%
(Table 6), and virtually do not affect the fatigue strength.
It should also be noted that the new method for deposit -
ing ion-plasma diffusion coatings makes its possible to per -
form their purposeful alloying in a wide range not only with
elements that improve the protective properties of the coat -
ing and its thermal stability in contact with the high-tempera-
ture alloy, but also with elements that reinforce the /c98-phase,
which is important for blades of high-heat GTE.
Double-stage ADAC have higher protective properties
than single-stage ones and have become the basic variant for
coating GTE blades operating at a temperature of up to
1100°C.
Thus, the high protective properties of single-stage
ion-plasma diffusion coatings, the high accuracy of their for-
mation, and the simplicity of the process explain their wide
use in industry for the protection of aircraft GTE from
high-temperature oxidation and for repair of worn blades.
Complex and purposeful alloying of the diffusion aluminide
layer on high-temperature alloys opens wide possibilities for
the use of these layers as heat-resistant protective coatings of
blades of aircraft and industrial gas turbines. The suggested
method for depositing complexly alloyed diffusion coatings
makes it possible to widely vary the chemical composition of
the coatings without changing the matrix heat-resistant phase
of the coating, i.e., alloyed nickel monoaluminide, which
provides coatings fully meeting the operational conditions of
GTE blades of various types. We expect that the new alloyed
ion-plasma coatings will successfully compete with the now
used commercial domestic and foreign coatings for turbine
blades and in some cases will considerably prolong (more
than double) their service life.
CONCLUSIONS
1. Ion-plasma coatings can be formed on high-tempera -
ture alloys by single-stage and multistage processes. The
multistage process provides complex alloying of diffusion
layers and substantially improves the service characteristics
of such coatings relative to those of coatings formed by tradi-
tional methods.
