The volume fraction and morphology of the TCP-phase formed in two kinds of Ni-
Mo-Cr-Re superalloys under diﬀerent heat treatment conditions were investigated in
this paper. In Re-5% alloy, with increasing of the heat treatment temperature and
prolonging the holding time, the volume fraction of TCP-phase decreased and the
TCP-phase size increased. At relatively lower temperature, the TCP-phase prefers to
present in the dendrite cores. In Re-10% alloy, the volume fraction and size have the
same change tendency as that of in Re-5% alloy, but the morphology will change from
needle-like and block-like to sphere when the temperature increases. The TCP-phases
formed in these two Ni-Mo-Cr-Re alloys are σ and P phase.
KEY WORDS Heat treatment; TCP-phase; Superalloy
1 Introduction
Alloys based on the Ni-Mo-Cr ternary system constitute a very important class of super-
alloys utilizing long range order as a primary mechanism for providing room and elevated
temperature strength. In this class HAYNES 242 alloy is one of age-hardenable superalloy
that derives its good mechanical properties from a ﬁne dispersion of metastable Ni2(Mo,Cr)
ordered body centered orthorhombic (Pt2Mo-type) precipitates in fcc matrix[1−3]. It con-
tains nominally 25% molybdenum and 8% chromium (in wt pct), with nickel as balance.
HAYNES 242 alloy is typically used for gas turbine engine seal and containment rings,
duct segments, casings, fasteners, rocket nozzles and pumps.
In order to further increase the alloy′s strength and modulus, two kinds of new alloys
based on Haynes 242 were developed by adding of refractory element Re in place of part
Ni in the same weight percentage.
As we known, the nickel-based superalloys have the liability to create brittle phases of
the topologically close-packed (TCP-), such as σ, μ, δ, β and P

at the temperature of 1123 K, 1273 K and 1473 K, butμ phase only can be found when the

fore, they shown a greater tendency to form TCP-phases than the ﬁrst generation alloys
due to the presence of rhenium
[13]. The main purpose of this paper was to understand
the eﬀects of heat treatment and Re content of the new superalloys on the formation of
TCP-phase and its morphology change.

3 Results

precipitation region. This is an explanation why there are no reports about the TCP-phases

of part Ni, the microstructures of the modiﬁed alloys are changed signiﬁcantly when they
are exposed at the temperature between 1273 K to 1523 K for a period of time, the results
demonstrate clearly in Fig.1, Fig.2 and Fig.4, Fig.5. Re addition in the Haynes 242 (Ni-
25Mo-8Cr) alloy change the solubility of the TCP-phase, as a result the composition of two
new Ni-Mo-Cr-Re alloys will move to the TCP-phases precipitation region in the Cr-Ni-Mo
ternary system equilibrium diagram. The TCP-phases precipitated in the new alloys are
so quick and intensive, they are not consistent with other reports that TCP-phases will
form after long time exposure at high temperature
[16], implying that Re addition in the
Ni-Cr-Mo alloy not only change the TCP-phases solubility but also change the kinetics of
TCP-phases precipitation.
At the same temperature, the cooling methods have no eﬀect on the TCP-phases size,
volume fraction and morphology. These results can be draw out from the fact that when
the samples are heated at 1273 K for 2 h, 3 h and 24 h using the water quench, air cooling
and furnace cooling, the volume fraction, size and morphology of the TCP-phases are
not changed under the diﬀerent methods. However, the exposure time aﬀects the TCP-
phases volume fraction and has slight eﬀect on the size of the phases. With prolonging the
exposure time the smaller size of TCP-phases will accumulate together to form coarse one,
and due to the elements diﬀusion some phases are dissolved (Fig.1a and Fig.2b).
The EDS results show that the TCP-phases formed in these alloys are rich of Re and Mo
elements (Fig.7), this is consistent with the result of previous work
[17]. The above analysis
reveals that the TCP-phases in Re-5% alloy are mainly precipitated in the dendrite cores at
relatively lower temperature. Due to the higher melting point of Re and Mo, they mainly
concentrate in the dendrite cores which are solidiﬁed at ﬁrst during the solidiﬁcation and
thus the inter-dendrite region deﬁcient in these kinds of elements. As the alloy heat treated
at relatively lower temperatures the elements distribution have the beneﬁts to precipitate
the TCP phases in the dendrite cores. At relatively lower temperature such as 1273 K,
the elements have no suﬃcient kinetic energy to diﬀuse, the TCP-phases will remain in
the shape, size and volume fraction they formed at ﬁrst even prolong the exposure time to
24 h. With increasing the temperature the elements can diﬀuse in long distance, part of
instable TCP phases will dissolve and remain those in stable shape of sphere. This could

The hardness test results demonstrate that the TCP-phases precipitated in the modiﬁed

have the eﬀects of increasing the alloy ultimate tensile strength and yield strength, further
work need to be done to conﬁrm the results.
5 Conclusions
Examinations of the microstructures of the samples exposed at diﬀerent temperatures
for diﬀerent time periods indicate that the size, volume fraction and morphology of the
TCP-phases formed during these expose change signiﬁcantly with the temperature and
time.
(1) In Re-5% alloy most of the TCP-phases are in the shape of sphere and only few
of them are needle-like. The volume fraction of TCP-phases decreases with increasing the
temperature. The size of the sphere TCP-phases increase with increasing the temperature.
The TCP-phases distribute mainly in the dendrite core at relatively lower temperature.
(2) In Re-10% alloy the morphology of the TCP-phases is diﬀerent with that of in Re-
5% alloy, the TCP-phases have no preference region and the morphology at the relatively
lower temperature is needle-like. With temperature increasing the morphology tends to
be sphere. The volume fraction of TCP-phases decreases with increasing the temperature
and the size increase with increasing the temperature.
(3) TCP-phase precipitated in Re-5% alloy isσ and in Re-10% alloy areσ and P phases.
