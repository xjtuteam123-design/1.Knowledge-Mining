ABSTRACT
As oil and gas exploration advances into deeper formations, downhole instruments and drilling components are 
subjected to more complex loading conditions, demanding superior mechanical properties of corrosion-resistant 
alloys. In this study, regression models for 0.2 % offset yield strength (R
p0.2
) and elongation was developed using 
a three-factor, three-level orthogonal experimental design combined with the Box-Behnken Response Surface 
Methodology (RSM). High-throughput screening was then employed to identify ultra-high-strength corrosion- 
resistant alloys with yield strength exceeding 1300 MPa. The results indicate that the RSM models demonstrate 
high accuracy and reliability. The contents of Nb and Ti significantly influenced the alloy ’ s strength and elon -
gation, with Nb having the most pronounced effect. In contrast, the Mo content has a minimal impact on these 
properties. Through high-throughput screening, 1738 potential alloy compositions were identified from 70356 
candidates, with R
p0.2 
greater than 1300 MPa and elongation greater than 12 %, providing valuable insights for 
the design of ultra-high-strength corrosion-resistant alloys.
1. Introduction
Nickel-based corrosion resistant alloys have excellent mechanical 
properties and outstanding corrosion resistance in the field of oil and gas 
drilling and production [1 – 4] . After decades of research and develop -
ment, a variety of nickel-based alloy systems have been derived, such as 
Inconel, Incoloy and other commercial nickel-based corrosion resistant 
alloys [5,6] . As oil and gas exploration advances into deeper and more 
challenging environments, downhole drilling and production compo -
nents are exposed to increasingly complex and demanding service con -
ditions. These include not only elevated temperatures and pressures, but 
also multifaceted mechanical loads such as tension, compression, 
bending, and torsion — often acting simultaneously and cyclically. As a 
result, the mechanical performance requirements for the materials used 
in these components, particularly with respect to strength, ductility, and 
fatigue resistance, have become significantly more stringent. This places 
a critical emphasis on the development of high-performance alloys 
capable of maintaining structural integrity and reliability under such 
harsh conditions.
The exceptional performance of nickel-based corrosion-resistant 
alloys results from the synergistic effects of their constituent alloying 
elements. The incorporation of these elements alters the alloy ’ s micro -
structure, thereby influencing its properties [7,8] . The key alloying el -
ements commonly employed in nickel-based corrosion resistant alloys 
are Cr, Ti, Mo, Nb, and Al [9] . The presence of these elements signifi -
cantly affects both the corrosion resistance and mechanical properties of 
the alloys [10 – 13] . Specifically, the addition of Cr and Mo enhances 
pitting corrosion resistance to pitting corrosion [14] and increases its 
hardness. Al, Ti, and Nb are the primary elements responsible for the 
formation of γ’ and γ " phases, which notably influence the strength and 
ductility of the material [15 – 17] . Both phases act as strengthening 
mechanisms in nickel-based alloys, with γ " providing a considerably 
greater strengthening effect than γ’ [18 – 20] . Inconel 718 is a typical 
nickel-based corrosion resistant alloy that is mainly reinforced by γ " and 
assisted by γ’ [21,22] . However, the relationship between the content of 
alloying elements and the alloy ’ s strength and ductility is highly com -
plex, intricately dependent on the specific composition and proportions 
of the elements. The development of high-strength nickel-based corro -
sion resistant alloys remains challenging, as the interactions between 
alloying elements impose constraints on composition design.

In recent years, significant efforts have been made to enhance the 
mechanical properties of nickel-based corrosion-resistant alloys through 
various composition optimizations. Some studies have focused on the 
control of the Al/Ti ratio to improve the mechanical performance of 
these alloys [23,24] . Additionally, Fu et al. [25] utilized Thermo-Calc 
software to investigate the effects of various alloying elements on the 
precipitation behavior of Inconel 718. Their findings indicated that both 
Al and Ti affect the dissolution temperature and phase constitution of 
the γ’ phase. However, an increase in Al content significantly reduces 
the γ " phase content. In a comparative study, Saleem et al. [9] analyzed 
the mechanical properties of 945 and 945X alloys subjected to various 
heat treatments, and found that the 945X alloy, with a higher Nb con -
tent, exhibited superior strength compared to the 945 alloy under all 
heat treatment conditions. This enhancement in strength was attributed 
to the preferential diffusion of Nb to the boundaries of the γ’ pre -
cipitates. Han et al. [26] developed a novel M718 alloy with elevated Mo 
content, derived from the Mo levels in Inconel 718, and evaluated the 
effects of various heat treatments on the modified M718 alloy. Their 
results indicated that, despite the modifications, the strength of the 
M718 alloy was inferior to that of the conventional Inconel 718 alloy. 
Liu et al. [27] investigated the influence of different Nb concentrations 
(4.9, 7.0, 8.0, 9.0 and 11.0 wt%) on the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of Inconel 718. Their findings demonstrated that increasing 
the Nb content significantly promotes in both the γ " phase and the 
detrimental Laves phase within the alloy. The results indicate that an 
increase in Nb content significantly enhances the γ " phase and the 
detrimental Laves phase, which improves the material ’ s strength and 
microhardness but leads to a notable decrease in elongation. Alloys with 
high Nb content exhibit superior performance in the direct aging state.
Although numerous studies have focused on optimizing the me -
chanical properties of nickel-based corrosion resistant alloys, most of 
these studies have concentrated on the adjustment of single alloying 
elements, lacking a systematic approach to the synergistic optimization 
of multiple elements. Moreover, while optimizing individual elements 
can improve mechanical properties to some extent, it often leads to an 
increase in detrimental phases, which limits further performance 
enhancement. Therefore, a key challenge in current research is how to 
design alloy compositions through rational multi-element optimization 
to improve the overall performance of the alloy. In response to this issue, 
the present study proposes a multi-element synergistic adjustment 
strategy based on existing composition data of nickel-based corrosion 
resistant alloys. Specifically, based on the composition range outlined in 
API 6ACRA [28] for nickel-based corrosion-resistant alloys, this study 
selects Ti, Nb, and Mo as key alloying elements. A three-factor, three- -
level orthogonal experiment is conducted, and a regression model is 
constructed using the Response Surface Methodology (RSM). The goal is 
to adjust the contents of these elements to optimize the γ’ and γ " phase 
microstructures and enhance the alloy ’ s resistance to pitting corrosion. 
As the addition of Al significantly reduces the content of the γ " phase, 
which plays a crucial role in strengthening [25] , Ti is selected for the γ’ 
phase and Nb for the γ " phase. Additionally, Mo is considered for its 
known effect on improving pitting corrosion resistance [14,29] . This 
approach not only aids in predicting the alloy ’ s performance but also 
significantly reduces the time and cost of alloy development. Currently, 
experimental data combined with computational modeling has 
demonstrated excellent performance in material design and develop -
ment [30,31] .
In this study, composition optimization was carried out using a three- 
factor, three-level orthogonal experimental design, integrated with the 
RSM and high-throughput screening. Nb, Ti, and Mo contents were 
selected were selected as experimental factors, and their effects on the 
alloy strength and elongation were systematically analyzed. Based on 
this regression model, high-throughput computational methods were 
used to screen alloy compositions, from which 1738 potential alloy 
compositions were identified from a pool of 70356 candidates that meet 
the criteria of 0.2 % offset yield strength (R
p0.2
) greater than 1300 MPa 
and elongation greater than 12 %. Subsequently, one alloy composition 
was selected from the set of 1738 candidates for experimental valida -
tion, and the results confirmed the reliability of the regression model in 
predicting the alloy ’ s performance.
