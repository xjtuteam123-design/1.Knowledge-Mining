Abstract. High Nb containing TiAl alloys have been investiga ted traditionally as potential high 
temperature structural materials because of their h igh strength, good oxidation and creep resistance. 
However, the poor ductility and fracture toughness at room temperature limit their application, which 
could be improved by controlling microstructure to get refine and homogeneous fully lamellar 
structure. In this study, a high Nb containing TiAl alloy alloying Mn, B and Y with refined 
microstructure was produced. The solidification path was analyzed by DSC and SEM microstructure 
of the alloy was observed, after heating at a certa in temperature for 1-24hrs and then quenching in 
water. The dissolution of β phase was also investig ated. The results showed that the β phase could 
decompose only by heating in single β or near α phase field. 
Introduction 
High Nb containing TiAl alloys have been investigated traditionally as potential high temperature 
structural materials applied in automotive and aero space industries because of their high strength, 
good oxidation and creep resistance [1-3] . However, the poor ductility and fracture toughnes s at room 
temperature limit their application, which could be improved by controlling microstructure to get 
refined and homogeneous fully lamellar structure. T he alloy solidified from β phase region can get 
refined and less segregated microstructure, because α phase solidifying from primary β phase has

Lowering Al content or adding β-stabilizing elements such as Fe, Ru, W, Mo, Cr, V, Nb and Ta into 
TiAl alloys, solidification from β phase can occur. Whereas, as adding high content β-stabilizing 
elements, the β phase can be retained at room tempe rature; the retained B2 phase usually at grain 
boundaries is detrimental to their mechanical prope rties [1,7] . Additional heat treatment is needed to 
eliminate the bulk B2 phase . It has been reported that annealing at (α+γ) phase field for enough time 
then furnace cooling can dissolve the β phase, and the time used depends on annealing temperature, so 
higher temperature decreases the holding time [8-10] . Before the heat treatment, we should know the 
thermodynamics of phase transformation, the solidif ication paths, volume fraction and 
thermodynamic stability of the phases present at gi ven temperatures. Knowledge of the constituent 
phases and their transition temperatures is the bas is for smart heat treatments [11] . So our aim in this 
work is to study the phase transformation of a very fine microstructure of high Nb containing TiAl 
alloy then use annealing heat treatment to optimize the microstructure.

Experimental procedure 
The alloy ingot of 9kg used in this study having a nominal composition of 
Ti-45Al-8Nb-2Mn-0.3B-0.3Y (at.%) was prepared using induction skull melting a mixture of high 
purity elements (more than 99.9% purity) and master alloys (Nb-Al, Y-Al, Al-Ti-B) under argon 
atmosphere. The ingot diameter is 140mm. The analyz ed composition of the ingot was 
Ti-45.2Al-6.91Nb-0.65Mn-0.25B-0.27Y (at.%) and the oxygen, nitrogen impurity levels are 500 
wppm, 88 wppm, respectively. In order to eliminate the porosity, the ingot was HIPed at 1280°C/130 
MPa for 4 hrs. The heat treatment using the HIPed a lloys was conducted in a box furnace. The 
samples with dimensions of 10 ×10 ×10mm 3 were cut using electro-discharge machining from th e 
large ingot and packaged in a quartz tube fill with argon atmosphere. Heat treatment was performed in 
the temperature between 1100-1400˚C for various tim e and by furnace cooling (FC) or air cooling 
(AC), some samples after annealing at high temperat ure were rapidly transfer to another preset 
furnace holding at 900˚C for 1h and then air cooled. 
The microstructures were observed by optical micros copy (OP), scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM) using backscattered electron imaging (BSE). The line-intercept method was used to obtain an 
average grain size for each heat-treatment conditio n. Metallographic samples were polished and 
etched in a Kroll’s reagent of 3 ml HF, 5 ml HNO 3 and 92 ml distilled water. X-ray diffraction 
measurements were carried out at room temperature w ith a TTRAX3 diffractometer, using Cu Ka 
radiation for an angle range of 20–100˚ (2θ). The d ifferential scanning calorimetry (DSC) 
measurements were performed under a flow of high pu rity argon gas and were taken upon heating 
with a rate of 20ºC/min up to a temperature of 1400ºC. 
Results and Discussions 
The microstructure of the as-cast alloy is presented in Fig. 1(a). The alloy is very fine and mainly 
consists of γ/α 2 lamellar structure with mean colony size of about 60µm and the lamellar spacing of 
140nm. There is evidence of bright B2 phase and dar k γ phase along grain boundaries. The 
Y-enriched phase in the form of particle or rod-like morphology and boride in the shape of ribbons are 
also observed at grain boundaries and in grains, as the arrow indicates. The HIP treatment at 
1280 °C/130MPa/4h produces significant changes in the mic rostructure, as shown in Fig. 1(b), parts 
of the lamellar structure has dissolved and most of the lamellar structure has coarsened. The average 
lamellar colony size becomes 220µm and lamella is a lso thicker. More fractions of B2 and γ phase 
locate at colony boundaries and the grain size of B2 phase increases. XRD results indicate that after 
HIP treatment the volume fraction of α2 phase decreases compared to the as-cast condition. According 
to Ti-Al-Nb ternary diagram, as increasing the equi valent Nb content up to 10at.%, the equilibrium 
phase is B2+γ at room temperature [1] . After heat treatment, the microstructure rearranges according to 
the phase equilibrium.

After annealing at 1400°C for 1h, the microstructur e consists of featureless matrix phase and 
dispersed Y-enriched phase and boride, as seen in Fig. 3(a) and XRD pattern identifies that the alloy 
in this temperature is composed of single α phase, as shown in Fig. 4(a). The α phase is likely to be 
massively transformed from β phase through β→α tran sformation during quenching, it is suggested 
that at 1400°C the alloy is in single β phase field. 
For samples annealed at 1380°C (i.e. within the two phase field, α+β), the structure mainly 
consists of α2, B2 phase, but minor granular γ phas e is also observed, as shown in Fig. 3(b), i.e. the 
bright, grey and dark phases are B2, α2 and γ, respectively. It is supposed that the γ phase is massive 
transformation products during cooling the initial higher temperature α phase, the evidence is that the 
(002) and (200) planes of γ phase are hardly separa ted, as shown in Fig. 4. After quenching from 
1340°C the structure is mainly composed of α2 and minor B2 phase, so from 1340°C to 1380°C, the 
alloy enters into (α+β) two-phase field. 
There are some phase transformations occurred between the temperature 1300°C and 1340°C, as 
the thermogram in Fig. 2 (a) shows. The image of the representative alloy quenched from 1320°C is 
shown in Fig. 3(d), which presents the major α2 pha se and minor B2 and γ phase. Comparing the 
image Fig. 3(c) and Fig. 3(e), it can be deduced that raising the annealing temperature in this range, γ 
phase decreases and β phase increases. So in this temperature range, the alloy locates at (α+β+γ) three 
phase field. 
At 1300ºC, the alloy is in (α+γ) two-phase field, after quenching the alloy shows major equiaxed α 
phase and small volume fractions of γ phase at grai n boundaries. It is also observed that some γ 
lamellae grow into α grain. When quenching from the lower temperature at 1280°C, the structure 
consists of more γ lamellae growing into α grain, a nd the grain boundaries of α grain are not clearly 
visible. Some thin α lamellae are also observed in initial γ grain. It is worth to notice that, quenching 
from the temperature equal or lower than 1280ºC, th e (002) and (200) planes of γ phase are well 
separated, which indicating that the γ phase is ord ered. As quenching from the temperature range of 
1150°C-1085°C, these alloys show duplex microstructure that is (α2+ γ) lamellar structure and α2 (or 
B2 phase depending on temperature) as well as γ pha se at lamellar colonies boundaries. At 1150°C 
the irregular structure at grain boundaries is supposed to be α2, because its contrast is very similar to 
that of the gray lamella in colony grain. But quenching from lower temperature 1085°C, the α2 phases 
at grain boundaries are disappeared, instead of B2 phase locating at the grain boundaries. As the XRD 
patterns shown that the volume fraction of α2 phase in the alloy quenched from 1085°C is much lower 
than that of the alloy quenched from 1150°C. There is a α→β phase transformation reaction near the 
temperature of 1105°C, as the circle shown in Fig. 3(h), from the higher magnified image inserted into 
Fig. 3(h) it can be observed the coexistence of light B2 phase and gray untransformed α2 phase. So in 
the temperature range of 1300°C-1150°C the alloy li es in the (α+γ) two-phase field, below the 
temperature of 1105°C the alloy enters into (α2+γ+B2) three-phase field.

This alloy shows more complicated phase relations t han other alloy with high contents of 
β-stabilized elements, the variations in the major phase volume fractions with temperature are painted 
in Fig. 2(b). It is noticed that there is no single α phase filed. Furthermore, the β phase exists in most of 
the phase filed except for the two phase (α+γ) shor t region from 1150°C-1300°C. According to the 
microstructural observation for the quenched alloys it can be speculated that the as-cast alloy follows 
such solidification and transformation pathway: L → L+β → β → (α+β) → (α+β+γ) → (α+γ) → (α2+γ) 
→α2+B2+γ. The phase transformation sequence is very similar to that of 4Nb-4Zr alloys[12], in 
which phase transforms as follows: L→L + β→ β → (α+β)→ (α+β+γ) → (α2+β+γ). The absence of 
single α phase filed in both alloys is owing to the higher β-stabilized element content. It has been 
reported that high Nb addition decreases the β and α transus temperatures of Al-lean TiAl alloy, with 
Nb addition the β phase field extends to higher Al concentration, but the α field is contracted[1]. Zr 
adding into TiAl alloy also results in contraction of α field by decreasing the β transus temperatures 
whereas rising the α transves temperature[13]. 
In order to minimize the B2 phase, the additional h eat treatment was processed. After the heat 
treatment at 1400 °C in the single β phase field or at 1300 °C in the near single α phase field, the β 
phase dissolves completely and the microstructure changes to fully lamellar structure with the average 
colony size of about 136µm and 158µm, respectively. When heated at 1300 °C there is no obvious 
restriction the α grains grow rapidly, however as l ow cooling from the single β phase field, α phase 
precipitates from the initial β phase with various orientation variants which transforms to lamellar 
colonies during cooling, so lamellar colony size of the alloy annealed in 1300 °C is larger than that of 
the alloy annealed in single β phase filed. However, when the heat treatment in the (α+β) or (α+β+γ) 
phase filed, the residual B2 phase still can be seen at grain boundaries. When heat treatment in (α+γ) 
phase filed, the irregular α2 phase at grain boundaries will transform to B2 phase. Even after aging at 
900ºC for long time, the B2 phase cannot decompose completely.

Summary 
The as-cast TiAl alloy with high Nb content had nea r fully lamellar structure with average 
microstructure of 60µm and irregular B2 phase and γ phase at colony boundaries. The phase 
transformation sequence was L → L +β →β→α+ β →α+β+γ →α+γ → (α2+γ) → α2+B2+γ. There was 
no single α phase field which was instead of the (α +β+γ) phase field, and the β phase existed in the 
most of the phase filed except for the two phase (α+γ) short region.
