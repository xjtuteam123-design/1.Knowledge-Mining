ABSTRACT 
The rapid synthesis of nanoprecipitation strengthened high-entropy alloys through additive manufacture shows 
great potential for high-temperature applications, but high-temperature intergranular brittleness limits the 
efficient structural application, especially in intermediate temperature, i.e., 650 - 900 
◦
C. The current study 
reported a combinatorial structure, i.e., hierarchical fiber-like structure (HFS) and dual-phase nanoprecipitates 
(DNP) using selective laser melting followed by annealing, exhibiting an exceptional high-temperature strength- 
plasticity synergy. The L1
2
- and L2
1
-type nanoprecipitates with superior thermal stability promote precipitation 
strengthening at elevated temperatures. Meanwhile, the presence of HFS persistently contributes to the plasticity 
through grain boundary sliding accompanied by multiscale crack deflection along with boundaries inhibiting 
final fracture. The representative plastic anisotropy facilitates the engineering application of this combined 
structure in more extreme conditions. This current innovative design of high-temperature structural materials is 
potentially applied to other additively manufactured alloys and opens a pathway for further optimization of 
thermostable alloy properties. 
The applications of heat-resistant metallic materials, such as those 
used for aircraft, gas turbines, turbocharger rotors, nuclear reactors, and 
liquid-fueled rockets, require metals and alloys with high strength and 
high ductility to avoid premature failure at high temperatures [1 – 3] . 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are a novel class of complex materials 
directed by the novel alloy design concept discovered in the center of the 
phase diagram, which is expected to meet the engineering application 
requirements in extreme environments [4 – 11] . Recently, HEAs have 
attracted much attention due to their remarkable heat-resistant me -
chanical properties caused by their unique four significant effects, e.g., 
high configurational entropy, sluggish diffusion, lattice distortion, and 
the cocktail effect [12 – 14] . Selective laser melting (SLM) as one of the 
most popular additive manufacturing techniques involves the fusion of 
powders layer by layer, which can one-time form many components 
with complex geometries without the fixed molds. Nowadays, the HEAs 
fabricated by SLM provide potential candidates for high-temperature 
applications besides previous superalloys [ 6 , 15 , 16 ]. However, yield 
strength drops sharply due to a rapid reduction in dislocation density at 
elevated temperatures for the SLMed products featuring high-density 
dislocations [ 17 , 18 ]. Usually, traditional heat-resistant superalloys are 
enlightened with precipitation strengthening to withstand yield strength 
dropping. It should also be beneficial to introduce the reinforced 
nanoparticles into HEAs to withstand yield strength dropping and thus 
achieve good heat-resistant properties [ 1 , 3 ]. Lamentedly, the precipi -
tation strengthening alloys including conventional Ni-based superalloys 
and high-entropy alloys suffer from intermediated-temperature inter -
granular embrittlement, especially at the temperature of 650 - 800 
◦
C 
[19 – 23] . The chemical element segregation and the precipitation of 
hard particles at the grain boundaries at medium temperature were 
considered to be the culprits of the regression of mechanical perfor -
mance [19] . Therefore, the composition design and thermo-mechanical 
processing were the strategies to eliminate intergranular precipitation

selective laser melting. It is expected to obtain a better strength-ductility 
balance at elevated temperatures. The microstructural characterization, 
mechanical responses, and associated deformation mechanisms were 
systematically investigated to evaluate the feasibility of the designed 
microstructure at high temperatures.

The current high-temperature resistance glass fiber materials and 
biological materials with multiscale fiber structures ( Figs. 1 a and b) can 
inspire new enlightenment to design novel metal material structures to 
enhance middle-temperature plastic. During deformation, the relative 
sliding between the fibers and the fasciculi composed of countless glass 
fibers creates a plastic space to ensure the safe work of the high- 
temperature rope at high-temperature conditions ( Fig. 1 a). In addi -
tion, a model for bioinspiration, muscle cells (muscle fibers) gather into 
bundles (fasciculus), which can freely achieve muscle contraction and 
relaxation under the action of blood vessel congestion and bloodletting. 
The coordinated deformation of muscle cells and fasciculi provides the 
most fundamental guarantee for biological activities ( Fig. 1 b). We report 
a hierarchical fiber-like structure (HFS) material with columnar grains 
(CG-HFS), dendritic fasciculi (DF-HFS), and cellular fibers (CF-HFS) by 
selective laser melting technology that embodies the requirements for 
microfiber sliding and overcomes the inherent middle-temperature 
brittleness ( Fig. 1 c).

observed. The cross-section of the PT-HFS specimen featured fractured 
fiber (confirmed as CG-HFS shear fracture through a localized magnified 
view of the insert). Typical cracks parallel to the HFS can propagate 
along the CGs boundaries, implying relative slip between grains and 
further cracking. In addition, we further found distinct intergranular 
fracture morphologies and interweave cracks accompanied by MPBs for 
PT-HFS specimens ( Fig. 4 b). The magnified view shows that the fracture 
surface crack propagates along the SGBs ( Fig. 4 c) related to high strain 
localization, as proved and unveiled by the EBSD KAM map (Fig. S6b). 
Microcracks have less chance to continuously expand because of the less 
grain boundary area in the direction of perpendicular to the loading axis. 
Even partial microcracks along SGBs can be hindered by small grains at 
MPBs. The small grains at the MPBs are caused by repeated laser heating 
on the hatch overlaps, which are confirmed by the EBSD result 
(Fig. S6c). Therefore, the crack traces (indicated by the white dash line 
in Fig. 4 c) changed at the local MPBs forming a crack deflection, which 
delayed fracture and increased elongation. Vertical cracks occur not 
only in the intergranular but also inside the MPBs along the DF-HFS 
(indicated by the gray arrows in Fig. 4 c), suggesting extensive slipping 
of SGBs. The crack deflection and the vertical cracking path formation 
can lower the elastic energy for driving crack propagation, which 
prohibits the crack to further propagate [ 55 , 56 ].

of a hierarchical fiber-like structure (HFS) and dual-phase nano -
precipitates (DNP) through selective laser melting and following 
annealing that overcame the rapid intergrain fracture and with high 
strength and good plasticity at high temperatures. The HFS involving the 
columnar grain (CG-HFS), the dendritic fasciculus (DF-HFS), and 
cellular fiber (CF-HFS) improve the high-temperature plasticity through 
the interface sliding with excellent plasticity of 19.3 ± 2.0% at 650

isotropy DNP microstructure and its superior thermal stability effect. 
The results have demonstrated the usefulness and effectiveness of a dual 
precipitation-strengthened HEA with a hierarchical fiber-like structure 
via SLM applied in a high-temperature environment. The excellent 
combination of strength and plasticity of the current SLMed HEA dem -
onstrates its potential for high-temperature service.
