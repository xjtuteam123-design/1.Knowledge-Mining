ABSTRACT
Improving the high-temperature mechanical performance of Mg – RE alloys without increasing rare-earth content 
remains a critical challenge. In this study, the effect of preset deformation twins on the high-temperature strength 
of Mg – 8.5Gd – 3Y – 0.5Zr (wt%) and Mg – 8.5Gd – 3Y – 2Cu – 0.5Zr (wt%) alloys was investigated. Preset twins and 
twin networks were introduced via multi-pass cold rolling at room temperature, followed by tensile testing at 
300 
◦
C. The results show that the tensile strength of the Mg – 8.5Gd – 3Y – 0.5Zr alloy at 300 
◦
C was significantly 
improved from 139 MPa to 227 MPa, representing an increase of 63 %. Microstructural analysis revealed that the 
twins serve as thermally stable interfaces that effectively impede dislocation motion and promote complex in -
teractions among dislocations, stacking faults, and twins. This interaction enhances the work-hardening capa -
bility and thus contributes to strength improvement at elevated temperatures. The strengthening effect of preset 
twins was further validated in the LPSO-containing Mg – 8.5Gd – 3Y – 2Cu – 0.5Zr (wt%) alloy, in which the tensile 
strength increased by 74 MPa, reaching a high level of 256 MPa at 300 
◦
C. These findings demonstrate that twin 
engineering provides an effective microstructural strategy to improve the thermal performance of Mg – RE alloys 
without altering their composition.
1. Introduction
Magnesium (Mg) alloys are among the lightest structural metals and 
are increasingly attractive for applications requiring weight reduction, 
such as in the automotive, aerospace, and electronics industries [1,2] . 
Currently, Mg alloys are primarily utilized in non-heat-resistant com -
ponents, such as automotive panels, seat brackets, and electronic 
housings [3,4] . However, with the growing demand for lightweight 
design in high-performance systems, there is an urgent need to develop 
Mg alloys with not only excellent room-temperature (RT) strength but 
also robust high-temperature mechanical properties [5] . Unfortunately, 
the strength of conventional Mg alloys typically falls below 200 MPa at 
temperatures at 300 
◦
C, due to the limited thermal stability of their 
microstructures [6 – 8] .
The addition of rare-earth (RE) elements, such as Gd and Y, has been 
demonstrated to significantly improve the high-temperature strength 
and thermal stability of Mg alloys. In general, the strength of Mg – RE 
alloys increases with higher RE content [9] . The strengthening mecha -
nisms of these alloys primarily include solid solution strengthening 
provided by RE solute atoms, as well as second-phase strengthening 
through the formation of high-melting-point and thermally stable β ′ 
intermetallic compounds [10 – 12] . However, the economic consider -
ations associated with increased RE content and the coarsening of 
second-phase particles during prolonged thermal exposure limit their 
high-temperature engineering applications [12] . Therefore, there is a 
pressing need for alternative strategies to enhance the high-temperature 
mechanical performance of Mg alloys without increasing RE content.
One promising approach involves microstructural design through 
controlled introduction of layered defects. Grain refinement is 
commonly used to improve yield strength, but grain boundaries are 
prone to coarsening at elevated temperatures due to their high interfa -
cial energy and poor thermal stability [13] . In contrast, layered defects 
such as deformation twins and stacking faults (SFs) exhibit relatively 
low interfacial energies and near-zero curvature, which grant them 
better stability under thermal exposure [14,15] . In aluminum alloys, 
high densities of twin boundaries have been shown to improve 
high-temperature microstructural stability [16] . In Mg alloys, efforts 
have been made to improve high-temperature performance by

nucleation sites for dynamic recrystallization (DRX), potentially accel -

In this work, we systematically investigate the effect of preset

further validated in a Mg – 8.5Gd – 3Y – 2Cu – 0.5Zr (wt%) alloy containing 
LPSO phases, indicating the potential of deformation twin engineering 
to serve as an effective and RE-efficient approach for enhancing the

2. Experimental procedures
The alloys used in this study were Mg – 8.5Gd – 3Y – 0.5Zr (wt%) and 
Mg – 8.5Gd – 3Y – 2Cu – 0.5Zr (wt%). Both were fabricated into billets with 
a diameter of 90 mm and a length of 1200 mm via semi-continuous 
casting. The billets were first homogenized at 500 
◦
C for 12 h, fol -
lowed by hot extrusion at 375 
◦
C. Prior to extrusion, both the die and the 
homogenized billets were preheated at 375 
◦
C for 30 min. The extrusion 
speed was set to 0.6 mm/s, and the extruded plates were immediately 
air-cooled to room temperature. The resulting extruded plates had di -
mensions of 1000 mm (length) × 45 mm (width) × 8 mm (thickness). 
Rectangular specimens with dimensions of 50 mm (ED) × 45 mm (TD) 
× 8 mm (ND) were sectioned from both two plates for subsequent so -
lution treatment and cold rolling, where ED, TD, and ND denote extru -
sion, transverse, and normal directions, respectively. Solution treatment 
was carried out at 500 
◦
C for 5 h in a muffle furnace with the specimens 
wrapped in aluminum foil, followed by immediate air cooling. After 
removing surface oxidation layers by grinding, the specimens were 
machined to a final thickness of 7.5 mm along the ND direction for 
multi-pass cold rolling.
Cold rolling was conducted at RT using a multi-pass strategy along 
the ED direction, with a thickness reduction of 2 % – 5 % per pass and a 
total reduction of approximately 25 %. This approach aims to maximize 
the incorporation of uniformly distributed deformation twins. Tensile 
specimens with a gauge length of 17 mm and a cross-sectional area of 
3.5 mm × 2 mm were machined from the rolled sheet along the rolling 
direction. Tensile testing was performed at 300 
◦
C using an Instron 5569 
testing machine equipped with a high-temperature chamber. Prior to 
testing, specimens were held at 300 
◦
C for 20 min to ensure thermal 
equilibrium. In the subsequent sections, the multi-pass cold-rolled 
samples are abbreviated as “ CR ” for simplicity.
Microstructural characterization was conducted using an Olympus 
BX60M optical microscope (OM), an Oxford Symmetry S2 electron 
backscatter diffraction (EBSD) detector, and a JEOL JEM-F200 field 
emission transmission electron microscope (FE-TEM) operating at 
200 kV. EBSD specimens were prepared by wire electrical discharge 
machining followed by mechanical polishing and final argon ion pol -
ishing. TEM foils were prepared using twin-jet electropolishing. EBSD 
and TEM data were analyzed using AZtec and Channel 5.0, and Digital 
Micrograph software, respectively.
3. Results
3.1. Microstructure characterization
To achieve a wider grain size distribution that facilitates the subse -
quent introduction of various deformation twins during multi-pass cold 
rolling [20,21] ， the as-extruded Mg – 8.5Gd – 3Y – 0.5Zr alloy was sub -
jected to a solid-solution treatment at 500 
◦
C for 5 h. Fig. 1 presents the 
EBSD band contrast (BC) maps and corresponding grain size distribu -
tions of the alloy in both the as-extruded and solution-treated 
conditions.
As shown in Fig. 1 a, the as-extruded alloy exhibits a distinct het -
erogeneous lamellar structure composed of alternating regions of fine 
grains and coarse equiaxed grains, with an average grain size of 1.79 μ m 
( Fig. 1 c). This heterogeneous microstructure originates from the severe 
plastic deformation and the non-uniform dynamic recrystallization 
induced by second-phase particles during hot extrusion, resulting in 
varied grain growth behavior across different regions [22] .
Following solution treatment at 500 
◦
C for 5 h, the microstructure 
significantly coarsens, as illustrated in Fig. 1 b. The solution treated alloy 
exhibits a homogeneous equiaxed grain structure with a markedly 
increased average grain size of 40.57 μ m ( Fig. 1 d). Most second-phase 
particles initially present in the as-extruded state dissolve into the ma -
trix, leading to a more uniform microstructure with well-defined grain 
boundaries. No obvious deformation bands or twins were observed, 
indicating that the heat treatment effectively promoted grain boundary 
migration and substantially reduced residual stress and dislocation 
density.
The grain coarsening strategy adopted here serves as a key micro -
structural design for this study. Previous studies have shown that fine 
grains tend to promote cross-slip and activation of non-basal 
< c + a > dislocations [23,24] . When the temperature exceeds 225 
◦
C, 
the critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) for non-basal slip decreases, 
facilitating the activation of multiple slip systems [25] . Therefore, it is 
inferred that coarse-grained Mg alloys are more prone to activate 
deformation twinning during cold deformation, promoting twin nucle -
ation and growth. Accordingly, the large-grained structure obtained 
after solution treatment provides an ideal microstructural foundation for 
the subsequent introduction of preset twins via multi-pass cold rolling.

effectiveness of preset twins for enhancing high-temperature perfor -

extruded and CR samples tested at 300 
◦

those of the CR alloy are increased to about 186 MPa and 227 MPa. 
These correspond to absolute increases of roughly 74 MPa and 88 MPa, 
reflecting relative improvements of about 66.1 % and 63.3 %, respec -
tively. Typically, most previous studies aimed at achieving UTS above

◦
C relied primarily on compositional adjustments, such 
as increasing the content of RE elements [26] . In contrast, the present 
strategy achieves comparable strength enhancement solely via preset

as-extruded sample. This reduction in ductility is attributed to the

3.3. Thermal stability of preset twins
To further elucidate the thermal stability of preset twins under high- 
temperature loading, the fractions of various characteristic twin 
boundaries in CR and CR-4 % samples were systematically quantified. 
Fig. 4 a and b present the corresponding twin boundary maps of these 
two conditions, where different colors represent distinct twin boundary 
types, with their specific misorientation angles/axes detailed in Table 1 . 
It can be observed that the predominant twin types include {10 12} 
tensile twins (TTW, marked in red) and {10 11}-{10 12} double twins 
(DTW, marked in blue), along with minor fractions of {10 11} 
compression twins (CTW, marked in yellow) and intersecting TTW – TTW 
twin structures (marked in green) [27] . A visual comparison reveals no 
significant variation in the distribution and density of these twin 
boundaries in CR and CR-4 % samples, which is further supported by 
statistical data provided in Table 1 . For instance, the fraction of TTW 
boundaries slightly decreased from 9.79 % before deformation to 
9.62 % after 4 %-strain deformation, a variation that falls within the 
experimental error range, especially considering that the deformation 
was not conducted in-situ. Thus, it can be reasonably concluded that the 
preset twins introduced during multi-pass cold rolling exhibit excellent 
thermal stability under tensile deformation at 300 
◦

ble and straight boundary morphologies. Previous studies have also 
demonstrated that twinning is difficult to activate at 300 
◦

largely neglected [28] , thus, no additional twins were formed during the 
tensile deformation.

4. Discussion
In the preceding sections, the preset twins and twin networks 
introduced by multi-pass cold rolling were confirmed to exhibit excel -
lent structural stability under high-temperature conditions. According to 
the classic grain boundary migration equation: 
V = MP (1) 
where V is the boundary migration rate, M is the boundary mobility, and 
P is the driving force, the migration rate is closely related to the 
boundary curvature. Specifically, the driving force P is proportional to 
γ / D , where γ is the grain boundary energy and D is the grain diameter 
[30] . Twin boundaries, characterized by their nearly flat geometry and 
negligible curvature, significantly reduce the migration driving force 
and rate, thus contributing to superior thermal stability during 
high-temperature exposure. Moreover, the strong interactions between 
twin boundaries and dislocations serve as a critical mechanism for 
strengthening the alloy at elevated temperatures.

strain-hardening capability — the CR sample exhibited stable twin

deformation. These twin boundaries promoted intense dislocation 
entanglement and accumulation, facilitating dislocation proliferation 
throughout the matrix. Such complex and high-density dislocation 
substructures are primarily responsible for the enhanced strain- 
hardening behavior observed in the CR sample, substantially contrib -
uting to the superior high-temperature mechanical performance of the 
alloy.
Building upon the KAM analysis, the distribution of Schmid factors 
was further evaluated for both the as-extruded and CR samples. The 
Schmid factor serves as a key indicator of the ease with which a slip 
system can be activated during plastic deformation. In theory, systems 
with values approaching 0.5 are more readily activated [33] . In Mg al -
loys, deformation temperature plays a critical role in determining the 
dominant slip modes. At RT, basal < a > slip is the most easily activated. 
However, the CRSS for non-basal slip systems decreases markedly at 
high temperature range, enabling their widespread activation [34] . 
Fig. 6 presents the Schmid factor maps and corresponding histograms for 
basal < a > , prismatic < a > , pyramidal < a > , and pyramidal 
< c + a > slip systems in the as-extruded ( Fig. 6 a – h) and CR ( Fig. 6 i – p) 
samples.
In the as-extruded alloy, relatively high Schmid factors are observed 
for prismatic < a > , pyramidal < a > , and pyramidal < c + a > systems, 
whereas basal < a > slip exhibits comparatively lower values. Never -
theless, due to the orientation hardness and high activation stress of 
prismatic < a > slip, combined with the absence of thermally stable 
pinning phases, the as-extruded sample shows limited potential for 
strength and work-hardening enhancement through dislocation 
interactions.
In contrast, the CR alloy displays significantly increased Schmid 
factors for basal < a > , prismatic < a > , and pyramidal < a > slip systems. 
This improvement is primarily attributed to the introduction of preset 
{10 12} twins during multi-pass cold rolling, which facilitates the acti -
vation of multiple slip systems [35] . Furthermore, the color maps reveal 
that both the twins and adjacent matrix regions exhibit higher Schmid 
factor values, promoting the activation of various dislocation slip modes 
at elevated temperatures. The strong pinning effect of the preset twins 
leads to substantial local strain accumulation near twin boundaries, 
resulting in the formation of dense dislocation networks. This enhances 
the alloy ’ s work-hardening capacity and contributes to improved 
high-temperature mechanical strength.

5. Conclusions
A thermomechanical strategy was developed to enhance the high- 
temperature mechanical performance of Mg – RE alloys without 
increasing RE content, through the introduction of preset twins by multi- 
pass cold rolling. The main conclusions are summarized as follows: 
1. In the Mg – 8.5Gd – 3Y – 0.5Zr alloy, 25 % reduce multi-pass cold roll -
ing successfully introduced a network of preset twins and twin net -
works primarily consisting of {10 12} tensile twins and {10 11}- 
{10 12} double twins. These twin structures exhibited excellent 
thermal stability during tensile deformation at 300 
◦
C, maintaining a 
stable morphology with no significant dynamic recrystallization.
2. The cold-rolled sample exhibited a remarkable increase in tensile 
strength at 300 
◦
C, from 139 MPa to 227 MPa (a 63.3 % improve -
ment). Microstructural analysis revealed that thermally stable twin 
boundaries effectively impeded dislocation motion, leading to dense 
dislocation entanglement and localized strain accumulation, which 
enhanced strain hardening and elevated high-temperature strength.
3. In addition to twinning – dislocation interactions, stacking faults were 
also activated during high-temperature deformation. Their interac -
tion with dislocations contributed further to dislocation pinning, 
indicating a synergistic strengthening mechanism involving multiple 
crystal defects.
4. The effectiveness of this strategy was also validated in a Cu- 
containing alloy. In Mg – 8.5Gd – 3Y – 2Cu – 0.5Zr, the cold-rolled sam -
ple achieved a tensile strength of 256 MPa at 300
