abstract
A ﬁrst principles calculation method was used to investigate the site preference of platinum group metals
(PGMs) at the c/c0 interface in Ni-based single-crystal superalloys. The calculation results show that the
addition of PGMs can decrease the total energy and the binding energy of c/c0 interface, which may result
in an improved microstructure stability of Ni-based single-crystal superalloys. Moreover, by calculation,
it is also found that PGMs can stabilize both c and c0 phases and have a preference for Ni site at the coher-
ent c/c0 interface. The inﬂuence of Ruthenium (Ru) on the partitioning behavior of W, Re, Cr and Mo in c
and c0 phases was studied by Dmol3 calculation as well. It is found that when Ru substitutes the central
Ni at the c/c0 interface, a reverse partitioning of W, Re and Cr occurs; while the partitioning behavior of
Mo is not affected. Furthermore, electronic structure analysis of c/c0 interface in terms of Mulliken pop-
ulation and partial density of states (PDOS) was performed to understand the alloying mechanism of Ru
in Ni-based single-crystal superalloys. The results show that the strengthening effect of Ru alloying is
mainly due to the reduction in binding energy of Ru.

1. Introduction
The Ni-based single-crystal superalloys have made substantial
contributions to the performance of aircraft engines and turbine
blades because of their high creep strength and low crack-growth
rates at high temperatures [1–8]. There are two primary phases
present in such superalloys, where the precipitate c0 phase (based
on L1 2 Ni3Al) is coherently embedded between narrow channels
of c phase (based on fcc Ni). Generally speaking, it is c0 phase that
is responsible for the high temperature strength of the Ni-based sin-
gle-crystal superalloys. This is the reason, at least in part, why most
researches have been focused on the alloying effect of refractory ele-
ments in the c0 phase [9–20]. Modern Ni-based superalloys usually
contain more than 10 alloying elements. These elements are effec-
tive in improving the solution strengthening effect and the elevated
temperature strength of Ni-based superalloys [21,22]. The refrac-
tory elements content has increased from /C24 14 wt.% for ﬁrst-gener-
ation (CMSX-2) superalloys to greater than 20 wt.% for third-
generation (CMSX-10) superalloys [23]. Platinum group metals
(PGMs), such as Ru, Rh, Pd, Os, Ir and Pt, usually have high melting
points and high hot-corrosion resistance, addition of PGMs is ex-
pected to improve the high-temperature mechanical properties of
superalloys [19]. In particular, the addition of Ru has generated re-
search interest as a promising element, due to its signiﬁcant contri-
butions to an enhanced microstructure stability and high-
temperature mechanical properties of Ni-based single-crystal
superalloys [24,25]. Recently, several different theoretical methods
have been used to elucidate the alloying mechanisms controlling
the mechanical properties of Ni-based single-crystal superalloys
[19,26–29]. The alloying effect has been studied in terms of the elec-
tronic structure calculation and atomic structure determination. In
spite of extensive investigations, some issues still remain unre-
solved. Ru is a typical element added in the fourth-generation
superalloys; Ru is also proven to be an effective element that can
suppress the formation of topologically close-packed (TCP) phases
in superalloys [30–32]. However, the strengthening mechanism of
Ru in superalloys is still unclear, especially at the level of the elec-
tronic and atomic structures.
The objective of this paper is to elucidate the alloying effect of
Ru on
c/c0 interface in Ni-based single-crystal superalloys. Due to
the difference in the atomic environment between c/c0 interface
and bulk c0 phase, Ni-site preference is invoked to interpret the
alloying mechanisms of Ru in Ni-based single-crystal superalloys.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we brieﬂy describe
the Dmol3 method and calculation model. In Section 3.1, we pres-
ent the calculation results that show the site preference of PGMs at
c/c0 interface. Section 3.2 presents the inﬂuence of Ru on the par-
titioning behavior of refractory elements W, Re, Cr and Mo. The
conclusion of this paper is presented in Section 4.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Site preference of PGMs
Both total energy and binding energy for PGMs atoms at either
Ni or Al site are required to investigate the site preference of PGMs
at the coherent c/c0 interface. With the relaxed conﬁguration, the
calculated total energy ( Et) and binding energy ( Eb) for the super-
cells are summarized in Table 1 . It can be seen that both Et and
Eb of c/c0 interface in the presence of PGMs atoms are lower than
those of c/c0 interface without PGMs atoms. Thus, the substitutions
of either Ni or Al site for PGMs atoms can decrease Et and Eb, which
may result in an improved microstructure stability of Ni-based sin-
gle-crystal superalloys. Moreover, for the same PGMs atoms, the Et
and Eb of the system with Ni atom at the center of a supercell are
lower than those of the system with Al atom centered at a super-
cell. Take Ru as an example, when Ru atom replaces the central
Al atom, the Et and Eb decrease by 2.1079 eV and 22.5702 eV
respectively; when Ru atom replaces the central Ni atom, the Et
and Eb decrease by 4.6959 eV and 33.8389 eV respectively. These
results indicate that PGMs atoms can stabilize both c and c0
phases, and have a preference for Ni site at the coherent c/c0 inter-
face. So far, there have been no experimental and theoretical works
to study the lattice site preference of PGMs atoms at coherent c/c0
interface. The above results may lay a foundation for further
studies on the role of PGMs atoms in Ni-based single-crystal
superalloys.

comparison with other PGMs atoms. This result is consistent with
a well-known fact that Ru is a critical element for stabilization of
the
c/c0 interfacial microstructure. Researches have been focused
on the site preference of Ru in c0 phase because c0 phase serves

3.2. Inﬂuence of Ru on the partitioning behavior of refractory elements
W, Re, Cr and Mo
Usually, refractory elements W, Re, Cr and Mo are added in
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys to form a strengthening so-
lid–solution phase. The partitioning behaviors of these refractory
elements also inﬂuence the strength and microstructure stability
of superalloys. Recent experimental results show that Ru can re-
verse the partitioning behavior of refractory elements between c
phase and c0 phase in a manner termed ‘reverse partitioning’, i.e.,
refractory elements W, Re, Cr, Mo, segregate to the c0 phase instead
of c phase [24,40]. In order for us to better understand the role of
Ru, we calculated the binding energies of the supercells with Ni
and Ru atoms centered at coherent interface, simultaneously with
W, Re, Cr and Mo atoms placed in the c phase and c0 phase respec-
tively. The Ni atom labeled by ‘‘2’’ in c phase is substituted for W,
Re, Cr and Mo, respectively. This substitution also applies to the Ni
atom labeled by ‘‘3’’ in c0 phase. The calculated binding energies
are presented in Table 2. W and Cr substitutions decrease the bind-
ing energy by 21.252 eV and 23.1872 eV respectively. When Ru
atom substitutes the central Ni atom, the binding energy decreases
by 2.4404 eV. In the case of c0 phase, when W atom replaces the Ni
atom labeled by ‘‘3’’, the binding energy decreases by 20.5589 eV.
For a Ru atom in place of the central Ni atom, the binding energy
decreases by 3.7659 eV. The above calculation results suggest that
W and Cr prefer the c phase, while the presence of Ru reverses
their partitioning behavior. This is in agreement with previous cal-
culations [40,41]. In addition, high-temperature mechanical prop-
erties of superalloys can be enhanced by Re due to its low
diffusivity as well as its tendency to form clusters in superalloys
[41–45]. To take advantage of this solid solution effect, it is neces-
sary for Re to be enriched in the c matrix. Considering the inﬂuence
of alloying elements on the partitioning behavior, the critical point
of superalloy design is to optimize the Re concentration. When the
central atom is Ni, the binding energy of Re located in c phase is
lower than that of Re in c0 phase, which is consistent with the
experimental and theoretical studies in which Re prefers to stay
in c phase [43,44,46–49]. Nevertheless, when Ru occupies the cen-
tral Ni site, the binding energies of Re in place of the atoms in c
phase and c0 phase are /C0 608.2554 eV and /C0 608.8034 eV respec-
tively. Consequently, Ru also exerts a similar reverse partitioning
effect on Re, resulting in a partition of Re in c0 phase rather than
c phase. When the central atom is Ni, as shown in Table 2 , the
binding energy is /C0 602.7337 eV and /C0 602.6591 eV for Mo in c
phase and c0 phase respectively. This result agrees with a well-
known fact that Mo atom prefers to stay in c phase [50–52]. When
Ru substitutes the central Ni atom, the binding energy of Mo in c
phase is lower than that of Mo in c0 phase. Ru seems to have no ef-
fect on the partitioning behavior of Mo. This conﬁrms the experi-
mental work conducted by Volek et al. [41]. It is necessary to
obtain a better understanding of the effect of Ru on the partitioning
behavior by electronic structure calculations.

Besides the Mulliken orbital population, we also calculated the
partial density of states (PDOS) to further elucidate the interaction
between Re atom and Ru atom. The calculation results of PDOS
are presented in Fig. 3 . The feature of PDOS indicates that there is
a strong interaction between Re atom and Ru atom through p–p
hybridization, which can be seen from the resonant hybridization
peaks labeled by the dashed lines in Fig. 3. Less p–p orbital hybrid-
ization peaks indicate that the bonding of interface with Re in c
phase are weaker than that of interface with Re in c0 phase. Hence,
the stability of c/c0 interface can be increased when Re stays in thec0
phase. These results provide additional insights into the alloying ef-
fects of Ru and Re in superalloys.
4. Conclusions
The atomic site preference of platinum group metals (PGMs) at
the
c/c0 interface in Ni-based single-crystal superalloys is studied
by Dmol3 calculation method. The calculation results show that
PGMs atoms prefer to occupy a Ni site at coherent c/c0 interface.
When PGMs replace the central atom of the c/c0 interface, the total
energy and binding energy decreased. Ru remarkably affects the
partitioning behavior of W, Re and Cr between c phase and c0
phase. However, Ru has no effect on the partitioning behavior of
Mo. Our calculation results of binding energy, Mulliken population
and partial density of states consistently elucidate the strengthen-
ing mechanism of Ru in Ni-based single-crystal superalloys.
