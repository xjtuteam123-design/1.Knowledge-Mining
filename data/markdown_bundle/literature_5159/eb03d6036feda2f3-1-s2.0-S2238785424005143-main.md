ABSTRACT 
Defect-free SLM-IN 738LC superalloy samples were fabricated using selective laser melting (SLM). The as-built 
samples exhibit a cellular structure with fine carbides, γ ′ phases, and high-density dislocations at the cell 
boundary and nano-size γ ′ phases in the interior, resulting in excellent deformation resistance and favorable 
mechanical properties at room temperature, with a yield strength (YS) of 970 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength 
(UTS) of 1263 MPa, and an elongation (EL) of 33%. This study investigates the evolution behavior of the γ ′ phase 
at different solid solution treatment (SHT) temperatures and its impact on alloy strength and toughness. The 
1020 + AHT sample exhibits a uniform distribution of coarse γ ′ phases. The samples subjected to 1070 + AHT 
and 1120 + AHT display a bimodal distribution of γ ′ phases. When the SHT temperature was further increased, 
fine spherical γ ′ phases are evenly distributed in the 1160 + AHT and 1200 + AHT samples. Moreover, the γ / γ ′ 
eutectics at the grain boundaries gradually dissolve with increasing SHT temperature, whereas the size and 
fraction of carbides increase. The room temperature tensile test of the 1070 + AHT sample demonstrated a 
favorable balance between strength and plasticity (YS = 1217 MPa, UTS = 1472 MPa, EL = 9.25%). This is 
attributed to the synergistic strengthening effect of the bimodal distribution of the γ ′ phase. The temperature- 
dependent deformation behavior of the 1070 + AHT sample was analyzed at 23

1. Introduction 
The IN 738LC superalloy is a typical precipitation-strengthened 
nickel-based superalloy, containing W, Mo, Nb, Ta, and other re -
fractory elements, particularly with a high content of Al + Ti (wt% > 6%) 
[ 1 , 2 ]. This composition results in a significant increase in the γ ′ phase 
(Ni
3 
(Al, Ti, Nb, Ta)) volume fraction, leading to excellent 
high-temperature mechanical properties [ 3 ]. The IN 738LC superalloy 
has been widely used in high-temperature components such as aero -
space engines and gas turbines [ 4 , 5 ]. Nevertheless, traditional casting 
and forging processes may result in extended production cycles and high 
costs [ 6 – 8 ]. Selective laser melting (SLM) technology can directly 
manufacture complicated geometry parts with high precision, short 
process cycles, and superior performance. Currently, SLM has been used 
in the investigation of the IN 738LC superalloy [ 9 – 15 ]. However, most 
previous studies have primarily focused on the cracking mechanism of 
the SLM-IN 738LC superalloy [ 16 – 26 ], with limited research on its 
mechanical properties, particularly its high-temperature deformation 
behavior. 
In several studies, the tensile properties of the IN 738LC superalloy, 
when prepared by SLM and subjected to conventional heat treatment 
(HT), were found to be inferior to the standard of cast alloy samples 
[ 27 – 29 ]. The main reason might be the improper HT regimes of the 
SLM-IN 738LC superalloy. As reported in many literature sources [ 30 ,

31 ], the IN 738LC sample prepared by SLM is always characterized by a 
cellular structure, fine internal carbide, and high-density dislocations, 
which differ from those of casting samples. Consequently, the conven -
tional HT scheme applied for casting alloys may not be suitable for SLM 
alloys. Therefore, it is necessary to reformulate the specialized HT 
regime for the SLM-IN 738LC superalloy to optimize γ ′ phases and 
enhance the mechanical properties. Recent studies have aimed to 
enhance the mechanical properties of SLM alloys by improving the HT 
regime [ 32 , 33 ]. However, the impact of γ ′ phase size and distribution on 
the mechanical properties of the SLM-IN 738LC superalloy under 
different HT regimes has not been determined. Therefore, this study will 
analyze the correlation between the microstructural evolution and the 
mechanical properties of the HT samples in order to determine a suitable 
heat treatment regime for the SLM-IN 738LC alloy, and it provides a 
research reference for the heat treatment of SLM-IN 738LC alloy. 
In addition, as previously stated, IN 738LC superalloy components 
are primarily used in high-temperature environments [ 34 – 36 ]. At high 
temperatures, the deformation behavior of alloys changes significantly 
in terms of dislocation movement and the morphology of the strength -
ening phase, which consequently influences the deformation mechanism 
of the alloy. Li et al. [ 37 ]reported that specimens fabricated using 
electron beam selective melting (EBSM) exhibited a transition of 
dominant deformation mechanisms from dislocation shearing of γ ′ 
phases to Orowan bypassing of γ ′ phases as the tensile test temperature 
increased. The mechanical properties of alloys used for long-term 
high-temperature service are closely related to the thermal stability of 
their microstructure. Abedi et al. [ 38 , 39 ] identified several factors that 
affect the thermal stability of alloys, including the segregation of 
insoluble atoms at grain boundaries, the morphology of carbides, and 
the interaction between γ ′ phases and dislocations. In addition, the au -
thors have successfully predicted the change in high-temperature flow 
behavior of additively manufactured alloys through computational 
simulation [ 40 , 41 ]. However, the specimens fabricated by SLM exhibit 
small-sized grain structures and fine γ ′ phases, and the microscopic 
deformation mechanism at elevated temperatures has not been sys -
temically investigated. Therefore, researching the deformation behavior 
of the IN 738LC superalloy at different temperatures and analyzing its 
fracture mechanism will establish a scientific foundation for the utili -
zation of SLM-IN 738LC superalloy components in high-temperature 
service conditions. 
In this study, defect-free samples were prepared using well-designed 
scanning strategies and laser process parameters. The as-built and HT 
samples were analyzed and then tensile tested at room temperature, and 
room temperature plasticity of as-built samples is higher than similar 
studies. This paper focuses on the evolution of the microstructure of the 
SLM-IN 738LC alloy after heat treatment, analyses the intrinsic 
connection between the room-temperature mechanical properties of the 
HT samples and their microstructures. and the optimal HT regime is 
determined on the basis of the microstructure and tensile properties. The 
mechanical properties of heat-treated SLM-IN738LC alloy samples at 
high temperatures were investigated comprehensively for the first time. 
The interaction mechanism between γ ′ and dislocations of the alloy at 
high temperatures was also analyzed and the relationship between the 
microstructure and the mechanical properties of the alloy at high tem -
peratures was illustrated. This study strongly supports the optimization 
of organization and property improvement of SLM-IN 738LC alloy. It 
also provides a foundation for service studies of SLM-IN 738LC alloy 
parts at high temperatures.

◦

4. Discussion 
4.1. Strengthening mechanisms of the as-built and HT samples 
To study the deformation mechanism of the as-built and HT samples 
at room temperature (23 
◦
C), TEM was performed on the deformed 
samples at 23 
◦
C ( Fig. 14 ). Fig. 14 (a1) shows a cellular structure that 
maintains a consistent size after deformation, and the as-built sample 
exhibits the formation of numerous dislocation slip bands in the close-

contains stacking faults that are distributed along the close-packed di -

cellular structure of the as-built sample. The high strength of the as-built

When the dislocations exhibit a shearing mechanism, the γ ′ phases 
contribute to strengthening in the following ways [ 60 ]: (1) coherent 
interface strengthening due to the different lattice misfit between the γ 
matrix and γ ′ phases, with Nb, Ti, and Ta being the main contributing 
elements; (2) elastic modulus strengthening due to the difference in 
elastic modulus between γ matrix and γ ′ phases; (3) the difference in 
stacking fault energy between the γ matrix and γ ′ phases leads to 
stacking fault strengthening, with the main contributions coming from 
Cr and Co; (4) dislocation shearing increases the surface area of the γ ′ 
phase, resulting in surface strengthening; and (5) dispersion strength -
ening due to the dislocation bending increases the slip resistance. In 
summary, the strengthening outcome is collectively affected by the five 
mechanisms of γ ′ phases, thereby enhancing the tensile strength.

5. Conclusion 
Defect-free SLM-IN 738LC superalloy samples were fabricated using 
the SLM technique. Five different HT regimes were employed for the as- 
built samples. The as-built and HT samples were analyzed and subse -
quently tensile tested at room temperature. The relationship between 
the microstructure and mechanical performance of the SLM-IN 738LC 
superalloy is discussed. Based on the microstructure and tensile prop -
erties, the optimal HT regime was determined. The mechanical prop -
erties of the HT samples at high temperatures were comprehensively 
investigated, and the temperature-dependent deformation mechanism 
was analyzed in detail. The following conclusions were drawn: 
(1) The as-built samples exhibited a cellular structure with fine car -
bides and γ ′ phases at the boundary and nano-size γ ′ phases at the 
interior, resulting in good deformation resistance. In addition, the 
deviated atoms at the boundary have a pinning effect that 
effectively stabilizes the dislocation network. This allows the 
cellular structure to maintain a constant size during the plastic 
deformation process, resulting in stabilized plastic deformation
