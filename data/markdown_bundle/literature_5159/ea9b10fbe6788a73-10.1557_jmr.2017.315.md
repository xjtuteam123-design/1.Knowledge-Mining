Lisa Patricia Freund, a) Sven Giese, Daniel Schwimmer, Heinz Werner Höppel, Steffen Neumeier, and

Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Friedrich-Alexander Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg, Institute I,

(Received 29 May 2017; accepted 12 July 2017)
This paper presents the high temperature yield and fatigue strength as well as thermophysical
properties of two polycrystalline wrought c/c9 Co-base superalloys developed for application in
a temperature regime above 700 °C. The alloys CoWAlloy1 (Co42Ni32Cr12Al6W3Ti2.5Ta1.5 1
Si,C,B,Zr,Hf) and CoWAlloy2 (Co41Ni32Cr12Al9W5 1 Ti,Ta,Si,C,B,Zr,Hf) exhibited solidus
temperatures of 1070 °C and 1030 °C, respectively, and a c9 fraction of about 50%. CoWAlloy2
displayed a rather high Young ’s modulus of 250 GPa. The two Co-base superalloys showed
a good high temperature strength exceeding Ni-base disc alloys U720Li and Waspaloy at
temperatures above 800 °C. When comparing the yield strength from tensile and compression
tests, no asymmetry could be found. The low-cycle fatigue life of CoWAlloy2 at a total strain
amplitude of 0.5% is similar to that of U720Li and Waspaloy (about 370 cycles, R 5 /C01).
During long-term aging for 1024 h at 750 °C, no additional phases were formed and the room
temperature hardness barely changed.
I. INTRODUCTION
Due to the pending need to increase the high temper-
ature capabilities of materials, several new polycrystalline
Ni-base superalloys have been developed in recent years
for increased temperature capability, strength, and dura-
bility.
1,2 These alloys are mostly produced by powder
metallurgy, because the rather high c9 content with
correspondingly high c9-solvus temperatures makes forg-
ing and achieving homogeneous microstructures very
difﬁcult. Co-base alloys provide a high potential for an
application as wrought alloys due to the large gap
between solidus and c9-solvus temperature along with
ah i g h c9 volume fraction.
3 However, potential wrought
alloys have to not only provide a good processability, but at
the same time exhibit good high temperature strength, high
creep resistance, high low-cycle fatigue resistance, oxida-
tion and corrosion resistance, and high temperature stability.
Compared to the simple ternary and quaternary Co–Al–W–
X systems, the CoWAlloys contain additional alloying
elements to improve the oxidation resistance and the grain
boundary strength and to tailor the microstructure and the
mechanical properties.
4 In this paper, two alloys
CoWAlloy1 and CoWAlloy2 are presented in detail,
focusing on mechanical prop erties at high temperatures
including workability, creep properties, tensile strength, and
ﬁrst fatigue results. Additionally, thermophysical properties
are listed and the long-term stability is addressed in terms of
microstructural stability and hardness.

III. RESULTS & DISCUSSION
A. Physical properties
Table II summarizes some general physical properties
of the two investigated CoWAlloys in comparison to
U720Li and Waspaloy. The c9-solvus temperatures are
lower than those of U720Li, while the solidus temper-
atures are higher (more than 100 °C). This large
difference between c9 solvus and solidus/incipient melt-
ing temperature is bene ﬁcial for cast-and-wrought alloys,
because it provides a large processing window. Forging
temperatures close to the solidus temperature bear the risk
of incipient melting due to adiabatic heating during
deformation, especially in regions with macrosegrega-
tion. However, at forging temperatures clearly below the
c9 solvus, the alloys exhibit very high strength due to the
precipitation of c9. The forging process has to take place
at temperatures carefully ba lanced between these two
limits. The forging of alloys with small processing
windows, like U720Li, is therefore rather dif ﬁcult,
especially for larger billet sizes.

C. Microstructure
After recrystallization, both alloys exhibit a slightly
bimodal grain size with a median of 4.3 lm for
CoWAlloy1 and 8.2 lm for CoWAlloy2, derived from
the EBSD images in Figs. 3(a) and 3(d). A fraction of
unrecrystallized grains remains in center parts of the
rolled bar. The two-step annealing heat treatment leads to
a trimodal c9 structure, with a small fraction of inhomo-
geneously distributed primary c9 precipitates at the grain
boundaries as well as inside the grains and about 50% of
secondary c9 with an average diameter of 60 nm [Figs. 3(b)
and 3(e)]. A small average size of the secondary precip-
itates below 100 nm has been shown to result in a high
strength
15–17 and to promote the formation of extended
stacking faults during deformation.15,17 A third distribution
of very small tertiary c9 precipitates can be identiﬁed from
the TEM images, especially in CoWAlloy2 [Figs. 3(c)
and 3(f)]. These precipitates could additionally increase the
yield strength and creep strength.
16,18 The different c9
morphologies relate to differences in lattice mis ﬁt. The
cuboidal shape of the c9 precipitates in CoWAlloy1
suggests a larger lattice misﬁt, as was found for quaternary
Co–Al–W alloys containing Ti and Ta. 19,20 CoWAlloy2
exhibits more spherical c9 precipitates similar to U720Li
and Waspaloy, so the mis ﬁt has to be rather small. For
U720Li, a mis ﬁt of about 0.13% was published, 21 so
a similar value might be expected for CoWAlloy2.

IV. CONCLUSIONS
Two new polycrystalline c/c9 Co-base superalloys
were compared to Ni-base disc alloys U720Li and
Waspaloy in terms of physical properties, microstructure,
high temperature strength, fatigue properties, and long-
term stability. They exhibited similar microstructures but
have a slightly lower c9-solvus temperature and high
solidus temperatures, therefore possessing large process-
ing windows. The alloys were stable for 1000 h at 750 °C
in air and showed no sign of TCP phase formation.
Regarding the high temperature strength, the CoWAlloys
showed excellent properties at temperatures above
750 °C, where CoWAlloy1 exceeded CoWAlloy2,
U720Li, and Waspaloy. No tension –compression asym-
metry was found for CoWAlloy2. The ﬁrst fatigue results
indicated that CoWAlloy2 achieves a similar fatigue life
and strength as U720Li, while showing a higher ductility.
The Young ’s modulus of CoWAlloy2 is higher than the
ones of U720Li and Waspaloy, while the CTE ranges in
between the Ni-base reference alloys.
