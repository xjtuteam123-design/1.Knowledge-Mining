ABSTRACT 
Obtaining a reliable NiTi – Ti6Al4V(TC4) heterogeneous alloy component exhibiting excellent strength and 
elasticity via integrating distinct material benefits can provide design flexibility in multifunctional device 
fabrication. However, the fabrication of this materials couple is challenging due to the development of excessive 
Ti
2
Ni intermetallic compound. Inspired by the bioheterogeneous layered structure, Nb interlayer was applied for 
the wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) of NiTi and TC4 to restrict the mixing of alloying elements on both 
sides. However, the strength of NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy component was limited by pure Nb characteristics when the 
NiTi and TC4 was completely separated by the unmolten Nb interlayer. In this work, the tensile strength 
perpendicular to the interface of NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy component was enhanced to introduce appropriate amount 
Ni and Ti in Nb interlayer by adjusting the composite WAAM process at the expense of Ti
2
Ni. Microstructure 
evolution, composition change and mechanical properties of composite WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy component 
were investigated. It was shown that the compression strength and fracture strain perpendicular to the interface 
of WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 sample was 1549.0 ± 17 MPa and 22.3 ± 8 %, and the compressive strength can reach 
63.6 % and 94.5 % of WAAM NiTi alloy and TC4 alloy, respectively. The composite WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 sample 
maintained 65.3 % recovery rate after ten compression cycles, which confirmed that partial superelasticity was 
maintained in the WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 multilayer structure. This fact further proved that WAAM can be suc -
cessfully applied to deposit shape memory/superelastic alloys. The use of the Nb interlayer enhanced the tensile 
strength perpendicular to the interface to 460.7 ± 16 MPa. The ultimate tensile strength perpendicular to the 
interface of composite WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 sample can reach 72.0 % and 60.0 % of WAAM NiTi alloy and TC4 
alloy, respectively. The formation of NiTi, TiNb and β -Nb phases in Nb interlayer and the network distribution of 
Ti
2
Ni and TiNb phases explained the improved mechanical performance of the composite WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 
alloy component. 
1. Introduction 
NiTi alloys are widely used in aerospace, biomedical and other ap -
plications due to its excellent superelasticity (SE), shape memory effects 
(SME) and high damping capability as well as good biocompatibility 
[ 1 – 3 ]. However, NiTi alloys are often expected to be joined with other 
engineering alloys to achieve unique but complementary properties in a 
single structural component in order to adapt to complex service envi -
ronments [ 4 – 6 ]. Ti6Al4V (TC4) alloys have attracted attention in 
numerous industries, such as aerospace, chemical and petroleum, 
medical and architectural industries owing to its low density, high 
specific strength, good corrosion resistance, low thermal conductivity, 
and good biocompatibility [ 7 – 9 ]. The combination of NiTi alloy and TC4 
alloy will allow the functional advantages of both alloys to be integrated

into a single component, which will greatly benefit the design and 
production of multifunctional alloy components. Currently, dissimilar 
metals are mostly joined by brazing [ 10 ], laser welding [ 11 ], electron 
beam welding [ 12 ], ultrasonic welding [ 13 ] and friction stir welding 
[ 14 ]. However, due to the poor machinability of NiTi alloys and TC4 
alloys, most of the raw materials used for welding are sheets and wires. 
Cumbersome machining is also required for customizing components 
that create complex geometries. 
Additive manufacturing (AM) has changed the landscape of 
manufacturing processes, enabling the fabrication of complex geomet -
rically shaped alloy components with a certain degree of precision [ 15 , 
16 ]. Among many metal additive manufacturing technologies, wire arc 
additive manufacturing (WAAM) is a very promising approach [ 17 ]. 
WAAM technology utilizes an electric arc as a heat source and offers 
significant advantages over traditional manufacturing methods [ 18 ]. 
The technology has the potential to produce fully dense and large-scale 
parts at relatively low molding costs and significant manufacturing ef -
ficiencies. Notably, the use of wire feedstock offers significant economic 
benefits, as the cost of wire is typically much lower than an equivalent 
weight of metal powder. In particular, multi-wire arc additive 
manufacturing (MWAAM) allows the use of multiple wires to create 
composite structures and multi-material parts with customized material 
compositions [ 19 – 22 ]. This advancement opens up new possibilities for 
improved performance, better material utilization, and increased design 
flexibility. By printing specific materials at specific locations on indi -
vidual alloy components, controlled integrated multi-material additive 
manufacturing of metals can be realized to meet a wide range of per -
formance needs, leading to groundbreaking engineering applications for 
joining dissimilar metals. 
Despite the many advantages of MWAAM technology, there are still 
inherent challenges and limitations in trying to successfully realize in -
tegrated additive manufacturing of NiTi/TC4 alloy component. The 
most important point is that the direct joining of NiTi to TC4 generates a 
large number of brittle phases dominated by Ti
2
Ni, which in turn leads 
to solidification cracks and premature failure of NiTi/TC4 heteroge -
neous alloy components [ 23 ]. Crysomallon squamiferum has evolved a 
multilayer heterostructure in order to resist the attack of natural en -
emies, and this shell structure has excellent mechanical properties [ 24 ]. 
The specific structural analysis of the snail shell has been described in 
detail in previous report [ 21 ]. Inspired by the heterogeneous layered 
structure of shell of snail, the use of an intermediate material transition 
is expected to realize the connection between NiTi and TC4 alloys. 
To date, some efforts have been made to reduce or avoid the large 
number of brittle phases produced when NiTi is connected to TC4 by 
adding intermediate layers. Most of the previously reported work used 
pure materials as intermediate layers, such as Al [ 13 ], Zr [ 25 ], Nb [ 26 ], 
Pd [ 23 ], Cu [ 27 ], etc. However, the strength of these pure metals is 
limited (about 300 MPa). As a result, the properties of the interlayer 
material will affect the properties of the overall heterogeneous alloy 
components [ 28 ]. This phenomenon had been confirmed in the work 
reported by J.P. Oliveira et al. [ 26 ]. However, zhou et al. [ 29 ] reported 
the effect of Nb interlayer on the mechanical properties of NiTiNb/TC4 
heterogeneous alloy component. They found that the tensile strength of 
NiTiNb/TC4 alloy component could reach 740 MPa by introducing TiNb 
phase and NiTi phase into the Nb interlayer, and the strength could 
reach 82 % of the base material TC4 alloy. Moreover, they also reported 
that the content of Ti
2
Ni brittle intermetallic compounds could be 
significantly reduced by replacing the Ti
2
Ni brittle phase with TiNb and 
NiTi phases. In particular, Nb filler can significantly increase the 
strength of the weld when it acted as a consumption rather than a barrier 
in the weld of NiTiNb alloy and TC4 alloy. Therefore, the results of their 
experiment demonstrated that tuning the phase composition of the 
interlayer improves the strength of the interlayer. 
In our previous work, TC4-Nb-NiTi alloy component with pure Nb as 
the interlayer were prepared by the MWAAM process using Tungsten 
Inert Gas (TIG) as the heat source [ 22 ]. The maximum tensile strength of 
MWAAM TC4-Nb-NiTi alloy was 340 MPa. There are two main reasons 
that affect the premature failure of alloy components: (1) High melting 
point Nb has poor plasticity in the process of Tig additive manufacturing 
and is prone to surface defects, which affects the metallurgical bonding 
with NiTi alloy. (2) The intermediate layer of pure Nb is limited in 
strength, which affects the overall strength of the alloy component. In 
order to further improve the performance of TC4-Nb-NiTi heteroge -
neous alloy components, we still selected Nb as the interlayer to try to 
connect NiTi alloy and TC4 alloy. The design structure of the alloy 
component is shown in Fig. 1 a. Moreover, we have made targeted im -
provements on the basis of previous work: (1) Considering the CMT 
(Cold Metal Transfer) WAAM technique with Metal Inert Gas (MIG) as 
the heat source has relatively small heat input and good printability 
when printing high melting point materials, NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy com -
ponents were prepared by Tig-Mig composite WAAM process. The NiTi 
and TC4 layers were deposited by Tig-WAAM and the high melting point 
Nb interlayer by Mig-WAAM. (2) The WAAM process parameters of the 
interfacial deposition layer were adjusted to control the element 
composition of Nb interlayer. It is expected that other elements can be 
introduced in the Nb interlayer by the effects of intense Marangoni 
convection and element diffusion to improve the strength of 
NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy component [ 30 ], as shown in Fig. 1 b. 
Hence, in this study, a Nb interlayer was selected to manufacture bio- 
inspired layered NiTi/TC4 alloy component. Optimized and regulated 
the distribution of elements in the interlayer through TIG-MIG com -
posite WAAM technology, promoting the formation of NiTi and TiNb 
phases in the interlayer. Combined with the above optimization strate -
gies, defect-free NiTi/TC4 heterogeneous alloys of withstanding a 
maximum of strain of 6.1 ± 5 % and a maximum of stress of 460.7 ± 16 
MPa were created for the first time. The control of metallurgical re -
actions in the interlayer region and microstructure evolution and asso -
ciated mechanical performance of the NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy were 
comprehensively detailed. This work would provide some first-hand 
observations from WAAM NiTi and TC4. 
2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Materials preparation 
In this work, TC4, NiTi and pure Nb ( > 99.95 %) wires with a 
diameter of 1.2 mm were used as raw materials to fabricate the bionic 
layered heterogeneous alloy. Details of the chemical composition of 
TC4, NiTi and Nb wires are shown in Table 1 . NiTi alloy plate with a size 
of 200 mm × 200 mm × 15 mm were used as the build substrate. 
2.2. WAAM process 
The experimental setup in this work was developed by the composite 
MWAAM system composed of WAAM system and CMT welding machine 
(Fronius TPS 4000). The WAAM system consisted of a 6-axis ABB robot 
arm with a IRC5 controller, a shielding gas unit and wire feeders. The 
other details were introduced in our previous publication [ 19 , 31 ]. The 
CMT welding machine consisted an industrial robot, a Fronius CMT 
welder (Fronius TPS 4000), a working platform and VR 7000 CMT wire 
feeding system. The schematic of composite WAAM system is presented 
in Fig. 1 c. The bionic layered heterogeneous NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy 
component with dimensions of 80 × 8 × 46 mm

NiTi substrate by composite WAAM. The diagram of the composite 
WAAM process is shown in Fig. 1 c. As shown in Fig. 1 c, the

TIG-WAAM. NiTi alloy repeatedly deposited NiTi substrate to a final

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Printability analysis of Nb layer fabricated by CMT 
In order to improve the formability of the CMT-prepared Nb inter -
layer, a layer of Nb was deposited on the TC4 substrate and the NiTi 
substrate respectively with the optimized process parameters to observe 
the wettability of the deposited layer as well as the elemental distribu -
tions in different regions. Fig. 2 a – b shows the cross-sectional 
morphology of CMT-prepared Nb on TC4 substrate and NiTi substrate 
respectively. The cross-sectional widths of the two Nb deposited layers 
were similar, while the deposited layer showed a greater height when 
Nb was deposited on the TC4 substrate, as shown in Fig. 2 a – b. The 
wettability of the Nb deposited layer on the two alloy materials was one 
of the main factors affecting the formability of the Nb interlayer. In 
general, the wettability of WAAM was related to the combined effect of 
height, width and wetting angle [ 33 ]. The combination of a smaller 
wetting angle, higher width and lower height resulted in better wetta -
bility. Therefore, combining the morphology, height, width and wetting 
angle of the Nb deposited layer, it can be seen that Nb showed better 
formability when deposited on NiTi substrate. In addition, SEM 
morphology observations were performed on the regions A-F in 
Fig. 2 a – b, as shown in Fig. 2 A-F. The microstructure was more homo -
geneous in the D-F regions compared to the A-C regions. The results 
showed a more homogeneous diffusion of elements between the sub -
strate and the deposited layer when Nb was deposited on NiTi substrate 
compared to when Nb was deposited on TC4 substrate. In addition, EDS 
tests were performed on the top regions of both deposited layers, and the 
results were shown in Fig. 2 A and D. The Nb contents of regions A and D 
were 92.76 % and 65.19 %, respectively. The reason for this phenom -
enon was that the melting point of TC4 alloy was higher than that of NiTi 
alloy, the melting degree of NiTi substrate was higher when the same 
heat input was deposited on the two alloy substrates. This result proved 
that Nb deposited on NiTi alloys can better utilize the dilution rate to 
introduce the alloying elements in NiTi into the interlayer. Therefore, 
the deposition sequence of NiTi → Nb → TC4 in the preparation of 
NiTi – Nb-TC4 heterogeneous bionic alloy component can better realize 
the design of this paper. 
3.2. Microstructure evolution and phase constitution 
As shown in Fig. 3 a, NiTi – Nb-TC4 heterogeneous alloy component 
was successfully prepared on NiTi substrate by composite WAAM tech -
nique. The width of the Nb interlayer was about 1.2 mm. Good metal -
lurgical bonding was formed between NiTi, Nb and TC4 deposited 
layers, and no defects such as cracks, porosities were observed, as shown 
in Fig. 3 a. The results of element line scanning showed that all elements 
showed continuous transition between the deposition layers without any 
interruption of the detection signal. The elements at the interfaces of 
different alloys transitioned in the form of gradient distribution, which 
enhanced the bonding strength between the interfaces. Moreover, the 
detection signals of Ni element and Ti element can be detected in the Nb 
intermediate layer. This indicated that the control of the diffusion dis -
tance of elements can be realized by adjusting the process parameters of 
composite WAAM. Based on the above results, it can be learned that the 
Ni and Ti elements were successfully introduced in the Nb interlayer 
through the adjustment and design of the preparation process.

Superelasticity was related to the stress-induced martensitic trans -
formation and its inverse strain [ 43 ]. Cyclic compression tests were 
investigated to evaluate the superelasticity of the WAAM NiTi and 
NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy samples. The results were shown in Fig. 12 c – d. In the 
first cycle, significant irrecoverable strains were observed in both sam -
ples, which were caused by plastic deformation and residual martensite 
changed during compression loading. The irrecoverable strains of 
WAAM NiTi and NiTi – Nb-TC4 samples in the first cycle were 0.42 % and 
1.17 %, respectively. The irrecoverable strain gradually increased as the 
cycling proceeded. The irrecoverable strains of WAAM NiTi and 
NiTi – Nb-TC4 samples in the tenth cycle were 0.43 % and 2.08 %, 
respectively. The results showed that both WAAM NiTi and 
NiTi – Nb-TC4 samples in this study had high superelastic properties. In 
addition, it can be seen through Fig. 12 c – d that the recovery rate 
gradually reached a steady state in the last five cycles. This phenomenon 
can be attributed to the fact that part of the martensite is retained during 
the unloading of the alloy sample due to the surrounding stress field and 
dislocation [ 44 ]. With the increase of the number of cycles, the residual 
martensite content gradually stabilized, resulting in a stable recovery 
rate. Moreover, the formation of precipitated phase will change the 
content ratio of Ni/Ti in the matrix, thus also affecting the superelastic 
effect [ 43 ]. In this study, the superelasticity was reduced compared to 
WAAM NiTi sample because the NiTi – Nb-TC4 sample was a heteroge -
neous alloy component. However, the composite WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 
sample maintained 65.3 % recovery rate after ten compression cycles, 
which indicated that it also had certain superelastic properties. This fact 
further proved that WAAM can be successfully applied to deposit shape 
memory/superelastic alloys.

NiTi in the same structure and new insights for dissimilar metal additive 
manufacturing, main conclusions were summarized as follows: 
(1) A reliable NiTi -TC4 bionic heterogeneous alloy component was 
obtained by utilizing a Nb interlayer. The near NiTi side and 
middle region of the Nb interlayer were composed of TiNi, NiTi 
and β -Nb, while the near TC4 side was composed of TiNi, NiTi, 
β -Nb and Ti
2
Ni. These phase distribution results were supported 
by the XRD and EBSD phase analysis results. Engaging a Nb 
interlayer constrained the formation of brittle Ti2Ni phase. 
(2) The phase evolution process from NiTi to TC4 side was as follows:

microstructure near TC4 side of Nb interlayer. The strong texture 
was formed in the Nb interlayer region as determined by EBSD

likely to occur in near TC4 side of Nb interlayer due to the for -
mation of Ti
2

(3) The addition of Nb interlayer played a transitional role in NiTi 
and TC4 alloys, reducing the microhardness gap between NiTi 
and TC4. The average elastic modulus of NiTi, Nb interlayer and 
TC4 region were 66.92 GPa, 105.26 GPa, and 115.61 GPa, 
respectively. The NiTi region of the composite WAAM NiTi-TC4 
heterogeneous alloy component had the highest resistance to 
plastic deformation, followed by the TC4 region and the Nb 
interlayer region according to the indentation evaluation. 
(4) The compression strength and fracture strain perpendicular to the 
interface of WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy component was 1549.0 ±
17 MPa and 22.3 ± 8 %, and the ultimate compressive strength 
perpendicular to the interface can reach 63.6 % and 94.5 % of 
WAAM NiTi alloy and TC4 alloy, respectively. The composite 
WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 sample maintained 65.3 % recovery rate 
after ten compression cycles, which confirmed that partial 
superelasticity was maintained in the WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 
multilayer structure. This fact further proved that WAAM can be 
successfully applied to deposit shape memory/superelastic 
alloys. 
(5) The use of the Nb interlayer enhanced the tensile strength 
perpendicular to the interface to 460.7 ± 16 MPa. The tensile 
strength perpendicular to the interface of composite WAAM 
NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy was improved by 53.3 % when compared to 
the laser welding NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy sample. The tensile strength 
perpendicular to the interface of composite WAAM NiTi – Nb-TC4 
alloy was higher than the previously reported NiTi-TC4 alloy 
components. Although the Ti
2
Ni brittle phase was formed in Nb 
interlayer, the fracture pattern of the NiTi – Nb-TC4 alloy sample 
exhibited ductile trails, which complied the higher ductility 
measured during tensile testing.
