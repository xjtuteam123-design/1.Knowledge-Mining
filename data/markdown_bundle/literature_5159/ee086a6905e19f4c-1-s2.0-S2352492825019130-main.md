ABSTRACT
FePt alloys with L10-ordered structures are promising candidates for next-generation hypersonic thermal-end 
components due to their excellent high-temperature stability and oxidation resistance. However, the lack of 
atomic scale understanding of their deformation and failure mechanisms under extreme thermal environments 
hinders their practical application. Conventional interatomic potentials such as Embedded Atom Method (EAM) 
face limitations in accurately describing high-temperature phase transitions, dislocation evolution, and interfa -
cial behavior, making them insufficient for predictive modeling under complex conditions. The Deep Potential 
(DP) method, combining first-principles accuracy with molecular dynamics efficiency, offers a powerful alter -
native. In this work, a DP model for FePt alloys is constructed over a wide temperature range (300 – 1200 K) using 
first-principles calculations and the DP-GEN active learning framework. The model is rigorously validated and 
used to investigate high-temperature mechanical properties. Results show that FePt alloys achieve a tensile 
strength of 18 GPa at 1200 K, with a 35-percent-point lower strength degradation rate compared to MEAM 
predictions. The DP model also predicts a fracture strain of 32 %, significantly higher than traditional potentials 
and consistent with experimental ductility. This work provides new atomic-level insight into FePt alloy me -
chanics and supports their optimization for extreme thermal environments.
1. Introduction
The increasing demand for high thrust-to-weight ratio engines in the 
aerospace industry has accelerated the search for advanced structural 
materials capable of withstanding extreme thermomechanical environ -
ments [1 – 3] . Among them, FePt alloys with an L1 ₀ -ordered structure 
have emerged as promising candidates for next-generation turbine 
components due to their exceptional high-temperature strength reten -
tion, oxidation resistance, and order-induced strengthening effects [4, 
5] . However, under elevated temperatures and high-pressure condi -
tions, microstructural evolution and changes in chemical order can 
significantly degrade the mechanical performance, compromising 
component reliability [6 – 8] . Understanding the formation and evolution 
of irradiation-induced defects and their impact on coupled 
thermal-mechanical behavior is therefore critical for both performance 
prediction and alloy design optimization.
Computational methods play a central role in evaluating the 
performance of Fe-Pt-based alloys under such extreme conditions [9, 
10] . Density functional theory (DFT), grounded in quantum mechanics, 
enables accurate modeling of electronic structures and interatomic in -
teractions, providing insights into defect energetics and phase stability 
[11,12] . Prior DFT studies have shown, for example, that increasing Pt 
content decreases the vacancy – Pt binding energy, offering mechanistic 
clues into radiation damage evolution [13] . Nevertheless, the scalability 
of DFT is inherently limited by its high computational cost, restricting 
simulations to systems of only a few hundred atoms, and making it 
unsuitable for modeling large-scale microstructures such as grain 
boundaries [14,15] . In contrast, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations 
can model systems containing millions of atoms, making them 
well-suited for studying microstructural responses to irradiation and 
high temperatures [16,17] . For instance, S.I. Konorev et al. used MD to 
investigate the vacancy diffusion behavior of Fe/Pt atoms in L10 - FePt 
alloy within the temperature range of 1300 – 1600 K [18] . However, the 
reliability of MD largely hinges on the quality of empirical potentials,

The emergence of deep potential molecular dynamics (DPMD) offers 
a compelling solution to this trade-off. By integrating machine learning 
with DFT data, DPMD combines quantum-level accuracy with the scal -
ability of classical MD, solved the shortcomings of traditional DFT and 
MD methods in terms of computational efficiency and accuracy [22 – 25] .
Zhang et al. developed DPMD models that maintain ab initio accu -
racy across large atomic systems, demonstrating clear advantages in 
modeling magnetic Fe-based materials [26] . Compared to other 
state-of-the-art approaches such as NequIP, which uses graph neural 
networks to preserve crystal symmetry but underperforms in modeling 
magnetic interactions [27] , and M3GNet, which offers good general -
ization but lacks accuracy in Fe-based alloys, DPMD stands out [28] . 
CHGNet, though effective in systems involving charge transfer, remains 
relatively untested for metallic alloys [29] . Veeresham et al. further 
confirmed that DPMD significantly outperforms other models in simu -
lating fcc metals [30] . Despite these advances, systematic studies of FePt 
alloys using DPMD remain scarce.
Here, a ’ deep learning – multiscale simulation ’ framework is estab -
lished to investigate the high-temperature mechanical behavior of FePt 
alloys. Using data from first-principles calculations, a temperature- 
robust deep potential model is developed via the DP-GEN active 
learning workflow. Molecular dynamics simulations based on this model 
show high accuracy in predicting temperature-dependent mechanical 
and lattice dynamical properties, with tensile strength errors below 5 % 
and phonon dispersion errors under 3 %. Our results reveal a 40 – 50 % 
reduction in tensile strength as temperature increases from 300 K to 
1200 K, with local Fe – Pt short-range order playing a critical role in 
suppressing lattice softening. Notably, even at temperatures above 
1500 K, Fe – Pt pairwise correlations persist, enabling structural stability 
under severe thermal perturbations. These findings provide crucial 
theoretical insights for optimizing the high-temperature performance of 
turbine components and advancing the design of next-generation heat- 
resistant alloys.
2. Computational method
2.1. DFT calculation
DFT calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simu -
lation Package (VASP) with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof functional, 
based on the generalized gradient approximation, and the projector 
augmented-wave method for the core region [31,32] . Through conver -
gence testing, the cutoff energy was set to 500 eV, and a Γ -centered 
k-point grid with a spacing of 0.15 Å ⁻ ¹ was used to sample the Brillouin 
zone [33] . For details on the key DFT settings (cell size, supercell 
composition, spin polarization treatment, magnetic effects, and k-point 
sampling strategy), please refer to Supporting Information S4 . The 
self-consistent electronic energy convergence criterion was set to 10 ⁻⁶ 
eV, and the force convergence threshold was set to 0.002 eV/Å. After 
performing DFT calculations for all candidate configurations, approxi -
mately 50,000 data points were collected for training the DP model. The 
energy-volume curve and stacking fault energy were also computed. The 
phonon spectrum was obtained using the Phonopy program in combi -
nation with VASP, employing the finite displacement method [34] . 
Meanwhile, the calculation methods for the DFT phonon spectrum at 
high temperatures and M3GNet are detailed in Supporting Information 
S5 .
2.2. MD simulation
The LAMMPS simulation package was employed to carry out both 
molecular dynamics and static computations [35] . In the MD simula -
tions, the time step was set to 0.001 ps, and periodic boundary condi -
tions were applied in all three directions [36] . The Velocity Verlet 
algorithm was used to update the position, velocity, and acceleration of 
each atom. The conjugate gradient method was employed to minimize 
the energy, with energy and force tolerances set to 10 ⁻⁷ eV and 10 ⁻⁷ 
eV/Å, respectively, to stabilize the structure [37] . Optimization was 
performed prior to the loading simulation. Under the canonical 
ensemble, the model was heated from room temperature to 4000 K at a 
rate of 20 K/ps and maintained at 4000 K for 100 ps to simulate the 
melting point of the FePt alloy [38] . To compute the stress-strain curve 
of the FePt alloy at different temperatures, the initial structure was first 
optimized, relaxed, and equilibrated for 30 ps under constant pressure 
and temperature (300 K, 0 bar). Then, stretching was performed under 
the microcanonical ensemble, with temperature control via the velocity 
rescaling method [39] . The stretching was applied along the x-direction 
at a strain rate of 5 × 10²/ps. To highlight the advantages of employing 
the DP potential in MD simulations, a comparison was made with results 
obtained using an empirical potential. For a detailed description of the 
DeepMD model architecture sensitivity and hyperparameter optimiza -
tion, please refer to Supporting Information S6 .
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Construction and validation process of the deep potential model for 
FePt alloys
DPMD method is a machine learning-based approach for construct -
ing interatomic potential energy surfaces using deep neural networks 
[40] . Its core concept lies in learning the nonlinear mapping between the 
local atomic environments and the total energy of the system ( E =

first-principles accuracy [41,42] . Trained on high-fidelity data from 
DFT, DPMD enables accurate modeling of interatomic interactions in 
complex multicomponent alloy systems such as FePt without compro -
mising computational precision. Compared to traditional empirical po -
tentials, DPMD offers superior scalability and quantum-level accuracy, 
making it feasible to simulate thermally activated dynamical behaviors 
from nanometer to micrometer scales under high-temperature condi -
tions [43 – 45] .
Based on this approach, this study developed a FePt-specific deep 
potential model via the three-stage workflow shown in Fig. 1 , including 
initial dataset construction, iterative optimization, and active learning- 
driven sample expansion. The initial dataset includes a diverse set of 
representative crystalline and defect configurations of Fe, Pt, and their 
alloys, providing a comprehensive structural representation for model 
training (see “ Computational Methods ” for details). During model 
optimization, this work utilized the DP-GEN automated framework 
coupled with stochastic gradient descent to iteratively optimize neural 
network parameters by minimizing a composite loss function of energy 
and atomic forces. To enhance model generalization across a broad 
configurational space, the descriptor distribution was analyzed to 
identify underrepresented structures, with new configurations selected 
from DPMD trajectories. These configurations were subsequently veri -
fied using DFT and added to the training set. This iterative strategy 
significantly improved both predictive accuracy and transferability, of -
fering a practical computational pathway to study the microstructural 
evolution of FePt alloys under extreme high-temperature environments. 
The method shows strong potential in the design of high-temperature- 
resistant alloys for aerospace engine applications, particularly in eval -
uating thermal stability, defect dynamics, and mechanical response. For 
DP model descriptors and related details, please refer to supporting in -
formation S7 .

3.2. Fundamental crystallographic properties of FePt alloy
In order to preliminarily assess the accuracy of the developed DP 
model, this study conducted a detailed comparative analysis between 
the E (energy) - V (volume) curves predicted by DP-based MD simula -
tions and those obtained from DFT-based first-principles calculations. As 
shown in Fig. 3 , the energy – volume relationships obtained by the DP 
model for Fe, Pt, and FePt crystal structures exhibit excellent agreement 
with DFT results. Within a ± 10 % volumetric strain range, the energy 
deviations remain below 3 meV/atom. Notably, near the equilibrium 
volume, the DP and DFT curves are nearly indistinguishable. This in -
dicates that the DP model accurately captures the total energy response 
to volume variations, providing a solid foundation for subsequent in -
vestigations of structural stability and phase transformations.
Further validation was performed by evaluating the crystal structure 
parameters of Fe, Pt, and FePt, as summarized in Table 1 . Compared 
with three representative EAM potentials (Zope, Farkas, and Kim) and a 
widely used machine learning potential (M3GNet), the DP model ex -
hibits significantly improved accuracy in predicting lattice constants. 
For example, the DP-predicted lattice constant of FePt is 2.76 Å, which is 
in excellent agreement with the DFT value (2.73 Å) and the 
experimental measurement (2.728 Å) [46] , yielding a deviation of only 
+ 0.05 Å. Although M3GNet also shows reasonably good performance, 
the DP model achieves even higher precision. In contrast, the EAM-Zope 
model produces a much larger error of 0.14 Å.Regarding the tetrag -
onal c/a ratio, the DP model yields a value of 1.36, which aligns well 
with both DFT (1.37) and experimental data (1.41) [46] , while the 
predictions from traditional EAM potentials vary widely, ranging from 
1.21 to 1.54. These results suggest that the DP model offers higher fi -
delity in describing lattice anisotropy.
Elastic constants serve as essential indicators of mechanical stability. 
For the FePt alloy (see Table 2 ), the DP model achieves relative errors 
below 0.8 % for C ₁₁ , C ₁₂ , C ₁₃ , and C ₄₄ when compared to DFT and 
experimental data [48] , outperforming the traditional EAM potentials. 
For instance, the EAM-Zope potential exhibits a deviation of + 2.85 % 
for C ₁₂ . Additionally, the DP model accurately reproduces the aniso -
tropic elastic response (e.g., C ₃₃ = 298.2 GPa) and shear modulus (e.g., 
C ₆₆ = 49.8 GPa), and fully satisfies the Born mechanical stability criteria 
(e.g., C ₁₁ |C ₁₂ | > 0, C ₄₄ > 0). In contrast, EAM potential tends to sys -
tematically underestimate shear moduli, potentially leading to incorrect 
assessments of mechanical stability. Overall, these results highlight the 
DP model ’ s superior accuracy and robustness in capturing elastic 
properties, underscoring its broad applicability to complex alloy 
systems.
Fig. 4 presents a comparison between the phonon dispersion re -
lations obtained from the DP model and those calculated using DFT. The 
DP model accurately reproduces the phonon dispersion characteristics 
of elemental Fe, Pt, and the FePt alloy. Specifically, for Fe, the frequency 
deviations of both acoustic and optical branches along the Γ to H path 
are below 0.5 cm ⁻ ¹ , indicating that the model effectively captures both 
long-range and short-range interatomic interactions. In the case of Pt, 
the DP model successfully captures the softening of the acoustic branch 
near the N point, demonstrating its ability to describe low-frequency 
vibrational features. In addition, as shown in Fig. S7 , the DP model 
still has excellent reproducibility for non harmonic effects at high tem -
peratures. It is particularly noteworthy that for FePt alloys, the DP 
model not only accurately reproduces the acoustic branch splitting 
caused by mass disparity (approximately 200 cm ⁻ ¹), but also captures 
the optical mode shift induced by changes in bonding stiffness. Through 
an analysis of the frequency deviation of the Γ -point LO mode, the su -
perior accuracy of the DP model is further demonstrated. Specifically, 
the frequency predicted by the DP model is 101 cm ⁻ ¹ , while the DFT 
calculated value is 105 cm ⁻ ¹ , resulting in an absolute error of 
4 cm ⁻ ¹ and a relative error of 3.9 %. This result is consistent with the 
average relative error (MRE = 3.6 %) reported in the study by Guido

To gain a deeper understanding of the microscopic deformation 
mechanisms in Fe-based alloys, this work first evaluated the DP model ’ s 
capability in describing dislocation slip barriers. Fig. 7 presents a com -
parison of generalized stacking fault energy (GSFE) profiles along the 
[110]/2 slip path for pure Fe, pure Pt, and FePt alloy, as predicted by the 
DP model and DFT calculations. The DP model successfully reproduced 
the trend of GSFE variations predicted by DFT, with the peak GSFE for 
FePt reaching 2.601 J/m² and a relative error of less than 7.7 %. 
Notably, the GSFE curve of FePt exhibits a multi-peak structure, 
reflecting the significant modulation of local slip behavior induced by 
compositional disorder. This result indicates that the DP model can 
capture the influence of alloying effects on microscopic plasticity 
mechanisms.
Building upon the validation of slip barriers, this work further 
examined the applicability of the DP model in capturing high- 
temperature diffusion behavior. Using FePt as a representative system, 
the atomic diffusion characteristics at different temperatures were sys -
tematically analyzed. Atomic diffusion plays a dominant role in high- 
temperature mechanical responses and critically affects phase trans -
formation and thermal stability in alloys; thus, its accurate character -
ization is essential for verifying the broad applicability of the DP 
potential. As shown in Fig. 8 (a), the total mean square displacement (Tot 
MSD) of the FePt alloy increases nearly linearly with time in the tem -
perature range of 1300 – 2100 K, with the slope rising with temperature, 
indicating a strong temperature dependence of atomic mobility. The 
self-diffusion coefficients obtained by linear fitting are shown in Fig. 8
(b), exhibiting a monotonic increase from 6.1 × 10 ⁻ ¹ ² m²/s at 1300 K to 
20.8 × 10 ⁻ ¹ ² m²/s at 2100 K — an increase of nearly threefold. Particu -
larly, the sharp increase in diffusion coefficients in the 1900 – 2000 K 
range coincides well with the melting region of FePt, suggesting a 
possible transition from ordered to disordered structures within this 
temperature interval.
To evaluate the performance of the DP model in predicting macro -
scopic mechanical properties, this work performed molecular dynamics 
simulations of Fe, Pt, and FePt alloys under tensile loading across a 
temperature range of 300 – 1200 K, with comparative results against the 
conventional Modified Embedded Atom Method (MEAM) potential as 
shown in Fig. 9 a-b. At room temperature (300 K), the DP model accu -
rately captured the ductile fracture behavior of all three materials (Fe, 
Pt, and FePt alloy), this is consistent with the toughness results deter -
mined using the toughness brittle transition criterion proposed by Pugh 
et al. [53] . In contrast, the MEAM potential significantly overestimated 
material strength and predicted premature brittle fracture. The DP 
model also demonstrated excellent consistency in high-temperature 
simulations, properly reproducing the thermal softening behavior of 
FePt alloy from 300 K to 1200 K ( Fig. 9 c-d). The uniformity and repre -
sentativeness of the sampled configurations across this temperature 
range are further clarified in Supporting Information S9 . Conversely, the 
MEAM model exhibited unphysical abrupt stress drops above 600 K and 
consistently predicted brittle failure at elevated temperatures. These 
comparative simulations demonstrate that the DP model provides more 
reliable predictions for plastic deformation across the entire investigated 
temperature range.
In summary, this work systematically validated the accuracy and 
transferability of the DP model in predicting multi-scale mechanical 
behavior — from microscopic slip and atomic diffusion to macroscopic 
tensile responses — thereby laying a solid foundation for its broad 
application in high-temperature alloy design, service performance pre -
diction, and mechanism-based materials modeling.
4. Conclusion
This study proposes a novel approach integrating the DP-GEN active 
learning framework with multiscale simulations to systematically 
investigate the high-temperature mechanical behavior of FePt alloys. A 
high-accuracy DP model was developed based on a high-quality first- 
principles dataset covering the temperature range of 300 – 1200 K. The 
model demonstrates significant advantages in predicting the tensile 
strength, plastic deformation behavior, and lattice dynamics of FePt 
alloys, with key property prediction errors controlled within 5 %, 
markedly outperforming traditional MEAM potentials. Simulation re -
sults show that FePt alloys retain a tensile strength of approximately 
18 GPa at temperatures as high as 1200 K, with stress drop limited to 
40 % – 50 %, indicating excellent mechanical stability at elevated tem -
peratures. This performance is primarily attributed to the pinning effect 
of Pt atoms on dislocation motion and the strengthening effect of short- 
range Fe – Pt ordering. Furthermore, the model predicts a fracture strain 
of up to 32 %, in excellent agreement with experimental observations, 
further validating the accuracy and generalizability of the constructed 
DP model. This work not only reveals the microscopic mechanisms un -
derlying the high-temperature mechanical performance of FePt alloys 
but also demonstrates the feasibility of using machine-learning-based 
potentials to model complex metallic systems. The findings provide
