Measurement of the Density and Surface Tension 
of Ni-Based Superalloys in the Liquid and Mushy States
ZUSHU LI, KENNETH C. MILLS, MALCOLM McLEAN, and KUSUHIRO MUKAI
The densities of three Ni-based superalloys have been measured in both liquid and mushy states by both
a modified sessile drop method (MSDM) and a modified pycnometric method (MPM) for alloys CMSX-4
and CM186LC, and for CMSX-10 alloy by MSDM only. The surface tensions of liquid CMSX-4,
CM186LC, and CMSX-10 superalloys were measured using the sessile drop method. All measurements
were carried out in a highly purified argon atmosphere with the oxygen partial pressure of less than
10
/H1100219 MPa in the gas outlet. The densities of all superalloys in both liquid and mushy states were found
to decrease with increasing temperature. The volume thermal expansion of each superalloy in the mushy
state was found to be higher than that in the liquid state. The densities determined by different methods
have been critically assessed and recommended values in both liquid and mushy states are given as a
linear function of temperature for the three Ni-based superalloys. The surface tension of liquid CMSX-4
superalloy was found to decrease with increasing temperature, while that of liquid CMSX-10 superalloy
increases with increasing temperature. The wetting behavior of liquid CM186LC on the alumina substrate
was found (1) to differ significantly from that of CMSX-4 and CMSX-10 and (2) to vary with time. A
HfO
2-rich layer was found in the contact area of CM186LC with the alumina substrate, which could
lead to some uncertainty in the value obtained for the surface tension determined for CM186LC.
I. INTRODUCTION
NUMERICAL modeling of casting and solidification of
metallic alloys is gaining increased importance in industrial
processing. Successful modeling can lead to improvements
of product quality, the optimization of the production
processes, including the acceleration of introduction of new
alloys into service, and to the design of new and improved
casting techniques. Reliable thermophysical property data
are required as input parameters in order to quantify the
numerical modeling. The density and the surface tension are
two critical parameters in modeling the fluid flow during
casting and solidification, affecting both the dendrite mor-
phology and the vulnerability of the solidifying alloys to the
formation of defects that can limit the engineering perfor-
mance. Ni-based superalloys contain heavy elements (e.g., W,
Re, and Nb) to improve microstructural stability and mechan-
ical performance. However, phenomena such as freckles,
white spot, and the nucleation of spurious grains can result
from convectional flows arising from differences in density
(
/H9267) and its temperature dependence (d/H9267/dT). Surface tension
(/H9253) and its temperature dependence (d /H9253/dT) may affect the
scale of microsegregation and the propensity for the forma-
tion of defects such as gas porosity as well as the evaluation
of the convective contribution to heat transport.
This study is in support of modeling to simulate the solid-
ification of single-crystal superalloy components used in gas-
turbine engines for both aircraft propulsion and power
generation. Ni-based superalloys contain elements ( e.g. Al,
Ti, and Hf) that can be very reactive at high temperatures
(with containers and atmosphere). This has made it difficult
to measure thermophysical properties reliably; these parame-
ters include densities in the liquid and “mushy” (i.e., liquid-
solid coexistence or semisolid) states and the surface tensions
of the liquid Ni-based superalloys. Sung et al.
[1] have reported
density values for five commercial Ni-based superalloys with-
out giving any details of the experimental method used. Two
recent reports describe the results of a Round Robin by a num-
ber of European laboratories (“Thermolab”) on the measure-
ment of densities and surface tensions of liquid commercial
superalloys.
[2,3] The density of liquid CMSX-4 was determined
using an optical measurement of the volume of an electro-
magnetically levitated specimen. Further measurements of the
surface tension of liquid CMSX-4 have been obtained by the
sessile drop and the pendant drop methods (at the liquidus
temperature) and by the oscillating drop method carried out
in reduced gravity force in parabolic flights.
Recently, two more accurate methods for density measure-
ment, denoted as the modified sessile drop method (MSDM)
or “big drop” and the modified pycnometric method (MPM),
have been developed by Mukai et al.
[4,5] These two new meth-
ods have been used to measure the densities of liquid Ni and
binary Ni-X alloys (X /H11005Cr, Co, W, Ta, and Al) in both liquid
and mushy states.[4,5,6] In the present study, both MSDM and
MPM have been used to determine the densities of three com-
mercial Ni-based superalloys in the liquid and mushy states. The
surface tensions of these three liquid Ni-based superalloys have
also been measured by the sessile drop method.

been shown to be within the range of uncertainty asso-

Ni-Based Superalloy
The apparatus used in the density determination by MSDM
was also used to measure the surface tensions of the liquid
Ni-based superalloys. A high-purity alumina (99.8 pct Al
2O3)
plate (25.7 /H1100325.7 /H110032.5 mm in thickness) was used as a
substrate. The surface of the Al 2O3 plate was washed with
ethanol, dried, and used without touching the surfaces, in
order to avoid any possible contamination. In this study, all
the measurements were carried out in a purified Ar atmos-
phere with an oxygen partial pressure of less than 10/H1100219 MPa
in the gas outlet. The surface tension of the liquid super-
alloys was measured for temperatures up to 1840 K.
The profiles of the sessile drops of the liquid superalloys
were photographed using a camera fitted with a telephoto-
graphic lens and a bellows in a fixed magnification of the
photographed image. Photographs of the liquid droplets were
taken every 180 seconds. The measurement time of each
experimental run was 5400 seconds. The surface tension of
liquid superalloys was calculated from the image contour
of the droplet using Rotenberg’s method.
[9] The densities of
liquid superalloys measured in this work were adopted to
calculate the surface tension values. The maximum meas-
urement error was estimated to be /H110062 pct for the surface ten-
sion and /H110060.5 pct for the contact angle. The liquid surface
tension measured remained almost constant at a fixed tem-
perature during the experiment. The values of surface ten-
sion measured in the last 1800 seconds were adopted for all
the preceding experiments.

in the movement of the droplet from its original position to
create an additional liquid/substrate contact area.

tension values, but the (d/H9253/dT ) was still controlled by the S
content (30 ppm similar to that in CMSX-10).
Another reason may be due to experimental errors. Jimbo
and Cramb[14] pointed out that it is extremely difficult to cal-
culate surface tension when contact angle is less than 90 deg
and any values derived are subject to larger errors. There-
fore, the surface tension values of liquid CM186LC meas-

Furthermore, the droplet may become asymmetric as a result
of the movement. This would also lead to a reduction in the
accuracy of the surface tension measurement. These factors
may account for the anomalously high values of surface ten-
sion measured on liquid CM186LC in the present study.
The findings in the present work demonstrate the
following:
(1) the difficulty in measuring the surface tension of indus-
trial alloys due to chemical interactions with the atmos-
phere and between the alloy and ceramics;
(2) a need for further experimental measurement under
extremely well-controlled conditions; and
(3) a need for theoretical research and modeling that may
explain and guide experimental measurements.
V. CONCLUSIONS
1. In both liquid and mushy states, the densities of the
superalloy investigated were found to decrease with
increasing temperature. The volume thermal expansion
of the superalloy in the mushy state was higher than
that of the liquid state. The recommended densities were
obtained as a linear function of temperature for all three
Ni-based superalloys in both liquid and mushy states.
2. The surface tension of liquid CMSX-4 decreased with
increasing temperature, in contrast to the surface
tension of liquid CMSX-10, which increased with
increasing temperature.
3. The wetting behavior of liquid CM186LC in contact with
dense alumina was different from those for liquid
CMSX-4 and CMSX-10. The contact angle decreased
from 160 to 86 deg within 900 seconds after melting, and
the droplet was found to move slowly after it was kept
at a certain time. A HfO
2 layer was found in the contact
area of the CM186LC alloy drop with alumina substrate.
