A ﬁrst-principles survey of /H9253//H9253′interface strengthening by alloying

K. Chen a,∗, L.R. Zhao a, John S. Tse b

Abstract
The strengthening effects induced by alloying elements on /H9253//H9253′interface in single crystal (SC) Ni–base superalloys have been investigated
using ﬁrst-principles quantum mechanics DMol3 calculations. The charge density difference indicates that a strong anisotropic bonding
forms between the ﬁrst-nearest alloying metal and Ni atoms due to alloying metal-d/Ni-d hybridization. The bond orders (BO) for a local
environmental cluster are proposed and calculated to describe the cohesion and shear strength of
/H9253//H9253′interface, respectively. Larger bond-order
values represent stronger interfacial cohesion and shear strength. Our calculations show that alloying elements signiﬁcantly increase the net
vertical and horizontal bond-order values, with Mo yielding the most effective cohesive strength, while W yielding the most effective shear
strength. A complete order of strengthening effects is presented for alloying elements that are typically added in SC superalloys.

fect induced by alloying element substitutions, we need to
consider the bond-order (BO) values between atoms. This

[11–13] and in environmental embrittlement investigations
of intermetallic compounds based on ﬁrst-principles calcu-
lations [14–16]. In this study, we use the Mayer bond order
and calculation approach [17]. The deﬁnition of Mayer bond
order between atoms A and B is as follows:

Alloying strengthening effects in Ni–base superalloys
have been studied extensively. However, the mechanism of
the interface strengthening associated with alloying addi-
tions is not yet well understood, especially at the atomic
level. In this study, the vertical and horizontal bond or-
der ∑BOs, deﬁned as parameters in the quantiﬁcation of
strengthening effects, consider not only the new bond for-
mation, but also the alternation of the old bond. Both the
charge accumulation (or depletion) and the electron spatial
redistribution can be reﬂected by the variation of the bond
orders. Naturally, ∑BO can be viewed as a new approach
to the evaluation of interface strengthening. Our calculations
indicate that the strong pining effect of alloying metals on
dislocation motion along /H9253//H9253
′interface due to strong atomic
bonding might be a contributor to the overall strengthening
effects of SC super alloys from the electronic point of view.
4. Conclusions
Fully ﬁrst-principles quantum mechanics DMol calcula-
tions have been performed to investigate the strengthening
mechanisms of alloying metal substitutions on /H9253//H9253
′ inter-
face of Ni–base SC superalloys. The strong bonding between
FNN alloying metal and Ni atoms within and across the in-
terface forms and is responsible for the interface cohesion
and shear strengthening, thus beneﬁcial for the creep rupture
strength from an electronic bonding point of view. In terms
of bond order, the increase in the net vertical and horizontal
