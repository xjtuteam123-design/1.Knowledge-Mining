3. 1. Ef fect of ball milling time on alloy powder 
Fig. 1 show s the XRD patterns of AlCuFeMn TiV L WHEA powders at 
different milling times. It can be seen from Fig. 1 (a) that the diffra ction 
peaks of each element of the ra w material powders before milling are 
clearl y visible, with high intens ity and sharp peak shape. The diffra ction 
peak of Al almost v anished after 6 h of milling, showing that Al w as 
alloy ed first. After 1 2 h of milling, most of the Cu and Mn diffra ction 
peaks graduall y faded a w ay , as seen in Fig. 1 (b). Fe and Ti wer e graduall y 
alloy ed after 24 h of milling. After further milling, the diffra ction peaks 
of the BCC phase, which corr espond t o the (1 1 0), (20 0), and (2 1 1) 
crystallogr aphic planes, appear , indicating that the alloying is complete 
and the single-phase solid solution of the BCC structure is formed after 
36 h of milling. Combined with the XRD patterns, the alloying seq uence 
during the MA pr ocess can be appro ximatel y described as follow s: 
Al → Cu → Mn → Fe → Ti → V. It can be shown that the alloying seq uence and 
the melting point ha ve a straightforw ard relationship, i.e., the elements 
with lo w melting point are pr eferentiall y allo y ed, and the alloying rat e 
is high. This corr elation can be e xplained throu gh the effect of melting 
point on the solid-state diffusion and mechanical disinte gration [4] . The 
melting point occupies a dominant position in determining the alloying 
seq uence of the elements, and for elements with similar melting points, 
the crystal structure and other secondary fact ors will play a substantial 
and decisiv e role. In the AlCuFeMn TiV alloy syst em, V has the highest 
melting point (about 2 1 7 3 K) and a BCC cry stal structure. Therefor e, 
other elements can be dissolv ed into the lattice of V to gener ate a solid 
solution with the BCC structure. 
Furthermor e, as milling time increased, the diffraction peak's 
intens ity declined rapidl y , and the diffraction peak shif ted slightl y 
and gradu ally broadened. This is because the powder particles and 
balls collide and impact for a long time during the high-energy 
milling process. The powder particles are continually split, e xtruded, 
and welded, re sulting in incremental grain re finement. Lattice dis -
tort ion is caused by the solid solution of the metal components. 
A dditionally , after 36 h of milling, the diffraction peak width of the 
alloy powders is q uite visible, and there is an amorphous occurrenc e. 
Fig. 2 show s the SEM morphology of the alloy powders at different 
ball milling time. F rom Fig. 2 , it can be seen that the surface of the 
original powder particles is smooth, and the shapes and the powder 
particles of different elements show differen t shapes and sizes, with

the e xtrusion and collision between particles and balls, the particles

time, the size of the particles v aried greatl y , and the particle size rang ed 
fro m 8 µm to 38 µm. After milling for 1 2 h, the powders wer e irregular 
and the surface roughness and particle size wer e increased, and the 
particle size rang ed from 1 0 µm to 60 µm. After milling for 24 h, the 
morphology of particles changed little, and the particle size difference 
decreased graduall y , with the particle size between 20 µm and 32 µm. 
After 36 h of milling, the morphology and size of particles tend to be 
uniform, show irregula r shape, and the particle size is between 8 µm and 
1 2 µm. In order to further understand the element distribution of 
L WHEA powders, the map scanning of the powders milled for 0 h and 
36 h (shown in Fig. 3 ) w as carried out, and three different powder par -
ticles wer e selected for EDS composition analy sis in the powder milled 
for 36 h, the results are shown in T able 1 . The comparison between (a)
