ABSTRACT
The design of light refractory high entropy alloys (LRHEAs) with good high temperature phase stability is 
important to overcome material failures in high temperature environments such as aerospace. In this paper, the 
effects of pressure and temperature on the stability and thermodynamic properties of the NbMoZrTiV LRHEA 
phase have been investigated using first-principles calculations of density functional theory calculations. Its 
microstructure and mechanical properties after annealing at different temperatures (600 
◦
C, 800 
◦
C and 1000 
◦
C) 
have also been studied. Both simulations and experiments show that NbMoZrTiV LRHEA is a single-phase BCC 
solid solution phase, and all the crystal structures satisfy thermodynamic stability without phase transformation 
with increasing pressure and temperature. With increasing annealing temperature, the grain size does not change 
significantly, showing excellent high temperature phase stability. Following prolonged annealing at 600 
◦
C, the 
microhardness of the alloy reaches a maximum of 667.88 HV
0.2
. It was observed that as the annealing tem -
perature increased, the residual stresses were gradually released, resulting in a corresponding decrease in alloy 
hardness. The results of the compression tests show that as the annealing temperature is increased, the yield 
strength of the alloy initially rises before declining. At an annealing temperature of 600 
◦
C, the alloy exhibits the 
highest yield strength (1763 MPa) and ultimate compressive strength (2145 MPa). This paper demonstrates the 
exceptional high-temperature phase stability and mechanical properties of NbMoZrTiV LRHEA through a com -
bination of simulation and experimental methods. It proposes the engineering applications of NbMoZrTiV LRHEA 
in high temperature fields such as aerospace.
1. Introduction
The concept of high entropy alloys was first proposed by a Chinese 
scholar from Taiwan, Ye Junwei, in 1995, and the related results were 
first published in 2004 [ 1 ]. The alloy breaks through the traditional 
alloy with one or two major elements as the base alloy design concept, 
from a variety of elements in accordance with the equiatomic ratio or 
approximate equiatomic ratio of the composition of the alloy, a variety 
of elements synergistic effect shows excellent performance [ 2 , 3 ]. 
Although the main elements of HEA are five or more. However, the high 
entropy effect, diffusion retardation and severe lattice distortion [ 4 – 6 ] 
caused by the mixture of multiple elements in the alloys make HEA tend 
to form simple solid solution structures. They are generally 
body-centered cubic (BCC), face-centered cubic (FCC) or a combination 
of the two, rather than having too many complex intermetallic com -
pounds. Based on the solid solution structure formed, high entropy al -
loys have excellent comprehensive performance that cannot be 
compared with conventional alloys, such as high hardness, high 
strength, high wear resistance, high corrosion resistance and special 
magnetic properties [ 7 – 11 ]. Therefore, high entropy alloys have great 
application prospects in aerospace, automotive industry and other en -
gineering collars, and have become one of the research hotspots of 
metallic materials [ 12 – 15 ]. To meet the needs of aerospace and other 
high-temperature fields, scientists from various countries have carried 
out in-depth research on the mechanical behavior and strengthening 
mechanism of HEAs and developed a variety of RHEAs with excellent 
performance. In 2010, based on the design concept of high entropy al -
loys, Professor Senkov of the US Air Force Laboratory first designed 
NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV RHEAs by mixing a variety of high melting 
point elements in an equiatomic ratio [ 16 ]. It was found that the 
compressive yield strengths of these two alloys at 1600 
◦

and 477 MPa, respectively. However, these RHEAs have good room 
temperature compressive strength ( > 1800 MPa) while their compres -

2. Computational models and experimental procedures
2.1. Computational models
Due to the high entropy effect of high entropy alloys, the mixing 
entropy of the alloy system is larger, which effectively reduces the free 
energy of the system, thus the whole system is more stable and promotes 
the mixing of elements to form a simple disordered single solid solution. 
Special quasi-stochastic structures (SQS) [ 30 ] are special periodic 
structures with a small number of atoms per unit cell, where the cor -
relation function of the first few nearest-neighbor shell layers is as close 
as possible to the correlation function of the target stochastic alloy, so 
that the periodic error exists only between the more distant nearest 
neighbors. Since interactions between distant neighbors usually 
contribute much less to the energy of the system than interactions be -
tween close neighbors, the SQS can be considered to represent the best 
possible periodic single cell for a given random alloy. The SQS method 
has been successfully applied to predict the formation energies, elastic 
properties, bond length distributions, density of states, band gaps, and 
optical properties of a range of materials with substitution disorder, 
including semiconductors, oxides, and metal alloys including HEAs 
[ 31 – 33 ]. Therefore, in this paper, SQS is used in conjunction with the 
‘mcsqs ’ code from the Alloy Theory Automation Toolkit (ATAT) to 
automate the model generation [ 34 ]. Fig. 1 shows the SQS supercell 
model of NbMoZrTiV LRHEA after relaxation treatment of three phase 
structures (BCC, FCC and HCP). In this case, when testing the conver -
gence of the NbMoZrTiV LRHEA supercell structure, the plane-wave 
energy cut-off is set to 480 eV and the Brillouin zone sampling is set 
to a grid of 8 × 8 × 2 special k-points, and the convergence satisfies the 
principle of energy minimization.
The first-principle calculations were performed using CASTEP 
(Cambridge SEquential Total Energy Package) software developed at the 
University of Cambridge, UK [ 35 ]. The Generalised Gradient Approxi -
mation (GGA) [ 36 ] and Perdew Burke Ernzerhof (PBE) [ 37 ] general -
izations are used in the calculations to describe the electron exchange 
correlation interactions. The Norm Conserving Pseudopotential (NCP) 
[ 38 ] was used to depict the electron-ion interrelationship. The 
Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) algorithm was used to solve 
the Schr ¨odinger Equation [ 39 ]. The elastic constants are determined by 
first-principles calculations and the internal degrees of freedom are 
optimized by applying a prescribed set of uniform deformations with 
finite values and calculating the resulting stresses. During the structure 
optimization process, the crystal structure and atoms were sufficiently 
relaxed until the SCF iterative convergence conditions were reached:

entropy, enthalpy and Gibbs free energy versus temperature curves of

is greater than the tendency of enthalpy H to increase with temperature, 
according to the expression of Gibbs ’ free energy (G = H-TS), the free 
energy decreases with increasing temperature. The smaller the free en -
ergy, the better the thermodynamic stability of the crystal structure.

free energy of the alloy as a function of temperature does not have any

3.3. Phase stability and elastic properties
The effect of different pressures and temperatures on the phase sta -
bility and mechanical properties of NbMoZrTiV LRHEA has been 
investigated based on first-principle calculations. Fig. 7 (a) shows the 
calculated enthalpy of the formation of NbMoZrTiV LRHEA at different 
pressures. The enthalpy of formation gradually increases with the 
gradual increase in pressure, because as the pressure increases, the po -
tential energy becomes larger and hence the energy becomes larger. 
However, the enthalpy of formation is negative for all pressure envi -
ronments, indicating that the structure has good structural stability at
