ABSTRACT 
The rafting of the microstructure of Ni-based superalloys significantly deteriorates the mechanical properties. 
According to the plastic rafting model based on the dislocation mechanism, the directional atomic diffusion 
induced by the interaction between the alloying atom and the interfacial dislocation is critical to the evolution of 
the rafting microstructure. In the present work, the interactions between the alloying atom and dislocation core 
in both matrix γ phase and the precipitate γ ′ phase are investigated by using a first principles method in com -
bination with the semi-discrete variational Peierls-Nabarro dislocation model. We show that the alloying atoms 
Al, Cr, Co, Mo, Ru, Ta, W, Re lower the (edge for Cr and Co) dislocation core energy in the γ phase, indicating 
that the alloying atoms are attractive to the dislocation core. For the γ ′ phase, Cr, Mo, Ta, W, Re raise whereas Co 
and Ru lower the dislocation core energy, i.e., Cr, Mo, Ta, W, Re are repulsive but Co and Ru are attractive to the 
dislocation core. With the calculated interaction between the alloying atoms and the dislocation core, the in -
fluence of these alloying elements on the rafting of the superalloy is discussed. 
1. Introduction 
Due to their excellent high temperature mechanical properties and 
oxidation corrosion resistance, Ni-based superalloys are widely used to 
fabricate hot-end components of turbine engines and gas turbines. With 
the development of technology, the engines/turbines confront harsher 
working conditions such as higher temperature and stress, which re -
quires improving mechanical properties of the superalloys [ 1 ]. The high 
strength of the superalloys mainly results from their unique micro -
structure, composed of γ -Ni solid solution matrix with face-centered 
cubic structure and ordered γ ′ -Ni
3
Al precipitates with L1
2 
structure 
[ 2 ]. The γ ′ phase appears as cubes embedded coherently in the γ matrix, 
which strengthens efficiently the alloys. However, under stress and 
elevated temperature, the γ ′ phase coarsens directionally and forms a 
raft structure, which significantly degrades the mechanical properties of 
the alloys [ 3 ]. It has been demonstrated that the rafting of the γ ′ phase 
accelerates the creep deformation and shortens the fracture and fatigue 
lifetime of the superalloys [ 4 , 5 ]. 
Because of the significant influence of the γ ′ rafting on the mechan -
ical properties of the superalloys, great efforts have been made to 
understand the rafting of the γ ′ phase. During the last half decade [ 6 – 9 ], 
various mechanisms have been put forward to account for the rafting 
and corresponding models have been developed to describe and predict 
the rafting behavior. Nabarro [ 7 ] ascribed the driving force for the 
rafting to the response of the elastic energy of the superalloy to the 
applied stress, with which he developed an elastic thermodynamic 
model for the rafting. In 1993, Socrate and Parks [ 8 ] considered the 
contribution of creep strain to the elastic energy and developed an 
elastic-plastic rafting model. In 1995, Buffiere and Ignat noticed that, in 
some superalloys, the dislocation distribution at the initial stage of creep 
is not uniform, which affects the rafting behavior of the alloys. Conse -
quently, they came up with a rafting model based on dislocations [ 9 ], i. 
e., the plastic rafting model. 
According to the plastic rafting model, to relax the interfacial co -
herency stress under the applied stress, interfacial dislocations occur in a 
certain direction depending on the direction of the applied stress as well 
as lattice parameter misfit between the γ and γ ′ phases. The anisotropic 
interfacial dislocation distribution leads to directional atomic diffusion 
because of the interaction between the interfacial dislocations and the 
alloying atoms. It was suggested that, after the first stage of the

2.5. Calculation details 
The Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP) based on density 
functional theory (DFT) [ 19 – 21 ] is used for all calculations. The 
ion-electron interactions are described by using the projector 
augmented plane wave (PAW) approximation [ 22 ], which can achieve 
the calculation accuracy of full electron potential. The generalized 
gradient approximation (GGA) parameterized by 
Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [ 23 ] is adopted for the 
exchange-correlation functional. The wave functions are expanded with 
a cutoff energy of 450 eV. The calculations are performed with spin 
polarizations. The Monkhorst-Pack special k point mesh is used to 
sample the Brillouin zone, which is 7 × 7 × 7 for the calculation of the 
formation energy and 9 × 9 × 3 for the calculation of the GSFE. The total 
energy tolerance for the electronic minimization is set to be 1 × 10
 7 
eV. 
For the calculation of the formation energy, the supercell is fully relaxed. 
For the calculations of the GSFE, the shape of the supercell is fixed, and 
the atoms are only allowed to relax along the c direction perpendicular 
to the slip plane. The Hellmann-Feynman force tolerance for the geo -
metric optimization is 1 × 10
 3 
eV/Å. The alloying elements M involved 
in present work are Al, Cr, Co, Mo, Ru, Ta, W and Re.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Interaction between alloying atoms and dislocation 
In principle, the interaction energy between the alloying atom and 
the dislocation may be directly calculated by using first principles 
method with the flexible boundary condition dislocation model [ 45 ] or 
the dislocation dipole model [ 46 ]. The interaction energy may be 
defined as the difference between the energies of the dislocation with 
alloying atom in the dislocation core and far from the dislocation core. 
For such calculations, a large supercell is needed to model the disloca -
tion, which is computationally costly. Therefore, in the present work, we 
resort to the computationally relative cheap SVPN model to investigate 
the interaction between the alloying atom and the dislocation. However, 
the dislocation core energy defined in the SVPN model is somehow 
distinct from the one from direct first principles calculations. Therefore, 
it is necessary to interpret the interaction between the alloying atom and 
dislocation within the framework of SVPN.

created by joining two semi-infinite half crystals, with the disregistry of

4.2. Implication of the M-dislocation interaction on the rafting of γ ′ phase 
Buffiere et al. [ 9 ] proposed a criterion based on dislocations to 
describe the raft formation of nickel-based single crystal superalloys 
with negative misfit under high temperature creep. Due to the lattice 
constant misfit between Ni
3
Al and Ni, coherency stresses occur at the 
γ / γ ′ interface. During the first stage of creep, the alloy deforms by the 
activation of 
1
2
〈 110 〉 dislocations on { 111 } planes in the γ matrix. When 
these dislocations approach the face of the γ ′ precipitates, they are either 
trapped by the precipitates to form interfacial dislocations if their Bur -
gers vector relieves the coherency stresses or pushed backward by the 
precipitates if their Burgers vector enhances the coherency stresses. 
Buffiere et al. carefully analyzed the interactions between the disloca -
tions and the faces of the precipitate with different orientations under 
tensile or compressive stresses along [ 001 ] , [ 110 ] , and [ 111 ] directions, 
with which, the precipitate faces that trap or repel the dislocations were 
determined. They concluded that the interaction between the disloca -
tions and the faces of the precipitates leads to interfacial dislocations on 
the interface with certain orientations excluding others depending on 
the applied stress and lattice constant misfit (see Fig. 8 a). 
According to Buffiere et al. [ 9 ], the atoms of the γ forming elements 
such as Co and Cr within the matrix γ channels containing interfacial 
dislocations diffuse toward the interfacial dislocation, forming Cr/Co 
depleted zones in the corresponding matrix channel as shown in Fig. 8 a 
and b. The Cr/Co atoms in the γ channel devoid of interfacial disloca -
tions diffuse to the one filled with interfacial dislocations due to the 
chemical gradient ( Fig. 8 c). This atomic flux is accompanied by a cross 
flux of the γ ′ forming elements (Ni and Al) ( Fig. 8 c), which results in the 
γ ′ phase growing toward the dislocation devoid γ channel and evolving 
from cube to raft ( Fig. 8 d). 
According to the above plastic rafting model, the attraction between 
the interfacial dislocations in the γ channel and the alloying atoms is the 
prerequisite for the rafting of the γ ′ phase. From our calculations, all the 
considered alloying atoms are attracted to the interfacial dislocations in 
the γ phase, which explains the experimentally observed accumulation 
of the Cr, Co, Re, etc. alloying atoms at the interfacial dislocations 
[ 47 – 50 ]. Therefore, the atomic flux of these atoms M to the interfacial 
dislocations and the formation of the M depleted zone is understandable. 
As shown in Fig. 8 , the cross flux of the γ ′ forming elements Ni and Al 
to the γ channel devoid of interfacial dislocations is also critical for the 
rafting of the γ ′ phase [ 9 ]. However, from our calculations, the Al atoms 
are attracted by the dislocations in the γ phase as well. In principle, Al 
should diffuse to the interfacial dislocations as the γ forming elements 
do, in contrast to the argument of Buffiere et al. [ 9 ]. Therefore, in the

A possible explanation for the diffusion of Al to the γ channel devoid 
of interfacial dislocations is that the γ alloying atom M attracted by the 
interfacial dislocations at the γ phase side enters into the γ ′ phase side 
due to the strong attractive interaction between M and the interfacial 
dislocation at the γ ′ phase side. The M atoms prefer to occupy the Al site 
such that a large amount of Al atoms is released from the γ ′ phase, 
leading to a high Al concentration at the γ phase side of the interface. 
Then, the Al atoms diffuse to the γ channel devoid of interfacial dislo -
cation, driven by the Al chemical gradient. 
If the above argument is true, the rafting of γ ′ phase will be facilitated 
by the alloying atoms with the following characteristics: (1) attractive to 
the dislocations of the γ phase but more strongly to those of the γ ′ phases; 
(2) preferring the Al site in the γ ′ phase. Our calculations demonstrate 
that all alloying atoms considered in the present work are attracted by 
the dislocations of γ phase. Namely, these alloying atoms may accu -
mulate at the interfacial dislocations of the γ phase side. Cr, Mo, Ta, W, 
Re are repulsed by the dislocations of γ ′ phase. Thus, it is not easy for 
them to enter the γ ′ phase side. Therefore, these alloying atoms may not 
increase the Al concentration at the γ phase side of the interface 
although they prefer the Al site in the γ ′ phase. Co and Ru are attracted 
by the dislocations of γ ′ phase such that they may migrate to the γ ′ phase 
site. However, Co and Ru are predicted to occupy the Ni site in the γ ′ 
phase, and, therefore, they do not help to increase the Al concentration 
at the γ phase side either. The above considerations lead us to the 
conclusion that all the alloying elements involved in present work do not 
facilitate the rafting of the γ ′ phase. 
The above model is applicable to the stoichiometric or Al-rich Ni
3
Al 
where the M atoms preferring the Al site are expected to directly replace 
the Al atoms. For the Al-poor Ni
3

Al
) is 
the dominant point defect in Al-poor Ni
3

occupy the Al sublattice (denoted as M

) and push out the Ni antisite 
atoms. On the one hand, under thermal equilibrium state, which one of 
Al and Ni antisite is to be pushed out may be determined by comparing 
the formation energies of M
Al 
and Ni
Al
. If the formation energy of M
Al 
is 
lower than that of Ni
Al
, then pushing the Al atoms out of the γ ′ phase is 
still preferred energetically. Otherwise, the Ni atoms will be pushed out 
by M. On the other hand, because the concentration of Al is generally 
much higher than Ni antisite in Al-poor Ni
3
Al, the M atoms have more 
opportunities to encounter the Al atoms and then to replace them and 
push them out of the γ ′ phase even though it is not energetically 
favorable. 
In the present work, we discuss the influence of the alloying elements 
on the rafting of the microstructure of Ni superalloys based on the 
interaction between the alloying atoms and the interfacial dislocations. 
It should be noted that the rafting is a very complex process involving 
various mechanisms. There might exist some other factors influencing 
the rafting behavior. For example, the alloying element may tune the 
rafting by changing the lattice constant misfit between the γ and γ ′ 
phases. The interaction between Al and other alloying atoms may also 
affect the rafting considering that the directional diffusion of Al atom is 
an important step of the rafting. If alloying atoms trap the Al atoms, the 
diffusion of Al atoms, and, subsequently, the growth of the raft should be 
slowed down. The kinetics of the γ / γ ′ interface movement during rafting 
is controlled by the atomic diffusion [ 52 – 54 ], which may be altered by 
the alloying element M. 
5. Conclusion 
In the present work, the interactions between the alloying atoms M 
= Al, Cr, Co, Mo, Ru, Ta, W, and Re and the dislocations in both γ -Ni and 
γ ′ -Ni
3
Al are investigated by using a first principles method in combi -
nation with the semi-discrete variational Peierls-Nabarro model. The 
main results may be summarized as follows. 
(1) The energy of the screw dislocations of the γ phase varies in the 
sequence of pure Ni ~ Ni – Co ~ Ni – Cr > Ni – Al > Ni – Ru > Ni – Ta 
> Ni – W > Ni – Re > Ni – Mo. The energy of the edge dislocations of 
the γ phase exhibits a similar trend except that Co and Cr slightly 
lower the dislocation energy. For the γ ′ phase, the alloying ele -
ments Cr, Re/Mo, W/Ta increasingly raise the dislocation energy 
whereas Co and Ru lower it. For both γ and γ ′ phases, the trend of 
the dislocation energy is in accordance with the sequence of the 
generalized stacking fault energy of the Ni/Ni
3
Al-M systems. 
(2) The calculated dislocation energies indicate that the alloying 
atoms are attracted by the dislocations of the γ phase. Co and Ru 
are attracted by the dislocations in the γ ′ phase whereas Cr, Mo, 
Ta, W, and Re are repelled by them. 
(3) According to the plastic rafting model suggested by Buffiere et al. 
[ 9 ], we propose that the rafting of γ ′ phase will be facilitated by 
the alloying atoms attracted by the dislocations of the γ phase and 
more strongly to that of the γ ′ phase if they also prefer the Al site 
in the γ ′ phase. Based on the interaction energies between the 
alloying atoms and the dislocations and the site preference of the
