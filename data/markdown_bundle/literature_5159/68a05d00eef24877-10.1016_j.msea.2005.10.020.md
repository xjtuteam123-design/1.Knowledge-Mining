High-temperature mechanics properties of in situ TiB2p
reinforced Al–Si alloy composites
Hongzhan Yi, Naiheng Ma, Xianfeng Li, Yijie Zhang, Haowei Wang∗

Received 10 February 2005; received in revised form 21 September 2005; accepted 5 October 2005
Abstract
The eutectic Al–Si alloy composites reinforced with sub-micron TiB 2 particles were successfully fabricated by means of reaction processing
method. The TiB2 particles were formed in situ through the reaction of Ti and B bearing salts and Al alloy. The test results showed that the ultimate
tensile strength of the composites were higher than that of Al–Si master alloy at the temperature ranging from 25 to 400 ◦C, and the contribution
ratio of the particles to the ultimate tensile strength reached its peak value at 260◦C, and the mechanism is also discussed in this essay.

1. Introduction
Due to high tensile strength, elastic modulus and good wear
resistance, the discontinuously reinforced aluminum matrix
composite (DRAMC) is a kind of potential material for appli-
cation in high-temperature environment [1,2]. The study of Al-
based composites has been focused on the SiC/Al system mainly
because of the availability and relatively low cost of SiC ﬁbers,
whiskers and particles[3–5], while for structural applications in
automotive and aerospace industries, the composites of SiC/Al
system will result in the degradation of the matrix-reinforcement
interface and deterioration of the mechanical properties at ele-
vated temperature.
In situ fabrication technologies for processing metal and
ceramic composites have been developed by chemical reaction
during the last decade. Lots of investigation results on ceramic
particles reinforced metal matrix composites (MMCs) are avail-
able in literature. However, little work has been conducted on
the behavior of in situ particle reinforced aluminum matrix com-
posites at high temperatures [6].
Method of reaction processing is used to obtain the in
situ TiB
2 particle reinforced Al–Si alloy composites, and

2. Experimental procedure
The matrix alloy used in this study was Al–12% Si–1.2%
Cu–1% Ni–1% Mg (named ZL109), and the reactive salts
used were K
2TiF6 and KBF4, both of which were with high-
purity quotient (more than 99.0%). The composites of different
quality fractions of the TiB
2 particles (1.9TiB 2p/ZL109 and
8.3TiB2p/ZL109) were manufactured in high-purity graphite
crucible using an electrical resistance furnace at 850 ◦C. A
small amount of Na 3AlF6 was added as reactive assistant, and
a little Sb was used for modifying. And a blade paddle mixer
stirred on the common boundary between the melt metal and
the salts with 600 rpm for 15–20 min then poured out the slag.
The specimens were cast in a permanent mold. Before ten-
sion test, the specimens were treated with T6 (solution treated
at 525
◦C for 1.5 h, oil quenched at 70 ◦C, and aging treated
at 170 ◦C for 8 h). When reaching the setting temperatures,
the specimen was held for 5 min, with the tension speed of
0.5 mm/min.
The specimens for microstructure and X-ray diffraction anal-
ysis were cut from the tested UTS specimens of 8.3TiB
2p/ZL109,
and a specimen of 8.3TiB2p/ZL109 was extracted for X-ray anal-
ysis. The morphology of the fracture specimens was observed

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructures of the composites
The X-ray diffraction analysis of 8.3TiB2p/ZL109 is shown in
Fig. 1. It can be found that the main phases of the composite are
/H9251-Al, /H9252-Si and TiB2. There is mass of TiB2 existing in extracted
residua, shown in Fig. 2. A typical SEM micrograph of the as-
prepared TiB2 particles is shown in Fig. 3. The TiB2 particles
synthesized in situ in the matrix are cubic and spherical in form,
with sizes ranging from 0.2 to 0.8 /H9262m and an average size of
approximate 0.5 /H9262m, which is one to two orders of magnitude
lower than that of ex situ particle in discontinuously reinforced
composites [7–9]. It can also be seen that the interface between
TiB
2 particle and aluminum matrix is clean.

range of the ZL109 alloy is narrow, with which the strength and
the ductility are closely related.

An excellent strength of the composites is caused by the inter-
action between dislocations and the TiB 2 particles. Plenty of
additional dislocations around the TiB2 particles are generated

posites undergo a brittle rupture accordingly, shown inFig. 7(a).
With the increasing of the temperature, the ductility increases
obviously, and the UTS of the composites also increase obvi-
ously. The increment of strength /Delta1σof the composite can be
simply calculated according to Eq. (2) [15]:
/Delta1σ= βGbρ

in shear and Burger’s vector of the matrix, respectively.
Eq. (2) shows that the increment of the UTS increases with
the increment of the dislocations, when the fraction of particles

obviously, the quantity ofρ should maintain after T6 at different
temperatures (the temperature should not exceed the melt point).
It is known that the slippage systems of the metal matrix are
easy to start-up at high-temperature, which is the reason for the
increasing of ductility, and so does composite.
One reason of the obvious increasing of contribution ratio k
at high-temperature attributes to the increasing of ductility of
the composites at high-temperature, which can offer more space
for the transfer of dislocations.
Another reason is that the matrix of the composites distort
greatly at high-temperature; with the rising of the temperature
the strength of the matrix falls quickly, and the ductile capability
increases swiftly; with the stress of the interface loosen by the
matrix, leading to the obvious falling of the stress concentration
in the composites, which reduces the tendency of the craze of
the interface. However, due to the high thermal activation pro-
cess, the alternating slipping of dislocations increases at 315
and 400
◦C, and the resisting force of the dislocation movement
generated by particles fall, so the contribution ratio k decreases
after 315 ◦C.
From the curve of the Fig. 6, it can be found that there is an
inﬂection point existing in three curves at 205◦C, the reason of
which is under research.
In a whole, a well heat-stable performance of TiB2 particles
and the inter-action between particles and dislocation makes an
excellent strength of the composites at high-temperature.
4. Conclusions
1. The in situ TiB
2p/ZL109 composites bear outstanding
high-temperature mechanical properties, the UTS of the
8.3TiB
2p/ZL109 increases 105 MPa than that of ZL109
matrix at 260 ◦C.
2. The UTS of the composites reinforced with in situ TiB2 par-
ticles are higher than that of ZL109 alloy from 25 to 400◦C.
Although above 260 ◦C, the strength of both the matrix and
the composites drops obviously, the composites still bear
excellent strength, and the UTS of the 8.3TiB
2p/ZL109 and
the 1.9TiB2p/ZL109 are 84 and 73 MPa at 400 ◦C, respec-
tively.
3. The high-temperature mechanical properties of the compos-
ites reinforced with in situ TiB2p were evaluated by the con-
tribution ratiok, and thek reaches peak value at 260◦C, which
are 44.6% of 1.9TiB2p/ZL109 and 63.3% of 8.3TiB2p/ZL109.
