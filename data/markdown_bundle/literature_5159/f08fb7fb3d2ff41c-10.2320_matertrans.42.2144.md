Kimi I Center for Non-crystalline Materials, Department of Metallurgical Engineering, Yonsei University, 134 Shinchondong Seodaemungu, Seoul, 120-749, Korea 2 Department of Physics, Chongju University, Chongju, 360-764, Korea A new magnesium alloy strengthened by icosahedral quasicrystalline particles and precipitates was developed by thermomechanical processes for an as-cast Mg-rich Mg95Zn4.3Y0.7 alloy. Quasicrystalline particles of 0.5��2 gm in size were distributed in the a-Mg matrix by the hot-rolling process, and nanoscale quasicrystals were also precipitated throughout the a-Mg grains during this thermomechanical process. The alloy exhibits high strengths and large elongations at room and elevated temperatures. High strength observed in the alloy is mainly due to the strengthening effect of large number of quasicrystals, where the volume fraction of quasicrystals is around 9%. The quasicrystalline phase is found to be in equilibrium with the a-Mg phase and stable against coarsening under deformation up to near the melting temperature of the eutectic. The stable quasicrystalline particle/matrix interface with a low interfacial energy can provide the improved mechamcal properties of the alloy. Introduction Icosahedral quasicrystals have been extensively studied for the last seventeen years after the first finding of an icosahe- dral phase (I-phase) in a rapidly solidified A184Mn16 alloy. l) The icosahedral quasicrystals have been found in many metal- lic alloy systems. For example, Al-, Mg-, Pd-, Zr- and Ti- based alloys.2'3) However, most I-phase was found to be a metastable phase. Even though the I-phases were brittle, they exhibited interesting properties such as high strength and hardness, Iow friction coefficient, and Iow interfacial energy. Thermally stable I-phase has been reported to form in the Zn��Mg��Y alloy system. Recently, Tsai et al.4) determined isothermal sections of the Zn��Mg��Y equilibrium phase dia- gram involving the I-phase, and Park et al.5) reported an I- phase equilibrating with a metallic solid solution in the Mg- enriched region in the Mg��Zn��Y alloy system. Primary a- Mg dendrite with eutectic pockets of I-phase having the sto- ichiometry about Mg3Zn6Y1 formed in the interdendritic re- gion up to the alloy composition of Mg73.2Zn23Y3.8 (The ratio of Zn to Y is around 6). Quasicrystals in Mg-rich Mg��Zn��Y alloys are of technological interest since they can be used as a strengthening agent in the metallic alloys due to their high strength at ambient and elevated temperatures with limited ductility6) and isotropic nature in the crystal structure.7) Fur- thermore, the Mg��Zn��Y alloy system has great advantages for the light structural materials since the alloys in some com- positional range have excellent formability at elevated tem- peratures. In this study, we are reporting the reliable appli- cation of quasicrystals as a strengthening agent and also the formation of the stable I-phase in a Mg-rich Mg��Zn��Y alloy. Distribution of I-phase in the alloy by the thermomecahni- cal processes improves strength significantly without losing eleongation. 2. Material and Experimental A testing alloy for this study was a Mg95Zn4.3Y0.7 (in atomic percentage). The alloy was prepared by induction melting from high purity 99.9%Mg, 99.9%Zn, and 99.95% Y under a dynamic Ar atmosphere, followed by pouring the melt into a rectangular cavity of width 6cm, thickness 1.5 cm, and height 10 cm. Figure I(a) shows the secondary electron mi- croscopy (SEM) image of the as-cast sample, in which pri- mary a-Mg dendrite and eutectic I-phase located in the in- terdendritic region of primary a-Mg phase were seen. The amount of I-phase in the alloy was around 9% in volume. The conventional thermomechanical processes, i.e. hot-rolling and annealing, were utilized to distribute a large number of small I-phase particles in the a-Mg matrix. The specimen was hot-rolled from 12 to 1.7 mm final thickness (reduction 86%). The rolls were heated to around 1000C prior to rolling. The block of the alloy was heated at 4000C for 20 min and then rolled with a reduction of 15% per pass. The equiaxied a-Mg grains of 14 gm in diameter were formed via the dynamic recrystallization process, and the as-cast I-phase structure was destroyed, providing the microscale size parti- cles (0.5��2.0 gm in size) as shown in the Fig. I(b) of SEM image of the hot-rolled sample. The broken particle/particle interfaces are found to be completely healed by the thermally activated processes. The hot-rolled sheets were annealed at 4000C for 0.5 h. During continuously heating the alloy in DSC, endothermic peak appears with an onset temperature of 4500C, corresponding to melting of the eutectic as shown in Fig. 2. Nanoscale spherical I-phase was also precipitated throughout the a-Mg matrix during the thermomechanical processes, which will be shown later. Results and Discussion Ultimate tensile strength, yield stress (at E = 0.2%), and elongation of the alloy are plotted as a function of test tem- perature in Figs. 3(a), (b), and (c), respectively. For com- parison, corresponding values of AZ31B, one of the typical wrought Mg alloys,8) are also given. The Mg95Zn4.3Y0.7 alloy exhibits the high level of ultimate tensile strength of 370 MPa and yield stress of 220 MPa with moderate elongation at room temperature. The higher strength of the alloy is mainly due to the large number of I-phase particles i.e. due to the effect of the interaction of mobile dislocations to a large number of particles in the a-Mg matrix. Nanoscale precipitates play an additional strengthening role by providing effective barriers to gliding dislocations. In a low-temperature regime, typi- cally up to 2000C, the Mg95Zru.3Y0.7 alloy also exhibits sig- nificantly higher strength than that of AZ31B alloy without deteriorating the elongation. In general, an alloy consisting of large amount of second phase exhibits higher strength but much lower elongation when compared to those values of a monolithic alloy due to the increased number of failure ini- tiation sites that are highly stressed. With increasing the test temperature, flow stress decreases. The Mg95Zn4.3 Yon alloy shows flow stress of 18 MPa and elongation of 220% at a test temperature of 4000C. The significantly low level of flow stress at high temperature is mainly due to the activities of dislocations in non-basal planes in the a-Mg matrix and the contribution of grain boundary activities to the total deforma- tion. 9) To understand the role of I-phase particles in the alloy at high temperature, microstructures of a deformed specimen were examined. Figs. 4(a) and (b) show, respectively, a SEM image and a bright-field TEM image of the specimen de- formed to a strain of around 1.0 with a strain rate of IO 4 s 1 at a test temperature of 4000C. The flow stress of the alloy was constant (AJ 10MPa) over the strain range and it failed by diffuse necking. The particles initially distributed near the eutectic pocket before testing (see Fig. 1 (b)) move away each other during deformation, being distributed more randomly in the test specimen. Surprisingly, the particles do not grow, but only the shape of the particle, initially faceted, evolves to be somewhat rounded as shown in Fig. 4(b). The inset in Fig. 4(b) shows the diffraction pattern taken from the particle, in which a 5-fold symmetry can be identified as an I-phase. The existence of I-phase in the deformed specimen clearly in- dicates that I-phase thermally equilibrates with a-Mg phase. Furthermore, any debonding or nanoscale defect at the parti- cle/matrix interface cannot be seen in the test specimen. In general, particle/matrix debonding has been considered as an initial stage of the failure mechanism at high temperature de- formation in the alloy consisting of intermetallic compound particles at grain boundaries, and the coalescence of many cavities at large strains can induce the failure of such an al- loy. For example, an intermetallic particle of Mg17A112 in an AZ91 alloy.10) Many defects have been reported to develop in the particle/matrix interface during the thermomechanical processing possibly due to the mismatched lattice structure of the particle to the matrix. Sometimes these defects can grow rapidly due to the constrained material flow near the particle at high temperature.ll) However, careful examination of the I-phase/matrix interface in the Mg95Zn4 3 Yon alloy does not reveal any defects.

However, due to the quasiperiodic lattice structure of I-phase, the mismatched strain may be compromised by the I- phase particle, decreasing the stress concentration in the ma- trix near the I-phase particle. Within our knowledge, the dif- fusivity of Y in Mg is not available. However, low diffusivity is expected by considering large difference in atomic size be- tween Mg and Y. Conclusions The hot-rolled Mg95Zn4.3 Yon alloy, containing of I-phase particles and I-phase precipitates in the a-Mg matrix, ex- hibits great strengths and large elongations at room and ele- vated temperatures. The I-phase thermally equilibrates with a metallic solid solution of a-Mg up to near the melting temper- ature of the eutectic. The high strength in the low temperature regime, up to 2000C, is due to the interaction of dislocations with a large number of particles, together with the strength- ening effect of precipitates. Large elongations observed in the alloy at room and elevated temperatures are mainly due to the formation of stable interface between the I-phase particles and the a-Mg matrix.
