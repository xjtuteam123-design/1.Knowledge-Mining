Effect of minor Sc on high temperature mechanical properties of
Ti–Al based alloys
Yin Songbo, Huang Boyun, Yin Zhimin *
Central South Uni6ersity of Technology , Changsha Hunan 410083, People’s Republic of China
Abstract
High temperature compression properties and microstructure of two Ti–Al based alloys with and without Sc were studied The
results show that minor addition may remarkably improve the high temperature yield strength of Ti–Al and Ti–48Al based alloy
which have either duplex structure or full lamellar structure, the yield strength increment is 120 and 125 MPa, respectively. Minor
Sc exists as Ti
3(Al,Sc) in plate precipitates which distribute mainly along with a2:g or g:gT boundaries, partly within g phases. At
high temperature, deformation of g phases is easier than that in the a2 phase, the deformation mode is dislocation slipping. The
obstacle to interlamellar sliding and dislocation slipping caused by Ti 3(Al, Sc) precipitates should be responsible for yield strength
increment of Ti–Al alloys with Sc. Published by Elsevier Science S.A.

1. Introduction

high temperature structure material which has low den-

4. Discussion
4.1. The causes of equiaxial grains formation
Some authors suggested [5] that according to the
orientation angle between lamellar and external force
axis; the deformation mode of lamellar eutectoid struc-
ture can be divided into two kinds of models. While the
orientation angle is at 30–75°C, the lamellar structure
exhibited lower ﬂow stress. While lamellar is orientated
parallel and perpendicular to external force axis, the
lamellar structure exhibited higher ﬂow stress. Thus,
while some lamellar eutectoid structure are orientated
at disadvantage condition, dislocations may pile up at
the boundaries which lead to the lamellar structure bent
in order to reach the easy slip orientation. Lamellar
structure bent lead to lattice distortion, at thermal
activation condition, recrystallization will occur (Fig.
3).
4.2. Difference of high temperature compression
mechanical properties in Ti –Al base alloys
Phase diagram and metallograph observation results
all show that Ti–43Al base alloys have full g:a2 lamel-
lar structure, Ti–48Al base alloys have more g phases,
so their high temperature strength is lower and the
ductility is higher.
4.3. Effect of minor Sc on high temperature
compression beha6ior of Ti –Al base alloy
Adding of minor Sc to Ti–Al base alloys causes the
precipitation of Ti 3(Al, Sc), most of them disturbed at
the boundaries between the lamellar eutectoid structure
and part of them existed within g phases. While the
sample was compressed, the shear stress will cause the
slipping within g phase and boundary sliding [6–8].

Ti3(Al, Sc) precipitates will be obstacles to the slipping

5. Conclusions
1. Adding of 0.4% Sc to Ti–Al and Ti–48Al base
alloys can remarkably increase their high tempera-
ture yield strength. The strength increment reached
110 MPa.
2. The deformation mechanism of the studied alloys at
900°C is mainly dislocation slipping. The intensive
slip bands can be seen within the lamellar structure.
At some lamellar eutectoid structure boundaries,
some equiaxed grains can also be seen, showing that
dynamic recrystallization occurred.
3. Minor Sc existed as thin plate Ti
3(Al, Sc) com-
pounds both at g:a2 phase boundaries and within g
phase in the studied alloys Ti 3(Al, Sc) precipitates
inhibit the slipping within g phase and sliding be-
tween g phase and a2 phases, leading to increasing
of high temperature strength of Ti–Al base alloy.
