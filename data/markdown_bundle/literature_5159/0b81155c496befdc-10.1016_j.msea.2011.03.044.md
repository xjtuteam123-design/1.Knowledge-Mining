abstract
A graphical method is proposed that can be used for comparing the kinetics of various time-dependent
crack tip processes operating during the dwell period of a trapezoidal creep-fatigue loading cycle for
Ni-base superalloys. A comparison is shown that evaluates the kinetics of the time-dependent processes
for the superalloy 718.

1. Introduction
Ni-base superalloys are used in a variety of elevated temper-
ature applications and are subjected to the interactive effects of
creep, fatigue, and the environment during creep-fatigue loading.
Temperatures at or above ∼650
◦C, while under creep-fatigue load-
ing, provide the necessary conditions for several time-dependent
processes to be operational at the crack tip, which include creep
deformation/stress relaxation, surface oxidation, and diffusion of
oxygen ahead of the crack tip. The creep-fatigue crack propagation
rate is highly dependent on the temperature and the environmen-
tal conditions, which indicates that it is governed by the kinetics
of these time-dependent processes. Therefore, characterizing the
kinetics of these processes is of paramount importance to under-
standing the relative contribution of each to determining the rate
controlling step for crack growth. The coupling effect of envi-
ronment, creep, and microstructure also occurs, complicating the
overall crack growth behavior. To date no sufﬁciently robust fatigue
crack growth model has been developed that adequately accounts
for all of these various time and cycle dependent processes. A pre-
liminary model has been proposed that accounts for most of these
processes, but it needs to be extended in order to be more compre-
hensive [1].

Elevated temperature creep-fatigue processes have been shown
to cause intergranular failure, which suggests that grain bound-
ary embrittlement occurs when subjected to air or an environment
containing oxygen. When tested in vacuum or low partial pressures
of oxygen, the fracture mode becomes predominantly transgranu-
lar in nature, indicating that an environmental agent, oxygen, is
deleterious to the grain boundaries at elevated temperature (see
Fig. 1) [2]. No universally accepted mechanistic model exists to fully
explain this type of grain boundary embrittlement. The present
research is focused on elucidating these complex phenomena.
The fatigue crack growth rate for nickel-base superalloys is
greater during lower frequency experiments. This effect is even
more pronounced during creep-fatigue tests where the load is
held at its maximum for some duration, because this allows time-
dependent processes to have a greater contribution (see Fig. 2). The
longer the dwell period, the longer the crack tip is exposed to the
maximum load and the oxidizing environment. Also, redistribution
of the crack tip stresses occurs during the dwell period causing a
reduction in the stress-assisted diffusion driving force. This redis-
tribution can, in turn, limit the effect that the damaging species
can have on the grain boundaries. From these results one can pos-
tulate that the faster the stress relaxation the material exhibits, the
less susceptible it will be to environment assisted creep-fatigue
crack growth when subjected to elevated temperature. This estab-
lishes a scenario of competing kinetics with the stress relaxation
rate reducing susceptibility and the diffusion and creep deforma-
tion increasing the damage. A means to compare these crack tip
kinetics is needed.

In the present paper the kinetics of creep-fatigue crack growth
rate are described and a methodology is proposed for evaluat-
ing the kinetics of each time-dependent process. The proposed
methodology involves the use of a chart that compares the
various rates associated with crack growth under creep-fatigue
conditions.
2. Method development
The various kinetic processes occurring during the dwell period
include the diffusion of oxygen through air to the crack tip [3], the
dissociation and chemisorption of oxygen at the crack tip, which is
captured by the reaction rate of oxides for Ni-base superalloys[4,5],
the diffusion of Ni out to the surface to form NiO [6], the diffusion
of oxygen into Ni either through the bulk [7] or grain boundaries
[8], and ﬁnally, stress relaxation [9].
A chart was developed to evaluate the different kinetic pro-
cesses occurring during the dwell period. This chart correlates all
the processes in terms of a ﬂux with units of cm
2/s as a function
of temperature, as is shown in Fig. 3. The processes represented on
the chart include the diffusion rate of oxygen in air, of oxygen in
nickel, of oxygen along a particle/matrix interface (which for this
analysis is being considered equivalent to diffusion of oxygen along
the nickel grain boundaries), and of nickel into an Fe–Ni–Cr alloy.
Also included are the rates for the formation of NiO, TiO, and what is
being termed, the strain ﬂux for the Ni-base superalloy 718, which
was determined from stress relaxation tests. Povolo and Reggiardo
[9] provide results from stress relaxation experiments performed
on alloy 718 at 500 and 550
◦C in bending. Also, data for only one
initial stress, 214 MPa, is shown in Fig. 3. This is the only test con-
dition reported in their study that produced a positive change in
stress relaxation rates over these test temperatures. This is a lim-
ited temperature range and set of loading conditions, nevertheless
due to a lack of obtainable data over greater test conditions, this set
of data will be used in this preliminary proposed method for com-
paring kinetics. The stress relaxation data was converted to strain
rate using the analysis provided by Povolo and Reggiardo [9]. The
tests were conducted in bending, so the maximum stress occurs in
the outer ﬁber of the samples. It is assumed that the outer surface
area is subjected to a uniform stress relaxation rate. Therefore, the
previously mentioned strain ﬂux is deﬁned, in this case, as the strain
rate multiplied by the surface area of the samples. In general it is
being deﬁned as the area over which strain is occurring times the
strain rate. This provides the necessary units, cm
2/s, needed for the
kinetics comparison method proposed herein. To compare with the
other data presented, the strain ﬂux plot on Fig. 3 is extrapolated

Time-dependent processes can be compared using a rate versus
temperature chart as is shown in Fig. 3. The faster the rate the less
likely that process is to be the rate limiting step. The data repre-
sented shows the diffusion of oxygen through air as the fastest
rate, indicating that the supply of oxygen to the crack tip is not
the controlling step in the crack growth process. Also, it has been
shown that intergranular embrittlement can occur in the ﬁrst few
seconds of the dwell period, but if the crack tip is exposed to oxy-
gen for the ﬁrst time near the end of a long dwell period, then the
severity of the embrittlement is substantially reduced [10]. These
results suggest that the faster the stress relaxation during the dwell
period, the less susceptible the material will be to grain bound-
ary embrittlement. This conclusion was also made by Pineau and
Antolovich [11], stating that, “when the crack tip experiences a neg-
ative strain rate (start of unloading) the effect of oxygen is virtually
eliminated. This leads to the conclusion that any heat treatment
that would show more rapid relaxation at the crack tip (i.e. more
rapid primary creep) would reduce the sensitivity of IN 718 to envi-
ronmental attack.” This not only applies to a speciﬁc heat treatment
but also to any conditions that would increase the stress relaxation
rate at the crack tip.
3. Results and discussion
The proposed chart (a kinetics map) can be used to determine
the rate controlling steps for various competing processes. The fol-
lowing information can be determined from the proposed chart
shown in Fig. 3. One can see that the strain ﬂux is faster than all the
other processes (except diffusion of oxygen through air) at tem-
peratures below ∼670
◦C. This chart also indicates that the creep
ﬂux outpaces the NiO growth rate at 650 ◦C (for the chart, 650 ◦C
is equivalent to 10.8), which is a typical operating temperature for
718, however this would only be signiﬁcant at the crack tip if the
oxygen diffusion is fast enough to reach the grain boundaries before
the stresses relax. For alloy 718 the oxygen grain boundary diffusion
is faster than the strain ﬂux at temperatures above ∼670
◦C, how-
ever the reaction rate of NiO is still lower than both the diffusion
and strain ﬂux. This would suggest that some other oxide may be
forming at the grain boundaries causing embrittlement. One possi-
ble candidate is titanium oxide. Titanium is in alloy 718 and is often
present as titanium carbides along the grain boundaries. This oxide,
then, is a potential candidate playing a role in the grain bound-
ary embrittlement mechanism. At temperatures above ∼670
◦C,
the TiO reaction rate outpaces the strain ﬂux for the presented
data.
Several limitations to the current chart exist and are recog-
nized. The strain ﬂux is a geometry dependent value in its current
form, and it assumes the strain is uniform across the entire cross-
sectional area. This is almost certainly not true at the crack tip. Also,
some of the diffusion data presented is for pure nickel given a lack of
data for a speciﬁc Ni-base superalloy. In addition, the diffusion rate
of oxygen along nickel grain boundaries is assumed for this com-
parison to be approximately the same as the diffusion of oxygen
along a particle/matrix interface, since the grain boundary diffu-
sion data was not readily available. This still represents interfacial
diffusion and may be a reasonably close value for grain boundary
diffusion. Other limitations may exist as well.
The current generation of this proposed comparison chart is pre-
sented as a ﬁrst approach at comparing the crack tip kinetics. It is
not an absolute, perfectly correct quantitative representation in its
current form. Nevertheless, this type of kinetics chart has poten-
tial for helping identify the rate limiting step in the crack growth
process. It can potentially also provide a means for identifying
the conditions which make the material most sensitive to oxygen
induced embrittlement. This type of chart could be developed for
a variety of Ni-base alloys and provide some needed kinetics anal-
yses. The chart proposed herein should be seen as a preliminary
method for developing a means of comparing the kinetics of the
crack growth process.
4. Conclusions
A chart has been proposed that compares the various time-
dependent processes that are operational during creep-fatigue
loading, including the rates of diffusion of oxygen in air, in nickel,
and along nickel grain boundaries. It also includes the diffusion
of nickel through an Fe–Ni–Cr alloy and also the reaction rates of
NiO and TiO. In addition, the chart includes a strain ﬂux, which is
calculated from the sample geometry and the stress relaxation rate.
As an example of this method, the kinetics for alloy 718 were
compared. For the data presented, the oxygen diffusion along grain
boundaries, the strain ﬂux, and the oxidation rate of titanium, all
converged at ∼670
◦C. This suggests that above this temperature,
diffusion and oxidation outpace the stress relaxation, and at lower
temperatures the stress relaxation has the highest rate. This shows
a potential use for a proposed method for comparing the various
time-dependent processes, such as those operating at the crack tip
during creep-fatigue loading.
