abstract
CueCreNb alloys have been application to thrust chamber of rocket engines due to its high
strength and excellent conductivity. Moreover, the size and distribution of Cr 2Nb phase in
CueCreNb alloys are closely related to these characteristics. To control the ﬁne dispersion
distribution of intermetallic compound Cr 2Nb, the solidiﬁcation characteristics and pre-
cipitation behavior of as-cast Cu eCreNb alloy at three compositions (Cu e8Cre4Nb, Cu
e4Cre2Nb, and Cu e2Cre1Nb) were systematically investigated. Consequently, the as-cast
microstructure of Cu eCreNb alloys mainly consists of an a-Cu matrix, primary Cr 2Nb
intermetallic compounds with the C15 structure, and Cu eCr eutectic structure. Further-
more, the reduction of Cr and Nb optimizes the precipitation characteristics of the Cu eCr
eNb alloys, facilitating the morphological transition from rod-like to granular with smaller
size. The precipitation of the intermetallic compound Cr 2Nb is delayed, while the overall
solidiﬁcation process time is shortened with the decrease of Cr and Nb content. Due to the
reduced nucleation and precipitation of Cr
2Nb phase, the growth of precipitation is limited.
Cr2Nb in Cu e8Cre4Nb is irregular block up to 200 mm in size, and Cr 2Nb in Cu e2Cre1Nb is
signiﬁcantly reduced in size by 85% at a slow-cooling condition. Such strategy of the
content reduction is of great signiﬁcance to control the diffuse precipitation of Cr
2Nb
phase, optimizing the mechanical properties.

1. Introduction
The Cu eCreNb alloy is a kind of precipitation-strengthened
copper alloy, such the strengthening effect attributes to the
intermetallic compound Cr2Nb. Among the Cu eCreNb alloys
not only have excellent elevated temperature strength but
also enhanced oxidation resistance, good creep resistance,
and thermal corrosion resistance, which have become fourth-
generation rocket engine main combustion chamber (MCC)
liners [ 1e3]. Meanwhile, Nb and Cr elements have unlimited
solubility in liquid copper, but the solid solubility of Nb and Cr
in copper is low at room temperature [ 4]. Cr
2Nb is a laves-type
intermetallic compound that melts at 1773 /C14 C and has
extremely high hardness (1000 VHN) [ 5,6]. The alloy elements
Cr and Nb only form intermetallic compound Cr 2Nb with good
thermal stability, high melting point, and high elastic
modulus [7e9]. The element segregation of alloy with a highly
alloyed degree is severe, resulting in various inter-dendritic
precipitations, that will not aid in mechanical strengthening
or retention of conductivity [ 10,11].
The principle of designing the composition of Cu eCreNb
alloy is that the atomic ratio of Cr to Nb is 2:1 [ 12]. All Cr and
Nb combine to form the Cr
2Nb compound to preserve the
high thermal conductivity of the pure copper matrix [ 5,6,12].
In addition, fully reacting the Nb with the Cr also avoids
forming Nb precipitates susceptible to hydrogen embrittle-
ment [ 13]. A slight chromium excess is sometimes
preferred. Over the past decades, Cu eCreNb alloy has been
successfully produced by chill block melt spinning (CBMS)
and conventional gas atomization. Other available produc-
tion methods include Cold Milling, Extrusion, Hot Isostatic
Pressing (HIP), Casting, Warm Rolling, Mechanical milling,
and Vacuum Plasma Spraying (VPS) [ 7,14]. The powder
metallurgy-fabricated alloys possess good room-
temperature performance and great material utilization,
but the process is complicated and the billets size is limited
[11,15]. However, reﬁning the large Cr
2Nb particles formed
during the solidiﬁcation process is challenging. On the other
hand, ingot metallurgy essentially consists of melting, fol-
lowed by thermomechanical processing which is compara-
tively less expensive and easy to implement at industrial
scale. Anderson [ 16e18]a n dS h u k l a[ 19,20]p r e p a r e dt h e
Cue8Cre4Nb and Cu e4Cre2Nb copper alloy powders by gas
atomization and mechanical alloying. The second phase of
micron-scale Cr
2Nb phase is broken into submicron. How-
ever, the content of Fe and O may increase easily, which has
the adverse effect on the conductivity and creep properties
of the alloy. Seltzman [ 20] studied precipitate sizes in gas
atomized powder particles were much larger than in As-
Printed GRCop-84 material with no heat treatment.
Although additive manufacturing of dispersion-reinforced
alloys using laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) additive
manufacturing processes have already been demonstrated,
additive manufacturing of diffusion-reinforced copper al-
loys leads to high porosity due to the difﬁculty of processing
copper and its alloys [ 21]. Dhokey et al. [ 13,22]. Prepared the
Cue8Cre4Nb alloy by casting, Cr
2Nb phase were mainly
distributed at the grain boundary with the size of particle
was 0.7 e7.0 mm. It is possible to synthesize Cr 2Nb in the
copper matrix using the casting route. Copper alloy with
Cr2Nb shows good improvement in the hardness, nearly two
times that of pure copper. The vacuum melting method is
currently available for preparing Cu eCreNb alloys [ 22]. In
spite of this, unfortunately, no researcher has studied the
solidiﬁcation characteristics and precipitation behavior of
CueCreNb alloys during the melting and casting process.
Apparently, it is necessary to understand the solidiﬁcation
process to effectively control the microstructure of the alloy
and improve its properties.
The overall properties of the copper alloy castings con-
taining high Cr and Nb content is affected and limited by the
formation of liquid precipitation intermetallic compounds
with large dimensions. It is essential to continue efforts for
a better understanding of the solidiﬁcation characteristics of
the Cu eCreNb alloys by controlling the content of Cr and Nb
and slow-cooling condition to investigate the evolution of
intermetallic compound morphology, size, quantity sys-
tematically. In this study, a series of Cu eCreNb alloys with
different Cr
2Nb contents, including Cu e8Cre4Nb (at.%),
Cue4Cre2Nb (at.%) and Cu e2Cre1Nb (at.%), were prepared
by vacuum induction melting and graphite casting. The
basic microstructural characteristics, such as microstruc-
ture morphology, precipitation of Cr 2Nb laves phase, was
observed by scanning electron microscope (SEM) and energy
dispersive spectrometer (EDS). In situ observations tech-
nique were explores the variation of the precipitation
growth of Cr
2Nb laves phase in the Cu eCreNb alloys with
slow-cooling condition.

The microstructure of as-cast Cu e8Cre4Nb, Cu e4Cre2Nb
and Cu e2Cre1Nb alloys are observed by SEM as shown in
Fig. 2 (a), (b) and (c). It shows that three alloys mainly

the dark region is the a-Cu matrix, the gray dendritic is Cr
and the network structure is Cu eCr eutectic, which con-
sists well with a previous study [ 23,24]. Actually, the lower
mixing solubility of Cr and Nb in the copper matrix
indicates that the stronger attractive force exists between
these two atoms [ 7], thus it is inferred that leads to the
formation of primary Cr
2Nb phase. In this study, Cr is
added slightly in excess to avoid the precipitation of Nb to
form hydrogen embrittlement [ 25], which is different from
the equilibrium solidiﬁcation phase precipitation in Fig. 1 ,

4. Discussion
4.1. Determination of precipitated phase in solidiﬁcation
process of Cu eCreNb alloy
In general, the solidiﬁcation characteristics of the alloys are
mainly the direct precipitation of intermetallic compounds
from the liquid phase, which can be seen from the analysis
of the microstructure of Cu eCreNb alloy castings. After
various tests and veriﬁcations, the intermetallic compound
is Cr
2Nb phase. The TEM image and selected area electron
diffraction (SAED) pattern of the as-cast structure of the
Cue8Cre4Nb alloy are shown in Fig. 8 . The short rod-like
Cr2Nb and granular Cr precipitates are found to be distrib-
uted in the matrix, corresponding to the structures in the
SEM image of Fig. 2 .T h eC r eNb phase diagram shows that
the intermetallic compounds Cr 2Nb phase has two poly-
morphs [ 33]. Cr 2Nb solidiﬁes ﬁrst as the hexagonal C14
Laves phase or MgZn 2 structure type and transforms to the
cubic C15 Laves phase or MgCu 2 between 1620 /C14 Ca n d
1650 /C14 C[ 34,35]. Fig. 8 (a) shows the SAED of granular Cr with
a body-centered cubic structure. The lattice parameters of
the compound are a ¼ b ¼ c ¼ 0.2786 nm. The rod-like Cr 2Nb
is identiﬁed as C15-type Cr 2Nb phases with face-centered
cubic structures, and the lattice parameters of the com-
pound are a ¼ b ¼ c ¼ 0.679 nm. To sum up, the primary
phases in Cu eCreNb alloys are C15-laves Cr
2Nb and Cr.
4.2. Effect of Cr and Nb content on the solidiﬁcation
process of Cu eCreNb alloys
Combined with the theory of solidiﬁcation of metals and the
theory of alloying, the inﬂuence of the ratio of Cr and Nb con-
tent on the formation of Cr
2Nb phase in the solidiﬁcation pro-
cess was analyzed. During the solidiﬁcation of metal castings,
elemental segregation of alloys is unavoidable. However,
elemental segregation can promote the formation of precipi-
tated phases [ 36,37]. Fig. 9 displays the elemental distribution
maps of the Cu eCreNb alloys obtained by EPMA test. In the
alloys, there is an obvious enrichment of Cr at the grain
boundaries, and the particles distributed between the inter-
dendritic region and dendritic core are Cr
2Nb particles. Segre-
gation is attributed to the insufﬁcient diffusion of element
during the solidiﬁcation process. Precipitation of Cr
2Nb phases
during the cooling process of the alloys is another reason for
segregation, as well as the Cu eCr eutectic at the ﬁnal stage of

4.3. Effect of cooling rate on intermetallic compounds
The microstructures of three alloys containing Cu e8Cre
4Nb, Cu e4Cre2Nb and Cu e2Cre1Nb were observed the
intermetallic compounds morphology changed from rod-
like to granular, size reduced signiﬁcantly. With cooling
rate reduced to 1
/C14 C/s in the CLSM experiment, the nucle-
ation of Cr 2Nb phase has been found to be dependent on Nb
being biased towards aggregation to some extent. Subse-
quently, the Cr atoms continuously aggregated and adsor-
bed, which promoted the growth of Cr
2Nb. On the other
hand, the intermetallic compound Cr 2Nb, which is ﬁrstly
nucleated from the liquid phase, can be used as a hetero-
geneous core to reﬁne the copper matrix organization. This
is consistent with the previous study [ 38]. From the above
study, it can be seen that the growth of Cr
2Nb is limited due
to the reduction of Cr and Nb content resulting in a short-
ening of the growth temperature interval of the interme-
tallic compound precipitation. Then the performance is
more obvious at slow-cooling rate. After CLSM experiments
at 1
/C14 C/s cooling rate, the morphology of the Cr 2Nb precip-
itated in the Cu e8Cre4Nb alloy is rod-like with the same
morphology obtained by pouring into the graphite mold
cooling, reaching a size of 200 mm; the Cr 2Nb morphology of
the Cu e2Cre1Nb alloy transforms into irregular blocky with
a size reduction of 85%, with the decrease of Cr and Nb
content. It shows that slowing down the cooling rate, the
intermetallic compound morphology size change is more
obvious. Consequently, alloy composition content is high,
more intermetallic compounds precipitate in the liquid
phase, and the cooling rate is difﬁcult to control its size;
alloy composition content is slightly reduced, and the
cooling rate obviously controls its size.

5. Conclusions
A detailed solidiﬁcation and precipitation behavior of inter-
metallic compounds of Cu eCreNb alloys with a slow-cooling
rate (1 /C14 C/s) and reduced Cr and Nb contents were investi-
gated. The following conclusions are drawn.
1. The as-cast microstructure of Cu eCreNb alloys obtained
by cooling in cast graphite molds at three compositions
(Cue8Cre4Nb, Cu e4Cre2Nb, and Cu e2Cre1Nb) mainly
consists of an a-Cu matrix, primary Cr
2Nb intermetallic
compounds with C15 structure, and Cu eCr eutectic struc-
ture. The rod-like shaped Cr 2Nb are reﬁned to granular
shaped ones, with average size reduced from 16.21 mmt o
10.9 mm, due to the decrease of Cr and Nb content.
2. The primary phase Cr 2Nb solidiﬁed from the liquid at a
cooling rate of 1 /C14 C/s was irregularly blocky, up to 200 mmi n
size in the high-content alloy, with no signiﬁcant change in
morphology comparing the microstructure obtained after
cooling of the cast graphite mold, while the low-content
alloy caused signiﬁcant changes in the size and
morphology of Cr
2Nb at a slow-cooling rate.
3. By the calculation of Themo-calc software combined with
CSLM, the intermetallic compound Cr 2Nb precipitation
temperature decreases from 1590 /C14 C to 1350 /C14 C with the
decrease of Cr and Nb contents, and the precipitation
growth temperature interval shortens from 510
/C14 Ct o
270 /C14 C, which controlling its growth, leading to a signiﬁcant
decrease in the number and size of Cr 2Nb. Meanwhile, the
segregation decreases obviously, Cr 2Nb in Cu e2Cre1Nb
alloy is dispersed in the interdendritic region and dendritic
core, which be more beneﬁcial to the comprehensive me-
chanical properties of the Cu eCreNb alloys.
