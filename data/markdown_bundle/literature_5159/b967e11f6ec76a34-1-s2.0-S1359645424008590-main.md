ABSTRACT
Elemental partitioning behaviors are critical in determining the high-temperature capabilities of superalloys. In
multi-component superalloys, these behaviors are influenced by the competition among elements. This study
examines the effects of Cr concentration on elemental distribution behaviors and γ ′ evolution in
Ni – 10Al – 4Mo – x Cr superalloys using CALPHAD-informed phase field modeling. Our simulations show that Mo ’ s
preference shifts from γ ′ precipitates to γ matrix as Cr concentration increases. This shift results from the
competitive interactions between Mo and Cr atoms at the corner sites of Ni
3
Al, reducing Mo ’ s solubility in γ ′
precipitates. Further analysis reveals that with increasing Cr concentration, more Mo is displaced by Cr at the B
sites of γ ′ -A
3
B. Additionally, the Mo content in γ ′ precipitate decreases with rising Cr content, resulting in an
abnormal rise in the γ ′ volume fraction based on Mo mass balance. Moreover, γ ′ coarsening rate initially rises and
then falls with increasing Cr concentration in an inverted “ V ” shape, a change attributable to variations in the γ
matrix supersaturation. These findings provide new insights into Mo distribution in nickel-based superalloys and
offer guidance for designing superalloys with improved microstructural stability.
1. Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys, renowned for their exceptional micro-
structural stability, high creep resistance, and superior oxidation resis-
tance, are extensively used in turbine blades and turbine discs for
advanced aero engines [ 1 ]. Over the past few decades, several alloying
elements such as Al, Ti, Mo, Cr, Ta, and Re have been incorporated into
nickel-based superalloys to enhance their microstructural stability and
mechanical properties [ 2 , 3 ]. Among these alloying elements, Al, Ta, and
Ti are γ ′ -like elements that significantly enhance mechanical properties
by promoting the precipitation of γ ′ particles [ 4 ]. The elements Mo, Cr,
W, and Re are γ -like elements that enhance the strength of the γ phase
and inhibit dislocation movement [ 5 , 6 ]. Unfortunately, statistical
analysis indicates that the weldability of nickel-based superalloys con-
taining > 6 wt.% of Al + Ti is very poor [ 7 , 8 ]. Moreover, the inclusion of
W and Re considerably enhances the mechanical properties of superal-
loys [ 9 ], it also promotes microsegregation [ 10 , 11 ], which in turn di-
minishes these properties [ 12 , 13 ].
Interestingly, the microsegregation of W and Re can be mitigated and
weldability enhanced by increasing the concentrations of low-density
elements Mo and Cr [ 14 – 16 ]. Additionally, it has been discovered that
the Mo-rich Ni – 6Al – 6Ta – 14Mo (wt.%) quaternary model alloy, which
has a lower density, exhibits higher creep resistance than the CMSX-10
superalloy [ 17 ]. Mo also boosts high-temperature creep resistance as the
increase in γ / γ ′ lattice mismatch leads to finer interfacial dislocation
networks [ 18 , 19 ]. Furthermore, Cr significantly enhances strength by
reducing the stacking fault energy in nickel-based superalloys [ 20 , 21 ].
High levels of Cr have been incorporated into the Ni matrix to effectively
improve the oxidation resistance of superalloys [ 22 , 23 ].
It is widely recognized that the microstructural stability of superal-
loys is significantly influenced by the γ / γ ′ lattice mismatch, which is
closely linked to the elemental distribution behaviors between the two
phases [ 3 ]. The γ / γ ′ partitioning behaviors of elements, however, are
greatly affected by elemental interactions [ 24 – 26 ]. For example, adding
Mo enhances the affinity of W and Re for the γ phase [ 18 ]. Specifically,
the preferential occupation of the Al sites in γ ′ -Ni
3
Al by stronger γ ′ -like
elements (Ti and Ta) in multi-component nickel-based superalloys is
generally believed to drive the preferential enrichment of Mo in the γ

3. Results
3.1. Evolution of γ ′ morphology with increasing Cr concentration
To reveal the precipitation process of ordered γ ′ precipitates in the
supersaturated γ phase, Fig. 1 displays the evolution of γ ′ morphology in
the studied alloys aged at 1073 K. The red region denotes the γ phase,
while the blue particle indicates the γ ′ particle. At t * = 100, γ ′ particles
initially precipitate randomly in the supersaturated γ matrix. Then the γ ′
particle becomes progressively larger with increasing aging time,
changing from almost circular to cubic or rectangular. It is observed that
the γ ′ particle size distribution (PSD) is inhomogeneous, indicating that
the γ ′ coarsening results in larger γ ′ particles during the later stages of
coarsening [ 65 ]. In the γ ′ coarsening process, some adjacent γ ′ particles
are coarsened by interconnection with each other, as highlighted by the
yellow circles in Fig. 1 . The Ostwald ripening mechanism, characterized
by the dissolution of smaller γ ′ particles and the growth of larger γ ′
particles, is also observed, as indicated by the white squares in Fig. 1 .
Noticeably, the γ ′ particle count significantly decreases at t * = 6000, a
result of the coalescence and Ostwald ripening of γ ′ precipitates [ 66 ].
Furthermore, γ ′ precipitates transform into cubic in this period. A similar
phenomenon was also observed in the Ni – 12.9Al – 2.1W – 10Cr (at.%)
model alloys, i.e., the γ ′ morphology varies from spherical to square with
rising aging time [ 67 ]. It is well known that the variation of γ ′
morphology with increasing aging time owing to the competition effect
of interfacial energy and elastic strain energy [ 68 , 69 ]. Similar phe-
nomena have been reported for Ni- and Co-based superalloys [ 70 – 72 ].
These findings indicate that the evolution of γ ′ morphology is plausible
in our simulations.
Fig. 2(a) illustrates the γ ′ number density N

number density values exhibit an increasing trend with rising Cr con-

alloys. This observation aligns with prior thermodynamic calculations,
experiments, and simulations [ 56 , 73 ]. The statistic average size and
error of γ ′ size is shown in Fig. 2(b) . It can be observed that the γ ′ size first

coarsening rate. Thus, it is crucial to study the γ ′ coarsening kinetics to
clarify the distinct changes in γ ′ size with increasing Cr concentration.
Fig. 2(c) illustrates the change in γ ′ volume fraction over time in the
examined alloys. Initially, the γ ′ volume fraction rises sharply with aging
time, indicating rapid growth of the γ ′ particles during early aging. The
equilibrium of γ ′ volume fraction is reached more swiftly as the Cr
concentration increases. Furthermore, the γ ′ volume fraction stabilizes
after an initial increase and reaches an equilibrium in the later coars-
ening stage. Notably, the γ ′ volume fraction surprisingly rises with
higher Cr concentrations, consistent with the experimental findings
shown in Fig. 2(d) [ 73 ]. It is important to mention that the γ ′ volume
fraction reported by Chen et al. [ 73 ] is higher than that in our simula-
tions, attributed to a Ta content of 8 at.% in their study compared to 0 at.
% in ours, as Ta significantly enhances the γ ′ phase. Since Cr is consid-
ered to be a γ former in nickel-based superalloys, the γ ′ volume fraction
should decrease with increasing Cr concentration based on experimental
results of multicomponent low-density single crystal superalloys [ 19 ].
This is in contrast to our current simulations and previous experiments
in the literature [ 73 ]. Therefore, it is indispensable to further investigate
the compositional evolution to clarify the anomalous variation by
considering the interaction between Mo and Cr.

4.1. Evolution of γ / γ ′ lattice mismatch with Cr concentration
The γ ′ shape during growth or coarsening process is determined by
minimizing the system ’ s total interfacial energy. This interfacial energy
encompasses both the elastic strain energy due to lattice mismatch
(coherence between two phases) and the chemical free energy at γ / γ ′
interface. In general, elastic strain energy drives γ ′ precipitation towards
cubic growth, while chemical free energy at interface drives γ ′ precipi-
tation towards spherical [ 81 ]. Since the chemical free energy at interface
scale is area dependent and the elastic energy scale is volume dependent,
the shape may evolve during the aging process as the ratio between
these energies changes. A considerable number of studies have disclosed
that the γ ′ morphology is controlled by the interplay of the chemical free
energy at interface and the elastic energy [ 71 , 81 – 83 ]. Actually, exper-
iment work has shown that the γ ′ morphology is closely related to the
γ / γ ′ lattice mismatch in Ni-based superalloys. The γ ′ phase is spherical
when the γ / γ ′ lattice mismatch is < 0.2 % and cubic when the γ / γ ′ lattice
mismatch is between 0.2 % and 0.5 % [ 3 , 84 , 85 ].

5. Conclusion
In summary, we uncover the pronounced impact of Cr content on
elemental distribution and kinetic evolution of γ ′ precipitates using
CALPHAD-informed phase field simulations in Ni – 10Al – 4Mo – x Cr model
superalloys. Specific conclusions can be drawn:
(1) The final γ ′ shape of the Ni – 10Al – 4Mo – x Cr ( x = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7
at.%) superalloys represents cubic due to the larger γ / γ ′ lattice
mismatch. The γ lattice parameter rises with rising Cr concen-
tration, while the γ ′ lattice parameter drops with rising Cr con-
centration. The γ / γ ′ lattice mismatch reduces with rising Cr
concentration because Cr increases the distribution coefficients of
Mo and Cr elements in the γ matrix.
(2) Cr has a remarkable influence on the distribution behavior of the
Mo element in nickel-based superalloys. Increasing the Cr con-
centration leads to an inversion of the partitioning coefficient of
Mo from K
γ
ʹ
/ γ
Mo
> 1 to K
γ
ʹ
/ γ
Mo
< 1, which is ascribed to the fact that
the partial Mo at the corner site of Ni
3
Al is superseded by Cr with
rising Cr concentration.
(3) The addition of Cr facilitates the formation of γ ′ precipitates in
Ni – Al – Mo – Cr superalloys and leads to an anomalous rise in γ ′
volume fraction, which is attributed to the addition of Cr changes
the partitioning behavior of Mo towards the γ matrix.
(4) The γ ′ coarsening rate first rises and then drops with rising Cr
concentration in Ni – 10Al – 4Mo – x Cr ( x = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7 at.%)
superalloys. This is mainly due to a similar change in the γ matrix
supersaturation with rising Cr concentration.
These findings deepen our understanding of Mo distribution and its
influence on the microstructural evolution of superalloys and contribute
to the development of high-performance superalloys.
