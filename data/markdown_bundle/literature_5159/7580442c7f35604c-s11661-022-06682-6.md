ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
The Effect of Ta Additions on a Low c¢ Volume
Fraction Ni–Fe Base Superalloy System
ZHUO LIU and SAMMY TIN
To understand the eﬀect of Ta additions on c¢ precipitate strengthening in low- c¢ fraction Ni–Fe
base superalloys, experimental alloys with varying levels of Ti, Nb and Ta were investigated to
study their eﬀect on the c¢ solvus temperature, yield strength and c¢ precipitates stability during
long-term aging. The results indicate that in the low c¢ fraction alloy system, the c¢ solvus
temperature and the fraction of c¢ formed by aging is mostly inﬂuenced by the summative value
of Al + Ti + Nb + Ta present in the alloy. Additions of Ta are weaker than Ti but stronger
than Nb in forming and stabilizing the c¢ precipitates within the microstructure. Correspond-
ingly, the overall yield strength is dominated by the fraction of c¢ precipitate when the sum of Al
+ Ti + Nb + Ta alloying additions are in the range of 7.3 to 8.3 pct, as about 60 pct of the
yield strength is provided via c¢ precipitate strengthening. The antiphase boundary (APB) energy
was found to be relatively independent with systematic changes in the Al + Ti + Nb + Ta but
determined by the concentration of solute in the c¢ precipitates. Additions of Ta were found to
exert an equivalent c¢ strengthening eﬀect as Ti at ambient temperature, but was more potent at
600 /C176 C. The ratio of (Nb + Ta)/(Al + Ti) in the alloy determines the stability and inﬂuences the
formation of d=g and c¢ phase. Compared to additions of Ti, the addition of Ta to Ni–Fe base
superalloys appear to cause the c¢ precipitates to transform into d=g phase.

I. INTRODUCTION
NICKEL base superalloys are strategic high temper-
ature structural materials that are vital to a number of
engineering applications that require a high degree of
corrosion resistance and are capable of sustaining
signiﬁcant stresses at elevated temperature.
[1–4] Their
high temperature strength can largely be attributed to
the microstructural strengthening mechanisms that
inhibit the dislocations at elevated temperatures through
a combination of solid solution
[5–7] and precipitate
strengthening.[8–10] The microstructure of Ni-based
superalloys, typically consists of carbides and various
intermetallic phases, form in the solid-state from the
supersaturated matrix or at grain boundaries. The
mechanical performance of these alloys is strongly
dependent on the microstructure and thermal stability
of the precipitates within the alloy. The intermetallic
Ni
3Al (L1 2) precipitates, known as the c¢ phase, is the
most common precipitate utilized for strengthening in
Ni-based superalloy because a large fraction of thermo-
dynamically stable c¢ precipitates can be formed at
elevated temperature in the matrix to oppose dislocation
shear.[8–10] As c¢ is coherent with the FCC Ni matrix,
dislocations can shear through the precipitates if the
stresses are suﬃciently high. When a single dislocation
shears a c¢ precipitate, it creates an antiphase boundary
(APB) with a locally disordered atom plane in the
precipitate, increasing the surface energy on that slip
plane. Since the chemical order within the precipitates
can be restored upon the movement of the trailing
dislocation along the same slip system, dislocations tend
to move in pairs as they interact with the c¢ precipitates.
However, for incoherent precipitates, dislocations tend
to loop and bypass the precipitates, which tends to
confer a lower degree of strengthening than that
provided by coherent c¢ precipitates.
[11–13] Modeling
approaches and simulations have conﬁrmed that pre-
cipitate strengthening is highly sensitive to interparticle
spacing, particle size and the APB energy of the c¢
phase.[14–16] Over the years, studies have shown that
these parameters can be eﬀectively modiﬁed via changes
in the alloy composition that impact both the thermo-
dynamics and kinetics of the microstructure.
For alloying of Ni-base superalloys, additions of Ti,
Nb are known to contribute to the formation of c¢
precipitates by substituting for Al atoms within the
ordered L1
2 (Ni3Al) lattice. This subsequently aﬀects

Ta additions have also been eﬀectively used as
strategic alloying additions in many high strength
Ni-base superalloys. As an alloying addition, Ta has
been reported to be signiﬁcantly more potent than Ti
addition in increasing the APB energy.
[35] As a result,
many of the advanced powder processed Ni-base
superalloy containing c¢ volume fraction that range
between 40 and 55 pct utilize signiﬁcant concentrations
of Ta to increase the APB energy of c¢ phase.
[19–21,36]
However, it is not clear whether Ta provides a strength-
ening eﬀect that is more potent than Ti and Nb in lower
alloyed Ni-base superalloys that contain c¢ volume
fractions that are lower than 30 pct. For many high
temperature applications, Ni–Fe or lower alloyed
Ni-base superalloys are desirable as they tend to be
more easily processed and are more cost eﬃcient.
Recently, for many advanced gas turbines, there is
demand for easily manufacturable and high strength
Ni-base superalloy. Commercial Ni-base superalloys,
such as ATI 718PLUS, Inconel 740 and Haynes 282,
have recently been developed such that the c¢ precipi-
tates within the microstructure are alloyed with Ti and
Nb addition to increase their elevated temperature
properties.
[29,31,37–40] It is of interest to understand
whether Ta additions have an equivalent eﬀect on
strengthening and maintaining the c¢ phase stability at
elevated temperature as Ti and Nb additions in a low c¢
fraction Ni–Fe alloy system. The aim of the present
investigation was to assess whether Ta additions could
be utilized eﬀectively in this class of alloys for increasing
the overall strength and temperature capability. The
eﬀect of Ta additions on the phase stability and strength
was evaluated over a range of experimental alloy
compositions and the alloying potency of Ta was
compared to additions of Ti and Nb.
II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
A. Alloy Preparation and Heat Treatments
A set of experimental Ni-based alloys with systematic
variation in the level of Al, Ti, Nb and Ta additions
were prepared using high purity elements that were arc
melted in a MRF ABJ-900 (MRF Inc., Suncook, NH,
USA) under a gettered argon atmosphere. The com-
positions of these experimental alloys are listed in
Table I and all of the alloys are nominally identical
outside of systematic variations in the level of Al, Nb,
Ti and Ta. To ensure chemical homogeneity and
complete dissolution of the elemental additions, the
ingots were melted, ﬂipped and remelted a minimum of
ﬁve times. Then the ingots were then encapsulated in
quartz tubes backﬁlled with Ar gas. Samples designated
A0, A3 were homogenized at 1200 /C176 C while samples
A1, A2, A4 and A5 were homogenized at 1250 /C176 C for
60 hours. After homogenization, samples of A0, A1,
A2, A3, A4 and A5 were sectioned from each ingot and
aged at 800 /C176 C for 100 and 1000 hours to understand
the eﬀect of composition on the thermal stability of the
c¢ precipitates.
The ingots of specimen A0, A3 and A4 were subjected
to a multi-pass hot rolling process at 1030 /C176 C to reﬁne
the coarse grained, homogenized microstructure. During
hot rolling, the ingots were subject to a total reduction
in thickness of approximately 70 pct and no strain
induced cracking was observed. After hot rolling, the
specimens were heat treated at 1000 /C176 C for 1 hour to
enable complete recrystallization of the microstructure
and form an equiaxed grain structure prior to being air
cooled to room temperature. The rolled specimens were
then aged using a two-step heat treatment consisting of
788 /C176 C for 8 hours followed by furnace cooling to 704 /C176 C
and soaking for another 8 hours before air cooling to
ambient temperature. The heat treatment temperatures
and time were determined based on the c¢ solvus
temperature from DSC results, Thermo-Calc and Pre-
cipi-Calc predictions and the general heat treatment
procedure of a commercially available low c¢ fraction
Nickel base superalloy with Ti and Nb/Ta addition, ATI
718 Plus.
B. Characterization and Analysis Method
A SETARAM SETSYS diﬀerential scanning
calorimeter (DSC) was used to measure the solvus
temperature of various phases in these alloys, providing
suﬃcient information for heat treatment windows.
Samples with an average mass on the order of 180 mg
were sectioned from the as-cast and as-aged specimens.
The DSC experiment was conducted under a ﬂowing Ar
atmosphere using a sapphire reference. The samples
were heated to 500 /C176 Ca t2 0 /C176 C/min and held at the
temperature for 10 minutes prior to heating to 1450 /C176 C
at 5 /C176 C/min. The samples were then soaked at 1450 /C176 C
for 10 minutes and cooled to room temperature at 20
/C176 C/min. The solvus temperatures of the c¢ precipitates
were determined using the slope intercept method from
the exothermal curve.

remove the EDM re-cast layer and surface oxidation.
The tensile samples possessed gage dimensions were 7.62
mm in length, 2.54 mm in width and 1.25 mm in
thickness. Tensile tests were performed at ambient

IV. DISCUSSION
In the present study, diﬀerences in the overall con-
centrations of Ti, Nb and Ta alloying additions in the
specimens clearly inﬂuence on the properties of c¢
precipitates, including the solvus temperature, volume
fraction under same aging procedure, yield strength and
long-term phase stability at 800 /C176 C. It should be noted
that the initial microstructures of these specimens before
aging procedure were similar. The Nb or Nb/Ta rich
interdendrite eutectic was dissolved by homogenization
based on the DSC analysis on the as-cast specimens and
the microstructure. The grain sizes were reduced during
hot rolling and the residual strain was removed by
solution treatment. As the specimen were aged under
nominally identical conditions, the diﬀerences in c¢
solvus temperature, yield strength at ambient tempera-
ture, and c¢ phase stability after long-term aging can be
directly related to the concentration of Ti, Nb and Ta
additions in each alloy.

V. CONCLUSION
The eﬀects of Ti, Nb and Ta additions in a low ( < 30
pct) c¢ fraction multicomponent Ni-base superalloy
system were investigated. The focus on this investigation
concentrated on the eﬀect of these elements on c¢ solvus
temperature and fraction, the eﬀect on yield strength
and c¢ phase stability. The eﬀect of Ta on these
properties were also compared in the given alloy system.
Based on the experimental results and model predic-
tions, the following conclusions can be reported:
(1) Change in c¢ solvus temperature of the alloys
investigated were dominated by the overall level
of Al + Ti + Nb + Ta present in the alloy.
Additions of Ta is less effective than Ti but more
potent than Nb in increasing the c¢ solvus
temperature and volume fraction of precipitates
in the microstructure of the alloy systems.
