Abstract
Dual two-phase intermetallic alloy, which is composed of Ni3Al (L12) and Ni solid solution (A1) phases at high temperature and is reﬁned by a
eutectoid reaction during low temperature aging, was prepared on the basis of the Ni3Al–Ni3Nb–Ni3V pseudo-ternary alloy system. The eutectoid
reaction decomposes the A1 phase to a mixture of geometrically close packed (GCP) structures of Ni3Al (L12) and Ni3V (D022). Dual two-phase
intermetallic alloys were characterized by scanning electron and transmission electron microscopy. High-temperature compression and tensile
tests, and compression creep test were conducted. It was found that the dual two-phase intermetallic alloys showed high yield and tensile strength
with good temperature retention, accompanied with reasonable tensile ductility. The dual two-phase intermetallic alloys showed lower creep rate
than conventional Ni-base superalloys. Also, the present two-phase intermetallic alloys are superior to the counterparts containing Ti, and they are
promising for the development of a new-type of high-temperature structural material.

2. Experimental procedures
The alloys used in this study were prepared from starting
raw materials of 99.9 wt.% Ni, 99.99 wt.% Al, 99.9 wt.% Nb,
and 99.9 wt.% V . Alloy button ingots with a diameter of 30 mm
were prepared by arc melting using a non-consumable tung-
sten electrode in argon gas atmosphere on a copper hearth.

22 + B contain 500 ppm boron. All button ingots were then sec-

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Longitudinal section of Ni
3Al–Ni3Nb–Ni3V
pseudo-ternary phase diagram
The constructed longitudinal section of Ni3Al–Ni3Nb–Ni3V
pseudo-ternary phase diagram at 2.5 at.% Nb content is shown in
Fig. 2. This ﬁgure indicates that the A1 phase (disordered fcc):
(i) is stable at high temperature, (ii) equilibrates with L1

microstructure if appropriate heat treatment is done. It should
also be noted that the temperature range in which the Al phase
(disordered fcc) is stable becomes narrower as the Al content
increases.

Figs. 5 and 6 show yield strength measured by compression
test as a function of test temperature for the dual two-phase

alloys nos. 15 and 22 with and without boron are shown. The
yield strength of these alloys is high at low temperature, and
showed a broad maximum at intermediate temperature (around

is also noted that positive temperature dependence of the yield
strength is observed, and at a peak temperature (1000–1100 K),
the yield strength mostly exceeded 1 GPa.
The yield strength anomaly observed in the present inter-
metallic alloys is closely related to the fact that GCP Ni
3X
phases, Ni3Al (L12), Ni3V (D022) and Ni 3Nb (D0a), display
the positive temperature dependence of the ﬂow (or yield) stress
[14–17]. In these GCP Ni
3X phases, the anti-phase boundary
(APB) energy is highly anisotropic between glide plane and
non-glide plane. This anisotropy promotes thermally activated
cross-slip of the dissociated screw dislocation from the glide
plane to non-glide plane. The so-called Kear–Wilsdorf (K–W)
locking mechanism has been suggested to explain the strength
anomaly in these GCP Ni
3X phases [18–21].
Regarding the effect of Al content (Fig. 5), alloy no. 22 with
60–70% volume fraction of the Ni 3Al(L12) phase precipitates
resulted in higher yield strength than other alloys nos. 21 and
23. Besides strength increases due to the strength anomaly, large
interfacial strengthening is expected in the present dual two-
phase intermetallic alloys when a dislocation passes through the
interface between the ‘upper’ and ‘lower’ microstructures in the
micron size, and also through the interface between the L1
2 and
D022 phases within the ‘lower’ two-phase microstructure in the
sub-micron size. It is suggested that the interfacial strengthening
involves the processes of (i) generation of interfacial dislocations
during the dislocation propagation, (ii) transformation of the
dislocation core during intersection of the interface according
to the change of the crystal structure, or (iii) intersection of
the network of the misﬁt dislocations situated at the interface
[12,13].

Fig. 9 shows SEM fractography as a function of test tempera-

alloys, preferential fracture path was grain boundaries. However,
the fracture mode changed from intergranular fracture to trans-
granular fracture by boron doping, as clearly shown in Fig. 9.
This result suggests that boron enriches on grain boundaries and
enhances the grain boundary cohesive strength through modify-
ing electronic environment at grain boundaries. Consequently,
the intergranular fracture was suppressed. The observed trans-
granular fracture patterns appear to be basically dimple-like but
tend to be more or less facetted on a certain crystallographic
plane, reﬂecting the speciﬁc microstructure of the present alloy,
i.e. the dual two-phase microstructure. On the other hand, the
observed fracture patterns were not so much dependent on tem-
perature.
The present dual two-phase intermetallic alloys showed a
certain amount of tensile ductility over a wide range of tem-
peratures. One reason explaining this result is that Ni
3Al (L12),
Ni3V (D022) and Ni 3Nb (D0a) intermetallic compounds have
relatively simple structures, and as a result, they are plas-
tically deformable in a wide temperature range. It is well
known that Ni
3Al (L12) can deform by dislocation glide on 1/2
⟨110 ⟩{111 } systems, therefore satisfying the von Mises cri-
terion for the general plasticity of polycrystalline material [22].
Furthermore, in Ni 3V (D0 22), ⟨112 ⟩{111 } slip system has
been actually observed at a wide temperature range [18–21].
Second reason explaining this result is that the present dual
two-phase intermetallic alloys are composed of ﬁne microstruc-
tures and have certain orientation relationships between the
constituent phases with highly coherent interfacial structures.
Such a microstructure results in the decrease of the number of
dislocations piled-up against the interface, thereby suppressing
the initiation and subsequent propagation of the micro cracks.
The grain boundaries are however weak points in the present
dual two-phase microstructure and limit the tensile ductility of
the material. This may result from the intrinsically weak grain
boundary strength or hydrogen-related embrittlement univer-
sally taking place in L1
2-type Ni3Al polycrystalline materials
[23–26]. However, it was found that boron doping is effective in
suppressing the intergranular fracture in the present dual two-
phase microstructure, as the same effect has been found in the
L1
2-type Ni3Al polycrystalline materials [27,28].

4. Conclusions
Based on the Ni
3Al–Ni3Nb–Ni3V pseudo-ternary alloy sys-
tem, dual two-phase intermetallic alloys composed of Ni3X (X:
Al and V) were characterized and also assessed over a wide
temperature range by tensile test and creep test. The following
results were obtained from the present study.
It was found that the dual two-phase intermetallic alloys show
high yield and tensile strengths with good temperature retention,
accompanied with reasonable tensile ductility.
The compression creep test conducted at 1150 and 1200 K
showed the existence of the threshold stress. The dual two-phase
intermetallic alloys showed extremely lower creep rates than
conventional Ni-base superalloys.
Excellent creep strength and tensile ductility observed in
the present alloys were attributed to the dual two-phase
microstructures that are composed of the L1
2 and D022 phases,

accompanied by highly coherent interfaces between the con-

The high-temperature mechanical properties obtained in the
present dual two-phase intermetallic alloys containing Nb are
superior to those containing Ti, and promising for the develop-
ment of a new-type of high-temperature structural material.
