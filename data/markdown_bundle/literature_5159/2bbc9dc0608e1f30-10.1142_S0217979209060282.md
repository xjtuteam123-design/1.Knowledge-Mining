Mg-Cu-Zn ultrafine eutectic composites with differe nt length scale heterogeneity, consisting of 
micrometer size dendrites and/or ultrafine scale bimodal eutectics, exhibit high yield strength as well 
as good plasticity at room temperature compression. Among these alloys, micron-scale α -Mg 
dendrites reinforced ultrafine eutectic composites exhibit high yield strength of 310 ~ 420 MPa and 
large plasticity of 7 ~ 12%. Meanwhile, a Mg 72 Cu 5Zn 23 alloy comprising a bimodal eutectic structure 
without the micron-scale α -Mg dendrites shows the optimized mechanical proper ties the highest 
yield strength of 455 MPa combined with a considerable plastic strain of ~5%. 
Keywords : Composites; ternary alloy systems; alloy design; casting; microstructure. 
1. Introduction 
Bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) and nano-/ultrafine gr ained metallic materials have been 
attracted tremendous attention due to their excelle nt mechanical properties i.e. strength 
compared to conventional coarse-grained metallic ma terials 
[1-4]. However, such high 
strength metallic materials often exhibit a catastr ophic failure without macroscopic 
plastic strain due to an occurrence of shear locali zation upon deformation [5,6]. In order to 
improve the room temperature plasticity of the high strength BMGs and nano-/ultrafine 
grained materials, so far several investigations ha ve been performed by controlling the 
length-scale of the microstructure and phase select ions [7-21]. For example, Ti-based 
nanostructure-dendrite composites consisting of the micron-scale β-Ti dendrites and 
nanostructured eutectic matrix reveal high ultimate compressive strength of ~2.4 GPa 
with the significant improvement of compressive plasticity of ~14%[12].

2. Experimental Procedure 
Bulk samples of the Mg-Cu-Zn alloys were prepared b y induction melting with mixture 
of high purity magnesium (99.9%), Cu (99.99%) and Z n (99.95%) in the boron nitride 
(BN) coated graphite crucible under an argon gas at mosphere. A large-scale plates with a 
dimension of about 55 mm in length, 35 mm in width and 2 ~ 3 mm in thickness were 
produced through a squeeze casting. The structure o f the samples was examined by X-ray 
diffraction (XRD) with Cu K 
α radiation using D/MAX-2500/PC and scanning electro n 
microscope (SEM: JEOL JSM-6390). The samples for co mpression testing were prepared 
by machining the as-cast specimens to 4 mm long rec tangular parallelepiped with 2 × 2 
mm cross-section. Room temperature mechanical prope rties of these alloys were 
measured with an instron-type testing machine under uniaxial compression loading at a 
strain rate of 1 × 10 -3s-1.

Therefore, it is suggested that the compressive pla sticity of the ductile dendrites

it has been reported that the morphology and distri bution of the eutectic matrix also has 
an important role to improve the plasticity with ma intaining their high strength [19,26-28]. 
These favorable mechanical properties are attribute d to the bimodal distribution of 
eutectic structure with different length scale [26]. As well as, the rotational motion of the 
spherical eutectic colonies in the ultrafine eutect ic alloy is effective to improve the 
plasticity i.e. plastic strain of 7.0% [26]. Similarly, for the Mg 72 Cu 5Zn 23 alloy comprising 
bimodal eutectic structure without the micron-scale α -Mg dendrites i.e. fine eutectic ( α-
Mg + MgZn 2) and coarse eutectic ( α-Mg + MgZn 2 + MgCuZn), morphology of the fine 
eutectic (α-Mg + MgZn 2) colonies was changed to more spherical shape with increasing 
the volume fraction of the coarse eutectic ( α-Mg + MgZn 2 + MgCuZn) region compared 
with the Mg-Cu-Zn alloys containing the micron-scal e α -Mg dendrites. Based on these 
microstructural investigations, it is considered th at high Zn content increases the volume 
fraction of the coarse eutectic ( α-Mg + MgZn 2 + MgCuZn) leading the transition of the 
morphology for the fine eutectic ( α-Mg + MgZn 2) from lamellar to cellular. Therefore, it 
is possible to suggest that a change of the fine eu tectic ( α-Mg + MgZn 2) colony 
morphology accompanied by the increase of the volum e fraction of the coarse eutectic 
(α-Mg + MgZn 2 + MgCuZn) to cellular morphology play an important role to improve 
the plasticity of the Mg-Cu-Zn bimodal eutectic alloy. 
4. Summary 
The Mg-Cu-Zn ultrafine eutectic composites with inh omogeneous microstructure 
consisting of micrometer size dendrites and/or ultr afine scale eutectics have been 
successfully fabricated by copper mould squeeze cas ting. The Mg-Cu-Zn ultrafine 
eutectic composites (Mg content of 77 ~ 84 at.%) co nsist of the α-Mg dendrites and 
bimodal eutectic structure; fine eutectic ( α-Mg + MgZn 
2) and coarse eutectic ( α-Mg + 
MgZn 2 + MgCuZn). The soft α -Mg phase reinforced ultrafine eutectic composite 
(Mg 84 Cu 3Zn 13 ) exhibits the largest plasticity (12%). In contras t, the Mg-Cu-Zn eutectic 
alloy (Mg content of 72 at.%) comprises only the bi modal eutectic structure, whose 
constituent phases are identical to those of the bi modal eutectic in the Mg-Cu-Zn 
ultrafine eutectic composites, without the micron-s cale α -Mg dendrites. The bimodal 
eutectic alloy (Mg 72 Cu 5Zn 22 ) shows the highest yield and fracture strength (45 5 and 700 
MPa) as well as reasonable plasticity (5%). The sph erical morphology of the fine eutectic 
(α-Mg + MgZn 2) colony caused by increasing the volume fraction o f the coarse eutectic 
(α-Mg + MgZn 2 + MgCuZn) can be effective to enhance the room temperature plasticity.
