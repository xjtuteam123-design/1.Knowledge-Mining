The effects of replacement of Ni by Fe on the glass-forming ability /H20849GFA/H20850and mechanical
properties of Ni–B–Si–Nb glassy alloys were examined. The substitution of Fe for Ni caused
increases of GFA and mechanical properties. Ni-based bulk glassy alloys /H20849BGAs/H20850with diameters up
to 3 mm were formed in a /H20851/H20849Ni1− xFex/H208500.75B0.2Si0.05/H2085296Nb4 alloy system. The BGAs exhibit a
superhigh fracture strength of 3680–3840 MPa and Young’s modulus of 172–186 GPa, which are
the highest values for Ni-based BGAs. The improvement of GFA and fracture strength is interpreted
to result from the enhanced atomic bonding nature among the constituent elements by adding Fe.

Beginning in 1988, systematic studies of glass formation
in a variety of multicomponent alloys were carried out, and
bulk glassy alloys /H20849BGAs/H20850in Mg- and lanthanide /H20849Ln/H20850-based
systems were synthesized by copper mold casting.
1,2 Since
then, a large number of BGAs have been developed and
some glassy alloys have been used as practical materials.
3
Now, BGAs have been drawing increasing attention due to
their scientiﬁc and engineering signiﬁcance.
4,5 For the tradi-
tional Fe–B–Si and Co–B–Si amorphous alloys, which could
only be prepared in an amorphous alloy wire form as high-
strength structural materials about 25 years ago,
6,7 we have
synthesized Fe–B–Si- and Co–B–Si-based bulk BGAs with
diameters up to 4–5 mm by adding small amounts of Nb as
well as adjusting alloy compositions in these two alloy
series.

superhigh fracture strength /H20849

Here, we discuss the reason why the Ni–Fe–B–Si–Nb
alloy system exhibits a high GFA. One reason considered
here is based on the result that the Ni–Fe-based alloy com-
positions are close to the eutectic composition. The addition
of Fe effectively causes the approaching of alloy composi-
tion to the eutectic point as shown in Fig. 2, resulting in the
increases of reduced glass transition temperature /H20849T
g / Tl/H20850and
/H9253parameter /H20849Tx / /H20849Tg + Tl/H20850/H20850from 0.528 to 0.557 and 0.360 to
0.379, respectively, as shown in Table I. That is, the multi-
component effect caused by the addition of Fe seems to play
an important role in the increase of GFA through the de-
crease in T
l.22 In other words, Fe contributes largely to low-
ering of the liquidus and improving ease of vitriﬁcation,
which could be considered being similar to the effect of ad-
dition of Cu or Fe to Pd
40Ni40P20 and Pt 40Ni40P20 alloys.23
On the other hand, it has been pointed out by Chen et al. 24
that electrons could transfer from the metalloid elements,
such as B and Si, ﬁll the d shells of transition metal ele-
ments, such as Fe and Co, and then s-d hybrid bonding is
formed. The elastic moduli of Fe- and Co-based glassy alloys
could be increased with metalloid addition, resulting from
the strong interaction between the metal and metalloid atoms
through s-d hybrid bonding.
25 In this study, Nb holds one 5 s
electron, but B and Si hold two 2 s and 3s electrons, respec-
tively. It is considered that one 5 s electron is more active
than those two 2s and 3s electrons, because energy level of a
pair of s electrons with inverse spin direction is much lower
than that of one s electron. So that the 5 s electrons from Nb
atoms would move more easily compared with those 2 s and
3s electrons from B and Si atoms. Otherwise, the numbers of
3d band electrons for Fe and Ni elements are 6 and 8,
respectively, and hence the percentage of empty shells of
3d band increases with increasing Fe content. Therefore, the
s-d hybrid bonding nature would become much stronger by
adding Nb and partial substituting Fe for Ni, because Nb
could supply more s electrons, and Fe could supply more
empty d shells, resulting in the increases of GFA and
/H9268f.
In conclusion, Ni-based BGAs with high GFA and
high fracture strength were synthesized in a
/H20851/H20849Ni
1− xFex/H208500.75B0.2Si0.05/H2085296Nb4 alloy system. The fracture
strength of this alloy series is much superior to those of
previously reported Ni-based BGAs. Thus, the present Ni-
based BGA system is promising for future applications as
structural and functional materials such as brazing, pressure
sensor, and micromotor gear.
