abstract
In general, the tensile strengths of the solid-recycled Mg alloys are increased by T6 treatment after
extrusion. For Mg alloy with high rare earth (RE) content, it is difﬁcult to obtain the desired higher tensile
strength compared with those extruded ones from the original ingots. In this paper, according to some
characteristic of Mg-RE alloys, effect of heat treatment and extrusion temperature on the properties of
the recycled Mg–10Gd–2Y–0.5Zr was studied. The results show that the aging response in the recycled
specimens is similar to that in as-extruded reference samples. The deformation temperature is increased
in order to inhibit Mg
24(Gd,Y)5 particles precipitation and realize interfacial bonding between chips.
After peak-aged treatment at 225 ◦ C, the GW102K alloy consolidated at 450 ◦C exhibits excellent tensile
strength. It is feasible to obtain the desired tensile properties of the recycled alloys by simultaneously
promoting the precipitation of the metastable RE-containing phases and retaining the work-hardening
effect during heat treatment.

1. Introduction
Mg alloys as currently the lightest structural materials have been
applied to such uses as automobile parts and electrical appliance
cases [1,2]. In particular, RE alloying additions to Mg alloys have
been known to improve the elevated temperature properties of Mg
alloys [3]. The improvement has mainly been attributed to the for-
mation of metastable RE-containing phases during the aging [4].
Mg-RE based alloys are becoming high performance materials. In
recent years, a widespread increase in the application of Mg has
been achieved. As a result, large amounts of machined chips are
anticipated with an increase in demand [4]. Thus, it is very impor-
tant to recycle them, especially those from high-cost Mg alloys
containing heavy RE element. Several recycling processes, such as
re-melting, electro-reﬁning in molten salt and vacuum distillation,
have been proposed and some of which have been carried out [5].
Recently, solid-state recycling by hot extrusion has been pro-
posed as a new recycling method for machined chips because of its
low cost and environmental beneﬁts [5]. The Mg alloys recycled

2. Experimental procedure
The material used in this study was a GW102K
(Mg–9.95 wt%Gd–2.3 wt%Y–0.46 wt%Zr) Mg alloy. Chips were
prepared from machining an original ingot (which was previously
solution treated at 490
◦C for 8 h) in a lathe. The as-machined chips
were initially converted into low-density blocks by pre-compaction
method before deformation. Pre-compaction was as follows: the
chips were placed in a cylindrical container with a diameter of
39 mm, and then compressed at 300
◦C under the pressure of
200 MPa in air. After a gasket was removed, the pre-compactions
were extruded into cylindrical specimens with a diameter of
12 mm at elevated temperature. The extrusion temperatures are
400
◦C, 450 ◦C and 500 ◦C. The extrusion ratio and rate is 10:1
and 2 mm/s, respectively. For comparison purposes, the samples
of the original ingot were directly extruded at 400–500
◦C with
a ratio of 10:1 and hereinafter known as the reference samples.
After specimens were cut from as-consolidated samples, they
were isothermally aged at 225
◦C in an oil-bath furnace (i.e. T5
treatment). In order to get the peak-aging time at 225 ◦C, they
were T5 treated from 0.5 h to 128 h. According to the peak-aging
time gotten, the recycled and reference samples were T5 treated
to obtain the highest strength.
The ﬂat tensile specimens with a gauge section of
10 mm× 3m m× 1.5 mm were cut from the samples with an
electric-spark wire-cutting machine. Tensile tests were carried
out in 5 × 10
−4 s−1 at room temperature using the Zwick T1-
Fr020TN.A50. Vickers hardness testing was conducted using 49 N
load and holding time of 15 s. Microstructural examinations were
done using optical microscope (OM) and transmission electron
microscope (TEM, TECNAI G
2).

4. Discussion
According to the above results, the aging hardening response
in the recycled specimens is similar to that in as-extruded refer-
ence specimens. The peak-aged time of the recycled chips is also
the same as that of the reference. For the Mg-RE alloys, particu-
larly for the Mg alloy with high RE content, it is very important to
control the precipitation of the RE-included compounds and to pro-
mote the precipitation of the metastable phase in order to obtain
the desired properties of the recycled alloys. Thus, it is essential
to estimate the as-received chips state prior to deformation, and
to choose the appropriate heat treatment following the deforming
processing. The aim of making some estimate is to make the coarse
eutectic phase dissolved in the matrix in order to improve the aging
precipitation strengthening following deforming processing.
The tensile properties of the recycled Mg-RE alloy from the
different heat treatments and deformation temperatures are sum-
marized in Fig. 7. ZM7 (or ZM6) alloy mainly contains 2.4–3.0 wt%
Nd element besides 0.4–0.6 wt% Zn. Compared with ZM7 (or ZM6),
GW102K contains /H9253phase coming into being with a small amount
during solution treatment. Besides, Mg
24(Gd,Y)5 compounds are
precipitated during deformation at 400 ◦C. For /H9253is stable and per-
sists during subsequent aging treatment, Mg24(Gd,Y)5 and /H9252′phase
are only considered in this discussion. Although the chemical com-
positions of the/H9252
′phase in ZM7 (or ZM6) and GW102K are different,
they have the same crystal structure (cbco) and similar shape (plate
and convex lens). It is the metastable /H9252
′ phase precipitates that
make the tensile strength of the recycled alloys to obtain the max-
imum value as shown in Fig. 7 .H ee ta l . [17] reported wrought
GW102K showed high tensile strength due to the metastable /H9252

phase precipitates in large amount. For wrought Mg alloys with
small RE contents, in order to make the metastable /H9252′phase precip-

into the matrix. Compared with that of the as-extruded rods, the
UTS of the extruded-T5 specimens increased due to the precipita-
tion hardening. Large improvements of the UTS were obtained from
extruded-T4 to extruded-T6 condition. The extruded-T6 rods had
the highest UTS of 294.5 MPa. After solution heat treatment and
aging treatment, the second phase particles precipitated from the
matrix and retarded the dislocation movement and grain boundary
migration. Ji et al. [4] also improved the UTS of the recycled alloy by
T6 heat treatment. To obtain a ﬁne precipitation in the matrix fol-
lowing T6 treatment, the as-extruded alloys were given isothermal
treatments followed by water quenching (i.e. T4 treatment). Dur-
ing the high temperature annealing, the strain-hardening of the
extruded Mg alloys can be recovered easily. As a result, the tensile
strength of the recycled alloy decreases as shown in Fig. 7 .A st h e
recycled specimens were directly aged without high temperature
solution treatment (i.e. T5 treatment), small amount of the second
phase particles was precipitated so that the tensile strength of the
recycled alloy was only increased by 35 MPa.
To resolve the contradictory problem on solution treatment and
hardening recovery, it is feasible to make solution treatment of
chips prior to deformation. In this paper, chips were prepared from
machining an original ingot which was initially solution treated
at 490
◦C for 8 h. The aim of solution treatment is to make the
coarse eutectic phase dissolved in the matrix in order to improve
the aging precipitation strengthening following deforming pro-
cessing. Besides, effect of deformation temperature on mechanical
properties of the recycled Mg-RE alloy is considered and shown in
Fig. 6. Elevated deformation temperature (such as 450–450
◦C) not
only enables the chips consolidation, but also prevents Mg24(Gd,Y)5
compounds from precipitating during deformation. Therefore, after
peak-aged at 225
◦C, the GW102K alloy consolidated at 450 ◦C
exhibits the excellent tensile strengths, which are higher than those
of the reference extruded at 450
◦C, and almost same to those of the
reference extruded at 400 ◦C.
5. Conclusions
(1) Aging response in the recycled GW102K alloy is similar to that
in as-extruded reference specimens. Hardness of the recycled
sample consolidated at 400
◦C is highest before aging treatment,
then it exhibits the least hardening response after peak-
aging treatment. The contradictory phenomenon is ascribed
to the precipitated Mg
24(Gd,Y)5 particles. The precipitated
Mg24(Gd,Y)5 would reduce the RE content in the matrix, and
inﬂuence the amount of the precipitated /H9252′ phase, as the
strengthening phase, during the aging treatment.
(2) After peak-aged at 225 ◦C, the GW102K alloy consolidated at
450 ◦C exhibits the excellent tensile strengths, which are higher
than those of the reference extruded at 450◦C, and almost same
to those of the reference extruded at 400◦C. It is feasible to real-
ize the desired tensile properties of the recycled Mg alloys with
high RE content by simultaneously promoting the precipita-
tion of the metastable RE-containing phases and retaining the
work-hardening effect during heat treatment.
