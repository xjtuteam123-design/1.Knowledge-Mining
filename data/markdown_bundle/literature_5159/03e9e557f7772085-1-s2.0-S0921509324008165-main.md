ABSTRACT 
In this study, we enhance the strength of L1
2
-strengthened high-entropy alloy, proposed in our previous study, by 
Ta alloying. Continuous precipitation is promoted, resulting in a remarkably high yield strength of 1144.8 ±
19.3 MPa. Furthermore, we suggest a refined strengthening mechanism model, based on the superposition of 
multiple mechanisms. 
1. Introduction 
In the context of the endeavor to enhance the strength of high- 
entropy alloys (HEAs), the strategy of utilizing L1
2 
precipitation hard -
ening in HEAs has successfully been implemented [ 1 ], and numerous 
studies on L1
2
-strengthened HEAs are actively ongoing [ 2 – 8 ]. 
L1
2
-strengthened HEAs stand out for high-temperature applications [ 3 , 
9 , 10 ] and soft magnetic materials [ 11 ], ensuring distinctive superiority 
compared to other HEA families, such as transformation-induced plas -
ticity (TRIP)-assisted HEAs [ 12 – 14 ] and eutectic HEAs [ 15 , 16 ]. Our 
research group has proposed a simplified processing route for 
L1
2
-strengthened HEAs that enables an increase in strength while 
maintaining reasonable ductility by leveraging a coarse yet strong 
face-centered cubic (FCC) matrix [ 17 ]. 
Alongside the progress in processing, research to enhance strength 
through the addition of other alloying elements has been vigorously 
underway [ 18 – 20 ]. Among them, tantalum (Ta) is one of the most 
prominent alloying elements for L1
2
-strengthened HEAs. Qi et al. re -
ported that Ta microalloying increases strength without causing the 
appearance of undesired phases [ 21 ]. Therefore, it can be anticipated 
that concurrently employing both novel processing and Ta alloying 
would lead to further improvement in mechanical properties. 
In this study, we report enhanced strength of L1
2
-strengthened HEA 
achieved by adding 1 at% of Ta into (FeCoNi)
79.5
Cr
10.5
Al
5
Ti
5 
(at%) alloy 
investigated by our previous study [ 17 ]. Via careful microstructural 
analyses, it is discovered that while the FCC matrix retains similar 
characteristics, continuous precipitation is promoted by Ta alloying. To 
analyze the reasons for the increase in strength, we endeavor to quantify 
strengthening mechanisms. Given various strengthening mechanisms 
induced by L1
2 
precipitation, we apply a refined strengthening mecha -
nism model and achieve more precise yield strength predictions.

4. Summary 
In summary, we have successfully enhanced the strength of our 
previously designed L1
2
-strengthened HEA, fabricated via novel 
simplified processing, by utilizing Ta alloying. Thermodynamically and 
kinetically, Ta alloying promotes continuous precipitation (CP) within 
the grains, resulting in an increase in yield strength from 1065.5 ± 0.4 
MPa (HEA-A700) to 1144.8 ± 19.3 MPa (TaHEA-A700). A more precise 
quantification of the strengthening mechanisms has been accomplished 
by incorporating a refined superposition model, which elucidates the 
origin of the increase in yield strength. This work provides significant 
insights for future L1
