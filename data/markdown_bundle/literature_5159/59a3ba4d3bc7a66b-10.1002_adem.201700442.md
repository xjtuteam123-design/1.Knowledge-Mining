Fracture Mode Transition in Nb –1Si Alloys Triggered by
Annealing Heat Treatment
Bin Kong, Lina Jia,* Songxin Shi, Yueling Guo, and Hu Zhang
The concentration of Si plays a crucial role in the ductile-brittle transition of
Nb solid solution (Nb SS) alloys, and an appropriate annealing treatment
contributes to controlling the Si concentration in Nb SS alloy. In this paper,
Nb–1Si (at%) alloy is arc-melted and annealed at different temperature (1300,
1400, and 1500 /C14 C) for 10 h. After annealing, both the strength and ductility
increase. Particularly, after annealing at 1300 /C14 C, the fracture feature trans-
forms from cleavage to dimples, and the alloy possesses good strength and
retains decent ductility due to the low Si concentration and small-sized
intragranular Nb
3Si particles precipitating in the Nb SS. The significant effect
of annealing on the fracture mechanism of Nb –1Si provides guidance for the
design of Nb –Si based alloys.
1. Introduction
Nb–silicide based in situ composites have attracted considerable
attention due to their high melting points, relatively low
densities, and attractive elevated temperature strength. They
are expected to be promising candidate materials for ultrahigh
temperatures applications and potentially to replace Ni-based
superalloys in high temperature structural applications in future
gas turbine engines.
[1–8] T o enable the brittle silicide to achieve a
balance of toughness and strength, the incorporation of the
silicide to a ductile Nb
SS has been performed to create a Nb –Si
based system. [9–12]
In typical Nb –Si based alloys, most of the Nb SS phases are
body-centered cubic (bcc) structures that usually fail in a cleavage
mode.
[13,14] This damage mode limits the intrinsic toughness of
the Nb SS, resulting in a decreased fracture toughness and
ductility for Nb –Si based alloy. Finer grained Nb possesses
higher cleavage fracture stress and suppressing cleavage. [15]
Recently, Liu et al. [16] reported that the fracture mode of a
Nb–16Si alloy was transformed from cleavage to a mixed mode
of dimple, tearing and cleavage when the Nb grain size was
reﬁned from 60 to 10 mm, greatly improving the fracture
toughness. In addition, Rice
[17] suggested that the ratio of the
surface energy to unstable stacking energy can be used as a
measure of the propensity for brittle fracture. If the ratio is
greater than 6.3, then a dislocation emis-
sion will precede cleavage fracture. How-
ever, Chan
[18] suggested a computational
approach base on the ratio of the surface
energy to the Peierls –Nabarro barrier
energy as a material index for the ductile-
brittle transition in Nb
SS alloys, which has a
critical ratio value of 10.
Alloying elements also in ﬂuence the
ductile-brittle transition except for Ti and
Hf, which are the only elements not known
to increase the brittle-to-ductile transition
temperature for Nb.
[18] Elements such as
Cr, Al, W , V , Zr, and Mo cause embrittle-
ment in Nb, [19] and Si is also known as
being harmful to the toughness and
ductility of Nb.
[11]
In our previous study,[20] an addition of 1 at% Si caused severe
detrimental effects on the ductility of Nb accompany with a
cleavage failure. It is expected that improving the ductility and
achieving brittle-ductile transition can be performed by
decreasing the concentration of Si. Generally, Si is oversaturated
in the Nb
SS in arc-melted Nb –Si based alloys, and an annealing
heat treatment may be an effective way to reduce the Si
concentration in arc-melted Nb by promoting the precipitation of
Si.
[21–23] The inﬂuences that the heat treatment has for different
annealing temperatures on microstructure, mechanical proper-
ties, and fracture modes for a Nb –1Si alloy are systematically
investigated.
2. Experimental Section
AN b –1Si (at%) alloy was fabricated using pure Nb (99.9 wt%)
and high-purity Si (99.999 wt%). Homogeneous master alloy
round button shaped ingots were prepared by vacuum non-
consumable arc-melting over ﬁve repetitions in a high purity
protective argon (99.99 wt%) atmosphere. Each button ingot
weighs 600 g, is /C25 16 mm thick and /C25 60 mm in diameter. Three
ingots were annealed in a high vacuum heat treatment furnace at
1300, 1400, and 1500
/C14 C for 10 h, followed by furnace cooling
with an average cooling rate of approximately 10 /C14 C min/C0 1. The
arc-melted samples were marked as AM, and the annealed
samples were marked as HT1300, HT1400, and HT1500,
respectively, according to the different annealing temperature.
Samples for microstructural observation were cut from
similar positions in each alloy ingot using electro-discharge
machining (EDM). Optical microscopy (OM; Leica Optical
Microscope) was used for observation of the morphology and to
measure the dimensions of the grains. The samples were

Figure 1 shows the typical microstructures from the initial AM
and HT samples annealed at 1300, 1400, and 1500 /C14 C for 10 h

morphologies. The average grain sizes for the HT samples are
similar to the AM samples, as listed in Table 1 . Each value
reported here represents an average of at least 20 measurements,

The back scattered electron (BSE) images of the AM samples
are shown in Figure 2a and b. The AM sample mainly consists of
Nb
SS phases with bright contrast, and a few Nb SS/Nb3Si eutectic

The TEM bright ﬁeld images show that the Nb 3Si particles
arrange in a line at the Nb SS grain boundaries (Figure 2c), and
large NbSS/Nb3Si eutectic cells are observed within the interior of
grains (Figure 2d).
After the annealing treatment, no eutectic cluster is found.
Similarly, silicide precipitates arrange in a line along the Nb SS
grain boundaries in the HT samples, as shown in Figure 3 a–c.
Moreover, as the annealing temperature increases, the diffusion
rate of the Si in the Nb increases, resulting in the precipitation of
larger silicide particles at the grain boundaries of the Nb
SS.

3.2. Mechanical Properties
3.2.1. Vickers Hardness
The Vickers hardness distributions of the NbSS phases in the AM
and HT specimens are shown inT able 2. The average hardness is
216 HV for the AM samples, 140 HV for HT1300, 162 HV for
HT1400, and 196 HV for HT1500. Care was taken to indent the
matrix far from the grain boundaries and bulk silicide phases,
but intragranular silicide particles in the HT samples are
unavoidable due to their dense distribution. It is noteworthy that
the average micro-hardness values of the annealed specimens
are lower than those for the arc-melted ones, whereas increasing
the annealing temperature improves the hardness of the Nb –1Si
alloy.
3.2.2. Tensile Strength and Elongation at Room Temperature
The elongation, 0.2% yield strength (YS) and ultimate tensile
strength (UTS) of the AM and HT samples at room temperature

represents an average of at least ﬁve tests. The elongation of the
samples increases from 0% for the AM to 12.2, 5.1, and 1.1% of
HT1300, HT1400, and HT1500 samples, respectively. Particu-
larly, all the AM tensile samples rupture prior to yield. The
average YS for the HT samples increases with the annealing
temperatures: 303, 339, and 398 MPa, respectively. In addition,
the average UTS of the AM sample is 409 MPa, and the UTS of

3.2.3. Fracture Surfaces
SE images of the tensile fractured surfaces of the AM and HT
samples shown in Figure 4 reveal the fracture behavior in detail,

4. Discussions
4.1. The Effect of Annealing on the Vickers Hardness
The difference in the hardness is primarily treated as a
consequence of the solution strengthening effect of the Si.
Some Nb –Nb bonds are replaced by Nb –Si covalent bonds due
to the substitution of Nb by Si, and the difference in the atom
radii of the Nb and Si ( r
Nb/rSi ¼ 1.43/1.17)[29,30] resulting in
lattice distortion and impeding dislocation mobility, which
enhance the micro-hardness of the Nb. In addition, after
annealing, the elimination of residual stress from the annealing
heat treatment may additionally decrease the micro-hardness.

4.3. The Effect of Annealing on the Mechanical Properties
For HT alloys, the Si in solution increases with the annealing
temperature due to the increase of the Si solubility, meaning that
the volume fraction of sub-micron Nb
3Si particles should
decrease (Table 1). Therefore, the Si concentration is the
dominant mechanism affecting the yield strength due to
solution strengthening. In addition, the similar UTS values
are probably attributed to work hardening by the Nb
3Si particles.
In situ reinforcement of the intermetallic phases can supply the
Nb–Si alloy with considerable strengthening, and the strength
and plastic work hardening rate increase with the decreasing
particle size.
[40] As a result, since plenty of smaller-sized
intragranular precipitates distribute in the Nb SS for low amount
of Si in solution, the HT1300 alloy possesses excellent strength
and retains decent ductility.
As discussed, this raises the promising prospect of using
smaller hard particles and lower volume fractions compared
with conventional metal matrix composites, to achieve both great
strength and suf ﬁcient ductility. Figure 6 shows schematic
illustrations of the ideal microstructural morphology necessary
to maximize the material response for the comprehensive
properties of Nb

superior ductility and decent strength, and small-sized intra-
granular Nb –silicide particles precipitating in the Nb
SS further
improve the strength and permit good ductility, allowing the
Nb
SS to possess good deformability and fail in a ductile manner.
An appropriate annealing system may contribute to obtaining a
Nb
SS phase with an ideal microstructural morphology for Nb –Si
based alloy.
5. Conclusions
In this study, the in ﬂuences of annealing heat treatment on the
microstructures, mechanical properties, and fracture behavior at
room temperature of the Nb –1Si alloy were investigated. Finely
controlling the annealing temperature may endow the Nb
SS phase
with better properties and is greatly signi ﬁcant in its potential to
improve the comprehensive mechanical properties of Nb SS and
Nb–Si based alloys. The major conclusions are listed as follows:
1) The elongation of the Nb –1Si alloy is greatly improved after
annealing, mainly due to the toughening of the Nb SS by
decreasing the Si concentration and decreasing the plastic
constraints by decreasing the size of the intragranular
silicides. Particularly after annealing at 1300
/C14 C for 10 h, the
elongation increases from 0 to 12.2%, and the Nb SS phase
fracture transforms from a brittle cleavage to a ductile
fracture with ﬁne dimples.
2) The micro hardness and 0.2% yield strength increase with
the annealing temperature mainly due to the additional Si in
the solid solution.
3) In the AM sample, the Nb
SS phases fracture with a cleavage
mode due to b ¼ a<100> immobile dislocations. However, in
the HT1300 sample, the Nb SS phases fracture via a ductile
mode due to b ¼ a/2<111> active dislocations.
4) Designing an Nb SS phase with less Si and small-sized
intragranular silicide particles is likely beneﬁcial to achieving
excellent comprehensive mechanical properties.
