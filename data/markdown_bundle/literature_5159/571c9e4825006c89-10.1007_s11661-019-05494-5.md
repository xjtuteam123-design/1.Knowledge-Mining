Microstructure and Properties Evolution
of Co-Al-W-Ni-Cr Superalloys by Molybdenum
and Niobium Substitutions for Tungsten
HUADONG FU, YUHENG ZHANG, FEI XUE, YI ZHANG, HUAN XU,
and JIANXIN XIE
The microstructure and mechanical properties of Co-Al-W to Co-Al-Mo/Nb alloy systems have
been investigated in an attempt to identify the inﬂuence of replacement for W in multicom-
ponent Co-based alloys with 30 at. pct Ni and 12 at. pct Cr. Either Mo or Nb additions at the
expense of W reduce alloy density to a similar extent. Increasing Mo substitution signiﬁcantly
decreases the c¢ volume fraction and results in spherical c¢ morphology at 750 /C176 C, whereas Nb
substitution shows opposite eﬀects on the c¢ volume fraction and morphology. Accordingly, the
Mo and Nb additions raise and decrease the microhardness and high-temperature compression
strength, respectively. The results can provide reference for the development of Co-based
superalloys with low density and high strength.

I. INTRODUCTION
THE recent report of a c¢-strengthened Co-Al-W-
based alloy opened up a practical possibility for
developing new Co-based superalloys, which have
become an increasingly active research area over the
past decade.
[1–13] After studying their potentials, it was
found that the solvus temperature of c¢ precipitates can
be increased up to 1200 /C176 C with high solidus tempera-
ture,[2–5] mechanical performance [6–8] and oxidation
properties[9–13] as promising engineering materials can-
didate for high-temperature applications. However,
their high density and limited microstructural stability
remain the biggest challenges for their further
development.
Special eﬀorts have been devoted to reducing or
replacing the content of dense but essential c¢-former
element W. Some work studied the inﬂuence of alloying
elements on the density and microstructural stability of
Co-based superalloys.
[5,14–20] Neumeier et al .[21] and
Knop et al.[22] reported Co-based superalloys with better
high-temperature strength than most of polycrystalline
Ni-based superalloys, but the density is as high as 8.8
and 8.52 g/cm
3. As a result, variant alloy systems with
low or zero W content have been investigated, develop-
ing alloys based on Co-Al-Mo, [23,24] Co-Ge-W,[25,26]
Co-Ti-Cr,[27] Co-Ti-V,[28] Co-Nb-V,[29] and so on.
Accordingly, the density has been decreased from 9.5
to 8.5 g/cm 3, close to those of most commercial
Ni-based and Co-based polycrystalline alloys. Among
these FCC-L1 2 variants, Co-Al-Mo- and Co-N-
b-V-based alloys [23,24,29] are mostly investigated; how-
ever, in some cases, undesired secondary phases were
formed and harmful to the high-temperature mechanical
performance. This was especially true when Cr was
added to increase the oxidation resistance but further
narrow the c¢ phase ﬁeld, even when high NI content
was added to enlarge the c-c¢ two-phase ﬁeld and
microstructural stability.
In this study, the alloys with intermediate composi-
tions between systems based on Co-Al-W, Co-Al-Mo
and Co-Al-Nb have been studied with the presence of
high Cr (12 at. pct) and Ni (30 at. pct) content. The
density, phase transformation temperatures, c-c¢ two-
phase microstructure, long-term microstructural stabil-
ity and ﬂow stresses at intermediate temperature are
examined to understand their change when the W
content is replaced by an equal amount of Mo or Nb.
This study will provide insights into the development of
low-density, high-strength Co-based superalloys for
intermediate temperature applications.

II. EXPERIMENTAL MATERIALS
AND METHODS
The nominal compositions of the experimental alloys
are given in Table I. Compared with the Co-9Al-5W-30-
Ni-12Cr (at. pct) alloy, the compositional variants are
made using respective Mo and Nb as equal replacements
for W in a range of 1 to 4 at. pct. Hence, these alloys are
referred to their relevant elemental content as 5W,
4W1Mo, 3W2Mo, 1W4Mo, 4W1Nb, 3W2Nb and
1W4Nb. Button ingots of 20 g were prepared by arc
melting in an argon atmosphere and turned over eight
times for remelting to improve the chemical homogene-
ity. The actual compositions were measured by a
JXA8230 electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA) with
20 kV accelerating voltage and 10 nA beam current after
solution heat treatment at 1230 /C176 C for 24 hours, as
listed in Table I.
The samples were encapsulated in quartz tubes, which
were subsequently back-ﬁlled with pure Ar before solid
solution and aging treatments. Similar to the work of
Neumeier et al .,
[21] the solid solution process was set to
1230 /C176 C/24 hours (air cooling), followed by a two-step
heat treatment at 900 /C176 C/24 hours and 750 /C176 C/12 hours
(air cooling), as a standard heat treatment. An addi-
tional long-term heat treatment for 1000 hours at
750 /C176 C was used to evaluate the microstructure stability.
The microstructure was observed by using a Nikon
LV150 metallographic microscope after the polished
sample had been etched by hydrochloric acid hydrogen
peroxide reagent (proportion of erosion solution: 40 mL
water + 20 mL hydrochloric acid + 10 mL hydrogen
peroxide). For electron microscope observation, samples
were electrolytically etched in an etchant consisting of
42 mL phosphoric acid, 34 mL sulfuric acid and 24 mL
water. The c/c¢ microstructure was examined with a
Zeiss Ultra 55 ﬁeld-emission scanning electron micro-
scope (SEM) in secondary electron imaging mode. The
INCA X-MAX 50 energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy
(EDS) equipped to this SEM was used to evaluate the
phase compositions. The c¢ volume fraction was ana-
lyzed by the standard point count method, and the size
of c¢ precipitates (deﬁned as half of the average edge
length of the cuboidal precipitates) was measured by the
Image-Pro plus 6.0 software. For each alloy, at least ﬁve
images with the same magniﬁcation were used to obtain
suﬃcient statistics for the c¢ volume fraction and size
measurement. The density was measured by Archi-
medes’ principle. The DSC was used to obtain the c¢
solvus, solidus and liquidus temperatures on a discoid
sample with a diameter of 2 mm and mass of about
50 mg. The measurements were from room temperature
to 1600 /C176 C under Ar atmosphere with a heating rate of
10 /C176 C/minutes. The Vickers hardness of the alloys was
tested by a HXD-1000TM Vickers hardness testing
system, with a load of 300 g and dwell time of
15 seconds. Seven points were taken from diﬀerent
grains of the polished sample to obtain an average
hardness value. Compression tests were performed at
750 /C176 C at a strain rate of 10
/C0 4/s on cylindrical com-
pression samples with size of F4 9 5 mm.
III. RESULTS
A. Predicted Thermodynamic Properties
The thermodynamic information of the experimental
alloys is evaluated by Pandat software before conduct-
ing heat treatments. Table II lists the phase transfor-
mation temperatures, indicating that the c¢ solvus
temperature is decreased from 993 /C176 C in alloy 5W to
958 /C176 C in 1W4Mo and 943 /C176 C in 1W4Nb as W is
gradually reduced by Mo and Nb additions, respec-
tively. This tendency is diﬀerent from that in quaternary
alloys, in which Mo and Nb replacement for W
decreased and increased c¢ solvus temperature to diﬀer-
ent extents.
[30–32]
Figure 1 shows the predicted isothermal section of
Co-9Al-30Ni-12Cr-W as a function of Mo and Nb
contents at 750 /C176 C. Although 4W1Mo with 1 at. pct Mo
addition locates in c-c¢ two-phase region, increasing Mo
replacement implies reducing c¢ volume fraction and
formation of l phase in 3W2Mo and 1W4Mo. Similarly,
the increasing Nb substation for W rises secondary
phase d and AlNbNi2 in volume fraction in addition to l
phase. The calculation results suggest decreased the c-c¢
thermostability by the presence of secondary phases
with Mo and Nb replacements. According to above
calculation, the predicted phase constituent of the
experimental alloys at 750 /C176 C is shown in Table II.
B. Density and Phase Transformation Temperature
Figure 2 shows the density of the experimental alloys.
Replacing W with Mo and Nb signiﬁcantly reduced the
mass density from about 8.9 g/cm
3 in 5W to 8.40 g/cm 3
in 1W4Mo and 8.29 g/cm 3 in 1W4Nb. The substitution
of 1 at. pct Mo for 1 at. pct W approximately lowers
the alloy density by around 0.11 g/cm 3, slightly less than
Nb by about 0.15 g/cm 3. The density of 1W4Mo and
1W4Nb are close to the recent reported low-density
Co-based alloys Co-Al-Mo, Co-Ti-Cr, Co-Ti-V,
C-Nb-V,[25–29] etc. and commercial Co- and Ni-based
alloys IN718, U720Li, etc. for turbine disk application.
The phase transformation temperature measured by
DSC are included in Table II, together with predicted
values. Alloy 5W shows a c¢ solvus temperature of
973 /C176 C, which steadily drops to 949 /C176 C in 3W2Mo as W
content reduces. Unlike Mo replacement, Nb additions
continuously raises the c¢ solvus temperature from
996 /C176 C in 4W1Nb to 1035 /C176 C in 1W4Nb. Compared
to the thermodynamic calculation, although the trend of
decreasing c¢ solvus temperature by Mo replacement for
W is in agreement with the prediction (Table II), the
actual values are at least 20 /C176 C smaller. The measured
increasing c¢ solvus temperature with increasing Nb is
obviously inconsistence with the calculation, which
exhibits an even lowering accuracy perdiction of the
thermodynamic properties for the Nb-containing alloys
than that of Mo. The signiﬁcant diﬀerence between
prediction and experimental results in c¢ solvus temper-
ature may be from the limited thermodynamic data
input for Mo- and Nb-containing multicomponent
Co-base alloys.

C. Cast Microstructure
Figure 3 shows the as-cast microstructure of the
experimental alloys, which exhibit the similar dendritic
microstructure after solidiﬁcation. A small amount of
eutectic structure is found in alloy 5W at interdendritic
regions. The similar eutectic structure is detected in
4W1Mo but slightly decreases in volume fraction
(Figure 3(b)), and it disappears with higher levels of
Mo replacement in 4W2Mo and 1W4Mo (Figures 3(d)
and (f)). In contrast, increasing Nb content promotes
interdendritic eutectic structure with increasing volume
fraction (Figures 3(c), (e) and (g)). The composition of
eutectic structure in these alloys is analyzed by
SEM-EDS and displayed in Table III. It implies that
one phase of the eutectic structure in 5W (Figure 3(a)) is
enriched in Al, and most likely is b-(Co,Ni)Al phase in
c + b + l eutectic. The eutectic structure in 4W1Mo
exhibits the same chemical characteristics and is prob-
ably the same eutectic. Diﬀerently, all the bright
contrast eutectic phases in alloys 4W1Nb, 3W2Nb and
1W4Nb show enrichment of Nb and exhibits the same
chemical features as that of Laves-(Co,Ni,Cr)
2(Nb,W)
phases in c + Laves eutectic. [33] Due to the limited size
of eutectic structure in experimental alloys, only the
phase in 1W4Nb alloy is conﬁrmed of C15-Laves type in
chemistry by point EDS analysis. The above results
show that Mo addition to replace W may change the
solidiﬁcation path by reducing the segregation of Al
element during solidiﬁcation, while Nb replacement
appears to change the solidiﬁcation behavior by pro-
moting the formation of Laves phase.
D. Microstructure Evolution at 750 /C176 C
The c/c¢ two-phases microstructures are observed in
all experimental alloys except for alloy 1W4Mo after
aging heat treatment at 900 /C176 C for 24 hours and 750 /C176 C
for 12 hours, as shown in Figure 4. Compared to 5W
alloy, the morphology of the c¢ precipiates becames
more spherical with the increasing Mo replacement,
probably attributed to the decrease of the c/c¢ two-phase
misﬁt as reported in Mo-containing Co-base
alloys.
[30–32] However, replacing W with Nb leading to
less spherical c¢ precipiates in 4W1Nb (Figure 4) and
further cuboidal precipiates in 1W4Nb. The volume
fractions of c¢ precipitates in the experimental alloys are
given in Figure 5. It linearly decreases from 35.3 pct in
5W to 0 in 1W4Mo with the increasing Mo content,
while gradually raises to 65.1 pct as the levels of Nb
replacement becomes higher to 1W4Nb.
After long-term aging at 750 /C176 C for 1000 hours, the c¢
precipitates coarsen to diﬀerent degrees in all experi-
mental alloys, and the c¢ morphology become less
spherical and more cuboidal in Mo- and Nb-containing
alloys, respectively (Figure 4). The c¢ phase morphology
in 5W and 4W1Mo changes from spherical to near
cuboidal, and the c¢ morphology of 3W2Mo changed
from spherical to near spherical. This shape change
indicats that as the c¢ precipitates grow, the shape
changes from a c/c¢ two-phase interface to a c/c¢
two-phase coherent strain energy control. With the
increasing of Nb, the morphology of the c¢ precipitates
changes from nearly cuboidal to cuboidal, most likely
due to the relieved c/c¢ interfacial strain, which is
consistent with the phenomenon observed in the alloys
with the replacement of W by Nb after long-term aging.
After aging for 1000 hours, as the c¢ volume fractions
raise to diﬀerent extents to approach thermodynamic
equilibrium, the tendency of increasing c¢ volume
fraction with increasing Mo and Nb replacements seems
unchanged. It is remarkable that after the heat treat-
ment at 900 /C176 C for 24 hours and 750 /C176 C for 1000 hours,
the c¢ phase volume fraction in alloys with the replace-
ment of Mo for W increases more than twice as much as
that in alloys with the replacement of Nb for W. The
main reason is probably related to the diﬀusion rate of
Mo, which is slower than that of Nb comparing the
diﬀusion rate of Mo and Nb in Co matrix at 750 /C176 C.
[34]
Due to such slower diﬀusion rate, alloys with the Mo
replacement for W are supposed to deviate from the
equilibrium state more than alloys with Nb replacement
after heat treatment at 900 /C176 C for 24 hours and 750 /C176 C
for 12 hours. After long time aging like 1000 hours heat
treatment, the system is approaching to the equilibrium
state, and thus the volume fraction of the c¢ precipitates
in alloys with the Mo replacement increases more than
the alloys with the Nb replacement. The volume fraction
in 5W is 48.5 pct, which continuously declines to
32.7 pct in 1W4Mo with increasing Mo content but
raises to 74.4 pct in 1W4Nb containing higher content
of Nb (Figure 5).

E. Mechanical Property
Figure 6 shows the room-temperature Vickers hard-
ness of the experimental alloys after solidiﬁcation and
aging treatment. The hardness in as-cast condition is
signiﬁcantly lower than that in both 12 and 1000 hours
aging conditions, indicating that formation of c-c¢
two-phase mcrostructure apparently improves the resis-
tance to deform. With the extension of aging time to
1000 hours at 750 /C176 C, the hardness of the experimental
alloys with Mo replacement shows an increasing trend
than 12-hour-aged alloys, most likely due to the slightly
increasing c¢ volume fraction and cuboidal morphology,
whereas negligible diﬀerence is found in Nb-containing
alloys with the same increasing c¢ volume fraction. After
the same aging time, Mo replacement reduces the
hardness, while Nb replacement increases it. For exam-
ple, after aging at 900 /C176 C/24 hours+750 /C176 C/12 hours,
1 at. pct Mo substitution of W reduces the hardness of
the experimental alloys by ~ 15 HV, while 1 at. pct Nb
substitution of W increases the hardness by ~ 13 HV.
Figure 7 shows the 0.2 pct compression ﬂow stress of
the experimental alloys at 750 /C176 C after the aging
treatment of 900 /C176 C for 24 hours and 750 /C176 C for
12 hours. Compared to 5W, Mo substitution leads to
slightly lower compression strength in 4W1Mo and
3W2Mo, in agreement with hardness properties. The
ﬂow strength of 1W4Mo signiﬁcantly reduces to
256.8MPa, most likely due to the absence of c¢ precip-
iates. Instead, Nb additions make a signiﬁcant rise of
compression strength at 750 /C176 C, because of the 10 to
30 pct higher c¢ phase volume fraction than that in 5W
alloy (Figure 5). It can be inferred from the morpho-
logical evolution of c¢ in Figure 5 that 1 at. pct Nb
substitution of 1 at. pct W leads to a signiﬁcant increase
in the misﬁt of c/c¢ phase, and the increase in the lattice
strain between c/c¢ phase caused by the signiﬁcantly
increased misﬁt of c/c¢ phase may also contribute to the
increase in strength.
IV. DISCUSSION
Compared to the conventional Ni-based superalloys,
the high density of the novel Co-based superalloys may
severely limits their applications in the aerospace indus-
try. The high concentration of W in the Co-Al-W-based
superalloys—typically between 3 and 10 at. pct (10 and
30 wt pct)—is the primary cause of their high mass
density.
[14] Therefore, assessing the inﬂuence of reducing
the amount of W on their microstructure stability and
mechanical properties is reasonably necessary. In this
experiment, the density of the alloys is signiﬁcantly
reduced with the replacement of W by Mo and Nb, as
shown in Figure 2.
Superalloys need to service at high temperatures for a
long time. Therefore, one of the major concerns for the
alloys design is to improve the microstructure stability
of the alloys on the premise of acceptable mechanical
properties. The mechanical properties of c¢-strengthened
alloy phase is closely related to the c¢ composition,
morphology and size that aﬀect the stability of c¢
precipitates. In the present alloys, Replacing W with Mo
gradually reduces the hardness and the compression
yield strength, whereas the substituitons of Nb for W
show the opposite eﬀects (Figures 6 and 7). This is
because all W, Mo, Nb are c¢-formers,
[19] and the ﬁrst
principles calculation results show that in the c¢-Co3(Al,
W), Mo and Nb atoms tend to occupy the lattice site of
W atoms, [35] but Mo and Nb have diﬀerent formation
ability of c¢ phase. It leads to the results that replacing
W by Nb increase c¢ volume fraction, while replacing W
by Mo decreases the c¢ volume fraction under the same
heat treatment condition. Eventually the variations of
the c¢ volume fraction for the alloys result in the
opposite eﬀects on the mechanics performance.
As for the microstructure stability, replacing W by
Mo does not inhibit the formation of l-(Co,Ni)
7(Cr,
Mo,W)6 phase at grain boudnaries after aging at 750 /C176 C
for 1000 hours, as shown in Figure 4. The l phase is
enriched in W and Mo, which will decrease the solid
solution strengthening, and thus may reduce the creep
and other mechanical properties of the alloys. However,
given the small size and low volume fraction of l phase,
its inﬂuence on the mechanical properties of experimen-
tal alloys needs further study. Nevertheless, the results
displayed in Figure 4 show that replacing W by 1 at. pct
Nb can inhibit the formation of l-(Co,Ni)
7(Cr,Mo,W)6
phase, but a higher Nb content leads to the formation of
d-(Co,Ni)3Nb phase in 3W2Nb and 1W4Nb alloys. This
happens because both Mo and W are l-formers, and the
total content ((Mo + W) at. pct = 5 pct) is not chan-
ged by replacing W with Mo, so the similar forming
ability of l phase is expected. While replacing W with
Nb reduces the total content of l-formers, which, hence,
can inhibit the formation of l phase in 4W1Nb, 3W2Nb
and 1W4Nb alloys. It is worth noting that the formation
of l phase may be eﬀectively inhibited when (Mo +
W) at. pct < 5 pct as detected in the 4W1Nb. After
aging at 750 /C176 C for 1000 hours, the detrimental phase ( l
and d) volume fractions in the experimental alloys are
not more than 1 pct, which means that the experimental
alloy has good microstructure stability with lowered W

can signiﬁcantly reduce the c/c¢ two-phase misﬁt, while
the cuboidal c¢ precipitates in alloys with Nb suggest Nb
additions signiﬁcantly increase it. Furthermore, the
coarsening of the c¢ phase was inhibited by the substi-

of Nb for W.
To sum up, the eﬀects of replacing W with Mo and
Nb on c¢-strengthened Co-based superalloys are system-
atically investigated in this study. The results show that
they have diﬀerent eﬀects on the mechanical properties
and microstructure stability of the alloys despite the
similar inﬂuence on density reduction, as shown in
Figure 8. The substitution of Mo for W reduces the c¢
volume fraction, decreases the mechanical properties,
suppresses the coarsening of the c¢ phase, and reduces
the c¢/c¢ two-phases misﬁt. Replacing W with Nb
increases the c¢ volume fraction, improves the mechan-
ical properties, promotes the coarsening of c¢ phase,
increases the c/c¢ two-phases misﬁt and inhibiting the
formation of l phase. For the composition design of
Co-based superalloys, it can be predicted that Mo and
Nb may be added together to replace W elements to
balance microstructure stability and mechanical
properties.
V. CONCLUSION
(1) Replacing W with Mo can reduce the segregation
of Al during solidification and decrease the
volume fraction of c¢ phase. The replacement of
1 at. pct W by 1 at. pct Mo could decrease the
volume fraction of c¢ phase by 15.0 pct in
short-term aging microstructure at 750 /C176 C, and
decrease the volume fraction by 8.0 pct in long-
term aging microstructure up to 1000 hours.
(2) Replacing W with Nb can reduce the segregation
of Al during solidification and increase the
volume fraction of c¢
phase, which indiates that
the replacement of 1 at. pct W by 1 at. pct Nb
could increase the volume fraction of c¢ phase by
10.0 pct in short-term aging microstructure at
750 /C176 C for 12 hours, and increase the volume
fraction by 8.0 pct in long-term microstructure
aged for 1000 hours.
(3) Replacing W with Mo reduces the misfit of c/c¢
two phases, and l phase is precipitated after aging
at 900 /C176 C/24 h + 750 /C176 C/1000 h. Replacing W
with Nb increases the misfit of the c/c¢ two phases,
and d phase is formed at grain boundary after the
same aging treatment.
(4) Replacing 1 at. pct W with 1 at. pct Mo reduces
the density by ~ 0.10 g/cm

hardness by ~ 15 HV, reduces the 0.2 pct com-
