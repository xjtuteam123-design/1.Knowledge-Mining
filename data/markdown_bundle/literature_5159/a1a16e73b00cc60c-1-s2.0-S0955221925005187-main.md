ABSTRACT
A novel Si-18Y (at%) eutectic alloy enabled the achievement of brazing precursor impregnation and pyrolysis 
(PIP) prepared SiC
f
/SiC composites. The microstructure and mechanical performance of SiC
f
/SiC joints prepared 
at different joining parameters have been investigated using scanning electron microscopy, transmission electron 
microscopy, Raman spectroscopy and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy. The liquid Si-Y filler interacted with 
different phases (PyC, SiC fiber and SiC matrix) in SiC
f
/SiC substrate through the formation of coarse-SiC and a Y 
diffusion layer, thereby enhancing the bonding performance. The joint showed a shear strength of 36 MPa after 
being brazed at 1400 
◦
C for 10 min. The high temperature resistance of the joint is closely associated with the 
formation of Si-YSi
2 
eutectic phases within the brazed seam. The joint exhibited outstanding high-temperature 
shear strength, reaching 65 MPa at 1000 
◦
C, and maintained 39 % residual strength after undergoing 10 cy -
cles of thermal shock. This work furnished a way for the joining of SiC
f
/SiC composites.
1. Introduction
Continuous SiC fibers reinforced SiC (SiC
f
/SiC) composites are 
promising ceramic matrix composites (CMCs) for high-temperature 
structure applications. By retaining the excellent properties of SiC ce -
ramics, including high temperature resistance, high thermal conduc -
tivity, and low density, SiC
f
/SiC composites effectively overcome the 
limitations of brittleness, crack sensitivity, and catastrophic failure 
through the reinforcing effect of fibers. As a result, it possesses perfor -
mance that is both secure and dependable for utilization [1,2] . In recent 
years, the application of SiC
f
/SiC composites in aerospace and energy 
facilities has been studied, such as heat-resistant components for aircraft 
engines and containers for nuclear fusion reactors [3 – 5] . However, it is 
difficult to directly manufacture complex and large-sized SiC
f
/SiC 
components. The use of joining techniques for manufacturing SiC
f
/SiC 
components presents a viable strategy for expanding their engineering 
applications. In the current methods of joining CMCs, brazing offers the 
advantages of straightforward operation, minimal damage to the sub -
strate, and the capability to establish pressure-free bonding for intricate 
components of varying sizes. Consequently, it has gained extensive 
utilization in joining metals, ceramics, and CMCs [6 – 11] .
The wettability and thermal expansion compatibility between 
brazing fillers and substrates should be considered first. Through a re -
view of available literatures, we summarized two typical metal fillers 
that are common to use for joining SiC
f
/SiC composites. First, titanium 
containing alloys, such as Ag-Cu-Ti [12 – 15] and Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni [16,17] are 
the most popular fillers, because as an active element, titanium can 
promote the brazing alloys to wet or metallurgical bond ceramics by 
forming silicide or carbides. But the coefficient of thermal expansion 
(CTE) of them tend to be greater than CMCs (CTE 
(Ag-Cu-Ti) = 18.5 × 10
-6

[18] , CTE (Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni) = 9.8 × 10
-6

-6 
K
-1

the silicon-based brazing fillers, the CTE is affected by the Si element 
(CTE (Si) = 3.5 × 10

[21] ), resulting in little difference between the 
CTE and that of SiC
f

can be better controlled. At present, several silicon-based alloy, 
including Si-Ti [22,23] , Si-Zr [24 – 26] , Si-Mo [20] and Si-Cr [27] have 
been used to join ceramics or CMCs. Given the varied architectures of 
SiC
f
/SiC composites, which encompass a pyrolytic SiC matrix (including

2. Experimental procedure
SiC
f
/SiC composites were prepared by PIP process. Firstly, SiC fibers 
were braided into three-dimensional preforms. The SiC fibers used were 
second-generation fibers (Amosic-II, SiC1.41O0.04, Young ’ s modulus 
270 GPa, diameter ~12 μ m), and they underwent a chemical vapor 
deposition (CVD) process with a layer of pyrolytic carbon (PyC) 
measuring approximately 100 nm in thickness. The PyC interface was 
deposited on the surface of the fiber preforms using a CH
4
-Ar system in 
the constant temperature zone of the CVD furnace, with the temperature 
range of 1000 – 1200 
◦
C. Then, the composites were immersed in a pol -
ycarbosilane (PCS) solution. The impregnated samples were then placed 
in a vacuum tube furnace, heated to 1200 
◦
C for high-temperature py -
rolysis. Due to the shrinkage of SiC matrix during pyrolysis process, 
there is a small amount of pores inside the SiC
f
/SiC composites ( Fig. 1
(a)), showing a density of 2.4 g/cm
3
. In this work, the SiC fibers possess a 
nanocrystalline structure, whereas the pyrolytic SiC matrix obtained 
through PIP process has not undergone complete growth and conse -
quently contains a substantial amount of nanocrystalline structures, as 
reported in ref. [39] . It would affect the brazing interface structure.

materials. The process parameters were as follows: current 200 A, 
voltage 30 V, smelting time 15 s. In order to make the composition

The SiC

perature shear strength can be obtained using Si-Y filler. The main 
conclusions are as follows: 
(1) The YSi
2

coarse-SiC zone and a diffusion layer are generated at the inter -
face. The bonding strength of the joint is significantly influenced 
by both the brazing temperature and holding time. It shows that 
the shear strength of the joint is 36 MPa prepared at 1400
◦
C for 
10 min.
(2) The formation mechanism of the joint is systematically analyzed. 
Combined with TEM analysis, Y reacts with Si in the diffusion 
layer to form YSi
2

of metastable nano-SiC, as well as the Si-C reaction, a coarse-SiC 
zone is formed at the interface.
(3) The joint has an excellent shear strength of 65 MPa at 1000
