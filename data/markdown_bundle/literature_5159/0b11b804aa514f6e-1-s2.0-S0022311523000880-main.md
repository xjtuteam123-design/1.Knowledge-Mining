1. Introduction 
Tungsten alloy, as a typical high-temperature alloy material, is 
widely used in high-temperature ﬁeld because of its high melt- 
ing point, good high-temperature stability and good creep resis- 
tance [1–4] . At the same time, tungsten alloy is regarded as one of 
the most suitable plasma-facing materials (PFMs) because of its ex- 
cellent thermal conductivity, low sputtering yield and no reaction 
with H [5–7] . However, the defects in the application of tungsten 
alloy limit its development in military and nuclear industries. For 
example, ductile-to-brittle transition temperature (DBTT) and re- 
crystallization temperature (RCT) of commercial pure W are about 
80 0 °C and 120 0 °C [8] , respectively. Therefore, lower DBTT, higher 
high temperature strength, RCT and radiation resistance have be- 
come important research directions of new tungsten matrix com- 
posites [9–11] . 
In the related research, it is found that GB strengthening (or 
Hall-Petch strengthening) can effectively improve the compre- 
hensive mechanical properties of materials, and lower the DBTT 
[12] . Meanwhile, the impurity elements that segregate at the grain 
boundary have a signiﬁcant inﬂuence on the fracture toughness 
of tungsten [ 13 , 14 ]. Through severe plastic deformation, grains 
are reﬁned, the volume fraction of grains is increased, and the

thus improving the low-temperature toughness of tungsten alloys. 
In addition, grain boundaries can be used as effective sinks of 
radiation-induced point defects, thus improving the radiation 
resistance. It can also inhibit the growth of tungsten particles and 
improve the mechanical properties of tungsten alloy by doping 
the second phase. The grain reﬁnement induced by deformation 
techniques in metals and alloys is a well-known response [ 9 , 15 ]. 
For example, the cold-rolled 0.1 mm thick W foil and K-doped W 
foil are ductile at ambient temperature, with a UTS of ∼2 GPa and 
∼3 GPa at room temperature, respectively [ 16 , 17 ]. Nevertheless, it 
can result in an absolute majority of high-angle grain boundaries 
(HAGBs) which are weak in relieving the strain incompatibility 
and crack sensitivity [ 9 , 18 ]. The doped second phase needs to 
have the characteristics of high hardness, high melting point 
and good high temperature stability, so it is usually dominated 
by oxides and carbides, such as Y 2 O 3 , HfO 2 , ZrC, TiC and so on 
[19–24] . The dispersed carbides or oxides can signiﬁcantly improve 
the high temperature strength, recrystallization temperature and 
creep resistance of tungsten [25] . In addition, the GBS region 
and particle-matrix interface region with large volume fraction 
in these materials also provide sinks for radiation-induced point 
defects, thus improving the radiation resistance. For example, 
W-HfO 2 alloy can achieve 97% high densiﬁcation when sintered 
at low temperature, and its grain size is only about 200 nm, and 
its ultra-high compressive strength can reach 3GPa [26] . Ultra-ﬁne

and the reasons for the improvement of tungsten alloy properties 
were explored. 
2. Experiment 
In this work, pure W powder (average particle size of about 
2 μm, purity of 99.9%, trace metal elements), m-ZrO 2 powder (av- 
erage particle size of about 500 nm, purity of 99.9%) and Y 2 O 3 
powder (average particle size of about 1 μm, purity of 99.9%) 
were used as raw materials. W-0.5wt%Y 2 O 3 (WY), W-0.5wt%Y 2 O 3 - 
0.5wt%ZrO 2 (WYZ05), W-0.5wt%Y 2 O 3 -1.0wt%ZrO 2 (WYZ10) and W- 
0.5wt% Y 2 O 3 -2.0wt% ZrO 2 (WYZ20) were prepared. Roller ball mill 
was used for ball milling for 48 hours, the ratio of ball to material 
was 1: 6, and the rotating speed was 240rpm. Considering the in- 
ﬂuence of impurities on the test, the ball milling medium was ZrO 2 
ball. The milled powder was put into a graphite mold with a di- 
ameter of 30 mm for solid-phase sintering by spark plasma sinter- 
ing (SPS). Under the uniaxial pressure of 40 MPa, it was heated to 
20 0 0 °C by pulse current (10 0 0A-50 0 0A). The heating rates above 
600 °C and below 600 °C are 120 °C/min and 100 °C/min, respectively, 
and they are cooled with the furnace. All samples were sintered by 
the same sintering process. The thickness of the sintered sample is 
about 14 mm. The actual density of sintered samples was measured 
according to Archimedes principle. The formula for calculating the 
relative density is: 
ε = ρ/ρ0 (1) 
Where ε is the relative density, ρ is the actual density and ρ0 is 
the theoretical density. The theoretical density of as-sintered sam- 
ples is calculated according to the fractions and theoretical densi- 
ties of each component, in which the theoretical density of W is 
19.3 g/cm 3 , the theoretical density of ZrO 2 is 5.89 g/cm 3 , and the 
theoretical density of Y 2 O 3 is 5.01 g/cm 3 . The microhardness of the 
sample was measured by HVT-10 0 0 microhardness tester. The load 
was 200 g and the retention time was 10s. Each sample is collected 
at 30 positions, and the maximum and minimum values are re- 
moved and then averaged. The grain size of tungsten is calculated 
by electron backscattering diffraction (EBSD). The particle size is 
obtained by counting the sizes of not less than 200 particles by 
Nano measure software. The volume fraction is obtained by count- 
ing the area fraction of particles in not less than 20 SEM images 
by Image-pro plus software. TEM samples is ﬁrst polished to about 
50 μm by sandpaper, and then bombarded the surface of the sam- 
ple with high energy ion beam, so that the sample is gradually 
thinned (Gatan 695). The microstructure of the sample was char- 
acterized by transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEM-2100). 
The test sample (working length is 5 mm and effective cross- 
sectional area is 1.5 mm ×1 mm) as shown in Fig. 1 is obtained by 
wire cutting. After polishing, the tensile test was carried out on 
Instron-5582 double-column electronic universal testing machine 
at a constant rate of 0.06 mm/min, and the tensile temperatures 
were 300 °C, 400 °C and 500 °C.

also stabilize the electronic structure of vacancy and impurity in 
ZrO 2 crystal, thus changing the crystal structure of m-ZrO 2 [33] . 
The oxide particles at GBs could pin the GBs, and increase the high 
temperature strength. But the oxide particles at the GBs may also 
introduce stress concentrations and become the possible sites of 
crack initiation [34] , which would reduce the ductility. This can be 
the reason why WYZ05 has a lower ductility than WY alloy.

3.2.2. Tensile properties of tungsten alloys 
Fig. 10 shows the engineering stress-strain curves of WY, 
WYZ05, WYZ10 and WYZ20 alloys at different tem peratures. The 
ultimate tensile strength of each sample at different tem peratures 
can be found from the ﬁgure. Because the extensometer is not used 
to correct the curve, the total elongation of the material is mea- 
sured by vernier caliper. Four kinds of alloy materials show typical 
brittle fracture at 300 °C. When the temperature rises above 300 °C, 
the engineering stress-engineering strain curve shows three typical 
deformation stages of plastic materials: elastic deformation, plastic 
deformation and fracture, and there is obvious yield phenomenon. 
In the elastic deformation stage, the engineering stress increases 
rapidly with the increase of engineering strain, reaching the max- 
imum value-ultimate tensile strength (UTS). In the plastic defor- 
mation stage, with the further increase of engineering strain, the 
engineering stress decreases slightly. Then the engineering strain 
reaches the maximum value and the specimen breaks. When the 
temperature rises to 400 °C, the material shows the characteris- 
tics of plastic deformation, it shows that the DBTT of SPS sintered 
tungsten alloy is between 30 0 °C and 40 0 °C, and the nil ductility 
temperature (NDT) is about 300 °C. Generally, the DBTT of com- 
mercial pure W and as-sintered tungsten alloys that researchers 
have recently paid attention to is mostly between 40 0 °C and 60 0 °C 
[ 13 , 14 ]. Generally, in order to further reduce the DBTT of tung- 
sten alloys, researchers usually choose severe plastic deformation 
to produce a large number of broken crystals. However, the NDT of 
tungsten alloys in this work is lower than that of most as-sintered 
tungsten alloys.

grains, and the blocking effect on dislocations in the crystal re-

5. Conclusions 
W-0.5 wt% Y 2 O 3 -(0,0.5,1.0,2.0) wt% ZrO 2 material was prepared 
by SPS method from W and ZrO 2 /Y 2 O 3 powder, and the mechani- 
cal properties and microstructure of the material were studied. The 
results can be summarized as follows: 
1) The relative density of all samples is about 98%. The grain size 
of WYZ05 alloy is about 2.7 μm, which is much smaller than 
that of WY alloy, indicating that the addition of ZrO 2 can inhibit 
the grain growth during sintering. The DBTT of W-0.5wt% Y 2 O 3 - 
(0,0.5wt%, 1.0wt%, 2.0wt%) ZrO 2 material measured by tensile 
test is between 300 °C and 400 °C. In addition, the hardness at 
room temperature is higher than that of WY alloy. 
2) Adding a small amount of ZrO 2 can improve the high temper- 
ature strength of tungsten materials. However, excessive addi- 
tion of ZrO 2 (more than 1.0%) will greatly reduce the elonga- 
tion, but the tensile strength will still increase. The ultimate 
tensile strength and total elongation of WYZ05 alloy at 500 °C 
are 525.2 MPa and 13.5%, respectively. 
3) Microstructure analysis shows that the improvement of the 
strength of tungsten alloy is mainly due to the ﬁne grain 
strengthening caused by Y 2 O 3 /ZrO 2 pinning grain boundaries to 
prevent grain growth, and the oxide particles dispersed inside 
tungsten grains can promote the Orowan strengthening caused 
by dislocation movement.
