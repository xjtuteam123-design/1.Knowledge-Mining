Alloy Constitution Dependence of Strength and Deformation 
in Aluminum-Titanium-Vanadium Ternary Alloys 
Containing Gamma+Beta Dual Phase Microstructures 
Tohru Takahashi 1, a , Na Liu 1 , Yusuke Yazawa 1 and Takuya Nunome 1 
1 Department of Mechanical Systems Engineering, Faculty of Technology, Tokyo University of 
Agriculture and Technology, Koganei, Tokyo 184-8588, Japan 
atakahas@cc.tuat.ac.jp 
Keywords: intermetallics, titanium aluminide, ternary alloys, vanadium, dual phase microstructure 
alloy constitution, stress vs. strain curve, compressive creep, composition dependence 
 
Abstract. Compression and compressive creep behavior was studied on Al-Ti-V ternary alloys 
containing gamma+beta dual phase microstructures; the gamma phase was based on an L1 
0 face 
centered tetragonal lattice and the beta phase on a disordered body centered cubic lattice. Yield 
strength and its temperature dependence have been compared with those in the gamma and/or beta 
single phase materials. The ternary alloy compositions were located on one assumed conjugate line 
across the gamma+beta dula phase field: the terminal compositions for the gamma and beta phase 
constituents were Al 51 Ti 40 V9 and Al 35 Ti 20 V45 , respectively (numbers in atomic %). Three other alloys 
were prepared that contained different fractions of the constituent gamma and beta phases. The 
Al 47 Ti 35V18 , Al 43Ti 30 V27 , and Al 39 Ti 25 V36 alloys contained beta phase by about 22, 57, and 76 % in 
their area fractions. All these alloys showed limited deformability at temperature below 900K. The 
0.2% proof stresses of the alloys were described in a similar way as a combination law at the room 
temperature; the 0.2% proof stress increased from about 500 to 1000 MPa with increasing the 
vanadium content. The high strength of the alloys containing high level of vanadium retained up to 
900K, but the proof stress drastically diminished as the temperature was raised above 900K. Under 
compressive creep tests performed at temperatures ranging from 1100 to 1200 K, the minimum creep 
rates were smaller in the alloys containing less vanadium, and this could be ascribed to the fact that 
the beta phase was much softer than the gamma phase at higher temperatures than about 1000K. 
Introduction 
The gamma titanium aluminide is thought to be prospective for light-weight and heat-resistant 
applications because of its high specific strength at elevated temperatures[1-4]. The present authors 
are interested in the alloying effect of vanadium upon microstructure and strength of the alloys 
containing gamma+beta dual phase microstructures in aluminum-titanium-vanadium ternary alloy 
system[5-7]. The gamma single phase field expands toward aluminum- and vanadium-rich 
compositions in this alloy system[8-10], and vanadium addition stabilized beta phase solid solution at 
higher vanadium content to constitute a gamma+beta dual phase microstructure. In the present work, 
a conceivable conjugate line has been drawn on the aluminum-titanium-vanadium ternary alloy phase 
diagram, and five different alloys were prepared by arc melting. The two of them had alloy 
compositions near the both end point of the conjugate line, and the other three were located at the four 
fold positions between the two terminal compositions. The atomic compositions of the alloys were 
Al 51Ti 40 V9, Al 47 Ti 35V18 , Al 43Ti 30 V27, Al 39 Ti 25 V36 , and Al 35 Ti 20 V45 , the numbers after the elements' 
names showing their atomic fractions. The three alloys whose compositions were standing between 
the terminal compositions were expected to contain about 78%:22%, 48%:52%, and 27%:73% 
fractions of gamma and beta phases, respectively. The microstructure and crystallography have been 
characterized, and their mechanical behavior has been investigated in compression tests performed at 
various temperatures. Compressive creep behavior has also been studied on these five alloys.

Experimental Procedure 
Materials and Materials Preparation 
Chemical compositions of the alloys that have been prepaed in the present study are as follows, and 
they are shown on Fig.1 that illustrates the aluminum-titanium-vanadium ternary alloy phase diagram 
in an isothermal section at 1073K[9]. The atomic compositions of the prepared alloys were 
Al 51Ti 40 V9, Al 47 Ti 35V18 , Al 43 Ti 30 V27, Al 39 Ti 25 V36 , and Al 35 Ti 20 V45 , and hereafter they will be referred 
to as 9V, 18V, 27V, 36V, and 45V, respectivey by their atomic fractions of vanadium. These five 
alloys were prepared by arc melting in an argon atmosphere. The rectangular parallelepiped 
specimens with dimensions of about 5mm × 5mm × 8mm were cut from the ingot and were compressed 
at 1200-1300 K to about 50 % reduction in height. They were then vacuum annealed at 1300-1400K 
to produce stabilized microstructure with equiaxed shape as far as possible. Small specimens with 
dimensions of about 2mm × 2mm × 3mm were sparc cut and finished by polishing with emery papers.

Compression and Compressive Creep Tests 
Compression tests were performed in air at temperatures ranging from 293K to 1200K. When the test 
was carried out at elevated temperatures, the temperature of specimen was monitored with an 
alumel-chromel type thermocouple directly welded to the specimen surface. The temperature was 
kept constant within about 1 degree error from the set value. The true strain vs. true strain curves were 
obtained from the load-displacement curves recorded on the chart. Compressive creep tests were 
carried out in vacuum (10 -3Pa). The temperature of the specimen was monitored and kept constant in 
a similar way as in the compression test. Displacement of the creep specimen was measured with an 
LVDT type sensor that was installed directly on the quartz rod pushing the specimen. From the 
time-displacement curve recorded on the chart, creep curves and creep rate curves were obtained. The 
applied load was intermittently adjusted to keep the true stress constant by adding small weights to the 
creep load. The adjustment was made accordingly to the displacement of the specimen on an 
assumption of uniform deformation in order to keep the applied true stress constant within less than 
1% error.

Compression Behavior 
Compression behavior of the materials was tested at various temperatures in air, but not all result 
could be included in the present paper. Fig. 4(a) and Fig.4(b) show the stress vs. strain curves of the 
five alloys tested under compression at room temperature and 1200K, respectively. The 9V alloy 
containing gamma single-phase polycrystals showed about 500MPa yield strength at 293K, and it is 
deformable up to about 0.25 strain. The 45V alloy containing coarse grained beta phase showed 
almost negligible deformability at room temperature, but its yield stress was as high as 1000MPa. The 
gamma+beta dual phase alloys, 18V, 27V, and 36V, showed intermediate strength in between 9V and 
45V alloys. The 36V alloy containing about 73% beta phase showed a high yield strength that is near 
that in 45V alloy, and at the same time it showed about 15% deformability under the compression test 
at room temperature. It is conceivable that the brittleness of the beta phase can somewhat ameriolated 
when the softer gamma phase grains/particles are coexistent in the microstructure. Positive 
dependence of yield strength upon the beta phase fraction can be seen in Fig. 4(a). 
The five alloys did not largely soften below about 900K, but they all became much softer and highly 
deformable at temperature of 900 K and above. As can be seen in Fig. 4(b) the alloy containing more 
beta phase shows lower yield strength at 1200K because at this temperature the beta phase becomes 
much softer than the gamma phase. The 9V and 18V alloys showed bigger strain softening at 1200K. 
Strain softening was probably coming from the behavior of the gamma phase, because the degree of 
strain softening was smaller in the alloys containing more beta phase.

Compressive Creep Behavior 
Fig. 6(a) shows the creep curves observed in the compressive creep tests performed at 1100K under a 
constant true stress of 159MPa on the five alloys. The shape of creep curve is similar among the five 
alloys; the creep curves are comprised of a small normal transient, minimum or steady creep rate 
region. In the 45V alloy, a slight acceleration was observed after the minimum creep rate. As can be 
expected from the constitution dependence shown in Fig. 5, the creep rate decreases as the vanadium 
content is decreased due to the softness of the beta phase as compared with the gamma phase. Creep 
rate strongly depended upon the alloy constitution.

Summary 
Five different aluminum-titanium-vanadium alloys containing 9, 18, 27, 36, and 45 mol% vanadium 
were prepared to investigate the constitution dependence of microstructure, strength, and creep rate 
along a conceivable conjugate line (tie line) on the phase diagram. Results are summarized as follows. 
(1) Five ternary alloys have been prepared; 9V, 18V, 27V, 36V, and 45V (Al 
51Ti 40 V9, Al 47 Ti 35V18 , 
Al 43 Ti 30 V27, Al 39 Ti 25 V36 , and Al 35 Ti 20 V45 ). These compositions are located on a conceivable tie line 
across the gamma+beta dual phase region. The 9V and 45V alloys are located on the extreme ends of 
the tie line. The numbers after the element symbols shows the atomic % of the element. 
(2) Gamma+beta dual phase microstructures were observed in the 18V, 27V, and 36V, the beta phase 
fractions of which were measured to be 22, 52, and 73 %, respectively. 
(3) Crystallographic characterization showed the lattice parameters in both gamma phase (L1 0 face 
centered tetragonal) and beta phase (body centered cubic) are almost common among all the alloys. 
(4) Beta single-phase alloy, 45V, was hard, and almost brittle at room temperature. This considerably 
softens as the temperature was raised beyond 900K. 
(5) Alloys containing gamma+beta micro duplex structure showed a superior strength up to 900K, 
and this can be explained by a mixture rule. Above 1000K, these alloys became much softer. 
(6) The gamma+beta dual phase alloys with about 22% fraction of beta was the strongest among the 
five alloys at 1000K. This is interesting, but can not be well characterized at present. 
(7) At and above 1100K, the gamma single-phase alloy, 9V, was the strongest among the alloys. 
(8) Creep behavior was investigated in compressive creep tests. The alloys with more beta phase 
showed larger creep rates. Gamma single phase alloy showed the smallest creep rate.
