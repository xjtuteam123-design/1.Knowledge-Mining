OXIDATION RESISTANCE AND HIGH-TEMPERATURE 
STRENGTH OF WC − Co − Ni − Re(Mn) HARD ALLOYS 
I. B. Panteleev, T. V. Lukashova, and S. S. Ordan’yan 
UDC 621.762 
The basic regularities of hard alloy oxidation in the WC − Co − Ni − Re(Mn) system have been 
established. The temperature dependence of bending strength in the operation temperature range of 
turning tools has been investigated. Cutting blades from the investigated alloys with a modified metal 
binder have high operational properties, which is confirmed by tests. 
Keywords: hard metal, tungsten carbide, cobalt, nickel , rhenium, manganese, scaling resistance, 
oxidation resistance, high temperature strength, cutting tools. 
In metal cutting (machining), temperatures of about 700-800 °C (according to a published report [1], even as 
high as 1100 °C) may develop in the area of contact of the cutting tool with the material being machined. This causes 
oxidation (scaling) of the surface of the cutting edge, decr ease in the mechanical strength of the cutting tool material, 
and increase in its adhesion to the treated (machined) material, i.e., decrease in the effectiveness of the cutting material. 
The heat resistance of WC − Co − Ni − Re(Mn) hard alloys depends on the properties of the metal binder 
where, upon introduction of rhenium, the interatomic bond becomes stronger, which means that the fusion temperature 
rises. This also explains the Co − Re and Ni − Re phase diagrams which are characterized by the presence of a peritectic 
[2, 3]. Introduction of Ni, Re, and Mn, compared to Co, enhan ces wear and oxidation (scaling) resistance of hard alloys 
on account of formation of more oxidation-resistant alloys in the Co − Ni − Mn − W − Re − C system. Also, 
introduction of rhenium increases high-temperature strength [4]. This indicates that in a complex cementing alloy 
diffusional processes slow down considerably due to elevation of the strength of the metallic bond. 
The specimens were obtained by a technology described in [5, 6]. The metal binder content was 13.2 vol.% 
which matches cobalt concentration in the standard VK8 (WC −8% Co) alloy. The oxidation resistance of the hard alloy 
specimens was determined under nonisothermic conditions of heating of the specimens to 900°C at the rate of 300 deg/h 
in air with continuous recording of the change in the specimen mass. In each test the oxidation initiation temperature 
was determined and the weight gain Δm per unit surface was plotted against the temperature (Figs. 1-3).

As regards oxidation resistance, all alloy sp ecimens are superior to the hard alloy WC − Co. Simultaneous 
introduction of Ni − Re or Ni − Mn into the cobalt binder increases the temp erature of initiation of active oxidation by 
20-70°C and reduces the oxidation rate in the binder series CoNi, CoMn → CoNiMn, CoRe, CoNi → CoNiRe → NiRe. 
So, nickel produces the most beneficial effect on the oxidation (scaling) resistance, followed by manganese and 
rhenium. Even though the composition of the oxide film was not studied, nevertheless the protective role of nickel 
seems to consist in formation of nickel oxide film which is more stable than cobalt oxide. To a lesser degree, this applies 
to rhenium and manganese oxides. 
Such behavior of hard alloys when heated in air is typical especially for metal binders of a complex 
composition. The reasons for the increase in the oxidation resistance of hard al loys are the same as that for stainless and 
alloy steels. The reason for the elevati on of heat resistance of alloy steels is that the alloying element, having 
strengthened the lattice and reduced the diffusional mobility of the alloy atoms, enters the lattice of the oxide of the base 
metal, forming a film of complex oxides, oxygen diffusion through which slows down [7]. It is difficult to increase 
oxidation resistance of WC-based hard alloys, considering th e physicochemical properties of tungsten oxide and actual 
WC content in the alloys. The cementing phase which also de termines the mechanical characteristics of the alloys, may 
affect the corrosion properties. 
For selecting a cutting tool material, high oxidation resistance alone is not enough, also necessary is high 
strength at T = 800-900°C, which hard alloys with a cobalt-based binder do not possess. Moreover, a host of properties 
like heat resistance and strength, especially at the working cutting temperature (700-800 °C), is important. To appraise 
these properties, high-temperature strength of hard alloys with various binders was studied. 
The hard alloy specimens were tested on a high-temperature vacuum unit developed at the St. Petersburg State 
Technological Institute [8]. Th e test specimens measured 2.5 × 2.5 × 25 mm; the base for a four-point loading scheme 
was 20 mm; the measurement error was ±50 MPa. The ultimate bending strength was calculated by the formula 
σbend = 3/2 ⋅ a ⋅ P/(b⋅ h2), 
where a = (l1 − l2)/2; l1 and l2 are the distances between the lower and upper prisms, m; P is the breaking load, N; and b 
and h are the width and height of the specimen, m.

The specimen was put in a vacuum chamber heated indirectly. The temperat ure was measured by a 
thermocouple close to the specimen surface. After attainment of required temperature, the specimen was subjected to 
loading with the help of a loading device. The results of the tests at temperatures up to 1200 °C in vacuum are plotted in 
Fig. 4. 
The dependence of the bending strength of hard alloys with Co − Ni and Ni − Mn binders on the temperature 
differs little from the corresponding dependence for VK (WC − Co) type of hard alloys. Addition of rhenium to the 
binder dramatically alters the dependence pattern. Up to 600°C for the Ni − Re binder and 800°C for the Co − Re binder 
the strength of the hard alloys increases to some extent (up to 20%) and decreases thereafter, but remains higher than 
that of materials having cobalt and nickel binders right up to 1000°C and above. 
The factor contributing to the increase in the high-te mperature strength is decr ease in the mobility of the 
dislocations due to alloying and perhaps dispersion strengthening. Thus, the higher strength of the hard alloys containing 
rhenium in the metal binder is natural and expected, and its increase with rise of temperature most probably is associated 
with the breaking characteristics of the cementing metallic phase (pure cobalt and nickel) and their alloys with rhenium. 
The maximum strength of WC − Co − Ni − Re hard alloys is attained in the 600-800 °C range, which 
corresponds to the temperature of the working cutting material edge. This made it possible to suggest and confirm that 
the cutting tools from alloys with a modified binder possess hi gher operational properties. This is confirmed by the test 
data for the tested batch of cutting blades.
