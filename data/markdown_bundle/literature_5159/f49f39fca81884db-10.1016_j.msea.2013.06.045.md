abstract
The solidi ﬁcation pathways of Nb rich Nb – Si alloys when processed under non-equilibrium conditions
require understanding. Continuing with our earlier work on alloying additions in single eutectic
composition [1,2], we report a detailed characterization of the microstructures of Nb – Si binary alloys
with wide composition range (10 – 25 at% Si). The alloys are processed using chilled copper mould suction
casting. This has allowed us to correlate the evolution of microstructure and phases with different
possible solidiﬁcation pathways. Finally these are correlated with mechanical properties through studies
on deformation using mechanical testing under indentation and compressive loads. It is shown that
microstructure modi ﬁcation can signi ﬁcantly in ﬂuence the plasticity of these alloys.

1. Introduction
Alloys based on niobium and silicon offer possibilities for high
temperature structural application due to its high melting point
and high temperature strength [3,4]. However, they suffer from
drawbacks such as dif ﬁculties in processing, poor room tempera-
ture ductility and oxidation. The phase content, microstructure
and composition play crucial roles in determining the properties of
these alloys [5– 7].
There are several reports dealing with non-equilibrium solidi-
ﬁcation of binary Nb – Si alloys using techniques such as quenching,
levitation and directional solidi ﬁcation [8– 10]. In a major effort,
Abbaschian et al. [8] have explored the evolution of microstructure
in this system in terms of undercooling and growth velocity during
non-equilibrium processing. The experimental results and follow
up theoretical calculations allowed them to determine the eutectic
coupled zone in Nb rich Nb – Si alloys. Recently, Miura et al. [9]
have attempted to extend this understanding for the re ﬁnement of
lamellar spacing of the eutectics in terms of solidi ﬁcation para-
meters. Bewlay et al. [10] have attempted to correlate microstruc-
ture of Nb-10 at% Si and Nb-25 at% Si alloys with mechanical
properties.
However, the microstructure evolution under non-equilibrium
conditions in Nb – Si alloys is strongly process dependent and in
this paper we report a detailed experimental study on the
evolution of microstructures and hence mechanical properties of
a series of Nb – Si binary alloys (Nb-10 – 25 at% Si) solidi ﬁed from
melt using chill mould suction casting. We further attempt
rationalizing our observations in terms of current understanding
of non-equilibrium solidi ﬁcation.

test at low load of 0.2 N with a loading rate of 1 N/min is used to
indent primary phases and eutectic colonies. To conﬁrm the hardness
values obtained by the instruments the indent diagonals are also
measured using SEM to arrive at hardness values. Fig. 5(a)–(f) shows

2 N load with loading rate 1 N/min. The composite hardness of each
sample is plotted in Fig. 6(c) with respect to their alloy composition.

were indented at higher loads of 20 N, 50 N and 100 N. The crack
lengths at the edge of the indents were measured from the SEM

such undercooling does not affect the nature of primary phase in

5. Conclusion
The main conclusions from this work are following:
1. A decrease in eutectic spacings in microstructure and increase
in volume fraction of eutectic are observed in suction cast
samples due to large undercooling.
2. In the hypoeutectic alloy compositions (Nb-10 at% Si and Nb-
14 at% Si), microstructure consists of the primary Nb ss den-
drites and eutectic Nb/Nb 3Si.
3. In the hypereutectic alloy composition (Nb-22 at% Si and Nb-
25 at% Si), the microstructure consists of the primary β-Nb5Si3
phase and eutectic of Nb ss/Nb3Si. However for the Nb-22 at%
Si alloy composition we have also observed the peritectic
phase Nb 3Si. In the microstructure eutectic is surrounded by
both primary phase ( β-Nb5Si3) and peritectic phase (Nb 3Si).
4. No signature of eutectoid reaction is observed in all the
samples.
5. Mechanical properties are highly dependent on microstructure.
High strength with high plasticity is observed for Nb-10 at% Si
alloy, but the strength and plasticity decrease drastically as we
increase Si content up to Nb-18.7 at% Si. The strength increases for
Nb-25 at% Si alloy. Similar trend are also observe for fracture
toughness values. Among all the intermetallic the Nb 3Si phase
shows lowest fracture toughness.
