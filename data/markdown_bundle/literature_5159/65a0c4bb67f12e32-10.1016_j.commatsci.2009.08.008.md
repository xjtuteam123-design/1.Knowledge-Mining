abstract
The strengthening effects induced by alloying elements (Re, Ru, Cr, Co, Mo, W, Ta) on Ni/Ni 3Al interface in
single crystal Ni-based superalloys are investigated in terms of Grifﬁth work using ﬁrst-principles density
functional theory. The best strengthening effect on Ni/Ni 3Al interface can be attributed to Re, and the ele-
ments W and Ta appear to be less potent with Co having little effect. This sequence of creep-strengthen-
ing effect of alloying elements obtained from our calculations is consistent with relevant experimental
observations. The calculated Grifﬁth works for more elements of transition metals indicate that elements
near the centre of the d block of the periodic table are more favorable for improving the rupture strength
than those from the far west and far east of the transition metal block.
Crown Copyright /C2112009 Published by Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal (SC) superalloys used for turbine blades
and vanes in the most advanced gas turbine engines are character-
ized by a high volume fraction of cuboidal c0 phase (Ll2, ordered fcc,
Ni3Al based) precipitates coherently embedded in the c matrix
phase (disordered fcc, solid solution based on Ni) [1]. The overall
performance, especially the high temperature creep rupture
strength, thermal fatigue resistance, and oxidation resistance of
superalloys has been signiﬁcantly improved by the addition of
increasing amount of refractory elements [2]. However, the design
of new Ni-based SC superalloys is usually based on empirical expe-
riences, i.e., trial-and-error procedures. Further advances in the de-
sign require a thorough understanding of the creep-strengthening
effects and behaviors of various refractory elements, which has
been a challenge for current theories.
The creep process presents a universal feature and the creep
rate is described by an empirical formula [3]. Although some
parameters in the formula, such as the self-diffusion coefﬁcient,
can be evaluated by molecular simulations, the prefactor is hardly
determined theoretically, causing the formula can not be applied to
examine creep-strengthening effects of various alloying elements.
Chen and Peng et al. introduced the bond overlap populations
(BOP) and Grifﬁth work to investigate the strengthening effects
of alloying elements on the Ni/Ni
3Al interface in SC superalloys,
respectively [4–13], considering the improvement of creep perfor-
mance is mainly due to the strengthening effects of alloying ele-
ments on c/c0 interface [14,15]. Chen et al. calculated the
electronic structures and bonding characteristics of the Ni/Ni 3Al
interface using ﬁrst-principles Dmol3 package [5–8]. They sug-
gested that the strong bonding between ﬁrst-nearest neighbors
(FNN) alloying metal and Ni atoms across and within the interface
forms and is responsible for the interface cohesion and shear
strengthening measured by the sum of the vertical components
(across the
c/c0 interface) and that of the horizontal components
(within the c/c0 interface), respectively, of BOP. The strong bonding
is beneﬁcial to the creep rupture strength from an electronic bond-
ing point of view. In their opinion, the best strengthening effect on
the interface was achieved by Mo substitution. This seems incon-
sistent with the actual behavior of Ni-based SC superalloys since
it is Re that the best strengthener is in experiments [16]. The con-
troversial conclusion might be caused by the speciﬁc way of substi-
tution for alloying elements in the calculations by Chen et al., in
which only one substitution site was considered for each alloying
atom. In addition, the BOP represents only a relative measure of
covalent bonding between FNN atoms, and it is not even the real
cohesive bonding strength.
In terms of Grifﬁth work, Peng et al. investigated the inﬂuence
of the presence and location of Re and Ru on the properties of
Ni/Ni
3Al interface using the ﬁrst-principles plane-wave pseudopo-
tential method [11–13]. They concluded that the substitution of Re

While for other alloying elements, like Mo, W, Ta, etc., the relevant

fact motivate us predicting the creep-strengthening effects of more
elements in 3d, 4d and 5d transition metals.
2. Method and computational model
A supercell model of the Ni/Ni
3Al interface consisting of 64
atoms is devised for the present study. The atomic arrangements
in the c/c0 interface are shown in Fig. 1 a. The lattice parameters
of the supercell are taken to be equal for c and c0 blocks due to
the assumption of complete coherence [17]. The (0 0 2) atomic
layer is regarded as the coherent interface of the c-Ni and c0-Ni3Al
blocks based on the experimental result reported by Harada et al.
[17]. Here, we deﬁne this transition layer as the surface of the
c-Ni block and a schematic illustration of the deﬁnition is given
in Fig. 1 b. In order to investigate the effect of alloying element
on the strength and toughness of Ni/Ni 3Al interface, seven interfa-
cial models with alloying elements, i.e., Ni(1), Ni(2), Al(3), Ni(4),
Ni(5), Al(6) and Ni(7), are constructed.
We calculate the Grifﬁth work to qualitatively evaluate the
strengthening effects of alloying elements on the Ni/Ni 3Al inter-
face. The Grifﬁth work is deﬁned as the work needed to cleave a
crystal along the interface [18], and can be calculated from the fol-
lowing equation:

Since the effects introduced by each alloying element depend
upon the details of their bonding with the host atoms, the ﬁrst-
principles method are used to quantify the Grifﬁth work. The
ﬁrst-principles calculations presented in this paper are performed
using the ab initio program VASP [19]. The exchange-correlation
potential adopts the generalized gradient approximation by Per-
dew and Wang (PW91) [20,21]. Sampling of the irreducible wedge
of the Brillouin zone is done by adopting 4 /C2 4 /C2 2 with a regular
Monkhorst–Pack grid of special k-points [22].
For the sake of the symmetry in our calculations, the supercell is
doped with two alloying atom, i.e., one alloying atom for each
interface. All atomic positions in the supercell with or without
alloying elements have been relaxed to their equilibrium positions
according to the quantum-mechanical Hellmann–Feynman forces
using conjugate-gradient algorithm. After relaxation, the forces
acting on the atoms are <0.01 eV/Å.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Site preference of alloying elements and Grifﬁth work
In order to study the site preference of alloying elements at the
Ni/Ni3Al interface, the total energies of different interfacial models
corresponding to Fig. 1a are calculated and are shown in Fig. 2. The
No-add model in Fig. 2 represents Ni/Ni3Al interfaces free of alloy-
ing atoms. It is interesting to ﬁnd that with the Re, Ru, Cr, Co, Mo,
W, and Ta substitutions for all sites, the energies of the systems de-
crease, which indicates the promotion of microstructure stability
from an energetics point of view. W, Ta, and Re appear to give bet-
ter alloying stability effect than other alloying elements, and Co
presents the worst alloying stability effect, which gives a hint that
W, Ta, and Re might yield strong strengthening effects on the Ni/
Ni
3Al interface. The site preference can be predicted by the heat
of formation of the supercell. The heat of formation of a Ni nAlmXl

3.1. Electronic structures of Ni/Ni 3Al interface
To further examine the effect of alloying metal substitutions on
the interfacial bonding properties, we investigated the electronic
structure of Ni/Ni 3Al interface. As an example, Fig. 5 presents the
total valence charge density contour plots of c/c0 interfaces on
the (0 1 0) plane with or without Re and Co atoms. For the
No-add Ni/Ni 3Al interface, the contour plots show the electronic
interaction between the FNN Ni–Al atoms in the c0 block is stronger
than that between the FNN Ni–Ni atoms in the c block. The aniso-
tropic electron charge distribution at Ni(2) site is evident in Fig. 5a,
which is caused mainly by Ni- d/Al-p hybridizations. In the case of
replacing Al by Re at (0 0 1) c0 , Fig. 5b illustrates a strong electronic
interaction between Re and Ni atoms within the FNN separation,
which cause the (0 0 2)
c/c0 layer and c0-Ni3Al block to become
one whole block. Thus for cleavage along the (0 0 1) atomic layer
in the
c0-Ni3Al block of Al(3) models, a larger force is required to
be imposed to the Ni/Ni 3Al interface, which then leads to signiﬁ-
cant increase of the Grifﬁth work, as shown in Fig. 4c. Fig. 5c shows
the total valence charge density of the Ni/Ni 3Al interfacial region
with the substitution of Re for Al(6) atom. Similar to Fig. 5 b, the
anisotropic strong bonds appear between Re and Ni atoms. How-
ever, the strong enhancement of electron charge density between
FNN Re–Ni atoms in model Al(6) only increases the strength of
c0-Ni3Al block and has little inﬂuence on the Ni/Ni 3Al interface,
which is in agreement with Grifﬁth work for Al(6) sites in Fig. 4 f.
For the case where Al(3) atom is replaced by Co atom at (0 0 1) c0
layer, the contour plot of valence charge density is similar to that of
Re–Al(3) model. The decrease of Grifﬁth work in the Co–Al(3) mod-
el, as shown in Fig. 4 c, can be attributed to the less depletion of
covalent electron density between the FNN Co–Ni in the / c0-Ni3Al
block comparing with those of FNN Re–Ni.

4. Conclusions
The creep-strengthening effect of alloying elements can be well
described in terms of Grifﬁth work. The prediction of creep-
strengthening effects of alloying elements typically added in SC
superalloys based on the Grifﬁth work is in agreement with rele-
vant experimental observations and can be implemented easily
by ab initio calculations. It is interesting to note that elements near
the centre of the d block of the periodic table, for example Re and
W, are found to be more favorable for improving the rupture
strength of Ni-based SC superalloys than those from the far west
and far east of the transition metal block, for example, Zr and Au.
This strong correlation between the creep-strengthening effect of
alloying element and its position within the period table needs fur-
ther experimental examination.
