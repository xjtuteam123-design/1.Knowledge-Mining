Effects of the microstructural and compositional factors on creep properties of a+0e, titanium alloys wereexaminedusing ten Ti-Al-Sn-Zr al]oys designed by the thermodynamiccalculations based on the sublattice
model. Fromcreep tests for these alloys, it

creep properties were obtained by multiple regression analysis.
In the furnace cooled (FC) condition, a remarkable decrease in the elongation was observed at around
the calculated V*= of 0,1 (at 873 K) in room temperature tensile tests. Onthe other hand, the 0.2"/• proof

treated (ST) condition showing applicability of this type of alloys in the STcondition. Fromthese results,
the V., of about 0,1 (at 873K) was proposed as the best design condition for c(+ce, high temperature
titanium alloys.

Introduction
It is well knownl) that aluminium additions of larger
than 8wto/o cause precipitations of ordered oe2 phase
(Ti3Al) resulting in embrittlement of titanium alloys.
However, the extent of embrittlement depends on the
amount, size and shapeof cc2 phase. l)
Onthe other hand,
the high temperature strength of ce2 phase is much
stronger than that of c( phase. Then, it seemspossible to
use the ordered o(2 phase to strengthen the o( phase
titanium alloy in order to develop high temperature
titanium alloys. In order to design such alloys, it is
necessary to control microstructural and compositional
factors such as the volumefraction of cc2 phase(V..) and
the degree of solid solution strengthening of oe phase
(dDE"). For this purpose, the present authors have
evaluated thermodynamic parameters required for the
calculation of o(-o(2 phase equilibria in the Ti-Al-Sn-Zr
system in a previous report.2)
In the present study, effects of microstructural and
compositional parameters such as V*, and dDE"on high
temperature creep properties and room temperature
tensile properties were examinedusing ten Ti-Al-Sn-Zr
alloys designed by the above thermodynamic calcula-
tions.

3.4. RoomTemperature Tensile Properties
Results of room temperature tensile tests for re-
presentative alloys are correlated with calculated V*2 in
Fig. 7. In the FC condition, the tensile strength de-
creases with increasing V*2' A remarkable decrease in
the elongation is observed at around V*2 of O.1, and
premature fracture occurs in alloys GT-83 and 84
containing V.2 of larger than O.4. Onthe other hand,
solution treated (ST) materials does not show such
premature fracture. The0.2 o/o Proof stress and the tensile
strength in STmaterials increase with increasing V.2' The
elongation decreases gradually with increasing V*2' C. G.
Rhodeset al.13) reported that water quenching could

while large amount of o(2 phase were observed in the
solution treated and aged condition. Then, it seemsthat
the improvements of ductility in the STcondition seen
in Fig. 7are resulted from the suppression of c(2 formation
by quenching.
Fromthe results shownin Fig. 7, it can be concluded

results showthat the STcondition can give oe +0e2 type
