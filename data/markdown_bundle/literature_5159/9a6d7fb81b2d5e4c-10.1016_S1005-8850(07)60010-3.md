Effect of solid-solution temperature on the microstructure of Fe-Ni based 
high strength low thermal expansion (HSLTE) alloy 
Efan Jin Xu2), Jianfi Zhang2), Boping Zhang”, and Dongliang Zhao’)

Abstract: The influence of solid-solution temperature on the dissolution of carbide precipitates, the average grain size and the micro- 
hardness of the austenite matrix in an Fe-Ni based high strength low thermal expansion (HSLTE) alloy was investigated to obtain the 
proper temperature range of the solid-solution process. The XRD analysis, microstructure observations, and the theoretical calculations 
showed that the Mo-rich M,C-type precipitates in the Fe-Ni based HSLTE alloy dissolve completely at about llOO°C. The average 
grain size of the studied alloys increases from 14 to 46 pm in the temperature range of 1050 to 12OOOC. The microhardness of the matrix 
decreases first for the sake of solid-solution treatment, but then increases later with increasing solution temperature because of the so- 
lution strengthening effect. 
Key words: Fe-Ni based alloy; solid-solution temperature; microstructure; carbide dissolution

1. Introduction 
The Fe-Ni based Invar alloys have been used exten- 
sively in industrial applications, such as precision 
measurement instruments, shadow masks of cathode 
ray tubes for color televisions, core wires of long- 
distance power cables, and so on [l-51. But the Invar 
alloys based on the Fe-Ni system usually have rela- 
tively low strength (ultimate tensile strength R,S500 
MPa) and hardness (Hv,,1150) [6-71. To improve the- 
se properties, some elements (Ti, Al, Be, etc.) [8-101, 
which can form intermetallic phases to produce age 
hardening, are added to the Fe-Ni Invar alloys to form a 
new kind of alloy named the Fe-Ni based high strength 
low thermal expansion (HSLTE) alloy. For example, 
the addition of as little as 0.5wt% Be has been found to 
increase the ultimate tensile strength (R,) of Fe-36%Ni 
invar alloys up to 1 100 MPa in the peak-aged condition. 
However, the precipitation of the intermetallic phase 
NiBe has changed the composition of the matrix, which 
was responsible for the invar effect. The linear coeffi- 
cient of thermal expansion (LCTE), which is a primary 
parameter of these alloys, has increased along with the 
strength, when the concentration of addition is in- 
creased [lo]. Thus, it is desirable to have a precipitate 
strengthening the matrix even as it remains a low 
LCTE. In this investigation, small amounts of refracto- 
ry elements Mo and C are added into the Fe-Ni based 
alloy to form carbides, which do not affect the stability 
of the matrix. 
The size, volume fraction, and distribution of the 
carbide precipitates are closely related to the heat 
treatment of precipitation and further to the strength 
of 
Fe-Ni based HSLTE alloys. The Fe-Ni based HSLTE 
alloys generally undergo a two-stage heat treatment: 
solid-solution and aging treatment [ 1 1 - 121. The pur- 
pose of solid-solution treatment is to dissolve the pri- 
mary carbides and homogenize the microstructures that 
are helpful for the improvement of ductility. The pur- 
pose of the aging treatment is to precipitate these car- 
bides and obtain a high strength [13]. Unfortunately, 
published literatures rarely have dealt with the solution 
treatment temperature of the Mo-added Fe-Ni based 
HSLTE alloys. This article focused on the effect of 
solid-solution temperature on the microstructure of 
Fe-Ni based HSLTE alloys, as well as the proper tem- 
perature range of the solid-solution process.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Effect of solid-solution temperature on the mi- 
crostructure of the Fe-Ni based HSLTE alloy 
Fig. l(a) shows the metallograph of the Fe-Ni based 
HSLTE alloy without solid-solution treatment. It can 
be seen that some continuous bands consisting of black 
spots distribute along the hot-forged direction within 
the inhomogeneous matrix. As will be seen later, X-ray 
diffraction analysis (Fig. 2(a)) and energy dispersive 
X-ray analysis (EDAX) (Fig. 3) indicate that the alloy 
contains an M,C-type of primary carbide, which corre- 
sponds to these black spots within the Fe-Ni austenite 
(y) matrix. The typical composition of the M2C is rich 
in Mo, as shown by EDAX analysis in Table 2. The 
scanning electron image (Fig. 4(a)) shows much more 
clearly the morphology and distribution of the carbide 
phase. In Fig. 4(a), the Mo-rich M,C carbides appear 
light and have a spherical shape. Most of the carbides 
are located near the grain boundaries presenting as a 
discrete, blocky dispersion. 
Figs. l(b)-(e) compare the microstructures of the 
solid-solution treated alloys at different solution tem- 
peratures. The banded structure of the M,C carbides are 
still preserved when the alloy is treated at 1050°C (Fig. 
l(b)), but the breadth of this structure diminishes ap- 
parently. The alloys treated at the temperature over 
1 l00OC lose this banded structure and become a sin- 
gle-phase supersaturated solid solution. The amounts 
of M2C phase in different samples can be compared 
directly from the XRD results shown in Fig. 2(b), 
where the intensity of the diffraction peaks is normal- 
ized. Before solid solution, four peaks from M,C can be 
observed in the diffraction patterns, whereas only two 
peaks of M,C can be obviously seen when the alloy is 
treated at 1050°C. When the solution temperature in- 
creases to 11OO"C, the M,C phase cannot be detected 
anymore. 
SEM images demonstrate more obviously the ef- 
fects of the solid solution temperature on the dissolving 
of the M2C phase, as shown in Fig. 4. In the specimens 
without solution-treatment (Fig. 4(a)), some spherical 
precipitates can be observed on grain boundaries or 
within grains. When the specimen is solution-treated at 
1050°C (Fig. 4(b)), both the radius and amount of the 
carbides particles decrease. When the solution tem- 
perature reaches 1100°C (Fig. 4(c)), the precipitates 
can hardly be detected on the grain boundary in the 
specimen. 
Furthermore, the curve of the carbide amount vs so- 
lution temperature (Fig. (5)) is theoretically calculated 
by the Thermal-calc Software. As shown in Fig. 5, the 
precipitation of carbides decreases rapidly with an in- 
crease in solution temperature. The carbide amount is 
close to zero at about 1130°C. This result confirms that 
the solution temperature of the M2C carbide in the alloy 
is slightly above 1100°C. 
The results of microstructure observation, XRD 
analysis and theoretical calculations indicate that the 
solid-solution treatment at El 100°C can dissolve the 
primary carbides essentially. The dissolution of the 
carbides is of prime importance in the solution process 
of the alloy. Two different mechanisms have been dis- 
cussed in Refs. [15-171. One mechanism suggests that 
the carbide dissolution is controlled by local- 
equilibrium diffusion and the other mechanism states it 
is by an interface reaction. In the present study, the 
former model is applied to explain the dissolution of 
M,C carbides in austenite in this alloy. Assuming that a 
local-equilibrium is achieved at the interface between 
the austenite and the carbide, the dissolution of M,C

4. Conclusions 
(1) The solution treatment of the Fe-Ni based high 
strength low thermal expansion alloy leads to the dis- 
solution of Mo-rich M,C carbides. The higher the solu- 
tion temperature, the less the carbides are found. 
(2) The average grain size of the investigated alloys 
increases from 14 to 28 pm in the temperature range 
from 1050 to 1150°C. When the solution temperature 
reaches 1200"C, the grains grow up abnormally to 46 
Ym- 
(3) The microhardness of the alloy matrix decreases 
after the solution treatment. The hardness increases 
with an increase in treat temperature, which is largely 
owing to the increase in solute concentration associated 
with the dissolution of M2C carbides. The hardness of 
the matrix decreases at 1200°C on account of abnormal 
grain growth. 
(4) This study indicates that the optimal solid- 
solution temperature should be 1100°C for the Fe-Ni 
based HSLTE alloy.
