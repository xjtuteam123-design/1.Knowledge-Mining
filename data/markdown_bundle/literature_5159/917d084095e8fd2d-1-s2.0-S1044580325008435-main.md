as metal matrices. Adding 2D materials like MXene as reinforcements can further improve their properties. In this 
study, a specific HEA (AlCoCrFeNi

corrosion resistance are important.
1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEA) represent a new group of multicomponent 
alloys that possess five or more matrix elements [ 1 ]. As developing of 
strength-ductility trade-off HEAs [ 2 – 4 ], limits of quantities and con -
centrations for the definition of HEAs is fewer, which now become 
alloying combination of five or more, 5 – 35 at.% contents of metal or 
nonmetal elements [ 1 , 5 , 6 ]. AlCoCrFeNi HEAs are one kind of body- 
centered cubic (BCC) diphase (disordered BCC + ordered BCC) high- 
temperature high entropy alloys with excellent strength and ductility 
whenever at room or high temperature [ 7 , 8 ]. Ones found that increase 
of Ni and Al concentration in AlCoCrFeNi HEA is contributed to for -
mation of face-centered cubic (FCC) matrix that is benefit for the 
improvement of strength and ductility [ 9 , 10 ]. Thus, the AlCoCrFeNi
2 
HEA consist of disordered FCC matrix and ordered BCC (B
2
) interme -
tallic nano precipitates, possessing 1380 MPa ultimate tensile strength 
(UTS) and 40 %- elongation [ 9 , 11 ].
Nevertheless, at elevated temperatures, HEAs ’ high ductility may 
lead to premature softening and failure due to creeping. Consequently, 
the excessive elongation inherent in HEAs, a result of their strength- 
ductility balance, becomes a disadvantage in high-temperature appli -
cations. To address this, HEA composites are being developed by 
incorporating ceramic reinforcements [ 12 – 14 ]. These composites 
enhance high-temperature strength by sacrificing some of the unnec -
essary ductility [ 12 , 15 ]. Researchs [ 16 , 17 ] highlight that nanomaterials 
such as nanoparticles, nanofibers, and nanosheets exhibit strong 
strengthening and toughening effects when integrated with metals. 
These nanomaterials are particularly effective in improving high- 
temperature strength with minimal reduction in elongation, rendering 
them suited for HEAs that necessitate a harmonious equilibrium be -
tween strength and ductility.

3.4. Formation mechanism of superlattice particles from Cr2C MXene
As observed with MAX phases ’ transformation to stable carbides or 
nitrides at high temperatures, similar changes are initiated in MXenes. 
However, the unique feature lies in few-layered MXene or MAX phases 
(thickness < 10 nm). Our study [ 36 ] insights that the few-layers MAX 
phase (Cr
2
AlC) transforms into a layered twining nanoparticle due to 
matrix metal atoms substituting for A-site atoms of the MAX phase, a 
demarcation line for the twining structure. The layered twining nano -
particle maintains a coherent interface with the matrix and contains C 
atoms. For few-layers MXene (Cr
2
C MXene), the primary change is the 
formation of non-twining nanoparticles. This disparity is attributed to 
the relative displacement between the MXene ’ s layers, a challenge in the 
case of few-layers Cr
2
