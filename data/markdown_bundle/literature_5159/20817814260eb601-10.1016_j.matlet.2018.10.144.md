Abstract The mechanical behavior of (Fe 40Mn40Co10Cr10)100-xCx (x = 0, 2.2, 3.3 at.%) high entropy 
alloys (HEA) at various temperatures (298K, 159K and 77K) was investigated. For Fe 40Mn40Co10Cr10 
HEA, the jointly activated twinning and martensitic phase transformation concurrently increase the 
strength and ductility at 77K. In contrast, twinning and phase transformation gradually vanish as 
temperature decreases for carbon alloyed HEAs, resulting in ductile-brittle transition. Our findings 
indicate that the additional interstitial carbon atoms increase the f.c.c. phase stability, and inhibit the 
deformability of Fe40Mn40Co10Cr10 HEA at low temperature.
Keywords: Metals and alloys; Deformation and fracture; High entropy alloys; Phase transformation; 
Microstructure; Ductile-brittle transition.
1. Introduction
Design of high entropy alloys (HEAs) using boundary and/or metastability engineering to tune 
the strain accommodation via facilitating twinning and/or phase transformation during deformation, 
provides a new avenue to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off [1-3]. The twinning and phase 
transformation behaviors in HEAs are dependent on the specific chemical composition and temperature 
[4-5]. For example, transformation induced hardening simultaneously increases strength and ductility 
of Fe 50Mn30Co10Cr10 HEA [1]. The high strength and work hardening ability of Co 20Cr20Fe34Mn20Ni6

## Page 3

HEA are also due to transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) effect [6]. Moon et al. [7] discovered that 
phase transformation occurred in face-centered cubic (f.c.c.) Co 20Cr26Fe20Mn20Ni14 HEA under 
cryogenic high-pressure torsion, and proved that f.c.c. phase was unstable at lower temperature. Huang 
et al. [8] found that low temperature could promote twinning or phase transformation in all metallic 
elements constituted HEAs by reducing stacking fault (SF) energy. Accordingly, better mechanical 
properties are expected in all metallic elements constituted HEAs at low temperature by introducing 
twinning and/or phase transformation. Our previous study [9] revealed that carbon alloying greatly 
enhanced the strength and ductility of Fe40Mn40Cr10Co10 HEA by promoting twinning in f.c.c. matrix at 
room temperature. The phenomenon that carbon alloying both increases strength and ductility was also 
observed in other HEAs at room temperature [10-13]. However, the mechanical behaviors for the 
carbon alloyed interstitial HEAs at low temperature are still unclear.
Here, the mechanical properties and deformation mechanism of (Fe40Mn40Co10Cr10)100−xCx (x = 0, 
2.2, 3.3 at.%) HEAs at various temperatures (i.e. 298K, 159K and 77K) were systematically 
investigated. Our results show that twinning and phase transformation are both active in 
Fe40Mn40Co10Cr10 at 77K, and thus result in concurrent increase of strength and ductility. In contrast, 
the twinning vanishes and the ductile-brittle transition (DBT) appears in carbon alloyed HEAs at lower 
temperature.
2. Experimental procedure
(Fe40Mn40Co10Cr10)100-xCx (x = 0, 2.2, 3.3 at.%) alloy ingots, labeled as C0, C2.2, C3.3 
respectively, were prepared by arc melting constituent elements ( ＞99.8% purity ) in a water-cooled 
copper hearth under argon atmosphere. To ensure chemical homogeneity, each ingot was flipped and 
re-melted 5 times with electromagnetic stirring and then drop casted into a plat with dimension of 60

## Page 4

mm×40 mm×10 mm. Flat dog-bone shape specimens with gauge dimensions of 10 mm×2 mm×2 mm 
were cut from these plates. The uniaxial tensile tests were conducted using MTS810 testing machine 
with a nominal strain rate of 1×10−3 s−1 at 298K, 159K and 77K, respectively. The testing apparatus and 
temperature adjustment were detailed in ref. [14]. The engineering strains were determined following 
the methods described elsewhere [15]. Three specimens for each alloy were tested, and the average 
value was adopted. The crystal structure of the specimens was examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD) 
using Cu- Kα radiation. The microstructures as well as compositional homogeneity of the specimens 
were studied by back-scattered electron imaging (BSEI), secondary electron (SE) imaging, energy-
disperse X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) and electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD). In particular, all the 
EBSD examinations for fractured specimens were located in the sites 800μm offset from fracture 
surfaces.
3. Results and discussion
All the as-cast HEAs (i.e. C0, C2.2, C3.3) obviously reveal a single f.c.c. phase microstructure, in 
which all the elements homogeneously distributed (Fig. s1 and ref. 9). Fig.1a shows the tensile 
engineering stress-strain curves of the as-cast C0, C2.2 and C3.3 HEAs at 298K, 159K and 77K 
respectively. The yield strength (YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and fracture elongation (EG) are 
measured and listed in Table s1. In all HEAs, the stress–strain curves systematically shift up, and thus 
both YS and UTS increase with decreasing temperature. For C0 HEA, elongation increases with 
decreasing temperature, with maximum values of 69.5% attained at 77 K. That is to say, no DBT 
occurs till 77K. The elongation of C2.2 HEA increases with decreasing temperature from 298K to 
159K, and then decreases after temperature lower than 159K, i.e. maximum elongation (69.1%) is 
obtained at 159K. It can be inferred that the DBT temperature of C2.2 HEA is around 159K. For C3.3

HEA, elongation directly decreases with reducing temperature from 298K to 77 K. Although the C3.3 
HEA has the maximum elongation value (77.8%) among the HEAs at 298K, it shows quite low 
elongation value of 10.2% at 77 K. It is obviously that carbon content enhances the DBT temperature 
of the present HEAs. The corresponding strain hardening rate (SHR) versus true strain curves of C0, 
C2.2 and C3.3 at 298K and 77K are given in Fig. 1b. At 298K, the SHR apparently increases with 
increasing carbon content. However, when temperature reduces to 77K, the SHR reduces with 
increasing carbon content. It is implied that the strain hardening ability of carbon alloyed HEAs is 
reduced at low temperature. In order to reveal the deformation mechanism, special focuses are put on 
C0 and C3.3 HEAs afterwards.

Fig. 2 reveals the fracture feature images of C0 and C3.3 HEAs at low and high magnification, 
where the values of reduction in area (RA) and EG are listed. It is obvious that necking occurs before 
final fracture in C0 HEA at 298 K (Fig.2a). When temperature decreases to 77 K, the RA reduces but 
EG increases in C0 HEA, indicating improved uniform deformation ability (Fig.2b). At both 298 K and 
77K, the fracture surface of C0 HEA presents a lot of uniform dimples (Fig.2e, f), implying the 
excellent ductility. In addition, two kinds of dimples, i.e. deep dimple and shallow dimple, are observed 
on fracture surface of C0 HEA at 77 K (Fig.2f). The reason may be that C0 HEA sample contains two

In order to further understand the underlying mechanism of DBT in the carbon alloyed HEAs, 
EBSD observation was conducted to detect the microstructure of C0 and C3.3 in the undeformed and 
fractured states, as shown in Fig.3. It can be seen that these two HEAs have similar microstructures 
both in underformed and fractured states at 298K (Fig.3a 1, b1, and a2, b2). In particular, high angle grain 
boundaries (HAGBs) and many twins can be observed, yet no transformation induced plasticity (TRIP) 
occurs when they fracture at 298K (Fig.3a 2, b2). That is to say, the good elongations in these alloys are 
mainly attributed to twinning induced plasticity (TWIP) [9]. However, these two HEAs present 
completely different microstructures after fractured at 77K, as shown in Fig.3a 3, b 3. Besides a large 
number of HAGBs and twins, deformation induced h.c.p. phase also emerges in C0 HEA (Fig.3a 3). 
Thus, the better elongation in C0 HEA at 77K arises from both TWIP and TRIP effect. While for C3.3 
HEA (Fig.3b 3), neither twin, nor h.c.p. phase can be observed, which explains well the reduced

In this work, the martensitic phase transformation in C0 at 77K further confirms the h.c.p. phase 
is thermodynamically stable at lower temperature [7, 16]. While the addition of carbon into 
Fe40Mn40Co10Cr10 HEA increases f.c.c phase stability, especially at low temperature [17]. Similar 
phenomenon was also found in carbon alloyed Fe 50Mn30Co10Cr10 HEA, where the h.c.p. phase area 
fraction decreased from 32% to 0.7 % after addition of 0.5 at% carbon [17, 18]. The fundamental 
principle for carbon atoms promoting the stability of f.c.c phase needs further investigation.
4. Conclusions
The deformation behaviors of (Fe 40Mn40Co10Cr10)100−xCx (x = 0, 2.2, 3.3 at.%) HEAs at low 
temperature were systematically investigated. For the Fe 40Mn40Co10Cr10 HEAs, twinning and 
deformation induced phase transformation result in concurrent increment of strength and ductility, as 
well as improved strain hardening ability. The addition of interstitial carbon increases the stability of 
f.c.c phase and the DBT phenomenon among the carbon alloyed HEAs are attributed to the vanishing 
of twinning at low temperature.
