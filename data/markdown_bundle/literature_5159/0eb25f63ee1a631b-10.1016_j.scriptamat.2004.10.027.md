Strength of MoSi 2-based crystals at ultra-high temperature
Adel A. Sharif a,b,*, Amit Misra a, Terence E. Mitchell a
a Materials Science and Technology Division, Los Alamos National Laboratory, MS G755, Los Alamos, NM 87545, USA

Abstract
The ultra-high temperature compressive strengths of [0 0 1] oriented MoSi 2 single crystals are signiﬁcantly higher than other ori-
entations even at homologous temperatures approaching 0.95. A factor of two increase in the yield strength for [0 0 1] orientation
was observed with only 1 at.% of substitutional alloying with Nb.

1. Introduction
The mechanical properties of molybdenum disilicide
(MoSi2)-based alloys, with the body-centered tetragonal
C11b structure, have been studied extensively in the last
decade due to their potential for high-temperature struc-
tural applications [1–5]. Single crystals of MoSi
2 exhibit
strong plastic anisotropy, with [0 0 1] direction being the
hard orientation and all other directions away from
[0 0 1] being plastically much softer. Below the polycrys-
talline brittle-to-ductile transition temperature (BDTT)
of about 900 /C176 C, single crystals of MoSi
2 may be de-
formed plastically along any crystallographic orienta-
tion except for [0 0 1] [5]. Furthermore, the critical
resolved shear stress (CRSS) for slip at temperatures
above the BDTT is much greater for [0 0 1] oriented
stress axis than for [1 0 0] orientation [6]. For example,
at 1200 /C176 C, compression along the [0 0 1] direction results
in {0 1 3} h331 i slip with a CRSS of about 600 MPa,
which is an order of magnitude greater than the
{0 k l}h110 i slip that occurs when the stress direction
is diﬀerent than [0 0 1] [7]. However, the yield strength
of MoSi
2 has not been determined along any orientation
above 1600 /C176 C previously. Therefore, it is not known
whether or not such anisotropic eﬀect persists to ultra-
high temperatures.
Signiﬁcant improvements in low-temperature ductil-
ity (down to the ambient temperature) as well as high-
temperature (up to 1600 /C176 C) strength of polycrystalline
MoSi
2 have been observed by alloying with 1 at.% Nb
[8]. However, the eﬀects of Nb on the yield strength of
monocrystalline MoSi 2 above 1600 /C176 C are not known.
In this investigation, we report the deformation behav-
ior of pure and Nb-doped MoSi
2 monocrystals along
the hard orientation in the 1600–1900 /C176 C temperature
range.

3. Results and discussion
Due to the loss of Si from MoSi 2 during feed rod
preparation and crystal growth, obtaining single phase
MoSi
2 crystals with low ﬂaw density required careful
selection of the best crystals from many that were pre-
pared. Excess and deﬁcient Si compositions result in sec-
ondary phases of pure Si and Mo
5Si3 respectively that
aﬀect the mechanical properties of the crystal. Since
growth rate, power input, lamp quality, and several
other factors inﬂuence the rate of Si loss, prior experi-
ments in estimation of the premelt composition were
not enough to ensure stoichiometry [10]. Optical and
SEM examination of the samples along with Laue back
reﬂection X-ray diﬀraction patterns were utilized to en-
sure the integrity of the samples selected for the experi-
ments. Fig. 1 shows a crystal of MoSi
2 grown by the
optical ﬂoating zone melting technique. The Laue pat-
tern of a cross-section of a crystal shown in Fig. 2 indi-
cates growth direction within two degrees of [0 0 1].
Often double patterns were observed in Laue back
reﬂection X-ray diﬀraction indicating either the presence
of secondary phases or low angle boundaries. Only sam-
ples with clean Laue patterns similar to the one shown in
Fig. 2 were used. Only about 20% of the crystals pre-
pared, similar to those shown in Fig. 1 , met all the
experimental requirements.

First, the strong plastic anisotropy in unalloyed
MoSi

melting point of 2020 /C176 C, test temperature of 1900 /C176 C
corresponds to a homologous temperature of 0.95).

The yield strengths for the /C212hard/C213[0 0 1] orientation is an
order of magnitude higher than for /C212soft/C213orientations
such as h100 i. For soft orientations, strengths were ex-
tremely low (<10 MPa) at temperatures P1600 /C176 C mak-
ing precise measurements diﬃcult for samples with small
cross-sections (2 · 2 mm) in our test frame with a 90 kN
load cell. Thus, no measurements are reported for the
h100 i orientations in the 1600–1900 /C176 C range. However,
as is evident from Fig. 5 , the yield strength for the soft
orientation is already quite low at 1500 /C176 C and if the
strength–temperature curve for the soft orientation is
extrapolated to higher temperatures, we note that the
trend of approximately an order of magnitude higher
strength for the [0 0 1] orientation as compared to
[1 0 0] is maintained in the 1600–1900 /C176 C temperature
range. The Schmid factors for slip systems for deforma-
tion with stress axis along [0 0 1] are zero for all systems
with h001 i or h110 i Burgers vectors or {0 0 1} or {1 1 0}
planes, the latter containing the 1/2 h111 i Burgers vec-
tor, because the shear component of the normal stress
on those directions or planes are zero. Therefore, for
deformation along [0 0 1] all the so-called easy slip sys-
tems are eliminated. The Schmid factor for the remain-
ing three possible slip systems are 0.42 for {1 2 3}1/
2h111 i, 0.39 for {0 1 3}1/2 h331 i, and 0.33 for {0 1 1}1/
2h111 i for compression along [0 0 1]. Maloy et al. [3]
concluded that 1/2 h331 i are eﬀectively immobile at tem-
peratures >1200 /C176 C since they decompose into 1/2 h111 i
and h110 i dislocations resulting in zero Schmid factor.
At 1400 /C176 C, in samples tested along [0 0 1] they observed
h100 i dislocations on the (0 0 1) plane and 1/2 h111 i on
the {1 1 0} plane. This would indicate the activation of
climb since these systems have near-zero resolved shear
stress. Glide of 1/2 h111 i dislocations on non-close-
packed {1 2 3} or {0 1 1} planes was also suggested.
The high strength of [0 0 1] crystals as compared to
[1 0 0] orientations at 1200–1600 /C176 C range, thus, results
from the need to activate slip on non-close-packed
planes that have a high Peierls stress (and activation of
climb that would require high stresses at imposed strain
rates of 10
/C0 4 s/C0 1). The fact that our measurements of the
temperature dependence of strength for [0 0 1] crystals in
the 1600–1900 /C176 C temperature range appear to extrapo-
late the previous data on this orientation in the 1200–
1600 /C176 C temperature range seem to suggest that these
/C212hard/C213deformation modes are also operative at temper-
atures >1600 /C176 C. In other words, even at 1900 /C176 C, addi-
tional deformation modes that are /C212easy/C213to operate and
would have lowered the yield strength of [0 0 1] crystals
down to the level of [1 0 0] orientation do not get acti-
vated in the [0 0 1] orientation. Transmission electron
microscopy investigations of [0 0 1] crystals compressed
at temperatures >1600 /C176 C will be reported in a future
article [11].
Second, signiﬁcant solid solution hardening is noted
even at 1900 /C176 C. Addition of only 1 at.% Nb increases
the yield strength of [0 0 1] MoSi
2 by a factor of two.
Thus, the high rate of solution hardening reported by
Nb alloying for the [0 0 1] orientation in the 1300–
1500 /C176 C temperature range [12], for soft orientations at
temperatures <1500 /C176 C [12,13], and for polycrystals in
the 1200–1600 /C176 C range [8] is retained even at 1900 /C176 C.
Any increase in melting point or modulus by Nb alloy-
ing is expected to be minimal since only 1 at.% Nb is
added. Normally, substitutional solutes produce gradual
hardening since the strain ﬁelds are spherically symmet-
rical (i.e. pure dilatation) leading to interaction only
with edge dislocations and not with screw dislocations.
Nb can be an eﬀective solid solution hardening agent
even as a substitutional atom because it can produce
local tetragonal distortions in the lattice thereby result-
ing in obstacles for both edge and screw dislocations.
Alternatively, or in addition, Nb may also lower the dif-
fusion coeﬃcients in MoSi
2 resulting in higher stresses
for dislocation climb at the same imposed strain rate.

Third, comparison with other metals and ceramics
(Fig. 5 ) seems to indicate that the strength of [0 0 1]
MoSi2 and the signiﬁcant solid solution strengthening
by Nb at homologous temperature of 0.95 are not typi-
cal materials behavior. As shown in Fig. 5, the [0 0 1] ori-
ented Nb-doped MoSi
2 alloy improves on the
deformation temperature of polycrystalline Inconel at
any given yield strength by about 360 /C176 C. Similar analo-
gies may be made by comparing the yield strength vs.
temperature data of Nb-doped alloy to other high-tem-
perature structural materials shown in Fig. 5 such as
cubic yttria stabilized zirconia. At the range of available
data plotted in Fig. 5 , Nb-alloyed MoSi
2 has the same
strength along [0 0 1] direction as that of ZrO 2–
10%Y2O3 along the [1 0 0], the hard direction, at
/C24 400 /C176 C higher temperatures [14]. The diﬀerence is even
greater if the data are compared to those of ZrO 2–
10%Y2O3 along the [1 1 2] direction. Finally, the yield
strength data for [0 0 1] MoSi 2 and (Mo,Nb)Si2 are com-
pared with W and W–Re alloys in Fig. 5 . Although the
yield strengths of [0 0 1] MoSi 2 and W are comparable at
1900 /C176 C, W has a much higher melting point of 3422 /C176 C.
Also the modest solid solution hardening by Re in W at
/C24 1400–1500 /C176 C disappears at temperatures of /C24 1900 /C176 C
(homologous temperature of /C25 0.6) presumably due to
thermal activation. However, in [0 0 1] MoSi 2 that shows
Peierls stress controlled behavior even at ultra-high tem-
peratures, the potency of solution hardening is retained.
TEM observations of Nb-doped materials will be com-
pared with unalloyed [0 0 1] MoSi
2 in a future article
[11].
4. Summary
The yield strengths, in compression, of single crystal-
line MoSi 2 have been measured up to temperatures as
high as 1900 /C176 C (95% of the melting point in degrees
Kelvin). Even at these ultra-high temperatures, the plas-
tic anisotropy of MoSi 2 is maintained with the yield
strength for the [0 0 1] /C212hard/C213orientation signiﬁcantly
higher than for the /C212soft/C213orientations such as [1 0 0].
Addition of only 1 at.% Nb results in a factor of two in-
crease in the yield strength of [0 0 1] MoSi 2 even at
1900 /C176 C. Such large solid solution hardening at homolo-
gous temperatures of /C24 0.95 are typically not observed in
other materials and a detailed mechanistic study will be
presented in a future article. The retention of high
strengths in Nb-doped [0 0 1] MoSi
2 up to 1900 /C176 C oﬀers
the promise of designing composites for ultra-high tem-
perature use incorporating crystals with this orientation
as matrix or reinforcements.
