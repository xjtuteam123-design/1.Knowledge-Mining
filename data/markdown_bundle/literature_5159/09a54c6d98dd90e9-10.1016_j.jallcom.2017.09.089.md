Abstract 
The dual-phase AlCoCrFeNi alloy exhibits excellent specific strength in the 773–873 K 
temperature range. However, the phase transformation taking place above 773 K hinders its 
structural use at higher temperatures. In the prese nt study, the temperature dependence of 
the specific strength of the AlCoCrFeNi alloy has b een examined from a microstructural 
point of view. One of the key findings of this work is that the high-temperature phase 
stability of the dual-phase alloy is strongly affec ted by its microstructural morphology; this 
finding provides new insights into the application of high-entropy alloys as high-
temperature structural materials, for instance in aerospace engine components. 
 
 
Keywords : casting; microscopy and microanalysis techniques; high-entropy alloys; 
mechanical properties; phase transformations

1. Introduction 
 The untapped potential of high-entropy alloys (HE As) has attracted considerable interest 
in recent years. HEAs offer a new route to form hig hly stable and simpler solid solution 
structures (as compared to intermetallic compounds) in multicomponent alloys composed 
of more than five main elements, each ranging from 5 to 35 at.% [1-3]. These systems 
exhibit several remarkable features, such as high e ntropy, severe lattice distortion, sluggish 
diffusion, and cocktail effects [1,2]. Some previou s studies [4-8] have highlighted the 
considerable potential of high-entropy alloys in ap plications involving extreme conditions. 
Gludovatz et al. [7] reported that the CrMnFeCoNi a lloy exhibits excellent mechanical 
properties at cryogenic temperatures. In contrast, Senkov et al. [8] showed that refractory 
HEAs such as NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW exhibit good sing le-phase stability and 
consistent mechanical properties over the temperatu re range of 873–1273 K. In both cases, 
the unique properties of the alloys have been attri buted to the formation of a single-phase 
solid solution with high configurational entropy ( ≥1.6 R, where R is the gas constant). 
However, when more than five elements are present, the number of possible combinations 
capable of forming a single-phase solid solution is very limited [9,10] and needs to be 
expanded. Dual-phase HEAs (DP-HEAs) are a new categ ory of HEAs that represent good 
candidates in this context. 
Dual-phase structures commonly offer superior stren gth at high temperatures, 
particularly when the two phases have a high degree of coherence [11-14]. In the case of 
Ni-based superalloys, composed mainly of γ (disordered fcc A1 structure) and γʹ (ordered 
fcc L1
2 structure), the γʹ phase plays a key role in increasing the high-temp erature strength,

DP-HEA superalloy. However, the high-temperature pr operties of this alloy have not yet 
been investigated in detail. Therefore, in the pres ent study, we assess the high-temperature 
behavior of the AlCoCrFeNi alloy, focusing in particular on its specific yield strength. 
 
2. Experimental 
Master alloy buttons (10 g) with a nominal composit ion of Al 20 Co 20 Cr 20 Fe 20 Ni 20 (at.%) 
were prepared by arc-melting the high-purity (>99.9 at.%) constituent metals under an 
argon atmosphere. Each alloy button was remelted mo re than five times to obtain a 
homogeneous composition. Cylindrical samples with a diameter of 5 mm and height of 50 
mm were fabricated by suction casting. Phase transf ormations were examined using X-ray 
diffraction (XRD, Rigaku D/Max 2500). A series of u niaxial compression tests were 
performed at target temperatures ranging from 300 t o 1173 K with a strain rate of 1 × 10 -3 
s-1, using an electromechanical universal testing mach ine equipped with a halogen heater

confirmed by the selected area diffraction pattern shown in Fig. 1d. As shown in Figs. 1b 
and c, dendritic and interdendritic regions have a different microstructural morphology, as a 
result of solute segregation during dendritic solid ification. Previous studies [20,22] have 
reported that Cr and Fe segregate into the liquid p hase during dendritic solidification in the 
AlCoCrFeNi alloy. However, despite the solute segre gation, the constituent phases (Al-Ni-
rich B2 and Cr-Fe-rich A2) of the dendritic and int erdendritic regions are the same. 
Interestingly, the results of the 3D-APT analysis o f these regions indicate that each phase 
maintains essentially the same chemical composition , independent of its location, which 
reflects the different volume fraction of the B2 ph ase ( VB2 ) in the dendritic and 
interdendritic regions (see Figs. 1b and c). In par ticular, the chemical compositions of the 
B2 and A2 phases are Al 30.3 Co 20.2 Cr 5.2 Fe 13.9 Ni 30.4 and Al 1.4 Co 21.5 Cr 42.0 Fe 30.5 Ni 4.6 (at.%), 
respectively. These compositions are assumed to ref lect the tendency to minimize the 
lattice mismatch between the two phases. The select ed area electron diffraction patterns in 
Fig. 1d give a lattice constant of 0.292 nm for both phases. In particular, VB2 is higher in the 
dendrite than in the interdendrite fraction, as the former has higher contents of Al and Ni 
and lower contents of Cr and Fe than the latter.

VNbMoTaW, a well-known refractory single-phase bcc HEA, shows higher specific yield 
strength than superalloys such as Inconel 718 and H aynes 230 in the 873–1473 K

the specific strength of the AlCoCrFeNi alloy was t hus investigated in more detail, by 
comparing it with that of the high-temperature titanium alloy, IMI 834 [25]. 
Figure 3a shows that the specific yield strength of IMI 834 decreases almost linearly with 
increasing temperature. However, while at room temp erature the specific yield strength of 
AlCoCrFeNi is similar to that of IMI 834, it does n ot show a similar decrease between 
room temperature and 773 K. Interestingly, this eff ect is similar to the so-called yield 
strength anomaly, which is often observed in precipitation-hardened superalloys [14,26-28]. 
For these alloys, the yield strength exhibits littl e variation between room temperature and 
temperatures up to several hundred degrees Celsius. This is because the formation of 
antiphase boundaries within ordered intermetallics such as L12 leads to significant 
strengthening of the alloys [26,27]. Feuerbacher [2 9] suggested that the high strength of 
AlCoCrFeNi is because of its high antiphase boundar y energy, which suppresses the

temperature strength of AlCoCrFeNi can be ascribed to the formation of antiphase 
boundaries within the B2 matrix. However, before an alyzing in detail the high-temperature 
deformation mechanisms, the phase stability of the AlCoCrFeNi alloy at high temperatures 
should be discussed. The specific yield strength of the AlCoCrFeNi alloy exhibits a sharp 
drop above 773 K (see Fig. 3a). Therefore, we inves tigated the phase stability above 773 K 
to verify whether the sharp change in the specific yield strength can be attributed to a phase 
transformation. Figure 3b shows the XRD patterns of the as-cast AlCoCrFeNi and of 
samples heat-treated at 773, 873, and 973 K for 6 h. A phase transformation from bcc to fcc 
and sigma ( σ) phases can be observed between 773 and 973 K. Thi s result thus confirms 
that the sharp drop in the specific yield strength above 773 K is related to the solid-solid 
phase transformation, which may limit the maximum o perating temperature of the 
AlCoCrFeNi alloy. Figures 4a and b show the EBSD im age quality and phase maps of the 
AlCoCrFeNi sample heat-treated at 973 K for 6 h, re spectively. Despite the prolonged heat 
treatment at 973 K, dendrites and interdendrites ex hibit considerably different 
microstructural features: the interdendritic region s consist of bcc (green), fcc (red), and σ 
(yellow) phases, while the dendritic ones are still mainly composed of the bcc phase (see 
Fig. 4b). The magnified SEM image (Fig. 4c) corresp onding to the region marked by the 
dotted line in Fig. 4b clearly highlights the diffe rent microstructural morphology of 
dendritic and interdendritic regions. The dendritic region seems to have coarsened A2 + B2 
dual-phase structure, implying that isolated struct ures (see Fig. 1b, where cubic A2 
particles are embedded in the B2 matrix) are more e ffective at slowing down the phase

4. Conclusions 
In conclusion, the dual-phase AlCoCrFeNi alloy show ed considerable potential for high-
temperature structural applications such as aerospa ce engine components, owing to its 
lightweight characteristics and superior specific s trength at intermediate/high temperatures 
compared to commercial alloys. However, the solid-solid phase transformation taking place 
above 773 K limits the long-term use of the alloy a t high temperatures. The difference in 
microstructural evolution between dendritic and int erdendritic regions reveals that the 
initial microstructural morphology is one of the ke y factors determining the high-
temperature phase stability of the alloy. The prese nt study indicates that a fully isolated 
dual-phase structure can increase the maximum opera ting temperature of the DP-HEAs by 
enhancing the high-temperature phase stability of t he alloys without sacrificing their 
specific yield strength. Based on these results, we propose that tailoring the microstructural 
morphology through alloy design and heat treatment can represent an effective way to 
exploit the potential of the dual-phase high-entrop y alloys as high-temperature structural 
materials.
