ABSTRACT 
Re is an important alloying element to improve the high temperature performance of Ni-based single crystal 
superalloys, and has a segregation tendency to the γ / γ’ phase interface. Hydrogen embrittlement introduced by 
hydrogenated vacancies (H-Vacs) severely reduces the service life of alloys. However, the formation mechanism 
of hydrogenated vacancy (H-Vac) and its effects on the Re-segregated γ / γ’ phase interface are still unclear. In this 
work, the first-principles simulations are applied to clarify the formation of H-Vac and its effects on the ther -
modynamic stability and mechanical properties of Re-containing γ / γ’ phase interface. We find that at the Re- 
containing γ / γ’ phase interfaces, the hydrogen atom is more likely to dissolve in the first-nearest-neighbor 
(1
st
-NN) octahedral interstices of vacancy than in the 2
nd
-NN octahedral interstices. The Vac-containing γ / γ’ 
phase interface can be stabilized when hydrogen atom dissolves in 1
st
-NN octahedral interstices to form H-Vac 
complex. For the Re-containing γ / γ’ phase interface, the formation of the H-Vac complex does not change its 
preferential cleavage plane. Moreover, the fracture strength of the Re-containing γ / γ’ phase interface is always 
higher than that of the Re-free γ / γ’ phase interface. Thus, Re can enhance the interfacial fracture strength, which 
is also verified by the first-principles computational tensile tests. 
1. Introduction 
Due to the excellent high-temperature service performance of Ni- 
based single crystal superalloys, they are widely used in the turbine 
blades of aero engines and gas turbines [1,2] . A large amount of γ’ -Ni
3
Al 
phases with L1
2 
structure and a small amount of γ -Ni phases with face- 
centered cubic structure are present in the superalloys. Lattice mismatch 
between the γ and γ’ phases could hinder the movement of dislocations 
and thus improve the high-temperature properties of the superalloys. In 
order to obtain enhanced service temperature, composition optimiza -
tion of the superalloys is needed [3 – 8] . The addition of Re greatly in -
creases the service temperature of the superalloys, but the excessive 
amount of Re causes the formation of topological close packed (TCP) 
phase, which seriously degrades service performance [9 – 11] . In the later 
generations of Ni-based single crystal superalloys, the composition of 
alloying elements is optimized to obtain better high-temperature prop -
erties. For example, in the 4
th 
generation of the superalloys, about 5 % of 
Re is added. At the same time, Ru is also added to suppress the gener -
ation of TCP phases [12,13] . In the 5
th 
generation of the superalloys, the 
elemental ratios are further optimized. The use of alloying elements 
greatly improves the high-temperature properties such as, thermal sta -
bility, creep resistance, and endurance temperature of the superalloys. 
However, the greater the variety of alloying elements, the greater the 
challenge for subsequent recovery and reuse. Thus, a more precise 
exploration on the alloying mechanism of Ni-based single crystal su -
peralloys is required. 
The strengthening effects of Re on Ni-based single crystal superalloys 
are very significant, and the mechanism of Re segregation in the su -
peralloys was well studied [9,14 – 16] . For example, the segregation 
behavior of Re atoms at the γ / γ’ phase interface of Ni-based superalloys 
was investigated by 3D atom probe technology. It was found that Re is 
more likely to segregate on the γ -Ni phase side of the interface, while Re 
is rarely observed on the γ’ -Ni
3
Al phase side [15,17] . In addition, the 
research on the strengthening mechanism of Re in Ni-based superalloys 
was also in-depth. Blavette et al. found that the element Re segregates at 
the γ / γ’ phase interface to form clusters, and the existence of clusters can 
hinder the movement of dislocations, thereby improving the high- 
temperature properties of the superalloys [18] . Compared with

traditional experimental methods, computational simulations are often

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations showed that Re is strongly bound 
to the surrounding host atoms, Re in γ phase would significantly reduce 
stacking fault energy and thus increase the number of stacking faults in γ 
phase [19,20] . First-principles simulations were used to study the Re 
occupancy at the γ / γ’ phase interface. It was found that Re is easier to 
occupy on the γ phase side and can make the phase interface more stable 
energetically [21 – 24] . 
As a common failure form in metals, hydrogen embrittlement (HE) 
severely reduces the service life of metals. However, there are many 
conjectures on the failure mechanism of HE. For example, some theories 
found that VB group metal elements can combine with hydrogen atoms 
to form compounds, while some other theories believe that the presence 
of hydrogen atoms reduces the binding energy between atoms [25,26] . 
Different from the above theories, Sun et al. proposed the concept of 
hydrogenated vacancy (H-Vac), where hydrogen atoms enter the atomic 
interstices surrounding vacancies and combine with the vacancies to 
form hydrogenated vacancies (H-Vacs). Using MD simulations, they 
found that H-Vac is the smallest unit of HE. H-Vacs aggregate under the 
action of dislocations to generate nanovoids, which eventually lead to 
material failure. This conclusion is not only applicable to α -Fe, but also 
to Al and Ni [27] . Furthermore, the formation mechanism of H-Vac at 
the γ -Ni/ γ’ -Ni
3
Al phase interface and its effects on the thermodynamic 
stability and mechanical behavior of the interface were investigated by 
first-principles simulations in our previous work. It was found that H- 
Vac can stabilize the vacancy-containing (Vac-containing) γ / γ’ phase 
interface, but weaken the interfacial cohesion [28] . However, the study 
on H-Vac did not take into account the Re segregation at the γ / γ’ phase 
interface, further research is much needed. Therefore, in this study, the 
first-principles calculation is used to investigate the formation mecha -
nism of H-Vac at the Re-containing γ / γ’ phase interface and the changes 
in interfacial properties caused by H-Vac formation. 
2. Computational details 
2.1. Calculation method 
We carried out the study by Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package 
(VASP) based on density functional theory (DFT). All calculations 
involved used the Perdew- Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional based on 
the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) to describe the exchan -
ge – correlation energy; the pseudopotential was described by the method 
of projector augmented wave (PAW); the cutoff energy was set at 350 
eV. The convergence criterion for the electron self-consistent cycle was 
10
-5 
eV, and the force for each ion of less than 0.01 eV/Å. In the cal -
culations, VASPKIT was used to generate k -points. The k -mesh of the 
γ / γ’ phase interface was set to 6 × 6 × 3; the Ni, Ni
3
Al and Al unit cells 
were set to 6 × 6 × 6 [29] . Spin polarization was considered in all 
calculations due to the ferromagnetism of γ -Ni at 0 K.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Preferred position of vacancy at the γ / γ ’ phase interface 
Fig. 3 shows the formation energy of vacancy at 15 possible sites 
displayed in Fig. 1 (b). It can be seen that all the formation energies are 
positive, indicating that the formation process of vacancy in the γ / γ’ 
phase interface is endothermic. The lowest formation energy of vacancy 
is 1.24 eV and located at the site 2 of Layer 3 (abbreviated as L3-S2), 
where vacancy is the easiest to form among the 15 sites. A higher for -
mation energy means that the vacancy is harder to generate, due to a 
relatively higher energy consumption. The highest formation energy of 
vacancy in the γ / γ’ phase interface is 1.65 eV and located at L5-S4, 
which is an Al site of (001) γ’ layer as shown Fig. 1 (b). From Fig. 1 
(b), one can also find that the Layer 3 consists of Ni and Re atoms and the 
L3-S2 site is the second-nearest-neighbor (2
nd
-NN) of Re atom. As a 
result, the 2
nd
-NN Ni site of Re atom in the Re-containing atomic layer is 
most likely to form vacancy among 15 possible sites, and thus the Ni 
vacancy at the L3-S2 site was employed for further studying the H-Vac in 
this work. 
3.2. Site preference of hydrogen atom 
As found in our previous work [28] , hydrogen atom cannot directly 
occupy the Ni vacancy, but preferably occupies the octahedral in -
terstices in the vicinity of vacancy to form stable defect complex (i.e., 
hydrogenated vacancy). Thus, four first-nearest-neighbor (1
st
-NN) and 
five 2
nd
-NN octahedral interstices adjacent to the L3-S2 vacancy were 
considered as the possible occupation sites of hydrogen atom at the Re- 
containing γ / γ’ phase interface, as shown in Fig. 4 . In order to clearly 
illustrate the specific position, configuration and chemical composition 
of these octahedral interstices, the four 1
st
-NN octahedral interstices 
named as O1 ~ O4 are displayed in Fig. 4 (a) and (c), and the five 2

octahedral interstices named as O5 ~ O9 are displayed in Fig. 4 (b) and 
(c). 
As shown in Fig. 5 , the solution energy of hydrogen atom occupying 
the different octahedral interstices (O1 ~ O9) of Re-containing and Re- 
free γ / γ’ phase interfaces was calculated. It can be seen that the solution 
energy of hydrogen atom occupying the 1
st
-NN octahedral interstices is 
negative and generally lower than the 2

-NN interstices for both in -
terfaces. This indicates that the solution of hydrogen atom into the 1
st
- 
NN octahedral interstices is a spontaneous exothermic process, which is 
conducive to form stable H-Vac complexes. Moreover, the hydrogen 
atom prefers occupying the O1 ~ O4 interstices to occupying the O5 ~ 
O9 interstices. As the site preference of hydrogen atom at the Re- 
containing γ / γ’ phase interface is unchanged from the Re-free inter -
face as studied in Ref. [28] , so the site preference of hydrogen atom at 
the Vac-containing γ / γ’ phase interface is independent of the Re pres -
ence. Furthermore, compared with the Re-free γ / γ’ phase interface, the 
solution energy of hydrogen atom at the most of 1
st
-NN octahedral in -
terstices (O2, O3 and O4) of Re-containing interface becomes lower. 
Hence, the solubility of interstitial hydrogen atom at the Re-containing 
interface is likely to be enhanced, due to the presence of Re atom. In 
addition, the solution energy of hydrogen atom at the O2 interstice is 
 0.23 eV, which is the lowest among the four 1
st
-NN octahedral in -
terstices. Thus, the O2 interstice should be the most preferential target 
site for the occupation of hydrogen atom. 
3.3. Effect of hydrogenated vacancy on the thermodynamic stability of 
Re-containing γ / γ ’ phase interface 
As shown in Fig. 6 , the existence of L3-S2 Ni vacancy increases the 
formation energy of the Re-containing γ / γ’ phase interface from 0.094 
eV/atom (Perfect) to 0.079 eV/atom (Vac-containing), thus the 
interfacial stability becomes lower due to the presence of Ni vacancy. 
With the solution of hydrogen atom into the 1
st
-NN octahedral in -
terstices of Ni vacancy and the formation of H-Vac complexes, the 
interfacial formation energy becomes lower in comparison with the Vac- 
and Re-containing interface, while it is still higher than the perfect Re- 
containing γ / γ’ phase interface. Therefore, the formation of H-Vac can 
enhance the stability of the Vac- and Re-containing γ / γ’ phase interfaces 
to some extent, but the stability of Re-containing γ / γ’ phase interface 
with a H-Vac remains lower than the perfect Re-containing interface. In 
contrast, the solution of hydrogen atom into the 2
nd
-NN octahedral in -
terstices of Ni vacancy almost increases the interfacial formation energy 
to some degree and thus is harmful for the interfacial stability. The effect 
of H-Vac on the stability of Re-containing γ / γ’ phase interface is quite 
similar with the results on the Re-free interface as found in our previous 
work [28] . In addition, one can also find that the formation energy of the 
Re-containing γ / γ’ phase interface with a H-Vac is generally lower than 
that of the Re-free interface with a H-Vac. Hence, the presence of Re 
atom may stabilize the H-Vac containing γ / γ’ phase interface.

3.5. Electronic structure of Re-containing γ / γ ’ phase interfaces 
As shown in Fig. 8 , the electron localization function (ELF) of Re- 
containing and Re-free γ / γ’ phase interfaces in the (010) plane was 
calculated. The red area between atoms represents a relatively higher 
degree of electron localization and thus implies the covalent-like bonds 
between atoms. The green area between atoms represents a weak elec -
tron localization, and thus metallic bonds are formed [34] . From Fig. 8 
(a) and (d), it can be found that the bonding of Re atom with its sur -
rounding Ni atoms is covalent-like in the perfect Re-containing γ / γ’ 
phase interface, while metallic bonds forms between Ni atom and its 
surrounding Ni atoms at the perfect Re-free interface. Generally, the 
bonding strength of covalent-like bond is higher than that of metallic 
bond. Hence, the higher Griffith fracture work of perfect Re-containing 
γ / γ’ phase interface with the (002) γ’ || (001) γ cleavage plane than the 
Re-free interface should be attributed to the formation of covalent-like 
bonds. Moreover, there is also a covalent-like bonding between the Re 
atom and the Al atom at the 2
nd
-NN site of Re atom, while no similar 
covalent-like bonding between Ni-Ni atoms is observed at the Re-free 
γ / γ’ phase interface. Therefore, the presence of Re atom also slightly 
elevates the Griffith fracture work of γ / γ’ phase interface with the 
(001) γ’ || (002) γ cleavage plane. 
From Fig. 8 (a), (b) and (c), it can be found that the formation of Ni 
vacancy leads to a lower localization degree between the vacancy and its 
surrounding Ni atoms than the corresponding localization degree be -
tween Ni and its surrounding Ni atoms. As a result, the bonding strength 
of the vacancy to its surrounding Ni atoms is weaker than that of Ni-Ni 
atoms. This should be responsible for the reduced Griffith fracture work 
of Re-containing γ / γ’ phase interface with the (002) γ’ || (001) γ cleavage 
plane caused by the Ni vacancy. With the solution of hydrogen atom into 
the O2 interstice and the formation of H-Vac, a similar change in the ELF 
occurs at the Re-free γ / γ’ phase interface and could be applied to 
illustrate the change in Griffith fracture work of (002) γ’ || (001) γ 
cleavage plane.
