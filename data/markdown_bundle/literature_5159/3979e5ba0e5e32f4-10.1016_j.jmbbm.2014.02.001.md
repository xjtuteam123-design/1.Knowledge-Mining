abstract
Porous Ti –Nb–Zr alloys with different porosities from 6.06 to 62.8% are prepared by a two-
step foaming powder metallurgy method using TiH 2, Nb, and Zr powders together with 0 to
50 wt% of NH 4HCO3. The effects of the amounts of Nb and Zr as well as the sintering
temperature (1473 to 1673 K) on their phase composition, porosity, morphology, and
mechanical characteristics are investigated. By controlling the porosity, Nb and Zr
concentrations as well as the sintering temperature, porous Ti –Nb–Zr alloys with different
mechanical properties can be obtained, for example, the hardness between 290 and 63 HV,
the compressive strength between 1530.5 and 73.4 MPa, and the elastic modulus between
10.8 and 1.2 GPa. The mechanical properties of the sintered porous Ti –Nb–Zr alloys can be
tailored to match different requirements for the human bones and are thus potentially
useful in the hard tissue implants.

1. Introduction
Titanium alloys are widely used in bone tissue replacements
and ﬁxtures due to their unique combination of mechanical
properties and biocompatibility. However, stress shielding
occurs as a direct result of the stiffness mismatch between
the arti ﬁcial implant materials and surrounding natural
bones which have an elastic modulus of 2.3 –20 GPa, pore size
of 20–1000 μm, and porosity of 30 –95% (Gibson, 1985). This can
lead to problems such as bone resorption and implant
loosening. It is more dif ﬁcult if various mechanical require-
ments for implant are considered, i.e. elastic modulus of
subehondral bone is much lower than that of cortical bone. In
order to fabricate more compatible titanium implants for
different situation, scholars indicate two concepts as follow-
ing: on one hand, adding alloying elements such as Ta, Nb,
and Mo which have been reported to induce low elastic near β
type titanium alloys ( Nouri et al., 2010 ). On the other hand,
manufacture a bone-like porous structure to achieve a smal-
ler elastic modulus due to its unique geometric architecture.
Moreover, different pore sizes can affect the implant biologi-
cal performance: the micro pore improves cell adhesion and
bone tissue ingrowth; the macro connected pore allows body
ﬂuid transport ( Bansiddhi et al., 2008 ; Gu et al., 2009 ).

Titanium hydride (TiH 2) has been widely used as a con-
ventional pore forming active agent in the synthesis of
porous alloys ( Goi et al., 2008 ). However, TiH
2 is also a
potential raw material for Ti alloy fabrication. Concerning
the dehydrogenation reaction of TiH 2 (TiH2-TiHx-α-Ti)
(Bhosle et al., 2003 ), it is easily predicted that Ti alloy with
pore structure would be fabricated without any additional
pore forming agent. It is also noticed that hydrogen emitted
from the TiH
2 powders might become a protective atmo-
sphere during powder sintering ( Bertheville et al., 2004 ).
Although TiH 2 has so many advantages if it is used as raw
material, there have only been scattered reports in the
literature ( Yook et al., 2008 ; Li et al., 2009 ).
In this work, TiH
2 is used as the raw powders and also the
pore former in a metallurgical process together with
NH4HCO3. During the whole process, the decomposed
NH4HCO3 would pre-form the macro-pores at the ﬁrst step
and then TiH 2 is dehydrogenated to produce micro-pores at
the second step. Niobium is added because it is the strongest
beta phase stabilizer that ef ﬁciently transform the phase
from α to β during sintering. Zirconium is also selected for
alloy fabrication, although it is de ﬁned as a neutral or weak
beta stabilization element if taken individually in Ti alloy;
however this element exhibits positive beta transformation
ability when it is combined with Nb ( Geetha et al., 2004 ). This
effect is con ﬁrmed by Abdel-Hady et al. (2007) , the higher
cohesive energy presenting in Ti –Nb–Zr indicates co-addition
of Zr and Nb provides stronger average chemical bond
between atoms in the beta phase compared with that in the
Ti, Ti –Nb or the Ti –Zr binary alloy. Moreover, the addition of
Zr also bene ﬁts in improving shape memory effect and
superelasticity, enhancing corrosion resistance, promoting
solid solution hardening, etc. Brailovski et al. (2013) , Zhang
et al. (2013) , thereby shows a promising potential for biome-
dical application. The objective of this work is to fabricate a
novel low elastic modulus Ti –Nb–Zr alloy with macro/micro
porous architecture and then investigate its phase composi-
tion, microstructure and mechanical characteristics, and the
inter-relationships.
2. Experimental details
Commercial TiH 2 and Nb and Zr powders were mixed and
blended for 4 h at 400 rpm according to the nominal compo-
sition of Ti –20Nb–15Zr, Ti –35Nb–15Zr (wt%). NH
4HCO3 pow-
ders were blended with the metal powders (with contents of
0, 20, 35, and 50 wt%, respectively) to regulate and control the
pore and mechanical characteristics of the porous Ti –Nb–Zr
alloys. Compaction was achieved using die with dimensions
of 55 mm /C2 10 mm under a hydraulic press set at 200 MPa for
2 min. The green samples were placed into a furnace, evac-
uated, and heated to 393 K for 2 h to remove the NH
4HCO3
space holder. The foaming temperature was then increased
to 673 K for 2 h to decompose the TiH
2 thoroughly and
sintering was conducted at 1473 to 1673 K for 4 h.
The porous samples were characterized by scanning
electron microscopy (FE-SEM, JSM-6330F, JEOL Techniques,
Japan) in conjunction with energy dispersive X-ray spectro-
scopy (EDS). The structure was determined by X-ray
diffraction (TF-XRD, RAD IIA, Rigaku, Japan) and the metallo-
graphic constituents were studied by optical microscopy
(Olympus, Japan).

3.2. Morphology of porous surface and pore characteristics
The micro-morphology of the porous Ti –20Nb–15Zr alloys
fabricated with different NH 4HCO3 concentrations is exam-
ined using SEM as shown in Fig. 3 . Without NH 4HCO3, the
sample has micropores with a size of several micrometers
due to hydrogen decomposition from the TiH
2 powder, as
shown in Fig. 3 a. As shown in Fig. 3 b–d, the morphology and
size of the pores of the other samples are different. The pores
are strongly affected by the original space holder, implying
that the effect of the powder morphology on the pore
formation is negligible. The pores are interconnected and
this bodes well for nutrition delivery and body ﬂuid transport
in vivo ( Li et al., 2009 ).
Fig. 4a and b show the characteristics of the porous Ti –Nb–
Zr samples prepared with NH
4HCO3. As shown in Fig. 4 a,
there are two types of pores in the sintered part. The porous
structure is characterized by small and irregular pores located
on the walls of the interconnected open pores. The micro-
pores are present between the particles in the Ti –Nb–Zr as
discussed previously. In addition, these micropores in the cell
walls and edges of the foams are connected to macropores.
While interconnection in the low porosity samples is
achieved only by micropores, the macropores are connected
directly as well as via micropores in the high porosity
samples.
During sintering, the macropore framework remains
nearly unchanged and the pore morphology is similar to that
of the NH
4HCO3 powder particles. Regardless of the initial
NH4HCO3 content, all the pore walls and pore edges are
corrugated and rough, as shown in Fig. 4 b. Moreover, the
single powder particles are hardly observed, which means a
good sintering sample has been obtained in our study.
However, the cracks located near the pores are observed in
Fig. 4 c and d may decrease the compressive strength and
Young's modulus ( Dewidar and Lim, 2008 ). There are two
possibilities to explain the cracks formation: (1) the cracks are
formed during the compaction process before sintering; (2)
The cracks initially arise from gas release during the foaming
process and then are increased by the contraction of the as
foamed body on cooling ( Neikov et al., 2009 ). The comparison
of TiH
2 with and without foaming agent ( Fig. 3a and c) reveals
NH4HCO3 is the main factor: a surface without obvious cracks
is obtained in Fig. 3 a while the surface with many cracks is
shown in Fig. 4 c. The crack formation process is described as
following: During the both foaming steps, NH 4HCO3 (or TiH 2)
particles initially decrease due to decomposition and subse-
quently lead to pore appearance as well as cracks by repla-
cing the vacancies. Meanwhile gas release may push apart
the originally closely compacted particles, resulting in a gap
in between them. These factors together form cracks around
the pores during sintering ( Chen et al., 2014 ). Afterwards, the
contraction occurs in the porous structure when temperature
decreases. On one hand the residual stresses produce more
new cracks, and on the other hand it bene ﬁts in growing as-
formed cracks during cooling process. The higher content of
NH
4HCO3 is used, the stronger contraction is obtained after
sintering. The calculation results indicate the volume loss is
/C24 53.6% after sintering using NH 4HCO3 content at /C24 50 wt%.

3.3. Mechanical properties of porous samples
The measured micro-Vickers hardness values are plotted in
Fig. 7. The data are obtained under three different conditions,
that is, NH
4HCO3 concentration, alloy constituents, and
sintering temperature. The hardness decreases with increas-

3.4. Fracture surface analysis
The fractured surface of the porous samples shows some
dimples indicative of ductile fracture ( Esen and Bor, 2011 ). As
shown in Fig. 13 a, failure takes place by tearing the necks
between the powder particles as well as the necks between
the powder particles in the pore wall and edges of the space

4. Conclusions
Porous Ti –Nb–Zr alloys with different elementary composi-
tions, pore structures and porosities, which are suitable for
biomedical applications, are prepared by a two-step foaming
powder metallurgy method. Their phase compositions and
microstructures are sensitive to the Nb and Zr concentration
as well as the sintering temperature. The pore features
depend on the characteristics of the space holder particles.
Their mechanical properties such as the hardness
(290–63 HV), the compressive strength (1530.5 –73.4 MPa) and
the elastic modulus (10.8 –1.2 GPa) decrease with increasing
the porosity, the Nb and Zr concentration as well as the
sintering temperature. Various mechanical properties of
different porous Ti –Nb–Zr alloys prepared in this work could
fulﬁll different requirements for the replaced human bones.
