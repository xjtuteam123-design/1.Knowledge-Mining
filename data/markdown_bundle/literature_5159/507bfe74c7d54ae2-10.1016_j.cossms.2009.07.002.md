NiTi shape memory alloy fabricated by SHS as implant material

abstract
Porous NiTi shape memory alloy (SMA) with ideal porosity and high compressive strength as an implant
material was fabricated by self-propagating high-temperature synthesis (SHS). In this study, a new igni-
tion technique ‘‘high voltage electric arc” was used to ignite the green specimens and control the orien-
tation of combustion channels which effect compressive strength. It was determined that the
compressive strength of specimens was increased when the combustion channels were parallel along
the specimen axis, and the compressive strength was decreased when the combustion channels were
perpendicular to specimen axis. The desired phases such as B2(NiTi) and B19
0 (NiTi) were dominant while
the second phases (Ni 4Ti3 and NiTi 2) in small amount. The undesired phases (such as pure Ni and Ni 3Ti)
for biocompatibility are not found in the structure. The transformation temperatures were higher for
medical applications by heat treatment and partly decreased at every next thermal cycle where the heat-
ing rate of the specimen was increased.

1. Introduction
NiTi shape memory alloys (SMAs) have been successfully used
in engineering, medical and orthopaedic applications due to their
excellent properties, such as shape memory effect (SME), super-
elasticity (SE), corrosion resistant and biocompatibility which
make these alloys suitable for surgery and brackets, hard tissue
replacement and implantation [1–3]. Recently, as a promising bio-
material, porous NiTi shape memory alloys (SMAs) have brought
new advantages to biomedical applications for hard tissue replace-
ment, in particular for hip implantation and femur repair. These
porous alloys have the adjustable mechanical properties, reduced
weight and increased biocompatibility due to their porous struc-
ture allowing in-growth of the human tissue, nutrition exchange
and medicament transportation within human bodies [4]. In addi-
tion, lightweight, superelasticity and adjustable mechanical prop-
erties of the porous NiTi SMAs can decrease the stiffness
mismatches between implant and human bones [5,6].
Superelastic behavior of NiTi can recover up to 8% strain in uni-
axial deformation by a reversible stress-induced transformation.
Human bone also recovers high strains (up to 2%) and NiTi can thus
match this mechanical property as well [7]. The ideal implant
material should have the porosity in the range of 30–90%, the
optimal pore size (100–500
lm) for bone tissue ingress, elastic
modulus should have close values that of bone (20 GPa) and higher
compressive strength that of bone. The compressive strength of a
compact human bone can reach 170–193 MPa when the test direc-
tion is parallel to bone axis and 133 MPa when the test direction is
normal to bone axis [8,9]. For this reason, a porous structure of
similar compression strength as the bone is desired for implant.
On the other hand, it requires controlling pore size, morphology
and orientation.
Currently, there are several powder metallurgy (PM) methods
for fabrication of porous NiTi SMA, such as element powder sinter-
ing [10], SHS [11–15], hot isostatic pressing (HIP), capsule-free hot
isostatic pressing (CF-HIP) [16], spark plasma sintering (SPS) [17],
metal injection moulding (MIM) [18], and mechanical alloying
(MA) [19]. Evidence in the literature [13–17,20–23] indicates that
the SHS method results in higher levels of porosity and a higher de-
gree of NiTi formation. In addition, SHS offers more advantages
compared to conventional powder metallurgy (PM). This process
is simple and avoids the need for expensive processing facilities
and equipment. Furthermore, the rapid exothermic reaction pro-
cess leads to time and cost savings. The SHS process is associated
with a combustion wave and has the capability to expel volatile
impurities as a wave propagates through the sample. As a result,
it leads to high purity of products [24]. The process practically con-
sists of the mixing powder pressing into pellets and then ignition
by an external source locally [13]. Ignition plays role in the reaction

considering this alloy as implant material.
2. Experimental procedures
The raw materials used were Ni (99.8 wt%) and Ti (99.5 wt%)
powders with an average size of /C0 44
lm (Alfa Aesar). The mixed
powders of Ni and Ti with 50.5 at.%Ni were blended in a rotating
container for 24 h for a homogenous mixture. Then the blended
powder was pressed into cylindrical compacts of 10 mm in diam-
eter using a hydraulic press at a cold compaction pressure of
100 MPa. The green porosity of specimens was 50.5 vol%.
The green specimens then were preheated up to 350 /C176C with
approximately 15 /C176C/min heating rate in a furnace under the pro-
tection of high purity argon gas, subjected to electrical discharge
pulse (14 kV and 30 mA) [25] and then synthesized by igniting at
different locations to obtain combustion channels differently ori-
ented. The temperature of the green specimens increased in a short
time at the beginning of current application and ignition was
started. Once ignited, combustion wave self-propagated along the
axis of the specimen to the other end in a very short time, thus por-
ous NiTi SMA was synthesized. The typical processing steps of SHS
are shown in Fig. 1 [26] .
The general porosity of the specimens produced was deter-
mined by the formulae f ¼ 1 /C0 m=ðdVÞ; where V and m is the vol-
ume and mass of the porous specimens, respectively and d is
theoretical density. The theoretical density of NiTi (Ni 50.5 at.%)
is 6.21 g/cm
3. The general porosity is consisted of open porosity
and closed porosity. Open porosity was determined by the liquid
weighting method and the open porosity ratio was deﬁned as the
ratio of the open porosity to the general porosity [21].
The surface of sample was etched by a mixture of 10% HF, 5%
HNO
3 in water [4]. The microstructure was examined with a scan-
ning electron microscope (SEM) (LEO Evo-40VP) equipped with en-
ergy dispersive X-ray spectrometer (EDS) (Röntec). The phase
constituents were characterized on an X-ray diffractometer XRD
(Rigako Rad-B D-Max 2000 XRD) using CuK
a radiation with
1.54046 Å.
The phase transformation temperatures were measured by dif-
ferential scanning calorimeter (DSC, Perkin Elmer Pyris 6) to inves-
tigate the suitableness of the specimens for medical applications
by heat treatment. For this aim, the specimen, typically 15 mg in
weight, was placed in aluminum pan. The measurement was car-
ried out under nitrogen gas ﬂow. In the DSC thermal analysis, spec-
imen was heated from /C0 50 /C176C up to 140/C176C and kept isothermally
for 2 min to establish thermal equilibrium, then cooled down to
/C0 50 /C176C, and also kept isothermally for 2 min. For the same speci-
men, this procedure was carried out for several heating and cooling
rates, namely, ±5 /C176C/min, ±10/C176C/min, ±15/C176C/min. Thus, the effect
of thermal cycle on the phase transformation temperatures was
investigated. To investigate the compressive strength of the speci-
mens, uniaxial compression test was carried out at a constant rate
of 0.1 mm/min in an Instron testing machine (Instron-8503 type).
3. Results and discussions
3.1. Combustion channels and pores
Fig. 2 (a–c) shows macrographs of porous specimens synthe-
sized. The orientation of the channels indicates that combustion
propagated perpendicular to the arc axis as expected [27]. In our
technique, an electric arc heats the specimen up to ignition tem-
perature and starts ignition thus the combustion reaction occurs.
The liquid phase occurs temporarily during the reaction, thus the
synthesized specimen maintains the green compacted shape. The
combustion wave propagates in a manner similar to expanding
waves in water and as a result combustion channels form as per-
pendicular circles to the electrode axis. Therefore, the orientation
of combustion channels could be changed by igniting at different
locations with ignition technique used in this study. The volatiliza-
tion of impurities of low melting point and the out-gassing of en-
trapped gas could generate ﬂow of gas phase, causing pores
during reaction as expected. During combustion, the following
reaction mainly takes place
Ni þ Ti ! NiTi þ 67 kJ

The combustion reaction temperature could reach the melting
temperature (>1310 /C176C), and the liquid phase occurs transiently
during combustion [27].
The positions of the channels with respect to the location of the
ignitor are given in Fig. 2 . It can be seen in Figs. 2 and 3 that the
combustion channels have a width of 0.5 mm approximately. In
addition, numerous pores in various sizes and shapes are seen from
Fig. 3 . The pores have fairly low size in lm. The pores distributed
uniformly and most of them are isolated and rarely interconnected.
The specimens have a general porosity of 54.2% and an open poros-
ity of 83.4%. The ignition location has no effect on the porosity. The
pore characteristics and porosity ratios in fabricated specimens are
suitable for hard tissue implants [8].

3.2. Microstructure and phase transformation
The XRD pattern of the porous NiTi SMA is shown in Fig. 4. The
desired products such as B2(NiTi) and B19 0 (NiTi) are the predomi-
nant phases. In addition, several second phases, such as Ti 2Ni and
Ti3Ni4 formed. Recently, we determined that these second phases
could be eliminated completely and single phase B2(NiTi) occurred
by solution treatment under loading [28]. Porous NiTi SMA was
fabricated by different researchers with SHS method [13–15] and
MIM [18]. They determined the same phases seen from XRD results
in our study. They also reported that the amount of the B2 phase
increased whereas the rate of the Ni
4Ti3 and the Ti 2Ni phases de-
creased with increasing preheating temperature [13–15]. Zhu
et al. [10] determined TiNi, intermetallics and some free Ni from
XRD results in porous NiTi produced for the short sintering times.
They also reported that a sintering time of 8 h was enough to re-
move all free Ni but not other intermetallics (Ni
3Ti and NiTi 2).
Although the phases such as Ni 4Ti3 and NiTi 2 generally occur
[20] and the phases such as Ni 3Ti, pure Ni and pure Ti rarely occur
in porous NiTi alloys fabricated by SHS [13],N i3Ti, pure Ni and pure
Ti were not observed in this study. While pure Ni is toxic in living
tissue, Ti 2Ni, Ni 3Ti and Ni 4Ti3 phases existing in SHS-synthesized
porous NiTi SMA may increase the brittleness of the products.
Moreover, they can lead to the cavitation corrosion and deteriorate
the biocompatibility of porous NiTi SMAs in the physiological envi-
ronments [15,29]. The presence of Ni
4Ti3 and Ni 3Ti2 in the porous
NiTi SMA synthesized by SHS is due to the overall stoichiometry
of the Ni-rich (50.5 at.%Ni) specimen. If the mixing is not homoge-
neous, the amount of undesired phases becomes larger [13,17,20].
Also, the presence of Ti
2Ni phase and more ﬂammable nature of Ti
than Ni during synthesis could make the parent NiTi phase richer
in Ni.

The result of DSC measurement for the specimen fabricated by
SHS and thermal cycles in different heating–cooling rates is shown
in Fig. 5 . The broad peaks in the heating curves correspond to the
martensite (monoclinic) to austenite (cubic) transformation, and
the broad peaks in the cooling curves correspond to the transfor-
mations from austenite to martensite. There are three peaks in ﬁrst
heating curve. These peaks could form due to incoherent and the
coherence stress ﬁeld around dislocations and Ni
4Ti3 precipitates
respectively [30]. A similar transformation, B19 0 ? R ? B2 trans-
formation due to formation of Ni 4Ti3 after aged at 400 and 450 /C176C
and B19 0 ? B2 transformation after aged at 475, 500 and 550 /C176C,
were determined by Chu et al. [31]. It is seen that ﬁrst and second
peaks are disappeared from second and third heating cycles
(Fig. 5 ). It is thought that the incoherent stress ﬁelds in the struc-
ture disappeared with thermal cycle and R phase transformation
did not occur since amount of Ni
4Ti3 was little ( Fig. 4 ), thus one-
step (B19 0 ? B2) transformation occurred. A one-step transforma-
tion from the high-temperature parent phase B2 to the low-tem-
perature monoclinic martensite phase B19 0 in the cooling curves
is seen in Fig. 5. For equiatomic dense NiTi alloys, the similar mar-
tensitic transformation usually is seen in the cooling curve. Two-
steps or multi-stage martensitic transformation (MST) can also oc-
cur in dense NiTi alloys after certain thermal or mechanical treat-
ments which can be described as B2 ? R, R ? B190 ,o rB 2 ? B190
[30]. In this study, the presence of a single peak upon cooling indi-
cates the absence of any intermediate phase between the high-
temperature austenitic phase and the low-temperature martensitic
phase [23].
The transformation temperatures are shown in Table 1 . Ms and
Mf are martensite start, and ﬁnish temperatures, As and Af are aus-
tenite start and ﬁnish temperatures, Mp and Ap are peak tempera-
tures in cooling curve and heating curve, respectively. The
transformation temperatures particularly decreased at the ﬁrst
thermal cycle. Jiang and Rong [11] determined that the transfor-
mation temperatures decreased very little with thermal cycling
up to 10 times. Therefore, we did not attempt more than three cy-
cles in this study. Liu et al. [32] determined for Ni-at.%50Ti speci-
men deformed by 6.2%, the transformation to austenite from
reoriented martensite occurred at higher temperature as 27 K than
the second transformation cycle. In other words, the transforma-
tion behavior after the ﬁrst martensite to austenite transformation
was similar to that of the undeformed specimen. Ni content in NiTi
matrix strongly affects phase transformation temperatures, even
0.1 at.% of Ni can lower the phase transformation temperatures
by about 20 /C176C. Chu et al.[31] determined that phase transforma-
tion temperatures decreased with increasing of Ni content of NiTi
matrix after aged above 475 /C176C. Because amount of Ni
4Ti3 de-
creases with aged at high temperatures, thus parent NiTi phase is
richer in Ni. In this study, thermal cycle is realized at lower tem-
perature. Therefore, it is thought that amount of Ni
4Ti3 did not
be affected with thermal cycle and phase transformation tempera-
tures partly decreased due to the incoherent stress ﬁelds in the
structure disappeared with thermal cycle. If thermal cycle or aged
were realized at lower temperatures (<450 /C176C), Ni content in the
structure decreases due to formation of Ni
4Ti3 and phase transfor-
mation temperatures increase.
3.3. Compression strength
The compressive stress–strain curves of the porous NiTi SMAs
fabricated by such SHS technique with different orientations of
combustion channels are shown in Fig. 6 (curve a–c). It is obvious
that the orientations of the combustion channels have a very im-
portant effect on the compression behavior of porous NiTi SMAs.
The compressive strength increased in case of longitudinal com-
bustion channels when compression loads are applied parallel to
axis of channels ( Fig. 6 curve b and c). Some ﬂuctuations are seen
in the stress–strain curve of the specimens in which combustion
channels are perpendicular to the longitudinal axis (Fig. 6 curve
a). Under loading combustion channels close and consequently
lower compression strengths is recorded. After the exact closure
of the channels higher compression strength is observed and re-
corded. This repetitive behavior causes ﬂuctuations in the curves.
The ultimate compressive strengths and Young’s modulus of the
synthesized specimens are given in Table 2. The ultimate compres-
sive strengths and Young’s modulus of the specimens are 317 MPa
and 3.05 GPa for the channels perpendicular to longitudinal or load
axis, 455 MPa and 3.75 GPa for the oblique channels and 462 MPa
and 4.6 GPa for the channels parallel to the longitudinal or load
axis, as can be seen from Fig. 6 a, b and c respectively. The yield de-
formations of the specimens are approximately 48, 75, 95 MPa, re-
spectively for a, b, and c in Fig. 6. All of the specimens fabricated by
such SHS technique have higher compressive strengths than that of
the compact human bone and similar Young’s modulus to that of
natural bones, which is anticipated for the ideal biomaterials used
as the heavy load-bearing hard tissue implants [20].

of 52.8%. Zhu et al. [34] produced porous NiTi samples with a poros-
ity of 45% by conventional powder sintering method and recorded an

4. Conclusions
1. Porus NiTi SMA free from undesired pure Ni, pure Ti and Ni 3Ti
phases were fabricated by SHS.
2. Combustion channels can be oriented by changing the ignition
location. That is, it is possible to orient the combustion channels
as desired.
3. The compressive strength depends on the orientation of the
combustion channels and changes with the angle between the
combustion channels and loading direction
4. In case of longitudinal combustion channels, a loading parallel
to the channels gives maximum compression strength.
5. The properties such as the porosity, the microstructure, the
compressive strength and the Young’s modulus of the porous
NiTi SMA fabricated such SHS technique are suitable to be used
as hard tissue implants.
6. The phase transformation temperature of synthesized specimen
is higher for implant material. The transformation temperatures
particularly decreased at the ﬁrst thermal cycle.
