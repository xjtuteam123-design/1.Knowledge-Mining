EFFECT OF LANTHANUM ON THE HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH
OF SINGLE CRYSTALS OF HIGHLY REFRACTORY ALLOY VZhM4-VI
CONTAINING RHENIUM AND RUTHENIUM
V . V . Sidorov,1 A. V . Goryunov,1 and N. A. Kolmykova 1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 3, pp. 23 – 27, March, 2012.
The effect of lanthanum on the high-temperature oxidation resistance of single crystals of alloy VZhM4-VI
used for casting single-crystal blades of gas turbine engines is studied. It is shown that lanthanum affects posi-
tively the resistance of single-crystal specimens of alloy VZhM4-VI to high-temperature oxidation and raises
its heat resistance by a factor of 1.5 – 2.0 at 1150°C. This is proved by the data of an x-ray diffraction phase
analysis of an oxide film and of a metallographic analysis of the structure of the near-surface layer of speci -
mens of the alloy after oxidation.
Key words: refractory alloy, lanthanum, high-temperature strength, scale, oxidation.
INTRODUCTION
Permanent growth in the gas temperature at the inlet of
the turbine of aircraft gas turbine engines (GTE) and in their
service life makes the refractoriness, i.e., the capacity of the
material to resist the action of an external oxidizing atmo-
sphere at high temperatures, one of the most important char-
acteristics of castable high-temperature alloys, from which
the gas turbine blades are produced, in addition to the long-
term strength and fatigue resistance.
The process of oxidation of refractory alloys affects the
service life of blades due to reduction of their load-carrying
cross section and appearance of sources of stress concentra -
tion. The oxidation process includes formation of a surface
oxide film and internal oxidation. As a result, thermodynam-
ically stable phases (oxides) form on the surface and inside
the metal. The oxide film can be detached later and the ox -
ides can evaporate, which enhances the rate of the oxidation.
Various coatings used to protect blades from oxidation
can serve a long-term and effective barrier in the way of the
oxidative medium. However, despite the high protective
properties of such coatings local regions of the surface of a
blade may be exposed to direct action of an oxidative me -
dium in the case of mechanical damage (chipping, scuffing,
cracking). Consequently, the alloying elements can be
“burnt” from the surface of the blade, which can finally
lower its reliability and shorten the life.
Therefore, the use of protective coatings should be com-
bined with provision of high refractoriness of the material of
the blade.
In accordance with the international classification five
generations of refractory nickel alloys (RNA) have been de-
veloped for casting single crystal GTE blades [1]. The RNA
of the first generation contain traditional alloying elements,
i.e., Al, Ti, Cr, Mo, W, Ta, and Nb. The compositions of the
RNA of the second and third generations include Re in an
amount of 2 – 4 and 5 – 6%, respectively. The development
of this direction at VIAM has resulted in creation of refrac -
tory alloys in which the concentration of rhenium was in -
creased to 9 – 12%. The fourth and fifth generations of RNA
are rhenium-bearing alloys with an additive of ruthenium. It
has been shown that rhenium and ruthenium are distributed
primarily in the /c103-phase. In high-rhenium RNA ruthenium
stabilizes the phase composition and lowers the probability
of segregation of topologically close-packed (tcp) phases [2].
The dominant distribution of Re and Ru in the solid solution
has two important consequences. Firstly, the alloying of the
solid solution with refractory Re and Ru lowers the rate of
the diffusion processes that control the softening of RNA un-
der the conditions of high-temperature creep. Secondly, since
the atomic radii of rhenium and ruthenium are greater than
that of nickel, their introduction into the alloy increases the
parameter of the crystal lattice of the solid solution.
It follows from analysis of chemical compositions of the
fourth and fifth generations of RNA that the refractory alloys
with rhenium and ruthenium contain a reduced content of
chromium (Table 1) [3]. It is known that refractory alloys

METHODS OF STUDY
We studied specimens of alloy VZhM4-VI of the Ni –
Cr – Mo – W – Re – Ru – Ta – Co – Al system containing
2.1 – 3.3% Cr. Two heats of the same chemical composition
with respect of the main alloying elements were melted in a
vacuum induction furnace; one heat contained an additive of
lanthanum and in the other it was absent. The ingots were
subjected to mechanical treatment and then remelted by the
method of directed crystallization in an UVNK-9 device to
obtain single crystal preforms with crystallographic orienta-
tion /c225001/c241. These preforms were used to fabricate cylindrical
specimens 10 mm in diameter and 20 mm long to be tested
for high-temperature strength. We studied specimens in cast
condition and after a standard heat treatment (heating to
1320°C, holding for 24 h with subsequent cooling to 850°C,
heating to 1130°C, holding for 4 h with subsequent cooling
to 800°C, heating to 870°C, holding for 48 h with subsequent
cooling in air).
Before testing, we measured the area of the surface of all
of the specimens, degreased them in alcohol, weighed using
an analytical balance (accurate to 0.0001 g), and placed them
into ceramic crucibles.
The high-temperature strength was determined in terms
of the change in the specific mass (gain, in g /c47m2) of the spe-
cimens in 330 h at t = 1100°C; the specimens were cooled in
air every 80 – 100 h. After each cycle of heating the speci -
mens were weighed again together with the crucibles.
We performed an x-ray diffraction phase analysis of the
scale formed after testing alloy VZhM4-VI with and without
La for high-temperature strength in cast condition and after
heat treatment.
Diffractograms of scale powders were taken with the
help of a D/c47MAX-250 (RIGAKU) diffractometer with mono-
chromatic K/c97 radiation. The operating mode of the
diffractometer was as follows: voltage 40 kV , current 200 mA,
range of diffractometry with respect to 2/c11315 – 90°C. The re-
sults were interpreted with the help of the Jade5 software.
To identify the phases we used a computer data base with
allowance for the composition. Preliminary selection of the
phases present in the specimens was performed in accor-
dance with the chemical composition of the alloy. Com-
paring the experimental values of the interfacial distances
and intensities to the data of the reference data base PDF2 for
the preliminarily selected phases we determined the actual
phase composition of the studied scale powders.
Metallographic analysis of the structure of the surface
layer of the specimens of alloy VZhM4-VI after oxidation
was performed with the help of an Olympus GX51 micro -
scope.

CONCLUSIONS
Lanthanum has a positive effect on the resistance of sin -
gle crystals of alloy VZhM4-VI to high-temperature oxida -
tion. Addition of lanthanum raises the refractoriness of the
alloy by a factor of 1.5 – 2.0 at a temperature of 1100°C. The
results obtained are confirmed by the data of the x-ray dif -
fraction phase analysis of the oxide film and of the metallo -
graphic analysis of the structure of the surface layer of speci-
mens of the alloy after oxidation.
