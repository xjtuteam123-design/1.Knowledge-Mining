ABSTRACT 
Refractory multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) have attracted much attention as promising candidate ma -
terials for structural applications at elevated temperatures. Several studies have demonstrated that edge dislo -
cation takes an important role in controlling high-temperature strength in refractory MPEAs. Here in order to 
understand the contribution of edge dislocation to strength, we have studied the effect of local lattice distortion 
on the core structure of edge dislocation in NbMoTaW MPEAs and the subsystems using atomistic simulations. 
We found the magnitude of lattice distortion is strongly dependent on the constituent elements and their con -
centrations. The lattice distortion reaches the maximum at around equiatomic composition. Binary alloys with 
large atomic size mismatch have a lattice distortion comparable with ternary and quaternary systems. We then 
have analyzed the core structure of 1/2 < 111 > /{110} edge dislocation in NbMoTaW MPEAs and the subsystems 
in the framework of Peierls-Nabarro model. The dislocation core half-width decreases with increasing lattice 
distortion exponentially. As a consequence, the critical shear stress for dislocation motion normalized by shear 
modulus exhibits a linear increase as the lattice distortion increases. This may help understand the linear cor -
relation of yield strength normalized by Young ’ s modulus with lattice distortion in various refractory MPEAs 
reported in experiments. Our present work provides insights into the origin of the essential contribution of edge 
dislocation to strength in refractory compositional complex alloys.

It is widely accepted that screw dislocation controls the plastic flow 
in BCC elemental metals and dilute alloys [ 12 ]. The screw dislocation 
remains very important to mechanical properties in BCC MPEAs 
[ 13 – 16 ]. For example, cross-kinks could be generated spontaneously 
along screw dislocation line in BCC MPEAs [ 14 ]. Detrapping of these 
cross-kinks requires a higher stress, resulting in a novel strengthening 
mechanism. A new cross-slip locking mechanism has been revealed for 
screw dislocation in the refractory NbMoTaW MPEA at high tempera -
ture [ 13 ]. However, recent studies have demonstrated that the 
high-strength might originate from edge dislocation in BCC MPEAs. 
Maresca et al. [ 17 ] have studied the mechanistic origin of high strength 
in BCC refractory MPEAs up to 1900 K. Using an analytical model, they 
revealed that the edge dislocation takes an important role in controlling 
strength for BCC MPEAs. The edge dislocation would undergo a high 
barrier for gliding due to the local pinning effect by compositional in -
homogeneity [ 18 ]. Wang et al. [ 19 ] have unveiled the unexpected 
dominance of glide of nonscrew dislocations in MoNbTi alloy. Lee et al. 
have found a substantial reduction in the mobility of edge dislocation

Dislocation behavior is fundamentally governed by its core structure 
[ 22 ]. The Peierls-Nabarro (P – N) model has been widely used to under -
stand the core structure of dislocations and the associated Peierls stress 
[ 23 – 30 ]. Unlike pure metals, there exists an intrinsic lattice distortion 
due to the mismatch of atomic size and modulus in MPEAs [ 31 – 34 ]. 
Experimentally, the magnitude of lattice distortion could be investigated 
using X-ray or neutron diffraction techniques [ 32 , 33 ]. The local strain 
induced by lattice distortion could result in a decay of Bragg peaks. The 
local lattice distortion could inevitably influence the properties of 
dislocation [ 16 , 20 , 35 ]. The measurement of friction stress indicates 
edge dislocation has a much narrower core half-width as the number of 
constituent elements increases in MPEAs [ 36 ]. Thus, it is essential to 
investigate the core structure of edge dislocation to probe the origin of 
its contribution to strength in BCC MPEAs. 
Here we have explored the effect of local lattice distortion on the 
core structure of 1/2 < 111 > /{110} edge dislocation in NbMoTaW 
MPEAs and the subsystems using atomistic simulation. We found the 
magnitude of lattice distortion is strongly dependent on the composition 
and concentration. The local lattice distortion reaches the maximum at 
around equiatomic composition and generally becomes higher as the 
number of constituent elements increases. The dislocation core half- 
width shows an exponential decrease with increasing lattice distor -
tion, resulting in the linear dependence of critical shear stress for 
dislocation motion normalized by shear modulus with respect to lattice 
distortion. This could help understand the linear scaling of yield strength 
normalized by Young ’ s modulus with lattice distortion in various re -
fractory MPEAs reported in experiments.

ergy and dislocation core structure [ 37 ].

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Local lattice distortion in NbMoTaW MPEAs and the subsystems 
Lattice distortion, which essentially originates from the atomic size 
mismatch in a solid solution, constitutes one of the key features that 
distinguish MPEAs from conventional metals and dilute alloys [ 31 ]. 
Severe lattice distortion can be generated when atoms with large size 
mismatch are mixed. We characterized the local lattice distortion using 
the averaged atomic displacement d concerning the ideal lattice site (see 
Methods). Fig. 2 shows the ternary diagrams of lattice distortion d for 
the Ta – W – Mo, W – Mo – Nb, Mo – Nb – Ta and Nb – Ta – W subsystems. The 
edges in each ternary diagram correspond to the binary subsystems. For 
binary alloys, we found the peak value of d occurs at the equiatomic 
composition. With the composition deviating from the equiatomic po -
sition, d reduces subsequently. For ternary subsystems, a relatively 
larger lattice distortion is generated in comparison to the binary sub -
systems. The euqiatomic composition still corresponds to the largest d at 
the center position. With the d -maps, we observed that some binary

4. Conclusion 
We carried out atomistic simulations to explore the effect of local 
lattice distortion on the core structure of 1/2 < 111 > /{110} edge dislo -
cation in refractory NbMoTaW MPEAs and the subsystems. Our work 
provides the direct correlation between lattice distortion and dislocation 
core structure at the microscale. This may help to understand the origin 
of high-strength of refractory MPEAs at elevated temperatures. The 
findings could be summarized as follows: 
(1) For the six binary subsystems, the local lattice distortion d rea -
ches the maximum at the equiatomic composition and becomes 
weakened as the composition deviates from the equiatomic 
composition. As the number of constituent elements increases, 
the lattice distortion follows a general trend to become stronger. 
However, some binary alloys with large atomic size mismatch 
have a severe lattice distortion comparable with that of ternary
