Submitted: 4 March 2024 / Revised: 9 August 2024 / Accepted: 19 September 2024 / Published online: 23 October 2024
We report, for the ﬁrst time, the effect of lanthanum zirconate (La 2Zr2O7) particles on the microstructure
and mechanical behavior of an experimental molybdenum oxide dispersion-strengthened alloy. The focus
was on the preparation of the novel Mo–La 2Zr2O7 composite using high-energy ball milling and spark
plasma sintering and on the comparison of its microstructural and mechanical properties with pure Mo and
Mo–La
2O3 ODS alloy counterparts. Mechanical properties were assessed using a Vickers hardness test at
room temperature and a three-point ﬂexural test in the temperature range from 2 150 to 150 /C176C. The
microstructure of the studied materials and their fracture behavior were evaluated using x-ray diffraction,
energy-dispersive x-ray spectroscopy, and scanning electron and transmission electron microscopy. The
strengthening effect of La
2Zr2O7 particles was found to be lower than that of La 2O3 particles, resulting in a
30-35% lower yield stress and ﬂexural strength of the Mo–La 2Zr2O7 alloy compared to the Mo–La 2O3
alloy. The experimental Mo–La 2Zr2O7 alloy exhibited low plasticity and no distinct ductile-to-brittle
transition temperature (DBTT) in the tested temperature range, unlike pure Mo and the Mo–La 2O3 alloy,
which had the DBTT of 63 and 1 /C176C, respectively. Fracture occurred mainly in a brittle intergranular
manner in the entire testing temperature range, while the counterpart materials showed localized plastic
stretching at grain boundaries and within grains at and above the transition region. The observed behavior
was primarily related to lower strengthening and brittleness as well as less effective grain boundary
puriﬁcation.

1. Introduction
Molybdenum (Mo) is a plausible basis for creating high-
temperature materials because of its high melting point (2623
/C176 C), the absence of phase transformations above room temper-
ature, excellent high-temperature strength, good thermal con-
ductivity (138 Wm
/C0 1 C/C0 1), low coefﬁcient of thermal
expansion (4.8 9 10/C0 6 K/C0 1 at 25 /C176 C), low speciﬁc heat
(25.1-28.4 JK /C0 1 Æ mol1 in the range 127-727 /C176 C), and a density
of 10.2 gcm /C0 3 (Ref 1). However, a disadvantage of pure Mo is
its high brittleness at ambient temperatures, which reduces its
applicability as a high-performance material. The low ductility
of molybdenum is mainly related to the intrinsic intergranular
brittleness of its body-centered cubic (BCC) crystal lattice, as
well as to the sensitivity of grain boundaries cohesion toward
the segregation of impurities, such as oxygen and nitrogen (Ref
2, 3). As with other BCC metals, the transition from a brittle to
ductile state in Mo requires thermal activation to overcome the
Peierls potential for plastic deformation, leading to a pro-
nounced temperature dependence of ductility. This transition is
characterized by the ductile-to-brittle transition temperature
(DBTT), which depends on several factors, such as grain size,
grain shape, grain boundary conditions, stress state, and
admixtures content, and, therefore, varies for pure molybdenum
in the temperature range between 20 and 70 /C176 C[ 4]. To improve
the strength and ductility of Mo and to reduce DBTT,
approaches that modify the grain boundaries and Mo solid
solutions have been developed (Ref 5).

The strengthening of Mo with solid oxide particles to
produce oxide dispersion-strengthened Mo alloys (ODS-Mo)
has emerged as one of the most effective grain-engineering
methods for improving ductility and reducing the DBTT. The
presence of oxide particles at the grain boundaries ensures the
reﬁnement of the grain size by promoting grain nucleation and
hindering grain growth (Ref 6, 7). In addition, rare-earth oxide
particles purify grain boundaries due to strong rare-earth–
oxygen interactions (Ref 7, 8). Previous studies have investi-
gated the impact of Mo doping with different types of rare-earth
oxide particles, including CeO
2,L a 2O3,Y 2O3, ThO 2,N d 2O3,
Sm2O3, and Gd 2O3 (Ref 9-12). Additionally, various doping
technologies were used, such as powder metallurgy processes,
liquid rare-earth solution doping, or liquid–liquid precursor
mixing, which determined the microstructure and mechanical
properties of prepared ODS-Mo alloys (Ref 12-15). These
studies showed that La
2O3-strengthened molybdenum typically
exhibits higher strength, lower DBTT, and better creep
resistance at high temperatures than the other rare-earth
oxide-strengthened alloys.
On the contrary, the use of compounds more complex than
binary oxides, such as rare-earth metal zirconates with the
chemical formula Ln
2Zr2O7 (where Ln is a trivalent rare earth
or lanthanide element) and the crystal structure of pyrochlore,
has been a poorly investigated topic so far. To our knowledge,
there are currently no studies available on the effect of rare-
earth zirconate particles on the microstructure and mechanical
properties of Mo, except for our pioneering research on the Mo-
La
2Zr2O7 composite powder production and its spark plasma
sintering (Ref 16).
In principle, rare-earth zirconates can be prospective rein-
forcements in Mo ODS alloys since they are exceptionally
thermally and chemically stable, with potential applications
encompassing electro/photocatalysis, nuclear waste storage,
solid-state laser materials, and thermal barrier coatings (Ref 17-
21). Lanthanum zirconate (La
2Zr2O7) seems to be the potential
candidate for the strengthening phase in the novel ODS-Mo due
to its cubic pyrochlore structure with excellent thermal stability
up to its melting point of 2300 /C176 C, low thermal conductivity
(1.56 Wm
/C0 1K/C0 1), high thermal expansion coefﬁcient
(9.1 9 10/C0 6 K/C0 1), moderate Young’s modulus (175 GPa),
low tendency to grain coarsening, and high chemical resistance
(Ref 17, 18). In fact, La
2Zr2O7 was already found to have better
thermal stability than La 2O3 in ODS-Mo composite powders,
the latter of which formed lanthanum oxy-molybdate LaMo 5O8
and La 3MoO7 intermediate compounds at 1300 /C176 C in an inert
atmosphere (Ref 16). However, the impact of La 2Zr2O7
particles on the mechanical properties of ODS-Mo alloys,
speciﬁcally on their strength and ductile-to-brittle transition
behavior, has not been studied previously.
In this work, we utilized microstructural characterization
(scanning electron microscopy, x-ray diffraction, transmission
electron microscopy, and laser scanning microscopy) in com-
bination with simple ﬂexural testing to compare the mechanical
properties of the novel ODS Mo-La
2Zr2O7 alloy, and its ODS
Mo-La2O3 alloy and technically pure Mo counterparts. A
powder metallurgy approach based on mechanical ball milling
of precursor powders and their subsequent hot consolidation
using a fast-sintering technique was used to obtain dense
compacts with a ﬁne-grain structure and small and well-
distributed reinforcing particles. Speciﬁcally, the combination
of the high-energy ball milling of precursor powders in the
planetary ball mill and subsequent spark plasma sintering was
used for the fabrication of the studied materials. Such a
combination of production techniques is typically used for the
synthesis of ODS alloys with improved mechanical properties
(Ref 22, 23). The ﬂexural test was performed at temperatures
ranging from /C0 150 to 150 /C176 C. Special attention was paid to
the relationship of microstructure, ductile-to-brittle transition
behavior, and operating fracture mechanisms. The experimental
data on the effects of high-temperature ball milling and spark
plasma sintering in pure Mo and Mo-La
2O3 and Mo-
La2O3 ODS alloys will aid in the continued advancement of
these materials.
2. Experimental
2.1 Composites Fabrication
The Mo-La2Zr2O7 and Mo-La2O3 alloys were prepared with
the compositions listed in Table 1, using the oxide particle
content typically employed in ODS-Mo alloys. Commercial
molybdenum (Mo; GTV , Germany), lanthanum oxide (La 2O3;
Golder Dragon, China), and lanthanum zirconate (La 2Zr2O7;
Ganzhou Rea Chemicals, China) powders were utilized for the
fabrication. The initial La 2O3 and La 2Zr2O7 powders were
initially mixed with pure Mo at 50 rpm for 3 h. Then, pure Mo
and the obtained composite mixtures with a weight of 100 g
were placed in steel capsules along with milling balls under a
nitrogen atmosphere to prevent powder oxidation during high-
energy ball milling (HEBM). An 80-ml stainless steel (AISI
304) capsule and 25 steel (100Cr6) balls with a diameter of 5
mm were used, keeping a ball-to-powder ratio (BPR) of 12:1.
The powders were ball-milled in a planetary ball mill
Pulverisette 6 (Fritsch; Germany) at a rotational speed of 350
rpm and a total milling time of 16.7 h, which comprised 40
milling cycles. Each cycle consisted of 15 minutes of milling
and 10 minutes of cooling of a capsule at rest. The HEBM
process conditions were selected based on the previous
optimization of process parameters to guarantee the minimal
size of the particles and homogeneous distribution of oxides
(Ref 24).
The compaction of the produced powders was performed
using a spark plasma sintering technique (SPS) in a double-
sample sintering mode (Fig. 1a), following tried and tested
settings. Two doses of prepared powders of 50 g each were
separated with a thin graphite foil and then placed into a
graphite die of 40 mm inner diameter lined with a carbon foil to
prevent adhesion of samples to the die walls. With this
technique, two discs with a diameter of 40 mm and a height of
7 mm can be produced simultaneously during one sintering
experiment (Fig. 1b). SPS was carried out using pulses of direct
electric current in on/off mode with an on/off pulse ratio of 12:2
and a single-pulse duration of 3.3 ms, at a temperature of 1450
/C176 C, a pressure of 40 MPa and with a hold time of 5 min. The
SPS process was regulated based on the temperature measured
by a pyrometer. The sintering parameters were selected based
on preliminary optimization experiments as well as on previous
scientiﬁc reports (Ref 14), which showed that the recrystalliza-
tion of pure molybdenum begins in the temperature range of
1000-1050 /C176 C, while the Mo-La
2O3 alloy has a higher
recrystallization temperature of 1500-1600 /C176 C. The selected
sintering conditions aimed at preserving the advantages pro-
vided by HEBM, i.e., reduced Mo grain size and reﬁned second

3.3 Mechanical Behavior of Composites
Hardness measurements revealed that the MoLa alloy was
the hardest (2.28 ± 0.04 GPa) among the studied materials,
while pure molybdenum was the softest (1.68 ± 0.03 GPa),
and the MoLaZr alloy had an intermediate hardness value
(1.99 ± 0.04 GPa).
Figure 7 shows the ﬂexural stress–strain curves obtained at
different testing temperatures. Both ODS Mo alloys were
clearly reinforced by oxide dispersions with respect to pure
molybdenum. Consistent with the hardness results, the rein-
forcement of the MoLaZr alloy was, nonetheless, relatively
moderate, and, concurrently, the ductility decreased, particu-
larly at higher testing temperatures. In contrast, the reinforce-
ment of the MoLa alloy was signiﬁcant and all tested samples
achieved strains greater than 3%, even at the lowest temper-
ature.
The temperature dependences of the ﬂexural strength and
the yield stress are plotted in Fig. 8(a). It should be noted that it
was not possible to determine these characteristics for all tests
because several samples failed without yielding, and some tests
were stopped at the limits of the current experimental setup
without failure. The results conﬁrmed the superiority of the
MoLa ODS alloy and only a moderate increase in the properties
of the MoLaZr ODS alloy compared to those of pure Mo. Both
the ﬂexural strength and the yield stress tended to decrease
above 20 /C176 C with increasing temperature. In the case of pure
Mo, the highest ﬂexural strength was obtained at a temperature
of /C0 20 /C176 C, while it was reduced at lower temperatures due to
decreased ductility. Both samples tested at /C0 90 and /C0 150 /C176 C
failed shortly after the start of the test in a brittle manner
(Fig. 7a).
The variation in ﬂexural strain as a function of temperature
is presented in Fig. 7(b), together with the ﬁtting curves based
on Eq. 3. The DBTT of pure Mo was found to be 63 /C176 C,
whereas the DBTT of the MoLa alloy was 1 /C176 C, at which the
ﬂexural strain rapidly increased from below 4% to more than
10%. In the case of the MoLaZr alloy, the ﬂexural strain
remained below 4% across the entire testing temperature range,

4. Discussion
Data from the literature show that HEBM is one of the most
effective processing methods for ODS Mo alloys production
(Ref 8). HEBM of MoLa and MoLaZr ODS alloys resulted in
substantial grain reﬁnement, forming the ﬁne platelet
microstructure with La
2O3 and La 2Zr2O7 particles located
mainly between the platelets (Fig. 4). These reinforcing
particles retarded grain coarsening during the subsequent SPS
process and strengthened the MoLa and MoLaZr alloys
compared to pure Mo.
Microstructural examinations of sintered ODS alloys
revealed that, despite a relatively low sintering temperature
and a short sintering time, the ﬁne platelet microstructure of
powders obtained after HEBM recrystallized to form equiaxed
grains. As previously reported (Ref 14), the recrystallization
temperature of milled Mo is substantially increased by adding
La
2O3 particles, which exert a Zener pinning force opposing the
migration of grain boundaries during annealing (Ref 31, 32).
Indeed, it was reported (Ref 7) that while pure Mo recrystallizes
at approximately 1000-1050 /C176 C (Ref 33, 34), the recrystalliza-
tion temperature of MoLa alloys is in the range of 1500-1600
/C176 C (Ref 7, 35, 36).
Our experiments show that the deformation energy stored in
the powder particles during HEBM serves a driving force for
the signiﬁcant coarsening of the original sub-microcrystalline
platelet microstructure during subsequent sintering. Taking into
account a similar grain size in both ODS alloys, it can be
concluded that La
2Zr2O7 does not offer a notable advantage
over La2O3 in limiting recrystallization of deformed grains. The
presence of smaller and larger grains in the microstructure as
well as the presence of intergranular and intragranular second-
phase particles in both alloys conﬁrms that the recrystallization
process was non-uniform and was affected by the degree of
deformation of individual platelets at speciﬁc sites. Conse-
quently, as a result of mechanical milling and subsequent
sintering, the ﬁnal microstructure of ODS alloys has the
equiaxed Mo grains with smaller submicrometric intragranular
particles that were overcome by advancing grain boundaries
during recrystallization, and larger micrometer-sized intergran-
ular particles located at grain boundaries.
The ﬂexural properties determined for the pure Mo and
MoLa ODS alloy reported here were comparable to those
reported in the literature. The measured values of the yield
stress and the ﬂexural strength of pure Mo were close to those
obtained by Hiraoka et al. ( /C24 620 MPa and /C24 800 MPa,
respectively, at 27 /C176 C) (Ref 37), whereas their observed
increase (by 60 and 50%, respectively) due to La
2O3 particle
strengthening agreed with the data on tensile properties of Mo-
La2O3 ODS alloy with similar La 2O3 fraction (Ref 10, 11, 13,
14, 35). The pure molybdenum sample showed a DBTT value
of 63 /C176 C that ﬁts reasonably the temperature range reported in
the literature (Ref 37). Fractographic observations conﬁrmed

5. Conclusions
In this work, the experimental Mo-La 2Zr2O7 ODS alloy was
prepared using high-energy ball milling and spark plasma
sintering and compared to the technically pure molybdenum
and Mo-La 2O3 ODS alloy prepared via the same production
route. The following conclusions can be drawn:
• The high-energy ball milling in pure Mo and both Mo-
La
2Zr2O7 and Mo-La 2O3 ODS alloys resulted in grain
reﬁnement, which was partially abated during the subse-
quent spark plasma sintering due to the stored deformation
energy causing grain coarsening from recrystallization.
Both ODS alloys exhibited a low porosity of approxi-
mately 1% and the presence of larger oxide particles at
grain boundaries, along with smaller particles within the
grains. The larger intergranular particles acted as preferen-
tial crack initiation sites.
• Doping Mo with La 2Zr2O7 resulted in a less pronounced
strengthening effect compared to the strengthening
achieved by the La 2O3 particles. The Mo-La 2Zr2O7 ODS
alloy demonstrated intermediate yield and ﬂexural
strength, falling between those of pure molybdenum and
the Mo-La
2O3 ODS alloy.
• Pure molybdenum and the Mo-La 2O3 ODS alloy showed
a typical ductile to brittle transition behavior, with the
DBTT values of 63 and 1 /C176 C, respectively. In contrast,
the ﬂexural strain of the ODS Mo-La
2Zr2O7 alloy was
inadequate in the measured temperature interval and
lacked a clearly discernible transition from the brittle to
the ductile state. This behavior is most likely attributed to
the brittleness of La
2Zr2O7 oxide particles compared to
La2O3, as well as their potentially lower grain boundary
puriﬁcation efﬁciency.
