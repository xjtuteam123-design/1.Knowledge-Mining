ABSTRACT 
A ductile V
0.5
Nb
0.5
ZrTi refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) with relatively low density (~6 g/cm
 3
) is 
investigated, designed by controlling its valence electron concentration. The homogenized alloy exhibits a stable 
BCC structure, significant work hardening rate and considerable tensile stress (787 MPa) and elongation (21.9%). 
The solid-solution strengthening effect is estimated to be ~575 MPa by simplifying into a pseudo-binary alloy, 
which is in accordance with the test results. These findings can inspire a new strategy to design RHEA with a 
balanced matching of strength and ductility. 
1. Introduction 
Recently, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) provide a new strategy to 
design materials, dealing with alloys composed of four or more principal 
elements [ 1 , 2 ]. Many unique properties are discovered in HEAs, 
including high strength-ductility synergy [ 3 , 4 ], ultra high Hall-Petch 
coefficient [ 5 ], excellent radiation tolerance [ 6 ] and superior mag -
netic properties [ 7 ]. As the representative type of HEAs, refractory HEAs 
(RHEAs) based on high-melting elements (Ti, V, Cr, Zr, Nb, Mo, Hf, Ta, 
W) and Al are promising alternatives for superalloys [ 8 , 9 ]. It is reported 
that the high-temperature stress of RHEAs is clearly superior to the 
traditional Ni-based superalloys (Mar-M247 and Inconel 718), produced 
by the intense solid-solution strengthening (SSS) effect [ 10 – 12 ]. How -
ever, most of RHEAs display inferior ductility at room temperature (RT), 
which significantly restricts the commercial application. Notable ex -
ceptions include several ductile RHEAs with a single BCC structure, 
HfNbZrTi [ 13 ] and Hf-Nb-Zr-Ti-Ta [ 14 – 16 ], which exhibit the tensile 
strain ( ε ) of ~10% and yield stress of 550 MP ~a900 MPa. Nevertheless, 
these alloys possess high density ( > 8.5 g/cm
3
) and price due to the rich 
of Hf and Ta. 
In this context, our motivation is to explore a ductile RHEA utilizing 
V, Nb, Zr and Ti to reduce its cost and price. Earlier papers report that 
the equiatomic VNbZrTi alloy comprises a single-phase structure at as- 
cast condition but multiphases structure after annealing [ 17 , 18 ]. In 
addition, the compressive strain of this alloy is only 4.2% [ 19 ], which 
requires to improve its ductility. Knowing that the concentration of el -
ements significantly affects the phase and mechanical properties in 
RHEAs [ 20 , 21 ]. For example, the AlNbTiVZr
x 
alloys have B2 þ Zr
5
Al
3 
phases when x < 0.25, while new C14 Laves phases are formed on this 
basis as increasing the content of Zr (x > 0.5) [ 22 ]. Varying the indi -
vidual element from 11.11 at.% to 27.27 at.% in Mo – Nb-Hf-Zr-Ti alloy, 
the compressive yield stress and strain are in the range of 1200 
MPa – 1900 MPa and 5% – 25%, respectively [ 23 ]. However, it is confused 
that how the composition affects the plasticity in RHEAs. Saad Sheikh 
[ 24 ] proposes a new method to optimize the intrinsically ductile RHEAs 
by controlling the valence electron concentration (VEC), which is well 
supported by the experimental data. Our previous work confirms that 
the ductility of single-phase RHEAs improves as the VEC decreases [ 16 ]. 
In V – Nb – Zr – Ti alloys, the VEC of V and Nb (group V) is higher than Zr 
and Ti (group IV). The deformability can be improved by adjusting the 
content of V and Nb appropriately.

VNbZrTi alloy (4.50). The microstructure, phase stability, tensile prop -
erties and SSS effect of V
0.5
Nb
0.5

successfully. This alloy exhibits a single BCC phase, which maintains a
