abstract
Effects of Re content and crystallo graphic orientation on creep behavior of aluminized Ni-base single 
crystal superalloys have been investigated in the present study. The addition of Re seemed to be effective 
for the creep strength improvement of Ni-base single crystal superalloys. The creep strength was signif- 
icantly decreased in aluminized specimens due to the change in microstructure under coating layer by 
the formation of the interdiffusion zone (IDZ) and substrate diffusion zone (SDZ). The specimens with 
{1 0 0} side-surf ace showed longer creep rupture lives than the specimens with {1 10} side-surface which 
means that the anisotropic creep behavior took place. The anisotropic creep behavior was more evident 
in aluminized CM186LC than in aluminized PWA 1480. This anisotropic creep behavior was mainly 
induced by the discrepancy of fracture modes due to the different arrangement of slip systems, the geom- 
etry as well as the depth penetrat ion of topologically close-packed (TCP) phases between the two side- 
surface orientations.

1. Introduction 
The superior mechanical properties of Ni-based superalloys 
have established them as a potential candidate for industria l gas 
turbines and jet engines applicati ons. The efﬁciency of power gen- 
erating gas turbines, higher turbine inlet and turbine blade operat- 
ing temperatures are required [1–4]. To increase the service 
performanc e of power generating gas turbines and others, the 
mechanical properties of superalloys such as creep at high temper- 
atures should be improved. Therefore, some modiﬁcations and 
improvements for higher temperature strength of Ni-based super- 
alloys have intensively been carried out. Adding refractor y ele- 
ments is a method used to increase the creep properties of 
superalloys, they are expected to reduce the diffusion processes 
in superalloys due to the solid solution strengthening effect of 
these elements in the superalloys. For example, Re has been found 
to be particularly beneﬁcial element [5] where a signiﬁcant
improvement of the creep strength may be obtained by the addi- 
tion of rhenium (Re). Harris et al. [6] suggested that the high tem- 
perature creep, rupture and other mechanical properties of Ni-base 
superalloys, could be considerably improved by the addition of 
rhenium (Re).
Although Ni-based superalloys have an outstandi ng mechanical 
strength at high temperatures but they tend to be vulnerable to 
environment attack due to the addition of refractory elements that 
provide their high temperature strength. The protectiv e coatings 
are necessary to be applied on the surface of superallo ys to prevent 
from oxidation and hot corrosion in high temperature applications. 
Aluminide coatings have been widely used in Ni-based superalloys 
for high temperat ure applications [7,8]. Aluminide coatings which 
prepared by pack aluminizing process [9] are used to produce a
good protection against high temperat ure oxidation and hot corro- 
sion since the practical commercial beneﬁts rather than other coat- 
ing processes. However , interdiffusion of elements between the 
superallo y substrate and the coating layer takes place when alumi- 
nized superalloys are thermally exposed at high temperature s, 
resulting in a change in the microstru cture of the substrates [9].
IDZ between the coating layer and superalloy substrate brings 
about some problems such as the formation of secondary reaction 
zone (SRZ) [10] and TCP phases [9–13] beneath the IDZ. Besides, 
the mechanical properties of Ni-base single crystal superalloys 
have been inﬂuenced by the crystallogra phic orientation of the 
specimen surface [12,14]. The crystallogra phic orientati on effect 
is one of interesting subject in Ni-base single crystal superallo ys. 
However , there are several reports concerning on creep behavior 
of Ni-base single crystal superallo ys [15–18] but not so much 
attention has been paid to the creep behavior of aluminized Ni- 
base single crystal superalloys and its factors. 
This study investiga tes the effects of Re content and crystallo- 
graphic orientation on creep behavior of aluminized Ni-based sin- 
gle crystal superalloys. The microstructur e change during creep 
test was determined as a function of crystallogra phic orientation, 
and the relationship between deformation mechanism s and creep 
behavior was brieﬂy discussed.

2. Materials and experiment al procedures 
Two types of superalloys, PWA 1480 and CM186LC, were used 
as substrate materials in the present study. The chemical composi- 
tion (in wt.%) and heat treatment procedure of superalloys are pre- 
sented in Tables 1 and 2 , respectivel y. 
The crystallograph ic orientation of superalloys was analyzed by 
the X-ray Laue reﬂection method. The creep specimens with {100} 
and {110} side-surface orientati ons were then cut from the rod by 
electric discharge machining (EDM) with a cross section area of 
2.8 mm /C2 2.8 mm and the gauge length of 19.6 mm ( Fig. 1 ). The 
specimens were mechanicall y polished down to 1200 mesh by em- 
ery paper and ultrasonicall y cleaned in acetone bath for 10 min 
prior to aluminizi ng process. The polished specimens were then 
embedded in a Al 2O3 retort containing a mixture of 24.5 mass% 
Al, 24.5 mass% Fe, 49 mass% Al 2O3 and 2 mass% NH 4Cl powders 
and heated at 1000 /C176C for 5 h in ﬂowing argon for aluminizing 
treatment. The creep rupture test was performed at 900 /C176C/
392 MPa with a parallel load direction to h001 i direction. In this 
study, the uncoated specimens were also tested in order to com- 
pare the creep lifetime with the coated ones. The microstructur es 
of virgin materials, coated specimens and crept specimens were 
observed by optical microscopy (OM) and scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM).
3. Results 
3.1. Creep rupture test 
The creep rupture lives of coated and uncoated superalloys with 
{100} and {110} surface orientations are presented in Fig. 2 . The 
detailed information of the creep rupture lives and the difference 
in creep rupture lives for {100} and {110} side-surface orientati ons 
are presented in Table 3. CM186LC has a higher creep life than that 
of PWA 1480. This phenomeno n is possibly due to the chemical 
compositi on of superallo ys where CM186LC contained 2.9 mass% 
Re and PWA 1480 was containing Re free. Moreover, the uncoated 
specimens showed a higher creep rupture lives compared to the 
coated specimens in both orientati ons. It was also found that the 
{100} specimens have a higher creep rupture lives than those of 
{110} specimens. It indicated that the mechanical properties of 
the superallo ys have been affected by the crystallogra phic orienta- 
tion of the specimens. Concisely, the anisotropic creep behavior 
was occurred at different orientations in aluminized specimens.

3.3. Fracture surfaces 
The highest creep rupture lives was reached by aluminized 
CM186LC (Table 3). Accordingl y, the facture surfaces of aluminized 
CM186LC were observed as shown in Fig. 8 in order to clarify the 
anisotropic creep properties. The fracture surface consists of 
microvoids produced during plastic deformation on both side-sur- 
face orientations . The fracture surfaces exhibited two different 
fracture modes which can be categorized as dimple fracture region 
(mode I) and slip region (mode II). The presence of the two differ- 
ent fracture modes on the fracture surface concurrently proposed a
mixed mode of failure operating during creep rupture tests at 
900 /C176C.

tween the substrate and the surroundings to prevent degradation 
in a harsh environment. Although the protective coating is not par- 
ticularly designed to improve the mechanical properties of the 
material, its strain behavior and toughnes s are important with re- 
spect to its ability to protect the surface and to prevent crack initi- 
ation under mechanical loading [19]. As shown in Fig. 1, the creep 
rupture lives of the aluminized specimens are lower than those of 
the uncoated specimens for both superalloys. This suggested that

TCP phase were not precipitated in aluminized PWA 1480 superal-

4.2. Effect of Re content 
The creep rupture test result exhibited that CM186LC had a
longer creep lives compared to the PWA 1480. One of factors inﬂu-
ences the difference in creep lives is the chemical compositi on of 
the superalloys. CM186LC contains 2.9 mass% of Re whereas PWA 
1480 is free of Re content. The addition of Re has some advanta- 
geous effects on single crystal superalloys. The large and slow dif- 
fusing element Re segregates mainly in the matrix ( c) phase [14].
The addition of Re has a solid solution strengthening effect in the 
matrix, because about 80% element Re is mainly distributed in 
the c matrix to form the atomic group with short-range ordering 
structure of about several nm in size. This may diminish the diffu- 
sion rates of the elements and directional coarsenin g of c0 phase
during creep to improve the microstructure stability and preclude 
the dislocatio n movement [15,16]. Finally, since Re segregates 
mainly in the matrix phase and has a large atomic radius, it inﬂu-
ences the misﬁt between matrix and 
c0 lattice parameter [17]. In- 
deed, the limited Re addition to superallo y single crystals could be 
utilized to contribute in strengthening of superalloys by solid solu- 
tion means and also by retarding the c0 coarsenin g process which 
should increase rupture lives at elevated temperat ures. On the 
other hand, the addition of Re increases the density and gives rises 
to a large-scale increase of the production cost of superalloys, and 
Re addition might promote the propensi ty for precipitatio n of brit- 
tle intermetalli c phase known as TCP phase which leads to delete- 
rious effect on creep strength [22].
It has been conducte d the alloy investigation in order to mini- 
mize the Re concentration on the motive of maintaining the creep 
strength as reported [23]. It is of interest to evaluate how Re can be 
replaced with other strengthening elements in single crystal super- 
alloys, such as Mo and W to minimize cost and density. In most 
cases, the additions of Mo, W and Re are commonly incorporated 
to provide a high degree of solid solution strengthening [24]. This 
results in exception al improvem ents at high temperature creep 
resistance. Among these refractory elements , Re offers the highest 
degree of solid solution strengthening, which allows unusual 
improvements in high temperature creep resistance. However, 
superalloy containing high levels of these refractory alloying addi- 
tions are vulnerable to the formation of TCP phases during service 
at high temperatures [5–7].
4.3. Effect of crystallographic orientation 
As previously reported, the mechanical properties of Ni-base 
single crystal superalloys are noticeab ly inﬂuenced by crystallo -
graphic orientation [25,26]. Therefore, it might be concluded that 
aluminized coatings obtained by pack aluminizing treatment on 
different crystallogra phic surfaces of Ni-base single crystal super- 
alloy might result in different structures. It is obviously seen in alu- 
minized CM186LC where the creep lives between {100} and {110} 
side-surface orientations are distinctive as well as in aluminized 
PWA 1480 as presente d in Table 3. However, the anisotropi c creep 
properties are the most dominan t in aluminized superalloy 
CM186LC.
To explain this phenomenon, there are two possible reasons 
should be considered that is the TCP phase formatio n and the 
mechanism of deformat ion fracture. First, the 
r phase, one of the 
TCP phases, formed coherently on {111} planes in platinum-m od- 
iﬁed aluminized Ni-based single-cr ystal substrates [9]. The sche- 
matic of TCP phase growth both on {100} and {110} side-surface 
orientations is illustrated in Fig. 9 . The TCP phases grow up in a
shape of X-mark on {100} side-surface and in a shape of +-mark 
on {110} side-surface . The TCP preferred precipitation orientation 
has affected the extension of zone under the IDZ, termed as SDZ, 
which occurred during plastic deformation . In the present study, 
the SDZ was present. In fact, the presence of SDZ results in the deg- 
radation of creep rupture life of the aluminized superallo ys. This 
phenomeno n is caused by the differenc e in SDZ thickness which 
inﬂuenced by the geometry of TCP phase inside SDZ. The depth 
of TCP phase penetration on both can be measured from Figs. 6
and 7. TCP phase depth was approximat ely 9.0 ± 0.2 lm on {100} 
side-surface and 12.0 ± 0.2 lm on {110} side-surface. Hence, the 
depth of TCP phase penetration might have a signiﬁcant role of 
high diffusion path for refractor y elements to enhance further 
growth of diffusion layers on {110} side-surface . The r phase
formed as thin continuo us ﬁlm on the {111} planes coherently 
[9]. The interface between TCP and the matrix at high tempera- 
tures is regularly associated with interfacial decohesion which 
may potentially act as initiation sites for crack [18]. The presence 
of TCP during service depletes the elements such as W, Cr and 
Re, which enter into the TCP phase and reduce their solid strength- 
ening in the matrix located adjacent to the TCP phase. The deple- 
tion zone would provide the crack initiation site and its 
propagat ion path. 
Secondly , the fracture surfaces of creep ruptured specimens 
with different orientations could be considered in order to clarify 
the anisotropic creep behavior due to plastic deformat ion. As 
shown in Fig. 8, the fracture surfaces exhibited two different frac- 
ture modes which can be classiﬁed as dimple fracture region 
(mode I) and slip region (mode II) as separated by the black line. 
In the mode I region, small microvoids form and coalesce in the 
interior of the substrate. Whereas in the mode II region, crack 
propagat es along {111} planes which is the normal direction 
makes an angle of 54.7 /C176with the tensile stress axis. Thus, the oper- 
ation of {111} h101i slip system would be result in the slip-band 
decohesi on fracture along {111} slip planes near the surface. The 
ratio or percentage of {111} plane (compared to the overall area)

state are shown in Table 5. The arrangement of {111} h101i slip sys-

der a multi-axial stress state, the resolved shear stress s is
decreased by the lateral stresses: r11 in the width direction or 
r33 in the thickness direction. Consequentl y, a smaller s will be ap- 
plied on the slip plane. If one of slip systems (T group) operates 
preferentiall y, and this results in a contraction of the thickness of 
specimen. If one of slip systems (W group) operates, this will result 
in a contraction of the width. However, a free surface of the spec- 
imen will be under the plane stress condition, that is, r11 and r33 is
approximat ely equal to zero; therefore, a large shear stress s will
be applied on the slip plane near the surface. This large shear stress 
will promote the crack initiation and growth crack. However, a
crack propagation on {111} planes causes more decrease in the 
effective cross section area of the {110} side-surface than that of 
{100} side-surface. In conclusion, the {110} specimens exhibited 
lower creep rupture life than those of {100} specimens, this is 
due to the difference in the crystallograph ic geometry of {111} slip 
planes on which microcrack s form in the IDZ hard layer and along 
the interface of TCP. Therefore the hard layer in IDZ would be the 
reason for degradation mechanism in aluminized superalloys 
[29]. TCP precipita tion would be additional degradat ion factors. It 
is also reported that adding Re to single crystal superalloys tends 
to form Re-rich topologically closed packed (TCP) phase precipita- 
tion which is known to reduce creep rupture strength [30]. As 
shown in Fig. 2 , the side surface effect was more pronounced in 
CM186LC which showed TCP precipitate (Fig. 7), Re would amplify 
the side surface orientation effect through TCP precipitation. 
5. Conclusion s
The effect of Re content and crystallogra phic orientation on 
creep behavior of aluminized Ni-base single crystal superalloys 
has been investiga ted in this study. The following conclusio ns 
can be withdraw n from this study: 
(1) The Re content improved creep strength of substrate despite 
it provoked the TCP phase formatio n in the diffusion zone. 
Concisely, the advantag e by the solid solution strengthening 
effect due to Re addition would be larger than the disadvan- 
tage of Re-rich TCP precipitation. 
(2) The aluminizing treatment was decreased the creep strength 
of superalloys. Furthermore, the aluminized specimens with 
{100} side-surface showed longer creep rupture lives than 
the specimens with {110} side-surface . This phenomenon 
is due to the change in microstructur e between the coating 
and substrate region by the formation of IDZ and SDZ. 
(3) The effect of crystallograph ic orientati on on the creep rup- 
ture lives was the most evident in aluminized superallo y
CM186LC. This is due to the different arrangement in the 
crystallograph ic geometry of {111} slip planes on which 
micro-cracks formed. The TCP precipita tion develope d and 
would be additional factors for the decrease of creep rupture 
lives in aluminized CM186LC.
